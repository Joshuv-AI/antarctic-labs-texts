Gamma mixed fractional Lévy Ornstein - Uhlenbeck process
Héctor Araya Johanna Garzón Rolando Rubilar

Abstract
In this article, we introduce a non Gaussian long memory process constructed by the aggregation of independent copies of a fractional Lévy Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process with random coefficients. Several properties and a limit theorem are studied for this new process. Finally, some simulations of the limit process are shown.



2010 AMS Classification Numbers:
60G10, 60G17, 60H05, 60H30.

Key Words and Phrases
: non Gaussian, fractional Lévy process, Ornstein-Uhlenbeck, random coefficients.

1
Introduction

An Ornstein-Uhlenbeck (OU) process is a diffusion process introduced by the physicists Leonard Salomon Ornstein and George Eugene Uhlenbeck
[
28
]
to describe the stochastic behavior of the velocity of a particle undergoing Brownian motion. The OU process
X=\{X(t),\ {t\geq 0}\}
is the solution of the Langevin equation

dX(t)=\alpha X(t)dt+\sigma dB(t),\ \ t\geq 0,

(1)

where
X(0)=x\in\mathbb{R}
,
B=\{B(t),t\geq 0\}
is a Brownian motion and
\alpha,\sigma
are constants. This process is stationary, Gaussian and Markovian; in fact, it is the only stochastic process which has all these three properties. This process is used for modelling in many different fields such as physics, biology and finance among others (see
[
1
,
6
,
15
,
28
,
24
,
29
]
and references therein) and it has been widely generalized.

Different extensions of the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck processes have been obtained to replace the Brownian motion in (
1
) by more general noise processes; for example, Lévy OU
[
2
]
, fractional OU
[
7
]
, sub-fractional OU
[
23
]
or Hermite OU processes
[
17
]
. These are introduced by solution of the Langevin equation with driving noise given by a Lévy process, fractional Brownian motion, sub-fractional Brownian motion, Hermite process respectively.

In the study of long-range dependence, Igoli and Terdik
[
14
]
defined a generalization of OU process with this property. This process is called Gamma-mixed Ornstein-Uhlenbeck processes and it is built via aggregation of a sequence of random coefficient independent Ornstein-Uhlenbeck processes. Let us be more precise. Given a sequence
(X_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of stochastic processes such that for each
k\geq 1
, the process
X_{k}
is the solution of the Langevin equation

dX_{k}(t)=\alpha_{k}X(t)dt+dB(t),

(2)

where
B
is a Brownian motion with time parameter
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
(-\alpha_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
are independent random variables (also independent of
B
) with Gamma distribution
\Gamma(1-h,\lambda)
with
h\in(0,1)
and
\lambda>0
. The aggregated process is given by

Y_{n}(t)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}X_{k}(t),

and it converges, as
n\to\infty
, to a stochastic process
Y
which is a stationary Gaussian process, semimartingale, asymptotically self-similar and it has long-range dependence. This limit process is the so-called Gamma-mixed Ornstein-Uhlenbeck. In a similar way, in
[
9
]
and
[
10
]
the authors studied the case where
B
in (
2
) is generalized to be a fractional Brownian motion and Hermite process respectively; they define the fractional Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process mixed with a Gamma distribution and the Hermite Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process mixed with a Gamma distribution, both processes exhibit long range dependence, the first one is a Gaussian process but the second one is not Gaussian.

The aim of this paper is to define and study some properties of the Gamma mixed fractional Lévy Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process obtained as the limit of the aggregated OU process driven by fractional Lévy process and random coefficient with Gamma distribution.

The fractional Lévy process (fLp) was defined by
[
19
]
as a generalization of the moving average representation of fractional Brownian motion given by Mandelbrot and Van Ness
[
18
]
when replacing the Brownian motion in this integral representation by a Lévy process with zero mean, finite variance and without Gaussian part. FLp is almost surely Hölderian, has stationary increments and long range dependence, but unlike fractional Brownian motion, this process is neither Gaussian nor self-similar process. In
[
11
]
, authors introduced the fractional Lévy Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process (fLOUp) as the unique stationary pathwise solution of the Langevin equation driven by a fLp and prove that its increments exhibit long range dependence. Recently, many authors have studied fLp and the fractional Lévy Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process on theoretical and applicable levels, see for example
[
3
,
4
,
13
,
16
,
25
,
27
,
30
]
and the references therein.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we give a brief introduction to the fLp and the stochastic calculus related to this. Fractional Lévy Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process with random coefficient is introduced in Section 3. In Section 4, we define the aggregated processwhich of fLOUp with random coefficient and study its limit process, which we will call Gamma mixed fractional Lévy Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process. Finally, in Section 5 we present some simulations of the paths of the Gamma mixed fractional Lévy Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we briefly recall some relevant aspects of the fractional Lévy process (fLp), its main properties and stochastic integrals with respect to this fLp. This process will be used in the remainder of the paper. We work on a complete probability space
(\Omega_{L},{\cal F}_{L},\mathbb{P}_{L})
.

2.1
Fractional Lévy process

The fractional Lévy process
L^{d}=\{L^{d}_{t},\ t\in\mathbb{R}\}
, with
d\in(0,1/2)
, is a non-Gaussian process defined as follows (see
[
19
]
):

L_{t}^{d}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f_{t}^{(d)}(s)dL(s),\quad t\in\mathbb{R},

(3)

where the kernel function
f_{t}^{(d)}
is given by

f_{t}^{(d)}(s)={1\over\Gamma(d+1)}[(t-s)_{+}^{d}-(-s)_{+}^{d}],\ \ \ s\in\mathbb{R}

(4)

and
L=\{L(t),\ t\in\mathbb{R}\}
is a zero-mean two-sided Lévy process with
\mathbb{E}(L_{1}^{2})<\infty
and without Brownian component, i.e.

L_{t}=L^{(1)}_{t}1_{\{t\geq 0\}}-L^{(2)}_{-t}1_{\{t\leq 0\}},

where
L^{(1)}
and
L^{(2)}
are two independent copies of the same one-sided Lévy process.

The following Lemma (see
[
19
,
16
]
) establishes that the fLp is well defined in the
L^{2}(\Omega)
-sense and gives its characteristic function.

Lemma 1
.

Let
L=\{L_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
be a two-sided Lévy process without Brownian component such that
E[L(1)]=0
and
E[L(1)^{2}]<\infty
. For
t\in\mathbb{R}
, let
f_{t}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
. Then the integral
S(t):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f_{t}(u)dL(u)
exists in the
L^{2}(\Omega)
sense and
E[S(t)]=0
. Furthermore,
S(t)
satisfies the isometry

E[(S(t))^{2}]=E[(L(1))^{2}]\|f_{t}(\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})},\ \ \ t\in\mathbb{R},

the covariance function of process
S
is given by

\tilde{\Gamma}(s,t)=cov(S(s),S(t))=E[(L(1))^{2}]\int_{\mathbb{R}}f_{t}(u)f_{s}(u)du,\ \ s,t\in\mathbb{R}

and the characteristic function of
S(t_{1}),\ldots,S(t_{m})
for
t_{1}<\ldots<t_{m}
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
is given by

E_{L}\left[\exp\left(i\sum_{j=1}^{m}\theta_{j}S(t_{j})\right)\right]=\exp\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\theta_{j}f_{t_{j}}(s)\right)ds\right),\ \ \ \text{for}\ \theta_{j}\in\mathbb{R},\ j=1,\ldots,m,

where

\psi(u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}(e^{iux}-1-iux)\nu(dx),\quad u\in\mathbb{R}

and
\nu
is the Lévy measure of
L
.

From (
3
) we can see that the covariance function of
L^{d}
is given by

\mathbb{E}(L^{d}_{t}L^{d}_{s})=\frac{1}{2}V_{d}^{2}\left(|t|^{2d+1}+|s|^{2d+1}-|t-s|^{2d+1}\right),\quad t,s\in\mathbb{R},

(5)

where
V_{d}^{2}=\frac{\mathbb{E}(L_{1}^{2})}{2\Gamma(2d+2)\sin(\pi(d+1/2))}
. Up to a scaling constant, this is the same covariance as fractional Brownian motion.

The fractional Lévy process
L^{d}
defined by (
3
) has the following properties (see
[
19
]
for their proofs):

•

For any
\beta\in(0,d)
, the sample paths of
L^{d}
are a.s
\beta
- Hölder continuous.

•

L^{d}
is a process with stationary increments and symmetric, i.e.
\{L^{d}_{-t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(d)}}{{=}}\{-L^{d}_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
.

•

L^{d}
cannot be self-similar. However,
L^{d}
is asymptotically self-similar with parameter
0<d<0.5
, i.e

\lim\limits_{c\rightarrow\infty}\left\{\frac{L^{d}_{ct}}{c^{d}}\right\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(d)}}{{=}}\left\{B^{d}_{t}\right\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}},

where the equality is in the sense of finite dimensional distributions.

•

For
h>0
, the covariance between two increments
L^{d}_{t+h}-L^{d}_{t}
and
L^{d}_{s+h}-L^{d}_{s}
, where
s+h\leq t
and
t-s=nh
is

\displaystyle\delta_{d}(n)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle V_{d}^{2}h^{2d+1}\left[(n+1)^{2d+1}+(n-1)^{2d+1}-2n^{2d+1}\right]

(6)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle V_{d}^{2}d(2d+1)h^{2d+1}n^{2d-1}+O(n^{2d-2}),\quad n\rightarrow\infty.

•

The increments of
L^{d}
exhibit long memory, in the sense that for
d>0
, we have

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\delta_{d}(n)=\infty.

In the following, we recall two results from the reference
[
19
]
concerning to stochastic integrals with respect to fractional Lévy process.

Let
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
and
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
be the spaces of integrable and square integrable real functions, respectively and
H
the completion of
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
with respect to the norm
\|g\|^{2}_{H}=E[L_{1}^{2}]\int_{\mathbb{R}}(I^{d}_{-}g)^{2}(u)du
, where
(I^{d}_{-}{g})
denotes the right-sided Riemann-Liouville fractional integral defined by

(I^{d}_{-}{g})(x)=\dfrac{1}{\Gamma(d)}\int_{x}^{\infty}g(t)(t-x)^{d-1}dt.

Lemma 2
.

If
g\in H
, then

\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(s)dL^{d}_{s}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}(I^{d}_{-}{g})(s)dL_{s},

where the equality is in
L^{2}
sense.

The next second-order property of the stochastic integral respect to fLp will be a key tool in this article.

Lemma 3
.

If
|f|,|g|\in H
, then

E\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(s)dL^{d}_{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(s)dL^{d}_{s}\right]=C_{d}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(t)g(s)|t-s|^{2d-1}dsdt,

where

C_{d}=\dfrac{\Gamma(1-2d)E[L_{1}^{2}]}{\Gamma(d)\Gamma(1-d)}.

(7)

The following result from reference
[
11
]
, established the solution of the Langevin equation driven by a fractional Lévy process.

Theorem 1
.

Let
L^{d}
be an fLp,
d\in(0,1/2)
and
\lambda>0
. Then the unique stationary pathwise solution of the Langevin equation

dX^{d,\lambda}(t)=\lambda X^{d,\lambda}(t)dt+dL^{d}_{t}

is given a.s. by

X^{d,\lambda}(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{t}e^{\lambda(t-u)}dL^{d}(u),\quad t\in\mathbb{R},

(8)

this process is called fractional Lévy-Ornstein-Uhlenbeck processes (fLOUp).

3
Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process with random coefficient

In this section, we study the fractional Lévy-Ornstein-Uhlenbeck processes (fLOUp) with random coefficients. First, we establish the existence of the solution, and then some properties of the process are shown.

We consider the fractional Lévy Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process
V^{d}=(V^{d}(t),t\in\mathbb{R})
given as the solution of

dV^{d}(t)=\lambda V^{d}(t)dt+dL^{d}(t),\quad t\in\mathbb{R},

(9)

where
L^{d}
is a fLp with
d\in(0,1/2)
and defined on
(\Omega_{L},{\cal F}_{L},\mathbb{P}_{L})
; and the coefficient
\lambda
is a random variable defined on the probability space
(\Omega_{\lambda},{\cal F}_{\lambda},\mathbb{P}_{\lambda})
and independent by
L^{d}
. We assume that
-\lambda
follows a Gamma distribution with parameters
1-h
and
\alpha
, i.e.
-\lambda\sim\Gamma(1-h,\alpha)
with
h\in(0,1)
and
\alpha>0
.

From Theorem
1
, the SDE given by (
9
) has the explicit solution

V^{d}(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{t}e^{\lambda(t-u)}dL^{d}(u),\quad t\in\mathbb{R},

(10)

where the initial condition is given by

V^{d}(0)=\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{-\lambda u}dL^{d}(u).

Clearly, this process is stationary. Furthermore, by Lemma
2
we can get

V^{d}(t)=\dfrac{1}{\Gamma(d)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{-\infty}^{t}e^{\lambda(t-v)}(v-u)_{+}^{d-1}dvdL(u),\quad t\in\mathbb{R}.

We will prove that for every
\omega_{\lambda}\in\Omega_{\lambda}
, the process
V^{d}
is well defined in
L^{2}(\Omega_{L})
. In fact, by Lemma
3
we have that for
C_{d}=\dfrac{\Gamma(1-2d)}{\Gamma(d)\Gamma(1-d)}E[L_{1}^{2}]

\displaystyle E_{L}[(V^{d}(t))^{2}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{d}\int_{-\infty}^{t}\int_{-\infty}^{t}e^{\lambda(t-u)}e^{\lambda_{k}(t-v)}|u-v|^{2d-1}dudv

(11)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{d}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{\lambda(u+v)}|u-v|^{2d-1}dudv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2C_{d}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{u}e^{\lambda_{k}(u+v)}(u-v)^{2d-1}dvdu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2C_{d}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{2\lambda u}\int_{0}^{u}e^{-\lambda v}v^{2d-1}dvdu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2C_{d}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda v}v^{2d-1}\int_{v}^{\infty}e^{2\lambda u}dudv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\dfrac{C_{d}}{\lambda}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{\lambda v}v^{2d-1}dv=\dfrac{C_{d}}{(-\lambda)^{2d+1}}\Gamma(2d).

Remark 1
.

For
c\neq 0
, by (
11
) we have that
E_{L}[(V^{d}(ct))^{2}]=\dfrac{C_{d}}{(-\lambda)^{2d+1}}\Gamma(2d)
. Hence the processes
V^{d}
is no self-similar.

Lemma 4
.

The process
V^{d}
(for fixed
\omega_{\lambda}\in\Omega_{\lambda}
) is stationary, i.e. for
b>0
and
t_{1}<\ldots<t_{n}
, with
n\in\mathbb{N}

(V^{d}(t_{1}+b),\ldots,V^{d}(t_{n}+b))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(d)}}{{=}}(V^{d}(t_{1}),\ldots,V^{d}(t_{n})),

where
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(d)}}{{=}}
means equality in the sense of finite dimensional distributions.

Proof:
For
b>0
,
u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}
and
-\infty<t_{1}<\ldots<t_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{R}
, by the stationarity of the increments of
L^{d}
we get:

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}V^{d}(t_{i}+b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}\int_{-\infty}^{t_{i}+b}e^{\lambda(t_{i}+b-u)}dL^{d}(u)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(d)}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}\int_{-\infty}^{t_{i}}e^{\lambda(t_{i}-u)}dL^{d}(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}V^{d}(t_{i}).

∎
Using the results from
[
22
,
20
]
already used in the reference
[
21
]
to construct long memory process based on CARMA process driven by Lévy processes. We will provide a spectral representation of the process
V^{d}
given by (
10
).

Let
L=\{L(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
be a two sided square integrable Lévy process with
E[L(1)]=0
and

E[L(1)^{2}]=\Sigma_{L}
, then there exists a random orthogonal measure
\Phi_{L}
with spectral measure
F_{L}
such that
E[\Phi_{L}(\Delta)]=0
for any bounded Borel set
\Delta
,

F_{L}(dt)=\dfrac{\Sigma_{L}}{2\pi}dt.

Also, the random measure
\Phi_{L}
is uniquely determined by

\Phi_{L}([a,b))=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{e^{-i\alpha a}-e^{-i\alpha b}}{2\pi i\alpha}L(d\alpha),

(12)

for all
-\infty<a<b<\infty
. Moreover,

L(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{e^{i\alpha t}-1}{i\alpha}\Phi_{L}(d\alpha),\quad t\in\mathbb{R}.

Hence, for any function
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{C})
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(\alpha)\Phi_{L}(d\alpha)=\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-i\alpha t}f(\alpha)d\alpha L(dt)=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\hat{f}(t)L(dt),

(13)

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\hat{f}(t)L(dt)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{i\alpha t}\hat{f}(t)dt\Phi_{L}(d\alpha)=\sqrt{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(\alpha)\Phi_{L}(d\alpha),

(14)

where

\hat{f}(t)=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-i\alpha t}f(\alpha)d\alpha\quad\mbox{and}\quad f(\alpha)=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{i\alpha t}\hat{f}(t)d\alpha.

Lemma 5
.

Let
V^{d}
be a fLpOU with random coefficient given by (
10
). Then, the spectral representation of
V^{d}
is

V^{d}(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{it\alpha}\dfrac{1}{i\alpha-\lambda}\Phi_{M}(d\alpha),\quad t\in\mathbb{R},

(15)

where

\Phi_{M}([a,b])=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{e^{-ias}-e^{-ibs}}{2\pi is}dL^{d}(s).

Proof:
By Theorem 2.8 in
[
20
]
, we know that

L^{d}(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}(e^{i\alpha t}-1)(i\alpha)^{-(d+1)}\Phi_{L}(d\alpha),\quad t\in\mathbb{R}.

Furthermore, following the proof of Theorem 2.8, Remark 2.9 and equality (2.31) in the same reference, we can obtain

\displaystyle V^{d}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{\lambda(t-u)}1_{(-\infty,t)}(u)dL^{d}(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{i\alpha u}e^{\lambda(t-u)}1_{(-\infty,t)}(u)du\Phi_{M}(d\alpha)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{it\alpha}\dfrac{1}{i\alpha-\lambda}\Phi_{M}(d\alpha).

Then, the result is achieved. ∎

Corollary 1
.

With almost no major effort, we can obtain, by Remark 2.9 in
[
20
]
, that

V^{d}(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{it\alpha}\dfrac{1}{i\alpha-\lambda_{k}}(i\alpha)^{-d}\Phi_{L}(d\alpha),\quad t\in\mathbb{R}.

4
Gamma mixed fractional Lévy Ornstein - Uhlenbeck process

In this section, first, we will study a process defined by the aggregation of independent fLOUp with random coefficient. Then we study its limit process, which we call Gamma mixed fractional Lévy Ornstein - Uhlenbeck process. Some properties of this limit process are given, namely, we give the characteristic function of the finite dimensional distributions of the process, we determinate its covariance, stationarity and long memory property; and finally, we analyze the asymptotic behavior with respect to the parameter
\alpha
.

4.1
Aggregated fractional Lévy Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process

Consider a sequence of fLOUp with random coefficient
V_{k}^{d}=(V_{k}^{d}(t),t\in\mathbb{R})
,
k\geq 1
given by

V_{k}^{d}(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{t}e^{\lambda_{k}(t-u)}dL^{d}(u),\quad t\in\mathbb{R},\quad k\geq 1,

(16)

where
L^{d}
is a fLp with
d\in(0,1/2)
defined on
(\Omega_{L},{\cal F}_{L},\mathbb{P}_{L})
. The random variables
\lambda_{k}
are assumed independent and identically distributed defined on the probability space
(\Omega_{\lambda},{\cal F}_{\lambda},\mathbb{P}_{\lambda})
, and for
k\geq 1
we assume that
-\lambda_{k}
follows a Gamma distribution with parameters
1-h
and
\alpha
, i.e.
-\lambda_{k}\sim\Gamma(1-h,\alpha)
with
h\in(0,1)
and
\alpha>0
. Furthermore, we also assume that the random variables
(\lambda_{k})_{k\geq 1}
are independent from
L^{d}
.

The aggregated fractional Lévy Ornstein-Uhlenbeck processs
Z_{m}^{d}=(Z_{m}^{d}(t),t\in\mathbb{R})
is defined by

Z_{m}^{d}(t):=\dfrac{1}{m}\sum_{k=1}^{m}V_{k}^{d}(t),

(17)

for
m\geq 1
and
d\in(0,1/2)
.

Lemma 6
.

For all
m\geq 1
, the aggregated fractional Lévy Ornstein-Uhlenbeck processs
Z^{d}_{m}
is stationary and

E_{L}[(Z_{m}^{d}(t))^{2}]=\frac{C_{d}}{m^{2}}2\Gamma(2d)\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{1}{-(\lambda_{k}+\lambda_{j})(-\lambda_{k})^{2d}},

where
C_{d}
is given by (
7
).

Proof:
The similarity follows by Lemma
4
.

From Lemma
3
and the change of variables
\tilde{u}=t-u
and
\tilde{v}=t-v
, we can see

\displaystyle E_{L}[(Z_{m}^{d}(t))^{2}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{C_{d}}{m^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{t}\int_{-\infty}^{t}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}e^{\lambda_{k}(t-u)}e^{\lambda_{j}(t-v)}|u-v|^{2d-1}dvdu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{C_{d}}{m^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{\lambda_{k}u+\lambda_{j}v}|u-v|^{2d-1}dvdu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{C_{d}}{m^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{u}e^{\lambda_{k}u+\lambda_{j}v}(u-v)^{2d-1}dvdu\right.+\left.\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{u}^{\infty}e^{\lambda_{k}u+\lambda_{j}v}(v-u)^{2d-1}dvdu\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{C_{d}}{m^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{(\lambda_{k}+\lambda_{j})u}\int_{0}^{u}e^{-\lambda_{j}v}v^{2d-1}dvdu\right.+\left.\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{(\lambda_{k}+\lambda_{j})u}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{\lambda_{j}v}v^{2d-1}dvdu\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{C_{d}}{m^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{\lambda_{j}v}v^{2d-1}\int_{v}^{\infty}e^{(\lambda_{k}+\lambda_{j})u}dudv\right.+\left.\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{\lambda_{j}v}v^{2d-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{(\lambda_{k}+\lambda_{j})u}dudv\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{C_{d}}{m^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{1}{-(\lambda_{k}+\lambda_{j})}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{\lambda_{k}v}v^{2d-1}dv+\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{\lambda_{j}v}v^{2d-1}dv\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{C_{d}}{m^{2}}\Gamma(2d)\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{1}{-(\lambda_{k}+\lambda_{j})}\left(\dfrac{1}{(-\lambda_{k})^{2d}}+\dfrac{1}{(-\lambda_{j})^{2d}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{C_{d}}{m^{2}}2\Gamma(2d)\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{1}{-(\lambda_{k}+\lambda_{j})(-\lambda_{k})^{2d}}.

∎

4.2
Limit of aggregated fractional Lévy Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process

By (
10
) we can see that
Z_{m}^{d}
can be written as

\displaystyle Z_{m}^{d}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{t}\dfrac{1}{m}\sum_{k=1}^{m}e^{\lambda_{k}(t-u)}dL^{d}(u)

(18)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{t}f_{m}(t-u)dL^{d}(u).

Moreover, by the law of large numbers we get

\dfrac{1}{m}\sum_{k=1}^{m}e^{\lambda_{k}(t-u)}\rightarrow E_{\lambda}\left[e^{\lambda_{1}(t-u)}\right]=\left(\dfrac{\alpha}{\alpha+t-u}\right)^{1-h},

(19)

as
m\rightarrow\infty
, where the convergence is
P_{\lambda}
almost surely.

Due to the previous result, a natural candidate to be the limit of the aggregated fractional Lévy Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process is the process
Z^{d}=(Z^{d}(t),t\in\mathbb{R})
given by

\displaystyle Z^{d}(t)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{t}\left(\dfrac{\alpha}{\alpha+t-u}\right)^{1-h}dL^{d}(u)

(20)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{t}g(t-u)dL^{d}(u),

where

g(t)=\left(\dfrac{\alpha}{\alpha+t}\right)^{1-h}
.

We can see that the process
Z^{d}
belongs to
L^{2}(\Omega_{L})
if
0<h<1/2-d
. Actually, by Lemma
3
and applying consecutively the change of variables
\tilde{u}=t-u
,
\tilde{v}=t-v
,
x=u/\alpha
,
y=v/\alpha
,
r=1/(1+x)
,
s=1/(1+y)
, and
\hat{u}=r/s
, we obtain

\displaystyle E_{L}\left[(Z^{d}(t))^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{d}\int_{-\infty}^{t}\int_{-\infty}^{t}g(t-u)g(t-v)|u-v|^{2d-1}dvdu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{d}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}g(u)g(v)|u-v|^{2d-1}dvdu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{d}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(1+\dfrac{u}{\alpha}\right)^{h-1}\left(1+\dfrac{v}{\alpha}\right)^{h-1}|u-v|^{2d-1}dvdu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{d}\alpha^{2d+1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(1+x\right)^{h-1}\left(1+y\right)^{h-1}|x-y|^{2d-1}dydx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{d}\alpha^{2d+1}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}r^{1-h}s^{1-h}(rs)^{-2}\left|\dfrac{1}{r}-\dfrac{1}{s}\right|^{2d-1}drds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2C_{d}\alpha^{2d+1}\int_{0}^{1}s^{-1-h}\int_{0}^{s}r^{-h-2d}\left(1-\dfrac{r}{s}\right)^{2d-1}drds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2C_{d}\alpha^{2d+1}\int_{0}^{1}s^{-2(h+d)}\int_{0}^{1}\hat{u}^{-h-2d}(1-\hat{u})^{2d-1}d\hat{u}ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{2C_{d}\alpha^{2d+1}}{1-2(h+d)}B(1-h-2d,2d)=C_{\alpha,h,d}.

Clearly, from the last line emerges the condition
0<h<1/2-d
.

Now, we present the main result of this section related to the limit of the aggregated fractional Lévy Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process. This result is analogous to that obtained in Theorem 3 in
[
10
]
for the aggregated fractional Ornstein-Uhlenbeck processes.

Theorem 2
.

Let
Z_{m}^{d}
and
Z^{d}
be defined by (
17
) and (
20
), respectively. Assume that
0<h<1/2-d
. Then
P_{\lambda}
-a.s., for every
t\in\mathbb{R}

Z_{m}^{d}(t)\longrightarrow Z^{d}(t),\qquad\mbox{in}\quad L^{2}(\Omega_{L})

(21)

and for
a,b\in\mathbb{R}

Z_{m}^{d}(t)\longrightarrow Z^{d}(t),\qquad\mbox{weakly \ in}\ \ C[a,b]\ \ \mbox{under}\quad P_{L},

(22)

as
m\rightarrow\infty
.

Proof:
By Lemma
3
, (
18
) and (
20
), we have

\displaystyle E_{L}[(Z^{d}_{m}(t)-Z^{d}(t))^{2}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{L}\left[\left(\int_{-\infty}^{t}\left[\dfrac{1}{m}\sum_{k=1}^{m}e^{\lambda_{k}(t-u)}-\left(\dfrac{\alpha}{\alpha+t-u}\right)^{h}\right]dL^{d}(u)\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{L}\left[\left(\int_{-\infty}^{t}\left[f_{m}(t-u)-g(t-u)\right]dL^{d}(u)\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{d}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}(f_{m}(u)-g(u))(f_{m}(v)-g(v)))|u-v|^{2d-1}dudv.

Now, following the lines of the proof of Theorem 3 (first part) in
[
10
]
and the fact that
0<h<1/2-d
, we can obtain that
P_{\lambda}
-a.s.

\lim\limits_{m\rightarrow\infty}E_{L}[(Z^{d}_{m}(t)-Z^{d}(t))^{2}]=0.

Hence we have the
P_{\lambda}
-a.s. convergence of the sequence
(Z^{d}_{m})_{m\geq 1}
in
L^{2}(\Omega_{L})
, which in turn implies the
P_{\lambda}
-a.s. convergence of the finite dimensional distributions.

In order to prove the weak convergence, we only need to prove the tightness. Due Theorem 12.3 in
[
5
]
, it is sufficient to show that

E_{L}[(Z_{m}^{d}(t)-Z_{m}^{d}(s))^{2}]\leq C(t-s)^{1+\rho}
, for
T>0
fix and
0\leq s<t\leq T
where
\rho>0
and C may depend uppon parameters.

From Lemma
6
and (
18
)

\displaystyle Z_{m}^{d}(t)-Z_{m}^{d}(s)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(d)}}{{=}}

\displaystyle Z_{m}^{d}(t-s)-Z_{m}^{d}(0)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(d)}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{m}\sum_{k=1}^{m}(e^{\lambda_{k}(t-s)}-1)\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{-u\lambda_{k}}dL^{d}(u)+\int_{0}^{t-s}\dfrac{1}{m}\sum_{k=1}^{m}e^{\lambda_{k}(t-s-u)}dL^{d}(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{1}+I_{2}.

Clearly, this implies

E_{L}[(Z_{m}^{d}(t)-Z_{m}^{d}(s))^{2}]\leq 2E_{L}[I_{1}^{2}]+2E_{L}[I_{2}^{2}].

We will estimate every term separately. In fact, by Lemma
3
we have

\displaystyle E_{L}[I_{1}^{2}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{m^{2}}\sum_{k_{1},k_{2}=1}^{m}(e^{\lambda_{k_{1}}(t-s)}-1)(e^{\lambda_{k_{2}}(t-s)}-1)E_{L}\left[\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{-u\lambda_{k_{1}}}dL^{d}(u)\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{-v\lambda_{k_{1}}}dL^{d}(v)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{m^{2}}\sum_{k_{1},k_{2}=1}^{m}(e^{\lambda_{k_{1}}(t-s)}-1)(e^{\lambda_{k_{2}}(t-s)}-1)\int_{-\infty}^{0}\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{-u\lambda_{k_{1}}}e^{-v\lambda_{k_{1}}}|u-v|^{2d-1}dudv.

By similar arguments to those of
[
9
]
, the law of large number and the fact that
0<h<1/2-d

E_{L}[I_{1}^{2}]\leq C_{d,\lambda}(t-s)^{2}.

(23)

With respect to
I_{2}
, by Lemma
3
and making the change of variable
z=u-v
, we get

\displaystyle E_{L}[I_{2}^{2}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{L}\left[\int_{0}^{t-s}\dfrac{1}{m}\sum_{k=1}^{m}e^{\lambda_{k}(t-s-u)}dL^{d}(u)\cdot\int_{0}^{t-s}\dfrac{1}{m}\sum_{k=1}^{m}e^{\lambda_{k}(t-s-v)}dL^{d}(v)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{d}\dfrac{1}{m^{2}}\sum_{k_{1},k_{2}=1}^{m}\int_{0}^{t-s}\int_{0}^{t-s}e^{\lambda_{k_{1}}(t-s-u)}e^{\lambda_{k_{2}}(t-s-v)}|u-v|^{2d-1}dudv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{d}\dfrac{1}{m^{2}}\sum_{k_{1},k_{2}=1}^{m}e^{\lambda_{k_{1}}(t-s)}e^{\lambda_{k_{2}}(t-s)}\int_{0}^{t-s}\int_{0}^{t-s}e^{-\lambda_{k_{1}}u-\lambda_{k_{2}}v}|u-v|^{2d-1}dvdu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2C_{d}\dfrac{1}{m^{2}}\sum_{k_{1},k_{2}=1}^{m}e^{\lambda_{k_{1}}(t-s)}e^{\lambda_{k_{2}}(t-s)}\int_{0}^{t-s}e^{-\lambda_{k_{1}}u}\int_{0}^{u}e^{-\lambda_{k_{2}}v}(u-v)^{2d-1}dvdu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2C_{d}\dfrac{1}{m^{2}}\sum_{k_{1},k_{2}=1}^{m}e^{\lambda_{k_{1}}(t-s)}e^{\lambda_{k_{2}}(t-s)}\int_{0}^{t-s}e^{-u(\lambda_{k_{1}}+\lambda_{k_{2}})}\int_{0}^{u}e^{\lambda_{k_{2}}z}z^{2d-1}dvdu

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2C_{d}\dfrac{1}{m^{2}}\sum_{k_{1},k_{2}=1}^{m}e^{\lambda_{k_{1}}(t-s)}e^{\lambda_{k_{2}}(t-s)}\int_{0}^{t-s}e^{-\lambda_{k_{1}}u}u^{2d}du.

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2C_{d}(t-s)^{1+2d}\dfrac{1}{m}\sum_{k_{2}=1}^{m}e^{\lambda_{k_{2}}(t-s)}.

Then, using the fact that
-\lambda_{k}\sim\Gamma(1-h,\alpha)
with
h\in(0,1)
and
\alpha>0
, and
t>s
. We obtain

E_{L}[I_{2}^{2}]\leq 2C_{d}(t-s)^{1+2d}.

(24)

Finally, inequalities (
23
) and (
24
) imply

E_{L}[(Z_{m}^{d}(t)-Z_{m}^{d}(s))^{2}]\leq C_{T,d,\lambda,\alpha,h}(t-s)^{1+2d}.

Since
d\in(0,1/2)
the result is achieved.
∎

4.3
Properties of
\mathbf{Z^{d}}

Now, we study some properties of the limit process
Z^{d}
.

Proposition 1
.

Let
Z^{d}
be given by (
20
) with
h\in(0,1/2-d)
. Then, the characteristic function of
Z^{d}(t_{1}),Z^{d}(t_{2}),\ldots,Z^{d}(t_{m})
with
t_{1}<t_{2}<\ldots<t_{m}
is given by

E_{L}\left[\exp\left(i\sum_{j=1}^{m}\theta_{j}Z^{d}(t_{j})\right)\right]=\exp\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi\left[\sum_{j=1}^{m}\theta_{j}\tilde{f}_{t_{j},h,\alpha}(s)\right]ds\right),

where

\tilde{f}_{t_{j},h,\alpha}(s)=d\int_{-\infty}^{t_{j}}\left(\dfrac{\alpha}{\alpha+t_{j}-v}\right)^{1-h}(v-s)_{+}^{d-1}dv.

Proof:
The result follows by Lemma
1
in Section 2 (also see Proposition 3.3 in
[
16
]
) and the fact that
Z^{d}
belongs to
L^{2}(\Omega_{L})
for
h\in(0,1/2-d)
.
∎

Proposition 2
.

Let
Z^{d}
be given by (
20
) with
h\in(0,1/2-d)
. Then,
Z^{d}
is a stationary process with long memory.

Proof:
By (
20
), the stationarity of the increments of
L^{d}
and making the change of variable
\tilde{u}=u-b
, we can get for every
b>0

\displaystyle Z^{d}(t+b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{t+b}\left(\dfrac{\alpha}{\alpha+t+b-u}\right)^{1-h}dL^{d}(u)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(d)}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{t}\left(\dfrac{\alpha}{\alpha+t-u}\right)^{1-h}dL^{d}(u)=Z^{d}(t).

With respect to the long memory property we will study the non summability of the covariance. Since
Z^{d}
is a stionary process, we have

\displaystyle E_{L}[Z^{d}(a)Z^{d}(t+a)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{L}[Z^{d}(0)Z^{d}(t)].

Then, Lemma
3
implies

\displaystyle E_{L}[Z^{d}(0)Z^{d}(t)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{d}\int_{-\infty}^{0}\int_{-\infty}^{t}\left(\dfrac{\alpha}{\alpha+t-u}\right)^{1-h}\left(\dfrac{\alpha}{\alpha-v}\right)^{1-h}|u-v|^{2d-1}dudv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{d}\alpha^{2-2h}\int_{-\infty}^{0}\int_{-\infty}^{t}\left(\alpha+t-u\right)^{h-1}\left(\alpha-v\right)^{h-1}|u-v|^{2d-1}dudv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{d}\alpha^{2-2h}t^{2h+2d-1}\int_{-\infty}^{0}\int_{-\infty}^{1}\left(\dfrac{\alpha}{t}+1-y\right)^{h-1}\left(\dfrac{\alpha}{t}-x\right)^{h-1}|x-y|^{2d-1}dxdy

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle C_{d}\alpha^{2-2h}t^{2h+2d-1}\int_{-\infty}^{0}\int_{-\infty}^{1}\left(1-y\right)^{h-1}\left(-x\right)^{h-1}|x-y|^{2d-1}dxdy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{d,\alpha,h}t^{2h+2d-1}.

Then, the long memory property is obtain by just noticing that
h+d>0
.
∎

Remark 2
.

Even if
d=0
, the long memory property is satisfied if
h>0
.

Remark 3
.

We can see that the process
Z^{d}
has the following property

\dfrac{\rho(t)}{\rho(0)}\sim C_{\alpha,h,d}t^{2h+2d-1},\mbox{with}\quad\rho(t)=E_{L}[Z^{d}(0)Z^{d}(t)],

i.e., the process is almost self-similar (see
[
14
]
page 13 for details).

With respect to the behavior of the process
Z^{d}
with respect to the parameter
\alpha
we have the following results

Proposition 3
.

Let
t\geq 0
and
d\in(0,1/2)
, then the random variable

Z^{d}(t)-Z^{d}(0)

converges en
L^{2}(\Omega_{L})
as
\alpha\rightarrow\infty
to the random variable
L^{d}(t)
.

Proof:
By (
20
) we obtain

\displaystyle Z^{d}(t)-Z^{d}(0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{t}\left(\dfrac{\alpha}{\alpha+t-u}\right)^{1-h}dL^{d}(u)-\int_{-\infty}^{0}\left(\dfrac{\alpha}{\alpha-u}\right)^{1-h}dL^{d}(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left[\left(\dfrac{\alpha}{\alpha+t-u}\right)^{1-h}1_{(-\infty,t)}(u)-\left(\dfrac{\alpha}{\alpha-u}\right)^{1-h}1_{(-\infty,0)}(u)\right]dL^{d}(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}f_{t,d}(u)dL^{d}(u).

Clearly,
f_{t,d}
converges to
1_{(0,t)}
as
\alpha\rightarrow\infty
. Therefore, our candidate as a limit will be

L^{d}(t)
. In fact, by Lemma
3

\displaystyle E_{L}[(Z^{d}(t)-Z^{d}(0)-L^{d}(t))^{2}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{d}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left[\left(\dfrac{\alpha}{\alpha+t-u}\right)^{1-h}1_{(-\infty,t)}(u)-\left(\dfrac{\alpha}{\alpha-u}\right)^{1-h}1_{(-\infty,0)}(u)-1_{(0,t)}(u)\right]

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\left[\left(\dfrac{\alpha}{\alpha+t-v}\right)^{1-h}1_{(-\infty,t)}(v)-\left(\dfrac{\alpha}{\alpha-v}\right)^{1-h}1_{(-\infty,0)}(v)-1_{(0,t)}(v)\right]|u-v|^{2d-1}dudv.

Finally, the result is obtained by means of the dominated convergence theorem. ∎

If
\alpha\rightarrow 0
, then we get the following result.

Proposition 4
.

Let
t\geq 0
and let us define
\tilde{Z}^{d}(t)
by

\tilde{Z}^{d}(t)=\alpha^{h-1}\int_{0}^{t}Z^{d}(s)ds,

then, as
\alpha\rightarrow 0
the random variable
\tilde{Z}^{d}(d)
converges in
L^{2}(\Omega_{L})
to the random variable
Y^{d}
given by

Y^{d}(t):=\dfrac{1}{h}\int_{\mathbb{R}}[(t-u)^{h}_{+}-(-u)_{+}^{h}]dL^{d}(u)

(25)

Proof:
By (
20
), we get

\displaystyle\tilde{Z}^{d}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha^{h-1}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{-\infty}^{s}\left(\dfrac{\alpha}{\alpha+s-u}\right)^{1-h}dL^{d}(u)ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{-\infty}^{s}\left(\alpha+s-u\right)^{h-1}dL^{d}(u)ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{t}\int_{v\vee 0}^{t}\left(\alpha+s-v\right)^{h-1}dsdL^{d}(v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{h}\int_{-\infty}^{t}\left[\left(\alpha+t-v\right)^{h}-\left(\alpha+(v\vee 0)-v\right)^{h}\right]dL^{d}(v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{h}\int_{-\infty}^{t}r_{\alpha,t,h}(v)dL^{d}(v).

We can see that
r_{\alpha,t,h}(v)
converges to
(t-v)^{h}_{+}-(-v)_{+}^{h}
for every
v
as
\alpha\rightarrow 0
. Therefore, by similar arguments to the previous proposition the result is obtained. ∎

Proposition 5
.

Let
Y^{d}=(Y^{d}(t))_{t\geq 0}
with
Y^{d}(t)
be given by (
25
), then
Y^{d}
is a stationary process with

E_{L}[(Y^{d}(t))^{2}]=t^{2h+2d+1}E_{L}[(Y^{d}(1))^{2}].

Proof:
By (
25
) and taking
b>0

\displaystyle Y^{d}(t+b)-Y^{d}(b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{h}\int_{\mathbb{R}}[(t+b-u)^{h}_{+}-(b-u)^{h}_{+}]dL^{d}(u)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(d)}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{h}\int_{\mathbb{R}}[(t-v)^{h}_{+}-(-v)^{h}_{+}]dL^{d}(v)=Y^{d}(t),

here we have used the change of variable
v=u-b
and the fact that
L^{d}
is a stationary increment process. With respect to the second part of the statement, we have that

\displaystyle E_{L}[(Y^{d}(t))^{2}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{h^{2}}C_{d}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}((t-u)^{h}_{+}-(-u)_{+}^{h})((t-v)^{h}_{+}-(-v)_{+}^{h})|u-v|^{2d-1}dvdu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t^{2h+2d+1}\dfrac{1}{h^{2}}C_{d}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}((1-u)^{h}_{+}-(-u)_{+}^{h})((1-v)^{h}_{+}-(-v)_{+}^{h})|u-v|^{2d-1}dvdu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t^{2h+2d+1}E_{L}[(Y^{d}(1))^{2}].

∎

Remark 4
.

Let us note that we can write

\tilde{Y}^{d}(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}m_{t}(u)dL^{d}(u),

where
m_{t}(u)=\dfrac{1}{h}[(t-u)^{h}_{+}-(-u)_{+}^{h}]
. Then, Proposition
5
and Theorem 3.1 in
[
16
]
imply

Y^{d}(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}m_{t}(u)dL^{d}(u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}(I^{g}_{-m})(u)dL(s),

where (see Section 3 in the same reference for details)

(I^{g}_{-m})(u):=\int_{u}^{\infty}m_{t}(v)g^{\prime}(v-u)dv=\int_{\mathbb{R}}m_{t}(v)g^{\prime}(v-u)dv

and
g(u)=(u)^{d}_{+}
.

This implies that
Y^{d}
can be seen as a type of Generalized fractional Lévy process (see
[
16
]
for details).

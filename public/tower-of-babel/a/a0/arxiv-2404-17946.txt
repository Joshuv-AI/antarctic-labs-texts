Geometric Characteristics and Stable Guarantees in Phase Retrieval and Matrix Restoration This work was supported in part by the NSFC under grant numbers U21A20426 and 12071426
Gao Huang, Song Li

Abstract
In this paper, we first propose a unified framework for analyzing the stability of phaseless operators for both amplitude and intensity measurement on an arbitrary vector set, thereby characterizing the stable performance of phase retrieval via the empirical minimization method. We introduce the isomorphic random embedding of lifting operators to characterize the unified analysis of arbitrary geometric sets. Similarly, we investigate the stable performance of matrix restoration through the robust injectivity of a rank-one measurement operator on an arbitrary matrix set. The core of our analysis is to establish unified empirical chaos processes characterization for various geometric sets. We utilize Talagrand’s γ α \gamma_{\alpha} -functionals as geometric parameters to characterize the relationship between geometric constraints and the number of measurements necessary for ensuring stability or robust injectivity. We also construct adversarial noise to demonstrate the sharpness of the recovery bounds derived through the empirical minimization method in the both scenarios. Keywords: Phaseless Operators; Empirical Minimization; Talagrand’s Functionals; Chaos Processes; Adversarial Noise. Mathematics Subject Classification 2010: 94A12, 68Q87, 65C50, 60G12.

Introduction

Phase retrieval refers to the problem of reconstructing an unknown signal
\boldsymbol{x}_{0}\in\mathbb{F}^{n}
with
\mathbb{F}\in\{\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}\}
from
m
phaseless measurements
of the form

b_{k}=\left\lvert\langle\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{k},\boldsymbol{x}_{0}\rangle\right\lvert^{\ell},\quad k=1,\cdots,m,\ \ell=1,2.

(1)

Here, the sample may be in two forms: for
\ell=1
, the amplitude measurement
\left\lvert\langle\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{k},\boldsymbol{x}_{0}\rangle\right\lvert
, and for
\ell=2
, the intensity measurement
\left\lvert\langle\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{k},\boldsymbol{x}_{0}\rangle\right\lvert^{2}
.
The collection of measurement vectors
\Phi=\{\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{m}
in
V=\mathbb{F}^{n}
is known.
This phaseless reconstruction problem has gained significant attention in the past few decades in various fields due to its wide range of applications, including X-ray crystallography, astronomy, optics, and diffraction imaging; see e.g.,
[
10
,
31
]
.

As far as applications are concerned, the stable performance of reconstruction is perhaps the most important consideration.
Thus, we investigate the phaseless measurements with noise
\boldsymbol{z}
:

\boldsymbol{b}=\mathcal{A}_{\Phi}^{\ell}\left(\boldsymbol{x}_{0}\right)+\boldsymbol{z},

(2)

where
\mathcal{A}_{\Phi}^{\ell}\left(\ell=1,2\right)
are phaseless operators

\mathcal{A}_{\Phi}^{\ell}:\mathbb{F}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
defined by

\mathcal{A}_{\Phi}^{\ell}\left(\boldsymbol{x}\right)=\left(\begin{matrix}\left\lvert\langle\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{1},\boldsymbol{x}\rangle\right\lvert^{\ell}\\
\vdots\\
\left\lvert\langle\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{m},\boldsymbol{x}\rangle\right\lvert^{\ell}\end{matrix}\right).

(3)

The noise
\boldsymbol{z}
can be arbitrary noise, including deterministic and worst-case adversarial noise.
One of the main goals of this paper is to establish a unified framework for amplitude and intensity measurement that obtains stable recovery conditions while disregarding specific recovery approaches and establishes recovery assurances within the framework of the empirical minimization method.
These two motivations point to our focus on the stability of the phaseless operators
\mathcal{A}_{\Phi}^{\ell}
.

The stability of
\mathcal{A}_{\Phi}^{\ell}
can infer stable uniqueness, signifying the identification of conditions that lead to the determination of a unique solution.
It has been demonstrated that if there is no additional information about
\boldsymbol{x}_{0}
, a unique recovery requires at least
m=\mathcal{O}\left(n\right)
measurements
[
1
,
3
,
22
]
.
However, prior assumptions on
\boldsymbol{x}_{0}
, such as sparse, can greatly reduce the number of measurements
[
22
]
.
Thus, we assume that
\boldsymbol{x}_{0}\in\mathcal{T}
to capture the geometric structure of the signal.
In many cases of interest,
\mathcal{T}
behaves as if it is a low-dimensional set; thus,
m
is significantly smaller than the dimension
n
.
Now we provide a definition of the stability of
\mathcal{A}_{\Phi}^{\ell}
on
\mathcal{T}
, which is inspired by
[
22
,
3
,
5
]
.

Definition 1

(Stability of
\mathcal{A}_{\Phi}^{\ell}
)
.

For
q\geq 1,\ell=1,2
,
\mathcal{A}_{\Phi}^{\ell}:\mathbb{F}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
is
C
-stable on a set
\mathcal{T}
, if for every
\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathcal{T}
,

\left\lVert\mathcal{A}_{\Phi}^{\ell}\left(\boldsymbol{u}\right)-\mathcal{A}_{\Phi}^{\ell}\left(\boldsymbol{v}\right)\right\lVert_{q}\geq Cd_{\ell}\left(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v}\right).

(4)

We use two different metrics here.
For
\ell=1
,
d_{1}\left(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v}\right)=\min\limits_{\left\lvert c\right\lvert=1}\left\lVert\boldsymbol{u}-c\cdot\boldsymbol{v}\right\lVert_{2}
where
c=\left\{\pm 1\right\}
if
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R}
or
c=\left\{e^{i\theta}:\theta\in\left[0,2\pi\right)\right\}
if
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{C}
;
for
\ell=2
,
d_{2}\left(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v}\right)=\left\lVert\boldsymbol{u}\boldsymbol{u}^{*}-\boldsymbol{v}\boldsymbol{v}^{*}\right\lVert_{F}
.
Our motivation for considering
q\geq 1
is twofold.
First, it ensures theoretical completeness, as various
q
have been extensively examined in previous works through empirical
\ell_{q}
risk minimization;
for instance,
q=1

[
11
,
22
,
19
]
and
q=2

[
3
,
12
,
16
,
56
]
.
Second, since different
q
exhibit varying stable performance (see, e.g.,
[
11
,
12
]
), we are motivated to explore the optimal robustness of these models to inform model selection in applications.

As mentioned above, the most natural way to estimate
\boldsymbol{x}_{0}
is via an empirical
\ell_{q}\left(q\geq 1\right)
risk minimization such that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\text{minimize}&\quad\left\lVert\mathcal{A}_{\Phi}^{\ell}\left(\boldsymbol{x}\right)-\boldsymbol{b}\right\lVert_{q}\\
\text{subject to}&\quad\ \boldsymbol{x}\in\mathcal{T}.\\
\end{array}

(5)

We assume that
\boldsymbol{x}_{0}\in\mathcal{T}
and set the solutions of (
5
) as
\boldsymbol{x}^{\ell}_{\star}
.
All in all, we are interested in the relationship between the number of measurements
m
that ensure the stability of
\mathcal{A}_{\Phi}^{\ell}
and the intrinsic geometric features of
\mathcal{T}
, as well as the stable performance of (
5
) to antagonize noise
\boldsymbol{z}
through empirical
\ell_{q}
minimization method utilising various
q
.

By lifting intensity measurement, phaseless reconstruction
can be cast as a structured matrix restoration problem
[
10
]
,
which has wide applications in face recognition, recommender systems, linear system identification and control; see, e.g.,
[
45
]
.
We then focus on the matrix restoration from rank-one measurements, given the linear operator
\boldsymbol{A}_{\Phi}:\mathbb{F}^{n\times n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
and a corrupted vector of measurements as

\boldsymbol{b}=\boldsymbol{A}_{\Phi}\left(\boldsymbol{X}_{0}\right)+\boldsymbol{z}:=\left(\begin{matrix}\langle\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{1}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{1}^{*},\boldsymbol{X}_{0}\rangle\\
\vdots\\
\langle\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{m}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{m}^{*},\boldsymbol{X}_{0}\rangle\\
\end{matrix}\right)+\boldsymbol{z}.

(6)

We wish to recovery
\boldsymbol{X}_{0}\in\mathcal{S}^{n}_{\mathbb{F}}
(
\mathcal{S}^{n}_{\mathbb{R}}
denotes the vector space of all symmetric matrices in
\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
and
\mathcal{S}^{n}_{\mathbb{C}}
denotes the vector space of all Hermitian matrices in
\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
) with a specific structure; for instance, low rank or jointly sparse and low rank.
Here, measurement matrices are
\Phi=\{\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{k}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{k}^{*}\}_{k=1}^{m}
and
\boldsymbol{z}
is also assumed to be arbitrary.
The advantage of rank-one measurement systems over other measurement systems
lies in its efficient storage structure; see e.g.,
[
9
,
17
]
.

Restorating structured matrix
\boldsymbol{X}_{0}
from measurements (
6
)
via empirical
\ell_{q}\left(q\geq 1\right)
risk minimization:

\begin{array}[]{ll}\text{minimize}&\quad\left\lVert\boldsymbol{A}_{\Phi}\left(\boldsymbol{X}\right)-\boldsymbol{b}\right\lVert_{q}\\
\text{subject to}&\ \quad\boldsymbol{X}\in\mathcal{M},\end{array}

(7)

is favored by a lot of algorithms; for instance,
q=1

[
33
,
14
]
and
q=2

[
21
,
43
]
.
We assume
\boldsymbol{X}_{0}\in\mathcal{M}
to capture the geometric structure of the matrix.
Motivation is similar to deducing the stability of
\mathcal{A}_{\Phi}^{\ell}
, the stable performance of (
7
) requires investigating the robust injectivity of the rank-one measurement operator
\boldsymbol{A}_{\Phi}
on symmetric matrix set
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}:=\mathcal{M}-\mathcal{M}
, which is defined as follows.

Definition 2

(Robust Injectivity of
\boldsymbol{A}_{\Phi}
)
.

For
q\geq 1
,
\boldsymbol{A}_{\Phi}:\mathbb{F}^{n\times n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
is
C
-robust injective on a set
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}=\mathcal{M}-\mathcal{M}
if for all
\boldsymbol{X}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}
,

\left\lVert\boldsymbol{A}_{\Phi}\left(\boldsymbol{X}\right)\right\lVert_{q}\geq C\left\lVert\boldsymbol{X}\right\lVert_{F}.

(8)

We also take into account the measurement number that can ensure the robust injectivity of
\boldsymbol{A}_{\Phi}
based on the intrinsic geometric features of
\mathcal{M}
and the stable performance of (
7
) via empirical
\ell_{q}
minimization method with various
q
.

In summary, our goal is to address the following two specific issues.

1.

Could we understand the values of the required measurement number to ensure the stability of
\mathcal{A}_{\Phi}^{\ell}
or the robust injectivity of
\boldsymbol{A}_{\Phi}
as a function of the
intrinsic geometry of
\mathcal{T}
or
\mathcal{M}
?

2.

How to evaluate the stable performance of (
5
) and (
7
) using the empirical
\ell_{q}
minimization approach with various
q
, regardless of the algorithms used?

Contributions

Our main contributions are summarized as follows.

•

We provide a unified framework applicable to arbitrary geometric sets
\mathcal{T}
and
\mathcal{M}
.
We utilize Talagrand’s
\gamma_{\alpha}
-functionals as geometric parameters to quantify the required number of measurements to guarantee the stability of
\mathcal{A}_{\Phi}^{\ell}
and the robust injectivity of

We show a connection between the phaseless operators, matrix restoration with rank-one measurements and Talagrand’s

We establish the recovery bounds

.
Our results substantially enhance the existing upper bound given in
[

]

Preliminaries

2.1
Talagrand’s Functionals

The following definition is Talagrand’s
\gamma_{\alpha}
-functionals
[
49
, Definition 2.7.3]
and forms the core of the geometric characteristics of this paper.

Definition 3
.

For a metric space
\left(\mathcal{T},d\right)
, an admissible sequence of
\mathcal{T}
is an increasing sequence
\left(\mathcal{A}_{n}\right)_{n\geq 0}
of partitions of
\mathcal{T}
such that for every
n\geq 1,\left\lvert\mathcal{A}_{n}\right\lvert\leq 2^{2^{n}}
and
\left\lvert\mathcal{A}_{0}\right\lvert=1
.
We denote by
A_{n}\left(t\right)
the unique element of
\mathcal{A}_{n}
that contains
t
.
For
\alpha\geq 1
, define the
\gamma_{\alpha}
-functionals by

\gamma_{\alpha}\left(\mathcal{T},d\right)=\inf_{\mathcal{T}}\sup_{t\in\mathcal{T}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}2^{n/\alpha}\text{diam}\left(A_{n}\left(t\right)\right),

where the infimum is taken with respect to all admissible sequences of
\mathcal{T}
and
\text{diam}\left(A_{n}\left(t\right)\right)
denotes the diameter of
A_{n}\left(t\right)
for
d
.
The ”increasing sequence” means that every element of
\mathcal{A}_{n+1}
is contained in an element of
\mathcal{A}_{n}
.

We require some properties of
\gamma_{\alpha}
-functionals. The first is that they can be bounded in terms of covering number
\mathcal{N}\left(\mathcal{T},d,u\right)
by the well-known Dudley integral,

\gamma_{\alpha}\left(\mathcal{T},d\right)\leq C\int_{0}^{\text{diam}\left(\mathcal{T}\right)}\left(\log\mathcal{N}\left(\mathcal{T},d,u\right)\right)^{1/\alpha}du.

(9)

This type of entropy integral was introduced by Dudley
[
20
]
to bound the suprema of gaussian process.
In addition, Sudakov’s minoration inequality
[
49
, Exercise 2.7.8]
provides a lower bound for
\gamma_{2}\left(\mathcal{T},d\right)
,

\displaystyle\gamma_{2}\left(\mathcal{T},d\right)\geq cu\sqrt{\log\mathcal{N}\left(\mathcal{T},d,u\right)}.

(10)

The following proposition presents the subadditivity of
\gamma_{\alpha}
-functionals in vector space, its proof can be found in
[
41
, Lemma 2.1]
.

Proposition 1

(Subadditivity of
\gamma_{\alpha}
-Functionals)
.

Let
\left(\mathcal{T},d\right)
be an arbitrary vector space.
Suppose
\mathcal{T}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{2}\subset\mathcal{T}
.
Then

\displaystyle\gamma_{\alpha}\left(\mathcal{T}_{1}+\mathcal{T}_{2},d\right)\lesssim\gamma_{\alpha}\left(\mathcal{T}_{1},d\right)+\gamma_{\alpha}\left(\mathcal{T}_{2},d\right).

2.2
Subgaussian Measurements

Recall that for
s\geq 1
, the
\psi_{s}
-norm of a random variable
X
is defined as

\displaystyle\left\lVert X\right\lVert_{\psi_{s}}:=\inf\{t>0:\mathbb{E}\exp\left(\left\lvert X\right\lvert^{s}/t^{s}\right)\leq 2\}.

(11)

In particular, random variable
X
is called
K
-subgaussian if
\left\lVert X\right\lVert_{\psi_{2}}\leq K
.

Throughout this paper,

\{\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{m}
are given as independent copies of a random vector
\boldsymbol{\varphi}
that satisfies some properties, as follows.

Definition 4
.

We define a random vector
\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in\mathbb{F}^{n}
as a suitable
\left(K,\beta\right)
-subgaussian vector if its entries
\varphi_{i}
are i.i.d.,mean-zero, variance 1,
K
-subgaussian, and satisfy the following conditions:

\mathrm{(a)}

If
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R}
,
\mathbb{E}\left\lvert\varphi_{i}\right\lvert^{4}\geq 1+\beta
for some
\beta>0
;

\mathrm{(b)}

If
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{C}
,
\mathbb{E}\varphi_{i}^{2}=0
, and
\mathbb{E}\left\lvert\varphi_{i}\right\lvert^{4}\geq 1+\beta
for some
\beta>0
.

Then
\boldsymbol{\varphi}
is an istropic random vector on
\mathbb{F}^{n}
: for every
\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{F}^{n},\mathbb{E}\left\lvert\langle\boldsymbol{\varphi},\boldsymbol{x}\rangle\right\lvert^{2}=\left\lVert\boldsymbol{x}\right\lVert_{2}^{2}

and it is standard to verify that
\boldsymbol{\varphi}
is a
K
-subgaussian random vector as well (see, e.g.,
[
51
]
).
The assumption
\mathbb{E}\left\lvert\varphi_{i}\right\lvert^{4}>1
is to avoid some ambiguities when
\varphi_{i}
is a Rademacher variable.
Note that in this scenario we have
\mathbb{E}\left\lvert\varphi_{i}\right\lvert^{4}=\mathbb{E}\left\lvert\varphi_{i}\right\lvert^{2}=1
, and it is impossible to distinguish between
\boldsymbol{e}_{1}
and
\boldsymbol{e}_{2}
in phase retrieval; see e.g.,
[
36
,
37
]
.
Besides, when
\mathbb{E}\left\lvert\varphi^{2}_{i}\right\lvert=1
, it is impossible to distinguish
\boldsymbol{x}_{0}
from its complex-conjugate vector
\overline{\boldsymbol{x}}_{0}
;
see e.g., Remark 1 in
[
37
]
.
Thus for simplify, we assume
\mathbb{E}\varphi_{i}^{2}=0
in the case of
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{C}
.
As an example, complex Gaussian variable
\varphi_{i}\sim 1/\sqrt{2}\cdot\mathcal{N}\left(0,1\right)+i/\sqrt{2}\cdot\mathcal{N}\left(0,1\right)
satisfies this condition.

3
Main Results

3.1
Stability for Phase Retrieval

We first present the stability results of
\mathcal{A}_{\Phi}^{\ell}
for both amplitude and intensity operators on an arbitrary geometric set
\mathcal{T}
.
The outcome is the stable performance of phase retrieval within the framework of empirical
\ell_{q}
risk minimization.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\ell=1,2
and
q\geq 1
. Suppose that
\Phi=\{\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{m}
are i.i.d. suitable
\left(K,\beta\right)
-subgaussian random vectors and the measurement number satisfies

\displaystyle m\geq L_{\ell}\cdot\gamma_{2}^{2}\left(\text{cone}\left(\mathcal{T}\right)\cap\mathbb{S}_{\ell_{2}}\right).

(12)

Then with probability exceeding
1-\mathcal{O}\left(e^{-c_{\ell}m}\right)
, for all
\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathcal{T}
,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathcal{A}_{\Phi}^{\ell}\left(\boldsymbol{u}\right)-\mathcal{A}_{\Phi}^{\ell}\left(\boldsymbol{v}\right)\right\lVert_{q}\geq C_{\ell}m^{1/q}d_{\ell}\left(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v}\right).

(13)

Besides,
the solutions
\boldsymbol{x}_{\star}^{\ell}
to (
5
) satisfy

\displaystyle d_{\ell}\left(\boldsymbol{x}_{\star}^{\ell},\boldsymbol{x}_{0}\right)\leq\frac{2}{C_{\ell}}\frac{\left\lVert\boldsymbol{z}\right\lVert_{q}}{m^{1/q}}.

(14)

Here,
L_{1},L_{2},C_{1},C_{2},c_{1}
and
c_{2}
are positive constants dependent only on
K
and
\beta
.

Remark 1
.

We highlight the distinctions and improvements between the aforementioned theorem and other related works.

•

It was demonstrated by Eldar and Mendelson in
[
22
]
that
\mathcal{A}_{\Phi}^{2}
is stable in the real case with respect to
\ell_{1}
-norm, utilizing Gaussian mean width as geometric parameter.

4.1

Our stability analysis of phaseless operators
\mathcal{A}_{\Phi}^{\ell}

\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\Phi}

\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\Phi}\left(\boldsymbol{X}\right)=\frac{1}{m}\left(\begin{matrix}\left\lvert\langle\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{1}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{1}^{*},\boldsymbol{X}\rangle\right\lvert^{p}\\
\vdots\\
\left\lvert\langle\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{m}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{m}^{*},\boldsymbol{X}\rangle\right\lvert^{p}\end{matrix}\right),\quad\frac{1}{2}\leq p\leq 1.

(22)

Our motivation stems from the observation that the intensity operator
\mathcal{A}_{\Phi}^{2}
can be transformed into
\mathcal{B}^{1}_{\Phi}
by lifting; see, e.g.,
[
10
]

\mathcal{A}_{\Phi}^{1}

We identify such a correspondence to induce us to investigate the behavior of
\mathcal{B}^{1/2}_{\Phi}

which also drives our pursuit of a unified characterization.
The following theorem characterizes the isomorphic random embedding properties of
\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\Phi}
.

Theorem 5
.

Let
\mathcal{T}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{2}\subset\mathbb{F}^{n}
and
p\in\left[1/2,1\right]
.
Suppose that
\Phi=\{\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{m}
are i.i.d. suitable
\left(K,\beta\right)
-subgaussian random vectors and the measurement number satisfies

\displaystyle m\geq L\max\{\gamma_{2}^{2}\left(\text{cone}\left(\mathcal{T}_{1}\right)\cap\mathbb{S}_{\ell_{2}},\ell_{2}\right),\gamma^{2}_{2}\left(\text{cone}\left(\mathcal{T}_{2}\right)\cap\mathbb{S}_{\ell_{2}},\ell_{2}\right)\}.

Then with probability exceeding
1-\mathcal{O}\left(e^{-cm}\right)
, for all
\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathcal{T}_{1},\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathcal{T}_{2}
, operators
\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\Phi}
satisfy

\displaystyle C_{1}\left\lVert\boldsymbol{u}\boldsymbol{u}^{*}-\boldsymbol{v}\boldsymbol{v}^{*}\right\lVert_{F}^{p}\leq\left\lVert\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\Phi}\left(\boldsymbol{u}\boldsymbol{u}^{*}-\boldsymbol{v}\boldsymbol{v}^{*}\right)\right\lVert_{1}\leq C_{2}\left\lVert\boldsymbol{u}\boldsymbol{u}^{*}-\boldsymbol{v}\boldsymbol{v}^{*}\right\lVert_{F}^{p}.

(23)

Here,
L,c,C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are positive absolute constants dependent only on
K,\beta
, and
p
.

We provide the following corollary that will be used to prove the stability of
\mathcal{A}_{\Phi}^{\ell}
.

Corollary 3
.

Suppose

m\geq L\gamma_{2}^{2}\left(\text{cone}\left(\mathcal{T}\right)\cap\mathbb{S}_{\ell_{2}},\ell_{2}\right)
,
then with probability exceeding
1-\mathcal{O}\left(e^{-cm}\right)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left\lvert\langle\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{k},\boldsymbol{u}\rangle\right\lvert\leq C_{3}\left\lVert\boldsymbol{u}\right\lVert_{2},\ \text{for all}\ \boldsymbol{u}\in\mathcal{T};

(24a)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left\lvert\langle\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{k}\boldsymbol{\varphi}^{*}_{k},\boldsymbol{u}\boldsymbol{u}^{*}-\boldsymbol{v}\boldsymbol{v}^{*}\rangle\right\lvert^{1/2}\geq C_{4}d_{2}^{1/2}\left(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v}\right),\ \text{for all}\ \boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathcal{T};

(24b)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left\lvert\langle\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{k}\boldsymbol{\varphi}^{*}_{k},\boldsymbol{u}\boldsymbol{u}^{*}-\boldsymbol{v}\boldsymbol{v}^{*}\rangle\right\lvert\geq C_{5}d_{2}\left(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v}\right),\ \text{for all}\ \boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathcal{T}.

(24c)

Here,
L,c
and

are positive absolute constants depend only on
K,\beta
.

Proof.

and define
\mathcal{T}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{2}
accordingly in Theorem
5
.
For the first inequality, we set

and
\mathcal{T}_{2}=\varnothing

For the second, we set

5.1
Suprema of Chaos Processes

Let
\boldsymbol{\varphi}
be a random vector such that its entries
\varphi_{i}
are i.i.d, mean-zero, variance 1, and
K
-subgaussian.
We set
\mathcal{M}
be a matrix set,
we first investigate the upper bound for the quantity

\displaystyle\begin{split}S\left(\mathcal{M}\right)&:=\mathbb{E}\sup_{\boldsymbol{X}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}\langle\boldsymbol{\varphi}\boldsymbol{\varphi}^{*}-\mathbb{E}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\boldsymbol{\varphi}^{*},\boldsymbol{X}\rangle\\
\end{split}

where
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}=\mathcal{M}-\mathcal{M}
satisfies symmetry property
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}=-\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}
.
Besides, let
\{\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{m}
be independent copies of
\boldsymbol{\varphi}
, then we consider the empirical form for
S\left(\mathcal{M}\right)
,

\displaystyle\overline{S\left(\mathcal{M}\right)}:=\mathbb{E}\sup_{\boldsymbol{X}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}\langle\sum_{k=1}^{m}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{k}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{k}^{*}-m\mathbb{E}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\boldsymbol{\varphi}^{*},\boldsymbol{X}\rangle.

Finally, we study the Rademacher-type empirical chaos process,

\displaystyle\widetilde{S\left(\mathcal{M}\right)}:=\mathbb{E}\sup_{\boldsymbol{X}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}\langle\sum_{k=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{k}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{k}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{k}^{*},\boldsymbol{X}\rangle,

where
\{\varepsilon_{k}\}_{k=1}^{m}
is a Rademacher sequence independent of everything else.
We would like to understand the values of
S\left(\mathcal{M}\right)
,
\overline{S\left(\mathcal{M}\right)}
and
\widetilde{S\left(\mathcal{M}\right)}
as function of the geometry of
\mathcal{M}
.

We first establish upper bounds for the first two.
The proof is provided in Appendix
C
, employing Talagrand’s remarkable chaining method.
While our arguments follow the main steps in
[
49
, Theorem 15.1.4]
, they only differ by considering subgaussian random vectors and extending the analysis to the complex case.

Lemma 1
.

For a matrix set
\mathcal{M}\subset\mathcal{S}^{n}_{F}
, we have

\displaystyle S\left(\mathcal{M}\right)\lesssim K^{2}\cdot\gamma_{2}\left(\mathcal{M},\left\lVert\cdot\right\lVert_{F}\right)+K^{2}\cdot\gamma_{1}\left(\mathcal{M},\left\lVert\cdot\right\lVert_{op}\right)

(28)

\displaystyle\overline{S\left(\mathcal{M}\right)}\lesssim\sqrt{m}K^{2}\cdot\gamma_{2}\left(\mathcal{M},\left\lVert\cdot\right\lVert_{F}\right)+K^{2}\cdot\gamma_{1}\left(\mathcal{M},\left\lVert\cdot\right\lVert_{op}\right).

(29)

Then we use the above lemma to bound the Rademacher-type empirical chaos process.

Theorem 6

(Rademacher-type Empirical Chaos Process)
.

For a matrix set
\mathcal{M}\subset\mathcal{S}^{n}_{F}
, we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{S\left(\mathcal{M}\right)}\lesssim\sqrt{m}K^{2}\cdot\gamma_{2}\left(\mathcal{M},\left\lVert\cdot\right\lVert_{F}\right)+K^{2}\cdot\gamma_{1}\left(\mathcal{M},\left\lVert\cdot\right\lVert_{op}\right)+\sqrt{m}\sup_{\boldsymbol{X}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}\text{Tr}\left(\boldsymbol{X}\right).

(30)

Proof.

We have that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\widetilde{S\left(\mathcal{M}\right)}&\leq\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}\sup_{\boldsymbol{X}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}\langle\sum_{k=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{k}\left(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{k}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{k}^{*}-\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{k}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{k}^{*}\right),\boldsymbol{X}\rangle\\
&\ +\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}\sup_{\boldsymbol{X}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}\langle\sum_{k=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{k}\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{k}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{k}^{*},\boldsymbol{X}\rangle\\
&\leq 2\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}\sup_{\boldsymbol{X}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}\langle\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{k}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{k}^{*}-\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{k}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{k}^{*}\right),\boldsymbol{X}\rangle+\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\sup_{\boldsymbol{X}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}\langle\sum_{k=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{k}\boldsymbol{I}_{n},\boldsymbol{X}\rangle\\
\end{split}

In the second inequality we have used Giné–Zinn symmetrization principle
[
51
, Lemma 6.4.2]
and
\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{k}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{k}^{*}=\boldsymbol{I}_{n}
. We pause to bound the second term on the last line,
we have that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\sup_{\boldsymbol{X}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}\langle\sum_{k=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{k}\boldsymbol{I}_{n},\boldsymbol{X}\rangle&=\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\sup_{\boldsymbol{X}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{k}\text{Tr}\left(\boldsymbol{X}\right)\\
&\leq\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\left\lvert\sum_{k=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{k}\right\lvert\sup_{\boldsymbol{X}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}\text{Tr}\left(\boldsymbol{X}\right)\\
&\lesssim\sqrt{m}\sup_{\boldsymbol{X}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}\text{Tr}\left(\boldsymbol{X}\right).\end{split}

(31)

In the last line, we have used
\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\left\lvert\sum_{k=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{k}\right\lvert\lesssim\sqrt{m}
.
Thus, by Lemma
1
and (
31
), we have proved (
30
).
∎

Remark 7
.

In

\displaystyle\widetilde{S\left(\mathcal{M}\right)}\lesssim\sqrt{m}K^{2}\sqrt{\mathcal{R}_{0}}\cdot\gamma_{2}\left(\mathcal{M},\left\lVert\cdot\right\lVert_{F}\right)+K^{2}\cdot\gamma_{1}\left(\mathcal{M},\left\lVert\cdot\right\lVert_{op}\right)

However, this factor enormously reduces the sharpness of the estimation of

We compare (

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sqrt{\mathcal{R}_{0}}\cdot\gamma_{2}\left(\mathcal{M},\left\lVert\cdot\right\lVert_{F}\right)&=\sup\limits_{\boldsymbol{X}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}\frac{\left\lVert\boldsymbol{X}\right\lVert_{*}}{\left\lVert\boldsymbol{X}\right\lVert_{F}}\cdot\gamma_{2}\left(\mathcal{M},\left\lVert\cdot\right\lVert_{F}\right)\\

(33)

Since
\mathcal{R}_{0}\geq 1

]

Remark 8

For many cases of interest, such as the two examples of Corollary

But in the case

Step 1: Homogeneous Conversion.

For
\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathcal{T}_{1},\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathcal{T}_{2}
,

(50)

By homogeneity of
\boldsymbol{h},\boldsymbol{g}
in the above equality, that is,

we can assume that
\boldsymbol{h},\boldsymbol{g}\in\mathbb{S}_{\ell_{2}}
.
Thus we can futher set

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\boldsymbol{h}\in\text{cone}\left(\mathcal{T}_{1}+\mathcal{T}_{2}\right)\cap\mathbb{S}_{\ell_{2}}:=\mathcal{T}^{+}\\
\boldsymbol{g}\in\text{cone}\left(\mathcal{T}_{1}-\mathcal{T}_{2}\right)\cap\mathbb{S}_{\ell_{2}}:=\mathcal{T}^{-}\end{array}\right.

\boldsymbol{h}\in\mathcal{T}^{+}
and
\boldsymbol{g}\in\mathcal{T}^{-}

Furhermore, by Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, for any

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{1}{m}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left\lvert\langle\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{k}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{k}^{*},\boldsymbol{h}\boldsymbol{g}^{*}+\boldsymbol{g}\boldsymbol{h}^{*}\rangle\right\lvert^{p}\leq\left(\frac{1}{m}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left\lvert\langle\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{k}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{k}^{*},\boldsymbol{h}\boldsymbol{g}^{*}+\boldsymbol{g}\boldsymbol{h}^{*}\rangle\right\lvert\right)^{p},\end{split}

(52)

and

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{1}{m}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left\lvert\langle\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{k}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{k}^{*},\boldsymbol{h}\boldsymbol{g}^{*}+\boldsymbol{g}\boldsymbol{h}^{*}\rangle\right\lvert^{p}\geq\left(\frac{1}{m}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left\lvert\langle\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{k}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{k}^{*},\boldsymbol{h}\boldsymbol{g}^{*}+\boldsymbol{g}\boldsymbol{h}^{*}\rangle\right\lvert^{1/2}\right)^{2p}.\end{split}

(53)

Thus, we could only provide an upper bound for

Step 2: Upper Bound.

We first establish our upper bound.
We note the following equality,

\displaystyle 2\left\lvert\langle\boldsymbol{\varphi}\boldsymbol{\varphi}^{*},\boldsymbol{h}\boldsymbol{g}^{*}+\boldsymbol{g}\boldsymbol{h}^{*}\rangle\right\lvert=\left\lvert\left\lvert\langle\boldsymbol{\varphi},\boldsymbol{h}+\boldsymbol{g}\rangle\right\lvert^{2}-\left\lvert\langle\boldsymbol{\varphi},\boldsymbol{h}-\boldsymbol{g}\rangle\right\lvert^{2}\right\lvert.

Thus, we have that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{1}{m}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left\lvert\langle\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{k}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{k}^{*},\boldsymbol{h}\boldsymbol{g}^{*}+\boldsymbol{g}\boldsymbol{h}^{*}\rangle\right\lvert&=\frac{1}{2m}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left\lvert\left\lvert\langle\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{k},\boldsymbol{h}+\boldsymbol{g}\rangle\right\lvert^{2}-\left\lvert\langle\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{k},\boldsymbol{h}-\boldsymbol{g}\rangle\right\lvert^{2}\right\lvert\\
&\leq\frac{1}{2m}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left\lvert\langle\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{k},\boldsymbol{h}+\boldsymbol{g}\rangle\right\lvert^{2}+\frac{1}{2m}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left\lvert\langle\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{k},\boldsymbol{h}-\boldsymbol{g}\rangle\right\lvert^{2}\\

By Lemma

Step 1: Preliminary.

We denote by
\text{diam}_{1}\left(A\right)
and
\text{diam}_{2}\left(A\right)
the diameter of the set
A
for norm
\left\lVert\cdot\right\lVert_{op}
and
\left\lVert\cdot\right\lVert_{F}
, respectively.
We then consider an admissible sequence
\left(\mathcal{B}_{n}\right)_{n\geq 0}
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}2^{n}\text{diam}_{1}\left(B_{n}\left(\boldsymbol{X}\right)\right)\leq 2\gamma_{1}\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{M}},\left\lVert\cdot\right\lVert_{op}\right),\quad\forall\ \boldsymbol{X}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}

and an admissible sequence
\left(\mathcal{C}_{n}\right)_{n\geq 0}
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}2^{n/2}\text{diam}_{2}\left(C_{n}\left(\boldsymbol{X}\right)\right)\leq 2\gamma_{2}\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{M}},\left\lVert\cdot\right\lVert_{F}\right),\quad\forall\ \boldsymbol{X}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}.

Here
B_{n}\left(\boldsymbol{X}\right)
is the unique element of
\mathcal{B}_{n}
that contains
\boldsymbol{X}
and
C_{n}\left(\boldsymbol{X}\right)
is the unique element of
\mathcal{C}_{n}
that contains
\boldsymbol{X}
.
The definition of partitions
\mathcal{A}_{n}
of
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}
is as follows: we set
\mathcal{A}_{0}=\left\{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}\right\}
, and for
n\geq 1
, we define
\mathcal{A}_{n}
as the partition generated by
\mathcal{B}_{n-1}
and
\mathcal{C}_{n-1}
, i.e., the partition that consists of the sets
B\cap C
for
B\in\mathcal{B}_{n-1}
and
C\in\mathcal{C}_{n-1}
. Thus
\left\lvert\mathcal{A}_{n}\right\lvert\leq\left\lvert\mathcal{B}_{n-1}\right\lvert\cdot\left\lvert\mathcal{C}_{n-1}\right\lvert\leq 2^{2^{n}}
and the sequence
\left(\mathcal{A}_{n}\right)_{n\geq 0}
is admissible.

Step 2: Chaining Method.

We construct a subset
\mathcal{M}_{n}
of
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}
by taking exactly one point in each set
A
of
\mathcal{A}_{n}
and thus
\left\lvert\mathcal{M}_{n}\right\lvert\leq 2^{2^{n}}
.
For any
\boldsymbol{X}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}
, we consider
\pi_{n}\left(\boldsymbol{X}\right)\in\mathcal{M}_{n}
such that
\pi_{n}\left(\boldsymbol{X}\right)
are successive approximations of
\boldsymbol{X}
.
We assume that
\mathcal{M}_{0}
consists of a single element
\boldsymbol{X}_{0}
, which means that
\pi_{0}\left(\boldsymbol{X}\right)=\boldsymbol{X}_{0}
.
Let random variable
Y_{\boldsymbol{X}}=\langle\boldsymbol{\varphi}\boldsymbol{\varphi}^{*}-\mathbb{E}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\boldsymbol{\varphi}^{*},\boldsymbol{X}\rangle
, then we have that

\displaystyle Y_{\boldsymbol{X}}-Y_{\boldsymbol{X}_{0}}=\sum_{n\geq 1}\left(Y_{\pi_{n}\left(\boldsymbol{X}\right)}-Y_{\pi_{n-1}\left(\boldsymbol{X}\right)}\right).

By Hanson-Wright inequality in
[
46
]
, there exists numerical constant
c>0
and for
v>0

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{P}&\left(\left\lvert Y_{\pi_{n}\left(\boldsymbol{X}\right)}-Y_{\pi_{n-1}\left(\boldsymbol{X}\right)}\right\lvert\geq v\right)\\
&\leq 2\exp\left(-c\min\left\{\frac{v^{2}}{K^{4}\left\lVert\pi_{n}\left(\boldsymbol{X}\right)-\pi_{n-1}\left(\boldsymbol{X}\right)\right\lVert^{2}_{F}},\frac{v}{K^{2}\left\lVert\pi_{n}\left(\boldsymbol{X}\right)-\pi_{n-1}\left(\boldsymbol{X}\right)\right\lVert_{op}}\right\}\right).\end{split}

(64)

We reformulate the above inequality as follows: when
u\geq 0
, with probability exceeding
1-2\exp\left(-u^{2}2^{n-1}\right)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\lvert Y_{\pi_{n}\left(\boldsymbol{X}\right)}-Y_{\pi_{n-1}\left(\boldsymbol{X}\right)}\right\lvert&\leq u\cdot K^{2}2^{n/2}\left\lVert\pi_{n}\left(\boldsymbol{X}\right)-\pi_{n-1}\left(\boldsymbol{X}\right)\right\lVert_{F}/\sqrt{c}\\
&\quad+u^{2}\cdot K^{2}2^{n}\left\lVert\pi_{n}\left(\boldsymbol{X}\right)-\pi_{n-1}\left(\boldsymbol{X}\right)\right\lVert_{op}/c\\
\end{split}

We define the event
\Omega_{u,n}
by

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\lvert Y_{\pi_{n}\left(\boldsymbol{X}\right)}-Y_{\pi_{n-1}\left(\boldsymbol{X}\right)}\right\lvert&\leq u\cdot K^{2}2^{n/2}\left\lVert\pi_{n}\left(\boldsymbol{X}\right)-\pi_{n-1}\left(\boldsymbol{X}\right)\right\lVert_{F}/\sqrt{c}\\
&\quad+u^{2}\cdot K^{2}2^{n}\left\lVert\pi_{n}\left(\boldsymbol{X}\right)-\pi_{n-1}\left(\boldsymbol{X}\right)\right\lVert_{op}/c,\quad\forall\ \boldsymbol{X}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}.\end{split}

The number of possible pairs
\left(\pi_{n}\left(\boldsymbol{X}\right),\pi_{n-1}\left(\boldsymbol{X}\right)\right)
is bounded by

\displaystyle\left\lvert\mathcal{M}_{n}\right\lvert\cdot\left\lvert\mathcal{M}_{n-1}\right\lvert\leq 2^{2^{n+1}}.

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\Omega_{u,n}^{c}\right)\leq 2\cdot 2^{2^{n+1}}\exp\left(-u^{2}\cdot 2^{n-1}\right).

For
n\geq 1
and
u\geq 3
, we have

u^{2}\cdot 2^{n-1}\geq\frac{u^{2}}{2}+u^{2}\cdot 2^{n-2}\geq\frac{u^{2}}{2}+2^{n+1}.

We define
\Omega_{u}:=\underset{n\geq 1}{\cap}\Omega_{u,n}
.
Then when
u\geq 3
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{P}\left(u\right):=\mathbb{P}\left(\Omega_{u}^{c}\right)&\leq\sum_{n\geq 1}\mathbb{P}\left(\Omega_{u,n}^{c}\right)\leq\sum_{n\geq 1}2\cdot 2^{2^{n+1}}\exp\left(-u^{2}\cdot 2^{n-1}\right)\\
&\leq\sum_{n\geq 1}2\cdot 2^{2^{n+1}}\exp\left(-u^{2}/2-2^{n+1}\right)\\
&\leq\sum_{n\geq 1}2\cdot\left(\frac{2}{e}\right)^{2^{n+1}}\exp\left(-u^{2}/2\right)\leq 4\exp\left(-u^{2}/2\right).\end{split}

As
\mathbb{P}\left(u\right)\leq 1
, the above inequality also holds for
0\leq u\leq 3
.

Step 3: Expectation Form.

When
\Omega_{u}
occurs, we have for all
u\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\lvert Y_{\boldsymbol{X}}-Y_{\boldsymbol{X}_{0}}\right\lvert&\leq u\cdot K^{2}\sum_{n\geq 1}2^{n/2}\left\lVert\pi_{n}\left(\boldsymbol{X}\right)-\pi_{n-1}\left(\boldsymbol{X}\right)\right\lVert_{F}/\sqrt{c}\\
&+u^{2}\cdot K^{2}\sum_{n\geq 1}2^{n}\left\lVert\pi_{n}\left(\boldsymbol{X}\right)-\pi_{n-1}\left(\boldsymbol{X}\right)\right\lVert_{op}/c\\
&:=u\cdot K^{2}S_{1}+u^{2}\cdot K^{2}S_{2}.\end{split}

For
S_{1}
, we could bound it that

\displaystyle\begin{split}S_{1}&=\sum_{n\geq 1}2^{n/2}\left\lVert\pi_{n}\left(\boldsymbol{X}\right)-\pi_{n-1}\left(\boldsymbol{X}\right)\right\lVert_{F}/\sqrt{c}\\
&\leq\sum_{n\geq 1}2^{n/2}\left\lVert\boldsymbol{X}-\pi_{n-1}\left(\boldsymbol{X}\right)\right\lVert_{F}/\sqrt{c}+\sum_{n\geq 1}2^{n/2}\left\lVert\boldsymbol{X}-\pi_{n}\left(\boldsymbol{X}\right)\right\lVert_{F}/\sqrt{c}\\
&\lesssim\sum_{n\geq 0}2^{n/2}diam_{2}\left(C_{n}\left(\boldsymbol{X}\right)\right)\\
&\lesssim\gamma_{2}\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{M}},\left\lVert\cdot\right\lVert_{F}\right)\lesssim\gamma_{2}\left(\mathcal{M},\left\lVert\cdot\right\lVert_{F}\right):=\widetilde{S_{1}}.\end{split}

The last inequality is due to Proposition
1
.
Similarly, we have
S_{2}\lesssim\gamma_{1}\left(\mathcal{M},\left\lVert\cdot\right\lVert_{op}\right):=\widetilde{S_{2}}
.
Now we can deduce that for all
u\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{\boldsymbol{X}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}\left\lvert Y_{\boldsymbol{X}}-Y_{\boldsymbol{X}_{0}}\right\lvert\geq 2\max\left\{u\cdot K^{2}\widetilde{S_{1}},u^{2}\cdot K^{2}\widetilde{S_{2}}\right\}\right)\\
&\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{\boldsymbol{X}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}\left\lvert Y_{\boldsymbol{X}}-Y_{\boldsymbol{X}_{0}}\right\lvert\geq u\cdot K^{2}\widetilde{S_{1}}+u^{2}\cdot K^{2}\widetilde{S_{2}}\right)\\
&\leq\mathbb{P}\left(u\right)\leq 4\exp\left(-u^{2}/2\right).\end{split}

We can rewrite the above inequality as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{\boldsymbol{X}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}\left\lvert Y_{\boldsymbol{X}}-Y_{\boldsymbol{X}_{0}}\right\lvert\geq t\right)&\leq 4\exp\left(-\tilde{c}\min\left\{\frac{t}{K^{2}\widetilde{S_{2}}},\frac{t^{2}}{K^{4}\widetilde{S_{1}}^{2}}\right\}\right).\end{split}

(65)

In particular, since
Y_{\boldsymbol{X}_{0}}=0
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}
is symmetric set, we have that

\displaystyle\begin{split}S\left(\mathcal{M}\right)&=\mathbb{E}\ \sup_{\boldsymbol{X}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}Y_{\boldsymbol{X}}=\mathbb{E}\ \sup_{\boldsymbol{X}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}\left(Y_{\boldsymbol{X}}-Y_{\boldsymbol{X}_{0}}\right)=\mathbb{E}\ \sup_{\boldsymbol{X}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}\left\lvert Y_{\boldsymbol{X}}-Y_{\boldsymbol{X}_{0}}\right\lvert\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{\boldsymbol{X}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}\left\lvert Y_{\boldsymbol{X}}-Y_{\boldsymbol{X}_{0}}\right\lvert\geq t\right)dt\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}4\exp\left(-\tilde{c}\min\left\{\frac{t}{K^{2}\widetilde{S_{2}}},\frac{t^{2}}{K^{4}\widetilde{S_{1}}^{2}}\right\}\right)dt\\
&\lesssim\left(K^{2}\widetilde{S_{1}}\right)\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{\boldsymbol{X}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}\left\lvert Y_{\boldsymbol{X}}-Y_{\boldsymbol{X}_{0}}\right\lvert\geq u\cdot K^{2}\widetilde{S_{1}}\right)du\\
&+\left(K^{2}\widetilde{S_{2}}\right)\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{\boldsymbol{X}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}\left\lvert Y_{\boldsymbol{X}}-Y_{\boldsymbol{X}_{0}}\right\lvert\geq u^{2}\cdot K^{2}\widetilde{S_{2}}\right)du\lesssim K^{2}\widetilde{S_{1}}+K^{2}\widetilde{S_{2}}.\end{split}

Thus, we have proved (
28
).

Step 4: Empirical Form.

We then use (
28
) to prove (
29
).
Let random variable
\overline{Y_{\boldsymbol{X}}}=\langle\sum_{k=1}^{m}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{k}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{k}^{*}-m\mathbb{E}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\boldsymbol{\varphi}^{*},\boldsymbol{X}\rangle
. We can rewrite it as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\overline{Y_{\boldsymbol{X}}}&=\begin{pmatrix}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{1}^{*}&\cdots&\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{m}^{*}\\
\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\boldsymbol{X}&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&\boldsymbol{X}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{1}\\
\vdots\\
\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{m}\end{pmatrix}-m\langle\mathbb{E}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\boldsymbol{\varphi}^{*},\boldsymbol{X}\rangle\\
&:=\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\varphi}^{*}}\boldsymbol{Z}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}-\mathbb{E}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\varphi}^{*}}\boldsymbol{Z}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\varphi}},\end{split}

where
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}\in\mathbb{R}^{mn},\boldsymbol{Z}\in\mathbb{R}^{mn\times mn}
.
As
\left\lVert\boldsymbol{Z}\right\lVert_{F}=\sqrt{m}\left\lVert\boldsymbol{X}\right\lVert_{F}
and
\left\lVert\boldsymbol{Z}\right\lVert_{op}=\left\lVert\boldsymbol{X}\right\lVert_{op}
, we can rewrite (
64
) as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{P}&\left(\left\lvert\overline{Y_{\pi_{n}\left(\boldsymbol{X}\right)}}-\overline{Y_{\pi_{n-1}\left(\boldsymbol{X}\right)}}\right\lvert\geq v\right)\\
&\leq 2\exp\left(-c\min\left\{\frac{v^{2}}{mK^{4}\left\lVert\pi_{n}\left(\boldsymbol{X}\right)-\pi_{n-1}\left(\boldsymbol{X}\right)\right\lVert^{2}_{F}},\frac{v}{K^{2}\left\lVert\pi_{n}\left(\boldsymbol{X}\right)-\pi_{n-1}\left(\boldsymbol{X}\right)\right\lVert_{op}}\right\}\right).\end{split}

To obtain (
29
), the following arguments can be structured similarly to
Step 2
and
Step 3
.

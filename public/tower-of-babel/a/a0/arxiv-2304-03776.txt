A short ODE proof of the Fundamental Theorem of Algebra
Ramona Anton, Nicolae Mihalache, François Vigneron

Abstract
We propose a short proof of the Fundamental Theorem of Algebra based on the ODE that describes the Newton flow and the fact that the value | P ⁡ ( z ) | |P(z)| is a Lyapunov function. It clarifies an idea that goes back to Cauchy.



The Fundamental Theorem of Algebra is one of the major results of mathematics;
historical reviews can be found
e.g.
in
[
7
]
,
[
2
, Chap. II]
or
[
6
]
.
In practice, the roots of polynomials are computed with a variety of different algorithms (see
e.g.

[
5
]

for an exhaustive review) and, notably, with Newton-Raphson’s method
[
1
]
.
It is well known that the different basins of attraction of the iterative Newton method are deeply intricate.
The existence of universal sets of starting points such that the closure of all Newton’s iterates will always contain
all roots is remarkable though highly non-trivial (see Hubbard-Schleicher-Sutherland
[
4
]
).
On the other hand, the flow of Newton’s ODE presented below in (
1
) is topologically much simpler
and suitable for a short constructive proof of the fundamental Theorem of Algebra that we present in this note.

This idea can be found, at a higher level of generality, in Hirsch-Smale
[
3
]
.
However, the proof below is of great practical importance because, in addition to the general considerations of
[
3
]
, we
are able to control very explicitly the speed of convergence of each Newton trajectory.
For example, (
2
) implies that, away from roots and critical points, each step of Newton’s method approximately divides
the value of the polynomial by
e
(see Figure
1
), which is a property that, to the best of our knowledge,
had not been noticed before.
In
[
5
]
we use this idea to split a non-trivial polynomial of record degree
10^{12}
.
We believe it is important to highlight it.

Given a polynomial
P\in\mathbb{C}[z]
of degree
d\geq 1
, we denote by
\mathcal{Z}(P)=P^{-1}(0)
the set of its zeros
and by
\crits(P)=\mathcal{Z}(P^{\prime})
the set of its critical points, a finite set (by Euclidean division). Let also
\mathcal{V}(P)=P(\crits(P))
the
set of critical values of
P
.

The
Newton flow
of
P
is defined by the ODE

\varphi_{z}(0)=z\quad\text{and}\quad\varphi_{z}^{\prime}(t)=-\frac{P(\varphi_{z}(t))}{P^{\prime}(\varphi_{z}(t))}\cdotp

(1)

The set
\mathcal{Z}(P)
is composed of stationary solutions of (
1
).
Thanks to the Cauchy-Lipschitz theorem, the Newton flow is locally well defined starting from any point
z\notin\crits(P)

and can be extended locally as long as
\varphi_{z}(t)\notin\crits(P)
. For
z\notin\crits(P)
, let us denote by
T(z)\in(0,+\infty]
the
maximal forward time of existence of
\varphi_{z}
.
One has

\forall z\notin\crits(P),\quad\forall t\in[0,T(z))\qquad P(\varphi_{z}(t))=e^{-t}P(z)

(2)

because Leibniz’s rule and (
1
) imply

\frac{d}{dt}\left[e^{t}P(\varphi_{z}(t))\right]=e^{t}P(\varphi_{z}(t))+e^{t}P^{\prime}(\varphi_{z}(t))\varphi_{z}^{\prime}(t)=0.

In particular, from (
2
) we see that Newton’s flow is
iso-angle

i.e.

\arg P(\varphi_{z}(t))=\arg P(z)
if
P(z)\neq 0
. Moreover, for
t\in(0,T(z))
,

\varphi_{z}(t)\in\mathcal{B}(z)=\{y\in\mathbb{C}\,;\,|P(y)|\leq|P(z)|\}
, which
is a compact set because
\lim\limits_{|z|\to\infty}|P(z)|=+\infty
.

Theorem 1
.

Every non-constant polynomial
P\in\mathbb{C}[z]
has at least one root in
\mathbb{C}
.

Proof.

Our strategy to find a root of
P
is to choose a starting point
z_{0}
with
T(z_{0})=+\infty
and then pass to the limit in (
2
). We will see that it is enough that
\arg(P(z_{0}))
avoids the finite set
\arg(\mathcal{V}(P))
. Observe that
0\notin\mathcal{V}(P)
, otherwise
P
has a root in
\crits(P)
and we stop here.

If
T(z)<\infty
for some
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\crits(P)
, then
P(z)\neq 0
. As
t\to T(z)
, the orbit
\varphi_{z}(t)
leaves any compact subset of
\mathbb{C}\backslash\crit(P)
. As
\varphi_{z}(t)\in\mathcal{B}(z)
, a compact set, there is a sequence
t_{n}\to T(z)
such that
\varphi_{z}(t_{n})
converges to some point
c\in\crits(P)
.
The continuity of
P
at
c
implies that
\arg P(z)=\arg P(\varphi_{z}(t_{n}))=\arg(P(c))\in\arg(\mathcal{V}(P))
.

Let us find
z_{0}
with
\arg P(z_{0})\notin\arg(\mathcal{V}(P))
. As
\lim\limits_{|z|\rightarrow\infty}z^{-d}P(z)=\alpha\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
, taking
z_{0}=re^{i\vartheta}
with

d\vartheta+\arg\alpha\notin\arg(\mathcal{V}(P))\mod 2\pi

and
r>0
large enough ensures that
\arg(P(z_{0}))\notin\arg(\mathcal{V}(P))
.
Therefore
T(z_{0})=+\infty
and the bounded sequence
\left(\varphi_{z_{0}}(n)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{B}(z_{0})
admits an accumulation point
z_{\ast}
.
The continuity of
P
at
z_{\ast}
and (
2
) imply that
P(z_{\ast})=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}e^{-n}P(z_{0})=0
.
∎

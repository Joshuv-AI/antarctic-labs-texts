Decomposition of the longest element of the Weyl group using factors corresponding to the highest roots
Rafael Stekolshchik

Abstract
. Let Φ \varPhi be a root system of a finite Weyl group W W with simple roots Δ \Delta and corresponding simple reflections S S . For J ⊆ S J\subseteq S , denote by W J W_{J} the standard parabolic subgroup of W W generated by J J , and by Δ J ⊆ Δ \Delta_{J}\subseteq\Delta the subset corresponding to J J . We show that the longest element of W W is decomposed into a product of several ( ≤ | Δ | \leq|\Delta| ) reflections corresponding to mutually orthogonal roots, each of which is either the highest root of some subset Δ J ⊆ Δ \Delta_{J}\subseteq\Delta or is a simple root. For each type of the root system, the factors of the specified decomposition are listed. The relationship between the longest elements of different types is found out. The uniqueness of the considered decomposition is shown. It turns out that subsets of highest roots, which give the decomposition of longest elements in the Weyl group, coincide with the cascade of orthogonal roots constructed by B.Kostant and A.Joseph for calculations in the universal enveloping algebra.

1.2.3.
Uniqueness of decomposition

Definition 1.3
.

Let
T=\{\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\dots,\tau_{m}\}
be the subset of distinct roots in
\varPhi
.
This set is said to be
max-orthogonal
if

(a)

the roots of
T
are
mutually orthogonal
,

(b)

all non-simple roots in
T
form a
linearly ordered subset
in
\varPhi
.

(c)

each non-simple root
\tau_{i}
is the
highest root
in
some root subsystem
J\subset\varPhi
corresponding to a standard parabolic
subgroup
W_{J}\subset W
. The root
\tau_{1}
is the highest root in
\varPhi
, i.e.,

\tau_{1}=s_{\alpha_{max}}
.

Note that each root subset used in decompositions
of Theorem
1.1
is max-orthogonal.

Theorem 1.4
.

For any longest element
w_{0}
, there exists a unique max-orthogonal
subset
\{\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\dots,\tau_{m}\}
, where
m\leq n
, such that

w_{0}=\prod\limits^{m}_{i=1}s_{\tau_{i}}.

(1.2)

Definition 1.5
.

The decomposition (
1.2
) corresponding to some max-orthogonal subset
\{\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\dots,\tau_{m}\}

is said to be the max-orthogonal decomposition.

We will construct the max-orthogonal decomposition for
each type of root systems, see Tables
1.1
and Table
1.2

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

In eq. (
1.1
), for each relation, there exists a factor
that connects two different longest elements. We refer to this factor as the
linking factor
.
Denote it by
\mathcal{L}

(a)

\mathcal{L}=s_{\alpha_{max}}

E_{n}
and

(b)

\mathcal{L}=s_{\alpha_{max}}s_{\alpha_{1}}
, this holds for
B_{n}
and
D_{n}

The uniqueness of the max-orthogonal decomposition (
1.2
) will be proved
by induction on the length of the longest element.

(a) Let
w_{0}(\varPhi)=w_{0}(\varPhi^{\prime})s_{\alpha_{max}}

the decomposition (
1.2
) of
w_{0}(\varPhi)
contains the factor
s_{\alpha_{max}}
.
By induction hypothesis

Then, the max-orthogonal decomposition of

is unique.

1.4.1.
The cascade construction

A subset
\{\beta_{1},\beta_{2},...,\beta_{r}\in\varPhi\}
is said to be a
strongly orthogonal

set of roots if
\beta_{i}\pm\beta_{j}
is not a root for all pairs
\{i,j\}
.
Denote by
\Delta_{\lambda}
the subset of roots orthogonal to
\lambda
.
The sequence of roots obtained by taking the highest root
\alpha_{max}
of
\varPhi
,
the corresponding highest roots of components of
\Delta_{\alpha_{max}}
and so on
(see
\lx@sectionsign
1.4.2
), is a strongly orthogonal set.
In
[
10
, p.5]
, Joseph refers to a private communication with Kostant,
where the latter notes that:
“…any orthogonal
set of roots determines a strongly orthogonal set (by taking sums and differences)
and any maximal strongly orthogonal set is unique up to W”
.
In his following paper
[
11
]
, Joseph finds maximal such sets for each
root system and uses them for calculations in the universal
enveloping algebra
U(\mathfrak{g})
, where
\mathfrak{g}
is a semisimple Lie algebra.
Apparently, these maximal subsets were first presented in
[
11
, Table III]
.
Today they are known as
Kostant’s cascade
.

[

\mathcal{E}
be the linear space spanned by simple roots of
\Delta

(\;,\;)

Lemma B.1
.

For any two roots
\alpha
and
\beta

s_{\alpha}s_{\beta}s_{\alpha}=s_{s_{\alpha}(\beta)}.

(B.1)

Proof.
Really,

\begin{split}&s_{\beta}s_{\alpha}(x)=s_{\alpha}(x)-\frac{2(\beta,s_{\alpha}(x))}{(\beta,\beta)}\beta=s_{\alpha}(x)-\frac{2(s_{\alpha}(\beta),x)}{(s_{\alpha}(\beta),s_{\alpha}(\beta))}\beta,\text{ and }\\

&s_{\alpha}s_{\beta}s_{\alpha}=s_{s_{\alpha}(\beta)}.\end{split}

Then,

If
\lVert\alpha\rVert=\sqrt{2}\lVert\beta\rVert
and
\lVert\beta\rVert=1

then
(\beta,\alpha)=n(\beta,\alpha)=\pm{1}
.
This holds for two roots of different lengths in
B_{n}
,
F_{n}
.

(iii)
If
\lVert\alpha\rVert=\sqrt{2}\lVert\beta\rVert
and
\lVert\beta\rVert=\sqrt{2}

then
(\beta,\alpha)=2n(\beta,\alpha)=\pm{2}
.
This holds for the roots of different lengths in
C_{n}
.

(iv)
Let
\lVert\alpha\rVert=\sqrt{3}\lVert\beta\rVert
and
\lVert\beta\rVert=1
.
Then
(\beta,\alpha)=\frac{3}{2}n(\beta,\alpha)=\pm\frac{3}{2}
.
This holds for the roots of different lengths in
G_{2}
.

Proposition B.4

(case of a short root)
.

Let
\alpha
and
\beta
be
two roots in
\varPhi
, such that
\lVert\alpha\rVert>\lVert\beta\rVert
.

(i)

If
\lVert\alpha\rVert=\sqrt{2}\lVert\beta\rVert
,
n(\alpha,\beta)=\pm{2}
,
the conjugation of
s_{\alpha}
by
s_{\beta}

is as follows:

s_{\beta}s_{\alpha}s_{\beta}=\begin{cases}s_{\alpha+2\beta}\text{ if }n(\alpha,\beta)=-2,\\
s_{\alpha-2\beta}\text{ if }n(\alpha,\beta)=2.\\
\end{cases}

(B.5)

The root
\alpha+2\beta
(resp.
\alpha-2\beta
) in the case
n(\alpha,\beta)=-2

(resp.
n(\alpha,\beta)=2
) is long.
This case occurs for pairs of roots
\lVert\alpha\rVert>\lVert\beta\rVert
in

B_{n}
,
C_{n}
and
F_{4}
.

(ii)

If
\lVert\alpha\rVert=\sqrt{3}\lVert\beta\rVert
,
n(\alpha,\beta)=\pm{3}
,
the conjugation of
s_{\alpha}
by
s_{\beta}

is as follows:

s_{\beta}s_{\alpha}s_{\beta}=\begin{cases}s_{\alpha+3\beta}\text{ if }(\alpha,\beta)=-\frac{3}{2},\\
s_{\alpha-3\beta}\text{ if }(\alpha,\beta)=\frac{3}{2}.\\
\end{cases}

(B.6)

This case occurs for pairs of roots
\lVert\alpha\rVert>\lVert\beta\rVert
in
G_{2}
.

Proof.
(i)
Eq. (
B.5
) follows from (
B.3
):

s_{\beta}(\alpha)=\alpha-n(\alpha,\beta)\beta=\begin{cases}\alpha+2\beta\;\text{ if }n(\alpha,\beta)=-2,\\
\alpha-2\beta\;\text{ if }n(\alpha,\beta)=2.\end{cases}

For cases
B_{n}
,
C_{n}
,
F_{4}
,
since
(\beta,\beta)=1
, we have
(\alpha,\beta)=\displaystyle\frac{n(\alpha,\beta)}{2}=\pm{1}
.
By (
B.5
)
\alpha+2\beta
(similarly for
\alpha-2\beta
) is long, because

\footnotesize\begin{split}&(\alpha+2\beta,\alpha+2\beta)=(\alpha,\alpha)+4(\alpha,\beta)+4(\beta,\beta)=2-4+4=2\;\text{ if }(\alpha,\beta)=-1,\\
&(\alpha-2\beta,\alpha-2\beta)=(\alpha,\alpha)-4(\alpha,\beta)+4(\beta,\beta)=2-4+4=2\;\text{ if }(\alpha,\beta)=1.\end{split}

(ii) Here,
(\beta,\beta)=1
and

(\beta,\alpha)=\displaystyle\frac{n(\alpha,\beta)}{2}=\pm\frac{3}{2}
, then

s_{\beta}(\alpha)=\alpha-n(\beta,\alpha)\beta=\begin{cases}\alpha+3\beta\;\text{ if }n(\alpha,\beta)=-3,\\
\alpha-3\beta\;\text{ if }n(\alpha,\beta)=3.\end{cases}

By (
B.6
) the root of
\alpha+3\beta
(similarly for
\alpha-3\beta
) is long, because

\footnotesize\begin{split}&(\alpha+3\beta,\alpha+3\beta)=(\alpha,\alpha)+6(\alpha,\beta)+9(\beta,\beta)=3-6(\frac{3}{2})+9=3\text{ if }n(\alpha,\beta)=-3,\\
&(\alpha-3\beta,\alpha-3\beta)=(\alpha,\alpha)-6(\alpha,\beta)+9(\beta,\beta)=3-6(\frac{3}{2})+9=3\text{ if }n(\alpha,\beta)=3.\qed\end{split}

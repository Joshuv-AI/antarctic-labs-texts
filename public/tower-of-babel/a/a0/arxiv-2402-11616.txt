Conservation of Ramsey’s theorem for pairs and well-foundedness
Quentin Le Houérou, Ludovic Levy Patey, Keita Yokoyama

Abstract
In this article, we prove that Ramsey’s theorem for pairs and two colors is Π 1 1 \Pi^{1}_{1} -conservative over 𝖱𝖢𝖠 0 + 𝖡 ​ Σ 2 0 + 𝖶𝖥 ⁡ ( ϵ 0 ) \mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}+\mathsf{WF}(\epsilon_{0}) and over 𝖱𝖢𝖠 0 + 𝖡 ​ Σ 2 0 + ⋃ n 𝖶𝖥 ⁡ ( ω n ω ) \mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}+\bigcup_{n}\mathsf{WF}(\omega^{\omega}_{n}) . These results improve theorems from Chong, Slaman and Yang [ 6 ] and Kołodziejczyk and Yokoyama [ 20 ] and belong to a long line of research towards the characterization of the first-order part of Ramsey’s theorem for pairs.



1
Introduction

We investigate the proof-theoretic strength of Ramsey’s theorem for pairs and some of its consequences, by proving that they are
\Pi^{1}_{1}
-conservative over the base theory of Reverse Mathematics (
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
), augmented with the collection principle for
\Sigma^{0}_{2}
formulas (
\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}
) and the assumption that some appropriate ordinals are well-founded (
\mathsf{WF}(\alpha)
). This article belongs to a long line of research towards the characterization of the first-order part of Ramsey’s theorem for pairs, and is part of a larger foundational program known as Reverse Mathematics, and whose goal is to find optimal axioms for proving ordinary theorems. See Simpson
[
30
]
or Dzhafarov and Mummert
[
7
]
for a reference book on Reverse Mathematics, and Hirschfeldt
[
8
]
to a gentle introduction to the reverse mathematics of combinatorial principles.

Ramsey’s theorem is the founding theorem of Ramsey’s theory, a field of research which studies the conditions under which structure appears among a sufficiently large amount of data. We identify a set
k
with its ordinal
\{0,1,\dots,k-1\}
. Given a set
X\subseteq\mathbb{N}
and some integer
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we let
[X]^{n}
denote the set of all unordered
n
-tuples over
X
.
Given a coloring
f:[\mathbb{N}]^{n}\to k
, a set
H\subseteq\mathbb{N}
is
f
-homogeneous (for color
i<k
)
if
f(\sigma)=i
for every
\sigma\in[\mathbb{N}]^{n}
.

Statement 1.1

(Ramsey’s theorem)
.

Given
n,k\in\mathbb{N}
,
\mathsf{RT}^{n}_{k}
is the statement “Every coloring
f:[\mathbb{N}]^{n}\to k
admits an infinite
f
-homogeneous set”.

The proof-theoretic strength of Ramsey’s theorem for triples and larger size of tuples is well-understood:
\mathsf{RT}^{n}_{k}
is equivalent to the Arithmetic Comprehension Axiom (
\mathsf{ACA}_{0}
) over
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
whenever
n\geq 3
and
k\geq 2
(see Jockusch
[
18
]
for a computability-theoretic equivalence, and Simpson
[
30
, Theorem III.7.6]
for a proof-theoretic version of it). The first-order part of
\mathsf{ACA}_{0}
is precisely Peano Arithmetic.

Ramsey’s theorem for pairs and two colors (
\mathsf{RT}^{2}_{2}
) can be phrased as stating, for every infinite graph, the existence of an infinite subset of vertices such that the induced subgraph is either a clique or an anticlique.
\mathsf{RT}^{2}_{2}
is known to be strictly weaker than
\mathsf{ACA}_{0}
over
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
(see Seetapun and Slaman
[
29
]
), and incomparable with weak König’s lemma (
\mathsf{WKL}_{0}
), the restriction of König’s lemma to binary trees (see Liu
[
24
]
).
The characterization of the first-order part of Ramsey’s theorem for pairs and two colors is a long-standing open question.

Definition 1.2

(Collection principle)
.

Given a family
\Gamma
of formulas,
\mathsf{B}\Gamma
is the statement “For every formula
\varphi\in\Gamma
, and every
a\in\mathbb{N}
, if
\forall x<a\exists y\varphi(x,y)
, then there is some
b\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\forall x<a\exists y<b\varphi(x,y)
”.

Ramsey’s theorem for pairs implies
\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}
over
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
(see Hirst
[
16
]
). The following question is a major open problem in Reverse Mathematics:

Question 1.3
.

Is
\mathsf{RT}^{2}_{2}
a
\Pi^{1}_{1}
-conservative extension of
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}
?

1.1
Well-foundedness and bounded monotone enumerations

In Computability Theory, the ability to compute fast-growing functions can be seen as a unified framework to measure the strength of some Turing degrees, e.g., hyperimmune degrees, high degrees, and degrees admitting a modulus. In Proof Theory, the strength of a theory is better measured by the ability to prove the well-foundedness of some ordinals. The
\Pi^{1}_{1}
proof-theoretic ordinal
of a theory
T
is the least ordinal
\alpha
such that
T
does not prove its well-foundedness (
\mathsf{WF}(\alpha)
). For instance, the
\Pi^{1}_{1}
proof-theoretic ordinals of
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
and
\mathsf{ACA}_{0}
are
\omega^{\omega}
and
\epsilon_{0}
, respectively (see Simpson
[
30
]
).

The well-foundedness of ordinals naturally occured in the proof-theoretic analysis of Ramsey’s theorem for pairs. Chong, Slaman and Yang
[
6
]
proved that
\mathsf{RT}^{2}_{2}
does not imply the induction scheme for
\Sigma^{0}_{2}
formulas (
\mathsf{I}\Sigma^{0}_{2}
) over
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
using a model of
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}+\neg\mathsf{I}\Sigma^{0}_{2}
which furthermore satisfied a principle called Bounded Monotone Enumeration (
\mathsf{BME}
).

Definition 1.4

(
[
6
]
)
.

Let
E
be a procedure to recursively enumerate a finite branching enumerable tree. We say that
E
is a
monotone enumeration
if and only if the following conditions apply to its stage-by-stage behavior.

(1)

The empty sequence is enumerated by E during stage 0.

(2)

Only finitely many sequences are enumerated by
E
during any stage.

(3)

Suppose
T[s]
is the tree enumerated by
E
at the end of stage
s
. Then any sequence enumerated by
E
during stage
s+1
must extend some terminal node of
T[s]
.

A monotone enumeration
E
is
b
-bounded
if every node in
E
has length at most
b
.

Statement 1.5
.

\mathsf{BME}_{\star}
is the statement “Every bounded monotone enumeration is finite”.

The statement
\mathsf{BME}
is an iterated version of
\mathsf{BME}_{\star}
. Its formal definition is rather technical and will not be used within this article.
Kreuzer and Yokoyama
[
22
]
proved that
\mathsf{BME}_{\star}
is equivalent to
\mathsf{WF}(\omega^{\omega})
over
\mathsf{I}\Sigma^{0}_{1}
, and that
\mathsf{BME}
is equivalent to
\bigcup_{k}\mathsf{WF}(\omega^{\omega}_{k})
, where
\omega^{\alpha}_{0}=\alpha
and
\omega^{\alpha}_{k+1}=\omega^{\omega_{k}^{\alpha}}
.

The principles
\mathsf{BME}
and
\mathsf{BME}_{\star}
naturally occur in the proofs of conservation of Ramsey’s theorem for pairs. On the other hand, Patey and Yokoyama
[
28
]
proved that
\mathsf{RT}^{2}_{2}
is
\forall\Pi^{0}_{3}
-conservative over
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}
, which implies in particular that the
\Pi^{1}_{1}
proof-theoretic ordinal of
\mathsf{RT}^{2}_{2}
is
\omega^{\omega}
, hence that
\mathsf{RT}^{2}_{2}
does not imply
\mathsf{BME}_{\star}
over
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
.

In this article, we show that the separation of Chong, Slaman and Yang
[
6
]
of
\mathsf{RT}^{2}_{2}
from
\mathsf{I}\Sigma^{0}_{2}
only depends on the assumption that the original model satisfies
\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}+\mathsf{BME}
, by proving the following conservation theorems.

Main Theorem 1.6
.

Fix
n\geq 2
. Let
T_{n}
be either
\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{n}
or
\mathsf{I}\Sigma^{0}_{n}
.

\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+T_{n}+\mathsf{RT}^{2}_{2}+\mathsf{WKL}+\mathsf{WF}(\epsilon_{0})
is a
\Pi^{1}_{1}
-conservative extension of
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+T_{n}+\mathsf{WF}(\epsilon_{0})
.

Main Theorem 1.7
.

Fix
n\geq 2
. Let
T_{n}
be either
\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{n}
or
\mathsf{I}\Sigma^{0}_{n}
.

\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+T_{n}+\mathsf{RT}^{2}_{2}+\mathsf{WKL}+\bigcup_{k\in\omega}\mathsf{WF}(\omega^{\omega}_{k})
is a
\Pi^{1}_{1}
-conservative extension of
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+T_{n}+\bigcup_{k\in\omega}\mathsf{WF}(\omega^{\omega}_{k})
.

This strengthens a result from Kołodziejczyk and Yokoyama
[
20
]
who showed that
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{RT}^{2}_{2}+\mathsf{BME}
is a
\Pi^{0}_{4}
-conservative extension of
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}+\mathsf{BME}
.
By Cholak, Jockusch and Slaman
[
3
]
, the result holds when
\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}+\mathsf{WF}(\epsilon_{0})
is replaced by
\mathsf{I}\Sigma^{0}_{2}
. Thus, conservations over
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+(\mathsf{WF}(\epsilon_{0})\vee\mathsf{I}\Sigma^{0}_{2})
and over
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+(\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\mathsf{WF}(\omega^{\omega}_{n})\vee\mathsf{I}\Sigma^{0}_{2})
are the best known results.

In order to better understand the computational and proof-theoretic content of Ramsey’s theorem for pairs, two main decompositions of
\mathsf{RT}^{2}_{2}
into combinatorially weaker statements have been studied. These decompositions will be used to prove separate conservation theorems for simpler statements, and then combined to obtain our main theorems. We now detail those statements.

1.2
Tournaments and linear orders

Ramsey’s theorem for pairs and two colors can be decomposed into the Erdős-Moser theorem about infinite tournaments, and the Ascending Descending Sequence principle.

Definition 1.8
.

A
tournament
on a domain
D
is an irreflexive binary relation
R\subseteq D^{2}
such that for every
a,b\in D
with
a\neq b
, exactly one of
R(a,b)
and
R(b,a)
holds. A tournament
R
is
transitive
if for every
a,b,c\in D
, if
R(a,b)
and
R(b,c)
then
R(a,c)
.

A subtournament of
R\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{2}
is fully characterized by its domain and the parent tournament
R
. We therefore identify subtournaments with infinite sets
H\subseteq\mathbb{N}
.
The following statement is known as the Erdős-Moser theorem, and is an infinitary version of some theorem by Erdős and Moser
[
9
]
.

Statement 1.9

(Erdős-Moser)
.

\mathsf{EM}
is the statement “Every infinite tournament admits an infinite transitive subtournament”.

There exists a one-to-one correspondence between a tournament
R
and a coloring
f:[\mathbb{N}]^{2}\to 2
, by letting
f(\{x,y\})=1
if
(R(x,y)\leftrightarrow x<_{\mathbb{N}}y)
.
\mathsf{EM}
can the restated as “for every coloring
f:[\mathbb{N}]^{2}\to 2
, there exists an infinite
f
-transitive
subset
H
, that is, for every
x,y,z\in H
such that
x<y<z
and every
i<2
, then if
f(\{x,y\})=f(\{y,z\})=i
, we have
f(\{x,z\})=i
”.

Statement 1.10

(Ascending Descending Sequence)
.

\mathsf{ADS}
is the statement “Every infinite linear order admits an infinite ascending or descending subsequence”.

Here again, there is a one-to-one correspondence between linear orders
\mathcal{L}=(\omega,<_{\mathcal{L}})
, and transitive colorings
f:[\mathbb{N}]^{2}\to 2
, by letting
f(\{x,y\})=1
if
(x<_{\mathbb{N}}y\leftrightarrow x<_{\mathcal{L}}y)
. Then
\mathsf{ADS}
can be restated as “for every transitive coloring
f:[\mathbb{N}]^{2}\to 2
, there exists an infinite
f
-homogeneous set”.
Using the reformulations of
\mathsf{EM}
and
\mathsf{ADS}
, the following proposition is immediate:

Proposition 1.11

(Bovyking and Weiermann
[
2
]
)
.

\mathsf{RCA}_{0}\vdash\mathsf{RT}^{2}_{2}\leftrightarrow\mathsf{EM}\wedge\mathsf{ADS}

Proof.

\mathsf{EM}
and
\mathsf{ADS}
are both particular cases of
\mathsf{RT}^{2}_{2}
, hence
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}\vdash\mathsf{RT}^{2}_{2}\rightarrow\mathsf{EM}\wedge\mathsf{ADS}
. On the other hand, given a coloring
f:[\mathbb{N}]^{2}\to 2
, first apply
\mathsf{EM}
to obtain an infinite
f
-transitive subset
X=\{x_{0}<x_{1}<\dots\}
. Let
g:[\mathbb{N}]^{2}\to 2
be the transitive coloring defined by
g(a,b)=f(x_{a},x_{b})
. By
\mathsf{ADS}
, there exists an infinite
g
-homogeneous subset
H\subseteq\mathbb{N}
. The set
\{x_{a}:a\in H\}
is infinite and
f
-homogeneous.
∎

From a combinatorial viewpoint, the decomposition of
\mathsf{RT}^{2}_{2}
into
\mathsf{EM}
and
\mathsf{ADS}
divides the difficulty of Ramsey’s theorem for pairs as follows: the combinatorics of
\mathsf{EM}
are very close to those of
\mathsf{RT}^{2}_{2}
, but without the disjunctive nature of
\mathsf{RT}^{2}_{2}
. Thus, there is no need to work with pairing arguments. On the other hand,
\mathsf{ADS}
has very simple combinatorics, and does not imply any form of compactness, but is a disjunctive statement.

From a reverse mathematical viewpoint, the decomposition is non-trivial, in that
\mathsf{EM}
and
\mathsf{ADS}
are both strictly weaker than
\mathsf{RT}^{2}_{2}
(see Lerman, Solomon and Towsner
[
23
]
).

From a proof-theoretic viewpoint, both statements imply
\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}
over
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
(see Hirschfeldt and Shore
[
15
, Proposition 4.5]
and Kreuzer
[
21
, Proposition 16]
), and
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{ADS}
is known to be
\Pi^{1}_{1}
-conservative extension of
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}
(see Chong, Slaman and Yang
[
5
]
). By an amalgamation theorem of Yokoyama
[
33
]
, it follows that
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{RT}^{2}_{2}
is
\Pi^{1}_{1}
-conservative over
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}
iff so is
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{EM}
.

1.3
Cohesiveness and stability

The second (and historically the first) decomposition of
\mathsf{RT}^{2}_{2}
was done by Cholak, Jockusch and Slaman
[
3
]

in their seminal article on Ramsey’s theorem for pairs. It consists in reducing
\mathsf{RT}^{2}_{2}
to a stable version of it (
\mathsf{SRT}^{2}_{2}
), using the Cohesiveness principle (
\mathsf{COH}
). Then, it is easy to see that
\mathsf{SRT}^{2}_{2}
is equivalent to the infinite pigeonhole principle for
\Delta^{0}_{2}
instances, which is a combinatorialy simple statement.

Definition 1.12
.

A coloring
f:[\mathbb{N}]^{2}\to k
is
stable
if for every
x\in\mathbb{N}
,
\lim_{y}f(x,y)
exists. Given an infinite sequence of sets
\vec{R}=R_{0},R_{1},\dots
, an infinite set
C
is
\vec{R}
-cohesive
if for every
s\in\mathbb{N}
,
C\subseteq^{*}R_{s}
or
C\subseteq^{*}\overline{R}_{s}
, where
\subseteq^{*}
denotes inclusion up to finite changes.

Statement 1.13

(Stable Ramsey’s theorem for pairs)
.

\mathsf{SRT}^{2}_{k}
denotes the restriction of
\mathsf{RT}^{2}_{k}
to stable colorings.

Statement 1.14

(Cohesiveness)
.

\mathsf{COH}
is the statement “Every infinite sequence of sets has a cohesive set”.

The following equivalence is essentially due to Cholak, Jockusch and Slaman
[
3
]
, with the proof
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}\vdash\mathsf{RT}^{2}_{2}\to\mathsf{COH}
fixed by Mileti
[
25
, Appendix A]
.

Proposition 1.15
.

\mathsf{RCA}_{0}\vdash\mathsf{RT}^{2}_{2}\leftrightarrow\mathsf{SRT}^{2}_{2}\wedge\mathsf{COH}
.

Proof.

The proof of the forward direction will be omitted, as it will be of no use in the article. Let us prove that
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}\vdash\mathsf{SRT}^{2}_{2}\wedge\mathsf{COH}\to\mathsf{RT}^{2}_{2}
.
Given a coloring
f:[\mathbb{N}]^{2}\to 2
, define the sequence of sets
\vec{R}=R_{0},R_{1},\dots
by
R_{x}=\{y:f(x,y)=1\}
. Given an infinite
\vec{R}
-cohesive set
C=\{x_{0}<x_{1}<\dots\}
, let
g:[\mathbb{N}]^{2}\to 2
be defined by
g(\{a,b\})=f(\{x_{a},x_{b}\})
. By cohesiveness of
C
, the coloring
g
is stable, so by
\mathsf{SRT}^{2}_{2}
, there is an infinite
g
-homogeneous set
H
.
The set
\{x_{a}:a\in H\}
is infinite and
f
-homogeneous.
∎

From a reverse mathematical viewpoint, the decomposition of
\mathsf{RT}^{2}_{2}
into
\mathsf{SRT}^{2}_{2}
and
\mathsf{COH}
is also non-trivial (see Hirschfeldt et al.
[
14
]
and Monin and Patey
[
26
]
).

The computability-theoretic content of the cohesiveness principle is well-understood: it admits a maximally complex instance, which is the sequence of all primitive recursive sets. A cohesive set for this sequence is called
p-cohesive
. For every uniformly computable sequence of sets
\vec{R}
, every
p
-cohesive set computes an
\vec{R}
-cohesive set. Moreover, a Turing degree bounds a p-cohesive set iff its jump is PA over
\mathsf{0}^{\prime}
. See Jockusch and Stephan
[
17
]
.

The idea of using cohesiveness to transform a coloring into a stable one can be applied to
\mathsf{EM}
and
\mathsf{ADS}
. We call
\mathsf{SEM}
and
\mathsf{SADS}
the restriction of
\mathsf{EM}
and
\mathsf{ADS}
to stable tournaments and linear orders, respectively.
Stable linear orders can be of three types:
\omega+\omega^{*}
,
\omega+k
and
k+\omega^{*}
, for some
k\in\omega
. The two former cases are computably true, so the only interesting case if whenever it is of order type
\omega+\omega^{*}
. This is why
\mathsf{SADS}
is often defined as the statement “Every linear order of type
\omega+\omega^{*}
admits an infinite ascending or descending sequence”.

Combining the two kind of decompositions, we shall use the following equivalence:

Proposition 1.16
.

\mathsf{RCA}_{0}\vdash\mathsf{RT}^{2}_{2}\leftrightarrow\mathsf{SEM}\wedge\mathsf{SADS}\wedge\mathsf{COH}

We will therefore prove conservation results about
\mathsf{WKL},\mathsf{COH},\mathsf{SEM}
and
\mathsf{SADS}
independently, and use the previous decomposition to obtain our main theorems.

1.4
Definitions and notation

Since
[X]^{n}
is in one-to-one correspondence with the set of all
n
-tuples in increasing order, given a coloring
f:[\mathbb{N}]^{n}\to k
, we might omit the brackets, and simply write
f(x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1})
instead of
f(\{x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1}\})
, assuming that
x_{0}<\dots<x_{n-1}
.

A
\Pi^{1}_{2}
problem

\mathsf{P}
is a formula
\forall X[\varphi(X)\rightarrow\psi(X,Y)]
where
\varphi
and
\psi
are arithmetic formulas. A set
X
such that
\varphi(X)
holds is a
\mathsf{P}
-instance
, and a
Y
such that
\psi(X,Y)
holds is a
\mathsf{P}
-solution
to
X
. In particular,
\mathsf{RT}^{2}_{2}
,
\mathsf{EM}
and
\mathsf{ADS}
are
\Pi^{1}_{2}
-problems. For example, an
\mathsf{RT}^{2}_{2}
-instance is a coloring
f:[\mathbb{N}]^{2}\to 2

and an
\mathsf{RT}^{2}_{2}
-solution to
f
is an infinite
f
-homogeneous set.

Binary strings
. We let
2^{<\mathbb{N}}
denote the set of all finite binary strings, and
2^{\mathbb{N}}
denote the class of all infinite binary sequences. Note that
2^{\mathbb{N}}
is in bijection with
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{N})
and both are usually identified.
Finite binary strings are written with greek letters
\sigma,\tau,\mu,\dots
. The length of a binary string
\sigma
is written
|\sigma|
, and the concatenation of two binary strings
\sigma
and
\tau
is written
\sigma\cdot\tau
. Given an infinite binary sequence
X\in 2^{\mathbb{N}}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we write
X{\upharpoonright}_{n}
for its initial segment of length
n
. A string
\sigma
is a prefix of a string
\tau
, written
\sigma\preceq\tau
, if there is some
\mu
such that
\sigma\cdot\mu=\tau
.

Finite sets
. Finite binary strings, seen as finite characteristic functions, are often identified with finite sets, that is,
\sigma
is identified with
\{n<|\sigma|:\sigma(n)=1\}
. Based on this correspondence, we extend the set-theoretic notations to binary strings, and let for example
\sigma\cup\rho
denote the binary string of length
\max(|\sigma|,|\rho|)
, and such that
(\sigma\cup\rho)(n)=1
iff
\sigma(n)=1
or
\rho(n)=1
.
In particular, one shall distinguish the cardinality
\operatorname{card}\sigma=\operatorname{card}\{n<|\sigma|:\sigma(n)=1\}
of a string seen as a set, from its length
|\sigma|
.

Theories.

Let
\mathsf{I}\Sigma^{0}_{n}
be the following induction scheme for every
\Sigma^{0}_{n}
formula
\phi
:

\phi(0)\wedge\forall x[\phi(x)\rightarrow\phi(x+1)]\rightarrow\forall x\phi(x)

Let
\Delta^{0}_{n}
-
\mathsf{CA}
be the following comprehension scheme for every
\Sigma^{0}_{n}
formula
\phi
and every
\Pi^{0}_{n}
formula
\psi
:

\forall x[\phi(x)\leftrightarrow\psi(x)]\rightarrow\exists A\forall x[x\in A\leftrightarrow\phi(x)]

\mathsf{RCA}_{0}^{*}
denotes the theory of Robinson arithmetic (
Q
) augmented with the comprehension axiom for
\Delta^{0}_{1}
predicates (
\Delta^{0}_{1}
-
\mathsf{CA}
), the collection scheme for
\Sigma^{0}_{1}
formulas (
\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{1}
) and the totality of the exponentiation function (
\mathsf{exp}
).

\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
is the theory of
Q+\Delta^{0}_{1}\mbox{-}\mathsf{CA}+\mathsf{I}\Sigma^{0}_{1}
. Note that
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
entails
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}^{*}
. The theory
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
is the base theory of Reverse Mathematics, but
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}^{*}
is sometimes more convenient to use, as shown by Belanger
[
1
]
.

Models.

A
model
is a second-order structure
\mathcal{M}=(M,S,+_{M},\times_{M},<_{M})
, where
M
is the first-order part, that is, the set of integers and
S\subseteq\mathcal{P}(M)
is the second-order part. We will usually omit the operations, and simply write
\mathcal{M}=(M,S)
.
A model
\mathcal{M}=(M,S)
is
topped
if there is some set
X\in S
such that every
Y\in S
is
\Delta^{0}_{1}(X)
. A model
\mathcal{N}=(N,T)
is an
\omega
-submodel
of
\mathcal{M}=(M,S)
if
N=M
and
T\subseteq S
. Note that the notion of
\omega
-submodel must not be confused with the notion of
\omega
-model, in which
M=\omega
, that is, is the set of all standard integers.

Given a model
\mathcal{M}=(M,S)
and a set
G\subseteq M
, we write
\mathcal{M}[G]
for the model whose first-order part is
M
, and whose second-order part consists of all
\Delta^{0}_{1}(X,G)
sets for
X\in S

Ordinals.

In this article, we shall work with ordinals smaller than

in models of weak arithmetic.
By Cantor’s normal form theorem, every ordinal

\epsilon_{0}
can be written uniquely as

Many open questions remain around the first-order part of Ramsey’s theorem for pairs and its consequences. The first and most important question being the following:

Question 5.1
.

Is
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{RT}^{2}_{2}

\Pi^{1}_{1}
-conservative over
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}

For every
\alpha<\epsilon_{0}

\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}+\mathsf{WF}(\alpha)+\mathsf{WKL}+\mathsf{COH}

\Pi^{1}_{1}

\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}+\mathsf{WF}(\alpha)

\mathsf{ADS}
and
\mathsf{EM}

which both seem to require
\mathsf{WF}(\omega^{\alpha\dottimes\omega})
to preserve
\mathsf{WF}(\alpha)
.

Question 5.2
.

For which
\alpha<\epsilon_{0}
is
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{WF}(\alpha)+\mathsf{RT}^{2}_{2}

\Pi^{1}_{1}
-conservative over
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}+\mathsf{WF}(\alpha)
?

The proofs of
Proposition

3.1
and
Proposition

3.17
required the ground model to satisfy
\mathsf{WF}(\omega^{\alpha})
to preserve
\mathsf{WF}(\alpha)
.
It is natural to wonder whether this assumption is necessary. One way to formalize this idea would be to prove that
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{RT}^{2}_{2}\rightarrow\forall n(\mathsf{WF}(\omega_{n})\rightarrow\mathsf{WF}(\omega_{n+1}))
, where
\omega_{0}=1
and
\omega_{n+1}=\omega^{\omega_{n}}
. This is not the case, as
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{RT}^{2}_{2}
is
\forall\Pi^{0}_{3}
-conservative over
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}
, hence does not prove
\mathsf{WF}(\omega^{\omega})
. One could also wonder whether
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{RT}^{2}_{2}
proves some weaker well-foundedness closure properties which do not already follow from
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{B}\Sigma^{0}_{2}
. Here again, the answer is negative. Le Houérou and Levy Patey constructed a model
\mathcal{M}=(M,S)
of
\mathsf{RCA}_{0}+\mathsf{RT}^{2}_{2}
in which
\{b\in M:\mathcal{M}\models\mathsf{WF}(\omega^{b})\}=\sup\{a\cdot k:k\in\omega\}
for some
a\in M\setminus\omega
. In other words, this cut has no better closure properties than additivity [personal communication]. This result is tight, as

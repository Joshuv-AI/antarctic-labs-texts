Plane-parallel waves as Jacobi-Lie models
Ivo Petr, Ladislav Hlavatý

Abstract
T-duality and its generalizations are widely recognized either as symmetries or solution-generating techniques in string theory. Recently introduced Jacobi–Lie T-plurality is based on Leibniz algebras whose structure constants f a ​ b c , f c a ​ b , Z a , Z a {f_{ab}}^{c},{f_{c}}^{ab},Z_{a},Z^{a} satisfy further conditions. Low dimensional Jacobi–Lie bialgebras were classified a few years ago. We study four- and six-dimensional algebras with structure constants f b b ​ a = Z a = 0 {f_{b}}^{ba}=Z^{a}=0 and show that there are several classes consisting of mutually isomorphic algebras. Using isomorphisms between Jacobi–Lie bialgebras we investigate three- and four-dimensional sigma models related by Jacobi–Lie T-plurality with and without spectators. In the Double Field Theory formulation constant generalized fluxes F A F_{A} are used in the literature to transform dilaton field. We extend the procedure to non-constant fluxes and verify that obtained backgrounds and dilatons solve Supergravity Equations. Most of the resulting backgrounds have vanishing curvature scalars and, as can be seen by finding Brinkmann coordinates, represent plane-parallel waves solving Supergravity Equations.

Introduction

Gravitational plane-parallel (pp-) waves were repeatedly studied in general relativity and appear frequently in context of string theory as Penrose limits of AdS
\times S
spacetimes, Dp-brane backgrounds and cosmological models
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
. They also provide exact solutions to string beta function equations
[
4
]
. A few years ago pp-waves were found as duals of flat Minkowski background using Poisson–Lie T-duality with respect to subgroups of Poincare group
[
5
,
6
,
7
]
. Recently a new form of duality/plurality, namely Jacobi–Lie T-plurality, has appeared
[
8
,
9
]
. In this paper we show that various pp-waves can be constructed by this new form of duality/plurality as well. Moreover, we show that Jacobi–Lie T-plurality generates solutions of Supergravity Equations.

Nonlinear sigma models satisfying supplementary conditions are used in string theory to model behavior of strings and
p
-branes propagating in possibly curved backgrounds. They are given by metric
\mathcal{G}
, Kalb–Ramond field
\mathcal{B}
and dilaton
\Phi
. To define a consistent conformally invariant quantum field theory the background bosonic fields in the NS-NS sector have to satisfy one-loop beta function equations
[
10
,
11
]
that read

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=R_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{4}H_{\mu\rho\sigma}H_{\nu}^{\ \rho\sigma}+2\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\Phi,

(1)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{\rho}H_{\rho\mu\nu}+\nabla^{\rho}\Phi\,H_{\rho\mu\nu},

(2)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=R-\frac{1}{12}H_{\rho\sigma\tau}H^{\rho\sigma\tau}+4\nabla_{\mu}\nabla^{\mu}\Phi-4\nabla_{\mu}\Phi\nabla^{\mu}\Phi.

(3)

Here

and

and

is torsion. Equations (

Crucial insights in string theory were obtained after discovery of dualities. Target space duality (T-duality) relates sigma models in backgrounds with dramatically different curvatures and torsions. Abelian T-duality of bosonic fields
[

]
has been studied thoroughly
[

,

]
, extended to fermionic fields

\mathcal{H}_{MN}\equiv\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{G}_{mn}-\mathcal{B}_{mp}\,\mathcal{G}^{pq}\,\mathcal{B}_{qn}&\mathcal{B}_{mp}\,\mathcal{G}^{pn}\\
-\mathcal{G}^{mp}\,\mathcal{B}_{pn}&\mathcal{G}^{mn}\end{pmatrix}

(4)

Non-Abelian extensions

,
21
]

resolved many issues by identifying Lie bialgebra

that dualization with respect to groups

,

,

)–(

[
31
,
32
]
. In recent papers T-duality is often discussed in terms of DFT
[

, where the long-standing problem of dependence of dual dilaton on original coordinates can be eliminated at the cost of introduction of Killing vector

appearing in generalized SUGRA Equations

,
36

Recently introduced Jacobi–Lie T-duality
[

generalizes Poisson–Lie T-duality by taking Jacobi–Lie bialgebras

as starting point for construction of related sigma models
[

,
38

. Jacobi–Lie bialgebras are in one-to-one correspondence to Leibniz algebras DD

considered in Ref.

In this paper we shall consider Type 1 algebras, i.e. those with

In the absence of spectator fields the background fields are given by constant matrix

4
Examples of atomic Jacobi–Lie T-plurality

To be able to apply Jacobi–Lie T-plurality when looking for Jacobi–Lie models satisfying Supergravity Equations we must first find a model satisfying these equations for at least one of the algebras in the equivalence class. It means finding matrix
E_{0}
and dilaton for given algebra such that the Jacobi–Lie model constructed by procedure described in the preceding section satisfies Supergravity Equations. This may be a difficult task, but in many cases it is tractable.

When analyzing backgrounds obtained in the following sections we often encounter pp-waves. Their metric in the Brinkmann coordinates
(u,v,z_{3},z_{4},\ldots,z_{m-2})
is usually written as

ds^{2}=-K(u,\vec{z})du^{2}+2dudv+d{\vec{z}}^{2},

(34)

where
d{\vec{z}}^{2}
is the Euclidean metric in the transversal space with coordinates
(z_{3},z_{4},\ldots,z_{m-2})
.
Metric (
34
) also has particularly simple Ricci tensor with only one non-vanishing component

R_{uu}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\partial_{3}^{2}K+\partial_{4}^{2}K+\ldots+\partial_{m-2}^{2}K\right).

(35)

Moreover, any curvature invariants calculated for a pp-wave background vanish
[
2
]
. These properties allow us to identify pp-waves in the examples below. More specifically, we will be able to bring many backgrounds to the form (
34
) where

K(u,\vec{z})=K_{i}(u)z_{i}^{2}.

(36)

For further details on pp-waves and their appearance in string theory see e.g.
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
.

In this Section we present examples of atomic plurality of three-dimensional Jacobi–Lie models with initial models satisfying Supergravity Equations.

6
Conclusions

In this paper we have studied Jacobi–Lie T-plurality as a solution-generating technique to Supergravity Equations. Focusing on Type 1 Leibniz algebras, i.e. those with
{f_{b}}^{ba}=0
and
X_{0}=Z^{a}T_{a}=0
, we have found isomorphisms among four- and six-dimensional Jacobi–Lie bialgebras classified in
[
40
]
. These isomorphisms allow us to distinguish Type 1 algebras into several equivalence classes, see Sec.
2
, where Jacobi–Lie T-plurality transformation can be applied.

For each equivalence class we have found an initial Jacobi–Lie model that satisfies Supergravity Equations (
1
)–(
3
) and used Jacobi–Lie T-plurality to transform it to other Jacobi–Lie models satisfying Supergravity Equations. Examples of this procedure are given in Sections
4
and
5
.

It is usually assumed in the literature that initial flux
F_{A}
is constant. For models with
F_{A}
dependent on group coordinates we have used slightly modified formula (
30
) for their transformation, and checked it for pluralities in Sections
4.1
,
5.2
. Working with non-constant
F_{A}
was allowed by particular forms of
C
-matrices that did not bring dependency on dual coordinates in
\hat{F}_{A}
. Beside that we have fixed function
\hat{d}(y)
in (
33
) for models with spectators as

\hat{d}(y)=-\frac{1}{4}\ln\frac{(\det E_{0}(y))^{2}}{\det E_{0S}(y)},\quad E_{0S}=\frac{1}{2}(E_{0}+E_{0}^{T})

to obtain formula for dilaton. All plural backgrounds obtained this way satisfy Supergravity Equations.

Due to the particular choice of initial Jacobi–Lie models all plural backgrounds
except (
63
) have vanishing scalar curvature and simple Ricci tensor. For some of them we were able to bring them to the Brinkmann form, see Tables
4
,
5
and
6
to prove that they are plane-parallel waves. Besides that we have checked that the first
\alpha^{\prime}
correction to the Supergravity Equations
[
45
,
46
,
47
]
vanishes for all backgrounds with vanishing scalar curvature. That indicates that all Jacobi–Lie backgrounds with vanishing scalar curvature derived in this paper are probably pp-waves
[
4
]
.

In some cases Jacobi–Lie T-plurality is a mere coordinate transformation, see backgrounds in sections
4.1
,
4.1.2
,
4.1.3
, in others it relates different pp-waves, see (
38
) and (
42
).

Let us note that the backgrounds presented above could not be simply obtained by Poisson–Lie T-plurality for which
\Delta=\omega=0
and the factor
\operatorname{e}^{-2\omega}
in (
22
) is trivial. In cases where one would get a flat model by Poisson–Lie T-plurality, atomic Jacobi–Lie T-plurality produces conformally flat models.

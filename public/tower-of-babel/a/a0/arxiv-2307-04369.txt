Exact generalized Turán number for K 3 K_{3} versus suspension of P 4 P_{4}
Sayan Mukherjee

Abstract
Let P 4 P_{4} denote the path graph on 4 4 vertices. The suspension of P 4 P_{4} , denoted by P ^ 4 \widehat{P}_{4} , is the graph obtained via adding an extra vertex and joining it to all four vertices of P 4 P_{4} . In this note, we demonstrate that for n ≥ 8 n\geq 8 , the maximum number of triangles in any n n -vertex graph not containing P ^ 4 \widehat{P}_{4} is ⌊ n 2 / 8 ⌋ \left\lfloor n^{2}/8\right\rfloor . Our method uses simple induction along with computer programming to prove a base case of the induction hypothesis. Keywords: generalized Turán problem, suspension of a graph, computer programming. 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 05C35.

The generalized Turán number

The first systematic study of

[
1
]

In more recent years, several researchers have studied the asymptotic behavior of

(see, for example
[
2
,
3
,
5
]
).
It is known that when

\text{ex}(n,K_{3},H)\sim\binom{\chi(H)-1}{3}/{(\chi(H)-1)^{2}}\cdot n^{2}
, where
\chi(H)
denotes the chromatic number of

[
1
,
6
]
.
Alon and Shikhelman
[

]
extensively study the case when

.

Mubayi and the author
[
7
]
initiated the study of

for a simple family of graphs

with

.
For any graph

as the graph obtained from

by adding a new vertex

and joining it with all vertices of

They proceeded to analyze the asymptotic behavior of

One of the several bipartite graphs they consider is the path

on four vertices. It was shown that for any

\frac{n^{2}}{8}-O(1)\leq\text{ex}(n,K_{3},\widehat{P}_{4})<\frac{n^{2}}{8}+3n.

An exact result for sufficiently large

using the technique of progressive induction. In particular, they prove that for a number

They mention that a proof of the upper bound of (

Preliminaries

Throughout the rest of this paper, we assume without loss of generality that all graphs are

This implies that every edge of the graphs considered must lie in a triangle, as we can simply delete edges that do not help forming a triangle.
We also assume that the vertex set of any

Let

and

denote the number of vertices, edges and triangles in

Now we recall some definitions and state a two important lemmas from

]

Definition 2.1

(Triangle-connectivity)
.

For a graph
G

are said to be

if there is a sequence of triangles

,

A subgraph

is triangle-connected if

and
e^{\prime}
are triangle-connected for every edges

and
e^{\prime}
of
H
.

Definition 2.2

(Triangle block)

A subgraph

is a

(or simply a

By definition, the triangle blocks of any graph

are edge-disjoint.

Let

. The following lemma characterizes the triangle blocks of any

Lemma 2.4

(
[

, Section 2)
.

Suppose

is an

-free graph containing no

. Then, we have

.

Proof.

Let

be obtained from

As each triangle of

3
Upper bounds

In order to prove that
\text{ex}(n,K_{3},\widehat{P}_{4})\leq K
for some fixed
n
and
K
, we need to show that any
n
-vertex graph containing at least
K+1
triangles contains a copy of
\widehat{P}_{4}
.

3.1
The cases
5\leq n\leq 8
: brute force

While a case-by-case analysis is tractable by hand for
n=5
for example, we quickly run into several possible configurations while trying to prove
\text{ex}(8,K_{3},\widehat{P}_{4})=8
.
This is where we turn to a computer-generated check.
For example, to prove that all
8
-vertex graphs with more than
9
triangles is
\widehat{P}_{4}
-free, we can assume that
012
and
013
are two triangles in some
8
-vertex graph
G
containing
9
triangles.
Then triangles that have an edge from the set
\{02,03,12,13\}
and have a node from
\{4,5,6,7\}
are excluded from
G
since any of these patterns form a
\widehat{P}_{4}
.
This excludes
16
triangles.
Hence the plausible triangles that
G
may contain other than
012
and
013
are
\binom{8}{3}-18=38
in number.
We generate
\binom{38}{7}\approx 1.26\times 10^{7}
possible graphs, filter out the ones that have exactly
9
triangles, and check for
\widehat{P}_{4}
’s in each of them.

Our program is available at the Github repository in
[
8
]
.
We run
triangle_count_parallel.py
for different pairs of
(n,t)
to figure out both the extremal number and all extremal configurations for
n
-vertex graphs with
t
-triangles.
The results are compiled in the notebook
triangle_count.ipynb
.
Our computation shows that
\text{ex}(n,K_{3},\widehat{P}_{4})=4,5,8,8
for
n=5,6,7,8
, respectively.
The total computation time required for
(n,t)=(8,9)
on 7 threads of an
Intel(R) Core(TM) i7-8550U
laptop processor running at 1.80GHz was around 18 minutes.

3.2
The cases

: identifying

The main idea behind these cases is to follow the steps of the proof in

]

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose
(n,t)\in\{(9,11),(10,13),(11,16)\}
, and
G
is an (edge-minimal)
n
-vertex graph with

triangles.
Then
G
must contain a
\widehat{P}_{4}
.

Proof.

For the sake of contradiction, assume that
G
was
\widehat{P}_{4}
-free.
If
G
was also

-free, then by Lemma
2.4
,
t(G)\leq\lfloor n^{2}/8\rfloor=10,12,15
for

, contradicting our initial assumption on

.

Therefore

must contain a
K_{4}
.
Let this

be induced by vertex subset
S=\{u_{0},u_{1},u_{2},u_{3}\}\subset V(G)
.
Define
X_{i}:=N(u_{i})-S
for

.
As

is a triangle block,
X_{i}\cap X_{j}=\varnothing
for every
i\neq j
.
Further,
\sum_{i=0}^{3}|X_{i}|\leq n-4
.
Without loss of generality assume
|X_{0}|\leq\cdots\leq|X_{3}|
.
Now we consider each case separately.

•

Case 1.
{(n,t)=(9,11)}
:
In this case,
\sum_{i=0}^{3}|X_{i}|\leq 5
.
If

, by edge-minimality we would have

, implying

Thus,
|X_{0}|=|X_{1}|=0
, and by a similar argument,

.
This means the vertex
u_{2}
lies in at most one triangle outside of

.
Let

be obtained by deleting

from

. Clearly

and

.
As

by the discussion in Section

,

has a
\widehat{P}_{4}
, a contradiction.

•

Case 2.
(n,t)=(10,13)
:
Here,
\sum_{i=0}^{3}|X_{i}|\leq 6
.
By a similar analysis as before, we can infer that
|X_{0}|=0
and
|X_{1}|\leq 2
.
If
|X_{1}|=0
, we could consider

, which would have

and

, which would lead us to a

.
Thus, we have

,

, and hence

.
Now, if we consider

and

, again implying that

has a
\widehat{P}_{4}
.

•

Case 3.
(n,t)=(11,16)
:
For this pair of
(n,t)
, we have
\sum_{i=0}^{3}|X_{i}|\leq 7
, implying
|X_{0}|=0
again.
Since

lies in exactly three triangles of

,
G^{\prime}=G-\{u_{0}\}
has

and
t(G^{\prime})=13
, leading us to the previous case.

In either of the three cases, we obtain a contradiction, finishing the proof for these cases.
∎

3.3
The case

Now that we have proved

Let us assume that

We note that a simple case analysis leads to

\displaystyle\lfloor n^{2}/8\rfloor-\lfloor(n-1)^{2}/8\rfloor

\displaystyle\lfloor n^{2}/8\rfloor-\lfloor(n-4)^{2}/8\rfloor

For a subset

By (

\displaystyle|U|=1\implies t(U)

\displaystyle\geq\lfloor n/4\rfloor+1,

\displaystyle|U|=4\implies t(U)

Now, notice that by Lemma

As in the previous section, let

Observe that

\sum_{i=0}^{3}e(X_{i})\geq n-5.

On the other hand, since each

\sum_{i=0}^{3}e(X_{i})\leq\sum_{i=0}^{3}|X_{i}|\leq n-4

(3.3)

This implies that

for at least three

.
Assume that

for

and

.
This also means that

,
and

is a union of triangles and a star on

Further, (

(3.4)

We now continue with a more detailed analysis of the neighborhoods of vertices in

.
In what follows, let

denote the size of

.
For a subset

We now consider two cases.

Case 1:
\sum_{i=0}^{4}x_{i}=n-5

In this case, note that since

Now, observe that for

\sum_{v\in V(G)}\deg v=\sum_{i=0}^{3}\sum_{vwz\in\mathcal{T}(X_{i})}(\deg_{G^{\prime}}v+\deg_{G^{\prime}}w+\deg_{G^{\prime}}z)+\sum_{v\in S}\deg v+2\deg y.

As

For each

\deg v\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\frac{1}{3}(x_{0}+x_{1}+x_{2})+3,&v\not\in S^{\ast},\\

\frac{1}{3}(x_{0}+x_{1}+x_{2})+2,&v\in S^{\ast}-\{c\}.\end{array}\right.

Thus, if
r\geq 1
,

\sum_{v\in X_{3}}\deg v\leq\frac{x_{3}(x_{0}+x_{1}+x_{2})}{3}+3(x_{3}-r)+r+2(r-1)=\frac{x_{3}(x_{0}+x_{1}+x_{2})}{3}+3x_{3}-2,

and if
r=0
,

\sum_{v\in X_{3}}\deg v\leq\frac{x_{3}(x_{0}+x_{1}+x_{2})}{3}+3x_{3}.

We use the latter inequality as it holds for any value of
r
.

•

Finally, we have

\sum_{v\in S}\deg v=(x_{0}+x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3})+12=n+8.

Therefore, (
3.6
) along with
3t(G)\leq\sum_{v\in V(G)}\deg v
, gives us

\displaystyle 3t(G)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{3}\sum_{i<j}x_{i}x_{j}+3(x_{0}+x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3})+n+8.

(3.7)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}(n-4)^{2}-\frac{1}{3}\sum_{i=0}^{3}x_{i}^{2}+4n-4

(3.8)

Observe that by Cauchy-Schwarz,
\sum_{i=0}^{3}x_{i}^{2}\geq\frac{1}{4}(n-4)^{2}
.
Hence, (
3.8
) implies,

3t(G)\leq\frac{1}{4}(n-4)^{2}+4n-4\implies t(G)\leq\frac{1}{12}n(n+8).

By
t(G)\geq\lfloor n^{2}/8\rfloor+1
, this implies
n\leq 14
.
Note that as
n-4=\sum_{i=0}^{3}x_{i}\geq 9+x_{3}
, we would have
x_{3}\leq 1
.
By (
3.4
), this would mean
x_{3}=1
.
However, this contradicts edge-minimality of
G
, as the edge between
u_{3}
and the only vertex of
X_{3}
would not be incident to any triangle in
G
, again leading to a contradiction in this case.

\blacksquare

This completes the proof of the induction step, implying
\text{ex}(n,K_{3},\widehat{P}_{4})\leq\lfloor n^{2}/8\rfloor
for all
n\geq 12
.

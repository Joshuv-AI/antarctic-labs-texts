Steiner distance matrix of caterpillar graphs


Abstract
For a connected graph G := ( V , E ) G:=(V,E) , the Steiner distance d G ​ ( X ) d_{G}(X) among a set of vertices X X is the minimum size among all the connected subgraphs of G G whose vertex set contains X X . The k − k- Steiner distance matrix D k ​ ( G ) D_{k}(G) of G G is a matrix whose rows and columns are indexed by k − k- subsets of V V . For k k -subsets X 1 X_{1} and X 2 X_{2} , the ( X 1 , X 2 ) − (X_{1},X_{2})- entry of D k ​ ( G ) D_{k}(G) is d G ​ ( X 1 ∪ X 2 ) d_{G}(X_{1}\cup X_{2}) . In this paper, we show that the rank of 2 − 2- Steiner distance matrix of a caterpillar graph on N N vertices and with p p pendant veritices is 2 ​ N − p − 1 2N-p-1 .

Lemma 1
.

Let
D:=D_{2}(P_{n})[X,X]
. If
L
is the Laplacian of a path graph on
2n-1
vertices, then

LD+I=u\mathbf{1}^{\prime},

where
u:=(0,\dotsc,0,\underbrace{1}_{n},0,\dotsc,0)\in\mathbb{R}^{2n-1}.

Proof.

The Laplacian matrix of a path graph is a tridiagonal matrix with its main diagonal determined by the vector
(1,2,\dotsc,2,1)
. The first diagonal above and below the main diagonal have all the entries equal to
-1
. Using this, we first note that

(LD)[1]=D[1]-D[2],

(LD)[i]=2D[i]-D[i-1]-D[i+1],~\mbox{for}~2\leq i\leq 2n-2

and

(LD)[2n-1]=D[2n-1]-D[2n-2].

We now compute exact expressions for the rows of
LD
by using (
1
) and (
2
), repeatedly. This is done by considering several cases as listed below:

(i)

It is easy to see that

(LD)[1]=D[1]-D[2]=(-1,0,\dotsc,0).

(ii)

For
2\leq i\leq n-1

\displaystyle(LD)[i]

\displaystyle=2D[i]-D[i-1]-D[i+1]

\displaystyle=2(\underbrace{i,\dotsc,i}_{i},i+1,i+2,\dotsc,n,\dotsc,n)

\displaystyle-(\underbrace{i-1,\dotsc,i-1}_{i-1},i,i+1,i+2,\dotsc,n,\dotsc,n)

\displaystyle-(\underbrace{i+1,\dotsc,i+1}_{i+1},i+2,\dotsc,n,\dotsc,n)

\displaystyle=({0,\dotsc,0},\underbrace{-1}_{i},0,\dotsc,0).

(iii)

Next, we note that

\displaystyle(LD)[n]

\displaystyle=2D[n]-D[n-1]-D[n+1]

\displaystyle=2n\mathbf{1}^{\prime}-(\underbrace{n-1,\dotsc,n-1}_{n-1},n,\dotsc,n)-(\underbrace{n,\dotsc,n}_{n},n-1,\dotsc,n-1)

\displaystyle=({1,\dotsc,1},\underbrace{0}_{n},1,\dotsc,1),

and

\displaystyle(LD)[n+1]

\displaystyle=2D[n+1]-D[n]-D[n+2]

\displaystyle=2(\underbrace{n,\dotsc,n}_{n},n-1,\dotsc,n-1)-n\mathbf{1}^{\prime}

\displaystyle-(\underbrace{n,\dotsc,n}_{n},n-1,{n-2,\dotsc,n-2})

\displaystyle=({0,\dotsc,0},\underbrace{-1}_{n+1},0,\dotsc,0).

(iv)

Let
n+2\leq k\leq 2n-2
. Then

\displaystyle(LD)[k]

\displaystyle=2D[k]-D[k-1]-D[k+1]

\displaystyle=2(\underbrace{n,\dotsc,n}_{n},n-1,n-2,\dotsc,\underbrace{2n-k,\dotsc,2n-k}_{2n-k})

\displaystyle-(\underbrace{n,\dotsc,n}_{n},n-1,n-2,\dotsc,\underbrace{2n-k+1,\dotsc,2n-k+1}_{2n-k+1})

\displaystyle-(\underbrace{n,\dotsc,n}_{n},n-1,n-2,\dotsc,\underbrace{2n-k-1,\dotsc,2n-k-1}_{2n-k-1})

\displaystyle=(0,\dotsc,0,\underbrace{-1}_{k},0,0,\dotsc,0).

(v)

Finally

\displaystyle(LD)[2n-1]

\displaystyle=D[2n-1]-D[2n-2]

\displaystyle=(\underbrace{n,\dotsc,n}_{n},n-1,n-2,\dotsc,1)-(\underbrace{n,\dotsc,n}_{n},n-1,n-2,\dotsc,3,2,2)

\displaystyle=(0,\dotsc,0,-1).

From (i), (ii), (iii), (iv) and (v), we note that except the

row, all other rows of
LD
are equal to the corresonding rows of the matrix
-I
. Hence
LD+I
has all other rows except the

row as zero. Also, the

LD+I=u\mathbf{1}^{\prime},

where
u=(0,\dotsc,0,\underbrace{1}_{n},0,\dotsc,0)^{\prime}.
This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 1
.

Let
D=D_{2}(P_{n})[X,X]
and
L
be the Laplacian of a path graph on
2n-1
vertices. Then

D^{-1}=-L+\frac{1}{n}uu^{\prime},

where
u=(0,\dotsc,0,\underbrace{1}_{n},0,\dotsc,0)\in\mathbb{R}^{2n-1}.

Proof.

Since the
n^{\rm th}
row of
D
is
n\mathbf{1}^{\prime}
, it is easy to see that
u^{\prime}D=n\mathbf{1}^{\prime}
. Now

\displaystyle(-L+\frac{1}{n}uu^{\prime})D

\displaystyle=I-u\mathbf{1}^{\prime}+\frac{1}{n}uu^{\prime}D=I.

Thus,
D
is invertible and

D^{-1}=-L+\frac{1}{n}uu^{\prime}.

The proof is complete.
∎

\displaystyle=2M[n-1]-M[n-2]-M[n]

\displaystyle=(0,\dotsc,0,\underbrace{-1,\dotsc,-1}_{i_{n-1}}),

and

\displaystyle(LM)[n]

\displaystyle=2M[n]-M[n-1]-M[n+1]

\displaystyle=2(n+1)\mathbf{1}^{\prime}-n\mathbf{1}^{\prime}-(n+1)\mathbf{1}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{1}^{\prime}.

(iv)

Suppose
n+1\leq m\leq 2n-2
. Since
M[m]=(n+1)\mathbf{1}^{\prime}
, we have

(LM)[m]=2M[m]-M[m-1]-M[m+1]=(0,\dotsc,0).

(v)

Finally,

(LM)[2n-1]=M[2n-1]-[2n-2]=(0,\dotsc,0).

From (i), (ii), (iii), (iv) and (v), we deduce

(LM)[m]=\begin{cases}(0,\dotsc,0,\underbrace{-1,\dotsc,-1}_{i_{m}},0,\dotsc,0)&~\mbox{if}~1\leq m\leq n-1\\
(1,\dotsc,1)&~\mbox{if}~m=n\\
(0,\dotsc,0)&\mbox{otherwise}\end{cases}

The proof is complete.
∎

Theorem 3
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a caterpillar graph on
N
vertices and let
D_{2}(\mathcal{C})
be its
2-
Steiner distance matrix. Then

{\rm rank}(D_{2}(\mathcal{C}))=2N-p-1,

where
p
denotes the number of pendant vertices of
\mathcal{C}
.

Proof.

Suppose
\mathcal{C}
is labelled as described in Figure
4
. Using Lemma
5
-
8
, we conclude that

{\rm rank}(D_{2}(\mathcal{C}))=2n-1+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}i_{j},

Since

\displaystyle 2N-p-1

\displaystyle=2(n+1+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}i_{j})-(\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}i_{j}+2)-1

\displaystyle=2n-1+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}i_{j},

the proof is complete.

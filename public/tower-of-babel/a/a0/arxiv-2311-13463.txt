1
Introduction and Main Results

A number
n
is
squarefree
if
p^{2}\nmid\;n
for every
p\mid n
, i.e. all the exponents in its prime factorization are exactly one. In contrast, a number
n
is
squarefull
or
powerful
if
p^{2}\mid n
for every prime
p\mid n
, i.e. all the exponents in its prime factorization are at least two. It is well-known that every squarefull number
n
can be factored uniquely as
n=a^{2}b^{3}
for some integer
a
and squarefree number
b
. Let
Q(x)
denote the number of squarefull numbers which are less than or equal to
x
.
Then

Q(x)=\sum_{a^{2}b^{3}\leq x}\mu^{2}(b)

(1)

where
\mu(n)
is the Möbius function. Bateman and Grosswald
[
1
]
proved that

Q(x)=\frac{\zeta(3/2)}{\zeta(3)}x^{1/2}+\frac{\zeta(2/3)}{\zeta(2)}x^{1/3}+O\bigl(x^{1/6}e^{-C_{1}(\log x)^{4/7}(\log\log x)^{-3/7}}\bigr)

(2)

for some absolute constant
C_{1}>0
where
\zeta(s)
is the Riemann zeta function. We are interested in squarefull numbers in short intervals, namely

Q(x+y)-Q(x)\;\;\text{ with }\;\;y=o(x).

Bateman and Grosswald’s result (
2
) yields

Q\bigl(x+x^{1/2+\theta}\bigr)-Q(x)\sim\frac{\zeta(3/2)}{2\zeta(3)}x^{\theta}

(3)

for
1/6=0.1666...\leq\theta<1/2
. The current best range of validity for (
3
) was obtained by Trifonov
[
9
]
, showing that the asymptotic formula (
3
) is true for
19/154=0.1233...<\theta<1/2
.

Inspired by a recent breakthrough work of Gorodetsky, Matomäki, Radziwiłł and Rodgers
[
5
]
on variance of squarefree numbers in short intervals and arithmetic progressions, the author
[
4
]
studied the variance of squarefull numbers in short intervals:

\frac{1}{X}\int_{X}^{2X}\Big|Q(x+y)-Q(x)-\frac{\zeta(3/2)}{\zeta(3)}(\sqrt{x+y}-\sqrt{x})\Big|^{2}dx

and proved

Theorem 1
.

Given
\epsilon>0
,
X>1
and
X^{4\epsilon}\leq H\leq X^{1/2-\epsilon}
. With
y:=2\sqrt{x}H+H^{2}
,

\frac{1}{X}\int_{X}^{2X}\Big|Q(x+y)-Q(x)-\frac{\zeta(3/2)}{\zeta(3)}H\Big|^{2}dx\ll_{\epsilon}H^{16/11+\epsilon}.

Note that
16/11=1.454545\ldots
and
\sqrt{x+y}-\sqrt{x}=H
(which explains our choice of
y
).

This implies that, for any
0<\theta<1/2
, the asymptotic formula (
3
) is true for almost all
x\in[X,2X]
. The author also made the following

Conjecture 1
.

Given
\epsilon>0
,
X>1
and
X^{\epsilon}\leq H\leq X^{1/4-\epsilon}
. With
y:=2\sqrt{x}H+H^{2}
,

\frac{1}{X}\int_{X}^{2X}\Big|Q(x+y)-Q(x)-\frac{\zeta(3/2)}{\zeta(3)}H\Big|^{2}dx\sim\frac{4\zeta(4/3)}{3\zeta(2)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Bigl(\frac{\sin\pi y}{\pi y}\Bigr)^{2}y^{1/3}dy\cdot H^{2/3}.

The reason for the exponent
1/4-\epsilon
is that the secondary main term from (
2
) gives

\frac{\zeta(2/3)}{\zeta(2)}(\sqrt[3]{x+y}-\sqrt[3]{x})\gg\frac{y}{X^{2/3}}\gg\frac{H}{X^{1/6}}\gg H^{1/3}

when
H\geq X^{1/4}
. Assuming the Riemann Hypothesis (RH) or the Lindelöf Hypothesis
\zeta(1/2+it)\ll_{\epsilon}t^{\epsilon}
(LH), the author
[
4
]
proved that Conjecture
1
is true for
X^{\epsilon}\leq H\leq X^{3/14-\epsilon}
. Note that
3/14=0.2142857\ldots
.

In this paper, we obtain unconditionally

Theorem 2
.

Given
0<\epsilon<0.01
. For
X^{\epsilon}\leq H\leq X^{\frac{189}{1046}-2\epsilon}
, we have

\frac{1}{X}\int_{X}^{2X}\Big|Q(x+y)-Q(x)-\frac{\zeta(3/2)}{\zeta(3)}H\Big|^{2}dx=\frac{4\zeta(4/3)}{3\zeta(2)}\int_{0}^{\infty}S(y)^{2}y^{1/3}dy\cdot H^{2/3}+O_{\epsilon}(H^{2/3-\epsilon/16})

where
S(x)=\frac{\sin\pi x}{\pi x}
. Note that
\frac{189}{1046}=0.180688...
.

Conditionally, we have

Theorem 3
.

Conjecture
1
is true under the RH or the LH.

To prove Theorem
2
, we split the squarefull numbers into three ranges according to the size of
b
. For small
b
, we apply smooth weights, count by studying gaps between fractions, and obtain

Proposition 1
.

Given
0<\epsilon<0.01
. For
X^{\epsilon}\leq H\leq X^{3/10-3\epsilon}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{X}\int_{X}^{2X}

\displaystyle\bigg|\mathop{\sum_{x<a^{2}b^{3}\leq x+y}}_{b\leq H^{2/3+\epsilon}}\mu^{2}(b)-H\sum_{b\leq H^{2/3+\epsilon}}\frac{\mu^{2}(b)}{b^{3/2}}\biggr|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1+O(X^{-\epsilon/2}))2H^{2}\sum_{b\leq H^{2/3+\epsilon}}\frac{\mu^{2}(b)}{b^{3}}\sum_{n\geq 1}S\Bigl(\frac{nH}{b^{3/2}}\Bigr)^{2}+O_{\epsilon}(H^{2/3-\epsilon/2})

where
S(x)=\frac{\sin\pi x}{\pi x}
.

Proposition 2
.

Given
0<\epsilon<0.01
. For
1\leq H\leq X^{1/2}
, we have

2H^{2}\sum_{b\leq H^{2/3+\epsilon}}\frac{\mu^{2}(b)}{b^{3}}\sum_{n\geq 1}S\Bigl(\frac{nH}{b^{3/2}}\Bigr)^{2}=H^{2/3}\cdot\frac{4\zeta(4/3)}{3\zeta(2)}\int_{0}^{\infty}S(y)^{2}y^{1/3}dy+O_{\epsilon}(H^{2/3-\epsilon/6})

where
S(x)=\frac{\sin\pi x}{\pi x}
.

For medium size
b
, similar to
[
5
]
, we transform the counting problem into contour integrals over Dirichlet polynomials and
\zeta(s)
, and use mean-value and large-value theorems on Dirichlet polynomials as well as bounds on
\zeta(s)
and exponential sum techniques to obtain

Proposition 3
.

Given
0<\epsilon<0.01
and
\lambda=2/9-\epsilon/3
. For
X^{\epsilon}\leq H\leq X^{\frac{189}{1046}-2\epsilon}
, we have

\frac{1}{X}\int_{X}^{2X}\bigg|\mathop{\sum_{x<a^{2}b^{3}\leq x+y}}_{H^{2/3+\epsilon}<b\leq X^{1/3}/H^{\lambda}}\mu^{2}(b)-H\sum_{H^{2/3+\epsilon}<b\leq X^{1/3}/H^{\lambda}}\frac{\mu^{2}(b)}{b^{3/2}}\biggr|^{2}dx\ll H^{2/3-\epsilon/8}.

For large
b
, we simply estimate the situation trivially with a certain parameter
\lambda
. It is worth mentioning that one can use Erdős-Turán-Koksma inequality on equidistribution and exponential sum techniques to obtain a slightly bigger value for
\lambda
. This can extend the range of
H
for Theorem
2
slightly. Also, the interested readers may apply our proof of Theorem
2
to get a better upper bound (such as
H^{4/3+\epsilon}
) for Theorem
1
. With the resolution of Conjecture
1
under RH or LH, the next step of this study would be to investigate the following more precise variance:

\frac{1}{X}\int_{X}^{2X}\Big|Q(x+y)-Q(x)-\frac{\zeta(3/2)}{\zeta(3)}(\sqrt{x+y}-\sqrt{x})-\frac{\zeta(2/3)}{\zeta(2)}(\sqrt[3]{x+y}-\sqrt[3]{x})\Big|^{2}dx.

The paper is organized as follows. First, we collect some needed lemmas. Then, we perform an initial manipulation on the variance and estimate the large
b
contribution. Next, we deal with small
b
and prove Proposition
1
. After that, we study medium size
b
and obtain Proposition
3
. Then, we finish the proof of Theorem
2
and indicate modifications of the argument to yield Theorem
3
. At the end, we give a proof of Proposition
2
.

Notation.
We use

,

and

We need a lemma on cancellation of oscillatory integral over certain smooth function.

Lemma 1
.

Suppose
K>1
is some large integer. Let
H\geq 1
,
W,Q,L,R>0

QR/\sqrt{H}>1
and
RL>1

Suppose

is a smooth function on
[\alpha,\beta]
such that
|h^{\prime}(t)|\geq R
and

h^{(j)}(t)\ll_{j}HQ^{-j},\;\;\text{ for }j=2,3,...,2K-1.

Then the integral

I=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}w(t)e^{ih(t)}dt

satisfies

I\ll_{K}(\beta-\alpha)W[(QR/\sqrt{H})^{-K}+(RL)^{-K}].

Proof.

This is basically Lemma 8.1 in
[
2
]
or Lemma 1 in
[
4
]
.

Let

\int_{0}^{T}|D(it)|^{2}dt=(T+O(N))\sum_{n=1}^{N}|a_{n}|^{2}.

Proof.

See

for example.

Lemma 3

(Large-value theorem)
.

Let
N,T\geq 1
and
V>0
. Let
F(s)=\sum_{n\leq N}a_{n}n^{-s}

Proof.

This follows from the mean-value theorem and Huxley’s large value theorem. See

Without loss of generality, we may assume that

X^{1/(6-2\delta+2\epsilon)}\leq H
which contradicts our assumption on
H
. Hence, we must have
T>H^{2-\delta+\epsilon}
.

Secondly, consider
B\leq H^{4/3-\delta}
. This implies

T/H^{2/3+\epsilon}<H^{4/3-\delta}\;\;\text{ or }\;\;T<H^{2-\delta+\epsilon}

which contradicts the above range for
T
. Therefore, we can restrict our attention to
H^{2-\delta+\epsilon}<T\leq H^{2+2\epsilon}
and
H^{4/3-\delta}<B<H^{4/3+\epsilon}
. Over these ranges, we have

\frac{1}{H^{1/6+\epsilon}}\leq V\leq\Big|M\bigl(\frac{3}{4}+it\bigr)\Big|\ll\frac{H^{97/42+4\epsilon}}{H^{3-9\delta/4}}+\frac{H^{1/3+\epsilon}}{H^{(2-\delta)/2}}+\frac{\log H}{H^{1-3\delta/4}}\ll H^{9\delta/4-29/42+4\epsilon}

by Lemma
7
and
0.02<\delta<1/3
. This situation cannot happen when
\delta=44/189-3\epsilon
. Summarizing all of the above, we have an acceptable error for
J_{2,B}
and, hence, Proposition
3
when

X^{\epsilon}\leq H\leq X^{\frac{1}{6-2(44/189-3\epsilon)}-\epsilon}=X^{\frac{189}{1046}-2\epsilon}=X^{0.180688...-2\epsilon}.

6
Finishing the proof of Theorem
2

Applying Proposition
2
to Proposition
1
, we have

J_{1}=(1+O(X^{-\epsilon/2}))H^{2/3}\cdot\frac{4\zeta(4/3)}{3\zeta(2)}\int_{0}^{\infty}|S(y)|^{2}y^{1/3}dy+O_{\epsilon}(H^{2/3-\epsilon/6})

when
X^{\epsilon}\leq H\leq X^{3/10-3\epsilon}
. By Proposition
3
, we have
J_{2}\ll H^{2/3-\epsilon/8}
when
X^{\epsilon}\leq H\leq X^{\frac{189}{1046}-2\epsilon}
. Putting these and (
9
) into (
3
) and (
3
), we get

\frac{1}{X}\int_{X}^{2X}\Big|Q(x+y)-Q(x)-\frac{\zeta(3/2)}{\zeta(3)}H\Big|^{2}dx\\
=(1+O(X^{-\epsilon/2}))H^{2/3}\cdot\frac{4\zeta(4/3)}{3\zeta(2)}\int_{0}^{\infty}|S(y)|^{2}y^{1/3}dy+O_{\epsilon}(H^{2/3-\epsilon/16})

when
X^{\epsilon}\leq H\leq X^{\frac{189}{1046}-2\epsilon}
.

7
Proof of Theorem
3

We can do better assuming the RH or the LH. We set the parameter
\lambda=0
here. Then the sum over squarefull numbers
a^{2}b^{3}
in
I_{2}
of (
8
) is empty unless
a=1
. Hence,
I_{2}\ll H^{2}/X^{1/3}+1\ll H^{2/3-\epsilon}
when
H\leq X^{1/4-\epsilon}
. Under either RH or LH, the bound (
35
) becomes

J_{2,B}\ll_{\epsilon}HX^{\epsilon^{2}/4}\sup_{\sqrt{X}/H\leq T\leq X^{2}}\frac{1}{T}\int_{-T}^{T}\big|M(\frac{3}{4}+3it)\big|^{2}\,dt+1

(39)

and we do not need to worry about whether
B\leq T
or not. Using the same notation
S(V)
for large value of Dirichlet polynomial as in Section
5
, we again have the bound (
38
). Suppose the first term in (
38
) dominates, then such contribution to
J_{2,B}
is

\ll_{\epsilon}HX^{\epsilon^{2}/2}\sup_{\sqrt{X}/H\leq T\leq X^{2}}\frac{\sqrt{B}}{T}\ll\frac{H^{2}X^{\epsilon^{2}/2}\sqrt{B}}{\sqrt{X}}\ll_{\epsilon}H^{2/3-\epsilon/4}

when
B\ll X^{1-\epsilon^{2}}/H^{8/3+\epsilon}
. Since
B\ll X^{1/3}
, this is true when
H\leq X^{1/4-\epsilon}
.

Suppose the second term in (
38
) dominates, then such contribution to
J_{2,B}
is

\ll_{\epsilon}HX^{\epsilon^{2}/4}V^{2}\frac{1}{\sqrt{B}}\min\Bigl(\frac{1}{V^{2}},\frac{1}{V^{6}}\Bigr)\ll\frac{HX^{\epsilon^{2}/2}}{\sqrt{B}}\ll H^{2/3-\epsilon/4}

since
H^{2/3+\epsilon}\leq B
and
H\geq X^{\epsilon}
. Applying all these and Propositions
1
and
2
to (
3
) and (
3
), we get

\frac{1}{X}\int_{X}^{2X}\Big|Q(x+y)-Q(x)-\frac{\zeta(3/2)}{\zeta(3)}H\Big|^{2}dx\\
=H^{2/3}\cdot\frac{4\zeta(4/3)}{3\zeta(2)}\int_{0}^{\infty}|S(y)|^{2}y^{1/3}dy+O_{\epsilon}(H^{2/3-\epsilon/16})

when
X^{\epsilon}\leq H\leq X^{1/4-\epsilon}
, and hence the theorem.

8
Proof of Proposition
2

In this section, we focus on finding an asymptotic formula for the diagonal contribution:

2H^{2}\sum_{b\leq H^{2/3+\epsilon}}\frac{\mu^{2}(b)}{b^{3}}\sum_{n\geq 1}S\Bigl(\frac{nH}{b^{3/2}}\Bigr)^{2}\;\;\text{ where }\;\;S(x)=\frac{\sin\pi x}{\pi x}.

(40)

As in
[
5
]
and
[
4
]
, we consider a smoother sum first. Fix
\epsilon>0
and
K_{0}>0
. Suppose that
w:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
satisfies

|w^{(k)}(y)|\leq K_{0}\frac{H^{l\epsilon/4}}{(1+|y|)^{l}}

(41)

for all
k,l\in\{0,1,2,3,4\}
. We claim that

2H^{2}\sum_{b\leq H^{2/3+\epsilon}}\frac{\mu^{2}(b)}{b^{3}}\sum_{n\geq 1}\Big|w\Bigl(\frac{nH}{b^{3/2}}\Bigr)\Big|^{2}=H^{2/3}\frac{4\zeta(4/3)}{3\zeta(2)}\int_{0}^{\infty}|w(y)|^{2}y^{1/3}dy+O_{\epsilon}(H^{2/3-\epsilon/4}).

(42)

Proof of claim: This is very similar to Lemma 8 in
[
5
]
. Firstly, we complete the sum over all
b
. By (
41
) for
k=0
and
l=0,1
, we have

\sum_{n\geq 1}|w(n/\nu)|^{2}\ll\sum_{1\leq n\leq\nu H^{\epsilon/4}}1+\sum_{n>\nu H^{\epsilon/4}}\frac{H^{\epsilon/2}\nu^{2}}{n^{2}}\ll_{\epsilon}\nu H^{\epsilon/4}

for any
\nu>0
. Hence,

2H^{2}\sum_{b>H^{2/3+\epsilon}}\frac{\mu^{2}(b)}{b^{3}}\sum_{n\geq 1}\Big|w\Bigl(\frac{nH}{b^{3/2}}\Bigr)\Big|^{2}\ll_{\epsilon}H^{1+\epsilon/4}\sum_{b>H^{2/3+\epsilon}}\frac{1}{b^{3/2}}\ll H^{2/3-\epsilon/4}.

Thus, the left hand side of (
42
) is

2H^{2}\sum_{b\geq 1}\frac{\mu^{2}(b)}{b^{3}}\sum_{n\geq 1}\Big|w\Bigl(\frac{nH}{b^{3/2}}\Bigr)\Big|^{2}+O_{\epsilon}(H^{2/3-\epsilon/4}).

(43)

Secondly, we use contour integration to simplify (
43
). Define
g(x):=|w(e^{x})|^{2}e^{x}
, which is smooth and decays exponentially as
|x|\rightarrow\infty
by (
41
). Its Fourier transform is given by

\hat{g}(\xi)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|w(e^{x})|^{2}e^{x}e^{-2\pi ix\xi}dx=\int_{0}^{\infty}|w(y)|^{2}y^{-2\pi i\xi}dy,

and one can show that (i)
\hat{g}(\xi)
is entire and (ii)
\hat{g}(\xi)=O(H^{2\epsilon}/(|\xi|+1)^{3})
uniformly for
|\Im(\xi)|<1/(2\pi)
by (
41
) and standard partial summation argument. Then Fourier inversion gives, for
r>0
,

|w(r)|^{2}=r^{-1}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(c)}r^{s}\hat{g}\Bigl(\frac{s}{2\pi i}\Bigr)ds,

where the integral is over
\Re(s)=c
with
-1<c<1
. Hence, taking
c=-1/4
,

2H^{2}\sum_{b\geq 1}\frac{\mu^{2}(b)}{b^{3}}\sum_{n\geq 1}\Big|w\Bigl(\frac{nH}{b^{3/2}}\Bigr)\Big|^{2}=\frac{H}{i\pi}\sum_{b\geq 1}\frac{\mu^{2}(b)}{b^{3/2}}\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{n}\int_{(-1/4)}H^{s}n^{s}b^{-3s/2}\hat{g}\Bigl(\frac{s}{2\pi i}\Bigr)ds.

Due to absolute convergence of the series, one can take the sums inside the integral and the above becomes

\frac{H}{i\pi}\int_{(-1/4)}H^{s}\zeta(1-s)\frac{\zeta(\frac{3}{2}(1+s))}{\zeta(3(1+s))}\hat{g}\Bigl(\frac{s}{2\pi i}\Bigr)ds

by (
5
). Because of the bounds on
\hat{g}
and
\zeta(1/2+it)\ll t^{1/6}\log^{2}t
, we can push the contour integral above to the left to an integral over
\Re(s)=-2/3
. Picking out a residue at
s=-1/3
from the singularity of
\zeta(\frac{3}{2}(1+s))
, the above simplifies to

\frac{4\zeta(4/3)}{3\zeta(2)}\hat{g}\Bigl(-\frac{1}{6\pi i}\Bigr)H^{2/3}+O(H^{1/3+2\epsilon})=\frac{4\zeta(4/3)}{3\zeta(2)}\int_{0}^{\infty}|w(y)|^{2}y^{1/3}dy\cdot H^{2/3}+O(H^{1/3+2\epsilon}).

This and (
43
) finish the proof of the claim. To deal with (
40
), we introduce the function

w(y):=S(y)h\Bigl(\frac{y}{H^{\epsilon/4}}\Bigr)

where
h
is a smooth bump function such that
h(x)=1
for
|x|\leq 1
and
h(x)=0
for
|x|\geq 2
(for example, one can take
h(x)=\sigma_{10,2,0.1}^{+}(x+3)
). Then one can check that
w(y)
satisfies the hypothesis (
41
). For such
w
,

\displaystyle 2H^{2}\sum_{b\leq H^{2/3+\epsilon}}

\displaystyle\frac{\mu^{2}(b)}{b^{3}}\sum_{n\geq 1}\biggl(S\Bigl(\frac{nH}{b^{3/2}}\Bigr)^{2}-w\Bigl(\frac{nH}{b^{3/2}}\Bigr)^{2}\biggr)\ll H^{2}\mathop{\sum_{b\leq H^{2/3+\epsilon},n\geq 1}}_{nH^{1-\epsilon/4}\geq b^{3/2}}\frac{1}{b^{3}}\frac{1}{(nH/b^{3/2})^{2}}

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{b\leq H^{2/3-\epsilon/6}}\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{n^{2}}+\sum_{b>H^{2/3-\epsilon/6}}\sum_{n\geq b^{3/2}/H^{1-\epsilon/4}}\frac{1}{n^{2}}\ll_{\epsilon}H^{2/3-\epsilon/6}.

(44)

On the other hand,

\int_{0}^{\infty}|S(y)|^{2}y^{1/3}dy-\int_{0}^{\infty}|w(y)|^{2}y^{1/3}dy\ll\int_{H^{\epsilon/4}}^{\infty}y^{-5/3}dy\ll_{\epsilon}H^{-\epsilon/6}.

(45)

Combining (
42
), (
8
) and (
45
), we have

2H^{2}\sum_{b\leq H^{2/3+\epsilon}}\frac{\mu^{2}(b)}{b^{3}}\sum_{n\geq 1}\Big|S\Bigl(\frac{nH}{b^{3/2}}\Bigr)\Big|^{2}=H^{2/3}\frac{4\zeta(4/3)}{3\zeta(2)}\int_{0}^{\infty}|S(y)|^{2}y^{1/3}dy+O_{\epsilon}(H^{2/3-\epsilon/6})

which gives Proposition
2
.

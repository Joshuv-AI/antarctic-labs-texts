Epilegomena to the study of semiclassical orthogonal polynomials
K. Castillo, D. Mbouna

Abstract
. In his monograph [Classical and quantum orthogonal polynomials in one variable, Cambridge University Press, 2005 (paperback edition 2009)], Ismail conjectured that certain structure relations involving the Askey-Wilson operator characterize proper subsets of the set of all 𝒟 q \mathcal{D}_{q} -classical orthogonal polynomials, here to be understood as the Askey-Wilson polynomials and their limit cases. In this paper we give two characterization theorems for 𝒟 q \mathcal{D}_{q} -semiclassical (and classical) orthogonal polynomials in consonance with the pioneering works by Maroni [Ann. Mat. Pura. Appl. (1987)] and Bonan, Lubinsky, and Nevai [SIAM J. Math. Anal. 18 (1987)] for the standard derivative, re-establishing in this context the perfect “symmetry” between the standard derivative and the Askey-Wilson operator. As an application, we present a sequence of 𝒟 q \mathcal{D}_{q} -semiclassical orthogonal polynomials of class two that disproves Ismail’s conjectures. Further results are presented for Hahn’s operator.

2.
Preliminary results

Let
\mathcal{P}^{*}
be the set of all linear forms on
\mathcal{P}
and let
\mathcal{P}_{n}
be the subspace of
\mathcal{P}
of all polynomials with degree less than or equal to
n
. Set
\mathcal{P}_{-1}=\{0\}
. A free system in
\mathcal{P}
is a sequence
(Q_{n})_{n\geq 0}
such that

Q_{n}\in\mathcal{P}_{n}\setminus\mathcal{P}_{n-1}
for each
n
.
A free system
(P_{n})_{n\geq 0}
is called OP
with respect to
{\bf u}\in\mathcal{P}^{*}
if

\langle{\bf u},P_{n}P_{m}\rangle=h_{n}\delta_{n,m}\quad(m=0,1,\ldots;\,h_{n}\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}),

\langle{\bf u},f\rangle
being the action of
{\bf u}
on
f
.
{\bf u}
is called regular if there exists an OP with respect to it. Recall that a (monic) OP,
(P_{n})_{n\geq 0}
, satisfies the following recurrence relation:

(6)

\displaystyle xP_{n}(x)=P_{n+1}(x)+B_{n}P_{n}(x)+C_{n}P_{n-1}(x)\qquad(B_{n}\in\mathbb{C},\,C_{n+1}\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}),

with initial conditions
P_{-1}=0
and
P_{0}=1
. Hence it follows that

B_{n}=\frac{\langle\mathbf{u},xP^{2}_{n}(x)\rangle}{\langle\mathbf{u},P^{2}_{n}(x)\rangle},\qquad C_{n}=\frac{\langle\mathbf{u},P^{2}_{n}\rangle}{\langle\mathbf{u},P^{2}_{n-1}\rangle}.

(Of course, there is no loss of generality in assuming
C_{0}=0
.) Since the elements of
\mathcal{P}^{*}
are completely determined by its action on a system of generators of
\mathcal{P}
, we say that
\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{v}
if and only if

\mathbf{u}_{n}=\left\langle{\mathbf{u},x^{n}}\right\rangle=\left\langle{\mathbf{v},x^{n}}\right\rangle

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. In the set
\mathcal{P}^{*}
, addition and multiplications by scalars can be defined by

\displaystyle\left\langle{\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{v},x^{n}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle{\mathbf{u},x^{n}}\right\rangle+\left\langle{\mathbf{v},x^{n}}\right\rangle,

\displaystyle\left\langle{c\mathbf{u},x^{n}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=c\left\langle{\mathbf{u},x^{n}}\right\rangle\qquad(c\in\mathbb{C}),

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
\mathcal{P}^{*}
, endowed with these operations, is a vector space over
\mathcal{P}
. In
\mathcal{P}^{*}
, the identity for the additivity is denoted by
\mathbf{0}
and called the zero. The zero is therefore defined by the relation
\left\langle{\mathbf{0},x^{n}}\right\rangle=0
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Note that
f\,\mathbf{u}=g\,\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{0}

(f,g\in\mathcal{P})
if and only if
\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{0}
.
The left multiplication of
{\bf u}
by
f\in\mathcal{P}
, denoted by
f{\bf u}:\mathcal{P}\to\mathcal{P}
,
is the form defined by

\langle f{\bf u},x^{n}\rangle=\langle{\bf u},fx^{n}\rangle,

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. The division of
\mathbf{u}
by a polynomial, denoted by
(x-c)^{-1}\mathbf{u}:\mathcal{P}\to\mathcal{P}
, is the form defined by

\left\langle{(x-c)^{-1}\mathbf{u},f}\right\rangle=\left\langle{\mathbf{u},\frac{f(x)-f(c)}{x-c}}\right\rangle\qquad(c\in\mathbb{C};\,f\in\mathcal{P}).

Define also
\mathbf{\delta}_{c}:\mathcal{P}\to\mathbb{C}
by
\mathbf{\delta}_{c}f(x)=f(c)
. We check at once that

(x-c)((x-c)^{-1}\mathbf{u})=\mathbf{u},\qquad(x-c)^{-1}((x-c)\mathbf{u})=\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}_{0}\,\delta_{c}.

\mathcal{P}
may be endowed with an appropriate strict inductive limit topology such that the algebraic and
the topological dual spaces of
\mathcal{P}
coincide (see
[
29

(7)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{*}=\mathcal{P}^{\prime}.

Given a free system
(Q_{n})_{n\geq 0}
, the corresponding dual basis is a sequence of linear forms

such that

\langle{\bf a}_{n},Q_{m}\rangle=\delta_{n,m},

and so, for an OP

is explicitly given by

{\bf a}_{n}=\frac{P_{n}}{\langle{\bf a}_{n},P^{2}_{n}\rangle}\mathbf{u}.

In the sense of the weak dual topology, it would be easy to explicitly build bases in dual space. Indeed,

\mathbf{u}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\left\langle{\mathbf{u},Q_{j}}\right\rangle\mathbf{a}_{j}\qquad(\mathbf{u}\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime});

\mathcal{P}_{\bf u}
is the set of all pairs
(\phi,\psi)
of nonzero polynomials such that (
9
) holds. (Note that when
s=0
we have the classical linear forms.)
The pair
(\phi,\psi)\in\mathcal{P}_{\bf u}
where the class of
{\bf u}
is attained is unique up to a constant factor. OP with respect to a (
\mathbf{D}_{q}
-)semiclassical form of class
s
are called a (
\mathcal{D}_{q}
-)semiclassical orthogonal polynomials of class
s
.

We end this section with the following definition.

Definition 2.3
.

We call a pair of polynomials
(\phi,\psi)
,
\phi(x)=a_{p}\,x^{p}+(\text{lower degree}

\text{terms})
and
\psi(x)=b_{q}\,x^{q}+(\text{lower degree terms})

(p\in\mathbb{N},q\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\})
, admissible if
p-1\not=q
or
a_{p}\,\gamma_{n}+b_{q}\,\alpha_{n-1}\not=0
whenever

.

Remark 2.1
.

We emphasize that from the point of view of
[
9
]

. If we consider the lattice

, in the notation of
[
9
]
, we have
\alpha_{n-1}=1
and
\gamma_{n}=n
, and so Definition
2.3
reduces the admissibility condition for the standard derivative
(
see
[
25
, p. 119]
and
[

, p. 46]
)
.

3.
Characterization theorems

The next theorem characterize
\mathcal{D}_{q}
-classical and
\mathcal{D}_{q}
-semiclassical orthogonal polynomials from the structure relation (
4
).

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
{\bf u}\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
be regular and let
(P_{n})_{n\geq 0}
denote the corresponding sequence of orthogonal polynomials. The following conditions are equivalent:

i)

There exist three nonzero polynomials,
\phi
,
\psi
and
\rho
,
(\psi,\rho)
being an admissible pair, such that

\displaystyle{\bf D}_{q}(\phi{\bf u})=\psi{\bf u},\qquad{\bf S}_{q}(\phi{\bf u})=\rho{\bf u}.

ii)

There exist
s\in\mathbb{N}
, complex numbers
(a_{n,j})_{j=0}^{n}
, and a polynomial
\phi
such that

(19)

\displaystyle\phi\mathcal{D}_{q}P_{n}=\sum_{j=n-s}^{n+\deg\phi-1}a_{n,j}P_{j},

with
a_{n,n-s}\neq 0
for all
n\geq s
.

Proof.

i)\implies ii)
: Write
\rho(x)=a_{r}\,x^{r}+(\text{lower degree}

\text{terms})
and
\psi(x)=b_{s}\,x^{s}+(\text{lower degree terms})

(r\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\},p\in\mathbb{N})
. Set

d_{n}=a_{r}\alpha_{n-1}+b_{s}\gamma_{n}
. Clearly,

\displaystyle\phi\mathcal{D}_{q}P_{n}=\sum_{j=0}^{n+\deg\phi-1}a_{n,j}P_{j},

where

\displaystyle a_{n,j}=\frac{\left\langle{\bf u},\phi P_{j}\mathcal{D}_{q}P_{n}\right\rangle}{\left\langle{\bf u},P_{j}^{2}\right\rangle}.

From (
13
) we get

\displaystyle\left\langle{\bf u},P_{j}^{2}\right\rangle a_{n,j}

\displaystyle=\left\langle{\bf u},\phi P_{j}\mathcal{D}_{q}P_{n}\right\rangle=\left\langle\phi{\bf u},P_{j}\mathcal{D}_{q}P_{n}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\phi{\bf u},\mathcal{D}_{q}\left(\left(\mathcal{S}_{q}P_{j}-\alpha^{-1}\texttt{U}_{1}\mathcal{D}_{q}P_{j}\right)P_{n}\right)-\alpha^{-1}\mathcal{S}_{q}(P_{n}\mathcal{D}_{q}P_{j})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\left\langle{\bf D}_{q}(\phi{\bf u}),\left(\mathcal{S}_{q}P_{j}-\alpha^{-1}\texttt{U}_{1}\mathcal{D}_{q}P_{j}\right)P_{n}\right\rangle-\alpha^{-1}\left\langle{\bf S}_{q}(\phi{\bf u}),P_{n}\mathcal{D}_{q}P_{j}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\left\langle{\bf u},\left(\psi\left(\mathcal{S}_{q}P_{j}-\alpha^{-1}\texttt{U}_{1}\mathcal{D}_{q}P_{j}\right)+\alpha^{-1}\rho\mathcal{D}_{q}P_{j}\right)P_{n}\right\rangle.

There is no loss of generality in assuming
r-1\leq s
. For
r-1<s
, we have

\displaystyle-\alpha\left\langle{\bf u},P_{j}^{2}\right\rangle a_{n,j}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}a_{r}\alpha_{n-s-1}\left\langle{\bf u},P_{n}^{2}\right\rangle,&j=n-s,\\[7.0pt]
0,&j<n-s.\end{array}\right.

and for
r-1=s
, we get

\displaystyle-\alpha\left\langle{\bf u},P_{j}^{2}\right\rangle a_{n,j}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}d_{n-s}\left\langle{\bf u},P_{n}^{2}\right\rangle,&j=n-s,\\[7.0pt]
0,&j<n-s.\end{array}\right.

Hence
a_{n,n-s}\neq 0
, for
n\geq s
and
ii)
follows.

ii)\implies i)
: Let
({\bf a}_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be the dual basis associated to
(P_{n})_{n\geq 0}
. Note that
ii)
yields

\displaystyle\left\langle{\bf D}_{q}(\phi{\bf a}_{n}),P_{j}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\left\langle{\bf a}_{n},\phi\mathcal{D}_{q}P_{j}\right\rangle=-\sum_{l=j-s}^{j+\deg\phi-1}a_{j,l}\left\langle{\bf a}_{n},P_{l}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-a_{j,n},&n-\deg\phi+1\leq j\leq n+s,\\[7.0pt]
0,&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

Writing

\displaystyle{\bf D}_{q}(\phi{\bf a}_{n})=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\left\langle{\bf D}_{q}(\phi{\bf a}_{n}),P_{j}\right\rangle{\bf a}_{j},

in the sense of the weak dual topology in
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
, and taking into account that
\left\langle{\bf u},P_{n}^{2}\right\rangle{\bf a}_{n}=P_{n}{\bf u}
, we get

\displaystyle{\bf D}_{q}(\phi P_{n}{\bf u})=R_{n+s}{\bf u},\quad R_{n+s}=-\left\langle{\bf u},P_{n}^{2}\right\rangle\sum_{j=n-\deg\phi+1}^{n+s}\frac{a_{j,n}}{\left\langle{\bf u},P_{j}^{2}\right\rangle}P_{j}.

(Note that
R_{n+s}
is a polynomial of degree
n+s
.) Taking
n=0
and
n=1
in the above expression, we have

(20)

\displaystyle{\bf D}_{q}(\phi{\bf u})

\displaystyle=R_{s}{\bf u},

(21)

\displaystyle{\bf D}_{q}(\phi P_{1}{\bf u})

\displaystyle=R_{s+1}{\bf u}.

From (
21
), and using (
14
) and (
20
), we obtain

\displaystyle\alpha R_{s+1}{\bf u}

\displaystyle=\alpha{\bf D}_{q}(\phi P_{1}{\bf u})=\big(\alpha\mathcal{S}_{q}P_{1}-\texttt{U}_{1}\mathcal{D}_{q}P_{1}\big){\bf D}_{q}(\phi{\bf u})+\mathcal{D}_{q}P_{1}{\bf S}_{q}(\phi{\bf u})

\displaystyle=(x-\alpha B_{0})R_{s}{\bf u}+{\bf S}_{q}(\phi{\bf u}).

Hence

(22)

\displaystyle{\bf S}_{q}(\phi{\bf u})=\big(\alpha R_{s+1}-(x-\alpha B_{0})R_{s}\big){\bf u}.

Note that
\alpha R_{s+1}-(x-\alpha B_{0})R_{s}\neq 0
. To obtain a contradiction, suppose that the last assertion is false. Consequently,
\phi{\bf u}=0
with
\phi\neq 0
and
{\bf u}
regular, which is impossible. We claim that
\big(R_{s},\alpha R_{s+1}-(x-\alpha B_{0})R_{s}\big)
is an admissible pair. According to Definition
2.3
, this is equivalent to showing that

d_{n}=a_{r}\alpha_{n-1}+b_{s}\gamma_{n}\neq 0
,
where

a_{r}=-\frac{\left\langle{\bf u},P_{0}^{2}\right\rangle}{\left\langle{\bf u},P_{s}^{2}\right\rangle}a_{s,0},\quad b_{s}=-\alpha\frac{\left\langle{\bf u},P_{1}^{2}\right\rangle}{\left\langle{\bf u},P_{s+1}^{2}\right\rangle}a_{s+1,1}-a_{r}.

For
\deg\,(\alpha R_{s+1}-(x-\alpha B_{0})R_{s})<s+1
, we have
b_{s}=0
, and so
d_{n}=a_{r}\alpha_{n-1}\neq 0
. Assume
\deg\,(\alpha R_{s+1}-(x-\alpha B_{0})R_{s})=s+1
. (Note that in this case
b_{s}\neq 0
.) We now claim

(23)

\displaystyle\phi\mathcal{S}_{q}P_{n}=\displaystyle\sum_{j=n-s-1}^{n+\deg\phi}\widetilde{a}_{n,j}P_{j},\quad\widetilde{a}_{n,n-s-1}=-\alpha a_{n,n-s}C_{n-s}+a_{n-1,n-s-1}C_{n},

where we have assumed that
(P_{n})_{n\geq 0}
satisfies (
6
). Indeed, apply
\phi\mathcal{D}_{q}
to (
6
) to obtain

\displaystyle\phi(x)\mathcal{S}_{q}P_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=\phi(x)\left(-\alpha x\mathcal{D}_{q}P_{n}(x)+\mathcal{D}_{q}P_{n+1}(x)+B_{n}\mathcal{D}_{q}P_{n}(x)+C_{n}\mathcal{D}_{q}P_{n-1}(x)\right)

\displaystyle=-\alpha x\sum_{j=n-s}^{n+\deg\phi-1}a_{n,j}P_{j}(x)+\sum_{j=n-s+1}^{n+\deg\phi}a_{n+1,j}P_{j}(x)

\displaystyle\quad+B_{n}\sum_{j=n-s}^{n+\deg\phi-1}a_{n,j}P_{j}(x)+C_{n}\sum_{j=n-s-1}^{n+\deg\phi-2}a_{n-1,j}P_{j}(x),

and (
23
) follows by using (
6
). We also claim that

(24)

\displaystyle a_{n,n-s}=\left(k_{1}q^{n/2}+k_{2}q^{-n/2}\right)\prod_{j=n-s+1}^{n}C_{j},\qquad n\geq s.

(25)

\displaystyle 2\widetilde{a}_{n,n-s-1}=-(q^{1/2}-q^{-1/2})\left(k_{1}q^{n/2}-k_{2}q^{-n/2}\right)\prod_{j=n-s}^{n}C_{j},\qquad n\geq s+1,

where
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
are complex numbers. Indeed, apply
\phi\mathcal{S}_{q}
to (
6
) and use (
12
) to obtain

\displaystyle\texttt{U}_{2}(x)\phi(x)\mathcal{D}_{q}P_{n}(x)+\alpha x\phi(x)\mathcal{S}_{q}P_{n}(x)

\displaystyle\quad=\phi(x)\mathcal{S}_{q}P_{n+1}(x)+B_{n}\phi(x)\mathcal{S}_{q}P_{n}(x)+C_{n}\phi(x)\mathcal{S}_{q}P_{n-1}(x).

Combining (
19
), (
23
) and (
6
), we obtain

\sum_{j=n-s-2}^{n+\deg\phi+1}r_{n,j}P_{j}=0.

Since
(P_{n})_{n\geq 0}
is a free system, we have
r_{n,j}=0
for all
j
. By identifying the coefficient of
P_{n-s-2}
, we find

\displaystyle 0=r_{n,n-s-2}=(\alpha^{2}-1)a_{n,n-s}C_{n-s}C_{n-s-1}+\alpha\widetilde{a}_{n,n-s-1}C_{n-s-1}-\widetilde{a}_{n-1,n-s-2}C_{n}.

Using the expression of
\widetilde{a}_{n,n-s-1}
given in (
23
), we get the following second order linear homogeneous equation:

(26)

\displaystyle y(n)-2\alpha y(n-1)+y(n-2)=0,

where

y(n)=\displaystyle\frac{a_{n,n-s}}{\prod_{j=n-s+1}^{n}C_{j}},\qquad n\geq s.

Note that
q^{1/2}
and
q^{-1/2}
are the solutions of the characteristic equation of (
26
) and, therefore, we find

\displaystyle y(n)=k_{1}q^{n/2}+k_{2}q^{-n/2},

and (
24
) follows. Moreover, from the expression of
\widetilde{a}_{n,n-s-1}
given in (
23
), (
25
) follows. Finally, using (
24
) and (
25
), we obtain

\displaystyle d_{n}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\left\langle{\bf u},P_{0}^{2}\right\rangle}{\left\langle{\bf u},P_{s}^{2}\right\rangle}a_{s,0}\alpha_{n-1}+\frac{\left\langle{\bf u},P_{0}^{2}\right\rangle}{\left\langle{\bf u},P_{s+1}^{2}\right\rangle}\widetilde{a}_{s+1,0}\gamma_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left\langle{\bf u},P_{0}^{2}\right\rangle}{\left\langle{\bf u},P_{s+1}^{2}\right\rangle}\left(-a_{s,0}C_{s+1}\alpha_{n-1}+\widetilde{a}_{s+1,0}\gamma_{n}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\left(2\alpha_{n-1}(k_{1}q^{s/2}+k_{2}q^{-s/2})+(q^{n/2}-q^{-n/2})(k_{1}q^{(s+1)/2}-k_{2}q^{-(s+1)/2})\right)

\displaystyle=-\alpha\big(k_{1}q^{(n+s)/2}+k_{2}q^{-(n+s)/2}\big)

\displaystyle=-\alpha\,a_{n+s,n}\prod_{j=1}^{n+s}C_{j}^{-1}\neq 0,

and so
(R_{s},\alpha R_{s+1}-(x-\alpha B_{0})R_{s})
is an admissible pair. Thus,
i)
follows from (
20
) and (
22
), and the theorem is proved.

Remark 3.1
.

A regular linear form
{\bf u}
satisfying Theorem
3.1

i)
is classical or semiclassical. Indeed, using (
18
) and (
16
) we get

\displaystyle{\bf D}_{q}(\rho{\bf u})

\displaystyle={\bf D}_{q}{\bf S}_{q}(\phi{\bf u})=(\alpha-\alpha^{-1}){\bf S}_{q}{\bf D}_{q}(\phi{\bf u})+\alpha^{-1}\texttt{U}_{1}{\bf D}_{q}^{2}(\phi{\bf u})

\displaystyle=(\alpha-\alpha^{-1}){\bf S}_{q}(\psi{\bf u})+\alpha^{-1}\texttt{U}_{1}{\bf D}_{q}(\psi{\bf u})

\displaystyle=(\alpha-\alpha^{-1}){\bf S}_{q}(\psi{\bf u})+\alpha^{-1}(\alpha{\bf D}_{q}(\texttt{U}_{1}\psi{\bf u})-(\alpha^{2}-1){\bf S}_{q}(\psi{\bf u}))

\displaystyle=\alpha{\bf S}_{q}(\psi{\bf u})+{\bf D}_{q}(\texttt{U}_{1}\psi{\bf u}).

Thus
{\bf D}_{q}\big((\rho-\texttt{U}_{1}\psi){\bf u}\big)=\alpha{\bf S}_{q}(\psi{\bf u})
as claimed. Theorem
3.1
is the analogue of
[
21
, Theorem 3.1]

(
see also
[
25
]
)
, from which a distributional version of
[
5
, Theorem 1.1]
follows.

Although Theorem
3.1
could be very useful in many situations it is little precise regarding the classical or semiclassical character of the linear form. In the following theorem we will be more precise in this sense, but in counterpart we lose the direct connection with the equation (
9
).

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
{\bf u}\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
be regular and let
(P_{n})_{n\geq 0}
denote the corresponding sequence of orthogonal polynomials satisfying (
6
). Suppose that there exist a nonnegative integer
s
, complex numbers
(a_{n,j})_{j=0}^{n}
, and a polynomial
\phi
such that

(27)

\displaystyle\phi\mathcal{D}_{q}P_{n}=\sum_{j=n-s}^{n+\deg\phi-1}a_{n,j}P_{j},

with
a_{n.n-s}\neq 0
for all
n\geq s
.
Then there exist
\Phi\in\mathcal{P}_{s+1}\setminus\mathcal{P}_{-1}
and
\Psi\in\mathcal{P}_{s}\setminus\mathcal{P}_{-1}
such that

(28)

\displaystyle\Phi\,{\bf D}_{q}{\bf u}=\Psi\,{\bf S}_{q}{\bf u},

where
\Phi
and
\Psi
never have more than
s-1
common zeros. If
\Phi
and
\Psi
have
s-1
common zeros, then
{\bf u}
is
{\bf D}_{q}
-classical. Otherwise,
{\bf u}
is
{\bf D}_{q}
-semiclassical of class
s-1-r
,
r
being the number of common zeros of
\Phi
and
\Psi
.

Proof.

We can now proceed analogously to the proof of Theorem
3.1
to obtain

\displaystyle{\bf D}_{q}(\phi P_{n}{\bf u})=-Q_{n+s}{\bf u},\quad Q_{n+s}=\left\langle{\bf u},P_{n}^{2}\right\rangle\sum_{j=n-\deg\phi+1}^{n+s}\frac{a_{j,n}}{\left\langle{\bf u},P_{j}^{2}\right\rangle}P_{j}.

(Note that
Q_{n+s}
is a polynomial of degree
n+s
.) Taking
n=0
and
n=1
, in the above expression, we have

(29)

\displaystyle{\bf D}_{q}(\phi{\bf u})

\displaystyle=-Q_{s}{\bf u},

(30)

\displaystyle{\bf D}_{q}(\phi P_{1}{\bf u})

\displaystyle=-Q_{s+1}{\bf u}.

Using (
29
) and (
14
), we have

\displaystyle-\alpha Q_{s+1}(x){\bf u}

\displaystyle=\alpha{\bf D}_{q}\big(P_{1}(x)\phi{\bf u})=(x-\alpha B_{0}){\bf D}_{q}(\phi{\bf u})+{\bf S}_{q}(\phi{\bf u})

\displaystyle=-(x-\alpha B_{0})Q_{s}(x){\bf u}+{\bf S}_{q}(\phi{\bf u}),

and so

((x-\alpha B_{0})Q_{s}(x)-\alpha Q_{s+1}(x)){\bf u}={\bf S}_{q}(\phi{\bf u}).

Applying
{\bf D}_{q}
to the above equation, and using (
18
), (
29
), and (
16
), we can assert that

\displaystyle-\alpha{\bf D}_{q}(((x-\alpha B_{0})Q_{s}(x)-\alpha Q_{s+1}(x)){\bf u})

\displaystyle\quad=-\alpha{\bf D}_{q}{\bf S}_{q}\big(\phi{\bf u}\big)=-(2\alpha^{2}-1){\bf S}_{q}{\bf D}_{q}\big(\phi{\bf u}\big)-\texttt{U}_{1}{\bf D}_{q}^{2}\big(\phi{\bf u}\big)

\displaystyle\quad=(2\alpha^{2}-1){\bf S}_{q}\big(Q_{s}(x){\bf u}\big)+\texttt{U}_{1}{\bf D}_{q}\big(Q_{s}(x){\bf u}\big)=\alpha^{2}{\bf S}_{q}\big(Q_{s}(x){\bf u}\big)+\alpha{\bf D}_{q}\big(\texttt{U}_{1}Q_{s}(x){\bf u}\big).

Therefore,

(31)

\displaystyle{\bf D}_{q}\big((Q_{s+1}(x)-(\alpha x-B_{0})Q_{s}(x)){\bf u}\big)={\bf S}_{q}(Q_{s}(x){\bf u}),

and
\mathbf{u}
is classical or semiclassical of class at most
s-1
. Let us now rewrite (
31
) in the form (
28
) to distinguish between cases. Using (
14
) and (
15
), (
31
) becomes

\displaystyle(\alpha\mathcal{S}_{q}R_{s+1}-\texttt{U}_{1}\mathcal{D}_{q}R_{s+1}+\big(\texttt{U}_{1}^{2}-\alpha^{2}\texttt{U}_{2}\big)\mathcal{D}_{q}Q_{s}){\bf D}_{q}{\bf u}

\displaystyle\quad=\big(\texttt{U}_{1}\mathcal{D}_{q}Q_{s}+\alpha\mathcal{S}_{q}Q_{s}-\mathcal{D}_{q}R_{s+1}\big){\bf S}_{q}{\bf u},

where
R_{s+1}(x)=Q_{s+1}(x)-(\alpha x-B_{0})Q_{s}(x)
. Consequently, (
28
) follows with

(32)

\displaystyle\Phi

\displaystyle=\alpha\mathcal{S}_{q}R_{s+1}-\texttt{U}_{1}\mathcal{D}_{q}R_{s+1}+(\texttt{U}_{1}^{2}-\alpha^{2}\texttt{U}_{2})\mathcal{D}_{q}Q_{s},

(33)

\displaystyle\Psi

\displaystyle=\texttt{U}_{1}\mathcal{D}_{q}Q_{s}+\alpha\mathcal{S}_{q}Q_{s}-\mathcal{D}_{q}R_{s+1}.

Of course,
\Phi(x)\not=0
and
\Psi(x)\not=0
, otherwise
{\bf u}={\bf 0}
, which contradicts the regularity of
\mathbf{u}
. Without restriction of generality, let us assume
\Phi(x)=(x-1)\Psi(x)
. From (
28
), and using (
16
) and (
17
), we get

\displaystyle{\bf D}_{q}\big(1/2(\alpha\,x-1){\bf u}\big)={\bf S}_{q}{\bf u}.

By Theorem
2.1
, this leads to a contradiction with the regularity of
\mathbf{u}
—
a=d=0
in the notation of Theorem
2.1
and so
d_{n}=0
therein—, and the first part of the theorem follows. Now suppose that
\Phi=\rho_{r}\phi
and
\Psi=\rho_{r}\psi
,
r<s
where
\rho_{r}\in\mathcal{P}_{r}
,
\phi\in\mathcal{P}_{s-r+1}
and
\psi\in\mathcal{P}_{s-r}
. Hence (
28
) reduces to
\phi\,{\bf D}_{q}{\bf u}=\psi\,{\bf S}_{q}{\bf u}
and, therefore, using (
16
) and (
17
), we have

{\bf D}_{q}\left((\mathcal{S}_{q}\phi+\texttt{U}_{2}\mathcal{D}_{q}\psi)\,{\bf u}\right)={\bf S}_{q}\left((\mathcal{S}_{q}\psi+\mathcal{D}_{q}\phi)\,{\bf u}\right).

Since
\mathcal{S}_{q}\phi
has degree at most
s-r+1
, and
\mathcal{S}_{q}\psi
and
\mathcal{D}_{q}\phi
have degree at most
s-r
,
\mathbf{u}
is classical whenever
r=s-1
or semiclassical of class at most
s-r-1
whenever
r<s-1
. Assume that (
28
) holds with
\Phi
and
\Psi
being coprime, i.e.,
r=0
. To obtain a contradiction, suppose that
{\bf u}
is semiclassical of class at most
s-2
: there exists
\phi\in\mathcal{P}_{s}
and
\psi\in\mathcal{P}_{s-1}
such that (
9
) holds. Taking into account (
14
) and (
15
), (
9
) holds if and only if

(34)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Phi}\,{\bf D}_{q}{\bf u}=\widetilde{\Psi}\,{\bf S}_{q}{\bf u},

where
\widetilde{\Phi}=\alpha\mathcal{S}_{q}\phi-\texttt{U}_{1}\mathcal{D}_{q}\phi+(\texttt{U}_{1}^{2}-\alpha^{2}\texttt{U}_{2})\mathcal{D}_{q}\psi
and
\widetilde{\Psi}=\alpha\mathcal{S}_{q}\psi+\texttt{U}_{1}\mathcal{D}_{q}\psi-\mathcal{D}_{q}\phi
. Combining (
28
) with (

) yields

(\widetilde{\Psi}\,\Phi-\widetilde{\Phi}\,\Psi)\,{\bf D}_{q}{\bf u}=\mathbf{0}.

By the regularity of
\mathbf{u}
, and the fact that
\Phi
and
\Psi
are coprime,
\Phi=a\widetilde{\Phi}
and

(a\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\})
, and so

which is impossible. Thus

is semiclassical of class

. The same conclusion can be drawn for

4.
Counterexamples

As an application of a particular case of Theorem
3.2
, we disprove Conjecture
1.1
and Conjecture
1.2
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
(P_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be a sequence of orthogonal polynomials satisfying (
6
) with

\displaystyle B_{n}=0,\qquad C_{n}=\frac{1}{4}\big(1-(-1)^{n}q^{n/2}\big)\big(1-(-1)^{n}q^{(n-1)/2}\big).

Then
(P_{n})_{n\geq 0}
is
\mathcal{D}_{q}
-semiclassical of class two and the corresponding linear form satisfies (
9
) with

\displaystyle\phi(x)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{8}(1-q^{-1})^{2}\big(4x^{4}-(q+5)x^{2}+q+1),

\displaystyle\psi(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}(q-1)q^{-3/2}x(4x^{2}-3-q).

Proof.

We claim that
(P_{n})_{n\geq 0}
satisfies

(35)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{q}P_{n}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{n}P_{n}+b_{n}C_{n-1}P_{n-2},

(36)

\displaystyle\texttt{U}_{2}\mathcal{D}_{q}P_{n}

\displaystyle=a_{n}P_{n+1}+c_{n}P_{n-1}+d_{n}P_{n-3},

with

\displaystyle a_{n}

\displaystyle=(\alpha^{2}-1)\gamma_{n},

\displaystyle b_{n}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\big(1-(-1)^{n}q^{n/2}\big)\big((-1)^{n}-q^{-(n-1)/2}\big),

\displaystyle c_{n}

\displaystyle=b_{n+1}C_{n}-\alpha b_{n}C_{n-1}-(\alpha^{2}-1)\gamma_{n}C_{n},\qquad d_{n}=(b_{n-1}C_{n}-\alpha b_{n}C_{n-1})C_{n-2}.

\displaystyle|1-q|+|\omega|\not=0,\qquad q\not\in\{0\}\cup\left\{e^{2ij\pi/n}\left|\right.1\leq j\leq n-1,n\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0,1\}\right\}.

.

[

[

, p. 855]

-classical, and there exist

\phi
and

45
) holds.

), and using (
47
), we get

\lambda_{+}=c,\qquad\lambda_{-}=c-\left(\left(B_{0}-B_{1}-\frac{c-B_{0}}{b_{2}C_{1}}C_{2}\right)^{2}+4C_{1}\right)^{1/2}.

Then (
48
) becomes

\displaystyle{\bf D}_{1/q,-\omega/q}\big((x-c){\bf u}\big)=-\frac{qb_{2}}{C_{2}}(x-c)(x-c+\Delta^{1/2}){\bf u},

where
\Delta=\left(B_{0}-B_{1}-\displaystyle\frac{c-B_{0}}{b_{2}C_{1}}C_{2}\right)^{2}+4C_{1}
or, equivalently,

\displaystyle{\bf D}_{1/q,-\omega/q}((x-c){\bf u})=\frac{1}{(q^{-1}-1)rs}(x-c)(x-r-s-\omega/(1-q)){\bf u},

where
r
and
s
are nonzero complex numbers such that
(q-1)rs=C_{2}/b_{2}
and
r+s=c-\omega/(1-q)-\Delta^{1/2}
. Define
{\bf v}=(x-c){\bf u}
. Hence

\displaystyle{\bf D}_{1/q,-\omega/q}{\bf v}=\frac{1}{(q^{-1}-1)rs}(x-r-s-\omega/(1-q)){\bf v}.

As above, by Theorem
5.1
,

d_{n}=\frac{q^{-n}}{(q^{-1}-1)rs}\neq 0,\qquad\phi=1\neq 0.

Moreover, also by Theorem
5.1
,
{\bf v}
is the linear form corresponding to the Al-Salam-Carlitz polynomials, and the theorem follows.
∎

The next proposition gives an explicit example of semiclassical orthogonal polynomials of class one satisfying (
46
), which prevents the reader from making any conjectures related to classical polynomials when faced with a relation of type (
1
) after changing the standard derivative by Hahn’s operator.

Proposition 5.1
.

Fix
\omega,q\in\mathbb{C}
such that (
44
) hold. Fix
a,b\in\mathbb{C}
such that

-a\not=b,\qquad b\not=0,\qquad a+(-1)^{n}b-(a+b)q^{n}\neq 0,

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
(P_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be a sequence of orthogonal polynomials satisfying (
6
) with

(49)

\displaystyle B_{n}=\frac{\omega}{1-q},\qquad C_{n}=(a+b)\left(\frac{a+(-1)^{n}b}{a+b}-q^{n}\right)q^{n}.

Then
(P_{n})_{n\geq 0}
is a
D_{1/q,-\omega/q}
-semiclassical of class one and its corresponding linear form
\mathbf{u}\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
satisfies

\displaystyle{\bf D}_{1/q,-\omega/q}\left(\left(x-\frac{\omega}{1-q}\right)\mathbf{u}\right)

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{1}{(a+b)(q-1)}\left(\frac{1}{q}\left(x-\frac{\omega}{1-q}\right)^{2}+b-a+(a+b)q\right){\bf u}.

Proof.

We claim that
(P_{n})_{n\geq 0}
satisfies (
46
) with

c=\frac{\omega}{1-q},\qquad a_{n}=\frac{1+(-1)^{n+1}}{(1-q)(a+b)}\,b,\qquad b_{n}=[n]_{q}-a_{n},\qquad c_{n}=-cb_{n}.

Indeed, the proof is by induction on
n
. For
n=1
, LHS of (
46
) gives

(x-c)D_{q,\omega}P_{1}(x)=x-c,

while RHS gives

a_{1}P_{1}(x)+b_{1}(x-c)P_{0}(x)=(a_{1}+b_{1})(x-c)=x-c.

Assuming (
46
) to hold, with
k
instead of
n
, for
k=1,2,\dots,n
, we will prove it for
k=n+1
. Apply
(x-c)D_{q,\omega}
to (
6
) to obtain

\displaystyle(x-c)D_{q,\omega}\big((x-c)P_{n}(x)\big)=(x-c)D_{q,\omega}\big(P_{n+1}(x)+C_{n}P_{n-1}(x)\big).

Using the identity (see
[
3
, (2.9)]
)

D_{q,\omega}(fg)=g(qx+\omega)D_{q,\omega}f+fD_{q,\omega}g\qquad(f,g\in\mathcal{P}),

we get

\displaystyle(x-c)D_{q,\omega}P_{n+1}(x)

\displaystyle=q(x-c)^{2}D_{q,\omega}P_{n}(x)+(x-c)P_{n}(x)-(x-c)C_{n}D_{q,\omega}P_{n-1}(x).

Now using successively (
46
), with
k
instead of
n
, for
k=n
and
k=n-1
and (
6
) it follows that

\displaystyle(x-c)D_{q,\omega}P_{n+1}(x)

\displaystyle=q(x-c)(a_{n}P_{n}(x)+b_{n}(x-c)P_{n-1}(x))

\displaystyle\quad+(x-c)P_{n}(x)-(a_{n-1}P_{n-1}(x)+b_{n-1}(x-c)P_{n-2}(x))C_{n}

\displaystyle=qa_{n}(P_{n+1}(x)+C_{n}P_{n-1}(x))+qb_{n}(x-c)(P_{n}(x)+C_{n-1}P_{n-2}(x))

\displaystyle\quad+(x-c)P_{n}(x)-(a_{n-1}P_{n-1}(x)+b_{n-1}(x-c)P_{n-2}(x))C_{n}

\displaystyle=qa_{n}P_{n+1}(x)+(qa_{n}-a_{n-1})C_{n}P_{n-1}(x)+(1+qb_{n})(x-c)P_{n}(x)

\displaystyle\quad+(qb_{n}C_{n-1}-b_{n-1}C_{n})(x-c)P_{n-2}(x)

\displaystyle=a_{n-1}P_{n+1}(x)+(1-a_{n-1}+q(a_{n}+b_{n}))(x-c)P_{n}(x)

\displaystyle\quad+(qb_{n}C_{n-1}-b_{n-1}C_{n})(x-c)P_{n-2}(x).

The reader should convince himself that the following relations hold:
a_{n-1}=a_{n+1}
,
1-a_{n-1}+q(a_{n}+b_{n})=b_{n+1}
, and
qb_{n}C_{n-1}-b_{n-1}C_{n}=0
. Thus (
46
) holds for
n+1
, and our claim follows. Note also that

\displaystyle\qquad b_{2}=q+1,\qquad C_{1}=(a-b-(a+b)q)q,\qquad C_{2}=(a+b)(1-q^{2})q^{2}.

Under the notation of Theorem
5.2
, we get

\displaystyle\lambda_{\pm}

\displaystyle=c\pm(a-b-(a+b)q)^{1/2}q^{1/2},

and so

\displaystyle q(\omega+qc-\lambda_{+})(\omega+qc-\lambda_{-})

\displaystyle\quad=(b-a+(a+b)q)q^{2}\not=(-b-a+(a+b)q)q^{2}=-\frac{C_{2}}{b_{2}},

because
\omega+q\,c=c
. Thus, from Theorem
5.2
, the result follows.
∎

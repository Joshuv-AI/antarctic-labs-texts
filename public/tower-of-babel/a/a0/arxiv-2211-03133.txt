Abstract
For a fixed graph F F , a graph G G is said to be F F -saturated if G G does not contain a subgraph isomorphic to F F but does contain F F after the addition of any new edge. Let M k M_{k} be a matching consisting of k k edges and S n , k S_{n,k} be the join graph of a complete graph K k K_{k} and an empty graph K n − k ¯ \overline{K_{n-k}} . In this paper, we prove that for s ≥ 3 s\geq 3 and k ≥ 2 k\geq 2 , S n , s − 2 S_{n,s-2} contains the minimum number of M k M_{k} among all n n -vertex K s K_{s} -saturated graphs for sufficiently large n n , and when k ≤ s − 2 k\leq s-2 , it is the unique extremal graph. In addition, we also show that S n , 1 S_{n,1} is the unique extremal graph when k = 2 k=2 and s = 3 s=3 . Keywords: saturation number, matching, extremal graph

Introduction

Given graphs
F
and
G
, if
G
does not contain
F
as a subgraph, we say that
G
is
F
-free. We write
\mbox{ex}(n,F)
for the Turán number of
F
, which is the maximum number of edges in an
F
-free graph on
n
vertices. This function which is considered to be a cornerstone in graph theory has been studied by many researchers (see, e.g., nice surveys such as
[
20
]
and
[
6
]
).
A graph
G
is said to be
F
-saturated if
G
does not contain
F
as a subgraph but adding any missing edge to
G
creates a copy of
F
.
Given graphs
G
and
H
, write
N(H,G)
for the number of copies of
H
in
G
, for a subset
S\subseteq V(G)
, write
N_{G}(H,S)
for the number of copies of
H
containing
S
in
G
.
The generalized Turán number is defined as

\mbox{ex}(n,H,F)=\max\{N(H,G):G\text{ is an $n$-vertex $F$-saturated graph}\}.

This function has been systematically studied by Alon and Shikhelman
[
1
]
and has received much attention, for example, in
[
8
,
7
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
14
,
19
]
.
As the dual of the generalized Turán number, the generalized saturation number is defined as

\mbox{sat}(n,H,F)=\min\{N(H,G):G\text{ is an $n$-vertex $F$-saturated graph}\}.

When
H=K_{2}
, it is the classical saturation number
\mbox{sat}(n,F)
.
Let
S_{n,k}
be the join graph of a complete graph
K_{k}
and an empty graph
\overline{K_{n-k}}
, which is the graph obtained from joining all the edges between
V(K_{k})
and
V(\overline{K_{n-k}})
.
Erdős, Hajnal, and Moon
[
3
]
studied the saturation number for complete graphs, which initiated the investigation of the graph saturation problem. They proved that

Theorem 1.1

(
[
3
]
)
.

For every
n\geq s\geq 2
, the saturation number

\displaystyle\mbox{sat}(n,K_{s})=(s-2)(n-s+2)+\binom{s-2}{2}.

Furthermore,
S_{n,s-2}
is the unique minimal extremal graph among
K_{s}
-saturated graphs on
n
vertices.

One can see a survey for significant results of the saturation problem of graphs written by Faudree, Faudree, and Schmitt
[
5
]
and some recent results
[
16
,
18
]
.
Recently, Kritschgau et al
[
15
]
investigated the generalized saturation function for the host graph is a clique or cycle.
Chakraborti and Loh
[
2
]
confirmed a conjecture of Kritschgau et al
[
15
]
by showing the following theorem.

Theorem 1.2

(
[
2
]
)
.

For every
s>r\geq 2
, there exists a constant
n_{r,s}
such that for all
n\geq n_{r,s}
,

\mbox{sat}(n,K_{r},K_{s})=(n-s+2){s-2\choose r-1}+\binom{s-2}{r}

Moreover, there exists a constant
c_{r,s}>0
such that the only

K_{s}
-saturated graph with up to
\mbox{sat}(n,K_{r},K_{s})+c_{r,s}n
many copies of
K_{r}
is
S_{n,s-2}
.

Ergemlidze et al
[
4
]
investigated the saturation number
\mbox{sat}(n,K_{a,b},K_{s})
and showed that
\mbox{sat}(n,K_{1,t},K_{s})=\Theta(n^{\frac{t}{2}})
, furthermore, they gave more discussion about
K_{1,2}
and
K_{2,t}
, answering a question of Chakraborti and Loh
[
2
]
.

In this note, we continue the study of the function
\mbox{sat}(n,H,K_{s})
when
H
is a matching with
k
edges.
We call an

-vertex

-saturated graph (minimal) extremal graph if

The main result of this article is as follows.

Theorem 1.3
.

For every

and

, there is a constant

such that

\mbox{sat}(n,M_{k},K_{s})=N(M_{k},S_{n,s-2})

for

.
Moreover,
S_{n,s-2}
is the unique extremal graph when
k\leq s-2
.
In addition,
S_{n,1}
is the unique extremal graph for

and

.

2
Preliminaries

It can be easily checked that
S_{n,s-2}
is
K_{s}
-saturated. Therefore, the number of copies of
M_{k}
in
S_{n,s-2}
gives an upper bound for
\mbox{sat}(n,M_{k},K_{s})
.
When
k>s-2
, we have

0\leq\mbox{sat}(n,M_{k},K_{s})\leq N(M_{k},S_{n,s-2})=0.

(1)

Now let us count
N(M_{k},S_{n,s-2})
when
2\leq k\leq s-2
. Let
A
be an independent set of size
k
, there are
\binom{n-s+2}{k}
many ways to pick an independent set of size
k
in the graph
S_{n,s-2}
. Then for an arbitrary independent set
A
, the number of
M_{k}
containing
A
is
(s-2)_{k}
, and each
M_{k}
is counted once. In addition, there does not exist a copy of
M_{k}
with more than
k
vertices in
\overline{K_{n-s+2}}
because
\alpha(M_{k})=k
. Therefore, we have

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
k,s
be integers with
2\leq k\leq s-2
. Then

\displaystyle\mbox{sat}(n,M_{k},K_{s})

\displaystyle\leq N(M_{k},S_{n,s-2})=\frac{(s-2)_{k}}{k!}n^{k}+O(n^{k-1}).

(2)

The following lemma counts the number of independent sets of fixed size in
K_{s}
-free graphs, which has been given in
[
2
]
.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
2
]
)
.

For every fixed
\ell
, there are
\Theta(n^{\ell})
independent sets of order
\ell
in every
n
-vertex
K_{s}
-free graph.

We give an asymptotic upper bound on the number of edges of a
K_{s}
-saturated graph on
n
vertices with the minimum number of copies of
M_{k}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
k,s
be integers with
2\leq k\leq s-2
and let
G
be a
K_{s}
-saturated graph on
n
vertices with the minimum number of copies of
M_{k}
. Then for any function
f(n)\rightarrow\infty
as
n\rightarrow\infty
, we have

|E(G)|=O(nf(n))
.

Proof.

We can assume that
f(n)=O(\log n)
and
G
has more than
O(nf(n))
edges. We will find a contradiction. Let
B=\{v\in V(G):d_{G}(v)>f(n)\}
. Then

\sum_{v\in B}d_{G}(v)=2|E(G)|-\sum_{v\notin B}d_{G}(v)\geq 2|E(G)|-(n-|B|)f(n)>O(nf(n)).

Choose a vertex
v\in B
, we select an independent set
I=\{v_{1},\cdots,v_{k-1}\}
in
V(G)\backslash\{v\}
.
Let
V_{0}
be an empty set if
vv_{1}\in E(G)
, otherwise, let
V_{0}
be the set such that the induced graph
G[V_{0}\cup\{v,v_{1}\}]\cong K_{s}
, where
V_{0}
exists since
G
is
K_{s}
-saturated. For every
i\in[k-2]
, denote
V_{i}
the set such that the induced subgraph
G[V_{i}\cup\{v_{i},v_{i+1}\}]\cong K_{s}
after adding the edge
v_{i}v_{i+1}
. Let
U=V_{0}\cup V_{1}\cup\cdots\cup V_{k-2}\cup I
and
V^{\prime}=N_{G}(v)-U
.
Therefore,
|V^{\prime}|\geq d_{G}(v)-|U|\geq d_{G}(v)-ks\geq\frac{1}{2}d_{G}(v)
because
|U|\leq ks<\frac{1}{2}f(n)<\frac{1}{2}d_{G}(v)
when
n
is large enough.

Claim 2.1
.

The number of
M_{k}
containing
I\cup\{v\}
is at least
\frac{1}{2}d(v)
.

Proof of Claim
2.1
.

To prove the claim, it is sufficient to show that there is at least one copy of matching
M_{k}
containing
I\cup\{v,u\}
for every
u\in V^{\prime}
.

Case 1:
vv_{1}\in E(G)
. Then
V_{0}=\emptyset
. Since
|V_{i}|=s-2
and
1\leq k-1<s-2
for all
i\in[k-2]
, we can find a copy of
M_{k}=\{uv,w_{1}^{\prime}v_{1},w_{1}v_{2},\ldots,w_{k-2}v_{k-1}\}
by selecting two distinct vertices
w_{1},w_{1}^{\prime}
from
V_{1}
and
w_{i}
from
V_{i}-\{w_{1}^{\prime},w_{1},w_{2},\ldots,w_{i-1}\}
for
i=2,\ldots,k-2
.

Case 2:
vv_{1}\notin E(G)
. Then
V_{0}\not=\emptyset
. Because
|V_{i}|=s-2
and
k-1<s-2
for all
i=0,1,\ldots,k-2
, we can find a copy of
M_{k}=\{uv,w_{0}v_{1},w_{1}v_{2},\cdots,w_{k-2}v_{k-1}\}
by selecting
w_{0}
from
V_{0}
and
w_{i}
from
V_{i}-\{w_{0},w_{1},\ldots,w_{i-1}\}
for
i=1,2,\ldots,k-2
.
∎

For any
v\in B
, the subgraph
G-\{v\}
is also
K_{s}
-free, so there are
\Theta(n^{k-1})
independent sets of size
k-1
in
G-\{v\}
by Lemma
2.2
. By Claim
2.1
, the number of copies of
M_{k}
containing such an independent set and
v
is at least
\frac{1}{2}d(v)
. Therefore, we have at least
\Theta\left(\sum_{v\in B}n^{k-1}d(v)\right)=\Theta(n^{k}f(n))
many copies of
M_{k}
in
G
(note that an
M_{k}
copy can only be counted at most a constant time that depends on
k
), which contradicts (
2
) for sufficiently large
n
.

∎

The following lemma counts the number of independent sets of fixed size in graphs with bounded edges.

Lemma 2.4

(
[
17
]
)
.

Let
\tau,\ell
be positive integers with
\ell\leq\tau+1
and let
G
be a graph on
n
vertices with
\frac{1}{\tau}\binom{n}{2}
many edges. Then the number of independent sets of order
\ell
in
G
is at least
\binom{\tau}{\ell}(\frac{n}{\tau})^{\ell}
.

Corollary 2.5
.

For every
K_{s}
-saturated graph
G
on
n
vertices with the minimum number of copies of
M_{k}
, there are
(1-o(1))\binom{n}{k}
independent sets of size
k
in
G
, for every
k\leq s-2
.

Proof.

Let
G
be a
K_{s}
-saturated graph on
n
vertices with the minimum number of copies of
M_{k}
. By Lemma
2.3
, we know that the number of edges of
G
is
O(nf(n))
. Take
\tau\rightarrow\frac{n}{f(n)}
and
\ell=k
. Applying Lemma
2.4
and by a simple counting, we have that there are
(1-o(1))\binom{n}{k}
many independent sets of size
k
in
G
.

∎

Proof of Theorem
1.3

Let
G
be a
K_{s}
-saturated graph on
n
vertices with
N(M_{k},G)=\mbox{sat}(n,M_{k},K_{s})
.
By Lemma
2.3
, we may assume
e(G)=O(n\log n)
.
Let
A=\{v\in V(G):d_{G}(v)\leq n^{\frac{1}{3}}\}
, then
|A|=n-o(n)
.

Claim 3.1
.

There are
(1-o(1))\binom{n}{k}
independent sets of size
k
of which all vertices are from
A
and any two of them do not have common neighbors in
A
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{I}
denote the collection of such independent sets. First, there are
o(n^{k})
independent sets of size
k
each containing at least one vertex from
V\backslash A
, since almost all vertices are in
A
. The number of independent sets of size
k
in which all vertices are from
A
and there exist two vertices with common neighbors in
A
is at most
|A|\binom{n^{\frac{1}{3}}}{2}n^{k-2}=o(n^{k})
. From Corollary
2.5
and the definition of
\mathcal{I}
, we know that there are
(1-o(1))\binom{n}{k}
independent sets in
\mathcal{I}
.
∎

Consider an arbitrary independent set
I=\{v_{1},\cdots,v_{k}\}\in\mathcal{I}
, there is a set
V_{i,j}\subseteq V\backslash A
of
s-2
vertices such that the induced graph
V_{i,j}\cup\{v_{i},v_{j}\}
is isomorphic to
K_{s}
after adding the edge
v_{i}v_{j}
for every
i,j\in\binom{[k]}{2}
.
For every independent set
I\in\mathcal{I}
, let
N^{\prime}_{G}(M_{k},I)
denote the number of copies of
M_{k}
containing
I
and
k
vertices from
V\backslash A
. Then

N^{\prime}_{G}(M_{k},I)\geq(s-2)_{k},

equality holds if and only if all
V_{i,j}
’s are the same and there is no common neighbor of
v_{i}
and
v_{j}
in
(V\backslash A)\backslash V_{ij}
. Therefore,

N(M_{k},G)\geq|\mathcal{I}|\cdot N^{\prime}_{G}(M_{k},I)\geq(1-o(1)){n\choose k}(s-2)_{k}.

Therefore, the upper bound (
2
) is asymptotically tight for all
k
, moreover, we conclude that the number of independent sets
I=\{v_{1},\cdots,v_{k}\}\in\mathcal{I}
for which
V_{i,j}
’s are the same and there is no common neighbor of
v_{i}
and
v_{j}
in
(V\backslash A)\backslash V_{ij}
is
(1-o(1))\binom{n}{k}
, since, otherwise, there will be more copies of
M_{k}
in
G
than the upper bound (
2
), which is a contradiction. Denote this collection of such independent sets by
\mathcal{J}
. Then
|\mathcal{J}|=(1-o(1))\binom{n}{k}
.

Claim 3.2
.

For
k\geq 3
,
G
contains a subgraph isomorphic to
S_{n-o(n),s-2}
.

Proof.

Since
|\mathcal{J}|=\binom{n}{k}-o(n^{k})
, there must exist two vertices
u,v
in
A
such that there are
\binom{n}{k-2}-o(n^{k-2})
independent sets in
\mathcal{J}
containing both
u
and
v
. We denote the collection of independent sets in
\mathcal{J}
containing both
u
and
v
by
\mathcal{K}
. Moreover, there is a set
T\subseteq V\backslash A
of
s-2
vertices such that the induced graph
T\cup\{u,v\}
is isomorphic to
K_{s}
after adding the edge
uv
. By the definition of
\mathcal{J}
, we can conclude that
T
is the only common
(s-2)
-neighborhood of every independent set in
\mathcal{K}
. Therefore,
\cup_{I\in\mathcal{K}}I
is an independent set since
G
does not have a copy of
K_{s}
. For
k\geq 3
, since
\mathcal{K}=\binom{n}{k-2}-o(n^{k-2})
, we can check that
|\cup_{I\in\mathcal{K}}I|=n-o(n)
easily. (Note that it is not true for
k=2
.)

∎

Now we are ready to complete the proof of Theorem
1.3
. First, let us consider the case when
k\geq 3
. Choose a maximum subgraph
S_{n_{1},s-2}
in
G
. By Claim
3.2
, we know that
n_{1}=n-o(n)
.
Denote the set of all vertices outside of
V(S_{n_{1},s-2})
by
M
. Let
m=|M|+s-2
. Then
m=n-n_{1}+s-2=o(n)
. We may assume that
M\not=\emptyset
for the sake of contradiction. Let
I
be the independent set of
S_{n_{1},s-2}
and
K=V(S_{n_{1},s-2})\setminus I
. Then
|I|=n_{1}-s+2=n-m
and
|K|=s-2
.

Claim 3.3
.

For any
v\in I
,
v
has at least one neighbor in
M
.

Proof.

Assume that there is one vertex
v\in I
that has no neighbor in
M
. Then for any
u\in M
, there is a set
U\subseteq V\backslash I
of
s-2
vertices such that the subgraph induced by
V\cup\{u,v\}
is isomorphic to
K_{s}
in
G+uv
. Clearly,
U\cap(I\cup M)=\emptyset
. Therefore,
U
must be
K
. This fact forces
u
to have no neighbor in
I
; otherwise, we have a contradiction to
G
being
K_{s}
-free. Thus
u
can be added in
I
to obtain a larger subgraph
S_{n_{1}+1,s-2}
, a contradiction. The claim holds.
∎

Let
z
be the integer such that
(z)_{k}=\lceil\sqrt{m+k}\cdot n^{k-\frac{1}{2}}\rceil
.

Claim 3.4
.

When
n
is sufficiently large, for any
v\in M
,
v
has at most
z
neighbors in
I
.

Proof.

Suppose there is a vertex
v\in M
that has more than
z=o(n)
neighbors in
I
.
We know that there are at least
(s-2)_{k}\cdot\binom{n-m}{k}
many copies of
M_{k}
in
S_{n_{1},s-2}
.
Now arbitrarily choose a
k
-set from
N_{G}(v)\cap I
and a
(k-1)
-set from
K
, then, together with
v
, we have at least one copy of
M_{k}
containing these vertices. The number of such copies of
M_{k}
is at least
\binom{z}{k}
.
Therefore, for large enough
n
,

\displaystyle N(M_{k},G)

\displaystyle\geq(s-2)_{k}\cdot\binom{n-m}{k}+\binom{z}{k}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(s-2)_{k}}{k!}\cdot(n-m-k)^{k}+\frac{(z)_{k}}{k!}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(s-2)_{k}}{k!}\cdot n^{k}-\frac{(s-2)_{k}}{k!}\cdot k(m+k)n^{k-1}+\frac{\sqrt{m+k}n^{k-\frac{1}{2}}}{k!}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(s-2)_{k}}{k!}\cdot n^{k}+\frac{\sqrt{m+k}n^{k-\frac{1}{2}}}{2k!}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(s-2)_{k}}{k!}\cdot n^{k}+\Theta(n^{k-\frac{1}{2}}),

which is a contradiction to the upper bound (
2
).
∎

Now we return to the analysis of
M
.
By Claim
3.3
,
|E_{G}(M,I)|\geq n-m
. By Claim
3.4
,
|E_{G}(M,I)|\leq z(m-s+2)
. If
m\leq\log n
,
then, for large enough
n
,
z(m-s+2)<n-m
, a contradiction.
Now we assume
m>\log n
. For a vertex
v\in M
, select a
(k-1)
-set
B
from
I-N_{G}(v)
(such a
B
exists guaranteed by Claim
3.4
).
Denote
B=\{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{k-1}\}
and let
V_{i}
be a
(s-2)
-set of
V(G)
such that
V_{i}\cup\{v,v_{i}\}
induces a subgraph isomorphic to
K_{s}
after adding the edge
vv_{i}
. Because of the maximality of
S_{n_{1},s-2}
,
K\nsubseteq N_{G}(v)
and thus
V_{i}\not=K
for any
i\in[k-1]
.
Let

\mathcal{M}(v,B)=\{M_{k}:M_{k}=\{vv^{\prime},v_{1}v_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,v_{k-1}v_{k-1}^{\prime}\}\text{ with $v^{\prime}\in V_{1}$ and $v_{i}^{\prime}\in K$ for $i\in[k-1]$}\}.

It is easy to check that
|\mathcal{M}(v,B)|\geq(s-2)_{k}+1
.
Clearly,
\mathcal{M}(v,B)\cap\mathcal{M}(v,B^{\prime})=\emptyset
if
B
and
B^{\prime}
are different
(k-1)
-sets of
I-N_{G}(v)
, and
\mathcal{M}(v,B)\cap\mathcal{M}(u,C)=\emptyset
for
u\not=v
in
M
, where
B
and
C
are
(k-1)
-sets of
I-N_{G}(v)
and
I-N_{G}(u)
, respectively.
Thus, the total number of such copies of
M_{k}
is

\sum_{v\in M,B\subseteq I-N_{G}(v)\atop|B|=k-1}|\mathcal{M}(v,B)|\geq\binom{n-m-z}{k-1}(m-s+2)((s-2)_{k}+1).

Therefore,

\displaystyle N(M_{k},G)

\displaystyle\geq(s-2)_{k}\cdot\binom{n-m}{k}+\binom{n-m-z}{k-1}(m-s+2)((s-2)_{k}+1)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(s-2)_{k}}{k!}\cdot(n-m-k)^{k}+\binom{n-m-z}{k-1}(m-s)((s-2)_{k}+1)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(s-2)_{k}}{k!}n^{k}-\frac{(s-2)_{k}}{(k-1)!}\cdot(m+k)n^{k-1}+\frac{(s-2)_{k}}{(k-1)!}(m-s)(n-m-z-k)_{k-1}

\displaystyle\quad+\binom{n-m-z}{k-1}(m-s)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(s-2)_{k}}{k!}n^{k}-\frac{(s-2)_{k}}{(k-1)!}\cdot mn^{k-1}-\Theta(n^{k-1})+\frac{(s-2)_{k}}{(k-1)!}\cdot mn^{k-1}+\Theta(mn^{k-1})

\displaystyle\quad-\Theta(m^{2}n^{k-2})-\Theta(mzn^{k-2})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(s-2)_{k}}{k!}n^{k}+\Theta(mn^{k-1}),

the last inequality holds when
n
is sufficiently large, but this is a contradiction to the upper bound (
2
) (since we assume
m>\log n
in this case). Therefore,
M
has to be an empty set, and
G\cong S_{n,s-2}
. This completes the proof of the case
k\geq 3
.

Next we assume
k=2
and divide the proof into two cases.

Case 1:

s=3
.

Then, by (
1
),
\mbox{sat}(n,M_{2},K_{3})=0
and
S_{n,1}
is an extremal graph.
We claim that
S_{n,1}
is the unique extremal graph.
Since
G
is
K_{3}
-saturated and contains the minimum number of copies of
M_{2}
,
G
is connected and contains no cycle, which implies that
G
is a tree.
Furthermore, adding any missing edge to
G
creates a copy of
K_{3}
forces that
G\cong S_{n,1}
.
Therefore,
S_{n,1}
is the unique extremal graph when
k=2
and
s=3
.

Case 2:

s\geq 4
.

Since
G
is
K_{s}
-saturated,
\delta(G)\geq s-2
. Suppose
m=|E(G)|
.
Then

N(M_{2},G)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{uv\in E(G)}\left(|E(G)|-d_{G}(v)-d_{G}(u)+1\right)=\frac{1}{2}\left(m^{2}+m-\sum_{v\in V(G)}d_{G}^{2}(v)\right).

If there are two vertices
u,v\in V(G)
such that
s-2<d_{G}(u)\leq d_{G}(v)<n-1
.
We construct a new graph
G^{*}
with
d_{G^{*}}(v)=d_{G}(v)+1
,
d_{G^{*}}(u)=d_{G}(u)-1
, and
d_{G^{*}}(x)=d_{G}(x)
for
x\in V(G)\backslash\{u,v\}
(this can be easily done just by deleting a neighbor of
v
and adding it to the neighborhood of
v
). Then

\displaystyle N(M_{2},G)-N(M_{2},G^{*})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(d_{G^{*}}^{2}(v)+d_{G^{*}}^{2}(u)-d_{G}^{2}(v)-d_{G}^{2}(u))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}((d_{G}(v)+1)^{2}+(d_{G}(u)-1)^{2}-d_{G}^{2}(v)-d_{G}^{2}(u))

\displaystyle=d_{G}(v)-d_{G}(u)+1>0,

Continue the above process until there is at most one vertex
v
with
d_{G}(v)\notin\{s-2,n-1\}
. For convenience, we still use
G
to denote the graph at the final step.

Subcase 2.1:

d_{G}(v)\in\{s-2,n-1\}
for every
v\in V(G)
.

Assume there are
a
vertices with degree
s-2
and
b
vertices with degree
n-1
. Then we have the following equation array,

\begin{cases}2m=a(s-2)+b(n-1)\\
n=a+b\end{cases}.

Solve the equation array, we have
a=\frac{n^{2}-n-2m}{n-s+1}
,
b=\frac{2m+2n-ns}{n-s+1}
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle N(M_{2},G)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(m^{2}+m-a(s-2)^{2}-b(n-1)^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(m^{2}+(7-2n-2s)m+n(n-1)(s-2)\right)

(3)

\displaystyle\geq N(M_{2},S_{n,s-2}),

(4)

the last inequality holds because the quadratic function (
3
) increases in the interval
[n+s-\frac{7}{2},+\infty)

and
m=|E(G)|\geq|E(S_{n,s-2})|=(s-2)(n-s+2)+\binom{s-2}{2}\geq n+s-\frac{7}{2}
by Theorem
1.1
, moreover, equality holds in (
4
) if and only if
G\cong S_{n,s-2}
.

Subcase 2.2:
There exists one vertex
v
with
s-2<d_{G}(v)=c<n-1
.

Assume there are
a
vertices with degree
s-2
and
b
vertices with degree
n-1
, then

\begin{cases}2m=a(s-2)+b(n-1)+c\\
n=a+b+1.\end{cases}

Solve the equation array, we have
a=\frac{(n-1)^{2}-2m+c}{n-s+1}
and
b=\frac{2m-c-(n-1)(s-2)}{n-s+1}
.

\displaystyle N(M_{2},G)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(m^{2}+m-a(s-2)^{2}-b(n-1)^{2}-c^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(-c^{2}+(n+s-3)c+m^{2}+(7-2n-2s)m+(n-1)^{2}(s-2)\right)

\displaystyle>\frac{1}{2}\left(m^{2}+(7-2n-2s)m+n(n-1)(s-2)\right)

(5)

\displaystyle\geq N(M_{2},S_{n,s-2})

where the inequality is tight in (
5
) because
\min\limits_{s-2<c<n-1}\{-c^{2}+(n+s-3)c\}>(n-1)(s-2)
.
The proof of Theorem
1.3
is completed. ∎

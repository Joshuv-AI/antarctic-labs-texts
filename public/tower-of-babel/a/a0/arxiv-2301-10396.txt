An equivariant generalisation of McDuff–Segal’s group–completion theorem Thanks: Date of publication in IMRN .
Kaif Hilman

Abstract
In this short note, we prove a G G –equivariant generalisation of McDuff–Segal’s group–completion theorem for finite groups G G . A new complication regarding genuine equivariant localisations arises and we resolve this by isolating a simple condition on the homotopy groups of 𝔼 ∞ \mathbb{E}_{\infty} –rings in G G –spectra. We check that this condition is satisfied when our inputs are a suitable variant of 𝔼 ∞ \mathbb{E}_{\infty} –monoids in G G –spaces via the existence of multiplicative norm structures, thus giving a localisation formula for their associated G G –spherical group rings.



1
Introduction

Group–completion is an important procedure in higher algebra for at least two reasons: (1) it is the main ingredient in constructing the K–theory of symmetric monoidal (
\infty-
)categories; (2) it allows one to port spectral methods to study questions regarding moduli spaces. The homotopy types of these group–completions are however mysterious in general, and the group–completion theorem of McDuff–Segal
[
MS76
,
Ran13
]
is a classical tool giving a homological formula for these objects. By now, the theorem has become a standard component, for example, in the active area burgeoning in the wake of the Madsen–Weiss theorem (cf.
[
GR14
, §7.4]
and
[
GR17
, §7]
) relating the homology of diffeomorphism groups to something amenable to stable homotopy theoretic methods. Very roughly speaking, the strategy is first to show that the group–completion of a geometrically defined cobordism category associated to the diffeomorphism groups is equivalent to a particular Thom spectrum. One then combines this identification with the group–completion theorem to compute, up to stabilisation, the homology of said diffeomorphism groups in terms of the homology of the Thom spectrum.

In this article, we investigate a
G
–equivariant generalisation of this classical result for finite groups
G
. This is not as contrived a question as it may first seem since one of the main steps for an equivariant generalisation of the “Madsen–Weiss program” above has already been explored in
[
GS21
, Thm. 1.1]
where they identified the group–completion of a certain equivariant cobordism category with an equivariant Madsen–Tillmann spectrum. Our hope is that the result we present here could provide one of the standard pieces in a future equivariant story and serve as a useful tool for making Bredon homological analyses of equivariant group–completions.

Construction 2.6

(Equivariant group–completions)
.

As explained for instance in
[

\mathrm{CGrp}(\mathcal{S})

is that the abelian monoid

has the property of being a group.

If we write

for the suspension in the category

(which is very different from the suspension on the underlying space!), then we know that we have the adjunction

so that
\Omega B
implements the group–completion functor on

[
CDH+20
, Prop. 3.3.5]
,

semiadditive category with pullbacks and pushouts. We can then cofreely make this into a

–adjunction of

–categories

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\underline{\mathrm{Cofree}}_{G}\big(\mathrm{CMon}(\mathcal{S})\big)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\underline{\mathrm{Cofree}}_{G}\big(\mathrm{CGrp}(\mathcal{S})\big)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega B}

(2)

Here for a nonequivariant category

,

is the
G
–category given by

(cf.
[
Nar16
, Def. 2.7]
for instance, where the notation was just an underline instead of the word “cofree”). In this notation, the

–category of

–spaces is therefore given by

. Both

The main theorem

In order to state and prove the theorem, we will need a few more terminologies and observations.

Notation 3.1
.

In this note, two kinds of ring localisations will feature and we define and relate them here. Let
R\in\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathrm{Sp}_{G})
and
\underline{S}=\{S_{H}\}_{H\leq G}
be a
G
–subset
of the zeroth equivariant homotopy Mackey functor
\underline{\pi}_{0}R
of
R
. That is, for any
H\leq G
,
\underline{S}
satisfies
\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{H}S_{G}\subseteq S_{H}\subseteq\pi_{0}^{H}R\coloneqq\pi_{0}R^{H}
. Now for any
A\in\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathrm{Sp}_{G})
, we define

\mathrm{Map}_{\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathrm{Sp}_{G})}^{\underline{S}^{-1}}(R,A)\quad\quad\text{ and }\quad\quad\mathrm{Map}_{\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathrm{Sp}_{G})}^{S_{G}^{-1}}(R,A)

to be subcomponents of
\mathrm{Map}_{\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathrm{Sp}_{G})}(R,A)
of
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
–algebra maps
R\rightarrow A
which send elements in
\underline{S}
to units in
\underline{\pi}_{0}A
and send elements in
S_{G}
to units in
\pi_{0}^{G}A
, respectively. By general theory (cf.
[
Nik17
, Appen. A]
for example), we know that the latter mapping space is corepresented by a telescopic localisation
S_{G}^{-1}R
of
R
against elements in
S_{G}\subseteq\pi_{0}^{G}R
(ie.
\mathrm{Map}_{\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathrm{Sp}_{G})}^{S_{G}^{-1}}(R,A)\simeq\mathrm{Map}_{\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathrm{Sp}_{G})}(S_{G}^{-1}R,A)
). In particular, we have that
\underline{\pi}_{\star}S_{G}^{-1}R\cong S_{G}^{-1}\underline{\pi}_{\star}R
.

On the other hand,
if
the former mapping space is corepresentable, then we will write the corepresenting object as
L_{\underline{S}^{-1}}R
. In general, this need not be given by a nice formula in terms of a telescopic localisation since we need to invert different sets of elements at different subgroups

that do not all come from restricting elements from
S_{G}
(ie. the inclusion
\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{H}S_{G}\subseteq S_{H}
might be proper), and so
\underline{\pi}_{*}L_{\underline{S}^{-1}}R
need not admit a nice description as a Mackey functor with elements in
\underline{S}
inverted. However, since maps
R\rightarrow A
which invert
\underline{S}
must necessarily invert
S_{G}
, we do have an inclusion

\mathrm{Map}_{\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathrm{Sp}_{G})}^{\underline{S}^{-1}}(R,-)\xhookrightarrow{\phantom{\hskip 8.19447pt}}\mathrm{Map}_{\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathrm{Sp}_{G})}^{S_{G}^{-1}}(R,-)

Thus, when
L_{\underline{S}^{-1}}R
exists, this inclusion is induced by a canonical comparison map in
\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathrm{Sp}_{G})

S_{G}^{-1}R\longrightarrow L_{\underline{S}^{-1}}R.

(4)

Notation 3.2
.

Let

. We write
\underline{\pi}_{M}\subseteq\underline{\pi}_{0}\mathbb{S}_{G}[M]
for the image of the Hurewicz map on the equivariant homotopy groups
\underline{\pi}_{0}M\rightarrow\underline{\pi}_{0}{\Omega}^{\infty}_{G}\mathbb{S}_{G}[M]=\underline{\pi}_{0}\mathbb{S}_{G}[M]
induced by the adjunction unit
\mathrm{id}\Rightarrow\Omega^{\infty}_{G}\mathbb{S}_{G}
. This is clearly a
G
–subset in the sense defined above.

We are now ready to state the main theorem of this note:

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
M\in\mathrm{CMon}(\mathcal{S}_{G})
be an
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
–monoid
G
–space.

(i)

The object

exists and the group–completion map

induces an equivalence in
\mathrm{CAlg}({\mathrm{Sp}}_{G})

L_{(\underline{\pi}_{M})^{-1}}\mathbb{S}_{G}[M]\xlongrightarrow{\simeq}\mathbb{S}_{G}[\Omega BM]

(ii)

Moreover, if

–monoid

–ring object. In other words, it lifts to an object in

. Furthermore, in this case, the canonical map from

We now turn to the task of refining to normed structures when the input is more highly structured, ie. when

. Before that, it would be useful to formulate the following intermediate notion together with a couple of easy consequences which would help us identify the homotopy groups of the abstract localisation we have so far.

Condition 3.4

(Torsion–extensions)
.

Let
R\in\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathrm{Sp}_{G})
and

be a

–subset of the zeroth equivariant homotopy groups of

. We say that

satisfies the

, the inclusion

is a torsion–extension, ie. for any

, there exists a

such that

.

Remark 3.5
.

The reason for this choice of terminology was an analogy in the case of modules: if

are

–submodules satisfying the analogous condition, then

is a torsion

–module. In any case, the next three lemmas should clarify our interest in this condition.

Lemma 3.6
.

If

\mathrm{Map}_{\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathrm{Sp}_{G})}^{\underline{S}^{-1}}(R,\Xi^{G}A)\xhookrightarrow{\phantom{\hskip 8.19447pt}}\mathrm{Map}_{\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathrm{Sp})}^{(\Phi^{G}S_{G})^{-1}}(\Phi^{G}R,A)

To see that this is even an equivalence, suppose we have

which inverts

. The adjoint

is given by the composite

–rings and sends elements in

to elements in

. Therefore,

must invert all elements in

. Moreover, since for

are equivalent to the zero rings, the maps

send everything to units for trivial reasons, and so in total

indeed inverts elements in

as was to be shown.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let

,
\underline{S}\subseteq\underline{\pi}_{0}R
a multiplicative subset, and suppose

exists. Then the canonical map

induces an equivalence

.

Proof.

Let

. Then

\begin{split}\mathrm{Map}_{\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathrm{Sp})}(\Phi^{G}L_{\underline{S}^{-1}}R,A)&\simeq\mathrm{Map}_{\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathrm{Sp}_{G})}(L_{\underline{S}^{-1}}R,\Xi^{G}A)\\
&\simeq\mathrm{Map}_{\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathrm{Sp}_{G})}^{\underline{S}^{-1}}(R,\Xi^{G}A)\\
&\simeq\mathrm{Map}_{\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathrm{Sp})}^{(\Phi^{G}S_{G})^{-1}}(\Phi^{G}R,A)\\
&\simeq\mathrm{Map}_{\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathrm{Sp})}((\Phi^{G}S_{G})^{-1}\Phi^{G}R,A)\\
\end{split}

where the third equivalence is by

.
∎

Remark 4.3

Next, we turn to the matter of recording a generic toy situation where our theorem might be useful. This manoeuvre is an immediate generalisation of its (standard) nonequivariant analogue.

Proposition 4.4

(Equivariant simply–connected homology Whitehead theorem)
.

Appendix A
Algebras in equivariant cartesian symmetric monoidal structures

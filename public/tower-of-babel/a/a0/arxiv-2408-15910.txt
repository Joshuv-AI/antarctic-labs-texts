Hybrid Type-II and Type-III seesaw model for the muon g − 2 g-2 anomaly
Lei Cai, Chengcheng Han, Shi-Ping He, Peiwen Wu

Abstract
In this work we investigate the muon anomalous magnetic dipole moment a μ a_{\mu} in a model that extends the Standard Model with a scalar triplet and a lepton triplet. Different from previous studies, we find that there is still viable parameter space in this model to explain the discrepancy Δ ​ a μ = a μ ​ ( Exp ) − a μ ​ ( SM ) \Delta a_{\mu}=a_{\mu}(\mathrm{Exp})-a_{\mu}(\mathrm{SM}) . While being consistent with the current data of neutrino mass, electroweak precision measurements and the perturbativity of couplings, our model can provide new physics contribution a μ NP a_{\mu}^{\textrm{NP}} to cover the central region of Δ ​ a μ \Delta a_{\mu} with new scalar and lepton mass as low as around TeV. This mass scale is allowed by the current collider searches for doubly charged scalars and the lepton triplet, and they can be tested at future high energy and/or high luminosity colliders.

Neutrino mass generation from hybrid seesaw

The neutrino mass in our hybrid seesaw model is determined after diagonalizing the neutral lepton mass matrix in Eq. (
9
) as follows

\displaystyle m_{\nu}=\frac{1}{2}[(M_{F}-\sqrt{2}x_{L}v_{\delta})-\sqrt{(M_{F}+\sqrt{2}x_{L}v_{\delta})^{2}+z_{L}^{2}v_{h}^{2}}]\approx-\sqrt{2}x_{L}v_{\delta}-\frac{z_{L}^{2}v_{h}^{2}}{4M_{F}},

(13)

in which

after EWSB. This implies that neutrino masses are generated by both Type-II (first term in the R.H.S of Eq. (
13
)) and Type-III seesaw mechanism (second term in R.H.S of Eq. (
13
)).
Since the neutrino mass is order of 0.1 eV, we require some fine-tuning to get the correct neutrino masses in our parameter space. We can define the following quantity to measure the degree of fine-tuning:

\displaystyle\Delta_{FT}\equiv\frac{m_{\nu}}{x_{L}v_{\delta}},

(14)

which turns out to be
\Delta_{FT}\sim\mathcal{O}(10^{-9})
when considering neutrino mass being at order of 0.1 eV and the parameter space in our numerical analyses. Such fine-tuning is difficult to justify by invoking additional symmetries. However, the fine-tuning of the neutrino mass might be explained by the anthropic principle
[
47
]
, as a neutrino mass larger than
\mathcal{O}(1)
eV would destroy the large-scale structure of our universe.

•

Correction to muon mass

Diagrams in Fig.
1

. They are divided into three parts according to the fermions appearing in the loop. Originating from the left, middle and right panel, respectively, the analytical results of
a^{\textrm{NP}}
generated by our model can be decomposed to

\displaystyle a^{\textrm{NP}}_{\mu}\equiv a_{\mu}^{\ell,\,\textrm{total}}+a_{\mu}^{F,\,\textrm{total}}+a_{\mu}^{\nu,\,\textrm{total}}\,.

(26)

Note that all Yukawa couplings in our model are chosen to be real, but we will present our analytical results in the context of complex Yukawa couplings for the more general scenario. To write down the analytical results, we first define the following expressions as the reduced form of loop functions (see
[
57
]
for a more complete list).

•

In calculating
a_{\mu}^{\ell,\,\textrm{total}}
from the left panel of Fig.
1
which satisfies
m_{\ell}\ll m_{h},\,m_{\delta}
, we define

\displaystyle F_{LL}^{f,1}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{6}+x(\frac{1}{2}\log x+\frac{25}{24})\,,\quad F_{LR}^{f,1}(x)=-\frac{1}{2}\log x-\frac{3}{4}+x(-2\log x-\frac{8}{3})\,,

\displaystyle F_{LL}^{S,1}(x)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{12}+\frac{1}{8}x\,,\qquad\qquad\quad\,F_{LR}^{S,1}(x)=-\frac{1}{4}+x(-\frac{1}{2}\log x-\frac{11}{12})\,.

(27)

•

In calculating
a_{\mu}^{F,\,\textrm{total}}
from the middle panel of Fig.
1
which satisfies
m_{\ell}\ll m_{F},\,m_{\delta}
, we define

\displaystyle F_{LL}^{f,2}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{2+3x-6x^{2}+x^{3}+6x\log x}{12(1-x)^{4}}\,,\quad\quad\,\,\,\,F_{LR}^{f,2}(x)=\frac{-3+4x-x^{2}-2\log x}{4(1-x)^{3}}\,,

\displaystyle F_{LL}^{S,2}(x)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1-6x+3x^{2}+2x^{3}-6x^{2}\log x}{12(1-x)^{4}}\,,\quad F_{LR}^{S,2}(x)=\frac{-1+x^{2}-2x\log x}{4(1-x)^{3}}\,.

(28)

•

In calculating
a_{\mu}^{\nu,\,\textrm{total}}
from the right panel of Fig.
1
which satisfies
m_{\nu}\ll m_{\ell}\ll m_{\delta}
, we define

\displaystyle F_{LL}^{S,3}(x)=-\frac{1}{12}(1+\frac{1}{2}x)\,,\quad F_{LR}^{S,3}(x)=-\frac{1}{4}(1+\frac{2}{3}x)\,.

(29)

As a result of the chiral enhancement effects,
a_{\mu}^{F,\,\textrm{total}}
in Eq. (
26
) turns out to play the absolutely dominant role compared to
a_{\mu}^{\ell,\,\textrm{total}}
and
a_{\mu}^{\nu,\,\textrm{total}}
, i.e.

a_{\mu}^{F,\,\textrm{total}}\,\gg\,a_{\mu}^{\ell,\,\textrm{total}}\,,\,a_{\mu}^{\nu,\,\textrm{total}}\,.

(30)

Furthermore,
a_{\mu}^{F}
originating from the middle panel of Fig.
1
can be written as

\displaystyle a_{\mu}^{F,\,\textrm{total}}=a_{\mu}^{F,\,h}+a_{\mu}^{F,\,\delta^{0}}+a_{\mu}^{F,\,a^{0}}+a_{\mu}^{F,\,\delta^{--}}+a_{\mu}^{F,\,\delta^{-}}\,,

(31)

in which we find that
a_{\mu}^{F,\,\delta^{--}}
and
a_{\mu}^{F,\,\delta^{-}}
contribute the dominant and sub-dominant part, respectively. Their expressions are

\displaystyle a_{\mu}^{F,\,\delta^{--}}

\displaystyle=\quad\frac{m_{\mu}^{2}}{8\pi^{2}m_{\delta^{--}}^{2}}[4|x_{L}|^{2}(s_{L}^{\ell}c_{L}^{\ell})^{2}+|\lambda_{L}|^{2}\big((c_{R}^{\ell})^{2}-(s_{R}^{\ell})^{2}\big)^{2}]\cdot[-F_{LL}^{f,2}(\frac{m_{F^{\pm}}^{2}}{m_{\delta^{--}}^{2}})+2F_{LL}^{S,2}(\frac{m_{F^{\pm}}^{2}}{m_{\delta^{--}}^{2}})]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{m_{\mu}m_{F^{\pm}}}{4\pi^{2}m_{\delta^{--}}^{2}}\textrm{Re}[(2x_{L}s_{L}^{\ell}c_{L}^{\ell})\big(\lambda_{L}^{\ast}((c_{R}^{\ell})^{2}-(s_{R}^{\ell})^{2})\big)]\cdot[-F_{LR}^{f,2}(\frac{m_{F^{\pm}}^{2}}{m_{\delta^{--}}^{2}})+2F_{LR}^{S,2}(\frac{m_{F^{\pm}}^{2}}{m_{\delta^{--}}^{2}})]\,,

\displaystyle a_{\mu}^{F,\,\delta^{-}}

\displaystyle=\quad\frac{m_{\mu}^{2}}{8\pi^{2}m_{\delta^{-}}^{2}}[2|x_{L}|^{2}(s_{L}^{\nu}c_{L}^{\ell}c^{G})^{2}+|\lambda_{L}|^{2}(c_{L}^{\nu}c_{R}^{\ell}c^{G})^{2}]\cdot F_{LL}^{S,2}(\frac{m_{F^{0}}^{2}}{m_{\delta^{-}}^{2}})

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{m_{\mu}m_{F^{0}}}{4\pi^{2}m_{\delta^{-}}^{2}}\textrm{Re}[(\sqrt{2}x_{L}s_{L}^{\nu}c_{L}^{\ell}c^{G})(\lambda_{L}^{\ast}c_{L}^{\nu}c_{R}^{\ell}c^{G})]\cdot F_{LR}^{S,2}(\frac{m_{F^{0}}^{2}}{m_{\delta^{-}}^{2}})\,.

(32)

To simplify our analytical results and highlight the chiral enhancement, we utilize the small mixing condition
s^{\nu}_{L},s^{\ell}_{L},s^{\ell}_{R},s^{h},s^{a},s^{G}\ll 1
discussed in
B.2
and extract the most relevant terms in Eq. (
78
) as follows

\displaystyle a^{\textrm{NP}}_{\mu}\approx

\displaystyle\,a_{\mu}^{F,\,\textrm{total}}\approx\,a_{\mu}^{F,\,\delta^{--}}+a_{\mu}^{F,\,\delta^{-}}\,,

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\quad\quad\frac{m_{\mu}m_{F^{\pm}}}{4\pi^{2}m_{\delta^{--}}^{2}}\textrm{Re}[(2x_{L}s_{L}^{\ell}c_{L}^{\ell})\big(\lambda_{L}^{\ast}((c_{R}^{\ell})^{2}-(s_{R}^{\ell})^{2})\big)]\cdot[-F_{LR}^{f,2}(\frac{m_{F^{\pm}}^{2}}{m_{\delta^{--}}^{2}})+2F_{LR}^{S,2}(\frac{m_{F^{\pm}}^{2}}{m_{\delta^{--}}^{2}})]

\displaystyle+\frac{m_{\mu}m_{F^{0}}}{4\pi^{2}m_{\delta^{-}}^{2}}\textrm{Re}[(\sqrt{2}x_{L}s_{L}^{\nu}c_{L}^{\ell}c^{G})(\lambda_{L}^{\ast}c_{L}^{\nu}c_{R}^{\ell}c^{G})]\cdot F_{LR}^{S,2}(\frac{m_{F^{0}}^{2}}{m_{\delta^{-}}^{2}})\,,

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\quad\quad\frac{m_{\mu}m_{F^{\pm}}}{4\pi^{2}m_{\delta^{--}}^{2}}\textrm{Re}[(2x_{L}s_{L}^{\ell})(\lambda_{L}^{\ast})]\cdot[-F_{LR}^{f,2}(\frac{m_{F^{\pm}}^{2}}{m_{\delta^{--}}^{2}})+2F_{LR}^{S,2}(\frac{m_{F^{\pm}}^{2}}{m_{\delta^{--}}^{2}})]

\displaystyle+\frac{m_{\mu}m_{F^{0}}}{4\pi^{2}m_{\delta^{-}}^{2}}\textrm{Re}[(\sqrt{2}x_{L}s_{L}^{\nu})(\lambda_{L}^{\ast})]\cdot F_{LR}^{S,2}(\frac{m_{F^{0}}^{2}}{m_{\delta^{-}}^{2}})\,.

(33)

Utilizing
s^{\ell}_{L}\approx\sqrt{2}s^{\nu}_{L}
from Eq. (
57
), as well as the mass relations
m_{\delta}\equiv m_{\delta^{0}}\approx m_{\delta^{-}}\approx m_{\delta^{--}}
from Eq. (
66
) and
m_{F}\equiv m_{F^{\pm}}\approx m_{F^{0}}
from Eq. (
18
), we can have the following simple expressions

\displaystyle a^{\textrm{NP}}_{\mu}\approx\frac{\sqrt{2}m_{\mu}m_{F}}{4\pi^{2}m_{\delta}^{2}}\textrm{Re}[x_{L}\lambda_{L}^{\ast}]s_{L}^{\nu}\cdot[-2F_{LR}^{f,2}(\frac{m_{F}^{2}}{m_{\delta}^{2}})+5F_{LR}^{S,2}(\frac{m_{F}^{2}}{m_{\delta}^{2}})]\,.

(34)

Note that only two of the parameters
x_{L},\lambda_{L},s_{L}^{\nu}
are independent.
We also checked that the modification to our results is negligible when further considering the scalar and lepton mass splittings in the calculations, which is less than
\mathcal{O}(10^{-2})
.

III.3
Phenomenological discussions

In this section we briefly discuss the phenomenological aspects of our model.

Note that the scalar triplet and lepton triplet have been scrutinized in neutrino mass generation mechanisms as Type-II
[
34
,
35
,
36
,
37
,
38
,
39
]
and Type-III
[
40
,
41
]
seesaw models, respectively. The relevant collier signals at LHC of these new physics triplets have been discussed in recent comprehensive reviews based on latest data collected at ATLAS
[
58
]
and CMS
[
59
]
experiments. Generally speaking, lower bounds on masses around 1 TeV have been established for the new triplets, with specific assumptions on their production and decay modes.
Further discussions on the prospects of searches at future high luminosity LHC and proposed 100 TeV
pp
collider can be found in
[
60
,
61
,
62
]
for scalar triplet and
[
63
,
64
,
65
]
for lepton triplet. Note that in this work we only concentrate on the physics of
(g-2)_{\mu}
and do not require our model to produce the experimentally suggested texture of neutrino mass matrix. Instead, we require our model parameters to ensure the theoretically predicted neutrino mass to be negligibly small and close to zero. Aside from the neutrino physics, recently it has been found that the Type-II seesaw model can also address the problem of baryon asymmetry of the universe (see e.g.
[
66
,
67
,
68
,
69
]
).

We need to point out that the current bounds listed in
[
58
]
and
[
59
]
cannot be applied directly to our model, given the fact that both scalar and lepton triplet exist in our model. Taking
m_{F}>m_{\delta}
as a case example, which is the set up in our numerical analyses and is capable of explaining
\Delta a_{\mu}
, there is one main aspect differentiating our model from the exclusive Type-III seesaw signals. That is, after
F^{0},F^{\pm}
being singly or pairly produced, there are additional decay channels
F^{0}\rightarrow\ell^{\pm}\delta^{\mp}
,
F^{-}\rightarrow\ell^{-}\delta^{0},\ell^{-}a^{0},\ell^{+}\delta^{--}
, which do not exist in the existing search channels for Type-III seesaw mainly consisting of
F^{0}\rightarrow\nu h,\nu Z,\ell^{\pm}W^{\mp}
and
F^{\pm}\rightarrow\ell^{\pm}h,\ell^{\pm}Z,\nu W^{\pm}
.

To explain the above point, we first consider the partial decay widths of neutral heavy lepton
F^{0}
given as follows.

\displaystyle\Gamma(F^{0}\rightarrow\nu h)

\displaystyle\approx\frac{1}{16\pi m_{F}}\frac{m_{F}^{2}z_{L}^{2}}{4}\approx\frac{1}{16\pi m_{F}}\frac{m_{F}^{4}(s_{L}^{\nu})^{2}}{v^{2}},

\displaystyle\Gamma(F^{0}\rightarrow\nu\delta^{0})

\displaystyle\approx\frac{1}{8\pi m_{F}}(1-\frac{m_{\delta}^{2}}{m_{F}^{2}})m_{F}^{2}x_{L}^{2}(s_{L}^{\nu})^{2}\approx\frac{1}{16\pi m_{F}}(1-\frac{m_{\delta}^{2}}{m_{F}^{2}})\frac{m_{F}^{4}(s_{L}^{\nu})^{6}}{v_{\delta}^{2}},

\displaystyle\Gamma(F^{0}\rightarrow\mu^{\pm}\delta^{\mp})

\displaystyle\approx\frac{1}{16\pi m_{F}}(1-\frac{m_{\delta}^{2}}{m_{F}^{2}})\frac{m_{F}^{2}\lambda_{L}^{2}}{2},

\displaystyle\Gamma(F^{0}\rightarrow\nu Z)

\displaystyle\approx\frac{1}{16\pi m_{F}}\frac{m_{F}^{4}(s_{L}^{\nu})^{2}}{v^{2}},

\displaystyle\Gamma(F^{0}\rightarrow\mu^{\pm}W^{\mp})

\displaystyle\approx\frac{1}{16\pi m_{F}}\frac{m_{F}^{4}(s_{L}^{\nu})^{2}}{v^{2}}.

(36)

Then, we find that

\displaystyle\Gamma(F^{0}\rightarrow\nu h):\Gamma(F^{0}\rightarrow\nu Z):\Gamma(F^{0}\rightarrow\mu^{\pm}W^{\mp})\approx 1:1:1,

\displaystyle\Gamma(F^{0}\rightarrow\nu h):\Gamma(F^{0}\rightarrow\nu\delta^{0}):\Gamma(F^{0}\rightarrow\mu^{\pm}\delta^{\mp})\approx 1:(1-\frac{m_{\delta}^{2}}{m_{F}^{2}})\frac{(s_{L}^{\nu})^{4}v^{2}}{v_{\delta}^{2}}:(1-\frac{m_{\delta}^{2}}{m_{F}^{2}})\frac{\lambda_{L}^{2}v^{2}}{2m_{F}^{2}(s_{L}^{\nu})^{2}}.

(37)

Taking
\lambda_{L}=1,v_{\delta}=5\ \mathrm{GeV},m_{\delta}=1\ \mathrm{TeV},m_{F}=2\ \mathrm{TeV},s_{L}^{\nu}=0.017
, the
\Gamma(F^{0}\rightarrow\nu\delta^{0})
is negligible. The other decay widths are estimated as

\displaystyle\Gamma(F^{0}\rightarrow\nu h):\Gamma(F^{0}\rightarrow\nu Z):\Gamma(F^{0}\rightarrow\mu^{\pm}W^{\mp}):\Gamma(F^{0}\rightarrow\mu^{\pm}\delta^{\mp})\approx 1:1:1:19.6.

(38)

We can see that
F^{0}\rightarrow\mu^{\pm}\delta^{\mp}
is the dominating decay channel in the our
\Delta a_{\mu}
-favored parameter space.

Similarly, the partial decay widths of charged heavy lepton
F^{-}
are computed as follows.

\displaystyle\Gamma(F^{-}\rightarrow\mu^{-}h)

\displaystyle\approx\frac{1}{16\pi m_{F}}\frac{m_{F}^{4}(s_{L}^{\nu})^{2}}{v^{2}},

\displaystyle\Gamma(F^{-}\rightarrow\mu^{-}\delta^{0})

\displaystyle\approx\frac{1}{16\pi m_{F}}(1-\frac{m_{\delta}^{2}}{m_{F}^{2}})\frac{m_{F}^{2}\lambda_{L}^{2}}{4},

\displaystyle\Gamma(F^{-}\rightarrow\nu\delta^{-})

\displaystyle\approx\frac{1}{16\pi m_{F}}(1-\frac{m_{\delta}^{2}}{m_{F}^{2}})m_{F}^{2}(s_{L}^{\nu})^{2}[2x_{L}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{L}^{2}(s_{R}^{\ell})^{2}]

\displaystyle\approx\frac{1}{16\pi m_{F}}(1-\frac{m_{\delta}^{2}}{m_{F}^{2}})m_{F}^{2}(s_{L}^{\nu})^{2}[\frac{m_{F}^{2}(s_{L}^{\nu})^{4}}{v_{\delta}^{2}}+\frac{\lambda_{L}^{4}v_{\delta}^{2}}{4m_{F}^{2}}],

\displaystyle\Gamma(F^{-}\rightarrow\mu^{+}\delta^{--})

\displaystyle\approx\frac{1}{16\pi m_{F}}(1-\frac{m_{\delta}^{2}}{m_{F}^{2}})\frac{m_{F}^{2}\lambda_{L}^{2}}{2},

\displaystyle\Gamma(F^{-}\rightarrow\mu^{-}Z)

\displaystyle\approx\frac{1}{16\pi m_{F}}\frac{m_{F}^{4}(s_{L}^{\nu})^{2}}{v^{2}},

\displaystyle\Gamma(F^{-}\rightarrow\nu W^{-})

\displaystyle\approx\frac{1}{16\pi m_{F}}\frac{2m_{F}^{4}(s_{L}^{\nu})^{2}}{v^{2}}.

(39)

Then, we find that

\displaystyle\Gamma(F^{-}\rightarrow\mu^{-}h):\Gamma(F^{-}\rightarrow\mu^{-}Z):\Gamma(F^{-}\rightarrow\nu W^{-})\approx 1:1:2,

\displaystyle\Gamma(F^{-}\rightarrow\mu^{-}h):\Gamma(F^{-}\rightarrow\mu^{-}\delta^{0}):\Gamma(F^{-}\rightarrow\nu\delta^{-}):\Gamma(F^{-}\rightarrow\mu^{+}\delta^{--})

\displaystyle\approx 1:(1-\frac{m_{\delta}^{2}}{m_{F}^{2}})\frac{\lambda_{L}^{2}v^{2}}{4m_{F}^{2}(s_{L}^{\nu})^{2}}:(1-\frac{m_{\delta}^{2}}{m_{F}^{2}})[\frac{(s_{L}^{\nu})^{4}v^{2}}{v_{\delta}^{2}}+\frac{\lambda_{L}^{4}v^{2}v_{\delta}^{2}}{4m_{F}^{4}}]:(1-\frac{m_{\delta}^{2}}{m_{F}^{2}})\frac{\lambda_{L}^{2}v^{2}}{2m_{F}^{2}(s_{L}^{\nu})^{2}}.

(40)

Taking
\lambda_{L}=1,v_{\delta}=5\ \mathrm{GeV},m_{\delta}=1\ \mathrm{TeV},m_{F}=2\ \mathrm{TeV},s_{L}^{\nu}=0.017
, the
\Gamma(F^{-}\rightarrow\nu\delta^{-})
is negligible. The other decay widths are estimated as

\displaystyle\Gamma(F^{-}\rightarrow\mu^{-}h):\Gamma(F^{-}\rightarrow\mu^{-}Z):\Gamma(F^{-}\rightarrow\nu W^{-}):\Gamma(F^{-}\rightarrow\mu^{-}\delta^{0}):\Gamma(F^{-}\rightarrow\mu^{+}\delta^{--})

\displaystyle\approx 1:1:2:9.8:19.6.

(41)

We can see that
F^{-}\rightarrow\mu^{+}\delta^{--}
and
F^{-}\rightarrow\mu^{-}\delta^{0}
are the leading and sub-leading contributions, respectively.

Now we continue to study the decay of
\delta^{0},\delta^{-},\delta^{--}
as the decay products from
F^{-}\rightarrow\mu^{+}\delta^{--}
,
F^{0}\rightarrow\mu^{\pm}\delta^{\mp}
and
F^{-}\rightarrow\mu^{-}\delta^{0}
. We have the following results

\displaystyle\Gamma(\delta^{0}\rightarrow\mu^{+}\mu^{-}):\Gamma(\delta^{0}\rightarrow W^{+}W^{-})\sim 1:\frac{2m_{\delta}^{2}v_{\delta}^{2}}{\lambda_{L}^{2}v^{4}(s_{L}^{\nu})^{2}}

\displaystyle\Gamma(\delta^{-}\rightarrow\mu^{-}\nu):\Gamma(\delta^{-}\rightarrow W^{-}Z)\sim 1:\frac{2m_{\delta}^{2}v_{\delta}^{2}}{v^{4}[\lambda_{L}^{2}(s_{L}^{\nu})^{2}+(s_{L}^{\nu})^{4}m_{F}^{2}/v_{\delta}^{2}]}

\displaystyle\Gamma(\delta^{--}\rightarrow\mu^{-}\mu^{-}):\Gamma(\delta^{--}\rightarrow W^{-}W^{-})\sim 1:\frac{4m_{\delta}^{2}v_{\delta}^{2}}{v^{4}[\lambda_{L}^{4}v_{\delta}^{2}/m_{F}^{2}+(s_{L}^{\nu})^{4}m_{F}^{2}/v_{\delta}^{2}]}

.

(42)

Taking
\lambda_{L}=1,v_{\delta}=5\ \mathrm{GeV},m_{\delta}=1\ \mathrm{TeV},m_{F}=2\ \mathrm{TeV},s_{L}^{\nu}=0.017
, the decay ratios are estimated as

\displaystyle\Gamma(\delta^{0}\rightarrow\mu^{+}\mu^{-}):\Gamma(\delta^{0}\rightarrow W^{+}W^{-})\sim 1:47,

\displaystyle\Gamma(\delta^{-}\rightarrow\mu^{-}\nu):\Gamma(\delta^{-}\rightarrow W^{-}Z)\sim 1:1,

\displaystyle\Gamma(\delta^{--}\rightarrow\mu^{-}\mu^{-}):\Gamma(\delta^{--}\rightarrow W^{-}W^{-})\sim 1:2.

(43)

We can see that
\delta^{0},\delta^{\pm},\delta^{\pm\pm}
will further decay into two gauge bosons
WW,WZ
with dominating or sizable branching ratios
[
70
,
60
]
. Finally,
WW,WZ
will decay into leptonic or hadronic states.

Therefore, in comparison to the existing searches for Type-III fermion triplets utilizing
2,3,4
leptons accompanied by jets, our signal profile include even more leptons and jets. This increased particle multiplicity can enable more dedicated signal selection and background reduction and we leave the analysis for future works.

IV
Conclusions

In this work, we investigated the muon anomalous dipole moment
a_{\mu}
in a model that extends the SM with a scalar triplet and a lepton triplet. We identify an important ingredient overlooked in previous studies, i.e. the Yukawa interaction involving the SM Higgs doublet and the newly introduced lepton triplet. This interaction is a four-dimensional operator and thus can naturally exist. We show that this Yukawa interaction can not only induce mass mixing between leptons in the SM and the new physics sector, but also provide an additional source of chiral flip to
a_{\mu}
.
We find that there is still viable parameter space in this model to explain
\Delta a_{\mu}
, which is different from the observation reported in the existing literature. More specifically, while being consistent with the current data of neutrino mass, electroweak precision measurements and the perturbativity of couplings, our model can provide new physics contribution
a_{\mu}^{\textrm{NP}}
to cover the central region of
\Delta a_{\mu}
with new scalar and fermion mass as low as around TeV. This mass scale is allowed by the current collider searches for doubly charged scalars and the lepton triplet, and they can be tested at future high energy and/or high luminosity colliders.

Appendix D
Estimations on the corrections to muon mass

For the general muon Yukawa interaction of
(y_{L}\overline{\mu_{R}}f_{L}+y_{R}\overline{\mu_{L}}f_{R})S+\mathrm{h.c.}
, the muon mass correction at one-loop is in the form of

m_{\mu}(|y_{L}|^{2}+|y_{R}|^{2})
and
m_{f}\left[y_{L}(y_{R})^{\ast}+y_{R}(y_{L})^{\ast}\right]
.
For
m_{\mu}\ll m_{f}
, the
m_{\mu}(|y_{L}|^{2}+|y_{R}|^{2})
part is usually small. Then, the loop corrections to the muon mass can be naively taken as

\displaystyle\delta m\sim\frac{1}{16\pi^{2}}m_{f}\left[y_{L}(y_{R})^{\ast}+y_{R}(y_{L})^{\ast}\right],

(85)

which agrees with the estimation in Ref.
[
84
]
.

We can make a rough estimation for our model. For gauge eigenstate interactions in (
8
), the relevant interactions are
x_{L}^{ij}\overline{L_{L}^{i}}S\epsilon(L_{L}^{j})^{C}
and
\lambda_{L}^{i}\overline{\ell_{R}^{i}}\mathrm{Tr}\left[F_{L}S\right]
. Besides, there should be a mixing between
L_{L}
and
F_{L}
induced by the
z_{L}^{i}\overline{L_{L}^{i}}(F_{L})^{C}\epsilon H^{\ast}
interaction. Then, the correction is estimated as

\displaystyle\frac{\delta m}{m_{\mu}}\sim\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}}\frac{m_{F}}{m_{\mu}}x_{L}\lambda_{L}\frac{z_{L}v_{h}}{m_{F}}=\frac{x_{L}\lambda_{L}}{8\pi^{2}}\frac{z_{L}v_{h}}{m_{\mu}}.

(86)

In our numerical results, we fix
v_{\delta}=5\mathrm{GeV}
. If we take
\{\lambda_{L},x_{L}\}
as input parameters,
z_{L}
is determined. Thus, the correction can be simplified as

\displaystyle\frac{\delta m}{m_{\mu}}\sim\frac{x_{L}\lambda_{L}}{4\pi^{2}}\sqrt{-\sqrt{2}\,x_{L}\frac{v_{\delta}\,m_{F}}{m_{\mu}^{2}}}\approx-20\lambda_{L}(-x_{L})^{\frac{3}{2}}\sqrt{\frac{m_{F}}{\mathrm{TeV}}}.

(87)

Then, we find that
\delta m/m_{\mu}\sim-0.5
for
\lambda_{L}=1,x_{L}=-0.06,m_{F}=3\,\mathrm{TeV}
. Hence, the mass correction is estimated as
\delta m/m_{\mu}\sim\mathcal{O}(1)
for the parameters in our numerical analysis.

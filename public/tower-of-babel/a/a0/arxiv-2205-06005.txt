Stochastic fractional conservation laws
Abhishek Chaudhary

Abstract
. In this paper, we consider the Cauchy problem for the nonlinear fractional conservation laws driven by a multiplicative noise. In particular, we are concerned with the well-posedness theory and the study of the long-time behavior of solutions for such equations. We show the existence of desired kinetic solution by using the vanishing viscosity method. In fact, we establish strong convergence of the approximate viscous solutions to a kinetic solution. Moreover, under a nonlinearity-diffusivity condition, we prove the existence of an invariant measure using the well-known Krylov-Bogoliubov theorem. Finally, we show the uniqueness and ergodicity of the invariant measure.

Nonlinear nonlocal integro-PDEs have great demand in mathematics due to their applications in several different areas such as mathematical finance

1.2.
Aim and outline of this paper

Equation (
1.1
) is a fractional convection-diffusion equation, and this class of equations have received considerable interest recently thanks to the wide variety of applications. Due to the nonlinearity involves in equation (
1.1
), classical solutions to (
1.1
) are hard to find, and weak solutions must be sought. We adapt the notion of kinetic formulation for solutions to (
1.1
), which is only weak in space variable but strong in time variable, as proposed in
[
22
]
. To sum up, we aim at developing the following results related to (
1.1
):

(i)

Our first aim is to establish the well-posedness theory for solutions to the Cauchy problem (
1.1
). The proof of well-posedness theory is rather classical but still quite technical, the hardest part is probably the uniqueness part (see step 3 in the proof of Theorem
3.3
). The proof of existence is based on the vanishing viscosity argument, while the uniqueness proof is settled by extending Kruzkov’s doubling of variable technique in the presence of multiplicative noise. Note that the noise coefficient
\Psi
has an explicit dependency on the spatial variable
x
as well. In this case, the proof of contraction principle (see Theorem
3.3
) requires a change in hierarchy while computing limits with respect to various parameters involved (for details, see (
3.1
)). Indeed, this technical hurdle forces us to analyze the general equation with values of
\alpha
in
(0,\frac{1}{2})
. However, when the noise coefficient
\Psi=\Psi(u)
, we can extend well-posedness theory for
\alpha\in(0,1)
(see Theorem
2.4
).

(ii)

The second main contribution of this paper is the analysis of the long-time behavior of the kinetic solution. In fact, we obtain existence of an invariant measure through the Krylov-Bogoliubov theorem under a nonlinearity-diffusivity condition (
2.14
) (see Theorem
2.6
). The proof of the existence of invariant measure itself is much more delicate than in the hyperbolic case
[
21
]
. Moreover, we show that the law of kinetic solution
u(t)
converges to an unique invariant measure as
t\to\,\infty
(see (
4.45
)). We also establish a well-posedness theory for initial data in
L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
which is needed for the framework of invariant measure (see Theorem
2.5
).

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we give the details of assumptions and introduce the basic setting, define the notion of a kinetic solution, and state our main results, Theorem
2.4
, Theorem
2.5
, Theorem
2.6
. Section
3
is devoted to well-posedness theory in
L^{p}
-setting. Subsection
3.1
is devoted to the proof of uniqueness together with the proof of
L^{1}
-contraction principle. In Subsection
3.2
, we prove the existence part of Theorem
2.4
which is divided into two parts. First, we prove the existence for the smooth initial condition. Second, we relax the hypothesis upon initial data and prove the existence for general initial data. In Section
4
, we prove the existence and uniqueness of invariant measure, Theorem
2.6
. In Appendix
A
, we briefly derive the kinetic formulation of equation (
1.1
). In Appendix
B
, we give the proof of Theorem
2.5
.

2.4.
Invariant measure

Suppose that noise is only additive in nature. Let

\mathcal{Q}_{t}:B_{b}(L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{N}))\to B_{b}(L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{N}))

\big(\mathcal{Q}_{t}\kappa\big)(v)=\mathbb{E}[\kappa(u(t,0,v))]

Suppose that

denotes the solution to (

\|u(s,t,u_{1})-u(s,t,u_{2})\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{N})}\,\leq\,\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{N})}

It implies that
\mathcal{Q}_{t}\kappa\,\in\,C_{b}(L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{N}))

\mathbb{E}[\kappa(u(t+s,0,v))|\mathcal{F}_{t}]=\mathcal{Q}_{s}\kappa\big(u(t,0,v)\big),\,\,\,\forall\,\,\kappa\,\in\,C_{b}(L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{N}))\,\,\,\forall\,t,s\,\textgreater\,0,\,\,\,\mathbb{P}-\text{almost surely},

holds. The equation

1.1

defines a Feller Markov process. The semigroup

We now denote the law of

\mathcal{Q}_{t}\kappa(v)=\mathbb{E}[\kappa(u(t,0,v))]=\int_{L^{1}({\mathbb{T}^{N}})}\kappa(y)\lambda_{t,v}(dy),

It follows that

2.6.
Some basic facts about Young measure

Roughly speaking a Young measure is a parametrized family of probability measures where the parameters are drawn from a finite measure space. Let
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R})
be the space of probability measure on
\mathbb{R}
.

Definition 2.6

(
Young measure
)
.

Suppose that
(\mathcal{X},\lambda)
is a finite measure space. A mapping
\mathfrak{\mathcal{V}}
from
\mathcal{X}
to
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R})
is said to be a Young measure if, for all
\kappa\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R})
, the map

from
\mathcal{X}
into
\mathbb{R}
is measurable. We say that a Young measure
\mathfrak{\mathcal{V}}
vanishes at infinity if , for all
p\geq 1
,

\int_{\mathcal{X}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\mathfrak{\zeta}|^{p}d\mathfrak{\mathcal{V}}_{w}(\mathfrak{\zeta})d\lambda(w)\textless\infty.

Definition 2.7

(
Kinetic function
)
.

Suppose that
(\mathcal{X}
,
\lambda
) is a finite measure space. A measurable function
f:\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}\to[0,1]
is said to be a kinetic function if there exists a Young measure
\mathfrak{\mathcal{V}}
on
\mathcal{X}
vanishing at infinity such that, for
\lambda
-a.e.
w\in\mathcal{X}
, for all
\mathfrak{\zeta}\in\mathbb{R}
,

f(w,\mathfrak{\zeta})=\mathfrak{\mathcal{V}}_{w}(\mathfrak{\zeta},\infty).

Definition 2.8

(
Equilibrium
)
.

A measurable function
f:\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}\to[0,1]
is said to be an equilibrium if there exists a measurable function
v:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
f(w,\mathfrak{\zeta})=\mathbbm{1}_{v(w)\textgreater\mathfrak{\zeta}}
almost every
w\in\mathcal{X}
.

Definition 2.9

(
Convergence of Young measure
)
.

Suppose that
(\mathcal{X},\lambda)
is a finite measure space. A sequence of Young measures
\mathcal{V}^{n}
on
\mathcal{X}
said to conveges to a Young measure
\mathcal{V}
on
\mathcal{X}
provided the following convergence holds: for all

, for all

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int_{\mathcal{X}}h(y)\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(\zeta)d\mathcal{V}_{z}^{n}(\zeta)d\lambda(y)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{X}}h(y)\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(\zeta)d\mathcal{V}_{y}(\zeta)d\lambda(y).

(2.21)

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{k\geq 1}\int_{0}^{t}\langle\varphi^{\prime}(u)h_{k}(x,u),\kappa\rangle d\beta_{k}(s)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\langle\varphi^{\prime\prime}(u)H^{2}(x,u),\kappa\rangle ds.

3.1
, Theorem
3.3
and Proposition
B.2
, we are in a position to apply
[
21
, Lemma 17]
, which implies the continuity in
L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
. Indeed, let us first show that
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely,
u
is right continuous in
L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
. From the Proposition
3.1

f(t+\delta_{n})\to f(t)

\mathbb{P}

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|(u(t)-n)^{\pm}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{N})}=0.

Therefore, there exists a subsequence which converges
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely, that is,
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|(u(t)-n)^{\pm}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{N})}=0.

Consequently,
[
21
, Lemma 17]
applies and gives the following convergence
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely,

u(t+\delta_{n})\,\to\,u(t)\,\,\,\,\,\text{in}\,\,\,\,L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{N})\,\,\,\,\text{as}\,\,\,\,\,\delta_{n}\,\to\,0.

By the same arguments we can show that
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely,
u^{-}

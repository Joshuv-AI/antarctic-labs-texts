Stability of the Positive Mass and Torus Rigidty Theorems Under Integral Curvature Bounds
Brian Allen, Edward Bryden, Demetre Kazaras

Abstract
. Work of D. Stern [ 46 ] and Bray-Kazaras-Khuri-Stern [ 9 ] provide differential-geometric identities which relate the scalar curvature of Riemannian 3 3 -manifolds to global invariants in terms of harmonic functions. These quantitative formulas are useful for stability results [ 46 , 27 ] and show promise for more applications of this type. In this paper, we analyze harmonic maps to flat model spaces in order to address conjectures concerning the geometric stability of the positive mass theorem and the Geroch conjecture. By imposing integral Ricci curvature and isoperimetric bounds, we leverage the previously mentioned formulas to establish strong control on these harmonic maps. When the mass of an asymptotically flat manifold is sufficiently small or when a Riemannian torus has almost non-negative scalar curvature, we upgrade the maps to diffeomorphisms and give quantitative Hölder closeness to the model spaces.

Through analysis of this equation, one is able to show that the class of manifolds with integral bounds on Ricci (or Riemann) curvature and injectivity radius bounds (or volume growth of balls) enjoys a system of harmonic coordinate charts in which the metric is controlled in Sobolev and Hölder spaces.
However, the arguments which obtain this control often proceed by contradiction, and the resulting metric control is generally not explicitly quantitative in terms of given geometric bounds.

In this paper we take a new approach to the harmonic coordinate method described above. On asymptotically flat
3

By using these breakthrough formulas in tandem with the geometric controls following from integral Ricci curvature and isoperimetric bounds, we strongly control the regularity and behavior of the harmonic functions. Next, when the appropriate quantities are chosen to be small enough, we are able to show that these harmonic functions produce a global coordinate system. This global coordinate system is the source of the topological stability as well as quantitative Hölder stability for both the positive mass theorem and scalar torus rigidity theorem. For the positive mass theorem, this stability is explicitly computable in terms of integral Riemann curvature and isoperimetric bounds. In the case of the scalar torus rigidity theorem, the stability remains implicitly quantitative due to the use of a contradiction argument in the spirit of Petersen.

The main tool we use to study the geometric stability of the positive mass theorem is the following formula relating the ADM mass to scalar curvature through an asymptotically linear harmonic function.

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem 1.2 of
[
9
]
)
.

Let
(M,g)
be an orientable complete asymptotically flat manifold with no spherical classes in
H_{2}(M;\mathbb{Z})
, and let
u
be an asymptotically linear harmonic function (see Section
4
). Then, the ADM mass satisfies

(1)

m(g)\geq\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{M}\frac{|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}}{|\nabla u|}+R_{g}|\nabla u|dV_{g},

where
R_{g}
denotes the scalar curvature of
(M,g)
.

The first main result leverages controlled asymptotic falloff with good control on the local analysis of Sobolev functions in order to show that ADM mass strongly controls geometry. In order to effectively utilize the mass formula (
1
), we will need Sobolev and Poincaré inequalities on our manifold. Crucially, these inequalities are known to be equivalent to isoperimetric constants (See Li
[
30
]
). Let us now recall the isoperimetric constant central to our result.

Definition 1.2

(Definition 9.2 in
[
30
]
)
.

Let us define
IN_{\alpha}(M^{n},g)
for
1\leq\alpha\leq\tfrac{n}{n-1}
as follows:

(2)

IN_{\alpha}(M^{n},g)=\inf\left\{\frac{\Area_{g}(S)}{\min\{\Vol_{g}(\Omega_{1}),\Vol_{g}(\Omega_{2})\}^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}}:M=\Omega_{1}\cup S\cup\Omega_{2},\partial\Omega_{1}=S=\partial\Omega_{2}\right\}.

IN_{\alpha}(M^{n},g)
is called the Neumann
\alpha-
isoperimetric constant of
M
.

With the above in hand, we can make precise what it means for a class of asymptotically flat Riemannian manifolds to have controlled asymptotic falloff and good control on the local analysis of Sobolev functions, by defining the following class of asymptotically flat Riemannian manifolds.

Fix

and
2

Ricci curvature has a unique character in dimension

The first main result of the paper gives quantitative stability of the positive mass theorem.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
b,\bar{m},\Lambda,\kappa>0,\tau>\tfrac{1}{2}
, and

(3)

\displaystyle\|g-g_{\mathbb{E}}\|_{C^{0,\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}<\varepsilon

where

denotes the flat metric on

The Hölder norm in (

Also, an interesting consequence of Theorem

Next, we turn to the second main application of our methods. The Geroch conjecture asserts that a Riemannian
3
-torus with non-negative scalar curvature must be flat. This rigidity theorem was first proven by Schoen-Yau
[

and soon after in higher dimensions by Gromov-Lawson
[

using different methods. The stability problem associated to the Geroch conjecture was first considered by Gromov
[
22
]
and made more precise by Sormani
[
45
]
, who observed the necessity of an extra geometric constraint known as a
minA condition
. A minA condition is a lower bound on the area of all closed minimal surfaces within the manifold, and is used to eliminate pathological bubbling examples first observed by Basilio-Sormani
[
7
]
. See the survey paper by Sormani
[
44
]
for a recent discussion of this conjecture as well as many other conjectures involving scalar curvature.

Various special cases of these conjectures have been studied by Allen
[
2
]
, Allen-Bryden
[
3
]
, Allen-Hernandez-Vazquez-Parise-Payne-Wang
[
1
]
, Cabrera Pacheco-Ketterer-Perales
[
31
]
, Chu-Lee
[
13
]
, and Lee-Naber-Neumeyer
[
29
]
. In this paper, we establish quantitative stability and Hölder convergence in the context of integral bounds on the Ricci curvature. We are able to avoid bubbling by requiring a uniform lower bound on the Neumann isoperimetric constant of metric balls. The present work suggests that a lower bound on the Neumann isoperimetric constant
is an analytically effective – yet still geometric – replacement to a minA condition when considering the stability of
3
-dimensional rigidity theorems related to scalar curvature.

The main ingredient we use to establish quantitative stability of the Geroch conjecture is a formula relating the scalar curvature of a closed
3
-manifold to
\mathcal{S}^{1}
-valued harmonic maps developed by D. Stern.

Theorem 1.7

(Theorem 1.1 of
[
46
]
)
.

Let
(M,g)
be a closed oriented Riemannian
3
-manifold, and suppose
u:M\rightarrow\mathcal{S}^{1}
is a non-constant harmonic map. Then

(4)

\begin{split}4\pi\int_{\mathcal{S}^{1}}\chi\left(\Sigma_{\theta}\right)d\theta&\geq\int_{M}\frac{|\nabla du|^{2}}{|du|}+R_{g}|du|dV_{g}.\end{split}

where
\chi(\Sigma_{\theta})
is the Euler characteristic of a regular level set
\Sigma_{\theta}=u^{-1}(\theta)
and
du
denotes the
1
-form
u^{*}d\theta
.

Remark 1.8
.

For closed manifolds
M
, recall that there is a bijection between
H^{1}(M;\mathbb{Z})
and homotopy classes of maps from
M
to a circle. For each such homotopy class of maps, there is an harmonic representative, unique up to rotations of the circle. Consequently, the first integral cohomology group of a manifold provides the harmonic maps appearing in Theorem
1.7
.

We can now define the class of manifolds which we will consider for our second main result.

Definition 1.9
.

Given parameters
\Lambda,V,\kappa>0
, we denote by
\mathcal{N}(\Lambda,V,\kappa)
the collection of closed connected oriented Riemannian
3
-manifolds
(M,g)
which satisfy the following:

(1)

(M,g)
is
(\Lambda,\frac{3}{2})
-Neumann-isoperimetrically bounded,

(2)

\frac{1}{V}\leq\Vol_{g}(M)\leq V
,

(3)

\|\mathrm{Rc}_{g}\|_{L^{3}}\leq\kappa
,

(4)

the second integral homology
H_{2}(M;\mathbb{Z})
contains no spherical classes,

(5)

there are
3

\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}\in H^{1}(M;\mathbb{Z})
so that the cup product
\alpha_{1}\cup\alpha_{2}\cup\alpha_{3}
generates
H^{3}(M;\mathbb{Z})
.

Remark 1.10
.

Let us briefly remark on the homological conditions
(4)
and
(5)
imposed on the underlying manifolds in the class
\mathcal{M}(\Lambda,V,\kappa)
. First, a class
\alpha\in H_{2}(M;\mathbb{Z})
is said to be

if there is an embedding
\mathcal{S}^{2}\hookrightarrow M
such that
\alpha
is the image of the fundamental class
[\mathcal{S}^{2}]
. The torus

is the prototypical example satisfying
(4)
and

, but manifolds of the form

where

has the topology of a closed hyperbolic manifold or a spherical space-form also satisfy these conditions. On the other hand, manifolds with

.

Next, we apply our methods to investigate the stability of the Geroch conjecture. Below, we adopt the notation
R_{g}^{-}=-\min(0,R_{g})
for the negative part of scalar curvature.

Theorem 1.11
.

Let
\Lambda,V,\kappa>0
and
\gamma\in(0,\tfrac{1}{2})
be given. For any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a
\delta=\delta(\Lambda,V,\kappa,\varepsilon)>0
so that the following holds:
If
(M^{3},g)\in\mathcal{N}(\Lambda,V,\kappa)
and
\|R_{g}^{-}\|_{L^{1}}\leq\delta
, then
M
is diffeomorphic to the torus
\mathbb{T}^{3}
and

(5)

\|g-g_{F}\|_{C^{0,\gamma}}<\varepsilon

for some flat metric
g_{F}
.

The following sequential stability result is a straight-forward consequence of Theorem
1.11
.

Corollary 1.12
.

Let
\Lambda,V,\kappa>0
be given and suppose
\{(M^{3}_{i},g_{i})\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
is a sequence in
\mathcal{N}(\Lambda,V,\kappa)
. If
\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\|R_{g_{i}}^{-}\|_{L^{1}}=0
, then
(M_{i},g_{i})
subsequentially converge to a flat torus in the

topology for some
\gamma\in(0,1)
.

To conclude this section, we give a brief outline of the paper. We begin in Section
2
by introducing the fundamental definitions and theorems which describe volume controls for manifolds with integral bounds on Ricci curvature. In Section
3
, we define isoperimetric and Sobolev constants for manifolds and prove various relationships between them. At the end of this section we show that uniform control on the volume growth of balls and a
(1,p)
Poincaré inequality are enough to guarantee a uniformly controlled constant in Morrey’s inequality.

Section
4
is devoted to the analysis of asymptotically linear harmonic functions on asymptotically flat manifolds. We review the asymptotic features of such functions established in
[
27
]
and develop related decay results which are needed in Sections
5
and
6
. Next, Section
5
develops
L^{2}
control on higher order derivatives of harmonic functions. These estimates are derived by integrating by parts and commuting covariant derivatives. This is an essential step where the integral Ricci bounds and asymptotic estimates show up as necessary assumptions to obtain uniform
W^{3,2}
regularity of harmonic functions. Finally, in Section
6
, we combine the mass formula, Sobolev inequalities, the asymptotic estimates, and the
W^{3,2}
regularity of harmonic functions in order to prove Theorem
1.5
.

This accomplished, we turn to the proof of Theorem
1.11
in section
7
. We follow a fairly similar argument as in the proof of Theorem
1.5
. The main difference is that we cannot leverage the asymptotic control which comes from the asymptotic region of asymptotically flat manifolds. Instead, we are able to establish control on the
L^{2}
norm of harmonic
1-
forms on tori and related manifolds which is used to produce the desired Hölder stability.

2.
Background

In this section we review some results and notions central to our strategy. We begin by recalling the following isoperimetric constant.

Definition 2.1

(Definition 9.1 in
[
30
]
)
.

Given an
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
and a number
1\leq\alpha\leq\tfrac{n}{n-1}
, the
Dirichlet
\alpha-
isoperimetric constant of
(M,g)
is denoted by
ID_{\alpha}(M,g)
and defined as

(6)

ID_{\alpha}(M^{n},g)=\inf\left\{\frac{|\partial\Omega|}{|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}}:\Omega\subset M,\text{ }\partial\Omega\cap\partial M=\emptyset\right\}.

Happily, a lower bound on
ID_{\alpha}(M,g)
for
\alpha>1
gives a lower bound on the volume growth of metric balls.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
(M,g)
be an n-dimensional Riemannian manifold with
ID_{\alpha}(M,g)>0
for some
\alpha\in(1,\tfrac{n}{n-1}]
. Then, we have

(7)

\displaystyle\frac{|\partial B_{r}(x)|}{|B_{r}(x)|^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}}\geq ID_{\alpha}(M,g),

and, for any
r\leq\Diam(M,g)
,

(8)

\displaystyle|B_{r}(x)|\geq ID_{\alpha}(M,g)^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}}r^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}}.

In particular, if
\alpha=\tfrac{n}{n-1}
and
r\leq\Diam(M,g)
, then

(9)

\displaystyle|B_{r}(x)|\geq ID_{\frac{n}{n-1}}(M,g)^{n}r^{n}.

Proof.

The following proof is taken from
[
23
]
chapter 3, page 25.
By taking the derivative of the volume of geodesic balls, we find for almost every
r>0

(10)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dr}|B_{r}(x)|=|\partial B_{r}(x)|\geq ID_{\alpha}|B_{r}(x)|^{\frac{1}{\alpha}},

and hence by integrating the ODE inequality we find

(11)

\displaystyle|B_{r}(x)|\geq ID_{\alpha}^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}}r^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}}.

∎

Whereas lower bounds on
ID_{\alpha}(M)
give lower bounds on the volume growth of metric balls, lower bounds on Ricci curvature give upper bounds on the volume growth of metric balls. This is the content of the following two important results due to Petersen-Wei.

Lemma 2.3

(Lemma 2.3 in
[
34
]
)
.

Given an
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
, let
h(x)
denote the smallest eigenvalue of the Ricci endomorphism at
x\in M
, and
k(\lambda,p)
be given by

(12)

k(\lambda,p)=\int_{M}\textrm{max}\left\{0,-h(x)+(n-1)\lambda\right\}^{p}dV_{g}.

If
\lambda\leq 0
,
r<R
, and
p>\tfrac{n}{2}
, then there is a constant
C(n,p,\lambda,R)
, such that

(13)

\left(\frac{|B_{r}(x)|}{v(n,\lambda,R)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2p}}-\left(\frac{|B_{r}(x)|}{v(n,\lambda,r)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2p}}\leq C\left(n,p,\lambda,R\right)\left(k(\lambda,p)\right)^{\frac{1}{2p}},

where
v(n,\lambda,t)
is the volume of a ball of radius
t
in the simply connected
n
-dimensional space-form with curvature
\lambda
.

As noted in
[
34
]
, the following is an immediate consequence.

Corollary 2.4
.

With the same notation and assumptions of Lemma
2.3
, for all
0\leq r\leq R
, we have

(14)

|B_{r}(x)|\leq\left(1+C(n,p,\lambda,R)k(\lambda,p)^{\frac{1}{2p}}\right)^{2p}v\left(n,\lambda,r\right).

3.
Sobolev, Poincaré, and Morrey inequalities

In this section we remind the reader of the definitions of various Sobolev and isoperimetric constants, review well known relationships between them, and prove some new relationships which are needed in later sections.

Notation 3.1
.

Below and throughout, if
f
is a function defined on a domain
B
in a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
, we write
f_{B}
for the average value
\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.98003pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.26338pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.6363pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.45924pt}}\!\int_{B}fdV_{g}:=\tfrac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}fdV_{g}
.
Secondly, without further mention, we will use
C(a_{1},\dots,a_{N})
to denote a constant which depends only on parameters
a_{1},\dots,a_{N}
. If we require the use of multiple such constants in the course of a given proof, we will use subscripts
C_{k}(a_{1},\dots,a_{N})
,

, to differentiate them.

Since the Neumann and Dirichlet isoperimetric constants are crucial to our forthcoming analysis, we begin with a result relating them to each other. This lemma follows from a remark in
[
16
]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
(M,g)
be an
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold. If domains
\Omega_{1}\subset\Omega_{2}\subset M
satisfy
\partial\Omega_{1}\cap\partial\Omega_{2}=\emptyset
and
|\Omega_{1}|\leq\frac{1}{2}|\Omega_{2}|
, then

(15)

\displaystyle IN_{\alpha}(\Omega_{2},g)\leq ID_{\alpha}(\Omega_{1},g).

Furthermore, if
\Omega_{1}^{\prime}\subset\Omega_{2}^{\prime}
so that
\partial\Omega_{1}^{\prime}\cap\partial\Omega_{2}^{\prime}=\emptyset
we find

(16)

\displaystyle ID_{\alpha}(\Omega_{2}^{\prime},g)\leq ID_{\alpha}(\Omega_{1}^{\prime},g).

Proof.

Consider
U\subset\Omega_{1}
so that
\partial U\cap\partial\Omega_{1}=\emptyset
. Then we see that
U
is a valid competitor for
ID_{\alpha}(\Omega_{1},g)
where

(17)

\displaystyle|U|\leq|\Omega_{1}|\leq\frac{1}{2}|\Omega_{2}|,

and

(18)

\displaystyle|\Omega_{2}\setminus U|=|\Omega_{2}|-|U|\geq\frac{1}{2}|\Omega_{2}|.

Now we notice that
U
is a valid competitor for
IN_{\alpha}(\Omega_{2},g)
and hence

(19)

\displaystyle IN_{\alpha}(\Omega_{2},g)\leq\frac{|\partial U|}{\min\{|U|,|\Omega_{2}\setminus U|\}^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}}\leq\frac{|\partial U|}{|U|^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}}.

Since this is true for any
U
so that
U\subset\Omega_{1}
and
\partial U\cap\partial\Omega_{1}=\emptyset
we can take the infimum over the right hand side of (
19
) to find the first result.

The second result follows from the fact that any competitor for
ID_{\alpha}(\Omega_{1}^{\prime},g)
is a competitor for
ID_{\alpha}(\Omega_{2}^{\prime},g)
.
∎

3.1.
Dirichlet and Neumann Sobolev Constants

We start by defining the Dirichlet and Neumann Sobolev constants.

Definition 3.3

(Definition 9.3 in
[
30
]
)
.

Let us define
SD_{\alpha}(M^{n},g)
for
1\leq\alpha\leq\frac{n}{n-1}
as follows:

(20)

SD_{\alpha}(M^{n},g)=\inf\left\{\frac{\|\nabla f\|_{L^{1}}}{\|f\|_{L^{\alpha}}}:f\in W_{0}^{1,1}(M^{n},g)\right\}.

SD_{\alpha}(M^{n},g)
is called the Dirichlet
\alpha-
Sobolev constant of
M
.

Definition 3.4

(Definition 9.4 in
[
30
]
)
.

Given an
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
, let us define
SN_{\alpha}(M^{n},g)
for
1\leq\alpha\leq\frac{n}{n-1}
as follows:

(21)

SN_{\alpha}(M,g)=\inf\left\{\frac{\|\nabla f\|_{L^{1}}}{\inf_{k\in\mathbb{R}}\|f-k\|_{L^{\alpha}}}:f\in W^{1,1}(M,g)\right\}

SN_{\alpha}(M,g)
is called the Neumann
\alpha
-Sobolev constant of
(M,g)
.

Let us now recall and observe some relationships between the above quantities. It is shown in
[
30
, Theorem 9.5]
that

(22)

\displaystyle SD_{\alpha}(M,g)=ID_{\alpha}(M,g),

and in
[
30
, Theorem 9.6]
we see that

(23)

\displaystyle\min\{1,2^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}\}IN_{\alpha}(M,g)\leq SN_{\alpha}(M,g)\leq\max\{1,2^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}\}IN_{\alpha}(M,g).

The quantity
SN_{\alpha}
is a bit mysterious in its present form, mainly because of the term involving
k
. However, the following result, mentioned in
[
30
]
, helps us understand it a little better. We will reproduce the statement and proof for the convenience of the reader.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
(M,g)
be an
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold. Suppose
|M|<\infty
and
f\in L^{q}
,
q\geq 1
. Then, there exists a unique
k_{q}(f)
such that

(24)

\inf_{k\in\mathbb{R}}\|f-k\|_{L^{q}}=\|f-k_{q}(f)\|_{L^{q}}.

Further,
k_{q}(f)
is given by the equation

(25)

\int_{M}\mathrm{sgn}\left(f-k_{q}(f)\right)|f-k_{q}(f)|^{q-1}dV_{g}=0.

In particular, when
q=2
we have that

(26)

k_{2}(f)=\frac{1}{|M|}\int_{M}fdV_{g}=\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.98003pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.26338pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.6363pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.45924pt}}\!\int_{M}fdV_{g}.

Proof.

Observe that for
q>1
the function
F(k)=\int_{M}|f-k|^{q}dV_{g}
is strictly convex and differentiable. Furthermore, observe that
\displaystyle\lim_{|k|\rightarrow\infty}\int|f-k|^{q}=\infty
. Therefore, the function has a unique minimal point, which is also its unique critical point. We calculate that

(27)

F^{\prime}(k)=\frac{d}{dk}\int_{M}|f-k|^{q}dV_{g}=q\int_{M}\text{sign}(f-k)|f-k|^{q-1}dV_{g}.

In the case that
q=1
, one can observe that for
k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
,
t\in[0,1]
that

(28)

\displaystyle F(k_{1}t+k_{2}(1-t))\leq tF(k_{1})+(1-t)F(k_{2}),

and hence
F
is convex, differentiable,
\displaystyle\lim_{|k|\rightarrow\infty}F(k)=\infty
, and with only one critical point

(29)

F^{\prime}(k)=\frac{d}{dk}\int_{M}|f-k|dV_{g}=\int_{M}\text{sign}(f-k)dV_{g}=|\{f\geq k\}|-|\{f\leq k\}|.

∎

3.2.
Relationship to Classical Poincaré and Sobolev Inequalities

Our goal in this subsection is to show how the Neumann and Dirichlet Sobolev constants are related to the classical Poincaré and Sobolev inequalities. We begin by recalling a result from
[
30
]
.

Proposition 3.6

(Found in
[
30
]
)
.

Let
(M,g)
be an
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold with non-empty boundary, and suppose that
SD_{\alpha}(M,g)>0
. Then, for any
1\leq p<\infty
we have for
f\in W^{1,p}_{0}(M)
that

(30)

\|f\|_{\frac{\alpha p}{p-(p-1)\alpha}}\leq SD^{-1}_{\alpha}(M,g)\frac{p}{p-\alpha(p-1)}\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}}.

Proof.

For the moment, suppose we are given
f\in W_{0}^{1,p}\bigcap L^{q}
, for
q=\frac{p}{p-\alpha(p-1)}
. Then, we have that
g=f^{q}

(31)

\begin{split}\|f\|^{\frac{p}{p-\alpha(p-1)}}_{\frac{\alpha p}{p-\alpha(p-1)}}&=\left(\int_{M}|g|^{\alpha}dV_{g}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\leq SD^{-1}_{\alpha}\left(M\right)\int_{M}|\nabla g|dV_{g}\\
&=qSD^{-1}_{\alpha}(M)\int_{M}|g|^{q-1}|\nabla f|dV_{g}\\
&\leq qSD^{-1}_{\alpha}(M)\|f\|^{\frac{\alpha p}{p-\alpha(p-1)}\frac{p-1}{p}}_{\frac{\alpha p}{p-\alpha(p-1)}}\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}}.\end{split}

Rearranging the above gives

(32)

\|f\|_{\frac{\alpha p}{p-\alpha(p-1)}}\leq qSD^{-1}_{\alpha}(M)\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}}.

In order to remove the assumption that
f
is in
L^{q}
, we note that
W_{0}^{1,p}\bigcap L^{q}
is dense in
W_{0}^{1,p}
.
∎

We now use this result to prove a Sobolev inequality.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
M
be asymptotically flat and suppose that
SD_{\alpha}(B_{r}(x),g)\geq\Lambda>0
,
\forall x\in M,r>0
. Then, for any
1\leq p<\infty
we have for
f\in W^{1,p}(M)
that

(33)

\|f\|_{\frac{\alpha p}{p-(p-1)\alpha}}\leq\Lambda^{-1}\frac{p}{p-\alpha(p-1)}\|f\|_{W^{1,p}}

Proof.

Consider
f\in W^{1,p}(M)
and let
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(M)
so that
0\leq\phi\leq 1
and
|\nabla\phi|\leq 1
. Then we have that
\phi f\in W_{0}^{1,p}(B_{r}(x))
for some
x\in M,r>0
and so by Proposition
3.6
we find

(34)

\displaystyle\|\phi f\|_{\frac{\alpha p}{p-(p-1)\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq SD^{-1}_{\alpha}(B_{r}(x))\frac{p}{p-\alpha(p-1)}\|\nabla(\phi f)\|_{L^{p}(B_{r}(x)}

(35)

\displaystyle=SD^{-1}_{\alpha}(B_{r}(x))\frac{p}{p-\alpha(p-1)}\left(\|\phi\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(B_{r}(x)}+\|f\nabla\phi\|_{L^{p}(B_{r}(x)}\right)

(36)

\displaystyle\leq\Lambda\frac{p}{p-\alpha(p-1)}\left(\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(B_{r}(x))}+\|f\|_{L^{p}(B_{r}(x))}\right)

(37)

\displaystyle\leq\Lambda\frac{p}{p-\alpha(p-1)}\left(\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(M)}+\|f\|_{L^{p}(M)}\right).

Now by choosing a sequence of functions
\phi_{n}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(M)
that converge locally uniformly to
1
so that
0\leq\phi_{n}\leq 1
,
|\nabla\phi_{n}|\leq 1
, and supp
(\phi_{n})\subset B_{n}(x)
for some
x\in M
we can apply the previous inequality to obtain the desired result

(38)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\frac{\alpha p}{p-(p-1)\alpha}}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|\phi_{n}f\|_{\frac{\alpha p}{p-(p-1)\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq\Lambda\frac{p}{p-\alpha(p-1)}\left(\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(M)}+\|f\|_{L^{p}(M)}\right).

∎

We have the following proposition, which can be found, in essence, in
[
30
, Corollary 9.9]
.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
(M,g)
be an
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold,
1<p<n
,
1<\alpha\leq\frac{n}{n-1}
,
f\in W^{1,p}(M)
, and let
k(f)
be the constant such that

(39)

\int_{M}\mathrm{sign}\left(f-k(f)\right)\left|f-k(f)\right|^{\frac{(\alpha-1)p}{p-\alpha(p-1)}}dV_{g}=0.

Then, we have

(40)

\begin{split}&\|f-k(f)\|_{L^{\frac{\alpha p}{p-(p-1)\alpha}}}\leq\frac{p}{p-(p-1)\alpha}SN^{-1}_{\alpha}(M)\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}}\\
&\|f\|_{L^{\frac{\alpha p}{p-\alpha(p-1)}}}\leq C\left(p,\alpha,SN_{\alpha}(M),|M|\right)\|f\|_{W^{1,p}}\end{split}

Proof.

Let
f\in W^{1,p}
and assume for the moment that
f\in L^{\alpha q}
, for some
q
to be determined. Let
k(f)
be the unique number such that

(41)

\int_{M}\mathrm{sign}\left(f-k(f)\right)\left|f-k(f)\right|^{q(\alpha-1)}dV_{g}=0.

Let us define a function
\psi
as follows

(42)

\psi=\mathrm{sign}\left(f-k(f)\right)\left|f-k(f)\right|^{q}.

Then, by our choice of
k(f)
, we see that

(43)

\int_{M}\mathrm{sign}\left(\psi\right)|\psi|^{\alpha-1}dV_{g}=0,

and so

(44)

\inf_{k\in\mathbb{R}}\int_{M}|\psi-k|^{\alpha}dV_{g}=\int_{M}|\psi|^{\alpha}dV_{g}.

Let us now apply the Sobolev-Neumann inequality to
\psi
to obtain

(45)

\left(\int_{M}|\psi|^{\alpha}dV_{g}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\leq SN^{-1}_{\alpha}\int_{M}\left|\nabla\psi\right|dV_{g}.

Plugging in our formula for
\psi
,

(46)

\left(\int_{M}|f-k(f)|^{q\alpha}dV_{g}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\leq qSN^{-1}_{\alpha}\int_{M}|f-k(f)|^{q-1}\left|\nabla f\right|dV_{g}.

Applying Hölder’s inequality to the right hand side leads to

(47)

\left(\int_{M}|f-k(f)|^{q\alpha}dV_{g}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\leq qSN^{-1}_{\alpha}\left(\int_{M}|f-k(f)|^{\frac{(q-1)p}{p-1}}dV_{g}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}}.

In order to match exponents on the left and the right of the above inequality, we choose
q
to be the solution to

(48)

q\alpha=\frac{(q-1)p}{p-1},

which is
q=\frac{p}{p-\alpha(p-1)}
. Note that because
p<n
we have
\tfrac{n}{n-1}<\frac{p}{p-1}
, so
q
is well defined. Plugging this value in for
q
shows that if
f
is in
L^{\alpha q}
, then we have

(49)

\|f-k(f)\|_{L^{\frac{\alpha p}{p-\alpha(p-1)}}}\leq SN^{-1}_{\alpha}\frac{p}{p-\alpha(p-1)}\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}}.

Still assuming that
f
is in
L^{\frac{\alpha p}{p-\alpha(p-1)}}
, we get that

(50)

SN^{-1}_{\alpha}\frac{p}{p-\alpha(p-1)}\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}}+|M|^{\frac{p-\alpha(p-1)}{p\alpha}}|k(f)|\geq\|f\|_{L^{\frac{\alpha p}{p-\alpha(p-1)}}}.

In fact, since
\alpha>1
, we can estimate
|k(f)|
in terms of
\|f\|_{W^{1,p}}
. This is done as follows: We begin with the estimate

(51)

|M||k(f)|=\int_{M}|k(f)|dV_{g}\leq\int_{M}|k(f)-f|+|f|dV_{g}.

We may now use Hölder’s inequality twice to obtain

(52)

|M||k(f)|\leq|M|^{\frac{2\alpha p-p-\alpha}{\alpha p}}\|f-k(f)\|_{\frac{\alpha p}{p-(p-1)\alpha}}+|M|^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\|f\|_{L^{p}}.

At this point, we can apply (
49
) to obtain

(53)

|M||k(f)|\leq|M|^{\frac{2\alpha p-p-\alpha}{\alpha p}}\frac{p}{p-(p-1)\alpha}SN^{-1}_{\alpha}(M)\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}}+|M|^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\|f\|_{p}.

We can now put everything together to get

(54)

\|f\|_{\frac{\alpha p}{p-(p-1)\alpha}}\leq C(p,\alpha,SN_{\alpha},|M|)\|f\|_{W^{1,p}}

so long as
f
is in
L^{\alpha q}
. Since it is well known that
W^{1,p}\bigcap L^{\alpha q}
is dense in
W^{1,p}
, this completes the proof.

∎

We now establish that a bound on the Neumann isoperimetric constant of balls gives a Poincaré inequality.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
(M,g)
be a
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold. Suppose there exists a
\Lambda>0
such that, for any ball
B_{r}(x)
, we have
SN_{\tfrac{n}{n-1}}(B_{r}(x),g)\geq\Lambda
. Then, for any
f
in
W^{1,p}(M)
we have

(55)

\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.98003pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.26338pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.6363pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.45924pt}}\!\int_{B_{r}(x)}|f-f_{B_{r}(x)}|^{n}dV_{g}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}\leq(n-1)\Lambda^{-1}|B_{r}(x)|^{\frac{1}{n}}\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.98003pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.26338pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.6363pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.45924pt}}\!\int_{B_{r}(x)}|\nabla f|^{\frac{n}{2}}dV_{g}\right)^{\frac{2}{n}},

from which it follows that

(56)

\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.98003pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.26338pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.6363pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.45924pt}}\!\int_{B_{r}(x)}|f-f_{B_{r}(x)}|dV_{g}\leq(n-1)\Lambda^{-1}|B_{r}(x)|^{\frac{1}{n}}\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.98003pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.26338pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.6363pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.45924pt}}\!\int_{B_{r}(x)}|\nabla f|^{p}dV_{g}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

for any
p\geq\frac{n}{2}
.

Proof.

The starting point is to make sure that the constant
k(f)
in Equation (
39
) is

(57)

f_{B_{r}(x)}=\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B_{r}(x)}fdV_{g}.

Inspecting the exponent in Equation (
39
), while keeping in mind that
\alpha=\tfrac{n}{n-1}
, shows that we need to pick
p
such that

(58)

1=\frac{(\alpha-1)p}{p-\alpha(p-1)}=\frac{p}{n-p}.

That is, we have
p=\tfrac{n}{2}
. So, using this exponent in Proposition
3.8
, we get

(59)

\left(\int_{B_{r}(x)}|f-f_{B_{r}(x)}|^{n}dV_{g}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}\leq(n-1)\Lambda^{-1}\left(\int_{B_{r}(x)}|\nabla f|^{\frac{n}{2}}dV_{g}\right)^{\frac{2}{n}}.

Now, we can rewrite this in terms of averages as follows:

(60)

\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B_{r}(x)}|f-f_{B_{r}(x)}|^{n}dV_{g}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}|B_{r}(x)|^{\frac{1}{n}}\leq(n-1)\Lambda^{-1}|B_{r}(x)|^{\frac{2}{n}}\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B_{r}(x)}|\nabla f|^{\frac{n}{2}}dV_{g}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}.

Dividing both sides by
|B_{r}(x)|^{\frac{1}{n}}
gives the first result.

To get the second result, we can begin by applying Hölder’s inequality to get

(61)

\begin{split}\int_{B_{r}(x)}|f-f_{B_{r}(x)}|dV_{g}&\leq|B_{r}(x)|^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\left(\int_{B_{r}(x)}|f-f_{B_{r}(x)}|^{n}dV_{g}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}\\
&\leq|B_{r}(x)|^{\frac{n-1}{n}}(n-1)\Lambda^{-1}\left(\int_{B_{r}(x)}|\nabla f|^{\frac{n}{2}}dV_{g}\right)^{\frac{2}{n}}\\
&\leq|B_{r}(x)|^{\frac{n-1}{n}}(n-1)\Lambda^{-1}|B_{r}(x)|^{\frac{2(\sigma-1)}{n\sigma}}\left(\int_{B_{r}(x)}|\nabla f|^{\frac{n\sigma}{2}}dV_{g}\right)^{\frac{2}{n\sigma}}\\
&=(n-1)\Lambda^{-1}|B_{r}(x)|^{1+\frac{1}{n}}\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B_{r}(x)}|\nabla f|^{\frac{n\sigma}{2}}dV_{g}\right)^{\frac{2}{n\sigma}}\end{split}

So, if we divide out by
|B_{r}(x)|
and let
p=\frac{n\sigma}{2}
, we get the second result.
∎

3.3.
Morrey’s Inequality

In this subsection we would like to understand what conditions are needed to conclude a uniform Morrey inequality from a uniform Sobolev inequality. We start by defining a local Hölder norm for functions which will be advantageous when implying a Morrey inequality from a Poincaré inequality.

Definition 3.10
.

Let
(M,g)
be a Riemannian manifold. The Hölder norm of scale
R>0
and power
0<\gamma<1
is defined as follows:

(62)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{C_{R}^{0,\gamma}(M,g)}=\sup_{x\in M}|u(x)|+\sup_{x,y\in M,d_{g}(x,y)<R}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{d_{g}(x,y)^{\gamma}}.

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
(M,g)
be a Riemannian manifold. For any radius
R>0
and function
u
, we have

(63)

\|u\|_{C_{R}^{0,\gamma}(M,g)}\leq\|u\|_{C^{0,\gamma}(M,g)}\leq\left(1+\frac{2}{R^{\gamma}}\right)\|u\|_{C_{R}^{0,\gamma}(M,g)}.

Proof.

The first inequality
follows quickly from the definition of
\|u\|_{C_{R}^{0,\gamma}(M,g)}
. For the second inequality, for any
x,y
in
M
we have one of two cases. Either
d_{g}(x,y)<R
or
d_{g}(x,y)\geq R
. In the first case, we have

(64)

\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{d_{g}(x,y)^{\gamma}}\leq\sup_{x,y\in M,d_{g}(x,y)<R}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{d_{g}(x,y)^{\gamma}},

and in the second case, we have

(65)

\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{d_{g}(x,y)^{\gamma}}\leq\frac{|u(x)|+|u(y)|}{R^{\gamma}}\leq\frac{2}{R^{\gamma}}\sup_{x\in M}|u(x)|.

∎

Now we show that a uniform, local Poincaré inequality combined with volume growth bounds for small balls implies a uniform Morrey inequality exists. This is the analytical backbone of both Theorem
1.5
and Theorem
1.11
.

Theorem 3.12
.

Let
(M,g)
be an
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold. Fix
p>1
and
R>0
. Assume there are constants
C_{P}
and
C_{V}
so that the following hold for all
r\in(0,2R)
and
x\in M
:

(66)

\displaystyle\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.98003pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.26338pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.6363pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.45924pt}}\!\int_{B_{r}(x)}|u-u_{B_{r}(x)}|dV_{g}\leq C_{P}\Diam(B_{r}(x))^{\mu}\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.98003pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.26338pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.6363pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.45924pt}}\!\int_{B_{r}(x)}|\nabla u|^{p}dV_{g}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

for any
u\in W^{1,p}(M,g)
and

(67)

\displaystyle C_{V}^{-1}r^{n}\leq|B_{r}(x)|\leq C_{V}r^{n}.

Then, for all
u\in C^{1}(M)
, points
x,y\in M
such that
d_{g}(x,y)\leq R
, and
\mu>\tfrac{n}{p}
, we have

(68)

\displaystyle\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{d_{g}(x,y)^{\frac{p\mu-n}{p}}}\leq C(p,\mu,C_{V},C_{P})\left(\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{p}dV_{g}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

and

(69)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{C_{R}^{0,\gamma}(M,g)}\leq C(p,\mu,C_{V},C_{P})\left(\int_{M}|u|dV_{g}+\left(\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{p}dV_{g}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\right),

where
\gamma=\frac{p\mu-n}{p}
.

Proof.

Here we more or less follow the proof given by N. Shanmugalingam in
[
42
]
where our goal is to adapt the argument to our setting and keep careful track of the constants.
Since
u\in C^{1}(M)
we know that every
x\in M
is a Lebesgue point of
u
and hence

(70)

\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow 0^{+}}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B_{r}(x)}|u(x)-u(y)|dV_{g}(y)=0.

Since

(71)

\displaystyle\left|\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B_{r}(x)}udV_{g}-u(x)\right|=\left|\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B_{r}(x)}u(y)-u(x)dV_{g}(y)\right|\leq\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B_{r}(x)}|u(x)-u(y)|dV_{g}(y),

we see that

(72)

\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow 0^{+}}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B_{r}(x)}udV_{g}=u(x).

Now, for
x,y\in M
such that
d_{g}(x,y)<R
, we define nested families of balls
\left\{B_{i}\right\}_{-\infty}^{\infty}
where

(73)

\displaystyle B_{0}=B_{2d_{g}(x,y)}(x),\quad B_{i}=B_{2^{1-i}d_{g}(x,y)}(x),\quad B_{-i}=B_{2^{1-i}d_{g}(x,y)}(y),\quad i\in\mathbb{N}.

Now if we let
u_{B_{i}}=\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B_{i}}udV_{g}
then we notice that

(74)

\displaystyle|u(x)-u(y)|

\displaystyle=\lim_{r\rightarrow 0^{+}}\left|\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B_{r}(x)}udV_{g}-\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B(y,r)}udV_{g}\right|

(75)

\displaystyle=\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\left|\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B_{2^{1-i}d_{g}(x,y)}(x)}udV_{g}-\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B_{2^{1-i}d_{g}(x,y)}(y)}udV_{g}\right|

(76)

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\left(u_{B_{i+1}}-u_{B_{i}}\right)-\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\left(u_{B_{-i-1}}-u_{B_{-i}}\right)\right|

(77)

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(u_{B_{i+1}}-u_{B_{i}}\right)\right|\leq\sum_{-\infty}^{\infty}|u_{B_{i+1}}-u_{B_{i}}|.

Rewriting the terms on the right hand side of (
77
)

(78)

\displaystyle|u_{B_{i+1}}-u_{B_{i}}|

\displaystyle=\left|\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B_{i+1}}udV_{g}-\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B_{i}}udV_{g}\right|

(79)

\displaystyle=\left|\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B_{i+1}}u-u_{B_{i}}dV_{g}\right|

(80)

\displaystyle\leq\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B_{i+1}}|u-u_{B_{i}}|dV_{g}\leq\frac{|B_{i}|}{|B_{i+1}|}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B_{i}}|u-u_{B_{i}}|dV_{g}.

On the other hand, by using the volume growth assumption, the ratios of volumes of balls satisfy

(81)

\displaystyle\frac{|B_{i}|}{|B_{i+1}|}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(C_{V}2^{1-i}d_{g}(x,y))^{n}}{(C_{V}^{-1}2^{-i}d_{g}(x,y))^{n}}\leq C_{V}^{2n}2^{n}\quad i\geq 0,

(82)

\displaystyle\frac{|B_{i}|}{|B_{i+1}|}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(C_{V}2^{2+i}d_{g}(x,y))^{n}}{(C_{V}^{-n}2^{1+i}d_{g}(x,y))^{n}}\leq C_{V}^{2n}2^{-n}\leq C_{V}^{2n}2^{n}\quad i<0.

Combining the above inequalities yields

(83)

\displaystyle|u_{B_{i+1}}-u_{B_{i}}|

\displaystyle\leq C_{V}^{2n}2^{n}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B_{i}}|u-u_{B_{i}}|dV_{g}.

Next, we apply the Poincaré inequality to find

(84)

\displaystyle|u_{B_{i+1}}-u_{B_{i}}|

\displaystyle\leq(2C_{V})^{2n}C_{P}\Diam(B_{i})^{\mu}\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B_{i}}|\nabla u|^{p}dV_{g}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

(85)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(2C_{V})^{2n}C_{P}\Diam(B_{i})^{\mu}}{|B_{i}|^{\frac{1}{p}}}\left(\int_{B_{i}}|\nabla u|^{p}dV_{g}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

(86)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(2C_{V})^{2n}2^{\mu}C_{P}2^{\mu(1-|i|)}d_{g}(x,y)^{\mu}}{(C_{V}2^{1-|i|}d_{g}(x,y))^{\frac{n}{p}}}\left(\int_{B_{i}}|\nabla u|^{p}dV_{g}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

(87)

\displaystyle\leq(2C_{V})^{2n}2^{\mu}C_{P}C_{V}^{-n/p}2^{\frac{p\mu-n}{p}(1-|i|)}d_{g}(x,y)^{\frac{p\mu-n}{p}}\left(\int_{B_{i}}|\nabla u|^{p}dV_{g}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Putting everything together,

(88)

\displaystyle|u(x)-u(y)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{-\infty}^{\infty}(2C_{V})^{2n}2^{\mu}C_{P}C_{V}^{-n/p}2^{\frac{p\mu-n}{p}(1-|i|)}d_{g}(x,y)^{\frac{p\mu-n}{p}}\left(\int_{B_{i}}|\nabla u|^{p}dV_{g}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

(89)

\displaystyle\leq(2C_{V})^{2n}2^{\mu}C_{P}C_{V}^{-n/p}d_{g}(x,y)^{\frac{p\mu-n}{p}}\left(\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{p}dV_{g}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\sum_{-\infty}^{\infty}2^{\frac{p\mu-n}{p}(1-|i|)}.

Then since
p\mu>n
we find that

(90)

\displaystyle\sum_{-\infty}^{\infty}2^{\frac{p\mu-n}{p}(1-|i|)}

\displaystyle=2^{\frac{p\mu-n}{p}+1}+2\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}2^{\frac{p\mu-n}{p}(1-i)}

(91)

\displaystyle=2^{\frac{p\mu-n}{p}+1}+2\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\left(2^{\frac{n-p\mu}{p}}\right)^{i}=2^{\frac{p\mu-n}{p}+1}+\frac{2}{1-2^{\frac{n-p\mu}{p}}},

and hence

(92)

\displaystyle|u(x)-u(y)|

\displaystyle\leq C(p,\mu,C_{V},C_{P})d_{g}(x,y)^{\frac{p\mu-n}{p}}\left(\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{p}dV_{g}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Now, let
y\in B_{1}(x)
, and integrate both sides of
|u(x)|\leq|u(y)|+|u(x)-u(y)|
to obtain

(93)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}(x)}|u(x)|dV_{g}(y)\leq\int_{B_{1}(x)}|u(y)|dV_{g}(y)+\int_{B_{1}(x)}|u(x)-u(y)|dV_{g}(y),

or, by a slight rearrangement,

(94)

\displaystyle|u(x)|\leq\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B_{1}(x)}|u|dV_{g}+\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B_{1}(x)}|u(x)-u(y)|dV_{g}(y).

Apply (
92
), (
67
), and the fact that
d_{g}(x,y)\leq 1
, to find that

(95)

\displaystyle|u(x)|

\displaystyle\leq C_{V}\int_{B_{1}(x)}|u|dV_{g}

(96)

\displaystyle\quad+C(p,\mu,C_{V},C_{P})\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B_{1}(x)}d_{g}(x,y)^{\frac{p\mu-n}{p}}\left(\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{p}dV_{g}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}dV_{g}(y)

(97)

\displaystyle\leq C_{V}\int_{M}|u|dV_{g}+C(p,\mu,C_{V},C_{P})\left(\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{p}dV_{g}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

which, together with (
92
), implies the desired Hölder bound.
∎

Application

Fix
b,\Lambda,\kappa,\bar{m}>0
and
\tau>\tfrac{1}{2}
. Using the above work, we can now show that members of the family
\mathcal{M}(b,\tau,\bar{m},\Lambda,\kappa)
have well behaved Sobolev spaces. We begin by demonstrating that metric balls are well behaved.

Proposition 3.13
.

Given
R>0
there exists a constants

and

such that for any member

in

we have, for all

and all

, the following volume bounds

(98)

C(\Lambda)r^{3}\leq|B_{r}(x)|\leq C(\kappa,R)r^{3}.

Proof.

We observe that the upper bound on
|B_{r}(x)|
follows directly from the control we have on
Rm
applied to Corollary
2.4
.

Let us now concentrate on the lower bound on volume growth. From our assumption on asymptotic flatness, it follows that for any
x
in
M
we have

(99)

\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}|B_{r}(x)|=\infty,

and any compact subset is eventually contained in
B_{r}(x)
for some

large enough. Thus, we may apply Proposition
3.2
together with our assumption on the Neumann isoperimetric constant of metric balls to conclude that any compact domain in
M
has Dirichlet isoperimetric constant uniformly bounded away from zero, depending on
\Lambda>0
. At this point, we may apply Lemma
2.2
to get the desired result.
∎

We can now state the form of Morrey’s inequality which is valid uniformly for the family
\mathcal{M}(b,\tau,\bar{m},\Lambda,\kappa)
. It is obtained by gathering together all the information we established thus far.

Theorem 3.14
.

For all
(M,g)
in
\mathcal{M}(b,\tau,\bar{m},\Lambda,\kappa)
, points
x_{0}
in
M
, and functions

with

and

, we have

(100)

\|f\|_{C^{0,\frac{p-3}{p}}_{R}\left(B_{s}(x_{0})\right)}\leq C(b,\tau,\Lambda,\kappa,R,p)\left(\|f\|_{L^{1}\left(B_{s+R}(x_{0})\right)}+\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}\left(B_{s+R}(x_{0})\right)}\right).

Proof.

The proof will be complete when we show that every member of the family

satisfies the conditions of Theorem
3.12
, since then we can follow the proof of Theorem
3.12
word for word with

. Let us recall that we need to establish that metric balls around points in

have upper and lower volume bounds, and uniformly satisfy a

It follows from Proposition
3.13
that
B_{r}(x)
satisfies the required volume bounds for

and

in

. In addition, it follows from Lemma

that

has the desired
(1,p)
Poincaré inequality with

∎

4.
Asymptotic Estimates For the Harmonic Functions

In this section, we show how ADM mass and the condition of uniform asymptotic flatness strongly control the behavior of asymptotically linear harmonic functions within the asymptotically flat region. Let us first recall some information about asymptotically linear harmonic functions.

For a complete asymptotically flat
3
-manifold
(M,g)
, let
x^{i}
for
i=1,2,3
be the components of the asymptotic coordinate chart. Then we call
u^{i}
the
i^{\text{th}}
asymptotically linear harmonic function if
u^{i}
satisfies

(101)

\begin{split}&\Delta_{g}u^{i}=0\\
&u^{i}-x^{i}\in C^{2,\gamma}_{1-\tau}.\end{split}

In particular, the gradients of
u^{i}
are asymptotically constant in the sense that

(102)

|\nabla u^{i}-\partial_{x^{i}}|\in C^{1,\gamma}_{-\tau}.

Convention:
Given asymptotically flat
(M,g)
and
r>0
, we denote by
M_{r}
the bounded region of
M
lying inside the coordinate sphere of radius
r
, denoted
\mathcal{S}_{r}
. Secondly, if
\ell
is a linear map from
\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}
with
||d\ell||=1
, we will call any solution to
\Delta u=0
with
|u-\ell(x^{1},x^{2},x^{3})|\in C^{2,\gamma}_{1-\tau}(M)
, for some
\gamma\in(0,1)
, an
asymptotically linear harmonic function
, noting that we always require the linear function to have unit norm. Also, throughout this section, we will fix parameters
b,\Lambda,\kappa,\bar{m}>0
and
\tau>\tfrac{1}{2}
.

It was observed in
[
27
]
that the asymptotics (
102
) are uniform in the following sense.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be an orientable complete
3
-dimensional
(b,\tau,\bar{m})
asymptotically flat manifold. Assume that
H_{2}(M;\mathbb{Z})
contains no spherical classes and that
R_{g}\geq 0
. For a sufficiently large
r_{0}
(depending on
b
and
\tau
), there is a constant
C(r_{0},b,\tau,\bar{m})
so that any asymptotically linear functions
\{u^{i}\}_{i=1}^{3}
satisfy

(103)

||\nabla u^{i}-\partial_{x^{i}}||_{C^{1,\alpha}_{-\tau}(M\setminus M_{r_{0}})}\leq C(r_{0},b,\tau,\bar{m})

and in particular,

(104)

\left|g\left(\nabla u^{i},\nabla u^{j}\right)-\delta^{ij}\right|\leq C(r_{0},b,\tau,\bar{m})|x|^{-\tau}

holds on
M\setminus M_{r_{0}}
. Moreover, there is a particular collection of asymptotically linear functions
\{\hat{u}^{i}\}_{i=1}^{3}
, independent of
r_{0}
, which satisfy

(105)

\sup_{M_{r}}|\hat{u}^{i}|\leq C(r_{0},b,\tau,\bar{m})r

for all
r>r_{0}
.

Proof.

Only the final statement requires proof since the other statements are contained in
[
27
, Lemma 3.2]
. Fix the choice of
\{\hat{u}^{i}\}_{i=1}^{3}
by requiring that the average value of
\hat{u}^{i}
over the annulus
M_{r_{0}}\setminus M_{1}
vanishes. According to
[
27
, Proposition 3.1]
, using a Sobolev inequality and the mass formula, we have
\sup_{M_{r_{0}}}|\hat{u}^{i}|<C_{1}(r_{0},b,\tau,\bar{m})
. For a larger radius
r>r_{0}
, one can estimate
\hat{u}^{i}
at a point
x\in(M_{r}\setminus M_{r_{0}})
by integrating the gradient estimate (
103
) along a radial curve connecting
x
to
M_{r_{0}}
. The fundamental theorem of calculus then shows that
|\hat{u}^{i}(x)-C_{1}(r_{0},b,\tau,\bar{m})|<C_{2}(r_{0},b,\tau,\bar{m})r
and the result follows.
∎

Next, we leverage the uniform weighted control (
103
) to show that the difference between
g
and the flat metric on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
is small in the average sense within the asymptotic region.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
(M,g)
be an oriented complete
3
-dimensional
(b,\tau,\bar{m})
asymptotically flat manifold with mass
m
. Assume that
H_{2}(M;\mathbb{Z})
contains no spherical classes and that
R_{g}\geq 0
. Given a sufficiently large
r_{0}=r_{0}(b,\tau)>0
,
r>r_{0}
, and an
\varepsilon>0
, there is a
\delta=\delta(r_{0},b,\tau,\varepsilon)>0
so that the following holds: If
m(M,g)\leq\delta
, then

(106)

\int_{M_{r}\setminus M_{r_{0}}}\left|\left<\nabla u^{i},\nabla u^{j}\right>-\delta^{ij}\right|dV_{g}<\varepsilon,

for any
i,j\in\{1,2,3\}
where
\{u^{i}\}_{i=1}^{3}
are the harmonic coordinates of
(M,g)
.

Proof.

Let
r_{0}>0
be the radius given in Proposition
4.1
. By (
103
),

(107)

|\nabla^{2}u^{i}|+|\nabla u^{i}|\leq C_{1}(b,\tau)

holds on
M_{r_{0}}
. In light of the
|\nabla u^{i}|
’s boundedness, the mass formula (
1
) implies

(108)

\int_{M\setminus M_{r_{0}}}|\nabla^{2}u^{i}|^{2}dV\leq C_{2}(b,\tau)m.

The next step is to apply a Sobolev inequality on asymptotically flat manifolds due to Schoen-Yau
[
41
, Lemma 3.1]
,

(109)

\displaystyle\int_{M\setminus M_{r_{0}}}(\langle\nabla u^{i},\nabla u^{j}\rangle-\delta^{ij})^{6}dV

\displaystyle\leq C_{3}(b,\tau)\left(\int_{M\setminus M_{r_{0}}}|\nabla\langle\nabla u^{i},\nabla u^{j}\rangle|^{2}\right)^{3}

(110)

\displaystyle\leq C_{4}(b,\tau)\sup_{M\setminus M_{r_{0}}}|\nabla u^{i}|^{2}\left(\int_{M\setminus M_{r_{0}}}|\nabla^{2}u^{j}|^{2}dV\right)^{3}

(111)

\displaystyle+C_{4}(b,\tau)\sup_{M\setminus M_{r_{0}}}|\nabla u^{j}|^{2}\left(\int_{M\setminus M_{r_{0}}}|\nabla^{2}u^{i}|^{2}dV\right)^{3}

(112)

\displaystyle\leq C_{5}(b,\tau)m^{3}.

Also notice that the uniform asymptotic flatness condition implies that
|M_{r}\setminus M_{r_{0}}|\leq C_{6}(b,\tau)r^{3}
. Combining this observation with Hölder’s inequality and (
109
),

(113)

\displaystyle\int_{M_{r}\setminus M_{r_{0}}}|\langle\nabla u^{i},\nabla u^{j}\rangle-\delta^{ij}|dV

\displaystyle\leq|M_{r}\setminus M_{r_{0}}|^{5/6}\left(\int_{M_{r_{0}}\setminus M_{r}}|\langle\nabla u^{i},\nabla u^{j}\rangle-\delta^{ij}|^{6}dV\right)^{1/6}

(114)

\displaystyle\leq C_{7}(b,\tau)r^{5/2}\sqrt{m}

and the result follows.

∎

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
(M,g)
be a
(b,\tau,\bar{m})
asymptotically flat Riemannian manifold. If
u
is a asymptotically linear harmonic function, then there is a
r_{0}(b,\tau)
so that the following holds on
M\setminus M_{r_{0}}

(115)

\displaystyle|\nabla u|+|\nabla^{2}u|+|\nabla^{3}u|\leq C(b,\tau).

Proof.

Throughout this proof we will work in
(M,g)
’s asymptotically flat coordinate chart and all partial derivatives will be in these coordinates. Using Proposition
4.1
, we know that we can choose an
r_{0}
large enough so that the first and second derivatives satisfy
|\partial u|\leq C_{1}(b,\tau)
and
|\partial\partial u|\leq C_{2}(b,\tau)
on
M\setminus M_{r_{0}}
. It suffices, therefore, to estimate the third derivatives of
u
and relate the partial derivatives to covariant derivatives with respect to the connection induced by
g
.
Expanding the Hessian of
u
in asymptotically flat coordinates,

(116)

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}u=g^{lm}\left(\partial_{l}\partial_{m}-\Gamma_{lm}^{k}\partial_{k}\right)u.

According to the uniform asymptotics, the terms in (
116
) can be bounded in
M_{r_{0}}
to find
|\nabla^{2}u|\leq C_{3}(b,\tau)
.

To estimate the third derivatives of
u
, we apply the Böchner formula to find

(117)

\displaystyle\Delta\nabla u=g^{lm}\left(\partial_{l}\partial_{m}-\Gamma_{lm}^{k}\partial_{k}\right)\nabla u=Rc(\nabla u,\cdot)=:\mathbf{f},

which we consider as a PDE for
\nabla u
. Again using the uniform asymptotics,
|\mathbf{f}|,|g^{lm}|,|\Gamma_{lm}^{k}|\leq C_{4}(b,\tau)
on
M\setminus M_{r_{0}}
. Applying elliptic
L^{p}
estimates
[
20
, Theorem 9.11]
, for every
p>1

(118)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{i}u\|_{W^{2,p}(M\setminus M_{r_{0}},\delta)}\leq C_{5}(b,\tau),\quad 1\leq i\leq 3.

Since (
118
) holds for all
p>1
, the third partial derivatives
|\partial\partial\nabla u|\leq C_{6}(b,\tau)

and again since

(119)

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}\nabla_{i}u=g^{lm}\left(\partial_{l}\partial_{m}-\Gamma_{lm}^{k}\partial_{k}\right)\nabla_{i}u,

we find
|\nabla^{3}u|\leq C_{8}(b,\tau)
, as claimed.
∎

We conclude this section by showing that there is a strong relationship between the regions
M_{r}
and metric balls
B(x,r)
for manifolds in the class
\mathcal{M}(b,\tau,\bar{m},\Lambda,\kappa)
. This information is important since the regions
M_{r}
have well behaved boundary, but no inherent geometric significance, and the balls
B(x,r)
are geometrically important, but could have wild boundaries.
Before accomplishing this, we show that a uniform volume bound on the region within a given coordinate sphere.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
(M,g)
belong to
\mathcal{M}\left(b,\tau,\bar{m},\Lambda,\kappa\right)
. Then, there exists a constant
K=K(r_{0},b,\tau,\Lambda)
such that, for any
r>r_{0}
, we have
|M_{r}|\leq Kr^{3}.

Proof.

By the definition of
M_{r}
and the
(b,\tau)
asymptotic flatness of
M
on
M_{r_{0}}
, we know that

(120)

|\mathcal{S}_{r}|\leq C_{1}(r_{0},b,\tau)r^{2}.

Further,
\mathcal{S}_{r}
is a valid competitor for the isoperimetric ratio, and so it follows from the fact that
M
is
(\Lambda,\frac{3}{2})
Neumann-isoperimetrically bounded and Lemma
3.2
that

(121)

\displaystyle|M_{r}|\leq C_{2}(r_{0},b,\tau,\Lambda)r^{3}.

∎

The following lemma allows us to simultaneously take advantage of the good boundary behavior of the regions
M_{r}
and the good geometric behavior of the metric balls
B(x,r)
.

(a)

M_{r}
is contained in
B_{d}(x)

(b)

B_{r}(x)
is contained
M_{\hat{r}}

Lemma 4.5
.

Given
b,\tau,\Lambda,r
, there is

and
V=V(b,\tau,\Lambda,r)
so that the following holds: If
(M,g)\in\mathcal{M}(b,\tau,\bar{m},\Lambda,\kappa)
, then

\mathrm{diam}(M_{r})\leq d
and
|M_{r}|\leq V
.

Moreover, there is an
\hat{r}=\hat{r}(b,\tau,r)
so that the following holds: If
(M,g)\in\mathcal{M}(b,\tau,\bar{m},\Lambda,\kappa)
and

.

Proof.

From Lemma
4.4
it follows that
|M_{r}|
is bounded, say by

. Let
x
and
y
be any two points in
M_{r}
. By Lemma

, we know that

and

have volume bounded below by

. Let

(122)

\begin{split}B_{S}(x)\bigcap\partial M_{r}\neq\emptyset,\qquad B_{S}(y)\bigcap\partial M_{r}\neq\emptyset.\end{split}

Since

is in the asymptotically flat region, it follows that

is uniformly bounded above, by

. Putting the above together, we see that we have

(123)

\Diam(M_{r})\leq 2S+C=:d,

proving the first statement of Lemma

For the second statement, let

and consider the metric ball

so that any path from

∎

5.
Integral Estimates For Harmonic Functions

In this section we leave the asymptotically flat setting and work with general and fixed oriented complete
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
, and study harmonic functions
u:M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, which is to say,
\Delta u=0
. We would like to develop integral estimates for
u
. Let
\Omega\subset M
be bounded region with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
and outward pointing unit normal vector
\nu
. Our goal in this section is to obtain Sobolev type estimates for harmonic functions, which will ultimately lead to Hölder control.
We begin with an elementary observation which leads to
L^{2}
control on the gradient of harmonic functions.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be an oriented complete
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold,
u
a harmonic function, and
\Omega\subset M
a bounded domain with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
and outward pointing unit normal vector
\nu
. Then

(124)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dV_{g}

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Omega}u\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}dA_{g}.

Proof.

We can integrate by parts
-u\Delta u=0
to find

(125)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dV_{g}

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Omega}u\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}dA_{g}.

∎

Now we obtain an
L^{2}
estimate on the hessian of harmonic functions.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
(M,g)
be an oriented complete
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold,
u
a harmonic function, and
\Omega\subset M
a bounded domain with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
and outward pointing unit normal vector
\nu
. Then

(126)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}dV_{g}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\partial\Omega}|\nabla^{2}u||\nabla u|dA_{g}+\left(\int_{\Omega}|Rc|^{p}dV_{g}\right)^{1/p}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}dV_{g}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}.

Proof.

Now we calculate

(127)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}(\Delta u)^{2}dV_{g}

(128)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}g^{ij}g^{pq}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}u\nabla_{p}\nabla_{q}udV_{g}

(129)

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}g^{ij}g^{pq}\nabla_{j}u\nabla_{i}\nabla_{p}\nabla_{q}udV_{g}+\int_{\partial\Omega}g^{ij}g^{pq}\nabla_{j}u\nabla_{p}\nabla_{q}u\nu_{i}dA_{g}

(130)

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}g^{ij}g^{pq}\nabla_{j}u\nabla_{p}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{q}u-g^{ij}g^{pq}\nabla_{j}uR_{ipqk}\nabla_{k}udV_{g}+\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\Delta udA_{g}

(131)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}g^{ij}g^{pq}\nabla_{p}\nabla_{j}u\nabla_{i}\nabla_{q}u+Rc(\nabla u,\nabla u)dV_{g}-\int_{\partial\Omega}g^{ij}g^{pq}\nabla_{j}u\nabla_{i}\nabla_{q}u\nu_{p}dA_{g}

(132)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}g^{ij}g^{pq}\nabla_{p}\nabla_{j}u\nabla_{q}\nabla_{i}u+Rc(\nabla u,\nabla u)dV_{g}-\int_{\partial\Omega}\nabla\nabla u(\nabla u,\nu)dA_{g}

(133)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}+Rc(\nabla u,\nabla u)dV_{g}-\int_{\partial\Omega}\nabla\nabla u(\nabla u,\nu)dA_{g},

and so by rearranging we find

(134)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}dV_{g}=\int_{\partial\Omega}\nabla\nabla u(\nabla u,\nu)dA_{g}-\int_{\Omega}Rc(\nabla u,\nabla u)dV_{g}.

If we take the absolute value of both sides, use Cauchy-Schwarz, and then Hölder’s inequality we find

(135)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}dV_{g}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\partial\Omega}|\nabla^{2}u||\nabla u|dA_{g}+\int_{\Omega}|Rc||\nabla u|^{2}dV_{g}

(136)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\partial\Omega}|\nabla^{2}u||\nabla u|dA_{g}+\left(\int_{\Omega}|Rc|^{p}dV_{g}\right)^{1/p}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}dV_{g}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}.

∎

Now we would like to estimate the integral of third derivatives of harmonic functions in a similar way. Below and throughout, given two tensors
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
, we will use
T_{1}*T_{2}
as a stand in for any linear function of
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
(M,g)
be an oriented complete
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold,
u
a harmonic function, and
\Omega\subset M
a bounded domain with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
and outward pointing unit normal vector
\nu
. Then

(137)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla^{3}u|^{2}dV_{g}&=\int_{\Omega}2Rc(\nabla u,\Delta\nabla u)-Rc^{2}(\nabla u,\nabla u)-Rc*\nabla\nabla u*\nabla\nabla udV_{g}\\
&+\int_{\Omega}Rm*\nabla\nabla\nabla u*\nabla u-Rm*\nabla\nabla u*\nabla\nabla udV_{g}\\
&+\int_{\partial\Omega}\nabla\nabla\nabla u(\nabla\nabla u,\nu)-\nabla\nabla u(\Delta\nabla u,\nu)dA_{g},\end{split}

where
Rm
denotes the Riemann curvature tensor of
(M,g)
.

Proof.

Now we notice that since
u
is harmonic we can also deduce that
\nabla\Delta u=0
and calculate

(138)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\Delta u|^{2}dV_{g}

(139)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}g^{ij}g^{pq}g^{lm}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{p}\nabla_{q}u\nabla_{j}\nabla_{l}\nabla_{m}udV_{g}

(140)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}g^{ij}g^{pq}g^{lm}\left(\nabla_{p}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{q}u-g^{rs}R_{ipqr}\nabla_{s}u\right)\left(\nabla_{l}\nabla_{j}\nabla_{m}u-g^{vw}R_{jlmv}\nabla_{w}u\right)dV_{g}

(141)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}g^{ij}g^{pq}g^{lm}\left(\nabla_{p}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{q}u\nabla_{l}\nabla_{j}\nabla_{m}u-2g^{rs}R_{ipqr}\nabla_{s}u\nabla_{l}\nabla_{j}\nabla_{m}u+g^{vw}R_{jlmv}\nabla_{w}ug^{rs}R_{ipqr}\nabla_{s}u\right)dV_{g}

(142)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}g^{ij}g^{pq}g^{lm}\nabla_{p}\nabla_{q}\nabla_{i}u\nabla_{l}\nabla_{m}\nabla_{j}u-2g^{ij}g^{lm}g^{rs}R_{ir}\nabla_{s}u\nabla_{l}\nabla_{m}\nabla_{j}u+Rc^{2}(\nabla u,\nabla u)dV_{g}

(143)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}g^{ij}g^{pq}g^{lm}\nabla_{p}\nabla_{q}\nabla_{i}u\nabla_{l}\nabla_{m}\nabla_{j}u-2Rc(\nabla u,\Delta\nabla u)+Rc^{2}(\nabla u,\nabla u)dV_{g}

(144)

\displaystyle=I+II+III.

For
III
we note that

(145)

\displaystyle Rc^{2}(\nabla u,\nabla u)=g^{ij}g^{pq}g^{rs}R_{ip}R_{jr}\nabla_{q}u\nabla_{s}u,

and for
II
we note that

(146)

\displaystyle Rc(\nabla u,\Delta\nabla u)=g^{ij}g^{lm}g^{rs}R_{ir}\nabla_{s}u\nabla_{l}\nabla_{m}\nabla_{j}u.

Now we continue the calculation by focusing on
I

(147)

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}g^{ij}g^{pq}g^{lm}\nabla_{q}\nabla_{i}u\nabla_{p}\nabla_{l}\nabla_{m}\nabla_{j}udV_{g}+\int_{\partial\Omega}g^{ij}g^{pq}g^{lm}\nabla_{q}\nabla_{i}u\nabla_{l}\nabla_{m}\nabla_{j}u\nu_{p}dA_{g}

(148)

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}g^{ij}g^{pq}g^{lm}\nabla_{q}\nabla_{i}u\nabla_{p}\nabla_{l}\nabla_{m}\nabla_{j}udV_{g}+\int_{\partial\Omega}\nabla\nabla u(\Delta\nabla u,\nu)dA_{g}=A+B.

Now we continue with the first term

(149)

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}g^{ij}g^{pq}g^{lm}\nabla_{q}\nabla_{i}u\left(\nabla_{l}\nabla_{p}\nabla_{m}\nabla_{j}u-g^{rs}R_{plmr}\nabla_{s}\nabla_{j}u-g^{rs}R_{pljr}\nabla_{m}\nabla_{s}u\right)dV_{g}

(150)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}-g^{ij}g^{pq}g^{lm}\nabla_{q}\nabla_{i}u\nabla_{l}\nabla_{p}\nabla_{m}\nabla_{j}u+Rc*\nabla\nabla u*\nabla\nabla u+Rm*\nabla\nabla u*\nabla\nabla udV_{g}

(151)

\displaystyle=C+D+E.

For this calculation we are using

(152)

\displaystyle Rc*\nabla\nabla u*\nabla\nabla u

\displaystyle=g^{ij}g^{pq}g^{rs}R_{pr}\nabla_{s}\nabla_{j}u\nabla_{q}\nabla_{i}u,

(153)

\displaystyle Rm*\nabla\nabla u*\nabla\nabla u

\displaystyle=g^{ij}g^{lm}g^{rs}R_{pljr}\nabla_{m}\nabla_{s}u\nabla_{q}\nabla_{i}u.

Now we continue with the term
C
to find

(154)

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}g^{ij}g^{pq}g^{lm}\nabla_{l}\nabla_{q}\nabla_{i}u\nabla_{p}\nabla_{m}\nabla_{j}udV_{g}-\int_{\partial\Omega}g^{ij}g^{pq}g^{lm}\nabla_{q}\nabla_{i}u\nabla_{p}\nabla_{m}\nabla_{j}u\nu_{l}dA_{g}

(155)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}g^{ij}g^{pq}g^{lm}\nabla_{l}\nabla_{q}\nabla_{i}u\nabla_{p}\nabla_{m}\nabla_{j}udV_{g}-\int_{\partial\Omega}g^{ij}g^{pq}g^{lm}\nabla_{q}\nabla_{i}u\nabla_{p}\nabla_{j}\nabla_{m}u\nu_{l}dA_{g}

(156)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}g^{ij}g^{pq}g^{lm}\nabla_{l}\nabla_{q}\nabla_{i}u\left(\nabla_{m}\nabla_{p}\nabla_{j}u-g^{rs}R_{pmjr}\nabla_{s}u\right)dV_{g}-\int_{\partial\Omega}\nabla\nabla\nabla u(\nabla\nabla u,\nu)dA_{g}

(157)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla^{3}u|^{2}-g^{ij}g^{pq}g^{lm}g^{rs}R_{pmjr}\nabla_{l}\nabla_{q}\nabla_{i}u\nabla_{s}udV_{g}-\int_{\partial\Omega}\nabla\nabla\nabla u(\nabla\nabla u,\nu)dA_{g}

(158)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla^{3}u|^{2}-Rm*\nabla\nabla\nabla u*\nabla udV_{g}-\int_{\partial\Omega}\nabla\nabla\nabla u(\nabla\nabla u,\nu)dA_{g}.

In the calculation above we are using

(159)

\displaystyle Rm*\nabla\nabla\nabla u*\nabla u=g^{ij}g^{pq}g^{lm}g^{rs}R_{pmjr}\nabla_{l}\nabla_{q}\nabla_{i}u\nabla_{s}u.

Putting everything together we find

(160)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla^{3}u|^{2}dV_{g}&=\int_{\Omega}2Rc(\nabla u,\Delta\nabla u)-Rc^{2}(\nabla u,\nabla u)-Rc*\nabla\nabla u*\nabla\nabla udV_{g}\\
&+\int_{\Omega}Rm*\nabla\nabla\nabla u*\nabla u-Rm*\nabla\nabla u*\nabla\nabla udV_{g}\\
&+\int_{\partial\Omega}\nabla\nabla\nabla u(\nabla\nabla u,\nu)-\nabla\nabla u(\Delta\nabla u,\nu)dA_{g}\end{split}

∎

The terms on the right hand side of the estimate in Theorem
5.3
involving a square of the Hessian of
u
are concerning since we should only expect to have integral control on
|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}
and we will need to use Hölder’s inequality. To this end we prove the following proposition which will be used to overcome this issue. The proof of the following proposition is inspired by the calculations in
[
47
]
. However, we only need the following weak conclusion, whereas the result obtained in
[
47
]
is much more refined. Luckily, this significantly simplifies the proof.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
(M,g)
be an oriented complete
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold,
u
a harmonic function, and
\Omega\subset M
a bounded domain with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
. If
f\in C^{3}(\Omega)
, then we have that

(161)

\|\nabla^{2}f\|^{2}_{L^{3}}\leq\max\left(1,\|\nabla^{2}f\|^{2}_{L^{3}}\right)\leq\max\left(1,5\|\nabla f\|_{L^{6}}\|\nabla^{3}f\|_{L^{2}}+\int_{\partial\Omega}\left|\nabla^{2}f\right|^{2}|\nabla f|dA_{g}\right).

Proof.

The proof is the result of a nice integration by parts. In particular, we have that

(162)

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla^{2}f|^{3}dV_{g}=-\int_{\Omega}\left<\nabla f,\nabla^{*}(|\nabla^{2}f|\nabla^{2}f)\right>dV_{g}+\int_{\partial\Omega}\left|\nabla^{2}f\right|\nabla^{2}f\left(\nabla f,\nu\right)dA_{g}

where
\nu
is the unit outward normal to
\partial\Omega
.
Taking absolute values on the right hand side, the first term can be estimated as follows:

(163)

\displaystyle\left|\left<\nabla f,\nabla^{*}(|\nabla^{2}f|\nabla^{2}f)\right>\right|\leq|\nabla f|\left(4|\nabla^{2}f||\nabla^{3}f|+|\nabla^{2}f||\nabla^{3}f|\right)=5|\nabla f||\nabla^{2}f||\nabla^{3}f|.

Hence we have

(164)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla^{2}f|^{3}dV_{g}\leq 5\int_{\Omega}|\nabla f||\nabla^{2}f||\nabla^{3}f|dV_{g}+\int_{\partial\Omega}\left|\nabla^{2}f\right|^{2}|\nabla f|dA_{g}.

We may now use the generalized Hölder’s inequality with exponents
6
,
3
, and
2
, respectively to get

(165)

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla^{2}f|^{3}dV_{g}\leq 5\|\nabla f\|_{L^{6}}\|\nabla^{2}f\|_{L^{3}}\|\nabla^{3}f\|_{L^{2}}+\int_{\partial\Omega}\left|\nabla^{2}f\right|^{2}|\nabla f|dA_{g}.

Dividing out both sides of the above by
\|\nabla^{2}f\|_{L^{3}}
gives us that

(166)

\|\nabla^{2}f\|^{2}_{L^{3}}\leq 5\|\nabla f\|_{L^{6}}\|\nabla^{3}f\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla^{2}f\|_{L^{3}}^{-1}\int_{\partial\Omega}\left|\nabla^{2}f\right|^{2}|\nabla f|dA_{g}.

Now, if
\|\nabla^{2}f\|_{L^{3}}\geq 1
then we find that

(167)

\|\nabla^{2}f\|^{2}_{L^{3}}\leq 5\|\nabla f\|_{L^{6}}\|\nabla^{3}f\|_{L^{2}}+\int_{\partial\Omega}\left|\nabla^{2}f\right|^{2}|\nabla f|dA_{g},

and if not, then
\|\nabla^{2}f\|_{L^{3}}<1
, which is a reasonable bound in its own right.
∎

By using the previous proposition we are now able to overcome the issue of a quadratic Hessian term in order to obtain an
L^{2}
estimate on the third derivative of
u
in terms of quantities we expect to be able to estimate.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
(M,g)
be an oriented complete
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold,
u
a harmonic function, and
\Omega\subset M
a bounded domain with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
and outward pointing unit normal vector
\nu
.
Then, for any
p>1
,

(168)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla^{3}u|^{2}dV_{g}&\leq C\left(\int_{\Omega}|Rm|^{2p}+|Rc|^{2p}dV_{g}\right)^{1/p}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}dV_{g}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\\
&+C\max\left(2\left(\int_{\Omega}|Rm|^{3}+|Rc|^{3}dV_{g}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}},\left(\int_{\Omega}|Rm|^{3}+|Rc|^{3}dV_{g}\right)^{\frac{2}{3}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{6}(\Omega)}^{2}\right)\\
&+C\left(\int_{\Omega}|Rm|^{3}+|Rc|^{3}dV_{g}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}\max\left(1,\int_{\partial\Omega}|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}|\nabla u|dA_{g}\right)\\
&+\int_{\partial\Omega}\nabla\nabla\nabla u(\nabla\nabla u,\nu)-\nabla\nabla u(\Delta\nabla u,\nu)dA_{g}\end{split}

Proof.

If we apply Cauchy-Schwarz to the result from Theorem
5.3
, we find

(169)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla^{3}u|^{2}dV_{g}&\leq\int_{\Omega}2|Rc||\nabla u||\nabla^{3}u|+|Rc|^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+|Rc||\nabla^{2}u|^{2}dV_{g}\\
&+\int_{\Omega}|Rm||\nabla^{3}u||\nabla u|+|Rm||\nabla^{2}u|^{2}dV_{g}\\
&+\int_{\partial\Omega}\nabla\nabla\nabla u(\nabla\nabla u,\nu)-\nabla\nabla u(\Delta\nabla u,\nu)dA_{g}.\end{split}

Applying Hölder’s inequality with
p>1
, we find

(170)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|Rc|^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}dV_{g}\leq\left(\int_{\Omega}|Rc|^{2p}dV_{g}\right)^{1/p}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}dV_{g}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}.

We can deal with the remaining terms which are not quadratic in the Hessian of
u
by applying Young’s inequality with
\varepsilon
and Hölder’s inequality with
p>1
to find

(171)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}

\displaystyle 2|Rc||\nabla^{3}u||\nabla u|+|Rm||\nabla^{3}u||\nabla u|dV_{g}

(172)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}2\left(C(\varepsilon)|Rc|^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+\varepsilon|\nabla^{3}u|^{2}\right)+(C(\varepsilon)|Rm|^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+\varepsilon|\nabla^{3}u|^{2}dV_{g}

(173)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}C^{\prime}(\varepsilon)(|Rc|^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+|Rm|^{2}|\nabla u|^{2})+5\varepsilon|\nabla^{3}u|^{2}dV_{g}

(174)

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}(\varepsilon)\left(\int_{\Omega}|Rc|^{2p}+|Rm|^{2p}dV_{g}\right)^{1/p}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}dV_{g}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}+\int_{\Omega}5\varepsilon|\nabla^{3}u|^{2}dV_{g}.

For the terms which are quadratic in the Hessian we first apply Hólder’s inequality with
p=\frac{3}{2}
to find

(175)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(|Rm|+|Rc|)|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}dV_{g}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\Omega}|Rm|^{3}+|Rc|^{3}dV_{g}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla^{2}u|^{3}dV_{g}\right)^{\frac{2}{3}}

(176)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\Omega}|Rm|^{3}+|Rc|^{3}dV_{g}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}\max\left(1,\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{3}}^{2}\right),

and now the problem is that one cannot expect to have control on
\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{3}}^{2}
and hence we will control this term using
\|\nabla u\|_{L^{6}}
and
\|\nabla^{3}u\|_{L^{2}}
as follows.

From Proposition
5.4
, we have

(177)

\begin{split}\max\left(1,\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{3}(\Omega)}^{2}\right)&\leq\max\left(1,5\|\nabla u\|_{L^{6}(\Omega)}\|\nabla^{3}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\int_{\partial\Omega}\left|\nabla^{2}u\right|^{2}|\nabla u|\right)dA_{g},\end{split}

and hence

(178)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(|Rm|+|Rc|)|\nabla\nabla u|^{2}dV_{g}

(179)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\Omega}|Rm|^{3}+|Rc|^{3}dV_{g}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}\max\left(1,5\|\nabla u\|_{L^{6}}\|\nabla^{3}u\|_{L^{2}}+\int_{\partial\Omega}\left|\nabla^{2}u\right|^{2}|\nabla u|dA_{g}\right)

(180)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\Omega}|Rm|^{3}+|Rc|^{3}dV_{g}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}\left(\max\left(1,5\|\nabla u\|_{L^{6}}\|\nabla^{3}u\|_{L^{2}}\right)+\max\left(1,\int_{\partial\Omega}\left|\nabla^{2}u\right|^{2}|\nabla u|dA_{g}\right)\right).

Now, we may apply Young’s inequality to the first term in the last expression to find

(181)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega}|Rm|^{3}+|Rc|^{3}dV_{g}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}\max\left(1,5\|\nabla u\|_{L^{6}}\|\nabla^{3}u\|_{L^{2}}\right)

(182)

\displaystyle\leq\max\left(\left(\int_{\Omega}|Rm|^{3}+|Rc|^{3}dV_{g}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}},C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{6}}^{2}\left(\int_{\Omega}|Rm|^{3}+|Rc|^{3}dV_{g}\right)^{\frac{2}{3}}+\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla^{3}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right),

(183)

\displaystyle\leq\max\left(\left(\int_{\Omega}|Rm|^{3}+|Rc|^{3}dV_{g}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}},C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{6}}^{2}\left(\int_{\Omega}|Rm|^{3}+|Rc|^{3}dV_{g}\right)^{\frac{2}{3}}\right)

(184)

\displaystyle+\max\left(\left(\int_{\Omega}|Rm|^{3}+|Rc|^{3}dV_{g}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}},\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla^{3}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right).

Notice that if
\|\nabla^{3}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 2\left(\int_{\Omega}|Rm|^{3}+|Rc|^{3}dV_{g}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}
then we are done and if not then we also have our desired bound by subtracting off
\tfrac{1}{2}\|\nabla^{3}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
from both sides of Equation (
169
) and then keeping track of the sequence of inequalities which follow from the above equation.
∎

6.
Mass Estimates For Harmonic Functions

In this section our goal is to use the estimates of the previous section combined with the mass formula to obtain Hölder control on Harmonic functions and their inner products. One should notice that each of the estimates of the previous section involve boundary terms which involve control in the asymptotically flat region and so we require control on these terms. Since the boundaries of the
M_{r}
are in the asymptotically flat region and well understood, in this section we will apply the results of Section
5
to asymptotically linear harmonic functions over the regions
M_{r}
.

Convention:

In the following statement and throughout this section,
r_{0}
denotes the radius provided by the conclusions of Propositions
4.2
and
4.3
.
Also, throughout this section, we will fix parameters
b,\Lambda,\kappa,\bar{m}>0
and
\tau>\tfrac{1}{2}
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a member of the family
\mathcal{M}(b,\tau,\bar{m},\Lambda,\kappa)
and
let
u
be an asymptotically linear harmonic function. Then, for any
r>r_{0}
, we have

(185)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{M_{r}}|\nabla u|^{6}dV_{g}\right)^{1/6}

\displaystyle\leq C(r,r_{0},b,\tau,\kappa,\Lambda,\bar{m}),

(186)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{M_{r}}|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}dV_{g}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C(r,r_{0},b,\tau,\kappa,\Lambda,\bar{m}).

Proof.

First, assume that
u
is an asymptotically linear harmonic function from Proposition
4.1
.
By combining Proposition
4.1
with Proposition
5.1
we find that

(187)

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(M_{r})}\leq C_{1}(r,r_{0},b,\tau,\kappa,\Lambda).

Now, by the mass formula, see Equation (
1
), we know

(188)

\displaystyle 4\int_{M_{r}}|\nabla\sqrt{|\nabla u|}|^{2}dV_{g}\leq\int_{M_{r}}\frac{|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}}{|\nabla u|}dV_{g}\leq 16\pi m\leq 16\pi\bar{m}.

It follows from the fact that
M_{r}
can be contained in
B_{2d}(x)
for some
x\in M_{1}
and
d=d(r,r_{0},b,\tau,\bar{m})
, the Sobolev inequality on metric balls, Theorem
3.7
, and Equation (
187
) that we have

(189)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{M_{r}}|\nabla u|^{3}dV_{g}\right)^{1/6}\leq C_{2}(r,b,\tau,\kappa,\Lambda,\bar{m}).

Similarly, by combining Theorem
4.3
and Theorem
5.2
with
p=3
, we find that

(190)

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{2}(M_{r})}\leq C_{3}(r,b,\tau,\kappa,\Lambda,\bar{m}).

Hence by applying the fact that
|\nabla|\nabla u||^{2}\leq|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}
along with Theorem
3.7
we find that

(191)

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{6}(M_{r})}\leq C_{4}(r,b,\tau,\kappa,\Lambda,\bar{m}).

Now, since any asymptotically linear harmonic function differs from the harmonic functions of Proposition
4.1
by subtracting a constant, we obtain the desired result.
∎

Now we move to a bound on the Hessian of asymptotically linear harmonic functions in terms of the mass.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
(M,g)
be in
\mathcal{M}(b,\tau,\bar{m},\Lambda,\kappa)
and let
u
be an asymptotically linear harmonic function.
Then, for
r>r_{0}
,
2\leq q\leq 6
, and
\theta=\frac{6-q}{2q}
, the following hold

(192)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{M_{r}}|\nabla^{3}u|^{2}dV_{g}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C(r,r_{0},b,\tau,\kappa,\Lambda,\bar{m}),

(193)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{M_{r}}|\nabla^{2}u|^{q}dV_{g}\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\leq C(r,r_{0},b,\tau,\kappa,\Lambda,\bar{m})m^{\frac{\theta}{2}}.

Proof.

If we combine Theorem
4.3
, Theorem
6.1
, Theorem
3.8
, Theorem
5.5
with
p=\frac{3}{2}
, Remark
1.4
, and the fact that
M_{r}
can be contained in
B_{2d}(x)
for some
x\in M_{1}
and
d=d(r,r_{0},b,\tau,\bar{m})
, then we find that

(194)

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{3}u\|_{L^{2}(M_{r})}\leq C_{1}(r,r_{0},b,\tau,\kappa,\Lambda).

Now the fact that
|\nabla|\nabla^{2}u||^{2}\leq|\nabla^{3}u|^{2}
together with Theorem
3.8
implies that

(195)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{M_{r}}|\nabla^{2}u|^{6}dV_{g}\right)^{1/6}

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}(r,r_{0},b,\tau,\kappa,\Lambda)\left(\int_{M_{r}}|\nabla^{3}u|^{2}dV_{g}\right)^{1/2}

(196)

\displaystyle\leq C_{3}(r,r_{0},b,\tau,\kappa,\Lambda).

At this point we may use the mass formula with an
L^{q}
interpolation inequality where
2<q<6
and
\frac{1}{q}=\frac{\theta}{2}+\frac{1-\theta}{6}
to see that

(197)

7.
Estimates of Harmonic Functions on the Torus

In a similar way that we used the mass formula to control the geometry of asymptotically flat manifolds in terms of their mass, we can use the formula Theorem
1.7
to control the geometry of
3
-manifolds in
\mathcal{N}(\Lambda,V,\kappa)
in terms of the

norm of the negative part of their scalar curvature.
Before approaching Theorem
1.11
, some preliminary observations are required. The following is an important result which comes from combining information about isoperimetric profiles with the result
[
37
, Theorem 5.4]
.

Proposition 7.1
.

Let
\Lambda,V,\kappa>0
.
Then
\mathcal{N}\left(\Lambda,V,\kappa\right)
is pre-compact in the
C^{0,\gamma}
-topology for any
\gamma\in(0,1)
.
In particular, the underlying manifolds in
\mathcal{N}\left(\Lambda,V,\kappa\right)
range over a finite number of diffeomorphism types.

Proof.

According to Lemma
3.2
, we see that for any region
\Omega\subset M
such that
|\Omega|\leq\frac{1}{2V}
, we have

(233)

ID_{\frac{3}{2}}\left(\Omega\right)\geq IN_{\frac{3}{2}}\left(M,g\right)\geq\Lambda.

In particular, by Corollary
2.4
we see there is an
R(\kappa,V)
such that if

, then
|B_{r}(x)|\leq\frac{1}{2V}
. Combining this with (
233
), Lemma
2.2
, and another application of Corollary

, we obtain the following control above and below for

(234)

\frac{r^{3}}{C(\Lambda,\kappa)}\leq|B_{r}(x)|\leq C(\Lambda,\kappa)r^{3}.

This volume growth property shows that
\mathcal{N}\left(\Lambda,V,\kappa\right)
satisfies the assumptions of
[
37
, Theorem 5.4]
, allowing us to conclude that this class has bounded harmonic
\|*\|_{3,2,r}
-norm, see
[
39
,
37
]
for the definition of this norm. As a consequence, this class is precompact in the
C^{0,\gamma}
-topology for any
\gamma\in(0,1)
. Since a sequence of manifolds converging in the

sense must have a tail whose underlying manifolds are diffeomorphic, the class
\mathcal{N}\left(\Lambda,V,\kappa\right)
supports only finitely many diffeomorphism types.
∎

For each diffeomorphism type

supporting a metric in
\mathcal{N}\left(\Lambda,V,\kappa\right)
, fix once and for all three closed differential forms

. The existence of such forms follows from condition

in Definition

. Proposition

allows us to uniformly control the

-norm of the forms

.

Let
\Lambda,V,\kappa>0
. Then there is a constant

so that the following holds: if
(M,g)\in\mathcal{N}\left(\Lambda,V,\kappa\right)
, then

\|\omega_{i}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C,\qquad i=1,2,3.

Proof.

We argue by contradiction, using the
C^{0,\gamma}
, for
\gamma\in(0,1)
, pre-compactness of
\mathcal{N}\left(\Lambda,V,\kappa\right)
mentioned in the proof of Proposition
7.1
. Assuming there is no constant
C
uniformly bounding
\|\omega_{i}\|_{L^{2}}
for all

in
\mathcal{N}\left(\Lambda,V,\kappa\right)
, we may find a sequence
\{(M_{l},g_{l})\}^{\infty}_{l=1}
so that

(236)

\lim_{l\rightarrow\infty}\|\omega_{i}\|_{L^{2}(M_{l},g_{l})}=\infty

for

. As a consequence of

pre-compactness, we may pass to a subsequence and assume

are diffeomorphic to a single manifold

By the nature of this convergence,

regularity. Since the

are independent of the metric and smooth, that there exists a constant

such that

. It follows from the Dominated Convergence Theorem that

\lim_{l\rightarrow\infty}\|\omega_{i}\|_{L^{2}(g_{l})}=\|\omega_{i}\|_{L^{2}(g_{\infty})}\leq KV,

where the last inequality follows from the fact that

. This is a contradiction, and we conclude the desired result.
∎

We are now prepared to prove the stability result Theorem

(244)

\|\nabla du^{i}\|_{L^{q}}\leq\|\nabla du^{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{\theta}\|\nabla du^{i}\|_{L^{6}}^{1-\theta}\leq C_{8}(\theta,\Lambda,V,\kappa)\|R^{-}(g)\|^{\frac{\theta}{2}}_{L^{1}},

where
\tfrac{1}{q}=\tfrac{\theta}{2}+\tfrac{1-\theta}{6}
.

Since the wedge product of
\{\omega_{i}\}_{i=1}^{3}
generates the top integral cohomology of
M
,

(245)

\int_{M}du^{1}\wedge du^{2}\wedge du^{3}=1.

In addition, we have

(246)

\nabla\left(du^{1}\wedge du^{2}\wedge du^{3}\right)=\nabla du^{1}\wedge du^{2}\wedge du^{3}+du^{1}\wedge\nabla du^{2}\wedge du^{3}+du^{1}\wedge du^{2}\wedge\nabla du^{3},

so our
C^{0}

allows us to apply Hadamard’s inequality to find

(247)

\begin{split}&\left|\nabla g\left(\nabla u^{i},\nabla u^{j}\right)\right|\leq C_{3}(\Lambda,V,\kappa)\left(\left|\nabla^{2}u^{i}\right|+\left|\nabla^{2}u^{j}\right|\right)\\
&\left|\nabla\left(du^{1}\wedge du^{2}\wedge du^{3}\right)\right|\leq C_{4}(\Lambda,V,\kappa)\left(\left|\nabla du^{1}\right|+\left|\nabla du^{2}\right|+\left|\nabla du^{3}\right|\right),\end{split}

where we denote by
\nabla u^{i}
the vector field corresponding to

.
Considering the non-negative function
f

M

(248)

then (

(249)

\begin{split}&\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C_{5}(\Lambda,V,\kappa),\\
&\|\nabla f\|_{L^{q}}\leq C_{6}(\Lambda,V,\kappa)\left(\|\nabla du^{1}\|_{L^{q}}+\|\nabla du^{2}\|_{L^{q}}+\|\nabla du^{3}\|_{L^{q}}\right)\end{split}

for any

.

Combining (

) and (

, we find

(250)

\begin{split}&\|f-\frac{1}{|M|}\|_{C^{0,\gamma}}\leq C_{7}(\Lambda,V,\kappa)\left(\|\nabla du^{1}\|_{L^{q}}+\|\nabla du^{2}\|_{L^{q}}+\|\nabla du^{3}\|_{L^{q}}\right)\\
&\left\|g\left(\nabla u^{i},\nabla u^{j}\right)-g\left(\nabla u^{i},\nabla u^{j}\right)_{M}\right\|_{C^{0,\gamma}}\leq C_{8}(\Lambda,V,\kappa)\|\nabla du^{i}\|_{L^{q}},\end{split}

where
\gamma=\frac{q-3}{q}
. A technical remark is required concerning the second line of (
250
). Note that technically Theorem
3.12
only estimates the
C^{0,\gamma}_{R}
norm. However, in the present context, the
C^{0,\gamma}_{R}
norm of a function differs from its
C^{0,\gamma}
norm only by the uniformly controlled
R=R(\kappa,V)
and the
C^{0}
norm of that function.

As an immediate consequence of the first line of (
250
), if
||R^{-}_{g}||_{L^{1}}
is sufficiently small relative to
\Lambda,\kappa,
and
V
,
f
cannot vanish since
\tfrac{1}{|M|}\geq\tfrac{1}{V}

\Psi:M\to T^{3}

\Psi(x):=(u^{1}(x),u^{2}(x),u^{3}(x))

Now consider the coefficients

In light of (

), the fields

if

Consequently, the constant symmetric tensor on the torus

Proof of Corollary
1.12
.

In light of Theorem
1.11
, a diagonal argument shows that it suffices to show that the collection of flat Riemannian
3
-tori in
\mathcal{N}(\Lambda,V,\kappa)
is compact in the
C^{0,\gamma}
-topology. This follows, for instance, by Proposition
7.1
. Indeed, one may fix coordinates on
\mathbb{T}^{3}
and represent any flat metric
g
as a constant matrix
g_{ij}d\theta^{i}\otimes d\theta^{j}
.
∎

In light of Theorem
1.11
, a diagonal argument shows that it suffices to establish the following.

Proposition 7.3
.

Given
V,\Lambda>0
and
\alpha\in[1,\tfrac{3}{2}]
, the collection of flat Riemannian
3
-tori with Neumann
\alpha
-isoperimetric constant at least
\Lambda
and volume within
[V^{-1},V]
is compact in the
C^{\infty}
-topology (in particular in
C^{0,\gamma}
).

Proof.

Suppose
\mathcal{T}\in\mathcal{N}(\Lambda,V,\kappa)
is a flat torus, which we may be represent as a quotient
\mathbb{R}^{3}/\Gamma
of
3
-space by a lattice
\Gamma
generated by three vectors
\{X_{i}\}_{i=1}^{3}
. The main goal is to show
(1)
the lengths of
X_{i}
are bounded above and away from
0
in terms of
\Lambda,V
and
(2)
the angles between
X_{i},X_{j}
are bounded away from
0
and
\pi
in terms of
\Lambda,V
. Upon accomplishing this, one obtains the desired sequential compactness of flat tori in
\mathcal{N}(\Lambda,V,\kappa)
by the following argument: given a family of such tori, one applies
(1)
to find a subsequential limit of the underlying vectors to three non-zero vectors, then noting that
(2)
ensures these vectors form a lattice of full rank. The maps to the limiting torus which yield the
C^{\infty}
convergence are given by linear mappings, taking the lattice elements to their associated limits.

First, we’ll need a few computational observations. The volume of
\mathcal{T}
is computed by
|X_{1}\wedge X_{2}\wedge X_{3}|
. Meanwhile, the planes spanned by
X_{i},X_{j}
descend to
2
-dimensional tori with areas
|X_{i}\wedge X_{j}|
. Using one of these tori and an appropriate translate, one can cut
\mathcal{T}
into two pieces of equal volumes and conclude

(255)

|X_{i}\wedge X_{j}|\geq\frac{\Lambda}{2}|X_{1}\wedge X_{2}\wedge X_{3}|^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}

for
i\neq j
.

For
r>0
, consider the cubical domains
\mathcal{C}_{r}\subset\mathcal{T}
given by

(256)

\mathcal{C}_{r}=\left\{aX_{1}+b\frac{X_{2}}{|X_{2}|}+c\frac{X_{3}}{|X_{3}|}\colon a\in[0,1],\;b\in[0,r],\;c\in[0,r]\right\}.

Notice that the faces of
\mathcal{C}_{r}
where
a=0
and
a=1
are identified by the lattice
\Gamma
.
When
r<\min(|X_{2}|,|X_{3}|)
, one may compute

(257)

|\mathcal{C}_{r}|=r^{2}\frac{|X_{1}\wedge X_{2}\wedge X_{3}|}{|X_{2}||X_{3}|},\quad|\partial\;\mathcal{C}_{r}|=2r\frac{|X_{1}\wedge X_{2}|}{|X_{2}|}+2r\frac{|X_{1}\wedge X_{3}|}{|X_{3}|}.

On the other hand,
\partial_{r}|\mathcal{C}_{r}|=\tfrac{1}{2}|\partial\mathcal{C}_{r}|
. Combining these three equations, we have

(258)

\frac{|X_{1}\wedge X_{2}|}{|X_{2}|}+\frac{|X_{1}\wedge X_{3}|}{|X_{3}|}=2\frac{|X_{1}\wedge X_{2}\wedge X_{3}|}{|X_{2}||X_{3}|}.

After performing straight-forward manipulations, the identity (
258
) used with two applications of (
255
) yields

(259)

|X_{1}\wedge X_{2}\wedge X_{3}|^{\tfrac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}\geq\frac{\Lambda}{4}\left(|X_{3}|+|X_{2}|\right).

Since this inequality is symmetric in
X_{1},X_{2},X_{3}
, we may conclude an upper bound
|X_{i}|\leq 4V^{\tfrac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}/\Lambda
for
i=1,2,3
.
Using this, a lower bound for the lengths may be obtained by

(260)

\frac{1}{V}\leq|X_{1}\wedge X_{2}\wedge X_{3}|\leq|X_{1}||X_{2}||X_{3}|\leq|X_{i}|\frac{16V^{\tfrac{2\alpha-2}{\alpha}}}{\Lambda^{2}}

for any
i=1,2,3
. To summarize these last two observations, there is a constant
C(\Lambda,V)>0
so that
C(\Lambda,V)^{-1}\leq|X_{i}|\leq C(\Lambda,V)
.

All that is left is to establish that the angles
\theta_{ij}
between
X_{i},X_{j}
are bounded away from
0
and
\pi
in terms of
\Lambda,V
. This is accomplished by combining the isoperimetric inequality with the length bounds as follows

(261)

\displaystyle\sin^{2}(\theta_{ij})

\displaystyle=1-\cos^{2}(\theta_{ij})

(262)

\displaystyle=\frac{|X_{i}|^{2}|X_{j}|^{2}-\langle X_{i},X_{j}\rangle^{2}}{|X_{i}|^{2}|X_{j}|^{2}}

(263)

\displaystyle=\frac{|X_{i}\wedge X_{j}|^{2}}{|X_{i}|^{2}|X_{j}|^{2}}

(264)

\displaystyle\geq C(\Lambda,V)^{-4}\left(\frac{\Lambda}{2V^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}}\right)^{2}.

∎

Rigidity of locally symmetric rank one manifolds of infinite volume
Boris N. Apanasov

Abstract
We discuss questions by Mostow [ 26 ] , Bers [ 9 ] and Krushkal [ 22 , 23 ] about uniqueness of a conformal or spherical CR structure on the sphere at infinity ∂ H 𝔽 n \partial H_{\mathbb{F}}^{n} of symmetric rank one space H 𝔽 n H_{\mathbb{F}}^{n} over division algebra 𝔽 = ℝ , ℂ , ℍ , or ​ 𝕆 {\mathbb{F}}={\mathbb{R}}\,,{\mathbb{C}}\,,{\mathbb{H}}\,,\text{or}\,\,{\mathbb{O}} compatible with the action of a discrete group G ⊂ Isom ⁡ H 𝔽 n G\subset\operatorname{Isom}H_{\mathbb{F}}^{n} . Introducing a nilpotent Sierpiński carpet with a positive Lebesgue measure in the nilpotent geometry in ∂ H 𝔽 n ∖ { ∞ } \partial H_{\mathbb{F}}^{n}\setminus\{\infty\} and its stretching, we construct a non-rigid discrete 𝔽 \mathbb{F} -hyperbolic groups G ⊂ Isom ⁡ H 𝔽 n G\subset\operatorname{Isom}H_{\mathbb{F}}^{n} whose non-trivial deformations are induced by G G -equivariant homeomorphisms. Here we consider two situations: either the limit set Λ ⁡ ( G ) \Lambda(G) is the whole sphere at infinity ∂ H 𝔽 n \partial H_{\mathbb{F}}^{n} or restrictions of such non-trivial deformations to components of the discontinuity set Ω ⁡ ( G ) ⊂ ∂ H 𝔽 n \Omega(G)\subset\partial H_{\mathbb{F}}^{n} are given by restrictions of 𝔽 {\mathbb{F}} -hyperbolic isometries. In both cases the demonstrated non-rigidity is related to non-ergodic dynamics of the discrete group action on the limit set which could be the whole sphere at infinity.

Introduction

Many authors study deformations of negatively curved locally symmetric spaces of
{\mathbb{R}}
-rank one, that is spaces modeled
on
{\mathbb{F}}
-hyperbolic spaces
H^{n}_{{\mathbb{F}}}
over division algebras
{\mathbb{F}}
that are either
real
{\mathbb{R}}
, complex
{\mathbb{C}}
, quaternions
{\mathbb{H}}
, or
Cayley numbers (octonions)
{\mathbb{O}}
. Here the central result is the remarkable strong rigidity (see Mostow
[
26
,
27
]
) claiming that homotopy equivalence of such finite volume spaces of real dimension at least 3 implies their isometry. It can be reformulated for discrete isometric action of their fundamental groups in
{\mathbb{F}}
-hyperbolic spaces:

Theorem 1.1

Let
\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}\subset\operatorname{Isom}H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{m}
be isomorphic lattices in

{\mathbb{F}}
-hyperbolic space
H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{m}
with real dimension
n\geq 3
, that is isomorphic discrete isometry
groups with finite volume quotients
H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{m}/\Gamma_{i}<\infty
over real
{\mathbb{R}}
, complex
{\mathbb{C}}
, quaternionic
{\mathbb{H}}
or Cayley
{\mathbb{O}}
numbers. Then they are conjugate in

\operatorname{Isom}H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{m}
, and the original isomorphism is induced by this conjugation.

Examining the main stages of the proof of this rigidity theorem shows that the main question in extending this rigidity to a wider class of

\operatorname{F}
-hyperbolic structures which may have infinite volume, is concerned with
the uniqueness of a spherical CR-structure (or conformal one for real
\operatorname{F}={\mathbb{R}}
) on
the sphere at infinity
\partial H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{m}

compatible with the action of a discrete group
\Gamma\subset\operatorname{Isom}H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{m}
. It was
questioned by Mostow
[
26
]
and Krushkal
[
22
,
23
]
whether such uniqueness
takes place in the case of those discrete
\operatorname{F}
-hyperbolic groups whose limit sets

\Lambda(\Gamma)
coincide with the whole sphere
\partial H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{m}
.

The goal of this paper is to address these deformation questions and to show that in general, the uniqueness of such a spherical CR-structure (or conformal one for real
\operatorname{F}={\mathbb{R}}
, see
[
1
,
3
]
) at infinity
\partial H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{m}

and the rigidity of the corresponding
{\mathbb{F}}
-hyperbolic structure fails:

Theorem 1.2

Let
H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{n}
be a symmetric
{\mathbb{R}}
-rank one space over division algebra
{\mathbb{F}}
(either
{\mathbb{R}}
,
{\mathbb{C}}
,
{\mathbb{H}}
, or
{\mathbb{O}}
) of real dimension at least 3 whose nilpotent Carnot group
{\mathfrak{H}}\subset\operatorname{Isom}H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{n}
acting at infinity

\partial H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{n}\backslash\{\infty\}
has non-rigid lattices. Then there are non-rigid discrete
{\mathbb{F}}
-hyperbolic groups
\Gamma\subset\operatorname{Isom}H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{n}
whose limit set
\Lambda(\Gamma)
is the whole sphere
\partial H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{n}
.

Especially interesting case is related to quaternionic hyperbolic spaces
H_{{\mathbb{H}}}^{n}
or the Cayley (octonionic) hyperbolic plane
H_{{\mathbb{O}}}^{2}
. These geometries are symmetric rank one spaces having nevertheless the super rigidity of deformations of their lattices, see Corlette
[
12
]
, Pansu
[
29
]
, Gromov-Schoen
[
17
]
and Kamishima
[
19
]
.

Our construction of non-rigid discrete
{\mathbb{F}}
-hyperbolic groups
\Gamma\subset\operatorname{Isom}H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{n}
in the proof of this result allows some extension to non-trivial deformations of negatively curved locally symmetric
{\mathbb{R}}
-rank one spaces with boundary components at infinity where restrictions of homeomorphisms inducing such deformations are extensions of
{\mathbb{F}}
-hyperbolic isometries to those boundary components. This answers another question
of L. Bers
[
9
]
and S. Krushkal
[
22
]
and its analogue in
{\mathbb{F}}
-hyperbolic spaces on the support of deforming homeomorphisms at the sphere at infinity
\partial H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{n}
:

Theorem 1.3

For discrete
{\mathbb{F}}
-hyperbolic groups
\Gamma\subset\operatorname{Isom}H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{n}
isometrically acting in
symmetric
{\mathbb{R}}
-rank one spaces in Theorem
1.2
with non-empty discontinuity set

\Omega(\Gamma)\subset\partial H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{n}
,
there are their non-trivial deformations induced by continuous family of
\Gamma
-equivariant homeomorphisms

\{\Phi^{0}_{t}\}
whose restrictions to connected components of
\Omega(\Gamma)
are restrictions of some
{\mathbb{F}}
-hyperbolic isometries.
In particular, for real hyperbolic spaces
H_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}
,
n=2,3
, there are Beltrami
\Gamma
-differentials
\mu(z)dz/d\overline{z}
whose supports are contained in the limit set
\Lambda(\Gamma)\subset\overline{{\mathbb{C}}}
(of positive Lebesgue (n-1)-measure).

We should note that in both Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
, the crucial property of our constructed non-rigid discrete
{\mathbb{F}}
-hyperbolic groups
\Gamma
is that the limit
subset
\Lambda(\Gamma)\cap\overline{P(\Gamma)}
on the boundary of the fundamental polyhedron

P(\Gamma)\subset H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{m}
has a positive Lebesgue measure in the sphere at infinity
\partial H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{n}
. This is closely related
to non-ergodic dynamics of our
{\mathbb{F}}
-hyperbolic discrete holonomy group
\Gamma
action on the limit set
\Lambda(\Gamma)
in the sphere at infinity
\partial H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{n}
of the
{\mathbb{F}}
-hyperbolic space
H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{n}
, see Remarks
3.2
,
4.1
,
4.2
and
[
31
,
3
,
6
]
).

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Symmetric spaces of rank one

The symmetric spaces of
{\mathbb{R}}
-rank one of non-compact type
are the
hyperbolic spaces
H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{n}
over four division algebras
{\mathbb{F}}
- either
the real numbers
{\mathbb{R}}
, or
the complex numbers
{\mathbb{C}}
, or
the quaternions
{\mathbb{H}}
, or the Cayley numbers (octonionions)
{\mathbb{O}}
; in last case
n=2
.
They are respectively called as real, complex, quaternionic and octonionic hyperbolic
spaces (the latter one
H_{{\mathbb{O}}}^{2}
is also known as the Cayley hyperbolic plane).
Algebraically these spaces can be described as the corresponding quotients:

\operatorname{SO}(n,1)/\operatorname{SO}(n)
,

\operatorname{SU}(n,1)/\operatorname{SU}(n)
,

\operatorname{Sp}(n,1)/\operatorname{Sp}(n)\cdot\operatorname{Sp}(1)
and

\operatorname{F}_{4}^{-20}/\operatorname{Spin}(9)

where the latter group
\operatorname{F}_{4}^{-20}
of automorphisms of the Cayley plane
H_{{\mathbb{O}}}^{2}

is the real form of
\operatorname{F}_{4}
of rank one. We normalize
the metric so the (negative) sectional curvature of
H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{n}
is bounded
from below by
-1
.

Following Mostow
[
26
]
and using the standard involution (conjugation) in
{\mathbb{F}}
,

z\to\bar{z}
, one can define projective models of the hyperbolic spaces

H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{n}
as the set of negative lines in the Hermitian vector space
{\mathbb{F}}^{n,1}
,
with Hermitian structure given by the indefinite
(n,1)
-form

\langle\langle z,w\rangle\rangle=z_{1}\overline{w}_{1}+\cdots+z_{n}\overline{w}_{n}-z_{n+1}\overline{w}_{n+1}\,.

The boundary
\partial H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{n}
consists of all null
{\mathbb{F}}
-lines,

\partial H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{n}=\{[z]\in{\mathbb{P}}{\mathbb{F}}^{n,1}\!:\!\langle\langle z,z\rangle\rangle=0\}\,,

and is homeomorphic to the sphere
S^{kn-1}
with
k=\dim_{{\mathbb{R}}}{\mathbb{F}}
.

Taking non-homogeneous coordinates, one can obtain ball models (in the unit ball

B^{n}_{{\mathbb{F}}}
in
{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
) for the first three spaces. Here we note that since the multiplication by quaternions
is not commutative, we specify that we use
“left” vector space
{\mathbb{H}}^{n,1}

where the multiplication by quaternion numbers is on the left.
However, it does not work for
the Cayley hyperbolic plane since
{\mathbb{O}}
is non-associative, and one should use a Jordan algebra of

3\times 3
Hermitian matrices with entries from
{\mathbb{O}}
whose group of automorphisms is

\operatorname{F}_{4}
, see
[
30
,
26
]
.

2.2
Upper half-space model of
H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{n}

Another models of
H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{n}
use the so called horospherical coordinates
[
8
,
7
,
15
]

based on foliations
of
H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{n}
by horospheres centered at a fixed point
\infty
at infinity
\partial H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{n}
which is
homeomorphic to
(n\dim_{{\mathbb{R}}}{\mathbb{F}}-1)
-dimensional sphere. Such a horosphere can be
identified with the nilpotent
group
N
in the Iwasawa decomposition
KAN
of the automorphism group
of
H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{n}
. The nilpotent
group
N
can be identified with the product
{\mathfrak{H}}={\mathbb{F}}^{n-1}\times\operatorname{Im}{\mathbb{F}}
(see
[
26
]
)
equipped with the operations:

(\xi,v)\cdot(\xi^{\prime},v^{\prime})=(\xi+\xi^{\prime},v+v^{\prime}+2\operatorname{Im}\langle\xi,\xi^{\prime}\rangle)\quad\text{and}\quad(\xi,v)^{-1}=(-\xi,-v)\,,

where
\langle,\rangle
is the standard Hermitian product
in
{\mathbb{F}}^{n-1}
,
\langle z,w\rangle=\sum z_{i}\overline{w_{i}}
.
The group
{\mathfrak{H}}
is a 2-step
nilpotent Carnot group with center
\{0\}\times\operatorname{Im}{\mathbb{F}}\subset{\mathfrak{H}}={\mathbb{F}}^{n-1}\times\operatorname{Im}{\mathbb{F}}
,
and acts on itself by the left translations
T_{h}(g)=h\cdot g\,,\quad h,g\in{\mathfrak{H}}
.

Now we may identify

H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{n}\cup\partial H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{n}\backslash\{\infty\}\longrightarrow{\mathfrak{H}}\times[0,\infty)={\mathbb{F}}^{n-1}\times\operatorname{Im}{\mathbb{F}}\times[0,\infty)\,,

and call this identification the
“upper half-space model”
for
H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{n}

with the natural horospherical coordinates
(\xi,v,u)
.
In these coordinates, the above left action
of
{\mathfrak{H}}
on itself extends to
an isometric action (Carnot translations) on the
{\mathbb{F}}
-hyperbolic space
in the following form:

T_{(\xi_{0},v_{0})}\!:\!(\xi,v,u)\longmapsto(\xi_{0}+\xi\,,v_{0}+v+2\operatorname{Im}\langle\xi_{0},\xi\rangle\,,u)\,,

where
(\xi,v,u)\in{\mathbb{F}}^{n-1}\times\operatorname{Im}{\mathbb{F}}\times[0,\infty)
.

There are a natural norm and an induced by this norm distance on the Carnot group

{\mathfrak{H}}={\mathbb{F}}^{n-1}\times\operatorname{Im}{\mathbb{F}}
, which are known in the case of the
Heisenberg group (when
{\mathbb{F}}={\mathbb{C}}
) as the Korányi-Cygan’s norm and distance.
Using horospherical coordinates, they can be extended to
a norm on
H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{n}
, see
[
8
,
7
]
:

\displaystyle|(\xi,v,u)|_{c}=|\,(|\xi|^{2}+u-v)|_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{1/2}\,,

(1)

where
|.|_{{\mathbb{F}}}
is the norm in
{\mathbb{F}}
, and to
a metric
\rho_{c}
on
the upper half-space model
{\mathbb{F}}^{n-1}\times\operatorname{Im}{\mathbb{F}}\times(0,\infty)
of

H^{n}_{{\mathbb{F}}}
:

\displaystyle\rho_{c}\bigl((\xi,v,u),(\xi^{\prime},v^{\prime},u^{\prime})\bigr)=\bigl|\,|\xi-\xi^{\prime}|^{2}+|u-u^{\prime}|-(v-v^{\prime}+2\operatorname{Im}\langle\xi,\xi^{\prime}\rangle)\bigr|_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\,.

(2)

It follows directly from the definition that
Carnot translations and rotations are isometries
with respect to the Korányi-Cygan metric
\rho_{c}
.
Moreover, the restrictions of this metric to different
horospheres centered at
\infty
are the same, so the
Korányi-Cygan metric plays the same role as Euclidean metric does on the upper half-space model
for the real hyperbolic space
H_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}
.

Non-zero numbers
\lambda\in{\mathbb{F}}^{*}
act on the Carnot group
{\mathfrak{H}}={\mathbb{F}}^{n-1}\times\operatorname{Im}{\mathbb{F}}\times\{0\}

and on the complex hyperbolic space
H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{n}={\mathbb{F}}^{n-1}\times\operatorname{Im}{\mathbb{F}}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
by Carnot dilations (which, for

|\lambda|_{{\mathbb{F}}}=1
, are in fact rotations):

\displaystyle D_{\lambda}\!:\!(\xi,v,u)\longmapsto(\lambda\xi\,,|\lambda|_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{2}v\,,|\lambda|_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{2}u)\,.

(3)

2.3
Spinal spheres in
\partial H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{n}
, bisectors and inversions

For a
{\mathbb{F}}
-hyperbolic hypersubspace
V=H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{n-1}\subset H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{n}
, its boundary at infinity
\partial V\subset\partial H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{n}
is a
{\mathbb{F}}
-hyperchain. Assuming
H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{n}=\operatorname{F}^{n-1}\times\operatorname{Im}\operatorname{F}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
, one has infinite hyperchains containing
\infty
and finite ones. Let us denote
{\mathbb{F}}
-inversion of order 2 with respect to
V
by
{\cal I}_{V}
. Its set of fixed points is
V
. If a hyperchain
\partial V\subset{\mathbb{F}}^{n-1}\times\operatorname{Im}{\mathbb{F}}\times\{0\}\cong{\mathfrak{H}}
is finite, its center is
{\cal I}_{V}(\infty)\in{\mathfrak{H}}
.
In the unit ball model
B_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{n}\subset{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
of the
{\mathbb{F}}
-hyperbolic space
H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{n}
, the
{\mathbb{F}}
-inversion
{\cal I}_{V}
acts as
{\cal I}\,:\,(z^{\prime},z_{n})\mapsto(z^{\prime},-z_{n})
. Considering an isometry of this ball model to the upper half-space model of the
{\mathbb{F}}
-hyperbolic space
H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{n}
(where one has
the nilpotent geometry
{\mathfrak{H}}={\mathbb{F}}^{n-1}\times\operatorname{Im}{\mathbb{F}}\times\{0\}
at infinity of
\overline{H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{n}}\backslash\{\infty\}
),
one has the hyperchain
\partial V=(z^{\prime},0)\in\partial B^{n}_{{\mathbb{F}}}
as the hyperchain

(z,\{0\})\in{\mathbb{F}}^{n-1}\times\operatorname{Im}{\mathbb{F}}\!:\!\,\,\mid z\mid=1
in the upper half-space model of the
\operatorname{F}
-hyperbolic space

H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{n}
. Then the above
inversion
{\cal I}
in the unit ball is conjugate to the inversion in the finite hyperchain
\partial V=\partial H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{n-1}
in the upper half-space model of the
\operatorname{F}
-hyperbolic space
H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{n}
which acts in the Carnot group
{\mathfrak{H}}
as follows:

\displaystyle{\cal I}(\xi,v)=\left(\frac{\xi}{|\xi|^{2}-v}\,,\,\frac{-v}{|v|^{2}+|\xi|^{4}}\right)\quad\text{where}\,\,(\xi,v)\in{\mathfrak{H}}=\operatorname{F}^{n-1}\times\operatorname{Im}\operatorname{F}\,.

(4)

Obviously, the inversion
{\cal I}
interchanges the origin in the Carnot group
{\mathfrak{H}}
and

\infty
and preserves the unit sphere (in Korányi-Cygan metric
\rho_{c}
):

\displaystyle S(0,1)=\{(\xi,v)\!:\!\,\,|v|^{2}+|\xi|^{4}=1\}

(5)

containig the (bounded, horizontal) hyperchain
\partial V
which is pointwise fixed
by
{\cal I}
, and interchanges the points
(0,v)
and
(0,-v)
, where
(0,v)
are points in the unit sphere
S(0,1)
which belong to the center
\{0\}\times\operatorname{Im}{\mathbb{F}}
of the Carnot group
{\mathfrak{H}}={\mathbb{F}}^{n-1}\times\operatorname{Im}{\mathbb{F}}
. In the case
{\mathbb{F}}={\mathbb{C}}
,
the Carnot group
{\mathfrak{H}}
is the Heisenberg group
{\cal H}_{2n-1}={\mathbb{C}}^{n-1}\times{\mathbb{R}}
, and
the inversion
{\cal I}
interchanges the unit sphere
S(0,1)
poles
(0,1)
and
(0,-1)
in the center
\{0\}\times{\mathbb{R}}
of the Heisenberg group
{\cal H}_{2n-1}
.

For a given Carnot ball
B_{i}\subset{\mathfrak{H}}
at infinity of
{\mathbb{F}}
-hyperbolic space
H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{n}
, one has an isometry

h_{i}\in\operatorname{Isom}H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{n}
that maps this ball
B_{i}
to the unit
{\mathfrak{H}}
-ball
B(0,1)\subset{\mathfrak{H}}={\mathbb{F}}^{n-1}\times\operatorname{Im}{\mathbb{F}}

centered at the origin.
Then the
{\mathbb{F}}
-hyperbolic isometry

\displaystyle{\cal I}_{i}=h_{i}^{-1}{\cal I}h_{i}\in\operatorname{Isom}H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{n}

(6)

is the inversion of order 2 that preserves
the boundary
{\mathfrak{H}}
-sphere
S_{i}=\partial B_{i}
. It interchanges the balls in
\overline{{\mathfrak{H}}}

bounded by this sphere and pointwise fixes the (horizontal) hyperchain

c_{i}\subset S_{i}
.

The above
{\mathfrak{H}}
-spheres
S_{i}=\partial B_{i}
in the Carnot group
{\mathfrak{H}}={\mathbb{F}}^{n-1}\times\operatorname{Im}{\mathbb{F}}
and their isometric
{\mathbb{F}}
-hyperbolic images in the sphere at infinity
\partial H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{n}
(sometimes called spinal spheres, see
[
26
,
15
]
) are boundaries of
{\mathbb{F}}
-hyperbolic bisectors with respect the
{\mathbb{F}}
-hyperbolic metric
d
and pairs of points
z_{1}
,
z_{2}
:

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{S}}(z_{1},z_{2})=\{z\in H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{n}\!:\!\,\,d(z_{1},z)=d(z_{2},z)\}\,

(7)

which are hypersurfaces in
H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{n}
. In the case of
{\mathbb{F}}={\mathbb{R}}
, i.e. in the real hyperbolic space
H_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}
, they are totally geodesic hyperbolic hyperplanes. Bisectors in
{\mathbb{F}}
-hyperbolic spaces with complex structures (when
{\mathbb{F}}\neq{\mathbb{R}}
) cannot be totally geodesic due to a well-known result of H.Busemann
[
10
]
, Theorem 47.4, p.331
(see also
[
13
,
7
]
).
Nevertheless, as it was pointed out by G.D.Mostow in the complex hyperbolic geometry, such bisectors in
{\mathbb{F}}
-hyperbolic spaces with complex structures are as close to being totally geodesic as possible. They are
minimal hypersurfaces of cohomogenity (
\dim_{{\mathbb{R}}}{\mathbb{F}}-1
), all
equivalent under
{\mathbb{F}}
-hyperbolic isometries, and have a natural decomposition
into totally geodesic
{\mathbb{F}}
-hypersurfaces (
\cong H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{n-1}
), cf.
[
6
]
.

2.4
Spherical CR-structures

The
\operatorname{F}
-hyperbolic spaces
H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{m}
with complex structures (with
{\mathbb{F}}\neq{\mathbb{R}}
) are naturally connected with spherical Cauchy-Riemannian (CR) structures at their infinity. Here a CR-structure on a real hypersurface in a complex manifold is defined by its largest subbundle in the tangent bundle that is invariant under the complex structure. In our case of the sphere
S^{n}=\partial H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{m}
, its spherical CR-structure is arising from describing this sphere as the boundary of the unit
{\mathbb{F}}
-ball
B^{m}_{{\mathbb{F}}}\subset{\mathbb{F}}^{m}
. A CR-structure locally equivalent to the CR-structure of the boundary sphere of the
{\mathbb{F}}
-ball in
{\mathbb{F}}^{m}
is called a spherical CR-structure. A spherical CR-structure can also be viewed as a
(G,X)
-structure (see
[
3
,
6
]
) with
X=S^{n}
and
G
as the isometry groups of one of
{\mathbb{F}}
-hyperbolic spaces acting in the unit
{\mathbb{F}}
-ball

B^{m}_{{\mathbb{F}}}\subset{\mathbb{F}}^{m}
. A spherical CR-structure on a manifold/orbifold
M
is said to be uniformizable if it is obtained from a discrete subgroup
\Gamma\subset G
by taking the quotient of the discontinuous action of
\Gamma
on an invariant connected component
\Omega_{0}\subset\Omega(\Gamma)
of the discontinuity set
\Omega(\Gamma)\subset\partial{\mathbb{F}}^{m}=S^{n}
, see
[
3
,
6
]
.

3
Discrete groups with
\Lambda(G)=\partial H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{n}

Here we address a necessary condition for rigidity of deformations of locally symmetric
{\mathbb{R}}
-rank one manifolds/orbifolds
M
universally covered by one of
{\mathbb{F}}
-hyperbolic spaces
H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{n}
over division algebras
{\mathbb{F}}
- either
the real numbers
{\mathbb{R}}
, or
the complex numbers
{\mathbb{C}}
, or
the quaternions
{\mathbb{H}}
, or the Cayley numbers (octonions)
{\mathbb{O}}
(in the latter case
n=2
) -
the uniqueness of a spherical CR-structure (or
conformal one for real
{\mathbb{F}}={\mathbb{R}}
) at infinity
H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{n}
invariant for
{\mathbb{F}}
-hyperbolic isometric action of the fundamental group

\pi_{1}(M)\cong\Gamma\subset\operatorname{Isom}H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{n}
whose limit set
\Lambda(\Gamma)
is the whole sphere at infinity
\partial H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{n}
.
As Theorem
1.2
claims, such uniqueness of conformal/spherical CR-structures fails, and there are non-rigid discrete
{\mathbb{F}}
-hyperbolic isometry groups
\Gamma
with
\Lambda(\Gamma)=\partial H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{n}
and corresponding non-rigid
{\mathbb{F}}
-hyperbolic manifolds/orbifolds
M=H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{n}/\Gamma
(of infinite volume) without boundary at infinity.

Proof:

In order to prove Theorem
1.2
, here we provide a construction of desired non-rigid discrete groups

\Gamma\subset\operatorname{Isom}H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{n}
with
\Lambda(\Gamma)=\partial H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{n}
, together with their non-trivial
deformations, i.e. non-trivial curves in the discrete faithful representation varieties of
\Gamma
passing through the inclusion
\Gamma\subset\operatorname{Isom}H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{n}
. The discrete faithful representations
\rho_{t}\!:\!\,\,\Gamma\rightarrow\operatorname{Isom}H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{n}
defining such curves are obtained by families
\{\Phi_{t}\}
,
t\in(-\epsilon,\epsilon)
, of
\Gamma
-equivariant
homeomorphisms with bounded distortion,
\Phi_{t}\!:\!\overline{H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{n}}\rightarrow\overline{H_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{n}}
.

We shall start with partitions of unit cubes either in the
(n-1)
-dimensional Euclidean space,

\displaystyle Q=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}\!:\!|x_{i}|\leq 1/2\}\,,

(8)

or in the Carnot nilpotent group

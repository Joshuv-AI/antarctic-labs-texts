One-point thermal conformal blocks from four-point conformal integrals
Konstantin Alkalaev, and Semyon Mandrygin

Abstract
We develop the thermal shadow formalism to study the conformal blocks decomposition in D D -dimensional conformal field theory on 𝕊 β 1 × 𝕊 D − 1 \mathbb{S}_{\beta}^{1}\times\mathbb{S}^{D-1} , where the temperature is T = β − 1 T=\beta^{-1} . It is demonstrated that both the 1-point thermal ( T ≠ 0 T\neq 0 ) conformal blocks and the 4-point plane ( T = 0 T=0 ) conformal blocks are defined by the same 4-point conformal integral. It is shown that up to power prefactors the 1-point thermal conformal block is given by the fourth Appell function.

One-point correlation function at finite temperature

We consider a theory at finite temperature
T=\beta^{-1}
living on a cylinder
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{D-1}
with the standard metric
{\rm d}s^{2}_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{D-1}}={\rm d}\tau^{2}+{\rm d}\Omega^{2}_{D-1}
and define the 1-point correlation function of a scalar primary operator
\phi_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{D-1}}(\tau,\Omega)
of conformal dimension
h
as

\langle\phi_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{D-1}}(\tau,\Omega)\rangle_{\beta}=\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathcal{H}}\left[\phi_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{D-1}}(\tau,\Omega)e^{-\beta D}\right]\\
.

(2.1)

Here,
D
is a dilatation operator and the trace
\operatorname{Tr}
is taken over the space of states
2
2

2

Within the radial quantization the space of states
\mathcal{H}
is given by functions on
\mathbb{S}^{D-1}
and the dilatation
D
acts as the Hamiltonian evolving (in time on a cylinder
\tau
) the states from one sphere to another.

\mathcal{H}=\oplus V_{\Delta,{\bm{s}}}
, where
V_{\Delta,{\bm{s}}}
is a (generalized) Verma module for the conformal algebra
o(D+1,1)
with conformal dimension
\Delta
and generalized spin
{\bm{s}}=(s_{1},...,s_{[\frac{D+2}{2}]})
. We will be considering only scalar Verma modules in which case
{\bm{s}}=0
; then,
V_{\Delta,{\bm{s}}}\equiv V_{\Delta}
is a highest weight representation generated from a primary state
\ket{\Delta}
defined by

D\ket{\Delta}=\Delta\ket{\Delta}\,,\qquad J_{\mu\nu}\ket{\Delta}=0\,,\qquad K_{\mu}\ket{\Delta}=0\,,

(2.2)

where the conformal algebra generators obey the commutation relations

\begin{array}[]{l}{\left[D,P_{\mu}\right]}=P_{\mu}\,,\quad\left[D,K_{\mu}\right]=-K_{\mu}\,,\quad\left[K_{\mu},P_{\nu}\right]=2\delta_{\mu\nu}D-2J_{\mu\nu}\;,\\
\displaystyle{\left[J_{\mu\nu},P_{\rho}\right]}=\delta_{\nu\rho}P_{\mu}-\delta_{\mu\rho}P_{\nu}\,,\quad\left[J_{\mu\nu},K_{\rho}\right]=\delta_{\nu\rho}K_{\mu}-\delta_{\mu\rho}K_{\nu}\\
\displaystyle{\left[J_{\mu\nu},J_{\rho\sigma}\right]}=\delta_{\nu\rho}J_{\mu\sigma}-\delta_{\mu\rho}J_{\nu\sigma}+\delta_{\nu\sigma}J_{\rho\mu}-\delta_{\mu\sigma}J_{\rho\nu}\,,\end{array}

(2.3)

with
\delta_{\mu\nu}
being the
o(D)
invariant tensor,
\mu,\nu,...=1,...,D
. The inner product is defined by the conjugation rules:
D^{\dagger}=D
,
J_{\mu\nu}^{\dagger}=-J_{\mu\nu}
,
P_{\mu}^{\dagger}=K_{\mu}
, and
K_{\mu}^{\dagger}=P_{\mu}
. Descendant states in
V_{\Delta}
:

\ket{\Delta+n}_{\mu_{1}...\mu_{n}}=P_{\mu_{1}}\ldots P_{\mu_{n}}\ket{\Delta}\,,\quad D\ket{\Delta+n}_{\mu_{1}...\mu_{n}}=(\Delta+n)\ket{\Delta+n}_{\mu_{1}...\mu_{n}}\,,

(2.4)

n=0,1,2,...
is a level. Each level is reducible with respect to
o(D)
subalgebra and decomposes into
[n/2]+1
finite-dimensional irreducible
o(D)
representations of spins
j=n,n-2,n-4,...
with multiplicity 1.

The thermal correlation function (
2.1
) is periodic in time coordinate
\tau
:

\begin{array}[]{l}\langle\phi_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{D-1}}(\tau,\Omega)\rangle_{\beta}=\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathcal{H}}\Big[e^{\tau D}\phi_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{D-1}}(0,\Omega)e^{-\tau D}e^{-\beta D}\Big]\\
\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt=\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathcal{H}}\Big[e^{-\beta D}e^{(\beta+\tau)D}\phi_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{D-1}}(0,\Omega)e^{-(\beta+\tau)D}\Big]=\langle\phi_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{D-1}}(\tau+\beta,\Omega)\rangle_{\beta}\,,\end{array}

(2.5)

where in the second line we inserted
\mathbb{1}=e^{-\beta D}e^{\beta D}
and used the cyclic property. It means that this correlation function is actually defined on
\mathbb{S}_{\beta}^{1}\times\mathbb{S}^{D-1}
. When considering higher-point correlation functions the argument (
2.5
) turns into KMS condition, serving as a crossing symmetry equation in the thermal bootstrap
[
21
,
12
]
. In fact, the inverse temperature
\beta
is always present in thermal CFT
D
as the geometric parameter.

On the other hand,
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{D-1}
is related to
\mathbb{R}^{D}
with coordinates
x^{\mu}
through the standard Weyl map
r=e^{\tau}
, where
r^{2}=x^{\mu}x_{\mu}
. The transformation rule for a primary operator
\phi_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{D-1}}(\tau,\Omega)=r^{h}\phi_{\mathbb{R}^{D}}(x)
allows expressing (
2.1
) as

\langle\phi_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{D-1}}(\tau,\Omega)\rangle_{\beta}=r^{h}\,\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathcal{H}}\left[\phi_{\mathbb{R}^{D}}(x)e^{-\beta D}\right]\equiv r^{h}\langle\phi_{\mathbb{R}^{D}}(x)\rangle_{\beta}\,.

(2.6)

In what follows we will study thermal correlation functions of scalar primary operators
\phi(x)\equiv\phi_{\mathbb{R}^{D}}(x)
on
\mathbb{R}^{D}
defined by (
2.6
). Focusing on the contribution of scalar operators one can expand the thermal correlator
\langle\phi(x)\rangle_{\beta}
in conformal blocks

\begin{split}\langle\phi(x)\rangle_{\beta}\simeq\sum_{\Delta}C_{\Delta,h,\Delta}\mathcal{F}_{\Delta}^{h}(q,x)\,,\end{split}

(2.7)

the symbol
\simeq
means that we omitted contributions of spinning operators. Each scalar thermal conformal block here is the series in
q=\exp(-\beta)

\mathcal{F}_{\Delta}^{h}(q,x)=(C_{\Delta,h,\Delta})^{-1}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}q^{\Delta+n}\left(B^{-1}_{\Delta}\right)^{\mu_{1}\ldots\mu_{n};\nu_{1}\ldots\nu_{n}}{}_{\nu_{1}\ldots\nu_{n}}\langle\Delta+n|\phi(x)|\Delta+n\rangle_{\mu_{1}\ldots\mu_{n}}\,,

(2.8)

where
B_{\nu_{1}\ldots\nu_{n};\mu_{1}\ldots\mu_{n}}={}_{\nu_{1}\ldots\nu_{n}}\langle\Delta+n|\Delta+n\rangle_{\mu_{1}\ldots\mu_{n}}
is a Gram matrix in
V_{\Delta}
at
n
-th level. From (
2.6
) it follows that the scalar thermal conformal blocks in different coordinates are related as
\mathcal{F}_{\Delta}^{h}(q,\tau,\Omega)=r^{h}\,\mathcal{F}_{\Delta}^{h}(q,x)
.

The 1-point thermal correlation function (
2.1

\langle\phi(x)\rangle_{{\bm{\beta}}}\equiv\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathcal{H}}\left[q_{1}^{H_{1}}\cdots q_{r}^{H_{r}}\\
\phi(x)\right]\,,

(2.9)

Thermal shadow formalism

For a scalar primary operator
\mathcal{O}_{\Delta}(x)\equiv\mathcal{O}(x)
one can define the shadow operator
[

,
16
,
17
,
18
,
24
,

\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(x)=N_{\Delta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{D}}{\rm d}^{D}x_{0}\,(x_{0}-x)^{-2\tilde{\Delta}}\,\mathcal{O}(x_{0})\,,\qquad N_{\Delta}=\pi^{-D}\frac{\Gamma(\Delta)\Gamma(\tilde{\Delta})}{\Gamma(\frac{D}{2}-\Delta)\Gamma(\frac{D}{2}-\tilde{\Delta})}\,,

(3.1)

which is a primary operator of (dual/shadow) conformal dimension
\tilde{\Delta}=D-\Delta
; the power-law function
(x_{0}-x)^{-2\tilde{\Delta}}
is its propagator. With the chosen normalization factor
N_{\Delta}
the 2-point correlation function reads as

\langle\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(x_{1})\mathcal{O}(x_{2})\rangle=\delta(x_{1}-x_{2})\,,

(3.2)

so one can define the conformally invariant projecting operator which satisfy the idempotent and orthogonality properties

\Pi_{\Delta}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{D}}{\rm d}^{D}x\,\mathcal{O}(x)|0\rangle\langle 0|\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(x)\,,\qquad\Pi_{\Delta_{n}}\Pi_{\Delta_{m}}=\delta_{\Delta_{n}\Delta_{m}}\Pi_{\Delta_{m}}\,.

(3.3)

Since
\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}
is a primary operator one finds the following 3-point function

\langle\phi_{1}(x_{1})\phi_{2}(x_{2})\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(x_{3})\rangle=C_{h_{1},h_{2},\tilde{\Delta}}V_{h_{1},h_{2},\tilde{\Delta}}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\,,

(3.4)

where
\phi_{h_{i}}(x_{i})\equiv\phi_{i}(x_{i})
are primary operators of conformal dimensions
h_{i}
,
C_{h_{i},h_{j},h_{k}}
are structure constants, and
6
6

6

It is convenient to use
\Delta
and/or
\tilde{\Delta}
for internal (shadow) dimensions and
h_{i}
for external dimensions.

V_{h_{i},h_{j},h_{k}}(x_{i},x_{j},x_{k})=X_{ij}^{\frac{h_{k}-h_{i}-h_{j}}{2}}X_{ik}^{\frac{h_{j}-h_{i}-h_{k}}{2}}X_{jk}^{\frac{h_{i}-h_{j}-h_{k}}{2}}\,,\quad\text{where}\quad X_{ij}=|x_{i}-x_{j}|^{2}\,.

(3.5)

On the other hand, using the integral formula (

) one can show that

\langle\phi_{1}(x_{1})\phi_{2}(x_{2})\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(x_{3})\rangle=C_{h_{1},h_{2},\Delta}N_{\Delta}X_{12}^{\frac{\Delta-h_{1}-h_{2}}{2}}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{D}}{\rm d}^{D}x_{0}\,X_{01}^{\frac{-\Delta-h_{12}}{2}}X_{02}^{\frac{-\Delta+h_{12}}{2}}X_{03}^{-\tilde{\Delta}}\,,

(3.6)

where

. Recalling that

one observes that the sum of powers of propagators under the integral equals the space dimension

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{D}}{\rm d}^{D}x_{0}\,X_{01}^{-a_{1}}X_{02}^{-a_{2}}X_{03}^{-a_{3}}=\pi^{\frac{D}{2}}\Gamma\left[\begin{array}[]{l l}a^{\prime}_{1},a^{\prime}_{2},a^{\prime}_{3}\\

(3.7)

where
a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3}=D
and
a^{\prime}=\frac{D}{2}-a
, the notation for
\Gamma

\Gamma\left[\begin{array}[]{l l}a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\\
b_{1},\ldots,b_{m}\end{array}\right]=\frac{\Gamma(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})}{\Gamma(b_{1},\ldots,b_{m})}\,,\qquad\Gamma(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Gamma(a_{i})\,.

(3.8)

For later purposes, comparing (

and

3.1
One-point thermal conformal partial wave

Let us see how to use shadow operators in order to calculate the scalar conformal blocks in thermal CFT
D
. To this end, one inserts the projecting operator
\Pi_{\Delta}
(
3.3
) into the 1-point thermal correlation function (
2.7
)-(
2.8
), considering only the scalar operator contributions

\begin{split}\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathcal{H}}\left[\Pi_{\Delta}\phi(x)q^{D}\right]&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{D}}{\rm d}^{D}x_{0}\,\sum_{\Delta_{1}}\,\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(B^{-1}_{\Delta_{1}}\right)^{\mu_{1}\ldots\mu_{n};\nu_{1}\ldots\nu_{n}}\\
&\times{}_{\nu_{1}\ldots\nu_{n}}\langle\Delta_{1}+n|\mathcal{O}(x_{0})\ket{0}\bra{0}\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(x_{0})\phi(x)q^{D}|\Delta_{1}+n\rangle_{\mu_{1}\ldots\mu_{n}}\,,\end{split}

(3.10)

where the trace
\operatorname{Tr}
over the space of states
\mathcal{H}
is expanded into intermediate states of conformal dimensions
\Delta_{1}
. The action of
\mathcal{O}(x)
on (translationally invariant) vacuum state
\ket{0}
creates the sum over descendant states:

\begin{split}\mathcal{O}(x)\ket{0}&=e^{x\cdot P}\mathcal{O}(0)e^{-x\cdot P}\ket{0}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{\rho_{1}}\cdots x^{\rho_{m}}}{m!}P_{\rho_{1}}\cdots P_{\rho_{m}}\mathcal{O}(0)\ket{0}\\
&=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{\rho_{1}}\cdots x^{\rho_{m}}}{m!}\ket{\Delta+m}_{\rho_{1}\ldots\rho_{m}}\,.\end{split}

(3.11)

Then, it follows that

\left(B^{-1}_{\Delta_{1}}\right)^{\mu_{1}\ldots\mu_{n};\nu_{1}\ldots\nu_{n}}{}_{\nu_{1}\ldots\nu_{n}}\langle\Delta_{1}+n|\mathcal{O}(x_{0})\ket{0}\\
=\delta_{\Delta\Delta_{1}}\frac{x_{0}^{\rho_{1}}\cdots x_{0}^{\rho_{n}}}{n!}\,(B^{-1}_{\Delta_{1}})^{\mu_{1}\ldots\mu_{n};\nu_{1}\ldots\nu_{n}}\,(B_{\Delta})_{\nu_{1}\ldots\nu_{n};\rho_{1}\ldots\rho_{n}}=\delta_{\Delta\Delta_{1}}\frac{x_{0}^{\mu_{1}}\cdots x_{0}^{\mu_{n}}}{n!}\,,

(3.12)

where we used that the descendant states are orthogonal (by construction, they are either at different levels in the same module or belong to different modules). Coming back to (
3.10
) we see that

\begin{split}\operatorname{Tr}\left[\Pi_{\Delta}\phi(x)q^{D}\right]&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{D}}{\rm d}^{D}x_{0}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{x_{0}^{\mu_{1}}\cdots x_{0}^{\mu_{n}}}{n!}\,\bra{0}\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(x_{0})\phi(x)q^{D}|\Delta+n\rangle_{\mu_{1}\ldots\mu_{n}}\\
&=q^{\Delta}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{D}}{\rm d}^{D}x_{0}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{qx_{0}^{\mu_{1}}\cdots qx_{0}^{\mu_{n}}}{n!}\,\bra{0}\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(x_{0})\phi(x)|\Delta+n\rangle_{\mu_{1}\ldots\mu_{n}}\,.\end{split}

(3.13)

Using (
3.11
) we finally conclude that

\operatorname{Tr}\left[\Pi_{\Delta}\phi(x)q^{D}\right]=C_{\Delta,h,\Delta}\Upsilon_{\Delta}^{h}(q,x)\,,

(3.14)

where we explicitly singled out a 1-point
thermal conformal partial wave
defined as

\Upsilon_{\Delta}^{h}(q,x)=N_{\Delta}K_{\Delta}^{h,\Delta}\,q^{\Delta}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{D}}{\rm d}^{D}x_{0}V_{\tilde{\Delta},h,\Delta}(x_{0},x,qx_{0})\,.

(3.15)

When deriving this formula we used the relation between the structure constants (
3.9
). Substituting the 3-point function (
3.5
) into (
3.15
) one obtains

\Upsilon_{\Delta}^{h}(q,x)=N_{\Delta}\,K_{\Delta}^{h,\Delta}\,\frac{q^{D-h-\Delta}}{(1-q)^{D-h}}\;T_{2}^{a_{1},a_{2};a_{0}}(x_{1},x_{2})\,,

(3.16)

where
x_{1}=x/q
,
x_{2}=x
, the parameters
a_{i}
are expressed in terms of conformal dimensions:

a_{1}=\frac{h+2\Delta-D}{2}\,,\quad a_{2}=\frac{D+h-2\Delta}{2}\,,\quad a_{0}=\frac{D-h}{2}\,.

(3.17)

Here, we defined a
thermal conformal integral
:

T_{2}^{a_{1},a_{2};a_{0}}(x_{1},x_{2})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{D}}{\rm d}^{D}x_{0}X_{01}^{-a_{1}}\,X_{02}^{-a_{2}}\,(x_{0}^{2})^{-a_{0}}\,,\qquad a_{1}+a_{2}+2a_{0}=D\,.

(3.18)

The constraint on parameters
a_{i}
is satisfied due to (
3.17
).

One-loop Feynman integral.

Following

4
Conclusion

In this paper we have elaborated on the shadow formalism in thermal CFT
D
on
\mathbb{S}_{\beta}^{1}\times\mathbb{S}^{D-1}
. By way of illustration, we have explicitly calculated the 1-point thermal conformal block for scalar operators which proved to be the fourth Appell function.

The new ingredient introduced in this paper is the thermal conformal integral which defines the corresponding thermal conformal partial wave. It differs from the conventional conformal integrals by having a different constraint on parameters. It is known that, being a linear combination of the conformal and shadow blocks, the 4-point conformal partial wave is proportional to the 4-point conformal integral (
3.26
)
[
26
,
7
]
. On the other hand, by means of the limit relation (
3.32
) the same conformal integral defines the sum of thermal and shadow conformal blocks. Thus, we conclude that the 4-point conformal integral may be considered as the basic object which particular limits produces either 4-point conformal blocks on
\mathbb{R}^{D}
or 1-point thermal blocks on
\mathbb{S}^{1}_{\beta}\times\mathbb{S}^{D-1}
.
14
14

14

It is worth noting that conformal integrals in the context of thermal CFT
D
were recently considered in Ref.
[
23
]
, where they were shown to define 1-point thermal correlation functions of certain operators on
S^{1}_{\beta}\times R^{D-1}
(see also related works
[
38
,
39
]
). The conformal integrals were also proved to be Yangian invariant
[
40
,
41
]
.

It would be important to understand which
o(D+1,1)
Casimir equation is satisfied by the thermal 1-point block. In two dimensions this problem was solved for torus blocks in any channel
[
42
,
19
,
34
]
. In
D
dimensions the (plane) conformal partial waves obey the Casimir equations by construction
[
26
]
. It would be interesting to check this property for thermal partial waves introduced in this paper. On the other hand, the Casimir equation for correlation functions with more than one chemical potentials was proposed in Ref.
[
13
]
. Nonetheless, the Casimir equation for the 1-point thermal correlation function (
2.1
) is still unknown.

Finally, let us note that the present thermal shadow formalism can be naturally generalized to include spinning operators as well as to calculate
n
-point thermal scalar blocks
[
43
]
. Both of these directions are potentially important for understanding conformal bootstrap at finite temperature.

Applying this regularization inside (

(A.5)

\operatorname{Li_{2}}z+\operatorname{Li_{2}}(1-z)=\pi^{2}/6-\ln z\ln(1-z)

I_{4}^{1,1,1,1}({\bm{x}})=\frac{\pi^{2}}{X_{13}X_{24}}\frac{\Phi(z,y)}{\lambda(z,y)}\,,

Let us now consider a subspace in the

Odd edge-colorings of subdivisions of odd graphs
Mirko Petruševski, Riste Škrekovski

Abstract
An odd graph is a finite graph all of whose vertices have odd degrees. A graph G G is decomposable into k k odd subgraphs if its edge set can be partitioned into k k subsets each of which induces an odd subgraph of G G . The minimum value of k k for which such a decomposition of G G exists is the odd chromatic index, χ o ′ ​ ( G ) \chi_{o}^{\prime}(G) , introduced by Pyber (1991). For every k ≥ χ o ′ ​ ( G ) k\geq\chi_{o}^{\prime}(G) , the graph G G is said to be odd k k -edge-colorable. Apart from two particular exceptions, which are respectively odd 5 5 - and odd 6 6 -edge-colorable, the rest of connected loopless graphs are odd 4 4 -edge-colorable, and moreover one of the color classes can be reduced to size ≤ 2 \leq 2 . In addition, it has been conjectured that an odd 4 4 -edge-coloring with a color class of size at most 1 1 is always achievable. Atanasov et al. (2016) characterized the class of loopless subcubic graphs in terms of the value χ o ′ ​ ( G ) ≤ 4 \chi_{o}^{\prime}(G)\leq 4 . In this paper, we extend their result to a characterization of all loopless subdivisions of odd graphs in terms of the value of the odd chromatic index. This larger class 𝒮 \mathcal{S} is of a particular interest as it collects all ‘least instances’ of non-odd graphs. As a prelude to our main result, we show that every connected graph G ∈ 𝒮 G\in\mathcal{S} requiring the maximum number of four colors, becomes odd 3 3 -edge-colorable after removing a certain edge. Thus, we provide support for the mentioned conjecture by proving it for all subdivisions of odd graphs. The paper concludes with few problems for possible further work.



Keywords:
odd graph, odd edge-coloring, odd chromatic index, subdivision.

An assignment

An

induces an odd subgraph of

It is obvious that a necessary and sufficient condition for odd edge-colorability of

is the absence of vertices incident only to loops. Apart from this, the presence of loops
does not influence the existence nor changes the value of the index

2&\text{\hskip 10.22217ptif \,}G\text{ has 2-vertices, with an even number of them on each cycle}\,;\\
4&\text{\hskip 10.22217ptif \,}G\text{ is obtainable from a cubic bipartite graph by a single edge subdivision}\,;\\

The

For a graph

)

If

-edge-coloring of

extends to an odd

-edge-coloring of

if and only if the coloring is dichromatic at each

and monochromatic at each

.

So, if condition

fails to hold, then

. Indeed, simply select a vertex

, take a

-edge-coloring of

-edge-coloring of

.

On the other hand, assuming

-edge-colorability of

is equivalent to the existence of a

-edge-coloring of

that is dichromatic precisely at each vertex of

. The latter is clearly equivalent to the requirement that the set

∎

Corollary 2.5
.

Let

be the (unique) cycle and let

unless

3
Subdivisions of odd graphs

Recall that
\mathcal{S}
denotes the class of all loopless subdivisions of odd graphs. The following proposition is an overture to our subsequent study of
\mathcal{S}
in terms of the value of the odd chromatic index. The final product of the study, our main result, shall be formulated by the end of Section 4. As a warm-up, we commence by showing that four colors always suffice for an odd edge-coloring of any member of

. Moreover, the fourth color can be reduced to at most one appearance per component.

Proposition 3.1

Let

(ii)

No

Proof.

If there exists a cutvertex

in

, then

must be incident with two bridges. Consequently, Proposition

yields odd

-edge-colorability of

. Assuming

, the vertex

is internal. Therefore, the graph

to the edge

. This proves the first part and, in addition, confirms that

As for

, still assuming

, suppose there is an edge

whose endvertices

and

are

-vertices. Let

and

be the other edges (besides

and

are pendant edges in the (connected) graph

guarantees that there is an odd edge-coloring

Note that the first part of Proposition

. For our intended characterization of all members of the class

in terms of their odd chromatic index, let us denote by

Let

Our next result concerns odd

Proposition 3.3

The following statements are equivalent for every graph

(ii)

We may assume that

to

. Consider an edge

and

are its endvertices. Denote by

the (not necessarily distinct) edges of the

in

is good and, in the affirmative, such a coloring can be found in polynomial time.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let

if and only if

In the remainder of the paper we provide a structural characterization of the class

The multiplicity

Finally, we arrive at the main result, which succinctly summarizes our findings.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
G\in\mathcal{S}
be a connected graph. If
(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{V})
is the bipartition of the block-tree
B(G)
of

\mathcal{B}
is the set of blocks and
\mathcal{V}
the set of cutvertices of
G
, the following holds:

\chi^{\prime}_{o}(G)=\begin{cases}1&\text{\hskip 10.22217ptif \,}G\text{ is odd}\,;\\
2&\text{\hskip 10.22217ptif \,}G\text{ has 2-vertices, with an even number of them on each cycle}\,;\\
4&\text{\hskip 10.22217ptif \,}\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathcal{F}\text{ and for every }v\in\mathcal{V}\text{ there is a unique }B\in\mathcal{B}\text{ with odd }d_{B}(v)\,;\\
3&\text{\hskip 10.22217ptotherwise}\,.\end{cases}

Proof.

Straightforward from Corollary
3.4

3.5
and Theorem
4.1

5
Further work

It is implied by Theorem
4.3
that the problem of determining the odd chromatic index of a subdivision of an odd graph is solvable in polynomial time. In view of Theorems
1.3
and
1.5
, the complexity questions concerning the value of the odd chromatic index of general graphs amount to deciding on their odd
3
-edge-colorability. As already mentioned in Section 1.2, this question is still open.
Let us propose the study of a related set of questions arising from the following line of reasoning. The class
\mathcal{S}
can be captured by using the notion of
maximum even degree
, defined as follows. Let
\Delta_{\mathrm{even}}(G)
denote the maximum even value among the vertex degrees of
G
. Thus
\mathcal{S}=\{G:G\text{ is a loopless graph with }\Delta_{\mathrm{even}}(G)\leq 2\}
, and Theorem
4.3
tells that the problem of determining
\chi^{\prime}_{o}(G)
whenever
\Delta_{\mathrm{even}}(G)\leq 2
can be efficiently solved. For every
k=0,1,2,\ldots
, let
\mathcal{S}^{(2k)}=\{G:G\text{ is a loopless graph with }\Delta_{\mathrm{even}}(G)\leq 2k\}
. So, by ignoring isolated vertices,
\mathcal{S}^{(0)}=\mathcal{O}
; and obviously
\mathcal{S}^{(2)}=\mathcal{S}
. We find the next question interesting.

Question 5.1
.

Is the decision problem whether a graph
G\in\mathcal{S}^{(4)}
has
\chi^{\prime}_{o}(G)\leq 3
solvable in polynomial time?

A positive answer to Question
5.1
would open
the door for considering the following more general problem.

Question 5.2
.

Given a positive integer
k
, is the decision problem whether a graph
G\in\mathcal{S}^{(2k)}
has
\chi^{\prime}_{o}(G)\leq 3
solvable in polynomial time?

A Shannon triangle of type
(2,2,2)
that requires three colors for an
\mathcal{S}
-edge-coloring. The edges falling in distinct color classes of an optimal coloring are respectively depicted as dashed, normal and heavier.

Another possible field of study is to consider
\mathcal{S}^{(2k)}
-edge-colorability of graphs for a fixed positive integer
k
(instead of odd edge-colorability); that is, define a new type of edge-coloring by requiring that each color class is a member of
\mathcal{S}^{(2k)}
(rather than of
\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{S}^{(0)}
). Say the corresponding index (representing the minimum sufficient number of colors) is
\chi^{\prime}_{\mathcal{S}^{(2k)}}(G)
. For example, it is readily observed that
\chi^{\prime}_{\mathcal{S}}(W_{4})=2
as opposed to
\chi^{\prime}_{o}(W_{4})=4
. Similarly, if
G
is a Shannon triangle of type
(2,2,2)
, then
\chi^{\prime}_{\mathcal{S}}(G)\leq 3
in contrast to
\chi^{\prime}_{o}(G)=6
(cf. Figure
17
).
Note that there are graphs requiring at least four colors for an
\mathcal{S}
-edge-coloring. Namely, every Shannon triangle
G
of type
(2,2,1)
and multiplicity
\mu(G)\geq 3
has
\chi^{\prime}_{\mathcal{S}}(G)=4
(cf. Figure
18
).

-edge-coloring. Its bouquets are of size

and

We are tempted to end our discussion here with the following.

Conjecture 5.3
.

If
G
is a connected loopless graph that is not a Shannon triangle of type
(2,2,1)
and multiplicity
\mu(G)\geq 3
, then
\chi^{\prime}_{\mathcal{S}}(G)\leq 3
.

One wonders whether the bound
3
in Conjecture
5.3
may drop to
2
if
\mathcal{S}
is replaced with a certain
\mathcal{S}^{(2k)}
of sufficiently large
k
. Understandably, the list of excluded graphs might become longer.

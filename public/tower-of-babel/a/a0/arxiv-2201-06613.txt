Proposition 2.4
.

Let

and assume that

has generic boundaries with

and

.

Proof.

We assume
P_{m}
is not a monomial (thus
F_{d}
is not a monomial) since the statement is trivial otherwise.
Let

\displaystyle\widetilde{F}=F_{d}+F_{d-1}t+F_{d-2}t^{2}+\cdots\in\mathcal{R}[[t]],

\displaystyle Q=P_{m}+P_{m-1}t+\cdots+P_{m-h}t^{h}\in\mathcal{R}[[t]],

\displaystyle\text{and }T=\widetilde{F}-Q^{2}.

For each positive integer
z
, let
\mathcal{O}(t^{z})
denote an element of the form
\sum_{i\geq z}f_{i}t^{i}
in
\mathcal{R}[[t]]
, where each
f_{i}
is a polynomial in
\mathcal{R}
which we do not have to care about. Since
R_{d-\ell}=0
for
\ell<h
, we get

We will apply Theorem
2.2
to the case of
\mu=h(k+1)
. For that purpose, we need to check that
h(k+1)\leq d+e-u-v-1
. Observe:

\displaystyle d+e-u-v-1-h(k+1)

\displaystyle\geq 2m+(2k+1)m-u-v-1-(2m-1)(k+1)

\displaystyle=m-u-v+k\geq m-u-v+1=m+1-(w\text{-deg}(xy))\geq 0,

where the last inequality holds because
m-(w\text{-deg}(xy))\geq 0
if the lattice point
(1,1)
is contained in
N(P)
, or
w\text{-deg}(xy)=m+1
otherwise. Indeed, if
(1,1)
is not contained in
N(P)
, then
N(P)
is either the triangle with vertices
(0,0),(c,0),(0,1)
or the triangle with vertices
(0,0),(1,0),(0,c)
for some

. Without loss of generality we assume the former. Since
P_{m}
is not a monomial, both the points
(c,0)
and
(0,1)
must lie in the support of
P_{m}
. So
w=(1,c)
,

, and thus

.

Since
(F,G)
has generic boundaries, Theorem
2.2
gives the following:

\displaystyle G_{e-h(k+1)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=0}^{\lfloor h(k+1)/m\rfloor}c_{rm}\bigg[\widetilde{F}^{k+1/2-r/2}\bigg]_{t^{h(k+1)-rm}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=0}^{\lfloor h(k+1)/m\rfloor}c_{rm}\bigg[(Q^{2}+T)^{k+1/2-r/2}\bigg]_{t^{h(k+1)-rm}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=0}^{\lfloor h(k+1)/m\rfloor}c_{rm}\bigg[\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\binom{k+1/2-r/2}{i}Q^{2k+1-r-2i}T^{i}\bigg]_{t^{h(k+1)-rm}}.

Note that

, which implies

. Then it is enough to look at

and
i
such that

hi\leq h(k+1)-rm
, or equivalently
i\leq k+1-rm/h
.
Then the exponent of
Q
satisfies

2k+1-r-2i\geq 2k+1-r-2(k+1-rm/h)=2rm/h-r-1\geq 2rm/d-r-1=r-r-1=-1.

Here the first “

, and the second “
\geq
” becomes “
=
” only when

. Since the exponent of

is an integer, it is always nonnegative except when “

and

” (in which case the exponent is

). Since

and

, the following must also be a polynomial in

\displaystyle c_{0}\binom{k+1/2}{k+1}[Q^{-1}T^{k+1}]_{t^{h(k+1)}}

\displaystyle=c_{0}\binom{k+1/2}{k+1}\bigg[(P_{m}+\mathcal{O}(t))^{-1}\Big(R_{d-h}t^{h}+\mathcal{O}(t^{h+1})\Big)^{k+1}\bigg]_{t^{h(k+1)}}

Since

is a polynomial. Since

has generic boundaries, the polynomial

is not divisible by the square of any polynomial in

is algebraically closed.
∎

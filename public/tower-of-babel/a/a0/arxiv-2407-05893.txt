A general framework for inexact splitting algorithms with relative errors and applications to Chambolle-Pock and Davis-Yin methods
M. Marques Alves, Dirk A. Lorenz, Emanuele Naldi ∗

Abstract
In this work we apply the recently introduced framework of degenerate preconditioned proximal point algorithms to the hybrid proximal extragradient (HPE) method for maximal monotone inclusions. The latter is a method that allows inexact proximal (or resolvent) steps where the error is controlled by a relative-error criterion. Recently the HPE framework has been extended to the Douglas-Rachford method by Eckstein and Yao. In this paper we further extend the applicability of the HPE framework to splitting methods. To this end we use the framework of degenerate preconditioners that allows to write a large class of splitting methods as preconditioned proximal point algorithms. In this way, we modify many splitting methods such that one or more of the resolvents can be computed inexactly with an error that is controlled by an adaptive criterion. Further, we illustrate the algorithmic framework in the case of Chambolle-Pock’s primal dual hybrid gradient method and the Davis-Yin’s forward Douglas-Rachford method. In both cases, the inexact computation of the resolvent shows clear advantages in computing time and accuracy.

1
Introduction

In this paper we consider maximal monotone inclusions in a real Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
, i.e. inclusions of the type

\displaystyle 0\in\mathcal{A}u

(1)

where
\mathcal{A}:{\mathcal{H}}\to 2^{{\mathcal{H}}}
is a maximal monotone operator.
Our goal is to find solutions to these inclusions, i.e. zeros of the operator
\mathcal{A}
. These problems have a long history and many other problems are covered by this general formulation, e.g. convex optimization problems, convex-concave saddle-point problems
and variational inequalities (see, e.g,
Bauschke and Combettes (2017)
), but also split inclusions of the
type
0\in(A_{1}+\cdots+A_{N})x
, involving many maximal monotone operators, can be rewritten as an inclusion with a single operator
\mathcal{A}
in a larger space, see
Bredies et al. (2022)
.

A famous meta algorithm for monotone inclusions is the proximal point method
Rockafellar (1976)

that iterates the resolvent
J_{\mathcal{A}}=(I+\mathcal{A})^{-1}
, i.e. it computes the sequence defined by
u^{k+1}=J_{\mathcal{A}}u^{k}
, for an initialization
u^{0}
.
Resolvents are not always simple to compute, and if this is the case, the hybrid proximal extragradient (HPE) method
Solodov and Svaiter (1999)
provides a flexible framework to compute inexact resolvents with a relative error-criterion. The goal of the paper is to extend the HPE framework to the so-called preconditioned proximal point method with degenerate preconditoner as it has been proposed in
Bredies et al. (2022)
. It has been shown in
Bredies et al. (2022)
that this framework allows a concise treatment of many existing splitting methods and we will extend the HPE framework to that case.

We will show that this allows us to develop splitting methods with inexact evaluation of resolvents where the errors are controlled by a relative-error criterion. Applying the method to the case of the Douglas-Rachford splitting operator we will obtain exactly the method recently proposed by Eckstein and Yao in
Eckstein and Yao (2018)
. To show the flexibility of the method we also derive inexact versions of the Chambolle-Pock method
Chambolle and Pock (2011)
and the Davis-Yin (forward Douglas-Rachford) method
Davis and Yin (2017)
.

More precisely, as an application of our general degenerate preconditioned HPE framework for solving (
1
), we consider the more structured inclusions

\displaystyle 0\in A_{1}x+A_{2}x,

(2)

on a real Hilbert space
H
, a version of (
2
) allowing compositions with linear operators

\displaystyle 0\in A_{1}x+K^{*}A_{2}Kx,

(3)

as well as a three-operator case involving an additional cocoercive operator
B\colon H\to H
, namely

\displaystyle 0\in A_{1}x+A_{2}x+Bx,

(4)

where in all instances
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
denote set-valued maximal monotone operators,
K
is a bounded linear operator and

B
is a point-to-point cocoercive operator; more details below in Subsection
1.3
.
For instance, problem (
3
) can be written in the format of (
1
) using
u=(x,y)\in H^{2}={\mathcal{H}}

and
\mathcal{A}:{\mathcal{H}}\to 2^{\mathcal{H}}
defined by
\mathcal{A}u=\left(A_{1}x+K^{*}y,-Kx+A_{2}^{-1}y\right)
or, more shortly,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}:=\begin{bmatrix}\phantom{-}A_{1}&K^{*}\\
-K&A_{2}^{-1}\end{bmatrix}.

(5)

Using a similar reasoning, one can also recast (
4
) into the general framework of monotone inclusions for a single maximal monotone operator (see
Bredies et al. (2022)
).

More important than the fact that (
2
) – (
4
) are special instances of (
1
) is the fact that we are able to explicitly define (degenerate) preconditioners allowing us to design and analyze practical (relative-error) inexact operator splitting algorithms for solving the above structured inclusions. As we mentioned before, we will propose and study the asymptotic convergence of a degenerate preconditioned HPE method, combining ideas from the HPE theory and the more recent degenerate preconditioned proximal point algorithm
Bredies et al. (2022)
.

The hybrid proximal extragradient (HPE) method.

In the seminal paper
Rockafellar (1976)
, Rockafellar showed that
if at the current iterate
u^{k}
the next one, namely
u^{k+1}
, is computed satisfying the
summability
condition

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\,\left\|u^{k+1}-(I+\lambda_{k}\mathcal{A})^{-1}u^{k}\right\|<\infty

(6)

and
\left\{\lambda_{k}\right\}
is bounded away from zero, then the sequence of approximations
\left\{u^{k}\right\}
converges (weakly)
to a solution of (
1
) (assuming that there exists at least one).
As an alternative to (
6
), some modern inexact versions of the proximal point algorithm employ
relative-error tolerances
for solving subproblems, namely to compute

u^{k+1}\approx(I+\lambda_{k}\mathcal{A})^{-1}u^{k}
.
The first methods of this type were proposed by Solodov and Svaiter in
Solodov and Svaiter (1999)
;
Solodov and Svaiter (2001)
;
Solodov and Svaiter (1999)
and subsequently studied, e.g., in
Alves et al. (2016)
;
Monteiro and Svaiter (2010)
;
Monteiro and Svaiter (2012)
;
Monteiro and Svaiter (2013)
.
The main idea consists in decoupling the exact proximal point iteration

u^{k+1}=(I+\lambda_{k}\mathcal{A})^{-1}u^{k}
as

\displaystyle v^{k+1}\in\mathcal{A}u^{k+1},\quad\lambda_{k}v^{k+1}+u^{k+1}-u^{k}=0,

(7)

and then relaxing (
7
)
within relative-error tolerance criteria.
The hybrid proximal extragradient (HPE) method
Solodov and Svaiter (1999)
has been shown to be
very effective as a framework for the design and analysis
of many concrete algorithms and can be described (in its simplest form, without
\varepsilon
-enlargements) as follows:
for all
k\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}v^{k+1}\in\mathcal{A}{\tilde{u}}^{k+1},\quad\|{\lambda_{k}v^{k+1}+{\tilde{u}}^{k+1}-u^{k}}\|\leq\sigma\|{{\tilde{u}}^{k+1}-u^{k}}\|,\\[8.53581pt]
u^{k+1}=u^{k}-\lambda_{k}v^{k+1},\\
\end{array}\right.

where
\sigma\in[0,1)
.
If
\sigma=0
, then it is easy to see that (
1
) reduces to the exact proximal point method
(
7
).
We also mention that the update rule for
u^{k+1}
as in (
1
),
namely,
u^{k+1}=u^{k}-\lambda_{k}v^{k+1}
, is exactly what we mean by an
extragradient step
(this goes back to the seminal work of
Korpolevich
Korpelevič (1976)
, see also
Nemirovski (2005)
).

As it was already mentioned, the main contribution of this paper is to propose and study a
degenerate preconditioned version of the HPE method (
1
).

The degenerate preconditioned proximal point method.

In
Bredies et al. (2022)
, the authors introduced a generalization of the proximal point method for solving (
1
) as follows:
for all
k\geq 0
,

\displaystyle u^{k+1}=(\mathcal{M}+\mathcal{A})^{-1}\mathcal{M}u^{k},

(10)

where
\mathcal{M}
is a self-adjoint and positive
semidefinite
bounded linear operator in
{\mathcal{H}}
. By taking
\mathcal{M}=I
in (
10
) we recover the proximal point iteration
u^{k+1}=(I+\mathcal{A})^{-1}u^{k}

with
\lambda_{k}\equiv 1
.
In the case of positive definite
\mathcal{M}
, the iteration (
10
) can be written as

\displaystyle u^{k+1}=(I+\mathcal{M}^{-1}\mathcal{A})^{-1}u^{k}

and hence, is exactly a proximal point iteration in the space where the inner product is changed to the
\mathcal{M}
-inner product

\displaystyle\langle x,y\rangle_{\mathcal{M}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\langle x,\mathcal{M}y\rangle.

(11)

And since
\mathcal{M}^{-1}\mathcal{A}
is maximal monotone in the Hilbert space with the
\mathcal{M}
-inner product, convergence of the method follows from standard results on the proximal point method.
The main point of the contribution
Bredies et al. (2022)
was to include in their analysis the case in which
\mathcal{M}
may have a nontrivial kernel, in this way opening the possibility of designing new operator-splitting methods. The asymptotic analysis of
(
10
) was carried out by assuming that
(\mathcal{M}+\mathcal{A})^{-1}
is a (point-to-point) Lipschitz continuous operator in
{\mathcal{H}}
. This assumption is satisfied by interesting examples of
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{A}
in operator-splitting problems; for instance, for

\mathcal{A}
as in (
5
), considering

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}:=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{\theta}I&-K^{*}\\
-K&\frac{1}{\tau}I\end{bmatrix},

(12)

(with
0<\theta\tau\leq\|{K}\|^{-2}
), the generalized proximal point method (
10
) leads to the primal-dual hybrid gradient method by Chambolle-Pock
Chambolle and Pock (2011)
and the convergence follows (also in the edge case
\theta\tau=\|{K}\|^{-2}
) by the theory developed in
Bredies et al. (2022)
.

The reduced method.

The framework from
Bredies et al. (2022)
allows for degenerate preconditioners
\mathcal{M}
, i.e. the operator
\mathcal{M}
is only positive semidefinite and can have a non-trivial kernel. This gives more flexibility but also leads to so-called reduced methods which we illustrate here with an example.

In the case
K=I
and
\tau=\theta=1
in (
5
) and (
12
) the involved operators become

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}=\begin{bmatrix}A_{1}&I\\
-I&A_{2}^{-1}\end{bmatrix},\quad\mathcal{M}=\begin{bmatrix}I&-I\\
-I&I\end{bmatrix}.

Here, the preconditioner
\mathcal{M}
has a large kernel, namely the span of the vectors of the form
(w,\ w)
. As has been shown in
Bredies et al. (2022)
, this can be used to derive a “reduced” algorithm: Instead of iterating
u^{k}\in{\mathcal{H}}^{2}
one can use the decomposition
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}^{*}
with
\mathcal{C}:{\mathcal{H}}\to{\mathcal{H}}^{2}
,
\mathcal{C}w=(w,\ -w)
, introduce
w^{k}=\mathcal{C}^{*}u^{k}
for every
k
, and rewrite the iteration as

\displaystyle w^{k+1}=\mathcal{C}^{*}(\mathcal{M}+\mathcal{A})^{-1}\mathcal{C}w^{k}.

(13)

Moreover, from the weak limit
w^{*}
of this sequence one gets via
(\mathcal{M}+\mathcal{A})^{-1}\mathcal{C}w^{*}
a fixed point of
\mathcal{A}
(cf.
(
Bredies et al., 2022
, Theorem 2.14)
). In the above case this reduced algorithm is exactly the famous Douglas-Rachford iteration for the split inclusion
0\in A_{1}x+A_{2}x
(see
(
Bredies et al., 2022
, Section 3)
).

The main goal of the present work is to generalize (
10
) allowing errors in the computation of
(\mathcal{M}+\mathcal{A})^{-1}
within relative-error tolerances in the spirit of (
1
).
Similarly to (
7
), the iteration (
10
) can be decoupled as a inclusion/equation system leading to
the new error criterion

\mathcal{M}v^{k+1}\in\mathcal{A}{\tilde{u}}^{k+1},\quad\|{\lambda_{k}v^{k+1}+{\tilde{u}}^{k+1}-u^{k}}\|_{\mathcal{M}}\leq\sigma\|{{\tilde{u}}^{k+1}-u^{k}}\|_{\mathcal{M}},

where
\|{\cdot}\|_{\mathcal{M}}
denotes the seminorm induced by
\mathcal{M}
.

We apply our (relative-error) inexact degenerate preconditioned HPE framework for different instances of
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{M}
, obtaining in this way new flexible and efficient operator-splitting methods for (
2
), (
3
) and
(
4
) (more details below in
Subsection
1.2
).

1.1
Related work

Inexact steps for methods that solve monotone inclusions and which are based on resolvents have been investigated from different angles.
One line of work models additive errors in each step and derive conditions under which one still obtains convergence.
In
Combettes (2004)
, for example, it is shown that the proximal gradient method converges if one still allows for additive errors that are summable. A similar result for the Douglas-Rachford method can be found in
Svaiter (2011)
. In both cases, one has to drive the error down to zero in a pre-designed manner. Moreover, this condition is hard to check in practice (one would need a way to calculate or estimate the error to the exact step). Another line of work uses relative error conditions and this approach dates back to
Solodov and Svaiter (1999)
for the proximal point method. In these approaches the error condition only need quantities that can be computed from the iterates. Only recently, this approach has been extended to splitting methods, more specifically to the Douglas-Rachford method (and hence also to the alternating directions method of multipliers (ADMM)) in
Alves et al. (2020)
;
Eckstein and Yao (2018)
.

Many splitting methods can be written in the framework of the proximal point method. For the Douglas-Rachford method this has been observed in
Eckstein and Bertsekas (1992)
. In
Bredies and
Sun (2015)
it has been observed that a specific reformulation of the split inclusion
0\in(A_{1}+A_{2})x
in a product space and the use of a
degenerate
preconditioner for the proximal point method also leads to the Douglas-Rachford method (see also
Bredies et al. (2022)
). The paper
Bredies et al. (2022)
also showed that other splitting methods, involving any number of monotone operators, can be derived in this framework. In
Bredies et al. (2022)
it has then been shown that one can design splitting methods for any “communication structure” between the operators, i.e. one can prescribe in which order the resolvents of the operators are evaluated and how the results are passed among the operators. The communication structure can be encoded by a communication graph which is then used to design the respective algorithm. The methods introduced in
Bredies et al. (2022)
extended existing splitting algorithms such as the one introduced by Ryu in
Ryu (2020)
, the parallel Douglas-Rachford introduced and analyzed in
Ryu and Yin (2019)
;
Condat et al. (2023)
;
Campoy (2022)
and the method introduced by Malitsky and Tam in
Malitsky and
Tam (2023)
. In
Bredies et al. (2022)
the authors also showed that all these algorithms (and more in general all the graph-based ones) are a particular case of degenerate preconditioned proximal point. In the present work, we introduce relative errors criteria for the degenerate preconditioned proximal point algorithm and thus, all the methods mentioned above can benefit from our analysis. The connection established in this work between the HPE framework and the degenerate preconditioning setting lays also the basis for future exploration of (degenerate) variable metric analysis
Dirk A. Lorenz and Naldi (2024)
, potentially enabling the development of more adaptive and efficient splitting methods.

1.2
Contribution

In this paper we make the following contributions:

•

We extend the hybrid proximal extragradient method to proximal point methods with degenerate preconditioners and show weak convergence of the iterates.

•

We show how this approach can be applied to splitting methods, namely the Douglas-Rachford method (recovering the results by Eckstein and Yao from
Eckstein and Yao (2018)
), the Chambolle-Pock method
Chambolle and Pock (2011)
and the Davis-Yin method
Davis and Yin (2017)
, thereby developing inexact versions of the algorithms with relative error conditions.

•

We illustrate in numerical examples that the proposed methods are effective and efficient when applied to practical problems.

2
The hybrid proximal extragradient method with degenerate preconditioning

In this section we introduce and study the hybrid proximal extragradient method with a degenerate preconditioner. We consider a maximal monotone operator
\mathcal{A}
defined on a real Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
and a linear operator
\mathcal{M}
which is
admissible
in the sense of the following definition.

Definition 2.1

(
Bredies et al. (2022)
)
.

An admissible preconditioner for the multivalued operator
\mathcal{A}:{\mathcal{H}}\to 2^{{\mathcal{H}}}
is a linear, bounded, self-adjoint and positive semidefinite operator

\mathcal{M}:{\mathcal{H}}\to{\mathcal{H}}
such that

(\mathcal{M}+\mathcal{A})^{-1}\mathcal{M}\quad\mbox{is single-valued and has full domain}.

As noted in
Bredies et al. (2022)
, this condition on the preconditioner
\mathcal{M}
is quite mild, in particular for applications to splitting methods where it is always satisfied (see also
Naldi (2024)
for further details).

For a given initial value
u^{0}
, a constant
\sigma\in[0,1)
and stepsizes
\lambda_{k}>0
we consider sequences
\left\{u^{k}\right\},\left\{{\tilde{u}}^{k}\right\},\left\{v^{k}\right\}
that fulfill

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}v^{k+1}

\displaystyle\in\mathcal{A}{\tilde{u}}^{k+1},

(15)

\displaystyle\|{\lambda_{k+1}v^{k+1}+{\tilde{u}}^{k+1}-u^{k}}\|_{\mathcal{M}}

\displaystyle\leq\sigma\|{{\tilde{u}}^{k+1}-u^{k}}\|_{\mathcal{M}},

(16)

\displaystyle u^{k+1}

\displaystyle=u^{k}-\lambda_{k+1}v^{k+1}.

(17)

Before we analyze these sequences, let us provide some intuition about the iteration.

Remark 2.2
.

In the case
\sigma=0
, the inequality (
16
) says that

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(\lambda_{k+1}v^{k+1}+{\tilde{u}}^{k+1}-u^{k})=0

which, together with the inclusion (
15
), shows that

\displaystyle{\tilde{u}}^{k+1}\in(\mathcal{M}+\lambda_{k+1}\mathcal{A})^{-1}\mathcal{M}u^{k}.

Moreover, we get that the update (
17
) is (after application of
\mathcal{M}
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}u^{k+1}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{M}u^{k}-\lambda_{k+1}\mathcal{M}v^{k+1}=\mathcal{M}{\tilde{u}}^{k+1}.

This shows that the sequence

\left\{u^{k}\right\}
and
\left\{{\tilde{u}}^{k}\right\}
only differ in the kernel of
\mathcal{M}
and convergence of
\left\{u^{k}\right\}
or
\left\{{\tilde{u}}^{k}\right\}
may not be true in the general case. However, we will show below that the sequence
\left\{{\tilde{u}}^{k}\right\}
is indeed bounded and weakly converges to a zero of
\mathcal{A}
.

The next two proposition provide some foundamental properties of the sequences generated by (
15
)- (
17
).

Proposition 2.3

(Fundamental estimates)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be maximal monotone on
{\mathcal{H}}
,
u^{*}
a zero of
\mathcal{A}
,
\mathcal{M}
linear, bounded and positive semidefinite on
{\mathcal{H}}
and
u^{0}\in{\mathcal{H}}
.
Consider sequences
\left\{u^{k}\right\},\left\{{\tilde{u}}^{k}\right\},\left\{v^{k}\right\}
fulfilling (
15
)- (
17
) for
\lambda_{k}>0
and
\sigma\in[0,1)
. Then, for all
k\geq 0
,

i)

(1-\sigma)\|{{\tilde{u}}^{k+1}-u^{k}}\|_{\mathcal{M}}\leq\|{\lambda_{k+1}v^{k+1}}\|_{\mathcal{M}}\leq(1+\sigma)\|{{\tilde{u}}^{k+1}-u^{k}}\|_{\mathcal{M}}
,

ii)

\|{u^{k+1}-u^{*}}\|_{\mathcal{M}}^{2}+(1-\sigma^{2})\|{u^{k}-{\tilde{u}}^{k+1}}\|_{\mathcal{M}}^{2}\leq\|{u^{k}-u^{*}}\|_{\mathcal{M}}^{2}
,

iii)

\|{u^{k+1}-u^{*}}\|_{\mathcal{M}}^{2}+(1-\sigma^{2})\sum\limits_{i=0}^{k}\|{u^{i}-{\tilde{u}}^{i+1}}\|_{\mathcal{M}}^{2}\leq\|{u^{0}-u^{*}}\|_{\mathcal{M}}^{2}
.

Consequently we also have for
k\to\infty

iv)

\|{u^{k}-{\tilde{u}}^{k+1}}\|_{\mathcal{M}}\to 0,\quad\|{\lambda_{k}v^{k}}\|_{\mathcal{M}}\to 0,\quad\|{u^{k}-{\tilde{u}}^{k}}\|_{\mathcal{M}}\to 0
.

Proof.

The error condition (
16
) and the triangle inequality for the
\mathcal{M}
-seminorm give

\displaystyle\|{\lambda_{k+1}v^{k+1}}\|_{\mathcal{M}}

\displaystyle\leq\|{\lambda_{k+1}v^{k+1}+{\tilde{u}}^{k+1}-u^{k}}\|_{\mathcal{M}}+\|{{\tilde{u}}^{k+1}-u^{k}}\|_{\mathcal{M}}

\displaystyle\leq(1+\sigma)\|{{\tilde{u}}^{k+1}-u^{k}}\|_{\mathcal{M}}

which shows the right inequality in the first claim. For the left inequality we consider

\displaystyle\|{{\tilde{u}}^{k+1}-u^{k}}\|_{\mathcal{M}}

\displaystyle\leq\|{\lambda_{k+1}v^{k+1}+{\tilde{u}}^{k+1}-u^{k}}\|_{\mathcal{M}}+\|{\lambda_{k+1}v^{k+1}}\|_{\mathcal{M}}

\displaystyle\leq\sigma\|{{\tilde{u}}^{k+1}-u^{k}}\|_{\mathcal{M}}+\|{\lambda_{k+1}v^{k+1}}\|_{\mathcal{M}}

which can be rearranged to the desired inequality.

For the second claim we use the identity
\|{a}\|_{\mathcal{M}}^{2}-\|{b}\|_{\mathcal{M}}^{2}=\|{a-b}\|_{\mathcal{M}}^{2}+2\langle a-b,b\rangle_{\mathcal{M}}
with

a=u^{k}-u^{*}
and
b=u^{k+1}-u^{*}
and get

\displaystyle\|{u^{k}-u^{*}}\|_{\mathcal{M}}^{2}-\|{u^{k+1}-u^{*}}\|_{\mathcal{M}}^{2}=\|{u^{k}-u^{k+1}}\|_{\mathcal{M}}^{2}+2\langle u^{k}-u^{k+1},u^{k+1}-u^{*}\rangle_{\mathcal{M}}.

By repeating the same argument with
a=u^{k}-{\tilde{u}}^{k+1}
and
b=u^{k+1}-{\tilde{u}}^{k+1}
we also find

\displaystyle\|{u^{k}-{\tilde{u}}^{k+1}}\|_{\mathcal{M}}^{2}-\|{u^{k+1}-{\tilde{u}}^{k+1}}\|_{\mathcal{M}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|{u^{k}-u^{k+1}}\|_{\mathcal{M}}^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+2\langle u^{k}-u^{k+1},u^{k+1}-{\tilde{u}}^{k+1}\rangle_{\mathcal{M}}.

Now by subtracting the latter from the former equality and using the update (
17
) we obtain

\displaystyle\|{u^{k}-u^{*}}\|_{\mathcal{M}}^{2}-\|{u^{k+1}-u^{*}}\|_{\mathcal{M}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|{u^{k}-{\tilde{u}}^{k+1}}\|_{\mathcal{M}}^{2}-\|{u^{k+1}-{\tilde{u}}^{k+1}}\|_{\mathcal{M}}^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt+2\langle u^{k}-u^{k+1},{\tilde{u}}^{k+1}-u^{*}\rangle_{\mathcal{M}}

\displaystyle=\|{u^{k}-{\tilde{u}}^{k+1}}\|_{\mathcal{M}}^{2}-\|{u^{k+1}-{\tilde{u}}^{k+1}}\|_{\mathcal{M}}^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt+2\lambda_{k+1}\langle v^{k+1},{\tilde{u}}^{k+1}-u^{*}\rangle_{\mathcal{M}}.

Since
\mathcal{M}v^{k+1}\in\mathcal{A}{\tilde{u}}^{k+1}
and

0\in\mathcal{A}u^{*}
one can use the monotonicity of
\mathcal{A}
and the definition of

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{M}}
to conclude that

\displaystyle\langle v^{k+1},{\tilde{u}}^{k+1}-u^{*}\rangle_{\mathcal{M}}=\langle\mathcal{M}v^{k+1},{\tilde{u}}^{k+1}-u^{*}\rangle\geq 0.

Thus, we get from the update step (
17
) and the error condition (
16
)

\displaystyle\|{u^{k}-u^{*}}\|_{\mathcal{M}}^{2}-\|{u^{k+1}-u^{*}}\|_{\mathcal{M}}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\|{u^{k}-{\tilde{u}}^{k+1}}\|_{\mathcal{M}}^{2}-\|{u^{k+1}-{\tilde{u}}^{k+1}}\|_{\mathcal{M}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|{u^{k}-{\tilde{u}}^{k+1}}\|_{\mathcal{M}}^{2}-\|{\lambda_{k+1}v^{k+1}+{\tilde{u}}^{k+1}-u^{k}}\|_{\mathcal{M}}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq(1-\sigma^{2})\|{u^{k}-{\tilde{u}}^{k+1}}\|_{\mathcal{M}}^{2},

which proves the desired inequality.

The third claim is a direct consequence of second by summing up and using a telescope sum.

The first two final convergence statements follow directly and for the last statement observing that by (
16
) and (
17
) we have

\displaystyle\|{{\tilde{u}}^{k+1}-u^{k+1}}\|_{\mathcal{M}}\leq\sigma\|{{\tilde{u}}^{k+1}-u^{k}}\|_{\mathcal{M}}

which gives the desired convergence.
∎

Lemma 2.5

(Opial property for seminorms)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}:{\mathcal{H}}\to{\mathcal{H}}
be a linear bounded self-adjoint and positive semidefinite operator. Let
\left\{v^{k}\right\}
be a sequence weakly convergent to a point

, then

\liminf_{k\to\infty}\|v^{k}-v^{*}\|_{\mathcal{M}}^{2}=\liminf_{k\to\infty}\|v^{k}-v\|_{\mathcal{M}}^{2}-\|v^{*}-v\|_{\mathcal{M}}^{2}\quad\text{ for all }v\in{\mathcal{H}}.

In particular, for every
v\in{\mathcal{H}}\text{ with }\|v^{*}-v\|_{\mathcal{M}}>0
, it holds

\liminf_{k\to\infty}\|v^{k}-v^{*}\|_{\mathcal{M}}<\liminf_{k\to\infty}\|v^{k}-v\|_{\mathcal{M}}.

Proof.

We have

\|v^{k}-v\|_{\mathcal{M}}^{2}=\|v^{k}-v^{*}+v^{*}-v\|_{\mathcal{M}}^{2}=\|v^{k}-v^{*}\|_{\mathcal{M}}^{2}+\|v^{*}-v\|_{\mathcal{M}}^{2}+2\langle\mathcal{M}(v^{*}-v),v^{k}-v^{*}\rangle.

Since
v^{k}\rightharpoonup v^{*}
, we have and
\langle\mathcal{M}(v^{*}-v),v^{k}-v^{*}\rangle\to 0
for
k\to\infty
, and the claim follows.

Theorem 2.7

Remark 2.8
.

The theoretical guarantees provided in our work are in line with the related literature on proximal-point type methods as well as on degenerate preconditioned methods (see, e.g.,
Solodov and Svaiter (1999)
;
Solodov and Svaiter (2000)
;
Alves et al. (2020)
;
Eckstein and Yao (2018)
;
Eckstein and Silva (2013)
;
Bredies et al. (2022)
). Convergence rates for sequences generated by proximal point type methods can be proved, in general, under stronger assumptions on the maximal monotone operator as, for instance, strong monotonicity
Marques Alves and Svaiter (2016)
;
Alves et al. (2016)
. Convergence rates for the residuals (in terms of enlargements) were obtained in
Monteiro and Svaiter (2010)
. One could follow the latter work of Monteiro and Svaiter and try to prove convergence rates for the residuals of the outer iteration, but this is beyond the scope of the present paper. On the other hand, it is not clear how to prove rates when we consider outer and inner iterations, since we can’t predict how many inner iterations will be required to completed each inner loop. The situation was somehow improved for a relative-error version of the
Douglas-Rachford method in
Alves and Geremia (2019)
where a tolerance was prescribed for each inner loop (see equation (25) in Algorithm 3 of
Alves and Geremia (2019)
). Instead of following this idea and introducing a different method we wanted to keep the analysis and exposition as simple as possible.

3
Applications to splitting methods

In this section we consider the problem of finding a zero in the sum of maximal monotone operators. The aim of the section is to show that the degenerate HPE framework can be applied in the context of splitting methods, deriving new inexact schemes together with convergence results. We start by recovering the Eckstein-Yao method, which is an inexact version of the celebrated Douglas-Rachford method, continue with a new inexact version of the Chambolle-Pock method and conclude the section with a new inexact version of the Davis-Yin algorithm. We also comment about the fact that, thanks to the general framework we developed, many more methods can be included.

Remark 3.6

The Davis-Yin method comes as a generalization of the Douglas-Rachford algorithm that makes use of an additional forward term. Thus, the new Algorithm

We recall that the algorithm is well suited to solve problems of the form

Remark 3.8
.

As already mentioned in the introduction, the degenerate proximal point framework has been shown to include many existing methods. We presented in this section the inexact version of only a few of them. Other examples are: the splitting method introduced by Ryu in
Ryu (2020)
, the parallel Douglas-Rachford introduced and analyzed in
Ryu and Yin (2019)
;
Condat et al. (2023)
;
Campoy (2022)
, the method introduced by Malitsky and Tam in
Malitsky and
Tam (2023)
and the sequential method introduced in
Bredies et al. (2022)
. Through the framework introduced in the present paper, their inexact counterpart can be easily analyzed. We didn’t present here the inexact versions of such algorithms for sake of exposition, however, some generalizations are explicitly reported in
Naldi (2024)
. In a recent paper
Aragón-Artacho et al. (2024)
, Aragón-Artacho, Campoy and López-Pastor introduced new graph-based forward-backward methods which are connected to some graph-based extensions of the Davis-Yin method studied in
Naldi (2024)
. All of these methods are based on the degenerate proximal point framework proposed in
Bredies et al. (2022)
. For this reason, the present work also paves the way for possible new inexact generalizations of these algorithms.

4
Experiments

In this section we perform numerical experiments with the goal of illustrating the practical benefits of using approximate resolvents with relative errors. Even though the methodology we developed in this paper is applicable to a wide variety of splitting methods, we will focus our experiments on two cases: The Chambolle-Pock method as described in Section
3.2
and the Davis-yin method from Section
3.3
.

4.1
Chambolle-Pock

To illustrate the effectiveness of the HPE-version of the Chambolle-Pock method from Section
3.2
we consider the variational problem

\displaystyle\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\tfrac{1}{2}\|{Hx-f}\|_{2}^{2}+\lambda\|{Dx}\|_{1}

(36)

for
H\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
,
f\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
\lambda>0
and
D\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times n}
.
This problem is prototypical for several variational approaches to inverse problems and imaging, e.g. when
H
is a forward operator,
f
is given data and the inverse problem is to approximately solve
Hx=f
under the assumption that
f
is perturbed by noise and
H
is ill-conditioned. The parameter
\lambda
is the regularization parameter and the term
\|{Dx}\|_{1}
is a regularization term that enforces that
Dx
will be sparse. The famous (isotropic) total variation regularization is a special case of this when
D
is a matrix of first order finite differences
Rudin et al. (1992)
;
Bredies and
Lorenz (2018)
.

There are numerous ways to solve this problem with splitting methods. Especially the term
\tfrac{1}{2}\|{Hx-f}\|_{2}^{2}
can be treated in different ways:

•

Doing implicit steps, i.e. evaluating the respective resolvent of
\tfrac{1}{2}\|{Hf-f}\|_{2}^{2}
exactly. This amount to solving a linear system with the operator
I+\tau H^{T}H
. We will do this in what we call
Chambolle-Pock with implicit steps
or
implicit Chambolle-Pock
and explain the details below.

•

Doing approximate implicit steps by using the method of conjugate gradients (CG) for the solution of the linear systems within the HPE framework as described in Algorithm
3
.

•

Doing forward steps: Since the term is
L
-smooth, one can do forward steps of the form
H^{T}(Hx-f)
. This is, for example, possible within the Condat-Vu method
Condat (2013)
;
Vũ (2013)
.

•

One use a different splitting and also “dualize over
H
” (and
D
at the same time) and then apply the Chambolle-Pock method. This also avoids solutions of linear systems and we call this method
Chambolle-Pock with explicit steps
or
explicit Chambolle-Pock
and describe the details below.

4.1.1
Chambolle-Pock with implicit steps

We can write (
36
) in standard form

\displaystyle\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\tfrac{1}{2}\|{Hx-f}\|_{2}^{2}+\lambda\|{Dx}\|_{1}

\displaystyle=\min_{x}F(x)+G(Kx)

\displaystyle\text{with}\quad F(x)=\tfrac{1}{2}\|{Hx-f}\|_{2}^{2},\quad K

\displaystyle=D,\quad G(z)=\lambda\|{z}\|_{1}.

We put this in correspondence with (
27
) by setting
A_{1}x=H^{T}(Hx-f)
,
K=D
and
A_{2}x=\lambda\Sign(x)=\lambda\partial\|{\cdot}\|_{1}(x)
where
\Sign(x)
is the componentwise multivalued sign function.

The inverse of
A_{2}
is the multivalued map with the components

\displaystyle(A_{2}^{-1}x)_{i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l@{\quad:\quad}l}\emptyset&|x_{i}|>\lambda\\
]-\infty,0]&x_{i}=-\lambda\\
\left\{0\right\}&|x_{i}|<\lambda\\
{[0,\infty[}&x_{i}=\lambda\end{array}\right.

and its resolvent (i.e. the proximal map of
G^{*}(y)=I_{|\cdot|_{\infty}\leq\lambda}(y)
) is the clipping (and independent of
\theta>0
)

\displaystyle J_{\theta A_{2}^{-1}}(x)=\min(\max(x,-\lambda),\lambda)=:\operatorname{clip}_{[-\lambda,\lambda]}(x)

(all operations applied componentwise).
The resolvent of
A_{1}
(i.e. the proximal map of
F
) is

\displaystyle J_{\tau A_{1}}(x)=\prox_{\tau F}(x)=(I+\tau H^{T}H)^{-1}(x+\tau H^{T}f)

and hence, involves solving an
n\times n
linear system.
The full iteration is

\displaystyle\begin{split}x^{k+1}&=(I+\tau H^{T}H)^{-1}(x^{k}-\tau(D^{T}y^{k}-H^{T}f))\\
y^{k+1}&=\operatorname{clip}_{[-\lambda,\lambda]}(y^{k}+\theta D(2x^{k+1}-x^{k}))\end{split}

(37)

The method needs
\tau\theta\leq\|{D}\|^{-2}
and since we can estimate
\|{D}\|\leq 2
we take
\tau=\tfrac{1}{2\kappa}
,
\theta=\tfrac{\kappa}{2}
, with a scale factor
\kappa>0
.

4.1.2
HPE Chambolle-Pock

To avoid the exact solution of the linear system in the Chambolle-Pock method, we can apply the HPE framework from Section
3.2
, i.e. Algorithm
3
. Instead of evaluating the resolvent
J_{\tau A_{1}}
exactly
we apply the method of conjugate gradients to improve the pair
(\tilde{x}^{k+1},a^{k+1})
. Note that for a given candidate
\tilde{x}^{k+1}
there is a unique
a^{k+1}=H^{T}(H\tilde{x}^{k+1}-f)\in A_{1}\tilde{x}^{k+1}
.

For the HPE CP method we also need
\tau\theta\leq\|{D}\|^{-2}\leq\tfrac{1}{4}
and we take again
\tau=\tfrac{1}{2\kappa}
,
\theta=\tfrac{\kappa}{2}
, with a scale factor
\kappa>0
.

4.1.3
Condat-Vu

Another way to avoid the solution of the linear system is the Condat-Vu method. This method can be applied to problems of the form

\displaystyle\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}F(x)+\Phi(x)+G(Kx)

where
H
is an
L
-smooth convex function, and
F
and
G
are convex with simple proximal maps. We rewrite (
36
) in this form with

\displaystyle F(x)=0,\quad\Phi(x)=\tfrac{1}{2}\|{Hx-f}\|_{2}^{2},\quad G(z)=\lambda\|{z}\|_{1},\quad K=D.

The function
\Phi
is indeed
L
-smooth with
L=\|{H}\|^{2}
, and we can apply the Condat-Vu method
Condat (2013)
;
Vũ (2013)
which reads in this case as

\displaystyle\begin{split}x^{k+1}&=x^{k}-\tau(H^{T}(Hx^{k}-f)-D^{T}y^{k})\\
y^{k+1}&=\prox_{I_{\|{\cdot}\|_{\infty}\leq\lambda}}(y^{k}+\theta D(2x^{k+1}-x^{k}))\end{split}

(38)

and converges as soon as the stepsizes
\tau,\sigma
fulfill

\displaystyle 0<\tau<\tfrac{2}{\|{H}\|^{2}},\quad 0<\theta<\left(\tfrac{1}{\tau}-\tfrac{\|{H}\|^{2}}{2}\right)\tfrac{1}{\|{D}\|^{2}},

cf.
Condat (2013)
;
Vũ (2013)
.

4.1.4
Chambolle-Pock with explicit steps

Yet another way to avoid the solution of the linear system, we can also apply the plain Chambolle-Pock method after dualizing over both linear operators
H
and
D
, i.e. we rewrite (
36
) as

\displaystyle\min_{u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\tfrac{1}{2}\|{Hx-f}\|_{2}^{2}+\lambda\|{Dx}\|_{1}

\displaystyle=\min_{x}\tilde{F}(x)+\tilde{G}(\tilde{K}x)

\displaystyle\text{with}\quad\tilde{F}(x)=0,\quad\tilde{K}x

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}Hx\\
Dx\end{bmatrix},\quad\tilde{G}(a,b)=\tfrac{1}{2}\|{a-f}\|_{2}^{2}+\lambda\|{b}\|_{1}.

This leads to the method

\displaystyle\begin{split}x^{k+1}&=x^{k}-\tau(H^{T}u+D^{T}v)\\
u^{k+1}&=\tfrac{1}{1+\theta}(u^{k}+\theta(H(2x^{k+1}-x^{k}-f)\\
v^{k+1}&=\prox_{I_{\|{\cdot}\|_{\infty}\leq\lambda}}(v^{k}+\theta D(2x^{k+1}-x^{k}))\end{split}

(39)

and convergence is guaranteed if we have
\theta\tau\leq\|{K}\|^{2}
(and we estimate
\|{K}\|^{2}\leq\|{H}\|^{2}+\|{D}\|^{2}
). Here we take
\tau=\tfrac{1}{\kappa\|{K}\|}
,
\theta=\tfrac{\kappa}{\|{K}\|}
, for some
\kappa>0
.

4.2
Davis-Yin

We present here a numerical example to show the applicability of Algorithm
4
in practice. We consider
H\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
,
f\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}>0
and the problem

\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{2}\|Hx-f\|^{2}+\lambda_{1}\|x\|_{1}+\lambda_{2}L_{\delta}(Dx),

(40)

where
D\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times n}
is some dictionary matrix (we use again a finite difference matrix just as an example) and
L_{\delta}
is the Huber loss defined by
L_{\delta}(y)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}h_{\delta}(y_{i})
with

h_{\delta}(y_{i})=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}|y_{i}|^{2}&|y_{i}|\leq\delta\\
\delta(|y_{i}|-\frac{1}{2}\delta)&|y_{i}|>\delta\end{cases}

for some
\delta>0
. The Huber loss is usually adopted as a smooth approximation of the
1
-norm and in our case produce the term
L_{\delta}(Dx)
which is know as Huber-TV regularization
Werlberger
et al. (2009)
;
Hintermüller and
Wu (2013)
. Notice that the gradient of
L_{\delta}
is
\nabla L_{\delta}(y)=(\partial_{y_{1}}h_{\delta}(y_{1}),\dots,\partial_{y_{k}}h_{\delta}(y_{k}))
, where

\partial_{y_{i}}h_{\delta}(y_{i})=\begin{cases}y_{i}&|y_{i}|\leq\delta\\
\delta\,\text{sign}(y_{i})&|y_{i}|>\delta,\end{cases}

and thus
\nabla L_{\delta}
is
1
-Lipschitz and
L_{\delta}\circ D
is
\|D\|^{2}
-smooth. The parameters
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
serve as regularization parameters.

There are numerous approaches to solve this problem with splitting methods, and again, the term
\tfrac{1}{2}\|{Hx-f}\|_{2}^{2}
can be treated in different ways.

•

Doing implicit steps, i.e. evaluating the respective resolvent of
\tfrac{1}{2}\|{Hf-f}\|_{2}^{2}
exactly. This amount to solving a linear system with the operator
I+\tau H^{T}H
. As in the previous section, we will do this applying the method of conjugate gradients (CG) for the solution of the linear system up to a certain fixed tolerance. This is done in what we call
implicit Davis-Yin
and explain the details below.

•

Doing approximate implicit steps by using the method of conjugate gradients (CG) for the solution of the linear systems within the HPE framework as described in Algorithm
4
.

•

Doing forward steps: Since the term is
L
-smooth, one can do forward steps of the form
H^{T}(Hx-f)
. This is possible making use for example of the forward-backward (FB) algorithm (or proximal-gradient)
Tseng (1991)
;
Chen and Rockafellar (1997)
;
Combettes and Wajs (2005)
;
Attouch et al. (2011)
.

4.2.1
Davis-Yin with implicit steps

We put the optimization problem (
40
) in correspondence with (
30
) by using Remark
3.7
and setting
A_{1}x=H^{T}(Hx-b)
,
A_{2}x=\lambda_{1}\partial\|x\|_{1}
and
Bx=\lambda_{2}\nabla(L_{\delta}\circ D)(x)=\lambda_{2}D^{T}\nabla L_{\delta}(Dx)
. We apply the standard Davis-Yin method
Davis and Yin (2017)
with this choice of operators, resulting in the scheme

\displaystyle x_{1}^{k+1}

\displaystyle=(I+\gamma H^{T}H)^{-1}(w^{k}-\gamma H^{T}f))

(41)

\displaystyle x_{2}^{k+1}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{soft}_{[-\gamma\lambda_{1},\gamma\lambda_{1}]}(2x_{1}^{k+1}-w^{k}-\gamma\lambda_{2}D^{T}\nabla L_{\delta}(Dx_{1}^{k+1}))

\displaystyle w^{k+1}

\displaystyle=w^{k}+\frac{x_{2}^{k+1}-x_{1}^{k+1}}{1+\alpha}

where
\gamma\in(0,2/\beta)
is the stepsize (for some constant
\beta
),
\alpha=\frac{\gamma\beta}{4-\gamma\beta}
and the soft tresholding operator

\operatorname{soft}_{[-\eta,\eta]}(x)=\text{sign}(x)\max(|x|-\eta,0)

is computed componetwise. In order to guarantee convergence of the Davis-Yin algorithm, the constant
\beta
has to be greater than the cocoercivity constant of
B
, which coincides with its Lipschitz constant, since
B
is a gradient of a convex function (see
(
Bauschke and Combettes, 2017
, Theorem 18.15)
). In the experiments we estimated from above this constant, using the fact that
\|D\|\leq 2
and setting
\beta=4\lambda_{2}\geq\lambda_{2}\|D\|^{2}
. Then, we selected the stepsize
\gamma=\frac{1}{\beta}
.

4.2.2
HPE Davis-Yin

To avoid the exact solution of the linear system in the Davis-Yin method of the previous section, we can apply the HPE framework from Section
3.3
, i.e. Algorithm
4
. Instead of evaluating the resolvent
J_{\tau A_{1}}
exactly
we apply the method of conjugate gradients to improve the pair
(\tilde{x_{1}}^{k+1},a_{1}^{k+1})
. Note that for a given candidate
\tilde{x}_{1}^{k+1}
there is a unique
a_{1}^{k+1}=H^{T}(H\tilde{x}_{1}^{k+1}-f)\in A_{1}\tilde{x}_{1}^{k+1}
.

For the HPE DY method we need
\gamma\in\left(0,\frac{2}{\beta}\right)
and we take again
\gamma=\frac{1}{\beta}
, with
\beta
estimating the value of the cocoercivity constant of
B
from above as seen in Section
4.2.1
.

4.2.3
Forward-Backward

Introducing the operators
A,\tilde{B}
defined by
Ax=\lambda_{1}\partial\|x\|_{1}
and
\widetilde{B}x=H^{T}(Hx-f)+\lambda_{2}D^{T}\nabla L_{\delta}(Dx)
, respectively, the problem (
40
) can be written as

\text{find }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\quad\text{such that }\quad 0\in Ax+\tilde{B}x.

To this problem, one can apply the classical forward-backward algorithm (or proximal-gradient)
Tseng (1991)
;
Chen and Rockafellar (1997)
;
Combettes and Wajs (2005)
;
Attouch et al. (2011)
. The resulting scheme reads as

\displaystyle\begin{split}x^{k+1}&=\operatorname{soft}_{[-\gamma\lambda_{1},\gamma\lambda_{1}]}(x^{k}-\gamma H^{T}(Hx^{k}-f-\gamma\lambda_{2}D^{T}\nabla L_{\delta}(Dx^{k}))\end{split}

(42)

Also here, the stepsize
\gamma
has to be lower than a certain quantity. In particular, to guarantee convergence, we estimated from above the cocoercivity constant of the operator
\tilde{B}
, by setting
\tilde{\beta}=\|H\|^{2}+4\lambda_{2}\geq\|H\|^{2}+\lambda_{2}\|D\|^{2}
(using again the fact that
\|D\|\leq 2
), and then selected
\gamma=1/\tilde{\beta}
.

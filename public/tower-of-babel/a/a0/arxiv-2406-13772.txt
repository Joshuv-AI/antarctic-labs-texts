Abstract
. We extend the subrepresentation formula | f ⁡ ( x ) | ≤ c n ​ I 1 ​ ( | ∇ f | ) ​ ( x ) |f(x)|\leq c_{n}\,I_{1}(|\nabla f|)(x) in several ways. First, we consider more general A 1 A_{1} -potential operators on the right-hand side and prove local and global pointwise inequalities for these operators. Second, we show that we can improve the right-hand side using fractional derivatives. Finally, we extend our results to rough singular integral operators, similar to the main result in [ HMP1 ] .



1.
Motivation

It is well known that the pointwise inequality in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
,

(1)

|f(x)|\leq c_{n}\,I_{1}(|\nabla f|)(x),

plays a central role in relating a function and its smoothness by
L^{p}
type estimate. Recall here that

I_{1}
is the Riesz potential operator of order
{\alpha}=1
given by

I_{\alpha}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{f(y)}{|x-y|^{n-{\alpha}}}\,dy,\quad 0<{\alpha}<n.

This estimate goes back to Sobolev and gives rise to the Gagliardo-Nirenberg-Sobolev inequality

\|f\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\,\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

when
f\in C_{c}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

1\leq p<n
, and
\frac{1}{p^{*}}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{n}.

Moreover, local versions of (
1
), like,

|f(x)-f_{B}|\leq C\,I_{1}(\mathbf{1}_{B}|\nabla f|)(x),\qquad x\in B,

are paramount for studying the Poincaré-Sobolev inequalities

\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B}|f(x)-f_{B}|^{q}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq C\,r(B)\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B}|\nabla f|^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where
B
could be euclidean ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, or other type of domains.
With this result as a model, many similar results can be found where the gradient operator
\nabla
is substituted with generalized gradients, and the Riesz potential operator is replaced by other types of potential operators.
More precisely, one can bound the norms of
f
by the norms of
Xf
provided there is an integral operator
T
which is bounded on appropriate weighted
L^{p}
spaces and for which the pointwise estimate

|f(x)|\leq c\,T(|Xf(x)|)

is valid. Such inequality is called a subrepresentation. For example, this pointwise estimate is known
to hold for vector fields of Hörmander type when
T
is given by

Tf(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{d(x,y)}{|B(x,d(x,y))|}\,f(y)\,dy,

where
d(x,y)
is the associated Carnot–Carathéodory metric,
B(x,r)
denotes the metric ball with center
x
and radius
r
, and
|B(x,r)|
is the Lebesgue measure of the ball.

More generally, there are "potential type operators" within the context of doubling metric measure spaces
(\mathcal{S},d,\mu)
of the form

(2)

Tf(x)=\int_{\mathcal{S}}K(x,y)\,f(y)\,d\mu(y),

where the kernel
K(x,y)
is non-negative and satisfying appropriate estimates.
An important class of examples with metrics other than the usual
Euclidean metric consists of potential operators related to the
regularity of subelliptic differential equations. In particular,
vector fields of Hörmander type (see
[
H
]
) as well as the
classes of non-smooth vector fields studied in
[
FL
]
lead to
integral operators of the type we will study. In addition, the
differential operators of Grushin type considered in
[
FGuW
]

(at least in the simplest case of Lebesgue measure) are related to
integrals of type (
2
). In fact, for all these
examples, the associated potential operator has the form

(3)

Tf(x)=\int_{\mathcal{S}}\frac{d(x,y)}{\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}\,f(y)\,d\mu(y),

where
d(x,y)
is a distance function that is naturally related to the vector fields. For a nice survey on these operators we refer the reader to the work of Kairema
[
AK
]
.

Motivated by these results, we propose improvements of (
1
) by considering measures
\mu
induced by a non-negative locally Lebesgue integrable function
w
. More precisely, we will consider potential type operators
T_{w}
defined by

(4)

T_{w}f(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|x-y|}{w(B(x,|x-y|))}f(y)\,w(y)\,dy,

where
w(B)=\int_{B}w(x)\,dx
. We shall also consider the fractional version of
T_{w}
defined by

T_{w,{\alpha}}f(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|x-y|^{\alpha}}{w(B(x,|x-y|))}f(y)\,w(y)\,dy,

for any
0<\alpha<n
.

Recall that a weight
w
is said to be in the class
A_{1}
if and only if for any ball
B
,

\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B}w(x)\,dx\leq C\,w(x),

for almost every
x\in B
. The least constant
C
for the above inequality is denoted as
[w]_{A_{1}}
.
With weights from
A_{1}
, we will be able to derive a more general version for (
1
), namely

|f(x)|\leq c_{n,w}\,T_{w}(|\nabla f|)(x).

In addition, it was recently shown by the authors in
[
HMP1
,
HMP2
]
that (
1
) has a powerful extension to operators. Consider a degree zero homogeneous function
\Omega\in L^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
. The rough singular integral operator is defined by

T_{\Omega}f(x)=\mathsf{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\Omega(y)}{|y|^{n}}f(x-y)\,dy,

and its associated maximal truncation operator is given by

T^{\star}_{\Omega}f(x)=\sup_{t>0}\left|\int_{|y|>t}\frac{\Omega(y)}{|y|^{n}}f(x-y)\,dy\right|.

It is well-known that the size of
\Omega
determines the boundedness properties of the operators
T_{\Omega}
and
T^{\star}_{\Omega}
. It was shown in
[
HMP1
]
that

(5)

T^{\star}_{\Omega}f(x)\leq c_{n,\Omega}\,I_{1}(|\nabla f|)(x)

when
\Omega\in L^{n,\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
. We will extend this estimate to
T_{w}
, namely,

T^{\star}_{\Omega}f(x)\leq c_{n,w,\Omega}\,T_{w}(|\nabla f|)(x),

for an appropriate class of weights
w
.

Another approach to improve inequality (
1
) involves exploring smaller operators that could replace
I_{1}(|\nabla\cdot|)
on the right-hand side. One promising candidate is
I_{\alpha}(\mathcal{D}^{\alpha})
where
\mathcal{D}^{\alpha}
is a nonlinear fractional differential operator. The fractional derivative operator,
|\nabla|^{\alpha}=(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}
, is defined using the Fourier transform for
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

\mathcal{F}(|\nabla|^{\alpha}f)(\xi)=|\xi|^{{\alpha}}\hat{f}(\xi).

For
0<\alpha<2
, the fractional derivative can be realized via the integral formula

|\nabla|^{\alpha}f(x)=c_{n,\alpha}\,\mathsf{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{f(x)-f(y)}{|x-y|^{n+\alpha}}\,dy

(we refer to the book by Ponce
[
Pon
, p. 246]
for a related variant).
When
0<\alpha<1
, the above integral converges absolutely, and hence we may define the nonlinear fractional differential operator introduced in
[
Sp
]
,

\mathcal{D}^{{\alpha}}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|^{n+{\alpha}}}\,dy.

Indeed, we shall prove that

(6)

T^{\star}_{\Omega}f(x)\leq C_{n,{\alpha},\Omega}\,(1-\alpha)\,I_{{\alpha}}(\mathcal{D}^{{\alpha}}f)(x),\qquad f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\,0<{\alpha}<1

which improves the main result in
[
HMP1
]
as we shall prove later that

\displaystyle(1-\alpha)\,I_{{\alpha}}(\mathcal{D}^{{\alpha}}f)(x)\leq c_{n,{\alpha}}\,I_{1}(|\nabla f|)(x),

where the constant
c_{n,{\alpha}}
is bounded by a dimensional constant.

2.
The theorems

When
w\in A_{1}
, we have an analog of (
1
), as stated in the theorem below.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
w\in A_{1}
. There exists a dimensional constant
c_{n}
such that for any Lipschitz function
f

that has compact support, we have the global estimate

(7)

|f(x)|\leq c_{n}\,[w]_{A_{1}}\,T_{w}(|\nabla f|)(x).

Remark 2.2
.

We remark that (
7
) has been obtained using a variant of the usual estimates, where some "holes" have been created to obtain a family of disjoint annuli, as seen in (
15
) from the proof.
This family of "holes" allows us to insert the metric length
|x-y|
inside the integral. It seems that the usual methods do not permit this possibility.
A similar remark holds for the rest of the theorems stated in the section.

One can replace the left-hand-side of (
7
) by
T^{\star}_{\Omega}(f)
, and this yields an improvement for the main result in
[
HMP1
]
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Suppose that
\Omega
is homogeneous of degree zero with zero average on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
and belongs to
L^{n,\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}).
Let
w\in A_{1}
. For any smooth function
f
with compact support,

(8)

T^{\star}_{\Omega}f(x)\leq c_{n}\|\Omega\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.02777pt}L^{n,\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}[w]_{A_{1}}\,T_{w}(|\nabla f|)(x)

where
T_{w}
is the potential type operator defined above.

Remark 2.4
.

We have not encountered either (
7
) or (
8
) in the literature with
w
belonging to
A_{1}
(or any
A_{p}
), which may lead to new
L^{p}
–
L^{q}
estimates.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Suppose that
\Omega
is homogeneous of degree zero with zero average on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
and belongs to
L^{\frac{n}{\alpha},\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
. Then,

(9)

T^{\star}_{\Omega}f(x)\leq c_{n}(1-\alpha)\|\Omega\|_{L^{\frac{n}{\alpha},\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\,I_{\alpha}(\mathcal{D}^{\alpha}f)(x),\qquad f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

To see that Theorem
2.5
improves the main result in
[
HMP1
]
, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
0<\alpha<1
and
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then,

\displaystyle(1-\alpha)\,I_{\alpha}(\mathcal{D}^{{\alpha}}f)(x)\leq c_{\alpha,n}\,I_{1}(|\nabla f|)(x)

where
c_{\alpha,n}
is bounded by a dimensional constant as
\alpha\rightarrow 1^{-}
.

Remark 2.7
.

The explicit constant is given by

c_{\alpha,n}=\frac{(1-\alpha)\,\pi^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\,\Gamma(\frac{1-\alpha}{2})\,\Gamma(\frac{\alpha}{2})\,\Gamma(\frac{n-1}{2})}{\alpha\,\Gamma(\frac{n+\alpha-1}{2})\,\Gamma(\frac{n-\alpha}{2})}.

Since

(1-\alpha)\,\Gamma(\frac{1-\alpha}{2})=2\,\Gamma(\frac{3-\alpha}{2})

we have the limit

\lim_{\alpha\rightarrow 1^{-}}c_{\alpha,n}=\frac{2\pi^{n/2}}{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})}=\sigma(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})

where
\sigma
is the surface measure of the unit sphere.

3.
Proofs of the theorems

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Recall the
(1,1)
Poincaré inequality, for a dimensional constant
c_{n}

\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B}\lvert f(x)-f_{B}\rvert\,dx\leq c_{n}\,r(B)\,\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B}|\nabla f(x)|\,dx

for every ball
B
.

Fix a point
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and let
R>0
denote the radius of the ball
B
centered at
x
. Let
\{B_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be the family of nested balls centered at
x
such that
B_{1}=B
and
r(B_{k})=2^{1-k}R
.
Then,

\lvert f(x)-f_{B}\rvert=\left\lvert\lim_{k\to\infty}f_{B_{k}}-f_{B_{1}}\right\rvert\leq\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left\lvert f_{B_{k+1}}-f_{B_{k}}\right\rvert.

Using the dyadic structure of the chain and the Poincaré-type inequality we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left\lvert f_{B_{k+1}}-f_{B_{k}}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{|B_{k+1}|}\int_{B_{k+1}}\lvert f(y)-f_{B_{k}}\rvert\,dy

\displaystyle\leq 2^{n}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{|B_{k}|}\int_{B_{k}}\lvert f(y)-f_{B_{k}}\rvert\,dy

\displaystyle\leq c_{n}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{r(B_{k})}{|B_{k}|}\int_{B_{k}}|\nabla f(y)|\,dy.

Let us analyze the sum:

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{r(B_{k})}{|B_{k}|}\int_{B_{k}}|\nabla f(y)|\,dy=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{r(B_{k})}{|B_{k}|}\int_{B_{k}\setminus B_{k+1}}|\nabla f(y)|\,dy\,+\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{r(B_{k})}{|B_{k}|}\int_{B_{k+1}}|\nabla f(y)|\,dy,

but then

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{r(B_{k})}{|B_{k}|}\int_{B_{k+1}}|\nabla f(y)|\,dy

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{2\,r(B_{k+1})}{2^{n}|B_{k+1}|}\int_{B_{k+1}}|\nabla f(y)|\,dy

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{n-1}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{r(B_{k+1})}{|B_{k+1}|}\int_{B_{k+1}}|\nabla f(y)|\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2^{n-1}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{r(B_{k})}{|B_{k}|}\int_{B_{k}}|\nabla f(y)|\,dy.

Therefore we have

(15)

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{r(B_{k})}{|B_{k}|}\int_{B_{k}}|\nabla f(y)|\,dy\leq c_{n}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{r(B_{k})}{|B_{k}|}\int_{B_{k}\setminus B_{k+1}}|\nabla f(y)|\,dy.

Now, by the definition of
A_{1}
, we continue with

\displaystyle\lvert f(x)-f_{B}|

\displaystyle\leq c_{n}[w]_{A_{1}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{r(B_{k})}{w(B_{k})}\int_{B_{k}\setminus B_{k+1}}|\nabla f(y)|\,w(y)\,dy

\displaystyle\leq c_{n}[w]_{A_{1}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{w(B_{k})}\int_{B_{k}\setminus B_{k+1}}|x-y|\,|\nabla f(y)|\,w(y)\,dy

\displaystyle\leq c_{n}[w]_{A_{1}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\int_{B_{k}\setminus B_{k+1}}\frac{|x-y|}{w(B(x,|x-y|))}|\nabla f(y)|\,w(y)\,dy

\displaystyle\leq c_{n}[w]_{A_{1}}\int_{B}\frac{|x-y|}{w(B(x,|x-y|))}|\nabla f(y)|\,w(y)\,dy.

To obtain (
7
), we let
R\to\infty
and observe that
f_{B}\to 0
while the right-hand side is dominated by the integral over
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.3
.

As proved in
[
HMP1
]
, we have

T^{\star}_{\Omega}f(x)\leq c_{n}\,\|\Omega\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.02777pt}L^{n,\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}r(B_{k})\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B_{k}}|\nabla f(y)|\,dy

where
B_{k}=B(x,2^{k})
for each
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. Since

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{r(B_{k})}{|B_{k}|}\int_{B_{k}}|\nabla f(y)|\,\mathrm{d}y=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{r(B_{k})}{|B_{k}|}\int_{B_{k}\setminus B_{k-1}}|\nabla f(y)|\,dy\,+\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{r(B_{k})}{|B_{k}|}\int_{B_{k-1}}|\nabla f(y)|\,dy,

and

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{r(B_{k})}{|B_{k}|}\int_{B_{k-1}}|\nabla f(y)|\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{2\,r(B_{k-1})}{2^{n}|B_{k-1}|}\int_{B_{k-1}}|\nabla f(y)|\,dy

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{n-1}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{r(B_{k-1})}{|B_{k-1}|}\int_{B_{k-1}}|\nabla f(y)|\,dy

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{n-1}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{r(B_{k})}{|B_{k}|}\int_{B_{k}}|\nabla f(y)|\,dy,

we have

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{r(B_{k})}{|B_{k}|}\int_{B_{k}}|\nabla f(y)|\mathrm{d}y\leq c_{n}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{r(B_{k})}{|B_{k}|}\int_{B_{k}\setminus B_{k-1}}|\nabla f(y)|\,dy,

and hence

T^{\star}_{\Omega}f(x)\leq c_{n}\,\|\Omega\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.02777pt}L^{n,\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}r(B_{k})\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B_{k}\setminus B_{k-1}}|\nabla f(y)|\,dy.

By the definition of
A_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle T^{\star}_{\Omega}f(x)

\displaystyle\leq c_{n}\|\Omega\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.02777pt}L^{n,\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}[w]_{A_{1}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{r(B_{k})}{w(B_{k})}\int_{B_{k}\setminus B_{k-1}}|\nabla f(y)|\,w(y)\,dy

\displaystyle\leq c_{n}\|\Omega\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.02777pt}L^{n,\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}[w]_{A_{1}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{w(B_{k})}\int_{B_{k}\setminus B_{k-1}}|x-y|\,|\nabla f(y)|\,w(y)\,dy

\displaystyle\leq c_{n}\|\Omega\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.02777pt}L^{n,\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}[w]_{A_{1}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{B_{k}\setminus B_{k-1}}\frac{|x-y|}{w(B(x,|x-y|))}|\nabla f(y)|\,w(y)\,dy

\displaystyle\leq c_{n}\|\Omega\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.02777pt}L^{n,\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}[w]_{A_{1}}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|x-y|}{w(B(x,|x-y|))}|\nabla f(y)|\,w(y)\,dy.

∎

Proof of Theorem
2.5
.

As proved in
[
HMP1
]
, we have

T^{\star}_{\Omega}f(x)\leq C\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{2^{kn}}\int_{|y|\leq 2^{k}}|\Omega(y)|\,|f(x-y)-c_{k}|\,dy.

where
c_{k}
is a constant to be chosen for each
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. By Hölder’s inequality, we have

T^{\star}_{\Omega}f(x)\leq c_{n}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\|\Omega\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.02777pt}L^{\frac{n}{{\alpha}},\infty}(B(0,2^{k}))}\,\|f(x-\cdot)-c_{k}\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.02777pt}L^{\left(\frac{n}{{\alpha}}\right)^{\prime},1}(B(0,2^{k}))}.

Since

|\{y\in B(0,2^{k}):|\Omega(y)|>\lambda\}|=\frac{2^{kn}}{n}\sigma(\{y^{\prime}\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}:|\Omega(y^{\prime})|>\lambda\}),

we have

\|\Omega\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.02777pt}L^{\frac{n}{{\alpha}},\infty}(B(0,2^{k}))}=C_{n}\,\|\Omega\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.02777pt}L^{\frac{n}{{\alpha}},\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})},

and hence

T^{\star}_{\Omega}f(x)\leq c_{n}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\|\Omega\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.02777pt}L^{\frac{n}{{\alpha}},\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\,\|f(x-\cdot)-c_{k}\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.02777pt}L^{\left(\frac{n}{{\alpha}}\right)^{\prime},1}(B(0,2^{k}))}.

Let
B_{k}=B(x,2^{k})
and
c_{k}=f_{B_{k}}=\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B_{k}}f
, then

\|f(x-\cdot)-c_{k}\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.02777pt}L^{\left(\frac{n}{{\alpha}}\right)^{\prime},1}(B(0,2^{k}))}=\|f-f_{B_{k}}\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.02777pt}L^{\left(\frac{n}{{\alpha}}\right)^{\prime},1}(B_{k})}.

This fact combined with the previous inequality yields

T^{\star}_{\Omega}f(x)\leq c_{n}\,\|\Omega\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.02777pt}L^{\frac{n}{{\alpha}},\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\|f-f_{B_{k}}\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.02777pt}L^{\left(\frac{n}{{\alpha}}\right)^{\prime},1}(B_{k})}.

We continue by using the Poincaré-Sobolev inequality (
12
).
Recalling the definition of
\mathcal{D}^{{\alpha}}

\mathcal{D}^{{\alpha}}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|^{n+{\alpha}}}\,dy,

we can continue with,

\displaystyle T^{\star}_{\Omega}f(x)

\displaystyle\leq c_{n}(1-{\alpha})\,\|\Omega\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.02777pt}L^{\frac{n}{{\alpha}},\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}r(B_{k})^{{\alpha}}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B_{k}}\int_{B_{k}}\frac{\lvert f(z)-f(y)\rvert}{\lvert z-y\rvert^{n+{\alpha}}}\,dy\,dz

\displaystyle\leq c_{n}(1-{\alpha})\,\|\Omega\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.02777pt}L^{\frac{n}{{\alpha}},\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}r(B_{k})^{{\alpha}}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B_{k}}\mathcal{D}^{{\alpha}}f(y)\,dy,

We now analyze the sum:

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}r(B_{k})^{{\alpha}}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B_{k}}\mathcal{D}^{{\alpha}}f(y)\,dy

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{2^{k{\alpha}}}{\omega_{n}2^{kn}}\int_{B_{k}}\mathcal{D}^{{\alpha}}f(y)\,dy

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\omega_{n}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{2^{k(n-{\alpha})}}\int_{2^{k-1}<|x-y|\leq 2^{k}}\mathcal{D}^{{\alpha}}f(y)\,dy

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\frac{1}{\omega_{n}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{2^{k(n-{\alpha})}}\int_{|x-y|\leq 2^{k-1}}\mathcal{D}^{{\alpha}}f(y)\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\omega_{n}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{2^{k-1}<|x-y|\leq 2^{k}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n-{\alpha}}}\,\mathcal{D}^{{\alpha}}f(y)\,dy

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\frac{1}{2^{n-{\alpha}}}\,\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}r(B_{k-1})^{{\alpha}}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B_{k-1}}\mathcal{D}^{{\alpha}}f(y)\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\omega_{n}}I_{{\alpha}}(\mathcal{D}^{{\alpha}}f)(x)+\frac{1}{2^{n-{\alpha}}}\,\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}r(B_{k})^{\alpha}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B_{k}}\mathcal{D}^{{\alpha}}(y)\,dy.

Since
{\alpha}<1
and
n\geq 2
, we have
\frac{1}{2^{n-{\alpha}}}<1
, and we can rearrange the terms to obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}r(B_{k})^{{\alpha}}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B_{k}}\mathcal{D}^{{\alpha}}f(y)\,dy

\displaystyle\leq c_{n}(2^{n-{\alpha}})^{\prime}\,I_{{\alpha}}(\mathcal{D}^{{\alpha}}f)(x)

\displaystyle\leq c_{n}\,I_{{\alpha}}(\mathcal{D}^{{\alpha}}f)(x).

This concludes the proof of the theorem.

∎

Proof of Lemma
2.6
.

Since

f(y)-f(x)=\int_{0}^{1}\nabla f(x+t(y-x))\cdot(y-x)\,dt

by the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus for line integrals, we have

\displaystyle I_{{\alpha}}(\mathcal{D}^{{\alpha}}f)(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|f(y)-f(z)|}{|y-z|^{n+{\alpha}}}\,dz\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{n-{\alpha}}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{0}^{1}|\nabla f(y+t(z-y))|\,dt\frac{dz}{|y-z|^{n+{\alpha}-1}}\,\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{n-{\alpha}}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla f(y+t(z-y))|}{|y-z|^{n+{\alpha}-1}}dz\,dt\,\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{n-{\alpha}}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla f(u)|}{|u-y|^{n+{\alpha}-1}}du\int_{0}^{1}\frac{t^{n+{\alpha}-1}}{t^{n}}\,dt\right)\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{n-{\alpha}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{{\alpha}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla f(u)|}{|u-y|^{n+{\alpha}-1}}du\,\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{n-{\alpha}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{{\alpha}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla f(u)|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{|u-y|^{n+{\alpha}-1}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n-{\alpha}}}\,dy\,du

where we have made the change of variables
u=y+t(z-y)
in the fourth estimate. Now, we use the beta integral identity (see exercise 2.4.9 in
[
G
]
), namely

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{|t-x_{1}|^{{\alpha}_{1}}}\,\frac{1}{|t-x_{2}|^{{\alpha}_{2}}}\,dt=\pi^{n/2}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n-\alpha_{1}}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{\alpha_{1}}{2})}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n-\alpha_{2}}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{\alpha_{2}}{2})}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}-n}{2})}{\Gamma(n-\frac{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}{2})}|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{n-{\alpha}_{1}-{\alpha}_{2}}

for
0<\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}<n
,
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}>n
.

Applying this with
\alpha_{1}=n+{\alpha}-1
and
\alpha_{2}=n-{\alpha}
, we have

\displaystyle I_{{\alpha}}(\mathcal{D}^{{\alpha}}f)(x)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\pi^{n/2}\,\Gamma(\frac{1-{\alpha}}{2})\,\Gamma(\frac{{\alpha}}{2})\,\Gamma(\frac{n-1}{2})}{{\alpha}\,\Gamma(\frac{n+{\alpha}-1}{2})\,\Gamma(\frac{n-{\alpha}}{2})\,\Gamma(\frac{1}{2})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla f(u)|}{|x-u|^{n-1}}\,du

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\,\Gamma(\frac{1-{\alpha}}{2})\,\Gamma(\frac{{\alpha}}{2})\,\Gamma(\frac{n-1}{2})}{{\alpha}\,\Gamma(\frac{n+{\alpha}-1}{2})\,\Gamma(\frac{n-{\alpha}}{2})}\,I_{1}(|\nabla f|)(x).

∎

.

4.
Consequences of our main results

Recall that a weight
w
is said to be doubling if there exists a constant
D=D(w)
such that

w(B(x,2r))\leq D\,w(B(x,r))

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and all
r>0
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
d
be a positive constant bigger than one.
Let
p,q
be numbers such that
1<p<d
and
\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{d}
. Let
w
be a doubling weight and assume further that there exists a positive constant
c=c(w)
such that

(16)

w(B(x,r))\geq c\,r^{d},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},r>0,

then

\|T_{w}f\|_{L^{q}(w)}\leq C_{w,p,d}\,\|f\|_{L^{p}(w)}.

Remark 4.2
.

We want to emphasize the need to assume condition (
16
). Although it is known that the doubling condition implies the lower Alhfors condition (
16
), this is only true for bounded spaces
X
, i.e.
\textsf{diam}(X)<\infty
. When
X
is unbounded, the doubling condition no longer implies the lower Alhfors condition. For simplicity, consider
n=1
,
X=\mathbb{R}
and
w(x)=|x|^{-\frac{1}{2}}
. Then

w(B(x,r))=\int_{x-r}^{x+r}|y|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,dy.

This weight
w
is an
A_{1}
weight and hence doubling (
[
G
]
(example 9.1.6)). However,
w
does not satisfy the lower Alhfors condition. Indeed, suppose that there are constants
c,d>0
such that

\int_{x-r}^{x+r}|y|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,dy\geq c\,r^{d}

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
and all
r>0
. Fix
r>0
and consider
x>r
, then we have

2(\sqrt{x+r}-\sqrt{x-r})\geq c\,r^{d}\iff\frac{4r}{\sqrt{x+r}+\sqrt{x-r}}\geq c\,r^{d},

which is a contradiction as
x\to\infty
.

Corollary 4.3
.

Suppose
1<p<n
and
\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p^{*}}=\frac{1}{n}
. Let
w
be a doubling weight and
c=c(w)
be a positive constant such that

(17)

w(x)\geq c,\qquad a.e.x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Then

\|T_{w}f\|_{L^{p^{*}}(w)}\leq C_{w,p}\,\|f\|_{L^{p}(w)}.

Indeed, observe that (
16
) becomes
w(B(x,r))\geq c\,r^{n}
whenever
d=n
, and hence by the Lebesgue Differentiation Theorem we have
w(x)\geq c
, almost everywhere. The conditions are equivalent in this case.

From
[
G
]
(Proposition 9.1.5), if
w\in A_{1}
, then
w
is doubling; more precisely, we have

w(B(x,\lambda r))\leq\lambda^{n}[w]_{A_{1}}w(B(x,r))

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, all
r>0
and all
\lambda>0
. Using this, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 4.4
.

Suppose
1<p<n
and
\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p^{*}}=\frac{1}{n}
. Let
w
be an
A_{1}
weight such that for a positive constant
c=c(w)

(18)

w(x)\geq c,\qquad a.e.\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Then

\|f\|_{L^{p^{*}}(w)}\leq C_{w,p,d}\,\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(w)}

for all
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

For the proof we make a variation of the well known method of Hedberg
[
H
]
.
For any
R>0
, we have
T_{w}f(x)=T_{w}^{1}f(x)+T_{w}^{2}f(x)
where

T_{w}^{1}f(x)=\int_{|y-x|<R}\frac{|y-x|}{w(B(x,|y-x|))}\,f(y)\,w(y)\,dy

and

T_{w}^{2}f(x)=\int_{|y-x|\geq R}\frac{|y-x|}{w(B(x,|y-x|))}\,f(y)\,w(y)\,dy.

We first estimate
T^{1}_{w}f(x)
as follows:

\displaystyle T_{w}^{1}f(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{\frac{R}{2^{k}}<|y-x|\leq\frac{R}{2^{k-1}}}\frac{|y-x|}{w(B(x,|y-x|))}\,f(y)\,w(y)\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{R}{2^{k-1}}\cdot\frac{1}{w(B(x,\frac{R}{2^{k}}))}\int_{|y-x|\leq\frac{R}{2^{k-1}}}f(y)\,w(y)\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{R}{2^{k-1}}\cdot\frac{C_{w}}{w(B(x,\frac{R}{2^{k-1}}))}\int_{B(x,\frac{R}{2^{k-1}})}f(y)\,w(y)\,dy

\displaystyle\leq C_{w}\,R\,M^{c}_{w}f(x)

where we have utilized the doubling property of
w
in the third estimate.
For
T^{2}_{w}f(x)
, by Hölder’s inequality, we have:

\displaystyle T_{w}^{2}f(x)

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{L^{p}(w)}\left(\int_{|y-x|\geq R}\frac{|y-x|^{p^{\prime}}}{w(B(x,|y-x|))^{p^{\prime}}}\,w(y)\,dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=\|f\|_{L^{p}(w)}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{2^{k-1}R<|y-x|\leq 2^{k}R}\frac{|y-x|^{p^{\prime}}}{w(B(x,|y-x|))^{p^{\prime}}}\,w(y)\,dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq R\,\|f\|_{L^{p}(w)}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{2^{kp^{\prime}}w(B(x,2^{k}R))}{w(B(x,2^{k-1}R))^{p^{\prime}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{w,p}\,R\,\|f\|_{L^{p}(w)}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{2^{kp^{\prime}}}{w(B(x,2^{k-1}R))^{p^{\prime}-1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{w,p}\,R^{1-\frac{d}{p}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(w)}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{2^{kp^{\prime}}}{2^{(k-1)(p^{\prime}-1)d}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}

where we have utilized the doubling condition on
w
in the fourth estimate and condition (
16
) in the last estimate. Since
(p^{\prime}-1)d=\frac{d}{p-1}>\frac{p}{p-1}=p^{\prime}
, the sum in the last display converges, and hence

T_{w}^{2}f(x)\leq C_{w,p,d}\,R^{1-\frac{d}{p^{\prime}}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(w)}.

This estimate and the estimate for
T^{1}_{w}f(x)
imply

T_{w}f(x)\leq C_{w,p,d}\left[R^{1-\frac{d}{p}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(w)}+R\,M^{c}_{w}f(x)\right].

Notice that the right-hand side attains its minimum value at

R=\left(\frac{(\frac{d}{p}-1)\|f\|_{L^{p}(w)}}{M^{c}_{w}f(x)}\right)^{\frac{p}{d}},

so we have

T_{w}f(x)\leq C_{w,p,d}\,\|f\|_{L^{p}(w)}^{\frac{p}{d}}\,M^{c}_{w}f(x)^{1-\frac{p}{d}}.

Recall that the centered weighted maximal function is defined by

M^{c}_{w}f(x)\coloneq\sup_{r>0}\frac{1}{w(B(x,r))}\int_{B(x,r)}f(y)\,w(y)\,dy

is bounded from
L^{p}(w)
to
L^{p}(w)
. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\|T_{w}f\|_{L^{q}(w)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{w,p,d}\,\|f\|_{L^{p}(w)}^{\frac{p}{d}}\,\|(M^{c}_{w}f)^{1-\frac{p}{d}}\|_{L^{q}(w)}

\displaystyle=C_{w,p,d}\,\|f\|_{L^{p}(w)}^{\frac{p}{d}}\,\|M^{c}_{w}f\|_{L^{p}(w)}^{1-\frac{p}{d}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{w,p,d}\,\|f\|_{L^{p}(w)}^{\frac{p}{d}}\,\|f\|_{L^{p}(w)}^{1-\frac{p}{d}}=\|f\|_{L^{p}(w)}.

∎

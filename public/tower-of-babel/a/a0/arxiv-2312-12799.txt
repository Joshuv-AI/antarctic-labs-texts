An Algebraic Roadmap of Particle Theories Part II: Theoretical checkpoints
N. Furey

Abstract
An optimal algebraic model of particle physics has a number of checkpoints to pass. As a minimum, models should ⟨ 1 ⟩ \langle 1\rangle conform to the Coleman-Mandula theorem (or establish a loophole), ⟨ 2 ⟩ \langle 2\rangle evade familiar fermion doubling problems, ⟨ 3 ⟩ \langle 3\rangle naturally explain the Standard Model’s chirality, ⟨ 4 ⟩ \langle 4\rangle exclude B-L gauge symmetry at low energy, and ⟨ 5 ⟩ \langle 5\rangle explain the existence of three generations. We demonstrate how the model introduced in [ 1 ] passes checkpoints ⟨ 1 ⟩ , ⟨ 2 ⟩ , ⟨ 3 ⟩ , ⟨ 4 ⟩ \langle 1\rangle,\langle 2\rangle,\langle 3\rangle,\langle 4\rangle , and has yet to cross ⟨ 5 ⟩ \langle 5\rangle . We close by elucidating an unexpected appearance of spacetime symmetries.

&+&{\mathcal{D}}_{L}^{i\uparrow}\hskip 1.42262pti\alpha_{i}L_{\epsilon_{\downarrow\downarrow}}R_{\epsilon_{\downarrow\uparrow}}+\overline{{\mathcal{E}}^{-\downarrow}_{R}}\hskip 2.84526ptL_{\epsilon_{\uparrow\downarrow}}R_{\epsilon_{\downarrow\uparrow}}\\
&-&\hskip 2.84526pt\overline{\mathcal{V}_{R}^{\downarrow}}\hskip 1.42262pt\hskip 1.42262ptL_{\epsilon_{\downarrow\downarrow}}R_{\epsilon_{\downarrow\uparrow}}\Large)\hskip 2.84526pts^{*}S^{*}.\end{array}

(15)

At this point, we see that rewriting our Dirac action
\ell^{D}_{10}
as the Weyl action
\ell_{10}
allows us to evade the fermion doubling problem. That is, the model successfully incorporates
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})
spacetime symmetries, without doubling the spinor space. Hence, it passes checkpoint
\langle 2\rangle
.

Finally, readers may observe above that internal symmetries are described entirely using the left-multiplication algebra, while the local spacetime symmetries are are described using right-multiplication. This model cleanly separates the two. Again, this was an unexpected finding. It also demonstrates that this model conforms to the Coleman-Mandula theorem, and hence passes checkpoint
\langle 1\rangle
.

V
Reintroduction of
\mathfrak{u}(n)

It may at some point be useful to note that a
\mathfrak{u}(n)
Lie subalgebra is easily obtainable by a single constraint on
\mathfrak{so}(2n)
, similar to the multivector condition that gave us
\mathfrak{su}(n)
. While the multivector condition gave us
\mathfrak{su}(n)
, the alternative constraint

\begin{array}[]{c}[r_{k_{1}k_{2}}\Gamma_{k_{1}}\Gamma_{k_{2}},\hskip 1.42262pt\Psi_{\textup{L}}]\hskip 2.84526pt=r_{k_{1}k_{2}}^{\prime}\Gamma_{k_{1}}\Gamma_{k_{2}}\Psi_{\textup{L}}\end{array}

(16)

provides us with
\mathfrak{u}(n)
for some
r_{k_{1}k_{2}}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R},
not necessarily equal to
r_{k_{1}k_{2}}.

VI
Conclusion

The purpose of this second article was to work through the implications of the new model constructed in
[
1
]
. We listed a set of five checkpoints that serve as minimum conditions that an ideal algebraic description of particle physics should pass. Namely, such an ideal model should

\langle 1\rangle
Heed or evade the Coleman-Mandula theorem,

\langle 2\rangle
Solve the fermion doubling problem,

\langle 3\rangle
Explain the Standard Model’s chirality,

\langle 4\rangle
Exclude B-L gauge symmetry at low energies,

\langle 5\rangle
Explain the existence of three generations.

Then, we explained how
[
1
]
excluded B-L gauge symmetry and demonstrated the correct chiral fermion representations, thanks to the multivector condition (checkpoints
\langle 4\rangle
and
\langle 3\rangle
).

Of significance was the serendipitous appearance of spacetime symmetries. Namely, we constructed a Weyl
\mathfrak{spin}(10)
representation from the
*_{\overleftarrow{\mathbb{H}}}
-invariant part of the known Dirac
\mathfrak{spin}(10)
representation. It so happened that the maximal
\mathbb{C}l(10)
subspace commuting with this Weyl
\mathfrak{spin}(10)
representation produced the correct
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})
spacetime symmetries. This allowed us to cross checkpoint
\langle 2\rangle
.

Also fortuitous was the finding that internal symmetries can find their definition using the pure left-multiplication algebra of
\mathbb{R}\otimes\mathbb{C}\otimes\mathbb{H}\otimes\mathbb{O},
while spacetime symmetries find their definition using the pure right-multiplication algebra. That is, internal and spacetime symmetries exhibit a clean separation,
[
48
]
, (checkpoint
\langle 1\rangle
).

At this point, we see that
[
1
]
has passed checkpoints
\langle 1\rangle
,
\langle 2\rangle
,
\langle 3\rangle
,
\langle 4\rangle
. We do not attempt here to solve the three-generation problem (checkpoint
\langle 5\rangle
), but such steps are being made elsewhere. Interested readers are directed to a new line of research, known as
Bott Periodic Particle Physics,

Pure transcendental, immediate valuation ring extensions as limits of smooth algebras
Dorin Popescu

Abstract
. We show that a pure transcendental, immediate extension of valuation rings V ⊂ V ′ V\subset V^{\prime} containing a field is a filtered union of smooth V V -subalgebras of V ′ V^{\prime} . Key words : immediate extensions, pseudo convergent sequences, pseudo limits, smooth morphisms, Henselian rings. 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: Primary 13F30, Secondary 13A18,13F20,13B40.



Introduction

An immediate extension of valuation rings is an extension inducing trivial extensions on residue fields and group value extensions. The theorem stated below
[
9
, Theorem 2]
gives on immediate extensions of valuation rings a form of
Zariski’s Uniformization Theorem
[
16
]
.

Theorem 1
.

If
V\subset V^{\prime}
is an immediate extension of valuation rings containing
\bf Q

then
V^{\prime}
is a filtered direct limit of smooth
V
-algebras.

In the Noetherian case a morphism of rings is a filtered direct limit of smooth algebras iff it is a regular morphism (see
[
7
]
,
[
14
]
). This was important for a positive solution of some Artin’s conjectures
[
2
]
.
An extension of the above theorem stated in
[
10
, Theorem 2]
is the following theorem.

Theorem 2
.

Let
V\subset V^{\prime}
be an extension of valuation rings containing
\bf Q
,
\Gamma\subset\Gamma^{\prime}
the value group extension of
V\subset V^{\prime}
and
\mathrm{val}:K^{\prime*}\rightarrow\Gamma^{\prime}
the valuation of
V^{\prime}
. Then
V^{\prime}
is a filtered direct limit of smooth
V
-algebras if and only if the following statements hold

(1)

for each
q\in\operatorname{Spec}V
the ideal
qV^{\prime}
is prime,

(2)

for any prime ideals
q_{1},q_{2}\in\operatorname{Spec}V
such that
q_{1}\subset q_{2}
and height
(q_{2}/q_{1})=1
and any
x^{\prime}\in q_{2}V^{\prime}\setminus q_{1}^{\prime}
there exists
x\in V
such that
\mathrm{val}(x^{\prime})=\mathrm{val}(x)
, where
q_{1}^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Spec}V^{\prime}
is the prime ideal corresponding to the maximal ideal of
V_{q_{1}}\otimes_{V}V^{\prime}
, that is the maximal prime ideal of
V^{\prime}
lying on
q_{1}
.

It is well known that if the fraction field extension of an immediate extension
V\subset V^{\prime}
is finite and the characteristic of
V
is
>0
, then
V^{\prime}
fails to be a filtered direct limit of smooth
V
-algebras as shows
[
8
, Example 3.13]
inspired from
[
6
]
(see also
[
8
, Remark 6.10]
). However,
B. Antieau, R. Datta
[
1
, Theorem 4.1.1]
showed that every perfect valuation ring of characteristic
p>0
is a filtered union of its smooth
{\bf F}_{p}
-subalgebras. This result is an application of
[
15
, Theorem 1.2.5]
, which relies on some results from
[
4
]
.

In
[
11
, Theorem 1]
we state the following theorem.

Theorem 3
.

Let
V\subset V^{\prime}
be an immediate extension of valuation rings containing a field and
K\subset K^{\prime}
the fraction field extension. If
K^{\prime}/K
is algebraic then
V^{\prime}
is a filtered
union of its complete intersection
V
-subalgebras of finite type.

A
complete intersection

V
-algebra is a local
V
-algebra of type
C/(P)
, where
C
is a localization of a polynomial
V
-algebra of finite type and
P
is a regular sequence of elements of
C
.

One goal
of this paper is to show that Theorem
1
holds in a special case of positive characteristic.

Theorem 4
.

Let
V\subset V^{\prime}
be an immediate extension of valuation rings containing a field and
K\subset K^{\prime}
its fraction field extension. If
K^{\prime}=K(x)
for some algebraically independent system of elements
x
over
K

then
V^{\prime}
is a filtered union of smooth
V
-subalgebras of
V^{\prime}
.

The proof of Theorem
4
is given using Theorem
23
, which is based on Propositions
16
,
18
. A preliminary step of the hard proof of Theorem
23
is given in Lemma
22
using Corollary
17
(and so Proposition
16
). A consequence of Theorem
4
is the next corollary, which is a form of Zariski’s Uniformization Theorem in a special case of
positive characteristic.

Corollary 5
.

Let
V
be a valuation ring containing its residue field
k
with a finitely generated value group
\Gamma
and
K
its fraction field. Assume that
K=k(x,y)
for some algebraically independent elements
x=(x_{i})_{i\in I},y=(y_{j})_{j\in J}
over
k
such that
\mathrm{val}(y)
is a basis in
\Gamma
.
Then
V
is a filtered union of its smooth
k
-subalgebras.

Another goal is
to state the following theorem extending Theorem
3
.

Theorem 6
.

Let
V\subset V^{\prime}
be an immediate extension of valuation rings containing a field. Then
V^{\prime}
is a filtered
union of its complete intersection
V
-subalgebras of finite type.

Corollary 7
.

Let
V
be a valuation ring containing its residue field
k
with a free value group
\Gamma

\Gamma

Then
V
is a filtered union of its complete intersection
k

Theorem

1.
Some extensions of an Ostrowski’s lemma

Let
V
be a valuation ring,
\Gamma
its value group,
\mathrm{val}
its valuation,
\lambda
a fixed limit ordinal and
v=\{v_{i}\}_{1\leq i<\lambda}
a sequence of elements in
V
indexed by the ordinals
i
less than
\lambda
. Then
v
is
pseudo convergent
if

\mathrm{val}(v_{i}-v_{i^{\prime\prime}})<\mathrm{val}(v_{i^{\prime}}-v_{i^{\prime\prime}})\ \ \mbox{for}\ \ 1\leq i<i^{\prime}<i^{\prime\prime}<\lambda

(see
[
5
]
,
[
13
]
).
A
pseudo limit
of
v
is an element
w\in V
with

\mathrm{val}(w-v_{i})<\mathrm{val}(w-v_{i^{\prime}})\ \ \mbox{(that is,}\ \ \mathrm{val}(w-v_{i})=\mathrm{val}(v_{i}-v_{i^{\prime}})\ \ \mbox{for}\ \ 1\leq i<i^{\prime}<\lambda
.
When for any
\gamma\in\Gamma
it holds
\mathrm{val}(v_{i}-v_{i^{\prime}})>\gamma
for
i<i^{\prime}<\lambda

large, that is for
i^{\prime}>i>\nu
for some
\nu<\lambda
we call
v

fundamental
. In this case a pseudo limit of
v
is called
l
imit.

We say that
v
is

(1)

algebraic
if some
f\in V[T]
satisfies
\mathrm{val}(f(v_{i}))<\mathrm{val}(f(v_{i^{\prime}}))
for large enough
1\leq i<i^{\prime}<\lambda
;

(2)

transcendental
if each
f\in V[T]
satisfies
\mathrm{val}(f(v_{i}))=\mathrm{val}(f(v_{i^{\prime}}))
for large enough
1\leq i<i^{\prime}<\lambda
.

The following lemma is a variant of Ostrowski’s lemma (
[
6
, page 371, IV and III]
, see especially
[
13
, (II,4), Lemma 8]
).

Lemma 8
.

(Ostrowski) Let
\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{m}
,
m>1
be any elements of an ordered abelian group
G
,
\lambda
a limit ordinal and
let
\{\gamma_{s}\}_{1\leq s<\lambda}
be an increasing sequence of elements of G. Let
t_{1},\ldots,t_{m}
, be distinct integers.
Then the following statements hold

(1)

There exist an ordinal
1\leq\nu<\lambda
and an integer
1\leq r\leq m
such that

\beta_{i}+t_{i}\gamma_{s}>\beta_{r}+t_{r}\gamma_{s}

for all
i\not=r
and
s>\nu
.

(2)

There exists
an ordinal
1\leq\nu<\lambda
such that
(\beta_{i}+t_{i}\gamma_{s})_{1\leq i\leq m}
are different for all
s>\nu
.

(3)

If the integers
t_{1},\ldots,t_{m}
, are not distinct but

\beta_{i}\not=\beta_{j}
,
i\not=j
whenever
t_{i}=t_{j}
then there exists
an ordinal
1\leq\nu<\lambda
such that
(\beta_{i}+t_{i}\gamma_{s})_{1\leq i\leq m}
are different for all
s>\nu
.

Proof.

The proofs from the quoted papers gives the first statement even when the integers
t_{i}
are not necessarily positive. The second statement follows
applying iteratively the first one.

We need only (2), (3) in our paper and so we give a proof only for these two. Assume that
t_{1}<\ldots<t_{m}
. If
m=2
then there exists
1\leq\nu<\lambda
such that
(t_{2}-t_{1})\gamma_{s}\not=\beta_{1}-\beta_{2}
for all
s>\nu
, which ends the case
m=2
. Apply induction on
m
. Let
m>2
. By induction hypothesis there exists
1\leq\nu_{1}<\lambda
such that
(\beta_{i}+t_{i}\gamma_{s})_{1\leq i<m}
are different for all
s>\nu_{1}
. On the other hand, there exists
1\leq\nu_{2}<\lambda
such that
(t_{m}-t_{i})\gamma_{s}\not=\beta_{i}-\beta_{m}
for all
s>\nu_{2}
and
1\leq i<m
. Then
\nu=\operatorname{max}\{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}\}
works for (2).

For (3) apply induction on
m
. Let
m>2
, the case
m=2
being trivial.
Assume that if
t_{i}=t_{j}
for some
i\not=j
then
\beta_{i}\not=\beta_{j}
. By induction hypothesis we assume that there exists
1\leq\nu_{1}<\lambda
such that
(\beta_{e}+t_{e}\gamma_{s})_{1\leq e<m}
are different, if
s>\nu_{1}
. On the other hand, we see that there exists
1\leq\nu_{2}<\lambda
such that
\beta_{m}+t_{m}\gamma_{s}\not=\beta_{j}+t_{j}\gamma_{s}
for all
j\not=m
and
s>\nu_{2}
because
\beta_{m}\not=\beta_{j}
if
t_{m}=t_{j}
and so
(\beta_{e}+t_{e}\gamma_{s})_{1\leq e\leq m}
are different for
s>\nu=\operatorname{max}\{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}\}
.

∎

Lemma 9
.

Let
\beta_{0},\beta_{1}

Then there exist a subset

(1)

P_{0,j_{0}}=\beta_{0}+\gamma_{0,j_{0}},

(2)

P_{1,j_{1}}=\beta_{1}+\gamma_{1,j_{1}}

are different for all

j_{1}\not=\sigma(j_{0})

.

Proof.

If

for all
1\leq j_{0},j_{1}<\lambda
then take

and there exists nothing to show. Otherwise, let

be such that

. If

for some
(j_{0}^{\prime},j_{1}^{\prime})\not=(j_{0},j_{1})
then we get

\gamma_{0,j_{0}^{\prime}}-\gamma_{0,j_{0}}=\gamma_{1,j_{1}^{\prime}}-\gamma_{1,j_{1}}=\gamma_{0,j_{1}^{\prime}}-\gamma_{0,j_{1}}
. Clearly, for fix
j_{0}^{\prime}

Lemma 22

the maximal ideals of

,

their fraction field extension and

Let

be a nonzero polynomial and some

and an unit

. Then there exists
a smooth

-subalgebra of

containing

.

Proof.

By

, Theorem 1]

y

, which

By Corollary
17
there exist some

Theorem 23
.

Let
V^{\prime}
be an immediate extension of a valuation ring
V
containing a field,
{\mathfrak{m}},{\mathfrak{m}}^{\prime}
the maximal ideals of
V,V^{\prime}
,
K\subset K^{\prime}
their fraction field extension and
y_{0}\in V^{\prime}
an unit, transcendental element over
K
with
K^{\prime}=K(y_{0})
.
Then
V^{\prime}
is a filtered union of its smooth
V
-subalgebras.

Proof.

Let
\rho:V[Y_{0}]\rightarrow V^{\prime}
be the injective map given by
Y_{0}\rightarrow y_{0}
and
f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}\in V[Y_{0}]
nonzero polynomials. Then there exist some
d_{e}\in V\setminus\{0\}
and some units
y_{e}\in V^{\prime}
such that
f_{e}(y_{0})=d_{e}y_{e}
,
1\leq e\leq n
. We claim that there exists
a smooth
V
-subalgebra
A_{f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}}
of
V^{\prime}
containing
(y_{e})
.
By
[
5
, Theorem 1]

y_{e}
is a pseudo limit of a pseudo convergent sequence
v_{e}=(v_{e,j_{e}})_{j_{e}<\lambda_{e}}
, which has no pseudo limits in
K
,
0\leq e\leq n
.
Set
y_{e,j_{e}}=(y_{e}-v_{e,j_{e}})/(v_{e,j_{e}+1}-v_{e,j_{e}})
.

Apply induction on
n
. If
n=1
we apply Lemma
22
.
Assume that
n\geq 2
. Using induction hypothesis on
n
we may suppose after a change of the numbering of
(y_{e})

that there exist
j_{e}<\lambda_{e}
,
0\leq e<n
and
h_{1},\ldots,h_{n-1}\in V[Y_{0,j_{0}},\ldots,Y_{n-1,j_{n-1}}]

such that
h_{i}((y_{e^{\prime},j_{e^{\prime}}}))=0
,
1\leq i<n
and the determinant of the matrix

((\partial h_{i}/\partial Y_{e,j_{e}})((y_{e^{\prime},j_{e^{\prime}}})))_{1\leq i\leq n,0\leq e\leq n-2}

is not in
{\mathfrak{m}}^{\prime}.

As in the proof of Lemma
22
we have to consider two cases. First assume that applying Lemma
22
we arrive in the second case, namely that for some
j_{0}<\lambda_{0}
there exists
h_{1}\in V[Y_{0,j_{0}},Y_{1}]
such that

h_{1}(y_{0,j_{0}},y_{1})=0
and
(\partial h_{1}/\partial Y_{0,j_{0}})(y_{0,j_{0}},y_{1})\not\in{\mathfrak{m}}^{\prime}
.

Note that
y_{n}
is transcendental over
K
because
y_{0}
is so. Since
y_{0}
is algebraic over
K(y_{n})
and
y_{n-1}\in K(y_{0})
we see that
y_{n-1}
is algebraic over
K(y_{n})
. Thus there exists a nonzero polynomial
g_{n-1,n}\in K[Y_{n-1},Y_{n}]
with
g_{n-1,n}(y_{n-1},y_{n})=0
. Clearly, we may choose
g_{n-1,n}\in V[Y_{n-1},Y_{n}]
.

By Corollary
20

there exist some
j_{n-1}<t_{n-1}<\lambda_{n-1}
,
t_{n}<\lambda_{n}
and a polynomial
h_{n-1,n}\in V[Y_{n-1,t_{n-1}},Y_{n,t_{n}}]

such that

h_{n-1,n}-h_{n-1,n}(0)=c_{n-1}Y_{n-1,t_{n-1}}+c_{n}Y_{n,t_{n}}+h_{n-1,n}^{\prime}

for some
c_{n-1},c_{n}\in V
, at least one of them nonzero, and

h_{n-1,n}^{\prime}\in(c_{n-1},c_{n}){\mathfrak{m}}V[Y_{n-1,t_{n-1}},Y_{n,t_{n}}]

and

h_{n-1,n}(Y_{n-1,t_{n-1}},Y_{n,t_{n}})
corresponds to one of the following polynomials
g_{n-1,n}(Y_{n-1},Y_{n}),

Y_{n-1}g_{n-1,n}(Y_{n-1},Y_{n})
,

Y_{n}g_{n-1,n}(Y_{n-1},Y_{n})
,

Y_{n-1}Y_{n}g_{n-1,n}(Y_{n-1},Y_{n})
.
Set
t_{q}=j_{q}
for
0\leq q\leq n-2
.
The change from
j_{e}
to
t_{e}
will modify a little the coefficients of
h_{i}
,
1\leq i<n-1
but their forms remain.

Assume that
c_{n-1}\not=0
and
\mathrm{val}(c_{n-1})\leq\mathrm{val}(c_{n})

then
c_{n-1}|h_{n-1,n}(0)
because

h_{n-1,n}(y_{n-1,t_{n-1}},y_{n,t_{n}})=0
and we may change

h_{n-1,n}
by
h_{n-1}=h_{n-1,n}/c_{n-1}
independently if it was obtained from
g_{n-1,n}(Y_{n-1},Y_{n})
, or
Y_{n-1}g_{n-1,n}(Y_{n-1},Y_{n})
, or
Y_{n}g_{n-1,n}(Y_{n-1},Y_{n})
, or
Y_{n-1}Y_{n}g_{n-1,n}(Y_{n-1},Y_{n})
. Thus a minor of maximal rank of the matrix

((\partial h_{i}/\partial Y_{e,t_{e}})((y_{e^{\prime},t_{e^{\prime}}})))_{1\leq i\leq n,0\leq e<n}
is not contained in
{\mathfrak{m}}^{\prime}
.
By Lemma
21
we get a smooth
V
-subalgebra

A_{f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}}=V[y_{0,t_{0}},\ldots,y_{n,t_{n}}]_{{\mathfrak{m}}^{\prime}\cap V[y_{0,t_{0}},\ldots,y_{n,t_{n}}}
of
V^{\prime}
containing
(y_{e,t_{e}})
and so all
y_{e}
.
Certainly, we could have above also
c_{n}\not=0
and
\mathrm{val}(c_{n-1})>\mathrm{val}(c_{n})

in which case change
f_{n}
with
f_{n-1}
. Hence our claim is proved.

Now assume that applying Lemma
22
we arrive in the first case of the proof, namely
y_{1}\in V[y_{0,j_{0}}]_{{\mathfrak{m}}^{\prime}\cap V[y_{0,j_{0}}]}
for some
j_{0}<\lambda_{0}
. Then we may replace
y_{0}
by

(v_{0,j_{0}+1}-v_{0,j_{0}})y_{0}+v_{0,j_{0}}

and we may omit
y_{1}
, that is we denote
y_{e}
by
y_{e-1}
for
1<e\leq n
. So we arrive in the case
n-1
when we apply the induction hypothesis to show our claim.

The family
(A_{f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}})
given by all finite subsets
\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}\}
of nonzero polynomials of
V[Y_{0}]
is filtered and consider
their union
\mathcal{A}
. We claim that
V^{\prime}={\mathcal{A}}
. Indeed,
let
f_{1}(y_{0})/f_{2}(y_{0})
be a rational function from
V^{\prime}
(a general element of
V^{\prime}
has the form
f_{1}(y_{0})/f_{2}(y_{0})
with
f_{i}\in V[Y_{0}]
and

\mathrm{val}(f_{1}(y_{0}))\geq\mathrm{val}(f_{2}(y_{0}))
). We can assume
f_{i}(y_{0})=d_{i}y_{i}
,
i=1,2
, for some
d_{i}\in V\setminus\{0\}
and some units
y_{i}\in V^{\prime}
,
i=1,2
. For some
t_{e}<\lambda_{e}
,
0\leq e\leq 2
we find as above a smooth
V
-subalgebra
A_{f_{1},f_{2}}
of
V^{\prime}
containing
y_{0,t_{0}}
,
y_{1,t_{1}}
,
y_{2,t_{2}}
and so containing

(y_{e})
,
0\leq e\leq 2
.
As

\mathrm{val}(d_{1})\geq\mathrm{val}(d_{2})
because
f_{1}(y_{0})/f_{2}(y_{0})\in V^{\prime}
we get

f_{1}(y_{0})/f_{2}(y_{0})=(d_{1}/d_{2})y_{1}y_{2}^{-1}\in A_{f_{1},f_{2}}\subset{\mathcal{A}}.

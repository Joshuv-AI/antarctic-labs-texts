An enriched hybrid high-order method for the Stokes problem with application to flow around submerged cylinders
Liam Yemm

Abstract
An enriched hybrid high-order method is designed for the Stokes equations of fluid flow and is fully applicable to generic curved meshes. Minimal regularity requirements of the enrichment spaces are given, and an abstract error analysis of the scheme is provided. The method achieves consistency in the enrichment space and is proven to converge optimally in energy error. The scheme is applied to 2D flow around circular cylinders, for which the local behaviour of the velocity and pressure fields are known. By enriching the local spaces with these solutions, superior numerical results near the submerged cylinders are achieved. Key words: hybrid high-order methods, enriched scheme, error analysis, curved meshes. MSC2010: 65N12, 65N15, 65N30.



1
Introduction

In this paper, an enriched hybrid high-order scheme is developed for solving the Stokes equations. Such a method is particularly well-suited for situations where certain aspects of the solution can be determined, at least locally. By achieving consistency in these components of the solution, improved approximation rates can be expected. Moreover, high-order methods require high-order regularity to achieve optimal approximation. In the case of singular solutions, conventional methods fail to accurately capture the singularity and optimal convergence is lost. By enriching the local polynomial spaces with the singular components of the solution, optimal approximation rates are recovered. One common scenario where the exact solution lacks regularity is near corners in the domain
[
14
]
.

The enrichment of finite element schemes is typically achieved following a partition of unity approach
[
16
,
3
]
. A model for crack growth was described by
[
4
]
, and similarly by
[
17
]
, utilising the partition of unity functions to enrich the local spaces with the asymptotic solution around the crack. There have been numerous studies of enriched finite element methods for the Stokes equations. The earliest work on this topic appears to be that of
[
19
]
, which focuses on rigid particles suspended in a fluid. They developed an extended finite element method (XFEM) with polynomial spaces enriched with single-particle solutions in order to better approximate the solutions to multiple-particle flows.
The XFEM has also been used in the study of multi-phase flows
[
7
,
23
]
, aiming to capture velocity and pressure discontinuities at the fluid interfaces.
[
15
]
have applied the XFEM to mixed formulations for the treatment of holes, material inclusions, and cracks in the incompressible limit.

Within the framework of polytopal methods, an enriched virtual element method (VEM) for the Poisson problem has been proposed
[
5
]
, however, only the lowest order VEM was considered and no estimates of the error were given. The same authors have proposed an enriched VEM for a linear elasticity fracture problem
[
6
]
, however, this work also lacks an error analysis. The work of
[
2
]
designs an enriched non-conforming virtual element method (NC-VEM) for harmonic singularities arising from irregular domains in two dimensions. Moreover, the enriched NC-VEM is capable of handling highly irregular harmonic singularities, including those arising from cracked domains. However, the analysis requires a discrete trace inequality
[
2
, Equation (49)]
, which depends on the singular function. Following this approach, the same authors have defined an enriched NC-VEM for a plane elasticity problem with corner singularities
[
1
]
. An extended hybrid high-order method for the Poisson problem which avoids the use of discrete inequalities dependent on the enrichment function is provided by
[
21
]
. Notably, despite these advancements, no attempts have been made to design an enriched polytopal scheme for the Stokes equations.

Proposed by
[
9
,
10
]
, the hybrid high-order (HHO) method is a modern polytopal method for the discretisation of elliptic partial differential equations. HHO methods allow for arbitrary order approximation rates and are constructed to better represent the underlying physics. One of their key benefits is the facilitation of static condensation of the system matrix, which leads to a substantial reduction in globally coupled degrees of freedom. An overview of the design, analysis and applications of the HHO method is provided by
[
8
]
.

In this paper, an enriched hybrid high-order method for the Stokes equation is designed following the approach of
[
21
]
for the Poisson problem. However, due to the requirement to handle an additional pressure singularity - which is coupled to the velocity singularity - and the need to define an
\inf
–
\sup
scheme, the analysis is more involved. Given enrichment spaces satisfying Assumption
2
, an enriched method is designed and shown to be well-posed and achieve optimal error estimates in energy norm. Moreover, the method of
[
20
]
of defining polynomials on curved faces is followed, leading to the scheme proposed in this paper being valid for highly generic curved meshes.

The singularities which arise near corners of the domain in the Poisson problem have zero Laplacian. As such, the requirement of
[
21
, Assumption 2]
for the singularities to have square-integrable Laplacian is satisfied trivially for such cases, and the element polynomial spaces do not require enrichment. However, due to the nature of the Stokes problem, the singularities arising from corners of the domain do not have zero Laplacian, but rather have Laplacian equal to the gradient of the pressure singularity. As such, the assumption of square-integrable Laplacian is not trivially satisfied. Indeed, it is found that Assumption
2
is only satisfied for singularities arising from convex corners. For non-convex corners a different approach is required. As such, corner singularities are not considered. Instead, the method is tested on creeping flow around circular cylinders in Section
5
. The results show that by enriching the spaces with relevant functions describing the local behaviour of the velocity and pressure around the cylinders, the scheme performs much better than a classical HHO scheme.

The paper is organised as follows: in Section
2
, the Stokes problem and its enriched HHO discretisation is presented. The main results are stated in Section
3
and proven in Section
4
. The asymptotic behaviour of Stokes flow around submerged cylinders is derived in Section
5
, and an application of the enriched HHO scheme for flow around cylinders is given in Sections
5.1
and
5.2
.

Model Problem and HHO Discretisation

Given a bounded Lipschitz domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
d\geq 2

{\bm{u}}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
and the pressure
p:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
that satisfy,

\displaystyle-\nu{\bm{\Delta}}{\bm{u}}+\nabla p={}

\displaystyle{\bm{f}}

\displaystyle\textrm{in }\Omega,

(2.1a)

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot{\bm{u}}={}

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\textrm{in }\Omega,

(2.1b)

\displaystyle{\bm{u}}={}

\displaystyle\bm{0}

\displaystyle\textrm{on }\partial\Omega,

(2.1c)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}p={}

\displaystyle 0,

(2.1d)

where
{\bm{f}}\in L^{2}(\Omega)^{d}
is a prescribed volumetric force and
\nu>0
denotes a constant viscous term. Consider the following weak formulation: find
{\bm{u}}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)^{d}
,
p\in L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
such that

\displaystyle\nu{\rm{a}}({\bm{u}},{\bm{v}})+{\rm{b}}({\bm{v}},p)={}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}{\bm{f}}\cdot{\bm{v}}\quad

\displaystyle\forall

\displaystyle{\bm{v}}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)^{d},

(2.2a)

\displaystyle-{\rm{b}}({\bm{u}},q)={}

\displaystyle 0\quad

\displaystyle\forall

\displaystyle q\in L^{2}(\Omega),

(2.2b)

where
L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
denotes the subspace of functions in
L^{2}(\Omega)
with zero mean value, and the bilinear forms,
{\rm{a}}:H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)^{d}\times H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
and
{\rm{b}}:H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)^{d}\times L^{2}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}

{\rm{a}}({\bm{w}},{\bm{v}})\vcentcolon=\int_{\Omega}\nabla{\bm{w}}:\nabla{\bm{v}}\quad;\quad{\rm{b}}({\bm{v}},q)\vcentcolon=-\int_{\Omega}(\nabla\cdot{\bm{v}})q,

where, for a differentiable vector-valued function
{\bm{w}}
,
(\nabla{\bm{w}})_{ij}=\partial_{j}w_{i}

Let
\mathcal{H}\subset(0,\infty)
be a countable set of mesh sizes with a unique cluster point at
0
. For each
h\in\mathcal{H}
, partition the domain
\Omega
into a

\mathcal{M}_{h}=(\mathcal{T}_{h},\mathcal{F}_{h})
, where
\mathcal{T}_{h}
denotes the mesh elements and
\mathcal{F}_{h}

The mesh elements
\mathcal{T}_{h}
are a disjoint set of open, bounded, simply connected regions in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with piece-wise

\partial T

\overline{\Omega}=\bigcup_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\overline{T}
.

The mesh faces
\mathcal{F}_{h}
are a disjoint set of non-intersecting finite

\bigcup_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\partial T=\bigcup_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}\overline{F}
, and for each
F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}
there either exists two distinct elements
T_{1},T_{2}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
such that
F\subset\partial T_{1}\cap\partial T_{2}
and
F
is called an internal face, or there exists one element
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
such that
F\subset\partial T\cap\partial\Omega
and
F
is called a boundary face. The set of interior faces are collected in the set
\mathcal{F}_{h}^{i}
and boundary faces in the set
\mathcal{F}_{h}^{b}
.

The parameter
h
is given by
h\vcentcolon=\max_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{T}
where, for
X=T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
or
X=F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}
,
h_{X}
denotes the diameter of
X
. The set of faces attached to an element
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
are collected in the set
\mathcal{F}_{T}:=\{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}:F\subset\partial T\}
and the set of mesh elements attached to a face
F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}
are collected in the set
\mathcal{T}_{F}\vcentcolon==\{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}:F\subset\partial T\}
. The unit normal to
F\in\mathcal{F}_{T}
pointing outside
T
is denoted by
{\bm{n}}_{{T}{F}}
.

Consider the following regularity assumption on the mesh elements.

Assumption 1

(Regular mesh sequence)
.

There exists a constant
\varrho>0
such that, for each
h\in\mathcal{H}
, every
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
is connected by star-shaped sets with parameter

(see

Given a mesh sequence satisfying Assumption

, the following continuous trace inequality holds

12
]
:

Lemma 1

(Continuous trace inequality)
.

For all

, it holds,

h_{T}\|v\|_{\partial T}^{2}\lesssim\|v\|_{T}^{2}+h_{T}^{2}\|\nabla v\|_{T}^{2},

(2.3)

where the hidden constant depends on the mesh regularity parameter
\varrho
and the dimension

.

To handle the singular solutions that may arise due to the geometry of the domain or in the presence of irregular data, assume that both the velocity and pressure can be decomposed into a ‘regular part’ and a ‘singular part’ as follows:

{\bm{u}}={\bm{u}}_{r}+{\bm{\psi}}\quad;\quad p=p_{r}+\phi,

where
{\bm{u}}_{r}\in H^{k+2}(\mathcal{T}_{h})^{d}
and
p_{r}\in H^{k+1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
. The singular components
{\bm{\psi}}
and

are members of
{\bm{\Psi}}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
and
\Phi(\mathcal{T}_{h})
, respectively, where
{\bm{\Psi}}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
and
\Phi(\mathcal{T}_{h})
are finite dimensional enrichment spaces satisfying the following assumption.

Assumption 2

(Assumptions on the enrichment spaces)
.

1.

Assumptions on

: For every

,

(

: For every

,

(
P1
)

\Phi(T)\subset H^{1}(T)
.

2.1
Discretisation of the Velocity

For an integer
\ell\geq 0
, the space of scalar-valued and vector-valued polynomials on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
of degree at most
\ell
are denoted by
\mathcal{P}^{\ell}_{d}
and
{\bm{\mathcal{P}}}^{\ell}_{d}
, respectively. Their restrictions to an element
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
are denoted by
\mathcal{P}^{\ell}(T)=\mathcal{P}^{\ell}_{d}|_{T}
and
{\bm{\mathcal{P}}}^{\ell}(T)={\bm{\mathcal{P}}}^{\ell}_{d}|_{T}
.

In order to discretise the velocity unknowns, the following spaces and operators are introduced. For each element
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
and an integer
k\geq 0
, define the extended polynomial space

{\bm{\mathcal{P}}}_{{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k+1}(T)\vcentcolon={\bm{\mathcal{P}}}^{k+1}(T)+{\bm{\Psi}}(T)\subset H^{1}(T)^{d}.

(2.4)

To project onto the extended space
{\bm{\mathcal{P}}}_{{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k+1}(T)
, the extended elliptic projector
{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k+1}:H^{1}(T)^{d}\to{\bm{\mathcal{P}}}_{{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k+1}(T)
is introduced. This projector is uniquely defined such that for all
v\in H^{1}(T)^{d}
,

\int_{T}\nabla({\bm{v}}-{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k+1}{\bm{v}}):\nabla{\bm{w}}=0\qquad\forall\,{\bm{w}}\in{\bm{\mathcal{P}}}_{{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k+1}(T),

(2.5)

and

\int_{T}({\bm{v}}-{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k+1}{\bm{v}})=\bm{0}.

(2.6)

On curved faces, the space of vector-valued polynomials is defined analogously to the scalar case
[
20
, Equation (2.2)]
as

{\bm{\mathcal{P}}}^{k}(F)\vcentcolon={\bm{\mathcal{P}}}^{0}_{d}|_{F}+(\mathcal{P}^{k}_{d})^{d\times d}|_{F}{\bm{n}}_{{F}},

(2.7)

where
{\bm{n}}_{{F}}
is an arbitrary unit normal to the face
F
, and
(\mathcal{P}^{k}_{d})^{d\times d}
denotes the space of tensor-valued polynomials on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The choice of unit normal
{\bm{n}}_{{F}}
does not affect the definition of
{\bm{\mathcal{P}}}^{k}(F)
. If the face
F
is planar, the definition (
2.7
) of face polynomials reduces to

{\bm{\mathcal{P}}}^{k}(F)={\bm{\mathcal{P}}}^{k}_{d}|_{F}.

The locally enriched element and face spaces are defined for each
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
and
F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}
as

{\bm{\mathcal{P}}}_{{\bm{\Delta}}}^{k}(T)\vcentcolon={\bm{\mathcal{P}}}^{k}(T)+{\bm{\Delta}}{\bm{\Psi}}(T)+\nabla\Phi(T),

and

{\bm{\mathcal{P}}}_{\partial_{{\bm{n}}}}^{k}(F)\vcentcolon={\bm{\mathcal{P}}}^{k}(F)+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{F}}\Big(\nabla{\bm{\Psi}}(T){\bm{n}}_{{T}{F}}+\Phi(T){\bm{n}}_{{T}{F}}\Big).

Due to the assumptions on the singular spaces, it holds that:

{\bm{\mathcal{P}}}_{{\bm{\Delta}}}^{k}(T)\subset L^{2}(T)^{d}\quad\textrm{and}\quad{\bm{\mathcal{P}}}_{\partial_{{\bm{n}}}}^{k}(F)\subset L^{2}(F)^{d}.

Define the local discrete velocity space via:

\underline{\bm{U}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k}:={\bm{\mathcal{P}}}_{{\bm{\Delta}}}^{k}(T)\times\bigtimes_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{T}}{\bm{\mathcal{P}}}_{\partial_{{\bm{n}}}}^{k}(F).

(2.8)

The local interpolator
\underline{\bm{I}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k}:H^{1}(T)^{d}\to\underline{\bm{U}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k}
is defined as:

\underline{\bm{I}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k}{\bm{v}}=({\bm{\Pi}}_{{T},{\bm{\Delta}}}^{k}{\bm{v}},({\bm{\Pi}}_{{F},\partial_{{\bm{n}}}}^{k}{\bm{v}})_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{T}}),

(2.9)

where
{\bm{\Pi}}_{{T},{\bm{\Delta}}}^{k}
and
{\bm{\Pi}}_{{F},\partial_{{\bm{n}}}}^{k}
denote the
L^{2}
-orthogonal projectors on the spaces
{\bm{\mathcal{P}}}_{{\bm{\Delta}}}^{k}(T)
and
{\bm{\mathcal{P}}}_{\partial_{{\bm{n}}}}^{k}(F)
, respectively. For each element

, the local reconstruction operator
{\bm{{\rm r}}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k+1}:\underline{\bm{U}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k}\to{\bm{\mathcal{P}}}_{{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k+1}(T)
is introduced, which satisfies the following equations for every
\underline{\bm{v}}_{T}=({\bm{v}}_{T},({\bm{v}}_{F})_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{T}})\in\underline{\bm{U}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k}

\int_{T}\nabla{\bm{{\rm r}}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k+1}\underline{\bm{v}}_{T}:\nabla{\bm{w}}=-\int_{T}{\bm{v}}_{T}\cdot{\bm{\Delta}}{\bm{w}}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{T}}\int_{F}{\bm{v}}_{F}\cdot(\nabla{\bm{w}}){\bm{n}}_{{T}{F}}\qquad\forall{\bm{w}}\in{\bm{\mathcal{P}}}_{{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k+1}(T),

(2.10a)

and

\int_{T}({\bm{{\rm r}}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k+1}\underline{\bm{v}}_{T}-{\bm{v}}_{T})=\bm{0}.

(2.10b)

Note that the

–regularity of

is designed to mimic an integration by parts of equation (

Lemma 2
.

The following identity holds true:

{\bm{{\rm r}}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k+1}\circ\underline{\bm{I}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k}={\bm{\Sigma}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k+1}.

(2.11)

Proof.

Applying equation (

and

where the final inequality is derived by integrating by parts.
Substituting

(2.13)

As the elliptic projector is uniquely defined, and equations (

Due to the lack of

–regularity of the element space

Proof.

Consider equation (
2.17

\underline{\bm{v}}_{T}=\underline{\bm{I}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k}{\bm{w}}
and
{\bm{w}}\in{\bm{\mathcal{P}}}_{{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k+1}(T)
:

{\rm{s}}_{T}(\underline{\bm{I}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k}{\bm{w}},\underline{\bm{I}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k}{\bm{w}})\approx\|\underline{\bm{I}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k}{\bm{w}}-\underline{\bm{I}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k}{\bm{{\rm r}}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k+1}\underline{\bm{I}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k}w\|_{1,T,{\bm{\Psi}}}^{2}=\|\underline{\bm{I}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k}{\bm{w}}-\underline{\bm{I}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k}{\bm{w}}\|_{1,T,{\bm{\Psi}}}^{2}=0,

where the second equality follows from
{\bm{{\rm r}}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k+1}\underline{\bm{I}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k}{\bm{w}}={\bm{\Sigma}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k+1}{\bm{w}}={\bm{w}}
due to the commutation property (
2.11
) and the invariance of
{\bm{\Sigma}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k+1}
on
{\bm{\mathcal{P}}}_{{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k+1}(T)
. As
{\rm{s}}_{T}
is assumed to be bilinear and symmetric positive semi-definite, the following Cauchy–Schwarz inequality holds for all
\underline{\bm{v}}_{T}\in\underline{\bm{U}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k}
and
{\bm{w}}\in{\bm{\mathcal{P}}}_{{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k+1}(T)
:

|{\rm{s}}_{T}(\underline{\bm{v}}_{T},\underline{\bm{I}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k}{\bm{w}})|\leq{\rm{s}}_{T}(\underline{\bm{v}}_{T},\underline{\bm{v}}_{T})^{\frac{1}{2}}{\rm{s}}_{T}(\underline{\bm{I}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k}{\bm{w}},\underline{\bm{I}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k}{\bm{w}})^{\frac{1}{2}}=0,

thus proving (
2.18
). Equation (
2.19
) follows from expanding

{\rm{s}}_{T}(\underline{\bm{v}}_{T}-\underline{\bm{I}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k}{\bm{{\rm r}}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k+1}\underline{\bm{v}}_{T},\underline{\bm{w}}_{T}-\underline{\bm{I}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k}{\bm{{\rm r}}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k+1}\underline{\bm{w}}_{T})

due to the bilinearity of
{\rm{s}}_{T}
and applying equation (
2.18
). To prove (
2.20
) consider equation (
2.15
) and apply the assumption (
2.17
):

\displaystyle\|\underline{\bm{v}}_{T}\|_{1,T,{\bm{\Psi}}}^{2}={}

\displaystyle\|\underline{\bm{I}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k}{\bm{{\rm r}}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k+1}\underline{\bm{v}}_{T}\|_{1,T,{\bm{\Psi}}}^{2}+\|\underline{\bm{v}}_{T}-\underline{\bm{I}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k}{\bm{{\rm r}}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k+1}\underline{\bm{v}}_{T}\|_{1,T,{\bm{\Psi}}}^{2}

\displaystyle\approx{}

\displaystyle\|\underline{\bm{I}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k}{\bm{{\rm r}}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k+1}\underline{\bm{v}}_{T}\|_{1,T,{\bm{\Psi}}}^{2}+{\rm{s}}_{T}(\underline{\bm{v}}_{T},\underline{\bm{v}}_{T})

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\|\nabla{\bm{{\rm r}}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k+1}\underline{\bm{v}}_{T}\|_{T}^{2}+{\rm{s}}_{T}(\underline{\bm{v}}_{T},\underline{\bm{v}}_{T})={\rm{a}}_{T}(\underline{\bm{v}}_{T},\underline{\bm{v}}_{T}),

thus completing the proof.
∎

The stabilisation bilinear form,

{\rm{s}}_{T}(\underline{\bm{u}}_{T},\underline{\bm{v}}_{T})\vcentcolon=h_{T}^{-2}\int_{T}({\bm{u}}_{T}-{\bm{\Pi}}_{T}^{k}{\bm{{\rm r}}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k+1}\underline{\bm{u}}_{T})\cdot({\bm{v}}_{T}-{\bm{\Pi}}_{T}^{k}{\bm{{\rm r}}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k+1}\underline{\bm{v}}_{T})\\
+h_{T}^{-1}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{T}}\int_{F}({\bm{u}}_{F}-{\bm{\Pi}}_{F}^{k}{\bm{{\rm r}}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k+1}\underline{\bm{u}}_{T})\cdot({\bm{v}}_{F}-{\bm{\Pi}}_{F}^{k}{\bm{{\rm r}}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k+1}\underline{\bm{v}}_{T}),

(2.21)

is considered throughout the rest of this paper. It follows trivially that
{\rm{s}}_{T}
satisfies Assumption
3
. Indeed,

{\rm{s}}_{T}(\underline{\bm{v}}_{T},\underline{\bm{v}}_{T})=\|\underline{\bm{v}}_{T}-\underline{\bm{I}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k}{\bm{{\rm r}}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k+1}\underline{\bm{v}}_{T}\|_{1,T,{\bm{\Psi}}}^{2}.

2.2
Discretisation of the Pressure

The local discrete pressure space is defined as:

\mathcal{P}_{\Phi}^{k}(T):=\mathcal{P}^{k}(T)+\Phi(T).

The divergence reconstruction
{\rm{D}}_{{T},\Phi}^{k}:\underline{\bm{U}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k}\to\mathcal{P}_{\Phi}^{k}(T)
is defined to satisfy

\int_{T}{\rm{D}}_{{T},\Phi}^{k}\underline{\bm{v}}_{T}q=-\int_{T}{\bm{v}}_{T}\cdot\nabla q+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{T}}\int_{F}{\bm{v}}_{F}\cdot q{\bm{n}}_{{T}}\qquad\forall q\in\mathcal{P}_{\Phi}^{k}(T).

(2.22)

Similar to the velocity reconstruction, the divergence reconstruction is designed to produce the projection of the divergence when commuted with the interpolator. This is clarified in the following lemma.

Denote by
\Pi_{{T},\Phi}^{k}
the
L^{2}
-orthogonal projector on the space
\mathcal{P}_{\Phi}^{k}(T)
.

Lemma 4
.

The following identity holds true:

{\rm{D}}_{{T},\Phi}^{k}\circ\underline{\bm{I}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k}=\Pi_{{T},\Phi}^{k}\DIV.

(2.23)

Proof.

Substituting
\underline{\bm{v}}_{T}=\underline{\bm{I}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k}{\bm{v}}
into equation (
2.22
) yields

\int_{T}{\rm{D}}_{{T},\Phi}^{k}\underline{\bm{I}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k}{\bm{v}}q=-\int_{T}{\bm{\Pi}}_{{T},{\bm{\Delta}}}^{k}{\bm{v}}\cdot\nabla q+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{T}}\int_{F}{\bm{\Pi}}_{{F},\partial_{{\bm{n}}}}^{k}{\bm{v}}\cdot q{\bm{n}}_{{T}}\qquad\forall q\in\mathcal{P}_{\Phi}^{k}(T).

(2.24)

Analogously to the proof of Lemma
2
, the
L^{2}
-orthogonal projectors
{\bm{\Pi}}_{{T},{\bm{\Delta}}}^{k}
and
{\bm{\Pi}}_{{F},\partial_{{\bm{n}}}}^{k}
may be removed due to the inclusions
\nabla\mathcal{P}_{\Phi}^{k}(T)\subset{\bm{\mathcal{P}}}_{{\bm{\Delta}}}^{k}(T)
and
\mathcal{P}_{\Phi}^{k}(T){\bm{n}}_{{T}{F}}\subset{\bm{\mathcal{P}}}_{\partial_{{\bm{n}}}}^{k}(F)
to yield,

\int_{T}{\rm{D}}_{{T},\Phi}^{k}\underline{\bm{I}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k}{\bm{v}}q=-\int_{T}{\bm{v}}\cdot\nabla q+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{T}}\int_{F}{\bm{v}}\cdot q{\bm{n}}_{{T}}=\int_{T}\DIV{\bm{v}}q=\int_{T}\Pi_{{T},\Phi}^{k}\DIV{\bm{v}}q,

(2.25)

which follows by integrating by parts and introducing the projector
\Pi_{{T},\Phi}^{k}
. Rearranging and setting
q={\rm{D}}_{{T},\Phi}^{k}\underline{\bm{v}}_{T}-\Pi_{{T},\Phi}^{k}\DIV{\bm{v}}\in\mathcal{P}_{\Phi}^{k}(T)
, it is inferred that

\int_{T}({\rm{D}}_{{T},\Phi}^{k}\underline{\bm{I}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k}{\bm{v}}-\Pi_{{T},\Phi}^{k}\DIV{\bm{v}})^{2}=0,

as required.
∎

The discretisation of the continuous form
{\rm{b}}
is realised through the local discrete bilinear form
{\rm{b}}_{T}:\underline{\bm{U}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k}\times\mathcal{P}_{\Phi}^{k}(T)\to\mathbb{R}
, defined as:

{\rm{b}}_{T}(\underline{\bm{v}}_{T},q):=-\int_{T}{\rm{D}}_{{T},\Phi}^{k}\underline{\bm{v}}_{T}q.

(2.26)

3
Global Formulation and Main Results

The global discrete space is defined as

\underline{\bm{U}}_{h,{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k}\vcentcolon=\bigtimes_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}{\bm{\mathcal{P}}}_{{\bm{\Delta}}}^{k}(T)\times\bigtimes_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}{\bm{\mathcal{P}}}_{\partial_{{\bm{n}}}}^{k}(F),

and is equipped with the global seminorm
\|\cdot\|_{1,h,{\bm{\Psi}}}:\underline{\bm{U}}_{h,{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}
and interpolator
\underline{\bm{I}}_{h,{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k}:H^{1}(\Omega)^{d}\to\underline{\bm{U}}_{h}^{k}
defined via

\displaystyle\|\underline{\bm{v}}_{h}\|_{1,h,{\bm{\Psi}}}^{2}:={}

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|\underline{\bm{v}}_{T}\|_{1,T,{\bm{\Psi}}}^{2}\qquad\forall\underline{\bm{v}}_{h}\in\underline{\bm{U}}_{h}^{k},

\displaystyle\underline{\bm{I}}_{h,{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k}{\bm{v}}\big|_{T}={}

\displaystyle\underline{\bm{I}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k}{\bm{v}}\qquad\forall{\bm{v}}\in H^{1}(\Omega)^{d}.

Similarly, for all
\underline{\bm{v}}_{h},\underline{\bm{w}}_{h}\in\underline{\bm{U}}_{h,{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k}
and
q_{h}\in\mathcal{P}_{\Phi}^{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
, define

{\rm{a}}_{h}(\underline{\bm{v}}_{h},\underline{\bm{w}}_{h}):=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}{\rm{a}}_{T}(\underline{\bm{v}}_{T},\underline{\bm{w}}_{T}),\qquad{\rm{b}}_{h}(\underline{\bm{v}}_{h},q_{h})\vcentcolon=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}{\rm{b}}_{T}(\underline{\bm{v}}_{T},q_{T}).

(3.1)

The boundary conditions on
{\bm{u}}
are accounted for in the following homogeneous subspace of
\underline{\bm{U}}_{h,{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k}
:

\underline{\bm{U}}_{h,{\bm{\Psi}},0}^{k}:=\Big\{\underline{\bm{v}}_{h}=(({\bm{v}}_{T})_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}},({\bm{v}}_{F})_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}})\in\underline{\bm{U}}_{h,{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k}:{\bm{v}}_{F}=\bm{0}\quad\forall F\subset\partial\Omega\Big\}.

The global discrete pressure space is defined as

\mathcal{P}_{\Phi,0}^{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h})\vcentcolon=\left\{z\in L^{2}(\Omega)\,:\,z_{|T}\in\mathcal{P}_{\Phi}^{k}(T)\quad\forall T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\,,\quad\int_{\Omega}z=0\right\}.

(3.2)

The following coupled space is also defined,

\underline{\bm{X}}_{h,{\bm{\Psi}},\Phi}^{k}\vcentcolon=\underline{\bm{U}}_{h,{\bm{\Psi}},0}^{k}\times\mathcal{P}_{\Phi,0}^{k},

(3.3)

and equipped with the norm,

\|(\underline{\bm{v}}_{h},q_{h})\|_{X,h}^{2}:=\nu\|\underline{\bm{v}}_{h}\|_{1,h,{\bm{\Psi}}}^{2}+\nu^{-1}\|q_{h}\|_{\Omega}^{2}.

(3.4)

The discrete problem then reads: find
(\underline{\bm{u}}_{h},p_{h})\in\underline{\bm{X}}_{h,{\bm{\Psi}},\Phi}^{k}
such that

\displaystyle\nu{\rm{a}}_{h}(\underline{\bm{u}}_{h},\underline{\bm{v}}_{h})+{\rm{b}}_{h}(\underline{\bm{v}}_{h},p_{h})={}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}{\bm{f}}\cdot{\bm{v}}_{h}\qquad\forall\underline{\bm{v}}_{h}\in\underline{\bm{U}}_{h,{\bm{\Psi}},0}^{k},

(3.5a)

\displaystyle-{\rm{b}}_{h}(\underline{\bm{u}}_{h},q_{h})={}

\displaystyle 0\qquad\forall q_{h}\in\mathcal{P}_{\Phi,0}^{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h}).

(3.5b)

The main results are as follows:

Theorem 5

(Existence and uniqueness)
.

There exists a unique solution
(\underline{\bm{u}}_{h},p_{h})\in\underline{\bm{X}}_{h,{\bm{\Psi}},\Phi}^{k}
to problem (
3.5
).

Theorem 6

.

The discrete solution

\|(\underline{\bm{u}}_{h}-\underline{\bm{I}}_{h,{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k}{\bm{u}},p_{h}-\Sigma_{h,\Phi}^{k}p)\|_{X,h}\lesssim h^{k+1}\Big(\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}|{\bm{u}}_{r}|_{H^{k+2}(\mathcal{T}_{h})^{d}}+\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}|p_{r}|_{H^{k+1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}\Big),

(3.6)

where
({\bm{u}},p)=({\bm{u}}_{r}+{\bm{\psi}},p_{r}+\phi)\in H^{1}(\Omega)^{d}\times L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)

2.2
) satisfying the additional regularity
{\bm{\Delta}}{\bm{u}}\in L^{2}(\Omega)^{d}
and
p\in H^{1}(\Omega)
with

4
Proof of Main Results

In this section, the proofs of the main results stated in Section
3
are provided. The proof of these estimates is quite complex due to the limited regularity of the unknowns, and lack of typical functional analysis tools such as discrete inverse inequalities. Specifically, the proofs rely on the consistency of each of the discrete bilinear forms
{\rm{a}}_{h}
and
{\rm{b}}_{h}
stated in Theorems
10
and
11
, and the
\inf
–
\sup
stability of the saddle-point problem (
3.5
) as stated in Theorem
12
.

Many of the results in this section echo those for the regular (non-enriched) HHO discretisation of the Stokes scheme
[
8
, Section 8]
. However, the proofs presented in this paper possess many differences. Notably, assumptions about
H^{1}(T)^{d}
-regularity of
{\bm{\mathcal{P}}}_{{\bm{\Delta}}}^{k}(T)
were not made at any stage. Consequently, gradients or Dirichlet traces of the element unknowns cannot be considered. This necessity led to the definition of the alternate discrete norm (
3.4
) under which stability and error estimates are stated. The enrichment of discrete polynomial spaces with non-polynomial functions further complicates matters, as the validity of usual discrete inverse inequalities is unknown. Additionally, the approximation properties of each extended projector in any norm other than that induced by the inner-product under which it is defined are unknown.

Lemma 7
.

It holds, for all
\underline{\bm{v}}_{T}\in\underline{\bm{U}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k}
, that

h_{T}^{-1}\|{\bm{v}}_{T}-{\bm{{\rm r}}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k+1}\underline{\bm{v}}_{T}\|_{T}+h_{T}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{T}}\|{\bm{v}}_{F}-{\bm{{\rm r}}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k+1}\underline{\bm{v}}_{T}\|_{F}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}+{\rm{s}}_{T}(\underline{\bm{v}}_{T},\underline{\bm{v}}_{T})^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\|\underline{\bm{v}}_{T}\|_{1,T,{\bm{\Psi}}}.

(4.1)

Proof.

The upper bound on the stabilisation term follows trivially from the assumption (
2.17
) and the identity (
2.15
). Consider,

\displaystyle h_{T}^{-1}\|{\bm{v}}_{T}-{\bm{{\rm r}}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k+1}\underline{\bm{v}}_{T}\|_{T}\leq{}

\displaystyle h_{T}^{-1}\|{\bm{v}}_{T}-{\bm{\Pi}}_{{T},{\bm{\Delta}}}^{k}{\bm{{\rm r}}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k+1}\underline{\bm{v}}_{T}\|_{T}+h_{T}^{-1}\|{\bm{{\rm r}}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k+1}\underline{\bm{v}}_{T}-{\bm{\Pi}}_{{T},{\bm{\Delta}}}^{k}{\bm{{\rm r}}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k+1}\underline{\bm{v}}_{T}\|_{T}

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle h_{T}^{-1}\|{\bm{v}}_{T}-{\bm{\Pi}}_{{T},{\bm{\Delta}}}^{k}{\bm{{\rm r}}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k+1}\underline{\bm{v}}_{T}\|_{T}+h_{T}^{-1}\|{\bm{{\rm r}}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k+1}\underline{\bm{v}}_{T}-{\bm{\Pi}}_{T}^{0}{\bm{{\rm r}}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k+1}\underline{\bm{v}}_{T}\|_{T}

\displaystyle\lesssim{}

\displaystyle h_{T}^{-1}\|{\bm{v}}_{T}-{\bm{\Pi}}_{{T},{\bm{\Delta}}}^{k}{\bm{{\rm r}}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k+1}\underline{\bm{v}}_{T}\|_{T}+\|\nabla{\bm{{\rm r}}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k+1}\underline{\bm{v}}_{T}\|_{T},

(4.2)

where the term
{\bm{\Pi}}_{{T},{\bm{\Delta}}}^{k}{\bm{{\rm r}}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k+1}\underline{\bm{v}}_{T}
has been added and subtracted and a triangle inequality applied, and in the second line the term
{\bm{\Pi}}_{{T},{\bm{\Delta}}}^{k}{\bm{{\rm r}}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k+1}\underline{\bm{v}}_{T}
is replaced with
{\bm{\Pi}}_{T}^{0}{\bm{{\rm r}}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k+1}\underline{\bm{v}}_{T}
due to the optimal approximation properties of orthogonal projectors. The final inequality is a result of a Poincaré–Wirtinger inequality. Similarly, add and subtract
{\bm{\Pi}}_{{F},\partial_{{\bm{n}}}}^{k}{\bm{{\rm r}}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k+1}\underline{\bm{v}}_{T}
to the boundary term of (
4.1
) and apply a triangle inequality to yield,

\displaystyle h_{T}^{-\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{T}}\|{\bm{v}}_{F}-{\bm{{\rm r}}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k+1}\underline{\bm{v}}_{T}\|_{F}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle h_{T}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{T}}\|{\bm{v}}_{F}-{\bm{\Pi}}_{{F},\partial_{{\bm{n}}}}^{k}{\bm{{\rm r}}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k+1}\underline{\bm{v}}_{T}\|_{F}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}+h_{T}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{T}}\|{\bm{{\rm r}}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k+1}\underline{\bm{v}}_{T}-{\bm{\Pi}}_{{F},\partial_{{\bm{n}}}}^{k}{\bm{{\rm r}}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k+1}\underline{\bm{v}}_{T}\|_{F}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle h_{T}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{T}}\|{\bm{v}}_{F}-{\bm{\Pi}}_{{F},\partial_{{\bm{n}}}}^{k}{\bm{{\rm r}}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k+1}\underline{\bm{v}}_{T}\|_{F}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}+h_{T}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{T}}\|{\bm{{\rm r}}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k+1}\underline{\bm{v}}_{T}-{\bm{\Pi}}_{T}^{0}{\bm{{\rm r}}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k+1}\underline{\bm{v}}_{T}\|_{F}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim{}

\displaystyle h_{T}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{T}}\|{\bm{v}}_{F}-{\bm{\Pi}}_{{F},\partial_{{\bm{n}}}}^{k}{\bm{{\rm r}}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k+1}\underline{\bm{v}}_{T}\|_{F}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\|\nabla{\bm{{\rm r}}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k+1}\underline{\bm{v}}_{T}\|_{T},

(4.3)

where the term
{\bm{\Pi}}_{{F},\partial_{{\bm{n}}}}^{k}{\bm{{\rm r}}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k+1}\underline{\bm{v}}_{T}
is replaced with
{\bm{\Pi}}_{T}^{0}{\bm{{\rm r}}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k+1}\underline{\bm{v}}_{T}
in the second inequality due to the optimal approximation properties of orthogonal projectors and the fact that
{\bm{\mathcal{P}}}^{0}(T)|_{F}\subset{\bm{\mathcal{P}}}_{\partial_{{\bm{n}}}}^{k}(F)
, and the continuous trace inequality (
2.3
) and a Poincaré–Wirtinger inequality are applied to infer the final inequality. The result follows as each of the terms in (
4
) and (
4
) are bounded above by
\|\underline{\bm{v}}_{T}\|_{1,T,{\bm{\Psi}}}
.
∎

Lemma 8

(Boundedness of the local interpolator)

{\bm{w}}\in H^{1}(T)^{d}

\|\underline{\bm{I}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k}{\bm{w}}\|_{1,T,{\bm{\Psi}}}\lesssim|{\bm{w}}|_{H^{1}(T)^{d}}.

) of
\underline{\bm{I}}_{{T},{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k}

(Consistency of

(Consistency of

The discrete bilinear form

{\bm{\Delta}}{\bm{w}}\in L^{2}(\Omega)^{d}
,
{\bm{w}}_{r}\in H^{k+2}(\mathcal{T}_{h})^{d}

{\bm{\psi}}\in{\bm{\Psi}}(\mathcal{T}_{h})

\|(\underline{\bm{v}}_{h},q_{h})\|_{X,h}=1
, and noting that
\|\underline{\bm{v}}_{h}\|_{1,h,{\bm{\Psi}}}\leq\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|(\underline{\bm{v}}_{h},q_{h})\|_{X,h}
, it is inferred that

\sup_{(\underline{\bm{v}}_{h},q_{h})\in\underline{\bm{X}}_{h,{\bm{\Psi}},\Phi}^{k}:\|(\underline{\bm{v}}_{h},q_{h})\|_{X,h}=1}\Big(\nu{\rm{a}}_{h}(\underline{\bm{u}}_{h}-\underline{\bm{I}}_{h,{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k}{\bm{u}},\underline{\bm{v}}_{h})+{\rm{b}}_{h}(\underline{\bm{v}}_{h},p_{h}-\Sigma_{h,\Phi}^{k}p)-{\rm{b}}_{h}(\underline{\bm{u}}_{h}-\underline{\bm{I}}_{h,{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k}{\bm{u}},q_{h})\Big)\\
\lesssim h^{k+1}\Big(\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}|{\bm{u}}_{r}|_{H^{k+2}(\mathcal{T}_{h})^{d}}+\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}|p_{r}|_{H^{k+1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}\Big).

The result follows by applying the
\inf
–
\sup
condition (
4.27
) with
\underline{\bm{w}}_{h}=\underline{\bm{u}}_{h}-\underline{\bm{I}}_{h,{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k}{\bm{u}}
and
z_{h}=p_{h}-\Sigma_{h,\Phi}^{k}p
.
∎

5
Stokes Flow Around Submerged Cylinders

Inspired by the work of
[
19
]
, this study aims to explore the behaviour of the Stokes equations around submerged cylinders. The objective is to derive an analytical understanding of local fluid behaviour around a cylinder and subsequently enrich the local HHO spaces with the analytic solution.

The problem at hand involves finding solutions to the following set of equations:

\displaystyle-{\bm{\Delta}}{\bm{u}}+\nabla p

\displaystyle=\bm{0}\quad

in

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{2}\backslash B_{R},

(5.1a)

\displaystyle\DIV{\bm{u}}

\displaystyle=0\quad

in

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{2}\backslash B_{R},

(5.1b)

subject to the boundary conditions:

{\bm{u}}=\bm{U}_{\infty}\quad\textrm{as }|{\bm{x}}|\to\infty\quad;\quad{\bm{u}}=\bm{0}\quad\textrm{on }\partial B_{R},

(5.1c)

where
B_{R}
denotes the circle of radius
R>0
centred at the origin and
\bm{U}_{\infty}
represents a constant velocity field.

However, there exist no solutions to equations (
5.1
), an unexpected result initially observed by
[
18
]
. Consequently, less restrictive boundary conditions are considered, expressed as:

{\bm{u}}=\bm{U}_{\infty}\quad\textrm{at }|{\bm{x}}|=R_{\infty}\quad;\quad{\bm{u}}=\bm{0}\quad\textrm{on }\partial B_{R},

(5.2)

for some
R_{\infty}\gg 1
. For simplicity, the velocity field
\bm{U}_{\infty}
is assumed to be given by
U{\bm{e}}_{x}
, where
U\in\mathbb{R}
and
{\bm{e}}_{x}
represents the unit vector in the
x
-direction.

Consider a stream function
\psi
such that, in polar coordinates,

{\bm{u}}(r,\theta)=-\frac{1}{r}\partial_{\theta}\psi(r,\theta){\bm{e}}_{r}+\partial_{r}\psi(r,\theta){\bm{e}}_{\theta},

(5.3)

where
{\bm{e}}_{r}
and
{\bm{e}}_{\theta}
denote the radial and tangential unit vectors, respectively. Substituting equation (
5.3
) into (
5.1a
) and taking the
\CURL
, the following equation is obtained:

\Delta^{2}\psi=0,

(5.4a)

subject to the boundary conditions

\displaystyle\partial_{r}\psi(R_{\infty},\theta)={}

\displaystyle-U\sin(\theta),

\displaystyle\partial_{\theta}\psi(R_{\infty},\theta)={}

\displaystyle-UR_{\infty}\cos(\theta),

\displaystyle\partial_{r}\psi(R,\theta)={}

\displaystyle 0,

\displaystyle\partial_{\theta}\psi(R,\theta)={}

\displaystyle 0.

(5.4b)

Due to the boundary conditions at
R_{\infty}
, it is expected that the solution will take the form:

\psi(r,\theta)=\zeta(r)\sin(\theta).

Upon substituting this back into equation (
5.4
), a solution for
\zeta(r)
is obtained as follows:

\zeta(r)=\frac{(R^{2}+R_{\infty}^{2})U}{R^{2}-R_{\infty}^{2}+(R^{2}+R_{\infty}^{2})\ln(\frac{R_{\infty}}{R})}\left(\frac{r^{2}-R^{2}}{2r}-r\ln\Big(\frac{r}{R}\Big)+\frac{(r^{2}-R^{2})^{2}}{2r(R^{2}+R_{\infty}^{2})}\right).

A brief asymptotic analysis reveals that

\zeta(r)\sim\frac{U}{\ln(R_{\infty})}\zeta_{1}(r)+\frac{U}{R_{\infty}^{2}\ln(R_{\infty})}\zeta_{2}(r)\qquad\textrm{as }R_{\infty}\to\infty,

where

\zeta_{1}(r)=\frac{r^{2}-R^{2}}{2r}-r\ln\Big(\frac{r}{R}\Big)\quad\textrm{and}\quad\zeta_{2}(r)=\frac{(r^{2}-R^{2})^{2}}{2r}.

The two solutions
\zeta_{1}
and
\zeta_{2}
correspond to the two solutions considered by
[
19
, Equations A12, A13]
. However, as the multiplicative constant of
\zeta_{1}
dominates that of
\zeta_{2}
as
R_{\infty}\to\infty
, the focus here will be solely on the solution
\zeta_{1}
. Therefore, the stream function considered is

\psi(r,\theta)=\zeta_{1}(r)\sin(\theta).

The corresponding velocity and pressure fields are given by the following expressions:

{\bm{u}}=\frac{R^{2}-r^{2}}{2r^{4}}\Big((x^{2}-y^{2}){\bm{e}}_{x}+2xy{\bm{e}}_{y}\Big)+\ln\left(\frac{r}{R}\right){\bm{e}}_{x},

(5.5)

and

p=C-\frac{2x}{r^{2}},

(5.6)

where
C
is an arbitrary constant.

5.1
Single Submerged Cylinder with Manufactured Solution

Consider the domain
\Omega=(0,1)^{2}\setminus B_{R}({\bm{x}}_{0})
, where
B_{R}({\bm{x}}_{0})
represents a ball of radius
R
centred at
{\bm{x}}_{0}=(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})
.

Let
\hat{{\bm{u}}}
and
\hat{p}
denote the asymptotic solutions (
5.5
) and (
5.6
) around the cylinder
B_{R}({\bm{x}}_{0})
, with the additive constant in
\hat{p}
set to
0
.

An exact solution to the Stokes problem (
2.1
) with viscosity
\nu=1
is introduced as follows:

{\bm{u}}=\hat{{\bm{u}}}+\sin(\pi x)\sin(\pi y)\left(\sin(\pi x)\cos(\pi y){\bm{e}}_{x}-\cos(\pi x)\sin(\pi y){\bm{e}}_{y}\right),

and

p=\hat{p}+(x-\frac{1}{2})(y-\frac{1}{2})^{2}.

The enrichment spaces are defined as follows:

{\bm{\Psi}}(T)=\mbox{{span}}\big\{\delta_{\gamma}(T)\hat{{\bm{u}}}\big\},\quad\Phi(T)=\mbox{{span}}\big\{\delta_{\gamma}(T)\hat{p}\big\},

where
\delta_{\gamma}:\mathcal{T}_{h}\to\{0,1\}
is a cutoff function taking the value
1
on elements with a centre of mass within a distance
\gamma>0
of the cylinder
B_{R}({\bm{x}}_{0})
, and
0
otherwise.

While both
\hat{{\bm{u}}}
and
\hat{p}
are smooth in the domain
\Omega
, they exhibit singularities at the centre of the cylinder
B_{R}({\bm{x}}_{0})
. Thus, as the cylinder radius
R
approaches
0
,
\hat{{\bm{u}}}
and
\hat{p}
are expected to become nearly singular. Consequently, the enriched hybrid high-order method is anticipated to outperform the classical HHO method as
R\to 0
.

The enriched HHO method described in Section
2
is implemented using the open-source C++ library
PolyMesh

[
22
]
. A sequence of uniform Cartesian meshes is considered, and the cylinder
B_{R}({\bm{x}}_{0})
is ‘cut out’ of the mesh. The basis functions on curved edges are generated by first creating a spanning set, and removing the linearly dependent basis functions using the
FullPivLU
class found in the Eigen library, with documentation available at
https://eigen.tuxfamily.org/dox/classEigen_1_1FullPivLU.html
.

{\bm{\Pi}}_{h,{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k+1}|_{T}={\bm{\Pi}}_{T,{\bm{\Psi}}}^{k+1}

\textrm{DOFs}=\mbox{{Card}}\big(\mathcal{T}_{h}\big)+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm{i}}}}\dim{{\bm{\mathcal{P}}}_{\partial_{{\bm{n}}}}^{k}(F)}.

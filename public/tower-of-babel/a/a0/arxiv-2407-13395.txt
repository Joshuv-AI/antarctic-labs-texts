On Borsuk’s non-retract theorem
Waldemar Sieg

Abstract
The classical Borsuk’s non-retract theorem asserts that a unit sphere in ℝ n {\mathbb{R}}^{n} is not a continuous retract of the unit closed ball. We will show that such a unit sphere is a piecewise continuous retract of the unit closed ball.



1
Introduction

Let
X
and
Y
be the Hausdorff topological spaces and let
A
be a nonempty subset of
X
.
The mapping
r\colon X\to A
is an
algebraic retraction
if
r(x)=x
for every
x\in A
. We say that a function
f\colon X\to Y
is

piecewise continuous
, if there is an increasing sequence
\left(X_{n}\right)\subset X
of nonempty closed sets such
that
X=\bigcup^{\infty}_{n=0}X_{n}
and the restriction
f_{\mathord{\restriction}X_{n}}
is continuous for each
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
The family of all piecewise continuous functions
X\to Y
is denoted by
{\mathscr{P}}(X,Y)
(if
Y={\mathbb{R}}
we simply write
{\mathscr{P}}(X)
).
The symbol
{\mathscr{P}}^{bd}(X)
stands for the family of bounded elements of
{\mathscr{P}}(X)
.
We conclude this short introduction with an example of some ”nice” retraction of the real line.

Example 1
.

Let
E(t)
be an
entier
of a real number
t
,
A=[0,1)\subset{\mathbb{R}}
and let
g\colon{\mathbb{R}}\to A
be a mapping given by the formula
g(x)=x-E(x)
.
Since

[0,1)=\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}\left[0,1-\frac{1}{k}\right]

and

{\mathbb{R}}=\bigcup_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\left(n+[0,1)\right)=\bigcup_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\left[n,n+1\right),

g
is a piecewise continuous retraction of
{\mathbb{R}}
onto interval
A
.

2
Preliminaries

Our first statement gives an answer when the subset of the Hausdorff space is its piecewise continuous retract.

Proposition 1
.

If
A
is both an
F_{\sigma}
- and
G_{\delta}
- subset of the Hausdorff space
X
then it is its piecewise continuous retract.
Precisely, if
g\colon X\setminus A\to A
is a continuous mapping then the function
{\varphi}\colon X\to A
given by the formula

\varphi(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}x&\textrm{:}&x\in A,\\
g(x)&\textrm{:}&x\in X\setminus A\end{array}\right.

(1)

is the required piecewise continuous retraction.

Proof.

Let
i_{A}\colon A\to A
be an identity on
A
.
Since
A
is both an
F_{\sigma}
- and
G_{\delta}
- subset of
X
, there are sequences
(F_{n})
and
(G_{n})
of closed sets such that

\bigcup_{n}F_{n}=A
and
\bigcup_{n}G_{n}=X\setminus A
. Obviously
F_{j}\cap G_{k}=\emptyset
for every
j,k\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Moreover, since
g
is continuous,
the restrictions
i_{A_{\mathord{\restriction}F_{n}}}
and
g_{\mathord{\restriction}G_{n}}
are continuous for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Set
H_{n}=F_{n}\cup G_{n}
, for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Then

\bigcup_{n}H_{n}=\bigcup_{n}(F_{n}\cup G_{n})=\bigcup_{n}F_{n}\cup\bigcup_{n}G_{n}=A\cup(X\setminus A)=X.

The continuity of the restrictions
i_{A_{\mathord{\restriction}F_{n}}}
and
g_{\mathord{\restriction}G_{n}}
, implies the continuity of
{\varphi}
on every set
H_{n}
(
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
).
That completes the proof.
∎

There is another natural problem at this point.

(P)
Let
A
be both an
F_{\sigma}
- and
G_{\delta}
- subset of the Hausdorff space
X
and let
U\subset X
.
Under what assumptions a piecewise continuous retraction
U\to A
can be extended to a piecewise continuous retraction
X\to A
?

The following lemma gives an answer. It is a consequence of Proposition
1
.

Lemma 1
.

Let
U
be both an
F_{\sigma}
- and
G_{\delta}
- subset of the Hausdorff space
X
and let
A\subset U
. Let also
{\varphi}\colon U\to A
be a surjective
piecewise continuous retraction. Then
{\varphi}
can be extended to a piecewise continuous retraction
\Phi\colon X\to A
. Precisely, if
g\colon X\setminus U\to A

is a continuous mapping then the function
\Phi\colon X\to A
given by the formula

\Phi(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}{\varphi}(x)&\textrm{:}&x\in U,\\
g(x)&\textrm{:}&x\in X\setminus U\end{array}\right.

is the required extension.

From the above lemma we obtain the following

Corollary 1
.

Let
U
be an open subset of a perfectly normal space
X
and let
A
be both an
F_{\sigma}
- and
G_{\delta}
- subset of
X
with a fixed point
a_{0}\in A
.
Let also
{\varphi}\colon U\to A
be a piecewise continuous retraction. Then the function
\Phi\colon X\to A
given by the formula

\Phi(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}{\varphi}(x)&\textrm{:}&x\in U,\\
a_{0}&\textrm{:}&x\in X\setminus U\end{array}\right.

is a piecewise continuous retraction extending
{\varphi}
.

3
Borsuk’s non-retract theorem

The classical Borsuk’s non-retract theorem
[
2
, Theorem 8]
asserts that

Theorem 2

(Borsuk)
.

A unit sphere in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is not a continuous retract of the unit closed ball.

It turns out that if the word ”continuous retract” is replaced by ”piecewise continuous retract”,
the Borsuk’s claim is no longer true.

Theorem 3
.

Let
(X,\|\cdot\|)
be a normed space (or
X
is simply a closed unit ball in a normed space
Y
) and let
U=X\setminus\{\overline{0}\}
.
Let also
A
be the unit sphere of
X
and fix
t\in A
. The mapping
\Phi\colon X\to A
given by the formula

\Phi(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\frac{x}{\|x\|}&\textrm{:}&x\in U,\\
t&\textrm{:}&x\in X\setminus U\end{array}\right.

is piecewise continuous. Hence, the unit sphere is a piecewise contiunous retract of the unit closed ball,
as well as of
Y
.

Proof.

As elements of the increasing sequence
\left(X_{n}\right)
it is enough to take

X_{n}=\left\{x\in X\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\|x\|\geqslant\frac{1}{n}\right\}
).
∎

Next theorem describes a retract of a normed space
X
onto the open unit ball.
It is an extension of Theorem
3
.

Theorem 4
.

Let
(X,\|\cdot\|)
be at least
2
-dimensional normed space and let
K^{0}
be the open unit ball in
X
.
Then the function
{\varphi}\colon X\to K^{0}
given by the formula

\Phi(x)=\left(1-\frac{E(\|x\|)}{\|x\|}\right)\cdot x

is a piecewise continuous retraction from
X
onto
K^{0}
.

Proof.

Note first that

\Phi(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}x&\textrm{if}&\|x\|<1,\\
0&\textrm{if}&\|x\|=1.\end{array}\right.

Since

\Phi(x)=(\|x\|-E(\|x\|))\cdot\frac{x}{\|x\|}

and

\|\Phi(x)\|=\|x\|-E(\|x\|)<1,

\Phi
is a surjection from
X
onto
K^{0}
. Set

B_{n}=\{x\in X\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}n\leqslant\|x\|<n+1\}=

=\bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty}\left\{x\in X\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}n\leqslant\|x\|\leqslant n+1-\frac{1}{j}\right\}=\bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty}B_{n,j}.

Obviously
X=\bigcup\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigcup\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}B_{n,j}
, every set
B_{n,j}
is closed and every restriction
\Phi_{\mathord{\restriction}B_{n,j}}
is continuous.
∎

Our last result is about extending piecewise continuous mappings using piecewise continuous retractions.
It has a weaker assumption about
X
and is a generalization of
[
3
, Theorem 2]
.
It shows that the number of all extensions of
f\in{\mathscr{P}}(A)
is at least equal to a number of continuous mappings
g\colon X\setminus A\to A\subset X
.

Theorem 5
.

Let
A
be both an
F_{\sigma}
- and
G_{\delta}
- subset of the Hausdorff space
X
. Let also
{\varphi}\colon X\to A
be a piecewise continuous
retraction. The mapping
T_{{\varphi}}\colon{\mathscr{P}}(A)\to{\mathscr{P}}(X)
of the form

T_{{\varphi}}(f)=f\circ{\varphi},\ f\in{\mathscr{P}}(A),

is a positive linear extension operator such that its restriction to
{\mathscr{P}}^{bd}(A)
is an isometry into
{\mathscr{P}}^{bd}(X)
.

Proof.

Let
A
be both an
F_{\sigma}
- and
G_{\delta}
- subset of the Hausdorff space
X
.
From Proposition
1
it follows that a retraction
{\varphi}\colon X\to A
given by (
1
) is piecewise continuous.
Thus (by
[
1
, Theorem 5.2]
)
T_{\varphi}(f)=f\circ{\varphi}
is piecewise continuous on
X
. Its linearity and being an isometry are obvious.
∎

A Spin Analogue of Kerov Polynomials This paper is a contribution to the Special Issue on the Representation Theory of the Symmetric Groups and Related Topics. The full collection is available at https://www.emis.de/journals/SIGMA/symmetric-groups-2018.html
Sho MATSUMOTO

Abstract
Kerov polynomials describe normalized irreducible characters of the symmetric groups in terms of the free cumulants associated with Young diagrams. We suggest well-suited counterparts of the Kerov polynomials in spin (or projective) representation settings. We show that spin analogues of irreducible characters are polynomials in even free cumulants associated with double diagrams of strict partitions. Moreover, we present a conjecture for the positivity of their coefficients.

Introduction

1.1
Characters of symmetric groups

Irreducible representations of symmetric groups
S_{n}
are indexed by partitions
\lambda
of
n
, or equivalently by Young diagrams of size
n
. The corresponding character
\chi^{\lambda}
takes values at conjugacy classes
C_{\nu}
in
S_{n}
, which are also indexed by partitions
\nu
of
n
. The character values
\chi^{\lambda}_{\nu}=\chi^{\lambda}(C_{\nu})
have been studied for a long time by innumerable researchers, see, e.g.,
[
20
]
. Recently, for several problems in the asymptotic representation theory, we often deal with the
\chi^{\lambda}_{\nu}
, fixing
\nu
and letting
\lambda
vary. More precisely, for a partition
\nu
of
k
and a partition
\lambda
of
n
with
k\leq n
, we define

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ch}_{\nu}(\lambda)=n(n-1)\cdots(n-k+1)\frac{\chi^{\lambda}_{\nu\cup(1^{n-k})}}{f^{\lambda}},

(1.1)

where
f^{\lambda}:=\chi^{\lambda}_{(1^{|\lambda|})}
denotes the dimension of the irreducible representation of the symmetric group
S_{|\lambda|}
associated with
\lambda
. We set
\mathrm{Ch}_{\nu}(\lambda)=0
whenever
k>n
.
Then the
\mathrm{Ch}_{\nu}
are functions on all Young diagrams. An approach from this point of view is sometimes referred to as
dual approach
or
dual combinatorics
to characters of symmetric groups
[
6
,
9
]
.

1.2
Kerov polynomials

Biane and Kerov discovered that free cumulants play an important role in the asymptotic representation theory of symmetric groups
[
1
,
2
]
. Here the free cumulants
R_{j}(\lambda)

(j=1,2,\dots)
of the transition measure of a Young diagram
\lambda
are sequences of real numbers defined in the framework of the free probability theory. (Note that
R_{1}\equiv 0
.) Then the normalized character
\mathrm{Ch}_{k}:=\mathrm{Ch}_{(k)}
for one-row partition
\nu=(k)
can be expressed as a
polynomial
in
R_{2},\dots,R_{k+1}
with integer coefficients.

Theorem 1.1

(Kerov’s character formula
[
2
]
)
.

For each
k=1,2,3,\dots
, there exists a polynomial
K_{k}
in
k
variables with integer coefficients, such that

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ch}_{k}(\lambda)=K_{k}(R_{2}(\lambda),R_{3}(\lambda),\dots,R_{k+1}(\lambda))

for all partitions
\lambda
. Furthermore,
K_{k}
is of the form

\displaystyle K_{k}(R_{2},\dots,R_{k+1})=R_{k+1}+(\text{a polynomial in $R_{2},\dots,R_{k-1}$}).

Example 1.2

(
[
2
]
)
.

\displaystyle K_{1}=R_{2},

\displaystyle K_{2}=R_{3},

\displaystyle K_{3}=R_{4}+R_{2},

\displaystyle K_{4}=R_{5}+5R_{3},

\displaystyle K_{5}=R_{6}+15R_{4}+5R_{2}^{2}+8R_{2},

\displaystyle K_{7}=R_{8}+70R_{6}+84R_{4}R_{2}+56R_{3}^{2}+14R_{2}^{3}+469R_{4}+224R_{2}^{2}+180R_{2},

\displaystyle K_{9}=R_{10}+210R_{8}+300R_{6}R_{2}+480R_{5}R_{3}+270R_{4}^{2}+360R_{3}^{2}R_{2}+270R_{4}R_{2}^{2}+30R_{2}^{4}

\displaystyle\hphantom{K_9=}{}+5985R_{6}+10548R_{4}R_{2}+6714R_{3}^{2}+2400R_{2}^{3}+26060R_{4}+14580R_{2}^{2}+8064R_{2}.

Here we omit
K_{6}
and
K_{8}
because we will compare odd-numbered polynomials with Example
1.5
below. We can see a complete list of
K_{k}
up to
k=11
in
[
2
]
.

The polynomials
K_{k}
in the theorem are called
Kerov polynomials
. We can observe that all coefficients in the above examples are
nonnegative
integers. This surprising phenomenon is very nontrivial and had been called the Kerov (positivity) conjecture. It was finally proved by Féray
[
7
]
. The authors in
[
5
]
obtained clearer combinatorial interpretations.

Theorem 1.3

(Féray
[
7
]
)
.

All coefficients of
K_{k}
are nonnegative integers.

Recently, a generalization of Kerov polynomials involving Jack polynomials has been actively studied in
[
4
,
16
,
21
]
. In this paper, we work on research in another direction. Our aim here is to present an answer to the following question:
What is the counterpart of the Kerov polynomials in the spin representation setting?

1.3
Spin representations

Spin (or projective) representation theory of symmetric groups was introduced by Schur. Currently, it can be understood via a double covering of the symmetric group or via the Hecke–Clifford algebra, see, e.g.,
[
22
]
. In the spin case, the corresponding character values
X^{\lambda}_{\nu}
are indexed by strict partitions
\lambda
and odd partitions
\nu
. For a fixed odd partition
\nu
, we can define a spin version
\mathfrak{p}_{\nu}
of (
1.1
), which is a function on the set of all strict partitions. The function
\mathfrak{p}_{\nu}
was first introduced by Ivanov
[
12
,
13
]
(with notation
p_{\nu}^{\#}
) and developed in author’s recent work
[
18
]
.

1.4
Results

To define a spin analogue of Kerov polynomials, we need to find a spin analogue of free cumulants
R_{j}(\lambda)
. Furthermore, it is natural to expect that the same statement of Theorem
1.3
holds in the spin setting. The aim in this paper is to establish a spin analogue of Kerov polynomials by using a spin analogue
\mathfrak{R}_{2j}(\lambda)
of
R_{j}
, which is defined as the
half
of
even
free cumulants of the
double diagram
of a strict partition
\lambda
. Indeed, we obtain the following theorem for
\mathfrak{p}_{2k-1}
with one-row odd partition
\nu=(2k-1)
.

Theorem 1.4
.

For each
k=1,2,3,\dots
, there exists a polynomial
K_{2k-1}^{\mathrm{spin}}
in
k
variables with rational coefficients, such that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{p}_{2k-1}(\lambda)=K_{2k-1}^{\mathrm{spin}}(\mathfrak{R}_{2}(\lambda),\mathfrak{R}_{4}(\lambda),\dots,\mathfrak{R}_{2k}(\lambda))

for all strict partitions
\lambda
. Furthermore,
K_{2k-1}^{\mathrm{spin}}
is of the form

\displaystyle K_{2k-1}^{\mathrm{spin}}=\mathfrak{R}_{2k}+(\text{a polynomial in $\mathfrak{R}_{2},\mathfrak{R}_{4},\dots,\mathfrak{R}_{2k-2}$ of degree at most $2k-2$}).

Here we only show that the coefficients in
K_{2k-1}^{\mathrm{spin}}
are rational numbers. The degree of
\mathfrak{R}_{2k}
is regarded as
\deg\mathfrak{R}_{2k}=2k-1
. We call the polynomial
K_{2k-1}^{\mathrm{spin}}
a
spin Kerov polynomial
.

Example 1.5
.

\displaystyle K_{1}^{\mathrm{spin}}=\mathfrak{R}_{2},

\displaystyle K_{3}^{\mathrm{spin}}=\mathfrak{R}_{4}+\mathfrak{R}_{2},

\displaystyle K_{5}^{\mathrm{spin}}=\mathfrak{R}_{6}+15\mathfrak{R}_{4}+10\mathfrak{R}_{2}^{2}+8\mathfrak{R}_{2},

\displaystyle K_{7}^{\mathrm{spin}}=\mathfrak{R}_{8}+70\mathfrak{R}_{6}+168\mathfrak{R}_{4}\mathfrak{R}_{2}+56\mathfrak{R}_{2}^{3}+469\mathfrak{R}_{4}+560\mathfrak{R}_{2}^{2}+180\mathfrak{R}_{2},

\displaystyle K_{9}^{\mathrm{spin}}=\mathfrak{R}_{10}+210\mathfrak{R}_{8}+600\mathfrak{R}_{6}\mathfrak{R}_{2}+540\mathfrak{R}_{4}^{2}+1080\mathfrak{R}_{4}\mathfrak{R}_{2}^{2}+240\mathfrak{R}_{2}^{4}

\displaystyle\hphantom{K_9^{\mathrm{spin}}=}{}+5985\mathfrak{R}_{6}+23016\mathfrak{R}_{4}\mathfrak{R}_{2}+9120\mathfrak{R}_{2}^{3}+26060\mathfrak{R}_{4}+41628\mathfrak{R}_{2}^{2}+8064\mathfrak{R}_{2}.

It seems that formulas in Example
1.5
resemble those in Example
1.2
. The proof of Theorem
1.4
is accomplished by observing relations among some collections of generators in a symmetric function algebra
\Gamma
. We obtain formulas in Example
1.5
as the by-products. Not only Theorem
1.4
but also Example
1.5
is an important result in this paper because the latter one indicates a spin analogue of Theorem
1.3
to us.

Conjecture 1.6

(spin Kerov conjecture)
.

All coefficients in spin Kerov polynomials are nonnegative integers.

1.5
Discussion and outline

Comparing Example
1.5
with Example
1.2
, we can find some interesting coincidences for coefficients. For example, the coefficient of linear terms
\mathfrak{R}_{2j}
in
K_{2k-1}^{\mathrm{spin}}
likely coincides with that of
R_{2j}
in
K_{2k-1}
. Furthermore, if
2s_{2}+4s_{4}+\cdots=2k-2
, the coefficient of
\mathfrak{R}_{2}^{s_{2}}\mathfrak{R}_{4}^{s_{4}}\cdots
in
K_{2k-1}^{\mathrm{spin}}
likely coincide with
2^{s_{2}+s_{4}+\cdots-1}
times the coefficient of
R_{2}^{s_{2}}R_{4}^{s_{4}}\cdots
in
K_{2k-1}
. We do not discuss their coincidence or combinatorial interpretations here. We leave them in future work.

We give the proof of Theorem
1.4
and how to derive Example
1.5
with precise definitions in Section
3
after the review of preliminary facts for free cumulants Section
2
. As mentioned, the ordinary Kerov conjecture was first proved by Féray
[
7
]
. After that, the authors of
[
5
]
gave alternative proof by using Stanley–Féray polynomials. The Stanley–Féray polynomials were analyzed by using Young symmetrizers
[
8
]
. In order to attack Conjecture
1.6
, it is a natural practice to study projective Young symmetrizers. The projective Young symmetrizer was studied in
[
15
,
19
]
. Unfortunately it was quite complicated, so it seems difficult to apply it to our problem.

In Section
4
, we try another choice for free cumulants. The another free cumulants
\mathtt{R}_{2k}(\lambda)
comes from a symmetrized double diagram
\mathtt{D}(\lambda)
of
\lambda
. The symmetrized diagrams
\mathtt{D}(\lambda)
are useful for the study of asymptotics of Plancherel measures in the spin setting (see
[
13
]
and
[
3
, Chapter 4]
). Since odd-numbered quantities
\mathtt{R}_{2k-1}
vanish by virtue of the symmetry of
\mathtt{D}(\lambda)
, they are simpler than free cumulants of
D(\lambda)
in a sense. Even if we replace
\mathfrak{R}_{2k}
with
\mathtt{R}_{2k}
, we can show the existence of “spin Kerov polynomials” easily. However, the corresponding polynomial for
\mathfrak{p}_{2k-1}
with
k=2
has a
non-integer
coefficient. In this sense, we view the
\mathtt{R}_{2k}
as being unsuitable for spin Kerov polynomials. We thus believe that the
\mathfrak{R}_{2k}
are the most appropriate choice in the spin setting.

2.1
Partitions

A
partition

\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots)
is a weakly decreasing sequence of nonnegative integers satisfying
|\lambda|=\sum\limits_{i\geq 1}\lambda_{i}<\infty
. When
|\lambda|=n
, we say
\lambda
to be a partition of
n
and sometimes write as
\lambda\vdash n
. The number of nonzero
\lambda_{i}
is called the length of
\lambda
and written as
\ell(\lambda)
.

We usually identity a partition with its Young diagram. We denote by
\lambda^{\prime}=(\lambda_{1}^{\prime},\lambda_{2}^{\prime},\dots)
the conjugate partition of
\lambda
, i.e., the Young diagram of
\lambda^{\prime}
is the transpose of that of
\lambda
with respect to the diagonal line. Define
d=d(\lambda)
by the number of boxes on the diagonal line in the Young diagram of
\lambda
. The
modified Frobenius notation

\displaystyle[a_{1},\dots,a_{d}\,|\,b_{1},\dots,b_{d}]

of
\lambda
is determined by

\displaystyle a_{i}=\lambda_{i}-i+\frac{1}{2},\qquad b_{i}=\lambda_{i}^{\prime}-i+\frac{1}{2},\qquad i=1,2,\dots,d.

Note that
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{d}(a_{i}+b_{i})=|\lambda|
. For example, the modified Frobenius notation of
\lambda=(5,4,4,1,1)\vdash 15
is
\big[4+\frac{1}{2},2+\frac{1}{2},1+\frac{1}{2}\,|\,4+\frac{1}{2},1+\frac{1}{2},0+\frac{1}{2}\big]
.

2.2
Free cumulants

Let
\mu
be a probability measure on
\mathbb{R}
with a compact support. Define the Cauchy transform of
\mu
by

\displaystyle G_{\mu}(z)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{z-x}\mu(dx)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{M_{k}[\mu]}{z^{k+1}},\qquad z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R},\quad|z|\gg 1,

where the
M_{k}[\mu]
are moments of
\mu
:

\displaystyle M_{k}[\mu]=\int_{\mathbb{R}}x^{k}\mu(dx).

Free cumulants

R_{k}[\mu]

(k=1,2,\dots)
of
\mu
are defined via the famous free cumulant-moment formula, see, e.g.,
[
11
, Chapter 1]
. We do not need the explicit definition here. We only use the relation

\displaystyle R_{k}=M_{k}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\nu=(\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\dots)\vdash k\\
\nu_{1}<k\end{subarray}}c_{\nu}M_{\nu_{1}}M_{\nu_{2}}\cdots

(2.1)

with some integer coefficients
c_{\nu}
.

2.3
Kerov’s transition measures

We draw the Young diagram of a partition
\lambda
in Russian style, see, e.g.,
[
11
, Fig. 2.1]
. Let

\displaystyle x_{1}<y_{1}<\cdots<x_{r-1}<y_{r-1}<x_{r}

be the corresponding local minimas and maximas, which are integers by definition. We call these numbers the
Kerov interlacing coordinates
of
\lambda
. It is known that they satisfy the relation
[
11
, Lemma 2.1]

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{r}x_{r}=\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}y_{j}.

(2.2)

For each partition
\lambda
, we define a probability measure
\mathfrak{m}_{\lambda}
on
\mathbb{R}
via the Cauchy transform

\displaystyle G_{\mathfrak{m}_{\lambda}}(z)=\frac{\prod\limits_{i=1}^{r-1}(z-y_{i})}{\prod\limits_{i=1}^{r}(z-x_{i})}.

This probability measure, called
Kerov’s transition measure
of
\lambda
, is supported by the set
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{r}\}
. Denote by
R_{k}(\lambda)

(k=1,2,\dots)
the free cumulant of the measure
\mathfrak{m}_{\lambda}
:
R_{k}(\lambda)=R_{k}[\mathfrak{m}_{\lambda}]
. It has the expression
[
2
, Theorem 2]

\displaystyle R_{k}(\lambda)=-\frac{1}{k-1}\big[z^{-1}\big]G_{\mathfrak{m}_{\lambda}}(z)^{-(k-1)}

(2.3)

for
k\geq 2
.

2.4
Super power-sums
\bm{p_{k}^{\mathrm{super}}}

For each partition
\lambda
with modified Frobenius expression
[a_{1},\dots,a_{d}\,|\,b_{1},\dots,b_{d}]

and for
k=1,2,\dots
, we define

\displaystyle p_{k}^{\mathrm{super}}(\lambda)=\sum_{i=1}^{d}(a_{i}^{k}+(-1)^{k-1}b_{i}^{k}).

These are also determined via

\displaystyle\phi(z;\lambda):=\prod_{i=1}^{d}\frac{z+b_{i}}{z-a_{i}}=\exp\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{p_{k}^{\mathrm{super}}(\lambda)}{k}\frac{1}{z^{k}}\right).

(2.4)

Note that
p_{1}^{\mathrm{super}}(\lambda)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{d}(a_{i}+b_{i})=|\lambda|
.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
11
, Proposition 2.1]
)
.

For each partition
\lambda
, we have

\displaystyle zG_{\mathfrak{m}_{\lambda}}(z)=\frac{\phi\big(z-\frac{1}{2};\lambda\big)}{\phi\big(z+\frac{1}{2};\lambda\big)}.

2.5
Rayleigh measures

For each partition
\lambda
with Kerov’s interlacing coordinate
x_{1}<y_{1}<\cdots<y_{r-1}<x_{r}
, we introduce an
\mathbb{R}
-valued measure
\tau_{\lambda}
on
\mathbb{R}
by

\displaystyle\tau_{\lambda}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\delta_{x_{i}}-\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}\delta_{y_{i}},

where
\delta_{x}
is the Dirac measure at a point
x
. This is called the
Rayleigh measure
of
\lambda
. The moments are clearly given by

\displaystyle M_{k}[\tau_{\lambda}]=\sum_{i=1}^{r}x_{i}^{k}-\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}y_{i}^{k}

for
k=1,2,\dots
. Note that
M_{1}[\tau_{\lambda}]=0
by (
2.2
).

Lemma 2.2

(
[
11
, Proposition 2.4]
)
.

For
n\geq 2
,

\displaystyle M_{n}[\tau_{\lambda}]=\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor(n/2)-1\rfloor}\binom{n}{2j+1}2^{-2j}p_{n-2j-1}^{\mathrm{super}}(\lambda)=np_{n-1}^{\mathrm{super}}(\lambda)+\cdots.

2.6
Relations between two measures

Two measures
\mathfrak{m}_{\lambda}
and
\tau_{\lambda}
have the following relation, which is nothing but that the relation between complete symmetric functions and power-sum symmetric functions.

Lemma 2.3

(
[
11
, Proposition 2.2]
)
.

\displaystyle 1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}M_{n}[\mathfrak{m}_{\lambda}]\frac{1}{z^{n}}=\exp\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{M_{k}[\tau_{\lambda}]}{k}\frac{1}{z^{k}}\right).

Equivalently,

\displaystyle M_{n}[\mathfrak{m}_{\lambda}]=\sum_{\nu=(\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\dots)\vdash n}z_{\nu}^{-1}M_{\nu_{1}}[\tau_{\lambda}]M_{\nu_{2}}[\tau_{\lambda}]\cdots,

where
z_{\nu}=\prod\limits_{i\geq 1}i^{m_{i}}m_{i}!
with
m_{i}=m_{i}(\nu)=|\{j\geq 1\,|\,\nu_{j}=i\}|
.

Combining above formulas, we can express free cumulants
R_{k}(\lambda)
in terms of functions
p_{j}^{\mathrm{super}}(\lambda)
in principle. This fact will be applied in the next section.

3.2
Symmetric functions

We review some symmetric functions according to Macdonald’s book
[
17
, Chapter III.8]
. Recall the power-sum symmetric function

\displaystyle p_{k}(\bm{x})=x_{1}^{k}+x_{2}^{k}+x_{3}^{k}+\cdots

in infinitely many variables
\bm{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},\dots)
. For an odd partition
\rho=(\rho_{1},\dots,\rho_{l})
, set

p_{\rho}=p_{\rho_{1}}p_{\rho_{2}}\cdots p_{\rho_{l}}

Let
\Gamma
be the
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra generated by odd power-sum symmetric functions

(k=1,3,5,\dots)

The degree on
\Gamma

\displaystyle\deg p_{k}=k,\qquad k=1,3,5,\dots.

For each

form a linear basis of

, respectively. The quantity

\lambda

\displaystyle p_{\rho}=\sum_{\lambda:|\lambda|=|\rho|}X^{\lambda}_{\rho}P_{\lambda}.

Put

are integers and encode character values of irreducible spin representations of symmetric groups

times a power of

Each symmetric function

-valued function on

\displaystyle f(\lambda)=f(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{l},0,0,\dots),\qquad\lambda=(\lambda_{1}>\lambda_{2}>\cdots>\lambda_{l}>0).

In particular,
p_{k}(\lambda)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{l}\lambda_{i}^{k}
. For two functions

for all

\displaystyle\mathfrak{p}_{\rho}(\lambda)=n(n-1)\cdots(n-k+1)\frac{X^{\lambda}_{\rho\cup(1^{n-k})}}{g^{\lambda}},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{p}_{2k-1}=p_{2k-1}+(\text{a polynomial in $p_{1},p_{3},\dots,p_{2k-3}$ of degree smaller than $2k-1$)}.

3.4
Super symmetric polynomials

Let
\lambda
be a strict partition. Put

\displaystyle\Phi(z;\lambda)=\prod_{i=1}^{\ell(\lambda)}\frac{z+\lambda_{i}}{z-\lambda_{i}}.

(3.3)

It is easy to see that

\displaystyle\log\Phi(z;\lambda)=2\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{p_{2k-1}(\lambda)}{2k-1}\frac{1}{z^{2k-1}}.

(3.4)

Recall functions
p_{n}^{\mathrm{super}}
introduced in Section
2.4
. The following proposition is a key in the proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Proposition 3.4
.

For each strict partition
\lambda
and
n=1,2,3,\dots
, we have

\displaystyle p_{n}^{\mathrm{super}}(D(\lambda))=\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor}\binom{n}{2j+1}\frac{1}{2^{n-2j-2}}p_{2j+1}(\lambda).

In particular, for each
k=1,2,3,\dots
,

\displaystyle p_{2k-1}^{\mathrm{super}}(D(\lambda))=2p_{2k-1}(\lambda)+\cdots\qquad\text{and}\qquad p_{2k}^{\mathrm{super}}(D(\lambda))=2kp_{2k-1}(\lambda)+\cdots,

where dots are linear combinations of
\{p_{2j-1}\}_{j=1,2,\dots,k-1}
with
\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}
-coefficients. Therefore the family
\big\{p_{2k-1}^{\mathrm{super}}(D(\cdot))\big\}_{k=1,2,3,\dots}
is an algebraic basis of
\Gamma
.

Proof.

Using (
2.4
) and (
3.1
), we have

\displaystyle\phi(z;D(\lambda))=\prod_{i=1}^{\ell(\lambda)}\frac{z+\big(\lambda_{i}-\frac{1}{2}\big)}{z-\big(\lambda_{i}+\frac{1}{2}\big)}=\Phi\big(z-\tfrac{1}{2};\lambda\big).

(3.5)

Taking the logarithm of the right hand side and expanding it at
z=\infty
,

\displaystyle\log\Phi\big(z-\tfrac{1}{2};\lambda\big)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{2p_{2k+1}(\lambda)}{2k+1}\big(z-\tfrac{1}{2}\big)^{-(2k+1)}

\displaystyle\hphantom{\log\Phi\big(z-\tfrac{1}{2}; \lambda\big)}{}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{2p_{2k+1}(\lambda)}{2k+1}\frac{1}{z^{2k+1}}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(2k+1)(2k+2)\cdots(2k+j)}{j!}\frac{1}{(2z)^{j}}.

Changing variable
j\mapsto n=2k+j+1
, we have

\displaystyle\log\Phi\big(z-\tfrac{1}{2};\lambda\big)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{z^{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor}2p_{2k+1}(\lambda)\frac{(2k+2)(2k+3)\cdots(n-1)}{(n-2k-1)!}\frac{1}{2^{n-2k-1}}

\displaystyle\hphantom{\log\Phi\big(z-\tfrac{1}{2}; \lambda\big)}{}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{nz^{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor}p_{2k+1}(\lambda)\binom{n}{2k+1}\frac{1}{2^{n-2k-2}}.

On the other hand, from (
2.4
) we have

\displaystyle\log\phi(z;D(\lambda))=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{nz^{n}}p_{n}^{\mathrm{super}}(D(\lambda)).

Comparing the coefficient of
z^{-n}
in the above equations, we obtain the desired identity.
∎

3.5
Moments of Rayleigh measures

Recall the Rayleigh measure
\tau_{\lambda}
defined in Section
2.5
.

Proposition 3.5
.

For a strict partition
\lambda
and
k=1,2,3,\dots
, we have

\displaystyle M_{2k}[\tau_{D(\lambda)}]=4kp_{2k-1}(\lambda)+\cdots\qquad\text{and}\qquad M_{2k+1}[\tau_{D(\lambda)}]=2k(2k+1)p_{2k-1}(\lambda)+\cdots,

where dots are linear combinations of
\{p_{2j-1}(\lambda)\}_{j=1,2,\dots,k-1}
with
\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}
-coefficients. Therefore the family
\{M_{2k}[\tau_{D(\cdot)}]\}_{k=1,2,\dots}
is an algebraic basis of
\Gamma
.

Proof.

From Lemma
2.2
we have
M_{n}[\tau_{D(\lambda)}]=np^{\mathrm{super}}_{n-1}(D(\lambda))+\cdots
, where dots are a linear combination of
p^{\mathrm{super}}_{j}(D(\lambda))

(j=1,2,\dots,n-2)
, with
\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}
-coefficients. The desired claim follows from Proposition
3.4
immediately.
∎

3.6
Moments and free cumulants of transition measures

Recall the transition measure
\mathfrak{m}_{\lambda}
defined in Section
2.3
.

Proposition 3.6
.

For strict partition
\lambda
and
k=1,2,3,\dots
, we have

\displaystyle M_{2k}[\mathfrak{m}_{D(\lambda)}]=2p_{2k-1}(\lambda)+\cdots\qquad\text{and}\qquad M_{2k+1}[\mathfrak{m}_{D(\lambda)}]=2kp_{2k-1}(\lambda)+\cdots,

where dots are functions in
\Gamma
of degree at most
2k-2
with
\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}
-coefficients. Therefore the family
\{M_{2k}[\mathfrak{m}_{D(\cdot)}]\}_{k=1,2,\dots}
is an algebraic basis of
\Gamma
.

Proof.

Lemma
2.3
implies that

\displaystyle M_{n}[\mathfrak{m}_{D(\lambda)}]=\frac{1}{n}M_{n}[\tau_{D(\lambda)}]+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\nu=(\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\dots)\vdash n\\
\nu_{1}<n\end{subarray}}z_{\nu}^{-1}M_{\nu_{1}}[\tau_{D(\lambda)}]M_{\nu_{2}}[\tau_{D(\lambda)}]\cdots.

(3.6)

We here apply Proposition
3.5
. Let
n=2k+1
. Then
\frac{1}{n}M_{n}[\tau_{D(\lambda)}]=2kp_{2k-1}(\lambda)+f_{k}(\lambda)
, where
f_{k}\!\in\!\Gamma
is of degree at most
2k{-}2
. We will estimate the degree of each term
M_{\nu_{1}}[\tau_{D(\lambda)}]M_{\nu_{2}}[\tau_{D(\lambda)}]{\cdots}\!
in (
3.6
). A partition
\nu\vdash 2k+1
with
\nu_{1}<2k+1
has at least one odd part
\nu_{s}
with
\deg M_{\nu_{s}}[\tau_{D(\lambda)}]=\nu_{s}-2
and has another part
\nu_{t}

(t\not=s)

with
\deg M_{\nu_{t}}[\tau_{D(\lambda)}]\leq\nu_{t}-1
. Hence the degree of each term
M_{\nu_{1}}[\tau_{D(\lambda)}]M_{\nu_{2}}[\tau_{D(\lambda)}]\cdots
is at most
(2k+1)-3=2k-2
. The case with
n=2k
is similar (and easier).
∎

Now using the free cumulant-moment formula (
2.1
), we obtain the following result for free cumulants
R_{n}(D(\lambda))=R_{n}[\mathfrak{m}_{D(\lambda)}]
.

Corollary 3.7
.

For strict partition
\lambda
and
k=1,2,3,\dots
, we have

\displaystyle R_{2k}(D(\lambda))=2p_{2k-1}(\lambda)+\cdots\qquad\text{and}\qquad R_{2k+1}(D(\lambda))=2kp_{2k-1}(\lambda)+\cdots,

where dots are functions in
\Gamma
of degree at most
2k-2

(
with
\mathbb{Q}
-coefficients
)
. Therefore the family
\{R_{2k}(D(\cdot))\}_{k=1,2,\dots}
is an algebraic basis of
\Gamma
.

3.7
Proof of main theorem

For each
k=1,2,3,\dots
and
\lambda\in\mathcal{SP}
, we set

\displaystyle\mathfrak{R}_{2k}(\lambda)=\frac{1}{2}R_{2k}(D(\lambda)).

The degree of
\mathfrak{R}_{2k}
in
\Gamma
is
2k-1
as we proved in Corollary
3.7
.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

As we saw in (
3.2
), the difference
\mathfrak{p}_{2k-1}-p_{2k-1}
belongs to
\mathbb{Q}[p_{1},p_{3},

\dots,p_{2k-3}]
and has degree at most
2k-2
.
So does the difference
\mathfrak{R}_{2k}-p_{2k-1}
by Corollary
3.7
. Therefore

\displaystyle\mathfrak{p}_{2k-1}-\mathfrak{R}_{2k}=(\mathfrak{p}_{2k-1}-p_{2k-1})-(\mathfrak{R}_{2k}-p_{2k-1})

belongs to
\mathbb{Q}[p_{1},p_{3},\dots,p_{2k-3}]
and is of degree at most
2k-2
. Again, using Corollary
3.7
, we see that
\mathbb{Q}[p_{1},p_{3},\dots,p_{2k-3}]=\mathbb{Q}[\mathfrak{R}_{2},\mathfrak{R}_{4},\dots,\mathfrak{R}_{2k-2}]
. Hence
\mathfrak{p}_{2k-1}-\mathfrak{R}_{2k}
is a polynomial in

\mathfrak{R}_{2},\mathfrak{R}_{4},\dots,\mathfrak{R}_{2k-2}
of degree at most
2k-2
.
∎

4
Another double diagrams

In this section, we deal with another double diagram of a strict partition
\lambda
. It is considered in
[
13
]
and De Stavola’s thesis
[
3
]
. We denote it by
\mathtt{D}(\lambda)
and call it the symmetrized double diagram. We do not give its explicit definition here, see
[
3
, Fig. 11]
. The diagram
\mathtt{D}(\lambda)
is not a Young diagram associated to any partition but we can extend notions in Section
2
to such diagrams, see
[
11
, Chapter 2.2]
.

The diagram
\mathtt{D}(\lambda)
is drawn in the Russian style and has Kerov’s interlacing coordinate

\displaystyle\mathtt{x}_{-m}<\mathtt{y}_{-m}<\mathtt{x}_{-1}<\mathtt{y}_{-1}<\mathtt{x}_{0}<\mathtt{y}_{1}<\mathtt{x}_{1}<\cdots<\mathtt{y}_{m}<\mathtt{x}_{m},

where
\mathtt{x}_{-i}=-\mathtt{x}_{i}
,
\mathtt{y}_{-i}=-\mathtt{y}_{i}
, and
\mathtt{x}_{0}=0
. Note that these numbers except
\mathtt{x}_{0}
belong to
\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}
. The corresponding Kerov’s transition measure
\mathfrak{m}_{\mathtt{D}(\lambda)}
is characterized by its Cauchy transform

\displaystyle G_{\mathfrak{m}_{\mathtt{D}(\lambda)}}(z)=\frac{\prod\limits_{i=1}^{m}(z-\mathtt{y}_{-i})(z-\mathtt{y}_{i})}{(z-\mathtt{x}_{0})\prod\limits_{i=1}^{m}(z-\mathtt{x}_{-i})(z-\mathtt{x}_{i})}=\frac{\prod\limits_{i=1}^{m}\big(z^{2}-\mathtt{y}_{i}^{2}\big)}{z\prod\limits_{i=1}^{m}\big(z^{2}-\mathtt{x}_{i}^{2}\big)}.

Lemma 4.1

(
[
13
, Proposition 2.6]
)
.

For a strict partition
\lambda
, we have

\displaystyle G_{\mathfrak{m}_{\mathtt{D}(\lambda)}}(z)=\frac{\Phi\big(z-\tfrac{1}{2};\lambda\big)}{z\Phi\big(z+\tfrac{1}{2};\lambda\big)}.

Compare this lemma with Proposition
3.8
. The remaining discussion is the same with that in the previous section, so we write only results.

For each
k=1,2,3,\dots
and
\lambda\in\mathcal{SP}
, we set

\displaystyle\mathtt{R}_{k}(\lambda)=\frac{1}{2}R_{k}[\mathfrak{m}_{\mathtt{D}(\lambda)}].

Then it has the expression

\displaystyle\mathtt{R}_{k}(\lambda)=-\frac{1}{2(k-1)}\big[z^{-k}\big]\left(\frac{\Phi(z+\tfrac{1}{2};\lambda)}{\Phi\big(z-\tfrac{1}{2};\lambda\big)}\right)^{k-1}

\displaystyle\hphantom{\mathtt{R}_k(\lambda)}{}=-\frac{1}{2(k-1)}\big[z^{-k}\big]\left\{\exp\left((k-1)\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{2p_{2j-1}(\lambda)}{2j-1}\big(z+\tfrac{1}{2}\big)^{-(2j-1)}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.\hphantom{\mathtt{R}_{k}(\lambda)=}{}\times\exp\left(-(k-1)\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{2p_{2j-1}(\lambda)}{2j-1}\big(z-\tfrac{1}{2}\big)^{-(2j-1)}\right)\right\}.

It is easy to see that
\mathtt{R}_{k}=0
for odd
k
. We remark that
R_{k}(D(\lambda))
does not vanish even if
k
is odd.

Example 4.2
.

\mathtt{R}_{2}=p_{1}
,
\mathtt{R}_{4}=p_{3}-3p_{1}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}p_{1}
.

Proposition 4.3
.

For each
k=1,2,3,\dots
, the spin character
\mathfrak{p}_{2k-1}
is a polynomial in
\mathtt{R}_{2},\mathtt{R}_{4},

\dots,\mathtt{R}_{2k}
of the form

\displaystyle\mathfrak{p}_{2k-1}=\mathtt{R}_{2k}+(\text{a polynomial in $\mathtt{R}_{2},\mathtt{R}_{4},\dots,\mathtt{R}_{2k-2}$}).

Example 4.4
.

\mathfrak{p}_{1}=\mathtt{R}_{2}
,
\mathfrak{p}_{3}=\mathtt{R}_{4}+\frac{7}{4}\mathtt{R}_{2}
.

From this example, we find that coefficients of polynomials in Proposition
4.3
are
not
integers. This indicates that these polynomials are ineligible for “spin Kerov polynomials”. We thus believe that the
\mathfrak{R}_{2k}
defined in Section
3
are the most appropriate choice for spin Kerov polynomials.

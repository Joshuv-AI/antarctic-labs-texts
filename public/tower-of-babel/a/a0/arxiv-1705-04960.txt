Abelian right perpendicular subcategories in module categories
Leonid Positselski

Abstract
. We show that an abelian category can be exactly, fully faithfully embedded into a module category as the right perpendicular subcategory to a set of modules or module morphisms if and only if it is a locally presentable abelian category with a projective generator, or in other words, the category of models of an additive algebraic theory of possibly infinite bounded arity. This includes the categories of contramodules over topological rings and other examples. Various versions of the definition of the right perpendicular subcategory are considered, all leading to the same class of abelian categories. We also discuss sufficient conditions under which the natural forgetful functors from the categories of contramodules to the related categories of modules are fully faithful.



Introduction

0.1.

The word
contramodule
roughly means “an object of a locally
presentable abelian category with a projective generator”.
In fact, cocomplete abelian categories with a projective generator can
be described as the categories of algebras/modules over additive
monads on the category of sets (see the discussion in the introduction
to the paper
[
32
]
or in
[
35
, Section 6]
).
Such a category is locally presentable if and only if the monad

\mathbb{T}\colon\mathsf{Sets}\longrightarrow\mathsf{Sets}
is accessible, i. e., the functor
\mathbb{T}

commutes with
\kappa
-filtered colimits in
\mathsf{Sets}
for a large enough
cardinal
\kappa
.

Algebras over a monad
\mathbb{T}
are sets with (possibly infinitary)
operations: for any set
X
, elements of the set
\mathbb{T}(X)
can be
interpreted as
X
-ary operations in
\mathbb{T}
-algebras.
That is why
categories of models of
\kappa
-ary algebraic
theories
is another name for the categories of algebras over

\kappa
-accessible monads on
\mathsf{Sets}
.
Such a category is abelian if and only if it is additive; for this,
the monad
\mathbb{T}
needs to be additive, that is it has to contain
the operations of addition, zero element, and inverse element, and
all the other operations in
\mathbb{T}
have to be additive with respect
to the abelian group structure defined by these.
In this case, it might be preferable to modify the terminology and
speak about “
\mathbb{T}
-modules” rather than “
\mathbb{T}
-algebras”.

So objects of a cocomplete abelian category with a projective generator
can be viewed as module-like structures with infinitary additive
operations.
Contramodules over topological associative rings provide typical
examples.
In fact, to any complete, separated topological ring
\mathfrak{R}
with
a base of neighborhoods of zero formed by open right ideals one can
assign an additive, accessible monad
\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{R}}\colon\mathsf{Sets}\longrightarrow\mathsf{Sets}
.
Then one defines
left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules
as

\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{R}}
-modules
[
23
,
24
,
26
,
32
,
35
,
31
]
.

0.2.

Thus, contramodules are sets with infinitary additive operations of
the arity bounded by some cardinal.
Hence it comes as a bit of a surprise when the natural forgetful
functors from the categories of contramodules to the related categories
of modules turn out be fully faithful.
An infinitary additive operation is uniquely determined by its finite
aspects.
Why should it be?
Nevertheless, such results are known to hold in a number of situations.

The observation that weakly

0.3.

In the most straightforward terms, the situation can be explained as

One says that an

0.6.

As it was discussed above, the class of all categories of modules over
additive, accessible monads on
\mathsf{Sets}
coincides with that of locally
presentable abelian categories with a projective generator.
The main result of this paper can be described as stating that
the class of all right perpendicular subcategories to sets of objects
in the categories of modules over associative rings also coincides with
the class of locally presentable abelian categories with a projective
generator.
The caveat is that one has to specify what one means by “right
perpendicular subcategories”.

Right perpendicular subcategories to (sets of) modules of projective
dimension at most
1
are not enough, and right perpendicular
subcategories to arbitrary sets of modules, as defined in
[
13
]
,
do not even need to be abelian.
However, one can consider the maximal reasonable class of right
perpendicular subcategories in the categories of modules over
associative rings, namely, those right perpendicular subcategories to
sets of modules that happen to be abelian and exactly embedded.

One can also consider the minimal reasonable class of right
perpendicular subcategories in modules, namely, those
right
\Ext^{0,1}
-perpendicular subcategories which happen to
coincide with the right
\Ext^{0..\infty}
-perpendicular subcategories
to the same set of modules.
These are automatically abelian and exactly embedded (in fact,
it suffices that the
\Ext^{0,1}
-perpendicular and
the
\Ext^{0..2}
-perpendicular subcategories coincide, for
this conclusion to hold).
Then we show that both the “maximal” and the “minimal reasonable”
classes of right perpendicular subcategories in the categories of
modules over associative rings embody the same classes of abstract
abelian categories, viz., the locally presentable abelian categories
with a projective generator.

0.7.

Actually, given that an exact embedding of a certain abelian category

\mathsf{B}
into the category of modules over some associative
R
ring
is shown to exist, one naturally wants more than just an arbitrary
such embedding.
One wants the image of this fully faithful functor to be closed under
extensions in
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}
.
One wants the functor
\mathsf{B}\longrightarrow R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}
to induce isomorphisms on
all the
\Ext
groups.
In the final analysis, one wants the induced triangulated functor
between the unbounded derived categories
\mathsf{D}(\mathsf{B})\longrightarrow\mathsf{D}(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}})

to be fully faithful.

In this paper we explain that, for a locally presentable abelian
category
\mathsf{B}
with a projective generator, the task of finding a fully
faithful embedding
\mathsf{B}\longrightarrow R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}
inducing an isomorphism on
the groups
\Ext^{i}
for all
i\leq n
is related to the task of
representing
\mathsf{B}
as the right
\Ext^{0,1}
-perpendicular subcategory
to a set of objects in
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}
coinciding with the right

\Ext^{0..n}
-perpendicular subcategory to the same set of modules.
Then we proceed to construct, for an arbitrary locally presentable
abelian category with a projective generator, a fully faithful embedding

\mathsf{B}\longrightarrow R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}
satisfying these conditions for
n=\infty
.
In fact, we prove that the induced triangulated functor

\mathsf{D}^{-}(\mathsf{B})\longrightarrow\mathsf{D}^{-}(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}})
is fully faithful.

Additive Monads on the Category of Sets

A
monad on the category of sets
is a covariant functor

\mathbb{T}\colon\mathsf{Sets}\longrightarrow\mathsf{Sets}
endowed with the natural transformations
of
multiplication

\phi_{\mathbb{T}}\colon\mathbb{T}\circ\mathbb{T}\longrightarrow\mathbb{T}
and

\epsilon_{\mathbb{T}}\colon\Id_{\mathsf{Sets}}\longrightarrow\mathbb{T}

\mathbb{T}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{T}\circ\mathbb{T}\longrightarrow\mathbb{T}

\phi_{\mathbb{T}}(\mathbb{T}\circ\epsilon_{\mathbb{T}})=\Id_{\mathsf{Sets}}=\phi_{\mathbb{T}}(\epsilon_{\mathbb{T}}\circ\mathbb{T})
.

is a set

For any set

Let

-algebra,

Then the element

Let
\mathbb{T}\colon\mathsf{Sets}\longrightarrow\mathsf{Sets}
be an additive monad.
Then the underlying set of the free
\mathbb{T}
-module with one generator

\mathbb{T}(*)
has a natural associative ring structure.
This is the ring of unary operations in the monad
\mathbb{T}
, which can be
also defined as the opposite ring
\mathbb{T}(*)=\Hom_{\mathbb{T}}(\mathbb{T}(*),\mathbb{T}(*))^{\mathrm{op}}
to the ring of endomorphisms of
the free
\mathbb{T}
-module with one generator
\mathbb{T}(*)\in\mathbb{T}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}
.
Let
R
be another associative ring and
\theta\colon R\longrightarrow\mathbb{T}(*)
be
an associative ring homomorphism.
Then the natural bijection
C\simeq\Hom_{\mathbb{T}}(\mathbb{T}(*),C)
endows
the underlying set of every
\mathbb{T}
-module
C
with a left

R
-module structure, providing a forgetful functor

\mathbb{T}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}\longrightarrow R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}
.

Let
\mathsf{B}\subset R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}
be a reflective full subcategory closed under
cokernels in
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}
.
Let
\Delta\colon R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}\longrightarrow\mathsf{B}
denote the reflector, and set

P=\Delta(R)
.
Then
P
is a projective generator of the abelian category
\mathsf{B}
,
and the coproduct of
X
copies of
P
in
\mathsf{B}
can be computed as

(see Example
1.2
(4)).

The following proposition provides a sufficient condition (in fact,
a criterion) for existence of an isomorphism of monads

\mathbb{T}\simeq\mathbb{T}_{P}
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Suppose that for every set
X
there is an isomorphism of
left
R
-modules
\Delta(R[X])\simeq\mathbb{T}(X)
forming a commutative
diagram with the adjunction morphism
R[X]\longrightarrow\Delta(R[X])

and the natural
R
-module morphism

R[X]\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu\Hom_{\mathbb{T}}(\mathbb{T}(*),\mathbb{T}(*))[X]\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu\Hom_{\mathbb{T}}(\mathbb{T}(*),\mathbb{T}(X))\mskip 1.5mu=\mskip 1.5mu\mathbb{T}(X).

Then the forgetful functor
\mathbb{T}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}\longrightarrow R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}
is fully
faithful and its essential image coincides with the full subcategory

\mathsf{B}\subset R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}
.
So there is an equivalence of abelian categories
\mathbb{T}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}\simeq\mathsf{B}
identifying the free
\mathbb{T}
-module
\mathbb{T}(X)
with
the projective object
P^{(X)}\in\mathsf{B}
for every set
X
.

Conversely, if the forgetful functor
\mathbb{T}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}\longrightarrow R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}
is
fully faithful and its essential image coincides with the full
subcategory
\mathsf{B}\subset R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}
, then for every set
X
there is
a natural isomorphism of left
R
-modules
\Delta(R[X])\simeq\mathbb{T}(X)

forming a commutative diagram with the adjunction morphism
R[X]\longrightarrow\Delta(R[X])
and the natural morphism
R[X]\longrightarrow\mathbb{T}(X)
.

Proof.

The forgetful functor
\mathbb{T}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}\longrightarrow R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}
is exact by
construction, and in particular, it preserves cokernels.
As every
\mathbb{T}
-module is the cokernel of a morphism of free

\mathbb{T}
-modules
\mathbb{T}(X)\longrightarrow\mathbb{T}(Y)
for some sets
X
and
Y
,
and the cokernel of any
R
-module morphism
P^{(X)}\longrightarrow P^{(Y)}

belongs to
\mathsf{B}
, it follows that the image of the forgetful functor
is contained in the full subcategory
\mathsf{B}\subset R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}
.

Furthermore, for any sets
X
and
Y
one has

\Hom_{\mathbb{T}}(\mathbb{T}(X),\mathbb{T}(Y))\simeq\Hom_{R}(R[X],\mathbb{T}(Y))\\
\simeq\Hom_{R}(R[X],P^{(Y)})\simeq\Hom_{R}(P^{(X)},P^{(Y)}),

so the forgetful functor is fully faithful in restriction to the full
subcategory of free
\mathbb{T}
-modules in
\mathbb{T}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}
.
Commutativity of the diagram involved in this computation can be
checked using the assumption of commutativity of the diagram in
the formulation of the proposition.
It follows easily that the forgetful functor is fully faithful on
the whole abelian category of
\mathbb{T}
-modules.

Finally, it remains to notice that every object of
\mathsf{B}
is
the cokernel of a morphism
P^{(X)}\longrightarrow P^{(Y)}
for some
sets
X
and
Y
, since
P
is a projective generator on
\mathsf{B}
.
Hence every object of
\mathsf{B}
belongs to the essential image of
the forgetful functor.

Alternatively, one can argue in the following way.
Suppose that we already know (e. g., from the first paragraph of
this proof) that the image of the forgetful functor
\mathbb{T}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}\longrightarrow R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}
is contained in the full subcategory

\mathsf{B}\subset R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}
.
Then, by the adjunction property of the functor
\Delta
, for
every set
X
there exists a unique left
R
-module morphism

\Delta(R[X])\longrightarrow\mathbb{T}(X)
forming a commutative triangle
diagram with the morphisms
R[X]\longrightarrow\Delta(R[X])
and

R[X]\longrightarrow\mathbb{T}(X)
.

According to Example
1.2
(4), the category

\mathsf{B}
is equivalent to the category of modules over the monad

\mathbb{T}_{\mathsf{B}}=\mathbb{T}_{P}\colon X\longmapsto\Delta(R[X])
.
The functor
\mathbb{T}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}\longrightarrow\mathsf{B}\simeq\mathbb{T}_{\mathsf{B}}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}
is a functor
between two categories of modules over monads on the category of
sets, and it forms a commutative triangle diagram of functors with
the forgetful functors
\mathbb{T}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}\longrightarrow\mathsf{Sets}
and
\mathbb{T}_{\mathsf{B}}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}\longrightarrow\mathsf{Sets}
.
Hence our functor is induced by a morphism of monads
\mathbb{T}_{\mathsf{B}}\longrightarrow\mathbb{T}
, which is provided by the above construction.
Thus the functor
\mathbb{T}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}\longrightarrow\mathsf{B}
is an equivalence of categories
whenever the map
\Delta(R[X])\longrightarrow\mathbb{T}(X)
is bijective
for every set

.

Conversely, if the forgetful functor is an equivalence of categories

\mathbb{T}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}\simeq\mathsf{B}
, then this equivalence of categories also
identifies the forgetful functors to the category of sets,

\mathbb{T}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}\longrightarrow\mathsf{Sets}
and
\mathsf{B}\longrightarrow R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}\longrightarrow\mathsf{Sets}
.
Thus it also forms a commutative diagram with the functors adjoint
to the forgetful functors to sets,
X\longmapsto\mathbb{T}(X)\colon\mathsf{Sets}\longrightarrow\mathbb{T}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}
and
X\longmapsto\Delta(R[X])\colon\mathsf{Sets}\longrightarrow\mathsf{B}
.
Hence a natural isomorphism of left
R
-modules
\Delta(R[X])\simeq\mathbb{T}(X)
forming a commutative diagram with the adjunction
maps of sets
X\longrightarrow\Delta(R[X])
and
X\longrightarrow\mathbb{T}(X)
, and
consequently also with the morphisms of left
R
-modules

R[X]\longrightarrow\Delta(R[X])
and
R[X]\longrightarrow\mathbb{T}(X)
.

Full-and-Faithfulness for Contramodules over Topological Rings

In this section, we are interested in modules over the monads

\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{R}}
associated with topological associative rings
\mathfrak{R}
,
as defined in Example
1.3
(2).
We refer to
[
23
, Remark A.3]
,
[
24
, Section 1.2]
,

[
26
, Section 2.1]
,
[
32
, Section 5]
,
[
35
, Section 6]
,
or
[
31
, Section 2.7]
for further and more detailed discussions
of the definition of a contramodule over a topological ring
(see also
[
23
, Section D.5.2]
,
[
24
, Section 1.10]

or
[
26
, Section 2.3]
for comparison with the definition of
a contramodule over a topological associative algebra over a field).

Let
\mathfrak{R}
be a complete, separated topological associative ring with
a base of neighborhoods of zero formed by open right ideals.
As in Section
2
,
we observe that the datum of an associative ring
R
together with
an associative ring homomorphism
\theta\colon R\longrightarrow\mathfrak{R}
defines
an exact forgetful functor
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--contra}}}\longrightarrow R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}
.

Let
\mathfrak{J}\subset\mathfrak{R}
be a right ideal.
We will say that a finite set of elements
s_{1}
, …,
s_{m}\in\mathfrak{J}

strongly generates
the right ideal
\mathfrak{J}
if for every family of
elements
r_{x}\in\mathfrak{J}
, indexed by some set
X
and converging to zero
in the topology of
\mathfrak{R}
, there exist families of elements

r_{j,x}\in\mathfrak{R}
,
j=1
, …,
m
, each of them indexed by the set
X

and converging to zero in the topology of
\mathfrak{R}
, such that

r_{x}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}s_{j}r_{j,x}
for all
x\in X
.
Since any finite family of elements in
\mathfrak{R}
converges to zero in
the topology of
\mathfrak{R}
, any finite set of elements of a right ideal

\mathfrak{J}\subset\mathfrak{R}
strongly generating the ideal
\mathfrak{J}
also generates
the right ideal
\mathfrak{J}
in the conventional sense
(cf.
[
24
, Section B.4]
).

The following theorem is a generalization of
[
30
, Theorem 2.1]
,
and its proof is similar to that in
[
30
]
(see
Examples
3.4
below for a discussion).

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{R}
be a complete, separated topological associative ring,

R
be an associative ring, and
\theta\colon R\longrightarrow\mathfrak{R}
be
a ring homomorphism with a dense image.
Assume that
\mathfrak{R}
has a countable base of neighborhoods of zero
consisting of open two-sided ideals, each of which, viewed as a right
ideal, is strongly generated by a finite set of elements lying in
the image of the map
\theta
.
Then the forgetful functor
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--contra}}}\longrightarrow R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}
is
fully faithful.

Proof.

Given a set
X
and a complete, separated topological abelian group

\mathfrak{A}
with a base of neighborhoods of zero formed by open subgroups

\mathfrak{U}\subset\mathfrak{A}
, denote by
\mathfrak{A}[[X]]
the abelian group

\varprojlim_{\mathfrak{U}\subset\mathfrak{A}}\mathfrak{A}/\mathfrak{U}[X]
of all infinite formal linear
combinations of elements of
X
with the coefficients converging to
zero in the topology of
\mathfrak{A}
.
Following the notation in
[
25
, Section D.1]
,

[
32
, Sections 5–6]
,
[
35
, Section 7.3]
,
and
[
31
, Section 2.8]
, for any closed subgroup
\mathfrak{A}\subset\mathfrak{R}

and any left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule
B
, we denote by
\mathfrak{A}\rightthreetimes B\subset B

the image of the composition
\mathfrak{A}[[B]]\longrightarrow B
of the natural
embedding
\mathfrak{A}[[B]]\longrightarrow\mathfrak{R}[[B]]
and the contraaction map

\pi_{B}\colon\mathfrak{R}[[B]]\longrightarrow B
.
The map
\mathfrak{A}[[B]]\longrightarrow B
is an abelian group homomorphism,
so
\mathfrak{A}\rightthreetimes B
is a subgroup in
B
.

As usually, for any left
\mathfrak{R}
-module
M
and any subgroup

A\subset\mathfrak{R}
, we denote by
AM\subset M
the subgroup generated by
the products
am
, where
a\in A
and
m\in M
.
So we have
\mathfrak{A}B\subset\mathfrak{A}\rightthreetimes B
for any closed subgroup
\mathfrak{A}\subset\mathfrak{R}

and any left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule
B
.
For any left ideal
I\subset\mathfrak{R}
and any left
\mathfrak{R}
-module
M
,
the subgroup
IM\subset M
is a left
\mathfrak{R}
-submodule in
M
.
For any closed left ideal
\mathfrak{I}\subset\mathfrak{R}
and any left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule

B
, the subgroup
\mathfrak{I}\rightthreetimes B\subset B
is a left
\mathfrak{R}
-subcontramodule
in
B
.
On the other hand, when a closed right ideal
\mathfrak{J}\subset\mathfrak{R}
is strongly
generated by a finite set of elements
s_{1}
, …,
s_{m}\in\mathfrak{J}
, one has

\mathfrak{J}\rightthreetimes B=\mathfrak{J}B=s_{1}B+\dotsb+s_{m}B
for any left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule
B
.

Let
B
and
C
be two left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules, and let

f\colon B\longrightarrow C
be a left
R
-module morphism.
The contraaction map
\pi_{B}\colon\mathfrak{R}[[B]]\longrightarrow B
is a surjective
morphism of left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules.
In order to show that
f
is an
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule morphism, it
suffices to check that the composition
\mathfrak{R}[[B]]\longrightarrow B\longrightarrow C

is an
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule morphism.
Hence we can replace
B
with
\mathfrak{R}[[B]]
and suppose that
B=\mathfrak{R}[[X]]

is a free left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule generated by a set
X
.

Then the composition
X\longrightarrow C
of the natural embedding
X\longrightarrow\mathfrak{R}[[X]]
with a left
R
-module morphism
f\colon\mathfrak{R}[[X]]\longrightarrow C
can be
extended uniquely to a left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule morphism

f^{\prime}\colon\mathfrak{R}[[X]]\longrightarrow C
.
Setting
g=f-f^{\prime}
, we have a left
R
-module morphism
g\colon\mathfrak{R}[[X]]\longrightarrow C
taking the elements of the set
X
to zero elements in
C
.
We have to show that
g=0
.
Without loss of generality, we can assume that the left

\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule
C
is generated by its subset
g(\mathfrak{R}[[X]])\subset C

(otherwise, replace
C
with its subcontramodule generated by

g(\mathfrak{R}[[X]])
).
Then we have to show that
C=0
.

Let
\mathfrak{I}\subset\mathfrak{R}
be an open two-sided ideal strongly generated, as
a right ideal, by a finite set of elements
s_{1}
, …,
s_{m}\in\mathfrak{I}

belonging to the image of
\theta
.
Denote by
I\subset R
the full preimage of the ideal
\mathfrak{I}
with respect
to ring homomorphism
\theta
; so
I
is a two-sided ideal in
R

and
R/I\simeq\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{I}
(since the image of
\theta
is dense in
\mathfrak{R}
).
Then we have
\mathfrak{I}[[X]]=\mathfrak{I}\rightthreetimes\mathfrak{R}[[X]]=s_{1}\mathfrak{R}[[X]]+\dotsb+s_{m}\mathfrak{R}[[X]]=I\mathfrak{R}[[X]]
and
\mathfrak{I}\rightthreetimes C=s_{1}C+\dotsb+s_{m}C=IC
.

Now the induced left
(R/I)
-module morphism
g/I\colon\mathfrak{R}[[X]]/I\mathfrak{R}[[X]]\longrightarrow C/IC
vanishes, because the left
(R/I)
-module

\mathfrak{R}[[X]]/I\mathfrak{R}[[X]]=\mathfrak{R}[[X]]/\mathfrak{I}[[X]]=\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{I}[X]\allowbreak=R/I[X]
is
generated by elements from
X
.
So the image of the morphism
g
is contained in
IC
.
Since the left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule
C
is generated by
g(\mathfrak{R}[[X]])

and
IC=\mathfrak{I}\rightthreetimes C
is a left
\mathfrak{R}
-subcontramodule in
C
, it
follows that
C=IC
.

We have shown that
C=\mathfrak{I}\rightthreetimes C
for a countable set of open two-sided
ideals
\mathfrak{I}\subset\mathfrak{R}
forming a base of neighborhoods of zero in
\mathfrak{R}
.
According to the contramodule Nakayama lemma
(
[
25
, Lemma D.1.2]
or
[
32
, Lemma 6.14]
),
it follows that
C=0
.
∎

to complete, separated topological associative rings

We will explain below in

among the objects of the ambient category

.

In the rest of this section we list some examples of topological rings

together with ring homomorphisms

into
\mathfrak{R}
from

We also provide a certain nonexample and a certain counterexample.

Remark 3.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{R}
be a complete, separated topological associative ring with
a countable base of neighborhoods of zero consisting of open right
ideals.
Let
\mathfrak{K}\subset\mathfrak{R}
be a closed two-sided ideal and
\mathfrak{S}=\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{K}
be
the quotient ring endowed with the quotient topology.
Then
\mathfrak{S}
is also a complete, separated topological associative ring
with a countable base of neighborhoods of zero consisting of open
right ideals, and the functor of restriction of scalars

\mathfrak{S}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--contra}}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--contra}}}
is fully faithful.
Indeed, any family of elements of
\mathfrak{S}
converging to zero in
the topology of
\mathfrak{S}
can be lifted to a family of elements of
\mathfrak{R}

converging to zero in the topology of
\mathfrak{R}
.

Moreover, if a homomorphism of associative rings
\theta\colon R\longrightarrow\mathfrak{R}

satisfies the assumptions of
Theorem
3.1
, then so does
the composition
\bar{\theta}\colon R\longrightarrow\mathfrak{S}
of the homomorphism
\theta

with the natural surjective homomorphism
\mathfrak{R}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{S}
.
Indeed, for any open right/two-sided ideal
\mathfrak{I}\subset\mathfrak{R}
, one can

The definition of the perpendicular subcategory in

In the next series of definitions, we list the most important ones
in our context.

Let
\mathsf{A}
be an abelian category and

be a full

We will say that

-perpendicular

in

\mathsf{A}
such that

•

an object

\mathsf{B}
if and only if for
every morphism

,

groups
\Hom_{\mathsf{A}}(f,B)\colon\Hom_{\mathsf{A}}(U^{\prime\prime},B)\allowbreak\longrightarrow\Hom_{\mathsf{A}}(U^{\prime},B)

is an isomorphism.

In this case, we will say that
\mathsf{B}
is the right
0
-perpendicular
subcategory to the class of morphisms
\mathsf{F}
in
\mathsf{A}
and write

\mathsf{B}=\mathsf{F}^{\perp_{0}}\subset\mathsf{A}
.

Furthermore, we will say that
\mathsf{B}
is a
right

1
-perpendicular subcategory
in
\mathsf{A}
if there exists a class of
objects
\mathsf{E}
in the category
\mathsf{A}
such that

belongs to
\mathsf{B}
if and only if for
every object

one has
\Hom_{\mathsf{A}}(E,B)=0=\Ext_{\mathsf{A}}^{1}(E,B)
.

In this case, we will say that
\mathsf{B}
is the right

-perpendicular
subcategory to the class of objects
\mathsf{E}
in
\mathsf{A}
and write

\mathsf{B}=\mathsf{E}^{\perp_{0,1}}\subset\mathsf{A}
.

Let

We will say that a full subcategory
\mathsf{B}\subset\mathsf{A}
is a
right

-perpendicular subcategory
in
\mathsf{A}
if there exists a class of
objects
\mathsf{E}
in the category
\mathsf{A}
such that

•

belongs to

if and only if for

-perpendicular

\mathsf{B}
is the right

-perpendicular subcategory to

in
\mathsf{A}
and write

-perpendicular subcategory

\mathsf{E}\subset\mathsf{A}

in

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\mathsf{A}
be an abelian category. Then

(a)
any right
0
-perpendicular subcategory

\mathsf{B}\subset\mathsf{A}
is closed under arbitrary limits, and in particular,
under infinite products and kernels in
\mathsf{A}
;

(b)
any right
1
-perpendicular subcategory

\mathsf{B}\subset\mathsf{A}
is closed under infinite products, kernels, and
extensions in
\mathsf{A}
;

(c)
any right
2
-perpendicular subcategory

\mathsf{B}\subset\mathsf{A}
is closed under infinite products, kernels, extensions,
and cokernels in
\mathsf{A}
.
Hence any right
2
-perpendicular subcategory
\mathsf{B}\subset\mathsf{A}

is abelian and its embedding functor
\mathsf{B}\longrightarrow\mathsf{A}
is exact;

(d)
the right
0
-perpendicular subcategory

\mathsf{B}\subset\mathsf{A}
to any class of morphisms between projective objects
in
\mathsf{A}
is closed under infinite products, kernels, extensions, and
cokernels in
\mathsf{A}
.
Hence any such full subcategory
\mathsf{B}\subset\mathsf{A}
is abelian
and its embedding functor
\mathsf{B}\longrightarrow\mathsf{A}
is exact.

Proof.

Part (a) is
[
1
, Observation 1.34]
.
Part (b) is
[
13
, Proposition 1.1]
.
In part (c), one notices that any full subcategory in
\mathsf{A}
closed
under the kernels of all morphisms and the cokernels of monomorphisms
is also closed under the cokernels of all morphisms.
Checking that
\mathsf{B}=\mathsf{E}^{\perp_{0,1}}=\mathsf{E}^{\perp_{0..2}}
is closed under
the cokernels of monomorphisms is easy.
Part (d) holds, because the full subcategory of isomorphisms in
the category of morphisms in the category of abelian groups (or in
any other abelian category generally) is closed under infinite
products, kernels, extensions, and cokernels
(see
[
28
, proof of Theorem 1.2 and Remark 1.3]
for
an alternative argument).
∎

Example 4.3
.

Now the description of the category of quasi-coherent sheaves

Assuming Vopěnka’s principle, any right

The following lemma is a direct generalization of
Example

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\mathsf{A}
be an abelian category with a projective generator, and
let
\mathsf{B}
be an abelian, exactly embedded, reflective full
subcategory in
\mathsf{A}
.
Then
\mathsf{B}
is also an abelian category with a projective generator.

Proof.

Denote the reflector by
\Delta\colon\mathsf{A}\longrightarrow\mathsf{B}
, and let
Q
be
a projective generator of
\mathsf{A}
.
Then
P=\Delta(Q)
is a projective generator of
\mathsf{B}
.
∎

From this point on, we are interested in the abelian, exactly
embedded right
n
-perpendicular subcategories
\mathsf{B}
to sets
of objects or morphisms in the categories of modules over
associative rings,
\mathsf{A}=R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}
.
According to
Lemmas
4.4

and
4.5
, any such abelian

\mathsf{B}
is locally presentable and has a projective
generator.
Our aim is to prove the converse assertion, for every
n\geq 0

and

.

Let
\mathsf{B}
be a locally
\kappa
-presentable abelian category,
R

be an associative ring, and
\Theta\colon\mathsf{B}\longrightarrow R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}
be an exact
functor preserving infinite products and
\kappa
-filtered
colimits.
Then the functor
\Theta
is a right adjoint
[
1
, Theorem 1.66]

so it has a left adjoint functor
\Delta\colon R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}\longrightarrow\mathsf{B}
.
Set

, where
R
is the free left
R

generator; then

is a projective object in
\mathsf{B}

The functor

The projective object

if and
only if the exact functor

is

, i. e., it
takes nonisomorphisms to nonisomorphisms, or equivalently, takes

An exact functor between abelian categories is conservative if and
only if it is faithful.
Thus conservative exact functors

preserving infinite products and
\kappa
-filtered colimits are
indexed by projective generators

endowed with

We are interested in those of such functors

The following theorem is the main result of this section.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
\mathsf{B}
be a locally presentable abelian category with
a projective generator
P
, let
R
be an associative ring, and let

\theta\colon R\longrightarrow\Hom_{\mathsf{B}}(P,P)^{\mathrm{op}}

Assume that the related functor
\Theta=\Hom_{\mathsf{B}}(P,{-})\colon\mathsf{B}\longrightarrow R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}

(a)
the full subcategory
\Theta(\mathsf{B})\subset R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}
is
the right
0
-perpendicular subcategory to a set of morphisms in

R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}
;

(b)
assuming additionally that

is injective, the full

\Theta(\mathsf{B})\subset R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}
is a right

1
-perpendicular subcategory (to a set of objects) in

if and

(c)
assuming that
\theta

\Theta(\mathsf{B})\subset R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}

is the right
n
-perpendicular subcategory to a set of objects in

R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}
whenever the induced maps between the Ext groups

\Theta\colon\Ext_{\mathsf{B}}^{i}(B,C)\longrightarrow\Ext_{R}^{i}(\Theta(B),\Theta(C))
are
isomorphisms for all
B
,

and

;

(d)
assuming that
\theta
is injective, the full subcategory

\Theta(\mathsf{B})\subset R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}
is the right
\infty
-perpendicular
subcategory to a set of objects in
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}
whenever the induced maps

\Theta\colon\Ext_{\mathsf{B}}^{i}(B,C)\longrightarrow\Ext_{R}^{i}(\Theta(B),\Theta(C))
are
isomorphisms for all
B
,
C\in\mathsf{B}
and all

Proof.

According to Example
1.2
(3), the functor

\Hom_{\mathsf{B}}(P,{-})
identifies the category
\mathsf{B}
with the category of
modules over the additive monad
\mathbb{T}\colon X\longmapsto\Hom_{\mathsf{B}}(P,P^{(X)})
.
Assume that the category
\mathsf{B}
is locally
\kappa
-presentable and
the object
P
is
\kappa
-presentable; then the monad
\mathbb{T}
is

\kappa
-accessible.
Identifying
\mathsf{B}
with
\mathbb{T}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}
, the functor
\Theta
gets
identified with the forgetful functor
\Hom_{\mathbb{T}}(\mathbb{T}(*),{-})
discussed
in the beginning of

.
In particular, for every set
X
we have the free
\mathbb{T}
-module

\mathbb{T}(X)\in\mathbb{T}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}
corresponding to the object

,
and there is a natural left
R

, as in

.
Denote this morphism by

.

Part (a): we claim that a left
R

belongs to
the full subcategory
\Theta(\mathsf{B})\subset R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}
if and only if
the morphism of abelian groups

\Hom_{R}(\theta_{Z},C)\colon\Hom_{R}(\mathbb{T}(Z),C)\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu\Hom_{R}(R[Z],C)

is an isomorphism for all sets

of cardinality less than
\kappa
.
Indeed, if
C\in\Theta(\mathsf{B})
, then

\Hom_{R}(\mathbb{T}(X),C)\simeq C^{X}\simeq\Hom_{R}(R[X],C)

for all sets

, since the functor
\Theta
is fully faithful by

Conversely, assume that
\Hom_{R}(\theta_{Z},C)
is an isomorphism for
all sets

of cardinality less than
\kappa
.
Then
\Hom_{R}(\theta_{X},C)
is an isomorphism for all sets

,
because
R[X]=\varinjlim_{Z\subset X}R[Z]
and

\mathbb{T}(X)=\varinjlim_{Z\subset X}\mathbb{T}(Z)
in the category of left

R

\kappa
-filtered colimit is taken over
all the subsets

of cardinality less than
\kappa
.

In particular, we have
\Hom_{R}(\mathbb{T}(C),C)=\Hom_{R}(R[C],C)=C^{C}
,
so there is a natural surjective morphism of left
R

\mathbb{T}(C)\longrightarrow C
corresponding to the identity map

.
Let

denote the kernel of this morphism; then the morphism

\Hom_{R}(\theta_{X},K)
is an isomorphism for all sets

\Hom_{R}(\theta_{X},C)
are.
Hence we also have a natural surjective morphism of left

.
Now

, since

\mathbb{T}(K)

and the full subcategory

\Theta(\mathsf{B})\subset R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}
is closed under cokernels.

Part (b): obviously,

Proposition 4.8
.

Let

be a locally presentable abelian category with a projective
generator

, let

,

and

if and only if the induced triangulated functor
between the bounded above derived categories

\Theta\colon\mathsf{D}^{-}(\mathsf{B})\longrightarrow\mathsf{D}^{-}(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}})

Proof.

Clearly, an exact functor between abelian categories

induces isomorphisms on all the Ext groups if and only if the induced
triangulated functor between the bounded derived categories

According to Proposition
6.5

below, the functor
\mathsf{D}^{-}(\mathsf{B})\longrightarrow\mathsf{D}^{-}(\mathsf{A})

and only if the functor
\mathsf{D}^{\mathsf{b}}(\mathsf{B})\longrightarrow\mathsf{D}^{\mathsf{b}}(\mathsf{A})
is, provided
that there are enough projective objects in the category
\mathsf{A}
and
the exact functor
\mathsf{B}\longrightarrow\mathsf{A}
has a left adjoint

\Delta\colon\mathsf{A}\longrightarrow\mathsf{B}
.

In the situation at hand, obviously there are enough projectives in

R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--mod}}}

4.6
that
the functor
\Theta
has a left adjoint.
Alternatively, one can construct the functor

explicitly, as

R
-module

to the object

for all sets

and acting on morphisms

-modules in the natural way (determined by

).
∎

Now let us restrict to the particular case when the ring

Let
\mathsf{B}

.

Remark 6.3
.

We are not aware of a definition equivalent to our notion of

\kappa
-flatness appearing anywhere in the previously existing
literature, but certainly there are all kinds of similar or
related definitions known for many years.

In particular, there is the classical notion of
a
\kappa
-free
module
[
8
]
.
In the similar spirit (cf.
[
7
]
), one can define

\kappa
-projective
modules.
Specifically, an
R
-module
M
is said to be

\kappa
-projective if it admits a system of submodules

(which is said to
witness
the
\kappa
-projectivity of
M
)
such that (1) every
R
-module
M_{\alpha}
is projective with less
than
\kappa
generators; (2) every subset of
M
of the cardinality
less than
\alpha
is contained in one of the modules
M_{\alpha}
;
and (3) the set of submodules
M_{\alpha}\subset M
is closed under
unions of well-ordered chains of length smaller than
\kappa
.
An
R
-module
M
is said to be
\kappa
-projective
in the weak sense
if it has a witnessing system of submodules
satisfying the conditions (1) and (2)
[
8
, Section IV.1]
.

An
R
-module is
\kappa
-projective in the weak sense if and only
if it is the colimit of a
\kappa
-filtered diagram of projective

R
-modules with less than
\kappa
generators
and injective
morphisms between them
.
Indeed, given a module that is
\kappa
-projective in the weak
sense, all its projective submodules with less than
\kappa
generators
form such a diagram.
Thus
\kappa
-projectivity is a stronger condition than

\kappa
-flatness.
When

is a left hereditary ring, there is no difference between

\kappa
-projectivity and
\kappa
-projectivity in the weak sense
for left

-modules.
Assuming additionally that every left ideal in

is generated by

\kappa
elements (so any

-generated left

\kappa
-presentable), these two conditions are also

\kappa
-flatness.
Indeed, let

be a
\kappa
-flat left
R
-module.
Given a subset of cardinality less than
\kappa
in

R

generated by this subset.
Then

is
\kappa
-presentable, so the injective morphism

It follows that

is a submodule in

Another classical concept closely related to

A left

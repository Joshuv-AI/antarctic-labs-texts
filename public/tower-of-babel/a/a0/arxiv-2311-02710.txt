On reflections of roots in positive characteristic
Alexey Lebedev

Abstract
. For a Lie superalgebra with Cartan matrix over a field of positive characteristic, some information about its root system in terms of the system of simple roots corresponding to the Chevalley generators is described, under certain given assumptions. This information may be useful for finding other systems of simple roots of such a Lie superalgebra and other Cartan matrices which produce Lie superalgebras isomorphic to a given one.

1.2.
The aim of the paper.

If
{\mathfrak{g}}
is a finite-dimensional Lie algebra with Cartan matrix over
{\mathbb{C}}
, and
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}
is its system of simple roots, reflection in

(2)

\alpha_{k}

\alpha_{j}+m\alpha_{k}

The same description applies to the transformation of a system of simple roots under an “odd reflection” (which is not a reflection in the geometrical sense), first described by Serganova in
[

Given a Cartan matrix and the corresponding Lie superalgebra

This note is written in the hope that the same approach (

2.
Main result

In what follows, notations from Subsection
1.1
apply. The characteristic
p
is assumed to be positive. Let
m
always denote an integer; i.e., even when the upper bound of
m
is given by
m\leq B_{kj}
,
m
cannot be
+\infty
even if
B_{kj}=+\infty
.

Assumptions
.

1) For all
m
such that
0\leq m\leq B_{kj}
, the space
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\alpha_{j}+m\alpha_{k}}
is
1
-dimensional.

2) If
m
is such that
0\leq m<B_{kj}
, then the action of
e_{k}^{+}
on
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\alpha_{j}+m\alpha_{k}}
is non-zero;

if
m
is such that
1\leq m\leq B_{kj}
, then the action of
e_{k}^{-}
on
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\alpha_{j}+m\alpha_{k}}
is non-zero.

Statement
.

If the above assumptions are true, then
B_{kj}
can be found in the following way. Define the sequence
(d_{m})_{m=-1}^{\infty}
with values in
{\mathbb{K}}
as follows:

d_{-1}=0
;

d_{m}=(-1)^{i_{k}}(d_{m-1}-A_{kj}-mA_{kk})
for any
m\geq 0
.

Then,
B_{kj}=\inf\{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}\mid d_{m}=0\}
.

Proof.

For every
m
such that
0\leq m\leq B_{kj}
, select a non-zero vector
v_{m}\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\alpha_{j}+m\alpha_{k}}
. Then, for any
m
such that
0\leq m<B_{kj}
, we have
[e_{k}^{+},v_{m}]=c_{m}^{+}v_{m+1}
and
[e_{k}^{-},v_{m+1}]=c_{m}^{-}v_{m}
for some non-zero
c_{m}^{+},c_{m}^{-}\in{\mathbb{K}}
.

For any
m
such that
0\leq m<B_{kj}
, denote
d^{\prime}_{m}:=c_{m}^{+}c_{m}^{-}
; also, denote
d^{\prime}_{-1}:=0
and, if
B_{kj}<+\infty
, denote
d^{\prime}_{B_{kj}}:=0
. Then, for any
m
such that
0\leq m\leq B_{kj}
, we have
[e_{k}^{+},[e_{k}^{-},v_{m}]]=d^{\prime}_{m-1}v_{m}
and
[e_{k}^{-},[e_{k}^{+},v_{m}]]=d^{\prime}_{m}v_{m}
.

Since
[h_{k},v_{m}]=(A_{kj}+mA_{kk})v_{m}
is also equal to

[e_{k}^{+},[e_{k}^{-},v_{m}]]-(-1)^{i_{k}}[e_{k}^{-},[e_{k}^{+},v_{m}]]=(d^{\prime}_{m-1}-(-1)^{i_{k}}d^{\prime}_{m})v_{m},

we see that the
d^{\prime}_{m}
satisfy the relations for the
d_{m}
, i.e.,
d^{\prime}_{m}=d_{m}
for any
m
such that
0\leq m\leq B_{kj}
. Since
d^{\prime}_{m}\neq 0
for any
m
such that
0\leq m<B_{kj}
and
d^{\prime}_{B_{kj}}=0
if
B_{kj}<+\infty
, the statement is proved.∎

Now let us find what
B_{kj}
is equal to, using this approach. First of all, if
A_{kj}=0
, then
d_{0}=0
, and therefore
B_{kj}=0
.

In what follows, we will assume that
A_{kj}\neq 0
. For any

x\in{\mathbb{Z}}/p{\mathbb{Z}}
, we will denote by
x_{\mathbb{Z}}
the minimal non-negative integer congruent to
x
modulo
p
.

2.2.
i_{k}={\bar{0}}
.

In this case,
d_{m}=d_{m-1}-A_{kj}-mA_{kk}
for any
m\geq 0
, and by induction,

d_{m}=-(m+1)A_{kj}-\Big(\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.25pt}{$\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{m+1}{2}$}}{\raisebox{0.25pt}{$\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{m+1}{2}$}}{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{$\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{1}{m+1}{2}$}}{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{$\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{1}{m+1}{2}$}}\Big)A_{kk}.

2.2.1.
i_{k}={\bar{0}}
,
A_{kk}=0
.

In this case,

d_{m}=-(m+1)A_{kj}
, so
d_{m}=0
if and only if
m\equiv-1\pmod{p}
,

i.e.,
B_{kj}=p-1
.

2.2.2.
i_{k}={\bar{0}}
,
A_{kk}\neq 0
,
p\neq 2
.

In this case,
d_{m}=-\frac{1}{2}(m+1)(2A_{kj}+mA_{kk})
, so
d_{p-1}=0
, while for any
m
such that
0\leq m\leq p-2
, we have

d_{m}=0
if and only if
2A_{kj}+mA_{kk}=0
.

This means that

B_{kj}=\begin{cases}\left(-\frac{2A_{kj}}{A_{kk}}\right)_{\mathbb{Z}}&\text{if $\frac{A_{kj}}{A_{kk}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}/p{\mathbb{Z}}$;}\\
p-1&\text{if $\frac{A_{kj}}{A_{kk}}\not\in{\mathbb{Z}}/p{\mathbb{Z}}$.}\end{cases}

2.2.3.
i_{k}={\bar{0}}
,
A_{kk}\neq 0
,
p=2
.

In this case,

\begin{array}[]{ll}d_{0}&=A_{kj}\neq 0;\\
d_{1}&=A_{kk}\neq 0;\\
d_{2}&=A_{kj}+A_{kk};\\
d_{3}&=0.\\
\end{array}

This means that

B_{kj}=\begin{cases}2&\text{if $A_{kj}=A_{kk}$},\\
3&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

2.3.
i_{k}={\bar{1}}
.

In this case,
d_{m}=A_{kj}+mA_{kk}-d_{m-1}
for any
m\geq 0
. By induction,

d_{m}=\begin{cases}A_{kj}+lA_{kk}&\text{if $m=2l$, $l\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}$;}\\
lA_{kk}&\text{if $m=2l-1$, $l\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}$.}\end{cases}

2.3.1.
i_{k}={\bar{1}}
,
A_{kk}=0
.

In this case,
d_{0}=A_{kj}\neq 0
, and
d_{1}=0
, so
B_{kj}=1
.

2.3.2.
i_{k}={\bar{1}}
,
A_{kk}\neq 0
.

In this case,
d_{2p-1}=0
, and for any
m
such that
0\leq m\leq 2p-2
, we have
d_{m}=0
if and only if
m=2l
, where
l\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
A_{kj}+lA_{kk}=0
. Therefore,

B_{kj}=\begin{cases}2\left(-\frac{A_{kj}}{A_{kk}}\right)_{\mathbb{Z}}&\text{if $\frac{A_{kj}}{A_{kk}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}/p{\mathbb{Z}}$;}\\
2p-1&\text{if $\frac{A_{kj}}{A_{kk}}\not\in{\mathbb{Z}}/p{\mathbb{Z}}$.}\end{cases}

Finally, putting this all together, we get:

(3)

B_{kj}=\begin{cases}0&\text{if $A_{kj}=0$;}\\
p-1&\text{if $i_{k}={\bar{0}}$, $A_{kk}=0$, $A_{kj}\neq 0$;}\\
&\text{or if $i_{k}={\bar{0}}$, $p\neq 2$, $A_{kk}\neq 0$, $\frac{A_{kj}}{A_{kk}}\not\in{\mathbb{Z}}/p{\mathbb{Z}}$;}\\
\left(-\frac{2A_{kj}}{A_{kk}}\right)_{\mathbb{Z}}&\text{if $i_{k}={\bar{0}}$, $p\neq 2$, $A_{kk}\neq 0$, $\frac{A_{kj}}{A_{kk}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}/p{\mathbb{Z}}$;}\\
2&\text{if $i_{k}={\bar{0}}$, $p=2$, $A_{kj}=A_{kk}\neq 0$;}\\
3&\text{if $i_{k}={\bar{0}}$, $p=2$, $A_{kk}\neq 0$, $A_{kj}\neq A_{kk}$, $A_{kj}\neq 0$;}\\
1&\text{if $i_{k}={\bar{1}}$, $A_{kk}=0$, $A_{kj}\neq 0$;}\\
2\left(-\frac{A_{kj}}{A_{kk}}\right)_{\mathbb{Z}}&\text{if $i_{k}={\bar{1}}$, $A_{kk}\neq 0$, $\frac{A_{kj}}{A_{kk}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}/p{\mathbb{Z}}$;}\\
2p-1&\text{if $i_{k}={\bar{1}}$, $A_{kk}\neq 0$, $\frac{A_{kj}}{A_{kk}}\not\in{\mathbb{Z}}/p{\mathbb{Z}}$.}\end{cases}

Note that all the obtained values of
B_{kj}
for
p>0
are finite. It is not so for
p=0
: e.g., if
p=0
,
i_{k}={\bar{0}}
,
A_{kk}\neq 0
,
-\frac{A_{kj}}{2A_{kk}}\not\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
, then
B_{kj}=+\infty
.

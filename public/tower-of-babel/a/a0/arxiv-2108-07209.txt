Fractional Poincaré and localized Hardy inequalities on metric spaces Thanks: B.D. was partially supported by grant NCN 2015/18/E/ST1/00239.
Bartłomiej Dyda, Juha Lehrbäck, Antti V. Vähäkangas

Abstract
. We prove fractional Sobolev–Poincaré inequalities, capacitary versions of fractional Poincaré inequalities, and pointwise and localized fractional Hardy inequalities in a metric space equipped with a doubling measure. Our results generalize and extend earlier work where such inequalities have been considered in the Euclidean spaces or in the non-fractional setting in metric spaces. The results concerning pointwise and localized variants of fractional Hardy inequalities are new even in the Euclidean case.

1.
Introduction

Let
X=(X,d,\mu)
be a metric measure space
and let
1\leq p,q,t<\infty
and
0<s<1
.
The fractional
(s,q,p,t)
-Poincaré (or Sobolev–Poincaré)
inequality on
X
reads as

(1.1)

\biggl(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}\lvert u(x)-u_{B}\rvert^{q}\,dx\biggr)^{1/q}\leq c_{P}r^{s}\biggl(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773pt\lambda B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{\lambda B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{\lambda B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{\lambda B}}\biggl(\int_{\lambda B}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{t}}{d(x,y)^{st}\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}\,dy\biggr)^{p/t}\,dx\biggr)^{1/p},

where
dx=d\mu(x)
and
dy=d\mu(y)
.
We say that
X

supports a
(s,q,p,t)
-Poincaré inequality if
there are constants
c_{P}>0
and
\lambda\geq 1
such that
inequality (
1.1
)
holds for every ball
B=B(x_{0},r)\subset X

and for all functions
u\colon X\to{\mathbb{R}}
that are
integrable on balls.

If
q\leq\min\{p,t\}
and the measure
\mu
is doubling,
then it is straightforward to show that the space
X

supports a
(s,q,p,t)
-Poincaré inequality;
see Lemma
2.2
.
This is quite different compared to the usual (i.e. non-fractional) Poincaré inequalities,
whose validity in a metric measure space is usually an indication of
the existence of a rich geometric structure in the space;
we refer to the monographs
[
1
,
15
]

for more explanation and examples.

The main goal in this work is to prove stronger variants of
fractional inequalities,
such as (Sobolev–)Poincaré inequalities for
q>p
, capacitary versions of Poincaré inequalities,
and pointwise and localized Hardy inequalities.
The validity of these stronger variants
often requires additional assumptions on the space and the functions and
sets in the
inequalities. For example, in the so-called boundary Poincaré inequalities
the mean value
u_{B}
on the left-hand side of (
1.1
)
can be omitted if the set where
u=0
(i.e. the “boundary”)
is large enough.

The parameter
1\leq t<\infty

in inequality (
1.1
)
allows certain flexibility in the applications,
for instance in the proof of
the localized fractional Hardy inequality

(1.2)

\int_{B\setminus E}\frac{\lvert u(x)\rvert^{p}}{d(x,E)^{sp}}\,dx\leq C\int_{\lambda B}\int_{\lambda B}\frac{\lvert u(x)-u(y)\rvert^{p}}{d(x,y)^{sp}\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}\,dy\,dx,

where
0<s<1
,
1<p<\infty
,
\lambda\geq 1
,

E\subset X
is a closed set,

B=B(w,r)
for some
w\in E
and
0<r<\diam(E)
, and

u\colon X\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a continuous function with
u=0
on
E
.
As one of our main results we show that the validity of inequality (
1.2
)
is essentially characterized by dimensional information related to the set
E
.
More precisely,
\ucodima(E)<sp
is sufficient and
\ucodima(E)\leq sp
is necessary
for (
1.2
), where
\ucodima(E)
is the upper Assouad codimension of

E
, see Definition
4.4
.
The upper bound for this codimension means that the set
E

must be sufficiently large in comparison to the size of the ambient space
X
.
In the Euclidean case
X={\mathbb{R}}^{n}
we have
\ucodima(E)=n-\ldima(E)
, where
\ldima
is the
lower dimension (or lower Assouad dimension) of
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.

We obtain the localized inequality (
1.2
) as a consequence of
a pointwise fractional Hardy inequality, given in terms of a maximal operator.
In the non-fractional case in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
pointwise Hardy inequalities were introduced in
[
11
]
and
[
20
]
.
Sufficient and necessary
conditions for pointwise Hardy inequalities in metric spaces have been given
in
[
21
]
;
see also
[
22
]
for weighted variants.
Fractional Hardy inequalities on open sets
have been studied
in the Euclidean space
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

for instance in
[
4
,
7
,
9
,
10
,
18
,
24
]

and in general metric spaces in
[
5
,
8
]
,
but the present pointwise and localized versions of fractional Hardy inequalities,
as well as the boundary Poincaré inequalities, are new even in the Euclidean case.
Sobolev–Poincaré
inequalities have also been considered in
more general sets than balls,
in particular in the so-called John domains,
see
[
2
,
16
,
17
]

and the references therein.

The outline for the rest of the paper is as follows.
In Section
2
we review the necessary
definitions and notation on metric measure spaces
and give in Lemma
2.2
the basic versions of
fractional Poincaré inequalities for
q\leq p
;
these are used as a starting point in the proofs of the stronger inequalities
in the subsequent sections.
Section
3
is devoted to extending the range in the
fractional (Sobolev–)Poincaré inequalities to
q>p
, following the ideas in the proofs
of the corresponding fractional results in the Euclidean case
[
6
]
as well in the non-fractional
results in metric spaces
[
1
]
.
In Section
4
we introduce a variant of the relative fractional
capacity and prove a Maz
′
ya type capacitary Poincaré inequality in Theorem
4.3
.
Boundary Poincaré inequalities are obtained in Theorem
4.7

and Corollary
4.8
under
the dimensional condition
\ucodima(E)<sp
,
which is connected to the relative capacity via suitable Hausdorff contents;
see Definition
4.5
and Lemma
4.6
.
In Section
5
, the localized Hardy inequality (
1.2
)
is obtained in Theorem
5.2

as a consequence of a pointwise fractional Hardy inequality, see Theorem
5.1
,
which in turn is based on the boundary Poincaré inequality in Theorem
4.7
.
Theorem
5.3
then shows the necessity of the condition
\ucodima(E)\leq sp

for the localized inequality (
1.2
).
In Sections
4
and
5

our proofs often follow the main lines of the
proofs from the non-fractional case,
as for instance in
[
1
,
15
,
21
]
, but
due to the non-locality of the setting several modifications are needed in the proofs.

2.
Preliminaries

We assume throughout this paper that
X=(X,d,\mu)
is
a metric measure space (with at least two points), where

\mu
is a Borel measure supported on
X
such that

\mu(\{x\})=0
for all
x\in X
and

0<\mu(B)<\infty
for
all (open) balls

B=B(x,r):=\{y\in X:d(x,y)<r\}

with
x\in X
and
r>0
. We make the tacit assumption that each ball
B\subset X
has a fixed center
x_{B}

and radius
\operatorname{rad}(B)
, and thus notation such as
\lambda B=B(x_{B},\lambda\operatorname{rad}(B))

is well-defined for all
\lambda>0
. When
E,F\subset X
, we let
\diam(E)
denote the diameter of
E
and
\operatorname{dist}(E,F)
is the distance between the sets
E,F\subset X
. We use
d(x,E)=\operatorname{dist}(x,E)=\operatorname{dist}(\{x\},E)
to denote the distance from a point
x\in X
to the set
E
.
If
E\subset X
, then
{\mbox{\raise 1.0pt\hbox{\Large$\chi$}}}_{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle E\,$}}

denotes the characteristic function of
E
; that is,
{\mbox{\raise 1.0pt\hbox{\Large$\chi$}}}_{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle E\,$}}(x)=1
if
x\in E

and
{\mbox{\raise 1.0pt\hbox{\Large$\chi$}}}_{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle E\,$}}(x)=0
if
x\in X\setminus E
.

We also assume throughout
that
\mu
is
doubling
, that is, there is a constant
c_{D}\geq 1
such that
whenever
x\in X
and
r>0
, we have

(2.3)

\mu(B(x,2r))\leq c_{D}\,\mu(B(x,r)).

Iteration of (
2.3
) shows that if
\mu
is doubling, then there exist an exponent
Q>0
and a constant
c_{Q}>0
,
both only depending on
c_{D}
, such that the quantitative doubling condition

(2.4)

\frac{\mu(B(y,r))}{\mu(B(x,R))}\geq c_{Q}\Bigl(\frac{r}{R}\Bigr)^{Q}

holds whenever
y\in B(x,R)\subset X
and
0<r<R
. Condition (
2.4
) always holds
for
Q\geq\log_{2}c_{D}
, but it can hold for smaller values of
Q
as well. See
[
1
, Lemma 3.3]

for details.

In some of our results we also need
to assume that
\mu
is

reverse doubling
, in the sense that there are constants
0<\kappa<1
and
0<c_{R}<1
such that

(2.5)

\mu(B(x,\kappa r))\leq c_{R}\,\mu(B(x,r))

for every
x\in X
and
0<r<\diam(X)/2
.
If
X
is connected and
0<\kappa<1
, then
inequality (
2.5
) follows from the doubling property (
2.3
)
with
0<c_{R}=c_{R}(c_{D},\kappa)<1
. See for instance
[
1
, Lemma 3.7]
.
Iteration of (
2.5
) shows that
if
\mu
is reverse doubling, then there exist
an exponent
\sigma>0
and a constant
c_{\sigma}>0
,
both only depending on
\kappa
and
c_{R}
, such that the quantitative
reverse doubling condition

(2.6)

\frac{\mu(B(x,r))}{\mu(B(x,R))}\leq c_{\sigma}\Bigl(\frac{r}{R}\Bigr)^{\sigma}

holds for every
x\in X
and
0<r<R<2\diam(X)
.

If the measure
\mu
is Ahlfors
Q
-regular for some
Q>0
, that is, there is a constant
C>0

such that

\frac{1}{C}r^{Q}\leq\mu(B(x,r))\leq Cr^{Q}

for every
x\in X
and
0<r<\diam(X)
,
then
\mu
is both doubling and reverse doubling, and
the quantitative estimates (
2.4
) and (
2.6
) hold with
the exponent
Q
.

We abbreviate
d\mu(x)=dx
and say that a function
u\colon X\to{\mathbb{R}}
is integrable
on balls, if
u
is
\mu
-measurable and

\lVert u\rVert_{L^{1}(B)}=\int_{B}\lvert u(x)\rvert\,dx<\infty

for all
balls
B\subset X
. In particular, for such functions the
integral average

u_{B}=\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}u(x)\,dx=\frac{1}{\mu(B)}\int_{B}u(x)\,dx

is well-defined whenever
B
is a ball in
X
.
Observe that we do not always assume that the space
X
is complete,
and hence continuous functions are not necessarily integrable on balls.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
1\leq p,q,t<\infty
and
0<s<1
.
We say that

supports a
(s,q,p,t)
-Poincaré inequality, if
there are constants

and
\lambda\geq 1
such that inequality

(2.7)

\biggl(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}\lvert u(x)-u_{B}\rvert^{q}\,dx\biggr)^{1/q}\leq c_{P}r^{s}\biggl(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773pt\lambda B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{\lambda B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{\lambda B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{\lambda B}}\biggl(\int_{\lambda B}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{t}}{d(x,y)^{st}\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}\,dy\biggr)^{p/t}\,dx\biggr)^{1/p}

holds for every ball
B=B(x_{0},r)\subset X

and for all functions
u\colon X\to{\mathbb{R}}
that are
integrable on balls.

In particular the left-hand side of (
2.7
)
is finite, if the right-hand side is finite.

If
u\colon X\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a measurable function,
0<s<1
,
1\leq t<\infty
, and
A\subset X
is a measurable set, we write

g_{u,s,t,A}(x)=\biggl(\int_{A}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{t}}{d(x,y)^{st}\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}\,dy\biggr)^{1/t},\qquad\text{ for every }x\in X.

Using this notation,
the
(s,q,p,t)
-Poincaré inequality (
2.7
)
can be written as

\biggl(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}\lvert u(x)-u_{B}\rvert^{q}\,dx\biggr)^{1/q}\leq c_{P}r^{s}\biggl(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773pt\lambda B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{\lambda B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{\lambda B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{\lambda B}}g_{u,s,t,\lambda B}(x)^{p}\,dx\biggr)^{1/p}.

We will repeatedly use the
facts that
g_{\lvert u\rvert,s,t,A}\leq g_{u,s,t,A}
and
g_{u,s,t,A}\leq g_{u,s,t,A^{\prime}}
when

.

The following lemma shows that

supports a

-Poincaré inequality

.
We emphasize that the doubling condition

\displaystyle\leq Cr^{sq}\biggl(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}\biggl(\int_{B}\frac{\lvert u(x)-u(y)\rvert^{t}}{d(x,y)^{st}\mu(4B)}\,dy\,\biggr)^{p/t}dx\biggr)^{q/p}

\displaystyle\leq Cr^{sq}\biggl(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}\biggl(\int_{B}\frac{\lvert u(x)-u(y)\rvert^{t}}{d(x,y)^{st}\,\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}\,dy\,\biggr)^{p/t}dx\biggr)^{q/p}.

This yields the desired inequality
(
2.7
) with
\lambda=1
and

c_{P}=c_{P}(s,q,t,c_{D})
.
∎

3.
Sobolev–Poincaré inequalities

As with the usual Poincaré inequalities (see
[
1
,
15
]
),
also in the fractional case it is
possible to improve inequalities from the case
q\leq p
(in Lemma
2.2
) to the case
q>p
,
up to the “Sobolev exponent”
p^{*}=Qp/(Q-sp)
; see
Theorem
3.4
below.
For this purpose, we apply
a metric measure space version of the fractional truncation method in

[
9
, Proposition 5]
,
[
6
, Theorem 4.1]
; see also

[
3
, Proposition 2.14]
.
In the proof we need the following
auxiliary result, which is a special case of
[
12
, Lemma 5]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume that
g\geq 0
is a measurable function on a ball
B\subset X
with

\mu(\{x\in B:g(x)=0\})\geq\mu(B)/2.

Then inequality

\mu(\{x\in B:g(x)>t\})\leq 2\inf_{a\in{\mathbb{R}}}\mu(\{x\in B:\lvert g(x)-a\rvert>t/2\})

holds for every
t>0
.

Theorem
3.2
below is metric measure space version of
the Euclidean result in
[
6
, Theorem 4.1]
.
We will later apply this theorem
with the kernel

K(y,z)=\frac{1}{d(y,z)^{sp}\mu(B(y,d(y,z)))},\qquad y,z\in X,

but we formulate the result in terms of general kernels.
The proof is a straightforward adaptation of the proof in
[
6
]
,
and it is based on a fractional Maz
′
ya truncation method.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
0<s<1
,
0<p\leq q<\infty
,
and
\lambda\geq 1
.
Let
K\colon X\times X\to[0,\infty]
be a measurable function
and let
B=B(x_{0},r)\subset X
be a ball.
Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(A)

There is a constant
C_{1}>0
such that inequality

\displaystyle\inf_{a\in{\mathbb{R}}}\sup_{t>0}\mu(

\displaystyle\{x\in B:|u(x)-a|>t\})t^{q}\leq C_{1}\biggl(\int_{\lambda B}\int_{\lambda B}|u(y)-u(z)|^{p}K(y,z)\,dz\,dy\biggr)^{\frac{q}{p}}

holds for every
u\in L^{\infty}(\lambda B)
.

(B)

There is a constant
C_{2}>0
such that inequality

\displaystyle\inf_{a\in{\mathbb{R}}}\int_{B}|u(x)-a|^{q}\,dx\leq C_{2}\biggl(\int_{\lambda B}\int_{\lambda B}|u(y)-u(z)|^{p}K(y,z)\,dz\,dy\biggr)^{\frac{q}{p}}

holds for every
u\in L^{1}(\lambda B)
, and
the left-hand side is finite
if the right-hand side is finite.

Moreover, in
the implication from
(A)
to
(B)
the constant
C_{2}
is of the form
C(p,q)C_{1}
,
and in the implication from
(B)
to
(A)
we have

C_{1}=C_{2}
.

Proof.

The implication from (B) to (A) with
C_{1}=C_{2}
follows from Chebyshev’s inequality.
Let us then assume that condition (A) holds.
Fix
u\in L^{1}(\lambda B)
and let
b\in{\mathbb{R}}
be such that

(3.8)

\mu(\{x\in B:u(x)\geq b\})\geq\frac{\mu(B)}{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\mu(\{x\in B:u(x)\leq b\})\geq\frac{\mu(B)}{2}.

We write
v_{+}=\max\{u-b,0\}
and
v_{-}=-\min\{u-b,0\}
. In the sequel
v
denotes either
v_{+}
or
v_{-}
;
all the statements are valid in both cases. Moreover, without loss of generality,
we may assume that
v\geq 0
is defined and finite everywhere in
\lambda B
.

For
0<t_{1}<t_{2}<\infty
and every
x\in\lambda B
, we define

v_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}(x)=\begin{cases}t_{2}-t_{1},\qquad&\text{if }t_{2}\leq v(x),\\
v(x)-t_{1},&\text{if }t_{1}<v(x)<t_{2},\\
0,&\text{if }v(x)\leq t_{1}.\end{cases}

Observe from (
3.8
) that

\mu(\{x\in B:v_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}(x)=0\})\geq\mu(B)/2.

By Lemma
3.1
and condition (A), both applied to the non-negative function
v_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\in L^{\infty}(\lambda B)
,

(3.9)

\begin{split}\sup_{t>0}\mu(\{x\in B:v_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}(x)>t\})\,t^{q}&\leq 2^{1+q}\inf_{a\in{\mathbb{R}}}\sup_{t>0}\mu(\{x\in B:\lvert v_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}(x)-a\rvert>t\})\,t^{q}\\
&\leq 2^{1+q}C_{1}\biggl(\int_{\lambda B}\int_{\lambda B}\lvert v_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}(y)-v_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}(z)\rvert^{p}K(y,z)\,dz\,dy\biggr)^{\frac{q}{p}}.\end{split}

We write

E_{k}=\{x\in\lambda B:v(x)>2^{k}\}
and
A_{k}=E_{k-1}\setminus E_{k}
, where
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Since
v\geq 0
is finite everywhere in
B
, we can write

(3.10)

B=\{x\in B:0\leq v(x)<\infty\}=\Biggl(\bigcup_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}B\cap A_{i}\Biggr)\cup\Bigl(B\cap\underbrace{\{x\in\lambda B:v(x)=0\}}_{=:A_{-\infty}})\Bigr).

Hence, by inequality (
3.9
) and the fact that
\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|a_{k}|^{q/p}\leq\bigl(\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|a_{k}|\bigr)^{q/p}
for all real-valued sequences
(a_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{B}\lvert v(x)\rvert^{q}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}2^{(k+1)q}\mu(B\cap A_{k+1})\leq\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}2^{(k+1)q}\mu(\{x\in B:v_{2^{k-1}}^{2^{k}}(x)>2^{k-2}\})

\displaystyle\leq 2^{1+4q}C_{1}\Biggl(\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\int_{\lambda B}\int_{\lambda B}|v_{2^{k-1}}^{2^{k}}(y)-v_{2^{k-1}}^{2^{k}}(z)|^{p}K(y,z)\,dz\,dy\Biggr)^{\frac{q}{p}}.

Using the definition of
v_{2^{k-1}}^{2^{k}}
, we can now estimate

(3.11)

\begin{split}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}&\int_{\lambda B}\int_{\lambda B}|v_{2^{k-1}}^{2^{k}}(y)-v_{2^{k-1}}^{2^{k}}(z)|^{p}K(y,z)\,dz\,dy\\
&\leq\Biggl\{\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\sum_{-\infty\leq i\leq k}\sum_{j\geq k}\int_{A_{i}}\int_{A_{j}}+\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\sum_{i\geq k}\sum_{-\infty\leq j\leq k}\int_{A_{i}}\int_{A_{j}}\Biggr\}|v_{2^{k-1}}^{2^{k}}(y)-v_{2^{k-1}}^{2^{k}}(z)|^{p}K(y,z)\,dz\,dy.\end{split}

Let
y\in A_{i}
and
z\in A_{j}
, where
j-1>i\geq-\infty
, and let
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Then

\lvert v(y)-v(z)\rvert\geq\lvert v(z)\rvert-\lvert v(y)\rvert\geq 2^{j-2}

and
\lvert v_{2^{k-1}}^{2^{k}}(y)-v_{2^{k-1}}^{2^{k}}(z)\rvert\leq 2^{k}
,
and so

(3.12)

\lvert v_{2^{k-1}}^{2^{k}}(y)-v_{2^{k-1}}^{2^{k}}(z)\rvert\leq 4\cdot 2^{k-j}\lvert v(y)-v(z)\rvert.

On the other hand,
the estimate

\lvert v_{2^{k-1}}^{2^{k}}(y)-v_{2^{k-1}}^{2^{k}}(z)\rvert\leq\lvert v(y)-v(z)\rvert

holds for every
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
and thus we conclude that
inequality (
3.12
) holds whenever
-\infty\leq i\leq k\leq j

and
(y,z)\in A_{i}\times A_{j}
.

By inequality (
3.12
), we have

(3.13)

\begin{split}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\sum_{-\infty\leq i\leq k}&\sum_{j\geq k}\int_{A_{i}}\int_{A_{j}}|v_{2^{k-1}}^{2^{k}}(y)-v_{2^{k-1}}^{2^{k}}(z)|^{p}K(y,z)\,dz\,dy\\
&\leq 4^{p}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\sum_{-\infty\leq i\leq k}\sum_{j\geq k}2^{p(k-j)}\int_{A_{i}}\int_{A_{j}}\lvert v(y)-v(z)\rvert^{p}K(y,z)\,dz\,dy.\end{split}

Since
\sum_{k=i}^{j}2^{p(k-j)}\leq(1-2^{-p})^{-1}
, changing the order of the summation shows
that the right-hand side of inequality (
3.13
) is bounded by

\frac{4^{p}}{1-2^{-p}}\int_{\lambda B}\int_{\lambda B}\lvert v(y)-v(z)\rvert^{p}K(y,z)\,dz\,dy.

The second sum on the right-hand side of (
3.11
)
can be estimated in the same way.
To conclude that (B) holds with
C_{2}=C(p,q)C_{1}
it remains to recall that
\lvert u-b\rvert=v_{+}+v_{-}
and
q>0
. Observe also that

\lvert v_{\pm}(y)-v_{\pm}(z)\rvert\leq\lvert u(y)-u(z)\rvert
for all
y,z\in\lambda B
.
∎

We also need certain maximal functions.
If
B\subset X
is an (open) ball and
u\in L^{1}(B)
, then the noncentred maximal function restricted to

B
is

M_{B}^{*}u(x)=\sup_{B^{\prime}}\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB^{\prime}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B^{\prime}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B^{\prime}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B^{\prime}}}\lvert u(y)\rvert\,dy,

where the supremum is taken over all balls
B^{\prime}\subset B
containing
x\in B
.
We will apply the following lemma from
[
1
, Lemma 3.12]
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
B\subset X
be a ball and let
u\in L^{1}(B)
. Then
M_{B}^{*}u
is lower
semicontinuous in
B
and satisfies

\mu(E_{\tau})\leq\frac{c_{D}^{3}}{\tau}\int_{E_{\tau}}\lvert u(x)\rvert\,dx\quad\text{ and }\quad\lim_{\tau\to\infty}\tau\mu(E_{\tau})=0,

where
E_{\tau}=\{x\in B:M^{*}_{B}u(x)>\tau\}
and
\tau>0
.

The next theorem gives a sufficient condition for
the fractional
(s,q,p,p)
-Poincaré inequality
with
q=p^{*}=Qp/(Q-sp)
.
The proof is essentially the same as the argument in
[
1
, pp. 95–97]
,
but we present the details
for the sake of completeness.
In particular, the fractional Maz
′
ya truncation method is
needed with sufficiently careful tracking of the constants.
Recall that we assume throughout that
\mu
is doubling, with constant
c_{D}\geq 1
in (
2.3
).
Hence, for any fixed
1\leq p<\infty
and
0<s<1
there exists
an exponent
Q>sp
such that (
2.4
)
holds, and then
p^{*}=Qp/(Q-sp)>p
.
The exponent
Q
in (
2.4
) is not uniquely determined, and
a smaller value of
Q>sp
gives in Theorem
3.4
a larger exponent
p^{*}
, which
in turn yields a stronger version of the Sobolev–Poincaré inequality.

Theorem 3.4
.

Assume that
\mu
is reverse doubling, with
constants
\sigma>0
and
c_{\sigma}>0
in (
2.6
),
and
let
Q>0
and
c_{Q}>0
be the constants in (
2.4
).
Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
0<s<1
be such that
sp<Q
,
and let
p^{*}=Qp/(Q-sp)
.
Then
X
supports a
(s,p^{*},p,p)
-Poincaré inequality,
with constants
\lambda=2

and
c_{P}=c_{P}(Q,p,s,\sigma,c_{D},c_{Q},c_{\sigma})
.

Proof.

Let
B=B(x_{0},r)
be a ball in
X
and let
u\in L^{\infty}(2B)
.
It suffices to prove that there exists a constant
C=C(Q,p,s,\sigma,c_{D},c_{Q},c_{\sigma})
such that

(3.14)

\begin{split}\mu(\{x\in B:\lvert u(x)-u_{2B}\rvert>t\})\,t^{p^{*}}\leq Cr^{sp^{*}}\mu(B)^{1-p^{*}/p}\biggl(\int_{2B}g_{u,s,p,2B}(y)^{p}\,dy\biggr)^{\frac{p^{*}}{p}}\end{split}

whenever
t>0
. Then the

(s,p^{*},p,p)
-Poincaré inequality follows from
Theorem
3.2
, applied
with the kernel

K(y,z)=\frac{1}{d(y,z)^{sp}\mu(B(y,d(y,z)))},\qquad y,z\in X,

together with the doubling property of
\mu

and the inequality

\int_{B}\lvert u(x)-u_{B}\rvert^{p}\,dx\leq 2^{p}\inf_{a\in{\mathbb{R}}}\int_{B}|u(x)-a|^{p}\,dx,

which in turn follows from Hölder’s inequality.

We prove (
3.14
) for a fixed
t>0
. We may assume that

r<2\diam(X)
and

(3.15)

0<\int_{2B}g_{u,s,p,2B}(y)^{p}\,dy<\infty.

Indeed, if the integral in (
3.15
) vanishes, then
u
is a constant almost everywhere
in the ball
B
by the
(s,p,p,p)
-Poincaré inequality
given in Lemma
2.2
.
Write
B_{0}=2B
,
r_{0}=2r
and
M=M_{B_{0}}^{*}((g_{u,s,p,2B})^{p})
.
By
[
13
, Lemma 1.8]
,
\mu
-almost every point
x\in B
is a Lebesgue point of
u
.
Lemma
3.3
implies that the function
M
is finite

\mu
-almost everywhere in
B
.

Let
x\in B
be a Lebesgue point of
u
, with
M(x)<\infty
, and
write
r_{j}=2^{-j}r
and
B_{j}=B(x,r_{j})
, for
j=1,2,\ldots
.
By the doubling property of
\mu
and the
(s,1,p,p)
-Poincaré inequality in Lemma
2.2
,

\displaystyle\lvert u(x)-u_{B_{0}}\rvert

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\to\infty}\lvert u_{B_{k}}-u_{B_{0}}\rvert=\lim_{k\to\infty}\left\lvert\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}(u_{B_{j+1}}-u_{B_{j}})\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{j+1}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{j+1}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{j+1}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{j+1}}}\lvert u(y)-u_{B_{j}}\rvert\,dy\leq c_{D}^{3}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{j}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{j}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{j}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{j}}}\lvert u(y)-u_{B_{j}}\rvert\,dy

\displaystyle\leq C(p,s,c_{D})\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}r_{j}^{s}\biggl(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{j}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{j}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{j}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{j}}}g_{u,s,p,B_{j}}(y)^{p}\,dy\biggr)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq C(p,s,c_{D})\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}r_{j}^{s}\biggl(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{j}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{j}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{j}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{j}}}g_{u,s,p,2B}(y)^{p}\,dy\biggr)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Condition (
2.4
), applied to the balls
B_{j}\subset B_{0}
on the right-hand side, gives

(3.16)

\lvert u(x)-u_{B_{0}}\rvert\leq C(Q,p,s,c_{D},c_{Q})\frac{r^{s}}{\mu(B_{0})^{s/Q}}\underbrace{\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\mu(B_{j})^{s/Q-1/p}\biggl(\int_{B_{j}}g_{u,s,p,2B}(y)^{p}\,dy\biggr)^{\frac{1}{p}}}_{\Sigma^{\prime}+\Sigma^{\prime\prime}}.

We write the sum in (
3.16
) as
\Sigma^{\prime}+\Sigma^{\prime\prime}
, where the summations are
over
0\leq j<j_{0}
and
j\geq j_{0}
, respectively, and the cut-off number
j_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}

is chosen as follows (depending on
x
).
Since
B_{0}\subset 8B_{1}
and

0<\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{0}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{0}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{0}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{0}}}g_{u,s,p,2B}(y)^{p}\,dy\leq M(x)<\infty,

there exists
j_{0}\geq 1
such that

(3.17)

c_{D}^{2}\,\mu(B_{j_{0}})\leq\frac{1}{M(x)}\int_{B_{0}}g_{u,s,p,2B}(y)^{p}\,dy\leq c_{D}^{3}\,\mu(B_{j_{0}}).

More precisely, by (
2.6
)
\mu(B_{j})\to 0
as
j\to\infty
, and hence
we can choose the largest integer
j_{0}
for which the right inequality holds. The
left inequality then follows from the doubling property of
\mu
.

In the first sum
\Sigma^{\prime}
we
have
\mu(B_{j})\geq c_{\sigma}^{-1}2^{\sigma(j_{0}-j)}\mu(B_{j_{0}})

for every
0\leq j<j_{0}
,
by (
2.6
).
Since
s/Q-1/p<0
, we obtain

\displaystyle\Sigma^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{j_{0}-1}\mu(B_{j})^{s/Q-1/p}\biggl(\int_{B_{j}}g_{u,s,p,2B}(y)^{p}\,dy\biggr)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq C(Q,p,s,c_{\sigma})\,\mu(B_{j_{0}})^{s/Q-1/p}\biggl(\int_{B_{0}}g_{u,s,p,2B}(y)^{p}\,dy\biggr)^{\frac{1}{p}}\sum_{j=0}^{j_{0}-1}2^{\sigma(j_{0}-j)(s/Q-1/p)},

\displaystyle\leq C(Q,p,s,\sigma,c_{D},c_{\sigma})\,\biggl(\int_{B_{0}}g_{u,s,p,2B}(y)^{p}\,dy\biggr)^{\frac{s}{Q}}M(x)^{1/p-s/Q},

where the sum on the second line is bounded from above by a constant

0<C(Q,p,s,\sigma)<\infty
that can be chosen to be independent of
j_{0}
,
and the last step follows from the right-hand inequality in (
3.17
).

Correspondingly,
in the second sum
\Sigma^{\prime\prime}

we have
\mu(B_{j})\leq c_{\sigma}2^{\sigma(j_{0}-j)}\mu(B_{j_{0}})
for every
j\geq j_{0}
, by (
2.6
).
Using also the maximal function

M=M_{B_{0}}^{*}((g_{u,s,p,2B})^{p})
,
we obtain

\displaystyle\Sigma^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=j_{0}}^{\infty}\mu(B_{j})^{s/Q}\biggl(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{j}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{j}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{j}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{j}}}g_{u,s,p,2B}(y)^{p}\,dy\biggr)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq C(Q,s,c_{\sigma})\mu(B_{j_{0}})^{s/Q}M(x)^{1/p}\sum_{j=j_{0}}^{\infty}2^{\sigma(j_{0}-j)s/Q},

\displaystyle\leq C(Q,p,s,\sigma,c_{D},c_{\sigma})\,\biggl(\int_{B_{0}}g_{u,s,p,2B}(y)^{p}\,dy\biggr)^{\frac{s}{Q}}M(x)^{1/p-s/Q},

where the last sum is bounded from above by a constant
0<C(Q,s,\sigma)<\infty

and the final step follows from the left-hand inequality in (
3.17
).

Substituting the above estimates for
\Sigma^{\prime}
and
\Sigma^{\prime\prime}
to (
3.16
) gives

\displaystyle\lvert u(x)-u_{B_{0}}\rvert

\displaystyle\leq C(Q,p,s,c_{D},c_{Q})\frac{r^{s}}{\mu(B_{0})^{s/Q}}(\Sigma^{\prime}+\Sigma^{\prime\prime})

\displaystyle\leq C(Q,p,s,\sigma,c_{D},c_{Q},c_{\sigma})\,r^{s}\biggl(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{0}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{0}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{0}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{0}}}g_{u,s,p,2B}(y)^{p}\,dy\biggr)^{\frac{s}{Q}}M(x)^{\frac{1}{p^{*}}},

for
p^{*}=Qp/(Q-sp)
.
In particular, if
\lvert u(x)-u_{B_{0}}\rvert>t>0
, then

\displaystyle M(x)>C(Q,p,s,\sigma,c_{D},c_{Q},c_{\sigma})\,\frac{t^{p^{*}}}{r^{sp^{*}}}\biggl(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{0}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{0}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{0}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{0}}}g_{u,s,p,2B}(y)^{p}\,dy\biggr)^{-\frac{sp^{*}}{Q}}=\tau(t)>0.

From this estimate, which is valid for
\mu
-almost every
x\in B
, and Lemma
3.3
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mu(\{x\in B:\lvert u(x)-u_{B_{0}}\rvert>t\})\,t^{p^{*}}

\displaystyle\leq\mu(\{x\in B_{0}:M(x)>\tau(t)\})\,t^{p^{*}}\leq\frac{c_{D}^{3}t^{p^{*}}}{\tau(t)}\int_{B_{0}}g_{u,s,p,2B}(x)^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C(Q,p,s,\sigma,c_{D},c_{Q},c_{\sigma})\,r^{sp^{*}}\mu(B)^{1-p^{*}/p}\biggl(\int_{B_{0}}g_{u,s,p,2B}(x)^{p}\,dx\biggr)^{\frac{p^{*}}{p}}

for every
t>0
. Inequality (
3.14
) follows, and the proof is complete.
∎

4.
Capacitary and boundary Poincaré inequalities

Next we study versions of fractional Poincaré inequalities, in which the zero sets of
functions are taken into account. As a tool we will apply a variant of the
fractional relative capacity,
compare to
[
25
, Definition 7.1]
and see also
[
9
]
and
[
26
, §11]
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
0<s<1
,
1\leq t,p<\infty
, and
\Lambda\geq 2
.
Let
B\subset X
be a ball and let
E\subset{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}
be a closed set. Then we write

\Cp_{s,p,t}(E,2B,\Lambda B)=\inf_{\varphi}\int_{\Lambda B}\biggl(\int_{\Lambda B}\frac{|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|^{t}}{d(x,y)^{st}\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}\,dy\biggr)^{p/t}\,dx

where the infimum is taken over all continuous functions

\varphi\colon X\to{\mathbb{R}}
that are integrable on balls, such that
\varphi(x)\geq 1
for every
x\in E
and

\varphi(x)=0
for every
x\in X\setminus 2B
.

The following simple lemma is needed in the proof of Theorem
4.3
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\alpha>0
.
There is a constant
C(\alpha,c_{D})>0
such that

r^{-\alpha}\int_{B(x,r)}\frac{d(x,y)^{\alpha}}{\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}\,dy\leq C(\alpha,c_{D})

for every
x\in X
and
r>0
.

Proof.

Let
x\in X
and
r>0
.
For each
j\in\{0,1,\ldots\}
we write

A_{j}(x,r)=\{y\in X:2^{-j-1}r\leq d(x,y)<2^{-j}r\}.

By the doubling condition (
2.3
) of the measure
\mu
and the standing assumption that
\mu(\{x\})=0
,
we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{B(x,r)}\frac{d(x,y)^{\alpha}}{\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}\,dy

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\int_{A_{j}(x,r)}\frac{d(x,y)^{\alpha}}{\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(2^{-j}r)^{\alpha}\frac{\mu(A_{j}(x,r))}{\mu(B(x,2^{-j-1}r))}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(2^{-j}r)^{\alpha}\frac{\mu(B(x,2^{-j}r))}{\mu(B(x,2^{-j-1}r))}\leq C(\alpha,c_{D})\,r^{\alpha}.\qed

The next result is a fractional version of Maz
′
ya’s capacitary Poincaré inequality,
compare to
[
1
, Theorem 6.21]
.
The argument is similar to that in
[
1
]
, but
there are several technical differences due to
the present non-local setting.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
q\geq p\geq 1
,
0<s<1
,
1\leq t<\infty
and
\Lambda\geq 2
.
Assume that
X
supports a
(s,q,p,t)
-Poincaré inequality
with constants
c_{P}>0
and
\lambda\geq 1
.
Let
u\colon X\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a continuous function and let

Z=\{x\in X:u(x)=0\}.

Then, for all balls
B=B(x_{0},r)\subset X
,

(4.18)

\begin{split}\biggl(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773pt\Lambda B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{\Lambda B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{\Lambda B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{\Lambda B}}|u(x)|^{q}\,dx\biggr)^{p/q}\leq\frac{C(s,t,p,c_{D},c_{P},\Lambda)}{\Cp_{s,p,t}({\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}\cap Z,2B,\Lambda B)}\int_{\lambda\Lambda B}g_{u,s,t,\lambda\Lambda B}(x)^{p}\,dx.\end{split}

Proof.

By replacing
u
with
u_{k}=\min\{\lvert u\rvert,k\}
, for
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, applying Fatou’s lemma,
and using inequalities
g_{u_{k},s,t,\lambda\Lambda B}\leq g_{u,s,t,\lambda\Lambda B}
, we may assume that
u\geq 0

and that
u
is bounded.
Fix a ball
B=B(x_{0},r)
in
X
.
Without loss of generality we may assume that
the right-hand side of inequality (
4.18
) is finite. Let

\overline{u}=\biggl(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773pt\Lambda B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{\Lambda B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{\Lambda B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{\Lambda B}}|u(x)|^{q}\,dx\biggr)^{1/q}<\infty.

We may assume that
\overline{u}>0
, as otherwise there is nothing to prove.

Let
\eta(x)=\max\{0,1-\operatorname{dist}(x,B)/r\}
for every
x\in X
. Then

|\eta(x)-\eta(y)|\leq d(x,y)/r,\qquad\text{\lx@nobreakspace for every }x,y\in X,

0\leq\eta\leq 1
in
X
,
\eta=1
in
{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}
and
\eta=0
outside
2B
. The function

\varphi=(1-u/\overline{u})\eta
is bounded
and continuous,
\varphi=1
in
{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}\cap Z
,
and
\varphi=0
outside
2B
.
By Definition
4.1
of the capacity, we have

\begin{split}\Cp_{s,p,t}({\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}\cap Z,2B,\Lambda B)&\leq\int_{\Lambda B}\biggl(\int_{\Lambda B}\frac{|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|^{t}}{d(x,y)^{st}\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}\,dy\biggr)^{p/t}\,dx\\
&=\frac{1}{\overline{u}^{p}}\int_{\Lambda B}\biggl(\int_{\Lambda B}\frac{|\eta(x)(\overline{u}-u(x))-\eta(y)(\overline{u}-u(y))|^{t}}{d(x,y)^{st}\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}\,dy\biggr)^{p/t}\!\!\!dx\\
&=\frac{1}{\overline{u}^{p}}I.\end{split}

To estimate
I
, we write

\begin{split}I&=\int_{\Lambda B}\biggl(\int_{\Lambda B}\frac{|\eta(x)(\overline{u}-u(x))-\eta(y)(\overline{u}-u(x))+\eta(y)(\overline{u}-u(x))-\eta(y)(\overline{u}-u(y))|^{t}}{d(x,y)^{st}\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}\,dy\biggr)^{p/t}dx\\
&\leq C(t,p)\int_{\Lambda B}|\overline{u}-u(x)|^{p}\biggl(\int_{\Lambda B}\frac{|\eta(x)-\eta(y)|^{t}}{d(x,y)^{st}\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}\,dy\biggr)^{p/t}dx\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+C(t,p)\int_{\Lambda B}\biggl(\int_{\Lambda B}\eta(y)^{t}\frac{|u(y)-u(x)|^{t}}{d(x,y)^{st}\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}\,dy\biggr)^{p/t}dx.\end{split}

Fix
x\in\Lambda B
. Since
|\eta(x)-\eta(y)|\leq d(x,y)/r

for each
y\in\Lambda B
, by Lemma
4.2
we have

\int_{\Lambda B}\frac{|\eta(x)-\eta(y)|^{t}}{d(x,y)^{st}\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}\,dy\leq r^{-t}\int_{B(x,2\Lambda r)}\frac{d(x,y)^{t(1-s)}}{\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}\,dy\leq C(s,t,c_{D},\Lambda)r^{-st}.

Taking also into account that
0\leq\eta^{t}\leq 1
in
\Lambda B
, we obtain

I\leq C(s,t,p,c_{D},\Lambda)r^{-sp}\int_{\Lambda B}|\overline{u}-u(x)|^{p}\,dx+C(t,p)\int_{\Lambda B}g_{u,s,t,\Lambda B}(x)^{p}\,dx.

Hence, we are left with estimating the following integral, with
a=(q-p)/(pq)
,

\begin{split}\biggl(\int_{\Lambda B}|\overline{u}-u(x)|^{p}\,dx\biggr)^{1/p}&\leq\mu(\Lambda B)^{a}\biggl(\int_{\Lambda B}|\overline{u}-u(x)|^{q}\,dx\biggr)^{1/q}\\
&\leq\mu(\Lambda B)^{a}\biggl(\int_{\Lambda B}|u(x)-u_{\Lambda B}|^{q}\,dx\biggr)^{1/q}+|\overline{u}-u_{\Lambda B}|\mu(\Lambda B)^{a+1/q}.\end{split}

The first step above relies on the assumption
q\geq p
. The right-hand side can be estimated exactly as
in
[
1
, pp. 144–145]
.
Indeed, the second term may be estimated by the first one, since

\displaystyle\lvert\overline{u}-u_{\Lambda B}\rvert\mu(\Lambda B)^{a+1/q}

\displaystyle=\mu(\Lambda B)^{a}\big\lvert\lVert u\rVert_{L^{q}(\Lambda B)}-\lVert u_{\Lambda B}\rVert_{L^{q}(\Lambda B)}\big\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\mu(\Lambda B)^{a}\lVert u-u_{\Lambda B}\rVert_{L^{q}(\Lambda B)}=\mu(\Lambda B)^{a}\biggl(\int_{\Lambda B}\lvert u(x)-u_{\Lambda B}\rvert^{q}\,dx\biggr)^{1/q}.

The first term is in turn estimated by the assumed
(s,q,p,t)
-Poincaré inequality,

\displaystyle\mu(\Lambda B)^{a}\biggl(\int_{\Lambda B}\lvert u(x)-u_{\Lambda B}\rvert^{q}\,dx\biggr)^{1/q}

\displaystyle=\mu(\Lambda B)^{1/p}\biggl(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773pt\Lambda B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{\Lambda B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{\Lambda B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{\Lambda B}}\lvert u(x)-u_{\Lambda B}\rvert^{q}\,dx\biggr)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\leq c_{P}r^{s}\biggl(\int_{\lambda\Lambda B}g_{u,s,t,\lambda\Lambda B}(x)^{p}\,dx\biggr)^{1/p}.

This results in

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle\leq C(s,t,p,c_{D},c_{P},\Lambda)\int_{\lambda\Lambda B}g_{u,s,t,\lambda\Lambda B}(x)^{p}\,dx,

and it follows that

\displaystyle\biggl(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773pt\Lambda B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{\Lambda B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{\Lambda B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{\Lambda B}}|u(x)|^{q}dx\biggr)^{p/q}=\overline{u}^{p}\leq\frac{C(s,t,p,c_{D},c_{P},\Lambda)}{\Cp_{s,p,t}({\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}\cap Z,2B,\Lambda B)}\int_{\lambda\Lambda B}g_{u,s,t,\lambda\Lambda B}(x)^{p}\,dx,

as required.
∎

Next we consider two notions that are closely related to the relative capacity
but have a more geometric flavor.
The following concept of (co)dimension was introduced in
[
19
]
.

Definition 4.4
.

Let
E\subset X
. For
r>0
, the
open
r
-neighborhood of
E

is the set

E_{r}=\{x\in X:\operatorname{dist}(x,E)<r\}.

The upper Assouad codimension of
E
,
denoted by
\ucodima(E)
, is
the infimum of all
Q\geq 0
for
which there is a constant
c>0
such that

\frac{\mu(E_{r}\cap B(x,R))}{\mu(B(x,R))}\geq c\Bigl(\frac{r}{R}\Bigr)^{Q}

for every
x\in E
and all
0<r<R<\diam(E)
.
If
E
consists of one point, then
the restriction
R<\diam(E)
is removed.

If the measure
\mu
is
Q
-regular, then

\ucodima(E)=Q-\ldima(E)

for all
E\subset X
, where
\ldima(E)
is the
lower (Assouad) dimension of
E
;
see
[
19
, (3.11)]
.
In the Euclidean space
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, which is regular with
Q=n
, the connection between fractional Hardy inequalities and
the lower Assouad dimension (as well as its dual, the upper Assouad dimension)
has been considered in
[
7
,
8
]
;
see also
[
23
]
.

We also need suitable versions of Hausdorff contents,
which give lower bounds for capacities, as in Lemma
4.6
below.
In the case of non-fractional capacities, similar
ideas can be found for instance in
[
14
, Theorem 5.9]

and in several subsequent papers.

Definition 4.5
.

The (
\rho
-restricted) Hausdorff content of codimension
\eta\geq 0

is defined for sets
E\subset X
by setting

{\mathcal{H}}^{\mu,\eta}_{\rho}(E)=\inf\Biggl\{\sum_{k}\mu(B(x_{k},r_{k}))\,r_{k}^{-\eta}:E\subset\bigcup_{k}B(x_{k},r_{k})\text{ and }0<r_{k}\leq\rho\Biggr\}.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
0<s<1
,
1\leq p,t<\infty
,
0\leq\eta<p
and
\Lambda>2
.
Assume that
\mu
is
reverse doubling, with constants
\kappa=2/\Lambda

and
0<c_{R}<1
in (

B=B(x_{0},r)\subset X
be a ball with
r<\diam(X)/(2\Lambda)
,
and assume that
E\subset{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}
is a closed set.
Then

\mathcal{H}^{\mu,s\eta}_{5\Lambda r}(E)\leq C(s,t,p,\eta,c_{R},c_{D},\Lambda)r^{s(p-\eta)}\Cp_{s,p,t}(E,2B,\Lambda B).

Proof.

Fix
x\in E
and write
B_{0}=\Lambda B=B(x_{0},\Lambda r)
,

r_{0}=\Lambda r
,
r_{j}=2^{-j+1}r
and
B_{j}=B(x,r_{j})
,
j=1,2,\ldots
.
Observe that there are test functions for
\Cp_{s,p,t}(E,2B,\Lambda B)
; let
\varphi
be
any one of them.
By replacing
\varphi
with
\max\{0,\min\{\varphi,1\}\}
, if necessary, we may assume that
0\leq\varphi\leq 1
.
Thus
\varphi
is continuous on
X
,
\varphi=1
on
E
, and
\varphi=0
on
B_{0}\setminus 2B
. By inequality (

\displaystyle 0\leq\varphi_{B_{0}}=\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{0}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{0}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{0}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{0}}}\varphi(y)\,dy\leq\frac{\mu(2B)}{\mu(\Lambda B)}\leq c_{R}<1.

As a consequence, since

, we find that

\lvert\varphi(x)-\varphi_{B_{0}}\rvert\geq 1-c_{R}>0.

Let
\delta=s(p-\eta)/p>0
.
Proceeding as in the proof of Theorem
3.4

(s,1,p,t)
-Poincaré inequality given by Lemma
2.2
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}2^{-j\delta}

\displaystyle=C(s,p,\eta,c_{R})(1-c_{R})\leq C(s,p,\eta,c_{R})\lvert\varphi(x)-\varphi_{B_{0}}\rvert

\displaystyle\leq C(s,t,p,\eta,c_{R},c_{D},\Lambda)\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}r_{j}^{s}\biggl(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{j}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{j}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{j}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{j}}}g_{\varphi,s,t,B_{0}}(y)^{p}\,dy\biggr)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

2^{-j\delta p}\leq C(s,t,p,\eta,c_{R},c_{D},\Lambda)r_{j}^{sp}\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{j}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{j}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{j}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{j}}}g_{\varphi,s,t,B_{0}}(y)^{p}\,dy.

r_{x}=r_{j}

\mu(B_{x})r_{x}^{-s\eta}\leq C(s,t,p,\eta,c_{R},c_{D},\Lambda)r^{s(p-\eta)}\int_{B_{x}}g_{\varphi,s,t,B_{0}}(y)^{p}\,dy.

[

, we obtain points

are

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{\mu,s\eta}_{5\Lambda r}(E)

∎

The main result of this section
is the following version of the fractional (Sobolev–)Poincaré inequality,
where the mean value
u_{B}
can be omitted from the integral on the left-hand side.

\ucodima(E)<sp

the assumption that the space

is complete.
Alternatively, in the following results the condition

could be replaced by an explicit

or a suitable Hausdorff content, and then
the completeness assumption would not be needed.
However, in non-complete spaces one then has
to add to Theorems
5.1
and
5.2
also
the assumption that the continuous function

is integrable on balls.

5.
Pointwise and integral Hardy inequalities

In this section we apply the Sobolev–Poincaré and boundary Poincaré inequalities from the previous sections
to fractional Hardy-type inequalities involving distance weights.
We begin with a pointwise version of the fractional Hardy inequality, given in terms of
the fractional maximal function. For
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
and a measurable function
u
on
X
,
this is defined as

M_{\alpha}u(x)=\sup_{r>0}r^{\alpha}\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB(x,r)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x,r)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x,r)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x,r)}}\lvert u(y)\rvert\,dy,\qquad\text{ for every }\lx@nobreakspace x\in X.

In particular, if
\alpha=0
, then
M_{\alpha}=M_{0}=M
is the centered
Hardy–Littlewood maximal function.

\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}

.

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\lvert u_{B_{j}}-u_{B_{j+1}}\rvert\leq C\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{j}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{j}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{j}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{j}}}\lvert u(y)-u_{B_{j}}\rvert\,dy

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(2^{-j}r)^{s}\biggl(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773pt{B_{j}}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{{B_{j}}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{{B_{j}}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{{B_{j}}}}g_{u,s,t,B_{j}}(y)^{p}\,dy\biggr)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq Cr^{s-\alpha}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}2^{-j(s-\alpha)}\biggl((2^{-j}r)^{\alpha p}\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773pt{B_{j}}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{{B_{j}}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{{B_{j}}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{{B_{j}}}}{\mbox{\raise 1.0pt\hbox{\Large$\chi$}}}_{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle B\,$}}(y)g_{u,s,t,B}(y)^{p}\,dy\biggr)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq Cr^{s-\alpha}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}2^{-j(s-\alpha)}\bigl(M_{\alpha p}\bigl({\mbox{\raise 1.0pt\hbox{\Large$\chi$}}}_{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle B\,$}}(g_{u,s,t,B})^{p}\bigr)(x)\bigr)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=Cd(x,E)^{s-\alpha}\bigl(M_{\alpha p}\bigl({\mbox{\raise 1.0pt\hbox{\Large$\chi$}}}_{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle B\,$}}(g_{u,s,t,B})^{p}\bigr)(x)\bigr)^{1/p}.

The claim follows from (
5.20
) and the estimates above.
∎

Pointwise Hardy inequalities imply localized Hardy inequalities
for balls centered at
E
. Here we restrict ourselves to the case
q=p
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
0<s<1
and

1<t<\infty
.
Assume that
X
is complete
and that
\mu
is
reverse doubling.
Let
E\subset X
be a closed set with

\ucodima(E)<st
, and
let
u\colon X\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a continuous function such that

u=0
on
E
.
Then there is a constant
C>0
, independent of
u
, such that

\int_{B\setminus E}\frac{\lvert u(x)\rvert^{t}}{d(x,E)^{st}}\,dx\leq C\int_{3B}\int_{3B}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{t}}{d(x,y)^{st}\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}\,dy\,dx

whenever
B=B(w,r)
with
w\in E
and
0<r<\diam(E)
.

Proof.

Fix an exponent
1\leq p<t
in such a way that
\ucodima(E)<sp
.
By Lemma
2.2
we find that
X
supports
the
(s,p,p,t)
-Poincaré inequality with constants

\lambda=1
and
c_{P}=c_{P}(s,p,t,c_{D})
.
Fix
x\in B\setminus E
.
Then

0<d(x,E)<r<\diam(E)
and

B(x,2d(x,E))\subset 3B
.
Hence, by Theorem
5.1
with
\alpha=0
and
q=p
,

\displaystyle\frac{\lvert u(x)\rvert^{t}}{d(x,E)^{st}}

\displaystyle\leq C\bigl(M\bigl({\mbox{\raise 1.0pt\hbox{\Large$\chi$}}}_{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle B(x,2d(x,E))\,$}}(g_{u,s,t,B(x,2d(x,E))})^{p}\bigr)(x)\bigr)^{t/p}

\displaystyle\leq C\bigl(M\bigl({\mbox{\raise 1.0pt\hbox{\Large$\chi$}}}_{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 3B\,$}}(g_{u,s,t,3B})^{p}\bigr)(x)\bigr)^{t/p}.

Integrating this inequality over the set
B\setminus E
we obtain

\int_{B\setminus E}\frac{\lvert u(x)\rvert^{t}}{d(x,E)^{st}}\,dx\leq C\int_{X}\bigl(M\bigl({\mbox{\raise 1.0pt\hbox{\Large$\chi$}}}_{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 3B\,$}}(g_{u,s,t,3B})^{p}\bigr)(x)\bigr)^{t/p}\,dx.

Since
t>p
, the Hardy–Littlewood maximal theorem
[
1
, Theorem 3.13]

implies that

\int_{B\setminus E}\frac{\lvert u(x)\rvert^{t}}{d(x,E)^{st}}\,dx\leq C\int_{3B}g_{u,s,t,3B}(x)^{t}\,dx.

This concludes the proof.
∎

Next, we obtain a (partial) converse of Theorem
5.2
.
This shows that the dimensional condition
\ucodima(E)<st
in Theorems
5.1

and
5.2
is essentially sharp,
up to the end point. The idea behind the proof
goes back to
[
4
, Section 2]
, where the impossibility of a
fractional Hardy inequality was shown in certain open sets

\Omega
of the Euclidean space,
for instance if
\Omega
is a Lipschitz domain and
st\leq 1

(note that in this case
\ucodima(\partial\Omega)=1
).
On the other hand, a necessary condition for non-fractional
pointwise Hardy inequalities in metric spaces has been given
in
[
21
, Lemma 3]
in terms of a Hausdorff content
density condition.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
0<s<1
,
1<t<\infty
, and
\lambda\geq 1
. Assume
that
E\subset X
is a (nonempty) closed set such that

\int_{B\setminus E}\frac{\lvert u(x)\rvert^{t}}{d(x,E)^{st}}\,dx\leq C\int_{\lambda B}\int_{\lambda B}\frac{\lvert u(x)-u(y)\rvert^{t}}{d(x,y)^{st}\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}\,dy\,dx

whenever
u\colon X\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a bounded continuous function such that

u=0
on
E
,
and
B=B(w,r)
with
w\in E
and
0<r<\diam(E)
.
Then
\ucodima(E)\leq st
.

Proof.

Let
w\in E
and
0<r<R_{0}<\diam(E)
.
It suffices to show that

(5.21)

\frac{\mu(E_{r}\cap B(w,R_{0}))}{\mu(B(w,R_{0}))}\geq c\Bigl(\frac{r}{R_{0}}\Bigr)^{st},

where the constant
c
is independent of
w
,
r
and
R_{0}
.
For convenience, write
R=R_{0}/\lambda
and
B=B(w,R)
, so that

\lambda B=B(w,R_{0})
.

If
\mu(E_{r}\cap B(w,R))\geq\frac{1}{2}\mu(B(w,R))
, the claim is clear since then,
by doubling,

\mu(E_{r}\cap B(w,R_{0}))\geq\mu(E_{r}\cap B(w,R))\geq\tfrac{1}{2}\mu(B(w,R))\geq c\mu(B(w,R_{0})),

and on the other hand
\bigl(\frac{r}{R_{0}}\bigr)^{st}\leq 1
. Thus we may assume that

\mu(E_{r}\cap B(w,R))<\tfrac{1}{2}\mu(B(w,R))
, whence

(5.22)

\mu(B(w,R)\setminus E_{r})\geq\tfrac{1}{2}\mu(B(w,R))>0.

Notice that then in particular
r<R=R_{0}/\lambda

since otherwise
B(w,R)\setminus E_{r}=\emptyset
.

Let us now consider the continuous and bounded function
u\colon X\to{\mathbb{R}}
,

u(x)=\min\{1,4r^{-1}d(x,E)\},\qquad x\in X.

Then
u=0
on
E
,
u=1
in
X\setminus E_{r/4}
, and

\lvert u(x)-u(y)\rvert\leq\min\bigl\{1,4r^{-1}d(x,y)\bigr\}\quad\text{ for all }x,y\in X.

Since
d(x,E)^{-st}\geq R^{-st}
for
x\in B(w,R)\setminus E_{r}
, we obtain

(5.23)

\begin{split}\int_{B\setminus E}\frac{\lvert u(x)\rvert^{t}}{d(x,E)^{st}}\,dx&\geq\int_{B\setminus E_{r}}d(x,E)^{-st}\,dx\geq R^{-st}\mu(B(w,R)\setminus E_{r})\\
&\geq\tfrac{1}{2}R^{-st}\mu(B(w,R))\geq cR_{0}^{-st}\mu(B(w,R_{0})),\end{split}

where the penultimate step follows from (
5.22
)
and the final inequality holds by doubling.

To prove the claim (
5.21
), it hence suffices to show that

(5.24)

\int_{\lambda B}\int_{\lambda B}\frac{\lvert u(x)-u(y)\rvert^{t}}{d(x,y)^{st}\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}\,dy\,dx\leq Cr^{-st}\mu(E_{r}\cap B(w,R_{0})).

Then (
5.21
) follows directly from
estimates (
5.23
) and (
5.24
) and the assumed
local fractional Hardy inequality.

Write

K(x,y)=\frac{\lvert u(x)-u(y)\rvert^{t}}{d(x,y)^{st}\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}

whenever
x,y\in\lambda B
,
x\not=y
.
Since
u(x)=1
for
x\in\lambda B\setminus E_{r/4}
, and
K(x,y)\leq c_{D}K(y,x)
by doubling
for
x,y\in\lambda B
,
x\not=y
,
we have

\begin{split}\int_{\lambda B}\int_{\lambda B}K(x,y)\,dy\,dx&\leq\int_{E_{r}\cap\lambda B}\int_{E_{r}\cap\lambda B}K(x,y)\,dy\,dx+(1+c_{D})\int_{\lambda B\setminus E_{r}}\int_{E_{r/4}\cap\lambda B}K(x,y)\,dy\,dx\\
&=:I_{1}+(1+c_{D})I_{2}.\end{split}

Define

F_{k}=\{x\in\lambda B:2^{-k}\leq d(x,E)<2^{-k+1}\}

and

A_{j}(x)=\{y\in\lambda B:2^{-j-1}\leq d(y,x)<2^{-j}\},

for
k,j\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
x\in\lambda B
. Let also
k_{1},k_{2}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
be such that

2^{-k_{1}-1}\leq\lambda R<2^{-k_{1}}\quad\text{ and }\quad 2^{-k_{2}}\leq r<2^{-k_{2}+1}.

When
k\leq k_{2}
and
x\in F_{k}
, it holds that
E_{r/4}\cap A_{j}(x)=\emptyset
for all
j\geq k+1
.
Using the estimate
\lvert u(x)-u(y)\rvert\leq 1
and changing also the orders
of summation and integration, we thus obtain

\begin{split}I_{2}&\leq\sum_{k=k_{1}}^{k_{2}}\int_{F_{k}}\sum_{j=k_{1}-1}^{k}\int_{E_{r/4}\cap A_{j}(x)}\frac{1}{2^{-(j+1)st}\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}\,dy\,dx\\
&\leq C\sum_{j=k_{1}-1}^{k_{2}}2^{jst}\int_{E_{r}\cap\lambda B}\sum_{k=j}^{k_{2}}\int_{\{x\in F_{k}:y\in A_{j}(x)\}}\frac{1}{\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}\,dx\,dy.\end{split}

But if
y\in A_{j}(x)
, then
d(x,y)<2^{-j}
and so

\bigcup_{k=j}^{k_{2}}\{x\in F_{k}:y\in A_{j}(x)\}\subset B(y,2^{-j}).

Since
B(y,d(x,y))\subset B(x,2d(x,y))
, we obtain by doubling that (still for
y\in A_{j}(x)
)

\begin{split}\mu(B(y,2^{-j}))&\leq c_{D}\mu(B(y,2^{-j-1}))\leq c_{D}\mu(B(y,d(x,y)))\\
&\leq c_{D}\mu(B(x,2d(x,y)))\leq c_{D}^{2}\mu(B(x,d(x,y))).\end{split}

We conclude that

\begin{split}I_{2}&\leq C\sum_{j=k_{1}-1}^{k_{2}}2^{jst}\int_{E_{r}\cap\lambda B}\int_{B(y,2^{-j})}\frac{1}{\mu(B(y,2^{-j}))}\,dx\,dy\\
&\leq C\sum_{j=k_{1}-1}^{k_{2}}2^{jst}\mu(E_{r}\cap\lambda B)\leq C2^{k_{2}st}\mu(E_{r}\cap\lambda B)\leq Cr^{-st}\mu(E_{r}\cap\lambda B).\end{split}

On the other hand, since
\lvert u(x)-u(y)\rvert^{t}\leq\min\{1,4^{t}r^{-t}d(x,y)^{t}\}
for all
x,y\in\lambda B
,
integral
I_{1}
can be estimated as follows:

(5.25)

\begin{split}I_{1}&\leq\int_{E_{r}\cap\lambda B}\sum_{j=k_{1}-1}^{\infty}\int_{E_{r}\cap A_{j}(x)}\frac{\lvert u(x)-u(y)\rvert^{t}}{d(x,y)^{st}\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}\,dy\,dx\\
&\leq\int_{E_{r}\cap\lambda B}\sum_{j=k_{1}-1}^{k_{2}}\int_{E_{r}\cap A_{j}(x)}\frac{1}{2^{-(j+1)st}\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}\,dy\,dx\\
&\qquad+C\int_{E_{r}\cap\lambda B}\sum_{j=k_{2}}^{\infty}\int_{E_{r}\cap A_{j}(x)}\frac{r^{-t}d(x,y)^{t}}{d(x,y)^{st}\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}\,dy\,dx.\end{split}

In the first integral on the right-hand side of (
5.25
)

\int_{E_{r}\cap A_{j}(x)}\frac{1}{2^{-(j+1)st}\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}\,dy\leq\frac{1}{2^{-(j+1)st}}\int_{A_{j}(x)}\frac{1}{\mu(B(x,2^{-j-1}))}\,dy\leq C2^{jst}

since the measures of the balls
B(x,2^{-j})
and
B(x,2^{-j-1})
are comparable,
while in the second integral on the right-hand side of (
5.25
)

\begin{split}\sum_{j=k_{2}}^{\infty}\int_{E_{r}\cap A_{j}(x)}&\frac{r^{-t}d(x,y)^{t}}{d(x,y)^{st}\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}\,dy\leq\sum_{j=k_{2}}^{\infty}r^{-t}\int_{A_{j}(x)}\frac{d(x,y)^{t(1-s)}}{\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}\,dy\\
&\leq r^{-t}\sum_{j=k_{2}}^{\infty}2^{-jt(1-s)}\int_{B(x,2^{-j})}\frac{1}{\mu(B(x,2^{-j-1}))}\,dy\\
&\leq Cr^{-t}2^{-k_{2}t(1-s)}\leq Cr^{-t}r^{t(1-s)}=Cr^{-st}.\end{split}

Here we had again a converging geometric series since
t(1-s)>0
.

Substituting the above two estimates to (
5.25
), we obtain

\begin{split}I_{1}&\leq C\int_{E_{r}\cap\lambda B}\sum_{j=k_{1}-1}^{k_{2}}2^{jst}\,dx+C\int_{E_{r}\cap\lambda B}r^{-st}\,dx\\
&\leq C\int_{E_{r}\cap\lambda B}\bigl(2^{k_{2}st}+r^{-st}\bigr)\,dx\leq Cr^{-st}\mu(E_{r}\cap\lambda B).\end{split}

As
\lambda B=B(w,R_{0})
, we conclude that
I_{1}+(1+c_{D})I_{2}\leq Cr^{-st}\mu(E_{r}\cap B(w,R_{0}))
,
and this proves the claim.
∎

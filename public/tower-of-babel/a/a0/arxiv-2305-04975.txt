Isogeometric Tearing and Interconnecting Solvers for Linearized Elasticity in multi-patch Isogeometric Analysis Journal: arXiv
Jarle Sogn, Stefan Takacs

Abstract
We consider the linearized elasticity equation, discretized with multi-patch Isogeometric Analysis. A standard discretization error analysis is based on Korn’s inequality, which degrades for certain geometries, such as long and thin cantilevers. This phenomenon is known as geometry locking. We observe that high-order methods, like Isogeometric Analysis is beneficial in such a setting. The main focus of this paper is the construction and analysis of a domain decomposition solver, namely an Isogeometric Tearing and Interconnecting (IETI) solver, where we prove that the convergence behavior does not depend on the constant of Korn’s inequality for the overall domain, but only on the corresponding constants for the individual patches. Moreover, our analysis is explicit in the choice of the spline degree. Numerical experiments are provided which demonstrates the efficiency of the proposed solver.



1
Introduction

In this paper, we consider a discretization and a solution strategy for the linearized elasticity problem. For small deformations
\mathbf{u}
of a body in static equilibrium, the equation

-2\mu\;\text{div}\,\epsilon(\mathbf{u})-\lambda\;\nabla\text{div}\,\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{f}

(1)

holds in a variational sense, where
\epsilon(\mathbf{u})=\frac{1}{2}(\nabla\mathbf{u}+\nabla^{\top}\mathbf{u})
is the symmetric gradient,
\lambda,\mu>0
are Lamé coefficients modelling the material properties and
\mathbf{f}
is the external volumetric force acting on the body, cf.
[
10
]
. In this model, the computational domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
models the shape of the undeformed object with boundary
\partial\Omega
. As boundary conditions, we use Dirichlet boundary conditions to prescribe the displacement on some part
\Gamma_{D}
of the boundary with positive measure and Neumann boundary conditions on the remainder
\Gamma_{N}:=\partial\Omega\setminus\Gamma_{D}
of the boundary to prescribe acting forces:

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{g}_{D}

\displaystyle\mbox{on}\quad\Gamma_{D},

(2)

\displaystyle 2\mu\;\epsilon(\mathbf{u})\;\mathbf{n}+\lambda\;\text{div}\,\mathbf{u}\;\mathbf{n}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{g}_{N}

\displaystyle\mbox{on}\quad\Gamma_{N},

(3)

where
\mathbf{g}_{D}
denotes the prescribed displacement and
\mathbf{g}_{N}
the prescribed boundary force density.

Several challenges may arise when discretizing and solving Equation (
1
). The ratio
\mu/\lambda
takes small values for nearly incomressible materials. The limit case
\mu/\lambda\to 0
can be understood as the case of completely incompressible materials. In these cases, the well-posedness (in the sense of the Lax-Milgram lemma) deteriorates, which might lead to a poor approximation of the solution with standard Galerkin discretizations. This phenomenon is known as material locking and geometry locking. A common remedy is to introduce an auxiliary variable and derive a mixed variational formulation, cf.
[
5
,
24
,
2
,
3
]
. The focus of this paper is not on material locking; thus, we assume
\mu/\lambda
is well bounded away from zero.

Another challenge is related to the symmetric gradient. For showing well-posedness in the standard Sobolev space
H^{1}
, one typically uses Korn’s inequality, which introduces a constant that usually depends on the geometry of the object in question. This happens for example for long and thin cantilevers. Also such domains might lead to a poor approximation of the solution with standard Galerkin discretizations, which is known as geometry locking. We will discuss this issue in detail and observe that high-order methods help to mitigate related issues, compared to standard discretizations with piecewise linear functions.

We use Isogeometric Analysis (IgA) as discretization method. IgA was introduced in the seminal paper
[
12
]
as a new technique for discretizing partial differential equations (PDEs) that improves the integration of simulation and computer aided design (CAD). In IgA, both the computational domain and the solution of the PDE are represented as linear combination of tensor-product B-splines or non-uniform rational B-splines (NURBS). Only simple domains can be parameterized using a single geometry mapping. More complicated domains are usually composed of several patches, where each patch is parameterized independently (multi-patch domains). For more information on IgA, see the review paper
[
4
]
and the references therein.

The main focus of this paper is the intoduction and the analysis of a fast domain decomposition solver for the linearized elasticity problem, discretized using IgA on multi-patch domains. We consider FETI-DP methods, which were originally introduced in
[
8
]
and has been used to solve linear elasticity, see, e.g.,
[
13
,
9
,
14
]
. For multi-patch IgA domains, the patches themselves serve as a natural choice for the substructures. FETI-DP was first adapted to IgA in
[
15
]
and named the
Dual-Primal Isogeometric Tearing and Interconnecting
(IETI-DP) method. In recent years, there has been a lot of research in IETI-DP methods, see, e.g.,
[
11
,
20
,
23
,
27
,
21
,
17
]
.

Isogeometric domain decomposition methods for mixed linear elasticity systems have been studied in
[
27
,
18
]
, where convergence estimates are provided. We study the primal formulation and use the results from
[
16
,
20
]
to prove a convergence estimate which is explicit with respect to both the spline degree and grid-size (Theorem
7
). The analysis shows that the performance of the solver does not depend on the shape of the overall computational domain, but only on the shapes of the individual patches. This means that one can obtain a robust IETI solver by setting up an adequate subdivision of the computational domain into patches.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce the variational formulation of the model problem and its discretization. The well-posedness and the discretization errors are discussed in the subsequent Section
3
. The proposed solver is introduced in Section
4
. The following Section
5
is dedicated to its analysis. We close with numerical results and concluding remarks in Section
6
.

2
Problem formulation and discretization

In this section we formulate the variational formulation of the elasticity problem (
1
) – (
3
) and its discretization.
Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be an open, connected and bounded domain with Lipschitz boundary
\partial\Omega
.
L^{2}(\Omega)
and
H^{s}(\Omega)
denote the standard Lebesgue and Sobolev spaces with standard norms
\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
,
\|\cdot\|_{H^{s}(\Omega)}
, seminorms
|\cdot|_{H^{s}(\Omega)}
, and scalar products
(\cdot,\cdot)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
. Analogously, we define these spaces, norms, seminorms and scalar products on other domains and on manifolds in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, like boundaries. For ease of notation, we assume that Dirichlet boundary conditions are homogeneous. So, the displacement has to be in
H^{1}_{0,D}(\Omega):=\left\{v\in H^{1}(\Omega):v|_{\Gamma_{D}}=0\right\}
. Here and in what follows a restriction to (a part of) the boundary is to be understood in the sense of a trace operator. By standard arguments, the following problem is the weak form of the elasticity equations.

Problem 1
.

Given
\mathbf{f}\in[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{2}
and
\mathbf{g}_{N}\in[L^{2}(\Gamma_{N})]^{2}
,
find
\mathbf{u}\in[H^{1}_{0,D}(\Omega)]^{2}
such that

a(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})=\langle F,\mathbf{v}\rangle\quad\mbox{for all}\quad\mathbf{v}\in[H^{1}_{0,D}(\Omega)]^{2},

(4)

where

\displaystyle a(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})

\displaystyle:=2\mu(\epsilon(\mathbf{u}),\epsilon(\mathbf{v}))_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\lambda\;(\text{div}\,\mathbf{u},\text{div}\,\mathbf{v})_{L^{2}(\Omega)},\mbox{ and }

\displaystyle\langle F,\mathbf{v}\rangle

\displaystyle:=(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{v})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+(\mathbf{g}_{N},\mathbf{v})_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{N})}.

We discretize the elasticity equations by means of multi-patch Isogeometric Analysis.
We assume that the computational domain
\Omega
is composed
of
K
non-overlapping patches
\Omega^{(k)}
, i.e., the domains

\Omega^{(k)}
are open, connected and bounded with Lipschitz boundary such that

\displaystyle\overline{\Omega}=\bigcup_{k=1}^{K}\overline{\Omega^{(k)}}\quad\text{and}\quad\Omega^{(k)}\cap\Omega^{(\ell)}=\emptyset\quad\text{for all}\quad k\neq\ell,

where
\overline{T}
denotes the closure of the set
T
.
We require that the patches form an admissible decomposition, i.e.,
that there are no T-junctions. This means that for any two patch indices
k\not=\ell
, the set
\partial{\Omega^{(k)}}\cap\partial{\Omega^{(\ell)}}
is either a common edge

\Gamma^{(k,\ell)}:=\partial{\Omega^{(k)}}\cap\partial{\Omega^{(\ell)}}
,
a common vertex, or empty (cf.
[
20
, Ass. 2]
).
Moreover, we assume that both the Neumann boundary and the Dirichlet boundary consist of whole edges.

For each patch index
k
, the set
\mathcal{N}_{\Gamma}(k)
contains the indices
\ell
of
patches
\Omega^{(\ell)}
that share an edge with
\Omega^{(k)}
.
The common vertices of two or more
patches – that are not located on the Dirichlet boundary – are denoted by

\mathbf{x}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{x}_{J}
. For each
j=1,\ldots,J
, the set
\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{x}}(j)
contains the
indices of all patches
\Omega^{(k)}
such that
x_{j}\in\partial\Omega^{(k)}
.
We assume that there is a constant
C_{1}
such that

|\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{x}}(j)|\leq C_{1}\quad\mbox{for all}\quad j=1,\ldots,J

(5)

(cf.
[
20
, Ass. 3]
).
The set of all vertices associated to one patch is denoted by

\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{x}}^{-1}(k):=\{j:k\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{x}}(j)\}.

Each patch
\Omega^{(k)}
is parameterized by a geometry mapping

\displaystyle\mathbf{G}_{k}:\widehat{\Omega}:=(0,1)^{2}\rightarrow\Omega^{(k)}:=\mathbf{G}_{k}(\widehat{\Omega})\subset\mathbb{R}^{2},

(6)

which can be continuously extended to the closure of the parameter domain

\widehat{\Omega}
. In IgA, the geometry mapping is typically represented using B-splines or NURBS. As usual, the computational methods do not depend on such a representation. We only assume that the geometry mappings are not too much distorted, i.e., there is a constant
C_{2}>0
such that

\|\nabla\mathbf{G}_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(\widehat{\Omega})}\leq C_{2}\,H_{k}\quad\text{and}\quad\|(\nabla\mathbf{G}_{k})^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}(\widehat{\Omega})}\leq C_{2}\,H_{k}^{-1}

(7)

holds for all
k=1,\ldots,K
, where
H_{k}
is the diameter of the patch
\Omega^{(k)}

(cf.
[
20
, Ass. 1]
).

On the parameter domain
\widehat{\Omega}=(0,1)^{2}
, we choose a B-spline space of some freely chosen spline degree
\mathpzc{p}\in\mathbb{N}:=\{1,2,3,\ldots\}
. The spline space depends on a freely
chosen
\mathpzc{p}
-open knot vector

\Xi^{(k,\delta)}:=(\xi^{(k,\delta)}_{0},\cdots,\xi^{(k,\delta)}_{N^{(k,\delta)}})
for each patch
k
and each spacial direction
\delta\in\{1,2\}
. We allow repeated (inner) knots in order to reduce smoothness; since we require a
H^{1}
-conforming discretization, no more than
\mathpzc{p}
interior knots may be repeated. Based on these knot vectors, the corresponding B-splines are obtained by the Cox-de Boor formula (cf.
[
7
, (2.1) and (2.2)]
); these B-splines span the spline space
S^{(k,\delta,\mathpzc{p})}\subset H^{1}(0,1)
. Based on these univariate splines, we introduce the corresponding tensor product spline space as

S^{(k,\mathpzc{p})}:=S^{(k,1,\mathpzc{p})}\otimes S^{(k,2,\mathpzc{p})}

as discretization space on the parameter domain
\widehat{\Omega}
, equipped with the standard tensor product basis.

The function spaces on the physical patches
\Omega^{(k)}
are defined via
the
pull-back principle
:

\mathbf{V}^{(k)}:=\left\{v:v\circ\mathbf{G}_{k}\in S^{(k,\mathpzc{p})},\;v\big|_{\Gamma_{D}\cap\partial\Omega^{(k)}}=0\right\}^{2}.

The grid size
\widehat{h}_{k}
on the parameter domain and the grid size
h_{k}
on the physical patch are defined by

\widehat{h}_{k}:=\max\left\{\xi_{i}^{(k,\delta)}-\xi_{i-1}^{(k,\delta)}\,:\,i=1,\ldots,N^{(k,\delta)},\,\delta=1,2\right\}\quad\mbox{and}\quad h_{k}:=H_{k}\widehat{h}_{k},

(8)

where the definition of the latter is motivated by (
7
). Analogously, we
define the minimum grid size

\widehat{h}_{\min,k}:=\min\left\{\xi_{i}^{(k,\delta)}-\xi_{i-1}^{(k,\delta)}\neq 0\,:\,i=1,\ldots,N^{(k,\delta)},\,\delta=1,2\right\}

and
h_{\min,k}:=H_{k}\widehat{h}_{\min,k}
.
We assume the grid to be quasi-uniformity, i.e., there is a constant
C_{3}
such that

\frac{\widehat{h}_{k}}{\widehat{h}_{\min,k}}=\frac{h_{k}}{h_{\min,k}}\leq C_{3}\text{ for }k=1,\ldots,K,

(9)

cf.
[
20
, Ass. 4]
.

Finally, we introduce a global discretization space. We assume that the discretization is fully matching (see
[
20
, Ass. 5]
), this means that for any two patches
\Omega^{(k)}
and
\Omega^{(\ell)}
, sharing an edge
\Gamma^{(k,\ell)}
, the traces agree,
\mathbf{V}^{(k)}\big|_{\Gamma^{(k,\ell)}}=\mathbf{V}^{(\ell)}\big|_{\Gamma^{(k,\ell)}}
and are represented using the same basis. So, as global discretization space, we use

\mathbf{V}=\left\{\mathbf{v}\in[H^{1}_{0,D}(\Omega)]^{2}\,:\,\mathbf{v}|_{\Omega^{(k)}}\in\mathbf{V}^{(k)}\text{ for }k=1,\ldots,K\right\}.

For non-matching interfaces or for patches with T-junctions, discontinuous Galerkin methods could be considered, see, e.g.,
[
21
]
. This, however goes beyond this paper.

The discretized problem reads as follows.

Problem 2
.

Given
\mathbf{f}\in[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{2}
and
\mathbf{g}_{N}\in[L^{2}(\Gamma_{N})]^{2}
,
find
\mathbf{u}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}
such that

a(\mathbf{u}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})=\langle F,\mathbf{v}_{h}\rangle\quad\mbox{for all}\quad\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{V},

(10)

where
a(\cdot,\cdot)
and
\langle F,\cdot\rangle
are as in Problem
1
.

Notation 1
.

Here and in what follows,
c
denotes a generic positive constant that only
depends on the constants
C_{1}
,
C_{2}
and
C_{3}
from (
5
), (
7
) and (
9
). We write
a\lesssim b
if there is such a generic constant such that
a\leq c\,b
. We write
a\eqsim b
if
a\lesssim b\lesssim a
.

3
Well posedness and discretization error

To show well-posedness both of Problem
1
and the discretized Problem
2
, we use the Lax-Milgram lemma
[
6
, Theorem 2.7.7]
, which is applicable since both
[H^{1}_{0,D}(\Omega)]^{2}
and
\mathbf{V}
are Hilbert spaces.
Thus, existence and uniqueness of solutions to the Problems
1
and
2
and their continuous dependence on the data is guaranteed if
a(\cdot,\cdot)
is coercive and bounded and if
\langle F,\cdot\rangle
is bounded; the latter follows directly from the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the trace theorem. For the further analysis, the estimates on the bilinear form are more relevant; also here, the boundedness is straight forward:

a(\textbf{u},\textbf{v})\leq(2\mu+2\lambda)\|\epsilon(\mathbf{u})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\epsilon(\mathbf{v})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq(2\mu+2\lambda)|\mathbf{u}|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}|\mathbf{v}|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}.

(11)

The crucial point is the coercivity. Assuming

\alpha_{\Omega,\Gamma_{D}}:=\inf_{\mathbf{u}\in H^{1}_{0,D}(\Omega)}\frac{\|\epsilon(\mathbf{u})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}}{|\mathbf{u}|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}}>0,

(12)

we obtain

a(\textbf{u},\textbf{u})\geq 2\mu\|\epsilon(\mathbf{u})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\geq 2\mu\alpha_{\Omega,\Gamma_{D}}^{2}|\mathbf{u}|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Using a standard Poincaré–Friedrichs inequality, cf.
[
6
, Proposition 5.3.4]
, we know that

C_{F}\|\mathbf{u}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq|\mathbf{u}|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq\|\mathbf{u}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\quad\mbox{for all}\quad\mathbf{u}\in\mathbf{V},

where the constant
C_{F}>0
depends on
\Omega
and
\Gamma_{D}
. Since the Poincaré–Friedrichs inequality guarantees that
|\cdot|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}
is actually a norm on
H^{1}_{0,D}(\Omega)
, we state the following estimates using the
norm

|\cdot|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}
.

An estimate of the kind of (
12
) is obtained using Korn’s second inequality. The following Lemma
1
is a handy statement that allows to the construction of various kinds of Korn’s inequalities, which we will use throughout this paper.

Remark 1
.

Although the constants of both the Korn and the Poincaré–Friedrichs inequality depend on the size of
\Omega
, the dependence of the Poincaré–Friedrichs inequality is less severe since the
L^{2}
-norm and the
H^{1}
-seminorm scale differently with the diameter of
\Omega
. This is different for Korn’s inequality:
\|\epsilon(\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
and
|\cdot|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}
both are invariant under scaling (or scale like
(\mathrm{diam}\,\Omega)^{d/2-1}
for
d\neq 2
).

The following Lemma is used to show (
12
).

Lemma 1
.

Let
\ell(\mathbf{u}):[H^{1}(\Omega)]^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
be a bounded
linear operator such that
\ell(\mathbf{r})\neq 0
for

\mathbf{r}(x,y):=\begin{pmatrix}-y\\
x\end{pmatrix}.

(13)

Then, there is a constant
\alpha_{\Omega,\ell}>0
that only depends on

\Omega
and
\ell
such that

\|\epsilon(\mathbf{v})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+|\ell(\mathbf{v})|\geq\alpha_{\Omega,\ell}|\mathbf{v}|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\quad\mbox{for all}\quad\mathbf{v}\in[H^{1}(\Omega)]^{2}.

(14)

A poof of this Lemma is given at the end of this section.
Since the proof is not constructive, we cannot give a lower bound for

\alpha_{\Omega,\ell}
in general. Note that the conditions of the Lemma are satisfied for
\ell_{\Gamma_{D}}(\mathbf{v}):=(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{r})_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{D})}
with
\mathbf{r}
as in (
13
); thus we obtain (
12
) with a constant
\alpha_{\Omega,\Gamma_{D}}>0
that depends on
\Omega
and
\Gamma_{D}
.

Remark 2
.

A simple scaling argument shows that the constant
\alpha_{\Omega,\Gamma_{D}}
does not
depend on the diameter of
\Omega
, but only on its shape.

Since the assumptions of the Lax-Milgram lamma hold, we know existence and uniqueness of a solution
\mathbf{u}
to Problem
1
and
\mathbf{u}_{h}
to Problem
2
. Using Ceá’s lemma (in its version
[
6
, Remark 2.8.5]
for symmetric bilinear forms), we can bound the discretization error
|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}_{h}|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}
by the approximation error as follows:

|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}_{h}|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq\alpha_{\Omega,\Gamma_{D}}^{-1}\,\sqrt{\frac{\mu+\lambda}{\mu}}\inf_{\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}}|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v}_{h}|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}.

This can be further estimated using an approximation error estimate.

Lemma 2
.

Let
q\in\{1,\cdots,\mathpzc{p}\}
and assume that the geometry functions
\mathbf{G}_{k}
are smooth enough such that there is a constant
\widetilde{C}_{2}
such that

|\mathbf{v}\circ\mathbf{G}_{k}|_{H^{q+1}(\widehat{\Omega})}\leq\widetilde{C}_{2}H_{k}^{q}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{H^{q+1}(\Omega^{(k)})}\quad\mbox{ for all }\quad\mathbf{v}\in[H^{q+1}(\Omega)]^{2}.

(15)

Then, the estimate

\inf_{\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}}|\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{v}_{h}|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}\lesssim\widetilde{C}_{2}^{2}\pi^{-2q}\sum_{k=1}^{K}h_{k}^{2q}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{H^{q+1}(\Omega^{(k)})}^{2}\;\mbox{holds for all}\;\mathbf{v}\in[H^{q+1}(\Omega)]^{2}.

(16)

This lemma is a slight extension of
[
25
, Theorem 3.6]
; for completeness, a proof is given at the end of this section. Using this lemma, we immediately obtain the discretization error estimate

|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}_{h}|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}\lesssim\widetilde{C}_{2}^{2}\alpha_{\Omega,\Gamma_{D}}^{-2}\,\frac{\mu+\lambda}{\mu}\pi^{-2q}\sum_{k=1}^{K}h_{k}^{2q}\|\mathbf{u}\|_{H^{q+1}(\Omega^{(k)})}^{2}.

(17)

We observe that the convergece deteriorates if
\alpha_{\Omega,\Gamma_{D}}\to 0
or
\mu/\lambda\to 0
. The latter happens in case of almost incompressible materials; incompressible materials correspond to the limit case. This effect is known as material locking. To avoid material locking, one usually considers a saddle point formulation of the elasticity equations, however, this is not in the scope of this paper.

The deterioration of the convergence in case
\alpha_{\Omega,\Gamma_{D}}
is too small, is usually known as geometry locking. In the following, we show cases where this can happen and show that the actual convergence might still be good, even if
\alpha_{\Omega,\Gamma_{D}}\to 0
. These discussions are done for a spacial case. Consider a rectangular domain

\Omega_{K}:=(0,K)\times(0,1)
with
K\in\{1,2,3,\cdots\}
, consisting of square patches
\Omega^{(k)}:=(k-1,k)\times(0,1)
for
k=1,\ldots,K
, i.e.,

\overline{\Omega_{K}}=\overline{(0,K)\times(0,1)}=\bigcup_{k=1}^{K}\overline{\Omega^{(k)}}=\bigcup_{k=1}^{K}\overline{(k-1,k)\times(0,1)},

see Figure
1

\mathbf{G}_{k}(\widehat{x},\widehat{y}):=(\widehat{x}+k-1,\widehat{y})^{\top}
. We consider the linearized elasticity equation with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions on
\Gamma_{D}:=\Gamma^{(0)}
and Neumann boundary conditions on
\Gamma_{N}:=\partial\Omega\setminus\Gamma_{D}
,
the remainder of the boundary.

The following Lemma states that the constant

\alpha_{\Omega_{K},\Gamma_{D}}:=\inf_{\mathbf{u}\in H^{1}_{0,D}(\Omega_{K})}\frac{\|\epsilon(\mathbf{u})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{K})}}{|\mathbf{u}|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{K})}}

for the corresponding Korn inequality grows linearly with the number of patches.

Lemma 3
.

For all
K\in\{1,2,3,\ldots\}
, we have
\alpha_{\Omega_{K},\Gamma_{D}}\eqsim K^{-1}
.

Proof.

An upper bound for
\alpha_{\Omega_{K},\Gamma_{D}}
is obtained for the choice

\mathbf{v}(x,y):=((2y-1)x,-x^{2})^{\top}
, which obviously satisfies the Dirichlet
conditions. For this choice, we can explicitly
compute
\alpha_{\Omega_{K},\Gamma_{D}}^{-1}=\sqrt{1+2K^{2}}\geq K
. (Note that for
\mathpzc{p}\geq 2
, the function
\mathbf{v}(x,y)
is in the spline space
\mathbf{V}
, which means that the constant
\alpha_{\Omega_{K},\Gamma_{D}}
cannot be improved by restricting to the discrete space
\mathbf{V}
.)

For a lower bound, we use Korn’s second inequality for the
parameter domain
\widehat{\Omega}:=(0,1)^{2}
.
Let
\mathbf{v}(x,y)=(v_{1}(x,y),v_{2}(x,y))^{\top}
be arbitrary and define

\mathbf{w}(x,y)=(w_{1}(x,y),w_{2}(x,y))^{\top}:=(v_{1}(Kx,y),\allowbreak K^{-1}v_{2}(Kx,y))^{\top}
. Then, we have using
simple scaling arguments

\displaystyle\frac{|\mathbf{v}|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}}{\|\epsilon(\mathbf{v})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}}\leq 1+\frac{\|\partial_{y}v_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\partial_{x}v_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}}{\|\partial_{x}v_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\|\partial_{y}v_{1}+\partial_{x}v_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\partial_{y}v_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\leq 1+\frac{\|\partial_{y}w_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Omega})}^{2}+\|\partial_{x}w_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Omega})}^{2}}{K^{-2}\|\partial_{x}w_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Omega})}^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\|\partial_{y}w_{1}+\partial_{x}w_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Omega})}^{2}+K^{2}\|\partial_{y}w_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Omega})}^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim K^{2}\frac{|\mathbf{w}|_{H^{1}(\widehat{\Omega})}^{2}}{\|\epsilon(\mathbf{w})\|_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Omega})}^{2}}\leq K^{2}\alpha_{\widehat{\Omega}}^{2},

where
\alpha_{\widehat{\Omega}}>0
is the constant resulting from
the use of Korn’s inequality on the parameter domain
\widehat{\Omega}
.
Since this constant is independent of the actual
geometry (particularly the number of patches
K
), we have
\alpha_{\widehat{\Omega}}\eqsim 1
. This finishes the proof.
∎

Based on these statements, the discretization error estimate (
17
) degrades for
K\to\infty
.
For
\lambda=0
, the following lemma shows that the proposed isogeometric
discretization does not suffer from geometry locking. This estimate is restricted to

\mathpzc{p}\geq 2
, which hints that it make sense to consider discretizations other than
the standard linear finite elements.

Lemma 4
.

Let
\lambda=0
and
\mathpzc{p}\geq 2
.
For all
K\in\{1,2,3,\ldots\}
, we have

|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}_{h}|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}\lesssim\inf_{\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}}|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v}_{h}|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2},

(18)

where
\mathbf{u}
and
\mathbf{u}_{h}
are the solutions to the Problem

and the discretized Problem

, respectively. Under the assumptions of Lemma

, we have further

|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}_{h}|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}\lesssim\widetilde{C}_{2}^{2}\pi^{-2q}\sum_{k=1}^{K}h_{k}^{2q}\|\mathbf{u}\|_{H^{q+1}(\Omega^{(k)})}^{2}.

Proof.

Let
\ell(w_{1},w_{2}):=\int_{0}^{1}(2y-1)\,w_{1}(1,y)\,\mathrm{d}y
. Due to the trace inequality, it is well-defined and bounded in
[H^{1}(\widehat{\Omega})]^{2}
. Moreover, we immediately observe that
\ell(\mathbf{r})\neq 0
for
\mathbf{r}
as in (

). Thus, Lemma

yields

|\mathbf{w}|_{H^{1}(\widehat{\Omega})}\leq\alpha_{\widehat{\Omega},\ell}^{-1}\left(\|\epsilon(\mathbf{w})\|_{L^{2}(\widehat{\Omega})}+|\ell(\mathbf{w})|\right),

where
\alpha_{\widehat{\Omega},\ell}>0
is the constant from Lemma

geometry (particularly the number of patches

\alpha_{\widehat{\Omega},\ell}\eqsim 1
.
Since this statement is invariant under translations, we obtain also

|\mathbf{w}|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}\lesssim\left(\|\epsilon(\mathbf{w})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left(\int_{0}^{1}(2y-1)\,w_{1}(k,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{2}\right)

(19)

for all
\mathbf{w}(\mathbf{x})=(w_{1}(\mathbf{x}),w_{2}(\mathbf{x}))^{\top}
.
Define

\psi_{k}(x,y):=\begin{cases}((2y-1)x,-x^{2})^{\top}&\mbox{for }x<k,\\
((2y-1)k,k^{2}-2kx)^{\top}&\mbox{for }x\geq k\end{cases}

and observe that

\epsilon(\psi_{k})=\begin{pmatrix}2y-1&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\chi_{x<k},

where
\chi_{x<k}
is the characteristic function, i.e., it takes the value

the Galerkin orthogonality implies

\int_{\Omega}\epsilon(\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}_{h}):\epsilon(\psi_{k})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}=0\quad\mbox{for all}\quad k\in\{1,\ldots,K\}.

Let
\mathbf{w}:=\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}_{h}=(w_{1},w_{2})^{\top}
. Using the explicit representation of
\psi_{k}
and the Dirichlet conditions on
\Gamma_{D}=\Gamma^{(0)}
, we obtain

0=\int_{0}^{k}\int_{0}^{1}(2y-1)\partial_{x}w_{1}(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{0}^{1}(2y-1)\,w_{1}(k,y)\,\mathrm{d}y.

In combination with (

|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}_{h}|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}\lesssim\|\epsilon(\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Using Galerkin orthogonality, we immediately obtain (

The second results directly follows using Lemma

.

We are interested in a fast solver for this problem, which does not degrade for domains with small Korn constant. For the solution, we aim for a domain decomposition approach, where the convergence rates only depend on the Korn constants for the individual patches, which serve as substructures for the domain decomposition solver; specifically, we require that

\|\epsilon(\mathbf{v})\|_{L^{2}({\Omega^{(k)}}\raisebox{6.3778pt}{})}\geq\alpha_{k}|\mathbf{v}|_{H^{1}({\Omega^{(k)}}\raisebox{6.3778pt}{})}\;\mbox{for all}\;\mathbf{v}\in[H^{1}({{\Omega^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}})]^{2}\;\mbox{with}\;\int_{{\Omega^{(k)}}\raisebox{6.3778pt}{}}\text{curl}\,\mathbf{v}\;\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}=0,

4
A IETI-DP solver for the elasticity problem

In this section, we outline the setup of the proposed IETI-DP method for solving the discretized elasticity problem (
10
).
For the IETI-DP method, we have to assemble the variational problem
locally, i.e., we have

\displaystyle{a^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}({\mathbf{u}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{},{\mathbf{v}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{})

\displaystyle:=2\mu(\epsilon({\mathbf{u}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}),\epsilon({\mathbf{v}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}))_{L^{2}(\Omega^{(k)})}+\lambda(\text{div}\,{\mathbf{u}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{},\text{div}\,{\mathbf{v}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{})_{L^{2}(\Omega^{(k)})}

\displaystyle\langle{F^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{},{\mathbf{v}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}\rangle

\displaystyle:=(\mathbf{f},{\mathbf{v}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{})_{L^{2}(\Omega^{(k)})}+(\mathbf{g}_{N},{\mathbf{v}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{})_{L^{2}({\Gamma_{N}^{(k)}}\raisebox{6.3778pt}{})},

(22)

where
{\Gamma_{N}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}:=\Gamma_{N}\cap\partial{\Omega^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}
.
If the problems would not be coupled, we would have

{\mathbf{u}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}\in{\mathbf{V}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}
with

{a^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}({\mathbf{u}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{},{\mathbf{v}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{=}}\langle{F^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{},{\mathbf{v}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}\rangle\quad\mbox{for all}\quad{\mathbf{v}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}\in{\mathbf{V}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}.

By fixing bases, we obtain linear systems

{A^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}{\underline{\mathbf{u}}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{=}}\underline{\mathbf{f}}^{(k)}.

(23)

Here and in what follows, underlined quantities refer to the coefficient
representations of the corresponding functions.
We represent the spaces
\mathbf{V}^{(k)}
as direct sum

\mathbf{V}^{(k)}=\mathbf{V}^{(k)}_{\Gamma}\oplus\mathbf{V}^{(k)}_{\mathrm{I}}
, where
\mathbf{V}^{(k)}_{\mathrm{I}}
is spanned by the basis functions which vanish on the interfaces and
\mathbf{V}^{(k)}_{\Gamma}
is spanned by the remaining functions, i.e., the functions that are active on the interfaces (which includes the primal degrees of freedom). Assuming a corresponding ordering of the basis functions, we have

\begin{pmatrix}{A_{\Gamma\Gamma}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}&{A_{\Gamma\mathrm{I}}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}\\
{A_{\mathrm{I}\Gamma}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}&{A_{\mathrm{I}\mathrm{I}}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}\\
\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\underline{\mathbf{u}}_{\Gamma}^{(k)}\\
\underline{\mathbf{u}}_{\mathrm{I}}^{(k)}\end{pmatrix}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{=}}\begin{pmatrix}\underline{\mathbf{f}}_{\Gamma}^{(k)}\\
\underline{\mathbf{f}}_{\mathrm{I}}^{(k)}\end{pmatrix}.

(24)

These local systems correspond to pure Neumann problems, unless the corresponding patch contributes to the Dirichlet boundary. Therefore, these systems are not uniquely solvable since the constant is in the null space of
{A^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}
.
To ensure that the system matrices of the patch-local problems are non-singular, we introduce the function values on each of the corners of the patch are primal degrees of freedom, i.e.,

\mathbf{u}^{(k)}(\textbf{x}_{j})=\mathbf{u}^{(\ell)}(\textbf{x}_{j})\qquad\mbox{for all}\qquad j=1,\ldots,J\mbox{ and }k,\ell\in\mathcal{N}_{\textbf{x}}(j).

The convergence analysis that we develop carries over automatically to the case where additional primal degrees of freedom are introduced, like the edge averages of
\mathbf{u}
or its normal component; we consider such choices in the numerical experiments presented in Section
6
. For the theoretical considerations, we require only the corner values to be chosen as primal
degrees of freedom.

The corresponding Lagrangian multipliers are denoted by
{\mu^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}
. We obtain

\bar{A}^{(k)}\bar{\underline{\mathbf{u}}}^{(k)}:=\begin{pmatrix}{A_{\Gamma\Gamma}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}&{A_{\Gamma\mathrm{I}}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}&{C^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}^{\top}\\
{A_{\mathrm{I}\Gamma}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}&{A_{\mathrm{I}\mathrm{I}}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}&0\\
{C^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}&0&0\\
\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}{\underline{\mathbf{u}}_{\Gamma}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}\\
{\underline{\mathbf{u}}_{\mathrm{I}}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}\\
{\underline{\mu}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}\end{pmatrix}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{=}}\begin{pmatrix}{\underline{\mathbf{f}}_{\Gamma}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}\\
{\underline{\mathbf{f}}_{\mathrm{I}}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}\\
0\end{pmatrix}=:\bar{\underline{\mathbf{f}}}^{(k)},

(25)

where
{C^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}
evaluates the primal degrees of freedom associated to the patch
{\Omega^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}
,
such that
{C^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}{\underline{\mathbf{u}}_{\Gamma}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}=0
.
The continuity between the patches is enforced
by the matrices
{B^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}
. The term

\sum_{k=1}^{K}{B^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}\underline{\mathbf{u}}_{\Gamma}^{(k)}

evaluates to a vector containing the differences of the coefficients of any two matching
basis functions. Here, we do not include
the vertex values of (see Figure
2
) since these are primal
degrees of freedom. The relation

\sum_{k=1}^{K}{B^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}\underline{\mathbf{u}}_{\Gamma}^{(k)}=0

guarantees the continuity across the patches.

Moreover, we introduce the primal problem, i.e., the global problem for the primal degrees of freedom. We use a
A^{(k)}
-orthogonal basis for the primal degrees of freedom. This basis is represented in terms of the basis functions of the basis for
\mathbf{V}_{\Gamma}^{(k)}\times\mathbf{V}_{\mathrm{I}}^{(k)}
using the matrix
\Psi^{(k)}
, which are the solution of the system

\begin{pmatrix}{A_{\Gamma\Gamma}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}&{A_{\Gamma\mathrm{I}}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}&{C^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}^{\top}\\
{A_{\mathrm{I}\Gamma}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}&{A_{\mathrm{I}\mathrm{I}}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}&0\\
{C^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}&0&0\\
\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}{\Psi_{\Gamma}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}\\
{\Psi_{\mathrm{I}}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}\\
{\Phi^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}\\
\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
0\\
{R^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}\end{pmatrix},

(26)

where
{R^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}
is a boolean matrix that selects the primal degrees of freedom which are active on the patch
\Omega^{(k)}
.
We have
{R^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}\in\mathbb{R}^{N^{(k)}_{\Pi}\times N_{\Pi}}
, where
N^{(k)}_{\Pi}
is the number of primal degrees of freedom corresponding to the
k
’th patch (thus
N^{(k)}_{\Pi}=8
if the patch does not contribute to the Dirichlet boundary) and
N_{\Pi}
is the overall number of primal degrees of freedom.

We define the system matrix, right-hand side and jump matrix for the primal problem as

A_{\Pi}:=\sum^{K}_{k=1}{\Psi^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}^{\top}{A^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}{\Psi^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{},\quad\underline{\mathbf{f}}_{\Pi}:=\sum^{K}_{k=1}{\Psi^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}^{\top}{\underline{\bar{\mathbf{f}}}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}\quad\text{and}\quad B_{\Pi}:=\sum^{K}_{k=1}{B^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}{\Psi^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}.

Finally we are able to write down the overall IETI-DP system:

\begin{pmatrix}{\bar{A}^{(1)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}&&&&{\bar{B}^{(1)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}^{\top}\\
&\ddots&&&\vdots\\
&&{\bar{A}^{(K)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}&&{\bar{B}^{(K)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}^{\top}\\
&&&{A}_{\Pi}&{B}_{\Pi}^{\top}\\
{\bar{B}^{(1)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}&\ldots&{\bar{B}^{(K)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}&{B}_{\Pi}\\
\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}{\underline{\bar{\mathbf{x}}}^{(1)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}\\
\vdots\\
{\underline{\bar{\mathbf{x}}}^{(K)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}\\
\underline{{\mathbf{x}}}_{\Pi}\\
\underline{\lambda}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}{\underline{\bar{\mathbf{f}}}^{(1)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}\\
\vdots\\
{\underline{\bar{\mathbf{f}}}^{(K)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}\\
\underline{\mathbf{f}}_{\Pi}\\
0\end{pmatrix}.

(27)

For solving this linear system, we take the Schur complement with
respect to the Lagrange multipliers
\underline{\lambda}
.
This means that we solve

F\underline{\lambda}=\underline{g},

(28)

where

F:={B}_{\Pi}{A}^{-1}_{\Pi}{B}_{\Pi}^{\top}+\sum^{K}_{k=1}{\bar{B}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}{\bar{A}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}^{-1}{\bar{B}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}^{\top}\;\mbox{and}\;\underline{g}:={B}_{\Pi}{A}^{-1}_{\Pi}{\underline{\mathbf{f}}}_{\Pi}+\sum^{K}_{k=1}\bar{B}^{(k)}{\bar{A}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}^{-1}\bar{\underline{\mathbf{f}}}^{(k)}

The patch-local solutions are then recovered by

\underline{{\mathbf{x}}}_{\Pi}={A}_{\Pi}^{-1}\left(\underline{{\mathbf{f}}}_{\Pi}-B_{\Pi}^{\top}\underline{\lambda}\right)\quad\mbox{and}\quad{\underline{\bar{\mathbf{x}}}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}={\bar{A}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}^{-1}\left(\underline{\bar{\mathbf{f}}}^{(k)}-{\bar{B}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}^{\top}\underline{\lambda}\right).

The final solution is then obtained by distributing
\underline{{\mathbf{x}}}_{\Pi}
to the patches.

We solve the system (
28
) with a conjugate gradient solver, preconditioned with the scaled Dirichlet preconditioner

M_{\mathrm{sD}}:=\sum_{k=1}^{K}{B^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}\mathcal{D}_{k}^{-1}{S_{A}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}\mathcal{D}_{k}^{-1}{B^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}^{\top},

where
\mathcal{D}_{k}:=2I
is set up based on the principle of multiplicity scaling and
{S_{A}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}:={A_{\Gamma\Gamma}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}-{A_{\Gamma\mathrm{I}}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}{A_{\mathrm{I}\mathrm{I}}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}^{-1}{A_{\mathrm{I}\Gamma}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}
is the local Schur complement based on the splitting (
24
).

Remark 3
.

In this paper we focus on solving (
28
) for the symmetric positive definite matrix
F
.
This requires the exact representation of the inverses of
{\bar{A}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.17776pt}{}
. If these are unavailable, then efficient approximations of the inverses can be used by instead solving (

). For more information see, e.g.,

5
Condition number analysis for the IETI solver

The convergence rates of the conjugate gradient solver can be estimated based on the condition number of the preconditioned system
M_{\mathrm{sD}}\bar{F}
, based on the framework introduced in
[
16
]
and based on the results from
[
20
]
, where a IETI-DP solver for the Poisson problem has been analyzed in detail.

In this paper, we are interested in a IETI-DP solver for the linearized elasticity problem. The extension of the results from
[
20
]
to the vector-valued is not an issue since all proofs could be reused verbatim. The main difference is that we have to show the coercivity of the bilinear form associated to the linearized elasticity problem, which again requires the use of the Korn inequality. In general, not every patch touches the Dirichlet boundary
\Gamma_{D}
. Thus, we need a framework that allows the application of the Korn inequality.

Let
\mathbf{V}_{d}:=\mathbf{V}^{(1)}\times\cdots\times\mathbf{V}^{(K)}
. The space
\widetilde{\mathbf{V}}\subseteq\mathbf{V}_{d}
models all functions whose corner values are continuous:

\widetilde{\mathbf{V}}:=\left\{\mathbf{v}\in\mathbf{V}_{d}:\mathbf{v}^{(k)}(\textbf{x}_{j})=\mathbf{v}^{(\ell)}(\textbf{x}_{j})\mbox{ for all }k,\ell\in\mathcal{N}_{\textbf{x}}(j)\mbox{ with }j\in\{1,\ldots,J\}\right\},

where
\mathbf{v}=(\mathbf{v}^{(1)},\cdots,\mathbf{v}^{(K)})
.

In the following, we show that the
H^{1}
-seminorm can be bounded from above by the semi-norm
\|\epsilon(\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\Omega^{(k)}}\raisebox{6.3778pt}{})}
if we guarantee that the corner values vanish. This, of course, is only possible for spaces, where the notation of the corner values is well-defined, like the discrete space
{\mathbf{V}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}
. Let
\mathcal{Q}_{k}:=\{\mathbf{v}:\mathbf{v}\circ\mathbf{G}_{k}\mbox{ is bilinear}\}
be the space of all isogeometric functions with bilinear pre-image. Since a bilinear function is uniquely given by its values at the corners of the unit square
\widehat{\Omega}
, the following projector is well-defined:

{\Pi_{F}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}:{\mathbf{V}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}\to\mathcal{Q}_{k},\quad\mbox{such that}\quad({\Pi_{F}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}{\mathbf{v}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{})(\textbf{x}_{\ell})={\mathbf{v}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}(\textbf{x}_{\ell})\quad\mbox{for all}\quad\ell\in\mathcal{N}_{\textbf{x}}^{-1}(k).

The following lemma gives upper bounds both for the
H^{1}
-seminorm and the
L^{\infty}_{0}
-seminorm, given by

|{\mathbf{v}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}|_{L^{\infty}_{0}({\Omega^{(k)}}\raisebox{6.3778pt}{})}:=\inf_{\mu_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\|{\mathbf{v}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}-\mu_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}({\Omega^{(k)}}\raisebox{6.3778pt}{})},

of
{\mathbf{v}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}-{\Pi_{F}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}{\mathbf{v}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}
, which vanishes on the corners by construction.

Lemma 5
.

For all
k=1,\ldots,K
, the inequalities

\displaystyle|{\mathbf{v}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.17776pt}{}-{\Pi_{F}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.17776pt}{}{\mathbf{v}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.17776pt}{}|_{H^{1}({\Omega^{(k)}}\raisebox{6.75566pt}{})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\alpha_{k}^{-1}\Lambda_{k}^{1/2}\|\epsilon({\mathbf{v}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.17776pt}{})\|_{L^{2}({\Omega^{(k)}}\raisebox{6.75566pt}{})},

\displaystyle|{\mathbf{v}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.17776pt}{}-{\Pi_{F}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.17776pt}{}{\mathbf{v}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.17776pt}{}|_{L_{0}^{\infty}({\Omega^{(k)}}\raisebox{6.75566pt}{})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\alpha_{k}^{-1}\Lambda_{k}^{1/2}\|\epsilon({\mathbf{v}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.17776pt}{})\|_{L^{2}({\Omega^{(k)}}\raisebox{6.75566pt}{})}

hold for all
\mathbf{v}\in\widetilde{\mathbf{V}}
, where

\alpha_{k}
is the constant from (
20
),

\Lambda_{k}:=1+\log\mathpzc{p}+\log\frac{H_{k}}{h_{k}}
.

Proof.

For any
\mathbf{v}\in\widetilde{\mathbf{V}}
, there is a decomposition
{\mathbf{v}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}={\mathbf{w}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}+\rho_{k}\mathbf{r}
,
with
\int_{{\Omega^{(k)}}\raisebox{6.3778pt}{}}\text{curl}\,{\mathbf{w}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}\,\mathrm{d}x=0
,

\mathbf{r}
as in (
13
) and
\rho_{k}\in\mathbb{R}
. Using the triangle
inequality, we have

|{\mathbf{v}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}-{\Pi_{F}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}{\mathbf{v}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}|_{H^{1}({\Omega^{(k)}}\raisebox{6.3778pt}{})}\leq|{\mathbf{w}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}|_{H^{1}({\Omega^{(k)}}\raisebox{6.3778pt}{})}+|{\Pi_{F}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}{\mathbf{v}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}-\rho_{k}\mathbf{r}|_{H^{1}({\Omega^{(k)}}\raisebox{6.3778pt}{})}.

(29)

Since both
{\Pi_{F}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}{\mathbf{v}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}
and
\mathbf{r}
are bilinear functions,
the term
{\Pi_{F}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}{\mathbf{v}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}-\rho_{k}\mathbf{r}
is uniquely determined by the
function values at the corners. Since only the
H^{1}
-seminorm is considered, only
deviations from the average matter; thus

|{\Pi_{F}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}{\mathbf{v}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}-\rho_{k}\mathbf{r}|_{H^{1}({\Omega^{(k)}}\raisebox{6.3778pt}{})}\lesssim\psi_{k}:=\inf_{\mu_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{x}}^{-1}(k)}|({\Pi_{F}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}{\mathbf{v}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{})(\mathbf{x}_{\ell})-\rho_{k}\mathbf{r}(\mathbf{x}_{\ell})-\mu_{k}|.

(30)

Since
{\Pi_{F}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}{\mathbf{v}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}
and
{\mathbf{v}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}
coincide at the corners, we obtain

\psi_{k}=\inf_{\mu_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{x}}^{-1}(k)}|{\mathbf{v}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}(\mathbf{x}_{\ell})-\rho_{k}\mathbf{r}(\mathbf{x}_{\ell})-\mu_{k}|=\inf_{\mu_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{x}}^{-1}(k)}|{\mathbf{w}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}(\mathbf{x}_{\ell})-\mu_{k}|.

Using
[
20
, Lemma 4.14]
and the usual relation between
parameter domain and physical domain and the Poincaré–Friedrichs inequality, we obtain further

\psi_{k}\lesssim\Lambda_{k}^{1/2}\inf_{\mu_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\|{\mathbf{w}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}-\mu_{k}\|_{H^{1}({\Omega^{(k)}}\raisebox{6.3778pt}{})}\\
\lesssim\Lambda_{k}^{1/2}|{\mathbf{w}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}|_{H^{1}({\Omega^{(k)}}\raisebox{6.3778pt}{})}.

(31)

Together with (
30
) and (
29
), this yields

\displaystyle|{\mathbf{v}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}-{\Pi_{F}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}{\mathbf{v}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}|_{H^{1}({\Omega^{(k)}}\raisebox{6.3778pt}{})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Lambda_{k}^{1/2}|{\mathbf{w}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}|_{H^{1}({\Omega^{(k)}}\raisebox{6.3778pt}{})}.

Finally, (
20
) gives the desired first estimate. For the
second result, use again the triangle inequality, the fact that the
L_{0}^{\infty}
-seminorm of the bilinear function
{\Pi_{F}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}{\mathbf{v}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}-\rho_{k}\mathbf{r}
can be bounded by its values at the corner,

[
20
, Lemma 4.14]
, and (
30
) and (
31
) yield

\displaystyle|{\mathbf{v}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}-{\Pi_{F}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}{\mathbf{v}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}|_{L_{0}^{\infty}({\Omega^{(k)}}\raisebox{6.3778pt}{})}\lesssim|{\mathbf{w}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}|_{L_{0}^{\infty}({\Omega^{(k)}}\raisebox{6.3778pt}{})}+\psi_{k}\lesssim\Lambda_{k}^{1/2}|{\mathbf{w}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}|_{H^{1}({\Omega^{(k)}}\raisebox{6.3778pt}{})},

from which the second estimate follows using (
20
).
∎

We build our main condition number estimate on the framework from
[
16
]
. To obtain a concise notation, let
\mathbf{W}_{d}:=\mathbf{W}^{(1)}\times\cdots\times\mathbf{W}^{(K)}
with
\mathbf{W}^{(k)}:=\{\mathbf{v}|_{\partial\Omega^{(k)}}\,:\,\mathbf{v}\in\mathbf{V}^{(k)}\}
be the skeleton space and let

\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}:=\left\{\mathbf{w}\in\mathbf{W}_{d}:\mathbf{w}^{(k)}(\textbf{x}_{j})=\mathbf{w}^{(\ell)}(\textbf{x}_{j})\;\forall\;k,\ell\in\mathcal{N}_{\textbf{x}}(j)\mbox{ with }j\in\{1,\ldots,J\}\right\},

where
\mathbf{w}=(\mathbf{w}^{(1)},\cdots,\mathbf{w}^{(K)})
, be the corresponding space which is continuous at the corners.
Here and in what follows, for any function, say
{\mathbf{w}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}

in
{\mathbf{W}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}
, the corresponding underlined
symbol, here
{\underline{\mathbf{w}}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}
, denotes the representation
of the corresponding functions with respect to the basis for the space

{\mathbf{W}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}
. The overall vector
\underline{\mathbf{w}}
is a block-vector containing the local contributions
{\underline{\mathbf{w}}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}
.
Analogously,
B
is block-row matrix containing the local matrices
{B^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}
and
\mathcal{D}
and
S_{A}
are block-diagonal matrices containing the local matrices
\mathcal{D}_{k}
and
{S_{A}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}
, respectively.

[
16
, Theorem 22]
states that the condition number bound depends on the maximum value of the ratio

\frac{\|\mathcal{D}^{-1}B^{\top}B\underline{\mathbf{v}}\|_{S_{A}}^{2}}{\|\underline{\mathbf{v}}\|_{S_{A}}^{2}},

(32)

where the vector
\underline{\mathbf{v}}
is a coefficient representation of a function
\mathbf{v}\in\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}
. The following Lemma gives an upper bound for that ratio.

Lemma 6
.

For all
\mathbf{v}\in\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}
with coefficient representation
\underline{\mathbf{v}}
, the estimate

\|\mathcal{D}^{-1}B^{\top}B\underline{\mathbf{v}}\|_{S_{A}}^{2}\lesssim\frac{\mu+\lambda}{\mu}\,\mathpzc{p}\left(1+\log\mathpzc{p}+\max_{k=1,\ldots,K}\log\frac{H_{k}}{h_{k}}\right)^{2}\left(\max_{k=1,\ldots,K}\alpha_{k}^{-2}\right)\|\underline{\mathbf{v}}\|_{S_{A}}^{2}

holds.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{w},\mathbf{v}\in\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}
with coefficient representations

\underline{\mathbf{w}}
and
\underline{\mathbf{v}}
be such that

\underline{\mathbf{w}}:=\mathcal{D}^{-1}B^{\top}B\underline{\mathbf{v}}
.
Since the Schur-complement is the energy-minimizing extension and using the boundedness of
A
, we have
\|\mathcal{D}^{-1}B^{\top}B\underline{\mathbf{v}}\|_{S_{A}}^{2}=\|\underline{\mathbf{w}}\|_{S_{A}}^{2}
with

\displaystyle\|\underline{\mathbf{w}}\|_{S_{A}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\inf_{\begin{array}[]{c}\mbox{\scriptsize${\mathbf{u}^{(k)}}\raisebox{6.3778pt}{}\in{\mathbf{V}^{(k)}}\raisebox{6.3778pt}{}$}\\
\mbox{\scriptsize$\left.{\mathbf{u}^{(k)}}\raisebox{6.3778pt}{}\right|_{\partial{\Omega^{(k)}}\raisebox{3.92001pt}{}}={\mathbf{w}^{(k)}}\raisebox{6.3778pt}{}$}\end{array}}2\mu\|\epsilon({\mathbf{u}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{})\|_{L^{2}({\Omega^{(k)}}\raisebox{6.3778pt}{})}^{2}+\lambda\|\text{div}\,{\mathbf{u}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}\|_{L^{2}({\Omega^{(k)}}\raisebox{6.3778pt}{})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2(\mu+\lambda)\sum_{k=1}^{K}\inf_{\begin{array}[]{c}\mbox{\scriptsize${\mathbf{u}^{(k)}}\raisebox{6.3778pt}{}\in{\mathbf{V}^{(k)}}\raisebox{6.3778pt}{}$}\\
\mbox{\scriptsize$\left.{\mathbf{u}^{(k)}}\raisebox{6.3778pt}{}\right|_{\partial{\Omega^{(k)}}\raisebox{3.92001pt}{}}={\mathbf{w}^{(k)}}\raisebox{6.3778pt}{}$}\end{array}}|{\mathbf{u}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}|_{H^{1}({\Omega^{(k)}}\raisebox{6.3778pt}{})}^{2}.

This term is the
H^{1}
-energy of the discrete harmonic extension

\mathcal{H}^{(k)}:\mathbf{W}^{(k)}\rightarrow\mathbf{V}^{(k)}
,
which by definition minimizes

|\mathcal{H}^{(k)}\mathbf{w}^{(k)}|_{H^{1}(\Omega^{(k)})}

under the constraint

\left(\mathcal{H}^{(k)}\mathbf{w}^{(k)}\right)\Big|_{\partial\Omega^{(k)}}=\mathbf{w}^{(k)}
.
This means that we have

\|\underline{\mathbf{w}}\|_{S_{A}}^{2}\leq 2(\mu+\lambda)\sum_{k=1}^{K}|\mathcal{H}^{(k)}\mathbf{w}^{(k)}|_{H^{1}(\Omega^{(k)})}^{2}.

Using
[
20
, Theorem 4.2 and Lemma 4.15]
, we further have

\displaystyle\|\underline{\mathbf{w}}\|_{S_{A}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2(\mu+\lambda)\mathpzc{p}\sum_{k=1}^{K}|\mathbf{w}^{(k)}|_{H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega^{(k)})}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2(\mu+\lambda)\mathpzc{p}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{N}_{\Gamma}(k)}\left(|\mathbf{w}^{(k)}|_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma^{(k,\ell)})}^{2}+\Lambda|\mathbf{w}^{(k)}|_{L_{0}^{\infty}(\Gamma^{(k,\ell)})}^{2}\right),

where
\Lambda:=\left(1+\log\mathpzc{p}+\max_{k=1,\ldots,K}\log\frac{H_{k}}{h_{k}}\right)
.
Since
\mathbf{v}^{(k)}
is continuous at the corners, standard arguments yield

\mathbf{w}^{(k)}|_{\Gamma^{(k,\ell)}}=\mathbf{v}^{(k)}|_{\Gamma^{(k,\ell)}}-\mathbf{v}^{(\ell)}|_{\Gamma^{(k,\ell)}},

see, e.g.,
[
20
, Proof of Lemma 4.16]

for a proof. Using this statement, we further have

\|\underline{\mathbf{w}}\|_{S_{A}}^{2}\lesssim 2(\mu+\lambda)\mathpzc{p}\Lambda\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{N}_{\Gamma}(k)}\left(|\mathbf{v}^{(k)}|_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma^{(k,\ell)})}^{2}+|\mathbf{v}^{(k)}|_{L_{0}^{\infty}(\Gamma^{(k,\ell)})}^{2}\right).

We substitute
{\mathbf{v}_{0}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}:=(I-\Pi_{F}^{(k)}){\mathbf{v}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}
for
{\mathbf{v}^{(k)}}\raisebox{8.00003pt}{}
.
Since
\Pi_{F}^{(k)}\mathbf{v}^{(k)}|_{\Gamma^{(k,\ell)}}=\Pi_{F}^{(\ell)}\mathbf{v}^{(\ell)}|_{\Gamma^{(k,\ell)}}
,
we know that
\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{v}_{0}
vanishes on all interfaces. Thus,

B\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{0}=B\underline{\mathbf{v}}=0
and consequently

\|\underline{\mathbf{w}}\|_{S_{A}}^{2}\lesssim\tilde{\Lambda}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{N}_{\Gamma}(k)}\left(|(I-\Pi_{F}^{(k)})\mathbf{v}^{(k)}|_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma^{(k,\ell)})}^{2}+|(I-\Pi_{F}^{(k)})\mathbf{v}^{(k)}|_{L_{0}^{\infty}(\Gamma^{(k,\ell)})}^{2}\right),

where
\tilde{\Lambda}:=2(\mu+\lambda)\mathpzc{p}\Lambda
.
Using
[
20
, Theorem 4.2]
and the fact that the

\mathcal{H}^{(k)}
is the extension operator that minimizes the
H^{1}
-seminorm,
we obtain

\displaystyle\|\underline{\mathbf{w}}\|_{S_{A}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\tilde{\Lambda}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left(|\mathcal{H}^{(k)}(I-\Pi_{F}^{(k)})\mathbf{v}^{(k)}|_{H^{1}(\Omega^{(k)})}^{2}+\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{N}_{\Gamma}(k)}|(I-\Pi_{F}^{(k)})\mathbf{v}^{(k)}|_{L_{0}^{\infty}(\Gamma^{(k,\ell)})}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{\Lambda}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left(|\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}^{(k)}(I-\Pi_{F}^{(k)})\mathbf{v}^{(k)}|_{H^{1}(\Omega^{(k)})}^{2}+\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{N}_{\Gamma}(k)}|(I-\Pi_{F}^{(k)})\mathbf{v}^{(k)}|_{L_{0}^{\infty}(\Gamma^{(k,\ell)})}^{2}\right),

where
\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}^{(k)}:\mathbf{W}^{(k)}\rightarrow\mathbf{V}^{(k)}
,
which minimizes the energy

\|\epsilon(\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}^{(k)}\mathbf{w}^{(k)})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{(k)})}

under the constraint

\left(\mathcal{H}^{(k)}_{\epsilon}\mathbf{w}^{(k)}\right)\Big|_{\partial\Omega^{(k)}}=\mathbf{w}^{(k)}
.
We use the fact that

\mathcal{H}^{(k)}_{\epsilon}
does not change the values at the interfaces
and
\Pi_{F}^{(k)}\mathcal{H}^{(k)}_{\epsilon}=\Pi_{F}^{(k)}
to obtain

\displaystyle\|\underline{\mathbf{w}}\|_{S_{A}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\tilde{\Lambda}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left(|(\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}^{(k)}-\Pi_{F}^{(k)})\mathbf{v}^{(k)}|_{H^{1}(\Omega^{(k)})}^{2}+|(\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}^{(k)}-\Pi_{F}^{(k)})\mathbf{v}^{(k)}|_{L_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega^{(k)})}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\tilde{\Lambda}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left(|(I-\Pi_{F}^{(k)})\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}^{(k)}\mathbf{v}^{(k)}|_{H^{1}(\Omega^{(k)})}^{2}+|(I-\Pi_{F}^{(k)})\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}^{(k)}\mathbf{v}^{(k)}|_{L_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega^{(k)})}^{2}\right).

Since
(I-\Pi_{F}^{(k)})\mathbf{v}^{(k)}
vanishes at the corners,
Lemma
5
gives

\displaystyle\|\underline{\mathbf{w}}\|_{S_{A}}^{2}=\|\mathcal{D}^{-1}B^{\top}B\underline{\mathbf{v}}\|_{S_{A}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2(\mu+\lambda)\mathpzc{p}\,\Lambda^{2}\,\sum_{k=1}^{K}\alpha_{k}^{-2}\left(\|\epsilon(\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}^{(k)}\mathbf{v}^{(k)})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{(k)})}^{2}+\|\epsilon(\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}^{(k)}\mathbf{v}^{(k)})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{(k)})}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim 2(\mu+\lambda)\mathpzc{p}\,\Lambda^{2}\,\left(\max_{k=1,\ldots,K}\alpha_{k}^{-2}\right)\sum_{k=1}^{K}\|\epsilon(\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}^{(k)}\mathbf{v}^{(k)})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{(k)})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mu+\lambda}{\mu}\mathpzc{p}\,\Lambda^{2}\left(\max_{k=1,\ldots,K}\alpha_{k}^{-2}\right)\,\|\underline{\mathbf{v}}\|_{S_{A}}^{2},

which finishes the proof.
∎

The primal elasticity formulation considered in this paper is an elliptic problem, which directly fits into the framework of
[
16
]
. Altough, a scalar valued problem was considered there, the main results carry over directly to vector-valued problems. So, we can directly apply
[
16
, Theorem 22]
to obtain the following condition number bound.

Theorem 7
.

Provided that the IETI-DP solver is set up as outlined in the previous
section, the condition number of the preconditioned system satisfies

\kappa_{\mathrm{ess}}(M_{\mathrm{sD}}F)\leq c\,\frac{\mu+\lambda}{\mu}\,\mathpzc{p}\left(1+\log\mathpzc{p}+\max_{k=1,\ldots,K}\log\frac{H_{k}}{h_{k}}\right)^{2}\,\left(\max_{k=1,\ldots,K}\alpha_{k}^{-2}\right)\,,

where
c
is a constant that only depends on the constants from the assumptions (
5
), (
7
) and (
9
) (cf. Notation
1
),
\alpha_{k}
are the patch-local Korn constants from (
20
) and
\kappa_{\mathrm{ess}}
denotes the essential condition number, which is the ratio between the largest eigenvalue and the smallest non-zero eigenvalue.

Proof.

[
16
, Theorem 22]
states that the
bound follows directly from Lemma
6
.
∎

F
,
preconditioned with a non-singular preconditioner

all iterations live in the corresponding factor space. The convergence

6.3
Concluding remarks

In this paper, a IETI-DP solution strategy was proposed and a bound for the condition number of the preconditioned system was proven. This bound is explicit with respect to the grid size, the spline degree, and the local Korn constants. This means that if the patching of the domain is done well, the solution strategy will not suffer from geometry locking. The numerical experiments agree with our theoretical findings. Material locking, which occurs when
\lambda\gg\mu
, was not addressed in this paper. This will be addressed in a forthcoming paper.

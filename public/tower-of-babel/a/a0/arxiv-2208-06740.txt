Lipschitz decompositions of domains with bilaterally flat boundaries
Jared Krandel

Abstract
We study classes of domains in ℝ d + 1 , d ≥ 2 \mathbb{R}^{d+1},\ d\geq 2 with sufficiently flat boundaries that admit a decomposition or covering of bounded overlap by Lipschitz graph domains with controlled total surface area. This study is motivated by the following result proved by Peter Jones as a piece of his proof of the Analyst’s Traveling Salesman Theorem in the complex plane: Any simply connected domain Ω ⊆ ℂ \Omega\subseteq\mathbb{C} with finite boundary length ℋ 1 ​ ( ∂ Ω ) < ∞ \mathcal{H}^{1}(\partial\Omega)<\infty can be decomposed into Lipschitz graph domains with total boundary length at most M ​ ℋ 1 ​ ( ∂ Ω ) M\mathcal{H}^{1}(\partial\Omega) for some M > 0 M>0 independent of Ω \Omega . In this paper, we prove an analogous Lipschitz decomposition result in higher dimensions for domains with Reifenberg flat boundaries satisfying a uniform beta-squared sum bound. We use similar techniques to show that domains with general Reifenberg flat or uniformly rectifiable boundaries admit similar Lipschitz decompositions while allowing the constituent domains to have bounded overlaps rather than be disjoint.

Jones’s result is powerful, but it is confined to two dimensions. In this paper, we consider the following question:

Question 1.6
.

For
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d+1},\ d>1

One of the attractive features of Theorem

\partial\Omega
is bilaterally close to a
d

For

The full

b\beta^{d}_{E}(B)=\inf_{P\ \text{$d$-plane}}b\beta_{E}^{d}(B,P).

For fixed

Uniform rectifiability was studied in detail by David and Semmes in

]
where thea authors explore connections between the BWGL and numerous other equivalent definitions involving boundedness of singular integral operators, approximation by Lipschitz graphs (the existence of corona decompositions), “big piece” parameterizations by Lipschitz maps, and more. Uniform rectifiability has recently become of interest in the study of harmonic measure and the solvability of the homogeneous Dirichlet problem in rough domains. In

A related line of research studies the

[

was conceived independently from ours and is constructed using ideas more closely related to David and Semmes’s original work than the ideas used here involving Reifenberg parameterizations. We also mention that recent related work of Mourgoglu, Poggi, and Tolsa aims to extend the result of

[

In both of the above constructions, the authors use the corkscrew condition, making their setting slightly less general than that of Theorem

1.2
Outlines of the paper and of the proofs of the theorems

In Section
2
, we introduce the necessary notation and basic facts about Reifenberg parameterizations, Whitney decompositions, Christ-David cubes, coronizations, Reifenberg flat sets, and uniformly rectifiable sets. In Section
3
, we prove a number of preliminary results on decomposing “stopping time domains" of Whitney cubes into Lipschitz graph domains and mapping Lipschitz graph domains forward by small perturbations of affine maps. Section
4
contains further preliminary results on controlling the change in the derivative of a Reifenberg parameterization above geometrically nice regions. In Sections
5
and
6
we prove Theorems
A
and Theorems
B
and
C
respectively.

Roughly speaking, the proof of Theorem
A
in Section
5
proceeds as follows. The fact that
\partial\Omega
is Reifenberg flat means that we can produce a Reifenberg parameterization
g:\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
such that
g(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\{0\})\cap B(0,1)\supseteq\partial\Omega\cap B(0,1)
. The uniform bound on the beta-squared sum in condition
(ii)
of Theorem
A
ensures that
g
is
L^{\prime}(d,L)
-bi-Lipschitz so that
\partial\Omega
is in fact a bi-Lipschitz image, hence uniformly rectifiable. This means that there exists a Christ-David lattice
\mathscr{D}
for
\partial\Omega
with a graph coronization whose stopping time regions
\mathscr{F}=\{S\}
consist of cubes well-approximated by Lipschitz graphs with Lipschitz constant small in terms of
d
and
L^{\prime}
(this is a coronization that produces a corona decomposition). Proposition
4.8
implies that
Dg
is nearly constant on parts of its domain which are mapped into regions of
\Omega
sitting “above” a stopping time region
S
on the scale of the cubes inside
S
. By the results of Section
3
,
g
maps forward Lipschitz graph domains to Lipschitz graph domains when the change in
Dg
is small compared to the Lipschitz constants of the mapped domains. Therefore, to produce a Lipschitz decomposition of
\Omega\cap B(0,1)
, it suffices to produce a Lipschitz decomposition
\mathscr{L}_{0}
(see Definition
5.10
) of the domain of
g
into domains over which
Dg
is nearly constant so that the collection of images
\mathscr{L}=\{g(\mathcal{D}):\mathcal{D}\in\mathscr{L}_{0}\}
is a Lipschitz decomposition.

In order to form this decomposition, we produce a “coronization” of a lattice of Whitney boxes which parallels the coronization for
\mathscr{D}
on
\partial\Omega
(see
5.7
). That is, we separate Whitney boxes into bad boxes that
g
maps near bad cubes in
\mathscr{D}\cap\mathscr{B}
or cubes on the “edges” in scale and location of stopping time regions in
\mathscr{D}
. This decomposition then maps forward to a collection of domains whose total surface measure is bounded by the surface measure of
\partial\Omega
plus the Carleson packing sums for the bad and “edge” cubes of
\mathscr{D}
.

The proofs of Theorems
B
and
C
both follow a single similar argument to that of Theorem
A
. In the Reifenberg flat case, the difference is that any single global Reifenberg parameterization
g
produced for the set is not in general bi-Lipschitz, so we have no uniform estimates on how
g
distorts any given cube. In the uniformly rectifiable case, we have no global Reifenberg parameterization because there can be many scales and locations at which Reifenberg flatness fails. In either case, we sidestep these by producing a collection of local
(1+\delta)
-bi-Lipschitz parameterizations by parameterizing pieces of the domain above stopping time regions in a graph coronization (see Definition
2.14
) using single stopping time domains composed of Whitney cubes. By similar arguments, the surface measure of these domains is controlled by the surface measure of
\partial\Omega\cap B(0,1)
plus the Carleson packing sum of the same bad set of cubes in
\mathscr{D}\cap\mathscr{B}
and near “edges” of stopping time domains. We then fill parts of
\Omega\cap B(0,1)
that are missed by these domains with “buffer zones” of cubes on the exterior of these domains as well as families of cubes which sit above surface cubes in the bad set. By similar reasoning, the surface measure of these domains is bounded by the same Carleson packing sums as above.

Definition 2.1

(Hausdorff measure, Hausdorff distance, Nets)
.

For

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{}}{dist}(E,F)

\displaystyle=\inf\{|x-y|:x\in F,\ y\in E\},

\displaystyle=\mathop{\mathrm{}}{dist}(\{a\},E)

(2.1)

When

Given a subset

2.3
Coronizations for Reifenberg flat and uniformly rectifiable sets

The boundary measure bounds for our Lipschitz decompositions come from Carleson packing conditions for well-chosen
coronizations
of a
Christ-David lattice for

. A Christ-David lattice is best thought of as a family of intrinsic dyadic cubes for a doubling metric space. These were originally devised by Christ in
[

]
, but the formulation given here is due to Hytonen and Martikainen from
[
HM12
]
.

Theorem 2.9

(Christ-David cubes)
.

Let
X
be a doubling metric space. Let
X_{k}
be a nested sequence of maximal
\rho^{k}
-nets for
X
where
\rho<1/1000
and let

. For each
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
there is a collection
\mathscr{D}_{k}
of “cubes,” which are Borel subsets of

such that the following hold.

(i)

X=\bigcup_{Q\in\mathscr{D}_{k}}Q
,

(ii)

If
Q,Q^{\prime}\in\mathscr{D}=\bigcup\mathscr{D}_{k}
and
Q\cap Q^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
, then
Q\subseteq Q^{\prime}
or
Q^{\prime}\subseteq Q
,

(iii)

For
Q\in\mathscr{D}
, let
k(Q)
be the unique integer so that
Q\in\mathscr{D}_{k}
and set
\ell(Q)=5\rho^{k(Q)}
. There is
x_{Q}\in X_{k}
so that

B_{X}(x_{Q},c_{0}\ell(Q))\subseteq Q\subseteq B_{X}(x_{Q},\ell(Q))

and

X_{k}=\{x_{Q}:Q\in\mathscr{D}_{k}\}.

If in addition we assume
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
and that
X
is Ahlfors
d

(iv)

|Q|\simeq_{d}(\mathop{\mathrm{}}{diam}Q)^{d}\simeq_{d}\ell(Q)^{d}
.

For any
Q\in\mathscr{D}
, we will use the notation

to refer to the parent of
Q
. For any

, we also define

\lambda Q=\{x\in X:\mathop{\mathrm{}}{dist}(x,Q)\leq(\lambda-1)\mathop{\mathrm{}}{diam}(Q)\}.

We will now refer to any family of Christ-David cubes for
\partial\Omega
by
\mathscr{D}
and define

B_{Q}=B(x_{Q},\ell(Q)).

A coronization consists of a partition of

into “good” cubes

and “bad” cubes

and a further partition of

into disjoint

.

Definition 2.10

We call

if it is

. That is,

(i)

such that

for all

,

(ii)

, then

,

(iii)

If

Remark 2.11
.

We note that Definition

Definition 2.12

We say that a triple

We also record some important facts about using beta numbers to control the Hausdorff distance of planes. Given a set

for

and a collection of planes

in the second lemma below. First, we recall a general result that allows one to bound the Hausdorff distance between planes by beta numbers:

Lemma 2.18

(
[
AS18
]

.

Suppose

and

is a ball centered on

such that for all balls

\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\infty}(E\cap B^{\prime})\geq cr_{B^{\prime}}^{d}
. Let

and

The next lemma applies this to bound
\epsilon(Q)
by

on the class of
(c,d)
-lower content regular sets
.

Definition 2.19
.

We say that

is

-lower content regular
if for every

Lemma 2.20
.

Let
\mathscr{D}
be a Christ-David lattice for a
(c,d)
lower content regular set

and

be constants such that

\epsilon(Q)\lesssim_{\rho,M,d}\beta^{d,1}_{E}(MB_{Q}).

Proof.

Let

be cubes which achieve the supremum in the definition of

. Then

\epsilon(Q)=d_{KB_{R}}(P_{U},P_{R}).

We want to apply Lemma
2.18
with

KB_{R}\subseteq 2\rho^{-1}KB_{U}.

(2.26)

Indeed, we let

and we compute

\displaystyle|y-x_{U}|

\displaystyle\leq|y-x_{R}|+|x_{R}-x_{Q}|+|x_{Q}-x_{U}|

Second, we claim

where the second line follows from (

3
Preliminaries on Lipschitz graph domains

Our plan to produce Lipschitz graph domains is to produce a Reifenberg parameterization
g
from Theorem
2.4
, find a nice collection of Lipschitz graph domains in the domain of
g
on which
g
is a small perturbation of an affine map, then map these domains forward to Lipschitz graph domains in the image. In this section, we prove some preliminary geometric results that allow us to carry out the last two steps.

3.1
Whitney cubes and stopping time domains

In Section
5
, we will show how to decompose the domain of

into nice stopping time regions (Recall Definition

.) of a family of good
Whitney cubes
. We will make significant use of the standard Whitney decomposition of the upper half-space with respect to
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\{0\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d+1}

Definition 3.1

(Whitney cubes)
.

Define

\mathscr{W}=\left\{[k_{1}2^{-n},(k_{1}+1)2^{-n}]\times\cdots\times[k_{d}2^{-n},(k_{d}+1)2^{-n}]\times[2^{-n},2^{-n+1}]:k_{1},\ldots,k_{d},n\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}.

The collection

consists of exactly the dyadic cubes in

which satisfy

(3.1)

where
\ell(W)=h(W)
denote the
side length
of
W
and the
height
of
W
. Cubes
W,R\in\mathscr{W}
have a natural partial order induced by distance to
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\{0\}
. We define the projection
\pi:\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\{0\}
by
\pi(x,y)=x
where
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}
and write

W\leq R

if and only if
\pi(W)\subset\pi(R)
. If
W\leq R
and
h(W)=\frac{1}{2}h(R)
, we call
W
a
child
of
R
and
R
a
parent
of
W
. This gives a partial order on
\mathscr{W}
which we use to define the
descendants
of
W

D(W)=\{R\in\mathscr{W}:R\leq W\}.

(3.2)

The partial order on Whitney cubes gives a natural tree structure on
\mathscr{W}
which we will use in stopping time constructions.

Definition 3.2

(Stopping time regions and domains of Whitney cubes)
.

The partial order
\leq
and definition of child cubes in Definition
3.1
give a well-defined notion of
stopping time region
for subsets of
\mathscr{W}
. We say that
T\subseteq\mathscr{W}
is a stopping time region if it satisfies natural analogs of the three coherency conditions in Definition
2.10
. That is, the following hold:

(i)

There exists a
top cube

W(T)\in T
such that
R\subseteq W(T)
for all
R\in T
,

(ii)

If
R\in T
and
U\in\mathscr{D}
satisfies
R\subseteq U\subseteq W(T)
, then
U\in T
,

(iii)

If
R\in T
, then either every child of
R
is in
T
, or none of them are.

Given a stopping time region
T\subseteq\mathscr{W}
, we can form an associated
stopping time domain

\mathcal{D}_{T}
by taking the union of cubes in
T
, defining

\mathcal{D}_{T}=\bigcup_{W\in T}W.

(3.3)

We also define the set of
minimal cubes
in
T
to be

m(T)=\{W\in T:\text{ If $R\in T$ such that $R\leq W$, then $R=W$}\}.

(3.4)

This is the set of cubes that have no children inside
T
.

Observe that stopping time domains are not necessarily Lipschitz graph domains because their boundaries contain the faces of cubes, and any face whose normal vector is perpendicular to the vector between the center of the face and a choice of center point for the domain will not have a well-defined radius function in Definition
1.2
. Therefore, we need a process to carve stopping time domains into Lipschitz graph domains with uniform constant in a controlled way.

Fix some stopping time region
T\subseteq\mathscr{W}
. In the rest of this section, we will construct an Ahlfors
d
-regular union of subsets of
d
-planes
\Sigma_{T}
that carves
\mathcal{D}_{T}
into a collection of
c(d)
-Lipschitz graph domains. The images of these nicer domains under a Reifenberg parameterization whose derivative is nearly constant on the domain will then map them forward to Lipschitz graph domains as desired in the conclusions of Theorems
A
,
B
, and
C
.

Carving up stopping time domains

.

There exists a constant

3.3
Images of Lipschitz graph domains

We now show that the Lipschitz graph domain property is preserved under images of maps whose derivatives are nearly constant. We begin by observing that linear transformations preserve Lipschitz graph domains

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
be an
L_{0}
-Lipschitz graph domain and let
A:\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
be an
L^{\prime}
-bi-Lipschitz affine map. Then there exists a constant
L_{1}(L_{0},L^{\prime})
such that
A(\Omega)
is an
L_{1}
-Lipschitz graph domain.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, assume
A(0)=0
and set
\Omega^{\prime}=A(\Omega)
. Then since
\Omega=\{t\theta:0\leq t\leq r(\theta),\ \theta\in\mathbb{S}^{d}\}
, we know that
\Omega^{\prime}=\{tA(\theta):0\leq t\leq r(\theta),\ \theta\in\mathbb{S}^{d}\}
so that
\Omega^{\prime}
is star-shaped and
r_{\Omega^{\prime}}
is well-defined. We have

\partial\Omega^{\prime}=A(\partial\Omega)=\{A(r(\theta)\theta)=r(\theta)A(\theta):\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{d}\}.

Therefore, given
\psi\in\mathbb{S}^{d}
, we see that

r_{\Omega^{\prime}}(\psi)=r_{\Omega}\left(\frac{A^{-1}(\psi)}{|A^{-1}(\psi)|}\right)\frac{1}{|A^{-1}(\psi)|}.

Because
A^{-1}
is
L^{\prime}
-bi-Lipschitz and
r_{\Omega}
is
L_{0}
-Lipschitz on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
,
r_{\Omega^{\prime}}
is composed of products and compositions of bounded Lipschitz functions and it follows that there exists
L_{1}(L_{0},L^{\prime})
such that
r_{\Omega^{\prime}}
satisfies the requirements of Definition

where the deviations described are on the order of
\epsilon
. This implies
|\langle Dg(x,y)\cdot v_{x},Dg(x,y)\cdot v_{y}\rangle|\leq\frac{1}{2}|Dg(x,y)\cdot v_{x}|\cdot|Dg(x,y)\cdot v_{y}|
so that the lemma applies. With this, we now compute,

\displaystyle|Dg(z^{\prime})\cdot Dg(z)^{-1}\cdot v^{\prime}-v^{\prime}|=|[Dg(z^{\prime})-Dg(z)]\cdot Dg(z)^{-1}\cdot v^{\prime}|

\displaystyle\leq C\delta|Dg(z)\cdot Dg(z)^{-1}\cdot v^{\prime}|

\displaystyle=C\delta|v^{\prime}|.\qed

This concludes the computations we need to bound the change in
Dg
. By integrating
Dg
over paths in a quasiconvex domain
\Omega
, we get a companion result to Proposition
4.8
that roughly states that the map
g|_{\Omega}
is a
(1+C\delta)
-bi-Lipschitz perturbation of
Dg(z_{0})
for any
z_{0}\in\Omega
. More precisely, for any
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
define

L_{z_{0}}(z)=z_{0}+Dg(z_{0})(z-z_{0}).

(4.37)

This is the affine transformation that approximates
g
near
z_{0}
. Define

\varphi_{z_{0}}=g\circ L_{z_{0}}^{-1}

(4.38)

Proposition 4.13
.

Let
z_{0},M_{0},\epsilon
be as in Proposition
4.8
where
z=z_{0}
. Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
be a quasiconvex domain with constant
M_{0}
such that
z_{0}\in\Omega\subseteq G_{z_{0}}^{M_{0}}
. Then the map
\varphi_{z_{0}}:L_{z_{0}}(\Omega)\rightarrow g(\Omega)
is
(1+C\delta)
-bi-Lipschitz and

|D\varphi_{z_{0}}(w)-I|\leq C\delta

(4.39)

for all
w\in L_{z_{0}}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

Because
w\in L_{z_{0}}(G_{z_{0}}^{M_{0}})
by assumption, we get

D\varphi_{z_{0}}(w)=Dg(L_{z_{0}}^{-1}(w))\cdot DL_{z_{0}}^{-1}(w)=Dg(z)\cdot Dg(z_{0})^{-1}

for
z=L_{z_{0}}^{-1}(w)\in G_{z_{0}}^{M_{0}}
. Equation (
4.39
) follows from (
4.8
).

To prove that
\varphi_{z_{0}}
is
(1+C\delta)
-bi-Lipschitz, let
\gamma:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
be a path with
\gamma(0)=z_{0},\ \gamma(1)=z
, and
\ell(\gamma)\lesssim_{M_{0}}|z_{0}-z|
. Put
\tilde{\gamma}(t)=L_{z_{0}}(\gamma(t))
,
w=L_{z_{0}}(z)
, and
w_{0}=L_{z_{0}}(z_{0})=z_{0}
. Observe that

L_{z_{0}}^{-1}(w)=z_{0}+Dg(z_{0})^{-1}(w-z_{0}).

We estimate

\displaystyle|\varphi_{z_{0}}(w)-\varphi_{z_{0}}(w_{0})|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{0}^{1}D(g\circ L_{z_{0}}^{-1})(\tilde{\gamma}(t))\cdot\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}(t)dt\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{0}^{1}Dg(\gamma(t))\cdot DL_{z_{0}}^{-1}(\tilde{\gamma}(t))\cdot\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}(t)dt\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{0}^{1}Dg(\gamma(t))\cdot Dg(z_{0})^{-1}\cdot\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}(t)dt\right|

\displaystyle=\left|w-w_{0}+\int_{0}^{1}\left[Dg(\gamma(t))\cdot Dg(z_{0})^{-1}-I\right]\cdot\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}(t)dt\right|.

Using the fact that
\gamma(t)\in G_{z_{0}}^{M_{0}}
for all
t
, Proposition
4.8
implies, on one hand

\displaystyle|\varphi_{z_{0}}(w)-\varphi_{z_{0}}(w_{0})|

\displaystyle\leq|w-w_{0}|+\int_{0}^{1}\left|Dg(\gamma(t))\cdot Dg(z_{0})^{-1}-I\right|\cdot|\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}(t)|dt

\displaystyle\leq|w-w_{0}|+C\delta|Dg(z_{0})|\cdot\ell(\gamma)

\displaystyle\leq(1+C\delta)|w-w_{0}|.

On the other,

\displaystyle|\varphi_{z_{0}}(w)-\varphi_{z_{0}}(w_{0})|

\displaystyle\geq|w-w_{0}|-\int_{0}^{1}\left|Dg(\gamma(t))\cdot Dg(z_{0})^{-1}-I\right|\cdot|\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}(t)|dt

\displaystyle\geq|w-w_{0}|-C\delta|Dg(z_{0})|\cdot\ell(\gamma)

\displaystyle\geq(1-C\delta)|w-w_{0}|

where the final inequality on both hands comes from the fact that
|w^{\prime}-w|=|Dg(z)\cdot(z^{\prime}-z)|\leq|Dg(z)|\cdot|z-z^{\prime}|
and our assumption that
\ell(\gamma)\lesssim_{M_{0}}|z-z^{\prime}|
.
∎

Definition 5.4

(Whitney boxes)
.

We define the set of

These are like Whitney cubes, but they have lengths along the first

coordinate directions contracted by a factor of

\ell(R)=2^{-p-n}

and

\ell(R)=2^{-p}h(R).

\mathscr{R}=\cup_{p}\mathscr{R}_{p}
.

Now, we define

.

\displaystyle|g(x,y)-g(x,y^{\prime})|=\left|\sum_{k\geq 0}\rho_{k}(y)\{f_{k}(x)+R_{k}(x)(y)\}-\rho_{k}(y^{\prime})\{f_{k}(x)+R_{k}(x)(y^{\prime})\}\right|.

Further questions

Question 7.1
.

Can one find a disjoint decomposition rather than one of bounded overlap in Theorems
B
and
C
?

In the construction given in this paper for Theorems
B
and
C
, the only overlap between domains occurs in intersections between
(1+C\delta)
-bi-Lipschitz images of Whitney cubes of comparable side length inside the “buffer zones”. It seems plausible that one could devise a different scheme to divide the space between the disjoint “core” stopping time domains into Lipschitz graph domains.

Similarly, it seems plausible that one could obtain a similar result by modifying the methods of the proof to prove a version of Theorem
A
with assumption
(ii)
removed (see Remark
5.8
).

Question 7.2
.

Can Theorem
B
be extended to general lower-content
d
regular sets?

It seems possible that the necessary tools to handle this extension to non-Reifenberg flat sets are present in the
\beta
-number estimates in
[
AS18
]
and
[
Hyd22b
]
. The technical disconnect between this paper and those ones is caused by the “smoothing” procedure needed there in defining the stopping time regions makes this generalization non-obvious to the author. It seems that a proof would require new ideas.

Appendix A
Graph coronizations for Reifenberg flat sets

The goal of this section is to provide a proof of Proposition
2.17
which states that there exist (sufficiently small in terms of
d
) constants
\epsilon,\delta>0
such that Reifenberg flat sets admit
(M,\epsilon,\delta)
-graph coronizations.

Reifenberg flat sets are a subset of a more general class of sets called
lower content
d
-regular sets
studied by Azzam and Schul
[
AS18
]
and later Hyde
[
Hyd22a
]
as a class of objects for
d
-dimensional traveling salesman results.

Definition A.1

(lower content
d
-regularity)
.

A set
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
is said to be
lower content
d
-regular
in a ball
B(x,r)
if there exists a constant
c>0
and
r_{B}>0
such that

\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\infty}(E\cap B(x,r))\geq cr^{d}\ \text{for all $x\in E\cap B$ and $r\in(0,r_{B})$}.

A set
E
is
lower content
d
-regular
if there exists a constant
c
such that
E
is lower content regular with constant
c
in every ball centered on
E
.

Since a Reifenberg flat set
\Sigma
satisfies
b\beta_{\Sigma}(B)\leq\epsilon
in every ball by definition, the only remaining requirements for the existence of a graph coronization are control over
\beta^{d,1}_{\Sigma}
-squared sums and control over the frequency of angle turning of well-approximating planes. The necessary control over
\beta
-sums is contained in the following traveling salesman theorems formulated for general lower content regular sets:

Theorem A.2

(
[
Hyd22a
]
Theorem 1.6)
.

Let
H
be a Hilbert space and
1\leq d<\dim(H),\ 1\leq p<p(d),\ C_{0}>1,\
and
A>10^{5}
. Let
E\subseteq H
be a lower content
d
-regular set with regularity constant
c
and Christ-David cubes
\mathscr{D}
. There exists
\epsilon>0
small enough so that the following holds. Let
Q_{0}\in\mathscr{D}
and

\beta_{E,C_{0},d,p}(Q_{0})=\ell(Q_{0})^{d}+\sum_{Q\subseteq Q_{0}}\beta_{E}^{d,p}(C_{0}B_{Q})^{2}\ell(Q)^{d}.

Then

\beta_{E,C_{0},d,p}(Q_{0})\lesssim_{A,d,c,p,C_{0},\epsilon}\mathcal{H}^{d}(Q_{0})+\mathop{\mathrm{}}{BWGL}(Q_{0},A,\epsilon).

(A.1)

Theorem A.3

(
[
Hyd22a
]
Theorem 1.7)
.

Let
H
be a Hilbert space,
1\leq d<\dim(H),\ 1\leq p<\infty,\ A>1,\ \epsilon>0,
and
C_{0}>2\rho^{-1}
where
\rho
is as in the construction of the Christ-David lattice
\mathscr{D}
. Let
E\subseteq H
be lower content
d
-regular with regularity constant
c
and Christ-David cubes
\mathscr{D}
. For
Q_{0}\in\mathscr{D}
, we have

\mathcal{H}^{d}(Q_{0})+\mathop{\mathrm{}}{BWGL}(Q_{0},A,\epsilon)\lesssim_{A,d,c,C_{0},\epsilon}\beta_{E,C_{0},d,p}(Q_{0}).

If
E
is
(\epsilon,d)
-Reifenberg flat, then the
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{BWGL}
terms above vanish and (
A.1
) gives a Carleson packing condition for the content beta number sum reminiscent of the strong geometric lemma for uniformly rectifiable sets from which we will conclude the desired
\beta^{2}
sum control.

We will require small technical tweaks of the stopping time machinery of Azzam and Schul on Reifenberg flat sets. We review the necessary definitions here, but refer to
[
AS18
]

Definition A.4

(
d
-dimensional traveling salesman stopping time)
.

We fix constants
0<\epsilon\ll\alpha^{4}
with
\alpha(d),\epsilon(d)
to be chosen sufficiently small in terms of
\delta
as required in
[
AS18
]
. For any cube
Q\in\mathscr{D}
, we define a stopping time region
S_{Q}^{\alpha}
by adding cubes
R\subseteq Q
to

if

(i)

R^{(1)}\in S_{Q}^{\alpha}
,

(ii)

\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Angle}(P_{U},P_{Q})<\alpha
for any sibling
U
of
R
(including
R
itself).

For any collection of cubes
\mathscr{Q}
, define a distance function

d_{\mathscr{Q}}(x)=\inf\{\ell(Q)+\mathop{\mathrm{}}{dist}(x,Q):Q\in\mathscr{Q}\}.

For any
Q\in\mathscr{D}
, define

d_{\mathscr{Q}}(Q)=\inf_{x\in Q}d_{\mathscr{Q}}(x)=\inf\{\ell(R)+\mathop{\mathrm{}}{dist}(Q,R):R\in\mathscr{Q}\}.

We let
m(S)
be the set of minimal cubes of
S
, those which have no children contained in
S
and define

z(S)=Q(S)\setminus\bigcup_{Q\in m(S)}Q.

Let

\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Stop}(-1)=\mathscr{D}_{0}

and fix a small constant
\tau\in(0,1)
. Suppose we have defined
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Stop}(N-1)
for some integer
N\geq 0
and define

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Layer}(N)

\displaystyle=\bigcup\{S_{Q}^{\alpha}:Q\in\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Stop}(N-1)\}.

We then set
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Up}(-1)=\varnothing
and put

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Stop}(N)

\displaystyle=\{Q\in\mathscr{D}:\text{$Q$ maximal such that $Q$ has a sibling $Q^{\prime}$ with $\ell(Q^{\prime})<\tau d_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Layer}(N)}(Q^{\prime})$}\},

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Up}(N)

\displaystyle=\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Up}(N-1)\cup\{Q\in\mathscr{D}:Q\supset R\text{ for some $R\in\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Stop}(N)\cup\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Layer}(N)$}\}

[
AS18
]
Lemma 5.5 says that, in fact

\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Up}(N)=\{Q\in\mathscr{D}:Q\not\subset R\text{ for any $R\in\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Stop}(N)$}\}.

Essentially,
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Layer}(N)
is a layer of stopping time regions
S_{Q}^{\alpha}
beginning at the stopped cubes of the previous generation and continuing until reaching a cube
R
with a child
R^{\prime}
such that
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Angle}(P_{Q},P_{R^{\prime}})>\alpha

\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Stop}(N)

\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Stop}(N)
are of similar size. One forms a CCBP from the centers and

-minimizing planes of cubes in
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Up}(N)
which gives a surface
\Sigma_{N}
for any

which converges to
\Sigma
as

. Azzam and Schul give tools for proving bounds on the degree of stopping in this construction in the following lemma

(

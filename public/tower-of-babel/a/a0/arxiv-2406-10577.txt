Aspects of a novel nonlinear electrodynamics in flat spacetime and in a gravity-coupled scenario
Anjan Kar

Abstract
A novel nonlinear electrodynamics (NLE) model with two dimensionful parameters is introduced and investigated. Our model obeys the Maxwellian limit and exhibits behaviour similar to the Born-Infeld Lagrangian in the weak field limit. It is shown that the electric field of a point charge in this model has a definite maximum value. Thus, the self-energy of the point charge is finite. The phenomenon of vacuum birefringence is found to occur in the presence of an external uniform electric field. Causality and unitarity conditions for all background electric fields hold, whereas, for magnetic fields, a restricted domain of validity is found. Moreover, a minimal coupling of Einstein’s General Relativity (GR) with this NLE results in solutions of regular black holes or naked singularities, depending on whether the source is a nonlinear magnetic monopole or an electric charge, respectively.

Introduction

In Maxwell’s electrodynamics, the electric field and self-energy of a point charge exhibit a divergence as the radial distance approaches zero. One of the well-known procedures to address this issue involves modifying Maxwell’s linear Lagrangian in a nonlinear manner. In the 1930s, Born and Infeld first developed their nonlinear model, largely known as Born-Infeld (BI) electrodynamics

As a result, the energy of a point electric charge at its location becomes finite. Subsequently, several other exciting nonlinear modifications of Maxwell’s Lagrangian have emerged in the literature, collectively referred to as ‘nonlinear electrodynamics’ (NLE). For example, the effective Euler-Heisenberg Lagrangian

We also know that singularities are abundant in solutions in Einstein’s General Relativity (GR). These can be identified as causing a divergence of curvature scalars or incompleteness of causal geodesics in certain regions of spacetime. The first black hole solution, the Schwarzschild solution, shows a divergence or breakdown of geodesics when the radial coordinate becomes zero. Further, the Hawking-Penrose singularity theorems

Instead, they represent the limitations of classical GR. It is essential to tackle this problem in order to comprehend the ultimate result of gravitational collapse of initially stable structures.

There is widespread anticipation that quantum extensions of classical theories would remove or resolve the singularities present in those theories

One possible direction is to invoke ‘quantum gravity’, the quantum theory of classical GR, which is still in a state of ‘progress’. Second, one can modify Einstein’s GR (by deforming the Einstein-Hilbert action) and look for a divergence-free solution in that specific theory of gravity. An alternative method involves exploring various nonlinear generalisations of Einstein-Maxwell theory. Thus, it is expected that the problem of spacetime singularities may be resolved by minimal coupling of a suitable NLE Lagrangian with gravity

II
Nonlinear electrodynamics model and field equations

This section introduces our general nonlinear Lagrangian and imposes restrictions on the model parameters to ensure its viability. In this work, we choose some feasible values for model parameters to continue our further analysis. We examine the nonlinear Maxwell-like equations in flat spacetime for the specific Lagrangian (having particular values of model parameters), focusing on their electrostatic limit. The variation of the electric field with radial distance, due to a point charge, is demonstrated analytically via a power series (in the weak field limit), as well as graphically.

Let us begin with Maxwell’s Lagrangian, which is directly proportional to
F=\frac{1}{2}(\textbf{B}^{2}-\textbf{E}^{2})
, i.e.,

L_{Maxwell}=-F

(1)

As mentioned earlier, the BI model is defined by the following Lagrangian
[
1
,
2
]
.

L_{BI}=\frac{2}{\beta}(1-\sqrt{1+\beta F})

(2)

where
\beta
is an arbitrary constant. In the weak field limit
(\beta F<<1)
, the Lagrangian in Eq.(
2
) becomes,

L_{BI}\approx-F+\frac{1}{4}\beta F^{2}-\frac{1}{8}\beta^{2}F^{3}+O(\beta^{3}F^{4})

(3)

Therefore, it is evident that as
\beta\to 0
, we have the linear Maxwell Lagrangian. Let us now consider a more general Lagrangian, which defines our novel nonlinear electrodynamics model,

L_{general}(F)=-\frac{(aF+1)^{m}}{\delta(bF+1)^{n}}(\beta F)^{p}

(4)

where
m
,
n
and
p
are dimensionless constants, and
a
,
b
,
\beta
,
\delta
are arbitrary parameters with dimensions of squre length. Further, in the weak field limit, Eq.(
4
) has the following form,

L_{general}(F)\approx-c\left(F^{p}+c_{1}F^{p+1}+c_{2}F^{p+2}+O(c_{3}F^{p+3})\right)

(5)

The expression above indicates that the Maxwellian limit can be obtained when
p=1
. Since a viable matter must follow the Maxwellian limit, we are bound to obey this restriction. It will be shown in Section
V
that the background electric field due to a point charge holds causality if
n\geq(m+1)
,
a\leq 0
and
b\geq 0
. We choose the extreme condition
n=(m+1)
, particularly
m=1
, to continue our analysis. Moreover, in this study, we pick
a=-3b
, which represents certain unique features when coupled to Einstein’s GR, as discussed in Section
VI
. Hence, our chosen two-parameter Lagrangian, which obeys the Maxwellian limit, takes the following form,

L(F)=\frac{\gamma\left(3\eta F-1\right)F}{\left(1+\eta F\right)^{2}}

(6)

where
\gamma

(=\beta/\delta)
and
\eta
are arbitrary parameters. For
\eta F<<1
, i.e. for a weak field, the above Lagrangian becomes,

L(F)\approx-\gamma F+5\gamma\eta F^{2}-9\gamma\eta^{2}F^{3}+\gamma O(\eta^{3}F^{4})

(7)

As expected, the BI Lagrangian (
2
) as well as the new NLE model (
6
) (
\gamma=1
) are schematically identical in the weak field limit (in the two series expansion (
3
), (
7
), all powers of
F
are equal.). Additionally, by construction, both reduce to the standard Maxwellian theory (
1
) when the nonlinear parameter vanishes. In Figure
1
, we provide a graphical analysis that compares the Lagrangian in Eq.(
6
) with the linear model. It is apparent that for small values of
\eta
, the new NLE model exhibits nearly linear behaviour, replicating the limit of the BI model. However, the deviation from linearity becomes significant for higher values of
\eta
.

Next, the Euler-Lagrange equation of motion of the Lagrangian in Eq.(
6
), in flat spacetime, can be written as,

\partial_{\mu}(L_{F}F^{\mu\nu})=0

(8)

where,

L_{F}=\frac{\partial L}{\partial F}=\frac{\gamma(-1+7\eta F)}{\left(1+\eta F\right)^{3}}

(9)

and
F_{\mu\nu}
is the Maxwell field strength tensor. Now, by using the relation for the electric displacement field
\textbf{D}=\partial L/\partial\textbf{E}
, one has, from Eq.(
6
),

\textbf{D}=\gamma\frac{1-7\eta F}{(1+\eta F)^{3}}\textbf{E}

(10)

where
E
is the electric field. Eq.(
10
) can be expressed in tensor form as
\textbf{D}_{i}=\epsilon_{i}{}^{j}\textbf{E}_{j}
, where
\epsilon_{ij}
represents the electric permittivity tensor given below,

\epsilon_{ij}=\gamma\frac{1-7\eta F}{\left(1+\eta F\right)^{3}}\delta_{ij}

(11)

Similarly, the magnetic field
\textbf{H}=-\partial L/\partial\textbf{B}
for the same Lagrangian (
6
) can be expressed in the following form,

\textbf{H}=\gamma\frac{1-7\eta F}{(1+\eta F)^{3}}\textbf{B}

(12)

Now the magnetic induction field is
\textbf{B}_{i}=\mu_{i}{}^{j}\textbf{H}_{j}
, therefore the inverse of magnetic permeability tensor
(\mu^{-1})_{ij}
becomes,

(\mu^{-1})_{ij}=\gamma\frac{1-7\eta F}{\left(1+\eta F\right)^{3}}\delta_{ij}

(13)

Further, the Euler-Lagrange equation of motion (
8
) can be reformulated as the first set of source-free Maxwell’s equations using Eqs. (
10
) and (
12
),

\nabla\cdot\textbf{D}=0,\hskip 20.0pt\frac{\partial\textbf{D}}{\partial t}-\nabla\times\textbf{H}=0

(14)

And, from the Bianchi identity
\partial_{\mu}\tilde{F}^{\mu\nu}=0
, we have the other set,

\nabla\cdot\textbf{B}=0,\hskip 20.0pt\frac{\partial\textbf{B}}{\partial t}+\nabla\times\textbf{E}=0

(15)

where
\tilde{F}^{\mu\nu}
is the dual of the field strength tensor. It is apparent that the Maxwell’s equations (
14
) and (
15
) incorporate the electric permittivity tensor
\epsilon_{ij}
and the magnetic permeability tensor
\mu_{ij}
, both of which depend on the Maxwell invariant
F
. This means that the nonlinearity of the Lagrangian (
6
) is encoded via an ‘anisotropic’ medium with specific properties, as evident from Eqs. (
11
) and (
13
). It is important to note that
\textbf{D}\cdot\textbf{H}\neq\textbf{E}\cdot\textbf{B}
, indicating a violation of duality symmetry

. Setting the values of
\eta

\gamma

[

Electrostatic limit:

Let us examine the field equations of electrostatics when both the magnetic induction (
B
) and the magnetic field intensity (
H
) are equal to zero. The equation for a point charge can be expressed as,

\nabla\cdot\textbf{D}=e\delta(r)

(16)

where
e
is the electric charge. The solution of the above equation can be written as,

\textbf{D}=\frac{e}{4\pi r^{3}}\textbf{r}

(17)

With the help of Eq.(
10
), the above equation becomes,

E+\frac{7}{2}\eta E^{3}=\frac{e}{4\gamma\pi r^{2}}\left(1-\frac{\eta}{2}E^{2}\right)^{3}

(18)

where we have substituted
F=-E^{2}/2
and
E
is the magnitude of electric field
E
. As the radial coordinate
r\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, the above equation indicates a restriction on the domain of
F
, i.e.
F
is larger than
-\frac{1}{\eta}
. However, there is no such restriction on
F
for the purely magnetic case. Therefore, in general,
F
can vary between
-\frac{1}{\eta}
to
\infty
. One may note that in the Maxwellian limit, the boundary of
F
extends to
(-\infty,+\infty)
as
\eta\to 0
, which is expected.

Let us try to obtain an analytical solution of
E(r)
from the above polynomial equation (
18
). This is challenging for an arbitrary value of
\eta
. However, in the weak field limit
(\eta F<<1)
, one can express
E(r)
in a perturbative series in
\eta
. In this limit, the total electric field
E(r)
can be written as,

E=E_{(0)}+\eta E_{(1)}+\eta^{2}E_{(2)}+O(\eta^{3})

(19)

where
E_{(1)}
and
E_{(2)}
represent the first and second-order corrections to the electric field
E_{(0)}
, respectively. Although there are higher-order corrections, their contribution to the total electric field is negligible. Here,
E_{(0)}
,
E_{(1)}
, and
E_{(2)}
have the units of length inverse, the cube of length inverse, and the fifth power of length inverse, respectively. One can substitute the above expansion of
E(r)
into Eq.(
18
). And, by comparing the resultant expressions at different orders of
\eta
, we have,

Zeroth-order,

E_{(0)}=\frac{e}{4\pi\gamma r^{2}}

(20)

First-order,

E_{(1)}=-\frac{7}{2}E_{(0)}^{3}-\frac{e}{4\pi\gamma r^{2}}\frac{3}{2}E_{(0)}^{2}

(21)

Second-order,

E_{(2)}=-\frac{21}{2}E_{(0)}^{2}E_{(1)}+\frac{e}{4\pi\gamma r^{2}}\left(-3E_{(0)}E_{(1)}+\frac{3}{4}E_{(0)}^{4}\right)

(22)

Solving the above three equations, one can obtain the correction terms to the electric field
E_{(0)}
. As a result, we are led to the following expression for the total electric field of a point charge (in the weak field limit),

E\approx\frac{e}{4\pi\gamma r^{2}}-5\eta\left(\frac{e}{4\pi\gamma r^{2}}\right)^{3}+\frac{273}{4}\eta^{2}\left(\frac{e}{4\pi\gamma r^{2}}\right)^{5}+O(\eta^{3})

(23)

One may note that the zeroth order contribution is identical to that for Maxwell’s electrodynamics. Although from Eq.(
23
), the finiteness of the electric field is still unclear, one can get an analytical understanding of the electric field caused by a point charge. However, Eq.(
18
) indicates the following maximum value of electric field
E(r)
for small values of
r
and any arbitrary value of
\eta
,

E_{max}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\eta}}

(24)

Therefore, the maximum electric field of a point charge has a finite value in our model, similar to BI electrodynamics. Unlike linear electrodynamics, whose electric field strength exhibits a singularity leading to an unlimited amount of self-energy associated with a point charge, our new NLE model eliminates the Maxwellian singularity. In Figure

, we present the variation of the electric field by plotting Eq.(

\eta
(not necessarily in the weak field) to demonstrate its finiteness. One may note that for small values of
\eta
, the maximum value of the electric field increases, and as
\eta\to 0
, it will eventually diverge.

III
Energy of a point charge

We now turn to the total electric energy of the point charge. It is known that energy density in a classical field theory can be obtained by taking the negative of the
T^{t}_{t}
component of the energy-momentum tensor
T_{\mu}^{\nu}
. In the literature, there are several inequivalent definitions of stress-energy tensors in Minkowski spacetime, such as the canonical electromagnetic stress-energy tensor
[
67
]
, the Belinfante–Rosenfeld stress-energy tensor
[
68
]
, and the Hilbert stress-energy tensor
[
67
]
. We consider the Hilbert stress-energy tensor in flat spacetime, which is both symmetric and gauge invariant. It is defined as,

T_{\mu\nu}^{H}=-\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\left.\left(\frac{\partial\left(\sqrt{-g}L(F)\right)}{\partial g^{\mu\nu}}\right)\right|_{g=\eta}

(25)

where we have derived the stress-energy tensor in a general ‘curved spacetime’ defined by the metric tensor
g_{\mu\nu}
. Thus, the Hilbert stress-energy tensor corresponding to the Lagrangian in Eq.(
6
) can be written as,

T_{\mu\nu}^{H}=\eta_{\mu\nu}L(F)-L_{F}F_{\mu}^{\alpha}F_{\nu\alpha}

(26)

where
L_{F}
is defined in Eq.(
9
). Consequently, the electric energy density becomes,

\rho=-T_{t}^{t}=-L_{F}E^{2}-L(F)=\frac{\gamma E^{2}\left(1+\frac{3}{2}\eta E^{2}\left(4+\frac{\eta}{2}E^{2}\right)\right)}{2\left(1-\frac{\eta}{2}E^{2}\right)^{3}}

(27)

The total electric energy is given by

\mathbf{\epsilon}=4\pi\int_{0}^{\infty}\rho(r)r^{2}dr

(28)

Using Eq.(
18
), it is possible to transform the above integral over ‘
r
’ into an integral over the variable ‘
E
’. Following this transformation, the above integral becomes,

\mathbf{\epsilon}=\frac{e^{3/2}}{\sqrt{4\pi\gamma}}\int_{0}^{\sqrt{\frac{2}{\eta}}}\frac{\sqrt{(2-\eta E^{2})}\left(4+3\eta E^{2}(8+\eta E^{2})\right)\left(4+\eta E^{2}(52+21\eta E^{2})\right)}{16\sqrt{E}(2+7\eta E^{2})^{5/2}}dE

(29)

One can numerically solve the above integral for different values of
\eta
. This gives us the self-energy of a point charge, which depends on the electric charge
e
as well as the model parameters
\gamma
and
\eta
. In Figure
3
, the variation of the new variable
\bar{\mathbf{\epsilon}}\equiv\mathbf{\epsilon}\sqrt{4\pi\gamma}/e^{3/2}
, which is the scaled version of total energy
\epsilon
, with
\eta
is presented. It is evident that for an arbitrary value of
\eta
, the energy of a point charge is finite. And in the Maxwell limit (
\eta\to 0
), the integral in Eq.(
29
) becomes,

\mathbf{\epsilon}\approx\frac{e^{3/2}}{\sqrt{4\pi\gamma}}\left[\frac{\sqrt{E}}{2}\right]_{0}^{\infty}

(30)

which shows an infinite amount of energy (as expected). Thus, the electrostatic energy of a point charge is finite in our model (
\eta\neq 0
), like it is in the well-known BI model.

IV
Vacuum birefringence

In the realm of nonlinear electrodynamics (NLE), it is well recognised that photons do not travel along null geodesics of the background Minkowski geometry. Instead, they follow a different trajectory in an ‘effective geometry’
[
69
,
70
]
. The effective metric depends on the dynamics of the background electromagnetic field. It is possible to describe the null geodesics in the effective geometry as wave propagation, governed by Maxwellian electrodynamics, in a nonlinear dielectric medium
[
70
]
. Therefore, the nonlinearity in the Lagrangian has a
medium interpretation
, mentioned previously. Light may be considered as electromagnetic waves moving through a classical dispersive medium. Hence, several phenomena usually associated with a dielectric medium will occur even in vacuum in NLE.

The phenomenon of vacuum birefringence in quantum electrodynamics (QED) was examined in references
[
71
,
72
]
. Recently, several studies have been conducted on causal structure, birefringence and light propagation in a general nonlinear electrodynamics Lagrangian
[
73
,
74
,
75
,
76
,
77
]
. Further, vacuum birefringence in BI electrodynamics has been discussed in
[
78
,
79
]
. Let us now investigate this effect in our NLE model. We consider the propagation of plane electromagnetic waves
(\textbf{e},\textbf{b})
travelling in the
z
direction between two plates of a capacitor having a constant and uniform electric field along the
x
direction, i.e. the external field
\bar{\textbf{E}}=(\bar{E},0,0)
. The total electromagnetic field is the sum of the light field
(\textbf{e},\textbf{b})
and the background electric field, i.e.
\textbf{E}=\textbf{e}+\bar{\textbf{E}}
,
\textbf{B}=\textbf{b}
. We assume that the light fields are much weaker than the background electric field. Thus, the Lagrangian in Eq.(
6
) becomes:

L(\textbf{e}+\bar{\textbf{E}},\textbf{b})=\gamma\frac{\Big\{\frac{3}{2}\eta(\textbf{b}^{2}-(\textbf{e}+\bar{\textbf{E}})^{2})-1\Big\}(\textbf{b}^{2}-(\textbf{e}+\bar{\textbf{E}})^{2})}{2\big\{1+\frac{\eta}{2}(\textbf{b}^{2}-(\textbf{e}+\bar{\textbf{E}})^{2})\big\}^{2}}

(31)

However, neglecting the higher orders of the light field in the above Lagrangian, we are left only with quadratic terms in
e
and
b
, which yields,

L^{(2)}(\textbf{e}+\bar{\textbf{E}},\textbf{b})=\frac{\gamma\eta\left(5+\frac{7}{2}\eta\bar{\textbf{E}}^{2}\right)}{\left(1-\frac{\eta}{2}\bar{\textbf{E}}^{2}\right)^{4}}(\textbf{e}.\bar{\textbf{E}})^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\gamma\frac{\left(1+\frac{7}{2}\eta\bar{\textbf{E}}^{2}\right)}{\left(1-\frac{\eta}{2}\bar{\textbf{E}}^{2}\right)^{3}}(\textbf{b}^{2}-\textbf{e}^{2})

(32)

Next, it is possible to find the linearised electric displacement and magnetic field vectors on the background electric field from the above Lagrangian (
32
),

d_{i}=\frac{\partial L^{(2)}}{\partial e^{i}}=(\alpha\delta_{i}^{j}+\beta\bar{E}_{i}\bar{E}^{j})e_{j},\hskip 14.22636pth_{i}=-\frac{\partial L^{(2)}}{\partial b_{i}}=\alpha\delta_{i}^{j}b_{j}

(33)

where,

\beta=\frac{2\gamma\eta\left(5+\frac{7}{2}\eta\bar{\textbf{E}}^{2}\right)}{\left(1-\frac{\eta}{2}\bar{\textbf{E}}^{2}\right)^{4}},\hskip 28.45274pt\alpha=\gamma\frac{\left(1+\frac{7}{2}\eta\bar{\textbf{E}}^{2}\right)}{\left(1-\frac{\eta}{2}\bar{\textbf{E}}^{2}\right)^{3}}

(34)

Therefore, only linear terms in
e
and
b
are left in Eq.(
33
). Using the relations
d_{i}=\epsilon^{j}_{i}e_{j}
,
h_{i}=(\mu^{-1})_{i}^{j}b_{j}
, electric permittivity and permeability tensors can be written as,

\epsilon_{ij}=\alpha\delta_{ij}+\beta\bar{E}_{i}\bar{E}_{j},\hskip 14.22636pt(\mu^{-1})_{ij}=\alpha\delta_{ij}

(35)

The Maxwell equations obeyed by a plane electromagnetic wave imply,

k_{i}d^{i}=k_{i}b^{i}=0,\hskip 28.45274pt\textbf{k}\times\textbf{e}=\omega\textbf{b},\hskip 28.45274pt\textbf{k}\times\textbf{h}=-\omega\textbf{d}

(36)

where
k
is the wave vector. Substituting the vectors
d_{i}=\epsilon_{i}^{j}e_{j}
and
h_{i}=(\mu^{-1})_{i}^{j}b_{j}
in Eq.(
36
), one obtains the wave equation for the electric field
e
as follows
[
79
]
,

\Big\{\bm{\epsilon}^{ijk}\bm{\epsilon}_{lmn}\left(\mu^{-1}\right)^{l}_{k}k_{j}k^{m}+\omega^{2}\epsilon^{i}_{n}\Big\}e^{n}=0

(37)

where
\bm{\epsilon}_{ijk}
is the antisymmetric tensor. The above equation can be converted into the matrix equation,

\Lambda\textbf{e}=0

(38)

where,

\displaystyle\Lambda=\begin{bmatrix}-k^{2}\alpha+\omega^{2}(\alpha+\beta\bar{E}^{2})&0&0\\
0&-k^{2}\alpha+\omega^{2}\alpha&0\\
0&0&\omega^{2}\alpha\end{bmatrix}

Thus, when the matrix determinant is zero, there are nontrivial solutions to the homogeneous matrix equation (

VII
Conclusion

We now summarise our research with concluding remarks.

1.

In this study, we address the inconsistency in the electric field at its location as well as the infinite self-energy of a point charge in Maxwell’s electrodynamics and propose a novel NLE Lagrangian. We first examine our model in flat spacetime and provide a comprehensive analysis of the field equation, focusing specifically on the electrostatic limit. It has been shown that the electric field of a point charge reaches a maximum value, and its self-energy is finite.

2.

We demonstrate how a plane electromagnetic wave displays vacuum birefringence in the presence of a uniform background electric field. It has been argued that when an elementary excitation propagates across spacetime, the principles of causality and unitarity are maintained for all values of the electric field but in a restricted domain of the magnetic field.

3.

The coupling of Einstein’s GR with our NLE model leads to the formation of various spacetime solutions, which depend on the characteristics of the source field. For instance, a nonlinear magnetic monopole yields a family of regular solutions. These solutions can represent double-horizon and single-horizon regular black holes or a family of regular spacetimes without a horizon, depending on specific parameter values. However, if the source is an electric field caused by a point charge, we encounter naked singularities. Further, the non-singular and singular character of regular black holes and naked singularities, respectively, are confirmed by analyzing scalar invariants.

Although we claim that the NLE model (discussed in this article) is a viable source for generating different spacetimes (when coupled to GR), the causality
conditions do not hold for all values of the magnetic field. Only for a restricted domain of the magnetic field, the causality and unitarity conditions hold. Moreover, even though the matter Lagrangian does not contain any fractional power of

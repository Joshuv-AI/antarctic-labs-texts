Zeta Functions for Tensor Codes
Giuseppe Cotardo

Abstract
In this work we introduce a new class of optimal tensor codes related to the Ravagnani-type anticodes, namely the j j -tensor maximum rank distance codes. We show that it extends the family of j j -maximum rank distance codes and contains the j j -tensor binomial moment determined codes (with respect to the Ravagnani-type anticodes) as a proper subclass. We define and study the generalized zeta function for tensor codes. We establish connections between this object and the weight enumerator of a code with respect to the Ravagnani-type anticodes. We introduce a new refinement of the invariants of tensor codes exploiting the structure of product lattices of some classes of anticodes and we derive the corresponding MacWilliams identities. In this framework, we also define a multivariate version of the tensor weight enumerator and we establish relations with the corresponding zeta function. As an application we derive connections on the generalized tensor weights related to the Delsarte and Ravagnani-type anticodes.



1
Introduction

Tensor codes are linear
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear subspaces of the tensor space
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n_{r}}
endowed with the tensor rank as a distance function. They were introduced by Roth
[
20
,
21
]
for the case
n_{1}=\cdots=n_{r}
as a natural generalization of matrix rank-metric codes and used to show that the behaviour of matrix spaces is quite unique compared to the more general case of space of higher order tensors. The definition was then extended in
[
7
]
wherein four different types of anticodes were classified and exploited to study invariants of tensor codes, such as the tensor weight distribution, the tensor binomial moments and the generalized tensor weights. Although the theory of matrix codes has been the subject of intensive research over the last years, the study of tensor codes is still rather unexplored. In
[
20
]
, Roth provided a generalization of the ‘usual’ Singleton bound which turned out to not be sharp for
r>2
. Even though an improvement of this bound was derived for
3
-tensors, only asymptotic bounds are known for higher order tensors. Moreover, few classes of tensor codes are known (in fact only those proposed by Roth in
[
20
,
21
]
). Investigating invariants of these codes represents a crucial step for a better understanding of structural properties and constructing new families of such codes.

Recently, matrix rank-metric codes were investigated in the context of
3
-tensors
[
3
,
6
,
9
]
and the
tensor ranks
of some classes were determined. The tensor rank of a code is defined as the smallest dimension of a space spanned by rank-
1
tensors containing the code. Another object that captures properties of a code is its
zeta function
. This object was introduced in
[
13
,
14
]
for Hamming-metric codes and in
[
5
,
8
]
for matrix rank-metric codes as the generator function of the
generalized normalized binomial moments
. A central role in this theory is played by codes that are extremal with respect to the generalized Singleton-bounds, namely the
j
-maximum distance separable
(
j
-MDS in short) codes for the Hamming metric case and the
j
-maximum rank distance
(
j
-MRD in short) codes for the rank-metric case (see
[
23
]
and
[
8
]
respectively for further details). It is well-known that the set of the weight enumerators of such extremal codes provides a
\mathbb{Q}
-basis for the space of homogeneous polynomials in
\mathbb{Q}[X,Y]
of fixed degree. This property was used in the study of the zeta functions for codes wherein relations between the weight enumerator of a code and the Singleton-optimal weight enumerators were established. In
[
8
]
(for the Hamming and matrix rank-metric case) and in
[
7
]
(for the tensor case), the family of
j
-binomial moment determined
(
j
-BMD in short) codes has been investigated. These extremal codes have the property that all or some of their invariants are determined by the code parameters. In particular, in
[
7
]
the authors showed that the class of
1
-BMD matrix codes is partitioned by the well known families of
MRD
and
dually quasi-MRD
codes (see
[
12
]
and
[
11
]
for further details).

In this paper, we set the theory of zeta functions in the most general context of tensor codes with a perspective based on the families of anticodes claasified in
[
7
]
. In the first part we introduce the notion of zeta function of tensor codes. We then specialize it to the class of Ravagnani-type anticodes and we establish connections between this object and the generalized weight enumerators of tensor codes. As a consequence, we extend the theory developed in
[
8
]
for codes of matrices in the rank metric. The second part will be devoted to a refinement of the tensor zeta functions, binomial moments and weight distributions for closure-type anticodes using the underlying structure of their direct product lattice. We derive analogue relations on these invariants as in the first part of the work.

Outline.
The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we recall some definitions and results. In Section
3
we classify and study a new class of tensor codes that are extremal with respect to the Ravagnani-type anticodes, namely the
j
-tensor maximum rank distance codes. We establish relations between this family and that of
j
-tensor BMD codes. We prove that the latter forms a proper subclass of the former. In Section
4
we introduce the tensor zeta function for the different sets of anticodes classified in
[
7
]
. In particular, we show that this object strictly depends on the underlying set of anticodes. In Section
5
, we restrict to invariants related to the Ravagnani-type anticodes which allows us to generalize the theory developed in
[
8
]
for matrix codes. In this setting, we relate the tensor zeta function to the tensor weight enumerator and we compute the coefficients of the latter in terms of the weight enumerators of
j
-tensor maximum rank distance codes. The theory in this section can be seen as a generalization of the one in
[
8
]
. Section
6
will be devoted to the study of new refined invariants of tensor codes using the fact that the set closure-type anticodes is indeed a
direct product
lattice. More in detail, we define a multivariate version of the tensor weight enumerator and zeta function, and we show connections between these objects. As an application, in Section
7
, we derive connections on the tensor weights associated to the Delsarte and Ravagnani-type anticodes.

Preliminaries and Notation

We start recalling some basic definition on posets and lattices (a standard reference is
[
22
, Chapter 3]
). For any positive integer
i
, we denote by
[i]
the set
\{1,\ldots,i\}
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
partially ordered set
(
poset
for short) is a pair
(\mathscr{P},\leq)
where
\mathscr{P}
is a non-empty set and
\leq
is a binary operation satisfying the axioms of reflexivity, antisymmetry and transitivity. We sometimes abuse the notation and write
\mathscr{P}
for
(\mathscr{P},\leq)
.

For
s,t\in\mathscr{P}
we write
s<t
for
s\leq t
and
s\neq t
. Two posets
\mathscr{P}
and
\mathcal{Q}
are
isomorphic
if there exists an order-preserving map
\phi:\mathscr{P}\longrightarrow\mathcal{Q}
whose inverse is order preserving, that is
s\leq t\textup{ in }\mathscr{P}\Longleftrightarrow\phi(s)\leq\phi(t)\textup{ in }\mathcal{Q}
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\mathscr{P}_{1},\ldots,\mathscr{P}_{r}
be posets. The
direct product
of
\mathscr{P}_{1},\ldots,\mathscr{P}_{r}
is the poset
\mathscr{P}_{1}\times\ldots\times\mathscr{P}_{r}
on the set
\{(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}):s_{i}\in\mathscr{P}_{i}
and
i\in[r]\}
such that
(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})\leq(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})
in
\mathscr{P}_{1}\times\ldots\times\mathscr{P}_{r}
if
s_{i}\leq t_{i}
in
\mathscr{P}_{i}
for all
i\in[r]
.

Definition 2.3
.

The
join
of
s,t\in\mathscr{P}
, should it exist, is the element
u\in\mathscr{P}
satisfying
s\leq u
,
t\leq u
and
u\leq v
for all
v\in\mathscr{P}
such that
s\leq v
and
t\leq v
. The
meet
of
s,t\in\mathscr{P}
, should it exist, is defined dually as the element
u\in\mathscr{P}
satisfying
u\leq s
,
u\leq t
and
v\leq u
for all
v\in\mathscr{P}
such that
v\leq s
and
v\leq t
.

Notation 2.4
.

It easy to check that the join (resp., the meet) of
s,t\in\mathscr{P}
, should it exist, is unique and we denote it by
s\vee t
(resp.,
s\wedge t
).

Definition 2.5
.

Let
\mathscr{P}
be a poset and let
s,t\in\mathscr{P}
. The
interval

[s,t]
in
\mathscr{P}
is the set
\{u\in\mathscr{P}:s\leq u\leq t\}
. The poset
\mathscr{P}
is
locally finite
if each of its intervals has only finitely many elements.

Definition 2.6
.

The
Möbius function
of a locally finite poset
\mathscr{P}
is defined recursively by
\mu_{\mathscr{P}}(s,t)=1
if
s=t
,
\mu_{\mathscr{P}}(s,t)=-\sum_{s\leq u<t}\mu_{\mathscr{P}}(s,u)
if
s<t
and
\mu_{\mathscr{P}}(s,t)=0
otherwise. We sometimes write
\mu(s,t)
for
\mu_{\mathscr{P}}(s,t)
if the poset is clear from context.

Proposition 2.7

(Möbius Inversion Formula)
.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a field,
\mathscr{P}
be a locally finite poset and let
f:\mathscr{P}\longrightarrow\mathbb{K}
be a function. Define
g:\mathscr{P}\longrightarrow\mathbb{K}
by
g(t)=\sum_{s\leq t}f(s)
for all
t\in\mathscr{P}
. Then

f(t)=\sum_{s\leq t}\mu_{\mathscr{P}}(s,t)g(s)\quad\textup{for all $t\in\mathscr{P}$}.

Proposition 2.8

(The Product Theorem)
.

Let
\mathscr{P}_{1}\times\ldots\times\mathscr{P}_{r}
be the direct product of the locally finite posets
\mathscr{P}_{1},\ldots,\mathscr{P}_{r}
. If
(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})\leq(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})
in
\mathscr{P}_{1}\times\ldots\times\mathscr{P}_{r}
then

\mu_{\mathscr{P}_{1}\times\ldots\times\mathscr{P}_{r}}((s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}),(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}))=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\mu_{\mathscr{P}_{i}}(s_{i},t_{i}).

Definition 2.9
.

A
lattice

(\mathscr{L},\leq)
is a poset where every
s,t\in\mathscr{P}
have join and meet. In particular, the join (resp., the meet) of a non-empty subset
S\subseteq\mathscr{L}
is well-defined as the join (resp., the meet) of its elements and denoted by
\bigvee S
(resp.,
\bigwedge S
). A subset
\mathscr{M}
of
\mathscr{L}
is a
sublattice
of
\mathscr{L}
if
\mathscr{M}
is closed under the operations of
\vee
and
\wedge
in
\mathscr{L}
.

Throughout the paper we let
q
be a power prime. We let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be the field of cardinality
q
and let
\mathbb{F}
be the tensor product space
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n_{r}}
for some integers
r,n_{1},\ldots,n_{r}
such that
r,n_{1}\geq 2
. We assume
n_{1}=\min\{n_{1},\ldots,n_{r}\}
and
n_{r}=\max\{n_{1},\ldots,n_{r}\}
without loss of generality and we denote by
n
the product
n_{1}\cdots n_{r}
.

Definition 2.10
.

An
r
-tensor
X\in\mathbb{F}
is a
rank
-1 (or
simple
)
tensor
if
X
can be expressed as
x^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes x^{(r)}
, for some
x^{(i)}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n_{i}}
,
i\in[r]
.

We say that
V\leq\mathbb{F}
is
perfect
if
V
has a basis of simple tensors (c.f.
[
2
]
).

Definition 2.11
.

The
rank
of
X\in\mathbb{F}
is defined to be the least integer
R
such that
X
can be expressed as sum of
R
simple tensors, that is

\textup{rk}(X):=\left\{R\in\mathbb{Z}:X=\sum_{j=1}^{R}x_{j}^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{j}^{(r)}\right\}.

The rank function induces a metric on
\mathbb{F}
. More in detail, the function
\mathbb{F}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}:X\longmapsto\textup{rk}(X)
is a distance function (see
[
21
]
). A basis for
\mathbb{F}
is given by

\left\{\bigotimes_{i=1}^{r}u_{j_{i}}^{(i)}:j_{i}\in[n_{i}]\textup{ and }i\in[r]\right\}

provided that
\{u_{1}^{(i)},\ldots,u_{n_{i}}^{(i)}\}
is a basis for
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n_{i}}
, for any
i\in[r]
, and therefore we have
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\left(\mathbb{F}\right)=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\left(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n_{i}}\right)=n
. We recall that an
r
-tensor
X\in\mathbb{F}
can be represented as the map
X:[n_{1}]\times\cdots\times[n_{r}]\longmapsto\mathbb{F}_{q}
given by
X=(X_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{r}}:j_{i}\in[n_{i}],i\in[r])
. Therefore, the map

\mathbb{F}\longrightarrow\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n_{1}\times\cdots\times n_{r}}:X=\sum_{s=1}^{\textup{rk}(X)}\bigotimes_{i=1}^{r}x_{s}^{(i)}\longmapsto\left(X_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{r}}=\sum_{s=1}^{\textup{rk}(X)}x_{j_{i},s}^{(i)}\right)_{j_{i}\in[n_{i}],\;i\in[r]}

where,
x_{s}^{(i)}=(x_{j_{i},s}^{(i)}:j_{i}\in[n_{i}])
for any
i\in[r]
, is an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-isomorphism. As a consequence, we can identify
\mathbb{F}
with
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n_{1}\times\cdots\times n_{r}}
and represent an
r
-tensor as an
r
-dimensional array.

Tensor codes were introduced in
[
20
,
21
]
for the case
n_{1}=n_{2}=\cdots=n_{r}
as a generalization of codes in the rank-metric. We recall the more general definition given in
[
7
]
.

Definition 2.12
.

A
tensor code
is a
k
-dimensional subspace
\mathcal{C}
of
\mathbb{F}
. The
maximum rank

\textup{maxrk}(\mathcal{C})
of
\mathcal{C}
is
\textup{maxrk}(\mathcal{C}):=\max\{\textup{rk}(C):C\in\mathcal{C}\}
. The
tensor rank

\textup{trk}(\mathcal{C})
of
\mathcal{C}
is the minimum dimension of a perfect space containing
\mathcal{C}
. The
minimum
(
tensor rank
)
distance
of a non-zero code
\mathcal{C}
is
d(\mathcal{C}):=\min\{\textup{rk}(C):C\in\mathcal{C},C\neq 0\}
.

We refer to
n_{1}\times\cdots\times n_{r},k,d(\mathcal{C})
as the
code parameters
of
\mathcal{C}
and we say that
\mathcal{C}
is an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-
[n_{1}\times\cdots\times n_{r},k,d(\mathcal{C})]
code. Moreover, we simply write
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-
[n_{1}\times\cdots\times n_{r},k]
if the minimum distance is not known. For any
i\in[r]
, we let
\cdot
be the dot product on
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n_{i}}
, defined in the usual way as
x\cdot y=\sum_{j=1}^{n_{i}}x_{j}y_{j}
for
x,y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n_{i}}
. We furthermore denote by
*
the non-degenerate bilinear form defined by

X*Y=\sum_{j_{1}=1}^{n_{1}}\cdots\sum_{j_{r}=1}^{n_{r}}X_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{r}}Y_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{r}}.

Remark 2.13
.

One can check that if
X
and
Y
are have rank
1
, say
X=x^{(}1)\times x^{(r)}
and
Y=y^{(}1)\times y^{(r)}

*
then we have

X*Y=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\left(x^{(i)}\cdot y^{(i)}\right).

Definition 2.14
.

The
dual
of
\mathcal{C}
is defined to be the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspace of
\mathbb{F}
which is orthogonal to
\mathcal{C}
with respect to
*

\mathcal{C}^{\perp}:=\{X\in\mathbb{F}:X*C=0\textup{ for all }C\in\mathcal{C}\}
.

We summarize some basic facts on the dual tensor code.

Lemma 2.15
.

Let
\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}\leq\mathbb{F}
be codes. The following hold.

(1)

\left(\mathcal{C}^{\perp}\right)^{\perp}=\mathcal{C}
.

(2)

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\left(\mathcal{C}^{\perp}\right)=n-\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\left(\mathcal{C}\right)
.

(3)

(\mathcal{C}\cap\mathcal{D})^{\perp}=\mathcal{C}^{\perp}+\mathcal{D}^{\perp}
.

Notation 2.16
.

For ease of notation, in the examples throughout the paper we represent
3
-tensors in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n_{1}}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n_{2}}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n_{3}}
as vectors in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n_{1}n_{2}n_{3}}
according to the isomorphism

\begin{array}[]{cccccc}&\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n_{1}}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n_{2}}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n_{3}}&\longrightarrow&\left(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}}\right)^{n_{3}}&\longrightarrow&\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n_{1}n_{2}n_{3}}\\[5.0pt]
&X&\longmapsto&\left(X_{1}\mid\cdots\mid X_{n_{3}}\right)&\longmapsto&\left(\overline{X_{1}}\mid\cdots\mid\overline{X_{n_{3}}}\right).\end{array}

where, for a matrix

,

is the vector of length

with entries in

(1,1)\otimes(1,0,0)\otimes(1,1,1,0)+(1,1)\otimes(0,0,1)\otimes(0,1,1,1)\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2}^{3}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2}^{4}

can be represented as the vector

\left(\begin{array}[]{*{3}{*{3}c;{3pt/3pt}*{3}c|}*{3}c;{3pt/3pt}*{3}c}1&0&0&1&&0&1&0&1&1&0&1&1&0&1&1&0&1&0&&1&0&0&1\end{array}\right)\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{24}.

Definition 2.17
.

For a code
\mathcal{C}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n_{1}}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n_{2}}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n_{3}}

In this section, we introduce a new class of codes that show properties of extremality with respect to the Ravagnani-type anticodes and we relate them to the
j
-TBMD codes. We prove that the latter are a proper subclass of the former and we show that they extend the class of

[

]
on the generalized tensor weight related to

Proposition 3.1

(
[

, Proposition 5.10]
)
.

The following hold.

(1)

t_{j}^{\textup{R}}+\frac{n}{n_{1}}\leq t_{j+\frac{n}{n_{1}}}^{\textup{R}}
.

(2)

t_{j}^{\textup{R}}\leq n-\frac{n}{n_{1}}\left\lfloor\frac{n_{1}}{n}(k-j)\right\rfloor

(3)

t_{j}^{\textup{R}}\geq j
.

As observed in

Theorem 3.2

(
[

Define the sets

Lemma 3.6

If

.

We have that
\mathcal{C}

(2)

Remark 3.9

In Proposition

\displaystyle:=\left\{n_{1}+1-\frac{n_{1}}{n}t_{k+1-\frac{n}{n_{1}}}^{\textup{R}},n_{1}+1-\frac{n_{1}}{n}t_{k+1-2\frac{n}{n_{1}}}^{\textup{R}},\ldots,n_{1}+1-\frac{n_{1}}{n}t_{k+1-\alpha\frac{n}{n_{1}}}^{\textup{R}}\right\}.

The following holds.

36-t_{3}^{\textup{R}}(\mathcal{C}_{1})-t_{1}(\mathcal{C}_{1}^{\perp})=36-t_{3}^{\textup{R}}(\mathcal{C}_{2})-t_{1}(\mathcal{C}_{2}^{\perp})=36-36-12=-12<0
.

•

36-t_{14}^{\textup{R}}(\mathcal{C}_{1}^{\perp})-t_{1}(\mathcal{C}_{1})=36-24-12=0\not<0
.

•

36-t_{15}^{\textup{R}}(\mathcal{C}_{1}^{\perp})-t_{1}(\mathcal{C}_{1})=36-36-12=-12<0
.

•

36-t_{3}^{\textup{R}}(\mathcal{C}_{2}^{\perp})-t_{1}(\mathcal{C}_{2})=36-12-24=0\not<0
.

•

36-t_{15}^{\textup{R}}(\mathcal{C}_{1}^{\perp})-t_{1}(\mathcal{C}_{1})=36-24-24=-12<0
.

This implies that
\mathcal{C}_{1}
and
\mathcal{C}_{2}
are both minimally
3
-TBMD,
\mathcal{C}_{1}^{\perp}
is
15
-TBMD and
\mathcal{C}_{2}^{\perp}
is
4
-TBMD.

4
The Tensor Zeta Function

Following the work in
[
5
]
and
[
8
]
, in this section we introduce the zeta function for generalized tensor weights related to the different collections of anticodes classified in
[
7
]
and we then specialize the theory for the Delsarte and Ravagnani-type anticodes. Throughout this and the next section, we consider polynomials that are members of the polynomial rings
\mathbb{Q}[T]
and
\mathbb{Q}[X,Y,T]
. We introduce the following notation.

Notation 4.1
.

We let
\delta_{i}:=\{s\in[r]:n_{i}=n_{s}\}
, for any
i\in[r]
, and

\Delta:=\begin{cases}\{n_{1},\ldots,n_{r}\}&\textup{ if }|\delta_{r}|>1,\\[3.0pt]
\{n_{1},\ldots,n_{r-1}\}&\textup{ if }|\delta_{r}|=1.\end{cases}

Observe that
|\delta_{i}|\geq 1
for all
i\in[r]
. We define the following.

Definition 4.2
.

For any
a\in\mathbb{Z}
, the
(a,j)
-
normalized tensor binomial moment
of
\mathcal{C}
with respect to
\mathcal{A}
is

b_{a}^{(j)}(\mathcal{C}):=\begin{cases}0&\textup{ if }a<0,\\[3.0pt]
\displaystyle\frac{B_{a+t_{j}}^{(j)}(\mathcal{C})}{|\mathcal{A}_{a+t_{j}}|}&\textup{ if }0\leq a\leq n-t_{j}-s_{1}^{\perp},\\[12.0pt]
\begin{bmatrix}{k+a+t_{j}-n}\\
{j}\end{bmatrix}_{q}&\textup{ if }a>n-t_{j}-s_{1}^{\perp}\textup{ and }\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{A}},\\[15.0pt]
0&\textup{ if }a>n-t_{j}-s_{1}^{\perp}\textup{ and }\neg\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{A}},\end{cases}

where
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{A}}
is the predicate defined as follows

\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{A}}:=\begin{cases}\top&\textup{ if }\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}^{\textup{ps}},\\[2.0pt]
\mathcal{A}_{a}^{\textup{cl}}\neq\emptyset&\textup{ if }\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}^{\textup{cl}}\textup{ and }a\in\{0,\ldots n\},\\[2.0pt]
a\in\{a_{1}\cdots a_{r}:a_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq n_{i}}\textup{ for all }i\in[r]\}&\textup{ if }\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}^{\textup{cl}}\textup{ and }a>n,\\[2.0pt]
n\mid(a+t_{j}^{\textup{D}})m\textup{ for some }m\in\Delta&\textup{ if }\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}^{\textup{D}},\\[2.0pt]
n\mid(a+t_{j}^{\textup{R}})n_{1}&\textup{ if }\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}^{\textup{R}}.\end{cases}

Observe that, for any
a\in\mathbb{Z}
, the condition
n\mid(a+t_{j}^{\textup{R}})n_{1}
is equivalent to
n\mid an_{1}
since
n\mid t_{j}^{\textup{R}}n_{1}
by definition of Ravagnani-type anticodes. We now explicitly determine the cardinality of the set
\mathcal{A}_{a}^{\textup{D}}
and
\mathcal{A}_{a}^{\textup{R}}
for all
a\in\{0,\ldots n\}
using the fact that
\mathcal{A}^{\textup{D}}
and
\mathcal{A}^{\textup{R}}
are product lattices.

Proposition 4.3
.

For any
a\in\{0,\ldots n\}
we have

\left|\mathcal{A}_{a}^{\textup{D}}\right|=\begin{cases}0&\textup{ if }n\nmid am\textup{ for all }m\in\Delta,\\
\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in\Delta\\
n\mid am\end{subarray}}\begin{bmatrix}{m}\\
{a\frac{m}{n}}\end{bmatrix}_{q}&\textup{ if }n\mid am\textup{ for some }m\in\Delta\textup{ and }a\notin\{0,n\},\\
1&\textup{ if }a\in\{0,n\}.\end{cases}

Moreover, if
n\nmid am
for all
m\in\Delta
then
B_{a}^{(\textup{D},j)}(\mathcal{C})=0
and
W_{a}^{(\textup{D},j)}(\mathcal{C})=0
.

Proof.

Clearly,
\mathcal{A}^{\textup{D}}_{0}=\{\left<0\right>_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\}
and
\mathcal{A}^{\textup{D}}_{n}=\{\mathbb{F}\}
, and we can assume
a\in[n-1]
in the remainder of the proof. By Definition
2.19
, we have that
A\in\mathcal{A}^{\textup{D}}
if and only if

A=\bigotimes_{s=1}^{i-1}\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n_{s}}\otimes A^{(i)}\otimes\bigotimes_{s=i+1}^{r}\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n_{s}}

for some
A^{(i)}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n_{i}}
with
n_{i}\in\Delta
. It is not hard to check that a Delsarte-type anticode of dimension
a
exists if and only if
n\mid am
for some
m\in\Delta
. Therefore, if
n\nmid am
for all
m\in\Delta
then
B_{a}^{(\textup{D},j)}(\mathcal{C})=0
and
W_{a}^{(\textup{D},j)}(\mathcal{C})=0
. It remains to prove the case
n\mid am
for some
m\in\Delta
. Define the set

\mathcal{A}_{a,i}^{\textup{D}}:=\left\{\bigotimes_{j=1}^{i-1}\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n_{j}}\otimes A^{(i)}\otimes\bigotimes_{j=i+1}^{r}\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n_{j}}:A^{(i)}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n_{i}}\textup{ and }\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\left(A^{(i)}\right)=a\right\}

and observe that if
n_{i}=n_{s}
then
\left|\mathcal{A}_{a,i}^{\textup{D}}\right|=\left|\mathcal{A}_{a,s}^{\textup{D}}\right|
, for all
i,s\in[r]
. Finally, if there exists an
m\in N
such that
n\mid am
then we have

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{A}_{a}^{\textup{D}}\right|=\left|\mathop{\vphantom{\bigcup}\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\bigcup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\bigcup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\bigcup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\bigcup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}\displaylimits_{i=1}^{r}\mathcal{A}_{a,i}^{\textup{D}}\right|=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\left|\mathcal{A}_{a,i}^{\textup{D}}\right|=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
n\mid ai\end{subarray}}^{r}\left|\mathcal{A}_{a,i}^{\textup{D}}\right|=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in\Delta\\
n\mid am\end{subarray}}\begin{bmatrix}{m}\\
{a\frac{m}{n}}\end{bmatrix}_{q}

which concludes the proof.
∎

A similar argument implies the following result. We include a proof for completeness.

Proposition 4.4
.

For any
a\in\{0,\ldots n\}
we have

\left|\mathcal{A}_{a}^{\textup{R}}\right|=\begin{cases}0&\textup{ if }n\nmid an_{1},\\

{a\frac{n_{1}}{n}}\end{bmatrix}_{q}&\textup{ if }n\mid an_{1}\textup{ and }a\notin\{0,n\},\\

then
B_{a}^{(\textup{R},j)}(\mathcal{C})=0
and
W_{a}^{(\textup{R},j)}(\mathcal{C})=0

Proof.

Clearly,
\mathcal{A}_{0}^{\textup{R}}=\{\left<0\right>_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\}
and
\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\textup{R}}=\{\mathbb{F}\}
, and we assume
a\in[n-1]
in the remainder of the proof. By
[

, Theorem 4.21]
, we have that
A\in\mathcal{A}^{\textup{R}}
if and only if

for some

. Therefore, one can easily check that if

then it cannot exist an

of dimension

and therefore

and
W_{a}^{(\textup{R},j)}(\mathcal{C})=0
. Finally, if
n\mid an_{1}
then we have

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{A}_{a}^{\textup{R}}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\mathop{\vphantom{\bigcup}\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\bigcup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\bigcup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\bigcup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\bigcup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}\displaylimits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\[2.0pt]

5
The Tensor Zeta Function for Ravagnani-type Anticodes

Inspired by the theory developed in
[
8
]
, in this section we extend the study of the zeta function for tensor weights related to Ravagnani-type anticodes. We now recall the definition of the
q
-
Bernstein polynomial
. The reader is referred to
[
17
]
for further details.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
a,b
be nonnegative integers such that
a\leq b
. The
(b,a)
-
th

q
-
Bernstein polynomial
is

\mathcal{B}_{b,a}(X,Y;q):=\begin{bmatrix}{b}\\
{a}\end{bmatrix}_{q}Y^{a}\prod_{c=0}^{b-a-1}(X-q^{c}Y).

We also recall that the sets
\{X^{b-a}Y^{a}:a,b\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},a\leq b\}
and
\{\mathcal{B}_{b,a}(X,Y;q):a,b\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},a\leq b\}
are
\mathbb{Q}
-bases for the ring of homogeneous polynomials of degree
b
. The inversion formula associated with these polynomials is

X^{b-a}Y^{a}=\begin{bmatrix}{b}\\
{a}\end{bmatrix}_{q}^{-1}\sum_{c=a}^{b}\begin{bmatrix}{c}\\
{a}\end{bmatrix}_{q}\mathcal{B}_{b,c}(X,Y;q).

A direct application of Proposition
4.4
to Proposition
2.27
gives the following result.

Proposition 5.2
.

The following hold for all
a,b\in\{0,\ldots,n\}
.

(1)

\displaystyle B_{a}^{(\textup{R},j)}(\mathcal{C})=\sum_{b=0}^{a}W_{b}^{(\textup{R},j)}(\mathcal{C})|\delta_{1}|\begin{bmatrix}{n_{1}-a\frac{n_{1}}{n}}\\
{(a-b)\frac{n_{1}}{n}}\end{bmatrix}_{q}
.

(2)

\displaystyle W_{b}^{(\textup{R},j)}(\mathcal{C})=\sum_{a=0}^{b}B_{a}^{(\textup{R},j)}(\mathcal{C})|\delta_{1}|\begin{bmatrix}{n_{1}-b\frac{n_{1}}{n}}\\
{(b-a)\frac{n_{1}}{n}}\end{bmatrix}_{q}(-1)^{(b-a)\frac{n_{1}}{n}}q^{\binom{(b-a)\frac{n_{1}}{n}}{2}}
.

Proof.

Note that by Proposition
4.4
we have

\left|\left\{(A^{\prime},A)\in\mathcal{A}_{b}\times\mathcal{A}_{a}:A^{\prime}\leq A\right\}\right|=\begin{cases}0&\textup{ if }\;n\nmid n_{1}b\;\textup{ or }\;n\nmid n_{1}a,\\[8.61108pt]
\displaystyle|\delta_{1}|\begin{bmatrix}{n_{1}-b\frac{n_{1}}{n}}\\
{(a-b)\frac{n_{1}}{n}}\end{bmatrix}_{q}&\textup{ if }\;n\mid n_{1}b\;\textup{ and }\;n\mid n_{1}a.\end{cases}

Finally, one can check that the lattices

\left\{A^{(1)}\otimes\bigotimes_{s=2}^{r}\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n_{s}}:A^{(1)}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n_{1}}\right\}\qquad\textup{ and }\qquad\left\{A\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n_{1}}\right\}

are isomorphic and so the statement follows by Proposition
2.27
.
∎

In the next theorem, we compute the coefficients of
\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{C}}^{(\textup{R},j)}(X,Y)
in terms of the basis given by
q
-Bernstein polynomials. This result can be seen a generalization
[
8
, Theorem 3.11]
for
r\geq 2
.

Theorem 5.3
.

The following holds.

\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{C}}^{(\textup{R},j)}(X,Y)=\sum_{a=t_{j}^{\textup{R}}\frac{n_{1}}{n}}^{n_{1}}b_{a\frac{n}{n_{1}}-t_{j}^{\textup{R}}}^{(\textup{R},j)}(\mathcal{C})\mathcal{B}_{{n_{1}},a}\left(X^{\frac{n}{n_{1}}},Y^{\frac{n}{n_{1}}};q\right).

Proof.

Proposition
4.4
and the inversion formula for
q
-Bernstein polynomial imply

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{C}}^{(\textup{R},j)}(X,Y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\tiny\begin{matrix}b\leq n\\
n\mid bn_{1}\end{matrix}}W_{b}^{(\textup{R},j)}(\mathcal{C})X^{n-b}Y^{b}

\displaystyle=\sum_{b=0}^{n_{1}}W_{b\frac{n}{n_{1}}}^{(\textup{R},j)}(\mathcal{C})\left(X^{\frac{n}{n_{1}}}\right)^{n_{1}-b}\left(Y^{\frac{n}{n_{1}}}\right)^{b}

\displaystyle=\sum_{b=t_{j}^{\textup{R}}\frac{n_{1}}{n}}^{n_{1}}W_{b\frac{n}{n_{1}}}^{(\textup{R},j)}(\mathcal{C})\begin{bmatrix}{n_{1}}\\
{b}\end{bmatrix}_{q}^{-1}\sum_{a=b}^{n_{1}}\begin{bmatrix}{a}\\
{b}\end{bmatrix}_{q}\mathcal{B}_{{n_{1}},a}\left(X^{\frac{n}{n_{1}}},Y^{\frac{n}{n_{1}}};q\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a=t_{j}^{\textup{R}}\frac{n_{1}}{n}}^{n_{1}}B_{n_{1},a}\left(X^{\frac{n}{n_{1}}},Y^{\frac{n}{n_{1}}};q\right)\begin{bmatrix}{n_{1}}\\
{a}\end{bmatrix}_{q}^{-1}\sum_{b=t_{j}^{\textup{R}}\frac{n_{1}}{n}}^{a}\begin{bmatrix}{n_{1}-b}\\
{a-b}\end{bmatrix}_{q}W_{b\frac{n}{n_{1}}}^{(\textup{R},j)}(\mathcal{C})

where the last equality follows by Lemma
2.31
. Finally, by Proposition
5.2
and Definition
4.2
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{C}}^{(\textup{R},j)}(X,Y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a=t_{j}^{\textup{R}}\frac{n_{1}}{n}}^{n_{1}}\mathcal{B}_{{n_{1}},a\frac{n_{1}}{n}}\left(X^{\frac{n}{n_{1}}},Y^{\frac{n}{n_{1}}};q\right)\begin{bmatrix}{n_{1}}\\
{a}\end{bmatrix}_{q}^{-1}\sum_{b=t_{j}^{\textup{R}}\frac{n_{1}}{n}}^{a}\begin{bmatrix}{n_{1}-a}\\
{a-b}\end{bmatrix}_{q}W_{b\frac{n}{n_{1}}}^{(\textup{R},j)}(\mathcal{C})

\displaystyle=\sum_{a=t_{j}^{\textup{R}}\frac{n_{1}}{n}}^{n_{1}}\mathcal{B}_{{n_{1}},a}\left(X^{\frac{n}{n_{1}}},Y^{\frac{n}{n_{1}}};q\right)\begin{bmatrix}{n_{1}}\\
{a}\end{bmatrix}_{q}^{-1}|\delta_{1}|^{-1}B_{a\frac{n}{n_{1}}}^{(\textup{R},j)}(\mathcal{C})

\displaystyle=\sum_{a=t_{j}^{\textup{R}}\frac{n_{1}}{n}}^{n_{1}}b_{a\frac{n}{n_{1}}-t_{j}^{\textup{R}}}^{(\textup{R},j)}(\mathcal{C})\mathcal{B}_{{n_{1}},a}\left(X^{\frac{n}{n_{1}}},Y^{\frac{n}{n_{1}}};q\right)

which concludes the proof.
∎

The following result provides a connection between the
q
-Bernstein polynomials, the
j
-th generalized tensor zeta function and the
j
-th generalized tensor weight enumerator.

Proposition 5.4
.

Define the function

\varphi_{a}(X,Y,T):=\sum_{b=0}^{a}B_{a,b}(X,Y;q)T^{a-b}.

Then
\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{C}}^{(\textup{R},j)}(X,Y)
is the coefficient of
T^{n-t_{j}^{\textup{R}}}
in the expression
Z_{\mathcal{C}}^{(\textup{R},j)}(T)\varphi_{n_{1}}\left(X^{\frac{n}{n_{1}}},Y^{\frac{n}{n_{1}}},T^{\frac{n}{n_{1}}}\right)
.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle Z_{\mathcal{C}}^{(\textup{R},j)}(T)\varphi_{n_{1}}

\displaystyle\left(X^{\frac{n}{n_{1}}},Y^{\frac{n}{n_{1}}},T^{\frac{n}{n_{1}}}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{a\geq 0}b_{a\frac{n}{n_{1}}}(\mathcal{C})\left(T^{\frac{n}{n_{1}}}\right)^{a}\right)\left(\sum_{b=0}^{n_{1}}B_{n_{1},b}\left(X^{\frac{n}{n_{1}}},Y^{\frac{n}{n_{1}}};q\right)\left(T^{\frac{n}{n_{1}}}\right)^{n_{1}-b}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a\geq 0}\sum_{b=0}^{n_{1}}b_{a\frac{n}{n_{1}}}(\mathcal{C})B_{n_{1},b}\left(X^{\frac{n}{n_{1}}},Y^{\frac{n}{n_{1}}};q\right)\left(T^{\frac{n}{n_{1}}}\right)^{n_{1}-b+a}

\displaystyle=\sum_{b=0}^{n_{1}}\left(T^{\frac{n}{n_{1}}}\right)^{b}\sum_{a=0}^{b}b_{a\frac{n}{n_{1}}}(\mathcal{C})B_{n_{1},n_{1}-b+a}\left(X^{\frac{n}{n_{1}}},Y^{\frac{n}{n_{1}}};q\right)\mod T^{n+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{b=0}^{n_{1}}\left(T^{\frac{n}{n_{1}}}\right)^{n_{1}-b}\sum_{a=0}^{n_{1}-b}b_{a\frac{n}{n_{1}}}B_{n_{1},b+a}\left(X^{\frac{n}{n_{1}}},Y^{\frac{n}{n_{1}}};q\right)\mod T^{n+1}.

Therefore, by Theorem
5.3
,
\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{C}}^{(\textup{R},j)}(X,Y)
is the coefficient of
T^{n-t_{j}^{\textup{R}}}=\left(T^{\frac{n}{n_{1}}}\right)^{n_{1}-\frac{n_{1}}{n}t_{j}^{\textup{R}}}
in the expression
Z_{\mathcal{C}}^{(\textup{R},j)}(T)\varphi_{n_{1}}\left(X^{\frac{n}{n_{1}}},Y^{\frac{n}{n_{1}}},T^{\frac{n}{n_{1}}}\right)
.
∎

Theorem 5.6

There exists a unique polynomial

as in the statement. Moreover, a standard computation using Lemma

\sum_{s=0}^{j+1}\begin{bmatrix}{j+1}\\
{s}\end{bmatrix}_{q^{\frac{n}{n_{1}}}}(-1)^{s}q^{\frac{n}{n_{1}}\binom{s}{2}}b_{\frac{n}{n_{1}}(a-s)}^{(j)}(\mathcal{C})=0

for all

∎

Note that this result extends

, Theorem 5.2]
for

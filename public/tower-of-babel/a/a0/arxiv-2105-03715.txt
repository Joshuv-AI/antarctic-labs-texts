Introduction

An arithmetic dynamical system is a pair
(\mathbb{P}^{N}(K),\phi)
where
K
is a number field and
\phi:\mathbb{P}^{N}(K)\longrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{N}(K)
is a morphism on a projective space
\mathbb{P}^{N}(K)
of dimension
N
. For
x\in\mathbb{P}^{N}(K)
, we denote
\phi^{0}(x)=x
and
\phi^{m}(x)=\phi(\phi^{m-1}(x))
where
m\in\mathbb{N}.
We say a point
P\in\mathbb{P}^{N}(K)
is a
periodic point
of
\phi
if
\phi^{n}(P)=P
for some
n\geq 1.
If there is no positive integer
m<n
such that
\phi^{m}(P)=P
, then
P
is a periodic point of
exact period

n.
A point
Q\in\mathbb{P}^{N}(K)
is called a
preperiodic point
of
\phi
if
\phi^{n_{1}}(Q)=\phi^{n_{2}}(Q)
for some positive integers
n_{1}\neq n_{2}.
We denote
\per(\phi,\mathbb{P}^{N}(K))
and
\pper(\phi,\mathbb{P}^{N}(K))
the sets of periodic and preperiodic points of
\phi
on
\mathbb{P}^{N}(K)
, respectively.
Morton and Silverman
[
MR1264933
]
proposed a conjecture for morphisms over number fields :

Conjecture 1

(Morton-Silverman Uniform Boundedness Conjecture)
.

There exists a bound
B=B(D,N,d)
such that if
K
is a number field of degree
D,
and
\phi:\mathbb{P}^{N}(K)\longrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{N}(K)
is a morphism of degree
d\geq 2
defined over
K,
then the number of preperiodic points of
\phi
is bounded by
B.

This conjecture is remarkably strong. For
(D,n,d)=(1,1,4),
the conjecture implies that the size of the torsion subgroup of an elliptic curve is uniformly bounded (see
[
MR488287
]
). This can be done via the associated Lattès map. Similarly, for
(D,n,d)=(D,1,4),
the conjecture implies the Merel’s theorem
[
MR1369424
]
. Therefore, the set of associated Lattès maps of elliptic curves is a family of rational functions that is proved to satisfy the conjecture. Recently, under the assumption of the
abcd
-conjecture, Looper proved the Uniform Boundedness Conjecture by Morton and Silverman for the case of polynomials (see
[
Looper1
]
).The conjecture for quadratic polynomials over
\mathbb{Q}
was posed by Russo and Walde
[
MR1270956
]
as follows:

Conjecture 2

(Uniform Boundedness Conjecture for
x^{2}+c
)
.

If
n\geq 4,
then there is no quadratic polynomial
f_{c}(x)=x^{2}+c\in\mathbb{Q}[x]
with a rational point of exact period
n.

For exact period

and 3, Walde and Russo
[
MR1270956
]
described the periodic points of

2
2-Periodic Points of
f_{d,c}
for
d=4,6

For an even positive integer
d
, it is easy to see that
f_{d,0}(x)=x^{d}
has rational periodic points of exact period 1, i.e.,
0
and
1
. Similarly,
f_{d,-1}(x)=x^{d}-1
has rational periodic points of exact period 2, i.e.,
0
and
-1
. We will call these two cases, i.e.,
c\in\{0,-1\}
, as
trivial
. On the other hand, it is easy to see that if
f_{d,c}=x^{d}+c
has a rational point
x\in\{0,1\}
of period 1 or a set
\{0,-1\}
of exact period 2, then
c=0
or
c=-1
, respectively.

For a polynomial
f(x)\in\mathbb{Q}[x]
, we define the
2^{nd}

dynatomic polynomial

\Phi_{2}(x)
as

\Phi_{2}(x)=\dfrac{f^{2}(x)-x}{f(x)-x}.

It is easy to see that the roots of the
2^{nd}
dynatomic polynomial are periodic points of period 2.
For more details on dynatomic polynomials, see
[
MR2316407
]
.

The following theorem describes all rational periodic points of exact period 2 for
f_{4,c}(x)=x^{4}+c
.

Theorem 5
.

There are infinitely many
c\in\mathbb{Q}
such that
f_{4,c}(x)=x^{4}+c
has rational periodic points of exact period 2. The parametrization of such
c
and 2-periodic points
x_{1},x_{2}
are as follows. The parameter
c
is in the form

\displaystyle c=\dfrac{t^{6}+4t^{3}-1}{4t^{2}}

and the 2-periodic points are

\displaystyle x_{1},x_{2}=\dfrac{t^{2}\pm\sqrt{-t^{4}-2t}}{2t},

where
-t^{4}-2t=y^{2}
for some
y\in\mathbb{Q}
.

Proof.

Consider the
2^{nd}
dynatomic polynomial
\Phi_{2}(x,c)
of
f_{4,c}(x).

We have

\displaystyle\Phi_{2}(x,c)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{f_{4,c}^{2}(x)-x}{f_{4,c}(x)-x}

\displaystyle=x^{12}+x^{9}+3cx^{8}+x^{6}+2cx^{5}+3c^{2}x^{4}+x^{3}+cx^{2}+c^{2}x+1+c^{3}.

To find the roots of
\Phi_{2}
, we solve for the zeroes of the resultant of
\Phi_{2}(x,c)
and the trace of 2-periodic orbits

T(t,c):=t-(x+f_{4,c}(x))

with respect to
x
. We have

\res(\Phi_{2},T)=(1+4ct^{2}-4t^{3}-t^{6})^{2}=0.

Thus,

1+4ct^{2}-4t^{3}-t^{6}=0,

or equivalently,

\displaystyle c=\frac{t^{6}+4t^{3}-1}{4t^{2}}.

The
2^{nd}
dynatomic polynomial satisfies

\displaystyle 0=\Phi_{2}(x,c)

\displaystyle=\Phi_{2}(x,\dfrac{t^{6}+4t^{3}-1}{4t^{2}})

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\left(t^{3}-2t^{2}x+2tx^{2}+1\right)\left(t^{15}+2t^{14}x+\dots+8t^{2}x^{4}+2t^{2}x+2tx^{2}-1\right)}{64t^{6}}.

We solve for rational solutions to the equation

t^{3}-2t^{2}x+2tx^{2}+1=0,

equivalently,

x=\dfrac{t^{2}\pm\sqrt{-t^{4}-2t}}{2t}.

Therefore,
(x,t)\in\mathbb{Q}^{2}
if and only if there is
y\in\mathbb{Q}
such that
y^{2}=-t^{4}-2t
.
Consider the elliptic curve
E:y^{2}=-t^{4}-2t
. We have that
E(\mathbb{Q})\cong\mathbb{Z}
. Therefore, there are infinitely many
c\in\mathbb{Q}
such that
f_{4,c}
has rational periodic points of exact period 2.

Lemma 1
.

[
MR3266961
]

Let
f_{d,c}(x)=x^{d}+c\in K[x]
and let
\alpha\in\mathbb{A}(K)
be a preperiodic point of
f_{d,c}
. Then for each nonarchimedean place
v
such that
v(c)<0
, we have
v(c)=dv(\alpha)
. For each nonarchimedean place
v
such that
v(c)\geq 0
, we have
v(\alpha)\geq 0
.

The above lemma immediately implies the following corollary.

Corollary 1
.

If
X/Z
is a rational preperiodic point of
f_{d,c}(x)=x^{d}+c
with
c=M/N
and both
X/Z
and
M/N
are rational numbers expressed in the lowest terms with
Z
and
N
positive integers, then
N=Z^{d}.

We will use Corollary
1
to show that the periodic points of exact period 2 of
f_{d,c}(x)=x^{d}+c
can be described in the form of a Fermat-Catalan equation in the following theorem.

Theorem 8
.

Let
k,n
be positive integers such that
n>1
, and
f_{d,c}(x)=x^{d}+c\in\mathbb{Q}[x]
, where
d=2k
. If
X_{1}/Z
and
X_{2}/Z=f_{d,c}(X_{1}/Z)
are rational periodic points of exact period
n
of
f_{d,c}
with
c=C/Z^{d}
, and
X_{1}/Z,X_{2}/Z
and
C/Z^{d}
are rational numbers expressed in the lowest terms with integers
X_{1},X_{2}
and
Z
, then
\gcd(X_{1},X_{2})=1.
Moreover, for
n=2
,

a)

X_{1}^{k}+X_{2}^{k}=\delta Z_{1}^{2k-1}
for some
Z_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\delta\in\{1,2\}
,

b)

if
k
is odd, then
X_{1}^{k}+X_{2}^{k}=Z_{1}^{2k-1}
for some
Z_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}.

In the following proof, for integers
a,b
and positive integer
n
, we write
a^{n}||b
if
a^{n}|b
but
a^{n+1}\not|b
.

Proof.

Let
x_{i}=X_{i}/Z
be rational periodic points of exact period
n
of
f_{d,c}(x)
for
i=1,\dots,n.
Observe that

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}(x_{i}-x_{i+1})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(f_{d,c}(x_{i})-f_{d,c}(x_{i+1}))=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(x_{i}^{2k}-x_{i+1}^{2k}),

where
x_{n+1}=x_{1}
.
Thus,
\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}\dfrac{(x_{i}^{2k}-x_{i+1}^{2k})}{x_{i}-x_{i+1}}=1,
or equivalently,

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}\dfrac{(X_{i}^{2k}-X_{i+1}^{2k})}{X_{i}-X_{i+1}}=Z^{n(2k-1)}.

Assume that there is
q\in\mathbb{N}
such that
q\mid\gcd(X_{1},X_{2})
.

Since
q\mid\gcd(X_{1},X_{2})
and

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}(X_{i}^{k}+X_{i+1}^{k})\frac{(X_{i}^{k}-X_{i+1}^{k})}{X_{i}-X_{i+1}}=Z^{n(2k-1)},

we have
q\mid Z^{n(2k-1)}.
However,
X_{1}
and
Z
are coprime. Thus,
q=1.

For period
n=2
, we have that

\displaystyle(X_{1}^{k}+X_{2}^{k})\frac{(X_{1}^{k}-X_{2}^{k})}{X_{1}-X_{2}}=Z^{2k-1}.

Since
\gcd(X_{1},X_{2})=1
and

\gcd(X_{1}^{k}+X_{2}^{k},X_{1}^{k}-X_{2}^{k})\mid\gcd(2X_{1}^{k},2X_{2}^{k}),

we have that
\gcd(X_{1}^{k}+X_{2}^{k},X_{1}^{k}-X_{2}^{k})\mid 2.

We consider 2 cases.

Case 1.
\gcd(X_{1}^{k}+X_{2}^{k},\dfrac{X_{1}^{k}-X_{2}^{k}}{X_{1}-X_{2}})=1

If
\gcd(X_{1}^{k}+X_{2}^{k},\dfrac{X_{1}^{k}-X_{2}^{k}}{X_{1}-X_{2}})=1,
then we easily have that
X_{1}^{k}+X_{2}^{k}=Z_{1}^{2k-1}
for some integer
Z_{1}
such that
Z_{1}\mid Z.

Case 2.

\gcd(X_{1}^{k}+X_{2}^{k},\dfrac{X_{1}^{k}-X_{2}^{k}}{X_{1}-X_{2}})=2

If
\gcd(X_{1}^{k}+X_{2}^{k},\dfrac{X_{1}^{k}-X_{2}^{k}}{X_{1}-X_{2}})=2,
then we have that
Z
is even. Therefore,
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
are odd.

If
4\mid X_{1}^{k}+X_{2}^{k},
then
2\mathrel{\|}X_{1}^{k}-X_{2}^{k}.
Thus,
\dfrac{X_{1}^{k}-X_{2}^{k}}{X_{1}-X_{2}}
is odd and

\gcd(X_{1}^{k}+X_{2}^{k},\dfrac{X_{1}^{k}-X_{2}^{k}}{X_{1}-X_{2}})=1,

a contradiction. Therefore,
2\mathrel{\|}X_{1}^{k}+X_{2}^{k}.
Thus,

X_{1}^{k}+X_{2}^{k}=2Z_{1}^{2k-1}\textrm{ and }\dfrac{X_{1}^{k}-X_{2}^{k}}{X_{1}-X_{2}}=2^{2k-2}Z_{2}^{2k-1}

for some integers
Z_{1}
and
Z_{2}
such that
\gcd(Z_{1},Z_{2})=1.

If
k
is an odd integer, then lifting the exponent lemma (see, for example,
[

,
titu
]
) implies that
\dfrac{X_{1}^{k}-X_{2}^{k}}{X_{1}-X_{2}}
is odd. Thus,

\gcd(X_{1}^{k}+X_{2}^{k},\dfrac{X_{1}^{k}-X_{2}^{k}}{X_{1}-X_{2}})=1,

a contradiction.

From 2 cases, we can conclude that if
k
is odd, then
X_{1}^{k}+X_{2}^{k}=Z_{1}^{2k-1}
for some
Z_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}.

In the previous section, we discuss the 2-periodic points. Even for this case, we can only treat

for some

Let

be a positive integer such that
\displaystyle n=p_{1}^{r_{l}}p_{2}^{r_{2}}\cdots p_{l}^{r_{l}}
and
p_{i}
’s are different primes. The
radical
of

is defined by

\rad(n)=p_{1}p_{2}\cdots p_{l}.

By convention,
\rad(1):=1.
The
abc
-conjecture can be stated as follows.

Conjecture 5

(
abc-
conjecture).

For every
\epsilon>0,
there exists a constant
K_{\epsilon}
such that for all triples
(a,b,c)
of coprime integers, with
a+b=c
such that

\max(|a|,|b|,|c|)<K_{\epsilon}\rad(|abc|)^{1+\epsilon}.

The explicit version of the
abc
-conjecture was proposed by Baker
[
MR2107944
]
.

Conjecture 6

(
Explicit
abc-
conjecture).

[
MR2107944
]

There exists an absolute constant
K
such that for all triples
(a,b,c)
of coprime integers with
a+b=c
,
abc\neq 0
and
N=\rad(|abc|),

\max(|a|,|b|,|c|)<KN\dfrac{(\log(N))^{\omega}}{\omega!}

where
\omega
is the total number of distinct primes dividing a, b and c.

Laishram and Shorey
[
MR2997579
]
proved that the Baker’s version implies that for
\epsilon=\dfrac{3}{4}
,
K_{\epsilon}\leq 1
.
Later Chim, Shorey, and Sinha
[
ChimShoreySinha
]
improved the result in the following theorem.

Theorem 11
.

Assume the explicit
abc
-conjecture. For all triples
(a,b,c)
of coprime integers with
a+b=c
,
abc\neq 0
and
N=\rad(|abc|)
,

\displaystyle\max(|a|,|b|,|c|)<\min(N^{1.72},10N^{1.62991},32N^{1.6}).

The form of Theorem
11
is similar to Theorem 2.2 in
[
ChimShoreyNair
]
.
For a polynomial
F(x)\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
of degree greater than one, we can show that any periodic point of
F(x)
has exact period at most 2. This is because its periodic points must be integers as well. Therefore, we can simply apply divisibility properties to yield the result. Hence, for
f_{d,c}(x)=x^{d}+c
and rational points of period greater than 2, we can consider only the case
c\not\in\mathbb{Z}
.

We employ the
abc
-conjecture to analyze a Diophantine equation in the form, related to Fermat-Catalan equation,

\displaystyle x^{d}-y^{d}=\delta z^{d-1}

where
\delta
is a “
small
” variable compared to
x,y
and
z
. The following lemma is used to prove the nonexistence of rational periodic points of
f_{d,c}
for sufficiently large integer
d
.

Lemma 2
.

Assume the
abc-
conjecture. Then, for each
\epsilon>0
, there is
K_{\epsilon}
such that the system

\displaystyle\begin{cases}X_{2}^{d}-X_{1}^{d}=(X_{3}-X_{2})Z^{d-1}\neq 0\\
\gcd(X_{1},X_{2})=1\\
\max(|X_{1}|,|X_{2}|,|X_{3}|)=|X_{2}|\\
W=\max(|X_{1}|,|X_{2}|,|X_{3}|,|Z|)>1\end{cases}

satisfies
d<\log_{b}(2^{1+\epsilon}K_{\epsilon})+5+4\epsilon
, for all
b\in(1,W]
.
Therefore, the system has no integer solutions for sufficiently large positive integer
d.

Moreover, if we assume the explicit
abc
-conjecture, then the system has no integer solutions for
d\geq 10.

Proof.

Let
\epsilon>0.
By the
abc
-conjecture, there is a constant
K_{\epsilon}>0
such that

M:=\max(|X_{2}|^{d},|X_{1}|^{d},|(X_{3}-X_{2})Z^{d-1}|)<K_{\epsilon}\rad(|X_{2}X_{1}(X_{3}-X_{2})Z|)^{1+\epsilon}.

We consider 2 cases.

Case 1.

|X_{2}|\leq|Z|.

We have

\displaystyle|(X_{3}-X_{2})Z^{d-1}|\leq M

\displaystyle<K_{\epsilon}\rad(|X_{2}X_{1}(X_{3}-X_{2})Z|)^{1+\epsilon}

\displaystyle\leq K_{\epsilon}(|Z\cdot Z\cdot 2X_{2}\cdot Z|)^{1+\epsilon}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{1+\epsilon}K_{\epsilon}(|Z\cdot Z\cdot Z\cdot Z|)^{1+\epsilon}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{1+\epsilon}K_{\epsilon}|Z|^{4+4\epsilon}.

Thus,
|Z|^{d-1}\leq|X_{3}-X_{2}||Z|^{m}<2^{1+\epsilon}K_{\epsilon}|Z|^{4+4\epsilon}.
Since
|Z|>1
, we have

d-1<\log_{|Z|}(2^{1+\epsilon}K_{\epsilon})+4+4\epsilon.

Case 2.

|X_{2}|>|Z|.

We have

\displaystyle|X_{2}|^{d}\leq M

\displaystyle<K_{\epsilon}\rad(|X_{2}X_{1}(X_{3}-X_{2})Z|)^{1+\epsilon}

\displaystyle\leq K_{\epsilon}(|X_{2}\cdot X_{2}\cdot 2X_{2}\cdot X_{2}|)^{1+\epsilon}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{1+\epsilon}K_{\epsilon}|X_{2}|^{4+4\epsilon}.

Thus,
|X_{2}|^{d}<2^{1+\epsilon}K_{\epsilon}|X_{2}|^{4+4\epsilon}.
Since
|X_{2}|>1
, we have

d<\log_{|X_{2}|}(2^{1+\epsilon}K_{\epsilon})+4+4\epsilon.

Let
W=\max{(|X_{1}|,|X_{2}|,|X_{3}|,|Z|)}
. From both cases, we can conclude that

d-1<\log_{W}(2^{1+\epsilon}K_{\epsilon})+4+4\epsilon.

This implies
d<\log_{b}(2^{1+\epsilon}K_{\epsilon})+5+4\epsilon
, for all
b\in(1,W]
.

Thus, for
b=2
, the system has no integer solutions for
d\geq\log_{2}(2^{1+\epsilon}K_{\epsilon})+5+4\epsilon.

Now assume the explicit
abc
-conjecture.

Theorem
11
implies that for
\epsilon=0.72
, we can choose
K_{\epsilon}=1
. If

d\geq 10\geq\log_{2}(2^{1.72})+7.88=9.6,

then the system has no integer solutions.

∎

The following lemma is an observation for the relation between different fixed points and points of exact period 2.

Lemma 3
.

Let

be a positive even integer. Then
f_{d,c}(x)=x^{d}+c\in\mathbb{C}[x]
has different fixed points

if and only if

has points of exact period 2, namely,
y_{1}=-x_{1}
and
y_{2}=-x_{2}
, where
c^{\prime}=-x_{1}-x_{2}^{d}.

Proof.

Assume that
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
are different fixed points of
f_{d,c}(x)=x^{d}+c
. Therefore,
x_{1}^{d}+c=x_{1}
and
x_{2}^{d}+c=x_{2}.
Equivalently,
x_{1}-x_{1}^{d}=c=x_{2}-x_{2}^{d}.
This implies
x_{1}+x_{2}^{d}=x_{2}+x_{1}^{d}
. Let
y_{1}=-x_{1}
ans
y_{2}=-x_{2}.
Thus,
y_{1}-y_{2}^{d}=y_{2}-y_{1}^{d}.
Choose
c^{\prime}=y_{1}-y_{2}^{d}\,\,(=y_{2}-y_{1}^{d}).
Since
d
is even,
y_{2}^{d}+c^{\prime}=y_{1}
and
y_{1}^{d}+c^{\prime}=y_{2}.

Conversely, assume that
y_{1}
and
y_{2}
are different periodic points of exact period 2 of
f_{d,c^{\prime}}=x^{d}+c^{\prime}
. We can choose
x_{1}=-y_{1}
and
x_{2}=-y_{2}
. Then
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
are different fixed points of
f_{d,c}
, where
c=x_{1}-x_{1}^{d}\,\,(=x_{2}-x_{2}^{d})
.

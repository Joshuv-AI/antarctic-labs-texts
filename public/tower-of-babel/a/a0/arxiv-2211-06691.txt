Distribution Decomposition and Sum-Capacity Results of Two-User Optical Intensity Multiple Access Channels
Longguang Li, Ru-Han Chen, Jing Zhou

Abstract
This paper investigates the sum-capacity of two-user optical intensity multiple access channels with per-user peak- or/and average-intensity constraints. By leveraging tools from the decomposition of certain maxentropic distributions , we derive several lower bounds on the sum-capacity. In the high signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) regime, some bounds asymptotically match or approach the sum-capacity, thus closing or reducing the existing gaps to the high-SNR asymptotic sum-capacity. At moderate SNR, some bounds are also fairly close to the sum-capacity. Index terms— Peak- and average-intensity constraints, sum-capacity, direct detection, Gaussian noise, multiple access channel (MAC), optical communication.

This paper focuses on the sum-capacity of a two-user MAC with a per-user peak- or/and average-intensity constraints.
Instead of using truncated Gaussian input or existing capacity results on additive noise channels,

here we leverage tools from
the decomposition of distributions.
Although analyzing distributions of the sum of random
variables is a well-studied problem in network information theory, its inverse problem, i.e., the decomposition of distributions of random
variables, is less studied. In fact, distribution decomposition is an important topic in probability theory, where the employed mathematics lies between the theory of probability and the theory of functions of complex variables. The classical book
[
39
]
gives a comprehensive survey on important results in this field. However, most existing mathematical results are on the decomposability or the decomposition types of general random variables, with less restrictions on the decomposed random variables, which cannot be directly applied to analyze network channels. By taking into consideration the given constraints on the support or expectations (first moments) of decomposed random variables, we propose new decomposition results of certain random variables following maxentropic distributions.
These results can be used to derive new lower bounds on sum-capacity in the two-user MAC. The main contributions of the
paper
are as follows:

1.

Decomposition of Maxentropic Distributions:
By using the binary expansion of a uniform random variable, we establish that the uniform random variable can be decomposed as a sum of two independent random variables while satisfying
given
constraints on their support. Similarly, an exponentially or a truncated exponentially distributed random variable can be decomposed as a sum of two independent random
variables
while satisfying
given
constraints on their expectations. We also show that a uniform random variable can be decomposed as a sum of a ‘contracted’ uniform random variable and an equi-spaced discrete uniform random variable, and a truncated exponential distribution can be decomposed as a sum of a ‘contracted’ truncated exponential distribution and an equi-spaced truncated geometric distribution. Furthermore, we show that similar conclusions also hold for a discrete uniformly distributed, a geometrically distributed, or a truncated geometrically distributed random variable. We establish that a geometrically distributed random variable can be decomposed as a sum of two independent random variables while satisfying given expectation constraints. Also, a truncated geometrically distributed random variable can be decomposed as a sum of two truncated geometrically distributed random
variables
.

2.

New Capacity Lower Bounds and High-SNR Asymptotics:

Channel Model

A single-user discrete-time optical intensity channel impaired by Gaussian noise is given by

\displaystyle Y=X+Z,

(1)

where
X
denotes the channel input, and
Z
denotes Gaussian noise with variance
\sigma^{2}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2}).

(2)

Since
X
is proportional to
the
optical intensity, its support must satisfy

\displaystyle\mathsf{supp}X\subset\mathbb{R}^{+}.

(3)

Considering the limited dynamic range of LED devices and the requirement
for
illumination quality or energy consumption, the input is usually subject to a peak-intensity constraint:

\displaystyle\textnormal{Pr}(X\leq 1)=1,

(4)

or/and an average-intensity constraint:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[X]\leq\alpha,

(5)

where the constant
\alpha\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right]
, denotes the average intensity constraint.

When
\alpha\in(1/2,1]

In this paper, we mainly consider an optical intensity MAC that has two transmitters and one receiver, and the channel output is given by

\displaystyle Y=X_{1}+X_{2}+Z,

(6)

where

, denotes the channel input from User

Similar

\displaystyle\mathsf{supp}X_{j}\subset\mathbb{R}^{+},\,\,j=1,2.

We assume the input of the MAC is subject to a per-user peak-intensity constraint:

\displaystyle\textnormal{Pr}(X_{j}\leq\mathsf{A}_{j})=1,\,\,j=1,2,

(8)

or/and subject to a per-user average-intensity constraint:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[X_{j}]\leq\mathsf{E}_{j},\,\,j=1,2,

(9)

Without loss of generality,

Proposition 1.

Consider a random variable

{IEEEproof}

\displaystyle\phi_{\sum_{j=1}^{n}B_{j}2^{-j}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1+e^{it2^{-j}}}{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2^{-n}}{1-e^{it2^{-n}}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}({1+e^{it2^{-j}}})({1-e^{it2^{-n}}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2^{-n}(e^{it}-1)}{e^{it2^{-n}}-1}.

By letting

\displaystyle\phi_{\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}B_{j}2^{-j}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{it}-1}{it}

\displaystyle=

(24)

Hence,

\displaystyle U=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}{B_{j}}{2^{-j}}.

Then by the fact

\displaystyle U

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathcal{I}_{a}}{B_{j}}{2^{-j}}+\sum_{j\in{{\mathcal{I}}^{\textnormal{c}}_{a}}}{B_{j}}{2^{-j}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle U_{1}+U_{2}.

a_{-2}&\textnormal{if }n=3,\\
a_{2}&\textnormal{if }n=4,\\
a_{-n+2}+a_{n-2}&\textnormal{if }n\geq 5.\end{cases}

(43)

Then by (

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}b_{j}=1.

(44)

The
n
th remainder term
of

\displaystyle r_{n}=\sum_{j=n+1}^{\infty}b_{j}.

(45)

It is proved in Appendix C that

(46)

By Lemma

(47)

Then by (

Here we

Lemma 3 (

Proposition 5.

We first show a necessary condition on the distributions of the decomposed random variables from a discrete uniform distribution.

Proposition 7 (
[

2.1
]
).

Consider
three positive integers

,
k_{2}
, and

with

and

be independent random variables whose support are the subsets of
[k_{1}]
and
[k_{2}]
, respectively. If the sum of

and

[k]
, then
U_{D_{1}}
and

must be uniformly distributed over their support.

{IEEEproof}

Since
U_{D}
is uniformly distributed over the support
[k]
,
the support of
U_{D_{i}}
must contain
the points

0
and
k_{i}
, otherwise it contradicts the fact

\displaystyle P(x)=\sum_{j\in[k_{1}]}a_{j}x^{j},

(62)

and

\displaystyle Q(x)=\sum_{j\in[k_{2}]}b_{j}x^{j},

(63)

where
a_{j}=\frac{p_{U_{D_{1}}}(j)}{p_{U_{D_{1}}}(0)}
,

,
and
x\in\mathbb{C}
.

To prove

are uniformly distributed over their own support, by the definitions of

and

a_{j}
in (

) and

or

. To do this, we first show that

(and

) is palindromic, i.e.,

.

Since
U_{D}
is uniformly distributed over
the

[k]
, we have

\displaystyle P(x)Q(x)=\sum_{j\in[k]}x^{j}.

(64)

\displaystyle(1-x)P(x)Q(x)=1-x^{k+1}.

(65)

by

. Then

, by (

) we have

, hence

\bar{\gamma}\in\Gamma
. Note that

, we express

in terms of its roots, and yield

\displaystyle P(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{\gamma\in\Gamma}(x-\gamma)

(66)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x^{k_{1}}\prod_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\left(1-\frac{\gamma}{x}\right)

(67)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x^{k_{1}}\prod_{\gamma\in\Gamma}(-\gamma)\prod_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\left({\frac{1}{x}-\frac{1}{\gamma}}\right)

(68)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x^{k_{1}}P(0)\prod_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\left(\frac{1}{x}-\bar{\gamma}\right)

(69)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x^{k_{1}}\prod_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\left(\frac{1}{x}-{\gamma}\right)

(70)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x^{k_{1}}P\left(\frac{1}{x}\right).

(71)

Comparing the coefficients at both sides of

Now we prove that

\displaystyle a_{0}b_{l}+a_{1}b_{l-1}+\cdots+a_{k_{1}}b_{l-k_{1}}=1.

For a single-user

\Pi=\{2lj|j\in[K-1]\}

\displaystyle\mathsf{I}(X_{D};Y)=\mathsf{H}(X_{D})-\mathsf{H}(X_{D}|Y).

Now we lower bound the conditional entropy term

Lemma 4 (

Consider a discrete random variable

\displaystyle\mathsf{H}(X_{D}|X_{D}+Z)\leq R\left(\frac{\sigma}{l}\right),

(90)

where

5
Conclusion and Future Works

In this paper, we propose methods
for
decomposing a continuous or discrete random variable as a sum of two independent random variables. The distribution of the considered random variable achieves the maximum entropy under certain support or moment constraints. With the decomposition results, we derive several capacity lower bounds on the sum-capacity of two-user MAC when the input is subject to the peak- or/and average-intensity constraints. Compared with
the
existing results, at moderate SNR, some bounds give better approximations to the sum-capacity. At high SNR, some bounds are asymptotically tight or fairly close to the sum-capacity, thus determining or improving
evaluations
on
the
high-SNR asymptotic sum-capacity.

In the peak- and average-intensity constrained case
,
in order to apply the decomposition results, some lower bounds are derived by letting channel inputs satisfy slightly tighter constraints than the given ones, which may lose the optimality at high SNR. Evaluations on sum-capacity may be improved by finding admissible and decomposable distributions to make the inputs satisfy exactly the given constraints.

Furthermore, it is worth mentioning that the idea of decomposing a certain random variable (or vector) as a sum of two or several dependent or independent random
variables
(or
vectors
) under certain support or moment constraints
has also implicitly
applied to
some single-user
wireless optical intensity channels
[
17
,
18
,
19
]
. A natural extension of the topic is to apply the idea to other optical intensity networks, such as broadcast channels, interference channels, and relay channels. We leave this for future study.

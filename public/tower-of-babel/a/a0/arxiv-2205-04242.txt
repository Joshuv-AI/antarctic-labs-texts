Explicit Evaluation of Euler-Apéry Type Multiple Zeta Star Values and Multiple t t -Star Values
Ce Xu and Jianqiang Zhao[1mm] a. School of Mathematics and Statistics, Anhui Normal University, Wuhu 241002, PRCb. Department of Mathematics, The Bishop’s School, La Jolla, CA 92037, USA

Abstract




Abstract.
In this paper we establish several recurrence relations about Euler-Apéry type multiple zeta star values and a parametric variant of it by using the method of iterated integrals. Then using the formulas obtained, we find the explicit evaluations for some specific Euler-Apéry type multiple zeta star values and one of its parametric variant, and Euler-Apéry type multiple
t
-star values.

Keywords
: (alternating) multiple zeta (star) values; multiple
t
-(star) values; colored multiple zeta values; iterated integrals; central binomial coefficients; multiple polylogarithm function.

AMS Subject Classifications (2020):
11M06, 11M32.

1
Definition

We begin with some basic notations. Let
\mathbb{Z}
and
\mathbb{N}
be the set of integers and positive integers, respectively. A finite sequence
{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}:=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
is called a
composition
. We put

|{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}|:=k_{1}+\cdots+k_{r},\quad\dep({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}):=r,

and call them the weight and the depth of
{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}
, respectively. If
k_{1}>1
,
{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}
is called
admissible
.

For a composition
{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})
and positive integer
n
, the
multiple harmonic sums
(MHSs) and
multiple harmonic star sums
(MHSSs) are defined by

\displaystyle\zeta_{n}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}):=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq n_{1}>\cdots>n_{r}>0}\displaystyle\frac{1}{n_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots n_{r}^{k_{r}}}\quad\text{and}\quad\zeta^{\star}_{n}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}):=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{r}>0}\displaystyle\frac{1}{n_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots n_{r}^{k_{r}}},

(1.1)

respectively. If
n<k
then
{\zeta_{n}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}):=0
and
{\zeta_{n}}(\emptyset)={\zeta^{\star}_{n}}(\emptyset):=1
. As special cases,

H_{n}:=\zeta_{n}(1)=\zeta^{\star}_{n}(1)\quad\text{and}\quad H_{n}^{(k)}:=\zeta_{n}(k)=\zeta^{\star}_{n}(k)

are the
classical
and
generalized harmonic numbers
, respectively. When taking the limit
n\rightarrow\infty
in (
1.1
) we get the so-called
multiple zeta values
(MZVs) and the
multiple zeta star values
(MZSVs), respectively:

\displaystyle{\zeta}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}):=\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}{\zeta_{n}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}),\quad\text{and}\quad{\zeta^{\star}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}):=\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}{\zeta^{\star}_{n}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}),

defined for admissible compositions
{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}
to ensure convergence of the series. The systematic study of MZVs began in the early 1990s with the works of Hoffman
[
10
]
and Zagier
[
21
]
. Due to their surprising and sometimes mysterious appearance in the study of many branches of mathematics and theoretical physics, these special values have attracted a lot of attention and interest in the past three decades (for example, see the book by the
second author
[
23
]
).

In
[
12
]
, Hoffman introduced and studied odd variants of MZVs and MZSVs, which are
defined for an admissible composition
{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}=(k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{r})
by

\displaystyle t({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}):=\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1}>n_{2}>\cdots>n_{r}>0}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2n_{j}-1)^{k_{j}}}\quad\text{and}\quad t^{\star}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}):=\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1}\geq n_{2}\geq\cdots\geq n_{r}>0}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2n_{j}-1)^{k_{j}}},

(1.2)

and are called a
multiple
t
-value
and
multiple
t
-star value
, respectively.

Then, similar to multiple harmonic sums and multiple harmonic star sums, for a composition
{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})
and positive integer
n
, we define the
multiple t-harmonic sums
and
multiple t-harmonic star sums
respectively by

\displaystyle t_{n}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}):=\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq n_{1}>n_{2}>\cdots>n_{r}>0}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2n_{j}-1)^{k_{j}}}\quad\text{and}\quad t^{\star}_{n}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}):=\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq n_{1}\geq n_{2}\geq\cdots\geq n_{r}>0}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2n_{j}-1)^{k_{j}}}.

In general, for any
{{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}}=(k_{1},\dotsc,k_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
and
N
th roots of unity
z_{1},\dotsc,z_{r}
, we can define the
colored MZVs
(CMZVs) of level
N
as

\Li_{k_{1},\dotsc,k_{r}}(z_{1},\dotsc,z_{r}):=\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1}>\cdots>n_{r}>0}\displaystyle\frac{z_{1}^{n_{1}}\dots z_{r}^{n_{r}}}{n_{1}^{k_{1}}\dots n_{r}^{k_{r}}}

(1.3)

which converges if
(s_{1},z_{1})\neq(1,1)
(see
[
23
, Ch. 15]
), in which case we call
({\bf s};{\bf z})

admissible
. In particular, if all
z_{j}\in\{\pm 1\}
in (
1.4
), then the level two colored MZVs are called
alternating MZVs
(or
Euler sums
). In this case, namely, when
(z_{1},\ldots,z_{r})\in\{\pm 1\}^{r}
and
(k_{1},z_{1})\neq(1,1)
, we set
\zeta(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r};z_{1},\ldots,z_{r})=\Li_{k_{1},\dotsc,k_{r}}(z_{1},\dotsc,z_{r})
. Further, we put a bar on top of

k_{j}
if
z_{j}=-1
. For example,

\zeta(\bar{2},3,\bar{1},4)=\zeta(2,3,1,4;-1,1,-1,1).

More generally, for any composition
(k_{1},\dotsc,k_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
, the
classical multiple polylogarithm function
with
r
-variable is defined by

\displaystyle\Li_{k_{1},\dotsc,k_{r}}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{r}):=\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1}>n_{2}>\cdots>n_{r}>0}\displaystyle\frac{x_{1}^{n_{1}}\dotsm x_{r}^{n_{r}}}{n_{1}^{k_{1}}\dotsm n_{r}^{k_{r}}}

(1.4)

which converges if
|x_{j}\cdots x_{r}|<1
for all
j=1,\dotsc,r
. They can be analytically continued to a multi-valued meromorphic function on
\mathbb{C}^{r}
(see
[
22
]
). In particular, if
x_{1}=x,x_{2}=\cdots=x_{r}=1
, then
\Li_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}}(x,{1}_{r-1})
is the classical multiple polylogarithm function with single-variable. As a convention, we denote by
{1}_{d}
the sequence of 1’s with
d
repetitions. We also define the
multiple t-polylogarithm function
for any composition
{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})
by

\displaystyle\ti_{{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}}(x)

\displaystyle:=\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1}>n_{2}>\cdots>n_{r}>0}\displaystyle\frac{x^{2n_{1}-1}}{(2n_{1}-1)^{k_{1}}(2n_{2}-1)^{k_{2}}\ldots(2n_{r}-1)^{k_{r}}}\quad

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x}\displaystyle\frac{dt}{1-t^{2}}\left(\displaystyle\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{k_{r}-1}\displaystyle\frac{tdt}{1-t^{2}}\left(\displaystyle\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{k_{r-1}-1}\cdots\displaystyle\frac{tdt}{1-t^{2}}\left(\displaystyle\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{k_{1}-1},

where
|x|\leq 1
with
(k_{1},x)\neq(1,1)
. Clearly,
t({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}};1)=t({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}})
with
k_{1}\geq 2
.

Motivated by
[
1
]
, Xu and Wang
[
15
]
studied some Euler-Apéry type series of the form

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{H_{n}^{(k_{1})}H_{n}^{(k_{2})}\cdots H_{n}^{(k_{r})}}{n^{p}}a_{n}^{\pm 1}

(1.5)

where for
a_{n}=\binom{2n}{n}/4^{n}
. For these and some other similar series they established
the corresponding explicit formulas in terms of the alternating MZVs.
In particular, they discovered a few elegant explicit formulas for the series

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{a_{n}}{n^{p}}\,,\quad\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{a_{n}H_{n}^{(m)}}{n^{p}}\,,\quad\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{a_{n}H_{n}H_{n}^{(m)}}{n^{p}}\,,\quad\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{a_{n}H_{n}^{3}}{n^{p}}\,,

and

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{a_{n}\zeta_{n}^{\star}(1_{m})}{n^{p}}\,,\quad\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{a_{n}H_{n}\zeta_{n}^{\star}(1_{m})}{n^{p}}\,,

for
m,p\geq 1
, by using the method of iterated integrals. They also
found some expressions of the Euler-Apéry type series

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{a_{n}^{-1}}{n^{p}}\,,\quad\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{a_{n}^{-1}H_{n}}{n^{p}}\,,\quad\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{a_{n}^{-1}H_{2n}}{n^{p}}\,,\quad\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{a_{n}^{-1}O_{n}}{n^{p}}\,,

for
p\geq 2
, and

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{a_{n}}{n^{p}}\,,\quad\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{a_{n}H_{2n}}{n^{p}}\,,\quad\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{a_{n}O_{n}}{n^{p}}\,,\quad

for
p\geq 1
, by computing the contour integrals related to gamma functions, polygamma functions and
trigonometric functions. Here
O_{n}=\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\displaystyle\frac{1}{2k-1}
are the classical
odd harmonic numbers
. Obviously, by applying the stuffle relations, also called quasi-shuffle relations (see

[
11
]
), we know
that for any composition
{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})
, the product
H_{n}^{(k_{1})}\cdots H_{n}^{(k_{r})}
can be expressed in terms of a linear combination of multiple harmonic (star) sums (for the explicit formula, see
[
17
, Eq. (2.4)]
). For example

H_{n}H_{n}^{(2)}=\zeta_{n}(1)\zeta_{n}(2)=\zeta_{n}(1,2)+\zeta_{n}(2,1)+\zeta_{n}(3).

Hence, we can study the Euler-Apéry type MZVs

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{\zeta_{n}(k_{2},\ldots.k_{r})}{n^{k_{1}}}a_{n}^{\pm 1}

to obtain some explicit evaluations of (
1.5
). Recently, Au
[
1
]
proved the result that for
{{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
, the Euler-Apéry type MZVs above can be expressed in terms of alternating MZVs (but he did not give a general explicit formula), namely,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{\zeta_{n}(k_{2},\ldots.k_{r})}{n^{k_{1}}}a_{n}^{\pm 1}\in\mathsf{CMZV}^{2}_{|{{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}}|}.

Therefore, the Euler-Apéry type series (
1.5
) can be evaluated by alternating MZVs. Some related results may be found in
[
2
,
8
,
13
,
16
]
and references therein. Further, Au showed that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{\zeta_{n}(k_{2},\ldots.k_{r})}{n^{k_{1}}}a_{n}^{-2}\in\mathsf{CMZV}^{4}_{|{{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}}|},\quad\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{\zeta_{n}(k_{2},\ldots.k_{r})}{n^{k_{1}}}a_{n}^{2}\in\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}\mathsf{CMZV}^{4}_{|{{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}}|+1},

where
\mathsf{CMZV}^{N}_{n}
is the
\mathbb{Q}
-span of CMZVs of weight
n
and level
N
.

In this paper, we will study the following Euler-Apéry type MZSVs and MtSVs

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{\zeta_{n}^{\star}(k_{2},\ldots,k_{r})}{n^{k_{1}}}a_{n}=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{\zeta_{n}^{\star}(k_{2},\ldots,k_{r})}{n^{k_{1}}4^{n}}\binom{2n}{n},

(1.6)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{t_{n}^{\star}(k_{2},\ldots,k_{r})}{n^{k_{1}}}a_{n}=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{t_{n}^{\star}(k_{2},\ldots,k_{r})}{n^{k_{1}}4^{n}}\binom{2n}{n},

(1.7)

and a parametric variant of (
1.6
)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{\zeta_{n}^{\star}(k_{2},\ldots,k_{r};x)}{n^{k_{1}}}a_{n}=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{\zeta_{n}^{\star}(k_{2},\ldots,k_{r};x)}{n^{k_{1}}4^{n}}\binom{2n}{n},

(1.8)

where the
parametric multiple harmonic star sum
(PMHSS)
\zeta^{\star}_{n}(k_{1},\cdots,k_{r};x)
is defined by

\displaystyle\zeta_{n}^{\star}(k_{1},\cdots,k_{r};x):=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{r}\geq 1}\displaystyle\frac{x^{n_{r}}}{n^{k_{1}}_{1}\cdots n^{k_{r}}_{r}}\quad\text{and}\quad\zeta^{\star}_{n}(\emptyset;x):=x^{n}.

The primary goal of this paper is to study the explicit relations of (
1.6
) and (
1.8
).
We will use the method of iterated integrals to obtain some recurrence relations of (
1.6
) and (
1.8
),
which in turn will lead to some explicit evaluations of (
1.6
) and (
1.8
).

Euler-Apéry type MZSVs and its parametric variant

The theory iterated integrals was developed first by K.T. Chen in the 1960’s
[
6
,
7
]
. It has played important roles in the study of algebraic topology and algebraic geometry in past half century. Its simplest form is

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}f_{p}(t)dt\cdots f_{1}(t)dt:=\displaystyle\int\limits_{a<t_{p}<\cdots<t_{1}<b}f_{p}(t_{p})\cdots f_{1}(t_{1})\,dt_{1}\cdots dt_{p}.

In this section, we use the iterated integrals to establish two recurrence relations of (
1.6
) and (
1.8
). First, by
[
18

\displaystyle\Li_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}}(x_{1},x_{2}/x_{1}\dotsc,x_{r}/x_{r-1})=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\left(\displaystyle\frac{x_{r}\,dt}{1-x_{r}t}\right)\left(\displaystyle\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{k_{r}-1}\cdots\left(\displaystyle\frac{x_{1}\,dt}{1-x_{1}t}\right)\left(\displaystyle\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{k_{1}-1}.

(2.9)

In particular, CMZVs can be expressed using iterated integrals

\Li_{{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{z}}})=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}{\texttt{x}}_{\xi_{r}}{\texttt{x}}_{0}^{k_{r}-1}\cdots{\texttt{x}}_{\xi_{1}}{\texttt{x}}_{0}^{k_{1}-1},

(2.10)

where
\xi_{j}:=\prod_{i=1}^{j}z_{i}^{-1}
, and
{\texttt{x}}_{\xi}=dt/(\xi-t)

for any
N
th roots of unity
\xi

[

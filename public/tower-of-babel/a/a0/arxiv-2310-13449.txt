Differential Calculus on Hypergraphs and Mayer-Vietoris Sequences for the Constrained Persistent Homology


Abstract
In this paper, we study the discrete differential calculus on hypergraphs by using the Kouzul complexes. We define the constrained (co)homology for hypergraphs and give the corresponding Mayer-Vietoris sequences. We prove the functoriality of the Mayer-Vietoris sequences for the constrained homology and the functoriality of the Mayer-Vietoris sequences for the constrained cohomology with respect to morphisms of hypergraphs induced by bijective maps between the vertices. Consequently, we obtain the Mayer-Vietoris sequences for the constrained persistent (co)homology for filtrations of hypergraphs. As applications, we propose the constrained persistent (co)homology as a tool for the computation of higher-dimensional persistent homology of large networks.

is the sub-hypergraph of
\mathcal{H}
consisting of the
n
-hyperedges
in
\mathcal{H}
and
V\choose{n+1}
is the hypergraph consisting of all the possible
n
-hyperedges
with vertices from
V
. The density of
\mathcal{H}
can be measured by the ratio

\displaystyle m(\mathcal{H})=\frac{|\mathcal{H}|}{|\Delta[V]|}.

Note that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{H}|=\sum_{n\geq 0}|\mathcal{H}_{n}|,~~~~~~|\Delta[V]|=\sum_{n\geq 0}\Big|{V\choose{n+1}}\Big|.

We say that
\mathcal{H}
is
sparse
if
m(\mathcal{H})<<1
and say that

\mathcal{H}
is
dense
if
1-m(\mathcal{H})<<1
.
Similarly, we say that
\mathcal{H}
is
n
-sparse
if
m_{n}(\mathcal{H})<<1
and say that

\mathcal{H}
is
n
-dense
if
1-m_{n}(\mathcal{H})<<1
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a simplicial complex with vertices from
V
.
Let
\sigma\in\Delta[V]\setminus\mathcal{K}
.
We say that
\sigma
is an
external face
of
\mathcal{K}
if

\tau\in\mathcal{K}
for any proper subset

\tau
of
\sigma
.
Let
E(\mathcal{K})
be the set of all the external faces of
\mathcal{K}
.
Let
\mathcal{L}
be an independence hypergraph with vertices from
V
.
Let
\sigma\in\Delta[V]\setminus\mathcal{L}
.
We say that
\sigma
is a
co-external face
of
\mathcal{L}
if

\tau\in\mathcal{L}
for any proper superset

\tau
of
\sigma
, where
\tau
is a subset of
V
.
Let
\bar{E}(\mathcal{L})
be the set of all the co-external faces of
\mathcal{L}
.
Let
p:\Delta[V]\longrightarrow[0,1]
.
Consider the random hypergraph whose probability is given by

\displaystyle\bar{\rm P}_{p}(\mathcal{H})=\prod_{\sigma\in\mathcal{H}}p(\sigma)\prod_{\sigma\notin\mathcal{H}}\big(1-p(\sigma)\big)

where
\mathcal{H}
runs over all hypergraphs with vertices from
V
,
the random simplicial complex whose probability is given by

\displaystyle{\rm P}_{p}(\mathcal{K})=\prod_{\sigma\in\mathcal{K}}p(\sigma)\prod_{\sigma\in E(\mathcal{K})}\big(1-p(\sigma)\big)

where
\mathcal{K}
runs over all simplicial complexes with vertices from
V
,
and the random independence hypergraph whose probability is given by

\displaystyle{\rm Q}_{p}(\mathcal{L})=\prod_{\sigma\in\mathcal{L}}p(\sigma)\prod_{\sigma\in\bar{E}(\mathcal{L})}\big(1-p(\sigma)\big)

where
\mathcal{L}
runs over all independence hypergraphs with vertices from
V
.
Let
\mathcal{H}\sim\bar{\rm P}_{p}
be a randomly generated hypergraph. Then
we have (cf.
[
13
, Section 3]
,
[
33
, Theorem 1.5 (2)]
,
[
31
, Theorem 1.1]
)

(1).

the lower-associated simplicial complex of
\mathcal{H}
is a randomly generated
simplicial complex
\delta\mathcal{H}\sim{\rm P}_{p}
,

(2).

the lower-associated independence hypergraph of
\mathcal{H}
is a randomly generated
independence hypergraph
\bar{\delta}\mathcal{H}\sim{\rm Q}_{p}
,

(3).

the complement of
\mathcal{H}
is a randomly generated hypergraph
\gamma\mathcal{H}\sim\bar{\rm P}_{1-p}
,

(4).

the complement of the associated simplicial complex of
\mathcal{H}
is a randomly generated
independence hypergraph
\gamma\Delta\mathcal{H}\sim{\rm Q}_{1-p}
,

(5).

the complement of the associated independence hypergraph of
\mathcal{H}
is a randomly generated
simplicial complex
\gamma\bar{\Delta}\mathcal{H}\sim{\rm P}_{1-p}
.

We expect to use simplicial complexes as models to represent sparse networks and use independence hypergraphs as models
to represent dense networks. For example, if the networks are randomly generated by
\bar{\rm P}_{p}
,
then we expect the followings:

(1).

Suppose
\frac{p(\sigma)}{|\sigma|}<<1
for
\sigma\in\Delta[V]
such that
\mathcal{H}
is sparse and the
n
-sparsity of
\mathcal{H}
is increasing as
n
grows.
We use the lower-associated simplicial complex
\delta\mathcal{H}\sim{\rm P}_{p}

as well as the associated simplicial complex
\Delta\mathcal{H}
satisfying
\gamma\Delta\mathcal{H}\sim{\rm Q}_{1-p}

to represent
\mathcal{H}
;

(2).

Let
1-\frac{p(\sigma)}{|\sigma|}<<1
for
\sigma\in\Delta[V]
such that
\mathcal{H}
is dense and the
n
-density of
\mathcal{H}
is increasing as
n
grows.
We use
the associated independence hypergraph
\bar{\Delta}\mathcal{H}
satisfying
\gamma\bar{\Delta}\mathcal{H}\sim{\rm P}_{1-p}
as well as the lower-associated independence hypergraph
\bar{\delta}\mathcal{H}\sim{\rm Q}_{p}
to
represent
\mathcal{H}
.

6.2
Localizations of persistent homology

Let
V_{1},V_{2},\ldots,V_{k}
be a partition of
V
, i.e.
V
is the disjoint union of
V_{1},V_{2},\ldots,V_{k}
.
Let
\mathcal{K}
be a simplicial complex with its vertices from
V
.
For each
i=1,2,\ldots,k
, we define the
localized boundary map
with respect to
V_{i}
as a homomorphism of graded
R
-modules

\displaystyle\partial_{n}(w,V_{i}):~~~R_{n}(\mathcal{K})\longrightarrow R_{n-1}(\mathcal{K})

given by

\displaystyle\partial_{n}(w,V_{i})=\sum_{v\in V_{i}}w(v)\frac{\partial}{\partial v}.

We have

\displaystyle\partial_{n}(w)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\partial_{n}(w,V_{i}).

(6.1)

Take the homology group

\displaystyle H_{n}(\mathcal{K},w,V_{i};R)={\rm Ker}(\partial_{n}(w,V_{i}))/{\rm Im}(\partial_{n+1}(w,V_{i})).

(6.2)

We call (
6.1
) the
localization
of the boundary map
\partial_{n}(w)
with respect to
V_{i}
and call (
6.2
) the
localized homology
of
\mathcal{K}
with respect to
w
and
V_{i}
.
It is clear that (
6.2
) is the constrained homology with respect to
\partial_{n}(w,V_{i})\in{\rm Ext}_{1}(V)
.
Let
\mathcal{L}
be an independence hypergraph with its vertices from
V
. For each
i=1,2,\ldots,k
, we define the
localized coboundary map
with respect to
V_{i}
as a homomorphism of graded
R
-modules

\displaystyle\partial^{n}(w,V_{i}):~~~R_{n}(\mathcal{L})\longrightarrow R_{n+1}(\mathcal{L})

given by

\displaystyle\partial^{n}(w,V_{i})=\sum_{v\in V_{i}}w(v)dv.

We have

\displaystyle\partial^{n}(w)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\partial^{n}(w,V_{i}).

(6.3)

Take the cohomology group

\displaystyle H^{n}(\mathcal{L},w,V_{i};R)={\rm Ker}(\partial^{n}(w,V_{i}))/{\rm Im}(\partial^{n-1}(w,V_{i})).

(6.4)

We call (
6.3
) the
localization
of the coboundary map
\partial^{n}(w)
with respect to
V_{i}
and call (
6.4
) the
localized cohomology
of
\mathcal{L}
with respect to
w
and
V_{i}
.
It is clear that (
6.4
) is the constrained cohomology with respect to
\partial^{n}(w,V_{i})\in{\rm Ext}^{1}(V)
.
Represented as matrices, the size of
\partial_{n}(w,V_{i})
is smaller than the size of
\partial_{n}(w)
and the size of
\partial^{n}(w,V_{i})
is smaller than the size of
\partial^{n}(w)
.
In particular, take
w(v)\equiv 1
. The complexities for the computation of the persistent (co)homology (cf.
[
40
]
) would be reduced
if we substitute the usual (co)boundary maps with the localized (co)boundnary maps.

6.3
Perisistent localized (co)homology for hypergraphs

By the functoriality of the constrained homology of simplicial complexes, the localized homology groups

\displaystyle H_{n}(\delta(\mathcal{H}_{x}),w,V_{i};R),~~~~~~x\in\mathbb{R}

together with the induced homomorphisms

\displaystyle(\delta(\iota_{x}^{y}))_{*}:~~~H_{n}(\delta(\mathcal{H}_{x}),w,V_{i};R)\longrightarrow H_{n}(\delta(\mathcal{H}_{y}),w,V_{i};R),~~~~~~-\infty<x\leq y<+\infty,

gives a persistent
R
-module. We call this persistent
R
-module the
localized persistent homology
of the induced filtration
\{\delta(\mathcal{H}_{x})\}_{x\in\mathbb{R}}
of the lower-associated simplicial complexes and denote it as

\displaystyle{\bf PH}_{n}(\delta(\mathcal{H}_{x}),w,V_{i};R\mid x\in\mathbb{R}).

(6.5)

The localized homology groups

\displaystyle H_{n}(\Delta(\mathcal{H}_{x}),w,V_{i};R),~~~~~~x\in\mathbb{R}

together with the induced homomorphisms

\displaystyle(\Delta(\iota_{x}^{y}))_{*}:~~~H_{n}(\Delta(\mathcal{H}_{x}),w,V_{i};R)\longrightarrow H_{n}(\Delta(\mathcal{H}_{y}),w,V_{i};R),~~~~~~-\infty<x\leq y<+\infty,

gives a persistent
R
-module. We call this persistent
R
-module the
localized persistent homology
of the induced filtration
\{\Delta(\mathcal{H}_{x})\}_{x\in\mathbb{R}}
of the associated simplicial complexes and denote it as

\displaystyle{\bf PH}_{n}(\Delta(\mathcal{H}_{x}),w,V_{i};R\mid x\in\mathbb{R}).

(6.6)

For
\{\mathcal{H}_{x}\}_{x\in\mathbb{R}}
representing a filtration of sparse networks,
we expect to use (
6.5
) and (
6.6
) for the computations of the
n
-th persistent homology,
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Note that
(
6.5
) and (
6.6
) are special constrained persistent homology with
\alpha\in{\rm Ext}_{1}(V_{i})
and
m=0
.

By the functoriality of the constrained cohomology of independence hypergraphs, the localized cohomology groups

\displaystyle H^{n}(\bar{\delta}(\mathcal{H}_{x}),w,V_{i};R),~~~~~~x\in\mathbb{R}

together with the induced homomorphisms

\displaystyle(\bar{\delta}(\iota_{x}^{y}))_{*}:~~~H^{n}(\bar{\delta}(\mathcal{H}_{x}),w,V_{i};R)\longrightarrow H^{n}(\bar{\delta}(\mathcal{H}_{y}),w,V_{i};R),~~~~~~-\infty<x\leq y<+\infty,

gives a persistent
R
-module. We call this persistent
R
-module the
localized persistent cohomology
of the induced filtration
\{\bar{\delta}(\mathcal{H}_{x})\}_{x\in\mathbb{R}}
of the lower-associated independence hypergraphs and denote it as

\displaystyle{\bf PH}^{n}(\bar{\delta}(\mathcal{H}_{x}),w,V_{i};R\mid x\in\mathbb{R}).

(6.7)

The localized cohomology groups

\displaystyle H^{n}(\bar{\Delta}(\mathcal{H}_{x}),w,V_{i};R),~~~~~~x\in\mathbb{R}

together with the induced homomorphisms

\displaystyle(\bar{\Delta}(\iota_{x}^{y}))_{*}:~~~H^{n}(\bar{\Delta}(\mathcal{H}_{x}),w,V_{i};R)\longrightarrow H^{n}(\bar{\Delta}(\mathcal{H}_{y}),w,V_{i};R),~~~~~~-\infty<x\leq y<+\infty,

gives a persistent
R
-module. We call this persistent
R
-module the
localized persistent cohomology
of the induced filtration
\{\bar{\Delta}(\mathcal{H}_{x})\}_{x\in\mathbb{R}}
of the associated independence hypergraphs and denote it as

\displaystyle{\bf PH}^{n}(\bar{\Delta}(\mathcal{H}_{x}),w,V_{i};R\mid x\in\mathbb{R}).

(6.8)

For
\{\mathcal{H}_{x}\}_{x\in\mathbb{R}}
representing a filtration of dense networks,
we expect to use (
6.7
) and (
6.8
)
for the computations of the
n
-th persistent cohomology,
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Note that (
6.7
) and (
6.8
) are special constrained persistent cohomology with
\omega\in{\rm Ext}^{1}(V_{i})
and
m=0
.

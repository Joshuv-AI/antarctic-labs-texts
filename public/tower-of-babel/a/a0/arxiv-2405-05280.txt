1.
Motivations

Throughout this paper, we use the notation
\mathbb{N}=\{1,2,\dotsc\}
for the set of positive integers and
\mathbb{N}_{0}=\{0\}\cup\mathbb{N}
for the set of nonnegative integers.

It is well known
[
55
, p. 3]
that the classical Bernoulli polynomials
B_{\ell}(s)
for
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
are generated by

\frac{z\te^{sz}}{\te^{z}-1}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}B_{\ell}(s)\frac{z^{\ell}}{\ell!},\quad|z|<2\pi

and
B_{\ell}(0)=B_{\ell}
are the Bernoulli numbers, which satisfy

B_{0}=1,\quad B_{1}=-\frac{1}{2},\quad B_{2\ell}\neq 0,\quad B_{2\ell+1}=0,

for all
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
.

On 2 January 2024, Qi posed on MathOverflow (
https://mathoverflow.net/q/461427
) the problem of investigating the monotonicity of the ratio
\frac{B_{2\ell+1}(s)}{B_{2\ell+3}(s)}
for
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

Qi’s problem
.

The ratio
\bigl|\frac{B_{2\ell+1}(s)}{B_{2\ell+3}(s)}\bigr|
for
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
is decreasing in
s\in\bigl(0,\frac{1}{2}\bigr)
and increasing in
s\in\bigl(\frac{1}{2},1\bigr)
.

On 3 January 2024, Yang communicated with Qi via WeChat and provided a solution to Qi’s problem, which is essentially the original version of the proof presented in
[
75
, Proposition 1]
.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
75
, Proposition 1]
)
.

The ratio
\frac{B_{2\ell-1}(s)}{B_{2\ell+1}(s)}
for
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
increases in
s\in\bigl(0,\frac{1}{2}\bigr)
and decreases in
s\in\bigl(\frac{1}{2},1\bigr)
.

On 4 January 2024, Iosif Pinelis provided an alternative solution to Qi’s problem, together with some further results, at
https://mathoverflow.net/a/461546
. His proof was subsequently published in
[
42
]
.

In Section
3
, we present the third solution to Qi’s problem, namely, the third proof of Theorem
1.1
. In Section
4
, we establish the monotonicity of three additional ratios

\frac{B_{2\ell}(s)}{B_{2\ell+1}(s)}
,
\frac{B_{2m}(s)}{B_{2\ell}(s)}
, and
\frac{B_{2\ell}(s)}{B_{2\ell-1}(s)}
.
In Section
5
, we derive several new inequalities and recover some known inequalities for the Bernoulli polynomials
B_{\ell}(s)
, the Bernoulli numbers
B_{2\ell}
, and their ratios, such as
\frac{B_{2\ell+2}}{B_{2\ell}}
.

2.
Lemmas and properties of Bernoulli polynomials

To advance our objectives, we first recall two useful lemmas together with essential properties of the Bernoulli polynomials
B_{\ell}(s)
.

2.1.
Two lemmas

The following two lemmas are useful in Section
3
of this paper.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
4
, pp. 10–11, Theorem 1.25]
)
.

For
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
with
\alpha<\beta
, let
q(s)
and
p(s)
be continuous on
[\alpha,\beta]
, differentiable on
(\alpha,\beta)
, and
p^{\prime}(s)\neq 0
on
(\alpha,\beta)
.
If the ratio
\frac{q^{\prime}(s)}{p^{\prime}(s)}
increases in
s\in(\alpha,\beta)
, then both
\frac{q(s)-q(\alpha)}{p(s)-p(\alpha)}
and
\frac{q(s)-q(\beta)}{p(s)-p(\beta)}
increase in
s\in(\alpha,\beta)
.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
41
]
)
.

The Bernoulli polynomial
B_{2\ell}(s)
for
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
has one real zero, denoted by
r_{2\ell}
, on the interval
\bigl(0,\frac{1}{2}\bigr)
. The sequence
r_{2\ell}
increases in
\ell
and tends to
\frac{1}{4}
as
\ell\to\infty
.

Remark 2.1
.

In
[
39
]
, the double inequality
\frac{1}{6}<r_{2\ell}<\frac{1}{4}
was established for
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
. In
[
32
, p. 534]
, a more precise estimate
\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{2^{2\ell+1}\pi}<r_{2\ell}<\frac{1}{4}
was presented for
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
. See also
[
40
, p. 594, Section 24.12(i)]
.

2.2.
Some properties of Bernoulli polynomials

The Bernoulli polynomials
B_{\ell}(s)
have the following properties:

(1)

For
0<s<\frac{1}{2}
, the positivity

(-1)^{\ell+1}B_{2\ell+1}(s)>0,\quad\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

(2.1)

and

(-1)^{\ell+1}B_{2\ell}>0,\quad\ell\in\mathbb{N}

(2.2)

hold. See
[
1
, p. 805, Entry 23.1.14]
and
[
40
, p. 588, Entry 24.2.2]
.

(2)

For
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, the recursive relation

B_{\ell}^{\prime}(s)=\ell B_{\ell-1}(s)

(2.3)

validates. See
[
40
, p. 590, Entry 24.4.34]
.

(3)

For
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, the identity

B_{\ell}(1-s)=(-1)^{\ell}B_{\ell}(s)

(2.4)

validates. See
[
1
, p. 804, Entry 23.1.8]
.

(4)

For
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, the relation

B_{\ell}\biggl(\frac{1}{2}\biggr)=-\biggl(1-\frac{1}{2^{\ell-1}}\biggr)B_{\ell}

(2.5)

holds. See
[
40
, p. 590, Entry 24.4.27]
.

(5)

For
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, the relation

B_{\ell}\biggl(\frac{1}{4}\biggr)=(-1)^{\ell}B_{\ell}\biggl(\frac{3}{4}\biggr)=-\frac{1-2^{1-\ell}}{2^{\ell}}B_{\ell}-\frac{\ell}{4^{\ell}}E_{\ell-1}

(2.6)

validates, where
E_{\ell}
for
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
denotes the Euler numbers generated by

\frac{2\te^{z}}{\te^{2z}+1}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}E_{\ell}\frac{z^{\ell}}{\ell!}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}E_{2\ell}\frac{z^{2\ell}}{(2\ell)!},\quad|z|<\frac{\pi}{2}.

See
[
1
, p. 806, Entry 23.1.22]
,
[
32
, p. 534]
, and
[
40
, p. 590, Entry 24.4.31]
.

(6)

As
\ell\to\infty
, the asymptotic approximations

(-1)^{\lfloor\ell/2\rfloor-1}\frac{(2\pi)^{\ell}}{2(\ell!)}B_{\ell}(s)\sim\begin{cases}\cos(2\pi s),&\ell=2k\\
\sin(2\pi s),&\ell=2k-1\end{cases}

(2.7)

validate uniformly for
s
on compact subsets of
\mathbb{C}
, where
\lfloor\ell/2\rfloor
denotes the floor function whose value is the largest integer less than or equal to
\frac{\ell}{2}
. See
[
40
, p. 594, Entry 24.11.5]
.

(7)

Let
\zeta(z)
denote the Riemann zeta function which is defined by the series
\zeta(z)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\ell^{z}}
under the condition
\Re(z)>1
and by analytic continuation elsewhere. The Bernoulli numbers
B_{2\ell}
and the Riemmann zeta function
\zeta(z)
have the relation

B_{2\ell}=\frac{(-1)^{\ell+1}2(2\ell)!}{(2\pi)^{2\ell}}\zeta(2\ell),\quad\ell\in\mathbb{N}.

(2.8)

See
[
1
, pp. 807–808, Section 23.2]
and
[
55
, p. 5, (1.14)]
.

3.
Third solution to Qi’s problem

In this section, we present the third solution to Qi’s problem, namely the third proof of Theorem
1.1
. In fact, this third proof of Theorem
1.1
was written down slightly earlier than the original version of the proof of
[
75
, Proposition 1]
.

Third proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Define the function

H_{f,g}(s)=\frac{f^{\prime}(s)}{g^{\prime}(s)}g(s)-f(s),\quad s\in(a,b),

(3.1)

where
f(s)
and
g(s)
are two differentiable functions on
(a,b)
such that
g^{\prime}(s)\neq 0
on
(a,b)
. If
f(s)
and
g(s)
are twice differentiable on
(a,b)
, then

\biggl[\frac{f(s)}{g(s)}\biggr]^{\prime}=\frac{g^{\prime}(s)}{g^{2}(s)}\biggl[\frac{f^{\prime}(s)}{g^{\prime}(s)}g(s)-f(s)\biggr]=\frac{g^{\prime}(s)}{g^{2}(s)}H_{f,g}(s)

(3.2)

and

H_{f,g}^{\prime}(s)=\biggl[\frac{f^{\prime}(s)}{g^{\prime}(s)}\biggr]^{\prime}g(s).

The function
H_{f,g}(s)
in (
3.1
) was first introduced in the preprint
[
64
]
, it was called Yang’s
H
-function in
[
56
]
, and it has been applied, for example, in
[
3
,
50
,
57
,
67
,
71
]
and
[
68
, Theorem 2.1]
.

We write

\frac{B_{2\ell-1}(s)}{B_{2\ell+1}(s)}=-\frac{(-1)^{\ell}B_{2\ell-1}(s)}{(-1)^{\ell+1}B_{2\ell+1}(s)}=-\frac{\phi_{\ell}(s)}{\phi_{\ell+1}(s)},\quad\ell\in\mathbb{N},

(3.3)

where, by the positivity in (
2.1
),

\phi_{\ell}(s)=(-1)^{\ell}B_{2\ell-1}(s)>0,\quad\ell\in\mathbb{N}

is clearly valid in
s\in\bigl(0,\frac{1}{2}\bigr)
.

By the formula (
2.3
), we obtain

\phi_{\ell}^{\prime}(s)=(-1)^{\ell}B_{2\ell-1}^{\prime}(s)=(-1)^{\ell}(2\ell-1)B_{2\ell-2}(s),\quad\ell\in\mathbb{N}

(3.4)

and

\phi_{\ell}^{\prime\prime}(s)=(-1)^{\ell}B_{2\ell-1}^{\prime\prime}(s)=(-1)^{\ell}(2\ell-1)(2\ell-2)B_{2\ell-3}(s)\\
=-(2\ell-1)(2\ell-2)\phi_{\ell-1}(s),\quad\ell\geq 2.

(3.5)

Accordingly, the second derivative
\phi_{\ell}^{\prime\prime}(s)
for
\ell\geq 2
is negative in
s\in\bigl(0,\frac{1}{2}\bigr)
, the first derivative
\phi_{\ell}^{\prime}(s)
for
\ell\geq 2
decreases in
s\in\bigl(0,\frac{1}{2}\bigr)
, and the function
\phi_{\ell}(s)
for
\ell\geq 2
is concave in
s\in\bigl(0,\frac{1}{2}\bigr)
.

From the identity (
2.5
), it follows that

\phi_{\ell}^{\prime}(0)=(-1)^{\ell}(2\ell-1)B_{2\ell-2}(0)=(-1)^{\ell}(2\ell-1)B_{2\ell-2}>0

and

\phi_{\ell}^{\prime}\biggl(\frac{1}{2}\biggr)=(-1)^{\ell}(2\ell-1)B_{2\ell-2}\biggl(\frac{1}{2}\biggr)=(-1)^{\ell+1}\biggl(1-\frac{1}{2^{2\ell-3}}\biggr)(2\ell-1)B_{2\ell-2}<0

for
\ell\geq 2
, where we used the positivity in (
2.2
).
As a result, the first derivative
\phi_{\ell}^{\prime}(s)
has a unique zero
s_{\ell,0}\in\bigl(0,\frac{1}{2}\bigr)
for
\ell\geq 2
.

As done in the proof of
[
75
, Proposition 1]
, both
\frac{B_{1}(s)}{B_{3}(s)}
and
\frac{B_{3}(s)}{B_{5}(s)}
increase in
s\in\bigl(0,\frac{1}{2}\bigr)
. Equivalently, both
\frac{\phi_{1}(s)}{\phi_{2}(s)}
and
\frac{\phi_{2}(s)}{\phi_{3}(s)}
decrease in
s\in\bigl(0,\frac{1}{2}\bigr)
.

Assume that, for some
\ell\geq 3
, the ratio
\frac{\phi_{\ell}(s)}{\phi_{\ell+1}(s)}
decreases in
s\in\bigl(0,\frac{1}{2}\bigr)
. By (
3.5
), this inductive hypothesis is equivalent to the decreasing property of the ratio

\frac{\phi_{\ell+1}^{\prime\prime}(s)}{\phi_{\ell+2}^{\prime\prime}(s)}=\frac{(2\ell+1)(2\ell)}{(2\ell+1)(2\ell+2)}\frac{\phi_{\ell}(s)}{\phi_{\ell+1}(s)},\quad\ell\geq 3

in
s\in\bigl(0,\frac{1}{2}\bigr)
, that is,

\biggl[\frac{\phi_{\ell+1}^{\prime\prime}(s)}{\phi_{\ell+2}^{\prime\prime}(s)}\biggr]^{\prime}<0,\quad\ell\geq 3,\quad s\in\biggl(0,\frac{1}{2}\biggr).

This means that

H_{\phi_{\ell+1}^{\prime}(s),\phi_{\ell+2}^{\prime}(s)}^{\prime}(s)=\biggl[\frac{\phi_{\ell+1}^{\prime\prime}(s)}{\phi_{\ell+2}^{\prime\prime}(s)}\biggr]^{\prime}\phi_{\ell+2}^{\prime}(s),\quad\ell\geq 3

has a unique zero
s_{\ell+2,0}\in\bigl(0,\frac{1}{2}\bigr)
and that

H_{\phi_{\ell+1}^{\prime}(s),\phi_{\ell+2}^{\prime}(s)}(s)\geq H_{\phi_{\ell+1}^{\prime}(s),\phi_{\ell+2}^{\prime}(s)}(s_{\ell+2,0})\\
=\frac{\phi_{\ell+1}^{\prime\prime}(s_{\ell+2,0})}{\phi_{\ell+2}^{\prime\prime}(s_{\ell+2,0})}\phi_{\ell+2}^{\prime}(s_{\ell+2,0})-\phi_{\ell+1}^{\prime}(s_{\ell+2,0})=-\phi_{\ell+1}^{\prime}(s_{\ell+2,0})

for some
\ell\geq 3
and
s\in\bigl(0,\frac{1}{2}\bigr)
.

From the equation (
3.4
) and Lemma
2.2
, we see that the zero
s_{\ell+2,0}
is just the zero of the Bernoulli polynomial
B_{2\ell+2}(s)
, that is,
s_{\ell+2,0}=r_{2\ell+2}
, for some
\ell\geq 3
. Similarly, the function
\phi_{\ell+1}^{\prime}(s)
has a unique zero
r_{2\ell}=s_{\ell+1,0}\in\bigl(0,\frac{1}{2}\bigr)
for some
\ell\geq 3
. From Lemma
2.2
, it follows that
r_{2\ell}=s_{\ell+1,0}<s_{\ell+2,0}=r_{2\ell+2}
for some
\ell\geq 3
. Since the first derivative
\phi_{\ell}^{\prime}(s)
for
\ell\geq 2
decreases in
s\in\bigl(0,\frac{1}{2}\bigr)
, we acquire that

\phi_{\ell+1}^{\prime}(s_{\ell+2,0})<\phi_{\ell+1}^{\prime}(s_{\ell+1,0})=0,\quad\ell\geq 2.

Consequently, we conclude
H_{\phi_{\ell+1}^{\prime}(s),\phi_{\ell+2}^{\prime}(s)}(s)>0
for some
\ell\geq 3
and
s\in\bigl(0,\frac{1}{2}\bigr)
. This conclusion means that

\biggl[\frac{\phi_{\ell+1}^{\prime}(s)}{\phi_{\ell+2}^{\prime}(s)}\biggr]^{\prime}=\frac{\phi_{\ell+2}^{\prime\prime}(s)}{[\phi_{\ell+2}^{\prime}(s)]^{2}}H_{\phi_{\ell+1}^{\prime}(s),\phi_{\ell+2}^{\prime}(s)}(s)<0

for some
\ell\geq 3
and
s\in\bigl(0,\frac{1}{2}\bigr)\setminus\{s_{\ell+2,0}\}
, where we used the negativity
\phi_{\ell}^{\prime\prime}(s)<0
for
\ell\geq 2
and
s\in\bigl(0,\frac{1}{2}\bigr)
. Thus, the ratio
\frac{\phi_{\ell+1}^{\prime}(s)}{\phi_{\ell+2}^{\prime}(s)}
for some
\ell\geq 3
decreases on the intervals
(0,r_{2\ell+2})
and
\bigl(r_{2\ell+2},\frac{1}{2}\bigr)
, respectively.

Since

\phi_{\ell}(0)=(-1)^{\ell}B_{2\ell-1}(0)=(-1)^{\ell}B_{2\ell-1}=0

for
\ell\geq 2
, by virtue of Lemma
2.1
and the decreasing property of the ratio
\frac{\phi_{\ell+1}^{\prime}(s)}{\phi_{\ell+2}^{\prime}(s)}
for some
\ell\geq 3
on the interval
(0,r_{2\ell+2})
, we reveal that the ratio

\frac{\phi_{\ell+1}(s)}{\phi_{\ell+2}(s)}=\frac{\phi_{\ell+1}(s)-\phi_{\ell+1}(0)}{\phi_{\ell+2}(s)-\phi_{\ell+2}(0)}

for some
\ell\geq 3
decreases in
s\in(0,r_{2\ell+2})
.
By the identity (
2.4
), it follows that

\phi_{\ell}\biggl(\frac{1}{2}\biggr)=(-1)^{\ell}B_{2\ell-1}\biggl(\frac{1}{2}\biggr)=0

for
\ell\geq 2
. In view of Lemma
2.1
and the decreasing property of the ratio
\frac{\phi_{\ell+1}^{\prime}(s)}{\phi_{\ell+2}^{\prime}(s)}
for some
\ell\geq 3
on the interval
\bigl(r_{2\ell+2},\frac{1}{2}\bigr)
, we arrive at that the ratio

\frac{\phi_{\ell+1}(s)}{\phi_{\ell+2}(s)}=\frac{\phi_{\ell+1}(s)-\phi_{\ell+1}\bigl(\frac{1}{2}\bigr)}{\phi_{\ell+2}(s)-\phi_{\ell+2}\bigl(\frac{1}{2}\bigr)}

for some
\ell\geq 3
decreases in
\bigl(r_{2\ell+2},\frac{1}{2}\bigr)
. Since the ratio
\frac{\phi_{\ell+1}(s)}{\phi_{\ell+2}(s)}
is continuous at the point
r_{2\ell+2}=s_{\ell+2,0}
, which is the zero of
\phi_{\ell+2}^{\prime}(s)
on
\bigl(0,\frac{1}{2}\bigr)
, we conclude that the ratio
\frac{\phi_{\ell+1}(s)}{\phi_{\ell+2}(s)}
decreases in
s\in\bigl(0,\frac{1}{2}\bigr)
for some
\ell\geq 3
.

By induction, the ratio
\frac{\phi_{\ell}(s)}{\phi_{\ell+1}(s)}
decreases in
s\in\bigl(0,\frac{1}{2}\bigr)
for all
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
. Therefore, from the relation (
3.3
), the ratio
\frac{B_{2\ell-1}(s)}{B_{2\ell+1}(s)}
for
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
increases in
s\in\bigl(0,\frac{1}{2}\bigr)
.

By the identity (
2.4
), we obtain

\frac{B_{2\ell-1}(s)}{B_{2\ell+1}(s)}=\frac{B_{2\ell-1}(1-s)}{B_{2\ell+1}(1-s)}

for
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
.
Consequently, from the increasing property of the ratio
\frac{B_{2\ell-1}(s)}{B_{2\ell+1}(s)}
for
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
in
s\in\bigl(0,\frac{1}{2}\bigr)
, we derive that the ratio
\frac{B_{2\ell-1}(s)}{B_{2\ell+1}(s)}
for
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
decreases in
s\in\bigl(\frac{1}{2},1\bigr)
.
The third proof of Theorem
1.1
is complete.
∎

Corollary 3.1
.

For
m,\ell\in\mathbb{N}
such that
m<\ell
, the ratio
(-1)^{\ell-m}\frac{B_{2m-1}(s)}{B_{2\ell-1}(s)}
is positive and decreases in
s\in\bigl(0,\frac{1}{2}\bigr)
, while it is positive and increases in
s\in\bigl(\frac{1}{2},1\bigr)
.

Proof.

From (
2.1
) and (
2.4
), it follows that the ratio
\frac{B_{2\ell-1}(s)}{B_{2\ell+1}(s)}
for
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
is negative in
s\in(0,1)
. Hence, making use of Theorem
1.1
, the function
-\frac{B_{2\ell-1}(s)}{B_{2\ell+1}(s)}
for
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
is positive and decreases in
s\in\bigl(0,\frac{1}{2}\bigr)
, while it is positive and increases in
s\in\bigl(\frac{1}{2},1\bigr)
. Consequently, the ratio

(-1)^{\ell-m}\frac{B_{2m-1}(s)}{B_{2\ell-1}(s)}=\prod_{q=m}^{\ell-1}\biggl[-\frac{B_{2q-1}(s)}{B_{2q+1}(s)}\biggr]

is positive and decreases in
s\in\bigl(0,\frac{1}{2}\bigr)
, while it is positive and increases in
s\in\bigl(\frac{1}{2},1\bigr)
. The required proof is complete.
∎

Corollary 3.2
.

For
\ell>m\in\mathbb{N}
, both of the functions

(-1)^{\ell-m}\frac{B_{2m}(s)-B_{2m}}{B_{2\ell}(s)-B_{2\ell}}\quad\text{and}\quad(-1)^{\ell-m}\frac{B_{2m}(s)-B_{2m}\bigl(\frac{1}{2})}{B_{2\ell}(s)-B_{2\ell}\bigl(\frac{1}{2}\bigr)}

decrease in
s\in\bigl(0,\frac{1}{2}\bigr)
and increase in
s\in\bigl(\frac{1}{2},1\bigr)
.

Proof.

This follows from applying Lemma
2.1
, the identity (
2.4
), and Corollary
3.1
.
∎

4.
Monotonicity of more ratios between Bernoulli polynomials

In this section, we consider three additional ratios

\frac{B_{2\ell}(s)}{B_{2\ell+1}(s)},\quad\frac{B_{2m}(s)}{B_{2\ell}(s)},\quad\frac{B_{2\ell}(s)}{B_{2\ell-1}(s)}

and investigate their monotonicity on the intervals
\bigl(0,\tfrac{1}{2}\bigr)
and
\bigl(\tfrac{1}{2},1\bigr)
, respectively.

Theorem 4.1
.

The ratio
\frac{B_{2\ell}(s)}{B_{2\ell+1}(s)}
for
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
decreases in
s\in\bigl(0,\frac{1}{2}\bigr)
and in
s\in\bigl(\frac{1}{2},1\bigr)
.

For fixed
s\in\bigl(0,\frac{1}{2}\bigr)

(
or for fixed
s\in\bigl(\frac{1}{2},1\bigr)
, respectively
)
, the sequence
\frac{(2\ell+1)B_{2\ell}(s)}{B_{2\ell+1}(s)}
increases
(
or decreases, respectively
)
in
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, with the limit

\lim_{\ell\to\infty}\frac{(2\ell+1)B_{2\ell}(s)}{B_{2\ell+1}(s)}=2\pi\cot(2\pi s),\quad s\in(0,1)\setminus\biggl\{\frac{1}{2}\biggr\}.

(4.1)

Proof.

Let

f_{2\ell}(s)=(-1)^{\ell+1}\frac{B_{2\ell}(s)}{(2\ell)!}\quad\text{and}\quad f_{2\ell+1}(s)=(-1)^{\ell+1}\frac{B_{2\ell+1}(s)}{(2\ell+1)!}

(4.2)

for
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
Employing the recursive relation (
2.3
), we derive

f_{2\ell+1}^{\prime}(s)=f_{2\ell}(s)\quad\text{and}\quad f_{2\ell}^{\prime}(s)=-f_{2\ell-1}(s).

(4.3)

From (
2.1
), we see that the function
f_{2\ell+1}(s)
is positive for
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
0<s<\frac{1}{2}
. Utilizing the relations in (
4.3
) and differentiating yield

\biggl[\frac{f_{2\ell}(s)}{f_{2\ell+1}(s)}\biggr]^{\prime}=\frac{-f_{2\ell-1}(s)f_{2\ell+1}(s)-f_{2\ell}^{2}(s)}{f_{2\ell+1}^{2}(s)}<0

for
s\in\bigl(0,\frac{1}{2}\bigr)
and
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
. Accordingly, the ratio

\frac{f_{2\ell}(s)}{f_{2\ell+1}(s)}=\frac{(2\ell+1)B_{2\ell}(s)}{B_{2\ell+1}(s)},\quad\ell\in\mathbb{N}

decreases in
s\in\bigl(0,\frac{1}{2}\bigr)
.
In particular, it is obvious that the ratio

\frac{f_{0}(s)}{f_{1}(s)}=\frac{B_{0}(s)}{B_{1}(s)}=\frac{2}{2s-1}

decreases in
s\in\bigl(0,\frac{1}{2}\bigr)
. Hence, the ratio
\frac{B_{2\ell}(s)}{B_{2\ell+1}(s)}
for
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
decreases in
s\in\bigl(0,\frac{1}{2}\bigr)
.

Making use of the identity (
2.4
) gives

\frac{B_{2\ell}(s)}{B_{2\ell+1}(s)}=-\frac{B_{2\ell}(1-s)}{B_{2\ell+1}(1-s)}

for
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
s\in\bigl(\frac{1}{2},1\bigr)
. As a result, the ratio
\frac{B_{2\ell}(s)}{B_{2\ell+1}(s)}
for
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
decreases in
s\in\bigl(\frac{1}{2},1\bigr)
.

For
m,\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
with
m<\ell
, it is easy to see that

\frac{f_{2m}(s)}{f_{2m+1}(s)}-\frac{f_{2\ell}(s)}{f_{2\ell+1}(s)}=\frac{f_{2\ell+1}(s)}{f_{2m+1}(s)}\biggl[\frac{f_{2m+1}(s)}{f_{2\ell+1}(s)}\biggr]^{\prime}.

From Corollary
3.1
, it follows that the ratio

\frac{f_{2m+1}(s)}{f_{2\ell+1}(s)}=(-1)^{\ell-m}\frac{(2\ell+1)!}{(2m+1)!}\frac{B_{2m+1}(s)}{B_{2\ell+1}(s)}

s\in\bigl(0,\frac{1}{2}\bigr)
, and is positive and increases in
s\in\bigl(\frac{1}{2},1\bigr)
. Accordingly, we obtain

\biggl[\frac{f_{2m+1}(s)}{f_{2\ell+1}(s)}\biggr]^{\prime}\begin{cases}<0,&s\in\biggl(0,\frac{1}{2}\biggr)\\
>0,&s\in\biggl(\frac{1}{2},1\biggr)\end{cases}

(4.4)

for
m,\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
with
m<\ell
.
Consequently, for given
s\in\bigl(0,\frac{1}{2}\bigr)
(or for given
s\in\bigl(\frac{1}{2},1\bigr)
, respectively), the sequence
\frac{(2\ell+1)B_{2\ell}(s)}{B_{2\ell+1}(s)}
increases (or decreases, respectively) in
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

By virtue of the asymptotic approximations in (
2.7
), we obtain

\frac{(2\ell+1)B_{2\ell}(s)}{B_{2\ell+1}(s)}\sim\frac{(2\ell+1)\cos(2\pi s)}{(-1)^{\lfloor\ell\rfloor-1}\frac{(2\pi)^{2\ell}}{2(2\ell)!}}\frac{(-1)^{\lfloor\ell+1/2\rfloor-1}\frac{(2\pi)^{2\ell+1}}{2(2\ell+1)!}}{\sin(2\pi s)}=2\pi\cot(2\pi s)

as
\ell\to\infty
for
s\in(0,1)\setminus\bigl\{\frac{1}{2}\bigr\}
.
The required proof is complete.
∎

Corollary 4.1
.

For
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, the absolute polynomial

Proof.

On the interval

where we used the equality (
2.3
), which, in light of the first paragraph of Theorem

, decreases in
s\in\bigl(0,\frac{1}{2}\bigr)
for

s\in\bigl(0,\frac{1}{2}\bigr)
for
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

On the interval
\bigl(\frac{1}{2},1\bigr)
, by virtue of the identity (
2.4
) and (

) in sequence, we have

\displaystyle[\ln|B_{2\ell+1}(s)|]^{\prime}=\bigl(\ln\bigl|(-1)^{\ell+1}B_{2\ell+1}(1-s)\bigr|\bigr)^{\prime}=\bigl(\ln\bigl[(-1)^{\ell+1}B_{2\ell+1}(1-s)\bigr]\bigr)^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{\ell+2}B_{2\ell+1}^{\prime}(1-s)}{(-1)^{\ell+1}B_{2\ell+1}(1-s)}=\frac{(-1)^{\ell+2}(2\ell+1)B_{2\ell}(1-s)}{(-1)^{\ell+1}B_{2\ell+1}(1-s)}=-\frac{(2\ell+1)B_{2\ell}(1-s)}{B_{2\ell+1}(1-s)}

∎

Theorem 4.3
.

5.
Inequalities of Bernoulli polynomials and their ratios

In this section, using the monotonicity results established in Sections
3
and
4
, we derive several new inequalities and recover a number of known inequalities for the Bernoulli polynomials
B_{\ell}(s)
, the Bernoulli numbers
B_{2\ell}
, and their ratios such as
\frac{B_{2\ell+2}}{B_{2\ell}}
.

Theorem 5.1
.

For
\ell\geq 2
, the function
\frac{|B_{2\ell+1}(s)|}{s(1/2-s)(1-s)}
increases in
s\in\bigl(0,\frac{1}{2}\bigr)
and decreases in
s\in\bigl(\frac{1}{2},1\bigr)
. Consequently, for
\ell\geq 2
, the double inequality

2(2\ell+1)|B_{2\ell}|<\frac{|B_{2\ell+1}(s)|}{s\bigl(\frac{1}{2}-s\bigr)(1-s)}<4\biggl(1-\frac{1}{2^{2\ell-1}}\biggr)(2\ell+1)|B_{2\ell}|

(5.1)

validates in
s\in\bigl(0,\frac{1}{2}\bigr)
and reverses in
s\in\bigl(\frac{1}{2},1\bigr)
.

For
\ell\geq 2
, the inequality

|B_{2\ell+1}(s)|<\frac{\sqrt{3}\,}{9}\biggl(1-\frac{1}{2^{2\ell-1}}\biggr)(2\ell+1)|B_{2\ell}|

(5.2)

validates for
s\in\bigl(0,\frac{1}{2}\bigr)
.

Proof.

It is straightforward that

B_{3}(s)=s\biggl(\frac{1}{2}-s\biggr)(1-s).

From Corollary
3.1
, we see that the ratio

(-1)^{\ell-2}\frac{B_{3}(s)}{B_{2\ell-1}(s)}=(-1)^{\ell}\frac{s\bigl(\frac{1}{2}-s\bigr)(1-s)}{B_{2\ell-1}(s)},\quad\ell>2

is positive and decreases in
s\in\bigl(0,\frac{1}{2}\bigr)
, meanwhile it is positive and increases in
s\in\bigl(\frac{1}{2},1\bigr)
. The monotonicity in Theorem
5.1
is thus proved.

By virtue of the L’Hôpital rule and the formulas (
2.3
) and (
2.5
), we acquire

\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{B_{2\ell+1}(s)}{s\bigl(\frac{1}{2}-s\bigr)(1-s)}=2\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}(2\ell+1)B_{2\ell}(s)=2(2\ell+1)B_{2\ell}

and

\lim_{s\to\frac{1}{2}}\frac{B_{2\ell+1}(s)}{s\bigl(\frac{1}{2}-s\bigr)(1-s)}=4\lim_{s\to\frac{1}{2}}(2\ell+1)B_{2\ell}(s)\\
=-4(2\ell+1)B_{2\ell}\biggl(\frac{1}{2}\biggr)=4(2\ell+1)\biggl(1-\frac{1}{2^{2\ell-1}}\biggr)B_{2\ell}

for
\ell\geq 2
. Combining these two limits with the above monotonicity yields the double inequality (
5.1
).

Since the polynomial
B_{3}(s)
reaches the maximum value
\frac{\sqrt{3}\,}{36}
at
s=\frac{3-\sqrt{3}\,}{6}
on
\bigl(0,\frac{1}{2}\bigr)
, the right-hand side of the double inequality (
5.1
) becomes

|B_{2\ell+1}(s)|<4\biggl(1-\frac{1}{2^{2\ell-1}}\biggr)(2\ell+1)|B_{2\ell}|s\biggl(\frac{1}{2}-s\biggr)(1-s)\\
\leq 4\biggl(1-\frac{1}{2^{2\ell-1}}\biggr)(2\ell+1)|B_{2\ell}|\frac{\sqrt{3}\,}{36}=\frac{\sqrt{3}\,}{9}\biggl(1-\frac{1}{2^{2\ell-1}}\biggr)(2\ell+1)|B_{2\ell}|

for
\ell\geq 2
and
s\in\bigl(0,\frac{1}{2}\bigr)
. The inequality (
5.2
) is thus proved.
∎

Theorem 5.2
.

For
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, the double inequality

\biggl(1-\frac{1}{2^{2\ell-1}}\biggr)\frac{2\ell+1}{2\pi}|B_{2\ell}|\sin(2\pi s)<|B_{2\ell+1}(s)|<\frac{2\ell+1}{2\pi}|B_{2\ell}|\sin(2\pi s)

validates in
s\in\bigl(0,\frac{1}{2}\bigr)

Proof.

By Corollary
3.1
, we see that the double inequality

(-1)^{\ell-m}\lim_{s\to(\frac{1}{2})^{-}}\frac{B_{2m-1}(s)}{B_{2\ell-1}(s)}<(-1)^{\ell-m}\frac{B_{2m-1}(s)}{B_{2\ell-1}(s)}<(-1)^{\ell-m}\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{B_{2m-1}(s)}{B_{2\ell-1}(s)}

(5.4)

holds on
\bigl(0,\frac{1}{2}\bigr)
for
m,\ell\in\mathbb{N}
such that
m<\ell
. Since, by the L’Hôpital rule,

\lim_{s\to\frac{1}{2}}\frac{B_{2m-1}(s)}{B_{2\ell-1}(s)}=\lim_{s\to\frac{1}{2}}\frac{(2m-1)B_{2m-2}(s)}{(2\ell-1)B_{2\ell-2}(s)}=\frac{(2m-1)B_{2m-2}\bigl(\frac{1}{2}\bigr)}{(2\ell-1)B_{2\ell-2}\bigl(\frac{1}{2}\bigr)}

and

\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{B_{2m-1}(s)}{B_{2\ell-1}(s)}=\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{(2m-1)B_{2m-2}(s)}{(2\ell-1)B_{2\ell-2}(s)}=\frac{(2m-1)B_{2m-2}}{(2\ell-1)B_{2\ell-2}}

for
m,\ell\in\mathbb{N}
such that
m<\ell
, the double inequality (
5.4
) is equivalent to

(-1)^{m}(2m-1)B_{2m-2}\biggl(\frac{1}{2}\biggr)\frac{B_{2\ell-1}(s)}{(2\ell-1)B_{2\ell-2}\bigl(\frac{1}{2}\bigr)}<|B_{2m-1}(s)|\\
<(-1)^{m}(2m-1)B_{2m-2}\frac{B_{2\ell-1}(s)}{(2\ell-1)B_{2\ell-2}}

on
\bigl(0,\frac{1}{2}\bigr)
for
m,\ell\in\mathbb{N}
such that
m<\ell
.

When taking
\ell\to\infty
, by asymptotic approximations in (
2.7
), we obtain

\frac{B_{2\ell-1}(s)}{(2\ell-1)B_{2\ell-2}\bigl(\frac{1}{2}\bigr)}\sim\frac{(-1)^{\lfloor\ell-1/2\rfloor-1}\frac{2(2\ell-1)!}{(2\pi)^{2\ell-1}}\sin(2\pi s)}{(2\ell-1)(-1)^{\lfloor\ell-1\rfloor-1}\frac{2(2\ell-2)!}{(2\pi)^{2\ell-2}}\cos\pi}=-\frac{\sin(2\pi s)}{2\pi}

and

\frac{B_{2\ell-1}(s)}{(2\ell-1)B_{2\ell-2}}\sim\frac{(-1)^{\lfloor\ell-1/2\rfloor-1}\frac{2(2\ell-1)!}{(2\pi)^{2\ell-1}}\sin(2\pi s)}{(2\ell-1)(-1)^{\lfloor\ell-1\rfloor-1}\frac{2(2\ell-2)!}{(2\pi)^{2\ell-2}}\cos 0}=\frac{\sin(2\pi s)}{2\pi}

on
\bigl(0,\frac{1}{2}\bigr)
. Applying these two results to the double inequality (
5.5
) and utilizing (
2.5
) yield

(-1)^{m+1}(2m-1)B_{2m-2}\biggl(\frac{1}{2}\biggr)\frac{\sin(2\pi s)}{2\pi}\\
=(-1)^{m}(2m-1)\biggl(1-\frac{1}{2^{2m-3}}\biggr)B_{2m-2}\frac{\sin(2\pi s)}{2\pi}\\
<|B_{2m-1}(s)|<(-1)^{m}(2m-1)B_{2m-2}\frac{\sin(2\pi s)}{2\pi}

(5.6)

for
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Replacing
m
by
\ell+1

5.3

By the same argument as above and in view of the identity (
2.4
), we can derive the reversed version of (
5.3

\bigl(\frac{1}{2},1\bigr)
for
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

∎

Theorem 5.3

, we have

(-1)^{\ell+1}B_{2\ell}(s)<|B_{2\ell}|\cos(2\pi s),\quad s\in\biggl(0,\frac{1}{4}\biggr)

(5.10)

Proof.

Theorem 5.4

Theorem 5.5
.

For

, the double inequality

\frac{\ell(2\ell-1)}{2\pi^{2}}\biggl(1-\frac{1}{2^{2\ell-3}}\biggr)|B_{2\ell-2}|[1+\cos(2\pi s)]-\biggl(1-\frac{1}{2^{2\ell-1}}\biggr)|B_{2\ell}|\\

validates for

for
\ell>m\geq 2
and

\bigl(1-\frac{1}{2^{2\ell-1}}\bigr)\zeta(2\ell)
is logarithmically concave in
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

The decreasing property of the sequence
\frac{B_{2\ell}(s)}{\ell B_{2\ell-1}(s)}
in
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
for fixed
s\in\bigl(0,\frac{1}{2}\bigr)
and the limit (
4.6
) in Theorem
4.3
imply that, for
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
and
s\in\bigl(0,\frac{1}{2}\bigr)
,

\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{sB_{2\ell}(s)}{\ell B_{2\ell-1}(s)}\geq\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{sB_{2\ell+2}(s)}{(\ell+1)B_{2\ell+1}(s)}\geq-\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{s\cot(2\pi s)}{\pi}

and

\lim_{s\to(\frac{1}{2})^{-}}\frac{\bigl(s-\frac{1}{2}\bigr)B_{2\ell}(s)}{\ell B_{2\ell-1}(s)}\leq\lim_{s\to(\frac{1}{2})^{-}}\frac{\bigl(s-\frac{1}{2}\bigr)B_{2\ell+2}(s)}{(\ell+1)B_{2\ell+1}(s)}\leq-\lim_{s\to(\frac{1}{2})^{-}}\frac{\bigl(s-\frac{1}{2}\bigr)\cot(2\pi s)}{\pi}.

These inequalities are equivalent to, by the L’Hôpital rule and the formula (
2.3
),

\frac{B_{2\ell}}{\ell(2\ell-1)B_{2\ell-2}}\geq\frac{B_{2\ell+2}}{(\ell+1)(2\ell+1)B_{2\ell}}\geq-\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}

(5.28)

and

\frac{B_{2\ell}\bigl(\frac{1}{2}\bigr)}{\ell(2\ell-1)B_{2\ell-2}\bigl(\frac{1}{2}\bigr)}\leq\frac{B_{2\ell+2}\bigl(\frac{1}{2}\bigr)}{(\ell+1)(2\ell+1)B_{2\ell+1}\bigl(\frac{1}{2}\bigr)}\leq-\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}

(5.29)

for
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
. The left-hand sides in the inequalities (
5.28
) and (
5.29
) can be reformulated as

\frac{|B_{2\ell}|/(2\ell)!}{|B_{2\ell-2}|/(2\ell-2)!}\leq\frac{|B_{2\ell+2}|/(2\ell+2)!}{|B_{2\ell}|/(2\ell)!}

and

\frac{\bigl|B_{2\ell}\bigl(\frac{1}{2}\bigr)\bigr|/(2\ell)!}{\bigl|B_{2\ell-2}\bigl(\frac{1}{2}\bigr)\bigr|/(2\ell-2)!}\geq\frac{\bigl|B_{2\ell+2}\bigl(\frac{1}{2}\bigr)\bigr|/(2\ell+2)!}{\bigl|B_{2\ell}\bigl(\frac{1}{2}\bigr)\bigr|/(2\ell)!}

for
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
. Consequently, the sequence
\frac{|B_{2\ell}|}{(2\ell)!}
is logarithmically convex in
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, while the sequence
\frac{|B_{2\ell}(\frac{1}{2})|}{(2\ell)!}
is logarithmically concave in
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
.

Using the relations (
2.8
) and (
2.5
) in sequence leads to

\frac{|B_{2\ell}|}{(2\ell)!}=\frac{2\zeta(2\ell)}{(2\pi)^{2\ell}}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{|B_{2\ell}(\frac{1}{2})|}{(2\ell)!}=2\biggl(1-\frac{1}{2^{2\ell-1}}\biggr)\frac{\zeta(2\ell)}{(2\pi)^{2\ell}}

for
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
. As a result, the sequence
\zeta(2\ell)
is logarithmically convex in
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, while the sequence
\bigl(1-\frac{1}{2^{2\ell-1}}\bigr)\zeta(2\ell)
is logarithmically concave in
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
. The required proof is complete.
∎

6.
Conclusions

It is well known that the Bernoulli numbers
B_{2\ell}
and the Bernoulli polynomials
B_{\ell}(s)
are fundamental objects in mathematics and the mathematical sciences. Their equalities, identities, inequalities, explicit and closed-form formulas, recurrence relations, connections with other special numbers and polynomials such as the Euler and Stirling numbers and polynomials, integral representations, determinantal expressions, generalizations, analogues, and related topics have been extensively and insightfully investigated by numerous mathematicians over the past centuries. In particular, regarding inequalities for the Bernoulli numbers
B_{2\ell}
, we summarize them as follows:

(1)

Inequalities that bound the Bernoulli numbers
B_{2\ell}
; see the brief review in the first section of
[
43
]
and the papers
[
15
,
36
,
53
]
.

(2)

Inequalities that bound the ratios
\frac{B_{2\ell+2}}{B_{2\ell}}
of the Bernoulli numbers
B_{2\ell}
; see the initial paper
[
43
]
and subsequent works
[
12
,
36
,
44
,
73
,
83
]
.

In this work, we established the monotonicity of the four ratios

\frac{B_{2\ell-1}(s)}{B_{2\ell+1}(s)},\quad\frac{B_{2\ell}(s)}{B_{2\ell+1}(s)},\quad\frac{B_{2m}(s)}{B_{2\ell}(s)},\quad\frac{B_{2\ell}(s)}{B_{2\ell-1}(s)}

(6.1)

of the Bernoulli polynomials
B_{\ell}(s)
, derived several new inequalities, and recovered a number of known inequalities for the Bernoulli polynomials

, the Bernoulli numbers

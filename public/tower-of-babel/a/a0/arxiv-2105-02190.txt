Abstract

We give a partial classification of the
a,b,c\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
and
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
for which the equation
ax+by=cw^{m}z^{n}
is partition regular (PR) over
\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
. We show that if
m,n\geq 2
, then
ax+by=cw^{m}z^{n}
is PR over
\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
if and only if
a+b=0
. Next, we show that if
n
is odd, then the equation
ax+by=cwz^{n}
is PR over
\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
if and only if one of
\frac{a}{c},\frac{b}{c},
or
\frac{a+b}{c}
is an
n
th power in
\mathbb{Q}
, and we come close to a similar characterization for even
n
. We also obtain some results about when a system of equations of the form
a_{i}x_{i}+b_{i}y_{i}=c_{i}w_{i}z_{i}^{n},1\leq i\leq k
, is PR over an integral domain
R
. In order to show that the equation
ax+by=cwz^{n}
is not PR over
\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
for certain values of
a,b,c,
and
n
, we prove a partial generalization of the criteria of Grunwald and Wang for when
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}
is an
n
th power modulo every prime
p
. In particular, we show that for any odd
n
and any
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{Q}
that are not
n
th powers, there exist infinitely many primes
p\in\mathbb{N}
for which none of
\alpha,\beta,
and
\gamma
are
n
th powers modulo
p
, and we obtain a similar but weaker result for even
n
. In order to show that for an integral domain
R
the equation
ax+by=cwz^{n}
is PR over
R\setminus\{0\}
for certain values of
a,b,c
and
n
, we use ultrafilters and the algebra of the Stone-C̆ech Compactification. This necessitates us to prove some new results of independent interest about ultrafilters
v\in\beta R
for which every
A\in v
is additively and multiplicatively central.

Introduction

We say that an equation is
partition regular
over a set
S
if for any finite partition
S=\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}C_{i}
, there exists some
C_{i}
containing a solution to the equation. One of the first results about partition regular diophantine equations is the celebrated theorem of Schur
[
23

, which established the partition regularity of
x+y=z
over
\mathbb{N}

[
21

classified which finite systems of linear homogeneous equations are partition regular over
\mathbb{N}
. Since it is not known whether or not the equation
x^{2}+y^{2}=z^{2}
is partition regular over
\mathbb{N}
, we are still far from achieving a classification for which systems of polynomial equations are partition regular. One of the first results in this direction is a theorem of Bergelson
[
2

x-y=p(z)
is partition regular over
\mathbb{N}
when

is a polynomial with integer coefficients which satisfies
p(0)=0

x-y=z^{2}
is partition regular over
\mathbb{N}
, Csikvári, Gyarmati and Sárkozy
[
8

showed that the equation
x+y=z^{2}
is
not
partition regular over
\mathbb{N}
(see also
[
10
]
), and asked whether the equation
x+y=wz
is partition regular over
\mathbb{N}
. Their question was answered in the positive, independently by Bergelson
[
3
, Section 6]
and Hindman
[
12
]
. Both proofs make use of ultrafilters and the algebra of the Stone-Čech compactifation. More examples of polynomial equations whose partition regularity is known can be found in
[
1
]
,
[
6
]
,
[

]
,
[

[

The polynomial equations mentioned so far have a simple form, but the proofs of their partition regularity properties are quite specific to these cases.
Since there is currently no unified theory for the partition regularity of polynomial equations, any class of equations whose partition regularity is known may provide insight towards such a theory. In this paper, we give a partial classification of the partition regularity of equations of the form
ax+by=cw^{m}z^{n}
, where
a,b,c\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
and
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
are parameters, and

are variables. Theorem
1
is the main result of this paper. Before stating Theorem
1

in order to avoid trivial solutions. We also recall that any equation which is partition regular over a set

(such as

that contains

2
Preliminaries

In this section we review some facts and useful tools in the study of partition regular equations.

Definition 1
.

Given a set
S
, a ring
R
, and functions
f_{1},\cdots,f_{k}:S^{n}\rightarrow R
, the system of equations

\begin{array}[]{ccc}f_{1}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})&=&0\\
f_{2}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})&=&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
f_{k}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})&=&0\end{array}

(6)

is
partition regular over S
if for any finite partition
S=\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}C_{i}
, there exists
1\leq i_{0}\leq r
and
x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\in C_{i_{0}}
which satisfy (
6
). If the set
S
is understood from context, then we simply say that the system of equations is
partition regular
.

Definition 2
.

Let
R
be an integral domain with field of fractions
K
. A matrix
{\bf M}\in\mathrm{M}_{m\times n}(R)
satisfies the
columns condition
if there exists a partition
C_{1},\cdots,C_{k}
of the column indices such that for
\vec{s}_{i}=\sum_{j\in C_{i}}\vec{c}_{j}
we have

(i)

\vec{s}_{1}=(0,\cdots,0)^{T}
;

(ii)

for all
i\geq 2
, we have

\vec{s}_{i}\in\text{Span}_{K}\{\vec{c}_{j}\ |\ j\in C_{\ell},1\leq\ell<i\}.

(7)

The columns condition was used by Rado to classify when a finite system of homogeneous linear equations is partition regular.

Theorem 3

(
[
21
]
)
.

Given
{\bf M}\in\mathrm{M}_{m\times n}(\mathbb{Z})
, the system of equations

{\bf M}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})^{T}=0

(8)

is partition regular over
\mathbb{N}
if and only if
{\bf M}
satisfies the columns condition.

Corollary 1
.

For
a_{1},\cdots,a_{s}\in\mathbb{Z}
, the equation

a_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+a_{s}x_{s}=0

(9)

is partition regular over
\mathbb{N}
if and only if there exists
\emptyset\neq F\subseteq[1,s]
for which
\sum_{i\in F}a_{i}=0
.

Rado also characterized which finite, not necessarily homogeneous, linear systems of equations are partition regular.

Theorem 4

(
[
21
]
)
.

Given
{\bf M}\in M_{m\times n}(\mathbb{Z})
and
(b_{1},\cdots,b_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, the equation

{\bf M}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})^{T}=(b_{1},\cdots,b_{n})^{T}

(10)

is partition regular over
\mathbb{Z}
if and only if Equation (
10
) admits an integral solution in which
x_{1}=x_{2}=\cdots=x_{n}
.

We point out to the reader that in Theorem
4
it is possible to only obtain partition regularity in a trivial sense. For example, since
(x,y)=(0,0)
is the only solution of the system of equations

\begin{array}[]{ccrcc}x&-&y&=&0{\color[rgb]{1,1,1},}\\
x&-&2y&=&0,\end{array}

(11)

we see that the system is not partition regular over
\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
even though it is partition regular over

The theory of ultrafilters has been very useful in the study of Ramsey theory and partition regular equations. We briefly recall some basic facts here and give a more detailed introduction in Section
9
.

Definition 3
.

Given a set
S
let
\mathcal{P}(S)
be the power set of
S
. A collection of sets
p\subseteq\mathcal{P}(S)
is an
ultrafilter
over
S
if it satisfies the following properties:

(i)

The empty set is not in
p
.

(ii)

If
A\in p
and
A\subseteq B
then
B\in p
.

(iii)

If
A,B\in p
then
A\cap B\in p
.

(iv)

For any
A\subseteq S
, either
A\in p
or
A^{c}\in p
.

We let
\beta S
denote the space of all ultrafilters over
S
.

It is often useful to think about
\beta S
as the set of finitely additive
\{0,1\}
-valued measures on the set
S
. For now, we only require the following facts about ultrafilters. First, we see that for any finite partition of
S=\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}C_{i}
and any ultrafilter
p
, there exists
1\leq i_{0}\leq r
for which
C_{i}\in p
if and only if
i=i_{0}
. In fact, for any
A\in p
and any finite partition
A=\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}C_{i}
, there exists
1\leq i_{0}\leq r
for which
C_{i}\in p
if and only if
i=i_{0}
. Secondly, we note that if
p
is an ultrafilter,
A\in p
and
B\notin p
, then
B^{c}\in p
, so
A\setminus B=A\cap B^{c}\in p
. Lastly, we require the existence of a special kind of ultrafilter.

Theorem 6

(cf. Theorem
25
)
.

There exists an ultrafilter
p\in\beta\mathbb{N}
with the following properties.

(i)

For any
A\in p
and
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists

with

.

(ii)

For any
A\in p
and

, there exists

for which

.

(iii)

For any

, we have

.

The proof of Theorem

Lemma 2

A short proof of Lemma

can be obtained through the

by viewing the multiplicative group

as a

Definition 4
.

Let
p\in\mathbb{N}
be a prime. The
p
-adic valuation
is given by
v_{p}(n)=k
, where
n=p^{k}m
and
p\nmid m
for

. Since
v_{p}(mn)=v_{p}(m)+v_{p}(n)
for all

to a well-defined function on

v_{p}\left(\frac{t}{s}\right)=v_{p}(t)-v_{p}(s)
for all
\frac{t}{s}\in\mathbb{Q}\setminus\{0\}
.

Proof of Lemma
2
.

For the first direction, let us assume that
\frac{a}{c}
is not an
n
th power in
\mathbb{Q}
, and let
p
be a prime for which
n\nmid v_{p}(\frac{a}{c})
. Let
\mathbb{Q}\setminus\{0\}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}C_{i}
be the partition given by

C_{i}=\left\{\frac{t}{s}\in\mathbb{Q}\setminus\{0\}\ |\ v_{p}\left(\frac{t}{s}\right)\equiv i\pmod{n}\right\}.

(17)

We see that if
w,x,z\in C_{i_{0}}
for some
1\leq i_{0}\leq n
, then

v_{p}(ax)-v_{p}\left(cwz^{n}\right)\equiv v_{p}\left(\frac{a}{c}\right)+v_{p}(x)-v_{p}(w)-nv_{p}(z)\equiv v_{p}\left(\frac{a}{c}\right)\not\equiv 0\pmod{n},

(18)

so we cannot have
ax=cwz^{n}
.

For the next direction, let us assume that
\frac{a}{c}=(\frac{u}{v})^{n}
for some coprime
u,v\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
. Let
p
be an ultrafilter satisfying the conditions of Theorem
6
and let
\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}C_{i}
be a partition. By condition (i) of Theorem
6

A\in p
, there exists

for which
x=wz^{n}
. We observe that

v\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}\frac{v}{u}(C_{i}\cap u\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\})

(19)

is a partition, so we may assume without loss of generality that
\frac{v}{u}(C_{1}\cap u\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\})\in p
. It follows that there exist

for which

\frac{v}{u}x=\left(\frac{v}{u}w\right)\left(\frac{v}{u}z\right)^{n}\text{; hence }x=\left(\frac{v}{u}\right)^{n}wz^{n}=\frac{c}{a}wz^{n}\text{, and thus }ax=cwz^{n}.

(20)

The desired result follows from
\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}\subseteq\mathbb{Q}\setminus\{0\}

4
Number Theoretic Results

In this section we assume that the reader has had an introduction to algebraic number theory. Specifically, we assume familiarity with the content appearing in Chapters I, II, and IV of
[
15
]
and the Chebotarev Density Theorem. The main goal of this section is to prove Theorem 2. The reader willing to take the existence of such primes on faith can safely skip this section and the algebraic number theory content appearing here. We first handle the odd exponent case as some aspects of the argument are simplified and very general. Afterwards, we add a few details to handle the even exponent case.

We briefly recall some of the concepts we will need. We call
K
a number field if it is a finite field extension of
\mathbb{Q}
. We write
\mathcal{O}_{K}
for the ring of integers of
K
, which is the integral closure of
\mathbb{Z}
in
K
. This is a Dedekind domain, so nonzero ideals factor uniquely into a product of prime ideals.

Given an extension of number fields
L/K
, one can ask how a prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}
factors in
\mathcal{O}_{L}
. We have
\mathfrak{p}\mathcal{O}_{L}=\mathfrak{q}_{1}^{e_{1}}\cdots\mathfrak{q}_{g}^{e_{g}}
for some prime ideals
\mathfrak{q}_{i}\subset\mathcal{O}_{L}
. Recall that
e(\mathfrak{q}_{i}/\mathfrak{p}):=e_{i}
is the ramification degree of
\mathfrak{q}_{i}
over
\mathfrak{p}
and
f_{i}=f(\mathfrak{q}_{i}/\mathfrak{p}):=[\mathcal{O}_{L}/\mathfrak{q}_{i}:\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{p}]
is the inertia degree of
\mathfrak{q}_{i}
over
\mathfrak{p}
. We say
\mathfrak{p}
is unramified in
\mathcal{O}_{L}
, or just in
L
, if
e_{i}=1
for all
i
. It is a fact that only finitely many prime ideals of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
are ramified in
L
.

These invariants are bounded via the following classic formula:

[L:K]=\sum_{i=1}^{g}e_{i}f_{i}.

(40)

In the case
L/K
is Galois, we have all of the
e_{i}
’s and
f_{i}
’s are equal, so in fact
e_{i}
and
f_{i}
both divide
[L:K]
.

To compute these numbers in practice, one uses modular arithmetic and factoring polynomials. The process can be summarized as follows. Suppose
L=K(\alpha)
with
\alpha\in\mathcal{O}_{L}
. Then
\mathcal{O}_{K}[\alpha]\subset\mathcal{O}_{L}
and both are finite free
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-modules of rank
[L:K]
, so
[\mathcal{O}_{L}:\mathcal{O}_{K}[\alpha]]
is finite. If the residue characteristic of
\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}
does not divide
[\mathcal{O}_{L}:\mathcal{O}_{K}[\alpha]]
, then the factorization behavior of
\mathfrak{p}
in
\mathcal{O}_{L}
can be detected by factoring the minimal polynomial of
\alpha\bmod\mathfrak{p}
.

More precisely, let
f(x)\in\mathcal{O}_{K}[x]
be the minimal polynomial for
\alpha
. Then, under the divisibility assumption above, we have

\mathfrak{p}\mathcal{O}_{L}=\mathfrak{q}_{1}^{e_{1}}\cdots\mathfrak{q}_{g}^{e_{g}}\iff f(x)\equiv q_{1}(x)^{e_{1}}\cdots q_{g}(x)^{e_{g}}\bmod\mathfrak{p}

(41)

and
\deg(q_{i}(x))=f(\mathfrak{q}_{i}/\mathfrak{p})
. Hence for all but finitely many
\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}
, its factoring behavior in
\mathcal{O}_{L}
is detected by factoring
f(x)\bmod\mathfrak{p}
.

When
L/K
is Galois with group
G
, there is an important relationship between the arithmetic and algebra of the fields expressed via Frobenius elements. Suppose
\mathfrak{q}\subset\mathcal{O}_{L}
divides
\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}
. Then there exists a unique
\Frob_{\mathfrak{q}/\mathfrak{p}}\in G
defined by the property

\Frob_{\mathfrak{q}/\mathfrak{p}}(x)\equiv x^{q}\bmod\mathfrak{q}

(42)

where
q=\#\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{p}
. If
\mathfrak{q}
and
\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}
are primes dividing
\mathfrak{p}
in
\mathcal{O}_{L}
, then
\Frob_{\mathfrak{q}/\mathfrak{p}}
and
\Frob_{\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}/\mathfrak{p}}
are conjugate. Conversely, for every
\sigma\in G
in the conjugacy class of
\Frob_{\mathfrak{q}/\mathfrak{p}}
, there exists
\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}
dividing
\mathfrak{p}
so that
\sigma=\Frob_{\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}/\mathfrak{p}}
. Thus we can speak of a well-defined Frobenius conjugacy class
\Frob_{\mathfrak{p}}\subset G
. When
G
is abelian, Frobenius elements associated to primes in
K
are therefore well-defined.

In order for this to be useful to us, we need a way to construct primes with given Frobenius elements. The major tool for achieving this is the Chebotarev density theorem.

Theorem 9

(Chebotarev Density Theorem)
.

Let
L/K
be a Galois extension of number fields. Let
C\subset G=\Gal(L/K)
be a fixed conjugacy class. Then the natural density of primes
\mathfrak{p}\subset K
with
\Frob_{\mathfrak{p}}\in C
is given by
\#C/\#G
, i.e.,

\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\#\{\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{O}_{K}:\#\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{p}\leq x\mathrm{,and}\Frob_{\mathfrak{p}}\in C\}}{\#\{\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{O}_{K}:\#\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{p}\leq x\}}=\frac{\#C}{\#G}.

(43)

Fix
n>1
. We turn our attention to studying the polynomial

for

and its factoring behavior modulo various primes in
\mathcal{O}_{K}
. Suppose

is not an

th power. Let

for
\alpha\in\mathcal{O}_{K(\zeta_{n})}
with

maximal. Set

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L=C(\alpha^{1/m})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C=K(\zeta_{n})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K}

where

We can now prove the main existence result.

Lemma 9
.

Let

(ii)

Proof.

Recall that

\displaystyle\leq 1/m_{a}+1/m_{b}+1/m_{c}-1/(m_{a}\cdot m_{b})-1/(m_{b}\cdot m_{c}),

(ii)

When

•

(1/

•

(1/

(50)

•

(1/2, 1/2, 1/2): This case is genuine cause for concern. For example, it is possible to have 3 quadratic extensions of a field where every prime in the base splits in at least one of them (consider

•

Otherwise, we have already

In all cases we obtain that the density of primes

In the case

Theorem 2
.

Let
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{Q}
.

(i)

Suppose

is odd and
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
are not
n
th powers; or

(ii)

Suppose

is even,
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
are not
\frac{n}{2}
th powers, and
\alpha
is not an
\frac{n}{4}
th power if

.

Then there exists infinitely many primes
p\in\mathbb{N}
modulo which none of
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
are
n
th powers.

Proof.

Let

be such that for
\alpha^{\prime}:=c^{n}\alpha,\beta^{\prime}:=c^{n}\beta,
and
\gamma^{\prime}:=c^{n}\gamma
we have
\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}
. Since
\omega_{n}=1
when

is odd and
\omega_{n}=2
when

is even we may apply Lemma

to find infinitely many primes

for which
\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime},
and
\gamma^{\prime}
are not
n
th powers modulo

. If

is a prime for which

, then
\alpha^{\prime}
is an
n
th power modulo

if and only if
\alpha
is an
n
th power modulo

, and similarly for
\beta^{\prime}
and
\gamma^{\prime}
. It follows that there are infinitely many primes

for which
\alpha,\beta,
and
\gamma
are also not
n
th powers modulo

.
∎

Remark 3
.

When working over
\mathbb{Q}
, as long as
n\neq 4,8
, when specialized to our situation where

, the condition that one of the three numbers not be an
\frac{n}{4}
th power is automatic by Fermat’s Last Theorem.

Now we give a few more remarks about the subtleties that arose in this proof, and what obstacles prevent pushing it further, both over
\mathbb{Q}
and for general number fields
K
.

(i)

It could happen that
x^{n}-a
has a root mod all primes
\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}
even though
a
is not an
n
th power. For example, this happens when
K=\mathbb{Q}
; the polynomial
x^{8}-16
has a root mod
p
for all primes
p\in\mathbb{Z}
, but 16 is not an
8
th power (see
[
26
]
). The Grunwald-Wang theorem says that if
x^{n}-a
has a root mod
p
for all primes
p
, then
n
must be even of a special form, determined by
K
. This is ruled out above since
16
is a perfect
8/2=4
th power.

(ii)

Even if one were to exclude the situation arising in the Grunwald-Wang theorem, it is possible that
(x^{n}-a)(x^{n}-b)
could have a root mod
p
for all primes
p
, despite neither factor having this behavior. We consider
3
such examples when
K=\mathbb{Q}
, each of which also suggests the necessity of the conditions in item (ii) of Lemma
9
and item (ii) or Theorem 2.

(a)

The polynomial
(x^{12}-3^{6})(x^{12}-\beta^{4})
has this feature for any
\beta\in\mathbb{N}
, because
(x^{2}+3)(x^{3}-\beta)
has a root mod
p
for all
p\in\mathbb{Z}
. In particular, we see that
x^{2}+3
has a root modulo
p
if
p\equiv 0,1\pmod{3}
and
x^{3}-\beta
has a root modulo
p
if
p\equiv 2\pmod{3}
. The problem here is that
x^{12}-3^{6}
has a quadratic factor, which lives inside
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{12})
. In the language above, this means that
e=1
, so the density argument won’t work. (see
[
14
]
)

(b)

Since
x^{8}-16=(x^{4}-4)(x^{4}+4)
and
x^{8}-16
has a root modulo
p
for any prime
p\in\mathbb{N}
, one of
4
and
-4
is a
4
th power modulo
p
.

(c)

Since
36
is a fourth power modulo
p
for any prime
p\not\equiv 13\pmod{24}
and
9
is a fourth power modulo
p
for any prime
p\equiv 13\pmod{24}
, we see that
(x^{4}-36)(x^{4}-9)
has a root modulo
p
for any prime
p\in\mathbb{N}
.

For general
K
, one imagines it only gets harder to determine whether
x^{n}-a
has a factor whose splitting field lies in a cyclotomic extension. The proof above implies that if
K
does not have many roots of unity, then there are mild conditions on

to get the existence of the desired prime.

(iii)

When
K
doesn’t have unique factorization, we lose some of the power afforded by Lemma
7
because we cannot ensure that if
a^{x}=b^{y}
, then
a
is a perfect
y/(x,y)
th power. We can pass to ideals generated by
a
and
b
, and use the fact that
\mathcal{O}_{K}
is Dedekind, so its ideals satisfy unique factorization. But this only says that the ideal generated by
a
is a perfect
y/(x,y)
th power, and this need not imply

has the same property. For example, if
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-5})
and
\mathfrak{p}=(2,1+\sqrt{-5})\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}
, then
\mathfrak{p}^{2}=(2)
, but
2\neq\alpha^{2}
for any
\alpha\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
.

(iv)

When
K
has lots of units, it can also be difficult to deduce that
a
is a perfect
y/(x,y)
th power given
a^{x}=b^{y}
. For example, the ideal generated by
(-4)
in
\mathbb{Z}
is the square of the ideal
(2)
, but
-4
is of course not a perfect square. It is possible one could say more here by trying to control the units of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
, but this seems a bit intimidating and not of immediate interest.

We see that Theorem 2 is not useful when
n=2
or
n=4
, so we will address the case of
n=2
separately and observe the implications that it has for the case of
n=4
. We first require a lemma for constructing primes
p
for which certain numbers are not squares modulo

.

Lemma 10
.

Suppose that
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{Q}
are not squares, and
\alpha\beta\gamma
is also not a square. There exists a prime
p\in\mathbb{N}
for which
\alpha,\beta,
and
\gamma
are not squares modulo
p
.

Proof.

Suppose on the contrary that no such prime exists. Let
\left(\frac{a}{p}\right)
be the Legendre symbol and let
S_{\alpha,\beta}
be the set of primes
p
for which

\left(\frac{\alpha}{p}\right)=\left(\frac{\beta}{p}\right)=-1.

(52)

By our assumption, we have
\left(\frac{\gamma}{p}\right)=1
for all
p\in S_{\alpha,\beta}
since otherwise we would have produced a prime with the desired features. Using similar notation for the other pairs, we see that
S_{\alpha,\beta}\cap S_{\beta,\gamma}=\varnothing
because for primes
p
in the first set, we have
\left(\frac{\alpha}{p}\right)=-1
but for
p
in the second set we have
\left(\frac{\alpha}{p}\right)=1
.

Thus the density of
S:=S_{\alpha,\beta}\cup S_{\beta,\gamma}\cup S_{\gamma,\alpha}\geq 3/4
as the density of each
S_{\alpha,\beta}
is at least
1/4
by Quadratic Reciprocity. But for each
p\in S
, we have

\left(\frac{\alpha\beta\gamma}{p}\right)=1.

(53)

Hence a set of primes of density
>1/2
split in the extension
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{\alpha\beta\gamma})
. But by Quadratic Reciprocity, the set of primes which split in a degree 2 extension has density 1/2. Thus we have
[\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{\alpha\beta\gamma}):\mathbb{Q}]=1
, so we must have
\alpha\beta\gamma
is a square in
\mathbb{Q}
, contradicting the original assumption.
∎

Theorem 15
.

Let
R
be a Dedekind domain with field of fractions
K
. Let
a,b,c\in R\setminus\{0\}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
be such that none of
\frac{a}{c},\frac{b}{c},
or
\frac{a+b}{c}
are
n
th powers in
R/\mathfrak{p}
for some prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}\subseteq R
satisfying
a,b,a+b,c\notin\mathfrak{p}
and
[R:\mathfrak{p}]<\infty
. Let
K_{\mathfrak{p}}
denote the completion of
K
at
\mathfrak{p}
. The equation

ax+by=cwz^{n}

(127)

is not partition regular over
K_{\mathfrak{p}}\setminus\{0\}
.

Proof.

Since
R
is a Dedekind domain we see that
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a discrete valuation ring under the valuation
v_{\mathfrak{p}}
, so let
\pi
be a generator of the maximal ideal of
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Let
F\subseteq R
be a set of coset representatives of
(\pi)
such that
\bigcup_{f\in F}\left(f+(\pi)\right)=R\setminus\mathfrak{p}
and
\left(f_{1}+(\pi)\right)\cap\left(f_{2}+(\pi)\right)=\emptyset
whenever
f_{1}\neq f_{2}
. We note that
|F|=[R:\mathfrak{p}]-1<\infty
. Let
\chi:K_{\mathfrak{p}}\setminus\{0\}\rightarrow F
be given by

\frac{x}{\pi^{v_{\mathfrak{p}}(x)}}\equiv\chi(x)\pmod{\mathfrak{p}}.

(128)

Note that
\chi(rs)=\chi(r)\chi(s)\pmod{\pi}
for all
r,s\in R\setminus\{0\}
. We also see that

\chi(r+s)\equiv\begin{cases}\chi(r)+\chi(s)\hfill\pmod{\pi}&\text{ if }v_{\mathfrak{p}}(r)=v_{\mathfrak{p}}(s)\text{ and }r+s\not\equiv 0\pmod{\pi}\\
\chi(s)\hfill\pmod{\pi}&\text{ if }v_{\mathfrak{p}}(r)>v_{\mathfrak{p}}(s)\\
\chi(r)\hfill\pmod{\pi}&\text{ if }v_{\mathfrak{p}}(s)>v_{\mathfrak{p}}(r).\end{cases}

(129)

Let
K_{\mathfrak{p}}\setminus\{0\}=\bigcup_{f\in F}C_{f}
be the partition given by
C_{f}=\chi^{-1}(\{f\})
. Let us assume for the sake of contradiction that there exists
d\in F
for which
w,x,y,z\in C_{d}
and Equation (
127
) is satisfied. We now have
3
cases to consider. If
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(x)=v_{\mathfrak{p}}(y)
, then we see that

\displaystyle 0\not\equiv(a+b)d\equiv\chi(a)\chi(x)+\chi(b)\chi(y)\equiv\chi(ax+by)\equiv\chi(cwz^{n})\equiv cd^{n+1}\pmod{\mathfrak{p}},

\displaystyle\text{hence }(a+b)c^{-1}\equiv d^{n}\pmod{\mathfrak{p}},

which yields the desired contradiction. For our next case we assume that
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(x)<v_{\mathfrak{p}}(y)
and note that

\displaystyle 0\not\equiv ad\equiv\chi(a)\chi(x)\equiv\chi(ax+by)\equiv\chi(cwz^{n})\equiv cd^{n+1}\pmod{\mathfrak{p}},

\displaystyle\text{hence }ac^{-1}\equiv d^{n}\pmod{\mathfrak{p}},

which again yields the desired contradiction. Similarly, in our final case when
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(x)>v_{\mathfrak{p}}(y)
we have

\displaystyle 0\not\equiv bd\equiv\chi(b)\chi(y)\equiv\chi(ax+by)\equiv\chi(cwz^{n})\equiv cd^{n+1}\pmod{\mathfrak{p}},

\displaystyle\text{hence }bc^{-1}\equiv d^{n}\pmod{\mathfrak{p}},

which once more yields the desired contradiction.
∎

Theorem 17
.

Let
R

. Let

(ii)

.

(iii)

No element of

is an
n

\mathfrak{p}
.

The system of equations

\begin{array}[]{ccccc}a_{1}x_{1}&+&b_{1}y_{1}&=&c_{1}w_{1}z_{1}^{n}\\
a_{2}x_{2}&+&b_{2}y_{2}&=&c_{2}w_{2}z_{2}^{n}\\
&&\vdots&&\\
a_{k}x_{k}&+&b_{k}y_{k}&=&c_{k}w_{k}z_{k}^{n}\end{array}

is not partition regular over
K_{\mathfrak{p}}\setminus\{0\}
, where
K_{\mathfrak{p}}
is the localization of
K
at
\mathfrak{p}
.

Proof.

We begin the proof similarly to that of Theorem
15
. Since
R
is a Dedekind domain we see that
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a discrete valuation ring under the valuation
v_{\mathfrak{p}}
, so let
\pi
be a generator of the maximal ideal of
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Let
F\subseteq R
be a set of coset representatives of
(\pi)
such that
\bigcup_{f\in F}\left(f+(\pi)\right)=R\setminus\mathfrak{p}
and
\left(f_{1}+(\pi)\right)\cap\left(f_{2}+(\pi)\right)=\emptyset
whenever
f_{1}\neq f_{2}
. We note that
|F|=[R:\mathfrak{p}]-1<\infty
. Let
\chi:K_{\mathfrak{p}}\setminus\{0\}\rightarrow F
be given by

\frac{x}{\pi^{v_{\mathfrak{p}}(x)}}\equiv\chi(x)\pmod{\mathfrak{p}}.

(140)

Observe that
\chi(r)\equiv r\pmod{\mathfrak{p}}
for all
r\notin\mathfrak{p}
. Let
K_{\mathfrak{p}}\setminus\{0\}=\bigcup_{f\in F}C_{f}
be the partition given by

. Let us assume for the sake of contradiction that there exists

for which the system of equations in (

\chi(b_{i})d\hfill\pmod{\pi}&\text{ if }v_{\mathfrak{p}}(x_{i})>v_{\mathfrak{p}}(x_{i}),\end{cases}

\text{hence }d^{n}\equiv d^{-1}\chi(w_{i}z_{i}^{n})=\begin{cases}(a_{i}+b_{i})c^{-1}\hfill\pmod{\pi}&\text{ if }v_{\mathfrak{p}}(x_{i})=v_{\mathfrak{p}}(y_{i})\\

The Existence of Special Ultrafilters

In this section we will review some knowledge about
\beta S
, the space of ultrafilters over a set
S
, so that we can provide a thorough proof of Theorems
6
and
13
as Theorems
25
and
27
, respectively. After proving Theorem
27
, we give a brief discussion comparing the methods that we use to show that certain equations are partition regular with the methods used in
[
3
]
and
[
12
]
. As a result of this discussion (cf. Remark
8
), we decide to prove a generalization of Theorem
25
as Corollary
7
. For a more comprehensive study of ultrafilters and their usage in the study of semigroups the reader is referred to
[
13
]
.

Let us recall some notation before proceeding further. We let
\mathcal{P}(S)
denote the collection of subsets of
S
and
\mathcal{P}_{f}(S)
denote the collection of non-empty finite subsets of
S
. If
(S,\diamond)
is a semigroup, then for
s\in S
and
A\subseteq S
we define
sA=\{s\diamond a\ |\ a\in A\}
and
s^{-1}A=\{x\in S\ |\ s\diamond x\in A\}
.

Definition 3
.

Let
S
be a set.
p\subseteq\mathcal{P}(S)
is an ultrafilter if it satisfies the following properties:

(i)

The empty set is not a member of
p
, i.e.,
\emptyset\notin p
,

(ii)

if
A\in p
and
A\subseteq B
then
B\in p
,

(iii)

if
A,B\in p
then
A\cap B\in p
,

(iv)

for any
A\subseteq S
, either
A\in p
or
A^{c}\in p
.

We denote the space of all ultrafilters over
S
by
\beta S
. It is often useful to think about
\beta S
as the set of finitely additive
\{0,1\}
-valued measures on the set
S
. The topology of
\beta S
is generated by the basis of open sets
\big\{\hat{A}\big\}_{A\subseteq S}
, where

\hat{A}:=\left\{p\in\beta S\ |\ A\in p\right\}.

(166)

Since
(\hat{A})^{c}=\widehat{A^{c}}
for all
A\subseteq S
, we see that each
\hat{A}
is also a closed set, and it is a fact that
\{\hat{A}\}_{A\subseteq S}
also generates the topology of
\beta S
as a basis of closed sets. We note that for any
s\in S
, the collection of sets given by
e_{s}:=\{A\subseteq S\ |\ s\in A\}
is an ultrafilter over
S
. Let
e:S\rightarrow\beta S
be given by
e(s)=e_{s}
and observe that
e
is an injective map that naturally embeds
S
inside of
\beta S
as a dense subset. Furthermore, when we endow
S
with the discrete topology, which will always be the case for the rest of this section,
e
is a homeomorphism onto its image. An ultrafilter
p
is a principal ultrafilter if
p\in e(S)
, and a nonprincipal ultrafilter otherwise. Since we naturally identify each
s\in S
with the principal ultrafilter
e_{s}
, it is common to write
s
in place of
e_{s}
, and we will be using this convention for the rest of this section.

The following theorem is a universal property that can be used to characterize
\beta S
.

Theorem 19

(
[
13
, Theorem 3.27]
)
.

Let
S
be an infinite set with the discrete topology and let
\beta S
denote the space of ultrafilters over
S
. Given any compact space
Y
and any function
f:S\rightarrow Y
, there exists a unique continuous function
\tilde{f}:\beta S\rightarrow Y
such that
\tilde{f}|_{S}=f
.

A careful and repeated application of Theorem
19
also allows one to extend binary operations from
S\times S
to
\beta S\times\beta S
, which is of great use when
S
is a semigroup.

Theorem 20

(
[
13
, Theorem 4.1]
)
.

Let
S
be a set and let
\diamond
be a binary operation defined on
S
. There is a unique binary operation
*:\beta S\times\beta S\rightarrow\beta S
satisfying the following three conditions.

(a)

For every
s,t\in S
,
s*t=s\diamond t
.

(b)

For each
q\in\beta S
, the function
\rho_{q}:\beta S\rightarrow\beta S
is continuous, where
\rho_{q}(p)=p*q
.

(c)

For each
s\in S
, the function
\lambda_{s}:\beta S\rightarrow\beta S
is continuous, where
\lambda_{s}(q)=s*q
.

It is worth mentioning that our decision to make
\rho_{q}
continuous for all
q\in\beta S
and
\lambda_{s}
continuous only for all
s\in S\subseteq\beta S
is because we are choosing to use the same conventions used in
[
13
]
. Elsewhere in the literature it is common to work with the extension
*
of
\diamond
that makes
\lambda_{q}
continuous for all
q\in\beta S
and
\rho_{s}
continuous only for all
s\in S
.

Theorem 21

(
[
13
, Theorem 4.12]
)
.

If
(S,\diamond)
is a semigroup and
*:\beta S\times\beta S\rightarrow\beta S
is the operation given by Theorem
20
, then
*
is an associative operation. Furthermore, for
p,q\in\beta S
the ultrafilter
p*q
is given by

A\in p*q\text{ if and only if }\{s\in S\ |\ s^{-1}A\in q\}\in p.

(167)

It is customary to denote the operation
*
that is produced by Theorem
20
by
\diamond
so that
\diamond
represents an operation on
S\times S
as well as
\beta S\times\beta S
, and we shall adopt this practice. In light of Theorem
21
, we see that if
(S,\diamond)
is a semigroup, then
(\beta S,\diamond)
is also a semigroup. Since
\beta S
is a compact Hausdorff space (see
[
13
, Theorem 3.18]
), and for each
q\in\beta\mathbb{N}
the map
\rho_{q}:\beta\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\beta\mathbb{N}
given by
\rho_{q}(p)=p\diamond q
is continuous, we see that
(\beta S,\diamond)
is a compact right topological semigroup.
3
3

3

A
compact right topological semigroup
is a semigroup
(S,\diamond)
that is also a compact Hausdorff space for which each of the maps
\rho_{s}:S\rightarrow S
given by
\rho_{s}(t)=t\diamond s
are continuous. Note that other sources may use
\rho_{s}(t)=s\diamond t
in their definition and that we base our definition off of
[
13
, Definition 2.1]
.
We apologize to the reader for our seemingly excessive emphasis on the operation
\diamond
of our semigroup
(S,\diamond)
, but we choose to do this to avoid confusion later on when we work with rings
(R,+,\cdot)
and are forced to consider the semigroups
(R,+)
and
(R,\cdot)
separately. We will now collect some results from the literature on semigroups.

Definition 13
.

Let
(S,\diamond)
be a semigroup.

(i)

We say
e\in S
is an
idempotent
if
e\diamond e=e
. We let
E(S,\diamond)
denote the set of idempotents of
(S,\diamond)
.

(ii)

We say
\emptyset\neq L\subseteq S
is a
left ideal
if for any
s\in S
and
\ell\in L
we have
s\diamond\ell\in L
. We say
\emptyset\neq R\subseteq S
is a
right ideal
if for any
s\in S
and
r\in R
we have
r\diamond s\in R
. In general,
I\subseteq S
is an
ideal
if it is a left ideal and a right ideal.

(iii)

We call
L\subseteq S
a
minimal left ideal
if
L
is a left ideal that does not properly contain any other left ideal. Similarly,
the smallest ideal of S
, if it exists, is an ideal
I
that is contained in every other ideal of
S
.
4
4

4

Note that not every semigroup possesses a smallest ideal. In the semigroup
(\mathbb{N},+)
each of the sets
I_{n}:=\{m\in\mathbb{N}\ |\ m\geq n\}
is an ideal. Since there is no set that is contained in every
I_{n}
, we see that
(\mathbb{N},+)
does not have a smallest ideal.
If
(S,\diamond)
does possess a smallest ideal, then we denote it by
K(S,\diamond)
and observe that

is also a semigroup.

Theorem 22

(
[
13
, Corollary 2.6]
)
.

Let
(S,\diamond)
be a compact right topological semigroup. Then every left ideal of
S
contains a minimal left ideal. Minimal left ideals are closed, and each minimal left ideal has an idempotent.

Theorem 23

(
[
13
, Theorem 2.8]
)
.

Let
(S,\diamond)
be a semigroup. If
S
has a minimal left ideal, then
K(S,\diamond)
exists and
K(S,\diamond)=\bigcup\{L\ |\ L\text{ is a minimal left ideal of }S\}
.

Lemma 15

(
[
13
, Theorems 1.38(d) and 1.60]
)
.

Let
(S,\diamond)
be a semigroup and let
\leq
be the partial ordering on the set of idempotents of
S
given by
f\leq e
if and only if
fe=ef=f
. Assume that
S
has a minimal left ideal that has an idempotent.

(i)

If
e\in S
is an idempotent that is minimal with respect to
\leq
, then
e
is a member of some minimal left ideal of
S
. Such an idempotent is a minimal idempotent. It follows that
E(K(S,\diamond))
is the set of minimal idempotents of
(S,\diamond)
.

(ii)

If
f\in S
is an idempotent then there exists a minimal idempotent
e
such that
e\leq f
.

Definition 14
.

Let
(S,\diamond)
be a semigroup and let
A\subseteq S
. Then
A
is
central
if there exists an ultrafilter
p\in E(K(\beta S,\diamond))
for which

.

Theorem 24

(
[
4
, Theorem 3.5]
)
.

Let
(S,\diamond)
be a commutative semigroup, let
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, and let

be a central set.

(i)

There exists
b,g\in A
such that

(168)

(ii)

If
(S,\diamond)
is a commutative group and
c:S\rightarrow S
is a homomorphism for which

, then there exists
b,g\in S
such that

\{c(g)\}\cup\left\{c(b)\diamond g^{j}\right\}_{j=-\ell}^{\ell}\subseteq A.

(169)

Lemma 16

(
[
13
, Theorem 17.3]
)
.

There exists an ultrafilter
p\in\beta\mathbb{N}
such that every
A\in p
is a central subset of
(\mathbb{N},+)
and a central subset of
(\mathbb{N},\cdot)
.

We are now ready to prove Theorem

Theorem 25
.

Let
p\in\beta\mathbb{N}
be such that every
A\in p
is a central subset of
(\mathbb{N},+)
and a central subset of
(\mathbb{N},\cdot)
. Then

satisfies the following properties.

(i)

For any
A\in p
and

, there exist

with

.

(ii)

For any
A\in p
and

, there exist
a,d\in\mathbb{N}
for which
\{hd\}\cup\{ha+id\}_{i=-\ell}^{\ell}\subseteq A
.

(iii)

For any

, we have

.

Proof.

By Lemma
16
let
p\in\beta\mathbb{N}
be an ultrafilter such that every

is a central subset of
(\mathbb{N},+)
and a central subset of
(\mathbb{N},\cdot)
. To see that

satisfies (i), it suffices to observe that each

is a central subset of

, so Theorem

(i) applies. To see that

satisfies (ii) we first note that any central subset of

is also a central subset of
(\mathbb{Z},+)
by

, so we may apply Theorem

(ii) with the homomorphism

given by

for all

to find some

for which

. Since

, we see that

. To see that

satisfies
(iii)
, let

be arbitrary and let us assume for the sake of contradiction that

. It follows that

Remark 8
.

In
[
3
]
and
[
12
]
the authors also used an ultrafilter

for which every
A\in p
is a central subset of
(\mathbb{N},+)
and a central subset of
(\mathbb{N},\cdot)
in order to show that the equation

is partition regular over

, so it is unsurprising that we have managed to use such an ultrafilter to obtain our positive results over
\mathbb{N}
and
\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
. Unfortunately, if

is a general integral domain, then there may not exist an ultrafilter
p\in\beta R
for which every
A\in p
is a central subset of
(R,+)
and a central subset of
(R\setminus\{0\},\cdot)
. Thankfully, we will see as a consequence of Theorem
27
that we only need to work with central subsets of
(R\setminus\{0\},\cdot)
in order to get our desired results for a general integral domain. In particular, the ultrafilter from Theorems
13
and
27
is just a minimal idempotent in
(\beta R\setminus\{0\},\cdot)
, so it is a corollary of Lemma
14
that any central subset of
(\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\},\cdot)
(and consequently of
(\mathbb{N},\cdot)
) contains a solution to the equation
x+y=wz
. For the sake of completeness, we will still examine rings
R
for which there exists an ultrafilter
p\in\beta R
such that every
A\in p
is a central subset of
(R,+)
and a central subset of
(R\setminus\{0\},\cdot)
after we prove Theorem

. We remark that we do not require our rings to have a multiplicative unit.

We would also like to point out to the reader that we work with central subsets of
(R\setminus\{0\},\cdot)
instead of central subsets of
(R,\cdot)
because
K(\beta R,\cdot)=\{0\}
, so
\{0\}
is the only central subset of
(R,\cdot)
. For central subsets of
(R\setminus\{0\},\cdot)
to be defined, we need

to be a semigroup, which is why we will only work with division rings and integral domains for the rest of this section. We also observe that the natural inclusion map
\iota:\beta(R\setminus\{0\})\rightarrow(\beta R)\setminus\{0\}
given by

\iota(p)=\{A\subseteq R\ |\ A\setminus\{0\}\in p\}=p\cup\{A\cup\{0\}\ |\ A\in p\}

(170)

is a homeomorphism. Furthermore, we see that for any
p_{1},p_{2}\in\beta(R\setminus\{0\})
we have that
\iota(p_{1}\cdot p_{2})=\iota(p_{1})\cdot\iota(p_{2})
, so
\iota
is a semigroup isomorphism as well. Since
(\beta R)\setminus\{0\}
and
\beta(R\setminus\{0\})
are naturally isomorphic as compact right topological semigroups, we will write
\beta R\setminus\{0\}
for
(\beta R)\setminus\{0\}
without any worry for the potential confusion with
\beta(R\setminus\{0\})
.

Definition 15
.

Let
R
be a division ring. A family
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(R)
is
partition regular
if for every finite partition
R=\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}C_{i}
, there exists
1\leq i_{0}\leq r
and
F\in\mathcal{F}
for which
F\subseteq C_{i_{0}}
.
\mathcal{F}
is
multiplicatively shift invariant
if for every
F\in\mathcal{F}
and
r\in R\setminus\{0\}
we have
rF\in\mathcal{F}
.

Lemma 17
.

Let
R
be an infinite integral domain and let
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{P}_{f}(R\setminus\{0\})
be a partition regular family that is also multiplicatively shift invariant. Let
I_{\mathcal{F}}\subseteq\beta R\setminus\{0\}
denote the collection of ultrafilters
p
such that for every
A\in p
there exists
F\in\mathcal{F}
for which
F\subseteq A
.
I_{\mathcal{F}}
is an ideal of
(\beta R\setminus\{0\},\cdot)
.

Proof.

First let us show that
I_{\mathcal{F}}
is nonempty. To this end, let us assume for the sake of contradiction that for each
p\in\beta R\setminus\{0\}
there exists
A_{p}\in p
such that there is no
F\in\mathcal{F}
with
F\subseteq A
. Since
\big\{\widehat{A_{p}}\big\}_{p\in\beta R\setminus\{0\}}
is an open cover of the compact space
\beta R\setminus\{0\}
, let
\big\{\widehat{A_{p_{i}}}\big\}_{i=1}^{r}

Lemma 19
.

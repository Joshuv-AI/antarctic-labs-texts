Comment on “Analysis of Ringdown Overtones in GW150914”


Abstract




Comment on “Analysis of Ringdown Overtones in GW150914”

M. Isi, W. M. Farr (Flatiron Institute)

Reference
Cotesta et al. 2022
reanalyzes the GW150914 ringdown, arguing against the presence of an overtone and suggesting claims of its detection in
Isi et al. 2019
were driven by noise.
Here we point out a number of technical errors in that analysis, including a software bug, and show that features
highlighted as problematic are in fact expected and encountered in simulated data.
After fixes, the code in
Cotesta et al. 2022
produces results consistent with the presence of the overtone as in
Isi et al. 2019
;
Isi and Farr 2022
;
Finch and Moore 2022
, undermining several key claims in
Cotesta et al. 2022
.

Out of the different indicators for the overtone presented in

Isi et al. 2019
, Ref.
Cotesta et al. 2022
focuses on the posterior on the
overtone amplitude,
A_{1}
, as inferred by a two-tone model. For the
same model and data, the posterior is a mathematical object over which there
should be no disagreement. Yet, the
A_{1}
distribution in
Isi et al. 2019

visibly differs from the corresponding one in
Cotesta et al. 2022
(labeled
\Delta t_{\rm start}^{\rm H1}=-0.72M
;
t_{\rm H1}=1126259462.423\,\mathrm{s}
), in
spite of
Cotesta et al. 2022
claiming to emulate the analysis in

Isi et al. 2019
.

In a response
Isi and Farr 2022
, we subsequently failed to replicate the observations in
Cotesta et al. 2022
with a setup mimicking
Isi et al. 2019
; moreover, even when using the exact settings and software as in
Cotesta et al. 2022
, we remained unable to reproduce the posterior shown in
Cotesta et al. 2022
for the reference time in
Isi et al. 2019
.

We have now established that the analysis in
Cotesta et al. 2022
that is reported as
analogous to that in
Isi et al. 2019
actually
(i) started at a different time (
\Delta t_{\rm start}^{\rm H1}=-1.2M
;
t_{\rm H1}=1126259462.422828\,\mathrm{s}
),
(ii) used too short a segment of data given the response of the whitening filter, and
(iii) was affected by a coding bug that attributed erroneous timestamps to the LIGO data
bug
.
Correcting all this, the codes used in

Isi and Farr 2022
and
Cotesta et al. 2022
(
ringdown

Isi and Farr 2021a

and
pyRing

Carullo et al.
) yield indistinguishable
results, favoring the presence of the overtone at the time in
Isi et al. 2019

(Fig.
1
). This agrees with the independent amplitude
posteriors in
Finch and Moore 2022
, and contradicts the claim in

Cotesta et al. 2022
that “around the peak, the Bayes factor does not
indicate the presence of an overtone.”

Both
pyRing
and
ringdown
favor
A_{1}>0
over a broad range of start times after the peak, as identified in either
Isi and Farr 2022
or
Cotesta et al. 2022
:
the significance, as evaluated from the
A_{1}
posterior, does not ultimately drop below
2\sigma
until
{\sim}10\,\mathrm{ms}
after the reference time in
Isi and Farr 2022
, consistent with the expected decay of this mode (Fig.
2
, top).

Reference
Cotesta et al. 2022
argues that the overtone detection in
Isi et al. 2019
was “noise dominated” because the “amplitude is sensitive to changes in the starting time much smaller than the overtone damping time.”
However, the variation that remains after fixing the timing problems in
Cotesta et al. 2022
is expected: because different data are processed for different start times, the posterior will jitter due to high frequency noise.
With data sampled at 16 kHz, as in
Cotesta et al. 2022
, the
A_{1}
posterior may vary over times as short as
\approx 0.1\,\mathrm{ms}
—faster than for the 2 kHz sampling rate in
Isi et al. 2019
;
Isi and Farr 2022
.
We demonstrate this with simulated damped sinusoids in synthetic noise, showing similar fluctuations as for GW150914 (Fig.
2
, bottom).

In summary, both key points in
Cotesta et al. 2022
(namely, that there is no evidence for
A_{1}>0
at any time after the peak, and that posterior variability indicates the result was noise-dominated) were drawn from faulty posteriors affected by erroneous settings and a software bug.
These errors conspired to spuriously exacerbate the
A_{1}
posterior width and sensitivity to start time.
On fixing these errors, the code used in
Cotesta et al. 2022
returns results in agreement with the posteriors in the rest of the literature and in tension with central claims in
Cotesta et al. 2022
itself.

The 2 2 -rank of the class group of some real cyclic quartic number fields II
Abdelmalek Azizi, Mohammed Tamimi, Abdelkader Zekhnini

Abstract
. In this paper, we determine the 2 2 -rank of the class group of certain classes of real cyclic quartic number fields. Precisely, we consider the case in which the quadratic subfield is ℚ ⁡ ( l ) \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{l}) with l = 2 l=2 or a prime congruent to 1 ( mod 8 ) 1\,\pmod{8} .

1.
Introduction

Let
K
be a number field and
H
its
p
-class group, that is the Sylow
p
-subgroup of the ideal class group
\mathrm{Cl}(K)
of
K
in the wide sense, where
p
is a prime integer. Class groups of number fields have been studied for a long time, and
there are many very interesting problems concerning their behavior.
A particular quantity of interest is the rank
r_{p}(H)
of the
p
-class group
H
defined as the number of cyclic
p
-groups appearing in the decomposition of
\mathrm{Cl}(K)
, i.e. the dimension of the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-vector space
\mathrm{Cl}(K)/\mathrm{Cl}(K)^{p}
, where
\mathbb{F}_{p}
is the field of
p
elements.

For
p=2
, many mathematicians are interested in determining
r_{2}(H)
and the power of
2
dividing the class number of
K
. Hasse
[
13
]
, Bauer
[
5
]
and others gave methods for determining the exact power of
2
dividing the class number of a quadratic numbers field. These methods were developed by C. J. Parry and his co-authors to determine
r_{2}(H)
and the power of
2
dividing the class number of some cyclic quartic numbers fields
K
having a quadratic subfield
k
with odd class number (e.g.,
[
6
,
7
,
21
,
22
,
23
]
). To accomplish their task, they needed a suitable genus theory convenient to their situation. Hence they showed that, the theory firstly developed by Hilbert (
[
14
]
), assuming an imaginary base field
k
, can be adapted to the situation where
K
is a totally imaginary quartic cyclic extension of a totally real quadratic subfield
k
. In reality, this theory can be applied to any quartic number field
K
having a quadratic subfield
k
of odd class number (
[
23
]
).

The
2
-rank of the class group of any biquadratic number field
K
is determined (partially or totally) in many papers (
[
19
,
20
,
6
,
7
,
2
,
3
]
) up to the case:
K
is a real quartic cyclic extension of the rational number field
\mathbb{Q}
.
This paper is devoted to investigate the
2
-rank of the class group of this class of number fields. We will focus on the case where the unique quadratic subfield of
K
is
k=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{l})
with
l
is a prime congruent to
1\pmod{8}
or
l=2
. Note that the case
l
congruent to
5\pmod{8}
is studied separately.

An outline of the paper is as follows. In §
2
we summarize preliminary results on quartic cyclic number fields and the ambiguous class numbers formula. The main theorems are presented in §
3
and §
4
. In §
5
we characterize all real cyclic quartic number fields
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{n\epsilon_{0}\sqrt{l}})
, with

is a prime congruent to

or

, whose
2
-class group is trivial, cyclic, of rank
2
or
3
.

Lemma 3.1

(
[

Let

3.1.
Case
n=1

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{n\epsilon_{0}\sqrt{l}})
be a real cyclic quartic number field, where
l\equiv 1\pmod{8}
is a positive prime integer,
n
a square-free positive integer relatively prime to
l
and
\epsilon_{0}
the fundamental unit of
k=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{l})
. If
n=1
, then
r_{2}\left(H\right)=0
.

Proof.

Since
a=n=1
,
a+b\equiv 1+0\equiv 1\pmod{4}
, which implies, by Theorem
2.1
, that
f_{\mathbb{K}}=l\not\equiv 0\pmod{8}
. But
S=s_{l}=+1
, then Lemma
2.1
ensures the existence of real number field
\mathbb{K}
having as conductor
f_{\mathbb{K}}
and as quadratic subfield
k
.
Moreover, the only prime ideal of
k
that ramifies in
\mathbb{K}
is
(\sqrt{l})
, i.e.
\mu=1
. To prove the theorem, we have to compute the integer
r^{*}
(see Theorem
2.2
) by applying Remark
2.2
, and then we call the theorem
2.2
.
We have

\begin{array}[]{ll}\left(\frac{-1\mathchar 59\relax\,d}{\left(\sqrt{l}\right)}\right)=\left[\frac{-1}{\left(\sqrt{l}\right)}\right]=\left(\frac{-1}{l}\right)=1.\\
\left(\frac{\epsilon_{0}\mathchar 59\relax\,d}{(\sqrt{l})}\right)=\left[\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{(\sqrt{l})}\right]=\left[\frac{u}{(\sqrt{l})}\right]=\left(\frac{u}{l}\right)=\left(\frac{l}{u}\right)=\left(\frac{v^{2}l}{u}\right)=1\mathchar 59\relax\end{array}

indeed
\epsilon_{0}=u+v\sqrt{l}
, so
-1=u^{2}-v^{2}l
and thus
v^{2}l\equiv 1\pmod{u}.

Hence
r^{*}=2
, which implies that:

r_{2}\left(H\right)=\mu+r^{*}-3=1+2-3=0.

∎

3.2.
Case
n=2

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{n\epsilon_{0}\sqrt{l}})

is a positive prime integer,
n
a square-free positive integer relatively prime to

and
\epsilon_{0}
the fundamental unit of
k=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{l})
. For

, we have:

1.

If
\left(\frac{2}{l}\right)_{4}=(-1)^{\frac{l-1}{8}}
, then

2.

If
\left(\frac{2}{l}\right)_{4}\neq(-1)^{\frac{l-1}{8}}
, then
r_{2}\left(H\right)=1.

Proof.

Since
n=2
,
n
is even and according to Lemma
2.2

we get
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2a(l+b\sqrt{l}})=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{a(l+c\sqrt{l}})
with

.
As

, so, by Theorem
2.1
,
f_{\mathbb{K}}=2^{3}al=2^{3}l\equiv 0\pmod{8}
, thus there exists as many real cyclic fields as imaginary ones having as a conductor
f_{\mathbb{K}}
and as a quadratic subfield
k
. The prime ideals of
k
which ramify in
\mathbb{K}
are
(\sqrt{l})
and
\mathfrak{2}_{i}\mathchar 59\relax\;i\in\{1\mathchar 59\relax 2\}
, where
2\mathcal{O}_{k}=\mathfrak{2}_{1}\mathfrak{2}_{2}
is the decomposition of
2
in

, i.e.
\mu=3
. We have

\begin{array}[]{ll}\left(\frac{-1\mathchar 59\relax d}{\mathfrak{2}_{1}}\right)=\left(\frac{-1\mathchar 59\relax d}{\mathfrak{2}_{2}}\right)=\left(\frac{-1\mathchar 59\relax 2\epsilon_{0}\sqrt{l}}{\mathfrak{2}_{1}}\right)=\left(\frac{-1\mathchar 59\relax 2}{\mathfrak{2}_{1}}\right)\left(\frac{-1\mathchar 59\relax\epsilon_{0}\sqrt{l}}{\mathfrak{2}_{1}}\right)=\left(\frac{-1\mathchar 59\relax 2}{2}\right)=1\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\\
\text{indeed }\left(\frac{-1\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\;\epsilon_{0}\sqrt{l}}{\mathfrak{2}_{1}}\right)=\left(\frac{\epsilon_{0}\sqrt{l}}{\mathfrak{2}_{1}}\right)^{0}=1\text{ since }\mathfrak{2}_{1}\text{don't ramify in }\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{\epsilon_{0}\sqrt{l}})\ (\text{ see Lemma \ref{20}}).\\
\left(\frac{\epsilon_{0}\mathchar 59\relax d}{\mathfrak{2}_{1}}\right)=\left(\frac{\epsilon_{0}\mathchar 59\relax d}{\mathfrak{2}_{2}}\right)=\left(\frac{\epsilon_{0}\mathchar 59\relax 2\epsilon_{0}\sqrt{l}}{\mathfrak{2}_{1}}\right)=\left(\frac{\epsilon_{0}\mathchar 59\relax 2}{\mathfrak{2}_{1}}\right)\left(\frac{\epsilon_{0}\mathchar 59\relax\epsilon_{0}\sqrt{l}}{\mathfrak{2}_{1}}\right)=\left(\frac{\epsilon_{0}\mathchar 59\relax 2}{\mathfrak{2}_{1}}\right)=\left(\frac{2}{l}\right)_{4}(-1)^{\frac{l-1}{8}}\ \text{ (see \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{AM01}{}{}]})}.\\
\text{Hence}\left(\frac{-\epsilon_{0}\mathchar 59\relax d}{\mathfrak{2}_{1}}\right)=\left(\frac{-\epsilon_{0}\mathchar 59\relax d}{\mathfrak{2}_{2}}\right)=\left(\frac{-1\mathchar 59\relax d}{\mathfrak{2}_{1}}\right)\left(\frac{\epsilon_{0}\mathchar 59\relax d}{\mathfrak{2}_{1}}\right)=\left(\frac{2}{l}\right)_{4}(-1)^{\frac{l-1}{8}}.\end{array}

Since
\left(\frac{-1\mathchar 59\relax\,d}{(\sqrt{l})}\right)=\left(\frac{\pm\epsilon_{0}\mathchar 59\relax\,d}{(\sqrt{l})}\right)=1
as in the first case, therefore

a.

If
\left(\frac{2}{l}\right)_{4}=(-1)^{\frac{l-1}{8}}
, then
r^{*}=2
, which implies that:

r_{2}\left(H\right)=\mu+r^{*}-3=3+2-3=2.

b.

If
\left(\frac{2}{l}\right)_{4}\neq(-1)^{\frac{l-1}{8}}
, then
r^{*}=1
, which implies that:

r_{2}\left(H\right)=\mu+r^{*}-3=3+1-3=1.

∎

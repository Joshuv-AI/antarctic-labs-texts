Deficiency indices and discreteness property of block Jacobi matrices and Dirac operators with point interactions
Viktoriya S. Budyka, Mark M. Malamud

Abstract
The paper concerns with infinite symmetric block Jacobi matrices 𝐉 \bf J with p × p p\times p -matrix entries. We present new conditions for general block Jacobi matrices to be selfadjoint and have discrete spectrum. In our previous papers there was established a close relation between a class of such matrices and symmetric 2 ​ p × 2 ​ p 2p\times 2p Dirac operators 𝐃 X , α \mathrm{\bf D}_{X,\alpha} with point interactions in L 2 ​ ( ℝ , ℂ 2 ​ p ) L^{2}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{C}^{2p}) . In particular, their deficiency indices are related by n ± ​ ( 𝐃 X , α ) = n ± ​ ( 𝐉 X , α ) n_{\pm}(\mathrm{\bf D}_{X,\alpha})=n_{\pm}({\bf J}_{X,{\alpha}}) . For block Jacobi matrices of this class we present several conditions ensuring equality n ± ​ ( 𝐉 X , α ) = k n_{\pm}({\bf J}_{X,{\alpha}})=k with any k ≤ p k\leq p . Applications to matrix Schrodinger and Dirac operators with point interactions are given. It is worth mentioning that a connection between Dirac and Jacobi operators is employed here in both directions for the first time. In particular, to prove the equality n ± ​ ( 𝐉 X , α ) = p n_{\pm}({\bf J}_{X,{\alpha}})=p for 𝐉 X , α {\bf J}_{X,{\alpha}} we first establish it for Dirac operator 𝐃 X , α \mathrm{\bf D}_{X,\alpha} .

The selfadjointness conditions for general block Jacobi matrices

Consider the block Jacobi matrix

{\bf J}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccccccccc}\mathcal{A}_{0}&\mathcal{B}_{0}&\mathbb{O}_{p}&\mathbb{O}_{p}&\mathbb{O}_{p}&\ldots&\mathbb{O}_{p}&\mathbb{O}_{p}&\mathbb{O}_{p}&\mathbb{O}_{p}&\ldots\\

,

Definition 2.1

(

]
)

)

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
{\bf J}
be the block Jacobi matrix of the form (

) and let
\mathcal{A}:=\diag\{\mathcal{A}_{0},\ldots,\mathcal{A}_{n},\ldots\}
,
\ker\mathcal{A}=\{0\}
. Let for some

(2.3)

a_{2}(N):=\underset{n\geq N}{\sup}\Big(\|\mathcal{A}_{n}^{-1}\mathcal{B}_{n}\|+\|\mathcal{A}_{n+2}^{-1}\mathcal{B}_{n+1}^{*}\|\Big)<\infty.

(2.4)

If

then the operator

. Herewith

Introduce the block Jacobi submatrix
{\bf J}_{N}
of the matrix
{\bf J}
, by setting

{\bf J}_{N}:=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccc}\mathcal{A}_{N}&\mathcal{B}_{N}&\mathbb{O}_{p}&\ldots&\mathbb{O}_{p}&\ldots\\
\mathcal{B}_{N}^{*}&\mathcal{A}_{N+1}&\mathcal{B}_{N+1}&\ldots&\mathbb{O}_{p}&\ldots\\
\mathbb{O}_{p}&\mathcal{B}_{N+1}^{*}&\mathcal{A}_{N+2}&\ldots&\mathbb{O}_{p}&\ldots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ldots&\vdots&\ddots\end{array}\right).

(2.6)

(i)
First, let
N=0
and let
K
be the minimal symmetric operator associated to the
Jacobi matrix

K=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccc}\mathbb{O}_{p}&\mathcal{B}_{0}&\mathbb{O}_{p}&\mathbb{O}_{p}&\mathbb{O}_{p}&\ldots\\
\mathcal{B}_{0}^{*}&\mathbb{O}_{p}&\mathcal{B}_{1}&\mathbb{O}_{p}&\mathbb{O}_{p}&\ldots\\
\mathbb{O}_{p}&\mathcal{B}_{1}^{*}&\mathbb{O}_{p}&\mathcal{B}_{2}&\mathbb{O}_{p}&\ldots\\
\mathbb{O}_{p}&\mathbb{O}_{p}&\mathcal{B}_{2}^{*}&\mathbb{O}_{p}&\mathcal{B}_{3}&\ldots\\
\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots\\
\end{array}\right).

(2.7)

Then on finite vectors the following equality holds

{\bf J}h={\bf J}_{0}h=\mathcal{A}h+Kh,\qquad h\in l^{2}_{0}(\mathbb{N}_{0};\mathbb{C}^{p}).

(2.8)

Moreover, the operator
\mathcal{A}^{-1}K
on the lineal

is given by the matrix

\mathcal{A}^{-1}K=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccc}\mathbb{O}_{p}&\mathcal{A}_{0}^{-1}\mathcal{B}_{0}&\mathbb{O}_{p}&\mathbb{O}_{p}&\mathbb{O}_{p}&\ldots\\
\mathcal{A}_{1}^{-1}\mathcal{B}_{0}^{*}&\mathbb{O}_{p}&\mathcal{A}_{1}^{-1}\mathcal{B}_{1}&\mathbb{O}_{p}&\mathbb{O}_{p}&\ldots\\
\mathbb{O}_{p}&\mathcal{A}_{2}^{-1}\mathcal{B}_{1}^{*}&\mathbb{O}_{p}&\mathcal{A}_{2}^{-1}\mathcal{B}_{2}&\mathbb{O}_{p}&\ldots\\
\mathbb{O}_{p}&\mathbb{O}_{p}&\mathcal{A}_{3}^{-1}\mathcal{B}_{2}^{*}&\mathbb{O}_{p}&\mathcal{A}_{3}^{-1}\mathcal{B}_{3}&\ldots\\
\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots\\
\end{array}\right).

(2.9)

According to the Schur’s test (
[
7
, Theorem 2.10.1]
), conditions (
2.3
) and (
2.4
) guarantee

) in

and the estimate

\|\mathcal{A}^{-1}Kh\|\leq\sqrt{a_{1}(N)a_{2}(N)}\|h\|\leq\|h\|
,

.

Since
K\subseteq K^{*}
and
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}^{*}
, then
K\mathcal{A}^{-1}\subset K^{*}\mathcal{A}^{-1}\subseteq(\mathcal{A}^{-1}K)^{*}
(see

Therefore, the operator

is bounded on

and its closure
\overline{\mathcal{A}^{-1}K}\in\mathcal{B}(l^{2}(\mathbb{N}_{0};\mathbb{C}^{p}))

provided (

holds. Hence

(2.10)

Therefore, the operator
K
is subordinated to the operator
\mathcal{A}
with constants

b=0
(see (

)). By the Wüst’s theorem,

For

(ii)
Let

and

is a bounded block matrix with the only nontrivial entries

and
\mathcal{B}_{N-1}^{*}

3
Discreteness property of general block Jacobi matrices

The following statement complements known results on the spectrum of perturbations
(see e.g.
[
34
, Theorems 4.1.16, 4.3.17, 6.3.4]
).
Recall that as usual
\mathcal{S}_{q}(\mathfrak{H})
denotes the Neumann-Schatten ideal in
\mathfrak{H}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
T=T^{*}\in{\mathcal{C}}(\mathfrak{H})
and let
K(\subset K^{*})
be a symmetric operator in
\mathfrak{H}

strongly subordinated to
T
, i.e.
\dom K\supset\dom T
and estimate (
2.2
) is fulfilled with
a\in(0,1)
.
Then for each
q\in(0,\infty]
the following implication holds.

(T-\lambda)^{-1}\in{\bf\mathcal{S}}_{q}(\mathfrak{H})\Longrightarrow(R-\lambda)^{-1}\in{\bf\mathcal{S}}_{q}(\mathfrak{H}).

(3.1)

In particular, if
T
has discrete spectrum, then the operator
R:=T+K(=R^{*})
is
also discrete.

Proof.

By the Kato-Rellich theorem
R=R^{*}
and
\dom R=\dom T
. Clearly, alongside
(
2.2
) similar estimate holds with
T-\lambda I
in place of
T
, the same

a
, and
b+|\lambda|
in place of
b
.

Besides, it easily follows (and also known) that
K
is subordinated to the operator
R-\lambda I
with
(R-\lambda I)
-bound
a_{R-\lambda I}(K)\leq a(1-a)^{-1}
. Hence

K(R-\lambda)^{-1}\in{\mathcal{B}}(\mathfrak{H})
for
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}_{+}
and the resolvent
difference admits the factorization

(R-\lambda)^{-1}-(T-\lambda)^{-1}=(T-\lambda)^{-1}\left(K(R-\lambda)^{-1}\right),\qquad\lambda\in\mathbb{C}_{+}.

(3.2)

Since
(T-\lambda)^{-1}\in{\bf\mathcal{S}}_{q}(\mathfrak{H})
and
K(R-\lambda)^{-1}\in{\mathcal{B}}(\mathfrak{H})
, one gets that
(R-\lambda)^{-1}\in{\bf\mathcal{S}}_{q}(\mathfrak{H})
.

In particular, for
q=\infty
just proved implication
(
3.1
) means that the operator
R:=T+K(=R^{*})
is
discrete whenever
T
is.
∎

Next we present several conditions for Jacobi operator to be discrete.

\end{array}\!\right),\quad f_{I}:=\left\{\!\begin{array}[]{cccc}f_{1}&f_{2}&\!\ldots&f_{p}\end{array}\!\right\}^{\top},\ \ f_{II}:=\left\{\!\begin{array}[]{cccc}f_{p+1}&f_{p+2}&\!\ldots&f_{2p}\end{array}\!\right\}^{\top}.

Here
\top
means a transpose operation. We define the following
Sobolev spaces

\displaystyle W^{1,2}({\mathcal{I}}\setminus X;\mathbb{C}^{2p}):=\bigoplus_{n=1}^{\infty}W^{1,2}([x_{n-1},x_{n}];\mathbb{C}^{2p}),

\displaystyle W^{1,2}_{\mathrm{comp}}({\mathcal{I}}\setminus X;{\mathbb{C}}^{2p}):=\{f\in W^{k,2}({\mathcal{I}}\setminus X;{\mathbb{C}}^{2p}):\mathrm{supp}f\ \text{is compact in}\ {\mathcal{I}}\}.

Two families of Gesztesy-Šeba operators (in short, GS-operators or GS-realizations)
on the interval
(a,b)
are defined to be the closures of the operators

\begin{split}\mathrm{\bf D}_{X,\alpha}^{0}=&\mathrm{\bf D}\upharpoonright\dom(\mathrm{\bf D}_{X,\alpha}^{0}),\\
\dom(\mathrm{\bf D}_{X,\alpha}^{0})=&\Big\{f\in W^{1,2}_{\comp}({\mathcal{I}}\backslash X;{\mathbb{C}}^{2p}):f_{I}\in AC_{\loc}({\mathcal{I}}),\ f_{II}\in AC_{\loc}({\mathcal{I}}\backslash X);\\
&f_{II}(a+)=0\,,\quad f_{II}(x_{n}+)-f_{II}(x_{n}-)=-\frac{i\alpha_{n}}{c}f_{I}(x_{n}),\,\,n\in{\mathbb{N}}\Big\},\end{split}

(5.2)

and

\begin{split}{\mathrm{\bf D}}_{X,\beta}^{0}=&\mathrm{\bf D}\upharpoonright\dom({\mathrm{D}}_{X,\beta}^{0}),\\
\dom({\mathrm{\bf D}}_{X,\beta}^{0})=&\Big\{f\in W^{1,2}_{\comp}({\mathcal{I}}\backslash X;{\mathbb{C}}^{2p}):f_{I}\in AC_{\loc}({\mathcal{I}}\backslash X),\ f_{II}\in AC_{\loc}({\mathcal{I}});\\
&f_{II}(a+)=0\,,\quad f_{I}(x_{n}+)-f_{I}(x_{n}-)=i\beta_{n}cf_{II}(x_{n}),\,\,n\in{\mathbb{N}}\Big\},\end{split}

(5.3)

respectively, i.e.
\mathrm{\bf D}_{X,\alpha}=\overline{\mathrm{\bf D}_{X,\alpha}^{0}}
and
\mathrm{\bf D}_{X,\beta}=\overline{\mathrm{\bf D}_{X,\beta}^{0}}
.
It is easily seen that both operators
\mathrm{\bf D}_{X,\alpha}
and
\mathrm{\bf D}_{X,\beta}
are symmetric, but not necessarily self-adjoint, in general.

In the sequel we need he following proposition established in

[
17
]
(
p=1
) and
[
14
,
15
]
(
p>1
).

Proposition 5.1

(
[
17
,
14
,
15
]
)
.

Let
\mathrm{\bf D}_{X,\alpha}
be realization of the Dirac operator in

L^{2}({\mathcal{I}};\mathbb{C}^{2p})
. Then the operator
\mathrm{\bf D}_{X,\alpha}^{*}:=(\mathrm{\bf D}_{X,\alpha})^{*}
adjoint to the symmetric
operator
\mathrm{\bf D}_{X,\alpha}
is given by

\begin{split}\mathrm{\bf D}_{X,\alpha}^{*}=&\mathrm{\bf D}\upharpoonright\dom(\mathrm{\bf D}_{X,\alpha}^{*}),\\
\dom(\mathrm{\bf D}_{X,\alpha}^{*})=&\Big\{f\in W^{1,2}({\mathcal{I}}\backslash X;\mathbb{C}^{2p}):f_{I}\in AC_{\loc}({\mathcal{I}}),\ f_{II}\in AC_{\loc}({\mathcal{I}}\backslash X);\\
&f_{II}(a+)=0\,,\quad f_{II}(x_{n}+)-f_{II}(x_{n}-)=-\frac{i\alpha_{n}}{c}f_{I}(x_{n}),\,\,n\in{\mathbb{N}}\Big\}.\end{split}

(5.4)

Similarly, the operator
\mathrm{\bf D}_{X,\beta}^{*}
adjoint to
\mathrm{\bf D}_{X,\beta}
is given by the

) with

5.1
Realizations
{\mathrm{\bf D}}_{X,{\alpha}}
with maximal deficiency indices

It is well known that GS-realizations
\mathrm{\bf D}_{X,\alpha}
and
\mathrm{\bf D}_{X,\beta}
are selfadjoint whenever
|{\mathcal{I}}|=\infty
(see
[
17
,
14
,
15
]
).
Here assuming that
|{\mathcal{I}}|<\infty
,
we construct a class of
2p\times 2p
-matrix GS-realizations
\mathrm{\bf D}_{X,\alpha}
with maximal deficiency indices

n_{\pm}(\mathrm{\bf D}_{X,\alpha})=p
. Our main result in this direction reads as follows.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
{\alpha}:=\{\alpha_{n}\}_{1}^{\infty}(\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{p\times p})
be a sequence of selfadjoint
matrices,
\alpha_{n}=\alpha_{n}^{*}
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Then
n_{\pm}(\mathrm{\bf D}_{X,\alpha})=p

provided that the following condition holds

\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}d_{n}\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}\left(1+\frac{1}{c}\,\|\alpha_{k}\|_{\mathbb{C}^{p\times p}}\right)^{2}<+\infty.

(5.5)

Proof.

(i) We examine the operator

T_{X,\alpha}:=\mathrm{\bf D}_{X,\alpha}-\frac{c^{2}}{2}\otimes\left(\begin{matrix}\mathbb{I}_{p}&\mathbb{O}_{p}\\
\mathbb{O}_{p}&-\mathbb{I}_{p}\end{matrix}\right)=-i\,c\,\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\otimes\left(\begin{matrix}\mathbb{O}_{p}&\mathbb{I}_{p}\\
\mathbb{I}_{p}&\mathbb{O}_{p}\end{matrix}\right)

(5.6)

since obviously
n_{\pm}(\mathrm{\bf D}_{X,\alpha})=n_{\pm}(T_{X,\alpha}).

It suffices to show that under the assumption (
5.5
) the equation

(T^{*}_{X,\alpha}\pm i)F=0
has a non-trivial matrix
L^{2}({\mathcal{I}},{\mathbb{C}}^{2p\times p})
-solution of full rank, i.e.
\mathrm{rank}\,F(x)=p
.

We restrict ourselves to the case of equation
(T^{*}_{X,\alpha}+i)F=0
, which is equivalent to
the system

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}F=\frac{1}{c}AF,\quad A=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathbb{O}_{p}&\mathbb{I}_{p}\\
\mathbb{I}_{p}&\mathbb{O}_{p}\\
\end{array}\right).

(5.7)

Here

F=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}F^{1}&F^{2}&\ldots&F^{p}\\
\end{array}\right)\in{\mathbb{C}}^{2p\times p}
and

F^{j}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}F_{I}^{j}\\
F_{II}^{j}\\
\end{array}\right),\qquad F_{I}^{j}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}f_{1}^{j}\\
\vdots\\
f_{p}^{j}\\
\end{array}\right),\qquad F_{II}^{j}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}f_{p+1}^{j}\\
\vdots\\
f_{2p}^{j}\\
\end{array}\right),\ \ j\in\{1,\ldots,p\}.

Equation (
5.7
) has the following piecewise smooth
2p\times p
-matrix solutions

F=\bigoplus_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}F_{I,n}\\
F_{II,n}\\
\end{array}\right),

where

F_{I}=\bigoplus_{n=1}^{\infty}F_{I,n}\,,\quad F_{I,n}(x)=\mathcal{U}_{n}e^{-(x_{n}-x)/c}+\mathcal{V}_{n}e^{(x_{n}-x)/c}\,,\quad x\in[x_{n-1},x_{n}]\,,

(5.8)

F_{II}=\bigoplus_{n=1}^{\infty}F_{II,n}\,,\quad F_{II,n}(x)=\mathcal{U}_{n}e^{-(x_{n}-x)/c}-\mathcal{V}_{n}e^{(x_{n}-x)/c}\,,\quad x\in[x_{n-1},x_{n}]\,,

(5.9)

where
\{\mathcal{U}_{n}\}_{1}^{\infty}\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{p\times p}
,
\{\mathcal{V}_{n}\}_{1}^{\infty}\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{p\times p}
.

According to the description of
{\dom}(\mathrm{\bf D}^{*}_{X,\alpha})
(see Proposition
5.1
)
each component
F^{j}
of
F=\binom{F_{I}}{F_{II}}
,
j\in\{1,\ldots,p\}
,
should satisfy boundary conditions (
5.4
).
First we find recursive relations for matrix sequences
\{\mathcal{U}_{n}\}_{1}^{\infty}
,
\{\mathcal{V}_{n}\}_{1}^{\infty}

that ensure satisfying these conditions.

The condition
F_{II,n}(x_{0}+)=\mathbb{O}_{p}
yields
\mathcal{U}_{1}e^{-d_{1}/c}-\mathcal{V}_{1}e^{d_{1}/c}=\mathbb{O}_{p}
. Further, the condition

F_{I,n}(x_{n}+)=F_{I,n}(x_{n}-),\qquad n\in{\mathbb{N}},

due to the formulas (
5.8
), (
5.9
) is transformed into

\mathcal{U}_{n+1}e^{-d_{n+1}/c}+\mathcal{V}_{n+1}e^{d_{n+1}/c}=\mathcal{U}_{n}+\mathcal{V}_{n}\,,\qquad n\in{\mathbb{N}}.

(5.10)

Moreover, the jump condition

F_{II,n}(x_{n}+)-F_{II,n}(x_{n}-)=-i\,\frac{\alpha_{n}}{c}\,F_{I,n}(x_{n}),\qquad n\in{\mathbb{N}},

due to the formulas (
5.8
), (
5.9
) is equivalent to

\mathcal{U}_{n+1}e^{-d_{n+1}/c}-\mathcal{V}_{n+1}e^{d_{n+1}/c}-(\mathcal{U}_{n}-\mathcal{V}_{n})=-i\,\frac{\alpha_{n}}{c}\,(\mathcal{U}_{n}+\mathcal{V}_{n})\,.

(5.11)

Combining (
5.10
) and (
5.11
) we arrive to the following recursive equations

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{n+1}=\left(\mathcal{U}_{n}-i\,\frac{\alpha_{n}}{2c}\,(\mathcal{U}_{n}+\mathcal{V}_{n})\right)\,e^{d_{n+1}/c}\,,\qquad n\in{\mathbb{N}},

(5.12)

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{n+1}=\left(\mathcal{V}_{n}+i\,\frac{\alpha_{n}}{2c}\,(\mathcal{U}_{n}+\mathcal{V}_{n})\right)\,e^{-d_{n+1}/c}\,,\qquad n\in{\mathbb{N}},

(5.13)

for sequences
\{\mathcal{U}_{n}\}_{1}^{\infty}
and
\{\mathcal{V}_{n}\}_{1}^{\infty}

with the following initial data

\mathcal{U}_{1}=e^{d_{1}/c}\mathbb{I}_{p}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{V}_{1}=e^{-d_{1}/c}\mathbb{I}_{p}\,.

(5.14)

(ii) Let us prove that
\mathrm{rank}\,F_{n}(x)=p
for each
x\in[x_{n-1},x_{n}]
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
It follows from (
5.8
) and (
5.9
) that

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}F_{I,n}^{*}(x)&F_{II,n}^{*}(x)\\
\end{array}\right)\cdot\left(\begin{array}[]{c}F_{I,n}(x)\\
F_{II,n}(x)\\
\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathcal{U}_{n}^{*}e^{-(x_{n}-x)/c}+\mathcal{V}_{n}^{*}e^{(x_{n}-x)/c}&\mathcal{U}_{n}^{*}e^{-(x_{n}-x)/c}-\mathcal{V}_{n}^{*}e^{(x_{n}-x)/c}\\
\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\mathcal{U}_{n}e^{-(x_{n}-x)/c}+\mathcal{V}_{n}e^{(x_{n}-x)/c}\\
\mathcal{U}_{n}e^{-(x_{n}-x)/c}-\mathcal{V}_{n}e^{(x_{n}-x)/c}\\
\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{U}_{n}^{*}\mathcal{U}_{n}e^{-2(x_{n}-x)/c}+\mathcal{U}_{n}^{*}\mathcal{V}_{n}+\mathcal{V}_{n}^{*}\mathcal{U}_{n}+\mathcal{V}_{n}^{*}\mathcal{V}_{n}e^{2(x_{n}-x)/c}+\mathcal{U}_{n}^{*}\mathcal{U}_{n}e^{-2(x_{n}-x)/c}-\mathcal{U}_{n}^{*}\mathcal{V}_{n}-\mathcal{V}_{n}^{*}\mathcal{U}_{n}+\mathcal{V}_{n}^{*}\mathcal{V}_{n}e^{2(x_{n}-x)/c}

\displaystyle=2(\mathcal{U}_{n}^{*}\mathcal{U}_{n}e^{-2(x_{n}-x)/c}+\mathcal{V}_{n}^{*}\mathcal{V}_{n}e^{2(x_{n}-x)/c}).

(5.21)

It follows from (
5.1
), that for each
h\in{\mathbb{C}}^{p}
and
x\in[x_{n-1},x_{n}]

\left\|\left(\begin{array}[]{c}F_{I,n}(x)\\
F_{II,n}(x)\\
\end{array}\right)h\right\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{C}}^{p}}=2e^{-2(x_{n}-x)/c}\|\mathcal{U}_{n}h\|_{{\mathbb{C}}^{p}}^{2}+2e^{2(x_{n}-x)/c}\|\mathcal{V}_{n}h\|_{{\mathbb{C}}^{p}}^{2}.

(5.22)

It follows that for any fixed
a\in[x_{n-1},x_{n}]
the following important equivalence holds

F_{n}(a)h=0\Longleftrightarrow\mathcal{U}_{n}h=\mathcal{V}_{n}h=0.

(5.23)

Let us prove by induction that
\mathrm{rank}\,F_{n}(x)=p
for each
x\in[x_{n-1},x_{n}]
.
For
n=1
it follows from (
5.14
).
Assuming that
\mathrm{rank}\,F_{k}(x)=p
for each
x\in[x_{k-1},x_{k}]
let us prove
that
\mathrm{rank}\,F_{k+1}(x)=p
for
x\in[x_{k},x_{k+1}]
.
Assuming the contrary
we find
\widehat{x}\in[x_{k},x_{k+1}]
and vector
h\in{\mathbb{C}}^{p}
such that
F_{k+1}(\widehat{x})h=0
.
Combining this relation with equivalence (
5.23
) (with
a=\widehat{x}
and
n=k+1
)
yields

\mathcal{U}_{k+1}h=\mathcal{V}_{k+1}h=0.

In turn, inserting these relations in (
5.10
) and (
5.11
) implies

\mathcal{U}_{k}h=\mathcal{V}_{k}h=0.

Due to equivalence (
5.23
) these relations are equivalent to

F_{k}(x)h=0
for
x\in[x_{k-1},x_{k}]
which contradicts the induction hypothesis.
Thus,
\mathrm{rank}\,F(x)=p
for each
x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
.

Note also that in passing we have proved that the matrices
F_{I}(x)
and
F_{II}(x)
are nonsingular for each
x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
.

(iii) It remains to check that under condition (
5.5
) the inclusion
F_{I},F_{II}\in L^{2}({\mathcal{I}};{\mathbb{C}}^{p\times p})
holds. It follows from (
5.8
) and (
5.9
)

\displaystyle\|F_{k}\|_{2}^{2}=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|F_{k,n}\|^{2}_{2}\leq{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{d_{n}}(\|\mathcal{U}_{n}\|_{{\mathbb{C}}^{p\times p}}^{2}e^{-2x/c}+\|\mathcal{V}_{n}\|_{{\mathbb{C}}^{p\times p}}^{2}e^{2x/c})dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\|\mathcal{U}_{n}\|_{{\mathbb{C}}^{p\times p}}^{2}(1-e^{-2d_{n}/c})+\|\mathcal{V}_{n}\|_{{\mathbb{C}}^{p\times p}}^{2}(e^{2d_{n}/c}-1)\right),\quad k=\{I,II\}.

Since
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}d_{n}=|{\mathcal{I}}|<+\infty
,
d_{n}\to 0
and therefore
(1-e^{-2d_{n}/c})\sim(e^{2d_{n}/c}-1)\sim 2d_{n}/c
as
n\to\infty
.
This implies inequality
\|F_{k}\|_{2}<+\infty
provided that

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\|\mathcal{U}_{n}\|_{{\mathbb{C}}^{p\times p}}^{2}+\|\mathcal{V}_{n}\|_{{\mathbb{C}}^{p\times p}}^{2}\right)d_{n}<+\infty\,.

(5.24)

Let us prove by induction the following estimates

\|\mathcal{U}_{n+1}\|_{{\mathbb{C}}^{p\times p}},\ \|\mathcal{V}_{n+1}\|_{{\mathbb{C}}^{p\times p}}\leq\exp\bigl((d_{1}+\ldots+d_{n+1})/c\bigr)\cdot\prod^{n}_{k=1}\left(1+\frac{\|\alpha_{k}\|_{{\mathbb{C}}^{p\times p}}}{c}\right),\quad n\in{\mathbb{N}}.

(5.25)

For
n=1
these estimates are obvious. Assume that (
5.25
) are proved for
n\leq m-1
. Then using (
5.12
) for
n=m
we obtain

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{U}_{m+1}\|_{{\mathbb{C}}^{p\times p}}\leq\left(\|\mathcal{U}_{m}\|_{{\mathbb{C}}^{p\times p}}\left(1+\frac{\|\alpha_{m}\|_{{\mathbb{C}}^{p\times p}}}{2c}\right)+\frac{\|\alpha_{m}\|_{{\mathbb{C}}^{p\times p}}}{2c}\|\mathcal{V}_{m}\|_{{\mathbb{C}}^{p\times p}}\right)e^{d_{m+1}/c}

\displaystyle\leq\prod^{m-1}_{k=1}\left(1+\frac{\|\alpha_{k}\|_{{\mathbb{C}}^{p\times p}}}{c}\right)\left[\left(1+\frac{\|\alpha_{m}\|_{{\mathbb{C}}^{p\times p}}}{2c}\right)+\frac{\|\alpha_{m}\|_{{\mathbb{C}}^{p\times p}}}{2c}\right]e^{(d_{1}+\ldots+d_{m}+d_{m+1})/c}

\displaystyle=\exp\bigl((d_{1}+\ldots+d_{m+1})/c\bigr)\cdot\prod^{m}_{k=1}\left(1+\frac{\|\alpha_{k}\|_{{\mathbb{C}}^{p\times p}}}{c}\right).

This inequality proves the inductive hypothesis (
5.25
) for
\mathcal{U}_{n}
.
The estimate for
\mathcal{V}_{m+1}
is proved similarly. Thus, both inequalities (
5.25
) are established.
Combining (
5.24
) with (
5.25
) and the assumption (
5.5
) we conclude that
F_{I},F_{II}\in L^{2}({\mathcal{I}};{\mathbb{C}}^{p\times p})
.
∎

Corollary 5.3
.

If condition (
5.5
) is fulfilled, then any selfadjoint extension of the operator
\mathrm{\bf D}_{X,\alpha}
has discrete spectrum.

Remark 5.4
.

Clearly, the condition (
5.5
) implies the inclusion
\{d_{n}\}\in l^{1}(\mathbb{N})
, i.e.
|{\mathcal{I}}|<\infty
.

Corollary 5.5
.

The GS realization
\mathrm{\bf D}_{X,\alpha}
is symmetric with
n_{\pm}(\mathrm{\bf D}_{X,\alpha})=p
whenever
\{\alpha_{n}\}_{1}^{\infty}\in l^{1}({\mathbb{N}};{\mathbb{C}}^{p\times p})
.

Proof.

Clearly, for any positive sequence
\{s_{k}\}_{1}^{\infty}

\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}(1+s_{k})\leq\exp\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}s_{k}\right)\,.

It follows with account of the inclusion
\{{\alpha}_{n}\}_{1}^{\infty}\in l^{1}({\mathbb{N}};\mathbb{C}^{p\times p})

\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}d_{n}\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}\left(1+\frac{1}{c}\,\|\alpha_{k}\|_{\mathbb{C}^{p\times p}}\right)^{2}\leq\exp\left(\frac{2}{c}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\|\alpha_{k}\|_{\mathbb{C}^{p\times p}}\right)\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}d_{n}\leq|{\mathcal{I}}|\exp\left(\frac{2}{c}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\|\alpha_{k}\|_{\mathbb{C}^{p\times p}}\right)\,.

It remains to apply Theorem
5.2
.

By the ratio test condition (
5.26
) yields the convergence of the series
(
5.5

(6.1)

where

,

{\alpha}_{n}=\alpha_{n}^{*}
,
\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\prime}=(\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\prime})^{*},\ \mathcal{B}_{n}^{\prime}=(\mathcal{B}_{n}^{\prime})^{*}\ \in\mathbb{C}^{p\times p}
(

\nu(x):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+(c^{2}x^{2})^{-1}}}=\frac{cx}{\sqrt{1+c^{2}x^{2}}}.

(6.2)

In what follows we keep also the notation
\widehat{{\bf J}}_{X,{\alpha}}

6.3
Relations to the moment problem

Here we demonstrate the role of block Jacobi matrices with maximal deficiency indices
in matrix moment problem.
Following M. Krein
[
43
,
44
]
one associates to the matrix
\bf J
(
2.1
) a difference matrix expression

(LV)_{n}=\mathcal{B}_{n-1}^{*}V_{n-1}+\mathcal{B}_{n}V_{n+1}+\mathcal{A}_{n}V_{n},\quad V_{0}=\mathbb{I}_{p},\ V_{-1}=\mathbb{O}_{p},\quad V_{n}\in\mathbb{C}^{p\times p},\ n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

(6.29)

It is known (see
[
43
,
44
,
6
]
), that the solution to the Cauchy problem
(LV)_{n}=zV_{n}
subject to the initial condition (
6.29
) is a sequence of matrix polynomials
\{P_{n}(z)\}_{0}^{\infty}
. Subsequently one finds

P_{0}(z)=\mathbb{I},\quad P_{1}(z)=\mathcal{B}_{0}^{-1}(z\mathbb{I}-\mathcal{A}_{0}),\quad P_{2}(z)=\mathcal{B}_{1}^{-1}((z\mathbb{I}-\mathcal{A}_{1})P_{1}(z)\mathcal{B}_{0}),\quad\ldots\,.

(6.30)

M. Krein
[
43
]
(see also
[
6
]
) showed, that for all
z\in\mathbb{C}_{\pm}
there is a matrix limit

H(z)=\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\left(\sum\limits_{n=0}^{k}P_{n}^{*}(\overline{z})P_{n}(z)\right)^{-1}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{rank}(H(z))=n_{\pm}(\bf J).

(6.31)

He also established that certain matrix moment problem is associated to every Jacobi matrix
{\bf J}
and this problem has a unique (normalized in a sense) solution if
n_{-}({\bf J})\cdot n_{+}({\bf J})=0
.
Moreover, this case is known as a definite case of the matrix moment problem. If
n_{\pm}({\bf J})=p
(see
[
43
]
), then the series

\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}P_{n}^{*}(\overline{z})P_{n}(z)=:H^{-1}(z),\qquad z\in\mathbb{C},

(6.32)

converges uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{C}
.
Note, that in this case the defect subspace
\mathfrak{N}_{z}
is:

\mathfrak{N}_{z}:=\ker({\bf J}^{*}-zI)=\{\{P_{n}(z)h\}_{0}^{\infty}:\ h\in\mathbb{C}^{p}\}.

In this case, it is said that the matrix
{\bf J}
(and the corresponding matrix moment problem)
is in the completely indeterminant case (
[
43
,
44
]
,
[
6
, Ch. VII, §2]
).
In this case for each matrix solution
\Sigma
to the respective moment problem the series (
6.32
) defines a reproducing kernel
for the subspace of entire matrix functions generated in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R};\Sigma)
by the matrix
polynomials
\{P_{n}(z)\}_{0}^{\infty}
. In particular, the latter happens under the conditions (
5.5
) and (
5.27
)
for Jabobi matrices
{\bf J}_{X,\alpha}
and
{\bf J}_{X,\beta}
, respectively.

8
Jacobi matrices with intermediate deficiency indices

Here we complete Theorem

Theorem 8.1
.

Let

\widehat{{\alpha}}_{n}=\mathrm{diag}(\alpha_{n,1},\alpha_{n,2},\ldots,\alpha_{n,p})=\widehat{{\alpha}}_{n}^{*}
be diagonal matrices,
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
and let
{\bf J}_{X,\widehat{\alpha}}
be a minimal Jacobi operator associated with a matrix (
6.3
) with entries
\widehat{{\alpha}}_{n}
instead of
{\alpha}_{n}
. Let also

|\alpha_{n,1}|\leq|\alpha_{n,2}|\leq\ldots\leq|\alpha_{n,p}|,\qquad\text{and}\qquad\{d_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\in l^{1}(\mathbb{N}).

(8.1)

(i)

If following condition holds

\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\sqrt{d_{n}d_{n+1}}\,|\alpha_{n,1}|=+\infty,

(8.2)

then the operator

{\bf J}_{X,\widehat{\alpha}}
is selfadjoint,

{\bf J}_{X,\widehat{{\alpha}}}={\bf J}_{X,\widehat{{\alpha}}}^{*}
.

(ii)

Assume in addition that the following conditions hold

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{|\alpha_{n,1}|}{d_{n+1}}=\infty,\qquad\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{c}{\alpha_{n,1}}>-\frac{1}{4}.

(8.3)

Then the spectrum of Jacobi operator
{\bf J}_{X,\widehat{\alpha}}={\bf J}_{X,\widehat{{\alpha}}}^{*}
is discrete.

Proof.

(i) First we prove selfadjointness of the operator
{\bf J}_{X,\widehat{{\alpha}}}
.
Since the matrices
\widehat{{\alpha}}_{n}=\mathrm{diag}(\alpha_{n,1},\alpha_{n,2},\ldots,\alpha_{n,p})
are diagonal for each
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the operator
{\bf J}_{X,\widehat{{\alpha}}}
splits into the direct sum of
p
scalar Jacobi operators
{\bf J}_{X,\widetilde{\alpha}_{j}}
, where
\widetilde{\alpha}_{j}=\{\alpha_{n,j}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathbb{R}
,
j\in\{1,\ldots,p\}
.
By the Dennis-Wall test (see
[
1
]
, Problem 2, p.25),
{\bf J}_{X,\widetilde{\alpha}_{j}}
is self-adjoint whenever

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{d_{n+1}^{3/2}}{\nu(d_{n+1})}\left(\frac{d_{n}^{3/2}|\alpha_{n,j}|}{\nu(d_{n})}+d_{n+2}^{1/2}\right)=+\infty\,,\qquad j\in\{1,\ldots,p\}.

(8.4)

Since
\nu(d_{n})=\frac{cd_{n}}{\sqrt{1+c^{2}d_{n}^{2}}}\thicksim c\,d_{n}
as
n\to\infty
, one gets
\frac{d_{n}^{3/2}}{\nu(d_{n})}\thicksim c^{-1}\,d_{n}^{1/2}
as
n\to\infty
.
Taking these relations into account and noting that

2\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\sqrt{d_{n+1}}\,\sqrt{d_{n+2}}\leq\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}(d_{n+1}+d_{n+2})<+\infty,

one concludes that the series (
8.2
) and (
8.4
) diverge only simultaneously.
Thus series (
8.4
) diverges and the operator
{\bf J}_{X,\widehat{{\alpha}}}
is selfadjoint, i.e.

n_{\pm}({{\bf J}}_{X,\widehat{{\alpha}}})=0
.

(ii) Since the operator
{\bf J}_{X,\widehat{{\alpha}}}
is selfadjoint, then according to
[
18
, Theorem 8]
(see Remark
3.6
) conditions (
8.3
) guarantee the discreteness of the spectrum of
{\bf J}_{X,\widehat{{\alpha}}}
.
∎

Corollary 8.2
.

Let
\{d_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\in l^{1}(\mathbb{N})
and let
{\bf J}_{X,\alpha}
be the Jacobi matrix of form (
6.3
). Assume that
{\alpha}:=\{\alpha_{n}\}_{1}^{\infty}\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{p\times p}
, where
{\alpha}_{n}=\widehat{{\alpha}}_{n}+\widetilde{{\alpha}}_{n}={\alpha}_{n}^{*}
and matrices

\widehat{{\alpha}}_{n}=\mathrm{diag}(\alpha_{n,1},\alpha_{n,2},\ldots,\alpha_{n,p})=\widehat{{\alpha}}_{n}^{*}

are diagonal. If the condition (
8.2
) holds and

\|\widetilde{{\alpha}}_{n}\|_{\mathbb{C}^{p\times p}}=\mathcal{O}(d_{n+1})\qquad\text{for}\ n\to\infty,

(8.5)

then the (minimal) Jacobi operator
{\bf J}_{X,\alpha}
is selfadjoint,

{\bf J}_{X,{\alpha}}={\bf J}_{X,{\alpha}}^{*}
.

Next we construct Jacobi matrices
{\bf J}_{X,\alpha}
with intermediate deficiency indices
0<n_{\pm}({\bf J}_{X,\alpha})=p_{1}<p
. Let
p=p_{1}+p_{2}
, where
p_{1},\ p_{2}\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
To this end consider block–matrix representation of
{\alpha}_{n}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{p\times p}

with respect to the orthogonal decomposition

A test for normality and independence based on characteristic function
Wiktor Ejsmont, Bojana Milošević, Marko Obradović

Abstract
. In this article we prove a generalization of the Ejsmont characterization [ 8 ] of the multivariate normal distribution. Based on it, we propose a new test for independence and normality. The test uses an integral of the squared modulus of the difference between the product of empirical characteristic functions and some constant. Special attention is given to the case of testing univariate normality in which we derive the test statistic explicitly in terms of Bessel function, and the case of testing bivariate normality and independence. The tests show quality performance in comparison to some popular powerful competitors.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary: 62H15 Secondary: 62E10.

1.
Introduction

One of classical and important problems in statistics is testing independence between two
of more components of a random vector. The traditional approach is based on Pearson’s
correlation coefficient, but its lack of robustness to outliers and departures from normality
eventually led researchers to consider alternative nonparametric procedures. To overcome
this problem, some rank tests of independence are proposed, such as Savage, Spearman and van der Waerden, that in particular rely on linear rank statistics. The present paper uses
another way to test the independence and normality that is based on a distance between the empirical function and a constant
e^{-\frac{1}{2}}
.

Many statistical studies deal with the relationship between two random vectors, say
({\mathrm{X}}_{1},\dots,{\mathrm{X}}_{m})
and
({\mathrm{Y}}_{1},\dots,{\mathrm{Y}}_{n})
, and in particular, with the question whether random variables
{\mathrm{X}}_{i}
and
{\mathrm{Y}}_{j}
are independent and have the same normal distribution (see
[
13
]
).

Assuming multivariate normality of

({\mathrm{X}}_{1},\dots,{\mathrm{X}}_{m},{\mathrm{Y}}_{1},\dots,{\mathrm{Y}}_{n})

the problem reduces to testing the null hypothesis that the correlation coefficients are equal to
0
.
Indeed, it is well known from the general theory of probability that
if a random vector has a multivariate normal distribution (joint normality), then any two or more of its components that are uncorrelated, are independent. This implies that any two or more of its components that are pairwise independent, are independent.

A theoretical framework to study this aspect in a general sense was given by Ejsmont
[
8
]
. Ejsmont proved that the characterizations
of a normal law are given by a certain invariance of the noncentral chi-square distribution.
Namely in
[
8
]
it has been shown that if the random vectors

({\mathrm{X}}_{1},\dots,{\mathrm{X}}_{m})
and
({\mathrm{Y}}_{1},\dots,{\mathrm{Y}}_{n})
are independent with all moments, and the distribution
of
\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\mathrm{X}}_{i}a_{i}+A+\sum_{j=1}^{n}{\mathrm{Y}}_{j}b_{j}+B
depends only on
\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}b_{j}^{2}
, then
{\mathrm{X}}_{1},\dots,{\mathrm{X}}_{m},{\mathrm{Y}}_{1},\dots,{\mathrm{Y}}_{n}
are independent and have the same normal distribution.
In the above result we especially remove the requirement that corresponding random variables are independent and have the same distribution.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we state and prove the main results of
[
8
]
under the weakened assumption.
Next, in Section 3 using this result we propose a new test for normality. Finally, in section 4 we obtain the explicit representation for two random vectors in the language of Bessel functions of the first kind, and in this case we simulate critical values.

2.
The theoretical base for the construction of a test

Notation.
The scalar product of vectors
t,s\in{\mathbb{R}}^{p}
is denoted by
\langle t,s\rangle
and the Euclidean norm of
t
is
\|t\|=\sqrt{\langle t,t\rangle}
. Throughout this paper
{\mathrm{X}}:=({\mathrm{X}}_{1},\dots,{\mathrm{X}}_{m})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
and
{\mathrm{Y}}:=({\mathrm{Y}}_{1},\dots,{\mathrm{Y}}_{n})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
are random vectors, where
m

and
n
are positive integers. The characteristic functions of
{\mathrm{X}}
and
{\mathrm{Y}}
are denoted by

\varphi_{\mathrm{X}}(\cdot)=Ee^{i\langle\cdot,{\mathrm{X}}\rangle}
and
\varphi_{\mathrm{Y}}(\cdot)=Ee^{i\langle\cdot,{\mathrm{Y}}\rangle}
, respectively. For
complex-valued functions
f(\cdot)
, the complex conjugate of
f
is denoted by
\overline{f}
and

|f|^{2}=f\overline{f}
.
In order to simplify notation, we will denote
[n]=\{1,\dots,n\}
.
We denote
(a,b)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m+n}
the concatenation of the vectors
a\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
and
b\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}.

Our construction of a new test of normality is based on the following result.
This is a generalization of the main result of
[
8
]
, under omitted moment assumptions (in
[
8
]
we assume that random variables have all moments; our proof is also different).

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
({\mathrm{X}}_{1},\dots,{\mathrm{X}}_{m},A)\textrm{ and }({\mathrm{Y}}_{1},\dots,{\mathrm{Y}}_{n},B)
be independent random vectors, where
{\mathrm{X}}_{i}
and
{\mathrm{Y}}_{j}
are
nondegenerate for
i\in[m],j\in[n]
, and let statistic

\langle a,{\mathrm{X}}\rangle+\langle b,{\mathrm{Y}}\rangle+A+B=\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}{\mathrm{X}}_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}b_{j}{\mathrm{Y}}_{j}+A+B,

have a distribution which depends only on
\|a\|^{2}+\|b\|^{2}
, where
a\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
b\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Then random variables
{\mathrm{X}}_{1},\dots,{\mathrm{X}}_{m},{\mathrm{Y}}_{1},\dots,{\mathrm{Y}}_{n}
are independent and have the same normal distribution with zero means.

Proof.

Our proof is based on the analysis of the characteristic function and so we denote by
\varphi_{W}(\cdot)
the characteristic function
of
W
.
We write
(a,b)=r(\tilde{a},\tilde{b})
where
(\tilde{a},\tilde{b})
belongs to
the unit sphere of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+m}
i.e.,
r=\sqrt{\|a\|^{2}+\|b\|^{2}}
.
Thus for

r>0
and
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
, we have

(1)

\displaystyle\varphi_{\frac{\langle a,{\mathrm{X}}\rangle+\langle b,{\mathrm{Y}}\rangle+A+B}{r}}(t)=\varphi_{\langle\tilde{a},{\mathrm{X}}\rangle+\langle\tilde{b},{\mathrm{Y}}\rangle+\frac{A+B}{r}}(t).

By the hypothesis the left hand side of (
1
) does not depend on
(\tilde{a},\tilde{b})
(= depend on
r
), and thus the limit on the
right hand side
then

\varphi_{\langle\tilde{a},{\mathrm{X}}\rangle+\langle\tilde{b},{\mathrm{Y}}\rangle+\frac{A+B}{r}}(t)\xrightarrow{r\to+\infty}\varphi_{\langle\tilde{a},{\mathrm{X}}\rangle+\langle\tilde{b},{\mathrm{Y}}\rangle}(t),

does not depend on
(\tilde{a},\tilde{b})
.
In particular, we have that the distribution of a statistic

\displaystyle\langle{a},{\mathrm{X}}\rangle+\langle{b},{\mathrm{Y}}\rangle=(\langle\tilde{a},{\mathrm{X}}\rangle+\langle\tilde{b},{\mathrm{Y}}\rangle)\sqrt{\|a\|^{2}+\|b\|^{2}}

dependence on
\|{a}\|^{2}+\|{b}\|^{2}
only. Let

\displaystyle h(\|{a}\|^{2}+\|{b}\|^{2})=Ee^{i(\langle a,{\mathrm{X}}\rangle+\langle b,{\mathrm{Y}}\rangle)}.

Because of the independence of
{\mathrm{X}}
and
{\mathrm{Y}}
, we may write

(2)

\displaystyle h(\|{a}\|^{2}+\|{b}\|^{2})=Ee^{i\langle a,{\mathrm{X}}\rangle}Ee^{i\langle b,{\mathrm{Y}}\rangle}=\varphi_{\mathrm{X}}(a)\varphi_{\mathrm{Y}}(b).

Evaluating (
2
) first when
a=\mathbf{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
and then when
b=\mathbf{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, we get

\displaystyle h(\|{b}\|^{2})=\varphi_{\mathrm{Y}}(b)\text{ and }h(\|{a}\|^{2})=\varphi_{\mathrm{X}}(a),

respectively. Substituting this into (
2
), we obtain

\displaystyle h(\|{a}\|^{2}+\|{b}\|^{2})=h(\|{a}\|^{2})h(\|{b}\|^{2}).

Note that
h(\cdot)
is continuous, hence by multiplicative Cauchy functional equation we get

\displaystyle h\left(\|{a}\|^{2}+\|{b}\|^{2}\right)=e^{c(\|{a}\|^{2}+\|{b}\|^{2})}.

Substituting
a=(a_{1},0,0,\dots,0)
and
b=\mathbf{0}
in this equation, we see that it can be read as

\displaystyle Ee^{i{\mathrm{X}}_{1}a_{1}}=e^{ca_{1}^{2}},\text{ i.e. ${\mathrm{X}}_{1}$ have a normal distribution, with zero mean.}

Dragging this line of reasoning to other random variables,
we see that
{\mathrm{X}}_{i}
and
{\mathrm{Y}}_{j}
have the same normal distribution, with zero mean.
The independence of random variables
{\mathrm{X}}_{1},\dots,{\mathrm{X}}_{m}
follows from the observation that, for all
a=(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}

\displaystyle\varphi_{\mathrm{X}}(a)=e^{{c\sum_{j=1}^{m}{a_{j}^{2}}}}=\varphi_{{\mathrm{X}}_{1}}(a_{1})\dots\varphi_{{\mathrm{X}}_{m}}(a_{m}).

∎

The construction of a new test is based directly on the Proposition below, that follows, in a sense, from Theorem
2.1
, namely if
A=B=0
, then Theorem
2.1
can be rewritten as follows.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let

({\mathrm{X}}_{1},\dots,{\mathrm{X}}_{m})\textrm{ and }({\mathrm{Y}}_{1},\dots,{\mathrm{Y}}_{n})
be independent random vectors, where
{\mathrm{X}}_{i}
and
{\mathrm{Y}}_{j}
are
nondegenerate,
E({\mathrm{X}}_{i}^{2})=1
,
E({\mathrm{Y}}_{j}^{2})=1
for
i\in[m]
,
j\in[n]
.
Then the following statements are equivalent:

i)

statistic
\langle a,{\mathrm{X}}\rangle+\langle b,{\mathrm{Y}}\rangle
has a distribution which does not depend on

(a_{1},\dots,a_{m},b_{1},\dots,b_{n}),

whenever
\|a\|^{2}+\|b\|^{2}=1
;

ii)

random variables
{\mathrm{X}}_{1},\dots,{\mathrm{X}}_{m},{\mathrm{Y}}_{1},\dots,{\mathrm{Y}}_{n}
are independent and have the same normal distribution
N(0,1)
.

Proof.

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
. We see that the distribution of

\langle a,{\mathrm{X}}\rangle+\langle b,{\mathrm{Y}}\rangle=\sqrt{\|a\|^{2}+\|b\|^{2}}\frac{\langle a,{\mathrm{X}}\rangle+\langle b,{\mathrm{Y}}\rangle}{\sqrt{\|a\|^{2}+\|b\|^{2}}}

depends only on
\|a\|^{2}+\|b\|^{2}
, which by Theorem
2.1
implies that

{\mathrm{X}}_{i}
and
{\mathrm{Y}}_{j}
are independent and have the same normal distribution
N(0,1)
(because we assume that
E({\mathrm{X}}_{i}^{2})=1
,
E({\mathrm{Y}}_{j}^{2})=1
).

(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
. We compute the characteristic function

Ee^{i\langle a,{\mathrm{X}}\rangle+i\langle b,{\mathrm{Y}}\rangle}=e^{-(\|a\|^{2}+\|b\|^{2})/2},

from which we see that condition
(i)
is satisfied.

∎

3.
The test statistic

In this section we propose a new class of test statistics for testing the null hypothesis that the sample comes from a multivariate normal distribution with independent components. In the univarate case it reduces to the null normality hypothesis.

Our methodology applied in this construction is based on distances between empirical and theoretical quantities.
There are many types of distances in theory of hypothesis testing that can be defined between statistical objects. One of the best known and mostly applied is the
L_{2}
distance. If
F
is the cumulative distribution function (cdf) of a random variable and
F_{n}
is the empirical function (edf), then their
L_{2}
distance can be expressed as
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}(F_{n}(x)-F(x))^{2}dx
, introduced by Cramér
[
6
]
.
Later modifications of this distance lead to Cramér-von Mises test and to Kolmogorov-Smirnov test
[
18
]
.
There is, however, another important distance, if the sample comes from a
d
-dimensional space, where
d\geq 1
.
If we want to test multivariate normality then we can use the distance between empirical and theoretical characteristic function; see
[
3
,
9
]
.
More recently, the characterization of a test for multivariate independence was given in
[
19
,
20
]
. Suppose that
{\mathrm{X}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m},{\mathrm{Y}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
are real-valued random vectors with characteristic functions
{\varphi_{X}}
and
{\varphi_{\mathrm{Y}}}
,
respectively. Then, for measuring independence, we can use the following distance

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{m+n}}|{\varphi_{{\mathrm{X}},{\mathrm{Y}}}}(t,s)-{\varphi_{\mathrm{X}}}(t){\varphi_{\mathrm{Y}}}(s)|^{2}w(t,s)dtds
, where
w(t,s)
is an arbitrary positive weight function for which the integral above exists. We put forward a test that is also based on the distance between the characteristic function and some constant, and it was inspired by the articles
[
3
,
19
,
20
,
9
]
.
Our approach is based on the following reasoning.

Condition
(i)
from Proposition
2.2
simply tells us that we get statement
(ii)
if the distribution of the statistic

\langle a,{\mathrm{X}}\rangle+\langle b,{\mathrm{Y}}\rangle
is constant on the
(n+m)
-sphere with radius
1
.
This requirement
can be rewritten using the characteristic function, namely, we get statement
(ii)
if and only if the function

\displaystyle Ee^{i\langle a,{\mathrm{X}}\rangle+i\langle b,{\mathrm{Y}}\rangle}=\varphi_{\mathrm{X}}(a)\varphi_{\mathrm{Y}}(b)

is constant on the unit sphere
\|a\|^{2}+\|b\|^{2}=1
, where
a\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
b\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
From the proof of Proposition
2.2
we also know that this constant function must equal
e^{-\frac{1}{2}}
, namely

\displaystyle\varphi_{\mathrm{X}}(a)\varphi_{\mathrm{Y}}(b)-e^{-\frac{1}{2}}=0

for all
\|a\|^{2}+\|b\|^{2}=1
or equivalently,

(3)

\displaystyle\int_{S_{n+m}}|\varphi_{\mathrm{X}}(a)\varphi_{\mathrm{Y}}(b)-e^{-\frac{1}{2}}|^{2}dS_{n+m}=0,

where
\int_{S_{n+m}}\cdot dS_{n+m}
is the surface integral over
S_{n+m}=\{t\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n+m}\mid\|t\|=1\}
.
Finiteness of the integral above follows directly from
|\varphi_{\mathrm{X}}(a)\varphi_{\mathrm{Y}}(b)|\leq 1
and
e^{-\frac{1}{2}}<1
, namely we see that

\displaystyle\int_{S_{n+m}}|\varphi_{\mathrm{X}}(a)\varphi_{\mathrm{Y}}(b)-e^{-\frac{1}{2}}|^{2}dS_{n+m}\leq(1-e^{-\frac{1}{2}})^{2}|S_{n+m}|.

Let us assume that we have a simple random sample
{\mathrm{\mathbf{X}}}=(\boldsymbol{X}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{X}_{N})
from a multivariate distribution with
m
components, i.e. the data have the following structure:

{\mathrm{\mathbf{X}}}=\begin{bmatrix}x_{1,1}&x_{1,2}&\dots&x_{1,m}\\
x_{2,1}&x_{2,2}&\dots&x_{2,m}\\
\lx@intercol\hfil\leaders{\hbox{.}}{\hfill}\hfil\lx@intercol\\
x_{N,1}&x_{N,2}&\dots&x_{N,m}\end{bmatrix}.

We want to test the null hypothesis

\mathcal{H}^{(m)}_{0}
:

\mathcal{H}^{(m)}_{1}
:

all columns of
{\mathrm{\mathbf{X}}}
are independent,

vs.

\mathcal{H}^{(m)}_{0}
is not true.

and have a normal distribution

Let
\widetilde{{\mathrm{\mathbf{X}}}}
denote the matrix obtained from
{\mathrm{\mathbf{X}}}
by columnwise standardization.
Let

\varphi_{\widetilde{{\mathrm{\mathbf{X}}}}}(a)
be the empirical characteristic functions of
\widetilde{{\mathrm{\mathbf{X}}}}
defined by

\varphi_{\widetilde{{\mathrm{\mathbf{X}}}}}(a)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}e^{i\langle a,\widetilde{{\mathrm{\mathbf{X}}}}_{k}\rangle},

where
\widetilde{{\mathrm{\mathbf{X}}}}_{k}
is the
k
th row of the matricex
\widetilde{{\mathrm{\mathbf{X}}}}
.
Similarly, the empirical counterpart of characteristic function of random variable
\langle a,X\rangle+\langle b,Y\rangle
is

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{j,k}e^{i(\langle a,{\boldsymbol{X}}_{j}\rangle+\langle b,{\boldsymbol{X}}_{k}\rangle)},

where
a=(a_{1},...,a_{m})^{\textup{T}}
and
b=(b_{1},...,b_{m})^{\textup{T}}
.
Assuming that
X
and
Y
are equally distributed as
\boldsymbol{X}_{1}
, the natural test statistics based on (
3
) is

(4)

\displaystyle M_{m}

\displaystyle=N\int\left|\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{j,k}e^{i(\langle a,\widetilde{\boldsymbol{X}}_{j}\rangle+\langle b,\widetilde{\boldsymbol{X}}_{k}\rangle)}-e^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right|^{2}dS_{2m}(a,b),

where
a=(a_{1},...,a_{m})^{\textup{T}}
and
b=(b_{1},...,b_{m})^{\textup{T}}
such that
\langle a,a\rangle+\langle b,b\rangle=1
.

Clearly, we are interested in one-sided test, that is the right-tailed test, because we see from the above construction that we reject null hypothesis for large values of
M_{m}
. It is clear that test statistic is location-scale invariant under the null hypothesis, hence we may derive critical values for testing using Monte Carlo approach.

4.
Testing univariate normality

Consider now a univariate simple random sample
\boldsymbol{X}=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
. In this case test statistic
M_{1}
can be expressed in a simpler form.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\boldsymbol{\tilde{X}}=(\tilde{X}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{X}_{n})
be the standardized sample. The statistic
M_{1}
has the form

(5)

\displaystyle M_{1}

\displaystyle=2\pi N\Bigg[\frac{1}{N^{4}}\sum_{n,j,k,l=1}^{N}J(d(\tilde{X}_{n}-\tilde{X}_{k},\tilde{X}_{j}-\tilde{X}_{l}))-e^{-\frac{1}{2}}\frac{2}{N^{2}}\sum_{n,j=1}^{N}J(d(\tilde{X}_{n},\tilde{X}_{j}))+e^{-1}\Bigg].

where
J
is the Bessel function (of order zero) of the first kind, namely
J(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\frac{(z^{2}/4)^{k}}{(k!)^{2}}
and
d
is the distance from origin to point
(x,y)
i.e.,
d(x,y)=\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}
.

Proof.

Let us calculate the integral
in the right hand side of (
4
).

\displaystyle M_{1}

\displaystyle=2\pi N\int\left|\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{i,j}e^{i(a_{1}\tilde{X}_{i}+a_{2}\tilde{X}_{j})}-e^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right|^{2}dS_{2}(a,b)

\displaystyle=2\pi N\int U^{2}(a,b)dS_{2}(a,b).

Since

\displaystyle U^{2}(a,b)

\displaystyle=\Big|\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{i,j}e^{i(a\tilde{X}_{i}+b\tilde{X}_{j})}-e^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{i,j}\cos(a\tilde{X}_{i}+b\tilde{X}_{j}))-e^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{i,j}\sin(a\tilde{X}_{i}+b\tilde{X}_{j}))\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N^{4}}\sum_{i,j,k,l}\Bigg(\Big(\cos(a\tilde{X}_{i}+b\tilde{X}_{j})-e^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Big)\Big(\cos(a\tilde{X}_{k}+b\tilde{X}_{l})-e^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle+\sin(a\tilde{X}_{i}+b\tilde{X}_{j})\sin(a\tilde{X}_{k}+b\tilde{X}_{l})\Bigg),

switching to polar coordinates we obtain

\displaystyle U^{2}(a,b)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N^{4}}\sum_{i,j,k,l}\Big((\cos(a\tilde{X}_{i}+b\tilde{X}_{j}))((\cos(a\tilde{X}_{k}+b\tilde{X}_{l})))+\sin(a\tilde{X}_{i}+b\tilde{X}_{j})\sin(a\tilde{X}_{k}+b\tilde{X}_{l})\Big)

\displaystyle-\frac{2e^{-\frac{1}{2}}}{N^{2}}\sum_{i,j}\cos(a\tilde{X}_{i}+b\tilde{X}_{j})+e^{-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N^{4}}\sum_{i,j,k,l}(\cos(a(\tilde{X}_{i}-\tilde{X}_{k})+b(\tilde{X}_{j}-\tilde{X}_{l}))-\frac{2e^{-\frac{1}{2}}}{N^{2}}\sum_{i,j}\cos(a\tilde{X}_{i}+b\tilde{X}_{j})+e^{-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N^{4}}\sum_{i,j,k,l}(\cos(\cos\alpha(\tilde{X}_{i}-\tilde{X}_{k})+\sin\alpha(\tilde{X}_{j}-\tilde{X}_{l}))-\frac{2e^{-\frac{1}{2}}}{N^{2}}\sum_{i,j}\cos(\cos\alpha\tilde{X}_{i}+b\sin\alpha\tilde{X}_{j})+e^{-1},

Since we need integration over
S_{2}
, we have
to focus on computing
the following integral

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\cos(x\cos\alpha+y\sin\alpha)d\alpha,\qquad\text{ for }x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}.

By trigonometric
identities the linear combination, or harmonic addition, of sine and cosine waves is equivalent to a single cosine wave with a phase shift and scaled amplitude, namely

\displaystyle x\cos\alpha+y\sin\alpha=\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}\cos(\alpha-\textup{atan2}(y,x)),

where
\textup{atan2}(y,x)
is the generalization of
\arctan(y/x)
that covers the entire circular range (we don’t need a formal definition of
atan2
).
Thus for
x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
xy\neq 0
we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\cos(x\cos\alpha+y\sin\alpha)d\alpha

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\cos\big(\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}\cos\big(\alpha-\textup{atan2}(y,x)\big)\big)d\alpha

\displaystyle=\int_{-\textup{atan2}(y,x)}^{2\pi-\textup{atan2}(y,x)}\cos\Big(\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}\cos t\Big)dt

\displaystyle=2\pi J(\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}),

where we used the following identity – see
[
1
, page 360]

(6)

\displaystyle 2\pi J(z)=\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{iz\cos\alpha}d\alpha=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\cos(z\cos\alpha)d\alpha=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\cos(z\sin\alpha)d\alpha.

If either
x=0
or
y=0
, then the formula above is also true because we can use directly equation (
6
).

Therefore
M_{1}
has the representation (
5
).
∎

5.
Testing bivariate normality and independence

Consider now a bivariate simple random sample
{\mathrm{\mathbf{X}}}=(\boldsymbol{X}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{X}_{N})
, where
\boldsymbol{X}_{j}=(X_{j1},X_{j2})
,
j=1,\ldots,N
.
Let
\widetilde{\bf{X}}=(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{X}}_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{\boldsymbol{X}}_{N})
be its standardization.
Here, the test statistic (
4
) becomes

\displaystyle M_{2}

\displaystyle=N\int\left|\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{j,k}e^{i(a_{1}\widetilde{X}_{j1}+a_{2}\widetilde{X}_{j2}+b_{1}\widetilde{X}_{k1}+b_{2}\widetilde{X}_{k2})}-e^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right|^{2}dS_{4}(a,b)

\displaystyle=\int\Bigg(\frac{1}{N^{4}}\sum_{i,j,k,l}(\cos(a_{1}(\tilde{X}_{i1}-\tilde{X}_{k1})+a_{2}(\tilde{X}_{i2}-\tilde{X}_{k2})+b_{1}(\tilde{X}_{j1}-\tilde{X}_{l1})+b_{2}(\tilde{X}_{j2}-\tilde{X}_{l2}))

\displaystyle-\frac{2e^{-\frac{1}{2}}}{N^{2}}\sum_{i,j}\cos(a_{1}\tilde{X}_{i1}+a_{2}\tilde{X}_{i2}+b_{1}\tilde{X}_{j1}+b_{2}\tilde{X}_{j2})+e^{-1}\Bigg)dS_{4}(a,b),

where
a=(a_{1},a_{2})^{\textup{T}}
and
b=(b_{1},b_{2})^{\textup{T}}
such that
\langle a,a\rangle+\langle b,b\rangle=1
.

KS2

[

with data driven parameter selection introduced in
[

]

[
13

[

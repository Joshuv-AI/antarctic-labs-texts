Graph Reconstruction with Connectivity Queries
Kacper Kluk, Hoang La, Marta Piecyk

Abstract
We study a problem of reconstruction of connected graphs where the input gives all subsets of size k that induce a connected subgraph. Originally introduced by Bastide et al. (WG 2023) for triples ( k = 3 k=3 ), this problem received comprehensive attention in their work, alongside a study by Qi, who provided a complete characterization of graphs uniquely reconstructible via their connected triples, i.e. no other graphs share the same set of connected triples. Our contribution consists in output-polynomial time algorithms that enumerate every triangle-free graph (resp. every graph with bounded maximum degree) that is consistent with a specified set of connected k k -sets. Notably, we prove that triangle-free graphs are uniquely reconstructible, while graphs with bounded maximum degree that are consistent with the same k k -sets share a substantial common structure, differing only locally. We suspect that the problem is NP-hard in general and provide a NP-hardness proof for a variant where the connectivity is specified for only some k k -sets (with k k at least 4).



1
Introduction

Graph reconstruction primarily refers to the concept popularized by the Graph Reconstruction Conjecture by Kelly and Ulam
[
3
,
5
]
. This conjecture states that any graph with a minimum of three vertices can be uniquely reconstructed, up to isomorphism, using the multiset of subgraphs resulting from the removal of exactly one vertex. However, in this context, we delve into the reconstruction of labeled graphs, where we lack explicit knowledge about the structures of subgraphs of order
n-1
, as proposed in the Graph Reconstruction Conjecture. Instead, our focus lies on reconstructing graphs based solely on the connectivity of their subgraphs. Specifically, we address the task of reconstructing graphs by querying if a subgraph of order
k
is connected, for some fixed integer
k\geqslant 2
. This variant of graph reconstruction was recently introduced by Bastide, Cook, Erickson, Groenland, Kreveld, Mannens, and Vermeulen
[
1
]
, initially for triples, and more broadly for sets of vertices of size
k
, which we term
k
-sets for brevity.

Some problems lend themselves to straightforward solutions when access to answers for all possible queries is available, such as graph reconstruction using a distance oracle
[
2
]
. In such scenarios, our focus shifts towards optimizing the number of queries utilized. In graph reconstruction via connectivity queries on
k
-sets, it is evident that for
k=2
, we are effectively querying the edges of the graphs. However, for
k\geqslant 3
, even identifying a graph consistent with the answers to all queries becomes a nontrivial task.

Graph reconstruction inherently suggests the existence of a target graph we aim to rebuild. Yet, within this context, we may encounter arbitrary connected and disconnected
k
-sets. The questions regarding the existence and uniqueness of graphs satisfying these queries become significantly more intriguing. This problem bears potential applications in computational networks where concealing its exact structure is imperative while still being able to respond to local queries about potential data transfers to a sub-network, which would require connectivity, is necessary.

Hardness of the general case.
For the case where
k=3
, Bastide et al. showed an enumeration algorithm applicable to general graphs, leveraging a reduction of the problem to 2-SAT. Moreover, Qi
[
4
]
provided a characterization of graphs that are uniquely reconstructible from connected triples. However, extending this reduction to
k\geqslant 4
merely leads to the problem being reduced to 3-SAT or simply SAT. Moreover, we suspect that for
k\geqslant 4
, the task of identifying a graph consistent with a given set of connected
k
-sets may be NP-hard in general. To substantiate this suspicion, we show NP-hardness for a modified version of this problem, wherein not every connected and disconnected
k
-set is given. We point out, that such a version of the problem is still tractable for
k=3
, as the reduction to
2
-SAT can handle with an uncomplete information.

Motivation.
Shifting our focus away from the general scenario, what if we possess additional information regarding the target graph or specific graph classes? Our attention naturally gravitates towards classes wherein existence can be efficiently verified or uniqueness can be established structurally. For instance, what if we know that our target graph is triangle-free or has bounded maximum degree? In what follows, we delve into the rationale and intuition behind our findings.

The graphs that we aim to rebuild are all labeled, finite, and simple. It is essential to note that this form of reconstruction does not always yield a unique labeled graph: a path on vertices
a
,
b
,
c
,
d
has the same connected triples as the isomorphic path on
a
,
c
,
b
,
d
. More generally, we do not always obtain isomorphic graphs. For example, a clique and a clique lacking a matching have identical connected
k
-sets. We point out that from the connected
k
-sets, we are able to determine the vertex sets of all connected components of size at least
k
, but we cannot distinguish connected components of size at most
k-1
. Therefore, we will always assume that our graphs are connected and reasonably large.

Here, we direct your attention to two specific examples illustrating the challenge of reconstructing graphs solely through connectivity. The first example revolves around the existence of edges between twin vertices. Twin vertices share precisely the same neighbors, excluding themselves. Consequently, when examining their connected
k
-sets, we cannot discern between a graph where they share an edge and one where they do not.

The second example involves a star or, more generally, a graph with a universal vertex. Consider
G^{\prime}
a disconnected graph, where each connected component has order of at most
k-1
. Now, let
G
be
G^{\prime}
with an additional universal vertex. Analogous to the previous example, distinguishing between
G
and a star of degree
|V(G^{\prime})|
based on connected
k
-sets is impossible.

Our results.
In both of the previous examples, with the additional assumption of triangle-freeness, we are left with only one potential outcome. Indeed, not only do we prove that enumerating connected triangle-free graphs that are consistent with some given connected
k
-sets can be done efficiently, but we also show that when such a graph exists, there are no alternative connected triangle-free graphs sharing the same
k
-sets. More formally, we prove the following statements.

Theorem 1.1

Let
k
be an integer with
k\geqslant 2
. For a complete set of connected and disconnected
k
-sets on
V
, we can enumerate every connected triangle-free graph on
V
consistent with such sets in polynomial time in
|V|
.

Theorem 1.2

Let
k
be an integer with
k\geqslant 3
. If
G
is a large enough triangle-free connected graph, then
G
is uniquely reconstructible in the class of triangle-free graphs.

In the second example, the presence of the universal vertex, ensuring the graph’s connectivity, opens the door to a multitude of different structures that can be concealed within a set of vertices spanning (almost) the entire graph. Intuitively, bounding the maximum degree of the graph restricts such variability to only sets of constant size, at most the maximum degree. While this constraint does not guarantee uniqueness – in fact, quite the opposite; we may still have an exponential number of possible subquartic graphs consistent with the same connected
k
-sets (a path
u_{1}\dots u_{n}
where each
u_{i}
has two extra neighbors
v_{i}
and
w_{i}
that are potentially adjacent) – we anticipate these graphs to exhibit a substantial common structure (the common path
u_{1}\dots u_{n}
with the extra neighbors) and differing only locally (the potential edge between
v_{i}
and
w_{i}
). Indeed, we confirm these intuitions and prove the following.

Theorem 1.3

Let
k
and
d
be integers such that
k\geqslant 2
and
d\geqslant 1
. For a complete set of connected and disconnected
k
-sets on
V
, we can enumerate every connected graph of maximum degree
d
consistent with such
k
-sets in polynomial time in
|V|
and the size of the output.

This theorem comes from a more technical result in which we introduce the concept of a “skeleton” which captures this large common structure shared among consistent graphs and explicitly shows their local differences.

For a positive integer

Partial graphs.

For a partial graph

on

(resp.

For

, let

. We say that a set of vertices induces a connected subgraph in

. The open neighborhood of

.

Connected

We say that

be a partial graph. We say that

is consistent with

\operatorname{\overline{\mathcal{S}}}_{k}
if there exists an assignment of unknown edges (

) of

We say that a graph

Finally, fix a pair of vertices

Lemma 4

Fix

. Let

be a complete set of

Proof

Let

denote any triangle-free graph consistent with
\operatorname{\mathcal{S}}_{k},\operatorname{\overline{\mathcal{S}}}_{k}
such that

x_{1},\dots,x_{2k-4}\in N_{G}(u)
.

Applying

,

Let

. We will show that the assumptions of

Finally, we assemble the different components of our algorithm to enumerate every connected triangle-free graph that is consistent with

and

Theorem 3.1

Let

be an integer with

Let

of vertex of such degree and for every subset

In the second case, we pick any connected

We are left with a polynomial-size list of potential solutions. Finally, we filter out all solutions that do not satisfy all tuples

3.2
Graphs with bounded maximum degree

In this subsection, we introduce the notion of a skeleton. A skeleton is a partial graph where the unknown edges are contained in some disjoint small sets. The idea is that using a skeleton we can describe a set of graphs in which the differences between its members only occur in small sets of constant size which do not interact with one another. Formally, a skeleton is defined as follows.

Definition 1

Let a
skeleton
be a partial graph
H
with a family of disjoint vertex subsets
V_{1},V_{2},\dots,V_{m}
and a family of collections
\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2},\dots,\mathcal{C}_{m}
such that
E_{U}(H)=\bigcup_{i\in[\ell]}\binom{V_{i}}{2}
and
\mathcal{C}_{i}
is a collection of graphs on
V_{i}
for every
1\leqslant i\leqslant m
. The
width
of a skeleton is
\max_{i\in[m]}|V_{i}|
. A graph
G
is a
completion
of a skeleton if there exists graphs
C_{1}\in\mathcal{C}_{1},\dots,C_{m}\in\mathcal{C}_{m}
such that
G
is a supergraph of
H
and
G[V_{i}]=C_{i}
for every
1\leqslant i\leqslant m
.

Using this concept, we can capture the common structure present in connected graphs of bounded maximum degree that share the same
k
-sets even though there might be exponentially many of them.

Theorem 3.2

Let
k
and
d
be integers such that
k\geqslant 2
and
d\geqslant 1
. For a complete set of
k
-sets
\operatorname{\mathcal{S}}_{k},\operatorname{\overline{\mathcal{S}}}_{k}
on
V
, in polynomial time, we can find a constant-size (depending on

and
d
) family
\mathcal{F}
of skeletons of width at most
d
such that a connected graph of maximum degree
d
is consistent with
\operatorname{\mathcal{S}}_{k},\operatorname{\overline{\mathcal{S}}}_{k}
if and only if it is a completion of a skeleton of
\mathcal{F}
.

This implies that we can enumerate every connected graph of maximum degree
d
that is consistent with our given
k
-sets in a reasonable time. Moreover, the difference between these graphs are very local since any combination of graphs in
\mathcal{C}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{C}_{m}
will produce a desired consistent graph.

Corollary 1

Let
k
and
d
be integers such that
k\geqslant 2
and
d\geqslant 1
. For a complete set of
k
-sets
S_{k},\overline{S}_{k}
on
V
, we can enumerate every connected graph of maximum degree

consistent with
S_{k},\overline{S}_{k}
in polynomial time in
|V|
and the size of the output.

Before proving the main result of this section, we introduce a notion of importance for unknown edges of a

Definition 2
(Importance of an unknown edge)

Let

be a partial graph on

of

.

The fact that an unknown edge is not important for some

Proposition 2

Let

be a
k
-set, let

be the complete graph on vertices of

, let

be a partial graph on the vertices of

, and let

such that

. Then,
(a)

is not important for

in

if and only if

(b)
there exists a path on vertices of

and edges of
E(H)
between

and

or

(c)

is disconnected.

Proof

First, we prove that
(c)
implies

. Suppose that

is disconnected. Every supergraph

of

must be disconnected. Therefore,

is not important for

Now, we prove that

between

and

, then every supergraph

of

is not important for

in

Finally, we show that if

also cannot hold, i.e.

is an important edge for

in

of

between

and

on

from

. If

and

and removing another edge of

from this path until

is disconnected. This process must end because at worst we remove every path between

and

in

and

between

We are ready to prove

are completely determined). It suffices to find a connected subgraph
C
in
H^{*}[L_{0},\dots,L_{i^{\prime}-2}]
containing
r
and such that
C
along with
r
,
q
,
P
, and
v
has order
k
(such
C
exists since
L_{0}=T
has size order at least
k
and layers
0
to
i^{\prime}-2
are connected). Observe that
vq\in E_{N}(H^{*})
and
vc\in E_{N}(H^{*})
for every
c\in V(C)
due to there being at least one layer (
L_{i^{\prime}-1}
) separating
v
and these vertices. Such a set verifies the property required to determine unknown edges in Step 3, therefore
uv
must be known, a contradiction. Symmetrically, the same holds when we can choose
t=u
, i.e.
xy
must be a known edge. Finally, we look at the case where
t
is an internal vertex of
P
, i.e.
u
(resp.
x
) is in layer
L_{j^{\prime}}
for some
j^{\prime}>i
. The same argument as above will imply that both
uv
and
xy
must be known after Step 3. Indeed, a neighbor
q
of

in
L_{i^{\prime}-1}
is such that
qv\in E_{N}(H^{*})
since
v\in L_{j^{\prime}}
for
j^{\prime}>i
and the rest of the arguments unfolds in a similar fashion.

Step 4.
To finish our skeletons, we only need to generate the family of collections of graphs
\mathcal{C}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{C}_{m}
. For every
1\leqslant j\leqslant m
,
\mathcal{C}_{j}
has size at most
2^{\binom{d}{2}}
since
V_{j}
has size at most
d
. For every possible supergraph
G_{j}
on
V_{j}
that results in vertices with degree at most
d
in the whole graph (this is well defined since the
V_{1},\dots,V_{m}
are disjoint), we verify if
G_{j}
satisfy our
k
-sets by checking the following. For every
k
-set
S\in\operatorname{\mathcal{S}}_{k}
(resp.
S^{\prime}\in\operatorname{\overline{\mathcal{S}}}_{k}
) such that
S\cap V_{j}\neq\emptyset
(resp.
S^{\prime}\cap V_{j}\neq\emptyset
), we assume that every unknown edge in
S
(resp.
S^{\prime}
) that is not included in
V_{j}
is a non-edge (resp. an edge), the unknown edges in
V_{j}
will be replaced with edges of

. If

(resp.
S^{\prime}
) induces a connected (resp. disconnected) subgraph with these assumptions, then we say that
G_{j}
satisfies
S
(resp.
S^{\prime}
). If
G_{j}
satisfies every
k
-set with a non-empty intersection with
V_{j}
, then we keep

as a graph of our collection
\mathcal{C}_{j}
. For this, we need to check every
k
-set in
\operatorname{\mathcal{S}}_{k}
and
\operatorname{\overline{\mathcal{S}}}_{k}
. Therefore, Step 4 is done in

time.

Correctness.
Now, we prove that the completions of our family of skeletons correspond to all graphs with maximum degree
d
that are consistent with
\operatorname{\mathcal{S}}_{k}
and
\operatorname{\overline{\mathcal{S}}}_{k}
. Due to our invariant, up to the end of Step 3, a graph with maximum degree
d
that is consistent with
\operatorname{\mathcal{S}}_{k}
and
\operatorname{\overline{\mathcal{S}}}_{k}
must be a supergraph of one of our partial graphs. If for every skeleton,
\mathcal{C}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{C}_{m}
contain every possible supergraph on
V_{1},\dots,V_{m}
, then a consistent graph must be a completion of a skeleton. In Step 3, we go further by keeping only “relevant” supergraphs. Now, we show that a graph with maximum degree
d
is consistent with
\operatorname{\mathcal{S}}_{k}
and
\operatorname{\overline{\mathcal{S}}}_{k}
if and only if it is a completion of one of our skeleton. This implies that, despite our supergraphs being determined independently for each
V_{j}
, any combination of these graphs will produce a consistent graph.

Suppose by contradiction that there exists a completion
G
of a skeleton that consists of
H^{*},V_{1},\dots,V_{m},\mathcal{C}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{C}_{m}
that is not consistent with
\operatorname{\mathcal{S}}_{k}
and
\operatorname{\overline{\mathcal{S}}}_{k}
(since they all have maximum degree at most
d
). First, suppose that there exists
S\in\operatorname{\mathcal{S}}_{k}
(resp.
S\in\operatorname{\overline{\mathcal{S}}}_{k}
) such that
G[S]
is disconnected (resp. connected). Unimportant unknown edges for
S
in
H^{*}
cannot change the connectivity of
S
no matter their assignment; therefore, we can assume that every unimportant unknown edge for
S
in
H^{*}
is set to a non-edge in
G[S]
. The remaining unknown edges for
S
must be important and by the previous claim, we know these edges are all inside a
V_{i}
for some
1\leqslant i\leqslant m
. Since graphs in
\mathcal{C}_{i}
are chosen such that they satisfy all
k
-sets that intersect with
V_{i}
,
G[S]
cannot be disconnected; otherwise, no such graphs exist, i.e. no graphs with maximum degree
d
can be consistent with
\operatorname{\mathcal{S}}_{k}
and
\operatorname{\overline{\mathcal{S}}}_{k}
.
This concludes the proof of
Theorem

3.2
.∎

4
Uniqueness

In this section, we consider a graph
G
with
k
-sets
\operatorname{\mathcal{S}}_{k}(G)
and
\operatorname{\overline{\mathcal{S}}}_{k}(G)
and we study the structure of the family of graphs consistent with
\operatorname{\mathcal{S}}_{k}(G)
and
\operatorname{\overline{\mathcal{S}}}_{k}(G)
to see when
G
is uniquely reconstructible.

The following property of connected graphs will be crucial.

Proposition 3

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph and let
uv\in E
. For every
S\in\operatorname{\mathcal{S}}_{k}(G)
such that
v\in S,u\notin S
, there exists
v^{\prime}\in S-\{v\}
such that
(S-\{v^{\prime}\})\cup\{u\}\in\operatorname{\mathcal{S}}_{k}(G)
.

Proof

Since
G[S]
is connected, there exists a spanning tree of
G[S]
. Since a tree always has at least two leaves, consider
v^{\prime}\neq v
to be a leaf of such a spanning tree. Observe that
G[S-\{v^{\prime}\}]
also contains a spanning tree as a subgraph. Therefore, it is connected. Combining it with the fact that
uv\in E
, we conclude that
G[(S-\{v^{\prime}\})\cup\{u\}]
is connected. In other words,
(S-\{v^{\prime}\})\cup\{u\}\in\operatorname{\mathcal{S}}_{k}(G)
.
∎

This motivates the following definition.

Definition 3

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph and let
uv\notin E
. We say that
u
and
v
are
clear non-neighbors
if (i) there exists
S\in\operatorname{\mathcal{S}}_{k}(G)
such that
v\in S,u\notin S
and for every
v^{\prime}\in S-\{v\}
, it holds that
(S-\{v^{\prime}\})\cup\{u\}\in\operatorname{\overline{\mathcal{S}}}_{k}(G)
, or (ii) there exists
S\in\operatorname{\mathcal{S}}_{k}(G)
such that
u\in S,v\notin S
and for every
u^{\prime}\in S-\{u\}
, it holds that
(S-\{u^{\prime}\})\cup\{v\}\in\operatorname{\overline{\mathcal{S}}}_{k}(G)
. Otherwise, we say that they are
fake neighbors
.

Proposition

3
implies that non-edges between clear non-neighbors are present in every graph consistent with the same
k
-sets.

Corollary 2

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph and let
uv\notin E
. If
u
and
v
are clear non-neighbors, then for every graph
G^{\prime}
consistent with
\operatorname{\mathcal{S}}_{k}(G)
and
\operatorname{\overline{\mathcal{S}}}_{k}(G)
, we have
uv\notin E(G^{\prime})
.

What follows is a sufficient condition for two vertices to be clear non-neighbors.

Proposition 4

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph and let
u\in V
. If there exists a subgraph
G_{u}
such that
u
is isolated in
G_{u}
,
G_{u}-u
is connected, and
|V(G_{u}-u)|\geqslant k
, then for every
v\in V(G_{u})
,
u
and
v
are clear non-neighbors.

Proof

Consider
v\in V(G_{u})
, a connected subgraph of
G_{u}
of size
k
containing
v
, and let
S
be its set of vertices. We have
S\in\operatorname{\mathcal{S}}_{k}(G)
,
v\in S
and
u\notin S
. Moreover, for every vertex
u^{\prime}
in
S-\{v\}
,
S-\{u^{\prime}\}+\{u\}\in\operatorname{\overline{\mathcal{S}}}_{k}(G)
because
u
is an isolated vertex in
G_{u}
. Therefore, by definition,
u
and
v
are clear non-neighbors.
∎

Lemma

5
describes the structure of a graph in which there is some pair of fake neighbors.

Lemma 5

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph and let
uv\notin E
be such that
u
and
v
are fake neighbors.
Let
C_{u}
(resp.
C_{v}
) be the connected component containing
u
in
G-N(v)
(resp.
v
in
G-N(u)
). We have
|V(C_{u})|\leqslant k-1
and
|V(C_{v})|\leqslant k-1
. In particular, the connected component(s) containing
u
and
v
in
G-(N(u)\cap N(v))
has size (have size in total) at most
|V(C_{u})\cup V(C_{v})|\leqslant 2k-2
. Moreover, if
|V(C_{u})\cup V(C_{v})|\leqslant k-1
, then
G
is not uniquely reconstructible.

Proof

First, observe that
v
(resp.
u
) is not adjacent to any vertex in
C_{u}
(resp.
C_{v}
). Therefore,
v
is an isolated vertex in
C_{u}+v
. By
Proposition

4
,
|V(C_{u})|\leqslant k-1
. Symmetrically, the same reasoning shows that
|V(C_{v})|\leqslant k-1
. The connected component(s) containing
u
and
v
in
G-(N(u)\cap N(v))
is a subgraph of
G[V(C_{u})\cup V(C_{v})]
, so it (they) has size (have size in total) at most
|V(C_{u})\cup V(C_{v})|\leqslant 2k-2
.

Now, suppose that
|V(C_{u})\cup V(C_{v})|\leqslant k-1
. Let
G^{\prime}=G+uv
, we will show that
G^{\prime}
is consistent with
\operatorname{\mathcal{S}}_{k}(G)
and
\operatorname{\overline{\mathcal{S}}}_{k}(G)
; in other words,
G
is not uniquely reconstructible. If
G^{\prime}
is not consistent with some
k
-set of
G
, then it implies that there exists
S\in\operatorname{\overline{\mathcal{S}}}_{k}(G)
containing
u
and
v
such that
G^{\prime}[S]
is connected. Since
G[S]
is disconnected and
G^{\prime}=G+uv
,
uv
must be a bridge in
G^{\prime}[S]
and
G[S]
has exactly two connected components, one containing
u
, the other containing
v
. Therefore,
S
cannot contain any common neighbor of
u
and
v
, and
S\subseteq V(C_{u})\cup V(C_{v})
. This is a contradiction because
k=|S|\leqslant|V(C_{u})\cup V(C_{v})|\leqslant k-1
. This completes the proof.
∎

4.1
Uniqueness for triangle-free graphs

We start with showing that if in a triangle-free graph we have a vertex
v
of large degree, then the neighborhood of
v
is uniquely reconstructible.

Lemma 6

Let
k
be an integer with
k\geqslant 3
and
r\geqslant 3k-6
. The graph
K_{1,r}
is uniquely reconstructible in the class of triangle-free graphs.

Proof

Let
r\geqslant 3k-6
. Suppose that there exists
G
that is consistent with
\operatorname{\mathcal{S}}_{k}(K_{1,r})
and
\operatorname{\overline{\mathcal{S}}}_{k}(K_{1,r})
. Let
V
be the set of vertices of
K_{1,r}
and
v
be the vertex in
K_{1,r}
with degree
r
. For every
S\subseteq V-\{v\}
such that
|S|=k-1
, at least one vertex of
S
must be adjacent to
v
since
S\cup\{v\}\in\operatorname{\mathcal{S}}_{k}(K_{1,r})
. Let
A
be the set of the vertices non-adjacent to
v
. Then
A
has size at most
k-2
. If
A=\emptyset
, then by the fact that we consider only triangle-free graphs, we already have that
G
is exactly
K_{1,r}
.

So suppose that
A\neq\emptyset
. Let
B=V-(A\cup\{v\})
. For
a\in A
and
B^{\prime}\subseteq B
such that
|B^{\prime}|=k-2
, there is at least one vertex in
B^{\prime}
adjacent to
a
since we have
B^{\prime}\cup\{a,v\}\in\operatorname{\mathcal{S}}_{k}(K_{1,r})
and
av\notin E(G)
. Therefore, there are at most
k-3
vertices in
B
non-adjacent to
a
.
Consequently, the degree of any vertex
a\in A
is at least
|B|-k+3
, and we have that
|B|=r-|A|\geqslant 3k-6-k+2=2k-4
. Thus
a
is adjacent to at least
k-1
vertices of
B
, let
b_{1},\ldots,b_{k-1}
be neighbors of
a
in
B
. Then
\{a,b_{1}\ldots,b_{k-1}\}
induces a connected subgraph in
G
, but
\{a,b_{1}\ldots,b_{k-1}\}\notin\operatorname{\mathcal{S}}_{k}(K_{1,r})
as the only connected
k
-sets are the ones containing
v
, a contradiction.
∎

Now, we are ready to prove that triangle-free graphs are uniquely reconstructible.

Theorem 4.1

Let
k
be an integer with
k\geqslant 3
. If
G
is a triangle-free connected graph on at least
(2k-2)(3k-7)^{2}+3k-6
vertices, then
G
is uniquely reconstructible in the class of triangle-free graphs.

Proof

Suppose by that there exists
G^{\prime}
that is consistent with
\operatorname{\mathcal{S}}_{k}(G)
and
\operatorname{\overline{\mathcal{S}}}_{k}(G)
. If an induced subgraph
G[V^{\prime}]
of
G
is uniquely reconstructible, then
G^{\prime}[V^{\prime}]=G[V]
. Therefore, if there is a vertex
v
of degree at least
3k-6
, then by
Lemma

6
,
G[N[v]]
is uniquely determined. In this case,
G^{\prime}
can be uniquely reconstructed by
Lemma

4
since
3k-6\geqslant 2k-4
. In other words,
G^{\prime}=G
. Now, we can assume that the maximum degree of
G
is at most
3k-7
.

First, we show that if
uv\notin E(G)
, then
uv\notin E(G^{\prime})
. If
u,v
are clear non-neighbors, then by
corollary

2
,
uv\notin E(G^{\prime})
. So now assume that
u
and
v
are fake neighbors. By
Lemma

5
, the connected component(s) of
G-(N(u)\cap N(v))
containing
u
and
v
have size at most
2k-2
. In particular, it implies that
u
and
v
have a common neighbor since
|V(G)|>2k-2
. We will show that
u
and
v
also have a common neighbor in
G^{\prime}
, which will prove that
uv\notin E(G^{\prime})
since
G^{\prime}
must be triangle-free.

Observe that
|N(u)\cap N(v)|\leqslant 3k-7
due to the bound on the maximum degree of
G
. Furthermore, the number of connected components of
G-(N(u)\cap N(v))
is at most
(3k-7)^{2}
, again by the bound on the maximum degree. Thus, there is at least one connected component
C
of size at least
2k-1
since
|V(G)-(N(u)\cap N(v))|\geqslant(2k-2)(3k-7)^{2}+3k-6-3k+7=(2k-2)(3k-7)^{2}+1
. As a result,
C
cannot contain
u
or
v
. Moreover, for every vertex
w\in V(C)
, the
u
(resp.
v
) and
w
are clear non-neighbors due to
Proposition

4
since
u
(resp.
v
) are isolated in
C+u
(resp.
C+v
). By
Corollary

2
, the edges
uw
and
vw
are also not in
E(G^{\prime})
for every
w\in V(C)
. Since
G
is connected, there exists
w\in V(C)
and
z\in N(u)\cap N(v)
such that
wz\in E(G)
. Now, let us show that
zu
and
zv
are also present in
E(G^{\prime})
.

There exists
S\in\operatorname{\mathcal{S}}_{k}(G)
such that
u,z\in S
and
S-\{u,z\}\subseteq V(C)
. If
zu\notin E(G^{\prime})
, then
G^{\prime}[S]
is disconnected since
uw
is a non-edge in
G^{\prime}
for every
w\in S-\{u,z\}\subseteq V(C)
. This contradicts the fact that
G^{\prime}
is consistent with
\operatorname{\mathcal{S}}_{k}(G)
and
\operatorname{\overline{\mathcal{S}}}_{k}(G)
. Therefore,
zu\in E(G^{\prime})
and similarly, the same holds for
zv
. Finally, since
z
is a common neighbor of
u
and
v
in
G^{\prime}
and
G^{\prime}
is triangle-free,
uv
is a non-edge in
G^{\prime}
.

By symmetry, if
uv\notin E(G^{\prime})
, then
uv\notin E(G)
as we can treat
G
as a graph consistent with
\operatorname{\mathcal{S}}_{k}(G^{\prime})
and
\operatorname{\overline{\mathcal{S}}}_{k}(G^{\prime})
. Therefore
uv\in E(G)
if and only if
uv\in E(G^{\prime})
, which completes the proof.
∎

Suppose that both

are adjacent to

G
. Recall that we also have that
u_{j}v\in E(G)
, for every
j\in[k-3]
. Therefore, the graph induced by the set
\{x_{i},y_{i},v,u_{1},\ldots,u_{k-3}\}
is connected, which contradicts the fact that we added this set to
\overline{\mathcal{S}}_{k}
in 4.

Now we are ready to define a truth assignment
\varphi
of the variables of
\phi
. For
i\in[n]
, we set
\varphi(x_{i})=1
if
x_{i}v\in E(G)
, and
\varphi(x_{i})=0
if
y_{i}v\in E(G)

, we set

Suppose there is some clause
C
that is not satisfied by
\varphi
, let
\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell_{3}
be its literals. For
j\in[3]
, let

if
\ell_{i}=x_{i}
and
z_{j}=y_{i}
if

\{z_{1},z_{2},z_{3},v,u_{1},\ldots,u_{k-4}\}
to
\mathcal{S}_{k}

must be connected. As we already observed, there are no edges between
z_{1},z_{2},z_{3}
and
u_{1},\ldots,u_{k-3}
. Thus at least one of
z_{j}
must be adjacent to
v
. By the definition of
\varphi
the literal
\ell_{j}
is true and satisfies
C
, a contradiction.

It remains to show that if there is a satisfying assignment
\varphi
of
\phi

G

V

G

H

\varphi(x_{i})=1

x_{i}v\notin E(G),y_{i}v\in E(G)

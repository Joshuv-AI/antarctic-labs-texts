A Remark on Mathieu’s Series
M. Affouf

Abstract
We establish a new lower bound for Mathieu’s series and present a new derivation of its expansions in terms of Riemann Zeta functions. Key Words: Mathieu’s series, Riemann Zeta functions AMS(MOS) subject classifications. Primary 26D15, Secondary 41A60.



In this work, we study Mathieu’s series

\displaystyle F(h)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{n}{(n^{2}+h)^{2}}\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt(h>0)

(1)

which was derived in 1890 by Mathieu in his treatise on theory of elasticity and solid bodies
[
4
]
and Mathieu conjectured the inequality

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{2n}{(n^{2}+h)^{2}}<\dfrac{1}{2h}

(2)

where
h
is any real positive number.

In 1949, Schroder
[
5
]
proved the the Mathieu’s conjecture and another inequality:

\displaystyle F(h)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{n}{(n^{2}+h)^{2}}<\dfrac{1}{2h}\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt(h>0)

(3)

\displaystyle F(h)<\dfrac{1}{(1+h)^{2}}+\dfrac{2}{(4+h)^{2}}+\dfrac{1}{2(4+h)}\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt(0\leq h<2)

(4)

In 1960, Zmorovitch
[
6
]
provided an elegant elementary proof of Schroder inequality based on the integral formula

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-ax}\cos bxdx=\dfrac{a}{a^{2}+b^{2}}(a>0)

(5)

Substitute
a=n
and
b=\sqrt{h}
, the last integral will have the form

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-nx}\cos\sqrt{h}xdx=\dfrac{n}{n^{2}+h}

(6)

Since the integral (
6
) is absolutely convergent, we can take the derivative of both sides with respect to the parameter
h
, to obtain

\int_{0}^{\infty}xe^{-nx}\sin\sqrt{h}xdx=2\sqrt{h}\dfrac{n}{(n^{2}+h)^{2}}

(7)

By taking the sum of both sides with respect to
n
and using the geometric series formula, as follows:

\int_{0}^{\infty}x\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{-nx}\sin\sqrt{h}xdx=2\sqrt{h}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{n}{(n^{2}+h)^{2}}

(8)

\int_{0}^{\infty}\dfrac{x}{e^{x}-1}\sin\sqrt{h}xdx=2\sqrt{h}F(h)

(9)

We perform integration by parts three times to the integral on the left side of formula (
9
), and we use the following expressions:

\displaystyle f(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\dfrac{x}{e^{x}-1})^{\prime}=\dfrac{1}{e^{x}-1}-\dfrac{xe^{x}}{(e^{x}-1)^{2}}

(10)

\displaystyle f^{\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{2xe^{x}}{(e^{x}-1)^{3}}-\dfrac{xe^{x}+2e^{x}}{(e^{x}-1)^{2}}

\displaystyle f^{\prime\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{-6xe^{3x}}{(e^{x}-1)^{4}}-\dfrac{6xe^{2x}+6e^{2x}}{(e^{x}-1)^{3}}-\dfrac{xe^{x}+3e^{x}}{(e^{x}-1)^{2}}

We obtain the formula

F(h)=\dfrac{1}{2h}+\dfrac{1}{2h^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}f^{\prime\prime}(x)(1-\cos\sqrt{h}x)dx

(11)

Note that the function
f^{\prime\prime}(x)
in the integrand of (9) can be written in the form

f^{\prime\prime}(x)=\dfrac{(3-x)e^{2x}-4xe^{x}-x-3}{(e^{x}-1)^{4}}

(12)

We can expand the numerator into the Taylor series such that

(3-x)e^{2x}-4xe^{x}-x-3=-\dfrac{x^{5}}{10}[1+x+\dfrac{23}{126}x^{2}+\dfrac{1}{14}x^{3}+\cdots]

(13)

This implies that
f^{\prime\prime}(x)<0
and the Shroder inequality is directly obtained

F(h)<\dfrac{1}{2h}

(14)

Furthermore, By using L’hopital’s rule, we can find the limit

\lim_{x\to 0}f^{\prime}(x)=\dfrac{1}{6}

which implies that

F(h)>\dfrac{1}{2h}-\dfrac{1}{6h^{2}}

(15)

That is

\dfrac{1}{2h}-\dfrac{1}{6h^{2}}<F(h)<\dfrac{1}{2h}

(16)

for all
h>0
.

Using the above discussed integral transformations, we establish the following proposition on expanding Mathieu’s series in terms of Riemann Zeta functions.

Lemma:
The Mathieu’s series can be expanded in terms of Riemann zeta functions

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{n}{(n^{2}+h)^{2}}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{(-1)^{n+1}}{(2n-1)!}\zeta(2n)h^{n-1}

(17)

for small parameter
h>0
.

Proof:

We expand the sine function in formula (7) in Taylor series for small parameter
h
, as follows:

\displaystyle 2\sqrt{h}F(h)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{h}\int_{0}^{\infty}\dfrac{x}{e^{x}-1}[x-\dfrac{hx^{3}}{3!}+\dfrac{h^{2}x^{5}}{5!}-\dfrac{h^{3}x^{7}}{7!}-\dfrac{h^{4}x^{9}}{9!}+\cdots]dx

(18)

\displaystyle 2F(h)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\dfrac{x^{2}}{e^{x}-1}dx-\dfrac{h}{3!}\int_{0}^{\infty}\dfrac{x^{4}}{e^{x}-1}dx+\dfrac{h^{2}}{5!}\int_{0}^{\infty}\dfrac{x^{6}}{e^{x}-1}dx

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\dfrac{h^{3}}{7!}\int_{0}^{\infty}\dfrac{x^{8}}{e^{x}-1}dx+\cdots

These integrals are the Riemann zeta function so the new expression is

2F(h)=\zeta(2)-\dfrac{h}{3!}\zeta(4)+\dfrac{h^{2}}{5!}\zeta(6)-\dfrac{h^{3}}{7!}\varsigma(8)+\cdots

(19)

From the last expression, we derive the new expansion of the Mathieu series in terms of Riemann zeta functions

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{n}{(n^{2}+h)^{2}}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{(-1)^{n+1}}{(2n-1)!}\zeta(2n)h^{n-1}

(20)

or in an equivalent form

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{n}{(n^{2}+h)^{2}}=\dfrac{\pi^{2}}{6}-\dfrac{\pi^{4}}{90\cdot 3!}h+\dfrac{\pi^{6}}{945\cdot 5!}h^{2}-\cdots

(21)

Clearly, for small
h
the last formula implies that

F(h)>\dfrac{\pi^{2}}{6}

which is better than available estimates. The proof is complete
\blacksquare
.

Many questions remain to explain about the Mathieu series, here two of them:

1.

Investigate the generalized Mathieu series

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{n}{(n^{2}+h)^{\mu}}

(22)

for
\mu>1
.

2.

Investigate the alternating Mathieu series

S(h)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n-1}\dfrac{n}{(n^{2}+h)^{2}}

(23)

Numerical investigation of the alternating Mathieu series, point to the fact
S(h)
is a decreasing function with a range
[0,0.9015]
. However, this needs to be established analytically.

Linearity and Nonlinearity of groups of polynomial automorphisms of K 2 K^{2}
Olivier Mathieu

Abstract
Let K K be a field, and let Aut ​ K 2 \mathrm{Aut}\,K^{2} be the group of polynomial automorphisms of K 2 K^{2} . We investigate which subgroups are linear or not. In characteristic zero, there are small nonlinear subgroups and some big linear subgroups. When K K has finite characteristic, the whole group Aut ​ K 2 \mathrm{Aut}\,K^{2} is linear whenever K K is finite, and nonlinear otherwise. 1 1 1 Research supported by UMR 5208 du CNRS

1.4 The group
\mathrm{Aut}\,K^{2}

Let
K
be a field.
Recall that
\mathrm{Aut}\,K^{2}
is the group of polynomial automorphisms of

K^{2}
, so an element of
\mathrm{Aut}\,K^{2}
is a polynomial map

\phi:(x,y)\mapstochar\rightarrow(f(x,y),g(x,y))

which admits a polynomial inverse. When
\operatorname{ch}\,K=0

\phi:K^{2}\rightarrow K^{2}
is bijective. When
\operatorname{ch}\,K\neq 0
, this is not enough. Even when

is infinite, the existence
of a polynomial inverse requires some additional

1.5 The subgroup
\mathrm{Elem}(K^{2})
of elementary automorphism of
K^{2}

By definition, an

of

is an automorphism

of the form

\phi:(x,y)\mapstochar\rightarrow(z_{1}x+t,z_{2}y+f(x))

. Also set

For any

\mu(F):(x,y)\mapstochar\rightarrow(x,y+x^{2}F(x))

Clearly

Let

Recall the classical

We have

Amalgamated Products and
Linearity.

In this section, it is shown that, under a mild assumption, an amalgamated product
G_{1}*_{A}G_{2}

which is linear over a ring is also linear over a field,
see Lemma
6
.

2.1 Linearity Properties

Let
R
be a ring. For any
n\geq n
, let

GL(n,R)
be the group of invertible
n\times n
matrices
with entries in
R
. Indeed for a field
K
,
the notation
GL(K^{2})
denotes the subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut}\,K^{2}
of linear automorphisms of
K^{2}
although

GL(2,K)
is the abstract linear group of degree
2
.

A group
\Gamma
is called

linear over
R
if there is a group embbeding

\Gamma\subset GL(n,R)
for some integer
n
. The group

\Gamma
is called
linear
(or
linear over a ring
) if it is linear over some ring
R
. Similarly
\Gamma
is called
linear over a field
if it is linear over some field
K
.

For a group
\Gamma
, the strongest form of linearity is the linearity over a field. On the opposite, there are also groups

\Gamma
which contain a FG subgroup which is
not linear, even over a ring: these groups are nonlinear in the strongest sense.

2.2 Minimal embeddings

Let
R
be a commutative ring, let
n\geq 1
be an integer and let

\Gamma
be a subgroup of
GL(n,R)
. For any ideal
J
of
R
, let

GL(n,J)
be the kernel of the natural map

GL(n,R)\rightarrow GL(n,R/J)
. Any element
g\in GL(n,J)
can be written as
g=\mathrm{id}+A
, where
A
is a
n
-by-
n
matrix with entries in

J
.

The embbeding
\Gamma\subset GL(n,R)
is called
minimal
if
for any ideal
J\neq 0
we have
\Gamma\cap GL(n,J)\neq\{1\}
.

Lemma 3
.

Let
\Gamma\subset GL(n,R)
. There exist an ideal
J
with

\Gamma\cap GL(n,J)=\{1\}
such that the induced embbeding

\Gamma\rightarrow{\mathrm{GL}}(n,R/J)
is minimal.

Proof.

Since
R
is not necessarily noetherian,
the proof requires Zorn Lemma.

Let
\cal S
be the set of all ideals
J

of
R
such that
\Gamma\cap GL(n,J)=\{1\}
. The set

\cal S
is partially ordered with respect to the inclusion. For any chain
\cal C\subset\cal S
, the ideal

\cup_{I\in{\cal C}}\,I
belongs to
\cal S
. Therefore Zorn Lemma implies that
\cal S
contains a maximal element
J
. It follows that the induced embbeding
\Gamma\rightarrow{\mathrm{GL}}(n,R/J)
is minimal.
∎

2.3 Groups with trivial commutants

By definition, a group
\Gamma
has
trivial commutants if the commutant of any nontrivial normal subgroup
K
of
\Gamma
is trivial.
Equivalently, if
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
is are commuting invariant
subgroups of
\Gamma
, then one of them is trivial.

Lemma 4
.

Let
\Gamma
be a group with trivial commutants.

If
\Gamma
is linear over a ring,
then
\Gamma
is also linear over a field.

Proof.

By hypothesis we have

\Gamma\subset GL(n,R)
for some commutative ring
R
.
By lemma
3
, it can be assumed that the embbeding

\Gamma\rightarrow GL(n,R)
is minimal.

Let
I_{1},\,I_{2}
be ideals
of
R
with
I_{1}.I_{2}=0
. Let
A_{1}
(respectively
A_{2}
) be
an arbitrary
n
-by-
n
matrix with entries in
I_{1}

(respectively in
I_{2}
) and set
g_{1}=1+A_{1}
,

g_{2}=1+A_{2}
. Since we have
A_{1}.A_{2}=A_{2}.A_{1}=0
,
we have
g_{1}.g_{2}=g_{2}.g_{1}
, therefore

GL(n,I_{1})
and
GL(n,I_{2})
are commuting invariant subgroups
of
GL(n,R)
.

Since
K_{1}=\Gamma\cap GL(n,I_{1})
and
K_{2}=\Gamma\cap GL(n,I_{2})

are commuting invariant subgroups of
\Gamma
, one of them is trivial. By minimality hypothesis,
I_{1}
or
I_{2}
is trivial.
Thus
R
is prime.

It follows that
\Gamma\subset GL(n,K)
, where

K
is the fraction field of
R
.
∎

2.4 The hypothesis

Let
G_{1}
,
G_{2}
be two groups sharing a common subgroup

and
set
\Gamma=G_{1}*_{A}G_{2}

Let
S
be the set of all finite sequences

{\bf i}=i_{1},\dots,i_{n}
of alternating

and
2

For

, let

be

\epsilon=(\epsilon_{1},\dots,\epsilon_{n})\in S

and ending with

Let

be the set of all
\gamma\in\Gamma

Therefore we have

By definition, the amalgamated product

is called

if

, or

and

are isomorphic to

Let consider the following hypothesis

For any

Lemma 5
.

Let
\Gamma=G_{1}*_{A}\,G_{2}
be a

amalgamated product satisfying the hypothesis

. Let

with

.

There are

and

.

In particular

Proof.

First it should be noted that

cannot be

and in
G_{2}
.
Otherwise,

would be an invariant subgroup of
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
,

would imply that

Now we prove the first claim, namely that any nontrivial

\Gamma_{1,1}
and
\Gamma_{2,2}

It is clear that

. Similarly, we have

\Gamma_{i,j}.G^{*}_{k}\subset\Gamma_{i,k}

whenever
j\neq k
.

By hypothesis,

is not empty. Let

\gamma_{1}\in G^{*}_{1}
. We have

\Gamma_{2,2}^{\gamma_{1}}\subset\Gamma_{1,1}
. Simlarly we have

\Gamma_{1,1}^{\gamma_{2}}\subset\Gamma_{2,2}
for some

\gamma_{2}\in G_{2}^{*}
. Therefore the claim is proved for

.

It is now enought to prove that for any

g\in A\sqcup\Gamma_{1,2}\sqcup\Gamma_{2,1}
with

,
there is
\gamma\in\Gamma
such that

belongs to

\Gamma_{1,1}\cup\Gamma_{2,2}
.

Assume now

. We have

for some

and

3.1 Quasi-unipotent endomorphisms

Lemma 10

6.1 The Ping-Pong lemma

Let
(E_{p})_{p\in P}
be a collection of groups indexed by
a set
P
. Let
\Gamma:=*_{p\in P}\,E_{p}
be the free product of
these groups. Let
S
be the set of all finite sequences

(p_{1},\dots,p_{n})
such that
p_{i}\neq p_{i+1}
for any

i<n
. For each
p\in P
, set

E^{*}_{p}=E\setminus\{1\}
.

Let
\gamma\in\Gamma
. There is a unique

{\bf p}=(p_{1},\dots,p_{n})\in P
and a unique decomposition
of
\gamma

\gamma=\gamma_{1}\dots\gamma_{n}
,

where
\gamma_{i}\in E^{*}_{p_{i}}
. The sequence
{\bf p}
is
called the
type
of
\gamma
.

The free product
\Gamma:=*_{p\in P}\,E_{p}
is called

if

(i) for any
p\in P
,
E_{p}\neq\,\{1\}
,

(ii)
\mathrm{Card\,}\,P\geq 2
, and

(iii) if
\mathrm{Card\,}\,P=2
,
\Gamma
is not the trivial free product

\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}*\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
.

Lemma 18
.

Assume that a nontrivial
free product
\Gamma=*_{p\in P}\,E
acts on some set
\Omega
. Let
(\Omega_{p})_{p\in P}
be a collection of subsets
in
\Omega
. Assume

(i) The subsets
\Omega_{p}
are nonempty and disjoint, and

(ii) we have
E^{*}_{p}.\Omega_{q}\subset\Omega_{p}
whenever
p\neq q
.

Then the action of
\Gamma
on
\Omega
is faithful.

The hypothesis (ii) is called a Ping-Pong hypothesis.

Proof.

Let
\gamma\in\Gamma
with
\gamma\neq 1
,
and let
{\bf p}=(p_{1},\dots,p_{n})
be its type.
We claim that there is
x\in\Omega
such that
\gamma.x\neq x

If
\mathrm{Card\,}\,P\geq 3
let
q\in P
with

q\neq p_{1}
and
q\neq p_{n}
. Let
x\in\Omega_{q}
.
By the Ping-Pong hypothesis,
\gamma.x
belongs to

\Omega_{p_{1}}
, therefore
\gamma.x\neq x
.

If
\mathrm{Card\,}\,P=2
it can be assumed that
P=\{1,2\}
. It follows from Lemma
5
that
\gamma
has a conjugate
\gamma^{\prime}
of
type
{\bf q}=(q_{1},\dots,q_{m})
with
q_{1}=q_{m}=1
. Let

. By the Ping-Pong hypothesis,
\gamma^{\prime}.x^{\prime}
belongs to

\Omega_{1}
, therefore
\gamma^{\prime}.x\neq x^{\prime}
. It follows that there is some
x\in\Omega
with
\gamma.x\neq x
.

In both cases, any
\gamma\neq 1
acts nontrivially.
Hence the action of
\Gamma
on
\Omega
is faithful.
∎

This section is devoted to three technical lemmas,

Let

\mathrm{Aut}_{0}\,K^{2}=G_{1}*_{A}\,G_{2}=\Gamma_{0}

where
G_{1}=GL(K^{2})
,
G_{2}=\mathrm{Elem}_{0}(K^{2})
and

A=B_{0}
is the Borel subgroup of
GL(K^{2})
. We have

G_{2}=A\ltimes E
where
E=\mathrm{Elem}_{1}(K^{2})
. Clearly the
map
\chi:G_{1}*_{A}\,G_{2}\rightarrow G_{1}
is simply the map

\phi\in\mathrm{Aut}_{0}(K^{2})\rightarrow\textnormal{d}\phi_{\bf 0}
,

and its kernel is
\Gamma_{1}=\mathrm{Aut}_{1}(K^{2})
.
Since
G_{1}/A=GL(2)/B_{0}=\mathbb{P}^{1}_{K}
, and

E^{\gamma}=F_{\delta}
for any
\gamma\in GL(K^{2})
with

\gamma.\delta_{0}=\delta
,
it follows from

that

\mathrm{Aut}_{1}(K^{2})=*_{\gamma\in\mathbb{P}^{1}_{K}}\,F_{\delta}
.

∎

7.2 Proof of Theorem B

For any
\delta\in\mathbb{P}^{1}_{K}
, let

\psi_{\delta}:F_{\delta}\rightarrow E_{\delta}
be the isomorphism
defined by

\psi_{\delta}(\tau_{\delta}(f))=\mathrm{id}+f/t\otimes e_{\delta}
.

Theorem B
.

The collection of isomorphisms

(\psi_{\delta})_{\delta_{\mathbb{P}^{1}_{K}}}
induces an isomorphism

\psi:\mathrm{Aut}_{1}\,K^{2}\simeq GL_{1}(2,K[t])
.

In particular,
\mathrm{Aut}_{1}\,K^{2}
is linear over
K(t)

Proof.

The first statement is a consequence of
Lemmas
23
and
24
. It follows that

\mathrm{Aut}_{1}\,K^{2}\subset SL(2,K(t))
, therefore

\mathrm{Aut}_{1}\,K^{2}
is linear over
K(t)
.
∎

Remark
The isomorphism

\mathrm{Aut}_{1}\,K^{2}\simeq GL_{1}(2,K[t])
is not canonical, since
it depends upon a choice, for each
\delta\in\mathbb{P}^{1}_{P}
, of a basis
of
\delta
.

Moreover the formula defining
\psi
is complicated.
Indeed if an automorphism
\sigma\in\mathrm{Aut}_{1}(K^{2})
is written
as a free product

, where
\sigma_{i}\in F_{\delta_{i}}
has degree

, then
\sigma
has degree

m_{1}\dots m_{n}
although
\psi(\sigma)
has degree

m_{1}+\dots+m_{m}-m
.

\mathrm{Aut}_{S}\,K^{2}
for some
S\subset GL(2,K)

In this section, Theorem C1 and C2 are proved.

For a subgroup
S
of
GL(2,K)
,
the following hypotheses
\cal U
and
\cal{QU}
will be considered

(\cal U)
any
K
-reducible element
g\in S
is unipotent,

(\cal QU)
any
K
-reducible element
g\in S
is quasi-unipotent.

8.1 Proof of Theorem C1

Theorem C.1
.

Assume that the subgroup
S
satisfies the hypothesis
\cal U
. Then we have

\mathrm{Aut}_{S}\,K^{2}\simeq GL_{S}(2,K[t])
.

In particular,
\mathrm{Aut}_{S}\,K^{2}
is linear over
K(t)
.

Proof.

It follows from Lemmas
23
and
24

that

GL_{1}(2,K[t])=*_{\delta\in\mathbb{P}^{1}_{K}}\,E_{\delta}
and

\mathrm{Aut}_{1}\,K^{2}=*_{\delta\in\mathbb{P}^{1}_{K}}\,F_{\delta}
,

where the groups
E_{\delta}
and
F_{\delta}
are obviously isomorphic. For
\delta\in\mathbb{P}^{1}_{K}
, let
S_{\delta}
be
the stabilizer in
S
of
\delta
. Any element in
S_{\delta}
is

K
-reducible, so by hypothesis
(\cal U)
all elements in

S_{\delta}
are unipotent. It follows that
S_{\delta}
acts trivially on
E_{\delta}
and
F_{\delta}
, therefore the
S_{\delta}

groups
E_{\delta}
and
F_{\delta}
are
S_{\delta}
-isomorphic.
It follows from Lemma
21
that the
S
groups

*_{\delta\in\mathbb{P}^{1}_{K}}\,E_{\delta}
and

*_{\delta\in\mathbb{P}^{1}_{K}}\,F_{\delta}
are
S
-isomorphic. Therefore

GL_{S}(2,K[t])=S\ltimes(*_{\delta\in\mathbb{P}^{1}_{K}}\,E_{\delta})
and

\mathrm{Aut}_{1}\,K^{2}=S\ltimes(*_{\delta\in\mathbb{P}^{1}_{K}}\,F_{\delta})
are isomorphic.
∎

8.2 FG subgroups of
GL(2,K)

Lemma 25
.

Let
S
be a FG subgroup of
GL(2,K)
. There is an integer

N>0
such that any quasi-unipotent
g\in S
has quasi-order divisible by
N
.

Proof.

Let
F
be the prime field of
K
, namely

F=\mathbb{F}_{p}
if
K
has characteristic
p
and
F=\mathbb{Q}
otherwise.
There is a FG ring
R\subset K
such that

S
is a subgroup of
GL(2,R)
. It can be assumed that
K

is the fraction field of
R
.

Let
X
be the set of all roots of
unity which are eigenvalues of some element
g\in S
. We claim that the set
X
is finite.

Set
E=\overline{F}\cap K
. Since
K
is finitely generated,

E
is a finite extension of
F
. Any
\zeta\in X
lies in
E
or in a quadratic
extension of
E
. We will now consider separately the case where
K
has zero characteristic or finite characteristic.

First assume that
\operatorname{ch}\,K=0
. Let
m
be the order of
\zeta
. Since
[\mathbb{Q}(z):\mathbb{Q}]=\phi(m)
, we have
\phi(m)\leq 2[E:\mathbb{Q}]
. Thus

m
is bounded, and therefore
X
is finite.

Next asssume that
\operatorname{ch}\,K=p
. Then
E
is a finite field and

\zeta
lies in the unique quadratic extension
E^{\prime}
of
E
.
Therefore
X\subset E^{\prime}
is finite.

Since
X
is finite, we have
X\subset\mu_{N}
for some
N
,
where
\mu_{N}
is the set of
N^{th}
-root of unity. Therefore
any quasi-unipotent element
g\in S
has eigenvalues in
\mu_{N}
, and therefore
g^{N}
is unipotent.

∎

Lemma 26
.

Let
S
be a FG subgroup of
GL(2,K)
satisfying
the hypothesis
\cal{QU}
. Then
S
contains a finite
index subgroup
S^{\prime}
satisfying the hypothesis

{\cal U}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
25
, there exists
an integer
N\geq 1
such that any
K
-reducible element in
S

is quasi-idempotent of quasi-order divisible by
N
.

Let
C
be the set of pairs
(s,p)
in
K^{2}
such that

s=\zeta_{1}+\zeta_{2}
and
p=\zeta_{1}\zeta_{2}
for some

\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}\in\mu_{N}
.

There is a finitely generated subring
R
of
K
such that

S\subset GL(2,R)
. Since the intersection of all cofinite ideal
m
in
R
is zero, there is a cofinite ideal
m\subset R

such that
(s,p)\not\equiv(2,1)\,\mathrm{mod}\,m
for any
(s,p)\in C\cap R^{2}

with
(s,p)\neq(2,1)
.

Set
S^{\prime}=S\cap GL(2,m)
. Since
GL(2,m)
has finite index in
GL(2,R)
,
S^{\prime}
has finite index in
S
. We claim that

S^{\prime}
satisfies the hypothesis
{\cal U}
.

Let
g\in S
’ be

K
-reducible. Since
g
is quasi-idempotent of quasi-order
divible by
N
, the couple
(\operatorname{tr}\,g,\det\,g)
belongs to

C
. Since
g\equiv 1\,\mathrm{mod}\,m
, we have

(\operatorname{tr}\,g,\det\,g)\equiv(2,1)\,\mathrm{mod}\,m
. By the construction of
m
,
we have
(\operatorname{tr}\,g,\det\,g)=(2,1)
, and therefore
g
is
unipotent. Hence
S^{\prime}
satisfies the hypothesis
({\cal U})
.

∎

8.3 Proof of Theorem C.2

Theorem C.2
.

Let
S
be a FG subgroup of

satisfying hypothesis
({\cal QU})
. Then

\mathrm{Aut}_{S}\,K^{2}

By Lemma

, there is a subgroup

∎

9.3 Linearity over
K
of
\mathrm{Aut}\,K^{2}
for
a big enough field
K

Lemma 27
.

Let
K
,
L
be fields. If

\mathrm{Card\,}\,K=\mathrm{Card\,}\,L
and
\operatorname{ch}\,K=\operatorname{ch}\,L
,
then
\mathrm{Aut}_{1}\,K^{2}
and
\mathrm{Aut}_{1}\,L^{2}

are isomorphic.

Proof.

It can be assumed that
K
is infinite.
Let
F
be its prime field, let
P
be a set and let

E
be a
F
-vector space with

\mathrm{Card\,}P=\mathrm{Card\,}K
and
\dim_{F}\,E=\mathrm{Card\,}\,K
.

By lemma
24
, we have

\mathrm{Aut}_{1}\,K^{2}\simeq*_{p\in P}\,E_{p}
,

where each
E_{p}
is a copy of
E
. It follows that

\mathrm{Aut}_{1}\,K^{2}
and
\mathrm{Aut}_{1}\,L^{2}

are isomorphic if
\mathrm{Card\,}\,K=\mathrm{Card\,}\,L
and
\operatorname{ch}\,K=\operatorname{ch}\,L
.
∎

A field
K
is called
big enough
if its
absolute transcendence degree is

\geq 1
and if
K
is not a finite extension of

\mathbb{F}_{p}(t)
for some prime number
p
. For example,

\mathbb{Q}(t),\,\mathbb{F}_{p}(t_{1},t_{2})
and
L(t)
,
where
L
is an infinite subfield of
\overline{F}_{p}
, are big enough.

Corollary D
.

Let
K
be a big enough field. There is
an embedding

\mathrm{Aut}_{1}\,K^{2}\subset SL(2,K)
.

Proof.

Since
K
is big enough, there is an
embedding
L(t)\subset K
, where
L
is a field
with
\mathrm{Card\,}\,L=\mathrm{Card\,}\,K
.

It follows from Lemma
27
that

\mathrm{Aut}_{1}\,K^{2}
is isomorphic to

\mathrm{Aut}_{1}\,L^{2}
. Moreover by Theorem B,

\mathrm{Aut}_{1}\,L^{2}
is isomorphic to

GL_{1}(L[t])
. Therefore we have

\mathrm{Aut}_{1}\,K^{2}\simeq GL_{1}(L[t])\subset SL_{2}(L(t))\subset SL(2,K)
.

∎

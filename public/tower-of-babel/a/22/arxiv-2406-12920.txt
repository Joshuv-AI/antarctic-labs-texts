Cross-Dimensional Mathematics: A Foundation for Semi-Tensor Product/Semi-Tensor Addition
Daizhan Cheng

Abstract
A new mathematical structure, called the cross-dimensional mathematics (CDM), is proposed. The CDM considered in this paper consists of three parts: hyper algebra, hyper geometry, and hyper Lie group/Lie algebra. Hyper algebra proposes some new algebraic structures such as hyper group, hyper ring, and hyper module over matrices and vectors with mixed dimensions (MVMDs). They have sets of classical groups, rings, and modules as their components and cross-dimensional connections among their components. Their basic properties are investigated. Hyper geometry starts from mixed dimensional Euclidian space, and hyper vector space. Then the hyper topological vector space, hyper inner product space, and hyper manifold are constructed. They have a joined cross-dimensional geometric structure. Finally, hyper metric space, topological hyper group and hyper Lie algebra are built gradually, and finally, the corresponding hyper Lie group is introduced. All these concepts are built over MVMDs, and a couple of most general STP and STA are introduced. Some existing structures/results about STPs/STAs have also been resumed and integrated into this CDM.

Roughly speaking, the STPs and STAs are cross-dimensional operators, which means they are operators acting on objects with different dimensions, i.e., MVMDs,
which can formally be described as follows:

(i)

Vectors with mixed dimensions:
{\mathbb{R}}^{\infty}:=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

(ii)

Matrices with mixed dimensions:

In the long history of development of matrix theory, the algebraic structure of matrices, including group, ring, module, general linear algebra, etc., and their geometric structure, including vector space, inner product, metric, general linear group, etc., played fundamental roles in both theoretical investigations and practical applications. Since initiated two decades ago, the research on STP/STA has been mainly concentrated on its applications. When investigating the set of MVMDs, which are posed by cross-dimensional operators: STPs and STAs, the existing mathematical tools seem not very efficient.
Hence, it is now the time to explore the mathematical foundation of STP/STA.

When we tried to pose the existing algebraic and geometric structures on STPs/STAs with MVMDs, we found the existing mathematical framework is not suitable for them. The most critical contradiction lies on the fact that the existing algebraic or geometric structure, such as group or manifold, is for a set of objects with fixed dimensions or fixed sizes, while the STPs/STAs are cross-dimensional operators for MVMDs.

Observing carefully these objects with mixed dimensions, we found that the main difficult point for posing an algebraic or geometric structure on them lies on the trouble of “multiply unitary". For example, if we consider the group structure, then in
{\mathbb{R}}^{\infty}
there are infinite zeros, precisely speaking, there are
0_{n}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
n=1,2,\cdots
. Similarly,
{\mathcal{M}}
also have infinite
0_{m\times n}
,
m,n=1,2,\cdots

{\mathcal{M}}_{1}
, then it has also infinite identities,
I_{n}
,
n=1,2,\cdots
, which makes the group structure of matrices (with respect to product) also very difficult to define.

When we consider the geometric structure, say, vector space structure, we are also facing this “multiple zero" puzzle.

An inspiration for solving this problem comes from mechanics, where a pseudo-metric space is used.

It seems that the concept of “pseudo-metric space" can provide a way to address the distance of points with different dimensions. This stimulates us to define a new “group" which contains multiple identities. Instead of “pseudo-group" such a multi-identity group is called a “hyper group" later in this paper. The reason is that a hyper group contains a set of many (might be countable) groups as its components.

Motivated by this fact, we may modify the existing algebraic and geometric structures to suit the MVMDs with respect to (briefly, with respect to) STPs/STAs. The purpose of this paper is to propose a new mathematical framework, called the CDM, for objects with mixed dimensions. We hope that it can provide a solid mathematical foundation for STPs/STAs. Meanwhile, it may result in the development of a new cross-dimensional algebra and geometry.

The CDM proposed in this paper consists of three parts: (1) hyper algebra; (2) hyper geometry; (3) hyper Lie algebra and hyper Lie group. The hyper algebra contains hyper group, hyper ring, and hyper module; the hyper geometry includes hyper vector space, hyper inner product space, hyper manifold; and the hyper Lie algebra and hyper Lie group start from mixed dimensional Euclidian space, through constructing topological hyper group, and finally lead to hyper linear group and hyper linear algebra.

2.1
Algebraic Structure on Matrices/Vectors with proper dimensions

We first review some algebraic structures over matrices/vectors.

Definition 2.1

Consider a couple
{\mathcal{G}}=(G,*)
, where
G
is a set, and
*:G\times G\rightarrow G

(i)

{\mathcal{G}}
is a semi-group, if
(a*b)*c=a*(b*c),\quad a,b,c\in G

(ii)

A semi-group
{\mathcal{G}}
is a monoid (i.e., a semi-group with an identity), if
there exists an identity

a*e=e*a=a,\quad\forall a\in G
.

(iii)

A monoid
{\mathcal{G}}
is a group, if
for each
a\in G

a^{-1}
, such that
a*a^{-1}=a^{-1}*a=e,\quad a\in{\mathcal{G}}

In addition, if
a*b=b*a,\quad\forall a,b\in G
, then it is called an abelian group.

[

2.2
STP and STA of Matrices/Vectors

The main objects of linear algebra are vectors and matrices. In linear algebra both the addition and the product of two matrices, or matrix with vector, or two vectors, require the factors to meet certain dimension matching condition, here the product of two vectors means the inner product.

STP/STA are proposed to deal with MVMDs. The sets of vectors with mixed dimensions and matrices with mixed dimensions are described by
{\mathbb{R}}^{\infty}
and
{\mathcal{M}}
respectively. In addition we also need
{\mathcal{M}}_{\mu}=\{A\in{\mathcal{M}}_{m\times n}\;|\;m/n=\mu\},\quad\mu\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{+}
.

Remark 2.10

Consider
{\mathcal{M}}_{\mu}

\mu=\frac{\mu_{y}}{\mu_{x}}\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{+}

\gcd(\mu_{y},\mu_{x})=1

(i)

{\mathcal{M}}_{1}
is the set of square matrices.

(ii)

{\mathcal{M}}=\bigcup_{\mu\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{+}}{\mathcal{M}}_{\mu}

is a partition.

The STP and STA have been proposed to extend the matrix (vector) product and addition to overall (or larger then the original) sets.

\displaystyle a\prec_{L}b\Rightarrow\pi(a)\prec_{H}\pi(b),\quad a,b\in L.

\begin{array}[]{ccc}m\vee\alpha=a,&n\vee\beta=b,&p\vee\gamma=c,\\
q\vee\delta=d,&m\wedge p=s,&n\wedge q=t,\\
\alpha\wedge\gamma=\xi,&\beta\wedge\delta=\eta,&\\
s\vee\xi=u,&t\vee\eta=v.&\\
\end{array}

Using Lemma
3.16

and

such that

\begin{array}[]{l}A_{1}=A_{0}\otimes{\mathcal{E}}_{m/s\times n/t},\quad A_{2}=A_{0}\otimes{\mathcal{E}}_{p/s\times q/t};\\
B_{1}=B_{0}\otimes{\mathcal{E}}_{\alpha/\xi\times\beta/\eta},\quad B_{2}=B_{0}\otimes{\mathcal{E}}_{\gamma/\xi\times\delta/\eta}.\\
\end{array}

Now

\begin{array}[]{l}A_{1}\hat{+}B_{1}=A_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{E}}_{a/m\times b/n}+B_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{E}}_{a/\alpha\times b/\beta}\\
~=A_{0}\otimes{\mathcal{E}}_{a/s\times b/t}+B_{0}\otimes{\mathcal{E}}_{a/\xi\times b/\eta}\\
~=(A_{0}\otimes{\mathcal{E}}_{u/s\times v/t}+B_{0}\otimes{\mathcal{E}}_{u/\xi\times v/\eta})\otimes{\mathcal{E}}_{a/u\times b/v}.\end{array}

\begin{array}[]{l}A_{2}\hat{+}B_{2}=A_{2}\otimes{\mathcal{E}}_{c/p\times d/q}+B_{2}\otimes{\mathcal{E}}_{c/\gamma\times d/\delta}\\
~=A_{0}\otimes{\mathcal{E}}_{c/s\times d/t}+B_{0}\otimes{\mathcal{E}}_{c/\xi\times d/\eta}\\
~=(A_{0}\otimes{\mathcal{E}}_{u/s\times v/t}+B_{0}\otimes{\mathcal{E}}_{u/\xi\times v/\eta})\otimes{\mathcal{E}}_{c/u\times d/v}.\end{array}

It follows immediately that

A_{1}\hat{+}B_{1}\sim A_{2}\hat{+}B_{2}.

The condition (ii) of Definition
3.13
is straightforward verifiable.

\Box

We conclude that for these additive hyper-vector spaces the equivalence vector spaces always exist.

Definition 5.1

Let

,

be two hyper groups.
\pi:G^{1}\rightarrow G^{2}
is called a hyper group homomorphism, and

and
{\mathcal{G}}_{2}
are said to be homomorphic, denoted by

(i)

\displaystyle\pi(x\times^{1}y)=\pi(x)\times^{2}\pi(y),\quad x,y\in G_{1}.

(111)

(ii)

\displaystyle\pi(e^{1})\in{\bf e}^{2},\quad e^{1}\in{\bf e}^{1},

(112)

and
\pi:{\bf e}^{1}\rightarrow{\bf e}^{2}
is a lattice order-preserving mapping.

(iii)

If a hyper group homomorphism
\pi:G_{1}\rightarrow G_{2}
is bijective, and
\pi^{-1}:G_{2}\rightarrow G_{1}
is also a homomorphism, then

\pi
is called a hyper group isomorphism, and
{\mathcal{G}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{G}}_{2}
are said to be isomorphic, denoted by
{\mathcal{G}}_{1}\cong_{h}{\mathcal{G}}_{2}
.

5.2
Quotient Hyper Group Homomorphism/Isomorphism

Assume
\varphi:H\rightarrow G
is a hyper group homomorphism. Set

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{l}Im(\varphi):=\{g\in G\;|\;\exists h\in H~\mbox{such that}~g=\varphi(h)\},\\
{\rm Ker}(\varphi):=\{h\in H\;|\;\varphi(h)\in{\bf e}_{G}\}.\end{array}

Then we have the following result.

Proposition 5.5

(i)

{\rm Im}(\varphi)<_{h}G
.

(ii)

{\rm Ker}(\varphi)\lhd_{h}H
.

Proof.

(i)

Since
\varphi:{\bf e}_{H}\rightarrow{\bf e}_{G}
is a lattice homomorphism, it is easy to see that
\varphi({\bf e}_{H})
is a sub-lattice of
{\bf e}_{G}
. Let
x,y\in{\rm Im}(\varphi)
. Then there exist
a,b\in H
such that
\varphi(a)=x
,
\varphi(b)=y
. Since
H
is a hyper group,
y^{-1}\in H
. Then
\varphi(ab^{-1})=xy^{-1}\in{\rm Im}(\varphi)
. We conclude that
{\rm Im}(\varphi)<_{h}G
.

(ii)

Since
{\bf e}_{H}\subset K:={\rm Ker}(\varphi)
,
{\bf e}_{K}={\bf e}_{H}
. It is obvious that
K<_{h}H
. To see
K
is a normal hyper-sub-group, we need to show that for any
a\in H

aKa^{-1}=K
. This is true because

\varphi(aka^{-1})=\varphi(a)e(\varphi(a))^{-1}=e\varphi(a)(\varphi(a))^{-1}\in{\bf e}_{G}.

\Box

Proposition 5.6

Let
\varphi:H\rightarrow G
be a hyper group homomorphism, and for each
e\in{\bf e}_{H}
,
\varphi_{e}:H_{e}\rightarrow G
be the restriction of
\varphi
on
H_{e}
.
Then
\varphi_{e}:H_{e}\rightarrow G_{\varphi{e}}
is a group homomorphism. Moreover, for each
e\in{\bf e}_{H}

(i)

{\rm Im}(\varphi_{e})<G_{e}
;

(ii)

{\rm Ker}(\varphi_{e})\lhd H
.

Proof.
We claim that
\varphi_{e}(H_{e})\subset G_{\varphi(e)}
. If this claim is true, all the remaining statements are obvious. Let
h\in H_{e}
, then

\varphi(hh^{-1})=\varphi(h)\varphi(h^{-1})=\varphi(e)\in{\bf e}_{G}.

Hence
\varphi(h)\in G_{\varphi(e)}
, which proves the claim.

\Box

The three Isomorphism Theorems for groups can be modified to suit the hyper groups.

8.1
From Hyper Group to Hyper Vector Space

To be precise we first review the concept of vector space in linear algebra.

Definition 8.1

[
27
]
A vector space
{\mathcal{V}}
over
{\mathbb{R}}
is a triple

{\mathcal{V}}=(X,+,\cdot)
, where
X
is a set with elements
x\in X
are vectors; addition
+:X\times X\rightarrow X
, and scalar product
\cdot:{\mathbb{R}}\times X\rightarrow X
, satisfying

(1)

(X,+)
is an abelian group, i.e.,

(i)

Associativity:

\displaystyle(x+y)+z=x+(y+z),\quad x,y,z\in X.

(182)

(ii)

Commutativity:

\displaystyle x+y=y+x,\quad x,y\in X.

(183)

(iii)

Zero: There exists a unique
\vec{0}\in X
, such that

\displaystyle x+\vec{0}=\vec{0}+x=x,\quad\forall x\in X.

(184)

(iv)

Inverse: For each
x\in X
there exists an inverse
-x\in X
, such that

\displaystyle x+(-x)=0,\quad x\in X.

(185)

(2)

Scalar product satisfies

(i)

Associativity:

\displaystyle(r_{1}r_{2})\cdot x=r_{1}\cdot(r_{2}\cdot x),\quad r_{1},r_{2}\in{\mathbb{F}},~x\in X.

(186)

(ii)

Distributivity:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{l}(r_{1}+r_{2})\cdot x=r_{1}\cdot x+r_{2}\cdot x,\quad r,r_{1},r_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}},\\
r\cdot(x+y)=r\cdot x+r\cdot y,\quad~x,~y\in X.\end{array}

(iii)

Unit:

\displaystyle 1\cdot x=x,\quad 1\in{\mathbb{R}},x\in X.

(189)

When the MVMDs are considered, it is natural to consider “pseudo-" vector space, which is defined by modifying the definition of vector space a little bit.

Definition 8.2

A hyper vector space
{\mathcal{V}}
over
{\mathbb{R}}
is a triple

{\mathcal{V}}=(X,+,\cdot)
as in Definition
8.1
, satisfying

(1’)

(X,+)
ia an abelian hyper group.

(2)

Same as the (2) in Definition
8.1
.

Recall that each hyper group has its component groups and as the operator (it is addition now) is consistent with respect to equivalence then the equivalence group exists. Correspondingly, we have the following result.

Proposition 8.3

Consider a hyper vector space
{\mathcal{V}}=(X,+,\cdot)
. Assume
{\bf e}=\{e_{\lambda}\;|\;\lambda\in\Lambda\}
. Then

(i)

For each
\lambda\in\Lambda
,
X_{\lambda}
is a vector space.

(ii)

Assume the addition is consistent with respect to equivalence, then the equivalence group is a vector space.

Proof.

(i)

Let
x\in V_{\lambda}
. Then,

xe_{\lambda}=e_{\lambda}x=x,\quad x^{-1}e_{\lambda}=e_{\lambda}x^{-1}=x^{-1}.

It follows that for any
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}

\begin{array}[]{l}(\alpha x)e_{\lambda}=e_{\lambda}(\alpha x)=\alpha x,\\
(\alpha x)^{-1}e_{\lambda}=e_{\lambda}(\alpha x)^{-1}=(\alpha x)^{-1}.\end{array}

Hence
\alpha x\in X_{\lambda}
, which ensures that
V_{\lambda}
is a vector space.

(ii)

Since

\overline{\alpha x}=\alpha\bar{x},\quad\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}},

(\bar{V},+)
is also a vector space.

\Box

Differentiable Structure on
{\mathbb{R}}^{\infty}

Since each component space

Definition 10.1

(i)

A hyper vector space is said to be differentiable, if all its component subspaces are differentiable.

(ii)

Let
\Theta\subset{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
, with
(\Theta,\prec)
be a sublattice of MD-1. Then

{\mathbb{R}}^{\Theta}:=\bigcup_{\theta\in\Theta}{\mathbb{R}}^{\theta}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{\infty},

{\mathbb{R}}^{\theta}

Remark 10.2

(i)

is obviously differentiable. Then so is
{\mathbb{R}}^{\Theta}
.

(ii)

Unlike the differentiability, the topological structure of an Euclidean hyper manifold is globally defined.

In the following, the major objects of differential geometry will be defined over Euclidean hyper manifold.

Proposition 12.7

12.2

Define

as a formal identity for the algebra

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{l}I_{m\times n}\,\rotatebox[]{-90.0}{$\ltimes$}\,I_{m\times n}=I_{m\times n}\\

Then we have an extended matrix-ring as

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{m\times n}:=\{aI_{m\times n}+A_{0}\;|\;a\in{\mathbb{R}},~A_{0}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{m\times n}\},

(245)

with

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{l}(aI_{m\times n}+A_{0})+(bI_{m\times n}+B):=(a+b)I_{m\times n}+(A_{0}+B_{0}),\\

Note that where
m\neq n
the objects
\{aI_{m\times n}+A_{0}\}
of an extended matrix-ring are not matrices unless
a=0
.

Let
A\in{\mathcal{M}}_{m\times n}
. Then
\Pi_{A}=A\Psi_{n\times m}
, where
\Psi_{n\times m}
defined by (
19
), is called the restricted (square) form of

, because

A\,\rotatebox[]{-90.0}{$\ltimes$}\,x=\Pi_{A}x,\quad\forall x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}.

Define

\displaystyle A^{\langle k\rangle}=\begin{cases}I_{m\times n},\quad k=0,\\
\underbrace{A\,\rotatebox[]{-90.0}{$\ltimes$}\,\cdots\,\rotatebox[]{-90.0}{$\ltimes$}\,A}_{k},\quad k>0.\\
\end{cases}

(248)

In addition, let
p(x)=x^{n}+c_{n-1}x^{n-1}+\cdots+c_{1}x+c_{0}
, we define

\displaystyle p(\langle A\rangle):=A^{\langle n\rangle}+c_{n-1}A^{\langle n-1\rangle}+\cdots+c_{1}A+c_{0}I_{m\times n}\in\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{m\times n}.

(249)

Then we have the following result.

Theorem 12.9

(Generalized Cayley-Hamilton theorem)
[
19
]

Let
p(x)=x^{m}+c_{m-1}x^{m-1}+\cdots+c_{1}x+c_{0}
be the characteristic function of
\Pi_{A}
, which is also called the characteristic function of
A
. Then

\displaystyle A^{\langle m+1\rangle}+c_{m-1}A^{\langle m\rangle}+\cdots+c_{1}A^{\langle 2\rangle}+c_{0}A=0.

(250)

Remark 12.10

(i)

When
n<m
we can take the characteristic function of
\Pi_{A^{\rm T}}
as the characteristic function of
A
. This alternative choice can reduce the degree of the characteristic function.

(ii)

Formally, we may rewritten (
250
) into the form as

\displaystyle A^{\langle m\rangle}+c_{m-1}A^{\langle m-1\rangle}+\cdots+c_{1}A+c_{0}I^{A}_{m\times n}=0.

(251)

Then if
c_{0}\neq 0
we can define

\displaystyle I^{A}_{m\times n}:=-\frac{1}{c_{0}}[A^{\langle m\rangle}+c_{m-1}A^{\langle m-1\rangle}+\cdots+c_{1}A].

(252)

According to Generalized Cayley-Hamilton theorem, it is clear that

\displaystyle A\,\rotatebox[]{-90.0}{$\ltimes$}\,I^{A}_{m\times n}=I^{A}_{m\times n}\,\rotatebox[]{-90.0}{$\ltimes$}\,A=A.

(253)

Moreover, for any polynomial
p\langle A\rangle

\displaystyle p(\langle A\rangle)\,\rotatebox[]{-90.0}{$\ltimes$}\,I^{A}_{m\times n}=I^{A}_{m\times n}\,\rotatebox[]{-90.0}{$\ltimes$}\,p(\langle A\rangle)=p(\langle A\rangle).

(254)

Then
A
is said to be relatively invertible and define its relative inverse by

\displaystyle A^{\langle-1\rangle}=-\frac{1}{c_{0}}\left[A^{\langle m-1\rangle}+c_{m-1}A^{\langle m-2\rangle}+\cdots+c_{1}I^{A}_{m\times n}\right].

(255)

It follows that

\displaystyle A\,\rotatebox[]{-90.0}{$\ltimes$}\,A^{\langle-1\rangle}=A^{\langle-1\rangle}\,\rotatebox[]{-90.0}{$\ltimes$}\,A=I^{A}_{m\times n}.

(256)

It seems that if

is relatively invertible, then

is

. Unfortunately,

depends on

is not.

Theorem 12.13

Concluding Remarks

16.1
A Mathematics over MDMVs

To investigate linear (control) systems the classical matrix theory plays a fundamental rule. While the modern mathematics provides a solid foundation for the matrix theory. Particularly, the concepts from abstract algebra such as group, ring, and module are used to describe the matrices/vectors under linear algebraic operators; the geometric structures such as vector space, metric, inner product, topology, manifold, etc. are essential for describing the state space of the systems. Finally the Lie group and Lie algebra structure over matrices leads to the general linear group and general linear algebra, which reveal the inside structure of the dynamics of linear (control) systems.

Recently, the STPs/STAs have been developed to overcome the dimension barrier of classical matrix theory. As the cross-dimensional operators, STPs/STAs demonstrate their dimension-free characteristic, which makes the classical algebraic and geometric structures unsuitable for MVMDs. They, therefore, are stimulating a new matrix theory to merge. This new matrix theory demands a new mathematical foundation. This paper explores a new foundation, which is called the CDM. The CDM consists of three parts: hyper algebra, hyper geometry, and hyper linear algebra and hyper linear group.

In this paper the hyper algebra consists of three new concepts: the hyper group, hyper ring, and hyper module.
The hyper group extends the classical group to a group with multiple identities, each identity is used to construct a component group. The cross-dimensional operator joints all component groups together. The equivalence group shows the unified properties of component groups. Based on hyper group, the hyper ring and hyper module are also proposed in a natural way. They have similar structure: component objects and equivalence rings/modules.

The hyper geometry uses the hyper group structure to construct a hyper vector space, which has multiple zeros. Then the hyper inner product and hyper metric are also obtained for cross-dimensional Euclidian space
{\mathbb{R}}^{\infty}
. Furthermore, the metric-based topological structure and a differentiable structure have also been posed on
{\mathbb{R}}^{\infty}
. Using such topological and differentiable structure, the hyper manifold is constructed. These objects provide a cross-dimensional geometric structure for MVMDs.

Finally, the Lie algebra and Lie group structure for hyper matrix group with respect to
\hat{+}
and
\hat{\,\rotatebox[]{-90.0}{$\ltimes$}\,}
respectively, is proposed. The topological hyper group is also constructed. They eventually lead to the hyper-Lie algebra and the hyper-Lie group for MVMDs, called the hyper general linear algebra and hyper general linear group.

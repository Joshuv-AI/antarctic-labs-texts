Spectral gaps, critical exponents and representations of negatively curved groups


Abstract
. In this paper we introduce a notion of Poincaré exponent for isometric representations of discrete groups on Hilbert spaces. Similarly as growth exponents control the geometry this exponent is shown to control the size of spectral gaps. Following similar ideas as Patterson and Sullivan in [ 71 ] [ 78 ] it is used in the case of negatively curved groups to construct weakly contained boundary representations [ 8 ] reflecting the spectral properties of the original representation analogously as complementary series representations in the case of semi-simple Lie groups [ 77 ] . This is exploited to deduced sharp estimates on spectral invariants. A quantitive property (T) á la Cowling [ 32 ] is also established proving uniform bound on the mixing rate of representations of hyperbolic groups with property (T). Along the way some properties of boundary representations are discussed. A original characterisation of the positivity of the so-called Knapp-Stein operators and certain fusion rules on the boundary complementary series representations [ 15 ] ( [ 22 ] ) are established.

1.3.1.

2.1.
Strong hyperbolicity and consequences

A metric space

if for all

and

for all

The

on
(X,d_{X})
at

is defined as

(x,y)_{o}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\frac{1}{2}(d_{X}(x,o)+d_{X}(y,o)-d_{X}(x,y))

for all
x,y\in X
and
(X,d_{X})
is
Gromov hyperbolic
if there exists
C\geq 0
such that

(x,y)_{o}\geq\min\{(x,z)_{o},(z,y)_{o}\}-C

for all
x,y,z\in X
.

Assuming
(X,d_{X})
is Gromov hyperbolic the
Gromov boundary
of

can be defined as

{\partial}X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\{(x_{n})_{n}\in X^{\mathbb{N}}:\liminf_{n,m}(x_{n},x_{m})_{o}=+\infty\}/\sim

with
(x_{n})_{n}\sim(y_{n})_{n}\Leftrightarrow\liminf_{n,m}(x_{n},y_{m})_{o}=+\infty
.
The space
{\partial}X
endowed with the natural visual topology is compact as the space
(X,d_{X})
is proper. Moreover there exists a compatible topology on
\overline{X}=X\cup{\partial}X
that makes it a compact space
[
42
]
.

Definition 2.1

(
[
68
]
)
.

A roughly geodesic hyperbolic metric space

e^{-\varepsilon(x,y)_{o}}\leq e^{-\varepsilon(x,z)_{o}}+e^{-\varepsilon(z,y)_{o}}

Assuming

is strongly hyperbolic, the Gromov product extends continuously to the Gromov compactification

with

) and a visual distance

on

In particular

.

2.4.
Nets and positive families of vectors

The reader can refer to
[
3
, Chap. 2]
for details about the notion of net and order.

Given a Hilbert space,
\mathcal{H}
, and countable set,
I
,
a family of vectors
\{v_{i}\}_{i\in I}

belongs to a positive cone
in
\mathcal{H}
if
(v_{i},v_{j})\geq 0
for all
i,j\in I
.

Given such a family,
\{v_{i}\}_{i\in I}
, we define the summation net as

(2^{I}_{0},\subset)\rightarrow(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H}_{+});F\subset I\mapsto v_{F}=\sum_{F}v_{i}

where
(2^{I}_{0},\subset)
stands for the finite subsets of
I
with direct order induced by the inclusion. Observe that
(\|v_{F}\|)_{F}
is a non-decreasing net
as

\|v+w\|^{2}=\|v\|^{2}+\|w\|^{2}+2(v,w)\geq\max\{\|v\|^{2},\|w\|^{2}\}

for all
v,w\in\mathcal{C}_{+}

with
\mathcal{C}_{+}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\overline{\{\sum_{F}c_{i}v_{i}:\text{$c_{i}\geq 0$ for all $i\in F$}\}}\subset\mathcal{H}
.

Lemma 2.15
.

Given a family
\{v_{i}\}_{i\in I}
within a positive cone of
\mathcal{H}
, the summation net
(v_{F})_{F}
converges if and only if
(\|v_{F}\|)_{F}
is bounded.

In particular
\sum_{i\in I}v_{i}
is well defined iff there exists an exhaustion
(F_{n})_{n}
by finite sets of
I
such that
\sup_{n}\|\sum_{F_{n}}v_{i}\|
is finite.

Proof.

It is enough to prove that if
(\|v_{F}\|)_{F}
is bounded the net
(v_{F})_{F}
converges in
\mathcal{H}
.

As
(\|v_{F}\|)_{F}
is monotone and bounded it converges to
\sup_{2^{I}_{0}}\|v_{F}\|
.
In particular
(\|v_{F}\|)_{F}
is a Cauchy net.

On the other hand given
F_{0}\subset F
one has:

\|v_{F\setminus F_{0}}\|^{2}=\|v_{F}-v_{F_{0}}\|^{2}=\|v_{F}\|^{2}-\|v_{F_{0}}\|^{2}-2(v_{F_{0}},v_{F\setminus F_{0}})\leq\|v_{F}\|^{2}-\|v_{F_{0}}\|^{2}

that is small whenever
F_{0}
is taken large enough and as

\|v_{F}-v_{F^{\prime}}\|\leq\|v_{F^{\prime}\setminus F_{0}}\|+\|v_{F\setminus F_{0}}\|

for
F_{0}\subset F^{\prime},F
it follows that
(v_{F})_{F}
is a Cauchy net in the Banach space
\mathcal{H}
and thus converges to a unique limit point.
∎

Lemma 2.16
.

Given a summable family
\{v_{i}\}_{i\in I}
within a positive cone of
\mathcal{H}
and
u=(u_{i})_{i\in I},u^{\prime}=(u^{\prime}_{i})_{i\in I}\in\ell^{\infty}_{+}(I)
with
u\leq u^{\prime}
.
Then

\|\sum_{i\in I}u_{i}v_{i}\|\leq\|\sum_{i\in I}u_{i}^{\prime}v_{i}\|\leq\|u^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\|\sum_{i\in I}v_{i}\|

Proof.

It enough to prove the first inequality on a finite subset,
F
, of
I
. The result will follow from Lemma
2.15
.

In this case

\displaystyle\|\sum_{i\in F}u_{i}v_{i}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j\in F}u_{i}u_{j}(v_{i},v_{j})\leq\sum_{i,j\in F}u_{i}^{\prime}u_{j}^{\prime}(v_{i},v_{j})=\|\sum_{i\in F}u_{i}^{\prime}v_{i}\|^{2}

∎

2.5.
Observation about the GNS of pointwise positive functions

A function
\phi
is called positive definite if the quadratic form on

given by

as cyclic vector.

for all

.
This construction, called Gelfand-Naimak-Segal construction (or GNS), defines a bijection between positive definite functions and classes of cyclic representations of
\Gamma
.

Let

be the set of positive definite functions on
\Gamma
that are pointwise positive. Given

Observation that

belongs to a positive cones of
\mathcal{H}_{\phi}
as defined in Subsection
2.4
.
This follows from the fact that

(\pi_{\phi}(g){\bf 1}_{\phi},\pi_{\phi}(h){\bf 1}_{\phi})=(\pi_{\phi}(h^{-1}g){\bf 1}_{\phi},{\bf 1}_{\phi})=\phi(h^{-1}g)\geq 0

Given a weight
\omega
with
\omega(g)>0
for all

such that

is well defined, Lemma

implies that

is also well defined with

\|\sum_{g}\omega(g)|f|(g)\pi_{\phi}(g){\bf 1}_{\phi}\|\leq\|f\|_{\infty}\|\sum_{g}\omega(g)\pi_{\phi}(g){\bf 1}_{\phi}\|

for all

.

, on

as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\omega}^{2}.B_{\phi,\omega}(f)

\displaystyle=\|\sum_{g}\omega(g)f_{+}(g)\pi_{\phi}(g){\bf 1}_{\phi}-\sum_{g}\omega(g)f_{-}(g)\pi_{\phi}(g){\bf 1}_{\phi}\|^{2}

.
The case of complex-valued functions is obtained by scalar extension.

For this positive quadratic form the linear operators

for

Observe that

Moreover

In particular

3.1.

Given a pointwise positive function on
\Gamma

\mathcal{P}(f;\sigma)=\sum_{g\in\Gamma}e^{-\sigma\delta d_{\Gamma}(g,e)}f(g)

is
\delta(f)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}s(f)\delta
.
A pointwise positive function is of

if
\mathcal{P}(f;\sigma)\xrightarrow{\sigma\rightarrow s(f)^{+}}+\infty
.

Remark 3.1
.

The indicator function of a subgroup of
\Gamma
is of divergent type if and only if the subgroup is of divergent type
[
71
]
.

As in the previous constructions (e.g
[
29
]

[
30
]

[

]

]
.

5.2.
Spectral gap and exponential mixing

We establish an upper bound on the spectral radius of
\|\pi_{\phi}(f)\|_{\text{op}}
for
\phi\in\mathbb{P}_{+}(\Lambda)
and
f\in{\mathbb{C}}[\Lambda]
in terms of its Poincaré exponent and
regular spectral radius
, namely
\|\lambda(f)\|_{\text{op}}
.

Given
f\in{\mathbb{C}}[\Lambda]
we denote
r(f)
the minimal
r>0
such that
\{f\neq 0\}\subset B(e,r)
.

Theorem
1
.

Given a positive cyclic representation,
\phi
, on
\Lambda
one has:

\|\pi_{\phi}(f)\|_{\text{op}}\leq e^{\frac{1}{2}\delta(\phi)r(f)}\|\lambda(f)\|_{\text{op}}

for all

.

In order to prove Theorem
1
we recall the following technical Lemma’s proved in
[
31
]
(respectively p.101 and p.102):

Lemma 5.7
.

Given a unitary representation
(\pi,\mathcal{H})
,
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}\subset\mathcal{H}
a dense subspace and

one has:

\|\pi(f)\|_{\text{op}}=\sup_{v\in\mathcal{H}_{\infty}}\lim_{n}(\pi(f^{*}*f^{*2n})v,v)^{\frac{1}{4n}}

Lemma 5.8
.

For all
f\in{\mathbb{C}}[\Lambda]
,
\lim_{n}\|(f^{*}*f)^{*2n}\|_{2}^{\frac{1}{4n}}=\|\lambda(f)\|_{\text{op}}
.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

For all
f\in{\mathbb{C}}[\Lambda]
and
v=\pi_{\phi}(f){\bf 1}_{\phi}
one has

(\pi_{\phi}(g)v,v)=\overline{f}*\phi*f^{\vee}(g)
with
f^{\vee}(g)=f(g^{-1})
and, using Lemma
5.1
:

\delta(|(\pi_{\phi}v,v)|)\leq\delta(|f|*\phi*|f^{\vee}|)\leq\delta(\phi)

As
(\pi_{\phi},{\bf 1}_{\phi})
is a cyclic representation the subspace
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\langle\pi_{\phi}(f){\bf 1}_{\phi}:f\in{\mathbb{C}}[\Lambda]\rangle\subset\mathcal{H}
is dense.

It follows from Lemma
5.7
that

\displaystyle\|\pi_{\phi}(f)\|_{\text{op}}=\sup_{v\in\mathcal{H}_{\infty}}\lim_{n}(\sum_{\Lambda}(f^{*}*f)^{*2n}(g)(\pi_{\phi}(g)v,v))^{\frac{1}{4n}}

for all
f\in{\mathbb{C}}[\Lambda]
.

Let
S=\{f\neq 0\}\subset B(e,r(f))
, then
S^{(n)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\{(f^{*}*f)^{*2n}\neq 0\}\subset B(e,4nr(f))

and thus, for
s
such that
s\delta=\delta(\phi)
:

\displaystyle(\sum_{\Lambda}(f^{*}*f)^{*2n}(g)(\pi_{\phi}(g)v,v))^{2}=(\sum_{S^{(n)}}e^{\frac{s}{2}\delta d_{\Lambda}(g,e)}(f^{*}*f)^{*2n}(g)e^{-\frac{s}{2}\delta d_{\Lambda}(g,e)}(\pi_{\phi}(g)v,v))^{2}

\displaystyle\leq e^{s\delta 4nr(f)}\|(f^{*}*f)^{*2n}\|_{2}^{2}\sum_{S^{(n)}}e^{-s\delta d_{\Lambda}(g,e)}|(\pi_{\phi}(g)v,v)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq e^{s\delta 4nr(f)}\|(f^{*}*f)^{*2n}\|_{2}^{2}(\sum_{S^{(n)}}e^{-(1+\frac{\varepsilon}{2})s\delta d_{\Lambda}(g,e)}|(\pi_{\phi}(g)v,v)|^{2+\varepsilon})^{\frac{2}{2+\varepsilon}}|S^{(n)}|^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2+\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle\prec e^{s\delta 4nr(f)}e^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2+\varepsilon}\delta 8nr(f)}\mathcal{P}(|(\pi_{\phi}v,v)|^{2+\varepsilon};s+\frac{\varepsilon s}{2})\|(f^{*}*f)^{*2n}\|_{2}^{2}

for any
\varepsilon>0
, where the last inequality uses the fact that:

for some constant
C
.

As a consequence of Lemma
5.1

\delta(|(\pi_{\phi}v,v)|^{2+\varepsilon})\leq\delta(|(\pi_{\phi}v,v)|)
and using Lemma
5.8
:

\displaystyle\limsup_{n}(\sum_{\Lambda}(f^{*}*f)^{*2n}(g)(\pi(g)v,v))^{\frac{1}{4n}}

\displaystyle\leq e^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2+\varepsilon}\delta r(f)}e^{\frac{1}{2}s\delta r(f)}\|\lambda(f)\|_{\text{op}}

As
\varepsilon
can be taken arbitrarily small the inequality follows.
∎

5.3.
Quantitative property (T) for hyperbolic groups

This subsection is dedicated to the proof of Theorem
5
.

Let

be a finite subset of a discrete group
\Lambda
and
\varepsilon>0
.
A isometric representation on a Hilbert space,

\Lambda

-almost invariant vector if there exists

with

such that

for all

.
More generally a representation
\pi
has almost invariant vectors if

finite and
\varepsilon>0
can be chosen arbitrarily.

Definition 5.13
.

A discrete
\Lambda
has the Kazhdan property (T) if every unitary representation with almost invariant vectors has a non-zero invariant vector.

It follows from
[
82
]
that generic Gromov hyperbolic groups have the Kazhdan property (T) (see
[
11
]
for more about property (T)).

Proposition 5.14
.

Let
\Gamma
be a Gromov hyperbolic group with the Kazhdan property (T).
There exists

such that for all positive cyclic representation without invariant vector,
\phi
, the critical exponent of
\phi
satisfies
\delta(\phi)\leq\delta_{0}
.

Proof.

It follows from
[
11
, p.33]
that if
\Gamma
has the Kazhdan property (T) there exist
S\subset\Gamma
finite and
\varepsilon>0
such that any representation with a
(S,\varepsilon)
-almost invariant has a non-zero invariant vector.

Assume one can find
(\phi_{n}\simeq[\pi_{\phi_{n}},{\bf 1}_{\phi_{n}}])_{n}
a sequence of positive cyclic representations without
(S,\varepsilon)
-almost invariant vectors such that
\delta(\phi_{n})\rightarrow\delta
.
It follows from Lemma
4.3
and Theorem

that

\pi_{\phi_{n}}\xrightarrow{n}1
for the Fell topology.
In particular there exists
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
, for all

,
\pi_{\phi_{n}}
has a
(S,\varepsilon)
-almost invariant vector which is a contradiction.
Therefore one can find
0\leq\delta_{0}<\delta
such that whenever
\phi\in\mathbb{P}_{+}(\Gamma)
does not have a
(S,\varepsilon)
-almost invariant vectors
\delta(\phi)\leq\delta_{0}
.
∎

Remark 5.15
.

If we consider the spectrum of critical values:

\text{Spec}_{+}(\Gamma,d_{\Gamma})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\{\delta(\pi):[\pi,v]\in\mathbb{P}_{+}(\Gamma)\}

Proposition

.

.

Given a probability

\displaystyle\|\pi(\mu)v\|^{4}

\displaystyle=\sum_{g}\mu^{*}*\mu(g)(\pi\otimes\overline{\pi}(g)v\otimes\overline{v},v\otimes\overline{v})

\|\sum_{g:d_{\Gamma}(g,e)\leq L}\pi_{s}(g)|\mathcal{H}(\mu,E)\|_{\text{op}}\asymp\begin{cases}\frac{1}{(2s-1)}e^{s\delta L}&\text{for $s>\frac{1}{2}$}\\
e^{s\delta L}L&\text{for $s=\frac{1}{2}$}\end{cases}

for
L\geq R
.

Proof.

Observe that

\displaystyle\inf_{{\partial}\Gamma}\pi_{s}(f){\bf 1}

\displaystyle\leq(E(\pi_{s}(f){\bf 1}),\pi_{s}(f){\bf 1})_{L^{2}(\mu)}^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\|\pi_{s}(f)|\mathcal{H}(\mu,E)\|_{\text{op}}

\displaystyle\leq_{\text{Cor. \ref{cor:control:general:l2}}}\|\pi_{s}(f){\bf 1}|L^{\infty}(\mu)\|

and thus

\|\pi_{s}(f)|\mathcal{H}(\mu,E)\|_{\text{op}}\asymp\|\pi_{s}(f){\bf 1}|L^{\infty}(\mu)\|
whenever

\|\pi_{s}(f){\bf 1}|L^{\infty}(\mu)\|\prec\inf_{{\partial}\Gamma}\pi_{s}(f){\bf 1}
.

We conclude using Lemma
2.14
.
∎

Corollary 5.20
.

Let
\mu

{\partial}{\Gamma}

It follows from Proposition

that

\|\frac{1}{|B(e,r)|}\sum_{g:d_{\Gamma}(g,e)\leq L}\pi_{s}(g)|\mathcal{H}(\mu,E)\|_{\text{op}}\asymp\begin{cases}\frac{1}{(2s-1)}e^{-(1-s)\delta L}&\text{for $s>\frac{1}{2}$}\\
e^{-\frac{1}{2}\delta L}L&\text{for $s=\frac{1}{2}$}\end{cases}

and thus

\lim_{r\rightarrow+\infty}-\frac{1}{r}\log\|\frac{1}{|B(e,r)|}\sum_{g:d_{\Gamma}(g,e)\leq r}\pi_{s}(g)|\mathcal{H}(\nu,E)\|_{\text{op}}=(1-s)\delta

∎

In particular the boundary representations,

.

Appendix A
Norm transfer estimate

Lemma A.1
.

Let
(B,\|*\|_{B})\subset(\mathcal{H},\|*\|_{\mathcal{H}})
be a continuous embedding of a Banach space
B
into a Hilbert space,
\mathcal{H}
, with dense range and
T
a densely defined operator of domain
\mathcal{D}(T)
on
\mathcal{H}
such that
B\subset\mathcal{D}(T)
,
TB\subset\mathcal{D}(T^{*})
and
T^{*}T
restrict as a bounded operator on
B
, where
T^{*}
stands for the adjoint in
\mathcal{H}
.
Then
T
is bounded on
\mathcal{H}
and

\|T|\mathcal{H}\|_{\text{op}}\leq\|T^{*}T|B\|_{\text{op}}^{\frac{1}{2}}

Proof.

Identifying
B
with a dense subspace of
\mathcal{H}
:

\displaystyle\|T|\mathcal{H}\|_{\text{op}}=\sup_{v\in B\subset\mathcal{D}(T)}\frac{\|Tv\|_{\mathcal{H}}}{\|v\|_{\mathcal{H}}}

On the other hand, as
TB\subset\mathcal{D}(T^{*})
:

\|Tv\|_{\mathcal{H}}=(T^{*}Tv,v)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\|T^{*}Tv\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|v\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{1}{2}}

and by induction, as
T^{*}T
maps
B
to itself:

\|Tv\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leq\|(T^{*}T)^{2^{n}}v\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2^{-n-1}}\|v\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{1-2^{-n-1}}

It follows that:

\frac{\|Tv\|_{\mathcal{H}}}{\|v\|_{\mathcal{H}}}\leq\liminf_{n}\|(T^{*}T)^{2n}v\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{1}{4n}}

As
T^{*}T
induces a bounded operator on
B
:

\displaystyle\|(T^{*}T)^{2n}v\|_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle\leq c(B,\mathcal{H})\|(T^{*}T)^{2n}v\|_{B}

\displaystyle\leq c(B,\mathcal{H})\|(T^{*}T)^{2n}|B\|_{\text{op}}\|v\|_{B}

\displaystyle\leq c(B,\mathcal{H})\|T^{*}T|B\|_{\text{op}}^{2n}\|v\|_{B}

for all
v\in B
, where
c(B,\mathcal{H})
denote the norm of the embedding
B\subset\mathcal{H}
.

It follows that

\liminf_{n}\|(T^{*}T)^{2n}v\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{1}{4n}}\leq\|T^{*}T|B\|_{\text{op}}^{\frac{1}{2}}.\liminf_{n}\|v\|_{B}^{\frac{1}{4n}}

and thus

\|T|\mathcal{H}\|_{\text{op}}\leq\|T^{*}T|B\|_{\text{op}}^{\frac{1}{2}}

∎

Appendix B
Weak operator topology

Given two normed spaces
(X,N_{X}),(Y,N_{Y})
a sequence
(T_{n})_{n}
of uniformly bounded operators converges for the weak operator topology (WOT) to
T
if
T
is bounded and for all
x\in X
and
\varphi\in Y^{\prime}
one has
\varphi(T_{n}(x))\rightarrow\varphi(T_{n}(x))
.

Theorem 10
.

Let
X,Y
be two normed spaces.
Assume
Y
is a Banach reflexive space.
The space of bounded operators with norms at most
1
between
X
and
Y
,
\text{B}_{\leq 1}(X,Y)
, endowed with the weak operator topology is compact.

Proof.

The proof almost follows the same lines as the Hilbert case (see
[
51
, Thm. 5.1.3]
).
Using reflexivity together with Banach-Alaoglu and Tychonoff theorems (
[
3
]
) we deduce that the space
K=(Y_{\leq 1},\sigma(Y,Y^{\prime}))^{X_{\leq 1}}
is compact.
Moreover the map

A:(\text{B}_{\leq 1}(X,Y),WOT)\rightarrow K;\quad T\mapsto[A(T):x\mapsto T(x)]

is a homeomorphism onto its image
A((\text{B}_{\leq 1}(X,Y),WOT))=K^{\prime}\subset K
from the definition of the topologies involved.
To prove compactness
K^{\prime}
it is enough to prove that it is closed.
Using an approximation argument, every elements in
\overline{K^{\prime}}
define a linear map between
X
and
Y
.
Given
T\in\overline{K^{\prime}}
,
(T_{i})_{i}
a sequence in
K^{\prime}
converging to
T
,
x\in X_{\leq 1}
and
\varphi\in Y_{\leq 1}^{\prime}
such that
\|T(x)\|=\varphi(T(x))
, thanks to Hahn-Banach theorem, one has

\|T(x)\|=\varphi(T(x))=\liminf_{i}\varphi(T_{i}(x))\leq\liminf\|T_{i}\|\leq 1
.
In other words
T\in\text{B}_{\leq 1}(X,Y^{\prime})
.
∎

Lemma B.1
.

Let
X,Y
be two normed spaces.
The map

B:(\text{B}_{\leq 1}(X,Y^{\prime}),WOT)\times(X,N_{X})\rightarrow(Y^{\prime},\sigma(Y^{\prime},Y));(T,x)\mapsto T(x)

is continuous.

Proof.

Let
(T_{i})_{i}
and
(x_{j})_{j}
be two converging net respectively to
T
and
x
in
\text{B}_{\leq 1}(X,Y)
and
X
.
Given
y\in Y
one can find
i_{0}
and
j_{0}
such that
|T_{i}(x)(y)-T(x)(y)|\leq\varepsilon
and
N_{X}(x_{j}-x)\leq\varepsilon
for respectively
i\geq i_{0}
and
j\geq j_{0}
.
It follows follows

\displaystyle|T_{i}(x_{j})(y)-T(x)(y)|

\displaystyle\leq|T_{i}(x_{j})(y)-T_{i}(x)(y)|+|T_{i}(x)(y)-T(x)(y)|

\displaystyle\leq N_{X}(x_{j}-x)+\varepsilon\leq 2\varepsilon

for
i\geq i_{0}
and
j\geq j_{0}
.
∎

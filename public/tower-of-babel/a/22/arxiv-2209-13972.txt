On the speed of convergence of Piterbarg constants
Krzysztof Bisewski, Grigori Jasnovidov

Abstract




Abstract:
In this paper we derive an upper bound for the difference between
the continuous and discrete Piterbarg constants. Our result allows
us to approximate the classical Piterbarg constants by their discrete
counterparts using Monte Carlo simulations with an explicit error rate.

Key Words:
fractional Brownian motion; Piterbarg constants; discretization error; Monte Carlo simulation;

AMS Classification:
60G15; 60G70; 65C05

1.
Introduction

Let
B_{\alpha}(t),t\in\mathbb{R}
be a fractional Brownian motion (later on fBM), i.e., a
Gaussian process with zero expectation and covariance function given by

\displaystyle\cov(B_{\alpha}(t),B_{\alpha}(s))=\frac{|t|^{\alpha}+|s|^{\alpha}-|t-s|^{\alpha}}{2},\ \ \ \ \ t,s\in\mathbb{R},\ \alpha\in(0,2).

One of the constants that typically appear in the asymptotics of
the ruin probabilities for Gaussian processes are the Piterbarg constants
defined for
d>0
and
\alpha\in(0,2)
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}(d,\mathcal{K})=\mathbb{E}\left\{\sup\limits_{t\in\mathbb{R}\cap\mathcal{K}}e^{\sqrt{2}B_{\alpha}(t)-(1+d)|t|^{\alpha}}\right\},

where
\mathcal{K}
can be both
[0,\infty)
and
(-\infty,\infty)
.
We refer to Theorem 10.1 in
[
16
]
for finiteness and
positivity of
\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}(d,\mathcal{K})
; some other
contributions dealing with Piterbarg constants are

[
1
,
15
,
7
,
13
,
9
]
.
We notice, that the Piterbarg constants appear
in Gaussian queueing theory results, see
[
11
,
8
]
.
As for well-known Pickands constants (see, e.g.,
[
16
]
),
the exact value of the classical Piterbarg
constant is known only for special case
\alpha=1
, namely

\mathcal{P}_{1}(d,(-\infty,\infty))=1+\frac{2}{d}-\frac{1}{2d+1}

and
\mathcal{P}_{1}(d,[0,\infty))=1+\frac{1}{d}
, see
[
1
]
and

[
14
]
, respectively.
In other cases naturally arises the question of an approximation of
the classical Piterbarg constants.

Since it seems difficult to simulate fBM
on a continuous-time scale, one can approximate the
Piterbarg constants by their discrete
analogous defined for
\delta>0
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}^{\delta}(d,\mathcal{K})=\mathbb{E}\left\{\sup\limits_{t\in G(\delta)\cap\mathcal{K}}e^{\sqrt{2}B_{\alpha}(t)-(1+d)|t|^{\alpha}}\right\},

where
G(\delta)=\delta\mathbb{Z}
for
\delta>0
. Set in the following

\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}^{0}(d,\mathcal{K})=\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}(d,\mathcal{K})
and
G(0)=\mathbb{R}
.

The question of speed of convergence of the discrete Piterbarg constants
to continuous ones is related to the estimation of

\sup_{t\in[0,1]}B_{\alpha}(t)-\sup_{t\in[0,1]_{\delta}}B_{\alpha}(t)

as
\delta\to 0
.
We refer to
[
5
,
4
]
for
the interesting analysis of the expression above. For Brownian motion
case (later on BM), i.e.,
when
\alpha=1
, we refer to
[
10
]
for the survey of
the known results for the current moment.

2.
Main Results

Here we present the results needed for approximation
of
\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}(d,\mathcal{K})
by simulation
methods. The theorem below derives an upper bound for the difference
between the continuous and discrete Piterbarg constants.

Theorem 2.1
.

For any
d>0
with some constant
\mathcal{C}>0
that does not depend on

\delta
it holds, that
as
\delta\to 0

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}(d,\mathcal{K})-\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}^{\delta}(d,\mathcal{K})\leq\mathcal{C}\delta^{\alpha/2}(-\ln\delta)^{1/2}.

Since it is impossible to simulate a fBM on the infinite-time horizon
we deal with the truncated version of the Piterbarg constants. Namely,
for
d>0
and
\delta,T\geq 0
a truncated Piterbarg constant
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}^{\delta}(d,\mathcal{K};T)=\mathbb{E}\left\{\sup\limits_{t\in\mathcal{K}\cap[-T,T]_{\delta}}e^{\sqrt{2}B_{\alpha}(t)-(1+d)|t|^{\alpha}}\right\},

where for real
a,b
and
\eta>0

[a,b]_{\eta}=[a,b]\cap\eta\mathbb{Z}
. Set in the following

[a,b]_{0}:=[a,b]
for
a<b
.

The following theorem provide us an upper bound for the
difference between a Piterbarg constant and the corresponding
truncated Piterbarg constant:

Theorem 2.2
.

It holds with some uniform in
\delta\geq 0
constant
\mathcal{C}
and
all sufficiently large
T
, that

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}^{\delta}(d,\mathcal{K})-\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}^{\delta}(d,\mathcal{K};T)\leq e^{-\mathcal{C}T^{\alpha}}.

The results above imply the following approach for approximation of

\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}(d,\mathcal{K})
for
d>0
:

1)

For small
\delta>0
take
S_{\delta}=(-\ln\delta)^{2/\alpha}
and approximate
\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}^{\delta}(d,\mathcal{K};S_{\delta})
by

\widehat{\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}^{\delta}}(d,\mathcal{K};S_{\delta})

obtained by Monte-Carlo simulations.

2)

Theorem
2.1
and Theorem

2.2
imply that

\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}(d,\mathcal{K})-\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}^{\delta}(d,\mathcal{K};S_{\delta})\leq\mathcal{C}\delta^{\alpha/2}(-\ln\delta)^{1/2}
uniformly for sufficiently small
\delta>0
,
hence we can approximate
\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}(d,\mathcal{K})
by

\widehat{\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}^{\delta}}(d,\mathcal{K};S_{\delta})

with an error term not exceeding

\mathcal{C}\delta^{\alpha/2}(-\ln\delta)^{1/2}
combined with statistical
error from estimation of

\widehat{\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}^{\delta}}(d,\mathcal{K};S_{\delta})
.

Independence of the increments of Brownian motion allows us to
deduce more precise bounds for

\mathcal{P}_{1}(d,\mathcal{K})-\mathcal{P}_{1}^{\delta}(d,\mathcal{K})

than in Theorem
2.1
. Namely,

Theorem 2.3
.

For
d>0
as
\delta\to 0
it holds, that

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{1}(d,\mathcal{K})-\mathcal{P}_{1}^{\delta}(d,\mathcal{K})\sim-\frac{\zeta(1/2)}{\sqrt{\pi}}\sqrt{\delta}\cdot\mathcal{P}_{1}^{\delta}(d,\mathcal{K}),

where
\zeta
is the Euler-Riemann zeta function and

-\frac{\zeta(1/2)}{\sqrt{\pi}}>0
.

It is interesting, that the constant
-\zeta(1/2)/\sqrt{\pi}

appears above, the same constant plays the same role in the difference
between the classical Pickands constants, see
[
3
]
.
This constant appears in many
problems concerning the difference between supremum of BM on a continuous
and discrete grids, see
[
10
]
.

3.
Proofs

Define for
d>0
and
\alpha\in(0,2)

(1)

\displaystyle Z_{\alpha}(t)=\sqrt{2}B_{\alpha}(t)-(d+1)|t|^{\alpha},\qquad t\in\mathbb{R}.

Before giving proofs we present and prove several
lemmas needed for the proofs of
the main results.

Lemma 3.1
.

For all
d>0
there exists
C_{1},C_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{\sup\limits_{t\in[0,\infty)}e^{Z_{\alpha}(t)}>x\right\}\sim C_{1}(\ln x)^{C_{2}}x^{-1-d},\qquad x\to\infty.

Proof of Lemma
3.1
.

By the self-similarity of fBM, for
x>1
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{\sup\limits_{t\in[0,\infty)}e^{Z_{\alpha}(t)}>x\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists t\in[0,\infty):\sqrt{2}B_{\alpha}(t)-(d+1)t^{\alpha}>\ln x\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists t\in[0,\infty):\frac{\sqrt{2}B_{\alpha}(t)}{(d+1)t^{\alpha}+1}>\sqrt{\ln x}\right\}=:\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists t\in[0,\infty):V(t)>\sqrt{\ln x}\right\}.

The variance of
V(t)
for
t\geq 0
achieves its unique maximum at

t_{0}=(\frac{1}{\sqrt{d+1}})^{2/\alpha}
and

\var\{V(t_{0})\}=\frac{1}{2(d+1)},

hence applying
[
16
, Theorem 10.1]
we find that
there exist some
C_{1}>0
and
C_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\mathbb{P}\left\{\sup_{t\in[0,\infty)}e^{Z_{\alpha}(t)}>x\right\}\sim C_{1}(\ln x)^{C_{2}}x^{-1-d}
, as

x\to\infty
.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

For any
p,T>0
and
\alpha\in(0,2)
for
sufficiently small
\delta>0
with some
\mathcal{C}>0

that does not depend on
\delta
it holds, that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\{\sup\limits_{t,s\in[0,T],|t-s|\leq\delta}|B_{\alpha}(t)-B_{\alpha}(s)|^{p}\right\}^{1/p}\leq\mathcal{C}\delta^{\alpha/2}\sqrt{\ln(|\delta/T|)}.

Proof of Lemma
3.2
.

We have by Theorem 4.2 in
[
6
]
with
some non-negative random variable
\mathcal{A}
that does
not depend on
\delta
and has all finite moments that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\{\sup\limits_{t,s\in[0,T],|t-s|\leq\delta}|B_{\alpha}(t)-B_{\alpha}(s)|^{p}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T^{\frac{\alpha p}{2}}\mathbb{E}\left\{\sup\limits_{t,s\in[0,1],|t-s|\leq\frac{\delta}{T}}|B_{\alpha}(t)-B_{\alpha}(s)|^{p}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle T^{\frac{\alpha p}{2}}\mathbb{E}\left\{\sup\limits_{t,s\in[0,1],|t-s|\leq\frac{\delta}{T}}\left(\mathcal{A}|t-s|^{\alpha/2}\sqrt{|\ln(|t-s|/T)|}\right)^{p}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T^{\frac{\alpha p}{2}}(\frac{\delta}{T})^{\alpha p/2}\sqrt{|\ln(\delta/T)|}^{p}\mathbb{E}\left\{\mathcal{A}^{p}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\delta^{\alpha p/2}\sqrt{|\ln(\delta/T)|}^{p},

and the claim follows.
∎

Now we are ready to prove the main results.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

We have by Theorem
2.2

with
T_{\delta}=(-\ln\delta)^{2/\alpha}
that

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}(d,\mathcal{K})-\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}^{\delta}(d,\mathcal{K})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}(d,\mathcal{K};T_{\delta})-\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}^{\delta}(d,\mathcal{K};T_{\delta})+o(\delta^{\alpha/2}).

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\{\sup\limits_{t\in\mathcal{K}\cap[-T_{\delta},T_{\delta}]}e^{Z_{\alpha}(t)}-\sup\limits_{t\in\mathcal{K}\cap[-T_{\delta},T_{\delta}]_{\delta}}e^{Z_{\alpha}(t)}\right\}+o(\delta^{\alpha/2})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\mathbb{E}\left\{\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T_{\delta}]}e^{Z_{\alpha}(t)}-\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T_{\delta}]_{\delta}}e^{Z_{\alpha}(t)}\right\}+o(\delta^{\alpha/2}).

Note that for any
y\leq x
it holds, that
e^{x}-e^{y}\leq(x-y)e^{x}
.
Implementing this inequality we find that, for
s,t\in[0,T_{\delta}]
,

\displaystyle\left|e^{Z_{\alpha}(t)}-e^{Z_{\alpha}(s)}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{\max(Z_{\alpha}(t),Z_{\alpha}(s))}|Z_{\alpha}(t)-Z_{\alpha}(s)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{\max\limits_{w\in[0,\infty)}Z_{\alpha}(w)}\left|\sqrt{2}(B_{\alpha}(t)-B_{\alpha}(s))-(t^{\alpha}-s^{\alpha})\right|.

By the lines above we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\{\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T_{\delta}]}e^{Z_{\alpha}(t)}-\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T_{\delta}]_{\delta}}e^{Z_{\alpha}(t)}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\{\sup\limits_{t,s\in[0,T_{\delta}],|t-s|\leq\delta}|e^{Z_{\alpha}(t)}-e^{Z_{\alpha}(t)}|\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\{\sup\limits_{t,s\in[0,T_{\delta}],|t-s|\leq\delta}e^{\max\limits_{w\in[0,\infty)}Z_{\alpha}(w)}\left|\sqrt{2}(B_{\alpha}(t)-B_{\alpha}(s))-(t^{\alpha}-s^{\alpha})\right|\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{2}\mathbb{E}\left\{\sup\limits_{t,s\in[0,T_{\delta}],|t-s|\leq\delta}e^{\max\limits_{w\in[0,\infty)}Z_{\alpha}(w)}\left|B_{\alpha}(t)-B_{\alpha}(s)\right|\right\}+\mathbb{E}\left\{e^{\max\limits_{w\in[0,\infty)}Z_{\alpha}(w)}\right\}\sup\limits_{t,s\in[0,T_{\delta}],|t-s|\leq\delta}\left|t^{\alpha}-s^{\alpha}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{2}\mathbb{E}\left\{e^{q\max\limits_{w\in[0,\infty)}Z_{\alpha}(w)}\right\}^{1/q}\mathbb{E}\left\{\sup\limits_{t,s\in[0,T_{\delta}],|t-s|\leq\delta}\left|B_{\alpha}(t)-B_{\alpha}(s)\right|^{p}\right\}^{1/p}+o(\delta^{\alpha/2}),

where
1/q+1/p=1
and
q
is chosen such that

\mathbb{E}\left\{e^{q\max\limits_{w\in[0,\infty)}Z_{\alpha}(w)}\right\}<\infty
, this is possible to
do by Theorem
3.1
.
Next by Lemma
3.2
and lines above we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}(d,\mathcal{K})-\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}^{\delta}(d,\mathcal{K})\leq\mathcal{C}\delta^{\alpha/2}\sqrt{\ln(|\delta/T_{\delta}|)}+o(\delta^{\alpha/2})

and the claim follows.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.

We have

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}^{\delta}(a)-\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}^{\delta}(d,\mathcal{K};T)=\mathbb{E}\left\{\sup\limits_{t\in\mathcal{K}\cap(-\infty,\infty)_{\delta}}e^{Z_{\alpha}(t)}-\sup\limits_{t\in\mathcal{K}\cap[-T,T]_{\delta}}e^{Z_{\alpha}(t)}\right\}\leq\mathbb{E}\left\{\sup\limits_{t\in\mathbb{R}}e^{Z_{\alpha}(t)}\mathbb{I}(M_{T})\right\},

where
M_{T}=\{\omega\in\Omega:Z_{\alpha}(\omega,t)>0\text{ for some }t\in(-\infty,-T]\cap[T,\infty)\}
with
\Omega
being the general probability space.
We have by Borell-TIS inequality and the symmetry

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{M_{T}\right\}\leq 2\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists t\geq T:\sqrt{2}B_{\alpha}(t)-t^{\alpha}>0\right\}=2\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists t\geq T:\frac{\sqrt{2}B_{\alpha}(t)}{t^{\alpha/2}}>t^{\alpha/2}\right\}\leq e^{-CT^{\alpha}}.

By Lemma
3.1

\sup\limits_{t\in\mathbb{R}}e^{Z_{\alpha}(t)}
has the finite moment of
order
1+d/2
and hence applying Hölder inequality we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\{\sup\limits_{t\in\mathbb{R}}e^{Z_{\alpha}(t)}\mathbb{I}(M_{T})\right\}\leq C_{1}\mathbb{P}\left\{M_{T}\right\}^{C_{2}}\leq e^{-CT^{\alpha}}

and the claim follows.∎

Proof of Theorem
2.3
.

Let
Z_{\alpha}(t)=\sqrt{2}B_{\alpha}(t)-(1+a)|t|^{\alpha}
be
defined as in (
1
). We will
show the statement only for
\mathcal{K}=(-\infty,\infty)
;
the proof for
\mathcal{K}=[0,\infty)

will be analogous (but slightly simpler). Fix
a>0
for
J\subseteq\mathbb{R}
, we define

M^{J}=\sup_{t\in J}Z_{\alpha}(t),\quad M^{J}_{\delta}=\sup_{t\in J\cap\delta\mathbb{Z}}Z_{\alpha}(t),\quad\Delta^{J}_{\delta}:=\frac{M^{J}-M^{J}_{\delta}}{\delta^{1/2}}.

For breviety we write
M:=M^{\mathbb{R}},M_{\delta}:=M^{\mathbb{R}}_{\delta}
, and
\Delta_{\delta}:=\Delta^{\mathbb{R}}_{\delta}
. Then,

(2)

\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}(d,\mathcal{K})-\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}^{\delta}(d,\mathcal{K})=\mathbb{E}\left\{e^{M}-e^{M_{\delta}}\right\}.

Using mean value theorem and noticing that
M_{\delta}\leq M
a.s., we obtain the following bounds

(3)

\mathbb{E}\left\{(M-M_{\delta})\cdot e^{M_{\delta}}\right\}\leq\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}(d,\mathcal{K})-\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}^{\delta}(d,\mathcal{K})\leq\mathbb{E}\left\{(M-M_{\delta})\cdot e^{M}\right\}.

Till the end, we want to show that

(4)

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\mathbb{E}\left\{\Delta_{\delta}\cdot e^{M_{\delta}}\right\}=\lim_{\delta\to 0}\mathbb{E}\left\{\Delta_{\delta}\cdot e^{M}\right\}=\lim_{\delta\to 0}\mathbb{E}\left\{\Delta_{\delta}\right\}\cdot\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}(d,\mathcal{K})=-\frac{\zeta(1/2)}{\sqrt{\pi}}\cdot\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}(d,\mathcal{K}),

which, in combination with (
3
), will conclude the proof. We will show (
4
) in three steps.

Step 1.
Show that there exists a random variable
D
such that
\mathbb{E}\left\{D\right\}=-\zeta\big(\tfrac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}\big)\mathbb{E}\left\{\widehat{X}_{1}^{+}\right\}
and such that for any continuity set
A
of
D
and an arbitrary event
B\in\mathcal{F}
of positive probability, where
\mathcal{F}
is the borel
\sigma
-algebra, under which
Z
is adapted to the usual fitration, for
J=\mathbb{R}
, as
\delta\downarrow 0

(5)

\mathbb{P}\left\{\Delta_{\delta}^{J}\in A,B\right\}\to\mathbb{P}\left\{D\in A\right\}\mathbb{P}\left\{B\right\}.

The statement (
5
) for
J=\mathbb{R}
implies that
\Delta_{\delta}\overset{d}{\to}D
and, intuitively, that the sequence is ‘asymptotically independent’ of the process
\{Z_{\alpha}(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
.

Now,
[
12
, Thm. 4]
implies that (
5
) holds for
J=[0,T)
as well as
J=(-T,0]
for arbitrary
T\in(0,\infty)
.

Now, let
\tau
and
\tau_{\delta}
be the (almost surely unique) time epochs of the all-time supremum of
Z
over
\mathbb{R}
and over
\delta\mathbb{\mathbb{Z}}
respectively, i.e.
Z_{\alpha}(\tau)=M
and
Z_{\alpha}(\tau_{\delta})=M_{\delta}
. For any
T>0
, let
C_{T}^{+}
be the event that
\{\tau\in[0,T),\tau_{\delta}\in[0,T)\}
and similarly, let
C_{T}^{-}:=\{\tau\in(-T,0],\tau_{\delta}\in(-T,0]\}
. We have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{\Delta_{\delta}\in A,B\right\}=\mathbb{P}\left\{\Delta_{\delta}\in A,B,C_{T}^{-}\right\}+\mathbb{P}\left\{\Delta_{\delta}\in A,B,C_{T}^{+}\right\}+\mathbb{P}\left\{\Delta_{\delta}\in A,B,(C_{T}^{-}\cup C_{T}^{+})^{\prime}\right\}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left\{\Delta^{[0,T)}_{\delta}\in A,B,C_{T}^{+}\right\}+\mathbb{P}\left\{\Delta_{\delta}^{(-T,0]}\in A,B,C_{T}^{-}\right\}+\mathbb{P}\left\{\Delta_{\delta}\in A,B,(C_{T}^{-}\cup C_{T}^{+})^{\prime}\right\},

which implies that

\displaystyle\Big|\mathbb{P}\left\{\Delta_{\delta}\in A,B\right\}-\big(\mathbb{P}\left\{\Delta^{[0,T)}_{\delta}\in A,B,C_{T}^{+}\right\}+\mathbb{P}\left\{\Delta^{(-T,0]}_{\delta}\in A,B,C_{T}^{-}\right\}\big)\Big|\leq\mathbb{P}\left\{(C_{T}^{-}\cup C_{T}^{+})^{\prime}\right\}.

We now pass
\delta\to 0
, use (
5
) with
B:=B\cap C_{T}^{+}
and
B:=B\cap C_{T}^{-}
, and obtain

\displaystyle\Big|\mathbb{P}\left\{\Delta_{\delta}\in A,B\right\}-\mathbb{P}\left\{D\in A\right\}\big(\mathbb{P}\left\{B,C_{T}^{+}\right\}

\displaystyle+\mathbb{P}\left\{B,C_{T}^{-}\right\}\big)\Big|\leq\mathbb{P}\left\{(C_{T}^{-}\cup C_{T}^{+})^{\prime}\right\}.

We pass
T\to\infty
and notice that
C_{T}^{+}\cap C_{T}^{-}=\emptyset
and
\mathbb{P}\left\{C_{T}^{+}\cup C_{T}^{-}\right\}\to 1
, as
T\to\infty
, which shows (
5
) for
J=\mathbb{R}
. Finally, we note that
\mathbb{E}\left\{D\right\}=-\frac{\zeta(1/2)}{\sqrt{\pi}}
, which is established in
[
2
, Corollary 4]
.

Step 2.
Show that the family of random variables
\Delta_{\delta}\cdot e^{M_{\delta}}
, indexed by
\delta
, is uniformly integrable for
\delta
small enough.

According to de la Vallée-Poussin Theorem, it is enough to establish that there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that the sequence
\mathbb{E}\left\{(\Delta_{\delta}\cdot e^{M_{\delta}})^{1+\varepsilon}\right\}
is bounded for all
\delta
small enough. Due to Hölder’s inequality, with
p,q\in[1,\infty)
satisfying
1/p+1/q=1
we obtain

(6)

\mathbb{E}\left\{(\Delta_{\delta}\cdot e^{M_{\delta}})^{1+\varepsilon}\right\}\leq\left(\mathbb{E}\left\{\Delta_{\delta}^{p(1+\varepsilon)}\right\}\right)^{1/p}\cdot\left(\mathbb{E}\left\{e^{q(1+\varepsilon)M_{\delta}}\right\}\right)^{1/q}.

We now establish that for any
0<p<\alpha
, the
p
th moment of
\Delta_{\delta}
is bounded for all
\delta
small enough. Since
\Delta_{\delta}\leq\Delta_{\delta}^{[0,\infty)}+\Delta_{\delta}^{(-\infty,0]}
, then for any
p>0
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\{\Delta_{\delta}\right\}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\max\{1,2^{p-1}\}\cdot\left(\mathbb{E}\left\{\Delta^{[0,\infty)}_{\delta}\right\}^{p}+\mathbb{E}\left\{\Delta^{(-\infty,0]}_{\delta}\right\}^{p}\right),

where we used the fact that
(x+y)^{p}\leq\max\{1,2^{p-1}\}(x^{p}+y^{p})
for all
x,y>0
. The boundedness of
\mathbb{E}\left\{\Delta_{\delta}\right\}^{p}
readily follows from the boundedness of
\mathbb{E}\left\{\Delta^{[0,\infty)}_{\delta}\right\}^{p}
and
\mathbb{E}\left\{\Delta^{(-\infty,0]}_{\delta}\right\}^{p}
, which are established in
[
2
, Theorem 2]
. Moreover, since
M
is exponentially distributed with mean
(1+a)^{-1}
, then
\mathbb{E}\left\{e^{\alpha M}\right\}<\infty
for any
\alpha<1+a
. So, in (
6
), if we take
q>1
and
\varepsilon>0
small enough such that
q(1+\varepsilon)<1+a
, then
\mathbb{E}\left\{e^{q(1+\varepsilon)M_{\delta}}\right\}\leq\mathbb{E}\left\{e^{q(1+\varepsilon)M}\right\}
will be bounded, as
\delta\to 0
.

Step 3.
Since
e^{M_{\delta}}\to e^{M}
a.s., and the limit is
\mathcal{F}
-measurable, then due to the mixing condition in (
5
), the pair
(\Delta_{\delta},e^{M_{\delta}})
converges jointly in distribution to
(D,e^{M})
, where
D
is independent of
e^{M}
. Now, from the continuous mapping theorem we find that
\Delta_{\delta}\cdot e^{M_{\delta}}\overset{d}{\to}D\cdot e^{M}
. Finally, (
4
) follows from the uniform integrability of the family
\Delta_{\delta}\cdot e^{M_{\delta}}
.
∎

Black-Box k k -to- 1 1 -PCA Reductions: Theory and Applications
Arun Jambulapati, Syamantak Kumar, Jerry Li, Shourya Pandey, Ankit Pensia, Kevin Tian

Abstract
The k k -principal component analysis ( k k -PCA) problem is a fundamental algorithmic primitive that is widely-used in data analysis and dimensionality reduction applications. In statistical settings, the goal of k k -PCA is to identify a top eigenspace of the covariance matrix of a distribution, which we only have black-box access to via samples. Motivated by these settings, we analyze black-box deflation methods as a framework for designing k k -PCA algorithms, where we model access to the unknown target matrix via a black-box 1 1 -PCA oracle which returns an approximate top eigenvector, under two popular notions of approximation. Despite being arguably the most natural reduction-based approach to k k -PCA algorithm design, such black-box methods, which recursively call a 1 1 -PCA oracle k k times, were previously poorly-understood. Our main contribution is significantly sharper bounds on the approximation parameter degradation of deflation methods for k k -PCA. For a quadratic form notion of approximation we term ePCA (energy PCA) , we show deflation methods suffer no parameter loss. For an alternative well-studied approximation notion we term cPCA (correlation PCA) , we tightly characterize the parameter regimes where deflation methods are feasible. Moreover, we show that in all feasible regimes, k k -cPCA deflation algorithms suffer no asymptotic parameter loss for any constant k k . We apply our framework to obtain state-of-the-art k k -PCA algorithms robust to dataset contamination, improving prior work in sample complexity by a poly ​ ( k ) \textup{poly}(k) factor. † † Accepted for presentation at the Conference on Learning Theory (COLT) 2024

4.3
Black-box cPCA in the valid regime

In this section, we provide composition results used to bound the performance of Algorithm

, which is the asymptotically slowest decay rate on

In Lemma

Lemma 11

In the setting of Lemma

Lemma 12
.

Let
\mathbf{M}\in\mathbb{S}_{\succeq\mathbf{0}}^{d\times d}

\begin{gathered}\mathbf{U}_{[i+1,i+h]}\text{ is a }\left(\delta,\gamma\right)\text{-}h\text{-cPCA of }\widetilde{\mathbf{M}}_{i}:=\left(\mathbf{I}_{d}-\mathbf{U}_{[1,i]}\mathbf{U}_{[1,i]}^{\top}\right)\mathbf{M}\left(\mathbf{I}_{d}-\mathbf{U}_{[1,i]}\mathbf{U}_{[1,i]}^{\top}\right).\end{gathered}

Definition 8

where the inequality follows from Fact

\mathcal{D}

5.2

We define our contamination model formally below.

\max\left\{\delta,\gamma\right\}\leq\frac{1}{10}
. Then for
\rho<\frac{\gamma\lambda_{k}(\mathbf{\Sigma})}{2}
, the following hold.

1.

\mathbf{U}
is a
(\Delta,\Gamma)
-
k
-cPCA of
\mathbf{\Sigma}
with
\Delta:=8k(\frac{\rho}{\gamma\lambda_{k}(\mathbf{\Sigma})})^{2}+2\delta
,
\Gamma:=2\gamma
.

2.

\kappa_{k}(\widehat{\bm{\Sigma}})\leq 2\kappa_{k}(\bm{\Sigma})
.

Proof.

Let
\widehat{\mathbf{S}}:=\widehat{\mathbf{V}}^{\leq(1-\gamma)\lambda_{k}(\widehat{\mathbf{\Sigma}})}(\widehat{\mathbf{\Sigma}})
denote the eigenspace of
\widehat{\mathbf{\Sigma}}
with eigenvalues
\leq(1-\gamma)\lambda_{k}(\widehat{\mathbf{\Sigma}})
and let
\widehat{\mathbf{L}}
denote its complement subspace. Similarly, define
\mathbf{S}:=\mathbf{V}^{\leq(1-\Gamma)\lambda_{k}(\mathbf{\Sigma})}(\mathbf{\Sigma})
and let
\mathbf{L}
denote its complement subspace. By definition of
\mathbf{U}
, we have
\lVert\widehat{\mathbf{S}}^{\top}\mathbf{U}\rVert_{\textup{F}}^{2}=\textup{Tr}(\mathbf{U}^{\top}\widehat{\mathbf{S}}\widehat{\mathbf{S}}^{\top}\mathbf{U})\leq\delta
.
Hence,

\displaystyle\lVert\mathbf{S}^{\top}\mathbf{U}\rVert_{\textup{F}}^{2}=\textup{Tr}\left(\mathbf{U}^{\top}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{S}^{\top}\mathbf{U}\right)

\displaystyle=\textup{Tr}\left(\mathbf{U}^{\top}\left(\widehat{\mathbf{L}}\widehat{\mathbf{L}}^{\top}+\widehat{\mathbf{S}}\widehat{\mathbf{S}}^{\top}\right)\mathbf{S}\mathbf{S}^{\top}\left(\widehat{\mathbf{L}}\widehat{\mathbf{L}}^{\top}+\widehat{\mathbf{S}}\widehat{\mathbf{S}}^{\top}\right)\mathbf{U}\right)

\displaystyle=\textup{Tr}\left(\left(\mathbf{U}^{\top}\widehat{\mathbf{L}}\widehat{\mathbf{L}}^{\top}\mathbf{S}+\mathbf{U}^{\top}\widehat{\mathbf{S}}\widehat{\mathbf{S}}^{\top}\mathbf{S}\right)\left(\mathbf{S}^{\top}\widehat{\mathbf{L}}\widehat{\mathbf{L}}^{\top}\mathbf{U}+\mathbf{S}^{\top}\widehat{\mathbf{S}}\widehat{\mathbf{S}}^{\top}\mathbf{U}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\textup{Tr}\left(\mathbf{U}^{\top}\widehat{\mathbf{L}}\widehat{\mathbf{L}}^{\top}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{S}^{\top}\widehat{\mathbf{L}}\widehat{\mathbf{L}}^{\top}\mathbf{U}\right)+2\textup{Tr}\left(\mathbf{U}^{\top}\widehat{\mathbf{S}}\widehat{\mathbf{S}}^{\top}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{S}^{\top}\widehat{\mathbf{S}}\widehat{\mathbf{S}}^{\top}\mathbf{U}\right),\text{ using Fact}\penalty\ \ref{fact:trace_cs}

\displaystyle=2\textup{Tr}\left(\widehat{\mathbf{L}}^{\top}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{S}^{\top}\widehat{\mathbf{L}}\widehat{\mathbf{L}}^{\top}\mathbf{U}\mathbf{U}^{\top}\widehat{\mathbf{L}}\right)+2\textup{Tr}\left(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{S}^{\top}\widehat{\mathbf{S}}\widehat{\mathbf{S}}^{\top}\mathbf{U}\mathbf{U}^{\top}\widehat{\mathbf{S}}\widehat{\mathbf{S}}^{\top}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\left\lVert\widehat{\mathbf{L}}^{\top}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{S}^{\top}\widehat{\mathbf{L}}\right\rVert_{\textup{op}}\textup{Tr}\left(\widehat{\mathbf{L}}^{\top}\mathbf{U}\mathbf{U}^{\top}\widehat{\mathbf{L}}\right)+2\left\lVert\mathbf{S}\mathbf{S}^{\top}\right\rVert_{\textup{op}}\textup{Tr}\left(\widehat{\mathbf{S}}^{\top}\widehat{\mathbf{S}}\widehat{\mathbf{S}}^{\top}\mathbf{U}\mathbf{U}^{\top}\widehat{\mathbf{S}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\left\lVert\mathbf{S}^{\top}\widehat{\mathbf{L}}\right\rVert_{\textup{op}}^{2}\textup{Tr}\left(\widehat{\mathbf{L}}\widehat{\mathbf{L}}^{\top}\mathbf{U}\mathbf{U}^{\top}\right)+2\left\lVert\mathbf{S}\mathbf{S}^{\top}\right\rVert_{\textup{op}}\textup{Tr}\left(\widehat{\mathbf{S}}^{\top}\mathbf{U}\mathbf{U}^{\top}\widehat{\mathbf{S}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\left\lVert\mathbf{S}^{\top}\widehat{\mathbf{L}}\right\rVert_{\textup{op}}^{2}\left\lVert\widehat{\mathbf{L}}\widehat{\mathbf{L}}^{\top}\right\rVert_{\textup{op}}\textup{Tr}\left(\mathbf{U}\mathbf{U}^{\top}\right)+2\left\lVert\mathbf{S}\mathbf{S}^{\top}\right\rVert_{\textup{op}}\left\lVert\widehat{\mathbf{S}}^{\top}\mathbf{U}\right\rVert_{\textup{F}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2k\left\lVert\mathbf{S}^{\top}\widehat{\mathbf{L}}\right\rVert_{\textup{op}}^{2}+2\delta,\text{ using the cPCA guarantee.}

(33)

Further, using Fact
2
,

\displaystyle\left|\lambda_{k}(\widehat{\mathbf{\Sigma}})-\lambda_{k}\left(\bm{\Sigma}\right)\right|\leq\left\lVert\bm{\Sigma}-\widehat{\mathbf{\Sigma}}\right\rVert_{\textup{op}}\leq\rho.

(34)

Therefore for
\Gamma=2\gamma
,

\displaystyle(1-\gamma)\lambda_{k}(\mathbf{\widehat{\Sigma}})-(1-\Gamma)\lambda_{k}(\mathbf{\Sigma})

\displaystyle=\gamma\lambda_{k}\left(\mathbf{\Sigma}\right)+\left(1-\gamma\right)\left(\lambda_{k}(\mathbf{\widehat{\Sigma}})-\lambda_{k}(\mathbf{\Sigma})\right)

\displaystyle\geq\gamma\lambda_{k}\left(\mathbf{\Sigma}\right)-\rho\left(1-\gamma\right),\text{ using}\penalty\ \eqref{eq:weyl_eigenvalue_bound}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\gamma\lambda_{k}\left(\mathbf{\Sigma}\right)}{2},\text{ using }\rho\leq\frac{\gamma\lambda_{k}\left(\mathbf{\Sigma}\right)}{2}.

Hence, by applying the gap-free Wedin theorem (Lemma B.3,
[
AZL16
]
), we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathbf{S}^{\top}\widehat{\mathbf{L}}\right\rVert_{\textup{op}}\leq\frac{\rho}{(1-\gamma)\lambda_{k}(\widehat{\mathbf{\Sigma}})-(1-\Gamma)\lambda_{k}(\mathbf{\Sigma})}\leq\frac{2\rho}{\gamma\lambda_{k}(\mathbf{\Sigma})}.

(35)

Combining (
33
) and (
35
) yields the first claim, as desired:

\displaystyle\lVert\mathbf{S}^{\top}\mathbf{U}\rVert_{\textup{F}}^{2}\leq\frac{8k\rho^{2}}{\gamma^{2}\lambda_{k}(\mathbf{\Sigma})^{2}}+2\delta.

Finally, for the claim regarding
\kappa_{k}(\widehat{\bm{\Sigma}})
, we have by two applications of Fact
2
:

\displaystyle\kappa_{k}(\widehat{\bm{\Sigma}})

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda_{1}(\widehat{\bm{\Sigma}})}{\lambda_{k}(\widehat{\bm{\Sigma}})}\leq\frac{\lambda_{1}\left(\bm{\Sigma}\right)+\left|\lambda_{1}\left(\bm{\Sigma}\right)-\lambda_{1}(\widehat{\bm{\Sigma}})\right|}{\lambda_{k}\left(\bm{\Sigma}\right)-\left|\lambda_{k}\left(\bm{\Sigma}\right)-\lambda_{k}(\widehat{\bm{\Sigma}})\right|}\leq\frac{\lambda_{1}\left(\bm{\Sigma}\right)+\frac{\gamma\lambda_{k}\left(\mathbf{\Sigma}\right)}{2}}{\lambda_{k}\left(\bm{\Sigma}\right)-\frac{\gamma\lambda_{k}\left(\mathbf{\Sigma}\right)}{2}}\leq\frac{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}{1-\frac{\gamma}{2}}\kappa_{k}\left(\bm{\Sigma}\right)\leq 2\kappa_{k}(\bm{\Sigma}).

∎

\rho
for convenient application of Lemma
19
in the following.

Corollary 4
.

In the setting of Lemma
19
, if
\rho:=\sqrt{\frac{\delta}{8k}}\gamma\lambda_{k}\left(\bm{\Sigma}\right)
,
\mathbf{U}
is a
(3\delta,2\gamma)
-

-cPCA of
\mathbf{\Sigma}

Corollaries
2
and
4
make our roadmap clear for heavy-tailed PCA. We first use Corollary
2
to devise a clipping threshold for our data such that the clipped covariance matrix is close in operator norm to the original distribution’s covariance matrix. We then perform approximate
k
-cPCA on the clipped data, analyzed using our reduction in Theorem
2
, and finally use Corollary
4
to convert the resulting
k
-cPCA on the clipped covariance to a corresponding
k
-cPCA on the original covariance.

To demonstrate an instantiation of this strategy, we propose an online gap-free

-cPCA algorithm for heavy-tailed data using Oja’s algorithm
[

Proposition 4

(Theorem 2,
[
AZL17
]
))
.

There is an algorithm
\mathsf{Oja}
with the following guarantee. Let
\delta,\gamma\geq 0
such that
\max\left(\delta,\gamma\right)\leq\frac{1}{10}
and
\delta\leq\frac{\gamma^{2}}{256}

d\in\mathbb{N}
,

, and

Let

\{X_{i}\}_{i\in[n]}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

\mathcal{D}

\mathbb{R}^{d}

\mathbb{0}_{d}

\bm{\Sigma}

\left\lVert X\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq R

(\delta,\gamma)
-cPCA of

As a consequence of Proposition
4
and our roadmap described earlier, we conclude with the following.

Theorem 5
.

For an even integer
p\geq 4
, let
\mathcal{D}
be
(p,C_{p})
-hypercontractive on

with mean
\mathbb{0}_{d}

\bm{\Sigma}

(\Delta,\Gamma)\in(0,1)

\Delta\cdot\kappa_{k}(\bm{\Sigma})^{2}\leq\Gamma^{2}

,
\gamma=\frac{1}{\Theta(k^{3})}\cdot\Gamma
, for appropriate constants. Let

\alpha:=\left(\frac{C_{p}^{2}\kappa_{k}\left(\bm{\Sigma}\right)\sqrt{k}}{\Gamma\sqrt{\Delta}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-2}},\;R:=\Theta(\alpha\textup{Tr}\left(\bm{\Sigma}\right)).

Let

\mathsf{BlackBoxPCA}
(Algorithm
1

as a
(\delta,\gamma)
-

-cPCA oracle on

samples from

returns a
(\Delta,\Gamma)
-
k
-cPCA of
\bm{\Sigma}
with probability
\geq 1-\beta
, in
O(dk)
space.

Proof.

Let
\rho:=(\frac{\Delta}{24k})^{1/2}\frac{\Gamma}{\kappa_{k}\left(\bm{\Sigma}\right)}
. Using Corollary
4

, the covariance of the clipped distribution

Next, we obtain a

-cPCA,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\bm{\Sigma}^{\prime}-\bm{\Sigma}\right\rVert_{\textup{op}}\leq\rho\left\lVert\bm{\Sigma}\right\rVert_{\textup{op}}\leq 2\sqrt{\frac{\Delta}{24k}}\Gamma\lambda_{k}\left(\bm{\Sigma}\right).

(36)

The proof follows by substituting the definition of

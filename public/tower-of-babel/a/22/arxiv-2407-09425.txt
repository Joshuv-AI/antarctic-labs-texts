Non-potential systems with relativistic operators and maximal monotone boundary conditions
Petru JEBELEAN, Călin ŞERBAN, [0.10ex] Institute for Advanced Environmental Research, Department of Mathematics West University of Timişoara,Blvd. V. Pârvan, no. 4, 300223 - Timişoara, Romania

Abstract
We are concerned with solvability of a non-potential system involving two relativistic operators, subject to boundary conditions expressed in terms of maximal monotone operators. The approach makes use of a fixed point formulation and relies on a priori estimates and convergent to zero matrices.



Mathematics Subject Classification: 34B15, 34L30, 47H05, 47N20

Keywords and phrases: relativistic operator systems; maximal monotone operator; fixed point; a priori estimates; convergent to zero matrix

Introduction

We deal with solvability of a system of type

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\left[\phi_{n}(u^{\prime})\right]^{\prime}=f_{1}(t,u,v)\\
-\left[\phi_{m}(v^{\prime})\right]^{\prime}=f_{2}(t,u,v)\end{array}\quad\mbox{ in }[0,T],\right.

(1.1)

associated with the multivalued boundary conditions

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\left(\phi_{n}\left(u^{\prime}\right)(0),-\phi_{n}\left(u^{\prime}\right)(T)\right)\in\gamma(u(0),u(T))\\
\left(\phi_{m}\left(v^{\prime}\right)(0),-\phi_{m}\left(v^{\prime}\right)(T)\right)\in\eta(v(0),v(T))\end{array},\right.

(1.2)

where, for
q\in\{n,m\}
, the homeomorphism
\phi_{q}:\mathbb{B}_{1}(q)\to\mathbb{R}^{q}
is given by

\displaystyle\phi_{q}(y)=\frac{y}{\sqrt{1-|y|^{2}}}\quad(y\in\mathbb{B}_{1}(q));

here and hereafter,
\mathbb{B}_{r}(q)
denotes the open ball of radius
r
in
\mathbb{R}^{q}
endowed with the Euclidean norm
|\cdot|
corresponding to the usual inner product
\langle\cdot|\cdot\rangle
. Also, on the product space
\mathbb{R}^{q}\times\mathbb{R}^{q}
we consider the usual inner product
\langle\!\langle\cdot|\cdot\rangle\!\rangle
.
The operators
\gamma:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to 2^{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}
and
\eta:\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to 2^{\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}}
are maximal monotone, with
0_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\in\gamma(0_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
,
0_{\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}}\in\eta(0_{\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}})
and the mappings
f_{1}:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
f_{2}:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
are continuous.
The notations
D(\gamma)
,
D(\eta)
will stand for the domain of
\gamma
and
\eta
, respectively (recall, this means
D(\gamma)=\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,\gamma(z)\neq\emptyset\}
; similar definition for
D(\eta)
) and
we denote
C_{q}^{1}:=C^{1}(\left[0,T\right];\mathbb{R}^{q})
(
q\in\{n,m\}
).

By
a solution
of problem (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) we mean a couple of functions
(u,v)\in C_{n}^{1}\times C_{m}^{1}
with
|u^{\prime}(t)|<1
,
|v^{\prime}(t)|<1
for all
t\in[0,T]
, such that
\phi_{n}(u^{\prime})\in C_{n}^{1}
,
\phi_{m}(v^{\prime})\in C_{m}^{1}
,
(u(0),u(T))\in D\left(\gamma\right)
,
(v(0),v(T))\in D\left(\eta\right)
and which satisfies (
1.1
) and (
1.2
).

In recent years a special attention was paid to various qualitative aspects for boundary value problems involving the
relativistic operator
:
u\mapsto\left[\phi_{q}(u^{\prime})\right]^{\prime}
, which is known that occurs in the dynamics of special relativity. Mainly, the results obtained for this operator use both topological techniques as well as variational methods. Thus, among others and far from being exhausted, related to existence and multiplicity of solutions of nonlinear systems or scalar equations subject to classical boundary conditions, such as Dirichlet, Neumann or periodic, under various assumptions on the perturbing nonlinearity, we refer the reader to e.g.
[
1
]
-
[
6
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
13
]
,
[
14
]
and the references therein.
This work is mainly motivated by paper
[
8
]
, which is concerned with a system having the form

-\left[\phi_{n}(u^{\prime})\right]^{\prime}=\nabla_{u}F(t,u)\quad\mbox{in }[0,T],

(1.3)

subject to the boundary condition

\left(\phi_{n}\left(u^{\prime}\right)(0),-\phi_{n}\left(u^{\prime}\right)(T)\right)\in\partial j(u(0),u(T)),

(1.4)

under the assumptions that
j:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to(-\infty,+\infty]
is a proper lower semicontinuous convex function with
j(0_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})=0
and
0_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\in\partial j(0_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
; here,
\partial j
stands for the subdifferential of
j
in the sense of convex analysis
[
20
]
. The mapping
F=F(t,u):[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is supposed to be continuous, with
\nabla_{u}F
continuous on the set
[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
F(\,\cdot\,,0_{\mathbb{R}^{n}})=0.
Notice that by the lower semicontinuity assumption on the proper convex function
j
, the multivalued operator
\partial j:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow 2^{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}
is maximal monotone
[
19
]
. It is known (see e.g.
[
7
]
-
[
9
]
,
[
15
]
and the references therein) that different choices of
j
yield various boundary conditions, including the classical ones (Dirichlet, Neumann, periodic, antiperiodic, etc.) as well as other ones of special interest.

Observe that problem (
1.3
)-(
1.4
) is entirely of potential type, both through the system and through the boundary condition. As shown in
[
8
]
, this allows a variational formulation in the frame of critical point theory for convex, lower semicontinuous perturbations of
C^{1}
-functionals. Then, taking the advantage of this key feature, therein one obtains the existence of minimum energy as well as saddle-point solutions of the problem. It is worth to notice that, as seen by concrete examples of applications, the generality of (
1.3
)-(
1.4
) is quite broad. However, it is clear that firstly the potentiality of the system (
1.3
) and secondly of the boundary condition (
1.4
) restrict the applicability of the results from
[
8
]
. In this view, it appears as being natural to consider a non-potential system

-\left[\phi_{n}(u^{\prime})\right]^{\prime}=f(t,u)\quad\mbox{in }[0,T],

(1.5)

associated with the non-potential boundary condition

\left(\phi_{n}\left(u^{\prime}\right)(0),-\phi_{n}\left(u^{\prime}\right)(T)\right)\in\gamma(u(0),u(T)),

(1.6)

where the maximal monotone operator
\gamma
is as above and
f:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is continuous. For the sake of clarity of the fact that (
1.6
) is really more general than (
1.4
), we give here a simple example of a boundary condition which is expressed by a maximal monotone operator that is not of subdifferential type. In this respect, let
A
be a real (
2n\times 2n
)-matrix which is positive semi-definite (i.e.
\langle\!\langle Az\,|\,z\rangle\!\rangle\geq 0
, for all
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
) and

\gamma_{A}(z)=A\,z\qquad(z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}).

(1.7)

Then
\gamma_{A}
is maximal monotone
[
17
, Example 1.5 (b)]
. If
A
is not symmetric, then
\gamma_{A}
will not be cyclically monotone (see p. 240 in
[
20
]
) and thus, by a classical result on the potentiality of maximal monotone operators
[
19
]
, there is no lower semicontinuous proper convex function
j:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to(-\infty,+\infty]
such that
\gamma_{A}=\partial j
. Summarizing, if
A
is positive semi-definite, but it is not symmetric, the boundary condition

\left(\begin{array}[]{clrc}\phi_{n}\left(u^{\prime}\right)(0)\\[6.0pt]
-\phi_{n}\left(u^{\prime}\right)(T)\end{array}\right)=A\ \left(\begin{array}[]{clrc}u(0)\\[6.0pt]
u(T)\end{array}\right)

(1.8)

is of type (
1.6
), with
\gamma
which is not the subdifferential of a proper lower semicontinuous convex function
j
.

Coming back to system (
1.1
)-(
1.2
), let us note that it contains as a particular case problem (
1.5
)-(
1.6
). Indeed, given be
f
and
\gamma
, this can be seen by taking
m=n
,
\eta=\gamma
,
f_{1}(t,u,v)=f(t,u)
and
f_{2}(t,u,v)=f(t,v)
. Therefore, a qualitative result about (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) can be easily transposed to problem (
1.5
)-(
1.6
) (see Corollaries
3.1
and
3.2
). On the other hand, our main result (Theorem
3.1
) is of type of
[
11
, Theorem 2.1]
which is concerned with classical vector
p
and
q
-Laplacian operators. In this view, the brought novelties consist in the fact that the differential operators in (
1.1
) are singular and the boundary conditions (
1.2
) are of non-potential type. As we will see, both technically as well as structurally these bring specific approaches and features, even if broadly speaking, it is about a similar strategy to the one in
[
11
]
- this makes use of a priori estimates combined with convergent to zero matrices. We mention that unlike the refered classical case, for system (
1.1
) subject to a whole class of boundary conditions of type (
1.2
), we obtain an ”universal” existence result, meaning that no additional assumptions must be imposed on the nonlinearities
f_{1}
,
f_{2}
to ensure the existence of the solution (see Theorem
3.1

(i)
). Another notable aspect is that various choices of operators
\gamma
and
\eta
in (
1.2
) allow the coupling of boundary conditions of different types.

). Then, in Section 3 we present the main result concerning solvability of (
1.1
)-(
1.2

1.5

2
A fixed point approach

Let
q\in\{n,m\}
. By
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
we denote the sup-norm on
C_{q}:=C([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{q})
and the usual norm on
L^{r}:=L^{r}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{q})
will be denoted by
\|\cdot\|_{L^{r}}
(
1\leq r\leq\infty
). The Sobolev space
H^{1}_{q}:=W^{1,2}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{q})
will be considered with the norm

\|w\|_{H^{1}_{q}}=\left(\|w^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

The product spaces
\mathcal{C}:=C_{n}\times C_{m}
and
\mathcal{H}:=H^{1}_{n}\times H^{1}_{m}
will be considered with the usual product topology; we choose to see
\mathcal{H}
as being endowed with the norm

\|(u,v)\|_{\mathcal{H}}=\|u\|_{H^{1}_{n}}+\|v\|_{H^{1}_{m}}\quad((u,v)\in\mathcal{H}).

According to
[
8
, Theorem 2.8]
, we have that for any
h\in C_{n}
, the system

-\left[\phi_{n}(u^{\prime})\right]^{\prime}+u=h(t)\quad\mbox{in }[0,T],

(2.1)

has an unique solution satisfying the boundary condition (
1.6
).
This enables us to define the solution operator

S_{\gamma}:C_{n}\to H^{1}_{n}
by

S_{\gamma}(h):=\mbox{the unique solution of \eqref{eqpb2}-\eqref{bcsyspnew1}}\,(h\in C_{n}).

In a similar way we introduce
S_{\eta}:C_{m}\to H^{1}_{m}
by
S_{\eta}(l):=\mbox{the unique solution of}
problem

-\left[\phi_{m}(v^{\prime})\right]^{\prime}+v=l(t)\ \ \mbox{in }[0,T],\quad\left(\phi_{m}\left(v^{\prime}\right)(0),-\phi_{m}\left(v^{\prime}\right)(T)\right)\in\eta(v(0),v(T))\qquad(l\in C_{m}).

Proposition 2.1

The operators
S_{\gamma}
and
S_{\eta}
are continuous.

Proof.
We refer to
S_{\gamma}
; similar argument for
S_{\eta}
. Let
\{h_{k}\}\subset C_{n}
be a sequence such that
h_{k}\to h\in C_{n}
as
k\to\infty
, and set
u:=S_{\gamma}(h)
,
u_{k}:=S_{\gamma}(h_{k})
(
k\in\mathbb{N}
). As
\gamma
is (maximal) monotone, by virtue of (
1.6
) one has

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\langle\!\langle\left(\phi_{n}\left(u_{k}^{\prime}\right)(0),-\phi_{n}\left(u_{k}^{\prime}\right)(T)\right)-\left(\phi_{n}\left(u^{\prime}\right)(0),-\phi_{n}\left(u^{\prime}\right)(T)\right)|\left(u_{k}(0),u_{k}(T)\right)-\left(u(0),u(T)\right)\rangle\!\rangle

(2.2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\!\langle\left(\phi_{n}\left(u_{k}^{\prime}\right)(0)-\phi_{n}\left(u^{\prime}\right)(0),-\phi_{n}\left(u_{k}^{\prime}\right)(T)+\phi_{n}\left(u^{\prime}\right)(T)\right)|\left(u_{k}(0)-u(0),u_{k}(T)-u(T)\right)\rangle\!\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\phi_{n}\left(u_{k}^{\prime}\right)(0)-\phi_{n}\left(u^{\prime}\right)(0)|u_{k}(0)-u(0)\rangle-\langle\phi_{n}\left(u_{k}^{\prime}\right)(T)-\phi_{n}\left(u^{\prime}\right)(T)|u_{k}(T)-u(T)\rangle.

On the other hand, multiplying the equality

-\left[\phi_{n}(u_{k}^{\prime})-\phi_{n}(u^{\prime})\right]^{\prime}+u_{k}-u=h_{k}(t)-h(t)

by
u_{k}-u
, integrating over
[0,T]
and using the integration by parts formula, we derive

\langle\phi_{n}\left(u_{k}^{\prime}\right)(0)-\phi_{n}\left(u^{\prime}\right)(0)|u_{k}(0)-u(0)\rangle-\langle\phi_{n}\left(u_{k}^{\prime}\right)(T)-\phi_{n}\left(u^{\prime}\right)(T)|u_{k}(T)-u(T)\rangle+

+\int_{0}^{T}\langle\phi_{n}\left(u_{k}^{\prime}\right)-\phi_{n}\left(u^{\prime}\right)|u_{k}^{\prime}-u^{\prime}\rangle+\|u_{k}-u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\int_{0}^{T}\langle h_{k}(t)-h(t)|u_{k}-u\rangle

and from (
2.2
), one obtains

\int_{0}^{T}\langle\phi_{n}\left(u_{k}^{\prime}\right)-\phi_{n}\left(u^{\prime}\right)|u_{k}^{\prime}-u^{\prime}\rangle+\|u_{k}-u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\sqrt{T}\|h_{k}-h\|_{\infty}\|u_{k}-u\|_{L^{2}}.

Then, since
\phi_{n}
satisfies
[
10
, Lemma 2.1]
:

\langle\phi_{n}(x)-\phi_{n}(y)|x-y\rangle\geq|x-y|^{2}\quad(x,y\in\mathbb{B}_{1}(n)),

we get that
\{\|u_{k}-u\|_{L^{2}}\}
is bounded and

\|u_{k}-u\|_{H^{1}_{n}}^{2}\leq\sqrt{T}\|h_{k}-h\|_{\infty}\|u_{k}-u\|_{L^{2}},

which, as
h_{k}\to h
in
C_{n}
, yields that
u_{k}\to u
in
H^{1}_{n}
and the continuity of
S_{\gamma}
is proved.

Next, we introduce the mappings
g_{1}:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
g_{2}:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
by

g_{1}(t,x,y)=f_{1}(t,x,y)+x,\ \quad g_{2}(t,x,y)=f_{2}(t,x,y)+y\quad\,(t\in[0,T],\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\ y\in\mathbb{R}^{m})

and define the
Nemytskii operators

N_{g_{1}}:\mathcal{C}\to C_{n}
and
N_{g_{2}}:\mathcal{C}\to C_{m}
by

N_{g_{1}}(u,v)(t)=g_{1}(t,u(t),v(t)),\ \quad N_{g_{2}}(u,v)(t)=g_{2}(t,u(t),v(t))\quad\,(t\in[0,T],\ (u,v)\in\mathcal{C}).

It is well known that
N_{g_{1}}
,
N_{g_{2}}
are continuous and bounded (i.e., takes bounded sets from
\mathcal{C}
into bounded sets in
C_{q}
(
q\in\{n,m\}
)). Then, let
Q_{\gamma}:\mathcal{H}\to H^{1}_{n}
and
Q_{\eta}:\mathcal{H}\to H^{1}_{m}
be given by

Q_{\gamma}=S_{\gamma}\circ N_{g_{1}}\circ i_{d},\quad Q_{\eta}=S_{\eta}\circ N_{g_{2}}\circ i_{d},

(2.3)

where
i_{d}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{C}
is the identity map.

Proposition 2.2

(i)
The operator
Q=(Q_{\gamma},Q_{\eta}):\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
is compact.

(ii)
A couple of functions
(u,v)\in\mathcal{H}
is a solution of problem (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) iff it is a fixed point of
Q
.

Proof.
Statement
(i)
follows from Proposition
2.1
, the continuity of
N_{g_{1}}
,
N_{g_{2}}
and the compactness of
i_{d}
, while
(ii)
is straightforward.

Before concluding this section, we note for reader convenience some simple characterizations of the convergent to zero matrices, which will be needed in the sequel. Recall, a square matrix
M
is said to be
convergent to zero
if
M^{k}\to 0
as
k\to\infty
. The following result is proved in
[
18
, Lemma 2]
.

Lemma 2.1

Let
M
be a square matrix of non-negative numbers. The following
statements are equivalent:

(
i
)

M
is a convergent to zero matrix;

(
ii
)

I-M
is non-singular and

(I-M)^{-1}=I+M+M^{2}+\ldots;

(
iii
)

|\lambda|<1
for every
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
with
\det(M-\lambda I)=0
;

(
iv
)

I-M
is non-singular and
(I-M)^{-1}
has non-negative elements.

3
Main result

We denote

\mathcal{K}(q):=\left\{w\in W^{1,\infty}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{q})\,:\,\|w^{\prime}\|_{{}_{L^{\infty}}}\leq 1\right\}\quad(q\in\{n,m\})

and define the first eigenvalue-like constants:

\lambda_{1}(\gamma):=\inf\left\{\frac{\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}{\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}\,:\,u\in\mathcal{K}(n)\setminus\{0_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\},\,(u(0),u(T))\in D(\gamma)\right\},

(3.1)

\lambda_{1}(\eta):=\inf\left\{\frac{\|v^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}{\|v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}\,:\,v\in\mathcal{K}(m)\setminus\{0_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\},\,(v(0),v(T))\in D(\eta)\right\}.

(3.2)

The following main result provides sufficient conditions which ensures the solvability of the boundary value problem (
1.1
)-(
1.2
).

Theorem 3.1

Assume that one of the following four conditions is satisfied:

(i)

\lambda_{1}(\gamma)>0
,
\lambda_{1}(\eta)>0;

(ii)

\lambda_{1}(\eta)>0
and there are constants
a\in[0,1),b,\delta\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that

\langle f_{1}(t,x,y)\,|\,x\rangle\leq(a-1)|x|^{2}+b|y|^{2}+\delta\quad(t\in[0,T],\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\ y\in\mathbb{R}^{m});

(3.3)

(iii)

\lambda_{1}(\gamma)>0
and there are constants
c,\delta\in\mathbb{R}_{+},d\in[0,1)
such that

\langle f_{2}(t,x,y)\,|\,y\rangle\leq c|x|^{2}+(d-1)|y|^{2}+\delta\quad(t\in[0,T],\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\ y\in\mathbb{R}^{m});

(3.4)

(iv)
there are constants
a,b,c,d,\delta\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that the matrix

M:=\left[\begin{array}[]{clrc}a&b\\
c&d\end{array}\right]

(3.5)

is convergent to zero and

\begin{array}[]{ll}\langle f_{1}(t,x,y)\,|\,x\rangle\leq(a-1)|x|^{2}+b|y|^{2}+\delta\\
\langle f_{2}(t,x,y)\,|\,y\rangle\leq c|x|^{2}+(d-1)|y|^{2}+\delta\end{array}\quad(t\in[0,T],\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\ y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}).

(3.6)

Then problem (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) has at least one solution.

Proof.
On account of Proposition
2.2

(ii)
, it suffices to show that under any of the assumptions
(i)-(iv)
, the operator
Q
has a fixed point. In this view, according to Schaefer’s theorem (see e.g.
[
16
, Corollary 4.4.12]
), it suffices to prove that the set

\{(u,v)\in\mathcal{H}:\ \exists\ \lambda\in(0,1]\mbox{ such that }(u,v)=\lambda Q(u,v)\}

is bounded in
(\mathcal{H},\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}})
.

Let
(u,v)\in\mathcal{H}
be such that
Q(u,v)=\lambda^{-1}(u,v)
, with some
\lambda\in(0,1]
. From (
2.3
) and definition of operators
S_{\gamma}
and
S_{\eta}
, this means that
|\lambda^{-1}u^{\prime}(t)|<1
,
|\lambda^{-1}v^{\prime}(t)|<1
for all
t\in[0,T]
,
(\lambda^{-1}u(0),\lambda^{-1}u(T))\in D\left(\gamma\right)
,
(\lambda^{-1}v(0),\lambda^{-1}v(T))\in D\left(\eta\right)
,

-\left[\phi_{n}\left(\lambda^{-1}u^{\prime}\right)\right]^{\prime}+\lambda^{-1}u=g_{1}(t,u,v)\quad\,(t\in[0,T]),

(3.7)

-\left[\phi_{m}\left(\lambda^{-1}v^{\prime}\right)\right]^{\prime}+\lambda^{-1}v=g_{2}(t,u,v)\quad\,(t\in[0,T])

(3.8)

and

\left(\phi_{n}\left(\lambda^{-1}u^{\prime}\right)(0),-\phi_{n}\left(\lambda^{-1}u^{\prime}\right)(T)\right)\in\gamma(\lambda^{-1}u(0),\lambda^{-1}u(T)),

(3.9)

\left(\phi_{m}\left(\lambda^{-1}v^{\prime}\right)(0),-\phi_{m}\left(\lambda^{-1}v^{\prime}\right)(T)\right)\in\eta(\lambda^{-1}v(0),\lambda^{-1}v(T)).

(3.10)

(i)
From (
3.1
) and (
3.2
), we have that

\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}{\lambda_{1}(\gamma)}<\frac{T}{\lambda_{1}(\gamma)}\ \mbox{ and }\ \|v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{\|v^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}{\lambda_{1}(\eta)}<\frac{T}{\lambda_{1}(\eta)},

which gives
\|(u,v)\|_{\mathcal{H}}<\left(T+T/\lambda_{1}(\gamma)\right)^{1/2}+\left(T+T/\lambda_{1}(\eta)\right)^{1/2}
.

(ii)
Multiplying (
3.7
) by
\lambda^{-1}u
, then integrating over
[0,T]
and using integration by parts formula, we infer

\left\langle\phi_{n}\left(\lambda^{-1}u^{\prime}\right)(0)|\lambda^{-1}u(0)\right\rangle-\left\langle\phi_{n}\left(\lambda^{-1}u^{\prime}\right)(T)|\lambda^{-1}u(T)\right\rangle+

+\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle\phi_{n}\left(\lambda^{-1}u^{\prime}\right)|\lambda^{-1}u^{\prime}\right\rangle+\lambda^{-2}\int_{0}^{T}|u|^{2}=\lambda^{-1}\int_{0}^{T}\langle g_{1}(t,u,v)|u\rangle.

(3.11)

Using that
0_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\in\gamma(0_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
, the monotonicity of
\gamma
and (
3.9
), one has

\left\langle\phi_{n}\left(\lambda^{-1}u^{\prime}\right)(0)|\lambda^{-1}u(0)\right\rangle-\left\langle\phi_{n}\left(\lambda^{-1}u^{\prime}\right)(T)|\lambda^{-1}u(T)\right\rangle\geq 0,

which, on account of
\langle\phi_{n}(y)|y\rangle\geq|y|^{2}
(
y\in\mathbb{B}_{1}(n)
) and (
3.11
), yields

\|u\|_{H^{1}_{n}}^{2}\leq\lambda\int_{0}^{T}\langle g_{1}(t,u,v)|u\rangle=\lambda\int_{0}^{T}\langle f_{1}(t,u,v)|u\rangle+\lambda\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(3.12)

Then, from (
3.2
), since
\lambda_{1}(\eta)>0
, one has
\|v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}<T/\lambda_{1}(\eta)
and by virtue of (
3.3
) and (

), we get

\|u\|_{H^{1}_{n}}^{2}\leq a\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+b\|v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\delta T<a\|u\|_{H^{1}_{n}}^{2}+k_{1},

with
k_{1}=k_{1}(\eta):=(b/\lambda_{1}(\eta)+\delta)T
. Hence,

\|(u,v)\|_{\mathcal{H}}<\left(k_{1}/(1-a)\right)^{1/2}+\left(T+T/\lambda_{1}(\eta)\right)^{1/2}
.

(iii)
Similarly to
(ii)
, using (
3.8
), (
3.10
) and the monotonicity of
\eta
, we obtain

\|v\|_{H^{1}_{m}}^{2}\leq\lambda\int_{0}^{T}\langle g_{2}(t,u,v)|v\rangle\leq\lambda\int_{0}^{T}\langle f_{2}(t,u,v)|v\rangle+\lambda\|v\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

(3.13)

which, together with (
3.1
) and (
3.4
), imply
\|(u,v)\|_{\mathcal{H}}<\left(T+T/\lambda_{1}(\gamma)\right)^{1/2}+\left(k_{2}/(1-d)\right)^{1/2},

where
k_{2}=k_{2}(\gamma):=(c/\lambda_{1}(\gamma)+\delta)T
.

(iv)
From (
3.6
), (
3.12
) and (
3.13
), we derive

\|u\|_{H^{1}_{n}}^{2}\leq a\|u\|_{H^{1}_{n}}^{2}+b\|v\|_{H^{1}_{m}}^{2}+\delta T\ \mbox{ and }\ \|v\|_{H^{1}_{m}}^{2}\leq c\|u\|_{H^{1}_{n}}^{2}+d\|v\|_{H^{1}_{m}}^{2}+\delta T,

which can be written in the following vector form:

(I-M)\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\|u\|_{H^{1}_{n}}^{2}\\[6.0pt]
\|v\|_{H^{1}_{m}}^{2}\end{array}\right]\leq\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\delta T\\[6.0pt]
\delta T\end{array}\right].

(3.14)

Then, since matrix
M
is convergent to zero, Lemma
2.1
guarantees that
I-M
is invertible and the elements of
(I-M)^{-1}
are all non-negative. Therefore, we may multiply (

) by
(I-M)^{-1}
without changing the sense of the inequality and so, we get

\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\|u\|_{H^{1}_{n}}^{2}\\[6.0pt]
\|v\|_{H^{1}_{m}}^{2}\end{array}\right]\leq(I-M)^{-1}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\delta T\\[6.0pt]
\delta T\end{array}\right],

which shows that
\|(u,v)\|_{\mathcal{H}}
is bounded by a constant depending on

and independent of
\lambda\in(0,1]
.

Remark 3.1

Denote by

1.
Introduction

Throughout this paper, let
X
be a nonempty set, let
\mathcal{P}=\{X_{i}\colon i\in I\}
be a partition of
X
, and let
E
be the equivalence relation on
X
induced by
\mathcal{P}
. Denote by
T(X)
the full transformation semigroup on
X
. The semigroup
T(X)
and its subsemigroups have a crucial place in the theory of semigroups, since every semigroup is isomorphic to a subsemigroup of
T(Z)
for some nonempty set
Z

[
12
, Theorem 1.1.2]
. In 1994, Pei
[
14
]
introduced the following subsemigroup of
T(X)
.

\displaystyle T(X,\mathcal{P})

\displaystyle=\{f\in T(X)\colon\forall X_{i}\in\mathcal{P}~\exists X_{j}\in\mathcal{P},~X_{i}f\subseteq X_{j}\}

\displaystyle=\left\{f\in T(X)\colon\forall x,y\in X,~(x,y)\in E\Longrightarrow(xf,yf)\in E\right\}.

In that paper, Pei
[
14
, Theorem 2.8]
proved that
T(X,\mathcal{P})
is the semigroup of all continuous selfmaps on
X
endowed with the topology having
\mathcal{P}
as a basis. Since then the semigroup
T(X,\mathcal{P})
has been extensively studied in the literature; see, for example,
[
1
,
2
,
15
,
16
,
17
,
22
,
25
,
26
,
27
]
.

Let
f\in T(X,\mathcal{P})
. The
character
of
f
is the transformation
\chi^{(f)}\colon I\to I
defined by
i\chi^{(f)}=j
whenever
X_{i}f\subseteq X_{j}
. If
X
is finite, the character of
f\in T(X,\mathcal{P})
has been studied using the symbol
\bar{f}
by Araújo et al.
[
1
]
, Dolinka and East
[
8
]
, and Dolinka et al.
[
9
]
. The character of
f\in T(X,\mathcal{P})
for an arbitrary set
X
was first studied by Purisang and Rakbud
[
19
]
.
Let
\mathbb{S}(I)
be a subsemigroup of
T(I)
. By using the notion of character, Rakbud
[
20
]
introduced the following subsemigroup of
T(X,\mathcal{P})
:

T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})=\left\{f\in T(X,\mathcal{P})\colon\chi^{(f)}\in\mathbb{S}(I)\right\}.

In
[
21
]
, Rakbud and Chaiya studied the semigroup
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
. Semigroup
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
generalizes
the semigroup
T(X,\mathcal{P})
, since
T(X,\mathcal{P})=T_{T(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
. The semigroup
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
also generalizes both
T(X)
and
\mathbb{S}(I)
in the sense that
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})=T(X)
if
|\mathcal{P}|=1
, and
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})\simeq\mathbb{S}(I)
if
\mathcal{P}
consists of singleton sets. (Note that if
\mathcal{P}
consists of singleton sets, then
|I|=|X|
and
\mathbb{S}(I)\simeq\mathbb{S}(X)
.) For various proper subsemigroups
\mathbb{S}(I)
of
T(I)
, the semigroup
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
has been investigated; see
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
19
,
23
,
24
,
25
,
26
,
27
,
28
]
.

The purpose of this paper is to study the semigroup
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
, where
\mathbb{S}(I)
is a general subsemigroup of
T(I)
. The paper is organized as follows. In the next section, we present the definitions and notation
used throughout this paper. In Section 3, we describe regular and idempotent elements in
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
. We next determine when
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
is a regular semigroup, and when
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
is an inverse semigroup. Moreover, we prove that
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
is a regular semigroup if
\mathbb{S}(I)
is a subgroup of the symmetric group on
I
, and thus generalize the result given by Purisang and Rakbud in
[
19
, Theorem 3.5(1)]
. With the assumption that
\mathbb{S}(I)
contains the identity, we describe unit-regular elements in
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
, and determine when
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
is a unit-regular semigroup in Section 4. In Section 5, we characterize Green’s relations on
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
when
\mathbb{S}(I)
contains the identity. Using these characterizations, we also obtain concrete and noticeably different descriptions of Green’s relations on
T(X,\mathcal{P})
, whose Green’s relations are known; see
[
17
,
18
]
.

2.
Preliminaries and Notation

Let
X
be a nonempty set. The cardinality of
X
is denoted by
|X|
. For any nonempty set
Y
, we write
X\setminus Y=\{x\in X\colon x\notin Y\}
.
A
partition

\mathcal{P}
of
X
is a collection of nonempty disjoint subsets of
X
, called
blocks
of
\mathcal{P}
, whose union is
X
. A
trivial partition
is a partition that contains only singleton blocks or a single block. A
transversal
of an equivalence relation
\rho
on
X
is a subset of
X
that contains exactly one element from every
\rho
-class. We denote the identity mapping on
X
by
\id_{X}
, and the symmetric group on
X
by
\Sym(X)
.

Denote the composition of mappings by juxtaposition, and compose mappings from left to right. Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a mapping. We write
xf
for the image of
x
under
f
. For a nonempty
A\subseteq X
(resp.
B\subseteq Y
), we put
Af=\{af\colon a\in A\}
(resp.
Bf^{-1}=\{x\in X\colon xf\in B\}
). Furthermore, if
B=\{b\}
, then we write
bf^{-1}
instead of
\{b\}f^{-1}
.
We let
D(f)=Y\setminus Xf
and
\textnormal{d}(f)=|D(f)|
. The
kernel
of
f
, denoted by
\ker(f)
, is an equivalence relation on
X
defined by
\ker(f)=\{(a,b)\in X\times X\colon af=bf\}
. The symbol
\pi(f)
denotes the partition of
X
induced by
\ker(f)
, and
T_{f}
denotes a transversal of
\ker(f)
. Note that
|X\setminus T_{f}|
is independent of the choice of transversal of
\ker(f)
(cf.
[
11
, p. 1356]
). We let
\textnormal{c}(f)=|X\setminus T_{f}|
.

Let
f\in T(X)
. For a nonempty subset
A
of the domain of
f
, the
restriction
of
f
to
A
is the mapping
f_{\upharpoonright A}\colon A\to X
defined by
x(f_{\upharpoonright A})=xf
for all
x\in A
. Moreover if
B
is a subset of the codomain of
f
such that
Af\subseteq B
, then we use the same notation
f_{\upharpoonright A}
for the mapping from
A
to
B
that assigns
xf
to each
x\in A
. We say that
f

preserves
or
stabilizes
the partition
\mathcal{P}
if for each
X_{j}\in\mathcal{P}
, there exists
X_{k}\in\mathcal{P}
such that
X_{j}f\subseteq X_{k}
. Thus
T(X,\mathcal{P})
is a subsemigroup of
T(X)
consisting of all transformations that preserve the partition
\mathcal{P}
.

Let
S
be a semigroup. An element
a\in S
is an
idempotent
if
a^{2}=a
. The set of all idempotents of
S
is denoted by
E(S)
. It is well-known that
f\in T(X)
is an idempotent if and only if
f
acts as the identity map on its image set (cf.
[
3
, p. 6]
). An element
a\in S
is
regular
in
S
if there is
b\in S
such that
aba=a
. Such element
b
, if exists, is called an
inner inverse
of
a
. The set of all inner inverses of
a
is denoted by
\Pre(a)
, and the set of all regular elements in
S
is denoted by
\reg(S)
. If
\reg(S)=S
, then we say that
S
is
regular
. The semigroup
S
is an
inverse
semigroup if for every
x\in S
, there is a unique
y\in S
such that
xyx=x
and
yxy=y
. Equivalently, the semigroup
S
is an inverse semigroup if and only if
S
is regular and its idempotents commute (cf.
[
12
, Theorem 5.1.1]
).

Let
S
be a semigroup with identity. Denote by
U(S)
the set of all units in
S
. Note that
U(T(X))=\Sym(X)
, and write
U\big(T(X,\mathcal{P})\big)=S(X,\mathcal{P})
. Clearly
S(X,\mathcal{P})=T(X,\mathcal{P})\cap\Sym(X)
. An element
a\in S
is
unit-regular
in
S
if there is
u\in U(S)
such that
aua=a
. The set of all unit-regular elements in
S
is denoted by
\ureg(S)
. If
\ureg(S)=S
, then we say that
S
is
unit-regular
.

In what follows, the letter
I
denotes the index set of the partition
\mathcal{P}
, and the symbol
\mathbb{S}(I)
denotes a subsemigroup of
T(I)
. We refer the reader to
[
12
]
for any undefined notation and terminology of semigroup theory.

3.
Regular and Inverse semigroups
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})

In this section, we give a characterization of regular elements in
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
and determine when
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
is a regular semigroup. Using these, we obtain known descriptions of regular elements in
T(X,\mathcal{P})
and regularity of
T(X,\mathcal{P})
. If
\mathbb{S}(I)
is a subgroup of
\Sym(I)
, we prove that
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
is regular, and thus generalize Theorem 3.5(1) in
[
19
]
. Next, we give a characterization of idempotents in
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
and then determine when
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
is an inverse semigroup.

We begin by recalling a specific case of Lemma 2.3 in
[
19
]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

[
19
, Lemma 2.3]

We have
\chi^{(fg)}=\chi^{(f)}\chi^{(g)}
for all
f,g\in T(X,\mathcal{P})
.

In the next result, we observe that
\mathbb{S}(I)
is a homomorphic image of
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
.

Proposition 3.2
.

The mapping
\varphi\colon T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})\to\mathbb{S}(I)
defined by
f\varphi=\chi^{(f)}
is an epimorphism. Moreover, if
\id_{X}\in T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
, then
\id_{X}\varphi=\id_{I}
.

Proof
.

Note from Lemma
3.1
that
\chi^{(fg)}=\chi^{(f)}\chi^{(g)}
for all
f,g\in T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
. Then we see that
\varphi\colon T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})\to\mathbb{S}(I)
is a homomorphism, since

(fg)\varphi=\chi^{(fg)}=\chi^{(f)}\chi^{(g)}=(f\varphi)(g\varphi)

for all
f,g\in T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
. It remains to prove that
\varphi
is surjective. For this, let
\alpha\in\mathbb{S}(I)
. Fix
x_{i}\in X_{i}
for each
i\in I
, and define
h\in T(X)
by
xh=x_{i\alpha}
whenever
x\in X_{i}
for some
i\in I
. Clearly
X_{i}h\subseteq X_{i\alpha}
, and so
\chi^{(h)}=\alpha
. This gives
h\in T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
. We also obtain
h\varphi=\chi^{(h)}=\alpha
as required. If
\id_{X}\in T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
, then we get
\id_{X}\varphi=\chi^{(\id_{X})}=\id_{I}
. ∎

The following result gives a characterization of regular elements in
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
f\in T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
. Then
f\in\reg\big(T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})\big)
if and only if there exists
\alpha\in\mathbb{S}(I)
such that:

(i)

\chi^{(f)}\alpha\chi^{(f)}=\chi^{(f)}
;

(ii)

X_{i}\cap Xf\subseteq X_{i\alpha}f
for all
i\in I\chi^{(f)}
.

Proof
.

Assume that
f\in\reg\big(T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})\big)
. Then there exists
g\in T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
such that
fgf=f
. This yields
\chi^{(f)}\chi^{(g)}\chi^{(f)}=\chi^{(f)}
by Lemma
3.1
. Note that
\chi^{(g)}\in\mathbb{S}(I)
, and write
\chi^{(g)}=\alpha
. Then
\chi^{(f)}\alpha\chi^{(f)}=\chi^{(f)}
, and so (i) holds.

To prove (ii), let
i\in I\chi^{(f)}
and
y\in X_{i}\cap Xf
. Then, since
y\in Xf
, there exists
x\in X
such that
xf=y
. Also, since
y\in X_{i}
and
\chi^{(g)}=\alpha
, we have
yg\in X_{i\alpha}
. Therefore, since
f=fgf
, we obtain
y=xf=(xf)gf=(yg)f\in X_{i\alpha}f
. Thus
X_{i}\cap Xf\subseteq X_{j}f
, and so (ii) holds.

Conversely, assume that the given conditions hold. To prove
f\in\reg\big(T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})\big)
, we will construct
g\in T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
such that
fgf=f
. Let
i\in I\chi^{(f)}
. For each
x\in X_{i}\cap Xf
, by (ii) there exists
x^{\prime}\in X_{i\alpha}
such that
x=x^{\prime}f
. For each
j\in I
, we fix
x_{j}\in X_{j}
. Define
g\in T(X)
by:

\displaystyle xg=\begin{cases}x^{\prime}&\text{if $x\in X_{i}\cap Xf$, where $i\in I\chi^{(f)}$};\\
x_{i\alpha}&\text{if $x\in X_{i}\setminus Xf$, where $i\in I\chi^{(f)}$};\\
x_{j\alpha}&\text{if $x\in X_{j}$, where $j\in I\setminus I\chi^{(f)}$}.\end{cases}

Clearly
\chi^{(g)}=\alpha
. Since
\alpha\in\mathbb{S}(I)
, it follows that
g\in T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
. It is routine to verify that
fgf=f
, and hence
f\in\reg\big(T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})\big)
.
∎

Using
[
13
, Theorem 3.1]
, Pei
[
17
, Corollary 2.3]
obtained the following result that characterizes regular elements in
T(X,\mathcal{P})
. Using Theorem
3.3
, we give an alternative proof of the same result.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
f\in T(X,\mathcal{P})
. Then
f\in\reg\big(T(X,\mathcal{P})\big)
if and only if for every
i\in I
, there exists
j\in I
such that
X_{i}\cap Xf\subseteq X_{j}f
.

Proof
.

Assume that
f\in\reg\big(T(X,\mathcal{P})\big)
, and let
i\in I
. If
i\in I\setminus I\chi^{(f)}
, then we get
X_{i}\cap Xf=\varnothing
, and so
X_{i}\cap Xf\subseteq X_{j}f
for all
j\in I
. Assume that
i\in I\chi^{(f)}
. By Theorem
3.3
, since
T(X,\mathcal{P})=T_{T(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
, there exists
\alpha\in T(I)
such that
X_{i}\cap Xf\subseteq X_{i\alpha}f
. Thus we conclude that
X_{i}\cap Xf\subseteq X_{j}f
for some
j\in I
.

Conversely, assume that the given condition holds. To prove
f\in\reg\big(T(X,\mathcal{P})\big)
, since
T(X,\mathcal{P})=T_{T(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
, we will construct
\alpha\in T(I)
such that each condition of Theorem
3.3
holds. For each
i\in I
, by hypothesis we fix
j_{i}\in I
such that
X_{i}\cap Xf\subseteq X_{j_{i}}f
. Consider
\alpha\in T(I)
defined by
i\alpha=j_{i}
. We now show that
\chi^{(f)}\alpha\chi^{(f)}=\chi^{(f)}
. Let
k\in I
and write
k\chi^{(f)}=\ell
. Then

k\left(\chi^{(f)}\alpha\chi^{(f)}\right)=\left(k\chi^{(f)}\right)\alpha\chi^{(f)}=(\ell\alpha)\chi^{(f)}=j_{\ell}\chi^{(f)}=\ell=k\chi^{(f)},

and so
\chi^{(f)}\alpha\chi^{(f)}=\chi^{(f)}
. Thus the condition (i) of Theorem
3.3
holds.

Next, let
i\in I\chi^{(f)}
. Then
i\alpha=j_{i}
by the definition of
\alpha
. Therefore, by hypothesis and the choice of
j_{i}
, we get
X_{i}\cap Xf\subseteq X_{i\alpha}f
. Therefore the condition (ii) of Theorem
3.3
holds.

Thus, since
T_{T(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})=T(X,\mathcal{P})
, we conclude from Theorem
3.3
that
f\in\reg\big(T(X,\mathcal{P})\big)
.
∎

Note that the regularity of
\mathbb{S}(I)
may not imply the regularity of
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
(cf.
[
20
, p. 631]
).
In the next result, we determine when
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
is a regular semigroup.

Theorem 3.5
.

The semigroup
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
is regular if and only if:

(i)

\mathbb{S}(I)
is regular;

(ii)

for each
\alpha\in\mathbb{S}(I)
if there exists
i\in I
such that
|i\alpha^{-1}|\geq 2
, then
|X_{i}|=1
.

Proof
.

Assume that
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
is regular. Then (i) is true by Proposition
3.2
and the fact that any homomorphic image of a regular semigroup is regular (cf.
[
12
, Lemma 2.4.4]
).

To prove (ii), let
\alpha\in\mathbb{S}(I)
. If
\mathcal{P}
is trivial, then there is nothing to prove. Assume that
\mathcal{P}
is nontrivial. Suppose to the contrary that
|X_{j}|\geq 2
for some
j\in I
with
|j\alpha^{-1}|\geq 2
. Choose distinct
y_{1},y_{2}\in X_{j}
.
Fix
x_{t}\in X_{t}
for each
t\in I\alpha\setminus\{j\}
if
I\alpha\setminus\{j\}\neq\varnothing
. We also fix
\ell\in j\alpha^{-1}
. Define
f\in T(X)
by:

\displaystyle xf=\begin{cases}y_{1}&\text{if $x\in X_{\ell}$};\\
y_{2}&\text{if $x\in X_{k}$, where $k\in j\alpha^{-1}\setminus\{\ell\}$};\\
x_{i\alpha}&\text{if $x\in X_{i}$, where $i\in I\setminus j\alpha^{-1}$}.\end{cases}

Clearly
\chi^{(f)}=\alpha
. Since
\alpha\in\mathbb{S}(I)
, it follows that
f\in T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
. Therefore
f\in\reg\big(T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})\big)
by hypothesis. By (i), let
\beta\in\Pre(\alpha)
. Note that
\alpha\beta\alpha=\alpha
and
j\in I\alpha
, which gives
j\beta\in j\alpha^{-1}
. Therefore either
X_{j\beta}f=\{y_{1}\}
or
X_{j\beta}f=\{y_{2}\}
by the definition of
f
. However, we have
X_{j}\cap Xf=\{y_{1},y_{2}\}
, and so
X_{j}\cap Xf\not\subseteq X_{j\beta}f
. This is a contradiction by Theorem
3.3
, since
f\in\reg\big(T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})\big)
. Thus (ii) holds.

Conversely, assume that the given conditions hold and let
f\in T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
. To prove the desired result, we will show by Theorem
3.3
that

f\in\reg(T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P}))
. Since
f\in T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
, we have
\chi^{(f)}\in\mathbb{S}(I)
.
Then by (i), there exists
\beta\in\mathbb{S}(I)
such that
\chi^{(f)}\beta\chi^{(f)}=\chi^{(f)}
.

Next, let
i\in I\chi^{(f)}
. Then there are two cases:

Case 1:
Suppose
|i\left(\chi^{(f)}\right)^{-1}|=1
. Write
i\left(\chi^{(f)}\right)^{-1}=\{j\}
. Clearly
X_{i}\cap Xf=X_{j}f
. Since
\chi^{(f)}\beta\chi^{(f)}=\chi^{(f)}
and
j\chi^{(f)}=i
, we see that
i\beta=j
. Therefore
X_{i}\cap Xf\subseteq X_{i\beta}f
.

Case 2:
Suppose
|i\left(\chi^{(f)}\right)^{-1}|\geq 2
. Then by
\rm(ii)
, we get
|X_{i}|=1
. Write
i\beta=k
. Then, since
\chi^{(f)}\beta\chi^{(f)}=\chi^{(f)}
, we see that
k\chi^{(f)}=i
. It follows that
X_{k}f=X_{i}
and
X_{i}\cap Xf=X_{i}
. Therefore
X_{i}\cap Xf\subseteq X_{i\beta}f
.

Thus, in either case, we have
X_{i}\cap Xf\subseteq X_{i\beta}f
. Hence we conclude from Theorem
3.3
that
f\in\reg(T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P}))
.
∎

If
\mathbb{S}(I)=\Sym(I)

Corollary 3.6
.

If
\mathbb{S}(I)
is a subgroup of

, then

Proof
.

If
\mathbb{S}(I)

|i\alpha^{-1}|=1

Using Theorem

, we obtain the following result, which was first appeared in

Proposition 3.7
.

The semigroup

\mathcal{P}
is trivial.

Proof
.

Assume that

is regular. If

, then there is nothing to prove. Assume that

. Suppose to the contrary that
\mathcal{P}
is not trivial. Then
|\mathcal{P}|\geq 2
and

for some

.

Conversely, assume that

Recall that a
block mapping
is a
mapping whose both domain and codomain are blocks of a partition of a nonempty set (cf.
[
25
, Definition 5.1]
). Since
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
is a subsemigroup of

.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
f\in T(X)
. Then
f\in T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
if and only if there exists a unique indexed family
B(f,I)=\{f_{\upharpoonright{X_{i}}}\colon i\in I\}
of block mappings induced by
f
.

Note that an element
f\in T(X)
is an idempotent if and only if
f
acts as the identity on its image set (cf.
[
3
, p. 6]
). If
X
is finite, the idempotents of
T(X,\mathcal{P})
are characterized in
[
8
, Proposition 3.1]
and
[
9
, Proposition 3.1]
for a uniform partition
\mathcal{P}
and a non-uniform partition
\mathcal{P}
, respectively.

The following result describes idempotents of
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
f\in T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
. Then
f\in E\big(T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})\big)
if and only if:

(i)

\chi^{(f)}\in E(\mathbb{S}(I))
;

(ii)

f_{\upharpoonright{X_{i}}}\in E(T(X_{i}))
for all
i\in I\chi^{(f)}
;

(iii)

X_{i}f\subseteq X_{i\chi^{(f)}}f
for all
i\in I\setminus I\chi^{(f)}
.

Proof
.

Assume that
f\in E\big(T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})\big)
. Then
\chi^{(f)}\in\mathbb{S}(I)
and
f^{2}=f
.

(i)

It is clear that
\chi^{(f)}\in E(\mathbb{S}(I))
, since

\left(\chi^{(f)}\right)^{2}=\chi^{(f^{2})}=\chi^{(f)}
by Lemma
3.1
.

(ii)

Let
i\in I\chi^{(f)}
, and let
y\in X_{i}f_{\upharpoonright{X_{i}}}
. Then
i\chi^{(f)}=i
by
\rm(i)
, and
y\in Xf
. Therefore, since
f
is idempotent, we obtain
yf_{\upharpoonright{X_{i}}}=yf=y
. Hence
f_{\upharpoonright{X_{i}}}\in E(T(X_{i}))
.

(iii)

It is straightforward, since
f
stabilizes
\mathcal{P}
and
f^{2}=f
.

Conversely, assume that the given conditions hold. To prove
f\in E\big(T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})\big)
, let
y\in Xf
. Then
y\in X_{i}
for some
i\in I\chi^{(f)}
. Therefore
i\chi^{(f)}=i
by (i), and
f_{\upharpoonright{X_{i}}}\in E(T(X_{i}))
by (ii). By (iii), we then see that
y\in X_{i}f_{\upharpoonright{X_{i}}}
. Thus we obtain
yf=yf_{\upharpoonright{X_{i}}}=y
. Hence
f\in E\big(T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})\big)
.
∎

Recall that a semigroup is an inverse semigroup if and only if it is regular and its idempotents commute (cf.
[
12
, Theorem 5.1.1]
).

The following result determines when
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
is an inverse semigroup.

Theorem 3.10
.

The semigroup
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
is an inverse semigroup if and only if:

(i)

\mathbb{S}(I)
is an inverse semigroup;

(ii)

for each
\alpha\in E(\mathbb{S}(I))
, we have
|X_{i}|=1
for all
i\in I\alpha
.

Proof
.

Assume that
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
is an inverse semigroup. Then (i) is true by Proposition
3.2
and the fact that any homomorphic image of an inverse semigroup is an inverse semigroup (cf.
[
12
, Theorem 5.1.4]
).

To prove (ii), let
\alpha\in E(\mathbb{S}(I))
. If
|X|=1
, then there is nothing to prove. Assume that
|X|\geq 2
. Suppose to the contrary that there exists
j\in I\alpha
such that
|X_{j}|\geq 2
. Since
\alpha\in E(\mathbb{S}(I))
and
j\in I\alpha
, we get
j\alpha=j
. Now, choose distinct
y,z\in X_{j}
. If
I\setminus\{j\}\neq\varnothing
, then we fix
x_{i}\in X_{i}
for each
i\in I\setminus\{j\}
. Define
f,g\in T(X)
by:

\displaystyle xf=\begin{cases}y&\text{if $x\in X_{i}$, where $i\in j\alpha^{-1}$};\\
x_{i\alpha}&\text{if $x\in X_{i}$, where $i\in I\setminus j\alpha^{-1}$}.\end{cases}

and

\displaystyle xg=\begin{cases}z&\text{if $x\in X_{i}$, where $i\in j\alpha^{-1}$};\\
x_{i\alpha}&\text{if $x\in X_{i}$, where $i\in I\setminus j\alpha^{-1}$}.\end{cases}

Clearly
\chi^{(f)}=\alpha=\chi^{(g)}
. Since
\alpha\in E(\mathbb{S}(I))
, it follows that
\chi^{(f)},\chi^{(g)}\in E(\mathbb{S}(I))
. Now, we observe from the definition of
f
that
f_{\upharpoonright{X_{i}}}\in E(T(X_{i}))
for all
i\in I\alpha
, and
X_{i}f_{\upharpoonright{X_{i}}}=X_{i\alpha}f_{\upharpoonright{X_{i\alpha}}}
for all
i\in I\setminus I\alpha
. Therefore
f\in E\big(T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})\big)
by Proposition
3.9
. Similarly, we can obtain
g\in E\big(T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})\big)
. However, since
y(fg)\neq y(gf)
, we have
fg\neq gf
. This contradicts our assumption that
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
is an inverse semigroup. Thus
|X_{i}|=1
for all
i\in I\alpha
, and hence (ii) holds.

Conversely, assume that the given conditions hold. To prove that
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
is an inverse semigroup, we will show that

T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
is regular and its idempotents commute.

First, we show that
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
is regular. By (i), it is clear that
\mathbb{S}(I)
is regular. Now, let
\alpha\in\mathbb{S}(I)
. Since
\mathbb{S}(I)
is regular, there exists
\beta\in E(\mathbb{S}(I))
such that
I\alpha=I\beta
(cf.
[
12
, Theorem 2.3.2 and Exercise 16(a) on p. 63]
). Then by (ii), we have
|X_{i}|=1
for all
i\in I\beta
. It follows that
|X_{i}|=1
for all
i\in I\alpha
. Thus
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
is regular by Theorem
3.5
.

To prove that idempotents of
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
commute, let
f,g\in E\big(T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})\big)
and let
x\in X
. Then
\chi^{(f)},\chi^{(g)}\in E(\mathbb{S}(I))
by Proposition
3.9
, and
x\in X_{i}
for some
i\in I
. Write
i\chi^{(fg)}=j
. Observe from (i) and Lemma
3.1
that
i\chi^{(gf)}=i(\chi^{(g)}\chi^{(f)})=i(\chi^{(f)}\chi^{(g)})=i\chi^{(fg)}=j
. Note also from Lemma
3.1
that
I\chi^{(fg)}=I(\chi^{(f)}\chi^{(g)})\subseteq I\chi^{(g)}
, and so
j\in I\chi^{(g)}
. By (ii), we then get
X_{j}=\{y\}
for some
y\in X
. Therefore we obtain
x(fg)=y=x(gf)
. Hence idempotents of
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
commute.

Thus we conclude that
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
is an inverse semigroup.

∎

4.
Unit-regularity of
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})

Throughout this section, assume that
\mathbb{S}(I)
contains the identity
\id_{I}
. We describe unit-regular elements in
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
. Then we determine when
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
is a unit-regular semigroup. Using this, we determine when
T(X,\mathcal{P})
is a unit-regular semigroup.

Observe that
\mathbb{S}(I)
contains the identity
\id_{I}
if and only if
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
contains the identity
\id_{X}
. Note that
U\big(T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})\big)\subseteq U\big(T(X,\mathcal{P})\big)=S(X,\mathcal{P})
, and so
U\big(T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})\big)=T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})\cap S(X,\mathcal{P})
.

We require the next three results from
[
25
,
27
]
to prove Theorem

4.4
, which describes unit-regular elements in
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
.

Lemma 4.1
.

[
25
, Lemma 3.6
(i)]

If
f\in S(X,\mathcal{P})
, then
X_{i}f\in\mathcal{P}
for all
i\in I
.

Lemma 4.2
.

[
25
, Theorem 5.8]

Let
f\in T(X,\mathcal{P})
. Then
f\in S(X,\mathcal{P})
if and only if:

(i)

every element of
B(f,I)
is bijective;

(ii)

\chi^{(f)}
is bijective.

Lemma 4.3
.

[
27
, Lemma 3.1]

Let
f\colon X\to Y
and
g\colon Y\to X
be mappings. If
fgf=f
, then
X(fg)
is a transversal of the equivalence relation
\ker(f)
.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
f\in T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
. Then
f\in\ureg\big(T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})\big)
if and only if there exists
\alpha\in U(\mathbb{S}(I))
such that:

(i)

\chi^{(f)}\alpha\chi^{(f)}=\chi^{(f)}
;

(ii)

X_{i}\cap Xf\subseteq X_{i\alpha}f
for all
i\in I\chi^{(f)}
;

(iii)

|X_{i}|=|X_{i\alpha}|
for all
i\in I
;

(iv)

\textnormal{c}(f_{\upharpoonright{X_{i\alpha}}})=\textnormal{d}(f_{\upharpoonright{X_{i\alpha}}})

for all
i\in I\chi^{(f)}
.

Proof
.

Assume that
f\in\ureg\big(T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})\big)
. Then there exists
g\in U\big(T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})\big)
such that
fgf=f
. This gives
\chi^{(f)}\chi^{(g)}\chi^{(f)}=\chi^{(f)}
by Lemma
3.1
. Note that
\chi^{(g)}\in U(\mathbb{S}(I))
, and write
\chi^{(g)}=\alpha
.

(i)

It is clear from the above.

(ii)

Let
i\in I\chi^{(f)}
and
y\in X_{i}\cap Xf
. Then, since
y\in Xf
, there exists
x\in X
such that
xf=y
. Also, since
y\in X_{i}
and
\chi^{(g)}=\alpha
, we see that
yg\in X_{i\alpha}
. Therefore, since
f=fgf
, we obtain
y=xf=(xf)gf=(yg)f\in X_{i\alpha}f
. Hence
X_{i}\cap Xf\subseteq X_{i\alpha}f
.

(iii)

Let
i\in I
. Note that
g\in U\big(T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})\big)
and
\chi^{(g)}=\alpha
. Then, since
U\big(T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})\big)\subseteq S(X,\mathcal{P})
, we get
X_{i}g=X_{i\alpha}
by Lemma
4.1
, which implies
|X_{i}|=|X_{i\alpha}|
.

(iv)

Let
i\in I\chi^{(f)}
, and write
i\alpha=j
. Recall that
g\in U\big(T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})\big)
and
\chi^{(g)}=\alpha
. Since
U\big(T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})\big)\subseteq S(X,\mathcal{P})
, it follows from Lemma
4.1
that
X_{i}g=X_{j}
. This yields
X_{i}g_{\upharpoonright{X_{i}}}=X_{j}
. By (ii), we have
X_{j}f_{\upharpoonright{X_{j}}}\subseteq X_{i}
. Then, since
fgf=f
, we obtain
f_{\upharpoonright{X_{j}}}g_{\upharpoonright{X_{i}}}f_{\upharpoonright{X_{j}}}=f_{\upharpoonright{X_{j}}}
. It follows that
X_{j}(f_{\upharpoonright{X_{j}}}g_{\upharpoonright{X_{i}}})
is a transversal of
\ker(f_{\upharpoonright{X_{j}}})
by Lemma
4.3
. Write
T_{f_{\upharpoonright{X_{j}}}}=X_{j}(f_{\upharpoonright{X_{j}}}g_{\upharpoonright{X_{i}}})
. Since
g\in S(X,\mathcal{P})
, it follows that
g_{\upharpoonright{X_{i}}}
is bijective by Theorem
4.2
. Therefore
(X_{i}\setminus X_{j}f_{\upharpoonright{X_{j}}})g_{\upharpoonright{X_{i}}}=X_{j}\setminus T_{f_{\upharpoonright{X_{j}}}}
, and hence
\textnormal{d}(f_{\upharpoonright{X_{j}}})=\textnormal{c}(f_{\upharpoonright{X_{j}}})
.

Conversely, assume that the given conditions hold. To prove
f\in\ureg\big(T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})\big)
, we will construct
g\in U(T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P}))
such that
fgf=f
. Let
i\in I\chi^{(f)}
, and write
i\alpha=j
. Choose a transversal
T_{f_{\upharpoonright{X_{j}}}}
of
\ker(f_{\upharpoonright{X_{j}}})
, and define a mapping
h_{i}^{\prime}\colon X_{j}f_{\upharpoonright{X_{j}}}\to T_{f_{\upharpoonright{X_{j}}}}
by
xh_{i}^{\prime}=a_{x}
if and only if
x(f_{\upharpoonright{X_{j}}})^{-1}\cap T_{f_{\upharpoonright{X_{j}}}}=\{a_{x}\}
. It is easy to verify that
h_{i}^{\prime}
is a bijection. By (iv), there exists a bijection
h_{i}^{\prime\prime}\colon D(f_{\upharpoonright{X_{j}}})\to C(f_{\upharpoonright{X_{j}}})
, where
C(f_{\upharpoonright{X_{j}}})=X_{j}\setminus T_{f_{\upharpoonright{X_{j}}}}
. By (i), we see that
j\chi^{(f)}=i
. Thus we may write
X_{i}=X_{j}f_{\upharpoonright{X_{j}}}\cup D(f_{\upharpoonright{X_{j}}})
and
X_{j}=T_{f_{\upharpoonright{X_{j}}}}\cup C(f_{\upharpoonright{X_{j}}})
. Define a mapping
h_{i}\colon X_{i}\to X_{j}
by:

\displaystyle xh_{i}=\begin{cases}xh_{i}^{\prime}&\text{if $x\in X_{j}f_{\upharpoonright{X_{j}}}$};\\
xh_{i}^{\prime\prime}&\text{if $x\in D(f_{\upharpoonright{X_{j}}})$}.\end{cases}

Clearly
h_{i}
is a bijection. Now for each
j\in I\setminus I\chi^{(f)}
, by (iii) there is a bijection
g_{j}\colon X_{j}\to X_{j\alpha}
. Finally, define
g\in T(X)
by:

\displaystyle xg=\begin{cases}xh_{i}&\text{if $x\in X_{i}$, where $i\in I\chi^{(f)}$};\\
xg_{j}&\text{if $x\in X_{j}$, where $j\in I\setminus I\chi^{(f)}$}.\end{cases}

Clearly
\chi^{(g)}=\alpha
. Since
\alpha\in U(\mathbb{S}(I))
, it follows that
g
is a bijection. Therefore we have
g\in U\big(T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})\big)
. It is routine to verify that
fgf=f
, and hence
f\in\ureg\big(T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})\big)
.
∎

We require the following result to prove Theorem
4.6
, which determines unit-regularity of
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
X
and
Y
be nonempty sets. Then there is a mapping
f\colon X\to Y
such that
\textnormal{c}(f)\neq\textnormal{d}(f)
if any of the following conditions hold:

(i)

|X|\neq|Y|
;

(ii)

X
is infinite;

(iii)

Y
is infinite.

Proof
.

(i)

If
|X|<|Y|
, then there exists an injection
f\colon X\to Y
. Therefore
\textnormal{c}(f)=0
and
|Xf|=|X|
. Since
|X|<|Y|
, we have
|Xf|<|Y|
. Therefore
\textnormal{d}(f)\neq 0
, and hence
\textnormal{c}(f)\neq\textnormal{d}(f)
. If
|X|>|Y|
, we may dually prove that
\textnormal{c}(f)\neq\textnormal{d}(f)
.

(ii)

If
|X|\neq|Y|
, then we are done by (i). Assume that
|X|=|Y|
. Since
X
is infinite, there exists an injection
f\colon X\to Y
that is not a surjection. Therefore
\textnormal{c}(f)=0
but
\textnormal{d}(f)\neq 0
. Hence
\textnormal{c}(f)\neq\textnormal{d}(f)
.

(iii)

It is similar to (ii).

∎

Theorem 4.6
.

The semigroup
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
is unit-regular if and only if:

(i)

\mathbb{S}(I)
is unit-regular;

(ii)

for each
\alpha\in U(\mathbb{S}(I))
, we have
|X_{i}|=|X_{i\alpha}|
for all
i\in I
;

(iii)

|X_{i}|
is finite for all
i\in I
;

(iv)

for each
\beta\in\mathbb{S}(I)
if there exists
i\in I
such that
|i\beta^{-1}|\geq 2
, then
|X_{i}|=1
.

Proof
.

Assume that
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
is unit-regular.

(i)

The semigroup
\mathbb{S}(I)
is unit-regular by Proposition
3.2
and the fact that any homomorphic image of a unit-regular semigroup is unit-regular (cf.
[
10
, Proposition 2.7]
).

(ii)

Let
\alpha\in U(\mathbb{S}(I))
. Then
\alpha\in\Sym(I)
. Suppose to the contrary that there exists
j\in I
such that
|X_{j}|\neq|X_{j\alpha}|
. Then by Lemma
4.5
, there exists a mapping
h\colon X_{j}\to X_{j\alpha}
such that
\textnormal{c}(h)\neq\textnormal{d}(h)
. For each
i\in I\setminus\{j\alpha\}
, we fix
x_{i}\in X_{i}
. Define
f\in T(X)
by:

\displaystyle xf=\begin{cases}xh&\text{if $x\in X_{j}$};\\
x_{i\alpha}&\text{if $x\in X_{i}$, where $i\in I\setminus\{j\}$}.\end{cases}

Clearly
\chi^{(f)}=\alpha
, and so
f\in T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
. Now, let
j\alpha=k
and
\alpha^{-1}=\beta
. Clearly
\beta\in U(\mathbb{S}(I))
and
k\beta=j
. Note that
k\in I\chi^{(f)}
and
f_{\upharpoonright{X_{k\beta}}}=f_{\upharpoonright{X_{j}}}=h
. Since
\textnormal{c}(h)\neq\textnormal{d}(h)
, it follows that
\textnormal{c}(f_{\upharpoonright{X_{k\beta}}})\neq\textnormal{d}(f_{\upharpoonright{X_{k\beta}}})
. This means
f
does not satisfy the condition
\rm(iv)
of Theorem
4.4
, and so
f\notin\ureg\big(T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})\big)
by Theorem
4.4
. This is a contradiction, because
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
is unit-regular. Hence
|X_{i}|=|X_{i\alpha}|
for all
i\in I
.

(iii)

Suppose to the contrary that there exists
j\in I
such that
|X_{j}|
is infinite. Take
\alpha\in U(\mathbb{S}(I))
, and write
j\alpha=\ell
. Since
|X_{j}|
is infinite, by Lemma
4.5
there is a mapping
h\colon X_{j}\to X_{\ell}
such that
\textnormal{c}(h)\neq\textnormal{d}(h)
. For each
i\in I\setminus\{j\alpha\}
, we fix
x_{i}\in X_{i}
. Define
f\in T(X)
by:

\displaystyle xf=\begin{cases}xh&\text{if $x\in X_{j}$};\\
x_{i\alpha}&\text{if $x\in X_{i}$, where $i\in I\setminus\{j\}$}.\end{cases}

Clearly
\chi^{(f)}=\alpha
, and so
f\in T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
. Write
\alpha^{-1}=\beta
. Clearly
\beta\in U(\mathbb{S}(I))
and
\ell\beta=j
. Note that
\ell\in I\chi^{(f)}
and
f_{\upharpoonright{X_{\ell\beta}}}=f_{\upharpoonright{X_{j}}}=h
. Since
\textnormal{c}(h)\neq\textnormal{d}(h)
, it follows that
\textnormal{c}(f_{\upharpoonright{X_{\ell\beta}}})\neq\textnormal{d}(f_{\upharpoonright{X_{\ell\beta}}})
. This means
f
does not satisfy the condition
\rm(iv)
of Theorem
4.4
, and so
f\notin\ureg\big(T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})\big)
by Theorem
4.4
. This is a contradiction, because
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
is unit-regular. Hence
|X_{i}|
is finite for all
i\in I
.

(iv)

It is directly followed from Theorem
3.5
, since
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
is a regular semigroup.

Conversely, assume that the given conditions hold, and let
f\in T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
. To prove the desired result, we will show that
f\in\ureg(T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P}))
by Theorem
4.4
. Since
f\in T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
, we have
\chi^{(f)}\in\mathbb{S}(I)
. By (i), there exists
\alpha\in U(\mathbb{S}(I))
such that
\chi^{(f)}\alpha\chi^{(f)}=\chi^{(f)}
. By (ii), we have
|X_{i}|=|X_{i\alpha}|
for all
i\in I
.

Next, let
i\in I\chi^{(f)}
. Since
\chi^{(f)}\alpha\chi^{(f)}=\chi^{(f)}
, we see that
X_{i\alpha}f\subseteq X_{i}
. That means
f_{\upharpoonright{X_{i\alpha}}}
is a block mapping from
X_{i\alpha}
to
X_{i}
. Note from
\rm(ii)
and (iii) that
|X_{i}|=|X_{i\alpha}|
and
X_{i}
is finite, respectively. Therefore
\textnormal{c}(f_{\upharpoonright{X_{i\alpha}}})=\textnormal{d}(f_{\upharpoonright{X_{i\alpha}}})
.

Finally, since
i\in I\chi^{(f)}
, there are two cases:

Case 1:
Suppose
|i(\chi^{(f)})^{-1}|=1
. Since
\chi^{(f)}\alpha\chi^{(f)}=\chi^{(f)}
, we see that
(i\alpha)\chi^{(f)}=i
and so
i\left(\chi^{(f)}\right)^{-1}=\{i\alpha\}
. This gives
X_{i\alpha}f=X_{i}\cap Xf
, and so
X_{i}\cap Xf\subseteq X_{i\alpha}f
.

Case 2:
Suppose
|i(\chi^{(f)})^{-1}|\geq 2
. Then by (iv), we get
|X_{i}|=1
. Therefore, since
i\in I\chi^{(f)}
, we obtain
X_{i}\cap Xf=X_{i}
. Since
\chi^{(f)}\alpha\chi^{(f)}=\chi^{(f)}
, we see that
(i\alpha)\chi^{(f)}=i
. Therefore
X_{i\alpha}f=X_{i}
, and so
X_{i}\cap Xf\subseteq X_{i\alpha}f
.

In either case, we have
X_{i}\cap Xf\subseteq X_{i\alpha}f
. Thus
f
satisfies each condition of Theorem
4.4
, and hence
f\in\ureg(T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P}))
by Theorem
4.4
.
∎

The authors
[
27
, Theorem 4.7]
recently described unit-regular elements in
T(X,\mathcal{P})
. We require the following result to prove Proposition
4.8
, which determines unit-regularity of
T(X,\mathcal{P})
.

Proposition 4.7
.

[
4
, Proposition 5]

The semigroup
T(X)
is unit-regular if and only if
X
is finite.

Proposition 4.8
.

The semigroup
T(X,\mathcal{P})
is unit-regular if and only if
X
is finite and
\mathcal{P}
is trivial.

Proof
.

Assume that
T(X,\mathcal{P})
is unit-regular. Then, since
T(X,\mathcal{P})=T_{T(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
, the semigroup
T(I)
is unit-regular by Theorem
4.6
(i). Therefore
I
is finite by Proposition
4.7
. It follows from Theorem
4.6
(iii) that
X
is finite. Next, since
T(X,\mathcal{P})
is regular, the partition
\mathcal{P}
is trivial by Proposition
3.7
.

Conversely, assume that the given conditions hold. Since
\mathcal{P}
is trivial, it follows that
T(X,\mathcal{P})=T(X)
. Moreover, since
X
is finite, the semigroup
T(X,\mathcal{P})
is unit-regular by Proposition
4.7
.
∎

5.
Green’s Relations on
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})

Throughout this section, assume that the semigroup
\mathbb{S}(I)
contains the identity
\id_{I}
. We characterize Green’s relations on
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
. Using these characterizations, we obtain more concrete and noticeably different descriptions of Green’s relations on
T(X,\mathcal{P})
, whose Green’s relations are known; see
[
17
,
18
]
. We refer the reader to
[
12
, Chapter 2]
for the basic definitions and notation related to Green’s relations on a semigroup.

First, we recall the following well-known characterizations of Green’s relations
\mathcal{L}
,
\mathcal{R}
,
\mathcal{D}
, and
\mathcal{J}
on
T(X)
(cf.
[
3
, p. 52]
,
[
12
, p. 63, Exercise 16]
).

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
f,g\in T(X)
. Then:

(1)

L_{f}\leq L_{g}
if and only if
Xf\subseteq Xg
. Subsequently,
(f,g)\in\mathcal{L}
if and only if
Xf=Xg
;

(2)

R_{f}\leq R_{g}
if and only if
\ker(g)\subseteq\ker(f)
. Subsequently,
(f,g)\in\mathcal{R}
if and only if
\ker(f)=\ker(g)
;

(3)

J_{f}\leq J_{g}
if and only if
|Xf|\leq|Xg|
. Subsequently,
(f,g)\in\mathcal{J}
if and only if
|Xf|=|Xg|
;

(4)

\mathcal{D}=\mathcal{J}
.

We require the following result to obtain a characterization of the relation
\mathcal{L}
on
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
f,g\in T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
. Then
L_{f}\leq L_{g}
in
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
if and only if there exists
\alpha\in\mathbb{S}(I)
such that
\chi^{(f)}=\alpha\chi^{(g)}
and
X_{i}f\subseteq X_{i\alpha}g
for all
i\in I
.

Proof
.

Assume that
L_{f}\leq L_{g}
in
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
. Then
f=hg
for some
h\in T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
. This gives
\chi^{(f)}=\chi^{(hg)}=\chi^{(h)}\chi^{(g)}
by Lemma
3.1
. Write
\chi^{(h)}=\alpha
, and thus
\chi^{(f)}=\alpha\chi^{(g)}
. To prove the remaining part, let
i\in I
. Notice that
X_{i}h\subseteq X_{i\alpha}
, and therefore
X_{i}f=X_{i}(hg)=(X_{i}h)g\subseteq X_{i\alpha}g
.

Conversely, assume that the given conditions hold. To prove
L_{f}\leq L_{g}
in
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
, we will construct
h\in T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
such that
f=hg
. For each
i\in I
and for each
x\in X_{i}
, by hypothesis we fix
y\in X_{i\alpha}
such that
xf=yg
. Define
h\in T(X)
by
xh=y
whenever
x\in X_{i}
for some
i\in I
. It is clear by the definition of
h
that
\chi^{(h)}=\alpha
, and so
h\in T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
. To prove
f=hg
, let
x\in X
. Then
x\in X_{i}
for some
i\in I
. Therefore
x(hg)=(xh)g=yg=xf
, which yields
f=hg
. Hence
L_{f}\leq L_{g}
in
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
.
∎

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
f,g\in T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
. Then
(f,g)\in\mathcal{L}
in
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
if and only if there exist
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{S}(I)
such that:

(i)

\chi^{(f)}=\alpha\chi^{(g)}
and
\chi^{(g)}=\beta\chi^{(f)}
;

(ii)

X_{i}f\subseteq X_{i\alpha}g
and
X_{i}g\subseteq X_{i\beta}f
for all
i\in I
.

Proof
.

It follows directly from Lemma
5.2
. ∎

In 2005, Pei
[
17
, Theorem 3.2]
gave a characterization of the relation
\mathcal{L}
on
T(X,\mathcal{P})
. Using Theorem
5.3
, we also provide another characterization of the relation
\mathcal{L}
on
T(X,\mathcal{P})
as follows.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
f,g\in T(X,\mathcal{P})
. Then
(f,g)\in\mathcal{L}
in
T(X,\mathcal{P})
if and only if for each
i\in I
, there exist
j,k\in I
such that
X_{i}f\subseteq X_{j}g
and
X_{i}g\subseteq X_{k}f
.

Proof
.

Assume that
(f,g)\in\mathcal{L}
in
T(X,\mathcal{P})
, and let
i\in I
. By Theorem
5.3
, since
T(X,\mathcal{P})=T_{T(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
, there exist
\alpha,\beta\in T(I)
such that
X_{i}f\subseteq X_{i\alpha}g
and
X_{i}g\subseteq X_{i\beta}f
. Write
i\alpha=j
and
i\beta=k
. Thus
X_{i}f\subseteq X_{j}g
and
X_{i}g\subseteq X_{k}f
.

Conversely, assume that the given conditions hold. For each
i\in I
, by hypothesis we choose
j_{i},k_{i}\in I
such that
X_{i}f\subseteq X_{j_{i}}g
and
X_{i}g\subseteq X_{k_{i}}f
. Define
\alpha,\beta\in T(I)
by
i\alpha=j_{i}
and
i\beta=k_{i}
. Then we have
X_{i}f\subseteq X_{i\alpha}g
and
X_{i}g\subseteq X_{i\beta}f
for all
i\in I
. Next, we prove that
\chi^{(f)}=\alpha\chi^{(g)}
. For this, let
i\in I
. Then
X_{i}f\subseteq X_{j_{i}}g
, and so
i\chi^{(f)}=j_{i}\chi^{(g)}
. Therefore we obtain
i\left(\alpha\chi^{(g)}\right)=(i\alpha)\chi^{(g)}=j_{i}\chi^{(g)}=i\chi^{(f)}
, which yields
\chi^{(f)}=\alpha\chi^{(g)}
. Similarly, we may prove that
\chi^{(g)}=\beta\chi^{(f)}
by using
X_{i}g\subseteq X_{k_{i}}f
.
Thus, since
T_{T(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})=T(X,\mathcal{P})
, we conclude from Theorem
5.3
that
(f,g)\in\mathcal{L}
in
T(X,\mathcal{P})
. ∎

To obtain a characterization of the relation
\mathcal{R}
on
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
, we require the following definition and Lemma
5.5
.

Let
f,g\in T(X)
. We say that
\pi(f)

refines

\pi(g)
, denoted by
\pi(f)\preceq\pi(g)
, if
\ker(f)\subseteq\ker(g)
. We write
\pi(f)=\pi(g)
if
\pi(f)\preceq\pi(g)
and
\pi(g)\preceq\pi(f)
.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
f,g\in T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
. Then
R_{f}\leq R_{g}
in
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
if and only if
R_{\chi^{(f)}}\leq R_{\chi^{(g)}}
in
\mathbb{S}(I)
and
\pi(g)\preceq\pi(f)
.

Proof
.

Assume that
R_{f}\leq R_{g}
in
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
. Then
f=gh
for some
h\in T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
. This gives
\chi^{(f)}=\chi^{(gh)}=\chi^{(g)}\chi^{(h)}
by Lemma
3.1
. Since
\chi^{(f)},\chi^{(g)},\chi^{(h)}\in\mathbb{S}(I)
, it follows that
R_{\chi^{(f)}}\leq R_{\chi^{(g)}}
in
\mathbb{S}(I)
. Next, since
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})\subseteq T(X)
, we have
f,g,h\in T(X)
. Therefore
\ker(g)\subseteq\ker(f)
by Theorem
5.1
(2), and hence
\pi(g)\preceq\pi(f)
.

Conversely, assume that the given conditions hold. To prove
R_{f}\leq R_{g}
in
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
, we will construct
h\in T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
such that
f=gh
. Since
R_{\chi^{(f)}}\leq R_{\chi^{(g)}}
in
\mathbb{S}(I)
, we have
\chi^{(f)}=\chi^{(g)}\beta
for some
\beta\in\mathbb{S}(I)
. Now, for each
i\in I
, fix
x_{i}\in X_{i}
; for each
x\in Xg
, fix
x^{\prime}\in xg^{-1}
. Define
h\in T(X)
by:

\displaystyle xh=\begin{cases}x^{\prime}f&\text{if $x\in X_{i}\cap Xg$};\\
x_{i\beta}&\text{if $x\in X_{i}\setminus Xg$.}\end{cases}

It is clear that
h
is well-defined, since
\pi(g)\preceq\pi(f)
. Since
\chi^{(f)}=\chi^{(g)}\beta
, we see that
\chi^{(h)}=\beta
, and so
h\in T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
. To prove
f=gh
, let
x\in X
. Then
x\in X_{i}
for some
i\in I
. Therefore, since
x^{\prime}\in xg^{-1}
and
xg^{-1}\in\pi(f)
, we obtain
x(gh)=(xg)h=x^{\prime}f=xf
, which yields
f=gh
. Hence
R_{f}\leq R_{g}
in
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
.
∎

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
f,g\in T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
. Then
(f,g)\in\mathcal{R}
in
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
if and only if
\left(\chi^{(f)},\chi^{(g)}\right)\in\mathcal{R}
in
\mathbb{S}(I)
and
\pi(f)=\pi(g)
.

Proof
.

It follows directly from Lemma
5.5
. ∎

Recall that
T(X,\mathcal{P})=T_{T(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
. Using Theorem
5.6
and Theorem
5.1
(2), we immediately have the following characterization of the relation
\mathcal{R}
on
T(X,\mathcal{P})
, which was first appeared in
[
17
, Theorem 3.1]
.

Corollary 5.7
.

Let
f,g\in T(X,\mathcal{P})
. Then
(f,g)\in\mathcal{R}
in
T(X,\mathcal{P})
if and only if
\pi\left(\chi^{(f)}\right)=\pi\left(\chi^{(g)}\right)
and
\pi(f)=\pi(g)
.

We require the following result that is useful in establishing the relation
\mathcal{D}
on
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
.

Lemma 5.8
.

Let
f,g\in T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
.

(i)

If
L_{f}\leq L_{g}
in
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
, then
L_{\chi^{(f)}}\leq L_{\chi^{(g)}}
in
\mathbb{S}(I)
. Subsequently, if
(f,g)\in\mathcal{L}
in
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
, then
(\chi^{(f)},\chi^{(g)})\in\mathcal{L}
in
\mathbb{S}(I)
.

(ii)

If
R_{f}\leq R_{g}
in
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
, then
R_{\chi^{(f)}}\leq R_{\chi^{(g)}}
in
\mathbb{S}(I)
. Subsequently, if
(f,g)\in\mathcal{R}
in
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
, then
(\chi^{(f)},\chi^{(g)})\in\mathcal{R}
in
\mathbb{S}(I)
.

Proof
.

(i)

If
L_{f}\leq L_{g}
in
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
, then
f=hg
for some
h\in T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
. This gives
\chi^{(f)}=\chi^{(hg)}=\chi^{(h)}\chi^{(g)}
by Lemma
3.1
. Since
\chi^{(f)},\chi^{(g)},\chi^{(h)}\in\mathbb{S}(I)
, it follows that
L_{\chi^{(f)}}\leq L_{\chi^{(g)}}
in
\mathbb{S}(I)
. The rest of the proof is immediate from the previous statements.

(ii)

It is the dual of the proof above.

∎

For every
A\subseteq X
and every
f\in T(X)
, let
\pi_{A}(f)=\{M\in\pi(f)\colon M\cap A\neq\varnothing\}
. If
A=X
, then we write
\pi(f)
instead of
\pi_{X}(f)
.

The following result characterizes the relation
\mathcal{D}
on
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
.

Theorem 5.9
.

Let
f,g\in T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
. Then
(f,g)\in\mathcal{D}
in
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
if and only if there exist
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{S}(I)
and a bijection
\varphi\colon\pi(f)\to\pi(g)
such that:

(i)

\chi^{(f)}=\alpha\gamma
,
\gamma=\beta\chi^{(f)}
, and
\left(\gamma,\chi^{(g)}\right)\in\mathcal{R}
in
\mathbb{S}(I)
;

(ii)

\pi_{X_{i}}(f)\varphi\subseteq\pi_{X_{i\alpha}}(g)
for all
i\in I
;

(iii)

\pi_{X_{i}}(g)\varphi^{-1}\subseteq\pi_{X_{i\beta}}(f)
for all
i\in I
.

Proof
.

Assume that
(f,g)\in\mathcal{D}
in
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
. Then, since
\mathcal{D}=\mathcal{L}\circ\mathcal{R}
, there exists
h\in T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
such that
(f,h)\in\mathcal{L}
and
(h,g)\in\mathcal{R}
in
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
. Write
\chi^{(h)}=\gamma
. Clearly
\gamma\in\mathbb{S}(I)
. Since
(f,h)\in\mathcal{L}
in
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
, by Theorem
5.3
there exist
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{S}(I)
such that
\chi^{(f)}=\alpha\gamma
and
\gamma=\beta\chi^{(f)}
;
X_{i}f\subseteq X_{i\alpha}h
and
X_{i}h\subseteq X_{i\beta}f
for all
i\in I
. Also, since
(h,g)\in\mathcal{R}
in
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
, we get
(\gamma,\chi^{(g)})\in\mathcal{R}
in
\mathbb{S}(I)
by Lemma
5.8
(ii). Thus
\rm(i)
holds.

To prove (ii) and (iii), we first define a mapping
\varphi\colon\pi(f)\to\pi(g)
by
(af^{-1})\varphi=ah^{-1}
whenever
a\in Xf
. Since
(f,h)\in\mathcal{L}
in
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
, we have
(f,h)\in\mathcal{L}
in
T(X)
, and so
Xf=Xh
by Theorem
5.1
(1). Since
a\in Xf
, it follows that
a\in Xh
, and so
ah^{-1}\in\pi(h)
. Also, since
(h,g)\in\mathcal{R}
in
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
, we have
(h,g)\in\mathcal{R}
in
T(X)
, and so
\pi(h)=\pi(g)
by Theorem
5.1
(2). Therefore, since
ah^{-1}\in\pi(h)
, we obtain
ah^{-1}\in\pi(g)
. Thus
\varphi
is well-defined. Moreover, since
Xf=Xh
and
\pi(h)=\pi(g)
, it is a routine matter to verify that
\varphi
is bijective.

Now to prove
\rm(ii)
, let
i\in I
and let
A\in\pi_{X_{i}}(f)
. Then
A\cap X_{i}\neq\varnothing
. Write
Af=\{a\}
. Since
A\cap X_{i}\neq\varnothing
and
f
stabilizes
\mathcal{P}
, it is obvious that
a\in X_{i}f
. Note that
X_{i}f\subseteq X_{i\alpha}h
, and so
a\in X_{i\alpha}h
. This gives
ah^{-1}\cap X_{i\alpha}\neq\varnothing
. Therefore
A\varphi\cap X_{i\alpha}=(af^{-1})\varphi\cap X_{i\alpha}=ah^{-1}\cap X_{i\alpha}\neq\varnothing
, which yields
A\varphi\in\pi_{X_{i\alpha}}(g)
. Hence
\pi_{X_{i}}(f)\varphi\subseteq\pi_{X_{i\alpha}}(g)
, and thus
\rm(ii)
holds.

Next to prove
\rm(iii)
, let
i\in I
and let
B\in\pi_{X_{i}}(g)
. Then
B\cap X_{i}\neq\varnothing
. Since
\pi(h)=\pi(g)
, we get
\pi_{X_{i}}(h)=\pi_{X_{i}}(g)
. It follows that
B\in\pi_{X_{i}}(h)
. Write
Bh=\{b\}
. Clearly
b\in X_{i}h
. Note that
X_{i}h\subseteq X_{i\beta}f
, and so
b\in X_{i\beta}f
. This gives
bf^{-1}\cap X_{i\beta}\neq\varnothing
. Therefore
B\varphi^{-1}\cap X_{i\beta}=(bh^{-1})\varphi^{-1}\cap X_{i\beta}=bf^{-1}\cap X_{i\beta}\neq\varnothing
, which yields
B\varphi^{-1}\in\pi_{X_{i\beta}}(f)
. Hence
\pi_{X_{i}}(g)\varphi^{-1}\subseteq\pi_{X_{i\beta}}(f)
, and thus
\rm(iii)
holds.

Conversely, assume that the given conditions hold. To prove
(f,g)\in\mathcal{D}
in
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
, we will construct
h\in T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
such that
(f,h)\in\mathcal{L}
and
(h,g)\in\mathcal{R}
in
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
. Define
h\in T(X)
by
xh=y
whenever
\big((xgg^{-1})\varphi^{-1}\big)f=\{y\}
. First, we show that
\chi^{(h)}=\gamma
. For this, let
i\in I
and let
x\in X_{i}
. Clearly
xgg^{-1}\cap X_{i}\neq\varnothing
, and so
xgg^{-1}\in\pi_{X_{i}}(g)
. This gives
(xgg^{-1})\varphi^{-1}\in\pi_{X_{i\beta}}(f)
by
\rm(iii)
, and so
(xgg^{-1})\varphi^{-1}\cap X_{i\beta}\neq\varnothing
. By (i), since
\gamma=\beta\chi^{(f)}
, we have
(i\beta)\chi^{(f)}=i\gamma
, and so
\big((xgg^{-1})\varphi^{-1}\big)f=\{y\}\subseteq X_{i\gamma}
. By the definition of
h
, we therefore have
xh=y\in X_{i\gamma}
. This gives
X_{i}h\subseteq X_{i\gamma}
, and so
i\chi^{(h)}=i\gamma
. Hence
\chi^{(h)}=\gamma
, and thus
h\in T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
.

It is a routine matter to verify that
\pi(h)=\pi(g)
. By (i), we also get
\left(\chi^{(h)},\chi^{(g)}\right)\in\mathcal{R}
in
\mathbb{S}(I)
. Therefore
(h,g)\in\mathcal{R}
in
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
by Theorem
5.6
.

We only require to show that
(f,h)\in\mathcal{L}
in
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
. Note that
\chi^{(h)}=\gamma
. By
\rm(i)
, we have
\chi^{(f)}=\alpha\chi^{(h)}
and
\chi^{(h)}=\beta\chi^{(f)}
. Therefore by Theorem
5.3
, it remains to prove that
X_{i}f\subseteq X_{i\alpha}h
and
X_{i}h\subseteq X_{i\beta}f
for all
i\in I
. For this, let
i\in I
. To prove
X_{i}f\subseteq X_{i\alpha}h
, let
y\in X_{i}f
. Then
yf^{-1}\cap X_{i}\neq\varnothing
, and so
yf^{-1}\in\pi_{X_{i}}(f)
. Therefore by
\rm(ii)
, we have
(yf^{-1})\varphi\in\pi_{X_{i\alpha}}(g)
. This yields
(yf^{-1})\varphi\cap X_{i\alpha}\neq\varnothing
. Choose
z\in(yf^{-1})\varphi\cap X_{i\alpha}
. Then
zgg^{-1}\cap X_{i\alpha}\neq\varnothing
, and so
zgg^{-1}\in\pi_{X_{i\alpha}}(g)
.
Therefore, since
z\in zgg^{-1}
and
z\in(yf^{-1})\varphi
, we obtain
(yf^{-1})\varphi=zgg^{-1}
. This gives
\big((zgg^{-1})\varphi^{-1}\big)f=\{y\}
. By the definition of
h
, we get
zh=y
. Hence
y\in X_{i\alpha}h
, and thus
X_{i}f\subseteq X_{i\alpha}h
.

To prove
X_{i}h\subseteq X_{i\beta}f
, let
y\in X_{i}h
. Then
yh^{-1}\cap X_{i}\neq\varnothing
, and so
yh^{-1}\in\pi_{X_{i}}(h)
. Since
\pi(h)=\pi(g)
, it follows easily that
\pi_{X_{i}}(h)=\pi_{X_{i}}(g)
. Therefore
yh^{-1}\in\pi_{X_{i}}(g)
. By (iii), we then have
(yh^{-1})\varphi^{-1}\in\pi_{X_{i\beta}}(f)
, and so
(yh^{-1})\varphi^{-1}\cap X_{i\beta}\neq\varnothing
. Choose
z\in(yh^{-1})\varphi^{-1}\cap X_{i\beta}
. Then
zff^{-1}\cap X_{i\beta}\neq\varnothing
, and so
zff^{-1}\in\pi_{X_{i\beta}}(f)
. Therefore, since
z\in zff^{-1}
and
z\in(yh^{-1})\varphi^{-1}
, we obtain
(yh^{-1})\varphi^{-1}=zff^{-1}
. This gives
\big((yh^{-1})\varphi^{-1}\big)f=\{zf\}
. By the definition of
h
, we get
zf=y
. Hence
y\in X_{i\beta}f
, and thus
X_{i}h\subseteq X_{i\beta}f
. Thus
(f,h)\in\mathcal{L}
in
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
by Theorem
5.3
. Hence, since
\mathcal{L}\circ\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{D}
, we conclude that
(f,g)\in\mathcal{D}
in
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
.
∎

Proposition 5.10
.

Let
f,g\in T(X,\mathcal{P})
. Then
(f,g)\in\mathcal{D}
in
T(X,\mathcal{P})
if and only if there exist
\gamma\in T(I)
and a bijection
\varphi\colon\pi(f)\to\pi(g)
such that:

(i)

\left(\chi^{(f)},\gamma\right)\in\mathcal{L}
and
\left(\gamma,\chi^{(g)}\right)\in\mathcal{R}
in
T(I)
;

(ii)

for each
i\in I
, there exist
j\in\left(i\chi^{(f)}\right)\gamma^{-1}
and
k\in(i\gamma)\left(\chi^{(f)}\right)^{-1}
such that
\pi_{X_{i}}(f)\varphi\subseteq\pi_{X_{j}}(g)
and
\pi_{X_{i}}(g)\varphi^{-1}\subseteq\pi_{X_{k}}(f)
.

Proof
.

Assume that
(f,g)\in\mathcal{D}
in
T(X,\mathcal{P})
. By Theorem
5.9
, since
T(X,\mathcal{P})=T_{T(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
, there exist
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in T(I)
and a bijection
\varphi\colon\pi(f)\to\pi(g)
such that each condition of Theorem
5.9
holds. Thus (i) follows immediately from Theorem
5.9
(i).

To prove (ii), let
i\in I
. Write
i\alpha=j
and
i\beta=k
. First, we show that
j\in\left(i\chi^{(f)}\right)\gamma^{-1}
. By Theorem
5.9
\rm(i)
, we have
\chi^{(f)}=\alpha\gamma
. Therefore
i\chi^{(f)}=i(\alpha\gamma)=(i\alpha)\gamma=j\gamma
, which gives
j\in\left(i\chi^{(f)}\right)\gamma^{-1}
. Moreover, by Theorem
5.9
\rm(ii)
we deduce that
\pi_{X_{i}}(f)\varphi\subseteq\pi_{X_{j}}(g)
. Next by Theorem
5.9
\rm(i)
, we have
\gamma=\beta\chi^{(f)}
. A similar argument using
\gamma=\beta\chi^{(f)}
establishes that
k\in(i\gamma)\left(\chi^{(f)}\right)^{-1}
. Then by Theorem
5.9
(iii), we deduce that
\pi_{X_{i}}(g)\varphi^{-1}\subseteq\pi_{X_{k}}(f)
. Thus (ii) holds.

Conversely, assume that the given conditions hold. Note from (i) that
\left(\chi^{(f)},\gamma\right)\in\mathcal{L}
. For each
i\in I
, by (ii) we fix
j_{i}\in\left(i\chi^{(f)}\right)\gamma^{-1}
and
k_{i}\in(i\gamma)\left(\chi^{(f)}\right)^{-1}
such that
\pi_{X_{i}}(f)\varphi\subseteq\pi_{X_{j_{i}}}(g)
and
\pi_{X_{i}}(g)\varphi^{-1}\subseteq\pi_{X_{k_{i}}}(f)
. Define
\alpha,\beta\in T(I)
given by
i\alpha=j_{i}
and
i\beta=k_{i}
. Now we show that
\chi^{(f)}=\alpha\gamma
. For this, let
i\in I
. Since
j_{i}\in\left(i\chi^{(f)}\right)\gamma^{-1}
, we obtain
i\chi^{(f)}=j_{i}\gamma=(i\alpha)\gamma=i(\alpha\gamma)
, which yields
\chi^{(f)}=\alpha\gamma
. We can similarly prove that
\gamma=\beta\chi^{(f)}
, since
k_{i}\in(i\gamma)\left(\chi^{(f)}\right)^{-1}
. By (i), we have
(\gamma,\chi^{(g)})\in\mathcal{R}
in
T(I)
. Thus Theorem
5.9
(i) holds. By the definition of
\alpha
and
\beta
, we also deduce that
\pi_{X_{i}}(f)\varphi\subseteq\pi_{X_{i\alpha}}(g)
for all
i\in I
, and
\pi_{X_{i}}(g)\varphi^{-1}\subseteq\pi_{X_{i\beta}}(f)
for all
i\in I
. Thus each condition of Theorem
5.9
holds. Hence, since
T_{T(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})=T(X,\mathcal{P})
, we conclude from Theorem
5.9
that
(f,g)\in\mathcal{D}
in
T(X,\mathcal{P})
.
∎

For every
x\in X

\bar{x}

\mathcal{P}

We require the following result to characterize the relation
\mathcal{J}

Lemma 5.11
.

Let
f,g\in T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
. Then
J_{f}\leq J_{g}
in
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
if and only if there exist
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{S}(I)
and a mapping
\varphi\colon Xg\to X

(i)

for all
i\in I

(ii)

(X_{i}\cap Xg)\varphi\subseteq X_{i\beta}
for all
i\in I\chi^{(g)}
.

Proof
.

Assume that
J_{f}\leq J_{g}
in
T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})
. Then
f=h_{1}gh_{2}
for some

by Lemma

. Write
\chi^{(h_{1})}=\alpha

\chi^{(h_{2})}=\beta

If
Xg\setminus X(h_{1}g)\neq\varnothing

\bar{x}\cap X(h_{1}g)\neq\varnothing

z\in X_{i\beta}

, where
\bar{x}=X_{i}

\displaystyle x\varphi=\begin{cases}xh_{2}&\text{if $x\in X(h_{1}g)$};\\
yh_{2}&\text{if $x\in Xg\setminus X(h_{1}g)$ and $\bar{x}\cap X(h_{1}g)\neq\varnothing$};\\
z&\text{if $x\in Xg\setminus X(h_{1}g)$ and $\bar{x}\cap X(h_{1}g)=\varnothing$}.\end{cases}

f,g\in T_{\mathbb{S}(I)}(X,\mathcal{P})

(f,g)\in\mathcal{J}

Proof
.

It follows directly from Lemma

.

A source reconstruction method in two dimensional radiative transport using boundary data measured on an arc
Hiroshi Fujiwara, Kamran Sadiq, Alexandru Tamasan

Abstract
. We consider an inverse source problem in the stationary radiating transport through a two dimensional absorbing and scattering medium. Of specific interest, the exiting radiation is measured on an arc. The attenuation and scattering properties of the medium are assumed known. For scattering kernels of finite Fourier content in the angular variable, we show how to quantitatively recover the part of the isotropic sources restricted to the convex hull of the measurement arc. The approach is based on the Cauchy problem with partial data for a Beltrami-like equation associated with A A -analytic maps in the sense of Bukhgeim, and extends authors’ previous work to this specific partial data case. The robustness of the method is demonstrated by the results of several numerical experiments.

1.
Introduction

Let
\Omega
be a strictly convex planar domain. In the steady state case, when generated solely by a source
f
inside
\Omega
, the density
u(z,\boldsymbol{\theta})
of particles at
z
traveling in the direction
\boldsymbol{\theta}
through an absorbing and scattering domain
\Omega
solves the stationary transport boundary value problem

(1)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\theta}\cdot\nabla u(z,\boldsymbol{\theta})+a(z,\boldsymbol{\theta})u(z,\boldsymbol{\theta})-\int_{{{\mathbf{S}}^{1}}}k(z,\boldsymbol{\theta},\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\prime})u(z,\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\prime})d\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\prime}=f(z,\boldsymbol{\theta}),\quad(z,\boldsymbol{\theta})\in\Omega\times{{\mathbf{S}}^{1}},

\displaystyle u\lvert_{\Gamma_{-}}=0,

where
\varGamma_{-}:=\{(\zeta,\boldsymbol{\theta})\in\varGamma\times{{\mathbf{S}}^{1}}:\,\nu(\zeta)\cdot\boldsymbol{\theta}<0\}

with
\nu
being the outer unit normal field at the boundary. The boundary condition indicates that no radiation is coming from outside the domain. Throughout, the measure on the circle is normalized to
\int_{{{\mathbf{S}}^{1}}}d\boldsymbol{\theta}=1
.

The boundary value problem (
1
) is known to have a unique solution under various “subcritical” assumptions, e.g.,
[
11
,
9
,
10
,
1
,
22
]
, with a general result in
[
36
]
showing that, for an open and dense set of coefficients
a\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega}\times{{\mathbf{S}}^{1}})
and
k\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega}\times{{\mathbf{S}}^{1}}\times{{\mathbf{S}}^{1}})
,
the problem (
1
) has a unique solution
u\in L^{2}(\Omega\times{{\mathbf{S}}^{1}})
for any
f\in L^{2}(\Omega\times{{\mathbf{S}}^{1}})
. Some of our arguments in the reconstruction method here require solutions
u\in C^{1,\mu}(\overline{\Omega}\times{{\mathbf{S}}^{1}})
,
\frac{1}{2}<\mu<1
. We revisit the arguments in
[
36
]
and show that such a regularity can be achieved for sources
f\in W^{2.p}(\Omega\times{{\mathbf{S}}^{1}})
,
p>4
; see Theorem
2.2
(ii) below.

For an arc
\Lambda
of the boundary of
\Omega
, we consider the inverse problem of the determining
f
from measurements
g
of exiting radiation on
\Lambda
,

(2)

\displaystyle u|_{\Lambda_{+}}=g,

where
\Lambda_{+}:=\{(z,\boldsymbol{\theta})\in\Lambda\times{{\mathbf{S}}^{1}}:\,\nu(z)\cdot\boldsymbol{\theta}>0\}
with
\nu
being the outer unit normal field at the boundary. This is an inverse source problem with partial boundary data, which applies to multiple anisotropic scattering media of non-small anisotropy far from diffusive but also single-scattering regime with applications to Optical Molecular Imaging and Optical Tomography
[
3
,
14
]
.

Except for the results in Section 2, which concern the forward problem, in this work the source and attenuation coefficient are assumed isotropic,
f=f(z)
and
a=a(z)
, and that the scattering kernel
k(z,\boldsymbol{\theta},\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\prime})=k(z,\boldsymbol{\theta}\cdot\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\prime})
depends polynomially on the angle between the directions,

(3)

\displaystyle k(z,\cos\theta)=k_{0}+2\sum_{n=1}^{M}k_{-n}(z)\cos(n\theta),

for some fixed integer
M\geq 1
. Moreover, the functions
a,f,k
are assumed real valued. These assumptions occur naturally in radiative transfer models in Optics, see, e.g.,
[
8
]
.

Our main result, Theorem
4.1
, shows that
u|_{\Lambda_{+}}
determines both
f
and
u
in the convex hull of
\Lambda
, and provides a method of reconstruction. Specific to two dimensional domains, our approach is based on the Cauchy problem with partial data for a Beltrami-like equation associated with
A
-analytic maps in the sense of Bukhgeim
[
7
]
, and extends the authors’ previous work
[
16
]
, which used measurements on the entire boundary, to this specific partial data case. The new insight is that, similar to the non-scattering case
[
18
]
, the trace
u|_{\Lambda}
determines
u|_{L}
provided the scattering kernel has finite Fourier content as in (
3
). The role of the finite Fourier content has been independently recognized in
[
23
]
.

We are aware of only one existing work that reconstructs the source in the presence of scattering using partial boundary data in stationary radiative transport. Namely, in
[
35
]
the domain is a slab and data is available on each side on sufficiently long intervals. In contrast, our method here assumes only “one sided” boundary data, and does not require iterative solvability of the forward problem.

As demonstrated by the numerical experiments in Section
5
, the method is robust in the sense that it reconstructs discontinuous sources, even when embedded in media with a discontinuous absorption property, and a scattering kernel of infinite Fourier content in the angular variable.

4.

\Omega^{+}
of a sufficiently smooth isotropic source

The method of reconstruction proposed here considers the operator
[I-\mathrm{i}H_{t}]
, where
H_{t}
is the finite Hilbert transform

(27)

\displaystyle H_{t}g(x)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-l}^{l}\frac{g(s)}{x-s}ds,\quad x\in(-l,l),

with the integral understood in the sense of principal value.
It is well-known (
[
39
]
) that
\mathrm{i}H_{t}
is a bounded operator in

with spectrum
[-1,1]
, see
[
19
,
29
]
. However,

is not in the point spectrum, see
[
41
]
for a proof based on a Riemann-Hilbert problem. The arguments below (from
[
18
, Proposition 2.1]
) use the unitary property in

of the (infinite) Hilbert transform
H
in (

). We repeat them here for reader’s convenience.

Proposition 4.1
.

In
L^{2}(-l,l)
,

\displaystyle Ker[I-\mathrm{i}H_{t}]=\{0\}
.

Proof.

Let
f\in L^{2}(-l,l)
be extended by zero to the entire real line.
If
f\in Ker[I-\mathrm{i}H_{t}]
, then

|f|=|H_{t}f|=|Hf|
a.e. in
(-l,l)
, and

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(-l,l)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}=\left\|Hf\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}=\int_{-l}^{l}|H_{t}f|^{2}dx+\int_{|x|>l}|Hf(x)|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{-l}^{l}|f|^{2}dx+\int_{|x|>l}|Hf(x)|^{2}dx.

The latter identity shows that
|Hf(x)|=0
, for
|x|>l
. Thus, for
|x|>l
,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\int_{-l}^{l}\frac{f(s)}{x-s}ds=\frac{1}{x}\int_{-l}^{l}f(s)\sum_{j=0}^{{\infty}}\left(\frac{s}{x}\right)^{j}ds=\sum_{j=0}^{{\infty}}\frac{1}{x^{j+1}}\int_{-l}^{l}f(s)s^{j}ds,

yielding
f
orthogonal on any polynomial. By density of polynomials in
L^{2}(-l,l)
,
f=0
.
∎

Recall the boundary value problem (
1

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\theta}\cdot\nabla u(z,\boldsymbol{\theta})+a(z)u(z,\boldsymbol{\theta})-\int_{{{\mathbf{S}}^{1}}}k(z,\boldsymbol{\theta}\cdot\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\prime})u(z,\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\prime})d\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\prime}=f(z),\quad(z,\boldsymbol{\theta})\in\Omega\times{{\mathbf{S}}^{1}},

\displaystyle u\lvert_{\Gamma_{-}}=0,

\displaystyle\overline{\partial}u_{-n}(z)+\partial u_{-n-2}(z)+a(z)u_{-n-1}(z)

In our numerical reconstruction,

The number of triangles (

The attenuation coefficient

The Hilbert transform in (

All the integrations are approximated by the composite mid-point rule

Of particular interest, and key to our procedure, is the numerical computation of the integral equations (

43

which is done via the collocation method with the numerical integration rule

[I-\mathrm{i}H_{t}]u(x_{i})\approx u(x_{i})-\dfrac{\mathrm{i}}{\pi}\sum_{j\neq i}\dfrac{u(x_{j})}{x_{i}-x_{j}}\Delta x.

Except for the implicit regularization due to the discretization, no other regularization method is explicitly employed in numerical implementation. The numerical reconstruction takes approximately
115

by OpenMP parallel computation
on two Xeon E5-2650 v4 (2.20GHz, 12 cores) processors.

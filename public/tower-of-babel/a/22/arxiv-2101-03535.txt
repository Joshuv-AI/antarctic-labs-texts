1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{C}^{n}
be the complex
n
-dimensional space
with the inner product

z\cdot\bar{w}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}z_{j}\overline{w_{j}},\quad z=(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n}),w=(w_{1},\cdots,w_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}

and modulus
|z|=(z\cdot\bar{z})^{\frac{1}{2}}
.
The Fock space
{F}^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
is the set of all entire functions
F
on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that the norm

\|F\|_{{F}^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n})}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}|F(z)|^{2}d\lambda(z)\right)^{1\over 2}<\infty,

where

d\lambda(z):=\pi^{-n}e^{-|z|^{2}}dz

is the Gaussian measure on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
(
[
1
,
2
]
).

Let
\mathbb{N}
be the set of positive integers
and
\mathbb{N}_{0}=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}.
For any
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
we define

e_{\alpha}:=\frac{z^{\alpha}}{\|z^{\alpha}\|_{F^{2}}}=\frac{z^{\alpha}}{\sqrt{\alpha!}}.

Then
\{e_{\alpha}|\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}\}
is an orthonormal basis for
{F}^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
.
The Fock space
{F}^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
is a Hilbert space with the inner product inherited from
L^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n},d\lambda)
.
The fractional Fock-Sobolev space of order
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
is defined by

F^{s,2}({\mathbb{C}^{n}}):=\Big\{f=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}}c_{\alpha}e_{\alpha}:\|f\|_{F^{s,2}({\mathbb{C}^{n}})}<\infty\Big\}

with the norm given by

\|f\|_{F^{s,2}({\mathbb{C}^{n}})}:=\Big[\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}}(2|\alpha|+n)^{s}|c_{\alpha}|^{2}\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}.

The Fock-Sobolev space is a convenient tool for many problems in functional analysis, mathematical physics, and engineering.
We refer to
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
11
,
13
,
18
,
19
]
for an introduction.

In
[
18
]
, Zhu introduced the following integral operator

\displaystyle S_{\varphi}F(z):=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}F(w)e^{z\cdot\bar{w}}\varphi(z-\bar{w})d\lambda(w),

(1)

which recovers (or is linked to) many fundamental examples of integral operators in harmonic analysis and complex analysis with different choices of
\varphi\in F^{2}({\mathbb{C}^{n}})
,
including the Riesz transform on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and the Ahlfors–Beurling operator on
\mathbb{C}
.
Thus, characterizing the boundedness of
S_{\varphi}
is interesting and non-trivial.
In

[
7
]
, it was shown by Wick, Yan and the first, third and fourth authors
that the integral operator
S_{\varphi}
in (
1
)
is bounded on
F^{2}({\mathbb{C}^{n}})
if and only if there exists an
m\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})

such that

\displaystyle\varphi(z)=\left({2\over\pi}\right)^{n\over 2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}m(x)e^{-2\left(x-\frac{i}{2}z\right)^{2}}dx,\ \ \ \ z\in{\mathbb{C}^{n}}.

(2)

Moreover, we have that

\|S_{\varphi}\|_{F^{2}({\mathbb{C}^{n}})\to F^{2}({\mathbb{C}^{n}})}=\|m\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

The purpose of the paper is to contine the line in
[
7
,
18
]
to establish
a boundedness criterion for the integral operator

S_{\varphi}
on the fractional Fock-Sobolev space
F^{s,2}({\mathbb{C}}^{n})
by developing the
multipliers on the fractional Hermite-Sobolev space
W_{H}^{s,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})

(see Section 3 below about the multipliers on the fractional Hermite-Sobolev space
W_{H}^{s,2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
).
Our main result is the following.

Theorem 1
.

Let
s\geq 0
. Then the integral operator
S_{\varphi}
is bounded on
F^{s,2}({\mathbb{C}^{n}})
if and only if
there exists a multiplier
m
on the space
W_{H}^{s,2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
such that

\varphi(z)=\left({2\over\pi}\right)^{n\over 2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}m(x)e^{-2(x-\frac{i}{2}z)^{2}}dx,\qquad z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}.

In particular,
\varphi\in F^{s,2}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
if
S_{\varphi}
is bounded on
F^{s,2}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
.

We would like to mention that in a recent paper

[
16
]
, Wick and Wu obtained an isometry between the Fock-Sobolev space and the Gauss-Sobolev space.
As an application, they used multipliers on the Gauss-Sobolev space to characterize the boundedness of the integral operator

S_{\varphi}
in (
1

Note that when

2.
Preliminaries

Let
H
be the Hermite operator (also called the harmonic oscillator) in the
n
-dimension real space

\mathbb{R}^{n}
, which is defined by

\displaystyle H:=-\Delta+|x|^{2}:=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\partial^{2}\over\partial x_{i}^{2}}+|x|^{2},\quad x=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}).

(3)

The Hermite operator arises naturally in mathematical physics (see
[
10
]
).
For each non-negative integer
k
,
the Hermite polynomials
H_{k}
on
\mathbb{R}
are defined by

H_{k}=e^{x^{2}}\frac{d^{k}}{dx^{k}}\big(e^{-x^{2}}\big)

and by normalization in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, the Hermite functions

h_{k}(x)=(\sqrt{\pi}2^{k}k!)^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{2}}(-1)^{k}H_{k}(x),\qquad x\in{\mathbb{R}}.

It is not difficult to check that

\left(-\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+x^{2}\right)h_{k}(x)=(2k+1)h_{k}(x).

(4)

In the higher dimensions, for each multi-index

\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{n})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
, the Hermite function

h_{\alpha}
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is defined by

h_{\alpha}(x)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}h_{\alpha_{j}}(x_{j}),\hskip 14.22636ptx=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

By (
4
), we see that

Hh_{\alpha}=(2|\alpha|+n)h_{\alpha}.

(5)

That is, these
\{h_{\alpha}\}
are eigenfunctions of the Hermite operator
H
.
Moreover,
\{h_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}}
is an orthonormal basis of

L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Note there is a constant
C>0
such that
\|h_{\alpha}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C
for all

\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
, and for each
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

|\langle f,h_{\alpha}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}|\leq\|H^{m}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}(2|\alpha|+n)^{-m}.

Hence, if
f
is a rapidly decreasing function, then the Hermite series expansion

f=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}}\langle f,h_{\alpha}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}h_{\alpha}

converges to
f
uniformly in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and certainly, also in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Let
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
f\in\mathscr{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. One defines the fractional Hermite operator
H^{s}
by

H^{s}f:=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}}(2|\alpha|+n)^{s}\langle f,h_{\alpha}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}h_{\alpha}.

The fractional Hermite-Sobolev space of order
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
is defined by

W_{H}^{s,2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}}):=\Big\{f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}}):H^{\frac{s}{2}}f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\Big\}

with the norm given by
\|f\|_{W_{H}^{s,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}:=\|H^{\frac{s}{2}}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

(see
[
5
,
8
]
).

For
1\leq j\leq n
, let

H_{j}:=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}+x_{j}\hskip 14.22636pt\mbox{and}\hskip 14.22636ptH_{-j}:=H_{j}^{*}=-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}+x_{j}.

Then it is easy to check that

H=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}[H_{j}H_{-j}+H_{-j}H_{j}].

For any positive integer
k
we define
\widetilde{W}_{H}^{k,2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
as the space of functions
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

such that for every
1\leq|j_{1}|,\cdots,|j_{m}|\leq n
and
1\leq m\leq k,

H_{j_{1}}\cdots H_{j_{m}}f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

The norm on
\widetilde{W}_{H}^{k,2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
is given by

\|f\|_{\widetilde{W}_{H}^{k,2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}:=\sum_{1\leq|j_{1}|,\cdots,|j_{m}|\leq n,1\leq m\leq k}\|H_{j_{1}}\cdots H_{j_{m}}f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}+\|f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}.

Lemma 2

(
[
5
]
Theorem 4)
.

For
k\in\mathbb{N}
we have that
\widetilde{W}_{H}^{k,2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})=W_{H}^{k,2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
. Moreover,
the norms
\|\cdot\|_{\widetilde{W}_{H}^{k,2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}
and
\|\cdot\|_{W_{H}^{k,2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}
are equivalent.

For general integer
s\geq 1
, we can also characterize
W_{H}^{s,2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
with the help of operators
H_{j}
.

Lemma 3
.

For
s\geq 1
, there holds

\|f\|_{W_{H}^{s,2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}\approx\sum_{1\leq|j|\leq n}\|H_{j}f\|_{W_{H}^{s-1,2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}+\|f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}.

(6)

Proof.

It is obvious that (
6
) holds for
s\in\mathbb{N}
by Lemma
2
. Assume
k<s<k+1
, where
k\geq 1
is an integer.
Applying (
6
) to the norm
\|\cdot\|_{W_{H}^{k,2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}
indicates

\|f\|_{W_{H}^{s,2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}=\|H^{s-k\over 2}f\|_{W_{H}^{k,2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}\approx\sum_{j}\|H_{j}H^{s-k\over 2}f\|_{W_{H}^{k-1,2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}+\|f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}.

It follows from Lemma 4 in
[
5
]
that

H_{j}H^{s-k\over 2}f=(H+2)^{s-k\over 2}H_{j}f,\quad 1\leq j\leq n,

(7)

and

H_{j}H^{s-k\over 2}f=(H-2)^{s-k\over 2}H_{j}f,\quad-n\leq j\leq-1.

(8)

By (
7
), (
8
) and the definition of the
{W_{H}^{k,2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}
-norm, we have

\displaystyle\|H_{j}H^{s-k\over 2}f\|_{W_{H}^{k-1,2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|(H\pm 2)^{s-k\over 2}H_{j}f\|_{W_{H}^{k-1,2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|H^{k-1\over 2}(H\pm 2)^{s-k\over 2}H_{j}f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha}(2|\alpha|+n)^{k-1}(2|\alpha|+n\pm 2)^{s-k}|\langle H_{j}f,h_{\alpha}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle\approx\sum_{\alpha}(2|\alpha|+n)^{s-1}|\langle H_{j}f,h_{\alpha}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|H_{j}f\|_{W_{H}^{s-1,2}}^{2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}}).

Thus,

\|f\|_{W_{H}^{s,2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}\approx\sum_{j}\|H_{j}f\|_{W_{H}^{s-1,2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}+\|f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}.

The proof of Lemma
3
is complete.
∎

For any
a=(a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{n})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
we use
\tau_{a}
to denote the operator of translation by
a
, namely,

\tau_{a}f(x)=f(x-a)
.

Lemma 4
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
a\in\mathbb{R}
, and
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
. Then

H_{j_{1}}\cdots H_{j_{m}}\tau_{a}f(x)=\sum_{l\leq m}p_{l}(a)\tau_{a}H_{j^{\prime}_{1}}\cdots H_{j^{\prime}_{l}}f(x),

where

1\leq|j_{1}|,\cdots,|j_{m}|\leq n,1\leq|j^{\prime}_{1}|,\cdots,|j^{\prime}_{l}|\leq n,

and
p_{l}(\cdot)
is a polynomial of order
l,\ 1\leq l\leq m.

Proof.

For
1\leq|j|\leq n
,

\displaystyle H_{j}\tau_{a}f(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pm\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{|j|}}f(x-a)+x_{|j|}f(x-a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pm\frac{\partial}{\partial(x-a)_{|j|}}f(x-a)

\displaystyle\qquad+(x_{|j|}-a_{|j|})f(x-a)+a_{|j|}f(x-a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H_{j}f(x-a)+a_{|j|}f(x-a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau_{a}[H_{j}+a_{|j|}]f(x).

By mathematical induction, we obtain the desired result.
∎

The Bargmann transform is the classical tool in mathematics analysis and mathematical physics
(see
[
1
,
2
,
11
,
13
,
19
]
and references therein).
Consider
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
, and define

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}f(z)

\displaystyle:=\left({2\over\pi}\right)^{n\over 4}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}f(x)e^{2x\cdot z-x^{2}-{z^{2}\over 2}}dx

\displaystyle=\left({2\over\pi}\right)^{n\over 4}e^{{z^{2}\over 2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}f(x)e^{-(x-z)^{2}}dx,\ \ \ \ z\in{\mathbb{C}^{n}}.

(9)

For
s\geq 0

it is clear that
W_{H}^{s,2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})\subset L^{2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
, so
\mathcal{B}f
is well-defined on
W_{H}^{s,2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
. Also, it
is well known that, for

f=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}}c_{\alpha}h_{\alpha},

we have

\mathcal{B}f=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}}c_{\alpha}\mathcal{B}h_{\alpha}=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}}c_{\alpha}e_{\alpha}.

Consequently, the Bargmann transform is a unitary operator from
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
to
F^{2}({\mathbb{C}^{n}})
, and it is also
a unitary operator from the fractional Hermite-Sobolev space
W_{H}^{s,2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
to the fractional Fock-Sobolev
space
F^{s,2}({\mathbb{C}^{n}})
.

There is an equivalent definition for the Fock-Sobolev spaces, that is, so called the weighted Fock spaces. Given a real
number
s
we define
F_{s}^{2}({\mathbb{C}^{n}})
as the space of entire functions
f
on
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
with

\|f\|_{F_{s}^{2}({\mathbb{C}^{n}})}^{2}=\omega_{n,s}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}|(1+|z|)^{2s}|f(z)|^{2}e^{-|z|^{2}}\,dz<\infty,

where
\omega_{n,s}
is a normalizing constant such that the constant function 1 has norm 1.
It follows from Lemma
5
below that the fractional Fock-Sobolev spaces are the same as these
weighted Fock spaces whose definition does not involve derivatives. Sometimes, it is more convenient to
study function theoretic and operator theoretic properties on the weighted Fock spaces instead of the
Fock-Sobolev spaces.

Lemma 5

(
[
9
]
Theorem1.2)
.

For
s\in\mathbb{R}
we have
F^{s,2}({\mathbb{C}^{n}})=F_{s}^{2}({\mathbb{C}^{n}})
with equivalent norms.

Recall that the Weyl operators
W_{a}
,
a\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
, are defined by

W_{a}f(z):=f(z-a)e^{-\frac{|a|^{2}}{2}+z\cdot\bar{a}}.

Lemma 6
.

Suppose
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
a\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
. Then
W_{a}
is bounded on
F^{s,2}({\mathbb{C}^{n}})
. Moreover, for all
f\in F^{s,2}({\mathbb{C}^{n}})

\|W_{a}f\|_{F^{s,2}({\mathbb{C}^{n}})}\leq C(1+|a|^{|s|})\,\|f\|_{F^{s,2}({\mathbb{C}^{n}})}.

Proof.

By Lemma
5
, we have that for any
f\in F^{s,2}({\mathbb{C}^{n}})
,

\displaystyle\|W_{a}f\|_{F^{s,2}({\mathbb{C}^{n}})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|W_{a}f\|_{F^{2}_{s}({\mathbb{C}^{n}})}^{2}

\displaystyle=C\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}(1+|z|)^{2s}|f(z-a)|^{2}e^{-|z-a|^{2}}\,dz

\displaystyle=C\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}(1+|z+a|)^{2s}|f(z)|^{2}e^{-|z|^{2}}\,dz.

Note that

1+|z+a|\leq 1+|z|+|a|\leq(1+|z|)(1+|a|).

Also

1+|z|=1+|(z+a)-a|\leq(1+|z+a|)(1+|a|)

and so

(1+|z+a|)^{-1}\leq(1+a)(1+|z|)^{-1}.

Thus for
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
,

\displaystyle\|W_{a}f\|_{F^{s,2}({\mathbb{C}^{n}})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+|a|)^{2|s|}\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}(1+|z|)^{2s}|f(z)|^{2}e^{-|z|^{2}}\,dz

\displaystyle\leq C(1+|a|)^{2|s|}\|f\|^{2}_{F^{s,2}({\mathbb{C}^{n}})}.

The proof of Lemma
6
is complete.
∎

Multipliers on the fractional Hermite-Sobolev spaces

Lemma 9

If

Proof.

It suffices to show that for

Denote by
\psi(z)=z^{-s}(1-e^{-z^{2}})^{K}

It follows from the spectral theory (
[
17

) that for any

\displaystyle\|G_{s,K,H}(H^{-s/2}f)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}

\displaystyle=\Big\{\int_{0}^{\infty}\|\psi(t\sqrt{H})f\|_{L^{2}(X)}^{2}{dt\over t}\Big\}^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\Big\{\int_{0}^{\infty}\big\langle\,\overline{\psi}(t\sqrt{H})\,\psi(t\sqrt{H})f,f\big\rangle{dt\over t}\Big\}^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\Big\{\big\langle\int_{0}^{\infty}|\psi|^{2}(t\sqrt{H}){dt\over t}f,f\big\rangle\Big\}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\kappa\|f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})},

(12)

where

.
This shows that

On the other hand, from the spectral theory (
[
17
]
) we see that for any

Hence for every

\displaystyle=c\left|\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\langle(t^{2}H)^{-s}(1-e^{-t^{2}H})^{2K}f,g\rangle{dt\over t}\right|

\displaystyle=c\left|\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\langle(t^{2}H)^{-s/2}(1-e^{-t^{2}H})^{K}f,(t^{2}H)^{-s/2}(1-e^{-t^{2}H})^{K}g\rangle{dt\over t}\right|

which shows that

This proves the lemma.

Lemma 11
.

\mathcal{M}(W_{H}^{s,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}))\subset\mathcal{M}(W_{H}^{t,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}))
if
s\geq t\geq 0
. In particular,

\mathcal{M}(W_{H}^{s,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}))\subset L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})

Proof.

Let
C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
be the space of smooth functions with compact support on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
Suppose
m\in\mathcal{M}(W_{H}^{s,2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}}))
, then for any positive integer

and any

we have

\|m^{N}f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}^{\frac{1}{N}}\leq\|m^{N}f\|_{W_{H}^{s,2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}^{\frac{1}{N}}\leq\|m\|_{\mathcal{M}(W_{H}^{s,2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}}))}\|f\|_{W_{H}^{s,2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}^{\frac{1}{N}}.

In addition, for
\Omega:=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}:\ |m(x)|>\|m\|_{\mathcal{M}(W_{H}^{s,2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}}))}+1\}
we have

\|m^{N}f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}^{\frac{1}{N}}\geq(\|m\|_{\mathcal{M}(W_{H}^{s,2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}}))}+1)\Big(\int_{\Omega}|f(x)|^{2}dx\Big)^{1\over 2N}.

We first claim
m\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. If it’s not true, then
\Omega

is of positive measure and hence one can find a proper function
f\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
such that
\int_{\Omega}|f(x)|^{2}dx>0
. Let

,
then there is a obvious contradiction.

The left of the proof is to use interpolation to the operator of multiplication by
m
between
W_{H}^{s,2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
and
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
.

For

, let

Lemma 12
.

For

, we have that

Lemma 13

(Leibniz’s Rule)
.

Let
s\geq 0
. Then for any
f,g\in\widetilde{W}_{H}^{s,2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
and any integer
k\leq s
, we have the generalized Leibniz’s rule

\displaystyle H_{j_{1}}\cdots H_{j_{k}}(fg)

\displaystyle=\sum_{h+l+m=k}\sum_{1\leq|j^{\prime}_{1}|\leq n,\cdots,1\leq|j^{\prime}_{l}|\leq n}\sum_{1\leq|j^{\prime\prime}_{1}|\leq n,\cdots,1\leq|j^{\prime\prime}_{m}|\leq n}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad p_{h}(x)(H_{j^{\prime}_{1}}\cdots H_{j^{\prime}_{l}}f)(H_{j^{\prime\prime}_{1}}\cdots H_{j^{\prime\prime}_{m}}g)

\displaystyle=\sum_{|\alpha|+m=k}\sum_{1\leq|j^{\prime}_{1}|\leq n,\cdots,1\leq|j^{\prime}_{m}|\leq n}(\frac{\partial^{\alpha}}{\partial x^{\alpha}}f)(H_{j^{\prime}_{1}}\cdots H_{j^{\prime}_{m}}g)

\displaystyle=\sum_{h+|\alpha|+|\beta|=k}p_{h}(\frac{\partial^{\alpha}}{\partial x^{\alpha}}f)(\frac{\partial^{\beta}}{\partial x^{\beta}}g),

where
p_{h}
is a polynomial in
x
of order
h
.

Proof.

We only give the proof for the first equality since the other two equalities can be
obtained by a minor modifications with it.

For any
1\leq|j|\leq n,

\displaystyle H_{j}(fg)

\displaystyle=\pm\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{|j|}}(fg)+x_{|j|}fg

\displaystyle=\pm[(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{|j|}}f)g+f(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{|j|}}g)]+x_{|j|}fg

\displaystyle=[\pm(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{|j|}}f)+x_{|j|}f]g+f[\pm(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{|j|}}g)+x_{|j|}g]-x_{|j|}fg

\displaystyle=(H_{j}f)g+f(H_{j}g)-x_{|j|}fg.

Assume the first equality holds for any
k^{\prime}<k
, and for convenience, we omit the subscripts of the sum.
Then for any
1\leq|j|\leq n
,

\displaystyle H_{j}H_{j_{1}}\cdots H_{j_{k^{\prime}}}(fg)

\displaystyle=H_{j}\sum p_{h}(x)\cdot(H_{j^{\prime}_{1}}\cdots H_{j^{\prime}_{l}}f)(H_{j^{\prime\prime}_{1}}\cdots H_{j^{\prime\prime}_{m}}g)]

\displaystyle=\sum H_{j}[p_{h}(x)\cdot(H_{j^{\prime}_{1}}\cdots H_{j^{\prime}_{l}}f)(H_{j^{\prime\prime}_{1}}\cdots H_{j^{\prime\prime}_{m}}g)]

\displaystyle=\sum\pm\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{|j|}}[p_{h}(x)\cdot(H_{j^{\prime}_{1}}\cdots H_{j^{\prime}_{l}}f)(H_{j^{\prime\prime}_{1}}\cdots H_{j^{\prime\prime}_{m}}g)]

\displaystyle\quad+x_{|j|}p_{h}(x)\cdot(H_{j^{\prime}_{1}}\cdots H_{j^{\prime}_{l}}f)(H_{j^{\prime\prime}_{1}}\cdots H_{j^{\prime\prime}_{m}}g)

\displaystyle=\sum[\pm\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{|j|}}p_{h}(x)]\cdot(H_{j^{\prime}_{1}}\cdots H_{j^{\prime}_{l}}f)(H_{j^{\prime\prime}_{1}}\cdots H_{j^{\prime\prime}_{m}}g)

\displaystyle\quad+p_{h}(x)\cdot[\pm\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{|j|}}(H_{j^{\prime}_{1}}\cdots H_{j^{\prime}_{l}}f)](H_{j^{\prime\prime}_{1}}\cdots H_{j^{\prime\prime}_{m}}g)

\displaystyle\quad+p_{h}(x)\cdot(H_{j^{\prime}_{1}}\cdots H_{j^{\prime}_{l}}f)[\pm\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{|j|}}(H_{j^{\prime\prime}_{1}}\cdots H_{j^{\prime\prime}_{m}}g)]

\displaystyle\quad+x_{|j|}p_{h}(x)\cdot(H_{j^{\prime}_{1}}\cdots H_{j^{\prime}_{l}}f)(H_{j^{\prime\prime}_{1}}\cdots H_{j^{\prime\prime}_{m}}g)

\displaystyle=\sum[p_{h-1}(x)+x_{|j|}p_{h}(x)]\cdot(H_{j^{\prime}_{1}}\cdots H_{j^{\prime}_{l}}f)(H_{j^{\prime\prime}_{1}}\cdots H_{j^{\prime\prime}_{m}}g)

\displaystyle\quad+p_{h}(x)\cdot[(H_{j}-x_{|j|})(H_{j^{\prime}_{1}}\cdots H_{j^{\prime}_{l}}f)](H_{j^{\prime\prime}_{1}}\cdots H_{j^{\prime\prime}_{m}}g)

\displaystyle\quad+p_{h}(x)\cdot(H_{j^{\prime}_{1}}\cdots H_{j^{\prime}_{l}}f)[(H_{j}-x_{|j|})(H_{j^{\prime\prime}_{1}}\cdots H_{j^{\prime\prime}_{m}}g)]

\displaystyle=\sum[p_{h-1}(x)-p_{h+1}(x)]\cdot(H_{j^{\prime}_{1}}\cdots H_{j^{\prime}_{l}}f)(H_{j^{\prime\prime}_{1}}\cdots H_{j^{\prime\prime}_{m}}g)

\displaystyle\quad+p_{h}(x)\cdot(H_{j}H_{j^{\prime}_{1}}\cdots H_{j^{\prime}_{l}}f)(H_{j^{\prime\prime}_{1}}\cdots H_{j^{\prime\prime}_{m}}g)

\displaystyle\quad+p_{h}(x)\cdot(H_{j^{\prime}_{1}}\cdots H_{j^{\prime}_{l}}f)(H_{j}H_{j^{\prime\prime}_{1}}\cdots H_{j^{\prime\prime}_{m}}g),

where
h+l+m+1=k^{\prime}+1

Proposition 14
.

Let
k>n/2
be an integer. Then we have

(i)

is an algebra and

.

(ii)

W^{k,2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})\subset\mathcal{M}(W_{H}^{k,2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}}))
.

(iii)

\mathcal{M}(W^{k,2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}}))\subset\mathcal{M}(W_{H}^{k,2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}}))

Proof.

Since

\displaystyle H_{j_{1}}\cdots H_{j_{k^{\prime}}}(fg)

\displaystyle=\sum_{h+l+m=k^{\prime}}\sum_{1\leq|j^{\prime}_{1}|\leq n,\cdots,1\leq|j^{\prime}_{l}|\leq n}\sum_{1\leq|j^{\prime\prime}_{1}|\leq n,\cdots,1\leq|j^{\prime\prime}_{m}|\leq n}p_{h}(x)

\displaystyle\|\eta(x+m)\|_{\widetilde{W}_{H}^{k,2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}

. Then

\varphi(z)=C\mathcal{F}^{-1}[m(x-\frac{1}{2}v)e^{-2x^{2}}](u)e^{|u|^{2}\over 2}.

Notice that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(1+|\xi|)^{2s}|\mathcal{F}^{-1}f(\xi)|^{2}d\xi\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{W}^{s,2}}^{2}\leq C\|f\|_{W_{H}^{s,2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}^{2},

and from (
10
),

\displaystyle\|m(x-\frac{1}{2}v)e^{-2x^{2}}\|_{W_{H}^{s,2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\big(1+{|v|\over 2}\big)^{2s}\|m(x)e^{-2(x+\frac{1}{2}v)^{2}}\|_{W_{H}^{s,2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|m\|_{\mathcal{M}(W_{H}^{s,2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}}))}^{2}\big(1+{|v|\over 2}\big)^{2s}\|e^{-2(x+\frac{1}{2}v)^{2}}\|_{W_{H}^{s,2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|m\|_{\mathcal{M}(W_{H}^{s,2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}}))}^{2}\big(1+{|v|\over 2}\big)^{4s}\|e^{-2x^{2}}\|_{W_{H}^{s,2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}^{2}.

By Lemma
5
, we have

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|_{F^{s,2}({\mathbb{C}^{n}})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}(1+|z|)^{2s}|\varphi(z)|^{2}e^{-|z|^{2}}dz

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(1+|u|)^{2s}(1+|v|)^{2s}|\varphi(u+iv)|^{2}e^{-(|u|^{2}+|v|^{2})}dudv

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(1+|v|)^{2s}e^{-|v|^{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(1+|u|)^{2s}|\mathcal{F}^{-1}[m(x-\frac{1}{2}v)e^{-2x^{2}}](u)|^{2}dudv

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(1+|v|)^{2s}e^{-|v|^{2}}\|m(x-\frac{1}{2}v)e^{-2x^{2}}\|_{W_{H}^{s,2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}^{2}dv

\displaystyle\leq C\|m\|_{\mathcal{M}(W_{H}^{s,2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}}))}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(1+|v|)^{2s}\big(1+{|v|\over 2}\big)^{4s}e^{-|v|^{2}}dv

\displaystyle\leq C\|m\|_{\mathcal{M}(W_{H}^{s,2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}}))}^{2}.

This proves
\varphi\in F^{s,2}({\mathbb{C}^{n}})
. The proof of Proposition
19
is complete.
∎

Finally, we are ready to prove our Theorem
1
.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

First, we assume that

\varphi(z)=\left({2\over\pi}\right)^{n\over 2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}m(x)e^{-2(x-\frac{i}{2}z)^{2}}dx,\qquad z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}

where
m
is a multiplier on the space
W_{H}^{s,2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}}),s\geq 0
.
Let
S_{\varphi}
be an integral operator as in (
1
). To prove that
S_{\varphi}

is bounded on the space
F^{s,2}({\mathbb{C}^{n}}),

we notice that from Proposition
19
,
\varphi\in F^{s,2}({\mathbb{C}^{n}})
and

S_{\varphi}=\mathcal{B}T\mathcal{B}^{-1},

where
T
is given by

\mathcal{F}(Tf)(x)=m(x)\mathcal{F}(f)(x)\hskip 11.38109pt\text{for all}\hskip 11.38109ptf\in W^{s,2}_{H}({\mathbb{R}^{n}}).

By Lemma
7
, the operator
T
is bounded on the space

W^{s,2}_{H}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
. From the properties of the operators
\mathcal{B}
and

\mathcal{B}^{-1}
, we see that
S_{\varphi}
is bounded on the space
F^{s,2}({\mathbb{C}^{n}}).

Conversely, let
S_{\varphi}
be a bounded operator on
F^{s,2}({\mathbb{C}^{n}})
as in (
1
) . Then
from the properties of the operators
\mathcal{B}
and

\mathcal{B}^{-1}
, we have that

T=\mathcal{B}^{-1}S_{\varphi}\mathcal{B}

is bounded on
W_{H}^{s,2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
. Note that
S_{\varphi}W_{a}f=W_{a}S_{\varphi}f
for any
a\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
f\in F^{s,2}({\mathbb{C}^{n}})
.
It follows that
T\tau_{a}f=\tau_{a}Tf
for any
a\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
f\in W_{H}^{s,2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
.
Thus by Proposition
18
, there is an
m\in\mathcal{M}(W_{H}^{s,2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}}))
such that
\mathcal{F}(Tf)=m\mathcal{F}f
.
This implies

S_{\varphi}=\mathcal{B}\mathcal{F}^{-1}M_{m}\mathcal{F}\mathcal{B}^{-1}.

By Proposition
19
,
\mathcal{B}\mathcal{F}^{-1}M_{m}\mathcal{F}\mathcal{B}^{-1}

is an integral operator
S_{\varphi_{0}}
, where

\varphi_{0}(z)=\left({2\over\pi}\right)^{n\over 2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}m(x)e^{-2(x-\frac{i}{2}z)^{2}}dx\in F^{s,2}({\mathbb{C}^{n}}).

This implies for all
g\in F^{s,2}({\mathbb{C}}^{n})
, there holds

\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}g(w)e^{z\cdot\bar{w}}(\varphi(z-\bar{w})-\varphi_{0}(z-\bar{w}))d\lambda(w)=0,\qquad z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}.

To finish the proof, it suffices to show that
\varphi=\varphi_{0}
. Taking
z=0
in the above equality,
we see that for all
g\in F^{s,2}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
,

\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}g(w)(\varphi(-\bar{w})-\varphi_{0}(-\bar{w}))\,d\lambda(w)=0.

Write
\psi(w)=\varphi(-w)-\varphi_{0}(-w)\in F^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
. Then
\psi
has the series expansion

\psi(w)=\sum\limits_{\alpha}c_{\alpha}e_{\alpha}(w)=\sum\limits_{\alpha}c_{\alpha}\left({1\over\alpha!}\right)^{1\over 2}\,w^{\alpha}

with
\sum_{\alpha}|c_{\alpha}|^{2}=\|\psi\|^{2}_{F^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n})}
. Letting
g=e_{\alpha}
for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
,
we obtain

\displaystyle c_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}e_{\alpha}(w)\psi({\bar{w}})d\lambda(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}e_{\alpha}(w)(\varphi(-\bar{w})-\varphi_{0}(-\bar{w}))d\lambda(w)=0.

This shows
\varphi=\varphi_{0}
. Hence, the proof of Theorem
1
is complete.
∎

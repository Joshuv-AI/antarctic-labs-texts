Towards a characterization of the inverse systems of complete intersections.
J. Elias

Abstract
In this paper we give conditions on a homogeneous polynomial for which the associated graded Artin algebra is a complete intersection.

Mathematics Subject Classification
.

Key words and Phrases:
Number of generators, Artin Gorenstein local rings, Inverse system.

Introduction

We denote by
S={\bf k}[x_{0},\dots,x_{n}]
the polynomial ring on the variables
x_{0},\dots,x_{n}
over an algebraically closed field
{\bf k}
of characteristic zero.
We denote the homogeneous maximal ideal of
S
by
{\mathfrak{m}}=(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})
.

Macaulay’s duality, instance of Matlis’ duality, establish a reversing one-to-one correspondence between the Artin Gorenstein quotients
A=S/I
and their inverse system
I^{\perp}=\langle H\rangle
, see Proposition
1.1
.
In this paper we address the following problem:
characterize the polynomials
H
such that its Macaulay’s dual
I=\operatorname{Ann_{R}}\langle H\rangle
is a complete intersection, see
[
8
, Chapter 9, L]
.
Very few instances of this problem are known. For example, if
H
is a monomial then
\operatorname{Ann_{R}}\langle H\rangle
is a monomial complete intersection.
In
[
7
]
the case of quadratic complete intersections is considered; see
[
7
]
and its reference list for some other known cases.

In this paper we give explicit conditions on a homogeneous polynomial
H
for which
I=\operatorname{Ann_{R}}\langle H\rangle
is a complete intersection, Proposition
2.2
.
The key idea of this paper is to consider the resultant of a family of homogeneous forms

{\underline{f}}=(f_{0},\dots,d_{n})
and the determinant of its jacobian matrix.
Recall that the resultant of a family of homogeneous forms is the generator of a closed projective hypersurface,
[
4
]
.
In the case considered in this paper, we get a locally constructible set, Proposition
2.4
.

In the second section we characterize the polynomials
H
such that its Macaulay’s dual
I=\operatorname{Ann_{R}}\langle H\rangle
is a complete intersection, Proposition
2.2
.
In the third section we apply this result to short algebras.

Preliminaries.

Next, we fix some notations and we recall the basic results on Macaulay inverse systems,
see
[
8
]
for more results on inverse systems.

Let
\Gamma={\bf k}[y_{0},\dots,y_{n}]
be the polynomial ring on the variables
y_{0},\dots,y_{n}
.
This polynomial ring can be considered a
S
-module by derivation that we denote by
\circ
, defined by

\begin{array}[]{ cccc}\circ:&S\times\Gamma&\longrightarrow&\Gamma\\
&(f,g)&\to&f\circ g=f(\partial_{y_{1}},\dots,\partial_{y_{n}})(g)\end{array}

where
\partial_{y_{i}}
denotes the partial derivative with respect to
y_{i}
;

S
is the injective hull of the residue field
{\bf k}
as
S
-module,
[
5
]
.

Given a polynomial
H\in\Gamma
we denote by

\langle H\rangle
the sub-
S
-module of
\Gamma
generated by

H
.
Notice that
\langle H\rangle
is the
{\bf k}
-vector subspace of
\Gamma
generated
by
H
and its derivatives of any order.
On the other hand, we denote by
\langle H\rangle_{\bf k}
the one-dimensional
{\bf k}
-vector space generated by
H
.

Proposition 1.1

(Macaulay-Matlis duality,
[
8
]
)
.

There is a order-reversing bijection
\perp
between the set of finitely generated (graded) sub-
S
-submodules of
\Gamma
and the set of (homogeneous)
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideals of
S
given by:
if
M
is a submodule of
\Gamma
then
M^{\perp}=(0:_{S}M)\subset S
, and
I^{\perp}=(0:_{\Gamma}I)\subset\Gamma
for an ideal
I\subset S
.
An ideal
I
is Gorenstein if and only in
I^{\perp}
is generated by a single polynomial
F\in\Gamma
.

I^{\perp}
is the so-called Macaulay’s inverse system of
I
.

Given an Artinian graded quotient
A=S/I

we denote by
\operatorname{soc}(A)=(0:_{A}{\mathfrak{m}})
the socle of
A
.
The ring
A
is Gorenstein if and only if
\operatorname{dim}_{\bf k}(\operatorname{soc}(A))=1
, if this is the case then
\operatorname{soc}(A)
is a principal ideal of
A
generated by a homogeneous element of degree
s
. This integer is the socle degree of
A
.
By Macaulay’s duality there exists a homogeneous element
F
of degree
s
such that
I^{\perp}=\langle F\rangle
.

The examples of this paper are done by using the Singular library
[
2
]
,

[
1
]
.

2
Characterization of complete intersections

The following result is a consequence of
[
9
]
, but in the Artinian case we can give an easier proof by using Macaulay’s duality.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
A=S/K
be a graded Gorenstein ring of socle degree
s
, and let
J
be a homogeneous ideal such that
K\subset J
.
If
\operatorname{soc}(R/J)_{s}\neq 0
then
K=J
.

Proof.

We have
J^{\perp}\subset K^{\perp}=\langle F\rangle

with
F
a homogeneous form of degree
s
.
Since
\operatorname{soc}(R/J)_{s}\neq 0
there exist a homogeneous polynomial
G\in J^{\perp}\setminus\{0\}
with
\operatorname{deg}(G)=s
.
Hence
0\neq G\in K^{\perp}_{s}=\langle F\rangle_{\bf k}
.
Since
\operatorname{dim}_{\bf k}\langle F\rangle_{\bf k}=1
there exist

\lambda\in{\bf k}\setminus\{0\}
such that

G=\lambda F
.
From this we get that

K^{\perp}=\langle F\rangle=\langle G\rangle\subset J^{\perp}.

Hence
K^{\perp}=J^{\perp}
and by Macaulay’s duality
K=J
.
∎

We say that a homogeneous ideal
I\subset S
is a complete intersection of type
{\underline{d}}=(d_{0},\dots,d_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{d+1}
, with
2\leq d_{0}\leq\dots\leq d_{n}
,
if there exists homogeneous regular sequence
{\underline{f}}=\{f_{0},\dots,f_{n}\}
minimally generating
I
such that
\operatorname{deg}(f_{i})=d_{i}
,
i=0,\dots,n
.

Given an integer
d\geq 1
we denote by
\mathbb{P}_{{\bf k}}^{N(d)}
the projective space parametrizating the forms of degree
d
on
x_{0},\dots,x_{n}
, where
N(d)=\binom{n+d}{n}-1
.
Given integers
2\leq d_{0}\leq\dots\leq d_{n}
we consider the multi-projective space

{\mathbb{V}}=\prod_{i=0}^{n}\mathbb{P}_{{\bf k}}^{N(d_{i})}

parameterizing the
(n+1)
-pla of homogeneous forms
{\underline{F}}=\{F_{0},\dots,F_{n}\}
, where
F_{i}
is a homogeneous form of degree
d_{i}
,
i=0,\dots,n
.
Recall that the resultant
\operatorname{Res}({\underline{F}})
defines an irreducible hypersurface of
\mathbb{V}
,

[
4
, Chapitre 5, Section 5.3]
.
In fact,
\operatorname{Res}({\underline{F}})
is the generator of the defining ideal of the variety of
\mathbb{V}
obtained by projection on the first component of the incidence variety

\{({\underline{F}},p)\in{\mathbb{V}}\times{\mathbb{P}}_{\bf k}^{n}\mid F_{i}(p)=0,i=0,\dots,n\}
.
The resultant
\operatorname{Res}({\underline{F}})
is a polynomial of degree
\prod_{i\neq j}d_{j}
on the coefficients of
F_{i}
,

i=0,\dots,n
,
[
4
, Proposition 5.21]
.
The resultant can be computed effectively,

[
4
, Algorithme 5.22]
, and can be a dense polynomial, see
[
4
, Example 5.20]
.

Given a
n+1
-pla
{\underline{f}}=(f_{0},\dots,f_{n})
of homogeneous elements of
S
of degree
d_{0},\dots,d_{n}
the ideal
({\underline{f}})
is
{\mathfrak{m}}
-primary if and only if
\operatorname{Res}({\underline{f}})\neq 0
,
[
4
, Proposition 5.17]
.
These conditions are equivalent to
\operatorname{dim}_{\bf k}(S/({\underline{f}}))<\infty
, or that
{\underline{f}}
is a maximal regular sequence of
S
.

The main result of this paper is the following:

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
H\in S\setminus\{0\}
be a homogeneous polynomial.
Then the ideal
\operatorname{Ann_{R}}\langle H\rangle
is a complete intersection of type

{\underline{d}}=(d_{0},\dots,d_{n})
if and only if there exists homogeneous forms

{\underline{f}}=\{f_{0},\dots,f_{n}\}\subset S
such that
\operatorname{deg}(f_{i})=d_{i}
,
i=0,\dots,n
, and

(i)

\operatorname{Res}({\underline{f}})\neq 0
,

(ii)

{\underline{f}}\circ H=0
, and

(iii)

\operatorname{det}(\operatorname{Jac}({\underline{f}}))\circ H\neq 0
.

If these equivalent conditions hold then

\operatorname{Ann_{R}}\langle H\rangle=(f_{0},\dots,f_{n})
and

\operatorname{deg}(H)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}d_{i}-(n+1).

Proof.

Assume that
K=\operatorname{Ann_{R}}\langle H\rangle
is a complete intersection ideal generated by a homogeneous regular sequence
{\underline{f}}=\{f_{0},\dots,f_{n}\}\in S
of type
(d_{0},\dots,d_{n})
.
Since
(f_{0},\dots,f_{n})\subset K
, by Macaulay duality we get
(ii)
.
Being
\{f_{0},\dots,f_{n}\}
a regular sequence we have
\operatorname{Res}({\underline{f}})\neq 0
,
[
11
]
or
[
4
, Proposition 5.17]
.
By hypothesis
K
is a complete intersection, so from
[
10
]
, see also
[
12
, Theorem 2.4]
, we have that the coset of
\operatorname{det}(\operatorname{Jac}({\underline{f}}))

in
S/K
is a generator of its socle, in particular
\operatorname{det}(\operatorname{Jac}({\underline{f}}))\circ H\neq 0
.

Let now
{\underline{f}}=\{f_{1},\dots,f_{n}\}\in S
be a sequence of homogeneous elements of
S
satisfying the conditions
(i)
,
(ii)
and
(iii)
.
From
(i)
we get that
{\underline{f}}
is a regular sequence,
[
11
]
or
[
4
, Proposition 5.17]
.
From
(ii)
and Macaulay’s duality we get that
K=(f_{0},\dots,f_{n})\subset J=\operatorname{Ann_{R}}\langle H\rangle
, so we can consider the natural projection

\frac{S}{K}\longrightarrow\frac{S}{J}

(1)

Being
K
a complete intersection of height
n+1
the socle of
S/K
is concentrated in a single degree

s=\sum_{i=0}^{n}d_{i}-(n+1)
, and it is generated by the coset of
\operatorname{det}(\operatorname{Jac}({\underline{f}}))
,

[
10
]
, see also
[
12
, Theorem 2.4]
.
By (
1
) the coset of
\operatorname{det}(\operatorname{Jac}({\underline{f}}))
in
S/J
belongs to its socle.

From
(iii)
we get that the ideal of
S/J
generated by
\operatorname{det}(\operatorname{Jac}({\underline{f}}))
is not zero and of degree
s
.
Since
the coset of
\operatorname{det}(\operatorname{Jac}({\underline{f}}))
in
S/J
is non-zero and belongs to
\operatorname{soc}(R/J)
,
from Lemma
2.1
we deduce that
K=J
, i.e.
(f_{0},\dots,f_{n})=\operatorname{Ann_{R}}\langle H\rangle
.

We know that the coset of
\operatorname{det}(\operatorname{Jac}({\underline{f}}))
in
S/K
is a generator of its socle, so

\operatorname{deg}(H)=\operatorname{deg}(\operatorname{det}(\operatorname{Jac}({\underline{f}})))=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\operatorname{deg}(f_{i})-(n+1)
.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

Let
H\neq 0
be a homogeneous polynomial.
Since
\operatorname{Ann_{R}}\langle H\rangle
is of maximal height
there always exists a sequence
{\underline{f}}
satisfying
(i)
and
(ii)
.

Given integers
1\leq d_{0}\leq\dots\leq d_{n}
we consider the multi-projective space

{\mathbb{E}}=\mathbb{V}\times\mathbb{P}_{{\bf k}}^{N(s)}

parameterizing the pairs
({\underline{f}},H)
where
{\underline{f}}=\{f_{0},\dots,f_{n}\}
is a
(n+1)
-pla of homogeneous forms of degrees
d_{1},\dots,d_{n}
, and
H
is a homogeneous polynomial on
y_{0},\dots,y_{n}
of degree
s=\sum_{i=0}^{n}d_{i}-(n+1)
.
We denote by
\pi
the natural projection in the second component of
\mathbb{E}
:

\pi:{\mathbb{E}}\longrightarrow\mathbb{P}_{{\bf k}}^{N(s)}.

Let
U
be the open subset of
\mathbb{E}
defined by the
(n+1)
-plas
{\underline{f}}
such that

\operatorname{Res}({\underline{f}})\neq 0
.
We consider the locally closed subset
Z_{1}
(resp. closed subset
Z_{2}
) of
\mathbb{E}
defined by the condition

{\underline{f}}\circ H=0
and
{\underline{f}}\in U
(resp.
\operatorname{det}(\operatorname{Jac}({\underline{f}}))\circ H=0
).
For all
[H]\in\mathbb{P}_{{\bf k}}^{N(s)}
, we denote the fiber of
[H]
in
Z_{i}
by
\pi
,
i=1,2
,

(Z_{i})_{H}=Z_{i}\cap\pi^{-1}([H])=\{({\underline{f}},[H])\in Z_{i}\}.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
H
be a degree
s
homogeneous form.
Then
\operatorname{Ann_{R}}\langle H\rangle
is a complete intersection of type
(d_{0},\dots,d_{n})
if and only if

(Z_{1})_{H}\nsubseteq(Z_{2})_{H}.

The set of homogeneous polynomials
[H]\in\mathbb{P}_{{\bf k}}^{N(s)}
such that
(Z_{1})_{H}\nsubseteq(Z_{2})_{H}

is a locally constructible set.

Proof.

Assume that
\operatorname{Ann_{R}}\langle H\rangle
is not a complete intersection.
Let
({\underline{f}},H)
be an element of
(Z_{1})_{H}
.
From Proposition
2.2
we get
\operatorname{det}(\operatorname{Jac}({\underline{f}}))\circ H=0
, so
({\underline{f}},H)\in(Z_{2})_{H}
.

Let
H
be an homogeneous form of degree
s
such that

(Z_{1})_{H}\subset(Z_{2})_{H}
.
Assume that

\operatorname{Ann_{R}}\langle H\rangle
is a complete intersection of type
d_{0},\dots,d_{n}
.
Then there exists a family
{\underline{f}}=(f_{0},\dots,f_{n})
satisfying the conditions of
Proposition
2.2
.
In particular, condition
(i)
and
(ii)
is equivalent to
({\underline{f}},H)\in Z_{1}
.
By hypothesis
({\underline{f}},H)\in Z_{2}
so
\operatorname{det}(\operatorname{Jac}({\underline{f}}))=0
.
This a contradiction with the condition
(iii)
of Proposition
2.2
.

From
[
6
, Corollaire 9.5.2]
we get that
the set of homogeneous polynomials
[H]\in\mathbb{P}_{{\bf k}}^{N(s)}
such that
(Z_{1})_{H}\nsubseteq(Z_{2})_{H}

is a locally constructible set.
∎

The computation of the defining equations of the locally constructible set of the last result, in contrast the case of the resultant, could be difficult.
In the next section we will study a concrete example where Proposition
2.2
is applied for proving that some Gorenstein ideal
\operatorname{Ann_{R}}\langle H\rangle
is
a complete intersection.
First we review the example 3.5 of
[
7
]
.

Example 2.5
.

In this example we follow the notations of
[
7
, Example 3.5]
.
We consider the ring of polynomials
S={\bf k}[v,w,x,y,z]
.

(1)
of
[
7
, Example 3.5]
.
Given
H=vwxyz+wxyz^{2}
,
\operatorname{Ann_{R}}\langle H\rangle
contains the sequence
{\underline{f}}=\{v^{2},w^{2},x^{2},y^{2}z^{2}-2vz\}
, i.e. satisfies the condition
(ii)
of Proposition
2.2
.
Since
S/({\underline{f}})
is Artininan we get condition
(i)
of Proposition
2.2
.
On the other hand, we get
\operatorname{det}(\operatorname{Jac}({\underline{f}}))=2^{5}vwxy(z-v)
, so

\operatorname{det}(\operatorname{Jac}({\underline{f}}))\circ H=2^{5}\neq 0
.
Hence
\operatorname{Ann_{R}}\langle H\rangle=({\underline{f}})
is a complete intersection.

(2)
of
[
7
, Example 3.5]
.
We proceed as the previous case.

(3)
of
[
7
, Example 3.5]
.
Let us consider
H=vwxyz+yz^{4}
.
If
\operatorname{Ann_{R}}\langle H\rangle
is a complete intersection, since
5=d_{0}+\dots+d_{4}-5
, then we get that
d_{0}=\dots=d_{4}=2
.
The
{\bf k}
-vector space of forms of degree two of
\operatorname{Ann_{R}}\langle H\rangle
admits as
{\bf k}
-base the monomials:
v^{2},w^{2},x^{2},y^{2}
, i.e. a
{\bf k}
-base of
{\underline{f}}
satisfying
(ii)
of Proposition
2.2
.
Hence, any
5
-pla
{\underline{f}}=(f_{0},\dots,f_{5})
formed by
5
forms of degree two are linearly dependent, so
\operatorname{det}(\operatorname{Jac}({\underline{f}}))=0
and then
\operatorname{Ann_{R}}\langle H\rangle
is not a complete intersection.

3
Case study: short algebras

In this section we consider short Gorenstein algebras: local Gorenstein algebras
A={\bf k}[\![x_{1},\dots,x_{r}]\!]/I
with Hilbert function
\{1,r,r,1\}
.
Recall that in the main result of
[
3
]
we prove that these algebras are isomorphic
to their associated graded ring, i.e. we can consider that
A\cong{\bf k}[x_{1},\dots,x_{r}]/K
with
K
a homogeneous ideal.
On the other hand if
K
is a complete intersection then
r\leq 3
.
The cases
r\leq 2
are computed directly, see
[
3
]
.
In this example we study the case
r=3
, following the general notations of this paper we have that
n=2
.
Hence
K=\operatorname{Ann_{R}}\langle H\rangle
is an ideal of
S={\bf k}[x_{0},x_{1},x_{2}]
with
H
a degree
3
homogeneous polynomial.

In
[
3
]
we computed the isomorphism classes of these algebras in terms of the moduli of the degree three projective plane curve defined by
H
.
Recall that a plane elliptic cubic curve
C\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\bf k}
is defined, in a suitable
system of coordinates, by a Legendre’s equation

H_{\lambda}=y_{1}^{2}y_{2}-y_{0}(y_{0}-y_{2})(y_{0}-\lambda y_{2})

with
\lambda\neq 0,1
.
The
j
-invariant of
C
is:

j(\lambda)=2^{8}\;\frac{(\lambda^{2}-\lambda+1)^{3}}{\lambda^{2}(\lambda-1)^{2}}.

It is well known that
two plane elliptic cubic curves
C_{i}=V(H_{\lambda_{i}})\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\bf k}={\bf Spec}(S)
,
i=1,2
, are projectively isomorphic
if and only if
j(\lambda_{1})=j(\lambda_{2})
.
In
[
3
, Proposition 3.7]
we proved that:

Proposition 3.1
.

[
3
, Proposition 3.7]

Let
A
be an Artinian Gorenstein local
{\bf k}
-algebra with Hilbert function

\operatorname{HF}_{A}=\{1,3,3,1\}
. Then
A
is isomorphic to one and only one of the following graded
quotients of
S={\bf k}[x_{0},x_{1},x_{2}]
:

\begin{array}[]{|c|c|c|}\hline\cr\text{ Model }A=S/K&\text{Inverse system }&\text{Geometry of }C=V(K)\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\bf k}\\
\hline\cr(x_{0}^{2},x_{1}^{2},x_{2}^{2})&y_{0}y_{1}y_{2}&\text{Three independent lines}\\
\hline\cr(x_{0}^{2},x_{0}x_{3}^{2},x_{2}x_{1}^{2},x_{1}^{3},x_{2}^{2}+x_{0}x_{1})&y_{1}(y_{0}y_{1}-y_{2}^{2})&\text{Conic and a tangent line}\\
\hline\cr(x_{0}^{2},x_{1}^{2},x_{2}^{2}+6x_{0}x_{1})&y_{3}(y_{1}y_{2}-y_{3}^{2})&\text{Conic and a non-tangent line}\\
\hline\cr(x_{1}^{2},x_{0}x_{1},x_{0}^{2}+x_{1}^{2}-3x_{0}x_{2})&y_{1}^{2}y_{2}-y_{0}^{2}(y_{0}+y_{2})&\text{Irreducible nodal cubic}\\
\hline\cr(x_{2}^{2},x_{0}x_{1},x_{0}x_{2},x_{1}^{3},x_{0}^{3}+3x_{1}^{2}x_{2})&y_{1}^{2}y_{2}-y_{0}^{3}&\text{Irreducible cuspidal cubic}\\
\hline\cr(x_{1}x_{2},x_{0}x_{2},x_{0}x_{1},x_{1}^{3}-x_{2}^{3},x_{0}^{3}-x_{2}^{3})&y_{0}^{3}+y_{1}^{3}+y_{2}^{3}&\text{Elliptic Fermat curve}\\
\hline\cr K_{\lambda}=(x_{0}x_{1},F_{1},F_{2})&H_{\lambda},\;j(\lambda)\neq 0&\text{Elliptic non Fermat curve}\\
\hline\cr\end{array}

with

F_{1}=\lambda^{2}x_{0}^{2}+\lambda(1+\lambda)x_{0}x_{2}+(\lambda^{2}-\lambda+1)x_{2}^{2}
,

F_{2}=\lambda^{2}x_{1}^{2}+\lambda x_{0}x_{2}+(1+\lambda)x_{2}^{2}
,
and
K_{\lambda_{1}}\cong K_{\lambda_{2}}
if and only if
j(\lambda_{1})=j(\lambda_{2})
.

Notice that the minimal number of generators of the height three Gorenstein ideal
K
of the last result are odd, as the main theorem of
[
13
]
prescribes.
All cases in Proposition
3.1
can be easily computed by using Singular but the last one,
[
2
]
.
In this case a parameter is involved: the
j
-invariant of the elliptic curve.
In
[
3
]
we settle this case by showing first that

J=(x_{0}x_{1},F_{1},F_{2})\subset K_{\lambda}
and then
proving that
\operatorname{dim}_{\bf k}(S/J)=8
.
We can prove this fact by using Proposition
2.2
.
We know that
J=(x_{0}x_{1},F_{1},F_{2})\subset K_{\lambda}=\operatorname{Ann_{R}}\langle H_{\lambda}\rangle
, so
{\underline{f}}=\{x_{0}x_{1},F_{1},F_{2}\}
satisfies condition
(ii)
of Proposition
2.2
.
On the other hand it is easy to prove that

J
is
{\mathfrak{m}}
-primary, so we have condition
(i)
of Proposition
2.2
.
Since
j(\lambda)\neq 0

\operatorname{det}(\operatorname{Jac}(x_{0}x_{1},F_{1},F_{2}))\circ H_{\lambda}=-16\lambda^{2}(\lambda^{2}-\lambda+1)\neq 0.

Hence we get condition
(iii)
of Proposition
2.2
and then
(x_{0}x_{1},F_{1},F_{2})=K_{\lambda}
.

Large Deviations for Stochastic Evolution Equations in the Critical Variational Setting Thanks: The authors are supported by the VICI subsidy VI.C.212.027 of the Netherlands Organisation for Scientific Research (NWO)
Esmée Theewis, Mark Veraar

Abstract
. Using the weak convergence approach, we prove the large deviation principle (LDP) for solutions to quasilinear stochastic evolution equations with small Gaussian noise in the critical variational setting, a recently developed general variational framework. No additional assumptions are made apart from those required for well-posedness. In particular, no monotonicity is required, nor a compact embedding in the Gelfand triple. Moreover, we allow for flexible growth of the diffusion coefficient, including gradient noise. This leads to numerous applications for which the LDP was not established yet, in particular equations on unbounded domains with gradient noise.

1.
Introduction

In this paper we study large deviations for solutions to small-noise stochastic evolution equations of the form

(1.1)

\,\mathrm{d}Y^{\varepsilon}(t)=-A(t,Y^{\varepsilon}(t))\,\mathrm{d}t+\sqrt{\varepsilon}B(t,Y^{\varepsilon}(t))\,\mathrm{d}W(t)

in the new variational framework of
[
5
]
by Agresti and the second author. This framework, the
critical variational setting
, has been developed to extend the classical variational approach to stochastic evolution equations originating from
[
7
]
,
[
33
]
,
[
25
]
. In the classical variational approach, the drift and diffusion coefficients
A
and
B
need to satisfy several conditions to ensure well-posedness of (
1.1
). The usual weak monotonicity condition is especially restrictive. It is therefore no surprise that efforts have been made to weaken the monotonicity condition, e.g. ​in
[
28
, §5.2]
and very recently
[
40
]
with a much weaker local monotonicity condition. One of the advantages of the critical variational setting of
[
5
]
used in this paper, is that no form of monotonicity is assumed. In return,
A
and
B
are of a slightly less (but still very) general form:

A(t,v)=A_{0}(t,v)v-F(t,v)+f(t),\quad B(t,v)=B_{0}(t,v)v+G(t,v)+g(t)

for
t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
and
v\in V
, where
(V,H,V^{*})
is a Gelfand triple belonging to the stochastic evolution equation.
That is,
(A,B)
contains a quasilinear part
(A_{0},B_{0})
and a semilinear part
(F,G)
and it is assumed that both parts satisfy certain critical local Lipschitz conditions, where the Lipschitz constant may depend arbitrarily on
\|v\|_{H}
and, allowing even more flexibility, polynomially on interpolation norms
\|v\|_{V_{\beta}}
, where
V_{\beta}=[V^{*},V]_{\beta}
denotes the complex interpolation space. Besides the absence of any monotonicity assumption, another major improvement of the critical variational setting is the weakening of the usual growth conditions on the diffusion coefficient
B
, allowing e.g. ​for gradient noise. Lastly, a special feature is that critical nonlinearities are allowed (see (
2.6
)), which is not the case in other settings. The critical variational setting covers many semilinear and some quasilinear equations that were not covered by more classical variational settings. In particular, this holds for many equations that require an (analytically) strong setting, in which monotonicity often fails, for example the Cahn-Hilliard equation, the tamed Navier-Stokes equations and the Allen-Cahn equation. See
[
5
, §5]
for details. The exact assumptions in the critical variational setting can be found in Section
2
. Finally, it should be stressed that unlike the settings in
[
40
]
, the critical variational setting does not require a compact (Sobolev) embedding
V\hookrightarrow H
and is thus suited to treat equations on unbounded spatial domains.

The goal of this paper is to establish the LDP for solutions to small-noise stochastic evolution equations in the critical variational setting. Large deviations have been studied for SPDEs in many different frameworks. The first results for SPDEs were inspired by the pioneering paper for SDEs by Freidlin and Wentzell
[
17
]
(see also
[
13
, §5.6]
), relying on discretizations and the contraction principle. These techniques were extended to several SPDE settings with Gaussian noise, notably in
[
12
]
,
[
34
]
,
[
9
]
(stochastic
reaction-diffusion equations),
[
10
]
(semilinear parabolic equations) and
[
41
]
(stochastic porous media equations). However, for less regular
A
and
B
such techniques are difficult to use in general settings.
In 2001, Budhiraja and Dupuis proved a substantially generalized contraction principle, the so-called weak convergence approach to large deviations
[
8
]
. This approach turned out to be extremely powerful for SPDEs and subsequently, it was successfully applied to many SPDEs with less regular coefficients, e.g. ​in
[
43
]
,
[
11
]
(2D Navier-Stokes and hydrodynamical models),
[
14
]
(Boussinesq equations), as well as
[
38
]
and
[
27
]
(general classical variational settings).

Also for the recent variational settings with even weaker conditions on the coefficients
A
and
B
, the weak convergence approach has led to new LDP proofs. In
[
19
]
the LDP is obtained for McKean-Vlasov quasilinear stochastic evolution equations, in
[
31
]
for a setting from
[
40
]
, in
[
26
]
for the same setting extended to Lévy noise, and most recently,
[
32
]
obtained the LDP for the strongest setting of
[
40
]
. The latter allows flexible growth bounds on
B
, including gradient noise. Still, the combination of flexible growth of
B
and unbounded spatial domains (that is, no compact embedding
V\hookrightarrow H
) has not been covered in any of the papers so far. The main improvement of our work is that we allow for both. In fact, no additional bounds on
A
and
B
are assumed for the LDP apart from those in
[
5
]
required for well-posedness, nor do we assume a compact embedding in the Gelfand triple. New techniques are used to replace the usual compactness arguments. The paper contains new approaches for

-

well-posedness of the skeleton equation and compact sublevel sets of the rate function in the LDP, by means of maximal regularity theory and a strong approximation argument,

-

the stochastic continuity criterion from the weak convergence approach, using critical estimates for the nonlinearities and an effective combination of deterministic and stochastic Gronwall inequalities.

The LDP result in this paper opens up many new applications.
In particular, the following examples are included on bounded and unbounded domains in

,

-

[

, §5.6]
,

-

many reaction-diffusion equations, e.g. ​for
d\leq 4
:

–

Allen-Cahn equations
[

, §5.4]
,

–

,

This list is far from extensive.

2.
Main result

We specify our setting for stochastic evolution equations and recall the definition of the large deviation principle before we state our main result, Theorem
2.6
.

2.1.
The critical variational setting

We let
(V,H,V^{*})
be a Gelfand triple of real Hilbert spaces. That is,
(V,(\cdot,\cdot)_{V})
and
(H,(\cdot,\cdot)_{H})
are real Hilbert spaces such that there exists a continuous and dense embedding
\iota\colon V\hookrightarrow H
. Then,
j\colon H\hookrightarrow V^{*}\colon x\mapsto(x,\iota(\cdot))_{H}
is a continuous embedding and
j(H)
is dense in
V^{*}
by reflexivity of
V
(
j=\iota^{*}
under Riesz’ identification
H\cong H^{*}
). From now on we identify
x\in V
with
\iota(x)\in H
and
x\in H
with
j(x)\in V^{*}
. Then, if
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the duality pairing between the abstract dual
V^{*}
and
V
, one has

\langle x,v\rangle=(v,x)_{H}\quad\text{for all }x\in H,v\in V.

For convenience of the reader, we recall that in applications, one does not work with the abstract dual
V^{*}
but with a space
V^{\prime}
which, under some assumptions and with the correct duality pairing, is isomorphic to
V^{*}
, see also
[
25
, p. 1244]
. One starts with reflexive Banach (or Hilbert) (sub)spaces
V\subset H\subset V^{\prime}
, where each inclusion is dense and continuous and one defines
j\colon H\hookrightarrow V^{*}\colon x\mapsto(x,\cdot)_{H}
. Then, provided that

(2.1)

|(x,v)_{H}|\leq\|x\|_{V^{\prime}}\|v\|_{V}\quad\text{for all }x\in H,v\in V,

there exists a unique continuous extension to a map
j_{1}\colon V^{\prime}\to V^{*}
. Furthermore, if
j_{1}
is bijective, then it follows that
j_{1}\colon V^{\prime}\cong V^{*}
as normed spaces, although not necessarily isometrically. The duality pairing is then given by
\langle v^{\prime},v\rangle\coloneqq j_{1}(v^{\prime})(v)
and for
x\in H,v\in V
we have
\langle x,v\rangle=(x,v)_{H}
since
j_{1}
is the extension of
j
. The triple
(V,H,V^{\prime})
is also called a Gelfand triple and simply denoted by
(V,H,V^{*})
, where as explained, the correct duality pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\colon V^{\prime}\times V\to{\mathbb{R}}
is given by
\langle v^{\prime},v\rangle\coloneqq j_{1}(v^{\prime})(v)
.

In fact, bijectivity of
j_{1}
holds if and only if there exists
\alpha>0
such that

(2.2)

\alpha\|x\|_{V^{\prime}}\leq\sup_{v\in V,\|v\|_{V}\leq 1}|(x,v)_{H}|\eqqcolon\|j_{1}(x)\|_{V^{*}}\quad\text{for all }x\in H.

The equivalence follows from
[
42
, Th. 4.48]
, density of
H\subset V^{\prime}
and continuity of
j_{1}
, and density of
\mathrm{Im}(j_{1})\subset V^{*}
. The latter holds since
j_{1}(V^{\prime})\supset j_{1}(H)=j(H)
and one can verify that
j(H)
is dense in
V^{*}
using reflexivity of
V
. In conclusion, provided that
V\subset H\subset V^{\prime}
continuously and densely, one only has to verify (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) to have
j_{1}\colon V^{\prime}\cong V^{*}
.

Popular choices for the Gelfand triple are the weak and strong setting for a given differential operator. For example, if

on

, then one can use

\displaystyle V=H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),\;H=L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),\;V^{\prime}=H^{-1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})=V^{*}

\displaystyle\text{(weak setting)},

\displaystyle V=H^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),\;H=H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),\;V^{\prime}=L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\cong V^{*}

See also

Assumption 2.2

We assume that:

(1)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad 2\beta_{j}\leq 1+\frac{1}{1+\rho_{j}},\quad j\in\{1,\ldots,m_{F}+m_{G}\},

((sub)criticality)

\displaystyle\|A_{0}(t,u)w\|_{V^{*}}

\displaystyle\|A_{0}(t,u)w-A_{0}(t,v)w\|_{V^{*}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{n,T}\|u-v\|_{H}\|w\|_{V},

\displaystyle|\!|\!|B_{0}(t,u)w|\!|\!|_{H}

]
.

2.2.
Statement of the main result

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a Polish space, let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
be a probability space and let
(Y^{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}
be a collection of
\mathcal{E}
-valued random variables on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
. Let
I\colon\mathcal{E}\to[0,\infty]
be a function. Then
(Y^{\varepsilon})

satisfies the large deviation principle (LDP) on
\mathcal{E}
with rate function
I\colon S\to[0,\infty]
if

(i)

I
has compact sublevel sets,

(ii)

for all open
E\subset\mathcal{E}
:
\liminf_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\varepsilon\log\mathbb{P}(Y^{\varepsilon}\in E)\geq-\inf_{z\in E}I(z)
,

(iii)

for all closed
E\subset\mathcal{E}
:
\limsup_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\varepsilon\log\mathbb{P}(Y^{\varepsilon}\in E)\leq-\inf_{z\in E}I(z)
.

Before we formulate our LDP result, we define the skeleton equation, which appears in the rate function of our LDP.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
x\in H
be fixed.
For
\psi\in L^{2}(0,T;U)
, the
skeleton equation
associated to the stochastic evolution equation (
2.4
) is given by

(2.8)

\begin{cases}&(u^{\psi})^{\prime}(t)=-A(t,u^{\psi}(t))+B(t,u^{\psi}(t))\psi(t),\quad t\in[0,T],\\
&u^{\psi}(0)=x.\end{cases}

The main theorem of this paper is as follows.

Theorem 2.6
.

Suppose that
(A,B)
satisfies Assumption
2.2
and coercivity (
2.7
). Let
x\in H
. For
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
, let
Y^{\varepsilon}
be the strong solution to

\begin{cases}&\,\mathrm{d}Y^{\varepsilon}(t)=-A(t,Y^{\varepsilon}(t))\,\mathrm{d}t+\sqrt{\varepsilon}B(t,Y^{\varepsilon}(t))\,\mathrm{d}W(t),\quad t\in[0,T],\\
&u(0)=x,\end{cases}

Then
(Y^{\varepsilon})
satisfies the LDP on
\mathrm{MR}(0,T)
with rate function
I\colon\mathrm{MR}(0,T)\to[0,+\infty]
given by

(2.9)

I(z)=\frac{1}{2}\inf\Big\{\int_{0}^{T}\|\psi(s)\|_{U}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s:\psi\in L^{2}(0,T;U),z=u^{\psi}\Big\},

where
\inf\varnothing\coloneqq+\infty
and
u^{\psi}
is the strong solution to (
2.8
).

We have taken
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
to ensure that
(A,\sqrt{\varepsilon}B)
satisfies coercivity (
2.7
), so that the equation for
Y^{\varepsilon}
is well-posed by Theorem
2.3
.

To have
u^{\psi}
appearing in (
2.9
) well-defined, (
2.8
) needs to be (globally) well-posed. In Section
3
, we prove that this is the case.
Finally, we recall that the LDP is equivalent to the
Laplace principle

[
15
, Def. 1.2.2, Th. 1.2.1, Th. 1.2.3]
. The weak convergence approach from
[
8
]
offers sufficient conditions for the latter, hence for the LDP. The approach is stated in Subsection
4.1
, after which we apply it to prove Theorem
2.6
in the remainder of Section
4
.

3.
Well-posedness of the skeleton equation

Before we turn to large deviations, we prove global well-posedness of the skeleton equation (
2.8
) under Assumption
2.2
and coercivity (
2.7
). This is needed, since the solution to (
2.8
) appears in the rate function (
2.9
) of the LDP.

Unfortunately, well-posedness cannot be proved at once.
Instead, we first achieve well-posedness of an appropriate linearized version of the skeleton equation in Corollary
3.5
, together with a maximal regularity estimate.
Then, we can borrow the strategies from
[
21
, Chap. 18]
,
[
37
]
,
[
36
]
,
[
35
]
.
That is, we use the maximal regularity estimate of Corollary
3.5
for the linearized equation in a fixed point argument, yielding existence of a local solution to the skeleton equation in Theorem
3.7
. Finally, we extend to a global solution in Theorem
3.16
, making use of a blow-up criterion. Uniqueness will be obtained along the way.

3.1.
Linearized skeleton equation

We consider the following linearization of (
2.8
). We discard the non-linearities
F
and
G
and for fixed
w\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H)
, we consider

(3.1)

\begin{cases}&u^{\prime}(t)+A_{0}(t,w(t))u(t)-B_{0}(t,w(t))u(t)\psi(t)=\bar{f}(t)+\bar{g}(t)\psi(t),\\
&u(0)=x,\end{cases}

where
A_{0}
and
B_{0}
are as in Assumption
2.2
and
\bar{f}\in L^{2}(0,T;V^{*})
,
\bar{g}\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{L}_{2}(U,H))
.
In this subsection we prove well-posedness of (
3.1
) using the method of continuity
[
21
, Lem. 16.2.2]
, together with a suitable maximal regularity estimate. We prove it for more general equations as this does not require any more effort and makes the exposition more transparent. Let us introduce spaces
S
and
E
that will be used in the method of continuity.

Definition 3.1
.

For
T>0
, we let

S\coloneqq L^{2}(0,T;V^{*})+L^{1}(0,T;H)

be the sum space of the interpolation couple
(L^{2}(0,T;V^{*}),L^{1}(0,T;H))
, where we note that both components embed continuously into the Hausdorff topological vector space
L^{1}(0,T;V^{*})
. The norm on
S
is given by

\|h\|_{S}\coloneqq\inf\big\{\|f\|_{L^{2}(0,T;V^{*})}+\|g\|_{L^{1}(0,T;H)}:h=f+g,f\in L^{2}(0,T;V^{*}),g\in L^{1}(0,T;H\}\big\}.

Note that
S
is a Banach space
[
20
, Prop. C.1.3]
and
S\hookrightarrow L^{1}(0,T;V^{*})
.
Moreover, we define

\displaystyle E\coloneqq\{u\in\mathrm{MR}(0,T):u\text{ is weakly differentiable, }u^{\prime}\in S\},\quad\|u\|_{E}\coloneqq\|u\|_{\mathrm{MR}(0,T)}+\|u^{\prime}\|_{S}.

Note that trivially,
E\hookrightarrow\mathrm{MR}(0,T)
.

Dealing with the sum space
S
is not standard in part of the literature. However, it is covered excellently in Pardoux’ thesis
[
33
]
.

The following proposition is a direct consequence of
[
33
, Th. 2.1]
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\bar{A}\colon[0,T]\to\mathcal{L}(V,V^{*})
be such that

[0,T]\to V^{*}\colon t\mapsto\bar{A}(t)v
is strongly Borel measurable for all
v\in V

and suppose that
a_{T}\coloneqq\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\bar{A}(t)\|_{\mathcal{L}(V,V^{*})}<\infty
.
Suppose that there exists
\theta>0
such that for all
t\in[0,T]
and
v\in V
:

\langle\bar{A}(t)v,v\rangle\geq\theta\|v\|_{V}^{2}.

Then, for any
h\in S
and
x\in H
, there exists a unique
u\in E
satisfying

(3.2)

\begin{cases}u^{\prime}(t)+\bar{A}(t)u(t)=h(t),\quad t\in[0,T],\\
u(0)=x.\end{cases}

We will need an extension of Proposition
3.2
with the coercivity condition replaced by the weaker condition (
3.3
) below. As a preparation, we first prove a maximal regularity estimate.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\bar{A}\colon[0,T]\to\mathcal{L}(V,V^{*})
be such that

\bar{A}(\cdot)u(\cdot)\in S
for any
u\in\mathrm{MR}(0,T)
.
Suppose that there exist
\theta>0
and
M\in L^{1}(0,T)
,
M\geq 0
such that for all
t\in[0,T]
and
v\in V
:

(3.3)

\langle\bar{A}(t)v,v\rangle\geq\theta\|v\|_{V}^{2}-M(t)\|v\|_{H}^{2}.

Let
h\in S
and
x\in H
and suppose that
u\in\mathrm{MR}(0,T)
is a strong solution to (
3.2
).
Then

(3.4)

\|u\|_{\mathrm{MR}(0,T)}\leq C_{\theta}\exp(2\|M\|_{L^{1}(0,T)})\big(\|h\|_{S}+\|x\|_{H}\big),

for a constant
C_{\theta}>0
depending only on
\theta
.

Proof.

Write
h=f+g
with
f\in L^{2}(0,T;V^{*})
,
g\in L^{1}(0,T;H)
.
We apply
[
33
, Th. 2.2]
. Since
u\in\mathrm{MR}(0,T)
is a strong solution, we have
u(t)=x+\int_{0}^{t}v(s)\,\mathrm{d}s
with
v\coloneqq h(\cdot)-\bar{A}(\cdot)u(\cdot)\in S=L^{2}(0,T;V^{*})+L^{1}(0,T;H)
.
Hence, the chain rule (
A.2
) and (
3.3
) yield for all
t\in[0,T]
:

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{H}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|x\|_{H}^{2}+2\int_{0}^{t}\langle h(s),u(s)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s-2\int_{0}^{t}\langle\bar{A}(s)u(s),u(s)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s

(3.5)

\displaystyle\leq\|x\|_{H}^{2}+2\int_{0}^{t}\langle h(s),u(s)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s-2\theta\|u\|_{L^{2}(0,t;V)}^{2}+2\int_{0}^{t}M(s)\|u(s)\|_{H}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s.

Note that by Young’s inequality, we have for all
s\in[0,t]
:

\displaystyle\langle h(s),u(s)\rangle=\langle f(s),u(s)\rangle+\langle g(s),u(s)\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\|u(s)\|_{V}\|f(s)\|_{V^{*}}+\|u(s)\|_{H}\|g(s)\|_{H}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\theta}{2}\|u(s)\|_{V}^{2}+\frac{1}{2\theta}\|f(s)\|_{V^{*}}^{2}+\sup_{r\in[0,t]}\|u(r)\|_{H}\|g(s)\|_{H}.

Entering this into (
3.5
) we obtain for all
0\leq t\leq t_{1}\leq T
:

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{H}^{2}+\theta\|u\|_{L^{2}(0,t;V)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|x\|_{H}^{2}+\frac{1}{\theta}\|f\|_{L^{2}(0,T;V^{*})}^{2}+2\sup_{r\in[0,t_{1}]}\|u(r)\|_{H}\|g\|_{L^{1}(0,T;H)}

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{0}^{t_{1}}2M(s)\|u(s)\|_{H}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\|x\|_{H}^{2}+\frac{1}{\theta}\|f\|_{L^{2}(0,T;V^{*})}^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\sup_{r\in[0,t_{1}]}\|u(r)\|_{H}^{2}+2\|g\|_{L^{1}(0,T;H)}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}2M(s)\|u(s)\|_{H}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s.

Hence, taking
\sup_{t\in[0,t_{1}]}
in the above and writing
F(t_{1})\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\sup_{t\in[0,t_{1}]}\big(\|u(t)\|_{H}^{2}+\theta\|u\|_{L^{2}(0,t;V)}\big)
gives for all
0\leq t_{1}\leq T
:

\displaystyle F(t_{1})

\displaystyle\leq 2F(t_{1})-\frac{1}{2}\sup_{r\in[0,t_{1}]}\|u(r)\|_{H}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|x\|_{H}^{2}+\frac{1}{\theta}\|f\|_{L^{2}(0,T;V^{*})}^{2}+2\|g\|_{L^{1}(0,T;H)}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t_{1}}2M(s)\|u(s)\|_{H}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\|x\|_{H}^{2}+\frac{1}{\theta}\|f\|_{L^{2}(0,T;V^{*})}^{2}+2\|g\|_{L^{1}(0,T;H)}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t_{1}}4M(s)F(s)\,\mathrm{d}s,

so by Gronwall’s inequality, we obtain

\|u\|_{C([0,T];H)}^{2}+\theta\|u\|_{L^{2}(0,T;V)}^{2}\leq 4F(T)\leq 4\big(\|x\|_{H}^{2}+\frac{1}{\theta}\|f\|_{L^{2}(0,T;V^{*})}^{2}+2\|g\|_{L^{1}(0,T;H)}^{2}\big)\exp(4\|M\|_{L^{1}(0,T)}).

Thus

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathrm{MR}(0,T)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(1\vee\theta^{-1})4\big(\|x\|_{H}^{2}+(\theta^{-1}\vee 2)(\|f\|_{L^{2}(0,T;V^{*})}+\|g\|_{L^{1}(0,T;H)})^{2}\big)\exp(4\|M\|_{L^{1}(0,T)}).

Since
f
and
g
with
h=f+g
were arbitrary, taking the infimum over
\{(f,g)\in L^{2}(0,T;V^{*})\times L^{1}(0,T;H):h=f+g\}
gives

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathrm{MR}(0,T)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\theta}^{2}\big(\|x\|_{H}^{2}+\|h\|_{S}^{2}\big)\exp(4\|M\|_{L^{1}(0,T)}).

where
C_{\theta}\coloneqq\big(4(1\vee\theta^{-1})(\theta^{-1}\vee 2)\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Taking square roots on both sides yields (
3.4
).
∎

We now prove Proposition
3.2
under the weaker coercivity (
3.3
).

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
\bar{A}\colon[0,T]\to\mathcal{L}(V,V^{*})
and suppose that
for all
u\in\mathrm{MR}(0,T)
:

(3.6)

\displaystyle\bar{A}(\cdot)u(\cdot)\in S,\qquad\|\bar{A}(\cdot)u(\cdot)\|_{S}\leq\alpha\|u\|_{\mathrm{MR}(0,T)},

for some constant
\alpha>0
independent of
u
.
Suppose that coercivity (
3.3
) is satisfied for some
\theta>0
and
M\in L^{1}(0,T)
. Then for any
h\in S
, there exists a unique strong solution
u\in\mathrm{MR}(0,T)
to (
3.2
). Moreover, the estimate (
3.4
) holds.

Proof.

We use the method of continuity
[
21
, Lem. 16.2.2]
.
Define
A_{0}\in\mathcal{L}(V,V^{*})
by
A_{0}v\coloneqq\theta(\cdot,v)_{V}
.
For
\lambda\in[0,1]
, put

\displaystyle A_{\lambda}\colon[0,T]\to\mathcal{L}(V,V^{*})\colon t\mapsto(1-\lambda){A}_{0}+\lambda\bar{A}(t),

\displaystyle L_{\lambda}\colon E\to S\times H\colon\big(L_{\lambda}u\big)\coloneqq\big(u^{\prime}(\cdot)+A_{\lambda}(\cdot)(u(\cdot)),u(0)\big).

Clearly,
L_{\lambda}
is linear. We show that
L_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{L}(E,S\times H)
and that

[0,1]\to\mathcal{L}(E,S\times H)\colon\lambda\mapsto L_{\lambda}
is continuous.

Let
u\in E
be arbitrary. For all
t\in[0,T]
we have
A_{0}u(t)=\theta\langle\cdot,u(t)\rangle\in V^{*}
, so by the Riesz isomorphism,
\|A_{0}u(t)\|_{V^{*}}=\theta\|u(t)\|_{V}
. Since
u\in L^{2}(0,T;V)
, it follows that
A_{0}u(\cdot)\in L^{2}(0,T;V^{*})\subset S
and

\displaystyle\|{A}_{0}u(\cdot)\|_{S}

\displaystyle\leq\|{A}_{0}u(\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(0,T;V^{*})}=\theta\|u\|_{L^{2}(0,T;V)}\leq\theta\|u\|_{\mathrm{MR}(0,T)}

Combining with (
3.6
) gives
{A}_{\lambda}(\cdot)u(\cdot)\in S
and

(3.7)

\|{A}_{\lambda}(\cdot)u(\cdot)\|_{S}\leq(1-\lambda)\|A_{0}u(\cdot)\|_{S}+\lambda\|\bar{A}(\cdot)u(\cdot)\|_{S}\leq(\theta+\alpha)\|u\|_{\mathrm{MR}(0,T)}.

Note that
u^{\prime}\in S
and
\|u^{\prime}\|_{S}\leq\|u\|_{E}
by definition of
E
. Moreover,
E\hookrightarrow\mathrm{MR}(0,T)
, thus

\displaystyle\|L_{\lambda}u\|_{S\times H}

\displaystyle\leq\|u^{\prime}\|_{S}+\|{A}_{\lambda}(\cdot)u(\cdot)\|_{S}+\|u(0)\|_{H}

\displaystyle\leq\|u\|_{E}+(\theta+\alpha)\|u\|_{\mathrm{MR}(0,T)}+\|u\|_{C([0,T];H)}

\displaystyle\leq(2+\theta+\alpha)\|u\|_{E},

proving
L_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{L}(E,S\times H)
. Moreover, we have for any
\lambda,\mu\in[0,1]
and
u\in E
:

\displaystyle\|(L_{\lambda}-L_{\mu})u\|_{S\times H}

\displaystyle=\|\big((\mu-\lambda)A_{0}u(\cdot)+(\lambda-\mu)\bar{A}(\cdot)u(\cdot),0\big)\|_{S\times H}

\displaystyle\leq|\mu-\lambda|\|A_{0}u(\cdot)\|_{S}+|\lambda-\mu|\|\bar{A}(\cdot)u(\cdot)\|_{S}

\displaystyle\leq|\mu-\lambda|(\theta+\alpha)\|u\|_{\mathrm{MR}(0,T)}

\displaystyle\leq|\mu-\lambda|(\theta+\alpha)\|u\|_{E},

i.e.
\|L_{\lambda}-L_{\mu}\|_{\mathcal{L}(E,S\times H)}\leq|\mu-\lambda|(\theta+\alpha)
. Thus
\lambda\mapsto L_{\lambda}
is (Lipschitz) continuous.

Next, we verify that
\|u\|_{E}\leq K\|L_{\lambda}u\|_{S\times H}
for some
K>0
independent of
\lambda
.
Note that
A_{\lambda}
satisfies all conditions of Lemma
3.3
. Coercivity (
3.3
) holds since

\displaystyle\langle v,A_{\lambda}(t)v\rangle=(1-\lambda)\langle v,{A}_{0}v\rangle+\lambda\langle v,\bar{A}(t)v\rangle

\displaystyle\geq(1-\lambda)\theta\|v\|_{V}^{2}+\lambda(\theta\|v\|_{V}^{2}-|M(t)|\|v\|_{H}^{2})

\displaystyle\geq\theta\|v\|_{V}^{2}-|M(t)|\|v\|_{H}^{2}.

Thus, by (
3.4
) applied to
h\coloneqq u^{\prime}+A_{\lambda}u\in S
and
x\coloneqq u(0)
:

\|u\|_{\mathrm{MR}(0,T)}\leq C_{\theta}\exp(2\|M\|_{L^{1}(0,T)})\big(\|h\|_{S}+\|u(0)\|_{H}\big)=C\|L_{\lambda}u\|_{S\times H},

with
C\coloneqq C_{\theta}\exp(2\|M\|_{L^{1}(0,T)})
. Together with (
3.7
) this gives for all
u\in E
:

\displaystyle\|u\|_{E}=\|u\|_{\mathrm{MR}(0,T)}+\|u^{\prime}\|_{S}

\displaystyle=\|u\|_{\mathrm{MR}(0,T)}+\|h(\cdot)-A_{\lambda}(\cdot)u(\cdot)\|_{S}

\displaystyle\leq\|u\|_{\mathrm{MR}(0,T)}+\|L_{\lambda}u\|_{S\times H}+\|A_{\lambda}(\cdot)u(\cdot)\|_{S}

\displaystyle\leq(1+\theta+\alpha)\|u\|_{\mathrm{MR}(0,T)}+\|L_{\lambda}u\|_{S\times H}

\displaystyle\leq(1+C(1+\theta+\alpha))\|L_{\lambda}u\|_{S\times H}.

Finally, note that
L_{0}\colon E\to S\times H:\big(L_{0}u\big)=\big(u^{\prime}(\cdot)+A_{0}u(\cdot),u(0)\big)
is surjective. This follows from Proposition
3.2
applied to
\bar{A}_{0}\colon[0,T]\to\mathcal{L}(V,V^{*})
given by
\bar{A}_{0}(t)v\coloneqq A_{0}v=\theta(\cdot,v)_{V}
.

All requirements for the method of continuity are fulfilled and we conclude that
L_{1}
is surjective, giving existence of strong solutions. The a priori estimate (
3.4
) now follows from Lemma
3.3
and proves uniqueness of strong solutions at once, since
\bar{A}(t)
is linear.
∎

As promised, a mere application of Theorem
3.4
now gives us the desired well-posedness and maximal regularity estimate for (
3.1
).

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
A_{0}
and
B_{0}
satisfy the conditions concerning
A_{0},B_{0}
in Assumption
2.2
and let
\psi\in L^{2}(0,T;U)
.
Let
T>0
and
w\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H)
.
Define
\bar{A}\colon[0,T]\to\mathcal{L}(V,V^{*})
by
\bar{A}(t)v\coloneqq A_{0}(t,w(t))v-B_{0}(t,w(t))v\psi(t)
.
Then
\bar{A}
satisfies all conditions of Theorem
3.4
. Consequently, for any
\bar{f}\in L^{2}(0,T;V^{*})
and
\bar{g}\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{L}_{2}(U,H))
, there exists a unique strong solution
u\in\mathrm{MR}(0,T)
to

\begin{cases}&u^{\prime}(t)+A_{0}(t,w(t))u(t)-B_{0}(t,w(t))u(t)\psi(t)=\bar{f}(t)+\bar{g}(t)\psi(t),\\
&u(0)=x,\end{cases}

Moreover, for any
\tilde{T}\in[0,T]
there exists a constant
K_{\tilde{T}}>0
such that

(3.8)

\|u\|_{\mathrm{MR}(0,\tilde{T})}\leq K_{\tilde{T}}\left(\|x\|_{H}+\|\bar{f}\|_{L^{2}(0,\tilde{T};V^{*})}+\|\bar{g}\|_{L^{2}(0,\tilde{T};\mathcal{L}_{2}(U,H))}\right),

and
K_{\tilde{T}}
is non-decreasing in
\tilde{T}
and depends further only on
T
,
\|w\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H)}
and
\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(0,\tilde{T};U)}
.

Proof.

Put
n\coloneqq\|w\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H)}
.
Since strong solutions only depend on
\bar{A}
through an integral, we can fix a strongly measurable, pointwise defined measurable version of
w
which satisfies
\|w(t)\|_{H}\leq n
for all
t\in[0,T]
.
Strong measurability of
\bar{A}(\cdot)u(\cdot)
is then satisfied if
u\in\mathrm{MR}(0,T)
, see Remark
3.6
. Moreover, Assumption
(3)
gives for all
u\in\mathrm{MR}(0,T)
:

\|A_{0}(\cdot,w(\cdot))u(\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(0,T;V^{*})}\leq C_{n,T}(1+n)\|u\|_{L^{2}(0,T;V)}<\infty

and by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

\displaystyle\|B_{0}(\cdot,w(\cdot))u(\cdot)\psi(\cdot)\|_{L^{1}(0,T;H)}

\displaystyle\leq\|B_{0}(\cdot,w(\cdot))u(\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{L}_{2}(U,H))}\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(0,T;U)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{n,T}(1+n)\|u\|_{L^{2}(0,T;V)}\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(0,T;U)}<\infty.

Hence,
\bar{A}(\cdot)u(\cdot)\in S
and we have

\displaystyle\|\bar{A}(\cdot)u(\cdot)\|_{S}

\displaystyle\leq\|A_{0}(\cdot,w(\cdot))u(\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(0,T;V^{*})}+\|B_{0}(\cdot,w(\cdot))u(\cdot)\psi(\cdot)\|_{L^{1}(0,T;H)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{n,T}(1+n)(1+\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(0,T;U))})\|u\|_{L^{2}(0,T;V)}

\displaystyle\leq\alpha\|u\|_{\mathrm{MR}(0,T)},

where
\alpha\coloneqq C_{n,T}(1+n)(1+\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(0,T;U))})
.

Furthermore, by Assumption
(2)
, we have for all
v\in V
and
t\in[0,T]
:

\displaystyle\langle\bar{A}(t)v,v\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle A_{0}(t,w(t))v,v\rangle-\langle B_{0}(t,w(t))\psi(t)v,v\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\langle A_{0}(t,w(t))v,v\rangle-\frac{1}{2}|\!|\!|B_{0}(t,w(t))v|\!|\!|_{H}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|\psi(t)\|_{U}^{2}\|v\|_{H}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\theta_{n,T}\|v\|_{V}^{2}-({M}_{n,T}+\frac{1}{2}\|\psi(t)\|_{U}^{2})\|v\|_{H}^{2},

so coercivity (
3.3
) is satisfied with
{\theta}\coloneqq\theta_{n,T}
and
{M}(\cdot)\coloneqq{M}_{n,T}+\frac{1}{2}\|\psi(\cdot)\|_{U}^{2}\in L^{1}(0,T)
.

As before by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,
h\coloneqq\bar{f}+\bar{g}\psi\in S
.
Now Theorem
3.4
yields existence of a unique strong solution
u\in\mathrm{MR}(0,T)
to (
3.1
).
Finally, let
\tilde{T}\in(0,T]
be arbitrary and
put
\tilde{S}\coloneqq L^{2}(0,\tilde{T};V^{*})+L^{1}(0,\tilde{T};H)
. We have

(3.9)

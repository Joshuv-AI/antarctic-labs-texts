Twisted Yang-Baxter sets, cohomology theory, and application to knots
Mohamed Elhamdadi, Manpreet Singh

Abstract
. We introduce twisted set-theoretic Yang-Baxter solutions and develop an associated cohomology theory, which extends the standard cohomology theory of Yang-Baxter solutions. By employing cocycles of twisted biquandles along with Alexander numbering, we construct state-sum invariants for knots and knotted surfaces. As an application, we use our approach to distinguish the 2 2 -twist spun trefoil from its reverse orientation, in line with prior findings.

1.
Introduction

The Yang-Baxter equation, independently discovered by Yang
[
26
]
and Baxter
[
4
]
, has been pivotal in the fields of quantum groups, braided categories, and knot theory. A Yang-Baxter set consists of a pair
(X,R)
, where
X
is a set and
R:X\times X\to X\times X
is an invertible map that satisfies:

(R\times\mathrm{id}_{X})(\mathrm{id}_{X}\times R)(R\times\mathrm{id}_{X})=(\mathrm{id}_{X}\times R)(R\times\mathrm{id}_{X})(\mathrm{id}_{X}\times R)

with
\mathrm{id}_{X}:X\to X
being the identity map in
X
.
Solutions to the Yang-Baxter equation enable the construction of knot invariants
[
25
,
12
]
, such as the Jones polynomial
[
11
]
. Algebraic structures like racks, quandles
[
13
,
19
]
, and biquandles
[
18
,
15
]
also serve as solutions to the Yang-Baxter equation.

In
[
7
]
, a comprehensive homological theory of quandles was established, utilizing quandle cocycles to create state-sum invariants for codimension
2
-embeddings. Advancing this work,
[
6
]
develops a homology theory for the Yang-Baxter sets through the application of Yang-Baxter coloring of cubical complexes, and invariants of knots are defined by use of biquandle cocycles. Additionally,
[
22
]
proposes a graphical approach to define a homology theory for Yang-Baxter sets. Remarkably,
[
23
]
proves that both the cubical complex and graphical approaches give rise to the same homology theory.

In
[
7
]
, a quandle cocycle was pivotal in proving that the
2
-twist spun trefoil knot is non-invertible. The remarkable potential of quandle cocycle invariants is further highlighted in
[
24
]
, where they were used to determine the exact triple point number of the
2
-twist spun trefoil knot, which is four. Additionally,
[
1
]
proves the existence of infinitely many non-invertible knotted surfaces by employing quandle cocycle invariants associated with Mochizuki’s
3
-cocycles
[
20
]
. These findings underscore the profound implications of quandle cocycle invariants in knot theory.

In this article, we introduce a notion of twisted set-theoretic Yang-Baxter solution. A twisted set-theoretic Yang-Baxter solution consists of a triplet
(X,f,R)
, where
(X,R)
denotes a Yang-Baxter set and
f:X\to X
is an automorphism of
(X,R)
. We introduce a (co)homology theory for this concept using a graphical approach. Subsequently, we apply this theory to define cohomology for twisted biquandles. We then employ cocycles of twisted biquandles, along with Alexander numbering, to formulate a state-sum invariant for knots and knotted surfaces. We compute a state-sum invariant of
2
-twist spun trefoil and of its reverse orientation.

Throughout the paper we refer to knots and links by the generic term “knots”.

The article is organized as follows. In Section
2
we provide an overview of the Yang-Baxter sets and Yang-Baxter homology theory, focusing on the graphical approach
[
22
]
. Section
3
introduces
twisted set-theoretic Yang-Baxter solutions
. In Section
4
we introduce a (co)homology theory for twisted Yang-Baxter sets and twisted biquandles. Section
5
discusses the computation of second homology groups for certain twisted quandles, highlighting differences from their counterparts in quandles. Section
6
delves into the extension theory of twisted Yang-Baxter sets using cocycles. In Section
7
, we introduce a state-sum knot invariant by utilizing
2
-cocycles for twisted biquandles alongside Alexander numbering of knots. A similar approach is taken in Section
8
to define state-sum invariants for knotted surfaces. Section
9
describes a method for computing state-sum invariants for knotted surfaces using braid charts. In Section
10
, we note that one can analogously define state sum invariants for knots on compact oriented surfaces and broken surface diagrams in orientable compact
3
-manifolds.

2.
Preliminary

Let
X
be a non-empty set and
R:X\times X\to X\times X
an invertible map, satisfying the following
set-theoretic Yang-Baxter equation:

(R\times\mathrm{id}_{X})(\mathrm{id}_{X}\times R)(R\times\mathrm{id}_{X})=(\mathrm{id}_{X}\times R)(R\times\mathrm{id}_{X})(\mathrm{id}_{X}\times R),

where
\mathrm{id}_{X}
denotes the identity map on
X
. Then
R
is termed a
set-theoretic Yang-Baxter operator
, and the pair
(X,R)
is said to be a
set-theoretic Yang-Baxter solution
. Sometimes, for brevity, we call
(X,R)
a
Yang-Baxter set
.

We denote the components of
R
by
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
, that is,
R(x_{1},x_{2})=\big(R_{1}(x_{1},x_{2}),R_{2}(x_{1},x_{2})\big)
for
x_{1},x_{2}\in X
. Moreover,
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muR\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
denotes the inverse of
R
, and
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muR_{1}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
and
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muR_{2}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
are its components.

A Yang-Baxter set
(X,R)
is called a
birack
if

(1)

The map
R_{1}
is
left-invertible
, that is, for any
x,z\in X
, there is a unique
y\in X
such that
R_{1}(x,y)=z
.

(2)

The map
R_{2}
is
right-invertible
, that is, for any
y,w\in X
, there is unique
x\in X
such that
R_{2}(x,y)=w
.

A
biquandle
is a birack
(X,R)
satisfying the
type I condition
, that is, for a given
a\in X
there exists a unique
x\in X
such that
R(x,a)=(x,a)
. Note that the above condition is equivalent to the following: given an element
a\in X
, there exists a unique
x\in X
such that
R(a,x)=(a,x)
. For more details, see
[
3
,
10
]
and
[
6
, Remark 3.3]
.

Examples
.

(1)

Let
(X,*)
be a quandle. Define
R:X\times X\to X\times X
as
R(x,y)=(y,x*y)
. Then
(X,R)
is a biquandle.

(2)

Let
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
be a cyclic group of order
n
. Define
R:\mathbb{Z}_{n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{n}\to\mathbb{Z}_{n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{n}
as
R(x,y)=(y+1,x-1)
. Then
(\mathbb{Z}_{n},R)
is a biquandle.

(3)

Let
G
be a group. Define

as
R(x,y)=(y^{-1},yxy)
and
R^{\prime}(x,y)=(x^{-1}y^{-1}x,y^{2}x)
. Then
(G,R)
and
(G,R^{\prime})
are biquandles, called
Wada biquandles
.

(4)

Let
K
be a commutative ring with unit
1
. Let
\alpha,\beta
are units in
K
such that
(1-\alpha)(1-\beta)=0
. Then
\displaystyle R=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}1-\alpha&\alpha\\
\beta&1-\beta\end{array}\right]
makes the ring

into a biquandle and is known as

.

A
precubical set
is a graded set
P=(P_{n})_{n\geq 0}
with
boundary operators

\partial^{k}_{i}:P_{n}\to P_{n-1}

(n>0,~k=0,1,~i=1,\ldots,n)
satisfying the equations
\partial^{k}_{i}\circ\partial_{j}^{l}=\partial_{j-1}^{l}\circ\partial_{i}^{k}
for
k,l=0,1,
and
i<j
.

There are two approaches for homology theory of the set-theoretic Yang-Baxter sets: one is algebraic
[

]
, and the other is graphical
[

]
. Both of them give the the same theory
[

Let
(X,R)
be a Yang-Baxter set. Let

be the free abelian group generated by the elements in

. For each

, define a homomorphism

\displaystyle\partial_{n}\colon

\displaystyle C_{n}^{\operatorname{YB}}\to C_{n-1}^{\operatorname{YB}}(X)~\textrm{as}

\displaystyle\partial_{n}=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i}\partial_{i,n},

where
\partial_{i,n}=\partial^{l}_{i,n}-\partial^{r}_{i,n}
. Graphically, the maps

,

and

(C_{*}^{\operatorname{YB}}(X),\partial_{n})

4.
Twisted Yang-Baxter (co)homology theory

In this section we introduce a cohomology theory for twisted-Yang Baxter sets, which we term the
twisted set-theoretic Yang-Baxter cohomology theory
.

Let
(X,f,R)
be a twisted Yang-Baxter set, and
(t,m_{1},m_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}
. Consider the ring
\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}[T,T^{-1}]
of Laurent polynomials over the integers. For each integer
n>0
,
C_{n}^{\operatorname{TYB}}(X)
is a
\Lambda
-module over the generating set
X^{n}
, where

T.(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})=(f(x_{1}),f(x_{2}),\ldots,f(x_{n})),

for all
(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})\in X^{n}
.
For each
n
, we define the
n
-boundary homomorphism

\partial_{n}^{(t,m_{1},m_{2})}:C_{n}^{\operatorname{TYB}}(X)\to C_{n-1}^{\operatorname{TYB}}(X)

as

\partial_{n}^{(t,m_{1},m_{2})}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i}\partial^{(t,m_{1},m_{2})}_{i,n},

where

\partial_{i,n}^{(t,m_{1},m_{2})}=\partial_{i,n}^{l,(t,m_{1})}-\partial_{i,n}^{r,(t,m_{2})}.

The face maps
\partial_{i,n}^{l,(t,m_{1})}
and
\partial_{i,n}^{r,(t,m_{2})}
are illustrated graphically in Figures

and
5
.

Figure 5.

We define
C_{0}^{\operatorname{TYB}}(X)=\{0\}
. It is easy to check that the graded set
(X^{n})_{n\geq 0}
along with the boundary maps
\partial_{i,n}^{l,(t,m_{1})}
and
\partial_{i,n}^{r,(t,m_{2})}
, forms a precubical set. This implies that
C_{*}^{\operatorname{TYB},(t,m_{1},m_{2})}(X):=\big(C_{n}^{\operatorname{TYB}}(X),\partial_{n}^{(t,m_{1},m_{2})}\big)
is a chain complex.

For a given
\Lambda
-module

, consider the chain and cochain complexes

\displaystyle C_{*}^{\operatorname{TYB},(t,m_{1},m_{2})}(X;M)=C_{*}^{\operatorname{TYB},(t,m_{1},m_{2})}(X)\otimes_{\Lambda}M,

\displaystyle\partial^{(t,m_{1},m_{2})}=\partial^{(t,m_{1},m_{2})}\otimes_{\Lambda}\mathrm{id}_{M};

\displaystyle C^{*}_{\operatorname{TYB},(t,m_{1},m_{2})}(X;M)=\operatorname{Hom}_{\Lambda}\big(C_{*}^{\operatorname{TYB},(t,m_{1},m_{2})}(X),M\big)

\displaystyle\delta_{(t,m_{1},m_{2})}=\operatorname{Hom}(\partial^{(t,m_{1},m_{2})},\mathrm{id}_{M}).

n
-th homology and cohomology groups of these complexes are called

-
(m_{1},m_{2})
- twisted Yang-Baxter homology group

Let

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
V=M\times X
,

and

S((a,x),(b,y))=(~(b+T^{m_{1}}\phi_{1}(x,y),R_{1}(x,y)),(a+T^{m_{2}}\phi_{2}(x,y),R_{2}(x,y))~)

is a Yang-Baxter set, then

Proof.

We compute

\displaystyle(S\times\mathrm{id}_{V})(\mathrm{id}_{V}\times S)(S\times\mathrm{id}_{V})((a,x),(b,y),(c,z))=

\displaystyle(~(c+T^{m_{1}}\phi_{1}(R_{2}(x,y),z)+T^{m_{1}}\phi_{1}(R_{1}(x,y),R_{1}(R_{2}(x,y),z)),R_{1}(R_{1}(x,y),R_{1}(R_{2}(x,y),z))),

\displaystyle(a+T^{m_{2}}\phi_{2}(x,y)+T^{m_{2}}\phi_{2}(R_{2}(x,y),z),R_{2}(R_{2}(x,y),z))~)

\displaystyle(\mathrm{id}_{V}\times S)(S\times\mathrm{id}_{V})(\mathrm{id}_{V}\times S)((a,x),(b,y),(c,z))=

\displaystyle(~(c+T^{m_{1}}\phi_{1}(y,z)+T^{m_{1}}\phi_{1}(x,R_{1}(y,z)),R_{1}(x,R_{1}(y,z))),

\displaystyle(b+T^{m_{2}}\phi_{2}(y,z)+T^{m_{1}}\phi_{1}(R_{2}(x,R_{1}(y,z)),R_{2}(y,z)),R_{1}(R_{2}(x,R_{1}(y,z)),R_{2}(y,z))),

10.
Concluding remarks

Analogous to Section
7
and Section
8
, twisted biquandle cocycles can be used to defined invariants for knot diagrams on compact oriented surfaces up to Reidemeister moves and broken surface diagrams in compact oriented
4
-manifolds up to Roseman moves. Here we briefly describe the process for knots on compact surfaces.

Let
K
be an oriented knot diagram on a compact oriented surface
S
, and a finite twisted biquandle
(X,f,R)
, where the order of
f
is
p\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. Let
\phi\in Z^{2}_{\operatorname{TBQ},(0,n)}(X;M)
, where
M
is a
\mathbb{Z}[T,T^{-1}]
-module and
\mathcal{C}
a coloring of
K
by
(X,f,R)
. The diagram
K
divides
S
into regions. Fix a base region denoted by
R_{0}
, and define
(\mod p)
-
Alexander numbering
of the regions (and crossings) as done in Section
7
, where
\mathcal{L}(R_{0})=0\mod p
. If
(\mod p)
-Alexander numbering is not defined, then set the state sum invariant
\Phi(K)
to be
0
. Otherwise, to each crossing
\tau
, assign a
twisted Boltzmann weight

B_{\operatorname{T}}(\tau,\mathcal{C})=T^{-n\mathcal{L}(\tau)}(\phi(x,y))^{\epsilon(\tau)}
as done in Section
7
, and define the state-sum

\Phi(K)=\sum_{\mathcal{C}}\prod_{\tau}B_{\operatorname{T}}(\tau,\mathcal{C})

Note that
\Phi(K)
depends on the choice of the base region
R_{0}
. To overcome this, we consider
\Phi(K)
up to action of the free abelian group generated by
T
. Thus we have the following result.

Theorem 10.1
.

The state-sum is well defined up to the action of
\mathbb{Z}=<T>
for knots on surfaces.

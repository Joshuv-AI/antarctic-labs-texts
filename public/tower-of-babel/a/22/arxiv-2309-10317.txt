Every concept that is valid for graphs automatically applies to digraphs too.

in

,

D
.
For

, we denote

the induced subgraph of
D
obtained by removing the vertices in
P
and the
edges incident to these vertices. If
P=\{x\}
consists of a single element, then we write
D\setminus x
for

.
For

, we define

to be the subgraph of
D

When
W=\{e\}
consists of a single edge, we write
D\setminus e
instead of
D\setminus\{e\}
.
An
oriented
path or
oriented
cycle is an orientation of a
path or cycle in which each vertex dominates its successor in the sequence.
An
oriented
acyclic graph is a simple digraph without oriented cycles.
An
oriented tree
or

is an oriented acyclic graph formed by orienting the edges of undirected acyclic graphs.
A

An

is a disjoint union of oriented trees. A

is a disjoint union of rooted trees.

A vertex-weighted oriented graph is a triplet
D=(V(D),E(D),w)

is the vertex set,

V(D)

Given a vertex-weighted oriented graph
D=(V,E,w)

n

For any homogeneous ideal

S=k[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]

0\rightarrow\bigoplus\limits_{j}S(-j)^{\beta_{p,j}(I)}\rightarrow\bigoplus\limits_{j}S(-j)^{\beta_{p-1,j}(I)}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow\bigoplus\limits_{j}S(-j)^{\beta_{0,j}(I)}\rightarrow I\rightarrow 0,

where the maps are exact,

, and

is the

-module obtained by shifting

by

. The number

\beta_{i,j}(I)

(i,j)
-th graded Betti number of

that equals the number of minimal generators of degree

in the

i
th syzygy module of
I
.
Of particular interests are the following invariants which measure the “size” of the minimal graded
free resolution of
I
.
The projective dimension of
I
, denoted pd
(I)
, is defined to be

\mbox{pd}\,(I):=\mbox{max}\,\{i\ |\ \beta_{i,j}(I)\neq 0\}.

The regularity of
I
, denoted
\mbox{reg}\,(I)
, is defined by

\mbox{reg}\,(I):=\mbox{max}\,\{j-i\ |\ \beta_{i,j}(I)\neq 0\}.

We now derive some formulas for
\mbox{pd}\,(I)
and
\mbox{reg}\,(I)
in some special cases by using some
tools developed in

Definition 2.1

Let

\beta_{i,j}(I)=\beta_{i,j}(J)+\beta_{i,j}(K)+\beta_{i-1,j}(J\cap K)\hskip 5.69054pt\mbox{for all}\hskip 5.69054pti,j\geq 0,

\beta_{i-1,j}(J\cap K)=0\hskip 5.69054pt\mbox{if}\hskip 5.69054pti=0

3.
Projective dimension and regularity of edge ideals of vertex-weighted oriented unicyclic graphs

In this section, we will provide some exact formulas for the projective dimension and the regularity of the edge ideals of vertex-weighted unicyclic graphs. Meanwhile, we give some examples to show the projective dimension and the regularity of the edge
ideals of vertex-weighted oriented unicyclic graphs are related to direction selection and
the assumption that
w(x)\geq 2
if
d(x)\neq 1
cannot be dropped.
We shall start from the definition of the union of some digraphs.

Definition 3.1
.

Let

for

with vertex set

and edge set
\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{k}E(D_{i})
.

The following two lemmas is needed to facilitate calculating the projective dimension

\displaystyle x_{i_{s}}y_{s1}^{w_{y_{s1}}},\ldots,x_{i_{s}}y_{s,t_{s}}^{w_{y_{s,t_{s}}}}).

Case (2): If there exists some

T_{j}
is a rooted tree but not a star digraph for any

\displaystyle I(D)=

\displaystyle(x_{1}x_{2}^{w_{2}}\!,\ldots,x_{n-1}x_{n}^{w_{n}}\!,x_{n}x_{1}^{w_{1}}\!,x_{i_{1}}y_{11}^{w_{y_{11}}}\!\!\!,\ldots,x_{i_{1}}y_{1,t_{1}}^{w_{y_{1,t_{1}}}}\!\!\!\!\!,x_{i_{2}}y_{21}^{w_{y_{21}}}\!\!\!,\ldots,x_{i_{2}}y_{2,t_{2}}^{w_{y_{2,t_{2}}}}\!\!\!\!,

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
D=(V(D),E(D),w)
be a vertex-weighted oriented unicyclic graph such that

(1)

\mbox{reg}\,(I(D))=\sum\limits_{x\in V(D)}w(x)-|E(D)|+1

(2)

\mbox{pd}\,(I(D))=|E(D)|-1

Proof.

Let
D_{1},\ldots,D_{m}

D

2.6
, we get

\displaystyle\mbox{reg}\,(I(D))

\displaystyle=\mbox{reg}\,(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}I(D_{i}))=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\mbox{reg}\,(I(D_{i}))-(m-1),

\displaystyle\mbox{pd}\,(I(D))

\displaystyle=\mbox{pd}\,(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}I(D_{i}))=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\mbox{pd}\,(I(D_{i}))+(m-1).

By Lemma
3.2
, it is enough to show

\displaystyle\mbox{reg}\,(I(D))

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{x\in V(D)}w(x)-|E(D)|+1,

\displaystyle\mbox{pd}\,(I(D))

\displaystyle=|E(D)|-1

The conclusion follows from Theorem
3.4
.
∎

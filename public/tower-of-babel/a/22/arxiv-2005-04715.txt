A Compositional Sheaf-Theoretic Framework for Event-Based Systems (Extended Version)
Gioele Zardini, David I. Spivak, Andrea Censi ∗, Emilio Frazzoli ∗

Abstract
A compositional sheaf-theoretic framework for the modeling of complex event-based systems is presented. We show that event-based systems are machines, with inputs and outputs, and that they can be composed with machines of different types, all within a unified, sheaf-theoretic formalism. We take robotic systems as an exemplar of complex systems and rigorously describe actuators, sensors, and algorithms using this framework.

Chapter 1
Introduction

This paper presents a unified modeling framework for event-based systems, focusing on the standard example of cybernetic systems. Specifically, we present event-based systems as machines, showing how to compose them in various ways and how to describe diverse interactions (between systems that are continuous, event-based, synchronous, etc). We showcase the efficacy of our framework by using it to describe a robotic system, and we demonstrate how apparently different robotic components, such as actuators, sensors, and algorithms, can be described within a common sheaf-theoretic formalism.

Definition 6.1

(Category)
.

A
category

\mathsf{C}
is defined by describing four components: Its
objects
, its
morphisms
,
its
identities
, and its
composition operations
. A category consists of:

1.

Objects
: A collection
\ob_{C}
, whose elements are called
objects
.

2.

Morphisms
: For every pair of objects
X,Y\in\ob_{C}
, there is
a way to define the set
\Hom_{C}(X,Y)
, which is called the “hom-set from

X
to
Y
”. The elements of this set are called
morphisms
.

3.

Identity morphism
: For each object
X
, there is
an element
\text{id}_{X}\in\Hom_{C}(X,X)
which is called
the identity morphism in
X
.

4.

Composition operation
: Given a morphism
f\in\Hom(X,Y)
and a morphism
g\in\Hom(Y,Z)
, then
there exists a morphism
f\fatsemi g\in\Hom(X,Z)
.

Additionally, there are two properties that must hold:

(a)

Unitality: For a morphism
f\in\Hom(x,y)
:

\text{id}_{x}\fatsemi f=f\fatsemi\text{id}_{y}.

(b)

Associativity: Given a morphism

such that for any
x,y,z\in M
, the following holds:

1.

\mathrm{dist}(x,y)=0\Leftrightarrow x=y
.

2.

\mathrm{dist}(x,y)=(y,x)
.

3.

\mathrm{dist}(x,z)\leq\mathrm{dist}(x,y)+\mathrm{dist}(y,z)
.

The function
\mathrm{dist}
is also called
distance function
.

Proposition 6.37

(
Proposition

2.2
)
.

\mathsf{Int}
is indeed a category: It satisfies associativity and unitality.

Proof.

In the following, we check unitality and associativity. Given a morphism
\text{Ph}_{a}:\ell\rightarrow\ell^{\prime}
,
\text{id}_{\ell}:\ell\rightarrow\ell
, and
\text{id}_{\ell^{\prime}}:\ell^{\prime}\rightarrow\ell^{\prime}
, with
\text{id}_{\ell}=\text{Ph}_{0}\in\mathsf{Int}(\ell,\ell)
and
\text{id}_{\ell^{\prime}}=\text{Ph}_{0}^{\prime}\in\mathsf{Int}(\ell^{\prime},\ell^{\prime})
, we know that

\begin{split}\text{id}_{\ell}\fatsemi\text{Ph}_{a}&=\text{Ph}_{0}\fatsemi\text{Ph}_{a}\\
&=\text{Ph}_{a},\end{split}

and

\begin{split}\text{Ph}_{a}\fatsemi\text{id}_{\ell^{\prime}}&=\text{Ph}_{a}\fatsemi\text{Ph}_{0}^{\prime}\\
&=\text{Ph}_{a}.\end{split}

This is unitality. Furthermore, given
\text{Ph}_{a}:\ell\rightarrow\ell^{\prime}
,
\text{Ph}_{b}:\ell^{\prime}\rightarrow\ell^{\prime\prime}
, and
\text{Ph}_{c}:\ell^{\prime\prime}\rightarrow\ell^{\prime\prime\prime}
, we know that

\begin{split}\left(\text{Ph}_{a}\fatsemi\text{Ph}_{b}\right)\fatsemi\text{Ph}_{c}&=\text{Ph}_{a+b}\fatsemi\text{Ph}_{c}\\
&=\text{Ph}_{a+b+c},\end{split}

and

\begin{split}\text{Ph}_{a}\fatsemi\left(\text{Ph}_{b}\fatsemi\text{Ph}_{c}\right)&=\text{Ph}_{a}\fatsemi\text{Ph}_{b+c}\\
&=\text{Ph}_{a+b+c}.\end{split}

This is associativity.
∎

Proposition 6.38

(
Proposition

3.4
)
.

Ev
is functorial: Given a function
f\colon A\to B
there is an induced morphism
\text{Ev}_{f}:\text{Ev}_{A}\to\text{Ev}_{B}
in
\widetilde{\mathsf{Int}}
, and this assignment preserves identities and composition.

Proof.

We define a functor
\text{Ev}:\mathsf{Set}\rightarrow\widetilde{\mathsf{Int}}
sending each object
A\in\mathsf{Set}
to an object
\text{Ev}_{A}\in\widetilde{\mathsf{Int}}
. Given a morphism
f\colon A\to B
, we define
\text{Ev}_{f}\colon\text{Ev}_{A}\to\text{Ev}_{B}
on
\ell
to be

\text{Ev}_{f}(\ell)(S,a)\coloneqq(S,a\fatsemi f).

We need to check that composition and identities are preserved. We first show that composition is preserved. Given
f\colon A\to B
and
g\colon B\to C
, one knows

\begin{split}\text{Ev}_{f\fatsemi g}(\ell)(S,a)&=(S,a\fatsemi(f\fatsemi g))\\
&=(S,(a\fatsemi f)\fatsemi g)\\
&=(\text{Ev}_{f}\fatsemi\text{Ev}_{g})(S,a).\ \end{split}

We now show that identities are preserved:

\begin{split}\text{Ev}_{\text{id}_{A}}(\ell)(S,a)&=(S,a\fatsemi\text{id}_{A})\\
&=(S,a)\\
&=\text{id}_{\text{Ev}_{A}(\ell)}(S,a).\end{split}

∎

Proposition 6.39

(
Proposition

3.5
)
.

For any set
A
, the presheaf
\text{Ev}_{A}
is in fact a sheaf.

Proof.

Consider
\ell,\ell^{\prime}\in\mathsf{Int}
and
e\in\text{Ev}_{A}(\ell)
,
e^{\prime}\in\text{Ev}_{A}(\ell^{\prime})
with

\begin{split}e(\ell)&\colon\{(T,s)\mid T\subseteq\tilde{\ell}\text{ finite },s:T\rightarrow A\},\\
e(\ell^{\prime})&\colon\{(T^{\prime},s^{\prime})\mid T^{\prime}\subseteq\tilde{\ell^{\prime}}\text{ finite },s^{\prime}\colon T^{\prime}\rightarrow A\},\end{split}

and suppose that

{e}|_{[\ell,\ell]}={e^{\prime}}|_{[0,0]}.

This last condition can be rewritten as

\begin{cases}(\varnothing,!)&\text{if }\ell\notin T\\
(\{\ell\},s(\ell))&\text{if }\ell\in T\end{cases}=\begin{cases}(\varnothing,!)&\text{if }0\notin T^{\prime}\\
(\{0\},s^{\prime}(0))&\text{if }0\in T^{\prime}\end{cases}

or in words, if either both are
nothing
or both are just
a
for the same
a\in A
. Then, we can
glue

e
and
e^{\prime}
together to form a
unique

\bar{e}\in\text{Ev}_{A}(\ell+\ell^{\prime})

\bar{e}\coloneqq\left(T\cup\{t^{\prime}+\ell|t^{\prime}\in T^{\prime}\},s^{\prime\prime}\right),

where
s^{\prime\prime}
is the universal map out of the union restricting to both
s
and
s^{\prime}
. Since

{\bar{e}}|_{[0,\ell]}=e\qquad\text{and}\qquad{\bar{e}}|_{[\ell,\ell+\ell^{\prime}]}=e^{\prime},

\bar{e}
is indeed the required gluing.
∎

Proposition 6.40

(
Proposition

3.7
)
.

\text{Ev}\colon(\mathsf{Set},\odot,\varnothing)\to(\widetilde{\mathsf{Int}},\times,1)
is a strong monoidal functor:
1\cong\text{Ev}_{\varnothing}
and for any sets
A,B
, we have

\text{Ev}_{A}\times\text{Ev}_{B}\cong\text{Ev}_{A\odot B}.

Proof.

Consider the functor

\text{Ev}:\mathsf{Set}\rightarrow\widetilde{\mathsf{Int}},

with the identity isomorphism

e:1\rightarrow\text{Ev}_{\varnothing},

serving as the unit (see
Example

2.6
). There is a natural isomorphism

\mu_{A,A^{\prime}}\colon\text{Ev}_{A}\times\text{Ev}_{A^{\prime}}\to\text{Ev}_{A\odot A^{\prime}}\cong\text{Ev}_{A+A^{\prime}+A\times A^{\prime}},\quad\forall A,A^{\prime}\in\mathsf{Set}.

To define it, we first give the components and then show that they are natural. Recall

\text{Ev}_{A}(\ell)=\{S\subseteq\tilde{\ell}\text{ finite},a\colon S\rightarrow A\}.

Then,

\begin{split}\text{Ev}_{A}\times\text{Ev}_{A^{\prime}}&=\{(S,a),(S^{\prime},a^{\prime}),a\colon S\to A,a^{\prime}\colon S^{\prime}\to A^{\prime}\}\\
&=\{s_{1},\ldots,s_{n},a_{1},\ldots,a_{n},s_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,s_{n^{\prime}}^{\prime},a_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,a_{n^{\prime}}^{\prime}\}\\
&=\{S^{\prime\prime},c\colon S^{\prime\prime}\to A+A^{\prime}+A\times A^{\prime}\}\\
&=\text{Ev}_{A+A^{\prime}+A\times A^{\prime}}\\
\end{split}

where
S^{\prime\prime}=S\cup S^{\prime}\subseteq\tilde{\ell}
and
c
is defined as follows:

\begin{split}c\colon S\cup S^{\prime}&\to A+A^{\prime}+A\times A^{\prime}\\
t&\mapsto c(t)\coloneqq\begin{cases}a(t),&t\in S,t\notin S^{\prime},\\
a^{\prime}(t),&t\notin S,t\in S^{\prime},\\
\left({a(t)},{a^{\prime}(t)}\right),&t\in S,t\in S^{\prime}.\end{cases}\end{split}

This is natural meaning that for any
f\colon A\to B
and
f^{\prime}\colon A^{\prime}\to B^{\prime}
, the square
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{Ev}_{A}\times\text{Ev}_{A^{\prime}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{Ev}_{A+A^{\prime}+A\times A^{\prime}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{Ev}_{B}\times\text{Ev}_{B^{\prime}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{Ev}_{B+B^{\prime}+B\times B^{\prime}}}

commutes. Associativity and unitality follow easily from the functor and monoidal structure definitions.
∎

Introduction

We consider simple, undirected graphs in this paper. The standard
graph-theoretical terminology and notations not defined here follow

[
3
]
. For a graph
G
, let
V(G)
and
E(G)
represent its
sets of vertices and edges, respectively. Let
|E(G)|=m
be the size
of
G
. The complement of
G
is conventionally denoted by

\overline{G}
. For a vertex
v\in V(G)
,
deg_{G}(v)
is the degree
of
v
. The maximum and minimum degrees are, respectively, denoted
by
\Delta
and
\delta
. Like degrees, distance is a fundamental
concept of graph theory
[
4
]
. For two vertices
u,v\in V(G)

with connected
G
, the
distance

d(u,v)=d_{G}(u,v)
between
these two vertices is defined as the length of a shortest path
connecting them. An excellent survey paper on this subject can be
found in
[
12
]
.

The above classical graph distance has been extended by Chartrand et
al. in 1989 to the Steiner distance, which since then has become an
essential concept of graph theory. Given a graph
G(V,E)
and a
vertex set
S\subseteq V(G)
containing no less than two vertices,

an
S
-Steiner tree
(or
an
S
-tree
,
a Steiner
tree connecting
S
) is defined as a subgraph
T(V^{\prime},E^{\prime})
of
G

which is a subtree satisfying
S\subseteq V^{\prime}
. If
G
is connected
with order no less than
2
and
S\subseteq V
is nonempty, the

Steiner distance

d(S)
among the vertices of
S
(sometimes
simply put as the distance of
S
) is the minimum size of connected
subgraph whose vertex sets contain the set
S
. Clearly, for a
connected subgraph
H\subseteq G
with
S\subseteq V(H)
and

|E(H)|=d(S)
,
H
is a tree. When
T
is subtree of
G
, we have

d(S)=\min\{|E(T)|\,\,,\,S\subseteq V(T)\}
. For
S=\{u,v\}
,

d(S)=d(u,v)
reduces to the classical distance between the two
vertices
u
and
v
. Another basic observation is that if
|S|=k
,

d(S)\geq k-1
. For more results regarding varied properties of the
Steiner distance, we refer to the reader to
[
1
,
5
,
6
,
10
,
12
,
24
]
.

In
[
16
]
, Li et al. generalized the concept of Wiener index
through incorporating the Steiner distance. The
Steiner

k
-Wiener index

\operatorname{SW}_{k}(G)
of
G
is defined by

\operatorname{SW}_{k}(G)=\sum_{\overset{S\subseteq V(G)}{|S|=k}}d(S)\,.

For
k=2
, it is easy to see the Steiner Wiener index coincides with
the ordinary Wiener index. The interesting range of the Steiner

k
-Wiener index
\operatorname{SW}_{k}
resides in
2\leq k\leq n-1
, and the two trivial cases give
\operatorname{SW}_{1}(G)=0
and

\operatorname{SW}_{n}(G)=n-1
.

Gutman
[
13
]
studied the Steiner degree distance, which
is a generalization of ordinary degree distance. Formally, the

k
-center Steiner degree distance

\operatorname{SDD}_{k}(G)

of
G
is given as

\operatorname{SDD}_{k}(G)=\sum_{\overset{S\subseteq V(G)}{|S|=k}}\left(\sum_{v\in S}deg_{G}(v)\right)d_{G}(S)\,.

The Gutman index of a connected graph
G
is defined as

\operatorname{Gut}(G)=\sum_{u,v\in V(G)}\,deg_{G}(u)\,deg_{G}(v)\,d_{G}(u,v).

The Gutman index of graphs attracts attention very recently. For its basic properties and applications, including various lower and upper bounds, see
[
7
,
8
,
11
]
and the references cited therein.
Recently, Mao and Das
[
18
]
further extended the concept of
Gutman index by incorporating Steiner distance and consider the
weights as a multiplication of degrees. The
Steiner
k
-Gutman
index

\operatorname{SGut}_{k}(G)
of
G
is defined by

\operatorname{SGut}_{k}(G)=\sum_{\overset{S\subseteq V(G)}{|S|=k}}\left(\prod_{v\in S}deg_{G}(v)\right)d_{G}(S)\,.

Note that this index is a natural generalization of
classical Gutman index. In particular, for
k=2
,
\operatorname{SGut}_{k}(G)=Gut(G)
. This is the reason the product of the
degrees comes to the definition of Steiner
k
-Gutman index.
The
weighting of multiplication of degree or expected degree has also
been extensively explored in, for example, the field of random
graphs
[
19
,
20
]
and proves to be very prolific. For
more results on Steiner Wiener index, Steiner degree distance, and
Steiner Gutman index, we refer to the reader to
[
13
,
16
,
18
,
21
,
22
,
23
]
.

For a given a graph parameter
f(G)
and a positive integer
n
, the
well-known
Nordhaus-Gaddum problem
is to determine sharp
bounds for:
(1)

f(G)+f(\overline{G})
and
(2)

f(G)\cdot f(\overline{G})
over the class of connected graph
G
, with order

n
,
m
edges, maximum degree
\Delta
and minimum degree
\delta

characterizing the extremal graphs. Many Nordhaus-Gaddum type
relations have attracted considerable attention in graph theory.
Comprehensive results regarding this topic can be found in e.g.

[
2
,
17
,
15
,
27
,
28
]
.

In Section 2, we obtain sharp upper and lower bounds on
{\rm SGut}_{k}
of graph
G
. In Section 3, we obtain sharp upper and lower
bounds of
{\rm SGut}_{k}(G)+{\rm SGut}_{k}(\overline{G})
and
{\rm SGut}_{k}(G)\cdot{\rm SGut}_{k}(\overline{G})
for a connected graph

G
in terms of
n
,
m
, maximum degree
\Delta
and minimum degree

\delta
.

2
Sharp bounds for Steiner Gutman index

In
[
18
]
, the following results have been obtained:

Lemma 2.1
.

[
18
]

Let
K_{n}
,
S_{n}
and
P_{n}
be the complete graph, star graph and
path graph of order
n
, respectively, and let
k
be an integer
such that
2\leq k\leq n
. Then

(1)

{\rm SGut}_{k}(K_{n})=\binom{n}{k}(n-1)^{n}(k-1)
;

(2)

{\rm SGut}_{k}(S_{n})=(kn-2k+1)\binom{n-1}{k-1}
;

(3)

{\rm SGut}_{k}(P_{n})=2^{k}(k-1)\binom{n}{k+1}
.

For connected graph
G
of order
n
with
m
edges, the authors in

[
18
]
derived the following upper and lower bounds on
{\rm SGut}_{k}(G)
.

Lemma 2.2
.

[
18
]

Let
G
be a connected graph of order
n
with
m
edges, and let

k
be an integer with
2\leq k\leq n
. Then

(n-1)\left(\frac{2m}{k}\right)^{k}\binom{n-1}{k-1}^{k}\geq{\rm SGut}_{k}(G)\geq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}2m(k-1)\binom{n-1}{k-1}&\mbox{\rm if}~\delta\geq 2\\[5.69054pt]
(k-1)\binom{n}{k}&\mbox{\rm if}~\delta=1.\end{array}\right.

We now give a lower and upper bounds on
{\rm SGut}_{k}(G)
in terms
of
n
,
m
, maximum degree
\Delta
and minimum degree
\delta
:

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
G
be a connected graph of order
n\geq 3
with
m
edges and
maximum degree
\Delta
, minimum degree
\delta
. Also let
k
be an
integer with
2\leq k\leq n
. Then

2m(n-1){n-1\choose k-1}\frac{\Delta^{k-1}}{k}\geq{\rm SGut}_{k}(G)\geq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}2m(k-1){n-1\choose k-1}\frac{\delta^{k-1}}{k}&\mbox{\rm if}~\delta\geq 2\\[11.38109pt]
k{p\choose k}+2^{q}(k-1)\left[{n\choose k}-{p\choose k}\right]&\mbox{\rm if}~\delta=1,\end{array}\right.

where
p
is the number of pendant vertices in
G
, and

q=\max\{k-p,1\}
. The equality of upper bound holds if and only if

G
is a regular graph with
k=n
. The equality of lower bound holds
if and only if
G
is a regular
(n-k+1)
-connected graph of order

n

(\delta\geq 2)
, or
G\cong P_{n}
and
k=n>3

(\delta=1)
, or
G\cong P_{3}
and
k=2

(\delta=1)
.

Proof.
Upper bound: For any
S\subseteq V(G)
and
|S|=k
, we have
k-1\leq d_{G}(S)\leq n-1
, and hence

(k-1)\sum_{\overset{S\subseteq V(G)}{|S|=k}}\left(\prod_{v\in S}deg_{G}(v)\right)\leq{\rm SGut}_{k}(G)\leq(n-1)\sum_{\overset{S\subseteq V(G)}{|S|=k}}\left(\prod_{v\in S}deg_{G}(v)\right)\,.

(1)

Let

M=\sum_{\overset{S\subseteq V(G)}{|S|=k}}\left(\prod_{v\in S}deg_{G}(v)\right)=\sum_{\{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{k}\}\subseteq V(G)}deg_{G}(v_{1})deg_{G}(v_{2})\cdots deg_{G}(v_{k}).

and

N=\sum_{\{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{k}\}\subseteq V(G)}[deg_{G}(v_{1})+deg_{G}(v_{2})+\cdots+deg_{G}(v_{k})].

We first prove the upper bound. Without loss of generality, we can
assume that
deg_{G}(v_{1})\leq deg_{G}(v_{2})\leq\ldots\leq deg_{G}(v_{k})
.
Since

\displaystyle deg_{G}(v_{1})deg_{G}(v_{2})\ldots deg_{G}(v_{k})\leq\Delta^{k-1}deg_{G}(v_{1})

(2)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta^{k-1}}{k}(deg_{G}(v_{1})+deg_{G}(v_{2})+\cdots+deg_{G}(v_{k})),

(3)

it follows that

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{k}\}\subseteq V(G)}deg_{G}(v_{1})deg_{G}(v_{2})\ldots deg_{G}(v_{k})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta^{k-1}}{k}\sum_{\{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{k}\}\subseteq V(G)}[deg_{G}(v_{1})+deg_{G}(v_{2})+\cdots+deg_{G}(v_{k})]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta^{k-1}}{k}N.

For each
v\in V(G)
, there are
{n-1\choose k-1}

k
-subsets in

G
such that each of them contains
v
. The contribution of vertex

v
is exactly
{n-1\choose k-1}deg_{G}(v)
. From the arbitrariness of

v
, we have

N={n-1\choose k-1}\sum_{v\in V(G)}deg_{G}(v)=2m{n-1\choose k-1},

and hence

{\rm SGut}_{k}(G)\leq(n-1)M\leq(n-1)\frac{\Delta^{k-1}}{k}N=2m(n-1){n-1\choose k-1}\frac{\Delta^{k-1}}{k}.

(4)

Suppose that the left equality holds. Then all the inequalities in the
above must be equalities. From the equality in (
3
), one can
easily see that
G
is a regular
graph. From the equality in (
4
), we have
d(S)=n-1
for any
S\subseteq V(G)
,
|S|=k
.
Since
G
is connected, then there exists an
S\subseteq V(G)
such that
|d_{G}(S)|=k-1
.
If

, then one can easily see that the upper bound is strict as

for some

. Otherwise,

. Since

is connected, we have
|d_{G}(S)|=n-1
for any

. Hence
G
is a regular graph with
k=n
.

Conversely, one can see easily that the left equality holds for regular graph with
k=n
.

Lower bound:
Without loss of generality, we can assume that
deg_{G}(v_{1})\leq deg_{G}(v_{2})\leq\ldots\leq deg_{G}(v_{k})
. First we assume that
\delta\geq 2
. Then

\displaystyle deg_{G}(v_{1})deg_{G}(v_{2})\cdots deg_{G}(v_{k})\geq\delta^{k-1}deg_{G}(v_{k})

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\geq\frac{\delta^{k-1}}{k}(deg_{G}(v_{1})+deg_{G}(v_{2})+\cdots+deg_{G}(v_{k})),~~~

(5)

since

.
Furthermore, we have

\displaystyle{\rm SGut}_{k}(G)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(k-1)\sum_{\{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{k}\}\subseteq V(G)}deg_{G}(v_{1})deg_{G}(v_{2})\ldots deg_{G}(v_{k})~~

(6)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(k-1)\frac{\delta^{k-1}}{k}\sum_{\{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{k}\}\subseteq V(G)}[deg_{G}(v_{1})+deg_{G}(v_{2})+\cdots+deg_{G}(v_{k})]~~

(7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(k-1)\frac{\delta^{k-1}}{k}N

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2m(k-1){n-1\choose k-1}\frac{\delta^{k-1}}{k}.

Next we assume that
\delta=1
. If
deg_{G}(v_{1})=deg_{G}(v_{2})=\cdots=deg_{G}(v_{k})=1

, where

. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle{\rm SGut}_{k}(G)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle k\sum_{\overset{\{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{k}\}\subseteq V(G),}{deg_{G}(v_{1})=deg_{G}(v_{2})=\cdots=deg_{G}(v_{k})=1}}deg_{G}(v_{1})deg_{G}(v_{2})\ldots deg_{G}(v_{k})

(8)

\displaystyle+(k-1)\sum_{\overset{\{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{k}\}\subseteq V(G),}{some~deg_{G}(v_{i})\geq 2}}deg_{G}(v_{1})deg_{G}(v_{2})\ldots deg_{G}(v_{k})~~~

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle k{p\choose k}+2^{q}(k-1)\left[{n\choose k}-{p\choose k}\right].~~~

(9)

Suppose that the right equality holds. Then all the inequalities in the above must be equalities. Suppose that
\delta\geq 2
. From the equality in (

),

d_{G}(S)=k-1
for any

and
|S|=k
, that is,

3
Nordhaus-Gaddum type results on
{\rm SGut}_{k}(G)

We now in a position to give the Nordhaus-Gaddum type results on

{\rm SGut}_{k}(G)
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
G
be a connected graph of order
n
with
m
edges, maximum
degree
\Delta
, minimum degree
\delta
and a connected

\overline{G}
. Also let
k
be an integer with
2\leq k\leq n
.
Then

(1)

{\rm SGut}_{k}(G)+{\rm SGut}_{k}(\overline{G})\leq(n-1)^{2}\binom{n}{k}s_{1}^{k-1}

and

{\rm SGut}_{k}(G)\cdot{\rm SGut}_{k}(\overline{G})\leq 2m(n^{2}-n-2m)(n-1)^{2}\binom{n-1}{k-1}^{2}\frac{\Delta^{k-1}\,(n-\delta-1)^{k-1}}{k^{2}},

where
s_{1}=\max\{\Delta,\,n-\delta-1\}
. Moreover, the upper bounds
are sharp.

(2)

\displaystyle{\rm SGut}_{k}(G)+{\rm SGut}_{k}(\overline{G})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(n-1)(k-1)\binom{n}{k}\,t_{1}^{k-1}&\mbox{\rm if}~\delta\geq 2,\ \Delta\leq n-3\\[8.53581pt]
2m(k-1){n-1\choose k-1}\frac{\delta^{k-1}}{k}+k{n\choose k}&\mbox{\rm if}~\delta\geq 2,\ \Delta=n-2\\[8.53581pt]
k{n\choose k}+[n(n-1)-2m](k-1){n-1\choose k-1}\frac{(n-\Delta-1)^{k-1}}{k}&\mbox{\rm if}~\delta=1,\ \Delta\leq n-3\\[8.53581pt]
2k{n\choose k}&\mbox{\rm if}~\delta=1,\ \Delta=n-2,\end{array}\right.

where
t_{1}=\min\{\delta,\,n-\Delta-1\}
.

(3)

\displaystyle{\rm SGut}_{k}(G)\cdot{\rm SGut}_{k}(\overline{G})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}2m(n^{2}-n-2m)(k-1)^{2}\binom{n-1}{k-1}^{2}\,\frac{\delta^{k-1}\,(n-\Delta-1)^{k-1}}{k^{2}}&\mbox{\rm if}~\delta\geq 2,\ \Delta\leq n-3\\[8.53581pt]
2m(k-1){n\choose k}{n-1\choose k-1}\delta^{k-1}&\mbox{\rm if}~\delta\geq 2,\ \Delta=n-2\\[8.53581pt]
[n(n-1)-2m](k-1){n\choose k}{n-1\choose k-1}(n-\Delta-1)^{k-1}&\mbox{\rm if}~\delta=1,\ \Delta\leq n-3\\[8.53581pt]
k^{2}{n\choose k}^{2}&\mbox{\rm if}~\delta=1,\ \Delta=n-2.\end{array}\right.

Proof.

(1)
From Proposition
2.1
, we have

{\rm SGut}_{k}(G)\leq 2m(n-1){n-1\choose k-1}\frac{\Delta^{k-1}}{k}

and

{\rm SGut}_{k}(\overline{G})\leq[n(n-1)-2m](n-1){n-1\choose k-1}\frac{(n-\delta-1)^{k-1}}{k},

and hence

{\rm SGut}_{k}(G)+{\rm SGut}_{k}(\overline{G})\leq(n-1)^{2}\binom{n}{k}\,s_{1}^{k-1}

and

{\rm SGut}_{k}(G)\cdot{\rm SGut}_{k}(\overline{G})\leq 2m(n^{2}-n-2m)(n-1)^{2}\binom{n-1}{k-1}^{2}\frac{\Delta^{k-1}(n-\delta-1)^{k-1}}{k^{2}}.

(2)
From Proposition
2.1
, if
\delta\geq 2
and

\Delta\leq n-3
, then

\displaystyle{\rm SGut}_{k}(G)+{\rm SGut}_{k}(\overline{G})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 2m(k-1){n-1\choose k-1}\frac{\delta^{k-1}}{k}+[n(n-1)-2m](k-1){n-1\choose k-1}\frac{(n-\Delta-1)^{k-1}}{k}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(n-1)(k-1)\binom{n}{k}\,t^{k-1}_{1}.

If
\delta(G)\geq 2
and
\Delta=n-2
, then

\displaystyle{\rm SGut}_{k}(G)+{\rm SGut}_{k}(\overline{G})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 2m(k-1){n-1\choose k-1}\frac{\delta^{k-1}}{k}+k{p^{\prime}\choose k}+2^{q^{\prime}}(k-1)\left[{n\choose k}-{p^{\prime}\choose k}\right]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 2m(k-1){n-1\choose k-1}\frac{\delta^{k-1}}{k}+k{p^{\prime}\choose k}+2(k-1)\left[{n\choose k}-{p^{\prime}\choose k}\right]

\overline{\Delta}=\overline{\delta}=2
and hence

{\rm SGut}_{k}(G)+{\rm SGut}_{k}(\overline{G})=(n-1)\,\Big[2^{n}+(n-3)^{n}\Big]=(n-1)\,\Big[\Delta^{k}+(n-\delta-1)^{k}\Big]\,\binom{n}{k}.

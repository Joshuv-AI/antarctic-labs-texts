Inhomogeneous percolation on the hierarchical configuration model with a heavy-tailed degree distribution
David Clancy, Jr

Abstract
. We consider inhomogeneous percolation on a hierarchical configuration model with a heavy-tailed degree distribution. This graph is the configuration model where all the half-edges are colored either black or white, and edges are formed by uniformly matching edges of the same color. When only the white half-edges are paired, we provide sufficient conditions for the size and total number of incident black half-edges of the connected components to converge in an ℓ 2 \ell^{2} -sense. The limiting vector is described by an ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} -valued thinned Lévy process. We also establish an ℓ 2 \ell^{2} -limit for the number of vertices in connected components when a critical proportion of the black edges are included. A key part of our analysis is establishing a Feller-type property for the multiplicative coalescent with mass and weight recently studied in [ 10 , 9 ] .



Keywords:
critical percolation, multiplicative coalescent, random graphs, hierarchical configuration model, edge-colored configuration model, thinned Lévy processes

Introduction

There has been a lot of interest in understanding the component structure of random graphs at and near the phase transition where a giant connected component emerges. A major breakthrough came when Aldous
[
1
]
introduced the multiplicative coalsecent to describe the dynamic merging of the connected components of the near-critical Erdős-Rényi random graph
G(n,n^{-1}+\lambda n^{-4/3})
as the parameter

varies. In short, Aldous showed that in the

{\operatorname{MC}}_{1}=({\operatorname{MC}}_{1}({\bf x},t);t\geq 0)
starting at
{\operatorname{MC}}_{1}({\bf x},0)={\bf x}
and taking values in

\ell^{2}_{\downarrow}:=\left\{{\bf x}=(x_{1},x_{2},\dotsm)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\infty}:x_{1}\geq x_{2}\geq\dotsm\geq 0,\,\,\sum_{i}x_{i}^{2}<\infty\right\}

whose dynamics are described as follows. View
{\bf X}
as a vector of masses of countably many blocks listed from largest to smallest. Independently for all pairs of blocks
i
and
j
of respective masses
x_{i}
and
x_{j}
, they merge into single block of mass
x_{i}+x_{j}
at rate
x_{i}x_{j}
. The multiplicative coalescent has been quite useful in describing the component sizes of many near-critical random graph models; see
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
19
,
10
,
9
]
for an incomplete list of references.

It has recently been observed
[
10
,
9
]
that critical bond percolation on the super-critical configuration model is better understood in the discrete using a generalization of the multiplicative coalescent where the merging rates depend on a “hidden” feature called the weight. More formally,
[
10
,
9
]
introduce the multiplicative coalescent with mass and weight to describe the merging of blocks of various masses
(x_{i})_{i\geq 1}
and weights
(y_{i})_{i\geq 1}
where independently for all pairs of blocks:

blocks
i
and
j
merge into a single block of mass
x_{i}+x_{j}
and weight
y_{i}+y_{j}
at rate
y_{i}y_{j}
.

Following
[
10
,
9
]
, we write
{\operatorname{MC}}_{2}({\bf x},{\bf y},t)
for the ordered vector of component masses at time
t\geq 0
. Originally, the multiplicative coalescent with mass and weight (MCMW in the sequel) was used to describe the dynamics of near-critical percolation on the configuration model. Here the object of interest was the size of the connected components; however, the components coalesced at a rate depending on the number of “open half-edges” that were incident to vertices in the component. That is the size of the component was the mass, but the number of incident open half-edges was the weight. In the works mentioned above, asymptotically for each of the large components the weight was approximately a constant multiple of the mass. Consequently, the merging dynamics of MCMW were essentially the multiplicative coalescent run at a different speed:
{\operatorname{MC}}_{2}({\bf x},c{\bf x},t)={\operatorname{MC}}_{1}({\bf x},c^{2}t)
.

In this work, we are interested in inhomogeneous bond percolation on a hierarchical random graph model recently introduced in

,

17

under the name hierarchical configuration model and in

In its full generality, the hierarchical model exhibits arbitrary community structure on the mesoscopic scale, but on the macroscopic scale behaves like a configuration model. We show that critical bond percolation of the edges in the macroscopic configuration model is described asymptotically by the MCMW where the weight vector
{\bf y}

1.1.
Description of Model

Recently in
[
18
,
17

Stegehuis, van der Hofstad and van Leeuwaarden have introduced and studied proprieties of the

The hierarchical configuration model (HCM in the sequel) is a random graph model which is constructed using the configuration model (which gives the macroscopic picture) and then by blowing up each vertex
v
into a graph
F_{v}
. Formally, the HCM is a random graph constructed as follows. A
community

is a connected multigraph
(V,E)
on a finite number of vertices
V
equipped with a
V

\bm{d}=(d_{v}^{(b)};v\in V)
of non-negative integers
d_{v}^{(b)}\in\{0,1,\dotsm\}
. We denote by

in the graph
F
. The superscript “
(b)
” stands for “between” and the superscript “
(w)
” stands for “within.” We view

as the number of

communities to which the vertex
v

to another vertex in the same community.

We will say that
d_{v}^{(b)}
is the
black degree
of the vertex
v
and
d_{v}^{(w)}
is the
white degree
of the vertex
v
. We call the corresponding edges black and white as well.

Given
n
communities
F_{1},F_{2},\dotsm,F_{n}
, we let
V=\bigsqcup_{j\in[n]}V_{j}
. Let
G=(V,E_{{\operatorname{CM}}})
the random graph formed using the configuration model on the vertex set
V
with the degree sequence given by the black degrees
\bm{d}^{(b)}=(d^{(b)}_{v};v\in V)
. That is, each vertex
v\in V
is incident to
d_{v}^{(b)}
many half-edges and we uniformly at random pair these half-edges (and possibly deleting a single unmatched last half-edge). The HCM with communities
F=(F_{j};j\in[n])
is the graph on
V=\bigsqcup_{j\in[n]}V_{j}
and edges
E=E_{{\operatorname{CM}}}\cup\bigcup_{j}E_{j}
and we will denote this graph by
{\operatorname{HCM}}(F)
if the communities are already given.

In the above set-up the mescoscopic communities (formed by the white edges) are deterministic and the randomness comes into play by considering the macroscopic (black) edges. We will be examining the HCM where both the mesoscopic communities and the macroscopic graph are formed using the configuration model. More formally, let
\bm{d}^{(w)}=(d_{1}^{(w)},\dotsm,d_{n}^{(w)})
be a sequence of positive integers and let
\bm{d}^{(b)}=(d_{1}^{(b)},\dotsm,d_{n}^{(b)})
be a sequence of non-negative integers. We will assume that

\ell^{(*)}_{n}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}^{(\ast)}\text{ is even }\hskip 18.49988pt\text{for }\ast\in\{b,w\}.

The graph
{\operatorname{HCM}}_{n}(\bm{d}^{(w)},\bm{d}^{(b)})
is a random graph on
[n]
constructed as follows. Start with
n
vertices
1,2,\dotsm,n
where vertex
i
has
d_{i}^{(w)}
many white half-edges connected to it and
d_{i}^{(b)}
many black half-edges connected to it. Uniformly at random generate a matching of the
\ell^{(w)}
many white half-edges and independently generate a uniform matching of the
\ell^{(b)}
many black half-edges. For each pair of half-edges in each matching, turn the two half-edges into an edge in
{\operatorname{HCM}}_{n}(\bm{d}^{(w)},\bm{d}^{(b)})
.

We also note that this model also appears in
[
15
]
under the name
edge-colored configuration model
and is one reason why we call the edges white and black. This is also an example of a graph model which is a
network of networks

[
5
]
. We prefer to stick with the name hierarchical configuration model.

We make the formation of the macroscopic graph dynamic. Attach an independent exponential rate
1
clock to each black edge. We set
{\mathcal{G}}_{n}=({\mathcal{G}}_{n}(s);s\geq 0)

where
{\mathcal{G}}_{n}(s)
includes all the white edges and only the black edges whose clock has rung before time

. Observe that
{\mathcal{G}}_{n}(s)
is obtained by
{\operatorname{HCM}}_{n}(\bm{d}^{(w)},\bm{d}^{(b)})
by keeping all white edges and keeping each black edge independently with probability

Lemma 3.2

Let

be a compact subset. Then

A=\{{\bf x}:\exists\,{\bf y}\in K\text{ s.t. }{\bf x}\preceq{\bf y}\}\hskip 9.24994pt\text{ is pre-compact in }\ell^{2}_{\downarrow}.

Proof.

We follow the proof of Proposition 36 (ii) in
[

Since

\lim_{k\to\infty}\sup_{{\bf x}\in A}\sum_{i\geq k}x_{i}^{2}=0.

Fix

. Note that if
y_{i,j}\geq 0
and
\sum_{j}y_{i,j}\leq y_{i}
then both

\sum_{j}y_{i,j}^{2}\leq y_{i}^{2}\hskip 18.49988pt\textup{and}\hskip 18.49988pt\sum_{j}y_{i,j}^{2}1_{[y_{i,j}\leq\varepsilon]}\leq\varepsilon y_{i}.

Hence for any fixed

\displaystyle\sup_{{\bf x}\in A}\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}x_{i}^{2}1_{[x_{i}\leq\varepsilon]}\right\}\leq\sup_{{\bf y}\in K}\left\{\varepsilon\sum_{i<k}y_{i}+\sum_{i\geq k}y_{i}^{2}\right\}.

Taking the limit superior as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
gives

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}

\displaystyle\sup_{{\bf x}\in A}\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}x_{i}^{2}1_{[x_{i}\leq\varepsilon]}\right\}\leq\limsup_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\left(\sup_{{\bf y}\in K}\left\{\varepsilon\sum_{i<k}y_{i}\right\}+\sup_{{\bf y}\in K}\left\{\sum_{i\geq k}y_{i}^{2}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=\sup_{{\bf y}\in K}\left\{\sum_{i\geq k}y_{i}^{2}\right\}.

Taking

and use compactness of

by, for example, Dini’s theorem. Hence

\limsup_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\,\sup_{{\bf x}\in A}\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}x_{i}^{2}1_{[x_{i}\leq\varepsilon]}\right\}=0.

Also note that

for all

. Now for each fixed

for all

Hence, for all

Taking the limit as

\displaystyle\left[Q_{m+1}-Q_{m}\right]=2\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}x_{m+1}(1-e^{-ty_{i}y_{m+1}})+\operatornamewithlimits{{\sum}^{*}}_{i,j\leq m}x_{i}x_{j}(1-e^{-ty_{i}y_{m+1}})(1-e^{-ty_{j}y_{m+1}})

\displaystyle=2t\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}y_{i}\right)x_{m+1}y_{m+1}+t^{2}\left(\operatornamewithlimits{{\sum}^{*}}_{i,j\leq m}x_{i}y_{i}x_{j}y_{j}\right)y_{m+1}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2t\sqrt{Q_{m}\tilde{Q}_{m}}x_{m+1}y_{m+1}+t^{2}Q_{m}\tilde{Q}_{m}y_{m+1}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(t\sqrt{Q_{m}\tilde{Q}_{m}}+t^{2}Q_{m}\tilde{Q}_{m}\right)(x_{m+1}+y_{m+1})^{2},

for

\displaystyle\left[Q_{k+1}-Q_{k}|\mathscr{B}_{k}\right]\leq\left(t\sqrt{Q_{k}\tilde{Q}_{k}}+t^{2}Q_{k}\tilde{Q}_{k}\right)(x_{k+1}+y_{k+1})^{2}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\left[\tilde{Q}_{m+1}-\tilde{Q}_{m}|\mathscr{B}_{k}\right]\leq\left(t\tilde{Q}_{k}+t^{2}\tilde{Q}_{k}^{2}\right)(x_{k+1}+y_{k+1})^{2}.

In particular, the process

\displaystyle M_{k}=Q_{k}+

\displaystyle\tilde{Q}_{k}-Q_{m}-\tilde{Q}_{m}

\displaystyle-\sum_{j=m}^{k-1}\left(t\sqrt{Q_{j}\tilde{Q}_{j}}+t\tilde{Q}_{j}+t^{2}Q_{j}\tilde{Q}_{j}+t^{2}\tilde{Q}^{2}\right)(x_{j+1}+y_{j+1})^{2}\hskip 18.49988ptm\leq k\leq n

.

Let

Expanding the left-hand side and using the definition of

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\left[Q_{T\wedge n}+\tilde{Q}_{T\wedge n}-Q_{m}-\tilde{Q}_{m}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{j=m}^{(T\wedge n)-1}\left(t\sqrt{Q_{j}\tilde{Q}_{j}}+t\tilde{Q}_{j}+t^{2}Q_{j}\tilde{Q}_{j}+t^{2}\tilde{Q}_{j}^{2}\right)(x_{j+1}+y_{j+1})^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\left(t\sqrt{(Q_{m}+\varepsilon)(\tilde{Q}_{m}+\varepsilon)}+t(\tilde{Q}_{m}+\varepsilon)+t^{2}(Q_{m}+\varepsilon)(\tilde{Q}_{m}+\varepsilon)+t^{2}(Q_{m}+\varepsilon)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 18.49988pt\hskip 18.49988pt\times\sum_{j=m+1}^{n}(x_{j}+y_{j})^{2}.

For any positive numbers

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

Since

4.3.2.

Observe that we can write

4.3.3.

Observe that a direct consequence of Lemma

5.
Convergence of Component Sizes

5.1.
Good functions

In this section we establish and recall some results about the
J_{1}
topology and convergence of excursions. For an interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
we let
{\mathbb{D}}_{+}(I)
the collection of càdlàg functions
f
such that
f(t)\geq f(t-)
for all
t\in I
. Recall that
\mathcal{E}(f)
is the collection of excursion intervals of the function
f
. Following
[
9
]
, define
\mathcal{Y}(f)=\{r:(l,r)\in\mathcal{E}(f)\}
.

Definition 5.1

(Good function on
[0,T]
)
.

We say that a function
f\in{\mathbb{D}}_{+}([0,T])
is
good (on
[0,T]
)
if the following three properties hold

(i)

For all
\mathcal{Y}(f)
does not have any isolated points;

(ii)

[0,\sup\mathcal{Y}(f)]\setminus\bigcup_{e\in\mathcal{E}(f)}e
has Lebesgue measure
0
;

(iii)

f
does not attain a local minimum at any point
r\in\mathcal{Y}(f)
.

The following result is established in
[
9
, Remark 12]
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Suppose that
f\in{\mathbb{D}}_{+}([0,T])
is good. Then
f
is continuous at each
r\in\mathcal{Y}(f)
.

The following lemma is also easy to see.

Lemma 5.3
.

Suppose that
f\in{\mathbb{D}}_{+}([0,T])
is good and let
(l_{j},r_{j})\in\mathcal{E}(f)
for
j\in[2]
with
l_{1}<l_{2}
. Then
r_{1}<l_{2}
. In other words, the excursions are not nested.

Proof.

Since
l_{1}<l_{2}
and
(l_{1},r_{1})
is an excursion interval, we have

f(r_{1})=\inf_{s\leq r_{1}}f(s)=\inf_{s\leq l_{1}}f(s)\geq\inf_{s\leq l_{2}}f(s),

where the first equality is valid by Lemma
5.2
. If
r_{1}\geq l_{2}
, then we would have equality above, implying that
r_{1}
is a local minimum of
f
contradicting Definition
5.1
(
iii
).
∎

Definition 5.4

(Good on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
)
.

A function
f\in{\mathbb{D}}_{+}={\mathbb{D}}_{+}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
is
good
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
if the following hold:

(i)

f
is good on
[0,T]
for each
T>0
;

(ii)

For all
\varepsilon>0
, the set
\mathcal{E}(f)
has only finitely many excursions of length
r-l>\varepsilon
;

(iii)

f
does not possess an infinite length excursion.

We now turn to a key lemma which is a simple extension of
[
1
, Lemma 7]
. The result of Aldous holds for continuous limits.

Lemma 5.5
.

Suppose that
f:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
is good. Let
\Xi^{(\infty)}=\{(r,r-l);(l,r)\in\mathcal{E}(f)\}
. Suppose that
f_{n}\to f
in the
J_{1}
topology as
n\to\infty
and suppose that for each
n
there is a sequence
(t_{n,i};i\geq 0)
such that

(i)

0=t_{n,0}<t_{n,1}<\dotsm
and
\lim_{i\to\infty}t_{n,i}=+\infty
for all
n
;

(ii)

f_{n}(t_{n,i})=\inf_{s\leq t_{n,i}}f_{n}(s)
for all
i
and all
n
;

(iii)

\max_{i:t_{n,i}\leq s_{0}}f_{n}(t_{n,i})-f_{n}(t_{n,i+1})\longrightarrow 0
as
n\to\infty
for each
s_{0}<\infty
.

Write
\Xi^{(n)}=\{(t_{n,i},t_{n,i}-t_{n,i-1});i\geq 1\}
. Then
\Xi^{(n)}\to\Xi^{(\infty)}
in the topology of vague convergence of the associated counting measures on
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times(0,\infty)
.

Proof.

It suffices to show the following. Suppose that
K=[a,b]\times[c,d]\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}\times(0,\infty)
is a compact rectangle with
\Xi^{(\infty)}\cap\partial K=\emptyset
, then for all
(r,r-l)\in\Xi^{(\infty)}\cap K
, there exists a sequence
i_{n}=i_{n}(r)
such that for all large
n

(t_{n,i_{n}-1},t_{n,i_{n}}-t_{n,i_{n}-1})\in K
and

(5.1)

t_{n,i_{n}-1}\to l\hskip 18.49988pt\textup{and}\hskip 18.49988ptt_{n,i_{n}}\to r,

as well as
\#(\Xi^{(n)}\cap K)\longrightarrow\#(\Xi^{(\infty)}\cap K)
.

Before continuing to the proof, it is useful to recall that that
\inf_{s\leq t}f(s)
is continuous, and

(5.2)

\inf_{s\leq u}f_{n}(s)\longrightarrow\inf_{s\leq u}f(s)\hskip 18.49988pt\textup{ locally uniformly in }u.

First we show (
5.1
). To do this let us fix an excursion
(l,r)
with
(r,r-l)\in K
. Let
t\in(l,r)
be a point of continuity of
f
. Note that since
t
is a continuity point it holds that

(5.3)

\lim_{n\to\infty}f_{n}(t)=f(t).

Set
i_{n}=\min\{i:t_{n,i}>t\}
. We claim

(5.4)

\liminf_{n\to\infty}t_{n,i_{n}-1}\geq l\hskip 18.49988pt\textup{ and }\hskip 18.49988pt\limsup_{n\to\infty}t_{n,i_{n}}\leq r.

To see this note that by hypothesis (
ii
) and
t_{n,i_{n}}\geq t
, we have

f_{n}(t_{n,i_{n}})=\inf_{s\leq t_{n,i_{n}}}f_{n}(s)\leq\inf_{s\leq t}f_{n}(s)\longrightarrow\inf_{s\leq t}f(s)=f(r)

and

f_{n}(t_{n,i_{n}-1})=\inf_{s\leq t_{n,i_{n}-1}}f_{n}(s)\geq\inf_{s\leq t}f_{n}(s)\longrightarrow f(r).

So

\limsup_{n\to\infty}f_{n}(t_{n,i_{n}})\leq f(r)\hskip 18.49988pt\textup{and}\hskip 18.49988pt\liminf_{n\to\infty}f_{n}(t_{n,i_{n}-1})\geq f(r).

Since
t_{n,i_{n}-1}\leq t
for all
t
, we can use hypothesis (
iii
) to conclude that

f_{n}(t_{n,i_{n}})\geq f_{n}(t_{n,i_{n}-1})-o(1)

and so

(5.5)

f_{n}(t_{n,i_{n}})=\inf_{s\leq t_{n,i_{n}}}f_{n}(s)\to f(r)=\inf_{s\leq r}f(s).

But if there were some
\delta>0
such that
t_{n,i_{n}}>r+\delta
for infinitely many
n
, then

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{s\leq t_{n,i_{n}}}f_{n}(s)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{s\leq r+\delta}f_{n}(s)=\inf_{s\leq r+\delta}f_{n}(s)\longrightarrow\inf_{s\leq r+\delta}f(s)<\inf_{s\leq r}f(r)

where we used (
5.2
) to take the limit and Definition
5.1
(
iii
) to conclude the strict inequality. This contradicts (
5.5
). Hence the right-hand side of (
5.4
) holds. A similar argument holds for the left-hand side.

We now show that

\liminf_{n\to\infty}t_{n,i_{n}}\geq r.

By (
5.4
) we see that
\sup_{n}t_{n,i_{n}}
is bounded, and therefore there exists a
M
sufficiently large such that for all
n
,
t_{n,i_{n}}\in[t,M]
. Let
r^{\prime}
be any subsequential limit of
(t_{n,i_{n}})
. First, by (
5.4
),
r^{\prime}\in[t,r]
. Also for all
s\in[t,r)
it holds that
f(s-)>f(r)
and so it suffices to show that
f(r^{\prime}-)\leq f(r)
. Then for the relevant subsequence denoted by
n^{\prime}
, by Proposition 2.1 (b.1) and (b.3) in
[
11
, Chapter VI]
we have

f(r^{\prime}-)\leq\liminf_{n^{\prime}}f_{n^{\prime}}(t_{n^{\prime},i_{n^{\prime}}}).

But by (
5.5
), we know that
\lim_{n^{\prime}}f_{n^{\prime}}(t_{n^{\prime},i_{n^{\prime}}})=f(r)
giving us the desired result. A similar argument holds to show that
\limsup_{n\to\infty}t_{n,i_{n}}\leq l
and we have thus established the existence of some
i_{n}
such that (
5.1
) holds. Finally, note that for all
n
large enough it must be the case that
t_{n,i_{n}}\in[a,b]
and
t_{n,i_{n}}-t_{n,i_{n}-1}\in[c,d]
as
r-l\in(c,d)
and
r\in(a,b)
since
\Xi^{(\infty)}
does not intersect
\partial K
.

To show that
\#(\Xi^{(n)}\cap K)\to\#(\Xi^{(\infty)}\cap K)
, it suffices to show that for any subsequence
n^{\prime}
and any convergent sequence
(x_{n^{\prime}},y_{n^{\prime}})\in\Xi^{(n^{\prime})}\cap K
must satisfy
\lim_{n^{\prime}}(x_{n^{\prime}},y_{n^{\prime}})\in\Xi^{(\infty)}\cap K
as we have already shown
\liminf_{n}\#(\Xi^{(n)}\cap K)\geq\#(\Xi^{(\infty)}\cap K)
.

Let us denote the subsequence by
n
and suppose, for the sake of contradiction, that there is some sequence
j_{n}
such that for all
n

(t_{n,j_{n}},t_{n,j_{n}}-t_{n,j_{n}-1})\in\Xi^{(n)}\cap K

but

(t_{n,j_{n}},t_{n,j_{n}}-t_{n,j_{n}-1})=(v,v-u)\notin\Xi^{(\infty)}\cap K.

Clearly, we must have
v\in[a,b]
and
v-u\in[c,d]
. To establish the contradiction, we show
(v,v-u)\in\Xi^{(\infty)}
. That means
(u,v)
is actually an excursion interval of
f
.

As
c>0
, we must have
v>u
. By (
5.2
) we can use both hypotheses (
ii
) and (
iii
) as we did in the first part of the proof, to conclude that

\lim_{n\to\infty}f_{n}(t_{n,j_{n}})=\inf_{s\leq v}f(s)=\lim_{n\to\infty}f_{n}(t_{n,j_{n}-1})=\inf_{s\leq u}f(u).

Since
f
is good
(u,v)\subseteq(l,r)
for some uniquely defined excursion interval
(l,r)
. We claim
v=r
and
u=l
.

To see that, note that since
(u,v)\subseteq(l,r)
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}f_{n}(t_{n,j_{n}})=\lim_{n\to\infty}f_{n}(t_{n,j_{n}-1})=f(r-)=f(r),

where we also used Lemma
5.2
. By Proposition 2.1 (b.1) and (b.3) in
[
11
, Chapter VI]
implies

\inf_{s\leq v}f(s)=\liminf_{n\to\infty}f_{n}(t_{n,j_{n}})\geq f(v-).

Which implies that
f(v-)=\inf_{s\leq v}f(s)
. Similarly,
f(u-)\geq\inf_{s\leq u}f(s)
. This implies that
(u,v)
are an excursion interval since the excursion intervals of
f
cannot be nested, this implies that
(u,v)=(l,r)
, the desired contradiction. This establishes the lemma.
∎

5.2.
Jointly good functions

We now extend this to convergence of pairs of functions
(f,g).
Let
{\mathbb{D}}_{\uparrow}(I)
denote the collection of non-decreasing càdlàg functions on an interval
I
. Let
{\mathbb{D}}_{+,\uparrow}(I)\subset{\mathbb{D}}(I)^{2}
denote the collection of functions
F=(f,g):I\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that
f\in{\mathbb{D}}_{+}(I)
and
g\in{\mathbb{D}}_{\uparrow}(I)
.

Definition 5.6

(Jointly good function)
.

We will say that the pair
(f,g)\in{\mathbb{D}}_{+,\uparrow}([0,T])
are
jointly good on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
if
f
is good on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
g
is continuous at each endpoint of an excursion of
f
. That is
g
is continuous at each
t\in\bigcup_{(l,r)\in\mathcal{E}(f)}\{l,r\}
.

The following is a simple extension of Lemma
5.5
, we only sketch the proof.

Lemma 5.7
.

Suppose that
(f_{n},g_{n})\to(f,g)
in the product
J_{1}
topology and that
(f,g)
are jointly good on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Suppose
(t_{n,i};i\geq 0)
is as in Lemma

. Then

\displaystyle\Xi^{(n)}

\displaystyle:=\left\{(t_{n,i},t_{n,i}-t_{n,i-1},g_{n}(t_{n,i})-g_{n}(t_{n,i-1}));i\geq 1\right\}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\Xi^{(\infty)}:=\{(r,r-l,g(r)-g(l)):(l,r)\in\mathcal{E}(f)\}

in the vague topology for the associated counting measures on
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
.

Proof Sketch.

Proposition VI.2.1(b.5) in
[
11
]
states that for càdlàg functions
g_{n}\to g
in the
J_{1}
topology then for any continuity point
t
of
g
and any sequence
t_{n}\to t
it holds that
g_{n}(t_{n})\to g(t)
and
g_{n}(t_{n}-)\to g(t)
. Therefore, if
t_{n,i_{n}}\to r
and
t_{n,i_{n}-1}\to l
, since
g
is continuous at both
l,r
it holds that
g_{n}(t_{n,i_{n}})\to g(r)
and
g_{n}(t_{n,i_{n}-1})\to g_{n}(l).

∎

5.3.
Goodness of
(X,Y)

The following lemma is needed to apply Lemma
5.7
.

Lemma 5.8
.

The process
(X,Y)
of Proposition

are jointly good on

Consider the graph

By standard properties of Poisson processes, we can see that the family

Algorithm 6.1

(Dynamic construction of

)
.

(1)

At time

and

(3)

The graph

Observe that we have the following merging dynamics. Initially, we have some connected components with (integer) masses

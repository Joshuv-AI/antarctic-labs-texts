Finite p p -groups of nilpotency class 3 3 with two conjugacy class sizes
Tushar Kanta Naik, Rahul Dattatraya Kitture, Manoj K. Yadav

Abstract
. It is proved that, for a prime p > 2 p>2 and an integer n ≥ 1 n\geq 1 , finite p p -groups of nilpotency class 3 3 and having only two conjugacy class sizes 1 1 and p n p^{n} exist if and only if n n is even; moreover, for a given even positive integer, such a group is unique up to isoclinism (in the sense of Philip Hall).

1.
Introduction

A finite group
G
is said to be of
conjugate type

(1=m_{1},m_{2},\cdots,m_{r})
,
m_{i}<m_{i+1}
, if
m_{i}
’s are precisely the different sizes of conjugacy
classes of
G
. In this paper we restrict our attention on finite groups of
conjugate type
(1,m)
. Investigation on such groups was initiated by N. Ito

[
6
]
in 1953.
He proved that groups of conjugate type
(1,m)
are nilpotent,
with
m
a prime power, say
p^{n}
. In particular,
G
is a direct product of its
Sylow-
p
subgroup and some abelian
p^{\prime}
-subgroup. So, it is sufficient to study
finite
p
-
groups
of conjugate type
(1,p^{n})
for
p
a prime and
n\geq 1
an integer. It was proved by K. Ishikawa
[
5
]
that the
nilpotency class of such
finite
p
-groups is either
2
or
3
.

It follows (see Section 2) that any two isoclinic groups are of same conjugate
type. The study of finite
p
-groups of conjugate type
(1,p^{n})
, up to
isoclinism, was initiated by Ishikawa
[
4
]
. He classified
such groups for
n\leq 2
. As a consequence, it follows that there is no finite

p
-group of nilpotency class
3
and conjugate type
(1,p)
, and there is a
unique finite
p
-group, up to isoclinism, of nilpotency class
3
and conjugate
type
(1,p^{2})
.
The classification, up to isoclinism, of finite
p
-groups of conjugate type

(1,p^{3})
was recently done in
[
9
]
, wherein, among other results, it
was observed that there is no finite
p
-group of nilpotency class
3
and
of conjugate type
(1,p^{3})
.

The examples of
p
-group of nilpotency class
3
and of conjugate type

(1,p^{2m})
are known for all
m\geq 1
. These examples appeared in the
construction of certain Camina
p
-groups of nilpotency class
3
by Dark and
Scoppola
[
1
, p. 796-797]
. It can be shown that for a given
integer
m\geq 1
and a prime
p>2
, the

p
-group of conjugate type
(1,p^{2m})
and class
3
, constructed by Dark and
Scoppola, is isomorphic to
\mathcal{H}_{m}/\operatorname{Z}(\mathcal{H}_{m})
, where

\mathcal{H}_{m}
is presented as follows (see Section

4
for more details):

(1.1)

\mathcal{H}_{m}=\begin{Bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1&0&0&0&0\\
a&1&0&0&0\\
c&b&1&0&0\\
d&ab-c&a&1&0\\
f&e&c&b&1\\
\end{bmatrix}:a,b,c,d,e,f\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}\end{Bmatrix}.

In view of these examples, the question asked in
[

]
reduces to the

Does there exist a finite

-group of nilpotency class

(1,p^{n})

We answer this question, by proving the following much general result.

Main Theorem.

Let

be a prime and

if and only if

, every finite

We set some notations for a multiplicatively written finite group

2.
Reductions

In
1940
, P. Hall
[
2
]
introduced the concept of isoclinism among
groups.
Let
X
be a finite group and
\overline{X}=X/\operatorname{Z}(X)
. Then
commutation in
X
gives a well defined map
a_{X}:\overline{X}\times\overline{X}\mapsto X^{\prime}
such that
a_{X}(x\operatorname{Z}(X),y\operatorname{Z}(X))=[x,y]
for
(x,y)\in X\times X
. Two finite groups
G
and
H
are said to be
isoclinic
, if
there
exists an isomorphism
\phi
of the factor group
\overline{G}=G/\operatorname{Z}(G)

onto

\overline{H}=H/\operatorname{Z}(H)
, and an isomorphism

\theta
of the subgroup
G^{\prime}

onto
H^{\prime}
such that the
following diagram is commutative:

\begin{CD}\overline{G}\times\overline{G}@>{a_{G}}>{}>G^{\prime}\\
@V{\phi\times\phi}V{}V@V{}V{\theta}V\\
\overline{H}\times\overline{H}@>{a_{H}}>{}>H^{\prime}.\end{CD}

Note that isoclinism is an equivalence relation among groups. Equivalence
classes under this relation are called

isoclinism families
.
We recall the following two results of Hall.

Proposition 2.1
.

[
2
, p. 136]

Let
G
and
H
be two isoclinic finite groups. Then
G
and
H
are of the same
conjugate type.

Proposition 2.2
.

[
2
, p. 135]

Let
G
be a finite group. Then there exists a finite group
H
in the
isoclinism family of
G
such that
\operatorname{Z}(H)\leq H^{\prime}
.

The following interesting result is due to M. Isaacs.

Theorem 2.3
.

[
3
, p. 501]

Let
G
be a finite group, which contains a proper normal subgroup
N
such that
all of the conjugacy classes of
G
which lie outside of
N
have the same
sizes. Then either
G/N
is cyclic or every non-identity element of
G/N
has
prime order.

As an immediate consequence of the preceding result, we get

Corollary 2.4
.

For a prime
p
and an integer
n\geq 1
, let

G
be a finite
p
-group of conjugate type
(1,p^{n})
. Then
exp(G/\operatorname{Z}(G))=p
. In particular, if
G
is of nilpotency class
>2
, then
p>2
.

The following result is due to Ishikawa.

Theorem 2.5
.

[
5
, Main Theorem]

For a prime
p
and an integer
n\geq 1
,
let
G
be a finite
p
-group of
conjugate type
(1,p^{n})
. Then the nilpotency class of
G
is at most
3
. As a
consequence,
G^{\prime}
is elementary abelian.

The preceding results reduce our study to the finite
p
-groups
G
satisfying
the following conditions:

(1)

G
is of conjugate type
(1,p^{n})
,
n\geq 2
.

(2)

Nilpotency class of
G
is
3
.

(3)

\operatorname{Z}(G)\leq G^{\prime}
.

(4)

p>2
.

For notational convenience, we set

Hypothesis (A1).
We say that a finite
p
-group
G
satisfies

Hypothesis
(A1)
, if (1)-(4) above hold for
G
.

3.
Key results

In this section we determine some important invariants associated to a finite

p
-group satisfying Hypothesis (A1).

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
G
satisfy Hypothesis (A1). Then
[G^{\prime}:\operatorname{Z}(G)]<p^{n}
.

Proof.

Let
[G:G^{\prime}]=p^{m}
for some integer
m\geq 1
. Since the nilpotency class of
G

is
3
,

G^{\prime}
is abelian; hence by the given hypothesis, for any
y\in G^{\prime}\setminus\operatorname{Z}(G)
, we have

p^{n}=[G:\operatorname{C}_{G}(y)]\leq[G:G^{\prime}]=p^{m};

hence
n\leq m
.

We proceed by the way of contradiction.
Contrarily suppose that
[G^{\prime}:\operatorname{Z}(G)]=p^{k}
with
n\leq k
.
Our plan is to count the
cardinality of following set in two different ways:

X=\Big\{\big(\langle xG^{\prime}\rangle,\langle y\operatorname{Z}(G)\rangle\big)\mid x\in G\setminus G^{\prime},\;y\in G^{\prime}\setminus\operatorname{Z}(G),\;[x,y]=1\Big\}.

Note that
X
is well defined. For, if
\langle xG^{\prime}\rangle=\langle x_{1}G^{\prime}\rangle

and
\langle y\operatorname{Z}(G)\rangle=\langle y_{1}\operatorname{Z}(G)\rangle
, then
[x,y]=1
if and only if

[x_{1},y_{1}]=1
. Note that
G/\operatorname{Z}(G)
is of exponent
p
(by Corollary
2.4
)
and
\operatorname{Z}(G)\leq G^{\prime}
. Thus

exp(G/G^{\prime})=exp(G^{\prime}/\operatorname{Z}(G))=p.

It follows that there are
(p^{k}-1)/(p-1)
subgroups of order
p

in

G^{\prime}/\operatorname{Z}(G)
. Since
G^{\prime}
is abelian, for each
y\in G^{\prime}\setminus\operatorname{Z}(G)
,

G^{\prime}\subseteq C_{G}(y)
and
[\operatorname{C}_{G}(y):G^{\prime}]=p^{m-n}
. As there are
(p^{m-n}-1)/(p-1)
subgroups of order
p
in
\operatorname{C}_{G}(y)/G^{\prime}
, we get

|X|=\frac{(p^{m-n}-1)(p^{k}-1)}{(p-1)^{2}}.

On the other hand, fix
x\in G\setminus G^{\prime}
. Set
\operatorname{C}_{G^{\prime}}(x):=\operatorname{C}_{G}(x)\cap G^{\prime}
. If
[G^{\prime}:\operatorname{C}_{G^{\prime}}(x)]\geq p^{n}
, then there are at least
p^{n}
conjugates of

x
in
G^{\prime}
; hence, by the given hypothesis, there are exactly
p^{n}

conjugates of
x

in
G^{\prime}
. Consequently we have
x^{G}=x^{G^{\prime}}
. Let
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}\}
be
a minimal generating set of
G
. Then
x^{x_{i}}=x^{h_{i}}
for some

h_{i}\in G^{\prime}
; hence
x^{x_{i}h_{i}^{-1}}=x
for
1\leq i\leq m
. Hence

\{x_{1}h_{1}^{-1},\ldots,x_{m}h_{m}^{-1}\}
is also a generating set for
G
, which centralizes
x
, which
implies that
x\in\operatorname{Z}(G)
,
a contradiction. Thus
[G^{\prime}:\operatorname{C}_{G^{\prime}}(x)]\leq p^{n-1}
and
[\operatorname{C}_{G^{\prime}}(x):\operatorname{Z}(G)]\geq p^{k+1-n}\geq p
. Thus, there are at least
(p^{k+1-n}-1)/(p-1)

subgroups
\langle y\operatorname{Z}(G)\rangle
of order
p
in
G^{\prime}/\operatorname{Z}(G)
with
[x,y]=1
.
Counting all together, we get

|X|\geq\frac{(p^{m}-1)(p^{k+1-n}-1)}{(p-1)^{2}}.

Comparing the size of
X
, we get

(p^{m}-1)(p^{k+1-n}-1)\leq(p^{m-n}-1)(p^{k}-1),

which on simplification gives

p+p^{-k}+p^{n-m}\leq 1+p^{1-m}+p^{n-k}<3,

a contradiction on the choice of
p
. Hence
[G^{\prime}:\operatorname{Z}(G)]<p^{n}
.
∎

Before proceeding further, we introduce the notion of breadth in
p
-groups. Let

G
be a finite
p
-group. For
x\in G
, the
breadth

of

in

is defined as
p^{b(x)}=[G:\operatorname{C}_{G}(x)]
. The
breadth

of G is
defined as

b_{G}=\mbox{max}\{b(x)\mid x\in G\}.

Let
A
be an abelian normal subgroup of
G
. Then we define the following:

\displaystyle p^{b_{A}(x)}

\displaystyle=[A:C_{A}(x)].

\displaystyle b_{A}(G)

\displaystyle=\mbox{max}\{b_{A}(x)\mid x\in G\}.

\displaystyle B_{A}(G)

\displaystyle=\{x\in G\mid b_{A}(x)=b_{A}(G)\}.

For the ease of notation, we denote

by

.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
G
satisfiy Hypothesis (A1). Then

B_{G^{\prime}}=\{x\in G\mid\operatorname{C}_{G^{\prime}}(x)=\operatorname{Z}(G)\}

and
\langle B_{G^{\prime}}\rangle=G
.

Proof.

Let
[G:G^{\prime}]=p^{m}
for some integer
m
. As in the proof of Lemma
3.1
, we
have
n\leq m
. Let
[G^{\prime}:\operatorname{Z}(G)]=p^{k}
. Then, by Lemma
3.1
,

.
Define

T:=\{x\in G\mid x\mbox{ commutes with some element }y\in G^{\prime}\setminus\operatorname{Z}(G)\}.

Note that
T=\cup\operatorname{C}_{G}(h)
, where union is taken over all subgroups
\langle hG^{\prime}\rangle
of order
p
in
G^{\prime}/\operatorname{Z}(G)
.
Since
G^{\prime}
is abelian, for each
h\in G^{\prime}\setminus\operatorname{Z}(G)
,
G^{\prime}\leq\operatorname{C}_{G}(h)
and
|\operatorname{C}_{G}(h)|=|G^{\prime}|\,p^{m-n}
.
By Corollary

,
exp(G/\operatorname{Z}(G))=p
, and hence
exp(G^{\prime}/\operatorname{Z}(G))=p
. Thus,

|T|\,\leq\,|G^{\prime}|\,p^{m-n}\frac{(p^{k}-1)}{p-1}\,<\,|G^{\prime}|\,p^{m-n+k}\,\leq\,|G^{\prime}|\,p^{m-1}\,<\,|G|.

Consequently,
T
is a proper subset of
G
, and therefore there exists an

x\in G\setminus G^{\prime}
such that
\operatorname{C}_{G^{\prime}}(x)=\operatorname{Z}(G)
. Thus

B_{G^{\prime}}=\{x\in G\mid\operatorname{C}_{G^{\prime}}(x)=\operatorname{Z}(G)\}=G\setminus T.

If
\langle B_{G^{\prime}}\rangle=H<G
, then
|H|\leq|G^{\prime}|\,p^{m-1}
. Thus we have

\displaystyle|G|=|T\cup B_{G^{\prime}}|\leq|G^{\prime}|p^{m-1}+|G^{\prime}|\,p^{m-1}<|G^{\prime}|p^{m}=|G|,

which is a contradiction. Hence
\langle B_{G^{\prime}}\rangle=G
, which completes
the proof.

As a consequence of Hall-Witt identity, we get

Let

Lemma 3.4
.

If

[G:G^{\prime}]=p^{n}

Proof.

For

Step 1.

For all

\operatorname{C}_{G}(h)=\operatorname{C}_{G}(G^{\prime})

Let

If

y_{k+1},\dots,y_{m+k}
, it follows that

\left<y_{k+1},\dots,y_{k+m},\operatorname{Z}(G)\right>=\operatorname{C}_{G}(y)\cap H;

hence
|(\operatorname{C}_{G}(y)\cap H)/\operatorname{Z}(G)|=p^{m}
. This, along with (
3.6
) and (
3.7
),
proves Step 2.

Step 3.

The cardinality of

S:=\Big\{\big(\langle x\operatorname{Z}(G)\rangle,\langle y\operatorname{Z}(G)\rangle\big)\mid x\in G\setminus H,\,\,y\in H\setminus G^{\prime},\,\,[x,y]=1\Big\}

is

p^{m+k}(p^{n}-1)(p^{m}-1)/(p-1)^{2}
.

Since the exponent of
G/\operatorname{Z}(G)
is
p
, it follows that
G/\operatorname{Z}(G)
,
H/\operatorname{Z}(G)
and

G^{\prime}/\operatorname{Z}(G)
have
(p^{n+m+k}-1)/(p-1)
,
(p^{m+k}-1)/(p-1)
and
(p^{k}-1)/(p-1)

number of subgroups of order
p
respectively. Consequently

(3.8)

|\{\left<x\operatorname{Z}(G)\right>\mid x\in G\setminus H\}|=\frac{p^{m+k}(p^{n}-1)}{p-1}

and

(3.9)

|\{\left<y\operatorname{Z}(G)\right>\mid y\in H\setminus G^{\prime}\}|=\frac{p^{k}(p^{m}-1)}{p-1}.

For each
x\in G\setminus H
, by Step
2
, we have

|(\operatorname{C}_{G}(x)\cap H)/\operatorname{Z}(G)|=p^{m}
; hence

(3.10)

|\{\left<y\operatorname{Z}(G)\right>\mid y\in H\setminus G^{\prime},[x,y]=1\}|=\frac{p^{m}-1}{p-1}.

Hence, by (
3.8
) and (
3.10
), we have

(3.11)

|S|=\frac{p^{m+k}(p^{n}-1)(p^{m}-1)}{(p-1)^{2}},

and the proof of Step 3 is complete.

We now proceed to get the final contradiction.
Note that there exists some element
y_{0}\in H\setminus G^{\prime}
such that

(3.12)

|\{\left<x\operatorname{Z}(G)\right>\mid x\in G\setminus H,[x,y_{0}]=1\}|\geq\frac{p^{m}(p^{n}-1)}{p-1}.

For, if there is no such
y_{0}
, then for each
y\in H\setminus G^{\prime}
, we get

|\{\left<x\operatorname{Z}(G)\right>\mid x\in G\setminus H,[x,y]=1\}|<\frac{p^{m}(p^{n}-1)}{p-1}.

But then

|S|<\frac{p^{m+k}(p^{n}-1)(p^{m}-1)}{(p-1)^{2}},

which is absurd.

Note that
G^{\prime}\leq\operatorname{C}_{G}(y_{0})
,
[C_{G}(y_{0}):G^{\prime}]=p^{m}
,
|G/H|=p^{n}
and
|H/G^{\prime}|=p^{m}
.
Assume that

|\operatorname{C}_{G}(y_{0})H/H|=p^{n-s}~~\text{and}~~|\operatorname{C}_{H}(y_{0})/G^{\prime}|=p^{m-r},

for some integers
s
and
r
.
Since
[G:\operatorname{C}_{G}(y_{0})]=p^{n}
, it follows that
n=s+r
.
Then

|\operatorname{C}_{G}(y_{0})/\operatorname{Z}(G)|=p^{k+(m-r)+(n-s)}=p^{k+m}

and

|\operatorname{C}_{H}(y_{0})/\operatorname{Z}(G)|=p^{k+(m-r)}.

Thus

\displaystyle|\{\left<x\operatorname{Z}(G)\right>\mid x\in G\setminus H,[x,y_{0}]=1\}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(p^{k+m}-1)-(p^{k+m-r}-1)}{p-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{p^{m+k-r}(p^{n-s}-1)}{p-1}.

The preceding equation along with (
3.12
) gives

p^{m}(p^{n}-1)/(p-1)\leq p^{m+k-r}(p^{n-s}-1)/(p-1),

which implies that
(p^{n}-1)\leq p^{k}-p^{k-r}.

Hence, by Lemma
3.1
,

p^{n}<p^{k}+1\leq p^{n-1}+1,

which is absurd, and the proof of the lemma is complete.
∎

[h,x_{i}]\in\langle z_{1},\ldots,z_{m}\rangle

The second assertion follows on the same lines.
∎

Before proceeding further, we recall the following commutator identities in a
finite
p
-group
G
of nilpotency class
3
with
p
odd, which will be used in
computations without any reference. Note that, in this case
G^{\prime}
is abelian, so
the ordering of commutators is immaterial. For
a,b,c\in G
,

(1)

[ab,c]=[a,c][b,c][a,c,b]
and
[a,bc]=[a,b][a,c][a,b,c]
;

(2)

[a^{i},b^{j},c^{k}]=[a,b,c]^{ijk}
(since
G
is of class
3
);

(3)

[a^{s},b]=[a,b]^{s}[a,b,a]^{\binom{s}{2}}
and
[a,b^{s}]=[a,b]^{s}[a,b,b]^{\binom{s}{2}}
(
s\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
),

where
\binom{s}{2}=\frac{s(s-1)}{2}
in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
,
p>2
.

Lemma 5.15
.

For
x=x_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots x_{m}^{a_{m}}
and
y=y_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots y_{m}^{a_{m}}
in
G
, the following hold:

(1)

[x_{1},y]\equiv[x,y_{1}]\pmod{\operatorname{Z}(G)}
.

(2)

If

[x_{1},y]=[x,y_{1}]\prod_{l=1}^{2m}z_{l}^{c_{l}},

then
c_{l}
’s are uniquely determined by
a_{i}
’s and the structure
constants of
{\rm U}_{3}(p^{m})
.

Proof.

If
a_{i}=0
for all
i
, then
x=y=1
, and so
c_{i}=0
for
all
i
, there is nothing to prove. Thus, assume that not all
a_{i}
’s are
0
.
In
{\rm U}_{3}(p^{m})
, consider
X=X_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots X_{m}^{a_{m}}
and
Y=Y_{1}^{a_{1}}Y_{2}^{a_{2}}\cdots Y_{m}^{a_{m}}
. Since

[X_{i},Y_{j}]=[X_{j},Y_{i}]
for all
i,j
(by (
5.4
)), we have

[X_{1},Y]=[X,Y_{1}]
; hence

[x_{1},y]\equiv[x,y_{1}]\pmod{\operatorname{Z}(G)}
in
G
. This proves assertion (1).

We prove assertion (2) in two steps, as the arguments of Step 1 will
be used further.

Step 1.

For
m+1\leq l\leq 2m
,
c_{l}
is uniquely
determined by the structure constants of
{\rm U}_{3}(p^{m})
.

With
X,Y
as in the proof of assertion (1), we have
[X_{1},Y]=[X,Y_{1}]
, which
implies that

[X_{1}Y_{1}^{r},XY^{r}]=1
for any

; hence, in
G
, we get

[x_{1}y_{1}^{r},xy^{r}]\equiv 1\pmod{\operatorname{Z}(G)}.

By Corollary
3.17
, there exists an element
h(r)
, depending on
r
,
in
G^{\prime}
such that

[x_{1}y_{1}^{r},xy^{r}h(r)]=1.

(see Lemma
5.11
), we have

\displaystyle 1=[x_{1}y_{1}^{r},xy^{r}h(r)]=

\displaystyle[x_{1},y]^{r}[x_{1},y,y]^{\binom{r}{2}}[x_{1},y,y_{1}]^{r^{2}}[x_{1},h(r)]

\displaystyle[y_{1},x]^{r}[y_{1},x,y_{1}]^{\binom{r}{2}}[y_{1},x,y]^{r^{2}}[y_{1},h(r)]^{r}.

Consequently, by the given hypothesis, we get

(5.16)

\displaystyle(\prod_{l=1}^{2m}z_{l}^{c_{l}})^{-r}=([x_{1},y][y_{1},x])^{-r}=

\displaystyle[x_{1},y,y]^{\binom{r}{2}}[x_{1},y,y_{1}]^{r^{2}}[x_{1},h(r)]

\displaystyle[y_{1},x,y_{1}]^{\binom{r}{2}}[y_{1},x,y]^{r^{2}}[y_{1},h(r)]^{r}.

By Theorem
5.12
,
[G^{\prime},y],[G^{\prime},y_{1}]\leq\langle z_{m+1},\ldots,z_{2m}\rangle
and

[x_{1},h(r)]\in\langle z_{1},\ldots,z_{m}\rangle

the right

5.16

\langle z_{m+1},\ldots,z_{2m}\rangle

(5.17)

\displaystyle(\prod_{l=1}^{m}z_{l}^{-c_{l}})^{r}

\displaystyle=[x_{1},h(r)]

and

(5.18)

\displaystyle(\prod_{l=m+1}^{2m}z_{l}^{-c_{l}})^{r}

\displaystyle=[x_{1},y,y]^{\binom{r}{2}}[x_{1},y,y_{1}]^{r^{2}}[y_{1},x,y_{1}]^{\binom{r}{2}}[y_{1},x,y]^{r^{2}}[y_{1},h(r)]^{r}.

Since, by Theorem
5.12
,

z_{i}=[h_{1},x_{i}]=[h_{i},x_{1}]
for

\prod_{l=1}^{m}z_{l}^{-c_{l}}=\prod_{l=1}^{m}[x_{1},h_{l}]^{c_{l}}=[x_{1},h_{1}^{c_{1}}\cdots h_{m}^{c_{m}}]=[x_{1},\tilde{h}],

r
.

Using this in (

\displaystyle\prod_{l=m+1}^{2m}z_{l}^{-c_{l}}

\displaystyle=[x_{1},y,y_{1}][y_{1},x,y][y_{1},\tilde{h}]

\displaystyle\prod_{l=m+1}^{2m}z_{l}^{c_{l}}

\displaystyle=[x_{1},y,y][x_{1},y,y_{1}][y_{1},x,y_{1}][y_{1},x,y][y_{1},\tilde{h}].

From these equations, we obtain

5.13

a_{i}

Step 2.

For

2.1
Differential forms on almost Kähler manifold

We recall some definitions and results on the differential forms for almost complex and almost Hermitian manifolds. Let

where all the pieces are graded algebra derivations,

|A_{J}|=(2,-1),\ |\partial_{J}|=(1,0),\ |\bar{\partial}_{J}|=(0,1),\ |\bar{A}_{J}|=(-1,2).

The integrability theorem of Newlander and Nirenberg states that the almost complex structure

is integrable if and only if

, where

denotes the Nijenhuis tensor

For

(A_{J}(\alpha)+\bar{A}_{J}(\alpha))(X,Y)=\frac{1}{4}\alpha(N_{J}(X,Y)),

In particular,

, i.e,

we obtain the following set of equations:

\begin{split}&A_{J}^{2}=0,\\
&A_{J}\partial_{J}+\partial_{J}A_{J}=0,\\
&\partial^{2}_{J}+A_{J}\bar{\partial}_{J}+\bar{\partial}_{J}A_{J}=0,\\
&\partial_{J}\bar{\partial}_{J}+\bar{\partial}_{J}\partial_{J}+A_{J}\bar{A}_{J}+\bar{A}_{J}A_{J}=0,\\
&\bar{\partial}^{2}_{J}+\bar{A}_{J}\partial_{J}+\partial_{J}\bar{A}_{J}=0,\\
&\bar{A}_{J}\bar{\partial}_{J}+\bar{\partial}_{J}\bar{A}_{J}=0.\\
&\bar{A}_{J}^{2}=0.\\

3
Harmonic forms on symplectic manifold

3.1
\Delta_{d^{\prime}}
-harmonic forms

In this section, we discuss the harmonic forms that can be constructed
from the two differential operators
d^{\prime}
and
d^{\prime\prime}
. We begin with the
known cohomologies with
d
(for de Rham
H_{d}
) and
d^{\Lambda}
(for
H_{d^{\Lambda}}
).

We define the operators
d^{\prime}=\partial_{J}+\bar{A}_{J}
and
d^{\prime\prime}=\bar{\partial}_{J}+A_{J}
. Those have adjoint
d^{\prime\ast}=\partial^{\ast}_{J}+\bar{A}^{\ast}_{J}
and
d^{\prime\prime\ast}=\bar{\partial}^{\ast}_{J}+A^{\ast}_{J}
. Hence

d=d^{\prime}+d^{\prime\prime},\ d^{\ast}=d^{\prime\ast}+d^{\prime\prime\ast}

and

d^{\Lambda}=[d,\Lambda]=\sqrt{-1}d^{\prime\ast}-\sqrt{-1}d^{\prime\prime\ast},

d^{\Lambda_{\ast}}=([d,\Lambda])^{\ast}=[L,d^{\ast}]=-\sqrt{-1}d^{\prime}+\sqrt{-1}d^{\prime\prime}.

The operators
d^{\prime}
and
d^{\prime\prime}
were also previously introduced by
de Bartolomeis and Tomassini
[
8
]
. They also established some almost Kähler identities which obtained by Cirici-Wilson.

With the exterior derivative
d
, there is of course the de Rham cohomology

H^{k}_{dR}(X)=\frac{\ker d\cap\Omega^{k}(X)}{{\rm{Im}}d\cap\Omega^{k}(X)},

that is present on all Riemannian manifolds. Since
d^{\Lambda}d^{\Lambda}=0
, there is also a natural cohomology

H^{k}_{d^{\Lambda}}(X)=\frac{\ker d^{\Lambda}\cap\Omega^{k}(X)}{{\rm{Im}}d^{\Lambda}\cap\Omega^{k}(X)}.

This cohomology has been discussed in
[
21
,
30
,
31
,
32
,
33
]
.

We utilize the compatible triple
(X,J,g)
on
X
to write the Laplacian associated with the
d^{\Lambda}
-cohomology:

\Delta_{d^{\Lambda}}=[d^{\Lambda},d^{\Lambda_{\ast}}].

The self-adjoint Laplacian naturally defines a harmonic form.

We consider an self-adjoint Laplacian

\Delta_{d}+\Delta_{d^{\Lambda}}=[d,d^{\ast}]+[d^{\Lambda},d^{\Lambda_{\ast}}].

Suppose that
J
is integrable, i.e.,
N_{J}=0
, then

\Delta_{d}=\Delta_{d^{\Lambda}}=2\Delta_{\partial}=2\Delta_{\bar{\partial}}.

In symplectic case, we introduce two self-adjoint operators

\Delta_{d^{\prime}}:=[d^{\prime},d^{\prime\ast}]\ (resp.\ \Delta_{d^{\prime\prime}}:=[d^{\prime\prime},d^{\prime\prime\ast}]).

By the inner product

\langle\alpha,\Delta_{d^{\bullet}}\alpha\rangle_{L^{2}(X)}=\|d^{\bullet}\alpha\|^{2}+\|d^{\bullet\ast}\alpha\|^{2}

we are led to the following definition.

Definition 3.1
.

A differential form
\alpha\in\Omega^{\ast}(X)
is call
d^{\bullet}
-harmonic if
\Delta_{d^{\bullet}}\alpha=0
, or equivalently,
d^{\bullet}\alpha=d^{\bullet\ast}\alpha=0
. We denote the space of
d^{\bullet}
-harmonic
k
-forms by
\mathcal{H}_{d^{\bullet}}^{k}(X)
.

We will show that

\ker\Delta_{d^{\prime}}\cap\Omega^{k}(X)=\ker\Delta_{d^{\prime\prime}}\cap\Omega^{k}(X)=\ker(\Delta_{d}+\Delta_{d^{\Lambda}})\cap\Omega^{k}(X).

Proposition 3.2
.

For any almost Kähler manifold the following identities hold:

(1)
\Delta_{d}=[d,d^{\ast}]=[d^{\prime},d^{\prime\ast}]+[d^{\prime},d^{\prime\prime\ast}]+[d^{\prime\prime},d^{\prime\ast}]+[d^{\prime\prime},d^{\prime\prime\ast}].

(2)
\Delta_{d^{\Lambda}}=[d^{\Lambda},d^{\Lambda_{\ast}}]=[d^{\prime},d^{\prime\ast}]-[d^{\prime},d^{\prime\prime\ast}]-[d^{\prime\prime},d^{\prime\ast}]+[d^{\prime\prime},d^{\prime\prime\ast}].

In
[
8
, Lemma 3.8]
, the authors gave a relation between
\ker\Delta_{d}
and
\ker\Delta_{d^{\Lambda}}
as follows. If
\alpha\in\Omega^{k}(X)
is a
\Delta_{d}
-harmonic form in a closed almost Kähler manifold
(X,J,\omega)
, then

\|d^{\Lambda}\alpha\|^{2}+\|d^{\Lambda_{\ast}}\alpha\|^{2}=4\textrm{Re}(J^{-1}(A_{J}+\bar{A}_{J})J\alpha,d^{\Lambda_{\ast}}\alpha)+4\textrm{Re}(J^{-1}(A^{\ast}_{J}+\bar{A}^{\ast}_{J})J\alpha,d^{\Lambda}\alpha).

Lemma 3.3
.

(c.f.
[
8
, Lemma 3.6]
)
Let
(X,J,\omega)
be a closed almost Kähler manifold. Then

\Delta_{d^{\prime}}=\Delta_{d^{\prime\prime}}=\frac{1}{4}(\Delta_{d}+\Delta_{d^{\Lambda}}).

In particular,

\ker{\Delta_{d}}\cap\ker{\Delta_{d^{\Lambda}}}\cap\Omega^{k}(X)=\ker d^{\prime}\cap\ker d^{\prime\ast}\cap\Omega^{k}(X)=\ker d^{\prime\prime}\cap\ker d^{\prime\prime\ast}\cap\Omega^{k}(X).

Proof.

Following Proposition
3.2
, we get

\Delta_{d}+\Delta_{d^{\Lambda}}=2[d^{\prime},d^{\prime\ast}]+2[d^{\prime\prime},d^{\prime\prime\ast}]=2\Delta_{d^{\prime}}+2\Delta_{d^{\prime\prime}}.

Noting that

\begin{split}[d^{\prime},d^{\prime\ast}]&=[\partial_{J},\partial^{\ast}_{J}]+[\partial_{J},\bar{A}^{\ast}_{J}]+[\bar{A}_{J},\partial^{\ast}_{J}]+[\bar{A}_{J},\bar{A}^{\ast}_{J}]\\
&=\Delta_{\partial_{J}}+\Delta_{\bar{A}_{J}}+[\partial_{J},\bar{A}^{\ast}_{J}]+[\bar{A}_{J},\partial^{\ast}_{J}]\\
&=\Delta_{\bar{\partial}_{J}}+\Delta_{A_{J}}+[A_{J},\bar{\partial}^{\ast}_{J}]+[\bar{\partial}_{J},A^{\ast}_{J}]\\
&=[\bar{\partial}_{J}+A_{J},\bar{\partial}_{J}^{\ast}+A^{\ast}_{J}]\\
&=[d^{\prime\prime},d^{\prime\prime\ast}].\\
\end{split}

Here we use the identities
\Delta_{\partial_{J}}+\Delta_{\bar{A}_{J}}=\Delta_{\bar{\partial}_{J}}+\Delta_{A_{J}}
,
[\partial_{J},\bar{A}^{\ast}_{J}]=[A_{J},\bar{\partial}^{\ast}_{J}]
and
[\bar{A}_{J},\partial^{\ast}_{J}]=[\bar{\partial}_{J},A^{\ast}_{J}]
(see Proposition
2.5
and
2.6
). Therefore, we get

\Delta_{d^{\prime}}=\Delta_{d^{\prime\prime}}=\frac{1}{4}(\Delta_{d}+\Delta_{d^{\Lambda}})
.
∎

3.2
Hard Lefschetz Condition

From Lemma
3.3
, we know that
\mathcal{H}^{k}_{d^{\prime}}(X)=\mathcal{H}^{k}_{d^{\prime\prime}}(X)\subset\mathcal{H}^{k}_{d}(X)
and

\dim\mathcal{H}^{k}_{d^{\prime}}(X)=\dim\mathcal{H}^{k}_{d^{\prime\prime}}(X)\leq\dim\mathcal{H}^{k}_{d}(X),

it implies that
\mathcal{H}^{k}_{d^{\bullet}}(X)
is finite dimensional. We denote the Betti numbers of
X
by
b_{i}(X):=\dim\mathcal{H}^{i}_{d}(X)
. The Euler number of
X
is given by

\chi(X)=\sum_{i\geq 0}(-1)^{i}\dim\mathcal{H}^{i}_{d}(X)=\sum_{i\geq 0}(-1)^{i}b_{i}.

We denote the
i
-th almost Käher Betti numbers by
b^{AK}_{i}:=\dim\mathcal{H}^{i}_{d^{\prime}}(X)
. Therefore we define the almost Käher Euler number as follows

\chi^{AK}(X)=\sum_{i\geq 0}(-1)^{i}\dim\mathcal{H}^{i}_{d^{\prime}}(X)=\sum_{i\geq 0}(-1)^{i}b^{AK}_{i}.

Remark 3.4
.

Suppose the almost complex structure
J
on
X
is integrable, i.e.,
(X,J,\omega)
is Kählerian. Hence
A_{J}=\bar{A}_{J}=0
. In this case,
\mathcal{H}^{k}_{d^{\prime}}(X)=\ker\Delta_{\partial}\cap\Omega^{k}
and
\mathcal{H}^{k}_{d^{\prime\prime}}(X)=\ker\Delta_{\bar{\partial}}\cap\Omega^{k}
. By the identity
\Delta_{\partial}=\Delta_{\bar{\partial}}=\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{d}
, one knows that

\mathcal{H}^{k}_{d^{\prime}}(X)=\mathcal{H}^{k}_{d^{\prime\prime}}(X)=\mathcal{H}^{k}_{dR}(X).

Hence the almost Käher Euler numbers
\chi^{AK}(X)
is the Euler number of
X
.

A special class of symplectic manifolds is represented by those ones satisfying the Hard Lefschetz Condition (HLC), i.e., those closed
2n
-dimensional symplectic manifolds
(X,J,\omega)
for which the map

L^{k}:H^{n-k}_{dR}(X)\rightarrow H^{n+k}_{dR}(X),\ \forall\ 0\leq k<n

are isomorphisms. In particular, a classical result states if
(X,J,\omega)
is a closed Kähler manifold, then
(X,J,\omega)
satisfies the HLC
[
18
]
. In
[
28
]
, the authors studied the Hard Lefschetz property of
\ker\Delta_{d}
on almost Kähler manifold.

Theorem 3.5
.

(
[
28
, Theorem 5.2]
)
Let
(X,J,\omega)
be a closed
2n
-dimensional almost Kähler manifold. Then
\mathcal{H}^{k}_{d^{\prime}}(X)
satisfies the HLC.

Proof.

Notice that
4\Delta_{d^{\prime}}=\Delta_{d}+\Delta_{d^{\Lambda}}
. We only to need to prove that
\ker\Delta_{d}\cap\ker\Delta_{d^{\Lambda}}
satisfies HLC. Let us start by showing that

[\Delta_{d}+\Delta_{d^{\Lambda}},L]=0.

In fact, by the Jacobi identity, we have

[L,[d,d^{\ast}]]+[d,[d^{\ast},L]]-[d^{\ast},[L,d]]=0,

and

[L,[d^{\Lambda_{\ast}},d^{\Lambda}]]+[d^{\Lambda_{\ast}},[d^{\Lambda},L]]-[d^{\Lambda},[L,d^{\Lambda_{\ast}}]]=0.

Since
[L,d]=[L,d^{\Lambda_{\ast}}]=0
,
[d^{\ast},L]=-d^{\Lambda_{\ast}}
,
[d^{\Lambda},L]=d
, and

[d,d^{\Lambda_{\ast}}]=[d^{\Lambda_{\ast}},d]=d^{\Lambda_{\ast}}d+dd^{\Lambda_{\ast}},

we have

[L,[d,d^{\ast}]]+[L,[d^{\Lambda_{\ast}},d^{\Lambda}]]=0.

which gives
[\Delta_{d}+\Delta_{d^{\Lambda}},L]=0
. Thus
L
maps
\ker{\Delta_{d}}\cap\ker{\Delta_{d^{\Lambda}}}=\ker{\Delta_{d}+\Delta_{d^{\Lambda}}}
to itself. A similar argument gives
[\Delta_{d}+\Delta_{d^{\Lambda}},\Lambda]=0
, it implies that
\Lambda(\ker{\Delta_{d}}\cap\ker{\Delta_{d^{\Lambda}}})\subset\ker{\Delta_{d}}\cap\ker{\Delta_{d^{\Lambda}}}
. Thus there is an
sl_{2}
-structure on
\ker{\Delta_{d}}\cap\ker{\Delta_{d^{\Lambda}}}
and our theorem follows.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

The above proof gives
[L,\Delta_{d}]=[d,d^{\Lambda_{\ast}}]
. Since
[d,d^{\Lambda_{\ast}}]=0
if and only if
J
is integrable. We then know that
X
is Kählerian if and only if
[L,\Delta_{d}]=0
.

3.3
Index of a family elliptic operators

Let

be a closed Riemannian manifold of real dimension

. Then for each

\mathcal{D}_{dR}=d+d^{\ast}

whose index is the Euler number of

\begin{split}Index(\mathcal{D}_{dR})&=\dim\ker{\mathcal{D}_{dR}}-\dim({\rm{coker}}{\mathcal{D}_{dR}})\\

&=\sum_{k=0}^{\dim X}(-1)^{k}b_{k}\\
&=\chi(X).\\
\end{split}

For the almost Kähler manifold
(X,J,\omega)
, we construct a family elliptic operator

\mathcal{D}(t)=\sqrt{\frac{2}{1+t^{2}}}\big((d^{\prime}+td^{\prime\prime})+(d^{\prime\ast}+td^{\prime\prime\ast})\big):\bigoplus_{k=even}\Omega^{k}(X)\rightarrow\bigoplus_{k=odd}\Omega^{k}(X).

(3.1)

Hence
\mathcal{D}(0)=d^{\prime}+d^{\prime\ast}
and
\mathcal{D}(1)=\mathcal{D}_{dR}

Proposition 3.9

(

For any

Proof.

To calculate the symbol of
\mathcal{D}^{2}(t)

and choose a basis

such that the metric is written as

with

4.1

We begin the proof the Theorem
1.3
by recalling some basis notions in Hodge theory and almost Kähler geometry. If
X
is an oriented complete Riemannian manifold, let
d^{\ast}
be the adjoint operator of

acting on the space of
L^{2}

k
-forms. Denote by
\Omega^{k}_{(2)}(X)
and
\mathcal{H}^{k}_{(2)}(X)
the spaces of
L^{2}

k
-forms and

\mathcal{H}^{k}_{(2)}(X)

Suoppse that
(X,J,\omega)
is a complete almost Kähler manifold. We denote by

\mathcal{H}^{k}_{(2);d^{\prime}}(X)=\{\alpha\in\Omega^{k}_{(2)}(X):\Delta_{d^{\prime}}\alpha=0\}

-harmonic

We follow the method of Gromov’s

]
to choose a sequence of cutoff functions
\{f_{\varepsilon}\}
satisfying the following conditions:

(i)
f_{\varepsilon}
is smooth and takes values in the interval

(ii) The subsets

(iii) The differential of

everywhere bounded by
\varepsilon

\|df_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(X)}=\sup_{x\in X}|df_{\varepsilon}|\leq\varepsilon.

Thus one obtains another useful

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
(X,J,\omega)
be a complete almost Kähler manifold. If an
L^{2}

k
-form

\Delta_{d^{\prime}}
-harmonic, then
d^{\prime}\alpha=d^{\prime\ast}\alpha=0

Proof.

We want to justify the integral identity

\langle\Delta_{d^{\prime}}\alpha,\alpha\rangle=\langle d^{\prime}\alpha,d^{\prime}\alpha\rangle+\langle d^{\prime\ast}\alpha,d^{\prime\ast}\alpha\rangle

If
d^{\prime}\alpha
and

are

(i.e., square integrable on

), then this follows by Lemma
[
12
, 1.1. A]

\begin{split}0&=\langle\Delta_{d^{\prime}}\alpha,f^{2}_{\varepsilon}\alpha\rangle\\
&=\langle d^{\prime}\alpha,d^{\prime}(f^{2}_{\varepsilon}\alpha)\rangle+\langle d^{\prime\ast}\alpha,d^{\prime\ast}(f^{2}_{\varepsilon}\alpha)\rangle\\
&=\langle d^{\prime}\alpha,f^{2}_{\varepsilon}(d^{\prime}\alpha)\rangle+\langle d^{\prime}\alpha,2f_{\varepsilon}\partial f_{\varepsilon}\wedge\alpha\rangle+\langle d^{\prime\ast}\alpha,f^{2}_{\varepsilon}(d^{\prime\ast}\alpha)\rangle-\langle d^{\prime\ast}\alpha,\ast(2f_{\varepsilon}\bar{\partial}f_{\varepsilon}\wedge\ast\alpha)\rangle\\
&=I_{1}(\varepsilon)+I_{2}(\varepsilon),\\
\end{split}

where

\begin{split}|I_{1}(\varepsilon)|&=\langle d^{\prime}\alpha,f^{2}_{\varepsilon}d^{\prime}\alpha\rangle+\langle d^{\prime\ast}\alpha,f^{2}_{\varepsilon}d^{\prime\ast}\alpha\rangle\\
&=\int_{X}f^{2}_{\varepsilon}(|d^{\prime}\alpha|^{2}+|d^{\prime\ast}\alpha|^{2})\\
\end{split}

and

\begin{split}|I_{2}(\varepsilon)|&=|\langle d^{\prime}\alpha,2f_{\varepsilon}\partial f_{\varepsilon}\wedge\alpha\rangle-\langle d^{\prime\ast}\alpha,\ast(2f_{\varepsilon}\bar{\partial}f_{\varepsilon}\wedge\ast\alpha)\rangle|\\
&\leq|\langle d^{\prime}\alpha,2f_{\varepsilon}\partial f_{\varepsilon}\wedge\alpha\rangle|+|\langle d^{\prime\ast}\alpha,\ast(2f_{\varepsilon}\bar{\partial}f_{\varepsilon}\wedge\ast\alpha)\rangle|\\
&\lesssim\int_{X}|df_{\varepsilon}|\cdot|f_{\varepsilon}|\cdot|\alpha|(|d^{\prime}\alpha|+|d^{\prime\ast}\alpha|).\\
\end{split}

Then we choose
f_{\varepsilon}
such that
|df_{\varepsilon}|^{2}<\varepsilon f_{\varepsilon}
on

and estimate

by Schwartz inequality. Then

|I_{2}(\varepsilon)|\lesssim\varepsilon\|f_{\varepsilon}\alpha\|_{L^{2}(X)}\big(\int_{X}f^{2}_{\varepsilon}(|d^{\prime}\alpha|^{2}+|d^{\prime\ast}\alpha|^{2})\big)^{\frac{1}{2}},

and hence

for
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
.
∎

Now, we begin to give the lower bound on the spectrum of the operator
\Delta_{d^{\prime}}
on
\Omega^{k}_{(2)}
for

.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

To simply notation we shall write
a\lesssim b
for

a\approx b
, for
b\lesssim a\lesssim b

-form

Since

\begin{split}\langle(\Delta_{d}+\Delta_{d^{\Lambda}})\psi,\psi\rangle&=\langle L^{k}(\Delta_{d}+\Delta_{d^{\Lambda}})\phi,L^{k}\phi\rangle\\
&\approx\langle(\Delta_{d}+\Delta_{d^{\Lambda}})\phi,\phi\rangle.\end{split}

Then we write
\psi=d\eta+\psi^{\prime}
, for
\eta=\theta\wedge\omega^{k-1}\wedge\phi
and

Next, since

\|d\phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(X)}\lesssim\langle\Delta_{d}\phi,\phi\rangle\lesssim\langle(\Delta_{d}+\Delta_{d^{\Lambda}})\phi,\phi\rangle\lesssim\langle(\Delta_{d}+\Delta_{d^{\Lambda}})\psi,\psi\rangle,

we have

\|\psi^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(X)}\lesssim\|\theta\|_{L^{\infty}(X)}\langle(\Delta_{d}+\Delta_{d^{\Lambda}})\psi,\psi\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Now,

This yields the desired estimate

\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(X)}\lesssim\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(X)}\lesssim\langle(\Delta_{d}+\Delta_{d^{\Lambda}})\psi,\psi\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\langle(\Delta_{d}+\Delta_{d^{\Lambda}})\phi,\phi\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}

for the forms

For the

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
(X,J,\omega)
be a complete 2n-dimensional almost Kähler manifold with a
d
(sublinear) symplectic form
\omega
. Then for any

\mathcal{H}^{k}_{(2);d^{\prime}}(X)=\{0\}.

Proof.

By hypothesis, there exists a 1-form
\theta
with
\omega=d\theta
and

\|\theta(x)\|_{L^{\infty}(X)}\leq c(1+\rho(x,x_{0})),

where
c
is an absolute constant. In what follows we assume that the distance function
\rho(x,x_{0})
is smooth for
x\neq x_{0}
. The general case follows easily by an approximation argument.

Let
\eta:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be smooth,
0\leq\eta\leq 1
,

\eta(t)=\left\{\begin{aligned} 1,&&t\leq 0\\
0,&&t\geq 1\end{aligned}\right.

and consider the compactly supported function

f_{j}(x)=\eta(\rho(x_{0},x)-j),

where

is a positive integer.

Let
\alpha
be a
(\Delta_{d}+\Delta_{d^{\Lambda}})
-harmonic
k
-form in
L^{2}
,

, and consider the form
\Phi=\alpha\wedge\theta
. Observing that
d^{\ast}(\alpha\wedge\omega)=d^{\Lambda_{\ast}}(\alpha\wedge\omega)=0

(\Delta_{d}+\Delta_{d^{\Lambda}})
-harmonic
(k+2)
-form in

, and noticing that

0=\langle d^{\ast}(\omega\wedge\alpha),f_{j}\Phi\rangle=\langle\omega\wedge\alpha,d(f_{j}\Phi)\rangle.

(4.1)

We further note that, since
\omega=d\theta
and

,

\begin{split}0&=\langle\omega\wedge\alpha,d(f_{j}\Phi)\rangle\\
&=\langle\omega\wedge\alpha,f_{j}d\Phi\rangle+\langle\omega\wedge\alpha,df_{j}\wedge\Phi\rangle\\
&=\langle\omega\wedge\alpha,f_{j}\omega\wedge\alpha\rangle+\langle\omega\wedge\alpha,df_{j}\wedge\theta\wedge\alpha\rangle.\\
\end{split}

(4.2)

Since
0\leq f_{j}\leq 1
and
\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}f_{j}(x)(\ast\alpha)(x)=\ast\alpha(x)
, it follows from the dominated convergence theorem that

\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\langle\omega\wedge\alpha,f_{j}\omega\wedge\alpha\rangle_{L^{2}(X)}=\|\omega\wedge\alpha\|^{2}_{L^{2}(X)}.

(4.3)

Since
\omega
is bounded,
supp(df_{j})\subset B_{j+1}\backslash B_{j}
and
\|\theta(x)\|_{L^{\infty}}=O(\rho(x_{0},x))
, one obtains that

|\langle\omega\wedge\alpha,df_{j}\wedge\theta\wedge\alpha\rangle|\leq(j+1)C\int_{B_{j+1}\backslash B_{j}}|\alpha(x)|^{2}dx,

(4.4)

where

is a constant independent of

.

We claim that there exists a subsequence
\{j_{i}\}_{i\geq 1}
such that

\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}(j_{i}+1)\int_{B_{j_{i}+1}\backslash B_{j_{i}}}|\alpha(x)|^{2}dx=0.

(4.5)

If not, there exists a positive constant

such that

\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}(j+1)\int_{B_{j+1}\backslash B_{j}}|\alpha(x)|^{2}dx\geq a>0.

This inequality implies

\begin{split}\int_{X}|\alpha(x)|^{2}dx&=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\int_{B_{j+1}\backslash B_{j}}|\alpha(x)|^{2}dx\\
&\geq a\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{j+1}\\
&=+\infty\\
\end{split}

which is a contradiction to the assumption
\int_{X}|\alpha(x)|^{2}dx<\infty
. Hence, there exists a subsequence
\{j_{i}\}_{i\geq 1}
for which (
4.5
) holds. Using (
4.4
) and (
4.5
), one obtains

\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\langle\omega\wedge\alpha,df_{j}\wedge\theta\wedge\alpha\rangle=0

(4.6)

It now follows from (

), (
4.3
) and (

) that

. Since

is injective

,

as desired.
∎

Following Theorem
1.3
, we then have

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
(X,J,\omega)
be a complete
2n
-dimensional almost Kähler manifold with
d
(bounded) symplectic form
\omega

-form

\ker(d^{\prime}+d^{\prime\ast})\cap\bigoplus_{k=even/odd}\Omega^{k}_{(2)}=\{0\}.

Proof.

We only prove the case of

\alpha=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\alpha_{2k}\in\Omega^{even}(X)
be an
L^{2}
-form on

, where
\alpha_{2k}\in\Omega^{2k}_{(2)}(X)

\begin{split}(d^{\prime}+d^{\prime\ast})^{2}&=[d^{\prime},d^{\prime\ast}]+(d^{\prime})^{2}+(d^{\prime\ast})^{2}\\
&=[d^{\prime},d^{\prime\ast}]+(\partial_{J}^{2}+[\partial_{J},\bar{A}_{J}])+((\partial^{\ast}_{J})^{2}+[\partial^{\ast}_{J},\bar{A}^{\ast}_{J}])\\
&=\frac{1}{4}(\Delta_{d}+\Delta_{d^{\Lambda}})+([\partial_{J},\bar{A}_{J}]-[\bar{\partial}_{J},A_{J}])+([\partial^{\ast}_{J},\bar{A}^{\ast}_{J}]-[\bar{\partial}^{\ast}_{J},A^{\ast}_{J}]).\\

Here we use the identities

By the inner product

\begin{split}I:&=\langle([\partial_{J},\bar{A}_{J}]-[\bar{\partial}_{J},A_{J}]+[\partial^{\ast}_{J},\bar{A}^{\ast}_{J}]-[\bar{\partial}^{\ast}_{J},A^{\ast}_{J}])\alpha,\alpha\rangle\\

where

\begin{split}\langle(\Delta_{d}+\Delta_{d^{\Lambda}})\alpha,\alpha\rangle&=\langle\sum_{k=0}^{n}(\Delta_{d}+\Delta_{d^{\Lambda}})\alpha_{2k},\alpha_{2k}\rangle\\

-Hodge numbers
h_{(2)}^{k}(X)
:

\chi(X)=\sum_{k=0}^{2n}(-1)^{k}h_{(2)}^{k}(X).

Now, We give a lower bound on the spectra of the Laplace operator
\Delta_{d}:=dd^{\ast}+d^{\ast}d
on
L^{2}
-forms
\Omega^{k}(X)
for
k\neq n
.

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
(X,J,\omega)
be a complete
2n
-dimensional almost Kähler manifold with
d
(bounded) symplectic form
\omega
, i.e., there exists a bounded
1
-form
\theta
such that
\omega=d\theta
. There is a uniform positive constant
C
only depends on
n
with following significance. If the Nijenhuis tensor
N_{J}
satisfies

C\|N_{J}\|_{L^{\infty}(X)}\leq\|\theta\|^{-1}_{L^{\infty}(X)},

then every
L^{2}

k
-form
\alpha
on
X
of degree
k\neq n
satisfies the inequality

\langle\Delta_{d}\alpha,\alpha\rangle\geq\frac{1}{8}\lambda^{2}_{0}\langle\alpha,\alpha\rangle,

where
\lambda_{0}
is positive constant in Theorem
1.3
. In particular,

\mathcal{H}^{k}_{d}(X)=\{0\}

unless
k\neq n
.

Proof.

Following the identity on Proposition
3.2
, we get

\begin{split}\Delta_{d}&=\frac{1}{2}(\Delta_{d}+\Delta_{d^{\Lambda}})+2[\partial_{J},\bar{\partial}^{\ast}_{J}]+2[\bar{\partial}_{J},\partial^{\ast}_{J}]\\
&=\frac{1}{2}(\Delta_{d}+\Delta_{d^{\Lambda}})+2[\bar{A}_{J}^{\ast},\bar{\partial}_{J}]+2[A_{J},\partial^{\ast}_{J}]+2[A^{\ast}_{J},\partial_{J}]+2[\bar{A}_{J},\bar{\partial}_{J}^{\ast}],\\
&=\frac{1}{2}(\Delta_{d}+\Delta_{d^{\Lambda}})+2(I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}+I_{4})\\
\end{split}

Here we use the identities (3) in Proposition
2.5
. By the inner product

\begin{split}\langle I_{1}\alpha,\alpha\rangle&=\langle\bar{\partial}_{J}\alpha,\bar{A}_{J}\alpha\rangle+\langle\bar{A}^{\ast}_{J}\alpha,\bar{\partial}^{\ast}_{J}\alpha\rangle\\
&=\langle(\bar{\partial}_{J}+A_{J})\alpha,\bar{A}_{J}\alpha\rangle-\langle A_{J}\alpha,\bar{A}_{J}\alpha\rangle+\langle\bar{A}^{\ast}_{J}\alpha,(\bar{\partial}^{\ast}_{J}+A^{\ast}_{J})\alpha\rangle-\langle\bar{A}^{\ast}_{J}\alpha,A^{\ast}_{J}\alpha\rangle\\
&=\langle d^{\prime\prime}\alpha,\bar{A}_{J}\alpha\rangle-\langle A_{J}\alpha,\bar{A}_{J}\alpha\rangle+\langle\bar{A}^{\ast}_{J}\alpha,d^{\prime\prime\ast}\alpha\rangle-\langle\bar{A}^{\ast}_{J}\alpha,A^{\ast}_{J}\alpha\rangle\\
&=\langle d^{\prime\prime}\alpha,\bar{A}_{J}\alpha\rangle+\langle\bar{A}^{\ast}_{J}\alpha,d^{\prime\prime\ast}\alpha\rangle-\langle[\bar{A}^{\ast}_{J},A_{J}]\alpha,\alpha\rangle\\
&=\langle d^{\prime\prime}\alpha,\bar{A}_{J}\alpha\rangle+\langle\bar{A}^{\ast}_{J}\alpha,d^{\prime\prime\ast}\alpha\rangle.\\
\end{split}

Here we use the identity
[\bar{A}^{\ast}_{J},A_{J}]=0
. Similarly,

\begin{split}&\langle I_{2}\alpha,\alpha\rangle=\langle d^{\prime\ast}\alpha,A^{\ast}_{J}\alpha\rangle+\langle A_{J}\alpha,d^{\prime}\alpha\rangle\\
&\langle I_{3}\alpha,\alpha\rangle=\langle d^{\prime}\alpha,A_{J}\alpha\rangle+\langle A^{\ast}_{J}\alpha,d^{\prime\ast}\alpha\rangle\\
&\langle I_{4}\alpha,\alpha\rangle=\langle d^{\prime\prime\ast}\alpha,\bar{A}^{\ast}_{J}\alpha\rangle+\langle\bar{A}_{J}\alpha,d^{\prime\prime}\alpha\rangle\\
\end{split}

Therefore, we obtain

\begin{split}|\langle(I_{1}+I_{4})\alpha,\alpha\rangle|&\leq 2\|d^{\prime\prime}\alpha\|\cdot\|\bar{A}_{J}\alpha\|+2\|\bar{A}^{\ast}_{J}\alpha\|\cdot\|d^{\prime\prime\ast}\alpha\|\\
&\leq\frac{1}{4}(\|d^{\prime\prime}\alpha\|^{2}_{L^{2}(X)}+\|d^{\prime\prime\ast}\alpha\|^{2}_{L^{2}(X)})+4\|N_{J}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(X)}\|\alpha\|^{2}_{L^{2}(X)}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{4}\langle[d^{\prime\prime},d^{\prime\prime\ast}]\alpha,\alpha\rangle+4\|N_{J}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(X)}\|\alpha\|^{2}_{L^{2}(X)}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{16}\langle(\Delta_{d}+\Delta_{d^{\Lambda}})\alpha,\alpha\rangle+4\|N_{J}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(X)}\|\alpha\|^{2}_{L^{2}(X)},\\
\end{split}

and

\begin{split}|\langle(I_{2}+I_{3})\alpha,\alpha\rangle|&\leq 2\|d^{\prime}\alpha\|\|A_{J}\alpha\|+2\|A^{\ast}_{J}\alpha\|\|d^{\prime\ast}\alpha\|\\
&\leq\frac{1}{4}(\|d^{\prime}\alpha\|^{2}_{L^{2}(X)}+\|d^{\prime\ast}\alpha\|^{2}_{L^{2}(X)})+4\|N_{J}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(X)}\|\alpha\|^{2}_{L^{2}(X)}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{4}\langle[d^{\prime},d^{\prime\ast}]\alpha,\alpha\rangle+4\|N_{J}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(X)}\|\alpha\|^{2}_{L^{2}(X)}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{16}\langle(\Delta_{d}+\Delta_{d^{\Lambda}})\alpha,\alpha\rangle+4\|N_{J}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(X)}\|\alpha\|^{2}_{L^{2}(X)},\\
\end{split}

where
C
is a positive constant only depend on
n
.
Combining above inequalities, we get

\begin{split}\langle\Delta_{d}\alpha,\alpha\rangle&\geq\frac{1}{2}\langle(\Delta_{d}+\Delta_{d^{\Lambda}})\alpha,\alpha\rangle-2|\langle(I_{1}+I_{4})\alpha,\alpha\rangle|-2|\langle(I_{2}+I_{3})\alpha,\alpha\rangle|\\
&\geq\frac{1}{4}\langle(\Delta_{d}+\Delta_{d^{\Lambda}})\alpha,\alpha\rangle-16C\|N_{J}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(X)}\|\alpha\|^{2}_{L^{2}(X)}\\
&\geq(\frac{1}{4}\lambda^{2}_{0}-16C\|N_{J}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(X)})\|\alpha\|^{2}_{L^{2}(X)}.\\
\end{split}

If the Nijenhuis tensor
N_{J}
satisfies

16C\|N_{J}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}\leq\frac{1}{8}\lambda^{2}_{0},

then

\langle\Delta_{d}\alpha,\alpha\rangle\geq\frac{\lambda^{2}_{0}}{8}\|\alpha\|^{2}_{L^{2}(X)}.

∎

Let

be a vector bundle equipped with a Hermitian metric and Hermitian connection
\nabla

Index(\mathcal{D})=\int_{X}\mathcal{L}_{X}\wedge Ch(L).

Here
\mathcal{L}_{X}

\mathcal{L}_{X}=1+\cdots+e(X)

where
1\in H^{0}(X)
and

is the Euler class. For each
\varepsilon
,
\nabla_{\varepsilon}=d+\sqrt{-1}\varepsilon\theta
is a unitary connection on the trivial line bundle
L=\tilde{X}\times\mathbb{C}
. The operator
\mathcal{D}_{\varepsilon}:=d^{\nabla_{\varepsilon}}+(d^{\ast})^{\nabla_{\varepsilon}}
can be view as a
G_{\varepsilon}
operator on the Hilbert space
H
of
U(1)
equivalent basis
L^{2}
differential forms on
\tilde{X}\times U(1)

[
24
]
.

Theorem 4.10
.

(
[
24

)
The operator
\tilde{D}_{\varepsilon}
has a finite projective
L^{2}

L^{2}Index_{G_{\varepsilon}}(\tilde{D}_{\varepsilon})=\int_{X}\mathcal{L}_{X}\wedge\exp(\frac{\varepsilon}{2\pi}[\omega]).

Now, we begin to proof the Singer conjecture under the special symplectic hyperbolic case.

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

This number is a polynomial in
\varepsilon
whose highest degree term is
\int_{X}(\frac{\omega}{2\pi})^{n}\neq 0
thus for

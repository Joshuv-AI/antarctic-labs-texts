Expansion and attraction of RDS: long time behavior of the solution to singular SDE
Chengcheng Ling and Michael Scheutzow

Abstract
. We provide a framework for studying the expansion rate of the image of a bounded set under a flow in Euclidean space and apply it to stochastic differential equations (SDEs for short) with singular coefficients. If the singular drift of the SDE can be split into two terms, one of which is singular and the radial component of the other term has a radial component of sufficient strength in the direction of the origin, then the random dynamical system generated by the SDE admits a pullback attractor. AMS 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 60H10, 60G17, 60J60, 60H50 Keywords: semi-flow, random dynamical system, pullback attractor, singular stochastic differential equation, Brownian motion, dispersion of random sets, chaining, Krylov estimate, regularization by noise, elliptic partial differential equation, Zvonkin transformation



1.
Introduction

Regularization by noise
, i.e. existence and uniqueness of solutions under the assumption of non-degenerate noise, has been established for a large class of singular stochastic differential equations (SDEs).
It was shown recently that these equations also generate a random dynamical system (RDS), see
[
18
]
,
and like in the classical (non-singular) case it therefore seems natural to establish asymptotic properties
of these RDS for large times, like expansion rates of bounded sets and the existence of attractors
or even
synchronization
(meaning that the attractor is a single random point).

We consider an SDE on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with time homogeneous coefficients

(1.1)

\displaystyle dX_{t}=b(X_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t+\sigma(X_{t})\,\mathrm{d}W_{t},\hskip 10.00002ptX_{s}=x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\hskip 10.00002ptt\geqslant s\geqslant 0,

where
d\geqslant 1
,
b:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
\sigma=(\sigma_{ij})_{1\leqslant i,j\leqslant d}:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\rightarrow L(\mathbb{R}^{d})

(:=d\times d
real valued matrices
)
are measurable, and
(W_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
is a standard
d
-dimensional Brownian motion defined on some filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geqslant 0},{\mathbb{P}})
. We assume that
b\in\tilde{L}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
(defined in
Section

2.1
), so
b
does not have to be continuous nor bounded, and
\sigma\sigma^{*}
(
\sigma^{*}
denotes the matrix transpose of
\sigma
) is bounded and uniformly elliptic and
\nabla\sigma\in\tilde{L}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
p>d
(time homogeneous
Krylov-Röckner
condition). These are sufficient conditions for the well-posedness of the equation (
1.1
), see
[
14
]
and
[
24
]
. They also imply the existence of a flow and random dynamical system (RDS) generated by the solution to (
1.1
)
[
18
]
.

First we analyse the linear expansion rate of the flow generated by a singular SDE. In classical results, see e.g.
[
21
]
,
[
7
]
, Lipschitz continuity or one-sided Lipschitz continuity of the coefficients of the SDE is assumed to obtain bounds on the expansion rate. Obviously we lack these properties in our current setting. Instead, we assume the noise to be non-degenerate, so we can apply the
Zvonkin transformation
to get an SDE which has Lipschitz-like coefficients and this SDE is (in an appropriate sense) equivalent to the original one (
1.1
). The
Zvonkin transformation
was invented by A. K. Zvonkin in
[
32
]
for
d=1
and then generalized by A. Yu. Veretennikov in
[
23
]
to
d\geqslant 1
. It has become a rather standard tool to study
well-posedness
of singular SDEs, see e.g.
[
27
]
,
[
25
]
and
[
24
]
.
This tool heavily relies on regularity estimates of the solution to Kolmogorov’s equation corresponding to (
1.1
) which can be found for instance in
[
13
]
in the classical setting.
In this paper we adapt the method to the study of the RDS induced by singular SDEs. We show that the flow expands linearly (see
Theorem

5.4
), a property which was established for non-singular SDEs with not necessarily non-degenerate noise in
[
3
,
4
,
19
,
20
,
21
]
.
Our proof mainly depends on stability estimates (see
Theorem

5.2
). These kind of estimates were studied before, see for instance
[
12
]
,
[
27
]
and
[
28
]
, but the dependence of the constants on the coefficients was not specified.
We give a formula in
Theorem

5.2
which states this dependence explicitly. It also yields the expansion rate constant in
Theorem

5.4
.

Secondly, we aim at conditions which guarantee the existence of an attractor for the RDS generated by a singular SDE. Clearly, one can not expect that an attractor exists without further conditions (an example without attractor is the case in which the drift is zero and the diffusion is constant).
Since
[
6
]
, numerous papers appeared in which the existence of attractors for various finite and infinite dimensional RDS was shown, e.g.
[
2
]
,
[
8
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
15
]
and
[
31
]
.
A common way to prove the existence of an attractor is to show the existence of a random compact
absorbing
set and then to apply the criterion from
[
6
, Theorem 3.11]
.
Just like
[
7
]
, we will use a different and more probabilistic criterion from
[
5
]
(Proposition
2.8
). Roughly speaking, all one has to show is that the image of a very large ball will be contained inside a fixed large ball after a (deterministic) long time with high probability. In
[
7
]
this was
shown under the assumption that the diffusion is bounded and Lipschitz and the drift
b(x)
has a component of sufficient strength (compared to the diffusion) in the direction of the origin for large
|x|
. In our set-up, this condition is too restrictive. Instead, we assume that the drift can be written in the form
b=b_{1}+b_{2}
,
in which
b_{1}
is singular and
b_{2}
has a component of sufficient strength (compared to the diffusion and the localized
L_{p}
-norm of
b_{1}
) in the direction of the origin for large
|x|
.

Structure of the paper

We introduce notation and the main results in
Section

2
.
In
Section

3
we study the expansion rate of the diameter of the image of a bounded set under a flow under rather general conditions. These results are minor modifications of results contained in
[
21
]
which are proved by
chaining techniques
.
Section

contains estimates on functionals of the solution to the singular SDE, namely quantitative versions of Krylov’s estimates and Khasminskii’s lemma. The first part of the main results of this paper is presented in

Section

we show the existence of an attractor of the RDS generated by the singular SDE. In
Appendix

we study regularity estimates of elliptic partial differential equations with emphasis on the dependence on the coefficients. We believe that these estimates are of independent interest.

\displaystyle\tilde{L}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):=\{f:\|f\|_{\tilde{L}_{p}}:=\sup_{z}\|\xi_{\delta}^{z}f\|_{L_{p}}<\infty\},

,

\xi(x)=1
for

, and
\xi(x)=0
for
|x|>1
. For
(\alpha,p)\in\mathbb{R}\times[1,\infty)
, let

be the usual Bessel potential space with norm

The localized

-space is defined as

\displaystyle\tilde{H}^{\alpha,p}:=\{f:\|f\|_{\tilde{H}^{\alpha,p}}:=\sup_{z}\|\xi_{\delta}^{z}f\|_{H^{\alpha,p}}<\infty\}.

From

and

and

, but the norm does, of course. More precisely, by
[

, Proposition 4.1]
, for the
\tilde{L}_{p}
-norms with different

and
\delta_{2}
and

, if we use the notation

to denote the

space with support radius

for localization, then

(2.2)

\displaystyle N_{1}\|\cdot\|_{(\tilde{L}_{p})_{\delta_{1}}}\leqslant\|\cdot\|_{(\tilde{L}_{p})_{\delta_{2}}}\leqslant N_{2}\Big(\frac{\delta_{2}}{\delta_{1}}\Big)^{d}\|\cdot\|_{(\tilde{L}_{p})_{\delta_{1}}},

where

are constants independent of
\delta_{1},\delta_{2}
. For convenience we take
\delta=1
in the following. For further properties of these spaces we refer to
[
24
]
. In the following, all derivatives should be interpreted in the weak sense. Occasionally we will use Einstein’s summation convention (omitting the summation sign for indices appearing twice). We will often use the notation
r_{+}=\max\{r,0\}
for
the positive part of

.

2.2.
Preliminaries

In the following, all random processes will be defined on a given probability space

(\Omega,{\mathcal{F}},{\mathbb{P}})
.

Definition 2.1
.

A flow
\phi
on a Polish (i.e. separable and completely metrizable) space
X
equipped with its Borel-
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{B}(X)

is a measurable map

\displaystyle\phi:\left\{(s,t,x,\omega)\in[0,\infty)^{2}\times X\times\Omega:s\leqslant t<\infty\right\}\rightarrow X

such that, for each
\omega\in\Omega
,

(1)

\phi_{s,s}(x)=x
for all
x\in X
and
s\geqslant 0
,

(2)

(s,t,x)\mapsto\phi_{s,t}(x)
is continuous,

(3)

for each
s,t
, the map
x\mapsto\phi_{s,t}(x)
is one-to-one,

(4)

for all
0\leqslant s\leqslant t<u
and
x\in X
, the following identity holds

\displaystyle\phi_{s,u}(x)=\phi_{t,u}(\phi_{s,t}(x)).

Next, we define the concepts of a metric dynamical system and a random dynamical system.

Definition 2.2
.

A
metric dynamical system
(MDS for short)

\theta=(\Omega,\mathcal{F},{\mathbb{P}},\{\theta_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}})
is a
probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},{\mathbb{P}})
with a family of measure preserving transformations
\{\theta_{t}:\Omega\rightarrow\Omega,t\in\mathbb{R}\}
such that

(1)

\theta_{0}=\mathrm{id},\theta_{t}\circ\theta_{s}=\theta_{t+s}
for all
t,s\in\mathbb{R}
;

(2)

the map
(t,\omega)\mapsto\theta_{t}\omega
is measurable and
\theta_{t}{\mathbb{P}}={\mathbb{P}}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Definition 2.3

(RDS,
[
1
]
)
.

A (global)
random dynamical system
(RDS)
(\theta,\varphi)

on a Polish space
(X,d)
over an MDS
\theta
is a mapping

\varphi:\left\{(s,x,\omega)\in[0,\infty)\times X\times\Omega\right\}\rightarrow X

such that, for each
\omega\in\Omega
,

(1)

measurability:
\varphi
is
(\mathcal{B}([0,\infty))\otimes\mathcal{X}\otimes\mathcal{F},\mathcal{X})
-measurable,

(2)

(t,x)\mapsto\varphi_{t}(x)
is continuous,

(3)

\varphi
satisfies the following (perfect)
cocycle property
: for all
t,s\geqslant 0
,
x\in X
,

(2.3)

\displaystyle\varphi_{0}(.,\omega)=\mathrm{id},\hskip 10.00002pt\varphi_{t+s}(x,\omega)=\varphi_{t}(\varphi_{s}(x,\omega),\theta_{s}\omega)

Clearly, an RDS
\varphi
induces a flow via
\phi_{s,t}(x):=\varphi_{t}(x,\theta_{s}.)
. We say that an SDE generates a flow resp. an RDS if its solution map has a modification which is a flow resp. an RDS.
The following study is based on the flow generated by the solution to the SDE with singular drift. Therefore we state the result from
[
18
, Theorem 4.5, Corollary 4.10]
on the existence of a global semi-flow and a global RDS for singular SDEs under the following condition.

Assumption 2.4
.

For
p,\rho\in(2d,\infty)
assume

(i)

b\in\tilde{L}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\sigma:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to L({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is measurable,
\|\nabla\sigma\|\in\tilde{L}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

(ii)

There exist
K_{1},K_{2}>0
such that for
a:=\sigma\sigma^{*}
we have

K_{1}|\zeta|^{2}\leqslant\langle a(x)\zeta,\zeta\rangle\leqslant K_{2}|\zeta|^{2},\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\zeta,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Remark 2.5
.

Note that
\tilde{L}_{p}\subset\tilde{L}_{p^{\prime}}
whenever
p>p^{\prime}
. Therefore, if
2.4
holds with different values of
p
and
\rho
, then it also holds with the larger of the two numbers replaced by
the smaller one. In particular, the following result which was formulated for
p=\rho
can still be applied.

Theorem 2.6
.

[
18
, Theorem 4.5, Corollary 4.10]

If
2.4
holds,
then the SDE (
1.1
) admits a flow
\phi
and a corresponding RDS
\varphi
.

We will often write
\psi_{t}(x)
instead of
\phi_{0,t}(x)
. Abusing notation we will sometimes say "Let
\psi_{t}(x)
(or just
\psi
) be a flow …" instead of "Let
\phi_{s,t}(x),x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},0\leqslant s\leqslant t<\infty
be a flow and
\psi_{t}(x):=\phi_{0,t}(x)
,

Definition 2.7

(Attractor,
[
6
]
)
.

Let
\varphi
be an RDS over the MDS
\theta=(\Omega,\mathcal{F},{\mathbb{P}},\{\theta_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}})
. The random set
A(\omega)
is a
(pullback) attractor
if

(1)

measurability:

is a random element in the metric space of nonempty compact subsets of

equipped with the Hausdorff distance,

(2)

with full measure such that

\varphi(t,\omega)(A(\omega))=A(\theta_{t}\omega),\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\,\omega\in\Omega_{t},

(3)

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{x\in B}\text{dist}(\varphi(t,\theta_{-t}\omega)(x),A(\omega))=0.

One way to verify the existence of an attractor is the following criterion.

Proposition 2.8
.

(
[
5
]
,
[

Let
\varphi

(i)

(ii)

\forall

\lim_{R\rightarrow\infty}{\mathbb{P}}\Big(\omega\in\Omega:B_{r}\subset\bigcup_{s=0}^{\infty}\bigcap_{t\geqslant s}\varphi^{-1}(t,B_{R},\theta_{-t}\omega)\Big)=1.

2.3.
Main results

Based on general estimates on the speed of dispersion of random sets in
Section

3
(cf.
Theorem

3.3
) and on quantitative estimates of the solution to singular SDE in
Section

4
, we will show the following result in

Theorem 2.9
.

If
2.4
holds, then there exists a constant
\kappa>0
such that for the flow
\psi
generated by the solution to (

) we have, for any compact

\displaystyle\limsup_{T\rightarrow\infty}\Big(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\frac{1}{T}|\psi_{t}(x)|\Big)\leqslant\kappa\hskip 10.00002pta.s..

The precise statement including a formula for

will be given in

5.4
. There, we can see that

as

(when all other parameters remain unchanged). The following example explains this fact: as the noise becomes more and more degenerate, the linear bound on the dispersion of a bounded set under the flow approaches infinity, so our non-degeneracy assumption on the noise cannot be avoided.

Example 2.10
.

In
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, for
\epsilon>0
, we consider the system

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\mathrm{d}X_{t}&=B(Y_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t+\epsilon\,\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{1},\quad X_{0}\in\mathbb{R},\\
\mathrm{d}Y_{t}&=[((-Y_{t})\vee(-1))\wedge 1]\,\mathrm{d}t+\epsilon\,\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{2},\quad Y_{0}\in\mathbb{R},\end{array}\right.

where

\displaystyle B(y):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}|y|^{-q}&\text{ if }y\neq 0,\\
0&\text{else},\end{array}\right.\hskip 10.00002ptq\in(0,\frac{1}{4}).

and

are two independent

, we have

for

. Clearly there exists a unique solution

to (

\displaystyle X_{t}=X_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}B(Y_{s})\mathrm{d}s+W_{t}^{1},\hskip 10.00002ptt\geqslant 0.

By the ergodic theorem, almost surely,

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}B(Y_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}B(y)\pi_{\epsilon}(\mathrm{d}y),

3.
Expansion of sets under a flow

In this section, we assume that
\psi:[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is measurable
such that

is continuous for every

and

(we do not require that

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume that there exists
\alpha>0
and a constant
c_{1}>0
such that for each
r>d
, there exists
c=c(r)>0
such that for all
x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
T>0
, we have

(3.1)

\displaystyle\Big({\mathbb{E}}\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}(|\psi_{t}(x)-\psi_{t}(y)|^{r})\Big)^{1/r}\leqslant c|x-y|{\mathrm{e}}^{c_{1}r^{\alpha}T}.

Then
\psi
has a modification (which we denote by the same symbol) which is jointly continuous in
(t,x)
and for each
\gamma>0
and
u>0
,

(3.2)

\displaystyle\limsup_{T\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{T}\sup_{\chi_{T,\gamma}}\log{\mathbb{P}}\Big(\sup_{x,y\in\chi_{T,\gamma}}\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}|\psi_{t}(x)-\psi_{t}(y)|\geqslant u\Big)\leqslant-I(\gamma),

where
\sup_{\chi_{T,\gamma}}
means that we take the supremum over all cubes
{\chi_{T,\gamma}}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with side length
{\mathrm{e}}^{-\gamma T}
, and
I:[0,\infty)\to{\mathbb{R}}
is defined as

(3.3)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} I(\gamma):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\gamma^{1+1/\alpha}\alpha(1+\alpha)^{-1-1/\alpha}c_{1}^{-1/\alpha}\quad\quad\quad\text{ if }&\gamma\geqslant c_{1}(\alpha+1)d^{\alpha}\\
d(\gamma-c_{1}d^{\alpha})\quad\quad\quad\text{ if }&c_{1}d^{\alpha}<\gamma\leqslant c_{1}(\alpha+1)d^{\alpha}\\
0\quad\quad\quad\text{ if }&\gamma\leqslant c_{1}d^{\alpha}.\end{array}\right.\end{aligned}

Proof.

We follow the argument in
[
21
, Proof of Theorem 3.1]
. Without loss of generality we take
\chi:=\chi_{T,\gamma}=[0,{\mathrm{e}}^{-\gamma T}]^{d}
and define
Z_{t}(x):=\phi_{t}({\mathrm{e}}^{-\gamma T}x)
,
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. From (
3.1
) we get

\displaystyle\Big({\mathbb{E}}\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}(|Z_{t}(x)-Z_{t}(y)|^{r})\Big)^{1/r}\leqslant ce^{-\gamma T}|x-y|{\mathrm{e}}^{c_{1}r^{\alpha}T}.

By Kolmogorov’s Theorem (see, e.g.
[
21
, Lemma 2.1]
),
\phi
admits a jointly continuous modification and for any
\rho\in(0,\frac{r-d}{r})
:

(3.4)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\Big(\sup_{x,y\in\chi_{T,\gamma}}\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}|\psi_{t}(x)-\psi_{t}(y)|\geqslant u\Big)\leqslant\tilde{c}{\mathrm{e}}^{(c_{1}r^{\alpha}-\gamma)rT}u^{-r},

where
\tilde{c}
depends on
r,d,\rho
only. Taking logarithms, dividing by
T
, then letting
T\rightarrow\infty
and optimizing over
r>d
we get the desired result (
3.2
).
∎

Remark 3.2
.

Since
I(\gamma)=\sup_{r>d}\big\{r\big(\gamma-c_{1}r^{\alpha}\big)\big\}
is the supremum of affine functions, the map
\gamma\mapsto I(\gamma)
is convex. Further,
I
grows faster than linearly.

The following theorem is a reformulation of
[
21
, Theorem 2.3]
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\psi:[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
be jointly continuous and satisfy the assumptions of
Lemma

3.1
and (
3.1
) hold with constants
c_{1}
and
\alpha
. Assume further, that there exist
c_{2}
and
c_{3}\geqslant 0
such that, for each
k>0
and each bounded set
S\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, the following holds

(3.5)

\displaystyle\limsup_{T\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{T}\log\sup_{x\in S}{\mathbb{P}}\Big(\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}|\psi_{t}(x)|\geqslant kT\Big)\leqslant-c_{2}k^{2}+c_{3}.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a compact subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with box (or upper entropy) dimension
\Delta>0
. Then

(3.6)

\displaystyle\limsup_{T\rightarrow\infty}\Big(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\frac{1}{T}|\psi_{t}(x)|\Big)\leqslant\kappa\hskip 10.00002pta.s.,

where

\kappa:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\Big(\frac{c_{3}+\gamma_{1}\Delta}{c_{2}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}&\text{ if }\frac{d}{d-\Delta}<\alpha+1,\\
\Big(\frac{c_{3}+\gamma_{2}\Delta}{c_{2}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}&\text{otherwise},\end{array}\right.\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ with }\hskip 10.00002pt\gamma_{1}=\frac{c_{1}d^{\alpha+1}}{d-\Delta},\hskip 10.00002pt\gamma_{2}=c_{1}(\alpha^{-1}\Delta)^{\alpha}(1+\alpha)^{1+\alpha}.

Remark 3.4
.

In addition to the assumptions of the previous theorem, let us assume that
\psi_{t}(x)=\phi_{0,t}(x)
where
\phi
is a flow (later, we will only consider this case). Let
\mathcal{X}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
be any compact set and let
B
be a ball in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
containing
\mathcal{X}
. Clearly, the boundary
\partial B
of
B
has box dimension
d-1
. The flow property of
\phi
implies that for each
t\geqslant 0
, the boundary of
\phi_{0,t}(B)
is contained in
\phi_{0,t}(\partial B)
and therefore
any almost sure upper bound
\kappa
for the linear expansion rate of the set
\partial B
is at the same time an upper bound for the linear expansion rate of the set
B
and hence of
\mathcal{X}
. This means that
in the case of a flow, the formula for
\kappa
in the theorem always holds with
\Delta
replaced by
d-1
(or the minimum of
\Delta
and
d-1
).

4.
Quantitative version of Krylov estimates

We will show a quantitative version of Krylov estimates (
4.1
). One can find similar results in the literature with implicit constants, for instance
[

]
,
[
27
]
and
[

]
, which however do not fit our needs since some proofs in later sections rely on the explicit dependence of the constants on the coefficients of the SDE. In the following lemma, a constant
C_{\mathrm{Kry}}
appears
which depends on
q,p,\rho,d
only. While we will regard
p,\rho,d
as fixed throughout, we will apply the formula with different values of
q
and we will therefore write
C_{\mathrm{Kry}}(q)
for clarity.

Lemma 4.1
.

If
2.4
holds and
(X_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
solves (
1.1
), then, for
f\in\tilde{L}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

C_{\mathrm{Kry}}(q)>0

only such that for

(4.1)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[\int_{s}^{t}|f(X_{r})|\mathrm{d}r\Big|\mathcal{F}_{s}]\leqslant C_{\mathrm{Kry}}(q)\Gamma\big(K_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2}}+(t-s)\big)\|f\|_{\tilde{L}_{q}},

where
\Gamma:=\big(\frac{K_{2}}{K_{1}}\big)^{\frac{4d^{2}}{1-d/\rho}}+\big(\frac{\|\nabla\sigma\|_{\tilde{L}_{\rho}}^{2}}{K_{1}}\big)^{\frac{4d^{2}}{1-d/\rho}}+\big(\frac{\|b\|_{\tilde{L}_{p}}}{K_{1}}\big)^{\frac{4d}{1-d/p}}
.

Proof.

It is sufficient to show the estimate for positive
f
. (
4.1
) clearly holds when
q=\infty
, so we assume
q\in(d,\infty)
. All positive constants
C_{i}
,
i=0,\cdots,7
appearing in the proof only depend on
p,\rho,q,d
. We will regard
p,\rho
and
d
as fixed but we will vary
q
in the following proof and we will therefore highlight the dependence of constants on
q
in some cases (for
C_{0}
and
C_{1})
. First we show that
a:=\sigma\sigma^{*}
is
1-\frac{d}{\rho}
-Hölder continuous using Sobolev’s embedding theorem and the condition that
\sigma\in\tilde{H}^{1,\rho}
with
\rho>d
. Indeed

\displaystyle\omega_{1-d/\rho}(a):=

\displaystyle\sup_{x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},x\neq y,|x-y|\leqslant 1}\frac{\|a(x)-a(y)\|}{|x-y|^{1-d/\rho}}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\sup_{x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},x\neq y,|x-y|\leqslant 1}\big(\frac{\|(\sigma\sigma^{*})(x)-\sigma(x)\sigma^{*}(y)\|}{|x-y|^{1-d/\rho}}+\frac{\|\sigma(x)\sigma^{*}(y)-(\sigma\sigma^{*})(y)\|}{|x-y|^{1-d/\rho}}\big)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\sup_{x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},x\neq y,|x-y|\leqslant 1}\big(\frac{\|\sigma^{*}(x)-\sigma^{*}(y)\|\|\sigma\|_{\infty}}{|x-y|^{1-d/\rho}}+\frac{\|\sigma(x)-\sigma(y)\|\|\sigma\|_{\infty}}{|x-y|^{1-d/\rho}}\big)

(4.2)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C_{\rho,d}\sqrt{K_{2}}\|\nabla\sigma\|_{\tilde{L}_{\rho}}.

We follow the idea from
[
29
, Theorem 3.4]
. Applying
Theorem

A.3
with
p^{\prime}=\infty
, we see that there is a unique solution
u\in\tilde{H}^{2,q}
to

(4.3)

\displaystyle\lambda u-\frac{1}{2}a_{ij}\partial_{ij}u=f

provided that
\lambda\geqslant C_{0}(q)\frac{K_{2}^{2}}{K_{1}}(\frac{K_{1}+\sqrt{K_{2}}\|\nabla\sigma\|_{\tilde{L}_{\rho}}}{K_{1}})^{\frac{2}{1-d/\rho}}=:\lambda_{0}(q)
. Further, for
\lambda\geqslant\lambda_{0}(q)
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u(x)|

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{1}(q)\lambda^{-\frac{2-d/q}{2}}{K_{1}}^{-\frac{d}{2q}}\big(\frac{K_{1}+\sqrt{K_{2}}\|\nabla\sigma\|_{\tilde{L}_{\rho}}}{K_{1}}\big)^{\frac{d}{1-d/\rho}}\|f\|_{\tilde{L}_{q}}=:U_{1,q}(\lambda)\|f\|_{\tilde{L}_{q}},

(4.4)

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla u(x)|

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{1}(q)\lambda^{-\frac{1-d/q}{2}}K_{1}^{-\frac{1+d/q}{2}}\big(\frac{K_{1}+\sqrt{K_{2}}\|\nabla\sigma\|_{\tilde{L}_{\rho}}}{K_{1}}\big)^{\frac{d}{1-d/\rho}}\|f\|_{\tilde{L}_{q}}=:U_{2,q}(\lambda)\|f\|_{\tilde{L}_{q}}.

Fix
t\geqslant s\geqslant 0
and define the stopping time

\tau_{R}:=\inf\Big\{\bar{s}>s:\int_{s}^{\bar{s}}\big|b(X_{r})\big|\,\mathrm{d}r\geqslant R\Big\},\hskip 10.00002pt0<R<\infty.

By the generalized Itô’s formula (see e.g.
[
24
, Lemma 4.1 (iii)]
)

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle(X_{t\wedge\tau_{R}})-u(X_{s\wedge\tau_{R}})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{s\wedge\tau_{R}}^{t\wedge\tau_{R}}a_{ij}(X_{r})\partial_{ij}u(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}r+\int_{s\wedge\tau_{R}}^{t\wedge\tau_{R}}\big(\nabla u(X_{r})\big)^{*}\sigma(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}W_{r}+\int_{s\wedge\tau_{R}}^{t\wedge\tau_{R}}b(X_{r})\cdot\nabla u(X_{r})\mathrm{d}r.

Using (
4.3
), the mean value theorem, (
4
) and BDG’s inequality, we get that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[

\displaystyle\int_{s\wedge\tau_{R}}^{t\wedge\tau_{R}}f(X_{r})\mathrm{d}r\Big|\mathcal{F}_{s}\big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[(u(X_{s\wedge\tau_{R}})-u(X_{t\wedge\tau_{R}}))\Big|\mathcal{F}_{s}\big]+{\mathbb{E}}\big[\lambda\int_{s\wedge\tau_{R}}^{t\wedge\tau_{R}}u(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}r\Big|\mathcal{F}_{s}\big]+{\mathbb{E}}\big[\int_{s\wedge\tau_{R}}^{t\wedge\tau_{R}}b(X_{r})\cdot\nabla u(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}r\Big|\mathcal{F}_{s}\big]

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla u(x)|{\mathbb{E}}\big[\big|\int_{s\wedge\tau_{R}}^{t\wedge\tau_{R}}b(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}r+\int_{s\wedge\tau_{R}}^{t\wedge\tau_{R}}\sigma(X_{r})\mathrm{d}W_{r}\big|\Big|\mathcal{F}_{s}\big]+\lambda(t-s)\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u(x)|

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\hskip 10.00002pt+\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\nabla u(x)|{\mathbb{E}}\big[\int_{s\wedge\tau_{R}}^{t\wedge\tau_{R}}\big|b(X_{r})\big|\mathrm{d}r\Big|\mathcal{F}_{s}\big]

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla u(x)|C_{2}\sqrt{K_{2}}(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\lambda(t-s)\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u(x)|+2\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\nabla u(x)|{\mathbb{E}}\big[\int_{s\wedge\tau_{R}}^{t\wedge\tau_{R}}\big|b(X_{r})\big|\mathrm{d}r\Big|\mathcal{F}_{s}\big]

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C_{2}\sqrt{K_{2}}(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2}}U_{2,q}(\lambda)\|f\|_{\tilde{L}_{q}}+\lambda(t-s)U_{1,q}(\lambda)\|f\|_{\tilde{L}_{q}}

(4.5)

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\hskip 10.00002pt\hskip 10.00002pt\hskip 10.00002pt+2U_{2,q}(\lambda)\|f\|_{\tilde{L}_{q}}{\mathbb{E}}\big[\int_{s\wedge\tau_{R}}^{t\wedge\tau_{R}}\big|b(X_{r})\big|\mathrm{d}r\Big|\mathcal{F}_{s}\big].

Here, the constant
C_{2}>0
comes from BDG’s inequality.
We apply this inequality to
f=|b|
with
q=p
. Then, for
\lambda\geqslant\lambda_{0}(p)
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[\int_{s\wedge\tau_{R}}^{t\wedge\tau_{R}}|b(X_{r})|\mathrm{d}r\Big|\mathcal{F}_{s}\big]\leqslant

\displaystyle C_{2}\sqrt{K_{2}}(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2}}U_{2,p}(\lambda)\|b\|_{\tilde{L}_{p}}+\lambda(t-s)U_{1,p}(\lambda)\|b\|_{\tilde{L}_{p}}

\displaystyle+2U_{2,p}(\lambda)\|b\|_{\tilde{L}_{p}}{\mathbb{E}}\big[\int_{s\wedge\tau_{R}}^{t\wedge\tau_{R}}|b(X_{r})|\mathrm{d}r\Big|\mathcal{F}_{s}\big].

If
\lambda\geqslant\lambda_{0}(p)
is so large that
U_{2,p}(\lambda)\|b\|_{\tilde{L}_{p}}=C_{1}(p)\lambda^{-\frac{1-d/p}{2}}K_{1}^{-\frac{1+d/p}{2}}\big(\frac{K_{1}+\sqrt{K_{2}}\|\nabla\sigma\|_{\tilde{L}_{\rho}}}{K_{1}}\big)^{\frac{d}{1-d/\rho}}\|b\|_{\tilde{L}_{p}}\leqslant\frac{1}{4},

(4.6)

\displaystyle\lambda\geqslant\big(4C_{1}(p){K_{1}}^{\frac{-1-d/p}{2}}\big(\frac{K_{1}+\sqrt{K_{2}}\|\nabla\sigma\|_{\tilde{L}_{\rho}}}{K_{1}}\big)^{\frac{d}{1-d/\rho}}\|b\|_{\tilde{L}_{p}}\big)^{\frac{2}{1-d/p}},

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[\int_{s\wedge\tau_{R}}^{t\wedge\tau_{R}}|b(X_{r})|\mathrm{d}r\Big|\mathcal{F}_{s}\big]

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{C_{2}}{2}\sqrt{K_{2}}(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2}}+2\lambda(t-s)U_{1,p}(\lambda)\|b\|_{\tilde{L}_{p}}.

Plugging this into (
4
), observing that, by definition,
U_{1,p}(\lambda)U_{2,q}(\lambda)=U_{1,q}(\lambda)U_{2,p}(\lambda)
, and using (
4.6
) yields, for
\lambda\geqslant\lambda_{0}(p)\vee\lambda_{0}(q)

satisfying (
4.6
),

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}

\displaystyle\big[\int_{s\wedge\tau_{R}}^{t\wedge\tau_{R}}f(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}r\Big|\mathcal{F}_{s}\big]

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C_{3}\big(\sqrt{K_{2}}(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2}}U_{2,q}(\lambda)+\lambda(t-s)(U_{1,q}(\lambda)+U_{1,p}(\lambda)U_{2,q}(\lambda)\|b\|_{\tilde{L}_{p}}\big)\|f\|_{\tilde{L}_{q}}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle 2C_{3}\big(\sqrt{K_{2}}(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2}}U_{2,q}(\lambda)+\lambda(t-s)U_{1,q}(\lambda)\big)\|f\|_{\tilde{L}_{q}}.

Let
\lambda=C_{4}\big(\frac{K_{2}^{2}}{K_{1}}(\frac{K_{1}+\sqrt{K_{2}}\|\nabla\sigma\|_{\tilde{L}_{\rho}}}{K_{1}})^{\frac{2}{1-d/\rho}}+(4C_{1}(p){K_{1}}^{\frac{-1-d/p}{2}}\big(\frac{K_{1}+\sqrt{K_{2}}\|\nabla\sigma\|_{\tilde{L}_{\rho}}}{K_{1}}\big)^{\frac{d}{1-d/\rho}}\|b\|_{\tilde{L}_{p}})^{\frac{2}{1-d/p}}\big)
with

,
which implies

\displaystyle\sqrt{K_{2}}U_{2,q}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=C_{1}(q)\sqrt{K_{2}}(\lambda K_{1})^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\lambda K_{1}^{-1})^{\frac{d}{2q}}\big(\frac{K_{1}+\sqrt{K_{2}}\|\nabla\sigma\|_{\tilde{L}_{\rho}}}{K_{1}}\big)^{\frac{d}{1-d/\rho}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{5}K_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\lambda K_{1}^{-1})^{\frac{d}{2q}}\big(\frac{K_{1}+\sqrt{K_{2}}\|\nabla\sigma\|_{\tilde{L}_{\rho}}}{K_{1}}\big)^{\frac{d}{1-d/\rho}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{6}K_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\big(\frac{K_{2}}{K_{1}}\big)^{\frac{4d^{2}}{1-d/\rho}}+\big(\frac{\|\nabla\sigma\|_{\tilde{L}_{\rho}}^{2}}{K_{1}}\big)^{\frac{4d^{2}}{1-d/\rho}}+\big(\frac{\|b\|_{\tilde{L}_{p}}}{K_{1}}\big)^{\frac{4d}{1-d/p}}\Big)

\displaystyle\lambda U_{1,q}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=C_{1}(q)(\lambda K_{1}^{-1})^{\frac{d}{2q}}\big(\frac{K_{1}+\sqrt{K_{2}}\|\nabla\sigma\|_{\tilde{L}_{\rho}}}{K_{1}}\big)^{\frac{d}{1-d/\rho}}

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}

\displaystyle\Big[\int_{s\wedge\tau_{R}}^{t\wedge\tau_{R}}f(X_{r})\mathrm{d}r\Big|\mathcal{F}_{s}\Big]

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{\mathrm{Kry}}(q)\Big(\big(\frac{K_{2}}{K_{1}}\big)^{\frac{4d^{2}}{1-d/\rho}}+\big(\frac{\|\nabla\sigma\|_{\tilde{L}_{\rho}}^{2}}{K_{1}}\big)^{\frac{4d^{2}}{1-d/\rho}}+\big(\frac{\|b\|_{\tilde{L}_{p}}}{K_{1}}\big)^{\frac{4d}{1-d/p}}\Big)[K_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2}}+(t-s)]\|f\|_{\tilde{L}_{q}}.

appearing in there is the same as in the previous lemma.

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
2.4
hold, let
\Gamma:=\Big(\big(\frac{K_{2}}{K_{1}}\big)^{\frac{4d^{2}}{1-d/\rho}}+\big(\frac{\|\nabla\sigma\|_{\tilde{L}_{\rho}}^{2}}{K_{1}}\big)^{\frac{4d^{2}}{1-d/\rho}}+\big(\frac{\|b\|_{\tilde{L}_{p}}}{K_{1}}\big)^{\frac{4d}{1-d/p}}\Big)

, and any

of (

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\exp\Big(\lambda\int_{S}^{T}|f(X_{r})|\mathrm{d}r\Big)\leqslant 2\cdot 2^{(T-S)\big(\frac{\kappa}{2}K_{2}^{-1/2}+\sqrt{\frac{\kappa^{2}}{4}K_{2}^{-1}+\kappa}\big)^{2}}\leqslant 2\cdot 2^{(T-S)\big(\frac{\kappa^{2}}{K_{2}}+2\kappa\big)},

Proof.

The second inequality is an application of the general inequality

.

Lemma

4.1
shows that there exists some positive integer

such that, for

\displaystyle\lambda{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\int_{\frac{(T-S)j}{n}}^{\frac{(T-S)(j+1)}{n}}\Big|f(X_{r})\Big|\mathrm{d}r\Big|\mathcal{F}_{\frac{(T-S)j}{n}}\Big]\leqslant\frac{1}{2}

Here,

5.1.
Stability estimates of the SDE with weakly differentiable coefficients

Consider the equation

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}Y_{t}^{i}=\tilde{b}(Y_{t}^{i})\,\mathrm{d}t+\tilde{\sigma}(Y_{t}^{i})\,\mathrm{d}W_{t},\hskip 10.00002ptY_{0}^{i}=y_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\hskip 10.00002pti=1,2.

\tilde{a}:=\tilde{\sigma}\tilde{\sigma}^{*}

\tilde{K}_{1},\tilde{K}_{2}>0
such that for all

,

\tilde{K}_{1}|\zeta|^{2}\leqslant\langle\tilde{a}(x)\zeta,\zeta\rangle\leqslant\tilde{K}_{2}|\zeta|^{2},\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let

hold. There exist constants
\kappa_{0},\kappa_{1}>0
depending only on
p,d
,

, such that for any

,

,

,

the solutions

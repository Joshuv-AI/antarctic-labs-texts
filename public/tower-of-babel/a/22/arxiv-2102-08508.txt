Abstract
. A ballot permutation is a permutation π \pi such that in any prefix of π \pi the descent number is not more than the ascent number. In this article, we obtained a formula in close form for the multivariate generating function of { A ⁡ ( n , d , j ) } n , d , j \{A(n,d,j)\}_{n,d,j} , which denote the number of permutations of length n n with d d descents and j j as the first letter. Besides, by a series of calculations with generatingfunctionology, we confirm a recent conjecture of Wang and Zhang for ballot permutations.

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{S}_{n}
be the symmetric group of all permutations of
[n]=\{1,2,\dots,n\}
.
A position
i\in[n-1]
in a permutation
\pi=\pi_{1}\pi_{2}\dotsm\pi_{n}\in\mathcal{S}_{n}
is a
descent
if
\pi_{i}>\pi_{i+1}
,
and an
ascent
if
\pi_{i}<\pi_{i+1}
.
Denote the number of descents of
\pi
by
\des(\pi)
,
and the number of ascents by
\asc(\pi)
.
We call the number
h(\pi)=\asc(\pi)-\des(\pi)

the
height
of
\pi
.
The permutation
\pi
is said to be a
ballot permutation

if
h(\pi_{1}\pi_{2}\cdots\pi_{i})\geq 0

for all
i\in[n]
.
Let
\mathscr{B}_{n}
denote the set of ballot permutations on
[n]
. Define
\mathscr{B}_{0}=\{\epsilon\}
,
where
\epsilon
is the empty permutation.

The problem of enumerating ballot permutations is closely related with
that of enumerating ordinary permutations with
a given up–down signature, see
[
4
,
11
,
25
,
28
]
.
A ballot permutation of length
2n+1
with
n
descents is said to be a
Dyck permutation
,
whose enumeration is the Eulerian-Catalan number, see Bidkhori and Sullivant
[
8
]
.
Let
\mathscr{O}_{n}
be the set of
odd order permutations

of
[n]
, viz., the set of permutations of
[n]
which are the products of cycles with odd lengths. Bernardi, Duplantier and Nadeau
[
6
]
proved
that the number of ballot permutations of length
n

equals the number of odd order permutations of length
n
,
by using compositions of bijections, and thus
1.1
follows.
A short proof is given by Wang and Zhang
[
31
]
.

Theorem 1.1

(Bernardi, Duplantier and Nadeau)
.

The number of ballot permutations of length
n
is

b_{n}=\begin{cases}[(n-1)!!]^{2},&\text{if $n$ is even},\\
n!!(n-2)!!,&\text{if $n$ is odd},\end{cases}

where
(-1)!!=1
.

In order to give a refinement for
1.1
, Spiro
[
1
]
introduced a statistic
M(\pi)
which is defined for a permutation
\pi
such that

M(\pi)=\sum_{c}\min\mathopen{{\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\left(\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\left(\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\left(\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\left(\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}}}}{\cdes(c),\,\casc(c)}\mathclose{{\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\left)\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\left)\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\left)\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\left)\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}}}},

where the sum runs over all cycles
c=(c_{1}c_{2}\dotsm c_{k})
of
\pi
, with the
cyclic descent

\cdes(c)=\mathopen{{\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\left\lvert\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}}}}{\{i\in[k]\colon c_{i}>c_{i+1}\ \text{where $c_{k+1}=c_{1}$}\}}\mathclose{{\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\left\rvert\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\left\rvert\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\left\rvert\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\rvert\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}}}},

and the
cyclic ascent

\casc(c)=\mathopen{{\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\left\lvert\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}}}}{\{i\in[k]\colon c_{i}<c_{i+1}\ \text{where $c_{k+1}=c_{1}$}\}}\mathclose{{\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\left\rvert\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\left\rvert\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\left\rvert\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\rvert\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}}}}=\mathopen{{\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\left\lvert\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}}}}{c}\mathclose{{\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\left\rvert\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\left\rvert\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\left\rvert\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\rvert\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}}}}-\cdes(c),

where
\mathopen{{\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\left\lvert\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}}}}{c}\mathclose{{\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\left\rvert\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\left\rvert\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\left\rvert\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\rvert\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}}}}
is the length of
c
. Spiro conjectured that
the number of ballot permutations of length
n
with
d
descents
equals the number of odd order permutations
\pi
of length
n

such that
M(\pi)=d
, which is confirmed by Wang and the first author
[
32
]
,
and thus
1.2
follows.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
n\geq 1
and
0\leq d\leq\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor
.
The number of ballot permutations of length
n

with
d
descents equals the number of odd order permutations
\pi

of length
n
with
M(\pi)=d
.

1.2
is proved by computing their bivariate generating functions in terms of the
Eulerian number

[
31
]
.
For
n\geq 1
and
0\leq d\leq n-1
,
the Eulerian number, denoted as
A(n,d)
or
\genfrac{<}{>}{0.0pt}{1}{n}{d}
,
is the number of
permutations of
[n]
with
d
descents, see OEIS
[
29
, A008292]
.
We adopt the convention
A(0,0)=1
and

A(n,d)=0,\qquad\text{if $n<0$, or $d<0$, or $d=n\geq 1$, or $d>n$}.

The
n
th
Eulerian polynomial
is

A_{n}(t)=\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{S}_{n}}t^{\des(\pi)}=\sum_{d}A(n,d)t^{d}\quad\text{for $n\geq 1$},

where the notation
\sum_{i}

implies that the index
i
runs over all nonnegative integers
making the summation meaningful, and
A_{0}(t)=1
,
see Kyle Petersen
[
22
, §1.4]
.
The exponential generating function of the Eulerian polynomials is

(1.1)

\sum_{n}A_{n}(t)\frac{x^{n}}{n!}=\frac{t-1}{t-e^{(t-1)x}},

see
[
22
, Theorem 1.6]
and
[
17
, Formula (75)]
. Then the bivariate generating function

(1.2)

A(t,x)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\sum_{d}\frac{A(n,d)t^{d}x^{n}}{n!}=\sum_{n\geq 1}A_{n}(t)\frac{x^{n}}{n!}\\
=\frac{t-1}{t-e^{(t-1)x}}-1=\frac{e^{(1-t)x}-1}{1-te^{(1-t)x}}.

Denote by
b(n,d)
the number of ballot permutations of length
n
with
d
descents,
and denote the bivariate generating function of
\{b(n,d)\}_{n,d}
by

B(t,x)=\sum_{n}\sum_{d\leq n-1}\frac{b(n,d)\,t^{d}x^{n}}{n!}.

Wang and the first author
[
32
]
give the following Theorem by calculating the joint distribution of the peak and descent statistics over ballot permutations.

Theorem 1.3

(Wang and Zhao)
.

We have

B(t,x)=\exp\mathopen{{\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\left(\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\left(\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\left(\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\left(\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}}}}{x+2\sum_{k\geq 1}\sum_{d\leq k-1}A(2k,d)t^{d+1}\frac{x^{2k+1}}{(2k+1)!}}\mathclose{{\mathchoice{\hbox{$\displaystyle\left)\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\textstyle\left)\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\left)\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\left)\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}}}}.

Wang and Zhang
[
31
]

gave another conjecture which refined
1.2
by tracking the neighbors of the
largest letter in these permutations. They defined a word
u
as a
factor
of a word
w
if there exist words
x
and
y
such that

w=xuy
, and a word
u
as a
cyclic factor
of a permutation
\pi\in\mathcal{S}_{n}
if
u
is a factor of some word
v
such that
(v)
is a cycle of
\pi
. This conjecture is confirmed in this paper by computing their multivariate generating functions respectively,
and thus
1.4
follows.

Theorem 1.4

(Wang and Zhang)
.

For all
n
,
d
, and
2\leq j\leq n-1
, we have
b_{n,d}(1,j)+b_{n,d}(j,1)=2p_{n,d}(1,j)
,
where
b_{n,d}(i,j)
is the number of ballot permutations of length
n
with
d
descents which have
inj
as a factor, and
p_{n,d}(i,j)
is the number of odd order permutations of length
n
with
M(\pi)=d
which have
inj
as a cyclic factor.

With
1.4
and the Toeplitz property of
b_{n,d}(i,j)+b_{n,d}(j,i)
and
p_{n,d}(i,j)
showed in
[
31
]
, we obtain in
4.1
the generating function which P. R. Stanley expressed interest in private communication, and is defined by

P(t,x,y,z)=\sum_{n,d}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n-1}\dfrac{2p_{n,d}(i,j)t^{d}x^{n}y^{i}z^{j}}{(j-i-1)!(n-j+i-2)!}.

There are various generalizations of Eulerian numbers. Here we focus on a refinement
A(n,d,j)
of
A(n,d)
defined by Brenti and Welker
[
10
]
, which is the number of permutations of length
n
with
d
descents and first letter
j
for
n\geq 1
,
0\leq d\leq n-1
and
1\leq j\leq n
. They show the real-rootedness of the following polynomials

A^{\langle j\rangle}_{n}(t)=\sum_{d}A(n,d,j)t^{d}\quad\text{for $n\geq 1$},

which refine the Eulerian polynomials. We adopt the convention

A(n,d,j)=0
for other
n,d
and
j
. Define

A(t,x,y):=\sum_{n,d}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{A(n,d,j)t^{d}x^{n}y^{j}}{(j-1)!(n-j)!},

We would give a formula for
A(t,x,y)
in close form, based what a series of formulas with generatingfunctionology could be established, and prove
1.4
.

For this purpose, we give several definitions of enumerating sequences
and calculate their multivariate generating functions in the following sections.
For
n\geq 2,1\leq j\leq n
and

0\leq d\leq n
, let
U(n,\,d,\,j)
denote the number of
permutations of length
n
with
d-1
descents or
n-d-1
ascents and
first letter
j
. Then

(1.3)

U(n,d,j)=A(n,\,n-d-1,\,j)+A(n,\,d-1,\,j),

and

(1.4)

U(n,d,j)=U(n,\,n-d,\,j).

We define the multivariate generating function for
\{U(n,d,j)\}_{n,d,j}

\hat{U}(t,x,y):=\sum_{n,d}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{U(n,d,j)t^{d}x^{n}y^{j}}{(j-1)!(n-j)!},

and

U(t,x,y):=\sum_{n~\text{is odd},j}\sum_{d\leq\frac{n-1}{2}}\frac{U(n,d,j)t^{d}x^{n}y^{j}}{(j-1)!(n-j)!}.

For
n\geq 3,2\leq j\leq n-1
and
0\leq d\leq n-1
, we denote by
E(n,d,j)
the number of permutations in
S_{n}
of length
n
with
d
descents which have
1nj
or
jn1
as a factor,
and define
b(n,d,j):=b_{n,d}(1,j)+b_{n,d}(j,1)
.
Define

E(t,x,y):=\sum_{n,d}\sum_{j=2}^{n-1}\dfrac{E(n,d,j)t^{d}x^{n}y^{j}}{(n-j-1)!(j-2)!},

B(t,x,y):=\sum_{n,d}\sum_{j=2}^{n-1}\dfrac{b(n,d,j)t^{d}x^{n}y^{j}}{(n-j-1)!(j-2)!},

and

P(t,x,y):=\sum_{n,d}\sum_{j=2}^{n-1}\dfrac{p_{n,d}(1,j)t^{d}x^{n}y^{j}}{(j-2)!(n-j-1)!}.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we calculate the formulas for
A(t,x,y),\hat{U}(t,x,y)
and
U(t,x,y)
. In Section 3, we calculate the formulas for
E(t,x,y)
,
P(t,x,y)
and a relation between
B(t,x),B(t,x,y)
and
E(t,x,y)
. Finally,
in Section 4 we confirm the conjecture of Wang and Zhang and calculate
P(t,x,y,z)
.

2.
Formulas for
A(t,x,y),\hat{U}(t,x,y)
and
U(t,x,y)

This section is devoted to giving the generating functions of

\{A(n,d,j)\}_{n,d,j}
, which is the basis of section 3.
One can get the following recursion for
A(n,d,j)
by the values of the second letters in permutations, which could be found in
[
10
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

For
n\geq 2
,
1\leq j\leq n
and
0\leq d\leq n-1
, we have

(2.1)

\begin{split}A(n,d,j)=\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}A(n-1,\,d-1,\,i)+\sum_{i=j+1}^{n}A(n-1,\,d,\,i-1).\end{split}

By
2.1
, we can obtain the following Theorem by generating function calculation.

Theorem 2.2
.

We have

A(t,x,y)=\dfrac{(t-1)xye^{(t-1)xy}}{t-e^{(t-1)x(1+y)}}.

Proof.

Substituting
j
by
j+1
in (
2.1
),we have

(2.2)

\begin{split}A(n,\,d,\,j+1)=\sum_{i=1}^{j}A(n-1,\,d-1,\,i)+\sum_{i=j+2}^{n}A(n-1,\,d,\,i-1).\end{split}

subtracting (
2.1
) from (
2.2
),we get

(2.3)

\begin{split}A(n,\,d,\,j+1)=A(n,d,j)+A(n-1,\,d-1,\,j)-A(n-1,\,d,\,j),\end{split}

where
n\geq 2
,
1\leq j\leq n-1
and
0\leq d\leq n-1
.

Multiplying each term in (
2.3
) by
\dfrac{t^{d}x^{n}y^{j}}{(j-1)!(n-j)!}
and summing over all integers
n,d
and
1\leq j\leq n-1
with subscript transformation, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n,d}\sum_{1\leq\,j\leq\,n-1}\dfrac{A(n,\,d,\,j+1)t^{d}x^{n}y^{j+1}}{(n-j-1)!(j-1)!}

\displaystyle=\,\sum_{n,d}\sum_{j\geq\,1}\dfrac{(j-1)A(n,\,d,\,j)t^{d}x^{n}y^{j}}{(n-j)!(j-1)!}

\displaystyle=y\sum_{n,d}\sum_{j\geq 1}\dfrac{A(n,d,j)jt^{d}x^{n}y^{j-1}}{(n-j)!(j-1)!}-A(t,x,y)

(2.4)

\displaystyle=y\dfrac{\partial A(t,x,y)}{\partial y}-A(t,x,y),

\displaystyle\sum_{n,d}\sum_{1\leq\,j\leq\,n-1}\dfrac{A(n,d,j)t^{d}x^{n}y^{j+1}}{(n-j-1)!(j-1)!}

\displaystyle=\,\sum_{n,d}\sum_{1\leq\,j\leq\,n-1}A(n,d,j)\dfrac{(n-j)t^{d}x^{n}z^{j+1}}{(n-j)!(j-1)!}

\displaystyle=xy\sum_{n,d}\sum_{1\leq\,j\leq\,n-1}\dfrac{nA(n,d,j)t^{d}x^{n-1}y^{j}}{(n-j)!(j-1)!}

\displaystyle\quad-y^{2}\sum_{n,d}\sum_{1\leq\,j\leq\,n-1}\dfrac{jA(n,d,j)t^{d}x^{n}y^{j-1}}{(n-j)!(j-1)!}

(2.5)

\displaystyle=xy\dfrac{\partial A(t,x,y)}{\partial x}-y^{2}\dfrac{\partial A(t,x,y)}{\partial y},

(2.6)

\displaystyle\sum_{n,d}\sum_{1\leq\,j\leq\,n-1}\dfrac{A(n-1,\,d-1,\,j)t^{d}x^{n}y^{j+1}}{(n-j-1)!(j-1)!}=txyA(t,x,y),

(2.7)

\displaystyle\sum_{n,d}\sum_{1\leq\,j\leq\,n-1}\dfrac{A(n-1,\,d,\,j)t^{d}x^{n}y^{j+1}}{(n-j-1)!(j-1)!}=xyA(t,x,y).

Combining (
2
), (
2
), (
2.6
) and (
2.7
), we get

y\dfrac{\partial A(t,x,y)}{\partial y}-A(t,x,y)=xy\dfrac{\partial A(t,x,y)}{\partial x}-y^{2}\dfrac{\partial A(t,x,y)}{\partial y}+txyA(t,x,y)-xyA(t,x,y).

By Maple, we have

(2.8)

\begin{split}A(t,x,y)=xye^{(t-1)xy}F(t,x+xy),\end{split}

where
F(t,x)
is an arbitrary differentiable function.

In the following we will determine
F(t,x)
.
Take derivatives on both sides of (
2.8
), we have

\dfrac{\partial A(t,x,y)}{\partial y}=x[(t-1)xy+1]e^{(t-1)xy}F(t,x+xy)+x^{2}ye^{(t-1)xy}F_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}(t,x+xy),

then

\dfrac{\partial A}{\partial z}(t,x,0)=xF(t,x).

On the other hand, it is easy to see that
A(n,d,1)=A(n-1,\,d)
, then

\dfrac{\partial A}{\partial z}(t,x,0)=\sum_{n,d}\dfrac{A(n,d,1)t^{d}x^{n}}{(n-1)!}=x\,\sum_{n,d}\dfrac{A(n,d)\,x^{n}y^{d}}{n!}=xA(t,x)+x.

Thus

(2.9)

F(t,x)=A(t,x)+1=\frac{t-1}{t-e^{(t-1)x}}.

Plugging (
2.9
) into (
2.8
),one can complete the proof.
∎

According to (
1.4
) and using subscript transformation, we have

\displaystyle U(t,x,y)+U(\frac{1}{t},tx,y)

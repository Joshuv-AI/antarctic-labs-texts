Axial perturbations of black holes in scalar-tensor gravity: near-horizon behaviour
Karim Noui, Hugo Roussille, David Langlois

Abstract
We consider axial (or odd-parity) perturbations of non-spinning hairy black holes (BH) in shift-symmetric DHOST (Degenerate Higher-Order Scalar-Tensor) theories, including terms quartic and cubic in second derivatives of the scalar field. We give a new formulation of the effective metric in which axial perturbations propagate as in general relativity. We then introduce a generic parametrization of the effective metric in the vicinity of the background BH horizon. Writing the dynamics of the perturbations in terms of a Schrödinger-like operator, we discuss in which cases the operator is (essentially) self-adjoint, thus leading to an unambiguous time evolution, according to the choice of parameters characterizing the near-horizon effective metric. This is in particular useful to investigate the stability of the perturbations. We finally illustrate our general analysis with two examples of BH solutions.



I
Introduction

Future observations of binary black hole mergers via GWs offer the tantalizing prospect of detecting the oscillations of the newly created back hole (BH) in the so-called post-merger ringdown phase. The main contribution to these oscillations can be decomposed into discrete modes known as quasi-normal modes (QNMs) – instead of normal modes because these modes decay as they are radiated away. The measurement of QNM frequencies and decay rates provides a very powerful test of general relativity (GR) in the vicinity of a black hole. Indeed, the frequencies and decay rates of QNMs (labelled by the integers
\ell
,
m
and
n
) depend only on the two parameters of a Kerr black hole solution, namely its mass and angular momentum (assuming the BH to be electrically neutral). The measurement of the frequency
\sigma
and the decay rate
\tau
of a single QNM, associated with the complex frequency
\omega=\sigma+i/\tau
thus completely determines the two parameters and consistency can be checked by measuring any other QNM. (Note that the astrophysical environment of the BH could potentially affect the QNMs spectrum and lead to an instability, see e.g.
Jaramillo:2020tuu
;
Jaramillo:2021tmt
.)

With these observational prospects in mind, it is interesting to explore possible deviations from the GR predictions within models of modified gravity. The theory of BH perturbations is already quite involved in general relativity (see e.g.
Chandrasekhar:1985kt
). In the context of modified gravity, the analysis is even more daunting, due to different factors: paucity of exact BH solutions, in particular of rotating BHs; presence of extra modes (e.g. scalar modes in scalar-tensor theories); modified dynamical equations.
In the present work, we avoid some of these difficulties by concentrating on nonrotating configurations and on purely tensorial modes. In the context of scalar-tensor theories, this means that we focus our attention on axial, or odd-parity, modes which are analogous to those of GR but now possess different dynamics. By contrast, we do not discuss here the polar, or even-parity, modes, which contain additional modes due to the presence of the scalar field perturbations.

In the present study,
we work in the most general framework of scalar-tensor theories propagating a single scalar degree of freedom, known as DHOST (Degenerate Higher-Order Scalar-Tensor) theories
Langlois:2015cwa
;
Langlois:2015skt
;
BenAchour:2016cay
;
Crisostomi:2016czh
;
BenAchour:2016fzp
(see
Langlois:2018dxi
and
Kobayashi:2019hrl
for reviews), which include previously studied families of scalar-tensor theories. General DHOST Lagrangians contain second derivatives of the scalar field and can lead to equations of motion of order higher than two, but imposing the degeneracy conditions ensures that the solutions of these equations of motion do not depend on more initial conditions than standard second-order equations of motion. In other words, these degeneracy conditions guarantee the absence of a ghost-like extra degree of freedom.

II
Effective metric for BH Axial Perturbations

II.1
Static and spherically symmetric geometries in DHOST Theories

As discussed in the introduction, we work within the framework of DHOST theories, whose action, up to cubic order in second derivatives of the scalar field, can be written in the form

;
BenAchour:2016fzp

\displaystyle S[g_{\mu\nu},\phi]=\int\differential^{4}{x}\sqrt{-g}\Big(P(X,\phi)+Q(X,\phi)\square\phi+L^{(2)}+L^{(3)}\Big)\,,

\displaystyle L^{(2)}_{4}=\phi^{\mu}\phi_{\mu\rho}\phi^{\rho\nu}\phi_{\nu}\,,\qquad L^{(2)}_{5}=(\phi^{\mu}\phi_{\mu\nu}\phi^{\nu})^{2}\,,

\displaystyle L^{(3)}_{4}=\left(\Box\phi\right)^{2}\phi_{\mu}\phi^{\mu\nu}\phi_{\nu}\,,\quad L^{(3)}_{5}=\Box\phi\,\phi_{\mu}\phi^{\mu\nu}\phi_{\nu\rho}\phi^{\rho}\,,\quad L^{(3)}_{6}=\phi_{\mu\nu}\phi^{\mu\nu}\phi_{\rho}\phi^{\rho\sigma}\phi_{\sigma}\,,

\displaystyle L^{(3)}_{7}=\phi_{\mu}\phi^{\mu\nu}\phi_{\nu\rho}\phi^{\rho\sigma}\phi_{\sigma}\,,\quad L^{(3)}_{8}=\phi_{\mu}\phi^{\mu\nu}\phi_{\nu\rho}\phi^{\rho}\,\phi_{\sigma}\phi^{\sigma\lambda}\phi_{\lambda}\,,

\displaystyle L^{(3)}_{9}=\Box\phi\left(\phi_{\mu}\phi^{\mu\nu}\phi_{\nu}\right)^{2}\,,\quad L^{(3)}_{10}=\left(\phi_{\mu}\phi^{\mu\nu}\phi_{\nu}\right)^{3}\,.

(4)

Note that, for axial modes, one does not need to take into account the degeneracy conditions since the scalar perturbations vanish by construction in the odd-parity sector. So our results would be unchanged for non degenerate scalar-tensor theories. This applies for example to U-DHOST theories

In the rest of this paper, we will restrict our study to shift-symmetric theories, which entails that

In the following, we consider static and spherically symmetric solutions of these theories, characterised by a metric

II.2
Effective metric of axial perturbations

As shown in several previous studies
Tomikawa:2021pca
;
Takahashi:2021bml
;
Langlois:2022ulw

which in general is distinct from the background metric

the background scalar field in addition to the background metric. This affects their dynamics, in contrast with other fields that are minimally coupled to the metric
\mathfrak{g}_{\mu\nu}
, such as an electromagnetic field with a standard action. As a consequence, axial gravitational waves and, say, photons effectively propagate in different geometries.

More specifically, we have shown in

that, for

perturbations about a static and spherically symmetric
background solution of the form (
5
)-(
6

any higher-order scalar-tensor theory of the form (
1

2

Note that (
7
) has a priori no reason to be a

solution of General Relativity, but it can always be seen as a GR solution with appropriate, although artificial in general, energy-momentum tensor.

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}\differential{x}^{\mu}\differential{x}^{\nu}=-A(r)\differential{t}_{*}^{2}+\frac{1}{B(r)}\differential{r}^{2}+C(r)(\differential\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta\,\differential\varphi^{2})\,,

(7)

\displaystyle t_{*}=t-\int\mathrm{d}r\,\Psi(r)\,.

(8)

The effective metric components

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}=\Lambda\left[\Omega\,\mathfrak{g}_{\mu\nu}\,+\,D\,\phi_{\mu}\phi_{\nu}\,+\,S\,\phi_{\mu\nu}+\frac{T}{2}\left(\phi_{\mu}X_{\nu}+\phi_{\nu}X_{\mu}\right)\right]\,,

II.3
Schrödinger-like equation for axial perturbations

As recalled in the previous sections, the dynamics of axial perturbations in higher-order scalar-tensor theories (not necessarily degenerate) corresponds to that of General Relativity with the effective metric (
7

The GR dynamics of linear perturbations

\displaystyle{\cal E}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle{\Box}h_{\mu\nu}+\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}h+(\nabla_{\alpha}\nabla_{\beta}h^{\alpha\beta}-\Box h)g_{\mu\nu}+2\nabla_{(\mu}\nabla_{\alpha}h^{\alpha}_{\nu)}-6\nabla_{\alpha}\nabla_{(\mu}h_{\nu)}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle R_{\mu\nu}h-Rh_{\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{2}Rg_{\mu\nu}h+R^{\alpha\beta}g_{\mu\nu}h_{\alpha\beta}+8R_{\alpha(\mu}h^{\alpha}_{\nu)}=0\,,

where

and we use the standard notation

III
Axial perturbations: asymptotics and stability

III.1
Asymptotic behaviour of the effective metric at the singularity

As our starting point we consider a black hole background metric with (at least) one event horizon located at
r=\mathfrak{r}_{\!s}
in the static coordinates system
used in (
5
). The corresponding effective metric
g_{\mu\nu}
might not be that of a black hole: a priori, it can describe any static and spherically symmetric geometry, such as, for instance, a naked singularity, a regular space-time or a black hole whose horizon differs from the background one. Different examples of such effective metrics have been considered in
Langlois:2022ulw
and some of them will be discussed later on.

Here, we assume the effective metric to be singular (i.e. one its components either vanishes or diverges) for some value of the radius
r={r}_{\!s}
, which can be different from
\mathfrak{r}_{\!s}
or not. The singularity at
r={r}_{\!s}
can be a naked singularity or a coordinate singularity.
Furthermore, we assume the metric to be well-defined and well-behaved in the domain
r>{r}_{\!s}
, by which we mean that all coefficients
A(r)
,
B(r)
and
C(r)
remain strictly positive for
r>{r}_{\!s}
, and give an asymptotically flat metric.

To describe the behaviour of the metric near the singularity, it is convenient to introduce the dimensionless coordinate
\varepsilon
defined by

\displaystyle\varepsilon=\frac{r}{{r}_{\!s}}-1\,.

(22)

In the following, we will assume that the effective metric coefficients behave as power laws of
\varepsilon
in the vicinity of the singularity, so that their leading order terms is of the form

\displaystyle A(r)\,=\,a\,\varepsilon^{\alpha}\left(1+o(1)\right)\,,\quad B(r)\,=\,b\,\varepsilon^{\beta}\left(1+o(1)\right)\,,\quad C(r)\,=\,c\,\varepsilon^{\gamma}\left(1+o(1)\right)\,\ {\rm when}\ \varepsilon\to 0\,,

(23)

where
\alpha
,
\beta
and
\gamma
are real constants, and
a
,
b
and
c
are non-negative numbers.

Rewriting the effective metric in terms of the tortoise coordinate (
19
),

\displaystyle\differential s^{2}\;=\;A\left[-\differential t_{*}^{2}+\differential r_{*}^{2}+\frac{C}{A}\left(\differential\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta\,\differential\varphi^{2}\right)\right]\,,

(24)

the near-singularity behaviour of the effective metric is described by

\displaystyle\differential s^{2}\;\simeq\;a\,\varepsilon^{\alpha}\left[-\differential t_{*}^{2}+\differential r_{*}^{2}+\kappa^{2}\,\varepsilon^{\xi}\left(\differential\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta\,\differential\varphi^{2}\right)\right]\,,\qquad\differential r_{*}={\rho}\,\varepsilon^{\sigma-1}\,\differential\varepsilon\,,

(25)

where we have introduced the new parameters

\displaystyle\kappa^{2}\,\equiv\,\frac{c}{a}\,,\qquad{\rho}\,\equiv\,\frac{{r}_{\!s}}{\sqrt{ab}}\,,\qquad\sigma\,\equiv\,1-\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}\,,\qquad\xi\,\equiv\,\gamma-\alpha\,.

(26)

In order to determine the nature of the singularity of the effective metric, it is useful to express
r_{*}
in terms of
\varepsilon
near the singularity. This gives

\displaystyle{\text{if}}\,\,\,\sigma=0\,,\,\,\ r_{*}\simeq\,{\rho}\ln\varepsilon\,,\qquad{\text{if}}\,\,\,\sigma\neq 0\,,\,\,\ \sigma\,r_{*}\simeq{{\rho}}\,\varepsilon^{\sigma}\,.

(27)

When
\sigma\leq 0
, the singularity
r={r}_{\!s}
corresponds to the limit
r_{*}\rightarrow-\infty
and the
domain of the Schrödinger-like equation (
17
) is therefore the full real line
\mathbb{R}
. As a consequence, following the arguments of
Horowitz:1995gi
, the singularity is null and the space-time is globally hyperbolic. By contrast, when
\sigma>0
, the singularity is located at
r_{*}=0
. This gives
a time-like singularity and the domain of the Schrödinger-like equation is reduced to the half-line
\mathbb{R}_{*}^{+}
.

As argued in
Horowitz:1995gi
, one expects the Schrödinger operator in (
17
) to be essentially self-adjoint
when the singularity is null, which means that the evolution of the perturbation is well-defined and does not need any extra boundary conditions at the singularity. However, this might not be the case when the singularity is time-like and a careful study of the asymptotic behaviour of the potential at the singularity is needed to conclude about the self-adjointness.

III.2
Asymptotic of the potential and self-adjointness of the Schrödinger operator

The detailed study of the asymptotic behaviour of the potential at the singularity is given in Appendix
B
. This is crucial to conclude about the self-adjoint properties of the Schrödinger operator (
17

Lewin2022

We summarise below the main results of Appendix
B
. We treat separately the cases
\sigma=0
,
\sigma<0
and
\sigma>0

and
\gamma+2\sigma\neq 0

\gamma=0

III.3
Stability of axial perturbations

We now discuss the stability of the axial perturbations, using arguments similar to those given in
Kodama:2003kk
(see also
Ganguly:2017ort
;
Kimura:2018whv
;
Takahashi:2019oxz
). Stability can be shown by using the property that the Schrödinger-like operator is a positive self-adjoint operator in the space of square integrable functions, which implies that there is no
normalisable unstable mode.

One way to show stability is to show that
\omega^{2}>0
for any square integrable function
\chi
in the domain of the Schrödinger-like equation (
17
).

Multiplying the Schrödinger equation (
17
) by
\overline{\chi}
, one obtains

\displaystyle I_{\omega}\equiv\omega^{2}\,\int_{r_{*{\rm min}}}^{+\infty}\differential r_{*}\,|\chi|^{2}=\int_{r_{*{\rm min}}}^{+\infty}\differential r_{*}\left[-\overline{\chi}\,\partial_{*}^{2}\chi+(S^{2}-\partial_{*}S+V_{\lambda})|\chi|^{2}\right]\,,

(46)

where
\partial_{*}
denotes a derivative with respect to
r_{*}
and where we have used the decomposition (
20
) of the potential.
By introducing the derivative operator
D
defined by

\displaystyle D\chi\equiv\partial_{*}\chi+S\,\chi\,,

(47)

we can rewrite the above integral in the convenient form

I_{\omega}=\int_{r_{*{\rm min}}}^{+\infty}\differential r_{*}\left(|D\chi|^{2}+V_{\lambda}|\chi|^{2}\right)-[\overline{\chi}\partial_{*}\chi+S|\chi|^{2}]_{r_{*{\rm min}}}^{+\infty}\,.

(48)

If the right-hand side can be shown to be positive then this implies
\omega^{2}>0
and therefore the stability of the mode.
Let us therefore analyse the boundary terms.

First of all, since the effective metric is supposed to be asymptotically flat, the boundary term for
r_{*}\rightarrow\infty
vanishes and the only remaining
boundary term is

\displaystyle\lim_{r_{*}\rightarrow r_{*{\rm min}}}\left(\overline{\chi}\partial_{*}\chi+S|\chi|^{2}\right)\,.

(49)

When
\sigma\leq 0
, the singularity is sent to infinity, i.e.
r_{*{\rm min}}=-\infty
. Using the explicit expression of
S
,

S=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial_{*}C}{C}=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{AB}\,\frac{C^{\prime}}{C}\,,

(50)

and substituting the behaviour of the effective metric functions near the singularity, one finds the following behaviours for
S
, depending on the value of
\gamma
:

\displaystyle\text{if}\,\gamma\neq 0\,,\;\;S\simeq\frac{\gamma}{2{\rho}}\varepsilon^{-\sigma}\,,\qquad\text{if}\,\gamma=0\,,\;\;S\simeq\frac{c_{1}}{2{\rho}}\varepsilon^{1-\sigma}\,,

(51)

where
c_{1}
is introduced in (
95
). Hence,
S
is bounded (it even goes to zero when
\sigma<0
) and the boundary term (
49
) vanishes, which implies
the stability of axial perturbations.

When
\sigma>0
, the situation is different because the domain reduces to the real half-line, with the singularity located at
{\rho}=0
. In this case, the function
S
diverges at the singularity, according to

\displaystyle S\simeq\frac{\gamma}{2\sigma r_{*}}\,,

(52)

where
\gamma
is supposed not to vanish. To go further, we need to distinguish between the cases
\xi>2\sigma
and
\xi\leq 2\sigma
(
38
). In the former case (which corresponds to an essentially self-adjoint Schrödinger operator), we showed that the solution of the Schrödinger equation behaves as

\displaystyle\chi(r_{*})\sim\frac{1}{r_{*}^{\nu/4}}\,\exp\left[\frac{2}{\nu+2}r_{*}^{1+\nu/2}\right]\,,

(53)

where
\nu+2<0
, and then we restrict the functions in (
46
) to behave exactly as the solutions at the singularity. As a consequence, the boundary term in (
46
) vanishes and axial perturbations are stable.

In the latter case (
\xi\leq 2\sigma
), we need to treat separately the cases where the Schrödinger operator is essentially self-adjoint and where it is not. When it is essentially self-adjoint, i.e.
V_{*s}\geq 3/4
, the asymptotic behaviour of the solution is given by a power law

\displaystyle\chi(r_{*})\sim\,r_{*}^{n}\,,\qquad n=\frac{1+\sqrt{1+4V_{*s}}}{2}\,,

(54)

where
n\geq 3/2
. Therefore, the boundary term vanishes and axial perturbations are stable.

When
V_{*s}<3/4
, the situation is different as the Schrödinger operator is not essentially self-adjoint and boundary conditions at the singularity need to be prescribed. Indeed, any solution behaves as follows at the singularity,

\displaystyle\chi(r_{*})\,\simeq\,\alpha_{+}r_{*}^{n_{+}}+\alpha_{-}r_{*}^{n_{-}}\,,\qquad n_{\pm}=\frac{1\pm\sqrt{1+4V_{*s}}}{2}\,,

(55)

where
\alpha_{\pm}
are constants. We immediately show that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\leq\real(n_{+})<\frac{3}{2}\,,\qquad-\frac{1}{2}\leq\real(n_{-})<\frac{1}{2}\,,

(56)

which confirms that both solutions are square integrable at the singularity. If we choose the boundary condition such that

\alpha_{+}=0
, then the boundary term diverges at the singularity and then we cannot conclude that axial perturbations are stable
using the properties of the integral (
46
). On the contrary, if we choose
\alpha_{-}=0
, the boundary term does not
diverge anymore and vanishes when
V_{*s}\neq 0
which may be the sign of stability.

V
Conclusions

In this work, we have studied the axial perturbations of non-spinning black holes in DHOST theories, the most general family of scalar-tensor theories with a single scalar degree of freedom. Although the family of DHOST theories is huge, the dynamics of axial perturbations can be encapsulated in a few functions that depend on the background metric and on the DHOST functions evaluated on the background. Moreover, there is a correspondence between the dynamics of axial perturbations in DHOST theories and that of axial perturbations in GR in a different metric, which we call the effective metric. Since we assume that all ordinary fields, like the electromagnetic field, are minimally coupled to the background metric, this implies that axial gravitational waves propagate (in the ordinary sense) in a different metric compared with other fields.

This effective metric depends explicitly on the choice of the DHOST theory, as well as on the particular background BH solution. Depending on the particular case at hand, the effective metric can either describe another BH geometry, with the same or a different horizon, or a naked singularity. Specific examples of all these possibilities were presented in
Langlois:2022ulw

and are recalled in Section
IV
.

We have studied the effective metric from a general point of view, by assuming a generic power-law behaviour of the effective metric coefficients near the singularity, be it a curvature or a coordinate singularity. Given our ansatz characterized by a few exponents, we have classified all possible behaviours of the effective tortoise coordinate and of the potential for the effective Schrödinger-like equation. This has enabled us to discuss, in a generic way, the self-adjointness status of the Schrödinger-like operator and the stability of the perturbations.

Whereas most cases lead to self-adjoint operators, we have nevertheless identified a small region of our parameter space where the operator fails to be self-adjoint. Interestingly, one of our examples turns out to be in this particular region: this is the case of the 4D Einstein-Gauss-Bonnet spacetime. This means that, in this particular case, the time evolution of the axial perturbations seems ambiguously defined, requiring additional information.

In future work, we would like to extend our analysis to polar perturbations. The situation is however more delicate since the polar perturbations now include an additional mode, that of the scalar field.

\displaystyle\Psi^{2}+\frac{{\cal F}}{\mathfrak{A}\mathfrak{B}}\left[\mathfrak{A}F_{2}+\mathfrak{A}YF_{3X}+q^{2}(A_{1}+YB_{6}+\Box\phi(B_{2}+\frac{3}{2}B_{3})+\frac{3}{2}\mathfrak{B}\phi_{rr}B_{3})-\frac{3}{2}q\mathfrak{B}\psi^{\prime}\phi_{rt}\right]\,,

where we have introduced the notation

\displaystyle Y\equiv\phi^{\mu}\phi_{\mu\nu}\phi^{\nu}\,,

(78)

and used the explicit expressions of the non-trivial second derivatives of the scalar field, namely
\phi_{\mu}^{\nu}=g^{\nu\rho}\phi_{\mu\rho}
,

\displaystyle\phi_{t}^{t}\,=\,\frac{1}{2}\mathfrak{B}\frac{\mathfrak{A}^{\prime}}{\mathfrak{A}}\,,\quad\phi_{r}^{r}\,=\,{\mathfrak{B}\psi^{\prime\prime}}-\frac{1}{2}\mathfrak{B}^{\prime}\psi^{\prime}\,,\quad\phi_{\theta}^{\theta}=\phi_{\varphi}^{\varphi}=\frac{1}{2}\mathfrak{B}\frac{\mathfrak{C}^{\prime}}{\mathfrak{C}}\psi^{\prime}\,,\quad\phi_{r}^{t}=\frac{1}{2}q\frac{\mathfrak{A}^{\prime}}{\mathfrak{A}}\,,

(79)

and the identities

\displaystyle\Box\phi=\phi_{t}^{t}+\phi_{r}^{r}+2\phi_{\theta}^{\theta}\,,\quad\phi^{\mu}X_{\mu}=\mathfrak{B}\psi^{\prime}X^{\prime}\,,\quad X^{\prime}=\frac{2}{\mathfrak{A}}\left(-q\phi_{tr}+\mathfrak{A}\mathfrak{B}\psi^{\prime}\phi_{rr}\right)\,.

(80)

Remarkably, the DHOST dynamics of the axial perturbations in the background metric is equivalent to the GR dynamics in the effective metric

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}\differential x^{\mu}\differential x^{\nu}\;=\;\Lambda\left[-{\cal F}\,\differential t^{2}\,+\,N\,\differential r^{2}\,+\,2P\,\differential r\differential t\,+\,M\,\mathfrak{C}\,(\differential\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta\,\differential\varphi^{2})\right]\,,

(81)

where we have introduced

\displaystyle\Lambda=\Phi\sqrt{\frac{\mathfrak{B}}{\mathfrak{A}}\Gamma}\,,\quad N={\cal F}(\Gamma-\Psi^{2})\,,\quad P={\cal F}\Psi\,,\quad M={\cal F}/\Phi\,.

(82)

From (
81
) and (
82
), we immediately deduce the coefficients of the effective metric (
7
)

\displaystyle A=-\Lambda{\cal F}\,,\qquad\frac{1}{B}=N+\frac{P^{2}}{\cal F}\,,\qquad C=\Lambda M\mathfrak{C}\,,

(83)

and the new time coordinate
t_{*}
from the relation

\displaystyle\differential t_{*}=\differential t-\Psi(r)\,\differential r\,.

(84)

A.2
A “more covariant” formulation

We see that the effective metric involves second derivatives of the scalar field when one considers cubic higher order scalar-tensor theories. This suggests that we can try to reformulate
g_{\mu\nu}
in the following form,

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}=\Lambda\left[\Omega\,\mathfrak{g}_{\mu\nu}\,+\,D\,\phi_{\mu}\phi_{\nu}\,+\,S\,\phi_{\mu\nu}+\frac{T}{2}\left(\phi_{\mu}X_{\nu}+\phi_{\nu}X_{\mu}\right)\right]\,,

(85)

for some “covariant” functions
\Omega
,
D
,
S
and
T
to be determined (in terms of covariant quantities only).
This is possible if these functions satisfy the relations

\displaystyle{\cal F}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Omega\mathfrak{A}-q^{2}D-S\phi_{tt}\,\,,

(86)

\displaystyle N

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\Omega}{\mathfrak{B}}+\psi^{\prime 2}D+S\phi_{rr}+2T\psi^{\prime}\phi^{\alpha}\phi_{\alpha r}\,,

(87)

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q\psi^{\prime}D+S\phi_{rt}+qT\phi^{\alpha}\phi_{\alpha r}\,,

(88)

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Omega+\frac{S}{C}\phi_{\theta\theta}\,.

(89)

Let us underline that these relations impose more than 4 equations for the 4 unknowns as we require that the unknowns take a covariant form as explained above.

The last equation (
89
) together with the expression of
M
immediately lead to a solution for
\Omega
and
S
:

\displaystyle\Omega

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F_{2}-X[A_{1}+\Box\Phi\,(B_{2}+\frac{2}{3}B_{3})+Y\,B_{6}]-Y\,(F_{3X}-\frac{3}{2}B_{3})\,,

(90)

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{3}{2}XB_{3}\,,

(91)

where we recall that
Y
has been defined in (
78
).
Then, the first equation (
86
) enables us to find the expression of D,

\displaystyle D=A_{1}+\Box\Phi\,(B_{2}+\frac{2}{3}B_{3})+Y\,B_{6}\,,

(92)

while the second one (
87
) leads to

\displaystyle T=F_{3X}-\frac{3}{2}B_{3}\,.

(93)

Finally, one shows that the third equation (
88
) is consistent with the expressions above. As a consequence, we obtain
the desired form of the effective metric with (
10
).

Appendix B
Asymptotic behaviour of the potential at the singularity

In this appendix, we give more details on the analysis of the asymptotic behaviour of the potential near the singularity.
For that, it is convenient to first decompose the potential into,

\displaystyle V=V_{\lambda}+V_{0}\,\qquad\text{with}\qquad V_{\lambda}=2\lambda\frac{A}{C}\,,\qquad V_{0}=S^{2}-\partial_{*}S\,,

(94)

and then study the behaviours of
V_{\lambda}
and
V_{0}
separately.

B.1
Generalities

As we are going to see, we need to expand the effective metric coefficients, introduced in (
7
), up to sub-leading orders as follows

\displaystyle A(r)\,\simeq\,a\,\varepsilon^{\alpha}\left(1+a_{1}\varepsilon\right)\,,\qquad B(r)\,\simeq\,b\,\varepsilon^{\beta}\left(1+b_{1}\varepsilon\right)\,,\qquad C(r)\,\simeq\,c\,\varepsilon^{\gamma}\left(1+c_{1}\varepsilon\right)\,,

(95)

where (
\alpha
,
\beta
,
\gamma
) and (
a_{1}
,
b_{1}
,
c_{1}
) are real constants, while (
a
,
b
,
c
) are positive real numbers.
We will also use the notations introduced in (
25
),

\displaystyle\kappa^{2}\,=\,\frac{c}{a}\,,\qquad{\rho}\,=\,\frac{{r}_{\!s}}{\sqrt{ab}}\,,\qquad\sigma\,=\,1-\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}\,,\qquad\xi\,=\,\gamma-\alpha\,.

(96)

The leading order term in the expansion of
V_{\lambda}
in powers of
\varepsilon
is straightforward to obtain, it does not depend on

(a_{1},b_{1},c_{1})
and is given by

\displaystyle V_{\lambda}\simeq 2\frac{\lambda}{\kappa^{2}}\varepsilon^{-\xi}\,.

(97)

The calculation of the leading order term in the expansion of
V_{0}
is subtler.
Indeed, after a direct calculation, one obtains,

\displaystyle V_{0}\,\simeq\,V_{0}^{(0)}\varepsilon^{-2\sigma}+V_{0}^{(1)}\varepsilon^{-2\sigma+1}\,,

(98)

with

\displaystyle V_{0}^{(0)}\,=\,\frac{\gamma(\gamma+2\sigma)}{4{\rho}^{2}}\,,\quad V_{0}^{(1)}=\frac{1}{4{\rho}^{2}}\quantity[(a_1 + b_1)(\gamma+ 2\sigma- 1) \gamma+ 2 c_1 (\gamma+ \sigma- 1)]\,.

(99)

We see that
V_{0}^{(0)}
vanishes when
\gamma=0
or
\gamma+2\sigma=0
and in that case we have to consider the term proportional to
V_{0}^{(1)}
. Therefore, we will distinguish between the cases
\gamma\neq 0
and
\gamma=0
.

In any case, the leading order term in the expansion of the potential in powers of
\varepsilon
is

\displaystyle V\;\simeq\;V_{s}\,\varepsilon^{\eta}\,,

(100)

where the expressions of
V_{s}
and
\eta
depends on the parameters entering in the metric near the singularity.

B.2
The case
\gamma\neq 0

When
\gamma\neq 0
(and also
\gamma+2\sigma\neq 0
), the expressions of
V_{s}
and
\eta
depend on the sign of
\xi-2\sigma
according to:

\displaystyle\text{if}\,\,\xi>2\sigma\quad({\rm i.e.}\ \gamma>2-\beta)\,,\qquad

\displaystyle V_{s}\,=\,2\frac{\lambda}{\kappa^{2}}\,,\quad\eta=-\xi\,,

(101)

\displaystyle\text{if}\,\,\xi\leq 2\sigma\quad({\rm i.e.}\ \gamma\leq 2-\beta)\,,\qquad

\displaystyle V_{s}\,=\,\frac{\gamma(\gamma+2\sigma)}{4{\rho}^{2}}+2\frac{\lambda}{\kappa^{2}}\delta_{\xi-2\sigma},,\quad\eta=-2\sigma\,,

(102)

where we introduced the Kronecker symbol
\delta_{\xi}
which satisfies
\delta_{\xi}=0
if
\xi\neq 0
and
\delta_{0}=1
.

In order to express the asymptotic behaviour of the potential in terms of the tortoise coordinate
r_{*}
, we must distinguish between the
cases
\sigma=0
and
\sigma\neq 0
.

When
\sigma=0
, we find, using (
27
),

V\,\simeq\,V_{s}\,\exp\left(\eta\frac{r_{*}}{{\rho}}\right)\,,\qquad(\sigma=0)\,.

(103)

where
V_{s}
and
\eta
have been given above.
The singularity is located at
r_{*}\rightarrow-\infty
and

\displaystyle\bullet\;\;\text{if}\,\,\xi>0\,,\qquad V(r_{*})\simeq 2\frac{\lambda}{\kappa^{2}}\exp\left(-\xi\frac{r_{*}}{{\rho}}\right)\,,

(104)

\displaystyle\bullet\;\;\text{if}\,\,\xi\leq 0\,,\qquad V(r_{*})\simeq 2\frac{\lambda}{\kappa^{2}}\,\delta_{\xi}+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{4{\rho}^{2}}\,.

(105)

When
\sigma\neq 0
, we have

V\,\simeq\,V_{*s}\,|r_{*}|^{\nu}\,,\qquad V_{*s}=V_{s}\left(\frac{|\sigma|}{{\rho}}\right)^{\nu}\,,\qquad\nu=\frac{\eta}{\sigma}\,,\qquad(\sigma\neq 0)\,.

(106)

The singularity is located at
r_{*}\rightarrow-\infty
if
\sigma<0
, at
r_{*}=0
if
\sigma>0
, and the parameters
are given by

\displaystyle\bullet\;\;\text{if}\,\,\xi>2\sigma\,,\qquad\nu=-\frac{\xi}{\sigma}\,,\quad V_{s}=2\frac{\lambda}{\kappa^{2}}\,,

(107)

\displaystyle\bullet\;\;\text{if}\,\,\xi\leq 2\sigma\,,\qquad\nu=-2\,,\quad V_{s}=2\frac{\lambda}{\kappa^{2}}\delta_{\xi-2\sigma}+\frac{\gamma(\gamma+2\sigma)}{4{\rho}^{2}}\,.

(108)

B.3
The cases
\gamma=0
and
\gamma+2\sigma=0

We proceed as in the previous section but now we compare the two terms

V_{\lambda}\simeq 2\frac{\lambda}{\kappa^{2}}\varepsilon^{-\xi}\quad{\rm and}\quad V_{0}\simeq V_{0}^{(1)}\varepsilon^{-2\sigma+1}

(109)

with

V_{0}^{(1)}=\frac{(\sigma-1)c_{1}}{2{\rho}^{2}}\ (\gamma=0)\quad{\rm or}\quad V_{0}^{(1)}=\frac{\sigma(a_{1}+b_{1}-c_{1})-c_{1}}{2{\rho}^{2}}\ (\gamma=-2\sigma)\,.

(110)

We assume that
\sigma
is such that the corresponding expression above does not vanish. Otherwise we should consider the next term in the expansion of
V_{0}
and the computation would then be similar.

Following the same analysis as before, we still have the behaviour (
100
) for the potential with different expressions of
V_{s}
and
\eta
,

\displaystyle\bullet\;\;\text{if}\,\,\xi>2\sigma-1\,,\quad V_{s}\,=\,2\frac{\lambda}{\kappa^{2}}\,,\quad\eta=-\xi\,,

(111)

\displaystyle\bullet\;\;\text{if}\,\,\xi\leq 2\sigma-1\,,\quad V_{s}\,=\,V_{0}^{(1)}+2\frac{\lambda}{\kappa^{2}}\delta_{\beta+\gamma-1}\,,\quad\eta=-2\sigma+1\,.

(112)

We now express the results in terms of
r_{*}
. The potential takes the same form as in (
103
) and (
106
) with
different parameters.

When
\sigma=0
, the singularity is located at
r_{*}\rightarrow-\infty
and

\displaystyle\bullet\;\;\text{if}\,\,\xi>-1\,,\qquad V(r_{*})\simeq 2\frac{\lambda}{\kappa^{2}}\exp\left(-\xi\frac{r_{*}}{{\rho}}\right)\,,

(113)

\displaystyle\bullet\;\;\text{if}\,\,\xi\leq-1\,,\qquad V(r_{*})\simeq\left(V_{0}^{(1)}+2\frac{\lambda}{\kappa^{2}}\delta_{\xi+1}\right)\exp\left(\frac{r_{*}}{{\rho}}\right)\,.

(114)

Notice that the Schwarzschild metric falls in this case and more precisely it corresponds to
\gamma=0
,
\sigma=0
(as
\alpha=\beta=1
)
and
\xi=-\alpha=-1
. Therefore, we recover the well-known exponential fall-off of the potential.

When
\sigma\neq 0
, we have,

\displaystyle\bullet\;\;\text{if}\,\,\xi>2\sigma-1\,,\qquad\nu=-\frac{\xi}{\sigma}\,,\quad V_{s}=2\frac{\lambda}{\kappa^{2}}\,,

(115)

\displaystyle\bullet\;\;\text{if}\,\,\xi\leq 2\sigma-1\,,\qquad\nu=-2+\frac{1}{\sigma}\,,\quad V_{s}=2\frac{\lambda}{\kappa^{2}}\delta_{\beta+\gamma-1}+V_{0}^{(1)}\,.

(116)

The main differences with the case
\gamma(\gamma+2\sigma)\neq 0
are the existence of new behaviours of the potential which correspond to,

•

\sigma=0
and
-1<\xi<0
where the potential decreases exponentially when
r_{*}\rightarrow-\infty
;

•

\sigma<0
where
\nu\geq-2+1/\sigma
whereas
\nu\geq-2
in the previous case, which means the possibility for the potential
to tend to zero faster than
1/r_{*}^{2}
when
r_{*}\rightarrow-\infty
;

•

\sigma>0
where
\nu\leq-2+1/\sigma
whereas
\nu\leq-2
in the previous case, which means the possibility for the
potential
V(r_{*})\simeq V_{*s}\,r_{*}^{\nu}
to tend to zero at the singularity.

Appendix C
Asymptotic behaviour of the solutions and self-adjointness of the Schrödinger operator

In this section, we summarise some of the results given in the chapter 2 of the book
berezin1991one
concerning the
asymptotic behaviour of the solutions of the Schrödinger equation. Then, we apply these general results to the particular cases we are interested in.

C.1
Asymptotic behaviour of the solution

Let us consider the one-dimensional Schrödinger equation for the wave function
\chi(x)
,

\displaystyle-\frac{\differential^{2}\chi}{\differential x^{2}}+V(x)\chi=\omega^{2}\chi\,,

(117)

where we use the variable
x
instead of
r_{*}
(to lighten the notations).

The authors of the book
berezin1991one
state a theorem (Theorem 4.6. in Chap. 2) where they give the asymptotic behaviour when

x\rightarrow+\infty
of the two solutions of the Schrödinger equation depending on the behaviour of the potential itself at infinity.

Let us start with the case where
|V(x)|\rightarrow\infty
. It is stated
berezin1991one
that, if the following two integrals

\displaystyle\int_{x_{0}}^{\infty}\differential x\,\frac{|V^{\prime}(x)|^{2}}{|V(x)|^{5/2}}\,,\qquad\int_{x_{0}}^{\infty}\differential x\,\frac{|V^{\prime\prime}(x)|}{|V(x)|^{3/2}}\,,

(118)

are convergent for
x_{0}
arbitrary large, then there exist a pair
\chi_{\pm}(x)
of two independent solutions of the
Schrödinger equation whose asymptotic behaviours at infinity are given by,

\displaystyle\chi_{\pm}(x)\simeq\frac{1}{|V(x)|^{1/4}}\exp\left[\pm\epsilon\int\differential x\,\sqrt{|V(x)-\omega^{2}|}\right]\,,

(119)

where
\epsilon=1
if
V(x)\rightarrow+\infty
and
\epsilon=i
if
V(x)\rightarrow-\infty
.

The case where
V(x)\rightarrow 0
is different. It is stated
berezin1991one
that, if the following two integrals

\displaystyle\int_{x_{0}}^{\infty}\differential x\,{|V^{\prime}(x)|^{2}}\,,\qquad\int_{x_{0}}^{\infty}\differential x\,{|V^{\prime\prime}(x)|}\,,

(120)

are convergent for
x_{0}
arbitrary large, then there exist a pair
\chi_{\pm}(x)
of two independent solutions of the
Schrödinger equation whose asymptotic behaviours at infinity are now given by,

\displaystyle\chi_{\pm}(x)\simeq\exp\left[\pm i\,\omega\int\differential x\,\sqrt{1-V(x)/\omega^{2}}\right]\,.

(121)

C.2
Application to some potentials

When, we study the dynamics of axial perturbations, we find Schrödinger equivations associated with potentials which are equivalent (when
x\rightarrow+\infty
) to one of the following,

\displaystyle V(x)=V_{0}\exp(\lambda x)\qquad\text{or}\qquad V(x)=V_{0}\,x^{\nu}\,.

(122)

C.2.1
Exponential potentials

When the potential is exponential, we have two cases to consider. The first one is
\lambda>0
and
V_{0}>0
where
V(x)\rightarrow+\infty
.
We immediately check the conditions (
118
) are satisfied and therefore the two independent solutions
\chi_{\pm}
(
119
) are equivalent to

\displaystyle\chi_{\pm}(x)\simeq\exp\left[-\frac{\lambda}{4}x\pm\frac{2\sqrt{V_{0}}}{\lambda}\exp\left(\frac{\lambda}{2}x\right)\right]\,.

(123)

The second case is
\lambda<0
with no conditions on
V_{0}>0
, hence
V(x)\rightarrow 0
. The conditions (
120
) are satisfied
and then the two independent solutions
\chi_{\pm}
(
121
) are equivalent to

\displaystyle\chi_{\pm}(x)\simeq\exp(\pm i \, \omega x)\,.

(124)

C.2.2
Power law potentials

When the potential is a power law, we also encounter two cases. The first one corresponds to
\nu>0
and
V_{0}>0
where
V(x)\rightarrow+\infty
. The conditions (
118
) are satisfied and therefore the two independent solutions
\chi_{\pm}
(
119
) are equivalent to

\displaystyle\chi_{\pm}(x)\simeq\,x^{-\nu/4}\,\exp\left[\pm\frac{2\sqrt{V_{0}}}{\nu+2}\,x^{1+\nu/2}\right]\,.

(125)

The second case corresponds to
\nu<0
while the sign of
V_{0}
is arbitrary, hence
V(x)\rightarrow 0
. The conditions (
118
) are satisfied and we obtain

\displaystyle\chi_{\pm}(x)\simeq\exp(\pm i \, \omega x)\,.

(126)

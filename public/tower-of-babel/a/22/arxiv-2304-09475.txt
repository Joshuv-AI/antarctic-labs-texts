1.2.
Lefschetz decomposition of the exceptional divisor and weakly categorical crepant resolutions

In
[
2
]
, Kuznetsov introduced the notion of Lefschetz decomposition:

Definition 1.5

(Definition 4.1 of
[
2
]
)
.

Let
X
be an algebraic variety with a line bundle
\mathcal{L}
on
X
. A Lefschetz decomposition of the derived category
D^{b}(X)
is a semi-orthogonal decomposition of
D^{b}(X)
of the form

\displaystyle D^{b}(X)=<A_{0},A_{1}\otimes\mathcal{L},\cdots,\mathcal{A}_{m-1}\otimes\mathcal{L}^{m-1}>

\displaystyle 0\subset A_{m-1}\subset A_{m-2}\subset\cdots\subset A_{1}\subset A_{0}\subset D^{b}(X)

such that
0\subset A_{m-1}\subset A_{m-2}\subset\cdots\subset A_{1}\subset A_{0}\subset D^{b}(X)
is a chain of admissible sub-categories of
D^{b}(X)
. Lefschetz decomposition is called rectangular if
A_{m-1}=\cdots=A_{1}=A_{0}
.

Similarly, a dual Lefschetz decomposition of
D^{b}(X)
is a semi-orthogonal decomposition of the form

D^{b}(X)=<B_{m-1}\otimes\mathcal{L}^{1-m},\cdots,B_{1}\otimes\mathcal{L}^{-1},B_{0}>

where

0\subset B_{m-1}\subset\cdots\subset B_{1}\subset B_{0}\subset D^{b}(X).

From now on we assume the assumptions in
Theorem

1.3
hold.

Theorem 1.6
.

If
k_{i}<d_{i}
, the functor

is fully faithful for any

. Moreover, let

A_{1}^{i}=A_{2}^{i}=\cdots=A_{d_{i}-k_{i}-1}^{i}:=Lpr_{i}^{*}(D^{b}(X_{i}))

and

be the left orthogonal of

<A_{1}^{i}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}_{X_{i}(c_{i})}}(1),\cdots A_{d_{i}-k_{i}-1}^{i}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}_{X_{i}(c_{i})}}(d_{i}-k_{i}-1)>

.

Similarly, let

B_{d_{i}-k_{i}-1}^{i}\cong\cdots\cong B_{1}^{i}:=Lpr_{i}^{*}(D^{b}(X_{i}))

<B_{d_{i}-k_{i}-1}^{i}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}_{X_{i}(c_{i})}}(1+k_{i}-d_{i}),\cdots,B_{1}^{i}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}_{X_{i}(c_{i})}}(-1)>

In
[
3
]
, Kuznetsov introduced the notion of categorical resolutions of singularities:

Definition 1.7

(Definition 3.1 of
[
3
]
)
.

A triangulated category
D
is regular if it is equivalent to an admissible subcategory of the bounded derived category of a smooth variety.

Given a triangulated category
D
, we denote
D^{perf}
as the full subcategory of perfect objects in
D
.

Definition 1.8

(Categorical resolution, Definition 3.2 of
[
3
]
)
.

A categorical resolution of a triangulated category
D
is a regular triangulated category
\tilde{D}
and a pair of functors

\pi_{*}:\tilde{D}\to D,\pi^{*}:D^{perf}\to\tilde{D},

such that

(1)

\pi^{*}
is left adjoint to
\pi_{*}
on
D^{perf}
, that is

Hom_{\tilde{D}}(\pi^{*}F,G)\cong Hom_{D}(F,\pi^{*}G)

for any
F\in D^{perf}
,
G\in\tilde{D}
,

(2)

the natural morphism of functors
id_{D^{perf}}\to\pi_{*}\pi^{*}
is an isomorphism.

Definition 1.9

(Definition 3.4 of
[
3
]
)
.

A categorical resolution
(\tilde{D},\pi_{*},\pi^{*})
of

is weakly crepant if the functor
\pi^{*}
is right adjoint to
\pi_{*}
on
D^{perf}

Hom_{\tilde{D}}(G,\pi^{*}F)\cong Hom_{D}(\pi_{*}G,F)

for any

.

Remark 1.10
.

In contrast to the weakly crepant categorical resolution, Kuznetsov
[
3
]
also introduced the notion of strong crepant categorical resolution by requiring the relative Serre functor as identity.

In
[
3
]
, Kuznetsov revealed the relation between the Lefschetz decomposition of the exceptional divisor and weakly (and strongly) crepant categorical resolution of singularities. By applying his theorem (with a very mild generalization), we prove that

2.
Smoothness of the Strict Transform

Before proving
Theorem

1.3
, we first prove the following lemma:

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
X
be a smooth variety with pairwise disjoint effective smooth divisors
E_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq m
. Let
Y
be an effective divisor of
X
. If
Y-\cup_{i=1}^{m}E_{i}
is smooth and
Y\cap E_{i}
is a smooth effective divisor of
E_{i}
for each
i
, then
Y
is also smooth.

Proof.

By induction, we can reduce to the case that
m=1
. As the smoothness property is a local property, and preserved under the etale morphisms, we can assume that
X
is an open subvariety of the affine space
\mathbb{A}^{n}=Spec\ \mathbb{F}[x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n}]
, such that
E_{1}
is the zero locus of
x_{n}
and
Y
is the zero locus of

f=x_{n}^{l}g_{n}+x_{n}^{l-1}g_{l-1}+\cdots+g_{0}

where
g_{i}\in k[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1}]
. Then for any
p\in E_{1}
such that
f(p)=0
, we have

\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}(p)=\frac{\partial g_{0}}{\partial x_{i}}(p)

for any
1\leq i<n
. Hence the singular locus of
f
in
Y
is inside the singular locus of
g_{0}
in
Y\cap E_{1}
.
∎

Proof of
Theorem

1.3
.

As
\mathcal{O}(-Y)\subset\mathcal{I}_{i}^{k_{i}}
, by
[
5
]
,
\pi^{*}\mathcal{O}(Y)-\sum_{i=1}^{n}k_{i}E_{X_{i}}Z
is also an effective divisor of
X
, which we denote as
\mathcal{O}(\tilde{Y})
. Moreover, we have
E_{X_{i}}Z\cong\mathbb{P}_{X_{i}}(C_{i})
and
E_{X_{i}}Z\cap\tilde{Y}
is the zero locus of
s_{i}
. If all the zero locus of
s_{i}
are smooth effective divisors of
E_{X_{i}}Z
respectively, by
Lemma

2.1

\tilde{Y}
is smooth. Moreover, it is the schematic closure of
Y-X
in
Bl_{X}Z
, and thus is the strict transform of
Y
. The preimage of
X_{i}
in
\tilde{Y}
is the zero locus of
s_{i}
, and hence is the exceptional divisor
E_{X_{i}}Y
.
∎

1.4

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
X
be a smooth variety. Let

be a locally free sheaf over

L
be a line bundle over

. Let
\Phi

Proof.

Actually, it follows from linear Koszul duality

or the singularity category of projectivization of two-term complexes

1
]

As the smoothness of

X
as an open subvariety of
\mathbb{A}^{n}=Spec\ \mathbb{F}[x_{1},\cdots x_{n}]

and

for some positive integer

. Then
\Phi
is represented by

f=(f_{1},f_{2},\cdots f_{m})

where
f_{i}\in k[x_{1},\cdots x_{m}]
. We consider
\mathbb{A}^{n+m}=Spec\ \mathbb{F}[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n},y_{1},\cdots,y_{m}]
and

g:=\sum_{i=1}^{m}y_{i}f_{i}.

Here is a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
action on
\mathbb{A}^{n+m}
by

t\cdot(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n},y_{1},\cdots,y_{m})=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n},ty_{1},\cdots,ty_{m})

and we denote

as the zero locus of

. Then

Given

, we consider the deravative matrix with the

and the critical locus of

, which we denote as

has rank less than

is

. The singular locus of

is

\{(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n},y_{1},\cdots,y_{m})|x\in S,Jac(f)_{x}(y)=0\}

where

and

(and also

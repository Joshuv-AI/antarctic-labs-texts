Abstract
. Let Bun {\operatorname{Bun}} be the moduli stack of rank 2 2 bundles with fixed determinant on a smooth proper irreducible curve C C over a local field F F . We show how to associate with a Schwartz κ \kappa -density, for Re ⁡ ( κ ) ≥ 1 / 2 {\operatorname{Re}}(\kappa)\geq 1/2 , a smooth function on the corresponding coarse moduli space of very stable bundles. In the non-archimedean case we also prove that the stack Bun {\operatorname{Bun}} is κ \kappa -bounded in the sense of [ 4 , Def. 2.10] for any κ ∈ ℂ \kappa\in\C .



1.
Introduction

Let
C
be a smooth (connected) proper curve over a local field
F
and let
G
be a reductive group.

The
analytic Langlands correspondence
(that is still only partially understood)
is concerned with the study of certain Hecke operators on Schwartz vector spaces associated with the stack of
G
-bundles over
C
(see
[
10
,
11
,
12
]
,

[
4
]
and
[
5
]
). Conjecturally, these (commuting) operators extend to compact operators on the corresponding Hilbert space with
a discrete joint spectrum, which is expected to be related to some “Galois side” objects for the Langlands dual group
G^{\vee}
.

In this paper we study the Schwartz spaces
{\mathcal{S}}({\operatorname{Bun}}_{G},|\omega|^{\kappa})
of
\kappa
-densities (where
\kappa\in\C
) on the stack
{\operatorname{Bun}}_{G}
over
F
. In the non-archimedean case
the Schwartz spaces of algebraic stacks were introduced in
[
13
]
(see also
[
4
]
and
[
5
]
). In the archimedean case similar spaces were introduced in

[
15
]
. These Schwartz spaces are defined as inductive limits of the spaces
{\mathcal{S}}(X,|p^{*}\omega|^{\kappa})_{H(F)}
of coinvariants, where
[X/H]\hookrightarrow{\operatorname{Bun}}_{G}
is an open substack
with
X
a smooth variety,
p:X\to{\operatorname{Bun}}_{G}
is the corresponding map, and
H\simeq\operatorname{GL}_{N}
(in the archimedean case taking coinvariants involves taking the closure of
the subspace of elements of the form
hv-v
).

Recall that a
G
-bundle
P
on
C
is called
very stable
if there are no nonzero nilpotent Higgs fields
\phi\in H^{0}(C,P_{{\mathfrak{g}}})
where

P_{{\mathfrak{g}}}
is the vector bundle corresponding to
P
and the adjoint representation of
G
.
Let
M_{G}^{vs}\subset{\operatorname{Bun}}_{G}
denote the coarse moduli space of very stable bundles. Then
[
4

states in particular that for
\operatorname{Re}(\kappa)\geq 1/2
,
every Schwartz
\kappa
-density in
{\mathcal{S}}({\operatorname{Bun}}_{G},|\omega|^{\kappa})
defines in a natural way

\kappa
-density on
M_{G}^{vs}(F)

The main result of the present paper is a proof of this Conjecture in the case when
G={\operatorname{SL}}_{2}
in both archimedean and non-archimedean cases.

Namely, for each line bundle
L_{0}
on
C
, we consider the stacks
{\operatorname{Bun}}_{L_{0}}
of rank
2
bundles
E
with
\det(E)\simeq L_{0}
(for trivial
L_{0}
this is exactly

{\operatorname{Bun}}_{{\operatorname{SL}}_{2}}
), and the open DM-substack of very stable bundles
{\mathcal{M}}_{L_{0}}^{vs}\subset{\operatorname{Bun}}_{L_{0}}
, which is dense if
C
has genus
g\geq 2
.
The stack
{\mathcal{M}}_{L_{0}}^{vs}
is a quotient of a smooth variety
M_{L_{0}}^{vs}
by the trivial action of
\mu_{2}
(so
M_{L_{0}}^{vs}
is also the coarse moduli space of
{\mathcal{M}}^{vs}_{L_{0}}
).
We prove
that for
{\operatorname{Re}}(\kappa)\geq 1/2
, for finite type substacks
[X/G]\subset{\operatorname{Bun}}_{L_{0}}
(with
G=\operatorname{GL}_{N}
), the integrals of Schwartz
\kappa
-densities on
X(F)

over
F
-points of very stable

-orbits are absolutely convergent (away from a subset of orbits of measure zero) and define a map

where in the non-archimedean case the class

consists of locally constant sections.
More precisely, we consider push-forwards of Schwartz
\kappa
-densities on
X(F)
(restricted to the very stable locus) to

in the sense of distributions,
and prove that the obtained distributions are of class

(see Theorem
6.8

In addition, in the non-archimedean case we prove a certain boundedness result (announced in
[
4

6.9

{\mathcal{S}}({\operatorname{Bun}}_{L_{0}},|\omega|^{\kappa})

Note that in
[
10

the authors consider in the archimedean case
another Schwartz space of half-densities on
{\operatorname{Bun}}_{G}
defined using global (twisted) differential operators on
{\operatorname{Bun}}_{G}

Conjecturally their space should coincide with the space

2.
Schwartz spaces

2.1.
Schwartz spaces for varieties

Let
F
be a local field. We denote by
|\cdot|:F^{*}\to\C^{*}
the standard absolute value (so for
F=\C
we have
|z|=z\overline{z}
, and for a non-archimedean field,

|a|=q^{-v(a)}
, where
q
is the number of elements in the residue field).

For any smooth
F
-variety
X
, an (algebraic) line bundle
L
over
X
, and a continuous character
\chi:F^{*}\to\C^{*}
we have the corresponding
complex line bundle
L^{\chi}
over
X(F)
. In particular, for
\kappa\in\C
we denote by
|L|^{\kappa}
the complex line bundle corresponding
to the character
\chi(a)=|a|^{\kappa}
. Note that for a non-archimedean field
F
,
the topological space
X(F)
is an
l
-space
1
1

1

This means that it is locally compact, Hausdorff, and every point has a basis of compact open
neighborhoods.
, and the transition functions of
|L|^{\kappa}
are locally constant.

For example, if
\omega_{X}
is the algebraic line bundle of top forms on
X
then
|\omega_{X}|
is the line bundle of complex densities on
X(F)
.

Assume first that
F
is non-archimedean. Then we can define Schwartz spaces of line bundles on
X(F)
. More generally,
if
X
is an
l
-space and
L
is a complex line bundle on
X

(so the transition functions of
L
are are locally constant), we denote by
C^{\infty}(X,L)
the space of locally constant sections,
and define the Schwartz subspace
{\mathcal{S}}(X,L)\subset C^{\infty}(X,L)
as sections
with compact support. These definitions also make sense for arbitrary sheaves of
\C
-vector spaces (see
[
6
, Sec. 1.3]
and Sec.
3.2
below).

Now consider the archimedean case,
F=\C
or
{\mathbb{R}}
.
Recall that for any smooth variety
X
over
F
, Aizenbud and Gourevitch define in
[
1
]
the space
{\mathcal{S}}(X)
of Schwartz functions on
X(F)

(which agrees with the standard Schwartz space in the case
X={\mathbb{A}}^{n}
).
They also define the space of tempered functions
{\mathcal{T}}(X)
, which are smooth functions
f
such that for any affine open
U\subset X
and a polynomial differential
operator
D
on
U
, the function
D(f|_{U})
grows at most polynomially. An important property of tempered functions is that
for
f\in{\mathcal{T}}(X)
, one has
f\cdot{\mathcal{S}}(X)\subset{\mathcal{S}}(X)
.
Note that for any open embedding
U\hookrightarrow X
there is a natural linear embedding
{\mathcal{S}}(U)\to{\mathcal{S}}(X)
.

Now let
V
be an algebraic vector bundle over
X
,
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
are algebraic line bundles, and
\kappa\in\C
.
We consider the corresponding complex vector bundle

E_{\kappa}:=V\otimes_{F}|L_{1}|^{\kappa}\otimes|L_{2}|

over
X(F)
.
We denote by
C^{\infty}(X,E_{\kappa})
the space of smooth sections.

Similarly to
[
1
]
we can define the Schwartz subspace
{\mathcal{S}}(X,E_{\kappa})\subset C^{\infty}(X,E_{\kappa})
.
Note that
E_{\kappa}
is not a Nash bundle, so the definitions of
[
1
]
do not directly apply. However,
we can still mimic the definition of
[
1
]
by choosing a finite open covering
(U_{i})
such that
V|_{U_{i}}
,
L_{1}|_{U_{i}}
and
L_{2}|_{U_{i}}

are trivial and considering the linear span of the spaces of
C^{\infty}
-sections of
E_{\kappa}
coming from the Schwartz sections of the trivial bundles

E_{\kappa}|_{U_{i}}
over
U_{i}
(see Definition
2.2
below for a similar definition with holomorphic dependence on
\kappa
).
One can show that the usual properties still hold for the obtained Schwartz spaces
{\mathcal{S}}(X,E_{\kappa})
using tempered partitions of unity
(see
[
1
, Thm. 5.2.1]
) and the fact that the transition functions of
E_{\kappa}
are tempered.

2.2.
Schwartz sections with holomorphic dependence on a parameter

Let
X
be a smooth variety over
F
. We can define Schwartz functions on
X
depending holomorphically on
\kappa\in\C
.
Let
D\subset\C
be an open region.

Definition 2.1
.

We define
{\mathcal{S}}^{hol}(X\times D)
as the space of holomorphic functions on
D
with values in
{\mathcal{S}}(X)
. By definition, these are functions

f:D\to{\mathcal{S}}(X)
, such that for every continuous linear functional (any functional if
F
is non-archimedean)
\varphi:{\mathcal{S}}(X)\to\C
the function
\varphi\circ f
is holomorphic.

Note that if
f
is a tempered function on
X(F)
(locally constant in the non-archimedean case), then we have a well defined continuous operator on
{\mathcal{S}}^{hol}(X\times D)
given by

\varphi(x,\kappa)\mapsto f(x)\cdot\varphi(x,\kappa)
.

We can also talk about Schwartz sections of the trivial bundle over
X
, depending holomorphically on a parameter
\kappa\in D

(and this notion does not change under a tempered
change of trivializations).

It is known that in the archimedean case,

{\mathcal{S}}^{hol}(X\times D)\simeq{\mathcal{S}}(X)\hat{\otimes}{\mathcal{H}}(D),

(2.1)

where
{\mathcal{H}}(D)
is the space of holomorphic functions on
D
, and
\hat{\otimes}
is the topological tensor product of nuclear spaces
(see
[
14
, ch.II,§3, n.3, Ex.2,3]
).

In the non-archimedean case we have instead

{\mathcal{S}}^{hol}(X\times D)\simeq{\mathcal{S}}(X)\otimes{\mathcal{H}}(D).

Next, we want to consider Schwartz sections of nontrivial vector bundles. Assume first that
F={\mathbb{R}}
or
\C
.
As before, let
V
be an algebraic vector bundle over
X
,

L_{1}
and
L_{2}
are algebraic line bundles, and

E_{\kappa}:=V\otimes_{F}|L_{1}|^{\kappa}\otimes|L_{2}|,

where
\kappa\in\C
.

Then we can define Schwartz sections of
E_{\kappa}
.
depending holomorphically on
\kappa\in D
.

Definition 2.2
.

We define
{\mathcal{S}}^{hol}(X\times D,E_{\kappa})
as the space of smooth sections of
E_{\kappa}
over
X\times D
,
lying in the image of

\bigoplus_{i}{\mathcal{S}}^{hol}(U_{i}\times D,E_{\kappa}|_{U_{i}})\to C^{\infty}(X\times D,E_{\kappa})

for some open covering
(U_{i})
of
X
such that
V|_{U_{i}}
and
L_{1}|_{U_{i}}
,
L_{2}|_{U_{i}}
are trivial.

The existence of tempered partition of unity shows that this definition does not depend on a choice of an open covering.

If
F
is non-archimedean, we can give
an analogous definition for the Schwartz space
{\mathcal{S}}^{hol}(X\times D,|L_{1}|^{\kappa}\otimes|L_{2}|)
(i.e., for trivial
V
).

Note that if
U\subset X
is Zariski open then we have a natural embedding (extension by zero),

{\mathcal{S}}^{hol}(U\times D,E_{\kappa})\to{\mathcal{S}}^{hol}(X\times D,E_{\kappa}).

Also, for every
\kappa\in D
we have a continuous map

{\mathcal{S}}^{hol}(X\times D,E_{\kappa})\to{\mathcal{S}}^{hol}(X,E_{\kappa})

given by the evaluation at
\kappa
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
f:Y\to X
be a smooth morphism of algebraic varieties, surjective on
F
-points, and let
E^{\prime}_{\kappa}=f^{*}E_{\kappa}\otimes|\omega_{f}|
(in the nonarchimedean case
we assume
V
to be trivial).
Then for each
D
, the integration map

f_{*}:{\mathcal{S}}^{hol}(Y\times D,E^{\prime}_{\kappa})\to{\mathcal{S}}^{hol}(X\times D,E_{\kappa})

is surjective.

Proof.

Let us give a proof for archimedean
F
. The non-archimedean case is analogous but simpler.
By the partition of unity, the assertion is local in
X
, so we can assume that
E_{\kappa}
is trivial, and we just need to prove the surjectivity
of the map

f_{*}:{\mathcal{S}}^{hol}(Y\times D)\to{\mathcal{S}}^{hol}(X\times D).

But this follows from the surjectivity of
f_{*}:{\mathcal{S}}(Y)\to{\mathcal{S}}(X)
(see
[
3
, Thm. B.2.4]
) and from isomorphisms (
2.1
)
(using the fact that all the spaces involved are nuclear Frechet spaces).
∎

2.3.
Schwartz spaces for stacks and coinvariants

We consider admissible smooth algebraic stacks over
F
(see
[
5
, Sec. 2.2]
).
Recall that they are increasing unions of open substacks of the form
[X/G]
where
G
is a linear algebraic group over
F
acting on a smooth
F
-variety
X
.
The Schwartz spaces can still be defined for line bundles over such stacks. In the non-archimedean case this is done in
[
13
]

(see also
[
5
, Sec. 2.2]
). In the archimedean case this is due to Sakellaridis (see
[
15
]
).

In the case of a quotient stack
[X/G]
, where
G=\operatorname{GL}_{N}
, the Schwartz spaces
{\mathcal{S}}([X/G],|{\mathcal{L}}|^{\kappa})
can be computed as (algebraic) coinvariants of

{\mathcal{S}}(X,|L|^{\kappa}\otimes|{\bigwedge}^{top}({\mathfrak{g}})|^{-1})
with respect to the
G(F)
-action, where
L
is the pull-back of
{\mathcal{L}}
to
X
.
In the archimedean case, the definition of coinvariants
{\mathcal{S}}(X,|L|^{\kappa}\otimes|{\bigwedge}^{top}({\mathfrak{g}})|^{-1})_{G(F)}

is slightly different: one has to take the quotient by the closure of the span of elements of the form
gv-v
.
Note that we can pull out the factor
|{\bigwedge}^{top}({\mathfrak{g}})|^{-1}
when taking coinvariants since
G=\operatorname{GL}_{N}
acts trivially on it.

Similarly, for an open domain
D\subset\C
, we define
{\mathcal{S}}^{hol}([X/G]\times D,|{\mathcal{L}}|^{\kappa})
as appropriate coinvariants
of
{\mathcal{S}}(X\times D,|L|^{\kappa}\otimes|{\bigwedge}^{top}({\mathfrak{g}})|^{-1})
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Assume
G=\operatorname{GL}_{N}
and
F
is archimedean. Then there are natural surjective maps

{\mathcal{S}}(X,|L|^{\kappa})_{{\mathfrak{g}}}\to{\mathcal{S}}(X,|L|^{\kappa})_{G(F)},

{\mathcal{S}}^{hol}(X\times D,|L|^{\kappa})_{{\mathfrak{g}}}\to{\mathcal{S}}^{hol}(X\times D,|L|^{\kappa})_{G(F)},

where we use the usual algebraic coinvariants with respect to the Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
.

Proof.

This follows from the fact that
{\mathfrak{g}}
acts trivially on
{\mathcal{S}}(X,|L|^{\kappa})_{G(F)}
(resp.,
{\mathcal{S}}^{hol}(X\times D,|L|^{\kappa})_{G(F)}
).
∎

2.4.
Borel’s theorem with a holomorphic parameter

Here we assume that
F={\mathbb{R}}
or
\C
.
Let
X
be a smooth
F
-variety,
Z\subset X
a smooth closed subvariety, and let
N_{Z}
be the normal bundle to
Z
in
X
.
Let us consider the decreasing filtration on
{\mathcal{S}}^{hol}(X\times D,E_{\kappa})
defined as follows:

F_{Z}^{i}{\mathcal{S}}^{hol}(X\times D,E_{\kappa})

consists of
f(x,\kappa)
such that for any algebraic differential operator
D
of order
\leq i-1
defined
on an open
U\subset X
, one has
Df|_{U\cap Z}=0
.

Lemma 2.5
.

One has

F_{Z}^{i}{\mathcal{S}}^{hol}(X\times D,E_{\kappa})/F_{Z}^{i+1}{\mathcal{S}}^{hol}(X\times D,E_{\kappa})\simeq{\mathcal{S}}^{hol}(Z\times D,S^{i}N_{Z}^{\vee}\otimes E|_{Z}).

Proof.

It is clear that the natural map from the left-hand side to the right-hand side is injective. Thus, we need to prove surjectivity.
Using partition of unity and local étale coordinates, we can reduce to the case of a trivial bundle
E
and of an embedding
Z={\mathbb{A}}^{k}\subset{\mathbb{A}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{A}}^{k}=X
.
Let us partition coordinates on
X
as
(y,z)
where
z
are coordinates on
Z
. Then we can identify
{\mathcal{S}}^{hol}(Z\times D,S^{i}N_{Z}^{\vee})
with
functions
f(y,z,\kappa)
which are homogeneous polynomial of degree
i
in
y
, with coefficients that are in
{\mathcal{S}}^{hol}(Z\times D)
. But we can view such
f
as elements
of
F_{Z}^{i}{\mathcal{S}}^{hol}(X\times D,E_{\kappa})
.
∎

We need the following analog of Borel’s theorem.

Proposition 2.6
.

For each open subset
D\subset\C
let us consider the map

\tau_{D}:{\mathcal{S}}^{hol}(X\times D,E_{\kappa})/{\mathcal{S}}^{hol}((X-Z)\times D,E_{\kappa})\to\varprojlim{\mathcal{S}}^{hol}(X\times D,E_{\kappa})/F_{Z}^{i}{\mathcal{S}}^{hol}(X\times D,E_{\kappa}).

Then it is injective,
and for any open
D^{\prime}
such that
\overline{D^{\prime}}
is compact and is contained in
D
, the natural restriction map

\varprojlim{\mathcal{S}}^{hol}(X\times D,E_{\kappa})/F_{Z}^{i}{\mathcal{S}}^{hol}(X\times D,E_{\kappa})\to\varprojlim{\mathcal{S}}^{hol}(X\times D^{\prime},E_{\kappa})/F_{Z}^{i}{\mathcal{S}}^{hol}(X\times D^{\prime},E_{\kappa})

(2.2)

factors through the image of
\tau_{D^{\prime}}
.

Proof.

First, we claim that
{\mathcal{S}}^{hol}((X-Z)\times D,E_{\kappa})
is exactly the kernel of the map

\widetilde{\tau}_{D}:{\mathcal{S}}^{hol}(X\times D,E_{\kappa})\to\varprojlim{\mathcal{S}}^{hol}(X\times D,E_{\kappa})/F_{Z}^{i}{\mathcal{S}}^{hol}(X\times D,E_{\kappa}).

Indeed, this immediately follows from the similar statement for a fixed
\kappa
.

Thus, it remains to check that the map (
2.2
) factors through the image of
\widetilde{\tau}_{D^{\prime}}
.
Using partition of unity and local étale coordinates, we reduce to the case of trivial
E
and of an embedding
Z={\mathbb{A}}^{k}\subset{\mathbb{A}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{A}}^{k}=X
.
Then the element of
\varprojlim{\mathcal{S}}^{hol}(X\times D,E_{\kappa})/F_{Z}^{i}{\mathcal{S}}^{hol}(X\times D,E_{\kappa})

can be viewed as a formal series

f(y,z,\kappa)=\sum_{i\geq 0}f_{i}(y,z,\kappa),

where
f_{i}(y,z,\kappa)
is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
i
in
y
, with coefficients that are in
{\mathcal{S}}^{hol}(Z\times D)
.
We claim that there exists then
F(y,z,\kappa)\in{\mathcal{S}}^{hol}(X\times D^{\prime})
,
such that the formal expansion of
F
in
y
is equal to
f|_{D^{\prime}}
.
Indeed, this follows from the standard proof of Borel’s theorem.
∎

3.
Coinvariants for Schwartz sections: non-archimedean case

3.1.
Homology for smooth representations of
l
-groups

We use the setup of
l
-spaces and
l
-groups from
[
7
]
(see also
[
6
]
).

Let
G
be an
l
-group,
{\mathcal{H}}(G)
the Hecke algebra of locally constant compactly supported distributions on
G
,

{\mathcal{M}}(G)
the category of smooth representations of
G
. Recall that it is equivalent to the category
{\mathcal{M}}({\mathcal{H}}(G))
of nondegenerate left
{\mathcal{H}}(G)
-modules
(see
[
6
, Sec. 2.1]
).
It is known that this category has enough projectives (see
[
6
, Sec. 2.2]
).

Lemma 3.1
.

{\mathcal{H}}(G)
is projective as a left
{\mathcal{H}}(G)
-module.

Proof.

{\mathcal{H}}(G)
is the direct limit of the projective modules
{\mathcal{H}}(G)e_{K}
, where
e_{K}
is the idempotent corresponding to a compact open subgroup
K\subset G
.
Hence, for any
M\in{\mathcal{M}}(G)
we have

\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathcal{H}}(G)}({\mathcal{H}}(G),M)\simeq\varprojlim_{K}\operatorname{Hom}({\mathcal{H}}(G)e_{K},M)=\varprojlim_{K}e_{K}M,

where the maps
e_{K}M\to e_{K^{\prime}}M
for
K\subset K^{\prime}
are given by the multiplication with
e_{K^{\prime}}
. But these maps are surjective (since
e_{K^{\prime}}=e_{K^{\prime}}e_{K}
),
so for an exact sequence
0\to M_{1}\to M_{2}\to M_{3}\to 0
we get an exact sequence of inverse systems satisfying the Mittag-Leffler condition, which implies
that the functor
\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathcal{H}}(G)}({\mathcal{H}}(G),\cdot)
is exact.
∎

For a smooth
G
-representation
M
, we denote by
M_{G}
the space of
G
-coinvariants.
The integration defines a homomorphism
{\mathcal{H}}(G)\to\C
, and we view
\C
as a right
{\mathcal{H}}(G)
-module via this homomorphism.
It is easy to see that for
M\in{\mathcal{M}}(G)
there is a natural isomorphism

M_{G}\simeq\C\otimes_{{\mathcal{H}}(G)}M.

(3.1)

Lemma 3.11

Z_{y}\subset U(F)
, and
for every
x\in U_{0}(F)
mapping to
V_{y}
, one has
G(F)x\subset Z_{y}
.
Then the pair
(U_{0},X)
is
|\omega|^{\kappa}
-nice.

Proof.

It is enough to prove the assertion about distributions
\pi_{X,U_{0},\kappa}(\varphi)
restricted to
V_{y}
, i.e.,
when
\psi
is supported on
V_{y}
.
Set
W_{y}:=X(F)\setminus Z_{y}
.
Consider the open covering
X(F)=U(F)\cup W_{y}
. For every
\varphi\in{\mathcal{S}}(X(F),|\omega|^{\kappa})
, we can write

\varphi=\varphi_{U}+\varphi_{W_{y}},

where
\varphi_{U}\in{\mathcal{S}}(U(F),|\omega|^{\kappa})
and
\varphi_{W_{y}}\in{\mathcal{S}}(W_{y},|\omega|^{\kappa})
.
Now by our assumptions, the distribution

\pi_{X,U_{0},\kappa}(\varphi_{U})=\pi_{U,U_{0},\kappa}(\varphi_{U})

is well defined and of class
C^{\infty}
, while
\pi_{X,U_{0},\kappa}(\varphi_{W_{y}})|_{V_{y}}\equiv 0
.
This immediately implies the assertion.
∎

Application to
{\operatorname{Bun}}_{G}

6.1.
Filtration of the stack of rank
2
bundles

Let
C
be a curve of genus
g\geq 2
.
Let
{\operatorname{Bun}}_{L_{0}}
denote the stack of rank
2
bundles
E
on
C
, equipped with an isomorphism
\det(E)\simeq L_{0}
, where
L_{0}
is a fixed line bundle.
Morphisms in this stack are isomorphisms compatible with the identifications of the determinant bundles.
In particular, for
L_{0}={\mathcal{O}}
,
{\operatorname{Bun}}_{{\mathcal{O}}}
is the stack of
{\operatorname{SL}}_{2}
-bundles on
C
.

It is well known that each stack
{\operatorname{Bun}}_{L_{0}}
is admissible (but not of finite type).
We denote by
{\operatorname{Bun}}^{ss}_{L_{0}}\subset{\operatorname{Bun}}_{L_{0}}
the substack of semistable bundles.

Definition 6.1
.

For each
n
, we define an open substack
{\operatorname{Bun}}_{L_{0}}^{\leq n}\subset{\operatorname{Bun}}_{L_{0}}
consisting of bundles
E
such that any line subbundle
A\subset E
has
\deg(A)\leq n
.

The filtration
({\operatorname{Bun}}_{{\mathcal{O}}}^{\leq n})
plays an important role in the proof that the stack
{\operatorname{Bun}}_{{\operatorname{SL}}_{2}}
is truncatable in
[
9
, Sec. 6]
.
Note that
{\operatorname{Bun}}_{L_{0}}^{\leq\lfloor\deg(L_{0})/2\rfloor}
is exactly the substack of semistable bundles
{\operatorname{Bun}}^{ss}_{L_{0}}
.

Definition 6.2
.

For any
n
, we define
({\operatorname{Bun}}_{L_{0}}^{\leq n})^{0}\subset{\operatorname{Bun}}_{L_{0}}^{\leq n}
to be the substack consisting of
E
such that for any degree
n
line subbundle
A\subset E
one has

H^{1}(A^{2}L_{0}^{-1})=0
.

Lemma 6.3
.

(i) For
E\in{\operatorname{Bun}}_{L_{0}}^{\leq n}
, we have
E\in({\operatorname{Bun}}_{L_{0}}^{\leq n})^{0}
if and only if
for any degree
n
line bundle
A
with
H^{1}(A^{2}L_{0}^{-1})\neq 0
one has
\operatorname{Hom}(A,E)=0
.

(ii)
({\operatorname{Bun}}_{L_{0}}^{\leq n})^{0}
is an open substack of
{\operatorname{Bun}}_{L_{0}}^{\leq n}
.

(iii) Every
E\in({\operatorname{Bun}}_{L_{0}}^{\leq n})^{0}\setminus{\operatorname{Bun}}_{L_{0}}^{\leq n-1}
has form
E=A\oplus A^{-1}L_{0}
, where
\deg(A)=n
and

H^{1}(A^{2}L_{0}^{-1})=0
.

(iv)
({\operatorname{Bun}}_{L_{0}}^{\leq n})^{0}={\operatorname{Bun}}_{L_{0}}^{\leq n-1}
unless
n\geq(g-1+\deg(L_{0}))/2
.

(v)
({\operatorname{Bun}}_{L_{0}}^{\leq n})^{0}={\operatorname{Bun}}_{L_{0}}^{\leq n}
for
n>g-1+\deg(L_{0})/2
.

Proof.

(i) We just have to note that if
A\to E
is a nonzero morphism from a line bundle
A
of degree
n
then
A
is necessarily is a subbundle of
E
(otherwise, there would be a subbundle in
E
of degree
>n
).

(ii) The subvariety
Z\subset\operatorname{Pic}^{n}(C)
of
A
with
H^{1}(A^{2}L_{0}^{-1})\neq 0
is closed in
\operatorname{Pic}^{n}(C)
, hence proper. Therefore, the locus of
E
such that
\operatorname{Hom}(A,E)\neq 0

for some
A\in Z
is closed, and so its complement is open.

(iii) For such
E
there exists a line subbundle
A\subset E
such that
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(A^{-1}L_{0},A)\simeq H^{1}(A^{2}L_{0}^{-1})=0
, so the extension
0\to A\to E\to E/A\to 0
, where

E/A\simeq A^{-1}L_{0}
, splits.

(iv) If
H^{1}(A^{2}L_{0}^{-1})=0
then
\deg(A^{2}L_{0}^{-1})=2n-\deg(L_{0})\geq g-1
. Hence,
E
cannot have a line subbundle
A
of degree
n
with
H^{1}(A^{2}L_{0}^{-1})=0

unless
n\geq(g-1+\deg(L_{0}))/2
.

(v) This follows from the fact that if
\deg(A^{2}L_{0}^{-1})=2n-\deg(L_{0})>2g-2
then
H^{1}(A^{2}L_{0}^{-1})=0
.
∎

Lemma 6.4
.

Assume
n\geq(g-1+\deg(L_{0}))/2
, and

\operatorname{Re}(\kappa)\cdot 2(2n-\deg(L_{0}))-(2n-\deg(L_{0})-g+1)\not\in\Z_{\leq 0}

(resp.,

\operatorname{Re}(\kappa)\neq\frac{2n-\deg(L_{0})-g+1}{2(2n-\deg(L_{0}))}

in the non-archimedean case).
Then for every point
x\in(({\operatorname{Bun}}_{L_{0}}^{\leq n})^{0}\setminus{\operatorname{Bun}}_{L_{0}}^{\leq n-1})(F)
, there exists a cocharacter

\lambda^{\vee}:{\mathbb{G}}_{m}\to\operatorname{Aut}(x)
such that in the archimedean case, for any character
a\mapsto a^{m}
of
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
appearing in

S^{\bullet}H^{0}T^{*}_{{\operatorname{Bun}}_{L_{0}}}|_{x}
, one has

{\operatorname{Re}}(\kappa)\cdot\langle\chi_{{\mathcal{L}},x},\lambda^{\vee}\rangle+m\neq-\langle\Delta^{alg}_{\operatorname{Aut}(x)},\lambda^{\vee}\rangle;

while in the non-archimedean case,

{\operatorname{Re}}(\kappa)\cdot\langle\chi_{\omega,x},\lambda^{\vee}\rangle\neq-\langle\Delta^{alg}_{\operatorname{Aut}(x)},\lambda^{\vee}\rangle.

Proof.

By Lemma
6.3
(iii), every
E
in

({\operatorname{Bun}}_{L_{0}}^{\leq n})^{0}\setminus{\operatorname{Bun}}_{L_{0}}^{\leq n-1}
has form

E=A\oplus A^{-1}L_{0}
, with
\deg(A)=n
and
H^{1}(A^{2}L_{0}^{-1})=0
.
Note that this implies that
\deg(A^{2}L_{0}^{-1})\geq g-1>0
, hence
H^{0}(A^{-2}L_{0})=0
.

One has

\underline{{\operatorname{End}}}_{0}(E)\simeq{\mathcal{O}}\oplus A^{2}L_{0}^{-1}\oplus A^{-2}L_{0}.

Hence, the Lie algebra of automorphisms of
E
(with trivial determinant) is
H^{0}({\mathcal{O}})\oplus H^{0}(A^{2}L_{0}^{-1})
,
and the fiber of the canonical line bundle of
{\operatorname{Bun}}_{L_{0}}
at
E
is identified with

\omega_{{\operatorname{Bun}}_{L_{0}}}|_{E}\simeq\det(H^{1}({\mathcal{O}})^{*})\otimes\det(H^{1}(A^{-2}L_{0})^{*})\otimes\det(H^{0}(A^{2}L_{0})).

Let us consider the subgroup
\lambda^{\vee}:{\mathbb{G}}_{m}\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(E)
acting by
(a,a^{-1})
on
A\oplus A^{-1}L_{0}
.
Since the induced action of
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
on
A^{2}L_{0}^{-1}
(resp.,
A^{-2}L_{0}
) has weight
2
(resp.,
-2
), we have

\langle\chi_{\omega},\lambda^{\vee}\rangle=2(\chi(C,A^{2}L_{0}^{-1})-\chi(C,A^{-2}L_{0}))=4(2n-\deg(L_{0})),

-\langle\Delta^{alg}_{\operatorname{Aut}(E)},\lambda^{\vee}\rangle=2\chi(C,A^{2}L_{0}^{-1})=2(2n-\deg(L_{0})-g+1)

(here we use the vanishing of
h^{1}(A^{2}L_{0}^{-1})
).

Also, the
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-weights of
H^{0}T^{*}_{{\operatorname{Bun}}_{L_{0}},E}\simeq H^{1}(A^{-2}L_{0})^{*}\oplus H^{1}({\mathcal{O}})^{*}
are
0
and
2
.
Hence, the weights on the symmetric algebra of
H^{0}T^{*}_{{\operatorname{Bun}}_{L_{0}},E}
are non-negative even integers.
Now the assertion follows from our assumption on
\kappa
.
∎

6.2.
Limits of very stable bundles

Recall that a rank
2
bundle
E
on
C
is
very stable
if any nilpotent Higgs field
\phi:E\to E\otimes\omega_{C}
is zero. It is known that a very stable bundle is stable.

We need some information on bundles which can appear as specializations of very stable bundles.
Recall that a bundle
E_{0}
is a specialization of
E
if there exists a family of bundles on
C
,
(E_{s})
, parametrized by an irreducible base
S
and a point
s_{0}\in S

such that
E_{s}\simeq E
for
s\neq s_{0}
and
E_{s_{0}}\simeq E_{0}
.

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
E
be a very stable bundle with
\det(E)\simeq L_{0}
.
Assume
E_{0}\in{\operatorname{Bun}}_{L_{0}}^{\leq n}
, where
n\geq\deg(L_{0})/2
, is such that
\operatorname{Hom}(E,E_{0})\neq 0
.
Then
E_{0}\in({\operatorname{Bun}}_{L_{0}}^{\leq n})^{0}
.
In particular,
E
does not specialize to any bundle in
{\operatorname{Bun}}_{L_{0}}^{\leq n}\setminus({\operatorname{Bun}}_{L_{0}}^{\leq n})^{0}
, where

n\geq\deg(L_{0})/2
.

Proof.

Assume that
\operatorname{Hom}(E,E_{0})\neq 0
and
E_{0}\in{\operatorname{Bun}}_{L_{0}}^{\leq n}\setminus({\operatorname{Bun}}_{L_{0}}^{\leq n})^{0}
.
Then there exists an exact sequence

0\to A\to E_{0}\to A^{-1}L_{0}\to 0

with
\deg(A)=n
, where
H^{1}(A^{2}L_{0}^{-1})\neq 0
. Since
n\geq\deg(L_{0})/2
, we have

\deg(A^{-1}L_{0})\leq\mu(E)=\deg(L_{0})/2
,
hence
\operatorname{Hom}(E,A^{-1}L_{0})=0
by the stability of
E
.

Since
\operatorname{Hom}(E,E_{0})\neq 0
, the exact sequence

0\to\operatorname{Hom}(E,A)\to\operatorname{Hom}(E,E_{0})\to\operatorname{Hom}(E,A^{-1}L_{0})=0

shows that
\operatorname{Hom}(E,A)\neq 0
.
Let
E\to A
be a nonzero map. Such a map factors through a surjection
E\to A(-D)
,
where
D
is an effective divisor.
Therefore,
E
fits into an extension

0\to A^{-1}L_{0}(D)\to E\to A(-D)\to 0

Next, we observe that

\operatorname{Hom}(A,\omega_{C}A^{-1}L_{0})\simeq H^{0}(\omega_{C}A^{-2}L_{0})\simeq H^{1}(A^{2}L_{0}^{-1})^{*}\neq 0,

Hence, the composition

E\to A(-D)\to A\to\omega_{C}A^{-1}L_{0}\to\omega_{C}A^{-1}L_{0}(D)\to KE

gives a nonzero nilpotent Higgs field on
E
, so
E
cannot be very stable.
∎

Corollary 6.6
.

For
n<(g-1+\deg(L_{0}))/2
, a very stable bundle
E
with determinant
L_{0}

never specializes to a bundle in
{\operatorname{Bun}}_{L_{0}}^{\leq n}
which is not isomorphic to
E
.

Proof.

Assume
E
specializes to
E_{0}\in{\operatorname{Bun}}_{L_{0}}^{\leq n}
, where
E_{0}\not\simeq E
. Applying Lemma
6.5
iteratively,
and taking into account Lemma
6.3
(iv), we see that
E_{0}
is semistable.
But then the condition
\operatorname{Hom}(E,E_{0})\neq 0
gives a contradiction, since
E
and
E_{0}
have the same slope and
E
is stable.
∎

The following result on possible specializations of very stable bundles is not needed for our argument but it clarifies the picture.

Proposition 6.7
.

Let
E
be a rank
2
very stable bundle, and let
E_{0}
be a specialization of
E
such that
E_{0}\not\simeq E
.
Then
E\simeq A\oplus B
, where
A
and
B
are line bundles such that
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(B,A)=0
.

Proof.

Let
L_{0}:=\det(E)
. By Corollary
6.6
,
E_{0}\in{\operatorname{Bun}}_{L_{0}}^{\leq n}\setminus{\operatorname{Bun}}_{L_{0}}^{\leq n-1}
for some
n\geq(g-1+\deg(L_{0}))/2
.
Hence, by Lemma
6.5
,
E\in({\operatorname{Bun}}_{L_{0}}^{\leq n})^{0}\setminus{\operatorname{Bun}}_{L_{0}}^{\leq n-1}
, and the assertion follows from
Lemma
6.3
(iii).
∎

6.3.
Main theorems

Consider the open substack

{\mathcal{M}}^{vs}_{L_{0}}\subset{\operatorname{Bun}}_{L_{0}}
of very stable bundles, and let
M^{vs}_{L_{0}}
be its coarse moduli space. The variety
M^{vs}_{L_{0}}
is smooth and the line
bundle
\omega
on
{\mathcal{M}}^{vs}_{L_{0}}
descends to
\omega
on
M^{vs}_{L_{0}}
.

Theorem 6.8
.

Assume that
g\geq 2
. For every open substack of the form
[X/\operatorname{GL}_{N}]
in
{\operatorname{Bun}}_{L_{0}}
, let
X^{vs}\subset X
denote the open subset corresponding
to very stable bundles. Then for
\operatorname{Re}(\kappa)\geq 1/2
, the pair
(X^{vs},X)
is
|\omega|^{\kappa}
-nice, so
there is a well defined map

\pi_{\kappa}:{\mathcal{S}}({\operatorname{Bun}}_{L_{0}},|\omega|^{\kappa})\to C^{\infty}(M^{vs}_{L_{0}},|\omega|^{\kappa}),

where
\pi_{\kappa}(\varphi)
, viewed as a distribution, is given by absolutely convergent integrals (
5.3
).

Proof.

First, let
X^{s}\subset X
be the
G
-invariant open subset corresponding to stable bundles (where
G=\operatorname{GL}_{N}
). Then we have a smooth geometric quotient
X^{s}/G
of the action of
G
on
X^{s}
,
which implies that the pair
(X^{vs},X^{s})
is
|\omega|^{\kappa}
-nice for any
\kappa\in\C
.

Next, let us show that for
n<(g-1+\deg(L_{0}))/2
, the pair
(X^{vs},X^{\leq n})
is
|\omega|^{\kappa}
-nice for any
\kappa\in\C
, where

X^{\leq n}\subset X
is the
G
-invariant open subset corresponding to
{\operatorname{Bun}}_{L_{0}}^{\leq n}
.
Indeed, since
({\operatorname{Bun}}_{L_{0}}^{\leq n})^{0}={\operatorname{Bun}}_{L_{0}}^{\leq n-1}
for
n<(g-1+\deg(L_{0}))/2
(see Lemma
6.3
(iv)),
by Lemma
6.5
, for such
n
the
G
-orbits of points in
X^{vs}
are contained in
X^{s}
.
Hence, the assertion follows from Lemma
5.4
applied to the embedding
X^{s}\hookrightarrow X^{\leq n}
.
In more detail, given a compact set
K
of
G
-orbits in
X^{vs}/G
,
we have a closed subset
Z_{K}\subset X(F)
consisting of points corresponding to bundles
E_{0}
such that
\operatorname{Hom}(E,E_{0})\neq 0
for some
E\in K
.
By Lemma
6.5
, we have
Z_{K}\subset X^{s}(F)
. On the other hand, by semicontinuity of
\dim\operatorname{Hom}(E,E_{0})
,
the closures of
G
-orbits in
K
are contained in
Z_{K}
. Hence, we can apply Lemma
6.5
to deduce that the pair
(X^{vs},X^{\leq n})

is
|\omega|^{\kappa}
-nice.

Next, we will use induction on
n
to prove that for every
n
, the pair
(X^{vs},X^{\leq n})
is
|\omega|^{\kappa}
-nice for
{\operatorname{Re}}(\kappa)\geq 1/2
.
For some
n\geq(g-1+\deg(L_{0}))/2
, let

X^{\leq n-1}\subset(X^{\leq n})^{0}\subset X

denote the open subsets corresponding to the substacks
{\operatorname{Bun}}_{L_{0}}^{\leq n-1}\subset({\operatorname{Bun}}_{L_{0}}^{\leq n})^{0}
.
By the induction assumption (or the case
n<(g-1+\deg(L_{0}))/2
), we can assume that the claim holds for
X^{\leq n-1}
, i.e, the pair
(X^{vs},X^{\leq n-1})

is
|\omega|^{\kappa}
-nice.

Note that we have

\operatorname{Re}(\kappa)\geq\frac{1}{2}>\frac{2n-\deg(L_{0})-g+1}{2(2n-\deg(L_{0}))}.

Hence, by Lemma
6.4
, we can apply by Lemma
5.2

to the inclusion

[X^{\leq n-1}/G]\subset[(X^{\leq n})^{0}/G]
and to the interval

(a,b)=(\frac{2n-\deg(L_{0})-g+1}{2(2n-\deg(L_{0}))},b)

with
b
sufficiently large,
and deduce that the pair
(X^{vs},(X^{\leq n})^{0})
is
|\omega|^{\kappa}
-nice.

Finally, we would like to apply Lemma
5.4
to the embedding
(X^{\leq n})^{0}\subset X^{\leq n}
.
We need to check that the assumptions of this lemma are satisfied. Given a compact set
K
of
G
-orbits in
X^{vs}/G
,
we have a closed subset
Z_{K}\subset X(F)
consisting of points corresponding to bundles
E_{0}
such that
\operatorname{Hom}(E,E_{0})\neq 0
for some
E\in K
.
By Lemma
6.5
, we have
Z_{K}\subset(X^{\leq n})^{0}(F)
. On the other hand, by semicontinuity of

,
the closures of
G
-orbits in
K
are contained in
Z_{K}
. Hence, by Lemma
5.4
, the pair
(X^{vs},X^{\leq n})
is
|\omega|^{\kappa}
-nice.

Since
X^{\leq n}=X
for sufficiently large
n
, the assertion follows.
∎

In the non-archimedean case we can also prove the following boundedness result, which implies that the stack
{\mathcal{S}}({\operatorname{Bun}}_{L_{0}})
is
\kappa
-bounded for each
\kappa\in\C

in the terminology of
[
4
, Def. 2.10]
.

Theorem 6.9
.

Assume
F
is non-archimedean and
g\geq 2
.

(i) For
n>g-2+\deg(L_{0})/2
and
{\operatorname{Re}}(\kappa)\geq 1/2
, the natural map

{\mathcal{S}}({\operatorname{Bun}}_{L_{0}}^{\leq n},|\omega|^{\kappa})\to{\mathcal{S}}({\operatorname{Bun}}_{L_{0}},|\omega|^{\kappa})

is an isomorphism.

(ii) For any
\kappa\in\C
, the map

{\mathcal{S}}({\operatorname{Bun}}_{L_{0}}^{\leq n},|\omega|^{\kappa})\to{\mathcal{S}}({\operatorname{Bun}}_{L_{0}},|\omega|^{\kappa})

is an isomorphism for large enough
n
.

Proof.

(i) It suffices to prove that under these assumptions the map

{\mathcal{S}}({\operatorname{Bun}}_{L_{0}}^{\leq n},|\omega|^{\kappa})\to{\mathcal{S}}({\operatorname{Bun}}_{L_{0}}^{\leq n+1},|\omega|^{\kappa})

(6.1)

is an isomorphism. We are going to apply Lemma
3.23
(i) to the inclusion
{\operatorname{Bun}}_{L_{0}}^{\leq n}\hookrightarrow{\operatorname{Bun}}_{L_{0}}^{\leq n+1}
.

First of all, we observe that by Lemma
6.3
(v), we have

{\operatorname{Bun}}_{L_{0}}^{\leq n+1}=({\operatorname{Bun}}_{L_{0}}^{\leq n+1})^{0}
, so by Lemma
6.3
(iii), every

E\in{\operatorname{Bun}}_{L_{0}}^{\leq n+1}\setminus{\operatorname{Bun}}_{L_{0}}^{\leq n}
has form
E=A\oplus A^{-1}L_{0}
, where
H^{1}(A^{2}L_{0}^{-1})=0
. In this case
\deg(A^{2}L_{0}^{-1})>0
,
so
H^{0}(A^{-2}L_{0})=0
. It follows that the group
\operatorname{Aut}(E)
is the semidirect product of
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
acting by
(a,a^{-1})
on
A\oplus A^{-1}L_{0}
with the additive group

\operatorname{Hom}(A^{-1}L_{0},A)=H^{0}(A^{2}L_{0}^{-1})
. Together with Lemma
6.4
this implies that the assumptions of Lemma
3.23
(i) are
satisfied and we deduce that the map (
6.1
) is an isomorphism.

(ii) Pick
N
such that
N>g-2+\deg(L_{0})/2
and
{\operatorname{Re}}(\kappa)\neq\frac{2n-\deg(L_{0})-g+1}{2(2n-\deg(L_{0}))}

for
n>N
. Then the same argument as in (i) shows that (
6.1
) is an isomorphism for
n\geq N
.
∎

Note that Theorems
6.8
and
6.9
imply
[
4
, Conj. 3.5(1)]
for

.

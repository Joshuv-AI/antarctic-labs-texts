The differential structure of generators of GNS-symmetric quantum Markov semigroups
Melchior Wirth

Abstract
. We show that the generator of a GNS-symmetric quantum Markov semigroup can be written as the square of a derivation. This generalizes a result of Cipriani and Sauvageot for tracially symmetric semigroups. Compared to the tracially symmetric case, the derivations in the general case satisfy a twisted product rule, reflecting the non-triviality of their modular group. This twist is captured by the new concept of Tomita bimodules we introduce. If the quantum Markov semigroup satisfies a certain additional regularity condition, the associated Tomita bimodule can be realized inside the L 2 L^{2} space of a bigger von Neumann algebra, whose construction is an operator-valued version of free Araki-Woods factors.



1.
Introduction

Quantum Markov semigroups, originally introduced in the study of the time evolution of open quantum systems
[
Ali76
,
GKS76
,
Lin76
]
, have long since permeated the boundaries of mathematical physics and become an object of interest across various disciplines of (noncommutative) mathematics such as noncommutative harmonic analysis
[
JX07
,
JMP14
]
, noncommutative probability, noncommutative geometry
[
Sau96
]
and the structure theory of von Neumann algebras
[
CS15
,
CS17
]
.

One of the central questions from the very beginning was to characterize the generators of quantum Markov semigroups. For quantum Markov semigroups on matrix algebras, the generators are characterized by the theorems of Lindblad
[
Lin76
]
and Gorini–Kossakowski–Sudarshan
[
GKS76
]
, while the generators of uniformly continuous quantum Markov semigroups on arbitrary von Neumann algebras have been described by Christensen–Evans
[
CE79
]
. While quantum Markov semigroups on matrix algebras are of interest in quantum information theory, many examples of classical and quantum Markov semigroups act on infinite-dimensional algebras and are not uniformly continuous, that is, they have unbounded generators.

To facilitate the study of quantum Markov semigroups with unbounded operators, one often focuses on semigroups that satisfy a certain symmetry condition so that one can apply techniques for self-adjoint operators on Hilbert spaces. The simplest such symmetry condition is tracial symmetry, where the reference state is a trace. The study of tracially symmetric quantum Markov semigroups and the associated quadratic forms, completely Dirichlet forms, was initiated by Alberverio–Høegh-Krohn
[
AH77
]
and further pursued by Davies–Lindsay
[
DL92
,
DL93
]
.

In this setting, Cipriani and Sauvageot
[
CS03a
]
proved that the
L_{2}
-generator
\mathcal{L}^{(2)}
can be written as
\mathcal{L}^{(2)}=\delta^{\ast}\bar{\delta}
, where
\delta
is a derivation with values in a Hilbert bimodule. This result spawned a lot of applications across various disciplines, from analysis on fractals
[
HT13
]
and metric graphs
[
BK19
]
over noncommutative geometry
[
CS03
]
, noncommutative probability
[
Dab10
,
JZ15
]
, quantum optimal transport
[
Wir20
,
WZ21
]
to the structure theory of von Neumann algebras and in particular Popa’s deformation and rigidity theory
[
Pet09
,
DI16
,
Cas21
,
CIW21
]
.

Despite this resounding success, the condition of tracial symmetry is too strong in various situations. For example, type III von Neumann algebras do not even admit a faithful normal trace. Quantum Markov semigroups on type III von Neumann algebras occur naturally for example in free probability and the study of compact quantum groups (see Subsections
8.4
,
8.5
for more details). But even on type I and type II von Neumann algebras there are many quantum Markov semigroups for which the natural reference state is not a trace, for example in mathematical physics when modelling of open quantum systems at finite temperature (see below). For this reason the problem of extending Cipriani and Sauvageot’s result to a larger class of quantum Markov semigroups has been raised (see
[
SV19
,
Cas21
]
for example).

In this article we prove a representation of the generator as square of a derivation in the spirit of Cipriani and Sauvageot for the class of GNS-symmetric quantum Markov semigroups on arbitrary von Neumann algebras.

The GNS symmetry (or detailed balance) condition for quantum Markov semigroups also comes with a clear motivation from physics – it describes the time evolution of open quantum systems coupled to an environment in thermal equilibrium
[
Ali76
]
. Tracial symmetry is a special case of GNS symmetry, which corresponds to an equilibrium at infinite temperature in this picture. But GNS-symmetric quantum Markov semigroups also occur naturally across other fields of mathematics, for example the free Ornstein–Uhlenbeck semigroup of free Araki–Woods factors or quantum Markov semigroups associated with Lévy processes on compact quantum groups that are not of Kac type (again, see Subsection
8.4
,
8.5
for details).

On matrix algebras, the structure of the generators of GNS-symmetric quantum Markov semigroups is well-understood
[
Ali76
]
and can be phrased in terms of derivations
[
CM17
,
CM20
]
. This has given rise to applications to
L_{p}
-regularity of quantum Dirichlet forms
[
Bar17
]
, quantum optimal transport
[
CM17
,
MM17
,
CM20
]
and logarithmic Sobolev inequalities
[
GR21
]
. Especially the first two results also offer interesting possible applications of the results of this article. Recently, also the generators of uniformly continuous GNS-symmetric quantum Markov semigroups have been characterized by the author
[
Wir22
]
.

These results already hint at a certain twist needed to represent the generators of GNS-symmetric quantum Markov semigroups as squares of derivations. In fact, it has been shown recently by Vernooij
[
Ver22
]
that there exist GNS-symmetric quantum Markov semigroups on matrix algebras that cannot be written as
\mathcal{L}^{(2)}=\delta^{\ast}\delta
for a derivation
\delta
with values in a Hilbert bimodule. Hence, to extend Cipriani and Sauvageot’s result to GNS-symmetric quantum Markov semigroups, new notions are required.

The characterizing property of traces on von Neumann algebras is that they have trivial modular group. To handle the case when the reference weight is not a trace, the interaction with the modular group gives rise to new features. In particular, a GNS-symmetric quantum Markov semigroups commutes with the modular group, a fact which we systematically exploit. It implies that the bimodule we construct comes with certain extra structure resembling the modular group and conjugation on a Tomita algebra, which we capture in our definition of Tomita bimodules. While the analog of the conjugation operator is also present in the tracially symmetric case, the analog of the modular group is a new phenomenon in the non-tracial case.

Another way to phrase that the modular group of a trace is trivial is to say that the left and right Hilbert algebra associated with it coincide. The twist that appears only in the non-tracial case is that the left action on the bimodule we construct is a
\ast
-homomorphism with respect to the involution from the left Hilbert algebra, while the right action is a
\ast
-anti-homomorphism with respect to the involution from the right Hilbert algebra.

Let us now describe the contents of this article in more detail. In
Section

we review some basic von Neumann algebra theory, in particular Tomita–Takesaki theory and modules over von Neumann algebras. In Section
Section

3
we recall some known facts on quantum Markov semigroups and quantum Dirichlet forms and characterize conservativeness in terms of the Dirichlet form (
Proposition

3.2
).

In

we introduce the new notion of Tomita bimodules (

), which serve as the codomains of the derivations we associate with GNS-symmetric quantum Markov semigroups later. Using a Fock space construction, we show that a Tomita bimodule
\mathcal{H}
over the Tomita algebra

can be embedded into the Hilbert completion of a Tomita algebra

, provided the actions of

are normal in a suitable sense (Subsection
4.2
).

In
Section

5
we consider derivations on Tomita algebras with values in Tomita bimodules that respect the modular automorphism group and conjugation operator (

5.1
). We show that if such a derivation

is inner or more generally can be approximated by inner derivations in a suitable sense, then

generates a GNS-symmetric quantum Markov semigroup (

5.5
,

5.7
).

In
Section

we study the reverse construction going from a quantum Markov semigroup with

-generator

to a derivation such that

. We first show how the results from
[

]
, upgraded to cover the case of a reference weight instead of a state, can be used to prove the existence in the case of a bounded generator (

To deal with unbounded generators, one problem is that of an appropriate domain for the associated derivation. For the product rule to make sense,
\delta
needs to be defined on an algebra. In the tracial setting, a suitable algebra was identified by Davies and Lindsay
[
DL92
]
. Here we show that for a GNS-symmetric quantum Markov semigroup with associated quantum Dirichlet form
\mathcal{E}
, the set

\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}=\{a\in\mathfrak{A}_{\varphi}\mid U_{z}a\in D(\mathcal{E})\text{ for all }z\in\mathbb{C}\}

is a Tomita algebra and a core for
\mathcal{E}
(
Theorem

6.3
). Here
\mathfrak{A}_{\varphi}
denotes the maximal Tomita algebra associated with the weight
\varphi
.

We then prove the main result of this article, namely the existence a Tomita bimodule
\mathcal{H}
over
\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
and a derivation
\delta\colon\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}\to\mathcal{H}
such that the
L_{2}
-generator satisfies
\mathcal{L}^{(2)}=\delta^{\ast}\bar{\delta}
(
Theorem

6.8
). Further we show that under natural assumptions, the Tomita bimodule
\mathcal{H}
and the derivation

are unique (

6.9
).

A desirable property of Tomita bimodules is that the left and right action are normal, which is used for example in our Fock space construction. Even if the underlying von Neumann algebra is commutative, it is known that this is not necessarily the case for the Tomita bimodule associated with a quantum Markov semigroup. In
Section

7
we give a characterization of GNS-symmetric quantum Markov semigroups that give rise to normal Tomita bimodules that runs parallel to the tracially symmetric case (
Theorem

7.2
). In this case, the quantum Markov semigroups induces a structure analog to the derivation triples from
[
JRS
]
in the tracially symmetric case (
Corollary

7.6
).

Finally, in
Section

8
we describe the derivation associated with a GNS-symmetric quantum Markov semigroups in concrete examples. First we show how tracially symmetric quantum Markov semigroups and GNS-symmetric quantum Markov semigroups on finite-dimensional von Neumann algebras fit into the picture (Subsections
8.1
,
8.2
). Then we discuss some genuinely new examples with depolarizing semigroups, the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck semigroup on free Araki–Woods factors and translation-invariant quantum Markov semigroups on compact quantum groups not of Kac type (Subsections
8.3
–
8.5
).

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we briefly recall some definitions and results on Neumann algebras, in particular on weights, Hilbert and Tomita algebras and correspondences, and we fix the notation for the rest of the article. For a more in-depth account of the material presented here, which is mostly standard, see
[
Tak03
]
. A detailed account of
C^{\ast}
- and
W^{\ast}
-modules can be found in
[
Ske01
]
.

To start with, let us fix some general notation. All vector spaces are over the complex numbers, unless explicitly stated otherwise, and (semi-) inner products are linear in the second argument. If
V
and
W
are vector spaces, then
V\odot W
denotes their algebraic tensor product, while
\otimes
is used for the tensor product of Hilbert spaces. Some other tensor products will be discussed below.

2.1.
Weights and semi-cyclic representations

Let
\mathcal{M}
a von Neumann algebra. A
weight
on
\mathcal{M}
is a map
\varphi\colon\mathcal{M}_{+}\to[0,\infty]
such that
\varphi(\lambda x)=\lambda\varphi(x)
and
\varphi(x+y)=\varphi(x)+\varphi(y)
for all
x,y\in\mathcal{M}_{+}
and
\lambda\geq 0
. Here we use the convention
0\cdot\infty=0
.

For a weight
\varphi
we define

\displaystyle\mathfrak{p}_{\varphi}

\displaystyle=\{x\in\mathcal{M}_{+}\mid\varphi(x)<\infty\},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}

\displaystyle=\{x\in\mathcal{M}\mid x^{\ast}x\in\mathfrak{p}_{\varphi}\},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}_{\varphi}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{lin}\{x^{\ast}y\mid x,y\in\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}\}.

The weight
\varphi
is called
normal
if
\sup_{j}\varphi(x_{j})=\varphi(\sup_{j}x_{j})
for every bounded increasing net
(x_{j})
in
\mathcal{M}_{+}
,
semi-finite
if
\mathfrak{p}_{\varphi}
generates
\mathcal{M}
as a von Neumann algebra, and
faithful
if
\varphi(x^{\ast}x)=0
implies
x=0
.

Every element of
\mathfrak{m}_{\varphi}
is a linear combination of four elements of
\mathfrak{p}_{\varphi}
, and
\varphi
can be linearly extended to
\mathfrak{m}_{\varphi}
. This extension will still be denoted by
\varphi
.

A semi-cyclic representation of
\mathcal{M}
is a triple
(\pi,H,\Lambda)
consisting of a normal representation of
\mathcal{M}
on
H
and a
\sigma
-strong
∗
closed linear map
\Lambda
from a dense left ideal
\mathfrak{n}
of
\mathcal{M}
into
H
with dense range such that

\displaystyle\pi(x)\Lambda(y)

\displaystyle=\Lambda(xy)

for all
x\in\mathcal{M}
and
y\in\mathfrak{n}
.

There does not seem to be an established term for
\mathfrak{n}
in this situation. We call it the
definition ideal
of the semi-cyclic representation
(\pi,H,\Lambda)
.

Given a normal semi-finite weight
\varphi
on
\mathcal{M}
, the associated semi-cyclic representation
(\pi_{\varphi},L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi),\Lambda_{\varphi})
is defined as follows:
L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
is the Hilbert space obtained from
\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}
after separation and completion with respect to the inner product

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\varphi}\colon\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}\times\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}\to\mathbb{C},\,(x,y)\mapsto\varphi(x^{\ast}y),

\Lambda_{\varphi}\colon\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}\to L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
is the quotient map and
\pi_{\varphi}
is given by
\pi_{\varphi}(x)\Lambda_{\varphi}(y)=\Lambda_{\varphi}(xy)
. We also use the alternative notation
x\varphi^{1/2}
for
\Lambda_{\varphi}(x)
.

This semi-cyclic representation is essentially uniquely determined by
\varphi
in the following sense: If
(\pi,H,\Lambda)
is another semi-cyclic representation of
\varphi
with definition ideal
\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}
and
\langle\Lambda(x),\Lambda(y)\rangle=\varphi(x^{\ast}y)
for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}
, then there exists a unitary
U\colon L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)\to H
such that
U\Lambda_{\varphi}=\Lambda
and
U\pi_{\varphi}(x)U^{\ast}=\pi(x)
for all
x\in\mathcal{M}
.

2.2.
Hilbert algebras and Tomita algebras

An algebra
\mathfrak{A}
with involution
♯
(resp.
♭
) and inner product
\langle\,\cdot\,,\cdot\,\rangle
is called
left (resp. right) Hilbert algebra
if

•

for every
a\in\mathfrak{A}
the map
\pi_{l}(a)\colon\mathfrak{A}\to\mathfrak{A}
,
b\mapsto ab
(resp.
b\mapsto ba
) is bounded with respect to
\langle\,\cdot\,,\cdot\,\rangle
,

•

\langle ab,c\rangle=\langle b,a^{\sharp}c\rangle
(resp.
\langle ab,c\rangle=\langle b,ca^{\flat}\rangle
) for all
a,b,c\in\mathfrak{A}
,

•

the involution
♯
(resp.
♭
) is closable,

•

the linear span of all products
ab
with
a,b\in\mathfrak{A}
is dense in
\mathfrak{A}
with respect to
\langle\,\cdot\,,\cdot\,\rangle
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a von Neumann algebra and
\varphi
a normal semi-finite faithful weight on
\mathcal{M}
. The prototypical example of a left Hilbert algebra is
\mathfrak{A}=\Lambda_{\varphi}(\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}\cap\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}^{\ast})
with the product
\Lambda_{\varphi}(x)\Lambda_{\varphi}(y)=\Lambda_{\varphi}(xy)
, the involution
\Lambda_{\varphi}(x)^{\sharp}=\Lambda_{\varphi}(x^{\ast})
and the inner product inherited from
L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
, that is,
\langle\,\Lambda_{\varphi}(x)\,,\Lambda_{\varphi}(y)\,\rangle=\varphi(x^{\ast}y)
. In this case,
\pi_{l}(\mathfrak{A})^{\prime\prime}=\pi_{\varphi}(\mathcal{M})
.

Conversely, every left Hilbert algebra
\mathfrak{A}
gives rise to a von Neumann algebra
\pi_{l}(\mathfrak{A})^{\prime\prime}
acting on the completion of
\mathfrak{A}
and a weight

\varphi\colon\pi_{l}(\mathfrak{A})^{\prime\prime}_{+}\to[0,\infty],\,\varphi(x)=\begin{cases}\lVert\xi\rVert^{2}&\text{if }x^{1/2}=\pi_{l}(\xi),\\
\infty&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

If
\mathfrak{A}
is a full left Hilbert algebra
[
Tak03
, Definition VI.1.16]
, then
\varphi
is a normal semi-finite faithful weight on
\pi_{l}(\mathfrak{A})^{\prime\prime}
.

Let
\mathfrak{H}
be the completion of the Hilbert algebra
\mathfrak{A}
. Since the involution
♯
on
\mathfrak{A}
is closable, its closure
S
on
\mathfrak{H}
exists and has a polar decomposition
S=J\Delta^{1/2}
. The operator
\Delta
is a non-singular positive self-adjoint operator, called the
modular operator
, and
J
is an anti-unitary involution, called the
modular conjugation
. If
\mathfrak{A}
is the left Hilbert algebra associated with a weight
\varphi
, we write
\Delta_{\varphi}
and
J_{\varphi}
for the associated modular operator and modular conjugation.

We write
\Lambda_{\varphi}^{\prime}\colon\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}^{\ast}\to L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
for the map
x\mapsto J_{\varphi}\Lambda_{\varphi}(x^{\ast})
. We also use the notation
\varphi^{1/2}x
instead of
\Lambda_{\varphi}^{\prime}(x)
.

If
\mathfrak{A}
is full, the modular conjugation
J
gives rise to the positive self-dual cone
P=\overline{\{\pi_{l}(a)Ja\mid a\in\mathfrak{A}\}}
and
\pi_{l}(\mathfrak{A})^{\prime\prime}
is in standard form
[
Tak03
, Definition IX.1.13]
.

The modular operator
\Delta
gives rise to a pointwise
\sigma
-weakly continuous group of automorphisms
x\mapsto\Delta^{it}x\Delta^{-it}
on
\pi_{l}(\mathfrak{A})^{\prime\prime}
. If
\varphi
is a normal semi-finite faithful weight on
\mathcal{M}
, the group
\sigma^{\varphi}
given by
\sigma^{\varphi}_{t}(x)=\pi_{\varphi}^{-1}(\Delta_{\varphi}^{it}\pi_{\varphi}(x)\Delta_{\varphi}^{-it})
is called the
modular group
associated with
\varphi
.

If
(\alpha_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is a pointwise weak
∗
continuous group of
\ast
-automorphisms on
\mathcal{M}
, then an element
x\in\mathcal{M}
is called
entire analytic
if the map
t\mapsto\alpha_{t}(x)
has an extension
z\mapsto\alpha_{z}(x)
to the complex plane such that
z\mapsto\omega(\alpha_{z}(x))
is analytic for every
\omega\in\mathcal{M}_{\ast}
. The entire analytic elements form a weak
∗
dense
\ast
-subalgebra of
\mathcal{M}
.

A
Tomita algebra
is a left Hilbert algebra
\mathfrak{A}
endowed with a complex one-parameter group
(U_{z})_{z\in\mathbb{C}}
of algebra automorphism such that

•

z\mapsto\langle a,U_{z}b\rangle
is analytic for all
a,b\in\mathfrak{A}
,

•

(U_{z}a)^{\sharp}=U_{\bar{z}}(a^{\sharp})
for all
a\in\mathfrak{A}
,
z\in\mathbb{C}
,

•

\langle U_{z}a,b\rangle=\langle a,U_{-\bar{z}}b\rangle
for all
a,b\in\mathfrak{A}
,
z\in\mathbb{C}
,

•

\langle a^{\sharp},b^{\sharp}\rangle=\langle U_{-i}b,a\rangle
for all
a,b\in\mathfrak{A}
.

Note that every Tomita algebra becomes a right Hilbert algebra when endowed with the involution

\mathfrak{A}\to\mathfrak{A},a\mapsto a^{\flat}=U_{-i}(a^{\sharp}).

For a full left Hilbert algebra
\mathfrak{A}
let

\mathfrak{A}_{0}=\left\{\xi\in\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}D(\Delta^{n})\,\bigg|\,\Delta^{n}\xi\in\mathfrak{A}\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}.

For every
\xi\in\mathfrak{A}_{0}
the map
t\mapsto\Delta^{it}\xi
has an entire analytic extension
z\mapsto U_{z}\xi
with
U_{z}\xi\in\mathfrak{A}_{0}
for all
z\in\mathbb{C}
. This makes
\mathfrak{A}_{0}
into a Tomita algebra such that
\pi_{l}(\mathfrak{A}_{0})^{\prime\prime}=\pi_{l}(\mathfrak{A})^{\prime\prime}
. If
\mathfrak{A}
is the left Hilbert algebra associated with a weight
\varphi
, we write
\mathfrak{A}_{\varphi}
for
\mathfrak{A}_{0}
and call it the
Tomita algebra associated with
\varphi
.

2.3.
Correspondences and relative tensor product

Let
\mathcal{M}
,
\mathcal{N}
be von Neumann algebras. An
\mathcal{M}
-
\mathcal{N}
-correspondence is a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
endowed with commuting normal representations of
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{N}^{\mathrm{op}}
. We write
x\xi
and
\xi y
for the left and right action, respectively. If
\varphi
is a normal semi-finite weight on
\mathcal{M}
, then
L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
is an
\mathcal{M}
-
\mathcal{M}
-correspondence with the usual left action and
\xi\cdot y=J_{\varphi}y^{\ast}J_{\varphi}\xi
.

If
\psi
is a normal semi-finite faithful weight on
\mathcal{N}
, a vector
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
is called
left-bounded
(with respect to
\psi
) if there exists a constant
C>0
such that
\lVert\xi y\rVert\leq C\lVert\psi^{1/2}y\rVert
for all
y\in\mathcal{N}
. The notation
\psi^{1/2}y
was explained in the previous subsection. We write
L_{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}},\psi)
for the set of all left-bounded vectors in
\mathcal{H}
. If
\xi\in L_{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}},\psi)
, then
\varphi^{1/2}y\mapsto\xi y
extends to a bounded linear operator from
L_{2}(\mathcal{N},\psi)
to
\mathcal{H}
, which we denote by
L_{\psi}(\xi)
.

There is also the dual notion of right-bounded vectors. A vector
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
is called
right-bounded
(with respect to
\varphi
) if there exists a constant
C>0
such that
\lVert x\xi\rVert\leq C\lVert x\varphi^{1/2}\rVert
for all
x\in\mathcal{M}
. We write
L_{\infty}(_{\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{H},\varphi)
for the set of all right-bounded vectors in
\mathcal{H}
. The bounded operator from
L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
to
\mathcal{H}
that extends
x\varphi^{1/2}\mapsto x\xi
is denoted by
R_{\varphi}(\xi)
.

A vector in
\mathcal{H}
is called
bounded
if it is both left- and right-bounded. We write
L_{\infty}(_{\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{N}},(\varphi,\psi))
for the set of all bounded vectors or simply
L_{\infty}(_{\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}},\varphi)
if
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{N}
and
\varphi=\psi
.

If
\mathcal{H}
,
\mathcal{K}
are
\mathcal{M}
-
\mathcal{N}
-correspondences, we write
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{N}},\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{N}})
for the set of all bounded right module maps from
\mathcal{H}
to
\mathcal{K}
. Similarly,
\mathcal{L}(_{\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{H},_{\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{K})
denotes the set of all bounded left module maps from
\mathcal{H}
to
\mathcal{K}
. Clearly
L_{\psi}(\xi)\in\mathcal{L}(L_{2}(\mathcal{N},\psi)_{\mathcal{N}},\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{N}})
for every left-bounded vector
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
and
R_{\psi}(\xi)\in\mathcal{L}(_{\mathcal{M}}L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi),_{\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{H})
for every right-bounded vector
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
. Since
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{N}
are in standard form on
L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
and
L_{2}(\mathcal{N},\psi)
, respectively, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(L_{2}(\mathcal{N},\psi)_{\mathcal{N}},L_{2}(\mathcal{N},\psi)_{\mathcal{N}})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{N}

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(_{\mathcal{M}}L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi),_{\mathcal{M}}L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi))

\displaystyle=\mathcal{M}^{\prime}.

Thus
L_{\psi}(\xi)^{\ast}L_{\psi}(\xi)\in\mathcal{N}
for every left-bounded vector
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
and
R_{\varphi}(\xi)^{\ast}R_{\varphi}(\xi)\in\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
for every right-bounded vector
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
. We also write
(\xi|\eta)
for
L_{\varphi}(\xi)^{\ast}L_{\varphi}(\eta)
to stress the connection to Hilbert bimodules.

If
\mathcal{M}
,
\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{R}
are von Neumann algebras,
\mathcal{H}
is an
\mathcal{M}
-
\mathcal{N}
-correspondence,
\mathcal{K}
is an
\mathcal{N}
-
\mathcal{R}
-correspondence and
\psi
is a normal semi-finite faithful weight on
\mathcal{N}
, the
relative tensor product

\mathcal{H}\otimes_{\psi}\mathcal{K}
is the Hilbert space obtained from the algebraic tensor product
L_{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{N}},\psi)\odot\mathcal{K}
after separation and completion with respect to the semi-inner product

\langle\xi_{1}\otimes\eta_{1},\xi_{2}\otimes\eta_{2}\rangle=\langle\eta_{1},L_{\psi}(\xi_{1})^{\ast}L_{\psi}(\xi_{2})\cdot\eta_{2}\rangle.

This expression makes sense since
L_{\psi}(\xi_{1})^{\ast}L_{\psi}(\xi_{2})\in\mathcal{N}
. The image of
\xi\otimes\eta
in
\mathcal{H}\otimes_{\psi}\mathcal{K}
is denoted by
\xi\otimes_{\psi}\eta
.

On
L_{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{N}},\psi)\odot L_{\infty}(_{\mathcal{N}}\mathcal{K},\psi)
one has

\langle\eta_{1},L_{\psi}(\xi_{1})^{\ast}L_{\psi}(\xi_{2})\eta_{2}\rangle=\langle\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\cdot J_{\psi}R_{\psi}(\eta_{2})^{\ast}R_{\psi}(\eta_{1})J_{\psi}\rangle.

Hence
\mathcal{H}\otimes_{\psi}\mathcal{K}
can equivalently be defined as the Hilbert space obtained from
\mathcal{H}\odot L_{\infty}(_{\mathcal{N}}\mathcal{H},\psi)
after separation and completion with respect to the semi-inner product

\langle\xi_{1}\otimes\eta_{1},\xi_{2}\otimes\eta_{2}\rangle=\langle\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\cdot J_{\psi}R_{\psi}(\eta_{2})^{\ast}R_{\psi}(\eta_{1})J_{\psi}\rangle.

-modules and von Neumann modules

Let

,

Let

-

3.
Quantum Markov semigroups and quantum Dirichlet forms

In this section we review some basic material on quantum Markov semigroups and the GNS symmetry condition, before we give a new characterization of conservativeness in terms of the associated quadratic forms on
L_{2}
(
Proposition

3.2
) and discuss some density properties.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a von Neumann algebra. A
quantum dynamical semigroup
on
\mathcal{M}
is a pointwise
\sigma
-weakly continuous semigroup
(P_{t})
of normal contractive completely positive operators on
\mathcal{M}
. If
P_{t}
is unital for every
t\geq 0
, we call
(P_{t})
a
quantum Markov semigroup
.

Note that these definitions are not universal – some authors use the term “quantum Markov semigroup” for the objects we call quantum dynamical semigroups and “conservative quantum Markov semigroup” for our quantum Markov semigroups. The focus of this article is exclusively on quantum Markov semigroups (in our sense), we only introduce quantum dynamical semigroups to relate some results from the literature that are formulated in the non-unital case.

The continuity requirement in the definition of quantum dynamical semigroups implies pointwise continuity for any of the “reasonable” locally convex operator topologies weaker than the norm topology. This is well-known among experts, but since we did not find a reference, we give the simple proof here.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a von Neumann algebra. If
(P_{t})
is a quantum dynamical semigroup, then
P_{t}(x)\to x
in the
\sigma
-strong
∗
topology as
t\searrow 0
for every
x\in\mathcal{M}
.

Proof.

Let
x\in\mathcal{M}
and
\omega\in\mathcal{M}_{\ast}
. By the Kadison–Schwarz inequality,

\omega((x-P_{t}(x))^{\ast}(x-P_{t}(x)))\leq\omega(x^{\ast}x)+\omega(P_{t}(x^{\ast}x))-\omega(x^{\ast}P_{t}(x))-\omega(P_{t}(x)^{\ast}x)\to 0.

Thus
P_{t}(x)\to x
in the
\sigma
-strong topology. Since
P_{t}
is a self-adjoint map, the same argument applies to
(P_{t}(x^{\ast}))
. Therefore
P_{t}(x)\to x
in the
\sigma
-strong
∗
topology.
∎

Now we turn to quantum Markov semigroups that satisfy a suitable symmetry condition which makes them amenable to self-adjoint operator techniques on Hilbert spaces. On general von Neumann algebras, the study of these semigroups in the non-tracial setting goes back to Goldstein and Lindsay
[
GL95
,
GL99
]
and Cipriani
[
Cip97
]
.

Now let
\varphi
be a normal semi-finite faithful weight on
\mathcal{M}
. We call a quantum dynamical semigroup
(P_{t})
on
\mathcal{M}

GNS-symmetric with respect to
\varphi
or simply
\varphi
-symmetric if
\varphi\circ P_{t}\leq\varphi
for all
t\geq 0
and

(1)

\varphi(P_{t}(x)^{\ast}y)=\varphi(x^{\ast}P_{t}(y))

for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}
and
t\geq 0
. If
x\in\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}
, then

\varphi(P_{t}(x)^{\ast}P_{t}(x))\leq\varphi(P_{t}(x^{\ast}x))\leq\varphi(x^{\ast}x)

by the Kadison–Schwarz inequality, so that both sides of (
1
) are well-defined.

If
(P_{t})
is a
\varphi
-symmetric quantum dynamical semigroup, then it commutes with the modular group
\sigma^{\varphi}
(the argument from
[
Wir22
, Proposition 2.2]
for a state
\varphi
and unital
(P_{t})
can be easily extended to the case when
\varphi
is a weight and
P_{t}
is only assumed to be contractive).

Moreover,
(P_{t})
gives rise to a strongly continuous symmetric contraction semigroup
(P_{t}^{(2)})
on
L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
that acts on
\Lambda_{\varphi}(\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi})
by

P_{t}^{(2)}\Lambda_{\varphi}(x)=\Lambda_{\varphi}(P_{t}x).

Since
(P_{t})
commutes with
\sigma^{\varphi}
, the semigroup
(P_{t}^{(2)})
commutes with
(\Delta_{\varphi}^{is})
. In particular, this definition of
(P_{t}^{(2)})
is consistent with the definition in terms of the symmetric embedding
\Delta_{\varphi}^{1/2}\Lambda_{\varphi}
instead of
\Lambda_{\varphi}
. Since
(P_{t})
consists of self-adjoint maps,
(P_{t}^{(2)})
also commutes with
J_{\varphi}
.

Let
C
be the closure of
\{\Delta_{\varphi}^{1/4}\Lambda_{\varphi}(x)\mid x\in\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}\cap\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}^{\ast},\,0\leq x\leq 1\}
. We call a strongly continuous symmetric contraction semigroup
(T_{t})
on
L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
a
symmetric quantum dynamical semigroup
if it leaves
C
invariant. We call
(T_{t})

GNS-symmetric
if it commutes with
(\Delta_{\varphi}^{is})
.

It follows from
[
GL99
, Theorem 4.9]
that
(P_{t})\mapsto(P_{t}^{(2)})
is a one-to-one correspondence between
\varphi
-symmetric quantum dynamical semigroups on
\mathcal{M}
and GNS-symmetric quantum dynamical semigroups on
L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
. We call a symmetric quantum dynamical semigroup on
L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
a
symmetric quantum Markov semigroup
if the associated quantum dynamical semigroup
(P_{t})
on
\mathcal{M}
is a quantum Markov semigroup, i.e.,
P_{t}
is unital for all
t\geq 0
. If
\varphi
is finite, this condition is equivalent to
T_{t}\varphi^{1/2}=\varphi^{1/2}
for all
t\geq 0
.

Next we discuss quantum Dirichlet forms. First a short detour on quadratic forms is in order. Let
H
be a Hilbert space. There are three equivalent points of view: A quadratic form
q
can be defined as a quadratic map from
H
to
[0,\infty]
, a quadratic map from a subspace of
H
to
[0,\infty)
, or as a sequilinear map from the cartesian product of a subspace of
H
with itself to
\mathbb{C}
. The first two viewpoints are related by restricting
q
to the elements where it is finite, and the second and third are related by the polarization identity. We will use all three viewpoints interchangeably and use the same symbol to denote all three objects.

A quadratic form
q
on
H
is called
densely defined
if
D(q)=\{\xi\in H\mid q(\xi)<\infty\}
is dense,
closable
if the map
q\colon D(q)\to[0,\infty)
is lower semicontinuous and
closed
if the map
q\colon H\to[0,\infty]
is lower semicontinuous. For every closed densely defined quadratic form
q
there exists a positive self-adjoint operator
A
on
H
such that
D(A^{1/2})=D(q)
and
\lVert A^{1/2}\xi\rVert^{2}=q(\xi)
for all
\xi\in D(q)
.

Now let us come back to the setting of
L_{2}
spaces associated with von Neumann algebras. Let
P_{C}
be the metric projection onto the cone
C
defined above. A densely defined closed quadratic form
\mathcal{E}
on
L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
is called
Dirichlet form
if
\mathcal{E}\circ\mathcal{J}=\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{E}\circ P_{C}\leq\mathcal{E}
. It is a
completely Dirichlet form
if for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
the amplification

\mathcal{E}_{n}\colon L_{2}(\mathcal{M}\otimes M_{n}(\mathbb{C}),\varphi\otimes\operatorname{tr}_{n})\to[0,\infty],\,\mathcal{E}_{n}([\xi_{ij}])=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\mathcal{E}(\xi_{ij})

is a Dirichlet form. Here
\operatorname{tr}_{n}
is the normalized trace and
L_{2}(\mathcal{M}\otimes M_{n}(\mathbb{C}),\varphi\otimes\operatorname{tr}_{n})
is identified with
M_{n}(L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi))
.

We call a completely Dirichlet form
\mathcal{E}

GNS-symmetric
if
\mathcal{E}\circ\Delta_{\varphi}^{is}=\mathcal{E}
for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
. It follows from
[
GL99
, Theorem 5.7]
that whenever
(T_{t})
is a symmetric quantum dynamical semigroup on
L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
with generator
\mathcal{L}
, then the quadratic form
\mathcal{E}
with domain
D(\mathcal{E})=D(\mathcal{L}^{1/2})
and
\mathcal{E}(\xi)=\lVert\mathcal{L}^{1/2}\xi\rVert^{2}
for all
\xi\in D(\mathcal{L}^{1/2})
is a GNS-symmetric completely Dirichlet form, and every GNS-symmetric completely Dirichlet form arises in this ways.

If
\varphi
is finite, the characterization of the quadratic forms associated with GNS-symmetric quantum Markov semigroups is also easy – the additional condition is simply
\mathcal{E}(\varphi^{1/2})=0
. In the case when
\varphi
is a weight, such a characterization seems to be missing in the literature. We give here a necessary and sufficient criterion that is analogous to the one in the classical case from
[
FOT11
, Theorem I.1.6.6]
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a von Neumann algebra,
\varphi
a normal semi-finite faithful weight and
\mathcal{E}
a GNS-symmetric completely Dirichlet form on
L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
. The associated quantum dynamical semigroup
(P_{t})
on
\mathcal{M}
is a quantum Markov semigroup if and only if there exists a sequence
(\xi_{n})
in
D(\mathcal{E})\cap\Lambda_{\varphi}^{\prime}(\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}^{\ast})
such that
\pi_{r}(\xi_{n})\to 1
strongly and

\mathcal{E}(\xi_{n},\eta)\to 0

for all
\eta\in D(\mathcal{E})\cap\Lambda_{\varphi}(\mathfrak{m}_{\varphi})
.

Proof.

Write
\mathcal{L}
for
\sigma
-weak generator of
(P_{t})
on
\mathcal{M}
. First assume that
(P_{t})
is a quantum Markov semigroup. Let
(x_{n})
be a sequence in
\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}^{\ast}
such that
x_{n}\to 1
strongly
∗
. Since
(\mathcal{L}+1)^{-1}
is normal, self-adjoint and
\mathcal{L}(1)=0
, we have
(\mathcal{L}+1)^{-1}(x_{n})\to 1
strongly
∗
. Moreover,
(\mathcal{L}+1)^{-1}
leaves
\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}^{\ast}
invariant by the Kadison–Schwarz inequality.

Let
\xi_{n}=\Lambda_{\varphi}^{\prime}((\mathcal{L}+1)^{-1}x_{n})
. If
\eta\in D(\mathcal{E})\cap\Lambda_{\varphi}(\mathfrak{m}_{\varphi})
, then

\mathcal{E}(\xi_{n},\eta)=\langle\mathcal{L}^{(2)}(\mathcal{L}^{(2)}+1)^{-1}\Lambda_{\varphi}^{\prime}(x_{n}),\eta\rangle=\langle\Lambda_{\varphi}^{\prime}(x_{n})-\Lambda_{\varphi}^{\prime}((\mathcal{L}+1)^{-1}(x_{n})),\eta\rangle.

Since
\eta\in\Lambda_{\varphi}(\mathfrak{m}_{\varphi})
, there exist
y_{1},\dots,y_{m},z_{1},\dots,z_{m}\in\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}
such that
\eta=\sum_{j}\Lambda_{\varphi}(y_{j}^{\ast}z_{j})
. Thus

\mathcal{E}(\xi_{n},\eta)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\langle\Lambda_{\varphi}(y_{j})\cdot(x_{n}-(\mathcal{L}+1)^{-1}(x_{n})),\Lambda_{\varphi}(z_{j})\rangle\to 0.

For the converse let
\eta=\Lambda_{\varphi}((\mathcal{L}+1)^{-1}(x))
with
x\in\mathfrak{m}_{\varphi}
. Since
x
is a linear combination of four elements from
\mathfrak{p}_{\varphi}
and
\varphi\circ(\mathcal{L}+1)^{-1}\leq\varphi
, we have
(\mathcal{L}+1)^{-1}(x)\in\mathfrak{m}_{\varphi}
. Moreover, writing
\mathcal{L}^{(2)}
for the generator of
(P_{t}^{(2)})
, we have
\eta=(\mathcal{L}^{(2)}+1)^{-1}\Lambda_{\varphi}(x)\in D(\mathcal{E})
.

Let
(\xi_{n})
be a sequence as in the proposition and set
x_{n}=J\pi_{r}(\xi_{n})^{\ast}J
, so that
\xi_{n}=\Lambda_{\varphi}^{\prime}(x_{n})
. Note that
\pi_{r}(\xi_{n})\to 1
strongly implies
x_{n}^{\ast}\to 1
strongly. By the GNS-symmetry of
(P_{t})
we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\xi_{n},\eta)

\displaystyle=\langle\xi_{n},\Lambda_{\varphi}(x)-\Lambda_{\varphi}((\mathcal{L}+1)^{-1}(x))\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\xi_{n}-(\mathcal{L}^{(2)}+1)^{-1}\xi_{n},\Lambda_{\varphi}(x)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\Lambda_{\varphi}^{\prime}(x_{n}-(\mathcal{L}+1)^{-1}(x_{n})),\Lambda_{\varphi}(x)\rangle.

Write
x=\sum_{j}y_{j}^{\ast}z_{j}
with
y_{1},\dots,y_{m},z_{1},\dots,z_{m}\in\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}
to get

\mathcal{E}(\xi_{n},\eta)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\langle\Lambda_{\varphi}(y_{j})\cdot(x_{n}-(\mathcal{L}+1)^{-1}(x_{n})),\Lambda_{\varphi}(z_{j})\rangle.

Taking the limit on both sides we obtain

0=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\langle(1-(\mathcal{L}+1)^{-1}(1))\Lambda_{\varphi}(y_{j}),\Lambda_{\varphi}(z_{j})\rangle.

As this holds for all
y_{j},z_{j}\in\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}
, we conclude
(\mathcal{L}+1)^{-1}(1)=1
, which implies that
1\in D(\mathcal{L})
and
\mathcal{L}(1)=0
.
∎

Definition 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a von Neumann algebra and
\varphi
a normal semi-finite faithful weight on
\mathcal{M}
. We say a completely Dirichlet form
\mathcal{E}
on
L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
is
quantum Dirichlet form
if there exists a sequence
(\xi_{n})
in
D(\mathcal{E})\cap\Lambda_{\varphi}^{\prime}(\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}^{\ast})
such that
\pi_{r}(\xi_{n})\to 1
strongly and
\mathcal{E}(\xi_{n},\eta)\to 0
for all
\eta\in D(\mathcal{E})\cap\Lambda_{\varphi}(\mathfrak{m}_{\varphi})
.

Thus, a GNS-symmetric completely Dirichlet form is a quantum Dirichlet form if and only if the associated semigroup is a quantum Markov semigroup.

We also need some density properties for the domain of completely Dirichlet forms. We start with an abstract approximation lemma in Hilbert spaces (see
[
AGS14
, Lemma 4.9]
for a similar result).

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
H
be a Hilbert space,
A
a positive self-adjoint operator on
H
and
\beta\geq 0
. If
V\subset H
is a dense subspace, then
\operatorname{lin}\bigcup_{t>0}e^{-tA}(V)
is a core for
A^{\beta}
.

Proof.

First note that
e^{-tA}(V)\subset D(A^{\beta})
by the spectral theorem. Let
\xi\in D(A^{\beta})
. By assumption there exists a sequence
(\xi_{n})
in
V
such that
\xi_{n}\to\xi
. By the spectral theorem, there exists
C>0
such that

\lVert A^{\beta}(e^{-tA}\xi_{n}-e^{-tA}\xi)\rVert\leq\frac{C}{t^{\beta}}\lVert\xi-\xi_{n}\rVert.

Thus
e^{-tA}\xi_{n}\to e^{-tA}\xi
in the graph norm of
A^{\beta}
for every
t>0
. Moreover,

A^{\beta}(e^{-tA}\xi-\xi)=e^{-tA}A^{\beta}\xi-A^{\beta}\xi\to 0

as
t\searrow 0
. Hence
\bigcup_{t>0}e^{-tA}(V)
is dense in
D(A^{\beta})
with respect to the graph norm.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

The same result holds when
A
is the generator of an analytic contraction semigroup on a Banach space, one just has to replace the application of the spectral theorem by holomorphic functional calculus.

As an application we immediately get the following density result. Recall that
\mathfrak{A}_{\varphi}
is the maximal Tomita algebra associated with
\Lambda_{\varphi}(\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}\cap\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}^{\ast})
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a von Neumann algebra and
\varphi
a normal semi-finite faithful weight on
\mathcal{M}
. If
\mathcal{E}
is a GNS-symmetric completely Dirichlet form on
L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
, then
\mathfrak{A}_{\varphi}\cap D(\mathcal{E})
is a core for
\mathcal{E}
.

Proof.

Since the associated semigroup
(T_{t})
is a GNS-symmetric quantum dynamical semigroup,
\mathfrak{A}_{\varphi}\cap D(\mathcal{E})
is invariant under
(T_{t})
. Now the result follows from
Lemma

3.4
.
∎

We also need a density result inside
\mathcal{M}
. We write
\mathcal{M}_{1}
for the closed unit ball in
\mathcal{M}
.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a von Neumann algebra and
\varphi
a normal semi-finite faithful weight on
\mathcal{M}
. If
(T_{t})
is a GNS-symmetric quantum dynamical semigroup on
L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
with generator
\mathcal{L}^{(2)}
, then
\{x\in\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}\cap\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}^{\ast}\mid\Lambda_{\varphi}(x)\in\mathfrak{A}_{\varphi}\cap D(\mathcal{E})\}\cap\mathcal{M}_{1}
is strong
∗
dense in
\mathcal{M}_{1}
.

Proof.

By standard approximation arguments, the set
D=\{x\in\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}\cap\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}^{\ast}\mid\Lambda_{\varphi}(x)\in\mathfrak{A}_{\varphi}\}\cap\mathcal{M}_{1}
is strong
∗
dense in
\mathcal{M}_{1}
. Let
x\in D
be self-adjoint and write
(P_{t})
for the quantum dynamical semigroup on
\mathcal{M}
associated with
(T_{t})
. By
Lemma

3.1
we have
P_{t}(x)^{\ast}=P_{t}x\to x
strongly as
t\searrow 0
.

By the spectral theorem and the definition of
(T_{t})
,

\Lambda_{\varphi}(P_{t}(x))=T_{t}\Lambda_{\varphi}(x)\in D(\mathcal{E}).

Finally,
P_{t}(x)\in D
since
(P_{t})
is contractive and GNS-symmetric.
∎

4.
Tomita bimodules and the Fock space construction

In this section we introduce the notion of Tomita bimodules (
Definition

4.1
). Tomita bimodules are bimodules over Tomita algebras with certain extra structure that resembles the modular group and modular conjugation on a Tomita algebra. Tomita bimodules will serve as codomains of the derivations we associate with GNS-symmetric quantum Markov semigroups.

The resemblance of the extra structure on a Tomita bimodule with the modular group and modular conjugation on a Tomita algebra is more than just an analogy. We show (
Theorem

4.15
) using a Fock space construction that if the actions of a Tomita algebra
\mathfrak{A}
on a Tomita bimodule are normal, this bimodule can be embedded into the GNS Hilbert space of a weight on a von Neumann algebra containing
\pi_{l}(\mathfrak{A})^{\prime\prime}
, this weight extends the canonical weight on
\pi_{l}(\mathfrak{A})^{\prime\prime}
and its modular group leaves
\pi_{l}(\mathfrak{A})^{\prime\prime}
invariant. This construction generalizes Shlyakhtenko’s free Araki–Woods factors
[
Shl97
]
, which correspond to the case
\mathfrak{A}=\mathbb{C}
(up to a change of sign).

4.1.
Tomita bimodules

Some notation: If
\mathcal{H}
is a pre-Hilbert space, we write
B(\mathcal{H})
for the set of all adjointable linear operators on
\mathcal{H}
. Of course this set coincides with the set of bounded linear operators if
\mathcal{H}
is complete. If
A
is a
\ast
-algebra, we write
A^{\mathrm{op}}
for the algebra with the same vector space structure and involution as
A
and the product
a\cdot_{\mathrm{op}}b=ba
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a Tomita algebra. A
Tomita bimodule
over
\mathfrak{A}
is a pre-Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
with non-degenerate commuting
\ast
-representations
L\colon(\mathfrak{A},^{\sharp})\to B(\mathcal{H})
,
R\colon(\mathfrak{A}^{\mathrm{op}},^{\flat})\to B(\mathcal{H})
, an anti-linear isometric involution
\mathcal{J}\colon\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
and a group
(\mathcal{U}_{z})_{z\in\mathbb{C}}
of adjointable operators on
\mathcal{H}
such that

(a)

\lVert L(a)\rVert\leq\lVert\pi_{l}(a)\rVert
,
\lVert R(a)\rVert\leq\lVert\pi_{r}(a)\rVert
for all
a\in\mathfrak{A}
,

(b)

\mathcal{J}L(a)=R(Ja)\mathcal{J}
for all

(c)

the map

(d)

\langle\xi,\mathcal{U}_{z}\eta\rangle=\langle\mathcal{U}_{-\bar{z}}\xi,\eta\rangle
for all
\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{H}
,
z\in\mathbb{C}
,

(e)

\mathcal{U}_{z}L(a)\mathcal{U}_{-z}=L(U_{z}a)

(f)

\mathcal{U}_{z}\mathcal{J}=\mathcal{J}\mathcal{U}_{\bar{z}}

.

Remark 4.2
.

One could also formulate the axioms of a Tomita bimodules also in terms of the involution
\mathcal{J}\mathcal{U}_{-i/2}
instead of
\mathcal{J}
, which would make the analogy to Tomita algebras even more apparent. However, since
\mathcal{J}
is bounded in contrast to
\mathcal{J}\mathcal{U}_{-i/2}
, it is easier to construct it in concrete examples. Moreover, as there is no (internal) multiplication on a Tomita bimodule, the operator
\mathcal{J}\mathcal{U}_{-i/2}
does not have the same distinguished role as
S_{0}=JU_{-i/2}
has for a Tomita algebra. For this reason we chose the presentation in terms of
\mathcal{J}
.

Remark 4.3
.

In addition to the axioms of a Tomita bimodule, one could also require that the elements
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
be bounded vectors (see below). However, this is typically not the case for the range of the derivations we associate with GNS-symmetric quantum Markov semigroups, so we do not include this condition in our definition of Tomita bimodules.

Remark 4.4
.

During the preparation of this manuscript, we learned that the term “Tomita bimodule” has already been used for a related, but different object in

. There the authors study
\mathfrak{A}
-bimodules with an

Let

Lemma 4.7

A vector
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
is left-bounded if and only if

As discussed above, every normal Tomita bimodule gives rise to a Tomita correspondence. Conversely, every Tomita correspondence contains a Tomita bimodule, as we show in the next proposition. Note however that these two operations are in general not inverse to each other.

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a von Neumann algebra and
\varphi
a normal semi-finite faithful weight on
\mathcal{M}
. Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Tomita
\mathcal{M}
-
\mathcal{M}
-correspondence and let
\mathcal{H}_{0}
be the set of all vectors
\xi
in
\mathcal{H}
for which

\mathbb{R}\to\mathcal{H},\,t\mapsto\mathcal{U}_{t}\xi

has an entire continuation
z\mapsto\mathcal{U}_{z}\xi
and
\mathcal{U}_{z}\xi
is bounded for all
z\in\mathbb{C}
. Then
\mathcal{H}_{0}
is dense in
\mathcal{H}
and equipped with the restriction of
\mathcal{J}
and
(\mathcal{U}_{z})
and the left and right actions

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle\colon\mathfrak{A}_{\varphi}\to B(\mathcal{H}),\,L(a)\xi=\pi_{l}(a)\cdot\xi,

\displaystyle R

\displaystyle\colon\mathfrak{A}_{\varphi}\to B(\mathcal{H}),\,R(a)\xi=\xi\cdot J\pi_{r}(a)^{\ast}J

it forms a normal Tomita bimodule over
\mathfrak{A}_{\varphi}
.

Proof.

By
[
OOT17
, Theorem 1]
the set of bounded vectors is dense in
\mathcal{H}
. For a bounded vector
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
let

\xi_{n}=\sqrt{\frac{n}{\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-nt^{2}}\mathcal{U}_{t}\xi\,dt.

By
[
Tak03
, Lemma VI.2.4]
we have
\xi_{n}\to\xi
. Moreover, it is not hard to see that

\mathbb{C}\to\mathcal{H},\,z\mapsto\mathcal{U}_{z}\xi=\sqrt{\frac{n}{\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-n(t-z)^{2}}\mathcal{U}_{t}\xi\,dt

is an entire continuation of
t\mapsto\mathcal{U}_{t}\xi_{n}
(compare
[
Tak03
, Theorem VI.2.2]
).

Furthermore,

\displaystyle\lVert(\mathcal{U}_{z}\xi_{n})x\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\frac{n}{\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-n(t-\mathrm{Re}z)^{2}}e^{n(\mathrm{Im}z)^{2}}\lVert(\mathcal{U}_{t}\xi)x\rVert\,dt

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{n}{\pi}}e^{n(\mathrm{Im}z)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-n(t-\mathrm{Re}z)^{2}}\lVert\mathcal{U}_{t}(\xi\sigma^{\varphi}_{-t}(x))\rVert\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\frac{n}{\pi}}e^{n(\mathrm{Im}z)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-n(t-\mathrm{Re}z)^{2}}\lVert L_{\varphi}(\xi)\rVert\lVert\varphi^{1/2}\sigma^{\varphi}_{-t}(x)\rVert\,dt

\displaystyle=e^{n(\mathrm{Im}z)^{2}}\lVert L_{\varphi}(\xi)\rVert\lVert\varphi^{1/2}x\rVert

so that
\mathcal{U}_{z}\xi_{n}
is left-bounded. An analog computation shows that
\mathcal{U}_{z}\xi_{n}
is right-bounded as well. This establishes the density of
\mathcal{H}_{0}
.

If
a\in\mathfrak{A}_{\varphi}
and
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
is bounded, then
L(a)\xi=\pi_{l}(a)\cdot\xi
is left-bounded. Moreover, if
x\in\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}
, then
x\pi_{l}(a)\in\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}
and

\displaystyle\lVert x\cdot L(a)\xi\rVert

\displaystyle=\lVert x\cdot\pi_{l}(a)\xi\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\lVert R_{\varphi}(\xi)\rVert\lVert x\pi_{l}(a)\varphi^{1/2}\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\lVert R_{\varphi}(\xi)\rVert\lVert\pi_{l}(U_{i/2}(a))\rVert\lVert x\varphi^{1/2}\rVert.

Thus
L(a)\xi
is right-bounded, as well. Similarly one shows that
R(a)\xi
is bounded.

Furthermore, if
z\mapsto\mathcal{U}_{z}\xi
is an entire analytic continuation of
t\mapsto\mathcal{U}_{t}\xi
, then
z\mapsto L(U_{z}a)R(U_{z}b)\mathcal{U}_{z}\xi
is an entire analytic continuation of
t\mapsto\mathcal{U}_{t}(L(a)R(b)\xi)
. Therefore
\mathcal{H}_{0}
is an
\mathfrak{A}_{\varphi}
-bimodule with the left and right actions
L
and
R
. Properties (a)–(f) of a Tomita bimodule now follow from the properties of a Tomita correspondence together with the uniqueness of analytic continuations.
∎

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Tomita
\mathcal{M}
-
\mathcal{M}
-correspondence. We write
\mathcal{H}_{a}
for the set of all
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
(not necessarily bounded) for which the map

has an entire continuation. On
\mathcal{H}_{a}
we define involutions
\mathcal{S}_{0}
and
\mathcal{F}_{0}

\mathcal{F}_{0}\xi=\mathcal{J}\mathcal{U}_{i/2}\xi

The Fock space over

where

denotes the relative tensor product of correspondences.

For a left-bounded vector
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
define the operator
a_{0}(\xi)
on the algebraic direct sum by

\displaystyle a_{0}(\xi)\varphi^{1/2}x

\displaystyle=\xi x,

\displaystyle a_{0}(\xi)(\xi_{1}\otimes_{\varphi}\dots\otimes_{\varphi}\xi_{n})

\displaystyle=\xi\otimes_{\varphi}\xi_{1}\otimes_{\varphi}\dots\otimes_{\varphi}\xi_{n}.

By the definition of the relative tensor product,

denote the bounded extension of

Likewise, for a right-bounded vector
\xi\in\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle b_{0}(\xi)x\varphi^{1/2}

\displaystyle=x\xi,

\displaystyle b_{0}(\xi)(\xi_{1}\otimes_{\varphi}\dots\otimes_{\varphi}\xi_{n})

\displaystyle=\xi_{1}\otimes_{\varphi}\dots\otimes_{\varphi}\xi_{n}\otimes_{\varphi}\xi.

Again,
b_{0}(\xi)
is bounded with norm

. Let
b(\xi)
denote the bounded extension of
b_{0}(\xi)
to
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{H})
and
t(\xi)=b(\xi)+b(\xi)^{\ast}
.

Lemma 4.10

If

and

Proof.

Since

If

\varphi(L_{\varphi}(\eta)^{\ast}L_{\varphi}(\xi)L_{\varphi}(\xi)^{\ast}L_{\varphi}(\eta))\leq\lVert L_{\varphi}(\xi)\rVert^{2}\varphi(L_{\varphi}(\eta)^{\ast}L_{\varphi}(\eta))<\infty.

Thus

. If
(y_{n})
is a sequence of self-adjoint elements in

\displaystyle L_{\varphi}(\xi)^{\ast}L_{\varphi}(\eta)\varphi^{1/2}=\lim_{n\to\infty}L_{\varphi}(\xi)^{\ast}L_{\varphi}(\eta)\varphi^{1/2}y_{n}=\lim_{n\to\infty}L_{\varphi}(\xi)^{\ast}\eta y_{n}=L_{\varphi}(\xi)^{\ast}\eta.

If

is entire analytic for

\displaystyle t(\eta)s(\xi)\varphi^{1/2}x

\displaystyle=t(\eta)\xi x

\displaystyle=\xi x\otimes_{\varphi}\eta+R_{\varphi}(\eta)^{\ast}\xi x

\displaystyle=\xi\otimes_{\varphi}\sigma^{\varphi}_{-i/2}(x)\eta+JL_{\varphi}(\mathcal{J}\eta)^{\ast}\mathcal{J}(\xi x)

\displaystyle=a(\xi)t(\eta)\sigma^{\varphi}_{-i/2}(x)\varphi^{1/2}+JL_{\varphi}(\mathcal{J}\eta)^{\ast}x^{\ast}L_{\varphi}(\mathcal{J}\xi)\varphi^{1/2}

\displaystyle=a(\xi)t(\eta)\sigma^{\varphi}_{-i/2}(x)\varphi^{1/2}+\varphi^{1/2}(\mathcal{J}\xi|x\mathcal{J}\eta)

\displaystyle=a(\xi)t(\eta)\sigma^{\varphi}_{-i/2}(x)\varphi^{1/2}+\sigma_{-i/2}^{\varphi}((\mathcal{J}\xi|(x\mathcal{J}\eta))\varphi^{1/2}

\displaystyle=a(\xi)t(\eta)\sigma^{\varphi}_{-i/2}(x)\varphi^{1/2}+(\mathcal{U}_{i/2}\mathcal{J}\xi|\sigma^{\varphi}_{-i/2}(x)\mathcal{U}_{-i/2}\mathcal{J}\eta)\varphi^{1/2}

\displaystyle=a(\xi)t(\eta)\sigma^{\varphi}_{-i/2}(x)\varphi^{1/2}+a(\xi)^{\ast}\sigma^{\varphi}_{-i/2}(x)L(\eta)\varphi^{1/2}

\displaystyle=a(\xi)t(\eta)\sigma^{\varphi}_{-i/2}(x)\varphi^{1/2}+a(\xi)^{\ast}t(\eta)\sigma^{\varphi}_{-i/2}(x)\varphi^{1/2}

By approximation, it follows that

. Commutation on

Lemma 4.14
.

If

is left-bounded, then
\varphi(L_{\varphi}(\xi)^{\ast}L_{\varphi}(\xi))=\lVert\xi\rVert^{2}

Proof.

Theorem 4.15
.

The weight
\hat{\varphi}
is semi-finite and faithful and the associated semi-cyclic representation is given by
H=\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{H})
,
\pi=\mathrm{id}
and
\Lambda(x)=L_{\varphi}^{-1}(xI)
for

. In particular, the conditional expectation
E
is faithful.

Proof.

Write
\pi_{l}\colon\mathcal{M}\to B(\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{H}))
for the left action. If
x\in\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}
, then

\hat{\varphi}(\pi_{l}(x)^{\ast}\pi_{l}(x))=\varphi(I^{\ast}\pi_{l}(x^{\ast}x)I)=\varphi(x^{\ast}x)<\infty.

Moreover, if
\xi\in L_{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}},\varphi)\cap\mathcal{H}_{a}
with
\mathcal{S}_{0}\xi=\xi

\hat{\varphi}(s(\xi)^{\ast}s(\xi))=\varphi(I^{\ast}s(\xi)^{2}I)=\varphi(L_{\varphi}(\xi)^{\ast}L_{\varphi}(\xi))<\infty.

Since
\mathfrak{n}_{\hat{\varphi}}
is an algebra, this implies that the algebra generated by
\pi_{l}(\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi})\cup\{s(\xi)\mid\xi\in L_{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}},\varphi),\,\mathcal{S}_{0}\xi=\xi\}
is contained in
\mathfrak{n}_{\hat{\varphi}}
. As the latter is dense in
\Gamma_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{H})
, this implies that
\hat{\varphi}
is semi-finite.

We show that
\hat{\varphi}
is faithful by proving that the associated semi-cyclic representation has the claimed form and the map
\Lambda
is injective.

First note that by definition if
x\in\mathfrak{n}_{\hat{\varphi}}
, then
xI
is a right module map and

\varphi((xI)^{\ast}(xI))=\hat{\varphi}(x^{\ast}x)<\infty.

Thus
\Lambda
is well-defined.

Next we show that
\Lambda
has dense image. If
x\in\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}
, then

x(\varphi^{1/2}y)=x\varphi^{1/2}y=L_{\varphi}(x\varphi^{1/2})\varphi^{1/2}y

for

. Thus
\Lambda(\pi_{l}(x))=x\varphi^{1/2}
. Hence
\Lambda(\mathfrak{n}_{\hat{\varphi}})\cap L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
is dense in
L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
.

If
\xi\in\mathcal{H}_{a}
is left-bounded and
\mathcal{S}_{0}\xi=\xi
, then

s(\xi)\varphi^{1/2}y=\xi y=L_{\varphi}(\xi)\varphi^{1/2}y

for
y\in\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}^{\ast}
. Thus
\Lambda(s(\xi))=\xi
. For arbitrary bounded
\xi\in\mathcal{H}_{a}
it follows that

\xi=\frac{1}{2}(\xi+\mathcal{S}_{0}\xi)+\frac{1}{2}(\xi-\mathcal{S}_{0}\xi)=\frac{1}{2}\Lambda(s(\xi-\mathcal{S}_{0}\xi))-\frac{i}{2}\Lambda(s(i(\xi-\mathcal{S}_{0}\xi))).

As the bounded vectors in
\mathcal{H}_{a}
are dense in
\mathcal{H}
by
Proposition

4.8
, it follows that
\Lambda(\mathfrak{n}_{\hat{\varphi}})\cap\mathcal{H}
is dense in
\mathcal{H}
.

Now we proceed by induction to show that
\Lambda(\mathfrak{n}_{\hat{\varphi}})\cap\mathcal{H}^{\otimes_{\varphi}n}
is dense in
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes_{\varphi}n}
. Assume it is already established for
k\leq n
. Let
\xi\in\mathcal{H}_{a}
be left-bounded with
\mathcal{S}_{0}\xi=\xi
and
\eta\in\mathcal{H}^{\otimes_{\varphi}n}
. By induction hypothesis there are sequences
(x_{n})
,
(y_{n})
in
\mathfrak{n}_{\hat{\varphi}}
such that
\Lambda(x_{n})\to\eta
and
\Lambda(y_{n})\to a^{\ast}(\xi)\eta
.

Hence

\displaystyle\xi\otimes\eta=s(\xi)\eta+a^{\ast}(\xi)\eta=\lim_{n\to\infty}s(\xi)\Lambda(x_{n})+\Lambda(y_{n})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\Lambda(s(\xi)x_{n}+y_{n}).

As above one concludes that
\Lambda(\mathfrak{n}_{\hat{\varphi}})
is dense in
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes_{\varphi}(n+1)}
. Since
\bigcup_{N\geq 1}(L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)\oplus\bigoplus_{n=1}^{N}\mathcal{H}^{\otimes_{\varphi}n})
is dense in
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{H})
, it follows that
\Lambda
has dense image. Clearly,

\langle\Lambda(x),\Lambda(y)\rangle=\varphi(L_{\varphi}(\Lambda(x))^{\ast}L_{\varphi}(\Lambda(y)))=\varphi((xI)^{\ast}(yI))=\hat{\varphi}(x^{\ast}y)

for
x,y\in\mathfrak{n}_{\hat{\varphi}}
.

Let
\pi_{r}\colon\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{H})
be the right action and
\mathcal{N}
the von Neumann algebra generated by
\pi_{r}(\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{op}})\cup\{t(\xi)\mid\xi\in L_{\infty}(_{\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{H},\varphi)\cap\mathcal{H}_{a},\,\mathcal{F}_{0}\xi=\xi\}
. By
Lemma

4.10
,
\mathcal{N}\subset\Gamma_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{H})^{\prime}
. As in the previous step one can show that
\mathcal{N}L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
is dense in
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{H})
.

If
x\in\Gamma_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{H})
with
\hat{\varphi}(x^{\ast}x)=0
, then
xI=0
since
\varphi
is faithful. In other words,
x\xi=0
for all
\xi\in L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
. As
\mathcal{N}\subset\Gamma_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{H})^{\prime}
, it follows that

0=yx\xi=xy\xi

for all
\xi\in L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
,
y\in\mathcal{N}
. Since
\mathcal{N}L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
is dense in
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{H})
, we conclude
x=0
.

Finally, if
x\in\Gamma_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{H})
with
E(x^{\ast}x)=0
, then
0=\varphi(E(x^{\ast}x))=\hat{\varphi}(x^{\ast}x)
. This implies
x=0
as we have just seen.
∎

Remark 4.16
.

Similar constructions to the one of
\Gamma_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{H})
have been explored before (usually in terms of
C^{\ast}
-bimodules instead of correspondences), most notably in Shlyakhtenko’s work operator-valued semicircular systems
[
Shl99
]
for a normal completely positive map and its extension to quantum Markov semigroups in
[
JRS
]
.

The central new element in our treatment is the use of the extra structure of
\mathcal{H}
in terms of
(\mathcal{U}_{z})
and
\mathcal{J}
, or equivalently,
\mathcal{S}
, which gives rise to a notion of “real vectors” in
\mathcal{H}
, combined with the insight the every GNS-symmetric quantum Markov semigroup gives rise to such extra structure. This ties the construction of
\Gamma_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{H})
closer to the definition of the free Araki–Woods factors from
[
Shl97
]
. In particular, this construction in terms of real vectors guarantees that the conditional expectation
E
on
\Gamma_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{H})
is always faithful (compare
[
Shl99
, Proposition 5.2]
).

5.
Derivations with values in Tomita bimodules

In this section we introduce the notion of symmetric derivations with values in a Tomita bimodule (
Definition

5.1
) and show that if a symmetric derivation is inner or can be approximated by inner derivations in a suitable sense, it gives rise to a GNS-symmetric completely Dirichlet form (
Theorem

5.7
).

Definition 5.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a Tomita algebra and
\mathcal{H}
a Tomita bimodule over
\mathfrak{A}
. A
symmetric derivation
is a linear map
\delta\colon\mathfrak{A}\to\mathcal{H}
such that

(a)

\delta(ab)=a\delta(b)+\delta(a)b
for all
a,b\in\mathfrak{A}
,

(b)

\delta(Ja)=\mathcal{J}\delta(a)
for all
a\in\mathfrak{A}
,

(c)

\delta(U_{z}a)=\mathcal{U}_{z}\delta(a)
for all
a\in\mathfrak{A}
,
z\in\mathbb{C}
.

A symmetric derivation is called closable (resp. bounded) if and only if it is closable (resp. bounded) as densely defined operator from the completion of
\mathfrak{A}
to the completion of
\mathcal{H}
. A symmetric derivation
\delta
is called
inner
if there exists a bounded vector
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
such that
\delta(a)=a\xi-\xi a
for all
a\in\mathfrak{A}
.

Recall that we introduced the notation
\xi^{\sharp}=\mathcal{J}\mathcal{U}_{-i/2}\xi
and
\xi^{\flat}=\mathcal{J}\mathcal{U}_{i/2}\xi
for
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
. With these definitions, the following lemma is immediate.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a Tomita algebra and
\mathcal{H}
a Tomita bimodule. If
\delta\colon\mathfrak{A}\to\mathcal{H}
is a symmetric derivation, then
\delta(a^{\sharp})=\delta(a)^{\sharp}
and
\delta(a^{\flat})=\delta(a)^{\flat}
for all
a\in\mathfrak{A}
.

If the Tomita bimodule is normal, the vector representing an inner symmetric derivation can be chosen (possibly in the completion of
\mathcal{H}
) so that it is invariant under
(\mathcal{U}_{z})
and
\mathcal{J}
. To prove this, we need some preparation. The key steps were already presented in
[
Wir22
]
, but since the result was proven only for derivations induced by quantum Markov semigroups and the case of weights needs some technical adjustments, we give a full proof here.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be an
\mathcal{M}
-
\mathcal{M}
-correspondence. The space
\mathcal{L}(L_{2}(\mathcal{M})_{\mathcal{M}},\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}})
is a
C^{\ast}
-bimodule over
\mathcal{M}
when equipped with the
\mathcal{M}
-valued inner product
(x,y)\mapsto x^{\ast}y
. In fact, since
\mathcal{L}(L_{2}(\mathcal{M})_{\mathcal{M}},\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}})
is strongly closed in
B(L_{2}(\mathcal{M}),\mathcal{H})
and the left action is normal, it is a von Neumann bimodule.

Recall that a von Neumann bimodule
F
has a unique (isometric) predual
[
Sch02
, Theorem 2.6]
. It can be realized
[
Pas73
, Proposition 3.8]
as
\overline{F}\otimes_{\pi}\mathcal{M}_{\ast}/\ker\iota
, where
\overline{F}
is the Banach space with the addition and norm from
F
and the scalar multiplication
(z,\xi)\mapsto\bar{z}\xi
,
\otimes_{\pi}
denotes the projective tensor product and
\iota(\xi\otimes\omega)=\omega((\xi|\cdot))
. The left and right action of
\mathcal{M}
on
F
are weak
∗
continuous.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be an
\mathcal{M}
-
\mathcal{M}
-correspondence and
\varphi
a normal semi-finite faithful weight on
\mathcal{M}
. For
\lambda,\mu>0
the set

C=\{L_{\varphi}(\xi)\mid\xi\in L_{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}},\varphi),\lVert\xi\rVert\leq\lambda,\lVert L_{\varphi}(\xi)\rVert\leq\mu\}

is weak
∗
-compact.

Proof.

By
Lemma

4.13
, the set
C
coincides with

\{x\in\mathcal{L}(L_{2}(\mathcal{M})_{\mathcal{M}},\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}})\mid\varphi(x^{\ast}x)\leq\lambda,\,\lVert x\rVert\leq\mu\},

which is weak
∗
precompact by the Banach-Alaoglu theorem.

Moreover,

\varphi(x^{\ast}x)=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\omega\in\mathcal{M}_{\ast}^{+}\\
\omega\leq\varphi\end{subarray}}\omega(x^{\ast}x)=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\omega\in\mathcal{M}_{\ast}^{+}\\
\omega\leq\varphi\end{subarray}}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in\mathcal{L}(L_{2}(\mathcal{M})_{\mathcal{M}},\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}})\\
\omega(y^{\ast}y)\leq 1\end{subarray}}\lvert\omega(x^{\ast}y)\rvert^{2}.

By the description of the predual given above, functionals of the form
x\mapsto\omega(y^{\ast}x)
with
y\in\mathcal{L}(L_{2}(\mathcal{M})_{\mathcal{M}},\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}})
and
\omega\in\mathcal{M}_{\ast}
are weak
∗
continuous. Thus
x\mapsto\varphi(x^{\ast}x)
is weak
∗
lower semicontinuous. Therefore
C
is weak
∗
closed.
∎

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a Tomita algebra and
\mathcal{H}
a normal Tomita bimodule over
\mathfrak{A}
. If
\delta\colon\mathfrak{A}\to\mathcal{H}
is a symmetric inner derivation, then there exists a vector
\xi\in\overline{\mathcal{H}}
such that
\mathcal{U}_{t}\xi=\xi
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
\mathcal{J}\xi=\xi
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{M}=\pi_{l}(\mathfrak{A})^{\prime\prime}
and
\varphi
the normal semi-finite faithful weight on
\mathcal{M}
induced by
\mathfrak{A}^{\prime\prime}
.

For
x,y\in\mathcal{L}(L_{2}(\mathcal{M})_{\mathcal{M}},\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{M}})
and
\omega\in\mathcal{M}_{\ast}
we have

\omega(y^{\ast}\mathcal{U}_{t}xU_{-t})=\omega(U_{t}(\mathcal{U}_{-t}yU_{t})^{\ast}xU_{-t}).

Since
x\mapsto U_{t}xU_{-t}
leaves
\mathcal{M}
invariant,
\omega(U_{t}\,\cdot\,U_{-t})\in\mathcal{M}_{\ast}
and
\lVert\omega(U_{t}\,\cdot\,U_{-t})\rVert=\lVert\omega\rVert
. Moreover,
\mathcal{U}_{-t}yU_{t}\in\mathcal{L}(L_{2}(\mathcal{M})_{\mathcal{M}},\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{M}})
and
\lVert\mathcal{U}_{-t}yU_{t}\rVert=\lVert y\rVert
. It follows from the description of the predual given above that
x\mapsto\mathcal{U}_{t}xU_{-t}
is a weak
∗
continuous map on
\mathcal{L}(L_{2}(\mathcal{M})_{\mathcal{M}},\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{M}})
.

For a bounded vector
\xi\in\overline{\mathcal{H}}
let
\delta_{\xi}\colon a\mapsto a\xi-\xi a
. By assumption there exists a bounded vector
\eta\in\mathcal{H}
such that
\delta=\delta_{\eta}
. Thus the set

C=\{L_{\varphi}(\xi)\mid\xi\in L_{\infty}(\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{M}},\varphi),\,\lVert\xi\rVert\leq\lVert\eta\rVert,\,\lVert L_{\varphi}(\xi)\rVert\leq\lVert L_{\varphi}(\eta)\rVert,\,\delta=\delta_{\xi}\}

is non-empty. By
Lemma

5.3
, it is weak
∗
-compact.

By the previous discussion,
x\mapsto\mathcal{U}_{t}xU_{-t}
is weak
∗
continuous. Note that by the definition of a Tomita algebra,
\mathcal{U}_{t}L_{\varphi}(\xi)U_{-t}=L_{\varphi}(\mathcal{U}_{t}\xi)
. Moreover, if
L_{\varphi}(\xi)\in C
and
a\in\mathfrak{A}
, then

\delta_{\mathcal{U}_{t}\xi}(a)=a\mathcal{U}_{t}\xi-(\mathcal{U}_{t}\xi)a=\mathcal{U}_{t}((U_{-t}a)\xi-\xi U_{-t}a)=\mathcal{U}_{t}\delta(U_{-t}a)=\delta(a).

Therefore,
x\mapsto\mathcal{U}_{t}xU_{-t}
leaves
C
invariant. It follows from the Ryll-Nardzewski fixed-point theorem that there exists
x\in C
such that
\mathcal{U}_{t}xU_{-t}=x
, or, in other words, a left-bounded vector
\xi\in\overline{\mathcal{H}}
such that
\mathcal{U}_{t}\xi=\xi
and
\delta_{\xi}=\delta
.

Since there exists a bounded vector
\eta\in\mathcal{H}
such that
\delta=\delta_{\eta}
, the derivation
\delta
is bounded from
\mathfrak{A}
to
\mathcal{H}
. Thus

\lVert a\xi\rVert\leq\lVert\delta(a)\rVert+\lVert\xi a\rVert\leq(\lVert\delta\rVert+\lVert\xi\rVert)\lVert a\rVert,

which means that
\xi
is right-bounded as well.

Finally, to ensure
\mathcal{J}\xi=\xi
, one can replace
\xi
by
\frac{1}{2}(\xi+\mathcal{J}\xi)
. Thus preserves bounded and invariance under
(\mathcal{U}_{t})
and still represents
\delta
by the property
\mathcal{J}\circ\delta=\delta\circ J
.
∎

For the following proposition note that if
\mathfrak{A}
is a Tomita algebra with Hilbert completion
\mathfrak{H}
and
\varphi
the associated weight on
\pi_{l}(\mathfrak{A})^{\prime\prime}
, then
a\mapsto\pi_{l}(a)\varphi^{1/2}
induces a unitary operator from
\mathfrak{H}
to
L_{2}(\pi_{l}(\mathfrak{A})^{\prime\prime},\varphi)
. Under this identification it makes sense to speak of GNS-symmetric quantum Markov semigroups on
\mathfrak{H}
.

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a Tomita algebra with completion
\mathfrak{H}
and
\mathcal{H}
a normal Tomita bimodule over
\mathfrak{A}
. If
\delta\colon\mathfrak{A}\to\mathcal{H}
is a symmetric inner derivation, then the quadratic form

\mathcal{E}\colon\mathfrak{A}\to[0,\infty),\,\mathcal{E}_{0}(a)=\lVert\delta(a)\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}

is bounded on
\mathfrak{H}
and its closure is a GNS-symmetric quantum Dirichlet form.

Proof.

Boundedness of
\mathcal{E}
follows immediately from the definition of an inner derivation. By
Proposition

5.4
there exists a bounded vector
\xi\in\overline{\mathcal{H}}
such that
\mathcal{U}_{t}\xi=\xi
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
\mathcal{J}\xi=\mathcal{J}
and
\delta(a)=a\xi-\xi a
for all
a\in\mathfrak{A}
. A direct computation shows

\mathcal{E}(a,b)=\langle(\xi|\xi)a+a(\xi|\xi)-2(\xi|L(a)\xi),b\rangle.

The operator

\pi_{l}(\mathfrak{A})^{\prime\prime}\to\pi_{l}(\mathfrak{A})^{\prime\prime},\,x\mapsto(\xi|\xi)x+x(\xi|\xi)-2(\xi|x\xi)

is conditionally negative definite, so that it generates a quantum Markov semigroup
(P_{t})
on
\mathcal{M}
by
[
EL77
, Theorem 14.7]
.

Since
\mathcal{U}_{t}\xi=\xi
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle\quad\;U_{t}((\xi|\xi)x+x(\xi|\xi)-2(\xi|x\xi))U_{-t}

\displaystyle=(\mathcal{U}_{t}\xi|\mathcal{U}_{t}\xi)U_{t}xU_{-t}+U_{t}xU_{-t}(\mathcal{U}_{t}\xi|\mathcal{U}_{t}\xi)-2(\mathcal{U}_{t}\xi,U_{t}xU_{-t}\cdot\mathcal{U}_{t}\xi)

\displaystyle=(\xi|\xi)U_{t}xU_{-t}+U_{t}xU_{-t}(\xi|\xi)-2(\xi|U_{t}xU_{-t}\cdot\xi).

Thus the semigroup
(P_{t})
commutes with the modular group on
\pi_{l}(\mathfrak{A})^{\prime\prime}
. Therefore the closure of
\mathcal{E}
is GNS-symmetric quantum Dirichlet form.
∎

Definition 5.6
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a Tomita algebra with completion
\mathfrak{H}
and
\mathcal{H}
a Tomita bimodule over
\mathfrak{A}
. A symmetric derivation
\delta\colon\mathfrak{A}\to\mathcal{H}
is called
nearly inner
if it is closable and there exists a sequence
(\mathcal{H}_{n})
of normal Tomita bimodules over
\mathfrak{A}
and a sequence
(\delta_{n})
of inner symmetric derivations from
\mathfrak{A}
to
\mathcal{H}_{n}
such that

\lVert\bar{\delta}_{n}(\xi)\rVert_{\mathcal{H}_{n}}\nearrow\begin{cases}\lVert\bar{\delta}(\xi)\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}&\text{if }\xi\in D(\bar{\delta}),\\
\infty&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a Tomita algebra and
\mathcal{H}
a Tomita bimodule over
\mathfrak{A}
. If
\delta\colon\mathfrak{A}\to\mathcal{H}
is a nearly inner symmetric derivation, then the quadratic form

\mathcal{E}_{0}\colon\mathfrak{A}\to[0,\infty),\,\mathcal{E}_{0}(a)=\lVert\delta(a)\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}

is closable and its closure is an GNS-symmetric completely Dirichlet form.

Proof.

Closability of
\mathcal{E}_{0}
follows directly from the closability of
\delta
. Clearly the closure
\mathcal{E}
is invariant under
(U_{t})
and
J
. If
\xi\in\mathfrak{H}
and
P
denotes the metric projection onto the closure of
\{a\in\mathfrak{A}\mid 0\leq\pi_{l}(a)\leq 1\}
, then

\mathcal{E}(P(\xi))=\lim_{n\to\infty}\lVert\bar{\delta}_{n}(P(\xi))\rVert^{2}\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\lVert\bar{\delta}_{n}(\xi)\rVert^{2}\leq\mathcal{E}(\xi),

where we used that
\eta\mapsto\lVert\delta_{n}(\eta)\rVert^{2}
is a Dirichlet form for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
by
Proposition

5.5
. The same argument applied to the matrix amplifications implies that
\mathcal{E}
is a completely Dirichlet form.
∎

Remark 5.8
.

If
\mathfrak{A}
is a unital Tomita algebra, then
\delta(1)=L(1)\delta(1)+R(1)\delta(1)=2\delta(1)
, hence
\delta(1)=0
. Therefore if
\delta
is nearly inner, the completely Dirichlet form induced by
\delta
is a quantum Dirichlet form. In general, this is not necessarily true, even in the commutative case. For example, if
(M,g)
is a complete Riemannian manifold,
\mathfrak{A}=C_{c}^{\infty}(M)
and
\delta=\nabla
, then the associated semigroup is the heat semigroup
(e^{t\Delta})
, which is not necessarily conservative (see
[
Gri99
, Section 3.2]
for example).

We note for later use that while the form
\mathcal{E}
is only defined in terms of inner product of elements in the range of
\delta
, it retains information about the inner product on the bimodule generated by the range of
\delta
due to the product rule.

Lemma 5.9
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a Tomita algebra and
\mathcal{H}
a Tomita bimodule over
\mathfrak{A}
. If
\delta\colon\mathfrak{A}\to\mathcal{H}
is a symmetric derivation and
\mathcal{E}(a)=\lVert\delta(a)\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}
, then

\langle\delta(a)b,\delta(c)d\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=\frac{1}{2}(\mathcal{E}(a,cdb^{\flat})+\mathcal{E}(abd^{\flat},c)-\mathcal{E}(bd^{\flat},a^{\sharp}c))

for all
a,b,c,d\in\mathfrak{A}
.

Proof.

Note that
\mathcal{E}(a,b)=\langle\delta(a),\delta(b)\rangle
for all
a,b\in\mathfrak{A}
by the polarization identity. Let
a,b,c,d\in\mathfrak{A}
. By the product rule and
Lemma

5.2
we have

\displaystyle\quad\;\mathcal{E}(a,cdb^{\flat})+\mathcal{E}(abd^{\flat},c)-\mathcal{E}(bd^{\flat},a^{\sharp}c)

\displaystyle=\langle\delta(a),\delta(c)db^{\flat}+c\delta(db^{\flat})\rangle+\langle\delta(a)bd^{\flat}+a\delta(bd^{\flat}),\delta(c)\rangle-\langle\delta(bd^{\flat}),\delta(a^{\sharp})c+a^{\sharp}\delta(c)\rangle

\displaystyle=2\langle\delta(a)b,\delta(c)d\rangle+\langle\delta(a),c\delta(db^{\flat})\rangle-\langle\delta(bd^{\flat}),\delta(a)^{\sharp}c\rangle.

For the last summand we have

\displaystyle\langle\delta(bd^{\flat}),\delta(a)^{\sharp}c\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\delta(bd^{\flat})c^{\flat},\delta(a)^{\sharp}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\mathcal{J}(\delta(a)^{\sharp}),\mathcal{J}(\delta(bd^{\flat})c^{\flat})\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\mathcal{U}_{-i/2}\delta(a),\mathcal{U}_{i/2}c\cdot\mathcal{J}(\delta(bd^{\flat}))\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\mathcal{U}_{-i/2}\delta(a),\mathcal{U}_{i/2}(c\delta(bd^{\flat})^{\flat})\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\delta(a),c\delta(db^{\flat})\rangle.

This gives the desired identity.
∎

6.
Symmetric derivations associated with quantum Dirichlet forms

In this section we prove the main result of this article, namely that every GNS-symmetric quantum Dirichlet form gives rise to a Tomita bimodule and derivation (Theorems
6.8
and
6.9
). In the case when the reference weight is finite, this yields a one-to-one correspondence between closable nearly inner symmetric derivations with maximal domain and GNS-symmetric quantum Dirichlet forms (
Corollary

6.10
).

One of the key challenges when constructing a derivation associated with a quantum Dirichlet form is that the domain of the derivation has to be an algebra for the product rule to make sense. In the tracially symmetric case it was proven by Davies and Lindsay
[
DL92
, Proposition 3.4]
that
D(\mathcal{E})\cap\mathcal{M}
is an algebra, and the derivation constructed by Cipriani and Sauvageot
[
CS03a
]
can be defined on this algebra.

Here we propose

\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}=\{a\in\mathfrak{A}_{\varphi}\mid U_{z}a\in D(\mathcal{E})\text{ for all }z\in\mathbb{C}\}

as a replacement for
D(\mathcal{E})\cap\mathcal{M}
in the non-tracial case. We show that
\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
is a Tomita algebra and a core for
\mathcal{E}
(
Theorem

6.3
). If
\varphi
is a trace, then
\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
coincides with
D(\mathcal{E})\cap\mathcal{M}
.

Once this is established, the construction of the Tomita bimodule
\mathcal{H}
and the derivation
\delta
can be summarized as follows: The pre-Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
consists of linear combinations of the (formal) elements
\delta(a)b
with the inner product determined by
Lemma

5.9
, the left and right action of
\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
are the ones suggested by the product rule and
\mathcal{U}_{z}
and
\mathcal{J}
are uniquely determined by the definition of a Tomita bimodule.

Since the inner product determined by
Lemma

5.9
is in general degenerate, the difficulty lies in proving that all these operations are well-defined. To prove this as well as the fact that
\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
is a Tomita algebra, we crucially rely on approximation by bounded Dirichlet forms and the construction from
[
Wir22
]
(upgraded here to cover the case of weights).

6.1.
The bounded case

In the case when
\varphi
is finite, it was shown in
[
Wir22
]
that every bounded GNS-symmetric quantum Dirichlet form
\mathcal{E}
can be written as

\mathcal{E}(\Lambda_{\varphi}(x),\Lambda_{\varphi}(y))=\varphi((\partial(x)|\partial(y)),

where
\partial
is a derivation with values in a von Neumann bimodule
F
that carries some extra structure reflecting the GNS symmetry, similar to the definition of Tomita bimodules and correspondences in the present article. In this subsection we extend this construction to the case of general weights and show how it relates to our formulation in terms of Tomita bimodules.

We will rely on the following interplay between correspondences and von Neumann bimodules (see
[
Wir22
, Proposition 3.1]
in the case of a finite weight).

Proposition 6.1
.

If
\mathcal{M}
is a von Neumann algebra and
\mathcal{H}
is an
\mathcal{M}
-
\mathcal{M}
-correspondence, then
\mathcal{L}(L_{2}(\mathcal{M})_{\mathcal{M}},\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}})
with the usual left and right actions and the
\mathcal{M}
-valued inner product
(x|y)=x^{\ast}y
is a von Neumann
\mathcal{M}
-bimodule.

Moreover, the map

\Phi\colon\mathcal{L}(L_{2}(\mathcal{M})_{\mathcal{M}},\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}})\bar{\odot}L_{2}(\mathcal{M})\to\mathcal{H},\,x\otimes\xi\mapsto x\xi

is a unitary bimodule map.

Conversely, if
F
is a von Neumann
\mathcal{M}
-bimodule, then
F\bar{\odot}L_{2}(\mathcal{M})
is an
\mathcal{M}
-
\mathcal{M}
-correspondence and the map

\Psi\colon F\mapsto\mathcal{L}(L_{2}(\mathcal{M})_{\mathcal{M}},(F\bar{\odot}L_{2}(\mathcal{M}))_{\mathcal{M}}),\,\xi\mapsto a(\xi)

is a bijective bimodule map that preserves the
\mathcal{M}
-valued inner products.

Proof.

Clearly
\mathcal{L}(L_{2}(\mathcal{M})_{\mathcal{M}},\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}})
is a
C^{\ast}

\mathcal{M}
-bimodule with the described operations. Since it is strongly dense in
B(L_{2}(\mathcal{M}),\mathcal{H})
, it is a von Neumann
\mathcal{M}
-bimodule. We have

\langle x\xi,y\eta\rangle=\langle\xi,x^{\ast}y\eta\rangle=\langle x\otimes\xi,y\otimes\eta\rangle.

Thus
\Phi
is isometric. By
[
Tak03
, Lemma IX.3.3]
it has dense range. Thus
\Phi
is unitary.

For the converse it is clear that
F\bar{\odot}L_{2}(\mathcal{M})
is an
\mathcal{M}
-
\mathcal{M}
-correspondence and
\Psi
is a bimodule map that preserves the
\mathcal{M}
-valued inner products. It remains to show that
\Psi
is surjective. Let
x\in\mathcal{L}(L_{2}(\mathcal{M})_{\mathcal{M}},\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}})
and define

T\colon F\to\mathcal{M},\,y\mapsto x^{\ast}a(y).

As
T
is a right module map and von Neumann bimodules are self-dual by
[
Ske01
, Theorem 3.2.11]
, there exists
z\in F
such that

x^{\ast}a(y)=T(y)=(z|y)=a(z)^{\ast}a(y).

Hence
x=a(z)
.
∎

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a von Neumann algebra and
\varphi
a normal semi-finite faithful weight on
\mathcal{M}
. If
\Phi\colon\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}
is a GNS-symmetric normal unital completely positive map, then there exists a normal Tomita bimodule
\mathcal{H}
over
\mathfrak{A}_{\varphi}
and a symmetric derivation
\delta\colon\mathfrak{A}_{\varphi}\to\mathcal{H}
such that

(2)

\langle\delta(a),\delta(a)\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=\langle a,b-\Phi^{(2)}(b)\rangle

for all
a,b\in\mathfrak{A}_{\varphi}
. If
\varphi
is finite, then
\delta
can be chosen inner.

Proof.

First let
F
be the GNS von Neumann bimodule (or Stinespring bimodule) associated with
\Phi
. It is constructed as follows. On
\{\sum_{j}x_{j}\otimes y_{j}\mid\sum_{j}x_{j}y_{j}=0\}
define an
\mathcal{M}
-valued sesquilinear form by

\left\langle\sum_{j}x_{j}\otimes y_{j},\sum_{k}\tilde{x}_{k}\otimes\tilde{y}_{k}\right\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j,k}y_{j}^{\ast}\Phi(x_{j}^{\ast}\tilde{x}_{k})\tilde{y}_{k}.

Let
F_{0}
be the
C^{\ast}

\mathcal{M}
-bimodule obtained after separation and completion with respect to
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{F}=\lVert(\cdot|\cdot)\rVert^{1/2}
. Then
F
is the strong closure of
F_{0}
inside
B(L_{2}(\mathcal{M}),F_{0}\bar{\odot}L_{2}(\mathcal{M}))
. We write
\sum_{j}x_{j}\otimes_{\Phi}y_{j}
for the image of
\sum_{j}x_{j}\otimes y_{j}
in
F
.

The map

\partial\colon\mathcal{M}\to F,\,x\mapsto x\otimes_{\Phi}1-1\otimes_{\Phi}x

is a derivation and

(\partial(x)|\partial(y))=\frac{1}{2}((I-\Phi)(x)^{\ast}y+x^{\ast}(I-\Phi)(y)-(I-\Phi)(x^{\ast}y)).

As in
[
Wir22
, Proposition 4.4]
one checks that the map

\sum_{j}x_{j}\otimes_{\Phi}y_{j}\mapsto\sum_{j}\sigma^{\varphi}_{t}(x_{j})\otimes_{\Phi}\sigma^{\varphi}_{t}(y_{j})

is well-defined and extends to a weak
∗
continuous linear map
V_{t}
on
F
that satisfies
V_{s}V_{t}=V_{s+t}
,
V_{t}(x\xi y)=\sigma^{\varphi}_{t}(x)V_{t}\xi\sigma^{\varphi}_{t}(y)
and
(V_{t}\xi|V_{t}\eta)=\sigma^{\varphi}_{t}((\xi|\eta))
. Since
\Phi
is normal, a direct computation shows that
V_{t}\xi\to\xi
strongly as
t\to 0
if
\xi=\sum_{j}x_{j}\otimes_{\Phi}y_{j}
. For arbitrary
\xi\in F
this follows from the strong density of elements of this form in
F
by standard arguments.

Let
\mathcal{K}=F\bar{\odot}L_{2}(\mathcal{M})
,
\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}(F)=\{\xi\in F\mid\varphi((\xi|\xi))<\infty\}
and

\Lambda\colon\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}(F)\to\mathcal{K},\,\xi\mapsto L_{\varphi}^{-1}(a(\xi)).

Since
\varphi((\xi|\xi))=\varphi(a(\xi)^{\ast}a(\xi))
, this map is well-defined by
Lemma

4.13
. Moreover, it has dense range by
[
Tak03
, Lemma IX.3.3]
. If
x\in\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}
, then

\displaystyle\varphi((\partial(x)|\partial(x)))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\varphi((I-\Phi)(x)^{\ast}x+x^{\ast}(I-\Phi)(x)-(I-\Phi)(x^{\ast}x))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\varphi((I-\Phi)(x)^{\ast}x+x^{\ast}(I-\Phi)(x))

\displaystyle=\varphi(x^{\ast}(x-\Phi(x))

\displaystyle=\langle x\varphi^{1/2},(I-\Phi^{(2)})x\varphi^{1/2}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\lVert I-\Phi^{(2)}\rVert\varphi(x^{\ast}x).

Thus
\partial(x)\in\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}(F)
. If we set
\delta(x\varphi^{1/2})=\Lambda(\partial(x))
, then (
2
) follows from the last computation by polarization.

For
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in F
and
\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\in L_{2}(\mathcal{M})
we have

\displaystyle\langle V_{t}\xi_{1}\otimes\Delta_{\varphi}^{it}\eta_{1},V_{t}\xi_{2}\otimes\Delta_{\varphi}^{it}\eta_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\Delta_{\varphi}^{it}\eta_{1},(V_{t}\xi_{1}|V_{t}\xi_{2})\Delta_{\varphi}^{it}\eta_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\Delta_{\varphi}^{it}\eta_{1},\sigma^{\varphi}_{t}((\xi_{1}|\xi_{2}))\Delta_{\varphi}^{it}\eta_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\eta_{1},(\xi_{1}|\xi_{2})\eta_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\xi_{1}\otimes\eta_{1},\xi_{2}\otimes\eta_{2}\rangle.

Thus the map
\xi\otimes\eta\mapsto V_{t}\xi\otimes\Delta_{\varphi}^{it}\eta
extends to a unitary operator
\mathcal{U}_{t}
on
\mathcal{K}
. The pointwise strong continuity of
(V_{t})
and
(\Delta_{\varphi}^{it})
implies the strong continuity of
(\mathcal{U}_{t})
.

If
x_{j},y_{j}\in\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}\cap\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}^{\ast}
are entire analytic with
\sum_{j}x_{j}y_{j}=0
we have

\displaystyle\quad\;\left\lVert\Lambda\left(\sum_{j}\sigma^{\varphi}_{i/2}(y_{j})^{\ast}\otimes_{\Phi}\sigma^{\varphi}_{i/2}(x_{j})^{\ast}\right)\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j,k}\varphi((\sigma^{\varphi}_{i/2}(y_{j})^{\ast}\otimes_{\Phi}\sigma^{\varphi}_{i/2}(x_{j})^{\ast}|\sigma^{\varphi}_{i/2}(y_{k})^{\ast}\otimes_{\Phi}\sigma^{\varphi}_{i/2}(x_{k})^{\ast}))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j,k}\varphi(\sigma^{\varphi}_{i/2}(x_{j})\Phi(\sigma^{\varphi}_{i/2}(y_{j})\sigma^{\varphi}_{i/2}(y_{k})^{\ast})\sigma^{\varphi}_{i/2}(x_{k})^{\ast})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j,k}\varphi(\Phi(\sigma^{\varphi}_{i/2}(y_{j})\sigma^{\varphi}_{-i/2}(y_{k}^{\ast}))\sigma^{\varphi}_{-i/2}(x_{k}^{\ast}x_{j}))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j,k}\varphi(\sigma^{\varphi}_{i/2}(y_{j})\sigma^{\varphi}_{-i/2}(y_{k}^{\ast})\sigma^{\varphi}_{-i/2}(\Phi(x_{k}^{\ast}x_{j})))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j,k}\varphi(\sigma^{\varphi}_{-i/2}(y_{k}^{\ast}\Phi(x_{k}^{\ast}x_{j})y_{j}))

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\Lambda\left(\sum_{j}x_{j}\otimes_{\Phi}y_{j}\right)\right\rVert^{2},

where we used the GNS-symmetry of
\Phi
and in particular the commutation with the modular group.

Thus

\Lambda\left(\sum_{j}x_{j}\otimes_{\Phi}y_{j}\right)\mapsto\Lambda\left(\sum_{j}\sigma^{\varphi}_{i/2}(y_{j})^{\ast}\otimes_{\Phi}\sigma^{\varphi}_{i/2}(x_{j})^{\ast}\right)

is well-defined and extends to an anti-unitary operator
\mathcal{J}
on
\mathcal{K}
. It is not hard to check that
(\mathcal{U}_{t})
and
\mathcal{J}
make
\mathcal{K}
into an
\mathcal{M}
-
\mathcal{M}
-Tomita correspondence. Clearly,
\delta(a)
is bounded and entire analytic for
(\mathcal{U}_{t})
for every
a\in\mathfrak{A}_{\varphi}
. Hence the Tomita bimodule
\mathcal{H}
constructed from
\mathcal{K}
by means of
Proposition

4.8
does the job.

By
[
CE79
, Theorem 2.1]
there exists
\xi\in F
such that
\partial(x)=x\xi-\xi x
. If
\varphi
is finite, then
\Lambda(\xi)
is a well-defined left-bounded vector in
\mathcal{K}
. For analytic
x\in\mathcal{M}
we have

L(x\varphi^{1/2})\xi-R(x\varphi^{1/2})\xi=\Lambda(x\xi)-\Lambda(x)\sigma^{\varphi}_{-i/2}(x)=\Lambda(x\xi-\xi x).

Thus
L(a)\xi-R(a)\xi=\delta(a)
for all
a\in\mathfrak{A}_{\varphi}
. Since
\delta
is bounded,
\xi
is also right-bounded. In this case we can take the Tomita bimodule generated by
\mathcal{H}
and
\xi
instead of
\mathcal{H}
.
∎

6.2.
The Tomita algebra
\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}

As announced before, we now turn to the domain of definition of the derivation associated with the quantum Dirichlet form
\mathcal{E}
.

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a von Neumann algebra and
\varphi
a normal semi-finite faithful weight on
\mathcal{M}
. If
\mathcal{E}
is a GNS-symmetric quantum Dirichlet form on
L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
, then

\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}=\{a\in\mathfrak{A}_{\varphi}\mid U_{z}a\in D(\mathcal{E})\text{ for all }z\in\mathbb{C}\}

is a Tomita subalgebra of
\mathfrak{A}_{\varphi}
and

\mathcal{E}(ab)^{1/2}\leq\lVert\pi_{l}(a)\rVert\mathcal{E}(b)^{1/2}+\mathcal{E}(a)^{1/2}\lVert\pi_{r}(b)\rVert

for all
a,b\in\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
.

For every
a\in D(\mathcal{E})\cap\Lambda_{\varphi}(\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}\cap\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}^{\ast})
there exists a sequence
(a_{n})
in
\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
such that

•

a_{n}\to a
,
a_{n}^{\sharp}\to a^{\sharp}
in
(D(\mathcal{E}),\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\mathcal{E}})
,

•

\lVert\pi_{l}(a_{n})\rVert\leq\lVert\pi_{l}(a)\rVert
,

•

\pi_{l}(a_{n})\to\pi_{l}(a)
in the strong
∗
topology.

In particular,
\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
is a core for
\mathcal{E}
and
\{x\in\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}\cap\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}^{\ast}\mid\Lambda_{\varphi}(x)\in\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}\}\cap\mathcal{M}_{1}
is strongly
∗
dense in
\mathcal{M}_{1}
.

Proof.

Let
(P_{t})
denote the quantum dynamical semigroup associated with
\mathcal{E}
. By
Proposition

6.2
for every
t\geq 0
there exists a normal Tomita bimodule
\mathcal{H}_{t}
over
\mathfrak{A}_{\varphi}
and a symmetric derivation
\delta_{t}\colon\mathfrak{A}_{\varphi}\to\mathcal{H}_{t}
such that

\lVert\delta_{t}(a)\rVert_{\mathcal{H}_{t}}^{2}=\frac{1}{t}\langle a-P_{t}^{(2)}(a),a\rangle

for all
a\in\mathfrak{A}_{\varphi}
.

Thus, if
a,b\in\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
, then

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t}\langle ab-P_{t}^{(2)}(ab),ab\rangle

\displaystyle=\lVert\delta_{t}(ab)\rVert_{\mathcal{H}_{t}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\lVert a\delta_{t}(b)+\delta_{t}(a)b\rVert_{\mathcal{H}_{t}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(\lVert\pi_{l}(a)\rVert\lVert\delta_{t}(b)\rVert_{\mathcal{H}_{t}}+\lVert\delta_{t}(a)\rVert_{\mathcal{H}_{t}}\lVert\pi_{r}(b)\rVert)^{2}.

By the spectral theorem, the left side converges to
\mathcal{E}(ab)
, while the right side converges to
(\lVert\pi_{l}(a)\rVert\mathcal{E}(b)^{1/2}+\mathcal{E}(a)^{1/2}\lVert\pi_{r}(b)\rVert)^{2}
. Therefore
ab\in D(\mathcal{E})
and the claimed inequality from the theorem holds. Replacing
a
and
b
by
U_{z}a
and
U_{z}b
, respectively, one sees that
U_{z}(ab)\in D(\mathcal{E})
. Thus
ab\in\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
.

Clearly,
\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
is invariant under
J
and
U_{z}
. The only property missing for a Tomita algebra is the density of
\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}
in
\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
. We will prove this later.

The density properties follow from a standard approximation argument (see
[
Haa75
, Lemma 1.3]
for example). For
a\in D(\mathcal{E})\cap\Lambda_{\varphi}(\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}\cap\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}^{\ast})
let

a_{n}=\sqrt{\frac{n}{\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-nt^{2}}\Delta_{\varphi}^{it}a\,dt.

Then
a_{n}\in\mathfrak{A}_{\varphi}
with

U_{z}a_{n}=\sqrt{\frac{n}{\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-n(t-z)^{2}}\Delta_{\varphi}^{it}a\,dt

and
a_{n}\to a,a_{n}^{\sharp}\to a^{\sharp}
in
L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
,
\lVert\pi_{l}(a_{n})\rVert\leq\lVert\pi_{l}(a)\rVert
and
\pi_{l}(a_{n})\to\pi_{l}(a)
in the strong
∗
topology. By Jensen’s inequality for vector-valued functions
[
Per74
, Theorem 3.10]
and the
(U_{t})
-invariance of
\mathcal{E}
we get

\mathcal{E}(U_{z}a_{n})\leq\sqrt{\frac{n}{\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-n(t-\mathrm{Re}z)^{2}}e^{n(\mathrm{Im}z)^{2}}\mathcal{E}(a)\,dt\leq e^{n(\mathrm{Im}z)^{2}}\mathcal{E}(a).

Thus
U_{z}a_{n}\in D(\mathcal{E})
and
z\mapsto\mathcal{E}(U_{z}a_{n})
is locally bounded. This means
a_{n}\in\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
.

Since
\mathcal{E}(a_{n})\leq\mathcal{E}(a)
, there exists a subsequence of
(a_{n})
that converges weakly in
(D(\mathcal{E}),\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{E}})
. As
a_{n}\to a
in
L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
, any subsequential weak limit of
(a_{n})
in
(D(\mathcal{E}),\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{E}})
is necessarily
a
. Hence
(a_{n})
itself converges weakly to
a
. Finally,
\mathcal{E}
is weakly lower semicontinuous by the Hahn–Banach theorem, so that

\mathcal{E}(a)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{E}(a_{n})\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{E}(a_{n})\leq\mathcal{E}(a).

This implies that
a_{n}\to a
strongly in
(D(\mathcal{E}),\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
.

That
\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
is a core for
\mathcal{E}
now follows from the fact that
\mathfrak{A}_{\varphi}\cap D(\mathcal{E})
is a core for
\mathcal{E}
by
Lemma

3.6
or from an application of Lemma
3.4
. The last density statement follows since
\{x\in\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}\cap\mathfrak{n}_{\varphi}^{\ast}\mid\Lambda_{\varphi}(x)\in D(\mathcal{E})\}\cap\mathcal{M}_{1}
is strongly
∗
dense in
\mathcal{M}_{1}
by
Lemma

3.7
.

In particular, there exists a sequence
a_{n}\in\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
such that
\pi_{l}(a_{n})\to 1
strongly. Thus if
b\in\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
, then
a_{n}b=\pi_{l}(a_{n})b\to b
. Hence
\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}
is dense in
\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
, which completes the proof that
\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
is a Tomita algebra.
∎

Definition 6.4
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a von Neumann algebra and
\varphi
a normal semi-finite faithful weight on
\mathcal{M}
. If
\mathcal{E}
is a GNS-symmetric quantum Dirichlet form on
L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
, then

\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}=\{a\in\mathfrak{A}_{\varphi}\mid U_{z}a\in D(\mathcal{E})\text{ for all }z\in\mathbb{C}\}

is called the
Tomita algebra associated with
\mathcal{E}
.

In the definition of
\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
, the map
t\mapsto U_{t}a
is only assumed to have an extension to
\mathbb{C}
that is analytic with respect to the
L_{2}
norm. Since
U_{t}a\in D(\mathcal{E})
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, one could also require that the extension is analytic with respect to the form norm
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\mathcal{E}}
. As it turns out, this is automatically true for
a\in\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
. We prove this in two steps.

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
H
be a Hilbert space,
A
,
B
strongly commuting positive self-adjoint operators on
H
with
B
non-singular. Let
\langle\xi,\eta\rangle_{A}=\langle\xi,\eta\rangle+\langle A\xi,A\eta\rangle
for
\xi,\eta\in D(A)
. The restriction of
(B^{it})
to
D(A)
is a strongly continuous unitary group with respect to
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{A}
, the operator
\tilde{B}
that is the restriction of
B
to
D(\tilde{B})=\{\xi\in D(A)\cap D(B)\mid B\xi\in D(A)\}
is non-singular, positive and self-adjoint with respect to
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{A}
, and
B^{it}|_{D(A)}=\tilde{B}^{it}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

Since
A
and
B
are strongly commuting self-adjoint operators, we can assume by the spectral theorem that
A
is the operator of multiplication by
f
and
B
is the operator of multiplication by
g
on
L_{2}(X,\mu)
for some measure space
(X,\mathcal{F},\mu)
and measurable functions
f,g\colon X\to[0,\infty)
with
g>0
a.e. Then
D(A)=L_{2}(X,(1+f^{2})\mu)
,
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{A}
is the
L_{2}
-inner product on
D(A)
, and
\tilde{B}
is the operator of multiplication by
g
on
L_{2}(X,(1+f^{2})\mu)
. From this, all claims follow easily.
∎

Lemma 6.6
.

If
\mathcal{E}
is a GNS-symmetric quantum Dirichlet form on
L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
and
a\in\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
, then
z\mapsto\mathcal{E}(b,U_{z}a)
is entire analytic for all
b\in D(\mathcal{E})
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{E}
is a GNS-symmetric quantum Dirichlet form, the operators
A=\mathcal{L}_{(2)}^{1/2}
and
B=\Delta_{\varphi}
commute strongly. Let
\tilde{B}
be as in
Lemma

6.5
. By
[
Tak03
, Theorem VI.2.2]
, if

, then

and
\Delta_{\varphi}^{n}a=U_{-in}a\in D(\mathcal{E})
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Thus
a\in\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}D(\tilde{B}^{n})
. By
[
Tak03
, Lemma VI.2.3]
, the map

has an entire analytic continuation with values in
(D(\mathcal{E}),\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{E}})
. Since
\tilde{B}^{it}a=\Delta_{\varphi}^{it}a
for
t\in\mathbb{R}

Lemma

6.5
, the claim follows from the uniqueness of analytic continuations.
∎

.

Lemma 6.7

(P_{t})

Theorem 6.8
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a von Neumann algebra and
\varphi
a normal semi-finite faithful weight on
\mathcal{M}
. If
\mathcal{E}
is a
(U_{t})
-invariant quantum Dirichlet form on

, then there exists a Tomita bimodule
\mathcal{H}
over
\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
and a symmetric derivation
\delta\colon\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}\to\mathcal{H}
such that

\langle\delta(a),\delta(b)\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=\mathcal{E}(a,b)

for all
a,b\in\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
. If
\varphi
is finite, then
\delta
can be chosen nearly inner.

Proof.

Step 1:
Construction of the auxiliary Hilbert space
\mathcal{K}
:

On
\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}\odot\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
define a sesquilinear form by

\langle a\otimes b,c\otimes d\rangle_{\mathcal{K}}=\frac{1}{2}(\mathcal{E}(a,cdb^{\flat})+\mathcal{E}(abd^{\flat},c)-\mathcal{E}(bd^{\flat},a^{\sharp}c)).

For
t\geq 0
let
\mathcal{E}_{t}(a)=\frac{1}{t}\langle a,a-P_{t}^{(2)}(a)\rangle
. It follows from
Proposition

6.2
that there exists a normal Tomita bimodule
\mathcal{K}_{t}
and a symmetric derivation
\delta_{t}\colon\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}\to\mathcal{K}
such that
\mathcal{E}_{t}(a)=\lVert\delta_{t}(a)\rVert^{2}
. Since
\mathcal{E}_{t}(a,b)\to\mathcal{E}(a,b)
as
t\searrow 0
for all
a,b\in\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
, we deduce from
Lemma

5.9
that

\displaystyle\langle\delta_{t}(a)b,\delta_{t}(c)d\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(\mathcal{E}_{t}(a,cdb^{\flat})+\mathcal{E}_{t}(abd^{\flat},c)-\mathcal{E}_{t}(bd^{\flat},a^{\sharp}c))

\displaystyle\to\frac{1}{2}(\mathcal{E}(a,cdb^{\flat})+\mathcal{E}(abd^{\flat},c)-\mathcal{E}(bd^{\flat},a^{\sharp}c))

\displaystyle=\langle a\otimes b,c\otimes d\rangle_{\mathcal{K}}

as
t\searrow 0
for all
a,b,c,d,\in\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
. In particular,

\left\langle\sum_{j}a_{j}\otimes b_{j},\sum_{k}a_{k}\otimes b_{k}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{K}}=\lim_{t\to 0}\left\lVert\sum_{j}\delta_{t}(a_{j})b_{j}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{K}_{t}}^{2}\geq 0

for all
a_{j},b_{j}\in\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
.

Thus
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{K}}
is a semi inner product on
\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}\odot\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
. Let
\mathcal{K}
denote the Hilbert space obtained from
\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}\odot\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
after separation and completion with respect to this semi inner product and write
a\otimes_{\mathcal{E}}b
for the image of
a\otimes b
in
\mathcal{K}
.

Step 2:
For all
a\in\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
the map
b\otimes_{\mathcal{E}}c\mapsto ab\otimes_{\mathcal{E}}c-a\otimes_{\mathcal{E}}bc
extends to a bounded linear operator
L(a)
on
\mathcal{K}
with
\lVert L(a)\rVert\leq\lVert\pi_{l}(a)\rVert
:

For
b_{j},c_{j}\in\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert\sum_{j}ab_{j}\otimes_{\mathcal{E}}c_{j}-a\otimes_{\mathcal{E}}b_{j}c_{j}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{K}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\searrow 0}\left\lVert\sum_{j}\delta_{t}(ab_{j})c_{j}-\delta_{t}(a)b_{j}c_{j}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\searrow 0}\left\lVert\sum_{j}a\delta_{t}(b_{j})c_{j}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\pi_{l}(a)\rVert^{2}\lim_{t\searrow 0}\left\lVert\sum_{j}\delta_{t}(b_{j})c_{j}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\lVert\pi_{l}(a)\rVert^{2}\left\lVert\sum_{j}a_{j}\otimes_{\mathcal{E}}b_{j}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{K}}^{2}.

Similarly one proves that for all
a\in\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
the map
b\otimes_{\mathcal{E}}c\mapsto b\otimes_{\mathcal{E}}ca
extends to a bounded linear operator
R(a)
on
\mathcal{K}
with
\lVert R(a)\rVert\leq\lVert\pi_{r}(a)\rVert
. It is then not hard to check that
L(a)
and
R(a)
are adjointable operators and
L
,
R
are commuting
\ast
-representations of
(\mathfrak{A},^{\sharp})
and
(\mathfrak{A}^{\mathrm{op}},^{\flat})
, respectively.

Step 3:
For every
a\in\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
the linear map
\psi_{a}\colon\mathcal{K}\to\mathbb{C}
determined by

\psi_{a}(b\otimes_{\mathcal{E}}c)=\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{E}(a,bc)+\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{E}(ac^{\flat},b)-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{E}(c^{\flat},a^{\sharp}b)

is bounded with
\lVert\psi_{a}\rVert\leq\mathcal{E}(a)^{1/2}
:

Again one can argue by approximation and get

\left\lvert\psi_{a}\left(\sum_{j}b_{j}\otimes_{\mathcal{E}}c_{j}\right)\right\rvert=\lim_{t\to 0}\left\lvert\left\langle\delta_{t}(a),\sum_{j}\delta_{t}(b_{j})c_{j}\right\rangle\right\rvert\leq\mathcal{E}(a)^{1/2}\left\lVert\sum_{j}b_{j}\otimes_{\mathcal{E}}c_{j}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{K}}.

Let
\delta(a)
denote the unique vector in
\mathcal{K}
such that
\psi_{a}=\langle\delta(a),\cdot\,\rangle
.

Step 4:
For all
a,b\in\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
we have
R(b)\delta(a)=a\otimes_{\mathcal{E}}b
and
R
is a non-degenerate representation:

For
c,d\in\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
we have

\displaystyle\langle R(b)\delta(a),c\otimes_{\mathcal{E}}d\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\delta(a),c\otimes db^{\flat}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(\mathcal{E}(a,cdb^{\flat})+\mathcal{E}(abd^{\flat},c)-\mathcal{E}(bd^{\flat},a^{\sharp}b))

\displaystyle=\langle a\otimes_{\mathcal{E}}b,c\otimes_{\mathcal{E}}d\rangle.

Thus
R(b)\delta(a)=a\otimes_{\mathcal{E}}b
. Since the linear span of elements of the form
a\otimes_{\mathcal{E}}b
with
a,b\in\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
is dense in
\mathcal{K}
by definition,
R
is non-generate.

Step 5:
For all
a,b\in\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
we have
\delta(ab)=L(a)\delta(b)+R(b)\delta(a)
:

By Step 4, if
c\in\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
, then

\displaystyle R(c)\delta(ab)

\displaystyle=ab\otimes_{\mathcal{E}}c

\displaystyle=L(a)(b\otimes_{\mathcal{E}}c)+R(c)(a\otimes_{\mathcal{E}}b)

\displaystyle=L(a)R(c)\delta(b)+R(c)R(b)\delta(a)

\displaystyle=R(c)(L(a)\delta(b)+R(b)\delta(a)).

Since
R
is non-degenerate again by Step 4, we conclude
\delta(ab)=L(a)\delta(b)+R(b)\delta(a)
.

Step 6:
For all
a\in\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
we have
\lVert\delta(a)\rVert_{\mathcal{K}}^{2}=\mathcal{E}(a)
:

We first prove the identity for
a\in\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}\cap D(\mathcal{L}^{(2)})
. By
Lemma

6.7
there exists a sequence
(b_{n})
in
\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
such that
b_{n}=b_{n}^{\flat}
,
\lVert\pi_{r}(b_{n})\rVert\leq 1
,
\pi_{r}(b_{n})\to 1
strongly, and
\mathcal{E}(b_{n},c^{\sharp}d)\to 0
for all
c,d\in\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
. Moreover,

\mathcal{E}(a,ab_{n})=\langle\mathcal{L}^{(2)}(a),ab_{n}\rangle=\langle\mathcal{L}^{(2)}(a),\pi_{r}(b_{n})a\rangle\to\mathcal{E}(a).

Combining this with Step 4, we get

\displaystyle\lVert\delta(a)\rVert_{\mathcal{K}}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\langle\delta(a),\delta(a)b_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle=\psi_{a}(a\otimes_{\mathcal{E}}b_{n})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\lvert\mathcal{E}(a,ab_{n})+\mathcal{E}(ab_{n},a)-\mathcal{E}(b_{n},a^{\sharp}a)\rvert

\displaystyle\to\mathcal{E}(a).

The converse inequality was already shown in Step 3.

For arbitrary
a\in\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
we have
P_{t}a\in\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}\cap D(\mathcal{L}^{(2)})
and
\mathcal{E}(P_{t}a-a)\to 0
. By Step 3,

\lVert\delta(a-P_{t}a)\rVert_{\mathcal{K}}\leq\mathcal{E}(a-P_{t}a)^{1/2}\to 0.

This establishes
\lVert\delta(a)\rVert_{\mathcal{K}}=\mathcal{E}(a)^{1/2}
in the general case.

Step 7:
For all
a,b,c\in\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
we have
\langle L(a)\delta(b),\delta(c)\rangle=\langle\delta(Jc),R(Ja)\delta(Jb)\rangle
:

By Step 5 we have
L(a)\delta(b)=\delta(ab)-R(b)\delta(a)
. For the first summand we can use Step 6 to get
\langle\delta(ab),\delta(c)\rangle=\mathcal{E}(ab,c)
. For the second summand, the result from Step 4 and the definition of
\delta
give

\langle R(b)\delta(a),\delta(c)\rangle=\frac{1}{2}(\mathcal{E}(a,cb^{\flat})+\mathcal{E}(ab,c)-\mathcal{E}(b,a^{\sharp}c)).

Thus

\displaystyle\langle L(a)\delta(b),\delta(c)\rangle

\displaystyle=\mathcal{E}(ab,c)-\frac{1}{2}(\mathcal{E}(a,cb^{\flat})+\mathcal{E}(ab,c)-\mathcal{E}(b,a^{\sharp}c))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{E}(Jc,Jb\cdot Ja)+\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{E}(Jc(Ja)^{\flat},Jb)-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{E}(U_{-i/2}a,U_{i/2}(cb^{\flat}))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{E}(Jc,Jb\cdot Ja)+\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{E}(Jc(Ja)^{\flat},Jb)-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{E}((Ja)^{\flat},(Jc)^{\sharp}Jb)

\displaystyle=\langle\delta(Jc),R(Ja)\delta(Jb)\rangle,

where we used
\mathcal{E}\circ J=\mathcal{E}
and the invariance of
\mathcal{E}
under
(U_{t})
in the second line.

It follows that
L(a)\delta(b)\mapsto R(Ja)\delta(Jb)
is well-defined and extends to an isometric anti-linear operator
\mathcal{J}
on
\mathcal{K}
. It is easy to see that
\mathcal{J}
is an anti-unitary involution and
\mathcal{J}L(a)=R(Ja)\mathcal{J}
for all
a\in\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
.

Step 8:
The
\ast
-representation
L
of
\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
on
\mathcal{K}
is non-degenerate:

By the previous step,
L(a)=\mathcal{J}R(Ja)\mathcal{J}
for all
a\in\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
. Since
R
is non-degenerate by Step 4, it follows that
L
is non-degenerate, too.

Step 9:
For all
t\in\mathbb{R}
the map
a\otimes_{\mathcal{E}}b\mapsto U_{t}a\otimes_{\mathcal{E}}U_{t}b
extends to a unitary operator
\mathcal{U}_{t}
on
\mathcal{K}
such that
\mathcal{U}_{t}(L(a)R(b)\xi)=L(U_{t}a)R(U_{t}b)\mathcal{U}_{t}\xi
for all
a,b\in\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
,
\xi\in\mathcal{K}
and
\mathcal{U}_{t}\mathcal{J}=\mathcal{J}\mathcal{U}_{t}
. Moreover, the family
(\mathcal{U}_{t})
is a strongly continuous unitary group:

For
a,b,c,d\in\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
we have

\displaystyle\quad\;\langle U_{t}a\otimes_{\mathcal{E}}U_{t}b,U_{t}c\otimes_{\mathcal{E}}U_{t}d\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(\mathcal{E}(U_{t}a,U_{t}(cdb^{\flat}))+\mathcal{E}(U_{t}(abd^{\flat}),U_{t}c)-\mathcal{E}(U_{t}(bd^{\flat}),U_{t}(a^{\flat}c)))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}((\mathcal{E}(a,cdb^{\flat})+\mathcal{E}(abd^{\flat},c)-\mathcal{E}(bd^{\flat},a^{\flat}c))

\displaystyle=\langle a\otimes_{\mathcal{E}}b,c\otimes_{\mathcal{E}}d\rangle,

where we used that
U_{t}
is an algebra homomorphism on
\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
that commutes with
J
and
\mathcal{E}
is invariant under
U_{t}
. From this it is easy to see that
(\mathcal{U}_{t})
is a unitary group.

To show that
(\mathcal{U}_{t})
is strongly continuous, it suffices to show that it is weakly continuous by standard semigroup theory. Moreover, as
(U_{t})
is uniformly bounded, it is enough to check that
t\mapsto\langle a\otimes_{\mathcal{E}}b,\mathcal{U}_{t}(c\otimes_{\mathcal{E}}d)\rangle
is continuous for all
a,b,c,d\in\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
. This follows from the fact that
(U_{t})
is a strongly continuous unitary group on
(D(\mathcal{E}),\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{E}})
by
Lemma

6.6
and multiplication by elements from
\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
is continuous with respect to the form norm
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\mathcal{E}}
by
Theorem

6.3
.

The remaining properties of
\mathcal{U}_{t}
follow by direct computation.

Step 10:

\mathcal{U}_{t}(\delta(a))=\delta(U_{t}a)
for all
a\in\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
:

For
b,c\in\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
we have

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{U}_{t}(\delta(a)),b\otimes_{\mathcal{E}}c\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\delta(a),U_{-t}a\otimes_{\mathcal{E}}U_{-t}b\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{E}(a,U_{-t}(bc))+\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{E}(a(U_{-t}c)^{\flat},U_{-t}b)-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{E}((U_{-t}c)^{\flat},a^{\sharp}U_{-t}b)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{E}(U_{t}a,bc)+\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{E}((U_{t}a)c^{\sharp},b)-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{E}(c^{\flat},(U_{t}a)^{\sharp}b)

\displaystyle=\langle\delta(U_{t}a),b\otimes_{\mathcal{E}}c\rangle,

where we used that
\mathcal{E}
is invariant under
U_{t}
in the third line. Since the linear span of elements of the form
b\otimes_{\mathcal{E}}c
with
b,c\in\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
is dense in
\mathcal{K}
, the claim follows.

Step 11:
For all
a\in\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
the map
z\mapsto\delta(U_{z}a)
is entire analytic:

By elementary properties of Banach-valued analytic functions, it suffices to show that
z\mapsto\langle\xi,\delta(U_{z}a)\rangle
is analytic for all
\xi
in the closure of
\delta(\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}})
. By
Lemma

6.6
the map
z\mapsto U_{z}a
is entire analytic with respect to the form norm
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\mathcal{E}}
. In particular, since
\lVert\delta(U_{z}a)\rVert=\mathcal{E}(U_{z}a)^{1/2}
by Step 6, the map
z\mapsto\delta(U_{z}a)
is locally bounded. Hence it is enough to show that
z\mapsto\langle\xi,\delta(U_{z}a)\rangle
is analytic for all
\xi\in\delta(\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}})
, which follows from
Lemma

6.6
combined with Step 6.

Conclusion:
Let
\mathcal{H}
be the space of all elements
\xi\in\mathcal{K}
for which
t\mapsto\mathcal{U}_{t}\xi
has an entire analytic continuation. By Steps 10 and 11,
\delta(\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}})
is contained in
\mathcal{H}
. Moreover, it follows easily from Step 9 that
L(a)
and
R(a)
leave
\mathcal{H}
invariant for all
a\in\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
, and the same holds for
\mathcal{J}
. By the properties established in the previous steps, this makes
\mathcal{K}
into a Tomita bimodule over
\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
and
\delta
into a symmetric derivation such that

\mathcal{E}(a,b)=\langle\delta(a),\delta(b)\rangle

for all
a,b\in\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
.

If
\varphi
is finite, then the derivations
\delta_{t}
from Step 1 can be chosen inner by
Proposition

6.2
, so that
\delta
is nearly inner by construction.
∎

6.4.
Uniqueness

In general, the Tomita bimodule and derivation in
Theorem

6.8
are not unique because one can always artificially enlarge
\mathcal{H}
. To get uniqueness, one needs to assume that the Tomita bimodule is generated by the derivation in the following sense. Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a Tomita algebra and
\mathfrak{H}
a Tomita bimodule over
\mathfrak{A}
. Write
\tilde{\mathfrak{A}}
for the unitization of
\mathfrak{A}
, and extend the maps
L
and
R
to
\tilde{\mathfrak{A}}
in the obvious way. If
\delta\colon\mathfrak{A}\to\mathcal{H}
is a symmetric derivation, say that
\mathcal{H}
is generated by
\delta
if

\operatorname{lin}\{L(a)R(b)\delta(c)\mid a,b\in\tilde{\mathfrak{A}},\,c\in\mathfrak{A}\}=\mathcal{H}.

By the product rule, this is equivalent to
\operatorname{lin}\{L(a)\delta(b)\mid a\in\tilde{\mathfrak{A}},\,b\in\mathfrak{A}\}=\mathcal{H}
or
\operatorname{lin}\{R(a)\delta(b)\mid a\in\tilde{\mathfrak{A}},\,b\in\mathfrak{A}\}=\mathcal{H}
.

Theorem 6.9
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a GNS-symmetric quantum Dirichlet form. If for
j\in\{1,2\}
,
\mathcal{H}_{j}
is a Tomita bimodule over
\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
and
\delta_{j}\colon\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}\to\mathcal{H}_{j}
is a symmetric dervation such that
\mathcal{H}_{j}
is generated by
\delta_{j}
and
\mathcal{E}(a)=\lVert\delta_{j}(a)\rVert^{2}
for all
a\in\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
, then there exists a unique bijective isometric bimodule map
V\colon\mathcal{H}_{1}\to\mathcal{H}_{2}
that intertwines the conjugation operators
\mathcal{J}_{1}
,
\mathcal{J}_{2}
and the groups
(\mathcal{U}_{z}^{(1)})
,
(\mathcal{U}_{z}^{(2)})
and satisfies
V\circ\delta_{1}=\delta_{2}
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{H}_{1}
and
\mathcal{H}_{2}
are generated by
\delta_{1}
and
\delta_{2}
, respectively, and
V
is a bimodule map, it is uniquely determined by the requirement
V\circ\delta_{1}=\delta_{2}
, if it exists.

For the existence, define
V(R_{1}(b)\delta_{1}(a))=R_{2}(b)\delta_{2}(a))
for
a\in\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
,
b\in\tilde{\mathfrak{A}}_{\mathcal{E}}
. By
Lemma

5.9
this map is well-defined and isometric. It follows from the product rule that
V
is a bimodule map and
V
is surjective because
\mathcal{H}_{2}
is generated by
\delta_{2}
. That
V
intertwines the conjugation operators
\mathcal{J}_{1}
,
\mathcal{J}_{2}
and the group
(\mathcal{U}_{z}^{(1)})
,
(\mathcal{U}_{z}^{(2)})
is immediate from the definition of a symmetric derivation.
∎

If
\varphi
is finite, we have seen in
Theorem

5.7
that every nearly inner symmetric derivation gives rise to a GNS-symmetric quantum Dirichlet form. Conversely, every GNS-symmetric quantum Dirichlet form induces a nearly inner symmetric derivation by
Theorem

6.8
. In general, this correspondence is not one-to-one, as different derivations on Tomita subalgebras of
\mathfrak{A}_{\varphi}
can have the same closure. Among these derivations, the one constructed in
Theorem

6.8
has the maximal domain in the following sense:

We say a symmetric derivation
\delta\colon\mathfrak{A}\to\mathcal{H}
is
maximally defined
if it is closable and

\mathfrak{A}=\{a\in\mathfrak{A}_{\varphi}\mid\Delta_{\varphi}^{n}a\in D(\bar{\delta})\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

The following result is then immediate from the discussion above.

Corollary 6.10
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a von Neumann algebra and
\varphi
a normal faithful state on
\mathcal{M}
. There is a one-to-one correspondence between maximally defined nearly inner symmetric derivations on Tomita subalgebras of
\mathfrak{A}_{\varphi}
and GNS-symmetric quantum Dirichlet forms on
L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
.

7.
Carré du champ and
\Gamma
-regularity

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a Tomita algebra and
\mathcal{H}
a Tomita bimodule over
\mathfrak{A}
. Recall that
\mathcal{H}
is called a normal Tomita bimodule if the map
\pi_{l}(a)\mapsto L(a)
extends to a normal representation of
\pi_{l}(\mathfrak{A})^{\prime\prime}
. Even if
(P_{t})
is a symmetric QMS on a commutative von Neumann algebra, the Tomita bimodule from
Theorem

6.8
is in general not normal. For example this is a typical feature of Dirichlet forms on fractals if the reference measure is taken to be the Hausdorff measure (see
[
Kus89
]
for example).

In [Wir20] we gave a characterization of quantum Dirichlet forms for which the associated Tomita bimodule is normal in the tracially symmetric case in terms of the so-called carré du champ. In this section we show that this characterization extends to GNS-symmetric quantum Dirichlet forms.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a von Neumann algebra,
\varphi
a normal semi-finite faithful weight on
\mathcal{M}
and
(P_{t})
a
\varphi
-symmetric QMS on
\mathcal{M}
with associated quantum Dirichlet form
\mathcal{E}
. We use the notation from
Theorem

6.8
for the associated Tomita algebra and Tomita bimodule.

Let
A
be the norm closure of
J\pi_{r}(\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}})J
, which is strongly dense in
\mathcal{M}
. For
\xi,\eta\in\overline{\mathcal{H}}
the functional

J\pi_{r}(\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}})J\to\mathbb{C},\,J\pi_{r}(a)^{\ast}J\mapsto\langle\xi,R(a)\eta\rangle

extends to a bounded linear functional
\hat{\Gamma}(\xi,\eta)
on
A
with
\lVert\hat{\Gamma}(\xi,\eta)\rVert\leq\lVert\xi\rVert\lVert\eta\rVert
. If
a,b\in D(\mathcal{E})
, we write
\Gamma(a,b)
for
\hat{\Gamma}(\bar{\delta}(a),\bar{\delta}(b))
. Furthermore, we use the usual convention
\hat{\Gamma}(\xi)=\hat{\Gamma}(\xi,\xi)
and the same for
\Gamma
.

Remark 7.1
.

If
a,b,c\in\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
, then

\langle\Gamma(a,b),J\pi_{r}(c)^{\ast}J\rangle_{A^{\ast},A}=\frac{1}{2}(\mathcal{E}(a,bc)+\mathcal{E}(ac^{\flat},b)-\mathcal{E}(c^{\flat},a^{\sharp}b))

by
Lemma

5.9
.

Theorem 7.2
.

For a quantum Dirichlet form
\mathcal{E}
with associated Tomita algebra
\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
, Tomita bimodule
\mathcal{H}
and derivation
\delta
, the following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

\mathcal{H}
is a normal Tomita bimodule.

(ii)

The map
J\pi_{r}(\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}})J\to B(\overline{\mathcal{H}}),\,J\pi_{r}(a)^{\ast}J\mapsto R(a)
extends to a normal representation of
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{op}}
on
\overline{\mathcal{H}}
.

(iii)

For all
\xi,\eta\in\overline{\mathcal{H}}
the map
\hat{\Gamma}(\xi,\eta)
is
\sigma
-weakly continuous.

(iv)

For every
a\in\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
the map
\Gamma(a)
is
\sigma
-weakly continuous.

Proof.

(i)
\iff
(ii): For
a\in\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
let
\tilde{L}(\pi_{l}(a))=L(a)
and
\tilde{R}(J\pi_{r}(a)^{\ast}J)=R(a)
. Since

J\pi_{r}(Ja)^{\ast}Jb=J(Jb\cdot(Ja)^{\flat})=a^{\flat}b=\pi_{l}(a)^{\ast}b

for all
b\in\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
, we have
J\pi_{r}(\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}})J=\pi_{l}(\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}})
. Moreover,

\mathcal{J}\tilde{L}(\pi_{l}(a))\xi=\mathcal{J}L(a)\xi=R(Ja)\mathcal{J}\xi=\tilde{R}(J\pi_{r}(Ja)^{\ast}J)\mathcal{J}\xi=\tilde{R}(\pi_{l}(a)^{\ast})\mathcal{J}\xi

for all
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
. Thus
\tilde{R}(x)=\mathcal{J}\tilde{L}(x)^{\ast}\mathcal{J}
for all
x\in\pi_{l}(\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}})
. From this it is easy to see that
\tilde{L}
extends to a normal representation if and only if
\tilde{R}
does.

(ii)
\implies
(iii), (iii)
\implies
(iv) are clear from the definitions.

(iv)
\implies
(iii): By polarization,
\Gamma(a,b)
is
\sigma
-weakly continuous for all
a,b\in\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
. If
\xi=R(b)\delta(a)
,
\eta=R(d)\delta(c)
with
a,b,c,d\in\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
, then

\displaystyle\langle\hat{\Gamma}(\xi,\eta),J\pi_{r}(a)^{\ast}J\rangle_{A^{\ast},A}

\displaystyle=\langle R(b)\delta(a),R(a)R(d)\delta(c)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\delta(a),R(b^{\flat}ad)\delta(c)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\Gamma(a,c),J\pi_{r}(b^{\flat}ad)^{\ast}J\rangle_{A^{\ast},A}.

Thus
\hat{\Gamma}(\xi,\eta)
is
\sigma
-weakly continuous. By sesquilinearity, this extends to
\xi,\eta
in
\mathcal{H}
. For arbitrary
\xi,\eta\in\overline{\mathcal{H}}
take sequences
(\xi_{k})
,
(\eta_{k})
in
\mathcal{H}
such that
\xi_{k}\to\xi
,
\eta_{k}\to\eta
. Since

\lVert\hat{\Gamma}(\xi,\eta)-\hat{\Gamma}(\xi_{k},\eta_{k})\rVert\leq\lVert\xi-\xi_{k}\rVert\lVert\eta_{k}\rVert+\lVert\eta_{k}-\eta\rVert\lVert\xi\rVert

and the norm limit of
\sigma
-weakly continuous functionals is
\sigma
-weakly continuous, we obtain that
\hat{\Gamma}(\xi,\eta)
is
\sigma
-weakly continuous.

(iii)
\implies
(ii): For
\xi,\eta\in\overline{\mathcal{H}}
let
\omega_{\xi,\eta}
denote the
\sigma
-weakly continuous extension of
\hat{\Gamma}(\xi,\eta)
to
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{op}}
. By the Kaplansky density theorem,
\lVert\omega_{\xi,\eta}\rVert=\lVert\hat{\Gamma}(\xi,\eta)\rVert\leq\lVert\xi\rVert\lVert\eta\rVert
. Thus for every
x\in\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{op}}
there exists
\tilde{R}(x)\in B(\overline{\mathcal{H}})
with
\lVert\tilde{R}(x)\rVert\leq\lVert x\rVert
such that
\omega_{\xi,\eta}(x)=\langle\xi,\tilde{R}(x)\eta\rangle
. Clearly
\tilde{R}
is linear, weakly continuous on the unit ball and extends the map from (ii). Since the restriction of
\tilde{R}
on
J\pi_{r}(\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}})J
is a
\ast
-homomorphism (for the multiplication in
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{op}}
) and it is weakly continuous, it follows that
\tilde{R}
is a
\ast
-homomorphism on
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{op}}
by a density argument. As
R
is non-degenerate,
\tilde{R}
is unital. Thus
\tilde{R}
is a normal representation of
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{op}}
on
\overline{\mathcal{H}}
.
∎

Remark 7.3
.

According to
Remark

7.1
, if
a\in\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
, then
\Gamma(a)
can be expressed in terms of the form
\mathcal{E}
without reference to
\mathcal{H}
and
\delta
. Thus (iv) in the previous theorem gives a criterion for the normality of
\mathcal{H}
that can be checked without the need for an explicit construction of
\mathcal{H}
and
\delta
.

Definition 7.4
.

We say the quantum Dirichlet form
\mathcal{E}
or the associated quantum Markov semigroup
(P_{t})
is
\Gamma
-regular
if one of the equivalent conditions from
Theorem

7.2
holds.

Remark 7.5
.

\Gamma
-regularity also has a probabilistic counterpart in terms of Markov dilations
[
JRS
]
, but we will not go into details here.

As a consequence of the construction from Subsection
4.2
we obtain that the generator of a
\Gamma
-regular GNS-symmetric quantum Dirichlet form is of the form
\delta^{\ast}\bar{\delta}
, where
\delta
is a twisted derivation with values in a noncommutative
L_{2}
space.

Corollary 7.6
.

If
\mathcal{E}
is a
\Gamma
-regular GNS-symmetric quantum Dirichlet form with generator
\mathcal{L}^{(2)}
, then there exists a von Neumann algebra
\hat{\mathcal{M}}\supset\mathcal{M}
, a weight
\hat{\varphi}
on
\hat{\mathcal{M}}
, a faithful normal conditional expectation
E\colon\hat{\mathcal{M}}\to\mathcal{M}
such that
\varphi\circ E=\hat{\varphi}
, and a closable linear map
\delta\colon\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}\to L_{2}(\hat{\mathcal{M}},\hat{\varphi})
such that

\delta(\Lambda_{\varphi}(xy))=x\delta(\Lambda_{\varphi}(y))+\delta(\Lambda_{\varphi}(x))\sigma^{\varphi}_{i/2}(y)

for all
x,y\in\Lambda_{\varphi}^{-1}(\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}})
and

\mathcal{L}^{(2)}=\delta^{\ast}\bar{\delta}.

Remark 7.7
.

The twisted product rule in the previous corollary can also be expressed in terms of the embeddings
\Delta_{\varphi}^{\alpha}\Lambda_{\varphi}
for
\alpha\in[0,\frac{1}{2}]
as follows:

\delta(\Delta_{\varphi}^{\alpha}\Lambda_{\varphi}(xy))=\sigma^{\varphi}_{-i\alpha}(x)\delta(\Delta_{\varphi}^{\alpha}\Lambda_{\varphi}(y))+\delta(\Delta_{\varphi}^{\alpha}\Lambda_{\varphi}(x))\sigma^{\varphi}_{i(1/2-\alpha)}(y).

Remark 7.8
.

In the case when
\varphi
is a trace, the existence of a derivation as in Subsection
4.2
for
\Gamma
-regular quantum Dirichlet forms was established in
[
JRS
]
. The triple
(\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}},\hat{\mathcal{M}},\delta)
is called a
derivation triple
in this setting
[
BGJ22
,
LJL20
]
.

8.
Examples

In this final section we give examples of quantum Dirichlet forms for which we can describe the associated Tomita bimodule and derivation more explicitly. We start by discussing how tracially symmetric quantum Markov semigroups and quantum Markov semigroups on finite-dimensional von Neumann algebras fit into the picture in the first two subsections, before we treat new examples with depolarizing semigroups, the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck semigroup on free Araki–Woods factors and translation-invariant quantum Markov semigroups on compact quantum groups in the last three subsections.

8.1.
Tracially symmetric quantum Markov semigroups

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a semi-finite von Neumann algebra and
\tau
a normal semi-finite faithful trace on
\mathcal{M}
. In this case the modular group
\sigma^{\tau}
is trivial and the involutions
♯
and
♭
on
\mathfrak{A}_{\tau}
coincide with
J_{\tau}
.

If
\mathcal{E}
is a quantum Dirichlet form on
L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
, then it is automatically GNS-symmetric, and
\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}=D(\mathcal{E})\cap\Lambda_{\tau}(\mathfrak{n}_{\tau})
. Let
\mathcal{H}
be the associated Tomita bimodule. Since

\mathcal{U}_{t}(\delta(a)b)=\delta(\sigma^{\tau}_{t}(a))\sigma^{\tau}_{t}(b)=\delta(a)b

and elements of the form
\delta(a)b
with
a,b\in\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
linearly span
\mathcal{H}
, the group
(\mathcal{U}_{t})
is trivial.

In other words, we have a Hilbert bimodule
\overline{\mathcal{H}}
over
\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
with contractive and non-degenerate left and right action, an anti-unitary involution
\mathcal{J}
on
\overline{\mathcal{H}}
that intertwines the left and right action and a derivation
\delta\colon\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}\to\overline{\mathcal{H}}
such that
\mathcal{J}\circ\delta=\delta\circ J
and

\mathcal{E}(a,b)=\langle\delta(a),\delta(b)\rangle

for all
a,b\in\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
. This is the same data as one gets from the construction in
[
CS03a
]
for tracially symmetric quantum Markov semigroups.

8.2.
QMS on finite-dimensional von Neumann algebras

Let
\mathcal{M}\subset M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
be a unital
∗
-subalgebra, necessarily weakly closed, and
h\in\mathcal{M}_{+}
with
\operatorname{tr}(h)=1
. Write
\varphi
for the state induced by
h
, that is,
\varphi(x)=\operatorname{tr}(xh)
. We assume that
\varphi
is faithful, or, equivalently, that
h
is invertible.

By Alicki’s theorem (see
[
Ali76
, Theorem 3]
,
[
CM17
, Theorem 3.1]
for the case of matrix algebras and
[
Wir22
, Corollary 5.4]
for the extension to arbitrary finite-dimensional von Neumann algebras) a continuous semigroup
(P_{t})
on
\mathcal{M}
is a
\varphi
-symmetric QMS if and only if there exist
\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{m}\in\mathbb{R}
and
v_{1},\dots,v_{m}\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
satisfying

•

\operatorname{tr}(v_{j})=0
for
1\leq j\leq m
,

•

\operatorname{tr}(v_{j}^{\ast}v_{k})=\delta_{jk}\operatorname{tr}(v_{j}^{\ast}v_{j})
for
1\leq j,k\leq m
,

•

\{v_{j}\mid 1\leq j\leq m\}=\{v_{j}^{\ast}\mid 1\leq j\leq m\}
,

•

hv_{j}h^{-1}=e^{-\omega_{j}}v_{j}
for
1\leq j\leq m

such that the generator
\mathcal{L}
of
(P_{t})
is given by

\mathcal{L}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}(e^{-\omega_{j}/2}v_{j}^{\ast}[v_{j},\cdot\,]+e^{\omega_{j}/2}[\,\cdot\,,v_{j}]v_{j}^{\ast}).

Note that in general the matrices
v_{j}
cannot be chosen in
\mathcal{M}
.

In this case, the differential structure from
Theorem

6.8
coincides with the differential structure studied in [CM17,Wir22]. Let us first fix some notation. The space
L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
can be identified with
\mathcal{M}
with the inner product
\langle x,y\rangle_{h}=\operatorname{tr}(x^{\ast}yh)
. Clearly, this inner product can be extended to
M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
, and we write
H
for the resulting Hilbert space.

With these identifications, the Tomita algebra
\mathfrak{A}_{\varphi}
is
L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
with the product from
\mathcal{M}
, the involution
a^{\sharp}=a^{\ast}
and
U_{z}a=h^{iz}ah^{-iz}
. Since the quantum Dirichlet form
\mathcal{E}
associated with
(P_{t})
is defined everywhere, we have
\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}=\mathfrak{A}_{\varphi}
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be the linear span of
\{([v_{j},a]b)_{j}\mid a,b\in L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)\}
inside
H^{\oplus m}
. If we endow
H^{\oplus m}
with the componentwise left and right actions of
L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
, then
\mathcal{H}
is a sub-bimodule: If
a,b,c\in L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
, then
([v_{j},a]bc)_{j}\in\mathcal{H}
and

(a[v_{j},b]c)_{j}=([v_{j},ab]c-[v_{j},a]bc)_{j}\in\mathcal{H}.

The space
\mathcal{H}
becomes a Tomita bimodule over
L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
with

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{z}\xi

\displaystyle=(e^{i\omega_{j}z}h^{iz}\xi_{j}h^{-iz})_{j},

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}([v_{j},a]b)_{j}

\displaystyle=(h^{1/2}(b^{\ast}[v_{j^{\ast}},a]^{\ast})h^{1/2})_{j},

where
j^{\ast}\in\{1,\dots,m\}
is the unique index such that
v_{j}^{\ast}=v_{j^{\ast}}
.

The map
\delta\colon L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)\to\mathcal{H}
given by
\delta(a)=(ie^{-\omega_{j}/4}[v_{j},a])_{j}
is a symmetric derivation and

\displaystyle\langle\delta(a),\delta(b)\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\operatorname{tr}((ie^{-\omega_{j}/4}[v_{j},a])^{\ast}(ie^{-\omega_{j}/4}[v_{j},b])h)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{m}e^{-\omega_{j}/2}\operatorname{tr}(a^{\ast}v_{j}^{\ast}[v_{j},b]h-a^{\ast}[v_{j},b]\sigma v_{j}^{\ast})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\operatorname{tr}(e^{-\omega_{j}/2}a^{\ast}v_{j}^{\ast}[v_{j},b]h-e^{\omega_{j}/2}a^{\ast}[v_{j},b]v_{j}^{\ast}h)

\displaystyle=\langle a,\mathcal{L}(b)\rangle.

8.3.
Depolarizing semigroups

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a von Neumann algebra and
\varphi
a normal faithful state on
\mathcal{M}
. The depolarizing semigroup (with respect to
\varphi
) is given by
P_{t}(x)=e^{-t}x+(1-e^{-t})\varphi(x)
. It is evident that
(P_{t})
is a GNS-symmetric quantum Markov semigroup on
\mathcal{M}
.

The associated Tomita algebra is simply
\mathfrak{A}_{\varphi}
and the associated Tomita bimodule is a subbimodule of
\mathfrak{A}_{\varphi}\odot\mathfrak{A}_{\varphi}
with the usual inner product,
L=\pi_{l}\otimes\pi_{l}
,
R=\pi_{r}\otimes\pi_{r}
,
\mathcal{U}_{z}=U_{z}\otimes U_{z}
and
\mathcal{J}(a\otimes b)=J_{\varphi}b\otimes J_{\varphi}a
. The associated derivation is then given by
\delta(a)=i(a\otimes\Omega_{\varphi}-\Omega_{\varphi}\otimes a)
, where
\Omega_{\varphi}
denotes the cyclic vector in
L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\varphi)
representing
\varphi
.

8.4.
Free Araki–Woods factors

Free Araki–Woods factors were introduced by Shlyakhtenko in the context of free probability
[
Shl97
]
. We give a slightly modified, but equivalent definition here, which stresses the analogy to the definition of algebras
\Gamma_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{H})
from Subsection
4.2
(see
[
Vae05
]
for example). Let
H
be a Hilbert space,
I\colon H\to H
an anti-unitary involution and
(V_{t})
a strongly continuous unitary group on
H
such that
V_{t}I=IV_{t}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Let
A
be the unique positive self-adjoint non-singular operator on
H
such that
V_{t}=A^{it}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, and let
T=IA^{-1/2}
.

On the full Fock space

\mathcal{F}(H)=\mathbb{C}\oplus\bigoplus_{n=1}^{\infty}H^{\otimes n}

define the creation and annihilation operators
a(\xi)
and
a^{\ast}(\xi)
for
\xi\in H
as in Subsection
4.2
. Let
s(\xi)=a(\xi)+a^{\ast}(\xi)
. The free Araki–Woods factor
\Gamma(H,(V_{t}))^{\prime\prime}
is the von Neumann algebra generated by the operators
s(\xi)
for
\xi\in D(T)
with
T\xi=\xi
.

If we view
H
as a Tomita
\mathbb{C}
-
\mathbb{C}
-correspondence with conjugation
I
and unitary group
(V_{-t})
, then
\Gamma(H,(V_{t}))^{\prime\prime}
is the same as the free Gaussian algebra
\Gamma_{\mathbb{C}}(H)
from Subsection
4.2
. The resulting von Neumann algebras can be viewed as operator-valued versions of Shlyakhtenko’s free Araki–Woods factors
[
Shl97
]
, which are a special case of our construction when
\mathfrak{A}=\mathbb{C}
, so that they might be of independent interest in operator-valued free probability theory. In this article, the construction is used to extend the construction of derivation triples from
[
JRS
]
to GNS-symmetric quantum Markov semigroups.

Write
\Omega
for the vector
(1,0,\dots)\in\mathcal{F}(H)
and
\varphi
for the associated vector state on
\Gamma(H,(V_{t}))^{\prime\prime}
. The state
\varphi
is called the free quasi-free state. The vector
\Omega
is cyclic and separating for
\Gamma(H,(V_{t}))^{\prime\prime}
, which means that the GNS Hilbert space associated with
\varphi
is
\mathcal{F}(H)
with the defining action of
\Gamma(H,(V_{t}))^{\prime\prime}
. The associated modular group acts as
\Delta_{\varphi}^{it}|_{H^{\otimes n}}=(V_{-t})^{\otimes n}
and the associated modular conjugation acts as
J_{\varphi}|_{H^{\otimes n}}=I^{\otimes n}\tau_{n}
, where
\tau_{n}
is the reversal map given by
\tau_{n}(\xi_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes\xi_{n})=\xi_{n}\otimes\dots\otimes\xi_{1}
.

The number operator
N
on
\mathcal{F}(H)
is the positive self-adjoint operator on
\mathcal{F}(H)
with domain

D(N)=\{\xi\in\mathcal{F}(H)\mid\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}n^{2}\lVert\xi_{n}\rVert^{2}<\infty\}

that acts on
H^{\otimes n}
as multiplication by
n
. It generates a symmetric QMS on
\mathcal{F}(H)
, which clearly commutes with
(\Delta_{\varphi}^{it})
. Let
(P_{t})
denote the associated GNS-symmetric QMS on
\Gamma(H,(V_{t}))^{\prime\prime}
and
\mathcal{E}
the associated quantum Dirichlet form.

The QMS
(P_{t})
can equivalently be described as
\Gamma^{\prime\prime}(e^{-t}\mathrm{id}_{H})
, where
\Gamma^{\prime\prime}
is Shlyakhtenko’s second quantization functor (see also
[
Hia03
]
or
[
BM21
]
, where this QMS is called the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck semigroup on
\Gamma(H,(V_{t}))^{\prime\prime}
).

Let
\mathfrak{A}=\operatorname{lin}\bigcup_{n}D(T^{\otimes n})
. Inductively one can show that every
\eta\in\mathfrak{A}
there exists
x
in the algebra generated by
\{s(\xi)\mid\xi\in D(T),\,T\xi=\xi\}
such that
x\Omega=\eta
, and
x
is unique since
\Omega
is separating for
\Gamma(H,(V_{t}))
. This is the Wick word decomposition of
\eta
and denoted by
x=W(\eta)
. In particular,
\mathfrak{A}
is a Tomita subalgebra of
\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
and the multiplication is given by
\xi\eta=W(\xi)W(\eta)\Omega
. Moreover,
\mathfrak{A}
is a core for
\mathcal{E}
by
Lemma

3.4
. For this reason we can restrict our attention to
\mathfrak{A}
instead of
\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{E}}
.

Let us first describe the Tomita bimodule
\mathcal{H}
. On
H\oplus H
we have the anti-linear involution
I\oplus I
and the strongly continuous unitary group
(V_{t}\oplus V_{t})
, which commutes with
I\oplus I
. Thus we can construct
\Gamma(H\oplus H,(V_{t}\oplus V_{t}))^{\prime\prime}
as before, and the GNS Hilbert space with respect to the free quasi-free state
\psi
on
\Gamma(H\oplus H,(V_{t}\oplus V_{t}))^{\prime\prime}
is canonically identified with
\mathcal{F}(H\oplus H)
. By a slight abuse of notation, we also write
\Omega
for the vacuum vector in
\mathcal{F}(H\oplus H)
.

In particular, the group generated by the modular operator and modular conjugation operator act as
\Delta_{\psi}^{it}|_{(H\oplus H)^{\otimes n}}=(V_{-t}\oplus V_{-t})^{\otimes n}
and
J_{\psi}|_{(H\oplus H)^{\otimes n}}=(I\oplus I)^{\otimes n}\Sigma_{n}
, where
\tau_{n}
is the reversal map as defined above. Let
\mathcal{H}=\operatorname{lin}\bigcup_{n}D((T\oplus T)^{\otimes n})
, which is contained in
\mathfrak{A}_{\psi}
under these identifications.

As another application of the second quantization theorem, the map that sends
s(\xi)\mapsto s((\xi,0))
for
\xi\in D(T)
extends to a normal unital state-preserving
\ast
-homomorphism
\Phi
from
\Gamma(H,(V_{t}))^{\prime\prime}
to
\Gamma(H\oplus H,(V_{t}\oplus V_{t}))^{\prime\prime}
. The defining property of
\Phi
implies that
\Phi(W(\xi))=W(\xi,0)
for
\xi\in\mathfrak{A}
.

Define left and right actions of
\mathfrak{A}
on
\mathcal{F}(H\oplus H)
by

\displaystyle L(\xi)x\Omega

\displaystyle=\Phi(W(\xi))x\Omega

\displaystyle R(\xi)x\Omega

\displaystyle=x\Phi(W(\xi))\Omega

for
\xi\in\mathfrak{A}
and
x\in\Gamma(H\oplus H,(V_{t}\oplus V_{t}))^{\prime\prime}
.

Clearly,
L(\xi)
and
R(\xi)
map
\mathcal{H}
into
\mathcal{H}
for
x\Omega\in\mathfrak{A}
. Together with the restrictions of
(\Delta_{\psi}^{it})
and
J_{\psi}
, this makes
\mathcal{H}
into a Tomita bimodule over
\mathfrak{A}
.

Now let us describe the derivation
\delta
. Let
\Sigma\colon H\oplus H\to H\oplus H
be the flip map, that is,
\Sigma(\xi,\eta)=(\eta,\xi)
. The family
(\alpha_{t})_{t}=(\mathcal{F}(e^{it\Sigma})\,\cdot\,\mathcal{F}(e^{-it\Sigma}))_{t}
is a weak
∗
continuous group of
\ast
-automorphisms on
B(\mathcal{F}(H\oplus H))
, and by the second quantization lemma, it maps
\Gamma(H\oplus H,(V_{t}\oplus V_{t}))^{\prime\prime}
into itself.

Let
D(\partial)
be the set of all
x\in\Gamma(H\oplus H,(V_{t}\oplus V_{t}))^{\prime\prime}
for which
\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{1}{t}(\alpha_{t}(x)-x)
exists in the strong
∗
-topology. Since
(\alpha_{t})
consists of
\ast
-automorphisms,
D(\partial)
is a
\ast
-subalgebra of
\Gamma(H\oplus H,(V_{t}\oplus V_{t}))^{\prime\prime}
and the operator

\partial\colon D(\partial)\to\Gamma(H\oplus H,(V_{t}\oplus V_{t}))^{\prime\prime},\,x\mapsto\frac{1}{i}\lim_{t\searrow 0}\frac{1}{t}(\alpha_{t}(x)-x)

is a
\ast
-derivation. Moreover, since
\Sigma
commutes with
\Delta_{\psi}^{it}
, the derivation
\partial
commutes with
\sigma^{\psi}
.

Let

\delta\colon\mathfrak{A}\to\mathcal{H},\,\xi\mapsto\partial(\Phi(W(\xi)))\Omega=\partial(W(\xi,0))\Omega.

We have

\displaystyle\delta(\xi\eta)

\displaystyle=\partial(\Phi(W(\xi)W(\eta)))\Omega

\displaystyle=\Phi(W(\xi))\partial(\Phi(W(\eta)))\Omega+\partial(\Phi(W(\xi)))\Phi(W(\eta))\Omega

\displaystyle=L(\xi)\delta(\eta)+R(\eta)\delta(\xi).

Since
\partial
commutes with the modular group, we have

\displaystyle\delta(\Delta_{\varphi}^{it}\xi)=\partial(W(\Delta_{\varphi}^{it}\xi,0))\Omega=\partial(\sigma^{\psi}_{t}(W(\xi,0)))\Omega=\sigma^{\psi}_{t}(\partial(W(\xi,0)))\Omega=\Delta_{\psi}^{it}\delta(\xi).

Similarly,

\displaystyle\delta(J_{\varphi}\xi)=\partial(W(J_{\varphi}\xi,0))\Omega=\partial(\sigma^{\psi}_{-i/2}(W(\xi,0))^{\ast})\Omega=\sigma^{\psi}_{-i/2}(\partial(W(\xi,0)))^{\ast}\Omega=J_{\psi}\delta(\xi).

Thus
\delta
is a symmetric derivation.

For
\xi\in D(T)^{\odot n}
we have

\displaystyle\delta(\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{i}\lim_{t\searrow 0}\frac{1}{t}(\mathcal{F}(e^{it\Sigma})W(\xi,0)\mathcal{F}(e^{-it\Sigma})\Omega-W(\xi,0)\Omega)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{i}\lim_{t\searrow 0}\frac{1}{t}((e^{it\Sigma})^{\otimes n}(\xi,0)-(\xi,0))

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}(\mathrm{id}^{k-1}\otimes\Sigma\otimes\mathrm{id}^{n-k})(\xi,0).

Thus, if
\xi\in D(T)^{\odot m}
and
\eta\in D(T)^{\odot n}
, then

\displaystyle\langle\delta(\xi),\delta(\eta)\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle=\delta_{m,n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\langle(\mathrm{id}^{k-1}\otimes\Sigma\otimes\mathrm{id}^{n-k})(\xi,0),(\mathrm{id}^{k-1}\otimes\Sigma\otimes\mathrm{id}^{n-k})(\eta,0)\rangle

\displaystyle=n\delta_{m,n}\langle\xi,\eta\rangle

\displaystyle=\mathcal{E}(\xi,\eta).

8.5.
Compact quantum groups

Our last example are translation-invariant quantum Markov semigroups on compact quantum groups. We refer to
[
Wor98
]
as a reference for the general theory of compact quantum groups and to
[
CFK14
]
for the result on quantum Markov semigroups on compact quantum groups we need. Note that some of the common notation for compact quantum groups conflicts with the notation used in Tomita–Takesaki theory. For example,
S
and
\varphi
are used for different objects than in the rest of the article.

A compact quantum group is a pair
(A,\Delta)
consisting of a unital
C^{\ast}
-algebra
A
and a unital
\ast
-homomorphism
\Delta\colon A\to A\otimes_{\min}A
such that

(\Delta\otimes\mathrm{id})\Delta=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\Delta)\Delta

and the sets

\operatorname{lin}(A\otimes 1)\Delta(A),\,\operatorname{lin}(1\otimes A)\Delta(A)

are dense in
A\otimes_{\min}A
.

We will use the sumless Sweedler notation
\Delta(a)=a_{(1)}\otimes a_{(2)}
for the comultiplication.

On a compact quantum group
A
there exists a unique state
h
, called the Haar state, such that

(h\otimes\mathrm{id})\Delta(a)=(\mathrm{id}\otimes h)\Delta(a)=h(a)1

for all
a\in A
.

Let
(\pi_{h},L_{2}(A,h),\Omega_{h})
be the cyclic representation associated with
h
, and let
\mathcal{M}=\pi_{h}(A)^{\prime\prime}
. We also denote the vector state
\langle\Omega_{h},\cdot\,\Omega_{h}\rangle
on
\mathcal{M}
by
h
.

An
n
-dimensional unitary corepresentation of
(A,\Delta)
is a unitary
u\in M_{n}(A)
such that

\Delta(u_{jk})=\sum_{l=1}^{n}u_{jl}\otimes u_{lk}.

The linear span of the matrix coefficients
u_{jk}
, where
u
runs over all unitary corepresentations of
(A,\Delta)
, forms a dense unital
\ast
-subalgebra
\mathcal{A}
of
A
.

A unitary corepresentation
u
is called irreducible if
\{u\}^{\prime}\cap M_{n}(\mathbb{C})=\mathbb{C}1
. Two unitary corepresentations
u
and
v
are called equivalent if there exists a unitary matrix
U\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
such that
v=U^{\ast}uU
. If
(u^{(\alpha)})_{\alpha\in I}
is a complete set of representatives of the equivalence classes of unitary corepresentations of
(A,\Delta)
and
n_{\alpha}
is the dimension of the corepresentation
u^{(\alpha)}
, then
\{u^{(\alpha)}_{jk}\mid\alpha\in I,\,1\leq j,k\leq n_{\alpha}\}
is a basis of
\mathcal{A}
.

With the counit
\varepsilon\colon\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{C}
and the antipode
S\colon\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}
given by

\displaystyle\varepsilon(u^{(\alpha)}_{jk})

\displaystyle=\delta_{jk}

\displaystyle S(u^{(\alpha)}_{jk})

\displaystyle=(u^{(\alpha)}_{kj})^{\ast},

the
\ast
-algebra
\mathcal{A}
becomes a Hopf
\ast
-algebra.

For
f\colon\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{C}
linear and
a\in\mathcal{A}
the convolution is defined by
f\ast a=(\mathrm{id}\otimes f)\Delta(a)
, or, in Sweedler notation,
f\ast a=f(a_{(2)})a_{(1)}
. Convolution by the counit is the identity map. Similarly, if
f,g\in\mathcal{A}^{\ast}
, then
f\ast g=(f\otimes g)\Delta
.

Let
\varphi\in\mathcal{A}^{\ast}
be a hermitian functional such that
\varphi(1)=0
,
\varphi(a^{\ast}a)\geq 0
if
a\in\ker\varepsilon
and
\varphi\circ S=\varphi
and define the associated convolution semigroup of states by

\varphi_{t}=\varepsilon+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{t^{k}}{k!}\varphi^{\ast k}.

This induces to a semigroup
(P_{t})
on
\mathcal{A}
given by
P_{t}=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\varphi_{t})\Delta
. The semigroup
(P_{t})
extends to a GNS-symmetric quantum Markov semigroup on
\mathcal{M}
, which is translation-invariant, that is,
\Delta P_{t}=(\mathrm{id}\otimes P_{t})\Delta
for all
t\geq 0
. Conversely, any translation-invariant GNS-symmetric quantum Markov semigroup on
\mathcal{M}
is of this form
[
CFK14
, Theorem 3.4, Corollary 4.6]
. Let
\mathcal{E}
denote the associated GNS-symmetric quantum Dirichlet form on
L_{2}(A,h)
.

The generating functional
\varphi
gives rise to a
Schürmann triple

((\pi,H),\eta,\varphi)
, where
\pi
is a contractive unital
\ast
-representation of
\mathcal{A}
on the pre-Hilbert space
H
and
\eta\colon\mathcal{A}\to H
is a surjective linear map such that

\displaystyle\eta(ab)

\displaystyle=\pi(a)\eta(b)+\varepsilon(b)\eta(a)

\displaystyle\langle\eta(a),\eta(b)\rangle

\displaystyle=\varphi((a-\varepsilon(a))^{\ast}(b-\varepsilon(b)))

for all
a,b\in\mathcal{A}
.

The bimodule and derivation associated with the quantum Dirichlet form
\mathcal{E}
have been essentially (without the group
(\mathcal{U}_{z})
and the conjugation
\mathcal{J}
) described in
[
CFK14
, Section 8]
. Since
\mathcal{A}\Omega_{h}
is a Tomita subalgebra of
\mathfrak{A}_{h}
by
[
Wor98
, Theorem 1.4]
and core for
\mathcal{E}
by
Lemma

3.4
, we can restrict our attention to
\mathcal{A}\Omega_{h}
.

Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{lin}\{a_{(1)}b\Omega_{h}\otimes\eta(a_{(2)})\mid a,b\in\mathcal{A}\},

\displaystyle L(a\Omega_{h})(b\Omega_{h}\otimes\eta(c))

\displaystyle=ab\Omega_{h}\otimes\pi(a)\eta(c),

\displaystyle R(a\Omega_{h})(b\Omega_{h}\otimes\eta(c))

\displaystyle=ba\Omega_{h}\otimes\eta(c),

\displaystyle\delta(a\Omega_{h})

\displaystyle=a_{(1)}\Omega_{h}\otimes\eta(a_{(2)})

for
a,b,c\in\mathcal{A}
. Note that
\mathcal{H}=\operatorname{lin}\{R(b\Omega_{h})\delta(a\Omega_{h})\mid a,b\in\mathcal{A}\}
.

It was shown in
[
CFK14
, Proposition 8.1]
that
\delta(\xi\cdot\eta)=L(\xi)\delta(\eta)+R(\eta)\delta(\xi)
and
\mathcal{E}(\xi,\zeta)=\langle\delta(\xi),\delta(\zeta)\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}
for
\xi,\zeta\in\mathcal{A}\Omega_{h}
. Clearly,
R(\xi)
maps
\mathcal{H}
into itself, and the product ensures that the same is the case for
L(\xi)
. In other words,

L(a\Omega_{h})(b_{(1)}c\Omega_{h}\otimes\eta(b_{(2)}))=(ab)_{(1)}c\Omega_{h}\otimes\eta((ab)_{(2)})-a_{(1)}bc\Omega_{h}\otimes\eta(a_{(2)}).

The group
(\mathcal{U}_{z})
can be described as follows. Let
\sigma
be the modular group associated with
h
. Define

\mathcal{U}_{z}(a_{(1)}b\Omega_{h}\otimes\eta(a_{(2)}))=\sigma_{z}(a)_{(1)}\sigma_{z}(b)\otimes\eta(\sigma_{z}(a)_{(2)}).

Clearly
\mathcal{U}_{z}\delta(a\Omega_{h})=\delta(\sigma_{z}(a)\Omega_{h})
and
\mathcal{U}_{z}R(a\Omega_{h})=R(\sigma_{z}(a)\Omega_{h})\mathcal{U}_{z}
, which also implies
\mathcal{U}_{z}L(a\Omega_{h})=L(\sigma_{z}(a)\Omega_{h})\mathcal{U}_{z}
by the product rule. Moreover,

\displaystyle\quad\;\langle\mathcal{U}_{z}(a_{(1)}b\Omega_{h}\otimes\eta(a_{(2)})),c_{(1)}d\Omega_{h}\otimes\eta(c_{(2)})\rangle

\displaystyle=h(\sigma_{z}(b)^{\ast}\sigma_{z}(a)^{\ast}_{(1)}c_{(1)}d)\varphi((\sigma_{z}(a)_{(2)}-\varepsilon(\sigma_{z}(a)_{(2)}))^{\ast}(c_{(2)}-\varepsilon(c_{(2)})))

\displaystyle=h(\sigma_{z}(b)^{\ast}\sigma_{z}(a)^{\ast}_{(1)}c_{(1)}d)(\varphi(\sigma_{z}(a)_{(2)}^{\ast}c_{(2)})-\overline{\varepsilon(\sigma_{z}(a)_{(2)})}\varphi(c_{(2)})-\varepsilon(c_{(2)})\overline{\varphi(\sigma_{z}(a)_{(2)})}),

where we used
\varphi(1)=0
in the last step. Now let us analyze the summands separately. For the first summand we have

\displaystyle h(\sigma_{z}(b)^{\ast}\sigma_{z}(a)^{\ast}_{(1)}c_{(1)}\varphi(\sigma_{z}(a)_{(2)}^{\ast}c_{(2)})d)

\displaystyle=h(\sigma_{z}(b)^{\ast}\varphi\ast(\sigma_{z}(a)^{\ast}c)d)

\displaystyle=h(\sigma_{-\bar{z}}(\sigma_{\bar{z}}(b^{\ast})\varphi\ast(\sigma_{\bar{z}}(a^{\ast})c)d)

\displaystyle=h(b^{\ast}\varphi\ast(a^{\ast}\sigma_{-\bar{z}}(c))\sigma_{-\bar{z}}(d))

since the modular group commutes with convolution by
\varphi
by GNS-symmetry [compare CFK].
For the second and third summand,

\displaystyle h(\sigma_{z}(b)^{\ast}\overline{\varepsilon(\sigma_{z}(a)_{(2)})}\sigma_{z}(a)^{\ast}_{(1)}\varphi(c_{(2)})c_{(1)}d)

\displaystyle=h(\sigma_{\bar{z}}(b^{\ast})(\varepsilon\ast\sigma_{\bar{z}}(a^{\ast}))(\varphi\ast c)d)

\displaystyle=h(b^{\ast}a^{\ast}(\varphi\ast\sigma_{-\bar{z}}(c))\sigma_{-\bar{z}}(d))

and

\displaystyle h(\sigma_{z}(b)^{\ast}\overline{\varphi(\sigma_{z}(a)_{(2)})}\sigma_{z}(a)^{\ast}_{(1)}\varepsilon(c_{(2)})c_{(1)}d)

\displaystyle=h(\sigma_{\bar{z}}(b^{\ast})(\varphi\ast\sigma_{\bar{z}}(a^{\ast}))(\varepsilon\ast c)d)

\displaystyle=h(b^{\ast}(\varphi\ast a^{\ast})\sigma_{-\bar{z}}(c)\sigma_{-\bar{z}}(d))

by the same reasoning.

Reversing these computations, we get

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{U}_{z}(a_{(1)}b\Omega_{h}\otimes\eta(a_{(2)})),c_{(1)}d\Omega_{h}\otimes\eta(c_{(2)})\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad=\langle a_{(1)}b\Omega_{h}\otimes\eta(a_{(2)}),\mathcal{U}_{-\bar{z}}(c_{(1)}d\Omega_{h}\otimes\eta(c_{(2)}))\rangle.

As for the conjugation operator
\mathcal{J}
, let

\mathcal{J}(a_{(1)}b\Omega_{h}\otimes\eta(a_{(2)}))=\sigma_{i/2}(b)^{\ast}\sigma_{i/2}(a)^{\ast}_{(1)}\otimes\pi(\sigma_{i/2}(b)^{\ast})\eta(\sigma_{i/2}(a)^{\ast}_{(2)}).

It is not hard to see that
\mathcal{J}R(a\Omega_{h})=L(\sigma_{i/2}(a)^{\ast}\Omega_{h})\mathcal{J}
,
\mathcal{J}\delta(a\Omega_{h})=\delta(\sigma_{i/2}(a)^{\ast}\Omega_{h})
and
\mathcal{U}_{z}\mathcal{J}=\mathcal{J}\mathcal{U}_{\bar{z}}
.

Furthermore,

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{2}(a_{(1)}b\Omega_{h}\otimes\eta(a_{(2)}))

\displaystyle=\mathcal{J}(\sigma_{i/2}(b)^{\ast}\sigma_{i/2}(a)^{\ast}_{(1)}\otimes\pi(\sigma_{i/2}(b)^{\ast})\eta(\sigma_{i/2}(a)^{\ast}_{(2)}))

\displaystyle=\mathcal{J}(L(\sigma_{i/2}(b)^{\ast}\Omega_{h})\delta(\sigma_{i/2}(a)^{\ast}\Omega_{h}))

\displaystyle=\mathcal{J}(\delta(\sigma_{i/2}(ab)^{\ast}\Omega_{h})-R(\sigma_{i/2}(a)^{\ast}\Omega_{h})\delta(\sigma_{i/2}(b)^{\ast}\Omega_{h}))

\displaystyle=\delta(ab\Omega_{h})-L(a\Omega_{h})\delta(b\Omega_{h})

\displaystyle=R(b\Omega_{h})\delta(a\Omega_{h})

\displaystyle=a_{(1)}b\Omega_{h}\otimes\eta(a_{(2)}),

so that
\mathcal{J}
is an involution, and

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{J}(a_{(1)}b\Omega_{h}\otimes\eta(a_{(2)})),c_{(1)}d\Omega_{h}\otimes\eta(c_{(2)})\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad=h(\sigma_{i/2}(a)\sigma_{i/2}(b)c_{(1)}d)\varphi((\sigma_{i/2}(ab)_{(2)}-\varepsilon(\sigma_{i/2}(a)_{(2)})\sigma_{i/2}(b))(c_{(2)}-\varepsilon(c_{(2)}))).

Considering all three non-zero summands separately as in the case of
\mathcal{U}_{z}
, one obtains

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{J}(a_{(1)}b\Omega_{h}\otimes\eta(a_{(2)})),c_{(1)}d\Omega_{h}\otimes\eta(c_{(2)})\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad=\langle a_{(1)}b\Omega_{h}\otimes\eta(a_{(2)}),\mathcal{J}(c_{(1)}d\Omega_{h}\otimes\eta(c_{(2)}))\rangle.

Thus
\mathcal{J}
and
(\mathcal{U}_{z})
make
\mathcal{H}
into a Tomita bimodule over
\mathcal{A}\Omega_{h}
and
\delta\colon\mathcal{A}\Omega_{h}\to\mathcal{H}
is a symmetric derivation such that
D(\bar{\delta})=D(\mathcal{E})
and
\langle\bar{\delta}(\xi),\bar{\delta}(\eta)\rangle=\mathcal{E}(\xi,\eta)
.

Rényi entropies for one-dimensional quantum systems with mixed boundary conditions
Benoit Estienne, Yacine Ikhlef, Andrei Rotaru

Abstract
We present a general method for calculating Rényi entropies in the ground state of a one-dimensional critical system with mixed open boundaries, for an interval starting at one of its ends. Technically, we consider the case when the boundary operator implementing the change of boundary conditions is degenerate under the Virasoro algebra. By exploiting the null-vectors conditions, we derive the ordinary differential equations that govern the scaling functions associated to the entropies. In particular, we provide an explicit formula for the second Rényi entropy. Additionally, we identify and compute the leading finite-size corrections to compare our theoretical results with numerical data for the Ising and three-state Potts critical chains.

Summary of results

2.1
Scaling functions for Rényi entropies

We consider a quantum system at criticality on an open chain of size
L
, with lattice spacing
a
.
The left and right ends of the system have distinct boundary conditions, denoted as
\alpha
and
\beta
, respectively. As the system size becomes sufficiently large, the universal properties of the bulk are described by a two-dimensional CFT, whereas the behaviour of the boundaries can be effectively characterised using
conformal boundary conditions
. Notably, we are excluding any consideration of subleading corrections resulting from irrelevant boundary perturbations
[
71
,
72
,
73
]
.

Our objective is to evaluate the entanglement entropy between the interval
A=[0,\ell]
and its complement
B=[\ell,L]
, in the ground state, as depicted in Figure
1
.
The scaling limit is defined by letting
a\to 0
, while keeping the lengths
L
and
\ell
finite. The
N{}^{\text{th}}
Rényi entropy
S_{N}^{(\alpha\beta)}(\ell)
is given (up to a non-universal additive constant) by the one-point function of the twist operator
\sigma
[
74
]
:

\displaystyle S_{N}^{(\alpha\beta)}(\ell)=\frac{1}{1-N}\log\langle\sigma(\ell,\bar{\ell})\rangle^{(\alpha\beta)}_{\mathbb{S}_{L}},

(2.1)

where
\mathbb{S}_{L}
denotes the infinite strip (with imaginary time running along the imaginary axis)

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}_{L}=\{w\in\mathbb{C},\,0<\textrm{Re}(w)<L\}\,,

(2.2)

with conformal BC
\alpha
(resp.
\beta
) on the left (resp. right) boundary.
The dimension of the twist operator
\sigma
is given by
[
2
,
44
,
45
]

h_{\sigma}=\frac{c}{24}\left(N-\frac{1}{N}\right)\,,

(2.3)

where
c
is the central charge.
Upon mapping to the upper half plane
\mathbb{H}
via the map

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\mathbb{S}_{L}\to\mathbb{H}\\
w\mapsto z(w)=\exp(i\pi w/L)\,,\end{cases}

(2.4)

one gets

\displaystyle\langle\sigma(w,\bar{w})\rangle^{(\alpha\beta)}_{\mathbb{S}_{L}}=\left|\frac{\pi z}{L}\right|^{2h_{\sigma}}\langle\sigma(z,\bar{z})\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}^{(\alpha\beta)}\,.

(2.5)

On the right-hand side, the upper half-plane
\mathbb{H}
has boundary conditions
\alpha
for
x>0
and
\beta
for
x<0
, as in Fig.
2
.
The corresponding Rényi entropy reads

\boxed{S_{N}^{(\alpha\beta)}(\ell)=\frac{c}{12}\frac{N+1}{N}\log\left[\frac{2L}{\pi a}\sin\left(\frac{\pi\ell}{L}\right)\right]+\frac{1}{1-N}\log\mathcal{F}_{N}^{(\alpha\beta)}(e^{i\pi\ell/L},e^{-i\pi\ell/L})\,,}

(2.6)

where we reintroduced the lattice spacing
a
, and the universal function
\mathcal{F}_{N}^{(\alpha\beta)}
is given by

\boxed{\mathcal{F}_{N}^{(\alpha\beta)}(z,\bar{z})=|z-\bar{z}|^{2h_{\sigma}}\,\langle\sigma(z,\bar{z})\rangle^{(\alpha\beta)}_{\mathbb{H}}\,,\qquad z\in\mathbb{H}\,.}

(2.7)

For
\alpha=\beta
, the function
\mathcal{F}_{N}^{(\alpha\alpha)}
reduces to a one-point function on
\mathbb{H}
, or equivalently on the unit disc
\mathbb{D}
:

\mathcal{F}_{N}^{(\alpha\alpha)}(z,\bar{z})=\langle\sigma(0)\rangle^{(\alpha)}_{\mathbb{D}}=g_{\alpha}^{1-N}\,,

(2.8)

[see (
3.24
)], and one recovers the well-known expression (
1.4
) of
[
2
]
.
In the generic situation
\alpha\neq\beta
, the function
\mathcal{F}_{N}^{(\alpha\beta)}
is non-trivial. It tends to a finite constant on each part of the boundary. Indeed, the leading behaviour is given by the bulk-boundary OPE
\sigma\to\mathbf{1}
, which yields

\lim_{\mathrm{Arg}(z)\to 0}\mathcal{F}_{N}^{(\alpha\beta)}(z,\bar{z})=g_{\alpha}^{1-N}\,,\qquad\lim_{\mathrm{Arg}(z)\to\pi}\mathcal{F}_{N}^{(\alpha\beta)}(z,\bar{z})=g_{\beta}^{1-N}\,.

(2.9)

The leading asymptotic behaviour of the entanglement entropy as the lattice spacing
a
approaches zero is captured by (
2.6

), up to an additive constant that is independent of the boundary condition and arises from the normalisation of the twist operator.
When comparing with a finite-size system, it is necessary to account for various sources of corrections originating from irrelevant deformations of the Hamiltonian in both the bulk and the boundary

or parity effects

]

Furthermore, it is well established

The most significant corrections arise from the subleading scaling of the lattice twist operators
[
46
]
. Like any other lattice operator, the lattice twist operator
\sigma_{\text{lattice}}
can be expressed, in the continuum limit, as a local combination of scaling operators

\displaystyle\sigma_{\text{lattice}}\sim\sum_{\phi\text{ primary}}C_{\phi}\,a^{2h_{\sigma_{\phi}}}\sigma_{\phi}+\dots

(2.10)

where
\dots
denotes the contribution of descendants under the orbifold Virasoro algebra.
Here,

is
the lattice spacing, the sum is over primary operators of the mother theory, and
\sigma_{\phi}
denotes the composite twist operator

,
79
,
46
]
associated to the primary operator
\phi

The conformal dimension of the composite twist operator is given by

h_{\sigma_{\phi}}=h_{\sigma}+\frac{h_{\phi}}{N}\,,

(2.11)

where
h_{\phi}
is the conformal dimension of
\phi
.
The constants
C_{\phi}
are non-universal, dimensionful amplitudes.
Thus, the expectation value of the twist operator reads, in the scaling limit,

\langle\sigma_{\text{lattice}}(\ell)\rangle_{\mathbb{S}_{L}}^{(\alpha\beta)}\sim\sum_{\phi\text{ primary}}C_{\phi}\,\left(\frac{\pi a}{2L\sin\frac{\pi\ell}{L}}\right)^{2h_{\sigma_{\phi}}}\mathcal{F}_{N,\phi}^{(\alpha\beta)}(z,\bar{z})+\dots

(2.12)

where
z=\exp(i\pi\ell/L)
, and the typical scaling function is given by

\displaystyle\boxed{\mathcal{F}_{N,\phi}^{(\alpha\beta)}(z,\bar{z})=|z-\bar{z}|^{2h_{\sigma_{\phi}}}\langle\sigma_{\phi}(z,\bar{z})\rangle^{(\alpha\beta)}_{\mathbb{H}}\,,\qquad z\in\mathbb{H}\,.}

(2.13)

It obeys the boundary conditions

\lim_{\mathrm{Arg}(z)\to 0}\mathcal{F}_{N,\phi}^{(\alpha\beta)}(z,\bar{z})=A_{\phi,\mathbf{1}}^{(\alpha)}\,g_{\alpha}^{1-N}\,,\qquad\lim_{\mathrm{Arg}(z)\to\pi}\mathcal{F}_{N,\phi}^{(\alpha\beta)}(z,\bar{z})=A_{\phi,\mathbf{1}}^{(\beta)}\,g_{\beta}^{1-N}\,,

(2.14)

where
A_{\phi,\mathbf{1}}^{(\alpha)}
and
A_{\phi,\mathbf{1}}^{(\beta)}
are bulk-boundary OPE coefficients in the mother CFT [see (
3.16
)].

One can implement the change of boundary conditions by the insertion of the BCCO
\Psi^{(\alpha\beta)}=(\psi^{(\alpha\beta)})^{\otimes N}
associated to the change of BC
\alpha\to\beta
on all replicas in the orbifold BCFT:

\displaystyle\langle X\rangle^{(\alpha\beta)}_{\mathbb{H}}=\frac{\langle\Psi^{(\beta\alpha)}(\infty)X\Psi^{(\alpha\beta)}(0)\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}}{\langle\Psi^{(\beta\alpha)}(\infty)\Psi^{(\alpha\beta)}(0)\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}}\,,

(2.15)

where
X
denotes any product of bulk operators.
An alternative expression for the scaling functions (
2.7
) and (
2.13
) can then be obtained through a conformal map from the upper half-plane
\mathbb{H}
to the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
, namely
u\mapsto(u-z)/(u-\bar{z})
. This yields

\boxed{\begin{aligned} &\mathcal{F}_{N}^{(\alpha\beta)}(z,\bar{z})=\frac{\langle\Psi^{(\beta\alpha)}(1)\sigma(0)\Psi^{(\alpha\beta)}(z/\bar{z})\rangle_{\mathbb{D}}}{\langle\Psi^{(\beta\alpha)}(1)\Psi^{(\alpha\beta)}(z/\bar{z})\rangle_{\mathbb{D}}}\,,\\
&\mathcal{F}_{N,\phi}^{(\alpha\beta)}(z,\bar{z})=\frac{\langle\Psi^{(\beta\alpha)}(1)\sigma_{\phi}(0)\Psi^{(\alpha\beta)}(z/\bar{z})\rangle_{\mathbb{D}}}{\langle\Psi^{(\beta\alpha)}(1)\Psi^{(\alpha\beta)}(z/\bar{z})\rangle_{\mathbb{D}}}\,.\end{aligned}}

(2.16)

As is customary with the
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
cyclic orbifold, one possible approach to studying the scaling function (
2.16
) is to work on the
N
-sheeted branched covering of the base manifold. In this approach, a three-point function as in the RHS of (
2.16
) becomes a disk partition function of the mother CFT, with the boundary partitioned into
2N
connected components, with alternating boundary conditions
\alpha
and
\beta
, as described in
[
58
]
. This can be generalized to the case of an excited twist field, yielding the expression

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{N,\phi}^{(\alpha\beta)}(z,\bar{z})=\frac{1}{N^{2Nh_{\psi}}}\frac{\Big\langle\prod_{a=0}^{N-1}\psi^{(\beta\alpha)}(\omega^{a})\psi^{(\alpha\beta)}\left(\omega^{a}(z/\bar{z})^{1/N}\right)\,\phi(0)\Big\rangle_{\mathbb{D}}}{\Big\langle\psi^{(\beta\alpha)}(1)\psi^{(\alpha\beta)}(z/\bar{z})\Big\rangle^{N}_{\mathbb{D}}}\,,

(2.17)

where
\omega=\exp(2i\pi/N)
. One of the main limitations of this unfolding method is the dramatic increase of moduli space. What starts as a simple one-variable problem, involving only the variable
\eta=z/\bar{z}
, becomes a more complicated problem with
2N
variables. Although, in the specific case under study, these
2N
positions of the operators are not entirely independent, calculating the
2N
-point correlation functions typically requires treating them as if they were. As a result, this method is only practical for certain CFTs, where the computation of such
2N
-point correlation functions can be carried out explicitly, such as in the Ising model or the free scalar field
[
58
]
. We thus adopt an alternative approach to calculate the scaling function.

Rather than unfolding the correlation function into the cumbersome expression (
2.17
), one can instead operate within the framework of the cyclic orbifold. Cardy’s doubling trick guarantees that the correlation function
\langle\Psi^{(\beta\alpha)}(\infty)\sigma_{\phi}(z,\bar{z})\Psi^{(\alpha\beta)}(0)\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}
is a linear combination of conformal blocks of the form

\sideset{{}_{\displaystyle\ \Psi^{(\alpha\beta)}(0)}^{\displaystyle\Psi^{(\beta\alpha)}(\infty)}}{{}_{\displaystyle\sigma_{\phi}(z)}^{\displaystyle\sigma_{\phi}^{\dagger}(\bar{z})}}{\mathop{\Bigg\rangle\overset{\displaystyle\Psi_{k}}{\!\!\!-\!\!\!-\!\!\!-\!\!\!-\!\!\!-\!\!\!-\!\!\!}\Bigg\langle}}\,.

(2.18)

After rescaling and observing that
|z-\bar{z}|=(z-\bar{z})/i
for any
z\in\mathbb{H}
, we thus obtain the scaling function as a linear combination of rescaled conformal blocks:

\mathcal{F}_{N,\phi}^{(\alpha\beta)}(z,\bar{z})=(\eta-1)^{2h_{\sigma_{\phi}}}\sum_{k}\,X_{k}\sideset{{}_{\displaystyle\ \Psi^{(\alpha\beta)}(0)}^{\displaystyle\Psi^{(\beta\alpha)}(\infty)}}{{}_{\displaystyle\sigma_{\phi}(\eta)}^{\displaystyle\sigma_{\phi}^{\dagger}(1)}}{\mathop{\Bigg\rangle\overset{\displaystyle\Psi_{k}}{\!\!\!-\!\!\!-\!\!\!-\!\!\!-\!\!\!-\!\!\!-\!\!\!}\Bigg\langle}}\,,\qquad\eta=z/\bar{z}\,.

(2.19)

The allowed intermediate states
\Psi_{k}
are in correspondence with untwisted boundary operators
[
80
]
, determined by the bulk-boundary OPE
\sigma_{\phi}\to\Psi_{k}
as the operator
\sigma_{\phi}
tends to the boundary and the boundary OPE
\Psi^{(\alpha\beta)}\cdot\Psi^{(\beta\alpha)}\to\Psi_{k}
, and the coefficients
X_{k}
can be related to the OPE structure constants.
Naturally, the complexity is conserved to some extent, and the main challenge here lies in dealing with orbifold conformal blocks, which are significantly more intricate than standard Virasoro ones
[
41
,
81
]
. However, it has been shown in
[
46
]
that provided
the operators
\psi^{(\alpha\beta)}
and/or
\phi
are degenerate under the Virasoro algebra in the mother CFT,
it is possible in some cases to derive a linear ODE for these conformal blocks. This is the approach we follow in this paper, under the assumption that the operator
\psi^{(\alpha\beta)}
is degenerate at level two.

2.2
Generic BCFT

We consider a
mother CFT with central charge
c<1
.
We shall use the Kac parameterisation for the central charge and the conformal dimensions

c=1-\frac{6(1-g)^{2}}{g}\,,\qquad h_{rs}=\frac{(r-sg)^{2}-(1-g)^{2}}{4g}\,,\qquad 0<g<1\,,

(2.20)

and we denote by
\phi_{rs}
the primary operator of dimension
h_{rs}
. As mentioned above, we restrict our analysis to the particular case when the operator
\psi^{(\alpha\beta)}

has conformal dimension
h_{\psi}=h_{12}=(3g-2)/4
, in which case we have

L_{-1}^{2}\psi^{(\alpha\beta)}=g\,L_{-2}\psi^{(\alpha\beta)}\,.

(2.21)

Under this assumption, we derive a linear ODE for the leading and subleading scaling functions at
N=2
, and for the leading scaling function at
N=3
. Using the Frobenius method, the solutions of these ODEs can always be represented as explicit, exact power series, which in some cases simplify to well-known special functions, such as hypergeometric functions.
The results presented below are expressed in terms of the parameterisation (see Appendix

)

\mathcal{F}(z,\bar{z})=F[\zeta(z/\bar{z})]\,,\qquad\zeta(\eta)=\frac{(1+\sqrt{\eta})^{2}}{4\sqrt{\eta}}\,.

In polar coordinates, if

and

The contribution to

from the conformal block with intermediate state

in the channel

is typically a power-series solution to the ODE, of the form

F_{k}(\zeta)=(1-\zeta)^{h_{\Psi_{k}}/2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n,k}\,(1-\zeta)^{n}\,.

Alternatively, one can work with the basis of solutions

, corresponding to the channel

.

3
The cyclic orbifold in the presence of a boundary

Here we review briefly the cyclic orbifold construction
[
44
,
45
,
80
]
, and we examine specifically the features of the corresponding orbifold boundary CFT, in the upper-half plane geometry. We follow the notations of
[
80
]
.

3.1
The cyclic orbifold construction

Consider the original CFT model
\mathcal{M}
, which we shall call the mother CFT. The cyclic orbifold CFT
\mathcal{M}_{N}
is obtained by taking
N
identical decoupled replicas of
\mathcal{M}
, and allowing for defect lines along which the copies are connected cyclically.

The underlying algebra for
\mathcal{M}_{N}
consists of currents
T^{(r)}(z)
, with
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{N}
playing the role of the discrete Fourier index with respect to the cyclic permutation of replicas. The total energy-momentum tensor of
\mathcal{M}_{N}
is given by
T^{(0)}
, and the other currents
T^{(r)}
can be viewed as additional conserved currents in an extended conformal algebra. The associated Laurent modes are denoted
L_{m}^{(r)}
,
where the index
m
belongs to
\mathbb{Z}/N
. They generate the orbifold Virasoro algebra
\mathrm{OVir}_{N}

\left[L_{m}^{(r)},L_{n}^{(s)}\right]=(m-n)L_{m+n}^{(r+s)}+\frac{Nc}{12}m(m^{2}-1)\,\delta_{m+n,0}\,\delta_{r+s,0}\,,

(3.1)

where
c
is the central charge of the mother CFT.
The invariant subalgebra (of the universal enveloping algebra) with respect to the
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
action is called the
neutral subalgebra

\mathcal{A}_{N}
. It is generated by monomials with a vanishing total Fourier index :

\mathcal{A}_{N}=\Big\langle L_{m_{1}}^{(r_{1})}\dots L_{m_{p}}^{(r_{p})}\,,\quad r_{1}+\dots+r_{p}\equiv 0\mod N\Big\rangle\,.

(3.2)

The orbifold Virasoro algebra and the neutral subalgebra have antiholomorphic counterparts
\overline{\rm OVir}_{N}
and
\overline{\cal A}_{N}
, respectively.

In the holomorphic part of the CFT
\mathcal{M}_{N}
for prime
N
, the operator content is classified in terms of the primary operators under the neutral subalgebra
\mathcal{A}_{N}
. It is shown in
[
80
]
that in the bulk CFT
\mathcal{M}_{N}
, the corresponding scalar operators form the full set of
\mathrm{OVir}_{N}\otimes\overline{\rm OVir}_{N}
primary operators which are invariant under cyclic permutations of the replicas.
There are three kinds of
\mathcal{A}_{N}
primary operators.
The
untwisted non-diagonal operators
are symmetrised products of non-identical primary operators
\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{N}
on the decoupled copies

[\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{N}]=\#\sum_{a=0}^{N-1}\phi_{a+1}\otimes\dots\otimes\phi_{a+N}\,,

(3.3)

The
untwisted diagonal operators
associated to a primary operator
\phi
of
\mathcal{M}
are of the form

\Phi^{(0)}=\Phi=\phi\otimes\dots\otimes\phi\,,\qquad\Phi^{(r)}=\begin{cases}\#L_{-1}^{(r)}\cdot(\phi\otimes\dots\otimes\phi)&\text{if }\phi\neq\mathbf{1}\\[5.0pt]
\#L_{-2}^{(r)}\cdot(\mathbf{1}\otimes\dots\otimes\mathbf{1})&\text{if }\phi=\mathbf{1}\end{cases}

(3.4)

where
r=1,\dots,N-1
.
The
twist operators
are the non-local operators inserting the endpoints of defect lines connecting the replicas cyclically. The bare twist operator
\sigma
corresponds to a cyclic permutation
a\mapsto a+1
across the defect, with boundary conditions around the branch point given by the vacuum state. The composite twist operator associated to the primary operator
\phi
of
\mathcal{M}
is the form
[
82
,
53
]

\sigma_{\phi}(z)=\#\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\left[\epsilon^{(N-1)h/N}\,[\phi,\mathbf{1},\dots,\mathbf{1}](z+\epsilon)\,\sigma(z)\right]\,,

(3.5)

where
h
is the conformal dimension of
\phi
.
For
\phi=\mathbf{1}
, one simply sets
\sigma_{\mathbf{1}}=\sigma
.
The twisted sector of the operator content also includes the conjugate twist operators
\sigma_{\phi}^{\dagger}
associated to
a\mapsto a-1
, and more generally the operators
\sigma_{\phi}^{[k]}
associated to
a\mapsto a+k
for
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{N}^{\times}
.
The orbifold Virasoro modes
L_{m}^{(r)}
acting on an untwisted operator must have an integer index
m
, otherwise they vanish. In contrast, due to the non-trivial monodromy of
T^{(r)}
around a branch point, the modes
L_{m}^{(r)}
mode acting on
\sigma
(resp.
\sigma^{\dagger}
) must have an index
m\in\mathbb{Z}-r/N
(resp.
m\in\mathbb{Z}+r/N
), and similarly for
\sigma_{\phi}
and
\sigma^{\dagger}_{\phi}
.
The following operators are also primary under
\mathcal{A}_{N}
:

\sigma_{\phi}^{(r)}=\#\,L_{-r/N}^{(r)}\cdot\sigma_{\phi}\,,\qquad\sigma_{\phi}^{{\dagger}(r)}=\#\,L_{-(N-r)/N}^{(r)}\cdot\sigma_{\phi}^{\dagger}\,,

(3.6)

for
r=1,\dots,N
.

The conformal dimensions of the above operators read

\displaystyle h_{[\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{N}]}=h_{\phi_{1}}+\dots+h_{\phi_{N}}\,,\qquad h_{\Phi}=Nh_{\phi}\,,\qquad h_{\Phi^{(r)}}=\begin{cases}Nh_{\phi}+1&\text{if }\phi\neq\mathbf{1}\\
2&\text{if }\phi=\mathbf{1}\end{cases}

(3.7)

\displaystyle h_{\sigma_{\phi}}=h_{\sigma_{\phi}^{\dagger}}=\frac{c}{24}\left(N-\frac{1}{N}\right)+\frac{h_{\phi}}{N}\,,\qquad h_{\sigma_{\phi}^{(r)}}=h_{\sigma_{\phi}^{{\dagger}(N-r)}}=h_{\sigma_{\phi}}+\frac{r}{N}\,,

where
h_{\phi}
denotes the conformal dimension of
\phi
.
The normalisation factors
\#
in (
3.3
–
3.6
) are chosen so that the two-point function of any
\mathcal{A}_{N}
primary operator
\mathcal{O}
on the full plane satisfies
\langle\mathcal{O}(0)\mathcal{O}^{\dagger}(1)\rangle_{\mathbb{C}}=1
.

3.2
The cyclic orbifold on the upper half-plane

To construct the cyclic orbifold BCFT, we will work on the upper half-plane
\mathbb{H}
, with the boundary along the real axis. We parameterise
\mathbb{H}
by
z=x+iy
with
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
y>0
, and we impose the gluing condition on the boundary for the stress-energy tensor components:

T^{(0)}(x)=\overline{T}^{(0)}(x)\quad\text{for}\quad x\in\mathbb{R}\,,

(3.8)

which ensures that the boundary is conformal,
i.e.
it preserves the total stress-energy tensor
[
83
]
. The
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
orbifold, however, has an extended symmetry, and we must choose if and how the components of the additional currents
T^{(r\neq 0)}
are glued at the boundary. Our usage of the replica trick provides a clear indication for these choices: since we are considering
N
copies of the
same
mother BCFT, we must impose the gluing condition
T_{a}(x)=\overline{T}_{a}(x)
on each of them. By taking the Fourier transform of this relation, we find that in the orbifold CFT we are effectively imposing:

\forall r\in\mathbb{Z}_{N}\,,\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}\,,\qquad T^{(r)}(x)=\overline{T}^{(r)}(x)\,.

(3.9)

This implies that the boundary preserves a full copy of the
\mathrm{OVir}_{N}
algebra. By the same reasoning on CFT replicas, the physically relevant orbifold boundary states correspond to having the same conformal BC on the
N
copies of the mother CFT.

On the upper half-plane, we will set the conformal BC
\alpha
on the positive real axis
x>0
and the conformal BC
\beta
on
x<0
. To implement such mixed conformal BC in a BCFT, we shall work with the formalism of
boundary condition changing operators
(BCCOs)
[
54
]
. These operators, restricted to live on the boundary, are placed at the points of suture of regions of different BC. The full operator algebra of a BCFT is then formed by considering the OPEs between both BCCOs and bulk operators.
For a given pair of conformal BCs
(\alpha,\beta)
, there can be several primary BCCOs implementing the change
\alpha\to\beta
: we denote such an operator
\psi_{h}^{(\alpha\beta)}
, where
h
specifies its conformal dimension. The most relevant BCCO implementing
\alpha\to\beta
is simply referred to as
\psi^{(\alpha\beta)}
.

In the
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
orbifold CFT, we only consider
untwisted
BCCOs. We define the diagonal BCCOs as

\Psi_{h}^{(\alpha\beta)}=\underbrace{\psi_{h}^{(\alpha\beta)}\otimes\dots\otimes\psi_{h}^{(\alpha\beta)}}_{N\text{ times}}\,,

(3.10)

and likewise for
\Psi^{(\alpha\beta)}
, while the non-diagonal BCCOs are

[\psi_{h_{1}},\dots,\psi_{h_{N}}]^{(\alpha\beta)}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{a=0}^{N-1}\psi_{h_{a+1}}^{(\alpha\beta)}\otimes\dots\otimes\psi_{h_{a+N}}^{(\alpha\beta)}\,,

(3.11)

where at least two dimensions
h_{i},h_{j}
are distinct.
Orbifold correlators with mixed BC are obtained by inserting the most relevant diagonal BCCOs:

\langle\mathcal{O}_{1}(z_{1},\bar{z}_{1})\dots\mathcal{O}_{n}(z_{n},\bar{z}_{n})\rangle^{\alpha\beta}_{\mathbb{H}}=\frac{\langle\Psi^{(\beta\alpha)}(\infty)\,\mathcal{O}_{1}(z_{1},\bar{z}_{1})\dots\mathcal{O}_{n}(z_{n},\bar{z}_{n})\Psi^{(\alpha\beta)}(0)\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}}{\langle\Psi^{(\beta\alpha)}(\infty)\Psi^{(\alpha\beta)}(0)\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}}\,.

(3.12)

By Cardy’s doubling trick
[
83
,
55
]
, such
(n+2)
-point correlators are given by linear combinations of the
(2n+2)
-point conformal blocks on the Riemann sphere
\mathbb{C}
with external operators

\Psi(\infty),\mathcal{O}_{1}(z_{1}),\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{1}(\bar{z}_{1}),\dots,\mathcal{O}_{n}(z_{n}),\overline{\cal O}_{n}(\bar{z}_{n}),\Psi(0)\,,

(3.13)

where
\overline{\mathcal{O}_{i}}(\bar{z})
is the antiholomorphic counterpart of
\mathcal{O}_{i}(z)
. In more precise terms,
\overline{\mathcal{O}_{i}}
is the operator conjugate to
\mathcal{O}_{i}
with respect to the symmetry algebra preserved by the boundary
[
84
]
. For
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
twist operators, conjugation acts as
\overline{\sigma}_{\phi}=\sigma^{\dagger}_{\phi}

[
43
]
. For instance, the one-twist function

\langle\sigma(z,\bar{z})\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}^{(\alpha\beta)}=\frac{\langle\Psi^{(\beta\alpha)}(\infty)\sigma(z,\bar{z})\Psi^{(\alpha\beta)}(0)\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}}{\langle\Psi^{(\beta\alpha)}(\infty)\Psi^{(\alpha\beta)}(0)\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}}

(3.14)

is given by a linear combination of conformal blocks of the form

\sideset{{}_{\displaystyle\ \Psi^{(\alpha\beta)}(0)}^{\displaystyle\Psi^{(\beta\alpha)}(\infty)}}{{}_{\displaystyle\sigma(z)}^{\displaystyle\sigma^{\dagger}(\bar{z})}}{\mathop{\Bigg\rangle\overset{\displaystyle\Psi_{k}}{\!\!\!-\!\!\!-\!\!\!-\!\!\!-\!\!\!-\!\!\!-\!\!\!}\Bigg\langle}}\quad.

(3.15)

The intermediate state
\Psi_{k}
is a diagonal or non-diagonal untwisted boundary operator. It must be compatible both with the boundary condition
\alpha
, and the boundary condition
\beta
. For instance, if
\Psi_{k}
is diagonal, it must be of the form
\Psi_{k}=\psi_{h}\otimes\dots\otimes\psi_{h}
, where
h
is such that both BCCOs
\psi_{h}^{(\alpha\alpha)}
and
\psi_{h}^{(\beta\beta)}
are allowed in the mother CFT.

In the mother BCFT, bulk-boundary and boundary-boundary OPEs read

\displaystyle\phi(x+iy,x-iy)\underset{y\to 0}{\sim}\sum_{h}A_{\phi,\psi_{h}}^{(\alpha)}\,(2y)^{h-2h_{\phi}}\,\psi_{h}(x)+\dots

(3.16)

\displaystyle\psi_{h_{1}}^{(\alpha\beta)}(x_{1})\psi_{h_{2}}^{(\beta\gamma)}(x_{2})\underset{x_{1}\to x_{2}}{\sim}\sum_{h_{3}}B^{(\alpha\beta\gamma)}_{h_{1},h_{2},h_{3}}(x_{1}-x_{2})^{-h_{1}-h_{2}+h_{3}}\psi_{h_{3}}^{(\alpha\gamma)}(x_{2})+\dots

(3.17)

In (
3.16
),
\phi
denotes a scalar bulk operator and
\alpha
is the BC on the real axis. In (
3.17
),

\psi_{h_{1}}^{(\alpha\beta)}(x_{1})
and
\psi_{h_{2}}^{(\beta\gamma)}(x_{2})
are two BCCOs, with
x_{1}>x_{2}
. The
\dots
denote the contributions from the Virasoro descendants.
We write these OPEs in shorthand notation as

\displaystyle\phi\big|_{\alpha}\to\sum_{h}\psi_{h}\,,\qquad\psi_{h_{1}}^{(\alpha\beta)}\cdot\psi_{h_{2}}^{(\beta\gamma)}\to\sum_{h_{3}}\psi_{h_{3}}^{(\alpha\gamma)}\,.

(3.18)

The OPE structure constants can be expressed as correlation functions:

\displaystyle A_{\phi,\psi_{h}}^{(\alpha)}=\langle\phi(i/2,-i/2)\psi_{h}(0)\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}^{(\alpha)}=\langle\phi(0)\psi_{h}(1)\rangle_{\mathbb{D}}^{(\alpha)}\,,

(3.19)

\displaystyle B^{(\alpha\beta\gamma)}_{h_{1},h_{2},h_{3}}=\langle\psi_{3}^{(\gamma\alpha)}(\infty)\psi_{1}^{(\alpha\beta)}(1)\psi_{2}^{(\beta\gamma)}(0)\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}\,.

(3.20)

Correspondingly, in the orbifold BCFT, we have the OPEs

\displaystyle\sigma_{\phi}(x+iy,x-iy)\underset{y\to 0}{\sim}\sum_{\Psi^{(\alpha\alpha)}}\mathcal{A}^{(\alpha)}_{\sigma_{\phi},\Psi}\,(2y)^{h_{\Psi}-2h_{\sigma_{\phi}}}\,\Psi^{(\alpha\alpha)}(x)+\dots

(3.21)

7
Conclusion

In this article, we have outlined a general method for computing Rényi entropies in the ground state of a one-dimensional critical system with mixed open boundaries, specifically for an interval starting at one of its ends. Given the significant finite size effects in open chains, we also examined the leading finite-size correction, which results from subleading scaling operators in the expansion of the lattice twist operators. Within the conformal field theory framework, this involves computing certain three-point functions on the upper-half plane in the
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
cyclic orbifold: two boundary condition changing operators, and one twist operator (bare or excited).

To achieve this, we derived Fuchsian differential equations satisfied by these correlation functions, by exploiting the null-vectors of the twisted and untwisted representations of its symmetry algebra
\mathrm{OVir}_{N}
, along with Ward identities obtained from the additional conserved currents of the theory. The Frobenius method then systematically provides a way to find power series solutions to these differential equations. The correlation functions are obtained as particular linear combinations of these conformal blocks, with coefficients given by certain boundary and bulk-boundary structure constants.

Among the cases we have analysed are the leading and subleading contributions to the one-interval second and third Rényi entropies of the Ising model, and the second Rényi entropy for the three-state Potts model. We have also derived differential equations for mixed BC twist correlators in the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
and
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
orbifolds of generic BCFTs, as well as an explicit solution for the second Rényi entropy.

We analyzed the CFT results, incorporating the first subleading correction, and compared them with numerical data for the critical Ising and three-state Potts spin chains. In both cases, we found remarkable agreement across all mixed boundary condition choices, despite the constraints of limited system sizes. Expanding to larger system sizes could be possible through advanced numerical approaches such as DMRG
[
97
,
98
]
or tensor network methods
[
69
]
.

An important limitation of our method, first identified in
[
46
]
, is that obtaining a differential equation becomes increasingly difficult as the Rényi index
N
is increased. We have verified, using the fusion rules in
[
80
]
, that the expected order of the ODEs grows with
N
for generic minimal models, indicating that more orbifold Ward identities will be needed to obtain the ODEs.

A possible extension of this work would be to generalize the setup for the calculation of Rényi entropies of an interval
contained in the bulk
, with mixed BC. However, in this situation, one would have to find a partial differential equation that a four-point function with two twist fields and two BCCOs satisfies. Cardy’s doubling trick tells us that such a correlator is given by a linear combination of six-point conformal blocks on the complex plane, so the corresponding differential equation would be partial instead of ordinary.

Change of variable for Fuchsian differential equations

In Appendix

\eta\mapsto 1/\eta

Consider a generic ODE of Fuchsian type in the variable

\eta=0,1

Appendix D
Orbifold structure constants

In the orbifold BCFT, the operator algebra consists of OPEs of three types. First, there is the operator subalgebra of bulk operators. We shall not directly use the structure constants of this subalgebra in this work, but they have been discussed in
[
45
,
46
,
100
,
81
]
.
The second type of OPE we need to consider in the orbifold BCFT, is the bulk-boundary OPE which encapsulates the singular behaviour of a bulk field as it approaches a conformal boundary. In our calculations, we will only need to work with the OPEs of generic (primary) twist field
\sigma_{\phi}(z,\bar{z})
approaches a (diagonal) conformal boundary
\alpha
:

\sigma_{\phi}(x,y)\underset{y\to 0}{\sim}\sum_{k}\mathcal{A}^{(\alpha)}_{\sigma_{\phi},\Psi_{k}}\,(2y)^{h_{\Psi_{k}}-2h_{\sigma_{\phi}}}\,\left(\Psi_{k}^{\alpha\alpha}(x)+\cdots\right)

(D.1)

where the sum runs over all the diagonal primary boundary operators
\Psi_{k}^{(\alpha\alpha)}=\psi_{k}^{(\alpha\alpha)}\otimes\cdots\otimes\psi_{k}^{(\alpha\alpha)}
, and the dots stand for their descendants under the neutral subalgebra
[
80
]
. We also need to consider the OPEs of orbifold boundary operators. For generic diagonal BCCOs, this takes the form

\Psi_{i}^{(\alpha\beta)}(x_{1})\Psi_{j}^{(\beta\gamma)}(x_{2})\underset{x_{1}\to x_{2}}{\sim}\sum_{k}\mathcal{B}^{(\alpha\beta\gamma)}_{\Psi_{i},\Psi_{j},\Psi_{k}}(x_{1}-x_{2})^{h_{k}-h_{i}-h_{j}}\left(\Psi_{k}^{(\alpha\gamma)}(x_{2})+\cdots\right)

(D.2)

for
x_{1}>x_{2}
, and where the index
k
runs over all the primary orbifold BCCOs interpolating between the conformal boundary conditions
\alpha
and
\gamma
. In the present work we are only concerned with untwisted boundary fields. The corresponding structure constants
\mathcal{B}^{(\alpha\beta\gamma)}_{\Psi_{i},\Psi_{j},\Psi_{k}}
are straightforward to evaluate as they simply factorize into a linear combination of products of mother BCFT three-point functions. Therefore, we will focus on the bulk-boundary structure constants
\mathcal{A}^{(\alpha)}_{\sigma_{\phi},\Psi_{k}}
. These structure constants can be computed using factorization and unfolding arguments, along the lines of
[
80
]
,
[
85
]
and
[
100
]
.

The main result derived in this appendix is that these orbifold structure constants are given in terms of the mother theory data by

\displaystyle\boxed{\mathcal{A}^{(\alpha)}_{\sigma_{\phi},\Psi_{k}}=g_{\alpha}^{1-N}\,N^{-Nh_{\psi_{k}}}\,\left\langle\phi(0)\psi_{k}^{(\alpha\alpha)}(1)\psi_{k}^{(\alpha\alpha)}(\omega)\cdots\psi_{k}^{(\alpha\alpha)}(\omega^{N-1})\right\rangle^{\alpha}_{\mathbb{D}}}\,.

(D.3)

Two particular cases are used in this paper :

•

when
\Psi_{k}^{\alpha\alpha}=\mathbf{1}
, this simply becomes

\boxed{\mathcal{A}^{(\alpha)}_{\sigma_{\phi},\mathbf{1}}=g_{\alpha}^{1-N}\,A^{(\alpha)}_{\phi}}

(D.4)

•

for
N=2
and
\Phi=\mathbf{1}
this simplifies to

\displaystyle\boxed{\mathcal{A}^{(\alpha)}_{\sigma,\Psi_{k}}=g_{\alpha}^{-1}\,2^{-2h_{\psi_{k}}}\left\langle\psi_{k}^{(\alpha\alpha)}(1)\psi_{k}^{(\alpha\alpha)}(-1)\right\rangle^{\alpha}_{\mathbb{D}}=g_{\alpha}^{-1}\,2^{-4h_{\psi_{k}}}}

(D.5)

provided the field
\psi_{k}
exists on the boundary
\alpha
.

The derivation of (
D.3
) can be done in two steps.
First, consider the bulk-boundary structure constant
\mathcal{A}^{(\alpha)}_{\sigma,\mathbf{1}}
, which can be expressed as the following one-point function on the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
:

\mathcal{A}^{(\alpha)}_{\sigma,\mathbf{1}}=\langle\sigma(0)\rangle_{\mathbb{D}}^{\alpha}\,,

(D.6)

This can be interpreted as the following ratio of mother CFT partition functions:

\langle\sigma(0)\rangle_{\mathbb{D}}^{\alpha}=\frac{\mathcal{Z}^{(\alpha)}_{\mathbb{D}_{N}}}{\left[\mathcal{Z}^{(\alpha)}_{\mathbb{D}}\right]^{N}}\,,

(D.7)

where
\mathbb{D}_{N}
denotes the
N

\mathbb{D}_{N}
is conformally equivalent to the standard unit disk
\mathbb{D}

101

\mathcal{Z}^{(\alpha)}_{\mathbb{D}_{N}}=\mathcal{Z}^{(\alpha)}_{\mathbb{D}}\,,

leading to the result

\langle\sigma(0)\rangle_{\mathbb{D}}^{\alpha}=\left[\mathcal{Z}^{(\alpha)}_{\mathbb{D}}\right]^{1-N}\,.

Additionally, the partition function

is equal to the

|0\rangle
and the boundary state
|\alpha\rangle

\mathcal{A}^{(\alpha)}_{\sigma,\Psi_{\mathbf{1}}}=g_{\alpha}^{1-N}\,.

(D.10)

This structure constant is responsible for the well-known universal additive correction

[
2
]

Consider now the generic structure constant

(D.11)

Now the ratio of correlation functions

(D.12)

N

and one bulk field
\phi

N

We note that the structure constant (

A bound on the L 2 L^{2} -norm of a projective structure by the length of the bending lamination
Martin Bridgeman, Kenneth Bromberg

Abstract
One can associate to a complex projective structure on a surface holomorphic quadratic differential Φ \Phi via the Schwarzian derivative and a bending lamination λ \lambda via the Thurston parameterization. In this note we obtain upper bounds on the L 2 L^{2} -norm of Φ \Phi in terms of the length of λ \lambda . The proof uses the theory of W W -volume introduced by Krasnov-Schlenker.



1
Introduction

Complex projective structures on a surface
S
have two parameterizations. Classically they are given via the Schwarzian derivative by a pair
(X,\Phi)
where
X
is a conformal structure on
S
and
\Phi
is a holomorphic quadratic differential on
X
. Thurston gave an alternative parameterization by pairs
(Y,\lambda)
where
Y
is a hyperbolic structure and
\lambda
is a measured geodesic lamination on
Y
. Both of these parameterizations give a way of measuring the “size” of the projective structure. The space of holomorphic quadratic differentials on
X
is similar to a function space and we can take the
L^{p}
-norms of
\Phi
with respect to the hyperbolic metric on
X
while a measured lamination on a hyperbolic surface has a length
L(\lambda)
. Naturally one would like to obtain comparisons between the norm of
\Phi
and the length
L(\lambda)
. Note that the subspace of projective structures where
\|\Phi\|_{p}=0
is equal to the subspace where
L(\lambda)=0
and this happens exactly when the conformal structure on
Y
is
X
.

One example of such a comparison is the following result which is a direct application of an inequality of G. Anderson (
[
And
, Theorem 4.2]
) comparing the hyperbolic and projective metrics on a projective structure.

Theorem 1.1
(Anderson,
[
BBB
, Theorem 2.10]
)

If
\Sigma
is a projective structure with Schwarzian parameterization
\left(X_{\Sigma},\Phi_{\Sigma}\right)
and Thurston parameterization
\left(Y_{\Sigma},\lambda_{\Sigma}\right)
then

L\left(\lambda_{\Sigma}\right)\leq 4\pi|\chi(\Sigma)|\left\|\Phi_{\Sigma}\right\|_{\infty}.

In
[
BBB
]
bounds on
L\left(\lambda_{\Sigma}\right)
in terms of the
L^{2}
-norm of
\Phi_{\Sigma}
are also obtained.

The goal of this note is to bound the
L^{2}
-norm of the quadratic differential in terms of the length of the lamination. We prove the following:

Theorem 1.2

Let
\Sigma
be a projective structure that is the quotient of a domain in
\widehat{{\mathbb{C}}}
with Schwarzian parameterization
\left(X_{\Sigma},\Phi_{\Sigma}\right)
and Thurston parameterization
\left(Y_{\Sigma},\lambda_{\Sigma}\right)
. Then

\left\|\Phi_{\Sigma}\right\|_{2}\leq\left(1+\left\|\Phi_{\Sigma}\right\|_{\infty}\right)\sqrt{L\left(\lambda_{\Sigma}\right)}.

The restriction to projective structures that are quotients of domains in
\widehat{{\mathbb{C}}}
is not necessary but it will simplify the proof. On the other hand, if
\Sigma
is the quotient of a disk in
\widehat{{\mathbb{C}}}
then by the Nehari bound on the Schwarzian (
[
Neh
]
)
\left\|\Phi_{\Sigma}\right\|_{\infty}\leq 3/2
and we have the immediate corollary:

Corollary 1.3

Let
\Sigma
be a projective structure that is the quotient of a disk in
\widehat{{\mathbb{C}}}
. Then

\left\|\Phi_{\Sigma}\right\|_{2}\leq\frac{5}{2}\sqrt{L\left(\lambda_{\Sigma}\right)}.

Thurston’s parameterization of projective structures involves associating the projective structure with a locally convex pleated surface in
{{\mathbb{H}}^{3}}
, pleated along the lamination
\lambda
. A parallel construction of Epstein gives an immersed surface whose geometry is described by the Schwarzian
\Phi
. The proof of Theorem
1.2
involves the
W
-volume
of the region between the two surfaces. This is a concept introduced by Krasnov and Schlenker
[
KS
]
. While it is generally applied to convex, compact hyperbolic 3-manifolds there is a natural relative version for pairs of conformal metrics on a projective structure and this is what we will use.

2
Epstein Surfaces

In
[
Eps
]
, Epstein described how to obtain a surface in
{{\mathbb{H}}^{3}}
from a conformal metric on a domain in
\partial{{\mathbb{H}}^{3}}=\hat{\mathbb{C}}
. Let
\hat{g}
be a conformal metric on a domain
\Omega
of
\hat{\mathbb{C}}
. For each
x\in{{\mathbb{H}}^{3}}
we define the
\rho_{x}
to be the
visual metric
on
\hat{\mathbb{C}}
from
x
. Thus
\rho_{x}
is the image of the round metric on the sphere under any mobius transformation sending
0
to
x
in the Poincare ball model of
{{\mathbb{H}}^{3}}
. Given
z\in\Omega
we define

\mathfrak{H}(\hat{g},z)=\left\{x\in{{\mathbb{H}}^{3}}\ |\ \hat{g}(z)=\rho_{x}(z)\right\}

Then
\mathfrak{H}(\hat{g},z)
is a horosphere based at
z
. Then the
Epstein map
is a map

\operatorname{Ep}_{\hat{g}}\colon\Omega\to{{\mathbb{H}}^{3}}

such that for each
z\in\Omega
the image surface is tangent to the horosphere
\mathfrak{H}(\hat{g},z)
.

We now collect some fundamental properties of Epstein surfaces and their shape operators that we will need.

Proposition 2.1
(Epstein
[
Eps
]
)

Let
\hat{g}
be a smooth conformal metric on a domain
\Omega\subset\widehat{{\mathbb{C}}}
and let
\hat{g}_{t}=e^{2t}\hat{g}
.

1.

For each
z\in\Omega
the map
\operatorname{Ep}_{\hat{g}_{t}}
is an immersion in a neighborhood of
z
for all but at most two values of
t
.

2.

The map
\operatorname{Ep}_{\hat{g}_{t}}
is the time
t
of the normal flow of
\operatorname{Ep}_{\hat{g}}
.

When
\operatorname{Ep}_{\hat{g}_{t}}
is an immersion let
g_{t}
is the pullback via
\operatorname{Ep}^{*}_{\hat{g}_{t}}
of the hyperbolic metric on
{{\mathbb{H}}^{3}}
and
B_{t}
the shape operator for the immersed surface. Then

3.

\hat{g}_{t}=({\operatorname{Id}}+B_{t})^{*}g_{t};

4.

B_{t}=(\cosh(t-s)\cdot{\operatorname{Id}}+\sinh(t-s)\cdot B_{s})^{-1}(\sinh(t-s)\cdot{\operatorname{Id}}+\cosh(t-s)\cdot B_{s});

5.

-1
is never an eigenvalue of
B_{t}
.

If we let
\hat{B}_{t}=({\operatorname{Id}}+B_{t})^{-1}({\operatorname{Id}}-B_{t})
then

6.

g_{t}=\frac{1}{4}({\operatorname{Id}}+\hat{B}_{t})^{*}\hat{g}_{t}
;

7.

\hat{B}_{t}=e^{-2(t-s)}\hat{B}_{s}
.

The eigenvalues of
B_{t}
are non-negative if and only if the eigenvalues of
\hat{B}_{t}
are in the interval
(-1,1]
.

Property (2) is
[
Eps
, Theorem 2.1]
while (3) and (4) are direct consequences of
[
Eps
, Theorem 3.1]
. The operator
\hat{B}_{t}
was introduced by Krasnov-Schlenker (
[
KS
]
). The remaining properties follow immediately from the others.

Note the formula in (4) only makes sense at a point where
B_{s}
is defined and when
(\cosh(t-s)\cdot{\operatorname{Id}}+\sinh(t-s)\cdot B_{s})^{-1}
is invertible. If
B_{s}
is defined then this holds as long as none of the eigenvalues of
B_{s}
are
\coth(t-s)
. As
B_{s}
has two eigenvalues (counted with multiplicity) this can happen at most twice and this explains (1) that at any point in
\Omega
the map
\operatorname{Ep}_{\hat{g}_{t}}
is an immersion for all but at most two values of
t
. By (5), whenever
B_{t}
is defined so is
\hat{B}_{t}
. However, from (7) it is clear that
\hat{B}_{t}
extends continuously and smoothly to all
t
. Note that all the information in
\hat{B}_{t}
is contained in
B_{t}
but this fact, along with the simplicity of the formula, are the advantage of considering
\hat{B}_{t}
.

The hyperbolic metric

If
\hat{g}_{h}
is the hyperbolic metric for
\Omega
then the shape operator
B
for the Epstein surface has a very explicit description in terms of the
Schwarzian derivative
of uniformizing map. In particular, if
\widehat{{\mathbb{C}}}\smallsetminus\Omega
contains at least 3 points there is a universal covering map

f\colon\Delta\to\Omega

from the unit disk
\Delta
that is conformal. This map is unique up to pre-composition with Möbius transformations fixing the disk and its Schwarzian derivative determines a holomorphic quadratic differential
\Phi_{\Omega}=\phi_{\Omega}dz^{2}
on
\Omega
. Then
\hat{g}_{h}=\rho_{h}\hat{g}_{\rm euc}
where
\rho_{h}
is a smooth function and
\hat{g}_{\rm euc}
is the Euclidean metric. The pointwise norm of the Schwarzian is given by the function
\|\Phi_{\Omega}(z)\|=\frac{|\phi_{\Omega}(z)|}{\rho_{h}(z)}
. We let
\|\Phi_{\Omega}\|_{\infty}
be the
L^{\infty}
-norm of this function.

Proposition 2.2
(Epstein,
[
Eps
, Proposition 7.4]
)

If
\hat{g}_{h}
is the hyperbolic metric for
\Omega
then the eigenvalues of the shape operator
B
at
z\in\Omega
are
-\frac{\|\Phi_{\Omega}(z)\|}{\|\Phi_{\Omega}(z)\|\pm 1}
. Therefore the eigenvalues of
\hat{B}_{t}
are
e^{-2t}(1\pm 2\|\Phi_{\Omega}(z)\|)
and the shape operator
B_{t}
has non-negative eigenvalues if
e^{2t}\geq 1+2\|\Phi_{\Omega}\|_{\infty}
.

Convex Epstein surfaces

For certain surfaces in
{{\mathbb{H}}^{3}}
we can also reverse this process (whenever the
hyperbolic Gauss map
is injective). The setting that will be important here is when the surface bounds a convex region in
{{\mathbb{H}}^{3}}
. Let
C\subset{{\mathbb{H}}^{3}}
be a closed convex region and
\bar{C}
the closure of
C
in
{{\mathbb{H}}^{3}}\cup\widehat{{\mathbb{C}}}
. Then
\Lambda=\bar{C}\cap\widehat{{\mathbb{C}}}
is a closed subset of
\widehat{{\mathbb{C}}}
. Then
\Omega=\widehat{{\mathbb{C}}}\smallsetminus\Lambda
is open and for each
z\in\Omega
there will be a unique horosphere that meets
C
at a single point
x
and we define
\hat{g}_{C}=\rho_{x}
at
z
. If
\partial C
is smooth then
\hat{g}_{C}
will be smooth and we have
\operatorname{Ep}_{\hat{g}_{C}}(z)=x
.

If the boundary of
C
is not smooth then the metric
\hat{g}
is still defined and it will be continuous but not necessarily differentiable. We can take
\operatorname{Ep}_{\hat{g}_{C}}(z)=x
as the definition of the Epstein map. The map will be continuous and surjective but not necessarily smooth or injective. As the map is not smooth we cannot pull back the hyperbolic metric on
{{\mathbb{H}}^{3}}
to
\Omega
. We will only be interested in a particular special case where
\partial C
is not smooth. We describe this next.

Let
{\rm CH}(\Lambda)
be the smallest closed, convex subset of
{{\mathbb{H}}^{3}}
such that
\overline{{\rm CH}(\Lambda)}\cap\widehat{{\mathbb{C}}}=\Lambda
.
The boundary of
{\rm CH}(\Lambda)
is a
convex pleated surface
.
In particular, the intrinsic path metric on
\partial{\rm CH}(\Lambda)
is a complete hyperbolic metric and in a complement of a geodesic lamination
\lambda
the surface is totally geodesic. The surface is bent along
\lambda
and the amount of bending gives
\lambda
a transverse measure making it a measured geodesic lamination.

There is another intrinsic description of
\hat{g}_{{\rm CH}(\Lambda)}
.
First, let
\hat{g}_{D}
be the hyperbolic metric on a round disk
D\subset\widehat{{\mathbb{C}}}
. Then for each
z\in\Omega
define
\hat{g}_{\Omega}
at
z
to be the infimum of
\hat{g}_{D}
at
z
over all
D\subset\Omega
. This is the
projective metric
for
\Omega
and
\hat{g}_{\Omega}=\hat{g}_{{\rm CH}(\Lambda)}
.

3
Complex projective structures

A
complex projective structure

\Sigma
on an surface
S
is an atlas to
\widehat{{\mathbb{C}}}
where the transition maps are restrictions of elements of
{\rm PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})
. One example is when
\Omega
is a domain in
\widehat{{\mathbb{C}}}
and
\Gamma\subset{\rm PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})
is a deck acton on
\Omega
. Then the quotient
\Sigma=\Omega/\Gamma
has a natural complex projective structure. As the Epstein map is natural, if
\hat{g}
is a
\Gamma
-invariant conformal metric on
\Omega
the
\operatorname{Ep}_{\hat{g}}
will also be
\Gamma
-invariant and the
\operatorname{Ep}_{\hat{g}}
-pullback
g
of the hyperbolic metric on
{{\mathbb{H}}^{3}}
will also be
\Gamma
-invariant. We will abuse notation and refer to the quotient metrics on
\Sigma
also as
\hat{g}
and
g
. Note that
g
may be singular but if the image is a convex surface
g
will be a smooth metric.

Proposition 3.1
(
[
BBB
, Lemma 3.1]
)

Let
\hat{g}
be a smooth conformal metric on a projective structure
\Sigma=\Omega/\Gamma
and assume that the eigenvalues of the shape operator
B
for Epstein surface are non-negative. If
g
is the Epstein metric then

\int_{S}HdA=\frac{1}{2}{\operatorname{{\bf area}}}(\hat{g})-{\operatorname{{\bf area}}}(g)-\pi\chi(S)

where
H=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{tr}B
is the mean curvature.

Now let
\tilde{C}\subset{{\mathbb{H}}^{3}}
be a
\Gamma
-invariant closed, convex subset such that intersection of the closure of
\tilde{C}
with
\widehat{{\mathbb{C}}}
is
\widehat{{\mathbb{C}}}\smallsetminus\Omega
. While the boundary of
C=\tilde{C}/\Gamma
may not be smooth we can still take the area of the induced path metric
g_{C}
and of the projective metric
\hat{g}_{C}
on
\Sigma
. In particular, we can define

{\cal B}(C)=\frac{1}{2}{\operatorname{{\bf area}}}(\hat{g}_{C})-{\operatorname{{\bf area}}}(g_{C})-\pi\chi(\Sigma).

Let
\hat{g}_{h}
be the hyperbolic metric for the conformal structure on
\Sigma
and let
\hat{g}_{\Sigma}
be the projective metric. By Proposition
2.2
the shape operators for the Epstein metric of
(\hat{g}_{h})_{t}=e^{2t}\hat{g}_{h}
have non-negative eigenvalues when
e^{2t}\geq 1+2\|\Phi_{\Omega}\|_{\infty}
. For the projective metric, the rescaled metrics
(\hat{g}_{\Sigma})_{t}=e^{2t}\hat{g}_{\Sigma}
are the conformal metric of a convex subset whenever
t\geq 0
. A key to our estimate is that in both case we can explicitly calculate the area of the metrics at infinity and the corresponding Epstein metric.

For the hyperbolic metric we have:

Lemma 3.2

Let
\hat{g}_{h}
be the hyperbolic metric on
\Sigma
and
(g_{h})_{t}
be the intrinsic metric on
\operatorname{Ep}_{e^{2t}\hat{g}_{h}}
. Then for
t>\frac{1}{2}\log\left(1+2\left\|\Phi_{\Sigma}\right\|_{\infty}\right)
we have

{\operatorname{{\bf area}}}((g_{h})_{t})=-2\pi\chi(\Sigma)\cosh^{2}(t)-e^{-2t}\left\|\Phi_{\Sigma}\right\|^{2}_{2}.

Proof:

If
d{\hat{A}}_{t}
is the area form for
(\hat{g}_{h})_{t}
and
dA_{t}
is the area form for
g_{t}
then by (4) and (5) of Proposition
2.1
we have

dA_{t}=\frac{1}{4}\left|\det\left({\operatorname{Id}}+\hat{B}_{t}\right)\right|d\hat{A}_{t}=\frac{1}{4}\left|\det\left({\operatorname{Id}}+e^{-2t}\hat{B}_{0}\right)\right|e^{2t}d\hat{A}_{0}.

The determinant of
{\operatorname{Id}}+\hat{B}_{t}
is non-negative when the eigenvalues of
\hat{B}_{t}
are
\geq-1
and by Proposition
2.2
this is exactly when
e^{2t}\geq 1+2\left\|\Phi_{\Sigma}\right\|_{\infty}
. Therefore when
t>\frac{1}{2}\log\left(1+2\left\|\Phi_{\Sigma}\right\|_{\infty}\right)
we can drop the absolute value signs and integrate to get

{\operatorname{{\bf area}}}((g_{h})_{t})=\int dA_{t}=\frac{1}{4}\int\det\left({\operatorname{Id}}+e^{-2t}\hat{B}_{0}\right)e^{2t}d\hat{A}_{0}.

By Proposition
2.2
,
\hat{B}
has eigenvalues
1\pm 2\|\phi_{\Sigma}(z)\|
. Thus

\det\left({\operatorname{Id}}+e^{-2t}\hat{B}_{0}\right)=\left(1+e^{-2t}(1+2\left\|\Phi_{\Sigma}\right\|)\right)\left(1+e^{-2t}(1-2\left\|\Phi_{\Sigma}\right\|)\right)=(1+e^{-2t})^{2}-4\left\|\Phi_{\Sigma}\right\|^{2}e^{-4t}.

As
{\operatorname{{\bf area}}}(\hat{g}_{h})=-2\pi\chi(\Sigma)
we have

{\operatorname{{\bf area}}}((g_{h})_{t})=\frac{1}{4}\left(e^{t}+e^{-t})^{2}(-2\pi\chi(\Sigma)\right)-e^{-2t}\left\|\Phi_{\Sigma}\right\|^{2}_{2}=-2\pi\chi(\Sigma)\cosh^{2}(t)-e^{-2t}\left\|\Phi_{\Sigma}\right\|^{2}_{2}.

\Box

For the projective metric
\hat{g}_{\Sigma}
we have the following;

Lemma 3.3

Let
\Sigma
be a projective structure with Thurston parameterization
\left(Y_{\Sigma},\lambda_{\Sigma}\right)
. Then

{\operatorname{{\bf area}}}(\hat{g}_{\Sigma})=-2\pi\chi(\Sigma)+L\left(\lambda_{\Sigma}\right)

and

{\operatorname{{\bf area}}}((g_{\Sigma})_{t})=-2\pi\cosh^{2}(t)\chi(\Sigma)+L\left(\lambda_{\Sigma}\right)\sinh(t)\cosh(t).

Proof:
In the Thurston parameterization of projective structures
\Sigma
is given by a pair
\left(Y_{\Sigma},\lambda_{\Sigma}\right)
(see
[
KT
]
for a complete description of Thurston’s parametrization). We can assume that the support of
\lambda_{\Sigma}
is a single curve
\gamma
with weight
\theta
so that
L(\lambda_{\Sigma})=\theta\ell_{Y_{\Sigma}}(\gamma)
. The Epstein surface at time
t
is the union of a Euclidean cylinder of circumference
\ell_{Y_{\Sigma}}(\gamma)\cosh(t)
and width
\theta\sinh(t)
with the complement a surface of constant negative curvature equal to
\tanh^{2}(t)-1
. The area of the cylinder is
\theta\ell_{Y_{\Sigma}}(\gamma)\sinh(t)\cosh(t)=L(\lambda_{\Sigma})\sinh(t)\cosh(t)
. By the Gauss-Bonnet formula the area of the remainder of the surface is
-2\pi\cosh^{2}(t)\chi(\Sigma)
. This gives the formula for
{\operatorname{{\bf area}}}((g_{\Sigma})_{t})
.

A similar computation gives the
{\operatorname{{\bf area}}}(\hat{g}_{\Sigma})
. In this metric the annulus has circumference
\ell_{Y_{\Sigma}}(\gamma)
and width
\theta
and the complementary surface is hyperbolic. The reasoning of the previous paragraph implies the formula for
{\operatorname{{\bf area}}}(\hat{g}_{\Sigma})
.

Laminations supported on single curve are dense in the space of all measure laminations and the lengths and areas will vary continuously. This gives the general statement.
\Box

4
W
-volume

We first describe
W
-volume for convex cocompact hyperbolic manifolds and then its generalization for projective structures. Let
M={{\mathbb{H}}^{3}}/\Gamma
be a convex co-compact hyperbolic three-manifold with domain of discontinuity
\Omega
, limit set
\Lambda=\widehat{{\mathbb{C}}}-\Omega
and conformal boundary
\partial_{c}M=\Omega/\Gamma
. As
\Gamma
acts by Möbius transformations, we have a natural projective structure
\Sigma
on
\partial_{c}M
.

Let
\tilde{N}
be a closed,
\Gamma
-invariant, convex subset of
{{\mathbb{H}}^{3}}
and assume that
N=\tilde{N}/\Gamma
is compact. This is equivalent to the closure of
\tilde{N}
in
\bar{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
intersects
\widehat{{\mathbb{C}}}
in
\Lambda
. The
W
-volume of
N
is

W(N)=\operatorname{vol}(N)-\frac{1}{2}{\cal B}(N).

In
[
KS
]
, Krasnov and Schlenker define the
W
-volume by subtracting one half the integral of the mean curvature of the boundary. By Proposition
3.1
, when
\partial N
is smooth this is an equivalent definition. If we gave the space of convex submanifolds the Gromov-Hausdorff topology
W
-volume will be continuous and convex submanifolds with smooth boundary will be dense.

The
W
-volume has a number of nice analytic properties.
Let
N_{t}
be the
t
-neighborhood of
N
. Then a simple calculation (see
[
KS
, Lemma 4.2]
) shows that the
W
-volume satisfies the scaling property:

W(N_{t})=W(N)+t\pi|\chi(\partial M)|.

(4.1)

Let
{\cal M}_{C}(\partial_{c}M)
be conformal metrics
\hat{g}
on
\partial_{c}M
such that
there exists a
t
for which the Epstein surface for
e^{2t}\hat{g}
is the metric at infinity for a convex submanifold
N_{t}\subset M
.
Using the scaling formula we can then define
W
-volume as a function on
{\cal M}_{C}(\partial_{c}M)
by setting

W(\hat{g})=W(N_{t})-t\pi|\chi(\partial M)|.

By (
4.1
) the definition of
W(\hat{g})
doesn’t depend on the choice of
t
.

Theorem 4.1
(Krasnov-Schlenker,
[
KS
, Section 7]
)

If
\hat{g}_{M}
is the hyperbolic metric on
\partial_{c}M
and
\hat{g}\in{\cal M}_{C}(\partial_{c}M)
has the same area as
\hat{g}_{M}
then
W(\hat{g}_{M})\geq W(\hat{g})
.

4.1
W
-volume for projective structures

We now define a relative version of
W
-volume and show that it naturally extends to a pair of conformal metrics on a projective structure.

For a convex co-compact hyperbolic 3-manifold
M
with metrics
\hat{g}_{0},\hat{g}_{1}\in{\cal M}_{C}(\partial_{c}M)
we define

W(\hat{g}_{0},\hat{g}_{1})=W(\hat{g}_{1})-W(\hat{g}_{0})

and note that if the
\hat{g}_{i}
are the metrics at infinity for convex submanifolds
N_{i}\subset M
with
N_{0}\subset N_{1}
then

W(\hat{g}_{0},\hat{g}_{1})=\operatorname{vol}(N_{1}\smallsetminus N_{0})-\frac{1}{2}\left({\cal B}(N_{1})-{\cal B}(N_{0})\right).

With this as motivation we now define a relative
W
-volume for pairs of conformal metrics on a projective structure. The construction can be made to work for any projective structure. However, for simplicity we’ll restrict to project structures that are the quotient of domains in
\widehat{{\mathbb{C}}}
.

Here is our setup. Let
\Omega
be a domain in
\widehat{{\mathbb{C}}}
and
\Gamma\subset{\rm PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})
a deck action on
\Omega
such that the projective structure
\Sigma=\Omega/\Gamma
is compact. Let
\Lambda=\widehat{{\mathbb{C}}}\smallsetminus\Omega
. As
\Gamma
is a deck action on
\Omega
it must be a discrete and torsion free subgroup of
{\rm PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})
, so the quotient
M={{\mathbb{H}}^{3}}/\Gamma
is a hyperbolic 3-manifold. If
\tilde{N}\subset{{\mathbb{H}}^{3}}
is a closed
\Gamma
-invariant, convex set whose closure in
\widehat{{\mathbb{C}}}
is
\Lambda
then the quotient
N=\tilde{N}/\Gamma
will be a convex submanifold of
M
. However,
N
will not necessarily be compact and the volume of
N
may be infinite.

Now take nested convex, submanifolds
N_{0}\subset N_{1}
in
M
such that the closure of the universal cover of the
N_{i}
in
\widehat{{\mathbb{C}}}
is
\Lambda
. Then
N_{1}\smallsetminus N_{0}
will have compact closure in
M
. If
\hat{g}_{0}
and
\hat{g}_{1}
are the metrics at infinity for
N_{0}
and
N_{1}
define

W\left(\hat{g}_{0},\hat{g}_{1}\right)=\operatorname{vol}(N_{1}\smallsetminus N_{0})-\frac{1}{2}\left({\cal B}(N_{1})-{\cal B}(N_{0})\right)

as above. Recalling that the metric at infinity for the
t
-neighborhood of
N_{1}
is
e^{2t}\hat{g}_{1}
as for the
W
-volume for convex co-compact hyperbolic 3-manifolds we have the scaling property

W\left(\hat{g}_{0},e^{2t}\hat{g}_{1}\right)=W\left(\hat{g}_{0},\hat{g}_{1}\right)+t\pi|\chi(\Sigma)|.

As before this formula only makes sense initially when
t\geq 0
. For
t<0
we can take it as a definition of
W\left(\hat{g}_{0},e^{2t}\hat{g}_{1}\right)
. The scaling property gives that this is well defined. If the
s
-neighborhood of
N_{0}
is contained in
N_{1}
then the scaling property gives

W\left(e^{2s}\hat{g}_{0},\hat{g}_{1}\right)=W\left(\hat{g}_{0},\hat{g}_{1}\right)-s\pi|\chi(\Sigma)|.

Again we take this as the definition of
W\left(\hat{g}_{0},e^{2s}\hat{g}_{1}\right)
for arbitrary
s
. Finally if
\hat{g}_{0}
and
\hat{g}_{1}
are arbitrary conformal metrics on
\Sigma
that can be rescaled to be the metrics at infinity for convex submanifolds of
M
we can choose the scaling factors
t
and
s
such that
e^{2t}\hat{g}_{1}\geq e^{2s}\hat{g}_{0}
and define

W(\hat{g}_{0},\hat{g}_{1})=W\left(e^{2t}\hat{g}_{0},e^{2s}\hat{g}_{1}\right)-(t-s)\pi|\chi(\Sigma)|.

It then follows that

W(\hat{g}_{0},\hat{g}_{1})=-W(\hat{g}_{1},\hat{g}_{0}).

As in the case of a convex co-compact hyperbolic 3-manifold we let
{\cal M}_{C}(\Sigma)
be the set of conformal metrics
\hat{g}
on
\Sigma
such that there exists a
t
with
e^{2t}\hat{g}
the metric at infinity for some convex
N\subset M
as above. We can define
W\left(\hat{g}_{0},\hat{g}_{1}\right)
for any pair of metric
\hat{g}_{0},\hat{g}_{1}
in
{\cal M}_{C}(\Sigma)
. The relative version of the Theorem
4.1
becomes:

Theorem 4.2

If
\hat{g}_{h}
is the hypebrolic metric for the conformal structure on
\Sigma=\Omega/\Gamma
and
\hat{g}\in{\cal M}_{C}(\Sigma)
has the same area as
\hat{g}_{h}
then

W(\hat{g}_{h},\hat{g})\leq 0.

5
Calculating
W(\hat{g}_{h},\hat{g}_{\Sigma})

The key to our estimate is that for both the hyperbolic metric and the projective metric on
\Sigma
we can explicitly calculate the area of the surface at infinity and the Epstein surface and furthermore we can make this calculation after rescaling.

Lemma 5.1

W(\hat{g}_{h},\hat{g}_{\Sigma})\leq\frac{1}{4}L\left(\lambda_{\Sigma}\right)

Proof:

By Lemma
3.3

{\operatorname{{\bf area}}}\left(\hat{g}_{\Sigma}\right)=2\pi|\chi(\Sigma)|+L(\lambda_{\Sigma}).

Applying the maximality property of Theorem
4.2
we have

W\left(\hat{g}_{h},\frac{2\pi|\chi(\Sigma)|}{2\pi|\chi(\Sigma)|+L(\lambda_{\Sigma})}\hat{g}_{\Sigma}\right)\leq 0.

Combing this with the scaling property we get

W(\hat{g}_{h},\hat{g}_{\Sigma})+\frac{1}{2}\log\left(\frac{2\pi|\chi(\Sigma)|}{2\pi|\chi(\Sigma)|+L(\lambda_{\Sigma})}\right)\pi|\chi(\Sigma)|\leq 0.

As
\log(1+x)\leq x
we have

W(\hat{g}_{h},\hat{g}_{\Sigma})\leq\frac{1}{2}\log\left(1+\frac{L(\lambda_{\Sigma})}{2\pi|\chi(\Sigma)|}\right)\pi|\chi(\Sigma)|\leq\frac{1}{4}L(\lambda_{\Sigma}).

\Box

Next we get a lower bound. The key here is that volumes are non-negative.

Lemma 5.2

W(\hat{g}_{h},\hat{g}_{\Sigma})\geq\frac{e^{-2T}\left\|\Phi_{\Sigma}\right\|_{2}^{2}}{2}-\frac{L\left(\lambda_{\Sigma}\right)\cosh(2T)}{4}

where
e^{2T}=1+2\left\|\Phi_{\Sigma}\right\|_{\infty}
.

Proof:

By Proposition
2.2
the eigenvalues for the shape operator for the Epstein surface of
(\hat{g}_{h})_{T}
are non-negative so
(\hat{g}_{h})_{T}
is the metric at infinity for a convex submanifold
(N_{h})_{T}
. For all
t\geq 0
,
(\hat{g}_{\Sigma})_{t}
is the metric at infinity for a convex submanifold
(N_{\Sigma})_{t}
. Since
\hat{g}_{h}\leq\hat{g}_{\Sigma}
we have
(N_{h})_{T}\subset(N_{\Sigma})_{T}
and therefore

\displaystyle W(\hat{g}_{h},\hat{g}_{\Sigma})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle W\left((\hat{g}_{h})_{T},(\hat{g}_{\Sigma})_{T}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{vol}\left((N_{\Sigma})_{T}\smallsetminus(N_{h})_{T}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\left({\cal B}\left((N_{\Sigma})_{T}\right)-{\cal B}\left((N_{h})_{T}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\left({\cal B}\left((N_{\Sigma})_{T}\right)-{\cal B}\left((N_{h})_{T}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{L\left(\lambda_{\Sigma}\right)e^{2T}}{2}+e^{-2T}\left\|\Phi_{\Sigma}\right\|_{2}^{2}-L(\lambda_{\Sigma})\sinh(T)\cosh(T)\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(e^{-2T}\left\|\Phi_{\Sigma}\right\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{L(\lambda_{\Sigma})}{2}\cosh(2T)\right).

Here we are applying Lemmas
3.2
and
3.3
to calculate
{\cal B}\left((N_{h})_{T}\right)
and
{\cal B}\left((N_{\Sigma})_{T}\right)
.

\Box

We now prove our main result.

Theorem
1.2

Let
\Sigma
be a projective structure that is the quotient of a domain in
\widehat{{\mathbb{C}}}
with Schwarzian parameterization
\left(X_{\Sigma},\Phi_{\Sigma}\right)
and Thurston parameterization
\left(Y_{\Sigma},\lambda_{\Sigma}\right)
. Then

\left\|\Phi_{\Sigma}\right\|_{2}\leq\left(1+\left\|\Phi_{\Sigma}\right\|_{\infty}\right)\sqrt{L\left(\lambda_{\Sigma}\right)}.

Proof:
Combining Lemmas
5.1
and
5.2
we get

\frac{e^{-2T}\left\|\Phi_{\Sigma}\right\|_{2}^{2}}{2}-\frac{L\left(\lambda_{\Sigma}\right)\cosh(2T)}{4}\leq\frac{1}{4}L(\lambda_{\Sigma})

giving

\left\|\Phi_{\Sigma}\right\|_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}L(\lambda_{\Sigma})e^{2T}(1+\cosh(2T))=\frac{1}{4}L(\lambda_{\Sigma})\left(e^{2T}+1\right)^{2}.

Applying
e^{2T}=1+2\left\|\Phi_{\Sigma}\right\|_{\infty}
we obtain

\left\|\Phi_{\Sigma}\right\|_{2}\leq(1+\left\|\Phi_{\Sigma}\right\|_{\infty})\sqrt{L(\lambda_{\Sigma})}.

\Box

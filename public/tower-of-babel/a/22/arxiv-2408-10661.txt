Vanishing viscosity solution to a 2 × 2 2\times 2 system of conservation laws with linear damping
Kayyunnapara Divya JosephDepartment of Mathematics,Indian Institute of Science Education and Research Pune,Pune 411008, India

Abstract
Systems of the first order partial differential equations with singular solutions appear in many multiphysics problems and the weak formulation of the solution involves, in many cases, the product of distributions. In this paper, we study such a system derived from Eulerian droplet model for air particle flow. This is a 2 x 2 non - strictly hyperbolic system of conservation laws with linear damping. We first study a regularized viscous system with variable viscosity term, obtain a weak asymptotic solution with general initial data and also get the solution in Colombeau algebra. We study the vanishing viscosity limit and show that this limit is a distributional solution. Further, we study the large time asymptotic behaviour of the viscous system. This important system is not very well studied due to complexities in the analysis. As far as we know, the only work done on this system is for Riemann type of initial data. The significance of this paper is that we work on the system having general initial data and not just initial data of the Riemann type. Introduction

One of the main features in the study of solutions of the initial value problem for nonlinear first order evolution equations is the finite time break down of classical solution, even if the initial data is smooth. Continuing the solution beyond this time and getting a global in time solution involves in many cases, the product of distributions. The Schwartz impossibility result shows that, there is no algebra with some basic properties, that contain the space of distributions, which allow such products. Since the product of distributions naturally arise in applications in physical problems, there are many attempts to give meaning to the products. For example, one of the unresolved problems is the solution of the continuity equation in

\rho_{t}+\nabla.(u\rho)=0,

which involves the non-conservative product. This problem comes in the large scale structure formation in the universe,

is velocity and

Weak formulation of solutions

2.2
Functions of bounded variation and Volpert product

In order to define the notion of distributional solutions, we should make sense of each term in the equation. The main difficulty is to define the term

In Volpert theory, the product of distributions is defined when they have some regularity properties.
Here, we recall some basic facts in the theory of functions of bounded variation from

Let

||u||_{BV(\Omega,R^{p})}=||u||_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+||Du||.

In this section, we consider the regularized system

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}+\frac{\partial}{\partial x}(\frac{u^{2}}{2})+\alpha u={\epsilon}e^{-\alpha t}\frac{{\partial}^{2}u}{\partial x^{2}},

(3.1)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial v}{\partial t}+\frac{\partial}{\partial x}(uv)={\epsilon}e^{-\alpha t}\frac{{\partial}^{2}v}{\partial x^{2}},

\tau(t)=\frac{1-e^{-\alpha t}}{\alpha}
and setting

, we get as
\epsilon\rightarrow 0

\displaystyle v^{\epsilon}\rightarrow v_{0}(y(x,t))\frac{\partial y(x,t)}{\partial x}

in distribution. The proof of the convergence of

as

is completed.

Coming to

we see that

\displaystyle u^{\epsilon}(x,t)=-2\epsilon e^{-\alpha t}\frac{\frac{\partial\tilde{S}^{\epsilon}}{\partial x}}{\tilde{S}^{\epsilon}}(x,\tau)=e^{-\alpha t}\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{(x-y)}{\tau}{\exp\{-\frac{\theta(x,y,\tau)}{2\epsilon}\}}dy}{\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\exp\{-\frac{\theta(x,y,\tau)}{2\epsilon}\}}dy}.

By a direct application of (

) to this formula for

, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}u^{\epsilon}(x,t)=e^{-\alpha t}\frac{(x-y(x,\tau))}{\tau}

where

is such that
\theta(x,y,\tau)=\min_{y\in R}{\theta(x,y,\tau)}
. Since
(u,v)
is the distributional limit of the weakly asymptotic solution, by definition it is a generalized solution of the inviscid problem (

).
∎

4.2
Volpert product and distributional solution

Suppose the initial data is more regular, then the generalized solution constructed in previous section is a distributional solution.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let

and

•

Proof.

) with the initial condition (

\displaystyle-\alpha u(x,t)\phi(x,t)+\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}u_{0}(x)\phi(x,0)dx=0

This is a standard as demonstrated, by passing to the limit as
\epsilon

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}(u^{\epsilon}(x,t)\phi_{t}(x,t)+\frac{(u^{\epsilon}(x,t)^{2})}{2}\phi_{x}(x,t)

\displaystyle+\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}u_{0}(x)\phi(x,0)dx=0.

and an application of dominated convergence theorem.

The proof that

.
For completeness, we give the details here. As in

, first we show

\mu=V_{t}+\overline{u}V_{x}=0

(4.8)

as a measure. First, we consider

that is a point of approximate continuity of

and compute the first derivatives of

\displaystyle u_{t}

\displaystyle=e^{-\alpha t}[-\alpha\frac{x-y(x,\tau(t))}{\tau(t)}-\frac{x-y(x,\tau(t))}{\tau(t)^{2}}e^{-\alpha t}-e^{-\alpha t}\frac{y_{t}(x,t)}{\tau(t)}]

\displaystyle u_{x}

Using these expressions in the equation for

and writing

\displaystyle u^{\epsilon}_{t}+(\frac{{u^{\epsilon}}^{2}}{2})_{x}+\alpha u

\displaystyle v^{\epsilon}_{t}+(u^{\epsilon}v^{\epsilon})_{x}

\displaystyle=\frac{\epsilon}{2}v^{\epsilon}_{xx},

in
\{(x,t):x\in R^{1},t>0\}

We shall prove the following result.

Theorem 4.3
.

Assume that
u_{0}=(u_{0}^{\epsilon}(x)_{0<\epsilon<1}
,

v_{0}=(\rho_{0}^{\epsilon}(x)_{0<\epsilon<1}
are in

{\mathcal{G}}(R^{1})
,
with the estimates (

) as described before. Let

(u^{\epsilon},v^{\epsilon})
be given by the formula (

)
with

and

To show that

5
Conclusion

The system of equations (
1.1
) is of extreme importance and has a variety of applications including the analysis of the Eulerian droplet model for air particle flow. The solutions of this system do not belong to the classical
L^{\infty}
or the space of functions of bounded variation, but generally are in the space of measures, even if we start with smooth initial data. This is due to concentration of mass which leads to formation of
\delta
waves in the density component. The particular case when
\alpha\neq 0
has important applications, like in the modeling of the large scale structure of the universe and sticky particle dynamics and is well understood. For general
\alpha>0
, the system (
1.1
) is not very well studied due to the complexities in the analysis. As far as we know, the only work done on this system is for Riemann type of initial data. The significance of our work is in the consideration of general initial data and not just initial data of the Riemann type. In this work, the exact solution to the Cauchy problem for a parabolic approximation of (
1.1
) with general initial condition (
1.2
) is studied by adding a viscous term with a small coefficient. Using this, we construct the explicit weak asymptotic solution for (
1.1
) having general initial data and the weak solution in the sense of Colombeau. Later, we derived the vanishing viscosity limit of the solution and showed that the limit satisfies the inviscid system in the sense of distributions. This regularization is shown to be justified, as the characteristic speed
u
satisfies the Lax shock inequality. In this study we also obtained the large time behaviour of solution to the viscous system.

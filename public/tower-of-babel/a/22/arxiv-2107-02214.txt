p p -Adic Scaling Set and Generalized Scaling Set
Debasis Haldar

Abstract
. The main goal of this paper is to develop the MRA theory along with wavelet theory in L 2 ​ ( ℚ p ) L^{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}) . Generalized scaling sets are important in wavelet theory because it determine multiwavelet sets. Although the theory of scaling set and generalized scaling set on ℝ \mathbb{R} and many other local field of positive characteristic are available but not on ℚ p \mathbb{Q}_{p} . This article contains discussion of some necessary conditions of scaling set and characterize generalized scaling set with examples.

2.
Preliminaries

Let us make here a brief review of
p
-adic analysis. We refer
[
20
,
22
,
23
]
for details and proof of the results in this section.
\mathbb{Q}
and
\mathbb{C}
denote the set of rational numbers and the set of complex numbers respectively. For a prime
p
, the
p
-adic norm
|\cdot|_{p}
on
\mathbb{Q}
is defined as follows

|x|_{p}=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }x=0,\\
p^{-\gamma}&\text{ if }x=p^{\gamma}\frac{m}{n}\neq 0,~~p\not|~mn.\end{cases}

This norm is non-Archimedean i.e. satisfies strong triangle inequality
\lvert x+y\rvert_{p}\leq\text{max}\{\lvert x\rvert_{p},\lvert y\rvert_{p}\}
. Here equality holds if and only if
|x|_{p}\neq|y|_{p}
. The field
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
of
p
-adic numbers is defined as the completion of
\mathbb{Q}
with respect to the norm
|\cdot|_{p}
. Every non-zero
p
-adic number has following formal Laurent series expansion in
p
as follows

x=\sum\limits_{j=\gamma}^{\infty}x_{j}p^{j},

where
x_{j}\in\{0,1,\ldots,p-1\}
with
x_{\gamma}\neq 0
and
\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}
. The fractional part
\{x\}_{p}
of
x
is defined by
\sum\limits_{j=\gamma}^{-1}x_{j}p^{j}
. Note that
\{x\}_{p}=0
if and only if
\gamma\geq 0
. Moreover,
\{0\}_{p}:=0
. Define
J_{p,m}:=\{\frac{s_{-m}}{p^{m}}+\ldots+\frac{s_{-1}}{p}:s_{-j}=0,1,\ldots,p-1;~j=1,2,\ldots,m;~s_{-m}\neq 0\}
for
m\in\mathbb{N}
. The set of
p
-adic integers
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and the set of
p
-adic fractional numbers
I_{p}
are given by

\mathbb{Z}_{p}=\{x\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}:\{x\}_{p}=0\}
and
I_{p}=\{x\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}:\{x\}_{p}=x\}
.

We can also write
\mathbb{Z}_{p}=\{x\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}:|x|_{p}\leq 1\}
. The translates of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
by the elements of
I_{p}
are mutually disjoint and their union equals
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. Therefore
I_{p}
can be considered as a set of translations for
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. Note that although
I_{p}
is a discrete subset of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
but does not form a group. Moreover, any choice of representatives in
\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is discrete but not a group as there is only trivial discrete subgroup of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. It is become the biggest constrain for development of
p
-adic MRA theory as well as wavelet theory compaired to
\mathbb{R}
, Vilenkin group, local field of positive characteristic etc. The additive character
\chi_{p}
on the field
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is defined by

\chi_{p}(x)=e^{2\pi i\{x\}_{p}},~~~x\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}.

A ball centered at
c\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}
with radius
p^{\gamma}
, where
\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}
, denoted as
B_{\gamma}(c)
and defined by

B_{\gamma}(c)=\{x\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}:\lvert x-c\rvert_{p}\leq p^{\gamma}\}.

The ball
B_{\gamma}(c)
is compact, open and every point of the ball is also a center of the ball. Further any two balls are either disjoint or one is contained in the other. The field
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is locally compact, totally disconnected and has no isolated points. There exists Haar measure
dx
on
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
which is positive and invariant under translations, i.e.,
d(x+a)=dx
, for all
a\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. It is normalized by the equality
\int\limits_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}dx=1
. Moreover,

d(ax)=|a|_{p}~dx,~~~a\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}\setminus\{0\}.

The Hilbert space of all complex-valued functions on
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, square integrable with respect to the measure
dx
, is denoted by
L^{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
. The inner product in this space is given by

\langle f,g\rangle=\int\limits_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}f(x)\overline{g(x)}dx,
where
f,g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}).

A complex valued function defined on
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is said to be locally constant if for any
x\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}
there exists an
l(x)\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
f(x+y)=f(x),~y\in B_{l(x)}(0)
. The linear space of locally-constant and compactly supported functions (also called the test functions) is denoted by
\mathcal{D}
. The set of all complex valued functions defined on
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
which are locally constant,
p^{M}
-periodic and supported on
B_{N}(0)
is denoted by
\mathcal{D}_{N}^{M}
. Note that
f\in\mathcal{D}_{N}^{M}
is equivalent to say that
f
is
p^{M}
-periodic and Fourier transform of
f
is supported on
B_{M}(0)
.
The Fourier transform of
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
is defined as

\displaystyle\hat{f}(\xi):=\mathcal{F}[f](\xi)=\int\limits_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\chi_{p}(\xi x)f(x)~dx

where
\xi\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. This Fourier transform can be extended to
L^{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
and we have the Parseval theorem
[
22
]
as follows

\langle f,g\rangle=\langle\hat{f},\hat{g}\rangle,\hskip 28.45274pt\forall~f,g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}).

The inverse Fourier transform of
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
is defined as

\check{f}(\xi):=\int\limits_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\chi_{p}(-\xi x)f(x)~dx.

It is easy to see that
\hat{\check{f}}=f
. If
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
and
a,b\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}
with
a\neq 0
, then Fourier transform of dilate along with translate of
f
is

\mathcal{F}[f(a\cdot+b)](\xi)=|a|^{-1}_{p}\chi_{p}\bigg(-\frac{b}{a}\xi\bigg)\mathcal{F}[f]\bigg(\frac{\xi}{a}\bigg).

For example, fourier tansform of the characteristic function of the set of
p
-adic integers is itself, i.e.,
\hat{\mathds{1}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}=\mathds{1}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
and moreover,
\mathds{1}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
is 1-periodic.

Lemma 2.1
.

If
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
, then
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}|f(p^{-n}x)|=0
for
x\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}
a.e.

Proof.

Let
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
. By applying monotone convergence theorem we have,

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}|f(p^{-n}x)|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}|f(p^{-n}x)|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}p^{-n}\int\limits_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}|f(x)|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p-1}\|f\|^{2}<\infty

It follows that,
\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}|f(p^{-n}x)|^{2}dx<\infty
for
x\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}
a.e.

Thus,
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}|f(p^{-n}x)|=0
for
x\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}
a.e.
∎

3.
Scaling Set

Shelkovich and Skopina
[
21
]
gave following real analogous definition of
p
-adic multiresolution analysis (MRA in sequel).

Definition 3.1
.

(Multiresolution Analysis)
A collection of closed subspaces
V_{j}\subset L^{2}({\mathbb{Q}}_{p})
, where
j\in\mathbb{Z}
, is called a multiresolution analysis if following conditions hold

(i)

V_{j}\subset V_{j+1}
for all
j\in\mathbb{Z}
,

(ii)

\overline{\bigcup\limits_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}V_{j}}=L^{2}({\mathbb{Q}}_{p})
,

(iii)

\bigcap\limits_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}V_{j}=\{0\}
,

(iv)

f(\cdot)\in V_{j}\iff f(p^{-1}\cdot)\in V_{j+1}
for all
j\in\mathbb{Z}
,

(v)

there exists
\phi\in V_{0}
such that the system
\{\phi(\cdot-a):a\in I_{p}\}
is an orthonormal basis for
V_{0}
.

This
\phi
is called a scaling function of the given MRA. We will also say that
\phi
generates the MRA. Axioms
(iv)
and
(v)
of Definition
3.1
immediately refer that
\{p^{\frac{j}{2}}\phi(p^{-j}\cdot-a):a\in I_{p}\}
forms an orthonormal basis for
V_{j}
. As
\phi\in V_{0}\subset V_{1}
and
\{p^{\frac{1}{2}}\phi(p^{-1}\cdot-a):a\in I_{p}\}
is an orthonormal basis for
V_{1}
,
\phi
can be written as

(1)

\phi(x)=\sum\limits_{a\in I_{p}}\alpha_{a}\phi(p^{-1}x-a),\hskip 14.22636pt\text{where }\alpha_{a}=p\int\limits_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\phi(x)\overline{\phi(p^{-1}x-a)}dx.

In order to construct an MRA, we start with a function
\phi
whose
I_{p}
-translates be an orthonormal system and set

(2)

V_{j}=\overline{span}\{\phi(p^{-j}\cdot-a):a\in I_{p}\},~~~~j\in\mathbb{Z}.

Now axioms
(iv)
and
(v)
clearly hold here. In order to hold
(i)
, we also need
\phi
such that
\phi(\cdot-b)\in V_{1},~~\forall~b\in I_{p}
, i.e.,

(3)

\phi(\cdot-b)=\sum_{a\in I_{p}}\alpha_{a,b}\phi(p^{-1}\cdot-a),~~~~~~~~\forall~b\in I_{p}.

But, in general,
\frac{b}{p}+a\notin I_{p}
for
a,b\in I_{p}
, therefore
\phi(\cdot-b)\notin V_{1},~~\forall~b\in I_{p}
. Nevertheless, there are
\phi
’s which satisfy equation (
3
) and we will discuss here.

Definition 3.2
.

(Scaling Set)
A measurable set
S\subset\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is said to be a scaling set if
\check{\mathds{1}}_{S}
is a scaling function of an MRA in
L^{2}({\mathbb{Q}}_{p})
.

Example 3.3
.

\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is a scaling set.

Lemma 3.4
.

Measure of a scaling set is 1.

Proof.

Let
S\subset\mathbb{Q}_{p}
be a scaling set. As dilation is an isomorphism,

\{p^{\frac{j}{2}}\check{\mathds{1}}_{S}(p^{-j}\cdot-a):a\in I_{p}\}
is an orthonormal basis for the space
V_{j}
. Now

1=\langle p^{\frac{j}{2}}\check{\mathds{1}}_{S}(p^{-j}\cdot),p^{\frac{j}{2}}\check{\mathds{1}}_{S}(p^{-j}\cdot)\rangle=p^{-j}\langle\mathds{1}_{p^{-j}S},\mathds{1}_{p^{-j}S}\rangle=p^{-j}\mu(p^{-j}S).

Therefore
\mu(p^{-j}S)=p^{j}
.
∎

Proposition 3.5
.

For a scaling set
S
,
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\mathds{1}_{p^{n}S}(\xi)=0
for
\xi\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}
a.e.

Proof.

By Plancherel Theorem, we know that,
\mathds{1}_{S}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{Q}}_{p})
. Now by Lemma
2.1
, for
\xi\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}
a.e.

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\mathds{1}_{S}(p^{-n}\xi)=0\Rightarrow\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\mathds{1}_{p^{n}S}(\xi)=0

∎

Haldar and Singh
[
11
]
defined following multiwavelet set analogous to
\mathbb{R}
.

Definition 3.6
.

(Multiwavelet Set)
A measurable set
W\subset\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is said to be a multiwavelet set of order
L
if
W=\bigcup\limits^{L}_{n=1}W_{n}
for measurable sets
W_{n}
;
n=1,\ldots,L
and
\{\psi_{1},\ldots,\psi_{L}\}
is a multiwavelet for
L^{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
where
\hat{\psi}_{n}=\mathds{1}_{W_{n}}
.

When
L=1
,
W
is simply said to be a wavelet set.

Lemma 3.7
.

For a MRA, scaling set and wavelet set are disjoint a.e.

Proof.

Let
W
and
S
be wavelet set and scaling set. As
\check{\mathds{1}}_{W}\perp V_{0}

0=\langle\check{\mathds{1}}_{W},\check{\mathds{1}}_{S}(\cdot-a)\rangle=\int\limits_{S\cap W}\chi_{p}(a\xi)d\xi

If
a=0
, then
\mu(S\cap W)=0
.
∎

Note that instead of wavelet set, if it is a multiwavelet set then

\mu((\bigcup\limits_{n=1}^{L}W_{n})\cap S)=0
i.e.
\mu(\bigcup\limits_{n=1}^{L}(W_{n}\cap S))=0.

Definition 3.8
.

(Refinable Function)
A function
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
is said to be a refinable function if it satisfy following refinement equation

f(x)=\sum\limits_{a\in I_{p}}r_{a}f\left(\frac{x}{p}-a\right),\hskip 28.45274ptr_{a}\in\mathbb{C}.

Note that every scaling function is refinable function but converse may not be true.

Lemma 3.9
.

If
S\subset\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is a scaling set, then
S\subset p^{-1}S
.

Proof.

Let
\phi
be the scaling function corresponding to
S
. We know scaling function satisfy following refinement equation

\phi(x)=\sum\limits_{a\in I_{p}}\alpha_{a}\phi(p^{-1}x-a),\hskip 28.45274pt\text{where }\alpha_{a}\in\mathbb{C}.

Taking Fourier transform in both side, we have

(4)

\hat{\phi}(\xi)=m_{0}(p\xi)\hat{\phi}(p\xi),\text{ where }m_{0}(\xi):=\sum\limits_{a\in I_{p}}\frac{\alpha_{a}}{p}\chi_{p}{(a\xi)}

\Rightarrow\mathds{1}_{S}(\xi)=\mathds{1}_{p^{-1}S}(\xi)m_{0}(p\xi)

This completes the proof. ∎

m_{0}
is said to be scaling filter. It is to be noted that
m_{0}
is trigonometric function satisfying
m_{0}(0)=1
whenever
\hat{\phi}(0)\neq 0.

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
S
be a scaling set and
m_{0}
be corresponding filter defined by equation (
4
). Then

m_{0}(p\xi)=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }\xi\in S\\
0&\text{ if }\xi\in p^{-1}S\setminus S.\end{cases}

Proof.

As
S
is the scaling set,

\mathds{1}_{S}(\xi)=\mathds{1}_{p^{-1}S}(\xi)m_{0}(p\xi).

If
\xi\in S
then, by Lemma
3.9
,
m_{0}(p\xi)=1
.

If
\xi\notin S
but
\xi\in p^{-1}S
then
m_{0}(p\xi)=0
.
∎

many integers are in

.

]

Theorem 3.13
.

Let
S
be a scaling set corresponding to scaling function
\phi\in\mathfrak{D}_{N}^{M}
where
M,N\geq 0
. If there are maximum
p^{N}
many integers in
p^{M}S\cap[0,p^{M+N}-1]
, then,
\forall~b\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}

\sum_{a\in I_{p}}\alpha_{a,b}\chi_{p}((a-b)\xi)=1,\hskip 14.22636pt\forall~\xi\in S.

As there are atmost

\phi(x-b)

Taking Fourier transform in both side, we have

(5)

\chi_{p}(b\xi)\hat{\phi}(\xi)=\sum_{a\in I_{p}}\alpha_{a,b}\chi_{p}(a\xi)\hat{\phi}(\xi)

When

, equation (

Theorem 3.14
.

Let
S\subset\mathbb{Q}_{p}
be a set such that
\check{\mathds{1}}_{S}\in\mathfrak{D}_{N}^{M}
, where
M,N\geq 0
. If
S
be a scaling set then
p^{N}
many integers are in
p^{M}S\cap[0,p^{M+N}-1]
.

Proof.

As
S
is a scaling set,
\{\check{\mathds{1}}_{S}(\cdot-a):a\in I_{p}\}
is a orthonormal set. Now using orthonormality and Parseval theorem

\displaystyle\delta_{a,0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\check{\mathds{1}}_{S}(\cdot-a),\check{\mathds{1}}_{S}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\mathds{1}_{S}\chi_{p}(a\cdot),\mathds{1}_{S}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{B_{M}(0)}\mathds{1}_{S}(\xi)\chi_{p}(a\xi)d\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{p^{M+N}-1}\int\limits_{B_{-N}\left(\frac{l}{p^{M}}\right)}\mathds{1}_{S}(\xi)\chi_{p}(a\xi)d\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{p^{M+N}-1}\mathds{1}_{S}\left(\frac{l}{p^{M}}\right)\chi_{p}\left(\frac{al}{p^{M}}\right)\int\limits_{B_{-N}\left(0\right)}\chi_{p}(a\xi)d\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p^{M}}\mathds{1}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(ap^{N})\sum_{l=0}^{p^{M+N}-1}\mathds{1}_{S}\left(\frac{l}{p^{M}}\right)\chi_{p}\left(\frac{al}{p^{M}}\right)

Now
a=0
give
\sum\limits_{l=0}^{p^{M+N}-1}\mathds{1}_{S}\left(\frac{l}{p^{M}}\right)=p^{N}
. This completes the proof.
∎

4.
Generalized Scaling Set

In this section our intension is to discuss generalized scaling set and give its characterization.

Definition 4.1
.

For a fixed
L\in\mathbb{N}
, a set
G\subset\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is said to be a generalized scaling set of order
L
if
\mu(G)=\frac{L}{p-1}
and
p^{-1}G\setminus G
is a multiwavelet set of order
L
.

Example 4.2
.

\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is a generalized scaling set of order
p-1
corresponding to Kozyrev’s multiwavelet
[
19
]
.

Example 4.3
.

p^{-m+1}\mathbb{Z}_{p}
be a generalized scaling set of order
(p-1)p^{m-1}
for
m\in\mathbb{N}
correspond to the multiwavelet given by Khrennikov and Shelkovich
[
16
]
.

In the following lemma we give a necessary and sufficient condition for a measurable set to be a generalized scaling set. One can observe similar result for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
in
[
8
]
.

Lemma 4.4
.

A measurable set
G\subset\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is a generalized scaling set of order
L
if and only if
G=\bigcup\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}p^{j}W
a.e. for some multiwavelet set
W
of order
L
.

Proof.

For some multiwavelet set
W
of order
L
, let
G=\bigcup\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}p^{j}W
.

Clearly,
p^{-1}G\setminus G=W
. From
[
11
]
, we know that
\mu(W)=L
. Now

\mu(p^{-1}G\setminus G)=\mu(W)\Rightarrow\mu(G)=\frac{L}{p-1}.

So
G
is a generalized scaling set of order
L
.

Conversely, suppose
G
is a generalized scaling set of order
L
. Then
W:=p^{-1}G\setminus G
is a multiwavelet set of order
L
.

As
\mu(W)=L
,
G\subset p^{-1}G
. Clearly,
\bigcup\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}p^{j}W\subset G
. Since both have same measure,
G=\bigcup\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}p^{j}W
a.e.

∎

Example 4.5
.

Consider
G=\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and
W=\bigcup\limits_{n=1}^{p-1}(-\frac{n}{p}+\mathbb{Z}_{p})
.

\bigcup\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}p^{j}W=pW\bigcup\bigcup\limits_{j=2}^{\infty}p^{j}W=\bigcup\limits_{n=1}^{p-1}(-n+p\mathbb{Z}_{p})\bigcup\bigcup\limits_{j=2}^{\infty}p^{j}W.

Here
0\notin pW
and
\bigcup\limits_{j=2}^{\infty}p^{j}W\subset\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. Therefore,
G=\bigcup\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}p^{j}W
a.e.

Example 4.6
.

Consider
G=p^{-m+1}\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and
W=\bigcup\limits_{s\in J_{p,m}}(-s+\mathbb{Z}_{p})
.

\bigcup\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}p^{j}W=\bigcup\limits_{s\in J_{p,m}}(-sp+p\mathbb{Z}_{p})\bigcup\bigcup\limits_{j=2}^{\infty}p^{j}W.

Here also
0\notin pW
and
\bigcup\limits_{j=2}^{\infty}p^{j}W\subset\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. Therefore,
G=\bigcup\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}p^{j}W
a.e.

Now we will give a necessary condition for a generalized scaling set.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
G\subset\mathbb{Q}_{p}
be a generalized scaling set of order
L
. Then

(i)

\mu(G)=\frac{L}{p-1}
.

(ii)

G\subset p^{-1}G
.

(iii)

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathds{1}_{G}(p^{n}\xi)=1
for
\xi\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}
a.e.

Proof.

Let
G
is a generalized scaling set of order
L
.

Then
(i)
is guaranteed by Definition
4.1
.

(ii)
easily follows from Lemma
4.4
.

Also from the last lemma we know that
G=\bigcup\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}p^{j}W
for some multiwavelet set
W
of order
L
. Furthermore, we know that,
\mu(p^{j}W\cap p^{l}W)=p^{-j}\delta_{j,l}L
. Therefore

\mathds{1}_{G}(p^{n}\xi)=\mathds{1}_{\bigcup\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}p^{j}W}(p^{n}\xi)=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}\mathds{1}_{p^{j}W}(p^{n}\xi)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\mathds{1}_{W}(p^{n-j}\xi).

As,
\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathds{1}_{W}(p^{j}\xi)=1
for
\xi
a.e., we get condition
(iii)
.
∎

1.
Introduction

Throughout this paper,
\mathscr{H}
is a real Hilbert space with inner product
\langle.,.\rangle
and associated norm
\left\|.\right\|,

Q
a nonempty
closed and convex subset of
\mathscr{H},

T:Q\rightarrow Q
is a nonexpansive
mapping (i.e.,
\left\|Tx-Ty\right\|\leq\left\|x-y\right\|

for all
x,y\in Q
) such that
C:=F_{ix}(T)=\{x\in Q:Tx=x\}
is nonempty,

f:Q\rightarrow\mathscr{H}
is a Lipschitzian mapping with coefficient

\alpha\geq 0
(i.e.,
\left\|f(x)-f(y)\right\|\leq\alpha\left\|x-y\right\|

for all
x,y\in Q
), and
F:Q\rightarrow\mathscr{H}
is a Lipschitizian mapping with
coefficient
\kappa>0.
We assume moreover that
F
is strongly monotone with
coefficient
\eta>0,
which means that

\langle F(x)-F(y),x-y\rangle\geq\eta\left\|x-y\right\|^{2}\text{ for
all }x,y\in Q.

We assume also that
\alpha<\eta.
this assumption ensures that the
operator
g:=F-f
is strongly monotone with coefficient
\eta-\alpha
; which implies that
the variational inequality problem

\text{Find }q\in C\text{ such that }\langle F(q)-f(q),x-q\rangle\geq 0\text{
for all }x\in C,

(VIP)

has a unique solution that we denote by
q^{\ast}.

In the present work, we are concerned with the construction of a general
iterative algorithm that generates sequences converging strongly to
q^{\ast}.

Let us first recall some previous results related to this subject. In the
particular case
Q=\mathscr{H},

f\equiv u
a constant, and
F=I
the
identity mapping from
\mathscr{H}
into itself, Halpern
[
6
]
introduced the iterative process

\left\{\begin{array}[l]{l}x_{0}\in\mathscr{H}\\
x_{n+1}=\alpha_{n}u+(1-\alpha_{n})Tx_{n},\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

with
\{\alpha_{n}\}\in[0,1].
He established that if
\alpha_{n}=\frac{1}{n^{\theta}}
with
\theta\in]0,1[
then the
generated sequence
\{x_{n}\}
converges strongly to
q^{\ast}
which is in
this case equal to
P_{C}(u)
where
P_{C}:\mathscr{H}\rightarrow C
is the
metric projection from
\mathscr{H}
onto the closed and convex subset

C=F_{ix}(T).
He also proved that the conditions

(C1)

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\alpha_{n}=0,

(C2)

\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\alpha_{n}=+\infty,

are necessary for the strong convergence of the algorithm (
1.1
).
In 1977, Lions
[
7
]
extended the result of Halpern. In fact, he proved
the strong convergence of sequences
\{x_{n}\}
generated by process
(
1.1
) to
q^{\ast}
provided the sequence
\{\alpha_{n}\}
satisfies
the necessary conditions (C1)-(C2) and the supplementary condition

(C3)

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\alpha_{n+1}-\alpha_{n}}{\alpha_{n}^{2}}=0.

In 2000, Moudafi
[
10
]
considered the case when
Q=\mathscr{H},

f:\mathscr{H}\rightarrow\mathscr{H}
is a contraction with coefficient

\alpha\in[0,1[,

F=I
the identity mapping from
\mathscr{H}
into
itself. He introduced the algorithm

\left\{\begin{array}[l]{l}x_{0}\in\mathscr{H}\\
x_{n+1}=\alpha_{n}f(x_{n})+(1-\alpha_{n})Tx_{n},\end{array}\right.

(1.2)

where
\{\alpha_{n}\}\in]0,1].
He established, under the conditions (C1),
(C2) and

(C4)

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\alpha_{n+1}-\alpha_{n}}{\alpha_{n+1}\alpha_{n}}=0,

the strong convergence of any sequence generated by this algorithm to
q^{\ast}
which in this case is equal to the unique fixed point of the contraction
mapping
P_{C}\circ f.

In 2004, Xu
[
18
]
improved Moudafi result; in fact, he followed a new
approach to prove the strong convergence property of the algorithm (
1.2
)
provided the sequence
\{\alpha_{n}\}
satisfies the conditions (C1), (C2) and

(C5)

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\alpha_{n+1}-\alpha_{n}}{\alpha_{n}}=0
or
\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\left|\alpha_{n+1}-\alpha_{n}\right|<+\infty.

Xu
[
17
]
has also considered the case when
Q=\mathscr{H},

f=u
a
constant and
F=A
a
\eta-
strongly positive, self adjoint, and bounded linear
operator from
\mathscr{H}
to
\mathscr{H}.
He established the strong convergence
of the algorithm

\left\{\begin{array}[l]{l}x_{0}\in\mathscr{H}\\
x_{n+1}=\alpha_{n}u+(I-\alpha_{n}A)Tx_{n}\end{array}\right.

to the unique solution
q^{\ast}
of (VIP) provided the real sequence

\{\alpha_{n}\}
satisfies the conditions (C1), (C2) and (C5). Let us notice
here that, in this case,
q^{\ast}
is the unique minimizer of the strongly
quadratic convex function
J(x)=\frac{1}{2}\langle Ax,x\rangle-\langle u,x\rangle

over the closed and convex subset
C=F_{ix}(T).

Later in 2006, Mariano and Xu
[
8
]
established that the previous strong
convergence result remains true in the more general case when
f:\mathscr{H}\rightarrow\mathscr{H}
is a Lipschitzian mapping with constant
\alpha

strictly less than
\eta
.

On the other hand, Yamada
[
19
]
studied the particular case when

Q=\mathscr{H},

f\equiv 0.
He proved that if the sequence
\{\alpha_{n}\}

satisfies the conditions (C1), (C2) and (C3) then for every starting point

x_{0}\in\mathscr{H}
the sequence
\{x_{n}\}
generated by the iterative
process

x_{n+1}=(I-\alpha_{n}F)Tx_{n}

converges strongly to
q^{\ast}.

In 2010, Tiang
[
15
]
, by combining the iterative method of Yamada and
the method of Mariano and Xu, had introduced the general algorithm

\left\{\begin{array}[l]{l}x_{0}\in\mathscr{H}\\
x_{n+1}=\alpha_{n}f(x_{n})+(I-\alpha_{n}F)Tx_{n}.\end{array}\right.

He established the strong convergence of this algorithm to
q^{\ast}
provided
the real sequence
\{\alpha_{n}\}
satisfies the conditions (C1), (C2)
and (C5).

Later, in 2011, Ceng, Ansari and Yao
[
5
]
extended Tiang’s result to the case
where
Q
is not necessary equal to the whole space
\mathscr{H}.
Precisely,
they proved that if the sequence
\{\alpha_{n}\}
satisfies the conditions
(C1), (C2) and (C5), then for any starting point
x_{0}
in
Q
the sequence

\{x_{n}\}
defined by the scheme

x_{n+1}=P_{Q}\left(\alpha_{n}f(x_{n})+(I-\alpha_{n}F)Tx_{n}\right)

converges strongly to
q^{\ast}.

In this paper, inspired by the previous works and the papers

[
20
]
and
[
3
]
, we introduce the following hybrid and perturbed
algorithm:

\left\{\begin{array}[l]{l}x_{0}\in Q\\
x_{n+1}=\beta_{n}x_{n}+(1-\beta_{n})P_{Q}\left(\alpha_{n}f(x_{n})+(I-\alpha_{n}F)Tx_{n}+e_{n}\right),\end{array}\right.

(HPA)

where
\{\alpha_{n}\}
and
\{\beta_{n}\}
are two real sequences in
[0,1]

and
\{e_{n}\}
is a sequence in
\mathscr{H}
representing the perturbation
term. Roughly speaking, we will prove that any
sequence
\{x_{n}\}
generated by the algorithm (HPA) converges strongly to

q^{\ast}
provided that the sequence
\{\alpha_{n}\}

satisfies only the necessary conditions (C1) and (C2), the sequence

\{\beta_{n}\}
is not too close to
0
or
1,
and the perturbation term

\{e_{n}\}
is relatively small with respect to
\{\alpha_{n}\}
.

The paper is organized as follows. In the next section, we recall some preliminary lemmas that will be used frequently in the proof of the results of the paper. The section 3 is devoted to the study of the convergence of an implicit version of the algorithm (HPA). The strong convergence of the iterative algorithm (HPA) will be investigated in Section4. The Last section will be devoted to the study of the limit case where the strong monotonicity coefficient of
F
is equal to the Lipschitzian coefficient of
f
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall some classical results that will be useful in the
proof of the main theorems of the paper.

The first result is a simple but powerful lemma proved by Xu in
[
16
]
.
This lemma is a generalization of a result due to Bertsekas (see
[
2
, Lemma 1.5.1]
).

Lemma 2.1
.

let
\{a_{n}\}
be a sequence of nonnegative real numbers such
that:

a_{n+1}\leq(1-\gamma_{n})a_{n}+\gamma_{n}r_{n}+\delta_{n},\text{ }n\geq 0,

where
\{\gamma_{n}\}\in[0,1]
and
\{r_{n}\}
and
\{\delta_{n}\}
are
two real sequences such that

(1)

\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\gamma_{n}=+\infty;

(2)

\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\left|\delta_{n}\right|<+\infty;

(3)

\lim\sup_{n\rightarrow+\infty}r_{n}\leq 0.

Then the sequence
\{a_{n}\}
converges to
0.

The second result is the following lemma due to Suzuki
[
14
]

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\{z_{n}\}
and
\{w_{n}\}
be two bounded sequence in a
Banach space
E
and let
\{\beta_{n}\}
be a sequence in
[0,1]
with

0<\lim\inf_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\beta_{n}\leq\lim\sup_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\beta_{n}<1.

Suppose that

z_{n+1}=\beta_{n}z_{n}+(1-\beta_{n})w_{n},\text{ }n\geq 0

and

\lim\sup_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\left(\left\|w_{n+1}-w_{n}\right\|-\left\|z_{n+1}-z_{n}\right\|\right)\leq 0.

Then

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\left\|z_{n}-w_{n}\right\|=0.

The last result of this section is a particular case of the well-known
demiclosedness principle (see
[
1
, Corollary 4.18]
).

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\{x_{n}\}
be a sequence in
Q
. If
\{x_{n}\}
converges weakly to some
x

and
\{x_{n}-Tx_{n}\}
converges strongly to
0
, then
x\in F_{ix}(T).

3.
The convergence of an implicit version of the algorithm (HPA)

In this section, we prove the strong convergence of the perturbed and implicit algorithm

x_{t}=P_{Q}(tf(x_{t})+(I-tF)Tx_{t}+e(t))

as
t\rightarrow 0^{+}
to the unique solution
q^{\ast}
of the variational inequality
problem (VIP) provided that the perturbation term
e(t)
is sufficiently small.
More precisely, we will prove the following theorem.

Theorem 3.1
.

let
\delta_{0}^{\ast}:=2\frac{\eta-\alpha}{\kappa^{2}}
and

e:]0,\delta_{0}^{\ast}[\rightarrow\mathscr{H}
such that

\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{\left\|e(t)\right\|}{t}=0.

Then for every
t\in]0,\delta_{0}^{\ast}[
there exists a unique
x_{t}\in Q

such that

x_{t}=P_{Q}(tf(x_{t})+(I-tF)Tx_{t}+e(t)).

Moreover,
x_{t}
converges strongly in
\mathscr{H}
as
t\rightarrow 0^{+}

toward
q^{\ast}
the unique solution of the variational inequality problem (VIP).

The proof relies essentially on the following lemma which will be also used in
the next section devoted to the study of the strong convergence of the
algorithm (HPA).

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\delta_{0}\in]0,\delta_{0}^{\ast}[.
For every
t\in]0,\delta_{0}],
the mapping
S_{t}:Q\rightarrow\mathscr{H}
defined by

S_{t}(x)=tf(x)+(I-tF)Tx

is Lipschitzian with coefficient
1-t\sigma_{0}
where
\sigma_{0}:=\eta-\alpha-\frac{\kappa^{2}\delta_{0}}{2}.

Proof.

Let
t\in]0,\delta_{0}]
and
x,y\in Q.
We have

\displaystyle\left\|(I-tF)Tx-(I-tF)Ty\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|Tx-Ty\right\|^{2}-2t\langle F(Tx)-F(Ty),Tx-Ty\rangle

\displaystyle+t^{2}\left\|F(Tx)-F(Ty)\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-2t\eta+t^{2}\kappa^{2}\right)\left\|Tx-Ty\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-2t(\eta-\frac{t\kappa^{2}}{2})\right)\left\|x-y\right\|^{2}.

Hence by using the elementary inequality

\sqrt{1-x}\leq 1-\frac{x}{2},\text{ for all }x\in[0,1],

we deduce that

\left\|(I-tF)Tx-(I-tF)Ty\right\|\leq\left(1-t(\eta-\frac{t\kappa^{2}}{2})\right)\left\|x-y\right\|.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\left\|S_{t}(x)-S_{t}(y)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq t\left\|f(x)-f(y)\right\|+\left\|(I-tF)Tx-(I-tF)Ty\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left(t\alpha+1-t(\eta-\frac{t\kappa^{2}}{2})\right)\left\|x-y\right\|

\displaystyle=\left(1-t(\eta-\alpha-\frac{t\kappa^{2}}{2})\right)\left\|x-y\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-t(\eta-\alpha-\frac{\kappa^{2}\delta_{0}}{2})\right)\left\|x-y\right\|

\displaystyle=(1-\sigma_{0}t)\left\|x-y\right\|.

This completes the proof.
∎

Now we are in position to prove the main result of this section.

Proof.

Let
\delta_{0}\in]0,\delta_{0}^{\ast}[
be a fixed
real. Let
t\in]0,\delta_{0}].
Since the operator
P_{Q}
is nonexpansive, it
follows from the previous lemma that the two mapping
\varphi_{t}
and

\phi_{t}
defined from
Q
to
Q
by

\displaystyle\varphi_{t}(x)

\displaystyle=P_{Q}\left(S_{t}(x)\right),

\displaystyle\phi_{t}(x)

\displaystyle=P_{Q}\left(S_{t}(x)+e(t)\right),

are contractions with the same coefficient
1-t\sigma_{0}\in[0,1[.

Hence, the classical Banach fixed point theorem ensures the existence of a
unique
x_{t}
and
y_{t}
in
Q
such that
x_{t}=P_{Q}\left(S_{t}(x_{t})\right)
and
y_{t}=P_{Q}\left(S_{t}(y_{t})+e(t)\right).
Using again Lemma
3.2
and the fact that
P_{Q}
is nonexpansive, we
obtain

\left\|x_{t}-y_{t}\right\|\leq(1-t\sigma_{0})\left\|x_{t}-y_{t}\right\|+\left\|e(t)\right\|,

which implies that

\left\|x_{t}-y_{t}\right\|\leq\frac{\left\|e(t)\right\|}{t\sigma_{0}}.

Hence, from the assumption on
e(t),
we get

\left\|x_{t}-y_{t}\right\|\rightarrow 0\text{ as }t\rightarrow 0^{+}.

Therefore, in order to prove that
x_{t}\rightarrow q^{\ast}
as
t\rightarrow 0^{+}
, it suffices to prove that
y_{t}\rightarrow q^{\ast}
as

t\rightarrow 0^{+}.

To do this let us first show that the family
(y_{t})_{0<t\leq\delta_{0}}
is
bounded in
\mathscr{H}.
Pick
q\in F_{ix}(T).
By using the fact that

P_{Q}
is nonexpansive and Lemma
3.2
, we easily deduce that for every

t\in]0,\delta_{0}]
we have

\displaystyle\left\|y_{t}-q\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|y_{t}-P_{Q}(q)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|P_{Q}(S_{t}(y_{t}))-P_{Q}(S_{t}(q))\right\|+\left\|P_{Q}(S_{t}(q))-P_{Q}(q)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|S_{t}(y_{t})-S_{t}(q)\right\|+\left\|S_{t}(q)-q\right\|

\displaystyle\leq(1-t\sigma_{0})\left\|y_{t}-q\right\|+t\left\|f(q)-F(q)\right\|.

Hence,

\sup_{0<t\leq\delta_{0}}\left\|y_{t}-q\right\|\leq\frac{\left\|f(q)-F(q)\right\|}{\sigma_{0}},

which implies that
(y_{t})_{0<t\leq\delta_{0}}
is bounded in
\mathscr{H}
,
and so is
\left(f(y_{t})-F(Ty_{t})\right)_{0<t\leq\delta_{0}}
since the
mapping
f-F\circ T
is Lipschitzian. Therefore, there exists a constant
M>0

such that for every
t\in]0,\delta_{0}]
we have

\displaystyle\left\|y_{t}-Ty_{t}\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|P_{Q}(S(y_{t}))-P_{Q}(Ty_{t})\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|S_{t}(y_{t})-Ty_{t}\right\|

\displaystyle=t\left\|f(y_{t})-F(Ty_{t})\right\|

\displaystyle\leq M~t.

Hence,

y_{t}-Ty_{t}\rightarrow 0\text{ in }\mathscr{H}\text{ as }t\rightarrow 0^{+}.

(3.1)

On the other hand, since
(y_{t})_{0<t\leq\delta_{0}}
is bounded in

\mathscr{H},
there exists a sequence
(t_{n})_{n}\in]0,\delta_{0}]
which
converges to
0
such that the sequence
\{y_{t_{n}}\}
converges weakly in

\mathscr{H}
to some
y
and

\displaystyle\lim\sup_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\langle y_{t}-q^{\ast},f(q^{\ast})-F(q^{\ast})\rangle

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\langle y_{t_{n}}-q^{\ast},f(q^{\ast})-F(q^{\ast})\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle y-q^{\ast},f(q^{\ast})-F(q^{\ast})\rangle.

Let us notice that, thanks to Lemma
2.3
, we deduce from (
3.1
) that

y\in F_{ix}(T);
hence, from the definition of
q^{\ast},
we conclude that

\lim\sup_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\langle y_{t}-q^{\ast},f(q^{\ast})-F(q^{\ast})\rangle\leq 0.

(3.2)

Finally, for every
t\in]0,\delta_{0}],
we have

\displaystyle\left\|y_{t}-q^{\ast}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|P_{Q}(S_{t}(y_{t}))-P_{Q}(q^{\ast})\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|S_{t}(y_{t})-q^{\ast}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|S_{t}(y_{t})-S_{t}(q^{\ast})\right\|^{2}+2\langle S_{t}(y_{t})-S_{t}(q^{\ast}),S_{t}(q^{\ast})-q^{\ast}\rangle+\left\|S_{t}(q^{\ast})-q^{\ast}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(1-t\sigma_{0})^{2}\left\|y_{t}-q^{\ast}\right\|^{2}+2t\langle y_{t}-q^{\ast},f(q^{\ast})-F(q^{\ast})\rangle

\displaystyle+2t^{2}\langle f(y_{t})-F(Ty_{t}),f(q^{\ast})-F(q^{\ast})\rangle-t^{2}\left\|f(q^{\ast})-F(q^{\ast})\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-2\sigma_{0}t\right)\left\|y_{t}-q^{\ast}\right\|^{2}+2t~\langle y_{t}-q^{\ast},f(q^{\ast})-F(q^{\ast})\rangle+Ct^{2},

where
C>0
is a constant independent of
n.
Therefore for every

t\in]0,\delta_{0}]

\left\|y_{t}-q^{\ast}\right\|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{\sigma_{0}}\left(\langle y_{t}-q^{\ast},f(q^{\ast})-F(q^{\ast})\rangle+\frac{C}{2}t\right)

Thus, by using the estimate (
3.2
), we deduce that
y_{t}\rightarrow q^{\ast}
in
\mathscr{H}
as
t\rightarrow 0^{+}.
This completes the proof of
Theorem
3.1
.
∎

4.
The convergence of the algorithm (HPA).

In this section, we study the strong convergence of the averaged and perturbed algorithm (HPA)

x_{n+1}=\beta_{n}x_{n}+(1-\beta_{n})P_{Q}\left(\alpha_{n}f(x_{n})+(I-\alpha_{n}F)Tx_{n}+e_{n}.\right)

(HPA)

Precisely, we will prove the following result.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\{e_{n}\}
be a sequence in
\mathscr{H}
and
\{\alpha_{n}\}\in]0,1]
and
\{\beta_{n}\}\in[0,1]
two real sequences such that:

(i)

\alpha_{n}\rightarrow 0
and
\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\alpha_{n}=+\infty

(ii)

One of the two following two conditions is satisfied:

(h1)

0<\lim\inf_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\beta_{n}\leq\lim\sup_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\beta_{n}<1.

(h2)

\lim\sup_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\beta_{n}<1
, either
\frac{\beta_{n+1}-\beta_{n}}{\alpha_{n}}\rightarrow 0
or
\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\left|\beta_{n+1}-\beta_{n}\right|<\infty
and either
\frac{\alpha_{n+1}-\alpha_{n}}{\alpha_{n}}\rightarrow 1
or
\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\left|\alpha_{n+1}-\alpha_{n}\right|<\infty.

(iii)

\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\left\|e_{n}\right\|<\infty
or

\frac{\left\|e_{n}\right\|}{\alpha_{n}}\rightarrow 0.

Then for every
x_{0}\in Q
, the sequence
\{x_{n}\}
generated
by the algorithm (HPA) converges strongly in
\mathscr{H}
to
q^{\ast}
the
unique solution of the variational inequality problem (VIP).

Proof.

Since
\alpha_{n}\rightarrow 0

as

n\rightarrow\infty
and
we are only interested in the study of the asymptotic behavior of the
sequence
\{x_{n}\}
, we can assume without loss of generality that for all
n\in\mathbb{N},

\alpha_{n}\in]0,\delta_{0}]
where
\delta_{0}\in]0,\delta_{0}^{\ast}[
is a
fixed real. Let
\{y_{n}\}
be the sequence defined as follows

\left\{\begin{array}[l]{l}y_{0}=x_{0}\\
y_{n+1}=\beta_{n}y_{n}+(1-\beta_{n})P_{Q}(\alpha_{n}f(y_{n})+(I-\alpha_{n}F)Ty_{n}),~n\geq 0.\end{array}\right.

Using the fact
P_{Q}
is nonexpansive and Lemma
3.2
, we easily obtain

\displaystyle\left\|y_{n+1}-x_{n+1}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\beta_{n}\left\|y_{n}-x_{n}\right\|+(1-\beta_{n})\left\|P_{Q}(S_{\alpha_{n}}(y_{n}))-P_{Q}(S_{\alpha_{n}}(x_{n})+e_{n})\right\|

\displaystyle\leq[\beta_{n}+(1-\beta_{n})\left(1-\sigma_{0}\alpha_{n}\right)]\left\|y_{n}-x_{n}\right\|+(1-\beta_{n})\left\|e_{n}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq(1-\gamma_{n})\left\|y_{n}-x_{n}\right\|+\left\|e_{n}\right\|,

where
\gamma_{n}=\sigma_{0}(1-\beta_{n})\alpha_{n}.

Since
\lim\sup_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\beta_{n}<1,
there exists
a>0
and
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
a\alpha_{n}\leq\gamma_{n}\leq 1
for all
n\geq n_{0}
. Hence, by applying Lemma
2.1
, we deduce that

y_{n}-x_{n}\rightarrow 0.

(4.1)

Therefore it suffices to prove that the sequence
\{y_{n}\}
converges
strongly to
q^{\ast}
to conclude that
\{x_{n}\}
also converges strongly to

q^{\ast}.

Let us first show that
\{y_{n}\}
is bounded in
\mathscr{H}.
Let
q\in F_{ix}(T).
For every
n\in\mathbb{N},
we have

\displaystyle\left\|y_{n+1}-q\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\beta_{n}\left\|y_{n}-q\right\|+(1-\beta_{n})[\left\|P_{Q}(S_{\alpha_{n}}(y_{n}))-P_{Q}(S_{\alpha_{n}}(q))\right\|

\displaystyle+\left\|P_{Q}(S_{\alpha_{n}}(q))-P_{Q}(q)\right\|]

\displaystyle\leq\beta_{n}\left\|y_{n}-q\right\|+(1-\beta_{n})[\left\|S_{\alpha_{n}}(y_{n})-S_{\alpha_{n}}(q)\right\|+\left\|S_{\alpha_{n}}(q)-q\right\|]

\displaystyle\leq\beta_{n}\left\|y_{n}-q\right\|+(1-\beta_{n})\left[\left(1-\sigma_{0}\alpha_{n}\right)\left\|y_{n}-q\right\|+\alpha_{n}\left\|f(q)-F(q)\right\|\right].

The last inequality immediately implies that the sequence

v_{n}:=\max\{\left\|y_{n}-q\right\|,\frac{\left\|f(q)-F(q)\right\|}{\sigma_{0}}\}

is decreasing. Therefore the sequence
\{y_{n}\}
is bounded
in
\mathscr{H}
and so are the sequences
\{f(y_{n})\}
and
\{F(Ty_{n})\}.

Now we are going to prove that

y_{n}-Ty_{n}\rightarrow 0.

(4.2)

Let us first assume that the condition (h1) is satisfied. For every

n\in\mathbb{N},
set
z_{n}=P_{Q}\left(\alpha_{n}f(y_{n})+(I-\alpha_{n}F)Ty_{n}\right).
We have, the sequences
\{y_{n}\}
and
\{z_{n}\}
are
bounded in
\mathscr{H},

y_{n+1}=\beta_{n}y_{n}+(1-\beta_{n})z_{n}\text{ for every }n,

and

\displaystyle\left\|z_{n+1}-z_{n}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq(\alpha_{n}+\alpha_{n+1})\sup_{m\geq 0}\left\|f(y_{m})-F(Ty_{m})\right\|+\left\|Ty_{n+1}-Ty_{n}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq(\alpha_{n}+\alpha_{n+1})\sup_{m\geq 0}\left\|f(y_{m})-F(Ty_{m})\right\|+\left\|y_{n+1}-y_{n}\right\|

which implies

\lim\sup_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\left\|z_{n+1}-z_{n}\right\|-\left\|y_{n+1}-y_{n}\right\|\leq 0.

Therefore, from Lemma
2.2
, we deduce that

z_{n}-y_{n}\rightarrow 0,

which combined with the fact that

\displaystyle\left\|z_{n}-Ty_{n}\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|P_{Q}\left(\alpha_{n}f(y_{n})+(I-\alpha_{n}F)Ty_{n}\right)-P_{Q}(Ty_{n})\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\alpha_{n}\sup_{m\geq 0}\left\|f(y_{m})-F(Ty_{m})\right\|\rightarrow 0\text{ as }n\rightarrow+\infty,

implies the required result (
4.2
).

Let us now establish (
4.2
) under the assumption (h2). A simple
computation using the fact that the sequence
\{y_{n}\}
and
\{P_{Q}\left(\alpha_{n}f(y_{n})+(I-\alpha_{n}F)Ty_{n}\right)\}
are bounded in

\mathscr{H},
ensures the existence of two real constants
M_{1},M_{2}>0

such that for every
n\in\mathbb{N},

\displaystyle\left\|y_{n+1}-y_{n}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\beta_{n}\left\|y_{n}-y_{n-1}\right\|+(1-\beta_{n})\left\|P_{Q}(S_{\alpha_{n}}(y_{n}))-P_{Q}(S_{\alpha_{n-1}}(y_{n-1}))\right\|+M_{1}\left|\beta_{n}-\beta_{n-1}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\beta_{n}\left\|y_{n}-y_{n-1}\right\|+(1-\beta_{n})\left\|S_{\alpha_{n}}(y_{n})-S_{\alpha_{n-1}}(y_{n-1})\right\|+M_{1}\left|\beta_{n}-\beta_{n-1}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\beta_{n}\left\|y_{n}-y_{n-1}\right\|+(1-\beta_{n})\left\|S_{\alpha_{n}}(y_{n})-S_{\alpha_{n}}(y_{n-1})\right\|+

\displaystyle(1-\beta_{n})\left\|S_{\alpha_{n}}(y_{n-1})-S_{\alpha_{n-1}}(y_{n-1})\right\|+M_{1}\left|\beta_{n}-\beta_{n-1}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\beta_{n}\left\|y_{n}-y_{n-1}\right\|+(1-\beta_{n})(1-\sigma_{0}\alpha_{n})\left\|y_{n}-y_{n-1}\right\|

\displaystyle+M_{1}\left|\beta_{n}-\beta_{n-1}\right|+M_{2}\left|\alpha_{n}-\alpha_{n-1}\right|

\displaystyle=(1-\sigma_{0}(1-\beta_{n})\alpha_{n})\left\|y_{n}-y_{n-1}\right\|+M_{1}\left|\beta_{n}-\beta_{n-1}\right|+M_{2}\left|\alpha_{n}-\alpha_{n-1}\right|.

Hence, by proceeding as in the proof of (
4.1
), we infer that

\left\|y_{n+1}-y_{n}\right\|\rightarrow 0.

(4.3)

On the other hand, for every
n\in\mathbb{N},
we have

\displaystyle\left\|y_{n+1}-Ty_{n}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\beta_{n}\left\|y_{n}-Ty_{n}\right\|+(1-\beta_{n})\left\|P_{Q}(S_{\alpha_{n}}(y_{n}))-P_{Q}(Ty_{n})\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\beta_{n}\left\|y_{n+1}-Ty_{n}\right\|+\beta_{n}\left\|y_{n+1}-y_{n}\right\|+\left\|S_{\alpha_{n}}(y_{n})-Ty_{n}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\beta_{n}\left\|y_{n+1}-Ty_{n}\right\|+\left\|y_{n+1}-y_{n}\right\|+\alpha_{n}\left\|f(y_{n})-F(Ty_{n})\right\|.

Hence, we obtain the inequality

\left\|y_{n+1}-Ty_{n}\right\|\leq\frac{1}{1-\beta_{n}}\left(\left\|y_{n+1}-y_{n}\right\|+\alpha_{n}\sup_{m\geq 0}\left\|f(y_{m})-F(Ty_{m})\right\|\right),

which, combined with (
4.3
) and the fact that
\lim\sup_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\beta_{n}<1
, implies that

\left\|y_{n+1}-Ty_{n}\right\|\rightarrow 0.

(4.4)

The required estimate (
4.2
), follows from (
4.3
) and (
4.4
).

Now we are going to apply the fundamental Lemma
2.1
to conclude that

\{y_{n}\}
converges strongly to
q^{\ast}.
But first let us notice that by
proceeding as in the proof of Theorem
3.1
and by using (
4.2
) and
the fact that
\{y_{n}\}
is bounded, we deduce that

\lim\sup_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\langle y_{n}-q^{\ast},f(q^{\ast})-F(q^{\ast})\rangle\leq 0.

Combined with the estimate (
4.2
), the last inequality yields

\lim\sup_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\langle Ty_{n}-q^{\ast},f(q^{\ast})-F(q^{\ast})\rangle\leq 0.

(4.5)

Finally, for every
n\in\mathbb{N},

\displaystyle\left\|y_{n+1}-q^{\ast}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\beta_{n}\left\|y_{n}-q^{\ast}\right\|^{2}+(1-\beta_{n})\left\|P_{Q}(S_{\alpha_{n}}(y_{n}))-P_{Q}(q^{\ast})\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\beta_{n}\left\|y_{n}-q^{\ast}\right\|^{2}+(1-\beta_{n})\left\|S_{\alpha_{n}}(y_{n}))-q^{\ast}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\beta_{n}\left\|y_{n}-q^{\ast}\right\|^{2}+(1-\beta_{n})[\left\|S_{\alpha_{n}}(y_{n}))-S_{\alpha_{n}}(q^{\ast})\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle+2\langle S_{\alpha_{n}}(y_{n}))-S_{\alpha_{n}}(q^{\ast}),S_{\alpha_{n}}(q^{\ast})-q^{\ast}\rangle+\left\|S_{\alpha_{n}}(q^{\ast})-q^{\ast}\right\|^{2}]

\displaystyle=\beta_{n}\left\|y_{n}-q^{\ast}\right\|^{2}+(1-\beta_{n})[\left\|S_{\alpha_{n}}(y_{n}))-S_{\alpha_{n}}(q^{\ast})\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle+2\langle S_{\alpha_{n}}(y_{n}))-q^{\ast},S_{\alpha_{n}}(q^{\ast})-q^{\ast}\rangle-\left\|S_{\alpha_{n}}(q^{\ast})-q^{\ast}\right\|^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq\beta_{n}\left\|y_{n}-q^{\ast}\right\|^{2}+(1-\beta_{n})[(1-\alpha_{n}\sigma_{0})^{2}\left\|y_{n}-q^{\ast}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle+2\alpha_{n}^{2}\langle f(y_{n})-F(Ty_{n}),f(q^{\ast})-F(q^{\ast})\rangle+2\alpha_{n}\langle Ty_{n}-q^{\ast},f(q^{\ast})-F(q^{\ast})\rangle]

\displaystyle\leq(1-\gamma_{n})\left\|y_{n}-q^{\ast}\right\|^{2}+2\alpha_{n}(1-\beta_{n})\left[\langle Ty_{n}-q^{\ast},f(q^{\ast})-F(q^{\ast})\rangle+C\alpha_{n}\right]

\displaystyle=(1-\gamma_{n})\left\|y_{n}-q^{\ast}\right\|^{2}+\gamma_{n}r_{n}

where
C>0
is a constant independent of
n,

\gamma_{n}=2\sigma_{0}(1-\beta_{n})\alpha_{n}
and

\ r_{n}=\frac{1}{\sigma_{0}}\left(\langle Ty_{n}-q^{\ast},f(q^{\ast})-F(q^{\ast})\rangle+C\alpha_{n}\right).

Using
the estimate (
4.5
), we obtain
\lim\sup r_{n}\leq 0;
hence, by applying
Lemma
2.1
we conclude as previously that the sequence
\{y_{n}\}

converges strongly to
q^{\ast}.
This completes the proof of Theorem

4.1
.
∎

5.
The study of the limit case
\mu=\alpha

Throughout this section, we assume that
\mu=\alpha.
In this limit case, the operator
F-f

is monotone but not necessary strongly monotone; so the uniqueness of the
solution of the variational inequality problem (VIP) is no long assured. Let us
assume that (VIP) has at least one solution. We denote by
S_{VIP}

the set of the solutions of (VIP). The following theorem provides a method to
approximate a particular element of the set
S_{VIP}.

Theorem 5.1
.

Assume that the sequences
\{\alpha_{n}\},~\{\beta_{n}\}
and
\{e_{n}\}

satisfy the same assumptions as in Theorem. Then, for every
\varepsilon>0

and
x_{0}\in Q
, the sequence
\{x_{n}^{\varepsilon}\}
defined by the
recursive formula

x_{n+1}^{\varepsilon}=\beta_{n}x_{n}^{\varepsilon}+(1-\beta_{n})P_{Q}\left(\alpha_{n}f(x_{n}^{\varepsilon})+((1-\alpha_{n}\varepsilon)I-\alpha_{n}F)Tx_{n}^{\varepsilon}+e_{n}\right),~n\geq 0,

converges strongly to
q^{\varepsilon}
the unique solution of the variational
inequality problem

\text{Find }q\in C\text{ such that }\langle F(q)+\varepsilon q-f(q),x-q\rangle\geq 0\text{ for all }x\in C.

(VIP
ε
)

Moreover, the set
S_{VIP}
is closed and convex and
q^{\varepsilon}

converges strongly as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
to the nearest element of

S_{VIP}
to the origin.

Proof.

For every
\varepsilon>0,
the operator
F_{\varepsilon}:=F+\varepsilon I
is

\mu+\varepsilon
strongly monotone and
\kappa+\varepsilon
Lipschitizian.
Since
\mu+\varepsilon>\alpha,
the first part of this theorem follows
immediately from Theorem
4.1
.

Let
\delta_{C}:C\rightarrow\mathscr{H}
be the indicator function associated
to the closed, convex and nonempty subset
C.
We recall that
\delta_{C\text{
}}
is given by

\delta_{C}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[l]{ll}0&,x\in C\\
+\infty&,x\notin C\end{array}\right.

It is well-known that
\delta_{C}
is a proper, lower semi-continuous, and convex function. Hence, its sub-gradient
\partial\delta_{C}
is a maximal and monotone operator with domain equal to
C
. We recall that, for every
x\in C
,

\partial\delta_{C}(x)=\{u\in\mathscr{H}:~\langle u,y-x\rangle\leq 0\}.

It is then easily seen that the set
S_{VIP}
is equal to
A^{-1}(0)
the
set of zeros of the operator
A:=F-f+\delta_{C}.
From
[
13
]
, the operator
A
is maximal and monotone;
therefore,
S_{VIP}
is a closed and convex subset of
\mathscr{H}.
On the
other hand, the unique solution
q^{\varepsilon}
of (VIP
ε
)
satisfies the relation

-\left(F(q^{\varepsilon})+\varepsilon q^{\varepsilon}-f(q^{\varepsilon})\right)\in\delta_{C}(q^{\varepsilon}),

which is equivalent to

0\in q^{\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}A(q^{\varepsilon}),

since
\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\delta_{C}(q^{\varepsilon})=\delta_{C}(q^{\varepsilon}).
Therefore,
q^{\varepsilon}=J_{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}(0),

where for every
\lambda>0
,
J_{\lambda}=(I+\lambda A)^{-1}
is the resolvant
of A (for more details, see the pioneer paper
[
9
]
of Minty). Hence,
from the following lemma due to Bruck
[
4
]
and Morosanu
[
11
]

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
A:D(A)\subset\mathscr{H}\rightarrow 2^{\mathscr{H}}
be a maximal monotone
operator with
A^{-1}(0)\neq\varnothing.
Then for any
u\in\mathscr{H},

(I+tA)^{-1}u\rightarrow P_{A^{-1}(0)}(u)
as
t\rightarrow+\infty.

we deduce that
q^{\varepsilon}
converges
strongly as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
to
P_{A^{-1}(0)}=P_{S_{VIP}}(0)
which
is the element of
S_{VIP}
with minimal norm.
∎

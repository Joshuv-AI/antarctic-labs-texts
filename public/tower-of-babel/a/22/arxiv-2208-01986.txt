Abstract
. For a commutative ring R R with unit 1 ≠ 0 1\neq 0 and a multiplicatively closed subset S S of R R , we introduce a new topology on the S S -prime spectrum Spec S ​ R \mathrm{Spec}_{S}R of R R called the S S -flat topology. Our aims is to give an algebraic descriptions of the topological properties like compactness, irreducibility, connectivity and noetherianess with respect to this new topology . Keywords: S S -prime ideal, S S -Zariski topology, S S -flat topology. MSC(2010): 13A15 ; 13A99; 54A99.

Introduction

In this paper, we focus on only commutative rings with
1\neq 0
. Let
R

S
of

S
a multiplicatively closed subset of

(i)

(ii)

, and

ss^{\prime}\in S
for all
s,s^{\prime}\in S
. Let

be a multiplicatively closed subset of
R
and

be an ideal of
R
. Then the ideal
P
is called an
S
-prime ideal of

if
P\cap S=\emptyset
and there exists
s\in S
and whenever
ab\in P
then
sa\in P
or
sb\in P
. If we take
S\subseteq u(R)
, where
u(R)
denotes the set of units in

, the notions of
S
-prime ideal and prime ideal are the same thing. Here, we denote the sets of all prime and all
S
-prime ideals by
\mathrm{Spec}R
and
\mathrm{Spec}_{S}R
, respectively. In
[
4
]
, the authors construct a topology on
\mathrm{Spec}_{S}R

-Zariski topology, precisely, the collection of closed subsets for this topology are
V_{S}(I)=\{P\in\mathrm{Spec}_{S}R\ /sI\subseteq P\text{ for some }s\in S\}
where

runs trough the set of all ideals of

. Note that, if

, then the

-Zariski and the classical Zariski topologies are equal.

In

, D. E. Dobbs, M. Fontana and I. J. Papick, in
[

, introduce and studied a new topology on the prime spectrum

, which behave completely as a dual of the Zariski topology, and where it is discussed its relation with the inverse-order topology studied by Hochster
[
9
]
. It is called the flat topology. For this topology the collection of subsets
V(I)
where
I
runs through the set of finitely generated ideals of

forms a basis of opens. Many authors have studied the flat topology, and have obtained several results which are useful studying commutative rings, for instance see,
[
5
]
,
[
14
]
,
[
6
]
,
[

]
.

In this paper, following
[
5
]
and
[
14
]
, we construct a new topology on the
S
-prime spectrum
\mathrm{Spec}_{S}R
called the
S
-flat topology. The

S
-prime spectrum
\mathrm{Spec}_{S}R

3.
The
S
-flat topology

Let
R
be a commutative ring. Then there is a unique topology over
\mathrm{Spec}R
such that the collection of subsets
V(f)
with
f\in R
forms a sub-basis for the opens of this topology. It is called the flat topology. Therefore the collection
V(I)
where
I
runs through the set of finitely generated ideals of
R
forms a basis for the flat opens, for more details see
[
5
]
,
[
14
]
. Motivated by this concept, we generalize the flat topology to the
S
-prime spectrum
\mathrm{Spec}_{S}R
of a commutative ring
R
. Let
S
be a multiplicative subset of
R
. Then there is a unique topology over
\mathrm{Spec}_{S}R
such that the collection
V_{S}(f)
with
f\in R
forms a sub-basis for the opens of this topology. It is called the
S
-flat topology. Therefore the collection
V_{S}(I)
where
I
runs through the set of finitely generated ideals of
R
forms a basis for the
S
-flat opens. Note that, if
S\subseteq u(R)
(consist of units of
R
) then
\mathrm{Spec}_{S}R=\mathrm{Spec}
and moreover the
S
-flat topology and the flat topology are the same.

Remark 3.1
.

If
P
is an
S
-prime ideal of
R
then there exists
s_{p}\in S
such that
(P:s_{p})
is a prime ideal, moreover, if
(P:s_{p}^{\prime})
is also a prime ideal form some
s_{p}^{\prime}\in S
, then
(P:s_{p})=(P:s_{p}^{\prime})
. In fact, let
a\in(P:s_{p})
then
s_{p}a\in P\subseteq(P:s_{p}^{\prime})
, so
a\in(P:s_{p}^{\prime}
since
a_{p}\not\in(P:s_{p}^{\prime})
, that is
(P:s_{p})\subseteq(P:s_{p}^{\prime})
. By the same argument, we have
(P:s_{p})=(P:s_{p}^{\prime})
.

Proposition 3.2
.

The map
\mathrm{Spec}_{S}R\to\mathrm{Spec}R
,
P\mapsto(P:s_{p})
is continuous with respect to the
S
-flat topology and flat topology.

Proof.

Let
a\in R
. We have
\{P\in\mathrm{Spec}_{S}R\ /(P:s_{p})\in V(a)\}=V_{S}(a)
. In fact, if
(P:s_{p})\in V(a)
then
s_{p}a\in P
, so
P\in V_{S}(a)
. Conversely, if
P\in V_{S}(a)
then
s^{\prime}a\in P
for some
s^{\prime}\in S
, so
s^{\prime}a\in(P:s_{p})
, hence
a\in(P:s_{p})
. Thus
(P:s_{p})\in V(a)
.
∎

If

4.
Compactness and irreducibility

We start this section with the following useful lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
R
be a commutative ring and
S
be a multiplicatively closed subset of
R
. If
I,J
are ideals of
R
then
\sqrt[S]{\sqrt[S]{I}\sqrt[S]{J}}=\sqrt[S]{IJ}
.

Proof.

Since
IJ\subseteq\sqrt[S]{I}\sqrt[S]{J}
, we get
\sqrt[S]{IJ}\subseteq\sqrt[S]{\sqrt[S]{I}\sqrt[S]{J}}
. Let
x\in\sqrt[S]{I}\sqrt[S]{J}
, then
x=\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{r}a_{k}b_{k}
where
a_{k}\in\sqrt[S]{I}
and
b_{k}\in\sqrt[S]{J}
. For each
k
,
s_{k}a_{k}^{n_{k}}\in I
and
t_{k}b_{k}^{m_{k}}\in J
for some
s_{k},t_{k}\in S
and
n_{k},m_{k}\in\mathbb{N}
. We have
t_{k}s_{k}(a_{k}b_{k})^{n_{k}+m_{k}}\in IJ
, so
a_{k}b_{k}\in\sqrt[S]{IJ}
. It follows that
x\in\sqrt[S]{IJ}
. Consequently,
\sqrt[S]{I}\sqrt[S]{J}\subseteq\sqrt[S]{IJ}
. Thus
\sqrt[S]{\sqrt[S]{I}\sqrt[S]{J}}=\subseteq\sqrt[S]{IJ}
.
∎

.

Let

be an ideal of

for some

. Suppose to the contrary that

\sqrt[S]{J}\not\subseteq\sqrt[S]{I}

Theorem 5.1
.

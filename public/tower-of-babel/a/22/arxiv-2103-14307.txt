On the asymptotic behavior of Sudler products along subsequences
Mario Neumüller

Abstract
Let α ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) \alpha\in(0,1) and irrational. We investigate the asymptotic behaviour of sequences of certain trigonometric products (Sudler products) ( P N ​ ( α ) ) N ∈ ℕ (P_{N}(\alpha))_{N\in{\mathbb{N}}} with P N ​ ( α ) = ∏ r = 1 N | 2 ​ sin ⁡ ( π ​ r ​ α ) | . P_{N}(\alpha)=\prod_{r=1}^{N}|2\sin(\pi r\alpha)|. More precisely, we are interested in the asymptotic behaviour of subsequences of the form ( P q n ​ ( α ) ​ ( α ) ) n ∈ ℕ (P_{q_{n}(\alpha)}(\alpha))_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}} , where q n ​ ( α ) q_{n}(\alpha) is the n n th best approximation denominator of α \alpha . Interesting upper and lower bounds for the growth of these subsequences are given, and convergence results, obtained by Mestel and Verschueren (see [ 24 ] ) and Grepstad and Neumüller (see [ 11 ] ), are generalized to the case of irrationals with bounded continued fraction coefficients.

Introduction

In this article we are going to study the asymptotic behaviour of a certain trigonometric product given by

P_{N}(\alpha):=\prod_{r=1}^{N}|2\sin(\pi r\alpha)|

will eventually become zero for sufficiently large

\alpha

(0,1)

These products are often referred to as Sudler products and have first been studied more than 60 years ago by Erdős and Szekeres
[
7
]
in 1959 and by Sudler
[
23
]
in 1964.
Sudler products appear in a variety of different fields in pure and applied mathematics e.g. partition theory
[
23
,
25
]
, Padé approximation, continued fractions (see
[
18
]
and the references therein), discrepancy and uniform distribution theory
[
2

14
]
and in different topics related to mathematical physics
[
1
,
4
,
5
,
8
,
15
,
17
]
. We refer to
[
24
]
for a very detailed and well structured introduction to Sudler products.

In 2016 Mestel and Verschueren
[
24
]
studied a certain subsequence of
(P_{N}(\alpha))_{N}{\in{\mathbb{N}}}
for the special case of
\alpha
being the golden ratio. The main result of
[

reads as follows.

Theorem A

(
[
24

)
.

Let
\varphi=(\sqrt{5}-1)/2
and
(F_{n})_{n\geq 0}=(0,1,1,2,3,\ldots)
the Fibonacci sequence. Then there exists a constant

such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}P_{F_{n}}(\varphi)=c.

(1.2)

It is well known that the Fibonacci sequence

is the sequence of best approximation denominators of the golden ratio. Looking at Theorem

the rather natural question arises if one can generalize the convergence behaviour described in (

Theorem B

(
[

)
.

Let
\alpha=[0;\overline{a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell}}]
be a purely periodic quadratic irrational
1

1

We write
[0;\overline{a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell}}]
as an abbreviation for the periodic continued fraction expansion
[0;a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell},a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell},\ldots]
.

of period length

.
Then there exist real constants

.

For simplicity Theorem

]
.

The author would like to point out that the machinery behind Theorem

and Aistleitner, Technau and Zafeiropoulos

on the behaviour of

for special choices of quadratic irrationals

In the same paper they could prove that for almost all

after proving that

has unbounded continued fraction coefficients. It was rather surprising when Grepstad, Kaltenböck and Neumüller

could prove that the golden ratio

\liminf_{N\to\infty}P_{N}(\varphi)>0.

For more detailed information on this topic we refer to the survey paper

could fully characterize which quadratic irrationals

Theorem C

(

)
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
and irrational with continued fraction expansion
\alpha=[a;a,a,a,\ldots]

1.

If

then

\liminf_{N\to\infty}P_{N}(\alpha)>0\text{ and }\limsup_{N\to\infty}P_{N}(\alpha)/N<\infty.

\liminf_{N\to\infty}P_{N}(\alpha)=0\text{ and }\limsup_{N\to\infty}P_{N}(\alpha)/N=\infty.

Inequality (

show that the asymptotic behaviour of

is not only depending on the structure of the continued fraction expansion of
\alpha
but also on the actual size of the continued fraction coefficients.

The main goal of this article is to understand the asymptotic behaviour of the sequence
(P_{q_{n}(\alpha)}(\alpha))_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
for a fixed irrational
\alpha
and where
q_{n}(\alpha)
is the
n
th best approximation denominator of
\alpha
. It will turn out that if
\alpha
has bounded continued fraction coefficients the convergence behaviour described in Theorem
B
can be generalized to this case (in some sense). On the other hand if the continued fraction coefficients of
\alpha
are unbounded this will lead to a different behaviour. We will give upper and lower bounds in this case.

Let us continue with some important notations and the main results of this article.

1.1
Notations and formulation of the main results

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
and irrational. We denote by
p_{n}(\alpha)
and

n

approximation numerator and denominator, respectively, of
\alpha

(a_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\in{\mathbb{N}}

\alpha=[0;a_{1},a_{2},\ldots]
for the continued fraction expansion (c.f.e.) of
\alpha

(a_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\in{\mathbb{N}}
are called the continued fraction coefficients (c.f.c.) of
\alpha
.

It will turn out that the first component which determines the asymptotic behaviour of
(P_{q_{n}(\alpha)}(\alpha))_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
are the sequences
(\alpha^{+}_{n}(\alpha))_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
and
(\alpha^{-}_{n}(\alpha))_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
given by

\displaystyle\alpha^{+}_{n}(\alpha):=[a_{n};a_{n+1},a_{n+2},\ldots]\text{ and }\alpha^{-}_{n}(\alpha):=[0;a_{n-1},a_{n-2},\ldots,a_{1}].

(1.4)

The sequences
(\alpha^{+}_{n}(\alpha))_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
and
(\alpha^{-}_{n}(\alpha))_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
describe sequences of c.f.e. which are depending on the c.f.c. of
\alpha
. More precisely, for
\alpha_{n}^{+}(\alpha)
we consider the c.f.e. consisting of the c.f.c.

of
\alpha
with

and
\left\lfloor\alpha_{n}^{+}(\alpha)\right\rfloor=a_{n}
. For
\alpha_{n}^{-}(\alpha)
we consider the c.f.e. consisting of the coefficients
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n-1}
of
\alpha
, but in reverse order.

The second component that influences the asymptotic behavior of the sequence
(P_{q_{n}(\alpha)}(\alpha))_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is of the following form. For
\beta\in{\mathbb{R}}
let

D_{t}(\beta):=\sum_{s=1}^{t}\{\beta s\}-\frac{1}{2}.

(1.5)

This function has been extensively studied by Hardy and Littlewood
[

]
and Ostrowski
[

]
, but also by Sós
[

]
and Brown and Shiue
[

[

]

In order to ease notation in what follows we set

c_{n}(\alpha):=\frac{1}{\alpha_{n}^{+}(\alpha)+\alpha_{n}^{-}(\alpha)}.

(1.6)

Moreover, we will drop the dependence on
\alpha
of the quantities
p_{n}(\alpha)
,
q_{n}(\alpha)
,
\alpha^{+}_{n}(\alpha)
,
\alpha^{-}_{n}(\alpha)
and
c_{n}(\alpha)
if it is clear from context.

The subsequent theorem relates the asymptotic behaviour of
(P_{q_{n}}(\alpha))_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
to the sequences
(\alpha^{+}_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
,
(\alpha^{-}_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
and
(D_{t}(\alpha_{n}^{-}))_{t\in{\mathbb{N}}}
for

where the implied constants

We will see later on that both of the sequences

Corollary 1.3

Let

Corollary 1.5

Let

Another immediate consequence of Corollary

Corollary 1.6

For every irrational
\alpha\in(0,1)
the sequence
(P_{q_{n}}(\alpha))_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}

In the presence of bounded c.f.c. it is even possible to give sufficient conditions for the convergence of a subsequence of
(P_{q_{n}}(\alpha))_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
be irrational with bounded c.f.c.
(a_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
. Let
(n_{i})_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a strictly increasing sequence of positive integers. If additionally both of the limits

\begin{split}&\lim_{i\to\infty}\alpha_{n_{i}}^{+}=\lim_{i\to\infty}[a_{n_{i}};a_{n_{i}+1},a_{n_{i}+2},\ldots];\\
&\lim_{i\to\infty}\alpha_{n_{i}}^{-}=\lim_{i\to\infty}[0;a_{n_{i}-1},a_{n_{i}-2},\ldots,a_{1}]\end{split}

(1.12)

exist then there exists a constant
C>0

\lim_{i\to\infty}P_{q_{n_{i}}}(\alpha)=C.

(1.13)

Observe that the convergence result for quadratic irrationals (Theorem

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\alpha_{n^{(k)}_{i}}^{-}

j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
we have
\lim_{i\to\infty}t_{n_{i}+j}
and
\lim_{i\to\infty}t_{n_{i}-j}
exist. Thus we can apply Theorem
1.7
and it follows that
\lim_{i\to\infty}P_{q_{n_{i}}}(\beta)
exist and is strictly greater zero.

The rest of this article is structured as follows. We give a short summary of the most important results on continued fractions and Ostrowski representations in Section
2
and continue with a short description of the proof strategy for Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.7
in Section
3
. The main part of this article is Section
4
, which contains the essential steps (Lemma
3.1
and Lemma
3.2
) for the proofs of Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.7
. Last but not least in Section
5
we will state the actual proofs of Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.7
. The proofs of corollaries
1.3
and
1.5
are stated in this section as well.

2
Preliminaries

Throughout the article we make use of the following common notations. For some functions
f,g:{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
and
a\in{\mathbb{R}}\cup\{\infty,-\infty\}
we write

1.

f(x)={\mathcal{O}}(g(x))
for
x\to a
if there exists a constant
C
independent of
x
such that if
x
is sufficiently close to
a
then
|f(x)|\leq C|g(x)|
.

2.

f(x)=\Theta(g(x))
for
x\to a
if there exist constants
c,C>0
independent of
x
such that if
x
is sufficiently close to
a
then
c|g(x)|\leq|f(x)|\leq C|g(x)|
.

3.

f(x)={\scriptstyle{\mathcal{O}}}(g(x))
for
x\to a
if
\lim_{x\to a}|f(x)/g(x)|=0
.

Further we state a helpful Lemma which will be used at several points later on.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
24
, Lemma 4.3]
)
.

For
n\geq 2
and real numbers
a_{t}
,
t=1,2,\ldots,n
, satisfying
|a_{t}|<1
and
A:=\sum_{t=1}^{n}|a_{t}|<1
, we have

1-A<\prod_{t=1}^{n}(1+a_{t})<\frac{1}{1-A}.

Let us continue by summarizing some basic knowledge and notations concerning the theory of continued fractions.

2.1
Continued fractions

The following notations and statements will be useful throughout the rest of this paper. In general, for any irrational, real
\alpha\in(0,1)
whose continued fraction expansion is given by

\alpha=[0;a_{1},a_{2},\ldots],

we denote its
n
th convergent by
p_{n}/q_{n}
. The numerators
p_{n}
and denominators
q_{n}
are given recursively by

\displaystyle q_{0}=0,

\displaystyle q_{1}=1\quad

\displaystyle q_{n+1}=a_{n}q_{n}+q_{n-1};

\displaystyle p_{0}=1,

\displaystyle p_{1}=0\quad

\displaystyle p_{n+1}=a_{n}p_{n}+p_{n-1}.

Note that the indexing of
p_{n}
and
q_{n}
is offset by one compared to what is normally seen in the literature. As a consequence, the
n
th convergent
p_{n}/q_{n}
is smaller than
\alpha
for every odd value of
n
, and greater than
\alpha
for every even value of
n
. It follows readily from the recurrences above that

p_{n}q_{n+1}-p_{n+1}q_{n}=(-1)^{n},

(2.1)

and as a consequence of this identity we have

p_{n}q_{n-1}=(-1)^{n}\pmod{q_{n}}.

(2.2)

Another important consequence of (
2.1
) is the error bound

\frac{1}{q_{n}(q_{n+1}+q_{n})}\leq\left|\alpha-\frac{p_{n}}{q_{n}}\right|<\frac{1}{q_{n+1}q_{n}}

(2.3)

for the
n
th convergent of
\alpha
.

Let us continue with stating two useful relations involving
\alpha_{n}^{+}
and
\alpha_{n}^{-}
defined as in (
1.4
). For
\alpha=[0;a_{1},a_{2},\ldots]
it can be easily checked that

\alpha_{n}^{-}=[0;a_{n-1},a_{n-2},\ldots,a_{1}]=\frac{q_{n-1}(\alpha)}{q_{n}(\alpha)}

(2.4)

and it is well known that

\alpha=\frac{p_{n}(\alpha)\alpha^{+}_{n}+p_{n-1}(\alpha)}{q_{n}(\alpha)\alpha_{n}^{+}+q_{n-1}(\alpha)}.

(2.5)

Further, we introduce a quantity which will appear frequently in the rest of this article

\Lambda_{n}(\alpha):=q_{n}\alpha-p_{n}.

(2.6)

Let us therefore summarize some well known properties of this object in the following Lemma.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
with best approximation denominators
(q_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
and for
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
let
\Lambda_{n}(\alpha)
be as in (
2.6
). Then it follows that

(a)

|\Lambda_{n}(\alpha)|=(-1)^{n-1}\Lambda_{n}(\alpha)
and
\Lambda_{n}(\alpha)\to 0
for
n\to\infty
.

(b)

\dfrac{1}{2q_{n+1}}\leq|\Lambda_{n}(\alpha)|\leq\dfrac{1}{q_{n+1}}<\dfrac{1}{a_{n}q_{n}}

(c)

|\Lambda_{n}(\alpha)|q_{n}=c_{n}
, where
c_{n}
is defined in (
1.6
).

Proof.

The first part of
(a)
follows from the fact that the
n
th convergent
p_{n}/q_{n}
is smaller than
\alpha
for every odd value of
n
, and greater than
\alpha
for every even value of
n
. The second part of
(a)
and the statement of
(b)
are direct consequences of (
2.3
).
For
(c)
it follows by (
2.5
) that

q_{n}|\Lambda_{n}(\alpha)|=q_{n}^{2}\left|\alpha-\frac{p_{n}}{q_{n}}\right|=q_{n}^{2}\left|\frac{p_{n}\alpha^{+}_{n}+p_{{n}-1}}{q_{n}\alpha^{+}_{n}+q_{n-1}}-\frac{p_{n}}{q_{n}}\right|=\frac{q_{n}}{q_{n}\alpha^{+}_{n}+q_{n-1}}=c_{n}(\alpha),

where we have used (
2.1
) and (
2.4
).
∎

Whenever it is clear from context, we will drop the dependence on
\alpha
of
\Lambda_{n}(\alpha)
.

2.2
Ostrowski representation

Another useful tool will be the fact that one can represent each integer
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
in a unique way with respect to the best approximation denominators of some irrational
\beta
.

Theorem D

(Ostrowski representation)
.

Let
\beta\in(0,1)
be an irrational number with continued fraction expansion
[0;b_{1},b_{2},\ldots]
and best approximation denominators
(q_{n}(\beta))_{n\geq 0}
. Then every non-negative integer
t
has a unique expansion

t=\sum_{n=1}^{N}v_{n}q_{n}(\beta),

(2.7)

where:

i)

0\leq v_{1}<b_{1}
and
0\leq v_{n}\leq b_{n}
for
n>1
.

ii)

If
v_{n}=b_{n}
for some
n
, then
v_{n-1}=0
.

iii)

N=N(t)=\mathcal{O}(\log(t))

We refer to (
2.7
) as the Ostrowski representation of
N
in base
\beta
.

The proof of Theorem
D
can be found in
[
16
, p. 126]
. Recall that we defined (see (
1.5
)) for an irrational
\beta

D_{t}(\beta)=\sum_{s=1}^{t}\{\beta t\}-\frac{1}{2}.

It is possible to give an explicit formula for
D_{t}(\beta)
in terms of the digits of the Ostrowski expansion of
t
in base
\beta
.

Theorem E

(
[
6
]
, Theorem 1)
.

Let
\beta\in(0,1)
and irrational. For any
t\geq 1
let
t=\sum_{i=1}^{N}v_{i}q_{i}(\beta)
be the Ostrowski expansion of
t
in base
\beta
. Then we have

D_{t}(\beta)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(v_{i}\Lambda_{i}(\beta)(v_{i}q_{i}(\beta)+1)+(-1)^{i}v_{i}\right)+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq N}v_{i}v_{j}q_{i}(\beta)\Lambda_{j}(\beta).

(2.8)

It is well known (see for example
[
12
,
20
]
) that for irrationals
\beta
with bounded c.f.c. we have that

D_{t}(\beta)={\mathcal{O}}(\log(t)).

(2.9)

Remark 2.3
.

This also follows from Theorem
E
together with the facts that
q_{n}(\beta)|\Lambda_{n}(\beta)|=c_{n}(\beta)
and that for the Ostrowski representation of
t
in base
\beta
i.e.
t=\sum_{i=1}^{N}v_{i}q_{i}(\beta)
we have
\sum_{i=1}^{k}v_{i}q_{i}(\beta)<q_{k+1}(\beta)
for all
k\in\{1,\ldots,N-1\}
. Using these facts we obtain for an irrational
\beta=[0;b_{1},b_{2},\ldots]
that

\displaystyle|D_{t}(\beta)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}v_{i}\left|1-c_{i}(\beta)v_{i}-|\Lambda_{i}(\beta)|\right|+\sum_{j=1}^{N}v_{j}|\Lambda_{j}(\beta)|\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}v_{i}q_{i}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}v_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}v_{i}\leq\frac{3}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}b_{i}.

Equation (
2.9
) now follows by the fact that
\beta
has bounded c.f.c. and since
N={\mathcal{O}}(\log(t))
(see Theorem
D
).

In fact it will turn out later on that we need analogs of (
2.8
) and (
2.9
) for certain rational
\beta
. More precisely, we are interested in the case
\beta_{n}=\alpha_{n}^{-}=q_{n-1}(\alpha)/q_{n}(\alpha)
for a fixed
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Observe that in this case the Ostrowski representation in base
\beta_{n}=[0;b_{1},\ldots,b_{n-1}]
of
t\in\{1,\ldots,q_{n}(\beta_{n})-1\}
is still well defined (here
b_{i}=a_{n-i}
). One can check, by following the exact same arguments as in
[
6
]
and Remark
2.3
, that the statement in Theorem
E
and (
2.9
) are still valid for
\beta_{n}=\alpha_{n}^{-}=q_{n-1}(\alpha)/q_{n}(\alpha)
as long as
t<q_{n}(\beta_{n})
.

Another fact that should be mentioned in this context is that

q_{n}(\beta_{n})=q_{n}(\alpha_{n}^{-})=q_{n}(\alpha)\text{ for all }n\in{\mathbb{N}}.

This can be seen by using the fact that for
\gamma=[0;c_{1},c_{2},\ldots]
we have
q_{n}(\gamma)=\det(C_{n-1}(\gamma))
combined with a Laplace expansion argument. Here
C_{n-1}(\gamma)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{(n-1)\times(n-1)}
is a tridiagonal matrix where the entries in the first upper minor diagonal are
-1
, the entries in the first lower minor diagonal are
1
and the main diagonal consists of
c_{1},\ldots,c_{n-1}
.

3
Proof-strategy for Theorems
1.2
and
1.7

In
[
24
]
Mestel and Verschueren were able to find a suitable decomposition of the product
P_{F_{n}}(\varphi)
, where
\varphi
is the golden ratio. With the help of this decomposition they managed to prove that the sequence
(P_{F_{n}}(\varphi))_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is convergent, where
(F_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
are the Fibonacci numbers (note that
q_{n}(\varphi)=F_{n}
). Exactly this approach was generalized to arbitrary quadratic irrationals in
[
11
, Lemma 4.2]
. This was one of the key steps in proving the corresponding convergence result for quadratic irrationals (see Theorem
B
). Therefore our first step will be to generalize
[
11
, Lemma 4.2]
to the case of an arbitrary irrational
\alpha
. This will result in the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
and irrational. For
n\geq 1
let
p_{n}/q_{n}
be the
n
th convergent of
\alpha
. Then we can rewrite the product
P_{q_{n}}(\alpha)
as

P_{q_{n}}(\alpha)=\prod_{r=1}^{q_{n}}|2\sin\pi r\alpha|=A_{n}B_{n}C_{n},

where

\displaystyle A_{n}

\displaystyle=\left|2q_{n}\sin(\pi\Lambda_{n})\right|;

(3.1)

\displaystyle B_{n}

\displaystyle=\left|\prod_{t=1}^{q_{n}-1}\frac{s_{nt}}{2\sin(\pi t/q_{n})}\right|;

(3.2)

\displaystyle C_{n}

\displaystyle=\prod_{t=1}^{q_{n}-1}\left(1-\frac{s_{n0}^{2}}{s_{nt}^{2}}\right)^{1/2},

(3.3)

where
\Lambda_{n}
is as in (
2.6
) and for
t\in\{0,1,\ldots,q_{n}-1\}
we have set

s_{nt}:=2\sin\left(\pi\left[\frac{t}{q_{n}}-|\Lambda_{n}|\left(\left\{\frac{tq_{n-1}}{q_{n}}\right\}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\right]\right).

(3.4)

The second step and also the main part of this article is to understand how the structure of the c.f.e. of the irrational
\alpha
influences the asymptotic behaviour of
(P_{q_{n}}(\alpha))_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
. This is will be achieved by a more detailed investigation of the factors
A_{n}
,
B_{n}
and
C_{n}
in Lemma
3.1
. The results of this investigation are described in the subsequent lemma.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
be irrational with c.f.e.
\alpha=[0;a_{1},a_{2},\ldots]
and let
D_{t}(\beta)
,
c_{n}
and
\Lambda_{n}
be defined as in (
1.5
), (
1.6
) and (
2.6
). Let
(\tau_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
and
(\kappa_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be eventually increasing sequences of natural numbers with

\tau_{n},\kappa_{n}={\mathcal{O}}(q^{1/2}_{n}).

(3.5)

Then for sufficiently large
n
we have that

P_{q_{n}}(\alpha)=A_{n}B_{n}C_{n},

where

\displaystyle A_{n}=

\displaystyle 2\pi c_{n}(1+{\mathcal{O}}(\Lambda_{n}^{2}));

(3.6)

\displaystyle\log(B_{n})=

\displaystyle-2\pi^{2}\frac{c_{n}}{q^{2}_{n}}\sum_{t=1}^{M_{n}-1}\frac{D_{t}(\alpha_{n}^{-})}{\sin(\pi t/q_{n})\sin(\pi(t+1)/q_{n})}

\displaystyle-2\sum_{t=1}^{\tau_{n}}\sum_{j=2}^{\tau_{n}}\frac{1}{j}\left(\frac{c_{n}\xi_{nt}}{t}\right)^{j}+{\mathcal{O}}\left(\tau_{n}^{-1}\right);

(3.7)

\displaystyle C_{n}=

\displaystyle\prod_{t=1}^{\kappa_{n}}\left(1-\frac{1}{4\left(t/c_{n}-\xi_{nt}\right)^{2}}\right)+{\mathcal{O}}\left(\kappa_{n}^{-1}\right),

(3.8)

with

\displaystyle\xi_{nt}

\displaystyle=\left\{\frac{tq_{n-1}}{q_{n}}\right\}-\frac{1}{2}\text{ for }t\in\{0,1,\ldots,q_{n}-1\}.

(3.9)

Remark 3.3
.

It should be mentioned at this point that the condition in (
3.5
) on the eventually increasing sequences
(\kappa_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
,
(\tau_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
could be relaxed to
\tau_{n},\kappa_{n}={\scriptstyle{\mathcal{O}}}(q_{n})
. This would lead to remainder terms of order
{\mathcal{O}}(\tau_{n}^{-1}+\tau_{n}^{2}q_{n}^{-2})
and
{\mathcal{O}}(\kappa_{n}^{-1}+\kappa_{n}^{2}q_{n}^{-2})
in (
3.7
) and (
3.8
), respectively. But it is enough for our purposes to stick to the less technical version described in (
3.5
).

We will see later on that the proof of Theorem
1.2
is then a direct consequence of Lemma
3.2
.

Another important observation is that in the case of quadratic irrationals the periodicity of the c.f.e. induces a certain structure (see Theorem
B
) on the convergent subsequences of
(P_{q_{n}}(\alpha))_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
. In the more general case of irrationals with bounded c.f.c. this effect is reflected by the sufficient conditions stated in Theorem
1.7
. It will turn out that Lemma
3.2
together with the requirements of Theorem
1.7
will give us the desired convergence results.

4
Proof of Lemma
3.1
and Lemma
3.2

The proof of Lemma
3.1
follows the lines of
[
24
]
and
[
11
]
, where Lemma
3.1
has been shown for the cases
\alpha=\varphi
(golden ratio) and
\alpha
being a quadratic irrational, respectively. For the sake of completeness we give the most important steps here.

Proof of Lemma
3.1
.

Following the exact same steps as in
[
11
, p.22]
and using that by definition of
\Lambda_{n}
we have
q_{n}\alpha=p_{n}+\Lambda_{n}
, one can easily check that

\displaystyle P_{q_{n}}^{2}(\alpha)

\displaystyle=\left(2\sin\pi\Lambda_{n}\right)^{2}\prod_{r=1}^{q_{n}-1}4\left(\sin^{2}\left(\pi r\alpha-\frac{\pi}{2}\Lambda_{n}\right)-\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\Lambda_{n}\right)\right).

If we further use the identity
\alpha=p_{n}/q_{n}+\Lambda_{n}/q_{n}
we obtain

\displaystyle\sin^{2}\left(\pi r\alpha-\frac{\pi}{2}\Lambda_{n}\right)=\sin^{2}\left(\pi\left[\frac{rp_{n}}{q_{n}}+\Lambda_{n}\left(\frac{r}{q_{n}}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\right]\right).

By the substitution
t=rp_{n}\bmod q_{n}
, and recalling from (
2.1
) that
p_{n}q_{n-1}=(-1)^{n}\bmod q_{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\sin^{2}\left(\pi r\alpha-\frac{\pi}{2}\Lambda_{n}\right)

\displaystyle=\sin^{2}\left(\pi\left[\frac{rp_{n}\bmod{q_{n}}}{q_{n}}+\Lambda_{n}\left(\frac{r}{q_{n}}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\right]\right)

\displaystyle=\sin^{2}\left(\pi\left[\frac{t}{q_{n}}+\Lambda_{n}\left(\frac{(-1)^{n}tq_{n-1}\bmod{q_{n}}}{q_{n}}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\right]\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}s_{nt}^{2},

with
s_{nt}
given in (
3.4
). Note that we applied the identities
\Lambda_{n}=(-1)^{n-1}|\Lambda_{n}|
(see Lemma
2.2
) and

\frac{(-1)^{n}tq_{n-1}\bmod{q_{n}}}{q_{n}}-\frac{1}{2}=\left\{\frac{(-1)^{n}tq_{n-1}}{q_{n}}\right\}-\frac{1}{2}=(-1)^{n}\left(\left\{\frac{tq_{n-1}}{q_{n}}\right\}-\frac{1}{2}\right).

As
r
runs through the values
1,2,\ldots,q_{n}-1
, so does
t=rp_{n}\bmod q_{n}
. Accordingly, we get

\displaystyle P^{2}_{q_{n}}(\alpha)

\displaystyle=(2\sin(\pi\Lambda_{n}))^{2}\prod_{t=1}^{q_{n}-1}\left(s^{2}_{nt}-s^{2}_{n0}\right)

\displaystyle=(2q_{n}\sin(\pi\Lambda_{n}))^{2}\prod_{t=1}^{q_{n}-1}\frac{s^{2}_{nt}}{4\sin^{2}(\pi t/q_{n})}\prod_{t=1}^{q_{n}-1}\left(1-\frac{s^{2}_{n0}}{s^{2}_{nt}}\right).

For the last equality above we have used the well-known identity

\prod_{r=1}^{q-1}2\sin\left(\frac{\pi rp}{q}\right)=q

whenever
p,q\in{\mathbb{Z}}
satisfy
\gcd(p,q)=1
(see e.g.
[
19
]
for a nice proof). Taking the square root of both sides, we arrive at

P_{q_{n}}(\alpha)=A_{n}B_{n}C_{n},

where
A_{n}
,
B_{n}
and
C_{n}
are given in (
3.1
), (
3.2
) and (
3.3
), respectively.
∎

In order to prove the statement of Lemma
3.2
we have to investigate each of the factors
A_{n}
,
B_{n}
and
C_{n}
more carefully. To do so we will follow again the ideas of
[
24
]
and
[
11
]
. This task will be split into three lemmata, one for each of the three factors. We start with the simplest one, the factor
A_{n}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
A_{n}
be as in (
3.1
). Then for sufficiently large
n
we have that

A_{n}=2\pi c_{n}\left(1+{\mathcal{O}}\left(\Lambda_{n}^{2}\right)\right).

(4.1)

Proof.

Recall that by (
3.1
) we know that
A_{n}=2\pi q_{n}\sin(\pi\Lambda_{n})
. Since
\Lambda_{n}

tends to 0 for
n\to\infty
(see Lemma
2.2
), we get by
\sin(x)=x(1+{\mathcal{O}}(x^{2}))
for
x\to 0
that for sufficiently large
n

A_{n}=2q_{n}\sin(\pi\Lambda_{n})=2\pi q_{n}|\Lambda_{n}|\left(1+{\mathcal{O}}(\Lambda_{n}^{2})\right)=2\pi c_{n}\left(1+{\mathcal{O}}(\Lambda_{n}^{2})\right).

Note that for the last step we have used the third property of Lemma
2.2
.
∎

Before moving on to the factor
C_{n}
recall from (
3.3
) that the sequences
s_{nt}
given in (
3.4
) and
\xi_{nt}
given in (
3.9
) play an important role for the behaviour of
C_{n}
. Further, it is not hard to see that

s_{nt}=2\sin\left(\pi\left[\frac{t}{q_{n}}-\xi_{nt}|\Lambda_{n}|\right]\right).

(4.2)

The situation is similar for the following sequence
h_{nt}
and the factor
B_{n}

h_{nt}:=\cot\left(\frac{\pi t}{q_{n}}\right)\sin\left(\pi|\Lambda_{n}|\xi_{nt}\right)\text{ for }t\in\{0,1,\ldots,q_{n}-1\}.

(4.3)

The exact role of
h_{nt}
will become more clear once we focus on the factor
B_{n}
, which happens in the last part of this section. But still it is convenient to introduce the sequence
h_{nt}
already at this point since otherwise we would have to repeat a similar version of Lemma
4.2
later on. We continue with some auxiliary results on
s_{nt}
,
\xi_{nt}
and
h_{nt}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
s_{nt}
,
\xi_{nt}
and
h_{nt}
be the sequences given in (
3.4
), (
3.9
) and (
4.3
). We have that:

(a)

s_{nt}=s_{n(q_{n}-t)}
,
h_{nt}=h_{n(q_{n}-t)}
and
\xi_{nt}=-\xi_{n(q_{n}-t)}
for all
1\leq t<q_{n}
.

(b)

s_{nt}>s_{n0}
for all
1\leq t<q_{n}
if
n
is
sufficiently large.

Proof.

We first verify
(a)
. The fact that
\{-x\}=1-\{x\}
for
x\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Z}
immediately implies
\xi_{nt}=-\xi_{n(q_{n}-t)}
. Combining this with
\sin(\pi-x)=\sin x
, we get

\displaystyle s_{n(q_{n}-t)}

\displaystyle=2\sin\left(\pi\left[\frac{q_{n}-t}{q_{n}}-\xi_{n(q_{n}-t)}|\Lambda_{n}|\right]\right)

\displaystyle=2\sin\left(\pi\left[1-\left(\frac{t}{q_{n}}-\xi_{nt}|\Lambda_{n}|\right)\right]\right)=s_{nt},

and likewise since
\cot(\pi-x)=-\cot(x)
and
\sin(x)
is an odd function, we immediately get by using
\xi_{nt}=-\xi_{n(q_{n}-t)}
that
h_{n(q_{n}-t)}=h_{nt}

for every
t\in\{1,\ldots,q_{n}-1\}
. Now let us verify
(b)
. In light of
(a)
, it is enough to verify

s_{nt}>s_{n0}
for
t\in\{1,2,\ldots,\lfloor q_{n}/2\rfloor\}
.
Writing again

s_{nt}=2\sin\left(\pi\left[\frac{t}{q_{n}}-\xi_{nt}|\Lambda_{n}|\right]\right)

and recalling that
|\xi_{nt}|<1/2
for these
values of
t
, it is clear that for all
t\in\{2,\ldots,\lfloor q_{n}/2\rfloor\}
it follows
s_{nt}>s_{n(t-1)}
, and in particular for
t=1

s_{n1}>2\sin\left(\pi\left[\frac{1}{q_{n}}-\frac{1}{2}|\Lambda_{n}|\right]\right)>2\sin\left(\frac{\pi|\Lambda_{n}|}{2}\right)=s_{n0},

since
|\Lambda_{n}|<1/q_{n+1}<1/q_{n}
which was shown in Lemma
2.2
.
∎

Now we are ready for the analysis of the factor
C_{n}
. It will be our next goal to prove the subsequent lemma.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
C_{n}
be as in (
3.3
). For sufficiently large
n
we have that

C_{n}=\prod_{t=1}^{\kappa_{n}}\left(1-\frac{1}{4\left(t/c_{n}-\xi_{nt}\right)^{2}}\right)+{\mathcal{O}}\left(\kappa_{n}^{-1}\right),

(4.4)

where
\kappa_{n}=\lfloor q_{n}^{1/2}\rfloor
.

Remark 4.4
.

Note that Lemma
4.3
is actually slightly weaker than the statement in Lemma
3.2
since we set
\kappa_{n}=\lfloor q_{n}^{1/2}\rfloor
. The more general version (i.e.
\kappa_{n}={\mathcal{O}}(q^{1/2}_{n})
) follows by the exact same proof steps. But the choice
\kappa_{n}=\lfloor q_{n}^{1/2}\rfloor
will increase the readability of the subsequent proof.

Proof.

We begin by developing estimates for
s_{n0}/s_{nt}
,
t\in\{1,\ldots,q_{n}-1\}
. If
n
is sufficiently large it follows by using
\sin(x)=x(1+{\mathcal{O}}(x^{2}))
that

s_{n0}=2\sin\left(\pi|\Lambda_{n}|/2\right)=\pi|\Lambda_{n}|\left(1+\mathcal{O}(\Lambda_{n}^{2})\right).

For
t\geq 1
we have that

\displaystyle s_{nt}

\displaystyle=2\sin\left(\pi\left[\frac{t}{q_{n}}-|\Lambda_{n}|\xi_{nt}\right]\right)=2\sin\left(\pi t|\Lambda_{n}|\left[\frac{1}{c_{n}}-\frac{\xi_{nt}}{t}\right]\right),

(4.5)

where we have used Lemma
2.2
. We now split the values of
t
at

\kappa_{n}:=\left\lfloor q_{n}^{1/2}\right\rfloor,

(4.6)

and treat
t\leq\kappa_{n}
and
t>\kappa_{n}
separately in order to find appropriate bounds on
s_{nt}
in (
4.5
). For
t>\kappa_{n}
, we apply
\sin(x)\geq 2x/\pi
for
x\in[0,\pi/2]
to obtain

s_{nt}\geq 4t|\Lambda_{n}|\left(\frac{1}{c_{n}}-\frac{\xi_{nt}}{t}\right)\geq 4t\frac{|\Lambda_{n}|}{c_{n}}\left(1-\frac{|\xi_{nt}|c_{n}}{t}\right).

Note that it follows from Lemma
2.2
that
0<c_{n}<1
and recall that
|\xi_{nt}|<1/2
. Thus, for sufficiently large
n
(and thereby sufficiently large
t
), we have
s_{nt}>2t|\Lambda_{n}|/c_{n}
and

\frac{s_{n0}}{s_{nt}}\leq\frac{\pi c_{n}|\Lambda_{n}|\left(1+\mathcal{O}(\Lambda_{n}^{2})\right)}{2t|\Lambda_{n}|}=\frac{\pi c_{n}}{2t}\left(1+\mathcal{O}(\Lambda_{n}^{2})\right).

Further it follows that

\sum_{t=\kappa_{n}+1}^{q_{n}-1}\frac{s_{n0}^{2}}{s_{nt}^{2}}\leq\frac{\pi^{2}c_{n}^{2}}{4}(1+{\mathcal{O}}(\Lambda_{n}^{2}))\sum_{t=\kappa_{n}+1}^{q_{n}-1}\frac{1}{t^{2}}={\mathcal{O}}(\kappa_{n}^{-1}),

and accordingly this sum is convergent and smaller than one for sufficiently large
n
.
Thus, by Lemma
2.1
and for sufficiently large
n
we get that

1\geq\prod_{t=\kappa_{n}+1}^{q_{n}-1}\left(1-\frac{s_{n0}^{2}}{s_{nt}^{2}}\right)^{1/2}>\left(1-\sum_{t=\kappa_{n}+1}^{q_{n}-1}\frac{s_{n0}^{2}}{s_{nt}^{2}}\right)^{1/2}=1-\mathcal{O}(\kappa_{n}^{-1}),

(4.7)

where we used the fact that
\sqrt{1-x}\geq 1-x
for
x\in(0,1)
. Now consider
t\leq\kappa_{n}.
It is clear from (
4.5
) that by choosing
n
sufficiently large, the argument in the sine function
s_{nt}
can be made arbitrarily small in this case. Applying
\sin(x)=x+\mathcal{O}(x^{3})
, we get

\displaystyle s_{nt}

\displaystyle=2\sin\left(\pi\left[\frac{t}{q_{n}}-|\Lambda_{n}|\xi_{nt}\right]\right)=2\pi|\Lambda_{n}|\left(\frac{t}{c_{n}}-\xi_{nt}\right)+{\mathcal{O}}(t^{3}q_{n}^{-3})

\displaystyle=\pi|\Lambda_{n}|u_{n}(t)\left(1+{\mathcal{O}}(t^{2}q_{n}^{-2})\right)=\pi|\Lambda_{n}|u_{n}(t)\left(1+{\mathcal{O}}(q_{n}^{-1})\right),

where we have used that
t\leq\kappa_{n}\leq q_{n}^{1/2}
and
(|\Lambda_{n}|u_{t})^{-1}={\mathcal{O}}(t^{-1}q_{n})
. Moreover, we introduced the notation

u_{n}(t)=2\left(\frac{t}{c_{n}}-\xi_{nt}\right)=2\left(\frac{t}{c_{n}}-\{t\alpha_{n}^{-}\}+\frac{1}{2}\right).

(4.8)

Thus,

\frac{s_{n0}}{s_{nt}}=\frac{\pi|\Lambda_{n}|\left(1+\mathcal{O}(\Lambda_{n}^{2})\right)}{\pi|\Lambda_{n}|u_{n}(t)\left(1+\mathcal{O}(q_{n}^{-1})\right)}=\frac{1+\mathcal{O}(q_{n}^{-1})}{u_{n}(t)}

and further

\displaystyle\prod_{t=1}^{\kappa_{n}}\left(1-\frac{s_{n0}^{2}}{s_{nt}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\prod_{t=1}^{\kappa_{n}}\left(1-\frac{1}{u_{n}(t)^{2}}\right)\prod_{t=1}^{\kappa_{n}}\left(1-\frac{\mathcal{O}(q_{n}^{-1})}{u_{n}(t)^{2}-1}\right).

(4.9)

Let us have a closer look at the two products on the final line above. Since
|\xi_{nt}|<1/2
and
c_{n}<1
, we see from (
4.8
) that
u_{n}(t)>1
for all
1\leq t\leq\kappa_{n}
. Therefore both products are well-defined and we see that

\sum_{t=1}^{\kappa_{n}}\frac{1}{u_{n}(t)^{2}-1}\leq\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{4(t/c_{n}-1/2)^{2}-1}<\infty.

Hence,
\sum_{t}\mathcal{O}(q_{n}^{-1})/(u_{n}(t)^{2}-1)=\mathcal{O}(q_{n}^{-1})
. The latter sum is thus smaller than one, provided
n
is sufficiently large, and again it follows from Lemma
2.1
that

1>\prod_{t=1}^{\kappa_{n}}\left(1-\frac{\mathcal{O}(q_{n}^{-1})}{u_{n}(t)^{2}-1}\right)\geq 1-\sum_{t=1}^{\kappa_{n}}\frac{\mathcal{O}(q_{n}^{-1})}{u_{n}(t)^{2}-1}=1-\mathcal{O}(q_{n}^{-1}).

(4.10)

Combining the estimates (
4.7
) with (
4.9
) and (
4.10
) we obtain that

\displaystyle C_{n}

\displaystyle=\prod_{t=1}^{\kappa_{n}}\left(1-\frac{1}{u_{n}(t)^{2}}\right)\cdot\prod_{t=1}^{\kappa_{n}}\left(1-\frac{\mathcal{O}(q_{n}^{-1})}{u_{n}(t)^{2}-1}\right)\cdot\prod_{t=\kappa_{n}+1}^{(q_{n}-1)/2}\left(1-\frac{s_{n0}^{2}}{s_{nt}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\prod_{t=1}^{\kappa_{n}}\left(1-\frac{1}{u_{n}(t)^{2}}\right)+{\mathcal{O}}(\kappa_{n}^{-1}).

This completes the proof of Lemma
4.3
.
∎

We continue with the factor
B_{n}
. What remains to be shown for Lemma
3.2
is the following statement.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
B_{n}
be as in (
3.2
),
M_{n}=\left\lfloor(q_{n}-1)/2\right\rfloor
. Then for sufficiently large
n
we have that

\displaystyle\log(B_{n})=

\displaystyle-2\pi^{2}\frac{c_{n}}{q_{n}^{2}}\sum_{t=1}^{M_{n}-1}\frac{D_{t}(\alpha_{n}^{-})}{\sin(\pi t/q_{n})\sin(\pi(t+1)/q_{n})}

\displaystyle-2\sum_{t=1}^{\tau_{n}}\sum_{j=2}^{\tau_{n}}\frac{1}{j}\left(\frac{c_{n}\xi_{nt}}{t}\right)^{j}+{\mathcal{O}}\left(\tau_{n}^{-1}\right),

(4.11)

where
\alpha_{n}^{-}
and
D_{t}(\alpha_{n}^{-})
are given in (
1.4
) and (
1.5
), respectively and
\tau_{n}=\lfloor q_{n}^{1/2}\rfloor
.

Remark 4.6
.

Similar as for the factor
C_{n}
and Lemma
4.5
we again prove a slightly simpler version (
\tau_{n}=\lfloor q_{n}^{1/2}\rfloor
) compared to the statement in Lemma
3.2
(
\tau_{n}={\mathcal{O}}(q_{n}^{1/2})
). Again the more general version of Lemma
3.2
can be proved by following the exact same steps as in the subsequent proof.

Proof.

Recall that we have

B_{n}=\left|\prod_{t=1}^{q_{n}-1}\frac{s_{nt}}{2\sin(\pi t/q_{n})}\right|.

We begin by examining each term of the product
B_{n}
. Recalling the definition of
s_{nt}
from (
3.4
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{s_{nt}}{2\sin(\pi t/q_{n})}

\displaystyle=\cos(\pi|\Lambda_{n}|\xi_{nt})-\cot(\pi t/q_{n})\sin\left(\pi|\Lambda_{n}|\xi_{nt}\right)

\displaystyle=1-2\sin^{2}(\pi|\Lambda_{n}|\xi_{nt}/2)-h_{nt},

with
h_{nt}
given in (
4.3
). Taking
\beta_{nt}:=2\sin^{2}(\pi|\Lambda_{n}|\xi_{nt}/2)
, it is easily verified that
\beta_{n(q_{n}-t)}=\beta_{nt}
for
t\in\{1,\ldots,q_{n}-1\}
. Likewise, we recall from Lemma
4.2

(a)
that
h_{n(q_{n}-t)}=h_{nt}
, and thus

B_{n}=\prod_{t=1}^{q_{n}-1}(1-\beta_{nt}-h_{nt})=\theta_{n}\prod_{t=1}^{\left\lfloor(q_{n}-1)/2\right\rfloor}(1-\beta_{nt}-h_{nt})^{2},

where
\theta_{n}=1
if
q_{n}
is odd and
\theta_{n}=1-\beta_{n(q_{n}/2)}
otherwise. (Note that
h_{n(q_{n}/2)}=0
.)
This shows that we only need to consider
t\in\{1,\ldots,\lfloor(q_{n}-1)/2\rfloor\}
.

Let us now show that rather than analyzing
B_{n}
, we may choose to analyze the simpler product

B_{n}^{*}:=\prod_{t=1}^{q_{n}-1}(1-h_{nt})=\prod_{t=1}^{\left\lfloor(q_{n}-1)/2\right\rfloor}(1-h_{nt})^{2}.

(4.12)

It can be shown completely analogously to
[
11
, p. 29]
that we have

\left|\log(B_{n})-\log(B_{n}^{*})\right|=\left|\log(\theta_{n})+{\mathcal{O}}(q_{n}\Lambda_{n}^{2})\right|=\mathcal{O}(|\Lambda_{n}|),

(4.13)

where we have additionally used that
|\log(\theta_{n})|\leq\beta_{n(q_{n}/2})\leq\pi^{2}/8\Lambda_{n}^{2}
and
|\Lambda_{n}|q_{n}=c_{n}\leq 1
.
Thus
\lim_{n\to\infty}\log(B_{n})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\log(B_{n}^{*})
. This confirms that we may choose to analyze
B_{n}^{*}
in (
4.12
) rather than
B_{n}
.

Finally, we set
M_{n}:=\left\lfloor(q_{n}-1)/2\right\rfloor
and rewrite
\log(B_{n}^{*})
using its Taylor expansion as

\displaystyle\log(B_{n}^{*})

\displaystyle=2\sum_{t=1}^{M_{n}}\log(1-h_{nt})=-2\sum_{t=1}^{M_{n}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{j}h_{nt}^{j}

(4.14)

\displaystyle=-2\left(\sum_{t=1}^{M_{n}}h_{nt}+\sum_{t=1}^{M_{n}}\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{j}h_{nt}^{j}\right)=:-2(H_{n}^{(1)}+H_{n}^{(2)}).

We continue by investigating the sums
H_{n}^{(1)}
and
H_{n}^{(2)}
separately in the following subsections.

Analysis of
H_{n}^{(2)}
:

We first treat the sum

H_{n}^{(2)}=\sum_{t=1}^{M_{n}}\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{j}h_{nt}^{j}.

Let us start by proving that terms where
t
or
j
is greater than some
\tau_{n}
will not contribute significantly to
H_{n}^{(2)}
. Note that by applying
\cot(x)=1/x(1+{\mathcal{O}}(x^{2}))
and
\sin(x)=x(1+{\mathcal{O}}(x^{2}))
we obtain for sufficiently large
n

h_{nt}=\cot(\pi t/q_{n})\sin(\pi|\Lambda_{n}|\xi_{nt})=\frac{c_{n}\xi_{nt}}{t}(1+{\mathcal{O}}(t^{2}q_{n}^{-2}))

(4.15)

and if we use the rougher bounds
\cot(x)<1/x
and
\sin(x)<x
we get for sufficiently large
n
that

h_{nt}=\cot(\pi t/q_{n})\sin(\pi|\Lambda_{n}|\xi_{nt})\leq\frac{c_{n}\xi_{nt}}{t}<\frac{1}{2t}.

(4.16)

Now for
J\geq 2
we obtain by (
4.16
)

\left|\sum_{j=J}^{\infty}\frac{1}{j}h^{j}_{nt}\right|\leq\sum_{j=J}^{\infty}|h^{j}_{nt}|=\frac{|h^{J}_{nt}|}{1-|h_{nt}|}<2\left(\frac{1}{2t}\right)^{J}.

(4.17)

Further we set

\tau_{n}=\lfloor q_{n}^{1/2}\rfloor

(4.18)

and therefore we have
1\leq\tau_{n}\leq\left\lfloor(q_{n}-1)/2\right\rfloor=M_{n}
if
n
is sufficiently large. We then get by (
4.17
)

\left|\sum_{t=\tau_{n}+1}^{M_{n}}\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{j}h^{j}_{nt}\right|<\sum_{t=\tau_{n}+1}^{M_{n}}2\left(\frac{1}{2t}\right)^{2}<\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t=\tau_{n}+1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t^{2}}<\frac{1}{2\tau_{n}}

and again by (
4.17
)

\left|\sum_{t=1}^{\tau_{n}}\sum_{j=\tau_{n}+1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{j}h^{j}_{nt}\right|<\sum_{t=1}^{\tau_{n}}2\left(\frac{1}{2t}\right)^{\tau_{n}+1}<\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{\tau_{n}}\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t^{2}}=\frac{\pi^{2}}{6\cdot 2^{\tau_{n}}}.

Both of these sums are at most of order
\mathcal{O}(\tau_{n}^{-1})
, and it follows by (
4.15
)

\displaystyle H_{n}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{\tau_{n}}\sum_{j=2}^{\tau_{n}}\frac{1}{j}h^{j}_{nt}+\sum_{t=1}^{\tau_{n}}\sum_{j=\tau_{n}+1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{j}h^{j}_{nt}+\sum_{t=\tau_{n}+1}^{M_{n}}\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{j}h^{j}_{nt}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{\tau_{n}}\sum_{j=2}^{\tau_{n}}\frac{1}{j}h^{j}_{nt}+\mathcal{O}(\tau_{n}^{-1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{\tau_{n}}\sum_{j=2}^{\tau_{n}}\frac{1}{j}\left(\frac{c_{n}\xi_{nt}}{t}\right)^{j}\left(1+{\mathcal{O}}(t^{2}q_{n}^{-2})\right)^{j}+\mathcal{O}(\tau_{n}^{-1}).

(4.19)

Now observe that for some
x>0

(1+x)^{j}=1+\sum_{i=1}^{j}\binom{j}{i}x^{i}\leq 1+2^{j}x\frac{1-x^{j}}{1-x}.

(4.20)

Moreover, note that by
t^{2}<\tau_{n}^{2}<q_{n}^{-1}
it follows for sufficiently large
n
that
{\mathcal{O}}(t^{2}q_{n}^{-2})<1
and by (
4.20
) that
(1+{\mathcal{O}}(t^{2}q_{n}^{-2}))^{j}=1+{\mathcal{O}}(2^{j}t^{2}q_{n}^{-2})
. Hence,

\displaystyle H_{n}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{\tau_{n}}\sum_{j=2}^{\tau_{n}}\frac{1}{j}\left(\frac{c_{n}\xi_{nt}}{t}\right)^{j}+{\mathcal{O}}\left(\sum_{t=1}^{\tau_{n}}\sum_{j=2}^{\tau_{n}}\frac{1}{j}\left(\frac{c_{n}\xi_{nt}}{t}\right)^{j}2^{j}t^{2}q_{n}^{-2}\right)+\mathcal{O}(\tau_{n}^{-1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{\tau_{n}}\sum_{j=2}^{\tau_{n}}\frac{1}{j}\left(\frac{c_{n}\xi_{nt}}{t}\right)^{j}+{\mathcal{O}}\bigg(q_{n}^{-2}\underbrace{\sum_{t=1}^{\tau_{n}}\sum_{j=2}^{\tau_{n}}t^{2-j}}_{\leq 3\tau_{n}}\bigg)+\mathcal{O}(\tau_{n}^{-1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{\tau_{n}}\sum_{j=2}^{\tau_{n}}\frac{1}{j}\left(\frac{c_{n}\xi_{nt}}{t}\right)^{j}+\mathcal{O}(\tau_{n}^{-1}),

(4.21)

where we have used that
|c_{n}\xi_{n}{t}|\leq 1/2
and
\tau_{n}\leq q^{1/2}_{n}
.
Let us now move on to the analysis of
H_{n}^{(1)}
.

Analysis of
H_{n}^{(1)}
:

We are left with investigating the behaviour of

H_{n}^{(1)}=\sum_{t=1}^{M_{n}}h_{nt}=\sum_{t=1}^{M_{n}}\cot\left(\frac{\pi t}{q_{n}}\right)\sin(\pi|\Lambda_{n}|\xi_{nt}).

It follows by a summation by parts argument (see
[
11
, p. 32/33]
) that

H_{n}^{(1)}=\sum_{t=1}^{M_{n}-1}C_{nt}S_{nt}+{\mathcal{O}}\left(q_{n}^{-1}\right),

(4.22)

where we set
M_{n}=\left\lfloor(q_{n}-1)/2\right\rfloor
and

\displaystyle C_{nt}:=\cot(\pi t/q_{n})-\cot(\pi(t+1)/q_{n});

\displaystyle S_{nt}:=\sum_{s=1}^{t}\sin(\pi|\Lambda_{n}|\xi_{ns}).

Let us investigate
C_{nt}
closer. For ease of notation we write
\phi=\pi/q_{n}
. Then we have

\displaystyle 0<C_{nt}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sin((t+1)\phi)\cos(t\phi)-\cos((t+1)\phi)\sin(t\phi)}{\sin(t\phi)\sin((t+1)\phi)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sin(\phi)}{\sin(t\phi)\sin((t+1)\phi)}=\frac{\phi\left(1+{\mathcal{O}}\left(\phi^{2}\right)\right)}{\sin(t\phi)\sin((t+1)\phi)}.

(4.23)

Thus, by using again
\sin(x)=x(1+{\mathcal{O}}(x^{2}))
we obtain

\displaystyle C_{nt}S_{nt}

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi}{q_{n}\sin(\pi t/q_{n})\sin(\pi(t+1)/q_{n})}\left(1+{\mathcal{O}}\left(\phi^{2}\right)\right)\sum_{s=1}^{t}\sin(\pi|\Lambda_{n}|\xi_{ns})

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi^{2}|\Lambda_{n}|}{q_{n}\sin(\pi t/q_{n})\sin(\pi(t+1)/q_{n})}\left(1+{\mathcal{O}}\left(q_{n}^{-2}\right)\right)\left(1+{\mathcal{O}}(\Lambda_{n}^{2})\right)\sum_{s=1}^{t}\xi_{ns}

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi^{2}c_{n}}{q^{2}_{n}}\frac{D_{t}(\alpha_{n}^{-})}{\sin(\pi t/q_{n})\sin(\pi(t+1)/q_{n})}\left(1+{\mathcal{O}}\left(q_{n}^{-2}\right)\right).

(4.24)

Note that we have used Lemma
2.2
and (
1.5
) in the above calculations. Further, observe that
0<(t+1)/q_{n}<1/2
for
t<M_{n}=\left\lfloor(q_{n}-1)/2\right\rfloor
. Now it follows by combining the trivial bounds
|D_{t}(\alpha_{n}^{-})|<t/2
,
c_{n}\leq 1
and
\sin(x)\geq 2x/\pi
for
x\in(0,\pi/2)
with (
4
) that

\left|\frac{\pi^{2}c_{n}}{q^{2}_{n}}\frac{D_{t}(\alpha_{n}^{-})}{\sin(\pi t/q_{n})\sin(\pi(t+1)/q_{n})}\right|=\frac{\pi^{2}c_{n}}{q^{2}_{n}}\frac{\left|D_{t}(\alpha_{n}^{-})\right|}{\sin(\pi t/q_{n})\sin(\pi(t+1)/q_{n})}\leq\frac{\pi^{2}}{8t}.

Therefore it follows from (
4
)

\sum_{t=1}^{M_{n}-1}C_{nt}S_{nt}=\frac{\pi^{2}c_{n}}{q_{n}^{2}}\sum_{t=1}^{M_{n}-1}\frac{D_{t}(\alpha_{n}^{-})}{\sin(\pi t/q_{n})\sin(\pi(t+1)/q_{n})}+{\mathcal{O}}\left(\frac{\log(q_{n})}{q_{n}^{2}}\right).

(4.25)

Combining (
4.14
), (
4.21
), (
4.22
) and (
4.25
) finally gives for sufficiently large
n

\displaystyle\log(B_{n})=

\displaystyle-2\frac{\pi^{2}c_{n}}{q_{n}^{2}}\sum_{t=1}^{M_{n}-1}\frac{D_{t}(\alpha_{n}^{-})}{\sin(\pi t/q_{n})\sin(\pi(t+1)/q_{n})}

\displaystyle-2\sum_{t=1}^{\tau_{n}}\sum_{j=2}^{\tau_{n}}\frac{1}{j}\left(\frac{c_{n}\xi_{nt}}{t}\right)^{j}+\mathcal{O}(\tau_{n}^{-1}).

(4.26)

This finishes the proof of Lemma
4.5
.
∎

The statement of Lemma
3.2
now follows by the combined assertions of Lemma
4.1
, Lemma
4.3
and Lemma
4.5
.

5
Proof of Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.7

In this final section we will give proofs for Theorem
1.7
and Theorem
1.2
, starting with the latter one. The proofs of Corollary
1.3
and Corollary
1.5
are stated in this section as well.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Lemma
3.2
already gives us detailed information concerning the structure of
P_{q_{n}}(\alpha)
for an arbitrary irrational
\alpha
. Recall that we have

P_{q_{n}}(\alpha)=A_{n}B_{n}C_{n},

(5.1)

where

\displaystyle A_{n}=

\displaystyle 2\pi c_{n}(1+{\mathcal{O}}(\Lambda_{n}^{2}));

\displaystyle\log(B_{n})=

\displaystyle-2\pi^{2}\frac{c_{n}}{q_{n}^{2}}\sum_{t=1}^{M_{n}-1}\frac{D_{t}(\alpha_{n}^{-})}{\sin(\pi t/q_{n})\sin(\pi(t+1)/q_{n})}

\displaystyle-2\sum_{t=1}^{\tau_{n}}\sum_{j=2}^{\tau_{n}}\frac{1}{j}\left(\frac{c_{n}\xi_{nt}}{t}\right)^{j}+{\mathcal{O}}\left(\tau_{n}^{-1}\right);

\displaystyle C_{n}=

\displaystyle\prod_{t=1}^{\kappa_{n}}\left(1-\frac{1}{4\left(t/c_{n}-\xi_{nt}\right)^{2}}\right)+{\mathcal{O}}\left(\kappa_{n}^{-1}\right)

and
(\kappa_{n})_{n}{\in{\mathbb{N}}}
and
(\tau_{n})_{n}{\in{\mathbb{N}}}
are eventually increasing sequences of natural numbers satisfying (
3.5
).

Clearly we have that

A_{n}=\Theta(c_{n})\text{ for }n\to\infty.

(5.2)

For the factor
C_{n}
recall that
1/c_{n}=(\alpha_{n}^{+}+\alpha_{n}^{-})\in(a_{n},a_{n}+2)
and
|\xi_{nt}|<1/2.
It is obvious that
C_{n}
can be bounded from above since

\prod_{t=1}^{\kappa_{n}}\left(1-\frac{1}{4\left(t/c_{n}-\xi_{nt}\right)^{2}}\right)\leq\prod_{t=1}^{\kappa_{n}}\left(1-\frac{1}{\left(2t(a_{n}+2)+1\right)^{2}}\right)<1.

(5.3)

For the lower bound note that

\sum_{t=2}^{\kappa_{n}}\frac{1}{4(t/c_{n}-\xi_{nt})^{2}}\leq\sum_{t=2}^{\kappa_{n}}\frac{1}{4(ta_{n}-\frac{1}{2})^{2}}\leq\sum_{t=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2t-1)^{2}}<\frac{1}{4}.

Therefore we can make use of Lemma
2.1
and obtain that

\displaystyle\prod_{t=1}^{\kappa_{n}}\left(1-\frac{1}{4\left(t/c_{n}-\xi_{nt}\right)^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{1}{4(1/c_{n}-\xi_{n1})^{2}}\right)\prod_{t=2}^{\kappa_{n}}\left(1-\frac{1}{4\left(t/c_{n}-\xi_{nt}\right)^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{(2a_{n}+1)^{2}}\right)\left(1-\sum_{t=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{4(t/c_{n}-\xi_{nt})^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle>\frac{3}{4}\left(1-\frac{1}{(2a_{n}+1)^{2}}\right)\geq\frac{2}{3}.

(5.4)

Thus, by (
5.3
) and (
5
) we get that

C_{n}=\Theta(1)\text{ for }n\to\infty.

(5.5)

Let us now investigate the remaining factor
B_{n}
. Observe that the double sum in (
3.7
) is actually bounded. We have that

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{t=1}^{\tau_{n}}\sum_{j=2}^{\tau_{n}}\frac{1}{j}\left(\frac{c_{n}\xi_{nt}}{t}\right)^{j}\right|\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2t)^{j}}=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t(2t-1)}=\frac{\log(2)}{2}.

(5.6)

It follows by (
5.6
) and
\sin(x)=x\left(1+{\mathcal{O}}(x^{2})\right)
that

\displaystyle\log(B_{n})

\displaystyle=-2\pi^{2}\frac{c_{n}}{q^{2}_{n}}\sum_{t=1}^{M_{n}-1}\frac{D_{t}(\alpha_{n}^{-})}{\sin(\pi t/q_{n})\sin(\pi(t+1)/q_{n})}+{\mathcal{O}}(1)

\displaystyle=-2c_{n}\sum_{t=1}^{M_{n}-1}\frac{D_{t}(\alpha_{n}^{-})}{t(t+1)}\left(1+{\mathcal{O}}(t^{2}q_{n}^{-2})\right)+{\mathcal{O}}(1)

\displaystyle=-2c_{n}\sum_{t=1}^{M_{n}-1}\frac{D_{t}(\alpha_{n}^{-})}{t(t+1)}+{\mathcal{O}}\left(1+2c_{n}q_{n}^{-2}\sum_{t=1}^{M_{n}-1}D_{t}(\alpha_{n}^{-})\right)

\displaystyle=-2c_{n}\sum_{t=1}^{M_{n}-1}\frac{D_{t}(\alpha_{n}^{-})}{t(t+1)}+{\mathcal{O}}(1).

Hence,

B_{n}=\Theta\left(\exp\Big(-2c_{n}\sum_{t=1}^{M_{n}-1}\frac{D_{t}(\alpha_{n}^{-})}{t(t+1)}\Big)\right)\text{ for }n\to\infty.

(5.7)

Therefore we obtain from (
5.1
) together with (
5.2
), (
5.5
) and (
5.7
) that

P_{q_{n}}(\alpha)=\Theta\left(c_{n}\exp\Big(-2c_{n}\sum_{t=1}^{M_{n}-1}\frac{D_{t}(\alpha_{n}^{-})}{t(t+1)}\Big)\right)\text{ for }n\to\infty.

(5.8)

This finishes the proof of Theorem
1.2
.
∎

Now that we have established Theorem
1.2
we are ready to prove Corollary
1.3
.

Proof of Corollary
1.3
.

If
\alpha=[0;a_{1},a_{2},\ldots]
has bounded c.f.c. then clearly we have

\frac{1}{A+2}\leq\frac{1}{a_{n}+2}<c_{n}<\frac{1}{a_{n}}\leq 1,

(5.9)

where
A:=\sup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}a_{n}
. Recall that for
\alpha_{n}^{-}=q_{n-1}(\alpha)/q_{n}(\alpha)
it was pointed out in Section
2
that

D_{t}(\alpha_{n}^{-})={\mathcal{O}}(\log(t))

as long as
t\in\{1,\ldots,q_{n}(\alpha)-1\}
. Note that
M_{n}=\left\lfloor(q_{n}(\alpha)-1)/2\right\rfloor<q_{n}(\alpha)
and therefore

\left|\sum_{t=1}^{M_{n}-1}\frac{D_{t}(\alpha_{n}^{-})}{t(t+1)}\right|\leq K\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}\frac{\log(t)}{t^{2}}<\infty,

(5.10)

where
K>0
is some constant independent of
n
. From (
5.9
) and (
5.10
) together with Theorem
1.2
it follows that

P_{q_{n}}(\alpha)=\Theta\left(c_{n}\exp\Big(-2c_{n}\sum_{t=1}^{M_{n}-1}\frac{D_{t}(\alpha_{n}^{-})}{t(t+1)}\Big)\right)=\Theta(1).

If
\alpha
has unbounded c.f.c. then we have that
c_{n}=\Theta(1/a_{n})
and by Theorem
E
it follows for
t\in\{1,\ldots,q_{n}(\alpha)-1\}
with Ostrowski representation in base
\alpha_{n}^{-}
of the form
t=\sum_{i=1}^{N(t)}v_{i}q_{i}(\alpha_{n}^{-})
that

\displaystyle D_{t}(\alpha_{n}^{-})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N(t)}\left(v_{i}\Lambda_{i}(\alpha_{n}^{-})(v_{i}q_{i}(\alpha_{n}^{-})+1)+(-1)^{i}v_{i}\right)+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq N(t)}v_{i}v_{j}q_{i}(\alpha_{n}^{-})\Lambda_{j}(\alpha_{n}^{-})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N(t)}(-1)^{i}v_{i}\left(1-v_{i}c_{i}(\alpha_{n}^{-})-|\Lambda_{i}|\right)+\sum_{j=1}^{N(t)}v_{j}\Lambda_{j}(\alpha_{n}^{-})\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}v_{i}q_{i}(\alpha_{n}^{-}).

By the properties of the Ostrowski representation we get that
v_{i}c_{i}(\alpha_{n}^{-})\leq 1
,
v_{i}|\Lambda_{i}|\leq 1
and
\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}v_{i}q_{i}(\alpha_{n}^{-})<q_{j}(\alpha_{n}^{-})
. Thus, by additionally using that
N(t)={\mathcal{O}}(\log(t))
we have

D_{t}(\alpha_{n}^{-})=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N(t)}(-1)^{i}v_{i}\left(1-v_{i}c_{i}(\alpha_{n}^{-})\right)+{\mathcal{O}}(\log(t)).

(5.11)

Inserting (
5.11
) in the assertion of Theorem
1.2
we obtain

P_{q_{n}}(\alpha)=\Theta\left(\frac{1}{a_{n}}\exp\left(-2\frac{1}{a_{n}}\sum_{t=1}^{M_{n}-1}\frac{D_{t}(\alpha_{n}^{-})}{t(t+1)}\right)\right)=\Theta\left(\frac{1}{a_{n}}\exp\left(\frac{1}{a_{n}}Y_{n}(\alpha)\right)\right),

(5.12)

where
Y_{n}=\sum_{t=1}^{M_{n}-1}1/(t(t+1))\sum_{i=1}^{N(t)}(-1)^{i-1}v_{i}\left(1-v_{i}c_{i}(\alpha_{n}^{-})\right)
. This verifies also the second statement of Corollary
1.3
.
∎

Let us now shift our attention to the proof of Theorem
1.7
. We will show that the factors
A_{n}
,
B_{n}
and
C_{n}
will converge, given that
\alpha
has bounded continued fraction coefficients and
\lim_{n\to\infty}\alpha_{n}^{+}
and
\lim_{n\to\infty}\alpha_{n}^{-}
exist.
We split the proof of Theorem
1.7
into three parts, one part for each of the factors
A_{n}
,
B_{n}
and
C_{n}
. Before starting with the convergence of
A_{n}
we introduce some notation that will be helpful later on, especially for the convergence of
B_{n}
and
C_{n}
.
We assume that the following limits exist and set

\alpha_{\infty}^{+}:=\lim_{n\to\infty}\alpha_{n}^{+}\text{ and }\alpha_{\infty}^{-}:=\lim_{n\to\infty}\alpha_{n}^{-}.

(5.13)

Further,

\displaystyle f_{n}^{+}:=|\alpha_{n}^{+}-\alpha_{\infty}^{+}|\text{ and }f_{n}^{-}:=|\alpha_{n}^{-}-\alpha_{\infty}^{-}|.

(5.14)

By definition we get that
f_{n}^{+},f_{n}^{-}\rightarrow 0^{+}
for
n\to\infty
and we also have

\alpha_{n}^{+}=\alpha_{\infty}^{+}+{\mathcal{O}}(f^{+}_{n})\text{ and }\alpha_{n}^{-}=\alpha_{\infty}^{-}+{\mathcal{O}}(f^{-}_{n}).

(5.15)

Proof of the convergence of
A_{n}
:

The desired convergence for
A_{n}
follows immediately from the assumptions of Theorem
1.7
together with Lemma
3.2
. We have that

\lim_{n\to\infty}A_{n}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{2\pi}{\alpha_{n}^{+}+\alpha_{n}^{-}}\left(1+{\mathcal{O}}(\Lambda_{n}^{2})\right)=\frac{2\pi}{\alpha_{\infty}^{+}+\alpha_{\infty}^{-}}.

(5.16)

∎

We need a little bit more effort to prove the convergence of
C_{n}
.

Proof of the convergence of
C_{n}
:

Recall that by Lemma
3.2
we have that

C_{n}=\prod_{t=1}^{\kappa_{n}}\left(1-\frac{1}{4\left(t/c_{n}-\xi_{nt}\right)^{2}}\right)+{\mathcal{O}}\left(\kappa_{n}^{-1}\right).

(5.17)

As stated in Lemma
3.2
and also pointed out in Remark
4.4
equation (
5.17
) also holds for the choice

\kappa_{n}=\left\lfloor\min\{1/(f^{-}_{n})^{1/2},q_{n}^{1/2}\}\right\rfloor.

Observe that by this choice we have for
t\in\{1,\ldots,\kappa_{n}\}
that
tf^{-}_{n}\leq\kappa_{n}f^{-}_{n}\leq\left(f^{-}_{n}\right)^{1/2}\rightarrow 0
for
n\to\infty
. Now together with (
5.15
) it follows that for sufficiently large
n
we obtain

\displaystyle 4\left(t/c_{n}-\xi_{nt}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=4\left(t(\alpha_{n}^{+}+\alpha_{n}^{-})-\{t\alpha_{n}^{-}\}+1/2\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=4\left(t(\alpha_{\infty}^{+}+\alpha_{\infty}^{-})-\{\alpha_{\infty}^{-}t\}+1/2\right)^{2}+t^{2}{\mathcal{O}}(f^{+}_{n}+f^{-}_{n})

\displaystyle=u_{\infty}^{2}(t)+t^{2}{\mathcal{O}}(f^{+}_{n}+f^{-}_{n}),

where we have set

u_{\infty}(t):=2\left(t(\alpha_{\infty}^{+}+\alpha_{\infty}^{-})-\{t\alpha_{\infty}^{-}\}+1/2\right).

(5.18)

Further, it follows that

\displaystyle\prod_{t=1}^{\kappa_{n}}\left(1-\frac{1}{4\left(t/c_{n}-\xi_{nt}\right)^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\prod_{t=1}^{\kappa_{n}}\left(1-\frac{1}{u_{\infty}(t)^{2}}\frac{1}{1+t^{2}/u_{\infty}(t)^{2}{\mathcal{O}}(f^{+}_{n}+f^{-}_{n})}\right)

\displaystyle=\prod_{t=1}^{\kappa_{n}}\left(1-\frac{1}{u_{\infty}(t)^{2}}-\frac{{\mathcal{O}}(f^{+}_{n}+f^{-}_{n})}{u_{\infty}(t)^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\prod_{t=1}^{\kappa_{n}}\left(1-\frac{1}{u_{\infty}(t)^{2}}\right)\prod_{t=1}^{\kappa_{n}}\left(1-\frac{{\mathcal{O}}(f^{+}_{n}+f^{-}_{n})}{u_{\infty}(t)^{2}-1}\right).

(5.19)

Let us analyze the convergence of the two products in (
5
) separately. First of all
it is clear that
\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}1/u_{\infty}(t)^{2}<\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}1/(u_{\infty}(t)^{2}-1)<\infty
by comparison with
\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}1/t^{2}
. The convergence of the product
\prod_{t=1}^{\kappa_{n}}\left(1-1/u_{\infty}(t)^{2}\right)
now follows by the fact that this is a decreasing sequence in
n
(note:
u_{\infty}(t)>2a_{n}-1\geq 1
) and the product is bounded from below by

\prod_{t=1}^{\kappa_{n}}\left(1-\frac{1}{u_{\infty}(t)^{2}}\right)\geq 1-\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{u_{\infty}(t)^{2}}\geq 1-\frac{1}{u_{\infty}(1)^{2}}-\sum_{t=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2t-1)^{2}}>\frac{3}{5}.

(5.20)

Note that we have used Lemma
2.1
and the fact that
\alpha_{\infty}^{+}>a_{n}\geq 1
. For the second product observe that
{\mathcal{O}}(f^{+}_{n}+f^{-}_{n})\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}1/(u_{\infty}(t)^{2}-1)<1
for sufficiently large
n
and we get again by Lemma
2.1
that

\displaystyle 1-S\leq\prod_{t=1}^{\kappa_{n}}\left(1-\frac{{\mathcal{O}}(f^{+}_{n}+f^{-}_{n})}{u_{\infty}(t)^{2}-1}\right)\leq\left(1-S\right)^{-1},

(5.21)

where
S:={\mathcal{O}}(f^{+}_{n}+f^{-}_{n})\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}1/(u_{\infty}(t)^{2}-1)\rightarrow 0
for
n\to\infty
. Summarizing the convergence analysis of the products in (
5
) it follows that

\lim_{n\to\infty}C_{n}=\prod_{t=1}^{\infty}\left(1-\frac{1}{u_{\infty}^{2}(t)}\right).

(5.22)

Observe that
L_{1}:=\lim_{n\to\infty}C_{n}>0
by (
5.20
). This finishes the proof of the convergence of
C_{n}
.
∎

Finally we have to show the convergence of the factor
B_{n}
. Recall that by Lemma
3.2
we have that

\displaystyle\log(B_{n})=

\displaystyle-2\pi^{2}\frac{c_{n}}{q^{2}_{n}}\sum_{t=1}^{M_{n}-1}\frac{D_{t}(\alpha_{n}^{-})}{\sin(\pi t/q_{n})\sin(\pi(t+1)/q_{n})}

\displaystyle-2\sum_{t=1}^{\tau_{n}}\sum_{j=2}^{\tau_{n}}\frac{1}{j}\left(\frac{c_{n}\xi_{nt}}{t}\right)^{j}+{\mathcal{O}}\left(\tau_{n}^{-1}\right).

(5.23)

As stated in Lemma
3.2
and also indicated in Remark
4.6
equation (
5
) also holds for

\tau_{n}:=\left\lfloor\min\{\log_{2}\left((f^{+}_{n}+f^{-}_{n})^{-1/2}\right),q_{n}^{1/2}\}\right\rfloor.

(5.24)

A consequence of this choice is that for
1\leq t,j\leq\tau_{n}
it follows that for some fixed
k\in{\mathbb{N}}

\displaystyle t^{k}f_{n}^{-}\leq-\frac{1}{2}\left(\log_{2}(f^{-}_{n})\right)^{k}f_{n}^{-}\rightarrow 0,\text{ for }n\to\infty;

(5.25)

\displaystyle 2^{j}f_{n}^{-}\leq(f_{n}^{+}+f_{n}^{-})^{-1/2}f_{n}^{-}\leq\sqrt{f_{n}^{-}}\rightarrow 0,\text{ for }n\to\infty.

(5.26)

These facts will be useful several times in the convergence analysis of
B_{n}
.

Proof of convergence of
B_{n}
.

We see from (
5
) that it is sufficient for the convergence of
B_{n}
to prove convergence of the following two terms:

\pi^{2}\frac{c_{n}}{q^{2}_{n}}\sum_{t=1}^{M_{n}-1}\frac{D_{t}(\alpha_{n}^{-})}{\sin(\pi t/q_{n})\sin(\pi(t+1)/q_{n})}\text{ and }\sum_{t=1}^{\tau_{n}}\sum_{j=2}^{\tau_{n}}\frac{1}{j}\left(\frac{c_{n}\xi_{nt}}{t}\right)^{j}.

(5.27)

We start with the double sum term in (
5.27
). Let us first analyze the summands more detailed. It follows by (
1.6
) and (
3.9
) for sufficiently large
n
that

\displaystyle\frac{c_{n}\xi_{nt}}{t}=\frac{\{t\alpha_{n}^{-}\}-1/2}{t(\alpha_{n}^{+}+\alpha_{n}^{-})}=\frac{\{t\alpha_{\infty}^{-}\}-1/2}{t(\alpha_{\infty}^{+}+\alpha_{\infty}^{-})}\left(1+{\mathcal{O}}(f^{+}_{n}+f^{-}_{n})\right)+{\mathcal{O}}(f_{n}^{-}).

(5.28)

In order to simplify notation let us set
v_{t}:=(\{t\alpha_{\infty}^{-}\}-1/2)/(t(\alpha_{\infty}^{+}+\alpha_{\infty}^{-}))
and recall that
v_{t}<1/(2t)
and
f_{n}^{+},f_{n}^{-}\rightarrow 0
for
n\to\infty
.
Since we have for
x,y\leq 1

(x+y)^{j}\leq x^{j}+2^{j}y

it follows for sufficiently large
n
together with (
5.28
) that

\displaystyle\left(\frac{c_{n}\xi_{nt}}{t}\right)^{j}

\displaystyle=v_{t}^{j}\left(1+{\mathcal{O}}(f^{+}_{n}+f^{-}_{n})\right)^{j}+{\mathcal{O}}(2^{j}f_{n}^{-})

\displaystyle=v_{t}^{j}\left(1+{\mathcal{O}}(2^{j}(f^{+}_{n}+f_{n}^{-}))\right)+{\mathcal{O}}(\sqrt{f_{n}^{-}})

\displaystyle=v_{t}^{j}+{\mathcal{O}}(\sqrt{f_{n}^{-}}).

(5.29)

Note that we used (
5.26
) in the last step. It follows by (
5
) together with the choice of
\tau_{n}
that

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{\tau_{n}}\sum_{j=2}^{\tau_{n}}\frac{1}{j}\left(\frac{c_{n}\xi_{nt}}{t}\right)^{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{\tau_{n}}\sum_{j=2}^{\tau_{n}}\frac{1}{j}v_{t}^{j}+{\mathcal{O}}\left(\tau_{n}^{2}\sqrt{f_{n}^{-}}\right)=\sum_{t=1}^{\tau_{n}}\sum_{j=2}^{\tau_{n}}\frac{1}{j}v_{t}^{j}+{\mathcal{O}}\left(\frac{\sqrt{f^{-}_{n}}}{\log_{2}((f^{-}_{n})^{-1/2})}\right).

Furthermore we see that

\sum_{t=1}^{\tau_{n}}\sum_{j=2}^{\tau_{n}}\left|\frac{1}{j}v_{t}^{j}\right|\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2t)^{j}}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2t(2t-1)}<\infty.

Thus
\sum_{t=1}^{\tau_{n}}\sum_{j=2}^{\tau_{n}}\frac{1}{j}v_{t}^{j}
is an absolutely convergent series and therefore there exists a constant
L_{2}
with

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{t=1}^{\tau_{n}}\sum_{j=2}^{\tau_{n}}\frac{1}{j}\left(\frac{\{t\alpha_{n}^{-}\}-1/2}{t(\alpha_{n}^{+}+\alpha_{n}^{-})}\right)^{j}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{t=1}^{\tau_{n}}\sum_{j=2}^{\tau_{n}}\frac{1}{j}v_{t}^{j}=L_{2}.

(5.30)

Let us shift our attention to the second term in (
5.27
). Making use of
\sin(x)=x(1+{\mathcal{O}}(x^{2}))
we obtain for sufficiently large
n
that

\displaystyle\pi^{2}\frac{c_{n}}{q^{2}_{n}}

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{M_{n}-1}\frac{D_{t}(\alpha_{n}^{-})}{\sin(\pi t/q_{n})\sin(\pi(t+1)/q_{n})}=c_{n}\sum_{t=1}^{M_{n}-1}\frac{D_{t}(\alpha_{n}^{-})}{t(t+1)}\left(1+{\mathcal{O}}(t^{2}q_{n}^{-2})\right)

\displaystyle=c_{n}\sum_{t=1}^{\tau_{n}}\frac{D_{t}(\alpha_{n}^{-})}{t(t+1)}+c_{n}\sum_{t=\tau_{n}+1}^{M_{n}-1}\frac{D_{t}(\alpha_{n}^{-})}{t(t+1)}+{\mathcal{O}}\left(q_{n}^{-2}\sum_{t=1}^{M_{n}}D_{t}(\alpha_{n}^{-})\right)

\displaystyle=c_{n}\sum_{t=1}^{\tau_{n}}\frac{D_{t}(\alpha_{n}^{-})}{t(t+1)}+{\mathcal{O}}\left(\frac{\log(\tau_{n})}{\tau_{n}}+\frac{\log(q_{n}!)}{q_{n}^{2}}\right),

(5.31)

where we have used in the last step that
D_{t}(\alpha_{n}^{-})={\mathcal{O}}(\log(t))
for each
t\in\{1,\ldots,M_{n}-1\}
(see Section
2
, equation (
2.9
)). Additionally by (
5.25
) we have for sufficiently large
n
that

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{\tau_{n}}\frac{D_{t}(\alpha_{n}^{-})}{t(t+1)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{\tau_{n}}\frac{1}{t(t+1)}\sum_{s=1}^{t}\left(\{s\alpha_{n}^{-}\}-\frac{1}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{\tau_{n}}\frac{1}{t(t+1)}\sum_{s=1}^{t}\left(\{s\alpha_{\infty}^{-}\}-\frac{1}{2}+s{\mathcal{O}}\left(f^{-}_{n}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{\tau_{n}}\left[\frac{1}{t(t+1)}\sum_{s=1}^{t}\left(\{s\alpha_{\infty}^{-}\}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\right]+{\mathcal{O}}\left(\tau_{n}f^{-}_{n}\right).

(5.32)

Observe that by the choice of
\tau_{n}
in (
5.24
) we clearly have that
\tau_{n}f_{n}^{-}\rightarrow 0
for
n\to\infty
. Additionally note that by (
2.9
) it follows that for some constant
K>0
independent of
n
we have

\left|\sum_{t=1}^{\tau_{n}}\frac{1}{t(t+1)}\sum_{s=1}^{t}\left(\{s\alpha_{\infty}^{-}\}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\right|\leq K\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}\frac{\log(t)}{t^{2}}<\infty.

Therefore,
\sum_{t=1}^{\tau_{n}}1/(t(t+1))\sum_{s=1}^{t}\left(\{s\alpha_{\infty}^{-}\}-1/2\right)
is an absolutely convergent series. Together with (
5
) this means that there exists a real number
L_{3}
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{t=1}^{\tau_{n}}\frac{D_{t}(\alpha_{n}^{-})}{t(t+1)}=L_{3}.

(5.33)

Finally, by combining (
5
) with (
5.30
) and (
5.33
) we get that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}B_{n}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\exp\left(-2c_{n}\sum_{t=1}^{\tau_{n}}\frac{D_{t}(\alpha_{n}^{-})}{t(t+1)}-2\sum_{t=1}^{\tau_{n}}\sum_{j=2}^{\tau_{n}}\frac{1}{j}\left(\frac{c_{n}\xi_{nt}}{t}\right)^{j}\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-\frac{2L_{3}}{\alpha_{\infty}^{+}+\alpha_{\infty}^{-}}-2L_{2}\right)>0.

(5.34)

This establishes the convergence of
B_{n}
.
∎

Finally, the statement of Theorem
1.7
is now a direct consequence of the convergence of the factors
A_{n}
,
B_{n}
and
C_{n}
. By (
5.16
), (
5.22
) and (
5
) it follows that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}P_{q_{n}}(\alpha)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}A_{n}\lim_{n\to\infty}B_{n}\lim_{n\to\infty}C_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi}{\alpha_{\infty}^{+}+\alpha_{\infty}^{-}}\exp\left(-\frac{2L_{2}}{\alpha_{\infty}^{+}+\alpha_{\infty}^{-}}-2L_{3}\right)L_{1}>0.

Last but not least we have to prove Corollary
1.5
.

Proof fo Corollary
1.5
.

Recall that
\alpha=\mathrm{e}=[2;\overline{1,2n,1}]_{n=1}^{\infty}
. Let
n_{i}^{(k)}=n_{i}=3i+k
for
k\in\{0,1,2\}
and recall that by (
1.4
) we have

\alpha^{-}_{n_{i}}=[0;a_{n_{i}-1},a_{n_{i}-2},\ldots,a_{1}]

and
a_{3i}=1
for
i\geq 1
,
a_{3i+1}=1
and
a_{3i+2}=2(i+1)
for
i\geq 0
.
Now consider the Ostrowski representation in base
\alpha^{-}_{n_{i}}
for
t\in\{1,\ldots,q_{n}(\alpha)-1\}=\{1,\ldots,q_{n}(\alpha_{n_{i}})-1\}
i.e.
t=\sum_{j=1}^{N(t)}v_{j}(t)q_{j}(\alpha_{n_{i}}^{-})
. (As already mentioned in Section
2
this representation is well defined.) By Theorem
1.2
we get that

P_{q_{n_{i}}}(\mathrm{e})=\Theta\left(c_{n_{i}}\exp\left(c_{n_{i}}\sum_{t=1}^{M_{n_{i}}-1}\frac{y_{n_{i}}(t)}{t(t+1)}\right)\right),

(5.35)

where we have set

\displaystyle y_{n_{i}}(t):=\sum_{j=1}^{N(t)}(-1)^{j-1}v_{j}(t)(1-v_{j}(t)c_{j}(\alpha_{n_{i}}^{-})).

Let
k=0
i.e.
n_{i}=3i
, then
\alpha_{n_{i}}^{-}=[0;2i,1,1,2(i-1),1,1,\ldots,1]
. For sufficiently large
i
we can split up the sum over
t
appearing in (
5.35
) in the following way

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{M_{n_{i}}-1}\frac{y_{n_{i}}(t)}{t(t+1)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{q_{2}(\alpha_{n_{i}}^{-})-1}\frac{y_{n_{i}}(t)}{t(t+1)}+\sum_{t=q_{2}(\alpha_{n_{i}}^{-})}^{q_{5}(\alpha_{n_{i}}^{-})-1}\frac{y_{n_{i}}(t)}{t(t+1)}+\sum_{t=q_{5}(\alpha_{n_{i}}^{-})}^{M_{n_{i}}-1}\frac{y_{n_{i}}(t)}{t(t+1)}

\displaystyle=:S_{1}+S_{2}+S_{3}.

We compute that

\displaystyle q_{2}(\alpha_{n_{i}}^{-})=2i;\penalty\ q_{3}(\alpha_{n_{i}}^{-})=2i+1;\penalty\ q_{4}(\alpha_{n_{i}}^{-})=4i+1;\penalty\ q_{5}(\alpha_{n_{i}}^{-})=8i^{2}-4i-1.

Observe further that the Ostrowski representation of
t
in base
\alpha_{n_{i}}
collapses to
t=v_{1}(t)
for
t\in\{1,\ldots,q_{2}(\alpha_{n_{i}}^{-})-1\}
. Using these observations it follows that

\displaystyle S_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{q_{2}(\alpha_{n_{i}}^{-})-1}\frac{1}{t(t+1)}\sum_{j=1}^{N(t)}(-1)^{j-1}v_{j}(t)(1-v_{j}(t)c_{j}(\alpha_{n_{i}}^{-}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{2i-1}\frac{1}{(t+1)}-c_{1}(\alpha_{n_{i}}^{-})\sum_{t=1}^{2i-1}\frac{t}{(t+1)}

\displaystyle\geq\log(2i)-1-\frac{2i-1}{a_{n_{i}-1}+2}\geq\log(2i)-2,

(5.36)

where we applied
c_{1}(\alpha_{n_{i}})\leq 1/a_{n_{i}-1}=1/(2i).
For the second sum we obtain

\displaystyle S_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=2i}^{8i^{2}-4i-2}\frac{1}{t(t+1)}\sum_{j=1}^{4}(-1)^{j-1}v_{j}(t)(1-v_{j}(t)c_{j}(\alpha_{n_{i}}^{-}))

\displaystyle\geq-\sum_{t=2i}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{4}a_{n_{i}-j}=-4i\sum_{t=2i}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t^{2}}\geq-4,

(5.37)

where we have applied the bounds
1-v_{j}(t)c_{j}(\alpha_{n_{i}})<1
and
v_{j}(t)\leq a_{n_{i}-j}
which are valid for all
t
. We can apply similar arguments for the third sum
S_{3}
and get

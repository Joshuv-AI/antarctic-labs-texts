L p L^{p} Liouville type theorems for harmonic functions on gradient Ricci solitons
Yong Luo

Abstract
In this paper we consider L p L^{p} Liouville type theorems for harmonic functions on gradient Ricci solitons. In particular, assume that ( M , g ) (M,g) is a gradient shrinking or steady Kähler-Ricci soliton, then we prove that any pluriharmonic function u u on M M with ∇ u ∈ L p ​ ( M ) \nabla u\in L^{p}(M) for some 1 < p ≤ 2 1<p\leq 2 is a constant function.



1
Introduction

The classical Liouville theorem states that any bounded holomorphic function on the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
must be constant, which also holds for harmonic functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In 1975, Yau
[
23
]
generalized this theorem by proving that any positive harmonic function on an
n
-dimension complete manifold with nonnegative Ricci curvature must be constant, and later Cheng and Yau
[
5
]
showed a gradient estimate on the manifold with Ricci curvature lower bound to give an effective version of the Liouville theorem. Cheng and Yau’s gradient estimate method plays a crucial role in the study of many partial differential equations on manifolds (see e.g.
[
7
,
14
]
and references therein for more information).

It is also very interesting to consider Liouville type theorem under integrability conditions. For example, it’s natural to ask whether a manifold possesses a non-trivial
L^{2}
harmonic function. In 1976, Yau
[
24
]
proved that any
L^{p}(p>1)
harmonic function on a complete manifold must be constant. As a corollary, by using the Bochner formula, he proved that any harmonic function
u
with
\nabla u
in
L^{p}(p>1)
on a complete manifold with nonnegative Ricci curvature must be constant.

In this paper we are curious with Liouville type theorems for harmonic functions on gradient Ricci solitons. A complete Riemannian manifold
(M^{n},g)
is a gradient Ricci soliton if there exists a smooth function
f
on
M
satisfying the equation

\rm Ric+\nabla\nabla f=\mu g,

(1.1)

where
\mu
is
\frac{1}{2}
, or
0
, or
-\frac{1}{2}
. It is called a gradient shrinking Ricci soliton if
\mu=\frac{1}{2}
, or a gradient steady Ricci soliton if
\mu=0
, or a gradient expanding Ricci soliton if
\mu=-\frac{1}{2}
. Gradient Ricci solitons could be seem as natural generalizations of Einstein manifolds, since when
f
is a constant function, the gradient Ricci soliton is simply an Einstein manifold. On the other hand, gradient Ricci solitons are also self-similar solutions to Hamilton’s Ricci flow and play a crucial role in the study of formation of singularities in the Ricci flow (see
[
1
]
).

It is well known that gradient shrinking or steady Ricci solitons share many properties with complete manifolds with nonnegative Ricci curvature. Therefore it is very natural to ask whether the above mentioned Liouville type theorems for harmonic functions due to Yau on complete manifold with nonnegative Ricci curvature still hold on shrinking or steady Ricci solitons. In fact much effort has been made along this direction in the past decade.

Munteanu and Wang
[
19
]
showed that any bounded holomorphic function on shrinking Kähler-Ricci solitons must be constant. Mai and Ou
[
16
]
proved that any bounded harmonic function on gradient shrinking Ricci solitons with constant scalar curvature must be constant. Note that though we already have these advances, the following problem remains a challenge.

Problem 1
. Is any positive harmonic function on gradient shrinking Ricci solitons a constant function?

There were also several results on
L^{p}
type Liouville theorems for harmonic functions on gradient shrinking or steady Ricci solitons. Munteanu and Sesum
[
17
]
proved that a harmonic function
u
on a gradient shrinking Kähler-Ricci soliton with
\nabla u\in L^{2}
is a constant function. In the same paper they proved that a harmonic function
u
on a gradient steady Ricci soliton with
\nabla u\in L^{2}
is a constant function. Dung, Le Hai and Thanh
[
9
]
proved that a harmonic function
u
on a gradient steady Ricci soliton
(M^{n},g)
with
\nabla u\in L^{p}(2<p<\frac{4n}{2n-1})
is a constant function, if
(M,g)
satisfies the Euclidean volume growth condition. Inspired by Yau’s
L^{p}
Liouville type theorem for harmonic functions on manifolds with nonnegative Ricci curvature and these results, it is very natural to study the following problem.

Problem 2
. Is any harmonic function
u
with
\nabla u\in L^{p}(p>1)
on gradient shrinking or steady Ricci solitons a constant function?

For gradient shrinking Kähler-Ricci solitons we answer Problem 2 partially in the affirmative, which extends Munteanu and Sesum’s result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a gradient shrinking Kähler-Ricci soliton. If
u
is a pluriharmonic function with
\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{p}<\infty(1<p\leq 2)
, then
u
is a constant function.

For gradient steady Kähler-Ricci solitons we answer Problem 2 partially in the affirmative too.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(M,g)
be a gradient steady Kähler-Ricci soliton. If
u
is a pluriharmonic function with
\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{p}<\infty(1<p\leq 2)
, then
u
is a constant function.

We remark that Liouville type theorems for
f
-harmonic functions on gradient shrinking Ricci solitons were studied by Ge and Zhang
[
11
]
and Wu and Wu
[
22
]
and others, which are relatively simpler and complete.

At last, we have the following result.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(M,g)
be a gradient Kähler-Ricci soliton with potential function
f
. Then if
f
is a harmonic function on
M
with
\int_{M}|\nabla f|^{p}<\infty(p>1)
,
(M,g)
is a Kähler Einstein manifold.

Organization.
Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.3
are proved in section 2, and Theorem
1.2
is proved in section 3. In the appendix we discuss more
L^{p}
Liouville type theorems for harmonic or pluriharmonic functions on gradient shrinking or expanding Ricci solitons.

(2.6)

Remark 2.3
.

We can see that in the proof of Theorem
2.1
, the shrinking Ricci soliton equation was not essentially used. Assume that
Vol(M)=\infty.
To prove
F=0
, we only need
f_{\alpha\beta}=f_{\bar{\alpha}\bar{\beta}}=0
,
|\nabla f|
growth linearly and
\nabla f
has an interior zero point. While at the last step, we used a
L^{p}(p>1)
Liouville type theorem for
\Delta_{f}|\nabla u|\geq 0
, where we need deduce that
\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{p}e^{-f}<\infty
from the assumption
\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{p}<\infty
, which holds if
f
has a lower bound.

From Remark
2.3
we have the following
L^{p}
Liouville type theorem for pluriharmonic functions on smooth Kähler measure metric spaces.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
(M,g,f)
be a smooth Kähler measure metric space such that
Vol(M)=\infty
,
Ric_{f}=Ric+\nabla\nabla f\geq 0
and
f
satisfies the following conditions:

(a)
f_{\alpha\beta}=f_{\bar{\alpha}\bar{\beta}}=0
;

(b)
|\nabla f(x)|\leq Cr(x)+o(r(x))
when
r(x)
is large enough, and
\nabla f
has an interior zero point;

(c)
f
is bounded from below.

Then if
u
is a pluriharmonic function on
M
with
\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{p}<\infty
for some constant
1<p\leq 2
,
u
is a constant function.

3
Gradient steady Kähler-Ricci solitons

Let
(M,g)
be a complete noncompact gradient steady Kähler-Ricci soliton, that is

R_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}+f_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}=0,

for a smooth function
f
on
M
satisfying

f_{\alpha\beta}=f_{\bar{\alpha}\bar{\beta}}=0.

Hamilton
[
12
]
showed that

R+|\nabla f|^{2}=\lambda,

for some constant
\lambda>0
. Since
R\geq 0
(see
[
4
]
), we see that
|\nabla f|\leq\sqrt{\lambda}
. In this section we will prove the following theorem.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a gradient steady Kähler-Ricci soliton, and assume that
u
is a pluriharmonic function on
M
with
\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{p}<\infty
for some constant
1<p\leq 2
, then
u
is a constant function.

Proof.

We only need to prove case of
1<p<2
. Define

F:=\langle\nabla f,\nabla u\rangle=\frac{1}{2}(u_{\alpha}f_{\bar{\alpha}}+u_{\bar{\alpha}}f_{\alpha}).

We show that
F=0
. Since
u
is a pluriharmonic function, we can see that
\Delta F=0
, i.e.
F
is a harmonic function on
M
. Then similarly with the proof of (
2.7
) we can obtain

\displaystyle\int_{M}\phi^{2}(F^{2}+\epsilon)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}|\nabla F|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(\frac{2}{p-1})^{2}\int_{M}|F|^{p}|\nabla\phi|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{r^{2}}\int_{B_{p}(2r)\setminus B_{p}(r)}|\nabla u|^{p}

where we used

|F|\leq|\nabla f||\nabla u|\leq\sqrt{\lambda}|\nabla u|.

Then by letting
r\to\infty
we have
\nabla F=0
, which yields that
F
is a constant function on
M
. Assume that
F=c
. Then by

\displaystyle|c|=|F|\leq|\nabla f||\nabla u|\leq\sqrt{\lambda}|\nabla u|,

(3.1)

we get
|\nabla u|\geq\frac{|c|}{\sqrt{\lambda}}
. Therefore

, since
\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{p}<\infty
and

has infinite volume
[
17
]
.

Let
\alpha<0
be a constant to be determined later. Then

\displaystyle\int_{M}Ric(\nabla u,\nabla u)\phi^{2}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}

(3.2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}R_{ij}u_{i}u_{j}\phi^{2}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}=-\int_{M}f_{ij}u_{i}u_{j}\phi^{2}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}f_{i}u_{ij}u_{j}\phi^{2}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}+\int_{M}f_{i}u_{i}u_{j}(\phi^{2})_{j}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\alpha\int_{M}f_{i}u_{i}u_{j}\phi^{2}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha-1}(|\nabla u|^{2})_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}f_{i}u_{ij}u_{j}\phi^{2}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha},

where in the last equality we used

f_{i}u_{i}=\langle\nabla f,\nabla u\rangle=F=0.

Note that

\displaystyle\int_{M}f_{i}u_{ij}u_{j}\phi^{2}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{M}\Delta fu_{j}u_{j}\phi^{2}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}-\int_{M}f_{i}u_{j}u_{ij}\phi^{2}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\int_{M}f_{i}u_{j}u_{j}(\phi^{2})_{i}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}-2\alpha\int_{M}f_{i}u_{j}u_{j}\phi^{2}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha-1}u_{ki}u_{k}.

Then by
\Delta f=-R
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{M}f_{i}u_{ij}u_{j}\phi^{2}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}

(3.3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}Ru_{j}u_{j}\phi^{2}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}f_{i}u_{j}u_{j}(\phi^{2})_{i}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}-\alpha\int_{M}f_{i}u_{j}u_{j}\phi^{2}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha-1}u_{ki}u_{k}.

Plug this in formula (
3.2
) we obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{M}Ric(\nabla u,\nabla u)\phi^{2}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}

(3.4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}Ru_{j}u_{j}\phi^{2}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}f_{i}u_{j}u_{j}(\phi^{2})_{i}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}-\alpha\int_{M}f_{i}u_{j}u_{j}\phi^{2}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha-1}u_{ki}u_{k}.

Recall that we have the following Bochner formula

\displaystyle\Delta|\nabla u|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2Ric(\nabla u,\nabla u)+2|u_{ij}|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 2Ric(\nabla u,\nabla u)+2|\nabla|\nabla u||^{2},

where we used the Kato inequality

|u_{ij}|^{2}\geq|\nabla|\nabla u||^{2}.

Then we have

\displaystyle\int_{M}\Delta|\nabla u|^{2}\phi^{2}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 2\int_{M}Ric(\nabla u,\nabla u)\phi^{2}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}+2\int_{M}|\nabla|\nabla u||^{2}\phi^{2}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}.

Plug this inequality into (
3.4
) we get

\displaystyle 2\int_{M}|\nabla|\nabla u||^{2}\phi^{2}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}+\int_{M}Ru_{j}u_{j}\phi^{2}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{M}f_{i}u_{j}u_{j}(\phi^{2})_{i}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}+2\alpha\int_{M}f_{i}u_{j}u_{j}\phi^{2}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha-1}u_{ki}u_{k}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{M}\Delta|\nabla u|^{2}\phi^{2}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{M}f_{i}u_{j}u_{j}(\phi^{2})_{i}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}+2\alpha\int_{M}f_{i}u_{j}u_{j}\phi^{2}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha-1}u_{ki}u_{k}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\int_{M}\nabla|\nabla u|^{2}\nabla\phi^{2}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}-\alpha\int_{M}\nabla|\nabla u|^{2}\nabla|\nabla u|^{2}\phi^{2}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}f_{i}u_{j}u_{j}(\phi^{2})_{i}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}+2\alpha\int_{M}f_{i}u_{j}u_{j}\phi^{2}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha-1}u_{ki}u_{k}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle 4\int_{M}\nabla|\nabla u||\nabla u|\nabla\phi\phi(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}-4\alpha\int_{M}|\nabla|\nabla u||^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}\phi^{2}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha-1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{M}f_{i}u_{j}u_{j}(\phi^{2})_{i}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}+2\alpha\int_{M}f_{i}u_{j}u_{j}\phi^{2}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha-1}u_{ki}u_{k}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle 4\int_{M}\nabla|\nabla u||\nabla u|\nabla\phi\phi(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}-4\alpha\int_{M}|\nabla|\nabla u||^{2}\phi^{2}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}.

Let
\alpha=\frac{p-2}{2}(<0)
. Then the above inequality implies that

\displaystyle 2(p-1)\int_{M}|\nabla|\nabla u||^{2}\phi^{2}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}+\int_{M}Ru_{j}u_{j}\phi^{2}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{M}f_{i}u_{j}u_{j}(\phi^{2})_{i}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}+2\alpha\int_{M}f_{i}u_{j}u_{j}\phi^{2}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha-1}u_{ki}u_{k}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle 4\int_{M}\nabla|\nabla u||\nabla u|\nabla\phi\phi(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{M}f_{i}u_{j}u_{j}(\phi^{2})_{i}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}+2\alpha\int_{M}f_{i}u_{j}u_{j}\phi^{2}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha-1}u_{ki}u_{k}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle(p-1)\int_{M}|\nabla|\nabla u||^{2}\phi^{2}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}-\frac{4}{p-1}\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}|\nabla\phi|^{2}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}.

Therefore

\displaystyle(p-1)\int_{M}|\nabla|\nabla u||^{2}\phi^{2}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}+\int_{M}Ru_{j}u_{j}\phi^{2}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}

(3.5)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{M}f_{i}u_{j}u_{j}(\phi^{2})_{i}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}+2\alpha\int_{M}f_{i}u_{j}u_{j}\phi^{2}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha-1}u_{ki}u_{k}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{4}{p-1}\int_{M}\nabla|\nabla u||\nabla u|\nabla\phi\phi(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{M}f_{i}u_{j}u_{j}(\phi^{2})_{i}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}+2\alpha\int_{M}f_{i}u_{j}u_{j}\phi^{2}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha-1}u_{ki}u_{k}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{4}{p-1}\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}|\nabla\phi|^{2}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}.

Note that

\displaystyle|\int_{M}f_{i}u_{j}u_{j}(\phi^{2})_{i}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\int_{M}|\nabla f||\nabla\phi||\nabla u|^{p}

(3.6)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2C\sqrt{\lambda}}{r}\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{p}\to 0\ as\ r\to\infty,

\displaystyle\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}|\nabla\phi|^{2}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int|\nabla u|^{p}|\nabla\phi|^{2}

(3.7)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{r^{2}}\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{p}\to 0\ as\ r\to\infty.

Denote by

I=\lim_{r\to\infty}\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\int_{M}f_{i}u_{j}u_{j}\phi^{2}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha-1}u_{ki}u_{k}.

Then from (
3.3
) we get

pI=\int_{M}R|\nabla u|^{p}\geq 0,

where we used the fact that
R\geq 0
(see
[
4
]
). Therefore
I\geq 0
, which together with (
3.5
)-(
3.7
) imply that

\displaystyle(p-1)\int_{M}|\nabla|\nabla u||^{2}|\nabla u|^{p-2}+\int_{M}R|\nabla u|^{p}\leq 2\alpha I\leq 0,

where we used
2\alpha=p-2<0
.
Therefore we have
|\nabla u|=c
, which must be zero since
\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{p}<\infty
and
M
has infinite volume.
\hfill\Box

4
Appendix

In this appendix we discuss more
L^{p}
Liouville type theorems for harmonic or pluriharmonic functions on gradient shrinking or expanding Ricci solitons. To our knowledge, till now no such kind of results were known except for gradient shrinking Kähler-Ricci solitons.

For gradient shrinking Ricci solitons we have

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a gradient shrinking Ricci soliton such that
R\geq\frac{n-2}{2}
. Assume that
u
is a harmonic function on
M
with
\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}<\infty
, then
u
is a constant function.

Proof.

We follow argument used by Munteanu and Sesum in their proof of Theorem 4.1 in
[
17
]
. We assume that
(M,g)
is complete noncompact. For a cut-off function
\phi
on
M
it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{M}Ric(\nabla u,\nabla u)\phi^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{M}f_{ij}u_{i}u_{j}\phi^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}\phi^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}u_{ij}f_{i}u_{j}\phi^{2}+\int_{M}f_{i}u_{i}u_{j}(\phi^{2})_{j}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}\phi^{2}.

Note that by integration by parts we have

\displaystyle\int_{M}u_{ij}f_{i}u_{j}\phi^{2}=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}\Delta f|\nabla u|^{2}\phi^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}\langle\nabla f,\nabla\phi^{2}\rangle.

Combing the above two formulas and using

-\Delta f=R-\frac{n}{2},

we get

\displaystyle\int_{M}Ric(\nabla u,\nabla u)\phi^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}(R-\frac{n-2}{2})|\nabla u|^{2}\phi^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}\langle\nabla f,\nabla\phi^{2}\rangle

(4.1)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{M}\langle\nabla f,\nabla u\rangle\langle\nabla u,\nabla\phi^{2}\rangle.

Recall that we have the following Bochner formula

\Delta|\nabla u|^{2}=2Ric(\nabla u,\nabla u)+2|u_{ij}|^{2}\geq 2Ric(\nabla u,\nabla u)+2|\nabla|\nabla u||^{2}.

Multiplying this inequality by
\phi^{2}
, using (
4.1
), and integrating by parts we have

\displaystyle 2\int_{M}|\nabla|\nabla u||^{2}\phi^{2}+\int_{M}(R-\frac{n-2}{2})|\nabla u|^{2}\phi^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}\langle\nabla f,\nabla\phi^{2}\rangle-2\int_{M}\langle\nabla f,\nabla u\rangle\langle\nabla u,\nabla\phi^{2}\rangle+\int_{M}\phi^{2}\Delta|\nabla u|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}\langle\nabla f,\nabla\phi^{2}\rangle-2\int_{M}\langle\nabla f,\nabla u\rangle\langle\nabla u,\nabla\phi^{2}\rangle

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{M}|\nabla|\nabla u||^{2}\phi^{2}+4\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}|\nabla\phi|^{2}.

Therefore we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{M}|\nabla|\nabla u||^{2}\phi^{2}+\int_{M}(R-\frac{n-2}{2})|\nabla u|^{2}\phi^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}\langle\nabla f,\nabla\phi^{2}\rangle-2\int_{M}\langle\nabla f,\nabla u\rangle\langle\nabla u,\nabla\phi^{2}\rangle+4\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}|\nabla\phi|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{r^{2}}\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}+C\int_{B_{p}(2r)\setminus B_{p}(r)}|\nabla u|^{2},

where in the last inequality we used (
2.4
). Let
r\to\infty
, since
R\geq\frac{n-2}{2}
, we get that
|\nabla u|=c
. We see that
c
must be zero, i.e.
u
is a constant function, since
\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}<\infty
and
M
has infinite volume.
\hfill\Box

Next we discuss
L^{p}
Liouville type theorems for harmonic or pluriharmonic functions on gradient expanding Ricci solitons. Before this, we collect some basic facts of gradient expanding Ricci solitons which will be used in the following. Munteanu and Wang
[
18
]
proved that

\inf_{B_{p}(r)}f\sim-\frac{r^{2}}{4},

as
r(x)
is large enough, then by

R+|\nabla f|^{2}+f=0

after a normalization of
f
(see
[
10
]
), we have

\displaystyle|\nabla f|^{2}=-R-f\leq\frac{n}{2}-f\sim\frac{r^{2}}{4},

where we used
R\geq-\frac{n}{2}
(see
[
21
]
[
25
]
). Therefore we have

\displaystyle|\nabla f|\leq\frac{r}{2}+o(r).

(4.2)

Carrillo and Ni
[
3
]
proved that for any
p\in M
and
r\geq r_{0}
, if
R\geq-\sigma
, where
\sigma\geq 0
, we have the following monotonicity formula for the volume of gradient expanding Ricci solitons:

\displaystyle|B_{p}(r)|\geq|B_{p}(r_{0})|(\frac{r+\tau}{r_{0}+\tau})^{n-2\sigma},

(4.3)

where
\tau:=\sqrt{\sigma-f(p)}
.

We have

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
(M,g)
be a gradient expanding Ricci soliton such that
R\geq-\frac{n-2}{2}
. Assume that
u
is a harmonic function on
M
with
\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}<\infty
, then
u
is a constant function.

Proof.

We follow argument used by Munteanu and Sesum in their proof of Theorem 4.1 in
[
17
]
. We assume that
(M,g)
is compete noncompact. For a cut-off function
\phi
on
M
it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{M}Ric(\nabla u,\nabla u)\phi^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{M}f_{ij}u_{i}u_{j}\phi^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}\phi^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}u_{ij}f_{i}u_{j}\phi^{2}+\int_{M}f_{i}u_{i}u_{j}(\phi^{2})_{j}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}\phi^{2}.

Note that by integration by parts we have

\displaystyle\int_{M}u_{ij}f_{i}u_{j}\phi^{2}=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}\Delta f|\nabla u|^{2}\phi^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}\langle\nabla f,\nabla\phi^{2}\rangle.

Combing the above two formulas and using
-\Delta f=R+\frac{n}{2}
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{M}Ric(\nabla u,\nabla u)\phi^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}(R+\frac{n-2}{2})|\nabla u|^{2}\phi^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}\langle\nabla f,\nabla\phi^{2}\rangle

(4.4)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{M}\langle\nabla f,\nabla u\rangle\langle\nabla u,\nabla\phi^{2}\rangle.

Recall that we have the following Bochner formula

\Delta|\nabla u|^{2}=2Ric(\nabla u,\nabla u)+2|u_{ij}|^{2}\geq 2Ric(\nabla u,\nabla u)+2|\nabla|\nabla u||^{2}.

Multiplying this inequality by
\phi^{2}
, using (
4.1
), and integrating by parts we have

\displaystyle 2\int_{M}|\nabla|\nabla u||^{2}\phi^{2}+\int_{M}(R+\frac{n-2}{2})|\nabla u|^{2}\phi^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}\langle\nabla f,\nabla\phi^{2}\rangle-2\int_{M}\langle\nabla f,\nabla u\rangle\langle\nabla u,\nabla\phi^{2}\rangle+\int_{M}\phi^{2}\Delta|\nabla u|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}\langle\nabla f,\nabla\phi^{2}\rangle-2\int_{M}\langle\nabla f,\nabla u\rangle\langle\nabla u,\nabla\phi^{2}\rangle

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{M}|\nabla|\nabla u||^{2}\phi^{2}+4\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}|\nabla\phi|^{2}.

Therefore we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{M}|\nabla|\nabla u||^{2}\phi^{2}+\int_{M}(R+\frac{n-2}{2})|\nabla u|^{2}\phi^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}\langle\nabla f,\nabla\phi^{2}\rangle-2\int_{M}\langle\nabla f,\nabla u\rangle\langle\nabla u,\nabla\phi^{2}\rangle+4\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}|\nabla\phi|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{r^{2}}\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}+C\int_{B_{p}(2r)\setminus B_{p}(r)}|\nabla u|^{2},

where in the last inequality we used (
4.2
). Let
r\to\infty
, since
R\geq-\frac{n-2}{2}
, we get that
|\nabla u|=c
. Since
R\geq-\frac{n-2}{2}
, we see from (
4.3
) that

|B_{p}(r)|\geq|B_{p}(r_{0})|(\frac{r+\tau}{r_{0}+\tau})^{2},

which implies that
M
has infinite volume. Then we have that
c
must be zero, i.e.
u
is a constant function, since
\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}<\infty
.
\hfill\Box

Furthermore for gradient expanding Kähler-Ricci solitons we have

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
(M,g)
be a gradient expanding Kähler-Ricci soliton. Assume that
u
is a pluriharmonic function on
M
,
R\geq-\frac{n-2}{2}
,
\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{p}<\infty
for some constant
1<p\leq 2
, and
\nabla f(o)=0
at some point
o\in M
, then
u
is a constant function.

Remark 4.4
.

A point
o\in M
satisfying
\nabla f(o)=0
is called an equilibrium point of
(M,g)
, while existence of an equilibrium point of
(M,g)
plays an important role in rigidity theorems due to Deng and Zhu in
[
8
]
for complete non-compact gradient expanding or steady Kähler-Ricci solitons.

Proof.

Since the argument is quite similar with that we have used in the proof of Theorem
3.1
, we only give a sketch of proofs here. We only need assume that
1<p<2
. From
u
is pluriharmonic we see that
\Delta F=0
, where
F=\langle\nabla f,\nabla u\rangle
. Then we can obtain

\displaystyle\int_{M}\phi^{2}(F^{2}+\epsilon)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}|\nabla F|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(\frac{2}{p-1})^{2}\int_{M}|F|^{p}|\nabla\phi|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{r^{2-p}}\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{p}

where we used (
4.2
) to get

|F|\leq|\nabla f||\nabla u|\leq Cr|\nabla u|.

Then by letting
r\to\infty
we have
\nabla F=0
, which yields that
F
is a constant function on
M
. Then since
\nabla f(o)=0
at some point
o\in M
, we see that
F=0
on
M
.

Let
\alpha<0
be a constant to be determined later. Then

\displaystyle\int_{M}Ric(\nabla u,\nabla u)\phi^{2}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}

(4.5)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}f_{i}u_{ij}u_{j}\phi^{2}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}\phi^{2},

where we used

f_{i}u_{i}=\langle\nabla f,\nabla u\rangle=F=0.

Note that

\displaystyle\int_{M}f_{i}u_{ij}u_{j}\phi^{2}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{M}\Delta fu_{j}u_{j}\phi^{2}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}-\int_{M}f_{i}u_{j}u_{ij}\phi^{2}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\int_{M}f_{i}u_{j}u_{j}(\phi^{2})_{i}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}-2\alpha\int_{M}f_{i}u_{j}u_{j}\phi^{2}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha-1}u_{ki}u_{k}.

Then by
\Delta f=-R-\frac{n}{2}
we get

\displaystyle\int_{M}Ric(\nabla u,\nabla u)\phi^{2}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}

(4.6)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}(R+\frac{n}{2}-1)u_{j}u_{j}\phi^{2}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}f_{i}u_{j}u_{j}(\phi^{2})_{i}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}-\alpha\int_{M}f_{i}u_{j}u_{j}\phi^{2}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha-1}u_{ki}u_{k}.

Recall that we have the following Bochner formula

\displaystyle\Delta|\nabla u|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2Ric(\nabla u,\nabla u)+2|u_{ij}|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 2Ric(\nabla u,\nabla u)+2|\nabla|\nabla u||^{2}.

Then we have

\displaystyle\int_{M}\Delta|\nabla u|^{2}\phi^{2}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 2\int_{M}Ric(\nabla u,\nabla u)\phi^{2}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}+2\int_{M}|\nabla|\nabla u||^{2}\phi^{2}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}.

Plug this inequality into (
4.6
) we get

\displaystyle 2\int_{M}|\nabla|\nabla u||^{2}\phi^{2}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}+\int_{M}(R+\frac{n}{2}-1)u_{j}u_{j}\phi^{2}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{M}f_{i}u_{j}u_{j}(\phi^{2})_{i}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}+2\alpha\int_{M}f_{i}u_{j}u_{j}\phi^{2}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha-1}u_{ki}u_{k}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle 4\int_{M}\nabla|\nabla u||\nabla u|\nabla\phi\phi(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}-4\alpha\int_{M}|\nabla|\nabla u||^{2}\phi^{2}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}.

Let
\alpha=\frac{p-2}{2}(<0)
. Then from the above inequality we have

\displaystyle(p-1)\int_{M}|\nabla|\nabla u||^{2}\phi^{2}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}+\int_{M}(R+\frac{n}{2}-1)u_{j}u_{j}\phi^{2}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}

(4.7)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{M}f_{i}u_{j}u_{j}(\phi^{2})_{i}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}+2\alpha\int_{M}f_{i}u_{j}u_{j}\phi^{2}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha-1}u_{ki}u_{k}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{4}{p-1}\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}|\nabla\phi|^{2}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}.

Note that

\displaystyle|\int_{M}f_{i}u_{j}u_{j}(\phi^{2})_{i}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}|

(4.8)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\int_{M}|\nabla f||\nabla\phi||\nabla u|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{B_{p}(2r)\setminus B_{p}(r)}|\nabla u|^{p}\to 0\ as\ r\to\infty,

and

\displaystyle\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}|\nabla\phi|^{2}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha}

(4.9)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int|\nabla u|^{p}|\nabla\phi|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{r^{2}}\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{p}\to 0\ as\ r\to\infty.

Denote by

I=\lim_{r\to\infty}\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\int_{M}f_{i}u_{j}u_{j}\phi^{2}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\alpha-1}u_{ki}u_{k}.

Similarly with the proof of Theorem
3.1
we get

pI=\int_{M}(R+\frac{n}{2})|\nabla u|^{p}\geq 0,

where we used the fact that
R\geq-\frac{n}{2}
. Therefore
I\geq 0
, which together with (
4.7
)-(
4.9
) imply that

\displaystyle(p-1)\int_{M}|\nabla|\nabla u||^{2}|\nabla u|^{p-2}+\int_{M}(R+\frac{n}{2}-1)|\nabla u|^{p}\leq 2\alpha I\leq 0,

where we used
2\alpha=p-2<0
. Therefore we have
|\nabla u|=c
, which must be zero since
\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{p}<\infty
and
M
has infinite volume.
\hfill\Box

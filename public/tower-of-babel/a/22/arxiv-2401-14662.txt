Liouville Irregular States of Half-Integer Ranks
Ryo Hamachika, Tomoki Nakanishi, Takahiro Nishinaka, Shou Tanigawa

Abstract
We conjecture a set of differential equations that characterizes the Liouville irregular states of half-integer ranks, which extends the generalized AGT correspondence to all the ( A 1 , A even ) (A_{1},A_{\text{even}}) and ( A 1 , D odd ) (A_{1},D_{\text{odd}}) types Argyres-Douglas theories. For lower half-integer ranks, our conjecture is verified by deriving it as a suitable limit of a similar set of differential equations for integer ranks. This limit is interpreted as the 2D counterpart of a 4D RG-flow from ( A 1 , D 2 ​ n ) (A_{1},D_{2n}) to ( A 1 , D 2 ​ n − 1 ) (A_{1},D_{2n-1}) . For rank 3 / 2 3/2 , we solve the conjectured differential equations and find a power series expression for the irregular state | I ( 3 / 2 ) ⟩ |I^{(3/2)}\rangle . For rank 5 / 2 5/2 , our conjecture is consistent with the differential equations recently discovered by H. Poghosyan and R. Poghossian.

2
Irregular states of integer ranks

2.1
Recursive construction of irregular states

In this section, we give a brief review of the irregular states of integer ranks
and their connection to the Nekrasov partition function of
Argyres-Douglas theories, following
[
12
,
25
]
.

As mentioned in Sec. 1, an irregular state of rank
n\in\mathbb{N}
is
a state
|I^{(n)}\rangle
satisfying the differential equations
(
1
), which we reproduce here for convenience:

\displaystyle L_{k}|I^{(n)}\rangle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}0\quad\text{for}\quad 2n<k\\[5.69054pt]
\Lambda_{k}|I^{(n)}\rangle\quad\text{for}\quad n\leq k\leq 2n\\[5.69054pt]
\left(\Lambda_{k}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{n-k}\ell\,c_{\ell+k}\frac{\partial}{\partial c_{\ell}}\right)|I^{(n)}\rangle\quad\text{for}\quad 0\leq k\leq n-1\end{array}\right.~,

where
\Lambda_{0},\cdots,\Lambda_{2n}
are determined by
c_{0},\cdots,c_{n}
as

\displaystyle\Lambda_{k}\equiv\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}-\sum_{i=k-n}^{n}c_{i}c_{k-i}\quad\text{for}\quad n<k\leq 2n\\[5.69054pt]
-\sum_{i=0}^{k}c_{i}c_{k-i}+(k+1)Qc_{k}\quad\text{for}\quad k\leq n\end{array}\right.~.

Note that these equations contain
(n+1)
parameters,

c_{0}
and
\boldsymbol{c}=(c_{1},\,\cdots,\,c_{n})
. In addition to them,
a solution to these equations depends on
n
extra parameters
corresponding to the “initial conditions” for the
n
differential
equations involved in (
2.1
). We denote these extra parameters by
\boldsymbol{\beta}=(\beta_{0},\cdots,\beta_{n-1})
as in
[
25
]

It was conjectured in

[
12
]
that the irregular state
|I^{(n)}\rangle
is
uniquely fixed by specifying these
(2n+1)
parameters,
c_{0}
,
\boldsymbol{c}
and

\boldsymbol{\beta}
, up to a constant prefactor.
We therefore write

|I^{(n)}\rangle
as

|I^{(n)}(c_{0},\boldsymbol{c};\boldsymbol{\beta})\rangle
.
To be more precise, it was
conjectured in
[
12
]
that the rank-
n
irregular state

|I^{(n)}\rangle
is expanded uniquely as

\displaystyle|I^{(n)}(c_{0},\boldsymbol{c};\boldsymbol{\beta})\rangle=\mathcal{M}(c_{0},\boldsymbol{c},\boldsymbol{\beta})\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(c_{n})^{k}|I^{(n-1)}_{k}(c_{0},\widetilde{\boldsymbol{c}};\boldsymbol{\beta})\rangle~,

(2.6)

where
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{c}}\equiv(c_{1},\cdots,c_{n-1})
.
The states
|I^{(n-1)}_{k}(c_{0},\widetilde{\boldsymbol{c}},\boldsymbol{\beta})\rangle
are linear
combinations of states of the form

\displaystyle(L_{-y_{1}})^{m_{1}}\cdots(L_{-y_{k}})^{m_{k}}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial c_{1}}\right)^{\ell_{1}}\cdots\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial c_{n-1}}\right)^{\ell_{n-1}}|I^{(n-1)}(\beta_{n-1},\widetilde{\boldsymbol{c}};\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\beta}})\rangle~,

(2.7)

where

\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\equiv(\beta_{0},\cdots\beta_{n-2})
,

y_{1}>\cdots>y_{k}>0
,
m_{i}
are positive integers, and
\ell_{j}
are non-negative integers. Such
linear combinations are called “generalized descendants” of the
rank-
(n-1)
state

|I^{(n-1)}(\beta_{n-1},\widetilde{\boldsymbol{c}};\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\beta}})\rangle
.

Note
that, since
|I^{(n-1)}(\beta_{n-1},\widetilde{\boldsymbol{c}};\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\beta}})\rangle
is independent of
c_{n}
and

c_{0}
,
identifying all
|I^{(n-1)}_{k}\rangle
in the expansion (
2.6
) completely
characterizes the dependence of
|I^{(n)}(c_{0},\boldsymbol{c};\boldsymbol{\beta})\rangle

on
c_{n}
and
c_{0}
. Similarly,

|I^{(n-1)}(\beta_{n-1},\widetilde{\boldsymbol{c}};\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\beta}})\rangle

is expected to be expanded in terms of generalized descendants of a
rank-
(n-2)
state. This expansion completely characterizes the dependence
of

|I^{(n-1)}(\beta_{n-1},\widetilde{\boldsymbol{c}};\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\beta}})\rangle

on
c_{n-1}
and
\beta_{n-1}
. By repeating this procedure, the rank-
n

state is ultimately constructed from a rank-zero state

|I^{(0)}(\beta_{0})\rangle
, which is identified as a Virasoro primary
state
|\Delta_{\beta_{0}}\rangle
with the highest weight

\Delta_{\beta_{0}}\equiv\beta_{0}(Q-\beta_{0})
. This implies
that
\beta_{0}
specifies the highest weight of the Verma module to which

|I^{(n)}(c_{0},\boldsymbol{c};\boldsymbol{\beta})\rangle
belongs.

2.2
Parameter identification between 4D and 2D

Let us now review how the
(2n+1)
parameters of the irregular state

|I^{(n)}(c_{0},\boldsymbol{c};\boldsymbol{\beta})\rangle
are related to the parameters
of the corresponding AD theory in four dimensions.

To that end, we focus on the
(A_{1},D_{2n})
theory whose Seiberg-Witten
(SW) curve is given by

\displaystyle x^{2}=z^{2n-2}+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}d_{i}z^{2n-2-i}+Mz^{n-2}+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}u_{i}z^{n-2-i}+\frac{m^{2}}{z^{2}}~,

(2.8)

where
d_{1},\cdots,d_{n-2}
and
d_{n-1}
are relevant couplings,
M
and

m
are mass parameters, and
u_{1},\cdots,u_{n-2}
and
u_{n-1}
are the
vacuum expectation values (VEVs) of Coulomb branch operators. The
scaling dimensions of these parameters are evaluated as

[d_{i}]=i/n,\,[u_{i}]=1+i/n
and
[M]=[m]=1
. This
follows from the fact that the SW 1-form
\lambda\equiv xdz
has scaling
dimension one.

The AGT correspondence implies that the partition function of this
theory is related to the inner product of a rank-
n
irregular state and
a primary state as

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{(A_{1},D_{2n})}=\langle\Delta|I^{(n)}(c_{0},\boldsymbol{c};\boldsymbol{\beta})\rangle~.

(2.9)

Note that, for the right-hand side to be non-vanishing,
|I^{(n)}\rangle

must be in the same Verma module as
|\Delta\rangle
. This fixes the
value of
\Delta
as

\displaystyle\Delta=\Delta_{\beta_{0}}~,

(2.10)

in terms of
\beta_{0}
.
The SW curve of
the four-dimensional theory is also written in terms of the irregular
state as
[
1
]

\displaystyle x^{2}=-\frac{\langle\Delta|T(z)|I^{(n)}\rangle}{\langle\Delta I^{(n)}\rangle}~,

(2.11)

where
T(z)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}L_{k}/z^{k+2}
is the 2D stress tensor.
From (
2.1
) and
\langle\Delta|L_{k}=0
for

k<0
, we see that this curve is expressed as
[
10
,
12
]

\displaystyle x^{2}=-\frac{\Lambda_{2n}}{z^{2n+2}}-\frac{\Lambda_{2n-1}}{z^{2n+1}}-\cdots-\frac{\Lambda_{n}}{z^{n+2}}-\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{v_{i}}{z^{n+2-i}}-\frac{\Delta}{z^{2}}~,

(2.12)

where

\displaystyle v_{i}\equiv\frac{\langle\Delta|L_{n-i}|I^{(n)}\rangle}{\langle\Delta|I^{(n)}\rangle}~,

(2.13)

for
i=1,\cdots,n-1
.
By changing variables as
z\to 1/z
and
x\to-z^{2}x
, we can rewrite
this curve into

\displaystyle x^{2}=-\Lambda_{2n}z^{2n-2}-\Lambda_{2n-1}z^{2n-3}-\cdots-\Lambda_{n}z^{n-2}-\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}v_{i}z^{n-2-i}-\frac{\Delta}{z^{2}}

(2.14)

without changing the expression for the SW 1-form
\lambda=xdz
. By
rescaling
z
and
x
further as
z\to(-\Lambda_{2n})^{-\frac{1}{2n}}z
and
x\to(-\Lambda_{2n})^{\frac{1}{2n}}x
,
this curve is further rewritten as

\displaystyle x^{2}=z^{2n-2}-\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{\Lambda_{2n-i}}{(-\Lambda_{2n})^{\frac{2n-i}{2n}}}z^{2n-2-i}-\frac{\Lambda_{n}}{(-\Lambda_{2n})^{\frac{n}{2n}}}z^{n-2}-\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{v_{i}}{(-\Lambda_{2n})^{\frac{n-i}{2n}}}z^{n-2-i}-\frac{\Delta}{z^{2}}~,

(2.15)

which is indeed of the same form as the
curve
(
2.8
) of the
(A_{1},D_{2n})
theory. Note that the above
changes of variables keep the SW 1-form
\lambda=xdz
invariant.

From the identification of (
2.8
) and (
2.15
), one can make a
precise identification of the 4D and 2D parameters:

\displaystyle d_{i}=-\frac{\Lambda_{2n-i}}{(-\Lambda_{2n})^{\frac{2n-i}{2n}}}~,\qquad u_{i}=-\frac{v_{i}}{(-\Lambda_{2n})^{\frac{n-i}{2n}}}~,\qquad M=-\frac{\Lambda_{n}}{\sqrt{-\Lambda_{2n}}}~,\qquad m=\sqrt{-\Delta}~.

(2.16)

Note that, since
\Lambda_{k}
are completely fixed by
c_{0},\cdots,c_{n}
, the
relevant couplings
d_{i}
(and a mass parameter) in four dimensions are related to the parameters that appear in
the differential equations (
2.1
). In contrast, the
VEVs of the Coulomb branch operators
u_{i}
in four dimensions are
translated into
v_{i}
and therefore related to the parameters

\beta_{1},\cdots,\beta_{n-1}
. These latter parameters do not appear in the
differential equations
(
2.1
), but are rather “initial
conditions” that we need to fix when solving the equations
(
2.1
).

The above interpretation is naturally understood from the viewpoint of
the class S construction of the
(A_{1},D_{2n})
theory. Indeed, the

(A_{1},D_{2n})
theory is realized by compactifying two M5-branes on
sphere with two punctures. One of these punctures is a regular
puncture, around which the behavior of the M5-branes is characterized by the parameter
\Delta
. The other puncture is
an irregular puncture, around which the M5-branes is characterized by

c_{0},\cdots,c_{n}
. Therefore, the 4D relevant couplings and mass
parameters are specified by the boundary conditions of the M5-branes
near the punctures.
This is natural since couplings and masses need to be
fixed when specifying the 4D theory by fixing the boundary behaviors of
the M5-branes.
In contrast,
the VEVs of the Coulomb
branch operators are
not
fixed just by specifying the 4D theory;
they are rather parameters specifying the vacuum of the 4D theory on the
Coulomb branch. Therefore, these VEVs are not fixed by the boundary
conditions of M5-branes, but are related to the moduli parameters of
BPS M5-branes that are now encoded in
\beta_{1},\cdots,\beta_{n-1}
.

Let us also mention that, while the SW curve has only
2n

parameters, there are
(2n+1)
parameters on the 2D side;
c_{0},\cdots,c_{n}
and
\boldsymbol{\beta}=(\beta_{0},\cdots,\beta_{n-1})
.
1
1

1

Recall here that
\Delta
is
already fixed by
\beta_{0}
as in (
2.10
).
This implies that one combination of the

(2n+1)
parameters on the 2D side is irrelevant on the 4D side. This
reflects the fact that the
(A_{1},D_{2n})
theory has conformal
invariance and therefore the dilatation preserves the SW curve
(
2.8
). Indeed, for
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
the following transformation preserves
the curve:

\displaystyle d_{i}\to\zeta^{[d_{i}]}d_{i}~,\qquad u_{i}\to\zeta^{[u_{i}]}u_{i}~,\qquad M\to\zeta M~,\qquad m\to\zeta m~,

(2.17)

up to the change of coordinates
z\to\zeta^{\frac{1}{n}}z
and
x\to\zeta^{1-\frac{1}{n}}x
. This conformal invariance then implies that
the coefficient of
z^{2n-2}
in (
2.14
) can be set to one as
in (
2.15
) by
a change of coordinates. As a result, the SW
curve in four dimensions depends only on
2n
combinations of the

(2n+1)
parameters in two dimensions.

(A_{1},D_{2n})

3.1
4D RG-flow

Such RG-flows are well-studied in the literature

[

,
30
,
31
]
. Indeed, turning on the relevant coupling of the lowest
dimension in
(A_{1},D_{2n})
triggers an RG-flow whose IR
end-point is
(A_{1},D_{2n-1})
.

The easiest way to see this is to look at
the
SW curve of the
(A_{1},D_{2n})
theory shown in (
2.8
).
In this curve, we make the relevant coupling
d_{1}

very large compared to the other parameters. This corresponds to the
RG-flow triggered by
d_{1}\neq 0
.
To describe the curve of the IR end-point of the RG-flow, we rescale the
coordinate as
x\to(d_{1})^{\frac{1}{2n-1}}x
and
z\to(d_{1})^{-\frac{1}{2n-1}}z
. Note that this
rescaling preserves the SW 1-form. We also define rescaled deformation
parameters as
\widetilde{d}_{i-1}\equiv(d_{1})^{-\frac{2n-i}{2n-1}}d_{i}
for

i\geq 2
,
\widetilde{d}_{n-1}\equiv(d_{1})^{-\frac{n}{2n-1}}M

Summary and discussions

In this paper, we have conjectured a set of differential equations that
the Liouville irregular state

of a general
half-integer rank
r
satisfies.
This extends the generalized AGT correspondence to all the

(A_{1},A_{\text{even}})
and
(A_{1},D_{\text{odd}})

to
(A_{1},D_{2n-1})

straightforward generalization of the ansatz used in

[

]
for
|I^{(5/2)}\rangle
. Inspired by these

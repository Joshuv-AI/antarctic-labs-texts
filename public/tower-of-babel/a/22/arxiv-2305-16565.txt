Normalized solutions to the biharmonic nonlinear Schrödinger equation with combined nonlinearities
Wenjing Chen, Zexi Wang

Abstract

Introduction and statement of main results

In this paper, we are concerned with the following biharmonic nonlinear Schrödinger equation with combined nonlinearities

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta^{2}u=\lambda u+\mu|u|^{q-2}u+|u|^{p-2}u,\quad\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{N}$},\\
\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2}dx=a^{2},\quad u\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\end{array}\right.\end{split}

(1.1)

where

The interest in studying (

[

24

The type of solution for (

looking for ground state solutions, i.e., solutions minimizing the associated energy functional among
all nontrivial solutions, and the associated energy is called ground state energy.

2.1
Several important lemmas

Lemma 2.1
.

[
9
]

For
N\geq 1
and
u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, let
u^{\sharp}
be the Fourier rearrangement of
u
defined by

u^{\sharp}:=\mathcal{F}^{-1}((\mathcal{F}u)^{*}),

where
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{F}^{-1}
denote the Fourier transform and the inverse Fourier transform respectively, and
f^{*}
stands for the Schwarz rearrangement of
f
. Then
u^{\sharp}
is radial,
\|u^{\sharp}\|_{2}=\|u\|_{2}
, and
\|\Delta u^{\sharp}\|_{2}\leq\|\Delta u\|_{2}
. Moreover, if
p\in 2\mathbb{N}
, we have

\|u^{\sharp}\|_{p}\geq\|u\|_{p}.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
N\geq 5
and
2<p<4^{*}
, then
\lim\limits_{p\rightarrow 4^{*}}B_{N,p}^{\frac{1}{p}}=S^{-\frac{1}{2}}
.

Proof.

For
u\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, by Lemma
1.2
, we have

\|u\|_{p}\leq B_{N,p}^{\frac{1}{p}}\|u\|_{2}^{1-\gamma_{p}}\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{\gamma_{p}}.

For
2<p<4^{*}
, since

|u|^{p}\leq|u|^{2}+|u|^{4^{*}},

using the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem, we obtain
\lim\limits_{p\rightarrow 4^{*}}\|u\|_{p}=\|u\|_{4^{*}}
. Noted that
\gamma_{p}\rightarrow 1
as
p\rightarrow 4^{*}
, thus

\|u\|_{4^{*}}\leq\liminf\limits_{p\rightarrow 4^{*}}B_{N,p}^{\frac{1}{p}}\|\Delta u\|_{2},

which implies that
S^{-\frac{1}{2}}\leq\liminf\limits_{p\rightarrow 4^{*}}B_{N,p}^{\frac{1}{p}}
. On the other hand, for
u\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, by Lemma
1.3
and Hölder inequality, we have

\|u\|_{p}\leq\|u\|_{2}^{1-\gamma_{p}}\|u\|_{4^{*}}^{\gamma_{p}}\leq S^{-\frac{\gamma_{p}}{2}}\|u\|_{2}^{1-\gamma_{p}}\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{\gamma_{p}},

so
S^{-\frac{\gamma_{p}}{2}}\geq B_{N,p}^{\frac{1}{p}}
, which means
S^{-\frac{1}{2}}\geq\limsup\limits_{p\rightarrow 4^{*}}B_{N,p}^{\frac{1}{p}}
.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\{u_{n}\}\subset H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
\{\tau_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{R}
, assume that
u_{n}\rightarrow u
in
H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, and
\tau_{n}\rightarrow\tau
in
\mathbb{R}
. Then
\tau_{n}*u_{n}\rightarrow\tau*u
in
H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

Proof.

First, we prove that
\tau_{n}*u_{n}\rightharpoonup\tau*u
in
H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Taking any
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, and for a compact set
K
containing the support of
\varphi(e^{-\tau_{n}}\cdot)
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
large enough, using the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{\frac{N}{2}\tau_{n}}u_{n}(e^{\tau_{n}}x)\varphi(x)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{K}e^{-\frac{N}{2}\tau_{n}}u_{n}(y)\varphi(e^{-\tau_{n}}y)dy

\displaystyle\rightarrow\int_{K}e^{-\frac{N}{2}\tau}u(y)\varphi(e^{-\tau}y)dy=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{\frac{N}{2}\tau}u(e^{\tau}x)\varphi(x)dx,\quad\text{as $n\rightarrow\infty$}.

Similarly, we can show that for any
i=1,2,\ldots,N
and
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\varphi\frac{\partial(e^{\frac{N}{2}\tau_{n}}u_{n}(e^{\tau_{n}}x))}{\partial{x_{i}}}dx\rightarrow\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\varphi\frac{\partial(e^{\frac{N}{2}\tau}u(e^{\tau}x))}{\partial{x_{i}}}dx,\quad\text{as $n\rightarrow\infty$},

and

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\varphi\frac{\partial^{2}(e^{\frac{N}{2}\tau_{n}}u_{n}(e^{\tau_{n}}x))}{\partial{x_{i}}^{2}}dx\rightarrow\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\varphi\frac{\partial^{2}(e^{\frac{N}{2}\tau}u(e^{\tau}x))}{\partial{x_{i}}^{2}}dx,\quad\text{as $n\rightarrow\infty$}.

As a consequence,
\tau_{n}*u_{n}\rightharpoonup\tau*u
in
H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Furthermore,

\displaystyle\|e^{\frac{N}{2}\tau_{n}}u_{n}(e^{\tau_{n}}x)\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u_{n}|^{2}dx+2e^{2\tau_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}dx+e^{4\tau_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\Delta u_{n}|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\rightarrow\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2}dx+2e^{2s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx+e^{4s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\Delta u|^{2}dx=\|e^{\frac{N}{2}\tau}u_{(}e^{\tau}x)\|^{2},\quad\text{as $n\rightarrow\infty$},

thus
\|\tau_{n}*u_{n}\|\rightarrow\|\tau*u\|
as
n\rightarrow\infty
, and we conclude the result.
∎

.

[

2.2
Decomposition of Pohoz̆aev manifold

We have the following Pohoz̆aev identity.

Lemma 2.5
.

[
8
, Lemma 2.1]

Let
N\geq 1
and
2<p,q<\frac{2N}{(N-4)^{+}}
, if
u\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is a weak solution of

\Delta^{2}u=\lambda u+\mu|u|^{q-2}u+|u|^{p-2}u,

then
u
satisfies the Pohoz̆aev identity

\frac{N-4}{2}\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{\lambda N}{2}\|u\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{\mu N}{q}\|u\|_{q}^{q}-\frac{N}{p}\|u\|_{p}^{p}=0.

(2.1)

As a consequence, it holds that

\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{2}-\mu\gamma_{q}\|u\|_{q}^{q}-\gamma_{p}\|u\|_{p}^{p}=0.

For any
u\in S_{a}
and
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
, we define

(\tau*u)(x):=e^{\frac{N}{2}\tau}u(e^{\tau}x),\quad\text{for a.e. $x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$},

and the fiber maps

\Psi_{u}(\tau):=\mathcal{J}_{p,q}(\tau*u)=\frac{e^{4\tau}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\Delta u|^{2}dx-\frac{e^{\frac{N(p-2)}{2}\tau}}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{p}dx-\frac{\mu e^{\frac{N(q-2)}{2}\tau}}{q}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{q}dx.

Then
\tau*u\in S_{a}
and
\Psi_{u}(\tau)
is coercive on
S_{a}
for
p,q<\bar{p}
, while
\Psi_{u}(\tau)
is not bounded from below on
S_{a}
for
p>\bar{p}
or
q>\bar{p}
, i.e.,
m_{p,q,a}=-\infty
. Based on this fact, we call
\bar{p}
the
L^{2}
-critical exponent.
In the case of
p>\bar{p}
or
q>\bar{p}
, a special role will played by the Pohoz̆aev manifold

\mathcal{P}_{p,q,a}:=\Big\{u\in S_{a}:P_{p,q}(u)=0\Big\},

where

\displaystyle P_{p,q}(u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}|\Delta u|^{2}dx-\gamma_{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}|u|^{p}dx-\mu\gamma_{q}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}|u|^{q}dx.

From Lemma
2.5
, we find that any critical point of
\mathcal{J}_{p,q}|_{S_{a}}
stays in
\mathcal{P}_{p,q,a}
. Moreover, if
\mathcal{P}_{p,q,a}^{0}=\emptyset
, then
\mathcal{P}_{p,q,a}
is a natural constraint, which means that if
u\in\mathcal{P}_{p,q,a}
is a critical point for
\mathcal{J}_{p,q}|_{\mathcal{P}_{p,q,a}}
, then
u
is a critical point for
\mathcal{J}_{p,q}|_{S_{a}}
(see Lemma
2.7
). This together with the fact that
m_{p,q,a}=-\infty
enlightens us to consider the minimization of
\mathcal{J}_{p,q}
on
{\mathcal{P}_{p,q,a}}

M_{p,q,a}:=\inf\limits_{u\in\mathcal{P}_{p,q,a}}\mathcal{J}_{p,q}(u).

(2.2)

We shall see that the critical points of
\Psi_{u}
allow to project a function on
\mathcal{P}_{p,q,a}
. Thus monotonicity and convexity properties of
\Psi_{u}
strongly affect the structure of
\mathcal{P}_{p,q,a}
, and in turn the geometry of
\mathcal{J}_{p,q}|_{S_{a}}
. In this direction, we consider the decomposition of
\mathcal{P}_{p,q,a}
into the disjoint union
\mathcal{P}_{p,q,a}=\mathcal{P}_{p,q,a}^{+}\cup\mathcal{P}_{p,q,a}^{0}\cup\mathcal{P}_{p,q,a}^{-}
, where

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{p,q,a}^{+}:=\Big\{u\in\mathcal{P}_{p,q,a}:2\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{2}>p\gamma_{p}^{2}\|u\|_{p}^{p}+\mu q\gamma_{q}^{2}\|u\|_{q}^{q}\Big\}=\Big\{u\in\mathcal{P}_{p,q,a}:\Psi_{u}^{\prime\prime}(0)>0\Big\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{p,q,a}^{0}:=\Big\{u\in\mathcal{P}_{p,q,a}:2\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{2}=p\gamma_{p}^{2}\|u\|_{p}^{p}+\mu q\gamma_{q}^{2}\|u\|_{q}^{q}\Big\}=\Big\{u\in\mathcal{P}_{p,q,a}:\Psi_{u}^{\prime\prime}(0)=0\Big\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{p,q,a}^{-}:=\Big\{u\in\mathcal{P}_{p,q,a}:2\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{2}<p\gamma_{p}^{2}\|u\|_{p}^{p}+\mu q\gamma_{q}^{2}\|u\|_{q}^{q}\Big\}=\Big\{u\in\mathcal{P}_{p,q,a}:\Psi_{u}^{\prime\prime}(0)<0\Big\}.

For
\mu=0
, we denote the associated fiber maps by
\Psi_{u,0}
,
\mathcal{P}_{p,a}=\Big\{u\in S_{a}:P_{p}(u)=0\Big\}
, and
M_{p,a}=\inf\limits_{u\in\mathcal{P}_{p,a}}\mathcal{J}_{p}(u)
, where

\displaystyle P_{p}(u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}|\Delta u|^{2}dx-\gamma_{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}|u|^{p}dx.

The decomposition of
\mathcal{P}_{p,a}
into the disjoint union
\mathcal{P}_{p,a}=\mathcal{P}_{p,a}^{+}\cup\mathcal{P}_{p,a}^{0}\cup\mathcal{P}_{p,a}^{-}
, where

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{p,a}^{+}:=\Big\{u\in\mathcal{P}_{p,a}:2\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{2}>p\gamma_{p}^{2}\|u\|_{p}^{p}\Big\}=\Big\{u\in\mathcal{P}_{p,a}:\Psi_{u,0}^{\prime\prime}(0)>0\Big\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{p,a}^{0}:=\Big\{u\in\mathcal{P}_{p,a}:2\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{2}=p\gamma_{p}^{2}\|u\|_{p}^{p}\Big\}=\Big\{u\in\mathcal{P}_{p,a}:\Psi_{u,0}^{\prime\prime}(0)=0\Big\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{p,a}^{-}:=\Big\{u\in\mathcal{P}_{p,a}:2\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{2}<p\gamma_{p}^{2}\|u\|_{p}^{p}\Big\}=\Big\{u\in\mathcal{P}_{p,a}:\Psi_{u,0}^{\prime\prime}(0)<0\Big\}.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
u\in S_{a}
, then
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
is a critical point of
\Psi_{u}
if and only if
\tau*u\in\mathcal{P}_{p,q,a}
. Similarly,
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
is a critical point of
\Psi_{u,0}
if and only if
\tau*u\in\mathcal{P}_{p,a}
.

Proof.

The proof is standard, we omit it.
∎

Lemma 2.7
.

If
\mathcal{P}_{p,q,a}^{0}=\emptyset
, then
\mathcal{P}_{p,q,a}
is a natural constraint. Similarly, if
\mathcal{P}_{p,a}^{0}=\emptyset
, then
\mathcal{P}_{p,a}
is a natural constraint.

Proof.

If
u\in\mathcal{P}_{p,q,a}
is a critical point for
\mathcal{J}_{p,q}|_{\mathcal{P}_{p,q,a}}
, then by the Lagrange multiplies rule
[
40
, Proposition 5.12]
, there exists
\lambda,\nu\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\mathcal{J}_{p,q}^{\prime}=\lambda u+\nu P_{p,q}^{\prime},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

i.e.,

\Delta^{2}u=|u|^{p-2}u+\mu|u|^{q-2}u+\lambda u+\nu(\Delta^{2}u-\frac{p\gamma_{p}}{2}|u|^{p-2}u-\frac{\mu q\gamma_{q}}{2}|u|^{q-2}u),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

that is

(1-\nu)\Delta^{2}u=\lambda u+(1-\frac{\nu p\gamma_{p}}{2})|u|^{p-2}u+\mu(1-\frac{\nu q\gamma_{q}}{2})|u|^{q-2}u,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

We have to prove that
\nu=0
. By Lemma
2.5
, we have

(1-\nu)\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{2}=(1-\frac{\nu p\gamma_{p}}{2})\gamma_{p}\|u\|_{p}^{p}+\mu(1-\frac{\nu q\gamma_{q}}{2})\gamma_{q}\|u\|_{q}^{q}.

Since
u\in\mathcal{P}_{p,q,a}
, we have

\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{2}=\gamma_{p}\|u\|_{p}^{p}+\mu\gamma_{q}\|u\|_{q}^{q}.

From the above equalities, we deduce that

\nu(2\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{2}-p\gamma_{p}^{2}\|u\|_{p}^{p}-\mu q\gamma_{q}^{2}\|u\|_{q}^{q})=0.

But the term inside the bracket cannot be
0
, since
\mathcal{P}_{p,q,a}^{0}=\emptyset
, so
\nu=0
. ∎

2.3
A general minimax theorem

In this subsection, we shall give a general minimax theorem to establish the existence of a Palais-Smale sequence.

Definition 2.1
.

[
16
, Definition 5.1]

Let
B
be a closed subset of
X
. A class of
\mathcal{F}
of compact subsets of
X
is a homotopy stable family with extended boundary
B

if for any set
A\in\mathcal{F}
and any
\eta\in C([0,1]\times X,X)
satisfying
\eta(t,x)=x
for all
(t,x)\in(\{0\}\times X)\cup([0,1]\times B)
, one has
\eta(\{1\}\times A)\in\mathcal{F}
.

Lemma 2.8
.

[
16
, Theorem 5.2]

Let
\varphi
be a
C^{1}
-functional on a complete connected
C^{1}
-Finsler manifold
X
, and consider a homotopy stable family
\mathcal{F}
with an extended closed boundary
B
. Set
c=c(\varphi,\mathcal{F})=\inf\limits_{A\in\mathcal{F}}\max\limits_{x\in A}\varphi(x)
and let
\mathcal{F}
be a closed subset of
X
satisfying

A\cap F\backslash B\neq\emptyset,\quad\text{for any $A\in\mathcal{F}$}

and

\sup\limits_{x\in B}\varphi(x)\leq c\leq\inf\limits_{x\in F}\varphi(x).

Then, for any sequence of sets
\{A_{n}\}\subset\mathcal{F}
satisfying
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup\limits_{x\in A_{n}}\varphi(x)=c
, there exists a sequence
\{x_{n}\}\subset X\backslash B
such that

(i)

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\varphi(x_{n})=c
;

(ii)

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|\varphi^{\prime}(x_{n})\|_{*}=0
;

(iii)

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}dist(x_{n},\mathcal{F})=0
;

(iii)

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}dist(x_{n},A_{n})=0
.

Since
G(u):=\|u\|_{2}^{2}-a^{2}
is of class
C^{1}
and for any
u\in S_{a}
, we have
\langle G^{\prime}(u),u\rangle=2a^{2}>0
. Therefore, by the implicit function theorem, we know
S_{a}
is a
C^{1}
-Finsler manifold.

2.4
A compactness lemma for Palais-Smale sequences

In this subsection, we will give a compactness lemma for the special Palais-Smale sequence, which satisfies a suitable additional condition.

Lemma 2.9
.

[
27
]

If
N\geq 2
, then
H^{2}_{rad}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is compactly embedding into
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for
2<p<\frac{2N}{(N-4)^{+}}
.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
N\geq 2
,
2<q<p<\frac{2N}{(N-4)^{+}}
,
\mu>0
, or
2<q\leq\bar{p}<p<\frac{2N}{(N-4)^{+}}
,
\mu<0
and (
1.8
) holds. If
\{u_{n}\}\subset S_{a,r}
is a Palais-Smale sequence for
\mathcal{J}_{p,q}|_{S_{a,r}}
at level

and

as

. Then there exists

such that, up to a subsequence,

in

as

, and
u
is a radial solution of (
1.1
) with some

.

Proof.

We divide the proof into three steps.

Setp 1:

\{u_{n}\}
is bounded in
H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

By

\displaystyle c+1\geq\mathcal{J}_{p,q}(u_{n})

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{\gamma_{p}}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\Big)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u_{n}|^{p}dx+\Big(\frac{\gamma_{q}}{2}-\frac{1}{q}\Big)\mu\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u_{n}|^{q}dx+o_{n}(1)

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q\gamma_{q}}\Big)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\Delta u_{n}|^{2}dx+\Big(\frac{\gamma_{p}}{q\gamma_{q}}-\frac{1}{p}\Big)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u_{n}|^{p}dx+o_{n}(1)

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p\gamma_{p}}\Big)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\Delta u_{n}|^{2}dx+\Big(\frac{\gamma_{q}}{p\gamma_{p}}-\frac{1}{q}\Big)\mu\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u_{n}|^{q}dx+o_{n}(1).

(2.3)

(i) If
2<q\leq\bar{p}<p<\frac{2N}{(N-4)^{+}}
,
\mu>0
, by the Hölder inequality, there exists

Then by the first equation of (
2.4
), we have

Since

and

, we deduce that
\|u_{n}\|_{p}
is bounded and hence
\|u_{n}\|_{q}
is bounded. Using

as
n\rightarrow\infty
again, we obtain the boundedness of
\|\Delta u_{n}\|_{2}
.

(ii) If

,
\mu>0

) and

(iii) If

2.4

Setp 2:

\{u_{n}\}
has a nontrivial weak limit in
H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

By using Lemma
2.9
, there exists
u\in H^{2}_{rad}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that, up to a subsequence,

in
H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
u_{n}\rightarrow u
in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for

and a.e. in

. Since
\{u_{n}\}\subset S_{a,r}

\mathcal{J}_{p,q}|_{S_{a,r}}

such that

\Delta^{2}u_{n}-\mu|u_{n}|^{q-2}u_{n}-|u_{n}|^{p-2}u_{n}=\lambda_{n}u_{n}+o_{n}(1).

(2.4)

Testing the above equation with
u_{n}
, we obtain

\lambda_{n}a^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\Delta u|^{2}dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{p}dx-\mu\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{q}dx+o_{n}(1),

this with the boundedness of
\{u_{n}\}
in

\{\lambda_{n}\}
is bounded in

. Without loss of generality, we assume that

as
n\rightarrow\infty
.
Claim that

. Assume by contradiction that

\|u_{n}\|_{p},\|u_{n}\|_{q}\rightarrow 0
, this together with

, hence

as

, which is contradict to

. Hence,

3.1
The case
N\geq 2
,
2<p<\frac{2N}{(N-4)^{+}}

Lemma 3.1
.

For
2<p<\bar{p}
, we have
-\infty<m_{p,a}<0
.

Proof.

By Lemma
1.2
, for any

, we get

\mathcal{J}_{p}(u)\geq\frac{1}{2}\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{B_{N,p}a^{p-\frac{N(p-2)}{4}}}{p}\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{\frac{N(p-2)}{4}}.

, we have
\frac{N(p-2)}{4}<2
, so
\mathcal{J}_{p}
is coercive on

, which implies

.

On the other hand, for any

and

\mathcal{J}_{p}(\tau*u)=\frac{e^{4\tau}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\Delta u|^{2}dx-\frac{e^{\frac{N(p-2)}{2}\tau}}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{p}dx\rightarrow 0^{-}.

Hence,
m_{p,a}\leq\mathcal{J}_{p}(\tau*u)<0
.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

For
2<p<\bar{p}
, let

be such that
a_{1}^{2}+a_{2}^{2}=a^{2}
, then
m_{p,a}<m_{p,a_{1}}+m_{p,a_{2}}
.

Proof.

Let
c>0
,
\theta>1
and
\{u_{n}\}\subset S_{c}
be a minimizing sequence for

, then by

, there exists a local strict minimum at negative level in
(0,R_{0})
. The fact that
h
has no other critical points can be verified by
h^{\prime}(t)=0
if and only if

\psi(t)=\frac{\mu NB_{N,q}(q-2)a^{q-\frac{N(q-2)}{4}}}{4q}>0,\quad\text{with}\quad\psi(t):=t^{\frac{N(\bar{p}-p)}{8}}-\frac{NB_{N,p}(p-2)a^{p-\frac{N(p-2)}{4}}}{4p}t^{\frac{N(p-q)}{8}}.

Since
0<\frac{N(\bar{p}-q)}{8}<\frac{N(p-q)}{8}
, the above equation has at most two solutions. This ends the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.6
.

\mathcal{P}_{p,q,a}^{0}=\emptyset
and
\mathcal{P}_{p,q,a}
is a smooth manifold of codimension
2
in
H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

Proof.

Assume that
u\in\mathcal{P}_{p,q,a}^{0}
, then by
P_{p,q}(u)=0
and
\Psi_{u}^{\prime\prime}(0)=0
, we deduce that

(p\gamma_{p}-2)\gamma_{p}\|u\|_{p}^{p}=(2-q\gamma_{q})\mu\gamma_{q}\|u\|_{q}^{q}.

Using
P_{p,q}(u)=0
again, we have

\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{2}=\frac{p\gamma_{p}-q\gamma_{q}}{2-q\gamma_{q}}\gamma_{p}\|u\|_{p}^{p}\leq\frac{p\gamma_{p}-q\gamma_{q}}{2-q\gamma_{q}}\gamma_{p}B_{N,p}a^{p(1-\gamma_{p})}\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{p\gamma_{p}}

and

\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{2}=\frac{p\gamma_{p}-q\gamma_{q}}{p\gamma_{p}-2}\mu\gamma_{q}\|u\|_{q}^{q}\leq\frac{p\gamma_{p}-q\gamma_{q}}{p\gamma_{p}-2}\mu\gamma_{q}B_{N,q}a^{q(1-\gamma_{q})}\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{q\gamma_{q}}.

Hence

\Big(\frac{2-q\gamma_{q}}{B_{N,p}\gamma_{p}(p\gamma_{p}-q\gamma_{q})}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p\gamma_{p}-2}}a^{\frac{p(\gamma_{p}-1)}{p\gamma_{p}-2}}\leq\Big(\frac{p\gamma_{p}-2}{B_{N,q}\gamma_{q}(p\gamma_{p}-q\gamma_{q})}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q\gamma_{q}-2}}\Big(\mu a^{q(1-\gamma_{q})}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2-q\gamma_{q}}},

that is

\mu a^{\gamma(p,q)}\geq\Big(\frac{\bar{p}-q}{B_{N,p}\gamma_{p}(p-q)}\Big)^{\frac{\bar{p}-q}{p-\bar{p}}}\Big(\frac{p-\bar{p}}{B_{N,q}\gamma_{q}(p-q)}\Big),

where
\gamma(p,q)
is defined in (
1.7
).
Since

\frac{q\gamma_{q}}{2}\Big(\frac{p\gamma_{p}}{2}\Big)^{\frac{\bar{p}-q}{p-\bar{p}}}\leq 1,

we obtain

\mu a^{\gamma(p,q)}\geq\Big(\frac{p(\bar{p}-q)}{2B_{N,p}(p-q)}\Big)^{\frac{\bar{p}-q}{p-\bar{p}}}\Big(\frac{q(p-\bar{p})}{2B_{N,q}(p-q)}\Big)

which is contradict to (
1.6
). This prove that
\mathcal{P}_{p,q,a}^{0}=\emptyset
.

Next, we check that
\mathcal{P}_{p,q,a}
is a smooth manifold of codimension
2
in
H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Noted that
\mathcal{P}_{p,q,a}=\{u\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):G(u)=0,\,P(u)=0\}
for
G(u)=\|u\|_{2}^{2}-a^{2}
, with
P
and
G
of class
C^{1}
in
H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Thus, we have to show that the differential
(dG(u),dP(u)):H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
is surjective for any
u\in\mathcal{P}_{p,q,a}
. To this end, we prove that for any
u\in\mathcal{P}_{p,q,a}
, there exists
\varphi\in T_{u}S_{a}
such that
dP(u)[\varphi]\neq 0
. Once that the existence of
\varphi
is established, the system

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \hfil\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}dG(u)[\alpha\varphi+\beta u]=x\\
dP(u)[\alpha\varphi+\beta u]=y\end{array}\right.\Leftrightarrow\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\beta a^{2}=x\\
\alpha dP(u)[\varphi]+\beta dP(u)[u]=y\end{array}\right.\end{split}\end{aligned}

is solvable with respect to
\alpha,\beta
for any
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and hence the surjectivity is proved.

Now, suppose by contradiction that:

There exists
u\in\mathcal{P}_{p,q,a}
such that
dP(u)[\varphi]=0
for any
\varphi\in T_{u}S_{a}
.

Then
u
is a constraint critical point for the functional
P_{p,q}
on
S_{a}
, and hence by the Lagrange multiplies rule, there exists
\nu\in\mathbb{R}
such that

P_{p,q}^{\prime}=\lambda u,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

i.e.,

\Delta^{2}u=\lambda u+\frac{\mu q\gamma_{q}}{2}|u|^{q-2}u+\frac{p\gamma_{p}}{2}|u|^{p-2}u,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

By Lemma
2.5
, we have

\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{2}=\frac{\mu q\gamma_{q}^{2}}{2}\|u\|_{q}^{q}+\frac{p\gamma_{p}^{2}}{2}\|u\|_{p}^{p},

which means
u\in\mathcal{P}_{p,q,a}^{0}
, a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 4.7
.

For
u\in S_{a}
, the function
\Psi_{u}
has exactly two critical points
s_{u}<t_{u}\in\mathbb{R}
and two zeros
c_{u}<d_{u}\in\mathbb{R}
, with
s_{u}<c_{u}<t_{u}<d_{u}
. Moreover:

(i)
s_{u}*u\in\mathcal{P}_{p,q,a}^{+}
and
t_{u}*u\in\mathcal{P}_{p,q,a}^{-}
. If
\tau*u\in\mathcal{P}_{p,q,a}
, then either
\tau=s_{u}
or
\tau=t_{u}
.

(ii) For any
\tau\leq c_{u}
,
\|\Delta(\tau*u)\|_{2}^{2}\leq R_{0}
, and

\mathcal{J}_{p,q}(s_{u}*u)=\min\Big\{\mathcal{J}_{p,q}(\tau*u):\tau\in\mathbb{R}\quad\text{and}\quad\|\Delta(\tau*u)\|_{2}^{2}<R_{0}\Big\}<0.

(iii) We have

\mathcal{J}_{p,q}(t_{u}*u)=\max\Big\{\mathcal{J}_{p,q}(\tau*u):\tau\in\mathbb{R}\Big\}>0,

and
\Psi_{u}
is strictly decreasing and concave on
(t_{u},\infty)
.

(iv)
The maps
u\in S_{a}\mapsto s_{u}\in\mathbb{R}
and
u\in S_{a}\mapsto t_{u}\in\mathbb{R}
are of class
C^{1}
.

Proof.

Recall that

\Psi_{u}(\tau)=\mathcal{J}_{p,q}(\tau*u)\geq h(\|\Delta(\tau*u)\|_{2}^{2})=h(e^{4\tau}\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{2}),

thus

\Psi_{u}
is positive on
(C(R_{0}),C(R_{1}))
, where

C(R_{0}):=\frac{1}{4}\ln\frac{R_{0}}{\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{2}},\quad C(R_{0}):=\frac{1}{4}\ln\frac{R_{1}}{\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{2}}.

It is clear that
\lim\limits_{\tau\rightarrow-\infty}\Psi_{u}(\tau)=0^{-}
and
\lim\limits_{\tau\rightarrow\infty}\Psi_{u}(\tau)=-\infty
, thus
\Psi_{u}
has at least two critical points. In fact,
\Psi_{u}^{\prime}(\tau)=0
is and only if

\varphi(\tau)=\mu\gamma_{q}\|u\|^{q}>0,\quad\text{with}\quad\varphi(\tau):=e^{\frac{N(\bar{p}-q)}{2}\tau}\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{2}-\gamma_{p}e^{\frac{N(p-q)}{2}\tau}\|u\|_{p}^{p}.

By
0<\frac{N(\bar{p}-q)\tau}{2}<\frac{N(p-q)\tau}{2}
, we know the above equation has at most two solutions. Thus
\Psi_{u}
has exactly two critical points
s_{u}<t_{u}
,
with
s_{u}
a local minimum point on
(0,C(R_{0}))
at negative level, and
t_{u}
a global maximum point at positive level.

By Lemma
2.6
, we know
\tau*u\in\mathcal{P}_{p,q,a}
implies
\tau=s_{u}
or
\tau=t_{u}
.
From
\Psi_{u}^{\prime\prime}(s_{u})\geq 0
,
\Psi_{u}^{\prime\prime}(t_{u})\leq 0
, and
\mathcal{P}_{p,q,a}^{0}=\emptyset
, we have
s_{u}*u\in\mathcal{P}_{p,q,a}^{+}
and
t_{u}*u\in\mathcal{P}_{p,q,a}^{-}
. Moreover,
\Psi_{u}
is strictly decreasing and concave on
(t_{u},\infty)
.
For any
\tau\leq c_{u}\leq C(R_{0})
,
\|\Delta(\tau*u)\|_{2}^{2}=e^{4\tau}\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{2}\leq R_{0}
. It remains to show that
u\in S_{a}\mapsto s_{u}\in\mathbb{R}
and
u\in S_{a}\mapsto t_{u}\in\mathbb{R}
are of class
C^{1}
. To this end, we apply the implicit function theorem on the
C^{1}
function
\Phi(u,\tau):=\Psi_{u}^{\prime}(\tau)
. Then
\Phi(u,s_{u})=0
,
\partial_{\tau}\Phi(u,s_{u})=\Psi_{u}^{\prime\prime}(s_{u})>0
. Since
\mathcal{P}_{p,q,a}^{0}=\emptyset
, it is not possible to pass with continuity from
\mathcal{P}_{p,q,a}^{+}
to
\mathcal{P}_{p,q,a}^{-}
, then
u\in S_{a}\mapsto s_{u}\in\mathbb{R}
is of class
C^{1}
. The same arguments proves that
u\in S_{a}\mapsto t_{u}\in\mathbb{R}
is of class
C^{1}
.
∎

For any

\mathcal{A}_{k}:=\Big\{u\in S_{a}:\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{2}<k\},

Lemma 4.9

It results that

For

From lemmas
4.6

4.8

|u_{p_{n}}^{-}|^{p_{n}-2}u_{p_{n}}^{-}-|u|^{4^{*}-2}u\in L^{\frac{4^{*}}{4^{*}-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, this together with
v_{p_{n}}^{-}\rightarrow 0
in
L^{4^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
yields that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|u_{p_{n}}^{-}|^{p_{n}-2}u_{p_{n}}^{-}-|u|^{4^{*}-2}u)v_{p_{n}}^{-}dx\rightarrow 0,\quad\text{as $n\rightarrow\infty$}.

Moreover, by
|u_{p_{n}}^{-}|^{q-2}u_{p_{n}}^{-}-|u|^{q-2}u\rightarrow 0
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, and
|u_{p_{n}}^{-}|^{q-2}u_{p_{n}}^{-}-|u|^{q-2}u
is bounded in
L^{\frac{q}{q-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, using Lemma
2.4
, we have
|u_{p_{n}}^{-}|^{q-2}u_{p_{n}}^{-}-|u|^{q-2}u\rightharpoonup 0
in
L^{\frac{q}{q-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. This with
v_{p_{n}}^{-}\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
yields that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|u_{p_{n}}^{-}|^{q-2}u_{p_{n}}^{-}-|u|^{q-2}u)v_{p_{n}}^{-}dx\rightarrow 0,\quad\text{as $n\rightarrow\infty$}.

From the above arguments, by
\lambda<0
, we immediately know
\|v_{p_{n}}^{-}\|_{2}\rightarrow 0
, which means
u_{p_{n}}^{-}\rightarrow u
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
as
n\rightarrow\infty
. So
u_{p_{n}}^{-}\rightarrow u
in
H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

Setp 6:

\tilde{\sigma}_{4^{*},q,a,r}
can be achieved.

In fact, by (
4.20
) and
\mathcal{P}_{4^{*},q,a,r}=\emptyset
, we have

\displaystyle 2\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{2}<4^{*}\|u\|_{4^{*}}^{4^{*}}+\mu q\gamma_{q}^{2}\|u\|_{q}^{q},

which means
u\in\mathcal{P}_{4^{*},q,a,r}^{-}
. Thus

\tilde{\sigma}_{4^{*},q,a,r}\leq\mathcal{J}_{4^{*},q}(u)=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{J}_{p_{n},q}(u_{p_{n}}^{-})=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\tilde{\sigma}_{p_{n},q,a,r}\leq\tilde{\sigma}_{4^{*},q,a,r}.

∎

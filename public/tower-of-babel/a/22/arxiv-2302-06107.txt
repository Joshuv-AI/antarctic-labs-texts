Moving vectors I: Representation type of blocks of Ariki-Koike algebras Thanks: The work is supported by the Natural Science Foundation of Hebei Province, China (A2021501002); China Scholarship Council (202008130184) and Natural Science Foundation of China (11871107).
Yanbo Li, Xiangyu Qi

Abstract
. We introduce a new invariant for blocks of Ariki-Koike algebras, called block moving vector, which is a vector of non-negative integers summing up to the weight of the block. In this paper, we use moving vectors to classify representation-finite blocks of Ariki-Koike algebras. As applications, we obtain examples of blocks with the same weight associated with the same multicharge that are not derived equivalent and examples of derived equivalent blocks being in different orbits under the adjoint action of the affine Weyl group. We also determine the representation type for blocks of cyclotomic q q -Schur algebras.

Introduction

The Ariki-Koike algebras (the cyclotomic Hecke algebras of type
G(r,1,n)
) were introduced in
[
8
]
,
[
13
]
and
[
17
]
,
which include Iwahori-Hecke algebras of types A and B as special cases. They play an important role
in modular representation theory of finite groups of Lie type, and are in relation
with various significant objects such as quantum groups and rational Cherednik algebras.
Consequently, the Ariki-Koike algebras have received intensive and
continuous study since their appearance (see
[
2
,
3
,
15
,
19
,
20
,
24
,
32
,
42
,
43
]
for examples).
The interest on these algebras has been strengthened
by a fundamental result of Brundan and Kleshchev
[
14
]
, in which an explicit isomorphism between blocks of Ariki-Koike algebras
and cyclotomic Khovanov-Lauda-Rouquier algebras (introduced in
[
36
,
48
]
) in type A were constructed, and many profound results emerged.

In the modular representation theory of blocks of an Iwahori-Hecke algebra of type A, the weight is important because
it can measure how complicated a block is. For example, a block
B
of an Iwahori-Hecke algebra of type A is of finite type
if and only if the weight of
B
is not more than one
[
23
]
. However, this is no longer true for Ariki-Koike algebras.
In fact, many classical results of type A related with weights have no cyclotomic versions,
including representation type of blocks. During our study, we found that the structure of a block, to some extent,
is controlled by the process of an arbitrary abacus lying in the block moving to its core
through elementary operations. Accordingly, we define a new invariant for each block, called

block moving vector
(see Definition
4.1.14
for details).
Roughly speaking, just as its name implies,
a block moving vector is a vector of non-negative integers that records the process of an
abacus in the block moving to its core. The sum of components of
a block moving vector is just the weight of the corresponding block (see Lemma
4.1.15
for details).
By using moving vectors, we classify representation-finite blocks of Ariki-Koike algebras in this paper.
Moreover, we consider in
[
41
]
the core blocks (introduced by Fayers in
[
25
]
)
and will apply moving vectors to relevant Lie theory in
[
31
]
.

In general, it is an essential and difficult problem in representation theory to
determine the representation type of a finite dimensional algebra.
Recall that a finite dimensional algebra
A
has finite type if the
number of indecomposable
A
-modules up to isomorphism is finite. Otherwise,
A
has infinite representation type.
If
A
has infinite type, Drozd
[
21
]
proved that
A
is either tame or wild but not both.
Back to Hecke algebras, the representation type of blocks of an Iwahori-Hecke algebra of type A was determined by Erdmann and Nakano in
[
23
]
.
Ariki and Mathas
[
9
]
determined the representation type of the Iwahori-Hecke algebras of type B and
Ariki
[
4
]
determined the representation type for all of
the Iwahori-Hecke algebras of classical type.
Then Ariki
[
5
]
determined representation type for blocks of Iwahori-Hecke algebras of type B
by using a result obtained in
[
7
]
and the techniques developed in
[
6
,
10
]
.
In 2010, Wada
[
50
]
gave a necessary and sufficient condition on parameters for an Ariki-Koike algebra
to have finite type. However, it is still an open problem to determine when a block of an
Ariki-Koike algebra has finite type (
after we had posted our paper on arXiv, Ariki et al posted a paper
[
11
]
,
in which tame blocks are completely described
).

Let

be an algebraically closed field,

. Define the

of

. If no such

. Fix positive integers

Let

be a

and
define

. Then

, the
Ariki-Koike algebra

with parameters

and

, is the unital

K
-algebra with generators

,

,

(H1)

(T_{0}-Q_{1})(T_{0}-Q_{2})\cdots(T_{0}-Q_{r})=0

(H2)

(H3)

(H4)

The

We now describe the main result of this paper. Note that the representation types of blocks of Iwahori-Hecke algebras
of type A and type B have been determined in
[

,

Theorem.

Let
K
be an algebraically closed field with

and

,

. Let
(s_{1},s_{2},\cdots,s_{r})\in{\mathcal{A}}^{r}_{e}
(see Section 3.1),

\mathcal{H}_{n}(q,Q)
an Ariki-Koike algebra with

Denote by

Then

In the later case, block

is Morita equivalent to

As a by-product, we can determine the representation type of blocks of a cyclotomic
q
-Schur algebra.

Corollary.

Let

be a non-simple block of
\mathcal{S}_{n,r}
and

.
Then
\mathcal{B}
has finite type if and only if

, or

Moreover, we can also study the derived equivalence among blocks of
\mathcal{H}_{n}(q,Q)

It is known that two blocks of Iwahori-Hecke algebras of type A are derived equivalent if and only if
they have the same weight, and if and only if they are in the
same orbit under the adjoint action of the affine Weyl group
[
18
]
.
This is no longer true in cyclotomic case. We will construct examples of blocks
with the same weight associated with the same multicharge that are not derived equivalent.
Examples of derived equivalent blocks in different orbits are also given.

The paper is organized as follows. We begin our study with the so-called multiplication poset of
an indecomposable self-injective cellular algebra

in Section 2.
The main result is that if

has finite representation type,
then

has only two different cell chains which implies that the multiplication poset must be a totally ordered set.
We emphasize that the result can be used to determine the representation type
of most blocks in an Ariki-Koike algebra and may be useful for studying blocks of other algebras.
Note that the cell modules of an Ariki-Koike algebra are indexed by
r
-partitions
and each
r
-partition can be represented by an abacus.
In Section 3, after reviewing some preliminaries on blocks and abaci,
we give a description of the action of an affine Weyl group on blocks by the language of abaci.
Then we define incomparable abaci and prove that given a block,
the existence of a pair of incomparable abaci implies the existence of a pair of
incomparable
r
-partitions with respect to the dominance order.
In Section 4, we define the (block) moving vector and conduct some preliminary study.
In particular, we give a construction that will be used not only in this paper, but also in coming papers
[
41
,
31
]
.
In Section 5, we give the proof of
Theorem
, which is divided into three parts. In Section 6, we apply our results to study derived equivalence
and in Section 7, we determine the representation type
of blocks of a cyclotomic
q
-Schur algebra.

2.1.
Cellular algebras

Cellular algebras were introduced by Graham and Lehrer in
[
28
]
in 1996. Cellular theory provides a systematic framework
for studying the representation theory of many interesting and important algebras
coming from mathematics and physics, such as Iwahori-Hecke algebras of finite type
[
27
]
, Brauer algebras
[
28
]
and so on.
The main research object of this paper, Ariki-Koike algebras, are cellular too.

Definition 2.1.1

(
[
28
, Definition 1.1]
)
.

Let
R
be a noetherian integral domain. An associative unital

R
-algebra
A
is called a cellular algebra with cell datum
(\check{\Lambda},M,C,\ast)
if the
following conditions are satisfied:

(C1)

The finite set
\check{\Lambda}
is a poset with
order relation
\geq
. Associated with
each
\lambda\in\check{\Lambda}
, there is a finite set
M(\lambda)
. The
algebra
A
has an
R
-basis
\{C_{S,T}^{\lambda}\mid\lambda\in\check{\Lambda},\,\,S,T\in M(\lambda)\}
.

(C2)

The map
\ast
is an
R
-linear anti-automorphism of
A

such that
(C_{S,T}^{\lambda})^{\ast}=C_{T,S}^{\lambda}
for
all
\lambda\in\check{\Lambda}
and
S,T\in M(\lambda)
.

(C3)

Let
\lambda\in\check{\Lambda}
and
S,T\in M(\lambda)
. For any
element

a\in A
, we have

aC_{S,T}^{\lambda}\equiv\sum_{S^{{}^{\prime}}\in M(\lambda)}r_{a}(S^{\prime},S)C_{S^{{}^{\prime}},T}^{\lambda}\,\,\,\,{\rm mod}\,\,\,A(>\lambda),

where
r_{a}(S^{{}^{\prime}},S)\in R
is independent of
T
and

A(>\lambda)
is the
R
-submodule of
A
generated by

\{C_{U,V}^{\mu}\mid U,\,\,V\in M(\mu),\,\,\mu>\lambda\}
.

Before giving a rather lengthy list of definitions associated with cellular algebras, let us
give a remark about the poset
\check{\Lambda}
in Definition
2.1.1
. Firstly, we define another poset
\Lambda
,
which is equal to
\check{\Lambda}
as a set, and a partial ordering is as follows.
For arbitrary two elements
\lambda,\mu\in\Lambda
, we say
\lambda\geq\mu
if
there exist some
C_{U,V}^{\mu}
and

such that certain
C_{S,T}^{\lambda}
appears
in the linear expansion of
aC_{U,V}^{\mu}
with nonzero coefficient, and then extend the relation “
\geq

The extension makes

to be a poset.
Clearly,
\check{\Lambda}
is a refinement of
\Lambda
. It is worthwhile to note that
\Lambda
is more
essential than
\check{\Lambda}
for our study and will be called the multiplication poset of

We will always write poset

by

We now turn to give a number of basic definitions and results connected with cellular algebras.
As a natural consequence of the axioms, the cell module is defined as follows.

Definition 2.1.2

(
[
28

)

Let

be a cellular algebra with cell datum
(\Lambda,M,C,\ast)

\lambda\in\Lambda

\{C_{S}\mid S\in M(\lambda)\}

aC_{S}=\sum_{S^{{}^{\prime}}\in M(\lambda)}r_{a}(S^{{}^{\prime}},S)C_{S^{{}^{\prime}}}\,\,\,\,(a\in A,\,\,S\in M(\lambda)),

Let

C_{S,T}^{\lambda}C_{U,V}^{\lambda}\equiv\Phi(T,U)C_{S,V}^{\lambda}\,\,\,\,{\rm mod}\,\,\,A(>\lambda),

\Phi_{\lambda}:\,\,W_{\lambda}\times W_{\lambda}\longrightarrow R

Define

If
\Phi_{\lambda}\neq 0

.

When

proved the following result.

Lemma 2.1.3
.

[

For any

. Let

For

.
Sometimes we write

Denote the matrix

Lemma 2.1.4
.

[

Let

An equivalent basis-free definition of a cellular algebra was given by Koenig and Xi

Definition 2.1.5

[

Let

A two-sided ideal

(1)

(2)

There exists a free

making the following diagram commutative:

\begin{CD}J@>{\alpha}>{}>\Delta\otimes_{R}\Delta^{\ast}\\
@V{\ast}V{}V@V{}V{v_{1}\otimes v_{2}\mapsto v_{2}^{\ast}\otimes v_{1}^{\ast}}V\\
J@>{\alpha}>{}>\Delta\otimes_{R}\Delta^{\ast}\end{CD}

The algebra

with

if there is an

-module decomposition

\ast

For
\lambda\in\Lambda_{0}
, let

be the projective cover of

.
Denote by

Lemma 2.1.6

[

For
\lambda\in\Lambda_{0}

Then

This implies that a simple module

Note that given a cellular algebra with cell datum

Lemma 2.1.7

Let

\mu,\,\nu

Clearly,

Since

2.2.
Brauer tree algebras

We fix some notations by recall the definition of a Brauer tree algebra.
The main references of this subsection are
[
1
,
12
]
.

A Brauer tree is a finite tree together with two additional structures on each vertex
i
:

(1)

a circular ordering of the edges adjacent to
i
;

(2)

a positive integer
m(i)
, called the multiplicity satisfying at most one
m(i)
is larger than one.

The vertex with multiplicity more than one is called the exceptional vertex, which is drawn customarily as a blackened circle.
A finite dimensional algebra
A
is called a
Brauer tree algebra
if the structure of the indecomposable projective modules
can be described in terms of a Brauer tree in the following way.

(1)

There is a one to one correspondence between the edges
\alpha
of the tree
and the indecomposable projective
A
-modules
P_{\alpha}
and hence the corresponding simple
A
-modules
L_{\alpha}
.

(2)

For an edge
\alpha
connecting vertices
i
and
j
, let
(\alpha=\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{t})
and
(\alpha=\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{r})

be the circular orderings of edges adjacent to
i
and
j
, respectively. Then
\rad P_{\alpha}=U_{\alpha}+V_{\alpha}
,
where
U_{\alpha}\cap V_{\alpha}=L_{\alpha}
,
U_{\alpha}
is uniserial with composition factors
L_{\alpha_{2}},\dots,L_{\alpha_{t}},L_{\alpha_{1}},

m(i)
times from top to bottom, and
V_{\alpha}
is uniserial with composition factors
L_{\beta_{2}},\dots,L_{\beta_{r}},L_{\beta_{1}},

m(j)
times from top to bottom.

Given a Brauer tree
T
, let
\Gamma
be the quiver whose vertices are in one to one correspondence with the edges of
T
.
For a vertex
i
in
T
, the circular ordering
(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{t})
give rise to an oriented cycle
C_{i}
.
Then the vertex
v_{\alpha}
in
\Gamma
corresponding to edge
\alpha
in
T
connecting vertices
i
and
j

belongs to exactly two oriented cycles
C_{i}
and
C_{j}
. Denote the path in
C_{i}

without repeated arrows starting and ending at
\alpha_{k}
by
p_{\alpha_{k}}^{(i)}
.
Define relations
\rho
to be
(p_{\alpha}^{(i)})^{m(i)}-(p_{\alpha}^{(j)})^{m(j)},uv
,
where
u
is the arrow in
C_{i}
with
e(u)=\alpha
and
v
the arrow in
C_{j}
with
s(v)=\alpha
, or

u
is the arrow in
C_{j}
with
e(u)=\alpha
and
v
the arrow in
C_{i}
with
s(v)=\alpha
.
Then the algebra
K(\Gamma,\rho)
is a Brauer tree algebra given by

.
It is worth to note that a Brauer tree determines a unique Brauer tree algebra
up to Morita equivalence (see
[
39
, Corollary 4.3.3]
).
Furthermore, in
[
26
]
Geck studied the Brauer tree of Hecke algebras,
and Koenig and Xi studied in
[

]
the cellularity of a Brauer tree algebra.

Lemma 2.2.1
.

[
37
, Proposition 5.3]

A Brauer tree algebra is a cellular algebra if and only if the Brauer tree is a straight line.

2.3.
Poset of a finite type self-injective cellular algebra

Let us illustrate all indecomposable projective modules of a cellular Brauer tree algebra as a lemma for later use.
By Lemma

(1)
Let

Based on the above preparation, we can give the following characterization on the poset of a cellular Brauer tree algebra.

In fact, let

.

and

On the other hand, all cell modules with two composition factors are of the same type by Lemma

.
If a type I cell module appears, Lemma

3.
Abacus orbits and incomparable abaci

It is known that blocks of an Ariki-Koike algebra are symmetric
[

\mathfrak{n}^{h-e}_{i+j-r}=\mathfrak{n}^{h}_{i+j-r}+e
. Then by Lemma
3.1.10
,

\mathfrak{n}^{h-e}_{i+j-r}-\mathfrak{n}^{h}_{i}=\mathfrak{n}^{h}_{i+j-r}+e-\mathfrak{n}^{h}_{i}=s_{i+j-r}+e-s_{i}\geq 0
. As a result,
If in
L_{\bf s}({\bm{\lambda}})
, position
(i,l_{t})
have a bead placed and
(i+j-r,l_{t}-e)
are empty,
t=1,\cdots,m
,
then there exist
h_{1},\cdots,h_{m}
such that position
(i,h_{x})
are empty and positions
(i+j-r,h_{x}-e)
have a bead placed for
x=1,\cdots,m
.

(4) Let
l
be an integer such that in
L_{\bf s}({\bm{\lambda}})
all positions
(x,y)
have a bead placed, where
1\leq x\leq r
and
y\leq l
.
Since
s_{i}+k\leq s_{j}
, by Lemma
3.1.10

\mathfrak{n}_{j}^{l}-\mathfrak{n}_{i}^{l}=s_{j}-s_{i}\geq k
.
We have proved the first half.
Moreover, if
L_{\bf s}({\bm{\lambda}})
is complete, then in
L_{\bf s}({\bm{\lambda}})
position
(i,h)
being empty
forces positions
(x,h)
to be empty for
1\leq x\leq i
,
h\in\mathbb{Z}
, and position
(j,h)

being occupied by a bead implies that all positions
(y,h)
have a bead placed,
where
j\leq y\leq r
. Consequently, the second half holds.
∎

3.2.
Orbits of abaci

In this subsection, we give an explanation by abaci
for the action of an affine Weyl group on blocks.
Given a pair
({\bm{\lambda}},{\bf s})
, it clearly belongs a unique block.
Denote by
{\bm{\Lambda}}_{{\bf s}}
and
{\bm{\beta}}_{{\bm{\lambda}},{\bf s}}
the
corresponding elements in dominant weight lattice and positive root lattice, respectively.
For
j\in I
and pair
({\bm{\lambda}},{\bf s})
, define an action of
\sigma_{j}
on
({\bm{\lambda}},{\bf s})

by
\sigma_{j}({\bm{\lambda}},{\bf s})=(\sigma_{j}({\bm{\lambda}}),\sigma_{j}({\bf s}))
,
where the abacus of
(\sigma_{j}({\bm{\lambda}}),\sigma_{j}({\bf s}))
is obtained
by interchanging columns
j-1+ke
and
j+ke
in
L_{\bf s}({\bm{\lambda}})
for all
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
A simple result about the action is that the charge is invariant.

Lemma 3.2.1
.

For
j\in I
,
\sigma_{j}({\bf s})={\bf s}
.

Proof.

It is a direct corollary of the definition of the action of
\sigma_{j}
on a pair and Lemma
3.1.8
(3).
∎

To achieve the target of this subsection, we first do some work on 1-abaci. Given an abacus
L_{s}(\lambda)
, let
x\geq 1
and
\CIRCLE_{x}
at the
h
-th position. If the
h+1
-th position is empty,
then move
\CIRCLE_{x}
to the
h+1
-th position and denote by
(\mu,u)
the new abacus.
By Lemma
3.1.8
(2),
\mu_{x}=\lambda_{x}+1
, and
\lambda_{i}=\mu_{i}
for each
i\neq x
.
Moreover, we have from Lemma
3.1.8
(3) that
u=s
.
Using this method, each abacus
L_{s}(\lambda)
can be obtained from
L_{s}(\varnothing)
by finite steps.
Clearly, this process is invertible.

Lemma 3.2.2
.

If
\CIRCLE_{i}
is at
j+ke
-th position in
L_{s}(\lambda)
, where
0\leq j\leq e-1
,
then the residue of node
(i,\lambda_{i})
is
j
.

Proof.

By the definition of abacus
L_{s}(\lambda)
, we have
s+\lambda_{i}-i=j+ke
, that is,
the residue of node
(i,\lambda_{i})
is
j
.
∎

Lemma 3.2.3
.

Assume that in abacus
L_{s}(\lambda)
, position
j+ke
is empty and there is a bead at position
j-1+ke
, where
0\leq j\leq e-1
.
Denote by
L_{s}(\mu)
the abacus obtained by moving the bead at position
j-1+ke
to position
j+ke
.
Then
{\bm{\beta}}_{\mu,s}={\bm{\beta}}_{\lambda,s}+{\bm{\alpha}}_{j}
.

Proof.

Suppose that the bead at position
j-1+ke
is
\CIRCLE_{i}
. Then
\mu_{i}=\lambda_{i}+1

and
\mu_{x}=\lambda_{x}
for all
x\neq i
. We can deduce from Lemma
3.2.2
that the residue of node
(i,\mu_{i})
in
[\mu]
is
j
.
That is,
{\bm{\beta}}_{\mu,s}={\bm{\beta}}_{\lambda,s}+{\bm{\alpha}}_{j}
.
∎

Recall Definition
3.1.12
of
\mathfrak{m}^{j-1}_{j}
.

Lemma 3.2.4
.

Given an abacus
L_{s}(\lambda)
and
j\in I
, we have

{\bm{\beta}}_{\sigma_{j}(\lambda),s}={\bm{\beta}}_{\lambda,s}+\mathfrak{m}^{j-1}_{j}{\bm{\alpha}}_{j}.

Proof.

Firstly, let us find all pairs of positions that need to interchange under
\sigma_{j}
.
Assume
X=\{b_{i}\mid i=1,\dots,l\}
are all integers such that
position
j-1+b_{i}e
is empty and position
j+b_{i}e
has a bead. Note that
X
may be empty.
Therefore, there is a set
Y=\{a_{h}\in{\mathbb{Z}}\mid h=1,\dots,l+\mathfrak{m}^{j-1}_{j}\}
such that
position
j-1+a_{h}e
has a bead and position
j+a_{h}e
is empty. By Lemma
3.2.3

{\bm{\beta}}_{\sigma_{j}(\lambda,s)}={\bm{\beta}}_{\lambda,s}+(\mathfrak{m}^{j-1}_{j}+l){\bm{\alpha}}_{j}-l{\bm{\alpha}}_{j}={\bm{\beta}}_{\lambda,s}+\mathfrak{m}^{j-1}_{j}{\bm{\alpha}}_{j}.

∎

The
r
-version of Lemma
3.2.4
is not difficult to know. We write it below without a proof.

Lemma 3.2.5
.

Given an abacus
L_{\bf s}({\bm{\lambda}})
and
j\in I
, we have

{\bm{\beta}}_{\sigma_{j}({\bm{\lambda}}),{\bf s}}={\bm{\beta}}_{{\bm{\lambda}},{\bf s}}+\mathfrak{m}^{j-1}_{j}{\bm{\alpha}}_{j}.

The following result can be proved by induction. We omit the details here.

Lemma 3.2.6
.

For
j\in I
and a pair
(\lambda,s)
, we have
\sigma_{j}({\bm{\Lambda}}_{s}-{\bm{\beta}}_{\lambda,s})={\bm{\Lambda}}_{s}-{\bm{\beta}}_{\sigma_{j}(\lambda),s}
.

The
r
-version of Lemma
3.2.6
is as follows.

Proposition 3.2.7
.

For
0\leq j\leq e-1
and pair
({\bm{\lambda}},{\bf s})
, we have

\sigma_{j}({\bm{\Lambda}}_{{\bf s}}-{\bm{\beta}}_{{\bm{\lambda}},{\bf s}})={\bm{\Lambda}}_{{\bf s}}-{\bm{\beta}}_{\sigma_{j}({\bm{\lambda}}),{\bf s}}.

Proof.

It follows from Lemma
3.2.6
that

\sigma_{j}({\bm{\Lambda}}_{{\bf s}}-{\bm{\beta}}_{{\bm{\lambda}},{\bf s}})=\sum^{r}_{i=1}\sigma_{j}({\bm{\Lambda}}_{s_{i}}-{\bm{\beta}}_{{\bm{\lambda}}^{(i)},s_{i}})=\sum^{r}_{i=1}({\bm{\Lambda}}_{s_{i}}-{\bm{\beta}}_{\sigma_{j}({\bm{\lambda}}^{(i)}),s_{i}})={\bm{\Lambda}}_{{\bf s}}-{\bm{\beta}}_{\sigma_{j}({\bm{\lambda}}),{\bf s}}.

∎

Lemma 3.2.8
.

Assume that the pair
({\bm{\lambda}},{\bf s})
is in block
\mathcal{H}^{\bm{\Lambda}}_{\bm{\beta}}
. Then for
0\leq j\leq e-1
,

\mathfrak{m}^{j-1}_{j}(L_{\bf s}({\bm{\lambda}}))=({\bm{\alpha}}_{j},{\bm{\Lambda}}-{\bm{\beta}}).

Proof.

For
0\leq j\leq e-1
, it follows from Lemmas
3.2.5
,
3.2.7
and the definition of
\sigma_{j}
that

{\bm{\Lambda}}-{\bm{\beta}}-({\bm{\alpha}}_{j},{\bm{\Lambda}}-{\bm{\beta}}){\bm{\alpha}}_{j}=\sigma_{j}({\bm{\Lambda}}-{\bm{\beta}})={\bm{\Lambda}}-{\bm{\beta}}_{\sigma_{j}({\bm{\lambda}}),{\bf s}}={\bm{\Lambda}}-{\bm{\beta}}_{{\bm{\lambda}},{\bf s}}-\mathfrak{m}^{j-1}_{j}{\bm{\alpha}}_{j}.

∎

3.3.
Incomparable abaci

In this subsection we introduce the so-called incomparable abaci.
The main result is that from incomparable abaci
one can construct incomparable
r
-partitions with respect to the dominance order.

Definition 3.3.1
.

Given two
r
-abaci
L_{\bf s}({\bm{\lambda}})
and
L_{\bf s}({\bm{\mu}})
with
|{\bm{\lambda}}|=|{\bm{\mu}}|
.
Assume that there exist
\iota_{1},\iota_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\kappa_{1}\neq\kappa_{2}
,
1\leq\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2}\leq r
such that

(1)

In
L_{\bf s}({\bm{\lambda}})
, position
(\kappa_{1},\iota_{1})
has a bead and position
(\kappa_{2},\iota_{2})
is empty,
and in
L_{\bf s}({\bm{\mu}})
, position
(\kappa_{1},\iota_{1})
is empty and position
(\kappa_{2},\iota_{2})
has a bead.

(2)

The beads on the right side of
\iota_{1}
-th position in
L_{s_{\kappa_{1}}}({\bm{\lambda}}^{(\kappa_{1})})

are the same as those in
L_{s_{\kappa_{1}}}({\bm{\mu}}^{(\kappa_{1})})
,
and the beads on the left side of
\iota_{2}
-th position in
L_{s_{\kappa_{2}}}({\bm{\lambda}}^{(\kappa_{2})})
are the same as those in
L_{s_{\kappa_{2}}}({\bm{\mu}}^{(\kappa_{2})})
.

Then we say
L_{\bf s}({\bm{\lambda}})
and
L_{\bf s}({\bm{\mu}})
are incomparable, which will be denoted by
L_{\bf s}({\bm{\lambda}})\parallel L_{\bf s}({\bm{\mu}})
, or
L_{\bf s}({\bm{\mu}})\parallel L_{\bf s}({\bm{\lambda}})
.

Example 3.3.2
.

Let
e=5
,
{\bm{\lambda}}=((2,1,1),(2,2,1,1),(3,1,1),(4,3,1,1))
, and
{\bf s}=(1,0,2,0)
. Then
L_{\bf s}({\bm{\lambda}})
is

\cdots
\cdots
\cdots
\cdots
\cdots
\cdots
\cdots
\cdots

Let
{\bm{\mu}}=((2,2,2),(5,1,1,1),(3),(4,2,1))
and
{\bf s}=(1,0,2,0)
. Then
L_{\bf s}({\bm{\mu}})
is

\cdots
\cdots
\cdots
\cdots
\cdots
\cdots
\cdots
\cdots

Take
\kappa_{1}=4,\,\kappa_{2}=2,\,\iota_{1}=1,\,\iota_{2}=-1
. It is not difficult to check that
the conditions
(1)
and
(2)
of Definition
3.3.1
are satisfied.
Then
L_{\bf s}({\bm{\lambda}})
and
L_{\bf s}({\bm{\mu}})
are incomparable abaci.

The original intention of defining incomparable abaci is
to find incomparable
r
-partitions that are in the same block.
An easy computation gives
{\bm{\mu}}\,\unrhd\,{\bm{\lambda}}
in Example
3.3.2
.
This implies that we can not conclude from two abaci
L_{\bf s}({\bm{\lambda}})
and
L_{\bf s}({\bm{\mu}})
being incomparable
that the corresponding
r
-partitions are incomparable with respect to the dominance order
\unrhd
.
A simple observation tells us that if
L_{\bf s}({\bm{\lambda}})
and
L_{\bf s}({\bm{\mu}})
are incomparable abaci,
then for arbitrary
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{r}
,
L_{{\bf s}^{\sigma}}({\bm{\lambda}}^{\sigma})
and
L_{{\bf s}^{\sigma}}({\bm{\mu}}^{\sigma})
are incomparable too,
where
{\bf s}^{\sigma}
is defined to be
({\bf s}_{\sigma(1)},\dots,{\bf s}_{\sigma(r)})
. If we choose
\sigma=(124)
in Example
3.3.2
,
then
{\bm{\lambda}}^{\sigma}=((4,3,1,1),(2,1,1),(3,1,1),(2,2,1,1))
,
{\bm{\mu}}^{\sigma}=((4,2,1),(2,2,2),(3),(5,1,1,1))
,
{\bf s}^{\sigma}=(0,1,2,0)

and
L_{{\bf s}^{\sigma}}({\bm{\lambda}}^{\sigma})
and

are

\cdots
\cdots
\cdots
\cdots
\cdots
\cdots
\cdots
\cdots

and
\cdots
\cdots
\cdots
\cdots
\cdots
\cdots
\cdots
\cdots

By a simple computation, we know
{\bm{\lambda}}^{\sigma}\parallel{\bm{\mu}}^{\sigma}
.
It is worth to point out that this has general significance.
That is, we can prove that if
L_{\bf s}({\bm{\lambda}})\parallel L_{\bf s}({\bm{\mu}})
, then
there exists some
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{r}
such that
{\bm{\lambda}}^{\sigma}
and
{\bm{\mu}}^{\sigma}
are incomparable.
To this aim, let us first give some characterizations of incomparable abaci.

Lemma 3.3.3
.

Let
L_{\bf s}({\bm{\lambda}})\parallel L_{\bf s}({\bm{\mu}})
. Then

(1)

The number of empty positions on the left side of position
(\kappa_{1},\iota_{1}+1)

in
L_{s_{\kappa_{1}}}({\bm{\lambda}}^{(\kappa_{1})})
is the same as that in
L_{s_{\kappa_{1}}}({\bm{\mu}}^{(\kappa_{1})})
.

(2)

The number of the beads on the right side of position
(\kappa_{2},\iota_{2}-1)

in
L_{s_{\kappa_{2}}}({\bm{\lambda}}^{(\kappa_{2})})
is the same as that in
L_{s_{\kappa_{2}}}({\bm{\mu}}^{(\kappa_{2})})
.

Proof.

(1) Choose
k\in\mathbb{Z}
such that all positions those are to the left of
k+1
-th
positions of both
L_{\bf s}({\bm{\lambda}})
and
L_{\bf s}({\bm{\mu}})
are occupied by a bead.
According to Definition
3.3.1
(1),
k<\iota_{1}
.
On the other hand, we have from Lemma
3.1.10
that

\mathfrak{n}^{k}_{\kappa_{1}}(L_{\bf s}({\bm{\lambda}}))=\mathfrak{n}^{k}_{\kappa_{1}}(L_{\bf s}({\bm{\mu}}))
.
Then the result follows from Definition
3.3.1
(2).

(2) is proved similarly as (1).
∎

With the above result, we can get a condition for two
r
-partitions being incomparable.

Lemma 3.3.4
.

Keep notations as in Definition
3.3.1
. Let
L_{\bf s}({\bm{\lambda}})\parallel L_{\bf s}({\bm{\mu}})
and
\kappa_{1}<\kappa_{2}
.
If
L_{\bf s}({\bm{\lambda}}^{(c)})
and
L_{\bf s}({\bm{\mu}}^{(c)})
are the same for
c\in\{1,\dots,\kappa_{1}-1\}\cup\{\kappa_{2}+1,\dots,r\}
, then
{\bm{\lambda}}\parallel{\bm{\mu}}
.

Proof.

Suppose that the bead at position
(\kappa_{1},\iota_{1})
in
L_{\bf s}({\bm{\lambda}})
is

\CIRCLE_{k}^{\kappa_{1}}({\bm{\lambda}},{\bf s})
. Since
L_{\bf s}({\bm{\lambda}})\parallel L_{\bf s}({\bm{\mu}})
,
by Definition
3.3.1
, the beads on the right side of

\iota_{1}
-th position in
L_{s_{\kappa_{1}}}({\bm{\lambda}}^{(\kappa_{1})})
are the same as those in
L_{s_{\kappa_{1}}}({\bm{\mu}}^{(\kappa_{1})})
.
This implies that for each
1\leq a<k
, if
\CIRCLE_{a}^{\kappa_{1}}({\bm{\lambda}},{\bf s})

is in position
b
, then so is
\CIRCLE_{a}^{\kappa_{1}}({\bm{\mu}},{\bf s})
.
Moreover, we have from Lemma
3.3.3
that the number of empty positions on the left side
of position
(\kappa_{1},\iota_{1}+1)
in
L_{s_{\kappa_{1}}}({\bm{\lambda}}^{(\kappa_{1})})

is the same as that in
L_{s_{\kappa_{1}}}({\bm{\mu}}^{(\kappa_{1})})
. Consequently,
the number of empty positions on the left side
of
\CIRCLE_{a}^{\kappa_{1}}({\bm{\lambda}},{\bf s})
in
L_{s_{\kappa_{1}}}({\bm{\lambda}}^{(\kappa_{1})})
is the same as that on the left side
of
\CIRCLE_{a}^{\kappa_{1}}({\bm{\mu}},{\bf s})
in
L_{s_{\kappa_{1}}}({\bm{\mu}}^{(\kappa_{1})})
for
1\leq a<k
.
Then we obtain by Lemma
3.1.8
(2) that

(3.3.1)

\displaystyle{\bm{\lambda}}_{a}^{(\kappa_{1})}={\bm{\mu}}_{a}^{(\kappa_{1})}.

Furthermore, since the position
(\kappa_{1},\iota_{1})
in
L_{\bf s}({\bm{\mu}})
is empty,
it is clear that
\CIRCLE_{k}^{\kappa_{1}}({\bm{\mu}},{\bf s})
is on the left side of position
\iota_{1}
.
Using Lemma
3.3.3
(1) again, there are fewer empty positions on the left side of
\CIRCLE_{k}^{\kappa_{1}}({\bm{\mu}},{\bf s})
than
on the left side of
\CIRCLE_{k}^{\kappa_{1}}({\bm{\lambda}},{\bf s})
. We deduce from Lemma
3.1.8
(2) that

(3.3.2)

\displaystyle{\bm{\mu}}_{k}^{(\kappa_{1})}<{\bm{\lambda}}_{k}^{(\kappa_{1})}.

Combining (3.3.1) with (3.3.2) yields

(3.3.3)

\displaystyle\sum^{k}_{a=1}{\bm{\mu}}^{(\kappa_{1})}_{a}<\sum^{k}_{a=1}{\bm{\lambda}}^{(\kappa_{1})}_{a}.

Note that the assumption implies that the rows below
\kappa_{1}
-th row
in
L_{\bf s}({\bm{\lambda}})
are the same as that in
L_{\bf s}({\bm{\mu}})
, and thus for
each
1\leq c<\kappa_{1}
,
{\bm{\lambda}}^{(c)}={\bm{\mu}}^{(c)}
. This gives

(3.3.4)

\displaystyle\sum^{\kappa_{1}-1}_{c=1}|{\bm{\mu}}^{c}|=\sum^{\kappa_{1}-1}_{c=1}|{\bm{\lambda}}^{c}|.

By combining (3.3.3) with (3.3.4), we get

(3.3.5)

\displaystyle\sum^{\kappa_{1}-1}_{c=1}|{\bm{\mu}}^{(c)}|+\sum^{k}_{a=1}{\bm{\mu}}^{(\kappa_{1})}_{a}<\sum^{\kappa_{1}-1}_{c=1}|{\bm{\lambda}}^{(c)}|+\sum^{k}_{a=1}{\bm{\lambda}}^{(\kappa_{1})}_{a}.

On the other hand, let
\CIRCLE_{t}^{\kappa_{2}}({\bm{\mu}},{\bf s})
be the bead at position
(\kappa_{2},\iota_{2})
in
L_{\bf s}({\bm{\mu}})
.
Then by analyzing the number of beads as above, we can obtain the following two formulas:

(1)

{\bm{\lambda}}_{x}^{(\kappa_{2})}={\bm{\mu}}_{x}^{(\kappa_{2})}
for arbitrary
x>t
.

(2)

{\bm{\mu}}_{t}^{(\kappa_{2})}<{\bm{\lambda}}_{t}^{(\kappa_{2})}
.

This implies that
\sum_{x\geq t}{\bm{\mu}}_{x}^{(\kappa_{2})}<\sum_{x\geq t}{\bm{\lambda}}_{x}^{(\kappa_{2})}.

Combing this formula with
\sum^{r}_{c=\kappa_{2}+1}|{\bm{\mu}}^{(c)}|=\sum^{r}_{c=\kappa_{2}+1}|{\bm{\lambda}}^{(c)}|,

which is an easy corollary of the assumption, leads to

(3.3.6)

\displaystyle\sum_{x\geq t}{\bm{\mu}}^{(\kappa_{2})}_{x}+\sum^{r}_{c=\kappa_{2}+1}|{\bm{\mu}}^{(c)}|<\sum_{x\geq t}{\bm{\lambda}}^{(\kappa_{2})}_{x}+\sum^{r}_{c=\kappa_{2}+1}|{\bm{\lambda}}^{(c)}|.

Moreover, it follows from
|{\bm{\lambda}}|=|{\bm{\mu}}|
that

(3.3.7)

\displaystyle\sum^{r}_{c=1}|{\bm{\mu}}^{(c)}|=\sum^{r}_{c=1}|{\bm{\lambda}}^{(c)}|.

Then
(3.3.7)-(3.3.6)
is

\displaystyle\sum^{r}_{c=1}|{\bm{\mu}}^{(c)}|-(\sum_{x\geq t}{\bm{\mu}}^{(\kappa_{2})}_{x}+\sum^{r}_{c=\kappa_{2}+1}|{\bm{\mu}}^{(c)}|)>\sum^{r}_{c=1}|{\bm{\lambda}}^{c}|-(\sum_{x\geq t}{\bm{\lambda}}^{(\kappa_{2})}_{x}+\sum^{r}_{c=\kappa_{2}+1}|{\bm{\lambda}}^{(c)}|).

Note that

\sum^{r}_{c=1}|{\bm{\mu}}^{(c)}|-(\sum_{x\geq t}{\bm{\mu}}^{(\kappa_{2})}_{x}+\sum^{r}_{c=\kappa_{2}+1}|{\bm{\mu}}^{(c)}|)=\sum^{\kappa_{2}-1}_{c=1}|{\bm{\mu}}^{(c)}|+\sum^{t-1}_{x=1}{\bm{\mu}}^{(\kappa_{2})}_{x}

and

\sum^{r}_{c=1}|{\bm{\lambda}}^{c}|-(\sum_{x\geq t}{\bm{\lambda}}^{(\kappa_{2})}_{x}+\sum^{r}_{c=\kappa_{2}+1}|{\bm{\lambda}}^{(c)}|)=\sum^{\kappa_{2}-1}_{c=1}|{\bm{\lambda}}^{(c)}|+\sum^{t-1}_{x=1}{\bm{\lambda}}^{(\kappa_{2})}_{x}.

We arrive at

(3.3.8)

\displaystyle\sum^{\kappa_{2}-1}_{c=1}|{\bm{\mu}}^{(c)}|+\sum^{t-1}_{x=1}{\bm{\mu}}^{(\kappa_{2})}_{x}>\sum^{\kappa_{2}-1}_{c=1}|{\bm{\lambda}}^{(c)}|+\sum^{t-1}_{x=1}{\bm{\lambda}}^{(\kappa_{2})}_{x}.

Now we come to the conclusion
{\bm{\lambda}}\parallel{\bm{\mu}}
by combining (3.3.5) with (3.3.8).
∎

We can deduce a simple fact immediately from Lemma
3.3.4
, that is, two incomparable abaci

L_{\bf s}({\bm{\lambda}})\parallel L_{\bf s}({\bm{\mu}})
with
\kappa_{1}=1
and
\kappa_{2}=r
give
a pair of incomparable
r
-partitions
{\bm{\lambda}}\parallel{\bm{\mu}}
.
Clearly, for
L_{\bf s}({\bm{\lambda}})\parallel L_{\bf s}({\bm{\mu}})
, we can always find

\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{r}
such that
\sigma(\kappa_{1})=1
and
\sigma(\kappa_{2})=r
.
As a result,
L_{{\bf s}^{\sigma}}({\bm{\lambda}}^{\sigma})\parallel L_{{\bf s}^{\sigma}}({\bm{\mu}}^{\sigma})
.

Proposition 3.3.5
.

Suppose that
L_{\bf s}({\bm{\lambda}})\parallel L_{\bf s}({\bm{\mu}})
. Then there exists
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{r}

such that
{\bm{\lambda}}^{\sigma}\parallel{\bm{\mu}}^{\sigma}
.

4.
Moving vectors

The weight can measure how complicated a block is in type A.
However, this is no longer true in the cyclotomic case.
As a result, most results of type A related with weights have no cyclotomic versions. In order to resolve this problem,
we shall introduce a refinement of the weight, which is the so-called moving vector. It is
the key notion of this series of papers.
We shall first give the definition of moving vectors and then study the simple properties.
In Section 4.3 we provide a basic construction, which will be used in the whole series of articles.
Finally, we study incomparable abaci by the language of moving vectors.

4.1.
Definition of moving vectors

We first do some preparation before giving the definition of moving vectors.
It is well-known that for an abacus
\mathcal{L}_{s}^{e}(\lambda)
of a partition
\lambda
,
moving a bead from position
(x,y)
to
(x,y-1)

is equivalent to unwrapping a rim
e
-hook from
[\lambda].

An
r
-version of this operation was introduced by Jacon and Lecouvey.

Definition 4.1.1
.

[
33
, Section 4.1]

Let
{\bm{\lambda}}
be an
r
-partition of rank
n
. An elementary operation in
row
x
on the
(e,{\bf s})
-abacus
L_{\bf s}({\bm{\lambda}})
is a move of one bead from
row
x
to another.

(1)

First kind: if there is a bead at position
(x,y)
with
1\leq x<r
and position
(x+1,y)
is empty,
then move the bead to position
(x+1,y)
.

(2)

Second kind: if
e\neq\infty
and there is a bead at position
(r,y)
and position
(1,y-e)
is empty,
then move the bead to position
(1,y-e)
.

To continue our study, we need to define concepts “before”, “after”
and “between”, which are about relationship between positions in an
r
-abacus.

Definition 4.1.2
.

Given an abacus
L_{\bf s}({\bm{\lambda}})
,
we say position
(j,h)
is before position
(i,l)
if one of the conditions below is satisfied

(1)

h=l
and
i<j\leq r
.

(2)

h=l-(k+1)e
, where
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

If position
(j,h)
is before position
(i,l)
, then we also say position
(i,l)
is after position
(j,h)
.
Let position
(j,h)
be before position
(i,l)
. We say position
(x,y)
is
between positions
(j,h)
and
(i,l)
if it is after
(j,h)
and before
(i,l)
.

According to Definition
4.1.2
, one position is before (after) another one only if both positions are in the same subabcus (see Definition
3.1.11
).
Let us illustrate an example below.

Example 4.1.3
.

Take an abacus as follows.
\cdots
\cdots
\cdots
\cdots
\cdots
\cdots
\cdots
\cdots

Positions
(2,-3)
,
(3,2)
and
(1,2)
are all in the second subabacus.
Positions
(2,-3)
and
(3,2)
are before position
(1,2)
;
positions
(3,2)
and
(1,2)
are after position
(2,-3)
;
position

is between positions
(1,2)
and
(2,-3)
.
Position
(1,-1)
is not in the same subabacus as others.
Then it is neither before nor after any other positions.

We now give a simple result about complete abaci, in which the notions “before” and “after” are used.

Lemma 4.1.4
.

Let
L_{\bf s}({\bm{\lambda}})
be a complete abacus. Then for each subabacus, there exists a bead such that all positions
before it have a bead placed and all positions after it are empty.

Let us list some observations about subabaci for later use.

(1)

None of the positions of
L_{\bf s}({\bm{\lambda}})
belong to two different subabaci simultaneously.

(2)

We can not move a bead from one subabacus to another by elementary operations.

(3)

Index a bead in a subabacus by
x
if there are exactly
x-1
beads after it.
Then elementary operations do not change the index of a bead.

Based on the above observations, elementary operations from
L_{\bf s}({\bm{\lambda}})
to
L_{\bf u}({\bm{\mu}})
is a definite set,
which is independent of the choice of the procedure of moving.
We often call this set the
operation set
and denote it by
\mathcal{F}
.
Moreover, an elementary operation happened in a subabacus does not affect other subabaci.
Therefore, each elementary operation of an abacus
L_{\bf s}({\bm{\lambda}})

can be naturally viewed as an elementary operation of a subabacus.
Given an elementary operation that moves the number
x
bead
at position
(i,l)
, we record it by a triple
[(i,l),\,x]
.
When the index need not to be pointed out, the triple will be written as
[(i,l),\,\ast]
.

The purpose of introducing concept “moving vector” is to study blocks of Ariki-Koike algebras.
Let us recall the definition of the core of a pair
({\bm{\lambda}},{\bf s})
, which introduced by Jacon and Lecouvey.

Definition 4.1.5
.

[
33
, Definition 4.2]

The core of pair
({\bm{\lambda}},{\bf s})
is a pair
({\bm{\lambda}}^{\ast},{\bf s}^{\ast})
,
whose abacus is complete and is obtained from
L_{\bf s}({\bm{\lambda}})
by elementary operations.

The following is an obvious result about operation sets.

Lemma 4.1.6
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be the operation set from
L_{{\bf s}}({\bm{\lambda}})
to its
core with
[(i,h),*]\in\mathcal{F}
,
where
1\leq i\leq r
(if
e=\infty
,
i\neq r
) and
h\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
We have in
L_{\bf s}({\bm{\lambda}})
,

(1)

if position
(i+1,h)
has a bead,
then there is an empty position before it;

(2)

if position
(i,h)
is empty, then there is a position with a bead after it.

An interesting fact about elementary operations is that they are “commute” with Uglov map.

Lemma 4.1.7
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be the operation set from
L_{\bf s}({\bm{\lambda}})
to
L_{\bf u}({\bm{\mu}})
. Then there exists a definite operation set
\tau_{\mathcal{F}}
from
L_{s}(\lambda)
to
L_{u}(\mu)
, where
(\lambda,s)
and
(\mu,u)
are images of
({\bm{\lambda}},{\bf s})
and
({\bm{\mu}},{\bf u})
under Uglov map, respectively, such that the following diagram commute.

\begin{CD}L_{\bf s}({\bm{\lambda}})@>{\tau_{e,r}}>{}>L_{s}(\lambda)\\
@V{\mathcal{F}}V{}V@V{}V{\tau_{\mathcal{F}}}V\\
L_{\bf u}({\bm{\mu}})@>{\tau_{e,r}}>{}>L_{u}(\mu)\end{CD}

Proof.

(Sketch) We only need to consider the case of
\mathcal{F}
containing only one elementary operation
[(x,y),\ast]
. Let
y=ke+c
. It is not difficult to check that if
x<r
, the corresponding elementary operation is moving in
\mathcal{L}_{s}^{e}(\lambda)
the bead in position
(c,r-x+kr)
to
(c,r-x-1+kr)
. If
x=r
, the corresponding elementary operation is moving in
\mathcal{L}_{s}^{e}(\lambda)
the bead in position
(c,kr)
to
(c,kr-1)
. Then it is a routine to check that the diagram commutes.
∎

Operation sets give rise to the definition of moving vectors.

Definition 4.1.8
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be the operation set from
L_{\bf s}({\bm{\lambda}})
to
L_{\bf u}({\bm{\mu}})
.
Define

m_{i}=\sharp\{[(i,h),x]\in\mathcal{F}\mid h\in\mathbb{Z},x\in\mathbb{N^{+}}\}.

Then
\mathcal{M}=(m_{1},m_{2},\dots,m_{r})
is called the moving vector from
L_{\bf s}({\bm{\lambda}})
to
L_{\bf u}({\bm{\mu}})
.
To simplify the description, we sometimes write
m_{r+i}=m_{i}
for
0\leq i<r
.

Let us give an example of operation sets and moving vectors.

Example 4.1.9
.

Let

,
{\bf s}=(0,2,1)
and
{\bm{\lambda}}=((2,1),(3,2),(4,3,1))
.
Then the associated
(e,{\bf s})
-abacus of pair
({\bm{\lambda}},{\bf s})
can be represented as follows.

\cdots
\cdots
\cdots
\cdots
\cdots
\cdots

Let
{\bm{\mu}}=(\varnothing,(4,3,1),(3,2))
and
{\bf v}=(0,1,2)
.
Then the associated
(e,{\bf s})
-abacus
L_{\bf v}({\bm{\mu}})
can be represented as follows.

\cdots
\cdots
\cdots
\cdots

The operation set from

to

and consequently,
\mathcal{M}=(1,2,1)
.

The following lemma reveals that the moving vector from abacus
L_{\bf s}({\bm{\lambda}})
to another one can reflect
variations of multicharge
{\bf s}
and the shape of
L_{\bf s}({\bm{\lambda}})
.
It is a simple corollary of Definition
4.1.8
and
Lemma
3.1.10
. We omit the proof and leave it as an exercise.
Recall Definition
3.1.9
of
\mathfrak{n}_{x}^{k}
.

Lemma 4.1.10
.

Let
\mathcal{M}=(m_{1},m_{2},\dots,m_{r})
be the moving vector from
L_{\bf s}({\bm{\lambda}})

to
L_{\bf u}({\bm{\mu}})
and let
h
be an integer such that all positions
\{(x,y)\mid 1\leq x\leq r,y\leq h\}
in both
L_{\bf s}({\bm{\lambda}})

and
L_{\bf u}({\bm{\mu}})
have a bead placed. Then for
1\leq i\leq r
, we have

\mathfrak{n}_{i}^{h}(L_{{\bf s}}({\bm{\lambda}}))-\mathfrak{n}_{i}^{h}(L_{{\bf u}}({\bm{\mu}}))=m_{i}-m_{i-1}=s_{i}-u_{i}
(the meaning of
m_{0}
is
m_{r}
).

Lemma 4.1.11
.

Let
{\bf s}\in\overline{\mathcal{A}}^{r}_{e}
and
{\bf u},{\bf v}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{r}
.
If the moving vector from
L_{\bf s}({\bm{\lambda}})
to
L_{{\bf v}}({\bm{\nu}})
is equal to that from
L_{{\bf u}}({\bm{\mu}})
to
L_{{\bf v}}({\bm{\nu}})
,
then
{\bf s}={\bf u}
and
({\bm{\lambda}},{\bf s})
and
({\bm{\mu}},{\bf s})
belong to the same block.

Proof.

Let
(m_{1},\dots,m_{r})
be the moving vector from
L_{\bf s}({\bm{\lambda}})
to
L_{\bf v}({\bm{\nu}})
with
m_{1}+\cdots+m_{r}=l
.
Then by Lemma
4.1.10

s_{i}-v_{i}=m_{i}-m_{i-1}=u_{i}-v_{i}

for
1\leq i\leq r
, in which
m_{0}
means
m_{r}
. This implies
{\bf s}={\bf u}
.

Let
\tau_{e,{\bf s}}({\bm{\lambda}})=\lambda,\,\tau_{e,{\bf s}}({\bm{\mu}})=\mu,\,\tau_{e,{\bf v}}({\bm{\nu}})=\nu
and
s=\sum^{r}_{i=1}s_{i}
,
v=\sum^{r}_{i}v_{i}
.
If we need
k
elementary operations from
\mathcal{L}^{e}_{v}(\nu)
to its
e
-core,
then by Lemma
4.1.7
we can obtain this
e
-core from both
\mathcal{L}^{e}_{s}(\lambda)
and

\mathcal{L}^{e}_{s}(\mu)
by
k+l
elementary operations, respectively.
Therefore,
(\lambda,s)
and
(\mu,s)
belong to the same block.
This is equivalent to
\mathcal{C}_{e,s}(\tau_{e,{\bf s}}({\bm{\lambda}}))=\mathcal{C}_{e,s}(\tau_{e,{\bf s}}({\bm{\mu}}))
.
By
[
33
, Corollary 2.23]
,
\mathcal{C}_{e,{\bf s}}({\bm{\lambda}})=\mathcal{C}_{e,{\bf s}}({\bm{\mu}})
.
We deduce from Lemma
3.1.1
that
({\bm{\lambda}},{\bf s})
and
({\bm{\mu}},{\bf s})
belong to the same block.
∎

The significance of a moving vector is that it induces a new block invariant,
which may play a more important role than the weight. Let us prove a lemma for giving the definition.

Lemma 4.1.12
.

Let
({\bm{\lambda}},{\bf s})\in\mathcal{H}^{\bm{\Lambda}}_{\bm{\beta}}
with
{\bf s}\in\overline{\mathcal{A}}^{r}_{e}

and
L_{{\bf s}^{*}}({\bm{\lambda}}^{*})
its core. If
({\bm{\mu}},{\bf s})\in\mathcal{H}^{\bm{\Lambda}}_{\bm{\beta}}
,
then its core is also
L_{{\bf s}^{*}}({\bm{\lambda}}^{*})

and the moving vector from
L_{\bf s}({\bm{\mu}})
to
L_{{\bf s}^{*}}({\bm{\lambda}}^{*})

is equal to that from
L_{\bf s}({\bm{\lambda}})
to
L_{{\bf s}^{*}}({\bm{\lambda}}^{*})
.

Proof.

Since
({\bm{\lambda}},{\bf s})
and
({\bm{\mu}},{\bf s})
are in the same block, we have from
[
33
, Corollary 2.23]

that
(\lambda,s)
and
(\mu,s)
are in the same block and therefore
(\lambda,s)
and
(\mu,s)

have the same
e
-core
(\lambda^{\ast},s^{\ast})
. Note that Uglov map is injective.
This implies that
(\lambda^{\ast},s^{\ast})
has a unique preimage, which is a reduced
(e,{\bf s})
-core by Lemma
4.1.7
.
Consequently, the preimage has to be
L_{{\bf s}^{*}}({\bm{\lambda}}^{*})
.

Suppose that the weight of
\mathcal{H}^{\bm{\Lambda}}_{\bm{\beta}}
is
w
, the moving vector
from
L_{\bf s}({\bm{\lambda}})
to
L_{{\bf s}^{*}}({\bm{\lambda}}^{*})
is
(m_{1},\dots,m_{r})
,
and the moving vector from
L_{\bf s}({\bm{\mu}})
to
L_{{\bf s}^{*}}({\bm{\lambda}}^{*})
is
(m^{\prime}_{1},\dots,m^{\prime}_{r})
.
Then Lemma
4.1.10
implies
m_{i}-m_{i-1}=s_{i}-s^{\ast}_{i}=m^{\prime}_{i}-m^{\prime}_{i-1}
for
1\leq i\leq r
, in which
m_{0}
means
m_{r}
.
Then the result follows from
m_{1}+\cdots+m_{r}=w=m^{\prime}_{1}+\cdots+m^{\prime}_{r}
.
∎

Given a pair
({\bm{\lambda}},{\bf s})\in\mathcal{H}^{\bm{\Lambda}}_{\bm{\beta}}
with
{\bf s}\in\overline{\mathcal{A}}^{r}_{e}
,
in the light of Lemma
4.1.12
, we also say
L_{{\bf s}^{*}}({\bm{\lambda}}^{*})
is the core of block
\mathcal{H}^{\bm{\Lambda}}_{\bm{\beta}}
.
Note that the core of a block depends on the choice of
{\bf s}
, or we can say the core of a block only if
{\bf s}
is fixed.

Fix a dominant weight
{\bm{\Lambda}}
. According to the definition of the action of affine Weyl group
W
, it is not difficult to check that in each
W
-orbit of blocks,
there exists
{\bm{\beta}}\in Q_{+}
such that
({\bm{\alpha}}_{j},{\bm{\Lambda}}-{\bm{\beta}})\geq 0
for all
0\leq j\leq e-1
.

Lemma 4.1.13
.

Let
{\bf s},\,{\bf s}^{*}\in\overline{\mathcal{A}}^{r}_{e}
and
{\bm{\lambda}}
an
r
-partition. Let
L_{{\bf s}^{*}}({\bm{\lambda}}^{*})
be the core of block
\mathcal{H}^{\bm{\Lambda}}_{\bm{\beta}}
. Then
{\bm{\lambda}}^{*}={\bm{\varnothing}}
if and only if
({\bm{\alpha}}_{j},{\bm{\Lambda}}-{\bm{\beta}})\geq 0
for all
0\leq j\leq e-1
.

Proof.

If
{\bm{\lambda}}^{*}={\bm{\varnothing}}
, then
\mathfrak{c}_{l}(L_{{\bf s}^{*}}({\bm{\lambda}}^{*}))\leq\mathfrak{c}_{k}(L_{{\bf s}^{*}}({\bm{\lambda}}^{*}))
for arbitrary
l,k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
with
l\geq k
since
{\bf s}^{*}\in\overline{\mathcal{A}}^{r}_{e}
. As a result,
\mathfrak{m}^{j-1}_{j}\geq 0
for all
0\leq j\leq e-1
. This implies that
({\bm{\alpha}}_{j},{\bm{\Lambda}}-{\bm{\beta}})\geq 0
for all
0\leq j\leq e-1
by Lemma
3.2.8
.

Now assume that
({\bm{\alpha}}_{j},{\bm{\Lambda}}-{\bm{\beta}})\geq 0
for all
0\leq j\leq e-1
. Note that
L_{{\bf s}^{*}}({\bm{\lambda}}^{*})
is complete. Then it is sufficient to prove
\mathfrak{c}_{l}\leq\mathfrak{c}_{l-1}
for arbitrary
l\in{\mathbb{Z}}
to prove
{\bm{\lambda}}^{*}={\bm{\varnothing}}
. This is an easy corollary of Lemmas
4.1.4
and
3.2.8
.
∎

Definition 4.1.14
.

Let
({\bm{\lambda}},{\bf s})\in\mathcal{H}^{\bm{\Lambda}}_{\bm{\beta}}
with
{\bf s}\in\overline{\mathcal{A}}^{r}_{e}
. Then the block moving vector of
\mathcal{H}^{\bm{\Lambda}}_{\bm{\beta}}
is defined to be the moving vector from
L_{\bf s}({\bm{\lambda}})
to its core.

By Lemma
3.2.7
the block moving vector is an invariant of an orbit of blocks.

Lemma 4.1.15
.

Assume that the block moving vector of block
\mathcal{H}^{\bm{\Lambda}}_{\bm{\beta}}
is
(m_{1},m_{2},\dots,m_{r})
. Then
\sum_{i}m_{i}=w(\mathcal{H}^{\bm{\Lambda}}_{\bm{\beta}})
.

Proof.

Let
L_{{\bf s}}({\bm{\lambda}})\in\mathcal{H}^{\bm{\Lambda}}_{\bm{\beta}}
with core
L_{{\bf s}^{*}}({\bm{\lambda}}^{*})
,
and
L_{s}(\lambda)
and
L_{s^{*}}(\lambda^{*})
the images under Uglov map, respectively.
Then by Lemma
4.1.7
there is a bijection between the operation set
\mathcal{F}
from
L_{{\bf s}}({\bm{\lambda}})
to
L_{{\bf s}^{*}}({\bm{\lambda}}^{*})

and the operation set
\tau_{\mathcal{F}}
from
L_{s}(\lambda)
to
L_{s^{*}}(\lambda^{*})
. It is well-known that the number of operations in
\tau_{\mathcal{F}}

is equal to the weight of
L_{s}(\lambda)
. Moreover, we have from
[
33
, Theorem 3.7]
that the weight of
L_{{\bf s}}({\bm{\lambda}})
is equal to that of
L_{s}(\lambda)
.
That is, the weight of
L_{{\bf s}}({\bm{\lambda}})
is equal to the number of operations in set
\mathcal{F}
, and the lemma follows.
∎

4.2.
Simple properties

In this subsection, we study some simple properties of operation sets and moving vectors from three aspects as follows.

Firstly, we study the moving vector related to a simple deformation of abaci.
Given an abacus
L_{{\bf s}}({\bm{\lambda}})
in
\mathcal{H}^{{\bm{\Lambda}}}_{{\bm{\beta}}}
with
{\bf s}\in\overline{\mathcal{A}}^{r}_{e}
and
e<\infty
,
denote by
L_{{\bf u}}({\bm{\mu}})
the abacus obtained by deleting the first
i
rows
and putting
L_{s_{x}+e}({\bm{\lambda}}^{(x)})
,
1\leq x\leq i
on the top in
L_{{\bf s}}({\bm{\lambda}})
.
Let
\mathcal{M}=(m_{1},\dots,m_{r})
and
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}=(m^{\prime}_{1},\dots,m^{\prime}_{r})

be the moving vectors and
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
the operation sets
from

and

to their cores, respectively.
Then we have some easy results as follows.

Lemma 4.2.1
.

The followings hold.

(1)

m^{\prime}_{j}=m_{i+j}

(2)

(5)

{\bf u}\in\overline{\mathcal{A}}^{r}_{e}

Secondly, we consider the composition of certain elementary operations.
The following lemma is a generalization of

, where

Let

.

Let

to

.
Moreover,
[{\bm{\mu}}]
can be obtained by deleting a rim

(2)

such that in

, position

Proof.

It is an evident corollary of Lemma

Finally, we introduce the so-called dual abacus, which in fact appeared in the work of Uglov
[

]
.

Definition 4.2.6
.

Given an abacus
L_{\bf s}({\bm{\lambda}})
, the dual of
L_{\bf s}({\bm{\lambda}})
, denoted by
L_{{\bf s}^{D}}({\bm{\lambda}}^{D})
,
is an abacus, in which position
(i,h)
is empty if and only if in
L_{\bf s}({\bm{\lambda}})
,
there is a bead at position
(r-i+1,-h-1)
. Furthermore,
({\bm{\lambda}}^{D},{\bf s}^{D})
is called
the dual pair of
({\bm{\lambda}},{\bf s})
.

The following two lemmas about dual abaci are easy.

Lemma 4.2.7
.

Let
L_{{\bf s}^{D}}({\bm{\lambda}}^{D})
be the dual of
L_{{\bf s}}({\bm{\lambda}})
. Then
{\bm{\lambda}}^{D}={\bm{\lambda}}^{\prime}
,
s_{i}^{D}=-s_{r-i+1}
for
1\leq i\leq r

and consequently, if
{\bf s}\in\overline{\mathcal{A}}^{r}_{e}
, then
{\bf s}^{D}\in\overline{\mathcal{A}}^{r}_{e}
and if
{\bf s}\in\mathcal{A}^{r}_{e}
, then
{\bf s}^{D}\in\mathcal{A}^{r}_{e}
.

Lemma 4.2.8
.

Let
({\bm{\lambda}},{\bf s})
and
({\bm{\mu}},{\bf s})
be two pairs. Then

(1)

({\bm{\lambda}},{\bf s})
and
({\bm{\mu}},{\bf s})
belong to the same block
if and only if
({\bm{\lambda}}^{D},{\bf s}^{D})
and
({\bm{\mu}}^{D},{\bf s}^{D})
belong to the same block.

(2)

L_{{\bf s}}({\bm{\lambda}})\parallel L_{{\bf s}}({\bm{\mu}})
if and only if
L_{{\bf s}^{D}}({\bm{\lambda}}^{D})\parallel L_{{\bf s}^{D}}({\bm{\mu}}^{D})
.

We end this subsection by a lemma as follows.

Lemma 4.2.9
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
,
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
,
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
be the operation sets and moving vectors
from
L_{{\bf s}}({\bm{\lambda}})
and
L_{{\bf s}^{D}}({\bm{\lambda}}^{D})
to their cores, respectively.
Then
[(i,h),*]\in\mathcal{F}
if and only if
[(r-i,-h-1),*]\in\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
.
Moreover,
m_{i}=m^{\prime}_{r-i+1}
for all
1\leq i\leq r
.

Proof.

We first consider operation sets. It is clear by Definition
4.2.6
that we only need to prove
if
[(i,h),*]\in\mathcal{F}
, then
[(r-i,-h-1),*]\in\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
.
In fact, take an element
[(i,h),*]\in\mathcal{F}
such that
in
L_{\bf s}({\bm{\lambda}})
, position is empty and position
(i,h)
has a bead.
Then in
L_{{\bf s}^{D}}({\bm{\lambda}}^{D})
, position
(r-i,-h-1)
has a bead and position
(r-i+1,-h-1)
is empty.
This implies
[(r-i,-h-1),*]\in\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
. By moving the bead at position
(i,h)
to position
(i+1,h)
in
L_{\bf s}({\bm{\lambda}})
,
and moving the bead at position
(r-i,-h-1)
to position
(r-i+1,-h-1)
in
L_{{\bf s}^{D}}({\bm{\lambda}}^{D})
, we clearly get two dual abaci.
Since
\mathcal{F}
is a finite set, by repeating this process finite many times,
abacus
L_{{\bf s}}({\bm{\lambda}})
arrives to its core.
As a direct corollary, we can obtain

m_{i}=m^{\prime}_{r-i+1}
for all
1\leq i\leq r
.
∎

4.3.
A construction

We will construct an abacus with block moving vector being
\mathcal{M}
whenever
\mathcal{M}
satisfies certain conditions.
This construction will be used in the whole series of papers. Let us consider a special case first.

Lemma 4.3.1
.

Let
r\geq 2
and
{\bf s},{\bf s}^{\ast}\in\overline{\mathcal{A}}^{r}_{e}
. If
\mathcal{M}=(m_{1},\dots,m_{r-1},0)\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
satisfies
s_{i}^{\ast}=s_{i}-m_{i}+m_{i-1}
for
1\leq i\leq r
, then there exists an
r
-partition
{\bm{\lambda}}
such that the moving vector from
L_{\bf s}({\bm{\lambda}})
to
L_{{\bf s}^{\ast}}({\bm{\varnothing}})
is
\mathcal{M}
.

Proof.

For later use let us first point out a simple fact:
\sum s_{i}=\sum s_{i}^{\ast}
.

We shall prove the lemma by induction. Let us verify the basic step at
r=2
. By conditions
s_{i}^{\ast}=s_{i}-m_{i}+m_{i-1}
and
m_{2}=0
, we get
s_{2}^{\ast}-s_{1}^{\ast}=s_{2}-s_{1}+2m_{1}
. Note that
{\bf s}\in\overline{\mathcal{A}}^{r}_{e}
implies that
s_{2}\geq s_{1}
. Therefore,
s_{2}^{\ast}-s_{1}^{\ast}\geq 2m_{1}
. Move in
L_{{\bf s}^{\ast}}({\bm{\varnothing}})
the beads
\CIRCLE_{1}^{2},\dots,\CIRCLE_{m_{1}}^{2}
to positions under them in row 1, respectively. Denote by
L_{\bf u}({\bm{\lambda}})
the new abacus. It is not difficult to check that
u_{1}=s_{1}^{\ast}+m_{1}=s_{1}
and
u_{2}=s_{2}^{\ast}-m_{1}=s_{2}
, that is,
{\bf u}={\bf s}
. The moving vector from
L_{\bf s}({\bm{\lambda}})
to
L_{{\bf s}^{\ast}}({\bm{\varnothing}})
is
(m_{1},0)
and the basic step is proved.

Suppose inductively that the lemma holds for
r=k-1
. Since
\sum_{i=1}^{k}s_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}s_{i}^{\ast}
, there exists
1\leq j\leq k
such that
s_{1}\leq s_{j}^{\ast}
. Assume that
j
is the least one. We consider two cases according to the value of
j
.

If
j=1
, then
m_{1}
is forced to be zero. Denote by
\underline{{\bf s}}=(s_{2},\dots,s_{k})
and
\underline{{\bf s}}^{\ast}=(s_{2}^{\ast},\dots,s_{k}^{\ast})
. By the inductive hypothesis there exists a
k-1
-partition
{\bm{\nu}}
such that the moving vector from
L_{\underline{{\bf s}}}({\bm{\nu}})
to
L_{\underline{{\bf s}}^{\ast}}({\bm{\varnothing}})
is
\underline{\mathcal{M}}=(m_{2},\dots,m_{k-1},0)
. Consequently, the moving vector from
L_{{\bf s}}(\varnothing,{\bm{\nu}})
to
L_{{\bf s}^{\ast}}({\bm{\varnothing}})
is
\mathcal{M}
.

If
j>1
, by the definition of
j
we know that
s_{i}^{\ast}<s_{1}
for all
1\leq i<j
. This yields that
s_{j-1}^{\ast}-s_{1}^{\ast}<m_{1}
. On the other hand, it follows from
s_{1}\leq s_{j}^{\ast}
, or
s_{1}-s_{j-1}^{\ast}\leq s_{j}^{\ast}-s_{j-1}^{\ast}
that is,
m_{1}-s_{j-1}^{\ast}+s_{1}^{\ast}\leq s_{j}^{\ast}-s_{j-1}^{\ast}
. This makes the following practicable. Move in
L_{{\bf s}^{\ast}}({\bm{\varnothing}})
the beads
\CIRCLE_{1}^{i},\dots,\CIRCLE_{s_{i}^{\ast}-s_{i-1}^{\ast}}^{i}
for all
2\leq i<j
and
\CIRCLE_{1}^{j},\dots,\CIRCLE_{m_{1}-s_{j-1}^{\ast}+s_{1}^{\ast}}^{j}
to positions under them in row 1, respectively. Denote the new abacus by
L_{{\bf u}}({\bm{\mu}})
. Clearly,
\underline{{\bm{\mu}}}=(\mu^{(2)},\dots,\mu^{(k)})={\bm{\varnothing}}
, and the moving vector from
L_{{\bf u}}({\bm{\mu}})
to
L_{{\bf s}^{\ast}}({\bm{\varnothing}})
is
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}=(m_{1}^{\prime},\dots,m_{k}^{\prime})
, where

m_{i}^{\prime}=\begin{cases}m_{1}-(s_{i}^{\ast}-s_{1}^{\ast}),&\text{if}\,\,1\leq i<j;\\
0,&\text{if}\,\,j\leq i\leq k.\end{cases}

We claim that
m_{i}^{\prime}\leq m_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq k
. Clearly,
m_{1}=m_{1}^{\prime}
and
0=m_{i}^{\prime}\leq m_{i}
for
j\leq i\leq k
. Moreover, for
2\leq i<j
we have
m_{i}-m_{i}^{\prime}=m_{i}-m_{1}+s_{i}^{\ast}-s_{1}^{\ast}=s_{i}-s_{1}+m_{i-1}.
Note that
{\bf s}\in\overline{\mathcal{A}}^{k}_{e}
and thus
s_{1}\leq s_{i}
. This completes the proof of the claim about
\mathcal{M^{\prime}}
.

Let us study
{\bf u}
now. We shall prove

u_{i}=\begin{cases}s_{1},&\text{if}\,\,i=1;\\
s_{i-1}^{\ast},&\text{if}\,\,2\leq i<j;\\
s_{j}^{\ast}+s_{j-1}^{\ast}-m_{1}-s_{1}^{\ast},&\text{if}\,\,i=j;\\
s_{i}^{\ast},&\text{if}\,\,j<i\leq k.\end{cases}

In fact, by Lemma
4.1.10
we have
u_{i}=s_{i}^{\ast}+m_{i}^{\prime}-m_{i-1}^{\prime}
for
1\leq i\leq k
. Then
u_{1}=s_{1}^{\ast}+m_{1}^{\prime}=s_{1}-m_{1}+m_{1}^{\prime}=s_{1}
and since
m_{j}^{\prime}=0
, we have

u_{j}=s_{j}^{\ast}-m_{1}+s_{j-1}^{\ast}-s_{1}^{\ast}.

For
2\leq i<j
,

u_{i}=s_{i}^{\ast}+m_{1}-(s_{i}^{\ast}-s_{1}^{\ast})-(m_{1}-(s_{i-1}^{\ast}-s_{1}^{\ast}))=s_{i-1}^{\ast}.

It is clear that
u_{i}=s_{i}^{\ast}
for
j<i\leq k
. So far, all cases have been considered.

Based on the above result we can prove
\underline{{\bf u}}\in\overline{\mathcal{A}}^{k-1}_{e}
now. In fact,
because
s_{j}^{\ast}-m_{1}-s_{1}^{\ast}=s_{j}^{\ast}-s_{1}\geq 0
, we get
u_{j}\geq s_{j-1}^{\ast}
and consequently

u_{j}\geq s_{j-2}^{\ast}=u_{j-1}
.
Note that
s_{j-1}^{\ast}-m_{1}-s_{1}^{\ast}=s_{j-1}^{\ast}-s_{1}<0
. This implies that
u_{j}<s_{j}^{\ast}
. If
j=k
, then
u_{k}<s_{1}^{\ast}+e=u_{2}+e
because
s_{j}^{\ast}\leq s_{1}^{\ast}+e
, and
\underline{{\bf u}}\in\overline{\mathcal{A}}^{k-1}_{e}
follows. If
j<k
, then
u_{j}<s_{j+1}^{\ast}=u_{j+1}
.
Moreover,
u_{k}=s_{k}^{\ast}\leq s_{1}^{\ast}+e=u_{2}+e
. We obtain
\underline{{\bf u}}\in\overline{\mathcal{A}}^{k-1}_{e}
too.

Define
\mathcal{M}^{\prime\prime}=\mathcal{M}-\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
. Then
s_{i}^{\ast}+m_{i}^{\prime}-m_{i-1}^{\prime}=s_{i}-m_{i}^{\prime\prime}+m_{i-1}^{\prime\prime}
for
1\leq i\leq k
. As a result,
u_{i}=s_{i}-m_{i}^{\prime\prime}+m_{i-1}^{\prime\prime}
for
1\leq i\leq k
.
Write
(m_{2}^{\prime\prime},\dots,m_{k-1}^{\prime\prime},0)
as
\underline{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime\prime}
. Then by the inductive hypothesis there exists a
k-1
-partition
{\bm{\nu}}
such that the moving vector from
L_{\underline{{\bf s}}}({\bm{\nu}})
to
L_{\underline{{\bf u}}}({\bm{\varnothing}})
is
\underline{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime\prime}
. Note that
s_{1}=u_{1}
,
m_{1}=m_{1}^{\prime}
and
\underline{{\bm{\mu}}}={\bm{\varnothing}}
. Define
{\bm{\lambda}}=(\mu^{(1)},{\bm{\nu}})
. We can derive that the moving vector from
L_{{\bf s}}({\bm{\lambda}})
to
L_{{\bf u}}({\bm{\mu}})
is
\mathcal{M}^{\prime\prime}
. This implies that the moving vector from
L_{{\bf s}}({\bm{\lambda}})
to
L_{{\bf s}^{\ast}}({\bm{\varnothing}})
is
\mathcal{M}
.
∎

By using Lemma
4.3.1
we can obtain the general construction.

Lemma 4.3.2
.

Let
r\geq 2
and
{\bf s},{\bf s}^{\ast}\in\overline{\mathcal{A}}^{r}_{e}
. If
\mathcal{M}=(m_{1},\dots,m_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
satisfies
s_{i}^{\ast}=s_{i}-m_{i}+m_{i-1}
for
1\leq i\leq r
, then there exists an
r
-partition
{\bm{\lambda}}
such that the moving vector from
L_{\bf s}({\bm{\lambda}})
to
L_{{\bf s}^{\ast}}({\bm{\varnothing}})
is
\mathcal{M}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
m_{i}
is a minimal in
\{m_{1},\dots,m_{r}\}
.
Define

{\bf s}^{\prime}=(s_{1}^{\prime},\dots,s_{r}^{\prime})=(s_{i+1}-e,\dots,s_{r}-e,\,s_{1},\dots,s_{i}),

{\bf s}^{\ast^{\prime}}=(s_{1}^{\ast^{\prime}},\dots,s_{r}^{\ast^{\prime}})=(s_{i+1}^{\ast}-e,\dots,s_{r}^{\ast}-e,\,s_{1}^{\ast},\dots,s_{i}^{\ast})

and

\mathcal{M^{\prime}}=(m_{1}^{\prime},\dots,m_{r}^{\prime})=(m_{i+1}-m_{i},\dots,m_{r}-m_{i},\,m_{1}-m_{i},\dots,m_{i-1}-m_{i},\,0).

Clearly,
{\bf s}^{\prime},{\bf s}^{\ast^{\prime}}\in\overline{\mathcal{A}}^{r}_{e}
and
s_{x}^{\ast^{\prime}}=s_{x}^{\prime}-m_{x}^{\prime}+m_{x-1}^{\prime}
for
1\leq x\leq r
.
Then by Lemma
4.3.1
there exists an
r
-partition
{\bm{\mu}}
such that the moving vector from
L_{{\bf s}^{\prime}}({\bm{\mu}})
to
L_{{\bf s}^{\ast^{\prime}}}({\bm{\varnothing}})
is
\mathcal{M^{\prime}}
. Assume that the bead
\CIRCLE_{1}^{1}
in
L_{{\bf s}^{\prime}}({\bm{\mu}})
is in column
h
.
Move in
L_{{\bf s}^{\prime}}({\bm{\mu}})
the bead to position
(1,h+m_{i}e)
and denote the new abacus by
L_{{\bf s}^{\prime}}(\overline{{\bm{\lambda}}})
.
Let
{\bm{\lambda}}=(\overline{{\bm{\lambda}}}^{(r-i+1)},\dots,\,\overline{{\bm{\lambda}}}^{(r)},\,\overline{{\bm{\lambda}}}^{(1)},\dots,\,\overline{{\bm{\lambda}}}^{(r-i)})
. Then the moving vector from
L_{\bf s}({\bm{\lambda}})
to
L_{{\bf s}^{\ast}}({\bm{\varnothing}})
is
\mathcal{M}
.
∎

Remarks 4.3.3
.

In fact, Lemma
4.3.2
still holds if we replace
{\bm{\varnothing}}
by an
r
-partition
{\bm{\lambda}}^{\ast}
with
L_{{\bf s}^{\ast}}({\bm{\lambda}}^{\ast})
being complete.

6.
Derived equivalence of blocks

It is an open problem to give a necessary and sufficient condition for two
blocks of Ariki-Koike algebras being derived equivalent.
In this section, we give examples of blocks with the same weight associated with the same multicharge that are not derived equivalent and examples
of derived equivalent blocks being in different orbits under the adjoint action of the affine Weyl group
by using the theory developed in this paper.

Note that we can easily find two blocks with the same weights, one of them being infinite type and the other
being Morita equivalent to a truncated polynomial ring, which has finite representation type.
So our discussion will center on constructing examples of derived equivalent blocks being in different orbits.

We first give a necessary and sufficient condition for two weight one blocks (Morita equivalent to Brauer tree algebras) being derived equivalent.
Note that the problem of derived equivalence of Brauer tree algebras is completely resolved in
[
47
]
.
We will translate the results
[
7
, Theorem 6.8]
and
[
47
, Theorem 4.2]

into the language of the so-called subabacus moving vector of a block.

Definition 6.0.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be the operation set from
L_{s}({\bm{\lambda}})\in\mathcal{H}^{\Lambda}_{{\bm{\beta}}}
to its core.
Define
\mathcal{W_{{\bm{\lambda}}}}=(w_{0{\bm{\lambda}}},w_{1{\bm{\lambda}}},\dots,w_{e-1{\bm{\lambda}}})
,
where
w_{i{\bm{\lambda}}}=\sharp\{[(x,h),\ast]\in\mathcal{F}\mid 1\leq x\leq r,\,h\equiv i\,({\rm mod}\,e)\}.

Then
\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{H}^{\Lambda}_{{\bm{\beta}}})=(w_{0},w_{1},\dots,w_{e-1})=\sum_{({\bm{\lambda}},{\bf s})\in\mathcal{H}^{\Lambda}_{{\bm{\beta}}}}\mathcal{W_{{\bm{\lambda}}}}
is
called the subabacus moving vector of block
\mathcal{H}^{\Lambda}_{{\bm{\beta}}}
.

Given two pairs
({\bm{\lambda}},{\bf s})
and
({\bm{\mu}},{\bf u})
with
{\bf s},{\bf u}\in\overline{\mathcal{A}}^{r}_{e}

and
({\bm{\lambda}}^{\ast},{\bf s}^{\ast})
and
({\bm{\mu}}^{\ast},{\bf u}^{\ast})
the corresponding cores, respectively.
Denote by
B_{{\bm{\lambda}},{\bf s}}
and
B_{{\bm{\mu}},{\bf u}}
the blocks containing
({\bm{\lambda}},{\bf s})
and
({\bm{\mu}},{\bf u})
, respectively.
Suppose that
w(B_{{\bm{\lambda}},{\bf s}})=w(B_{{\bm{\mu}},{\bf u}})=1
.

Proposition 6.0.2
.

Blocks
B_{{\bm{\lambda}},{\bf s}}
and
B_{{\bm{\mu}},{\bf u}}
are derived equivalent
if and only if the number of non-zero components in
\mathcal{W}(B_{{\bm{\lambda}},{\bf s}})

is equal to that of
\mathcal{W}(B_{{\bm{\mu}},{\bf u}})
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{M}=(m_{1},m_{2},\dots,m_{r})
be the block moving vector of
B_{{\bm{\lambda}},{\bf s}}

with
m_{j}=1
and
m_{i}=0
for all
1\leq i\leq r
,
i\neq j
.
Assume that
s_{j+1}-s_{j}=a
(
s_{r+1}
is defined to be
s_{1}+e
if
e<\infty
).
We claim that the number of
r
-partitions in
B_{{\bm{\lambda}},{\bf s}}
is
a+2
.
In fact, if
j<r
, we have from Lemma
4.1.10
that
s^{*}_{j+1}=s_{j+1}+1
and
s^{*}_{j}=s_{j}-1

and thus
s^{*}_{j}+a+2=s^{*}_{j+1}
. It follows from Lemma
3.1.16
(4) that
there exist
h_{1},\dots,h_{a+2}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
such that in
L_{{\bf s}^{*}}({\bm{\lambda}}^{*})
,
positions
(j,h_{1}),\dots,(j,h_{a+2})
are empty and
positions
(j+1,h_{1}),\dots,(j+1,h_{a+2})
have a bead placed.
Let
h
be an integer such that all positions
(x,y)
in
L_{{\bf s}^{*}}({\bm{\lambda}}^{*})
are occupied by a
bead, where
1\leq x\leq r
and
y\leq h
. In the light of Lemma
3.1.10
,
\mathfrak{n}^{h}_{j+1}-\mathfrak{n}^{h}_{j}=a+2
.
Note that
L_{{\bf s}^{*}}({\bm{\lambda}}^{*})
is complete. This implies that
if position
(j,k)
is empty and position
(j+1,k)
has a bead,
then
k\in\{h_{1},\dots,h_{a+2}\}
.
Because
w(B_{{\bm{\lambda}},{\bf s}})=1
, this forces each abacus of a pair in
B_{{\bm{\lambda}},{\bf s}}

has to be obtained by moving in
L_{{\bf s}^{*}}({\bm{\lambda}}^{*})
the bead at position
(j+1,k)
to position
(j,k)

with
k\in\{h_{1},\dots,h_{a+2}\}

can be transformed into the case

Combining
[
24

[
7

gives that

is Morita equivalent to a Brauer tree algebra, whose Brauer tree has

Moreover, by Lemma
3.1.16

for all

This implies that for arbitrary pair

By the same reason, block

[
47

of Brauer tree algebras are determined by the number of edges and the multiplicity of the exceptional vertex.
Then the proposition follows.
∎

7.
Blocks of cyclotomic
q
-Schur algebra

We end our paper by a remark on representation type of blocks of a cyclotomic
q
-Schur algebra.

Combining Theorem A with Theorem B in
[
5
]
gives that a block of an Iwahori-Hecke algebra of type B
has finite representation type if and only if its weight is not more than one.
This implies that the representation type of a block of the cyclotomic
q
-Schur algebra
associated to a type B Iwahori-Hecke algebra is finite if and only if its weight is not more than one.
On the other hand, in
[
50
, Corollary 3.20]
, Wada proved that under certain conditions
any block of
\mathcal{S}_{n,r}(q,Q_{1},Q_{2},\dots,Q_{r})
is Morita equivalent to a
certain block of
\mathcal{S}_{n^{\prime},2}(q,Q_{i},Q_{j})
for some
i,j\in\{1,2,\dots,r\}
.
By using
Theorem
, we can give a direct result under no conditions and without using the Morita equivalence mentioned above.

We point out that if a block
B
of
\mathcal{H}_{n}(q,Q)
is Morita equivalent to a truncated polynomial ring,
then the Auslander algebra (see
[
12
]
for definition) of
B
is just the corresponding block of
\mathcal{S}_{n,r}
.
It is well-known that the Auslander algebra of
K[x]/(x^{i})
has finite representation type if and only if
0<i<4
.
Let
\mathcal{B}
be a block of
\mathcal{S}_{n,r}
and
B
the corresponding block in
\mathcal{H}_{n}(q,Q)
.
Then by
Theorem
, we get

(1)

If
w(\mathcal{B})>2
, then
\mathcal{B}
has infinite representation type.

(2)

If
w(\mathcal{B})<2
, then
\mathcal{B}
has finite representation type.

(3)

If
w(\mathcal{B})=2
, then
\mathcal{B}
has finite type if and only if
B
has finite type.

According to the above result,
w(\mathcal{B})=1
is only a sufficient condition for
\mathcal{B}

being of finite representation type.

A single-sided all-at-once preconditioning for linear system from a non-local evolutionary equation with weakly singular kernels
Xuelei Lin, Jiamei Dong, Sean Hon

Abstract
In [X. L. Lin, M. K. Ng, and Y. Zhi. J. Comput. Phys. , 434 (2021), pp. 110221] and [Y. L. Zhao, J. Wu, X. M. Gu, and H. Li. Comput. Math. Appl. , 148(2023), pp. 200–210], two-sided preconditioning techniques are proposed for non-local evolutionary equations, which possesses (i) mesh-size independent theoretical bound of condition number of the two-sided preconditioned matrix; (ii) small and stable iteration numbers in numerical tests. In this paper, we modify the two-sided preconditioning by multiplying the left-sided and the right-sided preconditioners together as a single-sided preconditioner. Such a single-sided preconditioner essentially derives from approximating the spatial matrix with a fast diagonalizable matrix and keeping the temporal matrix unchanged. Clearly, the matrix-vector multiplication of the single-sided preconditioning is faster to compute than that of the two-sided one, since the single-sided preconditioned matrix has a simpler structure. More importantly, we show theoretically that the single-sided preconditioned generalized minimal residual (GMRES) method has a convergence rate no worse than the two-sided preconditioned one. As a result, the one-sided preconditioned GMRES solver requires less computational time than the two-sided preconditioned GMRES solver in total. Numerical results are reported to show the efficiency of the proposed single-sided preconditioning technique.



Key words:
Preconditioning, Krylov subspace, all-at-once system, Toeplitz matrix, parallel-in-time

Due to the nonlocal properties of the fractional differential operators, linear systems arising from these schemes are usually dense, which makes direct solvers like Gaussian elimination time-consuming and thus impractical to use. Fortunately, due to the shift-invariant kernel involved in the fractional differential operators, the dense matrices have Toeplitz-like structure, whose matrix-vector multiplications can be fast implemented by fast Fourier transforms (FFTs). Based on the fast matrix-vector multiplication, iterative solvers are developed for solving the linear systems.

The solvers for the linear systems are mainly classified as two types: time-stepping type and all-at-once type. The time-stepping solvers solve the linear system in a time-sequential manner (i.e., the unknowns at
n

36

Recently, new all-at-once type methods are studied in
[
37
,
21
]

\tau

[

) and the temporal discretization matrix keeps unchanged. Then, an inverse of the square root of the
\tau

\tau

The condition numbers of the two-sided preconditioned matrices are proven to be uniformly bounded by constants independent of discretization step sizes; see, e.g.,
[
37
,
21
]
. Numerical results in
[
37
,
21
]
have shown the efficiency of the two-sided preconditioning method in terms of small and stable iteration numbers.

Nevertheless, we find that the two-sided splitting is not necessary. In this paper, we show theoretically that the GMRES solver for the single-sided preconditioned system converges no slower than the GMRES solver for the two-sided preconditioned system.

Compared with the two-sided preconditioned system, solving the problem on the single-sided preconditioned system has two advantages: (i) computing the matrix-vector product associated with the single-sided preconditioned matrix requires less operations and is easier to implement (i.e., the code is simpler); (ii) the single-sided preconditioning is more flexible in the sense that the
\tau
matrix can be replaced by other spatial approximations without a fast computable square root.

Indeed, the numerical results in the later section further show that the single-sided preconditioning method is more efficient than the two-sided preconditioning method in terms of computational time and iteration numbers, which supports our theoretical results.

The contribution of this paper is twofold. Firstly, we significantly modify the two-sided preconditioning method by simplifying it to the single-sided preconditioning method that is easier to implement and requires less computational time. Secondly, the paper is not just focused on a single spatial discretization scheme. Actually, the theoretical results are established on common properties shared by several spatial discretization schemes. Hence, the proposed preconditioning method can be used to efficiently solve several discrete problems arising from several spatial discretization schemes in the literature, which demonstrates the range of its applicability.

For a set

and a point

Lemma 4

For any

and any

\sigma(\tau({\bf W}_{m}^{(\beta)})^{-1}{\bf W}_{m}^{(\beta)})\subset(1/2,3/2)

Proof:

as
{\bf H}_{m}^{(\beta)}=[h_{ij}]_{i,j=1}^{m}

h_{ij}=\begin{cases}w_{i+j}^{(\beta)},\quad i+j<m-1,\\
w_{2m+2-(i+j)}^{(\beta)},\quad i+j>m+1,\\

By Property
1
{\bf(i)}

h_{ij}\leq 0,\quad 1\leq i,j\leq m.

(3.11)

Denote

{\bf(i)}
,
{\bf(iii)}

p_{ij}=\begin{cases}w_{0}^{(\beta)}-h_{ii}>0,\quad i=j,\\
w_{|i-j|}^{(\beta)}-w_{i+j}^{(\beta)}\leq 0,\quad i+j<m-1{\rm~and~}i\neq j,\\
w_{|i-j|}^{(\beta)}-w_{2m+2-(i+j)}^{(\beta)}\leq 0,\quad i+j>m+1{\rm~and~}i\neq j,\\
w_{|i-j|}^{(\beta)}\leq 0,\quad{\rm otherwise}.\end{cases}

(3.12)

Let

be an eigen-pair of
\tau({\bf W}_{m}^{(\beta)})^{-1}{\bf H}_{m}^{(\beta)}

{\bf H}_{m}^{(\beta)}z=\lambda\tau({\bf W}_{m}^{(\beta)})z.

(3.13)

Rewrite

(
3.13

\sum_{j=1}^{m}h_{ij}z_{j}=\lambda\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{ij}z_{j}.

Hence,

\lambda p_{ii}z_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}h_{ij}z_{j}-\lambda\sum_{j=1,i\neq j}^{m}p_{ij}z_{j},\quad i=1,2,...,m.

As
||z||_{\infty}=1

such that
|z_{k_{0}}|=1
. Then,

|\lambda||p_{kk}|\leq\sum_{j=1}^{m}|h_{kj}|+|\lambda|\sum_{j=1,k\neq j}^{m}|p_{kj}|.

(3.14)

By (
3.12

{\bf(ii)}
, we have

\displaystyle|p_{kk}|-\sum_{j=1,j\neq k}^{m}|p_{kj}|-2\sum_{j=1}^{m}|h_{kj}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(w_{0}^{(\beta)}-h_{kk})-\sum_{j=1,j\neq k}^{m}(h_{kj}-w_{|k-j|}^{(\beta)})+2\sum_{j=1}^{m}h_{kj}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle w_{0}^{(\beta)}+\sum_{j=1,j\neq k}^{m}w_{|k-j|}^{(\beta)}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}h_{kj}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle w_{0}^{(\beta)}+\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}w_{j}^{(\beta)}+\sum_{j=1}^{m-k}w_{j}^{(\beta)}\right)+\left(\sum_{j=k+1}^{m-1}w_{j}^{(\beta)}+\sum_{j=m-k+2}^{m-1}w_{j}^{(\beta)}\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle w_{0}^{(\beta)}+2\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}w_{j}^{(\beta)}>0,

which combined with (

|\lambda|\leq\frac{\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}|h_{kj}|}{|p_{kk}|-\sum\limits_{j=1,k\neq j}^{m}|p_{kj}|}\leq\frac{\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}|h_{kj}|}{2\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}|h_{kj}|}=\frac{1}{2}.

\sigma(\tau({\bf W}_{m}^{(\beta)})^{-1}{\bf H}_{m}^{(\beta)})\subset(-1/2,1/2).

Since

which means
\sigma(\tau({\bf W}_{m}^{(\beta)})^{-1}{\bf W}_{m}^{(\beta)})=1+\sigma(\tau({\bf W}_{m}^{(\beta)})^{-1}{\bf H}_{m}^{(\beta)})\subset(1/2,3/2)

4
The Implementation

In this section, we propose a fast implementation of the Krylov subspace solver for solving the preconditioned system (
2.3
). It suffices to implement the underlying matrix-vector product efficiently to implement a Krylov subspace solver. In other words, we will discuss in this section how to efficiently compute a matrix-vector product
{\bf P}^{-1}{\bf A}{\bf v}
for an arbitrarily given vector
{\bf v}
.

In what follows, we discuss the fast computation of a matrix-vector product
\hat{\bf v}={\bf P}^{-1}{\bf A}{\bf v}
for a given vector
{\bf v}\in\mathbb{R}^{NJ\times 1}
. The computation of
\hat{\bf v}
can be divided into two steps.

\displaystyle{\rm Step~1}:\quad{\rm Compute~}\dot{\bf v}={\bf A}{\bf v},

(4.1)

\displaystyle{\rm Step~2}:\quad{\rm Compute~}\hat{\bf v}={\bf P}^{-1}\dot{\bf v}.

(4.2)

In step (
4.1
), notice that
\dot{\bf v}={\bf A}{\bf v}=({\bf B}\otimes{\bf I}_{N}){\bf v}+({\bf I}_{J}\otimes{\bf T}){\bf v}
. It is clear that
{\bf B}
and
{\bf T}
are both (multilevel) Toeplitz matrices. Hence, the computation of (
4.1
) requires
\mathcal{O}(JN\log(JN))
flops.

To see the fast implementation of step (
4.2
), we will utilize a block diagonalization form of
{\bf P}^{-1}
. By Equation (
3.6
) and Lemma
1
, we know that

{\bf B}_{\tau}={\bf S}\tilde{\bf\Lambda}{\bf S},\quad\tilde{\bf\Lambda}=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}{\bf\Lambda}\succ{\bf O}.

(4.3)

From the definition of
{\bf S}
, we know that
{\bf S}
is real symmetric and orthogonal, i.e.,
{\bf S}^{-1}={\bf S}^{\rm T}={\bf S}
.

By Equation (
4.3
) and (
3.8
), we know that

{\bf P}^{-1}=({\bf S}\otimes{\bf I}_{N})({\bf I}_{J}\otimes{\bf T}+\tilde{\bf\Lambda}\otimes{\bf I}_{N})^{-1}({\bf S}\otimes{\bf I}_{N}).

(4.4)

Thus the matrix-vector product
\hat{\bf v}={\bf P}^{-1}\dot{\bf v}
for a given vector
{\bf v}\in\mathbb{R}^{NJ\times 1}
can be fast computed as following stps,

\displaystyle{\rm Step~2.1}:\quad{\rm Compute~}\ddot{\bf v}=({\bf S}\otimes{\bf I}_{N})\dot{\bf v}.

\displaystyle{\rm Step~2.2}:\quad{\rm Compute~}\dddot{\bf v}=({\bf I}_{J}\otimes{\bf T}+\tilde{\bf\Lambda}\otimes{\bf I}_{N})^{-1}\ddot{\bf v}.

\displaystyle{\rm Step~2.3}:\quad{\rm Compute~}\hat{\bf v}=({\bf S}\otimes{\bf I}_{N})\dddot{\bf v}.

Steps 2.1, 2.3 can be fast implemented by multi-dimension fast sine transform, which requires
\mathcal{O}(NJ\log J)
operations.
It remains to discuss the computation of step 2.2. Rewrite
\tilde{\bf\Lambda}
in (
4.3
) as

\tilde{\bf\Lambda}=\mathrm{diag}(\tilde{\lambda}_{i})_{i=1}^{J}.

Clearly,
\tilde{\lambda}_{i}
’s (
i=1,2,...,J
) are all positive numbers. Then, from Step 2.2, we see that

\dddot{\bf v}={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathrm{blkdiag}}(\tilde{\bf T}_{i}^{-1})_{i=1}^{J}\ddot{\bf v},

(4.5)

where

\tilde{{\bf T}_{i}}={\bf T}+\tilde{\lambda}_{i}{\bf I}_{N},\quad i=1,2,...,J.

(4.6)

These
\tilde{\bf T}_{i}
’s are invertible lower triangular Toeplitz (ILTT) matrices.
[
7
]
proposed a fast algorithm for fast inversion of ILTT matrices. With the fast inversion algorithm proposed in
[
7
]
, step 2.2 can be fast computed within
\mathcal{O}(JN\log N)
flops. Summing over the operation cost for steps 2.1–2.3, we see that the computation of (
4.2
) requires
\mathcal{O}(NJ\log(NJ))
flops.

Summing up the above discussion, for a given vector
{\bf v}\in\mathbb{R}^{NJ\times 1}
, the matrix-vector product
{\bf P}^{-1}{\bf v}
can be fast computed within
\mathcal{O}(NJ\log(NJ))
flops.

Remark 3

Recall the two-sided preconditioning matrix in (
3.10
). As proposed in
[
37
,
21
]
, the matrix-vector product of the two-sided preconditioned matrix is computed by
{\bf P}_{l}^{-1}({\bf A}({\bf P}_{r}^{-1}{\bf v}))
. Meanwhile, the matrix-vector product of our single-sided preconditioned matrix is computed by
{\bf P}^{-1}{\bf A}{\bf v}
.
It is easy to see that the computation of a matrix vector product associated with
{\bf P}^{-1}=({\bf S}\otimes{\bf I}_{N})({\bf I}_{J}\otimes{\bf T}+\tilde{\bf\Lambda}\otimes{\bf I}_{N})^{-1}({\bf S}\otimes{\bf I}_{N})
is slightly faster than that of
{\bf P}_{l}^{-1}=({\bf S}\otimes{\bf I}_{N})(\tilde{\bf\Lambda}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\otimes{\bf T}+\tilde{\bf\Lambda}^{\frac{1}{2}}\otimes{\bf I}_{N})^{-1}({\bf S}\otimes{\bf I}_{N})
, not to mention that the two-sided one requires an additional matrix-vector product associated with
{\bf P}_{r}^{-1}
. Therefore, each matrix-vector product of our single-sided preconditioning method requires less operations than that of the two-sided one. Moreover, Theorem
3
shows that GMRES solver for the single-sided preconditioned system converges faster than that for the two-sided one. That means our single-sided preconditioning method requires less computational time in total than that of the two-sided one. Indeed, numerical results in
Section
5
show that our preconditioning method requires less computational than that of the two-sided one in the actual numerical tests.

Conclusion

This paper proposes a single-sided preconditioning method based on
\tau
-matrix approximation for a block triangular Toeplitz linear system arising from all-at-once of a non-local evolutionary equation with weakly singular kernels. Theoretically, we proved that the GMRES solver for the single-sided preconditioned system converges no slower than that for a auxiliary well-conditioned two-sided preconditioned system. Fast implementation is proposed for the matrix-vector multiplications associated with the preconditioned matrix. The theoretical results and the fast implementation are valid for several spatial discretization schemes. Numerical results reported have shown the efficiency and robustness of the proposed preconditioning technique.

\{w_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}^{(\beta)}
arising from spatial discretization schemes in the literature, which satisfy Property
1
.

Appendix A
Verification of
\{w_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}^{(\beta)}
arising from
[
4
]

\{w_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}^{(\beta)}
arising from
[
4
]
is defined by

w_{0}^{(\beta)}=\frac{\Gamma(\beta+1)}{\Gamma(\beta/2+1)^{2}},\quad w_{k+1}^{(\beta)}=\left(1-\frac{\beta+1}{\beta/2+k+1}\right)w_{k}^{(\beta)},\quad k\geq 0.

(A.1)

As
\Gamma(z)>0
for
z>0
and
\beta\in(1,2)
, it is clear that
w_{0}^{(\beta)}=\frac{\Gamma(\beta+1)}{\Gamma(\beta/2+1)^{2}}>0
and that

w_{1}^{(\beta)}=\left(1-\frac{\beta+1}{\beta/2+1}\right)w_{0}^{(\beta)}=\left(\frac{-\beta}{\beta+2}\right)w_{0}^{(\beta)}<0.

Notice that
1-\frac{\beta+1}{\beta/2+k+1}=\frac{2k-\beta}{2k+\beta+2}>0
for
k\geq 1
. Therefore, it is trivial to see by induction that

w_{k+1}^{(\beta)}=\left(1-\frac{\beta+1}{\beta/2+k+1}\right)w_{k}^{(\beta)}<0,\quad k\geq 1.

Hence, Property
1
{\bf(i)}
is valid. Moreover,
1-\frac{\beta+1}{\beta/2+k+1}=\frac{2k-\beta}{2k+\beta+2}\in(0,1)
for
k\geq 1
,

|w_{k+1}^{(\beta)}|=\left|\left(1-\frac{\beta+1}{\beta/2+k+1}\right)w_{k}^{(\beta)}\right|=\left|\frac{2k-\beta}{2k+\beta+2}\right||w_{k}^{(\beta)}|<|w_{k}^{(\beta)}|,\quad k\geq 1,

which combined with
w_{k}^{(\beta)}<0
for
k\geq 1
implies that
w_{k}^{(\beta)}<w_{k+1}^{(\beta)}
for
k\geq 1
. In other words, Property
1
{\bf(iii)}
is valid.

It is indicated in
[
4
]
that
2\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}|w_{k}^{(\beta)}|=w_{0}^{(\beta)}
, which together with
w_{k}^{(\beta)}<0
for
k\geq 1
implies that

\displaystyle w_{0}^{(\beta)}+2\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m-1}w_{k}^{(\beta)}=-2\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}w_{k}^{(\beta)}+2\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m-1}w_{k}^{(\beta)}=-2\sum\limits_{k=m}^{\infty}w_{k}^{(\beta)}=2\sum\limits_{k=m}^{\infty}|w_{k}^{(\beta)}|,\quad m\geq 1

It is also shown in
[
4
]
that
|w_{k}^{(\beta)}|=\mathcal{O}((k+1)^{-\beta-1})
, which means

\displaystyle w_{0}^{(\beta)}+2\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m-1}w_{k}^{(\beta)}

\displaystyle=2\sum\limits_{k=m}^{\infty}|w_{k}^{(\beta)}|

\displaystyle\gtrsim\sum\limits_{k=m}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(k+1)^{\beta+1}}\geq\int_{m}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(1+x)^{1+\beta}}dx=\frac{1}{\beta(1+m)^{\beta}},\quad m\geq 1.

Hence,

(m+1)^{\beta}\left(w_{0}^{(\beta)}+2\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m-1}w_{k}^{(\beta)}\right)\gtrsim\frac{1}{\beta}>0,\quad m\geq 1.

Therefore,

\inf\limits_{m\geq 1}(m+1)^{\beta}\left(w_{0}^{(\beta)}+2\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m-1}w_{k}^{(\beta)}\right)>0,

which means Property
1
{\bf(ii)}
is valid.

Moreover, it is shown in

29
, Lemma 4]
that
p_{k}^{(\beta)}\leq 0
for
k\geq 3
. Then,
w_{k}^{(\beta)}=\hat{\gamma}_{\beta}\hat{w}_{k}^{(\beta)}=\hat{\gamma}_{\beta}p_{k+1}^{(\beta)}\leq 0
for
k\geq 2
. So far, Property
1
{\bf(i)}
of
\{w_{k}^{(\beta)}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
defined in (
C.1
) is shown to be valid.

Notice that

\displaystyle\hat{w}_{1}^{(\beta)}-\hat{w}_{2}^{(\beta)}

\displaystyle=p_{0}^{(\beta)}+p_{2}^{(\beta)}-p_{3}^{(\beta)}

\displaystyle=4^{3-\beta}-5\times 3^{3-\beta}+10\times 2^{3-\beta}-11\leq 0,\quad\beta\in(1,2).

In other words,
w_{1}^{(\beta)}=\hat{\gamma}_{\beta}\hat{w}_{1}^{(\beta)}\leq\hat{\gamma}_{\beta}\hat{w}_{2}^{(\beta)}=w_{2}^{(\beta)}
. Moreover, it is shown in
[
29
, Lemma 4]
that
p_{k}^{(\beta)}\leq p_{k+1}^{(\beta)}
for
k\geq 3
. Thus,
w_{k}^{(\beta)}=\hat{\gamma}_{\beta}p_{k+1}^{(\beta)}\leq\hat{\gamma}_{\beta}p_{k+3}^{(\beta)}=w_{k+1}^{(\beta)}
for
k\geq 2
, which means Property
1
{\bf(iii)}
of
\{w_{k}^{(\beta)}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
defined in (
C.1
) is valid.

It thus remains to verify Property
1
{\bf(ii)}
. By
[
29
, Lemma 4]
, it holds

\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}p_{k}^{(\beta)}=0.

(C.2)

Therefore,

\displaystyle w_{0}^{(\beta)}+2\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}w_{k}^{(\beta)}

\displaystyle=\hat{\gamma}_{\beta}\left(\hat{w}_{0}^{(\beta)}+2\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\hat{w}_{k}^{(\beta)}\right)

\displaystyle=\hat{\gamma}_{\beta}\left[2p_{1}^{(\beta)}+2(p_{0}^{(\beta)}+p_{2}^{(\beta)})+2\sum\limits_{k=2}^{\infty}p_{k+1}^{(\beta)}\right]

\displaystyle=2\hat{\gamma}_{\beta}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}p_{k}^{(\beta)}=0,

which together with Property
1
{\bf(i)}
implies that

\displaystyle w_{0}^{(\beta)}+2\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m-1}w_{k}^{(\beta)}=-2\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}w_{k}^{(\beta)}+2\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m-1}w_{k}^{(\beta)}=-2\sum\limits_{m}^{\infty}w_{k}^{(\beta)}=2\sum\limits_{k=m}^{\infty}|w_{k}^{(\beta)}|

It is shown in
[
20
]
that
p_{k}^{(\beta)}=\mathcal{O}((k+1)^{-\beta-1})
for
k\geq 0
, Therefore,
|w_{k}^{(\beta)}|=\mathcal{O}((k+1)^{-\beta-1})
, which means

\displaystyle w_{0}^{(\beta)}+2\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m-1}w_{k}^{(\beta)}

\displaystyle=2\sum\limits_{k=m}^{\infty}|w_{k}^{(\beta)}|

\displaystyle\gtrsim\sum\limits_{k=m}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(k+1)^{\beta+1}}\geq\int_{m}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(1+x)^{1+\beta}}dx=\frac{1}{\beta(1+m)^{\beta}},\quad m\geq 1.

Hence,

(m+1)^{\beta}\left(w_{0}^{(\beta)}+2\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m-1}w_{k}^{(\beta)}\right)\gtrsim\frac{1}{\beta}>0,\quad m\geq 1.

Therefore,

\inf\limits_{m\geq 1}(m+1)^{\beta}\left(w_{0}^{(\beta)}+2\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m-1}w_{k}^{(\beta)}\right)>0.

Thus, Property
1
{\bf(ii)}
of
\{w_{k}^{(\beta)}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
defined in (
C.1
) is valid.

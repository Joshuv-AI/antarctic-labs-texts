Time-Like Heavy-Flavour Thresholds for Fragmentation Functions: the Light-Quark Matching Condition at NNLO
Christian Biello e1,addr1, Leonardo Bonino e2,add2

Abstract
Matching conditions are universal ingredients that describe how fragmentation functions change when heavy-flavour thresholds are crossed during the factorisation scale evolution. They are the last missing piece for a consistent description of observables with identified final-state hadrons at next-to-next-to leading order accuracy in quantum chromodynamics. We present an analytical form of the matching condition for light-flavour to hadron fragmentation function at next-to-next-to leading order. The derivation is performed by extending the formalism employed in the extraction of the next-to leading order matching conditions to the subsequent order, making use of e + ​ e − e^{+}e^{-} annihilation cross sections. We obtain the first non-trivial heavy-quark effect in the light-quark fragmentation functions and provide results in Mellin space.

Introduction

The production of identified hadrons in high energy collisions is described in quantum chromodynamics (QCD) through fragmentation functions (FFs). They parameterise the non-perturbative fragmentation of a parton (quark or gluon) into the identified hadron, which carries a fraction of the parent parton momentum
Field & Feynman, 1977
;
Field & Feynman, 1978
. Thanks to the factorisation theorem, a differential cross section for the production of an identified hadron can be written as the convolution of a process-dependent coefficient function, encoding the hard scattering of the process, a process-independent fragmentation function, and up to two process-independent parton distribution functions (PDFs), according to the collider under consideration. FFs and PDFs obey Dokshitzer-Gribov-Lipatov-Altarelli-Parisi (DGLAP) evolution equations in their resolution scale, bridging the gap between the hard scattering scale and non-perturbative scales
Altarelli & Parisi, 1977
.

Precision studies of processes with identified hadrons at colliders have received growing interest in the last years. Important progress was made with regard to light hadron production (e.g. pions) thanks to the efforts in global FFs fits with recent results including
e^{+}e^{-}
and approximate semi-inclusive deep-inelastic scattering (SIDIS) data
Borsa et al., 2022
;
Abdul et al., 2022
. The precision of these studies was in part limited due to the absence of SIDIS coefficient functions at next-to-next leading order (NNLO), which have only recently been computed
Bonino et al., 2024
;
Goyal et al., 2024
.

Regarding heavy hadrons, such as
B
and
D
mesons, the state-of-the-art of FFs is less developed. Using the perturbative fragmentation function formalism, fits have been performed at NNLO to
e^{+}e^{-}
data for
B

Czakon et al., 2023
;
Bonino et al., 2024a
and
D
mesons
Bonino et al., 2024a
only recently. From a theoretical perspective, the calculation is very similar to the massless one, the only difference being that perturbative fragmentation functions are used. These encode logarithmically enhanced massive effects
Mele & Nason, 1990
, which are known up to NNLO
Melnikov & Mitov, 2004
;
Mitov, 2005
. In order to properly account for soft gluon effects, the initial conditions must be resummed up to next-to-next-to leading logarithmic level
Czakon et al., 2023
;
Aglietti et al., 2007
;
Ridolfi et al., 2020
;
Maltoni et al., 2022
. Fits to heavy hadrons without the inclusion of perturbative initial conditions have been performed up to NNLO in e.g.
Kneesch et al., 2008
;
Salajegheh et al., 2019
.
In both cases, the resummation of logarithmically enhanced collinear divergences is achieved by DGLAP evolution, in which heavy-flavour thresholds are crossed. Therefore a time-like variable flavour number scheme (VFNS) up to NNLO is required. At NLO the ingredients were computed in
Cacciari et al., 2005
and used to study heavy hadron FFs at NLO
Cacciari et al., 2006
. These matching conditions are currently implemented in Mellin space in the public evolution library
MELA

Bertone et al., 2015
. In addition to this matching, including heavy-quark threshold effects in the
e^{+}e^{-}
coefficient functions reduces the discrepancy between theory and experimental data in the charm ratio
Cacciari et al., 2024
. At NNLO, these matching conditions are still missing, yet crucial for achieving full NNLO accuracy on for example
D
-mesons FFs. This is also relevant when it comes to LHC phenomenology, as highlighted in studies involving
D
-meson production in association with a vector boson
Caletti et al., 2024
.

From a broader perspective, these matching conditions are conceptually equivalent to their space-like counterparts, where crossing thresholds in PDFs evolution has had significant impact on global fits. The matching equations for PDFs
Collins & Tung, 1986
have long been known at NNLO level
Buza et al., 1998
, using the formalism of Operator Matrix Elements (OME)
3
3

3

The space-like results from the OME calculations cannot be directly mapped to their time-like counterparts. Indeed, these heavy-quark coefficient functions are expressed using dispersion integrals for off-shell forward Compton scattering, following the standard approach of the Operator Product Expansion (OPE) in deep inelastic scattering (DIS)
Neerven, 1997
. Therefore the time-like matching conditions are extracted from a physical process.
. Recently, all the necessary contributions for the next-to-NNLO (
\text{N}^{3}\text{LO}
) accuracy were computed
Bierenbaum et al., 2009
;
Bierenbaum et al., 2009a
;
Ablinger et al., 2014
;
Ablinger et al., 2022
;
Ablinger et al., 2024
;
Ablinger et al., 2024a
. In addition, matching equations played a crucial role in providing evidence for intrinsic charm in the proton
Ball et al., 2024
;
Ball et al., 2022
. It is thus important to increase the level of accuracy when crossing flavour thresholds, in the time-like case too. Matching conditions are an ingredient of the time-like DGLAP evolution in the VFNS that can be implemented in fits to FFs independently on the choice of the initial conditions.

The aim of this work is to pave the way for the calculation of these missing matching coefficients and provide first results.
Some of the ingredients needed for these calculations have already been computed in the context of the antenna subtraction formalism
Gehrmann-De et al., 2005
;
Gehrmann & Stagnitto, 2022
;
Bonino et al., 2024b
, which can now accommodate fragmentation in hadronic collisions. We make use of some of these results and analytically compute the missing massive pieces. All massive contributions are given in small-mass limit, neglecting power corrections. In section
2
we review the NLO matching conditions focusing on details relevant to the NNLO extension. In section
3
we present the master formulae for all matching conditions. In section
4
we provide results for the light quark matching condition, which employs existing massless antennae as well as novel massive corrections. Conclusions and outlooks are given in section
5
. Details of the calculation of the massless and massive elementary cross-sections are reported in the appendices.

2
Revising the NLO derivation

In this section we summarise the derivation of the threshold conditions at NLO accuracy closely following Ref.
Cacciari et al., 2005
. We consider the semi-inclusive production of an hadron
H
in
e^{+}e^{-}
annihilation

e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow\gamma^{*}\rightarrow H+X,

(1)

at a center-of-mass energy
Q
much greater than the heavy-flavour mass
m
, where power-suppressed terms of order
m/Q
can be neglected. On the other hand,
Q
should not be arbitrarily large, ensuring that
\alpha_{s}\log\frac{Q}{m}
remains smaller than 1.
Due to the factorisation theorem, the FFs factor out in the differential cross section, at the cost of introducing an unphysical factorisation scale
\mu
.

For scales
\mu
much below the heavy-flavour threshold, the differential cross section for single hadron production can be written as a convolution of a parton-level cross-sections and FFs
D_{j}^{(n_{L})}
, encoding the transition of the massless partons, namely the gluon and the quarks of
n_{L}
flavours, into hadrons

\displaystyle j\in\mathbb{I}_{n_{L}}=\left\{q_{1},\bar{q}_{1},\dots,q_{n_{L}},\bar{q}_{n_{L}},g\right\}.

(2)

The evolution of
D_{j}^{(n_{L})}
in terms of the scale
\mu
is given by the DGLAP equation with
n_{L}
flavours. Heavy-flavour effects are instead confined in the parton-level cross-section with a full mass dependence. This setup is called decoupling scheme, since the ultraviolet (UV) renormalisation is performed in the
\overline{\text{MS}}
scheme for
n_{L}
flavours and the divergences from heavy-quark loop are subtracted at zero-momentum. In contrast, for scales
\mu
significantly larger than the mass
m
, the FFs of the heavy-quark
h
and its antiparticle are introduced. Specifically we consider the set of functions
D_{k}^{(n)}
for
k\in\mathbb{I}_{n}=\mathbb{I}_{n_{L}}\cup\{h,\bar{h}\}
which obey DGLAP evolution equations for
n=n_{L}+1
flavours. The renormalisation is perfomed in the full
\overline{\text{MS}}
scheme for all flavours.

In the decoupling scheme, the differential cross section at
\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s})
for hadron
H
production with energy fraction
x=2E_{H}/Q
is

\displaystyle\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{H}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x}=\int_{x}^{1}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z}{z}\Bigg\{\sum_{j\in\mathbb{I}_{n_{L}}}D_{j}^{(n_{L})}\left(\frac{x}{z},\mu\right)\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{j}(z,\mu)}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z}

\displaystyle+D_{g}^{(n_{L})}\left(\frac{x}{z},\mu\right)\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{h\bar{h}g^{\text{id.}}}(z,m)}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z}\Bigg\},

(3)

where
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{j}
is the cross-section for an identified parton
j
with in principle heavy-flavour mass effects and
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{h\bar{h}g^{\text{id.}}}
describes the production of a massive heavy-quark pair in association with an identified gluon. From the massive cross-sections we retain only the leading contribution, namely the logarithmically enhanced and finite pieces, neglecting all power corrections in
m
.

Up to
\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s})
, in the first contribution of eq. (
3
) one can replace
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{j}
with the cross-section for an identified parton
j
when treating the heavy-quark
h
as massless (i.e. nominally heavy), since there are no fermion loop effects at NLO. This is denoted as
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\hat{\sigma}_{j}
, where the hat stands for the massless treatment of the heavy-flavour. The last contribution in eq. (
3
) represents the cross-section for the process

\gamma^{*}\left(q^{(Q)}\right)\rightarrow h\left(k_{1}^{(m)}\right)+\bar{h}\left(k_{2}^{(m)}\right)+g\left(k_{p}^{(0)}\right)\,,

(4)

where we indicate the four-momenta of the state in parenthesis with its invariant mass as superscript.

In the full (massless)
\overline{\text{MS}}
scheme, the differential cross-section of eq. (
3
) becomes

\displaystyle\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{H}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x}=\int_{x}^{1}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z}{z}\left\{\sum_{k\in\mathbb{I}_{n}}D_{k}^{(n)}(\tfrac{x}{z},\mu)\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\hat{\sigma}_{k}(z,\mu)}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z}\right\},

(5)

where all flavours, including the (nominally) heavy one, are treated as light. We stress that the quark
h
is a massless particle in
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\hat{\sigma}_{k}
.

The matching conditions can be derived by taking the difference at NLO accuracy between the predictions in the two schemes
Cacciari et al., 2005
, namely eq. (
3
) and eq. (
5
). The contributions in the difference are collected in terms of the electromagnetic coupling constants of the quarks and one can ask for the vanishing behaviour of each independent coefficient. Considering the coefficients proportional to the light-quark charge (
Q_{i}^{2}
), one can easily infer that the difference between the light quark fragmentation functions is beyond NLO accuracy

D_{i}^{(n)}(x,\mu)=D_{i}^{(n_{L})}(x,\mu)+\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{2}),\hskip 14.22636pti\in\mathbb{I}_{n_{L}}\smallsetminus\{g\}\,.

(6)

In sections
3
and
4
we compute the first non-trivial correction to the previous equation.

The contribution that comes with the gluon fragmentation function instead is proportional to

\displaystyle D_{g}^{(n)}\left(\frac{x}{z},\mu\right)\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\hat{\sigma}_{g}^{(n)}(z,\mu)}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z}-D_{g}^{(n_{L})}\left(\frac{x}{z},\mu\right)\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\hat{\sigma}_{g}^{(n_{L})}(z,\mu)}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z}

\displaystyle=D_{g}^{(n)}\left(\frac{x}{z},\mu\right)\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\hat{\sigma}_{h\bar{h}g^{\text{id.}}}(z,\mu)}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z}\,,

(7)

where we can set
D_{g}^{(n)}=D_{g}^{(n_{L})}
in the previous equation at NLO accuracy, defining the difference between the two differential cross-sections as
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\hat{\sigma}_{h\bar{h}g^{\text{id.}}}
. The latter is the probability of producing the massless quark pair of (nominally) heavy-flavour
h
in association with an identified gluon.

The leftover from the difference between the results in the two schemes is proportional to the heavy-quark charge (
Q_{h}^{2}
) and reads

\displaystyle\int_{x}^{1}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z}{z}D_{g}^{(n_{L})}\left(\frac{x}{z},\mu\right)\left[\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{h\bar{h}g^{\text{id.}}}(z,m)}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z}-\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\hat{\sigma}_{h\bar{h}g^{\text{id.}}}(z,\mu)}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z}\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\int_{x}^{1}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z}{z}\sum_{k\in\{h,\bar{h}\}}D_{k}^{(n)}\left(\frac{x}{z},\mu\right)\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\hat{\sigma}_{k}(z,\mu)}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z}=0\,.

(8)

At NLO accuracy, it is enough to replace the Born contribution in the last terms in eq. (
8
). Since the heavy-flavour production happens via gluon splitting, one can use the symmetry of
D_{h}
and
D_{\bar{h}}
and finally obtain the matching condition for the heavy-quark fragmentation function

\displaystyle D_{h}^{(n)}(x,\mu)=D_{\bar{h}}^{(n)}(x,\mu)=\frac{1}{2\sigma_{h\bar{h}}}\int_{x}^{1}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z}{z}D_{g}^{(n_{L})}\left(\frac{x}{z},\mu\right)

\displaystyle\left[\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{h\bar{h}g^{\text{id.}}}(z,m)}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z}-\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\hat{\sigma}_{h\bar{h}g^{\text{id.}}}(z,\mu)}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z}\right]\,.

(9)

In the above equation
\sigma_{h\bar{h}}
is the Born cross-section for the photon-induced production of a
h\bar{h}
pair. An explicit result for the elementary cross-sections in (
9
) can be found in
A
using ingredients computed in the antenna subtraction formalism.

The gluon matching condition instead can be derived by studying a process with a resolved gluon at tree-level. In
Cacciari et al., 2005
the authors considered identified gluon production in association with a “super-heavy” quark pair. A simple matching condition due to the different running of the coupling constant was found

\displaystyle\alpha_{s}^{(n)}(\mu)D_{g}^{(n)}(x,\mu)=\alpha_{s}^{(n_{L})}(\mu)D_{g}^{(n_{L})}(x,\mu)+\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{3})\,,

\displaystyle D_{g}^{(n)}(x,\mu)=D_{g}^{(n_{L})}(x,\mu)\left(1-\frac{T_{F}\alpha_{s}}{3\pi}\log\frac{\mu^{2}}{m^{2}}+\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{2})\right)\,.

(10)

Here
T_{F}
is the standard color normalisation of the fundamental representation. The scale choice for the strong coupling in eq. (
10
) is beyond NLO accuracy.

Due to the presence of an additional mass this channel is not promising for the extraction of the threshold condition for the gluon FF at NNLO.
At NLO there are no heavy-quark (i.e. bottom) effects for the super-heavy-quark (i.e. top) production and all the mass effects are captured by studying the gluon propagator (Fig. 2 of Ref.
Cacciari et al., 2005
). At NNLO it would require for example the double-virtual correction to
t\bar{t}j
production or the double-real correction
t\bar{t}b\bar{b}g
, with full top-mass effects and leading bottom-mass dependence.
We focused therefore on a different process: Higgs-induced hadron production in electron-position annihilation,
e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow h^{0}\rightarrow H+X
, from which eq. (
10
) can be re-derived.
This process is proportional to the electron Yukawa coupling
y_{e}^{2}
and to the Wilson coefficient
C_{h^{0}gg}
for the Higgs-gluon coupling. The hadronic cross sections take the same form as eq. (
3
) and (
5
), replacing the partonic cross sections which corresponding ones extracted from
h^{0}\rightarrow gg
decay. In eq. (
5
) the term containing
D_{h}^{(n)}
is beyond accuracy, due to the NLO matching conditions and since the production of heavy-flavour quark
h
via Yukawa electron fusion starts at
\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s})
. Analogously, since the leading terms for the light-quark production,
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{gi^{\text{id.}}\bar{i}}
and
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\hat{\sigma}_{gi^{\text{id.}}\bar{i}}
, start at
\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s})
and the difference
D_{i}^{(n)}-D_{i}^{(n_{L})}
is
\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{2})
, the terms with an identified light-quark vanish in the difference. We remain only with contributions proportional to the gluon FF at NLO. We isolate the difference between the partonic cross-sections
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{g}
and
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\hat{\sigma}_{g}

\displaystyle\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{g}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z}=\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\hat{\sigma}_{g}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z}

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\hat{\sigma}_{g,f}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z}+\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\hat{\sigma}_{g^{\text{id.}}h\bar{h}}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z}+\left.\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\hat{\sigma}_{g}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z}\right|_{\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{0})}\right)

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{g,f}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z}+\left.\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{g}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z}\right|_{\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{0})}\right)+\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{2})\,,

(11)

where
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\hat{\sigma}_{g,f}
and
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{g,f}
represent the virtual contributions from the massless and massive heavy-flavour loops respectively. These corrections at NLO are simple since the quark-loop can affect only the gluon self-energy as discussed in
Cacciari et al., 2005
. The difference between the Born cross-sections is encoded in the decoupling relation of the Wilson coefficient e.g.
Grozin et al., 2011

\displaystyle\delta_{C_{h^{0}gg}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left.\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\hat{\sigma}_{g}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z}\right|_{\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{0})}-\left.\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{g}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z}\right|_{\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{0})}

\displaystyle=2\frac{T_{F}\alpha_{s}}{3\pi}\log\frac{\mu^{2}}{m^{2}}\sigma_{0}\delta(1-z)\,.

(12)

The factor of
2
is present since the Wilson coefficient is proportional to two powers of
\alpha_{s}
. The choice of the scheme for the Born cross-section
\sigma_{0}
is beyond the accuracy. We can now combine all the remaining ingredients in the gluon master formula at NLO

\displaystyle D_{g}^{(n)}(x,\mu)

\displaystyle=D_{g}^{(n_{L})}(x,\mu)+\frac{1}{\sigma_{0}}\int_{x}^{1}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z}{z}D_{g}^{(n_{L})}\left(\frac{x}{z},\mu\right)

\displaystyle\times\left(\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{g,f}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z}-\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\hat{\sigma}_{g,f}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z}+\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{g^{\text{id.}}h\bar{h}}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z}-\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\hat{\sigma}_{g^{\text{id.}}h\bar{h}}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z}-\delta_{C_{h^{0}gg}}\right)

\displaystyle=D_{g}^{(n_{L})}(x,\mu)\left(1-\frac{T_{F}\alpha_{s}}{3\pi}\log\frac{\mu^{2}}{m^{2}}+\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{2})\right)\,.

(13)

The massive virtual correction (
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{g,f}
) is zero in the decoupling scheme, while the massless one (
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\hat{\sigma}_{g,f}
) contains a pole that cancels the divergence of the corresponding real correction (
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\hat{\sigma}_{g^{\text{id.}}h\bar{h}}
). By applying for example the procedure described in
A
to the gluon-gluon antenna
G_{3}^{0}
, one can prove that the real corrections in the two schemes exhibit the same
z
dependence that cancels in the difference. Therefore the combination of all the elementary cross-sections gives only a logarithmic term which cancels exactly one of the two
T_{F}
contributions coming from
\delta_{C_{h^{0}gg}}
. Eq. (
13
) is in agreement with eq. (
10
), as a consequence of the universality of the matching conditions, which do not depend on the process from which they are extracted.

3
Master formulae at NNLO

Following the idea of the NLO derivation described above, we consider the production of an identified hadron
H
in
e^{+}e^{-}
annihilation and we expand its cross-section to
\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{2})
.

3.1
Light-flavour matching equation

In the decoupling scheme, the hadron has different ways to be produced via the fragmentation of a light parton. In addition to the contributions involving only the light quarks and gluons, we should also consider the production of the fragmenting light parton in association with a heavy quark pair

\displaystyle\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{H}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x}=

\displaystyle\int_{x}^{1}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z}{z}\,\bigg\{\sum_{j\in\mathbb{I}_{n_{L}}}D_{j}^{(n_{L})}(\tfrac{x}{z},\mu)\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{j}(z,\mu)}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z}

\displaystyle\left.+D_{g}^{(n_{L})}(\tfrac{x}{z},\mu)\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{h\bar{h}g^{\text{id.}}}(z,\mu)}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z}\right.

\displaystyle+\sum_{i\in\mathbb{I}_{n_{L}}\smallsetminus\{g\}}D_{i}^{(n_{L})}(\tfrac{x}{z},\mu)\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{h\bar{h}i^{\text{id.}}\bar{i}}(z,m)}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z}\bigg\}\,.

(14)

Here the elementary cross sections are computed considering a gluon or a light (anti-)quark as resolved parton. Eq. (
14
) defines the prediction in the decoupling scheme, as opposed to the full massless cross-section which corresponds to the expansion of (
5
) up to NNLO. We recall that we indicate cross-sections in the decoupling scheme with
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma
while the ones in the full massless
\overline{\text{MS}}
scheme are denoted with
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\hat{\sigma}
. For the sake of simplicity, we will henceforth imply the dependencies of the elementary cross-sections on the variables
z
,
\mu
,
Q
and
m
.

Following the NLO derivation, we require the difference between the two schemes to vanish. We first consider the difference of the terms

\displaystyle\Delta_{D_{i}}\coloneqq\int_{x}^{1}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z}{z}\left\{D_{i}^{(n_{L})}(\tfrac{x}{z},\mu)\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{i}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z}-D_{i}^{(n)}(\tfrac{x}{z},\mu)\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\hat{\sigma}_{i}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z}\right\}\,,

(15)

respectively from eqs. (
14
) and (
5
) and focusing on a specific light quark
i\in\mathbb{I}_{n_{L}}\smallsetminus\{g\}
.

The elementary cross-sections in (
15
) for the production of a light quark
i
start to differ in the two schemes at NNLO because of the presence of quark loops and real corrections with unresolved quark pairs. All pure NNLO gluonic corrections and fermionic contributions from closed loops of
n_{L}
light quarks are treated in the same way.
Thus we can rewrite the massive cross-section starting from the massless one by isolating the only different diagrammatic contributions. Firstly, we subtract the NLO prediction
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\hat{\sigma}_{i}|_{\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s})}
and all the heavy-flavour fermion terms in the massless scheme. Secondly, we add the missing heavy-quark contributions with mass dependence and the massive NLO cross-section
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{i}|_{\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s})}
.
This observation is encoded in the following formula

\displaystyle\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{i}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z}=\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\hat{\sigma}_{i}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z}-\left(\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\hat{\sigma}_{i,f}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z}+\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\hat{\sigma}_{h\bar{h}i^{\text{id.}}\bar{i}}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z}+\left.\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\hat{\sigma}_{i}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z}\right|_{\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s})}\right)

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{i,f}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z}+\left.\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{i}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z}\right|_{\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s})}\right)+\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{3})\,.

(16)

Here
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\hat{\sigma}_{h\bar{h}i^{\text{id.}}\bar{i}}
is the double real correction in the scheme where the quark
h
is massless (nominally heavy). We introduce the notation
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{i,f}
for massive fermion
h
loop corrections, given by double virtual (VV) and real-virtual (RV) corrections with some representative diagrams in Fig.
1
. The massless counterpart is
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\hat{\sigma}_{i,f}
which includes all closed fermion loop contributions of a massless quark with flavour
h
.

Figure 1:
Representative diagrams which are treated in a different way in the two schemes. The quark
h
is massless in
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\hat{\sigma}_{i,f}
and
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\hat{\sigma}_{h\bar{h}i^{\text{id.}}\bar{i}}
while it has a mass
m
in
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{i,f_{h}}
and
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{h\bar{h}i^{\text{id.}}\bar{i}}
.

Despite the NLO contributions being described by the same diagrams in both schemes, the strong couplings have a different running, which produces a NNLO effect. The difference reads

\displaystyle\delta_{\alpha_{s}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left.\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\hat{\sigma}_{i}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z}\right|_{\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s})}-\left.\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{i}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z}\right|_{\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s})}=\left(\alpha_{s}^{(n)}-\alpha_{s}^{(n_{L})}\right)\left[\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{i}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z}\right]^{(1)}\,,

(17)

where
[\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{i}]^{(1)}

4.5
RGE dependence

Similarly to the space-like OMEs, the time-like matching condition satisfies a Renormalisation Group Equations (RGE). We can obtain it by taking the derivative of

\displaystyle D_{i}^{(n)}(N,\mu)=\left(1+\Delta D_{i}(N,\mu)+\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{3})\right)D_{i}^{(n_{L})}(N,\mu)

(48)

with respect to
\log\mu^{2}
and use the RGEs for the fragmentation functions,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\log\mu^{2}}D_{i}^{(n_{f})}=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{I}_{n_{f}}\smallsetminus\{g\}}\gamma_{ij}^{(n_{f})}D_{j}^{(n_{f})}+\gamma_{ig}^{(n_{f})}D_{g}^{(n_{f})}\,,

(49)

for
n_{f}=n,n_{L}
. Here the anomalous couplings
\gamma_{ij}
are the time-like splitting functions
P_{ji}
which admit a perturbative expansion

\displaystyle P_{ji}^{(n_{f})}=\alpha_{s}^{(n_{f})}P_{ii,0}\delta_{ji}+\left(\alpha_{s}^{(n_{f})}\right)^{2}P_{ii,1}^{(n_{f})}\delta_{ji}+\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{3})\,,

(50)

\displaystyle P_{gi}=\alpha_{s}^{(n_{f})}P_{gi,0}+\left(\alpha_{s}^{(n_{f})}\right)^{2}P_{gi,1}+\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{3})\,.

(51)

The well-known tree-level and one-loop splitting coefficients can be found for example in Ref.
Ellis et al., 2011
. By expanding all the contributions in the same scheme for the coupling constant and by using the NLO gluon matching condition, the RGE for the NNLO quark matching equation reads

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\log\mu^{2}}\delta D_{i}(z,\mu,m)=\beta_{0}P_{ii,0}(z)\log\frac{\mu^{2}}{m^{2}}+\hat{P}_{ii,1}(z)\,,

(52)

where
\hat{P}_{ii,1}=P_{ii,1}^{(n_{L})}-P_{ii,1}^{(n)}
. We verified (
52
) both in Mellin and direct space. Due to the Gribov-Lipatov reciprocity of the tree-level splitting coefficients, the time-like matching equation shares the same
\log^{2}\mu
of the OME for the non-singlet quark PDFs
Buza et al., 1998
.

5
Conclusions and outlooks

In this work we have studied the crossing of heavy-quark thresholds with fragmentation functions at NNLO accuracy. By considering identified hadron production in
e^{+}e^{-}
annihilation, we derived the NNLO matching equations for the fragmentation functions of light and heavy flavours. Combining novel and existing partonic cross-sections, we computed the light-quark matching condition and provided its analytical expression in Mellin space. The present calculation should be considered as a first step towards a full NNLO description of heavy-quark effects in fragmentation functions. The calculation of the heavy-flavour matching condition, for which some analytical elementary cross-sections are still missing, is a natural extension of this work. Regarding the gluon matching condition, more conceptual efforts are needed, as its derivation requires focusing on a different process. Once the complete set of matching equations is available, a detailed assessment of their impact on fragmentation functions fits at full NNLO accuracy will be possible, shedding light on their influence on identified hadrons predictions.

In order to integrate the massive ingredient
\bar{B}_{ih\bar{h}\bar{i}}^{0}
, we firstly have to derive a suitable phase-space (PS) measure. In the antenna formalism, this is the first and simplest integration of a massive antenna
X_{4}^{0}
with a fragmentation kinematics. For generality, in the first part of the derivation we avoid fixing the space-time dimension of the phase space, since the latter can be of use in the future for the integration of other massive antennae which are divergent in four dimensions and useful for a subtraction scheme. In order to simplify the problem, we consider the crossing-symmetric scattering

\displaystyle q+(-k_{p})^{(0)}\rightarrow k_{1}^{(m)}+k_{2}^{(m)}+k_{3}^{(0)}\,,

(66)

where
k_{p}
is the momentum of the massless identified quark.
We have to integrate the antenna in the full three-particle phase space of eq. (
66
),

\displaystyle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\Phi_{3}=\,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{d}k_{1}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{d}k_{2}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{d}k_{3}}{(2\pi)^{d}}(2\pi)\delta(k_{1}^{2}-m^{2})(2\pi)\delta(k_{2}^{2}-m^{2})

\displaystyle(2\pi)\delta(k_{3}^{2})(2\pi)^{d}\delta^{(d)}(q-k_{p}-k_{1}-k_{2}-k_{3})

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{-2d+3}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s_{12}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s_{23}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{d}k_{1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{d}k_{3}\delta(k_{1}^{2}-m^{2})\delta(k_{3}^{2})

\displaystyle\delta((q-k_{p}-k_{1}-k_{2})^{2}-m^{2})\delta(s_{12}-(k_{1}+k_{2})^{2})

\displaystyle\delta(s_{23}+m^{2}-(k_{2}+k_{3})^{2})\,.

(67)

In the center of mass of
q+(-k_{p})
, we can express the
\delta
-function for the on-shell condition of
k_{2}
in terms of the angle
z=\cos\theta_{13}
,

\displaystyle\delta((q-k_{p}-k_{1}-k_{2})^{2}-m^{2})=\frac{1}{2k_{3}^{0}\sqrt{(k_{1}^{0})^{2}-m^{2}}}\delta\left(z-\bar{z}\right)\,,

(68)

where

\displaystyle\bar{z}=\frac{s_{12}s_{23}+s(s_{12}+s_{23})-s^{2}}{(s-s_{12})\sqrt{(s-s_{23})^{2}-4m^{2}s}}\,,

(69)

with

\displaystyle s\coloneqq(q-k_{p})^{2}=Q^{2}(1-z)\,.

(70)

Using polar coordinates for the
d
-dimensional measures of
k_{1}
and
k_{3}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\Phi_{3}=\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s_{12}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s_{23}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\Omega_{1}^{(d-2)}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\Omega_{3}^{(d-1)}\frac{1}{32s}

\displaystyle\left[\frac{(m^{2}+s_{23})(-m^{2}s+s_{12}(s-s_{12}-s_{23}))}{4s}\right]^{\frac{d-4}{2}}(2\pi)^{-2d+3}\,.

(71)

The condition on
z=\cos\theta_{13}
fixes the coordinate system in such a way that the remaining integration over the solid angle of particle
1
is arbitrary and can immediately be performed. Regarding the measure
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\Omega_{3}^{(d-1)}
, we can parametrise it by introducing two angles,
\theta
and
\phi
, which represent the polar and azimuthal angles of the unresolved massless particle with respect to the identified quark. It yields

\displaystyle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\Phi_{3}=

\displaystyle\,\frac{s^{1-\frac{d}{2}}}{(4\pi^{2})^{d}\,\Gamma(3-d)}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s_{12}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s_{23}

\displaystyle\left[(m^{2}+s_{23})(-m^{2}s+s_{12}(s-s_{12}-s_{23}))\right]^{\frac{d-4}{2}}

\displaystyle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\cos\theta\,(1-\cos^{2}\theta)^{\frac{d-4}{2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\cos\phi\,(1-\cos^{2}\phi)^{\frac{d-5}{2}}\,.

(72)

In four dimension, we simply have

\displaystyle\int\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\Phi_{3}^{(d=4)}=\frac{1}{512\pi^{4}s}\int_{4m^{2}}^{s}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s_{12}\int_{s_{23}^{-}}^{s_{23}^{+}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s_{23}\int_{-1}^{1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\cos\theta\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\phi\,.

(73)

The limits of integration
s_{23}^{\pm}
can be derived computing the energy of particle
2
in two different ways,

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\left(k_{2}^{0}\right)^{2}=\left(\sqrt{s}-k_{1}^{0}-k_{3}^{0}\right)^{2},\\
\left(k_{2}^{0}\right)^{2}=\left(k_{1}^{0}\right)^{2}+\left(k_{3}^{0}\right)^{2}+2\sqrt{\left(k_{1}^{0}\right)^{2}+m^{2}}\,k_{3}^{0}\,\bar{z}\,.\end{cases}

(74)

By equalising the two right-hand sides written in terms of the invariants
s,s_{12}
and
s_{23}
, we find

\displaystyle s_{23}^{\pm}=\frac{(s-s_{12})\left(s_{12}\pm\sqrt{s_{12}(s_{12}-4m^{2})}\right)}{2s_{12}}\,.

(75)

Thus the
\mathcal{\bar{B}}_{ih\bar{h}\bar{i}}^{0,\text{id.}i}(z)
in four dimension is given by the integral

\displaystyle\mathcal{\bar{B}}_{ih\bar{h}\bar{i}}^{0,\text{id.}i}(z)=\,

\displaystyle\frac{z}{16\pi(1-z)}\int_{4m^{2}}^{s}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s_{12}\int_{s_{23}^{-}}^{s_{23}^{+}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s_{23}

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\cos\theta\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\phi\,\bar{B}_{ih\bar{h}\bar{i}}^{0}\,.

(76)

After performing the above integrals, we obtain the so-called fragmentation antenna in an analytical form. We check numerically the correctness of the integration considering several physical PS points.

In conclusion, the result is expanded in the small mass limit and we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{\bar{B}}_{ih\bar{h}\bar{i}}^{0,\text{id.}i}(z)=

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{216(z-1)}\bigg[-18\left(z^{2}+1\right)\log^{2}\frac{m^{2}}{Q^{2}}+6\left(-13z^{2}+6\left(z^{2}+1\right)\log(1-z)+6\left(z^{2}+1\right)\log(z)-16\right)\log\frac{m^{2}}{Q^{2}}

\displaystyle\left.+36\left(z^{2}+1\right)\text{Li}_{2}\left(\frac{z-1}{z}\right)+6\pi^{2}\left(z^{2}+1\right)-18\left(z^{2}+1\right)\log^{2}(1-z)+6\log(1-z)\left(13z^{2}-6\left(z^{2}+1\right)\log(z)+16\right)\right.

\displaystyle-z(115z+72)+12(z(8z+3)+8)\log(z)-172\bigg]+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{m}{Q}\right).

(77)

In the next section we provide an alternative integration of the massive double real antenna as a validation of the previous result.

Appendix D
Alternative integration of
\bar{B}_{ih\bar{h}\bar{i}}^{0}
antenna

Here we derive an alternative parametrisation of the PS for the four-particle production with a massive pair and a resolved massless quark. We consider the scattering process for the production of two pair of particles with masses
m_{1}
and
m_{2}

\displaystyle q\rightarrow k_{1}^{(m_{1})}+k_{2}^{(m_{1})}+k_{3}^{(m_{2})}+k_{p}^{(m_{2})}\,.

(78)

We will later take the limit
m_{2}\rightarrow 0
in order to obtain the needed PS. We first introduce the invariant variables

\displaystyle y=\frac{(k_{1}+k_{2})^{2}}{Q^{2}},\,\,z=\frac{2q\cdot k_{p}}{Q^{2}},\,\,x=\frac{2q\cdot k_{3}}{Q^{2}}\,,

(79)

and we want to remain differential in the variable
z
. We work in the frame of the pair of quarks with mass
m_{1}
, i.e. the three-momenta satisfy the condition
\mathbf{k_{1}}+\mathbf{k_{2}}=0
. In this frame the energies of the final states are

\displaystyle k_{1}^{0}=k_{2}^{0}=\sqrt{Q^{2}}\frac{\sqrt{y}}{2}\,,

(80)

\displaystyle k_{p}^{0}=\frac{\sqrt{Q^{2}}}{2\sqrt{y}}(1-x-y)\,,

(81)

\displaystyle k_{3}^{0}=\frac{\sqrt{Q^{2}}}{2\sqrt{y}}(1-z-y)\,.

(82)

We want to parametrise the PS measure in terms of the three invariants and the two angles
(\theta,\phi)
that describe the direction of the quark with mass
m_{1}
in its quark-pair frame. We start from

\displaystyle\int\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\Phi_{4}=

\displaystyle\,\int\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{3}k_{p}}{(2\pi)^{3}2k_{p}^{0}}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{3}k_{3}}{(2\pi)^{3}2k_{3}^{0}}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{3}k_{1}}{(2\pi)^{3}2k_{1}^{0}}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{3}k_{2}}{(2\pi)^{3}2k_{2}^{0}}

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{(4)}(q-r-r^{\prime}-p-p^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle\frac{Q^{4}}{(4\pi)^{6}}\int_{\rho_{1}}^{(1-\sqrt{\rho_{2}})^{2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\sqrt{1-\frac{\rho_{1}}{y}}\int_{\sqrt{\rho_{2}}}^{z_{+}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\int_{x_{-}}^{x_{+}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\int_{0}^{1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\phi\,,

(83)

where

\displaystyle v=\,\frac{1}{2}(1-\cos\theta),\,\,\rho_{i}=\frac{4m_{i}^{2}}{Q^{2}},\,\,z_{+}=1-y-\sqrt{y\rho_{2}},

(84)

\displaystyle x_{\pm}=\,\frac{1}{4(1-y)+\rho_{2}}\left[(2-y)(2+\rho_{2}-2y-2z)\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\left.\pm 2\sqrt{(y^{2}-\rho_{2})[(y-1+z)^{2}-\rho_{2}y]}\right]\,.

(85)

In the appendix A of
Nason & Oleari, 1998
, the authors derived the PS for four particles with the same mass: this is consistent with our parametrisation in eq. (
83
) when
m_{1}=m_{2}=m
.

After setting the mass
m_{2}
to zero, we need to change the order of the
y
and
z
integrations since we want to be differential in
z
, i.e. the energy of the quark
k_{p}
measured in the
q
-frame,

\displaystyle\int_{\rho_{1}}^{1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\sqrt{1-\frac{\rho_{1}}{y}}\int_{0}^{1-y}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z=\int_{0}^{1-\rho_{1}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z\int_{\rho_{1}}^{1-z}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\sqrt{1-\frac{\rho_{1}}{y}}\,.

(86)

Thus we obtain

\displaystyle\int\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\Phi_{4}=

\displaystyle\,\frac{Q^{4}}{(2\pi)^{6}}\int_{0}^{1-\rho_{1}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z\int_{\rho_{1}}^{1-z}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\sqrt{1-\frac{\rho_{1}}{y}}

\displaystyle\,\int_{1-z-y}^{\frac{1-z-y}{1-z}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\int_{0}^{1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\phi\,.

(87)

The integrated fragmentation antenna (
76
) using this alternative parametrisation reads as

\displaystyle\mathcal{\bar{B}}_{ih\bar{h}\bar{i}}^{0,\text{id.}i}(z)=

\displaystyle\,\frac{Q^{4}}{8\pi}\int_{\rho_{1}}^{1-z}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\sqrt{1-\frac{\rho_{1}}{y}}\int_{1-z-y}^{\frac{1-z-y}{1-z}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\,\int_{0}^{1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\cos\theta\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\phi\,\bar{B}_{4}^{0}\,.

(88)

A first check is the comparison of the volumes obtained by using the two PS descriptions. In the massless case, the parametrisations of eq. (
76
) and eq. (
88
) both give the following volume

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{\text{massless}}=\frac{Q^{4}}{8}(1-z)z\,.

(89)

By restoring the mass dependence, we again observe that the two PS parametrisations give the same volume, namely

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{\text{massive}}=\frac{Q^{4}z}{16(1-z)}\left\{\ 2m^{2}\left(4-4z+\frac{4m^{2}}{Q^{2}}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\left[2\log\left(1+\sqrt{1-\frac{4m^{2}}{Q^{2}(1-z)}}\right)+\log\left(\frac{4m^{2}}{Q^{2}(1-z)}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\left.+\left(2-2z+\frac{4m^{2}}{Q^{2}}\right)(1-z)\sqrt{1-\frac{4m^{2}}{Q^{2}(1-z)}}\right\}\,.

(90)

After checking the volume, we perform a numerical integration of the
\bar{B}_{4}^{0}
using the second parametrisation of eq. (
88
) and find agreement with the analytic result of eq. (
77
).

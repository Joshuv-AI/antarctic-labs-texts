New multivariable mean from nonlinear matrix equation associated to the harmonic mean
Sejong Kim and Vatsalkumar N. Mer

Abstract
. Various multivariable means have been defined for positive definite matrices, such as the Cartan mean, Wasserstein mean, and Rényi power mean. These multivariable means have corresponding matrix equations. In this paper, we consider the following non-linear matrix equation: X = [ ∑ i = 1 n w i ​ [ ( 1 − t ) ​ X + t ​ A i ] − 1 ] − 1 , X=\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}[(1-t)X+tA_{i}]^{-1}\right]^{-1}, where t ∈ ( 0 , 1 ] t\in(0,1] . We prove that this equation has a unique solution and define a new mean, which we denote as G t ​ ( ω , 𝔸 ) G_{t}(\omega;\mathbb{A}) . We explore important properties of the mean G t ​ ( ω , 𝔸 ) G_{t}(\omega;\mathbb{A}) including the relationship with matrix power mean, and show that the mean G t ​ ( ω , 𝔸 ) G_{t}(\omega;\mathbb{A}) is monotone in the parameter t t . Finally, we connect the mean G t ​ ( ω , 𝔸 ) G_{t}(\omega;\mathbb{A}) to a barycenter for the log-determinant divergence. 1.
Introduction

Several various types of multivariable means are found on the open convex cone
\mathbb{P}_{m}
consisting of positive definite matrices such as the Cartan mean, Wasserstein mean, and Rényi power mean. Correspondingly, each of these means is associated with specific matrix equations. For instance, the Cartan mean
\Lambda
is defined as a unique minimizer of the weighted sum of squares of Riemannian trace distances to each point: for any
\mathbb{A}=(A_{1},\ldots,A_{n})\in\mathbb{P}_{m}^{n}
and a positive probability vector
\omega=(w_{1},\dots,w_{n})

\Lambda(\omega;\mathbb{A})=\underset{X\in\mathbb{P}_{m}}{\arg\min}\,\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}d_{R}^{2}(X,A_{i}),

where
d_{R}(A,B)=\|\log(A^{-1/2}BA^{-1/2})\|_{2}
denotes the Riemannian trace distance between
A
and
B
.
We obtain from
[
14
]
that the Cartan mean coincides with the unique positive definite solution
X
of the following matrix equation, called the Karcher equation,

\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}\log(X^{-1/2}A_{i}X^{-1/2})=0.

An interesting approach of the Cartan mean is the power mean
P_{t}(\omega;\mathbb{A})
, defined as the unique solution
X\in\mathbb{P}_{m}
of the nonlinear matrix equation

\displaystyle X=\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}X\#_{t}A_{i}

for
t\in(0,1]
. We define
P_{t}(\omega;\mathbb{A})=P_{-t}(\omega;\mathbb{A}^{-1})^{-1}
for
t\in[0,1)
. It has been shown in
[
18
,
20
]
that the power mean
P_{t}(\omega;\mathbb{A})
converges to the Cartan mean
\Lambda(\omega;\mathbb{A})
as
t\to 0
, and furthermore

P_{-1}=\mathcal{H}\leq\cdots\leq P_{-t}\leq P_{-s}\leq\cdots\leq\Lambda=P_{0}\leq\cdots\leq P_{s}\leq P_{t}\leq\cdots\leq P_{1}=\mathcal{A}

(1.1)

for
0<s\leq t\leq 1
, where
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{H}
denote the weighted arithmetic and harmonic means, respectively.

The Wasserstein mean
\Omega
is defined as the least squares mean for the Bures-Wasserstein metric: for any
\mathbb{A}=(A_{1},\ldots,A_{n})\in\mathbb{P}_{m}^{n}
and
\omega=(w_{1},\dots,w_{n})\in\Delta_{n}

\Omega(\omega;\mathbb{A})=\underset{X\in\mathbb{P}_{m}}{\arg\min}\,\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}d_{W}^{2}(X,A_{i}),

where
d_{W}(A,B)=\left[\tr(A+B-2(A^{1/2}BA^{1/2})^{1/2})\right]^{1/2}
denotes the Bures-Wasserstein distance between
A
and
B
.
Note from
[
1
,
6
]
that the Wasserstein mean coincides with the unique positive definite solution
X
of the following matrix equation

X=\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}(X^{1/2}A_{i}X^{1/2})^{1/2}.

(1.2)

As a generalization of (
1.2
), the Rényi power mean
\mathcal{R}_{t,z}
for
0<t\leq z<1
is defined in
[
10
]
as a unique positive definite solution of the matrix equation

X=\sum_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}\left(A_{j}^{\frac{1-t}{2z}}X^{\frac{t}{z}}A_{j}^{\frac{1-t}{2z}}\right)^{z}.

where
Q_{t,z}(A,B):=\left(A^{\frac{1-t}{2z}}B^{\frac{t}{z}}A^{\frac{1-t}{2z}}\right)^{z}
is the matrix version of the
t
-
z
Rényi relative entropy
[
3
,
21
]
. One can see that
\mathcal{R}_{t,z}=\Omega
when
t=z=1/2
. See
[
7
,
13
]
for more properties about Wasserstein mean and Rényi power mean.

As we have seen previously, multivariable means correspond to their own matrix equations. In this paper we consider the following nonlinear matrix equation

X=\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}[(1-t)X+tA_{i}]^{-1}\right]^{-1},

where
t\in(0,1]
. We obtain new multivariable mean by proving that the right-hand side of the above equation is a strict contraction for the Thompson metric. In Section 3, we explore the fundamental properties of the mean from in the view point of Ando-Li-Mathias’s multivariable geometric mean and and we establish bounds in terms of the operator norm. One of the interesting results, appeared in Section 4, is the monotonicity for parameter
t
, which means that new multivariable mean interpolates the weighted arithmetic and harmonic means. Furthermore, we prove the relationship between
G_{t}
and the matrix power mean
P_{t}
. Finally, we see that new multivariable mean is connected to the right mean for quantum divergence when
\omega
is a uniformly-distributed probability vector. From the fact that new multivariable mean for
n=2
coincides with the metric geometric mean when
t=1/2
and
\omega=(1/2,1/2)
, we give some open questions.

2.
Thompson metric

The Thompson metric on
\mathbb{P}_{m}
is defined by

d(A,B)=\|\log(A^{-1/2}BA^{-1/2})\|
. It is known that
d
is a complete metric on
\mathbb{P}_{m}
and that

d(A,B)=\max\{\log M(B/A),\log M(A/B)\},

(2.3)

where
M(B/A)=\inf\{\alpha>0:B\leq\alpha A\}=\lambda_{1}(A^{-1/2}BA^{-1/2})
: see
[
9
,
22
,
24
]
.
The geometric mean curve

\displaystyle[0,1]\ni t\mapsto A\#_{t}B:=A^{1/2}(A^{-1/2}BA^{-1/2})^{t}A^{1/2}.

(2.4)

is a minimal geodesic from
A
to
B
for the Thompson metric. We call
A\#_{t}B
the
weighted geometric mean
of
A
and
B
on
\mathbb{P}_{m}
, and simply write
A\#B:=A\#_{1/2}B
.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
9
,
17
]

Basic properties of the Thompson metric on
\mathbb{P}_{m}
include

(1)

d(A,B)=d(A^{-1},B^{-1})=d(SAS^{*},SBS^{*})

for any
S\in\mathrm{GL};

(2)

d(A\#_{t}B,C\#_{t}D)\leq(1-t)d(A,C)+td(B,D)
for any
t\in[0,1];

(3)

d(A\#_{s}B,A\#_{t}B)=|s-t|d(A,B)
for any
s,t\in[0,1].

By Lemma
2.1
the geometric mean satisfies the following convexity for
d
:

d(A\#_{s}B,C\#_{t}D)\leq(1-t)\,d(A,C)+t\,d(B,D)+|s-t|\,d(C,D)

(2.5)

for
A,B,C,D\in\mathbb{P}_{m}
and
s,t\in[0,1]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

The Thompson metric satisfies
d(A+B,C+D)\leq\max\{d(A,C),d(B,D)\}
for any
A,B,C,D\in\mathbb{P}_{m}
. So inductively we have for any
A_{i},B_{i}\in\mathbb{P}_{m},\ 1\leq i\leq n

d\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}A_{i},\sum_{i=1}^{n}B_{i}\right)\leq\underset{1\leq i\leq n}{\max}\{d(A_{i},B_{i})\}.

The following is the additive contraction principle on
\mathbb{P}_{m}
in
[
19
]
:

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{P}_{m}
. Then

d(A+X,A+Y)\leq\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\beta}d(X,Y)

for any
X,Y\in\mathbb{P}_{m}
, where
\alpha=\max\{\lambda_{1}(X),\lambda_{1}(Y)\}
and
\beta=\lambda_{m}(A)
.

3.
Multivariable means from nonlinear matrix equation

Let
\mathbb{A}=(A_{1},\ldots,A_{n})\in\mathbb{P}_{m}^{n}
and let
\omega=(w_{1},\dots,w_{n})\in\Delta_{n}
, the set of all positive probability vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
We solve in this section the following nonlinear matrix equation

X=\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}[(1-t)X+tA_{i}]^{-1}\right]^{-1}

(3.6)

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\mathbb{A}=(A_{1},\ldots,A_{n})\in\mathbb{P}_{m}^{n}
and let
\omega=(w_{1},\dots,w_{n})\in\Delta_{n}
. Then the equation (
3.6
) has a unique positive definite solution for
t\in(0,1]
.

Proof.

When
t=1
, we obtain the weighted harmonic mean
\displaystyle X=\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}A_{i}^{-1}\right]^{-1}
. Assume that
t\in(0,1)
. Set
\displaystyle f(X)=\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}[(1-t)X+tA_{i}]^{-1}\right]^{-1}
for
X\in\mathbb{P}_{m}
. By Lemma
2.1
, Lemma
2.2
, and Lemma
2.3

\begin{split}\displaystyle d(f(X),f(Y))&=d\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}[(1-t)X+tA_{i}]^{-1},\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}[(1-t)Y+tA_{i}]^{-1}\right)\\
&\leq\underset{1\leq i\leq n}{\max}\,d((1-t)X+tA_{i},(1-t)Y+tA_{i})\\
&\leq\underset{1\leq i\leq n}{\max}\,\frac{(1-t)\alpha}{(1-t)\alpha+t\beta_{i}}d(X,Y),\end{split}

where
\displaystyle\alpha=\max\{\lambda_{1}(X),\lambda_{1}(Y)\}
, and
\beta_{i}=\lambda_{m}(A_{i})
. Note that

\displaystyle 0<\frac{(1-t)\alpha}{(1-t)\alpha+t\beta_{i}}<1,

since
\beta_{i}>0
for all
i=1,\dots,n
.
By the Banach fixed point theorem, there exists a unique solution
X\in\mathbb{P}_{m}
of the equation
X=f(X)
.
∎

Definition 3.2
.

Let
\mathbb{A}=(A_{1},\ldots,A_{n})\in\mathbb{P}_{m}^{n}
and let
\omega=(w_{1},\dots,w_{n})\in\Delta_{n}
. We define the matrix mean
G_{t}(\omega;\mathbb{A})
for
t\in(0,1]
as the unique positive definite solution
X
of the equation (
3.6
).

Remark 3.3
.

The function
\displaystyle f(X)=\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}[(1-t)X+tA_{i}]^{-1}\right]^{-1}
for
X\in\mathbb{P}_{m}
is monotone increasing. Moreover, the Banach fixed point theorem yields that

\lim_{k\to\infty}f^{k}(Z)=G_{t}(\omega;\mathbb{A})\ \textrm{for any}\ Z\in\mathbb{P}_{m}.

Remark 3.4
.

The unique positive definite solution
X
of the equation (
3.6
) satisfies the following alternative nonlinear equation

\displaystyle I=\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}X^{1/2}\left[(1-t)X+tA_{i}\right]^{-1}X^{1/2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}\left[(1-t)I+tX^{-1/2}A_{i}X^{-1/2}\right]^{-1}.

This can be written as

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\frac{1-t}{t}}\left(\omega;X^{-1/2}\mathbb{A}X^{-1/2}\right)=I,

(3.7)

where
\displaystyle X^{-1/2}\mathbb{A}X^{-1/2}:=(X^{-1/2}A_{1}X^{-1/2},\ldots,X^{-1/2}A_{n}X^{-1/2})
. Here,
\mathcal{R}_{\frac{1-t}{t}}
denotes the resolvent mean of parameter
\displaystyle\frac{1-t}{t}

[
15
]
. In other words,
G_{t}(\omega;\mathbb{A})
is the unique positive definite solution
X
of the equation (
3.7
).

We see fundamental properties of
G_{t}
in perspective on the Ando-Li-Mathias’s properties of multivariable geometric mean.
Let
p\in\mathbb{R}
,
\sigma
a permutation on
\{1,\dots,n\}
, and
S\in\mathrm{GL}_{m}
, the general linear group of all
m\times m
invertible matrices. For convenience, we denote

\begin{split}\mathbb{A}^{p}&=(A_{1}^{p},\ldots,A_{n}^{p})\\
S\mathbb{A}S^{*}&=(SA_{1}S^{*},\ldots,SA_{n}S^{*})\\
\mathbb{A}_{\sigma}&=(A_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,A_{\sigma(n)})\\
\omega_{\sigma}&=(w_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,w_{\sigma(n)}).\end{split}

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\mathbb{A}=(A_{1},\ldots,A_{n}),\mathbb{B}=(B_{1},\ldots,B_{n})\in\mathbb{P}_{m}^{n}
, and let
\omega=(w_{1},\dots,w_{n})\in\Delta_{n}
. Then for
t\in(0,1]

(1)

\mathrm{(Idempotency)}

G_{t}(\omega;A,\dots,A)=A
for any
A\in\mathbb{P}_{m}
;

(2)

\mathrm{(Homogeneity)}

G_{t}(\omega;\alpha\mathbb{A})=\alpha G_{t}(\omega;\mathbb{A})
for any
\alpha>0
;

(3)

\mathrm{(Permutation\ invariance)}

G_{t}(\omega_{\sigma};\mathbb{A}_{\sigma})=G_{t}(\omega;\mathbb{A})
for any permutation
\sigma
on
\{1,\dots,n\}
;

(4)

\mathrm{(Monotonicity)}

G_{t}(\omega;\mathbb{A})\leq G_{t}(\omega;\mathbb{B})
whenever
A_{i}\leq B_{i}
for all
i=1,\dots,n
;

(5)

\mathrm{(Continuity)}

G_{t}(\omega;\cdot):\mathbb{P}_{m}^{n}\to\mathbb{P}_{m}
is non-expansive for the Thompson metric:

d(G_{t}(\omega;\mathbb{A}),G_{t}(\omega;\mathbb{B}))\leq\underset{1\leq i\leq n}{\max}\,d(A_{i},B_{i});

(6)

\mathrm{(Congruence\ invariance)}

G_{t}(\omega;S\mathbb{A}S^{*})=SG_{t}(\omega;\mathbb{A})S^{*}
for any
S\in\mathrm{GL}_{m}
;

(7)

\mathrm{(Self-duality)}

G_{1-t}(\omega;\mathbb{A}^{-1})=G_{t}(\omega;\mathbb{A})^{-1}
for
t\in(0,1)
;

(8)

\mathrm{(Arithmetic-G-harmonic\ mean\ inequalities)}

\mathcal{H}(\omega;\mathbb{A})=\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}A_{i}^{-1}\right]^{-1}\leq G_{t}(\omega;\mathbb{A})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}A_{i}=\mathcal{A}(\omega;\mathbb{A}).

Proof.

Note that
\displaystyle G_{t=1}(\omega;\mathbb{A})=\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}A_{i}^{-1}\right]^{-1}
holds all properties, so we assume
t\in(0,1)
in the following. Items (1)-(3) are obvious.

(4)

Let
X=G_{t}(\omega;\mathbb{A})
, and let
\displaystyle g(Z)=\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}[(1-t)Z+tB_{i}]^{-1}\right]^{-1}
for
Z\in\mathbb{P}_{m}
. Assume that
A_{i}\leq B_{i}
for all
i=1,\dots,n
. Then

X=\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}[(1-t)X+tA_{i}]^{-1}\right]^{-1}\leq\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}[(1-t)X+tB_{i}]^{-1}\right]^{-1}=g(X).

Inductively,
X\leq g(X)\leq\cdots\leq g^{k}(X)
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
and hence, by Remark
3.3

X\leq G_{t}(\omega;\mathbb{B})
.

(5)

It is known from
[
16
]
that every monotonic and subhomogeneous map is non-expansive for the Thompson metric.
From item (2) the homogeneity of
G
implies the sub-homogeneity, and hence,
G
is non-expansive by item (4).

(6)

Let
X=G_{t}(\omega;S\mathbb{A}S^{*})
for any
S\in\mathrm{GL}_{m}
.
By Proposition
3.1
we have

\begin{split}X&=\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}[(1-t)X+tSA_{i}S^{*}]^{-1}\right]^{-1}\\
&=\left[(S^{*})^{-1}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}[(1-t)S^{-1}X(S^{*})^{-1}+tA_{i}]^{-1}\right]S^{-1}\right]^{-1}\\
&=S\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}[(1-t)S^{-1}X(S^{*})^{-1}+tA_{i}]^{-1}\right]^{-1}S^{*}.\end{split}

Thus,
S^{-1}X(S^{*})^{-1}=G_{t}(\omega;\mathbb{A})
, so it is proved.

(7)

Let
X=G_{t}(\omega;\mathbb{A})
. From Remark
3.4

\displaystyle I=\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}\left[(1-t)I+tX^{-1/2}A_{i}X^{-1/2}\right]^{-1}.

Applying the formula
(A+B)^{-1}=A^{-1}-A^{-1}(A^{-1}+B^{-1})^{-1}A^{-1}
in
[
25
]
to the above equation yields

\begin{split}\displaystyle I&=\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}\left\{\frac{1}{1-t}I-\frac{1}{(1-t)^{2}}\left[\frac{1}{1-t}I+\frac{1}{t}X^{1/2}A_{i}^{-1}X^{1/2}\right]^{-1}\right\}\\
&=\frac{1}{1-t}I-\frac{1}{(1-t)^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}\left[\frac{1}{1-t}I+\frac{1}{t}X^{1/2}A_{i}^{-1}X^{1/2}\right]^{-1}\\
&=\frac{1}{1-t}I-\frac{t}{1-t}\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}\left[tI+(1-t)X^{1/2}A_{i}^{-1}X^{1/2}\right]^{-1}.\end{split}

Equivalently, we have

\displaystyle I=\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}\left[tI+(1-t)X^{1/2}A_{i}^{-1}X^{1/2}\right]^{-1}.

Thus,
X^{-1}=G_{1-t}(\omega;\mathbb{A}^{-1})
.

(8)

Let
X=G_{t}(\omega;\mathbb{A})
. Then by the arithmetic-harmonic mean inequality

X=\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}[(1-t)X+tA_{i}]^{-1}\right]^{-1}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}[(1-t)X+tA_{i}]=(1-t)X+t\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}A_{i}.

So we obtain that
\displaystyle X\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}A_{i}
.

Replacing
t
and
A_{i}
by
1-t
and
A_{i}^{-1}
for all
i=1,\dots,n
in the previous result, we have

G_{1-t}(\omega;\mathbb{A}^{-1})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}A_{i}^{-1}.

By item (7) and taking inverse on both sides, we obtain
\displaystyle G_{t}(\omega;\mathbb{A})\geq\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}A_{i}^{-1}\right]^{-1}
.

∎

Remark 3.6
.

From Theorem
3.5
(8), the multivariable mean
G_{t}
is the Lie-Trotter mean
[
12
]
. As a consequence,

\lim_{p\to 0}G_{t}(\omega;A_{1}^{p},\dots,A_{n}^{p})^{1/p}=\exp\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}\log A_{i}\right),

(3.8)

where the right-hand side is known as the log-Euclidean mean.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
\mathbb{A}=(A_{1},\ldots,A_{n})\in\mathbb{P}_{m}^{n}
, and
\omega=(w_{1},\dots,w_{n})\in\Delta_{n}
. Then for
t\in(0,1]

(1)

\displaystyle\|G_{1-t}(\omega;\mathbb{A})\|\leq\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}\|A_{i}\|^{t}\right]^{1/t}
for an operator norm
\|\cdot\|
,

(2)

\Phi(G_{t}(\omega;\mathbb{A}))\leq G_{t}(\omega;\Phi(\mathbb{A}))
for any positive linear map
\Phi
, where

\Phi(\mathbb{A}):=(\Phi(A_{1}),\dots,\Phi(A_{n})).

Proof.

Let
X=G_{t}(\omega;\mathbb{A})
.

(1)

By
[
5
, Theorem 3]
and the sub-multiplicativity of operator norm,

\|A\#_{t}B\|\leq\|A^{1-t}B^{t}\|\leq\|A\|^{1-t}\|B^{t}\|

(3.9)

for
A,B\in\mathbb{P}_{m}
. Since

X^{-1}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}((1-t)X+tA_{i})^{-1}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}(X\#_{t}A_{i})^{-1}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}(X^{-1}\#_{t}A_{i}^{-1})

by the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality, we have from (
3.9
)

\|X^{-1}\|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}\|X^{-1}\#_{t}A_{i}^{-1}\|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}\|X^{-1}\|^{1-t}\|A_{i}^{-1}\|^{t}.

Solving for
\|X^{-1}\|
, we get

\|X^{-1}\|=\|G_{t}(\omega;\mathbb{A})^{-1}\|\leq\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}\|A_{i}^{-1}\|^{t}\right]^{1/t}.

Applying Theorem
3.5
(7) and replacing
A_{i}
by
A_{i}^{-1}
we obtain the desired inequality.

(2)

Set
Y=\Phi(X)
for any positive linear map
\Phi
.
Then we have from
[
4
, Theorem 4.1.5]

\begin{split}Y&=\Phi(X)=\Phi\left(\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}[(1-t)X+tA_{i}]^{-1}\right]^{-1}\right)\\
&\leq\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}[\Phi((1-t)X+tA_{i})]^{-1}\right]^{-1}=\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}[(1-t)\Phi(X)+t\Phi(A_{i})]^{-1}\right]^{-1}=:h(Y).\end{split}

Since the map
h
is monotone increasing,
Y\leq h(Y)\leq h^{2}(Y)\leq\cdots\leq h^{k}(Y)
for all
k\geq 2
. Taking limit as
k\to\infty
and applying Remark
3.3
yields

\Phi(G_{t}(\omega;\mathbb{A}))=Y\leq\lim_{k\to\infty}h^{k}(Y)=G_{t}(\omega;\Phi(\mathbb{A})).

∎

4.
Inequalities and monotonicity on parameters

In this section we focus on parameters of the multivariable mean
G_{t}
, and see that it interpolates backward the arithmetic mean and harmonic mean.

Theorem 4.1
.

For
0<s\leq t\leq 1

G_{s}(\omega;\mathbb{A})\geq G_{t}(\omega;\mathbb{A}).

Proof.

Let
X=G_{s}(\omega;\mathbb{A})
and
Y=G_{t}(\omega;\mathbb{A})
. Assume that
0<s\leq t\leq 1
. Set
\displaystyle f(Z)=\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}[(1-s)Z+sA_{i}]^{-1}\right]^{-1}
for
Z\in\mathbb{P}_{m}
. Then

\begin{split}f(Z)&=\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}\left\{\left(1-\frac{s}{t}\right)Z+\frac{s}{t}((1-t)Z+tA_{i})\right\}^{-1}\right]^{-1}\\
&\geq\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}\left\{\left(1-\frac{s}{t}\right)Z^{-1}+\frac{s}{t}((1-t)Z+tA_{i})^{-1}\right\}\right]^{-1}\\
&=\left[\left(1-\frac{s}{t}\right)Z^{-1}+\frac{s}{t}\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}((1-t)Z+tA_{i})^{-1}\right]^{-1}.\end{split}

The inequality follows from the two-variable arithmetic-harmonic mean inequality such that
[(1-\lambda)A+\lambda B]^{-1}\leq(1-\lambda)A^{-1}+\lambda B^{-1}
for any
A,B\in\mathbb{P}_{m}
and
\lambda\in[0,1]
.
Substituting
Z=Y
in the above and applying Proposition
3.1
to
Y
yields

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\mathbb{A}=(A_{1},\ldots,A_{n})\in\mathbb{P}_{m}^{n}

5.
Quantum divergence and barycenters

For any
\alpha\in(-1,1)
and
A,B\in\mathbb{P}_{m}
, the
log-determinant
\alpha
-divergence

D_{\alpha}(A|B)
is defined by

D_{\alpha}(A|B):=\frac{4}{1-\alpha^{2}}\log\frac{\det\left(\frac{1-\alpha}{2}A+\frac{1+\alpha}{2}B\right)}{(\det A)^{(1-\alpha)/2}(\det B)^{(1+\alpha)/2}}.

(5.16)

One can see from (
5.16
) that

\begin{split}D_{-1}(A|B)&:=\lim_{\alpha\to-1}D_{\alpha}(A|B)=\tr(A^{-1}B-I)-\log\det(A^{-1}B),\\
D_{1}(A|B)&:=\lim_{\alpha\to 1}D_{\alpha}(A|B)=\tr(B^{-1}A-I)-\log\det(B^{-1}A).\end{split}

This is a one-parameter family of divergences which is related with the Stein’s loss.
See
[
8
]
for more information.

Definition 5.1
.

A smooth function
\Phi:\mathbb{P}_{m}\times\mathbb{P}_{m}\to[0,\infty)
is a quantum divergence if it satisfies the following conditions:

(1)

\Phi(A,B)\geq 0
, and
\Phi(A,B)=0
if and only if
A=B
.

(2)

The first derivative
D\Phi
with respect to the second variable vanishes on the diagonal. That is,

\left.D\Phi(A,B)\right|_{B=A}=0.

(3)

The second derivative
D^{2}\Phi
is non-negative on the diagonal. That is,

\left.\frac{\partial^{2}\Phi}{\partial B^{2}}(A,B)\right|_{B=A}(X,X)\geq 0,\quad\forall X\in\mathbb{H}_{m}.

Theorem 5.2
.

For any
\alpha\in(-1,1)
, the log-determinant
\alpha
-divergence
D_{\alpha}(A|B):\mathbb{P}_{m}\times\mathbb{P}_{m}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
is a quantum divergence.

Proof.

The first condition of Definition
5.1
for the log-determinant
\alpha
-divergence
D_{\alpha}
has been proved in
[
8
, Proposition 3.5]
.

(2) For
X\in\mathbb{H}_{m}
we have

\frac{\partial D_{\alpha}(A|B)}{\partial B}(X)=\frac{2}{1-\alpha}\tr\left[(-B^{-1}+(\frac{1-\alpha}{2}A+\frac{1+\alpha}{2}B)^{-1})X\right].

When
B=A
, we get
\displaystyle\left.\frac{\partial D_{\alpha}(A|B)}{\partial B}\right|_{B=A}(X)=0
.

(3) For
X\in\mathbb{H}_{m}
we have

\begin{split}&\frac{\partial^{2}D_{\alpha}(A|B)}{\partial B^{2}}(X,X)\\
&=\frac{2}{1-\alpha}\tr\left[(B^{-1}XB^{-1}-\frac{1+\alpha}{2}(\frac{1-\alpha}{2}A+\frac{1+\alpha}{2}B)^{-1}X(\frac{1-\alpha}{2}A+\frac{1+\alpha}{2}B)^{-1})X\right].\end{split}

When
B=A
, we get

\left.{\frac{\partial^{2}D_{\alpha}(A|B)}{\partial B^{2}}}\right|_{\hbox{\hskip 6.36502pt\hskip-6.36502pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle B=A$}\hskip-6.36502pt\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{-1.0pt}{\hbox{}}\hskip 0.0pt\hskip 6.36502pt}}(X,X)=\tr(A^{-1}XA^{-1}X)=\tr((A^{-1/2}XA^{-1/2})^{2})\geq 0.

Hence,
D_{\alpha}(A|B)
is a quantum divergence.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
A,B\in\mathbb{P}_{m}
and
\alpha\in[-1,1]
. Then

(i)

D_{\alpha}(PAQ|PBQ)=D_{\alpha}(A|B)
for any
P,Q\in\mathrm{GL}_{m}
,

(ii)

D_{\alpha}(A^{-1}|B^{-1})=D_{\alpha}(B|A)
, and

(iii)

D_{\alpha}(A^{t}|B^{t})\leq t\,D_{\alpha}(A|B)
for any
t\in[0,1]
.

Proof.

Let
A,B\in\mathbb{P}_{m}
. It is enough to show (i), (ii) and (iii) for
\alpha\in(-1,1)
due to the limit process.

(i)

Since
\det(XY)=\det X\det Y
for any square matrices
X,Y
, we obtain (i) easily.

(ii)

Since

\begin{split}\frac{\det\left(\frac{1-\alpha}{2}A^{-1}+\frac{1+\alpha}{2}B^{-1}\right)}{(\det A)^{-(1-\alpha)/2}(\det B)^{-(1+\alpha)/2}}&=\frac{\det\left(A^{-1}\left(\frac{1-\alpha}{2}B+\frac{1+\alpha}{2}A\right)B^{-1}\right)}{(\det A)^{-(1-\alpha)/2}(\det B)^{-(1+\alpha)/2}}\\
&=\frac{\det\left(\frac{1-\alpha}{2}B+\frac{1+\alpha}{2}A\right)}{(\det A)^{(1+\alpha)/2}(\det B)^{(1-\alpha)/2}},\end{split}

we get

D_{\alpha}(A^{-1}|B^{-1})=\frac{4}{1-\alpha^{2}}\log\frac{\det\left(\frac{1-\alpha}{2}B+\frac{1+\alpha}{2}A\right)}{(\det B)^{(1-\alpha)/2}(\det A)^{(1+\alpha)/2}}=D_{\alpha}(B|A).

(iii)

Since the map
X\in\mathbb{P}_{m}\mapsto X^{t}
is concave for any
t\in[0,1]
,

\frac{1-\alpha}{2}A^{t}+\frac{1+\alpha}{2}B^{t}\leq\left(\frac{1-\alpha}{2}A+\frac{1+\alpha}{2}B\right)^{t}

and so

\begin{split}\frac{\det\left(\frac{1-\alpha}{2}A^{t}+\frac{1+\alpha}{2}B^{t}\right)}{(\det A)^{t(1-\alpha)/2}(\det B)^{t(1+\alpha)/2}}&\leq\frac{\det\left(\frac{1-\alpha}{2}A+\frac{1+\alpha}{2}B\right)^{t}}{(\det A)^{t(1-\alpha)/2}(\det B)^{t(1+\alpha)/2}}\\
&=\left[\frac{\det\left(\frac{1-\alpha}{2}A+\frac{1+\alpha}{2}B\right)}{(\det A)^{(1-\alpha)/2}(\det B)^{(1+\alpha)/2}}\right]^{t}.\end{split}

Thus,
D_{\alpha}(A^{t}|B^{t})\leq t\,D_{\alpha}(A|B)
.

∎

Theorem 5.4
.

The metric

is invariant under congruence transformations and inversion, i.e.,

d_{S}(SAS^{*},SBS^{*})=d_{S}(A,B)=d_{S}(A^{-1},B^{-1})

It has been shown in

G(\mathbb{A})=\underset{X\in\mathbb{P}_{m}}{\arg\min}\,\sum_{i=1}^{n}D_{\alpha}(A_{i}|X)

exists uniquely, and we call it the

. Indeed, the gradient of the objective function
\displaystyle\phi(X)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}D_{\alpha}(A_{i}|X)
is

\nabla\phi(X)=\frac{2}{1-\alpha}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{1-\alpha}{2}A_{i}+\frac{1+\alpha}{2}X\right)^{-1}-nX^{-1}\right]

and its Hessian is

\begin{split}&\nabla^{2}\phi(X)\\
&=\frac{2}{1-\alpha}\left[nX^{-1}\otimes X^{-1}-\frac{1+\alpha}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{1-\alpha}{2}A_{i}+\frac{1+\alpha}{2}X\right)^{-1}\otimes\left(\frac{1-\alpha}{2}A_{i}+\frac{1+\alpha}{2}X\right)^{-1}\right].\end{split}

\displaystyle\left((1-\alpha)A_{i}+(1+\alpha)X\right)^{-1}<(1+\alpha)^{-1}X^{-1}

for the local optimality of

.
Note that

coincides with the unique solution of the following matrix equation

O=\nabla\phi(X)=\frac{2}{1-\alpha}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{1-\alpha}{2}A_{i}+\frac{1+\alpha}{2}X\right)^{-1}-nX^{-1}\right].

It is equivalent by setting

Proposition 5.5
.

Let

and

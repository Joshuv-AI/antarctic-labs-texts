Uses of Killing and Killing-Yano Tensors
Özgür Sarıoğlu

Abstract
In this contribution we have collected some facts about Killing and Killing-Yano tensors that we feel are of general interest for researchers working on problems that rely on differential geometry. We also include some of our recent studies pertaining to currents, charges and (super)invariants for particles and tensionless strings.

2
Killing tensors

In this section we introduce Killing tensors (KTs), their generalisation to conformal Killing
tensors (CKTs) and illustrate their usefulness in a couple of examples.

An
n
​ th rank Killing tensor is a completely symmetric tensor
f^{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{n}}
that
satisfies the equation

\displaystyle\nabla_{(\mu_{1}}f_{\mu_{2}\dots\mu_{n+1})}=0~,

(1)

whereas a conformal Killing tensor is trace-free and satisfies

\displaystyle\nabla_{(\mu_{1}}f_{\mu_{2}\dots\mu_{n+1})}=ng_{(\mu_{1}\mu_{2}}\bar{f}_{\mu_{3}\dots\mu_{n+1})}~,

(2)

where the rank
n-1
tensor
\bar{f}
is determined by tracing both sides:

\displaystyle\bar{f}_{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{n-1}}=\frac{1}{D+2(n-1)}\nabla_{\nu}f^{\nu}_{~\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{n-1}}~.

(3)

2.1
Uses of KTs

In this subsection we indicate some of the most important uses of KTs.

Conservation along geodesics

Let
x^{\mu}(\tau)
be a geodesic, an over dot denote
\tau
-derivative and let
p^{\mu}:=\dot{x}^{\mu}

be the tangent vector to the geodesic. The covariant directional derivative along the geodesic is
denoted

\displaystyle\frac{D}{d\tau}=p^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}

(4)

and the geodesic equation is

\displaystyle\frac{Dp^{\mu}}{d\tau}=0~.

(5)

If
f
is an
n
th rank Killing tensor, the quantity

\displaystyle Q=f_{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{n}}p^{\mu_{1}}\dots p^{\mu_{n}}

(6)

is then conserved along the geodesic
[
23
]
,

\displaystyle\frac{D}{d\tau}Q=p^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}Q=p^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}f_{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{n}}p^{\mu_{1}}\dots p^{\mu_{n}}

(7)

\displaystyle=\nabla_{(\mu}f_{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{n})}p^{\mu}p^{\mu_{1}}\dots p^{\mu_{n}}=0~,

where we used (
5
) and (
1
). Note that this is a purely geometric construction. Since the equations of motion of point-particles are geodesics it can be used to construct invariants for
particles moving in geometries that allow KTs. It can also be extended to provide invariants for
spinning particles
[
13
]
and for tensionless strings
[
22
]
.

Separation of variables

Here we briefly describe one of the most important uses of KTs, for separation of variables. The
literature on this is vast, see, e.g.,
[
1
,
6
]
. Below we sketch
the logic following the presentation in
[
6
]
.

Let
S=W(X)+{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}t\mu^{2}
be Hamilton’s principal function and
W
his
characteristic function, for a system with Hamiltonian
H={\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}pg^{-1}p
. The
Hamilton-Jacobi equation

\displaystyle H(X,\frac{\partial S}{\partial q},t)+\frac{\partial S}{\partial t}=0~

(8)

then reads

\displaystyle g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}W\partial_{\nu}W+\mu^{2}=0~,

(9)

where
\mu
is a constant.
Assume that the coordinates
(X)
can be divided into two distinct groups denoted by
(x)
and

(y)
. If the following conditions are satisfied,

\displaystyle W=W_{x}(x_{1},...x_{k})+W_{y}(y_{k+1}...y_{n})

(10)

\displaystyle g^{\mu\nu}=\frac{X^{\mu\nu}(x)+Y^{\mu\nu}(y)}{f_{x}-f_{y}}~,\hskip 10.00002ptX^{y\nu}=0~,~~~Y^{x\nu}=0~,

\displaystyle\partial_{y}f_{x}=0,\hskip 10.00002pt\partial_{x}f_{y}=0~,

then (
9
) separates as

\displaystyle X^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}W\partial_{\nu}W+\mu^{2}f_{x}=-Y^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}W\partial_{\nu}W+\mu^{2}f_{y}

(11)

with the left hand side a function of
x
only and the right hand side a function of only
y
.
Applying the usual argument that the two sides must be separately constant, we find the following
integral of the motion

\displaystyle I=X^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}W_{x}\partial_{\nu}W_{x}+\mu^{2}f_{x}=\big(X^{\mu\nu}-g^{\mu\nu}f_{x}\big)\partial_{\mu}W_{y}\partial_{\nu}W_{y}~.

(12)

It can then be shown that

\displaystyle I=-\Big(\frac{f_{y}X^{\mu\nu}+f_{x}Y^{\mu\nu}}{f_{x}-f_{y}}\Big)\partial_{\mu}W\partial_{\nu}W=:K^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}W\partial_{\nu}W

(13)

with
K^{\mu\nu}
a second rank KT.

The key to the usefulness of this relation is that the converse is also true, as described in
[
6
]
: Given a second rank KT, one can use it to find the separation (
11
).

Symmetries of the Laplacian

A symmetry of the Laplacian
\Delta
is a linear differential operator
{\cal D}\neq\Delta
such that

[
9
]

\displaystyle\Delta{\cal D}=\delta\Delta

(14)

for some linear differential operator
\delta
. Any linear differential operator on a Riemannian
manifold may be written in the form

\displaystyle D=V^{\mu\nu\dots\rho}\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\dots\nabla_{\rho}+\makebox{lower order terms}~,

(15)

where
V^{\mu\nu\dots\rho}
is symmetric in its indices. This tensor is called
the symbol

of
D
.

It is shown in
[
9
]
that any symmetry
{\cal D}
of the Laplacian on a Riemannian
manifold is equivalent to one whose symbol is a CKT
f
:

\displaystyle{\cal D}=f^{\mu\nu\dots\rho}\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\dots\nabla_{\rho}+\makebox{lower order terms}.

(16)

Similar results hold for the Dirac operator
[
10
]
and in superspace for
super-Laplacians
[
14
]
.

3
Killing-Yano forms

Killing-Yano forms (KYFs) are generalisations of KTs to antisymmetric covariant tensors.

An
n~\!
th rank KYF is an
n
-form
k
that satisfies

\displaystyle\nabla_{(\mu_{1}}k_{\mu_{2})\dots\mu_{n+1}}=0~,

(17)

or, equivalently,

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu_{1}}k_{\mu_{2}\dots\mu_{n+1}}=\nabla_{[\mu_{1}}k_{\mu_{2}\dots\mu_{n+1}]}~.

(18)

A conformal Killing-Yano form (CKYF) satisfies

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu_{1}}{\mathscr{k}}_{\mu_{2}\dots\mu_{n+1}}=\nabla_{[\mu_{1}}{\mathscr{k}}_{\mu_{2}\dots\mu_{n+1}]}+\frac{n}{D-n+1}g_{\mu_{1}[\mu_{2}}\bar{\mathscr{k}}_{\mu_{3}\dots\mu_{n+1}]}~,

(19)

where

\displaystyle\bar{\mathscr{k}}_{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{n-1}}=\nabla_{\mu}{\mathscr{k}}^{\mu}_{~\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{n-1}}~.

(20)

Moreover, it is called
closed
if

\displaystyle\nabla_{[\mu_{1}}{\mathscr{k}}_{\mu_{2}\dots\mu_{n+1}]}=0~.

(21)

3.1
Uses of KYFs

In this subsection we indicate some of the most important uses of KYFs.

The square of a KYF is a KT

In applications, it is often easier to find KYFs than KTs for a given geometry. It is then gratifying
that KYFs square to second rank KTs:

Let
k_{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{n}}
be a KYF. Consider the second rank tensor

\displaystyle f_{\mu\nu}=k_{\mu\mu_{2}\dots\mu_{n}}k_{\nu}^{~~\!\mu_{2}\dots\mu_{n}}~.

(22)

Then

\displaystyle\nabla_{\sigma}f_{\mu\nu}=\nabla_{[\sigma}k_{\mu\mu_{2}\dots\mu_{n}]}k_{\nu}^{~\mu_{2}\dots\mu_{n}}+k_{\mu}^{~\mu_{2}\dots\mu_{n}}\nabla_{[\sigma}k_{\nu\mu_{2}\dots\mu_{n}]}

(23)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\nabla_{(\sigma}f_{\mu\nu)}=0~.

So
f_{\mu\nu}
is a Killing tensor. This construction also produces rank 2 CKTs from two
rank
n
CKYFs
[
24
]
. Note that when the CKYFs are different, we must explicitly symmetrise the free indices in (
22
).

Conserved currents

A covariantly conserved antisymmetric rank
n
tensor field
J
is equivalent to a co-closed

n
-form. By the Poincaré lemma, this means that it is equal to the co-derivative of an

(n+1)
-form
\ell
in a simply-connected open set

\displaystyle\nabla^{\mu}J_{\mu\dots\mu_{n}}=0\Rightarrow J_{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{n}}=\nabla^{\mu}\ell_{\mu\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{n}}~.

(24)

This can be used to construct conserved charges for a given
J
. An interesting example is the Kastor-Traschen
\mathbb{KT}
current
[
18
]
:

\displaystyle J^{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{n}}=-\frac{(n-1)}{4}\,R^{[\mu_{1}\mu_{2}}\,_{\rho\sigma}\,k^{\mu_{3}\dots\mu_{n}]\rho\sigma}+(-1)^{n+1}\,R_{\rho}\,^{[\mu_{1}}\,k^{\mu_{2}\dots\mu_{n}]\rho}-\frac{1}{2n}\,R\,k^{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{n}}\,,

(25)

where
k
is a KYF and the geometry is represented by the curvature tensor and its contractions. The covariant divergence of
J
vanishes due to Bianchi identities and the properties (
18
) of
k
.

In
[
20
]
we show how the
\mathbb{KT}
current may be rewritten in terms of the
Weyl and Schouten tensors and how it has several separately conserved constituents. Interestingly
only the full current seems to allow for Abbott-Deser (AD) charges, to which we now turn.

AD charges

In the spirit of
[
25
]
one may construct asymptotic charges for the Kastor-Traschen

\mathbb{KT}
current. If the metric has the asymptotic form

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}=\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}+h_{\mu\nu}

(26)

and the background geometry defined by
\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}
has a KYF
\bar{k}
, then the linearised

\mathbb{KT}
current

\displaystyle J^{(L)\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{n}}=\frac{(n-1)}{4}\,R_{L}^{[\mu_{1}\mu_{2}}\,{}_{\rho\sigma}\,\bar{k}^{\mu_{3}\dots\mu_{n}]\rho\sigma}+(-1)^{n+1}\,R_{L\rho}\,^{[\mu_{1}}\,\bar{k}^{\mu_{2}\dots\mu_{n}]\rho}-\frac{1}{2n}\,R_{L}\,\bar{k}^{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{n}}\,

(27)

will be background conserved for certain geometries such as asymptotically flat or asymptotically
AdS ones. For
n=2
, one finds

\displaystyle Q^{\mu\nu}\sim\int_{\Sigma}\,dS_{i}\,\sqrt{|\bar{\gamma}|}\,\bar{\ell}^{\mu\nu i}~,

(28)

which then represents a conserved “charge”. Note that this requires deriving the potential

\bar{\ell}^{\mu\nu\rho}
. This derivation prompted the development of some mathematical tools.

Identities

The study of identities and integrability conditions for KTs, CKTs, KYFs and CKYFs has a long history:

[

,
28

some recent results along this line of investigations.

In deriving the explicit relation

J^{(L)}_{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{n}}=\bar{\nabla}^{\mu}\bar{\ell}_{\mu\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{n}}
needed for
construction of the charge, we use

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}k_{\rho_{1}\dots\rho_{n}}=(-1)^{n+1}\frac{(n+1)}{2}\,R^{\sigma}\,_{\mu[\nu\rho_{1}}\,k_{\rho_{2}\dots\rho_{n}]\sigma}\,,

(29)

which generalises the Killing vector relation

\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}f_{\rho}=R^{\sigma}\,_{\mu\nu\rho}\,f_{\sigma}
.
From this, we derive a number of new relations such as

\displaystyle R_{\mu\nu}\,k^{\mu\sigma}+R^{\mu\sigma}\,k_{\mu\nu}=0

(30)

for a rank 2 KYF. The corresponding identity for a general rank KYF
k
reads

\displaystyle R^{\mu}\,_{\nu}\,k_{[\sigma_{1}\dots\sigma_{n-1}]\mu}+(-1)^{n}\,R^{\mu}\,_{[\sigma_{1}}\,k_{\sigma_{2}\dots\sigma_{n-1}]\nu\mu}\hskip 18.49988pt\hskip 18.49988pt\hskip 18.49988pt\hskip 18.49988pt\hskip 18.49988pt\hskip 18.49988pt

\displaystyle+(n-2)\left((-1)^{n}R_{\nu\mu\lambda[\sigma_{1}}\,k_{\sigma_{2}\dots\sigma_{n-1}]}\,^{\lambda\mu}+\frac{1}{2}\,R_{\lambda\mu[\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}}\,k_{\sigma_{3}\dots\sigma_{n-1}]\nu}\,^{\lambda\mu}\right)

\displaystyle=0\,.

(31)

Several other new identities are to be found in
[
20
]
. The following one, involving
the Einstein tensor

\displaystyle G_{\mu\nu}=R_{\mu\nu}-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}g_{\mu\nu}R,

(32)

reads

\displaystyle k^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\mu}G_{\nu\rho}=0~.

(33)

The corresponding identity for a general rank KYF
k
reads

\displaystyle(n-1)\left(\nabla^{\nu}\,R^{\mu}\,_{[\sigma_{1}}\right)k_{\sigma_{2}\dots\sigma_{n-1}]\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{2}\,\left(\nabla^{\mu}R\right)k_{\mu[\sigma_{1}\dots\sigma_{n-1}]}=0\,.

(34)

We use (
33
) to prove that

\displaystyle K^{\mu\nu}=2\,G_{\rho}\,^{[\mu}\,k^{\nu]\rho}

(35)

is a conserved “current”. Note that in a given geometry this leads to a relation between the KYF and
the energy-momentum tensor on shell. More details may be found in
[
20
]
.

The identity (
29
) may be generalized to CKYTs (
19
) (as well as to geometries with
torsion, see
[
21
]
). For a second rank CKYT
\mathscr{k}
, it reads

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\mathscr{k}_{\rho\sigma}=-\frac{3}{2}R^{\tau}{}_{\mu[\nu\rho}\mathscr{k}_{\sigma]\tau}-\frac{3}{D-1}g_{\mu[\nu}\nabla_{\rho}\bar{\mathscr{k}}_{\sigma]}+\frac{2}{D-1}\nabla_{\mu}\left(g_{\nu[\rho}\bar{\mathscr{k}}_{\sigma]}\right)\,.

(36)

PCCKYF

The closed conformal Killing-Yano forms defined in (
21
) play an important role when
n=2

and it is non-degenerate as a matrix. It is then called a
principal closed conformal Killing-Yano
tensor
and is the starting point for constructing a hierarchy of KYTs and KTs that can be used to

Cotton current comments

Above we have focused on the
\mathbb{KT}
current. Another current, the Killing-Yano Cotton
current, was recently constructed in
[
21
]
. Here we briefly describe this current.

The Cotton tensor is defined in
D\geq 3
dimensions as

\displaystyle C_{\mu\nu\rho}\equiv 2(D-2)\nabla_{[\rho}S_{\nu]\mu}=2\nabla_{[\rho}R_{\nu]\mu}-\frac{1}{(D-1)}g_{\mu[\nu}\nabla_{\rho]}R\,.

(37)

It satisfies

\displaystyle C_{\mu\nu\rho}=C_{\mu[\nu\rho]}\,,

\displaystyle C_{[\mu\nu\rho]}=0\,,

\displaystyle\nabla^{\mu}C_{\mu\nu\rho}=0~.

(38)

Recall that a second rank CKYF
{\mathscr{k}}
satisfies

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}{\mathscr{k}}_{\nu\rho}=\nabla_{[\mu}{\mathscr{k}}_{\nu\rho]}+\frac{2}{D-1}g_{\mu[\nu}\bar{\mathscr{k}}_{\rho]}~,

(39)

and consider

\displaystyle J^{\mu}\equiv C^{\mu\nu\rho}{\mathscr{k}}_{\nu\rho}\,.

(40)

That this is a conserved current follows from

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}J^{\mu}=(\nabla_{\mu}C^{\mu\nu\rho}){\mathscr{k}}_{\nu\rho}+C^{\mu\nu\rho}\nabla_{\mu}{\mathscr{k}}_{\nu\rho}

(41)

\displaystyle=0+C^{\mu\nu\rho}(\nabla_{[\mu}{\mathscr{k}}_{\nu\rho]}+\frac{2}{D-1}\,g_{\mu[\nu}\,\bar{{\mathscr{k}}}_{\rho]})=0~,

since
C
is conserved, symmetric and traceless. We define a charge for this current.
Since

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}J^{\mu}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|g|}}\partial_{\mu}\left(\sqrt{|g|}\,J^{\mu}\right)=0\,,

(42)

one can define a conserved charge
{\cal Q}
as

\displaystyle{\cal Q}\equiv\int_{\Sigma_{\tau}}d^{(D-1)}x\,J^{\mu}\,n_{\mu}\,,

(43)

with
n^{\mu}
normal to the spacelike surface
\Sigma
. We apply this construction to the
Plebański-Demiański metric where we can carry out the integration for certain values of the
metric parameters in
[
21
]
.

3D~
conformal supergravity

In this section we use Greek letters for spinor indices while vector indices are represented by
Latin letters or, equivalently, by pairs of spinor indices.

In three dimensions, conformal supergravity may be defined by the following algebra of
covariant derivatives
[
31
,
32
]

\displaystyle\{\nabla_{\alpha},\nabla_{\beta}\}=2i\nabla_{\alpha\beta}~,

\displaystyle[\nabla_{a},\nabla_{\alpha}]={\textstyle\frac{1}{4}}(\gamma_{a})_{\alpha}^{~~\beta}W_{\beta\gamma\delta}K^{\gamma\delta}~,

\displaystyle[\nabla_{a},\nabla_{b}]=-{\textstyle\frac{i}{8}}\epsilon_{abc}(\gamma^{c})^{\alpha\beta}\nabla_{\alpha}W_{\beta\gamma\delta}K^{\gamma\delta}-{\textstyle\frac{1}{4}}\epsilon_{abc}(\gamma^{c})^{\alpha\beta}W_{\alpha\beta\gamma}S^{\gamma}~.

(45)

The notation is that spinor indices are
\alpha,\beta,\dots
, vector tangent space indices are
a,b,\dots
. The usual convention that a vector index is represented by a symmetric pair of spinor indices
also applies. Thus
K_{\alpha\beta}
is the vector generator of special conformal transformations while

S^{\gamma}
generates
S
supersymmetry transformations.

The super Cotton tensor
[
32
]

W
obeys

\displaystyle W_{\alpha\beta\gamma}=W_{(\alpha\beta\gamma)}

\displaystyle\nabla^{\alpha}W_{\alpha\beta\gamma}=0

\displaystyle K_{a}W_{\alpha\beta\gamma}=0~,

(46)

where the last relation identifies
W
as a primary field.

The super Cotton current

Armed with these relations, we turn to the super Cotton current
[
21
]
.

We take a superconformal Killing supervector field
\xi
to be given by

\displaystyle\xi=\xi^{a}\nabla_{a}+\xi^{\alpha}\nabla_{\alpha}~,

(47)

with
\xi^{a}
a primary field. It follows that

\displaystyle\nabla_{(a}\xi_{b)}={\textstyle\frac{1}{3}}\eta_{ab}\nabla_{c}\xi^{c}

\displaystyle\nabla^{\beta\gamma}\xi^{\alpha}=-{\textstyle\frac{2}{3}}\epsilon^{\alpha(\beta}\nabla^{\gamma)}_{~\sigma}\xi^{\sigma}~,

(48)

where the next to last relation defines a conformal Killing vector and the last one a conformal
Killing spinor.

We may now construct a supergravity version of our Cotton current. To this end, we define

\displaystyle k_{\alpha}=W_{\alpha\beta\gamma}\xi^{\beta\gamma}

\displaystyle k_{\alpha\beta}=\nabla_{\alpha}k_{\beta}=(\nabla_{(\alpha}W_{\beta)\gamma\delta})\xi^{\gamma\delta}+4iW_{\alpha\beta\gamma}\xi^{\gamma}~.

(49)

These satisfy

\displaystyle\nabla^{\alpha}k_{\alpha}=0

\displaystyle\nabla^{\alpha\beta}k_{\alpha\beta}=0~,

(50)

and the lowest component of the first part of
k_{\alpha\beta}
is the bosonic Cotton current.
In a more covariant form we have

\displaystyle(k^{A})=(k^{\alpha\beta},k^{\alpha})

(51)

with

\displaystyle\nabla_{A}k^{A}=0~.

(52)

We note that this construction opens the novel field of super invariants from super Killing tensors.

Positive Ricci curvature through Cheeger deformations
Leonardo F. Cavenaghi, Renato J.M. e Silva, Llohann D. Sperança

Abstract
This paper is devoted to a deep analysis of the process known as Cheeger deformation, applied to manifolds with isometric group actions. Here, we provide new curvature estimates near singular orbits and present several applications. As the main result, we answer a question raised by a seminal result of Searle–Wilhelm about lifting positive Ricci curvature from the quotient of an isometric action. To answer this question, we develop techniques that can be used to provide a substantially streamlined version of a classical result of Lawson and Yau, generalize a curvature condition of Chavéz, Derdzinski, and Rigas, as well as, give an alternative proof of a result of Grove and Ziller.

Introduction

An interesting and challenging problem in Riemannian geometry is that of producing examples of metrics of positive (Ricci or sectional) curvatures. The current scarcity of known examples of metrics with positive sectional curvature compared to the known examples of metrics with non-negative sectional curvature illustrates the difficulty of the problem for the sectional curvature.

Of particular interest is the search for positively curved metrics on
exotic manifolds
. Gromoll and Meyer
[
GM74
]
constructed the first exotic sphere with a metric of non-negative sectional curvature; Wilhelm
[
Wil01
]
constructed metrics of positive Ricci curvature and almost non-negative sectional curvature in every exotic sphere which is a 3-sphere bundle over the 4-sphere; Grove and Ziller
[
GZ00
]
produced metrics of nonnegative sectional curvature on these examples and Goette, Kerin and Shankar
[
GKS20
]
extended Grove–Ziller’s result to all 7-spheres. Apart from spheres, Grove, Verdiani and Ziller
[
GVZ11
]
, and independently Dearricott
[
Dea11
]
, built an exotic unit tangent space with positive sectional curvature.

Concerning non-trivial examples of manifolds with metrics of positive Ricci curvature, Nash
[
Nas79
]
, Poor
[
Poo75
]
, Searle and Wilhelm
[
SW15
]
, Wraith
[
Wra97
,
Wra07
]
, Joachim
[
JW08
]
and Crowley and Nordström and Crowley and Wraith
[
CN15
,
CW17
]
proved the existence of such metrics on certain bundles and exotic manifolds. In
[
CS18
,
CS22
]
the first and third named authors built metrics of positive Ricci curvature on several exotic manifolds and on the total space of bundles where the fibers and/or the base spaces are exotic manifolds, or even when the base manifold is a so-called Shrinking Ricci Soliton (
[
CS18
,
CS22
]
are particularly related to the ones of Searle and Wilhelm, Nash and Poor). With different aims, the works of Gilkey, Park and Tuschmänn
[
GPT98
]
and Belegradek and Wei
[
BW02
]
are also interesting references for positive Ricci curvature on bundles.

Riemannian submersions play an important role in the construction of manifolds with positive curvature properties due to the O’Neill submersion formula, which implies that Riemannian submersions do not decrease sectional curvature. However, it is not necessarily true that Riemannian submersions preserve positive Ricci curvature (see
[
PW14
]
).

On the other hand, one may ask whether one can lift positive curvature from the base of a Riemannian submersion to its total space. There is an easy counterexample to this question: simply consider the projection onto the first coordinate
\mathbb{R}P^{2}\times\mathbb{R}P^{2}\to\mathbb{R}P^{2}
. The base of this submersion has positive sectional curvature, however
Synge’s Theorem
implies that the positive sectional curvature cannot be lifted to the total space since
\pi_{1}(\mathbb{R}P^{2}\times\mathbb{R}P^{2})\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.

A next natural question
is
can one lift positive Ricci curvature from the orbit space?
Searle and Wilhelm
[
SW15
]
answered this question in affirmative for a large class of submersions.

Theorem 1.1

(Searle–Wilhelm,
[
SW15
]
)
.

Let
(M,g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold endowed with a
G
-action satisfying

(SW1)

G
is a compact connected Lie group acting effectively and by isometries;

(SW2)

A
G
-principal orbit has finite fundamental group;

(SW3)

\Ricci_{M/G}\geq 1
in the orbital distance metric.

Then
M
carries a
G
-invariant metric of positive Ricci curvature.

The main idea in the proof of Theorem

is to perform a conformal change on the metric

followed by a standard deformation, commonly used for constructing metrics of nonnegative/positive sectional curvature, called
Cheeger deformation
(see
[
Che73
,
Müt87
]
for instance).
Based on the proof’s delicate estimates and the lack of examples, the question was raised:
is it possible to prove Theorem
1.1
using only Cheeger deformations?

Motivated by the two questions above, we provide a deep analysis of the behavior of the Cheeger deformation near singular orbits. As a consequence, we answer in negative the second question (Theorem
A
); simplify the proof of a result in
[
GZ02
]
(Theorem
1.3
); recover the celebrated result on the existence of metrics of positive scalar curvature under non-Abelian symmetry assumptions
[
LY74
]
(Theorem
1.2
); and improve the condition for positive sectional curvature in
[
CDR92
]
(Theorem
E
).

As the first result, we show that it is not always possible to lift positive Ricci curvature from the quotient only by using Cheeger deformation. Or, equivalently, we show that the conformal change in the proof of Theorem
1.1
was applied in an essential way
(see the family of examples presented in Section
5.1.1
for details):

Theorem A
.

There are Riemannian manifolds satisfying the hypotheses of Theorem
1.1
that do not develop positive Ricci curvature after any Cheeger deformation.

Theorem
A
is proved through a fully algebraic characterization of sufficient conditions for a manifold to admit a
G
-invariant metric that does not develop positive Ricci curvature after Cheeger deformation. Such a characterization is obtained by reducing the study to tangent vectors at singular orbits, that are fixed by the isotropy representation, which we call
fixed axes
. Afterwards, we recognize these algebraic conditions in terms of geometric obstructions. This is the content of Theorem
5.9

5.8

Related to what we just mentioned, given
p\in M
, denote by
\rho\colon G_{p}\to O(\mathcal{H}_{p})
the restriction of the isotropy representation at
p
to
\mathcal{H}_{p}=(T_{p}Gp)^{\perp}
, and let
G^{0}_{p}
denote the identity component of the isotropy subgroup

. We prove:

Theorem B
.

Let
(M,g)
and

satisfy
(SW1)
-
(SW3)
.
Then, if

has directions with negative Ricci curvature after any finite Cheeger deformation, it follows that

1.

there is a point

which is fixed by

2.

the restriction of

to
\mathcal{H}_{q}\cap\mathrm{span}\{X\}^{\perp}
is reducible.

In particular, we conclude some simple criteria for Cheeger deformations to allow the development of positive Ricci curvature under the hypotheses
(SW1)
-
(SW3)
:

Corollary C
.

Let
(M,g)
and

(SW1)
-
(SW3)

(a)

(b)

(c)

the isotropy representation
\rho:G^{0}_{q}\to O(\mathcal{H}_{q})

Moreover, item

can be viewed as a condition on the quotient

.

Suppose that
(M,g)

The analysis here employed is rich enough to furnish a better understanding of Cheeger deformations, making it possible to produce other positive curvatures, or at least furnishing some criteria for it. For instance, we recall that in
[
LY74
]
, Lawson and Yau construct a metric with positive scalar curvature on any compact manifold

endowed with an action of a compact non-Abelian Lie group
G
. The proof considers a copy of

(or

) and is broken in two parts: one first applies the

to the

[

]
for details) on the regular part; then delicate estimates are made near the singular strata, independent of the deformation parameter. A downside of the construction is the loss of symmetry.
By using Cheeger deformations, we maintain the original symmetry and reduce the problem near singular orbits to elementary estimates. More precisely, we re-prove:

Theorem 1.2

(Lawson–Yau)
.

Let
(M,g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold. Suppose that
G
is a compact Lie group with non-Abelian Lie algebra that acts effectively on
M
by isometries. Then
g
develops positive scalar curvature after a finite Cheeger deformation.

In the realm of sectional curvature, we generalize the condition for positive sectional curvature in
[
CDR92
]
, by replacing the canonical deformation, which would only work for principal
S^{3}
- or
SO(3)
-bundles with totally geodesic fibers, with Cheeger deformations. An interesting property of the condition below is an almost complete decoupling of the three main ingredients that characterize the geometry of the regular part
M^{reg}
: the geometry of the fiber; the dynamics of the horizontal distribution; and the geometry of the base. This fact should be extremely useful in applying analysis methods to problems of existence of positive sectional curvature on principal bundles.

To better understand the statement of Theorem
E
, we recall that given a biinvariant inner product
Q
on
\mathfrak{g}
, for every point
p
in a principal orbit, we denote by
\Omega:\mathcal{H}_{p}\times\mathcal{H}_{p}\to\mathfrak{m}_{p}
the
curvature 2-form
of the bundle
M^{reg}\to M^{reg}/G
, where
M^{reg}
stands for the open, dense and convex set where each two orbits are diffeomorphic to each other. More precisely, given two horizontal vector fields
X,Y
, we define
\Omega(X,Y)
as the unique element in
\mathfrak{m}_{p}
, where
\mathfrak{m}_{p}
is identified via isometric action vectors with the tangent space to the orbit through
p
, such that

\Omega(X,Y)^{*}=-[X,Y]^{\mathcal{V}}=-2A_{X}Y,

where
A:\mathcal{H}_{p}\times\mathcal{H}_{p}\rightarrow\mathcal{V}_{p}
stands to the so-called O’Neill tensor and the superscript
\ast
refers to action vectors. Observe that it can also be equivalently characterized by:

g(\Omega_{X}^{*}V^{*},Y)=Q(\Omega(X,Y),V)=-2g(A^{*}_{X}P^{-1}V^{*},Y),

where
P
is uniquely characterized by
g|_{\mathcal{V}_{p}}(\cdot,\cdot)=Q(P\cdot,\cdot)
. In particular,
-2A^{*}P^{-1}V^{*}=\Omega^{*}V
.

Theorem E
.

Suppose that
G
is compact and that
G/G_{p}
has positive sectional curvature as a normal homogeneous manifold for every
p\in M^{reg}
. Then
\sec_{g_{t}}>0
for any sufficiently large
t
if, and only if, there is
k>0
such that

(R_{M/G}(X,Y,Y,X)-k\|X\wedge Y\|^{2}_{g})\\
(\tfrac{1}{2}Q(\Hess P^{-1}(X)V,V)+\tfrac{1}{4}\|\Omega^{*}_{X}V\|_{g}^{2}-k\|X\|^{2}_{g}Q(P^{-1}V,V))\\
\geq Q((\nabla_{X}\Omega)_{X}Y,V)^{2}

(1)

for every
X,Y\in\mathcal{H}_{p}
,
V\in\mathfrak{g}
and
p\in M^{reg}
.

By recalling that Cheeger deformation preserves positive sectional curvature, one concludes that the condition in Theorem
E
is a necessary condition for a manifold to have positive sectional curvature (even before applying a Cheeger deformation).

Finally, Theorem
1.1
demands positive Ricci curvature on the quotient. This is not possible if the quotient is an interval, as in the case of cohomogeneity-one actions. By studying the limit behavior of the Cheeger deformation, we also use our techniques in this direction, recovering a special case of a result of Grove and Ziller:

Theorem 1.3

(Grove–Ziller,
[
GZ02
]
)
.

Suppose that
M
is a compact cohomogeneity one manifold such that

(i)

M
has two singular orbits;

(ii)

a principal orbit has a finite fundamental group.

Then, there is an invariant metric
g
on
M
with positive Ricci curvature.

2
Cheeger deformations on
G
-manifolds

We follow
[
Müt87
]
and
[
Zil
]
to give a brief review on the procedure known as
Cheeger deformations
. Specifically, we recall some results that we shall need in the rest of this work.

2.1
Cheeger deformations and its associated tensors

Let
(M,g)
be a Riemannian manifold endowed with an isometric action by a compact Lie group
G
with a biinvariant Riemannian metric
Q
. We recall that, for each point
p\in M
, the map
g\mapsto gp
induces a diffeomorphism of
G\big/G_{p}
onto the orbit
Gp
, where
G_{p}
is the isotropy subgroup at
p
. Moreover, the metric
Q
induces an orthogonal decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{p}\oplus\mathfrak{m}_{p}
, where
\mathfrak{g}_{p}
stands for the Lie algebra of
G_{p}
and
\mathfrak{m}_{p}
is isomorphic to
T_{p}Gp
via action fields:

\mathfrak{g}\ni U\mapsto U^{*}_{p}=\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}e^{tU}p,

where
e^{tU}
denotes the Lie group exponential map.

We call the tangent space to the orbit at
p

the vertical space at
p
and denote
T_{p}Gp=\mathcal{V}_{p}
; the
g
-orthogonal complement of
\mathcal{V}_{p}
in
T_{p}M
is called the
horizontal space at

p
and is denoted by
\mathcal{H}_{p}
. Thus, every tangent vector
\overline{X}\in T_{p}M
can be uniquely written as
\overline{X}=X+U^{\ast}_{p}
, where
X\in\mathcal{H}_{p}
and
U\in\mathfrak{m}_{p}
. We omit the subscript
p
in
U_{p}^{*}
whenever there is no risk of ambiguity.

The Cheeger deformation consists of a
1
-parameter family of
G
-invariant Riemannian metrics on
M
produced by an appropriate shrinking of the metric
g
in the orbit’s direction. It preserves the horizontal space at each point together with its metric. This procedure promptly generalizes the classical
Canonical Variation
(see
[
GW09
, Example 2.1.1, p. 56]
) on
G
-principal bundles.

To construct the family of metrics, consider the following free isometric
G
-action on
(M\times G,g+\tfrac{1}{t}Q)
:

r(p,g):=(rp,rg),\quad\forall r\in G.

(2)

The map
\bar{\pi}\colon(p,g)\mapsto g^{-1}p
defines a diffeomorphism between the quotient and
M
, thus inducing a
1
-parameter family of Riemannian metrics
g_{t}
. One observes that

g_{t}(\mathcal{H}_{p},\mathcal{V}_{p})=0
and that
g_{t}|_{\mathcal{H}_{p}}=g|_{\mathcal{H}_{p}}
for every
p\in M.

Definition 2.1
.

We call the resulting metric
g_{t}
the
Cheeger deformation
of
g
(at time
t
).

The metric
g_{t}
can be completely described by the following tensors:

Definition 2.2
.

•

The
orbit tensor
at
p
is the linear map
P:\mathfrak{m}_{p}\to\mathfrak{m}_{p}
defined by

g(U^{\ast},V^{\ast})=Q(PU,V),\quad\forall U^{\ast},V^{\ast}\in\mathcal{V}_{p}.

•

The
deformed orbit tensor
of
g_{t}
at
p
is defined to be the linear map
P_{t}:\mathfrak{m}_{p}\to\mathfrak{m}_{p}
such that

g_{t}(U^{\ast},V^{\ast})=Q(P_{t}U,V),\quad\forall U^{\ast},V^{\ast}\in\mathcal{V}_{p}.

•

The
metric tensor
of
g_{t}
at
p
is the linear map
C_{t}:T_{p}M\to T_{p}M
satisfying

g_{t}(\overline{X},\overline{Y})=g(C_{t}\overline{X},\overline{Y}),\quad\forall\overline{X},\overline{Y}\in T_{p}M.

All three tensors are symmetric, positive definite, and related in the following way:

Proposition 2.3

(Proposition 1.1,
[
Zil
]
)
.

1.

P_{t}=(P^{-1}+t1)^{-1}=P(1+tP)^{-1}
,

2.

Given
\overline{X}=X+U^{\ast}
then
C_{t}(\overline{X})=X+((1+tP)^{-1}U)^{\ast}
.

Remark
.

Concerning our comment on the fact that Cheeger deformations can be seen as a
1
-parameter subgroup, just observe that: at least in the case of principal bundles, we can see the orbit tensor
P
as a section of the bundle over
M
in which the fibers are automorphisms of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of
G
. In this manner, using the fact that
P
is a symmetric tensor, one can check that the Cheeger deformed tensor
P_{t}
corresponds to the flow associated to the following ODE:

\begin{cases}\frac{d}{dt}\psi(t)=-(\psi(t))^{2},\\
\psi(0)=P\end{cases}

(3)

The metric tensor
C_{t}
also plays a crucial role in the computation of the sectional curvature of
g_{t}.
As initially observed by Cheeger and essential in the work of Müter (see
[
Müt87
]
), the expression of the sectional curvature of
g_{t}
is much more natural when computed in the reparameterized plane
C_{t}^{-1}\overline{X}\wedge C_{t}^{-1}\overline{Y}
, instead of the original
\overline{X}\wedge\overline{Y}
. Specifically, it is better to consider the following quantity:

\kappa_{t}(\overline{X},\overline{Y}):=R_{g_{t}}(C_{t}^{-1}\overline{X},C_{t}^{-1}\overline{Y},C_{t}^{-1}\overline{Y},C_{t}^{-1}\overline{X})

(4)

where
R_{g_{t}}
stands for the (4,1) Riemannian curvature tensor

R_{g_{t}}(X,Y,Z,W)=g_{t}(\nabla_{X}\nabla_{Y}Z-\nabla_{Y}\nabla_{X}Z-\nabla_{[X,Y]}Z,W).

In particular, one concludes that the reparameterized sectional curvature is non-decreasing in
t
:

Theorem 2.4

(Proposition 1.3,
[
Zil
]
)
.

Let
\overline{X}=X+U^{\ast},~\overline{Y}=Y+V^{\ast}
be tangent vectors. Then,

\kappa_{t}(\overline{X},\overline{Y})=R_{g}(\overline{X},\overline{Y},\overline{Y},\overline{X})+\frac{t^{3}}{4}\|[PU,PV]\|_{Q}^{2}+z_{t}(\overline{X},\overline{Y}),

(5)

where
z_{t}
is bilinear in each entry, non-decreasing, and zero at
t=0
. Moreover, at points on the regular stratum, it can be written as

z_{t}(\overline{X},\overline{Y})=3t\left\|(1+tP)^{-1/2}P\nabla^{\mathbf{v}}_{\overline{X}}\overline{Y}-(1+tP)^{-1/2}t\frac{1}{2}[PU,PV]\right\|_{Q}^{2}.

Remark
.

A precise definition of
z_{t}
, together with important properties, is given in Lemma
3.5
.

2.2
The Ricci and scalar curvatures of a Cheeger deformation

Finally, we take advantage of Theorem
2.4
to present formulae for the limit Ricci curvature of
g_{t}
as
t\to\infty
as well as for the scalar curvature
\mathrm{scal}_{g_{t}}
for any
t>0.

In what follows, fix
p\in M
, denote
\overline{X}=X+U^{*}\in T_{p}M
and consider
\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}\}
, a
Q
-orthonormal basis of eigenvectors of
P
with eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}\leq\ldots\leq\lambda_{k}
. Additionally, consider
a
g
-orthonormal basis
\{e_{1},....,e_{n}\}
for
T_{p}M
, where
\{e_{k+1},...,e_{n}\}
is a basis for
\mathcal{H}_{p}
and
e_{i}=\lambda_{i}^{-1/2}v^{\ast}_{i}
for
i\leq k
.

Definition 2.5
.

We define the horizontal Ricci curvature of
g
at
p
as

\Ricci^{\mathcal{H}}(\overline{X}):=\sum_{i=k+1}^{n}R_{g}(\overline{X},e_{i},e_{i},\overline{X}).

(6)

Lemma 2.6
.

For any
\overline{X}=X+U^{*}\in T_{p}M
one has

\lim_{t\to\infty}\Ricci_{g_{t}}(\overline{X})=\Ricci_{g}^{\mathcal{H}}(X)+\lim_{t\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{n}z_{t}(C_{t}\overline{X},C_{t}^{1/2}e_{i})+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{j}\|[v_{j},U]\|^{2}_{Q}.

Moreover, the scalar curvature of
g_{t}
is given by:

\mathrm{scal}_{g_{t}}(p)=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\kappa_{0}(C_{t}^{1/2}e_{i},C^{1/2}_{t}e_{j})+z_{t}(C_{t}^{1/2}e_{i},C_{t}^{1/2}e_{j})\\
+\sum_{i,j=1}^{k}\frac{\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}t^{3}}{(1+t\lambda_{i})(1+t\lambda_{j})}\frac{1}{4}\|[v_{i},v_{j}]\|_{Q}^{2}.

(7)

Proof.

Using Proposition
2.3
, it is easy to check that
\{C_{t}^{-1/2}e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
is a
g_{t}
-orthonormal basis for
T_{p}M
. Moreover,
C_{t}^{-1/2}e_{i}=(1+t\lambda_{i})^{1/2}e_{i}
for
i\leq k
and
C_{t}^{-1/2}e_{i}=e_{i}
for
i>k.

We claim that the Ricci curvature of
g_{t}
satisfies:

\displaystyle\Ricci_{g_{t}}(\overline{X})=

\displaystyle\Ricci_{g}^{\mathcal{H}}(C_{t}\overline{X})+\sum_{i=1}^{n}z_{t}(C_{t}^{1/2}e_{i},C_{t}\overline{X})

(8)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{1}{1+t\lambda_{i}}\Big(\kappa_{0}(e_{i},C_{t}\overline{X})+\frac{\lambda_{i}t}{4}\|[v_{i},tP(1+tP)^{-1}U]\|_{Q}^{2}\Big).

Indeed, note that equation (
5
) implies that

\Ricci_{g_{t}}(C_{t}^{-1}\overline{X})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}R_{g_{t}}(C_{t}^{-1/2}e_{i},C_{t}^{-1}\overline{X},C_{t}^{-1}\overline{X},C_{t}^{-1/2}e_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\kappa_{t}(C_{t}^{1/2}e_{i},\overline{X})\\
=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\kappa_{0}(C_{t}^{1/2}e_{i},\overline{X})+\sum_{i=1}^{n}z_{t}(C_{t}^{1/2}e_{i},\overline{X})+\frac{t^{3}}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\|[PC_{t}^{1/2}\lambda_{i}^{-1/2}v_{i},PU]\|_{Q}^{2}\\
=\Ricci_{g}^{\mathcal{H}}(\overline{X})+\sum_{i=1}^{n}z_{t}(C_{t}^{1/2}e_{i},\overline{X})+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{1}{1+t\lambda_{i}}\Big(\kappa_{0}(e_{i},\overline{X})+\frac{\lambda_{i}t}{4}\|[v_{i},tPU]\|_{Q}^{2}\Big).

Equation (
8
) now follows by replacing
\overline{X}
by
C_{t}\overline{X}
above.
Besides,
\lim_{t\to\infty}C_{t}\overline{X}=X
. Therefore
\Ricci_{g}^{\mathcal{H}}(C_{t}\overline{X})\to\Ricci_{g}^{\mathcal{H}}(X)
and

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{1}{1+t\lambda_{i}}\kappa_{0}(e_{i},C_{t}\overline{X})\to 0.

Moreover,

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{t\lambda_{i}}{1+t\lambda_{i}}\frac{1}{4}\|[v_{i},tP(1+tP)^{-1}U]\|_{Q}^{2}\to\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{1}{4}\|[v_{i},U]\|_{Q}^{2}.

Equation (
7
) follows from an analogous calculation.

3
The behavior of Cheeger deformations at singular orbits

In this section, we shall explore the limit behavior of Cheeger deformations at a
singular orbit
(Definition
3.1
) and its influence on the sectional, Ricci, and scalar curvatures. Although relatively elementary, these consist of the main steps in the article.

Let
(M,g)
be a compact connected Riemannian manifold equipped with an isometric action by a compact Lie group
G
. We recall that, as a consequence of the Slice Theorem (see, for example
[
AB15
, Theorem 3.49, p. 65]
), there is an open dense convex set,
M^{reg}\subseteq M
, called
the regular stratum
of the
G
-action, where the orbits have maximal dimension. In particular, for all
p,q\in M^{reg}
it holds that
\mathfrak{m}_{p}
and
\mathfrak{m}_{q}
are isomorphic. Moreover, the restriction of the quotient projection
M^{reg}\to M^{reg}/G
defines a Riemannian submersion (see
[
AB15
, Theorem 3.82, p. 75]
).

Definition 3.1
.

The orbit through any point
p\in M^{reg}
is called either a
regular orbit
or a
principal orbit
.
Both the strata
M\setminus M^{reg}
and any orbit through it are called
singular
.

To motivate our interest in singular orbits, we recall that
[
CS18
, Theorem 6.3, p. 33]
and
[
SW15
, Proposition 6.7]
implies that, after Cheeger deformation, one can uniformly make the Ricci curvature positive in any compact
K\subseteq M^{reg}
, as long as principal orbits have finite fundamental group and the quotient
GK/G
has positive Ricci. It is then only left to produce positive Ricci around singular orbits. 4
Sufficient conditions for positive curvatures and Applications

In this section, we explore the blow-up behavior of

4.1
Lifting positive Ricci curvature via Cheeger deformations

This paragraph is dedicated to the proof of the following result, which concerns our first goal of this subsection.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold with a
G
-action satisfying
(SW1)
-
(SW3)
.
If, for every
t>0
, there exists a unit vector
\overline{X}\in T_{p}M
such that

then there exists a point

and a non-zero vector

fixed by the isotropy representation at

.

For the proof, we need the following auxiliary result. Although it follows from
[

, here we give a different proof based on equation (

Lemma 4.2
.

Consider the Riemannian submersion
\pi{:}~(M^{reg},g)\to(M^{reg}/G,\bar{g})
. If

then

\lim_{t\to\infty}\Ricci_{g_{t}}({X})=\Ricci_{\bar{g}}(d\pi X),\quad\forall X\in\mathcal{H}_{p}.

(21)

Proof.

Observe that

\lim_{t\to\infty}\Ricci_{g_{t}}({X})=\Ricci_{g}^{\mathcal{H}}({X})+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lim_{t\to\infty}z(C_{t}^{1/2}e_{i},{X})

as long as
\lim_{t\to\infty}z_{t}(C_{t}^{1/2}e_{i},X)
exists for all

.
Now, on the one hand, Lemma
3.5
(see also
[
Müt87
]
or
[
Zil
]
) gives:

\displaystyle z_{t}(X,Y)

\displaystyle={3}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}Z\in\mathfrak{g}\\
\|Z\|_{Q}=1\end{subarray}}\left\{\dfrac{g(A_{X}Y,Z^{*})^{2}}{g(Z^{\ast},Z^{\ast})+t^{-1}}\right\}

(22)

\displaystyle z_{t}(X,W^{*})

\displaystyle=3\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}Z\in\mathfrak{g}\\
\|Z\|_{Q}=1\end{subarray}}\left\{\dfrac{g(S_{X}W^{*},Z^{*})^{2}}{g(Z^{\ast},Z^{\ast})+t^{-1}}\right\}

for all

,

. In particular,

, since

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Assume that, for every

, there is a

.
We will show that

\rho(G^{0}_{q})X=X

stands for the connected component of the identity of

.

Indeed, using the same bases as in Lemma

, it gives

Writing

and noting that, by equation (

as

then implies that

lies in the singular strata, otherwise we would have

. Moreover,

, since, otherwise (

0\geq\lim_{m\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{n}z_{m}(C_{m}U^{*},C_{m}^{1/2}e_{i})+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{j}\|[v_{j},U]\|^{2}_{Q}.

is nonnegative and

In particular,

4.2
A condition for positive sectional curvature

To motivate our approach for the second goal, we make a small digression.
As observed in
[
SSW15
]
, Cheeger deformations “renormalizes” the metric in the foliation induced by the
G
-orbits as
t\to\infty
. More precisely,
g_{t}
approaches a metric with totally geodesic orbits as
t\to\infty
. For instance, assuming that the action is free, one can consider the connection metric
\tilde{g}_{t}
defined through the original vertical and
g
-horizontal spaces by:
\tilde{g}_{t}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{V})=0
;
\tilde{g}_{t}|_{\mathcal{H}}=g_{t}|_{\mathcal{H}}
; and
\tilde{g}_{t}(U^{*},V^{*})=t^{-1}Q(U,V)
.

Roughly,
g_{t}
approaches
\tilde{g}_{t}
as
1/t^{2}
. So much so that, recalling that a Cheeger deformation does not produce negative curvature, one is tempted to conjecture the following:

Conjecture 4.3
.

Suppose that
G
is a compact Lie group that acts freely and by isometries on a compact Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
. If
g
has positive sectional curvature, then
M
admits a metric with nonnegative sectional curvature where every
G
-orbit is totally geodesic.

As pointed out in
[
Spe17
]
, this conjecture implies strong restrictions in the manifold, including that the related principal bundle
M\to M/G
is
fat
, i.e., for any non-zero horizontal vector
X
and any horizontal extension
\widetilde{X}
of it, it holds that
[\widetilde{X},\mathcal{H}]^{\mathcal{V}}=\mathcal{V}
; (we redirect the reader to
[
Wei80
,
Zil00
]
or
[
GW09
]
for definitions and results). This implies, for instance, Petersen–Wilhelm’s dimension restriction conjecture (see
[
GÁR18
]
), for the case of principal bundles.

Although it provides a promising approach, Cheeger deformation does not solve conjecture
4.3
directly. Heuristically, the difference between the curvatures of
g_{t}
and
\tilde{g}_{t}
decreases as
1/t
. Since the (vertizontal) curvature of both decay with
1/t^{2}
, the approaching speed is not enough to guarantee that
\tilde{g}_{t}
has non-negative curvature. More specifically, Theorem
E
provides one (and the only one) curvature condition that is preserved under Cheeger deformation.

Here we explore the limit behavior of the Cheeger deformation and present a necessary and sufficient condition for a
G
-manifold to have positive sectional curvature after finite Cheeger deformation. Although seemly hard to apply, this condition amounts to the intrinsic geometry of the fiber (decoded by the orbit tensor
P
) being relatively decoupled from O’Neill’s integrability tensor.

Although the next lines are already presented in the Introduction, for the readers’ convenience, we recall some notation before proceeding to the proof of the Theorem
E
.
Given a biinvariant inner product
Q
on
\mathfrak{g}
, for every
p\in M^{reg}
we denote by
\Omega:\mathcal{H}_{p}\times\mathcal{H}_{p}\to\mathfrak{m}_{p}
the
curvature 2-form
of the bundle
M^{reg}\to M^{reg}/G
. Specifically, given two horizontal vector fields
X,Y
, we define
\Omega(X,Y)
as the unique element in
\mathfrak{m}_{p}
such that

\Omega(X,Y)^{*}=-[X,Y]

(see
[
GW09
, p. 70]
.) It is equivalently characterized by:

g(\Omega_{X}^{*}V^{*},Y)=Q(\Omega(X,Y),V)=-2g(A^{*}_{X}P^{-1}V^{*},Y).

In particular,
-2A^{*}P^{-1}V^{*}=\Omega^{*}V
.
We now prove Theorem
E
. That is, if
\sec_{G/G_{p}}>0
, then
g_{t}
has positive sectional for any
t
sufficiently large, if and only if

(R_{B}(X,Y,Y,X)-k\|X\wedge Y\|^{2}_{g})\\
(\tfrac{1}{2}Q(\Hess P^{-1}(X)V,V)+\tfrac{1}{4}\|\Omega^{*}_{X}V\|_{g}^{2}-k\|X\|^{2}_{g}Q(P^{-1}V,V))\\
\geq Q((\nabla_{X}\Omega)_{X}Y,V)^{2}

for every
X,Y\in\mathcal{H}_{p}
,
V\in\mathfrak{m}_{p}
and
p\in M^{reg}
.

Remark
.

Here we think of
P
as a function from
M
to the linear endomorphisms of
\mathfrak{g}
(setting
P\mathfrak{g}_{p}=\{0\}
). Given a basis of
\mathfrak{g}
,
\Hess
is computed as the Hessian of each entry in the matrix of
P
on that basis.

Proof of Theorem
E
.

Since

4.3
Positive Ricci curvature of cohomogeneity-one manifolds

Our next step is to prove Theorem
1.3
. Cohomogeneity-one manifolds present a special challenge. Here we again consider the limit behavior of the Cheeger deformation, but now considering that there is no horizontal curvature.
We deal with the case where the quotient space is a closed interval
M/G=[0,R]
and denote
\pi:M\to[0,R]
as the quotient map. Let
\gamma:[0,R]\to M
be a horizontal geodesic satisfying
\pi\circ\gamma(s)=s
. Denote
\dot{\gamma}=X
. From the
G
-invariance of the curvature, it suffices to show that
\Ricci_{g_{t}}>0
along
\gamma
. As in
[
GZ02
]
, we use diagonal metrics and (to a lesser extent) a disk bundle argument to provide suitable smoothness conditions at the singular orbits. In the process, we also show that not every invariant metric has positive Ricci curvature, even after Cheeger deformation. This points to the fact that
M/G
is flat, contrasting with Theorem
1.1
.

The natural steps would be to produce an initial metric
g
and then use a sequential argument in the spirit of the proof of Theorem
4.1
. To motivate the construction of the initial metric, we invert the order of the steps, making explicit which conditions
g
must satisfy before producing it. More precisely, Proposition
4.4
below provides necessary and sufficient conditions to the existence of the desired metric with positive Ricci curvature. Theorem
1.3
then follows verifying such conditions in a standard way. That said, the merit in our approach relies only on establishing such a proposition.

In what follows, to emphasize the dependence of the orbit tensor along the points in
M
, we denote the orbit tensor at
\gamma(s)
by
P_{s}
.

Proposition 4.4
.

Suppose that
(M,g)
is a cohomogeneity one manifold whose orbits have finite fundamental group. Then
g_{t}
has positive Ricci curvature for all large
t
if and only if there is
c>0
such that, for all
s\in(0,R)
,

\frac{d^{2}}{ds^{2}}\tr P^{-1}_{s}\geq c.

Proof.

We only prove the
if
part. The converse can be proven in a similar way.

Consider the function
F(\overline{Y},t)=t\Ricci_{g_{t}}(\overline{Y})

where
\overline{Y}
has unit
g
-norm (the
t
-factor is essential since the
\Ricci_{g_{t}}(\overline{Y})
might decay.) Arguing by contradiction, we assume that for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
there is
\overline{Y}_{m}
such that
F(\overline{Y}_{m},m)\leq 0
. By compactness, passing to a converging subsequence, we obtain a
g
-unit vector
\overline{Y}
such that
\lim_{m\to\infty}F(\overline{Y},m)\leq 0
.

Denote by
p\in\gamma([0,R])
the footpoint of
\overline{Y}
and decompose
\overline{Y}=Y+V^{*}
into its horizontal and vertical components. We first note that
p
must lie in a regular orbit: recall that
G_{\gamma(0)},G_{\gamma(R)}
act transitively on their respective horizontal spheres, as noted in
[
GZ00
,
GZ02
]
. Therefore,
d\rho(\mathfrak{g}_{p})Y\neq\{0\}
whenever
p\in M\setminus M^{reg}
. Hence, Lemma
2.6
and Proposition
3.4
imply that
\Ricci_{g_{t}}(\overline{Y})
would have an unbounded
z_{t}
-term whether
Y\neq 0
(by summing it with any fake horizontal) or
V^{*}\neq 0
(by summing it with
Y
).
Therefore,
p\in M^{reg}
, as claimed.

Now, since
p
lies in a regular orbit,
\Ricci_{g}^{\mathcal{H}}(Y)=0
and conclude that
V^{*}=0
; otherwise we would have
\lim\limits_{m\to\infty}\Ricci_{g_{m}}(\overline{Y})\geq\frac{1}{4}\sum\|[v_{i},V]\|^{2}_{Q}
, which is positive whenever
V^{*}\neq 0
.
Therefore, the limit vector

is precisely the velocity
\dot{\gamma}=X.
To finish the proof, observe that equation (
8
) gives

0\geq\lim_{m\to\infty}F(X,m)=\lim_{m\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{m}{1+m\lambda_{i}}\Big(\kappa_{0}(e_{i},X)+z_{m}(e_{i},X)\Big)\\
=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\Big(\kappa_{0}({\lambda_{i}^{-1}}v_{i}^{*},X)+3\|S_{X}({\lambda_{i}^{-1}}v_{i}^{*})\|^{2}_{g}\Big)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\Big(\kappa_{0}(P^{-1}_{s}v_{i}^{*},X)+3\|S_{X}(P^{-1}_{s}v_{i}^{*})\|^{2}_{g}\Big).

(33)

This finishes the proof since (

\kappa_{0}(P^{-1}_{s}v_{i}^{*},X)+3\|S_{X}(P^{-1}_{s}v_{i}^{*})\|^{2}_{g}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{d^{2}}{ds^{2}}Q(P^{-1}_{s}v_{i},v_{i})\geq c.\qed

We are now ready to prove Theorem
1.3
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Let
\pi:M\to[0,R]
be the quotient projection and
\gamma:[0,R]\to M
be a horizontal geodesic satisfying
\pi\circ\gamma(s)=s
. Set
\dot{\gamma}=X
and recall that both
\mathfrak{m}_{\gamma(s)}
and
G_{\gamma(s)}
do not depend on
s\in(0,R)
. Write
\mathfrak{m}_{\gamma(R/2)}=\mathfrak{n}
and
G_{\gamma(R/2)}=H
. A

is a metric satisfying
P_{s}|_{\mathfrak{n}_{i}}=f_{i}(s)^{2}\id
, where
\mathfrak{n}=\oplus\mathfrak{n}_{i}
is an

-invariant decomposition of
\mathfrak{n}
.

Since (see for instance Lemma 3.3 in
[
GZ02
]
)

\frac{d^{2}}{ds^{2}}\tr P_{s}^{-1}=\sum\frac{d^{2}f_{i}^{-2}}{ds^{2}}=\sum-2\frac{f_{i}^{\prime\prime}}{f_{i}^{3}}+6\frac{(f_{i}^{\prime})^{2}}{f_{i}^{4}},

we conclude Theorem
1.3
by showing that we can choose
\{f_{i}\}
as a set of concave functions such that, for each

, there is at least one index, say
i
, for which
f_{i}^{\prime\prime}<0
.
Such functions can be easily produced by imitating bundle metrics on the tubular neighborhoods of each singular orbit, then manipulating the behavior of the functions in the regular part.

For instance, we choose the decomposition
\mathfrak{n}=\mathfrak{n}_{-}+\mathfrak{n}_{0}+\mathfrak{n}_{R}+\mathfrak{n}_{+}
where:
\mathfrak{n}_{-}=\mathfrak{g}_{\gamma(0)}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{\gamma(R)}
is composed of vectors whose action fields vanish at both
0
and
R
;
\mathfrak{n}_{0}=\mathfrak{g}_{\gamma(0)}\cap(\mathfrak{n}_{-})^{\bot}
(respectively,
\mathfrak{n}_{R}=\mathfrak{g}_{\gamma(R)}\cap(\mathfrak{n}_{-})^{\bot}
), composed of vectors whose action fields vanish only at
0
(respectively, at
R
);
\mathfrak{n}_{+}=\mathfrak{n}\cap(\mathfrak{g}_{\gamma(0)}+\mathfrak{g}_{\gamma(R)})^{\perp}
is composed of fields that never vanish. Instead of choosing one function for each

, we choose

relative to the new decomposition.

Based on the geometry of disk bundles, see for instance the content of Lemma 3.3 in

]
, it is known that there is some

for which we can impose that:

agree with

near

;

near

is constant near an extreme point where it does not vanish (see

GZ02
]
.)
Since we are assuming that both

are at the singular orbits, both

are non-trivial, therefore, we guarantee that there are strictly convex functions around each singular orbit.
Fixing these conditions, it is straightforward to manipulate the functions

∎

4.4
Non-abelian symmetry and positive scalar curvature

Lawson and Yau in
[
LY74
]
prove that any Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
endowed with the action of a non-Abelian compact connected Lie group

has a metric of positive scalar curvature. The result was specially interesting at the time, since it guarantees both positive scalar curvature in a plethora of exotic spheres and lack of symmetry in the examples of exotic spheres without positive sectional curvature recently found by Hitchin
[
Hit74
]
.

The positively curved metric in
[

]
was obtained by reducing the group to

(or

) and then proceeding in two parts: they considered a bundle-like metric in a compact subset of

or
SO(3)
.
To our fortune, scalar curvature is much more susceptible to become positive through Cheeger deformation than Ricci or sectional curvature. Indeed, the blow-up of
z_{t}
and the last term in (
7
) guarantee positive scalar curvature in a straightforward manner, as long as
\mathfrak{g}
is non-abelian
1
1

1

C. Searle and F. Wilhelm were already aware that Cheeger deformation yields this result.
.

As a result, we provide a simpler proof of the result of Lawson and Yau.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Assume by contradiction that there is a sequence of points

. Since

5
The Ricci tensor on fixed axes

Throughout this section we consider compact
(M,g)
and
G
also satisfying the hypotheses of Theorem
1.1
. Our main goal now is to prove Theorem
A
, besides giving some concrete algebraic conditions for the existence of metrics whose no Cheeger deformation lifts positive Ricci curvature. These examples are presented in Section
5.1.1
. Also see Proposition
5.8
for a local description of the metrics to be produced.

We start by showing that the only obstruction for lifting positive Ricci curvature is the term
\Ricci^{\mathcal{H}}_{g}
at
fixed axes
(see definition
5.1
below. The term
\Ricci^{\mathcal{H}}_{g}
first appears in equation (
8
) in this paper). This emphasizes the need for using a conformal change on the metric to guarantee positive Ricci curvature everywhere. We soon shall see that the irreducibility of the isotropy representation for every orbit at the singular strata implies that Cheeger deformations do work on lifting positive Ricci curvature from the orbit space. Hence, in Section
5.1
we shall study some combinatorial and algebraic descriptions of the Ricci tensor on the fixed axes to provide a general picture of whether Cheeger deformations can be used as a single tool to produce positive Ricci curvature.

Though such algebraic characterization proves to be very useful in providing the needed examples to complete Theorem

5.2

Definition 5.1
.

Let
q\in M\setminus M^{reg}
. We say that a vector
X\in\mathcal{H}_{q}
is a

if it is fixed by
\rho(G^{0}_{q})
, or equivalently, if
\tilde{S}_{X}=0
.

Remark
.

•

Since fixed axes are only defined at points in a singular orbit, from now on it is implicit we are considering only such points.

•

The complete failure of Cheeger deformations for lifting positive Ricci curvature, as we shall see, is completely characterized by the isotropy representation action, parametrizing the so-called fake horizontal vectors. The equivalent definition of fixed axes, provided in Definition
5.1
, regarding
\tilde{S}_{X}
, provides that only the infinitesimal aspect of the isotropy group plays some role, namely, only its Lie algebra. This justifies why we restrict the definition of fixed axes only at the identity connected component of each isotropy subgroup.

Proposition 5.2

Let
X\in\mathcal{H}_{q}

Then for every

z_{t}(X,Y)=0,~\forall t.

(34)

\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\Ricci_{g_{t}}(X)=\Ricci_{g}^{\mathcal{H}}(X)
.

According to equation (

To do so, we use the definition of the exterior derivative also recalling that the isometric action field

is a Killing vector field:

\displaystyle 2dw_{Z}(

\displaystyle X,Y)=Xg(Z^{*},Y)-Yg(Z^{*},X)-g([X,Y],Z^{*})

\displaystyle=g(\nabla_{X}Z^{*},Y)+g(Z^{*},\nabla_{X}Y)-g(\nabla_{Y}Z^{*},X)-g(Z^{*},\nabla_{Y}X)-g([X,Y],Z^{*})

\displaystyle=2g(\nabla_{X}Z^{*},Y)+g(Z^{*},[X,Y])-g([X,Y],Z^{*})

\displaystyle 2d\omega_{Z}(X,V^{*})

\dim\mathcal{H}_{q}>l.
Now, since
\lim_{t\to\infty}\Ricci_{g_{t}}(X^{\prime})\geq 1
for every
X^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}
in the regular part (see Lemma
5.5
), whenever
\mathcal{W}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{q}
is a
l
-dimensional subspace which is the limit of horizontal subspaces on the regular stratum, one gets

\Ricci_{g}^{\mathcal{W}}(X):=\sum_{i=1}^{l-1}R_{g}(X,e_{i},e_{i},X)=\lim_{t\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{l-1}R_{g_{t}}(X,e_{i},e_{i},X)\geq 1,

(36)

where
\{e_{0}=X,e_{1},...,e_{l-1}\}
is an orthonormal basis for
\mathcal{W}
. The last equality follows since
z_{t}(X,e_{i})=0
for all
i
(Proposition
5.2
). On the other hand, the set of such
\mathcal{W}
’s can be restricted enough so that both (
36
) and
\Ricci_{g}^{\mathcal{H}}(X)<0
hold together.

To understand the former constrained relations, let
c(s)
be a smooth curve with
c(0)=q
such that
c(s)\in M^{reg}
for
s>0
. For every
s
,
\mathcal{H}_{c(s)}
defines a curve in
\mathrm{Gr}_{l}(TM)
. Moreover, any limit subspace
\mathcal{W}
of the curve
\mathcal{H}_{c(s)}\in\mathrm{Gr}_{l}(TM)
as
s\to 0
must satisfy (
36
). We conclude that every limit of a sequence
\mathcal{H}_{p_{i}}
,
p_{i}\in M^{reg}
, arises as the limit of the horizontal space along a curve and we call it as a

.

Denote the set of all limit horizontal spaces at
q
as
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}_{q}
. In the next result, we give an algebraic description of such spaces.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
\mathcal{W}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}_{q}
. Then there is
Y\in\mathcal{H}_{q}
such that
\mathcal{W}=(\tilde{S}_{Y}\mathfrak{g}_{q})^{\bot}=(d\rho(\mathfrak{g}_{q})Y)^{\bot}
.

Proof.

Let
c(s)
be a smooth curve,
c(0,\epsilon)\subseteq M^{reg}
, such that
\mathcal{W}
is the limit of
\mathcal{H}_{c(s)}.
If
\epsilon>0
is sufficiently small, according to Lemma
3.2
, a basis for
\mathcal{H}_{c(s)}
can be taken as

\{\frac{1}{s}v_{1}^{*},\ldots,\frac{1}{s}v^{*}_{d},v^{*}_{d+1},\ldots,v^{*}_{k}\},
where
\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}\}
is a basis for
\mathfrak{m}_{c(\epsilon)},~v_{1}^{*},\ldots,v_{d}^{*}\in\mathfrak{g}_{q}
and
v_{d+1}^{*},\ldots,v^{*}_{k}
are
Q
-orthogonal vectors to
\mathfrak{g}_{p}.
Since
v_{i}^{*}(c(s))\to 0
for all

, it follows that

\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{s}v_{i}^{*}(c(s))=\nabla_{c^{\prime}(0)}v_{i}^{*}=\tilde{S}_{c^{\prime}(0)}v_{i}^{*}.

The result then follows since the set
\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{d}\}
must span
\mathfrak{p}_{X}.

∎

Lemma

and its proof motivates the following definition, needed to understand Theorem

.

Definition 5.6
.

A non-zero vector

which generates a subspace

such as in Lemma

\rho

Lemma
5.5
implies that, since

is a fixed axis (and hence

), it holds that

for all
\mathcal{W}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}_{q}
. Gathering all the previous discussion, to prove Theorem

, and hence, better understanding when Cheeger deformations are the only tool needed to lift positive Ricci curvature from orbit spaces, we are interested in sets of vectors fulfilling the following requirements:

(a)

\Ricci^{\mathcal{H}}_{g}(X)<0;

(b)

\Ricci_{g}^{\mathcal{W}}(X)\geq 1
for all
\mathcal{W}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}_{q}.

Indeed, condition (

) obstructs the lift of positive Ricci while (

in Theorem

.

Now, observe that if

is
\rho

is diagonal and hence, condition (

) implies that

5.1
An algebraic description of the Ricci curvature

Here we present a combinatorial description of conditions (
a
) and (
b
). Specifically, we search for algebraic conditions for the existence of a symmetric operator
R_{X}
satisfying (
a
) and (
b
). A metric realizing

is then constructed

. For clarity sake, we first assume that
\mathcal{H}_{q}=\mathrm{span}\{X\}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{2}
, where the
\mathcal{H}_{i}
are
\rho
-irreducible subspaces. Lemma

provides an easily computable condition for
R_{X}
with exactly two distinct eigenvalues
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}
.

Remark
.

•

Although the space
\mathcal{H}_{q}
is metric dependent, the linear action of
G_{q}
in
\mathcal{H}_{q}
is equivalent to the classical isotropic representation of
G_{q}
in the quotient
T_{q}M/T_{q}Gq
. Therefore, all calculations can be done using a fixed complement of
T_{q}Gq
.

•

As we shall see, the irreducibility of
\mathcal{H}_{i}
is not needed to produce the examples in Theorem
A

Start by considering the complementary projections

p_{i}:\mathcal{H}_{q}\to\mathcal{H}_{i}.

Then for any
G
-invariant metric
g
and
Y\in\mathcal{H}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{2}
,

R_{X}(Y)=\lambda_{1}\|p_{1}Y\|_{g}^{2}+\lambda_{2}\|p_{2}Y\|_{g}^{2}.

In particular,
\Ricci^{\mathcal{H}}(X)=\lambda_{1}\dim\mathcal{H}_{1}+\lambda_{2}\dim\mathcal{H}_{2}
and for each
\mathcal{W}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}_{q}
one has

\Ricci^{\mathcal{W}}(X)_{g}=\sum_{i=1}^{l}\left(\lambda_{1}\|p_{1}e_{i}\|_{g}^{2}+\lambda_{2}\|p_{2}e_{i}\|_{g}^{2}\right)=\lambda_{1}\tr(p_{1}|_{\mathcal{W}})+\lambda_{2}\tr(p_{2}|_{\mathcal{W}}).

(37)

Remark
.

Although the definition of
\mathcal{W}=(d\rho(\mathfrak{g}_{q})Y)^{\bot}
is metric dependent, the quantity

\tr(p_{1}|_{\mathcal{W}})=\tr(p_{1})-\tr(p_{1}|_{d\rho(\mathfrak{g}_{q})Y})

is not, as a direct computation shows.

The existence of
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}
satisfying (
a
) and (
b
) can be totally translated as properties of the set

\mathcal{A}=\{(\tr(p_{1}|_{\mathcal{W}}),\tr(p_{2}|_{\mathcal{W}}))\in\mathbb{R}^{2}~~~|~~~\mathcal{W}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}_{q}\}:

Problem 1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a given collection of pairs of real numbers satisfying
a+b=l-1~\forall(a,b)\in\mathcal{A}
. Find
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

a\lambda_{1}+b\lambda_{2}\geq 1,~\forall(a,b)\in\mathcal{A}

(38)

and

\lambda_{1}\dim\mathcal{H}_{1}+\lambda_{2}\dim\mathcal{H}_{2}<0.

(39)

Lemma
5.7
gives necessary and sufficient conditions to solve Problem
1
.

Lemma 5.7
.

Denote by
A:=\dim\mathcal{H}_{1}
and
B:=\dim\mathcal{H}_{2}
. Problem
1
has an affirmative answer
if, and only if, either

\inf_{(a,b)\in\mathcal{A}}\{a\}>\frac{A(l-1)}{A+B},

(40)

or

\inf_{(a,b)\in\mathcal{A}}\{b\}>\frac{B(l-1)}{A+B},

(41)

Proof.

From conditions (
38
),(
39
) it is clear that
\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}<0
. Moreover, if
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}
gives a solution to Problem
1
then
-\lambda_{1},-\lambda_{2}
also gives a solution after interchanging the roles of
\mathcal{H}_{1}
and
\mathcal{H}_{2}
. Since the two conditions stated in Lemma
5.7
only differ by the sign of
\lambda_{1}
, we can assume without loss of generality that
\lambda_{1}>0
and prove (
40
).

To see that (
40
) is necessary, suppose that Problem
1
has an affirmative answer for
\lambda_{1}>0
. Let
(a,b)\in\mathcal{A}
. Equation (
38
) gives
a>-\frac{\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{1}}b+\frac{\epsilon}{\lambda_{1}}
for some
0<\epsilon<1
. Hence, since
a+b=l-1
, we have
a>(a-(l-1))\frac{\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{1}}+\frac{\epsilon}{\lambda_{1}}
. Therefore,

a>(l-1)\frac{-\frac{\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{1}}}{(1-\frac{\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{1}})}+\frac{\epsilon}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}.

On the other hand, equation (
39
) gives
\frac{A}{B}<-\frac{\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{1}}
. Since the function
f(x)=\frac{x}{x+1}
is increasing in
]0,\infty[
we conclude that

a>(l-1)\frac{\frac{A}{B}}{(\frac{A}{B}+1)}+\frac{\epsilon}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}=\frac{(l-1)A}{A+B}+\frac{\epsilon}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}},

for every
(a,b)\in\mathcal{A}
, proving condition (
40
).

Conversely, suppose that there is
\epsilon>0
such that

a\geq\frac{A(l-1)+2\epsilon B}{A+B}

for every
(a,b)\in\mathcal{A}
. Since
a+b=l-1
, we have
a(A+B)-2\epsilon B\geq A(a+b)
. Thus
\frac{a-2\epsilon}{b}\geq\frac{A}{B}
whenever
b\neq 0
.
Choose
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}
such that
\lambda_{1}>0
and
\frac{a-\epsilon}{b}\geq-\frac{\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{1}}>\frac{A}{B}
. We obtain
0>\lambda_{1}A+\lambda_{2}B
and
\lambda_{1}a+\lambda_{2}b\geq\epsilon
for every
(a,b)\in\mathcal{A}
,
b\neq 0
. The result then follows a rescaling of
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}
since whenever
\lambda_{1}>0
equation (
38
) is automatically satisfied for
b=0
.
∎

Having understood necessary and sufficient pointwise conditions to produce the needed counterexamples to Theorem

Proposition 5.8
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{H}_{q}

\rho
-invariant subspace
\mathcal{H}_{1}

Then there is a
G

2.

\Ricci_{g_{t}}(X)<0

Since (

5.7

Consider a biinvariant metric

G

\bar{g}=dt^{2}+\phi^{2}\left(t\right)ds^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}}+\psi^{2}\left(t\right)ds^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}}},

(46)

\displaystyle R_{\bar{g}}(X,W)

(51)

\Ricci^{\mathcal{W}}(\alpha X+V+W)=\alpha^{2}\Ricci^{\mathcal{W}}(X)+\Ricci^{\mathcal{W}}(V)+\Ricci^{\mathcal{W}}(W),

(this follows from equations (

\displaystyle\Ricci_{\bar{g}}^{\mathcal{W}}(W)\geq K(X,W)+\tr(p_{1}|_{\mathcal{W}})(-\sqrt{-\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}\cot(\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}t_{0}))\|W\|^{2}\geq\lambda_{1}\|W\|^{2},

where the last inequality follows from (

We now use the explicit local description given in Proposition

5.1.1
A family of counterexamples

Here we prove Theorem
A

1.1

5.8
. Our model example consists of a doubly warped metric on the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{5}\subset\mathbb{R}^{6}
with the usual mono-axial

-action on it.

Consider
\mathbb{S}^{5}\subset\mathbb{R}^{6}
endowed with the standard linear

-action given by the inclusion

Take

and

.
Note that

Moreover, the regular orbits are diffeomorphic to 2-spheres. Therefore, the dimension
l

Write
\mathcal{H}_{q}=\mathrm{span}\{X\}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{2}

5.2
The algebraic characterization in geometric terms

On the one hand, we have decoupled geometric data from Ricci tensors on fixed axes to algebraic terms (Problem

With this aim, to provide a complete description of the “failure” or not of Cheeger deformations for providing positive Ricci curvature, we proceed proving Theorem

Theorem 5.9
.

Let
(M,g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold with an effective isometric action by a compact Lie group
G

(SW1)
-
(SW3)
in Theorem

such that

is a non-zero vector fixed by the isotropy representation at

If

5.9

1

Let

\rho

Problem 2

Let
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}

(53)

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\dim\mathcal{H}_{i}<0.

(54)

Since the geometric data plays no role in the proof of Lemma

Lemma 5.10
.

Let
A_{1},\dots,A_{m}

(55)

Proof.

If Problem
2

\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}
, define
\lambda_{i_{0}}=\max_{i}\{\lambda_{i}\}
and
\lambda_{j_{0}}=\min_{j}\{\lambda_{j}\}
. Inequality (
55
) then follows from Lemma
5.7
applied to the problem

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{i\neq j_{0}}a_{i}\Big)\lambda_{i_{0}}+a_{j_{0}}\lambda_{j_{0}}

\displaystyle\geq 1,

(56)

\displaystyle A_{i_{0}}\lambda_{i_{0}}+\Big(\sum_{j\neq i_{0}}A_{j}\Big)\lambda_{j_{0}}

\displaystyle<0,

(57)

where
(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in\tilde{\mathcal{A}}=\left\{(\sum_{i\neq j_{0}}a_{i},a_{j_{0}}):(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})\in\mathcal{A}\right\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

The converse is a straightforward consequence of Lemma
5.7
since we can choose
i_{0}\neq j_{0}
arbitrarily and set
\lambda=\lambda_{j}=\lambda_{i}
for all
j,i\neq j_{0}
.
∎

We finally pass to the proof of Theorem
5.9
by recognizing
\inf\{\tr(p_{1}|_{\mathcal{W}})\}
as an invariant quantity associated to the
G
-action. To this aim, fix a
\rho
-invariant inner product on
\mathcal{H}_{q}
and let
\mathcal{R}
be the regular part of
\mathcal{H}_{q}
with respect to
\rho
(recall Definition
5.6
). Then:

Proposition 5.11
.

Suppose
d\rho(\mathfrak{g}_{q})X=0
and
\mathcal{H}_{q}=\mathrm{span}\{X\}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{2}
is a
\rho
-invariant decomposition. For every
Y\in\mathcal{R}
, write
Y_{i}
as its
\mathcal{H}_{i}
-component. Then

\displaystyle\inf_{\mathcal{W}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}_{q}}\{\tr(p_{1}|_{\mathcal{W}})\}=\inf_{Y\in\mathcal{R}}\{\dim\mathcal{H}_{1}-\dim\rho(G^{0}_{q})Y_{1}\}.

Proof.

Let us fix
Y=Y_{1}+Y_{2}\in\mathcal{R}
and, for every
\alpha>0
, define
\mathcal{W}^{\alpha}{=}\left(d\rho(\mathfrak{g}_{q})(\alpha Y_{1}+Y_{2})\right)^{\perp}
. It suffices to show that

\inf_{\alpha>0}\{\tr(p_{1}|_{\mathcal{W}^{\alpha}})\}=\dim\mathcal{H}_{1}-\dim\rho(G_{q})Y_{1}.

(58)

Write
\mathcal{W}^{\alpha}=\mathcal{W}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{W}_{2}\oplus\mathcal{W}_{12}^{\alpha}

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{1}

\displaystyle:=\ker~p_{2}\cap\mathcal{W}^{\alpha},

(59)

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{2}

\displaystyle:=\ker~p_{1}\cap\mathcal{W}^{\alpha},

(60)

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{12}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{W}^{\alpha}\cap\left(\mathcal{W}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{W}_{2}\right)^{\bot},

(61)

where
p_{i}
is the
\mathcal{H}_{i}
-projection. Note that
\mathcal{W}_{1},\mathcal{W}_{2}
do not depend on
\alpha
, since
\mathcal{W}_{i}=\{Z_{i}\in\mathcal{H}_{i}~|~Z_{i}\perp d\rho(\mathfrak{g}_{q})Y_{i}\}
. Observe also that the dimension of
\mathcal{W}^{\alpha}_{12}
does not depend on
\alpha
. Moreover:

Lemma 5.12
.

There are constants
c,C>0
, not depending on
\alpha
, such that

\frac{1}{{1+c\alpha^{2}}}\leq\frac{\|p_{1}(v_{\alpha})\|^{2}}{\|v_{\alpha}\|^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{{1+C\alpha^{2}}}

for every
v_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{W}_{12}^{\alpha}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{M}=d\rho(\mathfrak{g}_{q})Y_{1}\oplus d\rho(\mathfrak{g}_{q})Y_{2}
and note that
\mathcal{W}_{12}^{\alpha}\in\mathcal{M}
. Let
\mathcal{M}_{12}^{\alpha}
be the orthogonal complement of
\mathcal{W}_{12}^{\alpha}
in
\mathcal{M}
and note that both
p_{1}|_{\mathcal{M}_{12}^{\alpha}},p_{2}|_{\mathcal{M}_{12}^{\alpha}}
are isomorphisms onto their images: an element in
\ker p_{1}|_{\mathcal{M}_{12}^{\alpha}}
lies in
\mathcal{H}_{2}
and is orthogonal to both
\mathcal{W}_{2}
and
\mathcal{W}_{12}^{\alpha}
, however
p_{2}(\mathcal{W}_{2}+\mathcal{W}_{12}^{\alpha})=\mathcal{H}_{2}
for every
\alpha>0
. Analogously,
\ker p_{2}|_{\mathcal{M}_{12}^{\alpha}}=\{0\}
. Moreover, given
Z_{1}+Z_{2}\in\mathcal{M}_{12}^{1}
, we have
\alpha Z_{1}+Z_{2}\in\mathcal{M}_{12}^{\alpha}
. In particular, there is an invertible linear map
T:p_{1}(\mathcal{M})\to p_{2}(\mathcal{M})
such that

Z\in\mathcal{M}_{12}^{\alpha}\Longleftrightarrow Z=\alpha Z_{1}+T(Z_{1}),~\text{for some }Z_{1}\in p_{1}(\mathcal{M}).

We conclude that:

\displaystyle W\in\mathcal{W}_{12}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow W=W_{1}-\alpha(T^{*})^{-1}W_{1},~\text{for some }W_{1}\in p_{1}(\mathcal{M}).

Using that
\|T\|^{-1}\leq\|T^{-1}\|
and
\|T^{*}\|=\|T\|
, we have

\|W_{1}\|^{2}+\alpha^{2}\|T\|^{-2}\|W_{1}\|^{2}\leq\|W\|^{2}\leq\|W_{1}\|^{2}+\alpha^{2}\|(T^{-1})\|^{2}\|W_{1}\|^{2}.\qed

Now we estimate
\tr(p_{1}|_{\mathcal{W}^{\alpha}}).
Since
\mathcal{W}_{2}\subset\ker p_{1}
, we take orthonormal bases
\{e_{1},...,e_{d_{1}}\}
and
\{e_{1}^{\alpha},...,e_{d}^{\alpha}\}
for
\mathcal{W}_{1}
and
\mathcal{W}_{12}^{\alpha}
, respectively, and consider:

\tr(p_{1}|_{\mathcal{W}^{\alpha}})=\sum_{j}^{d_{1}}\langle p_{1}e_{j},e_{j}\rangle+\sum_{k}^{d}\langle p_{1}e_{k}^{\alpha},e_{k}^{\alpha}\rangle=\dim\mathcal{W}_{1}+\sum_{k}^{d}\|p_{1}e_{k}^{\alpha}\|^{2}.

Lemma
5.12
gives:

\dim\mathcal{W}_{1}+\frac{1}{1+c\alpha}\dim\mathcal{W}_{12}^{\alpha}\leq\mathrm{tr}(p_{1}|_{\mathcal{W}^{\alpha}})\leq\dim\mathcal{W}_{1}+\frac{1}{1+C\alpha}\dim\mathcal{W}_{12}^{\alpha}.

(62)

By taking
\alpha\to\infty
we conclude that
\inf_{\alpha}\{\tr(p_{1}|_{\mathcal{W}^{\alpha}})\}=\dim\mathcal{W}_{1}
. On the other hand,
\mathcal{H}_{1}=\mathcal{W}_{1}+d\rho(\mathfrak{g}_{q})Y_{1}
. So it follows that
\dim\mathcal{W}_{1}=\dim\mathcal{H}_{1}-\dim\rho(G_{q})Y_{1}
, completing the proof.∎

The proof of Proposition
5.11
can be adapted when
\mathcal{H}_{q}\cap{\mathrm{span}\{X\}}^{\perp}
is decomposed into
m

\rho
-invariant components:

Proposition 5.13
.

Suppose that
d\rho(\mathfrak{g}_{q})X=0
and that
\mathcal{H}_{q}\cap{\mathrm{span}\{X\}}^{\perp}=\mathrm{span}\{X\}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{2}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathcal{H}_{m}
is a
\rho
-invariant decomposition. For every
Y\in\mathcal{R}
, write
Y_{i}
as its
\mathcal{H}_{i}
-component. Then there exists
j_{0}\in\{1,2,\dots,m\}
for which

\displaystyle\inf_{\mathcal{W}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}_{q}}\{\tr(p_{j_{0}}|_{\mathcal{W}})\}=\inf_{Y\in\mathcal{R}}\{\dim\mathcal{H}_{j_{0}}-\dim\rho(G^{0}_{q})Y_{j_{0}}\}.

Proof.

Following the proof of Proposition
5.11
, fix
Y=Y_{1}+\dots+Y_{m}\in\mathcal{R}
. For every
\alpha>0
, define
\mathcal{W}^{\alpha}{=}\left(d\rho(\mathfrak{g}_{q})(\alpha Y_{1}+Y_{2}+\dots+Y_{m})\right)^{\perp}
, where
Y_{i}\in\mathcal{H}_{i}
.

Take
j_{0}
given by Lemma
5.7
and let
\mathcal{W}^{\alpha}=\mathcal{W}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{W}_{2}\oplus\mathcal{W}_{12}^{\alpha},

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{1}

\displaystyle:=(\bigcap_{i\neq j_{0}}\ker~p_{i})\cap\mathcal{W}^{\alpha},

(63)

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{2}

\displaystyle:=\ker~p_{j_{0}}\cap\mathcal{W}^{\alpha},

(64)

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{12}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{W}^{\alpha}\cap\left(\mathcal{W}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{W}_{2}\right)^{\bot},

(65)

where
p_{i}
is the
\mathcal{H}_{i}
projection. As in Proposition
5.11
,
\mathcal{W}_{1},\mathcal{W}_{2}
and the dimension of
\mathcal{W}_{12}^{\alpha}
do not depend on
\alpha
. Moreover, Lemma
5.12
remains valid in this case and we can estimate
\tr(p_{j_{0}}|_{\mathcal{W}^{\alpha}})
as well.

The same calculation performed in Proposition
5.11
yields

\inf_{\alpha}\{\tr(p_{j_{0}}|_{\mathcal{W}^{\alpha}})\}=\dim\mathcal{W}_{1}.

Since these dimensions are finite, it is clear that
\mathcal{W}_{1}{=}\mathcal{H}_{j_{0}}\cap\left(d\rho(\mathfrak{g}_{q})Y_{j_{0}}\right)^{\perp}
, which completes the proof.
∎

Now Theorem
5.9

follows by combining Theorem
4.1
, Propositions
5.11
and
5.13
and Lemma
5.10
.

Constraints on the χ \PQc ​ 1 \chi_{{\PQc}1} versus χ \PQc ​ 2 \chi_{{\PQc}2} polarizations in proton-proton collisions at s = 8 \sqrt{s}=8 \TeV


Abstract
The polarizations of promptly produced χ \PQc ​ 1 \chi_{{\PQc}1} and χ \PQc ​ 2 \chi_{{\PQc}2} mesons are studied using data collected by the CMS experiment at the LHC, in proton-proton collisions at s = 8 \sqrt{s}=8 TeV. The χ \PQc \chi_{\PQc} states are reconstructed via their radiative decays χ \PQc → \JPsi ​ γ \chi_{\PQc}\rightarrow\JPsi\,\gamma , with the photons being measured through conversions to \Pep ​ \Pem \Pep\Pem , which allows the two states to be well resolved. The polarizations are measured in the helicity frame, through the analysis of the χ \PQc ​ 2 \chi_{{\PQc}2} to χ \PQc ​ 1 \chi_{{\PQc}1} yield ratio as a function of the polar or azimuthal angle of the positive muon emitted in the \JPsi → μ + ​ μ − \JPsi\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-} decay, in three bins of \JPsi transverse momentum. While no differences are seen between the two states in terms of azimuthal decay angle distributions, they are observed to have significantly different polar anisotropies. The measurement favors a scenario where at least one of the two states is strongly polarized along the helicity quantization axis, in agreement with nonrelativistic quantum chromodynamics predictions. This is the first measurement of significantly polarized quarkonia produced at high transverse momentum.

This Letter reports the first measurement of the polarizations of promptly produced
\chi_{{\PQc}1}

\chi_{{\PQc}2}

The central feature of the CMS apparatus is a superconducting solenoid of 6
\unit
m internal diameter,
providing a magnetic field of 3.8
\unit
T.
Within the solenoid volume are a silicon pixel and strip tracker, a lead tungstate crystal electromagnetic calorimeter,

Muons are detected in gas-ionization chambers embedded in the steel flux-return yoke outside the solenoid.
A detailed description of the CMS detector, together with a definition of the coordinate system used and
relevant kinematic variables, can be found in Ref.
[

The event sample was collected with a two-level trigger system
[
46

.
At level-1, custom hardware processors select events with two muons.
The high-level trigger requires an opposite-sign muon pair of invariant mass 2.8–3.35
\GeV

a dimuon vertex fit
\chi^{2}
probability larger than 0.5%,
and a distance of closest approach between the two muons smaller than 0.5
\unit

The trigger also requires that the dimuon has
\pt>7.9
\GeV
and rapidity
\abs{y}<1.25

The offline reconstruction requires two oppositely charged muons matching those that triggered the detector readout.
The muon tracks must pass high-purity track quality requirements
[
47

,
have
\pt>3.5
\GeV

\abs{\eta}<1.6
, and
fulfill the soft muon identification requirements
[
48

which imply, in particular, more than five hits in the silicon tracker, of which at least one is in the pixel layers.
The muons are combined to form
\JPsi
candidates, which are kept for further processing if
\abs{y}<1.2
and
8<\pt<30
\GeV

Promptly produced
\JPsi

be smaller than 2.5 times its uncertainty.

The analysis uses
\chi_{\PQc}\to\JPsi\,\gamma

\JPsi

The photons are detected through their conversions to
\Pep\Pem

starting from two oppositely charged tracks, of which one has at least four tracker hits and the other at least three.
The tracks must have

A more detailed account of the reconstruction and selection procedures is given in Refs.

The photons must have

If the distance along the beam axis between the dimuon vertex and the extrapolated photon trajectory

constraining the dimuon mass to the
\JPsi

[

Only

\chi_{{\PQc}1}
and
\chi_{{\PQc}2}

After all selection criteria,
around 103 000, 106 000, and 45 000

in the
\JPsi

The seemingly natural way to measure the
\chi_{{\PQc}1}
and
\chi_{{\PQc}2}
polarizations
is to determine the angular distribution of the considered
\chi_{\PQc}
decay;
in the present case, this means the distribution of the photon direction in the
\chi_{\PQc}
rest frame.
However, that distribution depends not only on the
\chi_{\PQc}
angular momentum composition,
but also, and possibly in a very significant way,
on the (poorly known) contributions of photons with large orbital angular momentum (
J^{\gamma}>1
).
A cleaner determination of the
\chi_{\PQc}
polarization is obtained by measuring the dimuon angular decay distribution
in the rest frame of the daughter
\JPsi

[
51
]
.
It is crucial to choose as polarization axis for the
\JPsi
decay not the
\JPsi
direction in the
\chi_{\PQc}
rest frame,
as usually done in cascade decays,
but rather any axis (center-of-mass helicity or Collins–Soper
[
52
]
, for instance)
defined in terms of the beam momenta in the
\JPsi
rest frame
and ignoring its origin, as if it were observed inclusively.
With the latter choice, the shape of the dimuon distribution represents an exact “clone” of the photon distribution
in the
\chi_{\PQc}
rest frame, as it would be if it were undressed of its higher-order multipole contributions.
This method provides, therefore, a full sensitivity to the angular momentum state of the
\chi_{\PQc}
,
resulting in a (theoretically and experimentally) cleaner polarization measurement.
The present analysis is performed in the center-of-mass helicity frame
[
53
]

and does not use the measured photon momentum,
except to select, through the
\JPsi\,\gamma
invariant mass distribution, the
\JPsi
mesons
resulting from
\chi_{{\PQc}1}
or
\chi_{{\PQc}2}
decays.
The dimuon angular decay distribution is parametrized with the function

1+\lambda_{\vartheta}\cos^{2}\vartheta+\lambda_{\varphi}\sin^{2}\vartheta\cos 2\varphi+\lambda_{\vartheta\varphi}\sin 2\vartheta\cos\varphi
,
where
\vartheta
and
\varphi
are the polar and azimuthal coordinates of the
positive muon direction in the
\JPsi
rest frame, the system of axes
being defined with
z
in the direction of the polarization axis and
y

perpendicular to the production plane.
The
\chi_{\PQc}
angular momentum composition is encoded in the shape parameters
\lambda_{\vartheta}
,
\lambda_{\varphi}
, and
\lambda_{\vartheta\varphi}
,
whose values depend on the choice of polarization frame but
must always be within certain physical domains
[
51
]
,
narrower than the parameter space of inclusive vector-particle production
[
54
,
55
]
.
The relation between the shape parameters and the polarization configuration depends on the quarkonium state.
For example,

\lambda_{\vartheta}=+1
indicates
J_{z}=\pm 1
for the
\JPsi
,
J_{z}=0
for the
\chi_{{\PQc}1}
, and
J_{z}=+2
for the
\chi_{{\PQc}2}
;
conversely, states in the
J_{z}=0
angular momentum configuration lead to

\lambda_{\vartheta}=-1
for the
\JPsi
,
\lambda_{\vartheta}=+1
for the
\chi_{{\PQc}1}
, and
\lambda_{\vartheta}=-0.6
for the
\chi_{{\PQc}2}
.

The measurement of the
\lambda
parameters implies knowing the shapes
of the
\chi_{{\PQc}1}
and
\chi_{{\PQc}2}
differential cross sections as functions of
\abs{\cos\vartheta}
and
\varphi
,
which crucially depend on the accuracy of the corrections of the muon and photon detection efficiencies.
These efficiencies change by an order of magnitude in the low
\pt
bin covered by the present analysis
and shape variations within their uncertainties lead to very different
\lambda_{\vartheta}
values.
Increasing the muon
\pt
threshold to avoid the turn-on region of the
efficiency function would imply a strong reduction in the number of selected events
and a smaller coverage of the
\abs{\cos\vartheta}
variable, effectively preventing the evaluation of
\lambda_{\vartheta}
.
Instead, the difference between the
\chi_{{\PQc}1}
and
\chi_{{\PQc}2}
polarizations,
measured from the angular dependence of the
\chi_{{\PQc}2}/\chi_{{\PQc}1}
yield ratio,
is essentially insensitive to the detection efficiencies, given that they cancel to a large extent in that ratio.

The
\abs{\cos\vartheta}
and
\varphi
dependences of the yield ratio
are independently determined in three
\JPsi
\pt
bins: 8–12, 12–18, and 18–30
\GeV
.
For the study of possible azimuthal dependences of the
\chi_{{\PQc}2}/\chi_{{\PQc}1}
yield ratio,
the events are split into subsamples corresponding to six equidistant
\varphi
bins between 0 and 90
∘
.
Folding
\varphi
into the first quadrant reduces the effect of the statistical fluctuations without any loss of information,
given the four-fold
\varphi
symmetry that the angular distributions obey.
For each
\pt
bin, the six
\JPsi\,\gamma
invariant mass distributions are simultaneously fitted
with an unbinned maximum likelihood fit.
In the mass fit model, identical for all
\varphi
bins, each of the
\chi_{{\PQc}1}
and
\chi_{{\PQc}2}
signal peaks
is represented by a double-sided Crystal Ball (CB) function
[
56
]
,
which complements a Gaussian core distribution with lower and upper power-law tails.
The underlying combinatorial background, reflecting uncorrelated
\JPsi\,\gamma
associations,
is parametrized by an exponential function multiplied by a term that provides a low-mass turn-down,

(1+\erf((m-\mu^{\text{bg}})\,/\,\sigma^{\text{bg}}))\,\exp(-m/\lambda^{\text{bg}})
,
where
m
is the
\JPsi\,\gamma
invariant mass
and
\mu^{\text{bg}}
,
\sigma^{\text{bg}}
, and
\lambda^{\text{bg}}
are shape parameters.
Although the results of this analysis are insensitive to the presence of a small peak
reflecting the
\chi_{{\PQc}0}
decays, the fit model includes this background term,
represented by a Breit–Wigner convolved with a Gaussian resolution function.
To minimize fit instabilities, the
\chi_{{\PQc}0}
shape and yield parameters are determined from
the corresponding parameters of the
\chi_{{\PQc}1}
term.
The simultaneous fit has the advantage of reducing by a factor of six the number of free parameters
defining the shapes of the signal and background mass models,
by requiring that those parameters are independent of
\varphi
,
an assumption validated by studies of simulated and measured event samples.

To study the polar angle dependence of the
\chi_{{\PQc}2}/\chi_{{\PQc}1}
yield ratio,
6, 7, or 5
\abs{\cos\vartheta}
bins are considered, depending on the
\pt
bin.
The
\abs{\cos\vartheta}
coverage is smaller in the lowest
\pt
bin (up to 0.45 instead of up to 0.625)
because those events are the ones most affected by the single-muon
\pt
cut.
Analogously to the procedure just described for the
\varphi
dimension,
the
\chi_{{\PQc}2}/\chi_{{\PQc}1}
yield ratios are obtained as a function of
\abs{\cos\vartheta}
through a simultaneous fit of the
\JPsi\,\gamma
invariant mass distributions.
In this case, however, some of the shape parameters are not required to be independent of
\abs{\cos\vartheta}
.
More details can be found in Ref.
[
57
]
.

The

\chi_{{\PQc}J}

By correcting the detector acceptance and efficiency effects on an event-by-event basis,
with weights depending on three dimuon observables (
\abs{\cos\vartheta}

\varphi
, and
\pt
),
this procedure is immune to integration biases affecting certain one-dimensional analyses

58
]
.
Simulation studies have shown that, if the three-dimensional correction maps are sufficiently fine-grained,
the results do not depend on the polarization scenario nor on the
\pt

and that all physically allowed differences between the
\chi_{{\PQc}1}
and
\chi_{{\PQc}2}
polarizations, in any frame,
can be reliably determined from the dependences of the
\chi_{{\PQc}2}/\chi_{{\PQc}1}

\abs{\cos\vartheta}
and
\varphi
.

The corrected ratios are reported in
Tables
.1
and
.1
of Appendix

,
and shown in Fig.
2
,
where it can be seen that the uncorrected and corrected values are almost identical,
apart from normalization factors irrelevant for the determination of the polar and azimuthal anisotropies.

Several sources of potential systematic effects have been considered,
by redoing the analysis with different inputs
and comparing the obtained results with the nominal ones.
The results are insensitive to variations of the
thresholds used to reject the nonprompt contamination from
\cPqb
hadron decays,
estimated to be around 5%,
or events with a poor kinematic vertex fit quality in the reconstruction of the
\chi_{\PQc}
candidates.
The fits of the mass distributions were redone
using alternative options for the low- and high-mass tails of the double-sided CB functions,
and by varying the combinatorial background description, both by changing the floating parameters
of the nominal function and by using the alternative function
(x-x_{0})^{\lambda}\,\exp{(\nu(x-x_{0}))}
,
where
\nu
is left free,
\lambda
is fitted to a constant, and
x_{0}=3.2
\GeV
,
a value determined in fits to the background-only mass distributions
obtained by excluding the 3.37–3.6
\GeV
region.
The sensitivity of the results to the acceptance and efficiency corrections was evaluated by
redoing the analysis with maps computed with alternative single-muon and photon detection
efficiencies, as well as with
simulated samples generated with different
\pt/M
shapes for each of the two
\chi_{\PQc}
states.
All effects lead to similar variations in the yields of the two states and cancel,
to a large extent, in the
\chi_{{\PQc}2}/\chi_{{\PQc}1}
ratio,
apart from a normalization shift that has no impact on the angular anisotropies.
The total systematic uncertainties are less than 20% of the statistical ones.

values in each bin,
the vertical bars represent the total uncertainties, and the horizontal bars the bin widths.
The dashed lines indicate the physically allowed range of
\lambda_{\vartheta}^{\chi_{{\PQc}2}}
.

In summary, first experimental constraints
on the polarizations of promptly produced
\chi_{{\PQc}1}
and
\chi_{{\PQc}2}
mesons
have been obtained, using
\Pp\Pp
collisions at
\sqrt{s}=8\TeV
.
The analysis uses the
\JPsi\,\gamma
decay channel in three
\JPsi
\pt
bins between 8 and 30
\GeV
.
The measurement, made in the helicity frame,
shows a significant difference between the polar anisotropy parameters
\lambda_{\vartheta}^{\chi_{{\PQc}1}}
and
\lambda_{\vartheta}^{\chi_{{\PQc}2}}
,
in agreement with the NRQCD prediction.
This result is a new step in the experimental studies of quarkonium production and the
first significant indication of kinematic differences between the various quarkonia.

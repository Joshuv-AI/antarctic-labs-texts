Real analytic nonexpansive maps on polyhedral normed spaces
Brian Lins

Abstract
. If a real analytic nonexpansive map on a polyhedral normed space has a nonempty fixed point set, then we show that there is an isometry from an affine subspace onto the fixed point set. As a corollary, we prove that for any real analytic 1-norm or ∞ \infty -norm nonexpansive map on ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} , there is a positive integer q q such that the period of any periodic orbit divides q q and q q is the order, or twice the order, of a permutation on n n letters. This confirms Nussbaum’s 2 n 2^{n} Conjecture for ∞ \infty -norm nonexpansive maps in the special case where the maps are also real analytic.

1.
Introduction

Bruck
[
2
]
showed that the fixed point set of a nonexpansive map on a Banach space with the fixed point property is a nonexpansive retract. This implies that the fixed point set must be connected. In strictly convex spaces, the fixed point set must also be convex, but that is not true in other Banach spaces, and in general the fixed point sets of nonexpansive maps can be quite complicated. We show in this paper that the fixed point set of a real analytic nonexpansive map on a finite dimensional Banach space with a polyhedral norm must be a Lipschitz manifold that is isometric to a linear subspace.

Nonexpansive maps on polyhedral normed spaces arise naturally in nonlinear Perron–Frobenius theory. In some important cases, these maps are also real analytic. Nussbaum
[
12
, Section 2]
introduced and studied a large class
\mathcal{M}
of real analytic nonexpansive maps with applications in population biology, see also
[
4
, Section 6.6]
. Lim
[
8
]
and Qi
[
15
]
independently introduced the H-eigenproblem for nonnegative tensors which seeks entrywise positive eigenvectors for nonlinear maps corresponding to tensors. The eigenvectors of these maps correspond to fixed points of maps on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
which are real analytic and
\infty
-norm nonexpansive, see e.g.,
[
9
, Section 7.2]
. Real analytic nonexpansive maps also appear in the theory of neural networks when composing norm one linear transformations with real analytic activation functions, such as the sigmoid function
[
14
]
.

Let
X
be a real Banach space with norm
\|\cdot\|
and dual space
X^{*}
. Let
B_{X}
denote the closed unit ball in
X
. A finite dimensional Banach space has a
polyhedral norm
if the unit ball has a finite number of extreme points, or equivalently if the dual unit ball
B_{X^{*}}
has a finite number of extreme points. We use
\operatorname{ext}C
to denote the extreme points of a convex set
C
.

For any map
f:X\rightarrow X
, let
\operatorname{Fix}(f)
denote the set of fixed points of
f
.
A map
f:X\rightarrow X
is
nonexpansive
if
\|f(x)-f(y)\|\leq\|x-y\|
for every
x,y\in X
.
A function
f:X\rightarrow X
is
real analytic
if for every
x\in X
, there is a convergent power series for
f
in a neighborhood of
x
, that is, there is an
r>0
and continuous symmetric
n
-linear forms
A_{n}:X^{n}\rightarrow X
such that
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|A_{n}\|r^{n}<\infty
and

f(x+h)=f(x)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n}(h^{n})

for all
h
in a neighborhood of
0
in
X
. When
X=\mathbb{R}^{n}
, this definition is equivalent to requiring that each component of
f
be a real analytic function.

In this paper, our primary interest is real analytic nonexpansive maps on polyhedral normed spaces. However, for context we begin with a simple result about the fixed point sets of real analytic nonexpansive maps on strictly convex Banach spaces. A Banach space is
strictly convex
if the boundary of the unit ball does not contain any line segments.

Proposition 1.1
.

Let
X
be a strictly convex Banach space. Suppose
f:X\rightarrow X
is nonexpansive and real analytic. If
\operatorname{Fix}(f)
is nonempty, then it is an affine subspace of
X
.

Proof.

If
\operatorname{Fix}(f)
is empty or contains only a single point, there is nothing to prove. Suppose that
\operatorname{Fix}(f)
contains two distinct points,
x
and
y
. Let
\delta=\|x-y\|
. For any
0<t<1
, the closed balls
x+t\delta B_{X}
and
y+(1-t)\delta B_{X}
intersect at a single point
(1-t)x+ty
. Since both balls are invariant under
f
by nonexpansiveness, the intersection points are fixed points for all
0<t<1
. Since
f
is real analytic, so is the map

t\mapsto f((1-t)x+ty)-(1-t)x-ty.

As this map is identically zero for all
0<t<1
, it follows that it is identically zero for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Therefore every point on the line
\{(1-t)x+ty:t\in\mathbb{R}\}
is a fixed point of
f
. From this we conclude that the affine hull of
\operatorname{Fix}(f)
is contained in
\operatorname{Fix}(f)
, and therefore
\operatorname{Fix}(f)
is an affine subspace.
∎

The fixed point set of real analytic nonexpansive maps in a polyhedral normed space need not be an affine subspace. However, in section
2
we state and prove the main result of this paper which guarantees that the fixed point set of a real analytic polyhedral norm nonexpansive map must be isometric to an affine subspace.

In section
3
we consider periodic orbits of real analytic nonexpansive maps.
The
orbit
of a point
x\in X
under the action of a map
f:X\rightarrow X
is the set
\{f^{k}(x):k\geq 0\}
. A point
x\in X
is a
periodic point
of
f
if its orbit is finite. In that case, the
minimal period
of
x
, also referred to as the
period
of the orbit of
x
, is the number of elements in its orbit. The following proposition summarizes several known facts about the periodic points of polyhedral norm nonexpansive maps
[
4
, Theorem 4.2.1, Corollary 4.2.5, and Lemma 4.2.6]
.

Proposition 1.2
.

For any polyhedral normed space
X
, there is an upper bound
M
on the minimal periods of periodic points under the action of any nonexpansive map on
X
. Moreover, if
f:X\rightarrow X
is nonexpansive and
\operatorname{Fix}(f)
is not empty, then for every
x\in X
, there is a periodic point
\xi
of
f
with minimal period
q
such that
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}(f^{kq}(x))=\xi
.

Nussbaum has conjectured
[
13
]
that any periodic point of an
\infty
-norm nonexpansive map on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
has minimal period at most
2^{n}

[
4
, Conjecture 4.2.2]
. The conjecture has been confirmed only up to
n=3

[
10
]
, and the proof for
n=3
is nontrivial. The current best known upper bound on the minimal periods of
\infty
-norm nonexpansive maps is
\max_{0\leq k\leq n}2^{n}\binom{n}{k}

[
5
]
. The conjectured upper bound of
2^{n}
would be tight, since there are
\infty
-norm nonexpansive maps on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with periodic orbits containing
2^{n}
elements. In section
3
, we show that the periodic orbits of real analytic 1-norm and
\infty
-norm nonexpansive maps on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
correspond to the periodic orbits of norm one linear maps. Then, by a result of Lemmens and van Gaans, the maximum period of any orbit is at most twice the order of a permutation on
n
letters. This implies Nussbaum’s
2^{n}
Conjecture for real analytic
\infty
-norm nonexpansive maps on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

2.
Main Result

A
retract
, also known as a
projection
is a map
R:X\rightarrow X
such that
R^{2}(x)=R(x)
for all
x\in X
. We will reserve the term projection for retracts that are also linear. If
X
is a finite dimensional normed space,
f:X\rightarrow X
is nonexpansive, and
\operatorname{Fix}(f)
is nonempty, then the Krasnoselskii iterates, defined recursively by

x^{k+1}=\tfrac{1}{2}f(x^{k})+\tfrac{1}{2}x^{k},

converge to a fixed point for every
x^{0}\in X

[
3
]
. Define
R:X\rightarrow\operatorname{Fix}(f)
by

R(x)=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}(\tfrac{1}{2}f+\tfrac{1}{2}\operatorname{id})^{k}(x)

(2.1)

where
\operatorname{id}
denotes the identity map on
X
. Since
R
is the pointwise limit of the nonexpansive maps
(\tfrac{1}{2}f+\tfrac{1}{2}\operatorname{id})^{k}
, it follows that
R
is a nonexpansive retract onto
\operatorname{Fix}(f)
.

Theorem 2.1
.

If
X
is a polyhedral normed space,
f:X\rightarrow X
is nonexpansive and real analytic, and
\operatorname{Fix}(f)
is nonempty, then there is an affine subspace
W\subseteq X
such that the nonexpansive retract
R
defined by (
2.1
) is an isometry from
W
onto
\operatorname{Fix}(f)
.

In order to prove this result, we begin with the following observations. For each of the following lemmas, we assume that
X
is a polyhedral normed space and
f:X\rightarrow X
is real analytic and nonexpansive.
Let
J:X\rightrightarrows B_{X^{*}}
be the
duality map

J(x)=\{\phi\in B_{X^{*}}:\|x\|=\phi(x)\}.

Note that
J
maps points in
X
to faces of
B_{X^{*}}
.

For any set
E\subseteq B_{X^{*}}
and any map
f:X\rightarrow X
, let

S_{E}(f)=\{x\in X:\phi(f(x))=\phi(x)\text{ for all }\phi\in E\},

and

L_{E}=\{x\in X:\phi(x)=\psi(x)\text{ for all }\phi,\psi\in E\}.

Lemma 2.2
.

For any
x\in X
, there exists
\epsilon>0
such that
J(x+y)\subseteq J(x)
for every
y\in X
with
\|y\|\leq\epsilon
.

Proof.

The claim is trivial if
x=0
, since
J(x)=B_{X^{*}}
in that case. Suppose that
x\neq 0
. Since
X
is a polyhedral normed space, the dual unit ball
B_{X^{*}}
only has a finite number of extreme points. Therefore there is a constant
c<1
such that
\phi(x)<c\|x\|
for all
\phi\in\operatorname{ext}B_{X^{*}}\setminus J(x)
. Since
c<1
, we can choose
\epsilon
small enough so that
c\|x\|+\epsilon<\|x\|-\epsilon
. Then

\phi(x+y)\leq c\|x\|+\epsilon<\|x\|-\epsilon\leq\|x+y\|

for all
y\in B_{X}
and
\phi\in\operatorname{ext}B_{X^{*}}\setminus E
. Therefore
J(x+y)
is a face of
B_{X^{*}}
which does not contain any extreme points of
B_{X^{*}}
outside
J(x)
. Since every face of
B_{X^{*}}
is the convex hull of the elements of
\operatorname{ext}B_{X^{*}}
which are contained in the face
[
1
, Theorem 7.3]
, we conclude that
J(x+y)\subseteq J(x)
.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

If
E=J(y)
for some
y\in X
, then
L_{E}
is the linear span of the face

F_{E}=\{x\in B_{X}:\phi(x)=1\text{ for all }\phi\in E\}.

Proof.

It is clear from the definition that
\operatorname{span}F_{E}\subseteq L_{E}
. Let
x\in L_{E}
. By choosing
\epsilon>0
small enough, we can guarantee that
J(y-\epsilon x)
and
J(y+\epsilon x)
are subsets of
E
by Lemma
2.2
. Since both
y+\epsilon x
and
y-\epsilon x
are in
L_{E}
, we have
J(y-\epsilon x)=J(y+\epsilon x)=E
. Therefore

\frac{y-\epsilon x}{\|y-\epsilon x\|}\text{ and }\frac{y+\epsilon x}{\|y+\epsilon x\|}

are both contained in
F_{E}
, and
x
is a linear combination of these two points. Therefore
L_{E}=\operatorname{span}F_{E}
.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

If there exist
v,w\in S_{E}(f)
such that
J(v-w)=E
, then

w+L_{E}\subseteq S_{E}(f).

Proof.

Let
x=\tfrac{1}{2}(v+w)
. Observe that
J(x-w)=J(v-x)=E
. By Lemma
2.2
, there is a sufficiently small relatively open neighborhood
U
around
x
in
w+L_{E}
such that
J(u-w)
and
J(v-u)
are subsets of
E
for every
u\in U
. Then, since
u\in w+L_{E}
, it follows that
J(u-w)=J(v-u)=E
. For any
\phi\in E
and
u\in U
,

\displaystyle\phi(v)-\phi(u)

\displaystyle=\|v-u\|

\displaystyle\geq\|f(v)-f(u)\|

\displaystyle(\text{nonexpansiveness})

\displaystyle\geq\phi(f(v))-\phi(f(u))

\displaystyle(\text{since }\phi\in B_{X^{*}})

\displaystyle=\phi(v)-\phi(f(u)),

\displaystyle(\text{since }v\in S_{E}(f))

which implies that
\phi(f(u))\geq\phi(u)
. Similarly,

\displaystyle\phi(u)-\phi(w)

\displaystyle=\|u-w\|

\displaystyle\geq\|f(u)-f(w)\|

\displaystyle(\text{nonexpansiveness})

\displaystyle\geq\phi(f(u))-\phi(f(w))

\displaystyle(\text{since }\phi\in B_{X^{*}})

\displaystyle=\phi(f(u))-\phi(w).

\displaystyle(\text{since }w\in S_{E}(f))

Combining this with the previous inequality, we have
\phi(f(u))=\phi(u)
for all
u\in U
and
\phi\in E
.
Since
\phi(f(u))-\phi(u)
is real analytic and identically zero on
U
, it follows that it is identically zero on the affine span of
U
which is
w+L_{E}
. Therefore
w+L_{E}\subseteq S_{E}(f)
.
∎

We will say that a set
E\subseteq B_{X^{*}}
is
locked
if there exist
v,w\in S_{E}(f)
such that
J(v-w)=E
. A locked set
E
is
minimal
if no proper subset of
E
is locked.

Lemma 2.5
.

If
E\subseteq B_{X^{*}}
is a minimal locked set, then
S_{E}(f)=x+L_{E}
for any
x\in S_{E}(f)
.

Proof.

Since
E
is locked, there exist
v,w\in S_{E}(f)
such that
J(v-w)=E
. Choose any
x\in S_{E}(f)
. We will show that
x\in w+L_{E}
.

Let
y=v-w
. For any sufficiently large
t>0
,

J(w+ty-x)=J(y+t^{-1}(w-x))\subseteq E

by Lemma
2.2
. Let
G=J(w+ty-x)
. Observe that
S_{E}(f)\subseteq S_{G}(f)
since
G\subseteq E
.
By Lemma
2.4
,
w+L_{E}\subseteq S_{E}(f)
. Therefore both
x
and
w+ty
are elements of
S_{G}(f)
. Since
E
has no locked proper subsets, we conclude that
G=J(w+ty-x)=E
. This means that
x\in w+L_{E}
which proves that
S_{E}(f)=w+L_{E}
. Of course,
x+L_{E}=w+L_{E}
since
x-w\in L_{E}
.
∎

Let
\mathcal{M}(f)
be the union of all minimal locked subsets of
B_{X^{*}}
. The following lemma shows that the linear functionals in
\mathcal{M}(f)
separate points in
\operatorname{Fix}(f)
.

Lemma 2.6
.

If
x,y\in\operatorname{Fix}(f)
have
\phi(x)=\phi(y)
for all
\phi\in\mathcal{M}(f)
, then
x=y
.

Proof.

Consider the set
G=J(x-y)
. If
x\neq y
, then
G
cannot contain any
\phi\in\mathcal{M}(f)
. Furthermore,
G
is locked by definition since both
x,y
are fixed points of
f
and are therefore elements of
S_{G}(f)
. Then
G
contains a minimal locked subset, but that contracts the assumption that
\mathcal{M}(f)
is the union of every minimal locked subset. So
x=y
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

If
0
is not a fixed point of
f
, then we can replace
f(x)
with
f(x+y)-y
where
y\in\operatorname{Fix}(f)
which allows us to assume without loss of generality that
0\in\operatorname{Fix}(f)
. Note that
\operatorname{Fix}(f)\subseteq S_{E}(f)
for every
E\subseteq B_{X^{*}}
. In particular, since
0\in S_{E}(f)
for every minimal locked set
E
of
f
, Lemma
2.5
implies that
S_{E}(f)=L_{E}
. Let
V(f)
be the intersection of the subspaces
L_{E}
corresponding to each minimal locked subset
E
of
f
. Then
\operatorname{Fix}(f)\subseteq V(f)
.

Since the nonexpansive retract
R
defined by (
2.1
) is a 1-Lipschitz map from any subspace of
X
onto
\operatorname{Fix}(f)
, Rademacher’s theorem implies that there is a point
u\in V(f)
where
R
is differentiable as a map from
V(f)
into
\operatorname{Fix}(f)
. Let
A
be the derivative of
R
at
u
.

For any
x\in V(f)
and
\phi\in\mathcal{M}(f)
we know that
\phi(f(x))=\phi(x)
by the definition of
V(f)
. We also have
\phi(R(x))=\phi(x)
by (
2.1
). In addition,

\phi(Ax)=\lim_{h\rightarrow 0}\frac{\phi(R(u+hx)-R(u))}{h}=\lim_{h\rightarrow 0}\frac{\phi(u+hx)-\phi(u)}{h}=\phi(x).

Therefore

\phi(R(x))=\phi(Ax)=\phi(x)

(2.2)

for all
\phi\in\mathcal{M}(f)
and
x\in V(f)
.

Let
W
be the image of
V(f)
under
A
. As the derivative of a nonexpansive map,
A
is a nonexpansive linear transformation from
V(f)
onto
W
. By Lemma
2.6
, the functionals in
\mathcal{M}(f)
separate points in
\operatorname{Fix}(f)
. We will now prove that these functionals also separate points in
W
. Suppose that
w\in W
has
\phi(w)=0
for all
\phi\in\mathcal{M}(f)
. By definition, there must be a
v\in V(f)
such that
w=Av
. By (
2.2
)
\phi(v)=\phi(Av)=\phi(w)=0
and therefore

\phi(R(u+hv)-R(u))=0
for every
\phi\in\mathcal{M}(f)
and
h\in\mathbb{R}
. Then Lemma
2.6
implies that the fixed points
R(u+hv)
and
R(u)
must be identical. So

w=Av=\lim_{h\rightarrow 0}\frac{R(u+hv)-R(u)}{h}=0.

This means that if
x,y\in W
and
\phi(x)=\phi(y)
for all
\phi\in\mathcal{M}(f)
, then
x=y
, so
\mathcal{M}(f)
separates points on
W
.

Since
A
and
R
preserve the values of every
\phi\in\mathcal{M}(f)
on
V(f)
and
\mathcal{M}(f)
separates points in both
W
and
\operatorname{Fix}(f)
, it follows that
A
and
R
are inverse bijections between
W
and
\operatorname{Fix}(f)
. Since both maps are nonexpansive, they must also both be isometries.
∎

Remark 2.7
.

Let
A
be the map from the proof above. By (
2.2
),
\phi(A^{2}x)=\phi(Ax)
for all
x\in V(f)
and
\phi\in\mathcal{M}(f)
. Since
\mathcal{M}(f)
separates points in
W
, it follows that
A^{2}=A
. Therefore
A
is a nonexpansive linear projection from
V(f)
onto
W
.

3.
Periodic points

Let
[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Let
e_{i}
,
i\in[n]
, denote the standard basis vectors for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Recall that the
p
-norm
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is

\|x\|_{p}=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|x_{i}|^{p}\right)^{1/p}

for
1\leq p<\infty
, and

\|x\|_{p}=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}|x_{i}|

when
p=\infty
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
p=1
or
\infty
. If
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
is real analytic,
p
-norm nonexpansive, and
\operatorname{Fix}(f)
is nonempty, then there exists
q\in\mathbb{N}
such that:

(1)

for each
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the sequence
(f^{kq}(x))_{k}
is convergent;

(2)

the limit of
(f^{kq}(x))_{k}
is a periodic point of
f
with a minimal period that divides
q
;

(3)

there exists a permutation
\pi
on
n
letters such that
q
is either the order of
\pi
or twice the order of
\pi
.

The conclusions of Theorem
3.1
are known to hold for
1<p<\infty,p\neq 2
without requiring
f
to be real analytic. This is a result of Sine
[
16
, Theorem 3]
, see also
[
7
]
. Lemmens and van Gaans have shown that the conclusions of Theorem
3.1
are also true for linear nonexpansive maps when
p=1
or
\infty

[
6
, Theorem 2.1]
. We will prove Theorem
3.1
by using Theorem
2.1
to reduce to the linear case where we can apply the result of Lemmens and van Gaans.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

For every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, there is a periodic point
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with minimal period
q_{x}
such that
\xi=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}f^{kq_{x}}(x)
by Proposition
1.2
. Moreover, there is an upper bound on
q_{x}
which depends only on
n
and the norm. Let
q
be the least common multiple of the minimal periods
q_{x}
taken over all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then assertions
1
and
2
follow. Furthermore, every periodic point of
f
is contained in
\operatorname{Fix}(f^{q})
.

We may assume without loss of generality that
f(0)=0
. Note that compositions of real analytic maps are real analytic (see e.g.,
[
17
]
), so in particular
f^{q}
is real analytic. Then by the proof of Theorem
2.1
, there is a nonexpansive retract
R
from
\mathbb{R}^{n}
onto
\operatorname{Fix}(f^{q})
and a nonexpansive linear map
A
on
V(f^{q})
with range
W
such that
R
is an isometry from
W
onto
\operatorname{Fix}(f^{q})
and
A
is the inverse isometry from
\operatorname{Fix}(f^{q})
onto
W
. Since
f
is a surjective isometry on
\operatorname{Fix}(f^{q})
, it follows that
A\circ f\circ R
is a surjective isometry on
W
. By the Mazur–Ulam theorem (see e.g.,
[
11
]
),
A\circ f\circ R
is a linear map on
W
. Since the action of
f
on
\operatorname{Fix}(f^{q})
is isometric to the action of
A\circ f\circ R
on
W
, it follows that
q
is the least common multiple of the minimal periods of the periodic points of
A\circ f\circ R
.

One delicate issue remains. In order to reduce to the linear case where the desired bound on the period
q
is known, we need
A\circ f\circ R
to extend to a nonexpansive linear map on all of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We will give separate proofs for
p=1
and
p=\infty
that there are nonexpansive linear projections
P
from
\mathbb{R}^{n}
onto
V(f^{q})
. Since
A
is a nonexpansive linear projection from
V(f^{q})
onto
W
by Remark
2.7
, it will follow that

A\circ f\circ R\circ A\circ P

is a nonexpansive linear map on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
which is identical to
A\circ f\circ R
on
W
. Conclusion
3
will then follow from
[
6
, Theorem 2.1]
.

(
p=1
case). Let
X
be
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with the 1-norm. Consider any minimal locked set
E
for
f^{q}
. By Lemma
2.3
,
L_{E}
is the linear span of the face
F_{E}
of the unit ball
B_{X}
. Since
B_{X}
is a polytope, the extreme points of
F_{E}
are all extreme points of
B_{X}

[
1
, Theorem 7.3]
. In particular,
L_{E}
is the span of the extreme points of
B_{X}
that are contained in
F_{E}
. Since the extreme points of
B_{X}
are the vectors
\pm e_{i}
,
i\in[n]
, it follows that
L_{E}
is the set of vectors with support in a subset
I_{E}\subseteq[n]
, that is,

L_{E}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x_{i}=0\text{ for all }i\notin I_{E}\}.

Since
V(f^{q})
is the intersection of the subspaces
L_{E}
over all minimal locked sets
E
for
f^{q}
, it follows that
V(f^{q})=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x_{i}=0\text{ for all }i\notin I\}
where
I
is the intersection of the sets
I_{E}
taken over every minimal locked set
E
.

Let
P
be the linear projection

(Px)_{i}=\begin{cases}x_{i}&\text{ if }i\in I,\\
0&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

It is clear that
P
is a 1-norm nonexpansive map from
\mathbb{R}^{n}
onto
V(f^{q})
, which completes the proof when
p=1
.

(
p=\infty
case). Let
X
be
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with the
\infty
-norm. We can partition
[n]
into a union of disjoint sets
I_{1},I_{2},\ldots,I_{m}
where each
I_{k}
is a maximal set such that
|x_{i}|=|x_{j}|
for all
x\in V(f^{q})
and
i,j\in I_{k}
. Note that at most one
I_{k}
can have
x_{i}=0
for all
x\in V(f^{q})
and
i\in I_{k}
. For every other
I_{k}
, there exists
x\in V(f^{q})
such that
|x_{i}|=1
for all
i\in I_{k}
.

Consider any minimal locked set
E
for
f^{q}
. Since
E
is an face of
B_{X^{*}}
, the extreme points of
E
are extreme points of
B_{X^{*}}

[
1
, Theorem 7.3]
. The extreme points of
B_{X^{*}}
are
\pm e_{i}
,
i\in[n]
. Let

\operatorname{supp}(E)=\{i\in[n]:e_{i}\in E\text{ or }-e_{i}\in E\}

be the
support
of
E
. Note that
|x_{i}|=|x_{j}|
for every
x\in L_{E}
and
i,j\in\operatorname{supp}(E)
. Since
V(f^{q})\subseteq L_{E}
, it follows that
\operatorname{supp}(E)
must be a subset of one of the maximal sets
I_{k}
in our partition, and it is disjoint from all the other sets in the partition.

For each
I_{k}
, choose
x_{I_{k}}\in V(f)
such that
(x_{I_{k}})_{i}=1
for some

, if any such

exists, otherwise let
x_{I_{k}}=0
. Construct
v_{I_{k}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

(v_{I_{k}})_{i}=\begin{cases}(x_{I_{k}})_{i}&\text{ if }i\in I_{k},\\
0&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Observe that
\phi(v_{I_{k}})=\phi(x_{I_{k}})
for every

. In addition, if

, and therefore

.

Let

Then

\infty
-norm nonexpansive projection from

. This completes the proof for

∎

The maximum order of a permutation on
n
-elements is Landau’s function
g(n)
. It is well known that

which is less than
2^{n-1}
for all

. Therefore Theorem
3.1
confirms that the periods of any periodic orbit of a real analytic
\infty
-norm nonexpansive map on

is strictly less than

.

4.
Open Questions

Theorem
2.1
guarantees that the fixed point set of a real analytic polyhedral norm nonexpansive map is a Lipschitz manifold. In every example that we are aware of, the fixed point set is also a differentiable manifold. So it seems natural to ask the following.

Question 4.1
.

Suppose that
X
is a polyhedral normed space,
f:X\rightarrow X
is nonexpansive and real analytic, and
\operatorname{Fix}(f)
is nonempty. Is
\operatorname{Fix}(f)
always a differentiable manifold?

The following stronger assertion would resolve the previous question and lead to simpler proofs of both Theorems
2.1
and Theorem
3.1
.

Question 4.2
.

Suppose that
X
is a polyhedral normed space,
f:X\rightarrow X
is nonexpansive and real analytic, and
\operatorname{Fix}(f)
is nonempty. Is there a differentiable nonexpansive retract from
X
onto
\operatorname{Fix}(f)
?

Finally we ask whether Theorem
2.1
is true without assuming that the norm is either polyhedral or strictly convex?

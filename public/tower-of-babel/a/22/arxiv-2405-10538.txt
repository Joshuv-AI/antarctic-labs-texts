Abstract
. The theory of uniform approximation of real numbers motivates the study of products of consecutive partial quotients in regular continued fractions. 1.
Introduction

Regular continued fractions are a representation system of real numbers with outstanding approximation properties.
It is well-known that, for each
x\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Q}
there is a single sequence of integers
(a_{j}(x))_{j\geq 0}
with
a_{j}(x)\geq 1
for
j\geq 1
such that

x=a_{0}(x)+\cfrac{1}{a_{1}(x)+\cfrac{1}{a_{2}(x)+\cfrac{1}{\ddots}}}=[a_{0}(x);a_{1}(x),a_{2}(x),\ldots].

The numbers
a_{n}(x)
are the
partial quotients
of
x
.
A necessary and sufficient condition for
x\in[0,1)
is
a_{0}(x)=0
; in this case, we denote the continued fraction of
x
by
[a_{1}(x),a_{2}(x),\ldots]
.
A classical result (see, for example,
[
15
, Equation (34)]
) says that the
n
th
convergent
of
x
, given by

\frac{p_{n}(x)}{q_{n}(x)}=[a_{1}(x),\ldots,a_{n}(x)]=\cfrac{1}{a_{1}(x)+\cfrac{1}{\ddots+\cfrac{1}{a_{n}(x)}}},

where
q_{n}(x)\geq 1
and
p_{n}(x)
are coprime integers, satisfies

\frac{1}{(a_{n+1}(x)+2)q_{n}^{2}(x)}<\left|x-\frac{p_{n}(x)}{q_{n}(x)}\right|<\frac{1}{a_{n+1}(x)q_{n}^{2}(x)}.

Then, since the convergents of
x
are its best rational approximations, the rate at which rationals approximate
x
is closely related to the size of its partial quotients.
This leads us to consider, for any function
\varphi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, the set

\mathcal{E}_{1}(\varphi):=\{x\in[0,1):a_{n}(x)\geq\varphi(n)\text{ for i.m. }n\in\mathbb{N}\}.

Here and in what follows, “i.m.” stands for “infinitely many,” also,
\mathfrak{m}
stands for the Lebesgue measure on
[0,1]
.
The Borel-Bernstein Theorem establishes that

\mathfrak{m}\left(\mathcal{E}_{1}(\varphi)\right)=\begin{cases}0,\text{ if }\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{\varphi(n)}<\infty,\\
1,\text{ if }\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{\varphi(n)}=\infty,\\
\end{cases}

(see, for example,
[
1
, Theorem 1.11]
).
Łuczak
[
19
]
and Feng et al.
[
7
]
calculated the Hausdorff dimension, denoted as
\dim_{\mathrm{H}}
, of
\mathcal{E}_{1}(\varphi)
when
\varphi(n)=c^{b^{n}}
for some fixed
c,b>1
(see Lemma
2.7
).
In
[
26
]
, Wang and Wu computed
\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\mathcal{E}_{1}(\varphi)
for a general
\varphi
(Theorem
1.2
below with
\ell=1
).
While studying improvements to the classical Dirichlet theorem, Kleinbock and Wadleigh
[
17
]
showed that the set of Dirichlet non-improvable numbers is related to the set

\mathcal{E}_{2}(\varphi):=\{x\in[0,1):a_{n}(x)a_{n+1}(x)\geq\varphi(n)\text{ for i.m. }n\in\mathbb{N}\}

and computed
\mathfrak{m}(\mathcal{E}_{2}(\varphi))
. In
[
12
]
, Huang, Wu, and Xu extended this result to blocks of arbitrary length; namely, for
\varphi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, they obtained the Lebesgue measure and Hausdorff dimension of

\mathcal{E}_{\ell}(\varphi):=\{x\in[0,1):a_{n}(x)\cdots a_{n+\ell-1}(x)\geq\varphi(n)\text{ for i.m. }n\in\mathbb{N}\}.

Theorem 1.1

(Huang, Wu, Xu
[
12
]
)
.

If
\varphi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 2}
and
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, then

\mathfrak{m}\left(\mathcal{E}_{\ell}(\varphi)\right)=\begin{cases}0,&\text{\rm if }\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\log^{\ell-1}\varphi(n)}{\varphi(n)}<\infty,\\
1,&\text{\rm if }\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\log^{\ell-1}\varphi(n)}{\varphi(n)}=\infty.\end{cases}

To state the Hausdorff dimension result, for any
\varphi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 2}
, let
B_{\varphi},b_{\varphi}\in[1,\infty]
be determined by

(1)

\log B_{\varphi}:=\liminf_{n\xrightarrow{}\infty}\frac{\log\varphi(n)}{n}\;\text{ and }\;\log b_{\varphi}=\liminf_{n\xrightarrow{}\infty}\frac{\log\log\varphi(n)}{n}.

For the rest of the paper,
T
stands for the Gauss map (see Subsection
2.3
).
The pressure functions
P(T,\cdot)
appearing in Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.10
are defined in Subsection
2.4
.

Theorem 1.2

(Huang, Wu, Xu
[
12
]
)
.

Let
(f_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be the sequence of real functions on
[0,1]
given by

f_{1}(s)=s,\;\text{ and }\;f_{n+1}=\frac{sf_{n}(s)}{1-s+f_{n}(s)}\;\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N}.

For any
\varphi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 2}
and
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, then the Hausdorff dimension of
\mathcal{E}_{\ell}(\varphi)
is

\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\mathcal{E}_{\ell}(\varphi)=\begin{cases}1,&\text{\rm if }B_{\varphi}=1,\\
\inf\{s\geq 0:P(T,-f_{\ell}(s)\log B_{\varphi}-s\log|T^{\prime}|)\leq 0\},&\text{\rm if }1<B_{\varphi}<\infty,\\
\frac{1}{1+b_{\varphi}},&\text{\rm if }B_{\varphi}=\infty.\end{cases}

Let us adopt a probabilistic perspective and interpret partial quotients as random variables on
[0,1)
; for our purposes, their definition on rational numbers is irrelevant.
These random variables are not independent; hence, the Borel-Bernstein Theorem is not a trivial consequence of the Borel-Cantelli Lemma (Lemma
2.1
).
Moreover, the Borel-Bernstein theorem tells us that the inequality
a_{n}(x)\geq n\log n
holds infinitely often for almost every number
x
with respect to
\mathfrak{m}
. This implies that, if
S_{n}(x):=a_{1}(x)+\cdots+a_{n}(x)
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}S_{n}(x)=\infty\;\text{ for almost all }x\in[0,1).

Despite not being independent and having infinite expected value, i.e.,
\int a_{n}\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}=\infty
for all
n
, the partial quotients satisfy a weak law of large numbers: Khinchin
[
16
, §. 4]
proved that
S_{n}/(n\log n)
converges in Lebesgue measure to
\frac{1}{\log 2}
as
n\to\infty
. Furthermore, Philipp
[
21
, Theorem 1]
proved that there is no reasonable function
\sigma
for which
S_{n}/\sigma(n)
converges almost everywhere to a finite positive constant. Large partial quotients are to blame for this phenomenon; in fact, Diamond and Vaaler
[
4
, Corollary 1]
showed that convergence almost everywhere does hold for Khinchin’s averaging function when the largest partial quotient is omitted; that is,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n\log n}\left(S_{n}(x)-\max_{1\leq j\leq n}a_{j}(x)\right)=\frac{1}{\log 2}\;\text{ for $\mathfrak{m}$-almost all }x\in[0,1).

This result was extended by Hu, Hussain, and Yu for sums of products of two partial quotients
[
11
, Theorem 1.5]
. They showed that almost every
x\in[0,1)
satisfies

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n\log^{2}n}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}(x)a_{j+1}(x)-\max_{1\leq j\leq n}a_{j}(x)a_{j+1}(x)\right)=\frac{1}{2\log 2}.

A key aspect in the proofs of (
2
) and (
3
), respectively, is showing that, for
c>\frac{1}{2}
, we have

(2)

\mathfrak{m}\left(\left\{x\in[0,1):\begin{matrix}a_{j}(x)\geq n(\log n)^{c},\\
a_{k}(x)\geq n(\log n)^{c},\end{matrix}\;\text{ for some }1\leq j<k\leq n\text{ for i.m. }n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}\right)=0

and, for
d>\frac{3}{2}
,

(3)

\mathfrak{m}\left(\left\{x\in[0,1):\begin{matrix}a_{j}(x)a_{j+1}(x)\geq n(\log n)^{d},\\
a_{k}(x)a_{k+1}(x)\geq n(\log n)^{d},\\
\end{matrix}\;\text{ for some }1\leq j<k\leq n\text{ for i.m. }n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}\right)=0.

Recently, Tan, Tian, and Wang
[
24
]
generalized (
2
) to a wider family of functions (Theorem
1.3
), and Tan and Zhou generalized (
3
) in
[
25
]
(Theorem
1.4
).
To be precise, given a non-decreasing function
\varphi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 2}
and
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, define

\mathcal{F}_{\ell}(\varphi):=\left\{x\in[0,1):\begin{matrix}a_{j}(x)\cdots a_{j+\ell-1}(x)\geq\varphi(n),\\
a_{k}(x)\cdots a_{k+\ell-1}(x)\geq\varphi(n),\end{matrix}\;\text{ for some }1\leq j<k\leq n\text{ for i.m. }n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}.

The assumption
\varphi(n)\geq 2
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
is not restrictive at all, since
\mathcal{F}_{\ell}(\varphi)=[0,1)\setminus\mathbb{Q}
whenever
\varphi(n)\leq 1
for infinitely many
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Theorem 1.3

(Tan, Tian, Wang
[
24
]
)
.

If
\varphi:\mathbb{N}\to[2,\infty)
is non-decreasing, then

\mathfrak{m}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(\varphi))=\begin{cases}0,&\text{\rm if }\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{n}{\varphi(n)^{2}}<\infty,\\
1,&\text{\rm if }\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{n}{\varphi(n)^{2}}=\infty.\end{cases}

Theorem 1.4

(Tan, Zhou,
[
25
]
)
.

If
\varphi:\mathbb{N}\to[2,\infty)
is non-decreasing, then

\mathfrak{m}(\mathcal{F}_{2}(\varphi))=\begin{cases}0,&\text{\rm if }\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}\left(\frac{n\log^{2}\varphi(n)}{\varphi^{2}(n)}+\frac{1}{\varphi(n)}\right)<\infty,\\
1,&\text{\rm if }\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}\left(\frac{n\log^{2}\varphi(n)}{\varphi^{2}(n)}+\frac{1}{\varphi(n)}\right)=\infty.\end{cases}

Our first result gives the Lebesgue measure of
\mathcal{F}_{\ell}(\varphi)
for any
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2}
.

Theorem 1.5
.

If
\varphi:\mathbb{N}\to[2,\infty)
is non-decreasing and
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2}
, then

\mathfrak{m}(\mathcal{F}_{\ell}(\varphi))=\begin{cases}0&\text{\rm if }\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}\left(\frac{n\log^{2(\ell-1)}\varphi(n)}{\varphi^{2}(n)}+\frac{\log^{\ell-2}\varphi(n)}{\varphi(n)}\right)<\infty,\\
1&\text{\rm if }\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}\left(\frac{n\log^{2(\ell-1)}\varphi(n)}{\varphi^{2}(n)}+\frac{\log^{\ell-2}\varphi(n)}{\varphi(n)}\right)=\infty.\end{cases}

.

The lengths of such overlapping blocks range from

, whereas in Theorem

Remark 1.7
.

It is well-known that the measure
\mu
given by
\,\mathrm{d}\mu=\frac{1}{\log 2}\frac{1}{1+x}\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}
is
T

[

).
Then, Birkhoff’s Ergodic Theorem yields

(see, for example,
[

).
Moreover, by Theorem

, for almost every

there are infinitely many numbers

thus, for such

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}(x)\cdots a_{j+\ell-1}(x)-\max_{1\leq k\leq n}a_{k}(x)\cdots a_{k+\ell-1}(x)\right)=\infty.

.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall some definitions and elementary results on measure theory, Hausdorff dimension, continued fractions, and pressure functions.

2.1.
Full and null sets

Recall that the limsup set of a sequence
(E_{n})_{n\geq 1}
of subsets of a given set
X
is

\limsup_{n\to\infty}E_{n}=\bigcap_{n\geq 1}\bigcup_{m\geq n}E_{m}.

Theorem 2.1

(Borel-Cantelli Lemma)
.

Let
(X,\mathscr{A},\mu)
be a probability space and
(E_{k})_{k\geq 1}
a sequence of measurable sets.

i.

If
\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}\mu(E_{n})<\infty
, then
\mu(\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}E_{n})=0
.

ii.

If the sets
E_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, are independent and
\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}\mu(E_{n})=\infty
, then
\mu(\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}E_{n})=1
.

Proof.

See, for example,
[
14
, Lemma 4.18]
.
∎

The independence assumption is rather restrictive; it will not hold in our context.
A way to circumvent this problem is to show the positive measure of the limsup set and that the measure is either full or null by some other means.
We obtain the full measure statement in Theorem
1.5
by showing that our limsup set intersects a large portion of every interval and applying Knopp’s Lemma (Lemma
2.2
).

Lemma 2.2

(Knopp’s Lemma)
.

Let
A\subseteq[0,1)
be a Borel set and
\mathcal{C}
a class of subintervals of
[0,1)
satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

Every open subinterval of
[0,1)
is an at most countable union of disjoint elements from
\mathcal{C}
,

(2)

There is a constant
c>0
such that for any
B\in\mathcal{C}
we have
\mathfrak{m}(A\cap B)\geq c\mathfrak{m}(B)
.

Then,
\mathfrak{m}(A)=1
.

Proof.

See, for example,
[
3
, Lemma 2.3.1]

∎

To apply Lemma
2.2
, we need the following result due to Chung and Erdős
[
2
]
(see also
[
23
, Lemma 6]
for the formulation below).

Lemma 2.3

(Chung, Erdős,
[
2
]
)
.

Let
(X,\mathscr{A},\mu)
be a probability space and
(E_{k})_{k\geq 1}
a sequence of measurable sets.
If
\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}\mu(E_{n})=\infty
, then

\mu\left(\limsup_{n\to\infty}E_{n}\right)\geq\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{\left(\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq n\leq N}\mu(E_{n})\right)^{2}}{\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq N}\mu(E_{i}\cap E_{j})}.

2.2.
Hausdorff dimension

We recall the definition of the Hausdorff dimension and establish some notation.
For the general theory of the subject, see
[
6
]
.
Given a bounded subset
A
of
\mathbb{R}
, let
|A|
be its diameter, that is,

|A|=\begin{cases}\sup\{|a-b|:a,b\in A\},&\text{ if }A\neq\varnothing,\\
0,&\text{ if }A=\varnothing.\end{cases}

For any
\delta>0
, a
\delta
-cover
of
A
is a countable collection
\mathfrak{B}
of bounded subsets of real numbers such that

\sup_{B\in\mathfrak{B}}|B|\leq\delta\quad\text{ and }\quad A\subseteq\bigcup_{B\in\mathfrak{B}}B.

In this case, for
s>0
, we define

\Lambda_{s}(\mathfrak{B})=\sum_{B\in\mathfrak{B}}|B|^{s}

and

\mathcal{H}^{s}_{\delta}(A)=\inf\left\{\Lambda_{s}(\mathfrak{B}):\mathfrak{B}\text{ is a }\delta-\text{cover of }A\right\}.

The
s
-Hausdorff measure

\mathcal{H}^{s}
of
A
, denoted by
\mathcal{H}^{s}(A)
, is

\mathcal{H}^{s}(A):=\lim_{\delta\to 0}\mathcal{H}^{s}_{\delta}(A).

It is well-known that there is a number, called the
Hausdorff dimension of
A
and denoted by
\dim_{\mathrm{H}}A
, such that

\mathcal{H}^{s}(A):=\begin{cases}\infty,&\text{if}\ \ s<\dim_{\mathrm{H}}A,\\
0,&\text{if}\ \ s>\dim_{\mathrm{H}}A.\end{cases}

2.3.
Continued fractions

For each real number
t
, let
[t]
be its integer part.
Regular continued fractions can be generated with the Gauss map
T:[0,1)\to[0,1)
given by

T(x)=\begin{cases}x^{-1}-[x^{-1}],&x\neq 0,\\
0,&x=0.\end{cases}

For any
x\in[0,1)\setminus\mathbb{Q}
, let
a_{1}:[0,1)\setminus\mathbb{Q}\to\mathbb{N}
be given by
a_{1}(x)=[x^{-1}]
and define
a_{n}(x)=a_{1}(T^{n-1}(x))
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
As in the introduction, the natural numbers
(a_{n}(x))_{n\geq 1}
are the
partial quotients
of
x
.
This is the only sequence of natural numbers satisfying

x=[a_{1}(x),a_{2}(x),\ldots]=\lim_{n\to\infty}[a_{1}(x),a_{2}(x),\ldots,a_{n}].

For any non-negative integer
n
, the
n
th
convergent
of
x
is

\frac{p_{n}(x)}{q_{n}(x)}:=[a_{1}(x),\ldots,a_{n}(x)].

An elementary result (see, for example,
[
10
, Proposition 1.1]
) states that the sequences
(p_{n}(x))_{n\geq 0}
,
(q_{n}(x))_{n\geq 1}
are given by

q_{0}(x)=1,\quad q_{1}(x)=a_{1}(x),\quad p_{0}(x)=0,\quad p_{1}(x)=1,

and

p_{n+1}(x)=a_{n+1}(x)p_{n}(x)+p_{n-1}(x),\quad q_{n+1}(x)=a_{n+1}(x)q_{n}(x)+q_{n-1}(x)\;\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N}.

The terms of the sequence
(q_{n}(x))_{n\geq 1}
are called
continuants
.
Clearly, the
n
th continuant depends only on the first
n
partial quotients of
x
. Therefore, any other number
y
whose first
n
partial quotients are exactly those of
x
has the same first
n
continuants as
x
.

2.4.
Pressure functions

Pressure functions have become popular in Diophantine approximation because their zeros are the Hausdorff dimension of certain sets determined by dynamical means. In this paper, we restrict ourselves to the dynamical system
([0,1)\setminus\mathbb{Q},T)
.
For the general theory of pressure functions, see the monograph by Mauldin and Urbański
[
20
]
.

Given a function
\psi:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
, for any
x\in[0,1]
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we let
S_{n}(\psi;x)
be the ergodic sum

S_{n}(\psi;x):=\psi(x)+\psi(T(x))+\ldots+\psi\left(T^{n-1}(x)\right).

The
n
th variation of
\psi
is given by

\Var_{n}(\psi):=\sup\left\{|\psi(x)-\psi(y)|:I_{n}(x)=I_{n}(y)\right\}.

For any non-empty subset
A\subseteq\mathbb{N}
, we consider

X_{A}:=\left\{x\in[0,1):a_{n}(x)\in A\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}.

The pressure function
P_{A}(T,\cdot)
on
\psi
is given by

P_{A}(T,\psi):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\left(\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in A^{n}}\sup_{x\in I_{n}(\mathbf{a})\cap X_{A}}\exp\left(S_{n}(\psi,x)\right)\right).

In this context, we call
\psi
a
potential
.
When
A=\mathbb{N}
, we write
P(T,\psi)
instead of
P_{\mathbb{N}}(T,\psi)
. The existence of
P_{A}(T,\psi)
is not immediate, but it does exist under some regularity conditions. Furthermore, we can approximate
P(T,\psi)
by
P_{A}(T,\psi)
with
A\subseteq\mathbb{N}
finite.

Lemma 2.9

(
[
18
, Proposition 2.4]
)
.

Let
\psi:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
be such that
\Var_{1}(\psi)<\infty
and
\Var_{n}(\psi)\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. Then,
P_{A}(T,\psi)
is well defined.
Moreover, the supremum in the definition of
P_{A}(T,\psi)
may be replaced with
\exp(S_{n}(\psi;x))
for any
x\in I_{n}(\mathbf{a})\cap X_{A}
.

Lemma 2.10

(
[
8
, Proposition 2]
)
.

If
\psi:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
is such that
\Var_{1}(\psi)<\infty
and
\Var_{n}(\psi)\to 0
as
n\to\infty
, then

P(T,\psi)=\sup\left\{P_{A}(T,\psi):A\subseteq\mathbb{N}\;\text{\rm is finite}\right\}.

For any continuous function
f:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
and
0<s<1
, consider the potential

\psi_{s}(x):=-f(s)\log B-s\log|T^{\prime}(x)|.

Then,
\psi_{s}
satisfies the hypotheses of lemmas
2.9
and
2.10
. Since for every
x\in[0,1)
with
n
partial quotients we have

(T^{n})^{\prime}(x)=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{(xq_{n-1}-p_{n-1})^{2}},

the pressure function
P(T,\cdot)
evaluated on
\psi_{s}
becomes

P(T,\psi_{s})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\left(\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in A^{n}}\frac{1}{B^{nf(s)}q_{n}^{2s}(\mathbf{a})}\right).

In this paper, we work with potentials
\psi_{s}
given by

\psi_{s}(x)=-g(s)\log B-s\log|T^{\prime}(x)|,

where
B>1
is a given real number,
0<s<1
, and
g
is as in Theorem
1.10
.
Define

s_{B}:=\inf\{s\geq 0:P(T,-g(s)\log B-s\log|T^{\prime}(x)|)\leq 0\}

and, for
m\in\mathbb{N}
,

(4)

s_{m,B}:=\inf\left\{s\geq 0:\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{N}^{m}}\left(\frac{1}{q_{m}^{2s}(\mathbf{a})B^{mg(s)}}\right)\leq 1\right\}.

As in
[
26
, Section 2]
, we may show that

(5)

\lim_{m\to\infty}s_{m,B}=s_{B}.

The lower estimate for the Hausdorff dimension of sets resembling that in Theorem
1.10
tends to be computationally demanding.
A general strategy is to construct a Cantor set contained in the set of interest, define a probability measure on it, and apply the Mass Distribution Principle
[
6
, Proposition 4.2]
.
Recently, Hussain and Shulga
[
13
]
computed the Hausdorff dimension of a large family of sets defined by continued fractions restrictions.
For
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
and
A_{0},\ldots,A_{\ell-1}>1
, write

(6)

S_{\ell}(A_{0},\ldots,A_{\ell-1}):=\left\{x\in[0,1)\setminus\mathbb{Q}:\begin{matrix}A_{0}^{n}\leq a_{n}(x)\leq 2A_{0}^{n},\ldots,A_{\ell-1}^{n}\leq a_{n+\ell-1}(x)\leq 2A_{\ell-1}^{n}\\
\text{ for infinitely many }n\in\mathbb{N}\end{matrix}\right\}.

Put
\beta_{-1}=1
and
\beta_{i}=A_{i}\beta_{i-1}
for
i\in\{0,\ldots,\ell-1\}
and, for such
i
, put

d_{i}:=\inf\left\{s\geq 0:P(T,-s\log|T^{\prime}|-s\log\beta_{i}+(1-s)\log\beta_{i-1})\leq 0\right\}.

Lemma 2.11

(
[
13
, Theorem 1.1]
)
.

\dim_{\mathrm{H}}S_{\ell}(A_{0},\ldots,A_{\ell-1})=\displaystyle\min_{0\leq i\leq\ell-1}d_{i}
.

Lemma 3.1

Lemma 3.2

(Dirichlet-Piltz formula)
.

Take
k\in\mathbb{N}
. For each
x\in\mathbb{N}
, let
d_{k}(x)
denote the number of ways we can express

as a product of
k
natural numbers. For every

There exist a polynomial
P_{k}
of degree

and a constant

such that for any
\varepsilon>0
we have

D_{k}(M)=MP_{k}(\log M)+O(M^{\alpha_{k}+\varepsilon})\;\text{ as }\;M\to\infty.

Proof.

See, for example,
[
9
, Section 4]

∎

Clearly, for any
\alpha>0
we have

(8)

\displaystyle\int\frac{\log^{n}(\alpha t)}{t}\,\mathrm{d}t=\frac{\log^{n+1}(\alpha t)}{n+1}+C\quad\text{for all }n\in\mathbb{N},

(9)

\displaystyle\int\log(\alpha t)\,\mathrm{d}t=t\log(\alpha t)-t+C.

Lemma 3.3
.

For
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
and
M>1
, we have

(10)

\sum_{a_{1}\cdots a_{\ell}\leq M}\frac{1}{a_{1}\cdots a_{\ell}}\asymp_{\ell}\log^{\ell}(M),

(11)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1}\cdots a_{\ell}<M\\
a_{2}\cdots a_{\ell+1}\geq M\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{a_{1}^{2}\cdots a_{\ell}^{2}a_{\ell+1}^{2}}\asymp_{\ell}\frac{\log^{\ell-1}M}{M}.

Proof.

The estimate (
10
) is clear when
\ell=1
.
If it holds for some
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, then, by (
8
),

\sum_{a_{1}\cdots a_{\ell}a_{\ell+1}\leq M}\frac{1}{a_{1}\cdots a_{\ell}a_{\ell+1}}=\sum_{a_{\ell+1}=1}^{M}\frac{1}{a_{\ell+1}}\sum_{a_{1}\cdots a_{\ell}\leq\frac{M}{a_{\ell+1}}}\frac{1}{a_{1}\cdots a_{\ell}}\asymp\sum_{a_{\ell+1}=1}^{M}\frac{1}{a_{\ell+1}}\log^{\ell}\left(\frac{M}{a_{\ell+1}}\right)\asymp\log^{\ell+1}M.

For (
11
), note that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1}\cdots a_{\ell}<M\\
a_{2}\cdots a_{\ell+1}\geq M\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{a_{1}^{2}\cdots a_{\ell+1}^{2}}=\sum_{a_{2}\cdots a_{\ell}\leq M}\sum_{a_{1}\leq\frac{M}{a_{2}\ldots a_{\ell}}}\sum_{a_{\ell+1}\geq\frac{M}{a_{2}\cdots a_{\ell}}}\frac{1}{a_{1}^{2}\cdots a_{\ell+1}^{2}}.

We split the series to the right by conditioning on whether
1\leq a_{2}\ldots a_{\ell}\leq\frac{M}{2}
or
\frac{M}{2}<a_{2}\cdots a_{\ell}<M
.
In the first case, since super-harmonic series are convergent, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{a_{2}\cdots a_{\ell}\leq\frac{M}{2}}\sum_{a_{1}\leq\frac{M}{a_{2}\ldots a_{\ell}}}\sum_{a_{\ell+1}\geq\frac{M}{a_{2}\cdots a_{\ell}}}\frac{1}{a_{1}^{2}\cdots a_{\ell+1}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{a_{2}\cdots a_{\ell}\leq\frac{M}{2}}\frac{1}{a_{2}^{2}\cdots a_{\ell}^{2}}\sum_{a_{1}\leq\frac{M}{a_{2}\ldots a_{\ell+1}}}\frac{1}{a_{1}^{2}}\sum_{a_{\ell+1}\geq\frac{M}{a_{2}\cdots a_{\ell}}}\frac{1}{a_{\ell+1}^{2}}

\displaystyle\asymp\sum_{a_{2}\cdots a_{\ell}\leq\frac{M}{2}}\frac{1}{a_{2}^{2}\cdots a_{\ell}^{2}}\left(\sum_{a_{\ell+1}\geq\frac{M}{a_{2}\cdots a_{\ell}}}\frac{1}{a_{\ell+1}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\asymp\frac{1}{M}\sum_{a_{2}\cdots a_{\ell}\leq\frac{M}{2}}\frac{1}{a_{2}\cdots a_{\ell}}

\displaystyle\asymp\frac{\log^{\ell-1}M}{M}

\displaystyle\text{(by \eqref{Eq:Series:03})}.

When
\frac{M}{2}<a_{2}\cdots a_{\ell}<M
, the condition
a_{1}\leq\frac{M}{a_{2}\ldots a_{\ell}}
reduces to
a_{1}=1
and
a_{\ell+1}\geq\frac{M}{a_{2}\cdots a_{\ell}}
becomes
a_{\ell+1}\geq 2
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{\frac{M}{2}\leq a_{2}\cdots a_{\ell}\leq M}\sum_{a_{1}\leq\frac{M}{a_{2}\ldots a_{\ell}}}\sum_{a_{\ell+1}\geq\frac{M}{a_{1}\cdots a_{\ell}}}\frac{1}{a_{1}^{2}\cdots a_{\ell+1}^{2}}

\displaystyle\asymp\sum_{\frac{M}{2}\leq a_{2}\cdots a_{\ell}\leq M}\frac{1}{a_{2}^{2}\cdots a_{\ell}^{2}}

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{\frac{M}{2}\leq a_{2}\cdots a_{\ell}}\frac{1}{a_{2}^{2}\cdots a_{\ell}^{2}}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{\log^{\ell-2}M}{M}.

\displaystyle\text{(by Lemma \ref{Lem:HWX})}.

This proves (
11
).
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

P_{r,j},Q_{r,j}\in\mathbb{R}[X]

and

An immediate consequence of Lemma

is the next corollary.

Corollary 3.6
.

For each
r,j\in\mathbb{N}
,

\frac{1}{M^{2}}\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq x_{1}\cdots x_{j}\leq M\\
1\leq x_{1},\ldots 1\leq x_{j}\end{subarray}}\log^{2(r-1)}\left(\frac{x_{1}\cdots x_{j}}{M}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\asymp_{r,j}\frac{\log^{j-1}(M)}{M}\quad\text{ as }M\to\infty.

Lemmas
3.7
and
3.8
below are intermediate steps toward the proof of Lemma

Lemma 3.7
.

For each
j\in\mathbb{N}
, there is a polynomial
R_{j}\in\mathbb{R}[X]
satisfying
R_{1}=0
and
\deg R_{j}=j-2
for
j\geq 2
such that

\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq x_{1}\cdots x_{j}\leq M\\
1\leq x_{1},\ldots,1\leq x_{j}\end{subarray}}1\,\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}=\frac{1}{(j-1)!}M\log^{j-1}M+MR_{j}(\log M)+(-1)^{j}.

Proof.

The case
j=1
is trivial.
For
j=2
, we have

\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq x_{1}x_{2}\leq M\\
1\leq x_{1},1\leq x_{2}\end{subarray}}1\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}=\int_{1}^{M}\left(\int_{1}^{\frac{M}{x_{2}}}1\,\mathrm{d}x_{1}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x_{2}=\int_{1}^{M}\frac{M}{x_{2}}-1\,\mathrm{d}x_{2}=M\log M-M+1,

so
R_{2}(X)=-1
.
Assume the result holds for some
j\in\mathbb{N}
; then,

\displaystyle\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq x_{1}\cdots x_{j}x_{j+1}\leq M\\
1\leq x_{1},\ldots,1\leq x_{j+1}\end{subarray}}1\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle=\int_{1}^{M}\left(\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq x_{1}\cdots x_{j}\leq\frac{M}{x_{j+1}}\\
1\leq x_{1},\ldots,1\leq x_{j}\end{subarray}}1\,\mathrm{d}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{j})\right)\,\mathrm{d}x_{j+1}

\displaystyle=\int_{1}^{M}\frac{1}{(j-1)!}\frac{M}{x_{j+1}}\log^{j-1}\left(\frac{M}{x_{j+1}}\right)+\frac{M}{x_{j+1}}R_{j}\left(\log\frac{M}{x_{j+1}}\right)+(-1)^{j}\,\mathrm{d}x_{j+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{j!}M\log^{j}M+MR_{j+1}(\log M)+(-1)^{j+1}\quad\quad\text{(by \eqref{Eq:IntLog:01} and \eqref{Eq:IntLog:02}).}

Finally, note that
\deg R_{j+1}=1+\deg R_{j}=(j+1)-2
.

Lemma 3.8
.

For

\tilde{R}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}[X]

In particular, for each

there is a polynomial

of degree

and leading coefficient

\int_{1}^{M}\log^{2(r-1)}\left(\frac{x_{1}}{M}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x_{1}=(2r-2)!M+\tilde{R}_{2(r-1)}(\log M).

Proof.

For the base

Note that

Assume the result holds for some

Integrating by parts,

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{M}\log^{k+1}\left(\frac{x_{1}}{M}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x_{1}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k+2}\log^{k+1}(M)-(k+1)\int_{1}^{M}\log^{k}\left(\frac{x_{1}}{M}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x_{1}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k+2}\log^{k+1}(M)+(-1)^{k+1}(k+1)!M-(k+1)\tilde{R}_{k}(\log M)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k+1}(k+1)!M+\tilde{R}_{k+1}(\log M)

and

Proof of Theorem

, because there are no actual restrictions on the natural numbers

and

For the second term, we have

Now, let’s assume that

.
We split the series into two sums according to the size of

.
When

, we have

For the case

is true or false:

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1}\cdots a_{r}b_{1}\cdots b_{j}\geq M\\

If

for some

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1}\cdots a_{r}b_{1}\cdots b_{j}\geq M\\

\displaystyle=\sum_{2\leq b_{1}\cdots b_{j}<M}\frac{1}{b_{1}^{2}\cdots b_{j}^{2}}\left(\sum_{a_{1}\cdots a_{r}\geq\frac{M}{b_{1}\cdots b_{j}}}\frac{1}{a_{1}^{1}\cdots a_{r}^{2}}\right)^{2}

(12)

\displaystyle\asymp\frac{1}{M^{2}}\sum_{2\leq b_{1}\cdots b_{j}<M}\log^{2(r-1)}\left(\frac{M}{b_{1}\cdots b_{j}}\right).

Let

such that

Then, for all

and, since

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{M^{2}}\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq x_{1},\ldots,1\leq x_{j}\\

\displaystyle\asymp_{r}\frac{\log^{j-1}(2^{j}M)}{M}

Lemma 3.11
.

For any

and

For all

\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq x,1\leq y\\
1\leq xy\leq M\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{x^{s}y^{s}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y=\frac{M^{1-s}}{1-s}\log M+\frac{1}{(1-s)^{2}}\left(1-M^{1-s}\right).

For every

Let us check that

Define

\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\mathcal{F}_{3}(\varphi_{B})=\inf\left\{s\geq 0:P(T,-s\log|T^{\prime}|-g(s)\log B)\leq 0\right\}.

5.1.
Lower bound

To compute the lower bound, we construct a family of subsets
S(x,y)
of
\mathcal{F}_{3}(\varphi_{B})
parametrized by certain pairs
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
We then maximize
(x,y)\mapsto\dim_{\mathrm{H}}S(x,y)
.
For any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
such that
0\leq x\leq 1
,
0\leq y\leq 1
, and
0\leq x+y\leq 1
, define

A_{0}(x,y)=B^{1-x-y},\quad A_{1}(x,y)=B^{x},\quad A_{2}(x,y)=B^{y},\quad A_{3}(x,y)=B^{1-x-y}.

The set

S(x,y)=S_{4}(A_{0}(x,y),A_{1}(x,y),A_{2}(x,y),A_{3}(x,y))

defined in (
6
) is, thus, contained in
\mathcal{F}_{3}(\varphi_{B})
; so
\dim_{\mathrm{H}}S(x,y)\leq\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\mathcal{F}_{3}(B)
; therefore,

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq x,y\leq 1\\
0\leq x+y\leq 1\end{subarray}}\dim_{\mathrm{H}}S(x,y)\leq\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\mathcal{F}_{3}(\varphi_{B}).

To compute this supremum, for each
\frac{1}{2}<s<1
and
(x,y)
such that
x,y
are non-negative and
x+y\leq 1
, define the potentials

\displaystyle f_{0,s}(x,y)

\displaystyle=-s(1-x-y)\log B-s\log|T^{\prime}|,

\displaystyle f_{1,s}(x,y)

\displaystyle=-s(1-y)\log B+(1-s)(1-x-y)\log B-s\log|T^{\prime}|,

\displaystyle f_{2,s}(x,y)

\displaystyle=-s\log B+(1-s)(1-y)\log B-s\log|T^{\prime}|,

\displaystyle f_{3,s}(x,y)

\displaystyle=-s(2-x-y)\log B+(1-s)\log B-s\log|T^{\prime}|,

and, for
i\in\{0,1,2,3\}
,

d_{i}(x,y)=\inf\left\{s\geq 0:P(T,f_{i,s}(x,y))\leq 0\right\}.

Then, by Lemma
2.11
, we must compute

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq x,y\leq 1\\
0\leq x+y\leq 1\end{subarray}}\min_{0\leq i\leq 3}d_{i}(x,y).

To simplify the problem, rather than
f_{j,s}
, we consider the functions

\displaystyle F_{0,s}(x,y)

\displaystyle=-s(1-x-y),

\displaystyle F_{1,s}(x,y)

\displaystyle=-s(1-y)+(1-s)(1-x-y)=-2s+1+(s-1)x+(2s-1)y,

\displaystyle F_{2,s}(x,y)

\displaystyle=-s+(1-s)(1-y)=-2s+1+(s-1)y,

\displaystyle F_{3,s}(x,y)

\displaystyle=-s(2-x-y)+(1-s)=-2s+1-s(1-x-y).

Therefore,
f_{j,s}(x,y)=F_{j,s}(x,y)\log B-s\log|T^{\prime}|
for
j\in\{0,1,2,3\}
.
Since the potentials are increasing in
F_{j,s}(x,y)
, our problem is to find

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq x,y\leq 1\\
0\leq x+y\leq 1\end{subarray}}\min\{F_{0,s}(x,y),F_{1,s}(x,y),F_{2,s}(x,y),F_{3,s}(x,y)\}.

Clearly,
F_{0,s}(x,y)\geq F_{3,s}(x,y)
for all admissible values of
(x,y)
and
\frac{1}{2}\leq s\leq 1
, so our goal is to calculate

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq x,y\leq 1\\
0\leq x+y\leq 1\end{subarray}}\min\{F_{1,s}(x,y),F_{2,s}(x,y),F_{3,s}(x,y)\}.

Long yet completely elementary computations allow us to determine the regions where each of the three functions is the minimum.
In fact, we divide the triangle in the
xy
-plane determined by
0\leq x,y\leq 1
and
0\leq x+y\leq 1
into three or two regions depending on
s
.
From this and the signs of the coefficients of
x
and
y
on each
F_{j,s}
, we conclude that the supremum is in fact a maximum and that it is attained where all the functions in the minimum coincide.
This maximum is

-\frac{3s^{3}-5s^{2}+4s-1}{s^{2}-s+1}=-g(s)

and it is achieved when

(31)

x=\frac{s^{2}}{s^{2}-s+1}\quad\text{ and }\quad y=\frac{s-s^{2}}{s^{2}-s+1}.

Therefore,

\inf\{s\geq 0:P(T,-g(s)\log B-s\log|T^{\prime}|)\leq 0\}\leq\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\mathcal{F}_{3}(\varphi_{B}).

\displaystyle\asymp\frac{1}{q_{n-3}^{2s}(\mathbf{a})B^{n(1-y_{1})(2s-1)}B^{nsy_{1}}}

\displaystyle\asymp\frac{1}{q_{n-3}^{2s}(\mathbf{a})B^{n((2s-1)+y_{1}(1-s))}}.

Using the strict growth of
g
and (
33
), we conclude
\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}V_{n}^{0}\leq s_{B}
.

We now deal with
(V_{n}^{1})_{n\geq 1}
.
For
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n-3})\in\mathbb{N}^{n-3}
,
a_{n-2}
and
a_{n-1}
such that
a_{n-2}a_{n-1}\geq B^{n(1-y_{1})}
and
a_{n}<B^{ny_{1}}
, Lemma
5.2
gives us a covering
\mathfrak{D}(\mathbf{a},a_{n-2},a_{n-1},a_{n})
of

\bigcup_{a_{n+1}a_{n+2}\geq\frac{B^{n}}{a_{n}}}I_{n+2}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n+2})

such that, for a fixed
\tilde{s}
with
s_{B}<\tilde{s}<s
,

\Lambda_{s}(\mathfrak{D}(\mathbf{a},a_{n-2},a_{n-1},a_{n}))\ll\frac{1}{q_{n-3}^{2\tilde{s}}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n-3})a_{n-2}^{2\tilde{s}}a_{n-1}^{2\tilde{s}}a_{n}^{2\tilde{s}-\tilde{s}^{2}}B^{n\tilde{s}^{2}}}.

Since
0<2\tilde{s}-\tilde{s}^{2}<1
, we have that

\displaystyle\sum_{a_{n}\leq B^{ny_{1}}}\Lambda_{s}(\mathfrak{D}(\mathbf{a},a_{n-2},a_{n-1},a_{n}))

\displaystyle\asymp\frac{1}{q_{n-3}^{2\tilde{s}}(\mathbf{a})a_{n-2}^{2\tilde{s}}a_{n-1}^{2\tilde{s}}B^{ny_{1}(2\tilde{s}-\tilde{s}^{2}-1)}B^{n\tilde{s}^{2}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q_{n-3}^{2\tilde{s}}(\mathbf{a})a_{n-2}^{2\tilde{s}}a_{n-1}^{2\tilde{s}}B^{n(-y_{1}(1-\tilde{s})^{2}+\tilde{s}^{2})}}.

Then, by Lemma
3.9
,

\displaystyle\sum_{a_{n-2}a_{n-1}\geq B^{n(1-y_{1})}}\sum_{a_{n}\leq B^{ny_{1}}}\Lambda_{s}(\mathfrak{D}(\mathbf{a},a_{n-2},a_{n-1},a_{n}))

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{a_{n-2}a_{n-1}\geq B^{n(1-y_{1})}}\frac{1}{q_{n-3}^{2\tilde{s}}(\mathbf{a})a_{n-2}^{2\tilde{s}}a_{n-1}^{2\tilde{s}}B^{n(-y_{1}(1-\tilde{s})^{2}+\tilde{s}^{2})}}

\displaystyle\asymp\frac{1}{q_{n-3}^{2\tilde{s}}(\mathbf{a})B^{n(2\tilde{s}-1)(1-y_{1})}B^{n(-y_{1}(1-\tilde{s})^{2}+\tilde{s}^{2})}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q_{n-3}^{2\tilde{s}}(\mathbf{a})B^{n(2\tilde{s}-1+\tilde{s}^{2}(1-y_{1}))}}

\displaystyle<\frac{1}{q_{n-3}^{2\tilde{s}}(\mathbf{a})B^{ng(\tilde{s})}}

and we can conclude that
\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}V_{n}^{1}\leq s_{B}
.

Next, we focus on
(V_{n}^{2})_{n\geq 1}
.
For each
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{N}^{n-1}
such that
a_{n-2}a_{n-1}\leq B^{n(1-y_{1})}
define

J_{n}(\mathbf{a})=\bigcup_{a_{n}\geq\frac{B^{n}}{a_{n-2}a_{n-1}}}I_{n}(\mathbf{a},a_{n}),\;\text{ so }\;\left|J_{n}(\mathbf{a})\right|\asymp\frac{1}{q_{n-3}^{2}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n-3})a_{n-2}a_{n-1}B^{n}}.

From Lemma
3.9
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{a_{n-1}a_{n-2}\leq B^{n(1-y_{1})}}|J_{n}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n-1})|^{s}

\displaystyle\asymp\frac{n(1-y_{1})\log B}{q_{n-3}^{2s}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n-3})B^{ns}B^{n(1-y_{1})(1-s)}}

\displaystyle=\frac{n(1-y_{1})\log B}{q_{n-3}^{2s}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n-3})B^{n(2s-1+y_{1}(1-s))}}

\displaystyle=\frac{n(1-y_{1})\log B}{q_{n-3}^{2s}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n-3})B^{ng(s)}}.

Therefore,
\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}V_{n}^{2}\leq s_{B}
and, by (
38
),
\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\mathcal{F}_{3}^{1}\leq s_{B}
.

5.2.3.
Overlaps of length 2

To construct a useful covering of
\mathcal{F}_{3}^{2}
, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
define

\displaystyle C_{0,n}

\displaystyle=\left\{x\in[0,1):B^{n}\leq a_{n-1}(x)\right\},

\displaystyle C_{1,n}

\displaystyle=\left\{x\in[0,1):a_{n-1}(x)\leq B^{nz_{1}},\,B^{n}\leq a_{n}(x)a_{n+1}(x)a_{n+2}(x)\right\},

\displaystyle C_{2,n}

\displaystyle=\left\{x\in[0,1):B^{nz_{1}}\leq a_{n-1}(x)\leq B^{n},\,B^{n}\leq a_{n-1}(x)a_{n}(x)a_{n+1}(x),B^{nz_{1}}\leq a_{n+2}(x)\right\},

\displaystyle C_{3,n}

\displaystyle=\left\{x\in[0,1):B^{nz_{1}}\leq a_{n-1}(x)\leq B^{n},\,B^{n}\leq a_{n}(x)a_{n+1}(x)a_{n+2}(x),a_{n+2}(x)\leq B^{nz_{1}}\right\};

hence,

(39)

\mathcal{F}_{3}^{2}=\limsup_{n\to\infty}C_{0,n}\cup\limsup_{n\to\infty}C_{1,n}\cup\limsup_{n\to\infty}C_{2,n}\cup\limsup_{n\to\infty}C_{3,n}.

Since
g(s)<s
when
\frac{1}{2}<s<1
, Theorem
1.2
implies
\displaystyle\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\limsup_{n\to\infty}C_{0,n}\leq s_{B}
.

Let us discuss the limsup of
(C_{1,n})_{n\geq 1}
.
Let
f_{3}:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
,
f_{3}(s)=\frac{s^{3}}{s^{2}-s+1}
, be as in Theorem
1.2
.
Direct computations show that

g(s)<(2s-1)Z(s)+f_{3}(s)\;\text{ for }\;\frac{1}{2}<s<1.

Let
s,\tilde{s}
be such that
s_{B}<\tilde{s}<s<1
.
As we did in Lemma
5.2
, we can follow the proof of
[
12
, Theorem 1.7]
to construct for all large
n\in\mathbb{N}
and any
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{N}^{n-2}
,
a_{n-1}\in\mathbb{N}
a cover
\mathfrak{G}(\mathbf{a},a_{n-1},a_{n})
of

\bigcup_{a_{n}a_{n+1}a_{n+2}\geq B^{n}}I_{n+2}(\mathbf{a},a_{n-1},a_{n},a_{n+1},a_{n+2})

such that

\Lambda_{s}(\mathfrak{G}(\mathbf{a},a_{n-1}))\ll\frac{1}{q_{n-2}^{2\tilde{s}}(\mathbf{a})a_{n-1}^{2\tilde{s}}B^{nf_{3}(\tilde{s})}};

hence, assuming
s
is close to
s_{B}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{a_{n-1}\leq B^{nz_{1}}}\Lambda_{s}(\mathfrak{G}(\mathbf{a},a_{n-1}))

\displaystyle\ll\frac{1}{q_{n-2}^{2\tilde{s}}(\mathbf{a})B^{nz_{1}(2\tilde{s}-1)}B^{nf_{3}(\tilde{s})}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q_{n-2}^{2\tilde{s}}(\mathbf{a})B^{n(z_{1}(2\tilde{s}-1)+f_{3}(\tilde{s}))}}<\frac{1}{q_{n-2}^{2\tilde{s}}(\mathbf{a})B^{ng(\tilde{s})}}.

The estimate
\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}C_{2,n}\leq s_{B}
follows from
g(s_{B})=2s_{B}-1+s_{B}z_{1}
, the strict growth of
s\mapsto 2s-1+sz_{1}
, and

\displaystyle\sum_{B^{nz_{1}}\leq a_{n-1}\leq B^{n}}\sum_{\frac{B^{n}}{a_{n-1}}\leq a_{n}a_{n+1}}\frac{1}{a_{n-1}^{2s}a_{n}^{2s}a_{n+1}^{2s}B^{nsz_{1}}}

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{B^{nz_{1}}\leq a_{n-1}\leq B^{n}}\frac{1}{a_{n-1}^{2s}\left(\frac{B^{n}}{a_{n-1}}\right)^{2s-1}B^{nsz_{1}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{B^{nz_{1}}\leq a_{n-1}\leq B^{n}}\frac{1}{a_{n-1}B^{n(2s-1+sz_{1})}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n\log B}{B^{n(2s-1+sz_{1})}}.

Lastly, note that
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

C_{3,n}\subseteq C_{3,n}^{\prime}=\left\{x\in[0,1):B^{nz}\leq a_{n-1}(x)\leq B^{n},\,B^{n(1-z_{1})}\leq a_{n}(x)a_{n+1}(x)\right\}.

By Lemma
3.9
, for each
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{N}^{n-2}
and
B^{nz_{1}}\leq a_{n-1}(x)\leq B^{n}
there is a countable cover
\mathfrak{C}^{\prime}(\mathbf{a},a_{n-1})
of
C_{3,n}^{\prime}\cap I_{n-1}(\mathbf{a},a_{n-1})
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{B^{nz_{1}}\leq a_{n-1}(x)\leq B^{n}}\sum_{C\in\mathfrak{C}^{\prime}(\mathbf{a},a_{n-1})}|C|^{s}

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{B^{nz_{1}}\leq a_{n-1}(x)\leq B^{n}}\frac{1}{q_{n-2}^{2s}(\mathbf{a})a_{n-1}^{2s}B^{ns^{2}(1-z_{1})}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{B^{nz}\leq a_{n-1}(x)}\frac{1}{q_{n-2}^{2s}(\mathbf{a})a_{n-1}^{2s}B^{ns^{2}(1-z_{1})}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{q_{n-2}^{2s}(\mathbf{a})B^{n(2s-1)z}B^{ns^{2}(1-z_{1})}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q_{n-2}^{2s}(\mathbf{a})B^{n(z_{1}(2s-1)+s^{2}(1-z_{1}))}}.

Because
s\mapsto z_{1}(2s-1)+s^{2}(1-z_{1})
is strictly increasing and
g(s_{B})=z_{1}(2s_{B}-1)+s_{B}^{2}(1-z_{1})
, we conclude

\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\limsup_{n\to\infty}C_{3,n}\leq\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\limsup_{n\to\infty}C_{3,n}^{\prime}\leq s_{B}.

Therefore, (
39
) yields
\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\mathcal{F}_{3}^{2}\leq s_{B}
.

6.
Final remarks and further results

In the introduction, we said that Theorem
1.5
is an important piece in the extension of the laws of large numbers (
2
) and (
3
).
In a forthcoming article, we study the asymptotic behavior of the sums of products of consecutive partial quotients.
More precisely, for
n,\ell\in\mathbb{N}
and
x\in[0,1)\setminus\mathbb{Q}
, define

S_{n,\ell}(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}(x)\cdots a_{j+\ell-1}(x).

Conjecture 6.1
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathfrak{m}\left(\left\{x\in[0,1):\left|\frac{1}{n\log^{\ell}n}S_{n,\ell}(x)-\frac{1}{\ell\log 2}\right|\geq\varepsilon\right\}\right)=0.

Also, almost every
x\in[0,1)
satisfies

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n\log^{\ell}n}\left(S_{n,\ell}(x)-\max_{1\leq j\leq n}a_{j}(x)\cdots a_{j+\ell-1}(x)\right)=\frac{1}{\ell\log 2}.

Regarding the Hausdorff dimension of certain Lebesgue-null sets, for a non-decreasing function
\varphi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\varphi(n)\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
, we consider

G_{\ell}(\varphi):=\left\{x\in[0,1):\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{S_{n,\ell}(x)}{\varphi(n)}=1\right\}.

Conjecture 6.2
.

Let
\alpha
be a positive real number.

(I)

If
0<\alpha<\frac{1}{2}
and
\varphi(n)=e^{n^{\alpha}}
, then
\dim_{\mathrm{H}}G_{\ell}(\varphi)=1
.

(II)

If
\frac{1}{2}\leq\alpha
and
\varphi(n)=e^{n^{\alpha}}
, then
\dim_{\mathrm{H}}G_{\ell}(\varphi)=\frac{1}{2}
.

(III)

If
1<\alpha
and
\varphi(n)=e^{\alpha^{n}}
, then
\dim_{\mathrm{H}}G_{\ell}(\varphi)=\frac{1}{1+\alpha}
.

Recently, Song, Yu, and Yu
[
22
]
investigated the Hausdorff dimension of a related set concerning the product of two partial quotients.
To define it, for
x\in[0,1)\setminus\mathbb{Q}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, put

L_{n}(x)=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\,a_{i}(x)a_{i+1}(x),

For a non-decreasing function
\varphi:\mathbb{R}_{>0}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
satisfying certain regularity properties, they computed the Hausdorff dimension of

L(\varphi):=\left\{x\in(0,1):\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{L_{n}(x)}{\varphi(n)}=1\right\}.

Their work leads us to the following problem.

Problem 1
.

Let
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 3}
.
Can we extend the results in
[
22
]
to the product of
\ell
consecutive partial quotients?
That is, for
x\in[0,1)\setminus\mathbb{Q}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, define

L_{\ell,n}(x)=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\,a_{i}(x)a_{i+1}(x)\cdots a_{i+\ell-1}(x).

Given
\rho>0
and a function
\varphi:\mathbb{R}_{>0}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
such that
\log\varphi
is regularly increasing of index
\rho
, determine the Hausdorff dimension of the set

L_{\ell}(\varphi):=\left\{x\in(0,1):\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{L_{\ell,n}(x)}{\varphi(n)}=1\right\}.

We conclude our work with two remarks concerning our main theorems.

Remark 6.1
.

The
n
th term of one of the series in Theorem
1.5
is

\frac{n\log^{2(\ell-1)}\varphi(n)}{\varphi^{2}(n)}+\frac{\log^{\ell-2}\varphi(n)}{\varphi(n)}.

The first term accounts for large products that appear in non-overlapping blocks, and the second term corresponds to overlapping blocks. Hence, for
\ell=1
the second term is non-existent.

Remark 6.2
.

It seems that the proof of Theorem
1.10
can be adapted to compute
\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\mathcal{F}_{\ell}(\varphi)
for
\ell\geq 4
.
A recursive formula for the potential giving
\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\mathcal{F}_{\ell}(\varphi)
could be expected, we have not used the formulas corresponding to
\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\mathcal{F}_{1}(\varphi_{B})
or
\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\mathcal{F}_{2}(\varphi_{B})
. Instead, we used those for
\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\mathcal{E}_{1}(\varphi_{B})
and
\dim_{\mathrm{H}}\mathcal{E}_{2}(\varphi_{B})

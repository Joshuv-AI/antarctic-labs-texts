Uniqueness and asymptotic stability of time-periodic solution for the fractal Burgers equation
Yong Zhang, Fei Xu, Fengquan Li

Abstract
. The paper is concerned with the time-periodic (T-periodic) problem of the fractal Burgers equation with a T-periodic force on the real line. Based on the Galerkin approximates and Fourier series (transform) methods, we first prove the existence of T-periodic solution to a linearized version. Then, the existence and uniqueness of T-periodic solution to the nonlinear equation are established by the contraction mapping argument. Furthermore, we show that the unique T-periodic solution is asymptotically stable. This analysis, which is carried out in energy space H 1 ​ ( 0 , T , H α 2 ​ ( R ) ) ∩ L 2 ​ ( 0 , T , H ˙ α ) H^{1}(0,T;H^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(R))\cap L^{2}(0,T;\dot{H}^{\alpha}) with 1 < α < 3 2 1<\alpha<\frac{3}{2} , extends the T-periodic viscid Burgers equation in [ 5 ] to the T-periodic fractional case.



1.
Introduction and main result

In this paper, we are interested in T-periodic motions for the following forced Burgers equation of fractal order

u_{t}+uu_{x}+\kappa\Lambda^{\alpha}u=f,\quad(t,x)\in[0,T]\times R,

(1.1)

where
\kappa>0
is the viscosity coefficient and
1<\alpha<\frac{3}{2}
is the dissipation index. The fractional dissipation term
\Lambda^{\alpha}u=(-\partial_{xx})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}u
is defined by the Fourier transform

\widehat{\Lambda^{\alpha}u}(\xi)=|\xi|^{\alpha}\hat{u}(\xi)

and
f(t,x)
is a T-periodic external force, i.e.
f(t+T,x)=f(t,x)
.

The viscid Burgers (or Burgers) equation is one of the most simplest but important partial differential equation to model Navier-Stokes (or Euler) equation’s nonlinearity. In recent years, there has been a great deal of interest in using the fractional dissipation to describe diverse physical phenomena, such as anomalous diffusion and quasi-geostrophic flows, turbulence and water waves and molecular dynamics (see
[
1
,
6
,
7
,
8
]
and the references therein). The main purpose of this paper is to seek the T-periodic solution, i.e.
u(t,x)=u(t+T,x)
, to (
1.1
), which is expected to be unique and stable. Besides, the results and techniques presented here may be applied to a wider class of equations including quasi-geostrophic equation, Boussinesq system and so on.

Before stating our main results, let’s mention about some important work on Burgers equation involving fractional dissipation or periodic behavior. To our knowledge, the well-posedness of fractal Burgers equation (
1.1
) with
f=0
depends heavily on the index
\alpha
(see
[
2
,
3
,
16
]
). In the supercritical dissipative case (
0<\alpha<1
), the equation is locally well-posed and its solution develops gradient blow-up in finite time. In the critical dissipative case (
\alpha=1
) and subcritical dissipative case (
1<\alpha<2
), such singularity does not appear so that the solution always exists globally in time. Besides, the results on the global regularizing effects in the subcritical case and the non-uniqueness of weak solutions in the supercritical case were also established in
[
11
]
and
[
12
]
, respectively. Recently, the analyticity and large time behavior in the critical case were given in
[
17
]
.

On the other hand, the existence of the time-periodic solution of (
1.1
) with
\alpha=2
was obtained for
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times[-1,1]
in
[
5
]
, where the authors also proved that this time-periodic solution was unique and asymptotically stable in the
H^{1}
sense under an additional smallness condition on
f(t,x)
. The non-autonomously forced Burgers equation with periodic and Dirichlet boundary conditions was also studied in
[
20
]
. In addition, many authors concentrated on the existence and uniqueness of time-periodic solutions and investigated how the solutions near the time-periodic solutions behave as time goes on, see
[
4
,
9
,
10
,
13
,
14
,
18
]
.

Although the Burgers equation is fundamental, there is little information on the T-periodic solution to ((
1.1
)) with
\alpha\neq 2
. In some physical models, the T-periodic solution plays the key role in describing natural phenomena. This is a main motivation of the study. Throughout this paper, we denote
f\lesssim g
when
f\leq cg
for some constant

and let

and

be the nonhomogeneous and homogeneous Sobolev spaces. Our first result in this paper is about the existence and uniqueness of T-periodic solution with finite energy to (
1.1

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that
f\in X=H^{1}(0,T;\dot{H}^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}(R))\cap L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(R))\cap L^{2}(0,T;\dot{H}^{-\alpha}(R))
with
1<\alpha<\frac{3}{2}
and the force
f(t,x)
satisfies

f(0)=f(T)~~in~~\dot{H}^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}(R)~~and~~\|f\|_{X}<C(\kappa,\alpha),

where
C(\kappa,\alpha)=\min\{\frac{2\kappa^{2}\sqrt{2(3-2\alpha)}}{9(\kappa+1)^{2}(\sqrt{2(3-2\alpha)}+1)},\frac{\kappa^{2}}{6(\kappa+1)^{2}}\}
, then there exists a unique T-periodic strong solution

u\in H^{1}(0,T;H^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(R))\cap L^{2}(0,T;\dot{H}^{\alpha})

to problem ((
1.1
)) satisfying
u(0)=u(T)
in
H^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(R)
and

\|u\|_{H^{1}(0,T;H^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(R))}+\|\Lambda^{\alpha}u\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(R))}\leq 3(1+\frac{1}{\kappa})\|f\|_{X}.

(1.2)

Remark 1.2
.

It’s known that the condition

is necessary to ensure the global existence of strong solution to (
1.1
). Thus, it’s reasonable to expect that this result holds for

. However, the techniques of Sobolev embedding and Fourier analysis prevent us improving the range of

Besides, we also prove the unique T-periodic strong solution of (
1.1
) obtained in Theorem

is asymptotically stable in the following.

Theorem 1.3
.

Suppose

satisfies the assumption in Theorem
1.1
, then the T-periodic solution

of (
1.1
) is asymptotic stability in the sense: assume

be a viscosity weak solution of (
1.1
) with initial data

\|u-u_{T}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(R))\cap L^{2}(0,T;\dot{H}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(R))}\leq\|u_{0}-u_{T}(0)\|_{L^{2}(R)}.

(1.3)

The paper is organized as follows. In section 2, we are devoted to deal with a linearized version of problem (
1.1

(2.29)

and

\||\xi|^{\alpha}\hat{u}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}([-r,r]))}=\||\xi|^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}|\xi|^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\hat{u}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}([-r,r]))}\leq r^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\||\xi|^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\hat{u}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(R))}.

(2.30)

Taking the square on both sides of (
2.28
), we can obtain

|\partial_{t}\hat{u}|^{2}+\kappa^{2}|\xi|^{2\alpha}|\hat{u}|^{2}+\kappa\partial_{t}(|\xi|^{\alpha}|\hat{u}|^{2})=|\hat{f}|^{2}.

(2.31)

It follows from (
2.31
) that

\kappa^{2}|\hat{u}|^{2}+\kappa\partial_{t}(|\xi|^{-\alpha}|\hat{u}|^{2})\leq|\xi|^{-2\alpha}|\hat{f}|^{2}.

Thanks to (
2.29
) and the T-periodicity of
u(t)
, integrating both sides of above inequality over
[0,T]\times[-r,r]^{c}
yields

\int^{T}_{0}\|\hat{u}\|^{2}_{L^{2}([-r,r]^{c})}dt\leq\frac{1}{\kappa^{2}}\int^{T}_{0}\||\xi|^{-\alpha}\hat{f}\|^{2}_{L^{2}([-r,r]^{c})}dt\leq\frac{1}{\kappa^{2}}\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T;\dot{H}^{-\alpha}(R))},

(2.32)

then (
2.8
) follows by letting
r\rightarrow 0
in (
2.32
).

Similarly, we also can deduce that from (
2.31
)

\kappa^{2}|\xi|^{2\alpha}|\hat{u}|^{2}+\kappa\partial_{t}(|\xi|^{\alpha}|\hat{u}|^{2})\leq|\hat{f}|^{2}.

Thanks to (
2.30
) and the T-periodicity of
u(t)
, integrating both sides of above inequality over
[0,T]\times[-r,r]
yields

\int^{T}_{0}\||\xi|^{\alpha}\hat{u}\|^{2}_{L^{2}([-r,r])}dt\leq\frac{1}{\kappa^{2}}\int^{T}_{0}\|\hat{f}\|^{2}_{L^{2}([-r,r])}dt\leq\frac{1}{\kappa^{2}}\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(R))},

(2.33)

then (
2.9
) follows by letting
r\rightarrow\infty
in (
2.33
). It’s obvious that the solution to linearized problem (
2.1
) is unique, then the proof is completed.
∎

3.
The Proof of the Theorem
1.1

In this section, a contraction mapping argument would be constructed to establish the existence and uniqueness of T-periodic solution to (
1.1
).

Proof.

For convenience, let’s first introduce two spaces

X_{T}:=\{\phi\in X|~\phi(0)=\phi(T)~in~\dot{H}^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}(R)\}

and

Y_{T}:=\{\phi\in Y=H^{1}(0,T;H^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(R))\cap L^{2}(0,T;\dot{H}^{\alpha})|~\phi(0)=\phi(T)~in~H^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(R)\}.

By Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality and Kato-Ponce type commutator estimates in
[
15
]
, we will show that
vv_{x}\in X_{T}
for any
v\in Y_{T}
. It’s easy to see that
(vv_{x})(0)=(vv_{x})(T)
if
v\in Y_{T}
. In addition, we can use the interpolation theorem to obtain

\displaystyle(\int^{T}_{0}\|vv_{x}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}dt)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=(\int^{T}_{0}\int_{R}v^{2}v_{x}^{2}dxdt)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\max_{t\in[0,T]}\|v(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}(\int^{T}_{0}\|v_{x}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}dt)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|v\|_{Y}(\int^{T}_{0}\|\Lambda^{\alpha}v\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}\|v\|_{L^{2}}^{2-\frac{2}{\alpha}}dt)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|v\|_{Y}(\int^{T}_{0}\|\Lambda^{\alpha}v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt)^{\frac{1}{2\alpha}}(\int^{T}_{0}\|v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt)^{\frac{\alpha-1}{2\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq\|v\|_{Y}^{2},

(3.1)

\displaystyle(\int^{T}_{0}\|vv_{x}\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}}dt)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}(\int^{T}_{0}\|\Lambda^{1-\frac{\alpha}{2}}(vv)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}dt)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim(\int^{T}_{0}\|\Lambda^{1-\frac{\alpha}{2}}v\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\|v\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}dt)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|v\|_{Y}(\int^{T}_{0}\|\Lambda^{1-\frac{\alpha}{2}}v\|^{2}_{L^{2}}dt)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\|v\|_{Y}(\int^{T}_{0}\|\Lambda^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}v\|^{2-\alpha}_{L^{2}}\|v\|_{L^{2}}^{\alpha}dt)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|v\|_{Y}(\int^{T}_{0}\|\Lambda^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt)^{\frac{\alpha}{4}}(\int^{T}_{0}\|v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt)^{\frac{2-\alpha}{4}}

\displaystyle\leq\|v\|_{Y}^{2}

(3.2)

and

\displaystyle(\int^{T}_{0}\|\partial_{t}(vv_{x})\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}}dt)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=(\int^{T}_{0}\|\partial_{x}(vv_{t})\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}}dt)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq(\int^{T}_{0}\|\Lambda^{1-\frac{\alpha}{2}}(vv_{t})\|^{2}_{L^{2}}dt)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(\int^{T}_{0}\|\Lambda^{1-\frac{\alpha}{2}}v_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|v\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}dt+\int^{T}_{0}\|\Lambda^{1-\frac{\alpha}{2}}v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|v_{t}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}dt)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(\|v\|_{Y}^{2}\int^{T}_{0}\|\Lambda^{1-\frac{\alpha}{2}}v_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt+\|v\|_{Y}^{2}\max_{t\in[0,T]}\|\Lambda^{1-\frac{\alpha}{2}}v(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}})^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|v\|_{Y}^{2},

(3.3)

where the embedding
H^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(R)\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(R)
and Hölder inequality are also used.

At last, it remains to estimate
\|vv_{x}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\dot{H}^{-\alpha}(R))}
, where the restriction on
1<\alpha<\frac{3}{2}
is required. As
vv_{x}=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{x}(v^{2})
, we take the Fourier transform to obtain

\widehat{vv_{x}}(t,\xi)=\frac{1}{2}i\xi\widehat{v^{2}}(t,\xi)

and

|\widehat{v^{2}}(t,\xi)|\leq\|v^{2}\|_{L^{1}},\quad for~~(t,\xi)\in(0,T)\times R.

Hence, there hold

|\widehat{vv_{x}}(t,\xi)|\leq\frac{1}{2}|\xi|\|v^{2}\|_{L^{1}}

and

|\xi|^{-\alpha}|\widehat{vv_{x}}(t,\xi)|\leq\frac{1}{2}|\xi|^{1-\alpha}\|v^{2}\|_{L^{1}},\quad a.e~~on~~(0,T)\times R.

Then we have

\displaystyle(\int^{T}_{0}\|vv_{x}\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{-\alpha}}dt)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=(\int^{T}_{0}\int_{R}|\xi|^{-2\alpha}|\widehat{vv_{x}}(t,\xi)|^{2}d\xi dt)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq(\frac{1}{4}\int^{T}_{0}\int_{[-1,1]}|\xi|^{2-2\alpha}\|v^{2}\|^{2}_{L^{1}}d\xi dt

\displaystyle+\int^{T}_{0}\int_{[-1,1]^{c}}|\xi|^{-2\alpha}|\widehat{vv_{x}}(t,\xi)|^{2}d\xi dt)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq(\frac{1}{4}\int^{T}_{0}\|v^{2}\|^{2}_{L^{1}}dt\int_{[-1,1]}|\xi|^{2-2\alpha}d\xi+\int^{T}_{0}\int_{[-1,1]^{c}}|\widehat{vv_{x}}(t,\xi)|^{2}d\xi dt)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq(\frac{1}{2(3-2\alpha)}\|v\|^{4}_{L^{4}(0,T;L^{2}(R))}+\|vv_{x}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(R))})^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(\frac{1}{2(3-2\alpha)}\|v\|^{4}_{Y}+\|v\|^{4}_{Y})^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2(3-2\alpha)}})\|v\|_{Y}^{2}.

(3.4)

From (
3
)-(
3
), we obtain

\|vv_{x}\|_{X}\lesssim(1+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2(3-2\alpha)}})\|v\|^{2}_{Y}.

(3.5)

Based on the result in section 2, we find that the solution of (
1.1
) can be regarded as a fixed point of mapping
\Gamma:Y_{T}\rightarrow Y_{T}
, such that for each
v\in Y_{T}
,
u=\Gamma(v)
is the T-periodic solution of the following problem

u_{t}+\kappa\Lambda^{\alpha}u=f-vv_{x},\quad in~~(0,T)\times R.

(3.6)

However, it’s difficult to establish directly that the mapping
\Gamma
is a contraction mapping from
Y_{T}
to
Y_{T}
. In order to achieve the goal, we have to take some restrictions. Let’s define a subspace of
Y_{T}
by

Y^{*}_{T}=\{\phi\in Y_{T}|~\|\phi\|_{Y}\leq 3(1+\frac{1}{\kappa})\|f\|_{X}\}

According to Theorem
2.1
and (
3.5
), for a given
v\in Y_{T}^{*}
, we can conclude that there exists a unique solution
u\in Y_{T}
of (
3.6
), which also satisfies

\|u\|_{Y}\leq(1+\frac{1}{\kappa})(\|f\|_{X}+\|vv_{x}\|_{X})\leq(1+\frac{1}{\kappa})(\|f\|_{X}+\frac{9\sqrt{2(3-2\alpha)}+9}{\sqrt{2(3-2\alpha)}}(1+\frac{1}{\kappa})^{2}\|f\|_{X}^{2}).

(3.7)

In the following, we will prove that
\Gamma
is a contraction mapping from
Y^{*}_{T}
to
Y^{*}_{T}
. For any
v\in Y^{*}_{T}
, it follows from (
3.7
) and the smallness condition of
\|f\|_{X}
that

\|\Gamma(v)\|_{Y}=\|u\|_{Y}\leq(1+\frac{1}{\kappa})(\|f\|_{X}+\frac{9\sqrt{2(3-2\alpha)}+9}{\sqrt{2(3-2\alpha)}}(1+\frac{1}{\kappa})^{2}\|f\|_{X}^{2})\leq 3(1+\frac{1}{\kappa})\|f\|_{X},

which means

\Gamma(Y^{*}_{T})\subseteq Y^{*}_{T}.

On the other hand, let
v_{1},v_{2}\in Y^{*}_{T}
and
u_{1}=\Gamma(v_{1}),u_{2}=\Gamma(v_{2})
, Theorem
2.1
and the smallness condition of
\|f\|_{X}
yield

\displaystyle\|\Gamma(v_{1})-\Gamma(v_{2})\|_{Y}

\displaystyle=\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{Y}

\displaystyle\leq(1+\frac{1}{\kappa})(\|v_{1}\|_{Y}+\|v_{2}\|_{Y})\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{Y}

\displaystyle\leq 6(1+\frac{1}{\kappa})^{2}\|f\|_{X}\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{Y}<\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{Y}.

(3.8)

Thus
\Gamma
is a contraction mapping and the proof is completed.
∎

.

Based on the proof of Theorem

\|u\|_{H^{1}(0,T;H^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(R))}+\|\Lambda^{\alpha}u\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(R))}\leq\beta(1+\frac{1}{\kappa})\|f\|_{X}

4.
The proof of Theorem
1.3

In this section, we mainly discuss the asymptotic stability of the T-periodic solution
u_{T}(t,x)
obtained in section 3. It’s worth noting that the smallness condition on the external force will play an important role here.
Before proving Theorem
1.3
, let’s introduce the lemma on existence of viscosity solution of (
1.1
), which has been proved in
[
19
]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

If
u_{0}\in L^{2}(R)
and
f\in L^{2}(0,T;\dot{H}^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}(R)\cap L^{2}(R))
, then there exists a global viscosity weak solution
u\in L^{\infty}([0,T),L^{2}(R))\cap L^{2}([0,T),\dot{H}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(R))
to (
1.1
), which satisfies for any given
T>0
,

\displaystyle-\int^{T}_{0}\langle u,\psi_{t}\rangle dt-\frac{1}{2}\int^{T}_{0}\langle u^{2},\psi_{x}\rangle dt+\kappa\int^{T}_{0}\langle\Lambda^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}u,\Lambda^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\psi\rangle dt

\displaystyle=\langle u_{0},\psi(x,0)\rangle+\int^{T}_{0}\langle f,\psi\rangle dt,\quad for~~\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}([0,T)\times R).

Moreover, there holds

\sup_{t\geq 0}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\kappa\int^{T}_{0}\|\Lambda^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(R)}^{2}+\frac{1}{\kappa}\|f\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\dot{H}^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}(R)\cap L^{2}(R))}^{2}.

Now we are in the position to prove the Theorem
1.3
.

Proof.

Let
w(t,x)=u(t,x)-u_{T}(t,x)
, then
w
satisfies

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}w_{t}+ww_{x}+(u_{T}w)_{x}+\kappa\Lambda^{\alpha}w=0,\\
w_{0}(x)=u_{0}(x)-u_{T}(0,x),\end{array}\right.

(4.1)

Multiplying both sides of (
4.2
) by
w
and integrating on
R
give

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{R}|w|^{2}dx+\kappa\int_{R}|\Lambda^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}w|^{2}dx=-\int_{R}(u_{T}w)_{x}wdx.

(4.2)

On the other hand, there holds

\displaystyle|\int_{R}(u_{T}w)_{x}wdx|

\displaystyle\leq|\int_{R}\Lambda^{1-\frac{\alpha}{2}}(u_{T}w)\Lambda^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}wdx|

\displaystyle\leq\|\Lambda^{1-\frac{\alpha}{2}}(u_{T}w)\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}w\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq(\|\Lambda^{1-\frac{\alpha}{2}}u_{T}\|_{L^{2}}\|w\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|u_{T}\|_{L^{\frac{1}{\alpha-1}}}\|\Lambda^{1-\frac{\alpha}{2}}w\|_{L^{\frac{2}{3-2\alpha}}})\|\Lambda^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}w\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq(\|\Lambda^{1-\frac{\alpha}{2}}u_{T}\|_{L^{2}}+\|u_{T}\|_{L^{\frac{1}{\alpha-1}}})\|\Lambda^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|u_{T}\|_{H^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}\|\Lambda^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(4.3)

Therefore (
4.2
) and (
4
) imply

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{R}|w|^{2}dx+2\kappa\int_{R}|\Lambda^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}w|^{2}dx\leq 4\|u_{T}\|_{H^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}\|\Lambda^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(4.4)

Integrating (
4.4
) with respect to time variable over
(0,T)
, we obtain

\int_{R}|w|^{2}dx+2\kappa\int_{0}^{T}\int_{R}|\Lambda^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}w|^{2}dxdt\leq 4\max_{t\in[0,T]}\|u_{T}\|_{H\frac{\alpha}{2}}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{R}|\Lambda^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}w|^{2}dxdt+\int_{R}|w_{0}|^{2}dx.

(4.5)

Thus (
1.3
) follows from (
4.5
), (
1.2
) and the smallness of
\|f\|_{X}\leq\frac{\kappa^{2}}{6(\kappa+1)^{2}}<\frac{\kappa^{2}}{6(\kappa+1)}
.
∎

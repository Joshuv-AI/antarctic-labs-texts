Improvements to the theoretical estimates of the Schwarz preconditioner with Δ \Delta -GenEO coarse space for the indefinite Helmholtz problem
Victorita Dolean, Mark Fry, Matthias Langer †

Abstract
The purpose of this work is to improve the estimates for the Δ \Delta -GenEO method from the paper [ 2 ] when applied to the indefinite Helmholtz equation. We derive k k -dependent estimates of quantities of interest ensuring the robustness of the method.



MSC Classes:

65N22, 65N55, 65F10

Keywords:
elliptic PDEs, finite element methods, domain decomposition, preconditioning, GMRES, Helmholtz equation,
spectral coarse space, robustness

1
Introduction

In this work we focus on the indefinite version of Helmholtz boundary value problems with highly variable coefficients defined
on
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, given by:

\displaystyle-\diverg(A\nabla u)-k^{2}u

\displaystyle=f\qquad

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega,

(1.1)

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=0\qquad

\displaystyle\text{on }\partial\Omega.

We use the same setting as in
[
2
]
, namely we work on a computational domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
(
d=2,3
)
which is supposed to be polygonal or Lipschitz polyhedral. The matrix
A
is positive definite, and we assume that
(
1.1
) has a unique weak solution
u\in H^{1}(\Omega)
for all
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
.

The global domain
\Omega
is covered by a set of overlapping subdomains
\Omega_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,N
,
and the classical one-level additive Schwarz preconditioner is built from partial solutions on each subdomain.
Since this preconditioner is, in general, not scalable as the number of subdomains grows, an additional global coarse solve
is usually added to enhance scalability, as well as robustness with respect to coefficient heterogeneity or the increase in the wavenumber.
As in
[
2
]
we use local generalised eigenvalue problems that are obtained by a shift of the left-hand side of (
1.1
).
After discretisation by finite elements, the linear systems arising from (
1.1
) are symmetric but indefinite.
For this reason, we use GMRES as the iterative solver, and the convergence analysis relies on the ‘Elman theory’

[
3
]
, which requires an upper bound for the norm of the preconditioned matrix and a
lower bound on the distance of its field of values from the origin.
As a result, the number of GMRES iterations to achieve a given error tolerance is a function of these two bounds.

The main results of this preprint.

Our main theoretical result provides rigorous and
k
-explicit upper bounds on the coarse mesh
diameter
H
and on the ‘eigenvalue tolerance’
\tau
(for the local generalised eigenvalue problems) which ensure
that GMRES enjoys robust and mesh-independent convergence when applied to the preconditioned problem.
This rigorous explicit bound for (
1.1
) follows the methodology from
[
2
]
and provides an
improvement of the
k
-dependent estimates.
As a reminder, GenEO coarse spaces are usually based on the
m_{i}
(dominant) eigenfunctions corresponding to the smallest
eigenvalues
\lambda^{i}_{1}\leq\lambda^{i}_{2}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{m_{i}}^{i}
of the generalised eigenvalue problem on the subdomain
\Omega_{i}
.
To obtain a robust rate of convergence for GMRES that depends only on
\Lambda

(the maximum number of times any point is overlapped by the subdomains
\Omega_{i}
), we need some conditions on
H
and
the eigenvalue tolerance
\tau\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\min_{i=1}^{N}\lambda^{i}_{m_{i}+1}
, where
N
is the number of subdomains.
In the estimates
C_{\textup{{stab}}}>0
the stability constant for the problem (
1.1
) also appears.
The hidden constants usually depend only on
\Lambda
.

More explicitly, in
[
2
, Theorem 4.1]
it is proved that a robust rate is achieved if

H\lesssim k^{-2}\qquad\text{and}\qquad(1+C_{\textup{{stab}}})^{2}\,k^{8}\lesssim\tau.

(1.2)

In the current paper, we show that the bounds (
1.2
) can be improved:

H\lesssim k^{-1}\qquad\text{and}\qquad(1+C_{\textup{{stab}}})^{2}k^{4}\lesssim\tau.

(1.3)

Although, this is a first improvement, these bounds remain quite pessimistic, showing a strong dependence on the wavenumber
k
.

Useful Background

2.1
Problem formulation and discretisation

The weak formulation of (
1.1
) is to find
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
such that

b(u,v)=(f,v)\qquad\text{for all}\ v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),

(2.1)

where
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and
b(\cdot,\cdot):H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\times H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is defined as

b(u,v)=\int_{\Omega}(A\nabla u\cdot\nabla v-k^{2}uv)\,\mathrm{d}x.

We shall be making use of the positive definite bilinear form
a(\cdot,\cdot):H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\times H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
.

a(u,v)=\int_{\Omega}A\nabla u\cdot\nabla vdx,

If
a
and
b
are defined on a subdomain
\Omega^{\prime}
of
\Omega
we employ the notations
a_{\Omega^{\prime}}
and
b_{\Omega^{\prime}}
.
The following weak regularity assumptions are going to be used throughout this work.

Assumption 2.1
.

The coefficient
A
in problem (
1.1
) satisfies the following conditions.

(i)

A:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
is symmetric with
0<a_{\textup{{min}}}\leq a_{\textup{{max}}}
s.t.

a_{\textup{{min}}}|\bm{\xi}|^{2}\leq A(x)\bm{\xi}\cdot{\bm{\xi}}\leq a_{\textup{{max}}}|\bm{\xi}|^{2}\qquad\text{for all}\ x\in\Omega,\bm{\xi}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(2.2)

(ii)

Without loss of generality,
a_{\textup{{min}}}=1
and the diameter,
D_{\Omega}
, of the domain
\Omega
is such that
D_{\Omega}\leq 1
.
If this is not the case, then the problem can be scaled accordingly.

Notation 2.2
.

For any subdomain
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\Omega
, we use
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\Omega^{\prime}}
to denote the
L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime})
inner product,
with the norm denoted by
\|\cdot\|_{\Omega^{\prime}}
. When
\Omega^{\prime}=\Omega
, the inner product is written as
(\cdot,\cdot)

with norm
\|\cdot\|
. The norm induced by the positive bilinear form
a
is denoted by
\|u\|_{a,\Omega^{\prime}}=\sqrt{a_{\Omega^{\prime}}(u,u)}
.
When
\Omega^{\prime}=\Omega
we abandon the subscript
\Omega
. We see that
b(u,v)=a(u,v)-k^{2}(u,v)
.

The bilinear form
a(\cdot,\cdot)
is positive definite (SPD), whereas
b(\cdot,\cdot)
is symmetric, but in general indefinite.
In all what follows, solvability of (
2.1
) is assumed.

Assumption 2.3
.

For any
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, it is assumed that the problem
2.1
has a unique solution
u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
and
there exists a constant
C_{\textup{{stab}}}>0
such that

\|u\|_{a}\leq C_{\textup{{stab}}}\|f\|\qquad\text{for all}\ f\in L^{2}(\Omega).

(2.3)

Let
\mathcal{T}_{h}
be any shape regular triangular mesh over the domain,
\Omega
. For the purpose of this work, 2 or 3-dimensional simplices
are being considered, but this could easily be applied to
d
-dimensional simplices, where the maximum diameter is
h
.
Let
V^{h}\subset H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
be any conforming finite element space.
The Galerkin approximation of (
2.1
) is to find
u_{h}\in V^{h}
such that

b(u_{h},v)=(f,v)\qquad\text{for all}\ v\in V^{h}.

(2.4)

If
n
denotes the dimension of
V^{h}
, with a basis given by
\{\phi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
,
then (
2.4
) can be represented by the linear system

\mathbf{B}\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{f}.

(2.5)

The matrix
\mathbf{B}
is defined in terms of the basis functions as
(\mathbf{B})_{ij}=b(\phi_{j},\phi_{i})
and
(\mathbf{f})_{i}=(f,\phi_{i})
.
It is also possible to define the matrix
\mathbf{A}
using the same basis functions as
(\mathbf{A})_{ij}=a(\phi_{j},\phi_{i})
.

The solvability of (
2.4
) is assured by the following Lemma from
[
5
, Theorem 2]
.
This is required due to the indefiniteness of (
2.4
).

Lemma 2.4

(Schatz and Wang, 1996)
.

Let Assumptions
2.1
and
2.3
hold. Then there exists an
h_{0}>0
such that, for each
h
with
0<h<h_{0}
,
the problem (
2.4
) has a unique solution
u_{h}\in V_{H}
.
Moreover, let
u
be the unique solution of (
2.1
). Then, for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
h_{1}=h_{1}(\varepsilon)>0

such that, for every
h\in(0,h_{1})
,

\|u-u_{h}\|\leq\varepsilon\|u-u_{h}\|_{{H^{1}}(\Omega)}

(2.6)

and

\|u-u_{h}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq\varepsilon\|f\|.

(2.7)

Remark 2.5
.

The introduction of (
2.6
) differs from
[
2
]
, allowing for improvements to be made in later Lemmas.

By combining the Friedrichs inequality
[
4
, Theorem 13.19]

with Assumption
2.1
, it possible to state that, for any subdomain
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\Omega
with diameter
H
,
we obtain the estimate

\|u\|_{\Omega^{\prime}}\leq\frac{H}{\sqrt{2}\,}\|\nabla u\|_{\Omega^{\prime}}\leq\frac{H}{\sqrt{2}\,}\|u\|_{a_{\Omega^{\prime}}}\qquad\text{for all}\ u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega^{\prime}).

(2.8)

This inequality will be used extensively in what follows.

2.2
Domain decomposition

In order to construct the two-level Schwarz preconditioner, the first-level preconditioner needs to be formulated.
This is achieved by first partitioning the global domain,
\Omega
, into a set of
N
subdomains,
\{\Omega_{i}^{\prime}\}_{i=1}^{N}
.
It is being assumed that the global mesh,
\mathcal{T}_{h}
, is sufficiently fine to resolve the subdomains.
Then each subdomain,
\Omega^{\prime}
, is expanded by one or more layers of mesh elements, in the sense of Definition
2.6
,
creating the desired overlapping set of
N
subdomains,
\{\Omega_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
.

Definition 2.6
.

Given a subdomain
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\Omega
, which is resolved by the chosen mesh, the extension of
\Omega^{\prime}
by a layer of elements is

\Omega_{\textsf{e}}=\Int\Biggl(\bigcup_{\{\ell\mid\supp(\phi_{\ell})\cap\Omega^{\prime}\not=\emptyset\}}\supp(\phi_{\ell})\Biggr)

where
\Int(\cdot)
is the interior of domain. Extension by multiple layers can then be achieved by applying this recursively.

For the domains
i=1,\ldots,N
, it is possible to define the spaces,

\widetilde{V}_{i}=\{v|_{\Omega_{i}}:v\in V^{h}\}\subset H^{1}(\Omega_{i})\quad\text{and}\quad V_{i}=\{v\in\widetilde{V}_{i}:v|_{\partial\Omega_{i}}=0\}\subset H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{i}).

(2.9)

For each subdomain,
\Omega_{i}
, we denote its diameter by
H_{i}
, and we set
H\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\max\{H_{i}\}
.
For any
u,v\in\widetilde{V}_{i}
, the local bilinear forms can be defined

a_{\Omega_{i}}(u,v)=\int_{\Omega_{i}}A\nabla u\cdot\nabla v\,\mathrm{d}x,\qquad b_{\Omega_{i}}(u,v)=\int_{\Omega_{i}}(A\nabla u\cdot\nabla v-k^{2}uv)\,\mathrm{d}x.

Remark 2.7
.

Although
b_{\Omega_{i}}(\cdot,\cdot)
is generally indefinite, for small enough
H
the form restricted to
V_{i}
is positive definite.
This is discussed in Lemma
3.8
.

For any
v_{i}\in V_{i}
, let
E_{i}v_{i}
denote its zero extension to the whole of the domain
\Omega
. Then,

E_{i}:V_{i}\rightarrow V^{h},\quad i=1,\ldots,N.

(2.10)

The
L^{2}(\Omega)
adjoint of the extension operator is called restriction operator,

R_{i}:V^{h}\rightarrow V_{i}.

By use of the extension operator, the restriction of the bilinear forms to
V_{i}
can be given as

a_{\Omega_{i}}(u,v)=a(E_{i}u,E_{i}v),\qquad b_{\Omega_{i}}(u,v)=b(E_{i}u,E_{i}v),\qquad(u,v)_{\Omega_{i}}=(E_{i}u,E_{i}v)

for all
u,v\in V_{i}
.
The one-level additive Schwarz preconditioner can now be given in matrix form as

\mathbf{M}^{-1}_{AS,1}=\sum^{N}_{i=1}\mathbf{E}_{i}\mathbf{B}^{-1}_{i}\mathbf{R}_{i},\qquad\text{where }\mathbf{B}_{i}=\mathbf{R}_{i}\mathbf{B}\mathbf{E}_{i}.

(2.11)

Here,
\mathbf{E}_{i}
and
\mathbf{R}_{i}
denote the matrix representations of
E_{i}
and
R_{i}
with respect to the
basis functions
\{\phi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
and some basis in
V_{i}
.

In order to improve the effectiveness of the preconditioner, a coarse space is added.
This improves the global exchange of information between the subdomains. Let
V_{0}\subset V^{h}
be such a coarse space.
Let
E_{0}:V_{0}\rightarrow V^{h}
be the natural embedding, and let
R_{0}
be the
L^{2}
adjoint of
E_{0}
,

(v_{0},R_{0}w)=(E_{0}v_{0},w)\qquad\text{for all}\ w\in V^{h},v_{0}\in V_{0}.

The two-level additive Schwarz preconditioner can now be given in matrix form as,

\mathbf{M}^{-1}_{AS,2}=\sum^{N}_{i=0}\mathbf{E}_{i}\mathbf{B}^{-1}_{i}\mathbf{R}_{i},\qquad\text{where }\mathbf{B}_{i}=\mathbf{R}_{i}\mathbf{B}\mathbf{E}_{i}.

(2.12)

The preconditioned linear system from (
2.5
) reads as,

\mathbf{M}^{-1}_{AS,2}\mathbf{B}\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{M}_{AS,2}^{-1}\mathbf{f}

(2.13)

It is now possible to define the projectors used in the analysis. For each
i=0,\ldots,N
,
the projectors,
T_{i}:V^{h}\rightarrow V_{i}
,
are defined by,

b_{\Omega_{i}}(T_{i}u,v)=b(u,E_{i}v)\qquad\text{for all}\ v\in V_{i},

(2.14)

where
\Omega_{0}=\Omega
. The existence of the
T_{i}
operators are guaranteed by Lemma
3.8
.
Given the operators
T_{i}
, the operator
T:V^{h}\rightarrow V^{h}
is defined as

T=\sum_{i=0}^{N}E_{i}T_{i}.

(2.15)

This allows for the two-level additive Schwarz preconditioner to be represented in terms of the projector operator,
T
, as follows.
We recall here the result from
[
2
]
.

Proposition 2.8
.

For any
u,v\in V^{h}
, with corresponding nodal vectors
\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\langle\mathbf{M}_{AS,2}^{-1}\mathbf{B}\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\rangle_{\mathbf{A}}=a(Tu,v),

(2.16)

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathbf{A}}
is the inner product on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, using the matrix
\mathbf{A}
.

2.3
The
\Delta
-GenEO coarse space

In order to use the
\Delta
-GenEO coarse space, we need to recall some definitions from
[
6
]
.

Definition 2.9

(
[
6
]
Definition 3.2)
.

Given a subdomain
\Omega_{i}
, which is formed from a union of elements from the whole domain, let

\overline{\dof}(\Omega_{i})\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\bigl\{\ell\mid 1\leq\ell\leq n\text{ and }\supp(\phi_{\ell})\cap\Omega_{i}\neq\emptyset\bigr\}

denote the set of degrees of freedom that are active in the subdomain
\Omega_{i}
, including ones on the boundary.
In a similar manner, let

\dof(\Omega_{i})\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\bigl\{\ell\mid 1\leq\ell\leq n\text{ and }\supp(\phi_{\ell})\subset\overline{\Omega_{i}}\bigr\}

denote the internal degrees of freedom.

Definition 2.10

(Partition of unity)
.

Let
\dof(\Omega_{i})
be as in Definition
2.9
.
For any degree of freedom,
j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, let
\mu_{j}
denote the number of subdomains for which
j
is an internal degree of freedom, i.e.

\mu_{j}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\#\bigl\{i\mid 1\leq i\leq N,\,j\in\dof(\Omega_{i})\bigr\}.

Then, for
i\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
, the local partition of unity operator,
\Xi_{i}:\widetilde{V}_{i}\rightarrow V_{i}
is defined by

\Xi_{i}(v)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\sum_{j\in\dof(\Omega_{i})}\frac{1}{\mu_{j}}v_{j}\phi_{j}^{i}\qquad\text{for}\ v=\sum_{j\in\overline{\dof}(\Omega_{i})}v_{j}\phi_{j}^{i}\in\widetilde{V}_{i}.

The local generalised eigenvalue problem that is going to form the basis of the coarse space can now be introduced.

Definition 2.11

(
[
2
]
, Definition 2.8)
.

For each
j=1,\ldots,N
, we define the following generalised eigenvalue problem.
Find
(p_{\ell}^{j},\lambda_{j})\in\widetilde{V}_{j}\setminus\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}
such that

a_{\Omega_{j}}(p_{\ell}^{j},v)=\lambda_{j}a_{\Omega_{j}}\bigl(\Xi_{j}(p_{\ell}^{j}),\Xi_{j}(v)\bigr)\qquad\text{for all}\ v\in\widetilde{V}_{j}.

Remark 2.12
.

As
a_{\Omega_{j}}(\Xi_{j}(\cdot),\Xi_{j}(\cdot))
is symmetric positive definite on
V_{j}
, the finite eigenvectors,
p_{\ell}^{j}
,
can be normalised with respect to
a_{\Omega_{j}}(\Xi_{j}(\cdot),\Xi_{j}(\cdot))
to satisfy the orthogonality conditions

a_{\Omega_{j}}\bigl(\Xi_{j}(p_{m}^{j}),\Xi_{j}(p_{\ell}^{j})\bigr)=\delta_{m}^{\ell}\quad\text{ and }\quad a_{\Omega_{j}}\bigl(p_{m}^{j},p_{\ell}^{j}\bigr)=\lambda_{j}\delta_{m}^{\ell}.

(2.17)

For the treatment of the eigenvalue infinity see the discussion in
[
6
]
.

Definition 2.13

(
\Delta
-GenEO Coarse space)
.

For each
j\in 1,\ldots,N
, let
\{p_{\ell}^{j}\}^{m_{j}}_{\ell=1}
be the eigenfunctions corresponding
to the
m_{j}
smallest eigenvalues from Definition
2.11
.
The value of
m_{j}
is to be chosen, with more details on this later. The coarse space,
V_{0}
, is given by

V_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\linspan\{E_{j}\Xi_{j}(p_{\ell}^{j})\mid l=1,\ldots,m_{j}\text{ and }j=1,\ldots,N\}.

(2.18)

We will also need the following notations

\Lambda\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\max_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\!\left(\#\{\Omega_{j}\mid 1\leq j\leq N,T\subset\Omega_{j}\}\right),\qquad\tau\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\min_{1\leq j\leq N}\lambda^{j}_{m_{j}+1}.

Statement of the main result and theoretical tools

We start this section with the statement of the main result and continue by providing a few technical lemmas needed in the proof of this result,
which will be given later on. For convenience we set
\Theta\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\frac{1}{\tau}
.

Theorem 3.1

(GMRES convergence of the two-level preconditioned system)
.

Assume that
h\in(0,h_{1})
, where
h_{1}
is as in Lemma
3.11
, then select
H
and
\tau
such that

\displaystyle s

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=(1+\Lambda^{2}\Theta)16\sqrt{2}k^{2}\Lambda^{\frac{3}{2}}\Theta^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+C_{\textup{{stab}}})<1,

(3.1)

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=32H^{2}k^{2}(1+\Lambda^{2}\Theta)\Lambda<1.

If GMRES is applied in the
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathbf{A}}
-inner product to solve the preconditioned system
given by (
2.13
), then after
m
iterations, the norm of the residual,
{\bf r}^{(m)}
, is bounded as

\|{\bf r}^{(m)}\|_{\mathbf{A}}^{2}\leq\biggl(1-\frac{c_{1}^{2}}{c_{2}^{2}}\biggr)^{m}\|{\bf r}^{(0)}\|_{\mathbf{A}}^{2},

(3.2)

where
c_{1}
and

are given by

c_{1}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\\
bigl(4(1+\Lambda^{2}\Theta)\bigr)^{-1}\bigl(1-\max\{t,s\}\bigr),\qquad c_{2}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=18+18\Lambda^{2}.

(3.3)

Corollary 3.2
.

If (
3.1
) in Theorem
3.1
are satisfied, then this leads to the conditions

H\lesssim k^{-1}\quad\text{and}\quad(1+C_{\textup{{stab}}})^{2}k^{4}\lesssim\tau.

(3.4)

If these conditions are satisfied, then
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
are both independent on problem parameters
including the heterogeneity and the wavenumber

, leading to a robust GMRES convergence.

Proof.

It is known that
\Lambda\geq 1
; so we have
1\leq\Lambda\leq\Lambda^{3/2}
and

16\sqrt{2}k^{2}\Lambda\Theta^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+C_{\textup{{stab}}})\leq(1+\Lambda^{2}\Theta)16\sqrt{2}k^{2}\Lambda^{\frac{3}{2}}\Theta^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+C_{\textup{{stab}}})<1,

thus leading to the inequality

2k^{2}\Lambda\Theta^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+C_{\textup{{stab}}})<\frac{1}{8\sqrt{2}}.

(3.5)

If we use the definition of
\tau
, then (
3.5
) translates into

(1+C_{\textup{{stab}}})^{2}k^{4}\lesssim\tau.

Now, using the second inequality from (

), we obtain

64H^{2}k^{2}\leq 64H^{2}k^{2}(1+\Lambda^{2}\Theta)\Lambda<1,

which leads to the condition
H\lesssim k^{-1}
.
∎

In practice, conditions (
3.4
) will introduce a constraint in the size of the subdomains,
depending on
k
and on the number of modes to be added in the coarse space.

.

Note that the condition (

) is different from the one obtained in
[

]
,
and allows for the improved conditions on

and

. These results follow from the modifications made to the later lemmas.

3.1
Properties of the
\Delta
-GenEO coarse space

The following three lemmas and Proposition
3.7
are also given in
[
2
]
and adapted from
[
6
]
.
The first gives an error estimate for the local projection operator, which is used to approximate a function
v\in\widetilde{V}_{i}

in the space being spanned by the eigenfunctions from Definition
2.11
.

Lemma 3.4

(Projection operator onto the coarse space)
.

Let
i\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
and
\{(p_{\ell}^{i},\lambda_{\ell}^{i})\}
be the eigenpairs of the generalised eigenproblem,
given in Definition
2.11
. Suppose that
m_{i}\in\{1,\ldots,\dim(V_{i})-1\}

is such that
0<\lambda_{m_{i}+1}^{i}<\infty
, and the eigenvalues can be ordered
such that
\lambda_{1}^{i}\leq\lambda_{2}^{i}\leq\ldots\leq\lambda_{\dim(\widetilde{V}_{i})}^{i}
.
Then the local projector,
\Pi_{m_{i}}^{i}
, defined by

\Pi_{m_{i}}^{i}v\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\sum_{l=1}^{m_{i}}a_{\Omega_{i}}\bigl(\Xi_{i}(v),\Xi_{i}(p_{\ell}^{i})\bigr)p_{\ell}^{i},

(3.6)

satisfies

\|w\|_{a_{\Omega_{i}}}^{2}\leq\|v\|^{2}_{a_{\Omega_{i}}}\quad\text{ and }\quad\|\Xi(w)\|^{2}_{a_{\Omega_{i}}}\leq\frac{1}{\lambda_{m_{i}+1}^{i}}\|w\|_{a_{\Omega_{i}}}^{2}\qquad\text{for all}\ v\in\widetilde{V}_{i},

(3.7)

where
w=v-\Pi_{m_{i}}^{i}v
.

From these local error estimates it is possible to build a global approximation property.

Lemma 3.5

(Global approximation property)
.

Assume that the conditions in Lemma
3.4
are satisfied. Let
v\in V^{h}
; then

\inf_{z\in V_{0}}\left\|v-z\right\|_{a}^{2}\leq\left\|v-z_{0}\right\|_{a}^{2}\leq\Lambda^{2}\Theta\|v\|^{2}_{a},

(3.8)

where

z_{0}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}E_{i}\Xi_{i}\!\left(\Pi_{m_{i}}^{i}v|_{\Omega_{i}}\right)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Theta=\max_{1\leq i\leq N}\frac{1}{\lambda_{m_{i}+1}^{i}}.

The following lemma shows that the GenEO coarse space, in combination with the local finite element space, allows for a stable decomposition.
This property is a key component for bounding the condition number in the two-level Schwarz preconditioner for positive definite cases,
as used in
[
6
]
.

Lemma 3.6

(Stable decomposition)
.

Let
v\in V^{h}
. Then the decomposition

z_{0}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}E_{i}\Xi_{i}\bigl(\Pi_{m_{i}}^{i}{v|_{\Omega_{i}}}\bigr)\quad\text{ and }\quad z_{i}=\Xi_{i}\bigl(v|_{\Omega_{i}}-\Pi_{m_{i}}^{i}v|_{\Omega_{i}}\bigr)\qquad\text{for}\ i=1,\ldots,N

satisfies
v=\sum_{i=0}^{N}E_{i}z_{i}
and

3.2
Solvability and stability of
T_{i},i=1,\ldots,N

The next two lemmas are reformulations of those used in
[
2
]
when applied to (
1.1
),
which is a particular case of the problem from
[
2
]
.
These lemmas are necessary even if there are no improvements of the estimates at this stage.

Lemma 3.8

(
T_{i}
is well defined for
i=1,\ldots,N
)
.

If
Hk<\sqrt{2}
, then
b_{\Omega_{i}}(\cdot,\cdot)
is positive definite on
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{i})
and
the operators
T_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,N
are well defined.

Proof.

Using the definition of
b_{\Omega_{i}}(\cdot,\cdot)
and the Friedrichs inequality we obtain

b_{\Omega_{i}}(u,u)=a_{\Omega_{i}}(u,u)-k^{2}(u,u)_{\Omega_{i}}\geq\frac{2}{H^{2}}\|u\|_{\Omega_{i}}^{2}-k^{2}\|u\|_{\Omega_{i}}^{2}=\frac{2-H^{2}k^{2}}{H^{2}}\|u\|_{\Omega_{i}}^{2}.

If the condition
Hk<\sqrt{2}
is satisfied, then
b_{\Omega_{i}}(\cdot,\cdot)
is positive definite.
By the Lax–Milgram lemma, this ensures that
T_{i}
is well defined.
∎

Remark 3.9
.

Whilst this a sufficient condition for
T_{i}
to be well defined, it is not a necessary condition.
In general, if
k^{2}
is not an eigenvalue of the matrix corresponding to
a_{\Omega_{i}}
, then
T_{i}
is well defined.

In order to show the robustness of the two-level method additive Schwarz method, it is necessary to prove the stability estimates
for the
T_{i}
operators.

Lemma 3.10

(Stability of
T_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,N
)
.

Suppose that
Hk\leq 1
. Then, for all
u\in V^{h}
,

\|T_{i}u\|_{a_{\Omega_{i}}}\leq 2\|u\|_{a_{\Omega_{i}}}+k\sqrt{2}\|u\|_{\Omega_{i}}.

(3.13)

Proof.

Using the defining relation
b_{\Omega_{i}}(T_{i}u,T_{i}u)=b_{\Omega_{i}}(u,T_{i}u)
and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality we obtain

\displaystyle\|T_{i}u\|^{2}_{a_{\Omega_{i}}}

\displaystyle=a_{\Omega_{i}}(T_{i}u,T_{i}u)=b_{\Omega_{i}}(T_{i}u,T_{i}u)+k^{2}(T_{i}u,T_{i}u)_{\Omega_{i}}

\displaystyle=b_{\Omega_{i}}(u,T_{i}u)+k^{2}(T_{i}u,T_{i}u)_{\Omega_{i}}

\displaystyle=a_{\Omega_{i}}(u,T_{i}u)-k^{2}(u,T_{i}u)_{\Omega_{i}}+k^{2}(T_{i}u,T_{i}u)_{\Omega_{i}}

\displaystyle\leq\|u\|_{a_{\Omega_{i}}}\|T_{i}u\|_{a_{\Omega_{i}}}+k^{2}\|u\|_{\Omega_{i}}\|T_{i}u\|_{\Omega_{i}}+k^{2}\|T_{i}u\|^{2}_{\Omega_{i}}

\displaystyle\leq\|u\|_{a_{\Omega_{i}}}\|T_{i}u\|_{a_{\Omega_{i}}}+k^{2}\frac{H}{\sqrt{2}\,}\|u\|_{\Omega_{i}}\|T_{i}u\|_{a_{\Omega_{i}}}+k^{2}\frac{H^{2}}{2}\|T_{i}u\|^{2}_{a_{\Omega_{i}}}.

After simplification, this can be rewritten as

\biggl(1-k^{2}\frac{H^{2}}{2}\biggr)\|T_{i}u\|_{a_{\Omega_{i}}}\leq\|u\|_{a_{\Omega_{i}}}+k^{2}\frac{H}{\sqrt{2}\,}\|u\|_{\Omega_{i}}.

Together with the assumption
Hk\leq 1
, this implies the claimed inequality (
3.13
).
∎

Lemma 3.11

(

Proof.

Assume that there exists a

(3.15)

Let

\varepsilon>0

later.

2.4
implies that there exists

\displaystyle=b(w_{h},w_{0})=b(w_{h},w_{0})-b(z,w_{0})=b(w_{h}-z,w_{0})

\displaystyle\leq\|w_{h}-z\|_{a}\|w_{0}\|_{a}+\frac{1}{2}\|w_{0}\|^{2}

Combined with Lemma
3.5
this yields

\|w_{0}\|^{2}\leq 2\Lambda\Theta^{\frac{1}{2}}\|w_{0}\|_{a}\|w_{h}\|_{a}.

(3.16)

Choosing

) we have

0=b(w_{0},w_{0})=\|w_{0}\|^{2}_{a}-k^{2}\|w_{0}\|^{2}

and hence

. Together with (

) this gives

(3.18)

We can combine (
2.3
) and (

\displaystyle\|w_{h}\|_{a}

\displaystyle\leq\|w\|_{a}+\|w-w_{h}\|_{a}\leq\|w\|_{a}+a_{\textup{{max}}}\|w-w_{h}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}

if

.
We now obtain from (
3.19
) with (

With the assumption (

) this leads to a contradiction.
∎

Another requirement is to find the stability conditions for

Lemma 3.12

(Stability of

)

Proof.

This is the same procedure as in

\displaystyle\bigl(4(1+\Lambda^{2}\Theta)\bigr)^{-1}\|u\|_{a}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq a(Pu,u)=a\Biggl(\sum_{i=0}^{N}E_{i}P_{i}u,u\Biggr)=\sum_{i=0}^{N}a(E_{i}P_{i}u,u)

(4.3)

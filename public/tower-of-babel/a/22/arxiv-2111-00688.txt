Three Favorite Edges Occurs Infinitely Often for One-Dimensional Simple Random Walk
Chen-Xu Hao, Ze-Chun Hu, Ting Ma, Renming Song

Abstract




Abstract:
For a one-dimensional simple symmetric random walk
(S_{n})
,
an edge
x
(between points
x-1
and
x
) is called a favorite edge at time
n
if its local time at
n
achieves the maximum among all edges.
In this paper, we show that with probability 1 three favorite edges occurs infinitely often. Our work is inspired by Tóth and Werner [Combin. Probab. Comput.
6
(1997) 359-369], and Ding and Shen [Ann. Probab.
46
(2018) 2545-2561],
disproves a conjecture mentioned in Remark 1 on page 368 of Tóth and Werner [Combin. Probab. Comput.
6
(1997) 359-369].

Keywords and phrases:
Random walk, favorite edge, invariance principle for one-side local times, Wiener process.

Mathematics Subject Classification (2020)
60F15, 60J55.

1
Introduction

Let
(S_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

be a one-dimensional simple symmetric random walk with
S_{0}=0
. Following Tóth and Werner
[
35
]
, we define for any
i\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\widetilde{S}_{i}:=\dfrac{S_{i}+S_{i-1}+1}{2},

which characterizes the edge of
i
-th jump (edge
x
is between points
x-1
and
x
), and also define the “local time on the edge
x
at time
n
” as follows:

\displaystyle L(x,n):=\#\left\{1\leq j\leq n:\widetilde{S}_{j}=x\right\}.

(1.1)

Hereafter,
\#D
denotes the cardinality of the set
D
.
An edge
x
is called a favorite (or most visited) edge of the random walk at time
n
if

L(x,n)=\sup_{y\in{\mathbb{Z}}}L(y,n).

The set of favorite edges of the random walk at time
n
is denoted by
\mathcal{K}(n)
.
(\mathcal{K}(n))_{n\geq 1}
is called the favorite edge process of the one-dimensional simple symmetric random walk.
We say that
three favorite edges
occurs at time
n
if
\#\mathcal{K}(n)=3
.

Theorem 1.1
.

For one-dimensional simple symmetric random walk, with probability
1
, three favorite edges occurs infinitely often.

Theorem
1.1
complements the result in
[
35
]
which showed that eventually there are no more than three favorite edges, and disproves a conjecture
mentioned in
[
35
, Remark 1 on page 368]
.

For the related problem of the number of favorite sites of one-dimensional simple symmetric random walks, there are many more references (see Shi and Tóth
[
33
]
for an overview).
This problem was posed by Erdős and Révész
[
16
,
17
,
18
,
32
]
. Tóth
[
34
]
proved that there are no more than three favorite sites eventually. Ding and Shen
[
12
]
proved that with probability 1 three favorite sites occurs infinitely often.

Besides the number of favorite sites, a series of papers focus on the asymptotic behavior of favorite sites, see
[
3
,
7
,
8
,
28
]
.
In addition, there are a number of papers
on favorite sites for other processes
including Brownian motion, symmetric stable process, Lévy processes, random walks in random environments and so on, see
[
2
,
4
,
13
,
14
,
15
,
20
,
21
,
24
,
26
,
27
,
29
]
.

For papers on favorite sites of simple random walks in higher dimensions,
we refer to
[
1
,
10
,
11
,
30
]
.

Our proof of Theorem
1.1
is inspired by
[
12
]
, which in turn was inspired by
[
34
,
35
]
. Following
[
34
]
, we define the number of upcrossings and downcrossings
of the site
x
by time
n
to be

\displaystyle\xi_{U}(x,n):=\#\{0<k\leq n:S_{k}=x,S_{k-1}=x-1\},

\displaystyle\xi_{D}(x,n):=\#\{0<k\leq n:S_{k}=x,S_{k-1}=x+1\}

respectively. It is easy to check that

\displaystyle\xi_{U}(x,n)-\xi_{D}(x-1,n)=\textbf{1}_{\{0<x\leq S_{n}\}}-\textbf{1}_{\{S_{n}<x\leq 0\}}.

(1.2)

Using (
1.1
) and (
1.2
), we can easily get (see

[
34
]
)

\displaystyle L(x,n)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\#\left\{0<j\leq n:\widetilde{S}_{j}=x\right\}

(1.3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\#\left\{0<j\leq n:S_{j}=x,S_{j-1}=x-1\right\}+\#\left\{0<j\leq n:S_{j}=x-1,S_{j-1}=x\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\xi_{U}(x,n)+\xi_{D}(x-1,n)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\xi_{U}(x,n)+\textbf{1}_{\{S_{n}<x\leq 0\}}-\textbf{1}_{\{0<x\leq S_{n}\}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\xi_{D}(x-1,n)+\textbf{1}_{\{0<x\leq S_{n}\}}-\textbf{1}_{\{S_{n}<x\leq 0\}}.

For
r\geq 1
, let
f(r)
be the (possibly infinite) number of times when there are exactly
r
favorite edges:

\displaystyle f(r):=\#\left\{n\geq 1:\#\mathcal{K}(n)=r\right\}.

We will show that

\displaystyle f(3)=\infty\ \ \mbox{with probability}\ 1,

(1.4)

which implies Theorem
1.1
. The idea for the proof comes from
[
12
]
.
In the proof, we need a transience result for the favorite edge process of one-dimensional simple symmetric random walks. To prove this transience result, we establish an invariance principle for one-side local times of random walks.

We mention in passing that, similar to
[
12
,
34
]
, we could have defined
the following quantity related
f(r)
:

\widetilde{f}(r):=\#\left\{n\geq 1:\widetilde{S}_{n}\in\mathcal{K}(n)\mbox{ and }\#\mathcal{K}(n)=r\right\},

which is the number of times at which a new favorite
edge appears, tied with
r-1

other favorite edges. Using the recurrence of
one-dimensional simple symmetric random walk, one can easily see that

f(3)=\infty
a.s. is equivalent to
\widetilde{f}(3)=\infty
a.s.
See for instance, the paragraph below
[
35
, Theorem 1.1]
.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we
establish an invariance principle for one-side local times of random walks,
and then use it to prove the transience of the favorite edge process.
In Section 3, we set up the framework for our proof for Theorem
1.1
. In Section 4, we give the proof of Theorem
1.1
. We emphasize that the idea for the proof of Theorem
1.1
comes from
[
12
]
, and
our main contributions are the invariance principle for one-side local times and the transience of the favorite edge process. In final section, we give a remark.

2
The transience of the favorite edge process

In this section, we study the transience of
the favorite edge process
and prove the following:

Theorem 2.1
.

For any
\gamma>11
, we have

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\tilde{U}(n)}{\sqrt{n}(\log n)^{-\gamma}}=\infty\qquad a.s.,

(2.1)

where
\tilde{U}(n):=\min\{|x|:x\in\mathcal{K}(n)\}
.

To prove the above theorem, we will establish an invariance principle
for one-side local times of random walks in Section 2.2. The proof of Theorem
2.1
will be given in Section 2.3. In Section 2.1, we give a brief introduction for invariance principle for (two-side) local times.

2.1
Invariance principle for local times

Recall that the (site) local time process of the random walk
(S_{n},n\geq 0)
is defined by

\displaystyle\xi(x,n):=\#\left\{k:1\leq k\leq n,S_{k}=x\right\},\ x\in\mathbb{Z},\ n\geq 1.

Define

\xi^{*}(n):=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{Z}}\xi(x,n).

Let
(W(t))_{t\geq 0}
be a one-dimensional standard Brownian motion (Wiener process).
Recall that the local time process
(\eta(x,t))_{t\geq 0,x\in\mathbb{R}}
of
(W(t))_{t\geq 0}
is defined by

\displaystyle\eta(x,t):=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{t}\textbf{1}_{(x-\varepsilon,x+\varepsilon)}(W(s))ds=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{t}\textbf{1}_{[x,x+\varepsilon)}(W(s))ds.

(2.2)

Révész
[
31
]
established
the following strong invariance principle with a rate of convergence: On a rich enough probability space, as
n\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathbb{Z}}|\xi(x,n)-\eta(x,n)|=o(n^{\frac{1}{4}+\varepsilon})\quad a.s.

(2.3)

for any
\varepsilon>0
. Csörgő and Horváth

[
9
, Theorems 1 and 2]

showed that the Révész’s result is the best possible.

2.2
Invariance principle for one-side local times

Define the one-side local time of the Wiener process by

\eta_{R}(x,t):=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{t}\textbf{1}_{[x,x+\varepsilon)}\big(W(s)\big)ds.

Then by (
2.2
), we get that
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
t\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\eta_{R}(x,t)=\frac{1}{2}\eta(x,t).

(2.4)

The goal in this subsection is to extend (
2.3
) to the one-side case, that is, prove the following result.

Theorem 2.2
.

On a rich enough probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)
, one can define a Wiener process
(W(t))_{t\geq 0}
and one-dimensional simple symmetric random walk
(S_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
with
S_{0}=0
, such that for any
\varepsilon>0
, as
n\to\infty
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathbb{Z}}|\xi_{D}(x,n)-\eta_{R}(x,n)|=o(n^{\frac{1}{4}+\varepsilon})\ ~~~~a.s.

Before giving the proof of Theorem
2.2
, we prove the following lemma.

Lemma 2.3
.

For any
\varepsilon>0
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sup\limits_{x\in\mathbb{Z}}|\xi_{D}(x+1,n)-\xi_{D}(x,n)|}{n^{\frac{1}{4}+\varepsilon}}=0\quad a.s.

(2.5)

Proof.
The idea of the proof comes from Csáki and Révész
[
6
, Lemma 5]
, where the corresponding problem for
(two-side) local times was considered in a more general setting.
First we show that, for all
m\geq 2
and all
\delta>0
, there exists
a constant
C

(which may depends on
m
but not on
n
) such that

\displaystyle E(|\xi_{D}(1,n)-\xi_{D}(0,n)|^{m})\leq Cn^{\frac{m}{4}+\delta}.

(2.6)

Define

\displaystyle T_{k}:=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\xi_{D}(1,\alpha_{i})-\xi_{D}(1,\alpha_{i-1})-1\right),

(2.7)

where
\alpha_{0}=0
,
\alpha_{i}=\min\{j>\alpha_{i-1}:S_{j}=0,S_{j-1}=1\},\forall i\geq 1
.
We claim that

\displaystyle E(\xi_{D}(1,\alpha_{1}))=1,\quad E(\xi_{D}^{m}(1,\alpha_{1}))<+\infty,\forall m\geq 2.

(2.8)

To prove this claim, we follow Kesten and Spitzer
[
23
, Lemma 2]
and define,
for
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\tau_{0}(x):=0,\tau_{i}(x):=\min\{n>\tau_{i-1}(x):S_{n}=x,S_{n-1}=x+1\},\forall i\geq 1
. Note that
\alpha_{i}=\tau_{i}(0)
. For
j\geq 0
,

\displaystyle M_{j}(x,y):=\sum_{\tau_{j}(x)<n\leq\tau_{j+1}(x)}\textbf{1}_{\{S_{n}=y,S_{n-1}=y+1\}}

is the number of downcrossings to
y
between the
j^{th}
and
(j+1)^{th}
downcrossings to
x
.
In particular,
M_{0}(0,1)=\xi_{D}(1,\alpha_{1})
. By the strong Markov property, we know that the distribution of

M_{j}(0,y)
,
j\geq 0
, is independent of
j
, and the distribution of
M_{j}(x,y)
,
j\geq 1
, is independent of
j
.
Thus we can
define for
j\geq 1
,

\displaystyle p(x,y)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle P(M_{j}(x,y)\neq 0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P(S_{n}=y,S_{n-1}=y+1~\mbox{for some}\ n\ \mbox{with~}\tau_{j}(x)<n\leq\tau_{j+1}(x)).

We claim that, for any
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}
with
|x-y|=1
, it holds that
p(x,y)=p(y,x)=1/2
. To prove this claim,
it suffices to show that
p(0,1)=p(1,0)=1/2
.
When
\tau_{1}(0)<\infty
, there exists
n_{0}=\min\{k\geq 0:S_{k}=1\}<\tau_{1}(0)
. Then
S_{n_{0}}=1
,
\{S_{n_{0}+1}=0\}\subset\{M_{0}(0,1)=0\}

and
\{S_{n_{0}+1}=2\}\subset\{M_{0}(0,1)\neq 0\}
. Since
P(\tau_{1}(0)<\infty)=1
, we have

\displaystyle p(0,1)=P(M_{0}(0,1)\neq 0,\tau_{1}(0)<\infty)

\displaystyle=P(S_{n_{0}+1}=0,M_{0}(0,1)\neq 0,\tau_{1}(0)<\infty)+P(S_{n_{0}+1}=2,M_{0}(0,1)\neq 0,\tau_{1}(0)<\infty)

\displaystyle=P(S_{n_{0}+1}=2)=\frac{1}{2}.

Similarly, there exists
n_{1}=\min\{k\geq 0:S_{k}=1,S_{k-1}=2\}<\tau_{1}(1)
, then

\displaystyle p(1,0)=P(S_{n_{1}+1}=2,M_{1}(1,0)\neq 0)+P(S_{n_{1}+1}=0,M_{1}(1,0)\neq 0)

\displaystyle=P(S_{n_{1}+1}=0)=\frac{1}{2}.

Combining the claim above with the strong Markov property and the fact
S_{\tau_{j}(x)}=S_{\tau_{j+1}(x)}=x
, we get

P(M_{j}(0,1)=k)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1-p(0,1)=\frac{1}{2},&\mbox{if}\ k=0,\\
p(0,1)[1-p(1,0)]^{k-1}p(1,0)=\frac{1}{2^{k+1}},&\text{if}\ k\geq 1.\end{array}\right.

It follows that

\displaystyle E(\xi_{D}(1,\alpha_{1}))=E(M_{j}(0,1))=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k\cdot\frac{1}{2^{k+1}}=1,

\displaystyle E(\xi_{D}^{m}(1,\alpha_{1}))=E(M_{j}^{m}(0,1))=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k^{m}\cdot\frac{1}{2^{k+1}}<+\infty,

which implies that (
2.8
) holds.

It follows from the strong Markov property
that
T_{k}
is a sum of i.i.d. r.v.’s with mean
0
and
finite moments of all orders.
Therefore by Chung
[
5
]
and the
L^{p}
-maximal inequality for martingales, we have

\displaystyle E(|T_{k}|^{m})\leq C_{1}k^{\frac{m}{2}}

(2.9)

and for any
\delta_{1}>0
,

\displaystyle E\left(\max_{k\leq n^{\frac{1}{2}+\delta_{1}}}|T_{k}|^{m}\right)\leq C_{2}n^{\frac{m}{4}+\frac{m\delta_{1}}{2}}.

(2.10)

Note that

\displaystyle\xi_{D}(1,\alpha_{D_{n}})-\xi_{D}(0,n)\leq\xi_{D}(1,n)-\xi_{D}(0,n)\leq\xi_{D}(1,\alpha_{D_{n}+1})-(\xi_{D}(0,n)+1)+1,

where
D_{n}=\xi_{D}(0,n)
. Thus
on the event
\{D_{n}+1\leq n^{\frac{1}{2}+\delta_{1}}\}
, we have

\displaystyle|\xi_{D}(1,n)-\xi_{D}(0,n)|\leq\max_{1\leq k\leq n^{\frac{1}{2}+\delta_{1}}}|T_{k}|+1.

Hence

\displaystyle E(|\xi_{D}(1,n)-\xi_{D}(0,n)|^{m})\leq CE\left(\max_{1\leq k\leq n^{\frac{1}{2}+\delta_{1}}}(|T_{k}|+1)^{m}\right)+n^{m}P\left(D_{n}+1\geq n^{\frac{1}{2}+\delta_{1}}\right).

(2.11)

Let
N_{n}=N(0,n)=\#\{k:0<k\leq n,S_{k}=0\}
, then according to Kesten and Spitzer
[
23
]
,
E(N_{n}^{m})=O(n^{\frac{m}{2}})
. Combining this with the fact that
0\leq D_{n}\leq N_{n}
, we get that
E(D_{n}^{m})=O(n^{\frac{m}{2}})
.
Then by Markov’s inequality, (
2.10
) and (
2.11
),
letting
\delta_{1}
be small enough, we obtain (
2.6
).

By repeating the argument above and noticing that

p(-1,0)=p(0,-1)=\frac{1}{2}
, we can obtain the inequality (
2.6
) with
\xi_{D}(1,n)
replaced by
\xi_{D}(-1,n)
.

It is easy to see that for any
x\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\displaystyle E(|\xi_{D}(x+1,n)-\xi_{D}(x,n)|^{m})\leq Cn^{\frac{m}{4}+\delta},

(2.12)

where the constant
C
does not depend on
x
, because
\xi_{D}(x+1,n)-\xi_{D}(x,n)
is stochastically smaller than
\max(|\xi_{D}(1,n)-\xi_{D}(0,n)|,|\xi_{D}(-1,n)-\xi_{D}(0,n)|)
.

Choosing
m=\frac{2+\delta}{\varepsilon}
, we obtain by Markov’s inequality that

\displaystyle P\Big(|\xi_{D}(x+1,n)-\xi_{D}(x,n)|>n^{\frac{1}{4}+\varepsilon}\Big)\leq\dfrac{C}{n^{2}}

and hence

\displaystyle P\left(\sup_{|x|<(n\log n)^{\frac{1}{2}}}|\xi_{D}(x+1,n)-\xi_{D}(x,n)|>n^{\frac{1}{4}+\varepsilon}\right)\leq\dfrac{2C(\log n)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{n^{\frac{3}{2}}}.

Therefore, by the Borel-Cantelli Lemma, we get that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}\dfrac{\sup_{|x|\leq(n\log n)^{\frac{1}{2}}}|\xi_{D}(x+1,n)-\xi_{D}(x,n)|}{n^{\frac{1}{4}+\varepsilon}}=0~~~~a.s.

The law of the iterated logarithm for
S_{n}
implies that
\xi_{D}(x,n)=0~~a.s.
for
|x|\geq(n\log n)^{\frac{1}{2}}
and
n
sufficiently large, hence (
2.5
) holds.

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.

This proof is inspired by Révész
[
31
]
, and Csörgő and Horváth
[
9
]
.
Let
(W(t))_{t\geq 0}
be a Wiener process and define
\tau_{0}:=0,\tau_{1}:=\inf\{t:t>0,|W(t)|=1\},\tau_{n}:=\inf\{t:t>\tau_{n-1},|W(t)-W(\tau_{n-1})|=1\},\forall n\geq 2
. Then
X_{i}=W(\tau_{i})-W(\tau_{i-1})(i\geq 1)
are i.i.d. r.v.’s with
P\{X_{i}=1\}=P\{X_{i}=-1\}=1/2
, and
\tau_{i}-\tau_{i-1}(i\geq 1)
are i.i.d. r.v.’s with
E(\tau_{i}-\tau_{i-1})=1
and
E(\tau_{i}-\tau_{i-1})^{2}<\infty
. Put
\sigma^{2}=E(\tau_{1}-1)^{2}
.
Define
S_{k}=X_{1}+\cdots+X_{k}=W(\tau_{k})
.

Let
a_{i}(x)=\eta(x,\tau_{v(i)+1})-\eta(x,\tau_{v(i)-1}),b_{i}(x)=\eta_{R}(x,\tau_{v(i)+1})-\eta_{R}(x,\tau_{v(i)-1})(i\in\mathbb{Z}^{+})
, where

v(1)=\min\{k\geq 0,W(\tau_{k})(=S_{k})=x\},v(n)=\min\{k>v(n-1),W(\tau_{k})(=S_{k})=x\},\forall n\geq 2
.
Then by (
2.4
), we have

\displaystyle b_{i}(x)=\frac{a_{i}(x)}{2}.

(2.13)

Kesten
[
22
]
showed

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}(2n\log\log n)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\sup_{x\in\mathbb{Z}}\xi(x,n)=1~~~~~a.s.

(2.14)

Csörgő and Horváth
[
9
, (2.7)]
says that

\displaystyle\max_{-n\leq x\leq n}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{\xi(x,n)}a_{i}(x)-\eta(x,\tau_{n})\right|=O(\log n)\quad a.s.,

which together with (
2.4
) and (
2.13
) implies that

\displaystyle\max_{-n\leq x\leq n}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{\xi(x,n)}b_{i}(x)-\eta_{R}(x,\tau_{n})\right|=O(\log n)~~~~~a.s.

(2.15)

By
[
9
, (2.8)]
and (
2.13
), we have

\displaystyle P\left\{\max_{-k\leq x\leq k}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(b_{i}(x)-\frac{1}{2}\right)\right|>C_{1}(k\log k)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\}\leq C_{2}k^{-2}.

(2.16)

Combining this with the Borel-Cantelli Lemma and then using (
2.14
), we get that

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\max\limits_{-n\leq x\leq n}|\sum_{i=1}^{\xi(x,n)}b_{i}(x)-\frac{\xi(x,n)}{2}|}{n^{\frac{1}{4}}\cdot(\log n)^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot(\log\log n)^{\frac{1}{4}}}\leq C_{3}~~~~~a.s.

(2.17)

Since

\tau_{i}-\tau_{i-1},i\geq 1,
are i.i.d. r.v.’s with
E\tau_{1}=1
and
\sigma^{2}=E(\tau_{1}-1)^{2}<\infty
, by the law of iterated logarithm, we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{|\tau_{n}-n|}{\sqrt{2\sigma^{2}n\log\log n}}=1~~~~~a.s.

(2.18)

By
[
9
, (2.11)]
and (
2.4
), we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{\sup\limits_{x}|\eta_{R}(x,n\pm g(n))-\eta_{R}(x,n)|}{n^{\frac{1}{4}}\cdot(\log n)^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot(\log\log n)^{\frac{1}{4}}}\leq C_{4}\quad a.s.,

where
g(n)=(4\sigma^{2}n\log\log n)^{\frac{1}{2}}
.
(Note that there is a minor typo in the line below
[
9
, (2.11)]
, the
-\frac{1}{2}
there should be
\frac{1}{2}
.) Thus
by (
2.18
) we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{\sup\limits_{x}|\eta_{R}(x,\tau_{n})-\eta_{R}(x,n)|}{n^{\frac{1}{4}}\cdot(\log n)^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot(\log\log n)^{\frac{1}{4}}}\leq C_{5}~~~~~a.s.

(2.19)

Note that
\xi_{D}(x,n)=0
if
|x|>n
and that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{|x|>n}\eta_{R}(x,n)=0\ a.s.
Thus

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{|x|>n}\dfrac{\xi_{D}(x,n)-\eta_{R}(x,n)}{n^{\frac{1}{4}+\varepsilon}}=0~~~~~a.s.

(2.20)

Now by (
2.15
), (
2.17
), (
2.19
), (
2.20
) and Lemma
2.3
, we obtain

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{\sup\limits_{x}|\xi_{D}(x,n)-\eta_{R}(x,n)|}{n^{\frac{1}{4}+\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{\sup\limits_{x\leq n}|\xi_{D}(x,n)-\frac{\xi(x,n)}{2}|+\sup\limits_{x\leq n}|\frac{\xi(x,n)}{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{\xi(x,n)}b_{i}(x)|}{n^{\frac{1}{4}+\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle\quad+\limsup_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{\sup\limits_{x\leq n}|\sum_{i=1}^{\xi(x,n)}b_{i}(x)-\eta_{R}(x,\tau_{n})|+\sup\limits_{x\leq n}|\eta_{R}(x,\tau_{n})-\eta_{R}(x,n)|}{n^{\frac{1}{4}+\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle\quad+\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup\limits_{|x|>n}\dfrac{|\xi_{D}(x,n)-\eta_{R}(x,n)|}{n^{\frac{1}{4}+\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{\sup\limits_{x\leq n}\frac{|\xi_{D}(x,n)-\xi_{D}(x-1,n)|+1}{2}+\sup\limits_{x\leq n}|\frac{\xi(x,n)}{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{\xi(x,n)}b_{i}(x)|}{n^{\frac{1}{4}+\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle\quad+\limsup_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{\sup\limits_{x\leq n}|\sum_{i=1}^{\xi(x,n)}b_{i}(x)-\eta_{R}(x,\tau_{n})|+\sup\limits_{x\leq n}|\eta_{R}(x,\tau_{n})-\eta_{R}(x,n)|}{n^{\frac{1}{4}+\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle\quad+\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup\limits_{|x|>n}\dfrac{|\xi_{D}(x,n)-\eta_{R}(x,n)|}{n^{\frac{1}{4}+\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle=0\ a.s.,

where we used the following fact

\left|\xi_{D}(x,n)-\frac{\xi(x,n)}{2}\right|=\frac{|\xi_{D}(x,n)-\xi_{U}(x,n)|}{2}\leq\frac{|\xi_{D}(x,n)-\xi_{D}(x-1,n)|+1}{2}.

The proof is complete.

In the remainder of this paper, we denote by

The precise definitions of these processes are as follows: Let

\displaystyle P(Z_{n+1}=j|Z_{n}=i)=\rho(i,j):=\pi(i+1,j),

(3.5)

\displaystyle P(R_{n+1}=j|R_{n}=i)=\rho^{*}(i,j):=\pi(i,j-1).

\Delta_{x}^{(k)}(y)\triangleq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}Y_{y}^{{}^{\prime}(k)},&\text{if~$-1\leq y<\infty$},\\

\displaystyle(\xi_{D}(y,T_{U}(x-1,k+1)),y\in\mathbb{Z})\overset{{\rm law}}{=}(\Delta_{x-1}^{(k)}(y),y\in\mathbb{Z}).

4.1

The following is the counterpart to

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose that
Z_{0}=k\in K_{h}=[\frac{h-2\sqrt{h}}{2},\frac{h-\sqrt{h}}{2}]
. Then there exists a constant
c>0
such that for
any
h>4

E\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\tau_{\frac{h-1}{2}}}\frac{h/2-Z_{n}}{h/2}\right)\geq c\sqrt{h}.

Proof.
Let
M_{n}=\sum_{s=1}^{n}(Z_{s}-s)-n(Z_{n}-n)
, and let
\mathcal{F}_{n}=\sigma(Z_{0},Z_{1},...,Z_{n})
. By the proof of
[
12
, Lemma 3.1]
, we know that
(M_{n})
is a martingale. By the optional stopping theorem, we get that

E\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\tau_{\frac{h-1}{2}}}(Z_{n}-n)\right)=E\tau_{\frac{h-1}{2}}(Z_{\tau_{\frac{h-1}{2}}}-\tau_{\frac{h-1}{2}}),

and thus

\displaystyle E\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\tau_{\frac{h-1}{2}}}\frac{h/2-Z_{n}}{h/2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E\tau_{\frac{h-1}{2}}-\dfrac{E\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\tau_{\frac{h-1}{2}}}(Z_{n}-n)+n\right)}{h/2}

(4.1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E\tau_{\frac{h-1}{2}}-\dfrac{E\tau_{\frac{h-1}{2}}(Z_{\tau_{\frac{h-1}{2}}}-\tau_{\frac{h-1}{2}})}{h/2}-\dfrac{E\frac{(1+\tau_{\frac{h-1}{2}})\tau_{\frac{h-1}{2}}}{2}}{h/2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{1}{h}\right)E\tau_{\frac{h-1}{2}}-\frac{2}{h}E\left[\tau_{\frac{h-1}{2}}Z_{\tau_{\frac{h-1}{2}}}-\frac{1}{2}\tau_{\frac{h-1}{2}}^{2}\right].

Define the process
M_{n}^{\prime}=-\frac{1}{4}Z_{n}^{2}+nZ_{n}-\frac{1}{2}n^{2}+\frac{1}{4}n
. By the proof of
[
12
, Lemma 3.1]
, we know that
(M_{n}^{\prime})
is a martingale.
Applying the optional stopping theorem to
(M_{n}^{\prime})
at
\tau_{\frac{h-1}{2}}
,
we get

\displaystyle E\left[\tau_{\frac{h-1}{2}}Z_{\tau_{\frac{h-1}{2}}}-\frac{1}{2}\tau_{\frac{h-1}{2}}^{2}\right]=E\left[\frac{1}{4}Z_{\tau_{\frac{h-1}{2}}}^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\tau_{\frac{h-1}{2}}\right]-\frac{Z_{0}^{2}}{4}=\frac{1}{4}E\left[Z_{\tau_{\frac{h-1}{2}}}^{2}-\tau_{\frac{h-1}{2}}\right]-\frac{Z_{0}^{2}}{4}.

(4.2)

Combining (
4.1
), (
4.2
) and Lemma
3.2
, we get

\displaystyle E\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\tau_{\frac{h-1}{2}}}\frac{h/2-Z_{n}}{h/2}\right)=\left(1-\frac{1}{2h}\right)E\tau_{\frac{h-1}{2}}-\frac{1}{2h}\left(EZ_{\tau_{\frac{h-1}{2}}}^{2}-Z_{0}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{1}{2h}\right)E\left[Z_{\tau_{\frac{h-1}{2}}}-Z_{0}\right]-\frac{1}{2h}E\left[(Z_{\tau_{\frac{h-1}{2}}}-Z_{0})(Z_{\tau_{\frac{h-1}{2}}}+Z_{0})\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2h}E\left[\left(2h-1-(Z_{\tau_{\frac{h-1}{2}}}+Z_{0})\right)(Z_{\tau_{\frac{h-1}{2}}}-Z_{0})\right].

(4.3)

Obviously
Z_{\tau_{\frac{h-1}{2}}}-Z_{0}\geq\frac{h-1}{2}-\frac{h-\sqrt{h}}{2}\geq c_{1}\sqrt{h}
. Then by Lemmas
3.2
and
3.3
, we obtain

\displaystyle E\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\tau_{\frac{h-1}{2}}}\frac{h/2-Z_{n}}{h/2}\right)\geq\frac{1}{2h}\cdot\left(2h-1-\frac{h-1}{2}-c_{2}\sqrt{h}-\frac{h-\sqrt{h}}{2}\right)\cdot c_{1}\sqrt{h}\geq c\sqrt{h}.

(4.4)

Proposition 4.2
.

There exists
c>0
such that
EN_{H}\geq c\log H
for all

H\in[50,\infty)
.

Proof.
By the Ray-Knight representation, (
3.2
) and (
3.12
), we know that

\displaystyle EN_{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{h=8}^{H}\sum_{k\in K_{2h}}P\left(Y_{0}^{(k)}=Y_{1}^{(k)}=Y_{2}^{(k)}=h,\left\{Y_{n}^{(k)}<h,\forall n\geq 3\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\cdot\sum_{x=2}^{+\infty}P\left(\left\{Z_{n}^{(k)}<h-\frac{1}{2},1\leq n\leq x-2\right\},\left\{Y_{n}^{{}^{\prime}(k)}<h,\forall n\geq 1\right\}\right).

It follows that

\displaystyle EN_{H}

(4.5)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{h=50}^{H}\sum_{k\in K_{2h}}P\left(Y_{0}^{(k)}=Y_{1}^{(k)}=Y_{2}^{(k)}=h,\left\{Y_{3}^{(k)}\in\left(h-5\sqrt{2h},h-\frac{\sqrt{2h}}{2}\right),Y_{n}^{(k)}<h,\forall n\geq 4\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\cdot\sum_{x=2}^{+\infty}P\left(\left\{Z_{n}^{(k)}<h-\frac{1}{2},1\leq n\leq x-2\right\},\left\{Y_{n}^{{}^{\prime}(k)}<h,n\geq 1\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{h=50}^{H}\sum_{k\in K_{2h}}\pi(k,h)\cdot\pi(h,h)\cdot\pi(h,h)\cdot\sum_{m\in(h-5\sqrt{2h},h-\frac{\sqrt{2h}}{2})}\pi(h,m)P\left(Y_{n}^{(m)}<h,\forall n\geq 1\right)

\displaystyle\cdot\sum_{x=2}^{+\infty}P\left(\tau_{h-\frac{1}{2}}\geq x-1,\left\{Y_{n}^{{}^{\prime}(k)}<h,\forall n\geq 1\right\}\right).

By Lemma
3.1
, all the
\pi(\cdot,\cdot)
’s in the display above are of the order
h^{-\frac{1}{2}}
. Since
Y_{n}
is a martingale,
applying the optional stopping theorem at
\sigma_{h}\land\omega
,
where
\sigma_{h}
and
\omega
are defined in (
3.11
), we
get for
m\in(h-5\sqrt{2h},h-\frac{\sqrt{2h}}{2})
,

\displaystyle P\left(Y_{n}^{(m)}<h,\forall n\geq 1\right)=P\left(Y_{n}^{(m)}~\mbox{hits }0\mbox{ before exceeds }h\right)\geq\dfrac{h-m}{h}\geq c_{3}h^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

(4.6)

The last two inequalities hold since

\displaystyle m

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle EY_{\sigma_{h}\land\omega}=E\left(Y_{\sigma_{h}}\textbf{1}_{\{\omega\geq\sigma_{h}\}}\right)+E\left(Y_{\omega}\textbf{1}_{\{\omega<\sigma_{h}\}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle E\left(Y_{\sigma_{h}}\textbf{1}_{\{\omega\geq\sigma_{h}\}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle h\left(1-P\left(Y_{n}^{(m)}~\mbox{hits }0\mbox{ before exceeds }h\right)\right),

which implies that

\displaystyle P\left(Y_{n}^{(m)}~\mbox{hits }0\mbox{ before exceeds }h\right)\geq\dfrac{h-m}{h}\geq c_{3}h^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

(4.7)

By (
4.5
) and (
4.6
), we get

\displaystyle EN_{H}\geq c_{4}\sum_{h=50}^{H}\sum_{k\in K_{2h}}h^{-2}\cdot\sum_{x=2}^{+\infty}P\left(\tau_{h-\frac{1}{2}}\geq x-1,\left\{Y_{n}^{{}^{\prime}(k)}<h,\forall n\geq 1\right\}\right).

(4.8)

By the independence of the processes in the Ray-Knight representation, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{x=2}^{+\infty}P\left(\tau_{h-\frac{1}{2}}\geq x-1,\left\{Y_{n}^{{}^{\prime}(k)}<h,\forall n\geq 1\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{x=2}^{+\infty}\sum_{l=0}^{h-1}P\left(Z_{n}^{(k)}<h-\frac{1}{2}\mbox{ for }1\leq n\leq x-2,Z_{x-1}=l\right)\cdot P\left(Y_{n}^{(l)}~\mbox{hits }0\mbox{ before exceeds }h\right).

By the definitions of
(Z_{n})_{n\geq 0}
and
\tau_{h}
, we obtain

\displaystyle Z_{0}-Z_{\tau_{h-\frac{1}{2}}}\leq h-\frac{\sqrt{2h}}{2}-h+\frac{1}{2}=\frac{1-\sqrt{2h}}{2}\leq 0.

(4.9)

Similar to (
4.7
), we have

P(Y_{n}^{(l)}~\mbox{hits }0\mbox{ before exceeds }h)\geq\frac{h-l}{h}
,
which together with (
4.9
) and Lemma
4.1
implies that

\displaystyle\sum_{x=2}^{+\infty}P\left(\tau_{h-\frac{1}{2}}\geq x-1,\left\{Y_{n}^{{}^{\prime}(k)}<h,\forall n\geq 1\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{x=2}^{+\infty}\sum_{l=0}^{h}P\left(\tau_{h-\frac{1}{2}}\geq x-1,Z_{x-2}=l\right)\cdot\frac{h-l}{h}

\displaystyle=E\left(\sum_{l=0}^{h}\sum_{x=2}^{\tau_{h-\frac{1}{2}}+1}\frac{h-l}{h}\cdot\textbf{1}_{\{Z_{x-2}=l\}}\right)=E\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\tau_{h-\frac{1}{2}}-1}\frac{h-Z_{n}}{h}\right)

\displaystyle\geq E\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\tau_{h-\frac{1}{2}}-1}\frac{h-Z_{n}}{h}\right)+\left(\frac{h-Z_{\tau_{h-\frac{1}{2}}}}{h}-\frac{h-Z_{0}}{h}\right)

\displaystyle=E\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\tau_{h-\frac{1}{2}}}\frac{h-Z_{n}}{h}\right)\geq c_{5}\sqrt{h}.

(4.10)

By (
4.8
) and (
4.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle EN_{H}\geq c_{4}\sum_{h=50}^{H}\sum_{k\in K_{2h}}h^{-2}\cdot c_{5}\sqrt{h}\geq c_{6}\sum_{h=100}^{H}h^{-1}\geq c\log H.

The proof is complete.

4.2
Upper bound on the second moment

In this subsection, we will give an upper bound on the second moment
EN_{H}^{2}
following
[
12
]
. If we use the
N_{H}
defined in (
3.2
)–(
3.1
),
we could not prove the counterparts of
[
12
, Lemmas 3.3, 3.4]
.
To overcome this, we give a variant
\widetilde{N}_{H}
of
N_{H}
with
N_{H}\leq\widetilde{N}_{H}
a.s. In the remainder of this section, we assume
x\geq 2
unless
explicitly mentioned otherwise.

For any positive integer
h
, we define

\displaystyle A_{x,2h}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\left\{\mathcal{K}\left(T_{D}(x-1,h)\right)=\{x,x+1,x+2\},L\left(x,T_{D}(x-1,h)\right)=2h\right\}

(4.11)

and

\displaystyle\widetilde{N}_{H}:=\sum_{h=50}^{H}\sum_{x=2}^{\infty}\textbf{1}_{A_{x,2h}},\quad\widetilde{N}:=\lim_{H\to\infty}\widetilde{N}_{H}=\sum_{h=50}^{\infty}\sum_{x=2}^{\infty}\textbf{1}_{A_{x,2h}}.

We claim that

\displaystyle\cup_{k\in K_{2h}}A_{x,2h}^{(k)}\subset A_{x,2h},

(4.12)

where
A_{x,2h}^{(k)}
is defined by (
3.1
).
Suppose that
\omega\in A_{x,2h}^{(k)}
for some
k\in K_{2h}
. Define

\tilde{T}(\omega):=\{m<T_{U}(x-1,k+1)(\omega):S_{m}=x-1,S_{m-1}=x,\mathcal{K}(m)=\{x,x+1,x+2\},L(x,m)=2h\}.

By the definition of
A_{x,h}^{(k)}
, we know that

\tilde{T}(\omega)
consists of one unique element
t(\omega)
.
Further by (
1.3
), we get that

t(\omega)=T_{D}(x-1,h)(\omega)
. Hence
\omega\in A_{x,2h}
and thus (
4.12
) holds.

By (
4.12
), we get that
N_{H}\leq\widetilde{N}_{H}
a.s.
Now we study the second moment of
\widetilde{N}_{H}
following
[
12
, Section 3.2]
.

Let
D(n)=\big(\xi_{D}(x,n),x\in\mathbb{Z}\big)\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{Z}}
be the random vector that records the number of downcrossings of each site by time
n
.
For
l\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{Z}}
, we use
l(i),i\in\mathbb{Z}
to denote the
i
-th component of
l
. For
l\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{Z}}
, define
B_{x}(l)=\{\exists n<\infty:D(n)=l,S(n-1)=x,S(n)=x-1\}
. Note that if
B_{x}(l)
happens, there exists a unique
n\in\mathbb{N}
, such that
D(n)=l,S(n-1)=x
and
S(n)=x-1
.

Let
\mathcal{P}=\{l:P(B_{x}(l))>0~\mbox{for some}~x\}
. For
\mathcal{Q}\subset\mathcal{P}
, denote
B_{x}(\mathcal{Q})=\bigcup_{l\in\mathcal{Q}}B_{x}(l)
. Then by virtue of (
1.3
), we have

A_{x,2h}=B_{x}(\mathcal{P}_{x,2h})
, where
\mathcal{P}_{x,2h}
is the set of
l\in\mathcal{P}
such that

\displaystyle l(x-1)=l(x)=l(x+1)=h,\ l(i)<h\ \mbox{for all}\ i\neq x-1,x,x+1.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be the family of all subsets of
\mathcal{P}
. For any
x\in\mathbb{Z}
, we define a map
\varphi_{x}:\mathcal{P}\mapsto\mathcal{A}
by

\displaystyle\varphi_{x}(l):=\{l^{*}\in\mathcal{P}:l^{*}(i)<l(i)~\mbox{for}~i=x,x+1,l^{*}(i)=l(i)~\mbox{for}~i\neq x,x+1\}.

Following the argument of
[
12
, Lemma 3.3]
, we can prove

Lemma 4.3
.

Suppose
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
h
is a positive integer.
If
l_{i}^{*}\in\varphi_{x_{i}}(l_{i})
with
l_{i}\in\mathcal{P}_{x_{i},2h}
,
i=1,2
,
we have that
B_{x_{1}}(l_{1}^{*})\cap B_{x_{2}}(l_{2}^{*})=\emptyset
, if
(x_{1},l_{1})\neq(x_{2},l_{2})
. Further, we have
B_{x_{1}}(l_{1}^{*})\cap B_{x_{2}}(l_{2}^{*})=\emptyset
if
(x_{1},l_{1})=(x_{2},l_{2})
but

.

[
12
, Lemma 3.4]
.

Lemma 4.4
.

There exists a constant
c>0
such that for any

,

\displaystyle P\big(B_{x}(\varphi_{x}(l))\big)\geq chP\big(B_{x}(l)\big).

Proof.
We consider

l^{*}\in\varphi_{x}(l)
such that
l^{*}(x)\in[h-\frac{\sqrt{2h}}{2},h)
and
l^{*}(x+1)\in[h-\frac{\sqrt{2h}}{2},h)
.
According to Lemma
3.1

(1)
and
(3)
, there is a constant
c>0
such that

\displaystyle\dfrac{P\big(B_{x}(l^{*})\big)}{P\big(B_{x}(l)\big)}=\dfrac{\pi\big(h,l^{*}(x)\big)\cdot\pi\big(l^{*}(x),l^{*}(x+1)\big)\cdot\pi\big(l^{*}(x+1),l(x+2)\big)}{\pi(h,h)\cdot\pi(h,h)\cdot\pi(h,l(x+2))}\geq 4c.

h/2
of such

4.3
, we get that

Proposition 4.5
.

We have that

We have

4.3
0-1 law

Recall that
N=\lim_{H\to\infty}N_{H}
. First, we show that
N=\infty
with positive probability.

Proposition 4.7
.

There exists a constant
\delta>0
such that

P(N=\infty)\geq\delta
.

Proof.

By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we get that

\displaystyle EN_{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle EN_{H}\textbf{1}_{\{N_{H}>\log\log H\}}+EN_{H}\textbf{1}_{\{N_{H}\leq\log\log H\}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{EN_{H}^{2}\cdot P(N_{H}>\log\log H)}+\log\log H.

4.2
and Corollary

,
we get that there exist constants
c
,

such that

\displaystyle P(N_{H}>\log\log H)\geq\dfrac{(EN_{H}-\log\log H)^{2}}{EN_{H}^{2}}\geq c\dfrac{\log^{2}H}{EN_{H}^{2}}\geq\delta,

for all sufficiently large

. Letting

, we get that
P(N=\infty)\geq\delta
.
∎

Recall the definition of
\tilde{U}(n)
in Theorem
2.1
.
By Theorem

, we know that

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{\tilde{U}(n)}{\sqrt{n}(\log n)^{-\gamma}}=\infty\qquad a.s.,

Using Proposition
4.7

which completes the proof of Theorem

.

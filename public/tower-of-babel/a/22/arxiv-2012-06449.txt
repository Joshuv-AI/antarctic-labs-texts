Maximum principles for stochastic time-changed Volterra games
Giulia di Nunno, Michele Giordano

Abstract
We study a stochastic differential game between two players, controlling a forward stochastic Volterra integral equation (FSVIE). Each player has to optimize his own performance functional which includes a backward stochastic differential equation (BSDE). The dynamics considered are driven by time-changed Lévy noises, with absolutely continuous time-change process. We prove a sufficient maximum principle to characterize Nash equilibria and the related optimal strategies. For this we use techniques of control under partial information, and the non-anticipating stochastic derivative. The zero-sum game is presented as a particular case.



Keywords:
forward backward stochastic Volterra equations; optimal control; partial information; maximum principles; time-change; stochastic derivative

Introduction

Hereafter we consider a type of stochastic games with memory. The goal of each one of the two players
i=1,2
is to find an optimal control
u_{i}
that maximizes his personal performance functional

J_{i}(u):=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}F_{i}(t,u(t),X(t),Y_{i}(t))dt+\varphi_{i}(X(T))+\psi_{i}(Y_{i}(0))\right],

(1.1)

which includes profit rate
F_{i}
, utility function
\phi_{i}
and
\psi_{i}
, associated to recursive utility or risk evaluation. Here
u=(u_{1},u_{2})
, with
u_{i}
representing the control of player
i
. The controlled process
X
follows the forward dynamics of Volterra type :

X(t)=X_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}b(t,s,\lambda_{s},u(s),X(s\shortminus))ds+\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}}\kappa(t,s,z,\lambda_{s},u(s),X(s\shortminus))\mu(dsdz),\ t\in[0,T],

(1.2)

while, for each
i=1,2
,
Y_{i}
is a backward stochastic differential equation (BSDE) with dynamics given by:

\displaystyle Y_{i}(t)

\displaystyle=h_{i}(X(T))-\int_{t}^{T}g_{i}(s,\lambda_{s},u(s),X(s\shortminus),Y_{i}(s\shortminus),\Theta_{i}(s,\cdot))ds

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{t}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Theta_{i}(s,z)\mu(dsdz)+\int_{t}^{T}dM_{i}(s),\quad t\in[0,T].

(1.3)

The noise
\mu
, appearing in (
1.2
) and (
1.3
), is the mixture of a conditional Brownian motion and a conditional centred Poisson random measure. They can be regarded as a time-changed Lévy noises with rate
\lambda
. The inclusion of time-change allows to gain more flexibility in modelling than what is possible with the classical Lévy framework, as pointed out, e.g., in
[
33
,
4
]
. Indeed, time change has been suggested in the study of volatility modelling
[
8
,
7
,
20
,
37
]
, energy models
[
8
]
, default models
[
27
]
and also kinetic theory
[
28
]
. In this work, we deal with a time-change process of the form

\Lambda_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}\lambda_{s}ds,\quad t\in[0,T],

(1.4)

where
\lambda
is the stochastic time-change rate. In this case, the noise
\mu
may loose the independent increment structure proper of Lévy noise and the Markovianity, thus allowing for time dependencies. The above term
M_{i}
, (
i=1,2
) is a martingale orthogonal to
\mu
naturally appearing in the martingale representation theorem when working with noises and information flows that do not have the
perfect
stochastic integral representation property, see e.g.
[
24
]
. Indeed, we recall that Kunita-Watanabe result shows that, given a square integrable martingale
\mathcal{M}
, as integrator, and its natural filtration

\mathbb{F}^{\mathcal{M}}=\left\{\mathcal{F}^{\mathcal{M}}_{t},\ t\in[0,T]\right\},

any square integrable
\mathcal{F}_{T}^{\mathcal{M}}
-measurable random variable
\xi
admits representation

\xi=\xi^{\perp}+\int\varphi\ d\mathcal{M}

(1.5)

by means of a unique stochastic integrand
\varphi
. Here
\xi^{\perp}
is a stochastic remainder orthogonal to all the stochastic integrals with respect to
\mathcal{M}
. It is well known that
\xi^{\perp}
is a constant (naturally equal to
\mathbb{E}[\xi]
) whenever
\mathcal{M}
is a Gaussian or a centered Poisson random measure, or a mixture of the two, and the reference filtration is generated by
\mathcal{M}
, see e.g.
[
14
,
10
,
16
,
19
]
. We say that the representation (
1.5
) is
perfect
if
\xi^{\perp}
is a constant. In this case, the space of all stochastic integrals with respect to
\mathcal{M}
coincides with
L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F}_{T}^{\mathcal{M}},P)
modulo constants. This is tightly connected with chaos expansions (see e.g.
[
21
,
16
]
). The study of general BSDEs of type (
1.3
) can be found in e.g.
[
29
]
.

Each BSDEs in (
1.3
) corresponds to one of the players in the stochastic game and is connected to the FSVIE (
1.2
) in the sense that they depend on
X
and are driven by the same kind of noise.

In view of the dynamics (
1.2
), this work deals with stochastic games with memory. Some examples of papers that consider games with memory are
[
38
,
12
,
36
]
. In both
[
12
]
and
[
38
]
though, the memory effect is obtained by means of a delay whereas in
[
36
]
, similarly to what we do here, the authors consider a forward equation of Volterra type, but work with linear-quadratic stochastic integral games.

In terms of structure of the game studied, our work is in line with the stochastic differential games in as
[
27
,
26
,
12
]
, where the authors consider games based on one forward stochastic differential equation and two backward stochastic differential equations.
Other examples of stochastic games in the literature are the
purely forward
stochastic games (see, e.g.,
[
6
,
34
,
3
]
to cite a few) where there are no backward equations such as (
1.3
) and the
purely backward
stochastic games (see
[
38
]
) where there is no forward equation such as (
1.2
). By considering a system of forward-backward stochastic differential equations we are able to have a setting that is comprehensive of both cases above. Notice that our setting is also more general than the one in the above mentioned
[
27
,
26
,
12
]
as we consider the forward equation (
1.2
) to be of Volterra type and deal with backward equations with an additional martingale term which is due to the more general nature of the noise considered. Lastly, we remark that the structure of the game presented here is different from e.g.
[
36
,
9
]
, where the authors consider systems of forward-backward stochastic differential equations, but exploit the backward equations as a tool to express conditions on the existence of Nash equilibria for the game.

The first element of novelty of this paper with respect to, e.g.,
[
27
,
12
,
26
,
38
]
, is the introduction of the time-changed Lévy process
\mu
as a driver for both the forward and backward dynamics. This allows us to go beyond the classical Brownian motion and pure Lévy framework, by considering some quite general, but still treatable martingales. Moreover, contrarily to
[
27
,
26
,
38
]
we consider forward dynamics with memory, introduced by means of Volterra coefficients, similarly to what is presented in
[
1
,
35
]
and used there for describing coupled systems.

Another novelty introduced in the current work comes from considering the BSDEs under an enlargement of the filtration generated by
\mu
. Then we shall see how the different filtrations introduce different integral representations. This will be exploited in the definition of the adjoint backward equations associated to the maximum principle approach.

Last but not least, differently from
[
12
]
for instance, we do not make use of Malliavin derivatives. In fact, dealing with time-changed processes, we cannot directly apply the standard Malliavin calculus nor its Hida-Malliavin extension, see e.g.
[
21
]
, because the driving noise is not Brownian nor of Poisson type. Still one may think of applying a conditional form of the Malliavin calculus, as presented in
[
37
]
, but this would require to impose some additional condition on the domain of our processes, conditions that would be difficult to characterize since they would depend on the control process itself. At the current time there is no Hida-Malliavin type extension for conditional Malliavin calculus available, hence we cannot use either this approach in our work as it was instead done in the framework of
[
1
]
. For this reason we make use of the
non-anticipating derivative
(NA-derivative) with Itô-type calculus. The NA-derivative, introduced in
[
15
]
for general martingales and then extended to martingale random fields in
[
17
]
, is directly connected to the Itô integral and provides explicit stochastic integral representations. Moreover, being the domain of the NA-derivative the whole
L^{2}(dP)
, we are able to overcome the domain restriction issues that would emerge from using the (conditional) Malliavin calculus. See e.g.
[
17
,
15
]
and also
[
18
]
where the NA-derivative is used in the context of optimal portfolios.

We also put notice that stochastic Volterra games are connected with forward-backward stochastic Volterra integral equations (FBSVIEs). Even though some authors provided some interesting results concerning FBSVIEs (see e.g.
[
1
,
9
,
35
]
), the results in this paper stand out from those in bibliography by providing a different setting. This is achieved both by considering equations driven by a time-changed Lévy noise and by working under different information flows.

In our work, we use partial information techniques. In fact, we work under a filtration
\mathbb{F}\subseteq\mathbb{G}
regarded as
partial
with respect to a filtration
\mathbb{G}
, containing in addition the future values of the time-change process
\Lambda
. Also, similarly to e.g.
[
27
,
3
,
26
]
, we assume that the individual information flows available to each players are different. This is represented by taking two sub-filtrations
\mathcal{E}^{(i)}_{t}\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{t}
,
i=1,2
.

We provide sufficient maximum principle for non-zero sum stochastic games and we present a zero-sum game reformulation of these results as a particular case.

This paper is organized as follows: in Section 2 we present the framework for the study of time-changed Lévy process and we recall the concept of NA-derivative. In Section 3 we describe the optimization problem and provide a sufficient maximum principle for non-zero sum games under both the information flow
\mathbb{F}
and
\mathbb{G}
. As a particular case we also provide the zero-sum game reformulation of such theorem. In Section 4 we provide some utility consumption examples both for the zero and non zero-sum games and show that in some simple cases we can actually manage to derive the optimal control for our forward-backward game. Lastly, in Section 5, we make some conclusive remarks on the use of Volterra-type BSDEs in place of (
1.3
).

2
Time change and the framework

We adopt the framework introduced in
[
20
]
and also exploited in
[
18
]
. Hereafter we summarize the crucial points fundamental for this study.
Let us consider a complete probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)
and define

\mathbb{X}:=[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}=\Big([0,T]\times\{0\}\Big)\cup\Big([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}_{0}\Big),

where
\mathbb{R}_{0}:=\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}
and
\mathcal{B}_{\mathbb{X}}
is the Borel
\sigma
-algebra on
\mathbb{X}
. We define
\mathcal{L}
, the set of stochastic processes
\lambda=(\lambda^{B},\lambda^{H})
such that each component
l=B,H
is a non-negative, stochastically continuous process in
L^{1}(P\times dt)
.
Let
\nu
be a
\sigma
-finite measure on the Borel sets
\mathcal{B}_{\mathbb{R}_{0}}
of
\mathbb{R}_{0}
satisfying
\int_{\mathbb{R}_{0}}z^{2}\nu(dz)<\infty
.
We define the random measure
\Lambda
on
\mathcal{B}_{\mathbb{X}}
by

\Lambda(\Delta):=\int_{0}^{T}\mathds{1}_{\{(t,0)\in\Delta\}}(t)\lambda_{t}^{B}dt+\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}_{0}}\mathds{1}_{\Delta}(t,z)\nu(dz)\lambda_{t}^{H}dt,\quad\Delta\subseteq\mathbb{X},

and denote the
\sigma
-algebra generated by all the values of
\Lambda
by
\mathcal{F}^{\Lambda}
. Also, we define the filtration
\mathbb{F}^{\Lambda}:=\{\mathcal{F}_{t}^{\Lambda},t\in[0,T]\}
with
F_{t}^{\Lambda}
generated by the values
\Lambda(\Delta\cap[0,t])
,
\Delta\subseteq\mathbb{X}
. Set
\mathcal{F}^{\Lambda}:=\mathcal{F}_{T}^{\Lambda}
.

Definition 2.1
.

The signed random measure
\mu
on
\mathcal{B}_{\mathbb{X}}
is defined as the mixture

\mu(\Delta):=B\left(\Delta\cap[0,T]\times\{0\}\right)+\widetilde{H}\left(\Delta\cap[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}_{0}\right),\quad\Delta\subseteq\mathbb{X},

(2.1)

of
B
and
\widetilde{H}
, which are a conditional Gaussian measure (given
\mathcal{F}^{\Lambda}
) and a doubly stochastic centered Poisson random measure (given
\mathcal{F}^{\Lambda}
), respectively. The random measures
B
and
\widetilde{H}
are indeed connected to time-changed Lévy noises as shown in, e.g.,
[
32
]
,
[
23
, Theorem 3.1]
.

In the sequel we consider two types of information flows. The control problem is naturally associated with the filtration generated by the values
\mu(\Delta)
,
\Delta\subset[0,t]\times\mathbb{R}
,
t\in[0,T]
, i.e.
\mathbb{F}^{\mu}:=\{\mathcal{F}_{t}^{\mu},\ t\in[0,T]\}
.
Since the filtration
\mathbb{F}^{\mu}
might not be right-continuous, we consider the information flow

\mathbb{F}:=\{\mathcal{F}_{t},\ t\in[0,T]\},\quad\text{ where }\mathcal{F}_{t}:=\bigcap_{r>t}\mathcal{F}_{r}^{\mu}.

The second information flow of interest is

\mathbb{G}:=\{\mathcal{G}_{t},\ t\in[0,T]\},\quad\text{ where }\mathcal{G}_{t}:=\mathcal{F}_{t}^{\mu}\vee\mathcal{F}^{\Lambda}.

Notice that
\mathbb{G}
is right-continuous and that, while at the horizon
T
we have
\mathcal{G}_{T}=\mathcal{F}_{T}
, the initial information is different in the two flows:
\mathcal{G}_{0}=\mathcal{F}^{\Lambda}
and
\mathcal{F}_{0}
is trivial. Namely
\mathbb{G}
is enlarged of the information on the future values of
\Lambda
. In the sequel we technically exploit the interplay between the two filtrations regarding
\mathbb{F}
as partial with respect to
\mathbb{G}
.

The random measure
\mu
is a martingale random field with values in
L^{2}(dP)
with respect to both filtration
\mathbb{G}
and
\mathbb{F}
, see
[
17
]
and
[
20
, Remark 2.6]
. Given a predictable random field
\phi
in
L^{2}(d\Lambda\times dP)
, we have that

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\left(\iint_{\mathbb{X}}\phi(s,z)\mu(dsdz)\right)^{2}\bigg]=\mathbb{E}\bigg[\iint_{\mathbb{X}}\phi(s,z)^{2}\Lambda(dsdz)\bigg],

(2.2)

under both the information flows
\mathbb{G}
and
\mathbb{F}
.
We denote
\mathcal{I}^{\mathbb{G}}
(resp.
\mathcal{I}^{\mathbb{F}}
) the subspace of
L^{2}(d\Lambda\times dP)
of the random fields admitting a
\mathbb{G}
-predictable (resp.
\mathbb{F}
-predictable) modification.

When considering the (Itô type) stochastic integration we have the following integral representation with an explicitly characterized integrand by means of the non-anticipating (NA) derivative. This result first appeared in
[
15
]
(for general
L^{2}
-valued martingales as integrators) and its extension to the random fields in
[
17
]
. In case of integration with respect to the information flow
\mathbb{G}
, the statement reads as follows.

Theorem 2.2
.

For any
\xi\in L^{2}(dP)
the NA-derivative
\mathscr{D}\xi
is well defined in
\mathcal{I}^{\mathbb{G}}
as the
L^{2}(d\Lambda\times dP)
limit

\mathscr{D}\xi=\lim_{n\to\infty}\varphi_{n}
of the simple predictable random fields
(\varphi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in the form

\varphi_{n}(t,z):=\sum_{k=1}^{K_{n}}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\xi\frac{\mu(\Delta_{nk})}{\mathbb{E}[\Lambda(\Delta_{nk})|\mathcal{G}_{s_{nk}}]}\Big|\mathcal{G}_{s_{nk}}\bigg]\mathbf{1}_{\Delta_{nk}}(t,z),\quad(t,z)\in\mathbb{X}.

defined on some intersecting system
(\Delta_{nk})_{k=1,...,K_{n};n\in\mathbb{N}}
with
\Delta_{nk}=(s_{nk},u_{nk}]\times B_{nk}
and
B_{nk}
belonging to a countable semiring generating
\mathcal{B}_{\mathbb{R}}
. Furthermore, the following stochastic integral representation of
\xi
holds

\xi=\xi^{0}+\iint_{\mathbb{X}}\mathscr{D}_{t,z}\xi\ \mu(dtdz),

(2.3)

where the stochastic term
\xi^{0}\in L^{2}(dP)
is such that
\mathscr{D}\xi^{0}\equiv 0
and in particular
\xi^{0}=\mathbb{E}[\xi|\mathcal{F}^{\Lambda}]
.

The duality between the Itô stochastic integral and the NA-derivative is an immediate consequence.

Corollary 2.3
.

For all
\phi
in
\mathcal{I}^{\mathbb{G}}
and all
\xi
in
L^{2}(dP)
, we have

\mathbb{E}\left[\xi\iint_{\mathbb{X}}\phi(t,z)\mu(dtdz)\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\iint_{\mathbb{X}}\phi(t,z)\mathscr{D}_{t,z}\xi\ \Lambda(dtdz)\right].

(2.4)

We observe that representation (
2.3
) and the duality (
2.4
) would hold also for stochastic integration with respect to the filtration
\mathbb{F}
. In that case, however, we would still have
\mathscr{D}\xi^{0}=0
, but
no explicit
representation for
\xi^{0}
.

3
Maximum principles for time-changed Volterra games

Having in mind a financial context, the FSVIE (
1.2
) represents the market value, whereas each BSDE (
1.3
) is associated to some risk evaluation of the market as perceived by player
i=1,2
or to a recursive utility in the stochastic game we consider.
For this reason the BSDEs will depend on the values of
X
, with dynamics

X(t)=X_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}b(t,s,\lambda_{s},u(s),X(s\shortminus))ds+\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}}\kappa(t,s,z,\lambda_{s},u(s),X(s\shortminus))\mu(dsdz),

(3.1)

where
X(0)=X_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
and

\displaystyle b

\displaystyle:[0,T]\times[0,T]\times[0,\infty)^{2}\times\mathbb{U}\times\mathbb{R}\times\Omega\longrightarrow\mathbb{R},

\displaystyle\kappa

\displaystyle:[0,T]\times[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times[0,\infty)^{2}\times\mathbb{U}\times\mathbb{R}\times\Omega\longrightarrow\mathbb{R},

are given functions with
\mathbb{U}
specified in Definition
3.2
below.
We assume that
b
and
\kappa
are
C^{2}
with respect to their first variable and, for all
t\in[0,T]
,
\lambda\in[0,\infty)^{2}
,
u\in\mathbb{U}
,
x\in\mathbb{R}
, the stochastic processes
s\longmapsto b(t,s,\lambda,u,x)
and
s\longmapsto\kappa(t,s,z,\lambda,u,x)
, with
s\leq t
, are
\mathbb{F}
-predictable.
We also assume that
b
and
\kappa
are
C^{2}
with respect to
u
and
x
, with partial derivatives in
L^{2}(dt\times dP)
and
L^{2}(d\Lambda\times dP)
, respectively.
Later on we consider the coefficients
b
and
\kappa
in a functional setup:

\displaystyle b

\displaystyle:[0,T]\times[0,T]\times\Xi_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\times\Xi_{\mathbb{U}}\times\Xi_{\mathbb{R}}\times\Omega\longrightarrow\mathbb{R},

\displaystyle\kappa

\displaystyle:[0,T]\times[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times\Xi_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\times\Xi_{\mathbb{U}}\times\Xi_{\mathbb{R}}\times\Omega\longrightarrow\mathbb{R},

where we denoted by
\Xi_{S}
the space of measurable function on
[0,T]
with values in
S
. Then we can interpret the coefficients in (
3.1
) via the evaluation at the point
s\in[0,T]
:

\displaystyle b(t,\cdot,\lambda_{\cdot},u(\cdot),X^{u}(\cdot))(s)

\displaystyle=b(t,s,\lambda_{s},u(s),X^{u}(s\shortminus))

\displaystyle\kappa(t,\cdot,z,\lambda_{\cdot},u(\cdot),X^{u}(\cdot))(s)

\displaystyle=\kappa(t,s,z,\lambda_{s},u(s),X^{u}(s\shortminus)).

We assume that
b
and
\kappa
are Fréchet differentiable (in the standard topology of càdlàg paths) with
C^{2}
regularity in
t
,
x
and
u
(with the corresponding derivatives). In general, throughout the paper,
\partial_{x}
denotes the appropriate partial derivative with respect to the variable
x
.

Lastly, we assume that, for all
z\in\mathbb{R}
,
\lambda\in[0,\infty)^{2}
,
u\in\mathcal{U}
,
x\in\mathbb{R}
the partial derivative of
\kappa
with respect to
t
(denoted with
\partial_{t}\kappa(t,s,z,\lambda,u,x)
) is locally bounded (uniformly in
t
) and satisfies

|\partial_{t}\kappa(t_{1},s,z,\lambda,u,x)-\partial_{t}\kappa(t_{2},s,z,\lambda,u,x)|\leq K|t_{1}-t_{2}|,

(3.2)

for some
K>0
and for each fixed
s\leq t
,
\lambda\in[0,\infty)^{2}
,
u\in\mathcal{U}
,
x\in\mathbb{R}
.
In this case, in fact, we are able to apply the Transformation Rule (see
[
31
]
for the original result and see
[
18
]
Lemma 3.4 for its formulation in the context of time-change) and write
X
in differential notation as

\displaystyle dX(t)

\displaystyle=\Bigg[b(t,t,\lambda_{t},u(t),X(t))+\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{t}b(t,s,\lambda_{s},u(s),X(s))ds

(3.3)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{t}\kappa(t,s,z,\lambda_{s},u(s)X(s)\mu(dsdz)\Bigg]dt+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\kappa(t,t,\lambda_{t},u(t),X(t))\mu(dtdz).

The actions of the players
i=1,2
are conveyed into the controls
u_{i}
, which form the stochastic process
u(s)=(u_{1}(s),u_{2}(s))
,
s\in[0,T]
.

Note that if
b
and
\kappa
are Lipschitz continuous in

and have at most linear growth in

, uniformly in

,
\lambda\in[0,\infty)^{2}
,

, then
X
admits an
\mathbb{F}
-adapted solution in
L^{2}(dt\times dP)
. For details concerning the existence and uniqueness of the solution of (
3.1
) we refer to Theorem 4.2 in
[
18
]
.

Remark 3.1
.

We notice that by rewriting (
3.1
) in the differential form (
3.3
), we can link our Volterra optimization problem to a
functional
SDE optimization problem.
As stated also in
[
18
]
Remark 3.3 some existence results for (
3.3
) are available (see e.g.
[
11
,
5
]
) and could possibly be extended to the current framework. In that case some additional conditions on
b
and

For each player
i
, we consider the controlled BSDEs
(Y_{i},\Theta_{i},M_{i})
, depending on (
3.1
) of the form:

\displaystyle Y_{i}(t)

\displaystyle=h_{i}(X(T))-\int_{t}^{T}g_{i}(s,\lambda_{s},u(s),X(s\shortminus),Y_{i}(s\shortminus),\Theta_{i}(s,\cdot))ds

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{t}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Theta_{i}(s,z)\mu(dsdz)+\int_{t}^{T}dM_{i}(s),\quad t\in[0,T],

(3.4)

where
M_{i}\in\mathcal{M}^{\perp}
, which is the space of the
\mathbb{F}
-martingales in

orthogonal to
\mu
.
For all

,

\displaystyle h_{i}

\displaystyle:\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle g_{i}

\displaystyle:[0,T]\times[0,\infty)^{2}\times\mathbb{U}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathcal{Z}\times\Omega\longrightarrow\mathbb{R},

where
\mathcal{Z}
is the space of functions
\theta:\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that

We assume that, for

, the processes
s\longmapsto g_{i}(t,\lambda,u,x,y,\theta)
is
\mathbb{F}
-adapted, integrable in
L^{2}(dt\times dP)
for all
\lambda\in[0,\infty)^{2}
,
u\in\mathbb{U}
,
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
y\in\mathbb{R}
,
\theta\in\mathcal{Z}
,
C^{2}
with respect to

,
x
and

, and with partial derivatives in

. Also we assume that the functions
h_{i}:\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
are
\mathcal{F}_{T}
-measurable and

3.2
The zero-sum game case

In the zero-sum game, we recall that a player’s gain corresponds to the other player’s loss. This translates directly into having

J_{1}(u_{1},u_{2})=-J_{2}(u_{1},u_{2}).

(3.14)

In this case one can regard the Nash equilibrium of the game

as a saddle point for the functional

(3.15)

To prove this statement it is sufficient to notice that from Definition
3.3
and Remark

J(u_{1},\hat{u}_{2})\leq J(\hat{u}_{1},\hat{u}_{2})\leq J(\hat{u}_{1},u_{2}).

for a given function

\displaystyle\inf_{u_{2}\in\mathcal{A}^{\cdot}_{2}}\sup_{u_{1}\in\mathcal{A}^{\cdot}_{1}}J(u_{1},u_{2})=J(\hat{u}_{1},\hat{u}_{2})=\sup_{u_{1}\in\mathcal{A}^{\cdot}_{1}}\inf_{u_{2}\in\mathcal{A}^{\cdot}_{2}}J(u_{1},u_{2}).

.

(Transformation Rule for backward Volterra integral equations)

[

\displaystyle Y(t)

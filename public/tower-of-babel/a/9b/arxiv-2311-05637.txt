Non-absolute integrable function spaces on metric measure spaces
Parthapratim Saha

Abstract
. Kuelbs-Steadman spaces are introduced in this article on a separable metric space with finite diameter and finite positive Borel measure. Kuelbs-Steadman spaces of the Lipschitz type are also discussed. Various inclusion properties are also discussed. In the sequel, we introduce HK-Sobolev spaces on metric mesure space which coincides with HK-Sobolev space in the Euclidean case. In application, we discuss the boundedness of Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator on Kuelbs-Steadman spaces and HK-Sobolev spaces over a metric measure space. Keywords and phrases: Kuelbs-Steadman spaces; Lipschitz type Kuelbs-Steadman spaces; HK-Sobolev spaces; Poincaré type inequality; Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator 2020 Mathematical Subject Clasification : 46B25, 46E35, 46E36, 46F25.

Throughout the article, with

There must be a constant

|\mathfrak{f}(x)-\mathfrak{f}(y)|\leq Ld(x,y)~\forall~x,y\in\mathcal{X},

is a Lipschitz function,

\it Lip(\mathcal{X})
stands for the set of Lipschitz functions on

and lowest such constant

Whenever there are multiple points in
\mathcal{X}
, the following semi-norm is added to the space

.

l(\mathfrak{f})=\sup\limits_{x,y\in\mathcal{X}}\frac{|\mathfrak{f}(y)-\mathfrak{f}(x)|}{d(y,x)},~where~x\neq y,~\mathfrak{f}\in Lip(\mathcal{X}).

This is not a norm, because
l(\mathfrak{f})=0
if and only if
\mathfrak{f}
is constant (see
[
18
]
). We refer to the quotient space
\frac{Lip(\mathcal{X})}{const(\mathcal{X})}
as
LIP(\mathcal{X})
where
const(\mathcal{X})
is the set of all real-valued constant maps on
\mathcal{X}
. As far as its norm
L(\mathfrak{f}+const(\mathcal{X}))=l(\mathfrak{f}),~\mathfrak{f}\in Lip(\mathcal{X})
is concerned, the space
LIP(\mathcal{X})
is a Banach space. Also, recalling
LIP(\mathcal{X})
will be a function space if for
x,~y
are two points of
\mathcal{X},
such that the functional
\mathfrak{f}\to\mathfrak{f}(x)-\mathfrak{f}(y)\in\mathbb{R}
is well defined in
LIP(\mathcal{X}).
For a Lipschitz function
\mathfrak{f}
the modulus of gradient is given by the slope
|\nabla\mathfrak{f}|:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R},
by

\displaystyle|\nabla\mathfrak{f}|(x)=\lim\limits_{y\to x}\sup\frac{|\mathfrak{f}(y)-\mathfrak{f}(x)|}{d(y,x)}.

Recalling Sobolev spaces in the approach of Lipschitz as

\displaystyle W^{1,p}(\Omega)=\bigg\{\mathfrak{f}\in D^{\prime}(\Omega):~\mathfrak{f}\in L^{p}(\Omega),~\nabla\mathfrak{f}\in L^{p}(\Omega)\bigg\},

Kuelbs-Steadman spaces on metric measure spaces

We also introduce Lipschitz-type Kuelbs-Steadman spaces which in brif, we will call as Lipschitz-Steadman spaces. We will discuss the basic properties of these spaces. Finally, we will find the inclusion relations of Lipschitz-Steadman spaces and Lipschitz-type Lebesgue spaces

Here we recalling the Kuelbs-Steadman spaces
\mathcal{KS}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

We discuss a few results of
\mathcal{KS}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
that are useful in a later section of our work. For more details see
[
7
]

Theorem 3.1
.

For
1\leq p\leq\infty,~D^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a subset of
\mathcal{KS}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Theorem 3.2

For

\|M_{l}\mathfrak{f}\|_{\mathcal{KS}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C_{p}\|\mathfrak{f}\|_{\mathcal{KS}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

Now, we will extend the theory of Kuelbs-Steadman spaces on spearable metric spaces. Let
(\mathcal{X},d)
be separable metric space with a regular Borel measure
\mu
. Let
\mathcal{S}
be a countable dense subset of
\mathcal{X}
. Since
\mathcal{S}
is countable, we can arrange the elements of
\mathcal{S}
as
\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},...\}
. Now for each
r,j\in\mathbb{N}
let
B_{r}(x_{i})
be the closed ball centered at
x_{i}
with radius
k
. Using the natural order that translates
\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}
bijectively to
\mathbb{N}
, we can consider
\{B_{r},r\in\mathbb{N}\}
as countable collection of all closed balls
\{B_{r}(x_{i}):(r,i)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}\}
centered at a point in
\mathcal{S}.

Assuming the characteristic function
\chi_{k}
on
B_{k}
, we have
\chi_{k}(x)\in\mathit{L}^{p}(\mathcal{X})\cap\mathit{L}^{\infty}(\mathcal{X})
for
1\leq p<\infty
. Also, let
\{\tau_{r}\}
be a sequence of positive real numbers such that
\sum\limits_{r=1}^{\infty}\tau_{r}=1
. We define

\|\mathfrak{f}\|_{\mathcal{KS}^{p}(\mathcal{X})}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\left(\sum\limits_{r=1}^{\infty}\tau_{r}\left|\int_{\mathcal{X}}\chi_{r}(x)\mathfrak{f}(x)d\mu(x)\right|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}&\mbox{~for~}1\leq p<\infty\\
\sup\limits_{r\geq 1}\left|\int_{\mathcal{X}}\chi_{r}(x)\mathfrak{f}(x)d\mu(x)\right|&\mbox{~for~}p=\infty\end{array}\right.

One can easily check that
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{KS}^{p}(\mathcal{X})}
defines a norm on
L^{p}(\mathcal{X})
. The completion of
L^{p}(\mathcal{X})
with respect to this norm is called is defined as Kuelbs-Steadman space on
\mathcal{X}
, denoted by
\mathcal{KS}^{p}(\mathcal{X}).
If
p=1,
then
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{KS}^{1}(\mathcal{X})}
will be a norm of
\mathcal{KS}^{1}(\mathcal{X}).
In case, if the topology is weak one, we call
\mathcal{KS}^{1}(\mathcal{X})
as weak Kuelbs-Steadman spaces. We denote this spaces as
\mathcal{KS}^{1}_{w}(\mathcal{X}).
One can see
[
2
]
for details. We will discuss a few fundamental results of
\mathcal{KS}^{p}(\mathcal{X})
as follows:

Theorem 3.3
.

For every
q,~1\leq q\leq\infty,
the Lebesgue space
L^{q}(\mathcal{X})
is dense continuous embedding subset of
\mathcal{KS}^{p}(\mathcal{X}).

Proof.

The space
\mathcal{KS}^{p}(\mathcal{X})
is contained in
L^{p}(\mathcal{X})
densely by the construction of
\mathcal{KS}^{p}(\mathcal{X})
. Now, for
q\neq p
, we need to show that
L^{p}(\mathcal{X})\subset\mathcal{KS}^{p}(\mathcal{X})
. We consider the follwing cases:

Case 1
For
p=\infty
the result is trivial.

Case 2
For
p<\infty
, we consider
\mathfrak{f}\in L^{q}(\mathcal{X})
and
q<\infty
, then

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{f}\|_{\mathcal{KS}^{p}(\mathcal{X})}

\displaystyle=\left\{\sum\limits_{r=1}^{\infty}\tau_{r}\left|\int_{\mathcal{X}}\chi_{r}(t)\mathfrak{f}(t)d\mu(t)\right|^{\frac{qp}{q}}\right\}^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\{\sum\limits_{r=1}^{\infty}\tau_{r}\left(\int_{\mathcal{X}}\chi_{r}(t)|\mathfrak{f}(t)|^{q}d\mu(t)\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}\right\}^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\sup\limits_{r}\left(\int_{X}\chi_{r}(t)|\mathfrak{f}(t)|^{q}d\mu(t)\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq\|g\|_{q}.

This implies that
L^{p}(\mathcal{X})\subset\mathcal{KS}^{p}(\mathcal{X}).

For
q=\infty
, we have

\displaystyle\|g\|_{\mathcal{KS}^{p}}

\displaystyle=\left\{\sum\limits_{r=1}^{\infty}\tau_{r}\left|\int_{\mathcal{X}}\chi_{r}(t)\mathfrak{f}(t)d\mu(t)\right|^{p}\right\}^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\{\left(\sum\limits_{r=1}^{\infty}\tau_{r}(\mu(B_{r}))^{p}\right)(\operatorname{ess}\sup|\mathfrak{f}|)^{p}\right\}^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq M\|g\|_{\infty}.

Thus
L^{\infty}(\mathcal{X})\subset\mathcal{KS}^{p}(\mathcal{X})
.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

Two exponents
p
and

.

Theorem 3.5
.

(Hölder) Suppose
1\leq p,q<\infty
are conjugate exponent. If
\mathfrak{f}\in\mathcal{KS}^{p}(\mathcal{X})
and
\mathfrak{g}\in\mathcal{KS}^{q}(\mathcal{X})
, then
\mathfrak{f},\mathfrak{g}\in\mathcal{KS}^{1}(\mathcal{X})
and
\|\mathfrak{fg}\|_{\mathcal{KS}^{1}}\leq\|\mathfrak{f}\|_{\mathcal{KS}^{p}}+\|\mathfrak{g}\|_{\mathcal{KS}^{q}}

Proof.

To prove the inequality, we used generalised form of arithmitic-geometric mean inequality: if
A,b\geq 0
, and
0\leq\theta\leq 1
, then

A^{\theta}B^{\theta}\leq\theta A+(1-\theta)B.

(4)

If
\|\mathfrak{f}\|_{\mathcal{KS}^{p}(\mathcal{X})}=0
or
\|\mathfrak{g}\|_{\mathcal{KS}^{q}(\mathcal{X})}=0
, then
\mathfrak{fg}=0~a.e.
and the inequality is obvious. So, we consider neither
\|\mathfrak{f}\|_{\mathcal{KS}^{p}(\mathcal{X})}=0
nor
\|g\|_{\mathcal{KS}^{q}(\mathcal{X})}=0.
Now if we replace
\mathfrak{f}
by
\mathfrak{f}/{\|\mathfrak{f}\|_{\mathcal{KS}^{p}}}
and
\mathfrak{g}
by
\mathfrak{g}/{\|\mathfrak{g}\|_{\mathcal{KS}^{q}}}
and assume
\|\mathfrak{f}\|_{\mathcal{KS}^{p}(\mathcal{X})}=1
and
\|g\|_{\mathcal{KS}^{q}(\mathcal{X})}=1
, we need to show that
\|\mathfrak{f}g\|_{\mathcal{KS}^{1}}\leq 1.

Setting
A=|\mathfrak{f}(x)|^{p}
,
B=|\mathfrak{g}(x)|^{q}
and
\theta=1/p
so that
1-\theta=1/q,
we get

|\mathfrak{f}(x)g(x)|\leq\\
frac{1}{p}|\mathfrak{f}(x)|^{p}+\frac{1}{q}|\mathfrak{g}(x)|^{q}.

(5)

Now, Using inequality (
5
)

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{fg}\|_{\mathcal{KS}^{1}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{r=1}^{\infty}\tau_{r}\left|\int_{\mathcal{X}}\chi_{r}(x)\mathfrak{f}(x)\mathfrak{g}(x)d\mu(x)\right|\leq\sum\limits_{r=1}^{\infty}\tau_{r}\int_{X}\chi_{r}(x)\left|\mathfrak{f}(x)\mathfrak{g}(x)\right|d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{r=1}^{\infty}\tau_{r}\int_{\mathcal{X}}\chi_{r}(x)\left(\frac{1}{p}|\mathfrak{f}(x)|^{p}+\frac{1}{q}|\mathfrak{g}(x)|^{q}\right)d\mu(x).

This conclude that
\|\mathfrak{fg}\|_{\mathcal{KS}^{1}}\leq 1
and this completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 3.6
.

(Minkowski) Sum of two function in
\mathcal{KS}^{p}(\mathcal{X})
belongs to
\mathcal{KS}^{p}(\mathcal{X})
. Moreover,
\|\mathfrak{f}+\mathfrak{g}\|_{\mathcal{KS}^{p}(\mathcal{X})}\leq||\mathfrak{f}||_{\mathcal{KS}^{p}(\mathcal{X})}+||\mathfrak{g}||_{\mathcal{\mathcal{KS}}^{p}(\mathcal{X})}.

Proof.

For any
\mathfrak{f},\mathfrak{g}\in\mathcal{KS}^{p}(\mathcal{X})
and
p\neq\infty
, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{f}+\mathfrak{g}\|_{\mathcal{KS}^{p}(\mathcal{X})}

\displaystyle=\left[\sum\limits_{r=1}^{\infty}\tau_{r}\left|\int_{\mathcal{X}}\chi_{r}(x)(\mathfrak{f}+\mathfrak{g})(x)d\mu(x)\right|^{p}\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=\left[\sum\limits_{r=1}^{\infty}\tau_{r}\left|\left\{\int_{\mathcal{X}}\chi_{r}(x)\mathfrak{f}(x)d\mu(x)\right\}+\left\{\int_{\mathcal{X}}\chi_{r}(x)\mathfrak{g}(x)d\mu(x)\right\}\right|^{p}\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\left[\sum\limits_{r=1}^{\infty}\tau_{r}\left|\int_{\mathcal{X}}\chi_{r}(x)\mathfrak{f}(x)d\mu(x)\right|^{p}+\sum\limits_{r=1}^{\infty}\tau_{r}\left|\int_{\mathcal{X}}\chi_{r}(x)\mathfrak{g}(x)d\mu(x)\right|^{p}\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathfrak{f}\|_{\mathcal{KS}^{p}(\mathcal{X})}+\|\mathfrak{g}\|_{\mathcal{KS}^{p}(\mathcal{X})}.

For

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{f}+\mathfrak{g}\|_{\mathcal{KS}^{\infty}(\mathcal{X})}

\displaystyle=\sup\limits_{r\geq 1}\left|\int_{\mathcal{X}}\chi_{r}(x)(\mathfrak{f}+\mathfrak{g})(x)d\mu(x)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sup\limits_{r\geq 1}\left|\int_{\mathcal{X}}\chi_{r}(x)\mathfrak{f}(x)d\mu(x)\right|+\sup\limits_{r\geq 1}\left|\int_{X}\chi_{r}(x)\mathfrak{g}(x)d\mu(x)\right|=\|\mathfrak{f}\|_{\mathcal{KS}^{\infty}(X)}+\|\mathfrak{g}\|_{\mathcal{KS}^{\infty}(\mathcal{X})}.

∎

Theorem 3.11
.

For each

as a dense continuous embedding.

Proof.

If

is in

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{f}\|_{\mathcal{KS}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\|\nabla\mathfrak{f}\|_{\mathcal{KS}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\sum\limits_{z=1}^{\infty}t_{z}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{E}_{z}(x)\nabla\mathfrak{f}d\mu(x)\right|^{p}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{z=1}^{\infty}\bigg(t_{z}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{E}_{z}(x)|\nabla\mathfrak{f}|^{p}d\mu(x)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\sup\limits_{z}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{E}_{z}(x)|\nabla\mathfrak{f}|^{p}d\mu(x)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}

Theorem 3.12

For each

\mathfrak{f}_{n}\to\mathfrak{f}

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{f}_{n}\to f
weakly on compact subset

So,
\|\mathfrak{f}_{n}-\mathfrak{f}\|_{L^{1,p}(X)}\to 0
(weakly) on

Now from this collection taking a countable sub collection
\mathcal{G}
as in the basic covering theorem and using the doubling property of
\mu
and disjointness of family
\mathcal{G},
we get

\displaystyle\mu(\{\mathcal{M}\mathfrak{f}>t\})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\mathcal{G}}\mu(5B)\leq C_{1}\sum_{\mathcal{G}}\mu(B)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{1}}{t}\sum_{\mathcal{G}}\left|\int_{B}\zeta(x)\mathfrak{f}(x)d\mu\right|\leq\frac{C_{1}}{t}\|f\|_{\mathcal{KS}^{1}(\mathcal{X})}.

To prove inequality (
12
), for
\mathfrak{f}\in\mathcal{KS}^{p}(\mathcal{X})
we consider

\mathfrak{f}=\mathfrak{f}\cdot\chi_{\{\mathfrak{f}<1/2\}}+\mathfrak{f}\cdot\chi_{\{\mathfrak{f}>1/2\}}=:g+h

with fixed
t>0
, where
\chi_{\star}
denotes the characteristic function. Then
\mathcal{M}\mathfrak{f}\leq\mathcal{M}g+\mathcal{M}h\leq t/2+\mathcal{M}h
so that
\{\mathcal{M}\mathfrak{f}>t\}\subset\{\mathcal{M}h<t/2\}
. Now from weak estimate (
11
) we have, by using (
10
) with
\Psi(x)=\max(x-\frac{t}{2},0)
and
\psi=\chi_{(t/2,\infty)}
(Characteristic function on
(t/2,\infty)
) that

\displaystyle\mu(\{\mathcal{M}h>t/2\})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{t}\int_{X}|h|d\mu\leq\frac{C}{t}\int_{|\mathfrak{f}|>t/2}|\mathfrak{f}|d\mu

\displaystyle=\frac{C}{t}\left(\int_{|\mathfrak{f}|>t/2}(|\mathfrak{f}|-t/2)d\mu+\frac{t}{2}\mu(\{|\mathfrak{f}|>t/2)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{C}{t}\int_{t/2}^{\infty}\mu(\{|\mathfrak{f}|>s\})ds+C\mu(\{|\mathfrak{f}|>t/2\}).

Now from the formula for
\mathcal{KS}^{p}
-norm of
\mathcal{M}\mathfrak{f}
and applying (
10
) to
\mathcal{M}\mathfrak{f}
with
\Psi(x)=x^{p}
and the the above estimate we get

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{M}\mathfrak{f}\|_{\mathcal{KS}^{p}(\mathcal{X})}^{p}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{r=1}^{\infty}\tau_{r}\left|\int_{\mathcal{X}}\chi_{r}(x)\mathcal{M}\mathfrak{f}(x)d\mu\right|^{p}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{r=1}^{\infty}\tau_{r}\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}\chi_{r}(x)t^{p-1}\mu(\{\mathcal{M}\mathfrak{f}>t\})\right|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{r=1}^{\infty}\tau_{r}\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}\chi_{r}(x)t^{p-1}\mu(\{\mathcal{M}h>t\})\right|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum\limits_{r=1}^{\infty}\tau_{r}\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}\chi_{r}(x)t^{p-2}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu(\{\mathfrak{f}>s\})ds+\frac{t}{2}\mu(\{\mathfrak{f}>t/2\})\right)dt\right|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\tau_{r}\left|\int_{\mathcal{X}}\chi_{r}(x)\mathfrak{f}(x)d\mu\right|^{p}=C\|\mathfrak{f}\|_{\mathcal{KS}^{p}(\mathcal{X})}^{p}.

This implies that
\|\mathcal{M}\mathfrak{f}\|_{\mathcal{KS}^{p}(\mathcal{X})}\leq C_{p}\|\mathfrak{f}\|_{\mathcal{KS}^{p}}(\mathcal{X})
, where
C_{p}
depends only on
p
and the doubling constant of
\mu
.
∎

Theorem 5.3
.

The maximal operator
\mathcal{M}
is bounded in the space
WS^{1,p}(\mathcal{X})
for

.

Proof.

For any
\mathfrak{f}\in WS^{1,p}(\mathcal{X})
, by definition
4.8
and using the boundedness of
\mathcal{M}
in

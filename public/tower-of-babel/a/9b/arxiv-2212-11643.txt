Introduction

Let
G
be a simple undirected graph. We denote the number of vertices, edges, and connected components of
G
by
\mathrm{v}(G)
,
\mathrm{e}(G)
, and
\mathrm{c}(G)
, respectively. Degree of a vertex is the number of its incident edges. We say
G
is even (odd) if all of its vertices have even (odd) degree. We also denote the number of even degree vertices of
G
by
\mathrm{v}_{\mathrm{e}}(G)
, number of odd degree vertices of
G
by
\mathrm{v}_{\mathrm{o}}(G)
, and the number of even connected components of
G
by
\mathrm{c}_{\mathrm{e}}(G)
.

Let
V(G)=\{v_{1},\cdots,v_{n}\}
be the vertex set of
G
. Then the adjacency matrix of
G
is the
n\times n
symmetric matrix
A(G)=(a_{ij})
where
a_{ij}=1
if
v_{i}
is adjacent to
v_{j}
and
a_{ij}=0
, otherwise.
A(G)+I
is called the closed neighborhood matrix of
G
, where
I
is the
n\times n
identity matrix. Let
D(G)
be the
n\times n
diagonal matrix whose ith diagonal entry is equal to the degree of
v_{i}
. Then, the Laplacian matrix of
G
is
L(G)=D(G)-A(G)
, and the signless Laplacian matrix of
G
is
Q(G)=D(G)+A(G)
. We call all these matrices graph matrices of
G
. In general, we say
M(G)
is a graph matrix of
G
if
M(G)=\mathcal{D}(G)+tA(G)
for some
n\times n
diagonal matrix
\mathcal{D}(G)
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
. We denote the multiplicity of an eigenvalue
\lambda
of a graph matrix
M(G)
by
m_{M}(G,\lambda)
. We denote the kernel space of a matrix
M
over
\mathbb{R}
by
Ker(M)
and over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
by
Ker_{2}(M)
. We denote their dimension by
\eta_{M}
and
\text{\tiny{$2$}-}\eta_{M}
and call them the nullity and
2
-nullity of
M
, respectively. In the case
M=M(G)
is a graph matrix of
G
, we write
\eta_{M}=\eta_{M}(G)
(
\text{\tiny{$2$}-}\eta_{M}=\text{\tiny{$2$}-}\eta_{M}(G)
) and call it the nullity (
2
-nullity) of
G
with respect to
M(G)
. If
M(G)=A(G)
we may just call it the nullity (
2
-nullity) of
G
. Clearly,
\eta_{M}(G)=m_{M}(G,0)
for all graph matrices
M
. Similarly, we denote the column space of a matrix
M
over
\mathbb{R}
by
Col(M)
, over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
by
Col_{2}(M)
and denote its rank
over
\mathbb{R}
by
r_{M}
, over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
by
\text{\tiny{$2$}-}r_{M}
.

Let
E(G)=\{uv\;|\;\text{u is adjacent to v}\}
be the edge set of
G
. Let us denote the collection of subsets of
E(G)
by
Edge(G)
. It is a vector space over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
where addition is taken as the symmetric difference operation of sets. Two well known subspaces of
Edge(G)
are the cycle and cut spaces. The cycle space
Cycle(G)
consists of even subgraphs of
G
. We call the dimension
\theta(G)
of
Cycle(G)
the circuit rank of
G
. It satisfies

\theta(G)=(\text{e}-\text{v}+\text{c})(G).

On the other hand, every subset of vertices
S
of
V(G)
determines a cut set of edges
\{uv\in E(G)|\;u\in S,\;v\in S^{c}\},
and the collection of all cut sets (or spanning cut subgraphs)
Cut(G)
is the orthogonal complement of
Cycle(G)
. The intersection of
Cycle(G)
and
Cut(G)
is the bicycle space
Bicycle(G)
and we denote its dimension by
\beta(G)
.

which does not contain isolated vertices,

(1)

\eta_{A}(G)\leq(2\theta+p)(G),

where
p(G)
is the number of pendant (degree one) vertices of

is obtained by Wang et al. in

However, considering the facts that
\mathrm{v}(G)
is the trivial bound of
\eta_{A}(G)
, and
2\theta(G)
involves the term
2\mathrm{e}(G)-2\mathrm{v}(G)
, it is clear that this estimate is not applicable if
\mathrm{e}(G)\geq 3\mathrm{v}(G)/2
. So it would be better if we can replace the term
2\theta(G)
by
\theta(G)
in the bound of
\eta_{A}(G)
at the cost of some additional terms if necessary. Although this new estimate may not be stronger than (
1
) for all graph types, it would still be applicable to important graph types where (
1
) is not applicable. As far as we know, the only result which gives a bound of
\eta_{A}(G)
involving
\theta(G)
instead of
2\theta(G)
was obtained in a recent paper
[
2
]
of Wong et al. However, they have been able to obtain this bound by putting strict assumptions on
G
. More precisely, they showed that if
G
is a bipartite graph consisting of cycle blocks (thus, necessarily an even graph) then

(2)

\eta_{A}(G)\leq(\theta+\mathrm{c})(G).

As we will see in a moment these assumptions are not necessary. Indeed, (
2
) holds true for all even graphs
G
.

It seems like the classic approach which has been used up to now by the above authors has its own limits which makes it very hard if not impossible to obtain bounds of
\eta_{A}(G)
involving
\theta(G)
instead of
2\theta(G)
for an arbitrary graph
G
. By classic approach we mean that the methods which use main inequalities satisfied by
\eta_{A}(G)
under some graph operations and graph theoretical arguments which connects
\eta_{A}(G)
to
\theta(G)
.

In this paper we introduce a new approach to connect
\eta_{A}(G)
to
\theta(G)
which we think more natural and also shorter than the classical one. The idea lies in the following simple observation:

\eta_{A}(G)\leq\text{\tiny{$2$}-}\eta_{A}(G)

for all graphs
G
(See Lemma
1.1
). So instead of bounding
\eta_{A}(G)
directly, we can try to bound
\text{\tiny{$2$}-}\eta_{A}(G)
by
\theta(G)
instead. This sounds more natural because both
\text{\tiny{$2$}-}\eta_{A}(G)
and
\theta(G)
are dimensions of linear spaces over the same field
\mathbb{F}_{2}
, namely
Ker_{2}(A(G))
and
Cycle(G)
. So one can connect
\text{\tiny{$2$}-}\eta_{A}(G)
and
\theta(G)
more easily if one can find a linear algebraic correspondence between these spaces (or between spaces related to them). Indeed there is such a correspondence in the case
G
is an even graph. To explain this let us write
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mum\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu:=m\mod{2}
for an integer
m
and
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muM\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu:=(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mum_{ij}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)
for an integer valued matrix
M=(m_{ij})
. Then
Ker_{2}(A(G))
is equal to
Ker_{2}(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muL\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu(G))
if
G
is even and a well known connection (
[
12
, Lemma 14.15.3]
) between the latter space and the bicycle space gives us

(3)

\text{\tiny{$2$}-}\eta_{A}(G)=(\beta+\text{c})(G)\leq(\theta+\text{c})(G),

when
G
is an even graph. Hence we immediately obtain (
2
) for any even graph
G
using this approach.
Moreover, we can generalize (
3
) to any graph by a simple argument at the cost of adding the term
\mathrm{v}_{\mathrm{o}}(G)
to the right hand side (See Theorem
1.8
). This shows how powerful and practical following this approach might be considering the fact that obtaining (
2
) even only for very special graphs takes effort when we follow the classical approach.

More importantly, applicability of this approach is not restricted to finding bounds of
\eta_{A}(G)
. Indeed,
\text{\tiny{$2$}-}\eta_{A}(G)
bounds not only
\eta_{A}(G)
but also any
m_{A}(G,\lambda)
when
\lambda
is an even integer (See Lemma
1.1
). On the other hand, for odd eigenvalues
\lambda
, we have
m_{A}(G,\lambda)\leq\eta_{A+I}(G)
, where the latter nullity of
G
with respect to
A(G)+I
can be bounded by
(\theta+\text{c}+\mathrm{v}_{\mathrm{e}})(G)
(See Theorem
1.8
).

Adding the terms
\mathrm{v}_{\mathrm{e}}(G)
or
\mathrm{v}_{\mathrm{o}}(G)
to the corresponding bounds in the general case seems inevitable but there is a graph parameter that we call
\tau(G)
which we can subtract from these bounds as well. To define it, let us say a cycle is even (odd) if it has even (odd) number of edges and two cycles are disjoint if they do not share any edge. Then we define
\tau(G)
as the maximum of the cardinalities of the sets of disjoint odd cycles of
G
. The reason why we can subtract
\tau(G)
from the bounds is because of the fact that
\tau(G)
corresponds to the dimension of a subspace of
Cycle(G)
which intersects
Bicycle(G)
only trivially (See Lemma
1.3
and the discussion above it).

Similarly, the same approach can be directly used to estimate the multiplicities of the integer eigenvalues of
A(G)+I
,
L(G)
and
Q(G)
as well. More precisely, we have the following theorem as our main result.

Theorem 0.1
.

Let
G
be a graph and
\lambda
be an integer eigenvalue of the corresponding graph matrix. Then

\displaystyle m_{A}(G,\lambda)

\displaystyle\leq(\theta-\tau+\mathrm{v}_{\mathrm{o}}+2\mathrm{c}_{\mathrm{e}}-\mathrm{c}+2i)(G)

\displaystyle\text{if $\lambda$ is even},

\displaystyle m_{A}(G,\lambda)

\displaystyle\leq(\theta-\tau+\mathrm{v}_{\mathrm{e}}+\mathrm{c})(G)

\displaystyle\text{if $\lambda$ is odd},

\displaystyle m_{L}(G,\lambda),m_{Q}(G,\lambda)

\displaystyle\leq(\theta-\tau+\mathrm{c})(G)

\displaystyle\text{if $\lambda$ is even},

\displaystyle m_{L}(G,\lambda),m_{Q}(G,\lambda)

\displaystyle\leq(\mathrm{v}-\beta-\mathrm{c})(G)

\displaystyle\text{if $\lambda$ is odd},

where
i(G)=0
if
G
is even and
i(G)=1
otherwise.

The proof is given in Section
1
.
Note that for
m_{L}(G,\lambda)
and
m_{Q}(G,\lambda)
when
\lambda
is odd we cannot obtain a bound in terms of
\theta(G)
unlike the even case. This is something expected since it is known that
m_{L}(G,1)\geq(p-q)(G)
where
q(G)
is the number of vertices adjacent to a pendant vertex of
G

[
13
]
. Hence, even if
\theta(G)
is small,
m_{L}(G,\lambda)
can be very large for odd
\lambda
. It might be the case that the multiplicities of odd eigenvalues are bounded in terms of
\theta(G)
with some additional terms involving at least
(p-q)(G)
. However, such a bound, if it exists, cannot be found using solely our approach, it must involve some other arguments as well.

We already mentioned that Wang et al. in
[
3
]
generalized (

) to any
m_{A}(G,\lambda)
. Later Shuchao and Wei in
[

generalized the latter result further to any

, where
A_{\alpha}(G)
is the graph matrix defined by
A_{\alpha}(G):=\alpha D(G)+(1-\alpha)A(G)
where

A_{\alpha}(G)
matrix was first introduced by Nikiforov in
[
4
]
and its spectral analysis gained a lot of attention later on. We show that our approach is applicable to this graph matrix as well in the case where both
\alpha
and
\lambda
are rational numbers. The bound of
m_{A_{\alpha}}(G,\lambda)
changes drastically depending on the representations of
\alpha
and
\lambda
in their lowest forms as ratio of two integers. The results are summarized Section
2
in Theorem
2.1
.

Lemma 1.1
.

Let
M
be an integer valued matrix. Then

\eta_{M}\leq\text{\tiny{$2$}-}\eta_{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muM\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu}.

Moreover, if
M
is a graph matrix of
G
, then

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad m_{M}(G,\lambda)

\displaystyle\leq\text{\tiny{$2$}-}\eta_{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muM\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu}(G)

\displaystyle\text{if $\lambda$ is an even integer},

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad m_{M}(G,\lambda)

\displaystyle\leq\text{\tiny{$2$}-}\eta_{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muM\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu+I}(G)

\displaystyle\text{if $\lambda$ is an odd integer}.

Proof.

For the proof of the first inequality, we will show that
r_{M}\geq\text{\tiny{$2$}-}r_{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muM\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu}
. Then the result follows by the rank nullity theorem.

Take a set of column vectors
\{c_{1},\dots,c_{k}\}
of
M
such that
\{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muc_{1}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu,\dots,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muc_{k}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\}
is a linearly independent set in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
where
n
is the number of rows of
M
. Consider the following equation

(4)

\alpha_{1}c_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{k}c_{k}=0,

where
\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
for
i=1,\dots,k
. Since each
c_{i}
has integer entries, (
4
) has a nontrivial solution in
\mathbb{R}
if and only if it has a nontrivial solution in
\mathbb{Z}
. So without loss of generality, we can assume all
\alpha_{i}
’s are integer. Dividing (
4
) by a suitable power of
2
if necessary, we can further assume that all
\alpha_{i}
’s are integers such that not all of them are even unless all of them are zero. Then the corresponding column vectors in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
satisfy

(5)

\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\alpha_{1}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muc_{1}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu+\cdots+\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\alpha_{k}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muc_{k}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu=0,

which has only the trivial solution since
\{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muc_{1}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu,\dots,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muc_{k}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\}
is linearly independent. This means every
\alpha_{i}
is even and this is only possible if every
\alpha_{i}
is zero by our assumption. Hence,
\{c_{1},\dots,c_{k}\}
is a linearly independent set of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. This proves
r_{M}\geq\text{\tiny{$2$}-}r_{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muM\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu}
.

For the last two inequalities just note that

m_{M}(G,\lambda)=\eta_{M-\lambda I}(G)\leq\text{\tiny{$2$}-}\eta_{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muM-\lambda I\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu}(G)=\text{\tiny{$2$}-}\eta_{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muM\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu+\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\lambda\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5muI}(G).

∎

Let
P(G):=\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muL\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu(G)=\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muQ\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu(G)
. We call it the parity Laplacian matrix of
G
. We have the following elementary fact which can be found in
[
12
, Lemma 14.15.3]
:

Proposition 1.2
.

(
[
12
]
)

For any graph
G

(6)

\text{\tiny{$2$}-}\eta_{P}(G)=(\beta+\mathrm{c})(G).

Since the bicycle space is a subspace of the cycle space, the above lemma immediately implies

(7)

\text{\tiny{$2$}-}\eta_{P}(G)\leq(\theta+\mathrm{c})(G).

However this inequality can be improved. Note that, in general,
Bicycle(G)
is a proper subspace of
Cycle(G)
. It consists of those even subgraphs of
G
which are cuts as well. Let us call these subgraphs even cuts of
G
. Since
Cycle(G)
and
Cut(G)
are orthogonal to each other under the dot product “
\cdot
”, defined by
A\cdot B:=|A\cap B|\mod 2
, a necessary condition to be an even cut is to be orthogonal to every even subgraph. In other words, an even cut must share even number of edges with every even subgraph including itself. So trivially, no odd cycle or no subgraph including an odd cycle can be an even cut. This observation leads us to the following lemma.

Lemma 1.3
.

For any graph
G
,

\beta(G)\leq(\theta-\tau)(G).

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a set of disjoint odd cycles of
G
with
|\mathcal{T}|=\tau(G)
. Note that the span of
\mathcal{T}
,
Span(\mathcal{T})
, is a subspace of
Cycle(G)
with dimension
\tau(G)
and
Bicycle(G)\cap Span(\mathcal{T})=\{\emptyset\}
, where
\emptyset
denotes the empty spanning subgraph of
G
. Therefore,
Bicycle(G)+Span(\mathcal{T})
is a subspace of
Cycle(G)
with dimension
(\beta+\tau)(G)
. Hence,
(\beta+\tau)(G)\leq\theta(G)
.
∎

Proposition
1.2
and Lemma
1.3
give us the following.

Theorem 1.4
.

For any graph
G

(8)

\text{\tiny{$2$}-}\eta_{P}(G)\leq(\theta-\tau+\mathrm{c})(G).

Note that
P(G)=\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muD\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu(G)+A(G)
where
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muD\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
is the diagonal matrix whose diagonal entries are the parity of the degrees of the corresponding vertices. Hence,

if

is even, and

if

is odd. Therefore, from (

) we obtain

(9)

\displaystyle\text{\tiny{$2$}-}\eta_{A}(G)

\displaystyle\leq(\theta-\tau+\text{c})(G)

\displaystyle\text{if $G$ is even},

(10)

\displaystyle\text{\tiny{$2$}-}\eta_{A+I}(G)

\displaystyle\leq(\theta-\tau+\text{c})(G)

\displaystyle\text{if $G$ is odd}.

Remark 1.5
.

Recently, Wong et al. in
[
2
]
proved that
\eta_{A}(G)\leq\theta(G)+1
for bipartite, cycle spliced graphs (connected even graphs consisting of cycle blocks). On the other hand, Lemma

and (

) show us that this is true for any connected even graph. They also showed in
[

that if all cycles of a cycle spliced graph are odd, then
\eta_{A}(G)\leq 1
. This fact can also be realized by our approach.
Indeed, in such a case
\theta(G)=\tau(G)
. Therefore, Lemma

Definition 1.6
.

For a graph

we define
{G_{even}}
as
G

by connecting every odd degree vertex of

{G_{odd}}
as the graph obtained from
G

G

It is clear that
G_{odd}
is an odd graph. Also
G_{even}
is an even graph by the handshaking lemma. Let

be the binary valued function which takes the value
0

G

1

G

Lemma 1.7
.

Let
G
be a graph and
M

G
. Then the followings hold.

\displaystyle i)\;

\displaystyle\text{\tiny{$2$}-}\eta_{M}(G_{even})\geq(\text{\tiny{$2$}-}\eta_{M}-i)(G),

\displaystyle v)\;

\displaystyle\text{\tiny{$2$}-}\eta_{M}(G_{odd})\geq(\text{\tiny{$2$}-}\eta_{M}-\mathrm{v}_{\mathrm{e}})(G),

\displaystyle ii)\;

\displaystyle\theta(G_{even})=(\theta+\mathrm{v}_{\mathrm{o}}+\mathrm{c}_{\mathrm{e}}-\mathrm{c})(G),

\displaystyle vi)\;

\displaystyle\theta(G_{odd})=\theta(G),

\displaystyle iii)\;

\displaystyle\mathrm{c}(G_{even})=(\mathrm{c}_{\mathrm{e}}+i)(G),

\displaystyle vii)\;

\displaystyle iv)\;

\displaystyle viii)\;

Proof.

We will only prove (i), (ii), (iii), and (v) since the rest are obvious.

For (i) and (v) let

denote the induced subgraph obtained from a graph
H
by removing one of its vertices

is the principal submatrix of

obtained by deleting the row and column of

corresponding to the vertex

\text{\tiny{$2$}-}\eta_{M}(H-v)\leq\text{\tiny{$2$}-}\eta_{M}(H)+1
. If
G

and there is nothing to prove. Otherwise,

G

\mathrm{v}_{\mathrm{e}}(G)
number of pendant vertices of

(iii) follows from the fact that not even components of

become connected to each other in
G_{even}
via the paths of length two passing trough the external vertex.

(ii) follows from combining the facts that
\mathrm{e}(G_{even})=(\mathrm{e}+\mathrm{v}_{\mathrm{o}})(G)
,
\mathrm{v}(G_{even})=(\mathrm{v}+i)(G)
, and
\mathrm{c}(G_{even})=(\mathrm{c}_{\mathrm{e}}+i)(G)
.

3.
Existence of the extremal graphs

Before giving characterizations of the extremal graphs whose eigenvalue multiplicities attain the corresponding bound in Theorem
0.1

1.8

,
A(G)+I
,
P(G)
, and
P(G)+I
. Unfortunately, as far as we know,

-nullities of the corresponding matrices is not a well studied area and their properties such as how they change under certain graph operations are not examined enough yet. The one exception might be the
2
-nullity of
A+I
. Because of its connection with the Lights Out game and also with the parity domination theory some of its properties have been examined in several studies such as
[
41
]
,
[
42
]
,
[
43
]
,
[
44
]
,
[
45
]
. So we hope that after this study, there will be more attraction to the analysis of the
2
-nullities of the corresponding graph matrices by other researchers as well.

One thing we can say for sure for now is that all of the bounds in Theorem
0.1
, an hence in Theorems
1.4
,
1.8
, and
1.9
are attained. For example it is easy to check that for each
k
, the cycle

is an extremal graph attaining the bounds in (

) and (

). Moreover,

which is equal to the corresponding bounds in the first and third inequalities in Theorem

On the other hand, any odd tree

is an extremal graph attaining the bound in (

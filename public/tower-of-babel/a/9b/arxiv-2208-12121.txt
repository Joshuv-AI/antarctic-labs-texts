Annihilator of Top Local Cohomology and Lynch’s Conjecture
Ali Fathi

Abstract
. Let R R be a commutative Noetherian ring, 𝔞 \mathfrak{a} a proper ideal of R R and N N a non-zero finitely generated R R -module with N ≠ 𝔞 ​ N N\neq\mathfrak{a}N . Let d d (respectively c c ) be the smallest (respectively greatest) non-negative integer i i such that the local cohomology H 𝔞 i ⁡ ( N ) \operatorname{H}^{i}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N) is non-zero. In this paper, we provide sharp bounds under inclusion for the annihilators of the local cohomology modules H 𝔞 d ⁡ ( N ) \operatorname{H}^{d}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N) , H 𝔞 c ⁡ ( N ) \operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N) and these annihilators are computed in certain cases. Also, we construct a counterexample to Lynch’s conjecture.

Introduction

Throughout this paper,
R

\mathfrak{a}
be an ideal of
R
,
N
an
R
-module and

a non-negative integer. The
i

\operatorname{H}^{i}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N):={\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{\varinjlim}\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R}(R/\mathfrak{a}^{n},N)};

Throughout this section, let
\mathfrak{a}
be an ideal of
R
and
N
a finitely generated
R
-module.
We recall that the cohomological dimension (respectively, the depth) of
N
with respect to
\mathfrak{a}
, denoted by
\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},N)
(respectively,
\operatorname{depth}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},N)
), is defined as the supremum (respectively, infimum) of the non-negative integers

such that
\operatorname{H}^{i}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)
is non-zero.
The arithmetic rank of
\mathfrak{a}
, denoted by
\textrm{ara}(\mathfrak{a})
, is the least number of elements of
R
required to generate an ideal which has the same radical as
\mathfrak{a}
. The
N
-height of
\mathfrak{a}
is defined as
\operatorname{ht}_{N}{(\mathfrak{a})}:=\inf\{\dim_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(N_{\mathfrak{p}}):\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Supp}_{R}(N)\cap\operatorname{V}(\mathfrak{a})\}
, where
\operatorname{V}(\mathfrak{a})
denotes the set of all prime ideals of
R
containing
\mathfrak{a}
. We denote the set of minimal elements of
\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N)
by
\operatorname{MinAss}_{R}(N)
; also, the set of elements
\mathfrak{p}
of
\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N)
with
\dim_{R}(R/\mathfrak{p})=\dim_{R}(N)
is denoted by
\operatorname{Assh}_{R}(N)
.
For a submodule
L
of
N
and
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Supp}_{R}(N)
, we denote the contraction of
L_{\mathfrak{p}}
under the canonical map
N\rightarrow N_{\mathfrak{p}}
by
C^{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}(L)
. Finally, we denote the set of integers (respectively, positive integers, non-negative integers) by
\mathbb{Z}
(respectively,
\mathbb{N}
,
\mathbb{N}_{0}
). For any unexplained notation and terminology,
we refer the reader to
[
9
]
,
[
10
]
and
[
27
]
.

We adopt the convention that the intersection (respectively, union) of empty family of subsets of a set
A
is
A
(respectively, the empty set). Also, we adopt the convention that the infimum (respectively, supremum) of empty set of integers is
\infty
(respectively,
-\infty
).

Since
\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\operatorname{Supp}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{i}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))=\operatorname{Supp}_{R}(N/\mathfrak{a}N)
(see
[
17
, Lemma 2.4]
), we obtain
N=\mathfrak{a}N
if and only if
\operatorname{H}^{i}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)=0
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. In this case we have
\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},N)=-\infty
,
\operatorname{depth}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},N)=\infty
and
\operatorname{ht}_{N}{(\mathfrak{a})}=\infty
by above convention.

If
R
is a regular local ring containing a field and
\mathfrak{a}
a proper ideal of
R
, then it is known that
\operatorname{H}^{i}_{\mathfrak{a}}(R)\neq 0
if and only if
\operatorname{H}^{i}_{\mathfrak{a}}(R)
is faithful (i. e.,
\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{i}_{\mathfrak{a}}(R))=0
). This was proved by Huneke and Koh in prime characteristic (see
[
23
, Lemma 2.2]
) and by Lyubeznik in characteristic zero (see
[
26
, Corrolary 3.5]
and the proof of
[
23
, Theorem 2.3]
). Boix and Eghbali provided a characteristic-free proof of this result in
[
8
, Theorem 3.6]
. This leads to the following conjecture of Lynch; see
[
25
, Conjecture 1.2]
.

Lynch’s Conjecture.
If
R
is a local ring and
\mathfrak{a}
a proper ideal of
R
with
c:=\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R)>0
, then
\dim_{R}(R/\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(R)))=\dim_{R}(R/\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(R))
. In particular, if
\mathfrak{a}
contains a non-zerodivisor, then
\dim_{R}(R/\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(R)))=\dim_{R}(R)
.

Let
\mathfrak{a}\neq R
and
c:=\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R)
.
The conjecture is known to be false: the first counterexample was constructed by Bahmanpour
in
[
5
, Example 3.2]
over a nonequidimensional local ring of dimension at least
5
with cohomological dimension
c=2
. Also, Singh and Walther

[
36
]
provided a counterexample over a nonequidimensional local ring of dimension
3
with cohomological dimension
c=2
. In both examples
\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(R))

has height zero. Datta, Switala and Zhang, in
[
13
]
, produced a counterexample for this conjecture over a regular local ring of mixed characteristic such that

\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(R))
is non-zero and
\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R)=\operatorname{ara}(\mathfrak{a})
. There are some affirmative answers to Lynch’s conjecture. When
R
is either a ring of a prime
characteristic and
\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R)=\operatorname{ara}(\mathfrak{a})
or that
R
is pure in a regular ring containing a field, Hochster and Jeffries proved that
\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(R))
has
height zero; see

[
22
, Corollary 2.7 and Theorem 2.9]
. Also, we note that Datta-Switala-Zhang’s example shows that the result of Hochster and Jeffries does not hold for an arbitrary commutative Noetherian ring
R
and ideal
\mathfrak{a}
with
\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R)=\operatorname{ara}(\mathfrak{a})
. Note that if
\dim_{R}(R/\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(R)))=\dim_{R}(R)
, then
\operatorname{ht}_{R}{(\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(R)))}=0
and if in addition
c>0
, then
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(R)\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(R))
and so
\dim_{R}(R/\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(R)))=\dim_{R}(R/\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(R))=\dim_{R}(R)
.

We assume for the remainder of this section that
N\neq\mathfrak{a}N
,
c:=\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},N)
and
0=N_{1}\cap\dots\cap N_{n}
is a minimal primary decomposition of the zero submodule of
N

with
\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N/N_{i}):=\{\mathfrak{p}_{i}\}
for all
1\leq i\leq n
.
If
c=\dim_{R}(N)
, then we have

(1.1)

{\textstyle\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))=\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N/{\bigcap_{\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R/\mathfrak{p}_{i})=c}N_{i}})}.

This equality was proved by Lynch
[
25
, Theorem 2.4]
whenever
R
is a complete local ring and
N=R
. Bahmanpour et al.
[
6
, Theorem 1.1]
proved it when
R
is a complete local ring and
\mathfrak{a}
is the maximal ideal of
R
. Finally, it is proved in general form by Atazadeh et al. in
[
2
, Theorem 2.3]
. Therefore in the case
c=\dim_{R}(N)
, Lynch’s conjecture is true and the
N
-height of
\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))
is zero. Also recently, in
[
1
, Theorem 1.2]
, Atazadeh and Naghipour show that the
N
-height of
\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))
is zero in the case when
c=\dim_{R}(N)-1
.
If
c
is not necessarily equal
\dim_{R}(N)
, in
[
18
, Theorem 3.4]
, we gave the following bound for the annihilator of
\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)
:

(1.2)

\textstyle{\operatorname{Ann}_{R}({N}/{\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}_{i}\in\Delta}N_{i}})\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}_{R}({N}/{\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}_{i}\in\Sigma}N_{i}})},

where
\Delta:=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N):\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R/\mathfrak{p})=c\}
and
\Sigma:=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N):\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R/\mathfrak{p})=\dim_{R}(R/\mathfrak{p})=c\}
.
If
c=\dim_{R}(N)
, then
\Delta=\Sigma
and this bound yields the equation (1.1). This paper is divided into 5 section.

In Sec. 2, for a submodule
L
of
N
and
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Supp}_{R}(N)
, we present some properties of
C^{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}(L)
which are used in the sequal.

In Sec. 3, for an arbitrary non-negative integer
t
, we provide a lower bound for the annihilator of
\operatorname{H}^{t}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)
. More precisely, we show that

C^{t}(\mathfrak{a},N):=\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}_{i}\in\Delta(t)}N_{i}=\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Delta(t)}C^{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}(0)=\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}(t)}(N)
is the largest submodule
L
of
N
such that

\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},L)<t
, where
\Delta(t):=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N):\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R/\mathfrak{p})\geq t\}
and
\mathfrak{a}(t):=\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N)\setminus\Delta(t)}\mathfrak{p}
.
There is a following lower bound for the annihilator of
\operatorname{H}^{t}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)

(1.3)

\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N/C^{t}(\mathfrak{a},N))\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{t}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N));

see Theorem
3.2
.
Now set
d:=\operatorname{depth}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},N)
. We denote
C^{d}(\mathfrak{a},N)
and
C^{c}(\mathfrak{a},N)
by
{\rm S}(\mathfrak{a},N)
and
\operatorname{T}(\mathfrak{a},N)
respectively.
For each
t\leq d
, we have
C^{t}(\mathfrak{a},N)={\rm S}(\mathfrak{a},N)
and for each
t\geq c+1
we have
C^{t}(\mathfrak{a},N)=N
and there is the filtration

(1.4)

{\rm S}(\mathfrak{a},N)=C^{d}(\mathfrak{a},N)\subseteq\dots\subseteq C^{c}(\mathfrak{a},N)=\operatorname{T}(\mathfrak{a},N)\subset N

of submodules of
N
such that, for each
d\leq t\leq c
,

\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},C^{t+1}(\mathfrak{a},N)/C^{t}(\mathfrak{a},N))=t
whenever
C^{t}(\mathfrak{a},N)\neq C^{t+1}(\mathfrak{a},N)
; see Proposition
3.7
. The submodule
\operatorname{T}(\mathfrak{a},N)
(respectively
{\rm S}(\mathfrak{a},N)
) of
N
is used in Sec. 4 (respectively Sec. 5) to study the annihilator of the last (respectively first) non-zero local cohomology module of
N
with respect to
\mathfrak{a}
. For a submodule
L
of
N
, we have
L=\mathfrak{a}L
if and only if
L\subseteq{\rm S}(\mathfrak{a},N)
. We can regard this as a version of Nakayama’s Lemma because
{\rm S}(\mathfrak{a},N)=0
if and only if
1-a
is a non-zerodivisor on
N
for all
a\in\mathfrak{a}
; see Proposition
3.7
.

In Sec. 4, we consider the annihilator of the top local cohomology
\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)
. In Theorem
4.1
, the upper bound for the annihilator of
\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)
in (1.2) is improved as follows.

(1.5)

\textstyle{\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N/\operatorname{T}(\mathfrak{a},N))\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N/\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Sigma}C^{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}(0))},

where
\Sigma:=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Supp}_{R}(N):\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R/\mathfrak{p})=\dim_{R}(R/\mathfrak{p})=c\}
. Also, it is proved that if for each
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N)
with
\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R/\mathfrak{p})=c
there exists
\mathfrak{q}\in\Sigma
such that
\mathfrak{p}\subseteq\mathfrak{q}
, then the above upper and lower bounds are equal. By using this theorem, we construct a counterexample to Lynch’s conjecture (see Example
4.5
) which extends Bahmanpour’s example
[
5
]
and Singh–Walther’s example
[
36
]
. We also conclude from this theorem (see Corollary
4.3
) that if
\Sigma\neq\emptyset
or
c=\dim_{R}(N)-1
, then

(1.6)

\operatorname{ht}_{N}{(\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)))}=0.

Next, it is shown in Theorem
4.10
that if
(0:_{\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N/N_{i})}\mathfrak{a})
is finitely generated for all
\mathfrak{p}_{i}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N)
with
\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R/\mathfrak{p}_{i})=c
, then

(1.7)

\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))=\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N/\operatorname{T}(\mathfrak{a},N)).

In particular, if
N
is coprimary and
(0:_{\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)}\mathfrak{a})
is finitely generated, then we have
\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))=\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N)
. Therefore if

is domain and
(0:_{\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(R)}\mathfrak{a})
is finitely generated, then
\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(R)
is faithful.
As an application of Theorem
4.10
, we will prove in Corollary
4.11
that the equality (1.7) holds in the following cases:
(i)

;
(ii)
\dim_{R}(N/\mathfrak{a}N)\leq 1
;
(iii)
\dim_{R}(N)\leq 2
;
(iv)
\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)
is
\mathfrak{a}
-cofinite minimax.
Also, if
(R,\mathfrak{n})
is a complete local ring,
\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)
is Artinian and
(0:_{\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)}\mathfrak{a})
is finitely generated, then it is proved in Lemma 4.9 that

(1.8)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Att}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N):\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R/\mathfrak{p})=c\}

\displaystyle=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{MinAss}_{R}(N):\surd(\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{p})=\mathfrak{n},\,\dim_{R}(R/\mathfrak{p})=c\}.

Now, let
(R,\mathfrak{n})
be a local ring. Then
\dim_{R}(N)-\dim_{R}(N/\mathfrak{a}N)
is a lower bound for the cohomological dimension
c:=\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},N)
of
N
with respect to
\mathfrak{a}
. If
\dim_{R}(N)-\dim_{R}(N/\mathfrak{a}N)=c
, then we show in Theorem
4.15
that

(1.9)

\displaystyle\dim_{R}(N)=\dim_{R}(R/\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))),

\displaystyle\textstyle{\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N/\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Assh}_{R}(N)}C^{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}(0))}

and equality holds if
N
is unmixed, that is,
\dim_{R}(R/\mathfrak{p})=\dim_{R}(N)
for all
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N)
. Note that the above theorem gives an affirmative answer to Lynch’s conjecture. Then we deduce in Corollary
4.16
that if

0\leq t\leq\dim_{R}(N)
and
x_{1},\dots,x_{t}\in\mathfrak{n}
is a part of a system of parameters for
N
, then

(1.10)

\displaystyle\operatorname{cd}_{R}((x_{1},\dots,x_{t}),N)=t,

\displaystyle\dim_{R}(R/\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{t}_{(x_{1},\dots,x_{t})}(N)))=\dim_{R}(N),

\displaystyle\textstyle{\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{t}_{(x_{1},\dots,x_{t})}(N))\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N/\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Assh}_{R}(N)}C^{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}(0))}

and equality holds if
N
is unmixed. Hence in this case also Lynch’s conjecture holds.

In Sec. 5, we consider the annihilator of the first non-zero local cohomology
\operatorname{H}^{d}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)
, where
d:=\operatorname{depth}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},N)
. More precisely, we show in Theorem
5.4
that

(1.11)

\textstyle{\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N/{\rm S}(\mathfrak{a},N))\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{d}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N/\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Sigma}C^{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}(0))\cap(\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Sigma^{\prime}}\mathfrak{p})},

where
\Sigma:=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{MinAss}_{R}(N):\operatorname{ht}_{R/\mathfrak{p}}{((\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{p})/\mathfrak{p})}=d\}
and
\Sigma^{\prime}:=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N)\setminus\operatorname{MinAss}_{R}(N):\operatorname{ht}_{R/\mathfrak{p}}{((\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{p})/\mathfrak{p})}=d\}
. In Lemma
5.1
, for an arbitrary non-negative integer
t
, when
(R,\mathfrak{n})
is local we improve the upper bound that presented in
[
18
, Theorem 3.2]
for the annihilator
\operatorname{H}^{t}_{\mathfrak{n}}(N)
as follows

(1.12)

\textstyle{\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{t}_{\mathfrak{n}}(N))\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N/\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Sigma(t)}C_{\mathfrak{p}}^{N}(0))\cap(\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Sigma^{\prime}(t)}\mathfrak{p}),}

where
\Sigma(t):=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{MinAss}_{R}(N):\dim_{R}(R/\mathfrak{p})=t\}
and
\Sigma^{\prime}(t):=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N)\setminus\operatorname{MinAss}_{R}(N):\dim_{R}(R/\mathfrak{p})=t\}

. Note that for
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Supp}_{R}(N)
,

. Example

shows that to improve the upper bound for the annihilator of
\operatorname{H}^{d}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)
in (1.11), we can not replace
\operatorname{MinAss}_{R}(N)

\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N)

\operatorname{Supp}_{R}(N)

even if

is a complete regular local ring and
\mathfrak{a}
is its maximal ideal. Also, in Lemma
5.2
, when
N
is coprimary, we show that

(1.13)

\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{\operatorname{ht}_{N}{(\mathfrak{a})}}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))=\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N).

In particular, if

\operatorname{H}^{\operatorname{ht}_{R}{(\mathfrak{a})}}_{\mathfrak{a}}(R)

, it is proved that if

\operatorname{H}^{t}_{\mathfrak{n}}(N)\neq 0

.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
L
be a proper submodule of an
R
-module
N
. Then
L
is called a
primary submodule
of
N
when for all
r\in R
and
x\in N
if
rx\in L
, then
x\in L
or
r^{n}N\subseteq L
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
. If
L
is a primary submodule of
N
, then
\mathfrak{p}:=\surd(\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N/L))

is a prime ideal of
R
and
L
is called a
\mathfrak{p}
-primary submodule of
N
. An expression of
L
as an intersection of finitely many primary submodules of
N
is called a
primary decomposition
of
L
in
N
.
Such a primary decomposition

L=N_{1}\cap\dots\cap N_{n}\quad\textrm{with }N_{i}\ \textrm{ $\mathfrak{p}_{i}$-primary in }N\ (1\leq i\leq n)

of
L
in
N
is said to be
minimal primary decomposition
when
\mathfrak{p}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{p}_{n}
are distinct and
\bigcap_{1\leq j\neq i\leq n}N_{j}\nsubseteq N_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq n
. In this case, we have
\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N/L)=\{\mathfrak{p}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{p}_{n}\}
and hence
n
and the set
\{\mathfrak{p}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{p}_{n}\}
are uniquely determined by a minimal primary decomposition of
L
in
N
. When
\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N)
has just one element (or equivalently
0
is a primary submodule of
N
), then
N
is called

coprimary
. See
[
3
,
27
]
for more details about the primary decomposition of modules.

Let
S
be a multiplicatively closed subset of
R
and
L
a submodule of an
R
-module
N
. We denote the contraction of
S^{-1}L
under the canonical map
N\rightarrow S^{-1}N
by
C^{N}_{S}(L)
(in
[
18
]
it is denoted by
S_{N}(L)
). If
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Supp}_{R}(N)
and
S=R\setminus\mathfrak{p}
, we write
C_{\mathfrak{p}}^{N}(L)
instead of
C^{N}_{S}(L)
. For an ideal
\mathfrak{a}
and a prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
R
we show
C_{\mathfrak{p}}^{R}(\mathfrak{a})
by
C_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathfrak{a})
.
Also, a subset
\Sigma
of
\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N)
is called an
isolated subset
of
\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N)
when it satisfies the following condition: if
\mathfrak{q}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N)
and
\mathfrak{q}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
for some
\mathfrak{p}\in\Sigma
, then
\mathfrak{q}\in\Sigma
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
L
be a submodule of an
R
-module
N
. Let
\Sigma
be a finite subset of
\operatorname{Spec}(R)
and
S:=R\setminus\bigcup_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Sigma}\mathfrak{p}
. Then
C^{N}_{S}(L)=\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Sigma}C^{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}(L)
.

Proof.

If
\Sigma=\emptyset
, then
S=R
and so
S^{-1}(L)=S^{-1}(N)=0
,
C^{N}_{S}(L)=N
. On the other hand, the intersection of empty family of submodules of
N
is
N
. Hence the
assertion holds in this case. Now assume that
\Sigma
is not empty.
If
x\in C^{N}_{S}(L)
, then in
S^{-1}(N)
, we have
x/1=l/s
for some
s\in S
and
l\in L
. Now for each
\mathfrak{p}\in\Sigma
, it is easy to see that
x/1=l/s
in
N_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Therefore

x\in C^{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}(L)
for all
\mathfrak{p}\in\Sigma
and so
C^{N}_{S}(L)\subseteq\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Sigma}C^{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}(L)
.

To prove the reverse inclusion, assume that
x
is an arbitrary element in
N
with
x\notin C^{N}_{S}(L)
and it is sufficient for us to show that

x\notin\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Sigma}C^{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}(L)
. Since
x/1\notin S^{-1}(L)
in
S^{-1}(N)
, we have
sx/s=x/1\notin S^{-1}(L)
for all

s\in S
. Therefore
sx\notin L
for all
s\in S
. Consequently,
(L:_{R}x)\cap S=\emptyset
, where
(L:_{R}x)
denotes the ideal
\{r\in R:rx\in L\}
of
R
.
It follows that
(L:_{R}x)\subseteq\bigcup_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Sigma}\mathfrak{p}
and so
(L:_{R}x)\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
for some
\mathfrak{p}\in\Sigma
by the Prime Avoidance Theorem.
Now in
N_{\mathfrak{p}}
, if
x/1=l/s
for some
l\in L
and some
s\in R\setminus\mathfrak{p}
, then
there exists
t\in R\setminus\mathfrak{p}
such that
tsx=tl
and so

ts\in(L:_{R}x)\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
, which is impossible. Therefore
x/1\notin L_{\mathfrak{p}}
or equivalently
x\notin C^{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}(L)
, as required.
∎

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
L
be a proper submodule of a non-zero finitely generated
R
-module
N
and let
L=N_{1}\cap\dots\cap N_{n}
be a minimal primary decomposition of
L
in
N

with
\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N/N_{i}):=\{\mathfrak{p}_{i}\}
for all
1\leq i\leq n
. Let
\Sigma
be an isolated subset of
\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N/L)
. Then

\textstyle{\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}_{i}\in\Sigma}N_{i}=C^{N}_{S}(L)=\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Sigma}C^{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}(L)=\bigcup_{t\in\mathbb{N}}(L:_{N}\mathfrak{b}^{t})},

where
S:=R\setminus\bigcup_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Sigma}\mathfrak{p}
and
\mathfrak{b}:=\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N/L)\setminus\Sigma}\mathfrak{p}
.

Proof.

If
\Sigma=\emptyset
, then
S=R
and
\mathfrak{b}=\surd(\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N/L))
. Thus
\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}_{i}\in\Sigma}N_{i}=C^{N}_{S}(L)=\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Sigma}C^{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}(L)=\bigcup_{t\in\mathbb{N}}(L:_{N}\mathfrak{b}^{t})=N
. So assume that
\Sigma\neq\emptyset
. The first claimed equality is proved in
[
18
, Lemma 2.2]
and the second equality is proved in Lemma
2.1
. Now we show that
\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}_{i}\in\Sigma}N_{i}=\bigcup_{t\in\mathbb{N}}(L:_{N}\mathfrak{b}^{t})
. Assume that
x\in\bigcup_{t\in\mathbb{N}}(L:_{N}\mathfrak{b}^{t})
. Therefore
\mathfrak{b}^{t}x\subseteq L
for some
t\in\mathbb{N}
and so
\mathfrak{b}^{t}x\subseteq N_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq n
. Let
\mathfrak{p}_{i}\in\Sigma
. Since
N_{i}
is a
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
-primary submodule of
N
, it follows from
\mathfrak{b}^{t}x\subseteq N_{i}
that either
x\in N_{i}
or
\mathfrak{b}^{t}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}_{i}
. If
\mathfrak{b}^{t}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}_{i}
, then
\mathfrak{p}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}_{i}
for some
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N/L)\setminus\Sigma
. Since
\Sigma
is an isolated subset of
\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N/L)
, we obtain
\mathfrak{p}\in\Sigma
, which is impossible. Therefore
x\in N_{i}
and so

\bigcup_{t\in\mathbb{N}}(L:_{N}\mathfrak{b}^{t})\subseteq\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}_{i}\in\Sigma}N_{i}
. To prove the reverse inclusion, assume that
x\in\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}_{i}\in\Sigma}N_{i}
and we show that
x\in\bigcup_{t\in\mathbb{N}}(L:_{N}\mathfrak{b}^{t})
. For each
1\leq i\leq n
,
\mathfrak{p}_{i}=\surd(\operatorname{Ann}(N/N_{i}))
and hence there exists
r_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathfrak{p}_{i}^{r_{i}}N\subseteq N_{i}
. Set
r=\max\{r_{i}:\mathfrak{p}_{i}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N/L)\setminus\Sigma\}
. Then
\mathfrak{b}^{r}N\subseteq\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}_{i}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N)\setminus\Sigma}N_{i}
and so
\mathfrak{b}^{r}x\subseteq\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}_{i}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N/L)\setminus\Sigma}N_{i}
. Also
\mathfrak{b}^{r}x\subseteq\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}_{i}\in\Sigma}N_{i}
because
x\in\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}_{i}\in\Sigma}N_{i}
. Therefore

\textstyle{\mathfrak{b}^{r}x\subseteq(\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}_{i}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N/L)\setminus\Sigma}N_{i})\cap(\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}_{i}\in\Sigma}N_{i})=\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}_{i}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N/L)}N_{i}=L}

and consequently
x\in\bigcup_{t\in\mathbb{N}}(L:_{N}\mathfrak{b}^{t})
. This completes the proof.
∎

Let
N
be a non-zero finitely generated
R
-module,
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Supp}_{R}(N)
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then we have

\operatorname{MinAss}_{R}(N/\mathfrak{p}^{n}N)=\operatorname{MinSupp}_{R}(N/\mathfrak{p}^{n}N)={\rm Min}({\rm V}(\mathfrak{p})\cap\operatorname{Supp}_{R}(N))=\{\mathfrak{p}\}.

Thus
\{\mathfrak{p}\}
is an isolated subset of

. Therefore, by Proposition
2.2
, the
\mathfrak{p}
-primary component of each minimal primary decomposition of
\mathfrak{p}^{n}N
in

is

and hence it is uniquely determined by

,
\mathfrak{p}
and
N
.
The
\mathfrak{p}
-primary component of
\mathfrak{p}^{n}N
in
N
is called the
n-th symbolic power
of
\mathfrak{p}
with respect to
N
, denoted by
(\mathfrak{p}N)^{(n)}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
N
be a non-zero finitely generated
R
-module and
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Supp}_{R}(N)
. Then

\textstyle{C^{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}(0)=\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}(\mathfrak{p}N)^{(n)}=\bigcap_{L\in\mathcal{P}}L,}

where
\mathcal{P}
denotes the set of all
\mathfrak{p}
-primary submodules of
N
.

Proof.

We prove the claimed equalities in some steps.

1)
\ker(N\rightarrow N_{\mathfrak{p}})\subseteq\bigcap_{L\in\mathcal{P}}L
. Assume that
x\in\ker(N\rightarrow N_{\mathfrak{p}})
and
L
is an arbitrary
\mathfrak{p}
-primary submodule of
N
. Since
x/1
is zero in
N_{\mathfrak{p}}
, there exists
s\in R\setminus\mathfrak{p}
such that
sx=0\in L
. As
s\notin\mathfrak{p}
and
L
is a
\mathfrak{p}
-primary submodule of
N
, we have
x\in L
.

2)
\bigcap_{L\in\mathcal{P}}L\subseteq\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}(\mathfrak{p}N)^{(n)}
. This inclusion is obvious because
(\mathfrak{p}N)^{(n)}
is a
\mathfrak{p}
-primary submodule of
N
for all
n
. We recall that
(\mathfrak{p}N)^{(n)}=C^{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathfrak{p}^{n}N)
is the unique
\mathfrak{p}
-primary component of every minimal primary decomposition of
\mathfrak{p}^{n}N
in
N
.

3)
\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}(\mathfrak{p}N)^{(n)}\subseteq\ker(N\rightarrow N_{\mathfrak{p}})
. Assume that
x\in\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}(\mathfrak{p}N)^{(n)}
. Then
x/1\in\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}(\mathfrak{p}^{n}R_{\mathfrak{p}})N_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Now Krull’s Intersection Theorem
[
27
, Theorem 8.10]
implies that
\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}(\mathfrak{p}^{n}R_{\mathfrak{p}})N_{\mathfrak{p}}=0
and so

. Therefore
x\in\ker(N\rightarrow N_{\mathfrak{p}})
.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
L
be a submodule of a non-zero finitely generated
R
-module
N
. Then the following statements hold.

(i)

If
\mathfrak{p}_{1},\mathfrak{p}_{2}\in\operatorname{Supp}_{R}(N)
are such that
\mathfrak{p}_{1}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}_{2}
, then
C^{N}_{\mathfrak{p}_{2}}(L)\subseteq C^{N}_{\mathfrak{p}_{1}}(L)
.

(ii)

If
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Supp}_{R}(N)
, then
\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N/C^{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}(L))=C_{\mathfrak{p}}(\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N/L))
. In particular,
\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N/C^{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}(0))=C_{\mathfrak{p}}(\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N))
.

(iii)

If
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Supp}_{R}(N)
, then
C_{\mathfrak{p}}(\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N))\subseteq\mathfrak{q}
for all
\mathfrak{q}\in\operatorname{Supp}_{R}(N)
with
\mathfrak{q}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
. In particular,
\operatorname{ht}_{N}{(C_{\mathfrak{p}}(\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N)))}=0
.

Proof.

The statement (i) is obvious. To prove (ii), assume that
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Supp}_{R}(N)
and
r\in R
. Then we have

\displaystyle\textstyle{r\in\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N/C^{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}(L))\Leftrightarrow rN\subseteq C^{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}(L)\Leftrightarrow{r\over 1}N_{\mathfrak{p}}\subseteq L_{\mathfrak{p}}}

\displaystyle\textstyle{\Leftrightarrow{r\over 1}\in\operatorname{Ann}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(N_{\mathfrak{p}}/L_{\mathfrak{p}})\Leftrightarrow r\in C_{\mathfrak{p}}(\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N/L)).}

(Note that
(\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N/L))_{\mathfrak{p}}=\operatorname{Ann}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(N_{\mathfrak{p}}/L_{\mathfrak{p}})
by
[
3
, Proposition 3.14]
.) Now we prove (iii). Let
\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{q}\in\operatorname{Supp}_{R}(N)
with
\mathfrak{q}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
. Then, by (i),
C_{\mathfrak{p}}(\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N))\subseteq C_{\mathfrak{q}}(\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N))
. Also if
r\in C_{\mathfrak{q}}(\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N))
, then
(r/1)N_{\mathfrak{q}}=0
. Therefore
r/1
is not a unit in
R_{\mathfrak{q}}
and so
r\in\mathfrak{q}
. Thus
C_{\mathfrak{q}}(\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N))\subseteq\mathfrak{q}
. These inclusions prove (iii).
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
L
be a submodule of a non-zero
R
-module
N
and
\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{q}\in\operatorname{Supp}_{R}(N)
with
\mathfrak{p}\subseteq\mathfrak{q}
. Then
(C^{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}(L))_{\mathfrak{q}}=C^{N_{\mathfrak{q}}}_{\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{q}}}(L_{\mathfrak{q}})
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha\in(C^{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}(L))_{\mathfrak{q}}
. Hence, in
N_{\mathfrak{q}}
,
\alpha=x/s
for some
s\in R\setminus\mathfrak{q}
and some
x\in C^{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}(L)
. Therefore, in
N_{\mathfrak{p}}
,
x/1=l/t
for some
l\in L
and some
t\in R\setminus\mathfrak{p}
. It follows that
xtt^{\prime}=lt^{\prime}
for some
t^{\prime}\in R\setminus\mathfrak{p}
. Since
tt^{\prime}/1\in R_{\mathfrak{q}}\setminus\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{q}}
, in
(N_{\mathfrak{q}})_{\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{q}}}
we have

{x\over s}/{1\over 1}={tt^{\prime}\over 1}{x\over s}/{tt^{\prime}\over 1}={lt^{\prime}\over s}/{tt^{\prime}\over 1}\in(L_{\mathfrak{q}})_{\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{q}}}.

This means that
\alpha=x/s
is an element of
C^{N_{\mathfrak{q}}}_{\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{q}}}(L_{\mathfrak{q}})
and so
(C^{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}(L))_{\mathfrak{q}}\subseteq C^{N_{\mathfrak{q}}}_{\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{q}}}(L_{\mathfrak{q}})
.

Now we prove the reverse inclusion. Assume that
\alpha\in C^{N_{\mathfrak{q}}}_{\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{q}}}(L_{\mathfrak{q}})
. Hence
\alpha=x/s
for some
x\in N
and some
s\in R\setminus\mathfrak{q}
, and in
(N_{\mathfrak{q}})_{\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{q}}}
we have
{x\over s}/{1\over 1}\in(L_{\mathfrak{q}})_{\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{q}}}
. Therefore there exists
l/s^{\prime}\in L_{\mathfrak{q}}
with
l\in L,s^{\prime}\in R\setminus\mathfrak{q}
and
t/s^{\prime\prime}\in R_{\mathfrak{q}}\setminus\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{q}}
with
t\in R\setminus\mathfrak{p},s^{\prime\prime}\in R\setminus\mathfrak{q}
such that
{x\over s}/{1\over 1}={l\over s^{\prime}}/{t\over s^{\prime\prime}}
in
(N_{\mathfrak{q}})_{\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{q}}}
. It follows that there exists
t^{\prime}/s^{\prime\prime\prime}\in R_{\mathfrak{q}}\setminus\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{q}}
with
t^{\prime}\in R\setminus\mathfrak{p},s^{\prime\prime\prime}\in R\setminus\mathfrak{q}
such that

{x\over s}{t\over s^{\prime\prime}}{t^{\prime}\over s^{\prime\prime\prime}}={l\over s^{\prime}}{t^{\prime}\over s^{\prime\prime\prime}}
in
N_{\mathfrak{q}}
.
Thus
xtt^{\prime}s^{\prime}s^{\prime\prime\prime}s^{iv}=lt^{\prime}ss^{\prime\prime}s^{\prime\prime\prime}s^{iv}
for some
s^{iv}\in R\setminus\mathfrak{q}
. Since
R\setminus\mathfrak{q}\subseteq R\setminus\mathfrak{p}
,
xt^{\prime\prime}=l^{\prime}
for some
t^{\prime\prime}\in R\setminus\mathfrak{p}
and some
l^{\prime}\in L
. Hence, in
N_{\mathfrak{p}}
we have

x/1=xt^{\prime\prime}/t^{\prime\prime}=l^{\prime}/t^{\prime\prime}\in L_{\mathfrak{p}}

and so
x\in C^{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}(L)
. Therefore
\alpha=x/s\in(C^{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}(L))_{\mathfrak{q}}
and hence
C^{N_{\mathfrak{q}}}_{\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{q}}}(L_{\mathfrak{q}})\subseteq(C^{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}(L))_{\mathfrak{q}}
. This completes the proof.
∎

3.
A lower bound for the annihilator of local cohomology

Let
N
be a finitely generated
R
-module,
\mathfrak{a}
an ideal of
R
and
t
an arbitrary non-negative integer.
In this section, we provide a lower bound for the annihilator of the local cohomology
\operatorname{H}^{t}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)
; see Theorem
3.2
.

We recall that, the
cohomological dimension
of an
R
-module
N
with respect to an ideal
\mathfrak{a}
is defined as
\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},N):=\sup\{i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:\operatorname{H}^{i}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)\neq 0\}
. The
arithmetic rank
of
\mathfrak{a}
, denoted by
\textrm{ara}(\mathfrak{a})
, is the least number of elements of
R
required to generate an ideal which has the same radical as
\mathfrak{a}
. By
[
9
, Corollary 3.3.3]
,
\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},N)\leq\textrm{ara}(\mathfrak{a})<\infty
and hence
\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},N)\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\cup\{-\infty\}
. Also, it follows from
[
9
, Exercise 6.2.6 and Theorem 6.2.7]
that
\operatorname{H}^{i}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)=0
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
if and only if
N=\mathfrak{a}N
. Therefore
\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},N)\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
when
N\neq\mathfrak{a}N
and
\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},N)=-\infty
when
N=\mathfrak{a}N
.

Lemma 3.1

(See
[
15
, Theorem 1.2]
or
[
11
, Proposition 4.7]
)
.

Let
N
and
L
be two finitely generated
R
-modules and
\mathfrak{a}
an ideal of
R
. If
\operatorname{Supp}_{R}(N)\subseteq\operatorname{Supp}_{R}(L)
, then
\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},N)\leq\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},L)
.
In particular,

\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},N)=\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},L)
whenever
\operatorname{Supp}_{R}(N)=\operatorname{Supp}_{R}(L)
.

Let
N
be a finitely generated
R
-module and
\mathfrak{a}
an ideal of
R
. Since
\operatorname{Supp}_{R}(N)=\operatorname{Supp}_{R}(\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N)}R/\mathfrak{p})
, Lemma
3.1
implies that
\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},N)=\max\{\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R/\mathfrak{p}):\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N)\}
. We will often use this fact and Lemma
3.1
without explicit mention. We refer the reader to

[
11
, Sec. 4]
and
[
15
]
for more details about the cohomological dimension.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
N
be a non-zero finitely generated
R
-module and
\mathfrak{a}
an ideal of
R
. Let

0=N_{1}\cap\ldots\cap N_{n}
be a minimal primary decomposition of the zero submodule of
N
with
\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N/N_{i}):=\{\mathfrak{p}_{i}\}
for all
1\leq i\leq n
. For each
t\in\mathbb{Z}
, set
\Delta(t):=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N):\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R/\mathfrak{p})\geq t\}
.
Then the following statements hold.

(i)

There is the following equalities:

\textstyle{\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}_{i}\in\Delta(t)}N_{i}=\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Delta(t)}C_{\mathfrak{p}}^{N}(0)=C^{N}_{S(t)}(0)=\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}(t)}(N),}

where
S(t):=R\setminus\bigcup_{\mathfrak{p}_{i}\in\Delta(t)}\mathfrak{p}
and
\mathfrak{a}(t):=\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N),\ \operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R/\mathfrak{p})<t}\mathfrak{p}
. In particular,
\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}_{i}\in\Delta(t)}N_{i}
is independent of the choice of minimal primary decomposition of the zero submodule of
N
.

(ii)

For each submodule
L
of
N
,
\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},L)<t
if and only if
L\subseteq C^{N}_{S(t)}(0)
.

(iii)

There is the following lower bound for the annihilator of
\operatorname{H}^{t}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)
:

\textstyle{\operatorname{Ann}_{R}({N}/\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Delta(t)}C_{\mathfrak{p}}^{N}(0))=\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Delta(t)}C_{\mathfrak{p}}(\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N))\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{t}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)).}

Proof.

(i) If
\mathfrak{q}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N)
and
\mathfrak{q}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
for some
\mathfrak{p}\in\Delta(t)
, then
t\leq\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R/\mathfrak{p})\leq\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R/\mathfrak{q})
and so
\mathfrak{q}\in\Delta(t)
. Therefore

\Delta(t)
is an isolated subset of
\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N)
and hence (i) follows from Proposition
2.2
.

(ii) Set
C=C^{N}_{S(t)}(0)
. Then, by (i), we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(C)=\textstyle{\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}_{i}\in\Delta(t)}N_{i})=\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N)\setminus\Delta(t)}

\displaystyle=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N):\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R/\mathfrak{p})<t\}.

Hence,
\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},C)<t
and so, for each submodule
L
of
C
,
\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},L)\leq\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},C)<t
. Conversely,
if
L
is a submodule of
N
such that
\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},L)<t
, then

\operatorname{Ass}_{R}({L}/({L\cap C}))=\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(({L+C})/{C})\subseteq\operatorname{Ass}_{R}({N}/{C})=\Delta(t).

Thus if
\operatorname{Ass}_{R}({L}/({L\cap C}))\neq\emptyset
, then
t\leq\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},{L}/({L\cap C}))\leq\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},L)
, which is impossible. Therefore

L\subseteq C
and the proof of (ii) is completed.

(iii) By (ii),
\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},C)<t
. Therefore
\operatorname{H}^{t}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)\cong\operatorname{H}^{t}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N/C)
and hence

\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N/C)\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{t}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N/C))=\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{t}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)).

∎

.

N

and

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
N
be a finitely generated
R
-module and
\mathfrak{a}
an ideal of
R
with
N\neq\mathfrak{a}N
. Then for each
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N)
with
\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{p}\neq R
, there is the following inequalities:

\operatorname{depth}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},N)\leq\operatorname{ht}_{R/\mathfrak{p}}{(\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{p})}\leq\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R/\mathfrak{p}).

In particular, for each
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N)
,
\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R/\mathfrak{p})<\operatorname{depth}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},N)
if and only if
\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R/\mathfrak{p})=-\infty
or equivalently
\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{p}=R
.

Proof.

Assume that
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N)
with
\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{p}\neq R
and
\mathfrak{q}
is a prime ideal of
R
containing
\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{p}
such that
\operatorname{ht}_{R/\mathfrak{p}}{(\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{p})}=\operatorname{ht}_{R/\mathfrak{p}}{(\mathfrak{q}/\mathfrak{p})}
. Then we have

\operatorname{depth}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},N)\leq\operatorname{depth}_{R}(\mathfrak{q},N)\leq 0pt_{R_{\mathfrak{q}}}({N_{\mathfrak{q}}}).

Also, depth formula
[
9
, Lemma 9.3.2]
yields

0pt_{R_{\mathfrak{q}}}({N_{\mathfrak{q}}})\leq 0pt_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(N_{\mathfrak{p}})+\operatorname{ht}_{R/\mathfrak{p}}{(\mathfrak{q}/\mathfrak{p})}=\operatorname{ht}_{R/\mathfrak{p}}{(\mathfrak{q}/\mathfrak{p})}=\operatorname{ht}_{R/\mathfrak{p}}{(\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{p})}.

These inequalities prove the first claimed inequality. To prove the other inequality, we set
\bar{R}:=R/\mathfrak{p}
.
Since
\mathfrak{a}\bar{R}
is a proper ideal of
\bar{R}
, it follows from
[
9
, Exercise 7.3.4]
and the Independence Theorem that

\operatorname{ht}_{\bar{R}}{(\mathfrak{a}\bar{R})}\leq\operatorname{cd}_{\bar{R}}(\mathfrak{a}\bar{R},\bar{R})=\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R/\mathfrak{p}).

This proves the second claimed inequality. The last assertion follows immediately from the first part.
∎

Definition 3.5
.

Let
N
be a finitely generated
R
-module and
\mathfrak{a}
an ideal of
R
. We say that
N
is relative Cohen-Macaulay with respect to
\mathfrak{a}
if
\operatorname{depth}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},N)=\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},N)
or equivalently there is precisely one non-vanishing local cohomology
module of
N
with respect to
\mathfrak{a}
; see
[
31
]
.

Note that if
N=\mathfrak{a}N
, then
\operatorname{H}^{i}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)=0
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and hence
\operatorname{depth}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},N)=\inf\{i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:\operatorname{H}^{i}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)\neq 0\}=\infty
and
\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},N)=\sup\{i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:\operatorname{H}^{i}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)\neq 0\}=-\infty
. Therefore
\operatorname{depth}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},N)\neq\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},N)
and so
N
is not relative Cohen-Macaulay with respect to
\mathfrak{a}
in this case.

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
\mathfrak{a}
be an ideal of
R
and
N
a finitely generated
R
-module. If
N
is relative Cohen-Macaulay with respect to
\mathfrak{a}
, then, for each
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N)
with
\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{p}\neq R
, there is the following equalities:

\operatorname{depth}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},N)=\operatorname{ht}_{R/\mathfrak{p}}{(\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{p})}=\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R/\mathfrak{p})=\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},N).

Proof.

It is an immediate consequence of Lemmas
3.4
and
3.1
.
∎

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
N
be a finitely generated
R
-module and
\mathfrak{a}
an ideal of
R
such that
N\neq\mathfrak{a}N
. Set
d:=\operatorname{depth}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},N)
,
c:=\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},N)
and, for each
t\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\Delta(t):=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N):\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R/\mathfrak{p})\geq t\}
.
The following statements hold.

(i)

There is the following equalities:

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N/C^{t}(\mathfrak{a},N))=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N):\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R/\mathfrak{p})\geq t\},

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(C^{t}(\mathfrak{a},N))=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N):\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R/\mathfrak{p})<t\},

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(C^{t+1}(\mathfrak{a},N)/C^{t}(\mathfrak{a},N))=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N):\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R/\mathfrak{p})=t\}.

In particular,
C^{t}({\mathfrak{a}},N)=N
if and only if
\Delta(t)=\emptyset
; and
C^{t}({\mathfrak{a}},N)=0
if and only if
\Delta(t)=\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N)
.

(ii)

C^{t}(\mathfrak{a},N)=N
for all
t>c
;
C^{t}(\mathfrak{a},N)={\rm S}(\mathfrak{a},N)
for all
t\leq d
; and

\{C^{i}(\mathfrak{a},N)\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
gives the following bounded ascending chain

{\rm S}(\mathfrak{a},N)=C^{d}(\mathfrak{a},N)\subseteq\dots\subseteq C^{c}(\mathfrak{a},N)=\operatorname{T}(\mathfrak{a},N)\subsetneqq C^{c+1}(\mathfrak{a},N)=N

of submodules of
N
such that, for each
d\leq t\leq c
,
\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},C^{t+1}(\mathfrak{a},N)/C^{t}(\mathfrak{a},N))=t
whenever
C^{t}(\mathfrak{a},N)\neq C^{t+1}(\mathfrak{a},N)
.

(iii)

\operatorname{Ass}_{R}({\rm S}(\mathfrak{a},N))=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N):\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{p}=R\}
and
\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N/{\rm S}(\mathfrak{a},N))=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N):\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{p}\neq R\}.

(iv)

{\rm S}(\mathfrak{a},N)=0
if and only if for each
a\in\mathfrak{a}
,
1-a
is a non-zerodivisor on
N
.

(v)

For each submodule
L
of
N
,
L=\mathfrak{a}L
(or equivalently
\operatorname{H}^{i}_{\mathfrak{a}}(L)=0
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
) if and only if
L\subseteq{\rm S}(\mathfrak{a},N)
.
In particular, the following statements are equivalent.

(1)

For each
a\in\mathfrak{a}
,
1-a
is a non-zerodivisor on
N
.

(2)

For each submodule
L
of
N
,
L=\mathfrak{a}L
if and only if
L=0
.

Proof.

(i) Let
0=N_{1}\cap\dots\cap N_{n}
be a minimal primary decomposition of the zero submodule of
N
with
\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N/N_{i})=\{\mathfrak{p}_{i}\}
for all
1\leq i\leq n
. By definition,
C^{t}(\mathfrak{a},N)=\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}_{i}\in\Delta(t)}N_{i}
. Now by
[
18
, Lemma 2.1]
, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N/C^{t}(\mathfrak{a},N))=\Delta(t)=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N):\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R/\mathfrak{p})\geq t\},

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(C^{t}(\mathfrak{a},N))=\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N)\setminus\Delta(t)=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N):\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R/\mathfrak{p})<t\}.

To prove the last claimed equality, assume that
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(C^{t+1}(\mathfrak{a},N)/C^{t}(\mathfrak{a},N))
. Since
C^{t+1}(\mathfrak{a},N)/C^{t}(\mathfrak{a},N)
is a submodule of
N/C^{t}(\mathfrak{a},N)
,
\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R/\mathfrak{p})\geq t
. On the other hand,
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Supp}_{R}(C^{t+1}(\mathfrak{a},N))
and so
\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R/\mathfrak{p})\leq\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},C^{t+1}(\mathfrak{a},N))<t+1
. Therefore

\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(C^{t+1}(\mathfrak{a},N)/C^{t}(\mathfrak{a},N))\subseteq\{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N):\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R/\mathfrak{p})=t\}.

Now we prove the reverse inclusion. Assume that
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N)
and
\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R/\mathfrak{p})=t
. Hence
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N/C^{t}(\mathfrak{a},N))\setminus\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N/C^{t+1}(\mathfrak{a},N))
. It follows from the exact sequence
0\rightarrow C^{t+1}(\mathfrak{a},N)/C^{t}(\mathfrak{a},N)\rightarrow N/C^{t}(\mathfrak{a},N)\rightarrow N/C^{t+1}(\mathfrak{a},N)\rightarrow 0
that
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(C^{t+1}(\mathfrak{a},N)/C^{t}(\mathfrak{a},N))
. This proves the reverse inclusion.

(ii) If
t>c
, then
\Delta(t)=\emptyset
and so
C^{t}(\mathfrak{a},N)=N
by (i). Also for
t\leq d
and
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N)
, Lemma
3.4
implies that
\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R/\mathfrak{p})\geq t
when
\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{p}\neq R
, and
\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R/\mathfrak{p})=-\infty
when
\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{p}=R
. Thus
\Delta(t)=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N):\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{p}\neq R\}=\Delta(d)
and consequently
C^{t}(\mathfrak{a},N)={\rm S}(\mathfrak{a},N)
for all
t\leq d
. Also, it is clear by definition that
\operatorname{T}(\mathfrak{a},N)\neq N
and
C^{t}(\mathfrak{a},N)\subseteq C^{t+1}(\mathfrak{a},N)
for all
t
. The final assertion follows from (i).

(iii) By Lemma
3.4
, we have
\Delta(d)=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N):\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{p}\neq R\}
. Now (iii) follows from (i).

(iv) Since
\operatorname{Zdv}_{R}(N)=\bigcup_{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N)}\mathfrak{p}
,
1-a
is a non-zerodivisor on
N
for all
a\in\mathfrak{a}
, if and only if
\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{p}\neq R
for all
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N)
or equivalently
\operatorname{Ass}_{R}({\rm S}(\mathfrak{a},N))=\emptyset
. This proves (iv).

(v) By (ii),
C^{0}(\mathfrak{a},N)={\rm S}(\mathfrak{a},N)
. Therefore it follows from Theorem
3.2
(ii) that for each submodule
L
of
N
,
\operatorname{H}^{i}_{\mathfrak{a}}(L)=0
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
(or equivalently
L=\mathfrak{a}L
) if and only if
L\subseteq{\rm S}(\mathfrak{a},N)
. The last assertion follows from (iv). This completes the proof.
∎

4.
An upper bound for the annihilator of top local cohomology and Lynch’s conjecture

In
[
18
, Theorem 3.4]
, we provide a bound for the annihilator of top local cohomology. In the following theorem, we establish a sharper upper bound for this annihilator. Using this theorem, we can compute the annihilators of top local cohomology modules in certain cases and we are able to construct a counterexample to Lynch’s conjecture.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
N
be a finitely generated
R
-module and
\mathfrak{a}
an ideal of
R
with
N\neq\mathfrak{a}N
. Set
c:=\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},N)
,
\Delta:=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N):\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R/\mathfrak{p})=c\}
and
\Sigma:=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Supp}_{R}(N):\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R/\mathfrak{p})=\dim_{R}(R/\mathfrak{p})=c\}
.
Then

\displaystyle{\operatorname{Ann}_{R}({N}/\operatorname{T}({\mathfrak{a}},N))}\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))\textstyle{\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N/\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Sigma}C^{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}(0))}.

Moreover, if for each
\mathfrak{p}\in\Delta
there exists
\mathfrak{q}\in\Sigma
with
\mathfrak{p}\subseteq\mathfrak{q}
, then

\textstyle{\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))=\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N/\operatorname{T}(\mathfrak{a},N))=\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N/\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Sigma}C^{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}(0)).}

Proof.

The first claimed inclusion follows from Theorem
3.2
(iii). Now we prove the second inclusion. If
\Sigma=\emptyset
, then
\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Sigma}C^{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}(0)=N
and so there is nothing to prove. Hence assume that
\Sigma\neq\emptyset
and
\mathfrak{p}\in\Sigma
. Let
L
be an arbitrary
\mathfrak{p}
-primary submodule of
N
. Since
\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N/L)=\{\mathfrak{p}\}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\{\mathfrak{q}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N/L):\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R/\mathfrak{q})=\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},N/L)\}=\{\mathfrak{p}\}

\displaystyle=\{\mathfrak{q}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N/L):\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R/\mathfrak{q})=\dim_{R}(R/\mathfrak{q})=\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},N/L)\}.

Therefore
\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N/L))=\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N/L)
by
[
18
, Theorem 3.4(iii)]
.
Also, the exact sequence
0\rightarrow L\rightarrow N\rightarrow N/L\rightarrow 0
induces the epimorphism

\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)\rightarrow\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N/L)
and so

\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N/L))=\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N/L).

Since
\mathfrak{p}
is an arbitrary element of
\Sigma
and
L
is an arbitrary
\mathfrak{p}
-primary submodule of
N
, it follows from the above inclusion in view of Lemma
2.3
that

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))\subseteq\textstyle{\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Sigma}\,\bigcap_{L\in\mathcal{P}}\operatorname{Ann}_{R}({N/L})}=\textstyle{\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Sigma}\operatorname{Ann}_{R}({N/\bigcap_{L\in\mathcal{P}}L})}

\displaystyle=\textstyle{\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Sigma}\operatorname{Ann}_{R}({N/C^{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}(0)})}=\textstyle{\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N/\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Sigma}C^{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}(0)),}

where
\mathcal{P}
denotes the set of all
\mathfrak{p}
-primary submodules of
N
. This proves the second claimed inclusion.

Finally, assume that
0=N_{1}\cap\dots\cap N_{n}
is a minimal primary decomposition of the zero submodule of
N
with
\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N/N_{i}):=\{\mathfrak{p}_{i}\}
for all
1\leq i\leq n
and assume that for each
\mathfrak{p}_{i}\in\Delta
there exists
\mathfrak{q}_{i}\in\Sigma
such that
\mathfrak{p}_{i}\subseteq\mathfrak{q}_{i}
. Since
N_{i}
is a
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
-primary submodule of
N
, by Lemma
2.3
, we have
C^{N}_{\mathfrak{p}_{i}}(0)\subseteq N_{i}
and so
\bigcap_{\mathfrak{q}\in\Sigma}C^{N}_{\mathfrak{q}}(0)\subseteq C^{N}_{\mathfrak{q}_{i}}(0)\subseteq C^{N}_{\mathfrak{p}_{i}}(0)\subseteq N_{i}
. Since
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
is an arbitrary element of
\Delta
, we obtain
\bigcap_{\mathfrak{q}\in\Sigma}C^{N}_{\mathfrak{q}}(0)\subseteq\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}_{i}\in\Delta}N_{i}=\operatorname{T}(\mathfrak{a},N),
see Definition
3.3
. Hence

\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N/\bigcap_{\mathfrak{q}\in\Sigma}C^{N}_{\mathfrak{q}}(0))\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N/\operatorname{T}(\mathfrak{a},N))
.
Now the first part of theorem gives the claimed equalities and the proof is completed.
∎

Let
N
be a finitely generated
R
-module of dimension
n\geq 1
,
\mathfrak{a}
an ideal of
R
with
N\neq\mathfrak{a}N
and
c:=\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},N)
. In
[
1
, Theorems 1.1 and 1.2]
, Atazadeh and Naghipour as one of their main results proved that
\operatorname{ht}_{N}{(\operatorname{Ann}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)))}<n-c
. In particular,
\operatorname{ht}_{N}{(\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)))}=0
when
c=n-1
. In the following corollary, we prove their result by using Theorem
4.1
and we also show that if there exist
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Supp}_{R}(N)
with
\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R/\mathfrak{p})=\dim_{R}(R/\mathfrak{p})=c
(
c
is not necessarily equal
n-1
), then
\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)
has
N
-height zero.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
N
be a finitely generated
R
-module,
\mathfrak{a}
an ideal of
R
with
N\neq\mathfrak{a}N
and
c:=\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},N)
. Then

\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N\otimes_{R}(\cdot))\cong\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)\otimes_{R}(\cdot)
on the category of
R
-modules and
R
-homomorphisms.

Proof.

Set
\bar{R}:=R/\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N)
. It follows from the Independence Theorem
[
9
, Theore 4.2.1]
and Lemma
3.1
that
\operatorname{cd}_{\bar{R}}(\mathfrak{a}\bar{R},\bar{R})=\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},\bar{R})=\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},N)=c
. Hence
\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}\bar{R}}(\cdot)
is a right exact functor on the category of
\bar{R}
-modules. Furthermore
\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}\bar{R}}(\cdot)
is an additive functor that preserves direct sums. Therefore
\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}\bar{R}}(\cdot)
is naturally isomorphic to
\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}\bar{R}}(\bar{R})\otimes_{\bar{R}}(\cdot)
on the category of
\bar{R}
-modules; see
[
34
, Theorem 5.45]
. Since
N
is an
\bar{R}
-module,
N\otimes_{R}M
has
\bar{R}
-module structure for each
R
-module
M
and so, on the category of
R
-modules, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N\otimes_{R}(\cdot))\cong\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}\bar{R}}(N\otimes_{R}(\cdot))\cong N\otimes_{R}(\cdot)\otimes_{\bar{R}}\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}\bar{R}}(\bar{R})

\displaystyle\cong(\cdot)\otimes_{R}N\otimes_{\bar{R}}\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}\bar{R}}(\bar{R})\cong(\cdot)\otimes_{R}\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}\bar{R}}(N)\cong(\cdot)\otimes_{R}\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N).

∎

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{a}
be an ideal of
R
and
N
a finitely generated
R
-module of dimension
n
such that
N\neq\mathfrak{a}N
. Let
c:=\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},N)
and
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Supp}_{R}(N)
is such that
\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R/\mathfrak{p})=c
.

(i)

If
\dim_{R}(R/\mathfrak{p})=c
, then
\operatorname{ht}_{N}{(\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)))}=0
.

(ii)

If
\dim_{R}(R/\mathfrak{p})>c
, then
\operatorname{ht}_{N}{(\mathfrak{p})}\leq n-c-1
.

In particular, if
n\geq 1
and
c=n-1
, then
\operatorname{ht}_{N}{(\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)))}=0
.

Proof.

We set
J:=\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))
. If
\dim_{R}(R/\mathfrak{p})=c
, then
J\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N/C^{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}(0))=C_{\mathfrak{p}}(\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N))
by Theorem
4.1
. Hence (i) is an immediate consequence of Lemma
2.4
(iii). Now to prove (ii), suppose that
\dim_{R}(R/\mathfrak{p})>c
. Therefore

c=\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R/\mathfrak{p})<\dim_{R}(R/\mathfrak{p})\leq n-\operatorname{ht}_{N}{(\mathfrak{p})}

and hence
\operatorname{ht}_{N}{(\mathfrak{p})}<n-c
. This proves (ii). Finally, assume that
c=n-1
. By Lemma
4.2
,
\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N/JN)\cong\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)/J\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)
. Since
J\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)=0
,

\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N/JN)\cong\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)
.
Therefore
\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},N/JN)=c
. Hence there exists
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Supp}_{R}(N/JN)=\operatorname{V}(J)
such that
\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R/\mathfrak{p})=c
. If
\dim_{R}(R/\mathfrak{p})=c
, then
\operatorname{ht}_{N}{(J)}=0
by (i). Otherwise, by (ii),
\operatorname{ht}_{N}{(\mathfrak{p})}=0
. As
J\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
, we obtain
\operatorname{ht}_{N}{(J)}=0
.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b}
be ideals of
R
and
N
a finitely generated
R
-module. It follows from the Independence Theorem that
\operatorname{H}^{i}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)\cong\operatorname{H}^{i}_{\mathfrak{b}}(N)
for all
i
when

\mathfrak{a}+\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N)=\mathfrak{b}+\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N)
; see
[
9
, Theorem 4.2.1]
. This fact is used in Example
4.5
. Furthermore, we proved, in
[
17
, Theorem 2.2]
, that

\displaystyle\inf\{i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:\operatorname{H}^{i}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)\ncong\operatorname{H}^{i}_{\mathfrak{b}}(N)\}=\operatorname{f-grade}(\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{a}\cap\mathfrak{b},N)

\displaystyle=\inf\{0pt{N_{\mathfrak{p}}}:\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{V}(\mathfrak{a}+\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N))\triangle\operatorname{V}(\mathfrak{b}+\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N))\},

where
A\triangle B=A\cup B-A\cap B
denotes the symmetric difference of the sets
A
and
B
and for ideals
\mathfrak{a}
and
\mathfrak{b}
,
\operatorname{f-grade}(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b},N)
denotes
the
\mathfrak{a}
-filter grade of
\mathfrak{b}
on
N
; see
[
19
]
for definition and basic properties. In particular,
\operatorname{H}^{i}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)\cong\operatorname{H}^{i}_{\mathfrak{b}}(N)
for all
i
if and only if

\surd(\mathfrak{a}+\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N))=\surd(\mathfrak{b}+\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N))
.

Now, by using Theorem
4.1
, we construct a counterexample to Lynch’s conjecture which extends the examples given in
[
5
]
and
[
36
]
.

Example 4.5
.

Let
S
be a commutative Noetherian ring of finite dimension
d\geq 3
such that there exists a subset
U:=\{u_{1},\dots,u_{d}\}
of
S
with
\operatorname{ht}_{S}{(U)}=d
and suppose that each ideal generated by a subset of
U
is a prime ideal of
S
. (When
S
is a local ring, then it is easy to see that
S
must be a regular ring with maximal ideal
(U)
and
U
is a regular system of parameters for
S
.)
Let
X
,
Y
and
Z
be disjoint non-empty subsets of
U
such that
|X|\leq|Y|\leq|Z|
(here, for a set
A
,
|A|
denotes the cardinal number of
A
) and let
X^{\prime}
and
Y^{\prime}
be non-empty subsets of
X
and
Y
respectively. Set
J:=(X)\cap(Y)\cap(Z)
,
R:=S/J
and
I:=(X^{\prime})+(Y^{\prime})+J/J
. Then

(i)

\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(R)=\{\mathfrak{p}_{1}:=(X)/J,\ \mathfrak{p}_{2}:=(Y)/J,\ \mathfrak{p}_{3}:=(Z)/J\}
.

(ii)

\operatorname{depth}_{R}(I,R/\mathfrak{p}_{1})=\operatorname{cd}_{R}(I,R/\mathfrak{p}_{1})=|Y^{\prime}|
,
\operatorname{depth}_{R}(I,R/\mathfrak{p}_{2})=\operatorname{cd}_{R}(I,R/\mathfrak{p}_{2})=|X^{\prime}|
and
\operatorname{depth}_{R}(I,R/\mathfrak{p}_{3})=\operatorname{cd}_{R}(I,R/\mathfrak{p}_{3})=|X^{\prime}|+|Y^{\prime}|
.

(iii)

\dim_{R}(R/\mathfrak{p}_{1})=d-|X|
,
\dim_{R}(R/\mathfrak{p}_{2})=d-|Y|
and
\dim_{R}(R/\mathfrak{p}_{3})=d-|Z|
.

(iv)

\Gamma_{I}(R)=0
,
\dim_{R}(R)=\dim_{R}(R/\Gamma_{I}(R))=d-|X|
and
c:=\operatorname{cd}_{R}(I,R)=|X^{\prime}|+|Y^{\prime}|
.

(v)

Set
q:=(U-X^{\prime}\cup Y^{\prime})/J
, then
\mathfrak{p}_{3}\subseteq\mathfrak{q}
and
\operatorname{cd}_{R}(I,R/\mathfrak{q})=\dim_{R}(R/\mathfrak{q})=c
.

(vi)

\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{I}(R))=(Z)/J
and
\dim_{R}(R/\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{I}(R))=d-|Z|
. In particular,

\dim_{R}(R/\Gamma_{I}(R))-\dim_{R}(R/\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{I}(R))=|Z|-|X|.

Proof.

Since
\operatorname{ht}_{S}{(U)}=d
, Krull’s Generalized Principal Ideal Theorem
[
27
, Theorem 13.5]
implies that
u_{i}\notin(U\setminus\{u_{i}\})
for all
1\leq i\leq d
. For each subset
V
of
U
, since
(V\setminus\{u_{i}\})
is a prime ideal of
S
,
\operatorname{Ass}_{S}(S/(V\setminus\{u_{i}\}))=\{(V\setminus\{u_{i}\})\}
and so
u_{i}
is a non-zerodivisor on
S/(V\setminus\{u_{i}\})
for all
1\leq i\leq d
. Hence every permutation of
u_{1},\dots,u_{d}
is an
S
-sequence.

Next, for each
1\leq i\leq d
, since
u_{i}\notin(u_{1},\dots,u_{i-1})
and
(u_{1},\dots,u_{i-1})
is a prime ideal of
S
, we have
\dim_{S}(S/(u_{1},\dots,u_{i}))\leq\dim_{S}(S/(u_{1},\dots,u_{i-1}))-1
. We can now repeat this argument to deduce that
\dim_{S}(S/(u_{1},\dots,u_{i}))\leq\dim_{S}(S)-i=d-i
for all
1\leq i\leq d
. On the other hand, the strict chain of prime ideals
(u_{1},\dots,u_{i})\subset\dots\subset(u_{1},\dots,u_{d})
yields
\dim_{S}(S/(u_{1},\dots,u_{i}))\geq d-i
. Therefore
\dim_{S}(S/(u_{1},\dots,u_{i}))=d-i
for all
1\leq i\leq d
. By renaming the elements of
U
, we deduce that
\dim_{S}(S/(V))=d-|V|
for all subsets
V
of
U
. These facts are used in the sequel.

(i) It is clear that
\mathfrak{p}_{1}\cap\mathfrak{p}_{2}\cap\mathfrak{p}_{3}=0
is a minimal primary decomposition of
0
in
R
and
\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(R)=\{\mathfrak{p}_{1},\mathfrak{p}_{2},\mathfrak{p}_{3}\}
.

(ii) It follows from the Independence Theorem (see Remark
4.4
) that

\operatorname{H}^{i}_{I}(R/\mathfrak{p}_{1})\cong\operatorname{H}^{i}_{I}(S/(X))\cong\operatorname{H}^{i}_{(X^{\prime},Y^{\prime})}(S/(X))\cong\operatorname{H}^{i}_{(Y^{\prime})}(S/(X)).

We also have

|Y^{\prime}|=\operatorname{depth}_{S}((Y^{\prime}),S/(X))\leq\operatorname{cd}_{S}((Y^{\prime}),S/(X))\leq\operatorname{ara}(Y^{\prime})\leq|Y^{\prime}|.

Therefore
\operatorname{H}^{i}_{(Y^{\prime})}(S/(X))
and consequently
\operatorname{H}^{i}_{I}(R/\mathfrak{p}_{1})
are non-zero only at
i=|Y^{\prime}|
. Similarly, we have

\operatorname{H}^{i}_{I}(R/\mathfrak{p}_{2})\cong\operatorname{H}^{i}_{(X^{\prime})}(S/(Y))
and
\operatorname{H}^{i}_{I}(R/\mathfrak{p}_{3})\cong\operatorname{H}^{i}_{(X^{\prime},Y^{\prime})}(S/(Z)).

Thus
\operatorname{H}^{i}_{I}(R/\mathfrak{p}_{2})
is non-zero only at
i=|Y^{\prime}|
and
\operatorname{H}^{i}_{I}(R/\mathfrak{p}_{3})
is non-zero only at
i=|X^{\prime}|+|Y^{\prime}|
.

(iii) As was mentioned at the beginning of the proof, we have
\dim_{R}(R/\mathfrak{p}_{1})=\dim_{S}(S/(X))=d-|X|
,
\dim_{R}(R/\mathfrak{p}_{2})=\dim_{S}(S/(Y))=d-|Y|
and
\dim_{R}(R/\mathfrak{p}_{3})=\dim_{S}(S/(Z))=d-|Z|
.

(iv) Since
\operatorname{Spec}(R)=\operatorname{Supp}_{R}(\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(R)}R/\mathfrak{p})
, we obtain

\textstyle{c:=\operatorname{cd}_{R}(I,R)=\operatorname{cd}_{R}(I,\bigoplus_{1\leq i\leq 3}R/\mathfrak{p}_{i})=\max_{1\leq i\leq 3}\operatorname{cd}_{R}(I,R/\mathfrak{p}_{i})=|X^{\prime}|+|Y^{\prime}|},

\textstyle{\dim_{R}(R)=\max_{1\leq i\leq 3}\dim_{R}({R/\mathfrak{p}_{i}})=d-|X|.}

Next, as
X^{\prime}
and
Y^{\prime}
are nonempty sets, there exists
x\in X^{\prime}
and
y\in Y^{\prime}
. We have
x+y+J\in I\setminus\bigcup_{1\leq i\leq 3}\mathfrak{p}_{i}
because
X,Y,Z
are disjoint sets and
u_{i}\notin(U\setminus\{u_{i}\})
for all
1\leq i\leq d
. Therefore
x+y+J\in I
is a non-zerodivisor on
R
and hence
\Gamma_{I}(R)=0
. This completes the proof of (iv).

(v) It is clear that
Z\subseteq U-X\cup Y\subseteq U-X^{\prime}\cup Y^{\prime}
and consequently
\mathfrak{p}_{3}\subseteq\mathfrak{q}
. Also, it follows from the Independence Theorem that (note that
J\subseteq(U-X^{\prime}\cup Y^{\prime})
)

\operatorname{H}^{i}_{I}(R/\mathfrak{q})\cong\operatorname{H}^{i}_{I}(S/(U-X^{\prime}\cup Y^{\prime}))\cong\operatorname{H}^{i}_{(X^{\prime},Y^{\prime})}(S/(U-X^{\prime}\cup Y^{\prime})).

Similar to (ii) we can deduce that
\operatorname{H}^{i}_{(X^{\prime},Y^{\prime})}(S/(U-X^{\prime}\cup Y^{\prime}))
and consequently
\operatorname{H}^{i}_{I}(R/\mathfrak{q})
are non-zero only at
i=|X^{\prime}|+|Y^{\prime}|
. Therefore
\operatorname{cd}_{R}(I,R/\mathfrak{q})=c
. Finally, we have

\dim_{R}(R/\mathfrak{q})=\dim_{S}(S/(U-X^{\prime}\cup Y^{\prime}))=d-|U-X^{\prime}\cup Y^{\prime}|=|X^{\prime}|+|Y^{\prime}|=c.

(vi) It follows from (v) and Theorem
4.1
that

\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{I}(R))=\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(R/\mathfrak{p}_{3})=\mathfrak{p}_{3}

and hence
\dim_{R}(R/\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{I}(R)))=d-|Z|
. Therefore

\dim_{R}(R/\Gamma_{I}(R))-\dim_{R}(R/\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{I}(R)))=d-|X|-(d-|Z|)=|Z|-|X|.

∎

Remark 4.6
.

(i) (Bahmanpour’s example
[
5
, Example 3.2]
). Let
S
be a regular local ring of dimension
d\geq 7
and
U:=\{u_{1},\dots,u_{d}\}
a system of parameters for
S
. Let
l
be an integer with
7\leq l\leq d
. Set
X:=\{u_{1},u_{2}\}
,
Y:=\{u_{3},u_{4}\}
,
Z:=\{u_{5},\dots,u_{l}\}
,
X^{\prime}:=\{u_{1}\}
and
Y^{\prime}:=\{u_{3}\}
. Let

J:=(X)\cap(Y)\cap(Z)
,
R:=S/J
and
I:=(X^{\prime})+(Y^{\prime})+J/J
. Then, by Example
4.5
, we have

\displaystyle c:=\operatorname{cd}_{R}(I,R)=|X^{\prime}|+|Y^{\prime}|=2,

\displaystyle\dim_{R}(R/\Gamma_{I}(R))=\dim_{R}(R)=d-|X|=d-2,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{I}(R))=(Z)/J=(u_{5},\dots,u_{l})/J,

\displaystyle\dim_{R}(R/\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{I}(R)))=d-|Z|=d-l+4<d-2.

In particular, Lynch’s conjecture does not hold in this case. Note that Bahmanpour, in
[
5
, Example 3.2]
, obtained
\dim_{R}(R/\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{I}(R)))
without computing
\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{I}(R))
.

(ii) (Singh–Walther’s example
[
36
]
). Let
K
be a field and
S:=K[x,y,z_{1},z_{2}]
(or
S:=K[[x,y,z_{1},z_{2}]]
). If we set
U:=\{x,y,z_{1},z_{2}\}
,
X=X^{\prime}:=\{x\}
,
Y=Y^{\prime}:=\{y\}
,
Z:=\{z_{1},z_{2}\}
,
J:=(X)\cap(Y)\cap(Z)=(xyz_{1},xyz_{2})
,
R:=S/J
and
I:=(X^{\prime})+(Y^{\prime})+J/J=(x,y)/J
, then Example
4.5
implies that

\displaystyle c:=\operatorname{cd}_{R}(I,R)=|X^{\prime}|+|Y^{\prime}|=2,

\displaystyle\dim_{R}(R/\Gamma_{I}(R))=\dim_{R}(R)=d-|X|=4-1=3,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{I}(R))=(Z)/J=(z_{1},z_{2})/J,

\displaystyle\dim_{R}(R/\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{I}(R)))=d-|Z|=4-2=2.

It follows that Lynch’s conjecture is false. This example also shows that
[
25
, Proposition 4.3 and Theorem 4.4]
are not true.

We recall that an
R
-module
N
is called
minimax
if there exists a finitely generated submodule
L
of
N
such that
N/L
is Artinian. The class of minimax modules includes all Noetherian and all Artinian modules; see
[
38
]
. Also, for an ideal
\mathfrak{a}
of
R
, Hartshorne
[
21
]
defined an
R
-module
N
to be
\mathfrak{a}
-cofinite
if
\operatorname{Supp}_{R}(N)\subseteq\operatorname{V}(\mathfrak{a})
and
\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R}(R/\mathfrak{a},N)
is finitely generated for all
i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. The following lemmas are needed to prove our next theorem.

Lemma 4.7

(
[
28
, Theorem 1.6]
)
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{n})
be a complete local ring,
\mathfrak{a}
a proper ideal of
R
and
N
an Artinian
R
-module. Then the following conditions on
N
are equivalent:

(i)

N
is
\mathfrak{a}
-cofinite.

(ii)

(0:_{N}\mathfrak{a})
is finitely generated

(iii)

For each attached prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
N
,
\surd(\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{p})=\mathfrak{n}
.

Lemma 4.8

(
[
29
, Corollary 4.4]
)
.

Let
\mathfrak{a}
be an ideal of
R
. The class of
\mathfrak{a}
-cofinite minimax
R
-modules is closed under taking submodules,
quotients and extensions, i.e., it is a Serre subcategory of the category of
R
-modules.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{n})
be a complete local ring,
\mathfrak{a}
an ideal of
R
,
N
a finitely generated
R
-module with
N\neq\mathfrak{a}N
and
c:=\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},N)
. In
[
33
, Theorem 2.4]
, Rastgoo and Nazari proved that if
\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)
is
\mathfrak{a}
-cofinite Artinian, then
\operatorname{Att}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{MinAss}_{R}(N):\dim_{R}(R/\mathfrak{p})=c,\surd(\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{p})=\mathfrak{n}\}
. The following lemma generalizes this theorem.

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{n})
be a complete local ring,
\mathfrak{a}
an ideal of
R
,
N
a finitely generated
R
-module with
N\neq\mathfrak{a}N
and
c:=\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},N)
. Suppose that
\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)
is Artinian and
(0:_{\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)}\mathfrak{a})
is finitely generated. Set
\Delta:=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N):\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R/\mathfrak{p})=c\}
and

\Sigma:=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{MinAss}_{R}(N):\dim_{R}(R/\mathfrak{p})=c,\ \surd(\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{p})=\mathfrak{n}\}
. Then

\operatorname{MinAtt}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))=\operatorname{Att}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))=\Delta=\Sigma,

\textstyle{\operatorname{T}(\mathfrak{a},N)=\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Sigma}C^{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}(0)},

\textstyle{\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))=\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N/\operatorname{T}(\mathfrak{a},N))}.

Proof.

Note that, by Lemma
4.7
,
\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)
is
\mathfrak{a}
-cofinite and Artinian. We prove the claimed equalities in some steps.

1)
\operatorname{Att}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))\subseteq\Delta
. Assume that
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Att}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))
. Hence by Lemma
4.2
,
\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N/\mathfrak{p}N)\cong\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)/\mathfrak{p}\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)
.
Thus
\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N/\mathfrak{p}N)\neq 0
because
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Att}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))
. Since
\operatorname{Att}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))\subseteq\operatorname{V}(\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N))
, we have
\operatorname{Supp}_{R}(N/\mathfrak{p}N)=\operatorname{Supp}_{R}(R/\mathfrak{p})
and so
\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R/\mathfrak{p})=\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},N/\mathfrak{p}N)=c
. Therefore
\mathfrak{p}\in\Delta
.

2)
\Sigma\subseteq\Delta
. If
\mathfrak{p}\in\Sigma
, then
\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R/\mathfrak{p})=\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{p},R/\mathfrak{p})=\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{n},R/\mathfrak{p})=\dim_{R}(R/\mathfrak{p})=c
and so
\mathfrak{p}\in\Delta
.

3)
\Delta\subseteq\Sigma
and
\Delta\subseteq\operatorname{MinAtt}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))
. Assume that
\mathfrak{p}\in\Delta

and
L
is an arbitrary
\mathfrak{p}
-primary submodule of
N
. Since
\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N/L)=\{\mathfrak{p}\}
, we obtain
\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},N/L)=\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R/\mathfrak{p})=c
. Also, it follows from the exact sequence
\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)\rightarrow\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N/L)\rightarrow 0
in view of Lemma
4.8
that
\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N/L)
is
\mathfrak{a}
-cofinite Artinian. Now assume that
\mathfrak{q}\in\operatorname{Att}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N/L))
. Since

\operatorname{Att}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N/L))\subseteq\operatorname{V}({\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N/L))})\subseteq\operatorname{V}(\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N/L)),
we have
\mathfrak{p}\subseteq\mathfrak{q}
. By Lemma
4.2
,
\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N/\mathfrak{q}N)\cong\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)/\mathfrak{q}\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)
.
Thus
\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N/\mathfrak{q}N)\neq 0
because
\mathfrak{q}\in\operatorname{Att}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))
. Since
\operatorname{Supp}_{R}(N/\mathfrak{q}N)=\operatorname{Supp}_{R}(R/\mathfrak{q})
,
\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R/\mathfrak{q})=\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},N/\mathfrak{q}N)=c
. Also, Lemma
4.7
implies that
\surd(\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{q})=\mathfrak{n}
and hence, by Remark
4.4
,
c=\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R/\mathfrak{q})=\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{n},R/\mathfrak{q})=\dim_{R}{R/\mathfrak{q}}
. Therefore
\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))\subseteq C_{\mathfrak{q}}(\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N))
by Theorem
4.1
. We show that
\mathfrak{q}
is minimal in
\operatorname{Supp}_{R}(N)
. Assume that
\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}\in{\rm Min}\operatorname{Supp}_{R}(N)
is such that
\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathfrak{q}
. Then, by Lemma
2.4
(iii),

\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))\subseteq\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}
. Thus
\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}\in{\rm Min}\operatorname{V}(\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)))={\rm Min}\operatorname{Att}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))
and so
\surd(\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{q}^{\prime})=\mathfrak{n}
by Lemma
4.7
. Hence
\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R/\mathfrak{q}^{\prime})=\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{n},R/\mathfrak{q}^{\prime})=\dim_{R}{R/\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}}
. Also, we have
c=\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R/\mathfrak{q})\leq\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R/\mathfrak{q}^{\prime})\leq\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},N)=c
. It follows that
\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R/\mathfrak{q}^{\prime})=\dim_{R}{R/\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}}=c
. Therefore
\mathfrak{q}=\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}
and consequently
\mathfrak{q}
is a minimal element of
\operatorname{Supp}_{R}(N)
. Since
\mathfrak{p}\subseteq\mathfrak{q}
, we have
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{q}
. The equalities
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{q}=\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}
show that
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{MinAtt}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))
and
\mathfrak{p}\in\Sigma
. Therefore
\Delta\subseteq\Sigma
and
\Delta\subseteq\operatorname{MinAtt}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))
.

(1), (2) and (3) prove that
\operatorname{MinAtt}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))=\operatorname{Att}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))=\Delta=\Sigma
. Finally, since
\Delta=\Sigma
, Theorem
4.1
implies that
\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))=\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N/\operatorname{T}(\mathfrak{a},N))
. Also it is clear that
\operatorname{T}(\mathfrak{a},N)=\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Sigma}C^{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}(0)
(see Definition
3.3
). This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 4.10
.

Let
N
be a finitely generated
R
-module,
\mathfrak{a}
an ideal of
R
such that
N\neq\mathfrak{a}N
and
c:=\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},N)
. Let
0=N_{1}\cap\ldots\cap N_{n}
be a minimal primary decomposition of the zero submodule of
N
with
\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N/N_{i}):=\{\mathfrak{p}_{i}\}
for all
1\leq i\leq n
. Set
\Delta:=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N):\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R/\mathfrak{p})=c\}
. If
(0:_{\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N/N_{i})}\mathfrak{a})
is finitely generated for all
\mathfrak{p}_{i}\in\Delta
(or
(0:_{\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)/M}\mathfrak{a})
is finitely generated for all submodules
M
of
\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)
), then

\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))=\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N/\operatorname{T}(\mathfrak{a},N)).

In particular, if
N
is coprimary and
(0:_{\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)}\mathfrak{a})
is finitely generated, then

\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))=\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N).

Proof.

Set
T:=\operatorname{T}(\mathfrak{a},N)=\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}_{i}\in\Delta}N_{i}
. Since
\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},T)<c
,

\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)\cong\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N/T)
and hence
\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N/T)\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N/T))=\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))
. Now we show that

\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N/T).

To prove the claimed inclusion, we assume that
r\in R
,
r\notin\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N/T)
and it is sufficient for us to show that
r\notin\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))
. Since
rN\nsubseteq T
,
rN\nsubseteq N_{i}
for some
N_{i}
with
\mathfrak{p}_{i}\in\Delta
. As
\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(r(N/N_{i}))=\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N/N_{i})=\{\mathfrak{p}_{i}\}
, we see
\operatorname{Supp}_{R}(r(N/N_{i}))=\operatorname{Supp}_{R}(N/N_{i})=\operatorname{Supp}_{R}(R/\mathfrak{p}_{i})
and so

\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},r(N/N_{i}))=\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},N/N_{i})=c.

Now suppose that
\mathfrak{n}\in{\rm Min}\operatorname{Supp}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N/N_{i}))
. It follows from
\operatorname{Supp}_{R_{\mathfrak{n}}}((r(N/N_{i}))_{\mathfrak{n}})=\operatorname{Supp}_{R_{\mathfrak{n}}}((N/N_{i})_{\mathfrak{n}})=\operatorname{V}(\mathfrak{p}_{i}R_{\mathfrak{n}})
that

\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a}R_{\mathfrak{n}},r/1(N/N_{i})_{\mathfrak{n}})=\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a}R_{\mathfrak{n}},(N/N_{i})_{\mathfrak{n}})=c
. Therefore
\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}R_{\mathfrak{n}}}(r/1(N/N_{i})_{\mathfrak{n}})\neq 0
and hence

\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}\widehat{R_{\mathfrak{n}}}}((r/1)\hat{\,}((N/N_{i})_{\mathfrak{n}})\,\hat{}\,)\neq 0,

where ”
\hat{}
” denotes the
\mathfrak{n}R_{\mathfrak{n}}
-adic completion.

As
(0:_{\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N/N_{i})}\mathfrak{a})
is a finitely generated
R
-module, in view of
[
27
, Theorem 7.11]
,
(0:_{\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}\widehat{R_{\mathfrak{n}}}}(((N/N_{i})_{\mathfrak{n}})\,\hat{}\,)}\mathfrak{a}\widehat{R_{\mathfrak{n}}})
is a finitely generated
\widehat{R_{\mathfrak{n}}}
-module. Also, by
[
9
, Theorem 4.3.2]
,
[
27
, Theorem 23.2(ii)]
and these facts that
\operatorname{Ass}_{R_{\mathfrak{n}}}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}R_{\mathfrak{n}}}((N/N_{i})_{\mathfrak{n}})=\{\mathfrak{n}R_{\mathfrak{n}}\}
and
\widehat{R_{\mathfrak{n}}}
is a local ring with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{n}\widehat{R_{\mathfrak{n}}}
, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ass}_{\widehat{R_{\mathfrak{n}}}}(0:_{\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}\widehat{R_{\mathfrak{n}}}}(((N/N_{i})_{\mathfrak{n}})\,\hat{}\,)}\mathfrak{a}\widehat{R_{\mathfrak{n}}})=\operatorname{V}(\mathfrak{a}\widehat{R_{\mathfrak{n}}})\cap\operatorname{Ass}_{\widehat{R_{\mathfrak{n}}}}({\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}\widehat{R_{\mathfrak{n}}}}(((N/N_{i})_{\mathfrak{n}})\,\hat{}\,)})

It follows that
(0:_{\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}\widehat{R_{\mathfrak{n}}}}(((N/N_{i})_{\mathfrak{n}})\,\hat{}\,)}\mathfrak{a}\widehat{R_{\mathfrak{n}}})
has finite length and so
{\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}\widehat{R_{\mathfrak{n}}}}(((N/N_{i})_{\mathfrak{n}})\,\hat{}\,)}
is an Artinian
\widehat{R_{\mathfrak{n}}}
-module by Melkersson’s Theorem (see
[
9
, Theorem 7.1.2]
).

Now,
\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}\widehat{R_{\mathfrak{n}}}}((r/1)\hat{\,}((N/N_{i})_{\mathfrak{n}})\,\hat{}\,)\neq 0
yields
(r/1)\hat{\,}((N/N_{i})_{\mathfrak{n}})\,\hat{}\,\nsubseteq{\rm T}(\mathfrak{a}\widehat{R_{\mathfrak{n}}},((N/N_{i})_{\mathfrak{n}})\,\hat{}\,)
because
\operatorname{T}(\mathfrak{a}\widehat{R_{\mathfrak{n}}},((N/N_{i})_{\mathfrak{n}})\,\hat{}\,)
is the largest
\widehat{R_{\mathfrak{n}}}
-submodule
S
of
((N/N_{i})_{\mathfrak{n}})\,\hat{}

such that
\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}\widehat{R_{\mathfrak{n}}}}(S)=0
.
By Lemma
4.9
,

\operatorname{Ann}_{\widehat{R_{\mathfrak{n}}}}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}\widehat{R_{\mathfrak{n}}}}(((N/N_{i})_{\mathfrak{n}})\,\hat{}\,))=\operatorname{Ann}_{\widehat{R_{\mathfrak{n}}}}(((N/N_{i})_{\mathfrak{n}})\,\hat{}/\operatorname{T}(\mathfrak{a}\widehat{R_{\mathfrak{n}}},((N/N_{i})_{\mathfrak{n}})\,\hat{}\,)).

Thus
(r/1)\hat{\,}\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}\widehat{R_{\mathfrak{n}}}}(((N/N_{i})_{\mathfrak{n}})\,\hat{}\,)\neq 0
and so
r\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N/N_{i})\neq 0
. Next it follows from the exact sequence
\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)\rightarrow\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N/N_{i})\rightarrow 0
that
r\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)\neq 0
, as required. This proves the first claimed equality. Note that if
(0:_{\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)/M}\mathfrak{a})
is finitely generated for all submodules
M
of
\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)
, then the
R
-module
(0:_{\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N/N_{i})}\mathfrak{a})
is finitely generated for all
1\leq i\leq n
because
\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N/N_{i})
is a homomorphic image of
\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)
. Finally assume that
N
is coprimary and
\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N)=\{\mathfrak{p}\}
. Then
0
is a
\mathfrak{p}
-primary submodule of
N
and so
\operatorname{T}(\mathfrak{a},N)=0
. Therefore, by the first part of theorem, we have
\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))=\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N)
.
∎

Corollary 4.11
.

Let
N
be a non-zero finitely generated
R
-module,
\mathfrak{a}
an ideal of
R
with
N\neq\mathfrak{a}N
and
c:=\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},N)
. Suppose that one of the following conditions holds:

(i)

c\leq 1
;

(ii)

\dim_{R}(N/\mathfrak{a}N)\leq 1
;

(iii)

\dim_{R}(N)\leq 2
;

(iv)

\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)
is
\mathfrak{a}
-cofinite minimax.

Then

\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))=\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N/\operatorname{T}(\mathfrak{a},N)),

\operatorname{ht}_{N}{(\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)))}=0,

and

\displaystyle\dim_{R}(R/\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)))

\displaystyle=\max\{\dim_{R}(R/\mathfrak{p}):{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{MinAss}_{R}(N),}\ {\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R/\mathfrak{p})=c}\}

Proof.

Set
\Delta:=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N):\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R/\mathfrak{p})=c\}
. We have
\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N/\operatorname{T}(\mathfrak{a},N))=\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N/\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Delta}C^{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}(0))=\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Delta}C_{\mathfrak{p}}(\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N))
. Since
\Delta\neq\emptyset
, it follows from Lemma
2.4
(iii) that

\operatorname{ht}_{N}{(\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N/\operatorname{T}(\mathfrak{a},N)))}=0.

Also,
by Proposition
3.7
(i), we have

\dim_{R}(R/\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N/\operatorname{T}(\mathfrak{a},N)))=\max_{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N/\operatorname{T}(\mathfrak{a},N))}\dim_{R}(R/\mathfrak{p})=\max_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Delta}\ \dim_{R}(R/\mathfrak{p}).

Now if
\mathfrak{p}\in\Delta
and
\mathfrak{q}\in\operatorname{MinAss}_{R}(N)
is such that
\mathfrak{q}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
, then
c=\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R/\mathfrak{p})\leq\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R/\mathfrak{q})\leq c
and so
\mathfrak{q}\in\Delta
. Therefore

\dim_{R}(R/\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N/\operatorname{T}(\mathfrak{a},N)))=\max_{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{MinAss}_{R}(N),\ \operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R/\mathfrak{p})=c}\dim_{R}(R/\mathfrak{p}).

Thus, to complete our proof, we only need to prove that the first claimed equality
\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))=\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N/\operatorname{T}(\mathfrak{a},N))
holds in all the given cases.

Suppose that
0=N_{1}\cap\ldots\cap N_{n}
is a minimal primary decomposition of the zero submodule of
N
with
\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N/N_{j}):=\{\mathfrak{p}_{j}\}
for all
1\leq j\leq n
. Assume that
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
is an arbitrary element of
\Delta
. We claim that in all the given cases either
\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N/N_{i})
is
\mathfrak{a}
-cofinite or
(0:_{\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N/N_{i})}\mathfrak{a})
is finitely generated and hence we can deduce from Theorem
4.10
that the equality
\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))=\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N/\operatorname{T}(\mathfrak{a},N))
holds.

(i) If
\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},N)\leq 1
, then
\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},N/N_{i})\leq 1
and so, by
[
29
, Corollary 3.14]
,
\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N/N_{i})
is
\mathfrak{a}
-cofinite.

(ii) If
\dim_{R}(N/\mathfrak{a}N)\leq 1
, then
\dim_{R}((N/N_{i})/\mathfrak{a}(N/N_{i}))\leq 1
and so
\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N/N_{i})
is
\mathfrak{a}
-cofinite by
[
7
, Corollary 2.7]
(see also
[
14
, Theorem 1]
and
[
37
, Theorem 1.1]
for the local case).

(iii) If
c=\dim_{R}(N/N_{i})
, then
\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N/N_{i})
is
\mathfrak{a}
-cofinite by
[
29
, Proposition 5.1]
(see also
[
14
, Theorem 3]
for the local case). If
c<\dim_{R}(N/N_{i})
, then the result follows from (i).

(iv) It follows from the exact sequence
\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)\rightarrow\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N/N_{i})\rightarrow 0
that
\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N/N_{i})
is also
\mathfrak{a}
-cofinite minimax because the category of
\mathfrak{a}
-cofinite minimax modules is a Serre subcategory of the category of
R
-modules; see Lemma
4.8
.
∎

Bahmanpour in
[
4
, Theorem 2.9]
, as one of his main results, computed the annihilator of
\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)
when
c:=\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},N)=1
. The following corollary shows that our result in Corollary
4.11
(i) coincides with that of Bahmanpour and gives an affirmative answer to Lynch’s conjecture.

Lemma 4.12
.

Let
N
be a non-zero finitely generated (coprimary)
R
-module with
\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N)=\{\mathfrak{p}\}
and
\mathfrak{a}
an ideal of
R
.
Then
\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},N)=0
if and only if
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
.

Proof.

If
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
, then, since
\surd(\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N))=\mathfrak{p}
, we obtain
\mathfrak{a}^{n}N=0
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
and so
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)=N\neq 0
. It follows that
\operatorname{H}^{i}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)=0
for all
i>0
and consequently
\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},N)=0
. Conversely, assume that
\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},N)=0
. Hence
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)\neq 0
. Thus
\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))=\operatorname{V}{(\mathfrak{a})}\cap\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N)\neq\emptyset
and so
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
.
∎

Corollary 4.13
.

Let
\mathfrak{a}
be an ideal of
R
and
N
a finitely generated
R
-module such that
\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},N)=1
. Then
\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{1}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))=\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N/\Gamma_{\mathfrak{b}}(N)),
where
\mathfrak{b}:=\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Delta}\mathfrak{p}
and
\Delta:=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N):\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{p}=R\textrm{ or }\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}\}
.
In particular, if for each
a\in\mathfrak{a}
,
1-a
is a non-zerodivisor on
N
, then
\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{1}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))=\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N/\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))
.

Proof.

By Corollary
4.11
(i),
\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{1}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))=\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N/\operatorname{T}(\mathfrak{a},N))
. Also, by Definition
3.3
,
\operatorname{T}(\mathfrak{a},N)=\Gamma_{\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}}(N)
, where
\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}=\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Delta^{\prime}}\mathfrak{p}
and
\Delta^{\prime}=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N):\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R/\mathfrak{p})<1\}
.
Now assume that
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N)
. Then
\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R/\mathfrak{p})=-\infty
if and only if
\mathfrak{a}(R/\mathfrak{p})=R/\mathfrak{p}
or equivalently
\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{p}=R
. Also, by Lemma
4.12
,
\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R/\mathfrak{p})=0
if and only if
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
. Therefore
\Delta^{\prime}=\Delta
and so
\mathfrak{b}=\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}
. This proves the first part of the assertion.
Now assume that for each
a\in\mathfrak{a}
,
1-a
is a non-zerodivisor on
N
. Thus
\Delta=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N):\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}\}=\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))
and so

\mathfrak{b}=\surd(\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)))
. It follows that
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{b}}(N)=\Gamma_{\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))}(N)=\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)
because
\mathfrak{a}^{t}\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)=0
for some
t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
∎

Let
\mathfrak{a}
be a proper ideal of
R
. Then, by
[
9
, Corollary 3.3.3]
,
\operatorname{ara}(\mathfrak{a})
is an upper bound for the invariant
\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R)
.
Hochster and Jeffries
[
22
, Theorem 2.6]
proved that if
R
is a domain of prime characteristic and
\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R)=\operatorname{ara}(\mathfrak{a})
, then
\operatorname{H}^{\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R)}_{\mathfrak{a}}(R)
is faithful. On the other hand, if
R
is local, then
\dim_{R}(R)-\dim_{R}(R/\mathfrak{a})
is a lower bound for the invariant
\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R)
; see the following lemma. If
R
is a local domain and
\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R)=\dim_{R}(R)-\dim_{R}(R/\mathfrak{a})
, then
\operatorname{H}^{\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},R)}_{\mathfrak{a}}(R)
is faithful, see
[
4
, Theorem 2.7]
. In the following theorem we generalize this result.
We note that Theorem
4.15
and Corollary
4.16
give affirmative answers to Lynch’s conjecture.

Lemma 4.14

(
[
16
, Corrollary 2.3]
)
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{n})
be a local ring,
\mathfrak{a}
an ideal of
R
and
N
a finitely generated
R
-module with
N\neq\mathfrak{a}N
. Then

\dim_{R}(N)-\dim_{R}(N/\mathfrak{a}N)\leq\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},N).

Moreover, if
\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},N)=\dim_{R}(N)-\dim_{R}(N/\mathfrak{a}N)
, then

\operatorname{H}^{\dim_{R}(N/\mathfrak{a}N)}_{\mathfrak{n}}(\operatorname{H}^{\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},N)}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))\cong\operatorname{H}^{\dim_{R}(N)}_{\mathfrak{n}}(N)

and
\dim\operatorname{Supp}_{R}({\operatorname{H}^{\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},N)}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)})=\dim_{R}(N/\mathfrak{a}N)
.

Theorem 4.15
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{n})
be a local ring,
\mathfrak{a}
an ideal of
R
,
N
a finitely generated
R
-module with
N\neq\mathfrak{a}N
and
c:=\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},N)=\dim_{R}(N)-\dim_{R}(N/\mathfrak{a}N)
. Then

(i)

\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N/\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Assh}_{R}(N)}C^{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}(0)).

(ii)

If
N
is unmixed (that is,
\dim_{R}(N)=\dim_{R}(R/\mathfrak{p})
for all
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N)
), then
\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))=\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N)
.

(iii)

\operatorname{ht}_{N}{(\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)))}=0
.

(iv)

\dim_{R}(R/\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)))=\dim_{R}(N)
. Moreover, if
c>0
, then
\dim_{R}(N)=\dim_{R}(N/\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))=\dim_{R}(R/\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))
.

Proof.

(i) By Lemma
4.14
, we have
\operatorname{H}^{\dim_{R}(N/\mathfrak{a}N)}_{\mathfrak{n}}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))\cong\operatorname{H}^{\dim_{R}(N)}_{\mathfrak{n}}(N)
. Thus

\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{\dim_{R}(N)}_{\mathfrak{n}}(N)).

Now (i) follows from Theorem
4.1
(set
\mathfrak{a}=\mathfrak{n}
in Theorem
4.1
).

(ii) Assume that
N
is unmixed. Then we have
\operatorname{Assh}_{R}(N)=\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N)
and hence
\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Assh}_{R}(N)}C^{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}(0)=\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N)}C^{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}(0)=0
. Therefore, by (i),
\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N)
. The reverse inclusion is clear and so the claimed equality holds.

(iii) It is an immediate consequence of (i).

(iv) Let
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Assh}_{R}(N)
. By (i),
\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N)\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))\subseteq C_{\mathfrak{p}}(\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N))\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
and so

\dim_{R}(R/\mathfrak{p})\leq\dim_{R}(R/\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)))\leq\dim_{R}(R/\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N)).

Therefore
\dim_{R}(R/\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)))=\dim_{R}(N)
. Now assume that
c>0
. Hence

\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N)\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N/\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N/\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)))=\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))\subseteq\mathfrak{p}

and so the claimed equalities hold.
∎

The following corollary generalizes two main theorems of
[
4
]
(see
[
4
, Theorems 2.2 and 2.3]
).

Corollary 4.16
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{n})
be a local ring and
N
a non-zero finitely generated
R
-module. Let
0\leq t\leq\dim_{R}(N)
and
x_{1},\dots,x_{t}\in\mathfrak{n}
be a part of a system of parameters for
N
. Then the following statements hold.

(i)

\operatorname{cd}_{R}((x_{1},\dots,x_{t}),N)=t
.

(ii)

\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{t}_{(x_{1},\dots,x_{t})}(N))\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N/\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Assh}_{R}(N)}C_{\mathfrak{p}}^{N}(0))
. In particular, if
N
is unmixed, then
\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{t}_{(x_{1},\dots,x_{t})}(N))=\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N)
.

(iii)

\operatorname{ht}_{N}{(\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{t}_{(x_{1},\dots,x_{t})}(N)))}=0
.

(iv)

\dim_{R}(R/\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{t}_{(x_{1},\dots,x_{t})}(N)))=\dim_{R}(N)
and if
t>0
, then

\dim_{R}(R/\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{t}_{(x_{1},\dots,x_{t})}(N)))=\dim_{R}(N/\Gamma_{(x_{1},\dots,x_{t})}(N))=\dim_{R}(N).

(v)

\dim\operatorname{Supp}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{t}_{(x_{1},\dots,x_{t})}(N)))=\dim_{R}(N)-t
.

Proof.

We have
\dim_{R}(N/(x_{1},\dots,x_{t})N)=\dim_{R}(N)-t
because
x_{1},\dots,x_{t}
is a part of a system of parameters of
N
. Hence, by Lemma
4.14
and
[
9
, Corollary 3.3.3]
, we obtain

\displaystyle t=\dim_{R}(N)-\dim_{R}(N/(x_{1},\dots,x_{t})N)

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{cd}_{R}((x_{1},\dots,x_{t}),N)\leq\operatorname{ara}{(x_{1},\dots,x_{t})}\leq t.

It follows that

\operatorname{cd}_{R}((x_{1},\dots,x_{t}),N)=\dim_{R}(N)-\dim_{R}(N/(x_{1},\dots,x_{t})N)=t.

Therefore (i) holds; and (ii)–(iv) follow from Theorem
4.15
. Finally, by Lemma
4.14
, we have

\dim\operatorname{Supp}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{t}_{(x_{1},\dots,x_{t})}(N)))=\dim_{R}(N/(x_{1},\dots,x_{t})N)=\dim_{R}(N)-t.

∎

5.
Annihilator of first non-zero local cohomology

Let
\mathfrak{a}
be an ideal of
R
and
N
a finitely generated
R
-module with
N\neq\mathfrak{a}N
. Our purpose in this section is to provide a sharp upper bound for the annihilator of

\operatorname{H}^{\operatorname{depth}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},N)}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)
, see Theorem
5.4
and Corollary
5.5
. Also, we consider and compute
\dim_{R}(R/\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{\operatorname{depth}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},N)}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))
in certain cases. Before that we need some lemmas.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{n})
be a local ring and
N
a non-zero finitely generated
R
-module. For each
t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, set
\Delta(t):=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N):\dim_{R}(R/\mathfrak{p})\geq t\}
,

\Sigma(t):=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{MinAss}_{R}(N):\dim_{R}(R/\mathfrak{p})=t\}
and
\Sigma^{\prime}(t):=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N)\setminus\operatorname{MinAss}_{R}(N):\dim_{R}(R/\mathfrak{p})=t\}
. There is the following bound for the annihilator of
\operatorname{H}^{t}_{\mathfrak{n}}(N)
:

\displaystyle\textstyle{\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N/\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Delta(t)}C_{\mathfrak{p}}^{N}(0))\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{t}_{\mathfrak{n}}(N))}

\displaystyle\textstyle{\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N/\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Sigma(t)}C_{\mathfrak{p}}^{N}(0))\cap(\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Sigma^{\prime}(t)}\mathfrak{p}).}

Proof.

We set
S(t):=R\setminus\bigcup_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Delta(t)}\mathfrak{p}
and
T(t):=R\setminus\bigcup_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Sigma(t)}\mathfrak{p}
. By
[
18
, Theorem 3.2]
, we have

\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N/C_{S(t)}^{N}(0))\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{t}_{\mathfrak{n}}(N))\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N/C_{T(t)}^{N}(0)).

Also, if
\mathfrak{p}\in\Sigma^{\prime}(t)
, then
[
35
, Corollary 4.9]
implies that
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Att}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{t}_{\mathfrak{n}}(N)
and so
\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{t}_{\mathfrak{n}}(N))\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Note that if
N
is a non-zero finitely generated
R
-module and
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Supp}_{R}(N)
, then we have
\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N/C^{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}(0))=C_{\mathfrak{p}}(\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N))\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
. Example
5.6
shows that to improve the upper bound for the annihilator of local cohomology
\operatorname{H}^{t}_{\mathfrak{n}}(N)
in the above lemma we can not replace
\operatorname{MinAss}_{R}(N)
by
\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N)
in the index set
\Sigma(t)
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
N
be a finitely generated
R
-module and
\mathfrak{a}
an ideal of
R
with
N\neq\mathfrak{a}N
. Let
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N)
with
\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{p}\neq R
and
n:=\operatorname{ht}_{R/\mathfrak{p}}{((\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{p})/\mathfrak{p})}
. Then

\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{n}_{\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{p}}(N))\subseteq\mathfrak{p}.

If, in addition,
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{MinAss}_{R}(N)
, then

\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{n}_{\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{p}}(N))\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N/C^{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}(0)).

In particular, if
N
is coprimary, then
\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{\operatorname{ht}_{N}{(\mathfrak{a})}}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))=\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N).

Proof.

Assume that
\mathfrak{q}
is a prime ideal of
R
containing
\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{p}
such that
\operatorname{ht}_{R/\mathfrak{p}}{(\mathfrak{q}/\mathfrak{p})}=\operatorname{ht}_{R/\mathfrak{p}}{((\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{p})/\mathfrak{p})}
. Then

\dim_{R_{\mathfrak{q}}}(R_{\mathfrak{q}}/\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{q}})=\operatorname{ht}_{R_{\mathfrak{q}}/\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{q}}}{(\mathfrak{q}R_{\mathfrak{q}}/\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{q}})}=\operatorname{ht}_{R/\mathfrak{p}}{(\mathfrak{q}/\mathfrak{p})}=n.

Also we have

(\operatorname{H}^{n}_{\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{p}}(N))_{\mathfrak{q}}\cong\operatorname{H}^{n}_{(\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{p})R_{\mathfrak{q}}}(N_{\mathfrak{q}})\cong\operatorname{H}^{n}_{\mathfrak{q}R_{\mathfrak{q}}}(N_{\mathfrak{q}}).

Since
\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{q}}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R_{\mathfrak{q}}}(N_{\mathfrak{q}})
and
\dim_{R_{\mathfrak{q}}}(R_{\mathfrak{q}}/\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{q}})=n
, we have
\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{q}}\in\operatorname{Att}_{R_{\mathfrak{q}}}(\operatorname{H}^{n}_{\mathfrak{q}R_{\mathfrak{q}}}(N_{\mathfrak{q}}))
by
[
35
, Corollary 4.9]
and so

\operatorname{Ann}_{R_{\mathfrak{q}}}(\operatorname{H}^{n}_{\mathfrak{q}R_{\mathfrak{q}}}(N_{\mathfrak{q}}))\subseteq\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{q}}
. To prove
\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{n}_{\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{p}}(N))\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
, assume for the sake of contradiction that

x\in\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{n}_{\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{p}}(N))
and
x\notin\mathfrak{p}
. Since
\mathfrak{p}
is prime,
x/1\notin\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{q}}
and so
(x\operatorname{H}^{n}_{\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{p}}(N))_{\mathfrak{q}}\cong x/1\operatorname{H}^{n}_{\mathfrak{q}R_{\mathfrak{q}}}(N_{\mathfrak{q}})\neq 0
, a contradiction. This proves the first claimed inclusion.

Next, assume in addition that
\mathfrak{p}
is a minimal element of
\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N)
. Thus
C:=C^{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}(0)
is a
\mathfrak{p}
-primary submodule of
N
(in fact,
C^{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}(0)
is the unique
\mathfrak{p}
-primary component of every minimal primary decomposition of the zero submodule of
N
).
Therefore
\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N/C)=\{\mathfrak{p}\}
.
Since
\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{q}}
is a minimal element of
\operatorname{Ass}_{R_{\mathfrak{q}}}(N_{\mathfrak{q}})
with
\dim_{R_{\mathfrak{q}}}({R_{\mathfrak{q}}}/{\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{q}}})=n
, it follows from Lemma
5.1
that

\operatorname{Ann}_{R_{\mathfrak{q}}}((\operatorname{H}^{n}_{\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{p}}(N))_{\mathfrak{q}})=\operatorname{Ann}_{R_{\mathfrak{q}}}(\operatorname{H}^{n}_{\mathfrak{q}R_{\mathfrak{q}}}(N_{\mathfrak{q}}))\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}_{R_{\mathfrak{q}}}(N_{\mathfrak{q}}/C^{N_{\mathfrak{q}}}_{\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{q}}}(0)).

Now suppose that
x\in R
is such that
xN\nsubseteq C
. We have
\emptyset\neq\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(x(N/C))\subseteq\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N/C)=\{\mathfrak{p}\}
and so
\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(x(N/C))=\{\mathfrak{p}\}
. Therefore

\operatorname{Ass}_{R_{\mathfrak{q}}}(x/1(N_{\mathfrak{q}}/C_{\mathfrak{q}}))=\{\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{q}}\}
and hence
x/1\notin\operatorname{Ann}_{R_{\mathfrak{q}}}(N_{\mathfrak{q}}/C_{\mathfrak{q}})
. By Lemma
2.5
,
C_{\mathfrak{q}}=(C^{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}(0))_{\mathfrak{q}}=C^{N_{\mathfrak{q}}}_{\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{q}}}(0)
.
Hence the above inclusion implies that
x/1(\operatorname{H}^{n}_{\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{p}}(N))_{\mathfrak{q}}\neq 0
and so
x\operatorname{H}^{n}_{\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{p}}(N)\neq 0
. It follows that

\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{n}_{\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{p}}(N))\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N/C)

because
x
is an arbitrary element of
R
with
xN\nsubseteq C
. The last assertion follows immediately from the first part, because if
\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N)=\{\mathfrak{p}\}
, then
C^{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}(0)=0
,
\operatorname{ht}_{N}{(\mathfrak{a})}=\operatorname{ht}_{R/\mathfrak{p}}{((\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{p})/\mathfrak{p})}
and, since
\surd(\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N))=\mathfrak{p}
,
\operatorname{H}^{\operatorname{ht}_{N}{(\mathfrak{a})}}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)=\operatorname{H}^{\operatorname{ht}_{N}{(\mathfrak{a})}}_{\mathfrak{a}+\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N)}(N)=\operatorname{H}^{\operatorname{ht}_{N}{(\mathfrak{a})}}_{\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{p}}(N)
.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
N
be a finitely generated
R
-module,
\mathfrak{a}
an ideal of
R
such that
\mathfrak{a}N\neq N
and
n:=\operatorname{depth}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},N)
. Then, for each ideal
\mathfrak{b}
of
R
, there is the following isomorphism:

\operatorname{H}^{n}_{\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{b}}(N)\cong\Gamma_{\mathfrak{b}}(\operatorname{H}^{n}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)).

Proof.

Note that
n
is a finite non-negative integer because
\mathfrak{a}N\neq N
. By
[
34
, Theorem 10.47]
, there is the following Grothendieck’s third quadrant
spectral sequence

\textstyle{E_{2}^{p,q}=\operatorname{H}^{p}_{\mathfrak{b}}(\operatorname{H}^{q}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))\underset{p}{\Rightarrow}\operatorname{H}^{n}_{\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{b}}(N),}

where
n=p+q
.
We set
{H}^{n}:=\operatorname{H}^{n}_{\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{b}}(N)
. Hence there is a finite filtration

0=\Phi_{n+1}{H}^{n}\subseteq\Phi_{n}{H}^{n}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\Phi_{0}{H}^{n}={H}^{n}

of submodules of
{H}^{n}
such that
\Phi_{p}{H}^{n}/\Phi_{p+1}{H}^{n}\cong E^{p,q}_{\infty}
for all
0\leq p\leq n
.
If
p>0
, then
q<n
and so
E_{2}^{p,q}=0
. Therefore
E_{\infty}^{p,q}=0
because
E_{\infty}^{\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{q}}
is a subquotient of
E_{2}^{\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{q}}
. Thus

\Phi_{1}{H}^{n}=\Phi_{2}{H}^{n}=\dots=\Phi_{n+1}{H}^{n}=0
and
H^{n}=\Phi_{0}H^{n}\cong E_{\infty}^{0,n}
.

On the other hand, for each
r\geq 2
, there is the following exact sequence

E^{-r,n+r-1}_{r}
is a subquotients of

and so is zero. Also, since

,
E_{2}^{r,n-r+1}
and, consequently,

are zero. Therefore

E^{0,n}_{r+1}=\textrm{ker}\,d^{0,n}_{r}/\textrm{im}\,d^{-r,n+r-1}_{r}\cong E^{0,n}_{r}

for all

. It follows that
E_{\infty}^{0,n}\cong E_{2}^{0,n}
and so

\operatorname{H}^{n}_{\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{b}}(N)=H^{n}\cong E_{\infty}^{0,n}\cong E_{2}^{0,n}=\Gamma_{\mathfrak{b}}(\operatorname{H}^{n}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)).

Now we are ready to state and prove the main theorem of this section which provides a bound for the annihilator of the first non-zero local cohomology module. We recall that if
N
is a finitely generated
R
-module and
\mathfrak{a}
is an ideal of
R
with
N\neq\mathfrak{a}N
, then
{\rm S}(\mathfrak{a},N)=\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N),\,\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{p}\neq R}C^{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}(0)=\Gamma_{\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N),\,\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{p}=R}\mathfrak{p}}(N)
is the largest submodule
L
of
N
such that
L=\mathfrak{a}L
; also,
{\rm S}(\mathfrak{a},N)=0
if and only if for each
a\in\mathfrak{a}
,
1-a
is a non-zerodivisor on
N
; see Definition
3.3
, Lemma
3.4
and Proposition
3.7
.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
N
be a finitely generated
R
-module,
\mathfrak{a}
an ideal of
R
with
N\neq\mathfrak{a}N
and
n:=\operatorname{depth}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},N)
. Then

\textstyle{\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N/{\rm S}(\mathfrak{a},N))\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{n}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N/\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Sigma}C^{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}(0))\cap(\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Sigma^{\prime}}\mathfrak{p})}

where
\Sigma:=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{MinAss}_{R}(N):\operatorname{ht}_{R/\mathfrak{p}}{((\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{p})/\mathfrak{p})}=n\}
and
\Sigma^{\prime}:=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N)\setminus\operatorname{MinAss}_{R}(N):\operatorname{ht}_{R/\mathfrak{p}}{((\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{p})/\mathfrak{p})}=n\}
.

Proof.

The lower bound for the annihilator of
\operatorname{H}^{n}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)
follows from Theorem
3.2
(iii). Also, it follows from Lemmas
5.3
and
5.2
that

\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{n}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}(\operatorname{H}^{n}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)))=\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{n}_{\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{p}}(N))\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N/C^{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}(0))

for all
\mathfrak{p}\in\Sigma
. Similarly,
\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{n}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))\subseteq\mathfrak{q}
for all
\mathfrak{q}\in\Sigma^{\prime}
. Hence

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{n}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))

\displaystyle\textstyle{\subseteq\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Sigma}\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N/C^{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}(0))\cap(\bigcap_{\mathfrak{q}\in\Sigma^{\prime}}\mathfrak{q})}

\displaystyle=\textstyle{\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N/\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Sigma}C^{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}(0))\cap(\bigcap_{\mathfrak{q}\in\Sigma^{\prime}}\mathfrak{q}).}

∎

Corollary 5.5
.

Let
N
be a finitely generated Cohen-Macaulay
R
-module,
\mathfrak{a}
an ideal of
R
with
N\neq\mathfrak{a}N
,
n:=\operatorname{depth}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},N)
and
\Sigma:=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N):\operatorname{ht}_{R/\mathfrak{p}}{((\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{p})/\mathfrak{p})}=n\}
. Then

\textstyle{\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{n}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))=\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N/\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Sigma}C^{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}(0)).}

In particular,
\operatorname{ht}_{N}{(\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{n}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)))}=0
and
\dim_{R}(R/\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{n}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)))=\dim_{R}(N)
.

Proof.

Assume that
x\in R
and
x\operatorname{H}^{n}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)\neq 0
. Hence
(x\operatorname{H}^{n}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))_{\mathfrak{q}}\cong x/1\operatorname{H}^{n}_{\mathfrak{a}R_{\mathfrak{q}}}(N_{\mathfrak{q}})\neq 0
for some
\mathfrak{q}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{n}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))
. By
[
12
, Theorem 2.1]
,
0pt_{R_{\mathfrak{q}}}(N_{\mathfrak{q}})=\operatorname{depth}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},N)=n
and so
\operatorname{ht}_{N}{(\mathfrak{q})}=\operatorname{ht}_{N}{(\mathfrak{a})}
. Therefore
\mathfrak{q}
is a minimal prime ideal of
\mathfrak{a}+\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N)
and hence
\operatorname{H}^{n}_{\mathfrak{a}R_{\mathfrak{q}}}(N_{\mathfrak{q}})\cong\operatorname{H}^{n}_{\mathfrak{q}R_{\mathfrak{q}}}(N_{\mathfrak{q}})
. Since
\dim_{R_{\mathfrak{q}}}(N_{\mathfrak{q}})=n
, by
[
18
, Theorem 3.2(iii)]
,
\operatorname{Ann}_{R_{\mathfrak{q}}}(\operatorname{H}^{n}_{\mathfrak{q}R_{\mathfrak{q}}}(N_{\mathfrak{q}}))=\operatorname{Ann}_{R_{\mathfrak{q}}}(N_{\mathfrak{q}})
. Thus
(xN)_{\mathfrak{q}}\neq 0
and so there exists
\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{q}}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R_{\mathfrak{q}}}(N_{\mathfrak{q}})
such that
(xN_{\mathfrak{q}})_{\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{q}}}\cong(xN)_{\mathfrak{p}}\neq 0
. Since
N_{\mathfrak{q}}
is Cohen-Macaulay,
\dim_{R_{\mathfrak{q}}}(R_{\mathfrak{q}}/\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{q}})=\dim_{R_{\mathfrak{q}}}(N_{\mathfrak{q}})=n
and so Lemma
3.4
yields

n=\operatorname{depth}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},N)\leq\operatorname{ht}_{R/\mathfrak{p}}{(\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{p})}\leq\operatorname{ht}_{R/\mathfrak{p}}{(\mathfrak{q}/\mathfrak{p})}=\dim_{R_{\mathfrak{q}}}(R_{\mathfrak{q}}/\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{q}})=n.

Therefore
\mathfrak{p}\in\Sigma
and hence
(\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}\in\Sigma}C^{N}_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}(0))_{\mathfrak{p}}\subseteq(C^{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}(0))_{\mathfrak{p}}=0
. It follows that
(x(N/\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}\in\Sigma}C^{N}_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}(0)))_{\mathfrak{p}}\neq 0
and so
xN\nsubseteq\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}\in\Sigma}C^{N}_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}(0)
. Since
x
is an arbitrary element of
R
with
x\operatorname{H}^{n}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)\neq 0
, we have

\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N/\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}\in\Sigma}C^{N}_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}(0))\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{n}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))
. The reverse inclusion follows from Theorem
5.4
.
Finally since
\operatorname{H}^{n}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)\neq 0
,
\Sigma\neq\emptyset
. Assume that
\mathfrak{p}\in\Sigma
. Then

\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N)\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N/C^{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}(0))=C_{\mathfrak{p}}(\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N))\subseteq\mathfrak{p}.

Since
\operatorname{ht}_{N}{(\mathfrak{p})}=0
and
\dim_{R}(R/\mathfrak{p})=\dim_{R}(N)
, the above inclusions imply
the last assertion.
∎

Example 5.6
.

Let
K
be a field and
R:=K[[x,y]]
be the ring of formal power series over
K
in indeterminates
x,y
. Set
N:=R/(Rx^{2}+Rxy),N_{1}:=Rx/(Rx^{2}+Rxy)
and
N_{2}:=(Rx^{2}+Ry)/(Rx^{2}+Rxy)
. Then
0=N_{1}\cap N_{2}
is a minimal primary decomposition of the zero submodule of
N
with
\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N/N_{1})=\{\mathfrak{p}:=Rx\}
and
\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N/N_{2})=\{\mathfrak{n}:=Rx+Ry\}
. Thus
\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N)=\{\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{n}\}
.

We have
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{R}(N,R)\cong R/\mathfrak{p}
,
\operatorname{Ext}^{2}_{R}(N,R)\cong R/\mathfrak{n}
and
\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R}(N,R)=0
for all
i\neq 1,2
; see
[
18
, Example 2.7]
. Therefore
the Grothendieck’s Duality Theorem (see
[
9
, Theorem 11.2.5 or 11.2.8]
) implies that

\Gamma_{\mathfrak{n}}(N)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\operatorname{Ext}^{2}_{R}(N,R),E_{R}(R/\mathfrak{n}))\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(R/\mathfrak{n},E_{R}(R/\mathfrak{n}))\cong R/\mathfrak{n},

\displaystyle\operatorname{H}^{1}_{\mathfrak{n}}(N)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{R}(N,R),E_{R}(R/\mathfrak{n}))\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(R/\mathfrak{p},E_{R}(R/\mathfrak{n}))

\displaystyle\cong E_{R/\mathfrak{p}}(R/\mathfrak{n})\cong E_{K[[y]]}(K)\cong K[y^{-1}]

(
K[y^{-1}]
is a
K[[y]]
-module by the convention that
y^{r}.y^{-s}
is equal
y^{-(s-r)}
when
s\geq r
and zero otherwise) and
\operatorname{H}^{i}_{\mathfrak{n}}(N)=0
for all
i\neq 0,1
. Thus
0pt_{R}(N)=0
,
\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{n},N)=\dim_{R}(N)=1
and

\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{0pt_{R}(N)}_{\mathfrak{n}}(N))=Rx+Ry,

\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{\dim_{R}(N)}_{\mathfrak{n}}(N))=\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(R/\mathfrak{p},E_{R}(R/\mathfrak{n})))=\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(R/\mathfrak{p})=Rx.

On the other hand,
\operatorname{Supp}_{R}(N)=\operatorname{V}(\mathfrak{p})\cup\operatorname{V}(\mathfrak{n})=\{\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{n}\}
and since
\mathfrak{p}
is a minimal element of
\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N)
,
C^{N}_{\mathfrak{p}}(0)=N_{1}=Rx/(Rx^{2}+Rxy)
. Also, it is clear that
C^{N}_{\mathfrak{n}}(0)=0
. Now assume that
\Delta
is a subset of
\operatorname{Supp}_{R}(N)
. Then

\textstyle{\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N/\bigcap_{\mathfrak{q}\in\Delta}C^{N}_{\mathfrak{q}}(0))}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}R&\textrm{ if }\Delta=\emptyset,\\
Rx&\textrm{ if }\Delta=\{\mathfrak{p}\},\\
Rx^{2}+Rxy&\textrm{ otherwise. }\end{array}\right.

Therefore the following statements hold.

(i) There is not a subset
\Delta
of
\operatorname{Supp}_{R}(N)
such that
\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{0pt_{R}(N)}_{\mathfrak{n}}(N))=\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N/\bigcap_{\mathfrak{q}\in\Delta}C^{N}_{\mathfrak{q}}(0)).

(ii) By setting
\mathfrak{a}:=\mathfrak{n}
, this example shows that to improve the upper bound for the annihilator of
\operatorname{H}^{\operatorname{depth}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},N)}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)
in Theorem
5.4
, we can not replace
\operatorname{MinAss}_{R}(N)
by
\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N)
in the index set
\Sigma
.

In the following remark, we consider the analogue versions of Theorems
4.1
,
4.10
at
\operatorname{depth}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},N)
instead of at
\operatorname{cd}_{R}(\mathfrak{a},N)
.

Proposition 5.8
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{n})
be a homomorphic image of a Cohen-Macaulay local ring,
N
a non-zero finitely generated
R
-module and
t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
is such that
\operatorname{H}^{t}_{\mathfrak{n}}(N)\neq 0
. Then

\dim_{R}(R/\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{t}_{\mathfrak{n}}(N))\leq t.

Equality holds whenever there exists
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N)
with
\dim_{R}(R/\mathfrak{p})=t
.

Proof.

Since
\surd(\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{t}_{\mathfrak{n}}(N)))=\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Att}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{t}_{\mathfrak{n}}(N))}\mathfrak{p}
and
\operatorname{Att}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{t}_{\mathfrak{n}}(N))
is a finite set, we have
\dim_{R}(R/\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{t}_{\mathfrak{n}}(N))=\dim_{R}(R/\mathfrak{p})
for some
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Att}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{t}_{\mathfrak{n}}(N))
. By
[
24
, Corollary 1.2]
,
R
is universally catenary and all its formal fibers are Cohen-Macaulay. Thus
[
30
, Theorem 1.1]
implies that
\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}}\in\operatorname{Att}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(\operatorname{H}^{t-\dim_{R}(R/\mathfrak{p})}_{\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(N_{\mathfrak{p}}))
. Hence
t-\dim_{R}(R/\mathfrak{p})\geq 0
and so

\dim_{R}(R/\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{t}_{\mathfrak{n}}(N)))\leq t.

If there exists
\mathfrak{q}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N)
with
\dim_{R}(R/\mathfrak{q})=t
, then
[
35
, Corollary 4.9]
implies that
\mathfrak{q}\in\operatorname{Att}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{t}_{\mathfrak{n}}(N))
and so
\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{t}_{\mathfrak{n}}(N))\subseteq\mathfrak{q}
. Thus
\dim_{R}(R/\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{t}_{\mathfrak{n}}(N)))\geq\dim_{R}(R/\mathfrak{q})=t
and so the equality holds.
∎

The following example shows that there is a local ring
(A,\mathfrak{n})
which is a homomorphic image of a complete regular local ring such that

\dim_{A}(A/\operatorname{Ann}_{A}(\operatorname{H}^{0pt_{A}(A)}_{\mathfrak{n}}(A)))<0pt_{A}(A)=0pt_{A}(A/\Gamma_{\mathfrak{n}}(A)).

Therefore the inequality in Proposition
5.8
may be strict. Also, the analogue version of Lynch’s conjecture is not true for
\operatorname{H}^{0pt_{A}(A)}_{\mathfrak{n}}(A)
.

Example 5.9
.

Let
K
be a field and let
R:=K[[x,y,z,w]]
be the ring of formal power series over
K
in indeterminates
x,y,z,w
. We set
\mathfrak{m}:=(x,y,z,w)
and
I:=(x,y)\cap(z,w)
. Then
A:=R/I
is a local ring with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{n}:=\mathfrak{m}/I
. By
[
18
, Example 2.8]
and the Independence Theorem, we have
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{n}}(A)\cong\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}}(R/I)=0
and

\operatorname{H}^{1}_{\mathfrak{n}}(A)\cong\operatorname{H}^{1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R/I)\cong R/\mathfrak{m}\cong A/\mathfrak{n}
. Therefore
0pt_{A}(A)=0pt_{A}(A/\Gamma_{\mathfrak{n}}(A))=1
and

\dim_{A}(A/\operatorname{Ann}_{A}(\operatorname{H}^{0pt_{A}(A)}_{\mathfrak{n}}(A)))=\dim_{A}(A/\mathfrak{n})=0.

Therefore, in general,
\dim_{A}(A/\operatorname{Ann}_{A}(\operatorname{H}^{0pt_{A}(A)}_{\mathfrak{n}}(A)))
is not equal
0pt_{A}(A)
or
0pt_{A}(A/\Gamma_{\mathfrak{n}}(A))
and the inequality in Proposition
5.8
may be strict.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{n})
be a local ring and
N
a non-zero finitely generated
R
-module. Then, for each
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N)
,
0pt_{R}(N)\leq\dim_{R}(R/\mathfrak{p})
. We say that
N
has
maximal depth
if
0pt_{R}(N)=\dim_{R}(R/\mathfrak{p})
for some
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N)
. Cohen-Macaulay modules and sequentially Cohen-Macaulay modules have maximal depth; see
[
32
]
for more details. Now assume in addition that
R
is a homomorphic image of a Cohen-Macaulay local ring. Proposition
5.8
shows that
\dim_{R}(R/\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{0pt_{R}(N)}_{\mathfrak{n}}(N)))\leq 0pt_{R}(N)
and Example
5.9
shows that this inequality may be strict. In the following corollary we see that the equality holds if
N
has maximal depth.

Corollary 5.10
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{n})
be a homomorphic image of a Cohen-Macaulay local ring and let
N
be a non-zero finitely generated
R
-module which has maximal depth. Then

\dim_{R}(R/\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{H}^{0pt_{R}(N)}_{\mathfrak{n}}(N)))=0pt_{R}(N).

Proof.

It is an immediate consequence of Proposition
5.8
.
∎

Characterizations of complex Finsler Metrics
Hongjun Li, Hongchuan Xia

Abstract




Abstract
Munteanu
[
14
]
defined the canonical connection associated to a strongly pseudoconvex complex Finsler manifold
(M,F)
.
We first prove that the holomorphic sectional curvature tensors of the canonical connection coincide with those of the Chern-Finsler connection associated to
F
if and only if
F
is a Kähler-Finsler metric.
We also investigate the relationship of the Ricci curvatures (resp. scalar curvatures) of these two connections when
M
is compact. As an application, two characterizations of balanced complex Finsler metrics are given. Next, we obtain a sufficient and necessary condition for a balanced complex Finsler metric to be Kähler-Finsler.
Finally, we investigate conformal transformations of a balanced complex Finsler metric.

Key words
Chern-Finsler connection; canonical connection; holomorphic sectional curvature tensor; balanced complex Finsler metric; Rund Kähler-Finsler-like metric

53C56, 53C60, 32Q99

1
Introduction

Complex Finsler manifolds are complex manifolds endowed with complex Finsler metrics, which are
more general than Hermitian manifolds. Recently, complex Finsler geometry has attracted much interest because a number of complex Finsler metrics play an important role in geometric function theory of holomorphic mappings
[
1
]
.

In complex Finsler geometry, the Chern-Finsler connection associated to a strongly pseudoconvex complex Finsler metric enjoys some beautiful features as the Hermitian (aka Chern) connection associated to a Hermitian metric. According to the vanishing of some parts of the torsion of the Chern-Finsler connection, there are three kinds of metrics in complex Finsler geometry, which are the extension of Kähler metrics, and respectively, called the strongly Kähler-Finsler, Kähler-Finsler and weakly Kähler-Finsler
[
1
]
. In fact, Kähler-Finsler metrics are actually strongly Kähler
[
6
]
. Hence, there are only two kinds of Kähler-Finsler metrics with respect to the Chern-Finsler connection in complex Finsler geometry.

Let
F
be a strongly pseudoconvex complex Finsler metric on a complex manifold
M
, and
D
be the Chern-Finsler connection associated to
F
. The Chern-Finsler connection
D
can induce a canonical connection
\nabla

[
14
]
. Let
R_{\alpha\bar{\beta}\mu\bar{\nu}}
and
\Omega_{\alpha\bar{\beta};\mu\bar{\nu}}
denote the holomorphic sectional curvature tensors of the canonical connection
\nabla
and the Chern-Finsler connection
D
, respectively, where lowercase Greek indices run from 1 to
n={\rm dim}_{\mathbb{C}}(M)
.
When
F
is a Kähler-Finsler metric, one has
R_{\alpha\bar{\beta}\mu\bar{\nu}}=\Omega_{\alpha\bar{\beta};\mu\bar{\nu}}
. A naive question is, when a strongly pseudoconvex complex Finsler metric
F
satisfies
R_{\alpha\bar{\beta}\mu\bar{\nu}}=\Omega_{\alpha\bar{\beta};\mu\bar{\nu}}
, whether it is Kähler-Finsler. In this paper, we give a positive answer to this question.
Denote by
K_{\nabla}(H)
and
K_{D}(H)
(resp.
K_{\nabla}(v)
and
K_{D}(v)
) the holomorphic flag curvatures (resp. the holomorphic sectional curvatures) of the canonical connection and the Chern-Finsler connection along a horizontal vector
H\in{\cal H}
(resp. a holomorphic vector
v
), respectively.
We have the following result:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M,F)
be a strongly pseudoconvex complex Finsler manifold. Then

(1)

R_{\alpha\bar{\beta}\mu\bar{\nu}}=\Omega_{\alpha\bar{\beta};\mu\bar{\nu}}
, if and only if

K_{\nabla}(H)=K_{D}(H)
, if and only if
F
is a Kähler-Finsler metric;

(2)

K_{\nabla}(v)=K_{D}(v)
,
if and only if
F
is a weakly Kähler-Finsler metric.

Let
z=(z^{1},\cdots,z^{n})
be a local holomorphic coordinate system on
M
, and

{G}_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}
be the fundamental tensor of
(M,{F})
. Set
(G^{\bar{\beta}\alpha})=(G_{\alpha\bar{\beta}})^{-1}
.
We denote by

\mathbf{Ric}_{\nabla}=\sqrt{-1}R_{\mu\bar{\nu}}\mathrm{d}z^{\mu}\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar{z}^{\nu}\quad with\quad R_{\mu\bar{\nu}}=G^{\bar{\beta}\alpha}R_{\alpha\bar{\beta}\mu\bar{\nu}},

and

\mathbf{Ric}_{D}=\sqrt{-1}\Omega_{\mu\bar{\nu}}\mathrm{d}z^{\mu}\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar{z}^{\nu}\quad with\quad\Omega_{\mu\bar{\nu}}=G^{\bar{\beta}\alpha}\Omega_{\alpha\bar{\beta};\mu\bar{\nu}}

the Ricci curvatures of the canonical connection
\nabla
and the Chern-Finsler connection
D
, respectively.
The scalar curvatures of the canonical connection
\nabla
and the Chern-Finsler connection
D
are denoted
by
s_{\nabla}=G^{\bar{\nu}\mu}R_{\mu\bar{\nu}}
and
s_{D}=G^{\bar{\nu}\mu}\Omega_{\mu\bar{\nu}}
, respectively.
When
M
is compact, we obtain explicit relations between these two kinds of Ricci curvatures by using the horizontal fundamental form
\omega_{{\cal H}}
and furthermore give two characterizations
of balanced complex Finsler metrics. Here we say that a compact strongly pseudoconvex complex Finsler manifold
(M,F)
is balanced if the horizontal fundamental form
\omega_{{\cal H}}=\sqrt{-1}G_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}\mathrm{d}z^{\alpha}\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar{z}^{\beta}
is horizonal co-closed, i.e.,
\mathrm{d}_{{\cal H}}^{*}\omega_{{\cal H}}=0
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(M,F)
be a compact strongly pseudoconvex complex Finsler manifold. Then

\mathbf{Ric}_{D}-\mathbf{Ric}_{\nabla}=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left({\partial}_{{\cal H}}{\partial}_{{\cal H}}^{*}\omega_{{\cal H}}+\bar{\partial}_{{\cal H}}\bar{\partial}_{{\cal H}}^{*}\omega_{{\cal H}}\right),

(1.1)

s_{D}-s_{\nabla}=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\langle{\partial}_{{\cal H}}{\partial}_{{\cal H}}^{*}\omega_{{\cal H}}+\bar{\partial}_{{\cal H}}\bar{\partial}_{{\cal H}}^{*}\omega_{{\cal H}},\omega_{{\cal H}}\right\rangle_{{\mathbb{P}}\tilde{M}}.

(1.2)

Hence, the following three statements are equivalent:

(a)

F
is a balanced complex Finsler metric;

(b)

\mathbf{Ric}_{D}=\mathbf{Ric}_{\nabla}
;

(c)

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{P}}\tilde{M}}s_{D}\mathrm{d}\mu_{{\mathbb{P}}\tilde{M}}=\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{P}}\tilde{M}}s_{\nabla}\mathrm{d}\mu_{{\mathbb{P}}\tilde{M}}
.

We can extend the canonical connection
\nabla
to
T_{\mathbb{C}}\left(T^{1,0}M\backslash\{0\}\right)
, denoted by
\nabla^{\mathbbm{c}}
. The connection
\nabla^{\mathbbm{c}}
is a natural generalization of the complexified Levi-Civita connection on the complexified tangent bundle of a Hermitian manifold.
Denote by
\mathbf{Ric}_{\nabla^{\mathbbm{c}}}=\sqrt{-1}K_{\mu\bar{\nu}}\mathrm{d}z^{\mu}\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar{z}^{\nu}
and
s_{\nabla^{\mathbbm{c}}}=G^{\bar{\nu}\mu}K_{\mu\bar{\nu}}
the Ricci curvature and scalar curvature of the connection
\nabla^{\mathbbm{c}}
, respectively. We have

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(M,F)
be a compact strongly pseudoconvex complex Finsler manifold. Then

\mathbf{Ric}_{D}-\mathbf{Ric}_{\nabla^{\mathbbm{c}}}=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left({\partial}_{{\cal H}}{\partial}_{{\cal H}}^{*}\omega_{{\cal H}}+\bar{\partial}_{{\cal H}}\bar{\partial}_{{\cal H}}^{*}\omega_{{\cal H}}\right)+\sqrt{-1}\mathfrak{S}\circ\overline{\mathfrak{S}},

(1.3)

s_{D}-s_{\nabla^{\mathbbm{c}}}=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\langle{\partial}_{{\cal H}}{\partial}_{{\cal H}}^{*}\omega_{{\cal H}}+\bar{\partial}_{{\cal H}}\bar{\partial}_{{\cal H}}^{*}\omega_{{\cal H}},\omega_{{\cal H}}\right\rangle_{{\mathbb{P}}\tilde{M}}+\langle\mathfrak{S},\mathfrak{S}\rangle_{{\mathbb{P}}\tilde{M}},

(1.4)

where
\mathfrak{S}
is given by (
4.11
).
Hence, the following four statements are equivalent:

(a)

F
is a Kähler-Finsler metric;

(b)

\mathbf{Ric}_{D}=\mathbf{Ric}_{\nabla^{\mathbbm{c}}}
;

(c)

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{P}}\tilde{M}}s_{\nabla}\mathrm{d}\mu_{{\mathbb{P}}\tilde{M}}=\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{P}}\tilde{M}}s_{\nabla^{\mathbbm{c}}}\mathrm{d}\mu_{{\mathbb{P}}\tilde{M}}
;

(d)

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{P}}\tilde{M}}s_{D}\mathrm{d}\mu_{{\mathbb{P}}\tilde{M}}=\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{P}}\tilde{M}}s_{\nabla^{\mathbbm{c}}}\mathrm{d}\mu_{{\mathbb{P}}\tilde{M}}
.

Denote by
\hat{\Omega}^{\alpha}_{\beta;\mu\bar{\nu}}\mathrm{d}z^{\mu}\wedge\mathrm{d}{\bar{z}}^{\nu}
the horizontal part of the curvature form of the complex Rund connection
\hat{D}
. By analogy with the Kähler-like metric defined in
[
21
]
, we say
F
is a Rund Kähler-Finsler-like metric if
\hat{\Omega}^{\alpha}_{\beta;\mu\bar{\nu}}=\hat{\Omega}^{\alpha}_{\mu;\beta\bar{\nu}}
. When the corresponding manifold is compact, these metrics are more special than balanced complex
Finsler metrics.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
(M,F)
be a Rund Kähler-Finsler-like manifold. If
M
is compact, then
F
is a balanced complex Finsler metric.

We obtain a sufficient and necessary condition
for a balanced complex Finsler metric to be Kähler-Finsler.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
(M,F)
be a balanced complex Finsler manifold. Then
F
is a Kähler-Finsler metric, if and only if

\left\langle\partial_{{\cal H}}\bar{\partial}_{{\cal H}}\omega_{{\cal H}},\omega_{{\cal H}}^{2}\right\rangle_{{\mathbb{P}}\tilde{M}}=0.

In the end, we investigate conformal transformations of balanced complex Finsler metrics.
We find that balanced complex Finsler metrics are clearly unique within each conformal class.
In the non-compact case, we get a sufficient and necessary condition
for two Rund Kähler-Finsler metrics to be conformal.

Theorem 1.6
.

On a compact complex manifold
M
, there exists at most one balanced complex Finsler metric (up to constant multiples) in each conformal class of strongly pseudoconvex complex Finsler metrics. For a non-compact strongly pseudoconvex Rund Kähler-Finsler-like manifold
(M,F)
, the conformal metric

\tilde{F}(z,v)={\rm e}^{\frac{1}{2}\rho(z)}F(z,v)
is Rund Kähler-Finsler-like if and only if
\partial\bar{\partial}\rho=0
.

We investigate the characterization for a strongly pseudoconvex complex Finsler metric to be conformal to a balanced complex Finsler metric.

Theorem 1.7
.

On a compact strongly pseudoconvex complex Finsler manifold
(M,F)
, the conformal metric
\tilde{F}(z,v)={\rm e}^{\frac{1}{2}\rho(z)}F(z,v)
is balanced, if and only if

\mathbf{Ric}_{D}-\mathbf{Ric}_{\nabla}=(n-1)\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\rho,

(1.5)

if and only if

\int_{{\mathbb{P}}\tilde{M}}\left(s_{D}-s_{\nabla}\right)\mathrm{d}\mu_{{\mathbb{P}}\tilde{M}}=(n-1)\left(\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\rho,\omega_{{\cal H}}\right)_{{\mathbb{P}}\tilde{M}}.

(1.6)

We also study the Chern-Finsler total scalar curvature under conformal transformations of a balanced complex Finsler manifold.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
(M,F)
be a balanced complex Finsler manifold with positive Chern-Finsler scalar curvature. Then the conformal metric
\tilde{F}(z,v)={\rm e}^{\frac{1}{2}\rho(z)}F(z,v)
owns positive Chern-Finsler total scalar curvature, that is

\int_{{\mathbb{P}}\tilde{M}}\tilde{s}_{\tilde{D}}\mathrm{d}\tilde{\mu}_{{\mathbb{P}}\tilde{M}}>0.

(1.7)

The rest of this paper is arranged as follows.
In Section 2, we provide a brief overview of complex Finsler manifolds, and introduce balanced complex Finsler manifolds and Rund Kähler-Finsler-like manifolds.
In Section 3, we recall detailedly the canonical connection defined by Munteanu.
In Section 4, we examine the behavior of holomorphic sectional curvature tensors of the canonical connection and the Chern-Finsler connection, and also investigate the corresponding Ricci curvatures and scalar curvatures.
In Section 5, we prove Theorems 1.4 and 1.5, and give two examples of balanced complex Finsler metrics (resp. Rund Kähler-Finsler-like metrics). In Section 6, we investigate conformal transformations of
a balanced complex Finsler metric.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Complex Finsler manifolds

Let
M
be a complex manifold with
{\rm dim}_{\mathbb{C}}(M)=n\geq 2
.
We shall denote by
\pi:T^{1,0}M\rightarrow M
the holomorphic tangent bundle of
M
.
Suppose that
z=(z^{1},\cdots,z^{n})
is a local holomorphic coordinate system on
M
, then an arbitrary holomorphic vector
v\in T^{1,0}M
can be written as

v=v^{\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{\alpha}}.

So
(z,v)
is a local holomorphic coordinate system on
T^{1,0}M
.
Let
\tilde{M}=T^{1,0}M\backslash\{0\}
denote the slit holomorphic tangent bundle.

Definition 2.1
.

[
1
]

A complex Finsler metric on a
complex manifold
M
is a continuous function

F:\,T^{1,0}M\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
satisfying:

(a)

G=F^{2}
is smooth on
\tilde{M}
;

(b)

F(z,v)>0
for all
(z,v)\in\tilde{M}
;

(c)

F(z,\zeta v)=|\zeta|F(z,v)
for all
(z,v)\in T^{1,0}M
and
\zeta\in\mathbb{C}
.

For simplicity, we denote
\overline{z^{\beta}}
,
\overline{v^{\beta}}

by
\bar{z}^{\beta}
,
\bar{v}^{\beta}
, the derivatives of
G
with respect to
v
by

G_{\alpha}=\dot{\partial}_{\alpha}G=\displaystyle\frac{\partial G}{\partial v^{\alpha}},\quad G_{\bar{\alpha}}=\dot{\partial}_{\bar{\alpha}}G=\displaystyle\frac{\partial G}{\partial\bar{v}^{\alpha}},\quad G_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}=\dot{\partial}_{\alpha}\dot{\partial}_{\bar{\beta}}G=\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}G}{\partial v^{\alpha}\partial\bar{v}^{\beta}},

and the derivatives of
G
with respect to
z
by indices after a semicolon,
for instance

G_{;\alpha}=\partial_{\alpha}G=\displaystyle\frac{\partial G}{\partial z^{\alpha}},\quad G_{;\alpha\bar{\beta}}=\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\bar{\beta}}G=\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}G}{\partial z^{\alpha}\partial\bar{z}^{\beta}},\quad G_{\alpha;\bar{\beta}}=\dot{\partial}_{\alpha}\partial_{\bar{\beta}}G=\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}G}{\partial\bar{z}^{\beta}\partial v^{\alpha}}.

Definition 2.2
.

[
1
]

A complex Finsler metric
F
will be said strongly pseudoconvex
if the Levi matrix
\left(G_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}\right)
is positive definite on
\tilde{M}
.

Suppose
(M,F)
is a strongly pseudoconvex complex Finsler manifold.
Let us denote by

\varGamma^{\alpha}_{;\mu}=G^{\bar{\lambda}\alpha}G_{\bar{\lambda};\mu}

(2.1)

the Chern-Finsler nonlinear connection coefficients.
Set

\delta_{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}-\varGamma_{;\mu}^{\alpha}\dot{\partial}_{\alpha},\quad\delta v^{\alpha}={\rm d}v^{\alpha}+\varGamma_{;\beta}^{\alpha}{\rm d}z^{\beta},

(2.2)

then the holomorphic tangent bundle
T^{1,0}\tilde{M}
of
\tilde{M}
can
be decomposed into the direct sum of the horizontal bundle
\mathcal{H}
spanned by
\{\delta_{\mu}\}
and the vertical bundle
{\cal V}
spanned by
\{\dot{\partial}_{\alpha}\}
.
The dual frame of
\{\delta_{\mu},\dot{\partial}_{\alpha}\}
is
\{\mathrm{d}z^{\mu},\delta v^{\alpha}\}
.
In other words, we have

\displaystyle T^{1,0}\tilde{M}

\displaystyle={\cal H}\oplus{\cal V}={\rm span}\{\delta_{\mu}\}\oplus{\rm span}\{\dot{\partial}_{\alpha}\},

\displaystyle T^{*1,0}\tilde{M}

\displaystyle={\cal H}^{*}\oplus{\cal V}^{*}={\rm span}\{\mathrm{d}z^{\mu}\}\oplus{\rm span}\{\delta v^{\alpha}\}.

We can define a Hermitian metric
\langle\,,\,\rangle
on the vertical bundle
{\cal V}
by the strong pseudoconvexity of
F
. For any
v\in\tilde{M}_{z}
, we set

\left\langle Z,W\right\rangle_{v}=G_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}(z,v)Z^{\alpha}\bar{W}^{\beta},\quad Z=Z^{\alpha}\dot{\partial}_{\alpha},\,W=W^{\alpha}\dot{\partial}_{\alpha}\in{\cal V}_{v}.

There exists a unique complex vertical connection
D:{\cal X}({\cal V})\rightarrow{\cal X}(T_{\mathbb{C}}^{*}{\tilde{M}}\otimes{\cal V})

such that

X\left\langle Z,W\right\rangle=\left\langle\nabla_{X}Z,W\right\rangle+\left\langle Z,\nabla_{\bar{X}}W\right\rangle

for all
X\in T^{1,0}\tilde{M}
and
Z,W\in{\cal X}({\cal V})
.
Kobayashi
[
13
]
first introduced this connection
D
, Abate and Patrizio
[
1
]
called it the Chern-Finsler connection. The connection 1-forms of
D
are given by

\theta_{\beta}^{\alpha}=G^{\bar{\lambda}\alpha}\partial G_{\beta\bar{\lambda}}=\varGamma_{\beta;\mu}^{\alpha}\mathrm{d}z^{\mu}+\varGamma_{\beta\gamma}^{\alpha}\delta v^{\gamma},

(2.3)

where

\varGamma_{\beta;\mu}^{\alpha}=G^{\bar{\lambda}\alpha}\delta_{\mu}\left(G_{\beta\bar{\lambda}}\right),\quad\varGamma_{\beta\gamma}^{\alpha}=G^{\bar{\lambda}\alpha}G_{\beta\bar{\lambda}\gamma}.

(2.4)

Let
\Theta:\,{\cal V}\rightarrow{\cal H}
be a complex horizontal map locally defined by
\Theta(\dot{\partial}_{\alpha})=\delta_{\alpha}
.
We can transfer the Hermitian structure
\langle~,~\rangle
on
{\cal H}
just by setting

\left\langle H,K\right\rangle_{v}=\left\langle\Theta^{-1}\left(H\right),\Theta^{-1}\left(K\right)\right\rangle_{v},\quad\forall H,\;K\in{\cal H}_{v}.

Furthermore, there is a Sasaki–type lift Hermitian structure
[
14
]

\tilde{G}=G_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}\mathrm{d}z^{\alpha}\otimes\mathrm{d}\bar{z}^{\beta}+G_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}\delta v^{\alpha}\otimes\delta\bar{v}^{\beta}

(2.5)

on
\tilde{M}
. We can also extend
D
to a complex linear connection on
{\cal H}
by setting

D_{X}H=\Theta(D_{X}(\Theta^{-1}H)),\quad H\in{\cal X}({\cal H}),\ \ X\in T_{\mathbb{C}}\tilde{M}.

(2.6)

The
(2,0)
-torsion and
(1,1)
-torsion of the Chern-Finsler connection are denoted by
\theta=\theta^{\mu}\otimes\delta_{\mu}
and
\tau=\tau^{\alpha}\otimes\dot{\partial}_{\alpha}
respectively, where

\begin{split}\theta^{\mu}&=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\varGamma^{\mu}_{\nu;\sigma}-\varGamma^{\mu}_{\sigma;\nu}\right)\mathrm{d}z^{\sigma}\wedge\mathrm{d}z^{\nu}+\varGamma^{\mu}_{\nu\gamma}\delta v^{\gamma}\wedge\mathrm{d}z^{\nu},\\
\tau^{\alpha}&=-\delta_{\bar{\nu}}\left(\varGamma^{\alpha}_{;\mu}\right)\mathrm{d}z^{\mu}\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar{z}^{\nu}-\dot{\partial}_{\bar{\beta}}\left(\varGamma^{\alpha}_{;\mu}\right)\mathrm{d}z^{\mu}\wedge\delta\bar{z}^{\nu}.\end{split}

(2.7)

Definition 2.3
.

[
1
]

We call
F
strongly Kähler if
\theta(H,K)=0,\,\forall H,K\in{\cal H}
;
Kähler if
\theta(H,\chi)=0,\,\forall H\in{\cal H}
; weakly Kähler if
\langle\theta(H,\chi),\chi\rangle=0,\,\forall H\in{\cal H}
, where
\chi=v^{\alpha}\delta_{\alpha}
is the horizontal radical vector field.

In local coordinates,
F
is strongly Kähler if and only if

\varGamma_{\beta;\mu}^{\alpha}=\varGamma_{\mu;\beta}^{\alpha}
;
is Kähler if and only if
\big(\varGamma_{\beta;\mu}^{\alpha}-\varGamma_{\mu;\beta}^{\alpha}\big)v^{\beta}=0
;
is weakly Kähler if and only if
G_{\alpha}\big(\varGamma_{\beta;\mu}^{\alpha}-\varGamma_{\mu;\beta}^{\alpha}\big)v^{\beta}=0
. Chen and Shen
[
6
]
proved Kähler-Finsler metrics are actually strongly Kähler.

The curvature operator of the Chern-Finsler connection
D
is given by

\Omega=\Omega^{\alpha}_{\beta}\otimes\big(\mathrm{d}z^{\beta}\otimes\delta_{\alpha}+\delta{v}^{\beta}\otimes\dot{\partial}_{\alpha}\big),

(2.8)

where
\Omega_{\beta}^{\alpha}=\bar{\partial}\theta_{\beta}^{\alpha}.

In local coordinates,
\Omega_{\beta}^{\alpha}
can be decomposed as

\begin{split}\Omega_{\beta}^{\alpha}=&\Omega^{\alpha}_{\beta;\mu\bar{\nu}}\mathrm{d}z^{\mu}\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar{z}^{\nu}+\Omega^{\alpha}_{\beta\mu;\bar{\nu}}\delta v^{\mu}\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar{z}^{\mu}+\Omega^{\alpha}_{\beta\bar{\nu};\mu}\mathrm{d}z^{\mu}\wedge\delta\bar{v}^{\nu}+\Omega^{\alpha}_{\beta\mu\bar{\nu}}\delta v^{\mu}\wedge\delta\bar{v}^{\nu},\end{split}

(2.9)

where

\begin{split}&\Omega^{\alpha}_{\beta;\mu\bar{\nu}}=-\delta_{\bar{\nu}}\left(\varGamma^{\alpha}_{\beta;\mu}\right)-\varGamma^{\alpha}_{\beta\gamma}\delta_{\bar{\nu}}\left(\varGamma^{\gamma}_{;\mu}\right),\ \ \Omega^{\alpha}_{\beta\mu;\bar{\nu}}=-\delta_{\bar{\nu}}\left(\varGamma^{\alpha}_{\beta\mu}\right),\\
&\Omega^{\alpha}_{\beta\bar{\nu};\mu}=-\dot{\partial}_{\bar{\nu}}\left(\varGamma^{\alpha}_{\beta;\mu}\right)-\varGamma^{\alpha}_{\beta\gamma}\dot{\partial}_{\bar{\nu}}\left(\varGamma^{\gamma}_{;\mu}\right),\ \ \Omega^{\alpha}_{\beta\mu\bar{\nu}}=-\dot{\partial}_{\bar{\nu}}\left(\varGamma^{\alpha}_{\beta\mu}\right).\end{split}

(2.10)

We denote by
\Omega_{\alpha\bar{\beta};\mu\bar{\nu}}=G_{\gamma\bar{\beta}}\Omega^{\gamma}_{\alpha;\mu\bar{\nu}}
the holomorphic sectional curvature tensors of
D
.
Locally,

\Omega_{\alpha\bar{\beta};\mu\bar{\nu}}=-\delta_{\bar{\nu}}\delta_{\mu}\left(G_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}\right)+\delta_{\mu}\left(G_{\alpha\bar{\lambda}}\right)G^{\bar{\lambda}\kappa}\delta_{\bar{\nu}}\left(G_{\kappa\bar{\beta}}\right)-G_{\alpha\bar{\beta}\sigma}\delta_{\bar{\nu}}\left(\varGamma^{\sigma}_{;\mu}\right).

(2.11)

The horizontal holomorphic flag curvature
K_{D}(H)
of
F
along a horizontal vector
H=H^{\alpha}\delta_{\alpha}\in{\cal H}_{v}
is defined by

K_{D}(H)=\displaystyle\frac{\langle\Omega(H,\bar{H})H,H\rangle_{v}}{\langle H,H\rangle_{v}}.

(2.12)

The holomorphic sectional curvature
K_{D}(v)
of
F
along
v\in\tilde{M}
is defined by

K_{D}(v)=\displaystyle\frac{\langle\Omega\left(\chi,\bar{\chi}\right)\chi,\chi\rangle_{v}}{G(v)^{2}}.

(2.13)

The Ricci curvatures and scalar curvatures of the Chern-Finsler connection
D
are, respectively, given by

\mathbf{Ric}_{D}=\sqrt{-1}\Omega_{\mu\bar{\nu}}\mathrm{d}z^{\mu}\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar{z}^{\nu}\quad with\quad\Omega_{\mu\bar{\nu}}=G^{\bar{\beta}\alpha}\Omega_{\alpha\bar{\beta};\mu\bar{\nu}},

(2.14)

and

s_{D}=G^{\bar{\beta}\alpha}G^{\bar{\nu}\mu}\Omega_{\alpha\bar{\beta};\mu\bar{\nu}}.

(2.15)

2.2
Balanced complex Finsler metrics

A strongly pseudoconvex complex Finsler metric
F

can induce a Hermitian metric

\tilde{G}=G_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}\mathrm{d}z^{\alpha}\otimes\mathrm{d}\bar{z}^{\beta}+(\log G)_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}\delta v^{\alpha}\otimes\delta\bar{v}^{\beta}.

on the projective tangent bundle
{\mathbb{P}}\tilde{M}=\tilde{M}/(\mathbb{C}\backslash\{0\})
.
If we set
\omega_{\cal V}=\sqrt{-1}(\log G)_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}\delta v^{\alpha}\wedge\delta\bar{v}^{\beta}
and
\omega_{\cal H}=\sqrt{-1}G_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}\mathrm{d}z^{\alpha}\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar{z}^{\beta}
,
then the invariant volume form of
{\mathbb{P}}\tilde{M}
is
[
23
]

\mathrm{d}\mu_{{\mathbb{P}}\tilde{M}}=\displaystyle\frac{\omega_{{\cal V}}^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}\wedge\displaystyle\frac{\omega_{{\cal H}}^{n}}{n!}.

(2.16)

Let
{\cal A}^{p,q}
be the set of smooth horizontal
(p,q)
-forms on
{\mathbb{P}}\tilde{M}
, that is, all coefficients of any
\varphi\in{\cal A}^{p,q}
are zero homogeneous with respect to fibre coordinates
v
. In local coordinates,

\varphi=\displaystyle\frac{1}{p!q!}\varphi_{\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{p}\bar{\beta}_{1}\cdots\bar{\beta}_{q}}\mathrm{d}z^{\alpha_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathrm{d}z^{\alpha_{p}}\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar{z}^{\beta_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar{z}^{\beta_{q}}.

If
M
is compact, then for any
\varphi,\,\psi\in{\cal A}^{p,q}
, there is a natural Hermitian inner product on
{\cal A}^{p,q}
given by

(\varphi,\psi)_{{\mathbb{P}}\tilde{M}}=\int_{{\mathbb{P}}\tilde{M}}\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle_{{\mathbb{P}}\tilde{M}}\mathrm{d}\mu_{{\mathbb{P}}\tilde{M}},

(2.17)

where

\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle_{{\mathbb{P}}\tilde{M}}=\displaystyle\frac{1}{p!q!}\varphi_{\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{p}\bar{\beta}_{1}\cdots\bar{\beta}_{q}}G^{\bar{\gamma}_{1}\alpha_{1}}\cdots G^{\bar{\gamma}_{p}\alpha_{p}}G^{\bar{\beta}_{1}\delta_{1}}\cdots G^{\bar{\beta}_{q}\delta_{q}}\overline{\psi_{\gamma_{1}\cdots\gamma_{p}\bar{\delta}_{1}\cdots\bar{\delta}_{q}}}.

(2.18)

Let
\varphi\in{\cal A}^{p,q}
, then two horizonal operator
{\partial}_{{\cal H}}:{\cal A}^{p,q}\rightarrow{\cal A}^{p+1,q}
and
\bar{\partial}_{{\cal H}}:{\cal A}^{p,q}\rightarrow{\cal A}^{p,q+1}

are defined by

\displaystyle{\partial}_{{\cal H}}\varphi

where
\alpha_{1}\cdots\widehat{{\alpha}_{j}}\cdots\alpha_{p+1}
is the sequence which we get from

.
Let us denote by
\mathrm{d}_{{\cal H}}=\partial_{{\cal H}}+\bar{\partial}_{{\cal H}}

(resp.
{\partial}_{{\cal H}}^{*}
) of

(resp.

the inter product
(\,,\,)_{{\mathbb{P}}\tilde{M}}

\bar{\partial}_{{\cal H}}^{*}

*:{\cal A}^{p,q}\rightarrow{\cal A}^{n-q,n-p}

\int_{{\mathbb{P}}\tilde{M}}\varphi\wedge*\bar{\psi}\wedge\displaystyle\frac{\omega_{{\cal V}}^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}=(\varphi,\psi)_{{\mathbb{P}}\tilde{M}},\quad\varphi,\,\psi\in{\cal A}^{p,q}.

(2.19)

Then

(1)

\overline{*\psi}=*\bar{\psi}

(2)

**\psi=(-1)^{p+q}\psi

(3)

{\partial}_{{\cal H}}^{*}=-*\bar{\partial}_{{\cal H}}*

Definition 2.4
.

A compact strongly pseudoconvex complex Finsler manifold
(M,F)
is called balanced if
the horizontal fundamental form
\omega_{{\cal H}}
is horizonal co-closed, i.e.,
\mathrm{d}_{{\cal H}}^{*}\omega_{{\cal H}}=0
, where
\mathrm{d}_{{\cal H}}^{*}=\partial_{{\cal H}}^{*}+\bar{\partial}_{{\cal H}}^{*}
.

By setting the horizonal torsion tensor

S^{\gamma}_{\alpha\beta}=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\varGamma^{\gamma}_{\alpha;\beta}-\varGamma^{\gamma}_{\beta;\alpha}\right),

(2.20)

we introduce the following tensor
[
10
]

S_{\alpha}=\sum_{\gamma=1}^{n}S^{\gamma}_{\alpha\gamma}.

(2.21)

Put

L_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}^{\gamma}=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}G^{\bar{\lambda}\gamma}\left[\delta_{\bar{\beta}}(G_{\alpha\bar{\lambda}})-\delta_{\bar{\lambda}}(G_{\alpha\bar{\beta}})\right],

then

G^{\bar{\beta}\alpha}L_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}^{\gamma}G_{\gamma\bar{\lambda}}=S_{\bar{\lambda}}=-\sum_{\gamma=1}^{n}L_{\gamma\bar{\lambda}}^{\gamma}.

(2.22)

By Theorem 3.5 in
[
24
]
, we have

{\partial}_{{\cal H}}^{*}\omega_{{\cal H}}=2\sqrt{-1}S_{\bar{\alpha}}\mathrm{d}\bar{z}^{\alpha}{\quad\rm and\quad}\bar{\partial}_{{\cal H}}^{*}\omega_{{\cal H}}=-2\sqrt{-1}S_{\alpha}\mathrm{d}z^{\alpha}.

(2.23)

We set a horizontal (1,0)-form
\mathcal{S}=S_{\alpha}\mathrm{d}z^{\alpha}
.
A direct computation shows

*{\cal S}=-\sqrt{-1}{\cal S}\wedge\frac{\omega_{{\cal H}}^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}.

Since
*\omega_{{\cal H}}=\frac{\omega_{{\cal H}}^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}
, we have

\partial_{{\cal H}}\omega_{{\cal H}}^{n-1}=-2(n-1)!\sqrt{-1}*{\cal S}=-2{\cal S}\wedge\omega_{{\cal H}}^{n-1}.

(2.24)

Hence, we have

Proposition 2.5
.

The following four statements are equivalent:

(a)

(M,F)
is a balanced complex Finsler manifold;

(b)

{\cal S}=0
(
or
S_{\alpha}=0
, or
\sum\limits_{\gamma=1}^{n}L_{\gamma\bar{\alpha}}^{\gamma}=0
)
;

(c)

G^{\bar{\beta}\alpha}L_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}^{\gamma}=0
;

(d)

\mathrm{d}_{{\cal H}}\omega_{{\cal H}}^{n-1}=0
.

Clearly, a Kähler-Finsler metric on a compact complex manifold must be balanced. When
{\rm dim}_{\mathbb{C}}(M)=2
,
S_{1}=S_{2}=0
means

S^{1}_{12}=S^{2}_{12}=0
, so balanced complex Finsler surfaces are Kähler-Finsler.
It is natural to find a sufficient condition for a balanced complex Finsler metric to be Kähler-Finsler.

2.3
Rund Kähler-Finsler-like metrics

The complex Rund connection of a strongly pseudoconvex complex Finsler manifold
(M,F)
was first introduced in
[
15
]
, which is also a generalization of the Hermitian connection.
Denote by
\hat{D}:{\cal X}({\cal V})\rightarrow{\cal X}(T_{\mathbb{C}}^{*}{\tilde{M}}\otimes{\cal V})
the complex Rund connection.
Its connection 1-forms are given by

\hat{\theta}_{\beta}^{\alpha}=G^{\bar{\lambda}\alpha}\partial_{\cal H}G_{\beta\bar{\lambda}}=\varGamma_{\beta;\mu}^{\alpha}\mathrm{d}z^{\mu}.

(2.25)

Locally, the curvature form of the complex Rund connection
\hat{D}
is
[
16
]

\hat{\Omega}_{\beta}^{\alpha}=\hat{\Omega}^{\alpha}_{\beta;\mu\bar{\nu}}\mathrm{d}z^{\mu}\wedge\mathrm{d}{\bar{z}}^{\nu}+\hat{\Omega}^{\alpha}_{\beta\nu;\mu}\mathrm{d}z^{\mu}\wedge\delta v^{\nu}+\hat{\Omega}^{\alpha}_{\beta\bar{\nu};\mu}\mathrm{d}z^{\mu}\wedge\delta\bar{v}^{\nu},

where

\hat{\Omega}^{\alpha}_{\beta;\mu\bar{\nu}}=-\delta_{\bar{\nu}}\left(\varGamma_{\beta;\mu}^{\alpha}\right),\quad\hat{\Omega}^{\alpha}_{\beta\nu;\mu}=-\dot{\partial}_{\nu}\left(\varGamma_{\beta;\mu}^{\alpha}\right),\quad\hat{\Omega}^{\alpha}_{\beta\bar{\nu};\mu}=-\dot{\partial}_{\bar{\nu}}\left(\varGamma_{\beta;\mu}^{\alpha}\right).

(2.26)

Clearly,
\Omega^{\alpha}_{\beta;\mu\bar{\nu}}=\hat{\Omega}^{\alpha}_{\beta;\mu\bar{\nu}}-\varGamma^{\alpha}_{\beta\gamma}\delta_{\bar{\nu}}\left(\varGamma^{\gamma}_{;\mu}\right)
. Notice that if
F
is Kähler-Finsler then

\hat{\Omega}^{\alpha}_{\beta;\mu\bar{\nu}}=\hat{\Omega}^{\alpha}_{\mu;\beta\bar{\nu}}
, but
{\Omega}^{\alpha}_{\beta;\mu\bar{\nu}}={\Omega}^{\alpha}_{\mu;\beta\bar{\nu}}
may fail to be true.
In
[
21
]
, the authors call a Hermitian metric
h
is Kähler-like if its Chern curvature tensors
R^{h}

satisfy
R^{h}_{X\bar{Y}Z\bar{W}}=R^{h}_{Z\bar{Y}X\bar{W}}
for any type (1,0) tangent vectors
X,\,Y,\,Z
, and
W
. In
[
4
]
, a Kähler-like manifold is called a Hermitian manifold with holomorphic torsion tensors. Now we extend this definition to complex Finsler geometry. We shall define a class of complex Finsler metrics that are more general than Kähler-Finsler metrics.

Definition 2.6
.

A strongly pseudoconvex complex Finsler metric
F
is called Rund Kähler-Finsler-like if
the horizontal part of the curvature form of the complex Rund connection satisfies
\hat{\Omega}^{\alpha}_{\beta;\mu\bar{\nu}}=\hat{\Omega}^{\alpha}_{\mu;\beta\bar{\nu}}
for every
\alpha,\,\beta,\,\mu,\,\nu=1,\cdots,n
.

Clearly, every Kähler-Finsler metric is necessarily Rund Kähler-Finsler-like, and
F
is Rund Kähler-Finsler-like is equivalent to the equations
\delta_{\bar{\nu}}\big(S^{\alpha}_{\beta\mu}\big)=0
. A natural question is whether a Rund Kähler-Finsler-like metric
F
is Kähler-Finsler.

3
The canonical connection

Munteanu
[
14
]
introduced a metric connection
\nabla
with respect to the Sasaki-type lift Hermitian structure (
2.5
) with horizontal and vertical zero torsions.
Connection coefficients of
\nabla
are

\begin{split}L_{\beta\mu}^{\alpha}&=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}G^{\bar{\lambda}\alpha}\left(\delta_{\mu}(G_{\beta\bar{\lambda}})+\delta_{\beta}(G_{\mu\bar{\lambda}})\right),\quad C_{\beta\mu}^{\alpha}=\varGamma_{\beta\mu}^{\alpha},\\
L_{\beta\bar{\mu}}^{\alpha}&=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}G^{\bar{\lambda}\alpha}\left(\delta_{\bar{\mu}}(G_{\beta\bar{\lambda}})-\delta_{\bar{\lambda}}(G_{\beta\bar{\mu}})\right),\quad C_{\beta\bar{\mu}}^{\alpha}=0.\end{split}

(3.1)

Then connection 1-forms of
\nabla
are given by

\omega_{\beta}^{\alpha}=L_{\beta\mu}^{\alpha}\mathrm{d}z^{\mu}+L_{\beta\bar{\mu}}^{\alpha}\mathrm{d}\bar{z}^{\mu}+C_{\beta\mu}^{\alpha}\delta{v}^{\mu}.

(3.2)

Munteanu
[
14
]
called the connection
\nabla
the canonical connection induced by the Chern-Finsler connection
D
. The connection
\nabla
is a natural generalization of the complexified Levi-Civita connection on the holomorphic tangent bundle of a Hermitian manifold
[
12
]
.
Clearly, the connection
\nabla
and the Chern-Finsler connection
D
coincide if and only if
F
is a Kähler-Finsler metric.

The holomorphic sectional curvature tensor of
\nabla
is defined by

R_{\alpha\bar{\beta}\mu\bar{\nu}}=\left\langle\nabla_{\delta_{\mu}}\nabla_{\delta_{\bar{\nu}}}\delta_{\alpha}-\nabla_{\delta_{\bar{\nu}}}\nabla_{\delta_{\mu}}\delta_{\alpha}-\nabla_{[\delta_{\mu},\delta_{\bar{\nu}}]}\delta_{\alpha},\delta_{\beta}\right\rangle_{v}.

(3.3)

The horizontal holomorphic flag curvature
K_{\nabla}(H)
of
\nabla
along a horizontal vector
H=H^{\alpha}\delta_{\alpha}\in{\cal H}_{v}
is defined by

K_{\nabla}(H)=\displaystyle\frac{R_{\alpha\bar{\beta}\mu\bar{\nu}}(v)H^{\alpha}\bar{H}^{\beta}H^{\mu}\bar{H}^{\nu}}{\left(G_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}(v)H^{\alpha}\bar{H}^{\beta}\right)^{2}}.

(3.4)

The holomorphic sectional curvature
K_{\nabla}(v)
of
\nabla
along
v\in\tilde{M}
is defined by

K_{\nabla}(v)=\displaystyle\frac{R_{\alpha\bar{\beta}\mu\bar{\nu}}(v)v^{\alpha}\bar{v}^{\beta}v^{\mu}\bar{v}^{\nu}}{G(v)^{2}}.

(3.5)

The Ricci curvature and scalar curvature of the canonical connection
\nabla
are

\mathbf{Ric}_{\nabla}=\sqrt{-1}R_{\mu\bar{\nu}}\mathrm{d}z^{\mu}\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar{z}^{\nu}\quad with\quad R_{\mu\bar{\nu}}=G^{\bar{\beta}\alpha}R_{\alpha\bar{\beta}\mu\bar{\nu}},

(3.6)

s_{\nabla}=G^{\bar{\beta}\alpha}G^{\bar{\nu}\mu}R_{\alpha\bar{\beta}\mu\bar{\nu}}.

(3.7)

Now we give the explicit formula of

.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(M,F)
be a strongly pseudoconvex complex Finsler manifold. Then

\begin{split}&R_{\alpha\bar{\beta}\mu\bar{\nu}}\\
=&-\frac{1}{2}\left[\delta_{\bar{\nu}}\delta_{\alpha}(G_{\mu\bar{\beta}})+\delta_{\bar{\beta}}\delta_{\mu}(G_{\alpha\bar{\nu}})+\delta_{\bar{\nu}}\left(\varGamma^{\sigma}_{;\mu}\right)G_{\alpha\bar{\beta}\sigma}+\delta_{\bar{\beta}}\left(\varGamma^{\sigma}_{;\mu}\right)G_{\alpha\bar{\nu}\sigma}\right]\\
&+\frac{1}{4}\left[\delta_{\mu}(G_{\alpha\bar{\lambda}})+\delta_{\alpha}(G_{\mu\bar{\lambda}})\right]G^{\bar{\lambda}\gamma}\left[\delta_{\bar{\nu}}(G_{\gamma\bar{\beta}})+\delta_{\bar{\beta}}(G_{\gamma\bar{\nu}})\right]\\
&-\frac{1}{4}\big[\delta_{\bar{\nu}}(G_{\alpha\bar{\lambda}})-\delta_{\bar{\lambda}}(G_{\alpha\bar{\nu}})\big]G^{\bar{\lambda}\gamma}\left[\delta_{\mu}(G_{\gamma\bar{\beta}})-\delta_{\gamma}(G_{\mu\bar{\beta}})\right]\\
&+\frac{1}{2}\left[\delta_{\mu}\big(\varGamma^{\bar{\tau}}_{;\bar{\beta}}\big)G_{\alpha\bar{\nu}\bar{\tau}}-\delta_{\mu}\left(\varGamma^{\bar{\tau}}_{;\bar{\nu}}\right)G_{\alpha\bar{\beta}\bar{\tau}}\right].\end{split}

(3.8)

Proof.

Since

\displaystyle\nabla_{\delta_{\mu}}\nabla_{\delta_{\bar{\nu}}}\delta_{\alpha}=

\displaystyle\left(\delta_{\mu}\left(L_{\alpha\bar{\nu}}^{\gamma}\right)+L_{\alpha\bar{\nu}}^{\sigma}L_{\sigma\mu}^{\gamma}\right)\delta_{\gamma},

\displaystyle\nabla_{\delta_{\bar{\nu}}}\nabla_{\delta_{\mu}}\delta_{\alpha}=

\displaystyle\left(\delta_{\bar{\nu}}\left(L_{\alpha\mu}^{\gamma}\right)+L_{\alpha\mu}^{\sigma}L_{\sigma\bar{\nu}}^{\gamma}\right)\delta_{\gamma},

\left\langle\nabla_{[\delta_{\mu},\delta_{\bar{\nu}}]}\delta_{\alpha},\delta_{\beta}\right\rangle_{v}=G_{\alpha\bar{\beta}\sigma}\delta_{\bar{\nu}}(\varGamma^{\sigma}_{;\mu}),

by (
3.3
) we have

R_{\alpha\bar{\beta}\mu\bar{\nu}}=\left[\delta_{\mu}\left(L_{\alpha\bar{\nu}}^{\gamma}\right)-\delta_{\bar{\nu}}\left(L_{\alpha\mu}^{\gamma}\right)+L_{\alpha\bar{\nu}}^{\sigma}L_{\sigma\mu}^{\gamma}-L_{\alpha\mu}^{\sigma}L_{\sigma\bar{\nu}}^{\gamma}\right]G_{\gamma\bar{\beta}}-G_{\alpha\bar{\beta}\sigma}\delta_{\bar{\nu}}(\varGamma^{\sigma}_{;\mu}).

(3.9)

A direct computation shows that

\displaystyle\left(\delta_{\mu}\left(L_{\alpha\bar{\nu}}^{\gamma}\right)+L_{\alpha\bar{\nu}}^{\sigma}L_{\sigma\mu}^{\gamma}\right)G_{\gamma\bar{\beta}}

(3.10)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\{\delta_{\bar{\nu}}\delta_{\mu}(G_{\alpha\bar{\beta}})-\delta_{\bar{\beta}}\delta_{\mu}(G_{\alpha\bar{\nu}})+\left[\delta_{\mu},\delta_{\bar{\nu}}\right](G_{\alpha\bar{\beta}})-\left[\delta_{\mu},\delta_{\bar{\beta}}\right](G_{\alpha\bar{\nu}})\right\}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}\big[\delta_{\bar{\nu}}(G_{\alpha\bar{\lambda}})-\delta_{\bar{\lambda}}(G_{\alpha\bar{\nu}})\big]G^{\bar{\lambda}\gamma}\big[\delta_{\mu}(G_{\gamma\bar{\beta}})-\delta_{\gamma}(G_{\mu\bar{\beta}})\big],

and

\displaystyle-\left(\delta_{\bar{\nu}}\left(L_{\alpha\mu}^{\gamma}\right)+L_{\alpha\mu}^{\sigma}L_{\sigma\bar{\nu}}^{\gamma}\right)G_{\gamma\bar{\beta}}

(3.11)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\left[\delta_{\bar{\nu}}\delta_{\mu}(G_{\alpha\bar{\beta}})+\delta_{\bar{\nu}}\delta_{\alpha}(G_{\mu\bar{\beta}})\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\left[\delta_{\mu}(G_{\alpha\bar{\lambda}})+\delta_{\alpha}(G_{\mu\bar{\lambda}})\right]G^{\bar{\lambda}\gamma}\left[\delta_{\bar{\nu}}(G_{\gamma\bar{\beta}})+\delta_{\bar{\beta}}(G_{\gamma\bar{\nu}})\right].

Since

\left[\delta_{\mu},\delta_{\bar{\nu}}\right]=\delta_{\bar{\nu}}\left(\varGamma^{\sigma}_{;\mu}\right)\dot{\partial}_{\sigma}-\delta_{\mu}\left(\varGamma^{\bar{\tau}}_{;\bar{\nu}}\right)\dot{\partial}_{\bar{\tau}},

we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left[\left[\delta_{\mu},\delta_{\bar{\nu}}\right](G_{\alpha\bar{\beta}})-\left[\delta_{\mu},\delta_{\bar{\beta}}\right](G_{\alpha\bar{\nu}})\right]-G_{\alpha\bar{\beta}\sigma}\delta_{\bar{\nu}}(\varGamma^{\sigma}_{;\mu})

(3.12)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left[-\delta_{\bar{\nu}}\left(\varGamma^{\sigma}_{;\mu}\right)G_{\alpha\bar{\beta}\sigma}-\delta_{\mu}\left(\varGamma^{\bar{\tau}}_{;\bar{\nu}}\right)G_{\alpha\bar{\beta}\bar{\tau}}-\delta_{\bar{\beta}}\left(\varGamma^{\sigma}_{;\mu}\right)G_{\alpha\bar{\nu}\sigma}+\delta_{\mu}\big(\varGamma^{\bar{\tau}}_{;\bar{\beta}}\big)G_{\alpha\bar{\nu}\bar{\tau}}\right].

By substituting (
3.10
)-(
3.12
) into (
3.9
) we get (
3.8
). This completes the proof.

\square

Extending the product

4
Curvature relations

Munteanu
[
14
]
proved a strongly pseudoconvex complex Finsler metric
F
is a Kähler-Finsler metric if and only if the canonical connection
\nabla
and the Chern-Finsler connection
D
coincide.
If
F
is a Kähler-Finsler metric, then
R_{\alpha\bar{\beta}\mu\bar{\nu}}=\Omega_{\alpha\bar{\beta};\mu\bar{\nu}}
. When a strongly pseudoconvex complex Finsler metric
F
such that
R_{\alpha\bar{\beta}\mu\bar{\nu}}=\Omega_{\alpha\bar{\beta};\mu\bar{\nu}}
, is it necessarily Kähler-Finsler? By virtue of Lemma 3.1, we give a positive answer.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
(M,F)
be a strongly pseudoconvex complex Finsler manifold. Then

(1)

R_{\alpha\bar{\beta}\mu\bar{\nu}}=\Omega_{\alpha\bar{\beta};\mu\bar{\nu}}
, if and only if

K_{\nabla}(H)=K_{D}(H)
, if and only if
F
is a Kähler-Finsler metric;

(2)

K_{\nabla}(v)=K_{D}(v)
,
if and only if
F
is a weakly Kähler-Finsler metric.

Proof.

(1) By the definition of horizontal holomorphic flag curvature,

R_{\alpha\bar{\beta}\mu\bar{\nu}}=\Omega_{\alpha\bar{\beta};\mu\bar{\nu}}

is equivalent to

K_{\nabla}(H)=K_{D}(H),\quad\forall H=H^{\alpha}\delta_{\alpha}\in{\cal H}_{v}.

It follows from (
3.8
) in Lemma 3.1 that

\displaystyle R_{\alpha\bar{\beta}\mu\bar{\nu}}H^{\alpha}\bar{H}^{\beta}H^{\mu}\bar{H}^{\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left[\delta_{\bar{\nu}}\delta_{\mu}(G_{\alpha\bar{\beta}})-\delta_{\mu}(G_{\alpha\bar{\beta}})G^{\bar{\lambda}\gamma}\delta_{\bar{\nu}}(G_{\gamma\bar{\beta}})+G_{\alpha\bar{\beta}\sigma}\delta_{\bar{\nu}}\left(\varGamma^{\sigma}_{;\mu}\right)\right]H^{\alpha}\bar{H}^{\beta}H^{\mu}\bar{H}^{\nu}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}H^{\alpha}\bar{H}^{\nu}\big[\delta_{\bar{\nu}}(G_{\alpha\bar{\lambda}})-\delta_{\bar{\lambda}}(G_{\alpha\bar{\nu}})\big]G^{\bar{\lambda}\gamma}\left[\delta_{\mu}(G_{\gamma\bar{\beta}})-\delta_{\gamma}(G_{\mu\bar{\beta}})\right]\bar{H}^{\beta}H^{\mu},

furthermore

\displaystyle R_{\alpha\bar{\beta}\mu\bar{\nu}}H^{\alpha}\bar{H}^{\beta}H^{\mu}\bar{H}^{\nu}-\Omega_{\alpha\bar{\beta};\mu\bar{\nu}}H^{\alpha}\bar{H}^{\beta}H^{\mu}\bar{H}^{\nu}

(4.1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}H^{\alpha}\bar{H}^{\nu}\big[\delta_{\bar{\nu}}(G_{\alpha\bar{\lambda}})-\delta_{\bar{\lambda}}(G_{\alpha\bar{\nu}})\big]G^{\bar{\lambda}\gamma}\left[\delta_{\mu}(G_{\gamma\bar{\beta}})-\delta_{\gamma}(G_{\mu\bar{\beta}})\right]\bar{H}^{\beta}H^{\mu}.

Now we use the similar argument as in the proof of Main Theorem in
[
11

and the other horizontal curvature terms of the Rund connection are zero. Hence

6
Conformal transformations of a balanced complex Finsler manifold

Suppose that
\tilde{F}(z,v)={\rm e}^{\frac{1}{2}\rho(z)}F(z,v)
is a conformal metric of a strongly pseudoconvex complex Finsler metric
F
, where
\rho
is a smooth real function on
M
.
Set
\tilde{G}=\tilde{F}^{2}
and
\tilde{G}_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}=\dot{\partial}_{\alpha}\dot{\partial}_{\bar{\beta}}(\tilde{G})
.
From now on, we denote by an extra symbol “
\sim
” the corresponding quantities with respect to the conformal metric
\tilde{F}(z,v)
.

Lemma 6.1
.

[
2
]

Suppose
\tilde{F}(z,v)={\rm e}^{\frac{1}{2}\rho(z)}F(z,v)
is a conformal metric of
a strongly pseudoconvex complex Finsler metric
F
. Then

\displaystyle\tilde{\varGamma}_{;\beta}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\varGamma_{;\beta}^{\alpha}+\rho_{;\beta}v^{\alpha},\quad~\tilde{\delta}_{\beta}=\delta_{\beta}-\rho_{;\beta}\iota,

\displaystyle\tilde{\varGamma}_{\beta;\gamma}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\varGamma_{\beta;\gamma}^{\alpha}+\delta^{\alpha}_{\beta}\rho_{;\gamma},\quad\tilde{\varGamma}_{\beta\gamma}^{\alpha}=\varGamma_{\beta\gamma}^{\alpha},

where
\rho_{;\beta}=\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial z^{\beta}}
, and
\iota=v^{\alpha}\dot{\partial}_{\alpha}
is the radial vertical vector field associated to
F
.

Theorem 6.2
.

On a compact complex manifold
M
, there exists at most one balanced complex Finsler metric (up to constant multiples) in each conformal class of complex Finsler metrics. If
(M,F)
is a non-compact Rund Kähler-Finsler-like manifold, then the conformal
metric
\tilde{F}(z,v)={\rm e}^{\frac{1}{2}\rho(z)}F(z,v)
is Rund Kähler-Finsler-like if and only if
\partial\bar{\partial}\rho=0
.

Proof.

Suppose that
F
is a balanced complex Finsler metric on a compact complex manifold, and

\tilde{F}(z,v)={\rm e}^{\frac{1}{2}\rho(z)}F(z,v)
is a conformal transformation of
F
. By equality (3.4) in
[
10
]
, we have

\tilde{S}_{\alpha}=S_{\alpha}+\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(1-n)\rho_{;\alpha}.

(6.1)

If
\tilde{F}
is also balanced, then
\tilde{S}_{\alpha}=S_{\alpha}=0
and
\rho_{;\alpha}=0
,
i.e.,
\rho
is a constant.

Now assume that
(M,F)
is a non-compact strongly pseudoconvex complex Finsler manifold.
Noting that
{S}^{\alpha}_{\beta\gamma}
is
(0,0)
homogeneous with respect to the fiber coordinate
v
, we have
\iota({S}^{\alpha}_{\beta\gamma})=0
.
By Lemma
6.1
, we have

\tilde{\delta}_{\bar{\nu}}\left(\tilde{S}^{\alpha}_{\beta\gamma}\right)=\delta_{\bar{\nu}}\left(S_{\beta\gamma}^{\alpha}\right)+\frac{1}{2}(\delta^{\alpha}_{\beta}\rho_{;\gamma\bar{\nu}}-\delta^{\alpha}_{\gamma}\rho_{;\beta\bar{\nu}}).

(6.2)

If
F
is Rund Kähler-Finsler-like, then the conformal metric
\tilde{F}
is Rund Kähler-Finsler-like if and only if

\delta^{\alpha}_{\beta}\rho_{;\gamma\bar{\nu}}-\delta^{\alpha}_{\gamma}\rho_{;\beta\bar{\nu}}=0
.
Contracting on indices
\alpha
and
\beta
, it yields
(n-1)\rho_{;\gamma\bar{\nu}}=0
, which is equivalent to

\partial\bar{\partial}\rho=0
. This completes the proof.

\square

Remark 6.3
.

Obviously, the equality
\partial\bar{\partial}\rho=0
implies that
\rho
is a harmonic function, however, the converse is not ture. Especially for
{\rm dim}_{\mathbb{C}}M=2
, it is easy to check that
\rho_{1}(z_{1},z_{2})=x_{1}x_{2}-y_{1}y_{2}
and

\rho_{2}(z_{1},z_{2})=x_{1}y_{2}+y_{1}x_{2}
both satisfy
\partial\bar{\partial}\rho=0
.

Theorem 6.4
.

On a compact strongly pseudoconvex complex Finsler manifold
(M,F)
, the conformal metric
\tilde{F}(z,v)={\rm e}^{\frac{1}{2}\rho(z)}F(z,v)
is balanced, if and only if

\mathbf{Ric}_{D}-\mathbf{Ric}_{\nabla}=(n-1)\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\rho,

(6.3)

if and only if

\int_{{\mathbb{P}}\tilde{M}}\left(s_{D}-s_{\nabla}\right)\mathrm{d}\mu_{{\mathbb{P}}\tilde{M}}=(n-1)\left(\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\rho,\omega_{{\cal H}}\right)_{{\mathbb{P}}\tilde{M}}.

(6.4)

Proof.

By Lemma
6.1
and equalitis (
2.23
), (
6.1
), we obtain

\bar{\partial}_{\widetilde{{\cal H}}}^{*}\omega_{\widetilde{{\cal H}}}=\bar{\partial}_{{\cal H}}^{*}\omega_{{\cal H}}+(n-1)\sqrt{-1}\partial\rho.

(6.5)

Therefore

\bar{\partial}_{\widetilde{{\cal H}}}\bar{\partial}_{\widetilde{{\cal H}}}^{*}\omega_{\widetilde{{\cal H}}}=\bar{\partial}_{{\cal H}}\bar{\partial}_{{\cal H}}^{*}\omega_{{\cal H}}-(n-1)\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\rho

(6.6)

and

{\partial}_{\widetilde{{\cal H}}}\bar{\partial}_{\widetilde{{\cal H}}}^{*}\omega_{\widetilde{{\cal H}}}={\partial}_{{\cal H}}\bar{\partial}_{{\cal H}}^{*}\omega_{{\cal H}}.

(6.7)

If
\tilde{F}
is balanced, i.e.,
\bar{\partial}_{\widetilde{{\cal H}}}^{*}\omega_{\widetilde{{\cal H}}}=0
,
then

{\partial}_{{\cal H}}{\partial}_{{\cal H}}^{*}\omega_{{\cal H}}+\bar{\partial}_{{\cal H}}\bar{\partial}_{{\cal H}}^{*}\omega_{{\cal H}}=2(n-1)\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\rho.

Theorem 6.5
.

Suppose that
(M,F)
is a balanced complex Finsler manifold with positive Chern-Finsler scalar curvature. Then the conformal metric
\tilde{F}(z,v)={\rm e}^{\frac{1}{2}\rho(z)}F(z,v)
owns positive Chern-Finsler total scalar curvature, that is

\int_{{\mathbb{P}}\tilde{M}}\tilde{s}_{\tilde{D}}\mathrm{d}\tilde{\mu}_{{\mathbb{P}}\tilde{M}}>0.

(6.9)

Proof.

By Lemma
6.1
, a direct computation shows

\tilde{\Omega}_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}={\Omega}_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}-n\rho_{;\alpha\bar{\beta}},

(6.10)

and then

\tilde{s}_{\tilde{D}}={\rm e}^{-\rho}\left(s_{D}-nG^{\bar{\beta}\alpha}\rho_{;\alpha\bar{\beta}}\right).

(6.11)

Note that
[
8
]

\mathrm{d}\tilde{\mu}_{{\mathbb{P}}\tilde{M}}={\rm e}^{n\rho}\mathrm{d}{\mu}_{{\mathbb{P}}\tilde{M}},

thus

\int_{{\mathbb{P}}\tilde{M}}\tilde{s}_{\tilde{D}}\mathrm{d}\tilde{\mu}_{{\mathbb{P}}\tilde{M}}=\int_{{\mathbb{P}}\tilde{M}}{\rm e}^{(n-1)\rho}\left(s_{D}-nG^{\bar{\beta}\alpha}\rho_{;\alpha\bar{\beta}}\right)\mathrm{d}{\mu}_{{\mathbb{P}}\tilde{M}}.

(6.12)

By the balanced condition, we have

{\rm div}\left(\uparrow\partial{\rm e}^{(n-1)\rho}\right)=G^{\bar{\beta}\alpha}({\rm e}^{(n-1)\rho})_{;\alpha\bar{\beta}}.

(6.13)

Hence

\displaystyle n\int_{{\mathbb{P}}\tilde{M}}{\rm e}^{(n-1)\rho}G^{\bar{\beta}\alpha}\rho_{;\alpha\bar{\beta}}\mathrm{d}{\mu}_{{\mathbb{P}}\tilde{M}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{n}{n-1}\int_{{\mathbb{P}}\tilde{M}}G^{\bar{\beta}\alpha}\left[\left({\rm e}^{(n-1)\rho}\right)_{;\alpha\bar{\beta}}-(n-1)^{2}{\rm e}^{(n-1)\rho}\rho_{;\alpha}\rho_{;\bar{\beta}}\right]\mathrm{d}{\mu}_{{\mathbb{P}}\tilde{M}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{n}{n-1}\int_{{\mathbb{P}}\tilde{M}}\left[{\rm div}\left(\uparrow\partial{\rm e}^{(n-1)\rho}\right)-4\left\langle\partial{\rm e}^{\frac{1}{2}(n-1)\rho},\partial{\rm e}^{\frac{1}{2}(n-1)\rho}\right\rangle_{{\mathbb{P}}\tilde{M}}\right]\mathrm{d}{\mu}_{{\mathbb{P}}\tilde{M}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\displaystyle\frac{4n}{n-1}\left(\partial{\rm e}^{\frac{1}{2}(n-1)\rho},\partial{\rm e}^{\frac{1}{2}(n-1)\rho}\right)_{{\mathbb{P}}\tilde{M}}.

Therefore, we obtain

\int_{{\mathbb{P}}\tilde{M}}\tilde{s}_{\tilde{D}}\mathrm{d}\tilde{\mu}_{{\mathbb{P}}\tilde{M}}=\int_{{\mathbb{P}}\tilde{M}}{\rm e}^{(n-1)\rho}s_{D}\mathrm{d}{\mu}_{{\mathbb{P}}\tilde{M}}+\displaystyle\frac{4n}{n-1}\left(\partial{\rm e}^{\frac{1}{2}(n-1)\rho},\partial{\rm e}^{\frac{1}{2}(n-1)\rho}\right)_{{\mathbb{P}}\tilde{M}}.

(6.14)

If
s_{D}>0
, then
\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{P}}\tilde{M}}\tilde{s}_{\tilde{D}}\mathrm{d}\tilde{\mu}_{{\mathbb{P}}\tilde{M}}\geq\int_{{\mathbb{P}}\tilde{M}}{\rm e}^{(n-1)\rho}s_{D}\mathrm{d}{\mu}_{{\mathbb{P}}\tilde{M}}>0
.

\square

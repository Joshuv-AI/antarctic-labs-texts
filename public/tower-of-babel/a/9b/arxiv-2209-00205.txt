Abstract
. For an essentially small hereditary abelian category 𝒜 \mathcal{A} , we define a new kind of algebra ℋ Δ ​ ( 𝒜 ) \mathcal{H}_{\Delta}(\mathcal{A}) , called the Δ \Delta -Hall algebra of 𝒜 \mathcal{A} . The basis of ℋ Δ ​ ( 𝒜 ) \mathcal{H}_{\Delta}(\mathcal{A}) is the isomorphism classes of objects in 𝒜 \mathcal{A} , and the Δ \Delta -Hall numbers calculate certain three-cycles of exact sequences in 𝒜 \mathcal{A} . We show that the Δ \Delta -Hall algebra ℋ Δ ​ ( 𝒜 ) \mathcal{H}_{\Delta}(\mathcal{A}) is isomorphic to the 1-periodic derived Hall algebra of 𝒜 \mathcal{A} . By taking suitable extension and twisting, we can obtain the ı \imath Hall algebra and the semi-derived Hall algebra associated to 𝒜 \mathcal{A} respectively. When applied to the the nilpotent representation category 𝒜 = rep nil ​ ( 𝐤 ​ Q ) \mathcal{A}={\rm rep^{nil}}(\mathbf{k}Q) for an arbitrary quiver Q Q without loops, the ( resp. extended) Δ \Delta -Hall algebra provides a new realization of the ( resp. universal) ı \imath quantum group associated to Q Q .

1.
Introduction

The Hall algebra approach to quantum groups is a hot topic in representation theory of algebras.
In 1990, Ringel
[
22
]
constructed a Hall algebra associated to a quiver
Q
over a finite field, and identified its generic version with the positive part of quantum groups when
Q
is Dynkin type.
Later, Green
[
9
]
generalized it to Kac-Moody setting.

Since then, people made efforts to the realization of the whole quantum groups via Hall algebras. Xiao
[
25
]
constructed the Drinfeld double of Hall algebras. Bridgeland
[
6
]
considered the Hall algebra of 2-periodic complexes of projective modules for a hereditary algebra.
Lu-Peng
[
15
]
generalized Bridgeland’s construction to the
hereditary abelian categories (perhaps without projective objects).
The Hall type Lie algebras of root categories can also be used to realize Kac-Moody algebras, see Peng-Xiao
[
21
]
and Lin-Peng
[
14
]
.

Ringel’s version of Hall algebra has found further generalizations and improvements
which allow more flexibilities.
Toën
[
24
]
and Xiao-Xu
[
26
]
introduced the derived Hall algebras for
triangulated categories satisfying certain homological finiteness conditions.
Xu-Chen
[
27
]
constructed derived Hall algebras for odd periodic triangulated categories.
Gorsky
[
8
]
investigated semi-derived Hall algebras for Frobenius categories.
Lu defined semi-derived Hall algebras for the so-called weakly 1-Gorenstein categories in
[
20
, Appendix A]
.

The
\imath
quantum group is a generalization of quantum groups, which arises from the construction of quantum symmetric pairs by Letzter
[
11
]
; see
[
10
]
for an extension to Kac-Moody type.
A striking breakthrough of the
\imath
quantum groups is the discovery of canonical basis by Bao-Wang
[
2
]
. As outlined by Bao-Wang, most of the fundamental constructions of quantum groups should admit generalizations in the setting of
\imath
quantum groups, see
[
1

2

3

for generalizations of (quasi) R-matrix and canonical bases, and also see
[
4

In recent years, Lu-Wang

20

18

have developed
\imath
Hall algebras of
\imath

\imath
quantum groups of Kac-Moody type.
Let’s briefly recall the construction of
\imath
Hall algebras. For a hereditary abelian category
\mathcal{A}
over a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, we have the Ringel-Hall algebra
\mathcal{H}(\mathcal{C}_{1}(\mathcal{A}))

\imath

Recall that the
\imath

\imath

It has been proved in

2.
Definition of
\Delta
-Hall algebras

In this paper, we take the field
\mathbf{k}=\mathbb{F}_{q}
, a finite field of
q
elements.
Let
\mathcal{A}
be an essentially small hereditary abelian category, linear over
\mathbf{k}
. Assume
\mathcal{A}
has finite morphism and extension spaces, i.e.,

|\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits_{\mathcal{A}}(M,N)|<\infty,\quad|\operatorname{Ext}\nolimits^{1}_{\mathcal{A}}(M,N)|<\infty,\,\,\forall M,N\in\mathcal{A}.

Inspired by the constructions of the Ringel-Hall algebras due to Ringel
[
22
]
and the
\imath
Hall algebras due to
[
18
,
15
]
, we will define a new kind of algebras associated to
\mathcal{A}
in this section, called
\Delta
-Hall algebras.

2.1.
Ringel-Hall algebras and Green’s formula

First we will recall the definition of Ringel-Hall algebra of
\mathcal{A}
.
Let
{\rm Iso}(\mathcal{A})
be the collection of isomorphic classes in
\mathcal{A}
.
For any
M\in\mathcal{A}
, we always use the notation
a_{M}
to denote the cardinality of the automorphism group
{\rm Aut}(M)
of
M
.

For any three objects
A,B,M\in\mathcal{A}
,
define
\operatorname{Ext}\nolimits_{\mathcal{A}}^{1}(A,B)_{M}\subseteq\operatorname{Ext}\nolimits_{\mathcal{A}}^{1}(A,B)
as the subset parameterizing extensions whose middle term is isomorphic to
M
.
The
Ringel-Hall algebra
(or
Hall algebra
for short)
\mathcal{H}(\mathcal{A})
of

\mathcal{A}
is defined to be the
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space with the basis

\big\{[M]\mid[M]\in{\rm Iso}(\mathcal{A})\big\}
,
endowed with the multiplication defined by (see
[
6
]
)

{[A]\diamond[B]=\sum_{[M]\in{\rm Iso}(\mathcal{A})}\frac{|\operatorname{Ext}\nolimits_{\mathcal{A}}^{1}(A,B)_{M}|}{|\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits_{\mathcal{A}}(A,B)|}\cdot[M].}

We remark that the Ringel-Hall algebra used here is the dual version of the original one defined in
[
22
]
.
Throughout the paper, when we sum for
[M]
, we take all the elements
[M]\in{\rm Iso}(\mathcal{A})
unless stated otherwise.

For any three objects
A,B,M\in\mathcal{A}
, the
Hall number

F^{M}_{AB}
is given by

(2.1)

\displaystyle F_{AB}^{M}:=\big|\{X\subseteq M\mid X\cong B,M/X\cong A\}\big|.

The Riedtmann-Peng formula states that

F^{M}_{AB}=\frac{|\operatorname{Ext}\nolimits_{\mathcal{A}}^{1}(A,B)_{M}|}{|\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits_{\mathcal{A}}(A,B)|}\cdot\frac{a_{M}}{a_{A}a_{B}}.

The associativity of Hall numbers tells that

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{[X]}F_{AB}^{X}F_{XC}^{M}=\sum\limits_{[Y]}F_{AY}^{M}F_{BC}^{Y},

which will be denoted by
F_{ABC}^{M}
for short.

Since
\mathcal{A}
is hereditary, the following famous
Green’s formula

[
9
, Theorem 2]
holds:

(2.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{[E]}F_{MN}^{E}F_{XY}^{E}\frac{1}{a_{E}}=\sum\limits_{[A],[B],[C],[D]}q^{-\langle A,D\rangle}F_{AB}^{M}F_{CD}^{N}F_{AC}^{X}F_{BD}^{Y}\frac{a_{A}a_{B}a_{C}a_{D}}{a_{M}a_{N}a_{X}a_{Y}},

where
\langle A,D\rangle={\rm dim\,Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(A,D)-{\rm dim\,Ext}^{1}_{\mathcal{A}}(A,D)
is the Euler form in
\mathcal{A}
.

2.2.
\Delta
-Hall algebras

In order to give a realization of
\imath
quantum groups, Lu-Wang
[
18
]
introduce the notion of
\imath
Hall algebras. More precisely,
they firstly considered the Ringel-Hall algebra
\mathcal{H}(\mathcal{C}_{1}(\mathcal{A}))
of the category
\mathcal{C}_{1}(\mathcal{A})
of 1-periodic complexes over
\mathcal{A}
, and then used the techniques of quotient and localization successively to obtain the so-called semi-derived Hall algebra
\mathcal{SDH}(\mathcal{C}_{1}(\mathcal{A}))
of
\mathcal{C}_{1}(\mathcal{A})
, and finally by using certain twisting to obtain the
\imath
Hall algebra
{}^{\imath}\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{A})
. It turns out that the central reduction of the
\imath

\imath
quantum group.

Observe that the construction of
\imath
Hall algebras
{}^{\imath}\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{A})
is complicated, and it is difficult to obtain the basis of
{}^{\imath}\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{A})
. In this paper, inspired by Lu-Wang’s multiplication formula, we hope to define a new kind of Hall algebra
\mathcal{H}_{\Delta}(\mathcal{A})
following Ringel’s framework, called the
\Delta
-Hall algebra
, in order to provide a new way to realize the
\imath

Let
{\bf v}=\sqrt{q}
.
For any three objects
A,B,M\in\mathcal{A}
, we define the
\Delta
-Hall number
as follows,

(2.3)

\widehat{F}^{M}_{AB}=\sum_{[L],[I],[N]}{\bf v}^{\langle L,I,N\rangle}\cdot\frac{a_{L}a_{I}a_{N}}{a_{M}}\cdot F_{LI}^{B}F_{NL}^{M}F_{IN}^{A},

(2.4)

\langle L,I,N\rangle=\langle L,I\rangle+\langle I,I\rangle+\langle I,N\rangle-\langle L,N\rangle.

Roughly speaking, the
\Delta
-Hall number
\widehat{F}_{AB}^{M}

Theorem 2.2
.

The
\mathbb{Q}({\bf v})
-vector space
\mathcal{H}_{\Delta}(\mathcal{A})
with the basis

\big\{[M]\mid[M]\in{\rm Iso}(\mathcal{A})\big\},

endowed with the multiplication defined by

[A]*[B]=\sum_{[M]}\widehat{F}_{AB}^{M}\cdot[M],

forms an associative algebra with the unit
[0]
, called the

\Delta
-Hall algebra
of
\mathcal{A}
.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle([A]*[B])*[C]

\displaystyle=\sum_{[X]}\widehat{F}_{AB}^{X}\cdot[X]*[C]

\displaystyle=\sum_{[X]}\widehat{F}_{AB}^{X}\cdot\sum_{[M]}\widehat{F}_{XC}^{M}\cdot[M]

\displaystyle=\sum_{[M]}\sum_{[X]}\widehat{F}_{AB}^{X}\widehat{F}_{XC}^{M}\cdot[M].

On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle[A]*([B]*[C])

\displaystyle=[A]*(\sum_{[Y]}\widehat{F}_{BC}^{Y}\cdot[Y])

\displaystyle=\sum_{[Y]}\widehat{F}_{BC}^{Y}\cdot[A]*[Y]

\displaystyle=\sum_{[M]}\sum_{[Y]}\widehat{F}_{AY}^{M}\widehat{F}_{BC}^{Y}\cdot[M].

By Proposition
2.1
, we immediately get

Clearly,

3.1.
\Delta
-Hall algebras and 1-periodic derived Hall algebras

Recall that
\mathcal{A}
is an essentially small hereditary abelian category over the finite field
\mathbf{k}=\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Let
\mathcal{D}_{1}(\mathcal{A})
be the derived category of 1-periodic complexes on
\mathcal{A}
.
For any object
M^{\bullet}=(M,d)\in\mathcal{D}_{1}(\mathcal{A})

(M,d)\cong(H(M^{\bullet}),0)

\mathcal{D}_{1}(\mathcal{A})

is the cohomology group of

\rm{Iso}(\mathcal{D}_{1}(\mathcal{A}))
of
\mathcal{D}_{1}(\mathcal{A})
coincide with the isoclasses
\rm{Iso}(\mathcal{A})
of
\mathcal{A}
. From now on, we identify
\rm{Iso}(\mathcal{D}_{1}(\mathcal{A}))
and
\rm{Iso}(\mathcal{A})
.

For any object
M\in\mathcal{A}
, we denote
|{\rm Aut}_{\mathcal{D}_{1}(\mathcal{A})}(M)|
by
\tilde{a}_{M}
. Then we have

\tilde{a}_{M}=a_{M}\cdot|\operatorname{Ext}\nolimits^{1}_{\mathcal{A}}(M,M)|.

For any objects
A,B,M\in\mathcal{A}
, following
[
27
]
we use the notations

(A,B)_{M}:=\{f\in\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits_{\mathcal{D}_{1}(\mathcal{A})}(A,B)|{\rm Cone}(f)\cong M\}

and

\{A,B\}:=\frac{1}{|\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits_{\mathcal{D}_{1}(\mathcal{A})}(A,B)|}.

Then by
[
27
, Corollary 2.7]
we have

\frac{|(B,M)_{A}|}{\tilde{a}_{B}}\sqrt{\frac{\{B,M\}}{\{B,B\}}}=\frac{|(M,A)_{B}|}{\tilde{a}_{A}}\sqrt{\frac{\{M,A\}}{\{A,A\}}}.

We denote this number by
G_{AB}^{M}
in this paper, and call it the
derived Hall number
, which satisfies the following
derived Riedtmann-Peng formula by
[
23
, Proposition 3.3]
:

\displaystyle G_{AB}^{M}=\frac{\tilde{a}_{M}\cdot|(A,B)_{M}|}{\tilde{a}_{A}\cdot\tilde{a}_{B}}\sqrt{\frac{\{A,B\}\{M,M\}}{\{A,A\}\{B,B\}}}.

The
1-periodic derived Hall algebra

\mathcal{DH}_{1}(\mathcal{A})
of
\mathcal{A}
is a
\mathbb{Q}({\bf v})
-vector space with the basis
\big\{u_{[M]}\,|\,[M]\in\rm{Iso}(\mathcal{D}_{1}(\mathcal{A})):=\rm{Iso}(\mathcal{A})\big\}
, endowed with the multiplication defined by

u_{[A]}*u_{[B]}=\sum_{[M]}G_{AB}^{M}\cdot u_{[M]}.

The following proposition indicates that the derived Hall number
G_{AB}^{M}
can be calculated inside the abelian category
\mathcal{A}
.

Proposition 3.1
.

[
7
, Proposition 3.5]

For any objects
A,B,M\in\mathcal{A}
, we have

(3.1)

G_{AB}^{M}=\sum_{[L],[I],[N]}{\bf v}^{\langle L,I\rangle+\langle I,I\rangle+\langle I,N\rangle-\langle L,N\rangle}\cdot\frac{a_{L}a_{I}a_{N}}{a_{A}a_{B}}\cdot F^{B}_{LI}F_{NL}^{M}F^{A}_{IN}.

Comparing (
3.1
) with (
2.3
), and using (
2.4
),
we can obtain an isomorphism from the 1-periodic derived Hall algebra
\mathcal{DH}_{1}(\mathcal{A})
to the
\Delta
-Hall algebra
\mathcal{H}_{\Delta}(\mathcal{A})
.

Proposition 3.2
.

There is an algebra isomorphism

\displaystyle\Xi_{\mathcal{A}}:\mathcal{DH}_{1}(\mathcal{A})

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}_{\Delta}(\mathcal{A}).

\displaystyle u_{[M]}

\displaystyle\mapsto\frac{1}{a_{M}}\cdot[M]

Proof.

Recall that we have the identity
\rm{Iso}(\mathcal{D}_{1}(\mathcal{A}))=\rm{Iso}(\mathcal{A})
. For any
A,B\in\mathcal{A}
, we have

\displaystyle\Xi_{\mathcal{A}}(u_{[A]}*u_{[B]})

\displaystyle=\Xi_{\mathcal{A}}(\sum_{[M]}G_{AB}^{M}\cdot u_{[M]})

\displaystyle=\sum_{[M]}\ \sum_{[L],[I],[N]}{\bf v}^{\langle L,I,N\rangle}\cdot\frac{a_{L}a_{I}a_{N}}{a_{A}a_{B}}\cdot F^{B}_{LI}F_{NL}^{M}F^{A}_{IN}\cdot\Xi_{\mathcal{A}}(u_{[M]})

\displaystyle=\sum_{[M]}\ \sum_{[L],[I],[N]}{\bf v}^{\langle L,I,N\rangle}\cdot\frac{a_{L}a_{I}a_{N}}{a_{A}a_{B}a_{M}}\cdot F^{B}_{LI}F_{NL}^{M}F^{A}_{IN}\cdot[M].

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\Xi_{\mathcal{A}}(u_{[A]})*\Xi_{\mathcal{A}}(u_{[B]})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{a_{A}a_{B}}[A]*[B]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{a_{A}a_{B}}\cdot\sum_{[M]}\widehat{F}_{AB}^{M}\cdot[M]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{a_{A}a_{B}}\cdot\sum_{[M]}\ \sum_{[L],[I],[N]}{\bf v}^{\langle L,I,N\rangle}\cdot\frac{a_{L}a_{I}a_{N}}{a_{M}}\cdot F^{B}_{LI}F_{NL}^{M}F^{A}_{IN}\cdot[M]

\displaystyle=\sum_{[M]}\ \sum_{[L],[I],[N]}{\bf v}^{\langle L,I,N\rangle}\cdot\frac{a_{L}a_{I}a_{N}}{a_{A}a_{B}a_{M}}\cdot F^{B}_{LI}F_{NL}^{M}F^{A}_{IN}\cdot[M].

Hence

and then

is an algebra homomorphism. Since the algebras

and

share the same basis
\rm{Iso}(\mathcal{D}_{1}(\mathcal{A}))=\rm{Iso}(\mathcal{A})

∎

3.2.

Now we will recall the basic setting on (universal) quantum groups and (universal)

Let

Let

(3.2)

\displaystyle[E_{i},F_{j}]=\delta_{ij}\frac{\widetilde{K}_{i}-\widetilde{K}_{i}^{\prime}}{v-v^{-1}},

\displaystyle\qquad[\widetilde{K}_{i},\widetilde{K}_{j}]=[\widetilde{K}_{i},\widetilde{K}_{j}^{\prime}]=[\widetilde{K}_{i}^{\prime},\widetilde{K}_{j}^{\prime}]=0,

\displaystyle\qquad\widetilde{K}_{i}F_{j}=v^{-c_{ij}}F_{j}\widetilde{K}_{i},

[A,B]=AB-BA

\mathbb{Q}(v)
-subalgebra of

generated by central elements

, for

.

Analogous as for

{\mathbf{U}}:={\mathbf{U}}_{v}(\mathfrak{g})

-algebra generated by

\imath

-Hall algebra approach, we consider the nilpotent representation category

In this section, we define the extended

Similarly, the
\Delta
-Hall algebras also admit extended versions, which can be used to realize the universal
\imath

For technical reason we assume

is a free abelian group. Both the category of finite-dimensional representations of a quiver, and the category of coherent sheaves over a weighted projective curve, satisfy this assumption.

For any
M\in\mathcal{A}
, the image of
M
in

is denoted by
\widehat{M}
.
The symmetric Euler form in
\mathcal{A}
(or in
K_{0}(\mathcal{A})

(M,N)=\langle M,N\rangle+\langle N,M\rangle
for any
M,N\in\mathcal{A}
.
We have the following observation.

Lemma 4.1
.

For any three-cycle in
\mathcal{A}
as follows:

(4.1)

N
M
L
,
A
I
B

we have

(i)

2\widehat{L}=\widehat{M}+\widehat{B}-\widehat{A}
;

(ii)

2\widehat{I}=\widehat{A}+\widehat{B}-\widehat{M}
;

(iii)

2\widehat{N}=\widehat{M}+\widehat{A}-\widehat{B}
.

Consequently,

(4.2)

2(N,L)=\langle M,M\rangle-\langle A,A\rangle-\langle B,B\rangle+(A,B).

Proof.

The three-cycle (
4.1
) indicates that we have the following exact sequence:

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{N\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{B\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{M\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{N\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0.}

Hence,

, which proves (iii). Similarly, we can obtain (i) and (ii).

Observe that

\displaystyle 4(N,L)=

\displaystyle(2\widehat{N},2\widehat{L})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\widehat{M}+\widehat{A}-\widehat{B},\widehat{M}+\widehat{B}-\widehat{A})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\widehat{M},\widehat{M})-(\widehat{A}-\widehat{B},\widehat{A}-\widehat{B})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\widehat{M},\widehat{M})-(\widehat{A},\widehat{A})-(\widehat{B},\widehat{B})+2(\widehat{A},\widehat{B}).

By assumption,

is a free abelian group. Then (

) follows immediately.

Denote by
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\Delta}(\mathcal{A})
the
\mathbb{Q}({\bf v})
-vector space with the basis

.

Recall that

\operatorname{res}\nolimits:\mathcal{C}_{1}(\mathcal{A})\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
by forgetting differentials. That is,
{}^{\imath}\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{A})
is the
\mathbb{Q}({\bf v})
-algebra on the same vector space as
{\mathcal{S}}\mathcal{D}\mathcal{H}(\mathcal{C}_{1}(\mathcal{A}))
, equipped with the following modified multiplication

(4.3)

\displaystyle[M^{\bullet}]*[N^{\bullet}]={\bf v}^{\langle\operatorname{res}\nolimits(M^{\bullet}),\operatorname{res}\nolimits(N^{\bullet})\rangle}[M^{\bullet}]\diamond[N^{\bullet}];\quad\forall M^{\bullet},N^{\bullet}\in\mathcal{C}_{1}(\mathcal{A}).

By Theorem 4.5 and Proposition 4.10 in
[
20
]
, the
\imath
Hall algebra
{}^{\imath}\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{A})
has a basis

\big\{[M]*[K_{\alpha}]\mid[M]\in{\rm Iso}(\mathcal{A}),{\alpha\in K_{0}(\mathcal{A})}\big\},

and the elements
[K_{\alpha}]\;(\alpha\in K_{0}(\mathcal{A}))
are central in
{}^{\imath}\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{A})
. In order to give an explicit description of the multiplication formula for the basis elements in
{}^{\imath}\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{A})
, we introduce the following notation
for any
[A],[B]\in{\rm Iso}(\mathcal{A})
:

(4.4)

\displaystyle\widetilde{F}_{AB}^{M}=\sum\limits_{[L],[I],[N]}{\bf v}^{-\langle A,B\rangle}q^{\langle N,L\rangle}\cdot\frac{a_{L}a_{I}a_{N}}{a_{M}}\cdot F_{LI}^{B}F_{NL}^{M}F_{IN}^{A}.

Lemma 4.3
.

For any
A,B,M\in\mathcal{A}
,

\widetilde{F}_{AB}^{M}={\bf v}^{\langle M,M\rangle-\langle A,A\rangle-\langle B,B\rangle}\cdot\widehat{F}_{AB}^{M}.

Proof.

Recall from (
2.3
) that

(4.5)

\widehat{F}^{M}_{AB}=\sum_{[L],[I],[N]}{\bf v}^{\langle L,I,N\rangle}\cdot\frac{a_{L}a_{I}a_{N}}{a_{M}}\cdot F_{LI}^{B}F_{NL}^{M}F_{IN}^{A}.

For
F_{LI}^{B}F_{NL}^{M}F_{IN}^{A}\neq 0
, we have
\widehat{A}=\widehat{I}+\widehat{N}
and
\widehat{B}=\widehat{L}+\widehat{I}
, which implies

\langle B,A\rangle=\langle L,I\rangle+\langle I,I\rangle+\langle I,N\rangle+\langle L,N\rangle.

It follows from (
2.4
) that

\langle B,A\rangle-2\langle L,N\rangle=\langle L,I,N\rangle.

Now according to (
4.2
), we obtain

\displaystyle\quad{-\langle A,B\rangle}{+2\langle N,L\rangle}

\displaystyle=\langle M,M\rangle-\langle A,A\rangle-\langle B,B\rangle+\langle B,A\rangle{-2\langle L,N\rangle}

\displaystyle=\langle M,M\rangle{-\langle A,A\rangle-\langle B,B\rangle+\langle L,I,N\rangle}.

Then by comparing with (
4.4
) and (
4.5
), the result follows immediately.
∎

Proposition 4.4
.

For any
A,B\in\mathcal{A}
and
\alpha,\beta\in K_{0}(\mathcal{A})
, the following equation holds in
{}^{\imath}\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{A})
:

([A]*[K_{\alpha}])*([B]*[K_{\beta}])=\sum\limits_{[M]}\widetilde{F}_{AB}^{M}\cdot[M]*[K_{\frac{\widehat{A}+\widehat{B}-\widehat{M}}{2}+\alpha+\beta}].

Proof.

By
[
7
, Proposition 3.5]
, in
{}^{\imath}\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{A})
we have

[A]*[B]=\sum_{[M]}\sum_{[L],[I],[N]}{\bf v}^{-\langle A,B\rangle}q^{\langle N,L\rangle}\cdot\frac{a_{L}a_{I}a_{N}}{a_{M}}\cdot F_{LI}^{B}F_{NL}^{M}F_{IN}^{A}\cdot[M]*[K_{\widehat{A}-\widehat{N}}].

In case
F_{LI}^{B}F_{NL}^{M}F_{IN}^{A}\neq 0
, there is a three-cycle (
4.1
). Then by Lemma
4.1
, we obtain
\widehat{A}-\widehat{N}=\dfrac{\widehat{A}+\widehat{B}-\widehat{M}}{2}
, which is independent of
[L],[I],[N]
. Hence in
{}^{\imath}\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{A})
we have

[A]*[B]=\sum\limits_{[M]}\widetilde{F}_{AB}^{M}\cdot[M]*[K_{\frac{\widehat{A}+\widehat{B}-\widehat{M}}{2}}].

Recall that
[K_{\alpha}]\;(\alpha\in K_{0}(\mathcal{A}))
are central in
{}^{\imath}\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{A})
.
Then we are done.
∎

Now we can state the main result of this subsection.

Proposition 4.5
.

There is an algebra isomorphism

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Xi}_{\mathcal{A}}:{}^{\imath}\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{A})

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\Delta}(\mathcal{A}).

\displaystyle[M]*[K_{\alpha}]

\displaystyle\mapsto{\bf v}^{-\langle M,M\rangle}[M][K_{\alpha}]

Proof.

For any
A,B\in\mathcal{A}
and
\alpha,\beta\in K_{0}(\mathcal{A})
, by Proposition
4.4
we have

\displaystyle\quad\widetilde{\Xi}_{\mathcal{A}}\big(([A]*[K_{\alpha}])*([B]*[K_{\beta}])\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{[M]}\ {\widetilde{F}}_{AB}^{M}\cdot\widetilde{\Xi}_{\mathcal{A}}([M]*[K_{\frac{\widehat{A}+\widehat{B}-\widehat{M}}{2}+\alpha+\beta}])

\displaystyle=\sum_{[M]}\ {\bf v}^{-\langle M,M\rangle}{\widetilde{F}}_{AB}^{M}\cdot[M][K_{\frac{\widehat{A}+\widehat{B}-\widehat{M}}{2}+\alpha+\beta}].

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\quad\widetilde{\Xi}_{\mathcal{A}}([A]*[K_{\alpha}])*\widetilde{\Xi}_{\mathcal{A}}([B]*[K_{\beta}])

\displaystyle=({\bf v}^{-\langle A,A\rangle}[A][K_{\alpha}])*({\bf v}^{-\langle B,B\rangle}[B][K_{\beta}])

\displaystyle={\bf v}^{-\langle A,A\rangle-\langle B,B\rangle}{\sum_{[M]}\widehat{F}_{AB}^{M}\cdot}[M][K_{\frac{\widehat{A}+\widehat{B}-\widehat{M}}{2}+\alpha+\beta}].

It follows from Lemma
4.3
that

\widetilde{\Xi}_{\mathcal{A}}([A]*[K_{\alpha}])*\widetilde{\Xi}_{\mathcal{A}}([B]*[K_{\beta}])=\widetilde{\Xi}_{\mathcal{A}}\big(([A]*[K_{\alpha}])*([B]*[K_{\beta}])\big).

Hence
\widetilde{\Xi}_{\mathcal{A}}
is an algebra homomorphism. By comparing the basis of
{}^{\imath}\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{A})
and of
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\Delta}(\mathcal{A})
, we know that
\widetilde{\Xi}_{\mathcal{A}}
is bijective, so it is an algebra isomorphism.
∎

As a consequence of Propositions
4.5
and
3.2
, we obtain

Proposition 4.6
.

There exists an algebra epimorphism

\widetilde{\Phi}:\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\Delta}(\mathcal{A})\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}_{\Delta}(\mathcal{A}),

[M][K_{\alpha}]\longmapsto[M]

with
{\rm Ker}\ \widetilde{\Phi}=\langle[K_{\alpha}]-1,\alpha\in K_{0}(\mathcal{A})\rangle
.

Proof.

According to
[
7
, Theorem 3.4]
, there exists an algebra epimorphism

\Phi:{}^{\imath}\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{A})\longrightarrow\mathcal{DH}_{1}(\mathcal{A});\quad[M]*[K_{\alpha}]\mapsto\sqrt{\{M,M\}}\cdot\tilde{a}_{M}\cdot u_{[M]},

with
{\rm Ker}\ \Phi=\langle[K_{\alpha}]-1,\alpha\in K_{0}(\mathcal{A})\rangle
.
By Propositions
4.5
and
3.2
, there are isomorphisms

\widetilde{\Xi}_{\mathcal{A}}:{}^{\imath}\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{A})\to\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\Delta}(\mathcal{A})\text{\quad and\quad}\Xi_{\mathcal{A}}:\mathcal{DH}_{1}(\mathcal{A})\to\mathcal{H}_{\Delta}(\mathcal{A}).

Let
\widetilde{\Phi}
be the composition

\widetilde{\Phi}=\Xi_{\mathcal{A}}\circ\Phi\circ\widetilde{\Xi}_{\mathcal{A}}^{-1}:\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\Delta}(\mathcal{A})\longrightarrow{}^{\imath}\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{A})\longrightarrow\mathcal{DH}_{1}(\mathcal{A})\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{\Delta}(\mathcal{A}).

Therefore,
\widetilde{\Phi}([M][K_{\alpha}])=[M]
for any
M\in\mathcal{A}
and
\alpha\in K_{0}(\mathcal{A})
. In particular, we have the following commutative diagram:

{\rm Ker}\ {\Phi}
{\rm Ker}\ \widetilde{\Phi}
{}^{\imath}\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{A})
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\Delta}(\mathcal{A})
\mathcal{D}\mathcal{H}_{1}(\mathcal{A})
\mathcal{H}_{\Delta}(\mathcal{A})
.
\cong
\cong
\cong
\widetilde{\Xi}_{\mathcal{A}}
{\Xi}_{\mathcal{A}}
\widetilde{\Phi}
{\Phi}

It follows that
{\rm Ker}\ \widetilde{\Phi}={\widetilde{\Xi}}_{\mathcal{A}}({\rm Ker}\ {\Phi})=\langle[K_{\alpha}]-1,\alpha\in K_{0}(\mathcal{A})\rangle
. We are done.
∎

4.3.
New realization of universal
\imath
quantum groups

Let
Q
be an arbitrary quiver without loops. The simple
\mathbf{k}Q
-modules are denoted by
S_{i}
for
i\in\mathbb{I}=Q_{0}
. We consider the hereditary abelian category
\mathcal{A}={\rm rep^{nil}}(\mathbf{k}Q)
.
Denote by
{}^{\imath}\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}(\mathbf{k}Q):={}^{\imath}\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{A})
,
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\Delta}(\mathbf{k}Q):=\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\Delta}(\mathcal{A})
and
\widetilde{\Xi}_{Q}:=\widetilde{\Xi}_{\mathcal{A}}
. The following result states that we can realize the universal
\imath
quantum groups via extended
\Delta
-Hall algebras.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
Q
be an arbitrary quiver without loops.
There exists a
\mathbb{Q}({\bf v})
-algebra embedding

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Theta}_{Q}:\widetilde{{\mathbf{U}}}^{\imath}_{|v={\bf v}}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\Delta}(\mathbf{k}Q),

which sends

\displaystyle B_{i}\mapsto\frac{-1}{q-1}\cdot{\bf v}^{-\langle S_{i},S_{i}\rangle}\cdot[S_{i}],

\displaystyle\qquad\Bbbk_{i}\mapsto-q^{-1}[K_{i}],\qquad\text{ for }i\in\mathbb{I}.

In particular, if
Q
is a Dynkin quiver, the embedding
\widetilde{\Theta}_{Q}
is precisely an isomorphism.

Proof.

Recall from
[
20
,
18
,
17
]
that the
\imath
Hall algebra
{}^{\imath}\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}(\mathbf{k}Q)
of
{\rm rep^{nil}}(\mathbf{k}Q)
provides a realization of the universal
\imath
quantum group
\widetilde{{\mathbf{U}}}^{\imath}
associated to
Q
. Namely, there is an embedding

\widetilde{\psi}_{Q}:\widetilde{{\mathbf{U}}}^{\imath}_{|v={\bf v}}\longrightarrow{}^{\imath}\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}(\mathbf{k}Q),

which sends

\displaystyle B_{i}\mapsto\frac{-1}{q-1}[S_{i}],

\displaystyle\qquad\Bbbk_{i}\mapsto-q^{-1}[K_{i}],\qquad\text{ for }i\in\mathbb{I}.

In particular, if
Q
is a Dynkin quiver, the embedding
\widetilde{\psi}_{Q}
is precisely an isomorphism.

On the other hand, by Proposition
4.5
, there exists an algebra isomorphism

\widetilde{\Xi}_{Q}:{}^{\imath}\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}(\mathbf{k}Q)\rightarrow\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\Delta}(\mathbf{k}Q),

which sends

[M]*[K_{\alpha}]\mapsto{\bf v}^{-\langle M,M\rangle}[M][K_{\alpha}];\quad\forall M\in\mathcal{A},\;\alpha\in K_{0}(\mathcal{A}).

Set
\widetilde{\Theta}_{Q}=\widetilde{\Xi}_{Q}\circ\widetilde{\psi}_{Q}
, then we are done.
∎

5.
Twisting on extended
\Delta
-Hall algebras

In this section, we consider the twisting on the extended
\Delta
-Hall algebra
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\Delta}(\mathcal{A})
, in order to recover the semi-derived Hall algebra
\mathcal{SDH}(\mathcal{C}_{1}(\mathcal{A}))
of
\mathcal{C}_{1}(\mathcal{A})
. Moreover, by extending
K_{0}(\mathcal{A})
to
K_{0}(\mathcal{A})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\dfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
, we establish an algebra isomorphism between the extended version of the
\Delta
-Hall algebra and the derived Hall algebra of
\mathcal{A}
.

5.1.
Twisted extended
\Delta
-Hall algebras and semi-derived Hall algebras

Let
\varphi:K_{0}(\mathcal{A})\times K_{0}(\mathcal{A})\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}({\bf v})
be a multiplicative bilinear form on
K_{0}(\mathcal{A})
in the following sense:

\varphi(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2},\beta)=\varphi(\alpha_{1},\beta)\cdot\varphi(\alpha_{1},\beta)\quad{\text{and}\quad}\varphi(\alpha,\beta_{1}+\beta_{2})=\varphi(\alpha,\beta_{1})\cdot\varphi(\alpha,\beta_{2})

for
\alpha,\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\beta,\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\in K_{0}(\mathcal{A})
.

Proposition 5.1
.

For any multiplicative bilinear form
\varphi:K_{0}(\mathcal{A})\times K_{0}(\mathcal{A})\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}({\bf v})
, the following multiplication

[A][K_{\alpha}]\divideontimes[B][K_{\beta}]=\varphi(\widehat{A}+2\alpha,\widehat{B}+2\beta)\cdot[A][K_{\alpha}]*[B][K_{\beta}]

defines an associative algebra structure on
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\Delta}(\mathcal{A})
, called the
twisted extended
\Delta
-Hall algebra
of
\mathcal{A}
via
\varphi
, and denoted by

{}_{\varphi}\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\Delta}(\mathcal{A})
.

Proof.

For any
[A],[B],[C]\in{\rm Iso}(\mathcal{A})
and
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in K_{0}(\mathcal{A})
, we have

\displaystyle\qquad\big([A][K_{\alpha}]\divideontimes[B][K_{\beta}]\big)\divideontimes[C][K_{\gamma}]

\displaystyle=\varphi(\widehat{A}+2\alpha,\widehat{B}+2\beta)\cdot\big([A][K_{\alpha}]*[B][K_{\beta}]\big)\divideontimes[C][K_{\gamma}]

\displaystyle=\varphi(\widehat{A}+2\alpha,\widehat{B}+2\beta)\varphi(\widehat{A}+2\alpha+\widehat{B}+2\beta,\widehat{C}+2\gamma)\cdot\big([A][K_{\alpha}]*[B][K_{\beta}]\big)*[C][K_{\gamma}]

\displaystyle=\varphi(\widehat{A}+2\alpha,\widehat{B}+2\beta)\varphi(\widehat{A}+2\alpha,\widehat{C}+2\gamma)\varphi(\widehat{B}+2\beta,\widehat{C}+2\gamma)\cdot\big([A][K_{\alpha}]*[B][K_{\beta}]\big)*[C][K_{\gamma}].

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\qquad[A][K_{\alpha}]\divideontimes\big([B][K_{\beta}]\divideontimes[C][K_{\gamma}]\big)

\displaystyle=\varphi(\widehat{B}+2\beta,\widehat{C}+2\gamma)\cdot[A][K_{\alpha}]\divideontimes\big([B][K_{\beta}]*[C][K_{\gamma}]\big)

\displaystyle=\varphi(\widehat{B}+2\beta,\widehat{C}+2\gamma)\varphi(\widehat{A}+2\alpha,\widehat{B}+2\beta+\widehat{C}+2\gamma)\cdot[A][K_{\alpha}]*\big([B][K_{\beta}]*[C][K_{\gamma}]\big)

\displaystyle=\varphi(\widehat{A}+2\alpha,\widehat{B}+2\beta)\varphi(\widehat{A}+2\alpha,\widehat{C}+2\gamma)\varphi(\widehat{B}+2\beta,\widehat{C}+2\gamma)\cdot[A][K_{\alpha}]*\big([B][K_{\beta}]*[C][K_{\gamma}])\big.

Hence, by the associativity of
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\Delta}(\mathcal{A})
(see Proposition
4.2
) we obtain

\big([A][K_{\alpha}]\divideontimes[B][K_{\beta}]\big)\divideontimes[C][K_{\gamma}]=[A][K_{\alpha}]\divideontimes\big([B][K_{\beta}]\divideontimes[C][K_{\gamma}]\big).

Then we are done.
∎

As an application, we find that the semi-derived Hall algebra
\mathcal{SDH}(\mathcal{C}_{1}(\mathcal{A}))
of
\mathcal{C}_{1}(\mathcal{A})
,
can be obtained from the extended
\Delta
-Hall algebra by suitable twisting.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
\varphi:K_{0}(\mathcal{A})\times K_{0}(\mathcal{A})\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}({\bf v})
be a multiplicative bilinear form given by
\varphi(\alpha,\beta)={\bf v}^{-\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle}
. Then there is an algebra isomorphism:

{}_{\varphi}\widetilde{\Xi}_{\mathcal{A}}:\mathcal{SDH}(\mathcal{C}_{1}(\mathcal{A}))

\displaystyle\longrightarrow{}_{\varphi}\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\Delta}(\mathcal{A}).

\displaystyle[M]\diamond[K_{\alpha}]

\displaystyle\mapsto{\bf v}^{-\langle M,M\rangle}[M][K_{\alpha}]

Proof.

Recall that the basis of
\mathcal{SDH}(\mathcal{C}_{1}(\mathcal{A}))
is given by

\big\{[M]\diamond[K_{\alpha}]\mid[M]\in{\rm Iso}(\mathcal{A}),{\alpha\in K_{0}(\mathcal{A})}\big\},

and according to (
4.3
) and Proposition
4.4
, its multiplication is given by

([A]\diamond[K_{\alpha}])\diamond([B]\diamond[K_{\beta}])={\bf v}^{-\langle\widehat{A}+2\alpha,\widehat{B}+2\beta\rangle}\sum_{[M]}\widetilde{F}_{AB}^{M}\cdot[M]\diamond[K_{\frac{\widehat{A}+\widehat{B}-\widehat{M}}{2}+\alpha+\beta}].

On the other hand, the basis of
{}_{\varphi}\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\Delta}(\mathcal{A})
is given by

\big\{[M][K_{\alpha}]\mid[M]\in{\rm Iso}(\mathcal{A}),{\alpha\in K_{0}(\mathcal{A})}\big\},

and according to Proposition
5.1
, its multiplication is given by

[A][K_{\alpha}]\divideontimes[B][K_{\beta}]={\bf v}^{-\langle\widehat{A}+2\alpha,\widehat{B}+2\beta\rangle}\sum_{[M]}\widehat{F}_{AB}^{M}\cdot[M][K_{\frac{\widehat{A}+\widehat{B}-\widehat{M}}{2}+\alpha+\beta}].

Therefore, by Lemma
4.3
,

\quad{}_{\varphi}\widetilde{\Xi}_{\mathcal{A}}\big(([A]\diamond[K_{\alpha}])\diamond([B]\diamond[K_{\beta}])\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\bf v}^{-\langle\widehat{A}+2\alpha,\widehat{B}+2\beta\rangle-\langle M,M\rangle}\sum_{[M]}\widetilde{F}_{AB}^{M}\cdot[M][K_{\frac{\widehat{A}+\widehat{B}-\widehat{M}}{2}+\alpha+\beta}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\bf v}^{-\langle\widehat{A}+2\alpha,\widehat{B}+2\beta\rangle-\langle A,A\rangle-\langle B,B\rangle}\sum_{[M]}\widehat{F}_{AB}^{M}\cdot[M][K_{\frac{\widehat{A}+\widehat{B}-\widehat{M}}{2}+\alpha+\beta}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\bf v}^{-\langle A,A\rangle-\langle B,B\rangle}([A][K_{\alpha}]\divideontimes[B][K_{\beta}])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{}_{\varphi}\widetilde{\Xi}_{\mathcal{A}}([A]\diamond[K_{\alpha}])\divideontimes{}_{\varphi}\widetilde{\Xi}_{\mathcal{A}}([B]\diamond[K_{\beta}]).

Hence,
{}_{\varphi}\widetilde{\Xi}_{\mathcal{A}}
is an algebra homomorphism. Obviously, it is an isomorphism of
\mathbb{Q}({\bf v})
-vector spaces. Then we are done.
∎

5.2.
Extended
\Delta
-Hall algebras and extended derived Hall algebras

In this subsection, we consider a new kind of extension of the
\Delta
-Hall algebra
{\mathcal{H}}_{\Delta}(\mathcal{A})
, with the aim to establish an
algebraic isomorphism
with certain extended version of the derived Hall algebra
\mathcal{DH}_{1}(\mathcal{A})
.

Recall that the Grothendieck group
K_{0}(\mathcal{A})
is free. Consider the
\mathbb{Z}
-module

\dfrac{1}{2}K_{0}(\mathcal{A}):=K_{0}(\mathcal{A})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\dfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}=\{\frac{\beta}{2}\mid\beta\in K_{0}(\mathcal{A})\}.

Clearly,
K_{0}(\mathcal{A})
is a subgroup of
\dfrac{1}{2}K_{0}(\mathcal{A})
.

Denote by
\widetilde{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}_{\Delta}(\mathcal{A})
the
\mathbb{Q}({\bf v})
-vector space with the basis

\big\{[M][K_{\alpha}]\mid[M]\in{\rm Iso}(\mathcal{A}),{\alpha\in\dfrac{1}{2}K_{0}(\mathcal{A})}\big\}.

Similar as Proposition
4.2
, the following result holds.

Proposition 5.3
.

The
\mathbb{Q}({\bf v})
-vector space
\widetilde{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}_{\Delta}(\mathcal{A})
endowed with the multiplication defined by

[A][K_{\alpha}]*[B][K_{\beta}]=\sum_{[M]}\widehat{F}_{AB}^{M}\cdot[M][K_{\frac{\widehat{A}+\widehat{B}-\widehat{M}}{2}+\alpha+\beta}],

forms an associative algebra with unit
[0][K_{0}]
.

Consider the tensor product space
\mathcal{H}_{\Delta}(\mathcal{A})\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{Q}{(\dfrac{1}{2}K_{0}(\mathcal{A}))}
, equipped with the standard multiplication, i.e.,

(x\otimes u)\ast(y\otimes w)=(x\ast y)\otimes(u\ast w)
. It turns out that the tensor product algebra is isomorphic to the extended
\Delta
-Hall algebra
\widetilde{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}_{\Delta}(\mathcal{A})
.

Proposition 5.4
.

There is an algebra isomorphism

\displaystyle\Phi_{\otimes}:\widetilde{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}_{\Delta}(\mathcal{A})

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}_{\Delta}(\mathcal{A})\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{Q}(\dfrac{1}{2}K_{0}(\mathcal{A})).

\displaystyle[M][K_{\alpha}]

\displaystyle\mapsto[M]\otimes[K_{\frac{\widehat{M}}{2}+\alpha}]

Proof.

For any
[A],[B]\in{\rm Iso}(\mathcal{A})
and
\alpha,\beta\in\dfrac{1}{2}K_{0}(\mathcal{A})
, we have

\displaystyle\quad\Phi_{\otimes}([A][K_{\alpha}]*[B][K_{\beta}])

\displaystyle=\Phi_{\otimes}\big(\sum_{[M]}\widehat{F}_{AB}^{M}\cdot[M][K_{\frac{\widehat{A}+\widehat{B}-\widehat{M}}{2}+\alpha+\beta}]\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{[M]}\widehat{F}_{AB}^{M}\cdot[M]\otimes[K_{\frac{\widehat{M}}{2}+(\frac{\widehat{A}+\widehat{B}-\widehat{M}}{2}+\alpha+\beta)}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{[M]}\widehat{F}_{AB}^{M}\cdot[M]\otimes[K_{\frac{\widehat{A}+\widehat{B}}{2}+\alpha+\beta}],

and

\displaystyle\quad\Phi_{\otimes}([A][K_{\alpha}])*\Phi_{\otimes}([B][K_{\beta}])

\displaystyle=([A]\otimes[K_{\frac{\widehat{A}}{2}+\alpha}])*([B]\otimes[K_{\frac{\widehat{B}}{2}+\beta}])

\displaystyle=\sum_{[M]}\widehat{F}_{AB}^{M}\cdot[M]\otimes[K_{\frac{\widehat{A}+\widehat{B}}{2}+\alpha+\beta}].

It follows that
\Phi_{\otimes}
is an algebra homomorphism.

Note that
{\mathcal{H}_{\Delta}}(\mathcal{A})\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{Q}(\dfrac{1}{2}K_{0}(\mathcal{A}))
is a
\mathbb{Q}({\bf v})
-vector space with the basis

\big\{[M]\otimes[K_{\alpha}]\mid[M]\in{\rm Iso}(\mathcal{A}),\alpha\in\dfrac{1}{2}K_{0}(\mathcal{A})\big\}.

For each
[M]\otimes[K_{\alpha}]\in\mathcal{H}_{\Delta}(\mathcal{A})\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{Q}(\dfrac{1}{2}K_{0}(\mathcal{A}))
, there is an element
[M][K_{\alpha-\frac{\widehat{M}}{2}}]\in\widetilde{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}_{\Delta}(\mathcal{A})
, such that
\Phi_{\otimes}([M][K_{\alpha-\frac{\widehat{M}}{2}}])=[M]\otimes[K_{\alpha}]
. Hence
\Phi_{\otimes}
is surjective. Clearly,
\Phi_{\otimes}
is also injective. Therefore,
\Phi_{\otimes}
is an algebra isomorphism.
∎

As an immediately consequence of Propositions
3.2
and
5.4
, we obtain

Corollary 5.5
.

There is an algebra isomorphism

\displaystyle{\Psi}_{\otimes}:\widetilde{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}_{\Delta}(\mathcal{A})

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathcal{DH}_{1}(\mathcal{A})\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{Q}(\dfrac{1}{2}K_{0}(\mathcal{A})).

\displaystyle[M][K_{\alpha}]

\displaystyle\mapsto a_{M}\cdot u_{[M]}\otimes[K_{\frac{\widehat{M}}{2}+\alpha}]

Proof.

Recall from Propositions
3.2
and
5.4
that there are isomorphisms
\Xi_{\mathcal{A}}
and
\Phi_{\otimes}
respectively. Consider the composition
{\Psi}_{\otimes}:=(\Xi_{\mathcal{A}}^{-1}\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ\Phi_{\otimes}
as follows:

\widetilde{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}_{\Delta}(\mathcal{A})\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}_{\Delta}(\mathcal{A})\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{Q}(\dfrac{1}{2}K_{0}(\mathcal{A}))\longrightarrow\mathcal{DH}_{1}(\mathcal{A})\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{Q}(\dfrac{1}{2}K_{0}(\mathcal{A})).

Then we are done.
∎

Remark 5.6
.

(1)
By definition,
{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}_{\Delta}(\mathcal{A})
is a natural subalgebra of
\widetilde{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}_{\Delta}(\mathcal{A})
. Therefore, under the isomorphisms
\widetilde{\Xi}_{\mathcal{A}}:{}^{\imath}\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{A})\longrightarrow\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\Delta}(\mathcal{A})
and
{\Psi}_{\otimes}
, the
\imath
Hall algebra
{}^{\imath}\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{A})
is a subalgebra of
\mathcal{DH}_{1}(\mathcal{A})\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{Q}(\dfrac{1}{2}K_{0}(\mathcal{A}))
.

(2)
The inverse of
{\Psi}_{\otimes}
is given by:

\displaystyle{\Psi}_{\otimes}^{-1}:\;

\displaystyle\mathcal{DH}_{1}(\mathcal{A})\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{Q}(\dfrac{1}{2}K_{0}(\mathcal{A}))\longrightarrow\widetilde{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}_{\Delta}(\mathcal{A}).

\displaystyle u_{[M]}\otimes[K_{\alpha}]\mapsto\frac{1}{a_{M}}\cdot[M][K_{-\frac{\widehat{M}}{2}+\alpha}]

Define the degree function on
\widetilde{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}_{\Delta}(\mathcal{A})
by

\deg([M][K_{\alpha}])=\widehat{M}+2\alpha;\quad\forall M\in\mathcal{A},\;\alpha\in K_{0}(\mathcal{A}).

Then the image of
\mathcal{DH}_{1}(\mathcal{A})\otimes 1
under
{\Psi}_{\otimes}^{-1}
has the expression

\mathbb{Q}({\bf v})\{[M][K_{-\frac{\widehat{M}}{2}}]\mid[M]\in{\rm Iso}(\mathcal{A})\},

which is actually the subspace of
\widetilde{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}_{\Delta}(\mathcal{A})
with degree
0
.

Therefore, the 1-periodic derived Hall algebra
\mathcal{DH}_{1}(\mathcal{A})
can be embedded into

\widetilde{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}_{\Delta}(\mathcal{A})
, which is an extended version of
{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}_{\Delta}(\mathcal{A})\cong{}^{\imath}\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{A})
, hence embedded into an extended version of the twisted semi-derived Hall algebra.
In general, for
t
-periodic cases with
t\geq 1
, the embeddings from the derived Hall algebras to certain extended version of twisted semi-derived Hall algebras also hold, see Lin-Peng
[
13
, Theorem 5.6]
.

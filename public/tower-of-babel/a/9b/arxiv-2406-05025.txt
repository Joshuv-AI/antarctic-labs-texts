Unraveling Trace Anomaly of Supradense Matter via Neutron Star Compactness Scaling
Bao-Jun Cai, Bao-An Li

Abstract
The trace anomaly Δ ≡ 1 / 3 − P / ε = 1 / 3 − ϕ \Delta\equiv 1/3-P/\varepsilon=1/3-\phi quantifies the possibly broken conformal symmetry in supradense matter under pressure P P at energy density ε \varepsilon . Perturbative QCD (pQCD) predicts a vanishing Δ \Delta at extremely high energy or baryon densities when the conformal symmetry is realized but its behavior at intermediate densities reachable in neutron stars (NSs) are still very uncertain. The extraction of Δ \Delta from NS observations strongly depends on the employed model for nuclear Equation of State (EOS). Using the IPAD-TOV method based on an Intrinsic and Perturbatively Analysis of the Dimensionless (IPAD) Tolman-Oppenheimer-Volkoff (TOV) equations that are further verified numerically by using 10 5 10^{5} EOSs generated randomly with a meta-model in a very broad EOS parameter space constrained by terrestrial nuclear experiments and astrophysical observations, here we first show that the compactness ξ ≡ G ​ M NS / R ​ c 2 ≡ M NS / R \xi\equiv GM_{\textrm{NS}}/Rc^{2}\equiv M_{\textrm{NS}}/R of a NS with mass M NS M_{\textrm{NS}} and radius R R scales very accurately with Π ¯ c ≡ Π c ⋅ ( 1 + 18 ​ X / 25 ) ≡ X / ( 1 + 3 ​ X 2 + 4 ​ X ) ⋅ ( 1 + 18 ​ X / 25 ) \overline{\Pi}_{\textrm{c}}\equiv\Pi_{\textrm{c}}\cdot(1+18\mathrm{X}/25)\equiv\mathrm{X}/(1+3\mathrm{X}^{2}+4\mathrm{X})\cdot(1+18\textrm{X}/25) where X ≡ ϕ c = P c / ε c \mathrm{X}\equiv\phi_{\textrm{c}}=P_{\textrm{c}}/\varepsilon_{\textrm{c}} is the ratio of pressure over energy density at NS centers. The scaling of NS compactness thus enables one to readily read off the central trace anomaly Δ c = 1 / 3 − X \Delta_{\textrm{c}}=1/3-\mathrm{X} directly from the observational data of either the mass-radius or red-shift measurements. We then demonstrate indeed that the available NS data themselves from recent X-ray and gravitational wave observations can determine model-insensitively the trace anomaly as a function of energy density in NS cores, providing a stringent test of existing NS models and a clear guidance in a new direction for further understanding the nature and EOS of supradense matter.

III.  Extracting NS Observational Constraints on Central Trace Anomalies Insensitive of EOS Models

The strong linear correlation between
\xi
and
\overline{\Pi}_{\textrm{c}}
of Eq. (
3
) enables us to read off the
\mathrm{X}
straightforwardly from the
\xi
obtained either using NS mass-radius observation or the red-shift measurement; and therefore the central trace anomaly
\Delta_{\textrm{c}}=1/3-\mathrm{X}
.
Since the ratio
\phi=P/\varepsilon
is an increasing function of
\varepsilon
near NS centers,
\phi
reaches its local maximum value
\mathrm{X}
there, where the trace anomaly takes its local minimum
\Delta_{\textrm{c}}
. This means
\phi\lesssim\mathrm{X}
near the NS centers. We show it generally in our approach by anatomizing the structures of the TOV equations
[
57
]
:

\phi\approx\mathrm{X}-\frac{1}{6}\frac{1+\Psi}{4+\Psi}\left(1+\frac{7+\Psi}{4+\Psi}\cdot 4\mathrm{X}\right)\widehat{r}^{2}\to\mathrm{X}-\frac{1+7\mathrm{X}}{24}\widehat{r}^{2},

(5)

where
\widehat{r}
is the dimensionless radial distance from the center; the second relation follows for TOV NSs (
\Psi=0
).
A lower bound for trace anomaly
\Delta\gtrsim\Delta_{\textrm{GR}}\approx-0.048
from the GR limit
\mathrm{X}\lesssim 0.381
is equivalent to
\xi\lesssim 0.276_{-0.003}^{+0.003}
for the compactness discussed earlier.

We notice that in a recent study
[
19
]
, the minimum of
\Delta
is found to be slightly away from the NS center when incorporating constraints on
\Delta
at extremely high densities from pQCD theories. On the other hand, the state-of-the-art calculation indicates that
\Delta
likely decreases all the way to
M_{\textrm{TOV}}
, as shown by the lower-right panel of figure 2 of Ref.
[
24
]
.
In fact, the position of a local minimum of
\Delta
(if it exists) being smaller than
\varepsilon_{\textrm{c}}
has a strong implication on the existence of a possible peak in the density profile of
s^{2}
within NS densities. However, the inverse is not generally true: If there is a peak in
s^{2}
within NS densities, the
\Delta
within NS densities may or may not develop a local minimum, we prove this statement in the last paragraph of Appendix
C
.

IV.  Do Existing NS Data Require Inevitably a Peaked Density Profile for the Speed of Sound?

The answer to this question is strongly dependent on the energy dependence of the trace anomaly underlying the available NS observational data. Our results above from analyzing NS compactness and mass scalings can help address this issue. This is because the trace anomaly
\Delta
and its derivative with respect to energy density are crucial for understanding the

(6)

V.  Summary and Outlook

In summary, within the IPAD-TOV approach based on the predicted scaling of NS compactness with its central pressure/energy density ratio that is also verified by
10^{5}
meta-model EOSs, we have shown that NS central trace anomaly can be extracted reliably from its observational data directly insensitive of the EOS models. Using the available data on the mass, radius and/or compactness of several NSs from recent X-ray and gravitational wave observations, we extracted the central trace anomaly as a function of energy density. It stringently tests the existing EOS models and sets a clear guidance in a new direction for further understanding the nature and EOS of supradense matter in a model-insensitive manner.

The central trace anomaly plays an important role in shaping the density or radius profile of the SSS
s^{2}
in NSs.
Our analyses of the central trace anomaly
indicate that a peaked shape of
s^{2}
in NSs is an implication considering some well-founded theoretical limits but not practically inevitable, and it is not a direct consequence of the currently available NS observational data.

Looking forward, as more data of NS masses and radii or red-shifts become available, we may be able to finally determine whether a peaked
s^{2}
profile can emerge in NSs. We emphasize that our scalings provide a useful tool to extract the
\Delta
and
\varepsilon/\varepsilon_{0}
directly from the observational data (masses, radii).
Sketched in FIG.

are two typical shapes of the function
\Delta(\varepsilon/\varepsilon_{0})
.
In the left panel, we expect the
s^{2}
profile to have a peak somewhere since there is a quick decreasing of
\Delta
and two approximate plateaus at low and high energy densities. While the
\Delta
in the right panel indicates that
-\mathrm{d}\Delta/\mathrm{d}\overline{\varepsilon}
is approximately a positive constant, both the derivative part
-\overline{\varepsilon}\mathrm{d}\Delta/\mathrm{d}\overline{\varepsilon}
and the non-derivative term
1/3-\Delta=P/\varepsilon
are thus expected to monotonically increase with
\varepsilon/\varepsilon_{0}
.

Appendix A
Meta-model EOS of NS Matter

We adopt a meta-model for generating randomly NS EOSs in a broad parameter space that can mimic diverse model predictions consistent will existing constraints from terrestrial experiments and astrophysical observations as well as general physics principles
[
67
,
68
,
69
,
70
]
.
It is based on a so-called minimum model of NSs consisting of neutrons, protons, electrons and muons (npe
\mu
matter) at
\beta
-equilibrium. Its most basic input is the EOS of isospin-asymmetric nucleonic matter in the form of energy per nucleon
E(\rho,\delta)=E_{0}(\rho)+E_{\textrm{sym}}(\rho)\delta^{2}
, here
\rho=\rho_{\textrm{n}}+\rho_{\textrm{p}}
and
\delta\equiv(\rho_{\textrm{n}}-\rho_{\textrm{p}})/(\rho_{\textrm{n}}+\rho_{\textrm{p}})
is the isospin asymmetry of neutron-rich system with neutron density
\rho_{\textrm{n}}
and proton density
\rho_{\textrm{p}}
, respectively. The EOS of symmetric nuclear matter
E_{0}(\rho)
and the symmetry energy
E_{\textrm{sym}}(\rho)
are parameterized respectively as
E_{0}(\rho)=B+2^{-1}K_{0}\chi^{2}+6^{-1}J_{0}\chi^{3}+24^{-1}I_{0}\chi^{4}
and
E_{\textrm{sym}}(\rho)=S+L\chi+2^{-1}K_{\textrm{sym}}\chi^{2}+6^{-1}J_{\textrm{sym}}\chi^{3}
with
\chi\equiv(\rho-\rho_{0})/3\rho_{0}
,
B\equiv E_{0}(\rho_{0})
and
S\equiv E_{\textrm{sym}}(\rho_{0})
, therefore defining the coefficients
K_{0}
,
J_{0}
,
\cdots
.

The total energy density is
\varepsilon(\rho,\delta)=[E(\rho,\delta)+M_{\textrm{N}}]\rho+\varepsilon_{\ell}(\rho,\delta)
where
M_{\textrm{N}}\approx 939\,\textrm{MeV}
and
\varepsilon_{\ell}(\rho,\delta)
is the energy density of leptons from an ideal Fermi gas model
[

Using our notations of Ref.

The dimensionless NS mass is given by
\widehat{M}_{\textrm{NS}}\approx 3^{-1}\widehat{R}^{3}+5^{-1}a_{2}\widehat{R}^{5}+\cdots

a_{2}=b_{2}/s_{\textrm{c}}^{2}

[
60
]
with
s_{\textrm{c}}^{2}
the central speed of sound squared.
Keeping only the leading-order term
3^{-1}\widehat{R}^{3}
in
\widehat{M}_{\textrm{NS}}

\widehat{M}_{\textrm{NS}}\sim\Pi_{\textrm{c}}^{3/2}

M_{\textrm{NS}}\sim\varepsilon_{\textrm{c}}^{-1/2}\widehat{M}_{\textrm{NS}}\sim\varepsilon_{\textrm{c}}^{-1/2}\Pi_{\textrm{c}}^{3/2}

[

The

[

(9)

Including this term in

(\widehat{\varepsilon}=1)

\langle\widehat{\varepsilon}\rangle

\mathrm{X}

Matching the two expressions Eq. (

Appendix C
Understanding the Peaked/valleyed Behavior of
s^{2}
: Analytical Analyses

The results of FIG.
4
in the main text could be understood by considering the general feature of the derivative term
\Delta^{\prime}\equiv\mathrm{d}\Delta/\mathrm{d}\overline{\varepsilon}
of the dimensionless trace anomaly.
There is a point
\overline{\varepsilon}_{\textrm{p}}
in energy density where
\Delta
decreases fastest; around this point we can expand
\Delta^{\prime}
as

\Delta^{\prime}\approx\Delta_{\textrm{p}}^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{\textrm{p}}^{\prime\prime\prime}\left(\overline{\varepsilon}-\overline{\varepsilon}_{\textrm{p}}\right)^{2},~~\Delta_{\textrm{p}}^{\prime}<0,~~\Delta_{\textrm{p}}^{\prime\prime\prime}>0,

(16)

since the first-order derivative of
\Delta^{\prime}
, namely
\Delta^{\prime\prime}
at such point is zero, see S.FIG.
8
.
Since
\Delta_{\textrm{p}}^{\prime\prime\prime}
characterizes the curvature of
\Delta^{\prime}
, it is small for a shallow-shaped
\Delta^{\prime}
but large for a sharp-shaped one.
Correspondingly, the trace anomaly
\Delta
itself around the point can be approximated by

\Delta\approx\Delta_{\textrm{p}}^{\prime}\left(\overline{\varepsilon}-\overline{\varepsilon}_{\textrm{p}}\right)+\frac{1}{6}\Delta_{\textrm{p}}^{\prime\prime\prime}\left(\overline{\varepsilon}-\overline{\varepsilon}_{\textrm{p}}\right)^{3}+\Delta_{\textrm{p}}.

(17)

We obtain the
s^{2}
using the formula (
6
).
Calculating the derivative
\mathrm{d}s^{2}/\mathrm{d}\overline{\varepsilon}
gives

{\mathrm{d}s^{2}}/{\mathrm{d}\overline{\varepsilon}}=-2\Delta_{\textrm{p}}^{\prime}+\left(3\overline{\varepsilon}_{\textrm{p}}\overline{\varepsilon}-2\overline{\varepsilon}^{2}-\overline{\varepsilon}_{\textrm{p}}^{2}\right)\Delta_{\textrm{p}}^{\prime\prime\prime}.

(18)

We discuss two cases:
(i) If the valley of the
\Delta^{\prime}
curve is shallow, i.e.,
\Delta_{\textrm{p}}^{\prime\prime\prime}
is positively small, we can neglect the second term in (
18
),

{\mathrm{d}s^{2}}/{\mathrm{d}\overline{\varepsilon}}\approx-2\Delta_{\textrm{p}}^{\prime}>0.

(19)

This means that even when there exists a peak in the derivative term
-\overline{\varepsilon}\mathrm{d}\Delta/\mathrm{d}\overline{\varepsilon}
(equivalently a valley in
\mathrm{d}\Delta/\mathrm{d}\overline{\varepsilon}
), the
s^{2}
is still a monotonic increasing function of
\overline{\varepsilon}
.
This corresponds to the panel (b) of FIG.
4
in the main text. (ii) On the other hand, if
|\Delta_{\textrm{p}}^{\prime}|
is smaller than
\Delta_{\textrm{p}}^{\prime\prime\prime}
, i.e., the valley in
\Delta^{\prime}
is sharp, we can treat the first term in (
6
) as a perturbation and solve the equation
\mathrm{d}s^{2}/\mathrm{d}\overline{\varepsilon}=0
for its extreme point
\overline{\varepsilon}_{\textrm{p}}^{\ast}
. The result is

\displaystyle\overline{\varepsilon}_{\textrm{p}}^{\ast}=

\displaystyle\frac{3\overline{\varepsilon}_{\textrm{p}}\Delta_{\textrm{p}}^{\prime\prime\prime}+\sqrt{\overline{\varepsilon}_{\textrm{p}}^{2}\Delta_{\textrm{p}}^{\prime\prime\prime 2}-16\Delta_{\textrm{p}}^{\prime}\Delta_{\textrm{p}}^{\prime\prime\prime}}}{4\Delta_{\textrm{p}}^{\prime\prime\prime}}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\overline{\varepsilon}_{\textrm{p}}\left(1-\frac{2}{\overline{\varepsilon}_{\textrm{p}}^{2}}\frac{\Delta_{\textrm{p}}^{\prime}}{\Delta_{\textrm{p}}^{\prime\prime\prime}}\right)>\overline{\varepsilon}_{\textrm{p}},

(20)

the second approximation follows for small
\Delta_{\textrm{p}}^{\prime}
.
Moreover, we have

\displaystyle s^{2}(\overline{\varepsilon}_{\textrm{p}}^{\ast})\approx

\displaystyle 3^{-1}-\Delta_{\textrm{p}}-\overline{\varepsilon}_{\textrm{p}}\Delta_{\textrm{p}}^{\prime},~~\mbox{as well as}

(21)

\displaystyle\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}s^{2}}{\mathrm{d}\overline{\varepsilon}^{2}}\right|_{\overline{\varepsilon}_{\textrm{p}}^{\ast}}\approx

\displaystyle\Delta_{\textrm{p}}^{\prime\prime\prime}\overline{\varepsilon}_{\textrm{p}}\left(\frac{8}{\overline{\varepsilon}_{\textrm{p}}^{2}}\frac{\Delta_{\textrm{p}}^{\prime}}{\Delta_{\textrm{p}}^{\prime\prime\prime}}-1\right)<0.

(22)

The negativeness of the second-order derivative shows that it is a maximum point (peak) of
s^{2}
.
The correction in the bracket of (
20
) is positive, this means that the peak in
s^{2}
occurs on the right side of the peak in its derivative term
-\overline{\varepsilon}\mathrm{d}\Delta/\mathrm{d}\overline{\varepsilon}
. See TAB.
3
for an example, i.e,
5.2\approx\overline{\varepsilon}_{\textrm{pk}}>\overline{\varepsilon}_{\textrm{deriv,pk}}\approx 4.1
;
this corresponds to the panel (a) of FIG.
4
in the main text. We can also evaluate the decomposition terms
s_{\textrm{deriv}}^{2}\equiv-\overline{\varepsilon}\mathrm{d}\Delta/\mathrm{d}\overline{\varepsilon}
and
s_{\textrm{non}\mbox{-}\textrm{deriv}}^{2}\equiv 3^{-1}-\Delta
of
s^{2}
at
\overline{\varepsilon}_{\textrm{p}}^{\ast}
,
namely
s_{\textrm{deriv}}^{2}(\overline{\varepsilon}_{\textrm{p}}^{\ast})\approx-\overline{\varepsilon}_{\textrm{p}}\Delta_{\textrm{p}}^{\prime}
and
s_{\textrm{non}\mbox{-}\textrm{deriv}}^{2}(\overline{\varepsilon}_{\textrm{p}}^{\ast})\approx 3^{-1}-\Delta_{\textrm{p}}

Obviously, the derivative term is small positive.

Limitedly L-weakly compact operators


Abstract
We introduce new class of limitedly L-weakly compact operators from a Banach space to a Banach lattice. This class is a proper subclass of the Bourgain-Diestel operators and it contains properly the class of L-weakly compact operators. We give its efficient characterization in term of sequences, investigate the domination problem, and study the completeness of this class of operators.

Introduction and Preliminaries

The L-weakly compact operators were introduced by P. Meyer-Nieberg in the beginning of seventies in
order to diversify the concept of weakly compact operators via imposing Banach lattice structure
on the range of operators. Limited operators and the Bourgain-Diestel operators were introduced
a decade later. Since then, these operators attract permanent attention and
inspire many researchers. In the last decade further related classes were introduced
and studied by many authors
[
3
,
6
,
10
,
11
]
.
Applying the Meyer-Nieberg approach to the Bourgain-Diestel operators,
we introduce new classes of limitedly and bi-limitedly
L
-weakly
compact operators, give their sequential characterization, and study the domination
problem in these classes of operators.

In the present note: vector spaces are real; operators are linear and bounded; the letters
X
and
Y

stand for Banach spaces,
E
and
F
for Banach lattices;
B_{X}
denotes the closed unit ball of
X
;

\text{\rm L}(X,Y)
the space of all bounded operators from
X
to
Y
;

E_{+}
the positive cone of
E
;

\text{\rm sol}(A)=\bigcup\limits_{a\in A}[-|a|,|a|]

the solid hull of
A\subseteq E
;
and
E^{a}=\{x\in E:|x|\geq x_{n}\downarrow 0\Rightarrow\|x_{n}\|\to 0\}

the
o
-continuous part of
E
.
For further unexplained terminology on Banach lattices used in this note, we refer the reader to
[
1
,
2
,
9
,
12
]
.

In Section 2, we introduce limitedly and bi-limitedly
L
-weakly
compact operators and give a sequential characterization of such operators.
Section 3 is devoted to the domination problem in the class of limitedly
L
-weakly
compact operators, and to the completeness of the spaces of such operators.

2
Main Definitions and Basic Results

Here we collect crucial definitions and prove the main technical theorem of the present note.

2.1

The following definition is due to P. Meyer-Nieberg
[
12
]
.

Definition 2.1.1
.

A subset
A
of
F
is an
Lwc
-set whenever
each disjoint sequence
(a_{n})
in
\text{\rm sol}(A)
is norm-null.
A bounded operator
T:X\to F
is an
Lwc
-operator
(
T\in\text{\rm Lwc}(X,F)
) if
T(B_{X})
is an
Lwc
-subset of
F
.

As war as we know, the first part of the following definition goes back to J. Diestel.

Definition 2.1.2
.

A bounded subset
A
of
X
is called:

a)

limited if each
w
∗
-null sequence in
X^{\prime}
is
uniformly null on
A
.

b)

bi
-limited if each
w
∗
-null sequence in
X^{\prime\prime\prime}
is
uniformly null on
A
.

It is well known that
B_{X}
is not limited in
X
when
\dim(X)=\infty
, and

A\ \text{\rm is relatively compact}\ \Longrightarrow A\ \text{\rm is limited}\ \Longrightarrow A\ \text{\rm is bi-limited}.

Each
bi
-limited subset of a reflexive Banach space is relatively compact.

B_{c_{0}}
is not limited, yet is
bi
-limited in
c_{0}
by Phillip’s lemma.
The following definition goes back to G. Emmanuele
[
8
]
.

Definition 2.1.3
.

A Banach space
X
is said to possess the Bourgain–Diestel property
(briefly,
X\in(\text{\rm BDP})
)
if each limited subset of
X
is relatively weakly compact.
An operator
T:X\to Y
is a Bourgain–Diestel operator
(briefly,
T\in\text{\rm BD}(X,Y)
) if
T
carries
limited sets onto relatively weakly compact sets.

It was shown in
[
4
]
that all separable and all reflexive Banach spaces belong to (
BDP
).
In particular,
\ell^{1},c_{0},c\in(\text{\rm BDP})
,
yet
\ell^{\infty}\not\in(\text{\rm BDP})
as
B_{c_{0}}
is a limited but not weakly
compact subset of
\ell^{\infty}
.

2.2

Here, we present two new classes of operators.
The first one lies strictly between
L
-weakly compact and Bourgain–Diestel operators.
It refines the class of Bourgain–Diestel operators
in the same manner as
L
-weakly compact operators refine weakly compact operators.

Definition 2.2.1
.

An operator
T:X\to F
is called:

i)

limitedly
L
-weakly compact (
T\in\text{\rm l-Lwc}(X,F)
),
if
T
carries limited subsets of
X
onto
Lwc
-subsets of
F
.

ii)

bi
-limitedly
L
-weakly compact (
T\in\text{\rm bi-l-Lwc}(X,F)
),
if
T
carries
bi
-limited subsets of
X
onto
Lwc
-subsets of
F
.

Clearly,
\text{\rm l-Lwc}(X,F)
and
\text{\rm bi-l-Lwc}(X,F)
are vector spaces and

\text{\rm Lwc}(X,F)\subseteq\text{\rm bi-l-Lwc}(X,F)\subseteq\text{\rm l-Lwc}(X,F)\subseteq\text{\rm BD}(X,F).

All three inclusions here are proper in general.

Example 2.2.2
.

Consider operator
I_{\ell^{2}}
.
Since each limited subset of
\ell^{2}
is relatively compact and relatively compact
subsets of
\ell^{2}
are
Lwc
-sets, then

I_{\ell^{2}}\in\text{\rm l-Lwc}(\ell^{2})
and hence
I_{\ell^{2}}\in\text{\rm bi-l-Lwc}(\ell^{2})

because
\ell^{2}
is reflexive. Since
B_{\ell^{2}}
is not an
Lwc
-set then

I_{\ell^{2}}\notin\text{\rm Lwc}(\ell^{2})
.

Example 2.2.3
.

Consider operator
I_{c_{0}}
.
Since each limited subset of
c_{0}
is relatively compact and relatively compact
subsets of
c_{0}
are
Lwc
-sets, then

I_{c_{0}}\in\text{\rm l-Lwc}(c_{0})
.
Since
B_{c_{0}}
is a
bi
-limited subset of
c_{0}
which is not
an
Lwc
-set then
I_{c_{0}}\notin\text{\rm bi-l-Lwc}(c_{0})
.

Example 2.2.4
.

Consider
I_{c}
. Since
c
is separable, limited subsets of
c
coincide
with relatively compact subsets,
and hence
I_{c}\in\text{\rm BD}(c)
.
However, the singleton
\{(1,1,1,...)\}
is limited
yet not an
Lwc
-set in
c
. Thus

I_{c}\notin\text{\rm l-Lwc}(c)
.

Combining the above examples we obtain:

\text{\rm Lwc}(\ell^{2}\oplus c_{0}\oplus c)\subsetneqq\text{\rm bi-l-Lwc}(\ell^{2}\oplus c_{0}\oplus c)\subsetneqq\text{\rm l-Lwc}(\ell^{2}\oplus c_{0}\oplus c)\subsetneqq\text{\rm BD}(\ell^{2}\oplus c_{0}\oplus c).

2.3

The following sequential characterization of
l-Lwc
-operators
restates the definition of
l-Lwc
-operators in an applicable way,
with no use of both limited and
l-Lwc
-sets.

Theorem 2.3.1
.

T\in\text{\rm l-Lwc}(X,F)
iff

T^{\prime}f_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{\rm w}^{\ast}}}{{\to}}0
in
X^{\prime}
for each disjoint bounded
sequence
(f_{n})
in
F^{\prime}
.

The proof is based on the next important fact due to Meyer-Nieberg
(see
[
1
, Thm.5.63]
and
[
5
, Prop.2.2]
for a slightly more general setting).

Proposition 2.3.2
.

Let
A\subseteq E
and
B\subseteq E^{\prime}
be nonempty bounded sets. Then
every disjoint sequence
(a_{n})
of
\text{\rm sol}(A)
is uniformly null
on
B
iff every disjoint sequence
(b_{n})
of
\text{\rm sol}(B)
is uniformly null on
A
.

Proof.

(of Theorem
2.3.1
).
(
\Longrightarrow
) Let
T\in\text{\rm l-Lwc}(X,F)
,
(f_{n})
be disjoint bounded in
F^{\prime}
,
and
x\in X
. As
\{Tx\}\subset F
is an
Lwc
-set, then

T^{\prime}f_{n}(x)=f_{n}(Tx)\to 0
by Proposition
2.3.2
. Since
x\in X

is arbitrary,
(T^{\prime}f_{n})
is
\text{\rm w}^{\ast}
-null.
(
\Longleftarrow
) Let
T^{\prime}f_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{\rm w}^{\ast}}}{{\to}}0

for each disjoint bounded
(f_{n})
in
F^{\prime}
. Suppose
T\not\in\text{\rm l-Lwc}(X,F)
.
So, there is a non-empty limited subset
L
of
X
such that
T(L)
is not
an
Lwc
-set in
F
.
Then, by Proposition
2.3.2
, there exists a disjoint sequence

(g_{n})
of
B_{F^{\prime}}
that is not uniformly null on
T(L)
.
Therefore,
(T^{\prime}g_{n})
is not uniformly null on
L
violating

T^{\prime}g_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{\rm w}^{\ast}}}{{\to}}0
and the fact that
L
is limited in
X
.
The obtained contradiction completes the proof.
∎

3
Main Results

In the present section we investigate: the domination problem
in the class of
l-Lwc
-operators; the completeness
of the spaces of such operators in operator norm; and complete norms
on the linear spans of such operators. We focus on
l-Lwc
-operators,
as the results for
bi-l-Lwc
-operators are basically similar.

3.1

We begin with the following domination result.

Proposition 3.1.1
.

Let
0\leq S\leq T\in\text{\rm l-Lwc}(E,F)
then
S\in\text{\rm l-Lwc}(E,F)
.

Proof.

Let
(f_{n})
be disjoint bounded in
F^{\prime}
. Then
(|f_{n}|)
is also disjoint bounded,
and hence
T^{\prime}|f_{n}|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{\rm w}^{\ast}}}{{\to}}0
by Theorem
2.3.1
.
It follows from

|S^{\prime}f_{n}(x)|\leq S^{\prime}|f_{n}|(|x|)\leq T^{\prime}|f_{n}|(|x|)\to 0\ \ \ \ (\forall x\in E)

that
S^{\prime}f_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{\rm w}^{\ast}}}{{\to}}0
. Using Theorem
2.3.1
again, we conclude
S\in\text{\rm l-Lwc}(E,F)
.

∎

3.2

The following useful fact is another application of Theorem
2.3.1
.

Proposition 3.2.1
.

Let
\text{\rm l-Lwc}(X,F)\ni T_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\|\cdot\|}}{{\to}}T
.
Then
T\in\text{\rm l-Lwc}(X,F)
.

Proof.

Let
(f_{n})
be disjoint bounded in
F^{\prime}
, and
x\in X
. By Theorem
2.3.1
,
we need to show
T^{\prime}f_{n}(x)\to 0
. Let
\varepsilon>0
. Pick any
k\in\mathbb{N}

with
\|T-T_{k}\|\leq\varepsilon
. Since
T_{k}\in\text{\rm l-Lwc}(X,F)

then
|T_{k}^{\prime}f_{n}(x)|\leq\varepsilon
for
n\geq n_{0}
. As
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrary,
it follows from

|T^{\prime}f_{n}(x)|\leq|T^{\prime}f_{n}(x)-T^{\prime}_{k}f_{n}(x)|+|T_{k}^{\prime}f_{n}(x)|\leq

\|T^{\prime}-T^{\prime}_{k}\|\|f_{n}\|\|x\|+|T_{k}^{\prime}f_{n}(x)|\leq(\|f_{n}\|\|x\|+1)\varepsilon\ \ \ \ (\forall n\geq n_{0})

that
T^{\prime}f_{n}(x)\to 0
, as desired.

∎

Corollary 3.2.2
.

\text{\rm l-Lwc}(E)
is a closed right ideal in
\text{\rm L}(E)

(
and hence a subalgebra of
\text{\rm L}(E)
)
, and it is unital iff

I_{E}
is
l-Lwc
.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.2.1
,

\text{\rm l-Lwc}(E)
is a closed subspace of
\text{\rm L}(E)
.
Since each bounded operator maps limited sets onto limited sets,
\text{\rm l-Lwc}(E)

is a right ideal in
\text{\rm L}(E)
.
The condition on
I_{E}
making
\text{\rm l-Lwc}(E)
unital is trivial.

∎

3.3

Let
\emptyset\neq{\cal P}\subseteq\text{\rm L}(E,F)
be a set of operators
(the set of
{\cal P}
-
operators
). An operator
T:E\to F
is
an
r
-
{\cal P}
-
operator
, if
T=T_{1}-T_{2}
where
T_{1}
and

T_{2}
are positive
{\cal P}
-operators.

{\cal P}
-operators are said to satisfy the
domination property
if

0\leq S\leq T\in{\cal P}\ \Longrightarrow\ S\in{\cal P}
.
We say that
T\in\text{\rm L}(E,F)
is

{\cal P}
-
dominated
if
\pm T\leq U\in{\cal P}
.

Proposition 3.3.1
.

Let
{\cal P}\subseteq\text{\rm L}(E,F)
,
{\cal P}\pm{\cal P}\subseteq{\cal P}\neq\emptyset
,
and
T\in\text{\rm L}(E,F)
.

i)
T\ \text{is an {\rm r}-}{\cal P}\text{-operator}\Longleftrightarrow T\ \text{is a}\ {\cal P}\text{-dominated}\ {\cal P}\text{-operator.}

ii) Assume the modulus
|T|
of
T
exists in
\text{\rm L}(E,F)
and suppose that
{\cal P}
-operators satisfy
the domination property. Then

T\ \text{is an {\rm r}-}{\cal P}\text{-operator}\Longleftrightarrow|T|\in{\cal P}
.

Proof.

i) Let
T=T_{1}-T_{2}
, where
T_{1},T_{2}\in{\cal P}
are positive.

{\cal P}\pm{\cal P}\subseteq{\cal P}
implies
T\in{\cal P}
and

U=T_{1}+T_{2}\in{\cal P}
. From
\pm T\leq U
obtain that
T
is
{\cal P}
-dominated.

Now, let
T\in{\cal P}
be a
{\cal P}
-dominated.
Take
U\in{\cal P}
such that
\pm T\leq U
.
Since
T=U-(U-T)
, and both
U
and
U-T
are
positive
{\cal P}
-operators,
T
is an
r
-
{\cal P}
-operator.

ii) First assume
|T|\in{\cal P}
.
Since
T=T_{+}-T_{-}
,
0\leq T_{\pm}\leq|T|\in{\cal P}
, the domination property
implies that
T_{+}
and
T_{-}
are positive
{\cal P}
-operators, and hence
T=T_{+}-T_{-}

is an
r
-
{\cal P}
-operator.

Now, assume
T
is an
r
-
{\cal P}
-operator.
Then there are positive
T_{1},T_{2}\in{\cal P}

satisfying
T=T_{1}-T_{2}
. Since
0\leq T_{+}\leq T_{1}
and

0\leq T_{-}\leq T_{2}
, the domination property implies
T_{+},T_{-}\in{\cal P}
.
Hence
|T|=T_{+}+T_{-}
is likewise a
{\cal P}
-operator.
∎

Proposition 3.3.2
.

Let
F
be Dedekind complete, and
{\cal P}

a subspace in
\text{\rm L}(E,F)
,
satisfying the domination property.
Then
\text{\rm r-}{\cal P}(E,F)

is an order ideal in the Dedekind complete vector lattice

\text{\rm L}_{r}(E,F)
.

Proof.

Since
F
is Dedekind complete,
\text{\rm L}_{r}(E,F)

is a Dedekind complete vector lattice.
By Proposition
3.3.1
ii),

T\in\text{\rm r-}{\cal P}(E,F)\Longrightarrow|T|\in\text{\rm r-}{\cal P}(E,F)
,
and hence
\text{\rm r-}{\cal P}(E,F)
is a vector sublattice of
\text{\rm L}_{r}(E,F)
.
Since
{\cal P}
satisfies the domination property,
\text{\rm r-}{\cal P}(E,F)

is an order ideal in
\text{\rm L}_{r}(E,F)
.
∎

The next lemma follows from Proposition
3.3.1
ii)
due to Proposition
3.1.1
.

Lemma 3.3.3
.

Let a linear operator
T:E\to F
possess the modulus. Then

T\in\text{\rm r-l-Lwc}(E,F)
iff
|T|\in\text{\rm r-l-Lwc}(E,F)
.

Theorem 3.3.4
.

The following statements hold.

i)
\text{\rm r-l-Lwc}(E)
is a subalgebra of
\text{\rm L}_{r}(E)
. Moreover,

\text{\rm r-l-Lwc}(E)=\text{\rm L}_{r}(E)\ \Longleftrightarrow I_{E}\in\text{\rm l-Lwc}(E).

ii) If
E
is Dedekind complete then

\text{\rm r-l-Lwc}(E)
is a closed order ideal of the Banach lattice
(\text{\rm L}_{r}(E),\ \|\cdot\|_{r})
,
where

\ \|x\|_{r}:=\|~|x|~\|
is the regular norm on
\text{\rm L}_{r}(E)
.

Proof.

i) It follows from Corollary
3.2.2

that
\text{\rm r-l-Lwc}(E)
is a right ideal and hence is
a subalgebra of
\text{\rm L}_{r}(E)
.
The condition on
I_{E}
under that
\text{\rm r-l-Lwc}(E)=\text{\rm L}_{r}(E)
is trivial.

ii) It follows from Lemma
3.3.3
that
\text{\rm r-l-LW}(E)
is
a Riesz subalgebra of
\text{\rm L}_{r}(E)
. Proposition
3.3.2
implies
that
\text{\rm r-l-Lwc}(E)
is an order ideal of
\text{\rm L}_{r}(E)
.
Let
S,T\in\text{\rm r-l-LW}(E)
satisfy
|S|\leq|T|
.
In order to show
\|S\|_{r}\leq\|T\|_{r}
,
observe that
|S|,|T|\in\text{\rm l-LW}(E)
by Lemma
3.3.3
. Then

\|S\|_{r}=\|~|S|~\|\leq\|~|T|~\|=\|T\|_{r}.

Due to

implies
that

Clearly, the regular norm

is submultiplicative. Indeed,
let

, and hence

\|ST\|_{r}\leq\|~|S||T|~\|\leq\|~|S|~\|\cdot\|~|T|~\|=\|S\|_{r}\cdot\|T\|_{r},

as desired.
∎

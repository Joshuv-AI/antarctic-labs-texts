Abstract
. In this paper we study the asymptotic behavior of solutions of fractional differential equations of the form D C α u ( t ) = A u ( t ) + f ( t ) , u ( 0 ) = x , 0 < α ≤ 1 , ( ∗ ) D^{\alpha}_{C}u(t)=Au(t)+f(t),u(0)=x,0<\alpha\leq 1,(*) where D C α ​ u ​ ( t ) D^{\alpha}_{C}u(t) is the derivative of the function u u in the Caputo’s sense, A A is a linear operator in a Banach space 𝕏 \mathbb{X} that may be unbounded and f f satisfies the property that lim t → ∞ ( f ⁡ ( t + 1 ) − f ⁡ ( t ) ) = 0 \lim_{t\to\infty}(f(t+1)-f(t))=0 which we will call asymptotic 1 1 -periodicity. By using the spectral theory of functions on the half line we derive analogs of Katznelson-Tzafriri and Massera Theorems. Namely, we give sufficient conditions in terms of spectral properties of the operator A A for all asymptotic mild solutions of Eq. 1.
Introduction

We are interested in the asymptotic behavior of solutions to linear fractional differential equations of the form

(1.1)

D^{\alpha}_{C}u(t)=Au(t)+f(t),u(0)=x,0<\alpha\leq 1,

where
D^{\alpha}_{C}u(t)
is the derivative of the function
u
in the Caputo’s sense,
A
is a linear operator in a Banach space
\mathbb{X}
that may be unbounded and
f
satisfies the property that
\lim_{t\to\infty}(f(t+1)-f(t))=0
which we will call ”asymptotic
1
-periodicity”.

In recent decades fractional differential equations are of growing interests as this kind of equations allows us to model more complex processes. We refer the reader to the monographs
[
11
,
15
]
for an account of applications in Physics and Engineering. For general results and concepts in abstract spaces the reader is referred to
[
3
,
6
,
7
]
.

Asymptotic behavior of solutions of differential equations is a central topic in the qualitative theory of differential equations and dynamical systems that is rich of results and ideas. The very first behavior of solutions that are of interest to mathematicians because of its applicability is the periodicity of solutions. There are many classic results in this direction that are models for subsequent researches. Among many such works we refer the reader to very well known results by Massera
[
20
]
and Katznelson-Tsafriri
[
13
]
due to their very different techniques of study. These results are recently extended to wider classes of equations and solutions in
[
19
]
and
[
22
]
.
It is natural to ask if the concepts and results in these recent works could be extended to a larger class of Eq.(
1.1
). In this paper we will take an attempt to address this question.

More specifically, we look for conditions under which
some (or all) solutions
x(\cdot)
are asymptotic
1
-periodic. We will prove an analog of the Katznelson-Tzafiri Theorem and Massera Theorem for this kind of solutions for Eq.(
1.1
). Our method is to use the spectral theory of functions and the technique of decomposition rather than the period map as it is often used in the area since the equation is actually not periodic and its homogeneous equation does not generate a dynamical system even in the autonomous case. In the recent paper
[
19
]
similar problems in case
\alpha=1
were studied for evolution equations with periodic coefficients based on a spectral theory of functions. Namely, in
[
19
]
the spectrum of a function
f\in BUC(\mathbb{R}^{+},\mathbb{X})
(the space of all uniformly continuous and bounded functions on
[0,\infty)
) is defined to be the subset
\sigma(f)
of the unit circle
\Gamma
in the complex plane consisting of all points
\xi_{0}
such that the complex function
R(\lambda,\bar{S})\bar{f}
has no analytic extension to any neighborhood of
\xi_{0}
. Here
S
is the translation by
1
and
\bar{S}
is the induced operator by
S
in the quotient space
\mathbb{Y}:=BUC(\mathbb{R}^{+},\mathbb{X})/C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{+},\mathbb{X})
(the space of continuous functions approaching zero at infinity and
R(\lambda,\bar{S})
stands for the resolvent of
\bar{S}
. In
[
19
]
, the asymptotic
1
-periodicity of a function
f\in BUC(\mathbb{R}^{+},\mathbb{X})
is characterized by the property
\sigma(f)\subset\{1\}
. Note that for fractional equations (
1.1
), using the transform
R(\lambda,\bar{S})\bar{f}
to capture the spectrum of an asymptotic mild solution of (
1.1
) does not work. As shown in
[
18
]
, the spectrum
sp(f)
determined by the transform
R(\lambda,\bar{\mathcal{D}})\bar{f}
, where
{\mathcal{D}}
is the differential operator
d/dt
and
\bar{\mathcal{D}}
stands for the induced operator by
{\mathcal{D}}
in
\mathbb{Y}
, can be used to study the asymptotic behavior of Eq.(
1.1
). Our next step is to characterize the asymptotic
1
-periodicity in terms of
sp(f)
. This will be done by proving the identity
\sigma(f)=\overline{e^{i\cdot sp(f)}}
that is based on the Weak Spectral Mapping Theorem for
C_{0}
-groups of isometries. This characterization of the asymptotic
1
-periodicity allows us to study the asymptotic
1
-periodicity of all asymptotic mild solutions existence of Eq.(
1.1
), an analog of the Katznelson-Tzifriri for fractional equations. We also prove an analog of Massera Theorem for this class of equations based on the technique of decomposition. Our main results are stated in Theorems
3.9
,
3.12
and
3.16
.

3.
Main results

3.1.
Spectral characterization of asymptotic
1
-periodicity

Definition 3.1
.

Let
p
be a given real number in
[0,2\pi)
. A function
g\in BUC(\mathbb{R}^{+},\mathbb{X})
is said to be an asymptotic Bloch
1
-periodic function of type
p
if

\lim_{t\to\infty}(g(t+1)-e^{ip}g(t))=0.

If
p=0
, an asymptotic Bloch
1
-periodic function
g
of type
p
will be called an asymptotic
1
-periodic function. When
p=\pi
we call the function asymptotic anti
1
-periodic.

Below we will show that the spectrum of a function
f\in BUC(\mathbb{R}^{+},\mathbb{X})
can be determined as the spectrum of a linear operator. Let us define
{\mathcal{M}}_{f}
as the closure of the linear space spanned by the set
\{\tilde{S}(t)f:\ t\in\mathbb{R}\}\subset\mathbb{Y}
. Obviously
\bar{S}(t)
leaves
{\mathcal{M}}_{f}
invariant for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
. We consider the
C_{0}
-group of isometries
\{\tilde{S}|_{{\mathcal{M}}_{f}},t\in\mathbb{R}\}
. The generator of this group will be denoted by
\tilde{D}|_{{\mathcal{M}}_{f}}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

For each
f\in BUC(\mathbb{R}^{+},\mathbb{X})
, we have

(3.1)

i\cdot sp(f)=\sigma(\tilde{\mathcal{D}}|_{{\mathcal{M}}_{f}}).

Proof.

If
\lambda_{0}\in\rho(\tilde{D}|_{{\mathcal{M}}_{f}})
, then, for
\lambda\in U(\lambda_{0})\backslash i\mathbb{R}
, where
U(\lambda_{0})
is a small neighborhood of
\lambda_{0}

\hat{f}(\lambda)=(\lambda-\tilde{\mathcal{D}})^{-1}\tilde{f}=(\lambda-\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_{{\mathcal{M}}_{f}})^{-1}\tilde{f}.

Therefore,
\hat{f}(\lambda)
has
(\lambda-\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_{{\mathcal{M}}_{f}})^{-1}\tilde{f}
as
an analytic extension to a neighborhood of
\lambda_{0}
. That means
\lambda_{0}\not\in i\cdot sp(f)
.

Conversely, let
\lambda_{0}\in i\mathbb{R}\backslash i\cdot sp(f)
. Then,
\hat{f}(\lambda)
has an analytic extension to a connected neighborhood
U(\lambda_{0})
of
\lambda_{0}
. We will show that the equation

(3.2)

\lambda_{o}y-\tilde{\mathcal{D}}y=w

has a unique solution
y\in{\mathcal{M}}_{f}
for each
w\in{\mathcal{M}}_{f}
. First, we show that Eq.(
3.2
) has a solution. For
\lambda\in U(\lambda_{0})\backslash i\mathbb{R}
such that
\Re\lambda>0
, using the formula

R(\lambda,\tilde{\mathcal{D}})\tilde{f}=\int^{\infty}_{0}e^{-\lambda t}\tilde{S}(t)\tilde{f}dt,

we see that
\hat{f}(\lambda)=R(\lambda,\tilde{D})\tilde{f}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{f}
. Subsequently,
\hat{f}(\lambda_{0})\in{\mathcal{M}}_{f}
. As the operator
\tilde{D}
is a closed operator and
\hat{f}(\lambda)
is analytic at
\lambda_{0}
, we see that

(3.3)

\lambda_{0}\hat{f}(\lambda_{0})-\tilde{\mathcal{D}}\hat{f}(\lambda_{0})=w.

Next, we will show that the solution of Eq.(
3.2
) is unique for each given
w\in{\mathcal{M}}_{f}
. This is equivalent to show that the homogeneous equation has only trivial solution. Indeed, suppose
y_{0}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{f}
is a solution of

\lambda_{0}y_{0}-\tilde{\mathcal{D}}y_{0}=0.

Using the maximum modulus principle of holomorphic functions as in
[
5
, Proof of Theorem 2.2,p. 2074]
and then the Vitali’s Theorem on convergence of sequences of holomorphic functions we can show that for each
w\in{\mathcal{M}}_{f}
, the function
\hat{w}(\lambda)
has an analytic extension in the connected neighborhood
U(\lambda_{0})
of
\lambda_{0}
.
Next, for
\lambda\in U(\lambda_{0})\backslash i\mathbb{R}
by the identity

R(\lambda,\tilde{\mathcal{D}})(\lambda-\tilde{\mathcal{D}})y_{0}=y_{0}

we have

\lambda R(\lambda,\tilde{\mathcal{D}})y_{0}-y_{0}=R(\lambda,\tilde{\mathcal{D}})\tilde{\mathcal{D}}y_{0}=R(\lambda,\tilde{\mathcal{D}})\lambda_{0}y_{0}.

Hence

(3.4)

\hat{y}_{0}(\lambda)=R(\lambda,\tilde{\mathcal{D}})y_{0}=\frac{y_{0}}{\lambda-\lambda_{0}}.

This function has an analytic extension to a neighborhood of
\lambda_{0}
if and only if
y_{0}=0
. That is,
\lambda_{0}
is in
\rho(\tilde{\mathcal{D}}|_{{\mathcal{M}}f})
.
This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Theorem 3.12
.

Assume that

is a bounded operator,

is asymptotic

-periodic. Then, if Eq.(

Proof.

Consider the operator

\displaystyle=PL\tilde{u}

Now we can state an analog of Theorem

\displaystyle S_{\alpha}(t)

\displaystyle P_{\alpha}(t)

where

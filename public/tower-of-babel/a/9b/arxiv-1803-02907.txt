The tangent space to the space of 0-cycles

Abstract
. Let S S be a Noetherian scheme, and let X X be a scheme over S S , such that all relative symmetric powers of X X over S S exist. 1.
Introduction

The aim of this paper is to make it precise the intuitive feeling that rational equivalence of
0
-cycles on an algebraic variety is the same as rational connectedness of the corresponding points on the group completed infinite symmetric power of that variety. To be more precise, let
X
be a smooth projective variety over a field
k
, and assume for simplicity that
k
is algebraically closed of zero characteristic. Fix a point on
X
and use it to embed the
d
-th symmetric power in to the
(d+1)
-th symmetric power of
X
. Passing to colimit, we obtain the infinite connective symmetric power
{\rm{Sym}}^{\infty}(X)
of the variety
X
over
k
. Looking at this infinite symmetric power as a commutative monoid, we can consider its group completion
{\rm{Sym}}^{\infty}(X)^{+}
in the category of groups. If now
P
and
Q
are closed points on
X
, they can be also considered as elements of the group completed symmetric power
{\rm{Sym}}^{\infty}(X)^{+}
. Then
P
is rationally equivalent to
Q
on
X
if and only if one can draw a rational curve through
P
and
Q
on
{\rm{Sym}}^{\infty}(X)^{+}
.

This philosophy tracks back through the cult paper by Mumford,
[
15
]
, to Francesco Severi and possibly earlier, but it does not give us too much, as the object
{\rm{Sym}}^{\infty}(X)^{+}
is not a variety, and it is not clear what could be a rational curve on it and, more importantly, an appropriate deformation theory of rational curves on the object
{\rm{Sym}}^{\infty}(X)^{+}
in the style of Kollár’s book
[
14
]
. Though Roitman had managed working with the group
{\rm{Sym}}^{\infty}(X)^{+}
as a geometrical object replacing it by the products
{\rm{Sym}}^{d}(X)\times{\rm{Sym}}^{d}(X)
, see
[
16
]
and
[
17
]
, his approach seems to be a compromise, which is not amazing as the necessary technique to deform weird objects was not developed in the early seventies.

So, this is our aim in this paper to develop a technical foundation of deformation theory of rational curves on
{\rm{Sym}}^{\infty}(X)^{+}
, as we see it, and now we are going to explain and justify the concepts promoted in the paper. First of all, we should ask ourselves what is the broadest notion of a geometrical object nowadays? One possible answer might be that a geometrical object is a locally ringed site whose Grothendieck topology is of some geometric nature. On the other hand, whereas the monoid
{\rm{Sym}}^{\infty}(X)
is an ind-scheme, so can be managed in terms of schemes, the group completion
{\rm{Sym}}^{\infty}(X)^{+}
clearly requires a spacewalk in the category of sheaves on schemes with an appropriate topology, such as étale topology or maybe the better Nisnevich one. Therefore, we choose that our initial environment is the category of set valued Nisnevich sheaves on locally Noetherian schemes over a base scheme
S
, and the latter will be always Noetherian.

But sheaves on a site are still not geometrical enough. To produce geometry on a sheaf
{\mathscr{X}}
we suggest to use the notion of an
atlas
, which roughly means that we have a collection of schemes
X_{i}
and morphisms of sheaves
X_{i}\to{\mathscr{X}}
, such that the induced morphism from the coproduct
\coprod_{i}X_{i}
to
{\mathscr{X}}
is an effective epimorphism (see
nLab
). Sheaves with atlases will be called
spaces
. The idea of an atlas gives us a possibility to speak about whether a morphism from a scheme to a Nisnevich sheaf
{\mathscr{X}}
is étale with regard to a given atlas on
{\mathscr{X}}
. A Nisnevich-étale site
{\mathscr{X}}_{\rm{Nis}\mathchar 45\relax\rm{\acute{e}t}}
is then the site whose underlying category is the category of morphisms from schemes to
{\mathscr{X}}
, which are étale with regard to the atlas on
{\mathscr{X}}
, and whose topology is the restriction of the Nisnevich topology on schemes.

For the local study, let
P
be a point on
{\mathscr{X}}
, i.e. a morphism from the spectrum of a field to
{\mathscr{X}}
, and let
{\mathscr{N}}_{P}
be the category of étale neighbourhoods of the point
P
on the site
{\mathscr{X}}_{\rm{Nis}\mathchar 45\relax\rm{\acute{e}t}}
. If the category
{\mathscr{N}}_{P}
is cofiltered, we obtain an honest stalk functor at
P
, which yields the corresponding point of the topos of sheaves on the site
{\mathscr{X}}_{\rm{Nis}\mathchar 45\relax\rm{\acute{e}t}}
. If now
{\mathscr{O}}_{{\mathscr{X}}}
is the sheaf of rings on the site
{\mathscr{X}}_{\rm{Nis}\mathchar 45\relax\rm{\acute{e}t}}
, inherited from the regular functions on schemes, its stalk
{\mathscr{O}}_{{\mathscr{X}}\!,\,P}
is a local ring, for each point
P
on
{\mathscr{X}}
. Then
({\mathscr{X}}_{\rm{Nis}\mathchar 45\relax\rm{\acute{e}t}},{\mathscr{O}}_{{\mathscr{X}}})
is a locally ringed site. The standard procedure then gives us the sheaf of Kähler differentials
\Omega^{1}_{{\mathscr{X}}/S}
and its dual, the tangent sheaf
T_{{\mathscr{X}}/S}
to the space
{\mathscr{X}}
. Applying the stalk at
P
functor to the latter, we obtain the stalk
T_{{\mathscr{X}}\!,\,P}
, and tensoring by the residue field
\kappa(P)
of the local ring
{\mathscr{O}}_{{\mathscr{X}}\!,\,P}
we obtain the tangent space

T_{{\mathscr{X}}}(P)=T_{{\mathscr{X}}\!,\,P}\otimes\kappa(P)

to the space
{\mathscr{X}}
at
P
, with regard to the atlas on
{\mathscr{X}}
. Thus, a geometrical object to us is a sheaf
{\mathscr{X}}
with an atlas, such that
{\mathscr{N}}_{P}
is cofiltered for each point
P
on
{\mathscr{X}}
, and hence the site
{\mathscr{X}}_{\rm{Nis}\mathchar 45\relax\rm{\acute{e}t}}
is locally ringed by the ring
{\mathscr{O}}_{{\mathscr{X}}}
.

This approach works very well when we want to geometrize groups of
0
-cycles. Indeed, let
X
be a locally Noetherian scheme over
S
, such that the relative symmetric power
{\rm{Sym}}^{d}(X/S)
exists for each
d
(this is always the case if, say,
X
is quasi-affine or quasi-projective over
S
). Assume, moreover, that the structural morphism from
X
to
S
admits a section. Use this section to construct the monoid
{\rm{Sym}}^{\infty}(X/S)
, which is and ind-scheme over
S
. Then we look at the group completion
{\rm{Sym}}^{\infty}(X/S)^{+}
in the category of Nisnevich sheaves on locally Noetherian schemes over
S
. The point here is that if
S
is either of pure characteristic
0
or flat over
S
, then
{\rm{Sym}}^{\infty}(X/S)^{+}
is isomorphic to the sheaf of relative
0
-cycles in the sense of Rydh, see
[
18
]
. If, moreover,
S
is seminormal over
{\rm{Spec}}({\mathbb{Q}})
, then the restriction of the sheaf
{\rm{Sym}}^{\infty}(X/S)^{+}
on schemes seminormal over
S
gives us a sheaf isomorphic to the sheaves of relative
0
-cycles constructed by Suslin and Voevodsky,
[
21
]
, and by Kollár,
[
14
]
. This is why the sheaf
{\rm{Sym}}^{\infty}(X/S)^{+}
is really the best reincarnation of a sheaf of relative
0
-cycles on
X
over
S
.

Now, the fibred squares
{\rm{Sym}}^{d}(X/S)\times_{S}{\rm{Sym}}^{d}(X/S)
yield a natural atlas, the
Chow atlas
, on the sheaf
{\rm{Sym}}^{\infty}(X/S)^{+}
. The problem, however, is that we do not know a priori whether the category
{\mathscr{N}}_{P}
of étale neigbourhoods of a point
P
on
{\rm{Sym}}^{\infty}(X/S)^{+}
, constructed with regard to the Chow atlas, is cofiltered. This is our main technical result in the paper (Theorem
6
) which asserts that
{\mathscr{N}}_{P}
is cofiltered indeed, for every point
P
on
{\rm{Sym}}^{\infty}(X/S)^{+}
. It follows that we obtain the locally ringed site
{\rm{Sym}}^{\infty}(X/S)^{+}_{\rm{Nis}\mathchar 45\relax\rm{\acute{e}t}}
with the structural sheaf
{\mathscr{O}}_{{\rm{Sym}}^{\infty}(X/S)^{+}}
on it. As a consequence of that, we also obtain the sheaf of Kähler differentials
\Omega^{1}_{{\rm{Sym}}^{\infty}(X/S)^{+}}
and the tangent sheaf
T_{{\rm{Sym}}^{\infty}(X/S)^{+}}
on
{\rm{Sym}}^{\infty}(X/S)^{+}
, as well as the tangent space

T_{{\rm{Sym}}^{\infty}(X/S)^{+}}(P)

to the space
{\rm{Sym}}^{\infty}(X/S)^{+}
at a point
P
.

Assume now for simplicity that
S
is the spectrum of an algebraically closed field
k
of zero characteristic, such as
{\mathbb{C}}
or
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}
, for example. Any
k
-rational point
P
on
{\rm{Sym}}^{\infty}(X/S)^{+}
corresponds to a
0
-cycle on
X
, which we denote by the same symbol
P
. Two points
P
and
Q
are rationally equivalent, as two
0
-cycles on
X
, if and only if there exists a rational curve

f:{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\to{\rm{Sym}}^{\infty}(X/S)^{+}

on the space of
0
-cycles passing through
P
and
Q
. Suppose, for example, that
X
is a smooth projective surface of general type with trivial transcendental part in the second étale
l
-adic cohomology group
H^{2}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(X)
. Bloch’s conjecture predicts that any two closed points on
X
are rationally equivalent to each other. Reformulating, the space of
0
-cycles
{\rm{Sym}}^{\infty}(X)^{+}
is rationally connected. The usual way of proving that a variety is rationally connected is that we first find a rational curve on it, and then prove that this curve is sufficiently free. As we have now Kähler differentials and the tangent sheaf with tangent spaces at points on the space of
0
-cycles, one can try to do the same on
{\rm{Sym}}^{\infty}(X)^{+}
. The pullback of the tangent sheaf on
0
-cycles to
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
by
f
is a coherent sheaf. Therefore,

f^{*}T_{{\rm{Sym}}^{\infty}(X)^{+}}={\mathscr{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}}(a_{1})\oplus\ldots\oplus{\mathscr{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}}(a_{n})\oplus{\mathscr{T}}\;,

where
{\mathscr{T}}
is a torsion sheaf, and
{\mathscr{O}}(a_{i})
are Serre’s twists. If the deformation theory of rational curves on
{\rm{Sym}}^{\infty}(X/S)^{+}
would be properly developed, we could apply the “same” arguments as in deforming curves on varieties, to prove that
{\rm{Sym}}^{\infty}(X)^{+}
is rationally connected, in case when
X
is a surface of general type with no transcendental part in the second cohomology group.

Another approach to the same subject had been developed by Green and Griffiths in the book
[
7
]
, which contains a lot of new deep ideas, supported by masterly computations, towards infinitesimal study of
0
-cycles on algebraic varieties. The problem to us with Green-Griffiths’ approach is, however, that their tangent space is the stalk of a sheaf on the variety itself, but not on a space of
0
-cycles, see, for example, the definition on page 90, or formula (8.1) on page 105 in
[
7
]
, and, moreover, the space of
0
-cycles, as a geometrical object, is missing in the book. Our standpoint here is that the concept of a space of
0
-cycles should be taken seriously, and we believe that many of our constructions are implicitly there, in the Green-Griffiths’ book. In a sense, the present paper can be also considered as an attempt to prepare a technical basis to rethink the approach by Green and Griffiths, and then try to put a “functorial order” upon the heuristic discoveries in
[
7
]
.

The following definitions are slightly extended versions of the definitions in stack theory. An

for all morphisms

f^{-1}{\mathscr{O}}_{{\mathscr{Y}}}\to{\mathscr{O}}_{{\mathscr{X}}}

If we assume that the category

in terms of page 115 in the first part of

Any Gothendieck topos is a cartesian closed category. In particular, the topos

in

{\it Cycl}^{{\rm{eff}}}(X/S)=\{Z=\sum m_{i}Z_{i}\in{\it Cycl}(X/S)\;|\;m_{i}\geq 0\;\forall i\;\}

be a monoid of effective relative cycles in
X
over
S
.

In general the monoid
{\it Cycl}(X/S)
is
not
a free monoid generated by prime relative cycles, and the group
{\it Cycl}(X/S)
is
not
a free abelian group generated by prime relative cycles.

If
\zeta\in t(X)
, the dimension of
\zeta
in
X
,

\dim(\zeta,X)\;,

is, by definition, the dimension of the closure

Z=\overline{\{\zeta\}}

inside
X
. A relative cycle

Z=\sum m_{i}Z_{i}\in{\it Cycl}(X/S)

is said to be of
relative dimension

r
if the generic point
\zeta_{i}
of each prime cycle
Z_{i}
has dimension
r
in its fibre over
S
. In other words, if

\eta_{i}=f(\zeta_{i})\;,

we look at the fibre
X_{\eta_{i}}
of the morphism
f
at
\eta_{i}
. The cycle
Z
is of relative dimension
r
over
S
if

\dim(\zeta_{i},X_{\eta_{i}})=r

for each index
i
. If
Z
is a relative cycle of relative dimension
r
on
X
, then we write

\dim_{S}(Z)=r\;.

Following
[
21
]
, p 24, we define

{\it Cycl}(X/S,r)=\{Z\in{\it Cycl}(X/S)\;|\;\dim_{S}(Z)=r\}

to be the subset of relative algebraic cycles of relative dimension
r
on
X
, which is obviously a subgroup in
{\it Cycl}(X/S)
. The definition of

{\it Cycl}^{{\rm{eff}}}(X/S,r)=\{Z=\sum m_{i}Z_{i}\in{\it Cycl}(X/S,r)\;|\;m_{i}\geq 0\;\forall i\;\}

is straightforward.

Notice that if
Z
is a relative cycle of relative dimension
r
, it does not mean that all the components
Z_{i}
are of the same dimension
r
. To pick up equidimensional cycles, we need the following definition. For any point
\zeta\in t(X)
let

\dim(X/S)(x)=\dim_{\zeta}(f^{-1}(f(\zeta)))

be the dimension of the fibre
f^{-1}(f(\zeta))
of the morphism
f
at
\zeta
. The morphism
f
is said to be
equidimensional
of dimension
r
if every irreducible component of
X
dominates an irreducible component of
S
and the function

\dim(X/S):t(X)\to{\mathbb{Z}}

is constant and equals
r
for every point
\zeta
on the scheme
X
. A cycle
Z\in Cycl(X/S)
is equidimensional of dimension
r
over
S
if so is the composition

{\rm Supp}(Z)\to X\to S\;.

Let then

{\it Cycl}_{{\rm{equi}}}(X/S,r)=\{Z\in{\it Cycl}(X/S,r)\;|\;\hbox{$Z$ is equidim. of dim. $r$}\}\;.

Accordingly,

{\it Cycl}_{{\rm{equi}}}^{{\rm{eff}}}(X/S,r)=\{Z=\sum_{i}m_{i}Z_{i}\in{\it Cycl}_{{\rm{equi}}}(X/S,r)\;|\;m_{i}\geq 0\;\forall i\;\}\;.

Next, let

U\to S

be a locally Noetherian scheme over
S
(not necessarily of finite type over
S
). In
[
21
]
, for any cycle

Z\in{\it Cycl}(X/S,r)

Suslin and Voevodsky constructed a uniquely defined cycle

Z_{U}\in{\it Cycl}(X\times_{S}U/U,r)_{{\mathbb{Q}}}\;,

a pullback of
Z
along
U\to S
, such that it is compatible with pullbacks long fat points. Here and below, for any abelian group
A
we denote by
A_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
the tensor product
A\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathbb{Q}}
.

Thus, following Suslin and Voevodsky, we obtain the obvious presheaf

{\it Cycl}(X/S,r)_{{\mathbb{Q}}}

on the category
{\sf Noe}/S
, such that for any morphism

U\to S

in
{\sf Noe}/S
,

{\it Cycl}(X/S,r)_{{\mathbb{Q}}}(U)={\it Cycl}(X\times_{S}U/U,r)_{{\mathbb{Q}}}\;,

and the restriction morphisms are induced by the Suslin-Voevodsky’s pullbacks of relative cycles.

Following
[
21
]
, we will say that the pullback
Z_{U}
of a cycle
Z\in{\it Cycl}(X/S,r)
is
integral
if it lies in the image of the canonical homomorphism

{\it Cycl}(X\times_{S}U/U,r)\to{\it Cycl}(X\times_{S}U/U,r)_{{\mathbb{Q}}}

for all schemes
U
in
{\sf Noe}/S
, and define the subgroup

z(X/S,r)=\{Z\in{\it Cycl}(X/S,r)\;|\;\hbox{$Z_{U}$ is integral}\}\;.

Then
z(X/S,r)
is an abelian subpresheaf in the presheaf
{\it Cycl}(X/S,r)_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
on the category
{\sf Noe}/S
.

Let also

z^{{\rm{eff}}}(X/S,r)=\{Z=\sum m_{i}Z_{i}\in z(X/S,r)\;|\;m_{i}\geq 0\;\forall i\}

and

z_{{\rm{equi}}}(X/S,r)=\{Z\in z(X/S,r)\;|\;\hbox{$Z$ is equidim. of dim. $r$ over $S$}\}\;.

Clearly,
z^{{\rm{eff}}}(X/S,r)
is a subpresheaf of monoids and
z^{{\rm{equi}}}(X/S,r)
is a presheaf of abelian groups in
z(X/S,r)
.

For any morphism

U\to S\;,

which is an object of
{\sf Noe}/S
, set

{\it PrimeCycl}(X\times_{S}U/U,r)=\{Z\in{\it Cycl}(X\times_{S}U/U,r)\;|\;\hbox{$Z$ is prime}\}

and

{\it PrimeCycl}_{{\rm{equi}}}(X\times_{S}U/U,r)=\{Z\in{\it PrimeCycl}(X\times_{S}U/U,r)\;|\;\hbox{$Z$ is equidim.}\}

If
S
is regular, and if the morphism
U\to S
is an object of
{\sf Reg}/S
, then

z^{{\rm{eff}}}(X/S,r)={\mathbb{N}}({\it PrimeCycl}_{{\rm{equi}}}(X\times_{S}U/U,r))\;,

and

z_{{\rm{equi}}}(X/S,r)={\mathbb{N}}({\it PrimeCycl}_{{\rm{equi}}}(X\times_{S}U/U,r))^{+}\;,

see Corollary 3.4.5 in
[
21
]
.

It does not mean, however, that
z^{{\rm{eff}}}(X/S,r)
is a free monoid in the category of set valued presheaves freely generated by a set valued “presheaf of relative prime cycles of dimension
r
” on the category
{\sf Reg}/S
, as the Suslin-Voevodsky pullback of a relative prime cycle is not necessarily a prime cycle, so that the needed set valued presheaf does not exist. But
z_{{\rm{equi}}}(X/S,r)
is certainly the group completion of
z^{{\rm{eff}}}(X/S,r)
as a presheaf on
{\sf Reg}/S
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
S
be a Noetherian scheme, and let
X
be a scheme of finite type over
S
. Then the presheaves
z(X/S,r)
and

are sheaves in
\rm{cdh}
-topology and, as a consequence, in the Nisnevich topology on the category

Proof
.
See Theorem 4.2.9(1) on page 65 in
[
21
]
.

Relative cycles can be classified by their degrees, provided there exists a projective embedding of
X
over
S
. Indeed, assume that
X
is projective over
S
, i.e. there is a closed embedding

i:X\to{\mathbb{P}}^{n}_{S}

over
S
. For each cycle

one can define its degree

\deg(Z,i)=\sum\deg(i(Z_{j}))

with regard to the embedding
i
. Let also

z^{{\rm{eff}}}_{d}((X,i)/S,r)=\{Z\in z_{{\rm{equi}}}(X/S,r)\;|\;\deg(Z,i)=d\}\;.

The set valued presheaf

z^{{\rm{eff}}}_{d}((X,i)/S,r):{\sf Noe}/S\to{\sf Set}

is given by the formula

z^{{\rm{eff}}}_{d}((X,i)/S,r)(U)=\{Z\in z_{{\rm{equi}}}(X\times_{S}U/U,r)\;|\;\deg(Z,i\times_{S}{\rm{id}}_{U})=d\}\;,

for any locally Noetherian scheme
U
over
S
.

Now recall that if
{\mathscr{F}}
is a set-valued presheaf on
{\sf Noe}/S
then
{\mathscr{F}}
is said to be
\rm{h}
-representable if there is a scheme

over

, such that the
\rm{h}
-sheafification

of the sheaf

is isomorphic to the
\rm{h}
-sheafification

of the representable presheaf

, see Definition 4.4.1 in
[

]
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
X
be a projective scheme of finite type over

and fix a projective embedding

over

. Then, for any two nonnegative integers

and

A disadvantage of Theorem
2
is in the presence of
\rm{h}
-sheafification. The latter is a retribution for the generality of the representability result. For relative
0
-cycles this obstacle can be avoided as follows.

Recall that we have already defined the category
{\sf Nor}/S
, a full subcategory in
{\sf Sch}/S
generated by schemes over
S
whose structural morphism is normal, i.e. the fibre at every point is a normal scheme, see Definition 36.18.1 in
[
23
]
. Similarly, one can define the notion of a seminormal morphism and introduce a full subcategory
{\sf sNor}/S
generated by locally Noetherian schemes over
S
whose structural morphisms are seminormal, so that we have a chain of subcategories

{\sf Nor}/S\subset{\sf sNor}/S\subset{\sf Noe}/S\;.

For any presheaf
{\mathscr{F}}
on
{\sf Noe}/S
let
{\mathscr{F}}|_{{\sf sNor}/S}
be the restriction of
{\mathscr{F}}
on the subcategory
{\sf sNor}/S
.

To avoid divided powers, suppose that either the base scheme
S
is of pure characteristic
0
or
X
is flat over
S
. Recall that it follows that

\Gamma^{d}(X/S)={\rm{Sym}}^{d}(X/S)

by Corollary 4.2.5 in Paper I in
[
18
]
, and hence one can work with symmetric powers instead of divided ones. By Theorem 3.1.11 on page 30 of the same paper, we have the canonical identifications

(15)

{\mathscr{Y}}_{0,d}(X/S)={\rm{Sym}}^{d}(X/S)\;,

{\mathscr{Y}}_{0}(X/S)=\left(\coprod_{d=0}^{\infty}{\rm{Sym}}^{d}(X/S)\right)\;,

{\mathscr{Y}}_{0}^{\infty}(X/S)={\rm{Sym}}^{\infty}(X/S)\;,

{\mathscr{Z}}_{0}(X/S)=\left(\coprod_{d=0}^{\infty}{\rm{Sym}}^{d}(X/S)\right)^{+}

and

{\mathscr{Z}}_{0}^{\infty}(X/S)={\rm{Sym}}^{\infty}(X/S)^{+}\;.

In other words, we do not need
\rm{h}
-sheafification to prove representability of sheaves of
0
-cycles in Rydh’s terms.

The point here is that, assuming that
S
is semi-normal over
{\rm{Spec}}({\mathbb{Q}})
, after restricting of these five sheaves on the category
{\sf sNor}/S
, we also have the corresponding canonical isomorphisms

(16)

{\mathscr{Y}}_{0,d}(X/S)|_{{\sf sNor}/S}\simeq z^{{\rm{eff}}}_{d}((X,i)/S,0)|_{{\sf sNor}/S}\;,

(17)

{\mathscr{Y}}_{0}(X/S)|_{{\sf sNor}/S}\simeq z^{{\rm{eff}}}(X/S,0)|_{{\sf sNor}/S}\;,

(18)

{\mathscr{Y}}_{0}^{\infty}(X/S)|_{{\sf sNor}/S}\simeq z^{{\rm{eff}}}(X/S,0)_{\infty}|_{{\sf sNor}/S}\;,

(19)

{\mathscr{Z}}_{0}(X/S)|_{{\sf sNor}/S}\simeq z(X/S,0)|_{{\sf sNor}/S}

and

(20)

{\mathscr{Z}}_{0}^{\infty}(X/S)|_{{\sf sNor}/S}\simeq z(X/S,0)_{\infty}|_{{\sf sNor}/S}\;.

Moreover, the same result holds true when we compare Rydh’s sheaves of
0
-cycles with Kollár’s sheaves constructed in Chapter I of the book
[
14
]
. These important comparison results are proven in Section 10 of Paper IV in
[
18
]
.

Thus, since now we will always assume that either the base scheme

is of pure characteristic
0
or

is flat over

, to work with symmetric powers, and in all cases when

will be semi-normal over
{\mathbb{Q}}
, we will systematically identify the restrictions of Suslin-Voevodsky’s and Rydh’s sheaves of
0
-cycles on semi-normal schemes via the isomorphisms (

), (
17
), (
18
), (
19
) and (

).

The Nisnevich sheaf
{\rm{Sym}}^{\infty}(X/S)^{+}
will be now used to construct what then will be the most preferable reincarnation of the space of

-cycles on

over the base scheme

.

5.
Chow atlases on the Nisnevich spaces of
0
-cycles

To consider the sheaf
{\rm{Sym}}^{\infty}(X/S)^{+}
as a geometrical object, we need to endow it with an atlas, in the line of the definitions in Section
2
. The aim of this section is to present a natural atlas, the Chow atlas, on the sheaf of
0
-cycles
{\rm{Sym}}^{\infty}(X/S)^{+}
.

First of all, the sheaf of
0
-cycles possesses a natural inductive structure on it. For each non-negative integer
d
let

\iota_{d}:{\rm{Sym}}^{d}(X/S)\to{\rm{Sym}}^{\infty}(X/S)

be the canonical morphism in to the colimit. For short of notation, let also

{\rm{Sym}}^{d,d}(X/S)={\rm{Sym}}^{d}(X/S)\times_{S}{\rm{Sym}}^{d}(X/S)\;,

{\rm{Sym}}^{\infty,\infty}(X/S)={\rm{Sym}}^{\infty}(X/S)\times_{S}{\rm{Sym}}^{\infty}(X/S)

and let

\iota_{d,d}:{\rm{Sym}}^{d,d}(X/S)\to{\rm{Sym}}^{\infty,\infty}(X/S)

be the fibred product of
\iota_{d}
with itself over
S
. Recall that
{\rm{Sym}}^{\infty}(X/S)^{+}
is the group completion of the monoid
{\rm{Sym}}^{\infty}(X/S)
in the category
{\sf Shv}(({\sf Noe}/S)_{\rm{Nis}})
. It means that the we have a pushout square

\textstyle{{\rm{Sym}}^{\infty}(X/S)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Delta}
\textstyle{{\rm{Sym}}^{\infty,\infty}(X/S)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\sigma_{\infty}}
\textstyle{S\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{{\rm{Sym}}(X/S)^{+}}

in the category
{\sf Mon}({\sf Shv}(({\sf Noe}/S)_{\rm{Nis}}))
. In particular, the quotient morphism
\sigma_{\infty}
is a morphism of monoids, i.e. it respects the monoidal operations in the source and target. Let

\sigma_{d}:{\rm{Sym}}^{d,d}(X/S)\to{\rm{Sym}}^{\infty}(X/S)^{+}

be the composition of the morphisms
\iota_{d,d}
and
\sigma_{\infty}
in the category
{\sf Shv}(({\sf Noe}/S)_{\rm{Nis}})
, and let

{\rm{Sym}}^{d}(X/S)^{+}

be the sheaf-theoretical image of the morphism
\sigma_{d}
, i.e. the image of
\sigma_{d}
in the category
{\sf Shv}(({\sf Noe}/S)_{\rm{Nis}})
.

Some explanation is in place here. A priori, for any nonnegative integer
d
, one can compute the
d
-th symmetric power

S^{d}(X/S)

in the category of presheaves
{\sf PShv}({\sf Noe}/S)
, and the
d
-th symmetric power

{\rm{Sym}}^{d}(X/S)\;,

computed in the category of sheaves
{\sf Shv}(({\sf Noe}/S)_{\rm{Nis}})
, is the Nisnevich sheafification of the presheaf
S^{d}(X/S)
. But since the symmetric power
S^{d}(X/S)
exists already as a scheme in the category
{\sf Noe}/S
, and since the Nisnevich topology is subcanonical, we have that

S^{d}(X/S)={\rm{Sym}}^{d}(X/S)\;,

for any
d\geq 0
.

Let

\coprod_{d=0}^{\infty}S^{d}(X/S)

be the free monoid
{\mathbb{N}}(X/S)
of
X
over
S
computed in the category of presheaves
{\sf PShv}({\sf Noe}/S)
. Since the category
{\sf Noe}/S
is a Noetherian category, one can show that this infinite coproduct is a Nisnevich sheaf, and hence it coincides with the free monoid
{\mathbb{N}}(X/S)
of
X
over
S
computed in the category of sheaves
{\sf Shv}(({\sf Noe}/S)_{\rm{Nis}})
. In other words, there is no difference between
{\mathbb{N}}(X/S)
in
{\sf PShv}({\sf Noe}/S)
and
{\mathbb{N}}(X/S)
in
{\sf Shv}(({\sf Noe}/S)_{\rm{Nis}})
, and we write

{\mathbb{N}}(X/S)=\coprod_{d=0}^{\infty}{\rm{Sym}}^{d}(X/S)=\coprod_{d=0}^{\infty}S^{d}(X/S)\;.

Similarly, let

S^{\infty}(X/S)

be the free connective monoid
{\mathbb{N}}(X/S)_{\infty}
of
X
over
S
computed in the category of presheaves
{\sf PShv}({\sf Noe}/S)
, so that the free connective monoid
{\rm{Sym}}^{\infty}(X/S)
of
X
over
S
, computed in the category of sheaves
{\sf Shv}(({\sf Noe}/S)_{\rm{Nis}})
, is nothing else but the Nisnevich sheafification of
S^{\infty}(X/S)
. Again, as the category
{\sf Noe}/S
is a Noetherian category, one can show that
S^{\infty}(X/S)
is a sheaf in Nisnevich topology, and hence

S^{\infty}(X/S)={\rm{Sym}}^{\infty}(X/S)\;.

This gives us that, if

S^{\infty}(X/S)^{+}

is the group completion of the presheaf free monoid
S^{\infty}(X/S)
in the category
{\sf Mon}({\sf PShv}({\sf Noe}/S))
, i.e. the square

(21)

\textstyle{S^{\infty}(X/S)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Delta}
\textstyle{S^{\infty,\infty}(X/S)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\sigma_{\infty}}
\textstyle{S\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

is co-Cartesian, the sheaf group completion
{\rm{Sym}}^{\infty}(X/S)^{+}
of
{\rm{Sym}}^{\infty}(X/S)
in the category
{\sf Mon}({\sf Shv}(({\sf Noe}/S)_{\rm{Nis}}))
is the sheafification of
S^{\infty}(X/S)^{+}
, i.e.

{\rm{Sym}}^{\infty}(X/S)^{+}=S^{\infty}(X/S)^{+}_{{\rm{a}}}\;.

Lemma 3
.

The presheaf
S^{\infty}(X/S)^{+}
is separated. Equivalently, the canonical morphism

S^{\infty}(X/S)^{+}\to{\rm{Sym}}^{\infty}(X/S)^{+}

is a monomorphism in
{\sf PShv}({\sf Noe}/S)
.

Proof
.
Since
S^{\infty}(X/S)^{+}
is an abelian group object in the category
{\sf PShv}({\sf Noe}/S)
, to prove the lemma it is enough to show that, if

F\in S^{\infty}(X/S)^{+}(U)

is a section of the presheaf
S^{\infty}(X/S)^{+}
on some locally Noetherian scheme
U
over
S
, and if there exists a Nisnevich covering

\{f_{i}:U_{i}\to U\}_{i\in I}\;,

such that the pullback
F_{i}
of the section
F
to
U_{i}
along each morphism
U_{i}\to U
is
0
in the abelian group
S^{\infty}(X/S)^{+}(U_{i})
, then
F
is
0
in the abelian group
S^{\infty}(X/S)^{+}(U)
.

The section
F
can be interpreted as a morphism

F:U\to S^{\infty}(X/S)^{+}\;.

For short of notation, let

S^{\infty,\infty}(X/S)=S^{\infty}(X/S)\times_{S}S^{\infty}(X/S)\;,

and, for any nonnegative integer
d
let

S^{d,d}(X/S)=S^{d}(X/S)\times_{S}S^{d}(X/S)\;.

In these terms, the morphism
F
is the composition of a certain morphism

(f_{1},f_{2}):U\to S^{\infty,\infty}(X/S)\;,

induced by two morphisms of presheaves

f_{1}:U\to S^{\infty}(X/S){\quad\hbox{and}\quad}f_{1}:U\to S^{\infty}(X/S)\;,

and the quotient morphism

\sigma_{\infty}:S^{\infty,\infty}(X/S)\to S^{\infty}(X/S)^{+}\;.

Moreover, there exists
d
, such that both morphisms
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
factorize through
S^{d}(X/S)
, and then
F
is the composition

(22)

U\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(f_{1},f_{2})}}{{\longrightarrow}}S^{d,d}(X/S)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\iota_{d,d}}}{{\longrightarrow}}S^{\infty,\infty}(X/S)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sigma_{\infty}}}{{\longrightarrow}}S^{\infty}(X/S)^{+}\;.

and the morphisms
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
are morphisms of locally Noetherian schemes over the base scheme
S
.

Now, since
S^{\infty}(X/S)
is a cancellative monoid in
{\sf PShv}({\sf Noe}/S)
, the commutative square (
21
) is a Cartesian square in
{\sf PShv}({\sf Noe}/S)
. It follows that, since
F_{i}=0
for all
i\in I
, the images of the compositions

U_{i}\to U\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(f_{1},f_{2})}}{{\longrightarrow}}S^{d,d}(X/S)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\iota_{d,d}}}{{\longrightarrow}}S^{\infty,\infty}(X/S)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sigma_{\infty}}}{{\longrightarrow}}S^{\infty}(X/S)^{+}

are all in the image of the diagonal morphism

\Delta:S^{\infty}(X/S)\to S^{\infty,\infty}(X/S)\;.

And since the morphism

\coprod_{i\in I}U_{i}\to U

is a scheme-theoretical epimorphism, we see that the image of the morphism (
22
) is also in the image of the diagonal morphism
\Delta
. The latter means that the section
F
equals
0
.

Let

\sigma_{d}:S^{d,d}(X/S)\to S^{\infty}(X/S)^{+}

be the composition of the morphisms
\iota_{d,d}
and
\sigma_{\infty}
in the category
{\sf PShv}({\sf Noe}/S)
, and let

S^{d}(X/S)^{+}

be the image of the morphism
\sigma_{d}
in the category
{\sf PShv}({\sf Noe}/S)
. Then
S^{d}(X/S)^{+}
is a sub-presheaf in
{\rm{Sym}}^{\infty}(X/S)^{+}
. As the sheafification functor is exact, it preserves monomorphisms. It follows that

{\rm{Sym}}^{d}(X/S)^{+}=(S^{d}(X/S)^{+})^{{\rm{a}}}\;,

i.e.
{\rm{Sym}}^{d}(X/S)^{+}
is the Nisnevich sheafification of the preshaef
S^{d}(X/S)^{+}
. And, once again, the sheaf-theoretical image
{\rm{Sym}}^{d}(X/S)^{+}
of the morphism
\sigma_{d}
comes together with the epimorphism

(23)

\sigma_{d}:{\rm{Sym}}^{d,d}(X/S)\to{\rm{Sym}}^{d}(X/S)^{+}

in the category
{\sf Shv}(({\sf Noe}/S)_{\rm{Nis}})
.

Next, the section
S\to X
of the structural morphism
X\to S
induces the closed embeddings

{\rm{Sym}}^{d}(X/S)\to{\rm{Sym}}^{d+1}(X/S)\;,

which, in turn, induce the closed embeddings

{\rm{Sym}}^{d,d}(X/S)\to{\rm{Sym}}^{d+1,d+1}(X/S)\;.

The latter morphisms induce the corresponding morphisms

{\rm{Sym}}^{d}(X/S)^{+}\to{\rm{Sym}}^{d+1}(X/S)^{+}

in the category
{\sf Shv}(({\sf Noe}/S)_{\rm{Nis}})
. Then

(24)

{\rm{Sym}}^{d}(X/S)^{+}={{\rm colim}\,}_{d}\;{\rm{Sym}}^{d}(X/S)^{+}\;,

i.e. the space
{\rm{Sym}}^{d}(X/S)^{+}
is naturally the colimit of the spaces
{\rm{Sym}}^{d}(X/S)^{+}
.

Rational curves on the locally ringed site of

Theorem

has the following important implication. Namely, since all the items of Definition 7.31.2 in
[
23
]
are now satisfied, the stack functor

Moreover, the ringed site

is a locally ringed site in the sense of the definition appearing in Exercise 13.9 on page 512 in
[
1
]
(see page 313 in the newly typeset version), as well as in the sense of a sightly different Definition 18.39.4 in
[
23
]
. This is explained in Section
2

For short of notation, let us write

{\mathscr{O}}_{P}={\mathscr{O}}_{{\rm{Sym}}^{\infty}(X/S)^{+}\!,\,P}\;.

This should not lead to a confusion, as the point
P
is a point on
{\rm{Sym}}^{\infty}(X/S)^{+}
. Since the site
{\rm{Sym}}^{\infty}(X/S)^{+}_{\rm{Nis}\mathchar 45\relax\rm{\acute{e}t}}

on this site the stalk

[

]

and the residue field

The stalk functor also gives us the stalks

over the residue field

.

The second vector space

is then our

to the space of
0
-cycles
{\rm{Sym}}^{\infty}(X/S)^{+}
at the point

. Notice that, since

is an abelian group object in the category of Nisnevich sheaves on locally Noetherian schemes over

Now we are fully equipped to promote the idea of understanding of rational equivalence of

. First of all, looking at any scheme

over

as a representable sheaf, we have the corresponding locally ringed site

in the sense of Definition 18.39.9 in
[
23
]

is a morphism of sheaves, then it induces the corresponding morphism of locally ringed sites.

A rational curve on
{\rm{Sym}}^{\infty}(X/S)^{+}
is a morphism of sheaves

If

is a point on the sheaf

, then we will be saying that

passes through the point

if

, as a morphism to

, factorizes through the morphism

Now, two points

Let

where

It is important to understand the action of the isomorphism obtained by composing the isomorphisms (

Recall that symmetric powers can be also defined for objects in an abitrary symmetric monoidal category with finite colimits. Let, for example,
R
be a commutative ring, and let
M
a module over
R

d

{\rm{Sym}}^{d}(M)
of the module

in the category of modules over

can be defined as the quotient of

by the submodule generated over

by the differences

m_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes m_{d}-m_{\sigma(1)}\otimes\dots\otimes m_{\sigma(d)}\;,

where
\sigma\in\Sigma_{d}
. For any collection
\{m_{1},\dots,m_{d}\}
in
M
let
(m_{1},\dots,m_{d})
be the same collection as an element of the
d
-th symmetric power
{\rm{Sym}}^{d}(M)
of the module
M
, i.e. the image of the tensor
m_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes m_{d}
under the quotient homomorphism

M^{\otimes d}\to{\rm{Sym}}^{d}(M)\;.

The image of the injective homomorphism

{\rm{Sym}}^{d}(M)\to M^{\otimes d}\;,

sending
(m_{1},\dots,m_{d})
to the sum

\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{d}}m_{\sigma(1)}\otimes\dots\otimes m_{\sigma(d)}\;,

coincides with submodule of invariants
(M^{\otimes d})^{\Sigma_{d}}
of the action of
\Sigma_{d}
on
M^{\otimes d}
. Therefore, one can identify
{\rm{Sym}}^{d}(M)
with submodule of invariants
(M^{\otimes d})^{\Sigma_{d}}
.

A similar but Koszul dual theory applies to wedge powers, where the wedge power
\wedge^{d}M
can be initially constructed as the quotient of
M^{\otimes d}
by the submodule

in
M^{\otimes d}
generated by the tensors

in which at least two vectors

and
v_{j}
are equal. This all is a folklore and can be found in, for example,
\lx@sectionsign
B.2 in
[
6
]
.

In schematic terms, let
B
be an algebra over a ring

, i.e. one has a ring homomorphism

\phi:A\to B\;,

and let

be the affine morphism induced by the homomorphism

. Then one has the diagonal ring homomorphism

\phi_{d}:A\to B^{\otimes d}\;,

where the
d
-fold tensor product
B^{\otimes d}
is taken over
A
. The homomorphism
\phi_{d}
gives the structural morphism

(X/Y)^{d}\to Y\;,

where
(X/Y)^{d}
is the
d
-fold product of
X
over
Y
. Let
(B^{\otimes d})^{\Sigma_{d}}
be the subring of invariants of the action of the symmetric group. Since the image of
\phi_{d}
is obviously in
(B^{\otimes d})^{\Sigma_{d}}
, we obtain the surjective homomorphism

\phi_{d}^{\prime}:A\to(B^{\otimes d})^{\Sigma_{d}}

induced by
\phi_{d}
. This gives us the decomposition

(X/Y)^{d}\to{\rm{Sym}}^{d}(X/Y)\to Y\;,

where the second morphism is
{\rm{Spec}}(\phi_{d}^{\prime})
.

The multiplication in the
A
-algebra
B
induces the multiplication in
B^{\otimes d}
by the formula

(b_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes b_{d})\cdot(b^{\prime}_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes b^{\prime}_{d})=(b_{1}b^{\prime}_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes b_{1}b^{\prime}_{d})\;.

It is easy to see that if
(b_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes b_{d})
is in
(B^{\otimes d})^{\Sigma_{d}}
and
(b^{\prime}_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes b^{\prime}_{d})
is in
E(B^{\otimes d})
, then the product
(b_{1}b^{\prime}_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes b_{1}b^{\prime}_{d})
is again in
E(B^{\otimes d})
. This is why the above product induces the product

(B^{\otimes d})^{\Sigma_{d}}\otimes\wedge^{d}B\to\wedge^{d}B\;.

If
B
is, moreover, is freely generated of dimension
d
, as an
A
-module, then the determinant

\det:\wedge^{d}B\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}A

is an isomorphism, and we obtain a homomorphism

\psi_{d}:(B^{\otimes d})^{\Sigma_{d}}\longrightarrow A\;,

such that
\phi_{d}
is the section for
\psi_{d}
, thus bringing the section

s_{X/Y,d}:Y\to{\rm{Sym}}^{d}(X/Y)

of the above morphism
{\rm{Sym}}^{d}(X/Y)\to Y
.

Let us now explore the same situation globally. Let

f:X\to Y

be a morphism of schemes over a field
k
. Recall that
f
is said to be affine if and only if
Y
can be covered by affine open subsets

V_{i}={\rm{Spec}}(A_{i})\;,

such that

U_{i}=f^{-1}(V_{i})

is affine for each
i
, so

U_{i}={\rm{Spec}}(B_{i})\;,

and

f|_{U_{i}}:U_{i}\to V_{i}

is induced by the homomorphism

A_{i}\to B_{i}\;,

see page 128 in
[
12
]
. If
f
is affine, then

{\mathscr{B}}=f_{*}{\mathscr{O}}_{X}

is a quasi-coherent sheaf of
{\mathscr{O}}_{Y}
-algebras on
Y
, and

X={\bf{Spec}}({\mathscr{B}})

in the sense of loc.cit. The
d
-fold fibred product of
X
over
Y
is

{\bf{Spec}}({\mathscr{B}}^{\otimes d})\;,

and the structural morphism from
(X/Y)^{\times d}
to
Y
is induced by the homomorphism

\phi_{d}:{\mathscr{O}}_{Y}\to{\mathscr{B}}^{\otimes d}\;,

where the tensor product
{\mathscr{B}}^{\otimes d}
is over
{\mathscr{O}}_{Y}
. The image of
\phi_{d}
is
\Sigma_{d}
-invariant, so that we obtain the homomorphism

\phi_{d}:{\mathscr{O}}_{Y}\to({\mathscr{B}}^{\otimes d})^{\Sigma_{d}}\;.

Then the relative
d
-th symmetric power
{\rm{Sym}}^{d}(X/Y)
exists and in fact

{\rm{Sym}}^{d}(X/Y)={\bf{Spec}}(({\mathscr{B}}^{\otimes d})^{\Sigma_{d}})\;.

The structural morphism

{\rm{Sym}}^{d}(X/Y)\to Y

is induced by the homomorphism
\phi_{d}
above.

Following
[
20
]
, let us now show that there exists also a section of the structural morphism
{\rm{Sym}}^{d}(X/Y)\to Y
, provided
X
is finite surjective of degree
d
over
Y
.

Assume first that
f
is finite and flat. The finiteness of
f
means, by definition, that
f
is affine and
B_{i}
is a finitely generated
A_{i}
-module for each
i
, see page 84 in
[
12
]
. Then
{\mathscr{B}}
is a coherent flat
{\mathscr{O}}_{Y}
-module, with respect to the morphism

{\mathscr{O}}_{Y}\to{\mathscr{B}}=f_{*}{\mathscr{O}}_{X}\;,

and so
{\mathscr{B}}
is a locally free
{\mathscr{O}}_{Y}
-module by Proposition 9.2 (e) on page 254 in
[
12
]
.

Let
W
be an irreducible component of the scheme
X
, and let
V
be the closure of
f(W)
in
Y
. Since
f
is flat,
V
is an irreducible component of
Y
. Moreover, if
\xi
is the generic point of
W
in
X
, then
f(\xi)
is the generic point of
V
in
Y
. Let
d_{\xi}
be the degree
[R(W):R(V)]
, where
R(W)
and
R(V)
stay for the fields of rational functions on
W
and
V
respectively, endowed with the induced reduced closed subscheme structures on them.

We will say that
f:X\to Y
is of constant degree
d
if the degrees
d_{\xi}
are equal to
d
for all irreducible components of the scheme
X
. If
f
is finite flat of constant degree
d
, then
{\mathscr{B}}
is a locally free sheaf of rank
d
on
{\mathscr{O}}_{Y}
, so that one has the determinantal isomorphism

\det:\wedge^{d}{\mathscr{B}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\mathscr{O}}_{Y}\;.

Applying the sheaf-theoretical version of the above local construction, we get the morphism of
{\mathscr{O}}_{Y}
-modules

({\mathscr{B}}^{\otimes d})^{\Sigma_{d}}\otimes_{{\mathscr{O}}_{Y}}\wedge^{d}{\mathscr{B}}\to\wedge^{d}{\mathscr{B}}\;,

where the tensor power
{\mathscr{B}}^{\otimes d}
is taken over
{\mathscr{O}}_{Y}
. For one’s turn, this gives the morphism

({\mathscr{B}}^{\otimes d})^{\Sigma_{d}}\to{\rm{End}}_{{\mathscr{O}}_{Y}}(\wedge^{d}{\mathscr{B}})\;.

Composing it with the above determinantal isomorphism we get the homomorphism of
{\mathscr{O}}_{Y}
-algebras

\psi_{d}:({\mathscr{B}}^{\otimes d})^{\Sigma_{d}}\to{\mathscr{O}}_{Y}\;.

Since
\psi_{d}\circ\phi_{d}={\rm{id}}_{{\mathscr{O}}_{Y}}
we see that
\psi_{d}
induces the canonical section

s_{X/Y,d}:Y\to{\rm{Sym}}^{d}(X/Y)

of the structural morphism

{\rm{Sym}}^{d}(X/Y)\to Y\;.

Following
[
20
]
, assume now that
f
is a finite and surjective (but maybe not flat) morphism of schemes over
k
. For our interests in this paper, it is sufficient to assume that the scheme
X
is integral and the scheme
Y
is normal and connected. Since
X
is integral, it is irreducible. As
f
is surjective,
Y
is irreducible too. Moreover, since
f
is finite, it is affine. As
f
is surjective, locally
f
is a collection of morphisms

\phi^{*}:{\rm{Spec}}(B)\to{\rm{Spec}}(A)\;,

such that
\phi:A\to B
is injective. Since
X
is integral, it is reduced, so that there is no nilpotens in
B
. Then there is also no nilpotens in
A
. Therefore,
Y
is reduced as well. Collecting these small observations we conclude that
Y
is integral.

Now, take any affine open

V={\rm{Spec}}(A)

in
Y
with the preimage

f^{-1}(V)={\rm{Spec}}(B)

in
X
, so that
A
is a subring in
B
, as
f|_{U}
is surjective and both
A
and
B
are integral domains. Since
B
is a finitely generated
A
-module, it follows that
B
is integral over
A
by Proposition 5.1 in
[
2
]
. Then, for any non-zero element
b
in
B
there exists a monic polynomial

x^{n}+a_{n-1}x^{n-1}+\dots+a_{1}x+a_{0}

with coefficients in
A
, such that
b
is a root of it. Without loss of generality one can assume that
a_{0}\neq 0
. Then

1/b=1/a_{0}\cdot(-b^{n-1}-a_{n-1}b^{n-2}-\dots-a_{1})\;.

It means that the localization
B_{(0)}
is a finitely generated
A_{(0)}
-module, i.e.
R(X)
is a finite field extension of
R(Y)
. Let
d
be the degree
[R(X):R(Y)]
.

Let
U
be the set of points
x\in X
, such that
f
is flat at
x
. Then
U
is open in
X
, see 9.4 on page 266 in
[
12
]
. Since both
X
and
Y
are integral,
f
is flat at the generic point of
X
. Therefore, the set
U
is non-empty.

Next, shrink
U
if necessary and assume that it is affine,

U={\rm{Spec}}(B)\;,

which is surjectively mapped onto the affine set
V={\rm{Spec}}(A)
in
Y
. Then

f|_{U}:U\to V

is a finite surjective flat morphism of schemes over the ground field
k
. Since
R(X)
is a flat algebra over
R(Y)
, by the above local construction, we get the homomorphism

\psi_{d}:(R(X)^{\otimes d})^{\Sigma_{d}}\longrightarrow R(Y)\;.

Let now again
{\mathscr{B}}
be the quasi-coherent sheaf
f_{*}{\mathscr{O}}_{X}
of
{\mathscr{O}}_{Y}
-algebras on
Y
, so that

X={\bf{Spec}}({\mathscr{B}})\;.

Let
y
be a point on
Y
. Locally,

y\in V\subset Y\;,

where

V={\rm{Spec}}(A)

and
y
is a prime ideal
{\mathfrak{p}}
in
A
. Let

U=f^{-1}(V)={\rm{Spec}}(B)\;.

By Propositions 5.1 and 5.2 on pages 110 - 111 in
[
12
]
, we have that the stalk
{\mathscr{B}}_{y}
is

((f|_{U})_{*}{\mathscr{O}}_{U})_{y}=((f|_{U})_{*}B)_{{\mathfrak{p}}}=B_{{\mathfrak{p}}}

and

B_{{\mathfrak{p}}}\subset B_{(0)}\;,

i.e.
{\mathscr{B}}_{y}
is canonically embedded into
R(X)
. Respectively,
{\mathscr{B}}_{y}^{\otimes d}
is canonically embedded into
R(X)^{\otimes d}
. The homomorphism
\psi_{d}
is nothing but the homomorphism

\psi_{(0),d}:(B_{(0)}^{\otimes d})^{\Sigma_{d}}\to A_{(0)}\;,

where the tensor product is taken over
A_{(0)}
. As above,
\psi_{(0),d}
has the section

\phi_{(0),d}:A_{(0)}\to(B_{(0)}^{\otimes d})^{\Sigma_{d}}\;,

induced by the canonical homomorphism
A_{(0)}\to B_{(0)}^{\otimes d}
.

Since
A_{{\mathfrak{p}}}
is embedded into
A_{(0)}
and

is embedded into

, we have the homomorphism from
(B_{{\mathfrak{p}}}^{\otimes d})^{\Sigma_{d}}
, where the tensor product is taken over

, to
(B_{(0)}^{\otimes d})^{\Sigma_{d}}
. Certainly, the canonical homomorphism
\phi_{{\mathfrak{p}},d}:A_{{\mathfrak{p}}}\to B_{{\mathfrak{p}}}^{\otimes d}
induces the homomorphism
\phi_{{\mathfrak{p}},d}:A_{{\mathfrak{p}}}\to(B_{{\mathfrak{p}}}^{\otimes d})^{\Sigma_{d}}
, so that we have the obvious commutative diagram

The local homomorphism

of the structural morphism

{\rm{Sym}}^{d}(X/Y)\to Y\;.

Remark 7
.

The section
s_{X/Y,d}
has been achieved specifically for the
d
-th symmetric power of
X
over
Y
, where
d
is the degree of the morphism from
X
onto
Y
. In other circumstances the existence of the section section
s_{X/Y,d}
is not guaranteed at all.

Example 8
.

Let
X
be the affine plane
{\mathbb{A}}^{2}
and
Y
be the cone. The morphism from
{\mathbb{A}}^{2}
onto
Y
is given by the embedding of the ring of symmetric polynomials

k[x^{2},xy,y^{2}]

into the ring
k[x,y]
. In other words, the morphism
X\to Y
glues any two antipodal points into one. Then
s_{X/Y,d}
doesn’t exists for
d=1
, as there is no way to send the vertex of the cone to the plane. But
s_{X/Y,2}
does exist as we can send the vertex to the doubled origin of coordinates as a point of the symmetric square.

Now, let
S
be a scheme of finite type over a field
k
, let
X
be a scheme projective over
S
, and fix a closed embedding

i:X\to{\mathbb{P}}^{n}_{S}

over
S
. In particular,
X
is AF over
S
and all relative symmetric powers
{\rm{Sym}}^{d}(X/S)
exist in
{\sf Noe}/S
. Notice that since
X
is projective over
S
, so is the scheme
{\rm{Sym}}^{d}(X/S)
, for every nonnegative integer
d
.

Let
U
be a noetherian scheme of finite type over
S
and let
Z
be a prime cycle in
z^{{\rm{eff}}}_{d}(X/S,0)(U)
, considered with the induced reduced close subscheme structure on it. Let

f_{Z}:Z\to X\times_{S}U\to U

be the composition of the closed embedding of
Z
in to
X\times_{S}U
with the projection onto
U
.

Since the morphism
f_{Z}
is finite,
f_{Z}
is affine, and hence the relative symmetric powers of
Z/U
exist. Then, as above, we have the canonical section

s_{Z/U,d}:U\to{\rm{Sym}}^{d}(Z/U)

of the structural morphism

{\rm{Sym}}^{d}(Z/U)\to U\;.

The closed embedding

Z\to X\times_{S}U

induces the morphism

{\rm{Sym}}^{d}(Z/U)\to{\rm{Sym}}^{d}(X\times_{S}U/U)\;,

and we also have the obvious morphism

{\rm{Sym}}^{d}(X\times_{S}U/U)\to{\rm{Sym}}^{d}(X/S)\;.

Composing all these morphisms, we obtain the morphism

\theta_{X/S}(U,Z):U\to{\rm{Sym}}^{d}(X/S)

over
S
.

The morphisms
\theta_{X/S}(U,Z)
for degrees
d^{\prime}\leq d
extend by linearity and induce a map

\theta_{X/S,d}(U):z^{{\rm{eff}}}_{d}((X,i)/S,0)(U)\to{\rm Hom}_{S}(U,{\rm{Sym}}^{d}(X/S))\;.

The latter maps for all schemes
U
yield a morphism of set valued presheaves

\theta_{X/S,d}:z^{{\rm{eff}}}_{d}((X,i)/S,0)\to{\rm Hom}_{S}(-,{\rm{Sym}}^{d}(X/S))

on
{\sf Noe}/S
.

Assume now that
S
is semi-normal over
{\mathbb{Q}}
. We claim that the restriction of the morphism
\theta_{X/S,d}
on seminormal schemes is exactly the isomorphism obtained by composing the isomorphisms (
15
) and (
16
) considered in Section
4
.

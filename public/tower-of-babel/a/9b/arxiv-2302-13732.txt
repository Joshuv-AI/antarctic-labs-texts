Non-integrability of the Painlevé IV equation in the Liouville-Arnold sense and Stokes phenomena
Tsvetana Stoyanova

Abstract
. In this paper we study the integrability of the Hamiltonian system associated to the fourth Painlevé equation. We prove that one two parametric family of this Hamiltonian system is non integrable in the sense of the Liouville-Arnold theorem. Computing explicitly the Stokes matrices and the formal invariants of the second variational equations we deduce that the connected component of the unit element of corresponding differential Galois group is not Abelian. Thus the Morales-Ramis-Simó theory leads to a non-integrable result. Moreover, combining the new result with our previous one we establish that for all values of the parameters for which the P I ​ V P_{IV} equation has a particular rational solution the corresponding Hamiltonian system is not integrable by meromorphic first integrals which are rational in t t .

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Non-intagrability and differential Galos theory

In this paragraph we briefly recall the theory of Morales-Ruiz, Ramis and Simó about non-integrability of
Hamiltonian systems following
[
20
,
21
]
.

Let
M
be a symplectic analytical complex manifold of complex dimension
2n
. Consider on
M
a Hamiltonian system

\displaystyle\dot{x}=X_{H}(x)

(2.2)

with a Hamiltonian
H:M\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
. Recall that by the theorem of Liouville-Arnold
[
2
]
the Hamiltonian system (
2.2
)
is completely integrable in the Louville-Arnold sense if there exist
n
first integrals

f_{1}=H,f_{2},\ldots,f_{n}
functionally independent and in involution.
Let
x(t)
be a particular solution of (
2.2
) which is not an equilibrium point of the Hamiltonian vector field
X_{H}
. Denote by

\Gamma
the phase curve corresponding to this solution. Then the first variational equations along
\Gamma
are given by

\displaystyle\dot{\xi}=\frac{\partial X_{H}}{\partial x}(x(t))\,\xi\,.

(2.3)

Using the Hamiltonian
H
we can reduce the variational equation (
2.3
) in the following sense. Consider the normal bundle
of
\Gamma
on the level variety
M_{h}=\{x\,|\,H(x)=h\}
. The projection of the variational equation (
2.3
) on this
bundle is the so called normal variational equation
(\textrm{NVE})
. The dimension of the
NVE
is
2n-2
.
The solutions of the
NVE
define a Picard-Vessiot extension
L_{1}
of the differential field
K
of the coefficients of

NVE
. This in its turn defines a differential Galois group
G_{1}=Gal(L_{1}/K)
.
Then the main theorem of the Morales-Ruiz and Ramis theory states
[
20
,
21
]

Theorem 2.1
.

(
Morales-Ruiz and Ramis
)

If the Hamilton system (
2.2
) is completely integrable with meromorphic first integrals in a
neighbourhood of
\Gamma
, not necessarily independent on
\Gamma
itself, then the identity component
G_{1}^{0}
of
the differential Galois group
G_{1}=Gal(L_{1}/K)
is Abelian.

The opposite is not true in general, i.e. if the connected component of the unit element of the differential
Galois group
Gal(L_{1}/K)
is Abelin, it is not sure that the corresponding Hamiltonian system (
2.2
) is integrable.
To overcome this problem Morales-Ruiz, Ramis and Simó propose to use higher order variational equations
[
21
]
.
Let again
x(t)
be a particular solution of (
2.2
) which is not an equilibrium point of the vector field
X_{H}
.
We write the general solution as
x(t,z)
, where
z
parametrizes it near
x(t)
as
x(t,z_{0})=x(t)
. Then we write
the system (
2.2
) as

\displaystyle\dot{x}(t,z)=X_{H}(x(t,z))\,.

(2.4)

Denote by
x^{(1)}(t,z),x^{(2)}(t,z),\ldots
the derivatives of
x(t,z)
with resect to
z
and by

X_{H}^{(1)}(x),X_{H}^{(2)}(x),\ldots
the derivatives of
X_{H}(x)
with respect to
x
. By successive derivation of
(
2.4
) with respect to
z
and evaluation at
z_{0}
we obtain the so called
k
-th variational equations

(\textrm{VE})_{k}
for the function
x(t)

\displaystyle\qquad\dot{x}^{(k)}(t)=X_{H}^{(1)}(x(t))\,x^{(k)}(t)+P\left(x^{(1)}(t),x^{(2)}(t),\ldots,x^{(k-1)}(t)\right)\,.

(2.5)

Here
P
is a polynomial. The coefficients depend on
t
through
X_{H}^{(j)}(x(t),j<k
. For every
k>1
the linear non-homogeneous
system (
2.5
) can be arranged as a linear homogeneous system of higher dimension. This chain of
linear homogeneous systems defines a chain of successive Picard-Vessiot extensions of the main differential field
K
of the
coefficients of
NVE
, i.e. we have

K\subset L_{1}\subset L_{2}\subset\cdots\subset L_{k}
, where
L_{1}
is as above,
L_{2}
is the Picard-Vessiot extension of
K

associated with
(\textrm{VE})_{2}
, etc.
We can define the Galois groups
G_{1}=Gal(L_{1}/L),G_{2}=Gal(L_{2}/K),\ldots
. Then the main theorem in the Morales-Ramis-Simó
theory states
[
21
]

Theorem 2.2
.

(
Morales-Ruiz, Ramis and Simó
)

If the Hamiltonian system (
2.2
) is completely integrable then for every
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
the connected component of
the unit element
G_{m}^{0}
of the Galois group
G_{m}=Gal(L_{m}/K)
is Abelian.

Assume that
G_{1}^{0}
is Abelian.
Then the theorem Theorem
2.2
says that if we want to obtain a non-integrable result we have to find a group

G_{m}^{0},m>1
which is not Abelian. Note that this group will be a solvable group (see
[
21
]
for details).
In this case the non-integrability in the sense of Hamiltonian dynamics corresponds to integrability in the
Picard-Vessiot sense.

2.2.
The fundamental matrix solution of the
(\textrm{NVE})_{2}

The main result of this paper is based on the study of the differential Galois group of the second normal variational
equations since the differential Galois group of the first variational equations is Abelian.
Throughout this paper we assume that
K={\mathbb{C}}(z)

the homogeneous linear system associated with

of the corresponding differential Galois groups

[
29

33

It turns out that the

For this reason from here to the end of this section we

\displaystyle L\,y=\left(L_{2}\circ L_{1}\right)\,y=0,\quad L_{2}\circ L_{1}\neq L_{1}\circ L_{2}\,,

(2.6)

\displaystyle L_{1}=\partial^{2}+a_{1}(z)\,\partial+a_{0}(z),\quad L_{2}=\partial^{2}+b_{1}(z)\,\partial+b_{0}(z)

2.3.
The differential Galois group of the
(\textrm{NVE})_{2}

In this paragraph we review some definitions, facts and notation from the theory of linear ordinary differential equations,
as well as, from the Picard-Vessiot theory which are required to describe the differential Galois group of
one equation of the kind (
2.6
). Most of them can be found in
[
27
,
29
,
33
]
.
Throughout this paragraph we assume that the equation (
2.6
)
has only two singular points over
{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
: one non-resonant irregular singularity
at the origin of Poincaré rank 1 and one regular singularity at
z=\infty
.

We start by describing the local differential Galois group at the origin.
The restriction of the theorem of Hukuhara-Turrittin-Wasow
[
34
]
gives us

Theorem 2.7
.

Assume that the equation (
2.6
) has a non-resonant singularity at the origin.
Then the fundamental matrix solution
\Phi(z)
from (
2.5
) is represented at the origin as

\displaystyle\hat{\Phi}_{0}(z)=\hat{H}(z)\,z^{\Lambda}\,\exp\left(\frac{Q}{z}\right)\,,

(2.16)

where
\Lambda
and
Q
are constant diagonal matrices. The entries of the matrix
\hat{H}(z)
are usually
divergent power series.

The formula (
2.16
) is usually regarded as a formal fundamental matrix solution at the origin.
To introduce the formal invariants of the equation (
2.6
) we consider the equation (
2.6
) and its
formal fundamental matrix solution
\hat{\Phi}(z)
from (
2.16
) over the field
{\mathbb{C}}((z))
of formal power series
in
z
.

Definition 2.8
.

With respect to the formal fundamental matrix solution
\hat{\Phi}_{0}(z)
given by (
2.16
) we define the formal monodromy
matrix
\hat{M}_{0}\in GL_{4}({\mathbb{C}})
around the origin as

\,\hat{\Phi}_{0}(z.e^{2\pi\,i})=\hat{\Phi}_{0}(z)\,\hat{M}_{0}\,.\,

In particular,

\,\hat{M}_{0}=e^{2\pi\,i\,\Lambda}\,.\,

Denote
\Lambda={\mathrm{diag}}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3},\lambda_{4})
and
Q={\mathrm{diag}}(q_{1},q_{2},q_{3},q_{4})
.

Definition 2.9
.

With respect to the formal fundamental matrix solution
\hat{\Phi}_{0}(z)
given by (
2.16
) we define the exponential torus

{\mathcal{T}}
as the differential Galois group
Gal(E/F)
, where

F={\mathbb{C}}((z))(z^{\lambda_{1}},z^{\lambda_{2}},z^{\lambda_{3}},z^{\lambda_{4}})
and
E=F(e^{q_{1}/z},e^{q_{2}/z},e^{q_{3}/z},e^{q_{4}/z})
.

Since for
\sigma\in{\mathcal{T}}
we have that
\sigma(z^{\lambda})=z^{\lambda}
and
\sigma(e^{q/z})=c\,e^{q/z},\,c\in{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
we can
consider
{\mathcal{T}}
as a subgroup of
({\mathbb{C}}^{*})^{4}
. The formal monodromy
\hat{M}_{0}
and the exponential torus

2.6

(see Theorem 1.4.9 in
[
29
]
).

To introduce the analytic invariants at the origin we consider the equation (
2.6
) and its solutions over the field

{\mathbb{C}}(\{z\})
of convergent power series in
z
.
A fourth order linear ordinary differential equation (
2.6
) with a non-resonant irregular singularity at the
origin of Poincaré rank 1 admits a local fundamental set of solutions at the origin in the form

\displaystyle y_{1}(z)=z^{l_{1}}\,e^{\frac{q_{1}}{z}}\,\hat{\varphi}_{1}(z),\quad y_{2}(z)=z^{l_{2}}\,e^{\frac{q_{2}}{z}}\,\hat{\varphi}_{2}(z),

\displaystyle y_{3}(z)=z^{l_{3}}\,e^{\frac{q_{3}}{z}}\,\hat{\varphi}_{3}(z),\quad y_{4}(z)=z^{l_{4}}\,e^{\frac{q_{4}}{z}}\,\hat{\varphi}_{4}(z)\,,

where

.

Under the above notations for every divergent power series

\,\Theta_{j}=\{\theta_{ji},\,0\leq\theta_{ji}<2\pi\}\,

The fourth Painlevé equation (

\displaystyle\frac{2\,(i\,\sqrt{3})^{5/2}\,\sqrt{\pi}}{3}\,\exp\left(\frac{i\,\sqrt{3}}{3z}\right)=\mu_{2}\,y_{1}(z)\,.

Theorem 3.9
.

Proof.

{\mathcal{T}}
and the Stokes matrices

St_{3\pi/2}

0&0&1&0\\

where

the Zariski closure of the subgroup generated by the exponential

{\mathcal{T}}

\tau_{c}

Lemma 3.10

The equation

possesses a local fundamental set of solutions near the origin in the form

\,w_{1}(x)=x^{3/2}\,u_{1}(x),\qquad w_{2}(x)=x^{-3/2}\,u_{2}(x)\,,\,

where

are holomorphic functions near the origin. In particular,

\,u_{1}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{2k}\,x^{2k},\quad a_{0}=1,\qquad u_{2}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}b_{2k}\,x^{2k},\quad b_{0}=1\,,\,

where the coefficients
a_{2k}
and
b_{2k}
satisfy the recurrence relations

\displaystyle 3k\,(2k+3)\,a_{2k}+2a_{2k-2}=0,\qquad 3k\,(2k-3)\,b_{2k}+2b_{2k-2}=0\,,

(3.41)

respectively.

Proof.

The characteristic equation at
x=0
for the equation

\,L_{2}\,y=y^{\prime\prime}+\frac{1}{x}\,y^{\prime}+\left[-\frac{9}{4x^{2}}+\frac{4}{3}\right]\,y=0\,

\,\rho\,(\rho-1)+\rho-\frac{9}{4}=0\,.\,

Its roots are
\rho_{1}=-\frac{3}{2}
and
\rho_{2}=\frac{3}{2}
. The local theory of regular (Fuchsian)
singularity ensures that the equation

admits a local solution
w_{1}(x)
near the origin in the
form

where

is a holomorphic function near the origin. The function

must satisfies the equation

\,3x\,u^{\prime\prime}_{1}(x)+12\,u^{\prime}_{1}(x)+4x\,u_{1}(x)=0\,.\,

Obviously the wanted function

is an even function in

. Looking

as a power series

\,u_{1}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{2k}\,x^{2k}=a_{0}+a_{2}\,x^{2}+a_{4}\,x^{2k}+\cdots+a_{2n-2}\,x^{2n-2}+a_{2n}\,x^{2n}+\cdots\,

we find that the coefficients

must satisfy the recurrence relation (
3.41
).

Since
\rho_{2}-\rho_{1}\in{\mathbb{N}}
the second solution

at the origin can contain logarithmic term. It turns out that

u_{1}(x)
forbids the existence of the logarithmic term. Indeed,

w_{2}(x)
depends on the solution
w_{1}(x)
by the formula (see
[
11

for details)

\,w_{2}(x)=w_{1}(x)\,\int\frac{e^{-\int\frac{dx}{x}}}{w^{2}_{1}(x)}\,dx=w_{1}(x)\int\frac{dx}{x^{4}\,u^{2}_{1}(x)}\,.\,

u_{1}(x)

1/u^{2}_{1}(x)
. Therefore the solution

As above we see that the function

\,u_{2}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}b_{2k}\,x^{2k}=b_{0}+b_{2}\,x^{2}+b_{4}\,x^{2k}+\cdots+b_{2n-2}\,x^{2n-2}+b_{2n}\,x^{2n}+\cdots\,

This ends the proof.

Reconstruction algorithms for source term recovery from dynamical samples in catalyst models
A. Aldroubi, L. Gong, I. Krishtal, B. Miller, S. Thareja

Abstract
. This paper investigates the problem of recovering source terms in abstract initial value problems (IVP) commonly used to model various scientific phenomena in physics, chemistry, economics, and other fields. We consider source terms of the form F = h + η F=h+\eta , where η \eta is a Lipschitz continuous background source. The primary objective is to estimate the unknown parameters of non-instantaneous sources h ⁡ ( t ) = ∑ j = 0 M h j ​ e − ρ j ​ ( t − t j ) ​ χ [ t j , ∞ ) ​ ( t ) h(t)=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{M}h_{j}e^{-\rho_{j}(t-t_{j})}\chi_{[t_{j},\infty)}(t) , such as the decay rates, initial intensities and activation times. We present two novel recovery algorithms that employ distinct sampling methods of the solution of the IVP. Algorithm 1 combines discrete and weighted average measurements, whereas Algorithm 2 uses a different variant of weighted average measurements. We analyze the performance of these algorithms, providing upper bounds on the recovery errors of the model parameters. Our focus is on the structure of the dynamical samples used by the algorithms and on the error guarantees they yield.

1.
Introduction

Numerous scientific phenomena in physics, chemistry, economics, and other fields can be effectively modeled using abstract initial value problems (IVP) such as:

(1)

\begin{cases}\dot{u}(t)=Au(t)+F(t)\\
u(0)=u_{0},\end{cases}\quad t\in\mathbb{R}_{+},\ u_{0}\in\mathcal{H}.

The above problem typically describes propagation of a phenomenon in time, represented by the variable
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
.
The function
u
in (
1
) is vector-valued; in this paper, we shall assume that
u(t)
is a vector in some Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. Most often,
\mathcal{H}
will be a space of functions on a subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, such as
L^{2}([0,1]^{d})
.
We then have that
\dot{u}:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathcal{H}
is the time derivative of
u
and
F:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
is the forcing term. The operator
A:D(A)\subseteq\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
is assumed to be a generator of a strongly continuous semigroup
T:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to B(\mathcal{H})
, where
B(\mathcal{H})
denotes the Banach algebra of all bounded linear operators on
\mathcal{H}
.

One common example of (
1
) is the diffusion equation, which is widely used to model, for instance, the dispersion of biochemical wastes or the spread of fungal diseases
[
19
,
25
,
26
]
. In these cases, the vector
u(t)
describes the concentration of the released substance in various spatial locations at time
t
, thus giving the researchers a model for substance propagation across both space and time. Problem (
1
) may also describe an ongoing chemical reaction or a combined effect of various medications over time. In these cases, the vector
u(t)
may be finite dimensional and describe the amounts of various chemicals in the system at time
t
.

To study scientific phenomena, researchers commonly utilize sensors to gather spatiotemporal measurements within a specified area. Within the above framework, the gathered measurements are samples of the solution
u
of (
1
) and the goal of study is to determine more information about
u
itself or about other parameters of (
1
). Thus effective and robust sampling and reconstruction methods greatly influence various practical applications and new approaches may lead to significant advances.

We place the above sampling-reconstruction problem within the framework of dynamical sampling. The framework encompasses a range of problems in which various samples of a signal
u
evolving over time under the influence of a linear operator
A
are used to recover various aspects of the system, such as
u_{0}
,
A
or
F.
Recovering the initial condition
u_{0}
is known as the space-time trade-off sampling problem (see
[
3
]
). Recovering the linear operator
A
gives rise to the system identification problem (see, e.g.,
[
6
,
7
,
13
,
30
]
). The problem investigated in this paper falls within the realm of identifying specific types of source terms
F
that drive the dynamical system
[
4
,
5
]
. Additionally, dynamical sampling problems are closely connected to multiple branches of mathematics, such as frame theory, control theory, functional analysis, and harmonic analysis (see
[
1
,
2
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
,
18
,
22
,
23
,
24
,
27
,
28
]
).

In this paper, we will only consider sources of the form
F=h+\eta
, where
\eta
is a Lipschitz continuous background source which is of no interest to us. In some cases, we are concerned with the classical solutions of (
1
) and will additionally assume that
\eta
belongs to the Sobolev space
W^{1,1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathcal{H})
, which consists of all functions
f:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
such that both
f
and its weak first derivative are Lebesgue integrable on compact subsets of
\mathbb{R}_{+}

[
21
]
.

We will use various samples of
u
collected from sensors to solve the problem of estimating certain parameters of
h.

In the literature
[
4
,
5
,
29
]
, two types of the sources
h
are typically considered: instantaneous sources (
h(t)=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{M}h_{j}\delta(t-t_{j})
) and non-instantaneous sources that exhibit exponential decay in intensity over time after activation (
h(t)=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{M}h_{j}e^{-\rho_{j}(t-t_{j})}\chi_{[t_{j},\infty)}(t)
). The reconstruction of instantaneous sources has been thoroughly investigated in
[
5
]
, where accurate estimates of both the initial intensities and activation times have been obtained. The scenario involving non-instantaneous sources with a known uniform decay rate (i.e., where
\rho_{j}=\rho
for all
j
is known) has been extensively explored in
[
4
]
. In this paper, we will expand the study of non-instantaneous sources to estimate all of their parameters. Our focus here will be on non-instantaneous sources with varying unknown decay rates, which we would like to estimate together with their likewise unknown initial intensities and activation times.

Thus, the problem we study can be formalized as follows:

(2)

\begin{cases}\dot{u}(t)=Au(t)+\sum\limits_{j=0}^{M}h_{j}e^{-\rho_{j}(t-t_{j})}\chi_{[t_{j},\infty)}(t)+\eta(t)\\
u(0)=u_{0},\\
\end{cases}\quad t\in\mathbb{R}_{+},\ u_{0}\in\mathcal{H}.

The parameters
\rho_{j}\in[\check{\rho},\hat{\rho}]
,
t_{j}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
h_{j}\in\mathcal{H}
,
j=0,1,\ldots,M
, are unknown and to be recovered (subject to known bounds
0<\check{\rho}\leq\hat{\rho}
). As mentioned above,
\eta:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathcal{H}
is a Lipschitz continuous background source term, which is assumed to belong to
W^{1,1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathcal{H})
whenever necessary.

As the goal of this paper includes recovering of the decay rates
\rho_{j}
, the approach employed in our previous work
[
4
]
will not be applicable in this context.
In particular, the samples of
u
we utilize here will be different from
[
4
]
. In fact, we shall present two recovery algorithms that use different kinds of samples.
In Algorithm 1, we will employ a novel approach that combines discrete and weighted average measurements of
u
.
Algorithm 2 uses slightly different weighted average measurements of
u
, this approach mimics that of
[
5
]
.

Algorithm 1 studies classical solutions and has the prerequisite that the initial condition
u_{0}
must belong to the domain
D(A)
, which is a dense set within the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}.
Algorithm 2 can be applied in a broader context of mild solutions of (
2
). Both algorithms improve estimates in
[
4
]
for the case when
\check{\rho}=\hat{\rho}
.

1.2.
IVP toolkit

Here we remind the reader a few basic facts of operator semigroup theory and use them to provide a solution formula for IVPs of the form (
2
). We refer to
[
20
]
for more information.

A strongly continuous operator semigroup is a map
T:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow B(\mathcal{H})
, which satisfies

(i)

T(0)=I
,

(ii)

T(t+s)=T(t)T(s)
for all
t,s\geq 0
, and

(iii)

\|T(t)x-x\|\rightarrow 0
as
t\rightarrow 0
for all
x\in\mathcal{H}
.

The operator
A
is the (infinitesimal) generator of the semigroup
T
if, given

D(A)=\left\{x\in\mathcal{H}:\ \lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{t}(T(t)x-x)\mbox{ exists}\right\},

A
satisfies

Ah=\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{t}(T(t)x-x),\ x\in D(A).

For a strongly continuous semigroup, the domain
D(A)
of its generator
A
is known to be a dense subset of
\mathcal{H}
.

According to
[
20
, p. 436]
the (mild) solution of (
1
) can be represented as

(3)

u(t):=T(t)u_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}T(t-s)F(s)ds.

Therefore, the solution of (
2
) can be written as

(4)

u(t)=T(t)u_{0}+\sum\limits_{t_{j}<t}\int_{t_{j}}^{t}T(t-s)h_{j}e^{-\rho_{j}(s-t_{j})}ds+\int_{0}^{t}T(t-s)\eta(s)ds,\quad t\geq 0.

Moreover, if we take
u_{0}\in D(A)
and
\eta\in W^{1,1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathcal{H}),
then (
4
) is the unique classical solution of (
2
), i.e.
u\in C^{1}([0,\mathcal{T}],\mathcal{H})
for any
\mathcal{T}>0
.

2.
Reconstruction algorithms

In this section, we present two reconstruction algorithms for Problem (
2
) and some key ideas these algorithms are based upon. The algorithms’ derivation and error guarantees will be presented in the following section.

Recall that in (
2
) we represented the source term
F
of (
1
) as a sum of two terms
F=h+\eta
, where
\eta
is a background source term and
h
is the term of interest, for which we would like to recover its key features.

We assume that the background source
\eta
is Lipschitz continuous:

(5)

\|\eta(t+s)-\eta(t)\|\leq Ls\quad\forall t,s\in\mathbb{R}_{+}.

Furthermore, in Algorithm 1, we are interested in the classical solutions of (
2
) and additionally assume that
\eta

belongs to the Sobolev space
W^{1,1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathcal{H})
.

As evidenced by (
2
), the function
h

is of the form

(6)

h(t)=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{M}h_{j}e^{-\rho_{j}(t-t_{j})}\chi_{[t_{j},\infty)}(t).

Each term in the above sum can model, for example, a new substance entering a chemical reaction. With this motivation, we will refer to these terms as catalysts; vector
h_{j}\in\mathcal{H}
can be thought of as the content of the
j
-th catalyst,
t_{j}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
– as the time of its intake, and the exponent
\rho_{j}
– as its rate of decay in the system.

Thus, our algorithms are designed to recover the unknown times
t_{j}
of catalyst intake, the rates of decay
\rho_{j}
, and certain ingredients in the catalyst contents
h_{j}
, which will be represented by a set of inner products
\{\langle h_{j},g\rangle:g\in\mathcal{G}\}
for some known set
\mathcal{G}\subset\mathcal{H}
.

To accomplish their purpose, our algorithms use different kinds of weak measurements of the solution of (
2
). Although the designs of the measurements are different, in both cases, they are based on the same key idea. They combine information about the current state of the system with a prediction of its future state on condition that no new catalyst intake occurs in between. Consequently, thresholding certain linear combinations of the measurements allows us to determine (with a reasonable degree of accuracy) if a catalyst with ingredients of interest has entered the system in a given time period. Once a new catalyst has been detected, relatively simple computations with the measurements provide estimates for its decay rate and content. The technical difficulties of this research stem from the fact that we need to carefully select the thresholds and other parameters of the algorithms to provide meaningful error guarantees.

Before we proceed to the description of the algorithms let us add a few more fairly natural assumptions.

We assume that there is a known uniform bound
H
on the mass of each catalyst:

(7)

\sup_{j}\|h_{j}\|\leq H.

The set
\mathcal{G}
, which we choose to detect the catalyst ingredients, is likewise selected to be uniformly bounded:

R=\sup_{g\in\mathcal{G}}\|g\|<\infty.

We also assume that intake times of consecutive catalysts are separated:

(8)

t_{j+1}-t_{j}\geq 4\beta+D,

where
\beta
is the time-step parameter chosen by the user of the algorithms and
D>0
is a known parameter of the system. Clearly, a bigger value of
D
leads to a smaller effect of previous catalysts on the system at time of the new catalyst intake, which results in better error guarantees.

Additionally, we assume that we know some positive bounds
\check{\rho}
and
\hat{\rho}
on the minimal and maximal possible rates of catalyst decay:
\rho_{j}\in[\check{\rho},\hat{\rho}].

2.1.
Description of Algorithm 1.

This algorithm relies on a combination of discrete samples of the solution of (
2
) taken at times
n\beta
and weighted average samples over the periods of time in between the discrete samples. Specifically, Algorithm 1 uses the following numbers as inputs:

(9)

\mathfrak{m}_{n}(g,\beta)=\langle u((n+1)\beta),\frac{g}{\beta}\rangle-\langle u(n\beta),\frac{g}{\beta}\rangle-\int_{n\beta}^{(n+1)\beta}\langle u(t),A^{*}\frac{g}{\beta}\rangle dt+\nu_{n},\quad g\in\mathcal{G},

where
\mathcal{G}\subseteq D(A^{*})
and

\nu_{n}
represents bounded additive noise:

(10)

|\nu_{n}|\leq\sigma.

For determining the rates of decay,
Algorithm 1 also uses a second set of measurements at a finer time scale with the step size
\tilde{\beta}=\frac{\beta}{N}
:

(11)

\mathfrak{s}_{n}(g,\widetilde{\beta})=\langle u(n\beta+\widetilde{\beta}),\frac{g}{\widetilde{\beta}\beta}\rangle-\langle u(n\beta),\frac{g}{\widetilde{\beta}\beta}\rangle-\langle u(n\beta),A^{*}\frac{g}{\beta}\rangle+\widetilde{\nu}_{n},\quad g\in\mathcal{G},

where
\widetilde{\nu}_{n}
is the measurement noise satisfying
|\widetilde{\nu}_{n}|\leq\sigma
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
is a parameter chosen by the user that influences the accuracy of recovery.

Remark 2.1
.

We note that in this setting the upper bound
\sigma
on measurement noises is assumed to be independent on the time-step parameters
\beta
and
\widetilde{\beta}
. The noiseless part of the measurements, however, is amplified as
\beta
and
\widetilde{\beta}
decrease. In other words, we assume that measurement devices are designed to provide a higher signal-to-noise ratio when the time step
\beta
(and/or
\widetilde{\beta}
) is smaller. We justify the practicality of this setup by stipulating that expending the same amount of energy over a shorter period of time should indeed lead to more accurate measurements by a device.

Remark 2.2
.

Algorithm 1 deals with the classical solution of
(
2
). To guarantee its existence (see Section
1.2
), we make assumptions on the initial condition
u_{0}
to belong to the domain
D(A)
of the generator
A
and the background source
\eta
to belong to the Sobolev space
W^{1,1}_{loc}
. Working with the classical solution is necessary to effectively use the measurements
(
11
) as approximations of samples of the solution’s derivative
\dot{u}
.

2.2.
Description of Algorithm 2.

Just as the previous algorithm, this one also focuses on detecting catalysts one by one. In this case, however, we wish to make a more explicit use of this fact. To this end, we lump the previous catalysts together with the background source and write

(12)

F(t)=h_{j}e^{\rho_{j}(t_{j}-t)}\chi_{[t_{j},\infty)}(t)+\tilde{\eta}(t)

where

(13)

\tilde{\eta}(t)=\sum_{i<j}h_{i}e^{\rho_{i}(t_{i}-t)}\chi_{[t_{i},\infty)}(t)+\eta(t).

Algorithm 2 is designed to detect a new catalyst entering the system after the time
\ell_{0}\beta
, where
\ell_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
is chosen by the user of the algorithm.
For each time-step
\ell\beta>\ell_{0}\beta>t_{j-1}
, the function
\tilde{\eta}
in (
13
) is Lipschitz on
[\ell\beta,\infty)
with (local) Lipschitz constant estimated by

L_{\ell}=L+\frac{H\hat{\rho}e^{(\ell_{0}-\ell)\beta}}{1-e^{-\check{\rho}(4\beta+D)}}\leq L+\frac{H\hat{\rho}}{1-e^{-\check{\rho}D}},

where
L
is, as before, the (global) Lipschitz constant of the original background source
\eta
(see Appendix for the proof).

Algorithm 2 uses a single set of weighted average samples of the solution of (
2

(14)

m_{s,\ell}(g,\beta)=\int_{\ell\beta}^{(\ell+1)\beta}e^{-st}\langle u(t),(\overline{s}I-A^{*})\frac{g}{\beta^{2}}\rangle\;dt+\nu_{s,\ell},\ \ell\in\mathbb{Z},

and the term

represents the measurement noise, which is assumed to satisfy

. Once again, the noise bound
\sigma
is assumed to be independent of
\beta

3.
Derivation of algorithms and their guaranties

In this section, we present theorems outlining performance guarantees for Algorithms 1 and 2. The derivation of the algorithms will become apparent in the process of proving the theorems.

3.1.
Approximation Bounds for the Source Recovery by Algorithm 1

We begin with a theorem that collects theoretical error guarantees for Algorithm 1.

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume
u_{0}\in D(A).
Let
\beta
be the sampling time step and, given a parameter
D>0
, assume a minimal distance
t_{j+1}-t_{j}\geq D+4\beta
between any two consecutive times

t_{j+1}
and
t_{j}
of catalyst intake. Assume also that
\sup_{j}\|h_{j}\|\leq H,

\sup_{g\in\mathcal{G},}\|g\|\leq R
, and
\rho_{j}\in[\check{\rho},\hat{\rho}]
for some known bounds
\hat{\rho}\geq\check{\rho}>0
.
Let
\mathfrak{t}_{j}
,
\bar{\rho}_{j}
, and
\mathfrak{f}_{j}(g)
be the outputs of Algorithm 1. Then
|\mathfrak{t}_{j}-t_{j}|\leq\beta
and

(15)

|\mathfrak{f}_{j}(g)-\langle h_{j},g\rangle|\leq\widetilde{Q}(g,\beta)+v_{2}(h_{j},g,\beta)+\frac{HR}{e^{\check{\rho}D}-1}(1-e^{-3\hat{\rho}\beta})+3L\beta\|g\|+2\sigma+2Q(g,\beta)

where
Q(g,\beta)=K\widetilde{Q}(g,\beta)

is the threshold in Algorithm 1 given by (
17
) and
v_{2}(h_{j},g,\beta)
– by (
22
) below.

Additionally, if the output
\mathfrak{f}_{j}(g)
satisfies
|\mathfrak{f}_{j}(g)|>\widetilde{Q}(g,\beta)
, then the relative error of the decay rate
\rho_{j}
is bounded by

(16)

\left|\frac{\rho_{j}-\bar{\rho}_{j}}{\rho_{j}}\right|\leq\frac{1}{{|\mathfrak{f}_{j}(g)|}}\left(\frac{HR}{e^{\check{\rho}D}-1}(1-e^{-3\hat{\rho}\beta})\frac{\hat{\rho}-\check{\rho}}{\check{\rho}}+L\|g\|(\frac{2}{\check{\rho}}+3\beta)+\frac{\epsilon(\widetilde{\beta})}{\check{\rho}}+4\frac{\sigma}{\check{\rho}}+2\sigma\right),

where
\widetilde{\beta}=\frac{\beta}{N}
for a user-chosen parameter
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
\epsilon({\widetilde{\beta}})
represents the approximation error when estimating
u_{t}(t)
at some given time. Moreover, we have
\epsilon(\widetilde{\beta})\to 0
as
N\to\infty.

The following remark is intended to clarify the quality of the estimates in the above theorem.

Remark 3.2
.

(1)

When
\beta
is sufficiently small, the error in (
15
) will be smaller than
7\sigma
.

(2)

In the ideal scenario (where there is no background source and no measurement noise), the estimate in (
16
) reduces to

\left|\frac{\rho_{j}-\bar{\rho}_{j}}{\rho_{j}}\right|\leq\frac{1}{|\mathfrak{f}_{j}(g)|}\left(\alpha\frac{\hat{\rho}-\check{\rho}}{\check{\rho}}\mathbf{e}(3\beta)+\frac{\epsilon(\widetilde{\beta})}{\check{\rho}}\right),

where
\mathbf{e}(t)=1-e^{-\hat{\rho}t}
and
\alpha=\frac{1}{e^{\check{\rho}D}-1}HR.

(3)

The term
\frac{L}{\check{\rho}}
in (
16
) reflects the ability of Algorithm 1 to distinguish between the background source term and the catalysts
h_{j}e^{-\rho_{j}(\cdot-t_{j})}\chi_{[t_{j},\infty)}
,
j=1,2,\ldots
. The relative error improves as
\frac{L}{\check{\rho}}
decreases. On the other hand, when
L
is close to
\check{\rho}
, the background source and the catalysts behave similarly and are difficult to distinguish.

3.2.
Details of Algorithm 1

To detect the time of a catalyst intake in the presence of measurement noise and the background source, Algorithm 1 utilizes thresholds denoted by
Q(g,\beta)
for
g\in\mathcal{G}
and
\beta>0
. They are defined by

(17)

Q(g,\beta):=K\widetilde{Q}(g,\beta)=K\left((\alpha+H\|g\|)\mathbf{e}(\beta)+L\beta\|g\|+2\sigma\right),

where
\alpha=\frac{1}{e^{\check{\rho}D}-1}HR,

\mathbf{e}(t)=1-e^{-\hat{\rho}t},
and
K\geq 1
is a fixed parameter chosen by the user of the algorithm. As before,
L
is the Lipschitz constant of the background source
\eta
as given in (
5
),
\sigma
represents the upper bound for the sampling noise in (
10
), and
\beta
is the time step size in sampling scheme.

To see how the threshold is derived, we employ the following notation:

f_{n}\in\mathcal{H}
and
\tau_{n}\in[n\beta,(n+1)\beta)
for each
n
as follows:

(18)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}f_{n}=h_{j},&\tau_{n}=t_{j},&\text{if the $j$-th intake time occurred in }[n\beta,(n+1)\beta);\\
f_{n}=0,&\tau_{n}=n\beta,&\text{if no new source term emerged in }[n\beta,(n+1)\beta).\end{array}\right.

Using (
18
) and (
2
), we rewrite the measurements in (
9
) as

(19)

\begin{split}\mathfrak{m}_{n}(g,\beta)&=\sum\limits_{\tau_{k}<n\beta}\langle f_{k},g\rangle e^{-\rho_{k}(n\beta-\tau_{k})}\frac{e^{-\rho_{k}\beta}-1}{-\rho_{k}\beta}+\langle f_{n},g\rangle\frac{e^{-\rho_{n}((n+1)\beta-\tau_{n})}-1}{-\rho_{n}\beta}\\
&\quad+\int_{0}^{\beta}\langle\eta(n\beta+t),\frac{g}{\beta}\rangle dt+\nu_{n}.\end{split}

To estimate the catalyst intake time
t_{j}
, we use the quantity
\Delta_{n}(g)=\mathfrak{m}_{n}-\mathfrak{m}_{n-1}
rather than
\mathfrak{m}_{n}
alone. Considering this difference allows us to mitigate the effect of the background source on the process. Indeed, if there is no new catalyst entering the system during the time interval
[n\beta,(n+1)\beta)
(i.e.,
f_{n}=0
as in (
18
)), then

\begin{split}\Delta_{n}(g)&=\mathfrak{m}_{n}-\mathfrak{m}_{n-1}\\
&=\sum\limits_{\tau_{k}<(n-1)\beta}\langle f_{k},g\rangle\frac{e^{-\rho_{k}\beta}-1}{-\rho_{k}\beta}(e^{-\rho_{k}(n\beta-\tau_{k})}-e^{-\rho_{k}((n-1)\beta-\tau_{k})})\\
&\quad+\int_{0}^{\beta}\langle\eta(n\beta+t)-\eta((n-1)\beta+t),\frac{g}{\beta}\rangle dt+\nu_{n}-\nu_{n-1}\\
&=\sum\limits_{\tau_{k}<(n-1)\beta}\langle f_{k},g\rangle\frac{e^{-\rho_{k}\beta}-1}{-\rho_{k}\beta}e^{-\rho_{k}((n-1)\beta-\tau_{k})}(e^{-\rho_{k}\beta}-1)\\
&\quad+\int_{0}^{\beta}\langle\eta(n\beta+t)-\eta((n-1)\beta+t),\frac{g}{\beta}\rangle dt+\nu_{n}-\nu_{n-1}.\end{split}

Using the intake separation assumption (
8
), we only analyze
|\Delta_{n}(g)|
for intervals
[n\beta,(n+1)\beta)
that are at least
(3+\lfloor\frac{D}{\beta}\rfloor)\beta
behind the previously estimated intake time
t_{j-1}
. Because of this fact and the assumption that no new catalyst enters in
[n\beta,(n+1)\beta)
, we obtain the following estimate for
|\Delta_{n}(g)|
:

(20)

\begin{split}|\Delta_{n}(g)|&\leq\left|\sum\limits_{\tau_{k}<(n-1)\beta}\langle f_{k},g\rangle\frac{e^{-\rho_{k}\beta}-1}{-\rho_{k}\beta}e^{-\rho_{k}((n-1)\beta-\tau_{k})}(e^{-\rho_{k}\beta}-1)\right|\\
&\quad+\left|\int_{0}^{\beta}\langle\eta(n\beta+t)-\eta((n-1)\beta+t),\frac{g}{\beta}\rangle dt\right|+|\nu_{n}-\nu_{n-1}|\\
&\leq\sum\limits_{\tau_{k}<(n-1)\beta}|\langle f_{k},g\rangle e^{-\rho_{k}((n-1)\beta-\tau_{k})}|\mathbf{e}(\beta)+\|\eta(n\beta+t)-\eta((n-1)\beta+t)\|\|g\|+2\sigma\\
&\leq\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\|f_{k}\|\|g\|e^{-\check{\rho}kD}\mathbf{e}(\beta)+L\beta\|g\|+2\sigma\\
&\leq\frac{e^{-\check{\rho}D}}{1-e^{-\check{\rho}D}}\mathbf{e}(\beta)HR+L\beta\|g\|+2\sigma\\
&\leq\frac{1}{e^{\check{\rho}D}-1}\mathbf{e}(\beta)HR+L\beta\|g\|+2\sigma\\
&=\alpha\mathbf{e}(\beta)+L\beta\|g\|+2\sigma\end{split}

where
\alpha=\frac{1}{e^{\check{\rho}D}-1}HR
and
\mathbf{e}(\beta)=1-e^{-\hat{\rho}\beta}.

The last term of the set of inequalities above partially justifies our choice of the thresholds
Q(g,\beta)
in (
17
).

On the other hand, in the event of a new catalyst entering the system within the time interval
[n\beta,(n+1)\beta)
(i.e.,
f_{n}=h_{j}\neq 0
), it’s crucial that if it cannot be detected by
\Delta_{n}(g)
or
\Delta_{n+1}(g)
, it stays undetectable in all subsequent measurements. Otherwise, it could lead to a substantial error in the estimation of the intake time. Thus, we need to ensure
\Delta_{i}(g)
remain sufficiently small for
i\geq n+2
until the following intake time
t_{j+1}.
We estimate

(21)

\begin{split}|\Delta_{i}(g)|&=\left|\sum\limits_{\tau_{k}<(n-1)\beta}\langle f_{k},g\rangle\frac{e^{-\rho_{k}\beta}-1}{-\rho_{k}\beta}e^{-\rho_{k}((i-1)\beta-\tau_{k})}(e^{-\rho_{k}\beta}-1)\right|\\
&\quad+\left|\langle h_{j},g\rangle\frac{e^{-\rho_{k}\beta}-1}{-\rho_{k}\beta}e^{-\rho_{k}((i-1)\beta-t_{j})}(e^{-\rho_{k}\beta}-1)\right|\\
&\quad+\left|\int_{0}^{\beta}\langle\eta(i\beta+t)-\eta((i-1)\beta+t),\frac{g}{\beta}\rangle dt\right|+\left|\nu_{i}-\nu_{i-1}\right|\\
&\leq\alpha\mathbf{e}(\beta)+\|f_{j}\|\|g\|(1-e^{-\hat{\rho}\beta})+L\beta\|g\|+2\sigma\\
&\leq\alpha\mathbf{e}(\beta)+H\|g\|\mathbf{e}(\beta)+L\beta\|g\|+2\sigma:=\widetilde{Q}(g,\beta).\end{split}

The above set of inequalities serves as justification for our selection of the thresholds
\widetilde{Q}(g,\beta)
and completely justifies (
17
).

If the value of
|\Delta_{n}(g)|
is larger than the threshold
Q
, then we know that a new catalyst entered the system in the time interval
[(n-1)\beta,(n+1)\beta)
. The occurrence of this case doesn’t guarantee, however, that
t_{j}
is within the interval
[n\beta,(n+1)\beta)
. It is possible that
t_{j}
belongs to the interval
[(n-1)\beta,n\beta)
with
t_{j}
sufficiently close to
n\beta
. Nevertheless, in both scenarios, Algorithm 1 records estimates
\mathfrak{t}_{j}(g)
for the intake time
t_{j}
as
n\beta.
There is an extra intricacy that occurs due to the fact that there might be instances where
\mathfrak{t}_{j}(g_{i_{1}})=n\beta
and
\mathfrak{t}_{j}(g_{i_{2}})=(n+1)\beta
for distinct
g_{i_{1}},g_{i_{2}}\in\mathcal{G}
. To handle this, Algorithm 1 defines
\mathfrak{t}_{j}:=\min\limits_{g\in\mathcal{G}}\mathfrak{t}_{j}(g)
. As a result, we get the error estimate
|t_{j}-\mathfrak{t}_{j}|\leq\beta.

Next, We use the samples before and after the interval of intake to estimate the parameter values of the catalyst.
From the above discussion, it is clear that given any
g\in\mathcal{G}
, the detection of
h_{j}
is only possible through either
\Delta_{n}(g)
or
\Delta_{n+1}(g)
.
In particular, if the values of
|\Delta_{n}(g)|
and
|\Delta_{n+1}(g)|
are smaller than or equal to the threshold
Q
for each
g\in\mathcal{G}
, the output of Algorithm 1 will indicate that no new catalyst entered the system during the period. It may, however, be the case that
the value
|\langle h_{j},g\rangle|
happened to be too small for the ingredient to be detected. Thus, to establish estimate (
15
) for the error of recovery of
\langle h_{j},g\rangle
with
t_{j}\in[n\beta,(n+1)\beta)
, we need to distinguish between the following three outcomes of Algorithm 1:

Cases:

(1)

|\Delta_{n}(g)|>Q,
so that Algorithm 1 sets
\mathfrak{t}_{j}(g)=n\beta.

(2)

|\Delta_{n}(g)|\leq Q
and
|\Delta_{n+1}(g)|>Q
, so that Algorithm 1 sets

\mathfrak{t}_{j}(g)=(n+1)\beta.

(3)

|\Delta_{n}(g)|\leq Q
and
|\Delta_{n+1}(g)|\leq Q
, so that Algorithm 1 does not detect the intake (for example if
\|h_{j}\|
is too small).

From the point of view of the algorithm itself, however, Cases (1) and (2) are indistinguishable. Moreover, for the estimate (
16
) to be meaningful, the estimates
\mathfrak{f}_{j}(g)
of the quantities
\langle h_{j},g\rangle
,
g\in\mathcal{G}
, should have a lower bound, which Algorithm 1 sets as
\widetilde{Q}(g,\beta)
defined in (
21
). Thus, Algorithm 1 defines
\mathfrak{f}_{j}(g)
by

\mathfrak{f}_{j}(g):=\begin{cases}\mathfrak{m}_{n+1}(g,\beta)-\mathfrak{m}_{n-2}(g,\beta),\ \text{Case (1) with }\mathfrak{m}_{n+1}(g,\beta)-\mathfrak{m}_{n-2}(g,\beta)>\widetilde{Q}(g,\beta),\\
\mathfrak{m}_{n+2}(g,\beta)-\mathfrak{m}_{n-1}(g,\beta),\ \text{Case (2) with }\mathfrak{m}_{n+2}(g,\beta)-\mathfrak{m}_{n-1}(g,\beta)>\widetilde{Q}(g,\beta),\\
0,\ \text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Once
\mathfrak{f}_{j}(g)\neq 0
, Algorithm 1 chooses
\widetilde{g}
with the minimal norm among all such
g\in\mathcal{G}
and approximates the derivative of
u
near the intake time using the measurements
\mathfrak{s}_{i}(\widetilde{g},\frac{\beta}{N})
.
More precisely, Algorithm 1 uses the quantity
\widetilde{\Delta}_{i}=\mathfrak{s}_{i+1}(\widetilde{g},\frac{\beta}{N})-\mathfrak{s}_{i}(\widetilde{g},\frac{\beta}{N})
and estimates
the decay rate
\rho_{j}
by
\bar{\rho}_{j}:=\left|\frac{\widetilde{\Delta}_{i+1}-\widetilde{\Delta}_{i-2}}{\mathfrak{f}_{j}(\widetilde{g})}\right|
.

The reasons behind choosing the above estimates for
\langle h_{j},g\rangle
and
\rho_{j}
will be revealed in the following subsection.

3.3.
Proof of Theorem
3.1

The proof of Theorem
3.1
has two major parts. In Subsection
3.3.1
, we estimate the quantity
\langle h_{j},g\rangle
, whereas subsection
3.3.2
covers the approximation of the decay rates
\rho_{j}
.

3.3.1.
Derivation of (
15
) in Theorem
3.1

We start with the following Lemma.

Lemma 3.3
.

Assume that
t_{j}\in[n\beta,(n+1)\beta)
and

(22)

v_{k}(h_{j},g,\beta)=\left|\langle h_{j},g\rangle e^{-\rho_{j}((n+k)\beta-t_{j})}\frac{e^{-\rho_{j}\beta}-1}{-\rho_{j}\beta}-\langle h_{j},g\rangle\right|,\quad k=1,2.

Then

v_{k}(h_{j},g,\beta)\to 0
as
\beta\to 0.

Proof.

Observe that due to
0<\frac{e^{-\rho_{j}\beta}-1}{-\rho_{j}\beta}<1
we have

(23)

\begin{split}v_{k}(h_{j},g,\beta)&=|\langle h_{j},g\rangle|\left(1-e^{-\rho_{j}((n+k)\beta-t_{j})}\frac{e^{-\rho_{j}\beta}-1}{-\rho_{j}\beta}\right)\\
&\leq\|h_{j}\|\|g\|\left(1-e^{-\rho_{j}((n+k)\beta-t_{j})}\frac{e^{-\rho_{j}\beta}-1}{-\rho_{j}\beta}\right).\end{split}

Additionally,
0<(n+k)\beta-t_{j}\leq 2\beta

and

\frac{e^{-\rho_{j}\beta}-1}{-\rho_{j}\beta}\to 1
as
\beta\to 0.

Therefore,

0<1-e^{-\rho_{j}((n+k)\beta-t_{j})}\frac{e^{-\rho_{j}\beta}-1}{-\rho_{j}\beta}\leq 1-e^{-2\rho_{j}\beta}\frac{e^{-\rho_{j}\beta}-1}{-\rho_{j}\beta}\to 0\text{ as }\beta\to 0.

Hence
v_{k}(h_{j},g,\beta)\to 0
as
\beta\to 0.

∎

Remark 3.4
.

In view of the equality in (
23
), the
inequality
(n+2)\beta-t_{j}>(n+1)\beta-t_{j}
implies that
v_{1}(h_{j},g,\beta)\leq v_{2}(h_{j},g,\beta)
.

Next, we will analyze the three cases described in Subsection
3.2
. Recall that we assumed
that the
j
-th intake time
t_{j}
occurred within the time interval
[n\beta,(n+1)\beta)
(i.e.,
f_{n}=h_{j}
) and
Q=K\widetilde{Q}(g,\beta)=K((\alpha+H\|g\|)\mathbf{e}(\beta)+L\beta\|g\|+2\sigma)
, where
K\geq 1
, is the threshold given by (
17
).

For Case (1), we obtain the following result.

Lemma 3.5
.

Assume
|\Delta_{n}(g)|=|\mathfrak{m}_{n}-\mathfrak{m}_{n-1}|>Q.
If
|\mathfrak{m}_{n+1}-\mathfrak{m}_{n-2}|>\widetilde{Q},

\langle h_{j},g\rangle
can be approximated by
\mathfrak{f}_{j}(g)=\mathfrak{m}_{n+1}-\mathfrak{m}_{n-2}
and we have

(24)

\begin{split}|\mathfrak{f}_{j}(g)-\langle h_{j},g\rangle|\leq v_{1}(h_{j},g,\beta)+\alpha\mathbf{e}(3\beta)+3L\beta\|g\|+2\sigma,\end{split}

where
v_{1}(h_{j},g,\beta)
is given in Lemma
3.3
. Otherwise, we set
\mathfrak{f}_{j}(g)=0
and

(25)

\begin{split}|\langle h_{j},g\rangle|\leq\widetilde{Q}(g,\beta)+v_{1}(h_{j},g,\beta)+\alpha\mathbf{e}(3\beta)+3L\beta\|g\|+2\sigma.\end{split}

Proof.

Observe that using (
19
) we have

(26)

\begin{split}\mathfrak{m}_{n+1}-\mathfrak{m}_{n-2}&=\sum\limits_{\tau_{k}<(n-2)\beta}\langle f_{k},g\rangle\frac{e^{-\rho_{k}\beta}-1}{-\rho_{k}\beta}e^{-\rho_{k}((n-2)\beta-\tau_{k})}(e^{-3\rho_{k}\beta}-1)\\
&+\langle h_{j},g\rangle e^{-\rho_{j}((n+1)\beta-t_{j})}\frac{e^{-\rho_{j}\beta}-1}{-\rho_{j}\beta}\\
&+\int_{0}^{\beta}\langle\eta((n+1)\beta+t)-\eta((n-2)\beta+t),\frac{g}{\beta}\rangle dt+\nu_{n+1}-\nu_{n-2}.\end{split}

Therefore, if
|\mathfrak{m}_{n+1}-\mathfrak{m}_{n-2}|>\widetilde{Q},
then
\mathfrak{f}_{j}(g)=\mathfrak{m}_{n+1}-\mathfrak{m}_{n-2}
, and we get

(27)

\begin{split}|\mathfrak{f}_{j}(g)-\langle h_{j},g\rangle|&\leq\left|\sum\limits_{\tau_{k}<(n-2)\beta}\langle f_{k},g\rangle\frac{e^{-\rho_{k}\beta}-1}{-\rho_{k}\beta}e^{-\rho_{k}((n-2)\beta-\tau_{k})}(e^{-3\rho_{k}\beta}-1)\right|\\
&\quad+\left|\langle h_{j},g\rangle e^{-\rho_{j}((n+1)\beta-t_{j})}\frac{e^{-\rho_{j}\beta}-1}{-\rho_{j}\beta}-\langle h_{j},g\rangle\right|\\
&\quad+\left|\int_{0}^{\beta}\langle\eta((n+1)\beta+t)-\eta((n-2)\beta+t),\frac{g}{\beta}\rangle dt\right|+\left|\nu_{n+1}-\nu_{n-2}\right|\\
&\leq v_{1}(h_{j},g,\beta)+\alpha\mathbf{e}(3\beta)+3L\beta\|g\|+2\sigma,\end{split}

where
v_{1}(h_{j},g,\beta)=\left|\langle h_{j},g\rangle e^{-\rho_{j}((n+1)\beta-t_{j})}\frac{e^{-\rho_{j}\beta}-1}{-\rho_{j}\beta}-\langle h_{j},g\rangle\right|
is given in Lemma
3.3
and we estimated other terms as in (
20
).

If
|\mathfrak{m}_{n+1}-\mathfrak{m}_{n-2}|\leq\widetilde{Q},
then
\mathfrak{f}_{j}(g)=0
, and using (
26
) we get

\begin{split}|\langle h_{j},g\rangle|&\leq|\mathfrak{m}_{n+1}-\mathfrak{m}_{n-2}|+|(\mathfrak{m}_{n+1}-\mathfrak{m}_{n-2})-\langle h_{j},g\rangle|\\
&\leq\widetilde{Q}(g,\beta)+v_{1}(h_{j},g,\beta)+\alpha\mathbf{e}(3\beta)+3L\beta\|g\|+2\sigma.\end{split}

The lemma is proved.
∎

Through similar computations, we can establish a lemma pertaining to Case (2).

Lemma 3.6
.

Assume
|\Delta_{n}(g)|=|\mathfrak{m}_{n}-\mathfrak{m}_{n-1}|\leq Q
and
|\Delta_{n+1}(g)|=|\mathfrak{m}_{n+1}-\mathfrak{m}_{n}|>Q.
If
|\mathfrak{m}_{n+1}-\mathfrak{m}_{n-2}|>\widetilde{Q},

\langle h_{j},g\rangle
can be approximated by
\mathfrak{f}_{j}(g)=\mathfrak{m}_{n+2}-\mathfrak{m}_{n-1}
and we have

(28)

\begin{split}|\mathfrak{f}_{j}(g)-\langle h_{j},g\rangle|\leq v_{2}(h_{j},g,\beta)+\alpha\mathbf{e}(3\beta)+3L\beta\|g\|+2\sigma,\end{split}

where
v_{2}(h_{j},g,\beta)
is given in Lemma
3.3
. Otherwise, we set
\mathfrak{f}_{j}(g)=0
and

(29)

\begin{split}|\langle h_{j},g\rangle|\leq\widetilde{Q}(g,\beta)+v_{2}(h_{j},g,\beta)+\alpha\mathbf{e}(3\beta)+3L\beta\|g\|+2\sigma.\end{split}

Finally, Case (3) is covered by the following lemma.

Lemma 3.7
.

If
|\Delta_{n}(g)|\leq Q
and
|\Delta_{n+1}(g)|\leq Q,
let
\mathfrak{f}_{j}(g)=0,
then we have

(30)

|\mathfrak{f}_{j}(g)-\langle h_{j},g\rangle|\leq v_{1}(h_{j},g,\beta)+\alpha\mathbf{e}(2\beta)+2L\beta\|g\|+2\sigma+2Q(g,\beta).

Proof.

In this case, neither
|\Delta_{n}(g)|
nor
|\Delta_{n+1}(g)|
is capable of detecting
h_{j}
; thus, we can conclude that
\langle h_{j},g\rangle
is small and is of the order of the threshold. We set
\mathfrak{f}_{j}(g)
to 0 in this case, the following expression gives the error:

(31)

\begin{split}|\langle h_{j},g\rangle|&\leq|\langle h_{j},g\rangle-(\mathfrak{m}_{n+1}-\mathfrak{m}_{n-1})|+|\mathfrak{m}_{n+1}-\mathfrak{m}_{n-1}|\\
&\leq\left|\sum\limits_{\tau_{k}<(n-1)\beta}\langle f_{k},g\rangle\frac{e^{-\rho_{k}\beta}-1}{-\rho_{k}\beta}e^{-\rho_{k}((n-1)\beta-\tau_{k})}(e^{-2\rho_{k}\beta}-1)\right|\\
&\quad+\left|\langle h_{j},g\rangle e^{-\rho_{j}((n+1)\beta-t_{j})}\frac{e^{-\rho_{j}\beta}-1}{-\rho_{j}\beta}-\langle h_{j},g\rangle\right|\\
&\quad+\left|\int_{0}^{\beta}\langle\eta((n+1)\beta+t)-\eta((n-1)\beta+t),\frac{g}{\beta}\rangle dt\right|+\left|\nu_{n+1}-\nu_{n-1}\right|\\
&\quad+|\mathfrak{m}_{n+1}-\mathfrak{m}_{n}|+|\mathfrak{m}_{n}-\mathfrak{m}_{n-1}|\\
&\leq v_{1}(h_{j},g,\beta)+\alpha\mathbf{e}(2\beta)+2L\beta\|g\|+2\sigma+2Q(g,\beta),\end{split}

where we made a similar estimate as in (
27
) and used the fact that
|\Delta_{n}|=|\mathfrak{m}_{n}-\mathfrak{m}_{n-1}|\leq Q(g,\beta)
and
|\Delta_{n+1}(g)|=|\mathfrak{m}_{n+1}-\mathfrak{m}_{n}|\leq Q(g,\beta)
.
∎

Combining Lemmas
3.3
,
3.5
,
3.6
, and
3.7
with Remark
3.4
, we obtain (
15
).

3.3.2.
Recovery of Decay Rate.

Let us assume that an intake time
t_{j}
has been determined by
\Delta_{n}(g)
(i.e.,
|\Delta_{n}(g)|>Q(g,\beta)
). According to Algorithm 1,
\langle h_{j},g\rangle\approx\mathfrak{f}_{j}(g)=\mathfrak{m}_{n+1}-\mathfrak{m}_{n-2}
. In order to estimate the decay rate
\rho_{j}
, approximate values of
u_{t}((n-2)\beta),

u_{t}((n-1)\beta),

u_{t}((n+1)\beta)
, and
u_{t}((n+2)\beta)
are used by Algorithm 1. As we can only obtain noisy measurements of
u(t),
we use them to approximate
u_{t}(t)
; we expect the error of this approximation to be reasonable because
u\in C^{1}([0,\mathcal{T}],\mathcal{H})
. Consider
u_{t}(n\beta)
for example. We will fix
g
and
\beta
, and then we divide the time interval
[n\beta,(n+1)\beta]
into
N
subintervals of length
\widetilde{\beta}=\frac{\beta}{N}
(
N\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
) and consider
\frac{u(n\beta+\widetilde{\beta})-u(n\beta)}{\widetilde{\beta}}
as an approximation of
u_{t}(n\beta).
Observe that

\begin{split}\langle u_{t}(n\beta),\frac{g}{\beta}\rangle&=\langle\frac{u(n\beta+\widetilde{\beta})-u(n\beta)}{\widetilde{\beta}},\frac{g}{\beta}\rangle-\epsilon_{n}(\widetilde{\beta})\\
&=\langle u(n\beta+\widetilde{\beta})-u(n\beta),\frac{g}{\widetilde{\beta}\beta}\rangle-\epsilon_{n}(\widetilde{\beta})\end{split}

where
|\epsilon_{n}(\widetilde{\beta})|\to 0
as
\widetilde{\beta}\to 0
(i.e.
N\to\infty
). Therefore at
t=n\beta,
(
2
) implies

(32)

\begin{split}&\quad\langle u(n\beta+\widetilde{\beta})-u(n\beta),\frac{g}{\widetilde{\beta}\beta}\rangle-\langle u(n\beta),A^{*}\frac{g}{\beta}\rangle\\
&=\sum\limits_{\tau_{k}\leq n\beta}\langle f_{k},g\rangle\frac{e^{-\rho_{k}(n\beta-\tau_{k})}}{\beta}+\langle\eta(n\beta),\frac{g}{\beta}\rangle+\epsilon_{n}(\widetilde{\beta}).\end{split}

Under the assumption that
|\Delta_{n}(g)|>Q(g,\beta)
, using the expression (
32
), we evaluate the quantities
\widetilde{\Delta}_{n+1}(g)
,
\widetilde{\Delta}_{n-2}(g)
defined in Algorithm 1 and their difference from the measurements (
11
).

\begin{split}\widetilde{\Delta}_{n+1}(g)&=\mathfrak{s}_{n+2}-\mathfrak{s}_{n+1}\\
&=\sum\limits_{\tau_{k}<(n+1)\beta}\langle f_{k},g\rangle\frac{e^{-\rho_{k}((n+2)\beta-\tau_{k})}-e^{-\rho_{k}((n+1)\beta-\tau_{k})}}{\beta}+\langle\eta((n+2)\beta)-\eta((n+1)\beta),\frac{g}{\beta}\rangle\\
&\quad+\epsilon_{n+2}(\widetilde{\beta})-\epsilon_{n+1}(\widetilde{\beta})+\widetilde{\nu}_{n+2}-\widetilde{\nu}_{n+1}\\
&=\sum\limits_{\tau_{k}<(n+1)\beta}\langle f_{k},g\rangle e^{-\rho_{k}((n+1)\beta-\tau_{k})}\frac{e^{-\rho_{k}\beta}-1}{\beta}+\langle\eta((n+2)\beta)-\eta((n+1)\beta),\frac{g}{\beta}\rangle\\
&\quad+\epsilon_{n+2}(\widetilde{\beta})-\epsilon_{n+1}(\widetilde{\beta})+\widetilde{\nu}_{n+2}-\widetilde{\nu}_{n+1},\end{split}

\begin{split}\widetilde{\Delta}_{n-2}(g)&=\mathfrak{s}_{n-1}-\mathfrak{s}_{n-2}\\
&=\sum\limits_{\tau_{k}<(n-2)\beta}\langle f_{k},g\rangle e^{-\rho_{k}((n-2)\beta-\tau_{k})}\frac{e^{-\rho_{k}\beta}-1}{\beta}+\langle\eta((n-1)\beta)-\eta((n-2)\beta),\frac{g}{\beta}\rangle\\
&\quad+\epsilon_{n-1}(\widetilde{\beta})-\epsilon_{n-2}(\widetilde{\beta})+\widetilde{\nu}_{n-1}-\widetilde{\nu}_{n-2},\end{split}

and

\begin{split}\widetilde{\Delta}_{n+1}(g)-\widetilde{\Delta}_{n-2}(g)&=\sum\limits_{\tau_{k}<(n-2)\beta}\langle f_{k},g\rangle\frac{e^{-\rho_{k}\beta}-1}{\beta}e^{-\rho_{k}((n-2)\beta-\tau_{k})}(e^{-3\rho_{k}\beta}-1)\\

3.5.
Details of Algorithm 2: Prony-Laplace Method

Recall that Algorithm 2 uses measurements given by
(

These measurement values can be rewritten in a more practical form, as demonstrated by the following lemma, the proof of which can
be found in the Appendix.

Lemma 3.10
.

Assume
A
is the generator of a
C_{0}
-semigroup and
u_{0}\in\mathcal{H}
. If
t_{j}\in[\ell\beta,(\ell+1)\beta]
where
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
and
s=2\pi ik/\beta
for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, then

(39)

\begin{split}m_{s,\ell}(g,\beta)&=\frac{e^{-st_{j}}-e^{\rho_{j}(t_{j}-(\ell+1)\beta})}{(\rho_{j}+s)\beta^{2}}\langle h_{j},g\rangle\\
&+\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}\int_{\ell\beta}^{(\ell+1)\beta}e^{-st}\langle\tilde{\eta}(t),g\rangle\;dt+\nu_{s,\ell}(g)+\langle\varphi(\ell,\beta,t_{j}),\frac{g}{\beta^{2}}\rangle.\end{split}

Similarly, if
t_{j}<\ell\beta
, then

(40)

\begin{split}m_{s,\ell}(g,\beta)&=\frac{e^{\rho_{j}(t_{j}-\ell\beta)}-e^{\rho_{j}(t_{j}-(\ell+1)\beta})}{(\rho+s)\beta^{2}}\langle h_{j},g\rangle\\
&+\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}\int_{\ell\beta}^{(\ell+1)\beta}e^{-st}\langle\tilde{\eta}(t),g\rangle\;dt+\nu_{s,\ell}(g)+\langle\tilde{\varphi}(\ell,\beta,t_{j}),\frac{g}{\beta^{2}}\rangle.\end{split}

A particularly important feature of these quantities is that the values
\varphi
and
\tilde{\varphi}
do not depend on the value of
k
defining
s=2\pi ik/\beta
.

Algorithm 2 makes use of the values
\Delta_{s,\ell}(g,\beta)=m_{s,\ell}(g,\beta)-m_{0,\ell}(g,\beta)
, which, in lieu of Lemma
3.10
, are given by

(41)

\begin{split}\Delta_{s,\ell}(g,\beta)&=m_{s,\ell}(g,\beta)-m_{0,\ell}(g,\beta)\\
&=\frac{\rho_{j}(e^{-st_{j}}-1)+s(e^{\rho_{j}(t_{j}-(\ell+1)\beta)}-1)}{\rho_{j}(\rho_{j}+s)\beta^{2}}\langle h_{j},g\rangle+\varepsilon_{s,\ell}(g,\beta)\end{split}

when
\ell\beta\leq t_{j}\leq(\ell+1)\beta
, and

(42)

\begin{split}\Delta_{s,\ell}(g,\beta)&=m_{s,\ell}(g/\beta^{2})-m_{0,\ell}(g/\beta^{2})\\
&=s\frac{e^{\rho_{j}(t_{j}-(\ell+1)\beta)}-e^{\rho_{j}(t_{j}-\ell\beta)}}{\rho_{j}(\rho_{j}+s)\beta^{2}}\langle h_{j},g\rangle+\varepsilon_{s,\ell}(g,\beta)\end{split}

when
t_{j}<\ell\beta
. The error term
\varepsilon_{s,\ell}(g,\beta)
is given by

(43)

\ \varepsilon_{s,\ell}(g,\beta)=\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}\int_{\ell\beta}^{(\ell+1)\beta}(e^{-st}-1)\langle\eta(t),g\rangle\;dt+\nu_{s,\ell}-\nu_{0,\ell}.

Assume, for the moment, that
\varepsilon_{s,\ell}(g,\beta)\equiv 0
for all
s,\ell,\beta,
and
g
, and furthermore suppose that
t_{j}\in[\ell\beta,(\ell+1)\beta)
. Then

\displaystyle\lim_{\beta\to 0}\frac{\Delta_{s,\ell+1}(g,\beta)-\Delta_{s,\ell+2}(g,\beta)}{\beta\Delta_{s,\ell+1}(g,\beta)}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\beta\to 0}\frac{e^{\rho_{j}(t_{j}-(\ell+3)\beta)}-2e^{\rho_{j}(t_{j}-(\ell+2)\beta)}+e^{\rho_{j}(t_{j}-(\ell+1)\beta)}}{\beta(e^{\rho_{j}(t_{j}-(\ell+1)\beta)}-e^{\rho_{j}(t_{j}-(\ell+2)\beta)})}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\beta\to 0}\frac{1-e^{-\rho_{j}\beta}}{\beta}

\displaystyle=\rho_{j}.

Thus, it is natural to approximate
\rho_{j}
by

\tilde{\rho}_{j}:=\frac{\Delta_{s,\ell+2}(g,\beta)-\Delta_{s,\ell+1}(g,\beta)}{\beta\Delta_{s,\ell+1}(g,\beta)}.

Also,

\displaystyle\langle h_{j},g\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{\rho_{j}(\rho_{j}+s)\beta^{2}}{s(e^{\rho_{j}(t_{j}-(\ell+2)\beta)}-e^{\rho_{j}(t_{j}-(\ell+1)\beta)})}\Delta_{s,\ell+1}(g,\beta)

\displaystyle\approx\frac{\tilde{\rho}_{j}(\tilde{\rho}_{j}+s)\beta^{2}}{s(e^{-2\tilde{\rho}_{j}\beta}-e^{-\tilde{\rho}_{j}\beta})}\Delta_{s,\ell+1}(g,\beta).

This brief examination of the ideal scenario illuminates the core of Algorithm 2 (in this case
\Delta_{s,\ell-2}(g,\beta)=0
). To account for noise, we introduce threshold values as in Algorithm 1. One of the thresholds will be used to determine if a catalyst entered the system in the interval
[\ell\beta,(\ell+1)\beta)
, and another one to cut off the coefficients
\langle h_{j},g\rangle
of insufficiently large magnitude. The first threshold will, naturally, coincide with the upper bound on the magnitude of measurements when there is nothing to detect. In particular,

(44)

\begin{split}|\varepsilon_{s,\ell}(g,\beta)-\varepsilon_{s,\ell-n}(g,\beta)|&\leq\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}\bigg|\int_{\ell\beta}^{(\ell+1)\beta}(e^{-st}-1)\langle\eta(t)-\eta(t-n\beta),g\rangle\;dt\bigg|+4\sigma\\
&\leq\frac{nL\|g\|}{\beta}\int_{\ell\beta}^{(\ell+1)\beta}|e^{-st}-1|\;dt+4\sigma\\
&=\frac{4n}{\pi}L\|g\|+4\sigma,\end{split}

and
|\Delta_{s,\ell}(g,\beta)-\Delta_{s,\ell-n}(g,\beta)|=|\varepsilon_{s,\ell}(g,\beta)-\varepsilon_{s,\ell-n}(g,\beta)|
when
(\ell+1)\beta<t_{j}
. Thus, we define

(45)

Q_{n}(g)=\frac{4n}{\pi}L\|g\|+4\sigma,\ n\in\{1,2,3\}.

The second threshold is given by

Q_{*}(g)=Q_{1}(g)+\hat{\rho}H\|g\|.

To motivate this latter threshold value, let us examine the measurements (
42
). Let
\ell_{0}\beta
be the initial time passed to Algorithm 2, and suppose
t_{j}\in[\ell\beta,(\ell+1)\beta)
for some integer
\ell>\ell_{0}
. Then, for all
n\geq 2
, as long as
(\ell+n-1)\beta<t_{j+1}
, we have

\displaystyle|\Delta_{s,\ell+n}(g,\beta)-\Delta_{s,\ell+n-1}(g,\beta)|

\displaystyle=\bigg|s\frac{e^{\rho_{j}(t-(\ell+n+1)\beta)}(e^{\rho_{j}\beta}-1)^{2}}{\beta^{2}\rho_{j}(\rho_{j}+s)}\langle h_{j},g\rangle+\varepsilon_{s,\ell}(g,\beta)-\varepsilon_{s,\ell-1}(g,\beta)\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\pi ke^{-2\rho_{j}\beta}(e^{\rho_{j}\beta}-1)^{2}}{\rho_{j}\beta^{2}\sqrt{\rho_{j}^{2}\beta^{2}+4\pi^{2}k^{2}}}|\langle h_{j},g\rangle|+|\varepsilon_{s,\ell}(g,\beta)-\varepsilon_{s,\ell-1}(g,\beta)|

\displaystyle\leq\rho_{j}H\|g\|+Q_{1}(g)

\displaystyle\leq Q_{*}(g).

This means that if
\ell^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}
is the smallest integer for which
\ell^{\prime}>\ell_{0}
and

\sup_{g\in\mathcal{G}}\big(|\Delta_{s,\ell^{\prime}}(g,\beta)-\Delta_{s,\ell^{\prime}-1}(g,\beta)|-Q_{*}(g)\big)>0,

then
t_{j}\in[\ell^{\prime}\beta,(\ell^{\prime}+1)\beta)
or
t_{j}\in[(\ell^{\prime}-1)\beta,\ell^{\prime}\beta)
. Either way,
|\ell^{\prime}\beta-t_{j}|\leq\beta
. Since Algorithm 2 sets
\mathfrak{t}_{j}=\ell^{\prime}\beta
, we have shown that
|\mathfrak{t}_{j}-t_{j}|<\beta
when the intake is detected.

In recovering the decay rate and coefficient, there are three cases to consider. The first is when
t_{j}\in[\ell\beta,(\ell+1)\beta)
and

\displaystyle\max\{

\displaystyle\sup_{g\in\mathcal{G}}\big(|\Delta_{s,\ell}(g,\beta)-\Delta_{s,\ell-1}(g,\beta)|-Q_{*}(g)\big),

\displaystyle\sup_{g\in\mathcal{G}}\big(|\Delta_{s,\ell+1}(g,\beta)-\Delta_{s,\ell}(g,\beta)|-Q_{*}(g)\big)\}\leq 0.

When this happens, Algorithm 2 does not detect the catalyst at time

, and no data can be recovered. For this case, a uniform upper bound for

is given in Proposition

The second case occurs when there exists an

As the above three cases cover all possible scenarios, Theorem

Proposition 3.11
.

Assume the hypotheses of Theorem
3.8

t_{j}\in[\ell\beta,(\ell+1)\beta)

\displaystyle\max\{

\displaystyle\sup_{g\in\mathcal{G}}\big(|\Delta_{s,\ell}(g,\beta)-\Delta_{s,\ell-1}(g,\beta)|-Q_{*}(g)\big),

Proof.

Let
g\in\mathcal{G}
. Then, by assumption

|\Delta_{s,\ell}(g,\beta)-\Delta_{s,\ell-1}(g,\beta)|\leq Q_{*}(g)

and

|\Delta_{s,\ell+1}(g,\beta)-\Delta_{s,\ell}(g,\beta)|\leq Q_{*}(g).

From this it follows that

\displaystyle|\Delta_{s,\ell+1}(g,\beta)-\Delta_{s,\ell-1}(g,\beta)|

\displaystyle=|\Delta_{s,\ell+1}(g,\beta)-\Delta_{s,\ell}(g,\beta)+\Delta_{s,\ell}(g,\beta)-\Delta_{s,\ell-1}(g,\beta)|

\displaystyle\leq|\Delta_{s,\ell+1}(g,\beta)-\Delta_{s,\ell}(g,\beta)|+|\Delta_{s,\ell}(g,\beta)-\Delta_{s,\ell-1}(g,\beta)|

\displaystyle\leq 2Q_{*}(g).

Now consider the string of inequalities:

\displaystyle 2Q_{*}(g)

\displaystyle\geq|\Delta_{s,\ell+1}(g,\beta)-\Delta_{s,\ell-1}(g,\beta)|

\displaystyle=\bigg|s\frac{e^{\rho_{j}(t_{j}-(\ell+2)\beta)}-e^{\rho_{j}(t_{j}-(\ell+1)\beta)}}{\rho_{j}(\rho_{j}+s)\beta^{2}}\langle h_{j},g\rangle+\varepsilon_{s,\ell+1}(g,\beta)-\varepsilon_{s,\ell-1}(g,\beta)\bigg|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2\pi k(e^{\rho_{j}(t_{j}-(\ell+1)\beta)}-e^{\rho_{j}(t_{j}-(\ell+2)\beta)})}{\rho_{j}\beta^{2}\sqrt{\rho_{j}^{2}\beta^{2}+4\pi^{2}k^{2}}}|\langle h_{j},g\rangle|-Q_{2}(g)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{e^{-\rho_{j}\beta}-e^{-2\rho_{j}\beta}}{\sqrt{2}\rho_{j}\beta^{2}}|\langle h_{j},g\rangle|-Q_{2}(g),

where we have used the assumption that
\beta<2\pi k/\hat{\rho}
(and hence
\rho_{j}^{2}\beta^{2}<4\pi^{2}k^{2}
). An elementary calculation shows that

\frac{e^{-\rho_{j}\beta}-e^{-2\rho_{j}\beta}}{\sqrt{2}\rho_{j}\beta^{2}}\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}\beta e^{2\rho_{j}\beta}}\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}\beta e^{2\hat{\rho}\beta}}.

Therefore

2Q_{*}(g)>\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}\beta e^{2\hat{\rho}\beta}}|\langle h_{j},g\rangle|-Q_{2}(g).

Rearranging the above gives the stated result.

Proposition 3.13

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=\beta s\frac{e^{\rho_{j}(t_{j}-(\ell+2)\beta)}-e^{\rho_{j}(t_{j}-(\ell+1)\beta)}}{\rho_{j}(\rho_{j}+s)\beta^{2}}

\displaystyle\delta_{1}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{s,\ell+1}(g,\beta)-\varepsilon_{s,\ell+2}(g,\beta)

\displaystyle\delta_{2}

\displaystyle=\beta(\varepsilon_{s,\ell+1}(g,\beta)-\varepsilon_{s,\ell-2}(g,\beta))

This way,

\tilde{\rho}_{j}=\frac{\Delta_{s,\ell+1}(g,\beta)-\Delta_{s,\ell+2}(g,\beta)}{\beta(\Delta_{s,\ell+1}(g,\beta)-\Delta_{s,\ell-2}(g,\beta))}=\frac{A+\delta_{1}}{B+\delta_{2}},\text{ and }\frac{A}{B}=\frac{1-e^{-\rho_{j}\beta}}{\beta}.

In this notation, we can estimate:

\displaystyle|\frac{A+\delta_{1}}{B+\delta_{2}}-\rho_{j}|

\displaystyle\leq|\frac{A+\delta_{1}}{B+\delta_{2}}-\frac{A}{B}|+|\frac{A}{B}-\rho_{j}|

\displaystyle=\bigg|\frac{B\delta_{1}-A\delta_{2}}{B(B+\delta_{2})}\bigg|+\bigg(\rho_{j}-\frac{1-e^{-\rho_{j}\beta}}{\beta}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|\delta_{1}|+\frac{A}{B}|\delta_{2}|}{|B+\delta_{2}|}+\bigg(\rho_{j}-\frac{1-e^{-\rho_{j}\beta}}{\beta}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|\delta_{1}|+\rho_{j}|\delta_{2}|}{|B+\delta_{2}|}+\bigg(\rho_{j}-\frac{1-e^{-\rho_{j}\beta}}{\beta}\bigg).

Using (
44
), we have the upper bounds

|\delta_{1}|\leq\frac{4}{\pi}L\|g\|+4\sigma\text{ and }\rho_{j}|\delta_{2}|\leq\beta(\frac{12}{\pi}L\|g\|\hat{\rho}+4\sigma\hat{\rho}),

so

|\delta_{1}|+\rho_{j}|\delta_{2}|\leq\frac{4}{\pi}L\|g\|(1+3\hat{\rho}\beta)+4\sigma(1+\hat{\rho}\beta).

We conclude that

\displaystyle|\tilde{\rho}_{j}-\rho_{j}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\frac{4}{\pi}L_{\ell}\|g\|(1+3\hat{\rho}\beta)+4\sigma(1+\hat{\rho}\beta)}{\beta|\Delta_{s,\ell+1}(g,\beta)-\Delta_{s,\ell-2}(g,\beta)|}+\hat{\rho}-\frac{1-e^{-\hat{\rho}\beta}}{\beta}

as claimed.
∎

It remains to estimate the error in recovering the coefficient in the case when

|\Delta_{s,\ell+1}(g,\beta)-\Delta_{s,\ell-2}(g,\beta)|>Q_{3}(g).

This estimate is the most technical in this section and needs the following lemma.

Lemma 3.14
.

Suppose
\beta>0
, and consider the function

f(x)=\frac{e^{\beta x}-1}{x},\quad f(0)=\beta.

Then, for any
a\in\mathbb{R}
, the function

g_{a}(x)=\left|1-\frac{f(x)}{f(x+a)}\right|

is nondecreasing on
[0,\infty)
. In particular,

g_{a}(x)\leq\lim_{x\to\infty}g_{a}(x)=|1-e^{-a\beta}|\leq e^{|a|\beta}-1

The proof is given in the appendix. In the notation of the above lemma, it follows that if
a<0
, then
f(x)/f(x+a)\geq 1
for each
x\in[0,\infty)
, and that
f(x)/f(x+a)
is increasing. This fact is used in (
47
) below.

Proposition 3.15
.

Assume the hypotheses of Theorem
3.8
. Further assume that there is an
\ell>\ell_{0}
for which (
46
) holds, and that
\ell
is the smallest integer satisfying this condition. Assume

is such that

Then

\displaystyle|\mathfrak{f}_{j}(g)-\langle h_{j},g\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq H\|g\|\bigg(e^{\beta(3|\tilde{\rho}_{j}-\rho_{j}|+\hat{\rho})}-1+\frac{|\epsilon_{\rho}|\beta e^{\beta(3|\tilde{\rho}_{j}-\rho_{j}|)+\hat{\rho})}}{\sqrt{\rho_{j}^{2}\beta^{2}+4\pi^{2}k^{2}}}\bigg)

\displaystyle+4\sqrt{2}\beta e^{2\hat{\rho}\beta}\left(\frac{3}{\pi}L\|g\|+\sigma\right).

Proof.

Suppose that

|\Delta_{s,\ell+1}(g,\beta)-\Delta_{s,\ell-2}(g,\beta)|>Q_{3}(g),

and define
\epsilon_{\rho}=\tilde{\rho}_{j}-\rho_{j}
. Furthermore, we define the function:

\theta(\rho,t,\beta,\ell)=\frac{s(e^{\rho(t-(\ell+2)\beta)}-e^{\rho(t-(\ell+1)\beta)})}{\rho(\rho+s)\beta^{2}}.

Algorithm 2 will define

\displaystyle\mathfrak{f}_{j}(g)

\displaystyle:=\frac{\tilde{\rho}_{j}(\tilde{\rho}_{j}+s)\beta^{2}}{s\cdot(e^{-2\tilde{\rho}_{j}\beta}-e^{-\tilde{\rho}_{j}\beta})}(\Delta_{s,\ell+1}(g,\beta)-\Delta_{s,\ell-2}(g,\beta))

\displaystyle=\frac{\theta(\rho_{j},t_{j},\beta,\ell)}{\theta(\tilde{\rho}_{j},\ell\beta,\beta,\ell)}\langle h_{j},g\rangle+\frac{\varepsilon_{s,\ell+1}(g,\beta)-\varepsilon_{s,\ell-2}(g,\beta)}{\theta(\tilde{\rho}_{j},\ell\beta,\beta,\ell)}.

Using the triangle inequality, it follows that

\displaystyle|\mathfrak{f}_{j}(g)-\langle h_{j},g\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\bigg|\frac{\theta(\rho_{j},t_{j},\beta,\ell)}{\theta(\tilde{\rho}_{j},\ell\beta,\beta,\ell)}-1\bigg|\cdot|\langle h_{j},g\rangle|+\bigg|\frac{\varepsilon_{s,\ell+1}(g,\beta)-\varepsilon_{s,\ell-2}(g,\beta)}{\theta(\tilde{\rho}_{j},\ell\beta,\beta,\ell)}\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\bigg|\frac{\theta(\rho_{j},t_{j},\beta,\ell)}{\theta(\tilde{\rho}_{j},\ell\beta,\beta,\ell)}-1\bigg|H\|g\|+\bigg|\frac{\varepsilon_{s,\ell+1}(g,\beta)-\varepsilon_{s,\ell-2}(g,\beta)}{\theta(\tilde{\rho}_{j},\ell\beta,\beta,\ell)}\bigg|.

The latter term can be easily bounded:

\displaystyle\bigg|\frac{\varepsilon_{s,\ell+1}(g,\beta)-\varepsilon_{s,\ell-2}}{\theta(\tilde{\rho}_{j},\ell\beta,\beta,\ell)}\bigg|

\displaystyle=\bigg|\frac{\tilde{\rho}_{j}(\tilde{\rho}_{j}+s)\beta^{2}}{s(e^{-2\tilde{\rho}_{j}\beta}-e^{-\tilde{\rho}_{j}\beta})}\bigg|\cdot(\frac{12}{\pi}L_{\ell}\|g\|+4\sigma)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\tilde{\rho}_{j}|\tilde{\rho}_{j}+s|\beta^{2}}{|s|(e^{-\tilde{\rho}_{j}\beta}-e^{-2\tilde{\rho}_{j}\beta})}(\frac{12}{\pi}L_{\ell}\|g\|+4\sigma)

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2}\beta e^{2\hat{\rho}\beta}(\frac{12}{\pi}L_{\ell}\|g\|+4\sigma),

where we have used a similar calculation to that used in the proof of Proposition
3.11
.

As for the other term, using
s=2\pi ik/\beta
, we have

\displaystyle\bigg|\frac{\theta(\rho_{j},t_{j},\beta,\ell)}{\theta(\tilde{\rho}_{j},\ell\beta,\beta,\ell)}-1\bigg|

\displaystyle=\bigg|e^{2\beta\epsilon_{\rho}+\rho_{j}(t_{j}-\ell\beta)}\frac{(e^{\rho_{j}\beta}-1)(\rho_{j}+\epsilon_{\rho})(\rho_{j}+\epsilon_{\rho}+s)}{(e^{(\rho_{j}+\epsilon_{\rho})\beta}-1)\rho_{j}(\rho_{j}+s)}-1\bigg|

\displaystyle=\bigg|e^{2\beta\epsilon_{\rho}+\rho_{j}(t_{j}-\ell\beta)}\frac{(e^{\rho_{j}\beta}-1)(\rho_{j}+\epsilon_{\rho})(\rho_{j}\beta+2\pi ik+\epsilon_{\rho}\beta)}{(e^{(\rho_{j}+\epsilon_{\rho})\beta}-1)\rho_{j}(\rho_{j}\beta+2\pi ik)}-1\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq|1-e^{2\beta\epsilon_{\rho}+\rho_{j}(t_{j}-\ell\beta)}|+e^{2\beta\epsilon_{\rho}+\rho_{j}(t_{j}-\ell\beta)}\bigg|\frac{(e^{\rho_{j}\beta}-1)(\rho_{j}+\epsilon_{\rho})(\rho_{j}\beta+2\pi ik+\epsilon_{\rho}\beta)}{(e^{(\rho_{j}+\epsilon_{\rho})\beta}-1)\rho_{j}(\rho_{j}\beta+2\pi ik)}-1\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq(e^{\beta(2|\epsilon_{\rho}|+\rho_{j})}-1)+e^{\beta(2|\epsilon_{\rho}|+\rho_{j})}\bigg[\big|1-\frac{(e^{\rho_{j}\beta}-1)(\rho_{j}+\epsilon_{\rho})}{(e^{(\rho_{j}+\epsilon_{\rho})\beta}-1)\rho_{j}}\big|

\displaystyle+\big|\frac{\epsilon_{\rho}\beta(e^{\rho_{j}\beta}-1)(\rho_{j}+\epsilon_{\rho})}{(e^{(\rho_{j}+\epsilon_{\rho})\beta}-1)\rho_{j}(\rho_{j}\beta+2\pi ik)}\big|\bigg].

By Lemma

\big|1-\frac{(e^{\rho_{j}\beta}-1)(\rho_{j}+\epsilon_{\rho})}{(e^{(\rho_{j}+\epsilon_{\rho})\beta}-1)\rho_{j}}\big|\leq e^{|\epsilon_{\rho}|\beta}-1.

Similarly,

(47)

\begin{split}\big|\frac{\epsilon_{\rho}\beta(e^{\rho_{j}\beta}-1)(\rho_{j}+\epsilon_{\rho})}{(e^{(\rho_{j}+\epsilon_{\rho})\beta}-1)\rho_{j}(\rho_{j}\beta+2\pi ik)}\big|&\leq\frac{|\epsilon_{\rho}|\beta}{\sqrt{\rho_{j}^{2}\beta^{2}+4\pi^{2}k^{2}}}\bigg(\lim_{\rho_{j}\to\infty}\frac{(e^{\rho_{j}\beta}-1)(\rho_{j}-|\epsilon_{\rho}|)}{(e^{(\rho_{j}-|\epsilon_{\rho}|)\beta}-1)\rho_{j}}\bigg)\\

Putting this all together yields

\displaystyle|\mathfrak{f}_{j}(g)-\langle h_{j},g\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq H\|g\|\bigg(e^{\beta(2|\epsilon_{\rho}|+\rho_{j})}-1+e^{\beta(2|\epsilon_{\rho}|+\rho_{j})}\bigg[e^{|\epsilon_{\rho}|\beta}-1+\frac{|\epsilon_{\rho}|\beta e^{|\epsilon_{\rho}|\beta}}{\sqrt{\rho_{j}^{2}\beta^{2}+4\pi^{2}k^{2}}}\bigg]\bigg)

\displaystyle+\sqrt{2}\beta e^{2\hat{\rho}\beta}(\frac{12}{\pi}L_{\ell}\|g\|+4\sigma)

\displaystyle\leq H\|g\|\bigg(e^{\beta(3|\epsilon_{\rho}|+\hat{\rho})}-1+\frac{|\epsilon_{\rho}|\beta e^{\beta(3|\epsilon_{\rho}|)+\hat{\rho})}}{\sqrt{\rho_{j}^{2}\beta^{2}+4\pi^{2}k^{2}}}\bigg)

\displaystyle+\sqrt{2}\beta e^{2\hat{\rho}\beta}(\frac{12}{\pi}L_{\ell}\|g\|+4\sigma)

as claimed.
∎

Notice, all upper bounds given for

\displaystyle+\sqrt{2}\beta e^{3\hat{\rho}\beta}(\frac{24}{\pi}L_{\ell}\|g\|+12\sigma).

Using the formula for the remainder of the Taylor series, we can estimate:

5.1.
Derivation of Local Lipschitz Constants

Here we derive local Lipschitz constants of
\tilde{\eta}
defined in (
12
). Let
\ell_{0}\beta
be the initial time-step passed to Algorithm 2 so that
t_{j-1}<\ell_{0}\beta<t_{j}
. For each time step
\ell\beta>\ell_{0}\beta
around which the catalyst at time
t_{j}
is not detected, we can update the Lipschitz constant of
\tilde{\eta}
. Observe the string of inequalities

\displaystyle\sup_{t\geq\ell\beta}\|\frac{d}{dt}(\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}h_{i}e^{\rho_{i}(t_{i}-t)})\|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{t\geq\ell\beta}\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}\|h_{i}\|\rho_{i}e^{\rho_{i}(t_{i}-t)}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq H\hat{\rho}\sum_{i=0}^{j-2}e^{\check{\rho}(t_{j-1-i}-\ell\beta)}.

Using the assumption (
8
), and the fact that
t_{j-1}<\ell_{0}\beta
we have that
t_{j-1-i}\leq\ell_{0}\beta-i(4\beta+D)
for all
0\leq i\leq j-2
. This implies that

\displaystyle H\hat{\rho}\sum_{i=0}^{j-2}e^{\check{\rho}(t_{j-1-i}-\ell\beta)}

\displaystyle\leq H\hat{\rho}\sum_{i=0}^{j-2}e^{\check{\rho}(\ell_{0}\beta-i(4\beta+D)-\ell\beta)}

\displaystyle\leq H\hat{\rho}e^{\beta(\ell_{0}-\ell)}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}e^{-i\check{\rho}(4\beta+D)}

\displaystyle=\frac{H\hat{\rho}e^{\beta(\ell_{0}-\ell)}}{1-e^{-\check{\rho}(4\beta+D)}}.

It follows that
\tilde{\eta}
is Lipschitz on
[\ell\beta,\infty)
with (known) Lipschitz constant

L_{\ell}=L+\frac{H\hat{\rho}e^{\beta(\ell_{0}-\ell)}}{1-e^{-\check{\rho}(4\beta+D)}}\leq L+\frac{H\hat{\rho}}{1-e^{-\check{\rho}D}}.

5.2.
Proof of Lemma
3.14

We show only the case when
a\geq 0
, the case for
a<0
is similar. Since
a\geq 0
,
f
is increasing, so

1-\frac{f(x)}{f(x+a)}\geq 0

for each
x\in[0,\infty)
. This means we only need to show that
f(x)/f(x+a)
is monotonically nonincreasing.
By the quotient rule, it suffices to show that

f(x)f^{\prime}(x+a)-f^{\prime}(x)f(x+a)\geq 0

for each
x
. An elementary calculation shows that

(48)

\begin{split}&x^{2}(x+a)^{2}\big(f(x)f^{\prime}(x+a)-f^{\prime}(x)f(x+a)\big)\\
&=a\big(1+e^{\beta(2x+a)}+e^{\beta x}(\beta x-1)-e^{\beta(a+x)}(1+\beta x)\big)-x^{2}\beta e^{\beta x}(e^{a\beta}-1).\end{split}

Since the RHS of (
48
) vanishes for
x=0
, it suffices to show that this RHS is nondecreasing. Taking the
x
-derivative of the RHS of (
48
) and simplifying gives

(49)

\beta e^{\beta x}((e^{a\beta}-1)(2+\beta x)x+a(\beta x+2e^{\beta(a+x)}-e^{a\beta}(2+\beta x))).

The first term in (
49
) is nonnegative, and the second term is nonnegative for
x\in[0,\infty)
if

(50)

2(e^{\beta(a+x)}-e^{a\beta})+\beta x-e^{a\beta}\beta x\geq 0.

This is, again, an expression which vanishes when
x=0
, and is easily seen to be increasing. To summarize, (
50
) is nonnegative, which implies (
49
) is nonnegative, which means the RHS of (
48
) is nonnegative, so
f(x)/f(x+a)
is nonincreasing. This completes the proof.

The following Lemma was proven in

Proof.

Let
p\in\{\ell,\ell+1\}
. Then

\displaystyle\int_{p\beta}^{\infty}e^{-st}\left\langle\int_{0}^{t}T(t-\tau)\eta(\tau)d\tau,\left(\overline{s}I-A^{*}\right)\frac{g}{\beta^{2}}\right\rangle dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{p\beta}\int_{p\beta}^{\infty}e^{-st}\left\langle T(t-\tau)\eta(\tau),\left(\overline{s}I-A^{*}\right)\frac{g}{\beta^{2}}\right\rangle dtd\tau

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{p\beta}^{\infty}\int_{\tau}^{\infty}e^{-st}\left\langle T(t-\tau)\eta(\tau),\left(\overline{s}I-A^{*}\right)\frac{g}{\beta^{2}}\right\rangle dtd\tau

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{p\beta}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-s(t+p\beta)}\left\langle T(t+p\beta-\tau)\eta(\tau),\left(\overline{s}I-A^{*}\right)\frac{g}{\beta^{2}}\right\rangle dtd\tau

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{p\beta}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-s(t+\tau)}\left\langle T(t)\eta(\tau),\left(\overline{s}I-A^{*}\right)\frac{g}{\beta^{2}}\right\rangle dtd\tau

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{p\beta}e^{-sp\beta}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-st}\left\langle T(t)T(p\beta-\tau)\eta(\tau),\left(\overline{s}I-A^{*}\right)\frac{g}{\beta^{2}}\right\rangle dtd\tau

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{p\beta}^{\infty}e^{-s\tau}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-st}\left\langle T(t)\eta(\tau),\left(\overline{s}I-A^{*}\right)\frac{g}{\beta^{2}}\right\rangle dtd\tau

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{p\beta}e^{-sp\beta}\left\langle T(p\beta-\tau)\eta(\tau),\frac{g}{\beta^{2}}\right\rangle d\tau+\int_{p\beta}^{\infty}e^{-s\tau}\left\langle\eta(\tau),\frac{g}{\beta^{2}}\right\rangle d\tau,

where we changed the order of integration in the first inequality.

Therefore, for
s>\omega
,

(53)

\displaystyle\int_{\ell\beta}^{(\ell+1)\beta}e^{-st}\left\langle\int_{0}^{t}T(t-\tau)\eta(\tau)d\tau,\left(\overline{s}I-A^{*}\right)\frac{g}{\beta^{2}}\right\rangle dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\ell\beta}^{\infty}-\int_{(\ell+1)\beta}^{\infty}\right)e^{-st}\left\langle\int_{0}^{t}T(t-\tau)\eta(\tau)d\tau,\left(\overline{s}I-A^{*}\right)\frac{g}{\beta^{2}}\right\rangle dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\ell\beta}e^{-s\ell\beta}\left\langle T(\ell\beta-\tau)\eta(\tau),\frac{g}{\beta^{2}}\right\rangle d\tau+\int_{\ell\beta}^{\infty}e^{-s\tau}\left\langle\eta(\tau),\frac{g}{\beta^{2}}\right\rangle d\tau-

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{(\ell+1)\beta}e^{-s(\ell+1)\beta}\left\langle T((\ell+1)\beta-\tau)\eta(\tau),\frac{g}{\beta^{2}}\right\rangle d\tau+\int_{(\ell+1)\beta}^{\infty}e^{-s\tau}\left\langle\eta(\tau),\frac{g}{\beta^{2}}\right\rangle d\tau\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\ell\beta}e^{-s\ell\beta}\left\langle T(\ell\beta-\tau)\eta(\tau),\frac{g}{\beta^{2}}\right\rangle d\tau+\int_{\ell\beta}^{(\ell+1)\beta}e^{-s\tau}\left\langle\eta(\tau),\frac{g}{\beta^{2}}\right\rangle d\tau

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{(\ell+1)\beta}e^{-s(\ell+1)\beta}\left\langle T((\ell+1)\beta-\tau)\eta(\tau),\frac{g}{\beta^{2}}\right\rangle d\tau

as claimed.
∎

Proof of Lemma
3.10
.

As above, let
\omega
be the growth bound of the semigroup
\{T(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
. Assume, for now, that
s>\omega
so that the resolvent

R(s,A)=(sI-A)^{-1}=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-st}T(t)\;dt

is defined at
s
. Using this, we first compute:

(54)

\begin{split}&\int_{\ell\beta}^{(\ell+1)\beta}e^{-st}\langle T(t)u_{0},(\overline{s}I-A^{*})\frac{g}{\beta^{2}}\rangle dt\\
=&\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-s(t+\ell\beta)}\langle T(t)T(\ell\beta)u_{0},(\overline{s}I-A^{*})\frac{g}{\beta^{2}}\rangle dt\\
&\quad-\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-s(t+(\ell+1)\beta)}\langle T(t)T\big((\ell+1)\beta\big)u_{0},(\overline{s}I-A^{*})\frac{g}{\beta^{2}}\rangle dt\\
=&e^{-s\ell\beta}\langle R(s,A)T(\ell\beta)u_{0},(\overline{s}I-A^{*})\frac{g}{\beta^{2}}\rangle\\
&\quad-e^{-s(\ell+1)\beta}\langle R(s,A)T\big((\ell+1)\beta\big)u_{0},(\overline{s}I-A^{*})\frac{g}{\beta^{2}}\rangle\\
=&e^{-s\ell\beta}\langle T(\ell\beta)u_{0},\frac{g}{\beta^{2}}\rangle-e^{-s(\ell+1)\beta}\langle T\big((\ell+1)\beta\big)u_{0},\frac{g}{\beta^{2}}\rangle.\end{split}

Now we need to simplify

\int_{\ell\beta}^{(\ell+1)\beta}e^{-st}\chi_{[t_{j},\infty)}\int_{t_{j}}^{t}e^{\rho_{j}(t_{j}-\tau)}\langle T(t-\tau)h_{j},(\overline{s}I-A^{*})\frac{g}{\beta^{2}}\rangle\;d\tau dt.

Define
\alpha=\max{\{\ell\beta,t_{j}\}}
and
a(\tau)=\max{\{\alpha-\tau,0\}}
. Then we have the following string of equalities:

(55)

\begin{split}&\int_{\ell\beta}^{(\ell+1)\beta}e^{-st}\chi_{[t_{j},\infty)}\int_{t_{j}}^{t}e^{\rho(t_{j}-\tau)}\langle T(t-\tau)h_{j},(\overline{s}I-A^{*})\frac{g}{\beta^{2}}\rangle\;d\tau dt\\
=&\int_{\alpha}^{(\ell+1)\beta}e^{-st}\int_{t_{j}}^{t}e^{\rho(t_{j}-\tau)}\langle T(t-\tau)h_{j},(\overline{s}I-A^{*})\frac{g}{\beta^{2}}\rangle\;d\tau dt\\
=&e^{\rho t_{j}}\int_{t_{j}}^{(\ell+1)\beta}\int_{\max{\{\alpha,\tau\}}}^{(\ell+1)\beta}e^{-\rho\tau}e^{-st}\langle T(t-\tau)h_{j},(\overline{s}I-A^{*})\frac{g}{\beta^{2}}\rangle\;dtd\tau\\
=&e^{\rho t_{j}}\int_{t_{j}}^{(\ell+1)\beta}e^{-\tau(\rho+s)}\int_{a(\tau)}^{(\ell+1)\beta-\tau}e^{-st}\langle T(t)h_{j},(\overline{s}I-A^{*})\frac{g}{\beta^{2}}\rangle\;dtd\tau\\
=&e^{\rho t_{j}}\int_{t_{j}}^{(\ell+1)\beta}e^{-\tau(\rho+s)}\langle R(s,A)[e^{-sa(\tau)}T\big(a(\tau)\big)h_{j}-e^{-s((\ell+1)\beta-\tau}T\big((\ell+1)\beta-\tau\big)h_{j}],(\overline{s}I-A^{*})\frac{g}{\beta^{2}}\rangle\;d\tau\\
=&e^{\rho t_{j}}\int_{t_{j}}^{(\ell+1)\beta}e^{-\tau(\rho+s)}\langle e^{-sa(\tau)}T\big(a(\tau)\big)h_{j},\frac{g}{\beta^{2}}\rangle-e^{-\tau\rho}e^{-s(\ell+1)\beta}\langle T\big((\ell+1)\beta-\tau)\big)h_{j},\frac{g}{\beta^{2}}\rangle\;d\tau.\end{split}

If
\ell\beta\leq t_{j}
, then
a(\tau)\equiv 0
(for
t_{j}<\tau<(\ell+1)\beta)
. If
t_{j}<\ell\beta
, then

a(\tau)=\begin{cases}\ell\beta-\tau&\tau\in[t_{j},\ell\beta]\\
0&\tau\in(\ell\beta,((\ell+1)\beta).\end{cases}

In the former case,

(56)

\begin{split}&e^{\rho t_{j}}\int_{t_{j}}^{(\ell+1)\beta}e^{-\tau(\rho+s)}\langle e^{-sa(\tau)}T\big(a(\tau)\big)h_{j},\frac{g}{\beta^{2}}\rangle\;d\tau\\
=&e^{\rho t_{j}}\int_{t_{j}}^{(\ell+1)\beta}e^{-\tau(\rho+s)}\langle h_{j},\frac{g}{\beta^{2}}\rangle\;d\tau\\
=&\frac{e^{-st_{j}}-e^{\rho(t_{j}-(\ell+1)\beta)-s(\ell+1)\beta}}{(\rho+s)\beta^{2}}\langle h_{j},g\rangle.\end{split}

In the latter case,

(57)

\begin{split}&e^{\rho t_{j}}\int_{t_{j}}^{(\ell+1)\beta}e^{-\tau(\rho+s)}\langle e^{-sa(\tau)}T\big(a(\tau)\big)h_{j},\frac{g}{\beta^{2}}\rangle\;d\tau\\
=&e^{\rho t_{j}}\int_{t_{j}}^{\ell\beta}e^{-\tau(\rho+s)}\langle e^{-s(\ell\beta-\tau)}T\big(\ell\beta-\tau\big)h_{j},\frac{g}{\beta^{2}}\rangle d\tau\\
&\quad+e^{\rho}t_{j}\int_{\ell\beta}^{(\ell+1)\beta}e^{-\tau(\rho+s)}\langle h_{j},\frac{g}{\beta^{2}}\rangle d\tau\\
=&e^{\rho t_{j}}\int_{t_{j}}^{\ell\beta}e^{-\tau\rho}\langle e^{-s\ell\beta}T\big(\ell\beta-\tau\big)h_{j},\frac{g}{\beta^{2}}\rangle d\tau\\
&\quad+\frac{e^{\rho(t_{j}-\ell\beta)-s\ell\beta}-e^{\rho(t_{j}-(\ell+1)\beta)-s(\ell+1)\beta}}{(\rho+s)\beta^{2}}\langle h_{j},g\rangle.\end{split}

Putting together (
52
), (
54
), (
56
) and (
57
), we have

(58)

\begin{split}m_{s,\ell}(g,\beta)&=e^{-s\ell\beta}\langle T(\ell\beta)u_{0},\frac{g}{\beta^{2}}\rangle-e^{-s(\ell+1)\beta}\langle T\big((\ell+1)\beta\big)u_{0},\frac{g}{\beta^{2}}\rangle\\
&+\frac{e^{-st_{j}}-e^{\rho(t_{j}-(\ell+1)\beta)-s(\ell+1)\beta}}{(\rho+s)\beta^{2}}\langle h_{j},g\rangle+\int_{\ell\beta}^{(\ell+1)\beta}e^{-st}\langle\eta(\tau),\frac{g}{\beta^{2}}\rangle dt\\
&+\int_{0}^{\ell\beta}e^{-s\ell\beta}\langle T(\ell\beta-\tau)\eta(\tau),\frac{g}{\beta^{2}}\rangle d\tau-\int_{0}^{(\ell+1)\beta}e^{-s(\ell+1)\beta}\langle T((\ell+1)\beta-\tau)\eta(\tau),\frac{g}{\beta^{2}}\rangle d\tau\end{split}

when
\ell\beta\leq t_{j}
, and

(59)

\begin{split}m_{s,\ell}(g,\beta)&=e^{-s\ell\beta}\langle T(\ell\beta)u_{0},\frac{g}{\beta^{2}}\rangle-e^{-s(\ell+1)\beta}\langle T\big((\ell+1)\beta\big)u_{0},\frac{g}{\beta^{2}}\rangle\\
&+e^{\rho t_{j}}\int_{t_{j}}^{\ell\beta}e^{-\tau\rho}\langle e^{-s\ell\beta}T\big((\ell+1)\beta-\tau\big)h_{j},\frac{g}{\beta^{2}}\rangle\\
&+e^{\rho t_{j}}\int_{t_{j}}^{\ell\beta}e^{-\tau\rho}\langle e^{-s\ell\beta}T\big(\ell\beta-\tau\big)h_{j},\frac{g}{\beta^{2}}\rangle d\tau\\
&+\frac{e^{\rho(t_{j}-\ell\beta)-s\ell\beta}-e^{\rho(t_{j}-(\ell+1)\beta)-s(\ell+1)\beta}}{(\rho+s)\beta^{2}}\langle h_{j},g\rangle+\int_{\ell\beta}^{(\ell+1)\beta}e^{-st}\langle\eta(\tau),\frac{g}{\beta^{2}}\rangle dt\\
&+\int_{0}^{\ell\beta}e^{-s\ell\beta}\langle T(\ell\beta-\tau)\eta(\tau),\frac{g}{\beta^{2}}\rangle d\tau-\int_{0}^{(\ell+1)\beta}e^{-s(\ell+1)\beta}\langle T((\ell+1)\beta-\tau)\eta(\tau),\frac{g}{\beta^{2}}\rangle d\tau\end{split}

when
t_{j}<\ell\beta
.

We have thus shown that (
14
) and (
58
) are equivalent when
\ell\beta\leq t_{j}
, and (
14
) and (
59
) are equivalent when
t_{j}<\ell\beta
for all real
s>\omega
. Now, (
14
), (
58
), and (
59
) are all analytic functions of
s
. It follows that these equivalences are valid for all
s\in\mathbb{C}
. For
s=\frac{2\pi ik}{\beta}
, (
58
) becomes

\displaystyle m_{s,\ell}(g,\beta)

\displaystyle=\langle T(\ell\beta)u_{0},\frac{g}{\beta^{2}}\rangle-\langle T\big((\ell+1)\beta\big)u_{0},\frac{g}{\beta^{2}}\rangle+\frac{e^{-st_{j}}-e^{\rho(t_{j}-(\ell+1)\beta)-s(\ell+1)\beta}}{(\rho+s)\beta^{2}}\langle h_{j},g\rangle

\displaystyle+\int_{\ell\beta}^{(\ell+1)\beta}e^{-st}\langle\eta(\tau),\frac{g}{\beta^{2}}\rangle dt+\int_{0}^{\ell\beta}\langle T(\ell\beta-\tau)\eta(\tau),\frac{g}{\beta^{2}}\rangle\;d\tau

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{(\ell+1)\beta}\langle T((\ell+1)\beta-\tau)\eta(\tau),\frac{g}{\beta^{2}}\rangle\;d\tau

Similarly, (
59
) becomes

\displaystyle m_{s,\ell}(g,\beta)

\displaystyle=\langle T(\ell\beta)u_{0},\frac{g}{\beta^{2}}\rangle-\langle T\big((\ell+1)\beta\big)u_{0},\frac{g}{\beta^{2}}\rangle

\displaystyle+e^{\rho t_{j}}\int_{t_{j}}^{\ell\beta}e^{-\tau\rho}\langle T\big((\ell+1)\beta-\tau\big)h_{j},\frac{g}{\beta^{2}}\rangle+\frac{e^{\rho(t_{j}-\ell\beta)}-e^{\rho(t_{j}-(\ell+1)\beta)}}{(\rho+s)\beta^{2}}\langle h_{j},g\rangle

\displaystyle+\int_{\ell\beta}^{(\ell+1)\beta}e^{-st}\langle\eta(\tau),\frac{g}{\beta^{2}}\rangle dt+\int_{0}^{\ell\beta}\langle T(\ell\beta-\tau)\eta(\tau),\frac{g}{\beta^{2}}\rangle\;d\tau

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{(\ell+1)\beta}\langle T((\ell+1)\beta-\tau)\eta(\tau),\frac{g}{\beta^{2}}\rangle\;d\tau.

Collecting the terms that don’t depend on
s
proves the theorem.
∎

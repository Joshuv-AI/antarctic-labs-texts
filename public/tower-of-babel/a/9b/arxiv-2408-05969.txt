.

For

\Bigg|\sum_{n\leq X}\mu(n)\Bigg|\leq\frac{(0.006688\,\Log X-0.039)X}{\Log^{2}X}.

3.
Strategy of proof

Call L the function
t>0\mapsto\log t
and
\star
the convolution of any two arithmetic functions. By observing that
(1/\zeta)^{\prime\prime}=-\zeta^{\prime\prime}/\zeta^{2}+(\zeta^{\prime})^{2}/\zeta^{3}
, where
\zeta
corresponds to the Riemann zeta function, we can deduce that

\mu\cdot\text{L}^{2}=\mu\star\Lambda\star\Lambda-\mu\star\Lambda\cdot\text{L},

whence identity (
1
).

We define the remainder quantity
R_{2}^{*}
as follows

(19)

R_{2}^{*}(X)=\sum_{n\leq X}\left(\Lambda\star\Lambda(n)-\Lambda(n)\Log n+2\gamma\right).

Now, for any
K\in(0,X]\cap\mathbb{Z}
, we may derive from (
1
) the following expression

(20)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq X}\mu(n)\log^{2}n

\displaystyle=-2\gamma+\sum_{\ell\leq X/K}\mu(\ell)R_{2}^{*}\bigg(\frac{X}{\ell}\bigg)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\sum_{k<K}R_{2}^{*}(k)\sum_{X/(k+1)<\ell\leq X/k}\mu(\ell).

Observe that the range in the outermost sum of the above second expression is in fact
\max\{X/K,X/(k+1)\}<\ell\leq X/k
. Nonetheless, if
X/(k+1)<X/K
, since
k<K
, we have
k<K<k+1
, giving an empty sum as
K
is integer.

Moreover, by rearranging terms, we may express

\displaystyle\sum_{k\leq K-1}R_{2}^{*}(k)\sum_{X/(k+1)<\ell\leq X/k}\mu(\ell)=\sum_{k\leq K-1}R_{2}^{*}(k)\bigg[M\bigg(\frac{X}{k}\bigg)-M\bigg(\frac{X}{k+1}\bigg)\bigg]\qquad

(21)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\leq K}(\Lambda\star\Lambda(k)-\Lambda(k)\log k+2\gamma)M\bigg(\frac{X}{k}\bigg)-R_{2}^{*}(K)M\bigg(\frac{X}{K}\bigg).

Thus, by combining (
20
) and (
3
) together, we arrive at

(22)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq X}\mu(n)\log^{2}n=-2\gamma+\sum_{\ell\leq X/K}\mu(\ell)R_{2}^{*}\bigg(\frac{X}{\ell}\bigg)\quad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad

\displaystyle+\sum_{k\leq K}(\Lambda\star\Lambda(k)-\Lambda(k)\log k+2\gamma)M\bigg(\frac{X}{k}\bigg)-R_{2}^{*}(K)M\bigg(\frac{X}{K}\bigg).

In order to prove Lemma
7.3
, we use will identity (
22
), and for this reason, we will require estimates for

R_{2}^{*}(X)
when
X\geq 1.8\cdot 10^{9}
as well as Lemma
7.1
.

Concerning the estimation of
R_{2}^{*}
, as per Lemma
6.1
, it is enough to estimate two auxiliary functions

R_{3}
and
R_{4}
. First,
R_{3}
is defined as

(23)

R_{3}(X)=2\sqrt{X}|\sqrt{X}r(\sqrt{X})-R(\sqrt{X})|+R(\sqrt{X})^{2}+|R(X)|\Log X+\left|\int_{1}^{X}R(t)\frac{dt}{t}\right|,

and will be studied in
\lx@sectionsign
4
, whereas
R_{4}
is defined as

(24)

R_{4}(X)=\sum_{n\leq\sqrt{X}}\Lambda(n)R\bigg(\frac{X}{n}\bigg),

and will be studied in
\lx@sectionsign
5
.

Thus, upon estimating the right-hand side of (
22
), we will have an estimation for the sum
\sum_{n\leq X}\mu(n)\log^{2}n
within some specific range. This main bound is expressed in Lemma
7.3
and from it, we
may deduce estimates for
M
via Lemma
3.1
. Indeed, let
(f(n))_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}}
be a sequence of complex numbers and, for any integer

k\geq 0
and
X\geq 1
, consider the weighted summatory function

(25)

Then, by summation by parts or quoting
[
13
, Lemma 8.1]
, we have the following result.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any
X_{0}>1
and any
X\geq X_{0}
, we have

M_{0}(f,X)-M_{0}(f,X_{0})=\frac{M_{k}(f,X)}{\Log^{k}X}-\frac{M_{k}(f,X_{0})}{\Log^{k}X_{0}}+k\int_{X_{0}}^{X}\frac{M_{k}(f,t)}{t\Log^{k+1}(t)}dt.

Finally, upon having an estimation for

within some range, we are
able to derive bounds for

[

Lemma 3.2

[Balazard]
For any

|m(X)|\leq\frac{|M(X)|}{X}+\frac{1}{X^{2}}\int_{1}^{X}|M(t)|dt+\frac{8}{3X}.

Lemma 5.1
.

When
1.8\cdot 10^{9}\leq X\leq 5\cdot 10^{10}
, we have

\left|\sum_{1000<n\leq 40\,000}\Lambda(n)R\bigg(\frac{X}{n}\bigg)\right|\leq 0.000154\cdot X.

Whereas we have run the computations up to

the above result up to

Proof.

Let us set, for any two integers

and
A_{1}
:

(31)

f(A_{0},A_{1},D)=\sum_{A_{0}\leq a\leq A_{1}}\Lambda(a)\psi\bigg(\frac{X}{a}\bigg).

We compute and store the values of
\psi(n)
for
n\in[10^{9}/A_{1},\cdots,10^{10}/A_{0}]\cap\mathbb{Z}
.

Next, as
X
moves from the integer
N
to
N+1
, we may get a modification in the value of
\psi(X/a)
only if

[N/a]<[(N+1)/a]
. This may only happen when there exists an
integer

such that
N/a<c\leq(N+1)/a
, that is such that
N<ac\leq N+1
,
so only whenever
a
is a divisor of
N+1
, which must further belong to
the interval
[A_{0},A]
. This observation speeds up the program we use considerably and
makes the computations possible on a home computer, using merely a couple of days.
∎

Lemma 6.1

The reader should compare the above lemma with

appears instead of

Proof.

By summation by parts, and recalling definition (
2
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq X}\Lambda(n)\Log n

\displaystyle=\psi(X)\Log X-\int_{1}^{X}\frac{\psi(t)}{t}dt

\displaystyle=X\Log X-X+1+R(X)\Log X-\int_{1}^{X}\frac{R(t)}{t}dt.

On the other hand, Dirichlet hyperbola formula yields

\displaystyle\qquad\sum_{d_{1}d_{2}\leq X}\Lambda(d_{1})\Lambda(d_{2})=2\sum_{d_{1}\leq\sqrt{X}}\Lambda(d_{1})\psi\bigg(\frac{X}{d_{1}}\bigg)-\psi(\sqrt{X})^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2X\sum_{d_{1}\leq\sqrt{X}}\frac{\Lambda(d_{1})}{d_{1}}-X-2\sqrt{X}R(\sqrt{X})-R(\sqrt{X})^{2}+2\sum_{d_{1}\leq\sqrt{X}}\Lambda(d_{1})R\bigg(\frac{X}{d_{1}}\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle X\Log X-2X\gamma-X+2Xr(\sqrt{X})-2\sqrt{X}R(\sqrt{X})-R(\sqrt{X})^{2}+2R_{4}(X).

Hence, by recalling definition
19
and combining the above two expressions, we obtain

\displaystyle R^{*}_{2}(X)=\sum_{n\leq X}\left(\Lambda\star\Lambda(n)-\Lambda(n)\Log n\right)+2[X]\gamma=-1-2\{X\}\gamma+2R_{4}(X)

\displaystyle+\bigg[2\sqrt{X}(\sqrt{X}r(\sqrt{X})-R(\sqrt{X}))-R(\sqrt{X})^{2}-R(X)\Log X+\int_{1}^{X}\frac{R(t)}{t}dt\bigg],

whence, by recalling definition (
23
), we obtain the result.
∎

\displaystyle\sum_{X/K_{0}<n\leq X/K}\frac{\mu^{2}(n)}{\sqrt{n}}=\int_{X/K_{0}}^{X/K}\sum_{X/K_{0}<n\leq t}\mu^{2}(n)\frac{dt}{2t^{3/2}}+\sum_{X/K_{0}<n\leq X/K}\frac{\mu^{2}(n)}{\sqrt{X/K}}

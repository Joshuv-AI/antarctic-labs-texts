PAC-Learning Uniform Ergodic Communicative Networks
Yihan He

Abstract
This work addressed the problem of learning a network with communication between vertices. The communication between vertices is presented in the form of perturbation on the measure. We studied the scenario where samples are drawn from a uniform ergodic Random Graph Process (RGPs for short), which provides a natural mathematical context for the problem of interest. For the binary classification problem, the result we obtained gives uniform learn-ability as the worst-case theoretical limits. We introduced the structural Rademacher complexity, which naturally fused into the VC theory to upperbound the first moment. With the martingale method and Marton’s coupling, we establish the tail bound for uniform convergence and give consistency guarantee for empirical risk minimizer. The technique used in this work to obtain high probability bounds is of independent interest to other mixing processes with and without network structure.

Introduction

The problem of spatial statistical inference has great implications in the real world due to the large volume of geometric data today. In particular, the structured regression problem is of wide interest in signal processing and mathematical statistics. We consider the problem of structured prediction of labels in a network whose vertices communicate with each other frequently. This problem presents in different contexts, including epidemic on the network of person and social network inference based on history. In this problem, we are given a set of parties denoted as
V
with
|V|
parties. A subset of vertices will be activated and communicate with a fixed or varying neighborhood in each timestamp. The objective is to minimize the overall loss incurred by the miss-prediction on the label of activated vertices across a period of time. Related problems have been widely studied both by computer scientists
(
Birant and Kut 2007
)
and statisticians
(
Alkon and Rasmussen 1988
;
Handcock and Wallis 1994
)
in history, which is often referred to as spatial temporal inference.

We build the problem with the assumption that the data is sampled according to a random graph process. The samples generated by an RGP formed a sequence of networks indexed by their timestamps where the sample generated by a party is represented by a unique vertex, and the instant communication between vertices is broadcasted along the edges. Parties might be active (when they present in the network) or passive (when they are simply isolated vertices). A key idea here is that the communication to a vertex by its neighborhood will affect the distribution of its samples in the upcoming timestamp. The regularity is then characterized by the dependence of measure, or
mixing conditions
. For some random process
Z:(Z_{k}:k\in\mathbb{Z})
, we say a real-valued process is strongly mixing if

\bm{P}(Z_{i}=z_{1},Z_{i+n}=z_{2})\overset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\bm{P}(Z_{i}=z_{1})\bm{P}(Z_{i+n}=z_{2})

uniformly for
z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
and
i\in\mathbb{Z}
. In other words, the two random variables generated by the same process that stays far away from each other are asymptotically independent of each other.

To formalize the learning problem, at time slot
t
we activate a set of vertices
\pazocal{V}_{t}\subset V
with a graph
\pazocal{G}_{t}
with
\pazocal{E}_{t}
being its edge set. The vertex set
\pazocal{V}_{t}
contains
N_{t}
vertices indexed by their index in
V
. Coupled with the timestamp, vertex indexed jointly by
(t,i)
has its own feature and label written as
X_{t,i}\in\mathbb{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
Y_{t,i}\in\mathbb{Y}\subset\mathbb{R}
. We are given a sequence of hypothesis sets
\{\pazocal{H}_{1},\ldots,\pazocal{H}_{T}\}
adaptively or deterministically and the loss function
L:\mathbb{Y}\times\mathbb{Y}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
. Hence, the incurred average loss over the active set
\pazocal{V}_{t}
is
R_{t}=\frac{1}{N_{t}}\sum_{i\in\pazocal{V}_{t}}L(h_{t}(X_{t,i}),Y_{t,i})
over the whole network. We simplify the pair of feature/label as
Z_{t,i}=(X_{t,i},Y_{t,i})
for some
(t,i)
. The final loss over the whole network from time
1
to
T
is written as
\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}R_{t}
.

It is important to note the difference between this problem with adversarial online learning. In the latter problem, no consistency is expected from the learner. The best we can achieve is the sub-linear regret, while in this work, we established uniform learn-ability. In other words, we focused on the worst-case limits. When i.i.d. assumption is no longer present because of the network communication, the learn-ability will depend on the mixing condition of networks. We also gave guarantees under the non-communicative assumptions as a comparison, where the
party in the network is assumed to be independent of each other.

The technical difficulties come in two folds:
(1)
The expected worst-case loss with the dependence measure. To address this, we designed the structural Rademacher complexity, which yields a finer bound than the sequential Rademacher complexity proposed in
Rakhlin et al. 2010
. The structural Rademacher complexity can naturally fit into VC theory by chaining. It is estimated as
\pazocal{O}\bigg(\sqrt{\frac{1}{T\inf_{t}N_{t}}\mathtt{vc}(\mathscr{H})}\bigg)
where
\mathtt{vc}(\mathscr{H})
is the VC dimension of a product hypothesis set. The technique applied here is dimension reduction on the concentrated mixing measure.
(2)
A non-vacuous upper bound on the tail for the dependent measure. The idea to address this problem comes from the martingale method
(
Kontorovich 2007
)
and Marton Coupling
(
Marton et al. 2004
)
. To conclude, the problem we studied relies heavily on two regularity conditions: The rate of mixing ( or mixing time ) and the sparsity of the communication graph. For a strongly mixing process with an exponential rate of convergence and relatively sparse neighborhood, our bound is non-vacuous. Given this bound, we are able to obtain consistency guarantees of the empirical risk minimizer.

Related Works

The problem of learning RGP is mainly interlaced with three strings of work. The first one is adversarial online learning with a multi-agent communicative network, or cooperative learning pioneered by
Awerbuch and Kleinberg 2008
. In this problem, agents are represented as vertices in a fixed structured network and communicate with their neighbors in the learning process to jointly solve an online learning problem. Each agent represents a learner with its model updated independently from others. The more recent development of this line includes
Cesa-Bianchi et al. 2016
;
Rubio et al.
;
Cesa-Bianchi et al. 2020
;
Della Vecchia and Cesari 2021
, where they studied various cooperative online learning problems and gave efficient algorithms with asynchronous updates. This work is different from theirs as we focus on the structured prediction of networks, and each vertex in the network is considered as a sample in the learning problem instead of a learner. On the other hand, we not only focus on regret minimization but on establishing uniform learn-able classes and procedures like ERM. This can be seen through that our bound is high probability instead of the first moment.

The second line of work focuses on the measure concentration without independent assumptions. Previously, many attempts have been made to the asymptotics of mixing processes. The mixing condition is a broad literature that involves many different variants of requirements, which has been summarized in

(
)

. In particular,
Yu 1994
proved asymptotic rates of uniform convergence under stationary
\beta
-mixing processes. Other works also addressed the asymptotic rates of
\alpha
-mixing,
\phi
-mixing and
\eta
-mixing
(
Vidyasagar 2013
;
Kontorovich et al. 2008
;
Kontorovich 2007
)
.
Adams and Nobel 2010
proved the convergence of VC class under ergodic processes without the use of symmetrization.
The method in this work is inspired partially by the martingale methods, but the problem is fundamentally different from this line of work in the learning context.

The third string aims at developing the metric entropies and combinatorics to give guarantees of regret in online learning problems. A steady line of work by
Littlestone 1988
;
Ben-David et al. 2009
;
Shalev-Shwartz and Ben-David 2014
studies the online learnability via combinatorial dimensions like Littlestone’s and give guarantees for the realizable case through the depth of mistake tree. This line of work is further extended by
Rakhlin et al. 2010
;
Rakhlin et al. 2011
;
Rakhlin et al. 2015
, which introduced the sequential complexities and sequential covering numbers. Those combinatorics takes the maximum over the value of the process. Hence, their result gives an upper bound on the adversarial learnability in the form of the first moment of regret. However, as the learnability, if non-stochastic online learning guarantees no consistency, their bound will be pessimistic in our problem. Our introduction of structural Rademacher complexity generalizes the sequential process into networks. On the other hand, our guarantees will be strictly better than the sequential complexities as the expectation is always a lower bound on the supremum in the mixing case.

Preliminaries

3.1
Random Graph Process (RGP)

In the previous literature
(
van der Hofstad 2017
)
, the random graph process is a sequence of graphs with random structure on a fixed vertex set. This is different from the one studied in this work, where we focus on the random feature in the networks. For a random graph process
\pazocal{Z}_{\pazocal{T}}
defined on the domain
\mathbb{Z}=\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{Y}
as the product of feature and label.
\pazocal{Z}_{\pazocal{T}}
hence induces a product state space
\Omega_{1}^{T}=\Omega_{1}\times\cdots\times\Omega_{T}
. We can draw a sample consisting of
T

\Omega_{1}^{T}
according to
\pazocal{Z}_{\pazocal{T}}
and denote the graphs as
\pazocal{G}_{1}^{T}
. Each vertex can be active (when it stays within the network) or inactive (when it is isolated from other vertices) in
\pazocal{G}_{t}
. We also note that a bijection from
\pi_{t}:[N_{t}]\to\pazocal{V}_{t}
can be defined, which maps the re-indexed index to the active indices in
V
at timestamp
t
. The active set of vertices is denoted by
\pazocal{V}_{t}
and the set of all undirected edges connecting two non-identical vertices are denoted as
\pazocal{E}_{t}
. Vertex
i
at timestamp
t
is indexed by
(t,i)
and is given a random feature label pair
(X_{t,i},Y_{t,i})
that is simplified as
Z_{t,j}
. This form
T
batches of samples denoted by
Z_{\pazocal{T}}=\mathscr{Z}_{1}^{T}=\{\mathscr{Z}_{1},...,\mathscr{Z}_{T}\}
where we denote
\mathscr{Z}_{t}=\{Z_{t,j}:j\in\pazocal{V}_{t}\}\subset\mathbb{Z}
and
\pazocal{T}
as the universal vertex set
of
\pazocal{Z}_{\pazocal{T}}
. We simplify the notations by denoting
\mathscr{X}_{t}=\{X_{t,j}:j\in\pazocal{V}_{t}\}
,
\mathscr{Y}_{t}=\{Y_{t,j}:j\in\pazocal{V}_{t}\}
,
\mathscr{X}_{i}^{j}=\{\mathscr{X}_{i},...,\mathscr{X}_{j}\}
,
\mathscr{Y}_{i}^{j}=\{\mathscr{Y}_{i},...,\mathscr{Y}_{j}\}
,
\mathscr{Z}_{i}^{j}=\{\mathscr{Z}_{i},...,\mathscr{Z}_{j}\}
and
\Omega_{i}^{j}=\prod_{k=i}^{j}\Omega_{k}
. We assumed that
\mathscr{X}_{1}^{0}=\mathscr{Z}_{1}^{0}=\mathscr{Z}_{0}=\mathscr{X}_{0}=\emptyset
.

Then we introduced several assumptions on the dependence of measure. The first assumption is on the limits of communication. One can see that the perturbation from a single vertex will broadcast along the edges until the whole network receives it.

Assumption 1
.

Given a graph
\pazocal{G}_{t}(\pazocal{V}_{t},\pazocal{E}_{t})
with vertex set
\pazocal{V}_{t}
and unordered edge set
\pazocal{E}_{t}
generated at timestamp
t
. Denote the neighborhood of vertex
i
in
\pazocal{G}_{t}
by
\pazocal{N}_{i,t}=\{j:(i,j)\in\pazocal{E}\}
and the feature/label pair in the neighborhood as
Z_{\pazocal{N}_{i,t}}=\{Z_{k,t}:k\in\pazocal{N}_{i,t}\}
. Then the following holds

\displaystyle\bm{P}(Z_{t+1,i}|\mathscr{Z}_{1}^{t})=\bm{P}(Z_{t+1,i}|\mathscr{Z}_{1}^{t-1},Z_{\pazocal{N}_{i,t}})

The second assumption is that two vertices within the same timestamp correlated with each other only because they received the message from the same vertex. This assumption is referred to as the Local Markov assumption in graphical models. We put here for technical reason.

Assumption 2

(Conditional Independence)
.

Given
i,j\in V
, we assume that

\displaystyle\bm{P}(Z_{t,i},Z_{t,j}|\mathscr{Z}_{1}^{t-1})=\bm{P}(Z_{t,i}|\mathscr{Z}_{1}^{t-1})\bm{P}(Z_{t,j}|\mathscr{Z}_{1}^{t-1})

for all
t\in[T]
.

This assumption implies that the feature/label pair of vertices in the same timestamp is independent upon observing their common ancestors.

3.2
Ergodic and Independent RGP

We are interested in the effect of communicative vertices in the network learning problem. To approach it mathematically, we assumed an exponential rate of mixing on the dependence measure. ( also referred to as uniform ergodicity in the context of Markov chains ) This condition implies strong mixing in the context of strictly stationary Markov chains. Intuitively, if we pick a single path of message broadcast in the network, the
message
decays along its way. We also discuss its degenerated case where communication is eliminated from the process.

Definition 1

(Uniform Ergodic RGP)
.

An RGP with vertex set
V
has stationary distribution
\pi
for all vertices, state space
\Omega_{i}
for
\mathscr{Z}_{i}
is uniform ergodic if its convergence to the stationary measure is geometric e.g. for all

, we have:

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in\Omega_{1}^{i}}TV(P(Z_{t,j}|\mathscr{Z}_{1}^{i}=z),\pi)\leq\frac{k_{0}}{N_{t}}\rho^{t-i}

where
\rho\in(0,1)
,
k\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, and
TV
is the total variation difference defined by
TV(\mu,\nu):=\sup_{A\in\pazocal{F}}\bigg|\mu(A)-\nu(A)\bigg|
for measure
\mu,\nu
on
\sigma
algebra
\pazocal{F}
.

Remark 2
.

The assumption of uniform ergodicity is consistent with the one in univariate stochastic processes. The
\frac{1}{N}
term implies that uniform ergodic RGP is also a multivariate stochastic process defined by
\mathscr{Z}_{1}^{T}
. This
\frac{1}{N}

We used the martingale method
(
Kontorovich 2007

)
. The notations by

And the following notion of mixing matrix is defined, which is a slight modification from the one in

3.3
Learning Theory

We introduce several notations that is used through out this work. The learning process incurred the following empirical and generalization error.

\widehat{R}_{Z_{\pazocal{T}}}(\bm{h})=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{j\in\pazocal{V}_{t}}\frac{1}{TN_{t}}L(h_{t}(X_{t,j}),Y_{t,j})\;\;\text{, and}\;\;R_{\pazocal{Z}_{\pazocal{T}}}(\bm{h})=\mathbb{E}_{Z_{\pazocal{T}}\sim\pazocal{Z}_{\pazocal{T}}}\widehat{R}_{Z_{\pazocal{T}}}(\bm{h})

We will use

to denote

, that is, the expectation over the process. Similar to the online learning problem, we can measure the algorithm’s performance through regret, which is represented as the loss on the hindsight (empirical and expected). The empirical and expected regret is written as

\widehat{\pazocal{R}}_{Z_{\pazocal{T}}}(\bm{h})=\widehat{R}_{Z_{\pazocal{T}}}(\bm{h})-\inf_{\bm{h}\in\mathscr{H}}\widehat{R}_{Z_{\pazocal{T}}}(\bm{h})\;\;\text{, and}\;\;\pazocal{R}_{\pazocal{Z}_{\pazocal{T}}}(\bm{h})=R_{{\pazocal{Z}_{\pazocal{T}}}}(\bm{h})-\inf_{\bm{h}\in\mathscr{H}}R_{\pazocal{Z}_{\pazocal{T}}}(\bm{h})

4
Combinatorial Dimensions

To approach the learnability of uniform ergodic RGPs, we need to give an upper bound on the first moment of empirical processes. Typically, symmetrization and chaining is the standard method to approach its upper bound. However, those method does not directly apply to the problem of mixing processes. As a result, we proposed novel combinatorics termed
Structural Rademacher complexity
that is suitable for the problem of interest.
In the sequel, we present the main results with occasionally the proof sketch but delay detailed technicalities to the appendix for interested readers.

4.1
Rademacher Complexity

Other types of combinatorics are also introduced for the purpose of upper-bounding the regret of online learning in the adversarial setting. Typically,
a string of previous work by
Littlestone 1988
;
Ben-David et al. 2010
;
Ben-David et al. 2009
;
Rakhlin et al. 2010
;
Rakhlin et al. 2015
introduced sequential Rademacher process and complexities to give upper bound on the regret of online learning. Their argument takes supremum over all possible trees valued functions. In the problem of upper bounding mixing processes, their method is over-pessimistic. By switching the supremum argument to the conditional expectation, we can thus obtain a finer bound for the mixing processes.

We first review the standard form of Rademacher complexity and then introduce
structural Rademacher complexities
, followed by an upper bound for it via metric entropies.

Definition 5

(Structural Rademacher Complexities)
.

For a sequence of hypothesis sets
\{\pazocal{H}_{1},\ldots\pazocal{H}_{T}\}
and
{Z_{\pazocal{T}}}
sampled according to
{\pazocal{Z}_{\pazocal{T}}}

\pazocal{T}
. Let

(t,j)\in\pazocal{T}
be a set of i.i.d. Rademacher random variables,
For the set consisting of all
T
-dimensional hypothesis sequence
\mathscr{H}=\{(h_{1},...,h_{T}):h_{t}\in\pazocal{H}_{t}\text{ for all }t\in[T]\}
, the

and its empirical variant are defined by:

\displaystyle{{\mathfrak{R}}}_{\pazocal{T}}(\mathscr{H})=({\mathfrak{R}}_{1}(\pazocal{H}_{1}),...,{\mathfrak{R}}_{T}(\pazocal{H}_{T}))^{\top},\text{ with }\;{\mathfrak{R}}_{t}(\pazocal{H}_{t})=\mathbb{E}_{{\pazocal{Z}_{\pazocal{T}}},\bm{\sigma}}\bigg[\sup_{h\in\pazocal{H}_{t}}\sum_{j\in\pazocal{V}_{t}}\frac{\sigma_{t,j}}{TN_{t}}h(X_{t,j})\bigg|\mathscr{X}_{1}^{t-1}\bigg]

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathfrak{R}}_{\pazocal{T}}(\mathscr{H})=(\widehat{{\mathfrak{R}}}_{1}(\pazocal{H}_{1}),...,\widehat{{\mathfrak{R}}}_{T}(\pazocal{H}_{T}))^{\top},\;\;\text{ with }\;\widehat{{\mathfrak{R}}}_{t}(\pazocal{H}_{t})=\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\sigma}}\bigg[\sup_{h\in\pazocal{H}_{t}}\sum_{j\in\pazocal{V}_{t}}\frac{\sigma_{t,j}}{TN_{t}}h(X_{t,j})\bigg]

4.2
Metric Entropy

N(\mathscr{H},d_{\bm{L}^{2}({Z_{\pazocal{T}}})},\epsilon)\leq N(\mathscr{H},d_{\bm{L}^{\infty}({Z_{\pazocal{T}}})},\epsilon)\leq N(\mathscr{H},d_{\infty},\epsilon)

d_{\bm{L}^{\infty}({Z_{\pazocal{T}}})}(\bm{f},\bm{g})=\sup_{t,j}\big|f_{t}(X_{t,j})-g_{t}(X_{t,j})\big|\;\text{, and }\;d_{L^{\infty}}(\bm{f},\bm{g})=\sup_{t\in[T],x\in\mathbb{X}}\big|f_{t}(x)-g_{t}(x)\big|

4.3
VC Combinatorics

In this section, we give new VC-combinatorics for the Boolean valued function family equipped with the state space of a random graph process. Readers familiar with the context of adversarial online learning might remind combinatorics like Littlestone dimensions
(
Littlestone 1988
)
that guarantee the error of deterministic strategy. This line of work is different from this one since the bound given by those adversarial dimensions doesn’t guarantee consistency.

Our main results include an extension of Sauer’s lemma and an almost dimension-free upper bound on the covering number in
d_{\bm{L}^{2}({Z_{\pazocal{T}}})}
metric. Intuitively, when the number of active vertices is not steady, say it grows consistently with the number of timestamps, the hypothesis class used for the first timestamp might not be powerful enough to shatter all vertices at time
T
. Thus we will need a larger class of functions.

We first extend the definition of shattering, VC dimensions, and growth functions for RGPs. Then we discuss the connections of these combinatorics with the metric entropies introduced in previous sections, followed by the statement of the final dimension-free upper bound for them. This bound gives upper bound on the first moment error rate in
\pazocal{O}\bigg(\sqrt{\frac{1}{T\inf_{t}N_{t}}\tt vc(\mathscr{H})}\bigg)
.

Let
\pazocal{C}_{t}
be the concept class (A set of subspaces) on
\mathbb{X}
and
\mathscr{H}=\{(\bm{1}_{C_{1}},...,\bm{1}_{C_{T}}):C_{t}\in\pazocal{C}_{t}\text{ for all }t\in[T]\}
be the hypothesis set of
T
-dimensional binary hypotheses induced by
\mathscr{C}=(\pazocal{C}_{1},...,\pazocal{C}_{T})
. We extend the notion of shattering
(
Vapnik 2013
)
to the state space of RGPs.

Definition 14

(Shattering an RGP)
.

A RGP
Z_{\pazocal{T}}\in\Omega_{1}^{T}
is said to be shattered by a sequence of concept classes
\pazocal{C}_{1},...\pazocal{C}_{T}
(or shattered by
\mathscr{C}
) if

|\pazocal{C}_{t}\cap\mathscr{X}_{t}|=2^{N_{t}}\;\;\text{ for all }t\in[T]

then for any subset
\tau\subseteq\pazocal{V}_{t}
, denote the corresponding subset of
\mathscr{Z}_{t}
by
\mathscr{Z}_{\tau}=\{Z_{i}:i\in\tau\}
and
\mathscr{X}_{\tau}=\{X_{i}:i\in\tau\}
, there exists
C\in\pazocal{C}_{t}
such that
C\cap\mathscr{X}_{t}=\mathscr{X}_{\tau}
.
\Pi_{\mathscr{H}}(Z_{\pazocal{T}})=\prod_{t=1}^{T}|\pazocal{C}_{t}\cap\mathscr{X}_{t}|
is also called
Generalized Growth Function
of
\mathscr{H}
on
{Z_{\pazocal{T}}}
.

We give a VC dimension extension directly from shattering

Definition 15

(VC-dimension)
.

The VC dimension of concept class
\mathscr{C}
on
\mathbb{Z}
is defined by

\mathtt{vc}(\mathscr{C}):=\sup\{|\pazocal{T}|=\sum_{t=1}^{T}N_{t}:\text{There exists }\;Z_{\pazocal{T}}\in\Omega_{1}^{T}\text{ that is shattered by }(\pazocal{C}_{1},..,\pazocal{C}_{T})\}

We then immediately found the connection between the standard VC dimensions of concept classes and its RGP variant.

Lemma 16
.

When the RGP is independent,
\mathtt{vc}(\mathscr{C})=\sum_{i=1}^{T}\mathtt{vc}(\pazocal{C}_{i})
, otherwise
\mathtt{vc}(\mathscr{C})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{T}\mathtt{vc}(\pazocal{C}_{i})

Then we use the generalized growth function to upper bound the covering number in Lemma
13

Definition 17

(Covering Number of Concept Classes)
.

Using
N(\mathscr{C},d,\epsilon)
to denote the
\epsilon
-covering number of a
T
-dimensional concept class
\mathscr{C}=\{(C_{1},...,C_{T}):C_{i}\in\pazocal{C}_{i}\}
with the
L^{p}({Z_{\pazocal{T}}})
norm defined by
d_{L^{p}({Z_{\pazocal{T}}})}(\bm{C},\bm{C}^{\prime})=\big[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i\in[N_{t}]}\frac{1}{TN_{t}}(\bm{1}_{C_{t}}(X_{t,i})-\bm{1}_{C_{t}^{\prime}}(X_{t,i}))^{p}\big]^{1/p}
where
Z_{\pazocal{T}}
is an RGP and
\bm{C},\bm{C}^{\prime}\in\mathscr{C}
. By definition, we have
N(\mathscr{C},d,\epsilon)=N(\mathscr{H},d,\epsilon)
.

The following lemma connects covering number with the value of growth function for the same function family

Lemma 18
.

When
\epsilon\leq 1
, we have

N(\mathscr{H},d_{L^{\infty}(Z_{\pazocal{T}})},\epsilon)=\Pi_{\mathscr{H}}(Z_{\pazocal{T}}),\text{ and }N(\mathscr{H},d_{\infty},\epsilon)=|\mathscr{C}|

We then give the counterpart of Sauer’s lemma for RGP,

Lemma 19

(Sauer’s Lemma)
.

For
Z_{\pazocal{T}}\in\Omega_{1}^{T}
and hypothesis set
\mathscr{H}
with index space
\pazocal{T}
, the growth function is upper-bounded by:

\Pi_{\mathscr{H}}(Z_{\pazocal{T}})\leq\prod_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{k=0}^{\min\{\mathtt{vc}(\pazocal{C}_{t}),N_{t}\}}\binom{N_{t}}{k}\leq\prod_{t=1}^{T}\bigg(\frac{eN_{t}}{\min\{\mathtt{vc}(\pazocal{C}_{t}),N_{t}\}}\bigg)^{\min\{\mathtt{vc}(\pazocal{C}_{t}),N_{t}\}}

Remark 20
.

For the general RGP, the upper bound is sharp. When
N_{t}=\mathtt{vc}(\pazocal{C}_{t})
and assume that no set of cardinality greater than
N_{t}
can be shattered by
\pazocal{C}_{t}
and all subsets of cardinality
\mathtt{vc}(\pazocal{C}_{t})
can be shattered, the first inequality will become equality.

We note that the upper bound is still vacuous as the sample size
N
of each timestamp becomes large. This bound is the best we achieved without extra assumptions on the communication pattern. Then we give a finer upper bound for independent and uniform ergodic processes. Our technique to obtain upper bounds is probabilistic extraction
(
Dudley 1978
)
on the mixing measure. We start with the independent/non-communicative regime.

Lemma 21
.

Assuming that
{Z_{\pazocal{T}}}\in\Omega_{1}^{T}
is sampled according to a non-communicative
{\pazocal{Z}_{\pazocal{T}}}
. Let
f_{1},...,f_{m}
be the functions on
{Z_{\pazocal{T}}}
such that

\|\bm{f}_{i}\|_{\infty}\leq 1,\;\;\;\;\|\bm{f}_{i}-\bm{f}_{j}\|_{\mathbb{E}\bm{L}^{2}({Z_{\pazocal{T}}})}\geq\epsilon\;\;\;\;\text{for all }1\leq i<j\leq m

Or in other words,
\bm{f}_{1},\ldots,\bm{f}_{m}
formed a
\epsilon
-net on
\mathscr{H}
.
Then there exist a
z_{\pazocal{T}}
sampled according to
{\pazocal{Z}_{\pazocal{T}}}
with
\frac{T^{2}}{\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{1}{N_{t}}}>\frac{16}{9}\epsilon^{-4}\log m
such that:

d_{\bm{L}^{2}(z_{\pazocal{T}})}(\bm{f},\bm{g})=\|\bm{f}_{i}-\bm{f}_{j}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(z_{\pazocal{T}})}\geq\frac{\epsilon}{2}\;\;\;\;\text{for all }1\leq i<j\leq m,

with

\|\bm{f}_{i}-\bm{f}_{j}\|_{\mathbb{E}\bm{L}^{2}(Z_{\pazocal{T}})}=d_{\mathbb{E}\bm{L}^{2}({Z_{\pazocal{T}}})}(\bm{f},\bm{g})=\bigg(\mathbb{E}_{{Z_{\pazocal{T}}}}\bigg[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{j\in[N_{t}]}\frac{1}{TN_{t}}\big(f_{t}(X_{t,j})-g_{t}(X_{t,j})\big)^{2}\bigg]\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\

be the expected metric.

Then it comes to the upper bound for covering number. The idea is proving measure-independent upper bound.

Theorem 22
.

When the
{Z_{\pazocal{T}}}\in\Omega_{1}^{T}
is sampled according to a non-communicative
{\pazocal{Z}_{\pazocal{T}}}
, for all
\epsilon\in(0,1]
we have:

\displaystyle\log N(\mathscr{H},d_{\mathbb{E}\bm{L}^{2}({\pazocal{Z}_{\pazocal{T}}})},\epsilon)

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{t=1}^{T}\bigg(\frac{32e}{9T\epsilon^{4}}\bigg)^{2\mathtt{vc}(\pazocal{H}_{t})}

\displaystyle\|\widehat{\mathfrak{R}}_{\pazocal{T}}(\mathscr{H})\|_{1}

\displaystyle=\pazocal{O}\bigg(\sqrt{\frac{1}{T|V|}\mathtt{vc}(\mathscr{H})}\bigg)

Then we move to the uniform ergodic process. It is important to note the technicalities involved when moving from the concentration of independent to mixing measure. This follows the similar martingale method and coupling construction.

Lemma 23
.

Assume that
{Z_{\pazocal{T}}}\in\Omega_{1}^{T}
is sampled according to ergodic
{\pazocal{Z}_{\pazocal{T}}}
. Let
\bm{f}_{1},...,\bm{f}_{m}
be the functions with the same conditions of lemma
21
.
Then there exists a
z_{\pazocal{T}}
sampled according to
{\pazocal{Z}_{\pazocal{T}}}
with
\frac{1}{\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{1}{N_{t}^{2}}}\geq\frac{16\|\bm{\Gamma}(\pazocal{T})\|_{2}^{2}}{9}T^{-4}\epsilon^{-4}\log\frac{m(m-1)}{2}
such that:

d_{\bm{L}^{2}(z_{\pazocal{T}})}(\bm{f},\bm{g})=\|\bm{f}_{i}-\bm{f}_{j}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(z_{\pazocal{T}})}\geq\frac{\epsilon}{2}\;\;\;\;\text{for all }1\leq i<j\leq m,

with
\bm{\Gamma}(\pazocal{T})_{t,k}=\sqrt{N_{k}}\bm{\Gamma}_{t,k}
and
\bm{\Gamma}
is the mixing matrix of RGP.

Theorem 24
.

When the
{Z_{\pazocal{T}}}
is sampled according to ergodic
{\pazocal{Z}_{\pazocal{T}}}
with mixing matrix
\bm{\Gamma}
, for all
\epsilon\in(0,1]
we have:

\displaystyle N(\mathscr{H},d_{\bm{L}^{2}({Z_{\pazocal{T}}})},\epsilon)

\displaystyle=\prod_{t=1}^{T}\bigg(\frac{32e\|\bm{\Gamma}\|_{2}}{9T\epsilon^{4}}\bigg)^{2\mathtt{vc}(\pazocal{H}_{t})}

\displaystyle\|\widehat{\mathfrak{R}}_{\pazocal{T}}(\mathscr{H})\|_{1}

\displaystyle=\pazocal{O}\bigg(\sqrt{\frac{1}{T|V|}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathtt{vc}(\pazocal{H}_{i})}\bigg)

Remark 25
.

Our result suggests that
\|\bm{\Gamma}\|_{2}
mainly influences the logarithmic factor in the bound. When
\bm{\Gamma}=\bm{I}
, the communication is fully eliminated and the mixing case reduced to the independent case.

5
Consistency Guarantees

In this section, we leverage the newly proposed complexities to obtain uniform learnability, and furthermore, the consistency of the Empirical Risk Minimizer (ERM). The discussion focuses on the uniform ergodicity but is paired with the independent regime as a comparison.

5.1
Empirical Risk Minimizer

The empirical risk minimizer is a set of optimal hypotheses acting on the samples. For a given problem, we are interested in two main questions regarding the empirical risk minimizer.
(1)
How the empirical risk minimizer generalizes to unknown samples.
(2)
How the empirical risk minimizer compared with the Bayes risk minimizer. The first question can be understood via uniform learn-ability. The second question is more involved to answer, where the expected regret is a better characterization for online learning.

The empirical risk minimizer (ERM)
\bm{h}_{\text{ERM}}\in\mathscr{H}
for some
{Z_{\pazocal{T}}}
drawn from
\mathbb{Z}
according to
{\pazocal{Z}_{\pazocal{T}}}
satisfies:

h_{\text{ERM},t}=\argmin_{h\in\pazocal{H}_{t}}\sum_{j\in[N_{t}]}\frac{1}{TN_{t}}L(h(X_{t,j}),Y_{t,j})\;\;\text{ for all }t\in[T]

An important utility of structural Rademacher complexity is upper-bounding the expected regret of ERM. The following bound gives the first moment control on the regret.

Lemma 26

(Expected Regret Bound of ERM)
.

Let
\mathbb{Y}=\{-1,1\}
and
L(h(x),y)=\frac{1-h(x)y}{2}
, we have

\mathbb{E}_{{\pazocal{Z}_{\pazocal{T}}}}\pazocal{R}_{\pazocal{Z}_{\pazocal{T}}}(\bm{h}_{\text{ERM}})\leq\mathbb{E}_{{\pazocal{Z}_{\pazocal{T}}}}\|{{\mathfrak{R}}}_{\pazocal{T}}(\mathscr{H})\|_{1}

Remarkably, if we consider the degenerated case where
|V|=1
, we can easily see that the expected 1-norm of structural Rademacher complexity is a strict lower bound on the sequential Rademacher complexity
(
Rakhlin et al. 2015
)
, hence we gave a better guarantee for the expected regret.

The bound on expectation can also be strengthened to the non-asymptotics. However, this relies on more assumptions, which we discussed in the following sections.

5.2
Non-communicative Networks

Consider a non-communicative network, where the process is assumed to be independent. We can establish uniform learnability for it.

Theorem 27
.

Let
Z_{\pazocal{T}}
be a
\{-1,1\}
labeled sample drawn from
\mathbb{Z}
according to an independent RGP
\pazocal{Z}_{\pazocal{T}}
.
Assuming the loss function
L
is given by
L(h(x),y)=\frac{1-yh(x)}{2}
.
Then, for any
\delta\in(0,1)
, with probability at least
1-\delta
over
{Z_{\pazocal{T}}}
, the following holds for all
\bm{h}\in\mathscr{H}
.

\displaystyle R_{{\pazocal{Z}_{\pazocal{T}}}}(\bm{h})\leq\widehat{R}_{{Z_{\pazocal{T}}}}(\bm{h})+\|\widehat{\mathfrak{R}}_{\pazocal{T}}(\mathscr{H})\|_{1}+\frac{3}{T}\sqrt{\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{1}{2N_{t}}\log\frac{2}{\delta}}

With this result, we can obtain the consistency of ERM for this problem.

Theorem 28

(ERM on Non-communicative networks)
.

The empirical risk minimizer for the non-communicative network problem yield the following guarantees on the expected regret with probablity at least
1-\delta
for all
\delta\in(0,1)
:

\displaystyle\pazocal{R}_{{\pazocal{Z}_{\pazocal{T}}}}(\bm{h}_{\text{ERM}})\leq 2\|\widehat{\mathfrak{R}}_{\pazocal{T}}(\mathscr{H})\|_{1}+\frac{6}{T}\sqrt{\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{1}{2N_{t}}\log\frac{2}{\delta}}

Remark 29
.

Note that the non-communicative case has faster decay with regard to
T
as compared to the number of activated vertices
N_{t}
in each timestamp. If the selection of activated vertices in each timestamp is arbitrary, then there will be a trade-off between exploration (seeing more timestamps) and exploitation (seeing more samples in the same timestamp).

5.3
Uniform Ergodic Network

Here we formally obtain the learning guarantees for a uniform ergodic network. With the aforementioned assumptions, we can also achieve similar guarantees for the ERM on this problem. We first present a simplified version of uniform learnability using the mixing matrix. Then we refined the global ergodic setting to gave sharper analysis, followed by the guarantees on the expected regret.

Theorem 30

(Ergodic RGP)
.

Let
Z_{\pazocal{T}}
be a
-1/1
labeled sample drawn from
\Omega_{1}^{T}
according to an uniform ergodic RGP
\pazocal{Z}_{\pazocal{T}}
. Let
L
be the loss defined by
L(\widehat{y},y)=\frac{1-y\widehat{y}}{2}
. Then for any
\delta>0
, with probability at least
1-\delta
, for all
\bm{h}\in\mathscr{H}
where
\mathscr{H}
is
1
-Lipschitz, the following holds:

\displaystyle R_{Z_{\pazocal{T}}}(\bm{h})

\displaystyle\leq\widehat{R}_{Z_{\pazocal{T}}}(\bm{h})+\|\widehat{\mathfrak{R}}_{\pazocal{T}}(\mathscr{H})\|_{1}+\frac{3}{T}\|\bm{N}^{\frac{1}{2}}\bm{\Gamma}\cdot\bm{c}\|_{2}\sqrt{2\log\frac{2}{\delta}}

with
\bm{c}=(\frac{1}{N_{1}},\ldots,\frac{1}{N_{T}})^{\top}
and for all
t<k
:

\displaystyle\Gamma_{t,k}=\begin{cases}k_{0}\rho^{k-t}&\text{if}\ k\geq t\\
1,&\text{if}\ k=t\\
0,&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

Intuitively, we found that the non-asymptotic rate is
O\big(\frac{1}{T\sqrt{|V|}}\big)
. Coupled with the upperbound on structural Rademacher complexity we can achieve the right upperbound of order
O\big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{T|V|}}\big)
. This guarantees the consistency. However, we may expect a faster concentration, done through a refinement argument.

Note that in the previous bound, we consider a batch of vertices as a whole. This can sometimes yield over-pessimistic estimation. We can refine the upper bound through the introduction of reachable sets.

Definition 31

(Reachable Set)
.

We say a set
V_{t,j}^{k}\subset\pazocal{V}_{k}
is reachable from the vertex indexed by
(t,j)
if
\forall s\in V_{t,j}^{k}
, the message sent by
(t,j)
reaches
s
. We denote
card(V_{t,j}^{k})=\pazocal{N}_{t,j}^{k}

And the following bound gives a nicer guarantee.

Theorem 32

(Refined upper bound for globally ergodic RGP)
.

Under the analogous conditions and notations of theorem
30
, for any
\delta\geq 0
, with probability at least
1-\delta
:

\displaystyle R_{\pazocal{Z}_{\pazocal{T}}}(\bm{h})

\displaystyle\leq\widehat{R}_{Z_{\pazocal{T}}}(\bm{h})+\|\widehat{\mathfrak{R}}_{\pazocal{T}}(\mathscr{H})\|_{1}+3\sqrt{\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{t}}\bigg(\sum_{k=t+1}^{T}\frac{\pazocal{N}_{t,j}^{k}k_{0}\rho^{k-t}}{N_{k}^{2}T}\bigg)^{2}}\sqrt{2\log\frac{2}{\delta}}

when the RGP is globally ergodic.

Remark 33
.

The refined bound is remarkable since it yields a sharper tail, which converges in the order
O\big(\frac{1}{|V|^{3/2}T})
in sparse networks. When the network is dense (e.g.,
\pazocal{N}_{t,j}^{k}=\Theta(|N_{k}|)
), the rate remains the same with the unrefined one.

The following theorem then guarantees the consistency of ERM on learning the uniform ergodic network.

Theorem 34

(ERM on Ergodic Network)
.

The empirical risk minimizer for the uniform ergodic network yield the following with probability at least
1-\delta
:

\displaystyle\pazocal{R}_{{\pazocal{Z}_{\pazocal{T}}}}(\bm{h}_{\text{ERM}})\leq 2\mathbb{E}_{\pazocal{Z}_{\pazocal{T}}}\|{{\mathfrak{R}}}_{\pazocal{T}}(\mathscr{H})\|_{1}+2\sqrt{\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{t}}\bigg(\sum_{k=t+1}^{T}\frac{\pazocal{N}_{t,j}^{k}k_{0}\rho^{k-t}}{N_{k}^{2}T}\bigg)^{2}}\sqrt{2\log\frac{2}{\delta}}

Open Problems and Future Work

We conclude with several open problems for future exploration.

1.

The generalization bound for uniform ergodic RGP is improvable as the uniform argument can be relaxed.

2.

The uniformity in the ergodic condition is very strong. We conjectured that weaker regularity conditions also yield uniform convergence.

3.

We did not include efficient algorithms in this work and left those as future work.

7
Conclusions

This work studied the problem of learning a network with communicative vertices. We studied the uniform ergodic network and compared it with the non-communicative counterpart. We introduced the structural Rademacher complexity and discussed its relationship with the VC theory. Our bound yielded a sharper bound on the first moment error of the stochastic processes than the previous work. We gave uniform learn-ability via the martingale method and Marton coupling, which leads to the consistency guarantee of ERM.

A function

\displaystyle\psi(0)=0.\;\;\;\psi(x)\rightarrow\infty\;\text{ as }x\rightarrow\infty

Any sub-gaussian random variables

\displaystyle\bm{P}\bigg([\sup_{\bm{h}\in\mathscr{H}}[\widehat{R}_{{Z_{\pazocal{T}}}}(\bm{h})-R_{{\pazocal{Z}_{\pazocal{T}}}}(\bm{h})]]-\mathbb{E}_{{\pazocal{Z}_{\pazocal{T}}}}[\sup_{\bm{h}\in\mathscr{H}}[\widehat{R}_{{Z_{\pazocal{T}}}}(\bm{h})-R_{{\pazocal{Z}_{\pazocal{T}}}}(\bm{h})]]\geq\epsilon\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\bigg(-\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{j\in\pazocal{V}_{t}}\big(\sum_{k=t}^{T}\pazocal{N}_{t,j}^{k}\frac{k_{0}}{N_{k}^{2}T}\rho^{k-t}\big)^{2}}\bigg)

By substituting the r.h.s. of above two inequality with
\delta/2
and use union bound to combine them together, we complete the proof.

A Note on Knowledge Distillation Loss Function for Object Classification
Defang Chen

Abstract
This research note provides a quick introduction to the knowledge distillation loss function used in object classification. In particular, we discuss its connection to a previously proposed logits matching loss function. We further treat knowledge distillation as a specific form of output regularization and demonstrate its connection to label smoothing and entropy-based regularization.



1
Introduction

Knowledge distillation (KD) was proposed as a model compression technique to distill the knowledge in a powerful yet cumbersome teacher model into a lightweight student model, hoping that it will enhance the student generalization ability
[
8
]
. Since the advent of knowledge distillation, variant approaches have been developed to improve its effectiveness and widen its applications, including feature distillation
[
4
,
5
]
, collaborative distillation
[
3
]
, and diffusion-based distillation
[
6
,
15
,
7
]
. In the vanilla KD
[
8
]
, class predictions between teacher and student models are aligned with a newly introduced hyper-parameter—
temperature
in the softmax activation to control the softness of predicted distributions. In this way, Hinton and his collaborators
[
8
]
argued that the pioneering model compression technique termed logits matching
[
2
]
is actually a special case of their proposed approach, provided that the temperature is much higher than the logits in order of magnitude and the logits are zero-mean normalized explicitly. In this note, we provide detailed derivations to review and deepen our understanding of this connection.
We first prove that with a single
infinity temperature
condition, we could already build the connection between these two loss functions, although attached with an extra regularization. We further point out that
equal-mean normalization
for logits is enough to establish the exact equivalence. Finally, we discuss knowledge distillation from the output regularization perspective.

2
Preliminary

We first briefly recap the basic concept of object classification using deep neural networks, especially from the perspective of knowledge distillation. Then, a formal description of the standard knowledge distillation and logits matching loss functions are introduced with necessary notations.

2.1
Distilling Knowledge from Human Labellings

Given a training dataset
\mathcal{D}=\{(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\}
consisting of
n
objects from
k
categories, we denote the category label of the object
\mathbf{x}
as
\mathbf{y}
.
Take one-class image classification problem as an example, we have
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{c\times h\times w}
, where
c
denotes the channel dimension,
h
and
w
denote the spatial dimensions. Besides, only one element in the vector
\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
equals one and the other elements all equal zero (e.g.,
\mathbf{y}_{i}=1
, and
\mathbf{y}_{j}=0
,
\forall j\neq i
if this object belongs to the
i
-th category).
We then train a deep neural network with the parameter
\boldsymbol{\theta}
to learn a mapping from the object space to the category space.

Given the object
\mathbf{x}
, we denote the unnormalized prediction of a deep neural network as
\mathbf{z}
, which is also known as
logits
, and denote its softmax-based normalized version as
\mathbf{p}
, which is also known as
class predictions
. Mathematically, we have
\mathbf{p}_{i}=\exp\left(\mathbf{z}_{i}\right)/\sum_{j=1}^{k}\exp\left(\mathbf{z}_{j}\right)
, where
\mathbf{z}_{i}
and
\mathbf{p}_{i}
denote the
i
-th element of
\mathbf{z}
and
\mathbf{p}
, respectively.
The model parameter
\boldsymbol{\theta}
is randomly initialized and updated using the following cross-entropy loss function:

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CE}}(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{p})=-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathbf{y}_{i}\log\mathbf{p}_{i},\quad\text{or equivalently},\quad\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{p})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathbf{y}_{i}\log\frac{\mathbf{y}_{i}}{\mathbf{p}_{i}},

(1)

with the gradient as follows

\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CE}}}{\partial\mathbf{p}_{j}}=-\frac{\mathbf{y}_{j}}{\mathbf{p}_{j}},\qquad\frac{\partial\mathbf{p}_{j}}{\partial\mathbf{z}_{i}}=\begin{cases}\mathbf{p}_{j}(1-\mathbf{p}_{j})&\mbox{$i=j$}\\
-\mathbf{p}_{i}\mathbf{p}_{j}&\mbox{$i\neq j$}.\end{cases}

(2)

Then, we take the partial derivative of
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CE}}
with respect to
\mathbf{z}_{i}

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CE}}}{\partial\mathbf{z}_{i}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CE}}}{\partial\mathbf{p}_{j}}\frac{\partial\mathbf{p}_{j}}{\partial\mathbf{z}_{i}}=-\sum_{j}\frac{\mathbf{y}_{j}}{\mathbf{p}_{j}}\frac{\partial\mathbf{p}_{j}}{\partial\mathbf{z}_{i}}=-\left[\underbrace{\frac{\mathbf{y}_{j}}{\mathbf{p}_{j}}\mathbf{p}_{j}\left(1-\mathbf{p}_{j}\right)}_{j=i}+\underbrace{\sum_{j}\frac{\mathbf{y}_{j}}{\mathbf{p}_{j}}\left(-\mathbf{p}_{i}\mathbf{p}_{j}\right)}_{j\neq i}\right]

(3)

\displaystyle=-\left[\underbrace{\mathbf{y}_{j}\left(1-\mathbf{p}_{j}\right)}_{j=i}+\underbrace{\sum_{j}\mathbf{y}_{j}\left(-\mathbf{p}_{i}\right)}_{j\neq i}\right]=-\left[\mathbf{y}_{i}-\mathbf{p}_{i}\sum_{j}\mathbf{y}_{j}\right]=\mathbf{p}_{i}-\mathbf{y}_{i}.

Remark 1
.

The standard learning objective for object classification can be interpreted as distilling knowledge from human labellings, where the regular model acts as a “student” model and the ground-truth label for each object acts as the output of a(n) “teacher/oracle” model.

Label Smoothing
:
To alleviate the over-fitting issue in model training or the mis-labeling issue in data collection, label smoothing
[
13
]
technique was proposed. It replaces the hard label
\mathbf{y}
as the smoothed label
\mathbf{y}^{\prime}
, with
\mathbf{y}^{\prime}=(1-\alpha)\mathbf{y}+\alpha\mathbf{u}
and
\mathbf{u}_{i}=1/k
,
\forall i
. In this case, the above loss function becomes
1

1

We omit the constant term irrelevant to the parameter optimization.

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{LS}}(\mathbf{y}^{\prime},\mathbf{p})=(1-\alpha)\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CE}}(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{p})+\alpha\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{p}).

(4)

Confidence Penalty
: Another similar output-based regularization reverses the KL term in the above equation, aiming to penalize a low-entropy model prediction
[

]

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CP}}(\mathbf{y}^{\prime},\mathbf{p})=(1-\alpha)\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CE}}(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{p})+\alpha\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{u}).

(5)

The above two loss functions can be unified using skew-Jensen divergence

2.2
Distilling Knowledge from Neural Networks

We next denote class predictions of the teacher model with the parameter
\boldsymbol{\theta}^{t}
and the student model with the parameter
\boldsymbol{\theta}^{s}
as
\mathbf{p}^{t}
and
\mathbf{p}^{s}
, which are produced by normalized logits
\mathbf{z}^{t}
and
\mathbf{z}^{s}
, respectively.

A straightforward approach to distill knowledge from a pre-trained powerful teacher model to a simple student model is adopting the following logits matching loss function
[
2
,
1
]
to update the student model’s parameter
\boldsymbol{\theta}^{s}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{LM}}(\mathbf{z}^{t},\mathbf{z}^{s})=\frac{1}{2k}\|\mathbf{z}^{t}-\mathbf{z}^{s}\|^{2}_{2}=\frac{1}{2k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\mathbf{z}^{t}_{i}-\mathbf{z}^{s}_{i}\right)^{2},

(6)

with the gradient
\partial\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{LM}}/\partial\mathbf{z}^{s}_{i}=\left(\mathbf{z}^{s}_{i}-\mathbf{z}^{t}_{i}\right)/k
, and the gradient
\partial\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{LM}}/\partial\boldsymbol{\theta}^{s}=\partial\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{LM}}/\partial\mathbf{z}^{s}\cdot\partial\mathbf{z}^{s}/\partial\boldsymbol{\theta}^{s}
.

Similarly, we can also adopt the vanilla knowledge distillation loss function
[
8
]
for knowledge transfer. This approach introduces temperature
\tau
as a hyper-parameter to soften the model-predicted probability distributions, and modifies the relationship between logits
\mathbf{z}
and predictions
\mathbf{p}
as follows

\mathbf{p}^{t}_{i}=\frac{\exp\left(\mathbf{z}^{t}_{i}/\tau\right)}{\sum_{j=1}^{k}\exp\left(\mathbf{z}^{t}_{j}/\tau\right)},\qquad\mathbf{p}^{s}_{i}=\frac{\exp\left(\mathbf{z}^{s}_{i}/\tau\right)}{\sum_{j=1}^{k}\exp\left(\mathbf{z}^{s}_{j}/\tau\right)}.

(7)

The vanilla knowledge distillation loss function is

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{KD}}(\mathbf{p}^{t},\mathbf{p}^{s})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathbf{p}^{t}_{i}\log\frac{\mathbf{p}^{t}_{i}}{\mathbf{p}^{s}_{i}}=-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathbf{p}^{t}_{i}\log\mathbf{p}^{s}_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathbf{p}^{t}_{i}\log\mathbf{p}^{t}_{i}.

(8)

The second term in Equation (
8
) is a negative entropy of
\mathbf{p}^{t}
, which is irrelevant to the update of student’s parameters.
Similarly, we take the partial derivative of
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{KD}}
w.r.t.
\mathbf{z}^{s}_{i}
:

\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{KD}}}{\partial\mathbf{p}^{s}_{j}}=-\frac{\mathbf{p}^{t}_{j}}{\mathbf{p}^{s}_{j}},\qquad\frac{\partial\mathbf{p}^{s}_{j}}{\partial\mathbf{z}^{s}_{i}}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\tau}\mathbf{p}^{s}_{j}\left(1-\mathbf{p}^{s}_{j}\right)&\mbox{$i=j$}\\
-\frac{1}{\tau}\mathbf{p}^{s}_{i}\mathbf{p}^{s}_{j}&\mbox{$i\neq j$}.\end{cases}

(9)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{KD}}}{\partial\mathbf{z}^{s}_{i}}=\sum_{j}\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{KD}}}{\partial\mathbf{p}^{s}_{j}}\frac{\partial\mathbf{p}^{s}_{j}}{\partial\mathbf{z}^{s}_{i}}=-\sum_{j}\frac{\mathbf{p}^{t}_{j}}{\mathbf{p}^{s}_{j}}\frac{\partial\mathbf{p}^{s}_{j}}{\partial\mathbf{z}^{s}_{i}}=\frac{1}{\tau}\left(\mathbf{p}^{s}_{i}-\mathbf{p}^{t}_{i}\right).

(10)

For

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{LM_{r}}}=\frac{1}{2k}\|\mathbf{z}^{t}-\mathbf{z}^{s}\|^{2}_{2}-\frac{1}{2k^{2}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mathbf{z}^{s}_{j}-\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mathbf{z}^{t}_{j}\right)^{2}+\mathrm{Constant}.

Remark 3
.

Based on the infinity large temperature and equal-mean normalization for logits (i.e.,
\sum_{j}^{k}\mathbf{z}^{s}_{j}=\sum_{j}^{k}\mathbf{z}^{t}_{j}
), the gradient of
\tau^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{KD}}
equals to that of
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{LM}}
and thus we can conclude that the effects of these two losses are exactly the same.

2.3
Knowledge Distillation as Output Regularization

In practice, the knowledge distillation loss function is generally used together with the original cross-entropy loss function. That is to say, we adopt the following loss function to optimize parameters of a student model:

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{KD}^{\prime}}=(1-\alpha)\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CE}}(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{p}^{s})+\alpha\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{KD}}(\mathbf{p}^{t},\mathbf{p}^{s}).

(13)

Comparing the loss function in Equation (
4
), we conclude that class predictions of the teacher model act as adaptive label smoothing to prevent the student output being over-confident
[
14
]

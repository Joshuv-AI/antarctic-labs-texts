Ling Wang and Bin Zhou ∗ \text{Zhou}^{*}

Abstract
. In this paper, we give several new approaches to study interior estimates for a class of fourth order equations of Monge-Ampère type. First, we prove interior estimates for the homogeneous equation in dimension two by using the partial Legendre transform. As an application, we obtain a new proof of the Bernstein theorem without using Caffarelli-Gutiérrez’s estimate, including the Chern conjecture on affine maximal surfaces. For the inhomogeneous equation, we also obtain a new proof in dimension two by an integral method relying on the Monge-Ampère Sobolev inequality. This proof works even when the right hand side is singular. In higher dimensions, we obtain the interior regularity in terms of integral bounds on the second derivatives and the inverse of the determinant.

1.
Introduction

We study the regularity of the following fourth order equations of
Monge-Ampère type

(1.1)

U^{ij}w_{ij}=f,

where
\{U^{ij}\}
is the cofactor matrix of
D^{2}u
of an unknown uniformly convex function,
and

(1.2)

w=\begin{cases}\ [\det D^{2}u]^{-(1-\theta)},&\theta\geq 0,\ \ \theta\neq 1,\\[4.0pt]
\ \log\det D^{2}u,&\theta=1.\end{cases}

When
\theta=\frac{1}{n+2}
, this is the
affine mean curvature equation
in affine geometry
[
Ch
]
.
When
\theta=0
, it is
Abreu’s equation
arising from the problem of extremal metrics on toric manifolds in Kähler geometry
[
Ab
]
, and is equivalent to

\sum_{i,j}\frac{\partial^{2}u^{ij}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}=f,

where
\{u^{ij}\}
is the inverse matrix of
D^{2}u
. The regularity of (
1.1
) has been extensively studied before, see
[
TW1
,
TW2
,
D
,
Z1
,
Z2
,
CHLS
,
Le1
,
Le2
,
CW
]
. This equation is usually treated as a system of a Monge-Ampère equation and a linearized Monge-Ampère equation. Therefore,
in previous works, its regularity relies heavily on Caffarelli-Gutiérrez’s deep result on the interior regularity of the linearized Monge-Ampère equation
[
CG
]
, which was later extended by
[
LS
,
GN1
,
GN2
]
to the the boundary and to higher order estimates. In this paper, we investigate the interior estimates of (
1.1
) by
several new approaches.
We will mainly concentrate on the case
\theta\in[0,1]
due to the interesting geometric background.

We first consider the case of the homogeneous equation

(1.3)

U^{ij}w_{ij}=0,

where
w
is given by (
1.2
).
We apply the partial Legendre transform to give a new proof of the interior estimates of (
1.3
) in dimension two.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume
n=2
and
\theta\in[0,1]
.
Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a convex domain and let

u
be a smooth convex solution to equation (
1.3
) on
\Omega
satisfying

(1.4)

0<\lambda\leq\det D^{2}u\leq\Lambda.

Then for any
\Omega^{\prime}\Subset\Omega
, there exists a constant
C>0
depending on
\sup_{\Omega}|u|
,
\lambda
,
\Lambda
,
\theta
and
dist(\Omega^{\prime},\partial\Omega)
, such that

\|u\|_{C^{4,\alpha}(\Omega^{\prime})}\leq C.

The partial Legendre transform for the fourth order equation was first used in
[
LZ
]
where the authors deal with the second boundary value problem. After the partial Legendre transform, equation (
1.3
) becomes a quasi-linear second order equation (see (
2.3
)) for the determinant.
The main ingredient in our proof is an interior integral gradient estimate (Theorem
2.2
).
When
\theta\in\left[0,\frac{1}{4}\right]
, condition (
1.4
) holds by the determinant estimates and arguments of strict convexity
[
TW1
,
Z1
]
. By Theorem
1.1
and a rescaling argument as in
[
TW1
, Theorem 2.1]
, we obtain a new proof of the following Bernstein theorem
[
TW1
,
JL
,
Z1
]
without using Caffarelli-Gutiérrez’s theory.

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume
n=2
and
0\leq\theta\leq\frac{1}{4}
.
Let
u
be an entire smooth uniformly convex solution to (
1.3
) on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
Then
u
is a quadratic polynomial.

In the case of the inhomogeneous equation and in higher dimensions, the partial Legendre transform does not work.
We will investigate the interior regularity by an integral method motivated by De Giorgi-Nash-Moser’s theory.
Consider the inhomogeneous equation with general right hand side term

(1.5)

U^{ij}w_{ij}=f(x,u,Du,D^{2}u),

where
w
given by (
1.2
) with
\theta\in[0,1]
.
This equation is introduced by
[

,

]
in the study of convex functionals with a convexity constraint related to the Rochet-Choné model for the monopolist problem in economics.
It is said to be singular since the right hand side term depends on

.
A typical example considered in
[

,

,

Note that when

1.4

can be weakened to

defined as (

-estimate of the solution (Theorem

Assume

\theta\in[0,1]
. Let

(1) It is clear that the above theorem applies to the case (

) for any

(2) When

) may not hold. We give a partial result by assuming integral bounds on the second derivatives and the inverse of the determinant.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

,

Corollary 1.6

Let

be an entire smooth uniformly convex solution to (

) with
\theta\in[0,1]
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Suppose there are

and

(1.7)

\int_{B_{R}(0)}|D^{2}u|^{p}+(\det D^{2}u)^{-q}\,dx\leq CR^{n}\,,\quad\forall\,R>0.

2.
The homogeneous equation in dimension two

In this section, we present a new proof for the interior estimate for the homogeneous equation without Caffarelli-Gutiérrez’s theory.

2.1.
The new equation under partial Legendre transform

We first focus on the dimension two case. Write
u(x)=u(x_{1},x_{2})
.
The partial Legendre transform in the
x_{1}
-variable is

(2.1)

u^{\star}(\xi,\eta)=x_{1}u_{x_{1}}(x_{1},x_{2})-u(x_{1},x_{2}),

where

y=(\xi,\eta)=\mathcal{P}(x_{1},x_{2}):=(u_{x_{1}},x_{2})\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega)=:\Omega^{\star}.

We have

\frac{\partial(\xi,\eta)}{\partial(x_{1},x_{2})}=\begin{pmatrix}u_{x_{1}x_{1}}&\ \ u_{x_{1}x_{2}}\\[4.0pt]
0&\ \ 1\\
\end{pmatrix},\quad\text{and }\ \frac{\partial(x_{1},x_{2})}{\partial(\xi,\eta)}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{u_{x_{1}x_{1}}}&\ \ -\frac{u_{x_{1}x_{2}}}{u_{x_{1}x_{1}}}\\[4.0pt]
0&\ \ 1\\
\end{pmatrix}.

Hence,

(2.2)

u^{\star}_{\xi}=x_{1},\ u^{\star}_{\xi\xi}=\frac{1}{u_{x_{1}x_{1}}},\ u^{\star}_{\eta}=-u_{x_{2}},\ u^{\star}_{\eta\eta}=-\frac{\det D^{2}u}{u_{x_{1}x_{1}}},\ u^{\star}_{\xi\eta}=-\frac{u_{x_{1}x_{2}}}{u_{x_{1}x_{1}}}.

The partial Legendre transform has been used widely in the study of the Monge-Ampère equation
[
DS
,
F
,
GP
,
LS
,
Li
]
. Here we apply it to equation (
1.3
).

In order to derive the equation under the partial Legendre transform, we consider the associated functionals
of (
1.3
)

A_{\theta}(u)=\begin{cases}\ \int_{\Omega}[\det D^{2}u]^{\theta}\,dx,&\theta>0,\ \ \theta\neq 1,\\[4.0pt]
\ \int_{\Omega}\log\det D^{2}u\,dx,&\theta=0,\\[4.0pt]
\ \int_{\Omega}\det D^{2}u\log\det D^{2}u\,dx,&\theta=1.\end{cases}

The case of
\theta=0
is essentially included in
[
LZ
]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
u
be a uniformly convex solution to (
1.3
) in
\Omega
. Then in
\Omega^{\star}=\mathcal{P}(\Omega)
, its partial Legendre transform
u^{\star}
satisfies

(2.3)

w^{\star}w^{\star}_{\xi\xi}+w^{\star}_{\eta\eta}+(\theta-1){w^{\star}_{\xi}}^{2}+\frac{\theta-2}{w^{\star}}{w^{\star}_{\eta}}^{2}=0,

Here
w^{\star}=-\frac{u^{\star}_{\eta\eta}}{u^{\star}_{\xi\xi}}
.

Proof.

As

\det D^{2}u=-\frac{u^{\star}_{\eta\eta}}{u^{\star}_{\xi\xi}},\ \ dxdy=u^{\star}_{\xi\xi}\,d\xi d\eta,

we have

\displaystyle A_{\theta}(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega^{\star}}\left(-\frac{u^{\star}_{\eta\eta}}{u^{\star}_{\xi\xi}}\right)^{\theta}u^{\star}_{\xi\xi}\,d\xi d\eta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega^{\star}}(-u^{\star}_{\eta\eta})^{\theta}{u^{\star}_{\xi\xi}}^{1-\theta}\,d\xi d\eta=:A_{\theta}^{\star}(u^{\star}),\ \theta\in(0,1);

\displaystyle A_{0}(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega^{\star}}\log\left(-\frac{u^{\star}_{\eta\eta}}{u^{\star}_{\xi\xi}}\right)u^{\star}_{\xi\xi}\,d\xi d\eta=:A_{0}^{\star}(u^{\star});

\displaystyle A_{1}(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega^{\star}}\left(-\frac{u^{\star}_{\eta\eta}}{u^{\star}_{\xi\xi}}\right)\log\left(-\frac{u^{\star}_{\eta\eta}}{u^{\star}_{\xi\xi}}\right)u^{\star}_{\xi\xi}\,d\xi d\eta=:A_{1}^{\star}(u^{\star}).

Since
u
is maximal with respect to the functional
A_{\theta}
,
u^{\star}
is maximal with respect to the functional
A_{\theta}^{\star}
.
It suffices to derive the Euler-Lagrange equation of
A_{\theta}^{\star}
. See
[
TW1
,
Z1
]
for the case of the Legendre transform.

First, we consider
\theta\in(0,1)
.
For
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega^{\star})
, by integration by parts,

(2.4)

\displaystyle\left.\frac{dA_{\theta}^{\star}(u^{\star}+t\varphi)}{dt}\right|_{t=0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega^{\star}}[-\theta(-u^{\star}_{\eta\eta})^{\theta-1}{u^{\star}_{\xi\xi}}^{1-\theta}\varphi_{\eta\eta}+(1-\theta)(-u^{\star}_{\eta\eta})^{\theta}(u^{\star}_{\xi\xi})^{-\theta}\varphi_{\xi\xi}]\,d\xi d\eta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega^{\star}}\{-[\theta(-u^{\star}_{\eta\eta})^{\theta-1}{u^{\star}_{\xi\xi}}^{1-\theta}]_{\eta\eta}+[(1-\theta)(-u^{\star}_{\eta\eta})^{\theta}(u^{\star}_{\xi\xi})^{-\theta}]_{\xi\xi}\}\varphi\,d\xi d\eta.

Denote
w^{\star}=-\frac{u^{\star}_{\eta\eta}}{u^{\star}_{\xi\xi}}
.
Then the equation, after the transformation, becomes

-\theta({w^{\star}}^{\theta-1})_{\eta\eta}+(1-\theta)({w^{\star}}^{\theta})_{\xi\xi}=0.

After simplification, this is (
2.3
). Similarly,
for
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega^{*})
,

\displaystyle\left.\frac{dA_{0}^{\star}(u^{\star}+t\varphi)}{dt}\right|_{t=0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega^{\star}}-\frac{u^{\star}_{\xi\xi}}{u^{\star}_{\eta\eta}}\left(-\frac{\varphi_{\eta\eta}u^{\star}_{\xi\xi}-u^{\star}_{\eta\eta}\varphi_{\xi\xi}}{{u^{\star}_{\xi\xi}}^{2}}\right)u^{\star}_{\xi\xi}+\log\left(-\frac{u^{\star}_{\eta\eta}}{u^{\star}_{\xi\xi}}\right)\varphi_{\xi\xi}\,d\xi d\eta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega^{\star}}\frac{\varphi_{\eta\eta}u^{\star}_{\xi\xi}-u^{\star}_{\eta\eta}\varphi_{\xi\xi}}{u^{\star}_{\eta\eta}}+\log\left(-\frac{u^{\star}_{\eta\eta}}{u^{\star}_{\xi\xi}}\right)\varphi_{\xi\xi}\,d\xi d\eta,

and the equation, after the transformation, becomes

-[(w^{\star})^{-1}]_{\eta\eta}+(\log w^{\star})_{\xi\xi}=0.

After simplification, we obtain (
2.3
).
Finally,

\displaystyle\left.\frac{dA_{1}^{\star}(u^{\star}+t\varphi)}{dt}\right|_{t=0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega^{\star}}(1+\log w^{\star})\left(-\frac{\varphi_{\eta\eta}u^{\star}_{\xi\xi}-u^{\star}_{\eta\eta}\varphi_{\xi\xi}}{{u^{\star}_{\xi\xi}}^{2}}\right)u^{\star}_{\xi\xi}+w^{\star}\log w^{\star}\varphi_{\xi\xi}\,d\xi d\eta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega^{\star}}(1+\log w^{\star})(w^{\star}\varphi_{\xi\xi}-\varphi_{\eta\eta})+w^{\star}\log w^{\star}\varphi_{\xi\xi}\,d\xi d\eta.

Then the equation after transformation is

-w^{\star}_{\xi\xi}-(\log w^{\star})_{\eta\eta}=0,

which is equivalent to (
2.3
).
∎

2.2.
The interior gradient estimate of (
2.3
)

For simplicity, we change notations in this section and write (
2.3
) as

(2.5)

uu_{xx}+u_{yy}=(1-\theta)u_{x}^{2}+\frac{2-\theta}{u}u_{y}^{2}.

It is easy to see that this is a quasi-linear equation with the right-hand side depending on the gradient.
We prove the following interior gradient estimate.

Theorem 2.2
.

Assume
u
is a solution to (
2.5
) with
\theta\in[0,1]
on
B_{R}:=B_{R}(0)
and satisfies
0<\lambda\leq u\leq\Lambda
.
Then there exist
\alpha,C>0
depending on
\lambda
,
\Lambda
,
R
and
\theta
, such that

(2.6)

\int_{B_{R}}|\nabla u|^{3}(R^{2}-x^{2}-y^{2})^{\alpha}\,dV\leq C.

Proof.

Let
w=v\phi\eta
, where

v=\sqrt{u_{x}^{2}+u_{y}^{2}+1},\ \eta=(R^{2}-x^{2}-y^{2})^{\alpha},\ \alpha>3

and
\phi=\phi(u)
is a positive function of
u
to be determined later.

A direct calculation yields

(2.7)

\displaystyle uw_{xx}+w_{yy}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(uv_{xx}+v_{yy})\phi\eta+2[uv_{x}(\phi\eta)_{x}+v_{y}(\phi\eta)_{y}]+[u(\phi\eta)_{xx}+(\phi\eta)_{yy}]v.

It is clear that

(2.8)

\displaystyle v_{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle v^{-1}(u_{x}u_{xx}+u_{y}u_{xy}),\quad v_{y}=v^{-1}(u_{x}u_{xy}+u_{y}u_{yy}),

(2.9)

\displaystyle v_{xx}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-v^{-3}(u_{x}u_{xx}+u_{y}u_{xy})^{2}+v^{-1}(u_{xx}^{2}+u_{x}u_{xxx}+u_{xy}^{2}+u_{y}u_{xxy}),

(2.10)

\displaystyle v_{yy}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-v^{-3}(u_{x}u_{xy}+u_{y}u_{yy})^{2}+v^{-1}(u_{xy}^{2}+u_{x}u_{xyy}+u_{yy}^{2}+u_{y}u_{yyy}).

Differentiating the equation, we have

(2.11)

\displaystyle u_{x}u_{xx}+uu_{xxx}+u_{xyy}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2(1-\theta)u_{x}u_{xx}-\frac{2-\theta}{u^{2}}u_{x}u_{y}^{2}+\frac{2(2-\theta)}{u}u_{y}u_{xy},

(2.12)

\displaystyle u_{y}u_{xx}+uu_{xxy}+u_{yyy}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2(1-\theta)u_{x}u_{xy}-\frac{2-\theta}{u^{2}}u_{y}u_{y}^{2}+\frac{2(2-\theta)}{u}u_{y}u_{yy}.

By the Cauchy inequality, we have

(2.13)

(u_{x}u_{xx}+u_{y}u_{xy})^{2}\leq(v^{2}-1)(u_{xx}^{2}+u_{xy}^{2}),\ \ (u_{x}u_{xy}+u_{y}u_{yy})^{2}\leq(v^{2}-1)(u_{xy}^{2}+u_{yy}^{2}).

Then by (
2.8
), (
2.9
), (
2.10
), (
2.11
), (
2.12
) and (
2.13
),
the first term in (
2.7
) satisfies

\displaystyle uv_{xx}+v_{yy}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u[v^{-1}(u^{2}_{xx}+u^{2}_{xy})-v^{-3}(u_{x}u_{xx}+u_{y}u_{xy})^{2}]+v^{-1}u(u_{x}u_{xxx}+u_{y}u_{xxy})

\displaystyle+[v^{-1}(u^{2}_{xy}+u^{2}_{yy})-v^{-3}(u_{x}u_{xy}+u_{y}u_{yy})^{2}]+v^{-1}(u_{x}u_{xyy}+u_{y}u_{yyy}))

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle v^{-1}\left[2(1-\theta)u_{x}(u_{x}u_{xx}+u_{y}u_{xy})+\frac{2(2-\theta)}{u}u_{y}(u_{x}u_{xy}+u_{y}u_{yy})-(v^{2}-1)u_{xx}\right]

\displaystyle\ -v^{-1}\frac{2-\theta}{u^{2}}(u_{x}^{2}u_{y}^{2}+u_{y}^{4})+v^{-3}\left[u(u^{2}_{xx}+u^{2}_{xy})+u^{2}_{xy}+u^{2}_{yy}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2(1-\theta)u_{x}v_{x}+\frac{2(2-\theta)}{u}u_{y}v_{y}-\frac{2-\theta}{u^{2}}\frac{v^{2}-1}{v}u_{y}^{2}-\frac{v^{2}-1}{v}u_{xx}

\displaystyle\ +v^{-3}\left[u(u^{2}_{xx}+u^{2}_{xy})+u^{2}_{xy}+u^{2}_{yy}\right]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 2(1-\theta)u_{x}v_{x}+\frac{2(2-\theta)}{u}u_{y}v_{y}-\frac{2-\theta}{u^{2}}\frac{v^{2}-1}{v}u_{y}^{2}-vu_{xx}+v^{-1}u_{xx}+v^{-3}uu_{xx}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 2(1-\theta)u_{x}v_{x}+\frac{2(2-\theta)}{u}u_{y}v_{y}-\frac{2-\theta}{u^{2}}\frac{v^{2}-1}{v}u_{y}^{2}-vu_{xx}-\frac{v}{4u}-v^{-3}uu_{xx}^{2}+v^{-3}uu_{xx}^{2}

\displaystyle-\int_{B_{R}}2(1-\theta)[u_{xx}v\phi\eta+u_{x}v(\phi\eta)_{x}]\,dV

\displaystyle-\int_{B_{R}}\left(\frac{2-\theta}{u^{2}}\frac{v^{2}-1}{v}u_{y}^{2}\phi\eta+vu_{xx}\phi\eta+C\phi\eta v\right)\,dV

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}u_{xx}v\phi\eta\,dV-\int_{B_{R}}2(2-\theta)\frac{1}{u}\left[(1-\theta)u_{x}^{2}+\frac{2-\theta}{u}u_{y}^{2}\right]v\phi\eta\,dV

\displaystyle-\int_{B_{R}}\left[2(1-\theta)u_{x}v(\phi\eta)_{x}+2(2-\theta)\left(\frac{u_{y}v(\phi\eta)_{y}}{u}-\frac{u_{y}^{2}v\phi\eta}{u^{2}}\right)\right]\,dV

\displaystyle-\int_{B_{R}}\left[\frac{2-\theta}{u^{2}}\left(v-\frac{1}{v}\right)u_{y}^{2}\phi\eta+C\phi\eta v\right]\,dV

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}u_{xx}v\phi\eta\,dV-\int_{B_{R}}\left[2(1-\theta)\phi^{\prime}+\frac{2(2-\theta)(1-\theta)}{u}\phi\right]u_{x}^{2}v\eta\,dV

\displaystyle-\int_{B_{R}}\left[2(2-\theta)\frac{\phi^{\prime}}{u}+(2-\theta)(3-2\theta)\frac{1}{u^{2}}\phi\right]u_{y}^{2}v\eta\,dV

3.
The inhomogeneous equations in dimension two

In this section, we will study the interior estimate for the inhomogeneous equation (
1.5
).

We first recall the regularity theory of the second order elliptic equation in divergence form

(3.1)

D_{j}(a_{ij}(x)D_{i}u)=D_{i}g^{i}+h\quad\text{in}\,\,\Omega,

where
g=(g^{1},\cdots,g^{n})
is a vector valued function and

0<\lambda(x)|\xi|^{2}\leq a_{ij}(x)\xi_{i}\xi_{j}\leq\Lambda(x)|\xi|^{2},\ \ \forall\ \xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}

and can be discontinuous. When

is uniformly elliptic, a fundamental regularity theory
was established by De Giorgi, Nash, Moser, etc.
In
[
MS
,
Tr
]

\lambda(x)^{-1}\in L^{p}(\Omega)
and
\Lambda(x)\in L^{q}(\Omega)
where
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}<\frac{2}{n}

the set of

nonnegative definite matrices.

Theorem 3.1

(
[
Tr

)

Let

be such that

In
[

Now we turn to the fourth order equation

\displaystyle 2\int_{B_{1}}(\Delta u)^{n-1}|D\eta|^{2}w^{2}\,dx+\int_{B_{1}}|f|w\eta^{2}\,dx.

Hence
\int_{B_{\overline{R}}}u^{kl}w_{k}w_{l}\,dx\leq C
follows by
\Delta u\in L^{p_{n}}(B_{1})
,
f\in L^{q}(B_{1})
.
Then we complete the proof by choosing
r=\frac{1}{2}
and
R=\overline{R}
in (
3.26
).
∎

Lemma 3.5

.

In this section, we will prove Theorem

. By

In view of (

) and (

), we first consider the following degenerate linear elliptic equation

-D_{j}(a_{ij}(x)D_{i}u)+c(x)u=f(x)\quad\text{in}\,\,\Omega.

Lemma 4.1
.

Assume
\{a_{ij}(x)\}
satisfies

(4.3)

\frac{d(x)}{\lambda(x)}\leq a_{ij}(x)\leq\lambda(x)^{n-1}\quad\text{in}\,\,\,B_{1},

where
\lambda(x)\in L^{p}(B_{1})
,
d^{-1}\in L^{q}(B_{1})
. Assume
c(x),f(x)\in L^{p_{0}}(B_{1})
.
Let

be a subsolution in the following sense:

(4.4)

\int_{B_{1}}a_{ij}D_{i}uD_{j}\varphi+cu\varphi\,dx\leq\int_{B_{1}}f\varphi\,dx\quad\text{for\,\,any}\,\,\varphi\in W^{1,p}_{0}(B_{1})\text{\ and}\,\,\varphi\geq 0.

Suppose
p
,
q
and
p_{0}
satisfy
\frac{n}{p}+\frac{1}{q}<\frac{2}{n}
and
p_{0}\geq\frac{p}{n-1}
. Then

\sup_{B_{1/2}}u\leq C\left(\|u^{+}\|_{L^{1}(B_{1})}+\|f\|_{L^{p_{0}}(B_{1})}\right),

where
C
depends only on
p
,
\|\lambda\|_{L^{p}(B_{1})}
,
\|d^{-1}\|_{L^{q}(B_{1})}
and
\|c\|_{L^{p_{0}}(B_{1})}
.

Proof.

For some
k>0
and
m>0
, set
\overline{u}=u^{+}+k
and

\overline{u}_{m}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lc}\overline{u},&\ u<m,\\
k+m,&\ u\geq m.\end{array}\right.

Let
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{0}(B_{1})
. We choose a test function

\varphi=\eta^{2}(\overline{u}_{m}^{\beta}\overline{u}-k^{\beta+1})\in W^{1,p}_{0}(B_{1})

for some
\beta\geq 0
to be determined later. Substituting
\varphi
into (
4.4
), we have

\displaystyle\beta\int_{B_{1}}a_{ij}D_{i}\overline{u}D_{j}\overline{u}_{m}\overline{u}_{m}^{\beta-1}\overline{u}\eta^{2}\,dx+\int_{B_{1}}a_{ij}D_{i}\overline{u}D_{j}\overline{u}\,\overline{u}_{m}^{\beta}\eta^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \leq-2\int_{B_{1}}a_{ij}D_{i}\overline{u}D_{j}\eta\overline{u}_{m}^{\beta}\overline{u}\eta\,dx+\int_{B_{1}}\left(|c|\eta^{2}\overline{u}_{m}^{\beta}\overline{u}^{2}+|f|\eta^{2}\overline{u}_{m}^{\beta}\overline{u}\right)\,dx.

Note that
D\overline{u}=D\overline{u}_{m}
in
\{u<m\}
and
D\overline{u}_{m}=0
in
\{u\geq m\}
. By the Cauchy inequality, we have

\displaystyle\beta

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}a_{ij}D_{i}\overline{u}_{m}D_{j}\overline{u}_{m}\overline{u}_{m}^{\beta-1}\overline{u}\eta^{2}\,dx+\int_{B_{1}}a_{ij}D_{i}\overline{u}D_{j}\overline{u}\,\overline{u}_{m}^{\beta}\eta^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{1}}a_{ij}D_{i}\overline{u}D_{j}\overline{u}\,\overline{u}_{m}^{\beta}\eta^{2}\,dx+4\int_{B_{1}}a_{ij}D_{i}\eta D_{j}\eta\overline{u}_{m}^{\beta}\overline{u}^{2}\,dx+\int_{B_{1}}\left(|c|\eta^{2}\overline{u}_{m}^{\beta}\overline{u}^{2}+|f|\eta^{2}\overline{u}_{m}^{\beta}\overline{u}\right)\,dx.

Then by (
4.3
)

(4.5)

\displaystyle\beta\int_{B_{1}}\frac{d}{\lambda(x)}|D\overline{u}_{m}|^{2}\overline{u}_{m}^{\beta}\eta^{2}\,dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{1}}\frac{d}{\lambda(x)}|D\overline{u}|^{2}\overline{u}_{m}^{\beta}\eta^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\beta\int_{B_{1}}a_{ij}D_{i}\overline{u}_{m}D_{j}\overline{u}_{m}\overline{u}_{m}^{\beta-1}\overline{u}\eta^{2}\,dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{1}}a_{ij}D_{i}\overline{u}D_{j}\overline{u}\,\overline{u}_{m}^{\beta}\eta^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 4\int_{B_{1}}\lambda(x)^{n-1}|D\eta|^{2}\overline{u}_{m}^{\beta}\overline{u}^{2}\,dx+\int_{B_{1}}c_{0}\eta^{2}\overline{u}_{m}^{\beta}\overline{u}^{2}\,dx,

where
c_{0}=|c|+\frac{|f|}{k}
.
Choose
k=\|f\|_{L^{p_{0}}(B_{1})}
if
f
is not identically 0. Otherwise choose arbitrary
k>0
and let
k\to 0^{+}
.
Let
w=\overline{u}_{m}^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\overline{u}
. There holds

|Dw|^{2}=\left|\frac{\beta}{2}\overline{u}_{m}^{\frac{\beta}{2}-1}\overline{u}_{m}D\overline{u}_{m}+\overline{u}_{m}^{\frac{\beta}{2}}D\overline{u}\right|^{2}\leq(1+\beta)(\beta\overline{u}_{m}^{\beta}|D\overline{u}_{m}|^{2}+\overline{u}_{m}^{\beta}|D\overline{u}|^{2}).

Therefore by (
4.5
)

\int_{B_{1}}\frac{d}{\lambda(x)}|Dw|^{2}\eta^{2}\,dx\leq 8(1+\beta)\int_{B_{1}}\lambda(x)^{n-1}|D\eta|^{2}w^{2}\,dx+2(1+\beta)\int_{B_{1}}c_{0}w^{2}\eta^{2}\,dx.

By
|D(w\eta)|^{2}\leq 2|Dw|^{2}\eta^{2}+2|D\eta|^{2}w^{2}
,
we have

(4.6)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}\frac{d}{\lambda(x)}|D(w\eta)|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}\left(\frac{2d}{\lambda(x)}+16(1+\beta)\lambda(x)^{n-1}\right)|D\eta|^{2}w^{2}\,dx+4(1+\beta)\int_{B_{1}}c_{0}w^{2}\eta^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 18(1+\beta)\int_{B_{1}}\lambda(x)^{n-1}|D\eta|^{2}w^{2}\,dx+4(1+\beta)\int_{B_{1}}c_{0}w^{2}\eta^{2}\,dx.

Next, we deal with the
\lambda(x)
in the above estimate. By the assumptions
\lambda\in L^{p}(B_{1})
and
d^{-1}\in L^{q}(B_{1})
, we have
\lambda d^{-1}\in L^{\frac{pq}{p+q}}(B_{1})
.
By Hölder’s inequality, we have

(4.7)

\displaystyle\left\|D(w\eta)\right\|_{L^{\frac{2pq}{pq+p+q}}(B_{1})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\lambda d^{-1}\|_{L^{\frac{pq}{p+q}}(B_{1})}\cdot\int_{B_{1}}\frac{1}{\lambda(x)d^{-1}}\left|D(w\eta)\right|^{2}\,dx,

(4.8)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}\lambda(x)^{n-1}|D\eta|^{2}w^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\lambda\|_{L^{p}(B_{1})}\cdot\|wD\eta\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{p-n+1}}(B_{1})}^{2},

(4.9)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}c_{0}w^{2}\eta^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|c_{0}\|_{L^{p_{0}}(B_{1})}\cdot\left(\int_{B_{1}}(\eta w)^{\frac{2{p_{0}}}{{p_{0}}-1}}\,dx\right)^{1-\frac{1}{{p_{0}}}}.

Combining (
4.6
)-(
4.9
), we get

\displaystyle\|D(w\eta)\|_{L^{\frac{2pq}{pq+p+q}}(B_{1})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(1+\beta)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\|wD\eta\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{p-n+1}}(B_{1})}+\|w\eta\|_{L^{\frac{2{p_{0}}}{{p_{0}}-1}}(B_{1})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(1+\beta)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\|wD\eta\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{p-n+1}}(B_{1})}+\|w\eta\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{p-n+1}}(B_{1})}\right)

for
C
depending on
\|\lambda\|_{L^{p}(B_{1})}
,
\|d^{-1}\|_{L^{q}(B_{1})}
,
\|c\|_{L^{p_{0}}(B_{1})}
. Here we used
p_{0}\geq\frac{n-1}{p}
.
By the Sobolev inequality

\|w\eta\|_{L^{\alpha}(B_{1})}\leq C(1+\beta)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\|wD\eta\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{p-n+1}}(B_{1})}+\|w\eta\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{p-n+1}}(B_{1})}\right),

where

(4.10)

\frac{1}{\alpha}=\frac{pq+p+q}{2pq}-\frac{1}{n}.

Now for any
0<r<R\leq 1
, we choose a cutoff function
\eta\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{R})

0\leq\eta\leq 1,\quad\eta\equiv 1\,\,\text{in}\,\,B_{r}\quad\text{and}\quad|D\eta|\leq\frac{2}{R-r}.

Then we obtain

\|w\|_{L^{\alpha}(B_{r})}\leq\frac{C(1+\beta)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{R-r}\|w\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{p-n+1}}(B_{R})}.

By (
4.10
) and the assumption
\frac{n}{p}+\frac{1}{q}<\frac{2}{n}
,
we have
\alpha>\frac{2p}{p-n+1}
. We can do the iteration as follows.

Recalling the definition of
w
, we have

\|\overline{u}_{m}^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\overline{u}\|_{L^{\alpha}(B_{r})}\leq\frac{C(1+\beta)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{R-r}\|\overline{u}_{m}^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\overline{u}\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{p-n+1}}(B_{R})}.

Set
\gamma=\beta+2\geq 2
. By
\overline{u}_{m}\leq\overline{u}
, we obtain

\|\overline{u}_{m}^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\|_{L^{\alpha}(B_{r})}\leq\frac{C\gamma^{\frac{1}{2}}}{R-r}\|\overline{u}^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{p-n+1}}(B_{R})}.

Letting

, we get

\|\overline{u}^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\|_{L^{\alpha}(B_{r})}\leq\frac{C\gamma^{\frac{1}{2}}}{R-r}\|\overline{u}^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{p-n+1}}(B_{R})},

i.e.,

\|\overline{u}\|_{L^{{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\alpha}(B_{r})}\leq\frac{(C\gamma)^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}}{(R-r)^{\frac{2}{\gamma}}}\|\overline{u}\|_{L^{{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\frac{2p}{p-n+1}}(B_{R})}.

Denote
\chi=\alpha\cdot\frac{p-n+1}{2p}>1
. Then

(4.11)

\|\overline{u}\|_{L^{{\frac{{\gamma}p}{p-n+1}\chi}}(B_{r})}\leq\frac{(C\gamma)^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}}{(R-r)^{\frac{2}{\gamma}}}\|\overline{u}\|_{L^{{\frac{{\gamma}p}{p-n+1}}}(B_{R})}.

We iterate (
4.11
) to get the desired estimate. Set

\gamma_{i}=2\chi^{i}\quad\text{and}\quad R_{i}=r+\frac{R-r}{2^{i}},\ i=0,1,2,\cdots,

i.e.,

\gamma_{i}=\chi\gamma_{i-1}\ \text{and}\ R_{i-1}-R_{i}=\frac{R-r}{2^{i}},\ i=1,2,\cdots.

By (
4.11
),

\|\overline{u}\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{p-n+1}\chi^{i+1}}(B_{R_{i+1}})}\leq C^{\sum_{j=0}^{i}\frac{1}{\gamma_{j}}}\cdot\prod_{j=0}^{i}\gamma_{j}^{\frac{1}{\gamma_{j}}}\cdot 4^{\sum_{j=0}^{i}\frac{j}{\gamma_{j}}}\frac{1}{(R-r)^{\sum_{j=0}^{i}\frac{2}{\gamma_{j}}}}\cdot\|\overline{u}\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{p-n+1}}(B_{R})}.

Letting

, by Young’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\|\overline{u}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{(R-r)^{\frac{\chi}{\chi-1}}}\|\overline{u}\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{p-n+1}}(B_{R})}

\displaystyle=\frac{C}{(R-r)^{\frac{\chi}{\chi-1}}}\|\overline{u}\|_{L^{1}(B_{R})}^{\frac{p-n+1}{2p}}\cdot\|\overline{u}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R})}^{\frac{p+n-1}{2p}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\overline{u}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R})}+\frac{C}{(R-r)^{\frac{\chi}{\chi-1}\cdot\frac{2p}{p-n+1}}}\|\overline{u}\|_{L^{1}(B_{R})}.

Set
f(t)=\|\overline{u}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{t})}
for
t\in(0,1].
Then for any
0<r<R\leq 1

f(r)\leq\frac{1}{2}f(R)+\frac{C}{(R-r)^{\frac{\chi}{\chi-1}\cdot\frac{2p}{p-n+1}}}\|\overline{u}\|_{L^{1}(B_{1})}.

We apply Lemma
3.5
to get

f(r)\leq\frac{C}{(R-r)^{\frac{\chi}{\chi-1}\cdot\frac{2p}{p-n+1}}}\|\overline{u}\|_{L^{1}(B_{1})}.

The lemma follows by choosing
r=\frac{1}{2}
and
R=1
.
∎

Now we can use Lemma
4.1
to obtain the interior estimates for (

). For simplicity, we only consider the homogeneous equation.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Denote
a_{ij}=U^{ij}
,
\lambda=\Delta u
and
d=\det D^{2}u
.
Firstly, we consider the case
0\leq\theta<1
.
We apply Lemma
4.1
to equation (

), which yields

\sup_{B_{1/2}}w\leq C\|w\|_{L^{1}(B_{1})}\leq C\|w\|_{L^{\frac{q}{1-\theta}}(B_{1})}.

Since
w=(\det D^{2}u)^{-(1-\theta)}\in L^{\frac{q}{1-\theta}}(B_{1}),
we know

\sup_{B_{1/2}}\{(\det D^{2}u)^{-1}\}\leq C
.
For the upper bound of the determinant, we set
w=\frac{1}{v}
. A direct calculation yields

w_{i}=-\frac{v_{i}}{v^{2}},\ \ w_{ij}=\frac{2v_{i}v_{j}}{v^{3}}-\frac{v_{ij}}{v^{2}}.

Then
v
satisfies

0=U^{ij}w_{ij}=\frac{2}{v^{3}}U^{ij}v_{i}v_{j}-\frac{1}{v^{2}}U^{ij}v_{ij}\geq-\frac{1}{v^{2}}U^{ij}v_{ij},

Similarly, we use Lemma
4.1
to obtain

\sup_{B_{1/2}}\det D^{2}u\leq C
.

Next, we consider the case
\theta=1
. Write
w=\log d
, where
d\in L^{\frac{p}{n}}(B_{1})
. Then we have

w_{i}=\frac{d_{i}}{d},\ \ w_{ij}=-\frac{d_{i}d_{j}}{d^{2}}+\frac{d_{ij}}{d},

which yields

0=U^{ij}w_{ij}=-\frac{1}{d^{2}}U^{ij}d_{i}d_{j}+\frac{1}{d}U^{ij}d_{ij}\leq\frac{1}{d}U^{ij}d_{ij},

i.e.,

-D_{i}(U^{ij}D_{j}d)\leq 0\quad\text{in}\,\,B_{1}.

Similarly, write
w=-\log z
with

. We have

w_{i}=-\frac{z_{i}}{z},\ \ w_{ij}=\frac{z_{i}z_{j}}{z^{2}}-\frac{z_{ij}}{z},

which means

i.e.,

-D_{i}(U^{ij}D_{j}z)\leq 0\quad\text{in}\,\,B_{1}.

4.1
to obtain the bounds of

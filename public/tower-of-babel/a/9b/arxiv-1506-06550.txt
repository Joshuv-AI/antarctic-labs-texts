Slavnov and Gaudin–Korepin Formulas for Models without 𝐔 ⁡ ( 𝟏 ) \boldsymbol{{\rm U}(1)} Symmetry: the Twisted XXX Chain
Samuel BELLIARD † and Rodrigo A. PIMENTA ‡§

Abstract
We consider the XXX spin- 1 2 \frac{1}{2} Heisenberg chain on the circle with an arbitrary twist. We characterize its spectral problem using the modified algebraic Bethe anstaz and study the scalar product between the Bethe vector and its dual. We obtain modified Slavnov and Gaudin–Korepin formulas for the model. Thus we provide a first example of such formulas for quantum integrable models without U ⁡ ( 1 ) {\rm U}(1) symmetry characterized by an inhomogenous Baxter T-Q equation.

Here, we consider the question of the scalar product between the Bethe vectors obtained from the
modified algebraic Bethe ansatz. For simplicity, we consider the case of the isotropic spin
-\frac{1}{2}
Heisenberg chain on the circle with an arbitrary twist.
This is the simplest model which can be considered by the MABA. In fact, its solution contains the main features entailed by the method: the spectrum is characterized
by an inhomogeneous Baxter T-Q relation and the off-shell Bethe vector is generated by a modified creation operator.
In this context, we obtain a modified Slavnov formula for the scalar product between an on-shell Bethe state and its off-shell dual. The formula (see (
17
))
is given in terms of a determinant depending on the Jacobian of the inhomogenous eigenvalue expression. Moreover, it contains a new factor
related to a certain expansion of the Bethe vector. The square of the norm, i.e., the modified Gaudin–Korepin formula (see (
19

{\rm U}(1)
symmetry, has the same structure and will be presented in a separated publication.

The isotropic spin
-\frac{1}{2}
Heisenberg chain on the circle with an arbitrary twist is
given by the Hamiltonian

\displaystyle H=\,\sum_{k=1}^{N}\big(\sigma^{x}_{k}\otimes\sigma^{x}_{k+1}+\sigma^{y}_{k}\otimes\sigma^{y}_{k+1}+\sigma^{z}_{k}\otimes\sigma^{z}_{k+1}\big)

subject to the following boundary conditions

\displaystyle\gamma\sigma^{x}_{N+1}=\frac{\tilde{\kappa}^{2}+\kappa^{2}-\kappa_{+}^{2}-\kappa_{-}^{2}}{2}\sigma^{x}_{1}+i\frac{\kappa^{2}-\tilde{\kappa}^{2}-\kappa_{+}^{2}+\kappa_{-}^{2}}{2}\sigma^{y}_{1}+(\kappa\kappa_{-}-\tilde{\kappa}\kappa_{+})\sigma^{z}_{1},

\displaystyle\gamma\sigma^{y}_{N+1}=i\frac{\tilde{\kappa}^{2}-\kappa^{2}-\kappa_{+}^{2}+\kappa_{-}^{2}}{2}\sigma^{x}_{1}+\frac{\tilde{\kappa}^{2}+\kappa^{2}+\kappa_{+}^{2}+\kappa_{-}^{2}}{2}\sigma^{y}_{1}-i(\tilde{\kappa}\kappa_{+}+\kappa\kappa_{-})\sigma^{z}_{1},

\displaystyle\gamma\sigma^{z}_{N+1}=(\kappa\kappa_{+}-\tilde{\kappa}\kappa_{-})\sigma^{x}_{1}+i(\tilde{\kappa}\kappa_{-}+\kappa\kappa_{+})\sigma^{y}_{1}+(\tilde{\kappa}\kappa+\kappa_{+}\kappa_{-})\sigma^{z}_{1}.

\gamma=\tilde{\kappa}\kappa-\kappa_{+}\kappa_{-}

The Hamiltonian (

The key object is the rational

\displaystyle R(u)=\frac{u}{c}+P,

From the

\displaystyle T_{a}(u)=R_{a1}(u-\theta_{1})\cdots R_{aN}(u-\theta_{N})=\left(\begin{matrix}t_{11}(u)&t_{12}(u)\\

This monodromy matrix satisfies the RTT relation

\displaystyle R_{ab}(u-v)T_{a}(u)T_{b}(v)=T_{b}(v)T_{a}(u)R_{ab}(u-v)

A

The transfer matrix, generating function of the conserved quantities of the model, is given by

\displaystyle t(u)=\operatorname{Tr}_{a}\big(K_{a}T_{a}(u)\big)=\tilde{\kappa}t_{11}(u)+\kappa t_{22}(u)+\kappa^{+}t_{21}(u)+\kappa^{-}t_{12}(u),

with

\displaystyle K=\left(\begin{matrix}\tilde{\kappa}&\kappa^{+}\\
\kappa^{-}&\kappa\end{matrix}\right).

(2)

The commutation relation between transfer matrices with different spectral parameters

\displaystyle[t(u),t(v)]=0,

follows from the RTT relation and the
{\rm GL}(2)
invariance of the
R

\displaystyle[R_{ab}(u-v),K_{a}K_{b}]=0.

(3)

The Hamiltonian (
1

\displaystyle H=2c\frac{d}{du}\big(\ln(t(u))\big)\big|_{u\to 0,\,\theta_{i}\to 0}-N,

and thus its spectral problem is the same of the one of the transfer matrix.

\displaystyle K=LDL

\displaystyle\rho^{2}-(\tilde{\kappa}+\kappa)\rho+\kappa^{+}\kappa^{-}=0.

\displaystyle\nu_{12}(u)=\mu\left(t_{12}(u)+\frac{\rho}{\kappa^{-}}(t_{11}(u)+t_{22}(u))+\left(\frac{\rho}{\kappa^{-}}\right)^{2}t_{21}(u)\right),

\displaystyle\nu_{21}(u)=\mu\left(t_{21}(u)+\frac{\rho}{\kappa^{+}}(t_{11}(u)+t_{22}(u))+\left(\frac{\rho}{\kappa^{+}}\right)^{2}t_{12}(u)\right),

It follows that the transfer matrix has a modified diagonal form given by

\displaystyle t(u)=\operatorname{Tr}\big(D\bar{T}(u)\big)=(\tilde{\kappa}-\rho)\nu_{11}(u)+(\kappa-\rho)\nu_{22}(u).

To construct the Bethe vector we use the usual highest weight representation and the highest weight vector

\displaystyle|0\rangle=\left(\begin{matrix}1\\

\displaystyle t_{ii}(u)|0\rangle=\lambda_{i}(u)|0\rangle,\qquad t_{21}(u)|0\rangle=0,

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(u)=\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{u-\theta_{i}+c}{c},\qquad\lambda_{2}(u)=\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{u-\theta_{i}}{c}.

(4)

For the dual Bethe vector we use the dual highest weight vector

\displaystyle\langle 0|=(1,\,0)^{\otimes^{N}},

\displaystyle\langle 0|t_{ii}(u)=\lambda_{i}(u)\langle 0|,\qquad\langle 0|t_{12}(u)=0,\qquad\langle 0|0\rangle=1.

\{\nu_{ij}(u)\}

\displaystyle\nu_{11}(u)|0\rangle=\lambda_{1}(u)|0\rangle+\frac{\rho}{\kappa^{+}}\nu_{12}(u)|0\rangle,

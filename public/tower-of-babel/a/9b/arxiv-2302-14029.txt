Abstract
. We prove weighted inequalities between the Gagliardo and Sobolev seminorms and also between the Marcinkiewicz quasi-norm and the Sobolev seminorm. With A 1 A_{1} weights we improve earlier results of Bourgain, Brezis, and Mironescu.

1.
Introduction

The classical
(1,p)
-Poincaré inequality for
1\leq p<\infty
establishes the existence of a dimensional constant
C(n)>0
such that, for any continuously differentiable function
u
, the inequality

(1.1)

\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptQ}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}\lvert u(x)-u_{Q}\rvert\,\mathrm{d}x\leq C(n)\,\ell(Q)\left(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptQ}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}|\nabla u(x)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/p},

holds for any cube
Q
, that is, for any cartesian product of
n
intervals of the same side length
\ell(Q)
. Here, and along the rest of the paper,
f_{Q}
and
\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptQ}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}f
denote the average of the function
f
over
Q
, that is
f_{Q}=\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptQ}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}f=\lvert Q\rvert^{-1}\int_{Q}f(x)\mathrm{d}x
.

The importance of inequalities like (
1.1
) in both weighted and unweighted setting does not need any justification, since they are among the most important tools in the theory of partial differential equations and Sobolev spaces.
We refer to
[
HMPV
]
for a more complete list of references following our point of view.

More recently, fractional versions of these Poincaré inequalities have attracted the attention of many authors. Indeed, on one hand, one can easily prove the existence of a constant
C(n)>0
such that, for any locally integrable function
u
, the fractional
(1,p)
-Poincaré inequality

(1.2)

\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptQ}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}\lvert u(x)-u_{Q}\rvert\,\mathrm{d}x\leq C(n)\ell(Q)^{\delta}\left(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptQ}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}\int_{Q}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+\delta p}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/p}

holds for any cube
Q
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, for any
p\geq 1
and for any
\delta>0
. Nevertheless, it turns out that (
1.2
) is far from being optimal.
Indeed, the results in
[
BBM2
]
and a simple scaling argument shows the existence of a constant
C(n)
such that the highly interesting estimate

(1.3)

\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptQ}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}|u(x)-u_{Q}|\,\mathrm{d}x\leq C(n)\,\frac{(1-\delta)^{1/p}}{(n-\delta p)^{1/p^{\prime}}}\ell(Q)^{\delta}\left(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptQ}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}\int_{Q}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+\delta p}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/p}.

holds for any cube
Q
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, for any
1/2\leq\delta<1
and for any
1\leq p<n/\delta
.
Observe that the factor
(1-\delta)^{1/p}
can be very close to zero, providing an extra gain which may be thought as a new type of self-improving phenomenon. Actually, the factor
(1-\delta)^{1/p}
is needed to avoid the loss of information that occurs when the parameter
\delta
is close to
1
, as observed in
[
B
]
.
In
[
MS1
]
a different PDE approach on these inequalities was found, and in
[
Mi
]

a very interesting general method combining interpolation and extrapolation of functions was given.
We also refer to the recent paper
[
DM2
]
.

On the other hand, according to
[
BBM1
]
,
it turns out that the right hand side of (
1.3
) satisfies

(1.4)

\ell(Q)^{\delta}\left(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptQ}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}\int_{Q}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+\delta p}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/p}\leq\frac{C(n)}{(1-\delta)^{1/p}}\ell(Q)\left(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptQ}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}|\nabla u(x)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/p}

for some dimensional constant
C(n)>0
.
Hence, the
(1,p)
-Poincaré inequality (
1.1
)
for

1\leq p<n/\delta
with any
\delta\in[1/2,1)

can be proved as the result of the combination of the two intermediate inequalities (

) and (

).

2.
Statement of main results

This paper is an outgrowth of our work
[
HMPV
]
where we proved weighted extensions of inequality (
1.3
) in the case of
A_{1}
weights.
We omit their formulation here.
The purpose of this paper is to extend inequalities like (
1.4
) to the context of
A_{1}
weights.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
0<\delta<1
,
1<p<\infty
,
w\in A_{1}
and
n\geq 2
. There exists a dimensional constant
C(n)>0
such that, for any cube
Q
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and any
u\in C^{1}(Q)
, the inequality

\ell(Q)^{\delta}\left(\int_{Q}\int_{Q}\frac{\lvert u(x)-u(y)\rvert^{p}}{\lvert x-y\rvert^{n+\delta p}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,w(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\frac{C(n)p^{\prime}}{{(1-\delta)^{\frac{1}{p}}}}[w]_{A_{1}}^{\frac{2}{p}}\ell(Q)\left(\int_{Q}\lvert\nabla u(x)\rvert^{p}\,w(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

holds.

This is an interesting result since it provides a weighted inequality between the fractional and classical Sobolev norms. It is a weighted extension of the more classical inequality given in Section
6
. Since the weighted
(1,p)
-Poincaré inequality holds for any Muckenhoupt weight in
A_{p}
(see
[
FKS82
]
), we believe that the above result should also hold for any
A_{p}
weight.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
0<\delta<1
,
w\in A_{1}
and
n\geq 2
. There exists a dimensional constant
C(n)>0
such that, for any cube
Q
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and any
u\in C^{1}(Q)
, the inequality

\ell(Q)^{\delta}\int_{Q}\int_{Q}\frac{\lvert u(x)-u(y)\rvert}{\lvert x-y\rvert^{n+\delta}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,w(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq C(n)\,\frac{[w]_{A_{1}}}{(1-\delta)^{2}}\ell(Q)\,\int_{Q}|\nabla u(x)|w(x)\,\mathrm{d}x

holds.

We prove variants of Theorem
2.1
and Theorem
2.2
also for Borel measures
\mu
in general.
We refer to inequality (
5.1
) and
Section
5.2
.

We believe that neither of the two theorems above are fully sharp since the constant
[w]^{2}_{A_{1}}
we get is quadratic if
p>1
while in the case
p=1
the constant is linear. However, the factor
(1-\delta)^{-2}
in the case
p=1
is worse and we believe that
(1-\delta)^{-1}
should be the right constant in the inequality, as
it is in the unweighted case. Actually we have the following weak type result in which the conjectured constant is obtained, although the method of proof gives a higher power of
[w]_{A_{1}}
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
0<\delta<1
,
w\in A_{1}
and
n\geq 2
. Then there exists a constant
C(n)>0
such that, for any cube
Q
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and any
u\in C^{1}(Q)
, the inequality

\ell(Q)^{\delta}\,\bigg\|\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{\lvert x-y\rvert^{n+\delta}}\bigg\|_{L^{1,\infty}\big(Q\times Q,w(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\times\mathrm{d}y\big)}\leq C(n)\,\frac{[w]_{A_{1}}^{2+\frac{1-\delta}{n}}}{\delta(1-\delta)}\ell(Q)\int_{Q}|\nabla u(x)|w(x)\,\mathrm{d}x.

holds.

For the unweighted case we refer to the famous paper
[
BVSY
]
. We also refer to the related interesting results in
[
DM1
]
.
In any case, a result like the one
[
B
]
is not known for us in a different setting than the Euclidean one. It may happen that the right constant in terms of
\delta
is different from the one in the Euclidean case, namely
(1-\delta)^{-1}
.

3.
Muckenhoupt weights, maximal operators and Riesz potentials

We review here some definitions and known results on the theory of Muckenhoupt weights, the Marcinkiewicz norm, maximal operators and fractional integrals. It will be useful here to denote by
\mathcal{Q}
the set of all cubes in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

3.1.
Muckenhoupt weights

We start by recalling some definitions and known results about Muckenhoupt weights. A weight is a function
w\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfying
w(x)>0
for almost every point
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. When
1<p<\infty
, we say that a weight
w\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is in the
A_{p}
class if its
A_{p}
constant,

[w]_{A_{p}}=\sup_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptQ}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}w(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\left(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptQ}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}w(x)^{1-p^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{p-1},

is finite. In case
p=1
, we say that
w\in A_{1}
if there is a constant
C>0
such that, for any cube
Q
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}w(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq C\,\mathrm{ess\ inf}_{x\in Q}w(x),

and the
A_{1}
constant
[w]_{A_{1}}
is defined as the smallest of these constants
C
.

Given
1\leq p<\infty
, it turns out (see
[
GCRdF
, p. 396]
) that a weight
w\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is in the
A_{p}
class if and only if there is a constant
C>0
such that, for every cube
Q
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and every nonnegative measurable function
u
on
Q
, the inequality

(3.1)

\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}u(x)\ \mathrm{d}x\leq C\left(\frac{1}{w(Q)}\int_{Q}u(x)^{p}w(x)\ \mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

holds. Here, we denote the
weighted measure of a measurable set
E
by
w(E)=\int_{E}w(x)\,\mathrm{d}x
. Moreover, the smallest constant
C
satisfying the above inequality is precisely
[w]_{A_{p}}^{\frac{1}{p}}
.
Given an
A_{p}
weight, a cube
Q\in\mathcal{Q}
and a measurable subset
E\subset Q
, one can apply inequality (
3.1
) to the function
u=\chi_{E}
to get

(3.2)

\frac{|E|}{w(E)^{\frac{1}{p}}}\leq[w]^{\frac{1}{p}}_{A_{p}}\frac{|Q|}{w(Q)^{\frac{1}{p}}}.

3.2.
Marcinkiewicz norms and Kolmogorov’s inequality

Similarly, we will use the standard notation for the normalized Marcinkiewicz quasinorms: for any
0<q<\infty
, any measurable set
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and a Borel measure
\mu
, we define

\|u\|_{L^{q,\infty}\big(E,\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu}{\mu(E)}\big)}=\sup_{t>0}t\,\left(\frac{1}{\mu(E)}\mu(\{x\in E:|u(x)|>t\})\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

We shall use the following Kolmogorov’s inequality: given a Borel measure
\mu
, we have that, for every
0<q<r<\infty
and every nonnegative measurable function
u
on a cube
Q
,

(3.3)

\frac{1}{\mu(Q)}\int_{Q}u(x)^{q}\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\leq\frac{r}{r-q}\,\|u\|^{q}_{L^{r,\infty}\big(Q,\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu}{\mu(Q)}\big)}

See
[
GCRdF
, p. 485]
, for instance.

3.3.
Maximal functions and Riesz potentials

Let
\mu
be a Borel measure in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Let
\alpha\geq 0
. We will denote by
M^{c}_{\alpha}\mu
the fractional centered Hardy–Littlewood maximal function of
\mu
on cubes, which is defined by

(3.4)

M^{c}_{\alpha}\mu(x):=\sup_{\ell>0}\ell^{\alpha}\,\frac{\mu(Q(x,\ell))}{|Q(x,\ell)|},\qquad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},

where
Q(x,\ell)
is the cube of side length
\ell
centered at
x
and the supremum is taken over all
\ell>0
. The case
\alpha=0
corresponds to the usual centered Hardy–Littlewood maximal function, which we simply denote by
M^{c}\mu
. We remove the superscript
c
from the notation when the supremum is taken over all cubes
Q
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfying
x\in Q
. This allows to define the fractional and classical non-centered Hardy–Littlewood maximal functions
M_{\alpha}\mu
and
M\mu
, respectively, by

(3.5)

M_{\alpha}\mu(x):=\sup_{\mathcal{Q}\ni Q\ni x}\ell(Q)^{\alpha}\,\frac{\mu(Q)}{|Q|},\qquad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}.

When a cube
Q_{0}
is given, we define
M_{\alpha,Q_{0}}\mu
by replacing the supremum in (
3.5
) by the supremum over all cubes
Q
in the class
\mathcal{Q}(Q_{0})
of all cubes
Q\in\mathcal{Q}
satisfying
Q\subset Q_{0}
. When
\mu
is given by the integral of the absolute value of a function
u\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, that is,
\mathrm{d}\mu=\lvert u\rvert\,\mathrm{d}x
, we replace
\mu
by
u
in the notation. We also define, for a given weight
w\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the centered weighted maximal function
M_{w}^{c}u
of
u\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
by

(3.6)

M_{w}^{c}u(x):=\sup_{\ell>0}\frac{1}{w((Q(x,\ell)))}\,\int_{Q(x,\ell)}|u(y)|\,\mathrm{d}w(y),\qquad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}.

We will use the classical weighted Fefferman–Stein inequality
[
FS
]

for a Borel measure
\mu
and
u\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
,

(3.7)

\|Mu\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.02777pt}L^{1,\infty}(d\mu)}\leq C(n)\,\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|u(x)|\,M\mu(x)\,\mathrm{d}x.

The usual proof for weights, namely when
d\mu=wdx
, goes through using a covering lemma of Vitali type for instance.
From (
3.7
) we deduce, using Marcinkiewicz interpolation, that

(3.8)

\|Mu\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.02777pt}L^{p}(d\mu)}\leq C(n)\,p^{\prime}\,\|u\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.02777pt}L^{p}(M\mu)}

holds for every
p\in(1,\infty)
.

Another family of operators which we shall be using and which are closely related to the fractional maximal functions are the fractional integral operators or Riesz potentials which, for
0<\alpha<n
, are defined for a Borel measure
\mu
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
by

I_{\alpha}\mu(x)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu(y)}{\lvert x-y\rvert^{n-\alpha}}\,,\qquad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}.

Whenever the measure
\mu
is given by a nonnegative function
u\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, that is,
\mathrm{d}\mu(x)=u(x)\,\mathrm{d}x
, we get the usual fractional integral operator.

4.
Estimates for operators and representation formulas

In this section we establish some basic results which will be used in the sequel. We first prove several estimates involving fractional maximal functions and Riesz potentials. Then we prove some representation formulas relating the oscillations of the functions under study with the aforementioned operators.

4.1.
Estimates for fractional maximal functions and Riesz potentials

We start with the following boundedness result for
the local fractional maximal operator.

Lemma 4.1
.

There exists a constant
C(n)
such that, for any
0<\alpha<n
, any
w\in A_{1}
and any
u
nonnegative measurable function on a cube
Q_{0}\in\mathcal{Q}
,

\int_{Q_{0}}M_{\alpha,Q_{0}}u(x)\,w(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\,\frac{C(n)}{\alpha}\,[w]_{A_{1}}^{1+\frac{\alpha}{n}}\ell(Q_{0})^{\alpha}\int_{Q_{0}}u(x)\,w(x)\,\mathrm{d}x.

Proof.

Let
x\in Q_{0}
. We first show that for every
x\in Q_{0}
,

(4.1)

M_{\alpha,Q_{0}}u(x)\leq c_{n}\,[w]_{A_{1}}^{1+\frac{\alpha}{n}}\,\ell(Q_{0})^{\alpha}\left(\frac{1}{w(Q_{0})}\int_{Q_{0}}u(y)\,w(y)\mathrm{d}y\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}\,\left(M^{c}_{w}(\chi_{Q_{0}}u)(x)\right)^{1-\frac{\alpha}{n}}.

Indeed, let
\theta=\frac{\alpha}{n}\in(0,1)
.
Let
Q\subset Q_{0}
be a cube with
x\in Q
.
By using inequality (
3.1
), we obtain

\begin{split}&\frac{\ell(Q)^{\alpha}}{|Q|}\int_{Q}u(y)\,\mathrm{d}y=|Q|^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}\,\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}u(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{\theta}\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}u(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{1-\theta}\\
&\qquad\leq\,|Q|^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}\,\left(\frac{[w]_{A_{1}}}{w(Q)}\int_{Q}u(y)\,w(y)\mathrm{d}y\right)^{\theta}\left(\frac{C(n)}{|Q(x,2\ell(Q))|}\int_{Q(x,2\ell(Q))}\chi_{Q_{0}}(y)u(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{1-\theta}\\
&\qquad\leq[w]_{A_{1}}^{\theta}\,|Q|^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}\,\left(\frac{1}{w(Q)}\int_{Q}u(y)\,\mathrm{d}w(y)\right)^{\theta}\left(\frac{C(n)[w]_{A_{1}}}{w(Q(x,2\ell(Q)))}\int_{Q(x,2\ell(Q))}\chi_{Q_{0}}(y)u(y)\,\mathrm{d}w(y)\right)^{1-\theta}\\
&\qquad\leq C(n)[w]_{A_{1}}\left(\frac{|Q|}{w(Q)}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}\,\,\left(\int_{Q_{0}}u(y)\,\mathrm{d}w(y)\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}\,\left(M^{c}_{w}(\chi_{Q_{0}}u)(x)\right)^{1-\frac{\alpha}{n}}.\end{split}

Now we can apply (
3.2
) to get

\begin{split}\frac{\ell(Q)^{\alpha}}{|Q|}\int_{Q}u(y)\,\mathrm{d}y&\leq C(n)[w]_{A_{1}}\left(\frac{|Q|}{w(Q)}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}\,\,\left(\int_{Q_{0}}u(y)\,\mathrm{d}w(y)\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}\,\left(M^{c}_{w}(\chi_{Q_{0}}u)(x)\right)^{1-\frac{\alpha}{n}}\\
&\leq C(n)[w]_{A_{1}}\left([w]_{A_{1}}\frac{|Q_{0}|}{w(Q_{0})}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}\,\,\left(\int_{Q_{0}}u(y)\,\mathrm{d}w(y)\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}\,\left(M^{c}_{w}(\chi_{Q_{0}}u)(x)\right)^{1-\frac{\alpha}{n}}\\
&\leq C(n)[w]_{A_{1}}^{1+\frac{\alpha}{n}}\ell(Q_{0})^{\alpha}\,\left(\frac{1}{w(Q_{0})}\int_{Q_{0}}u(y)\,\mathrm{d}w(y)\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}\,\left(M^{c}_{w}(\chi_{Q_{0}}u)(x)\right)^{1-\frac{\alpha}{n}}.\end{split}

By taking supremum over
all cubes
Q\in\mathcal{Q}(Q_{0})
with
x\in Q
, we get inequality (
4.1
).

Now we can apply inequality (
4.1
) to obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{w(Q_{0})}\int_{Q_{0}}M_{\alpha,Q_{0}}u(x)\,\mathrm{d}w(x)

\displaystyle\leq c_{n}[w]_{A_{1}}^{1+\frac{\alpha}{n}}\,\ell(Q_{0})^{\alpha}\left(\frac{1}{w(Q_{0})}\int_{Q_{0}}u(x)\,\mathrm{d}w(x)\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}\,\frac{1}{w(Q_{0})}\int_{Q_{0}}\,\left(M^{c}_{w}(\chi_{Q_{0}}u)(x)\right)^{1-\frac{\alpha}{n}}\,\mathrm{d}w(x).

Since
M^{c}_{w}
is of weak type
(1,1)
with respect to
\mathrm{d}w(x)
and with norm depending just on
n
by the Besicovitch covering lemma, we can apply Kolmogorov’s inequality (
3.3
) to bound the last integral from above as follows

\begin{split}\frac{1}{w(Q_{0})}\int_{Q_{0}}\,\left(M^{c}_{w}(\chi_{Q_{0}}u)(x)\right)^{1-\frac{\alpha}{n}}\,w(x)\,\mathrm{d}x&\leq\frac{n}{\alpha}\,\|M^{c}_{w}(\chi_{Q_{0}}u)\|^{1-\frac{\alpha}{n}}_{L^{1,\infty}\big(Q_{0},\frac{\mathrm{d}w}{w(Q_{0})}\big)}\\
&\leq\frac{C(n)}{\alpha}\,\left(\frac{1}{w(Q_{0})}\int_{Q_{0}}u(x)\,w(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1-\frac{\alpha}{n}},\end{split}

By combining the above estimates, we get

\frac{1}{w(Q_{0})}\int_{Q_{0}}M_{\alpha,Q_{0}}u(x)\,w(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\frac{C(n)}{\alpha}\,[w]_{A_{1}}^{1+\frac{\alpha}{n}}\,\frac{\ell(Q_{0})^{\alpha}}{w(Q_{0})}\int_{Q_{0}}u(x)\,w(x)\,\mathrm{d}x

which is the result we wanted to prove.
∎

The following lemma will be very useful.
Inequality (
4.2
) is probably known but we provide a proof for convenience of the reader.
This can also be found in
[
GLP
]
.
Inequality (
4.3
) is well-known and follows from
[
He
]
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
Q_{0}
be a cube in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
\mu
be a Borel measure, and
0<\alpha<n
. Then there
is a constant
C(n)>0
such that the inequality

(4.2)

I_{\alpha}(\chi_{Q_{0}}\mu)(x)\leq\frac{C(n)}{\alpha}\mu(Q_{0})^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}M^{c}(\chi_{Q_{0}}\mu)(x)^{\frac{n-\alpha}{n}}

holds for every
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Furthermore, the inequality

(4.3)

I_{\alpha}(\chi_{Q_{0}}\mu)(x)\leq\frac{C(n)}{\alpha}\ell(Q_{0})^{\alpha}M(\chi_{Q_{0}}\mu)(x),

holds
for every
x\in Q_{0}
,.
If
w\in A_{1}
, then the inequality

(4.4)

I_{\alpha}(\chi_{Q_{0}}w)(x)\leq\frac{C(n)}{\alpha}[w]_{A_{1}}^{\frac{n-\alpha}{n}}w(Q_{0})^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}w(x)^{\frac{n-\alpha}{n}}

holds for almost every
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.

Proof.

For
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
t>0
we denote

Q_{x,t}=Q\left(x,2t^{-\frac{1}{n-\alpha}}\right).

By the layer-cake formula, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{0}}\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu(y)}{\left|x-y\right|^{n-\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu\left(\left\{y\in Q_{0}:\frac{1}{\left|x-y\right|^{n-\alpha}}>t\right\}\right)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu\left(\left\{y\in Q_{0}:\left|x-y\right|<t^{-\frac{1}{n-\alpha}}\right\}\right)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\min\left\{\mu(Q_{0}),\frac{\mu(Q_{0}\cap Q_{x,t})}{|Q_{x,t}|}\,|Q_{x,t}|\right\}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\min\left\{\mu(Q_{0}),M^{c}(\chi_{Q_{0}}\mu)(x)\left(2t^{-\frac{1}{n-\alpha}}\right)^{n}\right\}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\left(\frac{M^{c}(\chi_{Q_{0}}\mu)(x)}{\mu(Q_{0})}\right)^{\frac{n-\alpha}{n}}}\mu(Q_{0})\,\mathrm{d}t+C(n)\int_{\left(\frac{M^{c}(\chi_{Q_{0}}\mu)(x)}{\mu(Q_{0})}\right)^{\frac{n-\alpha}{n}}}^{\infty}M^{c}(\chi_{Q_{0}}\mu)(x)t^{-\frac{n}{n-\alpha}}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C(n)}{\alpha}\mu(Q_{0})^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}M^{c}(\chi_{Q_{0}}\mu)(x)^{\frac{n-\alpha}{n}},

which is inequality (
4.2
). Observe that inequality (
4.4
) is a direct consequence of (
4.2
).

To show (
4.3
), we use inequality (
4.2
) with the measure
\chi_{Q_{0}}\mu
to get

\begin{split}I_{\alpha}(\chi_{\rule{0.0pt}{5.12358pt}Q_{0}}\mu)(x)&\leq\frac{C(n)}{\alpha}\mu(Q_{0})^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}M^{c}(\chi_{\rule{0.0pt}{5.12358pt}Q_{0}}\mu)(x)^{\frac{n-\alpha}{n}}\\
&=\frac{C(n)}{\alpha}|Q_{0}|^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}\left(\frac{\mu(Q_{0})}{|Q_{0}|}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}M^{c}(\chi_{\rule{0.0pt}{5.12358pt}Q_{0}}\mu)(x)^{\frac{n-\alpha}{n}}\\
&\leq\frac{C(n)}{\alpha}|Q_{0}|^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}M(\chi_{\rule{0.0pt}{5.12358pt}Q_{0}}\mu)(x)^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}M^{c}(\chi_{\rule{0.0pt}{5.12358pt}Q_{0}}\mu)(x)^{\frac{n-\alpha}{n}}\\
&=\frac{C(n)}{\alpha}\ell(Q_{0})^{\alpha}M(\chi_{\rule{0.0pt}{5.12358pt}Q_{0}}\mu)(x)\end{split}

for every
x\in Q_{0}
, since
M^{c}(\chi_{Q_{0}}\mu)(x)\leq M(\chi_{Q_{0}}\mu)(x)
. This is the desired inequality (
4.3
).
∎

4.2.
Representation formulas from Poincaré-type inequalities

We present some local representation formulas which follow from general Poincaré-type inequalities. Then we will
introduce some consequences of these representation formulas.
The following lemma is a key in our arguments. It essentially follows from

[
FLW
]
or
[
Ha
]
, but it can also be obtained by following the proof of
[
HV
, Lemma 4.10]
, since cubes are examples of John domains. We are interested in the tracking of the constants involved in our estimates and so we will provide the proof here for the sake of clarity.

Lemma 4.3
.

Assume that
0<\beta\leq\alpha<n
. Let
Q_{0}
be a cube in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Suppose there exists a constant
\kappa>0
, a function
u\in L^{1}(Q_{0})
and a nonnegative measurable function
g
such that

\displaystyle\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptQ}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}\lvert u(x)-u_{Q}\rvert\,\mathrm{d}x\leq{\kappa}\,\ell(Q)^{\alpha}\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptQ}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}g(x)\,\mathrm{d}x

for every cube
Q\subset Q_{0}
.
Then there exists a dimensional constant
C(n)>0
such that

\lvert u(x)-u(y)\rvert\leq C(n)\,\frac{\kappa}{\beta}\,\lvert x-y\rvert^{\beta}\bigl(M_{\alpha-\beta,Q_{0}}(g\chi_{Q_{0}})(x)+M_{\alpha-\beta,Q_{0}}(g\chi_{Q_{0}})(y)\bigr)

for every pair
x,y\in Q_{0}
of Lebesgue points of
u
.

Proof.

Pick two Lebesgue points
x,y\in Q_{0}
of
u
and
let
R_{0}\subset Q_{0}
be a closed cube such that
\ell(R_{0})\leq\lvert x-y\rvert
and
x,y\in R_{0}
.
For every
j\in\mathbb{N}
there
exists a cube
R_{j}\subset R_{j-1}
such that
x\in R_{j}
and
\ell(R_{j})=2^{-1}\ell(R_{j-1})
.
Since
x
is a Lebesgue point of
u
, we can bound the oscillation of
u
over
R_{0}
by a telescopical sum of the distances between averages of
u
over the cubes
R_{j}
, and then use the Poincaré-type inequality as follows:

\displaystyle\lvert u(x)-u_{R_{0}}\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\lvert u_{R_{i+1}}-u_{R_{i}}\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lvert R_{i}\rvert}{\lvert R_{i+1}\rvert}\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptR_{i}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{R_{i}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{R_{i}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{R_{i}}}\lvert u(z)-u_{R_{i}}\rvert\,\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\leq C(n)\,\kappa\,\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}(2^{-i}\ell(R_{0}))^{\beta}(2^{-i}\ell(R_{0}))^{\alpha-\beta}\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptR_{i}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{R_{i}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{R_{i}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{R_{i}}}g(z)\,\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\leq C(n)\frac{{\,\kappa}\,}{1-2^{-\beta}}\ell(R_{0})^{\beta}M_{\alpha-\beta,Q_{0}}(g\chi_{Q_{0}})(x).

Repeating the argument with
y
yields the estimate

\displaystyle\lvert u(x)-u(y)\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\lvert u(x)-u_{R_{0}}\rvert+\lvert u(y)-u_{R_{0}}\rvert

\displaystyle\leq C(n)\frac{{\,\kappa}\,}{1-2^{-\beta}}\lvert x-y\rvert^{\beta}\bigl(M_{\alpha-\beta,Q_{0}}(g\chi_{Q_{0}})(x)+M_{\alpha-\beta,Q_{0}}(g\chi_{Q_{0}})(y)\bigr),

and this completes the proof, since
1-2^{-\beta}
is comparable to
\beta
as
\beta\to 0
.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
Q_{0}
be a cube in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
Assume that
0<\alpha<n
and consider
0<\eta<n-\alpha
and
1\leq r<\infty
. Suppose that there exists a constant
\kappa>0
, a function
u\in L^{1}(Q_{0})
and a nonnegative measurable function
g
such that

(4.5)

\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptQ}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}\lvert u(x)-u_{Q}\rvert\,\mathrm{d}x\leq{\kappa}\,\ell(Q)^{\alpha}\left(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptQ}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}g(x)^{r}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}

for every cube
Q\subset Q_{0}
. Then there exists a dimensional constant
C(n)

such that

(4.6)

\lvert u(x)-u_{Q_{0}}\rvert\leq C(n)\,\frac{\kappa}{\alpha^{1/r^{\prime}}\eta^{1/r}}\,\ell(Q_{0})^{\alpha/r^{\prime}}\left(I_{\alpha}(g^{r}\chi_{Q_{0}})(x)\right)^{{}^{\frac{1}{r}}}\\

for every Lebesgue point
x\in Q_{0}
of
u
.

Proof.

This result is well known but we need to be precise with the main parameters involved. We adapt the main ideas from
[
FLW
]
in the case
r=1
and
[
FH
]
when
r>1
and we also refer to
[
LP
]
.
Fix a Lebesgue point
x\in Q_{0}
of
u
. Then there exists a chain
\{Q_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
of nested dyadic subcubes of
Q_{0}
such that
Q_{1}=Q_{0}
,
Q_{k+1}\subset Q_{k}
with
|Q_{k}|=2^{n}|Q_{k+1}|
for all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
and

\{x\}=\bigcap_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}Q_{k}
.
Then, we can use an argument similar to the one in the proof of Lemma
4.3
to obtain that

\lvert u(x)-u_{Q_{0}}\rvert=\left\lvert\lim_{k\to\infty}u_{Q_{k}}-u_{Q_{1}}\right\rvert\leq\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}\left\lvert u_{Q_{k+1}}-u_{Q_{k}}\right\rvert.

Using the dyadic structure of the chain and the Poincaré-type inequality
(
4.5
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}\left\lvert u_{Q_{k+1}}-u_{Q_{k}}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}\frac{1}{|Q_{k+1}|}\int_{Q_{k+1}}\lvert u(y)-u_{Q_{k}}\rvert\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq 2^{n}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}\frac{1}{|Q_{k}|}\int_{Q_{k}}\lvert u(y)-u_{Q_{k}}\rvert\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq 2^{n}\kappa\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}\ell(Q_{k})^{\alpha}\left(\frac{1}{|Q_{k}|}\int_{Q_{k}}g(y)^{r}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{1/r}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{n}\kappa\left(\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}\ell(Q_{k})^{\alpha}\right)^{1/r^{\prime}}\left(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\ell(Q_{k})^{\alpha}}{|Q_{k}|}\int_{Q_{k}}g(y)^{r}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{1/r}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2^{n}}{(1-2^{-\alpha})^{1/r^{\prime}}}\kappa\ell(Q_{0})^{\alpha/r^{\prime}}\left(\int_{Q_{0}}g(y)^{r}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}\ell(Q_{k})^{\alpha-n}\chi_{Q_{k}}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{1/r}.

Note that the immediate estimate
|x-y|\leq\sqrt{n}\ell(Q_{k})
produces an extra unwanted
\log
factor when summing the series. We instead proceed as follows.
Fix
y\in Q_{0}\setminus\{x\}
and pick
0<\eta<n-\alpha
. Write
k_{0}(y)=\max\{j\in\mathbb{N}:2^{j-1}\leq\sqrt{n}\frac{\ell(Q_{0})}{|x-y|}\}
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}\ell(Q_{k})^{\alpha-n}\chi_{Q_{k}}(y)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C(n)}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha-\eta}}\sum_{k=1}^{k_{0}(y)}\ell(Q_{k})^{-\eta}\chi_{Q_{k}}(y)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C(n)}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha-\eta}\ell(Q_{0})^{\eta}}\sum_{k=1}^{k_{0}(y)}2^{(k-1)\eta}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C(n)\,2^{\eta k_{0}(y)}}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha-\eta}\ell(Q_{0})^{\eta}(1-2^{-\eta})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C(n)}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}(1-2^{-\eta})}.

We conclude that the desired inequality

\displaystyle|u(x)-u_{Q_{0}}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2^{n}C(n)^{1/r}\kappa}{(1-2^{-\alpha})^{1/r^{\prime}}(1-2^{-\eta})^{1/r}}\ell(Q_{0})^{\alpha/r^{\prime}}\left(\int_{Q_{0}}\frac{g(y)^{r}}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C(n)\kappa}{\alpha^{1/r^{\prime}}\eta^{1/r}}\ell(Q_{0})^{\alpha/r^{\prime}}(I_{\alpha}(g^{r}\chi_{Q_{0}})(x))^{1/r}

holds for Lebesgue points
x\in Q_{0}
of
u
.
∎

For the case
\alpha=1
we get a sharper result.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
Q_{0}
be a cube in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with
n\geq 2
. Suppose there exists a constant
\kappa>0
, a function
u\in L^{1}(Q_{0})
and a nonnegative measurable function

such that

\displaystyle\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptQ}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}\lvert u(x)-u_{Q}\rvert\,\mathrm{d}x\leq{\kappa}\,\ell(Q)\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptQ}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}g(x)\,\mathrm{d}x

for every cube
Q\subset Q_{0}
.
Then there exists a dimensional constant
C(n)
such that

(4.7)

\displaystyle\lvert u(x)-u(y)\rvert\leq C(n)\,\kappa\,\lvert x-y\rvert\min_{z\in\{x,y\}}M(g\chi_{Q_{0}})(z)^{\frac{1}{n}}\max_{z\in\{x,y\}}M(g\chi_{Q_{0}})(z)^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}

for every pair
x,y\in Q_{0}
of Lebesgue points of
u
.

Pick two Lebesgue points
x,y\in Q_{0}
of
u
,
and let
R_{0}\subset Q_{0}
be a closed cube such that
\ell(R_{0})\leq\lvert x-y\rvert
and
x,y\in R_{0}
.
The representation formula (
4.6
) in
Lemma
4.4
, with
\alpha=r=1
, yields

\lvert u(x)-u_{R_{0}}\rvert\leq C(n)\,\kappa\,I_{1}(g\chi_{R_{0}})(x).

Next we use inequality (
4.2
) in Lemma
4.2
to derive inequality

\lvert u(x)-u_{R_{0}}\rvert\leq C(n)\,\kappa\,\left(\int_{R_{0}}g(z)\,\mathrm{d}z\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}M^{c}(\chi_{R_{0}}g)(x)^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}.

Similar estimates hold for
y
.

Since
x,y\in R_{0}
, we get

\displaystyle\lvert u(x)-u(y)\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\lvert u(x)-u_{R_{0}}\rvert+\lvert u(y)-u_{R_{0}}\rvert

\displaystyle\leq C(n)\,\kappa\,\left(\left(\int_{R_{0}}g(z)\,\mathrm{d}z\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}M^{c}(\chi_{R_{0}}g)(x)^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}+\left(\int_{R_{0}}g(z)\,\mathrm{d}z\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}M^{c}(\chi_{R_{0}}g)(y)^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}\right)

\displaystyle=C(n)\,\kappa\,\ell(R_{0})\left(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptR_{0}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{R_{0}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{R_{0}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{R_{0}}}g(z)\chi_{Q_{0}}\,\mathrm{d}z\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}\left(M^{c}(\chi_{Q_{0}}g)(x)^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}+M^{c}(\chi_{Q_{0}}g)(y)^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2C(n)\,\kappa\lvert x-y\rvert\min_{z\in\{x,y\}}M(g\chi_{Q_{0}})(z)^{\frac{1}{n}}\max_{z\in\{x,y\}}M(g\chi_{Q_{0}})(z)^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}.

This is the desired inequality (
4.7
).
∎

of this approach appears in
[
CMPR
]
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
Q
be a cube in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

and let
u\in C^{1}(Q)
. Let
0<\delta<1
and
1\leq p<\infty
.
There is a dimensional constant
C(n)>0
such that

(6.1)

\left(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptQ}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}\int_{Q}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+\delta p}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\frac{C(n)}{\alpha(\delta,p)}\ell(Q)^{1-\delta}\left(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptQ}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}|\nabla u(x)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Furthermore,

\alpha(\delta,p)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}(1-(1-\delta)p)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,((1-\delta)p)^{\frac{1}{p}}&if&(1-\delta)p<1,\\
((1-\delta)p-1)^{\frac{1}{p}}&if&(1-\delta)p>1,\\
1&if&(1-\delta)p=1\end{array}\right.

and hence
\alpha(\delta,p)\approx(1-\delta)^{\frac{1}{p}}
as
\delta\to 1
.

Proof.

Let us first prove
(1)
.
By the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus one can write, for every
x,y\in Q
,

u(y)-u(x)=\int_{0}^{1}\nabla u(x+t(y-x))\cdot(y-x)\,\mathrm{d}t,

where
\cdot
represents the usual scalar product in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Thus, using this equality, Hölder’s inequality and Tonelli’s theorem,

(6.2)

\displaystyle\left(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptQ}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}\int_{Q}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+\delta p}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptQ}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}\int_{Q}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{|\nabla u(x+t(y-x))|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+\delta p-p}}\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=\left(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptQ}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{Q\cap B(x,\sqrt{n}\ell(Q))}\frac{|\nabla u(x+t(y-x))|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n-(1-\delta)p}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

since
Q\subset B(x,\sqrt{n}\ell(Q))
for any
x\in Q
. By change of variables
z=x+t(y-x)=(1-t)x+ty
, one has

(1)

By convexity,
x,y\in Q
implies
z\in Q
, so
\chi_{Q}(y)=\chi_{Q}(z)
.

(2)

|x-y|=|z-x|/t
.

Thus, we continue with

(6.3)

\displaystyle=\left(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptQ}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{((1-t)x+tQ)\cap B(x,\sqrt{n}t\ell(Q))}\frac{|\nabla u(z)|^{p}}{|z-x|^{n-(1-\delta)p}}\frac{t^{n-(1-\delta)p}}{t^{n}}\,\mathrm{d}z\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptQ}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{Q\cap B(x,\sqrt{n}t\ell(Q))}\frac{|\nabla u(z)|^{p}}{|z-x|^{n-(1-\delta)p}}t^{-(1-\delta)p}\,\mathrm{d}z\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=\left(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptQ}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}\int_{Q}\int_{\frac{|z-x|}{\sqrt{n}\ell(Q)}}^{1}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t^{(1-\delta)p}}\frac{|\nabla u(z)|^{p}}{|z-x|^{n-(1-\delta)p}}\,\mathrm{d}z\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where we used Tonelli’s theorem again in the last equality.

There are three possibilities depending on the value of
(1-\delta)p
.

Case 1.
If
(1-\delta)p<1
, then we can extend the integral to zero, and we get the following upper bound

(6.4)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1-(1-\delta)p)^{\frac{1}{p}}}\,\left(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptQ}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}\int_{Q}\frac{|\nabla u(z)|^{p}}{|z-x|^{n-(1-\delta)p}}\,\mathrm{d}z\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1-(1-\delta)p)^{\frac{1}{p}}}\,\left(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptQ}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}|\nabla u(z)|^{p}\int_{Q}\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{|z-x|^{n-(1-\delta)p}}\,\mathrm{d}z\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(1-(1-\delta)p)^{\frac{1}{p}}}\,\left(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptQ}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}|\nabla u(z)|^{p}\frac{C(n)\ell(Q)^{(1-\delta)p}}{v_{n}^{(1-\delta)p/n}(1-\delta)p}\mathrm{d}z\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C(n)\ell(Q)^{1-\delta}}{v_{n}^{\frac{1-\delta}{n}}((1-\delta)p)^{\frac{1}{p}}(1-(1-\delta)p)^{\frac{1}{p}}}\left(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptQ}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}|\nabla u(x)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where we used Tonelli’s theorem and the fact that, for a Lebesgue measurable set
E
and
0<\alpha<n
,

(6.5)

\int_{E}\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{|x-z|^{n-\alpha}}\leq C(n)v_{n}^{-\frac{\alpha}{n}}\alpha^{-1}|E|^{\frac{\alpha}{n}},\qquad\text{for all }x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},

where
v_{n}
is the volume of the unit ball of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
See Lemma
4.2
.

Case 2.
If
(1-\delta)p>1
, then we can extend the upper bound of the integral in
t
up to infinity and then compute it. This way we obtain the following bound

(6.6)

\displaystyle\left(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptQ}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}\int_{Q}\frac{|z-x|^{1-(1-\delta)p}}{((1-\delta)p-1)(\sqrt{n}\ell(Q))^{1-(1-\delta)p}}\frac{|\nabla u(z)|^{p}}{|z-x|^{n-(1-\delta)p}}\,\mathrm{d}z\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sqrt{n}}{((1-\delta)p-1)^{\frac{1}{p}}}\ell(Q)^{-\frac{1}{p}+1-\delta}\left(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptQ}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}|\nabla u(z)|^{p}\int_{Q}\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{|z-x|^{n-1}}\,\mathrm{d}z\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sqrt{n}}{((1-\delta)p-1)^{\frac{1}{p}}}\,\ell(Q)^{-\frac{1}{p}+1-\delta}\left(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptQ}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}|\nabla u(z)|^{p}C(n)\ell(Q)\mathrm{d}z\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C(n)}{((1-\delta)p-1)^{\frac{1}{p}}}\,\ell(Q)^{1-\delta}\left(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptQ}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}|\nabla u(z)|^{p}\mathrm{d}z\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where again we used Tonelli’s theorem and inequality (
6.5
).

Case 3.
If
(1-\delta)p=1
, then we compute the integral in
t
and use the elementary inequality

\log s\leq\frac{s^{q}-1}{q}\leq\frac{s^{q}}{q}

which holds whenever
s>1
and
q>0
. Applying this, say, with
q=\frac{1}{2}
we get the upper bound

(6.7)

\displaystyle\left(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptQ}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}\int_{Q}\log\left(\frac{\sqrt{n}\ell(Q)}{|z-x|}\right)\frac{|\nabla u(z)|^{p}}{|z-x|^{n-1}}\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq C(n)\ell(Q)^{\frac{q}{p}}\left(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptQ}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}|\nabla u(z)|^{p}\int_{Q}\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{|z-x|^{n-(1-q)}}\mathrm{d}z\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq C(n)\,\ell(Q)^{\frac{q}{p}+\frac{(1-q)}{p}}\left(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptQ}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}|\nabla u(z)|^{p}\mathrm{d}z\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=C(n)\,\ell(Q)^{1-\delta}\left(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptQ}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}|\nabla u(z)|^{p}\mathrm{d}z\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where Tonelli’s theorem and inequality (
6.5
) have been used one more time.

Remark 6.2
.

Let
Q
be a cube in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and let
u\in L^{1}(Q)
. Let
0<\beta\leq\delta<1
and
1\leq p<\infty
. Then it is straightforward to show that

\ell(Q)^{\beta}\left(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptQ}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}\int_{Q}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+\beta p}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq C(n)\ell(Q)^{\delta}\left(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptQ}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{Q}}\int_{Q}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+\delta p}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,.

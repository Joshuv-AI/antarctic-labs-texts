Results on left-right Artin approximation for algebraic morphisms and for analytic morphisms of weakly-finite singularity type Thanks: I was supported by the Israel Science Foundation, grants No. 1910/18 and 1405/22
Dmitry Kerner

Abstract
. The classical Artin approximation (AP) reads: any formal solution of a system of (analytic, resp. algebraic) equations of implicit function type is approximated by “ordinary” solutions (i.e. analytic, resp. algebraic). Morphisms of scheme-germs, e.g. M ​ a ​ p ​ s ​ ( ( 𝕜 n , o ) , ( 𝕜 m , o ) ) , Maps\big((\Bbbk^{n},o),(\Bbbk^{m},o)\big), are usually studied up to the left-right equivalence. The natural question is the left-right version of Artin approximation: when is the formal left-right equivalence of morphisms approximated by the “ordinary” (i.e. analytic, resp. algebraic) equivalence? In this case the standard Artin approximation is not directly applicable, as the involved (functional) equations are not of implicit function type. Moreover, the naive extension does not hold in the analytic case, because of Osgood-Gabrielov-Shiota examples. The left-right version of Artin approximation ( ℒ ​ ℛ \mathscr{L}\mathscr{R} .AP) was established by M. Shiota for morphisms that are either Nash or [real-analytic and of finite singularity type]. We establish ℒ ​ ℛ \mathscr{L}\mathscr{R} .AP and its stronger version of Płoski ( ℒ ​ ℛ \mathscr{L}\mathscr{R} .APP) for M ​ a ​ p ​ s ​ ( X , Y ) , Maps(X,Y), where X , Y X,Y are analytic/algebraic germs of schemes of any characteristic. More precisely: • ℒ ​ ℛ \mathscr{L}\mathscr{R} .AP, ℒ ​ ℛ \mathscr{L}\mathscr{R} .APP, the inverse Artin approximation (and its Płoski’s version) hold for algebraic morphisms and for finite analytic morphisms. • ℒ ​ ℛ \mathscr{L}\mathscr{R} .AP holds for analytic morphisms of weakly-finite singularity type. (For c ​ h ​ a ​ r > 0 char>0 we impose certain integrability condition.) This latter class of morphisms of “weakly-finite singularity type” (which we introduce) is of separate importance. It extends naturally the traditional class of morphisms of “finite singularity type”, while preserving their non-pathological behaviour. The definition goes via the higher critical loci and higher discriminants of morphisms with singular targets. We establish basic properties of these critical loci. In particular: any map is finitely (right) determined by its higher critical loci.

Introduction

1.1.
The ordinary AP and APP

Consider a system of implicit function equations,
F(x,y)=0.
Here
x,y
are multi-variables, while
F
is a (finite) vector of power series.
Assume
F
is either analytic or algebraic, i.e. its entries belong to one of the rings:

​​
\bullet
​

\Bbbk\{x,y\},
the convergent power series (
\Bbbk
is a normed field, complete with respect to its norm)

​​
\bullet
​

\Bbbk\langle x,y\rangle,
the algebraic power series (
\Bbbk
is any field). For
\Bbbk=\mathbb{R}
these are the Nash power series.

The classical approximation theorems
[
Artin.68
]
,
[
Artin.69
]
read: any formal solution,
F(x,\hat{y}(x))\!=\!0,
is
approximated (
x
-adically) by ordinary solutions,
y(x)
(analytic or algebraic power series).
(See §
2.2
for more detail.)

Moreover, there exists a “parameterized” (analytic/algebraic) solution,
F(x,y(x,z))=0,
whose specialization is the initial formal solution. Namely, there exists a formal power series
\hat{z}(x)\in\Bbbk[\![x]\!]
satisfying:
y(x,\hat{z}(x))=\hat{y}(x).

This says (roughly): the formal solutions are not “intrinsic”​​, they are just “unfortunate” specialization of the ordinary (analytic, resp. algebraic) solutions.
See
[
Płoski.74
]
for
\Bbbk\{x,y\}
and
[
Popescu.85
]
,
[
Popescu.86
]
for
\Bbbk\langle x,y\rangle.

(See also
[
Ogoma.94
]
,
[
Swan.98
]
.)

The subject “Artin approximations” has been studied extensively, see e.g.
[
Popescu.00
]
,
[
Rond.18
]
for numerous results and further references. It became an everyday tool in Local Algebraic/Analytic Geometry and in Commutative Algebra.

1.2.
The inverse AP

Fix some (analytic, resp. algebraic) power series,
f(x)\!=\!f_{1}(x),\!\dots\!,f_{m}(x).

One way to check their dependence is to resolve the functional equation
\Phi(f(x))\!=\!0,
with the unknown
\Phi.
The “inverse Artin question”, ​
[
Grothendieck.61
, pg.13-08]
, ​asks: is any formal solution,
\hat{\Phi}(f(x))\!=\!0,
​approximated by ordinary solutions, ​
\Phi(f(x))\!=\!0?

(Here
\Phi
is analytic, resp. algebraic.)

The condition
\Phi(f(x))=0
is not an implicit function equation on
\Phi
.
Thus the inverse AP is not a direct consequence of the classical version of AP.

This inverse Artin approximation holds in the algebraic case, the proof is based on the nested AP of
[
Popescu.85
]
,
[
Popescu.86
]
.
In the analytic case the inverse AP holds for the ring
\mathbb{C}\{x\}
of dimension
\leq\!\!2
,
[
Abhyankar-van der Put.70
]
. It
fails in
dim\geq 3
, see
[
Gabrielov.71
]
,
[
Gabrielov.73
]
.

The inverse AP was studied in several works, see the references in
[
Izumi.07
]
,
and is still under investigation, see
[
Belot.Curmi.Rond.21
]
,
[
Alon.Cas-Jim.Haus.Kout.18
]
.
For linear equations over admissible rings (in particular
\Bbbk\langle x\rangle,

\Bbbk\{x\}
) this inverse AP problem is transformed into a nested AP problem,
[
Cas-Jim.Pop.Ron.19
]
.

It seems, no Płoski version of the inverse AP is known.

1.3.
The left-right AP and APP

The germs of maps,
Maps\big((\Bbbk^{n},o),(\Bbbk^{m},o)\big),
are traditionally studied up to the left-right equivalence,

f\sim\Phi_{Y}\circ f\circ\Phi_{X}^{-1},

[
A.G.L.V.
]
,
[
Mond. Nuñ.-Bal.
]
. Here
f
is a vector of (analytic, resp. algebraic) power series, while
\Phi_{X}\in Aut_{(\Bbbk^{n},o)},\Phi_{Y}\in Aut_{(\Bbbk^{m},o)}

are (analytic, resp. algebraic) automorphisms, i.e. coordinate changes in the source and in the target.
The basic task is to verify whether two maps are equivalent,
f\ \lower 1.72218pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\sim}\limits^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathscr{L}\mathscr{R}}}}$}\ {\tilde{f}},
or to bring a map to a particular “normal” form.
The arguments are often inductive, they provide only the formal equivalence.
Hence the fundamental question is the Artin approximation and its Artin-Płoski-Popescu version, as in §
1.1
, for the equation
\Phi_{Y}\circ{\tilde{f}}=f\circ\Phi_{X}
:

•

\mathscr{L}\mathscr{R}
.AP
:
Is every formal solution,
\hat{\Phi}_{Y}\circ{\tilde{f}}=f\circ\hat{\Phi}_{X}
, approximated by ordinary solutions?

•

\mathscr{L}\mathscr{R}
.APP
:
Is ​every formal solution a specialization of a parameterized ordinary solution?

Here the maps
f,{\tilde{f}}
are prescribed, while the morphisms
\Phi_{X},\Phi_{Y}
are unknowns.
We do not require
\Phi_{X},\Phi_{Y}
to be isomorphisms. But if
\hat{\Phi}_{X},\hat{\Phi}_{Y}
are invertible, then so are their approximations.

As in §
1.2
,
the condition
\Phi_{Y}\circ{\tilde{f}}=f\circ\Phi_{X}
is not an implicit function equation.
Thus the properties
\mathscr{L}\mathscr{R}
.AP,
\mathscr{L}\mathscr{R}
.APP are not direct consequences of the classical version of AP.

Remark 1.1
.

i.

Occasionally we add the condition
\Phi_{X}=Id_{X},
resp.
\Phi_{Y}=Id_{Y},
to get the property
\mathscr{L}
.AP ​​, resp.
\mathscr{R}
.AP ​​​.
Geometrically the property
\mathscr{R}
.AP addresses isomorphisms of scheme-germs over a given base: any formal isomorphism

over

For
\mathscr{L}\mathscr{R}
.AP one allows the base change: any formal isomorphism
[X
over
Y]\hbox{$\xrightarrow[\,\smash{\raisebox{3.46594pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,]{}$}[{\tilde{X}}
over
{\tilde{Y}}]
is approximated by ordinary isomorphisms.

ii.

The classical Artin approximation is related to the
\mathscr{R}
.AP question: when is the formal right equivalence of maps,

approximated by ordinary equivalence? (See Example
2.2
.)

Similarly, the inverse Artin approximation can be stated as the
\mathscr{L}
.AP question: when is
the formal left equivalence of maps,
\hat{\Phi}_{Y}\circ f={\tilde{f}},

.)

iii.

Various approximation questions become trivial for finitely determined maps. A map
f
is called finitely-
\mathscr{L}\mathscr{R}
-determined if the equality modulo higher order terms,
f\equiv{\tilde{f}}\ mod(x)^{N}
(for

In other words, the group orbit

(x)
-adically open neighborhood of

For these maps the property

[

For Nash-germs,

.AP holds for any map,

1.4.2.
The weakly-finite singularity type

It is the natural extension of the classical “finite singularity type”, seems not previously studied.
We sketch now the ideas of the definition. The main ingredient is the critical locus for morphisms with
non-smooth targets
.
It is defined via the truncated cotangent complex.

​​
\bullet
​

The critical module of a dominant morphism
f:X\to Y
is defined via the exact sequence
Der_{X}\ \lower 1.72218pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{\scriptscriptstyle{f}}}$}\ T_{X\to Y}\to\mathscr{C}_{X,f}\to 0.

Here
Der_{X}:=Der_{\Bbbk}(R_{X})
is the module of derivations, while
T_{X\to Y}
is the tangent space to
Maps(X,Y)
at
f.

The critical locus,
Crit_{X}f\subseteq X,
is the support of the critical module,
\mathscr{C}_{X,f}.
For a
\Bbbk
-smooth target,
Y=(\Bbbk^{m},o),
one gets the classical critical locus, defined by the Jacobian ideal of the map
f.
Otherwise
Crit_{X}
is usually smaller than the branch locus (the points where the module of relative differentials,
\Omega_{X/Y},
is non-free) and than the tangential branch locus
(the points where the module of relative vector fields,
T_{X/Y},
is non-free).

The pathology
Crit_{X}f=X
can occur for non-finite morphisms due to Osgood-type examples, i.e. dominant maps
X\to Y
with
dim(Y)>dim(X).

For
char>0
the pathology
Crit_{X}f=X
can occur even for finite morphisms.

The discriminant of
f
is the image of the critical locus,
\Delta:=f(Crit_{X}f)\subset Y.

​​
\bullet
​

Take the restricted map
f_{|}:Crit_{X}f\to\Delta.
One gets the “second” critical locus and its discriminant,

Crit_{X}f\supseteq Crit_{Crit,f_{|}}\to\Delta_{f_{|}}\subseteq\Delta.
Iterating this we get the higher critical modules,
\mathscr{C}_{j}
, the higher critical loci,

X=Crit_{0}\supseteq Crit_{1}\supseteq\cdots,
and the higher discriminants,

Y:=\Delta_{0}\supseteq\Delta_{1}\supseteq\cdots.

​​
\bullet
​

A map
f:X\to Y
is called
of weakly-finite singularity type
(w.f.s.t.) if its restriction
f_{|}:Crit_{r}\to\Delta_{r}
is finite for some
r\geq 0.

-

For
r=0
one gets just a finite map.

-

For
r=1
one gets a map of finite singularity type (f.s.t.), extending the classical f.s.t. notion,
[
Mond. Nuñ.-Bal.
, pg.224]
.

-

For scalar-valued maps,
Maps(X,(\Bbbk^{1},o)),
the notions f.s.t. and w.f.s.t. coincide.

-

For higher dimensional targets the condition w.f.s.t. is much weaker than f.s.t.

Thus, even in the classical case,
Maps\big((\Bbbk^{n},o),(\Bbbk^{m},o)\big),
for
\Bbbk=\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}
, Theorem
5.1
is stronger than the known results.

The notion of
Crit_{X}f
for morphisms with singular target is known,
[
Käällströöm.13
]
. But we could not find a reference for even the basic properties.
We establish these in §
4.2
-§
4.6
.

1.4.3.

Two auxiliary results in this paper are of separate importance.

​​
\bullet
​

(Approximate lifting of automorphisms.) Take a subgerm
Z=Spec({\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$R_{X}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$I$}\right.})\subset X.
If
X
is singular, then not all automorphisms of

(There is no lifting from the group of automorphisms

to

)
Even for
N
-th thickening,
Z_{N}:=Spec({\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$R_{X}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$I^{N}$}\right.})\subset X,
with
N\gg 1,
the lifting from
Aut_{Z_{N}}
to

does not need to exist.
But an approximation of each automorphism can be lifted:

4.3
.
(for
char\!=\!0
)
For any ideal
I\!\subset\!R
and a suitable
N\!\gg\!1,
any automorphism
{\bar{\Phi}}\!\in\!Aut_{\Bbbk}({\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$R$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$I^{N}$}\right.})

has an approximate lifting. Namely, there exists
\Phi\!\in\!Aut_{\Bbbk}(R)
satisfying:
\Phi(I)\!=\!I
and ​
[\Phi]\!=\![{\bar{\Phi}}]\!\in\!Aut_{\Bbbk}({\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$R$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$I$}\right.}).

This property would follow (in any characteristic) by the Strong Artin Approximation for the filtration
I^{\bullet}\!\subset\!R.
But that version of SAP does not hold,

[
Rond.18

.

\bullet
​

Any morphism is finitely-right-determined by its higher critical loci.

4.16
.
(roughly)
Suppose dominant maps

1.5.
A bit about the (purely algebraic) proofs of the approximation results

​​
\bullet
​

(The proof of Theorem
3.4
) We reformulate the
\mathscr{L}\mathscr{R}
-condition,
\Phi_{Y}\circ f={\tilde{f}}\circ\Phi_{X},

as a homomorphism of algebras with base change. This translates the initial functional equation into a problem of implicit function type, with nested solutions. Then, for the algebraic case (
{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk\langle x\rangle$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J_{X}$}\right.},{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk\langle y\rangle$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J_{Y}$}\right.}
), one uses the nested Artin approximation/nested Płoski theorem.

​​
\bullet
​

(The proof of Theorem
3.5
)
In the analytic case, (
{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk\{x\}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J_{X}$}\right.},{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk\{y\}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J_{Y}$}\right.}
), the nested Artin approximation does not hold,
[
Rond.18
, §5.3]
.
However, we can consider the map
R_{X}\ \lower 1.72218pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{\scriptscriptstyle{\Phi_{X}}}}$}\ R_{\tilde{X}}
not as a homomorphism of algebras, but as a homomorphism of (finitely-generated) modules,

mod
-
R_{Y}\ni R_{X}\to R_{\tilde{X}}\in mod
-
R_{\tilde{Y}},

with a base change
R_{Y}\to R_{\tilde{Y}}.
This translates the initial conditions into a system of implicit function equations.
Eventually this homomorphism of modules is promoted to a homomorphism of algebras, ensuring
f\sim{\tilde{f}}.

​​
\bullet
​

(The proof of Theorem
5.1
) By the classical Artin approximation we can assume: all the higher critical loci of
f,{\tilde{f}}
coincide. And similarly for the higher discriminants. Thus we get two finite analytic maps
f_{|},{\tilde{f}}_{|}:Crit_{r}\to\Delta_{r},
that are formally left-right equivalent.
By Theorem
3.5
these restrictions are analytically left-right equivalent,
via the group
Aut_{Crit_{r}}\times Aut_{\Delta_{r}}
.
But this equivalence is not directly liftable to
Aut_{X}\times Aut_{Y},
see §
1.4.3
.

Therefore we start from
N
-th thickening of
Crit_{r}
, and repeat the argument. Then Lemma
4.3
ensures an approximate lifting
to
Aut_{X}\times Aut_{Y}.
Eventually one gets:
f-{\tilde{f}}\in(I_{Crit_{r}})^{N},
for
N\gg 1.
And now invoke Lemma
4.16
.

1.6.
Applications

Singularities of mapping-germs were classically studied in the real/complex cases. One of the main tools was vector field integration.
The case of arbitrary base field (of arbitrary characteristic) has been under investigation in the last 20 years, see e.g. Chapter 3 of
[
Gr.Lo.Sh
]
. In the absence of vector field integration, the proofs were by induction, thus only for rings of formal power series.

Therefore the left-right Artin approximation (in all its versions) is of key importance. E.g. in
[
Kerner.22
]
we develop the unfolding theory of (analytic/algebraic) map-germs, extending the classical criteria of triviality/versality/stability from
\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}
to an arbitrary field
\Bbbk
. The proofs there are heavily based on Theorems
3.4
,
3.5
,
5.1
.

Maps\big((\Bbbk^{n},o),(\Bbbk^{m},o)\big)

in the real/complex-analytic case see

]
. See

i.

We use multivariables,
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}),

y=(y_{1},\dots,y_{m}),

F=(f_{1},\dots,f_{r}).

The notation
F(x,y)\in(x,y)^{N},
resp.
y(x)\in(x)^{N},
means: every component of the vector
F
(resp.
y(x)
) satisfies this condition.

Denote the maximal ideal of
\Bbbk
by
{\mathfrak{m}}_{\Bbbk}.
(If
\Bbbk
is a field, then
{\mathfrak{m}}_{\Bbbk}=0
.) We always assume
F(x,y)\in{\mathfrak{m}}_{\Bbbk}+(x,y).

ii.

Consider (analytic, resp. algebraic) morphisms of scheme-germs,
Maps(X,Y)
. Here:

•

X\!=\!Spec(R_{X})
, where
R_{X}\!=\!{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk\{x\}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J_{X}$}\right.},
resp.
R_{X}\!=\!{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk\langle x\rangle$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J_{X}$}\right.}.

(We assume
J_{X}\!\subseteq\!(x)^{2}
.)

•

Y=Spec(R_{Y})
, where
R_{Y}\!=\!{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk\{y\}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J_{Y}$}\right.},
resp.
R_{Y}\!=\!{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk\langle y\rangle$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J_{Y}$}\right.}.

(We assume
J_{Y}\!\subseteq\!(y)^{2}
.)

•

For analytic power series,
{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk\{x\}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J_{X}$}\right.},

\Bbbk
is either a normed field (complete with respect to its norm), or a Henselian DVR over a field.
(The norm is always non-trivial.)

The ​main relevant examples of ​DVR are the rings

\Bbbk_{o}[\![\tau]\!],\Bbbk_{o}\langle\tau\rangle,\Bbbk_{o}\{\tau\}.
(Here
\Bbbk_{o}
is a field.)

•

For algebraic power series,
{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk\langle x\rangle$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J_{X}$}\right.},

\Bbbk
is any field or an excellent Henselian local ring over a field.
See
[
Moret-Bailly.21
]
for more detail. Occasionally we call this case “
\Bbbk
-Nash” to avoid any confusion with the algebraic rings like
\Bbbk[x]
or
\Bbbk[x]_{(x)}.

In the
\Bbbk
-smooth case,
J_{X}=0,J_{Y}=0,
we denote
(\Bbbk^{n},o):=Spec(R_{X})
,
(\Bbbk^{m},o):=Spec(R_{Y}).
These are formal/analytic/
\Bbbk
-Nash germs,
depending on the context.

Otherwise the singularities of
X,Y
are arbitrary.

Recall that the rings
R:=\Bbbk[\![x]\!],\Bbbk\{x\},\Bbbk\langle x\rangle
admit substitutions: if
f\in R
and
g_{1},\dots,g_{n}\in(x)\subset R,
then
f(g_{1},\dots,g_{n})\in R.

iii.

A (formal/analytic/
\Bbbk
-Nash) map
f\in Maps(X,Y)
is defined by the corresponding homomorphism of
\Bbbk
-algebras,
f^{\sharp}\in Hom_{\Bbbk}(R_{Y},R_{X}).
All our homomorphisms are local.

In the case
J_{X}=0,J_{Y}=0
one gets the maps of
\Bbbk
-smooth germs,
Maps\big((\Bbbk^{n},o),(\Bbbk^{m},o)\big).

Fix embeddings
X\subseteq(\Bbbk^{n},o),

Y\subseteq(\Bbbk^{m},o)
and some coordinates
(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
on
(\Bbbk^{n},o),
and
(y_{1},\dots,y_{m})
on
(\Bbbk^{m},o).

They are sent to the generators of ideals in
R_{X},

R_{Y}
, abusing notations we write just
(x)=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\subset R_{X}

(y)=(y_{1},\dots,y_{m})\subset R_{Y}.

If
\Bbbk
is a field, then
(x),(y)
are the maximal ideals. Otherwise the maximal ideals are
{\mathfrak{m}}_{X}:={\mathfrak{m}}_{\Bbbk}+(x)\subset R_{X}

and
{\mathfrak{m}}_{Y}:={\mathfrak{m}}_{\Bbbk}+(y)\subset R_{Y}.
When only
R_{X}
is involved we write just
{\mathfrak{m}}\subset R_{X}.

Then a map
f:X\to Y
is represented by
the tuple of power series,
(f_{1},\dots,f_{m})\in{\mathfrak{m}}\cdot R^{\oplus m}_{X}.

This embeds the space of maps as a subset
Maps(X,Y)\subseteq{\mathfrak{m}}\cdot R^{\oplus m}_{X}.

Explicitly, for
R_{Y}={\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk[\![y]\!]$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J_{Y}$}\right.},{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk\{y\}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J_{Y}$}\right.},{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk\langle y\rangle$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J_{Y}$}\right.},
one has:

(1)

Maps(X,Y)=Hom_{\Bbbk}(R_{Y},R_{X})=\{f\in{\mathfrak{m}}\cdot R^{\oplus m}_{X}|\ f^{\sharp}(J_{Y})\subseteq J_{X}.\}\vskip-2.84544pt

If the target is
\Bbbk
-smooth,
Y=(\Bbbk^{m},o),
(i.e.
R_{Y}=\Bbbk[\![y]\!],
resp.
\Bbbk\{y\},
resp.
\Bbbk\langle y\rangle
),
then we get the
R_{X}
-module
Maps\big(X,(\Bbbk^{m},o)\big)={\mathfrak{m}}\cdot R^{\oplus m}_{X}.

iv.

The (formal/analytic/
\Bbbk
-Nash) automorphisms of
X
over
Spec(\Bbbk)
are defined via their algebraic counterparts,
\Bbbk
-linear automorphisms of the local ring,
Aut_{X}:=Aut_{\Bbbk}(R_{X}).

Similarly we denote
Aut_{Y}:=Aut_{\Bbbk}(R_{Y}).

In the
\Bbbk
-smooth case,
Maps\big((\Bbbk^{n},o),(\Bbbk^{m},o)\big),
these automorphisms are the coordinate changes in the source and the target.

These automorphisms act on the space of maps, defining the right and left group actions:

•

\mathscr{R}:=Aut_{X}:=Aut_{\Bbbk}(R_{X})\circlearrowright Maps(X,Y)
, by
(\Phi_{X},f)\to f\circ\Phi^{-1}_{X}
;

•

\mathscr{L}:=Aut_{Y}:=Aut_{\Bbbk}(R_{Y})\circlearrowright Maps(X,Y)
, by
(\Phi_{Y},f)\to\Phi_{Y}\circ f
.

These define the right, left, and left-right equivalence of maps,
\mathscr{L}\times\mathscr{R}\!:f\sim\Phi_{Y}\circ f\circ\Phi^{-1}_{X}.

2.2.
The classical versions of Artin approximation

Below we assume
\hat{y}(x)\in{\mathfrak{m}}\cdot{\hat{\Bbbk}}[\![x]\!].

i.

(
The ordinary version for analytic power series,

[
Denef-Lipshitz
, Theorem 1.1]
)
Let
\Bbbk
be a complete normed field or a Henselian DVR.
Consider equations
F(x,y)=0
for
F(x,y)\in(x,y)\subset\Bbbk\{x,y\}.

Then any formal solution,
\hat{y}(x)\in{\mathfrak{m}}\cdot{\hat{\Bbbk}}[\![x]\!],
is
{\mathfrak{m}}
-adically approximated by analytic solutions.

ii.

(
The nested version for algebraic power series,

[
K.P.P.R.M.78
]
,
[
Popescu.86
]
,
[
Popescu.00
, Prop.3.5]
,
[
Rond.18
, Theorem 5.8]
)
Let
\Bbbk
be a field or
an excellent Henselian local ring.
Consider equations
F(x,y)=0
for
F(x,y)\in(x,y)\subset\Bbbk\langle x,y\rangle.
Suppose these equations have a formal solution
\hat{y}(x)\in{\mathfrak{m}}

that is nested:

(2)

\hat{y}_{1}(x)\in{\hat{\Bbbk}}[\![x_{1},\dots,x_{i_{1}}]\!],\dots,\hat{y}_{m}(x)\in{\hat{\Bbbk}}[\![x_{1},\dots,x_{i_{m}}]\!],\text{ for some }1\leq i_{1}\leq\cdots\leq i_{m}\leq n.\vskip-2.84544pt

This formal solution is
{\mathfrak{m}}
-adically approximated by nested algebraic solutions,

y_{1}(x)\in\Bbbk\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{i_{1}}\rangle,
…,
y_{r}(x)\in\Bbbk\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{i_{m}}\rangle.

iii.

(
The strong Artin approximation,

[
Pfister-Popescu.75
]
,
[
Denef-Lipshitz
, Theorem 7.1]
)

Let
\Bbbk
be a field or a complete DVR, and take the power series

F(x,y)\in\Bbbk[\![x,y]\!].
There exists a function
\beta_{F}:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
satisfying: for every
d\in\mathbb{N}
any approximate solution,
F(x,\bar{y}(x))\in{\mathfrak{m}}^{\beta_{F}(d)},
is
{\mathfrak{m}}^{\bullet}
-approximated by an ordinary solution, i.e.
F(x,y(x))=0
with
y(x)-\bar{y}(x)\in{\mathfrak{m}}^{d}.

iv.

(
Parametrization of solutions
,
[
Płoski.74
]
,
[
Popescu.85
]
,
[
Popescu.86
]
) Given a formal solution,

\hat{y}(x)\in{\mathfrak{m}}\cdot\Bbbk[\![x]\!],
there exist power series
y(x,z)\in\Bbbk\{x,z\}^{\oplus m},
resp.
\Bbbk\langle x,z\rangle^{\oplus m},

and (formal) power series
\hat{z}(x)\in{\mathfrak{m}}\cdot\Bbbk[\![x]\!]^{\oplus c},
satisfying:

(3)

F(x,y(x,z))=0,\quad\text{ and }\quad\hat{y}(x)=y(x,\hat{z}(x)).\vskip-2.84544pt

(
The nested version for
\Bbbk\langle x\rangle
, Theorem 11.4 of
[
Spivakovsky.99
]
, Theorem 2.1 of
[
Bil.Par.Ron.17
]
)
If the formal solution
\hat{y}(x)
is nested, as in (
2
), then the parameterizations

\hat{z}(x)

in equation (
3
) are nested as well.
Namely:

•

y_{1}(x,z)\in\Bbbk\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{i_{1}},z_{1},\dots,z_{j_{1}}\rangle,
…,
y_{m}(x,z)\in\Bbbk\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{i_{m}},z_{1},\dots,z_{j_{m}}\rangle,
and

•

\hat{z}_{1}(x)\dots\hat{z}_{j_{1}}(x)\in\Bbbk[\![x_{1},\dots,x_{i_{1}}]\!],

\hat{z}_{j_{1}+1}(x)\dots\hat{z}_{j_{2}}(x)\in\Bbbk[\![x_{1},\dots,x_{i_{2}}]\!],

for some

Remark 2.1
.

​​​​
i.​

Let

{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk[\![x]\!]$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J_{X}$}\right.},{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk\{x\}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J_{X}$}\right.},{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk\langle x\rangle$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J_{X}$}\right.}.

Here

resp.
R_{X}\{y\},R_{X}\langle y\rangle.

When

this system of equations is understood in the following sense.

\tilde{F}
of

\Bbbk[\![x,y]\!],
resp.
\Bbbk\{x,y\},\Bbbk\langle x,y\rangle.

Then

​​​​
ii.​

(Presenting the condition

as an implicit function equation)

Denote by
R
the rings
{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk[\![x]\!]$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$\mathfrak{a}$}\right.},

{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk\langle x\rangle$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$\mathfrak{a}$}\right.},

{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk\{x\}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$\mathfrak{a}$}\right.}.

Fix two ideals
I,J\subset R

and a homomorphism
\Phi:R\to R.
For the fixed generators,

in

we present
\Phi
by power series,

\{\Phi(x_{i})={\tilde{x}}_{i}(x)\}_{i}.

of

and
\{q^{J}_{j}\}

Then the condition

transforms into the set of equations:

(4)

[\quad\Phi(q^{I}_{i}(x))=\joinrel=]\hskip 28.45274ptq^{I}_{i}({\tilde{x}}(x))=\sum{\tilde{z}}_{ji}\cdot q^{J}_{j}(x),\quad\forall\ i.\vskip-2.84544pt

2.3.
The condition
f\circ\Phi_{X}=\Phi_{Y}\circ{\tilde{f}}
as a morphism of algebras with base-change

The map
f\in Maps(X,Y)
induces on
R_{X}
the structure of
R_{Y}
-algebra.
The structure map is
R_{Y}\ni g(y)\ \lower 1.72218pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{\scriptscriptstyle{f^{\sharp}}}}$}\ g(f)\in R_{X}
.
We observe:
f\ \lower 1.72218pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\sim}\limits^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathscr{L}\mathscr{R}}}}$}\ {\tilde{f}}
iff the corresponding algebras admit an isomorphism with (invertible) base-change, as on the diagram

(5)

\begin{matrix}f\in&R_{X}&\hbox{$\xrightarrow[\,\smash{\raisebox{3.46594pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,]{x\to x({\tilde{x}})}$}&R_{\tilde{X}}&\ni{\tilde{f}}\\
\uparrow\quad&\uparrow f^{\sharp}&&{\tilde{f}}^{\sharp}\uparrow&\quad\uparrow\\
y\in&R_{Y}&\hbox{$\xrightarrow[\,\smash{\raisebox{3.46594pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,]{y\to y({\tilde{y}})}$}&R_{\tilde{Y}}&\ni{\tilde{y}}\end{matrix}\quad.\vskip-2.84544pt

Indeed, the commutativity of the diagram means
f(x({\tilde{x}}))=y({\tilde{f}}({\tilde{x}}))
, i.e.:

f(\Phi_{X}({\tilde{x}}))=\Phi_{Y}({\tilde{f}}({\tilde{x}})),
where
\Phi_{X}({\tilde{x}})=x({\tilde{x}})
and
\Phi_{Y}({\tilde{y}})=y({\tilde{y}}).

In some cases one imposes the condition
f\circ\Phi_{X}=\Phi_{Y}\circ{\tilde{f}}
without invertibility of
\Phi_{X},\Phi_{Y}.
Then this diagram expresses just a homomorphism of algebras with base-change.

3.
Left-right Artin approximation in the Nash case and in the finite analytic case

Definition 3.1
.

The left-right Artin approximation,
\mathscr{L}\mathscr{R}
.AP, holds for a map
f\in Maps(X,Y)
if for any map
{\tilde{f}}\in Maps(X,Y),

and the equation
\Phi_{Y}\circ{\tilde{f}}=f\circ\Phi_{X},
any formal solution
\hat{\Phi}_{Y}\circ{\tilde{f}}=f\circ\hat{\Phi}_{X}

is
{\mathfrak{m}}
-adically approximated by ordinary solutions.

Namely, for any
d\in\mathbb{N}
there exist endomorphisms of
\Bbbk
-algebras
\Phi_{X}\circlearrowright R_{X}
and
\Phi_{Y}\circlearrowright R_{Y}
,
satisfying:

(6)

\Phi_{X}(x)-\hat{\Phi}_{X}(x)\in{\mathfrak{m}}_{X}^{d},\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\Phi_{Y}(y)-\hat{\Phi}_{Y}(y)\in{\mathfrak{m}}_{Y}^{d},\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\Phi_{Y}\circ{\tilde{f}}=f\circ\Phi_{X}.\vskip-2.84544pt

Definition 3.2
.

The left-right parameterized approximation,
\mathscr{L}\mathscr{R}
.APP,
holds for a map
f\in Maps(X,Y),
if for any map
{\tilde{f}}\in Maps(X,Y)

and the equation
\Phi_{Y}\circ{\tilde{f}}=f\circ\Phi_{X},
any formal solution
\hat{\Phi}_{Y}\!\circ\!{\tilde{f}}\!=\!f\!\circ\!\hat{\Phi}_{X}

is a specialization of a parameterized ordinary solution,
\Phi_{X,z_{X}}
,
\Phi_{Y,z_{Y}}.

Namely, there exist Nash homomorphisms
\Phi^{\sharp}_{Y,z_{Y}}:R_{Y}\to R_{\tilde{Y}}\langle z_{Y}\rangle,

\Phi^{\sharp}_{X,z_{X}}:R_{X}\to R_{\tilde{X}}\langle z_{X}\rangle,
(resp. their analytic versions), acting by
y\to y({\tilde{y}},z_{Y})
and
x\to x({\tilde{x}},z_{X}),
and
formal power series
\hat{z}_{X}(x)\in{\widehat{R}}_{X},

\hat{z}_{Y}(y)\in{\widehat{R}}_{Y},
that altogether satisfy:

(7)

\Phi_{Y,z_{Y}}\circ{\tilde{f}}=f\circ\Phi_{X,z_{X}},\quad\quad\quad\hat{\Phi}_{X}=\Phi_{X,\hat{z}_{X}(x)},\quad\quad\quad\hat{\Phi}_{Y}=\Phi_{Y,\hat{z}_{Y}(y)}.\vskip-2.84544pt

(Here
f,{\tilde{f}},\hat{\Phi}_{X},\hat{\Phi}_{Y}
are prescribed, while
\Phi_{Y,z_{Y}},\Phi_{X,z_{X}},\hat{z}_{X}(x),\hat{z}_{Y}(y)
are unknowns.)

Remark 3.3
.

​​​​
i.​

We do not impose invertibility of
\Phi_{X},\Phi_{Y}
.
But if
\hat{\Phi}_{X},\hat{\Phi}_{Y}
are invertible, then their approximations
\Phi_{X},\Phi_{Y}
are ring isomorphisms/automorphisms,

\Phi_{X}\!\in\!\mathscr{R}
and
\Phi_{Y}\!\in\!\mathscr{L}.

​​​​
ii.​

Imposing
\Phi_{Y}=Id_{Y},
one gets
the properties
\mathscr{R}
.AP,
\mathscr{R}
.APP. They are implied by AP, APP, Example
2.2
. Conversely, AP can be stated as a condition of nested
\mathscr{R}
.AP-type:

suppose a map
F(x,y)
is formally
\mathscr{R}
-equivalent (by
y\to y-{\hat{g}}(x)
) to a map of type
\sum y_{i}\cdot\tilde{F}_{i}(x,y).
Is this (formal, nested) equivalence approximated by ordinary nested equivalence?

​​​​
iii.​

Imposing
\Phi_{X}=Id_{X},
one gets the properties
\mathscr{L}
.AP,
\mathscr{L}
.APP.

Let
Y=(\Bbbk^{m},o)
. The property
\mathscr{L}
.AP implies the inverse Artin approximation for
f.
The property
\mathscr{L}
.APP implies the Płoski version of
the inverse Artin approximation.

Proof.
Take a formal relation
\hat{Q}(f)=0.
If
\hat{Q}(y)\subseteq(y)^{2}
then define
\hat{\Phi}_{Y}:=Id+\hat{Q}\in\hat{\mathscr{L}}.
Thus
\hat{\Phi}_{Y}(f)=f.
Approximate
\hat{\Phi}
by an ordinary automorphism,
\Phi_{Y}(f)=f.
Then
Q:=\Phi_{Y}-Id_{Y}
is the needed approximation of
\hat{Q}.

If
\hat{Q}(y)\not\subseteq(y)^{2}
then apply the previous argument to
\{y_{i}\cdot\hat{Q}(y)\}.

The Płoski version: using
\mathscr{L}
.APP one gets
\Phi_{Y,z}
satisfying:
\Phi_{Y,z}(f)=f
and
\Phi_{Y,\hat{z}(y)}=\hat{\Phi}_{Y}.
Then take
Q_{Y,z}:=\Phi_{Y,z}-Id_{Y}.

\blacksquare

​​​​
iv.​

Suppose for a given class of maps the condition
\Phi_{Y}\circ{\tilde{f}}=f\circ\Phi_{X}
can be translated into a (finite) system of implicit function equations, without nested conditions. Then
\mathscr{L}\mathscr{R}
.AP,
\mathscr{L}\mathscr{R}
.APP hold for this class of maps.

Moreover, the properties
\mathscr{R}
.AP and
\mathscr{L}
.AP hold as well.
Indeed, to the condition
\Phi_{Y}\circ{\tilde{f}}\!=\!f\circ\Phi_{X}
one just appends the
condition
\Phi_{Y}(y)\!=\!y
(resp.
\Phi_{X}(x)\!\!=\!\!x
). And the later conditions are implicit function equations.

We have formal horizontal homomorphisms in the diagram (

),
and want to approximate these by ordinary homomorphisms of the algebras

with a base change.
The needed homomorphism is a map

presentable by (algebraic) power series,

and
\psi(x)=x({\tilde{x}}).

And

(8)

\psi(J_{Y})\subseteq J_{\tilde{Y}},\hskip 28.45274pt\psi(J_{X}+(y-f(x)))\subseteq J_{\tilde{X}}+({\tilde{y}}-{\tilde{f}}({\tilde{x}})).\vskip-2.84544pt

.ii.
Indeed:

-

for

and
J_{Y}=0

-

for

or

take the additional conditions and further auxiliary variables.

By our assumption these implicit function equations admit a formal solution,

\hat{\psi}(y)=\hat{y}({\tilde{y}})\in\Bbbk[\![{\tilde{y}}]\!],\quad\quad\hat{\psi}(x)=\hat{x}({\tilde{x}})\in\Bbbk[\![{\tilde{x}}]\!],\quad\quad\hat{z}({\tilde{x}},{\tilde{y}})\in\Bbbk[\![{\tilde{x}},{\tilde{y}}]\!].\vskip-2.84544pt

We can think of it as a formal nested solution,
\hat{\psi}(y)=\hat{y}({\tilde{y}})
and
\hat{\psi}(x)=\hat{x}({\tilde{x}},{\tilde{y}})\in\Bbbk[\![{\tilde{x}},{\tilde{y}}]\!].

Altogether, we have transformed the initial condition into a system of implicit function equations with nested solution.

\bullet

.AP)

.ii.,
to approximate this formal nested solution by an algebraic nested solution,

and

Explicitly:

J_{Y}|_{y({\tilde{y}})}\subseteq{\tilde{J}}_{Y},\quad\quad J_{X}|_{x({\tilde{x}},{\tilde{y}})}\subseteq J_{\tilde{X}}+({\tilde{y}}-{\tilde{f}}({\tilde{x}})),\quad\quad y({\tilde{y}})-f(x({\tilde{x}},{\tilde{y}}))\in({\tilde{y}}-{\tilde{f}}({\tilde{x}}))\subset\Bbbk\langle{\tilde{x}},{\tilde{y}}\rangle.\vskip-2.84544pt

Then

Altogether we get:

and

3.2.
Approximations for finite analytic morphisms,
R_{X}\!=\!{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk\{x\}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J_{X}$}\right.},

R_{Y}\!=\!{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk\{y\}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J_{Y}$}\right.}

Theorem 3.5
.

The properties
\mathscr{L}\mathscr{R}
.AP,
\mathscr{L}\mathscr{R}
.APP,
\mathscr{L}
.AP,
\mathscr{L}
.APP, the inverse Artin approximation (and its Płoski version) hold for finite analytic morphisms,
Maps(X,Y).

Namely, if
f,{\tilde{f}}\in Maps(X,Y)
are finite analytic morphisms, and
\hat{\Phi}_{Y}\circ{\tilde{f}}=f\circ\hat{\Phi}_{X},
then
\hat{\Phi}_{Y},\hat{\Phi}_{X}
are approximated by analytic morphisms (resp. are Płoski-specializations, and so on).

Proof.
Suppose the map-germs
f,{\tilde{f}}:X\to Y
are formally
\mathscr{L}\mathscr{R}
-equivalent.

We have finitely-generated modules
R_{X}\in mod
-
R_{Y}
(via
f
) and
R_{\tilde{X}}\in mod
-
R_{\tilde{Y}}
(via
{\tilde{f}}
).
The (formal) relation
\hat{\Phi}_{Y}\circ{\tilde{f}}=f\circ\hat{\Phi}_{X}
implies the (formal) morphism of these
modules with the base change, as on the diagram (
5
).

​​
Step 1.

Fix some generators of the modules,
R_{X}=R_{Y}\langle\{v_{\bullet}\}\rangle,
and

R_{\tilde{X}}=R_{\tilde{Y}}\langle\{\tilde{v}_{\bullet}\}\rangle.

W.l.o.g. we assume: the set
\{v_{\bullet}\}
contains the generators
x=(x_{1}\dots x_{n})
of
R_{X},
while the set
\{\tilde{v}_{\bullet}\}
contains the generators
{\tilde{x}}=({\tilde{x}}_{1}\dots{\tilde{x}}_{\tilde{n}})
of
R_{\tilde{X}}.

For these sets of generators we get the presentation matrices of the modules,
R_{X}=coker[A(y)]
,
R_{\tilde{X}}=coker[\tilde{A}({\tilde{y}})]
.

The morphism of modules with base change,
R_{Y}\to R_{\tilde{Y}}
and
coker[A(y)]\to coker[\tilde{A}({\tilde{y}})],
yields the commutative diagram

(12)

\begin{matrix}R^{\oplus(\dots)}_{Y}&\ \lower 1.72218pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{\scriptscriptstyle{A(y)}}}$}\ &R^{\oplus(\dots)}_{Y}&\to&R_{X}&\to&0\\
\downarrow U&&\downarrow V&&\quad\downarrow\Phi_{X}\\
R^{\oplus(\dots)}_{\tilde{Y}}&\ \lower 1.72218pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{\scriptscriptstyle{\tilde{A}({\tilde{y}})}}}$}\ &R^{\oplus(\dots)}_{\tilde{Y}}&\to&R_{\tilde{X}}&\to&0\end{matrix}\vskip-2.84544pt

Thus we get the condition

V({\tilde{y}})\cdot A(y({\tilde{y}}))=\tilde{A}({\tilde{y}})\cdot U({\tilde{y}})
, for the unknowns
y({\tilde{y}}),

U({\tilde{y}}),V({\tilde{y}})

over
R_{\tilde{Y}}
.
This is a system of implicit function equations, linear in
U,V,
and analytic in
y
.

The formal relation
\hat{\Phi}_{Y}\circ{\tilde{f}}=f\circ\hat{\Phi}_{X}
yields a formal solution to this system,
\hat{y}({\tilde{y}}),\hat{U}({\tilde{y}}),\hat{V}({\tilde{y}})
.
Invoking the Artin approximation over the analytic ring
R_{\tilde{Y}},
§
2.2
.i., we approximate this formal
solution by an analytic solution,
y({\tilde{y}})
,
U({\tilde{y}})
,
V({\tilde{y}})
.
This gives an analytic morphism of modules with base change,
\Psi:coker[A(y)]\to coker[\tilde{A}({\tilde{y}})].

But this morphism of modules does not necessarily lift to an isomorphism of algebras of the diagram (
5
).

​​
Step 2.

To ensure the lifting from modules to algebras we impose additional equations.
Besides the relations of a module we have the multiplications of the algebra:

\{\prod_{\sum{\underline{m}}={\underline{d}}}\underline{v}^{\underline{m}}=\prod_{\sum{\underline{m}}^{\prime}={\underline{d}}}\underline{v}^{{\underline{m}}^{\prime}}\}
.
(Here
{\underline{d}}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
is any multi-index.)
We ask that the morphism of modules
\Psi:coker[A(y)]\to coker\tilde{A}[{\tilde{y}}]

be multiplicative. This gives the equations:

(13)

\Psi(\prod_{\sum{\underline{m}}={\underline{d}}}\underline{v}^{\underline{m}})=\prod_{\sum{\underline{m}}^{\prime}={\underline{d}}}\Psi(\underline{v}^{{\underline{m}}^{\prime}})\in R_{\tilde{X}},\quad\forall\ {\underline{d}}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}\vskip-2.84544pt

These conditions are polynomial equations in the entries of
V.

The set of these equations is infinite, but the ideal of these equations is generated by a finite subset.
(Because
R_{\tilde{Y}}
is Noetherian, while
R_{\tilde{X}}
is a finitely generated
R_{\tilde{Y}}
-module.)
We add this (finite) set of equations to the equations
y(J_{\tilde{Y}})=J_{\tilde{Y}},

V({\tilde{y}})\cdot A(y({\tilde{y}}))=\tilde{A}({\tilde{y}})\cdot U({\tilde{y}})
.

A remark: the ideal
J_{X}\subset\Bbbk\{x\}
represents zero in the
R_{Y}
-module
R_{X}.
Hence it is sent to zero in
R_{\tilde{X}}.

Altogether we get a (finite) system of implicit-function equations.
They are analytic power series in the unknowns
y,U,V
.
The formal morphism of the algebras
(R_{X},R_{Y},f)
,
(R_{\tilde{X}},R_{\tilde{Y}},{\tilde{f}})
is a formal solution to these equations,

\hat{U}({\tilde{y}}),\hat{V}({\tilde{y}}),\hat{y}({\tilde{y}})
.

•

(
\mathscr{L}\mathscr{R}
.AP)
By the Artin approximation, §
2.2
.i., this solution is approximated by an analytic solution. And this ordinary solution is the needed analytic morphism of algebras.

•

(
\mathscr{L}\mathscr{R}
.APP)Using Płoski theorem we present the formal solution as a specialization of a parameterized analytic solution,

U({\tilde{y}},z),

V({\tilde{y}},z),

y({\tilde{y}},z)
.

•

(
\mathscr{L}
.AP,
\mathscr{L}
.APP) Identify
R_{X}=R_{\tilde{X}}
and impose the condition
\Phi_{X}=Id_{X}.
This means:
Im(V-Id)\subseteq Im(\tilde{A}),
i.e.

V-Id=\tilde{A}\cdot\tilde{B}
for an unknown matrix
\tilde{B}.

We are still left with implicit function equations.

•

The inverse Artin approximation and its Płoski version follow by Remark
3.3
.

\blacksquare

4.
Morphisms of weakly-finite singularity type

4.1.
Preparations

Below
R_{X},R_{Y}
are as in §
2.1
.

​​​​​​
i.​

The image of a morphism
X\ \lower 1.72218pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{\scriptscriptstyle{f}}}$}\ Y
is the subscheme
f(X)\subseteq Y
defined by the ideal
ker[f^{\sharp}_{|}:R_{Y}\to R_{X}]\subset R_{Y}.

A morphism is called dominant, if this ideal vanishes.
In the analytic case one can also take small representatives of the germs
X,Y
and to consider the set-theoretic image of
f.

For a finite morphism
f
this set-theoretic image gives a (well-defined) analytic subgerm coinciding with
f(X),
e.g. by Grauert’s theorem on direct image.

For non-finite morphisms this set-theoretic image can be pathological, e.g. not a well defined germ or not an analytic germ,
[
Joiţţa-Tibar.21
]
.
Below we often refer to examples of Osgood-type: analytic morphisms
(\mathbb{C}^{n},o)\to(\mathbb{C}^{m},o),

2\leq n<m,
that are analytically dominant.
(I.e. their set-theoretic image does not lie inside any analytic hypersurface, i.e. their image via
ker[f^{\sharp}]
is the whole target.) See
[
Rond.18
, §5.3]
and
[
Abhyankar-van der Put.70
]
for examples of such pathologies.

Take a small representative of such a morphism,
\mathbb{C}^{n}\supset Ball^{n}_{\delta}\ \lower 1.72218pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{\scriptscriptstyle{f}}}$}\ Ball^{m}_{\epsilon}\subset\mathbb{C}^{m}.
Then the germ of the image can be different from the image of the germ,
f(Ball^{n}_{\delta},x)\neq(f(Ball^{n}_{\delta}),f(x))
. E.g. suppose
f
is finite at a point
x\in Ball^{n}_{\delta},
but of Osgood type at
o\in\mathbb{C}^{n}.
Then
dimf(Ball^{n}_{\delta},x)=n,
while

(f(Ball^{n}_{\delta}),f(x))=(\mathbb{C}^{m},f(x)).

​​​​​​
ii.​

Take a (formal/analytic/Nash) germ
X
and its module of
\Bbbk
-linear derivations (vector fields),
Der_{X}:=Der_{\Bbbk}R_{X}.

E.g. for
X=(\Bbbk^{n},o)
one has
Der_{(\Bbbk^{n},o)}:=R_{(\Bbbk^{n},o)}\langle\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}},\dots,\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}\rangle,

see Theorem 30.6(ii) of
[
Matsumura
]
.
For
X=V(J_{X})\subset(\Bbbk^{n},o)
the elements of
Der_{X}
lift to logarithmic derivations on
(\Bbbk^{n},o)
that preserve the ideal
J_{X}.

More generally, given a subgerm
Z=V(I_{Z})\subset X
one considers the module of logarithmic derivations,

Der_{X,Z}:=Der_{X}Log(Z):=\{\xi|\ \xi(I_{Z})\subseteq I_{Z}\}\subseteq Der_{X}.

The natural projection
Der_{X,Z}\to Der_{Z}
is not always surjective.

Yet more generally, for a set of subgerms
\{Z_{j}=V(I_{Z_{j}})\subset X\}_{j}
we take “multi-logarithmic” derivations:

(14)

Der_{X,\{Z_{\bullet}\}}:=\cap_{j}Der_{X}Log(Z_{j})=\{\xi|\ \xi(I_{Z_{j}})\subseteq I_{Z_{j}},\ \ \forall j\}.\vskip-2.84544pt

​​​​​​
iii.​

We use Kähler differentials. As our algebras are not f.g. over
\Bbbk,
we work with separated differentials,
\Omega^{sep}_{X/\Bbbk},
see Exercise 16.14 of
[
Eisenbud
]
.
E.g.
\Omega^{sep}_{(\Bbbk^{n},o)}=R_{(\Bbbk^{n},o)}\langle dx_{1},\dots,dx_{n}\rangle.

​​​​​​
iv.​

Suppose
\Bbbk
is a local ring, then

can be considered as a family of maps, over the base
Spec(\Bbbk).

Taking the maximal ideal,
{\mathfrak{m}}_{\Bbbk}\subset\Bbbk,
one gets the central fibres:

•

of the schemes,
X_{o}=Spec(R_{X}\otimes{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{${\mathfrak{m}}_{\Bbbk}$}\right.})
and
Y_{o}=Spec(R_{Y}\otimes{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{${\mathfrak{m}}_{\Bbbk}$}\right.}).

•

of the map,

f_{o}:=f\otimes{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{${\mathfrak{m}}_{\Bbbk}$}\right.}:X_{o}\to Y_{o}.

This
f_{o}
is defined algebraically via

(15)

f^{\sharp}_{o}:=f^{\sharp}\otimes{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{${\mathfrak{m}}_{\Bbbk}$}\right.}\in Hom_{{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{${\mathfrak{m}}_{\Bbbk}$}\right.}}(R_{Y}\otimes{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{${\mathfrak{m}}_{\Bbbk}$}\right.},R_{X}\otimes{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{${\mathfrak{m}}_{\Bbbk}$}\right.}).\vskip-2.84544pt

If
\Bbbk
is a field then (trivially)

4.1.1.
Approximate integrability of derivations

Take a (formal/analytic/Nash) germ
X\subseteq(\Bbbk^{n},o)
and a logarithmic derivation

\xi\in Der_{(\Bbbk^{n},o),X}.
Suppose
\xi({\mathfrak{m}})\!\subseteq\!{\mathfrak{m}}^{2},
i.e. this derivation is
{\mathfrak{m}}
-adically nilpotent. Define the self-map of
(\Bbbk^{n},o)
by
x\!\to\!x\!+\!\xi(x).
This is an automorphism of
(\Bbbk^{n},o),
but it does not necessarily descend to an automorphism of
X,
i.e. an automorphism of
R_{X}.

We want to get an automorphism of
X
by correcting this map by “higher order terms”.
As
\xi({\mathfrak{m}})\subseteq{\mathfrak{m}}^{2},
one has
\xi(\xi({\mathfrak{m}}))\subseteq{\mathfrak{m}}\cdot\xi({\mathfrak{m}})\subseteq{\mathfrak{m}}^{3}.

Definition 4.1
.

A logarithmic derivation
\xi\in Der_{(\Bbbk^{n},o),X}
is called approximately integrable if
there exists a (formal/analytic/Nash) automorphism
\Phi_{(\Bbbk^{n},o)}\circlearrowright(\Bbbk^{n},o)
that descends to an automorphism
\Phi_{X}\circlearrowright X
and satisfies:

\Phi_{(\Bbbk^{n},o)}(x)-x-\xi(x)\in\xi(\xi({\mathfrak{m}})).

More generally, for some subgerms
\{Z_{\bullet}\}\subset X
take a multi-log-derivation
\xi\in Der_{X,\{Z_{\bullet}\}}
satisfying
\xi({\mathfrak{m}})\subseteq{\mathfrak{m}}^{2}
. Call
\xi

approximately integrable
if
the automorphism
\Phi_{X}\circlearrowright(\Bbbk^{n},o)
as above defines automorphisms of
X
and
\{Z_{\bullet}\}.

Example 4.2
.

​​​​​​​
i.​

For
char(\Bbbk)=0
every log-derivation (that satisfies
\xi({\mathfrak{m}})\subseteq{\mathfrak{m}}^{2}
) is approximately integrable.

Proof.
Define
\Phi(x):=e^{\xi}x:=(\sum\frac{\xi^{j}}{j!})x,
see
[
Bourbaki.Lie
, Chapter II, §6]
. Thus
\Phi\circlearrowright\widehat{(\Bbbk^{n}\!,\!o)},
a formal automorphism. It
preserves the subgerms
​
X,\!\{Z_{\bullet}\}
and satisfies: ​
\Phi_{X}(x)\!-\!x\!-\!\xi(x)\!\in\!\xi(\xi(x)).

If needed, we approximate
\Phi
by an analytic/Nash automorphism that preserves
X,\{Z_{\bullet}\}.

(The later conditions are implicit function equations, see Remark
2.1
.)
\blacksquare

​​​​​​​
ii.​

For
char>0
the approximate integrability is a non-trivial restriction, see e.g.

[
Kerner.21
, §2.5]
.

4.1.2.
Approximate lifting of automorphisms

Take an ideal
I\subset R
and an automorphism
{\bar{\Phi}}\in Aut_{\Bbbk}({\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$R$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$I$}\right.}).
(Below we assume
{\bar{\Phi}}(x)-x\in{\mathfrak{m}}^{2},
i.e.
{\bar{\Phi}}
is “tangent to identity”.) It does not necessarily lift to an element of
Aut_{\Bbbk}(R).

But a weaker statement (used in the proof of Theorem
5.1
) often holds:

(16)

For any ideal
I\subset R
and a suitable
N\gg 1,
any automorphism
{\bar{\Phi}}\in Aut_{\Bbbk}({\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$R$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$I^{N}$}\right.})

has an approximate lifting, i.e.
\Phi\in Aut_{\Bbbk}(R)
such that
\Phi(I)=I
and
[\Phi]=[{\bar{\Phi}}]\in Aut_{\Bbbk}({\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$R$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$I$}\right.}).

(Here
N
depends on
I\subset R
only, not on
\Phi
.)

As was mentioned in §
1.4
, this property would follow from SAP for the filtration
I^{\bullet}\!\subset\!R.
But this version of SAP does not hold, see

[
Rond.18
, Example 3.20.]
. For
char(\Bbbk)\!=\!0
we give a different proof.

Lemma 4.3
.

Property (
16
) holds in zero characteristic.

Proof.
First we consider the formal case,
R={\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk[\![x]\!]$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J$}\right.}.
The proof goes by “iterations” on the (finite) number of generators of
J.

At each iteration we invoke the Artin-Rees lemma. Therefore we start with some
N\gg 1,
and by the end of iterations (Step 2) we specify conditions, which
N
must satisfy. (These conditions depend on
I,J
only, but not on
{\bar{\Phi}}.
)

​​
Step 1.

Take the full preimage of
I
in
\Bbbk[\![x]\!],
denote it (abusing notations) by

I\subset\Bbbk[\![x]\!].
Fix an automorphism
{\bar{\Phi}}\in Aut_{\Bbbk}({\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk[\![x]\!]$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J+I^{N}$}\right.}).
As
char(\Bbbk)\!=\!0,
we can present
{\bar{\Phi}}\!=\!exp({\bar{\xi}}),

for a derivation
{\bar{\xi}}\!\in\!Der_{\Bbbk}(\Bbbk[\![x]\!])=:Der_{(\Bbbk^{n},o)}
satisfying:
{\bar{\xi}}({\mathfrak{m}})\subseteq{\mathfrak{m}}^{2}
and
{\bar{\xi}}(J+I^{N})\subseteq J+I^{N}.
See
[
Bourbaki.Lie
, Chapter II, §6]
.

We should approximate
{\bar{\xi}}
by a derivation
\xi\!\in\!Der_{(\Bbbk^{n},o)}
satisfying:
\xi(J)\!\subseteq\!J
and
\xi\!\equiv\!{\bar{\xi}}\ \!mod\ \!I.
(Then one defines
\Phi\!:=\!e^{\xi}.
) This approximation is constructed iteratively in Step 2.

​​
Step 2.

Fix some generators
J=(q_{1},\dots,q_{c})\subset\Bbbk[\![x]\!].
Then
{\bar{\xi}}(q_{1})\in J+I^{N},
i.e.
{\bar{\xi}}(q_{1})-h_{1}\in I^{N}
for some
h_{1}\in J.

Thus

(17)

{\bar{\xi}}(q_{1})-h_{1}\in I^{N}\cap(Der_{(\Bbbk^{n},o)}(q_{1})+J)\ \lower 1.72218pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\subseteq}\limits^{\scriptscriptstyle{A.R.}}}$}\ I^{N-d_{1}}\cdot(Der_{(\Bbbk^{n},o)}(q_{1})+J).\vskip-2.84544pt

Here the inclusion
A.R.
holds by the Artin-Rees lemma, for some
d_{1}.
This
d_{1}
is independent of
N,{\bar{\Phi}},\xi.

Thus we can assume
N\gg d_{1}.

Therefore
{\bar{\xi}}(q_{1})-{\tilde{\xi}}(q_{1})\in J
for some
{\tilde{\xi}}\!\in\!I^{N-d_{1}}\!\cdot\!Der_{(\Bbbk^{n},o)}.
Replace
{\bar{\xi}}
by
{\bar{\xi}}-{\tilde{\xi}}
to get:

(18)

This adjusted derivation satisfies (as before):
{\bar{\xi}}(q_{2})-h_{2}\in I^{N-d_{1}}\cap(Der_{\Bbbk}((q_{1}),J)\cdot q_{2}+J),

for some
h_{2}\in J.
Invoking Artin-Rees we get:
{\bar{\xi}}(q_{2})-h_{2}\in I^{N-d_{1}-d_{2}}\cdot(Der_{\Bbbk}((q_{1}),J)\cdot q_{2}+J).

Thus
{\bar{\xi}}(q_{2})-{\tilde{\xi}}(q_{2})\in J,
for some
{\tilde{\xi}}\in I^{N-d_{1}-d_{2}}\cdot Der_{\Bbbk}((q_{1}),J).
Replacing
{\bar{\xi}}
by
{\bar{\xi}}-{\tilde{\xi}}
we get:

(19)

{\bar{\xi}}(q_{1},q_{2})\in J,\quad\text{ i.e. }\ {\bar{\xi}}\in Der_{\Bbbk}((q_{1},q_{2}),J),\quad\text{ and }\quad{\bar{\xi}}(q_{3},\dots,q_{c})\subseteq J+I^{N-d_{1}-d_{2}}.\vskip-2.84544pt

Iterate this up to the last generator
q_{c}.
Eventually one gets a derivation
\xi\in Der_{(\Bbbk^{n},o)}
satisfying
\xi(J)\subseteq J.

And this adjusted derivation
\xi
differs from the initial one by an element of
I^{N-d_{1}-\cdots-d_{c}}\cdot Der_{(\Bbbk^{n},o)}.

These integers
d_{1}\!\dots\!d_{c}
depend on
I,\!J\!\subset\!\Bbbk[\![x]\!]
only, not on
N,\!\xi,\!{\bar{\Phi}}.
Thus it is enough to choose

N\!>\!d_{1}+\cdots+d_{c},
to get: ​
\forall\ {\tilde{\xi}}
there exists
\xi\!\in\!Der_{(\Bbbk^{n},o)}Log(J)
such that
{\tilde{\xi}}\!-\!\xi\!\in\!I\!\cdot\!Der_{(\Bbbk^{n},o)}.

​​
Step 3.

Define the needed lifting of
{\bar{\Phi}},
i.e. the (formal) automorphism
\Phi:=e^{\xi}\in Aut_{\Bbbk}(\Bbbk[\![x]\!]).

It satisfies the claimed conditions:

(20)

\Phi(J)=J,\quad\Phi(I)=I,\quad\quad\text{and }\quad[\Phi]\equiv{\bar{\Phi}}\ mod\ I^{N-d_{1}-\cdots-d_{c}}.\vskip-2.84544pt

In the analytic/Nash cases one applies the Artin approximation to the formal solution of equation (
20
).
(These equations of are implicit function type.)

\blacksquare

Example 4.4
.

Fix a chain of radical ideals,
I_{1}\subset\cdots\subset I_{r}\subset R,
and the corresponding chain of projections,
R\to{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$R$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$I_{1}$}\right.}\to\cdots\to{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$R$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$I_{r}$}\right.}.
Given an automorphism
{\bar{\Phi}}\circlearrowright{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$R$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$I_{r}$}\right.}
we want to lift it to an automorphism
\Phi\circlearrowright R
that preserves the chain
I_{\bullet}
. Such a lifting does not exist in general (as before). However, condition (
16
) implies:

There exist
N_{1}\dots N_{r}\gg 1
(depending on
R,I_{\bullet}
only) such that any automorphism
{\bar{\Phi}}\!\circlearrowright\!{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$R$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$I^{N_{1}}_{1}\!+\!\cdots\!+\!I^{N_{r}}_{r}$}\right.}
lifts to an automorphism
\Phi\circlearrowright R
that preserves the chain
I_{\bullet}.

Proof.
Fix (successively) numbers
1\ll N_{1}\ll N_{2}\ll\cdots\ll N_{r},
such that condition (
16
)
holds for the projections
R\to{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$R$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$I^{N_{1}}_{1}$}\right.},

{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$R$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$I^{N_{1}}_{1}$}\right.}\to{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$R$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$I^{N_{1}}_{1}+I^{N_{2}}_{2}$}\right.},
…,
{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$R$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$\sum^{r-1}_{j=1}I^{N_{j}}_{j}$}\right.}\to{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$R$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$\sum^{r}_{j=1}I^{N_{j}}_{j}$}\right.}.
Fix any automorphism
\Phi_{r}\circlearrowright{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$R$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$\sum^{r}_{j=1}I^{N_{j}}_{j}$}\right.},
lift it to
\Phi_{r-1}\circlearrowright{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$R$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$\sum^{r-1}_{j=1}I^{N_{j}}_{j}$}\right.},
and so on, up to
\Phi\circlearrowright R.

Observe:
\Phi_{r-1}(I_{r})=I_{r}
and
{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$R$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$I_{r}+\sum^{r}_{j=1}I^{N_{j}}_{j}$}\right.}\circlearrowleft[\Phi_{r}]=[\Phi_{r-1}].

Similarly
\Phi_{r-2}(I_{r-1})=I_{r-1}
and
{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$R$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$I_{r-1}+\sum^{r-1}_{j=1}I^{N_{j}}_{j}$}\right.}\circlearrowleft[\Phi_{r-1}]=[\Phi_{r-2}].

Therefore
\Phi_{r-2}(I_{r})=I_{r}.
And so on…
\blacksquare

4.2.
The critical locus/discriminant of a morphism
X\ \lower 1.72218pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{\scriptscriptstyle{f}}}$}\ Y

Below we tacitly replace the target
Y
by the image
f(X)\subseteq Y.
Thus
f
becomes a dominant morphism.

Definition 4.5
.

​1.​

The critical module,
\mathscr{C}_{X,f},
is defined by the (exact) Zariski-Lipman sequence
Der_{X}f\to T_{X\to Y}\to\mathscr{C}_{X,f}\to 0.

Here
T_{X\to Y}:=Hom_{X}(f^{*}\Omega^{sep}_{Y},R_{X})
is the tangent to the space
Maps(X,Y).

​2.​

The critical locus,
Crit_{X}f\subseteq X,
is defined by the zeroth Fitting ideal
Fitt_{0}(\mathscr{C}_{X,f})\subseteq R_{X}.

​3.​

The discriminant
\Delta\subset Y
is the image
f(Crit_{X}f)\subseteq Y,
see §
4.1
.i.

For questions of Artin approximation the particular scheme structures on
Crit_{X}f
and
\Delta
are not important. Usually we take the reduced structure.

Example 4.6
.

Take a dominant morphism
X\ \lower 1.72218pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{\scriptscriptstyle{f}}}$}\ Y.

​​​​​
i.​

Let
Y=(\Bbbk^{m},o),
thus
\Omega^{sep}_{Y}\cong R_{Y}\langle dy_{1},\dots,dy_{m}\rangle.
As
f
is dominant,
ker[f^{\sharp}:R_{Y}\to R_{X}]=0.
Thus

f^{*}\Omega^{sep}_{Y}
is a free
R_{X}
-module.
Then
T_{X\to Y}\cong R^{\oplus m}_{X}
and

\mathscr{C}_{X,f}\cong{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$R^{\oplus m}_{X}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$Der_{X}(f)$}\right.}.
Denote by
[Der_{X}(f)]
the defining matrix of the submodule
Der_{X}(f)\subseteq R^{\oplus m}_{X}.
Then
Crit_{X}f\subseteq X
is defined by the determinantal ideal
I_{m}[Der_{X}(f)]\subseteq R_{X}.

For
X\!=\!(\Bbbk^{n},o)
this is the classical Jacobian matrix and
I_{m}[Der_{(\Bbbk^{n},o)}(f)]
is the classical Jacobian ideal.

​​​​​
ii.​

Let
Y\subseteq(\Bbbk^{m},o)
be defined by the ideal
I_{Y}\subset R_{(\Bbbk^{m},o)}.
Take the co-normal sequence

(21)

{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$I_{Y}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$I^{2}_{Y}$}\right.}\!\ \lower 1.72218pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{\scriptscriptstyle{d}}}$}\ \!R_{Y}\otimes\Omega^{sep}_{(\Bbbk^{m},o)}\!\to\!\Omega^{sep}_{Y}\!\to\!0.\vskip-2.84544pt

Then
Hom_{X}(f^{*}\Omega^{sep}_{Y},R_{X})\!\subseteq\!Hom_{X}(f^{*}\Omega^{sep}_{(\Bbbk^{m},o)},R_{X})\!\cong\!R^{\oplus m}_{X}.

Explicitly, fix some generators
I_{Y}=R_{Y}\langle\{q_{\bullet}\}\rangle
to get:

(22)

T_{X\to Y}=\{h\in R^{\oplus m}_{X}|\ dq_{1}|_{f}(h)=0=\cdots=dq_{c}|_{f}(h)\}\subset R^{\oplus m}_{X}.\vskip-2.84544pt

Here
dq_{i}|_{f}\in f^{\sharp}\Omega^{sep}_{(\Bbbk^{m},o)}
and
dq_{i}|_{f}(h)\in R_{X}.

​​​​​
iii.​

Take a map
f\!:(\Bbbk^{1},o)\!\to\!(\Bbbk^{m},o),
whose image is a curve-germ,
Y\!:=f(\Bbbk^{1},o)\!=\!V(q_{1},\dots,q_{c}).

Suppose
\Bbbk
is infinite.
If
char\!>\!0
we assume
(dq_{1}|_{f},\dots,dq_{c}|_{f})\!\neq\!(0,\dots,0).
Then
\mathscr{C}\!\cong\!{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$R_{(\Bbbk^{1},o)}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$(f^{\prime}_{1},\dots,f^{\prime}_{m})$}\right.}.

Proof.
In this case:
T_{(\Bbbk^{1},o)\to Y}=\{h|\ dq_{1}|_{f}(h)=0=\cdots=dq_{c}|_{f}(h)\}\subset R^{\oplus m}_{(\Bbbk^{n},o)}.

Take the generic coordinates in
(\Bbbk^{m},o),
so that

ord_{x}(\partial_{y_{1}}q_{j}|_{f})=\cdots=ord_{x}(\partial_{y_{m}}q_{j}|_{f})
for each
j.
Then for any
h_{1}\in R_{(\Bbbk^{1},o)}
the system

dq_{1}|_{f}(h)=0=\cdots=dq_{c}|_{f}(h)
has a unique solution. Denote this solution by
(h_{2},\dots,h_{m}).

Thus the projection
h=(h_{1},\dots,h_{m})\to h_{1}
defines the isomorphism
T_{(\Bbbk^{1},o)\to Y}\hbox{$\xrightarrow[\,\smash{\raisebox{3.46594pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,]{}$}R_{(\Bbbk^{1},o)}.

It sends
Der_{(\Bbbk^{1},o)}f\subset R^{\oplus m}_{(\Bbbk^{1},o)}
to
(f^{\prime}_{1})\subset R_{(\Bbbk^{1},o)}.

By the genericity
ord(f^{\prime}_{1})=\cdots=ord(f^{\prime}_{m}).

Hence the statement.

\blacksquare

​​​​​
iv.​

(Osgood-type examples,
char\!=\!0
, cf. §
4.1
) Suppose a non-finite analytic map
X\!\ \lower 1.72218pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{\scriptscriptstyle{f}}}$}\ \!(\Bbbk^{m},o)
is dominant, i.e.
\Bbbk\{y\}\!\supset\!ker[\Bbbk\{y\}\!\to\!R_{X}]\!=\!0,
i.e. the set-theoretic image of (a small representative of)
f
does not lie inside any analytic hypersurface.
Suppose moreover
dim(X)\!<\!m.
Thus
rankDer_{X}\!<\!m.
Then the critical module
\mathscr{C}_{X,f}\!=\!{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$R^{\oplus m}_{X}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$Der_{X}(f)$}\right.}
is supported on the whole
X.
Hence
Crit_{X}f\!=\!X.

​​​​​
v.​

When
char\!=\!p,
the pathology
Crit_{X}f\!=\!X
can occur due to ramification. E.g. for a map
f:X\!\to\!(\Bbbk^{m},o)
of the form
f\!=\!({\tilde{f}}^{p}_{1},\dots,{\tilde{f}}^{p}_{m}),
one has
Crit_{X}f\!=\!X.
Note that
f
can be finite.

​​​​​
vi.​

Given a map
f:X\to(\Bbbk^{m},o),
extend it to a map
{\tilde{f}}:=(f_{1},\dots,f_{m},\dots,f_{m+c}):X\to(\Bbbk^{m+c},o).
Suppose
f_{m+1},\dots,f_{m+c}\in\Bbbk[f_{1},\dots,f_{m}],
then the projection
{\tilde{f}}(X)\to f(X)
is an isomorphism. And thus
Crit_{X}f=Crit_{X}{\tilde{f}}.

​​​​​
vii.​

A trivial case is the zero map,
X\ \lower 1.72218pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{\scriptscriptstyle{f}}}$}\ \{o\}.
Then
\mathscr{C}_{X,f}=0
and
Crit_{X,f}=X.

4.2.1.
Basic properties of
\mathscr{C}_{X,f}
and
Crit_{X}f

​​​​​
i.​

The ramification locus of a morphism
X\!\ \lower 1.72218pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{\scriptscriptstyle{f}}}$}\ \!Y
is defined via the Fitting ideal of relative differentials,
Fitt_{d}[\Omega^{sep}_{X/Y}].
Here ​
d
=the dimension of the generic fibre. In the ​
\Bbbk
-smooth case, ​
X\!=\!(\Bbbk^{n},o),Y\!=\!(\Bbbk^{m},o),
the ramification locus coincides with the critical locus,

Fitt_{0}(\mathscr{C}_{X,f})\!=\!Fitt_{d}[\Omega^{sep}_{X/Y}],
see Proposition 1.3 of
[
Käällströöm.13
]
. ​Otherwise they differ.

​​​​​
ii.​

The critical module
\mathscr{C}_{X,f}
and the critical locus
Crit_{X}f
are independent of the choice of coordinates in the target
(\Bbbk^{m}\!,o).
Moreover, they do not depend on the choice of embedding
Y\!\hookrightarrow\!(\Bbbk^{m},o).

Proof.
The minimal embedding of
Y
is unique up to isomorphism, while any non-minimal embedding is equivalent to the extension of a minimal embedding by zeros.
\blacksquare

Any automorphism
\Phi_{X}\!\in\!Aut_{X}
induces the equality:
Crit_{X}(f\!\circ\!\Phi_{X})\!=\!\Phi_{X}(Crit_{X}f)\!\subset\!X.

​​​​​
iii.​

(Localization at a point) Let
\Bbbk={\bar{\Bbbk}},
e.g.
\Bbbk=\mathbb{C}.
Take a map of analytic germs,
f:X\to Y,
and fix some representatives of these germs.
Suppose
f(X,x)=(f(X),f(x))
for each
x\in X,
see §
4.1
.i.
Then
\mathscr{C}_{X,f}
becomes a coherent sheaf on
X.
For each (closed) point
x\in X
we can take the analytic stalk
(\mathscr{C}_{X,f})_{x}.

Alternatively we can start from the analytic map-germ
(X,x)\!\ \lower 1.72218pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{\scriptscriptstyle{f_{x}}}}$}\ \!(Y,f(x)),
and get the critical module
\mathscr{C}_{(X,x),f_{x}}.

Claim:

\mathscr{C}_{(X,x),f_{x}}\cong(\mathscr{C}_{X,f})_{x}.

Proof.
The sheaves of modules
Der_{X}f,T_{X\to Y}
of definition
4.5

are functorial with respect to analytic localization.
\blacksquare

​​​​​
iv.​

(
Crit_{X}f
measures the non-triviality of the fibration) The map of analytic spaces
f:X\to Y
is called a locally trivial fibration at
x\in X
if
(X,x)\cong(Y,f(x))\times B
(isomorphism of analytic germs), with
f
acting as the projection onto the first factor.
Suppose
f(X,x)=(f(X),f(x))
for each
x\in X,
see §
4.1
.i.

Claim:
Then

Crit_{X}f\subseteq\{x\in X|\ (X,x)\to(Y,f(x))\ \text{is not a locally trivial fibration}\}.

Proof.
If
(X,x)\to(Y,f(x))
is a locally trivial fibration, then

(23)

T_{(X,x)\to(Y,f(x))}\cong R_{(X,x)}\otimes Der_{(Y,f(x))}\text{ and }Der_{(X,x)}(f)\cong R_{(X,x)}\otimes Der_{(Y,f(x))}(f).\vskip-2.84544pt

Thus
\mathscr{C}_{(X,x),f}=0.

\blacksquare

4.2.2.
A way to compute
Crit_{X}f
via coverings

Take a dominant morphism

f\!:\!X\!\to\!\!Y\!\subset\!\!(\Bbbk^{m},\!o),
and the projection
(\Bbbk^{m},o)\!\to\!(\Bbbk^{m^{\prime}},o),

(y_{1},\dots,y_{m})\!\to\!(y_{1},\dots,y_{m^{\prime}}).
Suppose it restricts to a (finite, surjective) covering
Y\ \lower 1.72218pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{\scriptscriptstyle{\pi}}}$}\ (\Bbbk^{m^{\prime}},o).

We get the truncated dominant map
\pi\circ f:X\to(\Bbbk^{m^{\prime}},o),
whose critical locus is easy to work with, by Example
4.6
.i.
Take the ramification locus,
Ram(\pi)\subset Y,
see §
4.2.1
.i., and its preimage
f^{-1}(Ram(\pi))\subset X.

Lemma 4.7
.

Suppose
f^{-1}(Ram(\pi))\subset X
contains no irreducible components of
Crit_{X}(f)
or of
Crit(\pi\circ f).
Then

Crit_{X}f=Crit_{X}(\pi\circ f)\subset X.

Proof.
We compare the critical modules
\mathscr{C}_{X,f}
and
\mathscr{C}_{X,\pi\circ f}.
The truncation
\pi
induces the diagram

(24)

\begin{matrix}Der_{X}f&\to&T_{X\to Y}&\to&\mathscr{C}_{X,f}&\to&0\\
\downarrow\pi_{1}&&\downarrow\pi_{2}&&\downarrow\pi_{3}\\
Der_{X}(\pi\circ f)&\to&T_{X\to(\Bbbk^{m^{\prime}},o)}&\to&\mathscr{C}_{X,\pi\circ f}&\to&0\end{matrix}\vskip-2.84544pt

Here the maps
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
define
\pi_{3},
a homomorphism of
R_{X}
-modules.

We claim:
\pi_{1},

\pi_{2}
are isomorphisms over
\mathcal{U}:=X\setminus f^{-1}(Ram(\pi)).

Indeed fix some generators
I_{Y}=R_{Y}\langle q_{1},\dots,q_{c}\rangle,
thus
q_{\bullet}(f)=0.

Any derivation
\xi\in Der_{X}
satisfies:
dq_{\bullet}|_{f}(\xi(f))=\xi(q_{\bullet}(f))=0.

Over
\mathcal{U}
the matrix
[\partial_{m^{\prime}+1}q_{\bullet}|_{f},\dots,\partial_{m}q_{\bullet}|_{f}]\in Mat_{c\times(m-m^{\prime})}(R_{X})
is of constant rank
m-m^{\prime}.

Therefore over
\mathcal{U}
we get:

•

Der_{\mathcal{U}}(f_{m^{\prime}+1}),\dots,Der_{\mathcal{U}}(f_{m})\subseteq Der_{\mathcal{U}}(f_{1},\dots,f_{m^{\prime}}).
Hence
\pi_{1}
is an isomorphism.

•

For any
(h_{1},\dots,h_{m^{\prime}})\in R^{\oplus m^{\prime}}_{\mathcal{U}}
there exist (and unique)
h_{m^{\prime}+1},\dots,h_{m}\in R_{\mathcal{U}}
satisfying:

dq_{i}|_{f}(h)=0
for any
i.
Hence
\pi_{2}
is an isomorphism, see (
22
).

Therefore
\pi_{3}
is an isomorphism over
\mathcal{U}.

Therefore
Crit_{X}f\cap\mathcal{U}=Crit_{X}(\pi\circ f)\cap\mathcal{U}\subset X.
Finally:

(25)

Crit_{X}f\ \lower 1.72218pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{=}\limits^{\scriptscriptstyle{!}}}$}\ \overline{Crit_{X}f\cap\mathcal{U}}=\overline{Crit_{X}(\pi\circ f)\cap\mathcal{U}}\ \lower 1.72218pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{=}\limits^{\scriptscriptstyle{!}}}$}\ Crit_{X}(\pi\circ f)\subset X.\vskip-2.84544pt

The equalities (!) hold by the initial assumption.

\blacksquare

4.2.3.
Restricting to the central fibre

For
(\Bbbk,{\mathfrak{m}}_{\Bbbk})
a local ring, and
X\ \lower 1.72218pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{\scriptscriptstyle{f}}}$}\ Y
a dominant morphism,
we take the central fibre
X_{o}\ \lower 1.72218pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{\scriptscriptstyle{f_{o}}}}$}\ Y_{o}
, see §
4.1
.iv.
One can either compute the critical locus of the central fibre,
Crit_{X_{o}}f_{o},
or take the central fibre of the critical locus,
Crit_{X}f\cap V({\mathfrak{m}}_{\Bbbk}).

Under certain assumptions they coincide. We prove a more general statement.

Let
\Bbbk
be a local Cohen-Macaulay algebra (over a field
\Bbbk_{o}
), with a system of parameters
t=(t_{1},\dots,t_{c}).

Suppose this sequence is also
R_{X}
-regular.
Denote
X_{o}\!=\!Spec({\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$R_{X}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$(t)$}\right.}),

Y_{o}\!=\!Spec({\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$R_{Y}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$(t)$}\right.}).
Thus
f(X_{o})\subseteq f(X)\cap Y_{o}=Y_{o},
the images in the sense of §
4.1
.i.
But the inequality can occur.

Example 4.8
.

Let
\Bbbk=\Bbbk_{o}[\![t]\!]
and
X=X_{o}\times(\Bbbk^{1}_{o},o),

Y=(\Bbbk^{m}_{o},o)\times(\Bbbk^{1}_{o},o).
Fix a dominant morphism
X_{o}\ \lower 1.72218pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{\scriptscriptstyle{g}}}$}\ (\Bbbk^{m}_{o},o),

and define
f:X\to Y
by
(x_{o},t)\to(t\cdot g(x_{o}),t).
Then
f
is dominant as well. But
f_{o}(X_{o})=\{o\}\times\{o\}\subsetneq f(X)\cap Y_{o}=Y_{o}.

Lemma 4.9
.

If
X_{o}\ \lower 1.72218pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{\scriptscriptstyle{f_{o}}}}$}\ Y_{o}
is dominant, then
\mathscr{C}_{X_{o},f_{o}}={\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$(t)$}\right.}\otimes\mathscr{C}_{X,f}
and

Crit_{X_{o}}f_{o}=Crit_{X}f\cap X_{o}.

Proof.
To obtain the critical locus
\mathscr{C}_{X_{o},f_{o}}
we should restrict
Der_{X}
to
Der_{X_{o}}
and
T_{X\to Y}

to
T_{X_{o}\to Y_{o}}.
In both cases we use an auxiliary fact, recalled in Step 1.

​​
Step 1.

Take a homomorphism of rings
R\to S,
a f.g. module
M\in mod
-
R,
and its Auslander transpose
M^{t}.

In Step 2 we use the (standard) exact sequence:

(26)

0\to Tor^{R}_{2}(M^{t},S)\to S\otimes Hom_{R}(M,R)\to Hom_{S}(S\otimes M,S)\to 0.\vskip-2.84544pt

For completeness we recall its proof. Fix a presentation of the module,
F_{1}\ \lower 1.72218pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{\scriptscriptstyle{A}}}$}\ F_{0}\to M\to 0.
Apply
Hom_{R}(-,R)
to get

0\to M^{*}\to F^{*}_{0}\ \lower 1.72218pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{\scriptscriptstyle{A^{t}}}}$}\ F^{*}_{1}\to M^{t}\to 0.
Split this sequence into two

(27)

0\to M^{*}\to F^{*}_{0}\ \lower 1.72218pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{\scriptscriptstyle{A^{t}}}}$}\ Im(A^{t})\to 0\quad\quad\quad\text{and}\quad\quad\quad 0\to Im(A^{t})\to F^{*}_{1}\to M^{t}\to 0.\vskip-2.84544pt

Apply
S\otimes
to the first sequence to get:
0\to Tor^{R}_{1}(Im(A^{t}),S)\to S\otimes M^{*}\to S\otimes F^{*}_{0}\ \lower 1.72218pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{\scriptscriptstyle{S\otimes A^{t}}}}$}\ S\otimes Im(A^{t})\to 0.
Here
ker[S\otimes A^{t}]\cong(S\otimes M)^{*}.

Apply
S\otimes
to the second sequence to get:
0\to Tor^{R}_{2}(M^{t},S)\to Tor^{R}_{1}(Im(A^{t}),S)\to 0.

Combine this to get:
0\to Tor^{R}_{2}(M^{t},S)\to S\otimes M^{*}\to(S\otimes M)^{*}\to 0,
as claimed.

​​
Step 2.

It is enough to prove the claim for the restriction
R\to{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$R$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$(\tau)$}\right.},
where
\tau\in{\mathfrak{m}}_{\Bbbk}\subset\Bbbk
is any regular element.
Then the claim for the restriction
R\to{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$R$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$(t)$}\right.},
with the system of parameters
t=(t_{1},\dots,t_{c}),
follows by induction.

Thus we apply (
26
) for
S\!:=R_{X_{o}}\!:={\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$R$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$(\tau)$}\right.}.
In this case
0\!\to\!R_{X}\!\ \lower 1.72218pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{\scriptscriptstyle{\tau}}}$}\ \!R_{X}\!\to\!R_{X_{o}}\!\to\!0
is exact.
Therefore
Tor^{R}_{2}(-,R_{X_{o}})\!=\!0.

Hence
R_{X_{o}}\!\otimes Hom_{R}(M,R)\hbox{$\xrightarrow[\,\smash{\raisebox{3.46594pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,]{}$}Hom_{R_{X_{o}}}(R_{X_{o}}\otimes M,R_{X_{o}}).

•

For
M=\Omega^{sep}_{X}
one gets:
R_{X_{o}}\otimes Der_{X}\hbox{$\xrightarrow[\,\smash{\raisebox{3.46594pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,]{}$}Der_{X_{o}}.

•

For

one gets:

There exists

f
only, but not

Proof.
In the proof we use the Artin-Rees lemma twice, and then apply the integrability assumption. Therefore we fix
N\gg 1,
and give the precise bound at the end of Step 1.

​​
Step 1.

Fix some generators of the defining ideal of the image,
I_{Y}=R_{Y}\langle\{q_{\bullet}\}\rangle.
(Thus
q_{\bullet}(f)=0.
) Then

(28)

0=q_{i}({\tilde{f}})=q_{i}(f+{\tilde{f}}-f)=dq_{i}|_{f}({\tilde{f}}-f)+(\dots),\quad\text{ where }\quad(\dots)\in({\tilde{f}}-f)^{2}.\vskip-2.84544pt

dI_{Y}|_{f}=f^{-1}(dI_{Y})\subset f^{-1}(R_{Y}\otimes\Omega^{sep}_{(\Bbbk^{m},o)})\subseteq R^{\oplus m}_{X}

(cf. Example
4.6
.ii.)

28

Moreover, take the matrix

and the corresponding

[dI_{Y}|_{f}]:R^{\oplus m}_{X}\to R^{\oplus c}_{X}.
The image of this map is a (finitely generated) submodule of

Therefore:

(29)

[dI_{Y}|_{f}]({\tilde{f}}-f)\in Fitt_{0}(\mathscr{C})^{2N}\cdot R^{\oplus c}_{X}\ \cap\ [dI_{Y}|_{f}](R^{\oplus m}_{X})\ \lower 1.72218pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\subseteq}\limits^{\scriptscriptstyle{A.R.}}}$}\ Fitt_{0}(\mathscr{C})^{2N-d^{\prime}}\cdot[dI_{Y}|_{f}](R^{\oplus m}_{X}).\vskip-2.84544pt

The inclusion holds by the Artin-Rees lemma, and the integer

depends on
f

{\tilde{f}}
or
N.
(In particular,
d^{\prime}\ll N.
)

Thus
[dI_{Y}|_{f}]({\tilde{f}}-f)=[dI_{Y}|_{f}](h)\subset R^{\oplus c}_{X}

(Hence

And therefore

Altogether:

(30)

A remark: the integers

are preserved under

We assume

such that:

​​
Step 2.

By our assumption
\xi
can be approximately integrated to an automorphism
\Phi_{X}\in Aut_{X}
satisfying:

with

Then

In the analytic/Nash cases we apply the Artin approximation, Example

.

​​​​

[

​​​​

Suppose

. Then

​​​​

4.5.
Higher critical loci

Take a dominant morphism
f:X\to Y,
its critical locus
Crit_{1}:=Crit_{X}f\subseteq X,
and the discriminant
\Delta_{1}:=f(Crit_{1})\subset Y.

(With reduced scheme structures.)
One could restrict
f
to this critical locus,
Crit_{1}\ \lower 1.72218pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{\scriptscriptstyle{f_{|}}}}$}\ \Delta_{1},
and study the critical locus of the restriction,
Crit_{Crit_{1}}(f_{|}).
But this ignores the source and looses information about

E.g., for

not all derivations in
Der_{Crit}
come from those of

For our approximation results we need to keep

Hence the higher critical loci are defined (iteratively) via logarithmic drivations.
Put
\mathscr{C}_{0}:=R_{X},

Crit_{0}:=X,

\Delta_{0}:=Y.
Suppose we have defined the
triples
(\mathscr{C}_{0},Crit_{0},\Delta_{0}),\dots,(\mathscr{C}_{j},Crit_{j},\Delta_{j}).
Note that all the restrictions
Crit_{i}\ \lower 1.72218pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{\scriptscriptstyle{f_{|}}}}$}\ \Delta_{i}
are dominant.

Definition 4.15
.

​1.​

The
(j+1)
-critical module is defined by the sequence of
R_{X}
-modules

(32)

R_{Crit_{j}}\otimes Der_{Crit_{0}\dots Crit_{j}}f\to T_{Crit_{j}\to\Delta_{j}}\to\mathscr{C}_{j+1}\to 0,\vskip-2.84544pt

where
Der_{Crit_{0}\dots Crit_{j}}=\cap^{j}_{i=0}Der_{X}Log(Fitt_{0}(\mathscr{C}_{i}))
and
T_{Crit_{j}\to\Delta_{j}}=Hom_{Crit_{j}}(f^{*}\Omega^{sep}_{\Delta_{j}},R_{Crit_{j}}).

​2.​

The
(j+1)
-critical locus is the support of
\mathscr{C}_{j+1}.

Namely,
Crit_{j+1}=Supp(\mathscr{C}_{j+1})\subseteq X
is defined by the Fitting ideal
Fitt_{0}(\mathscr{C}_{j+1})\subset R_{X}.

​3.​

The
(j+1)
-discriminant is the image,
\Delta_{j+1}=f(Crit_{j+1})\subseteq Y,
see
4.1
.i.

We get the higher critical loci and higher discriminants (the formal/analytic/Nash germs):

(33)

​​​​

Suppose

​​​​
v.​

Take a map

​​​​
vi.​

(
\Bbbk

4.6
.vii.

.

The property
\mathscr{L}\mathscr{R}
.AP (with
\Phi_{X},\!\Phi_{Y}
isomorphisms) holds for
f.

Proof.
Suppose
\hat{\Phi}_{Y}\circ{\tilde{f}}=f\circ\hat{\Phi}_{X},
for some formal isomorphisms
\hat{\Phi}_{X}:{\hat{{\tilde{X}}}}\to{\hat{X}},

\hat{\Phi}_{Y}:{\hat{{\tilde{Y}}}}\to{\hat{Y}}.

​​
Step 1.

The formal isomorphism
\hat{\Phi}_{X}\!:{\hat{{\tilde{X}}}}\!\to\!{\hat{X}}
sends the chain of (formal germs of) higher criti-

cal loci,

{\tilde{X}}\!=\!Crit_{0}({\tilde{f}})\!\supseteq\!Crit_{1}({\tilde{f}})\!\supseteq\cdots,
to the chain

X\!=\!Crit_{0}(f)\!\supseteq\!Crit_{1}(f)\!\supseteq\!\cdots,
see §
4.2.1
.
As before, we take the loci
Crit_{\bullet},

\Delta_{\bullet}
with reduced scheme structure.

Therefore ​
\hat{\Phi}_{X}
​is a formal solution to ​the conditions
\Phi_{X}(\!\sqrt{\!Fitt_{0}(\mathscr{C}_{j,f})})\!=\!\!\sqrt{\!Fitt_{0}(\mathscr{C}_{j,{\tilde{f}}})}\!\subset\!R_{\tilde{X}},
for
j\!\geq\!0.

These are implicit-function equations, see Remark
2.1
.
Applying the ordinary Artin approximation, Example
2.2
, we identify
X\!=\!{\tilde{X}}
and assume:

Crit_{j}(f)\!=\!Crit_{j}({\tilde{f}})\!\subseteq\!X
for each
j\!\geq\!1.

Below we denote these subgerms by
Crit_{\bullet}\!\subseteq\!X.

Similarly, the formal isomorphism
\hat{\Phi}_{Y}:{\hat{{\tilde{Y}}}}\to{\hat{Y}}
sends the chain of (formal germs of) higher discriminants,

{\tilde{Y}}=\Delta_{0}({\tilde{f}})\supseteq\Delta_{1}({\tilde{f}})\supseteq\cdots,
to the chain

Y=\Delta_{0}(f)\supseteq\Delta_{1}(f)\supseteq\cdots.

By the same argument as before we identify
Y={\tilde{Y}}
and assume:

\Delta_{j}(f)=\Delta_{j}({\tilde{f}})\subseteq Y
for each
j\geq 1.

Below we denote these subgerms by
\Delta_{\bullet}\subseteq Y.

All the further automorphisms of
X,Y
below will preserve these subgerms
Crit_{\bullet},\Delta_{\bullet}
.

​​
Step 2.

The restrictions
f_{|},{\tilde{f}}_{|}:Crit_{r}\to\Delta_{r}
are formally
\mathscr{L}\mathscr{R}
-equivalent and finite. Thus
f_{|},{\tilde{f}}_{|}
are equivalent by an element of
Aut_{Crit_{r}}\times Aut_{\Delta_{r}}
, by §
3.2
.
More precisely, the formal equivalence is approximated (to any order) by analytic equivalences.

These (analytic) automorphisms of
Crit_{r},\Delta_{r}
do not necessarily extend to automorphisms of the ambient spaces
X,Y.

Therefore, we replace the germs
Crit_{r},\Delta_{r}
by their
N
-th infinitesimal neighborhoods,
Spec({\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$R_{X}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$I_{Crit_{r}}^{N}$}\right.})
and

Spec({\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$R_{Y}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$I_{\Delta_{r}}^{N}$}\right.}),
for
N\gg 1.
Here
N\gg 1,
the precise condition is given in Step 3.
The finite maps
Spec({\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$R_{X}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$I_{Crit_{r}}^{N}$}\right.})\ \lower 1.72218pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{\scriptscriptstyle{f_{|},{\tilde{f}}_{|}}}}$}\ Spec({\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$R_{Y}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$I_{\Delta_{r}}^{N}$}\right.})
are still formally left-right equivalent.
Invoke §
3.2
to approximate this formal equivalence by analytic equivalence.
The so obtained automorphisms of
Spec({\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$R_{X}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$I_{Crit_{r}}^{N}$}\right.})
and
Spec({\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$R_{Y}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$I_{\Delta_{r}}^{N}$}\right.})
admit approximate lifting to all the groups
Aut_{Crit_{r-1}}\times Aut_{\Delta_{r-1}},
…,
Aut_{X}\times Aut_{Y}.

•

In
char>0
this holds by the assumption (
36
).

•

In zero characteristic one invokes Example
4.4
to get an automorphism
\Phi_{X}\circlearrowright R_{X}

that preserves the chain
I_{Crit_{\bullet}},
and similarly
\Phi_{Y}\circlearrowright R_{Y}
that preserves the chain
I_{\Delta_{\bullet}}.

Altogether, we approximate
\hat{\Phi}_{X},\hat{\Phi}_{Y}
by analytic automorphisms
\Phi_{X},\Phi_{Y}
that preserve all the subgerms
Crit_{\bullet},\Delta_{\bullet},
and satisfy:
\Phi_{Y}\circ{\tilde{f}}_{|}\circ\Phi_{X}^{-1}=f_{|}.

Replace
{\tilde{f}}
by
\Phi_{Y}\circ{\tilde{f}}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{X}.

Thus below we assume
f_{|}={\tilde{f}}_{|}:Crit_{r}\to\Delta_{r},
i.e.
f-{\tilde{f}}\in I_{Crit_{r}}^{N^{\prime}}\cdot R^{\oplus m}_{X},
where
N^{\prime}\gg 1
(because
N\gg 1
).

For
r\!=\!0,
with
Crit_{0}\!=\!X,
i.e.
f
is finite, this ends the proof. Below we assume
r\!\geq\!1.

​​
Step 3.

If
r=1,
i.e.
f
is of finite singularity type, then we invoke the finite
\mathscr{R}
-determinacy by the critical locus, Lemma
4.11
.
We get:
{\tilde{f}}=f\circ\Phi_{X},
for an analytic automorphism
\Phi_{X}\in Aut_{X}
satisfying:
\Phi(x)-x\in I_{Crit_{r}}^{N^{\prime\prime}}
for some
N^{\prime\prime}\gg 1.

For
r\geq 2
one applies the finite
\mathscr{R}
-determinacy by higher critical loci, Lemma
4.16
, to get the same conclusion.

Finally, a lower bound on
N
comes via the condition on
N^{\prime},
which is:
N^{\prime}\gg 1
such that lemmas
4.11
and
4.16
apply, and the assumptions (
36
) hold.

\blacksquare

Remark 5.2
.

Unlike theorems
3.4
,
3.5
, here we assume that
\Phi_{X},\Phi_{Y}
are isomorphisms. This is used in the proof (Step 1). It is not clear, whether
\mathscr{L}\mathscr{R}
.AP holds when
\Phi_{X},\Phi_{Y}
are not invertible.

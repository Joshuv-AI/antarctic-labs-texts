Revisiting K 1 ​ ( 1270 ) − K 1 ​ ( 1400 ) K_{1}(1270)-K_{1}(1400) mixing in QCD sum rules
Yu-Ji Shi, Jun Zeng, Zhi-Fu Deng

Abstract
We investigate the K 1 ​ ( 1270 ) − K 1 ​ ( 1400 ) K_{1}(1270)-K_{1}(1400) mixing caused by the flavor S ​ U ​ ( 3 ) SU(3) symmetry breaking. The mixing angle is expressed by a K 1 ​ A → K 1 ​ B K_{1A}\to K_{1B} matrix element induced by the operators that breaks flavor S ​ U ​ ( 3 ) SU(3) symmetry. The QCD contribution to this matrix element is assumed to be dominated and calculated by QCD sum rules. A three-point correlation function is defined and handled both at the hadron and quark-gluon levels. The quark-gluon level calculation is based on operator product expansion up to dimension-5 condensates. A detailed numerical analysis is performed to determine the Borel parameters, and the obtained mixing angle is θ K 1 = 22 ∘ ± 7 ∘ \theta_{K_{1}}=22^{\circ}\pm 7^{\circ} or θ K 1 = 68 ∘ ± 7 ∘ \theta_{K_{1}}=68^{\circ}\pm 7^{\circ} .



I
Introduction

The flavor
SU(3)
symmetry plays an important role in the conventional quark model, which classifies hadrons into various of irreducible representations. Although the assumption of perfect flavor
SU(3)
symmetry succeeds in most of phenomenological analysis on hadron decays and spectrums
Zeppenfeld:1980ex
;
Savage:1989ub
;
Deshpande:1994ii
;
Chau:1986du
;
Chau:1990ay
;
Lu:2016ogy
;
Wang:2017azm
;
He:2018php
, its breaking effect still cannot be neglected. One of the physical effect due to the flavor
SU(3)
breaking is the hadron mixing.

According to the quark model, the two axial-vector nonets with
J^{P}=1^{+}
are expected as the orbital excitation of the
\bar{q}q
system. There are two types of P-wave axial-vector mesons: the
{}^{3}P_{1}
and
{}^{1}P_{1}
with the notation
{}^{2S+1}L_{J}
. Generally, in the flavor
SU(3)
limit these two nonets cannot be mixed since they have distinct
C
parities, explicitly
J^{PC}=1^{++},\ 1^{+-}
for
{}^{3}P_{1},\ ^{1}P_{1}
respectively. However, because of the mass difference between the strange and light quarks, the kaon nonets
K_{1A}(^{3}P_{1})
and
K_{1B}(^{1}P_{1})
are distinguished from the mass eigenstates
K_{1}(1270)
and
K_{1}(1400)
. As a result, there emerges a mixing between these two sets of axial-vector kaons:

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}|K_{1}(1270)\rangle\\
|K_{1}(1400)\rangle\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta_{K_{1}}&\sin\theta_{K_{1}}\\
-\sin\theta_{K_{1}}&\cos\theta_{K_{1}}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}|K_{1B}\rangle\\
|K_{1A}\rangle\end{pmatrix},

(1)

where
\theta_{K_{1}}
is the mixing angle.

Up to now, there are quite a number of studies on
\theta_{K_{1}}
in the literature, with most of them based on phenomenological analysis.
In Ref.
Suzuki:1993yc
, with the use of early experimental information on masses and the partial rates of
K_{1}(1270)
and
K_{1}(1400)
, the authors obtained
\theta_{K_{1}}=33^{\circ}
or
57^{\circ}
; The Refs.
Cheng:2003bn
;
Li:2009tx
phenomenologically analyzed the
\tau\to K_{1}(1270)\nu_{\tau}
and
\tau\to K_{1}(1400)\nu_{\tau}
decays and obtained
\theta_{K_{1}}=37^{\circ}
or
58^{\circ}
; The Ref.
Close:1997nm
studied the correlation of the
f_{1}(1285)-f_{1}(1420)
mixing angle
\theta_{{}^{3}P_{1}}
with
\theta_{K_{1}}
, and obtained
\theta_{K_{1}}=31.7^{\circ}
or
56.3^{\circ}
; In Ref.
Cheng:2013cwa
, the authors used the correspondence between
\theta_{K_{1}}
and the
f_{1}(1285)-f_{1}(1420)
,
h_{1}(1170)-h_{1}(1380)
mixing angles to rule out unreasonable
\theta_{K_{1}}
values, and announced a reasonable range as
28^{\circ}<\theta_{K_{1}}<30^{\circ}
. Besides the phenomenological analysis, in Ref.
Burakovsky:1997dd
the authors obtained
34^{\circ}<\theta_{K_{1}}<55^{\circ}
by non-relativistic constituent quark model with the inputs of the mass difference between the
a_{1}(1260)
and
b_{1}(1235)
mesons, as well as the ratio of the constituent quark masses. The Ref.
Dag:2012zz
related
\theta_{K_{1}}
with a two-point correlation function. By calculating this correlation function in QCD sum rules (QCDSR) the authors obtained
\theta_{K_{1}}=39^{\circ}\pm 4^{\circ}
.

It can be found that the phenomenological predictions on
\theta_{K_{1}}
mentioned above are not unified. In this work, we desire to give a theoretical and model independent calculation on
\theta_{K_{1}}
, with the use of QCDSR. It should be mentioned that the QCDSR calculation in Ref.
Dag:2012zz
has a defect. The authors missed the pseudoscalar kaon contribution when operating the
K_{1A}
current on the correlation function. In this work, we will calculate
\theta_{K_{1}}
in a different framework of QCDSR from that in Ref.
Dag:2012zz

\theta_{K_{1}}
will be expressed by a
K_{1A}\to K_{1B}

SU(3)

\Xi_{c}-\Xi_{c}^{\prime}
mixing

;
Deng:2023qaf

K_{1A}\to K_{1B}
matrix element will be calculated in QCDSR by a three-point correlation function. Some introductions on QCDSR and its applications can be found in Refs.
Onishchenko:2000wf
;
Onishchenko:2000yp
;
Kiselev:2001fw
;
Zhang:2008rt
;
Wang:2010hs
;
Wang:2010vn
;

;
Hu:2017dzi
;
Hu:2017dzi
;

.

II
K_{1}(1270)-K_{1}(1400)
mixing

There are two sources of the flavor
SU(3)
breaking. The first one comes from the mass difference between
s
and

u,d
(nearly massless) quarks, which only provides QCD contribution to
K_{1}(1270)-K_{1}(1400)
mixing. Another one comes from the electric charge difference among the
u,d,s
quarks, which involves the QED effect. In this work, we will focus on the QCD contribution since the QED effect is expected to be tiny as shown by our previous work on the
\Xi_{c}-\Xi_{c}^{\prime}
mixing
Liu:2023feb
;
Deng:2023qaf
.
The full QCD Lagrangian of the quark sector contains both the terms conserving and breaking the flavor
SU(3)
symmetry:
\mathcal{L}_{\rm QCD}=\mathcal{L}_{0}+\Delta\mathcal{L}
, where
\mathcal{L}_{0}
reads as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{q}\bar{q}(i\not{D}-m_{u})q,

(2)

with
D
being the QCD covariant derivative,
q=u,d,s
, and
m_{u}=m_{d}=0
is approximately assumed. The
SU(3)
symmetry breaking term
\Delta\mathcal{L}
, which arises from the quark mass difference reads as

\displaystyle\Delta\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{s}(m_{u}-m_{s})s.

(3)

Accordingly, the Hamiltonian is decomposed as
H=H_{0}+\Delta H
,
with

\displaystyle\Delta H=\int d^{3}x\Delta\mathcal{H}(x)=-\int d^{3}x\Delta\mathcal{L}(x).

(4)

The lowest axial vector kaons
K_{1}(1270)
and
K_{1}(1400)
are the mass eigenstates of the full Hamiltonian
H
:

\displaystyle H|K_{1}(1270)\rangle=m_{1270}|K_{1}(1270)\rangle,

\displaystyle H|K_{1}(1400)\rangle=m_{1400}|K_{1}(1270)\rangle.

(5)

On the other hand, in the
SU(3)
symmetry limit, the lowest axial vector kaons are classified into
K_{1B}(^{1}P_{1})
and
K_{1A}(^{3}P_{1})
states, which are eigenstates of the
SU(3)
conserved Hamiltonian
H_{0}
:

\displaystyle H_{0}|K_{1B}\rangle=m_{1B}|K_{1B}\rangle,

\displaystyle H_{0}|K_{1A}\rangle=m_{1A}|K_{1A}\rangle\rangle.

(6)

The mixing between the physical doublet
|K_{P}\rangle=(|K_{1}(1270)\rangle,|K_{1}(1400)\rangle)^{T}
and the
SU(3)
doublet
|K_{F}\rangle=(|K_{1B}\rangle,|K_{1A}\rangle)^{T}
is described by a unitary transforming matrix
U
with a mixing angle
\theta_{K_{1}}
:

\displaystyle|K_{P}\rangle=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta_{K_{1}}&\sin\theta_{K_{1}}\\
-\sin\theta_{K_{1}}&\cos\theta_{K_{1}}\end{pmatrix}|K_{F}\rangle=U|K_{F}\rangle.

(7)

Here we consider the matrix element for the
SU(3)
doublet
|K_{F}\rangle
:

\langle K_{F}(p^{\prime})|H|K_{F}(p)\rangle
. Both the initial and final states are set to be static
{\vec{p}}={\vec{p}}^{\prime}=0
and on-shell
p_{1B}^{0}=m_{1B},\ p_{1A}^{0}=m_{1A}
. With the use of the unitary transformation
U
defined in Eq. (
7
), and the physical masses defined in Eq. (
5
), we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\langle K_{1B}(\lambda^{\prime})|H|K_{1B}(\lambda)\rangle&\langle K_{1B}(\lambda^{\prime})|H|K_{1A}(\lambda)\rangle\\
\langle K_{1A}(\lambda^{\prime})|H|K_{1B}(\lambda)\rangle&\langle K_{1A}(\lambda^{\prime})|H|K_{1A}(\lambda)\rangle\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ 2(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{(3)}(\vec{0})\delta_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}m_{1270}^{2}\ c_{k}^{2}+m_{1400}^{2}\ s_{k}^{2}&(m_{1270}^{2}-m_{1400}^{2})\ c_{k}s_{k}\\
(m_{1270}^{2}-m_{1400}^{2})\ c_{k}s_{k}&m_{1270}^{2}\ s_{k}^{2}+m_{1400}^{2}\ c_{k}^{2}\end{pmatrix},

where
s_{k}=\sin\theta_{K_{1}},c_{k}=\cos\theta_{K_{1}}
for simplicity the momentum dependence of the
K_{1B,1A}
states are not shown in the matrix element.

It can be found that the upper-right off-diagonal component in the Eq. (
II
) leads to the equation

\displaystyle\langle K_{1B}(\lambda^{\prime})|H|K_{1A}(\lambda)\rangle

(9)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{(3)}(\vec{0})\delta_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}(m_{1270}^{2}-m_{1400}^{2})\sin 2\theta_{K_{1}}.

Therefore, the mixing angle
\theta_{K_{1}}
can be extracted as soon as one calculate the matrix element on the left hand side above, which can be further expressed as

\displaystyle\langle K_{1B}(\lambda^{\prime})|H|K_{1A}(\lambda)\rangle

(10)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{(3)}({\vec{0}})\langle K_{1A}(\lambda^{\prime})|\Delta\mathcal{H}(0)|K_{1B}(\lambda)\rangle,

by integrating out the coordinate. Equating the Eq. (
9
) and Eq. (
10
), and setting
\lambda=\lambda^{\prime}
we have

\displaystyle\sin 2\theta_{K_{1}}=\frac{m_{s}-m_{u}}{m_{1270}^{2}-m_{1400}^{2}}\langle K_{1B}|\bar{s}s(0)|K_{1A}\rangle.

(11)

Generally, the matrix element
\langle K_{1A}(p_{2})|\Delta\mathcal{H}(0)|K_{1B}(p_{1})\rangle
with nonzero initial and final momentums can be parameterized as

\displaystyle\langle K_{1B}(p_{2})|\bar{s}s(0)|K_{1A}(p_{1})\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \epsilon^{*}_{\mu}(p_{2})\bigg[F_{1}g^{\mu\nu}+\frac{F_{2}}{M^{2}}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}p_{1\alpha}p_{2\beta}+\frac{F_{3}}{M^{2}}p_{1}^{\mu}p_{2}^{\nu}\bigg]\epsilon_{\nu}(p_{1}),

(12)

where
F_{1,2,3}
are three transition functions of
q^{2}=(p_{1}-p_{2})^{2}
, and
M^{2}
can be the mass of
K_{1A}
or
K_{1B}
. Note that the matrix element we actually need in Eq. (
11
) requires
p_{1}=p_{2}
. Thus the
F_{2},F_{3}
terms in Eq. (
12
) vanishes and only
F_{1}(q^{2}=0)
is relevant. Accordingly the mixing angle can be obtained as

\displaystyle\sin 2\theta_{K_{1}}=\frac{m_{s}-m_{u}}{m_{1400}^{2}-m_{1270}^{2}}F_{1}(0).

(13)

III
Hadron Level calculation

In this and the next section, we will introduce a QCDSR calculation for the matrix element in Eq. (
12
). We firstly define a three-point correlation function:

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mu\nu\rho}(p_{1},p_{2})=

\displaystyle\ i^{2}\int d^{4}xd^{4}y\ e^{ip_{2}\cdot x}e^{-ip_{1}\cdot y}

\displaystyle\times\langle 0|T\{J_{\mu\nu}^{1B}(x)\bar{s}s(0)J_{\rho}^{1A\dagger}(y)\}|0\rangle,

(14)

where
J_{\mu\nu}^{1A}
and
J_{\mu\nu}^{1B}
are the currents of
K_{1A}(^{3}P_{1})
and
K_{1B}(^{1}P_{1})
, respectively:

\displaystyle J_{\mu\nu}^{1B}=\bar{q}\sigma_{\mu\nu}s,~~J_{\rho}^{1A}=\bar{q}\gamma_{\rho}\gamma_{5}s.

(15)

The correlation function defined in Eq. (
14
) should be calculated both at the hadron level and the quark-gluon level. At the hadron level, inserting the complete sets with the same quantum number of
K_{1B}
and
K_{1A}
into the correlation function, and using the following definition of kaon decay constants:

\displaystyle\langle 0|J_{\mu\nu}^{1B}(0)|K_{1B}(p,\lambda)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle if_{K_{1B}}^{\perp}\epsilon_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}\epsilon^{\alpha}(p,\lambda)p^{\beta},

\displaystyle\langle 0|J_{\rho}^{1A}(0)|K_{1A}(p,\lambda)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-if_{K_{1A}}m_{K_{1}A}\epsilon_{\rho}(p,\lambda),

\displaystyle\langle 0|J_{\rho}^{1A}(0)|K(p)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle if_{K}p_{\rho},

(16)

one can express the correlation function as

\displaystyle\Pi^{H}_{\mu\nu\rho}(p_{1},p_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-m_{A}f_{A}f_{B}^{\perp}\epsilon_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}p_{2}^{\beta}\bigg(-g^{\alpha\kappa}+\frac{p_{2}^{\alpha}p_{2}^{\kappa}}{m_{B}^{2}}\bigg)\frac{1}{p_{2}^{2}-m_{1B}^{2}}\bigg[F_{1}g_{\kappa\tau}+\frac{F_{2}}{M^{2}}\epsilon_{\kappa\tau\rho\sigma}p_{1}^{\rho}p_{2}^{\sigma}+\frac{F_{3}}{M^{2}}p_{1\kappa}p_{2\tau}\bigg]\bigg(-g^{\tau}_{\rho}+\frac{p_{1}^{\tau}p_{1\rho}}{m_{1A}^{2}}\bigg)\frac{1}{p_{1}^{2}-m_{1A}^{2}}

\displaystyle+f_{k}f_{1B}^{\perp}\epsilon_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}p_{2}^{\beta}p_{1\rho}\frac{1}{p_{2}^{2}-m_{1B}^{2}}\bigg(-g^{\alpha\kappa}+\frac{p_{2}^{\alpha}p_{2}^{\kappa}}{m_{1B}^{2}}\bigg)p_{1\kappa}\frac{G(q^{2})}{M}\frac{1}{p_{1}^{2}-m_{K}^{2}}

\displaystyle+\int_{s_{1}^{\rm th}}^{\infty}ds_{1}\int_{s_{2}^{\rm th}}^{\infty}ds_{2}\frac{\rho^{\rm conti}_{\mu\nu\rho}(s_{1},s_{2},q^{2})}{(s_{1}-p_{1}^{2})(s_{2}-p_{2}^{2})}+\int_{s_{1}^{\rm th}}^{\infty}ds_{1}\frac{\rho^{\rm conti}_{1,\mu\nu\rho}(s_{1},p_{2},q^{2})}{s_{1}-p_{1}^{2}}+\int_{s_{2}^{\rm th}}^{\infty}ds_{2}\frac{\rho^{\rm conti}_{2,\mu\nu\rho}(p_{1},s_{2},q^{2})}{s_{2}-p_{2}^{2}}.

(17)

The last three terms above denote the contribution from the excited and continuous spectrum, which begin at the thresholds
s_{1}^{\rm th}
and
s_{2}^{\rm th}
. It should be noted that the axial vector current
J_{\rho}^{1A}
can create both an axial vector and a pseudoscalar kaon from the vaccum. Therefore to obtain Eq. (
17
) both the
K_{1A}
and
K
have been inserted between
\bar{s}s(0)
and
J_{\rho}^{1A}(y)
, and we have used the parameterization for the
K\to K_{1B}
matrix element:

\displaystyle\langle K_{1B}(p_{2})|\bar{s}s(0)|K(p_{1})\rangle=\epsilon^{*}_{\mu}(p_{2})p_{1}^{\mu}\frac{G(q^{2})}{M},

(18)

where
G(q^{2})
is the corresponding form factor.

Now the hadron level correlation function in Eq. (
17
) depends on four form factors
F_{1},F_{2},F_{3},G
. However, only
F_{1}
is relevant to the mixing angle as shown in Eq. (
13
). To remove the irrelevant form factors we operate the following projection on the correlation function

\displaystyle\epsilon^{\mu\rho\alpha\beta}p_{1}^{\nu}\Pi_{\mu\nu\rho}(p_{1},p_{2})={\tilde{\Pi}}(p_{1},p_{2})(p_{1}^{\beta}p_{2}^{\alpha}-p_{1}^{\alpha}p_{2}^{\beta}).

(19)

{\tilde{\Pi}}(p_{1},p_{2})
is a newly defined scalar correlation function, which at hadron level only depends on
F_{1}
:

\displaystyle{\tilde{\Pi}}^{H}(p_{1},p_{2})=\frac{2m_{1A}f_{1A}f_{1B}^{\perp}F_{1}(q^{2})}{(p_{1}^{2}-m_{1A}^{2})(p_{2}^{2}-m_{1B}^{2})}+\cdots,

(20)

where the ellipse denotes the last three terms in Eq. (
17
) with the projection defined in Eq. (
19
) being operated. In principle, the
{\tilde{\Pi}}(p_{1},p_{2})
calculated at the hadron level and the quark-gluon level should be equivalent:

\displaystyle{\tilde{\Pi}}^{H}(p_{1},p_{2},q^{2})={\tilde{\Pi}}^{\rm QCD}(p_{1},p_{2},q^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}ds_{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}ds_{2}\frac{{\rm Im}^{2}{\tilde{\Pi}}^{QCD}(s_{1},s_{2},q^{2})}{(s_{1}-p_{1}^{2})(s_{2}-p_{2}^{2})}.

(21)

In the second equation above we have expressed the
{\tilde{\Pi}}^{\rm QCD}
as its dispersive integration form, with
s_{1}^{\rm min}
and
s_{2}^{\rm min}
being the quark level thresholds. According to the quark-hadron duality, the continuous spectrum contribution at hadron level is equivalent to that at QCD level. In other words the ellipse term in Eq. (
20
) is equal to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}

\displaystyle\left[\int_{s_{1}^{\rm th}}^{\infty}ds_{1}\int_{s_{2}^{\rm th}}^{\infty}ds_{2}+\int_{s_{1}^{\rm th}}^{\infty}ds_{1}\int_{0}^{s_{2}^{\rm th}}ds_{2}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\int_{0}^{s_{1}^{\rm th}}ds_{1}\int_{s_{2}^{\rm th}}^{\infty}ds_{2}\right]\frac{{\rm Im}^{2}{\tilde{\Pi}}^{\rm QCD}(s_{1},s_{2},q^{2})}{(s_{1}-p_{1}^{2})(s_{2}-p_{2}^{2})}.

(22)

Thus the continuous spectrum contribution can be canceled at both the hadron and QCD levels. After Borel transformation we arrive at the sum rules equation:

\displaystyle{\cal B}_{T_{1},T_{2}}\{{\tilde{\Pi}}^{H}\}(q^{2})

\displaystyle={\cal B}_{T_{1},T_{2}}\{{\tilde{\Pi}}^{\rm QCD}\}(q^{2}),

\displaystyle 2m_{1A}f_{1A}f_{1B}^{\perp}\ e^{-\frac{m_{1A}^{2}}{T_{1}^{2}}}e^{-\frac{m_{1B}^{2}}{T_{2}^{2}}}F_{1}(q^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{s_{1}^{\rm th}}ds_{1}\int_{0}^{s_{2}^{\rm th}}ds_{2}\ e^{-\frac{s_{1}}{T_{1}^{2}}}e^{-\frac{s_{2}}{T_{2}^{2}}}{\rm Im}^{2}{\tilde{\Pi}}^{\rm QCD}(s_{1},s_{2},q^{2}),

(23)

where
T_{1},T_{2}
are the two Borel parameters corresponding to
p_{1}^{2},p_{2}^{2}
. Now it is clear that
F_{1}
can be obtained through Eq. (
23
) if the imaginary part of
{\tilde{\Pi}}^{\rm QCD}
is calculated.

IV
Quark-gluon Level calculation

IV.1
Perturbative diagram

Figure 1:
The perturbative diagram contribution to the correlation function, where the lower two vertexes denote the kaon currents defined in Eq. (

) (left). A general triangle diagram corresponding to a three-point correlation function (right).

In this section, we present the QCD level calculation for
{\tilde{\Pi}}^{\rm QCD}
and extract its imaginary part. In the deep Euclidean region
p_{1}^{2},p_{2}^{2},q^{2}\ll 0
,
{\tilde{\Pi}}^{\rm QCD}
can be analytically calculated by operator product expansion (OPE).

The leading contribution to OPE is from the perturbation diagram as shown by the left diagram in Fig.
1
, with amplitude

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mu\nu\rho}^{\rm pert}(p_{1},p_{2},q^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{iN_{c}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\int d^{4}k_{1}d^{4}k_{2}d^{4}k\ \delta^{4}(p_{2}-k_{2}-k)\delta^{4}(p_{1}-k_{1}-k)

\displaystyle\times\frac{{\rm tr}[\not{k}\sigma_{\mu\nu}(\not{k}_{2}+m_{s})(\not{k}_{1}+m_{s})\gamma_{\rho}\gamma_{5}]}{k^{2}(k_{2}^{2}-m_{s}^{2})(k_{1}^{2}-m_{s}^{2})}.

(24)

The double imaginary part of the correlation function is related with its double discontinuity as

\displaystyle{\rm Im}^{2}\Pi_{\mu\nu\rho}^{\rm pert}(p_{1},p_{2},q^{2})=\frac{1}{(2i)^{2}}{\rm Disc}^{2}\Pi_{\mu\nu\rho}^{\rm pert}(p_{1},p_{2},q^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2i)^{2}}\frac{iN_{c}}{(2\pi)^{4}}(-2\pi i)^{3}\int d\Phi_{\Delta}(p_{1},p_{2},m_{s},m_{s},0)

\displaystyle\times{\rm tr}[\not{k}\sigma_{\mu\nu}(\not{k}_{2}+m_{s})(\not{k}_{1}+m_{s})\gamma_{\rho}\gamma_{5}],

(25)

which is obtained by the cutting rules:
{\rm Disc}\{1/(p^{2}-m^{2}+i\epsilon)\}=(-2\pi i)\delta(p^{2}-m^{2})
.
In the above expression, we have introduced a three body phase space integration measure
d\Phi_{\Delta}
for a triangle integration as shown by the right diagram in Fig.
1
:

\displaystyle\int d\Phi_{\Delta}(p_{1},p_{2},m_{1},m_{2},m)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d^{4}k_{1}d^{4}k_{2}d^{4}k\ \delta(k_{1}^{2}-m_{1}^{2})\delta(k_{2}^{2}-m_{2}^{2})\delta(k^{2}-m^{2})

\displaystyle\times\delta^{4}(p_{2}-k_{2}-k)\delta^{4}(p_{1}-k_{1}-k),

(26)

where the three internal lines are set on shell.
The scalar triangle integration with unit integrand reads as

\displaystyle I_{\Delta}=\int d\Phi_{\Delta}(p_{1},p_{2},m_{1},m_{2},m)\cdot 1=\frac{\pi}{2\sqrt{\lambda}}

\displaystyle\times\Theta[s_{1},s_{2},q^{2},m_{1},m_{2},m]\theta[s_{1}-s_{1}^{\rm min}]\theta[s_{2}-s_{2}^{\rm min}],

(27)

where
\lambda=(s_{1}+s_{2}-q^{2})^{2}-4s_{1}s_{2}
with
p_{1}^{2}=s_{1},p_{2}^{2}=s_{2}
.
\Theta
is a
\theta
function constraining the
s_{1},s_{2},q^{2}
:

\displaystyle\Theta[s_{1},s_{2},q^{2},m_{1},m_{2},m]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta\big[-m_{2}^{4}s_{1}-m_{1}^{4}s_{2}+m_{2}^{2}[m^{2}(q^{2}+s_{1}-s_{2})

\displaystyle+s_{1}(q^{2}-s_{1}+s_{2})]

\displaystyle-q^{2}(m^{4}+s_{1}s_{2}-m^{2}(-q^{2}+s_{1}+s_{2}))

\displaystyle+m_{1}^{2}[(q^{2}+s_{1}-s_{2})s_{2}

\displaystyle+m^{2}(q^{2}-s_{1}+s_{2})+m_{2}^{2}(-q^{2}+s_{1}+s_{2})]\big].

(28)

The rest two
\theta
functions ensure that
s_{1},s_{2}
are above the corresponding quark level thresholds , namely
s_{1,2}>s_{1,2}^{\rm min}=(m_{1,2}+m)^{2}
.
In the Eq. (
25
) one have to set
m_{1}=m_{2}=m_{s}
. The definitions and expressions of higher rank triangle diagram integrations are given in the Appendix
A
. The analytical expression of
{\rm Im}^{2}{\tilde{\Pi}}^{\rm pert}(p_{1},p_{2},q^{2})
is given in the Appendix

The dimension-3 operator contribution to OPE comes from the quark condensing diagrams as shown in Fig.

. It can be found that the amplitudes of the
s
quark condensing diagrams only contain one fraction form, either
1/(p_{1}^{2}-m_{s}^{2})
or
1/(p_{2}^{2}-m_{s}^{2})
. Therefore, the diagrams (b) and (c) will vanish under the double Borel transformation for
p_{1}^{2}
and
p_{2}^{2}
simultaneously. The amplitude of diagram (a) reads as

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mu\nu\rho}^{\bar{q}q}(p_{1},p_{2},q^{2})=i^{2}\int d^{4}xd^{4}y\ e^{ip_{2}\cdot x}e^{-ip_{1}\cdot y}

\displaystyle\times[\sigma_{\mu\nu}D^{(0)}_{s}(x,0)D^{(0)}_{s}(0,y)\gamma_{\rho}\gamma_{5}]\langle 0|{\bar{q}}_{a}^{i}(x)q_{b}^{i}(y)|0\rangle,

(29)

where
D^{(0)}_{s}
denotes free
s
quark propagator. The nonlocal
\bar{q}q
condensing matrix element can be expanded up to dimension-5 local operators as

\displaystyle\langle 0|{\bar{q}}_{a}^{i}(x)q_{b}^{i}(y)|0\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N_{c}\left[\langle\bar{q}q\rangle\frac{1}{12}\delta_{ba}+\langle\bar{q}Gq\rangle\frac{1}{192}(x-y)^{2}\delta_{ba}\right].

(30)

The contribution of dimension-3 operator, namely the
\bar{q}q
condensate, only comes from the first term given above. The corresponding amplitude reads as

\displaystyle{\rm Im}^{2}{\tilde{\Pi}}^{\bar{q}q}(p_{1},p_{2},q^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi^{2}}{3}N_{c}m_{s}\langle\bar{q}q\rangle\delta(s_{1}-m_{s}^{2})\delta(s_{2}-m_{s}^{2}).

(31)

The second term in Eq. (
30
) provides a contribution from the dimension-5 operator
\bar{q}g_{s}G_{\alpha\beta}q
. The corresponding amplitude reads as

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mu\nu\rho}^{\bar{q}Gq(1)}(p_{1},p_{2},q^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{N_{c}}{192}\langle\bar{q}Gq\rangle(-1)\left(\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial p_{2}^{\alpha}\partial p_{2\alpha}}+\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial p_{1}^{\alpha}\partial p_{1\alpha}}+2\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial p_{1}^{\alpha}\partial p_{2\alpha}}\right)

\displaystyle\times{\rm tr}[\sigma_{\mu\nu}(\not{p}_{2}+m_{s})(\not{p}_{1}+m_{s})\gamma_{\rho}\gamma_{5}]

\displaystyle\times\frac{1}{p_{1}^{2}-m_{s}^{2}}\frac{1}{p_{2}^{2}-m_{s}^{2}},

(32)

where we have transformed
x,y
to
-i\partial/\partial p_{2},i\partial/\partial p_{1}
through the exponential terms in Eq. (
29
). To simplify the calculation of Eq. (
32
) we can omit the terms suppressed by
m_{s}^{2}
and obtain

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mu\nu\rho}^{\bar{q}Gq(1)}(p_{1},p_{2},q^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{N_{c}}{12}\langle\bar{q}Gq\rangle m_{s}\epsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\alpha}(p_{1}^{\alpha}-p_{2}^{\alpha})\frac{\partial}{\partial M^{2}}

\displaystyle\times\left[\frac{1}{(p_{1}^{2}-M^{2})(p_{2}^{2}-m_{s}^{2})}-\frac{1}{(p_{1}^{2}-m_{s}^{2})(p_{2}^{2}-M^{2})}\right]|_{M^{2}=m_{s}^{2}}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{N_{c}}{12}\langle\bar{q}Gq\rangle m_{s}\epsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\alpha}(p_{1}^{\alpha}+p_{2}^{\alpha})q^{2}

\displaystyle\times\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial M_{1}^{2}\partial M_{2}^{2}}\left[\frac{1}{(p_{1}^{2}-M_{1}^{2})(p_{2}^{2}-M_{2}^{2})}\right]|_{M_{1}^{2}=M_{2}^{2}=m_{s}^{2}}.

(33)

To obtain the above expression, we have introduced derivatives on auxiliary masses
M_{1},M_{2}
to lower the power of the denominator, which means

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(p_{1,2}^{2}-m_{1,2}^{2})^{2}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial M^{2}}\frac{1}{p_{1,2}^{2}-M^{2}}|_{M^{2}\to m_{1,2}^{2}}.

(34)

Taking the imaginary part, using dispersive integration and conducting Borel transformation, we arrives at

\displaystyle{\cal B}_{T_{1},T_{2}}\{{\tilde{\Pi}}^{\bar{q}Gq(1)}\}(q^{2})=\frac{1}{6}N_{c}m_{s}\langle\bar{q}Gq\rangle

\displaystyle\times e^{-m_{s}^{2}/T_{1}^{2}-m_{s}^{2}/T_{2}^{2}}\left[\frac{1}{T_{1}^{2}}-\frac{1}{T_{2}^{2}}+\frac{q^{2}}{T_{1}^{2}T_{2}^{2}}\right].

(35)

The quark-gluon condensing diagrams as shown in Fig.

\displaystyle D_{s}(x,0)=

\displaystyle\ i\int\frac{d^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{4}}e^{-ik\cdot x}\left[\frac{\delta_{ij}}{\not{k}-m_{s}}-\frac{g_{s}G_{\alpha\beta}^{A}t_{ij}^{A}}{4}\frac{\sigma^{\alpha\beta}(\not{k}+m_{s})+(\not{k}+m_{s})\sigma^{\alpha\beta}}{(k^{2}-m_{s}^{2})^{2}}\right.

\displaystyle D_{q}(x,0)=

\displaystyle\ \frac{i\delta_{ij}\not{x}}{2\pi^{2}x^{4}}-\frac{ig_{s}G_{\alpha\beta}^{A}t_{ij}^{A}(\not{x}\sigma^{\alpha\beta}+\sigma^{\alpha\beta}\not{x})}{32\pi^{2}x^{2}}+\cdots,

where

(37)

and we have only present the terms relevant to the OPE up to dimension-5 operators in Eq.(

).

It can be found that the diagram (b), (c), (e) and (f) in Fig.

vanish after the Borel transformation due to the same reason as that happens in the

). Thus diagram (a) is the only non-vanishing diagram with amplitude:

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mu\nu\rho}^{\bar{q}Gq(2)}(p_{1},p_{2},q^{2})=

\displaystyle\ i^{2}\int d^{4}xd^{4}y\ e^{ip_{2}\cdot x}e^{-ip_{1}\cdot y}\int\frac{d^{4}k_{1}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{d^{4}k_{2}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\ e^{ik_{1}\cdot y}e^{-ik_{2}\cdot x}

\displaystyle\times\left[\sigma_{\mu\nu}\left(-i\over 4\right)\frac{\sigma^{\alpha\beta}(\not{k}_{2}+m_{s})+(\not{k}_{2}+m_{s})\sigma^{\alpha\beta}}{(k_{2}^{2}-m_{s}^{2})^{2}}\frac{i(\not{k}_{1}+m_{s})}{k_{1}^{2}-m_{s}^{2}}\gamma_{\rho}\gamma_{5}\right]_{ab}

\displaystyle\times t^{A}_{ij}\langle 0|{\bar{q}}_{a}^{i}(x)g_{s}G_{\alpha\beta}^{A}(0)q_{b}^{i}(y)|0\rangle.

Using the the quark-gluon condensate formula, keeping the leading term:

\displaystyle\langle 0|{\bar{q}}_{a}^{i}(x)g_{s}G_{\alpha\beta}^{A}(0)q_{b}^{i}(y)|0\rangle=\frac{1}{192}\langle\bar{q}Gq\rangle(\sigma_{\alpha\beta})_{ba}t_{ji}^{A}+\cdots,

(39)

and conducting the projection introduced in Eq. (

\displaystyle{\tilde{\Pi}}^{\bar{q}Gq(2)}(p_{1},p_{2},q^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{24}m_{s}\langle\bar{q}Gq\rangle\frac{\partial}{\partial M^{2}}\frac{1}{(p_{1}^{2}-m_{s}^{2})(p_{2}^{2}-M^{2})}|_{M^{2}=m_{s}^{2}}.

(40)

The Borel transformed form reads as

\displaystyle{\cal B}_{T_{1},T_{2}}\{{\tilde{\Pi}}^{\bar{q}Gq(2)}\}(q^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{6}m_{s}\langle\bar{q}Gq\rangle\frac{1}{T_{2}^{2}}e^{-m_{s}^{2}/T_{1}^{2}}e^{-m_{s}^{2}/T_{2}^{2}}.

(41)

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mu\nu\rho}^{GG(a)}(p_{1},p_{2},q^{2})

\displaystyle\int d^{4}xd^{4}y\ e^{ip_{2}\cdot x}e^{-ip_{1}\cdot y}\int\frac{d^{4}k_{1}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{d^{4}k_{2}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{d^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{4}}\ e^{ik_{1}\cdot y}e^{-ik_{2}\cdot x}e^{-ik\cdot(y-x)}\left(-\frac{i}{4}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\times{\rm tr}\left[\frac{-i\not{k}}{k^{2}}\sigma_{\mu\nu}\frac{\sigma^{\alpha\beta}(\not{k}_{2}+m_{s})+(\not{k}_{2}+m_{s})\sigma^{\alpha\beta}}{(k_{2}^{2}-m_{s}^{2})^{2}}\frac{\sigma^{\kappa\tau}(\not{k}_{1}+m_{s})+(\not{k}_{1}+m_{s})\sigma^{\kappa\tau}}{(k_{2}^{2}-m_{s}^{2})^{2}}\gamma_{\rho}\gamma_{5}\right]

\displaystyle g_{s}^{2}\langle 0|G_{\alpha\beta}^{A}(0)G_{\kappa\tau}^{A}(0)|0\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{96}\langle GG\rangle\delta_{AB}(g_{\alpha\kappa}g_{\beta\tau}-g_{\alpha\tau}{\beta\kappa}),

and extracting the imaginary part by cutting rules, we arrive at

\displaystyle{\rm Im}^{2}\Pi_{\mu\nu\rho}^{GG(a)}(p_{1},p_{2},q^{2})

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3072\pi}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial M_{1}^{2}\partial M_{2}^{2}}\int d\Phi_{\Delta}[p_{1},p_{2},M_{1},M_{2},0]

\displaystyle\times{\rm tr}\left[\not{k}\sigma_{\mu\nu}(\sigma^{\alpha\beta}(\not{k}_{2}+m_{s})+(\not{k}_{2}+m_{s})\sigma^{\alpha\beta})\right.

(44)

\displaystyle\left.\times(\sigma^{\kappa\tau}(\not{k}_{1}+m_{s})+(\not{k}_{1}+m_{s})\sigma^{\kappa\tau})\gamma_{\rho}\gamma_{5}\right]

\displaystyle\times(g_{\alpha\kappa}g_{\beta\tau}-g_{\alpha\tau}{\beta\kappa})|_{M_{1}^{2}=m_{s}^{2},M_{2}^{2}=m_{s}^{2}}.

(45)

lines to be
M_{1}^{2},M_{2}^{2}
. The analytical result of

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mu\nu\rho}^{GG(b)}(p_{1},p_{2},q^{2})

\displaystyle\int d^{4}xd^{4}y\ e^{ip_{2}\cdot x}e^{-ip_{1}\cdot y}\int\frac{d^{4}k_{1}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{d^{4}k_{2}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\ e^{ik_{1}\cdot y}e^{-ik_{2}\cdot x}\left(-\frac{i}{32\pi^{2}}\right)\left(-\frac{i}{4}\right){\rm tr}[t^{A}t^{B}]

\displaystyle\times{\rm tr}\left[\frac{(\not{y}-\not{x})\sigma^{\kappa\tau}+\sigma^{\kappa\tau}(\not{y}-\not{x})}{(y-x)^{2}}\sigma_{\mu\nu}\frac{i(\not{k}_{2}+m_{s})}{k_{2}^{2}-m_{s}^{2}}\frac{\sigma^{\alpha\beta}(\not{k}_{1}+m_{s})+(\not{k}_{1}+m_{s})\sigma^{\alpha\beta}}{(k_{1}^{2}-m_{s}^{2})^{2}}\gamma_{\rho}\gamma_{5}\right]

\displaystyle\int d^{4}w\ e^{-i(p_{1}-k_{1})\cdot w}\frac{1}{w^{2}}=(-4\pi^{2}i)\frac{1}{(p_{1}-k_{1})^{2}},

(47)

we have the imaginary part as

\displaystyle\ {\rm Im}^{2}{\tilde{\Pi}}^{GG(b)}(p_{1},p_{2},q^{2})(p_{1}^{\beta^{\prime}}p_{2}^{\alpha^{\prime}}-p_{2}^{\alpha^{\prime}}p_{2}^{\beta^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{6144\pi}\langle GG\rangle\epsilon^{\mu\rho\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime}}p_{1}^{\nu}\frac{\partial}{\partial M_{1}^{2}}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{1}^{\sigma}}+\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{2}^{\sigma}}\right)

\displaystyle\times\int d\Phi_{\Delta}[p_{1},p_{2},M_{1},m_{s},0][g_{\alpha\kappa}g_{\beta\tau}-g_{\alpha\tau}g_{\beta\kappa}]

where the linear term of

at the energy scale
\mu=m_{K_{1}}=1.3\ {\rm GeV}

ParticleDataGroup:2020ssz
. The condensate parameters are taken as
Ioffe:2005ym
;
Colangelo:2000dp
:
\langle\bar{q}q\rangle=-(0.24\pm 0.01\mathrm{GeV})^{3},\left\langle\bar{q}Gq\right\rangle=m_{0}^{2}\langle\bar{q}q\rangle\ {\rm with}\ m_{0}^{2}=(0.8\pm 0.2)\mathrm{GeV}^{2},\langle GG\rangle=(4\pi^{2})(0.012\pm 0.004)\mathrm{GeV}^{4}
.

In terms of the threshold parameters, note that since
J^{1A}_{\rho}
can create both pseudoscalar and axial vector kaons,
s_{1}^{\rm th}
should correspond to the excited pseudoscalar
K(1460)
. On the other hand,
J^{1B}_{\mu\nu}
can only create axial vector kaons, thus
s_{2}^{\rm th}
should correspond to the excited axial vector
K_{1}(1650)
. Therefore we choose
s_{1}^{\rm th}=1.46^{2}\ {\rm GeV}^{2}
and
s_{2}^{\rm th}=1.65^{2}\ {\rm GeV}^{2}
.

The determination of Borel parameters depends on two criteria. Firstly, the contribution from the continuous spectrum must be suppressed so that be smaller than the pole contribution. Quantitatively, this criterion can be expressed by the constraint:

\displaystyle\xi_{\rm conti}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{\int_{s_{1}^{\rm th}}^{\infty}ds_{1}\int_{s_{2}^{\rm th}}^{\infty}ds_{2}\ e^{-\frac{s_{1}}{T_{1}^{2}}}e^{-\frac{s_{2}}{T_{2}^{2}}}{\rm Im}^{2}{\tilde{\Pi}}^{\rm QCD}(s_{1},s_{2},q^{2})}{\int_{0}^{\infty}ds_{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}ds_{2}\ e^{-\frac{s_{1}}{T_{1}^{2}}}e^{-\frac{s_{2}}{T_{2}^{2}}}{\rm Im}^{2}{\tilde{\Pi}}^{\rm QCD}(s_{1},s_{2},q^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim 0.5,

(49)

where the nominator denotes the contribution from the continuous spectrum, while the denominator denotes all the spectrum contribution. Note that the Borel parameter is related with the corresponding hadron mass. Since the mass difference of the intial and final kaon is little, one can simply set
T_{1}=T_{2}=T
. Without loss of generality,
q^{2}
can be chosen by an arbitrary value in the deep Euclidean region when determining
T^{2}
. Here we choose
q^{2}=-6\ {\rm GeV}^{2}
and present
\xi_{\rm conti}
as a function of
T^{2}
in Fig.
5
, where the blue and red bands denote the errors from the uncertainties of condensate parameters and
m_{K_{1A}},\ f_{1A},\ f_{1B}^{\perp}
, respectively.
It can be found that
\xi_{\rm conti}
increases with the increasing of
T^{2}
.
\xi_{\rm conti}=50\%

occurs at
T^{2}=1.16\thicksim 1.81\ {\rm GeV}^{2}
, which gives the range of the upper limit for the Borel parameter:
1.16\ {\rm GeV}^{2}<T_{\rm upper}^{2}<1.81\ {\rm GeV}^{2}
.

VI
summary

In this work we investigate the
K_{1}(1270)-K_{1}(1400)
mixing caused by the flavor
SU(3)
symmetry breaking. The mixing angle is expressed by a
K_{1A}\to K_{1B}
matrix element induced by the
s
quark mass operator that breaks flavor
SU(3)
symmetry. We focus on the QCD contribution to this matrix element and calculate it by QCDSR, where a three-point correlation function is defined and calculated both at the hadron and quark-gluon levels. In the calculation at the quark-gluon level, the operator product expansion is up to dimension-5 condensates. A detailed numerical analysis is performed to determine the Borel parameters, and the obtained mixing angle is
\theta_{K_{1}}=22^{\circ}\pm 7^{\circ}
or
\theta_{K_{1}}=68^{\circ}\pm 7^{\circ}
, which is consistent with the phenomenological analysis on the relation between
\theta_{K_{1}}
and the mixing angle of strangeless axial vector mesons.

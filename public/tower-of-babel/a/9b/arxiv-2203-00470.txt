2. Order functions and a key lemma

2.1. Order functions

In general for order functions, cf. Hayman
[
9
]
Chap’s. 1, 2,
Noguchi-Ochiai
[
18
]
Chap. V,
and Noguchi-Winkelmann
[
20
]
Chap. 2.

Let
X
be a compact complex space with reduced structure sheaf
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
,
and let
\mathscr{I}\subset{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
be a coherent ideal sheaf.
Let
f:{\mathbf{C}}\to X
be an entire curve. We are going to define an
order function
T_{f}(r,\omega_{\mathscr{I}})
of
f
with respect to
\mathscr{I}

(see
[
20
]
§2.4 for details:
Note that the projective algebraic condition on
X
is unnecessary because
of
1
-dimensionality of the domain
{\mathbf{C}}
).

The pull-back
f^{*}\mathscr{I}
is an effective divisor on
{\mathbf{C}}
, unless

f({\mathbf{C}})\subset\mathrm{Supp}\,\mathscr{I}~(:=\{x\in X:\mathscr{I}_{x}\not={\mathcal{O}}(X)_{x}\})
, which we assume.
Denoting by
{\mathrm{ord}}_{z}f^{*}\mathscr{I}
the order of
f^{*}\mathscr{I}
at
z\in{\mathbf{C}}
,
we define the counting functions of
f^{*}\mathscr{I}
truncated at level

l\in\mathbf{N}\cup\{\infty\}
by

n_{l}(t,f^{*}\mathscr{I})=\sum_{|z|<t}\min\{{\mathrm{ord}}_{z}f^{*}\mathscr{I},l\},\quad N_{l}(t,f^{*}\mathscr{I})=\int_{1}^{r}\frac{n_{l}(t,f^{*}\mathscr{I})}{t}dt,\quad r>1.

Let
\phi_{\mathscr{I}}(x)~(x\in X)
be the proximity potential of
\mathscr{I}

(see
[
20
]
§2.4).
Then we have the pull-back
f^{*}\phi_{\mathscr{I}}(z)
such that it is of
C^{\infty}
-class
outside the singular set
f^{-1}(\mathrm{Supp}\,\mathscr{I})
and at a singular point

a\in f^{-1}(\mathrm{Supp}\,\mathscr{I})
it is written locally as

f^{*}\phi_{\mathscr{I}}(z)=\lambda_{a}\log|z-a|+\hbox{$C^{\infty}$-term},

where
\lambda_{a}\in\mathbf{N}
(positive integers).
We set the proximity function of
f
for
\mathscr{I}
by

m_{f}(r,\mathscr{I})=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{|z|=r}-f^{*}\phi_{\mathscr{I}}(re^{i\theta})d\theta.

With
\omega_{\mathscr{I}}:=\frac{1}{\pi i}{\partial}\bar{\partial}\phi_{\mathscr{I}}

we define the order function of
f
with respect to
\omega_{\mathscr{I}}
by

T_{f}(r,\omega_{\mathscr{I}})=\int_{1}^{r}\frac{dt}{t}\int_{|z|<t}f^{*}\omega_{\mathscr{I}}\quad(r>1).

We have a so-called First Main Theorem for
f
and
\mathscr{I}
(see

[
20
]
Theorem 2.4.9):

(2.1)

T_{f}(r,\omega_{\mathscr{I}})=N_{\infty}(r,f^{*}\mathscr{I})+m_{f}(r,\mathscr{I})-m_{f}(1,\mathscr{I}).

In the case where
\mathscr{I}=\mathscr{I}{\langle}Y{\rangle}
is the ideal sheaf of an analytic
subset
Y
possibly non-reduced of
X
, we write

f^{*}\mathscr{I}{\langle}Y{\rangle}=f^{*}Y,\quad\omega_{Y}=\omega_{\mathscr{I}{\langle}Y{\rangle}},\quad T_{f}(r,\omega_{Y})=T_{f}(r,\omega_{\mathscr{I}{\langle}Y{\rangle}}).

If
X
is smooth and
Y
is a divisor
D
on
X
. Then we have the line
bundle
L(D)\to X
associated with
D
, and the first Chern class

c_{1}(D)
. Then,
\omega_{D}\in c_{1}(D)
, and it is common to write

T_{f}(r,\omega_{D})=T_{f}(r,L(D))=T_{f}(r,c_{1}(D))

for the order functions.

Assume that
X
is projective algebraic. Let
D
and
D^{\prime}
be big divisors on

X
such that
f({\mathbf{C}})\not\subset\mathrm{Supp}\,D\cup\mathrm{Supp}\,D^{\prime}
.
Then we have

T_{f}(r,L(D))=O\left(T_{f}(r,L(D^{\prime}))\right),\quad T_{f}(r,L(D^{\prime}))=O\left(T_{f}(r,L(D))\right).

Therefore in an estimate such as
O\left(T_{f}(r,L(D))\right)
the
choice of of
D
or
L(D)
does not matter; in such a case we simply write

T_{f}(r)
for
T_{f}(r,L(D))
with respect to
some ample or big line bundle
L(D)
over
X
as far as

f({\mathbf{C}})\not\subset\mathrm{Supp}\,D
;
however, once it is chosen, it is fixed.

Note that the followings are equivalent for
f:{\mathbf{C}}\to X
:

2.2
.

(i)

f
is rational (not transcendental);

(ii)

\underset{r\to\infty}{\underline{\lim}}\;\frac{T_{f}(r)}{\log r}<\infty
.

(iii)

\underset{r\to\infty}{\overline{\lim}}\;\frac{T_{f}(r)}{\log r}<\infty
; i.e.,

T_{f}(r)=O(\log r)
.

The order
\rho_{f}
of
f:{\mathbf{C}}\to X
is defined by

0\leq\rho_{f}=\underset{r\to\infty}{\overline{\lim}}\;\frac{\log T_{f}(r)}{\log r}\leq\infty.

If
\rho_{f}<\infty
,
f
is said to be of finite order and

T_{f}(r)=O(r^{\rho_{f}+\varepsilon}),\qquad^{\forall}\varepsilon>0.

2.2. Key lemma

Let
A
be a semi-abelian variety.
Here we fix an isomorphism
{\mathop{\mathrm{Lie}}}(A)\cong{\mathbf{C}}^{n}
with coordinates

(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})
.
Let
f=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}):z\in{\mathbf{C}}\to f(z)\in{\mathbf{C}}^{n}(\cong{\mathop{\mathrm{Lie}}}(A))

be an entire curve. In the present paper we assume that
f
is
non-constant. We denote by
T(r,f_{j})
Nevanlinna’s order function
(see
[
9
]
,
[
20
]
Chap. 1), and set

T_{f}(r)=\max_{1\leq j\leq n}T(r,f_{j}).

Let
\bar{A}
be an equivariant smooth projective compactification of
A

with boundary divisor
{\partial}A=\bar{A}\setminus A

of simple normal crossing type (cf.
[
20
]
Chap. 5),
and let
L\to{\bar{A}}
be an ample line bundle.
We set the order function of
\exp_{A}f:{\mathbf{C}}\to A
by

T_{\exp_{A}f}(r)=T_{\exp_{A}f}(r,L).

For
\hskip 1.0pt{\widehat{\mathrm{ex}}}_{A}f=(\exp_{A}f,f):{\mathbf{C}}\to A\times{\mathbf{C}}^{n}
we set

T_{\hskip 1.0pt{\widehat{\mathrm{ex}}}_{A}f}(r):=T_{\exp_{A}f}(r)+T_{f}(r).

By
[
20
]
Theorem 6.1.9 and
2.2
above we see

Proposition 2.3
.

Let the notation be as above.
The followings are equivalent:

(i)

f:{\mathbf{C}}\to{\mathbf{C}}^{n}
is rational (i.e.,
f_{j}
are polynomials).

(ii)

\exp_{A}f
is of finite order.

(iii)

\hskip 1.0pt{\widehat{\mathrm{ex}}}_{A}f
is of finite order.

As usual in Nevanlinna theory we use the symbol
“
S_{\exp_{A}f}(r)
” for a small term such that

(2.4)

S_{\exp_{A}f}(r)=O(\log^{+}T_{\exp_{A}f}(r))+O(\log r)+O(1)~~||,

where “
||
” stands for the validity of the estimate except for
r

in exceptional intervals with finite total length, and
for
\exp_{A}f
of finite order there are no such exceptional intervals.
We use the notation through the paper.

The following is the key lemma for the estimates in the
arguments henceforth.

Lemma 2.5
.

With the notation above, we have:

(i)

T_{f}(r)=S_{\exp_{A}f}(r)
.

(ii)

T_{\hskip 1.0pt{\widehat{\mathrm{ex}}}_{A}f}(r)=T_{\exp_{A}f}(r)+S_{\exp_{A}f}(r)
.

Proof.
It suffices to prove (i).
When the order of
\exp_{A}f
is finite, then
f
is rational, and so

T_{f}(r)=O(\log r)
without exceptional intervals.

In general, we take a representation of the semi-abelian variety
A

0\to({\mathbf{C}}^{*})^{p}\to A\to A_{0}\to 0,

where
A_{0}
is an abelian variety. Then
A
has a structure of
locally flat
({\mathbf{C}}^{*})^{p}
-principal bundle with transition transformation
by
(S^{1})^{p}:=\{\zeta\in{\mathbf{C}}:|\zeta|=1\}^{p}
-multiplication
(cf.
[
20
]
§6.1).
After a change of indices of the coordinates
(z_{j})
and
a linear transform of
(z_{j})
we have the following
expression of the order function
T_{\exp_{A}f}(r)
(cf. ibid.):

(2.6)

\displaystyle f(z)

\displaystyle=(f_{1}(z),\ldots,f_{p}(z),f_{p+1},\ldots,f_{n}(z)),

\displaystyle T_{1}(r)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{j=1}^{p}T\left(r,e^{f_{j}}\right),

\displaystyle T_{2}(r)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{|z|=r}\sum_{j+1}^{n}|f_{j}(z)|^{2}\,d\theta-\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{|z|=1}\sum_{j+1}^{n}|f_{j}(z)|^{2}\,d\theta,

\displaystyle T_{\exp_{A}f}(r)

\displaystyle=T_{1}(r)+T_{2}(r).

For
f_{j}~(p+1\leq j\leq n)
we have

\displaystyle T(r,f_{j})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{|z|=r}\log^{+}|f_{j}|\,d\theta\leq\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{|z|=r}\log(1+|f_{j}|^{2})\,d\theta

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\log\left(1+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{|z|=r}|f_{j}|^{2}\,d\theta\right)\leq\frac{1}{2}\log^{+}T_{2}(r)+O(1)

\displaystyle=O(\log^{+}T_{\exp_{A}f}(r))+O(1).

For
f_{j}~(1\leq j\leq p)
we have

T\left(r,e^{f_{j}}\right)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{|z|=r}\log^{+}\left|e^{f_{j}(z)}\right|\,d\theta=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{|z|=r}{\Re\,}^{+}f_{j}(z)\,d\theta,

where
{\Re\,}^{+}f_{j}:=\max\{{\Re\,}f_{j},0\}
with the real part
{\Re\,}f_{j}
.
With the imaginary part
{\Im\,}f_{j}(0)
and the complex Poisson
kernel we write

f_{j}(z)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{|\zeta|=R}\frac{\zeta+z}{\zeta-z}{\Re\,}f_{j}(\zeta)\,d\theta+{\Im\,}f_{j}(0).

For
|z|=r<R
we get

|f_{j}(z)|\leq\frac{R+r}{R-r}\cdot\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{|\zeta|=R}{\Re\,}^{+}f_{j}(\zeta)\,d\theta+|{\Im\,}f_{j}(0)|.

Then we have

\displaystyle T(r,f_{j})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{|\zeta|=r}\log(1+|f_{j}(z)|)\,d\theta\leq\log\left(1+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{|z|=r}|f_{j}(z)|\,d\theta\right)

\displaystyle\leq\log\left(1+\frac{R+r}{R-r}\cdot\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{|\zeta|=R}{\Re\,}^{+}f_{j}(\zeta)\,d\theta+O(1)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\log\left(1+\frac{R+r}{R-r}\cdot T_{1}(R)+O(1)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\log^{+}\left(\frac{R+r}{R-r}\cdot T_{1}(R)\right)+O(1)

Now we take
R=r+1/T_{1}(r)
, so that

\displaystyle T(r,f_{j})\leq\log^{+}\left((2r+1)T_{1}(r)\cdot T_{1}\left(r+\frac{1}{T_{1}(r)}\right)\right)+O(1)

Borel’s Lemma (cf. Hayman
[
9
]
Lemma 2.4) implies

T_{1}\left(r+\frac{1}{T_{1}(r)}\right)\leq 2T_{1}(r)\quad||.

Therefore it follows that

\displaystyle T(r,f_{j})

\displaystyle=O(\log^{+}T_{1}(r))+O(\log r)+O(1)

\displaystyle=O(\log^{+}T_{\exp_{A}f}(r))+O(\log r)+O(1)\quad||.

The proof is completed.
∎

3. Proof of Theorem
1.3

By
[
14
]
we see that the Zariski closure

\overline{\exp_{A}f({\mathbf{C}})}^{\mathrm{Zar}}
in
A
is a translate of a connected algebraic subgroup
(a semi-abelian subvariety) of
A
.
It follows from
A
-nondegeneracy
1.2
that

\overline{\exp_{A}f({\mathbf{C}})}^{\mathrm{Zar}}=A
, so that

(3.1)

{\mathrm{tr.}\deg}\hskip 0.3pt_{\mathbf{C}}\,\exp_{A}f=n.

Let
{\mathbf{C}}(A)
(resp.
{\mathbf{C}}(f)
) be the rational function field of
A

(resp. the field generated by
f_{j}~(1\leq j\leq n)
over
{\mathbf{C}}
).
We denote by
{\mathrm{tr.}\deg}\hskip 0.3pt_{{\mathbf{C}}(f)}(\exp_{A}f)^{*}{\mathbf{C}}(A)
the transcendence degree
of the pull-backed field
(\exp_{A}f)^{*}{\mathbf{C}}(A)
over
{\mathbf{C}}(f)
.
We prove:

Lemma 3.2
.

With the notation above we have

(3.3)

{\mathrm{tr.}\deg}\hskip 0.3pt_{{\mathbf{C}}(f)}\,(\exp_{A}f)^{*}{\mathbf{C}}(A)\geq 1.

Proof.

We take a transcendence basis
\{\phi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
of

{\mathbf{C}}(A)
over
{\mathbf{C}}
such that
\hat{\phi}_{j}:=\phi_{j}\circ\exp_{A}f

are defined as non-constant meromorphic functions, and

\hat{\phi}:=\left(\hat{\phi}_{1},\ldots,\hat{\phi}_{n}\right).

Assume contrarily that (
3.3
) is false; i.e.,

(3.4)

{\mathrm{tr.}\deg}\hskip 0.3pt_{{\mathbf{C}}(f)}\hat{\phi}=0\quad(\hbox{regarded as }\hat{\phi}=\{\hat{\phi}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}).

Then all
\hat{\phi}_{j}
are algebraic over
{\mathbf{C}}(f)
.
There are non-zero polynomials
P_{j}(t)
in
one variable with coefficients in
{\mathbf{C}}(f)

such that

(3.5)

P_{j}(\hat{\phi}_{j})=0,\quad 1\leq j\leq n.

By
[
20
]
Lemma 2.5.15 we have

T(r,\hat{\phi}_{j})=O(T_{f}(r))+O(1).

With setting
\widehat{T}(r):=\max_{1\leq j\leq n}T(r,\hat{\phi}_{j})
we
thus obtain

\widehat{T}(r)=O(T_{f}(r))+O(1).

On the other hand, it follows from
[
20
]
Theorem 2.5.18 that

T_{\exp_{A}f}(r)=O(\widehat{T}(r))+O(1).

Therefore we see that

T_{\exp_{A}f}(r)=O(T_{f}(r))+O(1).

But this contradicts Lemma
2.5
.
∎

Continuation of the proof of Theorem
1.3
:
By (
3.1
),
{\mathrm{tr.}\deg}\hskip 0.3pt_{{\mathbf{C}}}\{f,\hat{\phi}\}\geq n
.
For proof by contradiction we assume that

{\mathrm{tr.}\deg}\hskip 0.3pt_{\mathbf{C}}\,\{f,\hat{\phi}\}=n.

Then all
f_{j}
are algebraic over
{\mathbf{C}}(\hat{\phi})
,
so that there are non-trivial algebraic relations,

(3.6)

P_{j}(f_{j},\hat{\phi})=P_{j}(f_{j},\hat{\phi}_{1},\ldots,\hat{\phi}_{n})=0,\quad 1\leq j\leq n.

If
{\mathrm{tr.}\deg}\hskip 0.3pt_{\mathbf{C}}\{f_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}=n
,
the assumption implies
{\mathrm{tr.}\deg}\hskip 0.3pt_{{\mathbf{C}}(f)}\{f,\hat{\phi}\}=0
;
this does not take place by Lemma
3.2
.
Therefore
{\mathrm{tr.}\deg}\hskip 0.3pt_{\mathbf{C}}\{f_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}<n
, and hence
there is a non-trivial algebraic relation over
{\mathbf{C}}
:

(3.7)

Q(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})=0.

If, to say,
f_{1}
is contained in (
3.7
), we take the
resultant of (
3.7
) and (
3.6
) (
j=1
) with respect to
f_{1}
,
which yields a non-trivial algebraic relation

Q_{1}(f_{2},\ldots,f_{n},\hat{\phi})=0.

After repeating this process at most
n
-times we eliminate

f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}
in (
3.7
) to obtain
a non-trivial algebraic relation

\hat{Q}\left(\hat{\phi}_{1},\ldots,\hat{\phi}_{n}\right)=0\,;

this again contradicts the algebraic independence of

\hat{\phi}_{1},\ldots,\hat{\phi}_{n}
.

The proof of Theorem
1.3
is completed. ∎

4.4. Jet bundles

We keep the same notation as in the previous subsections.
Let

Let

Because of the flat structure of the logarithmic tangent

are identified. Therefore we have

(4.5)

J_{k}(A\times{\mathop{\mathrm{Lie}}}(A))\cong A\times{\mathop{\mathrm{Lie}}}(A)\times J_{k,A}\times J_{k,A}.

Let
\Delta_{k}\subset J_{k,A}\times J_{k,A}
be the diagonal,
and let

be the

-jet
lift of
\hskip 1.0pt{\widehat{\mathrm{ex}}}_{A}f

(4.6)

J_{k}(\hskip 1.0pt{\widehat{\mathrm{ex}}}_{A}f):z\in{\mathbf{C}}\to(\exp_{A}f(z),f(z),J_{k,f}(z),J_{k,f}(z))\in A\times{\mathop{\mathrm{Lie}}}(A)\times\Delta_{k},

We put

\displaystyle{\widehat{J}_{k,A}}

\displaystyle={\mathop{\mathrm{Lie}}}(A)\times J_{k,A}~\cong{\mathbf{C}}^{n}\times{\mathbf{C}}^{nk}\quad(\hbox{extended jet part}),

\displaystyle X_{k}(\hskip 1.0pt{\widehat{\mathrm{ex}}}_{A}f)

\displaystyle=\overline{J_{k}(\hskip 1.0pt{\widehat{\mathrm{ex}}}_{A}f)({\mathbf{C}})}^{\mathrm{Zar}}\subset A\times{\widehat{J}_{k,A}}.

We define the

by

\widehat{I}_{k}:X_{k}(\hskip 1.0pt{\widehat{\mathrm{ex}}}_{A}f)~(\subset A\times{\widehat{J}_{k,A}})\longrightarrow{\widehat{J}_{k,A}},

which will play the role of the jet projection

(cf.
[

]

[

]

Let

be a big line bundle over a

of

We take a compactification
\hskip 3.0pt{\overline{\hskip-3.0pt\widehat{J}}_{k,A}}
of

For

4.5.
A
-action

We consider an
A
-action on

by

(a,(x,v,w))\in A\times\left(A\times{\mathop{\mathrm{Lie}}}(A)\times J_{k,A}\right)\to(a+x,v,w)\in A\times{\mathop{\mathrm{Lie}}}(A)\times J_{k,A}.

Lemma 4.12
.

With the notation above,
\mathrm{St}(X_{k}(\hskip 1.0pt{\widehat{\mathrm{ex}}}_{A}f))^{0}\not=\{0\}

Proof.

If otherwise,
\mathrm{St}(X_{k}(\hskip 1.0pt{\widehat{\mathrm{ex}}}_{A}f))^{0}=\{0\}
.
We consider the

[

The semi-abelian variety

\displaystyle\quad+l_{0}\sum_{i}N_{1}(r,J_{k+1}(\hskip 1.0pt{\widehat{\mathrm{ex}}}_{A}f)^{*}J_{1}(D_{i}))+S_{\exp_{A}f}(r)

(cf.

We have

(iii) The case of

(a) The optimality of (

Let

6. Remarks to affine algebraic curves and other domains

6.1. Affine algebraic curves

In this section
A
denotes a semi-abelian variety.

Let
R
be a complex affine algebraic curve and let
f:R\to{\mathop{\mathrm{Lie}}}(A)
be
a holomorphic curve.
We may consider
\exp_{A}f:R\to A
and

\hskip 1.0pt{\widehat{\mathrm{ex}}}_{A}f:R\to A\times{\mathop{\mathrm{Lie}}}(A)
.

In general for a holomorphic curve
g:R\to A
the arguments up to obtaining
an estimate such as (
4.15
) work (cf.
[
20
]
Chap. 6),
but further to advance to the estimates of the
2nd Main Theorem
1.6
with counting functions truncated at level
one, we need to lift
g
to
\tilde{g}:R\to{\mathop{\mathrm{Lie}}}(A)
,
which does not exists in general, since
R
is not simply connected.
But in the present case we begin with a holomorphic curve

f:R\to{\mathop{\mathrm{Lie}}}(A)
, which is a lift of
\exp_{A}f:R\to A
.
Therefore we can advance the arguments further there.

Let
\bar{R}
be the compactification of
R
by adding a finite number of
points. To study
\hskip 1.0pt{\widehat{\mathrm{ex}}}_{A}f
we may localize the problem about
an infinite point
a\in\bar{R}\setminus R
; we take a disk neighborhood

\Delta
of
a
in
\bar{R}
. Then
\Delta^{*}=\Delta\setminus\{a\}

is a punctured disk, and the analysis of transcendental properties of
\hskip 1.0pt{\widehat{\mathrm{ex}}}_{A}f

is reduced to that of the restriction
\hskip 1.0pt{\widehat{\mathrm{ex}}}_{A}f|_{\Delta^{*}}

(see the next).

6.2. Punctured disk

Let
\Delta^{*}
be a punctured disk.
As mentioned above, although
\Delta^{*}
is not simply connected,
it does not cause a difficulty here, since we give in first a holomorphic curve

(6.1)

f:z\in\Delta^{*}\longrightarrow(f_{1}(z),f_{2}(z),\ldots,f_{n}(z))\in{\mathbf{C}}^{n}\cong{\mathop{\mathrm{Lie}}}(A).

For a notational convenience we put the puncture at infinity and
introduce a coordinate
z
such that

\Delta^{*}=\{|z|>1\},\qquad\Delta=\Delta^{*}\cup\{\infty\}.

Let
F(z)
be one of
f_{j}(z)
.
Then
F(z)
is expanded to a Laurent series

(6.2)

F(z)=\sum_{\nu>0}c_{\nu}z^{\nu}+\sum_{\nu\leq 0}c_{\nu}z^{\nu}=F_{\mathrm{m}}(z)+F_{\mathrm{b}}(z).

Fix
r_{0}>1
. Then
F_{\mathrm{b}}(z)
and their derivatives
are bounded in
\{|z|\geq r_{0}\}
, and
F_{\mathrm{m}}(z)
is the
main part of the expansion:

(6.3)

\frac{d^{k}}{dz^{k}}F(z)=\frac{d^{k}}{dz^{k}}F_{\mathrm{m}}(z)+O(1),\qquad|z|\geq r_{0},~k\geq 0.

Note that
F_{\mathrm{m}}(z)
is holomorphic in
{\mathbf{C}}
.
Applying the key Lemma
2.5
for
F_{\mathrm{m}}(z)
, we deduce
the key Lemma
2.5
for
f
,
\exp_{A}f
and
r>r_{0}
.
We can then deduce the 2nd Main Theorem
1.6
for

\hskip 1.0pt{\widehat{\mathrm{ex}}}_{A}f:\Delta^{*}\to A\times{\mathop{\mathrm{Lie}}}(A)
and
J_{k}(\hskip 1.0pt{\widehat{\mathrm{ex}}}_{A}f)
as well
for
r>r_{0}
.

6.3. Finite disk

We consider the case of a disk of
{\mathbf{C}}
with finite radius, to say,
the unit disk
\Delta
.
Let
f:\Delta\to{\mathop{\mathrm{Lie}}}(A)
be a holomorphic curve.
In this hyperbolic case, to make the proofs of key Lemma
2.5

and Lemma on logarithmic derivatives to work at least for a sequence

r_{\nu}\nearrow 1~(\nu\to\infty)
,
we need a technical condition
on the growth of the order function
T_{\exp_{A}f}(r)
such that

(6.4)

\underset{r\to 1}{\overline{\lim}}\;\frac{T_{\exp_{A}f}(r)}{\log\frac{1}{1-r}}=\infty

(cf. Nevanlinna
[
12
]
Chap. VI, Hayman
[
9
]
§2.3).
Under this condition the 2nd Main Theorem
1.6
for

\hskip 1.0pt{\widehat{\mathrm{ex}}}_{A}f:\Delta\to A\times{\mathop{\mathrm{Lie}}}(A)
is deduced.

6.4. Open Riemann surfaces

Let
R
be an open Riemann surface.
The generalizations of Nevanlinna theory for meromorphic functions
on
R
, holomorphic maps from
R
to another Riemann surface and
holomorphic curves from
R
into
{\mathbf{P}^{n}(\mathbf{C})}
are
classical (cf., e.g., Sario-Noshiro
[
25
]
,
Wu
[
29
]
).
There one uses a finite (hyperbolic case) or infinite (parabolic case)
exhaustion function
\tau:R\to[0,r_{0})
with
r_{0}\leq\infty

such that
\tau
is harmonic outside a compact subset of
R
.

Similarly it is formally possible to extend the 2nd Main Theorem
1.6

for holomorphic curves
f:R\to{\mathop{\mathrm{Lie}}}(A)
and
\hskip 1.0pt{\widehat{\mathrm{ex}}}_{A}f:R\to A\times{\mathop{\mathrm{Lie}}}(A)
.
There we use the differential
{\partial}\tau
, holomorphic where

\tau
is harmonic. For a logarithmic
1
-form
\omega
on
A

we take the ratio
F=(\exp_{A}f)^{*}\omega/{\partial}\tau
,
which may have poles at zeros of
{\partial}\tau
.
The counting functions of those zeros is the counting function

E_{R}(r)
of the Euler numbers of
\{\tau<r\}
, which appears
in the estimates (
4.15
) and (
1.7
) under a growth assumption such
as (
6.4
) with
r_{0}=1
in hyperbolic case (i.e.,
r_{0}<\infty
):

(6.5)

\displaystyle T_{J_{k}(\hskip 1.0pt{\widehat{\mathrm{ex}}}_{A}f)}(r,\omega_{\bar{Z}})=N_{l_{0}}(r,J_{k}(\hskip 1.0pt{\widehat{\mathrm{ex}}}_{A}f)^{*}Z)+C(k,l_{0})E_{R}(r)

\displaystyle\hskip 120.0pt+O(\log^{+}T_{\exp_{A}f}(r))+O(\log r)~~||,

(6.6)

\displaystyle(1-\varepsilon)T_{J_{k}(\hskip 1.0pt{\widehat{\mathrm{ex}}}_{A}f)}(r,\omega_{\bar{Z},J_{k}(\hskip 1.0pt{\widehat{\mathrm{ex}}}_{A}f)})\leq N_{1}(r,J_{k}(\hskip 1.0pt{\widehat{\mathrm{ex}}}_{A}f)^{*}Z)+C^{\prime}(\varepsilon,k)E_{R}(r)+O(\log r)~~||_{\varepsilon}.

Here
C(k,l_{0})
and
C^{\prime}(\varepsilon,k)
are
positive constants such that
C(k,l_{0}),C^{\prime}(\varepsilon,k)\nearrow\infty
as

and
\varepsilon\searrow 0
; there are no estimates for

C(k,l_{0}),C^{\prime}(\varepsilon,k)
.
Therefore, in order to obtain a meaningful consequence
we need a technical condition
(besides (
6.4
) in hyperbolic case) such that

\underset{r\to r_{0}}{\overline{\lim}}\;\frac{E_{R}(r)}{T_{\exp_{A}f}(r)}=0.

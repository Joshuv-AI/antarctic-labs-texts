The Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund law of large numbers for exchangeable arrays
Laurent Davezies, Xavier D’Haultfœuille, Yannick Guyonvarch

Abstract
We show a Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund law of large numbers for jointly, dissociated exchangeable arrays, in L r L^{r} ( r ∈ ( 0 , 2 ) r\in(0,2) ) and almost surely. Then, we obtain a law of iterated logarithm for such arrays under a weaker moment condition than the existing one. Keywords: exchangeable arrays, Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund law of large numbers, law of iterated logarithm.



1
Introduction

Random variables with multidimensional indexes are useful to model interactions between several units of a population. An example is international trade, where each observation corresponds to a pair of countries, one exporting and the other importing. Another example is network formation, where binary variables between pairs of units (e.g., individuals) measure whether they share a link or not. Multi-indexed random variables are also useful in case of multiway clustering. For instance, wages of individuals may be indexed by geographical areas and sectors of activity, reflecting potential dependence in wages within the same area or sector.

There are two important properties for such arrays of random variables. The first, dissociation, was introduced by
McGinley and Sibson 1975
. It generalizes mutual independence, by allowing for dependence when elements of the array share at least one common component in their respective indexes. The second, joint exchangeability, was introduced by
Silverman 1976
, and is very similar to exchangeability for sequences. Taken together, these two conditions turn out to be sufficient to establish strong laws of large numbers, central limit theorems and weak convergence of empirical processes under the exact same moment conditions as in the i.i.d. case. We refer respectively to
Eagleson and Weber 1978
,
Silverman 1976
and
Davezies et al. 2021
for proofs of these statements.

The aim of this note is to show that we can also extend the Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund law of large numbers to dissociated, jointly exchangeable arrays. Recall that this law extends the usual law of large numbers by relating the convergence rate of partial sums to moment conditions on the variables. As with i.i.d. data, our result holds both in
L^{r}
(
r\in(0,2)
) and almost surely. The proof in
L^{r}
for the more difficult case (
r>1
) is based on a symmetrization lemma established in
Davezies et al. 2021
. Almost-sure convergence follows using the so-called Aldous-Hoover-Kallenberg (AHK) representation
(
Aldous 1981
;
Hoover 1979
;
Kallenberg 1989
)
and a similar strategy as that used for U-statistics
(
Giné and Zinn 1992
)
. As a consequence of our result, we obtain a law of iterated logarithm for dissociated, jointly exchangeable arrays with a finite second moment. We thus improve upon
Scott and Huggins 1985
, who obtain the same result under the existence of a moment of order
2+\delta
for some
\delta>0
.

We first present in Section
2
the set-up and the main results. Section
3
is devoted to the proof of the main theorem in the difficult case (
r>1
). The supplementary material includes in particular the proofs of the easier part (
0<r<1
) and of the law of iterated logarithm.

2
The set-up and results

We first define formally dissociated and jointly exchangeable arrays. Let
\mathbb{N}^{+}=\mathbb{N}\backslash\{0\}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
,
\text{card}(A)
denote the cardinal of a set
A
and for any
A\subset\mathbb{N}^{k}
,

\overline{A}=\left\{(a_{1},...,a_{k})\in A:\text{card}(\{a_{1},...,a_{k}\})=k\right\}.

Then, we denote by
\mathbb{I}_{k}=\overline{\mathbb{N}^{+k}}
the set of
k
-tuples of
\mathbb{N}^{+}
without repetition. Similarly, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, we let
\mathbb{I}_{n,k}=\overline{\{1,...,n\}^{k}}
. For any
A\subset\mathbb{N}^{+}
, we use
\mathfrak{S}(A)
to denote the set of permutations on
A
. For any
\bm{i}=(i_{1},...,i_{k})\in\mathbb{N}^{+k}
and
\pi\in\mathfrak{S}(\mathbb{N}^{+})
, we let
\pi(\bm{i})=(\pi(i_{1}),...,\pi(i_{k}))
. Finally, we let
\bm{k}=(1,...,k)
.

Definition 1
(Dissociated and exchangeable arrays)

X:=(X_{\bm{i}})_{\bm{i}\in\mathbb{I}_{k}}
is a dissociated array if for any
A,B
disjoint subsets of
\mathbb{N}^{+}
with
\min(\text{card}(A),

\text{card}(B))\geq k
,
(X_{\bm{i}})_{\bm{i}\in\overline{A^{k}}}
is independent of
(X_{\bm{i}})_{\bm{i}\in\overline{B^{k}}}
.

X
is a jointly exchangeable array if for any
\pi\in\mathfrak{S}(\mathbb{N}^{+})
,
X\overset{d}{=}(X_{\pi(\bm{i})})_{\bm{i}\in\mathbb{I}_{k}}
.

For
k=1
, a jointly exchangeable array is an exchangeable sequence of random variables whereas a dissociated jointly exchangeable array is a sequence of i.i.d. random variables. Otherwise (
k>1
), jointly exchangeable arrays may exhibit more complex forms of dependence: with
k=2
,
X_{(i_{1},i_{2})}
and
X_{(i_{1},j_{2})}
, but also
X_{(i_{1},i_{2})}
and
X_{(j_{2},i_{1})}
may be dependent, for instance. In the international trade example, exports from China to the USA may be correlated with exports from the USA to France, simply because the USA are a large country.

Note that with multiway clustering, the different components of the index (e.g., areas and sectors in the wage example above) do not belong to the same population. Separate exchangeability, where the permutation used may differ for all components of the index, is then better suited to such data than joint exchangeability. However, joint exchangeability is more general so the result below also applies to separately exchangeable arrays.

Theorem 1

Let
X
be a jointly exchangeable array. Then:

1.

If
\mathbb{E}(|X_{\bm{k}}|^{r})<\infty
for
1\leq r<2
and
X
is dissociated,

\frac{1}{n^{k-1+1/r}}\sum_{\bm{i}\in\mathbb{I}_{n,k}}\left(X_{\bm{i}}-\mathbb{E}(X_{\bm{k}})\right)\rightarrow 0\text{ in }L^{r}\text{ and almost surely (a.s.)}.

2.

If
\mathbb{E}(|X_{\bm{k}}|^{r})<\infty
for
0<r<1
,
n^{-k/r}\sum_{\bm{i}\in\mathbb{I}_{n,k}}X_{\bm{i}}\rightarrow 0
in
L^{r}
and a.s.

The case
0<r<1
follows by simply noting that existing proofs
(
Gut 2013
;
Giné and Zinn 1992
, see)
in fact only rely on the variables being identically distributed. The case
r=1
is Theorem 3 in
Eagleson and Weber 1978
. The case
r>1
is not as straightforward however. The key ingredients for convergence in
L^{r}
are a symmetrization lemma established in
Davezies et al. 2021
, see Lemma
1
below, and the Khintchine inequality. Almost-sure convergence follows using a similar strategy as for U-statistics. To understand the similarities and differences with U-statistics, let us consider the case
k=3
. By the AHK representation of dissociated and jointly exchangeable arrays, there exists a function
\tau
and i.i.d. and uniform
[0,1]
variables
(U_{\{i\}})_{i\geq 1}
,
(U_{\{i,i_{2}\}})_{(i,i_{2})\in\mathbb{I}_{2}}
and
(U_{\{i,i_{2},i_{3}\}})_{(i,i_{2},i_{3})\in\mathbb{I}_{3}}
such that for all
\bm{i}=(i_{1},i_{2},i_{3})\in\mathbb{I}_{3}
,

X_{\bm{i}}=\tau\left(U_{\{i_{1}\}},U_{\{i_{2}\}},U_{\{i_{3}\}},U_{\{i_{1},i_{2}\}},U_{\{i_{1},i_{3}\}},U_{\{i_{2},i_{3}\}},U_{\{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}\}}\right).

This result shows that
X_{\bm{i}}
is close to, but distinct from, a U-statistic, where the last four variables would not appear. Accordingly, we use a decomposition akin to but different from the Hoeffding decomposition in the proof. Specifically, let us define

\displaystyle H_{0}(X)_{\bm{i}}=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(X_{\bm{i}}),

\displaystyle H_{1}(X)_{\bm{i}}=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(X_{\bm{i}}|U_{\{i_{1}\}}\right)+\mathbb{E}\left(X_{\bm{i}}|U_{\{i_{2}\}}\right)+\mathbb{E}\left(X_{\bm{i}}|U_{\{i_{3}\}}\right)-3\mathbb{E}(X_{\bm{i}}),

\displaystyle H_{2}(X)_{\bm{i}}=

\displaystyle Q_{(1,1,0)}(X)_{\bm{i}}+Q_{(1,0,1)}(X)_{\bm{i}}+Q_{(0,1,1)}(X)_{\bm{i}},

\displaystyle H_{3}(X)_{\bm{i}}=

\displaystyle X_{\bm{i}}-\mathbb{E}\left(X_{\bm{i}}|U_{\{i_{1}\}},U_{\{i_{2}\}},U_{\{i_{1},i_{2}\}}\right)-\mathbb{E}\left(X_{\bm{i}}|U_{\{i_{1}\}},U_{\{i_{3}\}},U_{\{i_{1},i_{3}\}}\right)

\displaystyle-\mathbb{E}\left(X_{\bm{i}}|U_{\{i_{2}\}},U_{\{i_{3}\}},U_{\{i_{2},i_{3}\}}\right)+\mathbb{E}\left(X_{\bm{i}}|U_{\{i_{1}\}}\right)

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\left(X_{\bm{i}}|U_{\{i_{2}\}}\right)+\mathbb{E}\left(X_{\bm{i}}|U_{\{i_{3}\}}\right)-\mathbb{E}\left(X_{\bm{i}}\right),

where
Q_{(1,1,0)}(X)_{\bm{i}}=\mathbb{E}\left(X_{\bm{i}}|U_{\{i_{1}\}},U_{\{i_{2}\}},U_{\{i_{1},i_{2}\}}\right)-\mathbb{E}\left(X_{\bm{i}}|U_{\{i_{1}\}}\right)-\mathbb{E}\left(X_{\bm{i}}|U_{\{i_{2}\}}\right)+\mathbb{E}(X_{\bm{i}})
and
Q_{(1,0,1)}(X)_{\bm{i}}
and
Q_{(0,1,1)}(X)_{\bm{i}}
are defined similarly. Then,

X_{\bm{i}}=H_{0}(X)_{\bm{i}}+H_{1}(X)_{\bm{i}}+H_{2}(X)_{\bm{i}}+H_{3}(X)_{\bm{i}},

(1)

where the four terms can be shown to be orthogonal in
L^{2}
. Decomposition (
1
) allows us to extend
Giné and Zinn 1992
’s approach to our set-up.

Remark 1

The proof shows that a result similar to Point 1 holds without dissociation. The only difference, then, is that one should replace
\mathbb{E}(X_{\bm{k}})
by
\mathbb{E}(X_{\bm{k}}|U_{\emptyset})
in the statement of the theorem, where
U_{\emptyset}
is an additional argument in the AHK decomposition.

Remark 2

For
k\geq 2
, the function
r\mapsto(k-1+1/r)\mathds{1}\left\{r<1\right\}+k/r\mathds{1}\left\{r\geq 1\right\}
is continuous but not differentiable at
r=1
. There is therefore a kink at
r=1
in the rate of convergence.

Remark 3

The result is sharp in the following sense: there exist exchangeable arrays
(X_{\bm{i}})_{\bm{i}\in\mathbb{I}_{k}}
such that almost-sure convergence fails to hold, even though
\mathbb{E}(|X_{\bm{k}}|^{r^{\prime}})<\infty
for all
r^{\prime}\in(0,r)
. To see this, consider
X_{\bm{i}}=V_{i_{1}}
, where the
(V_{i})_{i\geq 1}
are i.i.d.
\alpha
-stable variables, with stability parameter
r\in(0,2)
(and mean 0 when
r>1
). Then
E[|X_{\bm{k}}|^{r^{\prime}}]<\infty
for all
r^{\prime}\in(0,r)
. On the other hand, considering here the case
r>1
(the case
r\leq 1
can be treated similarly),

\frac{1}{n^{k-1+1/r}}\sum_{\bm{i}\in\mathbb{I}_{n,k}}(X_{\bm{i}}-\mathbb{E}(X_{\bm{k}}))=\frac{(n-1)!}{(n-k)!n^{k-1}}\frac{1}{n^{1/r}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}.

Then, because
n^{-1/r}\sum_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}V_{i}
,
n^{-(k-1+1/r)}\sum_{\bm{i}\in\mathbb{I}_{n,k}}X_{\bm{i}}-\mathbb{E}[X_{\bm{k}}]
does not converge to 0. Still, weaker moment conditions involving projections were shown to be sufficient for U-statistics
(
Teicher 1998
)
. Similar conditions involving expectations conditional on unobserved terms
(U_{\{i\}})_{i\geq 1}
are likely to be sufficient here as well.

Theorem
1
, or rather a slight extension of it, implies the following law of iterated logarithm.

Theorem 2

Let
X
be a dissociated, jointly exchangeable array such that
\mathbb{E}(X_{\bm{k}}^{2})<\infty
and let

V:=\frac{1}{[(k-1)!]^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi\in\mathfrak{S}(\{1,...,k\})\\
\pi^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{S}(\{1,k+1,...,2k-1\})\end{subarray}}\mathbb{C}ov\left(X_{\pi(\bm{k})},X_{\pi(1,k+1,...,2k-1)}\right).

Then,
V\geq 0
and

\lim\sup_{n\to\infty}\pm\frac{\sum_{\bm{i}\in\mathbb{I}_{n,k}}\left(X_{\bm{i}}-\mathbb{E}(X_{\bm{k}})\right)}{\sqrt{2n^{2k-1}\log\log n}}=\sqrt{V}\;\text{a.s.}

The result improves upon that of
Scott and Huggins 1985
by only requiring
\mathbb{E}(X_{\bm{k}}^{2})<\infty
, exactly as with i.i.d. variables. Its proof can be summarized as follows (here again with
k=3
). First, we use Decomposition (
1
). The term
\sum_{\bm{i}\in\mathbb{I}_{n,k}}H_{1}(X)_{\bm{i}}
corresponds to a sum over i.i.d. terms, on which we apply the usual law of iterated logarithm. Next, it turns out that when
k\geq 2
, the proof of Theorem
1
extends to
r=2
for the terms
\sum_{\bm{i}\in\mathbb{I}_{n,k}}H_{2}(X)_{\bm{i}}
and
\sum_{\bm{i}\in\mathbb{I}_{n,k}}H_{3}(X)_{\bm{i}}
. As a result, these (properly normalized) terms are asymptotically negligible.

1

2

\bm{0}=(0,...,0)\in\mathbb{N}^{k}

\bm{1}=(1,...,1)\in\mathbb{N}^{k}

be the

at the first non-null entry of
\bm{e}
,
i_{2}
at the second non-null entry of
\bm{e}
etc. and 0 elsewhere. For instance, if
k=4
,
j=2
,
\bm{e}=(0,1,0,1)

\bm{i}=(5,3)

\bm{i}^{\bm{e}}=(0,5,0,3)
. Finally, for any

, we let
\overrightarrow{A}=\{\bm{i}\in\overline{A}:i_{1}<...<i_{q}\}
. We also use

.

\mathbb{E}(X_{\bm{k}})=0

Preliminary results

(Symmetrization lemma)

Let
r>1
and
X
be an exchangeable and dissociated array such that
\mathbb{E}\left(\left|X_{\bm{k}}\right|^{r}\right)<\infty
and
\mathbb{E}\left(X_{\bm{k}}\right)=0

(\varepsilon_{A})_{A\subset\mathbb{N}}

X
. There exists
D_{r,k}

and

X^{j}_{\bm{k}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}X_{\bm{k}}
and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\sum_{\bm{i}\in\mathbb{I}_{n,k}}X_{\bm{i}}\right|^{r}\right]

\displaystyle\leq D_{r,k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{\bm{e}\in\mathcal{E}_{j}}\mathbb{E}\left(\left|\sum_{\bm{i}\in\mathbb{I}_{n,k}}\varepsilon_{\{\bm{i}\odot\bm{e}\}^{+}}X^{j}_{\bm{i}}\right|^{r}\right).

(X_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{I}_{k}}
. It states that there exists a function
\tau
and i.i.d. uniform
[0,1]
variables
(U_{A})_{A\subset\cup_{r=1}^{k}\mathbb{I}_{k}}
(and
U_{\emptyset}=1
a.s.) such that
X_{\bm{i}}=\tau\left(\left(U_{\{\bm{e}\odot\bm{i}\}^{+}}\right)_{\bm{e}\in\mathcal{E}}\right)
. In the definition of
\tau
, the arguments are implicitly ordered by choosing a specific ordering on

. We can now derive decompositions similar to the Hoeffding decomposition of U-statistics. Specifically, consider
Z_{\bm{i}}=f_{\bm{i}}(X_{\bm{i}})
for any measurable maps
(f_{\bm{i}})_{\bm{i}\in\mathbb{I}_{k}}
. As soon as
\mathbb{E}|Z_{\bm{i}}|<\infty
for all
\bm{i}\in\mathbb{I}_{k}
, we define, for all
\bm{e}\in\mathcal{E}
,
P_{\bm{e}}(Z)_{\bm{i}}=\mathbb{E}\left(Z_{\bm{i}}|(U_{\{\bm{i}\odot\bm{e}^{\prime}\}^{+}})_{\bm{e}^{\prime}\leq\bm{e}}\right)
. We then let
H_{0}(Z)_{\bm{i}}=Q_{\bm{0}}(Z)_{\bm{i}}=P_{\bm{0}}(Z)_{\bm{i}}=\mathbb{E}(Z_{\bm{i}})_{\bm{i}}
for any
\bm{i}\in\mathbb{I}_{n,k}
. For

, let

Q_{\bm{e}}(Z)_{\bm{i}}=\sum_{\bm{e}^{\prime}\leq\bm{e}}(-1)^{\sum_{j=1}^{k}e_{j}-\sum_{j=1}^{k}e^{\prime}_{j}}P_{\bm{e}^{\prime}}(Z)_{\bm{i}}

. The following lemma gives three key properties on
P_{\bm{e}}(Z)_{\bm{i}},Q_{\bm{e}}(Z)_{\bm{i}}
and
H_{\ell}(Z)_{\bm{i}}
. In particular, Equality (
2
) may be seen as a Hoeffding decomposition of

(Three properties of

We have

\displaystyle Z_{\bm{i}}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}H_{\ell}(Z)_{\bm{i}},

(2)

\displaystyle H_{\ell}=\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}(-1)^{\ell-j}\binom{k-j}{\ell-j}\sum_{\bm{e}\in\mathcal{E}_{j}}P_{\bm{e}}.

(3)

3.2
Convergence in
L^{r}

Using Lemma
1
and the Khintchine inequality conditional on the
(X^{j}_{\bm{i}})_{\bm{i}\in\mathbb{I}_{k}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\sum_{\bm{i}\in\mathbb{I}_{n,k}}X_{\bm{i}}\right|^{r}\right]

\displaystyle\leq D_{r,k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{\bm{e}\in\mathcal{E}_{j}}\mathbb{E}\left(\left|\sum_{\bm{i}\in\mathbb{I}_{n,k}}\varepsilon_{\{\bm{i}\odot\bm{e}\}^{+}}X^{j}_{\bm{i}}\right|^{r}\right)

\displaystyle=D_{r,k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{\bm{e}\in\mathcal{E}_{j}}\mathbb{E}\left(\left|\sum_{\bm{i}\in\mathbb{I}_{n,j}}\varepsilon_{\{\bm{i}\}}\sum_{\bm{i}^{\prime}\in\overline{(\{1,...,n\}\backslash\{\bm{i}\})^{k-j}}}X^{j}_{\bm{i}^{\bm{e}}+\bm{i}^{\prime\bm{1}-\bm{e}}}\right|^{r}\right)

\displaystyle\leq D_{r,k}B_{r}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{\bm{e}\in\mathcal{E}_{j}}\mathbb{E}\left(\left|\sum_{\bm{i}\in\mathbb{I}_{n,j}}\left(\sum_{\bm{i}^{\prime}\in\overline{(\{1,...,n\}\backslash\{\bm{i}\})^{k-j}}}X^{j}_{\bm{i}^{\bm{e}}+\bm{i}^{\prime\bm{1}-\bm{e}}}\right)^{2}\right|^{r/2}\right),

where we recall that
\bm{i}^{\bm{e}}
is defined at the beginning of the section and
B_{r}
is a constant depending only on
r
. For each
j=1,...,k
and
\bm{e}\in\mathcal{E}_{j}
, use
(a+b)^{2}\leq 2a^{2}+2b^{2}
and
|a+b|^{r/2}\leq|a|^{r/2}+|b|^{r/2}
to deduce:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\left|\sum_{\bm{i}\in\mathbb{I}_{n,j}}\left(\sum_{\bm{i}^{\prime}\in\overline{(\{1,...,n\}\backslash\{\bm{i}\})^{k-j}}}X^{j}_{\bm{i}^{\bm{e}}+\bm{i}^{\prime\bm{1}-\bm{e}}}\right)^{2}\right|^{r/2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{r/2}\mathbb{E}\left(\left|\sum_{\bm{i}\in\mathbb{I}_{n,j}}\left(\sum_{\bm{i}^{\prime}\in\overline{(\{1,...,n\}\backslash\{\bm{i}\})^{k-j}}}X^{j}_{\bm{i}^{\bm{e}}+\bm{i}^{\prime\bm{1}-\bm{e}}}\mathds{1}\{|X^{j}_{\bm{i}^{\bm{e}}+\bm{i}^{\prime\bm{1}-\bm{e}}}|\leq M\}\right)^{2}\right|^{r/2}\right)

\displaystyle+2^{r/2}\mathbb{E}\left(\left|\sum_{\bm{i}\in\mathbb{I}_{n,j}}\left(\sum_{\bm{i}^{\prime}\in\overline{(\{1,...,n\}\backslash\{\bm{i}\})^{k-j}}}X^{j}_{\bm{i}^{\bm{e}}+\bm{i}^{\prime\bm{1}-\bm{e}}}\mathds{1}\{|X^{j}_{\bm{i}^{\bm{e}}+\bm{i}^{\prime\bm{1}-\bm{e}}}|>M\}\right)^{2}\right|^{r/2}\right).

By Jensen’s inequality
\mathbb{E}(|V|^{r/2})\leq\mathbb{E}(|V|)^{r/2}
, the first term can be bounded by
2^{r/2}n^{(k-j/2)r}M^{r}
. Using
|a+b|^{r/2}\leq|a|^{r/2}+|b|^{r/2}
, Jensen’s inequality
\left(\sum_{a\in A}|V_{a}|\right)^{r}\leq\text{card}(A)^{-1}\sum_{a\in A}\text{card}(A)^{r}|V_{a}|^{r}
and
\mathbb{E}\left(|X^{j}_{\bm{i}^{\bm{e}}+\bm{i}^{\prime\bm{1}-\bm{e}}}|^{r}\mathds{1}\{|X^{j}_{\bm{i}^{\bm{e}}+\bm{i}^{\prime\bm{1}-\bm{e}}}|>M\}\right)=\mathbb{E}\left(|X_{\bm{k}}|^{r}\mathds{1}\{|X_{\bm{k}}|>M\}\right)
, the second term can be bounded by
2^{r/2}n^{j}n^{(k-j)r}\mathbb{E}(|X_{\bm{k}}|^{r}\mathds{1}\{|X_{\bm{k}}|>M\})
. As a result,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n^{(k-1)r+1}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\sum_{\bm{i}\in\mathbb{I}_{n,k}}X_{\bm{i}}\right|^{r}\right]

\displaystyle\leq D_{r,k}B_{r}2^{r/2}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\binom{k}{j}\left(n^{r(1-j/2)-1}M^{r}+n^{(j-1)(1-r)}\mathbb{E}\left(|X_{\bm{k}}|^{r}\mathds{1}\{|X_{\bm{k}}|>M\}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq D_{r,k}B_{r}2^{r/2}(2^{k}-1)\left(n^{r/2-1}M^{r}+\mathbb{E}\left(|X_{\bm{k}}|^{r}\mathds{1}\{|X_{\bm{k}}|>M\}\right)\right).

Considering
M
sufficiently large to ensure that
\mathbb{E}\left(|X_{\bm{k}}|^{r}\mathds{1}\{|X_{\bm{k}}|>M\}\right)
is arbitrarily small, we deduce that
\lim\sup_{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|n^{-d}\sum_{\bm{i}\in\mathbb{I}_{n,k}}X_{\bm{i}}\right|^{r}\right]=0
.

3.3
Almost-sure convergence

Up to considering
Z_{\bm{i}}=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}(\{\bm{k}\})}[X_{\bm{i}_{\sigma}}-\mathbb{E}(X_{\bm{k}})]/k!
instead of
X_{\bm{i}}
, we can assume without loss of generality that
\mathbb{E}(X_{\bm{k}})=0
and that
X
is symmetric. Then:

\sum_{\bm{i}\in\mathbb{I}_{n,k}}X_{\bm{i}}=k!\sum_{\bm{i}\in\overrightarrow{\mathbb{I}_{n,k}}}X_{\bm{i}}=k!\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\sum_{\bm{i}\in\overrightarrow{\mathbb{I}_{n,k}}}H_{\ell}(X)_{\bm{i}},

where we recall the notation
\overrightarrow{A}=\{\bm{i}\in\overline{A}:i_{1}<...<i_{q}\}
. Following the U-statistic’s terminology, we say that
X
is degenerate of order
\ell-1
if
H_{\ell}(X)_{\bm{i}}
is not constant whereas for all
\ell^{\prime}\in\{1,...,\ell-1\}
,
H_{\ell^{\prime}}(X)_{\bm{i}}
is constant (and then equal to 0 as we assumed
\mathbb{E}(X_{1,...k})=0
).

Degenerate arrays of order
k-1
.

We first assume that
X
is degenerate of order
k-1
, that is
X_{\bm{i}}=H_{k}(X)_{\bm{i}}
. By Kronecker’s lemma, it is sufficient to show that

\sum_{j=k}^{n}\frac{1}{j^{d}}\sum_{\bm{i}\in\overrightarrow{\mathbb{I}_{j-1,k-1}}}X_{\bm{i},j}\to 0\quad\text{a.s.}

Let
T_{j}=j^{-d}\sum_{\bm{i}\in\overrightarrow{\mathbb{I}_{j-1,k-1}}}X_{\bm{i},j}
and
\mathcal{F}_{j}=\sigma\left(U_{A};A\subset\{1,...,j\},\text{card}(A)\leq k\right)
. Since
X
is degenerate of order
k-1
, we have
\mathbb{E}\left(X_{\bm{k}}|\left(U_{A}\right)_{A\subset\{1,...,k-1\}}\right)=0
. Then,
\mathbb{E}\left(T_{j}|\mathcal{F}_{j-1}\right)=0
, meaning that
(T_{j},\mathcal{F}_{j})
is a martingale difference. Hence,
\left(\sum_{j=\ell}^{n}T_{j},\mathcal{F}_{n}\right)
is a martingale. We then just have to prove that
\sup_{n}\mathbb{E}\left|\sum_{j=k}^{n}T_{j}\right|<\infty

(
Gut 2013
, see for instance Chapter 10, Theorem 12.2 in)
. To this end, we use

\mathbb{E}\left|\sum_{j=k}^{n}T_{j}\right|\leq A_{n}+B_{n}
, with

A_{n}=\mathbb{E}\left|\sum_{j=k}^{n}j^{-d}\sum_{\bm{i}\in\overrightarrow{\mathbb{I}_{j-1,k-1}}}X_{\bm{i},j}\right.

\left.\mathds{1}\{|X_{\bm{i},j}|\leq j^{d}\}\right|
and
B_{n}=\mathbb{E}\left|\sum_{j=k}^{n}j^{-d}\sum_{\bm{i}\in\overrightarrow{\mathbb{I}_{j-1,k-1}}}X_{\bm{i},j}\mathds{1}\{|X_{\bm{i},j}|>j^{d}\}\right|
.
We show that the suprema of both terms are finite. First,

\displaystyle B_{n}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=k}^{n}j^{-d}\frac{(j-1)!}{(j-k)!}\mathbb{E}\left(\left|X_{\bm{k}}\right|\mathds{1}\{|X_{\bm{k}}|>j^{d}\}\right)

(4)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left(\left|X_{\bm{k}}\right|\sum_{j=k}^{n}j^{-1/r}\mathds{1}\{|X_{\bm{k}}|>j^{d}\}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left(\left|X_{\bm{k}}\right|\mathds{1}\{|X_{\bm{k}}|>(k-1)^{d}\}\int_{k-1}^{|X_{\bm{k}}|^{1/d}}t^{-1/r}dt\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left(\left|X_{\bm{k}}\right|\mathds{1}\{|X_{\bm{k}}|>(k-1)^{d}\}\left[\frac{t^{1-1/r}}{1-1/r}\right]_{k-1}^{|X_{\bm{k}}|^{1/d}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{r}{r-1}\mathbb{E}\left(\left|X_{\bm{k}}\right|^{1+\frac{1-1/r}{d}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{r}{r-1}\mathbb{E}\left(\left|X_{\bm{k}}\right|^{r}\right),

(5)

where the last inequality follows using
1+(1-1/r)/d\leq r
. Hence,
\sup_{n}B_{n}<\infty
.

Now let us turn to
A_{n}
. First, applying Decomposition (
2
) to
X_{\bm{i},j}\mathds{1}\{|X_{\bm{i},j}|\leq j^{d}\}
for a fixed
j
and using the fact that
H_{\ell}(X)=0
for
\ell=0,...,k-1
, we get, for any
\bm{i}\in\mathbb{I}_{j-1,k-1}
,

\displaystyle X_{\bm{i},j}\mathds{1}\{|X_{\bm{i},j}|\leq j^{d}\}

\displaystyle=H_{k}(X\mathds{1}\{|X|\leq j^{d}\})_{\bm{i},j}+H_{0}(X\mathds{1}\{X\leq j^{d}\})_{\bm{i},j}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{k-1}H_{\ell}(X\mathds{1}\{|X|\leq j^{d}\})_{\bm{i},j}

\displaystyle=H_{k}(X\mathds{1}\{|X|\leq j^{d}\})_{\bm{i},j}+H_{0}(X\mathds{1}\{X>j^{d}\})_{\bm{i},j}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{k-1}H_{\ell}(X\mathds{1}\{|X|>j^{d}\})_{\bm{i},j}.

Let
A_{n}=A_{1n}+A_{2n}+A_{3n}
be the decomposition of
A_{n}
corresponding to the first, second and third terms in the previous display. We have

\displaystyle A_{2n}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=k}^{n}j^{-d}\frac{(j-1)!}{(j-k)!}\mathbb{E}\left(\left|X_{\bm{k}}\right|\mathds{1}\{|X_{\bm{k}}|>j^{d}\}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{r}{r-1}\mathbb{E}\left(\left|X_{\bm{k}}\right|^{r}\right),

(6)

where (
6
) uses the inequalities from (
4
) to (
5
). Thus,
\sup_{n}A_{2n}<\infty
. Let us turn to
A_{3n}
. First, for any
\bm{e}\in\mathcal{E}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left|\sum_{j=k}^{n}j^{-d}\sum_{\bm{i}\in\overrightarrow{\mathbb{I}_{j-1,k-1}}}P_{\bm{e}}(X\mathds{1}\{X>j^{d}\})_{\bm{i},j}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=k}^{n}j^{-d}\sum_{\bm{i}\in\overrightarrow{\mathbb{I}_{j-1,k-1}}}\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbb{E}\left(|X_{\bm{i},j}|\mathds{1}\{X>j^{d}\})_{\bm{i},j}|U_{\{(\bm{i},j)\odot\bm{e}\}^{+}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=k}^{n}j^{-d}\frac{(j-1)!}{(j-k)!}\mathbb{E}\left(\left|X_{\bm{k}}\right|\mathds{1}\{\left|X_{\bm{k}}\right|>j^{d}\}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{r}{r-1}\mathbb{E}\left(\left|X_{\bm{k}}\right|^{r}\right).

(7)

Then, in view of (
3
), we have
\sup_{n}A_{3n}<\infty
. Finally, let us consider
A_{1n}
. We can write

\displaystyle A_{1n}^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{j=k}^{n}j^{-d}\sum_{\bm{i}\in\overrightarrow{\mathbb{I}_{j-1,k-1}}}H_{k}(X\mathds{1}\{|X|\leq j^{d}\})_{\bm{i},j}\right]^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{j=k}^{n}j^{-d}\sum_{\bm{i}\in\overrightarrow{\mathbb{I}_{j-1,k-1}}}H_{k}(X\mathds{1}\{|X|\leq j^{d}\})_{\bm{i},j}\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=k}^{n}j^{-2d}\sum_{\bm{i}\in\overrightarrow{\mathbb{I}_{j-1,k-1}}}\mathbb{E}\left(H_{k}(X\mathds{1}\{|X|\leq j^{d}\})^{2}_{\bm{i},j}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=k}^{n}j^{-2d+k-1}\mathbb{E}\left(X_{\bm{k}}^{2}\mathds{1}\{|X_{\bm{k}}|\leq j^{d}\}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[X_{\bm{k}}^{2}\left(\mathds{1}\left\{k>|X_{\bm{k}}|^{1/d}\right\}\int_{k-1}^{\infty}t^{-k+1-2/r}dt+\mathds{1}\left\{k\leq|X_{\bm{k}}|^{1/d}\right\}\int_{|X_{\bm{k}}|^{1/d}-1}^{\infty}t^{-k+1-2/r}dt\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{k-2+2/r}\left\{k^{2d}+\mathbb{E}\left[X_{\bm{k}}^{2}\left(|X_{\bm{k}}|^{1/d}-1\right)^{-k+2-2/r}\mathds{1}\left\{|X_{\bm{k}}|^{1/d}\geq k\right\}\right]\right\},

(8)

where the second equality follows since the variables
(H_{k}(X\mathds{1}\{|X|\leq j^{d}\})_{\bm{i},j})
are uncorrelated. Now, note that for all
x\geq 0
,

x^{2}\left(x^{1/d}-1\right)^{-k+2-2/r}\mathds{1}\left\{x^{1/d}\geq k\right\}\leq\left(1-1/k\right)^{k-2d}x^{k/d}\leq\left(1-1/k\right)^{k-2d}x^{r}.

Therefore,

\mathbb{E}\left[X_{\bm{k}}^{2}\left(|X_{\bm{k}}|^{1/d}-1\right)^{-k+2-2/r}\mathds{1}\left\{|X_{\bm{k}}|^{1/d}\geq k\right\}\right]\leq\left(1-1/k\right)^{k-2d}\mathbb{E}\left[X_{\bm{k}}^{r}\right]<\infty.

Hence,
\sup_{n}A_{1n}<\infty
, which completes the proof when
X
is degenerate of order
k-1
.

Other cases.

In view of (
2
), it suffices to prove that for all
\ell\in\{0,...,k\}
, we have

\frac{1}{n^{d}}\sum_{\bm{i}\in\overrightarrow{\mathbb{I}_{n,k}}}H_{\ell}(X)_{\bm{i}}\to 0\;\text{a.s.}

(9)

First, note that
\sum_{\bm{i}\in\overrightarrow{\mathbb{I}_{n,k}}}H_{\ell}(X)_{\bm{i}}=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{\bm{i}\in\mathbb{I}_{n,k}}H_{\ell}(X)_{\bm{i}}=\sum_{\bm{e}\in\mathcal{E}_{\ell}}\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{\bm{i}\in\mathbb{I}_{n,k}}Q_{\bm{e}}(X)_{\bm{i}}.

By Lemma
2
,
Q_{\bm{e}}(X)_{\bm{i}_{\sigma}}=Q_{\bm{e}}(X)_{\bm{i}}
for all
\sigma
such that
\bm{e}_{\sigma}=\bm{e}
. Moreover, for each
\bm{e}\in\mathcal{E}_{\ell}
,
Q_{\bm{e}}(X)_{\bm{i}}
only depends on
\bm{i}\odot\bm{e}
rather than all components of
\bm{i}
. It follows that

\sum_{\bm{i}\in\mathbb{I}_{n,k}}Q_{\bm{e}}(X)_{\bm{i}}=\frac{(n-\ell)!}{(n-k)!}\sum_{\bm{i}\in\mathbb{I}_{n,\ell}}R^{\bm{e}}_{\bm{i}},

where
(R^{\bm{e}}_{\bm{i}})_{\bm{i}\in\mathbb{I}_{n,\ell}}
is a symmetric
\ell
-dimensional jointly exchangeable array degenerate of order
\ell-1
such that
\mathbb{E}\left(\left|R^{\bm{e}}_{1,...,\ell}\right|^{r}\right)<\infty
. It follows from the previous paragraph that

\frac{1}{n^{\ell-1+1/r}}\sum_{\bm{i}\in\overrightarrow{\mathbb{I}_{n,\ell}}}R^{\bm{e}}_{\bm{i}}=\frac{1}{n^{\ell-1+1/r}\times\ell!}\sum_{\bm{i}\in\mathbb{I}_{n,\ell}}R^{\bm{e}}_{\bm{i}}\to 0\;\text{a.s.}

Because
(n-\ell)!/(n-k)!\sim n^{k-\ell}
, we finally obtain (
9
).

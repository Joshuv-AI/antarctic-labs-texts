NORMAL OPERATORS IN REAL AND QUATERNIONIC HILBERT SPACES
Florian-Horia Vasilescu

Abstract
There exists already a concept of quaternionic normal operator, acting on Hilbert bimodules on the quaternionic algebra ℍ {\mathbb{H}} , such an operator having a spectral decomposition obtained via a spectral measure defined on subsets of ℍ {\mathbb{H}} . Unlike other authors, we start our investigation by a new approach to real normal operators, applying the results to quaternionic normal operators, regarded as special cases of the real ones, having spectrum and spectral measures defined on subsets of the complex plane.



Keywords:
real spectral measures; complex extensions of real and quaternionic normal operators; spectral decompositions

AMS Subject Classification 2020:
47B15; 47A60; 47S05

Introduction

The concept of normal operator, bounded or not, has a central role
in operator theory and its applications, and it has been preponderantly used in the framework of complex Hilbert spaces. Nevertheless, normal operators in real Hilbert spaces have been studied by several authors,
including Goodrich
[
11
]
, Agrawal and Kulkarni
[
1
]
, and Oreshina
[
14
]
, to quote some of them, but the oldest contribution in this respect seems to be that of Wong in
[
23
]
. We present a new, simplified approach, leading to a more natural formula for the associated spectral measures. Our results recapture the corresponding ones, valid for complex normal operators, as presented in the functional analysis monograph by Rudin in
[
15
]
. As a matter of fact, the real case is indispensable for our development in the quaternionic context.

The first investigations in the framework of quaternionic Hilbert spaces seemingly go back to
[
17
]
(see also
[
22
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
6
]
etc.). In the present work, after the discussion concerning the normal operators in real Hilbert spaces, we apply the results to quatrnionic normal operators, regarded as a special class of real operators. Although studied by some authors mentioned above, we shall present a simplified approach, derived directly from the real case, and leading to a more natural formula of the associated spectral measures.

For the case of real normal operators, we think useful to start with the bounded case, the unbounded one being subsequently approached, using some arguments from the former case. Our main results in this respect are Theorem
1
for the bounded case, and Theorem
2
for the unbounded one. These results state and prove the construction of some functional calculi with large classes of Borel functions, employing and
recapturing the corresponding results valid for complex normal operators, as developed
for instance in
[
15
]
. Moreover, using some elements of local spectral theory,
specifically the uniqueness of spectral capacities (see
[
18
]
), under some general conditions
we prove uniqueness results concerning the real spectral measure attached to a real normal
operator (see Propositions
1
and
2
).

Concerning the quaternionic normal operators, following the author’s ideas from
[
21
]
(see also
[
20
]
), we treat them in a more classical manner, also acting in Hilbert spaces with
quaternionic bimodule structure, but where the inner products are real or complex valued, rather than quaternionic valued, as used by many of the previous authors. Moreover, the spectra and the spectral measures are
defined in the complex plane, and not in the algebra
{\mathbb{H}}
.

The main results in the quaternionic case are Theorem
3
for the bounded case, and
Theorem
4
for the unbounded one. It should be stressed that these
results are more or less special cases and direct consequences of Theorem
1
and
2
, respectively. We should mention that, in the quaternionic case, we also have some uniqueness statements concerning the corresponding spectral measures
(see Propositions
3
and
4
), derived
directly from the results proved in the real case.

We end this work with an example of a quaternionic normal operator, induced by the multiplication with the
independent quaternionic variable.

Concerning the case of real normal operators, the starting point of our research was the paper by Oreshina, who made some useful remarks on a first version of this work. Thanks are also due to anonymous reader, who suggested the author to mention other contributions, as for instance those due to Goodrich, Agrawal
\&
Kulkarni, and Wong, whose approaches are different from that of this paper.

2
Normal Operators in Real Hilbert Spaces

2.1
Preliminaries

We start by recalling some elementary facts concerning the linear operators in real Hilbert spaces (see for instance
[
14
]
or
[
21
]
).

Let
{\mathfrak{H}}
be a real Hilbert space with the inner product
\langle*,*\rangle
, and corresponding norm
\|*\|
, and let
{\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}}
be the
the complexification of
{\mathfrak{H}}
, identified with the direct sum
{\mathfrak{H}}+i{\mathfrak{H}}
. The natural inner product of
{\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}}
is then given by

\langle x+iy,u+iv\rangle_{\mathbb{C}}=\langle x,u\rangle+\langle y,v\rangle+i\langle y,u\rangle-i\langle x,v\rangle

for all
x,y,u,v\in{\mathfrak{H}}
.

Let also
C:{\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}}\mapsto{\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}}
be the natural conjugation
x+iy\mapsto x-iy,\,x,y\in{\mathfrak{H}}
, which is an
{\mathbb{R}}
-linear isomorphism of
{\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}}
, whose square is the identity.

We denote by
\mathcal{B}({\mathfrak{H}})
the real algebra of all bounded
{\mathbb{R}}
-linear operators, acting on
{\mathfrak{H}}
. Similarly,
\mathcal{B}({\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}})
denotes the complex algebra consisting of all bounded
{\mathbb{C}}
-linear operators, acting on
{\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}}
.

Each operator

T\in\mathcal{B}({\mathfrak{H}})
has a natural extension to an operator

T_{\mathbb{C}}\in\mathcal{B}({\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}})
, given by
T_{\mathbb{C}}(x+iy)=Tx+iTy,\,x,y\in{\mathfrak{H}}
.
Moreover, the map
\mathcal{B}({\mathfrak{H}})\ni T\mapsto T_{\mathbb{C}}\in\mathcal{B}({\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}})
is unital,
{\mathbb{R}}
-linear, injective, and multiplicative. In particular,
T\in\mathcal{B}({\mathfrak{H}})
is invertible if and only if
T_{\mathbb{C}}\in\mathcal{B}({\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}})
is invertible. The operator
T_{\mathbb{C}}
will be
sometimes called the
complex extension
of the real operator
T
.

Fixing an operator
S\in\mathcal{B}({\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}})
, we define
the operator
S^{\flat}\in\mathcal{B}({\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}})
to be equal to
CSC
. It is easily seen that the map

\mathcal{B}({\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}})\ni S\mapsto S^{\flat}\in\mathcal{B}({\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}})
is a unital conjugate-linear automorphism, whose square is the identity on
\mathcal{B}({\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}})
.
Because
{\mathfrak{H}}=\{u\in{\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}};Cu=u\}
, we have
S^{\flat}=S
if and only if
S({\mathfrak{H}})\subset{\mathfrak{H}}
.
In this case,
S=(S|{\mathfrak{H}})_{\mathbb{C}}
, and so
T_{\mathbb{C}}^{\flat}=T_{\mathbb{C}}
for all
T\in\mathcal{B}({\mathfrak{H}})
.

In fact, because of the representation

S=\frac{1}{2}(S+S^{\flat})+i\frac{1}{2i}(S-S^{\flat}),\,\,S\in\mathcal{B}({\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}}),

where
(S+S^{\flat})({\mathfrak{H}})\subset{\mathfrak{H}},i(S-S^{\flat})({\mathfrak{H}})\subset{\mathfrak{H}}
, the algebras
\mathcal{B}({\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}})
and
\mathcal{B}({\mathfrak{H}})_{\mathbb{C}}
are isomorphic and they will be sometimes identified. In other words, the algebra
\mathcal{B}({\mathfrak{H}})
(in fact
\{T_{\mathbb{C}};T\in\mathcal{B}({\mathfrak{H}})\}
) may and will be regarded as a (real) subalgebra of
\mathcal{B}({\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}})
. In particular, if
S=U+iV
, with
U,V\in\mathcal{B}({\mathfrak{H}})
, we have

S^{\flat}=U-iV
, so the map
S\mapsto S^{\flat}
is the conjugation
of the complex algebra
\mathcal{B}({\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}})
induced
by the conjugation
C
of
{\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}}
.

For an operator
T\in\mathcal{B}({\mathfrak{H}})
acting on the real Hilbert space
{\mathfrak{H}}
,
we denote by
T^{*}
its (Hilbert space) adjoint, defined, as usually, via the equality

\langle T^{*}x,y\rangle=\langle x,Ty\rangle
for all
x,y\in{\mathfrak{H}}
. As we have

\langle C(x+iy),u+iv\rangle_{\mathbb{C}}=\langle C(u+iv),x+iy\rangle_{\mathbb{C}},

for the operator
T\in\mathcal{B}({\mathfrak{H}})
it is easily seen that
(T_{\mathbb{C}})^{*}=(T^{*})_{\mathbb{C}}
, and this operator will be simply denoted by
T^{*}_{\mathbb{C}}
. Moreover,
(T_{\mathbb{C}}^{*})^{\flat}=T_{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
.

For every operator
S\in\mathcal{B}({\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}})
, we denote,
as usually, by
\sigma(S)
its spectrum. Using a classical idea, for a real
operator
T\in\mathcal{B}({\mathfrak{H}})
its (complex) spectrum is defined by the equality

\sigma_{\mathbb{C}}(T)=\{u+iv;(u-T)^{2}+v^{2}\,\,{\rm is\,\,not\,\,invertible},u,v\in{\mathbb{R}}\},

where the scalars are identified with the corresponding
multiples of the identity. Because the operator
(u-T)^{2}+v^{2}
is invertible if and only if the operator

(u-T_{\mathbb{C}})^{2}+v^{2}
is invertible, which in turn is invertible if and only if
u+iv-T_{\mathbb{C}}
is
invertible, it follows that
\sigma_{\mathbb{C}}(T)=\sigma(T_{\mathbb{C}})
, as noticed in
[
14
]
or in
[
21
]
. Note also that the set
\sigma_{\mathbb{C}}(T)
is
conjugate symmetric
, that is

z\in\sigma_{\mathbb{C}}(T)
if and only if
\bar{z}\in\sigma_{\mathbb{C}}(T)
.

Of course, as in the complex case, a bounded linear operator
T
in a real Hilbert space
{\mathfrak{H}}
is said to be normal if
TT^{*}=T^{*}T
. It is easily seen that
T
is normal if and only if
T_{\mathbb{C}}
is normal.

We shall also work with some
{\mathbb{R}}
-linear operators, not necessarily bounded, called as usually unbounded operators. Such an operator
T
, which is defined on a vector subspace
D(T)\subset{\mathfrak{H}}
, with values in
{\mathfrak{H}}
, has a complex extension
T_{\mathbb{C}}
, defined on
D(T_{\mathbb{C}})=D(T)_{\mathbb{C}}
, with values in
{\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}}
. When
T
is closed and/or
densely defined, then the complex extension
T_{\mathbb{C}}
is also closed and/or
densely defined.

The family of closed and densely defined linear operators on
{\mathfrak{H}}
,
or
{\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}}
, will be denoted by
\mathcal{C}({\mathfrak{H}})
, or
\mathcal{C}({\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}})
,
respectively.

The adjoint of an operator
T\in\mathcal{C}({\mathfrak{H}})
is defined as for the complex operators. Adapting the corresponding definition valid in complex Hilbert spaces (as done in

[
14
]
), an operator
T\in\mathcal{C}({\mathfrak{H}})

is said to be
normal
if
D(T^{*})=D(T)
,
D(TT^{*})=D(T^{*}T)
, and
TT^{*}=T^{*}T
.

As in the bounded case, the complex extension
T_{\mathbb{C}}
is normal if and only if

T
is normal. Moreover, we have
(T_{\mathbb{C}})^{*}=(T^{*})_{\mathbb{C}}
, which will be denoted by
T_{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
.

The spectrum
\sigma(T_{\mathbb{C}})
of the operator
T_{\mathbb{C}}\in\mathcal{C}({\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}})
is defined following
[
15
]
, as the complement of the set of those points

\zeta\in{\mathbb{C}}
such that the operator
\zeta-T_{\mathbb{C}}
has an everywhere defined bounded inverse. As noticed in
[
14
]
, the set
\sigma(T_{\mathbb{C}})
is actually conjugate symmetric. Because we have
T\in\mathcal{C}({\mathfrak{H}})
invertible if and only if
T_{\mathbb{C}}\in\mathcal{C}({\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}})
is invertible,
and we have
(T_{\mathbb{C}})^{-1}=(T^{-1})_{\mathbb{C}}
, simply denoted by
T_{\mathbb{C}}^{-1}
,
we may define
\sigma_{\mathbb{C}}(T)=\sigma(T_{\mathbb{C}})
, which will be called the
complex spectrum
of the real operator
T\in\mathcal{C}({\mathfrak{H}})
, which is compatible with the bounded case.

2.2
Real and Complex Spectral Measures

In this subsection we recall some definitions, present some simple consequences of them, and fix some notation.

Remark 1

(1) In what follows, we shall often use the concept of
spectral measure
(see
[
8
]
, Part III), which is a particular case of the concept of
resolution of the identity
(see
[
15
]
), but we shall not distinct the former from the latter, designating them as spectral measures.

For our purpose, it is sufficient to consider only spectral measures supported by closed sets in the complex plane. More precisely, a spectral measure in this text is a map
defined on a
\sigma
-algebra, say
{\bf\Sigma}(\mathfrak{S})
, consisting of Borel subsets of
\mathfrak{S}
, where
\mathfrak{S}
is a closed subset of the complex plane. Specifically, fixing a real or complex Hilbert space
\mathcal{K}
, with the inner product

\langle*,*\rangle_{\mathcal{K}}
, a spectral measure on
{\bf\Sigma}(\mathfrak{S})
is a set function
F
on
{\bf\Sigma}(\mathfrak{S})
, whose values are self-adjoint projections on
\mathcal{K}
, with
F(\emptyset)=0
,
F(\mathfrak{S})
the identity of
\mathcal{K},\,F(A\cap B)=F(A)F(B)
for all
A,B\in{\bf\Sigma}(\mathfrak{S})
, and such that the maps
\nu_{x,y}
on
{\bf\Sigma}(\mathfrak{S})
, given by
\nu_{x,y}(A)=\langle F(A)x,y\rangle_{\mathcal{K}}
for all
x,y\in\mathcal{K}
, are countably additive measures. The scalar measures
\nu_{x,y}
, called the
associated measures
to
F
, are complex (resp. real) if the space
\mathcal{K}
is complex (resp. real; for the real case see also
[
14
]
).
In particular, if
\{A_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
is a countable family of mutually disjoint sets from
{\bf\Sigma}(\mathfrak{S})
, we have
F(\cup_{k=1}^{\infty}A_{k})x=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}F(A_{k})x
for every
x\in\mathcal{K}
.

(2) In the case of a complex Hilbert space
\mathcal{K}
, we mainly take as
{\bf\Sigma}(\mathfrak{S})
the
\sigma
-algebra
{\rm Bor}(\mathfrak{S})
, consisting of all Borel subsets of
\mathfrak{S}
.

If
{\mathfrak{H}}
is a real Hilbert space, considering its complexification
{\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}}
, and fixing
a spectral measure
E:{\rm Bor}(\mathfrak{S})\mapsto\mathcal{B}({\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}})
,
we may consider its restriction
E_{\mathbb{R}}(A)=E(A)|{\mathfrak{H}}
for all
A\in{\rm Bor}_{E}(\mathfrak{S})
, where
{\rm Bor}_{E}(\mathfrak{S})=\{A\in\rm{Bor}(\mathfrak{S});E(A)=E(A)^{\flat}\}
. The map

E_{\mathbb{R}}:{\rm Bor}_{E}(\mathfrak{S})\mapsto\mathcal{B}({\mathfrak{H}})
is a real spectral measure, because
{\rm Bor}_{E}(\mathfrak{S})
is a
\sigma
-algebra. Indeed, we have
\mathfrak{S}\setminus A\in{\rm Bor}_{E}(\mathfrak{S})
whenever
A\in{\rm Bor}_{E}(\mathfrak{S})
, and
E(\cup_{k=1}^{\infty}A_{k})=E(\cup_{k=1}^{\infty}A_{k})^{\flat}
if
\{A_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
is a sequence of mutually disjoint sets from
{\rm Bor}_{E}(\mathfrak{S})
, via the additivity and
continuity of the map
\mathcal{B}({\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}})\ni S\mapsto S^{\flat}\in\mathcal{B}({\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}})
. The general case of an arbitrary sequence
\{B_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
from
{\rm Bor}_{E}(\mathfrak{S})
can be reduced to the previous one, replacing it
by a sequence of mutually disjoint sets, having the same union.

(3) We denote by
\mathfrak{B(S)}
the complex algebra of all complex-valued Borel
functions, defined on
\mathfrak{S}
. When
\mathfrak{S}
is conjugate symmetric,
fixing a complex Hilbert space
\mathcal{K}
, and a spectral measure

F:{\rm Bor}(\mathfrak{S})\mapsto\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{K})
,
we denote by
\mathfrak{B_{\it s,F}(S)}
the real subalgebra of those functions
f\in\mathfrak{B(S)}
with the property
f(\bar{z})=\overline{f(z)}
for all
z\in\mathfrak{S}
, except for a set of null
F
-measure, which will be designated as

F
-
stem functions
.

(4) As in
[
15
]
, Section 12.20, fixing again a complex Hilbert space
\mathcal{K}
, and a spectral measure
F:{\rm Bor}(\mathfrak{S})\mapsto\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{K})
, we introduce the algebra
{\mathcal{L}}^{\infty}(\mathfrak{S},F)
consisting of
F
-essentially bounded measurable functions
f\in\mathfrak{B(S)}
, so that

\|f\|_{\infty}=\inf\{r>0;F(\{z\in\mathfrak{S},|f(z)|\geq r\})=0\}<\infty.

Setting
\mathcal{N}=\{f\in{\mathcal{L}}^{\infty}(\mathfrak{S},F);\|f\|_{\infty}=0\}
, which is un ideal in
{\mathcal{L}}^{\infty}(\mathfrak{S},F)
,
we define the quotient
L^{\infty}(\mathfrak{S},F)={\mathcal{L}}^{\infty}(\mathfrak{S},F)/\mathcal{N}
, which is a Banach algebra.
In fact,
L^{\infty}(\mathfrak{S},F)
is a
C^{*}
-algebra, with the involution induced by
the complex conjugation
f\mapsto\bar{f}
, where
\bar{f}(z)=\overline{f(z)},\,z\in\mathfrak{S}
.

Practically, we identify a function
f\in{\mathcal{L}}^{\infty}(\mathfrak{S},F)
with its
equivalence class
f+\mathcal{N}\in L^{\infty}(\mathfrak{S},F)
. Moreover, when no confusion is possible, the algebra
L^{\infty}(\mathfrak{S},E)
will be simply denoted by
L^{\infty}(\mathfrak{S})
.

In addition, assuming
\mathfrak{S}
conjugate symmetric, we denote by

L^{\infty}_{s}(\mathfrak{S},F)=L^{\infty}_{s}(\mathfrak{S})
the (real) subalgebra of

L^{\infty}(\mathfrak{S})
consisting of
F
-stem functions.

(5) As in (2), assume that
\mathfrak{S}
is conjugate symmetric, choose

{\mathfrak{H}}
to be a real Hilbert space, take its complexification
{\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}}
, and fix
a spectral measure
E:{\rm Bor}(\mathfrak{S})\mapsto\mathcal{B}({\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}})
.
Note that the finite sums of the form
\Sigma_{j\in J}(r_{j}\chi_{A_{j}}+is_{j}\theta_{A_{j}})
, with

r_{j},s_{j}\in{\mathbb{R}}
for all
j\in J,\,(A_{j})_{j\in J}\subset\rm{Bor}_{E}(\mathfrak{S})
a partition of
\mathfrak{S}
, where
\chi_{A}
is the characteristic function of the set
A
, and
\theta_{A}(z)=1,=0,=-1
if
\Im(z)>0,\Im(z)=0
or
z\in\mathfrak{S}\setminus A,\Im(z)<0
, respectively, are elements of
L^{\infty}_{s}(\mathfrak{S})
. The functions of this type will be designated in this text as

elementary functions
in
L^{\infty}_{s}(\mathfrak{S})
.

Let
F^{\infty}_{s}(\mathfrak{S})
be the real subspace of
L^{\infty}_{s}(\mathfrak{S})
,
generated by the elementary functions. We can easily see that the subspace
F^{\infty}_{s}(\mathfrak{S})
is dense in the algebra
L^{\infty}_{s}(\mathfrak{S})
. Fixing a function
f\in L^{\infty}_{s}(\mathfrak{S})
, we
can represent it as
f(z)=f_{1}(z)+if_{2}(z)
, with
f_{1},f_{2}
real valued, and with

f_{1}(\bar{z})=f_{1}(z)
, and
f_{2}(\bar{z})=-f_{2}(z)
.

Taking the restriction
g_{1}(z)=f_{1}(z)
to the set
\{z\in\mathfrak{S};\Im(z)\geq 0\}
, we can find a simple function
h
arbitrarily
close to the function
g_{1}
. Extending
h
to the whole set
\mathfrak{S}
by putting
h(\bar{z})=h(z)
, we obtain an elementary function, say
h_{1}
, which is arbitrarily close to
f_{1}
. Similarly, we can build an elementary function
h_{2}
, arbitrarily close
to
f_{2}
. Consequently, the family of elementary functions is dense in

L^{\infty}_{s}(\mathfrak{S})
.

2.3
Bounded Normal Operators in Real Hilbert Spaces

Following the corresponding part of
[
15
]
(see the subsections 12.17-12.26), we
recall some general results concerning the bounded normal operators, necessary for
our further development.

Remark 2

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a complex Hilbert space, and let
S\in\mathcal{B(K)}
be a
normal operator. If
\mathfrak{S}
is the spectrum of
S
, there exists a unique
spectral measure
F:\rm{Bor}(\mathfrak{S})\mapsto\mathcal{B(K)}
inducing a
unital
C^{*}
-algebra morphism

L^{\infty}(\mathfrak{S},F)\ni f\mapsto f(S):=\int_{\mathfrak{S}}f(z)dF(z)\in\mathcal{B(K)},

meaning that

\langle f(S)x,y\rangle_{\mathcal{K}}=\int_{\mathfrak{S}}f(z)d\nu_{x,y}(z),\,\,x,y\in\mathcal{K},

where
\{\nu_{x,y};x,y\in\mathcal{K}\}
are the scalar measures associated to
F
.

We are especially interested by the particular case of a real Hilbert space
{\mathfrak{H}}
and
a normal operator
T\in\mathcal{B(H)}
, applying the previous discussion to the
normal operator
T_{\mathbb{C}}\in\mathcal{B(H}_{\mathbb{C}})
, with the spectral measure

E_{\mathbb{C}}:\rm{Bor}(\mathfrak{S})\mapsto\mathcal{B(H}_{\mathbb{C}})
.
As the real Hilbert space
{\mathfrak{H}}
is not necessarily invariant under the spectral measure
E_{\mathbb{C}}(*)
, we shall try to charactrize those Borel subsets
A\subset\mathfrak{S}
such that
E_{\mathbb{C}}(A)^{\flat}=E_{\mathbb{C}}(A)
.

For an arbitrary subset
A\subset{\mathbb{C}}
, we put
A^{c}:=\{\bar{z};z\in A\}
. For a later use,
we designate by
[A]
the closure of the arbitrary subset
A\subset{\mathbb{C}}
.

Lemma 1

With
T,\,T_{\mathbb{C}}
and
E_{\mathbb{C}}
as above, we have the equality
E_{\mathbb{C}}(A)^{\flat}=E_{\mathbb{C}}(A)
for some
A\in{\rm Bor}(\mathfrak{S})
if and only if
E_{\mathbb{C}}(A^{c}\setminus A)=0
, or if and only if
E_{\mathbb{C}}(A\setminus A^{c})=0
, or if and only if
E_{\mathbb{C}}(A)=E_{\mathbb{C}}(A^{c})
.

Proof
We first note that the map
E_{\mathbb{C}}^{\flat}:{\rm Bor}(\mathfrak{S})\mapsto\mathcal{B}({\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}})
, with
E_{\mathbb{C}}^{\flat}(A)=E_{\mathbb{C}}(A)^{\flat}
, is also a
spectral measure, which is easily checked. Let us find the operator
S=\int_{\mathfrak{S}}zdE_{\mathbb{C}}^{\flat}(z)
. Note that for all

\xi,\eta\in{\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}}
, and
A\subset\mathfrak{S}
a Borel subset, we have

\langle E_{\mathbb{C}}^{\flat}(A)\xi,\eta\rangle_{\mathbb{C}}=\overline{\langle E_{\mathbb{C}}(A)C\xi,C\eta\rangle_{\mathbb{C}}},

and so
\mu^{\flat}_{\xi,\eta}=\bar{\mu}_{C\xi,C\eta}
, where
\mu^{\flat}_{\xi,\eta}

is the scalar measure
\langle E_{\mathbb{C}}^{\flat}(*)\xi,\eta\rangle_{\mathbb{C}}
. Therefore,

\langle S\xi,\eta\rangle_{\mathbb{C}}=\int_{\mathfrak{S}}zd\mu^{\flat}_{\xi,\eta}=\int_{\mathfrak{S}}zd\bar{\mu}_{C\xi,C\eta}=\overline{\int_{\mathfrak{S}}\bar{z}d\mu_{C\xi,C\eta}}

=\overline{\langle T_{\mathbb{C}}^{*}C\xi,C\eta\rangle_{\mathbb{C}}}=\langle CT_{\mathbb{C}}^{*}C\xi,\eta\rangle_{\mathbb{C}}=\langle T_{\mathbb{C}}^{*}\xi,\eta\rangle_{\mathbb{C}},

showing that
E_{\mathbb{C}}^{\flat}
is precisely the spectral measure of the normal operator

T^{*}_{\mathbb{C}}
.

The spectral measure of the adjoint
T^{*}_{\mathbb{C}}
of the normal operator
T_{\mathbb{C}}

can be also obtain by regarding
T^{*}_{\mathbb{C}}
as a function of
T_{\mathbb{C}}
, via the map

{\mathbb{C}}\ni z\mapsto\bar{z}\in{\mathbb{C}}
. Applying a particular case of the change of measure
principle (see
[
15
]
, Theorem 13.28), we obtain the equality
E_{\mathbb{C}}^{\flat}(A)=E_{\mathbb{C}}(A^{c})
for all
A\in{\rm Bor}(\mathfrak{S})
. Consequently,

E_{\mathbb{C}}(A^{c})=CE_{\mathbb{C}}(A)C
. This implies that
E_{\mathbb{C}}(A^{c})=0
if and only if

E_{\mathbb{C}}(A)=0
, or if and only if
E_{\mathbb{C}}^{\flat}(A)=0
. In addition,
E_{\mathbb{C}}^{\flat}(A)=E_{\mathbb{C}}(A)
if
A=A^{c}
.

Assume now that
E_{\mathbb{C}}(A^{c}\setminus A)=0
. In this case we also have
E_{\mathbb{C}}(A\setminus A^{c})=0
because
(A\setminus A^{c})^{c}=A\setminus A^{c}
. Writing
A^{c}=(A^{c}\setminus A)\cup(A\cap A^{c})
, and
A=(A\setminus A^{c})\cup(A\cap A^{c})
, we infer that
E_{\mathbb{C}}(A)^{\flat}=E_{\mathbb{C}}(A\cap A^{c})=E_{\mathbb{C}}(A)
for any
A\in{\rm Bor}(\mathfrak{S})
with
E_{\mathbb{C}}(A^{c}\setminus A)=0
because of the the equality
E_{\mathbb{C}}(A\cap A^{c})=E_{\mathbb{C}}^{\flat}(A\cap A^{c})
, via the equality

(A\cap A^{c})^{c}=A^{c}\cap A
and the previous discussion.

Conversely, assume that
E_{\mathbb{C}}(A)^{\flat}=E_{\mathbb{C}}(A)=E_{\mathbb{C}}(A^{c})
for some
A\in{\rm Bor}(\mathfrak{S})
. We use again the equalities
A=(A\setminus A^{c})\cup(A\cap A^{c})
, and
A^{c}=(A^{c}\setminus A)\cup(A\cap A^{c})
. Having the equality
E_{\mathbb{C}}(A\cap A^{c})=E_{\mathbb{C}}^{\flat}(A\cap A^{c})
, via
(A\cap A^{c})^{c}=A^{c}\cap A
, we obtain
E_{\mathbb{C}}(A\setminus A^{c})=E_{\mathbb{C}}(A^{c}\setminus A)
. On the other
hand,
E_{\mathbb{C}}(A\setminus A^{c})E_{\mathbb{C}}(A^{c}\setminus A)=E_{\mathbb{C}}(\emptyset)=0
.
Therefore,
E_{\mathbb{C}}(A\setminus A^{c})=E_{\mathbb{C}}(A^{c}\setminus A)=0
, which completes the proof.

Remark 3

Lemma
1
establishes a partial invariance of the real Hilbert space
{\mathfrak{H}}
, with respect to the spectral measure
E_{\mathbb{C}}
of the normal operator

, obtained via a normal operator

, acting on
{\mathfrak{H}}

\mathfrak{S}=\sigma(T_{\mathbb{C}})
, we get a family of self-adjoint projections on
{\mathfrak{H}}
. As a matter of fact, Lemma
1
shows the equality

\{A\in{Bor}(\mathfrak{S});E_{\mathbb{C}}(A^{c})=E_{\mathbb{C}}(A)\}=\{A\in{Bor}(\mathfrak{S});E_{\mathbb{C}}(A^{c}\setminus A)=0\},

which is a
\sigma
-algebra denoted by
\rm{Bor}_{E_{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathfrak{S})
(see Remark

(2)).
Therefore, the map

\rm{Bor}_{E_{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathfrak{S})\ni A\mapsto E_{\mathbb{R}}(A)\in\mathcal{B}({\mathfrak{H}})
is a real spectral measure (as in
[
14
]
, page 90). Clearly, the
\sigma
-algebra

\rm{Bor}_{E_{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathfrak{S})
depends eventually on the normal operator
T
.

Some important properties of a real spectral measure
E_{\mathbb{R}}
can and will be deduced from the properties of the spectral measure
E_{\mathbb{C}}
, proved in

, as already mentioned.

Theorem 1

Let
T\in\mathcal{B}({\mathfrak{H}})

T_{\mathbb{C}}
, where

\mathfrak{S}=\sigma(T_{\mathbb{C}})
. Then the restriction
E_{\mathbb{R}}
of the complex spectral measure
E_{\mathbb{C}}

\sigma

\rm{Bor}_{E_{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathfrak{S})

\mathcal{B}({\mathfrak{H}})
, such that the map
\Phi

\langle\Phi(f)x,y\rangle=\int_{\mathfrak{S}}fd\mu_{x,y},\,\,f\in L^{\infty}_{s}(\mathfrak{S}),\,x,y\in{\mathfrak{H}},

with

L^{\infty}_{s}(\mathfrak{S},E_{\mathbb{C}})
into
\mathcal{B}({\mathfrak{H}})
, where
\rm{Bor}_{E_{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathfrak{S})
is the
\sigma

\{A\in\rm{Bor}(\mathfrak{S});E_{\mathbb{C}}(A)=E_{\mathbb{C}}(A^{c})\}

following.

(i) For every polynomial with real coeficients

one has
\Phi(p)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}T^{j}
.

(ii)
\Phi(f)
is a real normal operator and
\Phi(\bar{f})=\Phi(f)^{*}
for all

f\in L^{\infty}_{s}(\mathfrak{S},E_{\mathbb{C}})

(iii)
\|\Phi(f)x\|^{2}=\int_{\mathfrak{S}}|f|^{2}d\mu_{x,x}
for all

f\in f\in L^{\infty}_{s}(\mathfrak{S},E_{\mathbb{C}})

.

(iv)
\sigma_{\mathbb{C}}(T|E_{\mathbb{R}}(A){\mathfrak{H}})\subset[A]

SE_{\mathbb{R}}(A)=E_{\mathbb{R}}(A)S

Proof
Put
\Phi(f)=f(T_{\mathbb{C}})|{\mathfrak{H}}

We first show that that the map
\Phi
has values in
\mathcal{B}({\mathfrak{H}})
.
For

{A^{+}}^{c}=A^{-}

Moreover, with
\theta_{A}

(5),

and
C[i(E_{\mathbb{C}}(A^{+})-E_{\mathbb{C}}(A^{-}))]C=i(E_{\mathbb{C}}(A^{+})-E_{\mathbb{C}}(A^{-}))

{\mathfrak{H}}

With
f=\Sigma_{j\in J}(r_{j}\chi_{A_{j}}+is_{j}\theta_{A_{j}})

\int_{\mathfrak{S}}\Sigma_{j\in J}(r_{j}\chi_{A_{j}}+is_{j}\theta_{A_{j}})d\mu_{x,y}

\Phi(f)=\Sigma_{j\in J}(r_{j}E_{\mathbb{C}}(A_{j})+is_{j}\theta_{A_{j}}(T_{\mathbb{C}}))|{\mathfrak{H}}=f(T_{\mathbb{C}})|{\mathfrak{H}}
.

L^{\infty}_{s0}(\mathfrak{S})
be the subalgebra of

,
generated by the elementary functions. Because the map
L^{\infty}(\mathfrak{S})\ni f\mapsto f(T_{\mathbb{C}})\in\mathcal{B}({\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}})
is an algebra morphism, it follows, in particular, that for every

{\mathfrak{H}}
is invariant under

is
{\mathfrak{H}}
-valued.

Therefore, using the density of

(see Remark
1
(5)), a direct continuity argument shows that the space

is invariant under
f(T_{\mathbb{C}})
for all

.

In fact, the map

is the restriction
of the map

with bounded Borel functions. Therefore, it inherits

when

we must have
(ii)
. The properties

The inclusion

\sigma_{\mathbb{C}}(T|E_{\mathbb{R}}(A){\mathfrak{H}})=\sigma(T_{\mathbb{C}}|((E_{\mathbb{R}}(A){\mathfrak{H}})_{\mathbb{C}})=\sigma(T_{\mathbb{C}}|E_{\mathbb{C}}(A){\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}})\subset[A]

for all

, via the corresponding spectral inclusion
property valid for the normal operator
T_{\mathbb{C}}
(see
[
8

, Part II, Corollary X.2.6).

If
S\in\mathcal{B}({\mathfrak{H}})
and

.
This implies

for all

, by Theorem 12.23 from
[

]
. Passing to restrictions to
{\mathfrak{H}}
, we obtain
SE_{\mathbb{R}}(A)=E_{\mathbb{R}}(A)S
for all

also holds.

The next assertion is a uniqueness result of the real spectral measure attached to
a real normal operator.

Proposition 1

Let
T\in\mathcal{B}({\mathfrak{H}})
be a real normal operator, and let
F_{\mathbb{R}}:\rm{Bor}_{E_{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathfrak{S})\penalty\mapsto\mathcal{B}({\mathfrak{H}})
be a real spectral
measure with the following properties.

(a)
TF_{\mathbb{R}}(A){\mathfrak{H}}\subset F_{\mathbb{R}}(A){\mathfrak{H}}
for all
A\in\rm{Bor}_{E_{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathfrak{S})
.

(b) We have the inclusion
\sigma_{\mathbb{C}}(T|F_{\mathbb{R}}(A){\mathfrak{H}})\subset[A]
for all
A\in\rm{Bor}_{E_{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathfrak{S})
.

Then the real spectral measure
F_{\mathbb{R}}
coincides with the real spectral measure obtained
by the restriction to the
\sigma
-algebra
\rm{Bor}_{E_{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathfrak{S})
of the spectral measure
E_{\mathbb{C}}
of the complex extension
T_{\mathbb{C}}
of

.

Proof
For every
A\in\rm{Bor}_{E_{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathfrak{S})
we set
F_{\mathbb{C}}(A)=F_{\mathbb{R}}(A)_{\mathbb{C}}
. Denoting by
\mathcal{F}^{c}(\mathfrak{S})
the family of all
closed subset in
\rm{Bor}_{E_{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathfrak{S})
, we get what is called a
pseudoring of closed subsets
of
\mathfrak{S}
(see
[
18
]
, Definition IV.1.1). Moreover, the restrictions

are
spectral capacities
attached to the normal operator
T_{\mathbb{C}}
(see
[
18
]
, Definitions IV.1.5 and IV.1.6), because a normal operator is
decomposable
(see

[
5
]
for the original definition, which is a special case of Definition IV.1.6
from
[
18

). Then a particular case of Theorem IV.1.9 from
[
18
]

implies the equality
F_{\mathbb{C}}(A)=E_{\mathbb{C}}(A)
for all
A\in\mathcal{F}^{c}(\mathfrak{S})
.
The regularity of the measures leads to the equality
F_{\mathbb{C}}(A)=E_{\mathbb{C}}(A)
for all
A\in\rm{Bor}_{E_{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathfrak{S})
, whence
F_{\mathbb{R}}(A)=E_{\mathbb{R}}(A)
for all
A\in\rm{Bor}_{E_{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathfrak{S})
.

2.4
Unbounded Normal Operators in Real Hilbert Spaces

As in the bounded case, some important properties of a real normal operator may and will be obtain from the well-known propertis of its complex normal extension.
For some properties of unbounded normal operators used in what follows, we refeer to
[
15
]
(especially the subsections 13.22-13.25).

If the operator
T
is a normal operator in the real Hilbert space
{\mathfrak{H}}
, and so its complex extension
T_{\mathbb{C}}
is a normal operator in
{\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}}
, the latter has a unique spectral measure

E_{\mathbb{C}}:\rm{Bor}(\mathfrak{S})\mapsto\mathcal{B}({\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}})
, whose values are orthogonal projections commuting with
T_{\mathbb{C}}
, that is
E_{\mathbb{C}}(A)T_{\mathbb{C}}\xi=T_{\mathbb{C}}E_{\mathbb{C}}(A)\xi
for all
\xi\in D(T_{\mathbb{C}})
and
A\in\rm{Bor}(\mathfrak{S})
, where

\mathfrak{S}=\sigma(T_{\mathbb{C}})
. We also denote by
\{\mu_{\xi,\eta};\xi,\eta\in{\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}}\}
the family of complex valued associated measures with the spectral
measure
E_{\mathbb{C}}
.

With this notation, given an arbitrary function
f\in\mathfrak{B(S)}
, we put

\mathcal{D}_{f}=\{\xi\in{\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}};\int_{\mathfrak{S}}|f|^{2}d\mu_{\xi,\xi}<\infty\}.

(1)

We recall that the functional calculus with unbounded Borel functions of the unbounded
normal operator
T_{\mathbb{C}}
is given by

\langle\Psi(f)\xi,\eta\rangle=\int_{\mathfrak{S}}fd\mu_{\xi,\eta},\,f\in\mathfrak{B(S)},\,\,\xi\in\mathcal{D}_{f},\,\,\eta\in{\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}}

(2)

(see Theorem 13.24 from
[
15
]
).

The next result is a version of Lemma
1
, valid for unbounded operators.

Lemma 2

With
T,\,T_{\mathbb{C}}
and
E_{\mathbb{C}}
as above, we have the equality
E_{\mathbb{C}}(A)^{\flat}=E_{\mathbb{C}}(A)
for some
A\in{\rm Bor}(\mathfrak{S})
if and only if
E_{\mathbb{C}}(A^{c}\setminus A)=0
, or if and only if
E_{\mathbb{C}}(A\setminus A^{c})=0
, or if and only if
E_{\mathbb{C}}(A)=E_{\mathbb{C}}(A^{c})
.

Proof
As in the proof of Lemma
1
, the map
E_{\mathbb{C}}^{\flat}:{\rm Bor}(\mathfrak{S})\mapsto\mathcal{B}({\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}})
, with
E_{\mathbb{C}}^{\flat}(A)=E_{\mathbb{C}}(A)^{\flat}
, is a
spectral measure. A similar argument used in the proof of Lemma
1

leads to the equality

\int_{\mathfrak{S}}zd\mu^{\flat}_{\xi,\eta}=\langle T_{\mathbb{C}}^{*}\xi,\eta\rangle_{\mathbb{C}}\,\,\,\forall\,\,\xi,\eta\in D(T_{\mathbb{C}})=D(T_{\mathbb{C}}^{*}),

where, as before,
\mu^{\flat}_{\xi,\eta}

is the scalar measure
\langle E_{\mathbb{C}}^{\flat}(*)\xi,\eta\rangle_{\mathbb{C}}
,
showing that
E_{\mathbb{C}}^{\flat}
is precisely the spectral measure of the normal operator

T^{*}_{\mathbb{C}}
.

As in the proof of Lemma
1
, the spectral measure of the adjoint
T^{*}_{\mathbb{C}}
of the normal operator
T_{\mathbb{C}}

can be also obtain by regarding
T^{*}_{\mathbb{C}}
as a function of
T_{\mathbb{C}}
, via the map

{\mathbb{C}}\ni z\mapsto\bar{z}\in{\mathbb{C}}
, and leading to the equality
E_{\mathbb{C}}^{\flat}(A)=E_{\mathbb{C}}(A^{c})
for all
A\in{\rm Bor}(\mathfrak{S})
. Consequently,

E_{\mathbb{C}}(A^{c})=CE_{\mathbb{C}}(A)C
.

The rest of the proof regards only the spectral measures
E_{\mathbb{C}}
and
E_{\mathbb{C}}^{\flat}
,
and can be performed as in the proof of Lemma
1
, leading to the desired
conclusion.

As in the bounded case, we set

\rm{Bor}_{E_{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathfrak{S})=\{A\in{Bor}(\mathfrak{S});E_{\mathbb{C}}(A^{c})=E_{\mathbb{C}}(A)\}=\{A\in{Bor}(\mathfrak{S});E_{\mathbb{C}}(A^{c}\setminus A)=0\},

which is a
\sigma
-algebra. Using the notation from the next statement (see also Remark
1
(3)), a functional calculus with functions from the space

\mathfrak{B}_{s,E_{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathfrak{S})
(that is,
E_{\mathbb{C}}
-stem functions), associated to a real unbounded normal operator is given by the following result.

Theorem 2

Let
T:D(T)\subset{\mathfrak{H}}\mapsto{\mathfrak{H}}
be a normal operator, let
T_{\mathbb{C}}:D(T_{\mathbb{C}})\subset{\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}}\mapsto{\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}}
be its complex extension, and let
E_{\mathbb{C}}:{\rm Bor}(\mathfrak{S})\mapsto\mathcal{B}({\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}})
be the spectral measure of
T_{\mathbb{C}}
, where
\mathfrak{S}=\sigma(T_{\mathbb{C}})
. Then the restriction
E_{\mathbb{R}}
of the complex spectral measure
E_{\mathbb{C}}
to
\rm{Bor}_{E_{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathfrak{S})
induces a real spectral measure
with values in
\mathcal{B}({\mathfrak{H}})
, such that

\langle Tx,y\rangle=\int_{\mathfrak{S}}zd\mu_{x,y},\,\,x\in D(T),y\in{\mathfrak{H}},

where
\mu_{x,y}(A)=\langle E_{\mathbb{R}}(A)x,y\rangle
for all
A\in\rm{Bor}_{E_{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathfrak{S})
.

Setting
\mathcal{D}_{f,{\mathfrak{H}}}=\mathcal{D}_{f}\cap{\mathfrak{H}}
,
for every
E_{\mathbb{C}}
-stem function
f\in\mathfrak{B}_{s,E_{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathfrak{S})
we define
the map
\Phi(f)
via the equality

\langle\Phi(f)x,y\rangle=\int_{\mathfrak{S}}fd\mu_{x,y},\,\,x\in\mathcal{D}_{f,{\mathfrak{H}}},y\in{\mathfrak{H}},

(3)

which is a normal operator with domain
D(\Phi(f))=\mathcal{D}_{f,{\mathfrak{H}}}
,
satisfying

\|\Phi(f)x\|^{2}=\int_{\mathfrak{S}}|f|^{2}d\mu_{x,x},\,\,x\in\mathcal{D}_{f,{\mathfrak{H}}}.

Moreover,

\Phi(f)\Phi(g)\subset\Phi(fg)
, with
D(\Phi(f)\Phi(g))=\mathcal{D}_{g,{\mathfrak{H}}}\cap\mathcal{D}_{fg,{\mathfrak{H}}}
. When
D(\Phi(f)\Phi(g))=\mathcal{D}_{g,{\mathfrak{H}}}
,
we actually have
\Phi(f)\Phi(g)=\Phi(fg)
.

In addition, if
S\in\mathcal{B}({\mathfrak{H}})
and
STx=TSx
for all
x\in D(T)
, then
SE_{\mathbb{R}}(A)=E_{\mathbb{R}}(A)S
for all
A\in\rm{Bor}_{E_{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathfrak{S})
.

Proof
Note that if
\{\mu_{\xi,\eta},\xi,\eta\in{\mathfrak{H}}_{C}\}
is the family of scalar measures associated to the spectral measure
E_{\mathbb{C}}
, it ”contains“ the family
\{\mu_{x,y},x,y\in{\mathfrak{H}}\}
, defined in the statement, via a natural identification.

Let
f\in\mathfrak{B}_{s,E_{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathfrak{S})
be bounded.
Then the operator
\Psi(f)
, given by the equation (
2
) is a bounded normal
operator, by Theorem 13.24 from
[
15
]
. Because
f
is bounded, it is possible to approximate it with elementary functions, as in Remark
1
(4), leading to
the conclusion that the space
{\mathfrak{H}}
is invariant under the operator
\Psi(f)
, via the
corresponding argument from the proof of Theorem
1
. Moreover, the formula

\langle\Psi(f)x,y\rangle=\int_{\mathfrak{S}}fd\mu_{x,y},\,\,x,y\in{\mathfrak{H}}

clearly holds.

If
f\in\mathfrak{B}_{s,E_{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathfrak{S})
is not bounded, we define the
sets
\mathfrak{S}_{n}=\{z\in\mathfrak{S};|f(z)|\leq n\}
, for every integer

n\geq 1
, which are conjugate symmetric. Setting
f_{n}=\chi_{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}f
,
we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|\Psi(f_{n})x-\Psi(f)x\|^{2}\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathfrak{S}}|f_{n}-f|^{2}d\mu_{x,x}=0,\,\,x\in\mathcal{D}_{f}\cap\in{\mathfrak{H}},

by Legesque’s theorem of dominated convergence (as in formula (7) from the proof
of Theorem 13.24 in
[
15
]

\Psi(f)x\in{\mathfrak{H}}
.

We therefore set

\Phi(f)=\Psi(f)|\mathcal{D}_{f,{\mathfrak{H}}}
for all
f\in\mathfrak{B}_{s,E_{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathfrak{S})
, which is precisely the map from (
3
). Note that we have

\|\Phi(f)x\|^{2}=\|\Psi(f)x\|^{2}=\int_{\mathfrak{S}}|f|^{2}d\mu_{x,x},\,\,x\in\mathcal{D}_{f,{\mathfrak{H}}},

via the corresponding property of the spectral measure
E_{\mathbb{C}}
, proved in Theorem 13.24 from
[
15
]
.

Next, if
x\in\mathcal{D}_{g,{\mathfrak{H}}}\cap\mathcal{D}_{fg,{\mathfrak{H}}}
, then
\Phi(f)\Phi(g)x=\Psi(f)\Psi(g)x=\Psi(fg)x=\Phi(fg)x
, because
\mathcal{D}_{g,{\mathfrak{H}}}\cap\mathcal{D}_{fg,{\mathfrak{H}}}\subset\mathcal{D}_{g}\cap\mathcal{D}_{fg}
. Then, if
\mathcal{D}_{g,{\mathfrak{H}}}\cap\mathcal{D}_{fg,{\mathfrak{H}}}=\mathcal{D}_{g,{\mathfrak{H}}}
, we must have

\Phi(f)\Phi(g)=\Phi(fg)
, again as in in Theorem 13.24 from
[
15
]
.

Finally, if
S\in\mathcal{B}({\mathfrak{H}})
and
STx=TSx
, we must have
S_{\mathbb{C}}T_{\mathbb{C}}\xi=T_{\mathbb{C}}S_{\mathbb{C}}\xi
for all
\xi\in D(T_{\mathbb{C}})
. Therefore,
S_{\mathbb{C}}E_{\mathbb{C}}(A)=E_{\mathbb{C}}(A)S_{\mathbb{C}}
for all
A\in{\rm Bor}(\mathfrak{S})
, via Theorem 13.33 from
[
15
]
. Passing to restrictions to
{\mathfrak{H}}
,
we obtain
SE_{\mathbb{R}}(A)=E_{\mathbb{R}}(A)S
for all
A\in\rm{Bor}_{E_{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathfrak{S})
.

A uniqueness result of the real spectral measure attached to a real normal operator
is also valid for unbounded operators.

Proposition 2

Let
T\in\mathcal{C}({\mathfrak{H}})
be a real normal operator, and let
F_{\mathbb{R}}:\rm{Bor}_{E_{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathfrak{S})\penalty\mapsto\mathcal{B}({\mathfrak{H}})

(a) The inclusion
T(F_{\mathbb{R}}(A){\mathfrak{H}}\cap D(T))\subset F_{\mathbb{R}}(A){\mathfrak{H}}

.

Then the real spectral measure
F_{\mathbb{R}}
coincides with the real spectral measure obtained
by the restriction to the
\sigma
-algebra
\rm{Bor}_{E_{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathfrak{S})
of the spectral measure
E_{\mathbb{C}}
of the complex extension
T_{\mathbb{C}}
of

.

Proof
. If
A\in\rm{Bor}_{E_{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathfrak{S})

\sigma_{\mathbb{C}}(T|F_{\mathbb{R}}(A){\mathfrak{H}}\cap D(T))\subset A

Hilbert Spaces

3.1
Quaternionic Algebra as a Hilbert Space

Let us recall some known definitions and elementary
facts (see, for instance,
[
7
]
, Section 4.6, and/or

[
19
]
).

Let
{\mathbb{H}}
be the abstract algebra of quaternions, which is the four-dimensional
{\mathbb{R}}
-algebra with
unit
1
, generated by the ”imaginary units“
\{\bf{j,k,l}\}
,
which satisfy

{\bf jk=-kj=l,\,kl=-lk=j,\,lj=-jl=k,\,jj=kk=ll}=-1.

We may assume that
{\mathbb{H}}\supset{\mathbb{R}}
identifying every number

x\in{\mathbb{R}}
with the element
x1\in{\mathbb{H}}
.

The algebra
{\mathbb{H}}
has a natural involution

{\mathbb{H}}\ni{\bf x}=x_{0}+x_{1}{\bf j}+x_{2}{\bf k}+x_{3}{\bf l}\mapsto{\bf x}^{*}=x_{0}-x_{1}{\bf j}-x_{2}{\bf k}-x_{3}{\bf l}\in{\mathfrak{H}}.

For an arbitrary quaternion
{{\bf x}}=x_{0}+x_{1}{\bf j}+x_{2}{\bf k}+x_{3}{\bf l},\,\,x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\in{\mathbb{R}}
, we set
\Re{\bf x}=x_{0}=({\bf x}+{\bf x}^{*})/2
, and
\Im{\bf x}=x_{1}{\bf j}+x_{2}{\bf k}+x_{3}{\bf l}=({\bf x}-{\bf x}^{*})/2
, that is, the
real
and

imaginary part
of
{\bf x}
, respectively.

The algebra
{\mathbb{H}}
may be regarded as a real Hilbert space, with the inner product

\langle{\bf x},{\bf y}\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}=\sum_{m=0}^{3}x_{m}y_{m},\,\,{\bf x}=x_{0}+x_{1}{\bf j}+x_{2}{\bf k}+x_{3}{\bf l},\,{\bf y}=y_{0}+y_{1}{\bf j}+y_{2}{\bf k}+y_{3}{\bf l}\in{\mathbb{H}},

denoted simply by
\langle{\bf x},{\bf y}\rangle
when no confusion is possible.

A direct computation shows that

\langle{\bf q}{\bf x},{\bf y}\rangle=\langle{\bf x},{\bf q}^{*}{\bf y}\rangle,\,\,\langle{\bf x}{\bf q},{\bf y}\rangle=\langle{\bf x},{\bf y}{\bf q}^{*}\rangle\,\,{\bf x},{\bf y},{\bf q}\in{\mathbb{H}}.

The natural norm associated to the inner product
\langle{\bf x},{\bf y}\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}
is given by

\|{\bf x}\|_{\mathbb{H}}=\sqrt{x_{0}^{2}+x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+x_{0}^{2}},\,\,{\bf x}=x_{0}+x_{1}{\bf j}+x_{2}{\bf k}+x_{3}{\bf l},\,\,x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\in{\mathbb{R}},

which is a multiplicative norm, simply denoted by
\|{\bf x}\|
.

Note that
{\bf x}{\bf x}^{*}={\bf x}^{*}{\bf x}=\|{\bf x}\|^{2}
, implying, in particular, that every element
{\bf x}\in{\mathbb{H}}\setminus\{0\}
is invertible, and
{\bf x}^{-1}=\|{\bf x}\|^{-2}{\bf x}^{*}
.

As an element of a real algebra, each
{\bf x}\in{\mathbb{H}}
has a complex spectrum given by
\sigma_{\mathbb{C}}({\bf x})=\{\Re{\bf x}\pm i\|\Im{\bf x}\|\}
(see
[
19
]
for details). In particular, if
\lambda\in\sigma_{\mathbb{C}}({\bf x})
, then
|\lambda|=\|{\bf x}\|
.

We now consider the complexification
{\mathbb{C}}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}{\mathbb{H}}

of the
{\mathbb{R}}
-algebra
{\mathbb{H}}
(see also
[
10
]
), which will be
identified with the direct sum
{\mathbb{M}}={\mathbb{H}}+i{\mathbb{H}}
.
Of course, the algebra
{\mathbb{M}}
contains the complex field
{\mathbb{C}}
. Moreover, in the algebra
{\mathbb{M}}
, the elements of
{\mathbb{H}}
commute with all complex numbers. In particular, the ”imaginary units“

\bf j,k,l
of the algebra
{\mathbb{H}}
are independent of and commute with the imaginary unit
i
of the complex plane
{\mathbb{C}}
.

The algebra
{\mathbb{M}}
is an involutive one, whose involution is given by

({\bf x}+i{\bf y})^{*}={\bf x}^{*}-i{\bf y}^{*}
for all
{\bf x},{\bf y}\in{\mathbb{H}}
.

In the algebra
{\mathbb{M}}
, there also exists a natural conjugation defined
by
\bar{\bf a}={\bf b}-i{\bf c}
, where
{\bf a}={\bf b}+i{\bf c}
is arbitrary in
{\mathbb{M}}
, with
{\bf b},{\bf c}\in{\mathbb{H}}
(see also

[
10
]
). Note that
\overline{\bf a+b}=\bar{\bf a}+\bar{\bf b}
, and
\overline{\bf ab}=\bar{\bf a}\bar{\bf b}
, in particular
\overline{r\bf a}=r\bar{\bf a}
for all
{\bf a},{\bf b}\in{\mathbb{M}}
, and
r\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Moreover,
\bar{{\bf a}}={\bf a}
if and only if
{\bf a}\in{\mathbb{H}}
, which is a useful characterization of the elements of
{\mathbb{H}}
among those of
{\mathbb{M}}
.

The algebra
{\mathbb{M}}
may also be regarded as a (complex) Hilbert space, with the inner product given by

\langle{\bf x}+i{\bf y},{\bf u}+i{\bf v}\rangle_{\mathbb{M}}=\langle{\bf x},{\bf u}\rangle-\langle{\bf y},{\bf v}\rangle+i\langle{\bf y},{\bf u}\rangle-i\langle x,{\bf v}\rangle

for all
{\bf x},{\bf y},{\bf u},{\bf v}\in{\mathbb{H}}
, which is the natural extension of the inner oroduct of
{\mathbb{H}}
. It will be
simply denoted by
\langle{\bf x}+i{\bf y},{\bf u}+i{\bf v}\rangle
, when no confusion is possiblde.

It is also clear that

\langle{\bf q}({\bf x}+i{\bf y}),{\bf u}+i{\bf v}\rangle=\langle{\bf x}+i{\bf y},{\bf q}^{*}({\bf u}+i{\bf v})\rangle,

\langle({\bf x}+i{\bf y}){\bf q},{\bf u}+i{\bf v}\rangle=\langle{\bf x}+i{\bf y},({\bf u}+i{\bf v}){\bf q}^{*}\rangle,

for all
{\bf x},{\bf y},{\bf q},{\bf u},{\bf v}\in{\mathbb{H}}

3.2
Quaternionic Hilbert spaces

Remark 5

Following
[
7
]
, a
right
{\mathbb{H}}
-vector space

\mathcal{V}
is a real vector space
having a right multiplication with the elements of
{\mathbb{H}}
, such that

x1=x,\,(x+y){\bf q}=x{\bf q}+y{\bf q},\,x({\bf q}+{\bf s})=x{\bf q}+x{\bf s},\,x({\bf q}{\bf s})=(x{\bf q}){\bf s}

for all
x,y\in\mathcal{V}
and
{\bf q},{\bf s}\in{\mathbb{H}}
.

If
{\mathfrak{H}}
is a Hilbert space, which is a right
{\mathbb{H}}
-vector space, the operator

T\in\mathcal{B}({\mathfrak{H}})
is
right
{\mathbb{H}}
-linear
if

T(x{\bf q})=T(x){\bf q}
for all
x\in{\mathfrak{H}}
and

{\bf q}\in{\mathbb{H}}
. The set of right
{\mathbb{H}}
linear operators will be
denoted by
\mathcal{B}^{\rm r}(\mathcal{V})
, which is, in particular, a unital
real algebra.

In a similar way, one defines the concept of a
left
{\mathbb{H}}
-vector space
. A real vector space
\mathcal{V}
will be said to be an
{\mathbb{H}}
-vector space
if it is simultaneously a right
{\mathbb{H}}
- and a left
{\mathbb{H}}
-vector space. As noticed in
[
7
]
,
it is the framework of
{\mathbb{H}}
-vector spaces an appropriate one
for the study of right
{\mathbb{H}}
-linear operators.

If
{\mathfrak{H}}
is
{\mathbb{H}}
-vector space which is also a Hilbert space with the inner product

\langle*,*\rangle
and norm
\|*\|
, assuming that

\langle{\bf q}x,y\rangle=\langle x,{\bf q}^{*}y\rangle,\,\langle x{\bf q},y\rangle=\langle x,y{\bf q}^{*}\rangle,\,\,x,y\in{\mathfrak{H}},\,{\bf q}\in{\mathbb{H}},

then
{\mathfrak{H}}
is said to be a
Hilbert
{\mathbb{H}}
-space
. In this case, we have that
R_{\bf q}\in\mathcal{B}({\mathfrak{H}})
, and
the map
{\mathbb{H}}\ni{\bf q}\mapsto R_{\bf q}\in\mathcal{B}({\mathfrak{H}})
is norm continuous, where
R_{\bf q}
is the right multiplication of the elements of
{\mathfrak{H}}
by a given quaternion
{{\bf q}}\in{\mathbb{H}}
. In fact, this map is actually an isometry because we have

\|R_{\bf q}x\|=\|{\bf q}\|\|x\|
for all
{\bf q}\in{\mathbb{H}}
and
x\in{\mathfrak{H}}
.

Similarly, if
L_{{\bf q}}
is the left multiplication of the elements of

{\mathfrak{H}}
by the quaternion
{{\bf q}}\in{\mathbb{H}}
, we have

L_{\bf q}\in\mathcal{B}({\mathfrak{H}})
for all
{\bf q}\in{\mathbb{H}}
, because
\|L_{\bf q}x\|=\|{\bf q}\|\|x\|
for all
{\bf q}\in{\mathbb{H}}
and
x\in{\mathfrak{H}}
.
Note also that

\mathcal{B}^{\rm r}(H)=\{T\in\mathcal{B}({\mathfrak{H}});TR_{\bf q}=R_{\bf q}T,\,{\bf q}\in{\mathbb{H}}\}.

Remark 6

To adapt the discussion regarding the real algebras to this case, we first consider the complexification
{\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}}
of
{\mathfrak{H}}
. Because
{\mathfrak{H}}
is an
{\mathbb{H}}
-bimodule, the space
{\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}}
is actually an
{\mathbb{M}}
-bimodule, via the multiplications

({\bf q}+i{\bf s})(x+iy)={\bf q}x-{\bf s}y+i({\bf q}y+{\bf s}x),(x+iy)({\bf q}+i{\bf s})=x{\bf q}-y{\bf s}+i(y{\bf q}+x{\bf s}),

for all
{\bf q}+i{\bf s}\in{\mathbb{M}},\,{\bf q},{\bf s}\in{\mathbb{H}},\,x+iy\in{\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}},\,x,y\in{\mathfrak{H}}
. Moreover, if the operator
T\in\mathcal{B}({\mathfrak{H}})
is right
{\mathbb{H}}
-linear, the operator

T_{\mathbb{C}}
is right
{\mathbb{M}}
-linear, that is
T_{\mathbb{C}}((x+iy)({\bf q}+i{\bf s}))=T_{\mathbb{C}}(x+iy)({\bf q}+i{\bf s})
for all
{\bf q}+i{\bf s}\in{\mathbb{M}},\,x+iy\in{\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}}
, via a direct computation.

Let
C
be the natural conjugation of
{\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}}
. As in the real case, for every

S\in\mathcal{B}({\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}})
, we put
S^{\flat}=CSC
. The left and right
multiplication with the quaternion
{\bf q}
on
{\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}}
will be also denoted by
L_{\bf q},R_{\bf q}
, respectively, as elements of
\mathcal{B}({\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}})
. We set

\mathcal{B}^{\rm r}({\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}})=\{S\in\mathcal{B}({\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}});SR_{\bf q}=R_{\bf q}S,\,{\bf q}\in{\mathbb{H}}\},

which is a unital complex algebra containing all operators
L_{\bf q},{\bf q}\in{\mathbb{H}}
.
Note that if
S\in\mathcal{B}^{\rm r}({\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}})
, then
S^{\flat}\in\mathcal{B}^{\rm r}({\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}})
. Indeed, because
CR_{\bf q}=R_{\bf q}C
, we also have
S^{\flat}R_{\bf q}=R_{\bf q}S^{\flat}
. In fact, as we have

(S+S^{\flat})({\mathfrak{H}})\subset{\mathfrak{H}}
and
i(S-S^{\flat})({\mathfrak{H}})\subset{\mathfrak{H}}
, it folows that
the algebras
\mathcal{B}^{\rm r}({\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}}),\,\mathcal{B}^{\rm r}({\mathfrak{H}})_{\mathbb{C}}

are isomorphic, and they will be often identified, where

\mathcal{B^{\rm r}({\mathfrak{H}})}_{\mathbb{C}}=\mathcal{B^{\rm r}({\mathfrak{H}})}+i\mathcal{B^{\rm r}({\mathfrak{H}})}
is the complexification of
\mathcal{B}^{\rm r}({\mathfrak{H}})
, which is also a unital complex Banach algebra.

3.3
Bounded Normal Operators in Quaternionic Hilbert Spaces

Let
{\mathfrak{H}}
be a Hilbert
{\mathbb{H}}
-space, and let
{\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}}
be its complexification. Being, in particular, a real Hilbert space, the adjoint
T^{*}\in\mathcal{B}({\mathfrak{H}})
of an operator

T\in\mathcal{B}({\mathfrak{H}})
and its normality are defined as in the framework of
real operators. We are mainly interested by normal operators

T\in\mathcal{B}^{\rm r}({\mathfrak{H}})
. In this case we also have
T^{*}\in\mathcal{B}^{\rm r}({\mathfrak{H}})
and, of course,
T
is normal if
TT^{*}=T^{*}T
.

If
T\in\mathcal{B}^{\rm r}({\mathfrak{H}})
is normal, then
T_{\mathbb{C}}\in\mathcal{B}^{\rm r}({\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}})

is normal, and it has a spectral measure
E_{\mathbb{C}}
defined on
{\rm Bor}(\mathfrak{S})
,
having a priori values in
\mathcal{B}({\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}})
, where

\mathfrak{S}=\sigma(T_{\mathbb{C}})
. Because the operators
R_{\bf q}({\bf q}\in{\mathbb{H}})

commute with
T_{\mathbb{C}}
, they should also commute with its spectral measure,
implying that the spectral measure
E_{\mathbb{C}}
takes values actually in
\mathcal{B}^{\rm r}({\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}})
.

Then we have the following spectral theorem for bounded quaternionic normal operators.

Theorem 3

Let
T\in\mathcal{B}^{\rm r}({\mathfrak{H}})
be a normal operator, let
T_{\mathbb{C}}\in\mathcal{B}^{\rm r}({\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}})
be its complex extension, and let
E_{\mathbb{C}}:{\rm Bor}(\mathfrak{S})\mapsto\mathcal{B}^{\rm r}({\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}})
be the spectral measure of
T_{\mathbb{C}}
, where

\mathfrak{S}=\sigma(T_{\mathbb{C}})
. Then the restriction
E_{\mathbb{R}}
of the complex spectral measure
E_{\mathbb{C}}
to the
\sigma
-algebra
\rm{Bor}_{E_{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathfrak{S})
induces a
real spectral measure with values in
\mathcal{B}^{\rm r}({\mathfrak{H}})
, such that the map
\Phi
, given by the formula

\langle\Phi(f)x,y\rangle=\int_{\mathfrak{S}}fd\mu_{x,y},\,\,f\in L^{\infty}_{s}(\mathfrak{S}),\,x,y\in{\mathfrak{H}},

with
\mu_{x,y}(A)=\langle E_{\mathbb{R}}(A)x,y\rangle
for all
A\in\rm{Bor}_{E_{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathfrak{S})
, is a unital real algebra isomorphic morphism from
L^{\infty}_{s}(\mathfrak{S},E_{\mathbb{C}})
into
\mathcal{B}^{\rm r}({\mathfrak{H}})
, where
\rm{Bor}_{E_{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathfrak{S})
is the
\sigma
-algebra

\{A\in\rm{Bor}(\mathfrak{S});E_{\mathbb{C}}(A)=E_{\mathbb{C}}(A^{c})\}
. Moreover,m

(i) For every polynomial with real coeficients
p(z)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}z^{j},\,z\in{\mathbb{C}},
one has
\Phi(p)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}T^{j}
.

(ii)
\Phi(f)
is a real normal operator and
\Phi(\bar{f})=\Phi(f)^{*}
for all

f\in L^{\infty}_{s}(\mathfrak{S},E_{\mathbb{C}})
.

(iii)
\|\Phi(f)x\|^{2}=\int_{\mathfrak{S}}|f|^{2}d\mu_{x,x}
for all

f\in L^{\infty}_{s}(\mathfrak{S},E_{\mathbb{C}})
and
x\in{\mathfrak{H}}
.

(iv)
\sigma_{\mathbb{C}}(T|E_{\mathbb{R}}(A){\mathfrak{H}})\subset[A]
for all
A\in\rm{Bor}_{E_{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathfrak{S})
.

(v) If
S\in\mathcal{B}^{\rm r}({\mathfrak{H}})
and
ST=TS
, then
SE_{\mathbb{R}}(A)=E_{\mathbb{R}}(A)S
for all
A\in\rm{Bor}_{E_{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathfrak{S})
.

Proof
This a version of Theorem
1
, whose proof is similar to the quoted result, via some minor modifications. For instance,
when
f=\Sigma_{j\in J}(r_{j}\chi_{A_{j}}+is_{j}\theta_{A_{j}})
is an elementary function, we must have

f(T_{\mathbb{C}})R_{\bf q}=\Sigma_{j\in J}(r_{j}E_{\mathbb{C}}(A_{j})+is_{j}\theta_{A_{j}}(T_{\mathbb{C}}))R_{\bf q}=R_{\bf q}f(T_{\mathbb{C}}),\,\,{\bf q}\in{\mathbb{H}},

because the spectral measure
E_{\mathbb{C}}
is
\mathcal{B}^{\rm r}({\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}})
-valued.
This commutation property also holds by passing to limits, showing that

f(T_{\mathbb{C}})\in\mathcal{B}^{\rm r}({\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}})
for all
f\in L^{\infty}_{s}(\mathfrak{S},E_{\mathbb{C}})
.
Consequently,
\Phi(f)=f(T_{\mathbb{C}})|{\mathfrak{H}}\in\mathcal{B}^{\rm r}({\mathfrak{H}})
for all
f\in L^{\infty}_{s}(\mathfrak{S},E_{\mathbb{C}})
.

Other details are left to the reader.

We also have a uniqueness result corresponding to Proposition
1
, having a similar proof, which will be omitted.

Proposition 3

Let
T\in\mathcal{B}^{\rm r}({\mathfrak{H}})
be a real normal operator, and let
F_{\mathbb{R}}:\rm{Bor}_{E_{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathfrak{S})\penalty\mapsto\mathcal{B}^{\rm r}({\mathfrak{H}})
be a real spectral
measure with the following properties.

(a)
TF_{\mathbb{R}}(A){\mathfrak{H}}\subset F_{\mathbb{R}}(A){\mathfrak{H}}
for all
A\in\rm{Bor}_{E_{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathfrak{S})
.

(b) We have the inclusion
\sigma_{\mathbb{C}}(T|F_{\mathbb{R}}(A){\mathfrak{H}})\subset[A]
for all
A\in\rm{Bor}_{E_{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathfrak{S})
.

Then the real spectral measure
F_{\mathbb{R}}
coincides with the real spectral measure obtained
by the restriction to the
\sigma
-algebra
\rm{Bor}_{E_{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathfrak{S})
of the spectral measure
E_{\mathbb{C}}
of the complex extension
T_{\mathbb{C}}
of
T
.

3.4
Unbounded Normal Operators in Quaternionic

Hilbert Spaces

Let
{\mathfrak{H}}
be a Hilbert
{\mathbb{H}}
-space, which is, in particular, a real Hilbert space.
Let also
T
be an operator from the family

\mathcal{C}({\mathfrak{H}})
. Therefore, the adjoint
T^{*}\in\mathcal{C}({\mathfrak{H}})
of
T
and its normality are defined as in the framework of real operators.

The operator
T\in\mathcal{C}({\mathfrak{H}})
is said to be
right
{\mathbb{H}}
-linear
if
R_{\bf q}D(T)\subset D(T)
, and
T(x{\bf q})=T(x){\bf q}
for all
x\in D(T)
and

{\bf q}\in{\mathbb{H}}
. The family of right
{\mathbb{H}}
-linear operators from
\mathcal{C}({\mathfrak{H}})
will be
denoted by
\mathcal{C}^{\rm r}(\mathcal{{\mathfrak{H}}})
. A direct calculation shows that, if
T\in\mathcal{C}^{\rm r}(\mathcal{{\mathfrak{H}}})
, we have
\langle(T^{*}R_{\bf q})x,y\rangle=\langle(R_{\bf q}T^{*})x,y\rangle
for all
x\in D(T^{*}),y\in D(T)
, showing that
T^{*}\in\mathcal{C}^{\rm r}(\mathcal{{\mathfrak{H}}})
.

As before, we are particularly interested by normal operators

T\in\mathcal{C}^{\rm r}({\mathfrak{H}})
. Then
T^{*}\in\mathcal{C}^{\rm r}({\mathfrak{H}})
, and
T

is normal if
D(T^{*})=D(T)
,
D(TT^{*})=D(T^{*}T)
, and
TT^{*}=T^{*}T
.

We now consider the complexification
{\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}}
of
{\mathfrak{H}}
. If
T\in\mathcal{C}({\mathfrak{H}})
, then

T_{\mathbb{C}}\in\mathcal{C}({\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}})
, and
D(T_{\mathbb{C}})=D(T)_{\mathbb{C}}
. Moreover, if
T\in\mathcal{C}^{\rm r}(\mathcal{{\mathfrak{H}}})
, then
T_{\mathbb{C}}\in\mathcal{C}^{\rm r}(\mathcal{{\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}}})
, where the latter space consists of those operators from

\mathcal{C}({\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}})
which are right
{\mathbb{H}}
-linear.

When the operator
T\in\mathcal{C}({\mathfrak{H}})
is normal, and so its complex extension
T_{\mathbb{C}}\in\mathcal{C}({\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}})
is also normal, having a unique spectral measure

E_{\mathbb{C}}:\rm{Bor}(\mathfrak{S})\mapsto\mathcal{B}({\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}})
, whose values are orthogonal projections commuting with
T_{\mathbb{C}}
, as in Subsection 2.4. Let also

\{\mu_{\xi,\eta};\xi,\eta\in{\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}}\}
be the family of complex valued associated measures with the spectral
measure
E_{\mathbb{C}}
. If
T\in\mathcal{C}^{\rm r}(\mathcal{{\mathfrak{H}}})
, and so
T_{\mathbb{C}}\in\mathcal{C}^{\rm r}(\mathcal{{\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}}})
because
R_{\bf q}T_{\mathbb{C}}\xi=T_{\mathbb{C}}R_{\bf q}\xi
for all
\xi\in D(T_{\mathbb{C}})
and
{\bf q}\in{\mathbb{H}}
, we must actually have

E_{\mathbb{C}}:\rm{Bor}(\mathfrak{S})\mapsto\mathcal{B}^{\rm r}({\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}})
, as follows from
Theorem 13.33 from
[
15
]
or Proposition 5.26 from
[
16
]
.

The next result is a functional calculus with a class of Borel functions, valid for unbounded
normal operators in Hilbert
{\mathbb{H}}
-spaces. The notation
\mathcal{D}_{f}
is given by the equality (
1
).

Theorem 4

Let
{\mathfrak{H}}
be an
{\mathbb{H}}
-space, and let

T\in\mathcal{C}^{\rm r}({\mathfrak{H}})
be a normal operator. Let also
T_{\mathbb{C}}\in\mathcal{C}^{\rm r}({\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}})
be its complex extension, and let
E_{\mathbb{C}}:{\rm Bor}(\mathfrak{S})\mapsto\mathcal{B}^{\rm r}({\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}})
be the spectral measure of
T_{\mathbb{C}}
, where
\mathfrak{S}=\sigma(T_{\mathbb{C}})
.
Then the restriction
E_{\mathbb{R}}
of the complex spectral measure
E_{\mathbb{C}}
to
\rm{Bor}_{E_{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathfrak{S})
induces a real spectral measure
E_{\mathbb{R}}

with values in
\mathcal{B}^{\rm r}({\mathfrak{H}})
, such that

\langle Tx,y\rangle=\int_{\mathfrak{S}}zd\mu_{x,y},\,\,x\in D(T),y\in{\mathfrak{H}},

where
\mu_{x,y}(A)=\langle E_{\mathbb{R}}(A)x,y\rangle
for all
A\in\rm{Bor}_{E_{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathfrak{S})
.

Setting
\mathcal{D}_{f,{\mathfrak{H}}}=\mathcal{D}_{f}\cap{\mathfrak{H}}
,
for every function
f\in\mathfrak{B}_{s,E_{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathfrak{S})
we put

\langle\Phi(f)x,y\rangle=\int_{\mathfrak{S}}fd\mu_{x,y},\,\,x\in\mathcal{D}_{f,{\mathfrak{H}}},y\in{\mathfrak{H}},

which is a normal operator in
\mathcal{C}^{\rm r}({\mathfrak{H}})
, with domain
D(\Phi(f))=\mathcal{D}_{f,{\mathfrak{H}}}
,
satisfying

\|\Phi(f)x\|^{2}=\int_{\mathfrak{S}}|f|^{2}d\mu_{x,x},\,\,x\in\mathcal{D}_{f,{\mathfrak{H}}}.

Moreover,

\Phi(f)\Phi(g)\subset\Phi(fg)
, with
D(\Phi(f)\Phi(g))=\mathcal{D}_{g,{\mathfrak{H}}}\cap\mathcal{D}_{fg,{\mathfrak{H}}}
. When
D(\Phi(f)\Phi(g))=\mathcal{D}_{g,{\mathfrak{H}}}
,
we actually have
\Phi(f)\Phi(g)=\Phi(fg)
.

In addition, if
S\in\mathcal{B}^{\rm r}({\mathfrak{H}})
and
STx=TSx
for all
x\in D(T)
, then
SE_{\mathbb{R}}(A)=E_{\mathbb{R}}(A)S
for all
A\in\rm{Bor}_{E_{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathfrak{S})
.

Proof
The proof of this assertion is similar to that of Theorem
2
, except for
some details. For instance, it is clear that for every
E_{\mathbb{C}}
-stem function
f\in\mathfrak{B}_{s,E_{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathfrak{S})
the operator
\Phi(f)
is normal as an
element of
\mathcal{C}({\mathfrak{H}})
. In fact, it actually belongs to
\mathcal{C}^{\rm r}({\mathfrak{H}})
.
Of course, this is true when
f
is bounded, as in the proof of Theorem
3
.
For an arbitrary unbounded function
f\in\mathfrak{B}_{s,E_{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathfrak{S})
, we refine an argument from the proof of Theorem
2
.

We set
\mathfrak{S}_{n}=\{z\in\mathfrak{S};|f(z)|\leq n\}
, for every integer

n\geq 1
, which are conjugate symmetric. Setting also
f_{n}=\chi_{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}f
,
as we have
\lim_{n\to\infty}\Psi(f_{n})x=\Psi(f)x
, and

R_{\bf q}\Psi(f_{n})x=\Psi(f_{n})R_{\bf q}x
, it follows

\lim_{n\to\infty}R_{\bf q}\Psi(f_{n})x=\lim_{n\to\infty}\Psi(f_{n})R_{\bf q}x=R_{q}\Psi(f)x,

showing that
R_{\bf q}x\in D(\Psi(f))
, and
R_{\bf q}\Psi(f)x=\Psi(f)R_{\bf q}x
for all
x\in\mathcal{D}_{f}\cap\in{\mathfrak{H}}
Therefore,
\Psi(f)\in\mathcal{C}^{\rm r}({\mathfrak{H}})
.

Other details are left to the reader.

As in some previous cases, we still have a uniqueness result.

Proposition 4

Let
{\mathfrak{H}}
be
{\mathbb{H}}
-space, let
T\in\mathcal{C}^{\rm r}({\mathfrak{H}})
be a real normal operator, and let
F_{\mathbb{R}}:\rm{Bor}_{E_{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathfrak{S})\mapsto\mathcal{B}^{\rm r}({\mathfrak{H}})
be a real spectral
measure with the following properties.

(a) The inclusion
T(F_{\mathbb{R}}(A){\mathfrak{H}}\cap D(T))\subset F_{\mathbb{R}}(A){\mathfrak{H}}
holds for all
A\in\rm{Bor}_{E_{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathfrak{S})
.

(b) The inclusion
\sigma_{\mathbb{C}}(T|F_{\mathbb{R}}(A){\mathfrak{H}})\subset[A]
holds for every
A\in\rm{Bor}_{E_{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathfrak{S})
.

Then the real spectral measure
F_{\mathbb{R}}
coincides with the real spectral measure obtained
by the restriction to the
\sigma
-algebra
\rm{Bor}_{E_{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathfrak{S})
of the spectral measure
E_{\mathbb{C}}
of the complex extension
T_{\mathbb{C}}
of
T
.

4
An Example

We shall give a basic example of a quaternionic normal operator, defined by the multiplication with the
independent quaternionic variable.

First of all, we recall some definitions and properties from
[
19
]
(see especially Remark 4), also adding a few simple observations. A
subset
S
in
{\mathbb{H}}
is said to be
spectrally saturated
if whenever for an element

{\bf q}\in{\mathbb{H}}
we have
\sigma_{\mathbb{C}}({\bf q})=\sigma_{\mathbb{C}}({\bf s})
for some
{\bf s}\in S
, then
{\bf q}\in S
.
Setting
\mathfrak{S}(\Omega)=\cup_{q\in\Omega}\sigma_{\mathbb{C}}(q)
, for an arbitrary subset
\Omega\in{\mathbb{H}}
, we have
that
\Omega
is spectrally saturared if an only if
\mathfrak{S}(\Omega)
is conjugate symmetric. If
A\subset{\mathbb{C}}
is conjugate symmetric, we put
A_{\mathbb{H}}=\{{\bf q}\in{\mathbb{H}};\sigma_{\mathbb{C}}({\bf q})\subset A\}
, which is spectrally saturated. The assignment
A\mapsto A_{\mathbb{H}}
is a bijection between the conjugate symmetric subsets of
{\mathbb{C}}
and the spectrally saturated subsets of
{\mathbb{H}}
. Moreover, we have the properties
(A\cap B)_{\mathbb{H}}=A_{\mathbb{H}}\cap B_{\mathbb{H}}
and

(A\cup B)_{\mathbb{H}}=A_{\mathbb{H}}\cup B_{\mathbb{H}}
for all conjugate symmetric subsets
A,B
of
{\mathbb{C}}
, as one can easily see. In fact, if
(A_{j})_{j\geq 1}
is a sequnce of conjugate symmetric subsets of
{\mathbb{C}}
, setting
A=\cup_{j\geq 1}A_{j}
, we still have
A_{\mathbb{H}}=\cup_{j\geq 1}A_{j{\mathbb{H}}}
.

Let
{\mathbb{K}}\subset{\mathfrak{H}}
be a compact subset, which is spectraly saturated. Then the conjugate symmetric set

\mathfrak{S}({\mathbb{K}})
is also compact because it is bounded and closed.

Let
\lambda
be the Legesgue measure on
{\mathbb{K}}
(identified with
a subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{4}
). As usually, we denote by
L^{2}({\mathbb{K}},{\mathbb{H}})
the space (of equivalence classes) of square integrable functions
f:{\mathbb{K}}\mapsto{\mathbb{H}}
, regarded as a real vector space. Using the inner product defined on
{\mathbb{H}}
(see Subsection 3.1), we consider the inner product

\langle f,g\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{K}}\langle f({\bf q}),g({\bf q})\rangle d\lambda({\bf q})\,\,\,\,f,g\in L^{2}({\mathbb{K}},{\mathbb{H}}),

on the vector space
L^{2}({\mathbb{K}},{\mathbb{H}})
, which becomes a Hilbert
{\mathbb{H}}
-space, simply denoted by
{\mathfrak{H}}
. Note that
{\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}}
is isomorphic to the complex Hilbert space
L^{2}({\mathbb{K}},{\mathbb{M}})
, whose inner product is induced by that of
{\mathbb{M}}
.

Let
T
be the map given by
Tf({\bf q})={\bf q}f({\bf q})
for all
f\in{\mathfrak{H}}
and
{\bf q}\in{\mathbb{K}}
, that is, the left multiplication with the quaternionic independent variable
{\bf q}
in the space
{\mathfrak{H}}
. Then
T\in\mathcal{B}^{\rm r}({\mathfrak{H}})

and it is a normal operator, because
T^{*}f({\bf q})=q^{*}f({\bf q})
for all
f\in{\mathfrak{H}}
and
{\bf q}\in{\mathbb{K}}
, and obviously

TT^{*}=T^{*}T
.

Note that for every
h\in{\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}}
we have
(\zeta-T_{\mathbb{C}})h=(\zeta-T_{\mathbb{C}})(1)h
. Therefore,
writing
h=1h=[(\zeta-T_{\mathbb{C}})(\zeta-T_{\mathbb{C}})^{-1}(1)]h=(\zeta-T_{\mathbb{C}})[(\zeta-T_{\mathbb{C}})^{-1}(1)h]
,
we obtain

(\zeta-T_{\mathbb{C}})^{-1}(h)=(\zeta-T_{\mathbb{C}})^{-1}[(\zeta-T_{\mathbb{C}})(\zeta-T_{\mathbb{C}})^{-1}(1)h]=

(\zeta-T_{\mathbb{C}})^{-1}(\zeta-T_{\mathbb{C}})[(\zeta-T_{\mathbb{C}})^{-1}(1)h]=[(\zeta-T_{\mathbb{C}})^{-1}(1)]h.

Let us compute the complex spectrum
\sigma_{\mathbb{C}}(T)=\sigma(T_{\mathbb{C}})
of
T
. The complex extension
T_{\mathbb{C}}
of
T

is clearly the left multiplication with the independent quaternionic variable
{\bf q}
in the space
{\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}}
. We shall prove that the spectrum
\sigma(T_{\mathbb{C}})
is given by the conjugate symmetric set
\mathfrak{S}({\mathbb{K}})
. If

\zeta\notin\mathfrak{S}({\mathbb{K}})
, the function

{\bf q}\mapsto(\zeta-{\bf q})^{-1}
is well defined on
{\mathbb{K}}
and the left multiplication with this function on
{\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}}
is equal to the operator
(\zeta-T_{\mathbb{C}})^{-1}
, and so
\zeta\notin\sigma_{\mathbb{C}}(T)
.

To prove the converse, we use an argument from above, and fix a point
\zeta\in\mathfrak{S}({\mathbb{K}})
. So there exists a point
{\bf q}_{0}\in{\mathbb{K}}
such that
\zeta\in\sigma_{\mathbb{C}}({\bf q}_{0})
. Let us show that
\zeta\in\sigma_{\mathbb{C}}(T)
. Assuming that
\zeta\notin\sigma(T_{\mathbb{C}})
, we can write

(\zeta-{\bf q})[(\zeta-T_{\mathbb{C}})^{-1}1]({\bf q})=1
for all
{\bf q}\in{\mathbb{K}}
. We also have
[(\zeta-T_{\mathbb{C}})^{-1}(\zeta-T_{\mathbb{C}})1]({\bf q})=[(\zeta-T_{\mathbb{C}})^{-1}1]({\bf q}](\zeta-{\bf q})=1
, implying, in particular, that

\zeta-{\bf q}_{0}
is invertible in
{\mathbb{M}}
. Hence
\zeta\notin\mathfrak{S}({\mathbb{K}})
, which is impossible. Consequently,
\zeta\in\sigma_{\mathbb{C}}(T)
, proving the equality
\mathfrak{S}({\mathbb{K}})=\sigma_{\mathbb{C}}(T)
.

Let
{\rm Bor}_{c}(\mathfrak{S}({\mathbb{K}}))=\{A=A^{c};A\in{\rm Bor}(\mathfrak{S}({\mathbb{K}}))\}
, which is a
\sigma
-algebra. For every subset
A
in
\mathfrak{S}({\mathbb{K}}))
, we put
A_{\mathbb{K}}=\{q\in{\mathbb{K}};\sigma_{\mathbb{C}}(q)\subset A\}
.
Because
{\mathbb{K}}
is spectrally saturated, we have in fact
A_{\mathbb{K}}=A_{\mathbb{H}}
. Put also

{\mathfrak{H}}_{T}(A)=\{f\in{\mathfrak{H}};{\rm supp}(f)\subset A_{\mathbb{K}}\}
. If
F(A)
is the orthogonal
projection on the subspace
{\mathfrak{H}}_{T}(A)
, the map
{\rm Bor}_{c}(\mathfrak{S}({\mathbb{K}}))\ni A\mapsto F(A)
is a spectral measure. This can be seen because the projection
F(A)
is the multiplication operator with
the characteristic function
\chi_{A_{\mathbb{K}}}
. Specifically, if
A,B\in\mathfrak{S}({\mathbb{K}}))
, we have

F(A\cap B)f=\chi_{(A\cap B)_{\mathbb{K}}}f=\chi_{A_{\mathbb{K}}\cap B_{\mathbb{K}}}f=\chi_{A_{\mathbb{K}}}\chi_{B_{\mathbb{K}}}f=F(A)F(B)f

for each
f\in{\mathfrak{H}}_{\mathbb{C}}
. Moreover, if
(A_{j})_{j\geq 1}
is a sequnce of mutually disjoint subsets in

{\rm Bor}_{c}(\mathfrak{S}({\mathbb{K}}))
, and
A=\cup_{j\geq 1}A_{j}
, we have

\langle F(A)f,g\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{K}}\langle\chi_{A_{\mathbb{K}}}({\bf q})f({\bf q}),g({\bf q})\rangle d\lambda({\bf q})=

\sum_{j\geq 1}\int_{\mathbb{K}}\langle\chi_{A_{j{\mathbb{K}}}}({\bf q})f({\bf q}),g({\bf q})\rangle d\lambda({\bf q})=\sum_{j\geq 1}\langle F(A_{j})f,g\rangle,

showing that the set map
A\mapsto\langle F(A)f,g\rangle
is countably additive, so it is a measure.

Let us finally remark that
TF(A){\mathfrak{H}}\subset F(A){\mathfrak{H}}
, and that

\sigma_{\mathbb{C}}(T|F(A){\mathfrak{H}})\subset[A]
, via a slight modification of the proof that
\sigma_{\mathbb{C}}(T)=\mathfrak{S}({\mathbb{K}})
from above. Consequently, the map
A\mapsto F(A)
is the spectral measure of the normal
operator
T
, by Proposition 3.

Supposing that the set
{\mathbb{K}}
is unbounded and closed, yet spectrally saturated, the similar multiplication operator with the independent quaternionic variable will be an unbounded normal operator. Specifically, if

Tf({\bf q})={\bf q}f({\bf q})
for all
f\in{\mathfrak{H}}
, with
{\mathfrak{H}}
as above, and
{\bf q}\in{\mathbb{K}}
, then
T
is an unbounded operator with

D(T)=\{f\in{\mathfrak{H}};Tf\in{\mathfrak{H}}\}
. Moreover,
T^{*}f({\bf q})={\bf q}^{*}f({\bf q})
for all
f\in D(T)
and
{\bf q}\in{\mathbb{K}}
. Clearly,

D(T)=D(T)
,
D(TT^{*})=D(T^{*}T)
, and
TT^{*}=T^{*}T
. Therefore, the operator
T
is normal.

The set
\mathfrak{S}({\mathbb{K}})
is closed and, as in the bounded case, we have the equality

\sigma(T_{\mathbb{C}})=\mathfrak{S}({\mathbb{K}})
. Indeed, the inclusion
\sigma(T_{\mathbb{C}})\subset\mathfrak{S}({\mathbb{K}})
can be obtained as
in the bounded case. For the converse, we adopt an appropriate argument.

Fixing
\zeta\in\mathfrak{S}({\mathbb{K}})
, and choosing a point
{\bf q}_{0}\in{\mathbb{K}}
such that
\zeta\in\sigma_{\mathbb{C}}({\bf q}_{0})
, we
want to us show that
\zeta\in\sigma_{\mathbb{C}}(T)
. Assuming that
\zeta\notin\sigma(T_{\mathbb{C}})
, we take a spectrally
saturated compact subset
{\mathbb{K}}_{0}\subset{\mathbb{K}}
containing
{\bf q}_{0}
, and consider the characteristic function
\chi_{0}

of
{\mathbb{K}}_{0}
. We can write

(\zeta-{\bf q})[(\zeta-T_{\mathbb{C}})^{-1}\chi_{0}]({\bf q})=\chi_{0}({\bf q})
for all
{\bf q}\in{\mathbb{K}}_{0}
.
We also have
[(\zeta-T_{\mathbb{C}})^{-1}(\zeta-T_{\mathbb{C}})\chi_{0}]({\bf q})=[(\zeta-T_{\mathbb{C}})^{-1}1]({\bf q})\chi_{0}({\bf q})(\zeta-{\bf q})=\chi_{0}({\bf q})
, implying, that

\zeta-{\bf q}_{0}
is invertible in
{\mathbb{M}}
. As
\zeta\notin\mathfrak{S}({\mathbb{K}})
is not possible, this completes the proof of the equality
\mathfrak{S}({\mathbb{K}})=\sigma_{\mathbb{C}}(T)
.

The spectral measure of
T
is also given by multiplications with characteristic functions of sets from

{\rm Bor}_{c}(\mathfrak{S}({\mathbb{K}}))
. We omit the details, left to the reader.

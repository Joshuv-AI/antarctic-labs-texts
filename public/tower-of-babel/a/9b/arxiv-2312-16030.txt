A note on the PDE approach to the L ∞ L^{\infty} estimates for complex Hessian equations on transverse Kähler manifolds
P. Sivaram

Abstract
. We adapt the PDE approach of Guo-Phong-Tong and Guo-Phong-Tong-Wang [ 17 , 18 ] to prove an L ∞ L^{\infty} estimate for transverse complex Monge-Ampère equations on homologically orientable transverse Kähler manifolds. As an application, we obtain a purely PDE proof of the regularity of Calabi-Yau cone metrics on ℚ \mathbb{Q} -Gorenstein 𝕋 \mathbb{T} -varieties.



1.
Introduction

The celebrated work of Yau
[
30
]
on the Calabi conjecture sparked a surge of interest in various aspects of the complex Monge-Ampère equation and its applications. Degenerate Monge-Ampère equations, which already appear in Yau’s original work, are unavoidable when dealing with singular varieties or cohomology classes that may not be Kähler (cf.
[
5
,
15
]
). We refer the reader to the survey article
[
28
]
and the references therein for more on complex Monge-Ampère equation on compact Kähler manifolds.

The
L^{\infty}
estimate was the hardest part in solving the complex Monge-Ampère equation and it was achieved in
[
30
]
by using the Moser iteration argument. This argument works if the right hand-side is in
L^{p}
with
p>n
, where
n
is the complex dimension of the manifold. In an important breakthrough, Kolodziej
[
21
]
obtained the
L^{\infty}
estimate, assuming that the right hand-side is in
L^{1}(\log L)^{p}
with
p>1
, and his proof relied completely on techniques from pluripotential theory. Kolodziej’s methods were later extended to the singular and degenerate settings (cf.
[
12
,
13
]
). The pluri-potential theoretic methods were recently extended to the Sasakian setting by Tran-Trung Nghiem
[
29
]
. For a long time a purely PDE approach to the sharp
L^{\infty}
estimates was lacking. The gap was finally filled in a series of works by Guo-Phong-Tong and Guo-Phong-Tong-Wang
[
17
,
18
]
. The main goal of this note is to extend the results in
[
17
,
18
]
to degenerate transverse complex Monge-Ampère equations on homologically orientable transverse Kähler manifolds, which includes Sasakian manifolds as a special case.

Before we state our main theorem let us introduce some definitions on transverse Kähler manifolds.
The setting is as follows. The reader can refer to the section below for more details. Let
(\tilde{S},\tilde{\mathcal{F}})
be a homologically orientable transverse Kähler manifold with a foliation
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}
of dimension one.
For
t\in(0,1],
we define

\omega_{t}=\omega+t\chi~\text{ and }\omega_{t,\varphi}=\omega_{t}+\sqrt{-1}\partial_{b}\overline{\partial}_{b}\varphi,\text{ for }\varphi\in C^{2}_{b}(\tilde{S}),

where
\omega
is a closed basic
(1,1)
form which is
nef
and
\chi
is the given transverse Kähler form. Then by definition
[\omega_{t}]
is a transverse Kähler class, but
\omega_{t}
may not be a transverse Kähler form. Since the foliation
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}
is homologically orientable, by
[
24
,
26
]
there exists a nowhere vanishing vector field
\xi
tangent to
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}
at all points and a Riemannian metric
g
such that
\xi
is a Killing field with respect to
g
. And we define a
1
-form
\eta
on
\tilde{S}
by

\eta(\cdot):=\frac{g(\xi,\cdot)}{g(\xi,\xi)}.

Then by definition
\eta(\xi)=1
and
d\eta
is a basic form.

Our goal is to obtain an
L^{\infty}
-estimate for the solutions
\varphi_{t}
that is independent of
t\in(0,1]
of the following transverse complex Monge-Ampère equations

(1.1)

\begin{cases}(\omega_{t}+\sqrt{-1}\partial_{b}\overline{\partial}_{b}\varphi_{t})^{n}\wedge\eta=c_{t}e^{F}\chi^{n}\wedge\eta,\\
\sup_{\tilde{S}}\varphi_{t}=0,\end{cases}

where

c_{t}=\frac{V_{t}}{\int_{\tilde{S}}\chi^{n}\wedge\eta},~V_{t}=\int_{\tilde{S}}\omega_{t}^{n}\wedge\eta

are positive constants, and
F
is a real, smooth, basic function, normalized by

\int_{\tilde{S}}e^{F}\chi^{n}\wedge\eta=\int_{\tilde{S}}\chi^{n}\wedge\eta.

Since
d\eta
is basic,
V_{t}
is independent of the representative chosen from the class
[\omega_{t}]
. Now we state our main result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(\tilde{S},\tilde{\mathcal{F}},\chi)
be a transverse Kähler manifold which is homologically orientable and
\omega
be a basic nef class. For a fixed
p>n,
there exist a positive constant
C=C(n,p,\xi,\omega,\chi,\|e^{F}\|_{L^{1}(\log L)^{p}})
such that for all
t\in(0,1]
, we have

0\leq-\varphi_{t}+\mathcal{V}_{t}\leq C,

where
\mathcal{V}_{t}
is the upper envelope of
\omega_{t}
defined by

(1.2)

\mathcal{V}_{t}(x):=\sup\{v(x):v\in PSH(\tilde{S},\omega_{t},\xi),v\leq 0\}.

Note that proof for the above theorem extends easily to more general complex Hessian equations on transverse Kähler manifolds of the kind considered in
[
17
,
18
]
. We outline the argument in the Remark
3.4
.

Remark 1.2
.

There is nothing special about the assumption on the dimension of the foliation
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}
, except it provides an application as the Corollary
1.6
. Once the foliation is transverse Kähler and homologically orientable, then by
[
26
,
24
]
we have the volume form
\nu
on the leaf such that
d\nu
is basic. And then we have the solution to the transverse complex Monge-Ampère equation by
[
1
]
. So, the results of the Theorem
1.1
and the Remark
3.4
are true for higher dimensional foliations which are homologically orientable transverse Kähler as well. Just for simplicity and readability we assume that the dimension of the foliation is one.

Next, we discuss an application of the above estimate to the regularity of conical Ricci flat metrics on an affine
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein
\mathbb{T}
-variety. We first recall some definitions and introduce some notations.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
Y
be a normal affine variety, and let
\mathbb{T}_{\mathbb{C}}\approx(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{k}
be a complex torus. We say that a
\mathbb{T}_{\mathbb{C}}
-action on
Y
is
good
if it is effective and there is a unique point
y_{0}\in Y
that lies in the orbit closure of any
\mathbb{T}
-orbit.

Definition 1.4
.

A normal variety
Y
is called
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein
if the canonical bundle
K_{Y}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier ie. there exist a integer
m
such that
K_{Y}^{\otimes m}
is Cartier. We say that it has Kawamata log terminal
(klt)
singularities if there exists a resolution
\pi:\tilde{Y}\rightarrow Y
is a resolution with

K_{\tilde{Y}}=\pi^{*}K_{Y}+\sum_{i}a_{i}E_{i},

where
a_{i}>-1
for all
i
.

We say that
Y
is a
\mathbb{T}
-variety, if it admits a good action of
\mathbb{T}_{\mathbb{C}}
and is
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein. In this note we will be concerned with
polarized

\mathbb{T}
-varieties
(Y,\xi)
, namely
\mathbb{T}
-varieties decorated with a
Reeb vector field
. Throughout, we will also assume that
Y
has klt singularities. If we let
J
denote the complex structure on the regular set
Y_{reg}
, then
-J\xi
is a vector field that generates a holomorphic
\mathbb{R}_{+}
action with a repulsive fixed point
y_{0}
.

Definition 1.5
.

(1)

A function
f:Y\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is said to be
radial with respect to
\xi
or
\xi
-radial if

\mathcal{L}_{\xi}f=0\text{ and }\mathcal{L}_{-J\xi}f=f.

(2)

We say that
f
is a
conical Kähler potential with respect to
\xi
if it can be expressed as
f=r^{2}
for a
\xi
-radial function
r
on
Y
and
f
is smooth and strictly plurisubharmonic on
Y_{reg}
and continuous on
Y.
For such a function
f
, we say that

\omega=\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}f

is a conical Kähler metric on
Y
.

In this note, we are interested in
Ricci flat
conical Kähler metrics. We say that a
\mathbb{T}
-invariant conical Kähler metric
\omega=\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}r^{2}
on
Y_{reg}
, for some
r\in C^{0}(Y)
is Ricci flat if there exist a
\mathbb{T}
-invariant no-where vanishing section
\Omega\in H^{0}(Y,mK_{Y})
, such that

(1.3)

\omega^{n+1}=\left((\sqrt{-1})^{m(n+1)^{2}}\Omega\wedge\bar{\Omega}\right)^{\frac{1}{m}},\text{ for some }m\geq 1

in the sense of Bedford-Taylor(cf.
[
2
]
). We say that
\omega
is weakly Ricci flat if
r
is only locally bounded. Note that the above equation still makes sense.

Weak Ricci flat metrics arise naturally in the study of tangent cones of Gromov-Hausdorff limits of non-collapsed Kähler-Einstein manifolds on Fano manifolds (cf.
[
11
]
). If the link of the tangent cone is smooth, or equivalently if the only singularity of the tangent cone is at the vertex, then the link is a Sasakian manifold with a Sasakian-Einstein metric. Building on the pioneering work of Martelli-Sparks-Yau
[
23
]
on the volume functional, Collins and Szekelyhidi
[
10
]
extended the notion of
K
-stability to affine Calabi-Yau cones, and proved that a polarized affine
\mathbb{T}
-variety
(Y,\xi)
with an isolated singularity
y_{0}
admits a Ricci flat Kähler cone metric if and only it is
K
-stable. The reader can refer to the excellent expository article
[
22
]
for more details about this circle of ideas relating normalised volume to Ricci flat cones. For the case of toric manifolds, Berman
[
4
]
extended this correspondence to general toric
\mathbb{T}
-varieties with possibly non-isolated singularities. Berman first constructed a weak Ricci flat cone metric, and then proved that the metric was smooth on the regular locus.

It is natural to expect that any weak Ricci flat cone metric is in fact smooth on
Y_{reg}
(cf.
[
22
]
). Indeed as an immediate consequence of our main theorem we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
(Y,\mathbb{T},\xi)
be a complex affine normal variety which is
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein. And
\omega=\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}r^{2}
is a weak Ricci-flat Kähler metric which is conical with respect to
\xi
on
Y
. Then
r^{2}
is smooth on
Y_{reg}.

That the corollary follows from the main theorem is rather standard (cf.
[
13
]
). While we obtained the
L^{\infty}
-estimate for
transverse nef
classes in Theorem
1.1
, to prove the corollary, we only need an estimate for non-negative transverse class. In the context of transverse Kähler manifold, as noted above, this appeared in
[
4
]
for the toric case, and recently in
[
29
]
for the general case. But as noted above, the main difference with
[
29
]
is that our proof is a completely PDE proof. We outline the general argument in Section
4
for the convenience of the reader.

This note is organized in the following way: Section
2
provides an introduction to essential concepts in transverse Kähler geometry and affine
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein varieties. Moving forward to Section
3
, we prove our main theorem, denoted as Theorem
1.1
. And in Section
4
, we establish the smoothness of the conical Calabi-Yau potential on the regular locus of the
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein affine variety, as articulated in Corollary
1.6
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Preliminaries on Transverse Kähler Geometry:

In this section, we introduce some definitions and preliminary results used in the note. Our introduction is very minimal, and we refer the reader to the monograph
[
6
]
by Boyer and Galicki for more on Sasaki manifolds and transverse Kähler manifolds and the book by Molino
[
25
]
for more on foliations.

Definition 2.1
.

(1)

A dimension-
k

foliation of a manifold

S
is described by the following data:

•

an open cover
\{U_{i}\}
,
S=\underset{i}{\cup}U_{i}.

•

a smooth manifold
T
of dimension
dim(S)-k
and smooth submersions
\gamma_{i}:U_{i}\to T

•

and if
U_{i}\cap U_{j}\neq\emptyset
, then there exist a local diffeomorphism
\pi_{ij}:\gamma_{i}(U_{i})\to\gamma_{j}(U_{j})
such that
\pi_{ij}\circ\gamma_{i}=\gamma_{j}.

(2)

If
T
is a complex manifold and the local diffeomorphisms
\pi_{ij}
are holomorphic, then we say that
S
is a transverse holomorphic manifold.

And a foliation defines a partition of the manifold
S
and it is denoted by
\mathcal{F}.

Definition 2.2
.

A
p
-form
\alpha
on a foliated manifold
(S,\mathcal{F})
is said to be a
basic form
if

i_{v}\alpha=0\text{ and }\mathcal{L}_{v}\alpha=0,\text{ for all }v\in T\mathcal{F}.

If the manifold
T
, mentioned in the Definition
2.1
is a complex manifold, then there exist an endomorphism
\Phi
on
TS
such that

\mathcal{L}_{v}\Phi=0,\text{ for all }v\in T\mathcal{F},\Phi|_{T\mathcal{F}}\equiv 0\text{ and }\Phi^{2}=-id\text{ on }TS/\mathcal{F}.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
(S,\mathcal{F})
be a foliated manifold, we say
S
is a
transverse Kähler manifold
if
(S,\mathcal{F})
is a transverse holomorphic manifold and there exist a basic
(1,1)
form
\omega
which is positive definite on
(TS/\mathcal{F})^{1,0}.

And we call
\omega
a transverse Kähler metric.

From the definition we can see that the exterior differential operator
d
maps basic form to a basic form, so we can define the cohomology of basic forms. Let
\Omega^{k}_{B}(S,\mathbb{R})
be the set of all basic forms which is a subset of
\Omega^{k}(S,\mathbb{R})
and we define

H^{k}_{B}(S,\mathbb{R}):=\frac{ker(d:\Omega^{k}_{B}(S,\mathbb{R})\to\Omega^{k+1}_{B}(S,\mathbb{R}))}{im(d:\Omega^{k}_{B}(S,\mathbb{R})\to\Omega^{k+1}_{B}(S,\mathbb{R}))}.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
S
be a compact manifold of dimension
m+k
with a dimension-
k
foliation
\mathcal{F}
, then we say that this foliation
(S,\mathcal{F})
is
homologically orientable
if
H^{m}_{B}(S,\mathbb{R})\neq\emptyset.

A homologically orientable transverse Kähler manifold has almost all the properties satisfied by the Kähler manifolds. For example, the
\partial\overline{\partial}
-lemma, Lefschetz decompostion, Hodge theorem and so on. See
[
1
,
6
,
24
,
27
]
, for further discussion on this.

Remark 2.5
.

Not all transverse Kähler manifolds are homologically orientable. A counter example was given by Carrière in
[
9
]
, see
[
25
, Appendix A]
also.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
(S,\mathcal{F},\omega)
be a compact transverse Kähler manifold with
\omega
a basic transverse Kähler form. Then

(1)

[\alpha]\in H^{1,1}_{B}(S,\mathbb{R})
is
basic Kähler
if it contains a smooth representative which is transverse Kähler, that is, there exist a smooth basic function
\varphi
such that
\alpha+\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\varphi
is a transverse Kähler metric.

(2)

[\alpha]\in H^{1,1}_{B}(S,\mathbb{R})
is said to be
basic nef
if for every
\varepsilon>0
the class
[\alpha]+\varepsilon[\omega]
contains a smooth transverse Kähler metric.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
(S,g)
be a
2n+1
-dimension Riemannian manifold. We say
S
is a
Sasaki manifold
if the Riemannian cone
(C(S),\bar{g})=(S\times\mathbb{R}_{+},dt^{2}+t^{2}g)
is a Kähler manifold.

We can identify
S
in
C(S)
by
\{1\}\times S
. Let
J
be the complex structure of
C(S)
and define a vector field
\xi
and the
1
-form
\eta
by

\xi=J\left(t\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\right),~\eta(\cdot)=\bar{g}(\xi,\cdot).

The restriction of
\xi
and
\eta
to
S
satisfies the following properties:

•

\xi
is a Killing vector field

•

the integral curve of
\xi
is a geodesic

•

\eta(\xi)=1
,
d\eta(\xi,v)=0
, for any vector field
v
on
S
and
d\eta
is a transverse Kähler form.

The vector field
\xi
defines a foliation of the manifold
S
called as the
Reeb foliation
we denote it by
\mathcal{F}_{\xi}
.
Let
\theta
be any basic
(1,1)
form, then

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{\theta}(x):=\sup\{u(x):u\in PSH(S,\xi,\theta),u\leq 0\}\text{ and }

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{\theta}(x):=\sup\{u(x):u\in PSH(S,\xi,\theta)\cap C^{\infty}(S),u\leq 0\}

are the envelope of the potentials. And we define

\mathcal{V}_{\theta}^{f}:=\sup\{u(x):u\in PSH(S,\xi,\theta),u\leq f\},

for any basic function
f.
And by
[
19
, Proposition 3.17]
,
\mathcal{V}_{\theta},\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{\theta},\mathcal{V}_{\theta}^{f}\in PSH(S,\xi,\theta).

The following proposition and theorem are essentially transverse Kähler versions of
[
3
, Proposition 2.3]
and
[
3
, Theorem 3.3]
respectively, which provides the approximations of the envelopes that will be needed in the course of proof of our main theorem, and its corollary.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
[\theta]
and
[\omega]
be any two transverse Kähler class on a transversely Kähler and homologically orientable manifold
(\tilde{S},\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\xi},g,\xi)
. Then the smooth solution
u_{\beta}
of the equation

(2.1)

(\theta+\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}u_{\beta})^{n}\wedge\eta=e^{\beta u_{\beta}}\omega^{n}\wedge\eta

satisfies

\|u_{\beta}-\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{\theta}\|_{C^{0}(\tilde{S})}\leq\frac{A\log\beta}{\beta},

where the constant
A
depends on an upper bound on
\frac{\theta^{n}\wedge\eta}{\omega^{n}\wedge\eta}.

Proof.

Let
x\in\tilde{S}
be such that
\sup_{s\in\tilde{S}}u_{\beta}(s)=u_{\beta}(x).
Then from (
2.1
) and the fact that
\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}u_{\beta}(x)\leq 0
, we get

\theta^{n}\wedge\eta(x)\geq e^{\beta u_{\beta}(x)}\omega^{n}\wedge\eta(x)
, thus

u_{\beta}(s)\leq\frac{C}{\beta},\text{ where }C=\sup_{\tilde{S}}\log\left|\frac{\theta^{n}\wedge\eta}{\omega^{n}\wedge\eta}\right|<\infty.

Now, since
u_{\beta}-\frac{C}{\beta}\in PSH(\tilde{S},\theta,\xi)
by definition

u_{\beta}-\frac{C}{\beta}\leq\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{\theta}.

Now let
u\in PSH(\tilde{S},\theta,\xi)\cap C^{\infty}(\tilde{S})
and
v
be a fixed smooth basic and strictly subharmonic function on
\tilde{S}.
Since
[\theta]
is a transverse Kähler class such a choice is possible.
And
u_{\varepsilon,\delta}=(1-\varepsilon)u+\varepsilon\ v-\delta\in PSH(\tilde{S},\theta,\xi)\cap C^{\infty}(\tilde{S}),
where
\varepsilon=\beta^{-1}
and
\delta
will be chosen later.
Then

\displaystyle\left((1-\varepsilon)\theta+\varepsilon\theta+\sqrt{-1}\partial_{b}\overline{\partial}_{b}u_{\varepsilon,\delta}\right)^{n}\wedge\eta=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(1-\varepsilon)^{n-k}\varepsilon^{k}\theta_{u}^{n-k}\theta_{v}^{k}\wedge\eta

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{n}\theta_{v}^{n}\wedge\eta

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle e^{-\delta\beta}\omega^{n}\wedge\eta,

where we chose
\delta=C^{\prime}\beta^{-1}\log\beta
for some large appropriate constant
C^{\prime}.

Thus

\theta_{u_{\varepsilon,\delta}}^{n}\wedge\eta\geq e^{\beta u_{\varepsilon,\delta}}\omega^{n}\wedge\eta,
since
u_{\varepsilon,\delta}\leq 0.

We already have
\theta_{u_{\beta}}^{n}\wedge\eta=e^{\beta u_{\beta}}\omega^{n}\wedge\eta
, thus by comparison principle we can get
u_{\varepsilon,\delta}\leq u_{\beta}
as we see below.

Claim
.

u_{\varepsilon,\delta}\leq u_{\beta}.

Suppose that
\sup_{\tilde{S}}(u_{\varepsilon,\delta}-u_{\beta})=(u_{\varepsilon,\delta}-u_{\beta})(x).

Then
\sqrt{-1}\partial_{b}\overline{\partial}_{b}(u_{\beta}-u_{\varepsilon,\delta})(x)\leq 0,
that is
\theta_{u_{\varepsilon,\delta}}(x)\leq\theta_{u_{\beta}}(x)
. Thus

\frac{\theta_{u_{\varepsilon,\delta}}^{n}\wedge\eta(x)}{\theta_{u_{\beta}}^{n}\wedge\eta(x)}\leq 1.

But

e^{\beta(u_{\varepsilon,\delta}-u_{\beta})(x)}\leq\frac{\theta_{u_{\varepsilon,\delta}}^{n}\wedge\eta(x)}{\theta_{u_{\beta}}^{n}\wedge\eta(x)}\leq 1.

Thus
u_{\varepsilon,\delta}-u_{\beta}\leq 0
on
\tilde{S}.

This implies that
\frac{\beta-1}{\beta}u\leq u_{\beta}-\frac{1}{\beta}v+\frac{C^{\prime}\log\beta}{\beta}.
Since
v\in C^{\infty}(\tilde{S})
, we get
u\leq\frac{\beta}{\beta-1}u_{\beta}+\frac{C_{1}\log\beta}{\beta},
for some constant
C_{1}.

And by definition of
\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{\theta}
we have

u_{\beta}-\frac{C}{\beta}\leq\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{\theta}\leq\frac{\beta}{\beta-1}u_{\beta}+\frac{C_{1}\log\beta}{\beta}.

Thus

\|u_{\beta}-\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{\theta}\|_{C^{0}(\tilde{S})}\leq\frac{A\log\beta}{\beta},

follows from applying the inequality
u_{\beta}\leq\frac{C}{\beta}
to the inequality above.
∎

Theorem 2.9
.

Let
\left(\tilde{S},\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\xi}\right)
be a transverse Kähler manifold which is homologically orientable and
u\in PSH(\tilde{S},\xi,\theta)
then there exist a sequence
\{u_{j}\}_{\{j\in\mathbb{N}\}}
such that
u_{j}\in PSH(\tilde{S},\xi,\theta)\cap C^{\infty}(\tilde{S})
and
u_{j}\to u
uniformly, for any basic
(1,1)
-form
\theta.

Proof.

Since
u
is upper semi-continuous basic function, it can be written as a decreasing limit of basic smooth functions
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}.
And we let
\varphi_{j}:=\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{\theta}^{f_{j}}
, then by definition
f_{j}\geq\varphi_{j}\geq u.
Moreover, for any fixed
x\in\tilde{S}
and an
\epsilon>0
we can find
j_{\epsilon}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

u(x)\leq\varphi_{j}(x)\leq f_{j}(x)\leq u(x)+\epsilon,\text{ for all }j\geq j_{\epsilon}.

Thus
\varphi_{j}
decreases to
u
.
Now, to find a sequence of basic smooth psh-functions decreases to
u
we consider the following transverse Complex Monge-Ampère equations

(\theta+\sqrt{-1}\partial_{b}\overline{\partial}_{b}\varphi_{\beta,j})^{n}=e^{\beta(\varphi_{\beta,j}-f_{j})}\omega^{n}

and
\varphi_{\beta,j}
be the solutions which exists by the transverse Calabi-Yau theorem(cf.
[
1
]
). And by Proposition
2.8
, we can see that
\varphi_{j,\beta}
converges uniformly to
\varphi_{j}
as
\beta\to\infty.
Hence, for an appropriate choices of
\beta_{j}\in\mathbb{N}
and
\epsilon_{j}>0
such that
\beta_{j}\to\infty
and
\epsilon_{j}\to 0
as
j\to\infty
and if we let

u_{j}:=\varphi_{j,\beta_{j}}+\epsilon_{j}

satisfies the necessary conditions we expected.
∎

2.9
and Proposition

2.2.
Affine
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein variety:

The cone over a Sasaki manifold is an affine variety, we will see in this section that a link of an affine

-Gorenstein variety can also given the structure of a Sasaki manifold. Let
Y
be any affine
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein variety with non-isolated singularity and a good torus
\mathbb{T}
action, and we let

be a Kähler potenital on

Then

\eta=J(d\log r)\text{ and }d\eta=\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\log r

defines a
\mathbb{T}
-invariant
1
-form and
2
-form respectively such that
\eta(\xi)=1
and we define the link
S
of

by

S:=(Y\smallsetminus\{y_{0}\})/\mathbb{R}_{+},

where

is the fixed point of
\mathbb{T}
and the action of
\mathbb{R}_{+}
is generated by
-J\xi
, and

Then the restriction of

to the link

In this section, we establish the proof of our main theorem, Theorem

,

]

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be a decreasing right continuous function with
\lim_{s\to\infty}f(s)=0.
Assume that
rf(s+r)\leq B_{0}f(s)^{1+\delta_{0}}
for some constant
B_{0}>0
and all
s>0
and
r\in[0,1]
. Then there exists a positive constant
s_{\infty}:=s_{\infty}(\delta_{0},B_{0},f)
such that
f(s)=0,
for all
s\geq s_{\infty}.

Now let
u_{t,\beta}
be the solution of the transverse complex Monge-Ampère equation

(\omega_{t}+\sqrt{-1}\partial_{b}\overline{\partial}_{b}u_{t,\beta})^{n}\wedge\eta=e^{\beta u_{t,\beta}}\chi^{n}\wedge\eta,

where the existence of
u_{t,\beta}
is given by transverse Calabi-Yau theorem(cf.
[
1
]
). By Proposition
2.8
and Theorem
2.9
,
u_{t,\beta}\to\mathcal{V}_{t}
as
\beta\to\infty
, where
\mathcal{V}_{t}:=\mathcal{V}_{\omega_{t}}
, this fact will be needed in the proof of the following lemma.

Lemma 3.2
.

There are positive constants
C=C(n,\omega,\chi)
and
\beta:=\beta(n,\omega,\chi,\xi)
such that for any
s>0

(3.1)

\int_{\Omega_{s}}exp\left\{\beta\left(\frac{-\varphi_{t}+\mathcal{V}_{t}-s}{A_{s}^{1/(n+1)}}\right)^{\frac{n+1}{n}}\right\}\chi^{n}\wedge\eta\leq Cexp(CE_{t}),

where
A_{s}=\int_{\Omega_{s}}(-\varphi_{t}+\mathcal{V}_{t}-s)e^{F}\chi^{n}\wedge\eta
is the energy of
(\varphi_{t}-\mathcal{V}_{t}+s)
on the sub-level set
\Omega_{s}:=\{\varphi_{t}-\mathcal{V}_{t}\leq-s\}
and
E_{t}=\int_{\tilde{S}}(-\varphi_{t}+\mathcal{V}_{t})e^{F}\chi^{n}\wedge\eta.

Proof.

The key idea of the proof is to dominate the function (upto scaling by some constants)
(-\varphi_{t}+\mathcal{V}_{t}-s)^{\frac{n+1}{n}}
by some other basic pluri subharmonic function which is a solution to a variant of transverse complex Monge-Ampère equation and apply the alpha invariant to that solution to get the desired inequality.
For that we choose a sequence of smooth functions
\tau_{k}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
defined by

\tau_{k}(x)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\sqrt{x^{2}+k^{-1}}+x\right),

which converges pointwise to
x\cdot\chi_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}(x)
, where
\chi_{\mathbb{R}+}
is the characteristic function on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
as
k\to\infty
and we solve an auxiliary transverse Monge-Ampère equation

(\omega_{t}+\sqrt{-1}\partial_{b}\overline{\partial}_{b}\psi_{t,k})^{n}\wedge\eta=c_{t}\frac{\tau_{k}(-\varphi_{t}+\mathcal{V}_{t}-s)}{A_{s,k,\beta}}e^{F}\chi^{n}\wedge\eta,\quad\sup_{\tilde{S}}\psi_{t,k}=0,

where
A_{s,k,\beta}=\int_{\tilde{S}}\tau_{k}(-\varphi_{t}+u_{t,\beta}-s)e^{F}\chi^{n}\wedge\eta.

By definition
\psi_{t,k}\leq\mathcal{V}_{t}
and by Proposition
2.8
and Theorem
2.9
,
u_{t,\beta}
converges to
\mathcal{V}_{t}
uniformly as
\beta\to\infty
, then we can take
\beta
large enough so that

\psi_{t,k}\leq u_{t,\beta}+1.

Now we define a basic smooth function

\Phi=-\varepsilon(-\psi_{t,k}+u_{t,\beta}+1+\Lambda)^{\frac{n}{n+1}}-(\varphi_{t}-u_{t,\beta}+s),

where the positive constants
\varepsilon,\Lambda
will be choosen later. And let

\Phi(x_{0})=\sup_{x\in\tilde{S}}\Phi(x).

Now either
x_{0}\in\Omega_{s}^{\circ}
or
x_{0}\in\tilde{S}\smallsetminus\Omega_{s}^{\circ}.
Let us look at each case separately.

Case.1
Suppose that
x_{0}\in\Omega_{s}^{\circ}.
Then let
\Delta_{t}(f)=n\frac{\sqrt{-1}\partial_{b}\overline{\partial}_{b}f\wedge\omega_{t,\varphi_{t}}^{n-1}\wedge\eta}{\omega_{t,\varphi_{t}}^{n}\wedge\eta}=\Lambda_{\omega_{t,\varphi_{t}}}\sqrt{-1}\partial_{b}\overline{\partial}_{b}f.
Since
x_{0}
is a point of maximum of
\Phi
, we get

\displaystyle 0\geq

\displaystyle\Delta_{t}\Phi(x_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\Delta_{t}\varphi_{t}+\Delta_{t}u_{t,\beta}-\frac{\varepsilon n}{n+1}(-\psi_{t,k}+u_{t,\beta}+1+\Lambda)^{\frac{-1}{n+1}}(-\Delta_{t}\psi_{t,k}+\Delta_{t}u_{t,\beta})

\displaystyle+\frac{\varepsilon n}{(n+1)^{2}}(-\psi_{t,k}+u_{t,\beta}+1+\Lambda)^{-\frac{n+2}{n+1}}\Lambda_{\omega_{t,\varphi_{t}}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\partial_{b}(\psi_{t,k}-u_{t,\beta})\wedge\overline{\partial}_{b}(\psi_{t,k}-u_{t,\beta})\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-n+\Lambda_{\omega_{t,\varphi_{t}}}\omega_{t,u_{t,\beta}}+\frac{\varepsilon n}{n+1}(-\psi_{t,k}+u_{t,\beta}+1+\Lambda)^{\frac{-1}{n+1}}\Lambda_{\omega_{t,\varphi_{t}}}(\omega_{t,\psi_{t,k}}-\omega_{t,u_{t,\beta}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-n+\frac{\varepsilon n}{n+1}(-\psi_{t,k}+u_{t,\beta}+1+\Lambda)^{\frac{-1}{n+1}}\Lambda_{\omega_{t,\varphi_{t}}}\omega_{t,\psi_{t,k}}

\displaystyle+\left(1-\frac{\varepsilon n}{n+1}(-\psi_{t,k}+u_{t,\beta}+1+\Lambda)^{\frac{-1}{n+1}}\right)\Lambda_{\omega_{t,\varphi_{t}}}\omega_{t,u_{t,\beta}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-n+\frac{\varepsilon n^{2}}{n+1}(-\psi_{t,k}+u_{t,\beta}+1+\Lambda)^{\frac{-1}{n+1}}\left(\frac{\omega_{t,\psi_{t,k}}^{n}\wedge\eta}{\omega_{t,\varphi_{t}}^{n}\wedge\eta}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}+\left(1-\frac{n\varepsilon}{n+1}\Lambda^{\frac{-1}{n+1}}\right)\Lambda_{\omega_{t,\varphi_{t}}}\omega_{t,u_{t,\beta}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-n+\frac{\varepsilon n^{2}}{n+1}(-\psi_{t,k}+u_{t,\beta}+1+\Lambda)^{\frac{-1}{n+1}}\left(\tau_{k}(-\varphi_{t}+u_{t,\beta}-s)A_{s,k,\beta}^{-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}

\displaystyle+\left(1-\frac{n\varepsilon}{n+1}\Lambda^{\frac{-1}{n+1}}\right)\Lambda_{\omega_{t,\varphi_{t}}}\omega_{t,u_{t,\beta}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-n+\frac{\varepsilon n^{2}}{n+1}(-\psi_{t,k}+u_{t,\beta}+1+\Lambda)^{\frac{-1}{n+1}}\left(-\varphi_{t}+u_{t,\beta}-s\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}(A_{s,k,\beta})^{\frac{-1}{n}}

\displaystyle+\left(1-\frac{n\varepsilon}{n+1}\Lambda^{\frac{-1}{n+1}}\right)\Lambda_{\omega_{t,\varphi_{t}}}\omega_{t,u_{t,\beta}}

Now we choose the constant
\Lambda
so that the last term in the sum is zero, that is

\Lambda=\left(\frac{n\varepsilon}{n+1}\right)^{n+1},

then at the point
x_{0}\in\Omega_{s}
, we get that

-(\varphi_{t}-u_{t,\beta}+s)\leq\left(\frac{n+1}{n\varepsilon}\right)^{n}A_{s,k,\beta}(-\psi_{t,k}+u_{t,\beta}+\Lambda+1)^{\frac{n}{n+1}}

now if we choose

\varepsilon^{n+1}=\left(\frac{n+1}{n}\right)^{n}A_{s,k,\beta},

then the above inequality becomes

-(\varphi_{t}-u_{t,\beta}+s)\leq\varepsilon(-\psi_{t,k}+u_{t,\beta}+\Lambda+1)^{\frac{n}{n+1}}.

That is
\Phi(x_{0})\leq 0.

Case.2
Suppose
x_{0}\in\Omega_{s}^{\circ}.
Then by definition of
\Omega_{s}
, we get

\Phi(x_{0})\leq-(\varphi_{t}-u_{t,\beta}+s)\leq-U_{t}+u_{t,\beta}\leq\epsilon_{\beta},

where we chose
\epsilon_{\beta}>0
using Proposition
2.8
so that
\epsilon_{\beta}\to 0
as
\beta\to\infty.

From both the cases, we can get that on
\tilde{S}
we have the following inequality

(\varphi_{t}+u_{t,\beta}-s)^{\frac{n+1}{n}}\leq C_{n}(A_{s,k,\beta})^{\frac{1}{n}}(-\psi_{t,k}+u_{t,\beta}+1+A_{s,k,\beta})+\epsilon_{\beta}^{\frac{n+1}{n}}.

Since
u_{t,\beta}\to U_{t}
uniformly as
\beta\to\infty
, by letting
\beta\to\infty
in the above inequality we get

(-\varphi_{t}+\mathcal{V}_{t}-s)^{\frac{n+1}{n}}\leq C_{n}(A_{s,k})^{\frac{1}{n}}(-\psi_{t,k}+\mathcal{V}_{t}+1+A_{s,k}),

where
A_{s,k}=\int_{\tilde{S}}\tau_{k}(-\varphi_{t}+\mathcal{V}_{t}-s)e^{F}\chi^{n}\wedge\eta.

And since
\mathcal{V}_{t}\leq 0
, we can reduce the above inequality becomes

(3.2)

\frac{(-\varphi_{t}+\mathcal{V}_{t}-s)^{\frac{n+1}{n}}}{(A_{s,k})^{\frac{1}{n}}}\leq C_{n}(-\psi_{t,k}+1+A_{s,k}).

By
[
31
, Proposition 3.3]
, there exists positive constants
\alpha
and
C^{\prime}
such that

\int_{\tilde{S}}e^{-\alpha\psi_{t,k}}\chi^{n}\wedge\eta<C^{\prime}.

And let
\beta=\frac{\alpha}{C_{n}}
, then there exist a positive constant
C
such that

\int_{\Omega_{s}}\exp{\left(\beta\frac{(-\varphi_{t}+U_{t}-s)^{\frac{n+1}{n}}}{(A_{s,k})^{\frac{1}{n}}}\right)}\chi^{n}\wedge\eta\leq C\exp(CA_{s,k}).

Since
A_{s,k}\to A_{s}
as
k\to\infty
, letting
k\to\infty
in the above inequality implies that

\int_{\Omega_{s}}\exp{\left(\beta\frac{(-\varphi_{t}+U_{t}-s)^{\frac{n+1}{n}}}{(A_{s})^{\frac{1}{n}}}\right)}\chi^{n}\wedge\eta\leq C\exp(CA_{s}).

Then the desired inequality follows from the inequality
A_{s}\leq E_{t}
for any
s>0.

∎

Next we prove that the constants
E_{t}
’s are bounded uniformly for
t\in(0,1].

Lemma 3.3
.

There is a constant
C:=C(n,\omega,\chi,\xi,\|e^{F}\|_{L^{1}(\log L)^{p}})
such that

E_{t}=\int_{\tilde{S}}(-\varphi_{t}+U_{t})e^{F}\chi^{n}\wedge\eta\leq C,\text{ for all }t\in(0,1].

Proof.

We have
\omega_{t}=\omega+t\chi
, then we can find a constant
a>0
such that
\omega_{t}<a\chi
for all
t\in(0,1]
then,
\varphi_{t}\in PSH(S,\omega_{t},\xi)
implies
\varphi_{t}\in PSH(S,a\chi,\xi)
. Then by the basic
\alpha
-invariant,
[
31
, Proposition 3.3]
there exist an
\alpha:=\alpha(S,\xi,\chi)>0
such that

\frac{1}{V_{t}}\int_{\tilde{S}}\exp\left(-\log\frac{\omega_{t,\varphi_{t}}^{n}\wedge\eta}{a^{n}\chi^{n}\wedge\eta}-\alpha\varphi_{t}\right)\omega_{t,\varphi_{t}}^{n}\wedge\eta=\frac{1}{V_{t}}\int_{\tilde{S}}a^{n}e^{-\alpha\varphi_{t}}\chi^{n}\wedge\eta\leq\frac{a^{n}C(n,\xi,\chi)}{V_{t}}.

By Jensen’s inequality we get

\frac{1}{V_{t}}\int_{\tilde{S}}\left(-\log\frac{\omega_{t,\varphi_{t}}^{n}\wedge\eta}{a^{n}\chi^{n}\wedge\eta}-\alpha\varphi_{t}\right)\omega_{t,\varphi_{t}}^{n}\wedge\eta\leq\log C(n,\xi,\chi)-\log V_{t},

this implies

\displaystyle\frac{1}{V_{t}}\int_{\tilde{S}}(-\alpha\varphi_{t})\omega_{t,\varphi_{t}}^{n}\wedge\eta\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{V_{t}}\int_{\tilde{S}}\log(c_{t}a^{-n}e^{F})\omega_{t,\varphi_{t}}^{n}\wedge\eta+\log C(n,\xi,\chi)-\log V_{t}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\log(c_{t}a^{-n})+\frac{c_{t}}{V_{t}}\int_{\tilde{S}}Fe^{F}\chi^{n}\wedge\eta+\log C(n,\xi,\chi)-\log V_{t}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\log\frac{V_{t}}{a^{n}\int_{\tilde{S}}\chi^{n}\wedge\eta}+\frac{1}{\int_{\tilde{S}}\chi^{n}\wedge\eta}\|e^{F}\|_{L^{1}(\log L)^{1}}+\log C(n,\xi,\chi)-\log V_{t}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{V_{t}}\int_{\tilde{S}}(-\alpha\varphi_{t})c_{t}e^{F}\chi^{n}\wedge\eta\leq

\displaystyle\log\frac{C(n,\xi,\chi)}{a^{n}\int_{\tilde{S}}\chi^{n}\wedge\eta}+\frac{1}{\int_{\tilde{S}}\chi^{n}\wedge\eta}\|e^{F}\|_{L^{1}(\log L)^{1}}

thus we get

\int_{\tilde{S}}(-\varphi_{t})e^{F}\chi^{n}\wedge\eta\leq\frac{\int_{\tilde{S}}\chi^{n}\wedge\eta}{\alpha}\log\frac{C(n,\xi,\chi)}{a^{n}\int_{\tilde{S}}\chi^{n}\wedge\eta}+\alpha^{-1}\|e^{F}\|_{L^{1}(\log L)^{1}}.

Since
\varphi_{t}\leq U_{t}\leq 0
the last inequality yields

\int_{\tilde{S}}(-\varphi_{t}+U_{t})e^{F}\chi^{n}\wedge\eta\leq C,

where the constant
C:=C(n,\xi,\chi,\|e^{F}\|_{L^{1}(\log L)^{1}}).

∎

Note that the constant
C
in the above lemma does not depend on

, which is a key point in establishing the
L^{\infty}
estimate. Now we proceed to prove our main theorem.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Define
h:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
by
h(x)=(\log(1+x))^{p}
. Then
h
is a strictly increasing function with
h(0)=0
and let

v:=\frac{\beta}{2}\left(\frac{-\varphi_{t}+\mathcal{V}_{t}-s}{(A_{s})^{\frac{1}{n+1}}}\right)^{\frac{n+1}{n}}

then by the generalized Young’s inequality with respect to
h,
for any

we have

\displaystyle v(x)^{p}e^{F(x)}\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{e^{F(x)}}h(y)dy+\int_{0}^{v(x)^{p}}h^{-1}(y)dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{F(x)}(1+|F(x)|)^{p}+C(p)e^{2v(x)}.

This implies that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{s}}v(x)^{p}e^{F(x)}\chi^{n}\wedge\eta\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{s}}e^{F(x)}(1+|F(x)|)^{p}\chi^{n}\wedge\eta+\int_{\Omega_{s}}e^{2v(x)}\chi^{n}\wedge\eta

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|e^{F}\|_{L^{1}(\log L)^{p}}+C+Ce^{CE_{t}},

where the second inequality followed from Lemma
3.2
and the constant
C=C(n,\xi,\chi,\omega)
. And by the definition of
v
we get from the last inequality that

\int_{\Omega_{s}}(-\varphi_{t}+\mathcal{V}_{t}-s)^{\frac{(n+1)p}{n}}e^{F}\chi^{n}\wedge\eta\leq 2^{p}\beta^{-p}(A_{s})^{\frac{p}{n}}(\|e^{F}\|_{L^{1}(\log L)^{p}}+C+Ce^{CE_{t}}).

Now,

\displaystyle A_{s}=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{s}}(-\varphi_{t}+\mathcal{V}_{t}-s)e^{F}\chi^{n}\wedge\eta

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega_{s}}(-\varphi_{t}+\mathcal{V}_{t}-s)^{\frac{(n+1)p}{n}}e^{F}\chi^{n}\wedge\eta\right)^{\frac{n}{(n+1)p}}\left(\int_{\Omega_{s}}e^{F}\chi^{n}\wedge\eta\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(A_{s})^{\frac{1}{n+1}}\left(2^{p}\beta^{-p}(\|e^{F}\|_{L^{1}(\log L)^{p}}+C+Ce^{CE_{t}})\right)^{\frac{n}{(n+1)p}}\left(\int_{\Omega_{s}}e^{F}\chi^{n}\wedge\eta\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},

where

satisfies
\frac{n}{(n+1)p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
and for the second inequality we use the Hölder inequality. Thus the above inequality implies

(3.3)

A_{s}\leq\left(2^{p}\beta^{-p}(\|e^{F}\|_{L^{1}(\log L)^{p}}+C+Ce^{CE_{t}})\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\int_{\Omega_{s}}e^{F}\chi^{n}\wedge\eta\right)^{\frac{n+1}{nq}}.

By the definition of

, we get that

\frac{n+1}{nq}=1+\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{p}=1+\delta_{0},

where
\delta_{0}=\frac{p-n}{pn}>0.
By Lemma
3.3
, we can find a

such that

\left(2^{p}\beta^{-p}(\|e^{F}\|_{L^{1}(\log L)^{p}}+C+Ce^{CE_{t}})\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<B_{0},\text{ for all }t\in(0,1].

Then the inequality (
3.3
) can be rewritten as

(3.4)

A_{s}\leq B_{0}\left(\int_{\Omega_{s}}e^{F}\chi^{n}\wedge\eta\right)^{1+\delta_{0}}.

In
\Omega_{s+r}
, we have
-\varphi_{t}+\mathcal{V}_{t}-s\geq r
and if we define the function
f:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
by

then

is decreasing, and using the inequality (

) we get that

rf(s+r)\leq B_{0}f(s)^{1+\delta_{0}},\ \text{ for any }r\in[0,1]\text{ and }s>0,

then by Lemma
3.1
we can find an

Theorem 4.1
.

The foliated manifold
\left(\tilde{S},\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\xi}\right)
is a transverse Kähler manifold which is homologically orientable.

Proof.

From the construction we can see that

is transversely holomorphic manifold. Moreover,

\xi

And we define a smooth nowhere vanishing

Suppose that

and

is a positive function on

for any folilated coordinate chart.
Thus

is homologically orientable.
∎

A>0
such that
|\Delta_{\chi}\varphi_{j,t}|\leq A
. Then we can find a sequence
t(j)
such that
t(j)\to 0
as
j\to\infty
and
\{\varphi_{j,t(j)}\}
converges to
\tilde{\varphi}_{0}
in
C^{1}
locally. Moreover
\tilde{\varphi}_{0}
satisfies the equation

(\tilde{\omega}+\sqrt{-1}\partial_{b}\overline{\partial}_{b}\tilde{\varphi}_{0})^{n}=e^{-(n+1)\tilde{\varphi}}e^{(n+1)(\Psi^{+}-\Psi^{-})}\chi^{n}

on
\tilde{S}\cap\mathcal{U}
with
\tilde{\omega}+\sqrt{-1}\partial_{b}\overline{\partial}_{b}\tilde{\varphi}\geq 0
. Since
\tilde{\omega}|_{E}\equiv 0
, by maximum principle
\tilde{\varphi}_{0}
is constant on each irreducible component of
E
and
\tilde{\varphi}_{0}
is unique up to addition of a constant. So,
\tilde{\varphi}_{0}=\tilde{\varphi}+c
, for some constant
c.

Since

\tilde{\varphi}\in L^{\infty}(\tilde{S})\text{ and }\Delta_{\chi}\tilde{\varphi}\in L^{\infty}_{loc}(\tilde{S}),

by Evans-Krylov theory we get the
C^{2,\alpha}
bound and by Schauder estimate we get the higher order estimates. Hence,
\tilde{\varphi}
is smooth.

Thus, the function
\tilde{\varphi}
can be pushed forward as a function
\varphi
on
S
and it is smooth on
S_{reg}
, this implies that
r^{2}
is smooth on
Y_{reg}
, since
r^{2}=e^{\varphi}f_{\xi}
.

∎

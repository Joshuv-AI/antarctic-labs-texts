Abstract
. A fully diagonalized spectral method using generalized Laguerre functions is proposed and analyzed for solving elliptic equations on the half line. We first define the generalized Laguerre functions which are complete and mutually orthogonal with respect to an equivalent Sobolev inner product. Then the Fourier-like Sobolev orthogonal basis functions are constructed for the diagonalized Laguerre spectral method of elliptic equations. Besides, a unified orthogonal Laguerre projection is established for various elliptic equations. On the basis of this orthogonal Laguerre projection, we obtain optimal error estimates of the fully diagonalized Laguerre spectral method for both Dirichlet and Robin boundary value problems. Finally, numerical experiments, which are in agreement with the theoretical analysis, demonstrate the effectiveness and the spectral accuracy of our diagonalized method.

1.
Introduction

Spectral methods for solving partial differential equations on unbounded domains
have gained a rapid development
during the last few decades. An abundance of literature on this research topic
has emerged, and their underlying approximation approaches
can be essentially classified into three catalogues
[
4
,
27
]
:

(i)

truncate an unbounded domain to a bounded one and solve the problem on the bounded domain subject to artificial or transparent boundary conditions
[
22
,
26
]
;

(ii)

map the original problem on an unbounded domain to one on a bounded domain and use classic spectral methods to solve the new problem
[
9
]
; or equivalently, approximate the original problem by some non-classical functions mapped from the classic orthogonal
polynomials/functions on a bounded domain
[
2
,
3
,
7
,
11
,
12
,
27
,
31
,
34
]
;

(iii)

directly approximate the original problem by genuine orthogonal functions such as Laguerre polynomials or functions on the unbounded domain
[
6
,
10
,
13
,
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
,
18
,
19
,
20
,
24
,
30
,
32
,
33
,
35
]
.

The third approach is of particular interest to researchers, and has won an increasing popularity in a broad class of applications, owing to its essential advantages over other two approaches.
These direct approximation schemes constitute an initial step
towards the efficient spectral methods, which admit fast and stable algorithms
for their efficient implementations.

As we know,
the Fourier spectral method makes use of
the eigenfunctions of the Laplace operator which are orthogonal to each other with respect to the Sobolev inner product involving derivatives, thus the corresponding algebraic system is diagonal
[
4
,
5
,
25
]
.
This fact together with the availability of the fast Fourier transform (FFT) makes
the Fourier spectral method be an ideal approximation approach for differential equations
with periodic boundary conditions.
Although the utilization of the genuine orthogonal polynomials/functions in this direct approach usually leads to a highly sparse (e.g., tri-diagonal, penta-diagonal) and well-conditioned algebraic system, however, in many cases, people still want to get
a set of Fourier-like basis functions for a fully diagonalized algebraic system
[
28
]
.

The main purpose of this paper is to construct the Fourier-like Sobolev orthogonal basis functions

[
8
,
21
]
for elliptic boundary value problems on the half line
\Lambda=(0,\infty)
. For this purpose, we shall first extend the definition of
Laguerre polynomials
\big\{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}_{k}(x)\big\}_{k\geq 0}

and Laguerre functions
\big\{l^{\alpha,\beta}_{k}(x)=e^{-\frac{\beta}{2}x}\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}_{k}(\beta x)\big\}_{k\geq 0}
for
\beta>0
to allow
\alpha
being any real number.
The resulting generalized Laguerre functions
are proven to be the eigenfunctions of certain high order Sturm-Liouville differential operators (see Lemma
2.6
of this paper).
Moreover, they are complete and mutually orthogonal in
H^{r}_{x^{r+\alpha}}(\Lambda)

for any nonnegative
integer
r>-\alpha-1
with respect to an equivalent Sobolev inner product (see (
2.23
) of this paper).

Since the problem is dependent on the inner product originated from
the coercive bilinear form of the elliptic equation, it does not necessarily coincide
with the equivalent Sobolev inner product,
further efforts should
be paid to obtain the Fourier-like basis functions for
a fully diagonalized spectral approximation,
in spite of the Sobolev orthogonality of
\big\{l^{\alpha,\beta}_{k}(x)\big\}_{k\geq 0}
.
Starting with
\big\{l^{-1,\beta}_{k}(x)\big\}_{k\geq 0}
,
stable and efficient algorithms are then proposed to construct the Fourier-like basis functions
for the non-homogeneous Dirichlet and Robin boundary value problems of the second order
elliptic equations.
In the sequel, both the exact solution and the approximate solution can be represented as
infinite and truncated Fourier series in
\big\{l^{-1,\beta}_{k}(x)\big\}
, respectively.
Although the fully diagonalized spectral methods are studied for second order equations,
they can be readily generalized to solve
2r
-th order equations
by starting with
\big\{l^{-r,\beta}_{k}(x)\big\}
.

An ideal spectral approximation to differential equations may guarantee an optimal error estimate in its convergence analysis. To match this requirement, various orthogonal projections involving different orders of derivatives
and boundary conditions
have been designed and studied case by case, which frequently make the numerical analysis
in spectral method a tedious
task.
Moreover, the traditional routine to measure the approximation error is first to establish the norm defined by a second-order self-adjoint differential operator, and then estimate the upper bound of the approximation error with the induced norms. However, this practical approach usually fails to characterize
the function space in which the orthogonal projection has an optimal error estimate.

To conquer these difficulties, we need a unified definition
of the orthogonal spectral projections with a systematic numerical analysis.
Fortunately, the Sobolev orthogonality of the generalized Laguerre functions
\big\{l^{\alpha,\beta}_{k}(x)\big\}
with
a negative integer
\alpha=-n
enables us to define the unified
orthogonal projection
\pi^{-n,\beta}_{N}
from
H^{n}_{x^{r-n}}(\Lambda)

to the finite approximation space
for all nonnegative integer
r\geq n
, ignoring the specific value of
r
.
More importantly, such an orthogonal projection
\pi^{-n,\beta}_{N}
interpolates
the endpoint function values up to the
(n-1)
-th derivative, i.e,

\partial_{x}^{\ell}\pi^{-n,\beta}_{N}u(0)=\partial_{x}^{\ell}u(0)
for any
0\leq\ell\leq n-1
and
N\geq n
.
This endpoint interpolation property ensures
u-\pi^{-n,\beta}_{N}u\in H_{0}^{n}(\Lambda)
, thus makes
\pi^{-n,\beta}_{N}
applicable to both the Dirichlet and Robin boundary value problems, and available to multi-domain spectral methods.
Besides, owing to the clarity of the orthogonality structure of the generalised Laguerre functions,
one can not only derive an optimal order of the convergence for the approximated function, but also get a generic characterization of the function space where the orthogonal projection has an optimal error estimate.

Therefore, the second purpose of this paper is to establish such a unified orthogonal Laguerre projection, and apply
it to the convergence analysis on the fully diagonalized Laguerre spectral method
for both the Dirichlet and Robin boundary value problems of second order elliptic equations.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we first make conventions on the
frequently used notations, and then introduce generalized Laguerre polynomials and functions
with arbitrary index
\alpha
. The fully diagonalized Laguerre spectral methods and the implementation of
algorithms
are proposed in Section 3 for the Dirichlet and Robin boundary value problems of second order elliptic equations. Section 4 is then devoted to the convergence analysis of
the unified orthogonal projection together with our Laguerre spectral methods.
Finally, numerical results are presented in Section 5 to demonstrate the effectiveness
and accuracy of
the proposed diagonalized Laguerre spectral methods, which are in agreement with
our theoretical predictions.

2.
Generalized Laguerre polynomials and functions

2.1.
Notations and preliminaries

Let
\Lambda=(0,\infty)
and

{\varpi}(x)
be a weight function which is not necessary in
L^{1}(\Lambda)
. We define

L^{2}_{\varpi}({\Lambda})=\{v~|~v\hbox{ is measurable on $\Lambda$ and~}\|v\|_{\varpi}<\infty\},

with the following inner product and norm,

(u,v)_{\varpi}=\displaystyle\int_{\Lambda}u(x)v(x){\varpi}(x)dx,\quad\|v\|_{\varpi}=(v,v)^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\varpi},\quad\forall u,v\in L_{\varpi}^{2}(\Lambda).

For simplicity, we denote

\frac{d^{k}v}{dx^{k}}=\partial^{k}_{x}v
and
\frac{dv}{dx}=v^{\prime}.

For any integer
m\geq 0
, we define

H_{\varpi}^{m}(\Lambda)=\{v~|~\partial_{x}^{k}v\in L^{2}_{\varpi}(\Lambda),~0\leq k\leq m\},

with the following semi-norm and norm,

|v|_{m,{\varpi}}=\|\partial_{x}^{m}v\|_{\varpi},\qquad\|v\|_{m,{\varpi}}=\Big(\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{m}|v|_{k,{\varpi}}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

For any real
r>0,
we define the
space
H^{r}_{\varpi}(\Lambda)
and its norm
\|v\|_{r,{\varpi}}
by function
space interpolation as in
[
1
]
. In cases where no confusion
arises,
{\varpi}
may be dropped from the notations whenever

{\varpi}(x)\equiv 1.
Specifically, we shall use the weight functions
w=w(x)=x
and
w^{\alpha}=w^{\alpha}(x)=x^{\alpha}

in the subsequent sections.

We denote by
\mathbb{R}
the collection of real numbers, by
\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
\mathbb{Z}^{-}
the collections of
nonnegative and negative integers, respectively. Further, we let
\mathbb{P}_{k}
be the space of polynomials of degree
\leq k
.

Let
\aleph:=\mathbb{Z}^{-}\cup(-1,+\infty)
. We also define the characteristic functions
\chi_{n}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,

\displaystyle\chi_{n}(\alpha)=\begin{cases}-\alpha,&\alpha+n\in\mathbb{Z}^{-},\\
0,&\alpha+n\in(-1,+\infty).\end{cases}

For short we write
\chi(\alpha)=\chi_{0}(\alpha)
.

2.2.
Generalized Laguerre polynomials

It is well known that, for
\alpha>-1,
the classical Laguerre polynomials
{\mathcal{L}}_{k}^{{\alpha}}(x),\ k=0,1,\dots,
admit an explicit representation (see
[
29
]
):

{\mathcal{L}}_{k}^{{\alpha}}(x)=\displaystyle\sum_{\nu=0}^{k}\frac{(\alpha+\nu+1)_{k-\nu}}{(k-\nu)!\nu!}(-x)^{\nu},\qquad x\in\Lambda,\quad k\geq 0,

(2.1)

where we use the Pochhammer symbol

(a)_{n}=a(a+1)\dots(a+n-1)
for any
a\in\mathbb{R}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

The classical Laguerre polynomials can be extended to cases with any
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}

and the same representation as (
2.1
), which are referred to as the
generalized Laguerre polynomials (cf.
[
23
]
). Obviously, the generalized Laguerre polynomials
{\mathcal{L}}_{k}^{{\alpha}}(x),\ k=0,1,\dots,
constitute a complete basis for the
linear space of real polynomials as well, since
\deg{\mathcal{L}}^{\alpha}_{k}=k
for all
k\geq 0
.

The generalized Laguerre polynomials fulfill the following
recurrence relations.

Lemma 2.1
.

For any
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, it
holds

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\mathcal{L}}^{\alpha}_{0}(x)=1,\qquad\qquad{\mathcal{L}}^{\alpha}_{1}(x)=-x+\alpha+1,\\[6.0pt]
(k+1){\mathcal{L}}^{\alpha}_{k+1}(x)=(2k+\alpha+1-x){\mathcal{L}}^{\alpha}_{k}(x)-(k+\alpha){\mathcal{L}}^{\alpha}_{k-1}(x),\qquad k\geq 1.\end{array}\right.

(2.2)

Proof.

The recurrence relation (
2.2
) for
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
can be derived from those of the classic
Laguerre polynomials for
\alpha>-1
by the continuation method. Here, we also give a concrete proof by the representation
(
2.1
).
Using the expression (
2.1
), we obtain that for integer
k\geq 1,

\begin{array}[]{ll}(2k+\alpha+1){\mathcal{L}}^{\alpha}_{k}(x)-(k+\alpha){\mathcal{L}}^{\alpha}_{k-1}(x)-(k+1){\mathcal{L}}^{\alpha}_{k+1}(x)\\[6.0pt]
=(2k+\alpha+1)\displaystyle\sum_{\nu=0}^{k}\frac{(-1)^{\nu}\Gamma(k+\alpha+1)}{\nu!(k-\nu)!\Gamma(\alpha+\nu+1)}x^{\nu}-(k+\alpha)\displaystyle\sum_{\nu=0}^{k-1}\frac{(-1)^{\nu}\Gamma(k+\alpha)}{\nu!(k-\nu-1)!\Gamma(\alpha+\nu+1)}x^{\nu}\\
\quad-(k+1)\displaystyle\sum_{\nu=0}^{k+1}\frac{(-1)^{\nu}\Gamma(k+\alpha+2)}{\nu!(k-\nu+1)!\Gamma(\alpha+\nu+1)}x^{\nu}.\end{array}

Then a direct computation shows that

\displaystyle(2k+\alpha

\displaystyle+1){\mathcal{L}}^{\alpha}_{k}(x)-(k+\alpha){\mathcal{L}}^{\alpha}_{k-1}(x)-(k+1){\mathcal{L}}^{\alpha}_{k+1}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\displaystyle\sum_{\nu=1}^{k+1}\dfrac{(-1)^{\nu-1}\Gamma(k+\alpha+1)}{(\nu-1)!(k-\nu+1)!\Gamma(\alpha+\nu)}x^{\nu}=x{\mathcal{L}}^{\alpha}_{k}(x).

The desired result is now derived.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

For any
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
k\geq 0
, it holds

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}^{\alpha}_{k}(x)={\mathcal{L}}^{\alpha+1}_{k}(x)-{\mathcal{L}}^{\alpha+1}_{k-1}(x),

(2.3)

\displaystyle\partial_{x}{\mathcal{L}}^{\alpha}_{k}(x)=-{\mathcal{L}}^{\alpha+1}_{k-1}(x),

(2.4)

\displaystyle x\partial_{x}{\mathcal{L}}^{\alpha}_{k}(x)=k{\mathcal{L}}^{\alpha}_{k}(x)-(k+\alpha){\mathcal{L}}^{\alpha}_{k-1}(x),

(2.5)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}^{\alpha}_{k}(x)=\partial_{x}{\mathcal{L}}^{\alpha}_{k}(x)-\partial_{x}{\mathcal{L}}^{\alpha}_{k+1}(x),

(2.6)

where
{\mathcal{L}}^{\alpha}_{k}(x)\equiv 0
for any
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{-}.

Proof.

The recurrence relations (
2.3
)-(
2.5
) can be obtained readily by using similar arguments as in Lemma
2.1
. Moreover, by (
2.3
) and (
2.4
), it is easy to derive (
2.6
).
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

For any
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, the generalized Laguerre polynomials
{\mathcal{L}}^{\alpha}_{k}(x)
satisfy the Sturm-Liouville equation

x^{-\alpha}e^{x}\partial_{x}(x^{\alpha+1}e^{-x}\partial_{x}\mathcal{L}_{k}^{\alpha}(x))+\lambda_{k}\mathcal{L}_{k}^{\alpha}(x)=0,\quad k\geq 0,

(2.7)

or equivalently,

x\partial_{x}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{k}^{\alpha}(x)+(\alpha+1-x)\partial_{x}\mathcal{L}_{k}^{\alpha}(x)+\lambda_{k}\mathcal{L}_{k}^{\alpha}(x)=0,\quad k\geq 0,

(2.8)

with the corresponding eigenvalue
\lambda_{k}=k
.

Proof.

Lemma
2.3
can be proved by the continuation method from the Sturm-Liouville equation of the classic
Laguerre polynomials for
\alpha>-1
. Also one can give a proof by using the representation
(
2.1
). We omit the details here.
∎

We are interested in those generalized Laguerre polynomials with an integer index

\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{-}
.

Lemma 2.4
.

For any
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{-}
, we have

\mathcal{L}_{k}^{\alpha}(x)=(-x)^{-\alpha}\frac{(k+\alpha)!}{k!}\mathcal{L}_{k+\alpha}^{-\alpha}(x),\qquad k\geq\chi(\alpha).

(2.9)

And for any
\alpha\in\aleph
, the following orthogonality relation holds:

\displaystyle\int_{\Lambda}{\mathcal{L}}^{\alpha}_{k}(x){\mathcal{L}}^{\alpha}_{m}(x)x^{\alpha}e^{-x}dx=\gamma_{k}^{\alpha}\delta_{k,m},\quad\gamma_{k}^{\alpha}=\frac{\Gamma(k+\alpha+1)}{k!},\qquad k,m\geq\chi(\alpha),

(2.10)

where
\delta_{k,m}
is the Kronecker symbol.

Proof.

The identity (
2.9
) comes directly from
[
29
]
. The orthogonality relation (
2.10
)
is known for classic Laguerre polynomials with
\alpha\in(-1,+\infty)
; while for
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{-}
,
(
2.10
)
can be obtained immediately from (
2.9
) together with (
2.10
) for
\alpha\in(-1,+\infty)
.
∎

We now conclude this subsection with some generalized Laguerre polynomials
{\mathcal{L}}^{\alpha}_{k}(x)
for
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{-}

\hline\cr\alpha=-3&1&-x-2&\tfrac{1}{2}x^{2}+x+1&-\tfrac{1}{6}x^{3}&\dots&-\frac{1}{k(k-1)(k-2)}x^{3}{\mathcal{L}}^{3}_{k-3}(x)\\

2.3.
Generalized Laguerre functions

In this subsection, we shall introduce the generalized Laguerre functions with arbitrary parameters
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
\beta>0

and present some properties.

The generalized Laguerre
functions
l_{k}^{\alpha,\beta}(x),

k\geq 0
are defined by

l_{k}^{\alpha,\beta}(x)=e^{-\frac{1}{2}{\beta}x}\mathcal{L}_{k}^{\alpha}(\beta x),\qquad\forall\alpha\in\mathbb{R},\quad\beta>0,

(2.11)

and the multiplication of

e^{-\frac{1}{2}\beta x}
and the leading term of
\mathcal{L}_{k}^{\alpha}(\beta x)

is simply referred to as the leading term of
l^{\alpha,\beta}_{k}(x)
.

According to (
2.9
), for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{-}
, we have

l_{k}^{\alpha,\beta}(x)=(-\beta x)^{-\alpha}\frac{(k+\alpha)!}{k!}l_{k+\alpha}^{-\alpha,\beta}(x),\quad k\geq\chi(\alpha),

(2.12)

which means that
x=0
is a zero of
l_{k}^{\alpha,\beta}(x)
with the multiplicity
-\alpha
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\partial_{x}^{\nu}l_{k}^{\alpha,\beta}(0)=0,\quad k\geq\chi(\alpha),\quad\nu=0,1,\dots,-\alpha-1.

(2.13)

Due to (
2.2
)-(
2.6
),
the generalized Laguerre functions satisfy the following recurrence
relations:

Lemma 2.5
.

For any
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, it holds that

\displaystyle{\beta}xl_{k}^{\alpha,\beta}(x)=-(k+1)l_{k+1}^{\alpha,\beta}(x)+(2k+\alpha+1)l_{k}^{\alpha,\beta}(x)-(k+\alpha)l_{k-1}^{\alpha,\beta}(x),

(2.14)

\displaystyle l^{\alpha,\beta}_{k}(x)=l^{\alpha+1,\beta}_{k}(x)-l^{\alpha+1,\beta}_{k-1}(x),

(2.15)

\displaystyle\partial_{x}l_{k}^{\alpha,\beta}(x)=-\beta l_{k-1}^{\alpha+1,\beta}(x)-\frac{\beta}{2}l_{k}^{\alpha,\beta}(x)=-\frac{\beta}{2}\big[l_{k}^{\alpha+1,\beta}(x)+l_{k-1}^{\alpha+1,\beta}(x)\big],

(2.16)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&x\partial_{x}l_{k}^{\alpha,\beta}(x)=\frac{k+1}{2}l_{k+1}^{\alpha,\beta}(x)-\frac{\alpha+1}{2}l_{k}^{\alpha,\beta}(x)-\frac{k+\alpha}{2}l_{k-1}^{\alpha,\beta}(x),\end{split}

(2.17)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{x}l_{k}^{\alpha,\beta}(x)-\partial_{x}l_{k+1}^{\alpha,\beta}(x)=\frac{1}{2}\beta(l_{k}^{\alpha,\beta}(x)+l_{k+1}^{\alpha,\beta}(x)).\end{split}

(2.18)

Hereafter, we use the convention that
l_{k}^{\alpha,\beta}(x)=0
whenever
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{-}
.

The generalized Laguerre functions are eigenfunctions of certain singular Sturm-Liouville differential operators.

Lemma 2.6
.

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\sum_{\nu=0}^{n}(-1)^{\nu}\binom{n}{\nu}\frac{\beta^{2n-2\nu}}{2^{2n-2\nu}}x^{-\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\nu}\Big(x^{\alpha+n}\partial_{x}^{\nu}l^{\alpha,\beta}_{k}\Big)=\frac{\beta^{n}}{2^{n}}\lambda_{k,n}^{\alpha}l^{\alpha,\beta}_{k},\quad k\geq 0,

(2.19)

where
\lambda^{\alpha}_{k,n}
satisfies the following recurrence relation,

\displaystyle\lambda^{\alpha}_{k,0}=1,\qquad\lambda^{\alpha}_{k,n}=(k+\alpha+1)\lambda^{\alpha+1}_{k,n-1}+k\lambda^{\alpha+1}_{k-1,n-1},\quad n\geq 1,\;k\geq 0.

(2.20)

Proof.

We prove (
2.19
) and (
2.20
) by induction.
It is obvious that (
2.19
) holds for
n=0
. Moreover, by virtue of (
2.7
) and (
2.11
) we have

\displaystyle-x^{-\alpha}\partial_{x}\Big(x^{\alpha+1}\partial_{x}l_{k}^{\alpha,\beta}(x)\Big)+\frac{\beta^{2}}{4}xl_{k}^{\alpha,\beta}(x)=\frac{\beta}{2}(2k+\alpha+1)l_{k}^{\alpha,\beta}(x)=\frac{\beta}{2}\lambda^{\alpha}_{k,1}l_{k}^{\alpha,\beta}(x),\quad k\geq 0,

which gives (
2.19
) and (
2.20
) for
n=1
.

We now assume that (
2.19
) and (
2.20
) hold for an integer
n\geq 1
. Then
by the recursive formula of binomial coefficients
together with (
2.15
) and (
2.16
),

\displaystyle I:=

\displaystyle\sum_{\nu=0}^{n+1}(-1)^{\nu}\binom{n+1}{\nu}\frac{\beta^{2n+2-2\nu}}{2^{2n+2-2\nu}}x^{-\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\nu}\Big(x^{n+1+\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\nu}l^{\alpha,\beta}_{k}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\nu=0}^{n+1}(-1)^{\nu}\left[\binom{n}{\nu}+\binom{n}{\nu-1}\right]\frac{\beta^{2n+2-2\nu}}{2^{2n+2-2\nu}}x^{-\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\nu}\Big(x^{n+1+\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\nu}l^{\alpha,\beta}_{k}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\beta^{2}}{4}x\sum_{\nu=0}^{n}(-1)^{\nu}\binom{n}{\nu}\frac{\beta^{2n-2\nu}}{2^{2n-2\nu}}x^{-(\alpha+1)}\partial_{x}^{\nu}\Big[x^{n+(\alpha+1)}\partial_{x}^{\nu}\Big(l^{\alpha+1,\beta}_{k}-l^{\alpha+1,\beta}_{k-1}\Big)\Big]

\displaystyle+\frac{\beta}{2}x^{-\alpha}\partial_{x}\sum_{\nu=1}^{n+1}(-1)^{\nu-1}\binom{n}{\nu-1}\frac{\beta^{2n-2(\nu-1)}}{2^{2n-2(\nu-1)}}\partial_{x}^{\nu-1}\Big[x^{n+(\alpha+1)}\partial_{x}^{\nu-1}\left(l^{\alpha+1,\beta}_{k}+l^{\alpha+1,\beta}_{k-1}\right)\Big].

Thus by the induction assumption, (
2.14
), (
2.17
) and (
2.15
), we derive that

\displaystyle I=

\displaystyle\frac{\beta^{n+2}}{2^{n+2}}x\left[\lambda^{\alpha+1}_{k,n}l^{\alpha+1,\beta}_{k}-\lambda^{\alpha+1}_{k-1,n}l^{\alpha+1,\beta}_{k-1}\right]+\frac{\beta^{n+1}}{2^{n+1}}x^{-\alpha}\partial_{x}\left[x^{\alpha+1}\left(\lambda^{\alpha+1}_{k,n}l^{\alpha+1,\beta}_{k}+\lambda^{\alpha+1}_{k-1,n}l^{\alpha+1,\beta}_{k-1}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\beta^{n+1}}{2^{n+1}}\lambda^{\alpha+1}_{k,n}\left[(\alpha+1+\tfrac{\beta}{2}x)l^{\alpha+1,\beta}_{k}+x\partial_{x}l^{\alpha+1,\beta}_{k}\right]+\frac{\beta^{n+1}}{2^{n+1}}\lambda^{\alpha+1}_{k-1,n}\left[(\alpha+1-\tfrac{\beta}{2}x)l^{\alpha+1,\beta}_{k-1}+x\partial_{x}l^{\alpha+1,\beta}_{k-1}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\beta^{n+1}}{2^{n+1}}\lambda^{\alpha+1}_{k,n}(k+\alpha+1)\big[l^{\alpha+1,\beta}_{k}-l^{\alpha+1,\beta}_{k-1}\big]+\frac{\beta^{n+1}}{2^{n+1}}\lambda^{\alpha+1}_{k-1,n}k\big[l^{\alpha+1,\beta}_{k}-l^{\alpha+1,\beta}_{k-1}\big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\beta^{n+1}}{2^{n+1}}\big[(k+\alpha+1)\lambda^{\alpha+1}_{k,n}+k\lambda^{\alpha+1}_{k-1,n}\big]l^{\alpha,\beta}_{k},

which is exactly (
2.19
) and (
2.20
) with
n+1
in place of
n
.
This ends the proof.
∎

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
\beta>0
, define the bilinear form on
H^{n}_{w^{\alpha+n}}(\Lambda)\times H^{n}_{w^{\alpha+n}}(\Lambda)
,

\displaystyle a^{\alpha,\beta}_{n}(u,v)=\sum_{\nu=0}^{n}\binom{n}{\nu}\frac{\beta^{2n-2\nu}}{2^{2n-2\nu}}(\partial_{x}^{\nu}u,\partial_{x}^{\nu}v)_{w^{\alpha+n}}.

(2.21)

It is obvious that
a^{\alpha,\beta}_{n}(\cdot,\cdot)

3.
Fully diagonalized spectral methods

In this section, we propose the fully diagonalized spectral methods
using generalized Laguerre functions for solving differential equations
on the half line. The main idea is to find a system of Sobolev orthogonal
functions
[
8
,
21
]
with respect to the coercive bilinear form arising from differential equation,
such that both the exact solution and the approximate solution can be explicitly expressed
as a Fourier series in the Sobolev orthogonal
functions. Although we only consider in this section
non-homogenous Robin/Drichlet boundary value problems of a second order equation,
one can extend the fully diagonalized spectral methods for solving partial differential equations
of an arbitrary high order.

3.1.
Robin boundary value problems

Consider the second order elliptic boundary value problem:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-u^{\prime\prime}(x)+\gamma u(x)=f(x),\quad\gamma\geq 0,\quad x\in\Lambda,\\[6.0pt]
-u^{\prime}(0)+\mu u(0)=\eta,\quad\displaystyle\lim_{{x\rightarrow+\infty}}u(x)=0,\quad\mu\geq 0.\end{array}\right.

(3.1)

A weak formulation of (
3.1
) is to find
u\in H^{1}(\Lambda)
such that

A_{\gamma,\mu}(u,v):=\mu u(0)v(0)+(u^{\prime},v^{\prime})+\gamma(u,v)=(f,v)+\eta\,v(0),\quad\forall v\in H^{1}(\Lambda).

(3.2)

The Lax-Milgram lemma guarantees a unique solution to (
3.2
)
if
f\in(H^{1}(\Lambda))^{\prime}
.

Let

X_{N}^{\beta}:=\{e^{-\frac{1}{2}\beta x}p(x):p\in\mathbb{P}_{N}\}=\{l^{-1,\beta}_{k}:0\leq k\leq N\}.

The generalized Laguerre spectral scheme for (

u_{N}\in X^{\beta}_{N}
, such that

A_{\gamma,\mu}(u_{N},v_{N})=(f,v_{N})+\eta v_{N}(0),\quad\forall v_{N}\in X^{\beta}_{N}.

(3.3)

For an efficient approximation scheme, one usually chooses the generalized Laguerre functions

\{l_{k}^{-1,\beta}(x)\}_{0\leq k\leq N}
as the basis functions for problem (
3.3
).
However, we are eager for an ideal approximation scheme whose (total) stiff matrix, in analogue to the Fourier spectral method for periodic problem, is diagonal. Obviously, the utilization of the basis functions
\{l_{k}^{-1,\beta}(x)\}_{0\leq k\leq N}

leads to a tridiagonal algebraic system. To this end, we shall construct new basis functions
\{\mathcal{R}_{k}^{\beta}(x)\}_{0\leq k\leq N}

which are mutually orthogonal with respect to the Sobolev inner product
A_{\gamma,\mu}(\cdot,\cdot)
instead of
a_{1}^{-1,\beta}(\cdot,\cdot)
defined in Theorem
2.1
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{R}_{k}^{\beta}\in X^{\beta}_{k},\,k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
be the Sobolev orthogonal Laguerre functions such that

\mathcal{R}_{k}^{\beta}-l^{-1,\beta}_{k}\in X^{\beta}_{k-1}

A_{\gamma,\mu}(\mathcal{R}_{k}^{\beta},\mathcal{R}_{m}^{\beta})=\rho_{k}\delta_{k,m},\qquad k,m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

(3.4)

\mathcal{R}_{0}^{\beta}(x)=l_{0}^{-1,\beta}(x),\quad\mathcal{R}_{k}^{\beta}(x)=l_{k}^{-1,\beta}(x)-d_{k-1}\mathcal{R}_{k-1}^{\beta}(x),\quad\forall\ k\geq 1,

where

and

\displaystyle d_{k-1}=\frac{\beta}{4\rho_{k-1}}-\frac{\gamma}{\beta\rho_{k-1}},\quad\rho_{k}=-d^{2}_{k-1}\rho_{k-1}+\frac{\beta}{2}+\frac{2\gamma}{\beta},\qquad k\geq 1.

By the orthogonality assumption (

4.
Convergence analysis

In this section, we shall derive the optimal error estimate for the spectral methods using generalized Laguerre functions.
To this end, we first conduct some numerical analysis on the orthogonal projections.

4.1.
Orthogonal projections

Let
r,N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
\alpha>-r-1
and
\beta>0

Define the orthogonal projection
\pi^{\alpha,\beta}_{r,N}:H^{r}_{w^{\alpha+r}}(\Lambda)\mapsto X^{\beta}_{N}

\displaystyle a^{\alpha,\beta}_{r}(u-\pi^{\alpha,\beta}_{r,N}u,v)=0,\quad v\in X^{\beta}_{N}.

(4.1)

In view of the orthogonality relation (

\pi^{\alpha,\beta}_{r,N}u

of

\displaystyle\pi^{\alpha,\beta}_{r,N}u(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{N}\hat{u}_{k}l^{\alpha,\beta}_{k}(x),\quad u(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\hat{u}_{k}l^{\alpha,\beta}_{k}(x),\qquad\hat{u}_{k}=\frac{a^{\alpha,\beta}_{r}(u,l^{\alpha,\beta}_{k})}{a^{\alpha,\beta}_{r}(l^{\alpha,\beta}_{k},l^{\alpha,\beta}_{k})}.

(4.2)

In return, one obtains that, for any nonnegative integers

\displaystyle a^{\alpha,\beta}_{s}(u-\pi^{\alpha,\beta}_{r,N}u,v)=0,\quad v\in X^{\beta}_{N},

\pi^{\alpha,\beta}_{r,N}=\pi^{\alpha,\beta}_{s,N}

\pi^{\alpha,\beta}_{N}=\pi^{\alpha,\beta}_{r,N}
.

Besides,
(
2.12
) clearly states
\partial_{x}^{\ell}l^{-n,\beta}_{k}(0)=0
,
\ell=0,1,\dots,n-1
for any
k\geq n\geq 1
.
Thus for
N\geq n
,
\pi_{N}^{-n,\beta}u
preserves the endpoint values of
u
up to the
(n-1)
-th order derivative, i.e.,

\displaystyle\partial_{x}^{\ell}\pi_{N}^{-n,\beta}u(0)=\partial_{x}^{\ell}u(0),\qquad\ell=0,1,\dots,n-1.

(4.3)

In other words,
u-\pi_{N}^{-n,\beta}u\in H^{n}_{0}(\Lambda)
for any
u\in H^{n}(\Lambda)
if
N\geq n
.

To measure the error between
u
and
\pi_{N}^{\alpha,\beta}u
, we introduce the equivalent norm in
H^{r}_{w^{\alpha+r}}(\Lambda)

for
r\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\|u\|_{r,\alpha,\beta}=\big[a^{\alpha,\beta}_{r}(u,u)\big]^{1/2}=\Big[\sum_{\nu=0}^{r}\binom{r}{\nu}\frac{\beta^{2r-2\nu}}{2^{2r-2\nu}}\big(\partial_{x}^{\nu}u,\partial_{x}^{\nu}u\big)_{w^{\alpha+r}}\Big]^{1/2}.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
r\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
\alpha>-r-1
and
\beta>0
. Then for any function
u\in H^{r}_{w^{\alpha+r}}(\Lambda)
and any nonnegative integers
N,s>-\alpha-1
,

\displaystyle\|u-\pi_{N}^{\alpha,\beta}u\|_{s,\alpha,\beta}\lesssim(\beta N)^{\frac{s-r}{2}}\|u-\pi_{N}^{\alpha,\beta}u\|_{r,\alpha,\beta}\lesssim(\beta N)^{\frac{s-r}{2}}\|u\|_{r,\alpha,\beta},\quad r\geq s,

(4.4)

where the implicit constants
c=c(\alpha,r)
is independent of
\beta
,
N
,
s
and
u
.

Proof.

By (
4.2
) and the orthogonality (
2.23
),

\displaystyle\|u-\pi^{\alpha,\beta}_{N}

\displaystyle u\|_{s,\alpha,\beta}^{2}=\sum_{k=N+1}^{\infty}\beta^{s-\alpha-1}\gamma^{\alpha}_{k,s}\hat{u}_{k}^{2}=\beta^{s-r}\sum_{k=N+1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\gamma^{\alpha}_{k,s}}{\gamma^{\alpha}_{k,r}}\right)\beta^{r-\alpha-1}\gamma^{\alpha}_{k,r}\hat{u}_{k}^{2}.

By (
2.29
), one reveals that

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma^{\alpha}_{k,s}}{\gamma^{\alpha}_{k,r}}\lesssim\frac{2^{r-s}}{(2k+\alpha+1)^{r-s}}\lesssim\frac{1}{N^{r-s}},\qquad k\geq N+1,\,r\geq s.

As a result,

\displaystyle\|u-

\displaystyle\pi^{\alpha,\beta}_{N}u\|_{s,\alpha,\beta}^{2}\lesssim(\beta N)^{s-r}\sum_{k=N+1}^{\infty}\beta^{r-\alpha-1}\gamma^{\alpha}_{k,r}\hat{u}_{k}^{2}

\displaystyle=(\beta N)^{s-r}\|u-\pi^{\alpha,\beta}_{N}u\|_{r,\alpha,\beta}^{2}\leq(\beta N)^{s-r}\|u\|_{r,\alpha,\beta}^{2},

which leads to (
4.4
).
∎

4.2.
Convergence analysis

We first give the error estimate of the generalized Laguerre spectral method
(
3.8
) for the non-homogenous Dirichlet boundary value problem (
3.6
).

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
u
and
u_{N}
be the solutions to (
3.6
) and (
3.8
), respectively.
If
u\in H^{r}_{w^{r-1}}(\Lambda)
and integer

r\geq 1
, then for sufficiently large
N
,

\|(u-u_{N})^{\prime}\|^{2}+\gamma\|u-u_{N}\|^{2}\lesssim\Big(1+\frac{4\gamma}{\beta^{2}}\Big)\,(\beta N)^{1-r}\|u\|^{2}_{r,-1,\beta},

(4.5)

and

\|u-u_{N}\|_{w^{-1}}\lesssim\Big(1+\frac{\beta^{2}}{4\gamma}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{2\sqrt{\gamma}}{\beta}\Big)^{2}\,(\beta N)^{-\frac{r}{2}}\|u\|_{r,-1,\beta}.

(4.6)

Proof.

We first prove the inequality (
4.5
).
By (
3.7
), we get

\displaystyle((\pi_{N}^{-1,\beta}

\displaystyle u)^{\prime},v_{N}^{\prime})+\gamma(\pi_{N}^{-1,\beta}u,v_{N})=(f,v_{N})+((\pi_{N}^{-1,\beta}u-u)^{\prime},v_{N}^{\prime})+\gamma(\pi_{N}^{-1,\beta}u-u,v_{N}),\quad v_{N}\in X^{0,\beta}_{N}.

Subtracting the above equation
from (
3.8
) yields

\displaystyle((\pi^{-1,\beta}_{N}

\displaystyle u-u_{N})^{\prime},v_{N}^{\prime})+\gamma(\pi^{-1,\beta}_{N}u-u_{N},v_{N})=((\pi_{N}^{-1,\beta}u-u)^{\prime},v_{N}^{\prime})+\gamma(\pi_{N}^{-1,\beta}u-u,v_{N}).

The above with (
4.1
) and the Cauchy-Schwartz
inequality gives that for any real number
q
,

\displaystyle\,\big|((\pi^{-1,\beta}_{N}u-u_{N})^{\prime},v_{N}^{\prime})+\gamma(\pi^{-1,\beta}_{N}u-u_{N},v_{N})\big|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\big|(1+q)((\pi_{N}^{-1,\beta}u-u)^{\prime},v_{N}^{\prime})+\frac{4\gamma+\beta^{2}q}{4}(\pi_{N}^{-1,\beta}u-u,v_{N})\big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,|1+q|\,\big\|(\pi_{N}^{-1,\beta}u-u)^{\prime}\big\|\,\big\|v_{N}^{\prime}\big\|+\frac{|4\gamma+\beta^{2}q|}{4}\big\|\pi_{N}^{-1,\beta}u-u\big\|\,\big\|v_{N}\big\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,\Big[(1+q)^{2}\big\|(\pi_{N}^{-1,\beta}u-u)^{\prime}\big\|^{2}+\frac{(4\gamma+\beta^{2}q)^{2}}{16\gamma}\big\|\pi_{N}^{-1,\beta}u-u\big\|^{2}\Big]^{1/2}\,\big[\|v_{N}^{\prime}\|^{2}+\gamma\|v_{N}\|^{2}\big]^{1/2}.

Taking
v_{N}=u_{N}-\pi_{N}^{-1,\beta}u\in X^{0,\beta}_{N}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|(u_{N}-

\displaystyle\pi^{-1,\beta}_{N}u)^{\prime}\|^{2}+\gamma\|u_{N}-\pi^{-1,\beta}_{N}u\|^{2}\leq(1+q)^{2}\big\|(\pi_{N}^{-1,\beta}u-u)^{\prime}\big\|^{2}+\frac{(4\gamma+\beta^{2}q)^{2}}{16\gamma}\big\|\pi_{N}^{-1,\beta}u-u\big\|^{2}.

This, along with the triangle inequality, leads to

\displaystyle\|(u-u_{N})^{\prime}\|^{2}+\gamma\|u-u_{N}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|(u-\pi^{-1,\beta}_{N}u)^{\prime}\|^{2}+2\gamma\|u-\pi^{-1,\beta}_{N}u\|^{2}+2\|(u_{N}-\pi^{-1,\beta}_{N}u)^{\prime}\|^{2}+2\gamma\|u_{N}-\pi^{-1,\beta}_{N}u\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2(q^{2}+2q+2)\big\|(\pi_{N}^{-1,\beta}u-u)^{\prime}\big\|^{2}+\frac{\beta^{4}q^{2}+8\beta^{2}\gamma q+32\gamma^{2}}{8\gamma}\big\|\pi_{N}^{-1,\beta}u-u\big\|^{2}.

Now taking
q=-\frac{2\sqrt{2\gamma}}{\beta}
and using (
4.4
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|(u-u_{N}

\displaystyle)^{\prime}\|^{2}+\gamma\|u-u_{N}\|^{2}\leq\big[2+2\big(1-\tfrac{2\sqrt{2\gamma}}{\beta}\big)^{2}\big]\,\Big[\big\|(\pi_{N}^{-1,\beta}u-u)^{\prime}\big\|^{2}+\frac{\beta^{2}}{4}\big\|\pi_{N}^{-1,\beta}u-u\big\|^{2}\Big].

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\,\big(1+4\gamma/\beta^{2}\big)\,(\beta N)^{1-r}\|u\|^{2}_{r,-1,\beta}.

We next verify the inequality (
4.6
) using a duality
argument. Consider the
auxiliary problem

-xv^{\prime\prime}(x)+\gamma xv(x)=u(x)-u_{N}(x)~\text{ in }~\Lambda,\qquad v(0)=0.

(4.7)

Its weak form is

(\varphi^{\prime},v^{\prime})+\gamma(\varphi,v)=(\varphi,u-u_{N})_{w^{-1}},\quad\forall\varphi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Lambda),

which admits a unique solution
v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Lambda)
.
Moreover, (
4.7
) yields

\displaystyle\|u-u_{N}\|_{w^{-1}}^{2}=(-xv^{\prime\prime}+\gamma xv,-v^{\prime\prime}+\gamma v)=\|v^{\prime\prime}\|_{w}^{2}+\gamma^{2}\|v\|_{w}^{2}+2\gamma\|v^{\prime}\|_{w}^{2}=\|v\|^{2}_{2,-1,2\sqrt{\gamma}},

(4.8)

where the second equality sign is derived by integration by parts.
Further, a direct computation leads to

\displaystyle\|v\|_{2,-1,\beta}\leq\Big(1+\frac{\beta^{2}}{4\gamma}\Big)\|v\|_{2,-1,2\sqrt{\gamma}}.

(4.9)

Hence, taking
\varphi=u-u_{N}
and using the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality, we have

\displaystyle\|u-u_{N}\|_{w^{-1}}^{2}

\displaystyle=(u-u_{N},u-u_{N})_{w^{-1}}=((u-u_{N})^{\prime},v^{\prime})+\gamma(u-u_{N},v)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{4.3s}}{=}((u-u_{N})^{\prime},(v-\pi^{-1,\beta}_{N}v)^{\prime})+\gamma(u-u_{N},v-\pi^{-1,\beta}_{N}v)

\displaystyle\leq\Big[\|(u-u_{N})^{\prime}\|^{2}+\frac{4\gamma^{2}}{\beta^{2}}\|u-u_{N}\|^{2}\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\Big[\|(v-\pi^{-1,\beta}_{N}v)^{\prime}\|^{2}+\frac{\beta^{2}}{4}\|v-\pi^{-1,\beta}_{N}v\|^{2}\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{errorProj}}{\lesssim}(\beta N)^{-1/2}\|v\|_{2,-1,\beta}\Big[\|(u-u_{N})^{\prime}\|^{2}+\frac{4\gamma^{2}}{\beta^{2}}\|u-u_{N}\|^{2}\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}

(4.10)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{tran}}{\lesssim}\Big(1+\frac{\beta^{2}}{4\gamma}\Big)(\beta N)^{-1/2}\|v\|_{2,-1,2\sqrt{\gamma}}\times\Big(1+\frac{2\sqrt{\gamma}}{\beta}\Big)\Big[\|(u-u_{N})^{\prime}\|^{2}+\gamma\|u-u_{N}\|^{2}\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{stab}}{\lesssim}\Big(1+\frac{\beta^{2}}{4\gamma}\Big)(\beta N)^{-1/2}\|u-u_{N}\|_{w^{-1}}\times\Big(1+\frac{2\sqrt{\gamma}}{\beta}\Big)^{2}(\beta N)^{1/2-r/2}\|u\|_{r,-1,\beta}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{4.4s}}{\lesssim}\Big(1+\frac{\beta^{2}}{4\gamma}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{2\sqrt{\gamma}}{\beta}\Big)^{2}(\beta N)^{-\frac{r}{2}}\|u-u_{N}\|_{w^{-1}}\|u\|_{r,-1,\beta},

which ends the proof of (
4.6
).
∎

Next, we present the main theorem on the generalized Laguerre spectral method for the Robin boundary value problem
(
3.1
).

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
u
and
u_{N}
be the solutions to (
3.1
) and (
3.3
), respectively.
If
u\in H^{r}_{w^{r-1}}(\Lambda)
and integer

r\geq 1
, then for sufficiently large
N
,

\mu\big|u(0)-u_{N}(0)\big|^{2}+\|(u-u_{N})^{\prime}\|^{2}+\gamma\|u-u_{N}\|^{2}\lesssim\Big(1+\frac{4\gamma}{\beta^{2}}\Big)\,(\beta N)^{1-r}\|u\|^{2}_{r,-1,\beta},

(4.11)

and

\|u-u_{N}\|_{(1+w)^{-1}}\lesssim\Big(1+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\mu}}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\beta^{2}}{4\gamma}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{2\sqrt{\gamma}}{\beta}\Big)^{2}\,(\beta N)^{-\frac{r}{2}}\|u\|_{r,-1,\beta}.

(4.12)

Proof.

By (
4.1
) and (
3.2
),

\displaystyle\mu

\displaystyle\pi_{N}^{-1,\beta}u(0)v_{N}(0)+((\pi_{N}^{-1,\beta}u)^{\prime},v_{N}^{\prime})+\gamma(\pi_{N}^{-1,\beta}u,v_{N})

\displaystyle=\mu u(0)v_{N}(0)+(u^{\prime},v_{N}^{\prime})+\gamma(u,v_{N})+((\pi_{N}^{-1,\beta}u-u)^{\prime},v_{N}^{\prime})+\gamma(\pi_{N}^{-1,\beta}u-u,v_{N})

\displaystyle=(f,v_{N})+\eta v_{N}(0)+((\pi_{N}^{-1,\beta}u-u)^{\prime},v_{N}^{\prime})+\gamma(\pi_{N}^{-1,\beta}u-u,v_{N}),\quad v_{N}\in X_{N}^{\beta}.

Substracting the above equation by (
3.3
) yields

\displaystyle\mu(\pi_{N}^{-1,\beta}

\displaystyle u(0)-u_{N}(0))v_{N}(0)+((\pi_{N}^{-1,\beta}u-u_{N})^{\prime},v_{N}^{\prime})+\gamma(\pi_{N}^{-1,\beta}u-u_{N},v_{N})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,((\pi_{N}^{-1,\beta}u-u)^{\prime},v_{N}^{\prime})+\gamma(\pi_{N}^{-1,\beta}u-u,v_{N}).

Then a similar argument as in the proof of (
4.5
) gives (
4.11
).

We next verify the inequality (
4.12
) using a duality argument.
For any
g\in L^{2}_{w^{-1}+w^{-2}}(\Lambda)
, we consider the auxiliary
problem

-xv^{\prime\prime}(x)+\gamma xv(x)=g(x)~\text{ in }~\Lambda,\qquad-v^{\prime}(0)+\mu v(0)=0.

(4.13)

Its weak form is

\mu\varphi(0)v(0)+(\varphi^{\prime},v^{\prime})+\gamma(\varphi,v)=(\varphi,g)_{w^{-1}},\quad\forall\varphi\in H^{1}(\Lambda),

which admits a unique solution
v\in H^{1}(\Lambda)
.
Taking
\varphi=u-u_{N}
, we have

\mu(u(0)-u_{N}(0))v(0)+(u^{\prime}-u_{N}^{\prime},v^{\prime})+\gamma(u-u_{N},v)=(u-u_{N},g)_{w^{-1}}.

(4.14)

Moreover, by (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) we get

\mu(u(0)-u_{N}(0))v_{N}(0)+(u^{\prime}-u_{N}^{\prime},v_{N}^{\prime})+\gamma(u-u_{N},v_{N})=0,\quad\forall v_{N}\in X^{\beta}_{N}.

(4.15)

Let

v_{N}=\pi_{N}^{-1,\beta}v
in (
4.15
). Then by (
4.14
),
(
4.15
) and (
4.3
) we deduce

((u-u_{N})^{\prime},(v-\pi_{N}^{-1,\beta}v)^{\prime})+\gamma(u-u_{N},v-\pi_{N}^{-1,\beta}v)=(u-u_{N},g)_{w^{-1}}.

(4.16)

Further by integration by parts, (
4.13
) yields

\displaystyle\|g\|_{w^{-2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=(-v^{\prime\prime}+\gamma v,-v^{\prime\prime}+\gamma v)=\|v^{\prime\prime}\|^{2}+\gamma^{2}\|v\|^{2}+2\gamma\|v^{\prime}\|^{2}+2\gamma\mu v^{2}(0),

(4.17)

and

\displaystyle\|g\|_{w^{-1}}^{2}

\displaystyle=(-xv^{\prime\prime}+\gamma xv,-v^{\prime\prime}+\gamma v)=\|v^{\prime\prime}\|^{2}_{w}+\gamma^{2}\|v\|^{2}_{w}+2\gamma\|v^{\prime}\|^{2}_{w}-\gamma v^{2}(0).

(4.18)

Hence

\displaystyle\|v\|^{2}_{2,-1,2\sqrt{\gamma}}=\|v^{\prime\prime}\|^{2}_{w}+\gamma^{2}\|v\|^{2}_{w}+2\gamma\|v^{\prime}\|^{2}_{w}\leq\|g\|_{w^{-1}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2\mu}\|g\|_{w^{-2}}^{2}\leq(1+\frac{1}{2\mu})\|g\|_{w^{-1}+w^{-2}}^{2}.

(4.19)

Thus, a similar argument as (
4.2
) gives

\displaystyle|(u-u_{N},g)_{w^{-1}}|

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{sr3}}{=}|((u-u_{N})^{\prime},(v-\pi^{-1,\beta}_{N}v)^{\prime})+\gamma(u-u_{N},v-\pi^{-1,\beta}_{N}v)|

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{errs}}{\lesssim}\Big(1+\frac{\beta^{2}}{4\gamma}\Big)(\beta N)^{-1/2}\|v\|_{2,-1,2\sqrt{\gamma}}\times\Big(1+\frac{2\sqrt{\gamma}}{\beta}\Big)\Big[\|(u-u_{N})^{\prime}\|^{2}+\gamma\|u-u_{N}\|^{2}\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{stabr2}}{\lesssim}\Big(1+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\mu}}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\beta^{2}}{4\gamma}\Big)(\beta N)^{-1/2}\|g\|_{w^{-1}+w^{-2}}\times\Big(1+\frac{2\sqrt{\gamma}}{\beta}\Big)^{2}(\beta N)^{1/2-r/2}\|u\|_{r,-1,\beta}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(1+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\mu}}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\beta^{2}}{4\gamma}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{2\sqrt{\gamma}}{\beta}\Big)^{2}(\beta N)^{-\frac{r}{2}}\|g\|_{w^{-1}+w^{-2}}\|u\|_{r,-1,\beta}.

Finally, we obtain

\displaystyle\|u-u_{N}\|_{(1+w)^{-1}}=\|\frac{x}{1+x}(u-u_{N})\|_{w^{-1}+w^{-2}}=\sup_{g\in L^{2}_{w^{-1}+w^{-2}}(\Lambda),~g\neq 0}\frac{|(\frac{x}{1+x}(u-u_{N}),g)_{w^{-1}+w^{-2}}|}{\|g\|_{w^{-1}+w^{-2}}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{g\in L^{2}_{w^{-1}+w^{-2}}(\Lambda),~g\neq 0}\frac{|(u-u_{N},g)_{w^{-1}}|}{\|g\|_{w^{-1}+w^{-2}}}\lesssim\Big(1+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\mu}}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\beta^{2}}{4\gamma}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{2\sqrt{\gamma}}{\beta}\Big)^{2}(\beta N)^{-\frac{r}{2}}\|u\|_{r,-1,\beta},

which ends the proof of (
4.12
).
∎

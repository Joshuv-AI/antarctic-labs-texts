Determinantal Formulas for Rational Perturbations of Multiple Orthogonality Measures
Rostyslav Kozhan 1, Marcus Vaktnäs 2

Abstract
. Given multiple orthogonal polynomials on the real line with respect to a system 𝝁 = ( μ 1 , … , μ r ) \bm{\mu}=(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{r}) , we investigate multiple orthogonal polynomials associated with any rational perturbation of the form 𝝁 ~ = ( Φ 1 Ψ 1 ​ μ 1 , … , Φ r Ψ r ​ μ r ) , \widetilde{\bm{\mu}}=\Big(\frac{\Phi_{1}}{\Psi_{1}}\mu_{1},\dots,\frac{\Phi_{r}}{\Psi_{r}}\mu_{r}\Big), for any polynomials Φ 1 , … , Φ r \Phi_{1},\dots,\Phi_{r} and Ψ 1 , … , Ψ r \Psi_{1},\dots,\Psi_{r} . We derive the analogues of Uvarov’s determinantal formula for the multiple orthogonal polynomials of type I and type II for 𝝁 ~ \widetilde{\bm{\mu}} and establish necessary and sufficient condition for normality of the indices. The result allows the polynomials { Φ j , Ψ j } j = 1 r \{\Phi_{j},\Psi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{r} to be arbitrary and permits the addition of finitely many point masses to each of the measures μ j \mu_{j} . Moreover, the measures μ j \mu_{j} may be taken as quasi-definite linear functionals, which is of interest even in the case r = 1 r=1 .



1.
Introduction

For a positive Borel measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}
with infinite support and finite moments, we have orthogonal polynomials
(P_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
defined by
the orthogonality condition

(1)

\begin{split}\int P_{n}(x)x^{p}d\mu(x)=0,\qquad p=0,\dots,n-1,\end{split}

along with the degree condition
\deg{P_{n}}\leq n
.
Each non-zero
P_{n}
necessarily has degree
n
and is unique up to multiplication by a constant. If we normalize
the polynomials to be monic (having leading coefficient
1
), then these polynomials are uniquely determined by (
1
).

Now consider the condition

(2)

\begin{split}\int\widehat{P}_{n}(x)x^{p}\Phi(x)d\mu(x)=0,\qquad p=0,\dots,n-1,\end{split}

along with the degree condition
\deg{\widehat{P}_{n}}\leq n
, for a polynomial
\Phi(x)=\prod_{k=1}^{N}(x-z_{k})
. If
\Phi
is positive on
\operatorname{supp}(\mu)
then (
2
) is just (
1
) for the positive measure
\widehat{\mu}
defined by

(3)

\begin{split}\int f(x)d\widehat{\mu}(x)=\int f(x)\Phi(x)d\mu(x).\end{split}

Note that this measure is also Borel with infinite support and finite moments. Assuming
\Phi
has only simple zeros, Christoffel’s Theorem
[
Christoffel
]
states that the monic orthogonal polynomials
\widehat{P}_{n}
with respect to
\widehat{\mu}
are given by

(4)

\begin{split}\widehat{P}_{n}(x)={\Phi(x)}^{-1}D_{n}^{-1}\det\begin{pmatrix}P_{n+N}(x)&P_{n+N-1}(x)&\cdots&P_{n}(x)\\
P_{n+N}(z_{1})&P_{n+N-1}(z_{1})&\cdots&P_{n}(z_{1})\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
P_{n+N}(z_{N})&P_{n+N-1}(z_{N})&\cdots&P_{n}(z_{N})\\
\end{pmatrix},\end{split}

where
D_{n}
is the normalizing constant

(5)

\begin{split}D_{n}=\det\begin{pmatrix}P_{n+N-1}(z_{1})&\cdots&P_{n}(z_{1})\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
P_{n+N-1}(z_{N})&\cdots&P_{n}(z_{N})\end{pmatrix}.\end{split}

We refer to
\widehat{\mu}
as a Christoffel transform of
\mu
. If
\deg{\Phi}=1
we say that
\widehat{\mu}
is a one-step Christoffel transform, and then (
4
) simplifies to

(6)

\begin{split}\widehat{P}_{n}(x)=(x-z_{1})^{-1}\left(P_{n+1}(x)-\frac{P_{n+1}(z_{1})}{P_{n}(z_{1})}P_{n}(x)\right).\end{split}

It is well-known that the zeros of
P_{n}
lie in the interior of every interval containing
\operatorname{supp}(\mu)
, so the denominator in (
6
) will always be non-zero, assuming
z_{1}
is outside the convex hull of
\operatorname{supp}(\mu)
. More generally, one can show that (
5
) is also non-zero, at least when
\Phi
does not change sign on
\operatorname{supp}(\mu)
.

Consider the more general rational transformations
\widetilde{\mu}
defined by

(7)

\begin{split}\int f(x)d\widetilde{\mu}(x)=\int f(x)\frac{\Phi(x)}{\Psi(x)}d\mu(x),\end{split}

for polynomials
\Phi(x)=\prod_{k=1}^{N}(x-z_{k})
and
\Psi(x)=\prod_{k=1}^{M}(x-w_{k})
such that
\Phi/\Psi
is positive and integrable on
\operatorname{supp}(\mu)
. Uvarov
[
Uvarov
]
showed that the monic orthogonal polynomials
\widetilde{P}_{n}
with respect to the measure
\widetilde{\mu}
are given by

(8)

\begin{split}\widetilde{P}_{n}(x)={\Phi(x)}^{-1}D_{n}^{-1}\det\begin{pmatrix}P_{n+N}(x)&P_{n+N-1}(x)&\cdots&P_{n-M}(x)\\
P_{n+N}(z_{1})&P_{n+N-1}(z_{1})&\cdots&P_{n-M}(z_{1})\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
P_{n+N}(z_{N})&P_{n+N-1}(z_{N})&\cdots&P_{n-M}(z_{N})\\
Q_{n+N}(w_{1})&Q_{n+N-1}(w_{1})&\cdots&Q_{n-M}(w_{1})\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
Q_{n+N}(w_{M})&Q_{n+N-1}(w_{M})&\cdots&Q_{n-M}(w_{M})\end{pmatrix},\qquad n\geq M.\end{split}

Here
Q_{n}(z)
are the so-called functions of the second kind defined by

(9)

\begin{split}Q_{n}(z)=\int\frac{P_{n}(x)}{x-z}d\mu(x),\end{split}

and
D_{n}
is the normalizing constant

(10)

\begin{split}D_{n}=\det{\begin{pmatrix}P_{n+N-1}(z_{1})&\cdots&P_{n-M}(z_{1})\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
P_{n+N-1}(z_{N})&\cdots&P_{n-M}(z_{N})\\
Q_{n+N-1}(w_{1})&\cdots&Q_{n-M}(w_{1})\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
Q_{n+N-1}(w_{M})&\cdots&Q_{n-M}(w_{M})\end{pmatrix}}.\end{split}

This determinant is always non-zero under the assumption that
\Phi/\Psi
is positive on
\operatorname{supp}(\mu)
.

The current paper provides a generalization of the formula (
8
) to the multiple orthogonality setting. Multiple orthogonal polynomials are polynomials that satisfy simultaneous orthogonality conditions with respect to multi-indices
(n_{1},\dots,n_{r})
and a system of measures
(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{r})
, where the case
r=1
reduces to the standard orthogonal polynomials. For an introduction to multiple orthogonal polynomials with applications, see
[
Aptekarev
,
Ismail
,
Applications
]
. The rational transformations of
(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{r})
considered in this paper are the systems
(\widetilde{\mu}_{1},\dots,\widetilde{\mu}_{r})
of the form

(11)

\begin{split}\int f(x)d\widetilde{\mu}_{j}(x)=\int f(x)\frac{\Phi_{j}(x)}{\Psi_{j}(x)}d\mu_{j}(x)+\sum_{k=1}^{M_{j}}c_{j,k}f({w_{j,k}}),\qquad j=1,\dots,r.\end{split}

Such a rational perturbation of
\mu
is not necessarily a positive measure, so we choose to work in the more general setting of orthogonality with respect to linear moment functionals.
We prove the determinantal formulas, both for type I and for type II polynomials, and give a necessary and sufficient condition for their existence and uniqueness (normality). This condition is
a simple determinantal condition
D_{\bm{n}}\neq 0
, where
D_{\bm{n}}
is an appropriately chosen analogue of (
10
).

For the case
r=1
, the transformation (
11
) with
\Phi(x)=1
is called the Geronimus transform
[
Geronimus
]
and (
11
) with
\Phi(x)=\Psi(x)
is called the Uvarov transform
[
Uvarov
]
.
Christoffel, Geronimus, and Uvarov transforms, as well as the closely connected topic of the Darboux transformation, are important in pure and applied mathematics. An inexhaustive list of papers on these topics is

For the case of multiple orthogonality, results in this direction include:
[
ADMVA

for one-step Geronimus and Christoffel transforms of type II polynomials;

appeared addressing the general Geronimus transformation for multiple orthogonal systems. Their method of proof and formulas differ substantially from ours, and their approach is restricted to step-line indices and without considering combined Christoffel and Geronimus perturbations.
Their results treat the more general
mixed
multiple orthogonality setting, and their perturbations have the form of a polynomial matrix multiplication/division. This allows them to treat linear combinations of
\widetilde{\mu}_{j}
in (

The organization of the paper is as follows. We remind the reader of the basics of orthogonal and multiple orthogonal polynomials with respect to linear functionals in Sections
2.1
–
2.2
. In Section
3.1
we prove a generalization of Uvarov’s formula for type I polynomials, and in Section
3.2
we do the same for type II polynomials. Some understanding of the duality between the type I and type II setting reveals the proofs to be parallel. The proof of the type I formula requires
\Psi_{1}=\cdots=\Psi_{r}
, while the proof of the type II formula requires
\Phi_{1}=\cdots=\Phi_{r}
. In Section
3.3
we then describe how these formulas can be generalized to the case of any rational transformation.

Through Uvarov’s formula (
8
),
\Phi\widetilde{P}_{n}
is expressed as a linear combination of
\left\{P_{n+N-j}\right\}_{j=0}^{N+M}
. When we generalize it to multiple orthogonal polynomials, there are several possible sequences of multi-indices we can consider. In Section
2.3
we define the most natural sequences (paths and frames), as well as a more general class of sequences containing the most natural ones, for which the determinantal formula holds. We remark that for type I polynomials we consider sequences moving upwards (see Definition
2.16
), and for type II polynomials we consider sequences moving downwards (see Definition
2.20
).

In Section
4
we specialize our general formula to a number of natural special cases that might be of interest. Here the duality of the type I and type II setting is further illustrated through the comparison of the formulas for Christoffel transforms and Geronimus transforms,
as well as the comparison of the systems
(\Phi\mu_{1},\Phi\mu_{2}\dots,\Phi\mu_{r})
and
(\Phi\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\dots,\mu_{r})
. We also note that Uvarov perturbations (adding point masses) arise as special cases of our formulas (the case
\Phi=\Psi
). The reader may find the generality of Section
3
easier to parse by reading it alongside Section
4
to compare.

Another interesting observation is that our determinantal formulas for the systems of the form
(\Phi\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\dots,\mu_{r})
requires a generalization of (
8
), rather than just a generalization of (
4
). This is because our type II formula require
\Phi_{1}=\dots=\Phi_{r}
, so we view the system
(\Phi\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\dots,\mu_{r})
as
(\frac{\Phi}{1}\mu_{1},\frac{\Phi}{\Phi}\mu_{2}\dots,\frac{\Phi}{\Phi}\mu_{r})
, and then generalizations of the
Q
’s of (
9
) enter into the determinantal formula (this is Section
4.1.4
). This somewhat surprising observation provided a motivation for studying the more general rational perturbations rather than just Christoffel perturbations. Note however that for the type I formula we can view
(\Phi\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\dots,\mu_{r})
as
(\frac{\Phi}{1}\mu_{1},\frac{1}{1}\mu_{2}\dots,\frac{1}{1}\mu_{r})
, and indeed no
Q
’s are needed there (Section
4.1.3
).

Finally, in Section
5
as a case study, we apply Theorems
3.1
and
3.3
to a specific perturbation (
133
)–(
134
) of the multiple Laguerre polynomials of the second kind.

It should be noted that the determinantal formulas for the one-step Christoffel transform in our paper
[
ctpaper
]
naturally lead to interlacing results between zeros of the multiple orthogonal polynomials for the initial and for the transformed systems. In particular this results in interlacing for the polynomials from the same classical multiple orthogonal sytems but with different parameters. It is straightforward to obtain analogous interlacing results from the determinantal formulas for the one-step Christoffel and one-step Geronimus transforms treated in this paper. This will be addressed in a forthcoming publication.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Rational Perturbations of Moment Functionals

The transformed orthogonality measure in (
2
) is not necessarily a positive measure. To deal with this issue we view the measure
\mu
as a linear functional

(12)

\begin{split}\mu[P(x)]=\int P(x)d\mu(x).\end{split}

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(\nu_{j})_{j=0}^{\infty}
be a sequence of complex numbers, which we refer to as the moments. Let
\mu
be the linear functional on the space of polynomials with complex coefficients defined by
\mu[x^{j}]=\nu_{j}
and extended by linearity.
The orthogonal polynomials
(P_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
with respect

\mu
are defined by the orthogonality condition

(13)

\begin{split}\mu\left[P_{n}(x)x^{p}\right]=0,\qquad p=0,\dots,n-1,\end{split}

and the degree condition

(14)

\begin{split}\deg P_{n}\leq n.\end{split}

For positive definite
\mu
this corresponds to orthogonality with respect to infinitely supported measures. If
\mu
is not given by a positive measure we may have non-zero solutions to (
13
)-(
14
) with
\deg{P_{n}}<n
, and the solution may not be unique up to multiplication by a constant. These cases need to be excluded, so we use the following definition.

Definition 2.2
.

We say that
n\in\mathbb{N}
is normal for
\mu
if
\deg P_{n}=n
for every non-zero solution of (
13
)-(
14
) at
n
. We say that
\mu
is quasi-definite if every
n\in\mathbb{N}
is normal.

It is easy to see that normality of
n
is equivalent to the existence and uniqueness of a degree
n
monic solution to (
13
)-(
14
). If we look for non-zero solutions of degree
<n
, i.e., of the form
P_{n}(x)=\kappa_{n-1}x^{n-1}+\cdots+\kappa_{0}
, then (
13
) turns into a linear system of equations for the coefficients
\kappa_{n-1},\dots,\kappa_{0}
. The coefficient matrix of this system is the Hankel matrix given by

(15)

{H_{n}=\begin{pmatrix}\nu_{0}&\nu_{1}&\cdots&\nu_{n-1}\\
\nu_{1}&\nu_{2}&\cdots&\nu_{n}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\nu_{n-1}&\nu_{n}&\cdots&\nu_{2n-2}\\
\end{pmatrix}.}

Hence
n
is normal for
\mu
if and only if
\det{H_{n}}\neq 0
(except in the trivial case
n=0
, where we always take
n
to be normal). For more about orthogonal polynomials with respect to quasi-definite functionals, see
[
Chihara
]
.

Definition 2.3
.

A Christoffel transform of the functional
\mu
is a functional
\widehat{\mu}
given by

(16)

\begin{split}\widehat{\mu}[P(x)]=\mu[\Phi(x)P(x)].\end{split}

for some polynomial
\Phi(x)=\prod_{k=1}^{N}(x-z_{k})
. If
\deg{\Phi}=1
then we say that
\widehat{\mu}
is a one-step Christoffel transform.

We employ the notation
\widehat{\mu}=\Phi\mu
. As long as
\mu
and
\widehat{\mu}
are quasi-definite we still have (
4
) and (
6
). If
\Phi(x)=x-z_{1}
and
\mu
is quasi-definite then it is well known that
\widehat{\mu}
is quasi-definite if and only if
P_{n}(z_{1})\neq 0
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
. More generally, for any
\Phi
with simple zeros, if
\mu
is quasi-definite then
\Phi\mu
is quasi-definite if and only if
D_{n}\neq 0
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
where
D_{n}
is given by (
5
), see, e.g.,
[
ctpaper
]
.

Definition 2.4
.

A Geronimus transform of the functional
\mu
is a functional
\widecheck{\mu}
satisfying

(17)

\begin{split}\widecheck{\mu}[\Psi(x)P(x)]=\mu[P(x)]\end{split}

for a polynomial
\Psi(x)=\prod_{k=1}^{M}(x-w_{k})
.

Note that this only uniquely determines
\widecheck{\mu}
on a subspace of codimension
M
(the subspace of polynomials divisible by
\Psi
). Indeed, the first
M
moments of
\widecheck{\mu}
can be chosen arbitrarily, and together with (
17
) the other moments are then uniquely determined. In other words, if we express the polynomial
P
as

(18)

P(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{M-1}\kappa_{j}x^{j}+\sum_{j=n}^{\infty}\kappa_{j}\Psi(x)x^{j},

then
\widecheck{\mu}
has to be given by

(19)

\widecheck{\mu}[P(x)]=\sum_{j=0}^{M-1}\kappa_{j}\widecheck{\mu}[x^{j}]+\sum_{j=n}^{\infty}\kappa_{j}\mu[x^{j}],

but the choice of
\widecheck{\mu}[x^{j}]
for
j=0,\dots,M-1
is arbitrary.

For any choice of Geronimus transform
\widecheck{\mu}
, adding any complex measure supported on
w_{1},\dots,w_{M}
to
\widecheck{\mu}
produces a new Geronimus transform satisfying (
17
). If some zero
w_{j}
has higher multiplicity we may also add derivatives of
\delta_{w_{j}}
. Note that for measures we had a canonical choice for the Geronimus transform with no additional point masses. For functionals there does not seem to be such a choice. In the one-step case one option would be

(20)

\begin{split}\widecheck{\mu}[P(x)]=\mu\left[\frac{P(x)-P(z_{1})}{x-z_{1}}\right],\end{split}

but we avoid any specific choice of Geronimus transform in this paper.

Definition 2.5
.

We refer to a rational perturbation of the functional
\mu
as any functional
\widetilde{\mu}
satisfying
\Psi\widetilde{\mu}=\Phi\mu
for some non-zero polynomials
\Phi
and
\Psi
.

If
\Psi=1
we get Christoffel transforms and if
\Phi=1
we get Geronimus transforms. This setting extends the general rational perturbations of measures (
11
)
to linear functionals.
Uvarov’s formula (
8
) can be extended to this general setting as follows.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
\mu
be any quasi-definite functional, and
\widetilde{\mu}
be any functional satisfying
\Psi\widetilde{\mu}=\Phi\mu
for polynomials
\Phi
and
\Psi
with simple zeros. Choose any Geronimus transform
\widecheck{\mu}
of
\widetilde{\mu}
corresponding to
\Phi
, that is,
\Phi\widecheck{\mu}=\widetilde{\mu}
. Finally, let

(21)

\begin{split}Q_{n}(z)=\widecheck{\mu}\left[P_{n}(x)\frac{\Psi(x)}{x-z}\right],\qquad z=w_{1},\dots,w_{M}.\end{split}

Then
n\geq M
is normal for
\widetilde{\mu}
if and only if
D_{n}
in (
10
) is non-zero, and then (
8
) holds with this choice of
Q_{n}
.

Remark 2.7
.

Note that
\frac{\Psi(x)}{x-w_{k}}
is a polynomial, so that (
21
) is well-defined for any choice of functional
\widecheck{\mu}
. Also note that the functional
\widecheck{\mu}
does not have to be quasi-definite.

Remark 2.8
.

The case when
n<M
can be handled by modifying the initial entries in the determinants (
8
) and (
10
), similarly to Uvarov’s
[
Uvarov
]
. We work this out in the multiple orthogonality case in Sections
3.1
-
3.2
. The case when zeros of
\Phi
and/or
\Psi
are not simple can be handled in the standard way, just as in the usual Christoffel/Uvarov formulas. See Section
3.3
for the details.

Proof.

This is a special case
r=1
of the more general Theorem
3.3
that we prove in Section
3.2
.
∎

We stress that in the simplest case when:

(22)

\begin{cases}(a)\mbox{ }\mu\mbox{ is a positive measure};\par\\
(b)\mbox{ }\{w_{k}\}_{k=1}^{M}\cap\operatorname{supp}\mu=\varnothing;\\
(c)\mbox{ }\widetilde{\mu}\{w_{k}\}=0\mbox{ for all }k=1,\ldots,M,\end{cases}

then
d\widecheck{\mu}(x)=\frac{1}{\Psi(x)}d\mu(x)
and therefore

(23)

Q_{n}(w_{k})=\int\frac{P_{n}(x)}{x-w_{k}}d\mu(x)

is reduced to the function of the second kind evaluated at
w_{k}
. This is exactly the setting of the celebrated result of Uvarov
[
Uvarov
]
.

If any of the conditions (a)–(c) fail, the expression for
Q_{n}(w_{k})
is no longer this simple, and its explicit form depends on the particular setting under consideration. In this case,
\widecheck{\mu}
, and hence
Q_{n}
, can be determined via a straightforward calculation, as illustrated in Corollaries
2.9
and
2.10
(with further examples in Sections
4
and
5
). For instance, if (a) and (b) hold but (c) fails, then
Q_{n}
can take either the form (
24
) in the case when
\Phi
and
\Psi
share no zeros, or (
27
) if
\Phi=\Psi
. Note that while both expressions involve functions of the second kind, the additional discrete terms vary greatly. In our opinion, it is remarkable that the expression (
21
) is able to accommodate all the possible cases simultaneously, and as a consequence, Theorem
2.6
(combined with Remark
2.8
) works for
any
rational perturbation of
any
quasi-definite linear functional.

Corollary 2.9
.

Assume
\Phi
and
\Psi
have non-overlapping simple zeros
\{z_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
and
\{w_{j}\}_{j=1}^{M}
, respectively. Suppose
\Phi/\Psi
is positive on
\operatorname{supp}(\mu)
. Let
\widetilde{\mu}
be the measure satisfying

(24)

\begin{split}\int f(x)d\widetilde{\mu}(x)=\int f(x)\frac{\Phi(x)}{\Psi(x)}d\mu(x)+\sum_{k=1}^{M}c_{k}f({w_{k}})\end{split}

with
c_{k}>0
. Then (
8
) holds with

(25)

\begin{split}Q_{n}(w_{k})=\int\frac{P_{n}(x)}{x-w_{k}}d\mu(x)+\frac{c_{k}\Psi^{\prime}(w_{k})}{\Phi(w_{k})}P_{n}(w_{k}),\qquad k=1,\dots,M.\end{split}

Proof.

Define

\begin{split}\int f(x)d\widecheck{\mu}(x)=\int f(x)\frac{1}{\Psi(x)}d\mu(x)+\sum_{k=1}^{M}\frac{c_{k}}{\Phi(w_{k})}f(w_{k}).\end{split}

Then (
21
) can be computed as

Q_{n}(w_{k})=\int f(x)\frac{P_{n}(x)}{x-w_{k}}d\mu(x)+\sum_{k=1}^{M}\frac{c_{k}}{\Phi(w_{k})}\left[P_{n}(x)\frac{\Psi(x)}{x-w_{k}}\right]_{x=w_{k}},

which becomes (
25
).
∎

Corollary 2.10
.

Assume
\{w_{k}\}_{k=1}^{M}\subset\mathbb{R}\setminus\operatorname{supp}(\mu)
are distinct and let

(26)

\begin{split}\int f(x)d\widetilde{\mu}(x)=\int f(x)d\mu(x)+\sum_{k=1}^{M}c_{k}f({w_{k}}),\end{split}

with
c_{k}>0
,
1\leq k\leq N
.
Then (
8
) holds with
w_{j}=z_{j}
,
1\leq k\leq M
, and

(27)

\begin{split}Q_{n}(w_{k})=\int\frac{P_{n}(x)}{x-w_{k}}d\mu(x)+c_{k}P_{n}^{\prime}(w_{k}),\qquad k=1,\dots,M.\end{split}

Remark 2.11
.

The result of Corollary
2.9
appears in the work of Zhedanov
[
Zhe97
]
. In
[
Uvarov
, Sect 2]
Uvarov obtained another formula for
\widetilde{P}_{n}
for the transformation (
26
) in terms of kernel polynomials.

2.2.
Multiple Orthogonal Polynomials of Type I and Type II

Let
r
be a positive integer and consider a system of linear functionals
\bm{\mu}=(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{r})
. We assume that each of
\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{r}
are quasi-definite throughout the rest of the paper. We remind the basics of multiple orthogonal polynomials. For more details, see
[
Aptekarev
,
Ismail
,
Nikishin
]
. We write
\bm{n}
for multi-indices
(n_{1},\dots,n_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
, and
\left|\bm{n}\right|=n_{1}+\cdots+n_{r}
. Here we use the notation
\mathbb{N}=\left\{0,1,2,\dots\right\}
.

Definition 2.12
.

A type I multiple orthogonal polynomial for the multi-index
\bm{n}
is a non-zero vector of polynomials
\bm{A}_{\bm{n}}=(A_{\bm{n}}^{(1)},\dots,A_{\bm{n}}^{(r)})
satisfying the orthogonality condition

(28)

\begin{split}\sum_{j=1}^{r}\mu_{j}[A_{\bm{n}}^{(j)}(x)x^{p}]=0,\qquad p=0,1,\dots,\left|\bm{n}\right|-2,\end{split}

and the degree conditions

(29)

\begin{split}&\deg{A_{\bm{n}}^{(1)}}\leq n_{1}-1,\\
&\qquad\qquad\vdots\\
&\deg{A_{\bm{n}}^{(r)}}\leq n_{r}-1.\end{split}

Note that
A_{\bm{n}}^{(j)}=0
is the only possible choice when
n_{j}=0
. In the special case
\bm{n}=\bm{0}
we allow
\bm{A}_{\bm{0}}=\bm{0}
to be a type I polynomial.

Definition 2.13
.

A type II multiple orthogonal polynomial for the multi-index
\bm{n}
is a non-zero polynomial
P_{\bm{n}}
satisfying the orthogonality conditions

(30)

\begin{split}&\mu_{1}[P_{\bm{n}}(x)x^{p}]=0,\qquad p=0,\dots,n_{1}-1,\\
&\qquad\qquad\vdots\\
&\mu_{r}[P_{\bm{n}}(x)x^{p}]=0,\qquad p=0,\dots,n_{r}-1,\end{split}

and the degree condition

(31)

\begin{split}\deg{P_{\bm{n}}}\leq\left|\bm{n}\right|.\end{split}

In both definitions we have less equations than coefficients to solve for, so there always exists non-zero type I and type II polynomials. The following result extends the notion of normality and quasi-definite functional to the multiple orthogonality setting (see
[
Ismail
]
and our discussion in
[
ctpaper
, Sect 2.6]
).

Lemma 2.14
.

For any multi-index
\bm{n}\neq\bm{0}
, the following statements are equivalent.

(i)

There is a unique type I polynomial
(A_{\bm{n}}^{(1)},\dots,A_{\bm{n}}^{(r)})
such that
\sum_{j=1}^{r}\mu_{j}[A_{\bm{n}}^{(j)}(x)x^{\left|\bm{n}\right|-1}]=1
.

(ii)

There is a unique type II polynomial
P_{\bm{n}}
with degree
\left|\bm{n}\right|
such that
P_{\bm{n}}
is monic.

(iii)

\sum_{j=1}^{r}\mu_{j}[A_{\bm{n},j}(x)x^{\left|\bm{n}\right|-1}]\neq 0
for every type I polynomial.

(iv)

\deg{P_{\bm{n}}}=\left|\bm{n}\right|
for every type II polynomial.

(v)

\deg{A_{\bm{n}+\bm{e}_{j}}^{(j)}}=n_{j}
for every type I polynomial (for the index
\bm{n}+\bm{e}_{j}
).

(vi)

If
n_{j}>0
, then
\mu_{j}[P_{\bm{n}-\bm{e}_{j}}(x)x^{n_{j}-1}]\neq 0
for every type II polynomial (for the index
\bm{n}-\bm{e}_{j}
).

(vii)

\det H_{\bm{n}}\neq 0
, where

(32)

H_{\bm{n}}=\begin{pmatrix}\nu_{0}^{(1)}&\nu_{1}^{(1)}&\cdots&\nu_{\left|\bm{n}\right|-1}^{(1)}\\
\nu_{1}^{(1)}&\nu_{2}^{(1)}&\cdots&\nu_{\left|\bm{n}\right|}^{(1)}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\nu_{n_{1}-1}^{(1)}&\nu_{n_{1}}^{(1)}&\cdots&\nu_{\left|\bm{n}\right|+n_{1}-2}^{(1)}\\
\hline\cr&&\vdots\\
\hline\cr\nu_{0}^{(r)}&\nu_{1}^{(r)}&\cdots&\nu_{\left|\bm{n}\right|-1}^{(r)}\\
\nu_{1}^{(r)}&\nu_{2}^{(r)}&\cdots&\nu_{\left|\bm{n}\right|}^{(r)}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\nu_{n_{r}-1}^{(r)}&\nu_{n_{r}}^{(r)}&\cdots&\nu_{\left|\bm{n}\right|+n_{r}-2}^{(r)}\\
\end{pmatrix},

and
\nu_{n}^{(j)}
are the moments
\nu_{n}^{(j)}=\mu_{j}[x^{n}]
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
j=1,\dots,r
.

Definition 2.15
.

We say that an index
\bm{n}\neq\bm{0}
is normal if any of the equivalent statements in Lemma
2.14
hold. For a normal index we refer to (i) and (ii) as the normalized polynomials. The index
\bm{n}=\bm{0}
is always taken to be normal. If every index
\bm{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
is normal we say that the system
\bm{\mu}
is perfect. Note that when
r=1
the perfect systems correspond to the quasi-definite moment functionals.

2.3.
Sequences of Linearly Independent Polynomials

We want to consider multiple orthogonal polynomials along various sequences of multi-indices in
\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{r}
. For our proofs in the next sections to work out we need linear independence of the polynomials along our chosen sequences. The following definition lists the most important sequences.

Definition 2.16
.

Consider
\bm{n},\bm{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
with
\bm{n}\leq\bm{m}
, and let
d=\left|\bm{m}\right|-\left|\bm{n}\right|
.

(i)

A sequence of multi-indices
\left\{\bm{n}+\bm{s}_{j}\right\}_{j=0}^{d}
is called a path from
\bm{n}
to
\bm{m}
if for some
k_{1},\dots,k_{d}\in\left\{1,\dots,r\right\}
,

(33)

\begin{split}\bm{s}_{0}=\bm{0},\qquad\bm{s}_{j}=\bm{s}_{j-1}+\bm{e}_{k_{j}},\qquad\bm{n}+\bm{s}_{d}=\bm{m},\qquad j=1,\dots,d.\end{split}

(ii)

We say that
\left\{\bm{n}+\bm{s}_{j}\right\}_{j=0}^{d}
is an (increasing) frame from
\bm{n}
towards
\bm{m}
if it consists of
\{\bm{n}+j\bm{e}_{k}:0\leq j\leq m_{k}-n_{k},1\leq k\leq r\}

ordered in such a way that
\left|\bm{s}_{i}\right|\leq\left|\bm{s}_{j}\right|
if
i<j
,
j=1,\dots,d
.

(iii)

We say that
\left\{\bm{n}+\bm{s}_{j}\right\}_{j=0}^{d}
is an admissible sequence from
\bm{n}
towards
\bm{m}
if for some
k_{1},\dots,k_{d}\in\left\{1,\dots,r\right\}
,

(34)

\begin{split}\bm{s}_{0}=\bm{0},\qquad(\bm{s}_{i})_{k_{j}}<(\bm{s}_{j})_{k_{j}},\qquad i<j,\qquad\bm{n}\leq\bm{n}+\bm{s}_{j}\leq\bm{m},\qquad j=1,\dots,d.\end{split}

Remark 2.17
.

We stress that frames towards
\bm{m}
do not pass through
\bm{m}
. Admissible sequences towards
\bm{m}
may pass through
\bm{m}
or not. For examples, see Figure 1.

Remark 2.18
.

It is easy to verify that both paths from
\bm{n}
to
\bm{m}
and frames from
\bm{n}
towards
\bm{m}
are admissible sequences from
\bm{n}
towards
\bm{m}
.
The main purpose of introducing (
34
) is that it offers minimal restrictions on the sequences of multi-indices that suffice for our proofs. In particular, (
34
) is the property that we use to prove linear independence for type I polynomials. If a reader is not interested in generality, they can replace any admissible sequence with their favorite path, or a frame.

Lemma 2.19
.

Assume
\bm{\mu}
is a perfect system and
\bm{n},\bm{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
. If
\bm{n}\leq\bm{m}
and
\left\{\bm{n}+\bm{s}_{j}\right\}_{j=0}^{d}
is an admissible sequence from
\bm{n}
towards
\bm{m}
, then the set of type I vectors
\left\{\bm{A}_{\bm{n}+\bm{s}_{j}}\right\}_{j=0}^{d}
is linearly independent.

Proof.

Consider the dependence equation

\begin{split}\sum_{j=0}^{d}c_{j}\bm{A}_{\bm{n}+\bm{s}_{j}}=\bm{0},\end{split}

and suppose
c_{j}=0
for
j>l
. By (
34
) and Lemma
2.14
(v) we have

\begin{split}\deg{A_{\bm{n}+\bm{s}_{j}}^{(k_{l})}}<\deg{A_{\bm{n}+\bm{s}_{l}}^{(k_{l})}},\qquad j<l,\end{split}

Hence
c_{l}=0
, so we must have
c_{0}=\dots=c_{d}=0
, and linear independence follows.
∎

For linear independence of type II polynomials, we need admissible sequences that go backwards.

Definition 2.20
.

Consider
\bm{n},\bm{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
with
\bm{n}\geq\bm{m}
, and let
d=\left|\bm{n}\right|-\left|\bm{m}\right|
.

(i)

A sequence of multi-indices
\left\{\bm{n}-\bm{s}_{j}\right\}_{j=0}^{d}
is called a path from
\bm{n}
to
\bm{m}
if for some
k_{1},\dots,k_{d}\in\left\{1,\dots,r\right\}
,

(35)

\begin{split}\bm{s}_{0}=\bm{0},\qquad\bm{s}_{j}=\bm{s}_{j-1}+\bm{e}_{k_{j}},\qquad\bm{n}-\bm{s}_{d}=\bm{m},\qquad j=1,\dots,d.\end{split}

(ii)

We say that
\left\{\bm{n}-\bm{s}_{j}\right\}_{j=0}^{d}
is a (decreasing) frame from
\bm{n}
towards
\bm{m}
if it consists of
\{\bm{n}-j\bm{e}_{k}:0\leq j\leq n_{k}-m_{k},1\leq k\leq r\}

ordered in such a way that
\left|\bm{s}_{i}\right|\leq\left|\bm{s}_{j}\right|
if
i<j
,
j=1,\dots,d
.

(iii)

We say that
\left\{\bm{n}-\bm{s}_{j}\right\}_{j=0}^{d}
is an admissible sequence from
\bm{n}
towards
\bm{m}
if for some
k_{1},\dots,k_{d}\in\left\{1,\dots,r\right\}
,

(36)

\begin{split}\bm{s}_{0}=\bm{0},\qquad(\bm{s}_{i})_{k_{j}}<(\bm{s}_{j})_{k_{j}},\qquad i<j,\qquad\bm{m}\leq\bm{n}-\bm{s}_{j}\leq\bm{n},\qquad j=1,\dots,d.\end{split}

Lemma 2.21
.

Assume
\bm{\mu}
is a perfect system and
\bm{n},\bm{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
. If
\bm{n}\geq\bm{m}
and
\left\{\bm{n}-\bm{s}_{j}\right\}_{j=0}^{d}
is an admissible sequence from
\bm{n}
to
\bm{m}
, then the set of type II polynomials
\left\{P_{\bm{n}-\bm{s}_{j}}\right\}_{j=0}^{d}
is linearly independent.

Proof.

Consider the dependence equation

\begin{split}\sum_{j=0}^{d}c_{j}P_{\bm{n}-\bm{s}_{j}}=0,\end{split}

with
c_{j}=0
for
j>l
. By (
36
) and Lemma
2.14
(vi) we have

\begin{split}\mu_{k_{l}}[P_{\bm{n}-\bm{s}_{l}}(x)x^{(\bm{n}-\bm{s}_{l})_{k_{l}}}]\neq 0,\qquad\mu_{k_{l}}[P_{\bm{n}-\bm{s}_{j}}(x)x^{(\bm{n}-\bm{s}_{l})_{k_{l}}}]=0,\qquad j<l.\end{split}

Hence we get

\begin{split}0=\mu_{k_{l}}\left[\sum_{j=0}^{d}c_{j}P_{\bm{n}-\bm{s}_{j}}(x)x^{(\bm{n}-\bm{s}_{l})_{k_{l}}}\right]=c_{l}\mu_{k_{l}}[P_{\bm{n}-\bm{s}_{l}}(x)x^{(\bm{n}-\bm{s}_{l})_{k_{l}}}],\end{split}

so we must have
c_{l}=0
, and linear independence follows.
∎

Remark 2.22
.

In
[
ctpaper
]
, we used sequences
\left\{\bm{n}+\bm{s}_{j}\right\}_{j=0}^{d}
with
\left|\bm{s}_{j}\right|=j
,
j=0,\dots,d
. This definition is more straightforward than (
34
) and (
36
), but it is worse for us since it does not contain frames, which we view as the most natural sequences to use for the results of this paper (but it does still contain paths). For these sequences, the linear independence of type I and type II polynomials holds as well, but through (iii) and (iv) of Lemma
2.14
rather than (v) and (vi). These sequences are also not always admissible.

Main Results

3.1.
Rational Perturbations of Type I polynomials

For a perfect system
\bm{\mu}=(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{r})
, we consider systems
\widetilde{\bm{\mu}}=(\widetilde{\mu}_{1},\dots,\widetilde{\mu}_{r})
with
\Psi\widetilde{\mu}_{j}=\Phi_{j}\mu_{j}
for polynomials
\Phi_{j}(x)=\prod_{k=1}^{N_{j}}(x-z_{j,k})
and
\Psi(x)=\prod_{k=1}^{M}(x-w_{k})
,
j=1,\dots,r
. For now we assume that
\Phi_{1},\dots,\Phi_{r}
and
\Psi
have only simple roots, but we allow the different polynomials to have common roots. We write
\bm{N}
for the index
(N_{1},\dots,N_{r})
.

For the determinantal formula we need to consider Geronimus transforms
\widecheck{\bm{\mu}}=(\widecheck{\mu}_{1},\dots,\widecheck{\mu}_{r})
of
\widetilde{\bm{\mu}}
, defined by
\Phi_{j}\widecheck{\mu}_{j}=\widetilde{\mu}_{j}
,
j=1,\dots,r
. We define numbers
B_{\bm{n}}(w_{1}),\dots,B_{\bm{n}}(w_{M})
, similarly to (
21
), by

(37)

\begin{split}B_{\bm{n}}(z)=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\widecheck{\mu}_{j}\left[A_{\bm{n}}^{(j)}(x)\frac{\Psi(x)}{x-z}\right],\qquad z=w_{1},\dots,w_{M},\qquad\bm{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}.\end{split}

We point out that in the simplest case when

(38)

\begin{cases}(a)\mbox{ }\mu_{j}\mbox{ is a positive measure};\\
(b)\mbox{ }\{w_{k}\}_{k=1}^{M}\cap\operatorname{supp}\mu_{j}=\varnothing;\\
(c)\mbox{ }\widetilde{\mu}_{j}\{w_{k}\}=0\mbox{ for all }k=1,\ldots,M,\end{cases}

hold true for every
j=1,\ldots,r
,
then
B_{\bm{n}}
reduces to

(39)

B_{\bm{n}}(z)=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\int\frac{A_{\bm{n}}^{(j)}(x)}{x-z}d\mu_{j}(x).

See also the discussion in the end of Section
2.1
, and Sections
4
,
5
for other examples.

To simplify the formulas below, we define the block matrices

(40)

\begin{split}A_{\bm{n}}^{(j)}(\bm{z}_{j})=\begin{pmatrix}A_{\bm{n}}^{(j)}(z_{j,1})\\
\vdots\\
A_{\bm{n}}^{(j)}(z_{j,N_{j}})\end{pmatrix},\qquad B_{\bm{n}}(\bm{w})=\begin{pmatrix}B_{\bm{n}}(w_{1})\\
\vdots\\
B_{\bm{n}}(w_{M})\end{pmatrix},\qquad j=1,\dots,r,\qquad\bm{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}.\end{split}

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume
\bm{\mu}
is perfect. Let
\bm{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
with
\left|\bm{n}\right|>M
. Consider an admissible sequence
\left\{\bm{m}+\bm{s}_{j}\right\}_{j=0}^{\left|\bm{N}\right|+M}
from
\bm{m}
towards
\bm{n}+\bm{N}
, for some
\bm{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
with
\left|\bm{m}\right|=\left|\bm{n}\right|-M
and
\bm{m}\leq\bm{n}+\bm{N}
(see Definition
2.16
and Remark
2.18
). Then the determinantal expression

(41)

\begin{split}\widetilde{A}_{\bm{n}}^{(j)}(x)=\Phi_{j}(x)^{-1}\det{\begin{pmatrix}A_{\bm{m}}^{(j)}(x)&A_{\bm{m}+\bm{s}_{1}}^{(j)}(x)&\cdots&A_{\bm{m}+\bm{s}_{\left|\bm{N}\right|+M}}^{(j)}(x)\\
A_{\bm{m}}^{(1)}(\bm{z}_{1})&A_{\bm{m}+\bm{s}_{1}}^{(1)}(\bm{z}_{1})&\cdots&A_{\bm{m}+\bm{s}_{\left|\bm{N}\right|+M}}^{(1)}(\bm{z}_{1})\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
A_{\bm{m}}^{(r)}(\bm{z}_{r})&A_{\bm{m}+\bm{s}_{1}}^{(r)}(\bm{z}_{r})&\cdots&A_{\bm{m}+\bm{s}_{\left|\bm{N}\right|+M}}^{(r)}(\bm{z}_{r})\\
B_{\bm{m}}(\bm{w})&B_{\bm{m}+\bm{s}_{1}}(\bm{w})&\cdots&B_{\bm{m}+\bm{s}_{\left|\bm{N}\right|+M}}(\bm{w})\end{pmatrix}},\qquad j=1\dots,r,\end{split}

defines a vector of polynomials
\bm{\widetilde{A}}_{\bm{n}}
that satisfies the type I orthogonality condition (
28
) and the type I degree conditions (
29
) for the index
\bm{n}
. The index
\bm{n}
is normal for
\widetilde{\bm{\mu}}
if and only if
D^{(I)}_{\bm{n}}\neq 0
, where

(42)

\begin{split}D^{(I)}_{\bm{n}}=\det{\begin{pmatrix}A_{\bm{m}+\bm{s}_{1}}^{(1)}(\bm{z}_{1})&\cdots&A_{\bm{m}+\bm{s}_{\left|\bm{N}\right|+M}}^{(1)}(\bm{z}_{1})\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
A_{\bm{m}+\bm{s}_{1}}^{(r)}(\bm{z}_{r})&\cdots&A_{\bm{m}+\bm{s}_{\left|\bm{N}\right|+M}}^{(r)}(\bm{z}_{r})\\
B_{\bm{m}+\bm{s}_{1}}(\bm{w})&\cdots&B_{\bm{m}+\bm{s}_{\left|\bm{N}\right|+M}}(\bm{w})\end{pmatrix}},\end{split}

and
\tfrac{1}{D^{(I)}_{\bm{n}}}\bm{\widetilde{A}}_{\bm{n}}
is the normalized type I polynomial with respect to the system
\widetilde{\bm{\mu}}
.

In the case
0<\left|\bm{n}\right|\leq M
the same results hold if we put
\bm{A}_{\bm{m}+\bm{s}_{j}}(x)=\bm{0}
and
B_{\bm{m}+\bm{s}_{j}}(z)=z^{M-\left|\bm{n}\right|-j}
for

, and let
\left\{\bm{m}+\bm{s}_{j}\right\}_{M-\left|\bm{n}\right|}^{\left|\bm{N}\right|+M}
be an admissible sequence from
\bm{0}
towards
\bm{n}+\bm{N}
. If
\bm{n}=\bm{0}
we have
\widetilde{\bm{A}}_{\bm{n}}=\bm{0}
.

Proof.

We first prove the result in the case
\left|\bm{n}\right|>M
.
Note that the right-hand side of (
41
) is a polynomial. Denote it by
\widetilde{A}_{\bm{n}}^{(j)}
, and let us show that these polynomials are indeed the type I multiple orthogonal polynomials for
\widetilde{\bm{\mu}}
. Since each element of the sequence
\left\{\bm{m}+\bm{s}_{j}\right\}_{j=0}^{\left|\bm{N}\right|+M}
is below
\bm{n}+\bm{N}
, we have
\deg{\widetilde{A}_{\bm{n}}^{(j)}}\leq(n_{j}+N_{j}-1)-N_{j}=n_{j}-1
for each
j=1,\dots,r
. Since the sequence is above
\bm{m}
, we have, by
|\bm{m}|=|\bm{n}|-M
,
\Psi\widetilde{\mu}_{j}=\Phi_{j}{\mu}_{j}
and (
41
),

\begin{split}\sum_{j=1}^{r}\widetilde{\mu}_{j}[\widetilde{A}_{\bm{n}}^{(j)}(x)\Psi(x)x^{p}]=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\mu_{j}[\Phi_{j}(x)\widetilde{A}_{\bm{n}}^{(j)}(x)x^{p}]=0,\qquad p=0,\dots,\left|\bm{n}\right|-M-2.\end{split}

If
p<M
, we apply the partial fraction decomposition to
x^{p}/\Psi(x)
to write

(43)

\begin{split}x^{p}=\sum_{k=1}^{M}\alpha_{p,k}\frac{\Psi(x)}{x-w_{k}}.\end{split}

From this we get

\begin{split}\sum_{j=1}^{r}\widetilde{\mu}_{j}[\widetilde{A}_{\bm{n}}^{(j)}(x)x^{p}]=\sum_{k=1}^{M}\alpha_{p,k}\sum_{j=1}^{r}\widetilde{\mu}_{j}\left[\widetilde{A}_{\bm{n}}^{(j)}(x)\frac{\Psi(x)}{x-w_{k}}\right]=\sum_{k=1}^{M}\alpha_{p,k}\sum_{j=1}^{r}\widecheck{\mu}_{j}\left[\Phi_{j}(x)\widetilde{A}_{\bm{n}}^{(j)}(x)\frac{\Psi(x)}{x-w_{k}}\right]=0,\end{split}

since we get the determinant in (
41
) but with the first row replaced by a linear combination of the last
M
rows. Since every polynomial of degree
\leq\left|\bm{n}\right|-2
is in the span of
\left\{x^{p}\right\}_{p=0}^{M-1}\cup\left\{\Psi(x)x^{p}\right\}_{p=0}^{\left|\bm{n}\right|-M-2}
, we end up with

\begin{split}\sum_{j=1}^{r}\widetilde{\mu}_{j}[\widetilde{A}_{\bm{n}}^{(j)}(x)x^{p}]=0,\qquad p=0,\dots,\left|\bm{n}\right|-2.\end{split}

The sequence starts at
\bm{m}\neq\bm{0}
and increases, so we also have

\begin{split}\sum_{j=1}^{r}\widetilde{\mu}_{j}[\widetilde{A}_{\bm{n}}^{(j)}(x)x^{\left|\bm{n}\right|-1}]=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\widetilde{\mu}_{j}[\widetilde{A}_{\bm{n}}^{(j)}(x)\Psi(x)x^{\left|\bm{n}\right|-M-1}]=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\mu_{j}[\Phi_{j}(x)\widetilde{A}_{\bm{n}}^{(j)}(x)x^{\left|\bm{n}\right|-M-1}]=D^{(I)}_{\bm{n}}.\end{split}

It remains to prove that
D^{(I)}_{\bm{n}}\neq 0
if and only if
\bm{n}
is normal for
\widetilde{\bm{\mu}}
. We have
D^{(I)}_{\bm{n}}=0
if and only if there are constants
c_{1},\dots,c_{\left|\bm{N}\right|+M}
, not all zero, such that

(44)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\left|\bm{N}\right|+M}c_{i}A_{\bm{m}+\bm{s}_{i}}^{(j)}(x)=0,\qquad x=z_{j,1},\dots,z_{j,N_{j}},\qquad j=1,\dots,r,

(45)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\left|\bm{N}\right|+M}c_{i}B_{\bm{m}+\bm{s}_{i}}(x)=0,\qquad x=w_{1},\dots,w_{M}.

Now suppose
D^{(I)}_{\bm{n}}=0
and choose
c_{1},\dots,c_{N+\left|\bm{M}\right|}
as above. Consider the vector
\bm{A}=(A^{(1)},\dots,A^{(r)})
given by

(46)

\begin{split}A^{(j)}(x)=\Phi_{j}(x)^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{\left|\bm{N}\right|+M}c_{i}A_{\bm{m}+\bm{s}_{i}}^{(j)}(x),\qquad j=1,\dots,r.\end{split}

By (
44
), each
A^{(j)}
is a polynomial. Since our sequence of indices is admissible, Lemma
2.19
implies that
\bm{A}\not\equiv 0
. Since every index in the sum is below
\bm{n}+\bm{N}
we have
\deg{A}^{(j)}\leq n_{j}-1
for each
j=1,\dots,r
. Since every index is also strictly above
\bm{m}
we have, by similar arguments as before,

(47)

\begin{split}\sum_{j=1}^{r}\widetilde{\mu}_{j}[A^{(j)}(x)x^{p}]=0,\qquad p=0,\dots,\left|\bm{n}\right|-1.\end{split}

Hence
\bm{A}
is a type I polynomial for the index
\bm{n}
with respect to
\widetilde{\bm{\mu}}
, so
\bm{n}
cannot be normal for
\widetilde{\bm{\mu}}
.

Conversely, suppose
\bm{n}
is not normal for
\widetilde{\bm{\mu}}
. Then there is some vector
\bm{A}=(A^{(1)},\dots,A^{(r)})\not\equiv\bm{0}
with
\deg A^{(j)}\leq n_{j}-1
such that (
47
) holds. As we have seen, this implies

\sum_{j=1}^{r}\mu_{j}[\Phi_{j}(x)A^{(j)}(x)x^{p}]=0,\qquad p=0,\dots,\left|\bm{n}\right|-M-1.

Note that
\deg\Phi_{j}(x)A^{(j)}(x)\leq n_{j}+N_{j}-1
.
For vectors
\bm{C}=(C^{(1)},\dots,C^{(r)})
of polynomials
C^{(j)}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n_{j}+N_{j}-1}\kappa_{j,k}x^{k}
,
j=1,\dots,r
, consider the orthogonality relations

(48)

\sum_{j=1}^{r}\mu_{j}[C^{(j)}(x)x^{p}]=0,\qquad p=0,\dots,\left|\bm{n}\right|-M-1.

Since
\left|\bm{n}\right|-M=\left|\bm{m}\right|
, this is an
\left|\bm{m}\right|\times(\left|\bm{n}\right|+\left|\bm{N}\right|)
system of equations. The coefficient matrix of this system is given by
H_{\bm{m}}
with
\left|\bm{n}\right|+\left|\bm{N}\right|-\left|\bm{m}\right|=\left|\bm{N}\right|+M
columns added. Since
\bm{m}
is normal for
\bm{\mu}
the rank of this system is equal to
\left|\bm{m}\right|
, so the solution space has dimension
\left|\bm{N}\right|+M
. Note that each
\bm{A}_{\bm{m}+\bm{s}_{i}}
,
i=1,\dots,\left|\bm{N}\right|+M
, has degree
\leq n_{j}+N_{j}-1
and solves (
48
). These vectors are linearly independent, by Lemma
2.19
, so every solution is a linear combination of these vectors. The non-zero vector
(\Phi_{1}A^{(1)},\dots,\Phi_{r}A^{(r)})
is also a solution, so we get (
46
) for some
(c_{1},\dots,c_{\left|\bm{N}\right|+M})\neq(0,\dots,0)
. This implies (
44
) since
\Phi_{j}(z_{j,k})=0
. We also get (
45
), since

\sum_{i=1}^{\left|\bm{N}\right|+M}c_{i}B_{\bm{m}+\bm{s}_{i}}(w_{k})=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\widecheck{\mu}_{j}\left[\Phi_{j}(x)A^{(j)}(x)\frac{\Psi(x)}{x-w_{k}}\right]=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\widetilde{\mu}_{j}\left[A^{(j)}(x)\frac{\Psi(x)}{x-w_{k}}\right]=0,

by (
40
) and
M-1\leq\left|\bm{n}\right|-2
. Since (
44
)-(
45
) holds, we must have
D^{(I)}_{\bm{n}}=0
.

Now consider the case
\left|\bm{n}\right|\leq M
. We want to check the orthogonality conditions with respect to
\widetilde{\bm{\mu}}
against
x^{p}
with
p\leq\left|\bm{n}\right|-1
. Hence
p<M
, so we again consider the decomposition (
43
). We use the identity

(49)

\begin{split}\frac{x^{q}}{x-z}=x^{q-1}+zx^{q-2}+\cdots+z^{q-1}+\frac{z^{q}}{x-z},\end{split}

substituted into (
43
) multiplied with
x^{q}
to get

\begin{split}\frac{x^{p+q}}{\Psi(x)}=x^{q-1}\sum_{k=1}^{M}\alpha_{p,k}+x^{q-2}\sum_{k=1}^{M}\alpha_{p,k}w_{k}+\cdots+\sum_{k=1}^{M}\alpha_{p,k}w_{k}^{q-1}+\sum_{k=1}^{M}\alpha_{p,k}\frac{w_{k}^{q}}{x-w_{k}}.\end{split}

For
q=M-p
the polynomial part of the left hand side is equal to one. Comparing with the right hand side we obtain

(50)

\begin{split}\sum_{k=1}^{M}\alpha_{p,k}w_{k}^{q}=\begin{cases}0,\qquad q=0,\dots,M-p-2,\\
1,\qquad q=M-p-1.\end{cases}.\end{split}

Again, since
p<M
, we can write

\begin{split}\sum_{j=1}^{r}\widetilde{\mu}_{j}[\widetilde{A}_{\bm{n}}^{(j)}(x)x^{p}]=\sum_{k=1}^{M}\alpha_{p,k}\sum_{j=1}^{r}\widecheck{\mu}_{j}\left[\Phi_{j}(x)\widetilde{A}_{\bm{n}}^{(j)}(x)\frac{\Psi(x)}{x-w_{k}}\right]\end{split}

to get the determinant in (
41
) but with each of the last
\left|\bm{N}\right|+\left|\bm{n}\right|
elements in the first row replaced with a linear combination of each of the last
\left|\bm{N}\right|+\left|\bm{n}\right|
elements of the last
M
rows. Use this to get the last
\left|\bm{N}\right|+\left|\bm{n}\right|
elements in the first row of the determinant equal to
0
through elementary row operations. Then we get
-\sum_{k=1}^{M}\alpha_{p,k}w_{k}^{M-\left|n\right|-j}
in entry
j+1
of the first row for
j=0,\dots,M-\left|\bm{n}\right|
. For
p\leq\left|\bm{n}\right|-2
this is
0
by (
50
), since here
M-p-2\geq M-\left|\bm{n}\right|
, which means that all the entries in the first row of the determinant are now
0
. Hence we get

\begin{split}\sum_{j=1}^{r}\widetilde{\mu}_{j}[\widetilde{A}_{\bm{n}}^{(j)}(x)x^{p}]=0,\qquad p=0,\dots,\left|\bm{n}\right|-2.\end{split}

For
p=\left|\bm{n}\right|-1
the same argument shows that the first row is now equal to
\begin{pmatrix}1&0&\cdots&0\end{pmatrix}
. Now expand along the first row, and since the other rows remain unchanged from (
41
) we get

\begin{split}\sum_{j=1}^{r}\widetilde{\mu}_{j}[\widetilde{A}_{\bm{n}}^{(j)}(x)x^{p}]=D^{(I)}_{\bm{n}}.\end{split}

Similar arguments then show that
D^{(I)}_{\bm{n}}\neq 0
when
\bm{n}
is normal for
\widetilde{\bm{\mu}}
.
The main difference is in showing that
\bm{A}
in (
46
) is non-zero. If
\bm{A}=\bm{0}
, then by Lemma
2.19
we get
c_{i}=0
for
i>M-\left|\bm{n}\right|
.
Since the coefficient matrix of the system of equations (
45
) with respect to the unknowns
c_{1},\ldots,c_{M-\left|\bm{n}\right|}
is a Vandermonde-type
M\times(M-|\bm{n}|)
matrix, with at least as many rows as columns, we obtain
c_{1}=\cdots=c_{M-\left|\bm{n}\right|}=0
. Hence
\bm{A}\neq\bm{0}
in (
46
). The rest of the proof works out similarly.
∎

3.2.
Rational Perturbations of Type II polynomials

For the type II case, we consider systems
\widetilde{\bm{\mu}}=(\widetilde{\mu}_{1},\dots,\widetilde{\mu}_{r})
where
\Psi_{j}\widetilde{\mu}_{j}=\Phi\mu_{j}
for polynomials
\Phi(x)=\prod_{k=1}^{N}(x-z_{k})
and
\Psi_{j}(x)=\prod_{k=1}^{M_{j}}(x-w_{j,k})
,
j=1,\dots,r
. Again we assume these polynomials only have simple zeros, and let
\bm{M}=(M_{1},\dots,M_{r})
.

The Geronimus transforms
\widecheck{\bm{\mu}}=(\widecheck{\mu}_{1},\dots,\widecheck{\mu}_{r})
are now defined by
\Phi\widecheck{\bm{\mu}}=\widetilde{\bm{\mu}}
, and we define vectors of numbers
\bm{Q}_{\bm{n}}(z)=(Q_{\bm{n}}^{(1)}(z),\dots,Q_{\bm{n}}^{(r)}(z))
by

(51)

\begin{split}Q_{\bm{n}}^{(j)}(z)=\widecheck{\mu}_{j}\left[P_{\bm{n}}(x)\frac{\Psi_{j}(x)}{x-z}\right],\qquad z=w_{j,1},\dots,w_{j,M_{j}},\qquad j=1,\dots,r,\qquad\bm{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}.\end{split}

We point out that in the simplest case when, for some
j=1,\ldots,r
,

(52)

\begin{cases}(a)\mbox{ }\mu_{j}\mbox{ is a positive measure};\\
(b)\mbox{ }\{w_{j,k}\}_{k=1}^{M_{j}}\cap\operatorname{supp}\mu_{j}=\varnothing;\\
(c)\mbox{ }\widetilde{\mu}_{j}\{w_{j,k}\}=0\mbox{ for all }k=1,\ldots,M_{j},\end{cases}

then
Q^{(j)}_{\bm{n}}
reduces to

(53)

Q^{(j)}_{\bm{n}}(z)=\int\frac{P_{\bm{n}}(x)}{x-z}d\mu_{j}(x).

See also the discussion in the end of Section
2.1
and Sections
4
,
5
for other examples.

We will make use of the block matrices

(54)

\begin{split}P_{\bm{n}}(\bm{z})=\begin{pmatrix}P_{\bm{n}}(z_{1})\\
\vdots\\
P_{\bm{n}}(z_{N})\end{pmatrix},\qquad Q_{\bm{n}}^{(j)}(\bm{w}_{j})=\begin{pmatrix}Q_{\bm{n}}^{(j)}(w_{j,1})\\
\vdots\\
Q_{\bm{n}}^{(j)}(w_{j,M_{j}})\end{pmatrix},\qquad j=1,\dots,r,\qquad\bm{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}.\end{split}

Theorem 3.3
.

Assume
\bm{\mu}
is perfect. Let
\bm{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
with
\bm{n}\geq\bm{M}
. Consider an admissible sequence
\left\{\bm{m}-\bm{s}_{j}\right\}_{j=0}^{N+\left|\bm{M}\right|}

\bm{m}

\bm{n}-\bm{M}

\bm{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
with
\left|\bm{m}\right|=\left|\bm{n}\right|+N
and
\bm{m}\geq\bm{n}-\bm{M}
(see Section
2.3
). Then the polynomial

(55)

\begin{split}\widetilde{P}_{\bm{n}}(x)=\Phi(x)^{-1}\det{\begin{pmatrix}P_{\bm{m}}(x)&P_{\bm{m}-\bm{s}_{1}}(x)&\cdots&P_{\bm{m}-\bm{s}_{N+\left|\bm{M}\right|}}(x)\\
P_{\bm{m}}(\bm{z})&P_{\bm{m}-\bm{s}_{1}}(\bm{z})&\cdots&P_{\bm{m}-\bm{s}_{N+\left|\bm{M}\right|}}(\bm{z})\\

\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\

\end{pmatrix}}\end{split}

satisfies the type II orthogonality conditions (
30
) and the type II degree condition (
31
) for the index
\bm{n}
. The index
\bm{n}
is normal for
\widetilde{\bm{\mu}}
if and only if

, where

\begin{split}D^{(II)}_{\bm{n}}=\det{\begin{pmatrix}P_{\bm{m}-\bm{s}_{1}}(\bm{z})&\cdots&P_{\bm{m}-\bm{s}_{N+\left|\bm{M}\right|}}(\bm{z})\\

\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
Q_{\bm{m}-\bm{s}_{1}}^{(r)}(\bm{w}_{r})&\cdots&Q_{\bm{m}-\bm{s}_{N+\left|\bm{M}\right|}}^{(r)}(\bm{w}_{r})\\
\end{pmatrix}},\end{split}

and then
\tfrac{1}{D^{(II)}_{\bm{n}}}P_{\bm{n}}
is the (unique) normalized type II polynomial with respect to the system
\widetilde{\bm{\mu}}
.

In the case where
n_{j}<M_{j}
for some
j
, let
\left\{\bm{m}-\bm{s}_{j}\right\}_{j=0}^{N+\left|\bm{M}^{*}\right|}
be an admissible sequence from
\bm{m}
towards
\bm{n}-\bm{M}^{*}
, where

is defined by

if

, and
M_{j}^{*}=M_{j}
if

, for

. The same results hold if we put

for

, and

\begin{split}&\begin{pmatrix}Q_{\bm{m}-\bm{s}_{N+\left|\bm{M}^{*}\right|+1}}^{(1)}(\bm{w}_{1})&\cdots&Q_{\bm{m}-\bm{s}_{N+\left|\bm{M}\right|}}^{(1)}(\bm{w}_{1})\\

is empty and no block corresponding to

3.3.
The Case of Zeros of Higher Multiplicity

Some modifications of Theorem
3.1
and Theorem
3.3
can give us a formula for any (diagonal) rational perturbation of functionals, i.e.,
\widetilde{\bm{\mu}}=(\widetilde{\mu}_{1},\dots,\widetilde{\mu}_{r})
satisfying

(64)

\begin{split}\Psi_{j}\widetilde{\mu}_{j}=\Phi_{j}\mu_{j},\qquad j=1,\dots,r,\end{split}

for any polynomials
\Phi_{j}(x)=\prod_{k=1}^{N_{j}}(x-z_{j,k})
and
\Psi_{j}(x)=\prod_{k=1}^{M_{j}}(x-w_{j,k})
,
j=1,\dots,r
. For the case of measures this includes all perturbations
\widetilde{\mu}_{j}
of the type

(65)

\begin{split}\int f(x)d\widetilde{\mu}_{j}(x)=\int f(x)\frac{\Phi_{j}(x)}{\Psi_{j}(x)}d\mu_{j}(x)+\sum_{k=1}^{K_{j}}c_{j,k}f(u_{j,k}),\qquad j=1,\dots,r,\end{split}

since with
\Lambda_{j}(x)=\prod_{k=1}^{K_{j}}(x-u_{j,k})
we have

(66)

\begin{split}\Lambda_{j}\Psi_{j}\widetilde{\mu}_{j}=\Lambda_{j}\Phi_{j}\mu_{j},\qquad j=1,\dots,r.\end{split}

First let
\Psi=\lcm(\Psi_{1},\dots,\Psi_{r})
and
\Phi_{j}^{*}=\Phi_{j}\Psi/\Psi_{j}
,
j=1,\dots,r
, and note that we have

(67)

\begin{split}\Psi\widetilde{\mu}_{j}=\Phi_{j}^{*}\mu_{j},\qquad j=1,\dots,r.\end{split}

From here we can apply Theorem
3.1
, as long as all
\Phi_{1},\dots,\Phi_{r}
and
\Psi_{1},\dots,\Phi_{r}
have simple roots, and
\Phi_{i}
and
\Psi_{j}
are coprime for
i\neq j
. Similarly, we can apply Theorem
3.3
using

(68)

\begin{split}\Psi_{j}^{*}\widetilde{\mu}_{j}=\Phi\mu_{j},\qquad j=1,\dots,r,\end{split}

where
\Phi=\lcm(\Phi_{1},\dots,\Phi_{r})
and
\Psi_{j}^{*}=\Psi_{j}\Phi/\Phi_{j}
,
j=1,\dots,r
.

We now describe how to modify Theorem
3.1
to allow multiplicity of the roots of
\Phi_{1},\dots,\Phi_{r}
and
\Psi
. The same ideas can then be used to modify Theorem
3.3
to the case of higher multiplicities. Using (
67
) and (
68
) we would then get determinantal formulas for type I and type II polynomials, for any choice of
\Phi_{1},\dots,\Phi_{r}
and
\Psi_{1},\dots,\Psi_{r}
in (
64
).

Recall that the zeros of
\Phi_{j}
are
\{z_{j,k}\}_{k=1}^{N_{j}}
, repeated according to their multiplicities. Denote
l_{j,k}

to be the number of
i<k
such that
z_{j,i}=z_{j,k}
,
and modify (
40
) by

(69)

\begin{split}A_{\bm{n}}^{(j)}(\bm{z}_{j})=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{d^{l_{j,1}}}{dz^{l_{j,1}}}A_{\bm{n}}^{(j)}(z){\Big|}_{z=z_{j,1}}\\
\vdots\\
\frac{d^{l_{j,N_{j}}}}{dz^{l_{j,N_{j}}}}A_{\bm{n}}^{(j)}(z){\Big|}_{z=z_{j,N_{j}}}\end{pmatrix},\qquad j=1,\dots,r,\qquad\bm{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}.\end{split}

This ensures that the determinant in (
41
) remains divisible by
\Phi_{j}
. From there the rest of the proof works out without any changes (and all of this includes the special case
\left|\bm{n}\right|\leq M
).

Similarly, write
l_{k}
for the number of
i<k
such that
w_{i}=w_{k}
. Here we modify (
37
) by

(70)

\begin{split}B_{\bm{n}}(w_{k})=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\widecheck{\mu}_{j}\left[A_{\bm{n}}^{(j)}(x)\frac{\partial^{l_{k}}}{\partial z^{l_{k}}}\frac{\Psi(x)}{x-z}\right]{\Bigg|}_{z=w_{k}}=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\widecheck{\mu}_{j}\left[A_{\bm{n}}^{(j)}(x)\frac{l_{k}!\Psi(x)}{(x-w_{k})^{l_{k}+1}}\right],\qquad k=1,\dots,N,\\
\bm{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}.\end{split}

When
\bm{m}+\bm{s}_{i}\notin\mathbb{N}^{r}\setminus\left\{\bm{0}\right\}
in Theorem
3.1
, we put
B_{\bm{m}+\bm{s}_{i}}(w_{k})=\frac{d^{l_{k}}}{dz^{l_{k}}}z^{M-\left|\bm{n}\right|-i}|_{z=w_{k}}
. For the proof, the orthogonality with respect to
\Psi(x)x^{p}
remains the same, but for the orthogonality with respect to
x^{p}
for
p<M
, we need to replace the partial fraction decomposition (
43
) with

(71)

\begin{split}x^{p}=\sum_{k=1}^{M}\alpha_{p,k}\frac{l_{k}!\Psi(x)}{(x-w_{k})^{l_{k}+1}}.\end{split}

By differentiating (
49
) in
z
we obtain

(72)

\begin{split}\frac{l!x^{q}}{(x-z)^{l+1}}=x^{q-1}\frac{d^{l}}{dz^{l}}1+x^{q-2}\frac{d^{l}}{dz^{l}}z+\dots+\frac{d^{l}}{dz^{l}}z^{q-1}+o(1)\qquad(x\rightarrow\infty).\end{split}

Substitute this into (
71
) multiplied with
x^{q}
to get

(73)

\begin{split}\sum_{k=1}^{M}\alpha_{p,k}\frac{d^{l_{k}}}{dz^{l_{k}}}z^{q}{\Big|}_{w_{k}}=\begin{cases}0,\qquad q=0,\dots,M-p-2,\\
1,\qquad q=M-p-1.\end{cases}\end{split}

From here the rest of the proof of Theorem
3.1
works out the same way as before.

4.
Special cases

4.1.
Christoffel Transforms

From now on we assume all systems we consider are perfect. We ignore the normalizing constants in most of our determinantal formulas, but since we assume perfectness, Theorem
3.1
and Theorem
3.3
ensure that our determinants are multiple orthogonal polynomials.

The most general Christoffel transforms of the system
\bm{\mu}=(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{r})
that we consider in this paper will be of the form

(74)

\begin{split}\widehat{\bm{\mu}}=(\widehat{\mu}_{1},\dots,\widehat{\mu}_{r})=(\Phi_{1}\mu_{1},\dots,\Phi_{r}\mu_{r}),\end{split}

for any choice of polynomials
\Phi_{j}(x)=\prod_{k=1}^{N_{j}}(x-z_{j,k})
,
j=1,\dots,r
. However, we will assume
\Phi_{1},\dots,\Phi_{r}
all have only simple zeros, to avoid having to add rows of derivatives as described in Section
3.3
. We write
\widehat{\bm{A}}_{\bm{n}}=(\widehat{A}_{\bm{n}}^{(1)},\dots,\widehat{A}_{\bm{n}}^{(r)})
for the type I polynomials with respect to
\widehat{\bm{\mu}}
,
\bm{A}_{\bm{n}}=(A_{\bm{n}}^{(1)},\dots,A_{\bm{n}}^{(r)})
for the type I polynomials with respect to
\bm{\mu}
,
\widehat{P}_{\bm{n}}
for the type II polynomials with respect to
\widehat{\bm{\mu}}
, and
P_{\bm{n}}
for the type II polynomials with respect to
\bm{\mu}
. We also write
\bm{N}
for the index
(N_{1},\dots,N_{r})
.

4.1.1.
Systems
\widehat{\bm{\mu}}=(\Phi_{1}\mu_{1},\Phi_{2}\mu_{2},\dots,\Phi_{r}\mu_{r})
, type I polynomials

For type I polynomials, Theorem
3.1
applies immediately with
\Psi_{1}=\cdots=\Psi_{r}=1
, reducing (
41
) to

(75)

\begin{split}\widehat{A}_{\bm{n}}^{(j)}(x)=\Phi_{j}(x)^{-1}\det{\begin{pmatrix}A_{\bm{n}}^{(j)}(x)&A_{\bm{n}+\bm{s}_{1}}^{(j)}(x)&\cdots&A_{\bm{n}+\bm{s}_{\left|\bm{N}\right|}}^{(j)}(x)\\
A_{\bm{n}}^{(1)}(\bm{z}_{1})&A_{\bm{n}+\bm{s}_{1}}^{(1)}(\bm{z}_{1})&\cdots&A_{\bm{n}+\bm{s}_{\left|\bm{N}\right|}}^{(1)}(\bm{z}_{1})\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
A_{\bm{n}}^{(r)}(\bm{z}_{r})&A_{\bm{n}+\bm{s}_{1}}^{(r)}(\bm{z}_{r})&\cdots&A_{\bm{n}+\bm{s}_{\left|\bm{N}\right|}}^{(r)}(\bm{z}_{r})\\
\end{pmatrix}},\qquad j=1\dots,r.\end{split}

Here we chose
\bm{m}=\bm{n}
, but note that Theorem
3.1
allows for any
\bm{m}\leq\bm{n}+\bm{N}
with
\left|\bm{m}\right|=\left|\bm{n}\right|
. For the sequence
\left\{\bm{n}+\bm{s}_{j}\right\}_{j=0}^{\left|\bm{N}\right|}
, the natural choice may be a path from
\bm{n}
to
\bm{n}+\bm{N}
, or perhaps
the increasing frame from
\bm{n}
towards
\bm{n}+\bm{N}
(see Definition
2.16
).

4.1.2.
Systems
\widehat{\bm{\mu}}=(\Phi\mu_{1},\Phi\mu_{2},\dots,\Phi\mu_{r})
, type II polynomials

For type II polynomials, Theorem
3.3
does not apply immediately in the general case, since it requires
\Phi_{1}=\cdots=\Phi_{r}
. We can still use the trick (
68
) to get a determinantal formula, and since
\Psi_{1}=\cdots=\Psi_{r}=1
we do not have to deal with any zeros of higher multiplicity.

If
\Phi_{1},\dots,\Phi_{r}
are coprime we use
\Phi(x)=\Phi_{1}(x)\cdots\Phi_{r}(x)
(of degree
|\bm{N}|
) and
\Psi_{j}(x)=\Phi(x)/\Phi_{j}(x)
(of degree
|\bm{N}|-N_{j}
) in Theorem
3.3
. The determinant (
55
) is then of size
(r\left|\bm{N}\right|+1)\times(r\left|\bm{N}\right|+1)
. Note that this is much larger than the
(\left|\bm{N}\right|+1)\times(\left|\bm{N}\right|+1)
determinant in (
75
).

The more zeros
\Phi_{1},\dots,\Phi_{r}
have in common the smaller the determinant in the type II formula will be. In particular the simplest case here is
\Phi_{1}(x)=\cdots=\Phi_{r}(x)=\Phi(x)=\prod_{k=1}^{N}(x-z_{k})
. This was the setting we considered in
[
ctpaper
]
. Here Theorem
3.3
even applies directly, reducing (
55
) to

(76)

\begin{split}\widehat{P}_{\bm{n}}(x)=\Phi(x)^{-1}\det{\begin{pmatrix}P_{\bm{n}+N\bm{e}_{1}}(x)&P_{\bm{n}+(N-1)\bm{e}_{1}}(x)&\cdots&P_{\bm{n}}(x)\\
P_{\bm{n}+N\bm{e}_{1}}(z_{1})&P_{\bm{n}+(N-1)\bm{e}_{1}}(z_{1})&\cdots&P_{\bm{n}}(z_{1})\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
P_{\bm{n}+N\bm{e}_{1}}(z_{N})&P_{\bm{n}+(N-1)\bm{e}_{1}}(z_{N})&\cdots&P_{\bm{n}}(z_{N})\end{pmatrix}}.\end{split}

Note the similarity with (
4
). We chose the sequence to be the straight path from
\bm{m}=\bm{n}+N\bm{e}_{1}
to
\bm{n}
. Of course a straight path from
\bm{m}=\bm{n}+N\bm{e}_{j}
to
\bm{n}
works for any
j
, or more generally, any path from
\bm{m}\geq\bm{n}
to
\bm{n}
with
\left|\bm{m}\right|=\left|\bm{n}\right|+N
.

4.1.3.
Systems
\widehat{\bm{\mu}}=(\Phi\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\dots,\mu_{r})
, type I polynomials

The determinant in (
76
) is of size
(N+1)\times(N+1)
, which is even smaller than the determinant in (
75
). Also note that even for that special case, the determinant in (
75
) remains at the same size
(\left|\bm{N}\right|+1)\times(\left|\bm{N}\right|+1)=(rN+1)\times(rN+1)
. We now consider Christoffel transforms of the form
(\Phi\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\dots,\mu_{r})
. This system satisfies dual properties to the system
(\Phi\mu_{1},\Phi_{2}\mu_{2},\dots,\Phi_{r}\mu_{r})
. In particular this system reduces the determinant in (
75
) to size
(N+1)\times(N+1)
. Indeed, (
75
) reduces to

(77)

\begin{split}&\widehat{A}_{\bm{n}}^{(1)}(x)=\Phi(x)^{-1}\det{\begin{pmatrix}A_{\bm{n}}^{(1)}(x)&A_{\bm{n}+\bm{e}_{1}}^{(1)}(x)&\cdots&A_{\bm{n}+N\bm{e}_{1}}^{(1)}(x)\\
A_{\bm{n}}^{(1)}(z_{1})&A_{\bm{n}+\bm{e}_{1}}^{(1)}(z_{1})&\cdots&A_{\bm{n}+N\bm{e}_{1}}^{(1)}(z_{1})\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
A_{\bm{n}}^{(1)}(z_{N})&A_{\bm{n}+\bm{e}_{1}}^{(1)}(z_{N})&\cdots&A_{\bm{n}+N\bm{e}_{1}}^{(1)}(z_{N})\\
\end{pmatrix}},\\
&\widehat{A}_{\bm{n}}^{(j)}(x)=\det{\begin{pmatrix}A_{\bm{n}}^{(j)}(x)&A_{\bm{n}+\bm{e}_{1}}^{(j)}(x)&\cdots&A_{\bm{n}+N\bm{e}_{1}}^{(j)}(x)\\
A_{\bm{n}}^{(1)}(z_{1})&A_{\bm{n}+\bm{e}_{1}}^{(1)}(z_{1})&\cdots&A_{\bm{n}+N\bm{e}_{1}}^{(1)}(z_{1})\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
A_{\bm{n}}^{(1)}(z_{N})&A_{\bm{n}+\bm{e}_{1}}^{(1)}(z_{N})&\cdots&A_{\bm{n}+N\bm{e}_{1}}^{(1)}(z_{N})\\
\end{pmatrix}},\qquad j=2,\dots,r.\end{split}

Note that with our choice
\bm{m}=\bm{n}
, the sequence of multi-indices
\{\bm{n}+j\bm{e}_{1}\}_{j=0}^{N}
we used here is the only possible choice.

4.1.4.
Systems
\widehat{\bm{\mu}}=(\Phi\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\dots,\mu_{r})
, type II polynomials

For the systems considered in the previous example, we can also get a type II formula, but with a determinant of larger size than in the type I formula. Similarly to Section
4.1.2
, Theorem
3.1
does not apply directly to this system for type II polynomials, so we again use the trick (
68
). We note that

(78)

\begin{split}\widehat{\mu}_{1}=\Phi{\mu}_{1},\qquad\Phi\widehat{\mu}_{j}=\Phi\mu_{j},\qquad j=2,\dots,r.\end{split}

Hence we apply Theorem
3.3
with
\Phi_{1}=\cdots=\Phi_{r}=\Phi
,
\Psi_{1}=1
,
\Psi_{2}=\cdots=\Psi_{r}=\Phi
. We obtain the determinantal formula

(79)

\begin{split}\widehat{P}_{\bm{n}-N\bm{e}_{1}}(x)&=\Phi(x)^{-1}\det{\begin{pmatrix}P_{\bm{n}}(x)&P_{\bm{n}-\bm{s}_{1}}(x)&\dots&P_{\bm{n}-\bm{s}_{rN}}(x)\\
P_{\bm{n}}(\bm{z})&P_{\bm{n}-\bm{s}_{1}}(\bm{z})&\dots&P_{\bm{n}-\bm{s}_{rN}}(\bm{z})\\
Q_{\bm{n}}^{(2)}(\bm{z})&Q_{\bm{n}-\bm{s}_{1}}^{(2)}(\bm{z})&\dots&Q_{\bm{n}-\bm{s}_{rN}}^{(2)}(\bm{z})\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
Q_{\bm{n}}^{(r)}(\bm{z})&Q_{\bm{n}-\bm{s}_{1}}^{(r)}(\bm{z})&\dots&Q_{\bm{n}-\bm{s}_{rN}}^{(r)}(\bm{z})\\
\end{pmatrix}},\qquad n_{1}\geq N.\end{split}

To simplify the formula we chose the index
\bm{n}-N\bm{e}_{1}
on the left-hand side instead of
\bm{n}
. This allows the sequence of indices in the determinant to start at
\bm{n}
, and
\left\{\bm{n}-\bm{s}_{j}\right\}_{j=0}^{rN}
is then any admissible sequence from
\bm{n}=(n_{1},\dots,n_{r})
towards
\bm{n}-N\bm{1}=(n_{1}-N,\dots,n_{r}-N)
. Here we assume that the multi-index
\bm{n}-N\bm{1}
stays in
\mathbb{N}^{r}
. For the special case when the sequence
\{\bm{n}-\bm{s}_{j}\}_{j=0}^{rN}
leaves
\mathbb{N}^{r}
, see Theorem
3.3
).

The determinant is of the larger size
(rN+1)\times(rN+1)
, so we are using the compact notation (
54
). Here the
Q
’s are given by

(80)

\begin{split}&Q_{\bm{n}}^{(j)}(z)=\widecheck{\mu}_{j}\left[P_{\bm{n}}(x)\frac{\Phi(x)}{x-z}\right],\qquad z=z_{1},\dots,z_{N},\\
&Q_{\bm{n}}^{(j)}(\bm{z})=\begin{pmatrix}Q_{\bm{n}}^{(j)}(z_{1})\\
\vdots\\
Q_{\bm{n}}^{(j)}(z_{N})\end{pmatrix}\qquad j=2,\dots,r,\qquad\bm{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{r},\end{split}

where
\widecheck{\mu}_{j}
satisfies
\Phi\widecheck{\mu}_{j}=\mu_{j}
,
j=2,\dots,r
.
In the case of measures, with all zeros outside the support of each
\mu_{2},\dots,\mu_{r}
, the choice
d\widecheck{\mu}_{j}(x)=\Phi(x)^{-1}d\mu_{j}(x)
turns (
80
) into the Cauchy transforms

(81)

\begin{split}Q_{\bm{n}}^{(j)}(z)=\int\frac{P_{\bm{n}}(x)}{x-z}d\mu_{j}(x),\qquad j=2,\dots,r,\qquad\bm{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}.\end{split}

The determinantal formula (
79
) was one of the main motivations for the more general Theorem
3.1
and Theorem
3.3
, as
Q
’s enter into the formula, similarly to Uvarov’s formula (
8
).

4.2.
Geronimus Transforms

The most general Geronimus transforms we consider in this paper are systems
\widecheck{\bm{\mu}}=(\widecheck{\mu}_{1},\dots,\widecheck{\mu}_{r})
satisfying

(82)

\begin{split}(\Psi_{1}\widecheck{\mu}_{1},\dots,\Psi_{r}\widecheck{\mu}_{r})=(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{r})\end{split}

for any choice of polynomials
\Psi_{j}(x)=\prod_{k=1}^{M_{j}}(x-w_{j,k})
,
j=1,\dots,r
. We assume that
\Psi_{1},\dots,\Psi_{r}
all have simple zeros. When
\mu_{j}
is a measure and
\Psi_{j}
has no zeros in
\operatorname{supp}(\mu_{j})
,
\widecheck{\mu}_{j}
is then a measure given by

(83)

\begin{split}\int f(x)d\widecheck{\mu}_{j}(x)=\int\frac{f(x)}{\Psi_{j}(x)}d\mu_{j}(x)+\sum_{k=1}^{M_{j}}c_{j,k}f(w_{j,k}).\end{split}

We write
\widecheck{\bm{A}}_{\bm{n}}=(\widecheck{A}_{\bm{n}}^{(1)},\dots,\widecheck{A}_{\bm{n}}^{(r)})
for the type I polynomials with respect to
\widecheck{\bm{\mu}}
,
\bm{A}_{\bm{n}}=(A_{\bm{n}}^{(1)},\dots,A_{\bm{n}}^{(r)})
for the type I polynomials with respect to
\bm{\mu}
,
\widecheck{P}_{\bm{n}}
for the type II polynomials with respect to
\widecheck{\bm{\mu}}
, and
P_{\bm{n}}
for the type II polynomials with respect to
\bm{\mu}
. We also write
\bm{M}
for the index
(M_{1},\dots,M_{r})
.

4.2.1.
Systems
\widecheck{\bm{\mu}}=(\Psi_{1}^{-1}\mu_{1},\Psi_{2}^{-1}\mu_{2},\dots,\Psi_{r}^{-1}\mu_{r})
, type II polynomials

The determinantal formulas presented in this section further illustrate the duality between the type I and type II setting, as well as the duality between multiplying all measures by a factor and multiplying only one of the measures by the same factor. We first see this by noting that the type II formula is better adapted to the general Geronimus transforms than the type I formula. Here we get

(84)

\begin{split}\widecheck{P}_{\bm{n}}(x)=\det{\begin{pmatrix}P_{\bm{n}}(x)&P_{\bm{n}-\bm{s}_{1}}(x)&\cdots&P_{\bm{n}-\bm{s}_{\left|\bm{M}\right|}}(x)\\
Q_{\bm{n}}^{(1)}(\bm{w}_{1})&Q_{\bm{n}-\bm{s}_{1}}^{(1)}(\bm{w}_{1})&\cdots&Q_{\bm{n}-\bm{s}_{\left|\bm{M}\right|}}^{(1)}(\bm{w}_{1})\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
Q_{\bm{n}}^{(r)}(\bm{w}_{r})&Q_{\bm{n}-\bm{s}_{1}}^{(r)}(\bm{w}_{r})&\cdots&Q_{\bm{n}-\bm{s}_{\left|\bm{M}\right|}}^{(r)}(\bm{w}_{r})\\
\end{pmatrix}},\end{split}

where
\left\{\bm{n}+\bm{s}_{j}\right\}_{j=0}^{\left|\bm{M}\right|}
is an admissible sequence from
\bm{n}
towards
\bm{n}-\bm{M}
, including paths from
\bm{n}
to
\bm{n}-\bm{M}
, and the frame from
\bm{n}
towards
\bm{n}-\bm{M}
.
Q_{\bm{n}}^{(j)}(\bm{w}_{j})
in (
84
) is given by

(85)

\begin{split}&Q_{\bm{n}}^{(j)}(w_{j,k})=\widecheck{\mu}_{j}\left[P_{\bm{n}}(x)\frac{\Psi_{j}(x)}{x-w_{j,k}}\right],\qquad k=1,\dots,M_{j},\\
&Q_{\bm{n}}^{(j)}(\bm{w}_{j})=\begin{pmatrix}Q_{\bm{n}}^{(j)}(w_{j,1})\\
\vdots\\
Q_{\bm{n}}^{(j)}(w_{j,M_{j}})\end{pmatrix},\qquad j=1,\dots,r,\qquad\bm{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}.\end{split}

When
\mu_{j}
is the measure (
83
) and
\Psi_{j}
has no zeros in
\operatorname{supp}(\mu_{j})
, we have

(86)

\begin{split}Q_{\bm{n}}^{(j)}(w_{j,k})=\int\frac{P_{\bm{n}}(x)}{x-w_{j,k}}d\mu_{j}(x)+c_{j,k}P_{\bm{n}}(w_{j,k})\Psi_{j}^{\prime}(w_{j,k}),\qquad k=1,\dots,M_{j}.\end{split}

In particular,
Q_{\bm{n}}
is a Cauchy transform when we have no discrete part.

4.2.2.
Systems
\widecheck{\bm{\mu}}=(\Psi^{-1}\mu_{1},\Psi^{-1}\mu_{2},\dots,\Psi^{-1}\mu_{r})
, type I polynomials

The case
\Phi_{1}(x)=\cdots=\Phi_{r}(x)=\Phi(x)=\prod_{k=1}^{N}(x-z_{k})
simplified the determinantal formula for the Christoffel transform of type II polynomials to an
(N+1)\times(N+1)
determinant. For the Geronimus transform, this now happens to type I polynomials when
\Psi_{1}(x)=\cdots=\Psi_{r}(x)=\Psi(x)=\prod_{k=1}^{M}(x-w_{k})
, as (
41
) reduces to

(87)

\begin{split}\widecheck{A}_{\bm{n}}^{(j)}(x)=\det{\begin{pmatrix}A_{\bm{n}-M\bm{e}_{1}}^{(j)}(x)&A_{\bm{n}-(M-1)\bm{e}_{1}}^{(j)}(x)&\cdots&A_{\bm{n}}^{(j)}(x)\\
B_{\bm{n}-M\bm{e}_{1}}(w_{1})&B_{\bm{n}-(M-1)\bm{e}_{1}}(w_{1})&\cdots&B_{\bm{n}}(w_{1})\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
B_{\bm{n}-M\bm{e}_{1}}(w_{M})&B_{\bm{n}-(M-1)\bm{e}_{1}}(w_{M})&\cdots&B_{\bm{n}}(w_{M})\end{pmatrix}},\qquad j=1\dots,r.\end{split}

Here
B_{\bm{n}}(w)
is given by

(88)

\begin{split}B_{\bm{n}}(w_{k})=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\widecheck{\mu}_{j}\left[A_{\bm{n}}^{(j)}(x)\frac{\Psi(x)}{x-w_{k}}\right],\qquad k=1,\dots,M,\qquad\bm{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}.\end{split}

When
\widecheck{\mu}_{j}
is given by (
83
) for each
j=1,\dots,r
we have

(89)

\begin{split}B_{\bm{n}}(w_{k})=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\left(\int\frac{A_{\bm{n}}^{(j)}(x)}{x-w_{k}}d\mu_{j}(x)+c_{j,k}A_{\bm{n}}^{(j)}(w_{k})\Psi_{j}^{\prime}(w_{k})\right),\qquad k=1,\dots,M.\end{split}

4.2.3.
Systems
\widecheck{\bm{\mu}}=(\Psi^{-1}\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\dots,\mu_{r})
, type II and type I polynomials

The determinant in (
87
) is of size
(M+1)\times(M+1)
, but for type I polynomials the determinant is of size
(rM+1)\times(rM+1)
. To get an
(M+1)\times(M+1)
determinant for type II polynomials we instead consider the case
\Psi_{1}=\Psi
and
\Psi_{2}=\cdots=\Psi_{r}=1
in (
82
). Then (
84
) reduces to

(90)

\begin{split}\widecheck{P}_{\bm{n}}(x)=\det{\begin{pmatrix}P_{\bm{n}}(x)&P_{\bm{n}-\bm{e}_{1}}(x)&\cdots&P_{\bm{n}-M\bm{e}_{1}}(x)\\
Q_{\bm{n}}^{(1)}(w_{1})&Q_{\bm{n}-\bm{e}_{1}}^{(1)}(w_{1})&\cdots&Q_{\bm{n}-M\bm{e}_{1}}^{(1)}(w_{1})\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
Q_{\bm{n}}^{(1)}(w_{M})&Q_{\bm{n}-\bm{e}_{1}}^{(1)}(w_{M})&\cdots&Q_{\bm{n}-M\bm{e}_{1}}^{(1)}(w_{M})\\
\end{pmatrix}},\end{split}

where
Q_{\bm{n}}^{(1)}
is given by (
85
) and (
86
) with
j=1
and
w_{1,k}=w_{k}
. Again, there is only possible sequence starting at
\bm{n}
.

On the other hand, for type I polynomials we get the more complicated formula

(91)

\begin{split}&\widecheck{A}_{\bm{n}+M\bm{e}_{1}}^{(1)}(x)=\det{\begin{pmatrix}A_{\bm{n}}^{(1)}(x)&A_{\bm{n}+\bm{s}_{1}}^{(1)}(x)&\cdots&A_{\bm{n}+\bm{s}_{rM}}^{(1)}(x)\\
A_{\bm{n}}^{(2)}(\bm{w})&A_{\bm{n}+\bm{s}_{1}}^{(2)}(\bm{w})&\cdots&A_{\bm{n}+\bm{s}_{rM}}^{(2)}(\bm{w})\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
A_{\bm{n}}^{(r)}(\bm{w})&A_{\bm{n}+\bm{s}_{1}}^{(r)}(\bm{w})&\cdots&A_{\bm{n}+\bm{s}_{rM}}^{(r)}(\bm{w})\\
B_{\bm{n}}(\bm{w})&B_{\bm{n}+\bm{s}_{1}}(\bm{w})&\cdots&B_{\bm{n}+\bm{s}_{rM}}(\bm{w})\end{pmatrix}},\\
&\widecheck{A}_{\bm{n}+M\bm{e}_{1}}^{(j)}(x)=\Phi(x)^{-1}\det{\begin{pmatrix}A_{\bm{n}}^{(j)}(x)&A_{\bm{n}+\bm{s}_{1}}^{(j)}(x)&\cdots&A_{\bm{n}+\bm{s}_{rM}}^{(j)}(x)\\
A_{\bm{n}}^{(2)}(\bm{w})&A_{\bm{n}+\bm{s}_{1}}^{(2)}(\bm{w})&\cdots&A_{\bm{n}+\bm{s}_{rM}}^{(2)}(\bm{w})\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
A_{\bm{n}}^{(r)}(\bm{w})&A_{\bm{n}+\bm{s}_{1}}^{(r)}(\bm{w})&\cdots&A_{\bm{n}+\bm{s}_{rM}}^{(r)}(\bm{w})\\
B_{\bm{n}}(\bm{w})&B_{\bm{n}+\bm{s}_{1}}(\bm{w})&\cdots&B_{\bm{n}+\bm{s}_{rM}}(\bm{w})\end{pmatrix}},\qquad j=2,\dots,r,\end{split}

where
\{\bm{n}+\bm{s}_{j}\}_{j=0}^{rM}
is any admissible sequence from
\bm{n}
towards
\bm{n}+M\bm{1}=(n_{1}+M,\dots,n_{r}+M)
.
Here we can use the same
B_{\bm{n}}
as in (
88
) and (
89
).

4.3.
Various One-Step Transforms

We consider measures/functionals perturbed by degree
1
polynomials. More specifically, we say that a one-step Christoffel transform is a system of the form

(92)

\begin{split}(\widehat{\mu}_{1},\dots,\widehat{\mu}_{r})=(\Phi_{1}\mu_{1},\dots\Phi_{r}\mu_{r}),\qquad\deg\Phi_{j}\leq 1,\qquad j=1,\dots,r,\end{split}

and a one-step Geronimus transform is a system
(\widecheck{\mu}_{1},\dots,\widecheck{\mu}_{r})
satisfying

(93)

\begin{split}(\Psi_{1}\widecheck{\mu}_{1},\dots,\Psi_{r}\widecheck{\mu}_{r})=(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{r}),\qquad\deg\Psi_{j}\leq 1,\qquad j=1,\dots,r.\end{split}

It is likely that the one-step transforms are going to be the most often used, so for the convenience of the reader we specialize our formulas for some of the simplest and most useful cases.

4.3.1.
The simplest one-step Christoffel transforms

First, we note that with
\Phi_{1}(x)=\cdots=\Phi_{r}(x)=x-z_{1}
in (
92
),
the monic type II polynomials are given by

(94)

\begin{split}\widehat{P}_{\bm{n}}(x)&=(x-z_{1})^{-1}P_{\bm{n}}(z_{1})^{-1}\det{\begin{pmatrix}P_{\bm{n}+\bm{e}_{k}}(x)&P_{\bm{n}}(x)\\
P_{\bm{n}+\bm{e}_{k}}(z_{1})&P_{\bm{n}}(z_{1})\end{pmatrix}}\\
&=(x-z_{1})^{-1}\left(P_{\bm{n}+\bm{e}_{k}}(x)-\frac{P_{\bm{n}+\bm{e}_{k}}(z_{1})}{P_{\bm{n}}(z_{1})}P_{\bm{n}}(x)\right),\qquad k=1,\dots,r.\end{split}

This is very similar to (
6
), and identical in the case
r=1
.

For type I polynomials, for the case
\Phi_{1}(x)=x-z_{1}
and
\Phi_{2}(x)=\cdots=\Phi_{r}(x)=1
we get the similar formula

(95)

\begin{split}&\widehat{A}_{\bm{n}}^{(1)}(x)=(x-z_{1})^{-1}A_{\bm{n}+\bm{e}_{1}}^{(1)}(z_{1})^{-1}\det{\begin{pmatrix}A_{\bm{n}}^{(1)}(x)&A_{\bm{n}+\bm{e}_{1}}^{(1)}(x)\\
A_{\bm{n}}^{(1)}(z_{1})&A_{\bm{n}+\bm{e}_{1}}^{(1)}(z_{1})\end{pmatrix}},\\
&\widehat{A}_{\bm{n}}^{(j)}(x)=A_{\bm{n}+\bm{e}_{1}}^{(1)}(z_{1})^{-1}\det{\begin{pmatrix}A_{\bm{n}}^{(j)}(x)&A_{\bm{n}+\bm{e}_{1}}^{(j)}(x)\\
A_{\bm{n}}^{(1)}(z_{1})&A_{\bm{n}+\bm{e}_{1}}^{(1)}(z_{1})\end{pmatrix}},\qquad j=2,\dots,r.\end{split}

In the case
r=1
this is also (
6
), but with a different normalization on the polynomials. (
95
) corresponds to
\bm{m}=\bm{n}
in Theorem
3.1
. More generally, the choice
\bm{m}=\bm{n}+\bm{e}_{1}-\bm{e}_{k}
works for any
k=1,\dots,r
, and then we have the determinantal formula

(96)

\begin{split}&\widehat{A}_{\bm{n}}^{(1)}(x)=(x-z_{1})^{-1}A_{\bm{n}+\bm{e}_{1}}^{(1)}(z_{1})^{-1}\det{\begin{pmatrix}A_{\bm{n}+\bm{e}_{1}-\bm{e}_{k}}^{(1)}(x)&A_{\bm{n}+\bm{e}_{1}}^{(1)}(x)\\
A_{\bm{n}+\bm{e}_{1}-\bm{e}_{k}}^{(1)}(z_{1})&A_{\bm{n}+\bm{e}_{1}}^{(1)}(z_{1})\end{pmatrix}},\\
&\widehat{A}_{\bm{n}}^{(j)}(x)=A_{\bm{n}+\bm{e}_{1}}^{(1)}(z_{1})^{-1}\det{\begin{pmatrix}A_{\bm{n}+\bm{e}_{1}-\bm{e}_{k}}^{(j)}(x)&A_{\bm{n}+\bm{e}_{1}}^{(j)}(x)\\
A_{\bm{n}+\bm{e}_{1}-\bm{e}_{k}}^{(1)}(z_{1})&A_{\bm{n}+\bm{e}_{1}}^{(1)}(z_{1})\end{pmatrix}},\qquad j=2,\dots,r.\end{split}

4.3.2.
The simplest one-step Geronimus transforms

The formulas in this example are dual to the ones in the previous example. For the one-step Geronimus transform with
\Psi_{1}(x)=\cdots=\Psi_{r}(x)=x-w_{1}
, the type I polynomials are given by

(97)

\begin{split}\widecheck{A}_{\bm{n}}^{(j)}(x)=A_{\bm{n}-\bm{e}_{k}}^{(j)}(x)-\frac{B_{\bm{n}-\bm{e}_{k}}(w_{1})}{B_{\bm{n}}(w_{1})}A_{\bm{n}}^{(j)}(x),\qquad j=1,\dots,r,\end{split}

assuming
n_{k}>0
. Here,
B_{\bm{n}}(w_{1})
is given by

(98)

\begin{split}B_{\bm{n}}(w_{1})=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\widecheck{\mu}_{j}[A_{\bm{n}}^{(j)}(x)],\qquad\bm{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}.\end{split}

Meanwhile, in the case
\Psi_{1}(x)=x-w_{1}
and
\Psi_{2}(x)=\cdots=\Psi_{r}(x)=1
, we get the type II formula

(99)

\begin{split}\widecheck{P}_{\bm{n}}(x)=P_{\bm{n}-\bm{e}_{1}+\bm{e}_{k}}(x)-\frac{Q^{(1)}_{\bm{n}-\bm{e}_{1}+\bm{e}_{k}}(w_{1})}{Q^{(1)}_{\bm{n}-\bm{e}_{1}}(w_{1})}P_{\bm{n}-\bm{e}_{1}}(x),\qquad k=1,\dots,r,\end{split}

where
Q^{(1)}_{\bm{n}}(w_{1})
is given by

(100)

\begin{split}Q^{(1)}_{\bm{n}}(w_{1})=\widecheck{\mu}_{1}[P_{\bm{n}}(x)],\qquad\bm{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}.\end{split}

4.3.3.
One-step Christoffel transform, type I polynomials

For the more general one-step Christoffel transform,
with
\Phi_{j}(x)=x-z_{j}
for
z_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
and
j=1,\dots,r
in (
92
)
(note that
z_{j}
’s here are allowed to coincide), we get the determinantal formula

(101)

\begin{split}\widehat{A}_{\bm{n}}^{(j)}(x)=\Phi_{j}(x)^{-1}\det{\begin{pmatrix}A_{\bm{n}}^{(j)}(x)&A_{\bm{n}+\bm{e}_{1}}^{(j)}(x)&\cdots&A_{\bm{n}+\bm{e}_{r}}^{(j)}(x)\\
A_{\bm{n}}^{(1)}(z_{1})&A_{\bm{n}+\bm{e}_{1}}^{(1)}(z_{1})&\cdots&A_{\bm{n}+\bm{e}_{r}}^{(1)}(z_{1})\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
A_{\bm{n}}^{(r)}(z_{r})&A_{\bm{n}+\bm{e}_{1}}^{(r)}(z_{r})&\cdots&A_{\bm{n}+\bm{e}_{r}}^{(r)}(z_{r})\\
\end{pmatrix}},\qquad j=1,\dots,r.\end{split}

Here we chose the frame from
\bm{n}
towards
\bm{n}+\bm{1}=(n_{1}+1,\dots,n_{r}+1)
, which corresponds to the sequence of the nearest neighbours in the one-step case. This is probably the nicest choice of sequence, but we remark that the sequence can also be chosen along the step-line if the index is on the step-line (more generally, we note that we can restrict all our determinantal formulas to the step-line when
\deg{\Phi_{1}}=\cdots=\deg{\Phi_{r}}
).

4.3.4.
One-step Geronimus transform, type II polynomials

In a similar fashion to the previous example, the type II formula for the one-step Geronimus transform
with
\Psi_{j}(x)=x-w_{j}
in (
93
)
is given by

(102)

\begin{split}\widecheck{P}_{\bm{n}}(x)=\det{\begin{pmatrix}P_{\bm{n}}(x)&P_{\bm{n}-\bm{e}_{1}}(x)&\cdots&P_{\bm{n}-\bm{e}_{r}}(x)\\
Q_{\bm{n}}^{(1)}(w_{1})&Q_{\bm{n}-\bm{e}_{1}}^{(1)}(w_{1})&\cdots&Q_{\bm{n}-\bm{e}_{r}}^{(1)}(w_{1})\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
Q_{\bm{n}}^{(r)}(w_{r})&Q_{\bm{n}-\bm{e}_{1}}^{(r)}(w_{r})&\cdots&Q_{\bm{n}-\bm{e}_{r}}^{(r)}(w_{r})\\
\end{pmatrix}}.\end{split}

Here we have

(103)

\begin{split}Q_{\bm{n}}^{(j)}(w_{j})=\widecheck{\mu}_{j}[P_{\bm{n}}(x)],\qquad\bm{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}.\end{split}

If
n_{j}=0
then
\bm{n}-\bm{e}_{j}\notin\mathbb{N}^{r}
, and according to Theorem
3.3
we can put
Q_{\bm{n}-\bm{e}_{j}}^{(j)}(w_{j})=1
. Equivalently, we can simply remove the row and column containing

Q_{\bm{n}-\bm{e}_{j}}^{(j)}(w_{j})
in (
102
).
If
\mu_{j}
is a measure and
w_{j}\notin\operatorname{supp}{(\mu_{j})}
we have Geronimus transforms

(104)

\begin{split}\int f(x)d\widecheck{\mu}_{j}(x)=\int\frac{f(x)}{x-w_{j}}d\mu_{j}(x)+c_{j}f(w_{j}),\end{split}

and then we get

(105)

\begin{split}Q_{\bm{n}}^{(j)}(w_{j})=\int\frac{P_{\bm{n}}(x)}{x-w_{j}}d\mu_{j}(x)+c_{j}P_{\bm{n}}(w_{j}),\qquad\bm{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}.\end{split}

4.3.5.
One-step Christoffel transform, type II polynomials

The formula here is more complicated than the one in the type I case. We choose to include it in the case of a system of two measures
(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
, but it will be clear how it generalizes to any system of measures/functionals. Here we have
(\widehat{\mu}_{1},\widehat{\mu}_{2})=(\Phi_{1}\mu_{1},\Phi_{2}\mu_{2})
, with
\Phi_{1}(x)=x-z_{1}
and
\Phi_{2}(x)=x-z_{2}
. To apply Theorem
3.3
we note that we have

(106)

\begin{split}(x-z_{2})d\widehat{\mu}_{1}(x)=(x-z_{1})(x-z_{2})d\mu_{1}(x),\qquad(x-z_{1})d\widehat{\mu}_{2}(x)=(x-z_{1})(x-z_{2})d\mu_{2}(x).\end{split}

The Geronimus transforms
\widecheck{\mu}_{1}
and
\widecheck{\mu}_{2}
satisfy

(107)

\begin{split}(x-z_{1})(x-z_{2})d\widecheck{\mu}_{1}(x)=(x-z_{1})d\mu_{1}(x),\qquad(x-z_{1})(x-z_{2})d\widecheck{\mu}_{1}(x)=(x-z_{2})d\mu_{1}(x).\end{split}

We assume
z_{2}\notin\operatorname{supp}(\mu_{1})
and
z_{1}\notin\operatorname{supp}(\mu_{2})
, and define Geronimus transforms
\widecheck{\mu}_{1}
and
\widecheck{\mu}_{2}
by

(108)

\begin{split}d\widecheck{\mu}_{1}(x)=(x-z_{2})^{-1}d\mu_{1}(x),\qquad d\widecheck{\mu}_{2}(x)=(x-z_{1})^{-1}d\mu_{2}(x).\end{split}

We then have

(109)

\begin{split}Q_{n,m}^{(1)}(z_{2})=\int\frac{P_{n,m}(x)}{x-z_{2}}d\mu_{1}(x),\qquad Q_{n,m}^{(2)}(z_{1})=\int\frac{P_{n,m}(x)}{x-z_{1}}d\mu_{2}(x),\qquad(n,m)\in\mathbb{N}^{2},\end{split}

and
(x-z_{1})(x-z_{2})\widehat{P}_{n,m}(x)
is given by the determinant

(110)

\begin{split}\det{\begin{pmatrix}P_{n+1,m+1}(x)&P_{n+1,m}(x)&P_{n,m+1}(x)&P_{n+1,m-1}(x)&P_{n-1,m+1}(x)\\
P_{n+1,m+1}(z_{1})&P_{n+1,m}(z_{1})&P_{n,m+1}(z_{1})&P_{n+1,m-1}(z_{1})&P_{n-1,m+1}(z_{1})\\
P_{n+1,m+1}(z_{2})&P_{n+1,m}(z_{2})&P_{n,m+1}(z_{2})&P_{n+1,m-1}(z_{2})&P_{n-1,m+1}(z_{2})\\
Q_{n+1,m+1}^{(2)}(z_{1})&Q_{n+1,m}^{(2)}(z_{1})&Q_{n,m+1}^{(2)}(z_{1})&Q_{n+1,m-1}^{(2)}(z_{1})&Q_{n-1,m+1}^{(2)}(z_{1})\\
Q_{n+1,m+1}^{(1)}(z_{2})&Q_{n+1,m}^{(1)}(z_{2})&Q_{n,m+1}^{(1)}(z_{2})&Q_{n+1,m-1}^{(1)}(z_{2})&Q_{n-1,m+1}^{(1)}(z_{2})\end{pmatrix}}.\end{split}

If
n=0
then we remove the row and column containing
Q_{n-1,m+1}^{(1)}(z_{2})
, and if
m=0
we remove the row and column containing
Q_{n+1,m-1}^{(2)}(z_{1})
. The sequence starts at
(n+1,m+1)
, but we could also choose a sequence starting at
(n+2,m)
or
(n,m+2)
. Here we chose the decreasing frame from
(n+1,m+1)
towards
(n-1,m-1)
as our sequence of multi-indices. It is also possible to choose a sequence
on the step-line, when
(n,m)
is on the step-line.

4.4.
Uvarov Transforms

Uvarov perturbations are also examples of rational perturbations of functionals. We first give type II determinantal formulas for a simple special case, and then give a type II formula for the most general case.

4.4.1.
One-step Uvarov transform, type II polynomials

We assume
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
are measures with
z_{1}\notin\operatorname{supp}(\mu_{1})\cup\operatorname{supp}(\mu_{2})
and consider Uvarov perturbations
(\widetilde{\mu}_{1},\widetilde{\mu}_{2})
of the type

(111)

\begin{split}\widetilde{\mu}_{1}=\mu_{1}+c_{1}\delta_{z_{1}},\qquad\widetilde{\mu}_{2}=\mu_{2}+c_{2}\delta_{z_{1}},\end{split}

where at most one of
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
are
0
. Theorem
3.1
and Theorem
3.3
can be applied to these systems as well, since we have

(112)

\begin{split}\int f(x)(x-z_{1})d\widetilde{\mu}_{j}(x)=\int f(x)(x-z_{1})d\mu_{j}(x),\qquad j=1,2.\end{split}

The Geronimus transforms
\widecheck{\mu}_{1}
and
\widecheck{\mu}_{2}
have to satisfy

(113)

\begin{split}\widecheck{\mu}_{j}[(x-z_{1})P(x)]=\int P(x)d\mu_{j}(x)+c_{j}P(z_{1}),\qquad j=1,2.\end{split}

We make the choice

(114)

\begin{split}\widecheck{\mu}_{j}[P(x)]=\int\frac{P(x)}{x-z_{1}}d\mu_{j}(x)+c_{j}P^{\prime}(z_{1}),\qquad j=1,2.\end{split}

Here,
\widecheck{\mu}_{j}=(x-z_{1})^{-1}\mu_{j}-c_{j}\delta_{z_{1}}^{\prime}
, where
\delta_{z_{1}}^{\prime}
is the functional
P\mapsto-P^{\prime}(z_{1})
, and
(x-z_{1})^{-1}\mu_{j}
is the functional generated by the measure
(x-z_{1})^{-1}d\mu(x)
. Hence
\widecheck{\mu}_{1}
and
\widecheck{\mu}_{2}
are functionals but not measures. We then have

(115)

\begin{split}Q_{n,m}^{(j)}(z_{1})=\int\frac{P_{n,m}(x)}{x-z_{1}}d\mu_{j}(x)+c_{j}P_{n,m}^{\prime}(z_{1}),\qquad j=1,2,\qquad(n,m)\in\mathbb{N}^{2},\end{split}

and we get a type II formula of the form

(116)

\begin{split}\widetilde{P}_{n,m}(x)=(x-z_{1})^{-1}\det{\begin{pmatrix}P_{n+1,m}(x)&P_{n,m}(x)&P_{n-1,m}(x)&P_{n,m-1}(x)\\
P_{n+1,m}(z_{1})&P_{n,m}(z_{1})&P_{n-1,m}(z_{1})&P_{n,m-1}(z_{1})\\
Q_{n+1,m}^{(1)}(z_{1})&Q_{n,m}^{(1)}(z_{1})&Q_{n-1,m}^{(1)}(z_{1})&Q_{n,m-1}^{(1)}(z_{1})\\
Q_{n+1,m}^{(2)}(z_{1})&Q_{n,m}^{(2)}(z_{1})&Q_{n-1,m}^{(2)}(z_{1})&Q_{n,m-1}^{(2)}(z_{1})\end{pmatrix}}.\end{split}

If
n=0
we remove the row and column containing
Q_{n-1,m}^{(1)}(z_{1})
, and if
m=0
we remove the row and column containing
Q_{n,m-1}^{(2)}(z_{1})
. Different choices of sequences can also restrict all polynomials to any choice of path. Note that the sequence chosen here is neither a path nor a frame, but rather a combination of the two, similar to sequence in the middle of the figure in Theorem
3.3
.

4.4.2.
General Uvarov transform, type II polynomials

We say that a general Uvarov transform is a system

(117)

\begin{split}(\widetilde{\mu}_{1},\dots,\widetilde{\mu}_{r})=\bigg(\mu_{1}+\sum_{k=1}^{N}c_{1,k}\delta_{z_{k}},\dots,\mu_{r}+\sum_{k=1}^{N}c_{r,k}\delta_{z_{k}}\bigg),\qquad z_{1},\dots,z_{N}\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\cup_{j=1}^{r}\operatorname{supp}\mu_{j}.\end{split}

Note that the location of the point masses are the same for each measure, but the weights are different. If we put different weights to
0
for different
\mu_{j}
’s we then have the case where we add different points to each measure. For example, with
N=r
,
c_{j,j}=c_{j}
, and
c_{j,k}=0
when
j\neq k
, we get the Uvarov transforms on the form

(118)

\begin{split}(\widetilde{\mu}_{1},\dots,\widetilde{\mu}_{r})=(\mu_{1}+c_{1}\delta_{z_{1}},\dots,\mu_{r}+c_{r}\delta_{z_{r}})\end{split}

The systems (
117
) satisfy

(119)

\begin{split}(\Phi\widetilde{\mu}_{1},\dots,\Phi\widetilde{\mu}_{r})=(\Phi\mu_{1},\dots,\Phi\mu_{r}),\end{split}

for the polynomial
\Phi(x)=\prod_{k=1}^{N}(x-z_{k})
. From here we can apply both Theorem
3.1
and Theorem
3.3
to these systems. If we allow
\Phi
to have zeros of higher multiplicity then we can also add derivatives of
\delta_{z_{k}}
’s.

The type II formula for systems satisfying (
119
) is

(120)

\begin{split}\widetilde{P}_{\bm{n}}=\Phi(x)^{-1}\det{\begin{pmatrix}P_{\bm{n}+N\bm{e}_{1}}(x)&P_{\bm{n}+N\bm{e}_{1}-\bm{s}_{1}}(x)&\dots&P_{\bm{n}+N\bm{e}_{1}-\bm{s}_{(r+1)N}}(x)\\
P_{\bm{n}+N\bm{e}_{1}}(\bm{z})&P_{\bm{n}+N\bm{e}_{1}-\bm{s}_{1}}(\bm{z})&\dots&P_{\bm{n}+N\bm{e}_{1}-\bm{s}_{(r+1)N}}(\bm{z})\\
Q_{\bm{n}+N\bm{e}_{1}}^{(1)}(\bm{z})&Q_{\bm{n}+N\bm{e}_{1}-\bm{s}_{1}}^{(1)}(\bm{z})&\dots&Q_{\bm{n}+N\bm{e}_{1}-\bm{s}_{(r+1)N}}^{(1)}(\bm{z})\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
Q_{\bm{n}+N\bm{e}_{1}}^{(r)}(\bm{z})&Q_{\bm{n}+N\bm{e}_{1}-\bm{s}_{1}}^{(r)}(\bm{z})&\dots&Q_{\bm{n}+N\bm{e}_{1}-\bm{s}_{(r+1)N}}^{(r)}(\bm{z})\\
\end{pmatrix}}.\end{split}

It is easy to check that
\Phi\widecheck{\mu}_{j}=\widetilde{\mu}_{j}
is satisfied by

(121)

\begin{split}\widecheck{\mu}_{j}=\frac{1}{\Phi}\mu_{j}-\sum_{l=1}^{N}\frac{c_{j,l}}{\Phi^{\prime}(z_{l})}\delta^{\prime}_{z_{l}}+\sum_{l=1}^{N}d_{j,l}\delta_{z_{l}},\qquad j=1,\dots,r,\end{split}

for any choice of weights
d_{j,l}
, where
(1/\Phi)\mu_{j}
is the functional given by the measure
\Phi(x)^{-1}d\mu_{j}(x)
, and
\delta_{z}^{\prime}[P(x)]=-P^{\prime}(z)
. Then the
Q
’s are given by

\begin{split}Q_{\bm{n}}^{(j)}(z_{k})&=\widecheck{\mu}_{j}\left[P_{\bm{n}}(x)\frac{\Phi(x)}{x-z_{k}}\right]\\
&=\int\frac{P_{\bm{n}}(x)}{x-z_{k}}d\mu_{j}(x)-\sum_{l=1}^{N}\frac{c_{j,l}}{\Phi^{\prime}(z_{l})}\delta_{z_{l}}^{\prime}\left[P_{\bm{n}}(x)\frac{\Phi(x)}{x-z_{k}}\right]+d_{j,k}P_{\bm{n}}(z_{k})\Phi^{\prime}(z_{k}),\end{split}

for
j=1,\ldots,r
and
k=1,\dots,N
.
A quick calculation shows that

\begin{split}-\delta_{z_{l}}^{\prime}\left[P_{\bm{n}}(x)\frac{\Phi(x)}{x-z_{k}}\right]&=\delta_{z_{l}}\left[P_{\bm{n}}^{\prime}(x)\frac{\Phi(x)}{x-z_{k}}+P_{\bm{n}}(x)\frac{\Phi^{\prime}(x)(x-z_{k})-\Phi(x)}{(x-z_{k})^{2}}\right]\\
&=\begin{cases}\Phi^{\prime}(z_{l})P_{\bm{n}}(z_{l})(z_{l}-z_{k})^{-1},&k\neq l,\\
\Phi^{\prime}(z_{k})P_{\bm{n}}^{\prime}(z_{k})+\frac{1}{2}P_{\bm{n}}(z_{k})\Phi^{\prime\prime}(z_{k}),&k=l.\end{cases}\end{split}

With the choice
d_{j,k}=-\frac{1}{2}c_{j,k}\Phi^{\prime\prime}(z_{l})/\Phi^{\prime}(z_{l})^{2}
we then end up with

(122)

\begin{split}Q_{\bm{n}}^{(j)}(z_{k})&=\int\frac{P_{\bm{n}}(x)}{x-z_{k}}d\mu_{j}(x)+c_{j,k}P_{\bm{n}}^{\prime}(z_{k})+\sum_{l\neq k}c_{j,l}\frac{P_{\bm{n}}(z_{k})}{z_{l}-z_{k}},\qquad j=1,\dots,r.\end{split}

Now observe that in the determinant (
120
) we can subtract multiples of the rows with
P_{\bm{n}}
’s from the rows with
Q_{\bm{n}}^{(j)}
’s. This shows that (
120
) holds
with
Q_{\bm{n}}^{(j)}(z_{k})
replaced by the simpler expression

(123)

\begin{split}\int\frac{P_{\bm{n}}(x)}{x-z_{k}}d\mu_{j}(x)+c_{j,k}P_{\bm{n}}^{\prime}(z_{k}),\qquad j=1,\dots,r,\qquad k=1,\dots,N.\end{split}

In particular, when
r=1
we get (
27
).

The sequence of multi-indices
\left\{\bm{n}+N\bm{e}_{1}-\bm{s}_{j}\right\}_{j=0}^{(r+1)N}
is admissible from
\bm{n}+N\bm{e}_{1}
to
\bm{n}-N\bm{1}
, for example a backwards frame. Note that if we remove the set of rows and columns containing
Q_{\bm{n}+N\bm{e}_{1}-\bm{s}_{i}}^{(1)}(\bm{z})
for
\bm{n}+N\bm{e}_{1}-\bm{s}_{i}\in\left\{\bm{n}-j\bm{e}_{1}\right\}_{j=1}^{N}
, we get (
79
) (with a shift, and possibly different
Q
’s). If we instead remove the rows and columns containing
P_{\bm{n}+N\bm{e}_{1}-\bm{s}_{i}}(\bm{z})
for
\bm{n}+N\bm{e}_{1}-\bm{s}_{i}
in
\left\{\bm{n}+j\bm{e}_{1}\right\}_{j=1}^{N}
, we get something more similar to (
84
). If we remove every row and column containing
Q
’s for indices in
\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}\left\{\bm{n}-j\bm{e}_{i}\right\}_{j=1}^{N}
, we get (
76
) with
\bm{z}_{1}=\cdots=\bm{z}_{r}=\bm{z}
.

5.
Example: Rational perturbations of multiple Laguerre polynomials of the second kind

Theorems
3.1
and
3.3
apply for any system
\bm{\mu}
with sufficiently many normal indices. If one has an explicit form of type I or type II multiple orthogonal polynomials
\bm{\mu}
then orthogonal polynomials of the perturbed system
\widetilde{\mu}

Remark 5.4
.

Normality of the system
\widetilde{\bm{\mu}}
in (
133
), (
134
) at a chosen location
\bm{n}
depends on
w
. This normality fails if and only if
D_{\bm{n}}^{(I)}=0
, where
D_{\bm{n}}^{(I)}
is given in (
145
). It is easy to see that
D_{\bm{n}}^{(I)}
is a polynomial of degree
\leq 1
in

, so there is at most one real value of

\widetilde{\bm{\mu}}
is not normal at
\bm{n}
. Consequently,
\widetilde{\bm{\mu}}
is always perfect if

where it is not perfect.

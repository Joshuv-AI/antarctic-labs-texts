Half canonical rings of Gorenstein spin curves of genus two
Stephen Coughlan, Marco Franciosi, Rita Pardini, Sönke Rollenske

Abstract
. We introduce the notion of generalised Gorenstein spin structure on a curve and we give an explicit description of the associated section ring for curves of genus two with ample canonical bundle, obtaining five different formats.



1991 Mathematics Subject Classification
14H42; 14H45, 14H20

1.
Introduction

Recall that a spin structure (or theta-characteristic) on a smooth curve
C
is a line bundle
{\mathcal{L}}
such that there is an isomorphism
\mu\colon{\mathcal{L}}\otimes{\mathcal{L}}\to\omega_{C}
.
The interest in such structures originated in the classical study of theta functions in the 19th century and they were taken up again in 1970’s in the context of the study of moduli spaces of curves
(see
[
Far12
]
). They also occur in the study of algebraic surfaces: if
X
is a smooth projective surface and
C\in|K_{X}|
is a smooth curve in the canonical linear system,
then
{\mathcal{L}}=K_{X}|_{C}
is a theta-characteristic by the adjunction formula.

Both in applications to moduli and to algebraic surfaces it soon became necessary to consider degenerations of the above description, with both the curve and the line bundle possibly acquiring singularities.
In the context of moduli of curves this problem has seen some attention
[
Cor89
,
Jar98
,
Lud10
]
.
However, the application to algebraic surfaces, that sparked our interest in these structures and will be explained in a subsequent paper, requires a different approach than what was available in the literature.

More precisely, the curves
C
we are led to consider are Gorenstein curves
C
of genus two with ample canonical bundle
\omega_{C}
.
Such curves, if reduced, can be classified into two types, Type A and Type B, according to the image of the bicanonical map (Proposition
3.3
).

A generalised Gorenstein spin (ggs) structure on
C
is given by a torsion-free sheaf
{\mathcal{L}}
everywhere of rank one such that
\chi({\mathcal{L}})=0
and a
multiplication map

\mu\colon{\mathcal{L}}\tensor{\mathcal{L}}\to\omega_{C}

which is an isomorphism at the generic points. If
{\mathcal{L}}
is in fact a line bundle, what we call a regular ggs structure, then this is the direct generalisation of the original notion.
We will see in Section
4
that otherwise the ggs structures on
C
are governed by regular ggs structures on partial normalisations of
C
in many cases.
This leads to a pretty complete understanding of the possible ggs structures on the reduced curves of interest.
The non-reduced cases, which presents more complications, is not addressed here.

For stable curves our definition of spin structure coincides with the definition given in
[
Jar98
]
but, in view of the application to stable surfaces, we need to include both curves with cusps and curves with elliptic tails in our analysis. As explained in the comments
after Definition 1.2 in
[
FS13
]
, these two classes of curves cannot occur at the same time in a separated moduli problem for curves, so it is not clear how to deal with families in our setup. We have chosen not to pursue this question here.

We then go on and analyse the half-canonical ring of such a ggs structure, that is,

R(C,\{{\mathcal{L}},\omega_{C}\})=\bigoplus_{n}R_{n}

where
R_{2n}=H^{0}(C,nK_{C})
and
R_{2n+1}=H^{0}(C,{\mathcal{L}}(nK_{C}))
. Note that this is not a standard section ring, because the multiplication of two elements of odd degree has to be defined via the multiplication map
\mu
. Leaving the precise statements to Section
5
we prove the following.

Theorem
5.2

(simplified version) ---

Let
({\mathcal{L}},\mu)
be a ggs structure on a reduced Gorenstein curve of genus two with ample canonical bundle. Then the following cases occur:

h^{0}({\mathcal{L}})

0

1

2

Type of ring

A(0)
,
B(0)

A(1)
,
B(1)

B(2)

In cases
A(0)
,
A(1)
, and
B(2)
we get complete intersections, in cases
B(0)
and
B(1)
the rings admit a more complicated presentation in codimension six and four.

Note that in each case we can reinterpret the ring as giving a natural embedding of
C
into a weighted projective space
{\mathbb{P}}
, such that
\omega_{C}={\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}}(2)|_{C}
and
{\mathcal{L}}={\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}}(1)|_{C}/\text{torsion}
.

We hope these considerations to be of independent interest, a direct application to the moduli space of stable surfaces will be given in a subsequent paper.

2.
Notation and preliminary results

2.A.
Set-up

We work with schemes of finite type over the complex numbers.

Given a sheaf
{\mathcal{F}}
on a scheme
X
, we denote by
{\mathcal{F}}^{\vee}=\curly{H}om({\mathcal{F}},\mathcal{O}_{X})
its dual and
by
{\mathcal{F}}^{[m]}
the
m
-th reflexive power, i.e., the double dual of

{\mathcal{F}}^{\tensor m}
. The sheaf
{\mathcal{F}}
is reflexive if
{\mathcal{F}}\cong{\mathcal{F}}^{\vee\vee}
.

A curve
C
is a Cohen-Macaulay projective scheme of pure dimension 1. It has a dualising sheaf
\omega_{C}
and we denote by
p_{a}(C)
the arithmetic genus of
C
, that is,
p_{a}(C)=1-\chi(\mathcal{O}_{C})
.
The curve
C
is Gorenstein if
\omega_{C}
is invertible; if this is the case
K_{C}
denotes a canonical divisor such that
\mathcal{O}_{C}(K_{C})\cong\omega_{C}
.

We say that
p\in C
is a singular point of type
A_{m}
if
the completion of the local ring
\mathcal{O}_{C,p}
is isomorphic to the ring
{\mathbb{C}}\llbracket x,y\rrbracket/(y^{2}-x^{m+1})
.

A variety is a reduced (possibly reducible) Cohen–Macaulay scheme.

2.B.
Rank one torsion-free sheaves on Gorenstein curves

A torsion-free sheaf on a curve
C
is a coherent sheaf
{\mathcal{F}}
such that
0pt({\mathcal{F}}_{x})=1
for every closed point
x\in C
;
equivalently
{\mathcal{F}}
has no subsheaves supported on a finite set of points, i.e.,
{\mathcal{F}}
satisfies Serre condition
S_{1}
.

Convention.
By a rank one torsion free sheaf on a curve
C
we mean a torsion free sheaf that has rank one at the general point of any component of
C
.

The degree of a rank one torsion-free sheaf
{\mathcal{F}}
on a curve
C
is defined by the formula
\deg({\mathcal{F}})=\chi({\mathcal{F}})-\chi(\mathcal{O})
, so that the Riemann–Roch formula is true by definition.

Remark 2.1

—

Note that by Serre duality on a Cohen–Macaulay curve
C
we still have
\chi(\omega_{C})=-\chi({\mathcal{O}}_{C})
, so
\deg\omega_{C}=2p_{a}(C)-2
.
If
{\mathcal{F}}\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}
is an injection with finite cokernel, then
\deg{\mathcal{F}}\leq\deg{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}
by the additivity of the Euler characteristic.

In general, every reflexive sheaf on a curve is torsion-free and by a result of Hartshorne these notions coincide for rank one sheaves on a Gorenstein curve.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
Har94
]
, Prop. 1.6) ---

Let
C
be a Gorenstein curve and
{\mathcal{F}}
a rank one coherent sheaf on
C
. Then
{\mathcal{F}}
is reflexive if and only if it is
torsion-free.

In particular, for any such sheaf
{\mathcal{F}}
we have

{\mathcal{F}}^{[m]}=\left({\mathcal{F}}^{\tensor m}\right)^{\vee\vee}=\left({\mathcal{F}}^{\tensor m}\right)/\langle\text{0-dimensional torsion}\rangle.

Moreover, in this case, if
{\mathcal{F}}
is a rank one torsion-free sheaf and
{\mathcal{H}}
is an invertible sheaf then
\deg({\mathcal{F}}\otimes{\mathcal{H}})=\deg({\mathcal{F}})+\deg({\mathcal{H}})
(cf.
[
Har94
, Prop. 2.8]
).
We point out a useful Lemma, which relates the behaviour of a rank one torsion free sheaf and its pull-back under a partial normalisation.

Lemma 2.3
---

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a rank one torsion free sheaf on a reduced Gorenstein curve.
Then:

(
i
)

there are a (uniquely determined) partial normalisation
f\colon\widetilde{C}\to C
and a line bundle
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
on
\widetilde{C}
such that

{\mathcal{F}}=f_{*}\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
;

(
ii
)

\Aut({\mathcal{F}})=\Aut(\widetilde{\mathcal{F}})
. In particular, if
\widetilde{C}
is connected then
\Aut({\mathcal{F}})={\mathbb{C}}^{*}
.

Proof.

(i)
Since at a smooth point of a curve every torsion-free sheaf is free, the question is local at the singular points of
C
.
Then the existence of the partial normalisation and the line bundle
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
follows from
[
Bas63
, Prop. 7.2]
.
For the unicity, note that the natural map
f^{*}{\mathcal{F}}=f^{*}f_{*}\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}\to\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
is surjective and therefore it induces an isomorphism
(f^{*}{\mathcal{F}})^{[1]}\cong\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
, since both sheaves have rank one and
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
is locally free. Hence
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
can be recovered from
{\mathcal{F}}
.

(ii)
Automorphisms of
{\mathcal{F}}
,
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
are in bijection. In fact an automorphism of
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
obviously induces an automorphism of
{\mathcal{F}}:=f_{*}\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
. Conversely an automorphism of
{\mathcal{F}}
induces an automorphism of
(f^{*}{\mathcal{F}})^{[1]}\cong\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
.

\square

The result that follows is certainly well known to experts, but we give a proof for lack of a suitable reference.

Lemma 2.4
---

Let
C
be a reduced Gorenstein curve and let
A,B\subset C
be subcurves without common components such that
C=A\cup B
. Then there is an exact sequence:

0\to\omega_{B}\to\omega_{C}\to\omega_{C}{\raisebox{-0.1pt}{$|_{A}$}}\to 0.

Proof.

Let
{\mathcal{I}}_{A},{\mathcal{I}}_{B}\subset{\mathcal{O}}_{C}
be the ideals of
A
,
B
, respectively. Both ideals are radical and they satisfy
{\mathcal{I}}_{A}{\mathcal{I}}_{B}=0
. More precisely,
{\mathcal{I}}_{A}
is the annihilator of
{\mathcal{I}}_{B}
, and viceversa.
By relative duality for finite morphisms
[
Sta23
,
Section 0AU3
]
we have an inclusion
\omega_{B}=\curly{H}om_{\mathcal{O}_{C}}({\mathcal{O}}_{B},\omega_{C})\hookrightarrow\omega_{C}
,
so it is enough to give an isomorphism
{\mathcal{I}}_{A}\omega_{C}\to\curly{H}om_{\mathcal{O}_{C}}({\mathcal{O}}_{B},\omega_{C})
.
In fact, there is a natural map
F\colon{\mathcal{I}}_{A}\omega_{C}\to\curly{H}om_{\mathcal{O}_{C}}({\mathcal{O}}_{B},\omega_{C})
, that maps
\sigma\in{\mathcal{I}}_{A}\omega_{C}
to the homomorphism that sends
1\in\mathcal{O}_{B}
to
\sigma
and,
using the fact that
\omega_{C}
is invertible and
{\mathcal{I}}_{A}
is the annihilator of
{\mathcal{I}}_{B}
, it is immediate to check that
F
is an isomorphism.

\square

For later reference, we recall two results related to sections and cohomology of torsion-free sheaves.

Lemma 2.5
---

Let
C
be a reduced Gorenstein curve and
{\mathcal{F}}
a rank one torsion-free sheaf on
C
. If
\deg\left({\mathcal{F}}{\raisebox{-0.1pt}{$|_{B}$}}^{[{1}]}\right)\geq 2p_{a}(B)-1
for every subcurve
B\subseteq C
, then
H^{1}(C,{\mathcal{F}})=0
.

Proof.

The proof follows by the arguments used in
[
CFHR99
, Thm.1.1]
.

By Serre duality
H^{1}(C,{\mathcal{F}})^{\vee}\cong\Hom({\mathcal{F}},\omega_{C})
. Assume it is not zero and
pick any nonzero map

\varphi\colon{\mathcal{F}}\to\omega_{C}
. By
[
CFHR99
, Lemma 2.4]
,
\varphi
comes from a generically surjective
map

{\mathcal{F}}{\raisebox{-0.1pt}{$|_{B}$}}\to\omega_{B}
for a subcurve
B\subseteq C
and yields an injection
{\mathcal{F}}{\raisebox{-0.1pt}{$|_{B}$}}^{[{1}]}\hookrightarrow\omega_{B}
whose
cokernel has
finite length. Therefore
\chi\left({\mathcal{F}}{\raisebox{-0.1pt}{$|_{B}$}}^{[{1}]}\right)\leq\chi(\omega_{B})
, and Remark
2.1
gives

\deg\left({\mathcal{F}}{\raisebox{-0.1pt}{$|_{B}$}}^{[{1}]}\right)\leq 2p_{a}(B)-2
, against the assumptions.

\square

To conclude this section we recall a slight generalisation of the
Base-Point Free Pencil Trick
, a way to study the behavior of multiplication of sections on a connected curve
(see
[
Mum70
, §1 Thm. 2]
,
[
Fra04
, Prop. 1.5]
).

Proposition 2.6
---

Let
C
be a connected reduced Gorenstein curve, let
{\mathcal{H}}
be an invertible sheaf
on
C
such that
|{\mathcal{H}}|
is a base point free system, and let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a rank one torsion-free sheaf on
C
.
Then
the multiplication map

\rho_{{\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{H}}}:H^{0}(C,{\mathcal{H}})\otimes H^{0}(C,{\mathcal{F}})\rightarrow H^{0}(C,{\mathcal{H}}\otimes{\mathcal{F}})

is surjective if one of the following conditions holds

(
i
)

H^{1}(C,{\mathcal{F}}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}^{-1})=0
; by Lemma
2.5
this holds
if
\deg\left(({\mathcal{F}}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}^{-1})_{|B}^{[{1}]}\right)\geq 2p_{a}(B)-1

for every subcurve
B\subseteq C
.

(
ii
)

h^{0}(C,{\mathcal{H}})\geq 3
and
{\mathcal{F}}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}^{-1}\cong\omega_{C}
.

Moreover, if
h^{0}(C,{\mathcal{H}})=2
then

\ker\rho_{{\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{H}}}\cong H^{0}(C,{\mathcal{F}}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}^{-1}).

Proof.

(i) follows by adapting to our case the arguments used in
[
Mum70
, §1 Thm. 2]
(which is stated for any coherent sheaf
{\mathcal{F}}
on a variety
X
). Indeed,
since
|{\mathcal{H}}|
is a base point free system, we can take a section which yields an inclusion
{\mathcal{F}}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}^{-1}\to{\mathcal{F}}
; denoting by

{\mathcal{G}}
its cokernel, by our assumptions
\dim(\operatorname{Supp}({\mathcal{G}}))=0
and we have an exact sequence

0\to H^{0}(C,{\mathcal{F}}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}^{-1})\otimes H^{0}(C,{\mathcal{H}})\to H^{0}(C,{\mathcal{F}})\otimes H^{0}(C,{\mathcal{H}})\to H^{0}(C,{\mathcal{G}})\otimes H^{0}(C,{\mathcal{H}})\to 0;

by a direct computation we may assume
H^{0}(C,{\mathcal{H}})\otimes H^{0}(C,{\mathcal{G}})\twoheadrightarrow H^{0}(C,{\mathcal{H}}\otimes{\mathcal{G}})
, and we may conclude
as in
[
Mum70
, §1 Thm. 2, p. 45]
by diagram chasing.

(ii)
follows from
[
Fra04
, Prop. 1.5]
, since in this case
{\mathcal{F}}
is invertible.

The last statement is the classical Castelnuovo’s Base-Point Free Pencil Trick as shown in
[
ACGH85
, p. 126]
.

\square

3.
Reduced Gorenstein curves of genus two with ample canonical bundle

The contribution of a singular point of a curve to the global genus, compared to the normalisation, is measured by the so-called
\delta
-invariant of the point. Singularities with small value of this invariant have been classified by Stevens
[
Ste96
]
and later independently in
[
Smy11
, Appendix A]
and
[
Bat22
, Sect. 2]
. However, without further conditions, there are infinitely many Gorenstein curves of any fixed genus.

Example 3.1
---

If
C
is any nodal curve such that every component is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
and the dual graph is a tree, then
C
is Gorenstein of arithmetic genus zero.

Clearly, every irreducible curve of genus zero is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
.

Example 3.2
---

There are three different types of irreducible Gorenstein curves
C
of arithmetic genus
1
,
namely a smooth elliptic curve, or a rational curve with a node
A_{1}
or a cusp
A_{2}
.

This can either be deduced from Stevens’ classification or by showing that a line bundle of degree three embeds
C
as a plane cubic reasoning as in Proposition
3.3
below.

There are several ways to understand and describe the curves we are interested in. For sake of brevity, we take a quick route via the canonical ring, where we double the degrees in consistency with later considerations.

Proposition 3.3
---

Let
C
be a reduced connected Gorenstein curve of arithmetic genus two with ample canonical bundle. Then the canonical ring is isomorphic to one of the following:

Type A:

R(C,\omega_{C})={\mathbb{C}}[y_{1},y_{2},u]/(u^{2}-f_{12}(y_{1},y_{2})),

where
\deg(y_{1},y_{2},u)=(2,2,6)
and
f_{12}
is a non-zero polynomial of weighted degree
12
.

Type B:

R(C,\omega_{C})\cong{\mathbb{C}}[y_{1},y_{2},v,u]/(y_{1}y_{2},u^{2}-f_{12}(y_{1},y_{2},v)),

where
\deg(y_{1},y_{2},v,u)=(2,2,4,6)
and
f_{12}
is a polynomial of degree
12
containing the monomial
v^{3}
with non-zero coefficient.

Proof.

Note that for a Gorenstein curve the Hilbert series of the canonical ring is determined by the genus and the Riemann–Roch formula, so in our case the series is
H(t)=1+2t^{2}+\sum_{n\geq 2}(2n-1)t^{2n}
. Since

t^{2}H(t)=t^{2}+2t^{4}+\sum_{n\geq 3}(2n-3)t^{2n},

we may subtract the latter series from the former to get

(1-t^{2})H(t)=1+t^{2}+t^{4}+\sum_{n\geq 3}2t^{2n}.

Repeating the above procedure, we have

t^{2}(1-t^{2})H(t)=t^{2}+t^{4}+t^{6}+\sum_{n\geq 4}2t^{2n}

and subtracting yields
(1-t^{2})^{2}H(t)=1+t^{6}
. Hence

H(t)=\frac{1+t^{6}}{(1-t^{2})^{2}}=\frac{(1-t^{12})}{(1-t^{2})^{2}(1-t^{6})}.

If
C
is irreducible, we can proceed as for smooth curves (compare
[
FPR17
, Section 3.1]
) and get a canonical ring of type A.

Now assume
C
is reducible. Note that an ample line bundle has positive degree on any component, so there are at most two components and we can write
C=C_{1}\cup C_{2}
with
\deg(\omega_{C}|_{C_{i}})=1
.

By Lemma
2.4
there is an exact sequence

(3.4)

0\to\omega_{C_{1}}\to\omega_{C}\to\omega_{C}|_{C_{2}}\to 0.

This implies that
\omega_{C_{i}}\hookrightarrow\omega_{C}|_{C_{i}}
, so by Remark
2.1
we get
\deg\omega_{C_{i}}\leq 0
by parity and thus
C_{i}
has arithmetic genus at most one. Therefore, each component is Gorenstein by
[
Cat82
, Lemma 1.19]
.

Taking global sections in (
3.4
) after twisting by
(m-1)K_{C}
with
m\geq 1
, we get an exact sequence

(3.5)

0\to H^{0}(K_{C_{1}}+(m-1)K_{C}|_{C_{1}})\to H^{0}(mK_{C})\to H^{0}(mK_{C}|_{C_{2}})\to 0

because by Serre duality
H^{1}(K_{C_{1}}+(m-1)K_{C}|_{C_{1}})\to H^{1}(mK_{C})
is an isomorphism for
m=1
and both are zero for
m\geq 2
.

Now assume
p_{a}(C_{2})=0
, that is,
C_{2}
is a smooth rational curve. Then
\omega_{C}|_{C_{2}}={\mathcal{O}}_{C_{2}}(1)
has two sections, so sequence (
3.5
) for
m=1
shows that
h^{0}(K_{C_{1}})=0
, and therefore both components are rational. Also, since
K_{C}|_{C_{i}}
has degree one, it is base-point free on the components and thus globally base point free, because all sections lift to
C
.
By Proposition
2.6

(i)
the multiplication map

H^{0}(C,K_{C})\otimes H^{0}(C,nK_{C})\to H^{0}(C,(n+1)K_{C})

is onto for
n\geq 3
.
For
n=1
one can check easily that
\langle y_{1}^{2},y_{2}^{2},y_{1}y_{2}\rangle
span
H^{0}(C,2K_{C})
, where
y_{1},y_{2}
is a basis of
H^{0}(K_{C})
.
For
n=2
the kernel of the multiplication map has dimension two (generated by the trivial relations
y_{1}\tensor y_{1}y_{2}-y_{2}\tensor y_{1}^{2},y_{2}\tensor y_{1}y_{2}-y_{1}\tensor y_{2}^{2}
),
hence we need a new generator
u\in H^{0}(3K_{C})
.

Concerning the relations, we obtain
a non-trivial relation between monomials in our generators in
H^{0}(6K_{C})
, which we can write in the given form by completing the square. Comparing the Hilbert series, we see that we have already found the full ring of type A.

Now we turn to the case where both components are of arithmetic genus one. By
[
Cat82
, Lemma 1.12]
the ideal
{\mathcal{O}}_{C_{2}}(-C_{1})
is invertible and defines a subscheme of length
\deg\omega_{C}|_{C_{2}}-\deg\omega_{C_{2}}=1
, i.e., a single point
p\in C
, which by
[
Cat82
, Prop. 1.10]
is a node of
C
.
Then sequence (
3.5
) for
m=1
shows that

p
is a base point of the canonical system. Let
y_{i}
be a generator of the kernel of
H^{0}(K_{C})\to H^{0}(K_{C}|_{C_{i}})
. Then
H^{0}(K_{C})=\langle y_{1},y_{2}\rangle
such that
y_{i}|_{C_{i}}=0
.
In particular,
y_{1}y_{2}=0
and we can write
H^{0}(2K_{C})=\langle y_{1}^{2},y_{2}^{2},v\rangle
.
Note by (
3.5
), this time for
m=2
, that
y_{1}^{2},v
restricts to
C_{2}
as a complete linear system of degree two, thus without base points. Hence also
|2K_{C}|
has no base points,
v
is non-zero on both components, and
by Proposition
2.6

(ii)
for
n=3
and
(i)
for
n\geq 4
, we obtain surjections

H^{0}(C,2K_{C})\otimes H^{0}(C,nK_{C})\twoheadrightarrow H^{0}(C,(n+2)K_{C})

for every
n\geq 3
.

Now consider the multiplication map
H^{0}(K_{C})\otimes H^{0}(2K_{C})\to H^{0}(3K_{C})
. The image of this map is generated by the monomials
\{y_{1}^{3},y_{2}^{3},y_{1}v,y_{2}v\}
.
We have no linear relations among them since a combination

a_{1}y_{1}^{3}+a_{2}y_{2}^{3}+b_{1}y_{1}v+b_{2}y_{2}v=0

when restricted to
C_{1}
yields
a_{2}y_{2}^{3}+b_{2}y_{2}v=0
, whence
a_{2}=b_{2}=0
, and then restricted to
C_{2}
gives
a_{1}=b_{1}=0
.
Therefore, since
h^{0}(C,3K_{C})=5
we need a new generator
u
of degree 6.

Similarly we can show that
H^{0}(4K_{C})
is generated by the monomials

\{y_{1}^{4},y_{2}^{4},y_{1}^{2}v,y_{2}^{2}v,v^{2},y_{1}u,y_{2}u\}.

Summing up, the canonical ring
R(C,K_{C})
is generated by
\{y_{1},y_{2},v,u\}
.

Concerning the relations, we have trivial relations induced by
y_{1}y_{2}=0
in degree
\leq 10
, and a new relation in degree
12
, which after completing the square can be written as
u^{2}-f_{12}(y_{1},y_{2},v)
.
Note that
f_{12}
must contain the monomial
v^{3}
with
non-zero coefficient, since otherwise the intersection point
C_{1}\cap C_{2}
corresponding to
the ideal
(y_{1},y_{2},u^{2}-f_{12}(y_{1},y_{2},v))
would be the point
(0:0:1:0)
and therefore singular for both
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
.
Comparing the Hilbert series,
\frac{(1-t^{12})(1-t^{4})}{(1-t^{2})^{2}(1-t^{6})(1-t^{4})}=\frac{(1-t^{12})}{(1-t^{2})^{2}(1-t^{6})}

we see that we have already found the full ring of type B.
\square

Corollary 3.6
---

Let
C
be a reduced connected Gorenstein curve of arithmetic genus two with ample canonical bundle.
Then the following hold:

(
i
)

The bicanonical map is a morphism of degree two onto a conic
Q
in
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
.

(
ii
)

The conic
Q
is reducible if and only if
C
is of type B.

(
iii
)

The rational involution
\iota
induced on
C
by the bicanonical map is regular.

Proof.

(i)
and
(ii)
follow immediately by Proposition
3.3
.

(iii)
By Proposition
3.3
the curve
C
is embedded in a weighted projective space
\mathbb{P}
in such a way that the regular involution of
\mathbb{P}
that sends

to
-u

\iota
on
C
.

\square

Remark 3.7

—

Let
X
be a variety (so in particular a Cohen–Macaulay scheme, cf. section
2.A
) with an involution
\iota
that acts non-trivially on every irreducible component of
X
and let
\pi\colon X\to Y:=X/\iota
be the quotient map. Then
\pi_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
decomposes under the action of
\iota
as a direct sum

{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}\oplus{\mathcal{F}}
, where
{\mathcal{F}}
is a torsion-free sheaf of rank one, and
\iota
acts on
{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}
as the identity and on
{\mathcal{F}}
as multiplication by
-1
. By relative duality we have
\pi_{*}\omega_{X}=\curly{H}om(\pi_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X},\omega_{Y})

and the decomposition of
\pi_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
induces a decomposition
\pi_{*}\omega_{X}=\omega_{Y}\oplus\curly{H}om({\mathcal{F}},\omega_{Y})
, where the first summand is
\iota

We call the corresponding linearisation of
\omega_{Y}
“natural”. Note that when both
X
and
Y
are smooth the natural linearisation corresponds to the natural action on differential forms, for which invariant

n
-forms on
X
correspond to pull-backs of
n
-forms on
Y
; note also that if the map
\pi
is flat, namely
{\mathcal{F}}
is a line bundle, then

\omega_{X}=\varphi^{*}(\omega_{Y}\otimes{\mathcal{F}}^{-1})

and the opposite of the natural linearisation is the one induced by the fact that
\omega_{X}
is a pull-back from
Y
.

Consider now a reduced Gorenstein curve
C
of genus two with
\omega_{C}
ample; the bicanonical map
\varphi\colon C\to Q
is the quotient map for the bicanonical involution
\iota
defined in Corollary
3.6
. Actually in Corollary
3.6

\iota
is defined by its action on the canonical ring

, which in addition determines a linearisation of
\omega_{C}
for which the elements of
H^{0}(\omega_{C})
are all invariant. Since
H^{0}(\omega_{Q})=0
, this is the opposite of the natural linearisation and is the linearisation that we will always consider in our computations

Corollary 3.8

With respect to the linearisation fixed in Remark

4.
Generalised Gorenstein spin curves

A spin structure (or theta-characteristic) on a smooth curve is a line bundle
{\mathcal{L}}
such that there is an isomorphism
\mu\colon{\mathcal{L}}\otimes{\mathcal{L}}\to\omega_{C}
.
Here we propose a generalisation of this notion to Gorenstein curves. We remark that in the case of (semi-)stable curves there are several analogous notions in the literature (see e.g.,
[
Cor89
,
Jar98
,
Lud10
]
).

Definition 4.1
---

Let
C
be a projective Gorenstein curve.
A generalised Gorenstein spin (ggs) structure on
C
is a pair
({\mathcal{L}},\mu)
where
{\mathcal{L}}
is a torsion-free sheaf of rank
1
on
C
with
\chi({\mathcal{L}})=0
, and

\mu\colon{\mathcal{L}}\tensor_{{\mathcal{O}}_{C}}{\mathcal{L}}\to{\mathcal{L}}^{[{2}]}\to\omega_{C}

is a map which is an isomorphism in codimension zero, that is, at the generic point of each component of
C
.
We say the ggs structure is regular if
{\mathcal{L}}
is a line bundle.

Isomorphisms of ggs structures are defined in the obvious way, namely, ggs structures
(C_{1},{\mathcal{L}}_{1},\mu_{1})
and
(C_{1},{\mathcal{L}}_{1},\mu_{1})

are isomorphic if there are an isomorphism
\varphi\colon C_{1}\to C_{2}
and an isomorphism
{\mathcal{L}}_{1}\to\varphi^{*}{\mathcal{L}}_{2}
compatible with
\mu_{1}
and
\varphi^{*}\mu_{2}
.
In particular, when
\varphi
is the identity we may rescale
\mu
by a non-zero scalar.

Note that the second condition implies that
{\mathcal{L}}
is generically locally free, since
\omega_{C}
is locally free. This is automatic on a reduced curve.

Note also that for reasons of degree the multiplication map is an isomorphism if the ggs structure is regular. If
C
is smooth, all ggs structures are regular
because a torsion-free sheaf is locally free at a smooth point of any curve.

In some applications ggs structures will appear naturally also on non-reduced curves but uniform results seem to be much harder to come by, so we do not address this case here.

Let
C
be a reduced curve and
f\colon\widetilde{C}\to C
a finite birational morphism. Then the normalisation of
C
factors over
f
, which is therefore often called a partial normalisation.
Note that a partial normalisation might not preserve the Gorenstein property in general: three general lines through the origin in
{\mathbb{C}}^{3}
are not Gorenstein, but map birationally to three general lines through the origin in the plane, a Gorenstein singularity.

Example 4.2
---

Let us describe all partial normalisations locally at a point of type
A_{m}
where we have two cases according to the parity of
m
, compare
[
Yos90
, 9.9, 5.11]
.

A_{2n}
:

The completion of the local ring is isomorphic to the ring
{\mathbb{C}}\llbracket x,y\rrbracket/(y^{2}-x^{2n+1})\cong{\mathbb{C}}\llbracket t^{2},t^{2n+1}\rrbracket
and the partial normalisations are given by the inclusions

{\mathbb{C}}\llbracket t\rrbracket\supset{\mathbb{C}}\llbracket t^{2},t^{3}\rrbracket\supset{\mathbb{C}}\llbracket t^{2},t^{5}\rrbracket\supset{\mathbb{C}}\llbracket t^{2},t^{7}\rrbracket\supset\dots

A_{2n-1}
:

The completion of the local ring is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{C}}\llbracket x,y\rrbracket/(y^{2}-x^{2n})
and we get a series of inclusions

{\mathbb{C}}\llbracket x,y\rrbracket/(y-x)\times{\mathbb{C}}\llbracket x,y\rrbracket/(y+x)\hookleftarrow{\mathbb{C}}\llbracket x,y\rrbracket/(y^{2}-x^{2})\supset{\mathbb{C}}\llbracket x,xy\rrbracket\supset{\mathbb{C}}\llbracket x,x^{2}y\rrbracket\supset\dots

where we consider
{\mathbb{C}}\llbracket x,x^{k}y\rrbracket
as a subring of

{\mathbb{C}}\llbracket x,y\rrbracket/(y^{2}-x^{2})
, so that with
y_{k}=x^{k}y
we get the desired relation
y_{k}^{2}-x^{k+2}=0
.

Note that in both series each successive subring has codimension one in the previous ring, when considered as a
{\mathbb{C}}
-vector space.

From this description we see that any partial normalisation of an
A_{n}
singularity is an
A_{m}
singularity with
m<n
of the same parity, or smooth. In particular, it is also Gorenstein.

The following is true if every partial normalisation of the Gorenstein curve
C
is again Gorenstein, but we state it only for the case that we need.

Proposition 4.3
---

Let
({\mathcal{L}},\mu)
be a ggs structure on a reduced Gorenstein curve with only singularities of type
A_{m}
. Then
there exists a (uniquely determined) partial normalisation
f\colon\widetilde{C}\to C
and a regular ggs structure
(\widetilde{\mathcal{L}},\widetilde{\mu})
on
\widetilde{C}
such that

{\mathcal{L}}=f_{*}\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}\text{ and }{\mathcal{L}}^{[{2}]}=f_{*}\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}^{\tensor 2}.

Proof.

Since at a smooth point of a curve every torsion-free sheaf is free, the question is local at the singular points of
C
.
Then the existence of the partial normalisation and the line bundle
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}
follows from Lemma
2.3
its unicity from the description in Example
4.2
.

For the last statement, fix a singular point
P\in C
and denote by
Z
its preimage in
\widetilde{C}
. Note that it is possible to find an affine open set of
\widetilde{C}
containing
Z
on which
\widetilde{{\mathcal{L}}}
is trivial. In fact, twisting
\widetilde{{\mathcal{L}}}
with a sufficiently ample effective divisor
H
disjoint from
Z
we may assume that
|{\mathcal{L}}(H)|
is base point free. So there is a section
\sigma\in H^{0}(\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}(H))
that does not vanish at any point of
Z
, and therefore trivializes
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}
on an affine open set containing
Z
and contained in the complement of
H
. Therefore we may replace
C
by affine open neighbourhood
U=\spec A
of
P
,
\widetilde{C}
by the preimage
\widetilde{U}=\spec\widetilde{A}
of
U
in
\widetilde{C}
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}
by the trivial bundle. The map
f_{*}\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}\otimes f_{*}\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}\to f_{*}\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}^{\otimes 2}
corresponds to the map of
A
-modules
\widetilde{A}\otimes_{A}\widetilde{A}\to\widetilde{A}
induced by the multiplication of
\widetilde{A}
, and thus it is surjective.
Since both sheaves have rank one and
f_{*}\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}
is torsion-free, the induced map
{\mathcal{L}}^{[{2}]}\to f_{*}\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}^{\tensor 2}
is an isomorphism.

It remains to show that
(\widetilde{\mathcal{L}},\widetilde{\mu})
is a regular ggs structure on
\widetilde{C}
.
We have seen in Example
4.2
that
\widetilde{C}
is again Gorenstein, so we can apply relative duality for a finite morphism of Gorenstein schemes, e.g.,
[
Sta23
,
Section 0AU3
]
, which gives

\Hom({\mathcal{L}}^{[2]},\omega_{C})=\Hom(f_{*}\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}^{\tensor 2},\omega_{C})\cong\Hom(\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}^{\tensor 2},\omega_{\widetilde{C}}),

and clearly
0=\chi({\mathcal{L}})=\chi(\widetilde{\mathcal{L}})
.
Restriction to the subset where
f
is an isomorphism shows that
\varphi\colon{\mathcal{L}}^{[{2}]}\to\omega_{C}
is generically an isomorphism if and only if the corresponding
\widetilde{\varphi}\colon\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}^{\tensor 2}\to\omega_{\widetilde{C}}
is, completing the correspondence between ggs structures.

\square

With Proposition
4.3
in place, the enumeration of regular and irregular ggs structures on any given curve with type
A_{n}
singularities is relatively simple, but the number of cases to consider grows quickly, if there are several possible partial normalisations to consider.

Example 4.4
---

On a smooth rational curve, the only ggs structure is
{\mathcal{L}}\cong{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}}(-1)
.

Proposition 4.5
---

Let
C=\bigcup_{i}C_{i}
be a reduced and connected Gorenstein curve, where
C_{i}
are the irreducible components.

(
i
)

The curve
C
admits a regular ggs structure
({\mathcal{L}},\mu)
if and only if
\deg\omega_{C}|_{C_{i}}
is even on every component
C_{i}
.

(
ii
)

If
C
admits a regular ggs structure, then it admits exactly
2^{b_{1}}
different regular ggs structures, where
b_{1}
is the first Betti number of
C
.

In particular, every irreducible Gorenstein curve admits a regular ggs structure while a curve with a separating node does not admit a regular ggs structure .

Proof.

Let
({\mathcal{L}},\mu)
be a regular ggs structure. Then for any
2
-torsion line bundle
\eta\in\Pic^{0}(C)
the bundle
{\mathcal{L}}\tensor\eta
is a again a ggs structure and the set of regular ggs structures is a torsor under the group of
2
-torsion line bundles
\Pic^{0}(C)[2]
. By the exponential sequence,
\Pic^{0}(C)
is a connected algebraic group that fits in an exact sequence

(4.6)

0\to H^{1}(C,{\mathbb{Z}})\to H^{1}(C,{\mathcal{O}}_{C})\to\Pic^{0}(C)\to 0

and therefore
2
-torsion elements in this group are given by the image of
\frac{1}{2}H^{1}(C,{\mathbb{Z}})
in
\Pic^{0}(C)
. This proves
(ii)
.

If
\omega_{C}|_{C_{i}}
has odd degree on any component, then there cannot exist a regular ggs structure
({\mathcal{L}},\mu)
on
C
, because
2\deg{\mathcal{L}}|_{C_{i}}=\deg\omega_{C}|_{C_{i}}
.

On the other hand, assume that
\deg\omega_{C}|_{C_{i}}=2d_{i}
for all
i
and pick a smooth point
p_{i}
in
C_{i}
. Then
\eta:=\omega_{C}\left(-\sum_{i}2d_{i}p_{i}\right)
has degree zero on each component and thus
\eta\in\Pic^{0}(C)
.
Since the group
\Pic^{0}(C)
is 2-divisible, for example by the description in (
4.6
), we can pick an element
\eta^{\prime}
such that
\eta^{\prime\tensor 2}=\eta
. Then
\eta^{\prime}\left(\sum_{i}d_{i}p_{i}\right)
is a regular ggs structure on
C
.

\square

We now collect the necessary information about ggs structures on the curves we are interested in.

Proposition 4.7
---

Let
C
be a Gorenstein curve of arithmetic genus two with
\omega_{C}
ample, bicanonical involution
\iota
and ggs structure
({\mathcal{L}},\mu)
. Then

(
i
)

{\mathcal{L}}
is invariant under the involution, that is,
\iota^{*}{\mathcal{L}}\cong{\mathcal{L}}
;

(
ii
)

h^{0}(C,{\mathcal{L}})\leq 2
and equality holds if and only if
C=C_{1}\cup C_{2}
is of Type
B
and
{\mathcal{L}}={\mathcal{O}}_{C_{1}}\oplus{\mathcal{O}}_{C_{2}}
is the pushforward of the unique ggs structure with a non-zero section on each component.

(
iii
)

\mu\colon{\mathcal{L}}\tensor{\mathcal{L}}\to\omega_{C}
is an isomorphism on the locus where
{\mathcal{L}}
is invertible.

Proof.

Let us write
{\mathcal{L}}=f_{*}\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}
for a regular ggs structure
(\widetilde{\mathcal{L}},\widetilde{\mu})
on a partial normalisation
f\colon\widetilde{C}\to C
. Recall that by Corollary
3.9
the curve
\widetilde{C}
is also Gorenstein.
The composition
f\circ\varphi:\widetilde{C}\to Q
is a degree 2 finite morphism, so it induces an involution

\widetilde{\iota}
on
\widetilde{C}
that lifts
\iota
.

We first observe that
\iota
acts trivially on
\Pic^{0}(\widetilde{C})[2]
. Indeed, considering the induced action on
H^{1}({\mathcal{O}}_{\widetilde{C}})\cong H^{0}(\widetilde{C},\omega_{\widetilde{C}})
and taking into account that
\widetilde{C}/\widetilde{\iota}
has arithmetic genus
0
we see
\widetilde{\iota}
acts on
H^{1}(\widetilde{C},{\mathcal{O}}_{\widetilde{C}})
as multiplication by
-1
. Then the claim follows from the exponential sequence.
It thus suffices to exhibit one regular
\widetilde{\iota}
-invariant ggs structure.

By the criterion in Proposition
4.5

(i)
the curve
\widetilde{C}
admits a regular ggs structure if and only if it is irreducible or
\deg\omega_{C}|_{C_{i}}
is even on both components.

So if
C
is of type B then
\widetilde{C}
is disconnected and its connected components are irreducible of genus zero or one (cf. Table
1
), so we can take the trivial bundle on components of genus one and the unique ggs structure on components of genus zero and the resulting ggs structure is obviously invariant.

Consider now
C
of type A. By Corollary
3.9
if
\widetilde{C}
is disconnected then it is the union of two smooth rational curves and we can take the unique ggs structure on each of them.
If
\widetilde{C}
is connected but reducible, then it is the union of two smooth rational curves, meeting either in two nodes or in an
A_{3}
point, so it has genus 1 and we can take the trivial ggs structure

{\mathcal{O}}_{\widetilde{C}}
on
\widetilde{C}
.
If
\widetilde{C}\neq C
is irreducible, then it has arithmetic genus at most one and admits either a trivial or a unique ggs structure, as above.

So we are left with the case when
C=\widetilde{C}
is irreducible.
If
\varphi\colon C\to C/\iota={\mathbb{P}}^{1}
admits a smooth branch point
p
, then the associated line bundle is a regular ggs structure, since
\omega_{C}=\varphi^{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}}(1)={\mathcal{O}}_{C}(2p)
. The only remaining case is when
\widetilde{C}
has two points of type
A_{2}
.
In this case,
b_{1}(C)=0
so by Proposition
4.5
there exists a unique ggs structure on
C
, which then has to be
\iota
-invariant.

We move on to the second item.
First observe that if
C
is a connected Gorenstein curve of genus
g\leq 2
, then for any regular ggs structure
{\mathcal{L}}
on
C
we have
h^{0}(C,{\mathcal{L}})\leq 1
, because
\deg{\mathcal{L}}=g-1
.

If
g=2
and
C
is reducible, then by Proposition
4.5
and the geometry of the curves (Proposition
3.3
) any ggs structure is irregular, so we consider the corresponding regular ggs
structure
(\widetilde{\mathcal{L}},\widetilde{\mu})
on a partial normalisation
\widetilde{C}
. If
\widetilde{C}
is connected, then
h^{0}(\widetilde{\mathcal{L}})\leq 1
by the above. If
\widetilde{C}
is disconnected, then both components have genus at most one, so
h^{0}(C,{\mathcal{L}})\leq 2
with equality if and only if
C=C_{1}\cup C_{2}
is of type
B
and
{\mathcal{L}}={\mathcal{O}}_{C_{1}}\oplus{\mathcal{O}}_{C_{2}}
is the pushforward of the unique ggs structure with a section on the components, because the unique regular ggs structure on
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
does not have a section.

(iii)
Follows from the fact that the points where
{\mathcal{L}}
is invertible are those where
\widetilde{C}\to C
is an isomorphism and from the fact that
\mu
is an isomorphism for regular ggs structures.

\square

We close this section with a technical result needed for the computations of Section
5
.

Lemma 4.8
---

Let
C
be a reduced connected Gorenstein curve of arithmetic genus two with
\omega_{C}
ample and ggs structure
({\mathcal{L}},\mu)
. Assume that
C
is reducible and let
B\subset C
be an irreducible component. Then
\deg({\mathcal{L}}{\raisebox{-0.1pt}{$|_{B}$}}^{[1]})\geq-1
and if equality holds then
p_{a}(B)=0
.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.3
we have
{\mathcal{L}}=f_{*}\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}
for a regular ggs structure
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}
on a partial normalisation
f\colon\widetilde{C}\to C
. Denote by
\widetilde{B}
the component of
\widetilde{C}
that maps to
B
and denote by
{\mathcal{I}}\subset{\mathcal{O}}_{\widetilde{C}}
the ideal of
\widetilde{B}
. Since
f
is finite, we have an exact sequence

(4.9)

0\to f_{*}({\mathcal{I}}\widetilde{\mathcal{L}})\to f_{*}\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}={\mathcal{L}}\to f_{*}(\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}{\raisebox{-0.1pt}{$|_{\widetilde{B}}$}})\to 0

The sheaf
f_{*}(\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}{\raisebox{-0.1pt}{$|_{\widetilde{B}}$}})
is torsion-free and supported on
B
, so the last map in the above equation gives a surjective map
{\mathcal{L}}{\raisebox{-0.1pt}{$|_{B}$}}^{[1]}\to f_{*}(\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}{\raisebox{-0.1pt}{$|_{\widetilde{B}}$}})
. Since both sheaves are torsion-free of rank one, the map is an isomorphism.

So we have
\chi({\mathcal{L}}{\raisebox{-0.1pt}{$|_{B}$}}^{[1]})=\chi({\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}{\raisebox{-0.1pt}{$|_{\widetilde{B}}$}})=\deg\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}{\raisebox{-0.1pt}{$|_{\widetilde{B}}$}}+1-p_{a}(\widetilde{B}})
and
2\deg(\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}{\raisebox{-0.1pt}{$|_{\widetilde{B}}$}})=\deg K_{\widetilde{C}}{\raisebox{-0.1pt}{$|_{\widetilde{B}}$}}
. Since there is an inclusion
\omega_{\widetilde{B}}\hookrightarrow\omega_{\widetilde{C}}
(cf. Lemma
2.4
), we get
\deg\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}{\raisebox{-0.1pt}{$|_{\widetilde{B}}$}}\geq p_{a}(\widetilde{B})-1\geq-1
, and if
\deg\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}{\raisebox{-0.1pt}{$|_{\widetilde{B}}$}}=-1
holds then
\widetilde{B}
is smooth rational.

Assume
\deg\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}{\raisebox{-0.1pt}{$|_{\widetilde{B}}$}}=-1
, hence
0=\chi(\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}{\raisebox{-0.1pt}{$|_{\widetilde{B}}$}})=\chi({\mathcal{L}}{\raisebox{-0.1pt}{$|_{B}$}}^{[1]})
: if
B
is not smooth rational then
p_{a}(B)=1
and
\deg{\mathcal{L}}{\raisebox{-0.1pt}{$|_{B}$}}^{[1]}=0
.
\square

Corollary 4.10
---

Let
C
be a reduced connected Gorenstein curve of arithmetic genus two with
\omega_{C}
ample and ggs structure
({\mathcal{L}},\mu)
. Then we have

H^{1}(C,{\mathcal{L}}\otimes\omega_{C}^{\otimes m})=0
for every
m\geq 1
.

Proof.

We have
\deg({\mathcal{L}}\otimes\omega_{C}^{\otimes m})=2m+1\geq 3=2p_{a}(C)-1
, since
\omega_{C}
is a line bundle of degree two. Therefore if
C
is irreducible we can conclude by Lemma
2.5
.

Assume now that
C
is reducible and let
B\subset C
be an irreducible component, so that
\omega_{C}{\raisebox{-0.1pt}{$|_{B}$}}
is a line bundle of degree one.
Then by the above Lemma
4.8
we have
\deg(({\mathcal{L}}\otimes\omega_{C}^{\otimes m}){\raisebox{-0.1pt}{$|_{B}$}}^{[1]})\geq m-1
if
p_{a}(B)=0
and
\geq m
if
p_{a}(B)=1
. Summing up, we have
\deg({\mathcal{L}}\otimes\omega_{C}^{\otimes m}){\raisebox{-0.1pt}{$|_{B}$}}^{[1]}\geq 2p_{a}(B)-1
for every
B\subseteq C
,
hence Lemma
2.5
gives
H^{1}({\mathcal{L}}\otimes\omega_{C})=0
.

\square

5.
Half-canonical rings

5.A.
Definition and classification of half-canonical rings

Definition 5.1
---

Let
C
be a projective and reduced Gorenstein curve and let

({\mathcal{L}},\mu)
be a ggs structure on
C
. Its half-canonical ring is

R(C,\{{\mathcal{L}},\omega_{C}\})=R(C,{\mathcal{L}})=\bigoplus_{n}R_{n},

where
R_{2n}=H^{0}(C,nK_{C})
and
R_{2n+1}=H^{0}(C,{\mathcal{L}}(nK_{C}))
and the multiplication of two elements of odd degree is defined via
\mu
.

Now let
({\mathcal{L}},\mu)

5.C.1.
Type

Here we prove that if

is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{C}}[x,y,z]/(z^{2}-f_{10})
, with
\deg(x,y,z)=(1,2,5)

Proof.

Choose a non-zero section

. Since

is reduced we have
x^{2}\neq 0
and thus we can choose the coordinates in the canonical ring such that
x^{2}=y_{1}
and let

.
We start by noting the following facts:

(a)

Multiplication by

gives an injective map
R(C,\{{\mathcal{L}},\omega_{C}\})\to R(C,\{{\mathcal{L}},\omega_{C}\})
. Indeed, we have seen in the proof of
Proposition
3.3
that a curve of type A either is irreducible, or it is the union of two smooth rational curves

and
C_{2}
and the restriction map

is an isomorphism for

. So

, and a fortiori

, does not vanish at the generic point of any component of

.

(b)

The map
j\colon{\mathbb{C}}[x,y]\to R(C,\{{\mathcal{L}},\omega_{C}\})
is injective. Let
v
be a nonzero element of
\ker j
: since
j
is a map of graded rings, we may assume that
v
is homogeneous. Then
\deg v
is odd, since the map
{\mathbb{C}}[x^{2},y]\to R(C,\omega_{C})
is an inclusion by Proposition
3.3
and we can write

with

homogeneous of even degree. By (a)

is in the kernel of

, a contradiction.

In view of fact (b) above, comparing dimensions we see that
{\mathbb{C}}[x,y]_{\leq 4}=R_{\leq 4}
and that

R_{5}=\langle x^{5},x^{3}y,xy^{2},z\rangle

for some section
z
.
The graded piece
R_{6}
contains
xz
and the four monomials of degree 6 in
x,y
. Assume by contradiction that there is a relation among these elements, so that
xf_{5}(x,y,z)=\lambda y^{3}
for some
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}
. If
\lambda=0
, then
f_{5}=0
by fact (a) and the linear relation is trivial. If
\lambda\neq 0
, then squaring the relation we obtain that
x^{2}
divides
y^{6}
, but this is impossible since
y
and
x^{2}
have no common zero on

.
So we see that we have no new generators in degree

, hence by Lemma
5.6
, we have found all generators of

.

Let

be as in Proposition
3.3
. We can write

with

and
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}
. Since

as in the proof of Proposition
3.3
we have

, with

, so up to changing

Now we study the relations. By Proposition
3.3
,
we may write

A basis of in

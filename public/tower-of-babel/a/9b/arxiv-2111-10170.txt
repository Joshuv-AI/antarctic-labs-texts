On a Class of Fully Nonlinear Curvature Flows in Hyperbolic Space
Fang Hong

Abstract
. In this paper, we study a class of flows of closed, star-shaped hypersurfaces in hyperbolic space ℍ n + 1 \mathbb{H}^{n+1} with speed ( sinh ⁡ r ) α / β ​ σ k 1 / β (\sinh r)^{{\alpha}/{\beta}}\sigma_{k}^{{1}/{\beta}} , where σ k \sigma_{k} is the k k -th elementary symmetric polynomial of the principal curvatures, α \alpha , β \beta are positive constants and r r is the distance from points on the hypersurface to the origin. We obtain convergence results under some assumptions of k k , α \alpha and β \beta . When k = 1 , α > 1 + β k=1,\alpha>1+\beta , and the initial hypersurface is mean convex, we prove that the mean convex solution to the flow for k = 1 k=1 exists for all time and converges smoothly to a sphere. When 1 ≤ k ≤ n , α > k + β 1\leq k\leq n,\alpha>k+\beta , and the initial hypersurface is uniformly convex, we prove that the uniformly convex solution to the flow exists for all time and converges smoothly to a sphere. In particular, we generalize Li-Sheng-Wang’s results in [ 10 ] from Euclidean space to hyperbolic space.

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{M}_{0}
be a smooth ,closed, and star-shaped hypersurface in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
which encloses the origin,
n\geq 2
. In this paper, we study the following geometric flow,

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \frac{\partial}{\partial t}X(x,t)&=-{\left(\phi(r)\right)}^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\sigma_{k}^{\frac{1}{\beta}}(\kappa)\nu(x,t)+\gamma V(x,t),\\
X(x,0)&=X_{0}(x)\in\mathcal{M}_{0},\end{aligned}\right.

where
\alpha,\beta
are positive constants,
\gamma=\tbinom{n}{k}
,
\sigma_{k}
is the
k
-curvature, given by

\sigma_{k}(\cdot,t)=\sum_{i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}}\kappa_{i_{1}}\cdots\kappa_{i_{k}}.

and
\kappa_{i}=\kappa_{i}(\cdot,t)
are the principal curvatures of the hypersurface
\mathcal{M}_{t}
, parametrized by
X(\cdot,t):\mathbb{S}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
, and
\nu(\cdot,t)
is the unit outer normal of
\mathcal{M}_{t}
at
X(\cdot,t)
. We denote by
r
the distance from the point
X(x,t)
to the origin,
\phi(r)=\sinh r
, and
V(x,t)=\phi(r)\frac{\partial}{\partial r}
is a conformal Killing vector field on
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
. We shall call
r
the radial function of
\mathcal{M}_{t}
in this paper.

The same kind of flow as (
1.1
) has been studied by Li, Sheng and Wang in the Euclidean space
[
10
]
. The purpose of this paper is to generalize their results from the Euclidean space to the hyperbolic space. The motivation of studying the flow (
1.1
) is its relationship with the prescribed curvature measure problem in hyperbolic space which has been studied recently by Fengrui Yang
[
13
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

(1)

We say that a smooth closed hypersurface
\mathcal{M}
is
k
-convex,
k=1,\cdots,n
, if at every point its principal curvatures satisfy
\kappa=(\kappa_{1},\cdots,\kappa_{n})\in\Gamma_{k}^{+}
, where

\Gamma_{k}^{+}:=\left\{(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\sigma_{i}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})>0,\text{ for all }1\leq i\leq k\right\},

and
\sigma_{k}
is the
k
-th elementary symmetric polynomial. For
k=1
,
1
-convex is also called mean convex.

(2)

We say that a smooth closed hypersurface
\mathcal{M}
is uniformly convex if its principal curvatures satisfy
\kappa_{i}>0
for all
i=1,\cdots,n
.

(3)

A hypersurface
\mathcal{M}
in the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
is called star-shaped (with respect to the origin), if its support function
u=\langle V,\nu\rangle
is positive everywhere, where
V=\phi(r)\frac{\partial}{\partial r}
.

1.1.
Main results

We now state our main results.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\mathcal{M}_{0}
be a smooth, closed, mean convex and star-shaped hypersurface in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
enclosing the origin. If
0<\beta\leq 1
,
\alpha>\beta+1
, the flow (
1.1
) with
k=1
has a unique smooth, mean convex and star-shaped solution
\mathcal{M}_{t}
for all time
t>0
. The flow converges exponentially to a sphere centered at the origin in the
C^{\infty}
topology.

Moreover, when
\mathcal{M}_{0}
is uniformly convex, we can prove the solution to (
1.1
) remains uniformly convex for all
k=1,\cdots,n
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\mathcal{M}_{0}
be a smooth, closed, uniformly convex star-shaped hypersurface in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
enclosing the origin. If
0<\beta\leq 1
,
\alpha>\beta+k
, the flow (
1.1
) has a unique smooth and uniformly convex solution
\mathcal{M}_{t}
for all time
t>0
. The flow converges exponentially to a sphere centered at the origin in the
C^{\infty}
topology.

In order to prove our main results in Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.3
, we shall establish the a priori estimates for the flow (
1.1
), and show that if
X(\cdot,t)
solves (
1.1
), then the radial function
r
converges exponentially to a constant as
t\rightarrow\infty
.

1.2.
Organization of the paper

This paper is organized as follows. In section
2
we collect some properties of star-shaped hypersurfaces, and show that the flow (
1.1
) can be reduced to a parabolic equation of the radial function. We will also derive the evolution equations for various geometric quantities in section
2
. In section
3
we establish the needed a priori estimates of the radial function
r
and its gradient
\bar{\nabla}r
of the solution
\mathcal{M}_{t}
. In section
4
we establish the needed a priori estimates of the
k
-curvature
\sigma_{k}
of the solution
\mathcal{M}_{t}
. In section
5
and section
6
we respectively prove the needed a priori estimates of the principal curvatures for mean convex and uniformly convex solutions. In section
7
we prove the main results of asymptotic convergence.

2.
Preliminaries

In this paper we view
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
as the warped product manifold with a coordinate chart homeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with Riemannian metric
g^{\mathbb{H}}=dr\otimes dr+(\phi(r))^{2}\bar{g}
, where
\bar{g}
is the standard metric on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
and
\phi(r)=\sinh r
.

2.1.
The vector field V

We denote the covariant derivative with respect to
g^{\mathbb{H}}
in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
by
D
. We have the following basic properties for the vector field
V=\phi(r)\partial_{r}
.

Lemma 2.1

(see
[
7
]
)
.

For any tangent vector field
X
on
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
, we have

D_{X}V=\phi^{\prime}(r)X.

Corollary 2.2
.

For any tangent vector field
X
on
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
, we have

D_{X}r=\frac{\left\langle X,V\right\rangle}{\phi(r)}.

where
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle
means the inner product of tangent vectors induced by
g^{\mathbb{H}}
.

Proof.

Since
V=\phi(r)\partial_{r}
,
\left\langle V,V\right\rangle=(\phi(r))^{2}
. Thus

D_{X}\left\langle V,V\right\rangle=D_{X}\left((\phi(r))^{2}\right)=2\phi(r)\phi^{\prime}(r)D_{X}r.

Meanwhile, by Lemma
2.1
, for the left hand side we have

D_{X}\left\langle V,V\right\rangle=2\left\langle D_{X}V,V\right\rangle=2\left\langle\phi^{\prime}(r)X,V\right\rangle=2\phi^{\prime}(r)\left\langle X,V\right\rangle.

Hence

D_{X}r=\frac{\left\langle X,V\right\rangle}{\phi(r)}
.
∎

2.2.
Elementary symmetric polynomials

For
n
variables
x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}
, their
k
th elementary symmetric polynomial is defined by

\sigma_{k}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq n}x_{i_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{n}},

Lemma 2.3

(Newton-Maclaurin’s inequalities)
.

When
(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})\in\Gamma_{m-1}^{+}
, for
l,m
such that
1\leq l<m\leq n
, we have

(2.1)

\sigma_{m}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})\leq\tbinom{n}{m}\left(\frac{\sigma_{l}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})}{\tbinom{n}{l}}\right)^{{m}/{l}}.

Equality holds if and only if
(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})=c(1,\cdots,1)
for some constant
c>0
.

Lemma 2.4

(see
[
6
]
)
.

For a real symmetric matrix
A=(a_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq n}
, we write
\sigma_{k}(A)=\sigma_{k}\left(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}\right)
, where
\left(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}\right)
are the eigenvalues of A. We write

\dot{\sigma_{k}}^{ij}=\frac{\partial\sigma_{k}}{\partial a_{ij}}
.
When the eigenvalues of A belong to
\Gamma_{k}^{+}
,
k=1,\cdots,n
, we have that
(\dot{\sigma_{k}}^{ij})
is positive definite.

In the most case of the following paper, by using
\sigma_{k}
, we mean the
k
th elementary symmetric polynomial of the principal curvatures, that is, locally, eigenvalues of the matrix
\left(h_{i}^{j}\right)
.
For consideration of the consistency of superscripts and subscripts, by using the notation
\dot{\sigma_{k}}^{ij}
, we mean

\dot{\sigma_{k}}^{ij}=g^{jl}\frac{\partial\sigma_{k}}{\partial h_{i}^{l}}
, and similarly we can define second derivatives by
\ddot{\sigma_{k}}^{pq,rs}=g^{ql}g^{sm}\frac{\partial^{2}\sigma_{k}}{\partial h_{p}^{l}\partial h_{r}^{m}}
.

For general functions on the eigenvalues of a real symmetric matrix, such as
F=(\sigma_{k})^{\frac{1}{\beta}}
, we also write

\dot{F}^{ij}=g^{jl}\frac{\partial F}{\partial h_{i}^{l}},\text{ and }\ddot{F}^{pq,rs}=g^{ql}g^{sm}\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial h_{p}^{l}\partial h_{r}^{m}}.

We recall the following result:

Lemma 2.5

(see
[
6
]
)
.

\displaystyle\dot{\sigma_{k}}^{ij}(h^{2})_{ij}=

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}\sigma_{k}-(k+1)\sigma_{k+1},

\displaystyle\dot{\sigma_{k}}^{ij}h_{ij}=

\displaystyle k\sigma_{k},

\displaystyle\dot{\sigma_{k}}^{ij}g_{ij}=

\displaystyle(n-k+1)\sigma_{k-1},

where
(h^{2})_{ij}=h^{ik}h_{kj}
.

For
F=(\sigma_{k})^{\frac{1}{\beta}}
, we have
\dot{F}^{ij}=\frac{1}{\beta}(\sigma_{k})^{\frac{1}{\beta}-1}\dot{\sigma_{k}}^{ij}
and

Corollary 2.6
.

\displaystyle\dot{F}^{ij}(h^{2})_{ij}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta}(\sigma_{k})^{\frac{1}{\beta}-1}\left(\sigma_{1}\sigma_{k}-(k+1)\sigma_{k+1}\right),

\displaystyle\dot{F}^{ij}h_{ij}=

\displaystyle\frac{k}{\beta}(\sigma_{k})^{\frac{1}{\beta}},

\displaystyle\dot{F}^{ij}g_{ij}=

\displaystyle\frac{n-k+1}{\beta}(\sigma_{k})^{\frac{1}{\beta}-1}\sigma_{k-1}.

2.3.
Radial function and radial graph

Let
e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}
be a smooth local orthonormal frame field on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
, and let
\bar{\nabla}
be the covariant derivative on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. We denote by
g_{ij},g^{ij},\nu,h_{ij}
the metric on
\mathcal{M}_{t}
induced by the metric of
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
, the inverse of the metric, the unit outer normal and the second fundamental form of
\mathcal{M}_{t}
, respectively. Then, in terms of
r
, we have

(2.2)

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=\frac{\phi^{2}}{\sqrt{\phi^{2}+|\bar{\nabla}r|^{2}}}

\displaystyle g_{ij}

\displaystyle=\phi^{2}\delta_{ij}+r_{i}r_{j},\quad g^{ij}=\frac{1}{\phi^{2}}\left(\delta^{ij}-\frac{r_{i}r_{j}}{\phi^{2}+|\bar{\nabla}r|^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle h_{ij}

\displaystyle=\left(\sqrt{\phi^{2}+|\bar{\nabla}r|^{2}}\right)^{-1}\left(-\phi\bar{\nabla}_{i}\bar{\nabla}_{j}r+2\phi^{\prime}r_{i}r_{j}+\phi^{2}\phi^{\prime}\delta_{ij}\right)

\displaystyle h_{j}^{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\phi^{2}\sqrt{\phi^{2}+|\bar{\nabla}r|^{2}}}\left(\delta^{ik}-\frac{r_{i}r_{k}}{\phi^{2}+|\bar{\nabla}r|^{2}}\right)\left(-\phi\bar{\nabla}_{k}\bar{\nabla}_{j}r+2\phi^{\prime}r_{k}r_{j}+\phi^{2}\phi^{\prime}\delta_{kj}\right),

where we denote
r_{i}=\bar{\nabla}_{i}(r)
. These formulae can be found in a number of papers, see, for example
[
7
]
.

It will be convenient if we introduce a new variable
\varphi=\varphi(r)
satisfying

(2.3)

\frac{\mathrm{d}\varphi}{\mathrm{d}r}=\frac{1}{\phi(r)}.

Let
\omega:=\sqrt{1+|\bar{\nabla}\varphi|^{2}}
, one can calculate the unit outward normal

\nu=\frac{1}{\omega}\left(1,-\frac{r_{1}}{\phi^{2}},\ldots,-\frac{r_{n}}{\phi^{2}}\right)

and the general support function
u=\langle V,\nu\rangle=\frac{\phi}{\omega}
. Then we can conclude how the radial function evolves along the flow.

Lemma 2.7
.

The star-shaped solution of the flow

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}X(x,t)=-{\left({\phi(r)}\right)}^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\sigma_{k}^{\frac{1}{\beta}}(\kappa)\nu(x,t)+\gamma V(x,t)

is equivalent to a solution to the scalar parabolic PDE of the radial function
r
:

(2.4)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}r=-{\left({\phi(r)}\right)}^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\sigma_{k}(\kappa)^{\frac{1}{\beta}}\omega+\gamma\phi.

Proof.

Up to a tangential diffeomorphism, the flow (
1.1
) is equvalent to the following flow

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}X(x,t)=\left(-{\left({\phi(r)}\right)}^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\sigma_{k}(\kappa)^{\frac{1}{\beta}}+\gamma u\right)\nu(x,t).

By
[
5
]
, It is known that if a closed hypersurface which is a radial graph satisfies

\partial_{t}X=f\nu,

then the evolution of the scalar function
r
satisfies

\partial_{t}r=f\omega.

Thus

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}r=\left(-{\left({\phi(r)}\right)}^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\sigma_{k}(\kappa)^{\frac{1}{\beta}}+\gamma u\right)\omega=-{\left({\phi(r)}\right)}^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\sigma_{k}(\kappa)^{\frac{1}{\beta}}\omega+\gamma\phi.

∎

2.4.
Evolution of geometric quantities

For convenience, we rewrite (
1.1
) as

(2.5)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}X(x,t)=-\Phi\nu(x,t)+\gamma V(x,t).

where
\gamma=\tbinom{n}{k}
and

(2.6)

\Phi={\left({\phi(r)}\right)}^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\sigma_{k}(\kappa)^{\frac{1}{\beta}}.

We now derive some evolution equations along the flow (
2.5
). Pick any local coordinate chart
\left\{x_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{n}
of the hypersurface
\mathcal{M}_{t}
. We denote
\nabla
to be the covariant derivative on
\mathcal{M}_{t}
induced by the covariant derivative
D
in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
. We denote
g_{ij}
and
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle
to be respectively the metric on
\mathcal{M}_{t}
induced by
g^{\mathbb{H}}
and the induced inner product of tangent vectors. We denote
\partial_{i}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}},X_{i}=\partial_{i}X
. Recall the following identities in hyperbolic space, see
[
2
]
,
[
4
]
.

(2.7)

\displaystyle D_{X_{i}}X_{j}

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{ij}^{k}X_{k}-h_{ij}\nu,

(Gauss formula)

(2.8)

\displaystyle\nu_{i}

\displaystyle=h_{ij}g^{jl}X_{l},

(Weingarten formula)

(2.9)

\displaystyle h_{ij,l}

\displaystyle=h_{il,j},

(Codazzi equation)

(2.10)

\displaystyle R_{ijrs}

\displaystyle=h_{ir}h_{js}-h_{is}h_{jr}-g_{ir}g_{js}+g_{is}g_{jr},

(Gauss equation)

where
\Gamma_{ij}^{k}
is the Christoffel symbol of the metric of
\mathcal{M}_{t}
. Combining the Gauss and Codazzi equations gives the following generalization of Simons’ identity, we have

(2.11)

\nabla_{(i}\nabla_{j)}h_{kl}=\nabla_{(k}\nabla_{l)}h_{ij}+h_{ij}(h^{2})_{kl}-h_{kl}(h^{2})_{ij}-g_{ij}h_{kl}+g_{kl}h_{ij}

where
(h^{2})_{ij}=h_{i}^{k}h_{kj}
and
A_{(ab)}
means the symmetrization of the tensor
A_{ab}
, that is,
A_{(ab)}=\frac{1}{2}\left(A_{ab}+A_{ba}\right)
.

Lemma 2.8
.

Along the flow (
2.5
), we have the following evolution equations

(2.12)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}g_{ij}=-2\Phi h_{ij}+2\gamma\phi^{\prime}g_{ij},\quad\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\nu=\nabla\Phi.

(2.13)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}u=-\phi^{\prime}\Phi+\phi^{\prime}\gamma u+\langle V,\nabla\Phi\rangle.

(2.14)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}h_{ij}=\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\Phi-\Phi h_{i}^{l}h_{lj}+\gamma\phi^{\prime}h_{ij}+(\gamma u-\Phi)g_{ij}.

Here
\nabla
denotes the covariant derivative on
\mathcal{M}_{t}
.

Proof.

By direct calculations using Lemma
2.1
and the equation (
2.8
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}g_{ij}

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}\left\langle\partial_{i}X,\partial_{j}X\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\nabla_{i}(-\Phi\nu+\gamma V),\partial_{j}X\right\rangle+\left\langle\partial_{i}X,\nabla_{j}(-\Phi\nu+\gamma V)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\Phi\left(\left\langle\partial_{i}\nu,\partial_{j}X\right\rangle+\left\langle\partial_{i}X,\partial_{j}\nu\right\rangle\right)+2\gamma\phi^{\prime}g_{ij}

\displaystyle=-2\Phi h_{ij}+2\gamma\phi^{\prime}g_{ij}.

Since
\partial_{t}\nu
is tangential, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\nu=\left\langle\partial_{t}\nu,\nabla_{j}X\right\rangle g^{il}\partial_{l}X

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left\langle\nu,\nabla_{j}(-\Phi\nu+\gamma V)\right\rangle g^{jl}\partial_{l}X=\nabla_{j}\Phi g^{jl}\partial_{l}X=\nabla\Phi.

Now we calculate the evolution of the support function
u

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}u=\partial_{t}\langle V,\nu\rangle=\langle\partial_{t}V,\nu\rangle+\langle V,\partial_{t}\nu\rangle=\langle\phi^{\prime}\partial_{t}X,\nu\rangle+\langle V,\partial_{t}\nu\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\phi^{\prime}(-\Phi\nu+\gamma V),\nu\rangle+\langle V,\nabla\Phi\rangle=-\phi^{\prime}\Phi+\phi^{\prime}\gamma u+\langle V,\nabla\Phi\rangle.

Now we calculate the evolution of
h_{ij}
. Note that
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
is of constant sectional curvature of
K=-1
, thus

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}h_{ij}=-{\partial_{t}}\left\langle D_{X_{i}}X_{j},\nu\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left\langle D_{X_{t}}D_{X_{i}}X_{j},\nu\right\rangle-\left\langle D_{X_{i}}X_{j},\partial_{t}\nu\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left\langle D_{X_{i}}D_{X_{t}}X_{j}+\bar{R}\left(X_{t},X_{i}\right)X_{j},\nu\right\rangle-\left\langle D_{X_{i}}X_{j},\nabla\Phi\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left\langle D_{X_{i}}D_{X_{j}}X_{t},\nu\right\rangle-\left\langle\bar{R}\left(X_{t},X_{i}\right)X_{j},\nu\right)-\left\langle D_{X_{i}}X_{j},g^{kl}\partial_{l}\Phi X_{k}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left\langle D_{X_{i}}D_{X_{j}}(-\Phi\nu+\gamma V),\nu\right\rangle+\left\langle X_{t},\nu\right\rangle\left\langle X_{i},X_{j}\right\rangle-\Gamma_{ij}^{k}\partial_{k}\Phi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left\langle D_{X_{i}}\left(-\partial_{j}\Phi\nu-\Phi h_{j}^{l}X_{l}+\gamma\phi^{\prime}X_{j}\right),\nu\right\rangle+g_{ij}(\gamma u-\Phi)-\Gamma_{ij}^{k}\partial_{k}\Phi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\Phi-\Gamma_{ij}^{k}\partial_{k}\Phi-\Phi h_{j}^{l}h_{l_{i}}+\gamma{\phi}^{\prime}h_{ij}+g_{ij}(\gamma u-\Phi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\Phi-\Phi h_{j}^{l}h_{l_{i}}+\gamma{\phi}^{\prime}h_{ij}+g_{ij}(\gamma u-\Phi),

where
\bar{R}
denotes the curvature tensor of the metric on
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
.
∎

Corollary 2.9
.

Along the flow (
2.5
), we have the following evolution equations

(2.15)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}g^{ij}=2\Phi h^{ij}-2\gamma\phi^{\prime}g^{ij}.

(2.16)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}h_{i}^{j}=\nabla_{i}\nabla^{j}\Phi+\Phi h_{i}^{k}h_{k}^{j}-\gamma\phi^{\prime}h_{i}^{j}+(\gamma u-\Phi)\delta_{i}^{j}.

Proof.

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}g^{ij}=-g^{il}\left(\partial_{t}g_{lm}\right)g^{mj}=2\Phi h^{ij}-2\gamma\phi^{\prime}g^{ij}.

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}h_{i}^{j}

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}h_{il}g^{lj}+h_{il}\partial_{t}g^{lj}

\displaystyle=\left(\nabla_{i}\nabla_{l}\Phi-\Phi h_{i}^{k}h_{kl}+\gamma\phi^{\prime}h_{il}+(\gamma u-\Phi)g_{il}\right)g^{lj}+h_{il}\left(2\Phi h^{lj}-2\gamma\phi^{\prime}g^{lj}\right)

\displaystyle=\nabla_{i}\nabla^{j}\Phi+\Phi h_{i}^{k}h_{k}^{j}-\gamma\phi^{\prime}h_{i}^{j}+(\gamma u-\Phi)\delta_{i}^{j}.

∎

Now we calculate
\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\Phi
more precisely. Recall that
\Phi=\phi^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}F
, where
F=\sigma_{k}^{\frac{1}{\beta}}
.

Lemma 2.10
.

\displaystyle\nabla_{(i}\nabla_{j)}\Phi=

\displaystyle(\phi(r))^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\dot{F}^{pq}\nabla_{p}\nabla_{q}h_{ij}+(\phi(r))^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\ddot{F}^{pq,rs}h_{rs,i}h_{pq,j}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\right)^{2}\frac{(\phi^{\prime}(r))^{2}}{(\phi(r))^{2}}(\nabla_{i}r)(\nabla_{j}r)\Phi-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\frac{1}{(\phi(r))^{2}}(\nabla_{i}r)(\nabla_{j}r)\Phi-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\frac{\phi^{\prime}(r)}{(\phi(r))^{2}}uh_{ij}\Phi

\displaystyle+\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\frac{(\phi^{\prime}(r))^{2}}{(\phi(r))^{2}}\left(g_{ij}-(\nabla_{i}r)(\nabla_{j}r)\right)\Phi+2\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\phi^{\prime}(r)(\phi(r))^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-1}(\nabla_{(i}r)(\nabla_{j)}F)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\beta}(\phi(r))^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}(\sigma_{k})^{\frac{1}{\beta}-1}\left(\sigma_{1}\sigma_{k}-(k+1)\sigma_{k+1}\right)h_{ij}-\frac{k}{\beta}\Phi\left(h^{2}\right)_{ij}

(2.17)

\displaystyle-\frac{k}{\beta}\Phi g_{ij}+\frac{n-k+1}{\beta}(\sigma_{k})^{\frac{1}{\beta}-1}(\phi(r))^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\sigma_{k-1}h_{ij}.

Proof.

Firstly

\displaystyle\nabla_{i}\Phi=

\displaystyle\nabla_{i}\left((\phi(r))^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}F\right)=\frac{\alpha}{\beta}(\phi(r))^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-1}\phi^{\prime}(r)(\nabla_{i}r)F+(\phi(r))^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\nabla_{i}F

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\frac{\phi^{\prime}(r)}{\phi(r)}\Phi\nabla_{i}r+(\phi(r))^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\nabla_{i}F.

Therefore,

(2.18)

\displaystyle\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\Phi=

\displaystyle\nabla_{i}\left(\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\frac{\phi^{\prime}(r)}{\phi(r)}\Phi\nabla_{j}r+(\phi(r))^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\nabla_{j}F\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\frac{1}{(\phi(r))^{2}}(\nabla_{i}r)(\nabla_{j}r)\Phi+\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\frac{\phi^{\prime}(r)}{\phi(r)}(\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}r)\Phi

Lemma 3.1

By (

∎

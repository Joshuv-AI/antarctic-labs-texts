EKMP: Generalized Imitation Learning with Adaptation, Nonlinear Hard Constraints and Obstacle Avoidance
Yanlong Huang

Abstract
As a user-friendly and straightforward solution for robot trajectory generation, imitation learning has been viewed as a vital direction in the context of robot skill learning. In contrast to unconstrained imitation learning which ignores possible internal and external constraints arising from environments and robot kinematics/dynamics, recent works on constrained imitation learning allow for transferring human skills to unstructured scenarios, further enlarging the application domain of imitation learning. While various constraints have been studied, e.g., joint limits, obstacle avoidance and plane constraints, the problem of nonlinear hard constraints has not been well-addressed. In this paper, we propose extended kernelized movement primitives (EKMP) to cope with most of the key problems in imitation learning, including nonlinear hard constraints. Specifically, EKMP is capable of learning the probabilistic features of multiple demonstrations, adapting the learned skills towards arbitrary desired points in terms of joint position and velocity, avoiding obstacles at the level of robot links, as well as satisfying arbitrary linear and nonlinear, equality and inequality hard constraints. Besides, the connections between EKMP and state-of-the-art motion planning approaches are discussed. Several evaluations including the planning of joint trajectories for a 7-DoF robotic arm are provided to verify the effectiveness of our framework.

\displaystyle=(1-\frac{\lambda}{\beta})\bm{\mathrm{\Theta}}^{{\!\scriptscriptstyle\top}}(t)\bm{\mathrm{w}}^{c}-\frac{1}{\beta}\bigl(\bm{\mathrm{\Theta}}^{{\!\scriptscriptstyle\top}}(t)\bm{\mathrm{\Phi}}\bm{\mathrm{\Sigma}}^{-1}(\bm{\mathrm{\Phi}}^{{\!\scriptscriptstyle\top}}\bm{\mathrm{w}}^{c}\!-\!\bm{\mathrm{\mu}})

\displaystyle+\lambda_{obs}\bm{\mathrm{\Theta}}^{{\!\scriptscriptstyle\top}}(t)\widetilde{\bm{\mathrm{\Phi}}}{\bm{\mathrm{H}}^{c}}-\bm{\mathrm{\Theta}}^{{\!\scriptscriptstyle\top}}(t)\bm{\mathrm{\Phi}}{\bm{\mathrm{G}}}^{c}\bm{\mathrm{\alpha}}^{c}\bigr).

Again, by employing the kernel trick, we have

\displaystyle\bm{\mathrm{k}}(t)

\displaystyle=\bm{\mathrm{\Theta}}^{{\!\scriptscriptstyle\top}}(t)\bm{\mathrm{\Phi}}=\left[\bm{\mathrm{k}}(t,t_{1})\ \bm{\mathrm{k}}(t,t_{2})\ \cdots\ \bm{\mathrm{k}}(t,t_{N})\right],

\displaystyle\widetilde{\bm{\mathrm{k}}}(t)

\displaystyle=\bm{\mathrm{\Theta}}^{{\!\scriptscriptstyle\top}}(t)\widetilde{\bm{\mathrm{\Phi}}}=\left[\bm{\mathrm{k}}(t,\tilde{t}_{1})\ \bm{\mathrm{k}}(t,\tilde{t}_{2})\ \cdots\ \bm{\mathrm{k}}(t,\tilde{t}_{M})\right].

\displaystyle\bm{\mathrm{\xi}}(t)=(1-\frac{\lambda}{\beta})\bm{\mathrm{\xi}}^{c}(t)-\frac{1}{\beta}\biggl(\bm{\mathrm{k}}(t)\bm{\mathrm{\Sigma}}^{-1}({\bm{\mathrm{\xi}}}^{c}-\bm{\mathrm{\mu}})

(24)

\displaystyle+\lambda_{obs}\widetilde{\bm{\mathrm{k}}}(t)\bm{\mathrm{H}}^{c}-\bm{\mathrm{k}}(t){\bm{\mathrm{G}}}^{c}\bm{\mathrm{\alpha}}^{c}\biggr).

II-E

Special Cases of EKMP

We have derived EKMP under nonlinear hard constraints.
Now, we consider the applications of EKMP to deal with some special cases.

Nonlinear equality constraints
: we can use inequalities to guarantee equality constraints with a tiny approximation error
\epsilon>0
, i.e.,
{g}_{n,f}(\bm{\mathrm{\xi}}(t_{n}))=0
is enforced by
{g}_{n,f}(\bm{\mathrm{\xi}}(t_{n}))+\epsilon\geq 0
and
-{g}_{n,f}(\bm{\mathrm{\xi}}(t_{n}))+\epsilon\geq 0
.

Adaptations towards desired points
:
an important feature in imitation learning is the adaptation of learned trajectories towards arbitrary desired points in terms of joint position and velocity,
including start-, via- and end-points. Assuming that

L
desired points, denoted by
\{\bar{t}_{l},\bar{\bm{\mathrm{\xi}}}_{l}\}_{l=1}^{L}
with
\bar{\bm{\mathrm{\xi}}}_{l}=[\bar{\bm{\mathrm{q}}}_{l}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\top}\ \bar{\dot{\bm{\mathrm{q}}}}_{l}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\top}]^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\top}\in\mathcal{R}^{2\mathcal{O}}
, are required in a task.
For each desired point
\{\bar{t}_{l},\bar{\bm{\mathrm{\xi}}}_{l}\}
we can separately define constraints as

\displaystyle{g}_{l,f}(\bm{\mathrm{\xi}}(t_{l}))

\displaystyle=\bm{\mathrm{1}}_{f}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\top}(\bm{\mathrm{\xi}}(t_{l})-\bar{\bm{\mathrm{\xi}}}_{l})+\epsilon\geq 0,

(29)

\displaystyle{g}_{l,2\mathcal{O}+f}(\bm{\mathrm{\xi}}(t_{l}))

\displaystyle=-\bm{\mathrm{1}}_{f}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\top}(\bm{\mathrm{\xi}}(t_{l})-\bar{\bm{\mathrm{\xi}}}_{l})+\epsilon\geq 0

for
\forall f\in\{1,2,\ldots,2\mathcal{O}\}
, where
\epsilon>0
is used to control the adaptation precision, i.e., how precisely the adapted trajectory can go through the desired point
\bar{\bm{\mathrm{\xi}}}_{l}
at time
\bar{t}_{l}
.
\bm{\mathrm{1}}_{f}\in\mathcal{R}^{2\mathcal{O}}
is an indicative vector with all elements being zero except for its
f
-
th
element.

Linear constraints
: we can simply write

{g}_{n,f}(\bm{\mathrm{\xi}}(t_{n}))=\bm{\mathrm{\theta}}^{{\!\scriptscriptstyle\top}}\bm{\mathrm{\xi}}(t_{n})+b\geq 0,

(30)

where
\bm{\mathrm{\theta}}
and
b
are constant, representing coefficients of linear constraints. Note that a
plane constraint
can be ensured by
\bm{\mathrm{\theta}}^{{\!\scriptscriptstyle\top}}\bm{\mathrm{\xi}}(t_{n})+b+\epsilon\geq 0
and
-\bm{\mathrm{\theta}}^{{\!\scriptscriptstyle\top}}\bm{\mathrm{\xi}}(t_{n})-b+\epsilon\geq 0
. In the case of
joint position limits
, for each joint
i
with motion range
[q_{i}^{min},q_{i}^{max}]
, its position limit can be formulated as
\bm{\mathrm{1}}_{i}^{{\!\scriptscriptstyle\top}}\bm{\mathrm{\xi}}(t_{n})-q_{i}^{min}\geq 0
and
-\bm{\mathrm{1}}_{i}^{{\!\scriptscriptstyle\top}}\bm{\mathrm{\xi}}(t_{n})+q_{i}^{max}\geq 0
. Similarly,
joint velocity limits
can also be imposed.

III-A

Comparison with CHOMP

Since in practice the implementation of CHOMP updates discrete waypoints of a trajectory
[
19
]
, we discretize
the update rule of EKMP (i.e., (
24
)) to establish the connection.
Supposing that we describe a trajectory by using its waypoints at a series of time steps
\{t_{n}\}_{n=1}^{N}
. By using (
24
), we update each waypoint as

\displaystyle\underbrace{\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\bm{\mathrm{\xi}}(t_{1})\\
\bm{\mathrm{\xi}}(t_{2})\\
\vdots\\
\bm{\mathrm{\xi}}(t_{N})\\
\end{array}\right]}_{\bm{\mathrm{\xi}}}=(1-\frac{\lambda}{\beta})\underbrace{\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\bm{\mathrm{\xi}}^{c}(t_{1})\\
\bm{\mathrm{\xi}}^{c}(t_{2})\\
\vdots\\
\bm{\mathrm{\xi}}^{c}(t_{N})\\
\end{array}\right]}_{\bm{\mathrm{\xi}}^{c}}

(31)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\beta}\bigl(\bm{\mathrm{K}}\bm{\mathrm{\Sigma}}^{-1}\bigl(\bm{\mathrm{\xi}}^{c}-\bm{\mathrm{\mu}}\bigr)+\lambda_{obs}\widetilde{\bm{\mathrm{K}}}\bm{\mathrm{H}}^{c}-\bm{\mathrm{K}}{\bm{\mathrm{G}}}^{c}\bm{\mathrm{\alpha}}^{c}\bigr),

which can be further simplified by letting
\widetilde{\bm{\mathrm{K}}}=\bm{\mathrm{K}}
, yielding

\bm{\mathrm{\xi}}=(1-\frac{\lambda}{\beta})\bm{\mathrm{\xi}}^{c}-\frac{1}{\beta}\bm{\mathrm{K}}\bigl(\bm{\mathrm{\Sigma}}^{-1}(\bm{\mathrm{\xi}}^{c}-\bm{\mathrm{\mu}})+\lambda_{obs}\bm{\mathrm{H}}^{c}-\bm{\mathrm{G}}^{c}\bm{\mathrm{\alpha}}^{c}\bigr).

(32)

If we let
\lambda=0
,
\bm{\mathrm{\mu}}=\bm{\mathrm{0}}
and

\bm{\mathrm{\alpha}}^{c}=\bm{\mathrm{0}}
, (
32
) will become

\bm{\mathrm{\xi}}=\bm{\mathrm{\xi}}^{c}-\frac{1}{\beta}\underbrace{\bm{\mathrm{K}}\bigl(\bm{\mathrm{\Sigma}}^{-1}\bm{\mathrm{\xi}}^{c}+\lambda_{obs}\bm{\mathrm{H}}^{c}\bigr)}_{update\,\,term},

(33)

which shares a similar form with the update rule in CHOMP
3
3

3

CHOMP also considered an additional vector dealing with boundary conditions in its update rule.
.
However, in CHOMP the coefficient of
\bm{\mathrm{\xi}}^{c}
in the update term is
\bm{\mathrm{K}}\bm{\mathrm{K}}^{-1}=\bm{\mathrm{I}}
rather than
\bm{\mathrm{K}}\bm{\mathrm{\Sigma}}^{-1}
. Moreover,

\bm{\mathrm{K}}
in CHOMP is defined using the finite difference matrix in order to ensure the smoothness of a trajectory, while
\bm{\mathrm{K}}
(i.e., (
20
)) in EKMP is a kernel matrix. Note that hard equality constraints were addressed in
[
17
]
, but without considering nonlinear inequality constraints.

III-B

Comparison with GPMP

Let us continue with (
32
) and if we let
\lambda=0
and neglect nonlinear hard constraints (i.e.,
\bm{\mathrm{\alpha}}^{c}=\bm{\mathrm{0}}
), (
32
) can be rewritten as

\bm{\mathrm{\xi}}=\bm{\mathrm{\xi}}^{c}-\frac{1}{\beta}\underbrace{\bm{\mathrm{K}}\bigl(\bm{\mathrm{\Sigma}}^{-1}(\bm{\mathrm{\xi}}^{c}-\bm{\mathrm{\mu}})+\lambda_{obs}\bm{\mathrm{H}}^{c}\bigr)}_{update\,\,term},

(34)

which resembles the update rule in GPMP
4
4

4

A projection matrix imposed on the gradient of the obstacle avoidance cost was also considered in GPMP.
. However, in GPMP the coefficient of
\bm{\mathrm{\xi}}^{c}-\bm{\mathrm{\mu}}
in the update term is
\bm{\mathrm{K}}\bm{\mathrm{K}}^{-1}=\bm{\mathrm{I}}
rather than
\bm{\mathrm{K}}\bm{\mathrm{\Sigma}}^{-1}
. Specifically,
\bm{\mathrm{K}}
in GPMP is obtained from a stochastic differential equation and has a specific form related to state transition matrix as well as noise distribution. In contrast,
\bm{\mathrm{K}}
in EKMP is a generic kernel function (e.g., Gaussian kernel and periodic kernel), which can be chosen depending on task requirements.

The advantage of using
\bm{\mathrm{\Sigma}}^{-1}
to weigh

\bm{\mathrm{\xi}}^{c}-\bm{\mathrm{\mu}}
in EKMP is that the importance of demonstrations can be incorporated into the process of updating trajectories, i.e., datapoints with small covariance (large consistency implies high importance) will have larger impact on trajectory update than those with large covariance. Similar insights have been discussed in previous works but in the context of unconstrained imitation learning, e.g.,
[
6
,
14
]
.
Note that GPMP was extended in
[
21
]
to encapsulate the prior distribution of demonstrations, where hard constraints were also neglected.

In addition, in terms of the trajectory update form,
[
18
,
21
]
optimizes trajectory waypoints while in EKMP, as shown in (
24
), the trajectory function is updated. Specifically, unlike
[
18
,
21
]
,
in EKMP the derivative relationship between the predicted joint position and velocity can be ensured over the course of optimizing (
6
), since the update rule (
24
) is derived from the parametric form (
2
) and the corresponding derivative relationship is encoded in
\bm{\mathrm{k}}(\cdot,\cdot)
(i.e., (
21
)).

III-C

Comparison with RKHS Motion Planning

Since RKHS motion planning
[
19
]
directly updates trajectory function, we compare it with the update rule in (
24
). In fact, if we do not consider imitation learning (i.e., setting
\bm{\mathrm{\Sigma}}^{-1}(\bm{\mathrm{\xi}}^{c}-\bm{\mathrm{\mu}})=\bm{\mathrm{0}}
) and replace nonlinear constraints with joint position and velocity limits (i.e., letting
F=2\mathcal{O}
and
\bm{\mathrm{G}}=-\bm{\mathrm{I}}
), (
24
) will become

\displaystyle\bm{\mathrm{\xi}}(\cdot)=(1-\frac{\lambda}{\beta})\bm{\mathrm{\xi}}^{c}(\cdot)-\frac{1}{\beta}\bigl(\lambda_{obs}\widetilde{\bm{\mathrm{k}}}(\cdot)\bm{\mathrm{H}}^{c}+\bm{\mathrm{k}}(\cdot)\bm{\mathrm{\alpha}}^{c}\bigr),

(35)

which has the same form as the update rule in
[
19
]
, except that
\bm{\mathrm{k}}
and
\widetilde{\bm{\mathrm{k}}}
have different definitions due to the simultaneous learning of
\bm{\mathrm{q}}
and
\dot{\bm{\mathrm{q}}}
in EKMP, and
\bm{\mathrm{H}}^{c}
includes a zero vector due to the optimization of
\dot{\bm{\mathrm{q}}}
. Note that the constraints of desired starting and ending joint positions in
[
19
]
can be handled by inequality constraints (see Section
II-E
).

Summary

: EKMP can be viewed as a generalization of
[
17
,
18
,
19
]
towards imitation learning as well as nonlinear hard (in particular inequality) constraints. Moreover, EKMP can learn both joint position and velocity simultaneously while maintaining the corresponding derivative relationship even under nonlinear hard constraints and obstacle avoidance requirement. Specifically, in contrast to
[
17
,
18
]
that optimize trajectory waypoints, EKMP updates the trajectory function, sharing the same advantages as
[
19
]
, including fast convergence and smooth trajectories.
For the discussion of advantages of optimizing trajectory function over trajectory waypoints, please refer to
[
19
]
.

V
Conclusions

We have extended KMP towards a more flexible framework capable of dealing with a bunch of key problems in imitation learning, including learning and adaptation, obstacle avoidance, linear and nonlinear hard constraints. Several evaluations including 2-D writing tasks and joint trajectory planning for reaching tasks verified the performance of our framework. While other constraints such as stiffness and orientation are also important in many scenarios, it would be of interest to address them within
the current framework.

Local variants of the Dixmier property and weak centrality for C*-algebras
Robert J. Archbold, Ilja Gogić, Leonel Robert

Abstract
. We study variants of the Dixmier property that apply to elements of a unital C*-algebra, rather than to the C*-algebra itself. By a Dixmier element in a C*-algebra we understand one that can be averaged into a central element by means of a sequence of unitary mixing operators. Examples include all self-commutators and all quasinilpotent elements. We do a parallel study of an element-wise version of weak centrality, where the averaging to the centre is done using unital completely positive elementary operators (as in Magajna’s characterization of weak centrality). We also obtain complete descriptions of more tractable sets of elements, where the corresponding averaging can be done arbitrarily close to the centre. This is achieved through several “spectral conditions”, involving numerical ranges and tracial states.

1.
Introduction

Let
A
be a unital
C^{*}
-algebra with centre
Z(A)
and unitary group
\mathcal{U}(A)
. By a
unitary mixing operator
on
A
we mean a map
\phi\colon A\to A
of the form

\phi(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}u_{i}^{*}xu_{i},

where
n
is a positive integer,
u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}\in\mathcal{U(A)}
and
t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}
non-negative real numbers such that
t_{1}+\ldots+t_{n}=1
. The set of all such maps is denoted by
\mathrm{Av}(A,\mathcal{U(A)})
. Given
a\in A
, we define the
Dixmier set

D_{A}(a)
as the norm-closure of the set
\{\phi(a):\phi\in\mathrm{Av}(A,\mathcal{U(A)})\}
. If
D_{A}(a)\cap Z(A)\neq\varnothing
for all
a\in A
, then
A
is said to have the Dixmier property.

It is well-known that if
A
satisfies the Dixmier property, then
A
is weakly central, that is, for any pair of maximal ideals
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
of
A
,
M_{1}\cap Z(A)=M_{2}\cap Z(A)
implies
M_{1}=M_{2}
(see e.g.
[
4
, p. 275]
). It was shown by Haagerup and Zsidó in
[
13
]
that a unital simple
C^{*}
-algebra satisfies the Dixmier property if and only if it admits at most one tracial state. In particular, weak centrality is not sufficient to guarantee the Dixmier property. A complete generalization of the Haagerup-Zsidó theorem was obtained in
[
6
, Theorem 2.6]
.

In
[
16
]
, Magajna gave a characterisation of weak centrality in terms of a more general averaging as follows. By a
unital completely positive elementary operator
on
A
we mean a map
\phi\colon A\to A
of the form

(1.1)

\phi(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{*}xa_{i},

where
n
is a positive integer and
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}
elements of
A
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{*}a_{i}=1
. The set of all such maps on
A
is denoted by
\mathrm{EUCP}(A)
. Given
a\in A
, we define the
Magajna set

M_{A}(a)
as the norm-closure of the set
\{\phi(a):\phi\in\mathrm{EUCP}(A)\}
(i.e. the closed
C^{*}
-convex hull of
a
in the sense of
[
15
]
). Magajna showed in
[
16
, Theorem 1.1]
that
A
is weakly central if and only if for any
a\in A
,
M_{A}(a)\cap Z(A)\neq\varnothing
.

By a well-known result of Vesterstrøm
[
24
]
a unital
C^{*}
-algebra
A
is weakly central if and only if it satisfies the centre-quotient property (the CQ-property), that is for any closed two-sided ideal
I
of
A
,
(Z(A)+I)/I=Z(A/I)
.
Motivated by results in
[
9
]
, where the setting is purely algebraic, the first two authors defined in
[
5
]
a local version of the CQ-property as follows. An element
a\in A
is called a
CQ-element
if for every closed two-sided ideal
I
of
A
,
a+I\in Z(A/I)
implies
a\in Z(A)+I
(
[
5
, Definition 4.1]
). In
[
5
, Theorem 4.8]
, a description of the set
\mathrm{CQ}(A)
of all CQ-elements of
A
was obtained in terms of those maximal ideals of
A
which witness the failure of the weak centrality of
A
. Further, it was shown that
\mathrm{CQ}(A)
contains all commutators and products by quasinilpotent elements. However, from both the algebraic and topological viewpoint the set
\mathrm{CQ}(A)
is in general rather strange. It always contains
Z(A)+J_{wc}(A)
(where
J_{wc}(A)
is the largest weakly central ideal of
A
, see
[
5
, Theorem 3.22]
), with equality if and only if
A/J_{wc}(A)
is abelian. Otherwise it fails dramatically to be a
C^{*}
-subalgebra of
A
: it is not norm-closed and it is neither closed under addition nor closed under multiplication.

In this paper we study local versions of the Dixmier property and of weak centrality, the latter motivated by Magajna’s characterization in terms of EUCP maps. More precisely,
given an element
a\in A
, we call
a
a Magajna element if
M_{A}(a)\cap Z(A)\neq\varnothing
, and we call it a Dixmier element if
D_{A}(a)\cap Z(A)\neq\varnothing
. We denote by
\mathrm{Mag}(A)
and
\mathrm{Dix}(A)
the sets of Magajna and Dixmier elements of
A
, respectively. That is,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Mag}(A)

\displaystyle:=\{a\in A:M_{A}(a)\cap Z(A)\neq\varnothing\},

\displaystyle\mathrm{Dix}(A)

\displaystyle:=\{a\in A:D_{A}(a)\cap Z(A)\neq\varnothing\}.

Obviously,
A
has the Dixmier property if and only if
\mathrm{Dix}(A)=A
, and
A
is weakly central if and only if
\mathrm{Mag}(A)=A
.
Quasinilpotent elements and self-commutators are Dixmier (and Magajna) elements. However, a complete description of

\mathrm{Dix}(A)
and
\mathrm{Mag}(A)
is in general difficult to obtain. This has led us to also consider the sets

\displaystyle\overline{\mathrm{Mag}}(A)

\displaystyle=\{a\in A:\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}(M_{A}(a),Z(A))=0\},

\displaystyle\overline{\mathrm{Dix}}(A)

\displaystyle=\{a\in A:\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}(D_{A}(a),Z(A))=0\}.

These are more tractable sets. In our two main results, Theorems
3.3
and
4.4
, we characterize membership of a given element in the sets
\overline{\mathrm{Mag}}(A)
and
\overline{\mathrm{Dix}}(A)
through several “spectral conditions”, involving numerical ranges and tracial states. Moreover, we show that on selfadjoint elements these spectral conditions also characterize membership in
\mathrm{Mag}(A)
and
\mathrm{Dix}(A)
, so that
\overline{\mathrm{Mag}}(A)\cap A_{sa}=\mathrm{Mag}(A)\cap A_{sa}
and
\overline{\mathrm{Dix}}(A)\cap A_{sa}=\mathrm{Dix}(A)\cap A_{sa}
. In general, one has the inclusions

\textstyle{\overline{\mathrm{Dix}}(A)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\subseteq}
\textstyle{\mathrm{Dix}(A)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\subseteq}
\textstyle{\subseteq}
\textstyle{\overline{\mathrm{Mag}}(A)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\subseteq}
\textstyle{\mathrm{CQ}(A).}
\textstyle{\mathrm{Mag}(A)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\subseteq}

All these inclusions may be proper. A question left unanswered in our investigations is whether the norm closures of
\mathrm{Mag}(A)
and
\mathrm{Dix}(A)
agree with
\overline{\mathrm{Mag}}(A)
and
\overline{\mathrm{Dix}}(A)
, respectively (the latter are always closed sets).

If
A=\mathrm{Mag}(A)
, i.e.,
A
is weakly central, then obviously
\mathrm{Mag}(A)
carries a
C^{*}
-algebra structure. It is thus natural to ask whether
\mathrm{Mag}(A)
can be in general closed under addition or multiplication. As it turns out, imposing either one of these conditions on
\mathrm{Mag}(A)
implies that
A
is not far from being weakly central. We show that if

\mathrm{Mag}(A)
is either closed under addition or multiplication, then
A/J_{wc}(A)
is abelian (Theorem
3.15
). In the case of
\mathrm{Dix}(A)
, the situation is somewhat different: For a large class of unital
C^{*}
-algebras, which includes those that have a tracial state on every simple quotient,
\mathrm{Dix}(A)=\overline{\mathrm{Dix}}(A)=Z(A)+\overline{[A,A]}
, and in particular,
\mathrm{Dix}(A)
is closed under addition (Theorem
4.11
). On the other hand, if
\mathrm{Dix}(A)
is closed under multiplication,
A
is not too far from having the Dixmier property, in the sense that
A/J_{dp}(A)
is abelian, where
J_{dp}(A)
is the largest ideal of
A
with the Dixmier property (Theorem
4.15
).

Although
\mathrm{Mag}(A)
and
\mathrm{Dix}(A)
are not necessarily closed under addition, these sets are always pervasive in the sense that
\overline{\mathop{\mathrm{span}}(\mathrm{Mag}(A))}=Z(A)+\mathrm{Ideal}([A,A])
(Corollary
3.12
) and
\overline{\mathop{\mathrm{span}}(\mathrm{Dix}(A))}=Z(A)+\overline{[A,A]}
(Lemma
4.10
).

As well as using earlier results on the Dixmier property and weak centrality

5

, the methods of this paper rely on the Dauns-Hofmann theorem

.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
A
be a unital
C^{*}
-algebra. Let
Z(A)
denote its centre. By an ideal of
A
we shall always mean a closed two-sided ideal. For any subset
X
of
A
, we denote by
\mathrm{Ideal}(X)
the ideal of
A
generated by
X
. It is well-known that if
I
is an ideal of
A
, then
Z(I)=I\cap Z(A)
. As usual, if
x,y\in A
, then
[x,y]
stands for the commutator
xy-yx
. By
[A,A]
we denote the linear span of all commutators in
A
.

By
S(A)
we denote the sets of all states on
A
. Given
a\in A
we denote by
W_{A}(a)
the (algebraic) numerical range of
a
, that is

W_{A}(a)=\{\omega(a):\omega\in S(A)\}.

It is well-known that
W_{A}(a)
is a compact convex set that contains the spectrum of
a
. We use frequently in what follows that if
I
is an ideal of
A
, then
W_{A/I}(a+I)\subseteq W_{A}(a)
.

By
\mathrm{Prim}(A)
and
\mathrm{Max}(A)
we respectively denote the sets of all primitive and all maximal ideals of
A
. As usual, we equip
\mathrm{Prim}(A)
with the Jacobson topology. As
A
is unital, both sets
\mathrm{Prim}(A)
and
\mathrm{Max}(A)
are compact. For any ideal
I
of
A
we write

\mathrm{Max}^{I}(A):=\{M\in\mathrm{Max}(A):I\subseteq M\}.

It is easy to check that the assignment
M\mapsto M/I
defines a homeomorphism from the set
\mathrm{Max}^{I}(A)
onto the set
\mathrm{Max}(A/I)
.

Let us recall some facts around the Dauns-Hofmann theorem and the complete regularization map (see
[
7
]
for further details).
For all
P,Q\in\mathrm{Prim}(A)
, define
P\approx Q
if
P\cap Z(A)=Q\cap Z(A)
.
By the Dauns-Hofmann theorem
[
22
, Theorem A.34]
), there exists a
C^{*}
-algebra isomorphism
Z(A)\ni z\mapsto\widehat{z}\in C(\mathrm{Prim}(A))
such that

z+P=\widehat{z}(P)1+P\quad(z\in Z(A),\,P\in\mathrm{Prim}(A)).

Hence, for all
P,Q\in\mathrm{Prim}(A)
we have

P\approx Q
if and only if
f(P)=f(Q)
for all
f\in C(\mathrm{Prim}(A))
.
Note that
\approx
is an equivalence relation on
\mathrm{Prim}(A)
and the equivalence
classes are closed subsets of
\mathrm{Prim}(A)
. It follows there is one-to-one correspondence between the quotient set
\mathrm{Prim}(A)/\!\approx

and a set of ideals of
A
given by

[P]_{\approx}\longleftrightarrow\bigcap[P]_{\approx}
,
where
[P]_{\approx}
denotes the equivalence class of
P\in\mathrm{Prim}(A)
. The set of ideals obtained in this way is denoted by
\mathrm{Glimm}(A)
, and its
elements are called
Glimm ideals
of
A
. The quotient map
\mathrm{Prim}(A)\to\mathrm{Glimm}(A)
given by

P\mapsto\bigcap[P]_{\approx}

is known as the
complete regularization map
.
We equip
\mathrm{Glimm}(A)
with the quotient topology, which coincides with the complete regularization topology, since
A
is unital. In this way
\mathrm{Glimm}(A)
becomes a compact Hausdorff space. In fact,
\mathrm{Glimm}(A)
is homeomorphic to
\mathrm{Max}(Z(A))
via the assignment

\mathrm{Glimm}(A)\ni N\mapsto N\cap Z(A)\in\mathrm{Max}(Z(A))
, whose inverse is given by
\mathrm{Max}(Z(A))\ni J\mapsto JA\in\mathrm{Glimm}(A)
(
JA
is closed by the Hewitt-Cohen factorization theorem).

For
z\in A
we also write
\widehat{z}
for the function in
C(\mathrm{Glimm}(A))
such that

z+N=\widehat{z}(N)1+N\quad(N\in\mathrm{Glimm}(A)).

Thus, the assignment
Z(A)\ni z\mapsto\widehat{z}\in C(\mathrm{Glimm}(A))
is a
C^{*}
-algebra isomorphism.

3.
The set
\mathrm{Mag}(A)

Recall that for
a\in A
we define the Magajna set
M_{A}(a)
of
a
as the norm-closure of the set
\{\phi(a):\phi\in\mathrm{EUCP}(A)\}
. Further, we denote by
\mathrm{Mag}(A)

the set of all
a\in A
such that
M_{A}(a)\cap Z(A)\neq\varnothing
.

Our starting point in the investigation of the set
\mathrm{Mag}(A)
is the following theorem of Magajna.

Theorem 3.1

(Theorem 4.1 of
[
15
]
)
.

Let
a\in A
. A normal element
b\in A
belongs to
M_{A}(a)
if and only if
W_{A/P}(b+P)\subseteq W_{A/P}(a+P)
for each
P\in\mathrm{Prim}(A)
.

Given
a\in A
, let us define the set-valued function
\Psi_{a}\colon\mathrm{Glimm}(A)\to 2^{{\mathbb{C}}}
by

\Psi_{a}(N):=\bigcap_{M\in\mathrm{Max}^{N}(A)}W_{A/M}(a+M),

for all
N\in\mathrm{Glimm}(A)
. From Theorem
3.1
we deduce the following proposition:

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
a\in A
and
z\in Z(A)
. Then
z\in M_{A}(a)
if and only if

\widehat{z}(N)\in\Psi_{a}(N)
for all
N\in\mathrm{Glimm}(A)
, i.e.,
\widehat{z}
is a continuous selection of the set-valued map
\Psi_{a}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\widehat{z}(N)\in\Psi_{a}(N)
for all
N\in\mathrm{Glimm}(A)
, i.e.,
\widehat{z}(N)\in W_{A/M}(a+M)
for all
N\in\mathrm{Glimm}(A)
and all
M\in\mathrm{Max}^{N}(A)
. Let
P\in\mathrm{Prim}(A)
. Let
N\in\mathrm{Glimm}(A)
be such that
N\subseteq P
, namely
N=(P\cap Z(A))A
. Since
z+P=\widehat{z}(N)1+P
, we have that
W_{A/P}(z+P)=\{\widehat{z}(N)\}
. Choose any
M\in\mathrm{Max}(A)
such that
P\subseteq M
. Then,

W_{A/P}(z+P)=\{\widehat{z}(N)\}\subseteq W_{A/M}(a+M)\subseteq W_{A/P}(a+P),

where we have used that
A/M
is a quotient of
A/P
in the last inclusion.
It follows from Theorem
3.1
that
z\in M_{A}(a)
. Conversely, if
z\in M_{A}(a)
, then again by
Theorem
3.1
we have
W_{A/P}(z+P)\subseteq W_{A/P}(a+P)
for all
P\in\mathrm{Prim}(A)
. Applying this to maximal ideals we get that

\{\widehat{z}(N)\}=W_{A/M}(z+M)\subseteq W_{A/M}(a+M)

for all
N\in\mathrm{Glimm}(A)
and
M\in\mathrm{Max}^{N}(A)
, as desired.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

For an element
a\in A
consider the following conditions:

(i)

a\in\mathrm{Mag}(A)
.

(ii)

\mathrm{dist}(M_{A}(a),Z(A))=0
.

(iii)

\Psi_{a}(N)\neq\varnothing
for all
N\in\mathrm{Glimm}(A)
.

Then (i)
\Longrightarrow
(ii)
\Longleftrightarrow
(iii). If
a
is selfadjoint then (i), (ii) and (iii) are equivalent.

Before proving Theorem
3.3
we establish some preliminary facts. First recall that if
X
and
Y
are topological spaces, a set-valued function
\Psi\colon X\to 2^{Y}
is said to be
lower semicontinuous
(l.s.c.) if for every open set
U\subseteq Y
, the set

\{x\in X:\Psi(x)\cap U\neq\varnothing\}

is open in
X
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
a\in A
.

(i)

The function
f_{a}\colon\mathrm{Glimm}(A)\to[0,\infty)
defined by

f_{a}(N):=\inf\{\|a+M\|:\ M\in\mathrm{Max}^{N}(A)\}.

is l.s.c.

(ii)

If
a
is selfadjoint, then the set-valued function
\Psi_{a}
is l.s.c.

Proof.

(i) Let
s\geq 0
and

C_{s}:=\{N\in\mathrm{Glimm}(A):f_{a}(N)\leq s\}.

We claim that
C_{s}
is closed in
\mathrm{Glimm}(A)
. Indeed, suppose that
(N_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
is a net in
C_{s}
converging to some
N_{0}\in\mathrm{Glimm}(A)
. Let
\epsilon>0
.
For each index
\alpha
there is
M_{\alpha}\in\mathrm{Max}^{N_{\alpha}}(A)
such that

\|a+M_{\alpha}\|\leq s+\epsilon.

As
A
is unital,
\mathrm{Max}(A)
is compact, so there is a subnet of
(M_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
convergent to some
M_{0}\in\mathrm{Max}(A)
.
Then, by the continuity of the complete regularization map from
\mathrm{Prim}(A)
to
\mathrm{Glimm}(A)
, and the Hausdorffness of
\mathrm{Glimm}(A)
, we conclude that
M_{0}
contains
N_{0}
. By the lower semi-continuity of the norm functions
P\mapsto\|a+P\|
on
\mathrm{Prim}(A)
(see e.g.
[
8
, Proposition II.6.5.6 (iii)]
) we get
\|a+M_{0}\|\leq s+\epsilon
.
Hence
f_{a}(N_{0})\leq s+\epsilon
. Since
\epsilon>0
was arbitrary,
f_{a}(N_{0})\leq s
, as required.

(ii) Given a
C^{*}
-algebra element
b
, let us denote by
\mathrm{sp}(b)
its spectrum. Now let
a\in A
be selfadjoint. Define two functions
g,h\colon\mathrm{Glimm}(A)\to{\mathbb{R}}
by

\displaystyle g(N)

\displaystyle:=\inf\{\max\mathrm{sp}(a+M):M\in\mathrm{Max}^{N}(A)\},

\displaystyle h(N)

\displaystyle:=\sup\{\min\mathrm{sp}(a+M):M\in\mathrm{Max}^{N}(A)\}.

As
\max\mathrm{sp}(a+M)=\|(\|a\|\cdot 1+a)+M\|-\|a\|
, we have that

g(N)=f_{\|a\|1+a}(N)-\|a\|.

Similarly,
\min\mathrm{sp}(a+M)=\|a\|-\|(\|a\|1-a)+M\|
, from which we deduce that

\displaystyle h(N)=-f_{\|a\|1-a}(N)+\|a\|.

Thus, by part (i),
g
is lower semi-continuous and
h
is upper semi-continuous. Since
the numerical range of a selfadjoint element is the convex hull of its spectrum,

W_{A/M}(a+M)=[\min\mathrm{sp}(a+M),\max\mathrm{sp}(a+M)]

for all
M\in\mathrm{Max}(A)
. We deduce at once that

\Psi_{a}(N)=[h(N),g(N)],

where
\Psi_{a}(N)=\varnothing
if
h(N)>g(N)
. It follows that
\Psi_{a}
is a l.s.c. set-valued function.
∎

Given a set
C\subseteq{\mathbb{C}}
and
\delta>0
, let us denote by
C^{\leq\delta}
the set of complex numbers whose distance to
C
is
\leq\delta
. For
a\in A
,
N\in\mathrm{Glimm}(A)
, and
\delta>0
, let us define

W_{N,\delta}(a):=\bigcap_{M\in\mathrm{Max}^{N}(A)}\Big(W_{A/M}(a+M)\Big)^{\leq\delta}.

Now fix

and define
\Psi_{a,r}\colon\mathrm{Glimm}(A)\to 2^{{\mathbb{C}}}
by

(3.1)

\Psi_{a,r}(N):=\bigcup_{\delta<r}W_{N,\delta}(a).

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
a\in A
. For each
r>0
the map
\Psi_{a,r}

is lower semi-continuous.

Proof.

Let
U\subseteq{\mathbb{C}}
be an open set and
N\in\mathrm{Glimm}(A)
such that
U\cap\Psi_{a,r}(N)\neq\varnothing
.
Let
\omega\in U\cap\Psi_{a,r}(N)
. Observe that this means that
\omega\in W_{N,\delta}(a)
for some
\delta<r
.
Choose
\epsilon>0
such that
\overline{B_{\epsilon}(\omega)}\subseteq U
.
Suppose, for the sake of contradiction, that there exists a convergent net
N_{\lambda}\to N
in
\mathrm{Glimm}(A)

such that
\overline{B_{\epsilon}(\omega)}\cap\Psi_{a,r}(N_{\lambda})=\varnothing
for all

. Choose
\delta<\delta^{\prime}<r
.
Then

\overline{B_{\epsilon}(\omega)}\cap\bigcap_{M\in\mathrm{Max}^{N_{\lambda}}(A)}\Big(W_{A/M}(a+M)\Big)^{\leq\delta^{\prime}}=\varnothing,

for all
\lambda
. By Helly’s theorem, for each
\lambda
there exist
M_{\lambda,1},M_{\lambda,2},M_{\lambda,3}
, maximal ideals containing

, such that

(3.2)

\overline{B_{\epsilon}(\omega)}\cap\bigcap_{i=1,2,3}\Big(W_{A/M_{\lambda,i}}(a+M_{\lambda,i})\Big)^{\leq\delta^{\prime}}=\varnothing.

Pass to subnets if necessary so that

, with

, we obtain that

for

.

Since

, we have that

. So for each

for

, which contradicts (

∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
a,b\in A
. Then the following numbers are equal:

(i)

The distance between
M_{A}(a)
and
M_{A}(b)
.

(ii)

The minimum number

satisfying

\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}(W_{A/M}(a+M),W_{A/M}(b+M))\leq r,\quad(M\in\mathrm{Max}(A)).

Proof.

It is straightforward to show that the number in (i) bounds from above the number in (ii), using that

W_{A/M}(\phi(a)+M)\subseteq W_{A/M}(a+M)
for any
\phi\in\mathrm{EUCP}(A)
. Let us prove that the number in (ii)
bounds the number in (i) from above. We adapt the proof of
[
6
, Theorem 4.12]
to the present context.

First, let us show that the distance between
M_{A}(a)
and
M_{A}(b)
is the same as the distance between
M_{A^{**}}(a)
and
M_{A^{**}}(b)
. On the one hand, since
M_{A}(a)\subseteq M_{A^{**}}(a)
and
M_{A}(b)\subseteq M_{A^{**}}(b)
,
the distance between the sets
M_{A}(a)
and
M_{A}(b)
bounds from above the distance between
the sets
M_{A^{**}}(a)
and
M_{A^{**}}(b)
. On the other hand, the opposite inequality follows from a standard Hahn-Banach/Kaplansky density argument. This argument runs exactly as in the proof of
[
6
, Lemma 4.1]
, except that unitary mixing operators are replaced by
operators in
\mathrm{EUCP}(A)
, and the application of the Glimm-Kadison theorem is replaced by the fact that, for

,
M_{A^{**}}(x)
is contained in the
\sigma(A^{**},A^{*})
-closure of
M_{A}(x)
(see the proof of
[
15
, Theorem 4.1]
).

Observe now that the number
r
described in (ii) does not increase if we replace
A
by
A^{**}
, since for each maximal ideal
M\in\mathrm{Max}(A^{**})
and
a\in A
we have that

W_{A/(A\cap M)}(a+A\cap M)=W_{A^{**}/M}(a+M).

In view of the facts observed above, it suffices to prove the desired inequality in the von Neumann algebra
A^{**}
. Let us therefore assume that
A
is a von Neumann algebra, and in particular, that it is weakly central. Then
\mathrm{Glimm}(A)\cong\mathrm{Max}(A)
via the complete regularization map, and for each
a\in A
the function
\Psi_{a}
is l.s.c. by
[
6
, Lemma 4.7]
and takes non-empty values (since
\mathrm{Max}^{N}(A)
is a singleton for all
N\in\mathrm{Glimm}(A)
).

Let
\epsilon>0
. Consider the set-valued map

\mathrm{Glimm}(A)\ni N\mapsto\overline{\Psi_{a}(N)\cap(\Psi_{b}(N))^{r+\epsilon}},

where
(\Psi_{b}(N))^{r+\epsilon}
denotes the set of complex numbers whose distance to
\Psi_{b}(N)
is
<r+\epsilon
. By
[
6
, Lemma 4.8]
,
this map is l.s.c. and its values are non-empty compact convex subsets of
{\mathbb{C}}
. By Michael’s selection theorem, it has a continuous selection, which is of the form
N\mapsto\widehat{z}_{1}(N)
for
z_{1}\in Z(A)
. By Proposition
3.2
,
z_{1}\in M_{A}(a)
. Consider the set-valued map

\mathrm{Glimm}(A)\ni N\mapsto\overline{B_{r+2\epsilon}(\widehat{z}_{1}(N))\cap\Psi_{b}(N)}.

This is again a l.s.c. map whose values are non-empty compact convex subsets of
{\mathbb{C}}
. Let
z_{2}\in Z(A)
be a central element corresponding to a continuous selection of this map. Then
z_{2}\in M_{A}(b)
and
|\widehat{z}_{1}(N)-\widehat{z}_{2}(N)|\leq r+2\epsilon
for all
N\in\mathrm{Glimm}(A)
. Hence,
\|z_{1}-z_{2}\|\leq r+2\epsilon
. Thus, the distance between
M_{A}(a)

and
M_{A}(b)
is bounded from above by
r+2\epsilon
for an arbitrary

, whence, also by
r
.
∎

(i)

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
A
be a unital
C^{*}
-algebra.

(i)

The set
\overline{\mathrm{Mag}}(A)

\mathrm{CQ}(A)

(ii)

For any

\overline{\mathrm{Mag}}(A)\cap A_{sa}=\mathrm{Mag}(A)\cap A_{sa}

Proof.

(i) We first show that
\overline{\mathrm{Mag}}(A)

x\in A

\overline{\mathrm{Mag}}(A)
and let
\epsilon>0
. Then there are

,
\phi\in\mathrm{EUCP}(A)
and

and
\|\phi(a)-z\|<\epsilon/2
. Then, as

\|\phi(x)-z\|\leq\|\phi(x)-\phi(a)\|+\|\phi(a)-z\|<\epsilon

.

We now show that
\overline{\mathrm{Mag}}(A)\subseteq\mathrm{CQ}(A)
. Assume, for the sake of contradiction, that there is
a\in\overline{\mathrm{Mag}}(A)\setminus\mathrm{CQ}(A)
. Then by
[
5
, Theorem 4.8]
there are distinct

with
M_{1}\cap Z(A)=M_{2}\cap Z(A)
and distinct scalars
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
such that

(

). As

, for
\epsilon:=|\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}|/2>0
there is
\phi\in\mathrm{EUCP}(A)
and

, there is a scalar

Theorem 3.15

(i)

\mathrm{Mag}(A)=\mathrm{CQ}(A)
.

(ii)

(iii)

\mathrm{Mag}(A)=\mathrm{CQ}(A)=Z(A)+J_{wc}(A).

(iv)

\mathrm{Mag}(A)
is closed under addition.

(v)

\mathrm{Mag}(A)
is closed under multiplication.

(vi)

\mathrm{Mag}(A)
is closed under EUCP operators.

\mathrm{Mag}(A)=\overline{\mathrm{Mag}}(A)

Proof.

Since

Z(A)+J_{wc}(A)\subseteq\mathrm{Mag}(A)\subseteq\mathrm{CQ}(A),

the equivalence (ii)
\Longleftrightarrow
(iii) follows from
[
5

The implication (iii)
\Longrightarrow
(i) is obvious. Let us show that (i)
\Longrightarrow
(ii). Assume that
\mathrm{Mag}(A)=\mathrm{CQ}(A)
. Then also
\overline{\mathrm{Mag}}(A)=\mathrm{CQ}(A)
, as
\mathrm{Mag}(A)\subseteq\overline{\mathrm{Mag}}(A)\subseteq\mathrm{CQ}(A)
. In particular, by Proposition
3.8
,
\mathrm{CQ}(A)
is norm-closed. By
[
5
, Theorem 4.12]
, this is equivalent to

being abelian.

We have so far shown that (i), (ii), and (iii) are equivalent, and that under these conditions

Let us show that (iv)
\Longrightarrow
(ii). Assume, for the sake of contradiction, that

is non-abelian. By the spectral characterization of

(

and distinct maximal ideals

is non-abelian. By
[

are square zero elements. Since

is mapped onto

by the quotient map, and square zero elements can be lifted to square zero elements, there exist

of these elements. Let

a=\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}z_{i}.

Each term in the sums on the right hand side belongs to
\mathrm{Mag}(A)
by Proposition

\Longrightarrow
(ii). Let us establish a preliminary fact: Let

and

Choose
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}
such that

is invertible. Then

\displaystyle x+y

\displaystyle=(x+\lambda 1)(1+(x+\lambda 1)^{-1}y)-\lambda 1,

\displaystyle x+yz

\displaystyle=(x+\lambda 1)(1+(x+\lambda 1)^{-1}yz)-\lambda 1.

Combining Proposition
3.11
with the fact that
\mathrm{Mag}(A)
is closed under multiplication, we deduce that the right hand sides of these equations are in
\mathrm{Mag}(A)
. Thus,
x+y
and
x+yz
belong to
\mathrm{Mag}(A)
. In particular,
\mathrm{Mag}(A)
contains all finite sums of the form

\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}z_{i},

where
x_{i},y_{i},z_{i}\in A
are all square zero elements.

Now, using the same arguments as in the proof of (iv)
\Longrightarrow
(ii), we conclude that
A/J_{wc}(A)
must be abelian.

Let us show that (vi)
\Longrightarrow
(ii). Again assume, for the sake of contradiction, that there exist
N\in\mathrm{Glimm}(A)
and distinct
M,M^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Max}^{N}(A)
such that
A/M
is not abelian. Let
\dot{x}\in A/M
be a square zero element of norm 1. Since
\dot{x}
is not invertible and of norm 1, the numerical range of
\dot{x}^{*}\dot{x}
is
[0,1]
. By Theorem
3.1
,
1\in M_{A}(\dot{x}^{*}\dot{x})
. Thus,
there exist
\dot{d}_{1},\ldots,\dot{d}_{n}\in A/M
such that

\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\dot{d}_{i})^{*}\dot{x}^{*}\dot{x}\dot{d}_{i}\geq\frac{1}{2}(1+M)

and
\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\dot{d}_{i})^{*}\dot{d}_{i}=1
. Since
M^{\prime}
maps onto
A/M
by the quotient map, we can lift
\dot{x}

to a square zero element of norm one
x\in M^{\prime}
(
[
1
, Proposition 2.8]
). Choose also lifts
d_{1},\ldots,d_{n}\in A
of
\dot{d}_{1},\ldots,\dot{d}_{n}

such that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}^{*}d_{i}\leq 1
. (This is always possible. Proof: Let
\dot{d}\in M_{n}(A/M)
be the contraction
with first column equal to
(\dot{d}_{1},\ldots,\dot{d}_{n})
and zeros elsewhere. Let
d\in M_{n}(A)
be a lift of
\dot{d}

of norm 1. Then the elements on the first column of
d
are the desired lifts.) Let
c\in A_{+}
be such that

\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}^{*}d_{i}+c^{2}=1
. Define

a:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}^{*}x^{*}xd_{i}+cx^{*}xc.

We have that
a\in\mathrm{Mag}(A)
, by the invariance of
\mathrm{Mag}(A)
under EUCP operators. On the other hand,

a\in M^{\prime}
and
a+M\geq 1/2\cdot 1+M
, which implies that
\Psi_{a}(N)=\varnothing
.
∎

We end this section with another example:

Example 3.16
.

Let
A
be the
C^{*}
-algebra consisting of all functions
a\in C([0,1],M_{3}({\mathbb{C}}))

such that

a(1)=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{11}(a)&\lambda_{12}(a)&0\\
\lambda_{21}(a)&\lambda_{22}(a)&0\\
0&0&\mu(a)\end{pmatrix},

for some complex numbers
\lambda_{ij}(a),\mu(a)
,
i,j=1,2
(see
[
5
, Example 4.19]
).
The centre of
A
is the set of
a
such that
a(t)\in{\mathbb{C}}\cdot 1_{3}
for all
t\in[0,1]
. The Glimm ideals
are indexed by
[0,1]
:

N_{t}=\{a\in A:a(t)=0\},\quad(t\in[0,1]).

Using (
3.4
), we find that

J_{wc}(A)=\{a\in A:a(1)=0\}.

Observe that, since
A/J_{wc}(A)\cong M_{2}({\mathbb{C}})\oplus{\mathbb{C}}
is non-abelian,
the equivalent conditions of the previous theorem are not met.
From Theorem
3.3
we obtain a description of
\overline{\mathrm{Mag}}(A)
:

(3.5)

\overline{\mathrm{Mag}}(A)=\Big\{a\in A:\mu(a)\in W\left(\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{11}(a)&\lambda_{12}(a)\\
\lambda_{21}(a)&\lambda_{22}(a)\end{pmatrix}\right)\Big\}.

In this case
\mathrm{Mag}(A)=\overline{\mathrm{Mag}}(A)
. To see this, let
a\in\overline{\mathrm{Mag}}(A)
. By (
3.5
) there is a state
\omega
on
M_{2}({\mathbb{C}})
such that
\omega((\lambda_{ij}(a)))=\mu(a)
. Since the map

M_{2}({\mathbb{C}})\to M_{2}({\mathbb{C}})
given by
b\mapsto\omega(b)1_{2}
is completely positive, it belongs to
\mathrm{EUCP}(M_{2}({\mathbb{C}}))
by Choi’s theorem (see
[
11
, Theorem 1]
). Hence, there is
\phi\in\mathrm{EUCP}(M_{2}({\mathbb{C}})\oplus{\mathbb{C}})
such that
\phi(a(1))\in{\mathbb{C}}1_{3}
. By considering the coefficients of
\phi
as constant functions in
A
, we may regard
\phi\in\mathrm{EUCP}(A)
. Then
\phi(a)\in{\mathbb{C}}\cdot 1_{A}+J_{wc}(A)
and this set is contained in
\mathrm{Mag}(A)
. Thus,
a\in\mathrm{Mag}(A)
.

4.
The set
\mathrm{Dix}(A)

Throughout this section, we continue to let
A
denote a unital
C^{*}
-algebra.
Recall that given an element
a\in A
, we define its Dixmier set
D_{A}(a)
by

\overline{\{\phi(a):\phi\colon\mathrm{Av}(A,\mathcal{U(A)})\}}
, where
\mathrm{Av}(A,\mathcal{U(A)})
denotes the set of unitary mixing operators on
A
.
Further, we denote by
\mathrm{Dix}(A)
the set of
a\in A
such that
D_{A}(a)\cap Z(A)\neq\varnothing
.

For a
C^{*}
-algebra
B
(not necessarily unital), let us denote by
T(B)
the set of tracial states on
B
.

Define

Y:=\{N\in\mathrm{Glimm}(A):T(A/N)\neq\varnothing\}.

Lemma 4.1
.

The set
Y
is a closed subset of
\mathrm{Glimm}(A)
.

Proof.

Let
(N_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
be a net in
Y
converging to some
N\in\mathrm{Glimm}(A)
. For each
\alpha
, there exists
\tau_{\alpha}\in T(A)
such that
\tau_{\alpha}(N_{\alpha})=\{0\}
. Since
T(A)
is
w^{*}
-compact, there exists
\tau\in T(A)
and a subnet
(\tau_{\alpha(\beta)})_{\beta}
such that
\tau_{\alpha(\beta)}\underset{\beta}{\to}\tau
in the
w^{*}
-topology.

Let us show that
\tau
vanishes on
N
.
For each index
\beta
,
\tau_{\alpha(\beta)}
vanishes on
N_{\alpha(\beta)}\cap Z(A)
, and so
\tau_{\alpha(\beta)}|_{Z(A)}
is a pure state of
Z(A)
with kernel
N_{\alpha(\beta)}\cap Z(A)
. Hence,
\tau|_{Z(A)}
is a pure state of
Z(A)
with kernel
K
, say. Since
N_{\alpha(\beta)}\underset{\beta}{\to}N
in
\mathrm{Glimm}(A)
, we have that

N_{\alpha(\beta)}\cap Z(A)\underset{\beta}{\to}N\cap Z(A)

in
\mathrm{Max}(Z(A))
. On the other hand,

N_{\alpha(\beta)}\cap Z(A)\underset{\beta}{\to}K

in
\mathrm{Max}(Z(A))
. Since
\mathrm{Max}(Z(A))
is Hausdorff,
K=N\cap Z(A)
. From
\tau(N\cap Z(A))=\{0\}
and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality for states, we get that

\tau(N)=\tau(A(N\cap Z(A)))=\{0\}.

Thus,
\tau
vanishes on
N
. It follows that
N\in Y
.
∎

Recall that given
a\in A
we denote by
\Psi_{a}\colon\mathrm{Glimm}(A)\to 2^{{\mathbb{C}}}
the function

\Psi_{a}(N)=\bigcap_{M\in\mathrm{Max}^{N}(A)}W_{A/M}(a+M)\quad(N\in\mathrm{Glimm}(A)).

In the following proposition we make crucial use of
[
6
, Theorem 4.4]
:

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
a\in A
and
z\in Z(A)
.
We have
z\in D_{A}(a)\cap Z(A)
if and only if

(i)

\widehat{z}(N)=\tau(a+N)
for all
N\in Y
and
\tau\in T(A/N)
,

(ii)

\widehat{z}(N)\in\Psi_{a}(N)
for all
N\in\mathrm{Glimm}(A)
.

Proof.

Let
z\in D_{A}(a)\cap Z(A)
. Let
N\in Y
and
\tau\in T(A/N)
. Then
z+N=\widehat{z}(N)1+N
, and evaluating
on
\tau
we get
\widehat{z}(N)=\tau(z+N)
. But
\tau
is constant on
D_{A/N}(a+N)

(since unitary mixing operators preserve the trace) and equal to
\tau(a+N)
. Hence,
\widehat{z}(N)=\tau(a+N)
.
This proves (i). Condition (ii) follows from the fact that
z
is in the Magajna set of
a
.

Conversely, suppose that
z\in Z(A)
satisfies (i) and (ii). To show that
z\in D_{A}(a)
, equivalently, that

0\in D_{A}(a-z)
, we rely on
[
6
, Theorem 4.4]
. In view of this result, it suffices to show that

\tau(a-z)=0
for all
\tau\in T(A)
and
0\in W_{A/M}((a-z)+M)
for all
M\in\mathrm{Max}(A)
.
Let
\tau\in T(A)
be an extreme tracial state. By
[
6
, Lemma 2.4]

\tau|_{Z(A)}
is a pure state on
Z(A)
. Hence, there exists
N\in\mathrm{Glimm}(A)
such that
\tau
vanishes on
N\cap Z(A)
. By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality for states, we also have that
\tau(N)=\{0\}
, so that
N\in Y
. By (i) we have

\tau(a)=\widehat{z}(N)=\tau(z).

Hence, by the Krein-Milman theorem,
\tau(a)=\tau(z)
for all
\tau\in T(A)
.
Let
M\in\mathrm{Max}(A)
, and let
N
be the unique Glimm ideal of
A
contained in
M
. By (ii), there is a state
\omega
of
A
such that
\omega(M)=\{0\}
and
\omega(a)=\widehat{z}(N)
. Since
z+M=\widehat{z}(N)1+M
,
\omega(a-z)=0
and so

0\in W_{A/M}((a-z)+M)
. It follows that
0\in D_{A}(a-z)
, as required.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

An immediate consequence of Proposition
4.2
is that
D_{A}(a)\cap Z(A)\neq\varnothing
, i.e.

a\in\mathrm{Dix}(A)
, if and only if

(1)

for each
N\in Y
all the tracial states of
A/N
have a common value on
a+N
, call it
f_{a}(N)
,

(2)

there is a continuous selection of
\Psi_{a}\colon\mathrm{Glimm}(A)\to 2^{{\mathbb{C}}}
that agrees with
f_{a}(N)
for
N\in Y
.

Indeed, such selections give rise to central elements that satisfy the conditions listed in the proposition. Observe also that if
Y=\varnothing
(equivalently,
A
has no tracial states), then the first condition is vacuously valid; hence
\mathrm{Dix}(A)=\mathrm{Mag}(A)
by Proposition
3.2
in this case.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
A
be a unital
C^{*}
-algebra, and let
a\in A
. Consider the following conditions:

(i)

a\in\mathrm{Dix}(A)
.

(ii)

\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}(D_{A}(a),Z(A))=0
.

(iii)

(a)

There is a function
f_{a}\colon Y\to{\mathbb{C}}
such that

(a1)

for all
N\in Y
and
\tau\in T(A/N)
,
f_{a}(N)=\tau(a+N)
,

(a2)

for all
N\in Y
,
f_{a}(N)\in\Psi_{a}(N)
.

(b)

For all
N\in\mathrm{Glimm}(A)\setminus Y
,
\Psi_{a}(N)\neq\varnothing
.

Then (i)
\Longrightarrow
(ii)
\Longleftrightarrow
(iii). Further, if
(iii)
holds then
f_{a}
is unique and it is continuous on
Y
. Finally, if
a
is selfadjoint, then (i), (ii), and (iii) are equivalent.

Proof.

(i)
\Longrightarrow
(ii). This is immediate.

(ii)
\Longrightarrow
(iii). Suppose that
\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}(D_{A}(a),Z(A))=0
. For each
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, there exist a unitary mixing operator
\phi_{n}
, and
z_{n}\in Z(A)
, such that

(4.1)

\|\phi_{n}(a)-z_{n}\|<\frac{1}{n}.

Each
z_{n}
gives rise to a continuous function
\widehat{z}_{n}\colon\mathrm{Glimm}(A)\to{\mathbb{C}}
such that

z_{n}+N=\widehat{z}_{n}(N)1+N
. Let
N\in Y
and
\tau\in T(A/N)
. Passing to the quotient
A/N
in (
4.1
) and evaluating on
\tau
, we get

|(\tau(a+N)-\widehat{z}_{n}(N)|<\frac{1}{n}\qquad(n\in{\mathbb{N}}).

Hence,
(\widehat{z}_{n}(N))_{n}
is a convergent sequence with limit
\tau(a+N)
. Thus
\tau(a+N)
is independent of the choice of
\tau\in T(A/N)
. Define
f_{a}(N)
to be this common value. Since
f_{a}
is the uniform limit of the sequence of continuous functions
(\widehat{z}_{n}|_{Y}
),
f_{a}
is continuous on
Y
. (As shown below, the continuity of
f_{a}
can also be derived from the formula
f_{a}(N)=\tau(a+N)
(
N\in Y
).)

Let
N\in Y
,
M
a maximal ideal of
A
containing
N
, and
\omega
a state of
A
such that
\omega(M)=\{0\}
. By (
4.1
),

|\omega(\phi_{n}(a))-\widehat{z}_{n}(N)|<\frac{1}{n}\qquad(n\in{\mathbb{N}}).

Since
\omega\circ\phi_{n}
is a state of
A
annihilating
M
,

(4.2)

\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}(\widehat{z}_{n}(N),W_{A/M}(a+M))<\frac{1}{n}\qquad(n\in{\mathbb{N}}).

But
W_{A/M}(a+M)
is closed and
\widehat{z}_{n}(N)\to f_{a}(N)
as
n\to\infty
. Hence
f_{a}(N)\in W_{A/M}(a+M)
. Since
M
can vary through all of
\mathrm{Max}^{N}(A)
,
we obtain that
f_{a}(N)\in\Psi_{a}(N)
. This proves (iii) (a).

As for (iii) (b), observe that
\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}(M_{A}(a),Z(A))=0
, as
D_{A}(a)\subseteq M_{A}(a)
(since unitary mixing operators are
\mathrm{EUCP}
operators). Thus,
\Psi_{a}(N)\neq\varnothing
for all
N\in\mathrm{Glimm}(A)
by Theorem
3.3
.

(iii)
\Longrightarrow
(ii). Suppose that the element
a
satisfies (iii). We begin by showing that
f_{a}
is continuous on
Y
. Let
(N_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
be a net in
Y
convergent to some
N\in Y
and let
(N_{\alpha(\beta)})_{\beta}
be an arbitrary subnet. For each
\beta
, there exists
\tau_{\alpha(\beta)}\in T(A)
such that
\tau_{\alpha(\beta)}(N_{\alpha(\beta)})=\{0\}
. Since
T(A)
is
w^{*}
-compact, there exists
\tau\in T(A)
and a subnet
(\tau_{\alpha(\beta(\gamma))})_{\gamma}
such that
\tau_{\alpha(\beta(\gamma))}\underset{\gamma}{\to}\tau
in the
w^{*}
-topology. As in the proof of Lemma
4.1
, we obtain that
\tau(N\cap Z(A))=\{0\}
and hence
\tau(N)=\{0\}
. We have

\lim_{\gamma}f_{a}(N_{\alpha(\beta(\gamma))})=\lim_{\gamma}\tau_{\alpha(\beta(\gamma))}(a)=\tau(a)=f_{a}(N).

It follows that
f_{a}(N_{\alpha})\underset{\alpha}{\to}f_{a}(N)
and so
f_{a}
is continuous on
Y
. The uniqueness of
f_{a}
is clear from the equation in (iii) (a1).

Let
r>0
. By (iii) (a2),
f_{a}(N)\in\Psi_{a,r}(N)
for each
N\in Y
, where
\Psi_{a,r}(N)
is as in (
3.1
). On the other hand, by (iii) (b),
\Psi_{a,r}(N)
is non-empty for each
N\in\mathrm{Glimm}(A)\setminus Y
. As the function
\Psi_{a,r}\colon\mathrm{Glimm}(A)\to 2^{{\mathbb{C}}}
is lower semi-continuous, by Michael’s selection theorem there exists a continuous function

F_{a}\colon\mathrm{Glimm}(A)\to{\mathbb{C}}
such that
F_{a}(N)\in\Psi_{a,r}(N)
for all
N\in Y
. Moreover,
F_{a}
may be chosen such that
F_{a}|_{Y}=f_{a}
(
[
18
, Proposition 1.4]
). Let
z\in Z(A)
be the central element such that
\widehat{z}=F_{a}
. We now use
[
6
, Theorem 4.12]
to show that
\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}(D_{A}(a),z)\leq r
.

Let
\tau\in T(A)
be an extreme trace. By
[
6
, Lemma 2.4]
,
\tau|_{Z(A)}
is a pure state on
Z(A)
. Hence, there exists
N\in\mathrm{Glimm}(A)
such that
\tau(N\cap Z(A))=0
. By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality for states,
\tau(N)=\{0\}
, so that
N\in Y
. Thus, by (iii) (a) and our construction of
z
,

\tau(a)=f_{a}(N)=\tau(z).

Then, by the Krein-Milman theorem,
\tau(a)=\tau(z)
for all
\tau\in T(A)
.

Let
M\in\mathrm{Max}(A)
and let
N
be the unique Glimm ideal contained in
M
. Then
z+N=\widehat{z}(N)1+N
, and so
W_{A/M}(z+M)=\{\widehat{z}(N)\}
. Since
\widehat{z}(N)\in\Psi_{a,r}(N)
, we have that

\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}(W_{A/M}(a+M),W_{A/M}(z+M))\leq r.

By
[
6
, Theorem 4.12]
, the distance between
D_{A}(a)
and
D_{A}(z)=\{z\}
is at most
r
. Since
r>0
is arbitrary,
\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}(D_{A}(a),Z(A))=0
.

Finally suppose that
a=a^{*}
. Let us show that (iii)
\Longrightarrow
(i). Suppose that we have (iii). In this case
\Psi_{a}
is l.s.c. by Lemma
3.4
. Thus, by the Michael selection theorem, there exists a continuous selection
F_{a}\colon\mathrm{Glimm}(A)\to{\mathbb{C}}
such that
F_{a}|_{Y}=f_{a}
and
F_{a}(N)\in\Psi_{a}(N)
for all
N\in\mathrm{Glimm}(A)
. Let
z\in Z(A)
be the central element corresponding to
F_{a}
. By Proposition
4.2
,
z\in D_{A}(a)\cap Z(A)
. Hence
a\in\mathrm{Dix}(A)
.
∎

Proposition 4.5
.

The following classes of elements
a
satisfy that
0\in D_{A}(a)
, and thus belong to
\mathrm{Dix}(A)
.

(i)

Self-commutators
[x^{*},x]
, with
x\in A
.

(ii)

Quasinilpotent elements.

Proof.

(i) Let
a=[x^{*},x]
for some
x\in A
. Then obviously
\tau(a)=0
for all
\tau\in T(A)
. Furthermore, as seen in the proof of Proposition
3.11

0\in W_{A/I}(a+I)
for every quotient
A/I
. Thus, we can apply
[
6
, Theorem 4.4]
to conclude that
0\in D_{A}(a)
.

(ii) If
a\in A
is quasinilpotent then, by the Murphy-West spectral radius formula, for each
\epsilon>0
there is an invertible element
x\in A
such that
\|xax^{-1}\|<\epsilon
(see
[
19
, Proposition 4]
). In particular,
\tau(a)=0
for all
\tau\in T(A)
. Also, for each ideal
I
of
A
,
a+I
is a quasinilpotent element of
A/I
so that
0\in W_{A/I}(a+I)
. Invoking again
[
6
, Theorem 4.4]
, we conclude that
0\in D_{A}(a)
.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

Unlike
\mathrm{Mag}(A)
, the set
\mathrm{Dix}(A)
does not have to contain finite products of nilpotent elements (see Proposition
3.11
). To demonstrate this, let
A
be the
C^{*}
-algebra from Example
3.16
. Consider the functions
\tilde{e}_{ij}
(
1\leq i,j\leq 2
) in
A
which are constant and equal to the matrix units

e_{ij}\in M_{2}({\mathbb{C}})
(
1\leq i,j\leq 2
). Then
\tilde{e}_{12}
and
\tilde{e}_{21}
are nilpotent elements of
A
, but
\tilde{e}_{12}\tilde{e}_{21}=\tilde{e}_{11}\notin\mathrm{Dix}(A)
. Indeed, if
N_{1}=\{a\in A:a(1)=0\}
, then
N_{1}\in\mathrm{Glimm}(A)
and
A/N_{1}\cong M_{2}({\mathbb{C}})\oplus{\mathbb{C}}
. The maps
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\colon M_{2}({\mathbb{C}})\oplus{\mathbb{C}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
given by
\tau_{1}((a_{ij}),\mu)):=1/2(a_{11}+a_{22})
and
\tau_{2}((a_{ij}),\mu)):=\mu
are tracial states of
M_{2}({\mathbb{C}})\oplus{\mathbb{C}}
such that
1/2=\tau_{1}(e_{11})\neq\tau_{2}(e_{11})=0
. In particular, the condition (iii) (a1) of Theorem
4.4
is not satisfied for
a=\tilde{e}_{11}
and
N=N_{1}
.

Let us denote by
\overline{\mathrm{Dix}}(A)
the set of
a\in A
such that
\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}(D_{A}(a),Z(A))=0
. It is straightforward to check that
\overline{\mathrm{Dix}}(A)
is a closed set (see the proof of Proposition
3.8
).

Note that if
T(A)=\varnothing
, then
\overline{\mathrm{Dix}}(A)=\overline{\mathrm{Mag}}(A)
, by Theorems
3.3
and
4.4
.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
A
be a unital
C^{*}
-algebra such that for all
N\in\mathrm{Glimm}(A)\backslash Y
,
\mathrm{Max}^{N}(A)
is a singleton. Then
\mathrm{Dix}(A)=\overline{\mathrm{Dix}}(A)
.

Proof.

For
N\in\mathrm{Glimm}(A)\backslash Y
, say
\mathrm{Max}^{N}(A)=\{M_{N}\}
. Let
a\in\overline{\mathrm{Dix}}(A)
. Let
f_{a}\colon Y\to{\mathbb{C}}
denote the function described in Theorem
4.4
.
Define
\tilde{\Psi}_{a}\colon\mathrm{Glimm}(A)\to 2^{{\mathbb{C}}}
by

\widetilde{\Psi}_{a}(N):=\begin{cases}\{f_{a}(N)\}&\hbox{if }N\in Y,\\
W_{A/M_{N}}(a+M_{N})&\hbox{if }N\in\mathrm{Glimm}(A)\backslash Y.\end{cases}

Claim:
\widetilde{\Psi}_{a}
is l.s.c. To prove this claim, let us first examine
\widetilde{\Psi}_{a}
on the open set
\mathrm{Glimm}(A)\backslash Y
. Consider the map

\mathrm{Glimm}(A)\backslash Y\ni N\mapsto M_{N}\in\mathrm{Max}(A).

Let us prove that it is continuous. Let

be a convergent net in
\mathrm{Glimm}(A)\backslash Y

(M_{N_{\alpha}})_{\alpha}
has convergent subnets, by the compactness of
\mathrm{Max}(A)
. Let us show that they all converge to

is a singleton for

\mathrm{Glimm}(A)\backslash Y

On the other hand, the set-valued map
\mathrm{Max}(A)\ni M\mapsto W_{A/M}(a+M)
is l.s.c. by

6

\mathrm{Glimm}(A)\backslash Y

Let us prove the lower semicontinuity of
\widetilde{\Psi}_{a}
at
N\in Y

N\in Y
. Let
\epsilon>0

(N_{\alpha})_{\alpha}

\mathrm{Glimm}(A)

and
\widetilde{\Psi}_{a}(N_{\alpha})\cap B_{\epsilon}(f_{a}(N))=\varnothing

on

Passing to a convergent subnet, and reindexing, let us assume that

for some

(by the characterization of elements of

in Theorem

on
\mathrm{Max}(A)
(

This completes the proof of the lower semicontinuity of

Let

∎

Lemma 4.10
.

The set
Z(A)+\overline{[A,A]}
contains
\overline{\mathrm{Dix}}(A)
and is equal to the closed linear span of
\mathrm{Dix}(A)

Proof.

Let us show first that

A/N
by a Glimm ideal

maps to an element in
{\mathbb{C}}1+\overline{[A/N,A/N]}
. Proof: Say
z_{n}+c_{n}\to a
, with
z_{n}\in Z(A)
and
c_{n}\in\overline{[A,A]}
for all
n
. Passing to the quotient
A/N
we get

\widehat{z}_{n}(N)1+(c_{n}+N)\to a+N.

Let
\tau\in T(A/N)
(recall that
N\in Y
). Evaluating on
\tau
, we see that
\widehat{z}_{n}(N)\to\tau(a)
. Hence,
c_{n}+N
also converges to an element of
\overline{[A/N,A/N]}

Let
f_{a}(N)
denote the common value of all tracial states of
A/N
on
a+N
, for
N\in Y
. As shown in the proof of Theorem
4.4
,
f_{a}\colon Y\to{\mathbb{C}}
is a continuous function (uniform limit of the functions
\widehat{z}_{n}|_{Y}

By the Tietze extension theorem, we can extend

to a continuous function on

such that
\widehat{z}(N)=f_{a}(N)
for all

a-z
is in the kernel of every extreme trace, since each extreme trace factors through
A/N
for some

a-z
is in the kernel of every trace. It is thus an element in
\overline{[A,A]}
. (Indeed, by the Hahn-Banach theorem
\overline{[A,A]}
agrees with the intersection of the kernels of all bounded functionals that vanish on commutators, and the latter are expressible as linear combinations of tracial states via the Hahn-Jordan decomposition.)

Let us show that
\overline{\mathrm{Dix}}(A)
is contained in
Z(A)+\overline{[A,A]}
. Let

and
\phi_{n}\in\mathrm{Av}(A,\mathcal{U(A)})
be such that
\phi_{n}(a)-z_{n}\to 0

z_{n}+(a-\phi_{n}(a))\to a

belongs to

\overline{Z(A)+[A,A]}

Z(A)+\overline{[A,A]}

Finally, since

, and the latter linearly span

.
∎

Theorem 4.11
.

The following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

\mathrm{Dix}(A)=Z(A)+\overline{[A,A]}
.

(ii)

\mathrm{Dix}(A)
is closed under
\mathrm{Av}(A,\mathcal{U(A)})
.

(iii)

\mathrm{Dix}(A)
is closed under addition.

(iv)

(a)

For all
N\in Y
and
M\in\mathrm{Max}^{N}(A)
,
T(A/M)\neq\varnothing
.

(b)

For all
N\in\mathrm{Glimm}(A)\setminus Y
,
\mathrm{Max}^{N}(A)
is a singleton set.

Moreover, when these equivalent conditions hold,
\mathrm{Dix}(A)=\overline{\mathrm{Dix}}(A)
.

Proof.

It is clear that (i) implies (ii).

(ii)
\Longrightarrow
(iii). Suppose that (ii) holds. Let
a,b\in\mathrm{Dix}(A)
. Let
\epsilon>0
. Choose a unitary mixing operator
\alpha

and
y_{1}\in Z(A)
such that
\|\alpha(a)-y_{1}\|<\epsilon
. Now choose a unitary mixing operator
\beta
and
z_{1}\in Z(A)
such that

\|\beta(\alpha(b))-z_{1}\|<\epsilon
. Then, setting
\phi:=\beta\circ\alpha
, we have that

\|\phi(a)-y_{1}\|<\epsilon
and
\|\phi(b)-z_{1}\|<\epsilon
. Moreover,
\phi(a),\phi(b)\in\mathrm{Dix}(A)
. It now follows by a well-known argument of successive averaging that we can find
\psi_{n}\in\mathrm{Av}(A,\mathcal{U(A)})
such that
\psi_{n}(a)\to y\in Z(A)
and
\psi_{n}(b)\to z\in Z(A)
. For completeness, we include the proof. Choose
\phi_{1}\in\mathrm{Av}(A,\mathcal{U(A)})
and
y_{1},z_{1}\in Z(A)
such that
\|\phi_{1}(a)-y_{1}\|<1/2
and
\|\phi_{1}(b)-z_{1}\|<1/2
.
Set
a_{1}:=\phi_{1}(a)
and
b_{1}:=\phi_{1}(b)
. As
a_{1},b_{1}\in\mathrm{Dix}(A)
, there is
\phi_{2}\in\mathrm{Av}(A,\mathcal{U(A)})
and
y_{2},z_{2}\in Z(A)
such that
\|\phi_{2}(a_{1})-y_{2}\|<1/4
,
\|\phi_{2}(b_{1})-z_{2}\|<1/4
. Set
a_{2}:=\phi_{2}(a_{1})
and
b_{2}:=\phi_{2}(b_{1})
. Inductively, we get
\phi_{n}\in\mathrm{Av}(A,\mathcal{U(A)})
,
y_{n},z_{n}\in Z(A)
,
a_{n}\in D_{A}(a)
, and
b_{n}\in D_{A}(b)
, for
n=2,3,\ldots
, such that

\|\phi_{n}(a_{n-1})-y_{n}\|<\frac{1}{2^{n}},\qquad\|\phi_{n}(b_{n-1})-z_{n}\|<\frac{1}{2^{n}}.

The sequences
(y_{n})_{n}
and
(z_{n})_{n}
are Cauchy. Consequently, setting

y:=\lim_{n\to\infty}y_{n},\quad z:=\lim_{n\to\infty}z_{n},\quad\mbox{and}\quad\psi_{n}:=\phi_{n}\circ\ldots\circ\phi_{1}\in\mathrm{Av}(A,\mathcal{U(A)}),

we have that
\psi_{n}(a)\to y
and
\psi_{n}(b)\to z
. Observe now that
\psi_{n}(a+b)\to y+z
. Hence,
\mathrm{Dix}(A)
is closed under addition, i.e., (iii) holds.

(iii)
\Longrightarrow
(iv). Assume that
\mathrm{Dix}(A)
is closed under addition. Let
N\in\mathrm{Glimm}(A)
.
Assume that
N\in Y
, so that
A/N
has a tracial state. Suppose, for the sake of contradiction, there exists
M\in\mathrm{Max}^{N}(A)
such that
T(A/M)=\varnothing
. By Pop’s Theorem
[
21
, Theorem 1]
we can write the unit of
A/M
as a finite sum of commutators. By
[
23
, Theorem 4.2]
, these commutators are finite sums of square zero elements.
Thus, we can write the unit of
A/M
as a finite sum of square zero elements. Lifting those square zero elements to square zero elements in
A
and adding, we obtain an element
a\in A
which is a lift of
1+M\in A/M
and is a sum of square zero elements (whence in
[A,A]
). Using Proposition
4.5
, and the assumption that
\mathrm{Dix}(A)
is closed under addition, we see that
a\in[A,A]\cap\mathrm{Dix}(A)
. Let
z\in D_{A}(a)\cap Z(A)
. Then
z+N=\lambda 1+N
and
z+M=\lambda 1+M
for the same scalar
\lambda
. Since
\lambda 1+N\in D_{A/N}(a+N)
and
T(A/N)\neq\varnothing
,
\lambda
is the common value of all tracial states of
A/N
on
a+N
. Since
a+N\in[A/N,A/N]
, we have
\lambda=0
. But
\lambda 1+M\in D_{A/M}(a+M)=\{1+M\}
, which gives
\lambda=1
. This proves (iv)(a).

As for (iv) (b), assume that
N\in\mathrm{Glimm}(A)\setminus Y
, so that
T(A/N)=\varnothing
. Let
\dot{a}
be an arbitrary element of
A/N
. Combining
[
21
, Theorem 1]
and
[
23
, Theorem 4.2]
, we can write
\dot{a}
as a sum of square zero elements in
A/N
. Lifting them back to
A
as square zero elements, using Proposition
4.5
and the assumption that
\mathrm{Dix}(A)
is closed under addition, we get a lift
a\in\mathrm{Dix}(A)
of
\dot{a}
. Let
z\in D_{A}(a)\cap Z(A)
. Then
z+N\in D_{A/N}(\dot{a})
. But
z+N
is a scalar in
A/N
. This shows that
A/N
has the Dixmier property and that
Z(A/N)={\mathbb{C}}(1+N)
. Since
A/N
is weakly central and has trivial centre, it has a unique maximal ideal.

(iv)
\Longrightarrow
(i). By assumption, for any
N\in\mathrm{Glimm}(A)\setminus Y
,
N
is contained in a unique maximal ideal
M_{N}
of
A
.

By Theorem
4.7
, it suffices to show that
\overline{\mathrm{Dix}}(A)=Z(A)+\overline{[A,A]}
. That
\overline{\mathrm{Dix}}(A)
is contained in
Z(A)+\overline{[A,A]}
has already been established in Lemma
4.10
. To prove the opposite inclusion, it suffices to show that
\overline{[A,A]}\subseteq\overline{\mathrm{Dix}}(A)
. Let
a\in\overline{[A,A]}
. Since traces vanish on commutators, we may define
f_{a}\colon Y\to{\mathbb{C}}
to be identically 0, and for
N\in Y
and
M\in\mathrm{Max}^{N}(A)
we obtain
0\in W_{A/M}(a+M)
by (iv)(a) so that
f_{a}(N)=0\in\Psi_{a}(N)
.
On the other hand, for
N\in\mathrm{Glimm}(A)\backslash Y
we have that
\Psi_{a}(N)=W_{A/M_{N}}(a+M_{N})\neq\varnothing
. It follows from Theorem
4.4
that
a\in\overline{\mathrm{Dix}}(A)
.
∎

Remark 4.12

Theorem

-algebras such that any non-zero simple quotient has at least one tracial state. These include all unital postliminal

Example 4.13
.

Let
A
be a unital
C^{*}
-algebra with a unique faithful trace
\tau
and a unique maximal ideal
M
such that
A/M
is traceless.
(See
[
6
, Section 2]
for concrete examples of
C^{*}
-algebras with these properties.) Since
A
is weakly central,
A=\mathrm{Mag}(A)
. On the other hand,

\mathrm{Dix}(A)=\{a\in A:\tau(a)\in W_{A/M}(a+M)\}.

This set is not closed under addition (Theorem
4.11
(iv) (a) fails) and maps onto
A/M
. Moreover,
\mathrm{Dix}(A)=\overline{\mathrm{Dix}}(A)
in this case, by Theorem
4.7
. This description of
\mathrm{Dix}(A)
can be considered in terms of convexity.
Viewing
S(A/M)
as a
w^{*}
-compact convex subset of
A^{*}
that does not contain the tracial state
\tau
, a suitable form of the Hahn-Banach theorem tells us that we can separate by an element of the dual space
A
(for example, any element
a\in M
such that
\tau(a)\neq 0
).
Then the above description of
\mathrm{Dix}(A)
tells us that the elements
a\in A
that can witness this separation are precisely those in the complement of
\mathrm{Dix}(A)
.

By
[
2
, Proposition 2.1.10]
any unital
C^{*}
-algebra
A
contains a largest ideal
J_{dp}(A)
with the Dixmier property (in the sense that
J_{dp}(A)+{\mathbb{C}}1_{A}
has the Dixmier property). Since
Z(J)=J\cap Z(A)
for any ideal
J
of
A
, we have that
Z(A)+J_{dp}(A)\subseteq\mathrm{Dix}(A)
. Note also that if
J
is an ideal of
A
that is contained in
\mathrm{Dix}(A)
, then
J
has the Dixmier property and hence is contained in
J_{dp}(A)
. This follows from the fact that
D_{J+{\mathbb{C}}1_{A}}(a)=D_{A}(a)
for all

(see
[
3
, Remark 2.6]
).

In the next theorem we describe
J_{dp}(A)
more explicitly. Before doing so, we introduce some notation. Consider the set
X\subseteq\mathrm{Glimm}(A)
defined as

X:=\{N\in\mathrm{Glimm}(A):\hbox{$A/N$ has the Dixmier property and trivial centre}\}.

By
[
6
, Corollary 2.10]
,
N\in X
if and only if
\mathrm{Max}^{N}(A)
is a singleton
\{M_{N}\}
,
A/N
has at most one tracial state, and if
A/N
does have a tracial state then it factors through

. Now, for

, define

I_{N}:=\left(\bigcap_{M\in\mathrm{Max}^{N}(A)}M\right)\cap\bigcap_{\tau\in T(A/N)}I_{\tau},

where, for
\tau\in T(A/N)

We adopt the usual convention that the intersection of an empty set of ideals of

Theorem 4.14
.

We have

J_{dp}(A)=\bigcap_{N\in\mathrm{Glimm}(A)\backslash X}I_{N},

with
X\subseteq\mathrm{Glimm}(A)
and
I_{N}
for
N\in\mathrm{Glimm}(A)\backslash X
as defined above.

Proof.

Call
J
the intersection of the ideals on the right hand side of the equation. Let us show that
J_{dp}(A)
is contained in
J
.
Since
J_{dp}(A)+{\mathbb{C}}1_{A}
has the Dixmier property, it is weakly central, and hence so is
J_{dp}(A)
(by
[
5
, Remark 3.10]
). Hence
J_{dp}(A)\subseteq J_{wc}(A)
. So,
by the spectral characterization of

(
3.4
), we have that
J_{dp}(A)\subseteq\bigcap_{M\in\mathrm{Max}^{N}(A)}M
for each
N\in\mathrm{Glimm}(A)\backslash X
such that
\mathrm{Max}^{N}(A)
is not a singleton.

Suppose that
N\in\mathrm{Glimm}(A)\backslash X
is such that
T(A/N)\neq\varnothing
. Let
\tau\in T(A/N)
, and suppose for the sake of contradiction that
J_{dp}(A)
is not contained in
I_{\tau}
. Then there is a positive element

such that
\tau(b+N)>0
. Since
b\in\mathrm{Dix}(A)
,

can be averaged in
A/N
by unitary mixing operators to a scalar multiple of the identity. Hence,
\tau(b+N)1_{A/N}\in D_{A/N}(b+N)
. It follows that the ideal
J_{dp}(A)
maps onto

A/N
under the quotient map
A\to A/N
. Since
J_{dp}(A)+{\mathbb{C}}1_{A}
has the Dixmier property, with centre contained in

,
A/N
has the Dixmier property and trivial centre. Thus

, contradicting our choice of
N
.

It remains to deal with the case where
N\in\mathrm{Glimm}(A)\setminus X
and
N
is contained in a unique maximal ideal
M_{N}
of
A
. Since
N\notin X
,
T(A/N)\neq\varnothing
. Let
\tau\in T(A/N)
. Then
I_{\tau}
is a proper ideal of

containing

, and hence it is contained in

. Since
J_{dp}(A)\subseteq I_{\tau}
(by the previous paragraph),
J_{dp}(A)\subseteq M_{N}
as required.

For the reverse inclusion, let

. We claim that
a\in\mathrm{Dix}(A)
. To prove this, we apply Theorem
4.4
. Observe that
\Psi_{a}(N)=\{0\}
for all
N\in\mathrm{Glimm}(A)
for which
\mathrm{Max}^{N}(A)
is not a singleton. Hence,
\Psi_{a}(N)\neq\varnothing
for all
N\in\mathrm{Glimm}(A)
. Let
N
be a Glimm ideal in the set
Y
(i.e., such that
T(A/N)
is non-empty). Suppose first that

. Then, since

for all
\tau\in T(A/N)
,

belongs to the kernel of all traces in
T(A/N)
. Set

. Suppose on the other hand that

. Define

, where
\tau_{N}
is the unique tracial state of

. Now

satisfies the conditions (a1) and (a2) of Theorem

. Hence,

(a set invariant under unitary mixing operators), it follows by the method of successive averaging already employed in the proof of Theorem

that

∎

Theorem 4.15
.

The following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

A/J_{dp}(A)
is abelian.

(ii)

\mathrm{Dix}(A)=Z(A)+J_{dp}(A)
,

(iii)

\mathrm{Dix}(A)
is closed under multiplication.

Moreover, under these equivalent conditions
J_{dp}(A)=J_{wc}(A)
, so that

(4.3)

\mathrm{Dix}(A)=\mathrm{Mag}(A)=Z(A)+J_{dp}(A)=Z(A)+\overline{[A,A]}.

Proof.

(i)
\Longrightarrow
(ii). Suppose that
A/J_{dp}(A)
is abelian and let
a\in\mathrm{Dix}(A)
. Then
a\in\mathrm{CQ}(A)
and so, since the image of
a
in
A/J_{dp}
is central,
a\in Z(A)+J_{dp}(A)
.
Thus
\mathrm{Dix}(A)\subseteq Z(A)+J_{dp}(A)
and the reverse
inclusion always holds as previously noted.

Clearly (ii)
\Longrightarrow
(iii).

(iii)
\Longrightarrow
(i). Let us show first that
\mathrm{Dix}(A)
is closed under addition. Let
x\in A
be a square zero element and let
y\in\mathrm{Dix}(A)
. Then

x+y=(1+x)(1+(1-x)y)-1.

Using that
\mathrm{Dix}(A)
is closed under multiplication (and translations by scalar multiples of 1) we deduce that the right hand side is in
\mathrm{Dix}(A)
. Hence,
x+y
is in
\mathrm{Dix}(A)
. It follows that
\mathrm{Dix}(A)
contains all finite sums of square zero elements. This enables us to check the spectral characterization in Theorem
4.11
(iv) for when
\mathrm{Dix}(A)
is closed under addition. Indeed, in the proof of Theorem
4.11
(iii)
\Longrightarrow
(iv), all we used from (iii) was that
\mathrm{Dix}(A)
contains all finite sums of square zero elements.

Since
\mathrm{Dix}(A)
is closed under addition,
\mathrm{Dix}(A)=Z(A)+\overline{[A,A]}
by Theorem
4.11
.
In particular,
\mathrm{Dix}(A)
is norm-closed and contains
\overline{[A,A]}
. Hence, it contains the
C^{*}
-algebra generated by

\overline{[A,A]}
. But this
C^{*}
-algebra agrees with the ideal generated by
\overline{[A,A]}
(
[
23
, Theorem 1.3]
). Thus
\mathrm{Ideal}([A,A])\subseteq\mathrm{Dix}(A)
.
Hence, as noted earlier, this ideal is contained in
J_{dp}(A)
. Thus
A/J_{dp}(A)
is abelian, as desired.

Finally, assume that
A/J_{dp}(A)
is abelian. Let
B:=J_{wc}(A)+{\mathbb{C}}1_{A}
, so that
B
is a unital weakly central
C^{*}
-algebra. Since
J_{dp}(B)
contains
J_{dp}(A)
,
B/J_{dp}(B)
is abelian. As weak centrality is equivalent to the CQ-property (
[
24
]
), this implies
B=Z(B)+J_{dp}(B)
. Hence
B
has the Dixmier property, and so
J_{wc}(A)=J_{dp}(A)
.
The equality (
4.3
) now follows directly from (ii), Theorem
4.11
, and Theorem
3.15
.
∎

Remark 4.16
.

Note that the equivalent conditions of Theorem
4.15
are satisfied for the “Dixmier
C^{*}
-algebra” and also for all
2
-subhomogeneous
C^{*}
-algebras (see
[
5
, Corollary 4.15]
). On the other hand, if
A
is the
C^{*}
-algebra from Example
3.16
, then
A/J_{wc}(A)
is non-abelian, and thus
\mathrm{Dix}(A)
fails to be closed under multiplication.

Corollary 4.17
.

Suppose that either
\mathrm{Mag}(A)
or
\mathrm{Dix}(A)
is closed under addition. Then
\mathrm{Mag}(A)=\mathrm{Dix}(A)
if and only if
A
satisfies the equivalent conditions of Theorem
4.15
.

Proof.

If
\mathrm{Mag}(A)=\mathrm{Dix}(A)
then
\mathrm{Dix}(A)
is closed under multiplication by Theorem
3.15
. Conversely, if
A
satisfies the equivalent conditions of Theorem
4.15
then
\mathrm{Mag}(A)=\mathrm{Dix}(A)
as shown above.
∎

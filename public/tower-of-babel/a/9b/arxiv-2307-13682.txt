Now it remains to show (3) in

Proposition 2.14
.

Let
\Theta=\Theta^{\mathsf{CDM}^{-}}
. Let
\nu

of Mitchell order 0 in

. Let

\mathcal{M}_{\infty}

Proof.

For any
\mathcal{Q}\in\mathcal{F}^{*}_{g}(\mathcal{P},\Sigma)

\mathcal{Q}\in\mathcal{F}^{*}_{g}(\mathcal{P},\Sigma)

\pi_{\ult(\mathcal{Q},\nu_{\mathcal{Q}}),\infty}(\kappa_{\mathcal{Q}})=\alpha_{\mathcal{Q}}

Claim 2.14.2
.

∎

We need to show the equivalence in

in
\mathsf{CDM}^{-}
, not
\mathcal{V}[g]
. The problem is that the iteration embeddings to
\mathcal{M}_{\infty}

•

For any non-dropping
\Sigma
-iterate

of
\mathcal{P}

•

For any

For any

-pullback strategy of

.
Also, let
\mathcal{M}_{\infty}(\mathcal{Q}_{X},\Psi_{X})

under iteration maps and let

•

For any

, we define
\tau_{X,Y}\colon\mathcal{Q}_{X}\to\mathcal{Q}_{Y}
by
\tau_{X,Y}=\tau_{Y}^{-1}\circ\tau_{X}

Definition 2.15
.

For any

, We say that

extends

\mathcal{Q}_{Y}=\{\tau_{X,Y}(f)(s)\mid f\in\mathcal{Q}_{X}\land f\colon[\kappa_{X}]^{<\omega}\to\mathcal{Q}_{X}\land s\in[\kappa_{Y}]^{<\omega}\}.

Definition 2.16
.

Let
X\in\wp_{\omega_{1}}(\mathcal{M}_{\infty}^{\mathsf{b}})
. We say that
X
is
condensing
if for any

extending

(1)

\tau_{X}=k^{X}_{Y}\circ\tau_{X,Y}

(2)

We also say that

Now we show

assuming

Theorem 2.17
.

Let
\Theta=\Theta^{\mathsf{CDM}^{-}}

of Mitchell order 0 in

if and only if

Proof.

Let

in
\mathcal{M}_{\infty}
.
Then there is a genericity iterate
\mathcal{Q}
of
\mathcal{P}
such that

By

There is a

\mathcal{Q}_{Z}=\ult(\mathcal{Q}_{Y},\nu_{Y})

is condensing,

Proof.

Note that
\nu
is countably complete by

Now notice that

It follows from

that

\displaystyle A\in\nu

\displaystyle\iff\tau_{Y}^{-1}(A)\in\nu_{Y}\iff\kappa_{Y}\in\pi_{\nu_{Y}}(\tau_{Y}^{-1}(A))

\displaystyle\iff k^{Z}_{Y}(\kappa_{Y})\in A\iff\alpha_{Y}\in A.

Therefore,
\{\alpha_{Y}\mid Y\in\wp_{\omega_{1}}(\mathcal{M}_{\infty}^{\mathsf{b}})\land\text{$Y$ extends $X$}\}\subseteq A
.

Claim 2.17.2
.

The set
\{\alpha_{Y}\mid Y\in\wp_{\omega_{1}}(\mathcal{M}_{\infty}^{\mathsf{b}})\land\text{ $Y$ extends $X$}\}
contains a club subset of

in
\mathsf{CDM}^{-}
.

Proof.

For
\eta<\Theta
, let

Let

.

is clearly a club subset of
\Theta\cap\mathrm{Cof}(\omega)
in
\mathsf{CDM}^{-}
. We shall show that for any

,
\eta
is
\alpha_{Y}
for some

such that
Y
extends
X
. Now fix

and take a countable cofinal subset

\eta\leq\sup(\Theta\cap\mathrm{Hull}^{\mathcal{M}_{\infty}^{\mathsf{b}}}(Y^{*}))=\alpha_{Y^{*}}

,

This completes the proof of

2.4.
Existence of condensing sets

Now our goal is to show
2.22
.
To give an easy but useful characterization of condensing sets, we introduce one more technical notion.

Definition 2.18
.

Let
X\in\wp_{\omega_{1}}(\mathcal{M}_{\infty}^{\mathsf{b}})
and let
A\in\mathcal{M}_{\infty}^{\mathsf{b}}
. Then we write

\displaystyle T_{X,A}

\displaystyle=\{\langle\phi,s\rangle\mid s\in[\kappa_{X}]^{<\omega}\land\mathcal{M}_{\infty}^{\mathsf{b}}\models\phi[\tau_{X}(s),A]\}

\displaystyle T^{*}_{X,A}

\displaystyle=\{\langle\phi,s\rangle\mid s\in[\kappa_{X}]^{<\omega}\land\mathcal{M}_{\infty}^{\mathsf{b}}\models\phi[\pi^{\Psi_{X}}_{\mathcal{Q}_{X},\infty}(s),A]\}

We say that
X
has
A
-condensation
if whenever
Y
extends
X
,
T_{Y,A}=T^{*}_{Y,A}
.

Remark 2.19
.

Note that if
A\in\mathrm{ran}(\tau_{X})
, then

T_{X,A}=\{\langle\phi,s\rangle\mid s\in[\kappa_{X}]^{<\omega}\land\mathcal{Q}_{X}\models\phi[s,\tau_{X}^{-1}(A)]\}

and thus
T_{X,A}\in\mathcal{Q}_{X}
.
So, if
X\subseteq Y
then
\tau_{X,Y}(T_{X,A})=T_{Y,A}
by the elementarity of
\tau_{X,Y}
.
On the other hand, we do not know
T^{*}_{X,A}\in\mathcal{Q}_{X}
a priori.
Even if
T^{*}_{X,A}\in\mathcal{Q}_{X}
, the elementarity of
\tau_{X,Y}
does not imply
\tau_{X,Y}(T^{*}_{X,A})=T^{*}_{Y,A}
.

Lemma 2.20
.

Let
X\in\wp_{\omega_{1}}(\mathcal{M}_{\infty}^{\mathsf{b}})
.
Then
X
is condensing if and only if
X
has
A
-condensation for any
A\in X
.

Proof.

Suppose that
X
is condensing.
Let
Y\in\wp_{\omega_{1}}(\mathcal{M}_{\infty}^{\mathsf{b}})
extending
X
and let
k^{X}_{Y}\colon\mathcal{Q}_{Y}\to\mathcal{M}_{\infty}^{\mathsf{b}}
be the unique elementary map witnessing it.
Also, let
A\in X
and write
A_{X}=\tau_{X}^{-1}(A)
.
Then for any formula
\phi
and
s\in[\kappa_{X}]^{<\omega}
,

\displaystyle\langle\phi,s\rangle\in T_{Y,A}

\displaystyle\iff\mathcal{M}_{\infty}^{\mathsf{b}}\models\phi[\tau_{Y}(s),\tau_{X}(A_{X})]

\displaystyle\iff\mathcal{Q}_{Y}\models[s,\tau_{X,Y}(A_{X})]\;\;\text{by the elementarity of $\tau_{Y}$}

\displaystyle\iff\mathcal{M}_{\infty}^{\mathsf{b}}\models\phi[k^{X}_{Y}(s),(k^{X}_{Y}\circ\tau_{X,Y})(A_{X})]\;\;\text{by the elementarity of $k^{X}_{Y}$}

\displaystyle\iff\mathcal{M}_{\infty}^{\mathsf{b}}\models\phi[\pi^{\Psi_{Y}}_{\mathcal{Q}_{Y},\infty}(s),\tau_{X}(A_{X})]\;\;\text{by the property of $k^{X}_{Y}$}

\displaystyle\iff\langle\phi,s\rangle\in T^{*}_{Y,A}.

Therefore,
X
has
A
-condensation.

To show the reverse direction, suppose that
X
has
A
-condensation for any
A\in X
.
It easily follows that
X
has
A
-condensation for any
A\in\mathrm{ran}(\tau_{X})
.
Let
Y\in\wp_{\omega_{1}}(\mathcal{M}_{\infty}^{\mathsf{b}})
extending
X
. We define
k^{X}_{Y}\colon\mathcal{Q}_{Y}\to\mathcal{M}_{\infty}^{\mathsf{b}}
by

k^{X}_{Y}(\tau_{X,Y}(f)(s))=\tau_{X}(f)(\pi^{\Psi_{Y}}_{\mathcal{Q}_{Y},\infty}(s))

for any
f\in\mathcal{Q}_{X}
and
s\in[\kappa_{Y}]^{<\omega}
. This is a well-defined elementary embedding; for any formula
\phi
and
s\in[\kappa_{Y}]^{<\omega}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{Y}\models\phi[\tau_{X,Y}(f)(s)]

\displaystyle\iff\mathcal{M}_{\infty}^{\mathsf{b}}\models\phi[\tau_{X}(f)(\tau_{Y}(s))]

\displaystyle\iff\langle\phi^{*},s\rangle\in T_{Y,\tau_{X}(f)}=T^{*}_{Y,\tau_{X}(f)}

\displaystyle\iff\mathcal{M}_{\infty}^{\mathsf{b}}\models\phi[\tau_{X}(f)(\pi^{\Psi_{Y}}_{\mathcal{Q}_{Y},\infty}(s))],

where
\phi^{*}
is a formula such that
\phi^{*}[\tau_{Y}(s),\tau_{X}(f)]\equiv\phi[\tau_{X}(f)(\tau_{Y}(s))]
.
∎

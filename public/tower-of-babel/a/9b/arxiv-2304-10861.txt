\supp B_{n}\subset\subset E_{2}\subset\subset\mathcal{C}
for
E_{1},E_{2}
being smooth subsets then using divergence theorem we have the following for an outside normal
\mathcal{n}
and
\phi\in W^{1,2}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{C}}div(A_{n}+B_{n})\phi+(A_{n}+B_{n},D\phi)dx=\int_{\partial E_{1}}\phi(A_{n},\mathcal{n})d\sigma+\int_{\partial E_{2}}\phi(B_{n},\mathcal{n})d\sigma=0

Since
|Z_{u}|=0
we get that
-div(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u+a(x)|\nabla u|^{q-2}\nabla u)=\frac{g(x)}{u^{\delta}}+h(x)f(u)
a.e. in
\mathcal{C}
. Choose
\phi\equiv k\neq 0
to get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{C}}\frac{g(x)}{u^{\delta}}+h(x)f(u)\phi

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{C}}-div(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u+a(x)|\nabla u|^{q-2}\nabla u)\phi

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\longrightarrow\infty}\int_{\mathcal{C}}-div(A_{n}+B_{n})\phi=0

This gives a contradiction to the fact that
\frac{g(x)}{u^{\delta}}+h(x)f(u)
is positive. Thanks to Hopf’s lemma, a neighbourhood of boundary belongs to
\mathcal{C}
of
\Omega\setminus Z_{u}
. If there exists another compactly contained component
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
then it would be a contradiction to what we have just proved. Hence
\Omega\setminus Z_{u}
is connected.
∎

4.
Symmetry And Monotonicity

In this section, we prove the main theorem, which is the symmetry result. The existence of
C^{1,\alpha}(\bar{\Omega})
solutions to problem
(
1.1
)
is guaranteed by Assumption
1
, as shown in
[
17
]
. Here, we provide a detailed discussion of the moving plane method. Without loss of generality, we demonstrate the symmetry and monotonicity results in the
x_{1}
direction. For a ball, we can apply the same analysis in any given direction, which yields the radial and radially decreasing nature of the solutions. We begin with introducing some notations.

Notations:

We define
a:=\inf_{x\in\Omega}\ x_{1}
. For
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
with
a<\lambda<0
we set
\Omega_{\lambda}:=\{x\in\Omega:x_{1}<\lambda\}
. The reflection of
x\in\Omega_{\lambda}
with respect to
T_{\lambda}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:x_{1}=\lambda\}
is denoted by
x_{\lambda}:=R_{\lambda}(x)=(2\lambda-x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n})
. We set
u_{\lambda}(x)=u(x_{\lambda})
. Finally we define

(4.13)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{0}:=\{a<\lambda<0:u\leq u_{t}\ \text{in}\ \Omega_{t}\ \text{for \ all \ }t\in(a,\lambda]\}

Proof of Theorem
1.4

Proof.

Our first claim is that
\Lambda_{0}\neq\emptyset
. Using theorem
2.1
, we can establish that
\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{1}}>0
in
\Omega_{\lambda}\cup R_{\lambda}(\Omega_{\lambda})
for small
\lambda-a
. Consequently,
u<u_{\lambda}
in
\Omega_{\lambda}
(near the boundary). This implies that
\Lambda_{0}
is not an empty set when
\lambda-a
is small. We can then define
\lambda_{0}=\sup\Lambda_{0}
.

Claim:

\lambda_{0}=0
i.e.
u\leq u_{\lambda}
in
\Omega_{\lambda}
for every
\lambda\in(a,0]
. By contradiction, if
\lambda_{0}<0
then we shall show
u\leq u_{\lambda_{0}+\tau}
in
\Omega_{\lambda_{0}+\tau}
for any
0<\tau<\bar{\tau}
for some
\bar{\tau}>0
(small).

We get
u\leq u_{\lambda_{0}}
in
\Omega_{\lambda_{0}}
using continuity. It implies that
h(x)f(u(x))\leq h(x_{\lambda_{0}})f(u(x_{\lambda_{0}}))
by
H_{h}
and
H_{f}
of Assumption
1
. Using the mean value theorem and
H_{g}
, for some
C\geq 0
we get

\displaystyle\frac{g(x)}{u(x)^{\delta}}-\frac{g(x_{\lambda_{0}})}{u(x_{\lambda_{0}})^{\delta}}\leq

\displaystyle g(x_{\lambda_{0}})\big(\frac{1}{u(x)^{\delta}}-\frac{1}{u(x_{\lambda_{0}})^{\delta}}\big)\leq C(u(x_{\lambda_{0}})-u(x)).

So we can use SCP (Theorem
2.5
) for
\Omega\setminus Z_{u}
to conclude that
u<u_{\lambda_{0}}
in
\Omega_{\lambda_{0}}\setminus Z_{u}
unless
u\not\equiv u_{\lambda_{0}}
. Next, we will elaborate on the reasons why the last situation is not feasible.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be any connected component of
\Omega\setminus Z_{u}
. If
\partial\mathcal{C}\cap\partial\Omega\neq\emptyset
then
u\equiv u_{\lambda}
is not possible due to the zero Dirichlet boundary condition and positivity of
u
in the interior of the domain. Else if
\partial\mathcal{C}\cap\partial\Omega=\emptyset
, then we get a contradiction to the fact that
\Omega\setminus Z_{u}
is connected using local symmetry region.

Using uniform continuity, it can be shown that for any compact set
K\subset\Omega_{\lambda_{0}}\setminus Z_{u}
and any small
\overline{\tau}>0
,
u<u_{\lambda_{0}+\tau}
in
K
for
0<\tau<\overline{\tau}
. By applying theorem
2.1
, we can find a
\delta>0
, such that
u<u_{\lambda_{0}+\tau}
in
I_{\delta}(\partial\Omega)\cap\Omega_{\lambda_{0}+\tau}
. Furthermore, as a result of Hopf’s Lemma (Theorem
2.1
), we can also achieve the same outcome in a neighborhood of
\partial\Omega\cap T_{\lambda_{0}+\tau}
, following a similar idea that in
[
15
, Page no 20]
.

Since
|Z_{u}|=0
(Corollary
3
), there exists a compact set
K
large enough and
\tau
small enough so that
|\Omega_{\lambda_{0}+\tau}\setminus K|<\tau
for any fixed
\tau>0
. Setting
w_{\lambda_{0}+\tau}:=(u-u_{\lambda_{0}+\tau})^{+}
for any
0<\tau<\overline{\tau}
we have
\supp w_{\lambda_{0}+\tau}\subset\subset\Omega_{\lambda_{0}+\tau}
for
\bar{\tau}
small. Moreover,
w_{\lambda_{0}+\tau}=0
in
K
by construction. Take
\bar{\tau}
sufficiently small in order to guarantee the applicability of weak comparison principle for narrow domain (Theorem
2.4
).

Now using
H_{g}
and
H_{h}
(Assumption
1
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{g(x)}{u(x)^{\delta}}-\frac{g(x_{\lambda})}{u(x_{\lambda})^{\delta}}+h(x)f(u(x))-h(x_{\lambda})f(u(x_{\lambda}))\leq g(x_{\lambda})\Bigg(\frac{1}{u^{\delta}}-\frac{1}{u_{\lambda}^{\delta}}

\displaystyle\Bigg)+h(x_{\lambda})\bigg(f(u)-f(u_{\lambda})\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\|h_{\lambda}\|_{\infty}\bigg(f(u)-f(u_{\lambda})\bigg).

Here we use the facts that
\int_{[u\geq u_{\lambda}]}g(x_{\lambda})\big(\frac{1}{u^{\delta}}-\frac{1}{u_{\lambda}^{\delta}}\big)(u-u_{\lambda})\leq 0
and
0<h(x_{\lambda})\leq\|h_{\lambda}\|_{\infty}
.
By applying theorem
2.4
, we can infer that
w_{\lambda_{0}+\tau}=0
in
\Omega_{\lambda_{0}+\tau}
for any
0<\tau<\overline{\tau}
, where
\overline{\tau}>0
is small. This contradicts the definition of
\lambda_{0}
, so we can conclude that
\lambda_{0}
must be equal to zero. Consequently, we can obtain the desired symmetry result in the
x_{1}
direction by applying the moving plane procedure in the opposite direction.
Lemma
2.1
will imply that
\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{1}}<0
, that gives the monotonicity in
x_{1}
direction.
∎

5.
Strong Comparison Principle

In this section, we are looking for an SCP of the following equations.

(5.14)

\displaystyle-\Delta_{p}u_{1}-\Delta_{q}u_{1}=\frac{c}{u_{1}^{\delta}}+h_{1}(x)\;\text{in}\;\Omega\hskip 13.942pt\text{and}\hskip 13.942ptu_{1}=0\;\text{on}\;\partial\Omega

(5.15)

\displaystyle-\Delta_{p}u_{2}-\Delta_{q}u_{2}=\frac{c}{u_{2}^{\delta}}+h_{2}(x)\;\text{in}\;\Omega\hskip 13.942pt\text{and}\hskip 13.942ptu_{2}=0\;\text{on}\;\partial\Omega

where
\Omega\equiv B_{R}(0)
and
h_{1},h_{2}
are continuous radial functions on
B_{R}
. We assume
0\leq h_{1}\leq h_{2}
but
h_{1}\not\equiv h_{2}
in
\Omega
and
c>0
. The existence of the positive solutions is guaranteed in
[
17
]
. Since
h_{1}\leq h_{2}
, we have
u_{1}\leq u_{2}
using test function method. We are interested in proving
0<u_{1}<u_{2}
in
\Omega
and
\frac{\partial u_{1}}{\partial\nu}>\frac{\partial u_{2}}{\partial\nu}
on
\partial\Omega
. Here we use a technique similar to that in
[
14
]
. For the sake of completeness, we briefly discuss the underlying idea of the proof.

Proof of Theorem
1.5

Proof.

We define
w=u_{2}-u_{1}
and
U_{r}:=B_{R}\setminus B_{r}
for any
0<r<R
where
B_{R}(0)\equiv B_{R}
. We rewrite the equations (
5.14
) and (
5.15
) as follows

(5.16)

\left\{\begin{aligned} -div(A(x)\nabla w)-\lambda B(x)w&\geq h_{2}-h_{1}\geq 0\hskip 8.53581pt\text{in}\ U_{r}\\
w&\geq 0\hskip 59.75095pt\text{on}\ \partial U_{r}\end{aligned}\right.

for a matrix
A(x)=[a_{ij}(x)]
and a scalar function
B(x).
Here,

\displaystyle a_{ij}(x)=\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{s=p,q}|(1-t)\nabla u_{1}(x)+t\nabla u_{2}(x)|^{s-2}\Big\{

\displaystyle\delta_{ij}

\displaystyle+(s-2)

\displaystyle\frac{((1-t)u_{1_{i}}+tu_{2_{i}})((1-t)u_{1_{j}}+tu_{2_{j}})}{|(1-t)\nabla u_{1}(x)+t\nabla u_{2}(x)|^{2}}\Big\}dt

and

\displaystyle B(x)=-\delta\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dt}{((1-t)u_{1}(x)+tu_{2}(x))^{\delta+1}}

To write in this form, we use the fact that
\frac{du_{i}}{dr}<0
for
i=1,2
near the boundary. Under the given assumption on
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
we get that
A(x)
is uniformly elliptic in
U_{r}
for each
r>0
. Using the continuity of
h_{1}
and
h_{2}
, we can confirm the existence of
r_{0}>0
such that
h_{1}-h_{2}\not\equiv 0
in
U_{r_{0}}
and hence
w>0
in
U_{r}
for all
r<r_{0}
using
[
28
, Theorem 2.5.2]
.

Now we shall show the positivity of
w
in
B_{r_{0}}
. Using the radial symmetry of the solutions the problem (
5.14
) can be reduced to a system of ODEs:

(5.17)

\left\{\begin{aligned} u_{11}^{{}^{\prime}}=\alpha(r,u_{12}),\;u_{11}(r_{1})=u_{11,0}\\
u_{12}^{{}^{\prime}}=-\frac{N-1}{r}u_{12}+B_{1}(r,u_{11}),\;u_{12}(r_{1})=u_{12,0}\end{aligned}\right.

where
r_{1}\in(0,R)
,
u_{11}(r)=u_{1}(r)
,
u_{12}(r)=|u_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}(r)|^{p-2}u_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}(r)+|u_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}(r)|^{q-2}u_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}(r)
,
B_{1}(r,y)=-(\frac{c}{y^{\delta}}+h_{1}(r))
. The function

\alpha(r,y):(0,R)\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is the inverse of the function
a(r,y)=|y|^{p-2}y+|y|^{q-2}y.

Clearly
u_{11}(R)=u_{12}(0)=0
. Analogously we can write the equation (
5.15
) as

(5.18)

\left\{\begin{aligned} u_{21}^{{}^{\prime}}=\alpha(r,u_{22}),\;u_{21}(r_{1})=u_{21,0}\\
u_{22}^{{}^{\prime}}=-\frac{N-1}{r}u_{22}+B_{2}(r,u_{21}),\;u_{22}(r_{1})=u_{22,0}\end{aligned}\right.

where
u_{21}(R)=u_{22}(0)=0
and
B_{2}(r,y)=-(\frac{c}{y^{\delta}}+h_{2}(r))
. Since
a(y)
is strictly monotone and continuous we have
\frac{\partial\alpha}{\partial y}\geq 0
.

Suppose that there exists some
r^{\prime}<r_{0}
such that
w(r^{\prime})=0
i.e.
w
attains its minimum at
r^{\prime}
and hence
w^{\prime}(r^{\prime})=0
. Taking
r_{1}=r^{\prime}
in the systems of ODEs,

u_{11,0}=u_{21,0}
and
u_{12,0}=u_{22,0}
. For the function
b(x,u)=cu^{-\delta}
, we have
0\leq-\frac{\partial b}{\partial u}\in L^{\infty}_{loc}((-R,R)\times(0,\infty)),
and hence by using
[
10
, Lemma 3.2]
, we obtain
u_{2}(r)\leq u_{1}(r)\;\forall\;r\in[r_{1},R)
which contradicts the fact that
w>0
in
U_{r_{0}}.
Therefore,
0<u_{1}<u_{2}
in
B_{R}.
Finally we note that since
w>0
in
B_{R}
we can apply
[
28
, Theorem 2.7.1]
to conclude that

\frac{\partial u_{2}}{\partial\nu}<\frac{\partial u_{1}}{\partial\nu}<0.

∎

We note that the hypothesis of Theorem
1.5
could be modified as below and still the SCP holds.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
1<p\leq q\leq 2
and
h_{1}
,

for

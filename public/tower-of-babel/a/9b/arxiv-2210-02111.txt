Twisted equivariant HKR theorem for torus action and the small quantum group
Nicolas Hemelsoet

Abstract
We show that when a torus T T acts on a smooth variety X X , the twisted HKR isomorphism is equivariant. The main consequence is that the Bezrukavnikov-Lachowska isomorphism, relating the Hochschild cohomology of the principal block of the small quantum group to certain sheaf cohomology groups on the Springer resolution 𝒩 ~ \widetilde{\mathcal{N}} , can be upgraded to a ring isomorphism by a twist.

Introduction

The starting point of this article is the following theorem :

Theorem 1.1

(
[
1
]
)
.

There exists an algebra isomorphism

HH^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{u}_{0}(\mathfrak{g}))\cong\bigoplus_{i+j+k=\bullet}H^{i}(\widetilde{\mathcal{N}},\wedge^{j}T\widetilde{\mathcal{N}})^{k}

This theorem relates the Hochschild cohomology of the principal block of the small quantum group with an ”equivariant” version of the Hochschild cohomology of the Springer resolution. More formally, let us detail the notation :
\mathfrak{g}
is a semisimple Lie algebra,
\mathfrak{u}_{0}(\mathfrak{g})
is the principal block of the small quantum group associated to

,
\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}
is the

\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}:=T^{*}(G/B)=G\times_{B}\mathfrak{n}
where

is the connected simple algebraic group of adjoint type associated to
\mathfrak{g}

is a Borel subgroup and
\mathfrak{n}=[\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{b}]
where
\mathfrak{b}=Lie(B)
.
HH^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{u}_{0}(\mathfrak{g}))
is the Hochschild cohomology of
\mathfrak{u}_{0}(\mathfrak{g})
. The subscript

correspond to the grading acquired from the

action by dilations on the fiber on

. We refer to
[
1
]

for a more detailled exposition.

The multiplication on the left-hand side is given by the Yoneda product, and on the right-hand side by the exterior algebra structure. The purpose of this paper is to explain precisely how to obtain a multiplicative isomorphism, using a theorem by Kontsevitch. We will check the compatibility of the multiplicative version of the Hochschild-Kostant-Rosenberg theorem with a action of a torus.

Let
T
be a complex torus, and
X
be an irreducible smooth complex variety acted on by
T
. More generally, one can consider an arbitrary complex reductive group acting on a smooth variety

, if there exists an affine cover by invariant open subset. Let

be the Hochschild cohomology of

denotes the bigraded vector space
\bigoplus_{i,j}H^{i}(X,\wedge^{j}TX)
. Our first result is :

Theorem 1.2
.

The twisted HKR morphism
HH^{\bullet}(X)\to HT^{\bullet}(X)
is
T
-equivariant.

This result relies on the following two propositions :

Proposition 1.3
.

In the derived category of coherent sheaves on
X

D^{b}(\text{Coh}(X))
, the quasi-isomorphism

\iota^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\Delta}\cong\bigoplus_{i\in X}\Omega^{i}_{X}[i]

Let

be the Todd class of

gives a

When no group action is involved, the two statements are well-known (
[
7
]
, section

). The first proposition implies the famous Hochschild-Kostant-Rosenberg (HKR) theorem relating the Hochschild cohomology of

with sheaf cohomology of poly-vector fields. The second statement relates the multiplicative structures on both sides. Hence the essence of our results is the compatibility with the action of

. Using our first theorem, we can deduce an explicit way to make the Bezrukavnikov-Lachowska isomorphism multiplicative. Setting

and

, we will deduce in section

the following theorem, describing geometrically the multiplicative structure of

:

Theorem 1.5
.

The composition

HH^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{u}_{0}(\mathfrak{g}))\xrightarrow{HKR}\bigoplus_{i+j+k=\bullet}H^{i}(\widetilde{\mathcal{N}},\wedge^{j}T\widetilde{\mathcal{N}})^{k}\xrightarrow{\langle-,Todd(\widetilde{\mathcal{N}})^{-1/2}\rangle}\bigoplus_{i+j+k=\bullet}H^{i}(\widetilde{\mathcal{N}},\wedge^{j}T\widetilde{\mathcal{N}})^{k}

is a ring isomorphism.

Taking the zero degree part of the previous isomorphism gives the following corollary:

Corollary 1.6

The natural bigraded vector space isomorphism

HH^{0}(\mathfrak{u}_{0}(\mathfrak{g}))\cong\bigoplus_{i+j+k=0}H^{i}(\widetilde{\mathcal{N}},\wedge^{j}T\widetilde{\mathcal{N}})^{k}

can be upgraded to a multiplicative isomorphism using a twist.

The theorem

and the corollary

also hold for singular blocks, where one needs to twist by the corresponding “parabolic Todd class”.

2.1
Equivariant sheaves

Let
X
be a variety over the complex numbers, with an algebraic action of a complex reductive group
K
(which is not related to the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
mentioned at the beginning of the introduction). We will use
\rm{QCoh}^{K}(X)
for the category of quasi-coherent
K
-equivariant sheaves. First we recall the definitions and standard facts of an equivariant sheaf :

Definition 2.2
.

A sheaf
\mathscr{F}\in\rm{QCoh}(X)
is
K
-equivariant if there is an isomorphism
\theta:m^{*}\mathscr{F}\cong pr_{2}^{*}\mathscr{F}
, where
m,pr_{2}:K\times X\to X
are respectively the action and the projection (moreover,
\theta
should satisfies a cocycle condition).

Taking the stalks of
\theta
at
(g,x)\in K\times X
gives an isomorphism
\theta_{x}:\mathscr{F}_{g\cdot x}\cong\mathscr{F}_{x}
. A
K
-equivariant sheaf
\mathscr{F}
is equivalent to the data of a sheaf
\mathscr{F}
with such isomorphisms. We also have

Lemma 2.3
.

A
K
-equivariant sheaf
\mathscr{F}
is equivalent to a sheaf
\mathscr{F}
with maps
g:\Gamma(U,\mathscr{F})\to\Gamma(g(U),\mathscr{F})
for each
g\in T
and
U
open, commuting with restrictions and such that
g_{1}\cdot(g_{2}\cdot s)=(g_{1}\cdot g_{2})\cdot s
for all
g_{1},g_{2}\in K
and all
s\in\mathscr{F}(U)
.

Corollary 2.4
.

There is a natural structure of
K
-modules on
\Gamma(X,\mathscr{F})
for any equivariant sheaf
\mathscr{F}
. More generally, there is a structure of
K
-modules on
Hom_{\rm{QCoh(X)}}(\mathscr{F}_{1},\mathscr{F}_{2})
if
\mathscr{F}_{i}\in\rm{QCoh}^{K}(X)
.

Proof.

The first point is clear. The action in general is defined as follow : if
\varphi:\mathscr{F}_{1}(U)\to\mathscr{F}_{2}(U)
, we define
g\cdot\varphi=g\circ\varphi\circ g^{-1}
, where
g
denotes the map in lemma
2.3
.
∎

Definition 2.5
.

The category
\rm{QCoh}^{K}(X)
is the category where objects are
K
-equivariant quasi-coherent sheaves of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules, and morphisms are equivariant morphisms of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules, i.e. the maps
f:\mathscr{F}_{1}\to\mathscr{F}_{2}
such that that
f(g\cdot s)=g\cdot f(s)
for all
s\in\mathscr{F}_{1}(U)
and all
g\in T
.

To give more intuition, we recall that there is an equivalence of categories between the category of locally free coherent
K
-equivariant sheaves and the category of finite-dimensional vector bundles with an action of
K
, linear on each fibers, where morphisms are
K
-equivariant morphisms of vector bundles (see
[
5
]
, chapter
5
).

Lemma 2.6
.

The
K
-module
V:=Hom_{\rm{QCoh}(X)}(\mathscr{F}_{1},\mathscr{F}_{2})
is semisimple. Moreover there is an isomorphism
Hom_{\rm{QCoh}^{K}(X)}(\mathscr{F}_{1},\mathscr{F}_{2})=(Hom_{\rm{QCoh}(X)}(\mathscr{F}_{1},\mathscr{F}_{2}))^{K}
.

Proof.

The induced
K
-action is algebraic, i.e given by a ring map
V\to V\otimes\mathcal{O}(K)
. It follows easily that
V
is the union of its finite-dimensional
K
-modules, and hence semi-simple. The second part follows from the definition of the
K
-action.
∎

We now investigate basic properties of
K
-equivariant sheaves in order to be able to describe derived functors, now computed in the equivariant category.

Remark 2.7
.

Let us emphasize that in geometric representation theory, the usual definition of the ”equivariant derived category” (as defined e.g in Bernstein-Lunts :
[

Lemma 2.8
.

For each
\mathscr{F}\in\rm{QCoh}^{K}(X)

I\in\rm{QCoh}^{K}(X)
and a
K
-equivariant monomorphism
\mathscr{F}\to I

\text{Fgt}(I)\in\rm{QCoh(X)}

\text{Fgt}:\rm{QCoh}^{K}(X)\to\rm{QCoh}(X)

Proof.

The first part is proposition 5.1.2 in

This lemma ensure that we can consider right derived functors of
Hom(\mathscr{F}_{1},-)

Corollary 2.9
.

Let
\mathscr{F}_{1}\in\rm{Coh(X)}^{K}(X)
and
\Gamma_{1}=Hom_{\rm{QCoh^{K}(X)}}(\mathscr{F}_{1},-):\rm{Coh^{K}(X)}\to K-Mod

R(\Gamma_{1}\circ\text{Fgt})\cong R\Gamma_{1}\circ\text{Fgt}
.

Corollary 2.10
.

For any equivariant sheaf
\mathscr{F}_{1}
, there is an isomorphism between the functors

R(\rm{Inv}\circ\Gamma_{\mathscr{F}_{1}})\cong\rm{Inv}\circ R\Gamma_{\mathscr{F}_{1}}

where
\Gamma_{\mathscr{F}_{1}}:=Hom_{\rm{QCoh(X)}}(\text{Fgt}(\mathscr{F}_{1}),\text{Fgt}(-))
, and

,

. In particular if
\mathscr{F}_{1},\mathscr{F}_{2}
are

-equivariant sheaves on

, there is an isomorphism

Ext^{q}_{\rm{QCoh}^{K}(X)}(\mathscr{F}_{1},\mathscr{F}_{2})\cong(Ext^{q}_{\rm{QCoh}(X)}(\mathscr{F}_{1},\mathscr{F}_{2}))^{K}

Proof.

Recall the Grothendieck formula to derive compatible left-exact functors :
R(F\circ F^{\prime})\cong RF\circ RF^{\prime}
. Taking invariant is exact on the subcategory of semisimple

and the formula follows.
∎

Corollary 2.11
.

If
\mathscr{F}
is a
K
-equivariant sheaf, then there is an isomorphism

RHom_{\rm{QCoh^{K}(X)}}(\mathcal{O}_{X},\mathscr{F})\cong(RHom_{\rm{QCoh(X)}}(\mathcal{O}_{X},\mathscr{F}))^{K}

Lemma 2.12
.

If
0\to\mathscr{F}_{1}\to\mathscr{F}_{2}\to\mathscr{F}_{3}\to 0
is a
K
-equivariant sequence of
K
-equivariant sheaves, and

By definition the short exact sequence gives a map

in

. This is the same as an element in

-invariant.

Now we specialize to the case where
K=T
is a complex algebraic torus.

Lemma 2.13
.

Assume that the
T
-action on
X
is trivial and that
\mathcal{E}
is a
T
-equivariant vector bundle on
X

\mathcal{E}=\oplus_{\lambda}\mathcal{E}[\lambda]
where
\lambda:T\to\mathbb{C}^{*}
is a character and we have
H^{i}_{T}(X,\mathcal{E})=H^{i}(X,\mathcal{E}[0])

Proof.

If

is affine, then
\mathcal{E}_{\mid U}\cong E\times U
for some vector space

\mathcal{E}_{\mid U}\cong\oplus_{\lambda}\mathcal{E}_{\mid U}[\lambda]

\Gamma_{T}(X,\mathcal{E})\cong\Gamma(X,\mathcal{E}[0])

2.2
Kontsevitch’s theorem

Let
X
be a smooth algebraic variety over the complex numbers. The Hochschild cohomology of
X
is defined as
HH^{\bullet}(X)=\bigoplus_{i}\text{Ext}^{i}_{\mathcal{O}_{X\times X}}(\mathcal{O}_{\Delta},\mathcal{O}_{\Delta})
where
\Delta\subset X\times X
is the diagonal. It contains information about deformations of
X
, for example
H^{1}(X,TX)
is the space parametrizing complex deformations of
X
. Let us recall two important theorems about the Hochschild cohomology of a smooth complex algebraic variety. The first theorem is the
Hochschild-Kostant-Rosenberg isomorphism

Theorem 2.14
.

(
[
7
]
,)
Let
\iota:\Delta\to X\times X
be the diagonal inclusion. There is a quasi-isomorphism

\iota^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\Delta}\cong\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\Omega_{X}^{i}[i]

in the derived category
D^{b}(\text{Coh}(X))
.

Here
\Omega_{X}^{i}
is the sheaf of differential forms on
X
of degree
i
, and
[i]
is the cohomological shift functor.

This is a stronger version of the HKR theorem (that appeared first in
[
9
]
) stating that there is an isomorphism of graded vector spaces

I_{HKR}:HH^{\bullet}(X)\to HT^{\bullet}(X):=\bigoplus_{i+j=\bullet}H^{i}(X,\wedge^{j}TX))

Let us recall the proof of theorem
2.14
: a non-trivial argument using sheaves (see
[
17
]
,
[
7
]
) reduces it to the statement when
X=\text{Spec}(R)
is affine. This is the case treated in
[
9
]
. We use the
bar complex

\mathscr{C}_{\bullet}(R)
, which is a flat resolution of
\mathcal{O}_{\Delta}
by
\mathcal{O}_{X\times X}
-modules. Recall that the bar resolution is given by
\mathscr{B}_{i}(R):=R^{\otimes(i+2)}
. We define
\mathscr{C}_{i}(R)=R\otimes_{R\times R}\mathscr{B}_{i}(R)
. The differential on
\mathscr{B}_{\bullet}(R)
is given by

d(1\otimes a_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes a_{i}\otimes 1)=a_{1}\otimes a_{2}\dots\otimes a_{i}\otimes 1-1\otimes a_{1}a_{2}\otimes a_{3}\otimes\dots\otimes a_{i}\otimes 1+\dots+(-1)^{i+1}1\otimes a_{0}\otimes\dots\otimes a_{i}

A quasi-isomorphism
I:\mathscr{C}_{\bullet}(R)\to(\bigwedge^{\bullet}(\Omega_{R}^{1}[1]),0)
is given by

1\otimes(1\otimes a_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes a_{i}\otimes 1)\mapsto da_{1}\wedge da_{2}\dots\wedge da_{i}

Since the bar complex resolves
R
as
R
-bimodule, this bar complex is isomorphic to
\mathcal{O}_{\Delta}

in
D^{b}(X\times X)
. This finishes the proof.

In general,
I_{HKR}
is not multiplicative. To understand how the multiplications are related, we need to introduce the Todd class. We use the formalism of Atiyah class explained in section
1
of
[
10
]
.

There is an exact sequence

0\to\Omega_{X}\otimes TX\to J^{1}(TX)\to TX\to 0

where
J^{1}(TX)
is the bundle of first-order jet of
TX
. Such sequence gives a class in
Ext^{1}(TX,\Omega_{X}\otimes TX)=H^{1}(X,\Omega_{X}\otimes End(TX))
.

Definition 2.15
.

The
Atiyah class
of
X
is defined as the class of this extension :
At(X)\in H^{1}(X,\Omega_{X}\otimes\text{End}(TX))
.

Definition 2.16
.

The
Todd class
of
X
is defined as

Td(X)=\det\left(\frac{At(X)}{1-e^{-At(X)}}\right)

A more familiar definition of the Todd class involves Chern classes of
TX
. Our definition is equivalent to that, according to the following statement (see
[
10
]
, formula
1.4.1
) :

Proposition 2.17
.

We have
tr(\wedge^{i}At(X))=c_{i}(X)
where
c_{i}(X)
is the
i
-th Chern class of the tangent bundle
TX
.

Now we can state Kontsevitch’s theorem :

Theorem 2.18
.

[

]
[

]

Let
X
be as before and

be the Todd class of

\langle t^{-1/2},-\rangle\circ I_{HKR}:HH^{\bullet}(X)\to HT^{\bullet}(X)

is a ring isomorphism.

2.3
Equivariance of the twisted HKR isomorphism

We now assume that
T
is a complex torus acting on a smooth variety
X
.

Lemma 2.19
.

If
X
is affine, the isomorphism
I_{HKR}
is
T
-equivariant.

Proof.

Say
X=\text{Spec}(R)
. Let us keep notation from the discussion after
2.14
. The differential of the bar-complex is
T
-equivariant, hence the bar-complex gives a flat
T
-equivariant resolution of
\mathcal{O}_{\Delta}
. Moreover the map
I:A\to\Omega_{R},a\mapsto da
is
T
-equivariant, where
\Omega_{R}
is the module of Kähler differential. This is because there is a natural identification
\Omega_{R}\cong J/J^{2},da\mapsto a\otimes 1-1\otimes a
, where
J
is the kernel of the multiplication map
R\otimes R\to R
. Under this identification, the map
a\mapsto da
is
T
-equivariant. It follows that the quasi-isomorphism
I:\mathscr{C}_{\bullet}(R)\to(\bigwedge^{\bullet}(\Omega_{R}^{1}[1]),0)
is also
T
-equivariant.
∎

Lemma 2.20
.

If
X
is a quasi-projective smooth variety acted upon by
T
then the previous lemma holds.

Proof.

By Sumihiro’s theorem, there is an
T
-invariant affine open cover. So we can check the statement on each affine open set
U\subset X
that is
T
-invariant. But recall that in the discussion sketching the proof of theorem
2.14
, we saw that we could glue together the bar complex to construct a global quasi-isomorphism
I_{HKR}
. So it means that the invariance can be only checked for the bar complex
\mathscr{C}_{\bullet}(R)
, that was precisely given by our previous lemma. Hence
I_{HKR}
is
T
-equivariant.
∎

Lemma 2.21
.

If
X
is as before, then the Atiyah class of
X
is
T
-invariant. Therefore the Todd class of
X
is also
T
-invariant.

Proof.

This follows from lemma
2.12
because the exact sequence defining the Atiyah class is
T
-equivariant.
∎

It follows that the twisted HKR morphism
HH^{\bullet}(X)\to HT^{\bullet}(X)
is

. It would be interesting to generalize theorem

3.1
The small quantum group

We summarize results from
[
1
]
. Let
G
be a semisimple Lie group of adjoint type,

a Cartan subgroup contained in a Borel subgroup and

the unipotent radical of

\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{n}
for the corresponding Lie algebras. If

, prime to the index of connection, and prime to

. To this data Lusztig associated a canonical finite-dimensional Hopf algebra
\mathfrak{u}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
, called the
small quantum group
, see e.g
[
14
]
. An important problem in representation theory is to understand its center. Since
\mathfrak{u}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
splits as sum of two-sided ideals,
blocks
. They are parametrized by the orbit of a certain extension of the Weyl group in the weight lattice. So it is enough to understand structure of the center for each block. The regular block that contains the trivial representation is the
principal block
(corresponding to
\lambda=0
), denoted by
\mathfrak{u}_{0}(\mathfrak{g})
or
\mathfrak{u}_{0}
for simplicity.

A major advance was obtained in
[
1
]
where the center was described geometrically via the Springer resolution. Before stating the main result, let us introduce some notations :
z
is the trivial line bundle on
\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}
with the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action given by
(z,v)\mapsto z^{2}v
,
R
is the principal block, viewed as bimodule over
\mathfrak{u}
. By definition
H^{i}(\widetilde{\mathcal{N}},(\wedge^{j}T\widetilde{\mathcal{N}})^{k}):=H^{i}(G/B,(pr_{*}\wedge^{j}T\widetilde{\mathcal{N}})^{k})
. By definition
HH^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{u}_{0})
is the graded ring

\bigoplus_{m}\mathrm{Hom}_{D^{b}(\mathfrak{u}_{0}\otimes\mathfrak{u}_{0}^{op})}(R,R[m])

where the multiplication is given by the composition. Moreover,
HH^{*}_{\mathbb{C}^{*}}(\widetilde{\mathcal{N}})
is by definition the ring

\bigoplus_{j+k=m}\mathrm{Hom}_{D^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}^{\mathbb{C}^{*}}(\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}\times\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}))}(\mathcal{O}_{\Delta},\mathcal{O}_{\Delta}[j]\otimes z^{k})

where the ring structure comes from the Yoneda product. The main theorem in
[

]
can be formulated :

Theorem 3.1

There is a ring isomorphism
HH^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{u}_{0})\cong HH^{*}_{\mathbb{C}^{*}}(\widetilde{\mathcal{N}})

We can now give a proof of the theorem
1.5

Theorem 3.2

The composition

HH^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{u}_{0}(\mathfrak{g}))\overset{BL}{\to}\bigoplus_{i+j+k=\bullet}H^{i}(\widetilde{\mathcal{N}},\wedge^{j}T\widetilde{\mathcal{N}})^{k}\overset{\langle-,Todd(\widetilde{\mathcal{N}})^{-1/2}\rangle}{\to}\bigoplus_{i+j+k=\bullet}H^{i}(\widetilde{\mathcal{N}},\wedge^{j}T\widetilde{\mathcal{N}})^{k}

Proof.

Let us recall how to deduce the isomorphism
1.1
from the theorem
3.1
. There are isomorphisms

HH^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{u}_{0})\overset{\ref{thmBL}}{\cong}\bigoplus_{q+k=\bullet}Ext^{q}_{\mathrm{Coh}^{\mathbb{C}^{*}}(\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}\times\widetilde{\mathcal{N}})}(\mathcal{O}_{\Delta},\mathcal{O}_{\Delta}\otimes z^{k})\cong\bigoplus_{i+j+k=\bullet}H^{i}_{\mathbb{C}^{*}}(\widetilde{\mathcal{N}},\wedge^{j}T\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}\otimes z^{k})

Here
H^{i}_{\mathbb{C}^{*}}(-)=R^{i}(Hom_{\mathbb{C}^{*}}(\mathcal{O}_{X},-))
. The second isomorphism follows by taking the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
invariant on both side of the usual HKR isomorphism (and using corollary
2.10
). Hence we know that

H^{i}_{\mathbb{C}^{*}}(\widetilde{\mathcal{N}},\wedge^{j}T\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}\otimes z^{k})=(H^{i}(\widetilde{\mathcal{N}},\wedge^{j}T\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}\otimes z^{k}))^{\mathbb{C}^{*}}=(H^{i}(G/B,pr_{*}\wedge^{j}T\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}\otimes z^{k}))^{\mathbb{C}^{*}}

since
pr:\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}\to G/B
is an affine
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-morphism. Since the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action is trivial on
G/B
by proposition
2.13
we obtain an isomorphism

(H^{i}(G/B,pr_{*}\wedge^{j}T\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}\otimes z^{k}))^{\mathbb{C}^{*}}\cong H^{i}(G/B,(pr_{*}\wedge^{j}T\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}\otimes z^{k})[0])\cong H^{i}(G/B,(pr_{*}\wedge^{j}T\widetilde{\mathcal{N}})^{-k})

Putting everything together we obtain indeed the isomorphism stated in Theorem
1.1
:

HH^{\bullet}(u_{0})\cong\bigoplus_{q+k=\bullet}Ext^{q}(\mathcal{O}_{\Delta},\mathcal{O}_{\Delta}\otimes z^{k})\cong\bigoplus_{i+j+k=\bullet}H^{i}(\widetilde{\mathcal{N}},(\wedge^{j}T\widetilde{\mathcal{N}})^{k})

Now the isomorphism
HH^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{u}_{0})\cong\bigoplus_{q+k=\bullet}Ext^{q}(\mathcal{O}_{\Delta},\mathcal{O}_{\Delta}\otimes z^{k})
is multiplicative. Since the Todd class is
T
-invariant by lemma
2.21
, and that
I_{HKR}
is
T
-equivariant by lemma
2.20
it follows that the usual multiplicative HKR theorem restricts to a multiplicative isomorphism

\bigoplus_{q+k=\bullet}Ext^{q}(\mathcal{O}_{\Delta},\mathcal{O}_{\Delta}\otimes z^{k})\cong\bigoplus_{i+j+k=\bullet}H^{i}_{\mathbb{C}^{*}}(\widetilde{\mathcal{N}},\wedge^{j}T\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}\otimes z^{k})

Finally the isomorphism

\bigoplus_{i+j+k=\bullet}H^{i}_{\mathbb{C}^{*}}(\widetilde{\mathcal{N}},\wedge^{j}T\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}\otimes z^{k})\cong\bigoplus_{i+j+k=\bullet}H^{i}(G/B,(pr_{*}\wedge^{j}T\widetilde{\mathcal{N}})^{k})

is multiplicative, because for an affine map
q:X\to Y
, the equivalence
\text{Coh}(X)\cong\text{Coh}(Y)-q_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}
is monoidal, hence respect the cup-product. ∎

3.2
Multiplicative structure of
HC[\tau]

We will compute the multiplicative structure of a subalgebra of
z_{0}(\mathfrak{g})
, already noticed in
[
12
]
, where the arguments was given at level of sheaf cohomology.

Lemma 3.3
.

If
Y=T^{*}X
is a cotangent bundle of a smooth algebraic variety
X
, with projection morphism
pr:Y\to X
, then the Todd class of
Y
is given by the pullback to
Y
of

\prod_{\alpha,\beta\in C}\frac{-\alpha\beta}{(1-e^{-\alpha})(1-e^{-\beta})}\in H\Omega^{\bullet}(X)

where
C
is the set of Chern roots of
TY
. In particular, this Todd class can be written
1+pr^{*}a
where
a\in H^{\geq 2}(X)
.

Proof.

The map
pr:Y\to X
induces a short exact sequence

0\to T_{Y/X}\to TY\to pr^{*}TX\to 0

Moreover,
T_{Y/X}\cong pr^{*}(T^{*}X)
. Using the naturality of the Todd class and the additivity on short exact sequences the result easily follows.
∎

For example if
X=G/B
then
C
is the set of positive roots of
\mathfrak{g}
and the Todd class of
G/B
is given by

Todd(G/B)=\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}\frac{\alpha}{1-e^{-\alpha}}

Let
\tau
be the the generator of
H^{0}(\widetilde{\mathcal{N}},End(T\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}))
given by the identity map.

Definition 3.4
.

We define
HC[\tau]
to be the subalgebra of
z_{0}(\mathfrak{g})
generated by the Harish-Chandra center
HC_{\lambda}
and
\tau
.

Of course, for singular blocks
\mathfrak{u}_{\lambda}(\mathfrak{g})
there is a similar subalgebra inside
z_{\lambda}(\mathfrak{g})
, also written
HC[\tau]
if there is no confusion.

Proposition 3.5
.

For any block, the multiplicative structure of
HC[\tau]
is untwisted.

Proof.

Let us take
U\subset G/P
an affine open set isomorphic to an affine space. If
pr:\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}_{P}\to G/P
is the projection, then
pr^{-1}(U)
is an affine space with coordinates
(x,y)
. In these coordinates the Poisson bivector field is
\tau=\sum_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\wedge\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i}}
and the Todd class is
t=1+\sum a_{ij}dx_{i}\wedge dx_{j}+\text{higher order terms}
. It follows that
(1-t,\tau)=0
where
(\cdot,\cdot)
is the pairing
T^{*}\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}_{P}\otimes T\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}_{P}\to\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}}
. In the exact sequence

0\to pr^{*}T^{*}(G/P)\to\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}_{P}\to pr^{*}T(G/P)\to 0

the left hand-side corresponds to the fiber of
pr
since
\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}_{P}=T^{*}(G/P)
. In particular an element
\alpha\in HT^{\bullet}(\widetilde{\mathcal{N}})
coming from
H^{\bullet}(X,\Omega^{\bullet})
can we written in local coordinates as a polynomial in
\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i}}
. For such a class it is again clear that
(1-t,\alpha)=0
. In particular it holds for the Harish-Chandra center
HC\cong H^{\bullet}(G/P)
.
∎

Corollary 3.6
.

If
G/P
is a projective space
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, then
HH^{0}(\mathfrak{u}_{\lambda}(\mathfrak{g}))=z_{\lambda}(\mathfrak{g})
is the polynomial algebra
\mathbb{C}[x,\tau]/(x^{a}\tau^{b}:a+b>n)
.

Proof.

It follows immediately from the previous proposition and the fact (proved in
[
13
]
) that
HC[\tau]=z_{\lambda}(\mathfrak{g})
if
G/P_{\lambda}
is a projective space.
∎

Anomalous Weak Values Without Post-Selection
Alastair A. Abbott, Ralph Silva, Julian Wechs, Nicolas Brunner, Cyril Branciard

Abstract
A weak measurement performed on a pre- and post-selected quantum system can result in an average value that lies outside of the observable’s spectrum. This effect, usually referred to as an “anomalous weak value”, is generally believed to be possible only when a non-trivial post-selection is performed, i.e., when only a particular subset of the data is considered. Here we show, however, that this is not the case in general: in scenarios in which several weak measurements are sequentially performed, an anomalous weak value can be obtained without post-selection, i.e., without discarding any data. We discuss several questions that this raises about the subtle relation between weak values and pointer positions for sequential weak measurements. Finally, we consider some implications of our results for the problem of distinguishing different causal structures.

Introduction

All quantum measurements are subjected to a fundamental trade-off between information gain and disturbance of the measured system.

While astonishing at first sight, anomalous weak values can in fact be intuitively understood in terms of destructive interference of the pointer state, which occurs as a result of post-selection.
With this in mind and given the rudimentary analysis above, it is rather natural to attribute the origin of anomalous weak values to the presence of post-selection; this opinion indeed seems to be widely shared in the community.

Here we show, however, that this is not the case in general, and that anomalous weak values can in fact be observed in the absence of post-selection and without discarding any outcomes.
Specifically, we consider a situation in which two successive weak measurements are performed on a quantum system.
The experiment thus involves two pointers, one associated to each weak measurement, which are measured jointly.
Considering observables that are simply given by projectors, one expects to find the mean position of each pointer between 0 (the system’s state being orthogonal to the projector) and 1 (the system’s state being in the range of the projector).
Yet, we will see that the average of the product of the pointer positions can become negative, something which cannot happen if the measurements are strong or classical.
This may be understood in terms of the second measurement acting as an effective post-selection of the system, thus creating the desired interference.
Importantly however, no data is discarded.

Below, after discussing in detail a simple example of this effect, we turn to the more general scenario of arbitrary sequences of weak measurements without post-selection, deriving bounds on how anomalous a weak value can be obtained in such a scenario and how these relate to the (jointly measured) pointer positions. We finish by discussing the use of anomalous weak values without post-selection to certify particular causal structures between measurements.

2
Illustrative example

To start with, let us consider a qubit system initially prepared in the state
\mathinner{|{0}\rangle}
, undergoing a sequence of two weak von Neumann measurements of the projection observables
\mathinner{|{\psi_{j}}\rangle\!\langle{\psi_{j}}|}

\mathinner{|{\psi_{j}}\rangle}

\displaystyle\mathinner{|{\psi_{j}}\rangle}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\mathinner{|{0}\rangle}-(-1)^{j}\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\mathinner{|{1}\rangle},

\displaystyle\mathinner{|{\psi_{j}^{\perp}}\rangle}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\mathinner{|{0}\rangle}+(-1)^{j}\frac{1}{2}\mathinner{|{1}\rangle}.

(7)

To each measurement is associated a pointer in the state

\displaystyle\mathinner{|{\Psi_{0}}\rangle}

\displaystyle=\mathinner{|{0}\rangle}\mathinner{|{\varphi_{1}(0)}\rangle}\mathinner{|{\varphi_{2}(0)}\rangle}.

\displaystyle\left(\mathinner{|{\psi_{1}}\rangle\!\langle{\psi_{1}}|}e^{-i\hat{p}_{1}}+\mathinner{|{\psi_{1}^{\perp}}\rangle\!\langle{\psi_{1}^{\perp}}|}\mathds{1}_{1}\right)\mathds{1}_{2}\,\mathinner{|{\Psi_{0}}\rangle}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathinner{|{\psi_{1}}\rangle}\mathinner{|{\varphi_{1}(1)}\rangle}\mathinner{|{\varphi_{2}(0)}\rangle}

\displaystyle+\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\mathinner{|{\psi_{1}^{\perp}}\rangle}\mathinner{|{\varphi_{1}(0)}\rangle}\mathinner{|{\varphi_{2}(0)}\rangle}.

(9)

After interacting with the second pointer, it evolves to

\displaystyle\mathinner{|{\Psi_{2}}\rangle}=

\displaystyle\left(\mathinner{|{\psi_{2}}\rangle\!\langle{\psi_{2}}|}e^{-i\hat{p}_{2}}+\mathinner{|{\psi_{2}^{\perp}}\rangle\!\langle{\psi_{2}^{\perp}}|}\mathds{1}_{2}\right)\mathds{1}_{1}\,\mathinner{|{\Psi_{1}}\rangle}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}\mathinner{|{\psi_{2}}\rangle}\mathinner{|{\varphi_{1}(1)}\rangle}\mathinner{|{\varphi_{2}(1)}\rangle}

\displaystyle+\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}\!\mathinner{|{\psi_{2}^{\perp}}\rangle}\!\mathinner{|{\varphi_{1}(0)}\rangle}\!\mathinner{|{\varphi_{2}(0)}\rangle}.

(10)

Tracing out the system, one finds that the joint pointer state is

\displaystyle\eta_{12}=

\displaystyle\mathinner{|{\Phi_{1}^{(1)}}\rangle\!\langle{\Phi_{1}^{(1)}}|}\otimes\mathinner{|{\phi_{2}(1)}\rangle\!\langle{\phi_{2}(1)}|}

\displaystyle+\mathinner{|{\Phi_{1}^{(0)}}\rangle\!\langle{\Phi_{1}^{(0)}}|}\otimes\mathinner{|{\phi_{2}(0)}\rangle\!\langle{\phi_{2}(0)}|},

(11)

where

\displaystyle\mathinner{|{\Phi_{1}^{(1)}}\rangle}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\big(\mathinner{|{\varphi_{1}(1)}\rangle}-3\mathinner{|{\varphi_{1}(0)}\rangle}\big),

\displaystyle\mathinner{|{\Phi_{1}^{(0)}}\rangle}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}\big(\mathinner{|{\varphi_{1}(1)}\rangle}+\mathinner{|{\varphi_{1}(0)}\rangle}\big)

(12)

are (generally unnormalised) states of the first pointer.

Finally the positions of the pointers are measured.
The quantity of interest is the average of the product of the pointer positions, i.e., the expectation value

\mathinner{|{0}\rangle}
of the Pauli matrix
\hat{\sigma}_{\textsc{z}}
, on which a sequential weak measurement of the Pauli observables
\hat{\sigma}_{\textsc{y}}
and
\hat{\sigma}_{\textsc{x}}
is performed.
One thus obtains
(\sigma_{\textsc{x}}\sigma_{\textsc{y}})_{0}^{\scalebox{.7}{$\mathbbm{1}$}}=i
.
The imaginary part of the weak value here can be detected by measuring the pointer momenta
[
27
,
16
]
(see Appendix).
Such complex anomalous weak values cannot be obtained without post-selection with only a single weak measurement or a sequence of weak measurements of commuting observables, and can thus themselves be considered anomalous in this sense.

4
More measurements

Eq. (
3
) might bound how anomalous a weak value can be without post-selection, but it is not generally tight.
For two projection observables
\hat{A}
and
\hat{B}
(with eigenvalues
\pm 1
), for example, it only implies a bound
\re[(BA)_{\psi}^{\scalebox{.7}{$\mathbbm{1}$}}]\geq-1
; nevertheless, as we prove in the Appendix, the value of
-1/8
obtained earlier for the real part of the weak value is the most negative value that one can obtain.
Can one do better by considering longer sequences of successive weak measurements?
Here we will see that this question has a subtle answer: the weak value itself can approach
-1
, but this will not mean the average product of the pointer positions does so as well.

For a sequence of
n
observables
\hat{A}_{1},\dots,\hat{A}_{n}
to be measured weakly on the state
\mathinner{|{\psi}\rangle}
before a post-selection on
\mathinner{|{\phi}\rangle}
, the sequential weak value is defined (following, e.g., Ref.
[
21
]
) as

(A_{n}\cdots A_{1})_{\psi}^{\phi}\coloneqq\frac{\mathinner{\langle{\phi|\hat{A}_{n}\cdots\hat{A}_{1}|\psi}\rangle}}{\mathinner{\langle{\phi|\psi}\rangle}}.

(22)

When no post-selection is performed, this can be generalised to

(A_{n}\cdots A_{1})_{\psi}^{\scalebox{.7}{$\mathbbm{1}$}}\coloneqq\mathinner{\langle{\psi|\hat{A}_{n}\cdots\hat{A}_{1}|\psi}\rangle},

(23)

in analogy to the cases discussed earlier.
As we show in the Appendix, a similar bound to Eq. (
3
) can be derived, namely

\displaystyle|(A_{n}\cdots A_{1})_{\psi}^{\scalebox{.7}{$\mathbbm{1}$}}|\ \leq\ \prod_{j=1}^{n}\lVert\hat{A}_{j}\rVert\,.

(24)

For
n
projection observables, this implies the bound
\re[(A_{n}\cdots A_{1})_{\psi}^{\scalebox{.7}{$\mathbbm{1}$}}]\geq-1
.
As it turns out, it is possible to obtain an anomalous sequential weak value without post-selection approaching
-1
and thus saturating this bound in the limit
n\to\infty
.
To see this, take the initial state of the system to be
\mathinner{|{\psi}\rangle}=\mathinner{|{0}\rangle}
and consider the sequence of
n
qubit projectors
\hat{A}_{j}=\mathinner{|{a_{j}}\rangle\!\langle{a_{j}}|}
with
\mathinner{|{a_{j}}\rangle}=\cos(\frac{j\pi}{n+1})\mathinner{|{0}\rangle}+\sin(\frac{j\pi}{n+1})\mathinner{|{1}\rangle}
for
j=1,\dots,n
.
This sequence of weak measurements gives

(A_{n}\cdots A_{1})_{\psi}^{\scalebox{.7}{$\mathbbm{1}$}}=-\left(\cos\tfrac{\pi}{n+1}\right)^{n+1}\xrightarrow{\hskip 8.19447ptn\to\infty\hskip 8.19447pt}-1.

(25)

Note that for
n=2
this coincides precisely with the explicit two-measurement example we began with.

As discussed above, for two sequential weak measurements in the absence of post-selection, the mean product of the pointer positions gives precisely the real part of the sequential weak value; see Eq. (
20
).
However, for
n>2
measurements this direct relationship is broken and the mean product of the pointer positions corresponds instead to a mixture of sequential weak values for
2^{n-2}
different permutations of the observables (see the Appendix for an explicit expression).
For example, for
n=3
, in the weak regime, we have
[
4
,
25
]

\displaystyle\mathinner{\langle{\hat{x}_{1}\otimes\hat{x}_{2}\otimes\hat{x}_{3}}\rangle}\approx\frac{1}{2}\big(

\displaystyle\re[(A_{3}A_{2}A_{1})_{\psi}^{\scalebox{.7}{$\mathbbm{1}$}}]

\displaystyle+\re[(A_{2}A_{3}A_{1})_{\psi}^{\scalebox{.7}{$\mathbbm{1}$}}]\big).

(26)

The real part of
(A_{3}A_{2}A_{1})_{\psi}^{\scalebox{.7}{$\mathbbm{1}$}}
is thus not directly observed.
However, as we show in the Appendix, its value (as well as the imaginary part) can nonetheless be deduced experimentally by measuring several different expectation values of the products of pointer positions and momenta
[
21
]
.

Interestingly, by numerically minimising the mean product of the pointer positions for sequences of up to 5 projection observables, we were unable to obtain a value smaller than
-1/8
, and we conjecture that this is in fact the case for all
n
.
Thus, although the weak value itself can be brought arbitrarily close to
-1
, it seems that additional sequential weak measurements may not lead to “more anomalous” pointer positions.
This behaviour highlights oft-overlooked subtleties in the connection between anomalous weak values and pointer positions: for individual weak measurements, there is a direct correspondence between the pointer position and (the real part of) the weak value, and an anomalous weak value has an immediate physical relevance.
For sequential weak measurements, a distinction must be made between anomalous weak values and anomalous pointer positions (with post-selection, this is already the case for two measurements; see Eq. (
18
) or Ref.
[
21
]
).
It is the latter of these phenomena that arguably provides the more important anomaly, leading to average measurement results that lie outside the range of values one would expect to be attainable.

In contrast, this divergence between weak values and pointer positions for sequential weak measurements means that, in general, it is more difficult to give a clear physical interpretation to sequential weak values, anomalous or not.
Indeed, while some authors have argued that weak values for single weak measurements should be considered real properties of quantum states with direct physical meaning
[
32
,
33
]
, it is unclear whether such arguments are justified for sequential weak values
[
21
]
given the lack of examples of physical scenarios where they play a direct, crucial role.

5
Further Discussion

Compare the situation of a sequential weak measurement of two observables
\hat{A}
and
\hat{B}
with the alternative in which a bipartite system
\mathinner{|{\psi_{ab}}\rangle}\in\mathcal{H}_{a}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{b}
is prepared and
\hat{A}
and
\hat{B}
are weakly measured on the two different substituent systems.
One can view this either as a measurement of the joint observable
\hat{A}\otimes\hat{B}
(with two different pointers, one coupled to each observable) or a sequential measurement of the commuting observables
\hat{A}\otimes\mathbbm{1}
and
\mathbbm{1}\otimes\hat{B}
.
In the absence of any post-selection, one has
(A\otimes B)_{\psi_{ab}}^{\scalebox{.7}{$\mathbbm{1}$}}=\mathinner{\langle{\psi_{ab}|\hat{A}\otimes\hat{B}|\psi_{ab}}\rangle}
which, being just an expectation value, cannot lie outside the spectrum of the product observable
\hat{A}\otimes\hat{B}
.
For tensor product measurements, an anomalous weak value is thus unobtainable without post-selection.

This observation raises some interesting implications.
Consider for example a scenario in which two parties, Alice and Bob, each operate in a closed laboratory.
Each receives a system, performs a weak measurement, and sends the resulting system out;
they then come together to jointly measure their pointers.
By repeating this many times (or on a large number of systems), they thus determine
\mathinner{\langle{\hat{x}_{1}\otimes\hat{x}_{2}}\rangle}
.
If Alice and Bob have no knowledge of their causal relationship, they could unknowingly be weakly measuring the same system at different times (either Alice then Bob, or vice versa), or measuring different parts of a (potentially entangled) bipartite system.
By observing an anomalous weak value they can differentiate between these two scenarios.

The problem of distinguishing these two causal structures for quantum systems – the former is known as a
direct cause
relationship, while the later a
common cause
relationship, since any correlations must be due to a (possibly quantum) common cause – has been the subject of recent interest; see, e.g., Refs.
[
34
,
35
,
36
,
37
,
38
]
.
An anomalous weak value thus provides a novel way to witness a direct causal relationship and distinguish between these cases.
We leave it as an open question whether such a witness can be found whenever Alice and Bob are capable of signalling to each other; i.e., if whenever they are connected by a quantum channel of nonzero capacity they can always find a pair of observables to measure that would generate an anomalous weak value without post-selection.

Recently, there has also been substantial interest in quantum processes that are not consistent with any definite (possibly stochastic) causal ordering
[
39
]
, and practical approaches to witness such “indefinite” causal orders have been developed
[
40
,
41
]
and experimentally tested
[
42
,
43
]
.
It would be interesting to see whether indefinite causal orders may also be witnessed by, for example, producing anomalous weak values or pointer positions lying further outside the expected range than possible in a well-defined causal structure.

The ability for sequential weak measurements to lead to anomalous measurement results even in the absence of post-selection is thus interesting not only in clarifying the relation between post-selection and anomalous weak values, but also in that it raises interesting foundational questions. This sequential weak measurement scheme has, for example, already been used to study time asymmetry in quantum measurements
[
28
,
25
,
44
]
and the role of anomalous weak values in this context would be interesting to study further. One may expect anomalous weak values without post-selection – just as for those with post-selection – to shed light on other foundational results; for example, it would be interesting to see whether they can lead to proofs of contextuality as they do in the standard single weak measurement scenario
[
17
]
.

Appendix A
Weak measurements with Gaussian pointers

The weak measurement regime can in general be defined for any type of von Neumann measurement interaction scheme, as introduced in the main text, by comparing the various parameters that describe it: the strength of the measurement interaction (
\gamma
), the time of the interaction (
\Delta t
), the width of the measurement pointer (
\sigma
), the eigenspectrum of the observable
\hat{A}
being measured as well as the weak values to be considered. The relation between weak values and pointer positions can then be obtained by taking the appropriate limits.

As the point of our paper is to analyse specific cases of anomalous weak values and anomalous pointer positions, for simplicity we choose a specific form for the pointer states, namely, Gaussian states.
By a Gaussian pointer, we mean a pointer whose state
\mathinner{|{\varphi(a)}\rangle}
is described by a Gaussian wavefunction as follows:

\displaystyle\mathinner{|{\varphi(a)}\rangle}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}x\ \left(\frac{1}{2\pi\sigma^{2}}\right)^{1/4}\exp\left[-\frac{(x-a)^{2}}{4\sigma^{2}}\right]\mathinner{|{x}\rangle},

(A1)

where
\{\mathinner{|{x}\rangle}\}_{x}
is a continuous eigenbasis of the pointer position
\hat{x}
.
For
a\in\mathbb{R}
,
\mathinner{|{\varphi(a)}\rangle}
is properly normalised; for a complex value of
a
, its norm is
e^{\frac{\im(a)^{2}}{4\sigma^{2}}}
.
The mean position of the pointer in the state
\mathinner{|{\varphi(a)}\rangle}
(possibly after renormalisation) is
\mathinner{\langle{\hat{x}}\rangle}=\re(a)
and its variance is
\mathinner{\langle{\hat{x}^{2}}\rangle}-\mathinner{\langle{\hat{x}}\rangle}^{2}=\sigma^{2}
, while the mean value of the momentum operator
\hat{p}=-i\frac{\partial}{\partial x}
(taking
\hbar=1
) is
\mathinner{\langle{\hat{p}}\rangle}=\frac{\im(a)}{2\sigma^{2}}
(
=0
for
a\in\mathbb{R}
).
Prior to the measurement, we always start with
a=0
. Note that an operator of the form
e^{-i\alpha\hat{p}}
(for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
) acts as a displacement operator, such that
e^{-i\alpha\hat{p}}\mathinner{|{\varphi(a)}\rangle}=\mathinner{|{\varphi(a+\alpha)}\rangle}
.

Let us clarify here the conditions that define the standard weak measurement regime, under which the approximations of Eqs. (
1
)–(
2
) of the main text are valid.
As everywhere in the paper, we choose units such that
g=\gamma\Delta t=1
; as we noted in Footnote
1
, this effectively fixes the scale for the pointer movement, while the weak regime in general depends on the spread of the pointer with respect to this scale, i.e., on
\sigma/g
.
By considering the spectral decomposition
\hat{A}=\sum_{k}a_{k}\mathinner{|{a_{k}}\rangle\!\langle{a_{k}}|}
of the observable under consideration and the completeness relation
\mathbbm{1}=\sum_{k}\mathinner{|{a_{k}}\rangle\!\langle{a_{k}}|}
(with
\{\mathinner{|{a_{k}}\rangle}\}_{k}
an orthonormal basis of the system Hilbert space), one can write
e^{-i\hat{A}\otimes\hat{p}}=\sum_{k}\mathinner{|{a_{k}}\rangle\!\langle{a_{k}}|}\otimes e^{-ia_{k}\hat{p}}
and
\mathbbm{1}-i\hat{A}\otimes\hat{p}=\sum_{k}\mathinner{|{a_{k}}\rangle\!\langle{a_{k}}|}\otimes(\mathbbm{1}-ia_{k}\hat{p})
, so that the difference between the left and right hand sides of Eq. (
1
) is

\displaystyle\sum_{k}\mathinner{\langle{a_{k}|\psi}\rangle}\mathinner{|{a_{k}}\rangle}\otimes\mathinner{|{\delta_{k}}\rangle}\quad\text{with}\quad\mathinner{|{\delta_{k}}\rangle}\coloneqq e^{-ia_{k}\hat{p}}\mathinner{|{\varphi(0)}\rangle}

\displaystyle-(\mathbbm{1}-ia_{k}\hat{p})\mathinner{|{\varphi(0)}\rangle}=\mathinner{|{\varphi(a_{k})}\rangle}-(\mathbbm{1}-ia_{k}\hat{p})\mathinner{|{\varphi(0)}\rangle}.

(A2)

The approximation of Eq. (
1
) is valid if for each
k
(for which
|\!\mathinner{\langle{a_{k}|\psi}\rangle}\!|
is non-negligible), the norm of
\mathinner{|{\delta_{k}}\rangle}
is small enough (compared e.g. to that of the lhs of Eq. (
1
), which is 1).
Using Eq. (
A1
), one finds

\displaystyle\mathinner{\langle{\delta_{k}|\delta_{k}}\rangle}=2\Big(1-e^{-\frac{a_{k}^{2}}{8\sigma^{2}}}\Big)+\frac{1}{4}\frac{a_{k}^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}\Big(1-2e^{-\frac{a_{k}^{2}}{8\sigma^{2}}}\Big)=\frac{3}{64}\Big(\frac{a_{k}}{\sigma}\Big)^{4}+O\Big[\Big(\frac{a_{k}}{\sigma}\Big)^{6}\Big],

(A3)

which is indeed small if

\displaystyle\sigma\gg|a_{k}|\quad\forall\,k.

(A4)

Similarly, the difference between the two lines of Eq. (
2
) is (ignoring the common prefactor
\mathinner{\langle{\phi|\psi}\rangle}
)

\displaystyle e^{-i\,A_{\psi}^{\phi}\,\hat{p}}\mathinner{|{\varphi(0)}\rangle}-(\mathbbm{1}-i\,A_{\psi}^{\phi}\,\hat{p})\mathinner{|{\varphi(0)}\rangle}=\mathinner{|{\varphi(A_{\psi}^{\phi})}\rangle}-(\mathbbm{1}-i\,A_{\psi}^{\phi}\,\hat{p})\mathinner{|{\varphi(0)}\rangle},

(A5)

which is also small if

\displaystyle\sigma\gg|A_{\psi}^{\phi}|.

(A6)

Thus the weak regime is valid whenever the two conditions (
A4
) and (
A6
) are fulfilled.

Appendix B
Relating weak values to pointer positions and momenta

B.1
The case of a single weak measurement

It will be useful to relate here the formula for a weak value to the pointer position and momentum in a more general setting than that considered in the main text, where the initial state is not considered
a priori
to be pure, and where post-selection is conditioned on a given result of an arbitrary Positive-Operator Valued Measure (POVM) measurement on the system, rather than a projective measurement.
In such a setting, Eq. (
3
) can be generalised to

A_{\rho}^{E}\coloneqq\frac{\Tr(E\hat{A}\rho)}{\Tr(E\rho)},

(A7)

which now defines the weak value of the observable
\hat{A}
, given the pre-selection in the state
\rho
and post-selection by the POVM element
E
.
This definition was first proposed explicitly in Ref.
[
2
]
(although earlier alluded to in Ref.
[
45
]
), and shown to be indeed the natural generalisation of the standard definition (
3
) of a weak value.
Note already that this definition indeed reduces to Eq. (
3
) if the preparation is a pure state
\mathinner{|{\psi}\rangle}
(i.e. for
\rho=\mathinner{|{\psi}\rangle\!\langle{\psi}|}
) and the post-selection is a projection onto another pure state
\mathinner{|{\phi}\rangle}
(for
E=\mathinner{|{\phi}\rangle\!\langle{\phi}|}
). It also allows one to define a weak value with no post-selection by taking a trivial POVM element
E=\mathbbm{1}
, which indeed reduces to the definition of Eq. (
6
) in the case of a pure state
\rho=\mathinner{|{\psi}\rangle\!\langle{\psi}|}
– and which simply coincides here, in the case of a single observable, with the expectation value of
\hat{A}
; note in particular that, contrary to a general weak value, the weak value with no post-selection is linear in the pre-selected state.

In the von Neumann measurement scenario that we consider here, one thus prepares the density matrix
\rho
, weakly measures
\hat{A}\,(=\sum_{k}a_{k}\mathinner{|{a_{k}}\rangle\!\langle{a_{k}}|})
(with a pointer in a Gaussian state as described above), and finally post-selects an outcome corresponding to the POVM element
E
. The initial density matrix of the system and pointer is given by

\displaystyle\varrho

\displaystyle=\rho\otimes\mathinner{|{\varphi(0)}\rangle\!\langle{\varphi(0)}|}=\sum_{k\ell}\mathinner{|{a_{k}}\rangle\!\langle{a_{k}}|}\rho\mathinner{|{a_{\ell}}\rangle\!\langle{a_{\ell}}|}\otimes\mathinner{|{\varphi(0)}\rangle\!\langle{\varphi(0)}|}.

(A8)

Under the interaction between the system and the pointer, the joint state evolves to

\displaystyle e^{-i\hat{A}\hat{p}}\varrho\,e^{+i\hat{A}\hat{p}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\ell}\mathinner{|{a_{k}}\rangle\!\langle{a_{k}}|}\rho\mathinner{|{a_{\ell}}\rangle\!\langle{a_{\ell}}|}\otimes e^{-ia_{k}\hat{p}}\mathinner{|{\varphi(0)}\rangle\!\langle{\varphi(0)}|}e^{+ia_{\ell}\hat{p}}

(A9)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\ell}\mathinner{|{a_{k}}\rangle\!\langle{a_{k}}|}\rho\mathinner{|{a_{\ell}}\rangle\!\langle{a_{\ell}}|}\otimes\mathinner{|{\varphi(a_{k})}\rangle\!\langle{\varphi(a_{\ell})}|}.

(A10)

Due to the post-selection upon
E
, the state of the pointer is then projected onto the (unnormalised) state

\displaystyle\eta=\Tr_{S}\left((E\otimes\mathbbm{1})\,\big(e^{-i\hat{A}\hat{p}}\varrho\,e^{+i\hat{A}\hat{p}}\big)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\ell}\Tr\Big(E\mathinner{|{a_{k}}\rangle\!\langle{a_{k}}|}\rho\mathinner{|{a_{\ell}}\rangle\!\langle{a_{\ell}}|}\Big)\mathinner{|{\varphi(a_{k})}\rangle\!\langle{\varphi(a_{\ell})}|}

(A11)

(where
\Tr_{S}
is the partial trace over the state of the system).

The expectation value of the position of the pointer, given that the post-selection was successful, is

\displaystyle\mathinner{\langle{\hat{x}}\rangle}=\frac{\Tr\left(\hat{x}\,\eta\right)}{\Tr\left(\eta\right)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{k\ell}\Tr\Big(E\mathinner{|{a_{k}}\rangle\!\langle{a_{k}}|}\rho\mathinner{|{a_{\ell}}\rangle\!\langle{a_{\ell}}|}\Big)\mathinner{\langle{\varphi(a_{\ell})|\hat{x}|\varphi(a_{k})}\rangle}}{\sum_{mn}\Tr\Big(E\mathinner{|{a_{m}}\rangle\!\langle{a_{m}}|}\rho\mathinner{|{a_{n}}\rangle\!\langle{a_{n}}|}\Big)\mathinner{\langle{\varphi(a_{n})|\varphi(a_{m})}\rangle}}.

(A12)

Evaluating the expressions in the fraction above for the Gaussian pointer of Eq. (
A1
) (with
a_{k},a_{\ell},a_{m},a_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
), and taking the weak regime approximation in which
\sigma\gg|a_{k}-a_{\ell}|
,
\sigma\gg|a_{m}-a_{n}|
, one finds, to the lowest order,

\displaystyle\mathinner{\langle{\varphi(a_{\ell})|\hat{x}|\varphi(a_{k})}\rangle}

\displaystyle=\frac{a_{k}+a_{\ell}}{2}e^{-\frac{(a_{k}-a_{\ell})^{2}}{8\sigma^{2}}}\approx\frac{a_{k}+a_{\ell}}{2},\qquad\mathinner{\langle{\varphi(a_{n})|\varphi(a_{m})}\rangle}=e^{-\frac{(a_{m}-a_{n})^{2}}{8\sigma^{2}}}\approx 1,

(A13)

so that we are left with

\displaystyle\mathinner{\langle{\hat{x}}\rangle}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{\sum_{k\ell}\Tr\Big(E\mathinner{|{a_{k}}\rangle\!\langle{a_{k}}|}\rho\mathinner{|{a_{\ell}}\rangle\!\langle{a_{\ell}}|}\Big)(a_{k}+a_{\ell})/2}{\sum_{mn}\Tr\Big(E\mathinner{|{a_{m}}\rangle\!\langle{a_{m}}|}\rho\mathinner{|{a_{n}}\rangle\!\langle{a_{n}}|}\Big)}=\frac{\big(\Tr(E\hat{A}\rho)+\Tr(E\rho\hat{A})\big)/2}{\Tr(E\rho)}=\re\left(\frac{\Tr(E\hat{A}\rho)}{\Tr(E\rho)}\right),

(A14)

where we used the spectral decomposition of
\hat{A}
and the cyclic property of the trace, together with the fact that
E
,
\hat{A}
and
\rho
are Hermitian and thus
\Tr(EP\hat{A})=\Tr((EP\hat{A})^{\dagger})^{*}
.
Recalling the generalised definition (
A7
) of a weak value, we thus find that the mean position of the pointer (when the post-selection is successful) is, as in Eq. (
4
),

\mathinner{\langle{\hat{x}}\rangle}\approx\re(A_{\rho}^{E}).

(A15)

Similarly, by using the fact that

\mathinner{\langle{\varphi(a_{\ell})|\hat{x}^{2}|\varphi(a_{k})}\rangle}=\left(\sigma^{2}+\Big(\frac{a_{k}+a_{\ell}}{2}\Big)^{2}\right)e^{-\frac{(a_{k}-a_{\ell})^{2}}{8\sigma^{2}}}\approx\sigma^{2}+\Big(\frac{a_{k}+a_{\ell}}{2}\Big)^{2}

(A16)

one may verify that the variance of the pointer position is

(\Delta\hat{x})^{2}=\mathinner{\langle{\hat{x}^{2}}\rangle}-\mathinner{\langle{\hat{x}}\rangle}^{2}\approx\sigma^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left[\re\!\big((A^{2})_{\rho}^{E}\big)+\frac{\Tr(E\hat{A}\rho\hat{A})}{\Tr(E\rho)}\right]-\big[\re(A^{E}_{\rho})\big]^{2},

(A17)

which, for a weak enough measurement is dominated by
\sigma^{2}
.
Note that for pure pre- and post-selected states (i.e. for
\rho=\mathinner{|{\psi}\rangle\!\langle{\psi}|}
and
E=\mathinner{|{\phi}\rangle\!\langle{\phi}|}
) one has
\frac{\Tr(E\hat{A}\rho\hat{A})}{\Tr(E\rho)}=\frac{\mathinner{\langle{\phi|\hat{A}|\psi}\rangle}\!\mathinner{\langle{\psi|\hat{A}|\phi}\rangle}}{\mathinner{\langle{\phi|\psi}\rangle}\!\mathinner{\langle{\psi|\phi}\rangle}}=|A_{\psi}^{\phi}|^{2}
.
In the absence of post-selection (i.e.,
E=\mathbbm{1}
) on the other hand, when the weak value is equal to the expectation value, one has simply
(\Delta\hat{x})^{2}=\sigma^{2}+(\Delta\hat{A})^{2}
(where
(\Delta\hat{A})^{2}
is the variance of the observable
\hat{A}
for the state
\rho
).

One may also consider measuring the expectation value of the
momentum
of the pointer instead, conditioned again on a successful post-selection:

\displaystyle\mathinner{\langle{\hat{p}}\rangle}=\frac{\Tr\left(\hat{p}\,\eta\right)}{\Tr\left(\eta\right)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{k\ell}\Tr\Big(E\mathinner{|{a_{k}}\rangle\!\langle{a_{k}}|}\rho\mathinner{|{a_{\ell}}\rangle\!\langle{a_{\ell}}|}\Big)\mathinner{\langle{\varphi(a_{\ell})|\hat{p}|\varphi(a_{k})}\rangle}}{\sum_{mn}\Tr\Big(E\mathinner{|{a_{m}}\rangle\!\langle{a_{m}}|}\rho\mathinner{|{a_{n}}\rangle\!\langle{a_{n}}|}\Big)\mathinner{\langle{\varphi(a_{n})|\varphi(a_{m})}\rangle}}.

(A18)

The relevant quantity for the pointer states is, in the weak regime approximation,

\displaystyle\mathinner{\langle{\varphi(a_{\ell})|\hat{p}|\varphi(a_{k})}\rangle}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}}\,\frac{a_{k}-a_{\ell}}{2i}e^{-\frac{(a_{k}-a_{\ell})^{2}}{8\sigma^{2}}}\approx\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}}\,\frac{a_{k}-a_{\ell}}{2i},

(A19)

from which (together with Eq. (
A13
)) we find that the expectation of the pointer’s momentum is

\displaystyle\mathinner{\langle{\hat{p}}\rangle}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}}\frac{\sum_{k\ell}\Tr\Big(E\mathinner{|{a_{k}}\rangle\!\langle{a_{k}}|}\rho\mathinner{|{a_{\ell}}\rangle\!\langle{a_{\ell}}|}\Big)(a_{k}-a_{\ell})/2i}{\sum_{mn}\Tr\Big(E\mathinner{|{a_{m}}\rangle\!\langle{a_{m}}|}\rho\mathinner{|{a_{n}}\rangle\!\langle{a_{n}}|}\Big)}=\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}}\frac{\big(\Tr(E\hat{A}\rho)-\Tr(E\rho\hat{A})\big)/2i}{\Tr(E\rho)}=\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}}\im\left(\frac{\Tr(E\hat{A}\rho)}{\Tr(E\rho)}\right),

(A20)

that is,

\mathinner{\langle{\hat{p}}\rangle}\approx\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}}\im(A_{\rho}^{E}).

(A21)

The expectation value of the momentum is thus directly linked here to the imaginary part of the weak value. Using Eqs. (
A15
) and (
A21
), one may therefore recover both the real and imaginary parts of
A_{\rho}^{E}
from the expectation values of the pointer’s position and momentum (in a regime where
\sigma
is large enough to ignore higher order terms, but not so small as to render the term that remains above, with the pre-factor
\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}}
, unmeasurable).

Note that, unlike the expression for
\mathinner{\langle{\hat{x}}\rangle}
, the above expression for
\mathinner{\langle{\hat{p}}\rangle}
depends explicitly on the width of the pointer.
However, by using the fact that

\mathinner{\langle{\varphi(a_{\ell})|\hat{p}^{2}|\varphi(a_{k})}\rangle}=\frac{1}{4\sigma^{4}}\left(\sigma^{2}-\Big(\frac{a_{k}-a_{\ell}}{2}\Big)^{2}\right)e^{-\frac{(a_{k}-a_{\ell})^{2}}{8\sigma^{2}}}\approx\frac{1}{4\sigma^{4}}\left(\sigma^{2}-\Big(\frac{a_{k}-a_{\ell}}{2}\Big)^{2}\right),

(A22)

the error in the pointer momentum is seen to scale similarly, and so the relative error scales in the same way as that of the pointer position.
Indeed, one finds

(\Delta\hat{p})^{2}=\mathinner{\langle{\hat{p}^{2}}\rangle}-\mathinner{\langle{\hat{p}}\rangle}^{2}\approx\frac{1}{4\sigma^{4}}\left(\sigma^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\left[\re\!\big((A^{2})_{\rho}^{E}\big)-\frac{\Tr(E\hat{A}\rho\hat{A})}{\Tr(E\rho)}\right]-\big[\im(A^{E}_{\rho})\big]^{2}\right).

(A23)

B.2
Two sequential weak measurements

Let us now turn to the sequential measurement of two observables
\hat{A}
and
\hat{B}
. The sequential weak value
(BA)_{\psi}^{\phi}
for a system prepared in the pure state
\mathinner{|{\psi}\rangle}
and post-selected in
\mathinner{|{\phi}\rangle}
, defined in Eq. (
17
), was introduced in Ref.
[
21
]
.
As in the previous section, one may consider a more general setting where the initial state
\rho
is not necessarily assumed to be pure, and the post-selection is conditioned on a general POVM element
E
. In such a case, the definition of Eq. (
17
) can, similarly to Eq. (
A7
), naturally be generalised to

(BA)_{\rho}^{E}\coloneqq\frac{\Tr(E\hat{B}\hat{A}\rho)}{\Tr(E\rho)}.

(A24)

One can indeed verify that one recovers Eq. (
17
) for
\rho=\mathinner{|{\psi}\rangle\!\langle{\psi}|}
and
E=\mathinner{|{\phi}\rangle\!\langle{\phi}|}
. As before, this definition also allows one to define a sequential weak value with no post-selection by taking the trivial POVM element
E=\mathbbm{1}
, as in Eq. (
19
) for the case of a pure state
\rho=\mathinner{|{\psi}\rangle\!\langle{\psi}|}
.
Again, and contrary to a general sequential weak value, the sequential weak value with no post-selection is linear in the pre-selected state; note, however, that it no longer coincides with an expectation value, as in general the product
\hat{B}\hat{A}
is not Hermitian, and thus does not define a valid observable.

We consider here a sequential von Neumann measurement scenario where two separate Gaussian pointers (labelled by the subscripts
j=1,2
) are used to measure
\hat{A}\,(=\sum_{k}a_{k}\mathinner{|{a_{k}}\rangle\!\langle{a_{k}}|})
and
\hat{B}\,(=\sum_{m}b_{m}\mathinner{|{b_{m}}\rangle\!\langle{b_{m}}|})
on a system prepared in the state
\rho
and post-selected on a POVM element
E
.
Similarly to the analysis in the previous section, the final (unnormalised) state of the two pointers after the post-selection is given by (with implicit identity operators)

\displaystyle\eta

\displaystyle=\Tr_{S}\left(E\,e^{-i\hat{B}\hat{p}_{2}}e^{-i\hat{A}\hat{p}_{1}}\big(\rho\otimes\mathinner{|{\varphi_{1}(0)}\rangle\!\langle{\varphi_{1}(0)}|}\otimes\mathinner{|{\varphi_{2}(0)}\rangle\!\langle{\varphi_{2}(0)}|}\big)e^{+i\hat{A}\hat{p}_{1}}e^{+i\hat{A}\hat{p}_{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\ell mn}\Tr\big(E\,\mathinner{|{b_{m}}\rangle}\!\!\mathinner{\langle{b_{m}|a_{k}}\rangle}\!\!\mathinner{\langle{a_{k}}|}\rho\mathinner{|{a_{\ell}}\rangle}\!\!\mathinner{\langle{a_{\ell}|b_{n}}\rangle}\!\!\mathinner{\langle{b_{n}}|}\big)\mathinner{|{\varphi_{1}(a_{k})}\rangle\!\langle{\varphi_{1}(a_{\ell})}|}\otimes\mathinner{|{\varphi_{2}(b_{m})}\rangle\!\langle{\varphi_{2}(b_{n})}|}.

Using the weak regime approximations of Eq. (
A13
), for both the weak measurement of
\hat{A}
and of
\hat{B}

\displaystyle\mathinner{\langle{\hat{x}_{1}\otimes\hat{x}_{2}}\rangle}=\frac{\Tr\left(\hat{x}_{1}\otimes\hat{x}_{2}\,\eta\right)}{\Tr\left(\eta\right)}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{\sum_{k\ell mn}\Tr\big(E\,\mathinner{|{b_{m}}\rangle}\!\!\mathinner{\langle{b_{m}|a_{k}}\rangle}\!\!\mathinner{\langle{a_{k}}|}\rho\mathinner{|{a_{\ell}}\rangle}\!\!\mathinner{\langle{a_{\ell}|b_{n}}\rangle}\!\!\mathinner{\langle{b_{n}}|}\big)(a_{k}+a_{\ell})(b_{m}+b_{n})/4}{\Tr(E\rho)}

For pure pre- and post-selected states (i.e. for

B.3

n

Considering now
n

\rho

E

(A_{n}\cdots A_{1})_{\rho}^{E}\coloneqq\frac{\Tr(E\hat{A}_{n}\cdots\hat{A}_{1}\rho)}{\Tr(E\rho)}.

(A37)

For
\rho=\mathinner{|{\psi}\rangle\!\langle{\psi}|}
and

[

Without post-selection (

(A_{n}\cdots A_{1})_{\rho}^{\scalebox{.7}{$\mathbbm{1}$}}\coloneqq\Tr(\hat{A}_{n}\cdots\hat{A}_{1}\rho)

which in turn reduces to Eq. (
23

\rho=\mathinner{|{\psi}\rangle\!\langle{\psi}|}
-- and which, once again (and contrary to a general sequential weak value), is linear in the pre-selected state.
6
6

6

Note that, as seen previously for one and two observables (cf. Footnotes

(A_{n}\cdots A_{1})_{\psi}^{\scalebox{.7}{$\mathbbm{1}$}}=(A_{n}\cdots A_{1})_{\psi}^{\psi}

\mathinner{|{\psi}\rangle}

Appendix C
Proof that
\re[(BA)_{\psi}^{\scalebox{.7}{$\mathbbm{1}$}}]\geq-1/8
for two projection observables
\hat{A}
and
\hat{B}
(with eigenvalues
0,1
)

Suppose the two observables that are sequentially weakly measured on some initial state
\mathinner{|{\psi}\rangle}
are projectors,
\hat{A}
and
\hat{B}
.
Define then the (normalised) states
\mathinner{|{\alpha}\rangle}\coloneqq\frac{\hat{A}\mathinner{|{\psi}\rangle}}{\sqrt{\mathinner{\langle{\psi|\hat{A}|\psi}\rangle}}}
and
\mathinner{|{\beta}\rangle}\coloneqq\frac{\hat{B}\mathinner{|{\psi}\rangle}}{\sqrt{\mathinner{\langle{\psi|\hat{B}|\psi}\rangle}}}
(in the case where
\mathinner{\langle{\psi|\hat{A}|\psi}\rangle}=0
or
\mathinner{\langle{\psi|\hat{B}|\psi}\rangle}=0
, one can define
\mathinner{|{\alpha}\rangle}
or
\mathinner{|{\beta}\rangle}
to be any state orthogonal to
\mathinner{|{\psi}\rangle}
), such that
\mathinner{\langle{\alpha|\psi}\rangle}=\sqrt{\mathinner{\langle{\psi|\hat{A}|\psi}\rangle}}\geq 0
,
\mathinner{\langle{\beta|\psi}\rangle}=\sqrt{\mathinner{\langle{\psi|\hat{B}|\psi}\rangle}}\geq 0
,
\hat{A}\mathinner{|{\psi}\rangle}=\mathinner{\langle{\alpha|\psi}\rangle}\mathinner{|{\alpha}\rangle}
and
\hat{B}\mathinner{|{\psi}\rangle}=\mathinner{\langle{\beta|\psi}\rangle}\mathinner{|{\beta}\rangle}
.
We then have:

\displaystyle\re[(BA)_{\psi}^{\scalebox{.7}{$\mathbbm{1}$}}]=\re[\mathinner{\langle{\psi|\hat{B}\hat{A}|\psi}\rangle}]=\mathinner{\langle{\alpha|\psi}\rangle}\mathinner{\langle{\beta|\psi}\rangle}\re[\mathinner{\langle{\beta|\alpha}\rangle}].

(A43)

Now, using the AM-GM and Cauchy-Schwartz inequalities,

\displaystyle\mathinner{\langle{\alpha|\psi}\rangle}\mathinner{\langle{\beta|\psi}\rangle}\leq\left(\frac{\mathinner{\langle{\alpha|\psi}\rangle}+\mathinner{\langle{\beta|\psi}\rangle}}{2}\right)^{2}=\left(\Big(\frac{\mathinner{\langle{\alpha}|}+\mathinner{\langle{\beta}|}}{2}\Big)\mathinner{|{\psi}\rangle}\right)^{2}\leq\left\lVert\frac{\mathinner{|{\alpha}\rangle}+\mathinner{|{\beta}\rangle}}{2}\right\rVert^{2}=\frac{1+\re[\mathinner{\langle{\beta|\alpha}\rangle}]}{2}.

(A44)

Hence, either
\re[\mathinner{\langle{\beta|\alpha}\rangle}]\geq 0
in Eq. (
A43
), in which case (recalling that
\mathinner{\langle{\alpha|\psi}\rangle}\mathinner{\langle{\beta|\psi}\rangle}\geq 0
) one has
\re[(BA)_{\psi}^{\scalebox{.7}{$\mathbbm{1}$}}]\geq 0\,(\geq-\frac{1}{8})
, or
\re[\mathinner{\langle{\beta|\alpha}\rangle}]<0
and one then has (using the fact that
\frac{1+x}{2}x\geq-\frac{1}{8}
for all
x
)

\displaystyle\re[(BA)_{\psi}^{\scalebox{.7}{$\mathbbm{1}$}}]\geq\frac{1+\re[\mathinner{\langle{\beta|\alpha}\rangle}]}{2}\re[\mathinner{\langle{\beta|\alpha}\rangle}]\geq-\frac{1}{8}\,,

(A45)

which concludes the proof.

Note, furthermore, that by the linearity of the weak value with no post-selection with respect to the pre-selected state,
(BA)_{\rho}^{\scalebox{.7}{$\mathbbm{1}$}}=\sum_{i}q_{i}(BA)_{\psi_{i}}^{\scalebox{.7}{$\mathbbm{1}$}}
for a mixed state
\rho=\sum_{i}q_{i}\mathinner{|{\psi_{i}}\rangle\!\langle{\psi_{i}}|}
, which implies that the bound above also holds for
(BA)_{\rho}^{\scalebox{.7}{$\mathbbm{1}$}}
; that is,
\re[(BA)_{\rho}^{\scalebox{.7}{$\mathbbm{1}$}}]\geq-1/8
for any two projection observables
\hat{A}
and

Design of supercurrent diode by vortex phase texture
Yuri Fukaya, Maria Teresa Mercaldo, Daniel Margineda, Alessandro Crippa, Elia Strambini, Francesco Giazotto, Carmine Ortix, Mario Cuoco

Abstract
We investigate supercurrent nonreciprocal effects in a superconducting weak-link hosting distinct types of vortices. We demonstrate how the winding number of the vortex, its spatial configuration, and the shape of the superconducting lead can steer the sign and amplitude of the supercurrent rectification. We find a general criterion for the vortex pattern to maximize the rectification amplitude of the supercurrent. The underlying strategy is the search for specific vortex core positions yielding a vanishing amplitude of the supercurrent first harmonic. We also prove that supercurrent nonreciprocal effects can be used to diagnose high-winding vortex and to distinguish between different types of vorticity. Our results thus provide a toolkit to control the supercurrent rectification by means of vortex phase textures and nonreciprocal signatures to detect vortex states with nonstandard phase patterns.

Introduction

Vortices represent fundamental topological excitations in superfluid and superconductors. They have been predicted and successfully observed in a broad range of systems including superconductors

, liquid helium

;

Vortices are generally characterized by a quantized phase-winding and a suppressed order parameter at their core.
A gradient of the phase
\phi

\Delta=|\Delta|\exp{(i\phi)}
yields a circulating supercurrent around the core of the vortex whereas the amplitude is vanishing, i.e.
|\Delta|\rightarrow 0

V_{0}

V_{0}
can assume values
larger than one.
Apart from having
V_{0}
vortices with winding number equal to one, a giant vortex with winding number
V_{0}
can also realize.
A giant vortex is expected to be relevant in small superconductors with confined geometries. To date, probing of such unconventional vortex state has been mostly addressed by magnetic means in suitably tailored geometric configurations, e.g. by Hall probe
Geim et al. 1997
;
Geim et al. 2000
;
Morelle et al. 2004

;
Cren et al. 2009
;
Cren et al. 2011
;
Timmermans et al. 2016
;
Samokhvalov et al. 2019
.

Here,
we unveil a specific relation
between the occurrence of vortex states with
any
given winding number in a Josephson weak-link and the rectification of the supercurrent flowing across the junction.

;

;

Vortices or circular phase gradients, associated to conventional winding, are also expected to yield supercurrent diode effects, due to the induced Josephson phase shift

However,

By studying nonreciprocal supercurrent effects arising from distinct types of vortex phase texture in Josephson weak-links, in this paper, we show that while
a superconducting phase with vortices can generally lead to nonvanishing supercurrent rectification,
the sign and amplitude of the rectification in the Josephson diode effect can be manipulated by the position of the vortex core and the winding of the phase vortex.
We thereby uncover a general criterion to single out which phase vortex configuration can maximize the rectification amplitude of the supercurrent. Our findings provide a toolkit for the design and control of supercurrent rectification by vortex phase texture.

The structure of the paper is as follows.
Sect. II provides the details of the model Hamiltonian, the formulation and the methodology employed in this study.
In Sect. III we present the results for the supercurrent rectification in the case of one and two vortices within the superconducting leads for different values of the vortex winding.
Sect. III is devoted to the analysis of the supercurrent rectification assuming that the supercurrent is vanishing at the boundary of the junction. In Section IV, we examine how the boundary affects the vortex phase texture and assess the resulting behavior of supercurrent rectification.
The conclusions are reported in Sect. V.

III
Supercurrent Rectification: superconducting lead larger than the vortex phase coherence

In this section we study the supercurrent rectification by examining one and two vortices situated within the superconducting lead of the junction and assuming that it can be marked by different winding numbers. Here, we will first consider the case where the size of the vortex is much smaller than the superconducting lateral dimension so that the edges do not affect the vortex phase texture. Then, in Sect. IV we also analyze the rectification for the case of small size superconductors by imposing that the supercurrent is vanishing at the edge.

III.1
Single vortex configuration

In the previous section, we did not take into account the effects of the boundary near the edge on the vortex phase texture, as we focused on a scenario where the edge was sufficiently distant from the vortex core, making its impact on the phase texture negligible. However, this assumption does not hold if the vortex is positioned close to the edge or if the size of the superconductor is comparable to the superconducting coherence length. In such cases, the requirement for the supercurrent to be zero perpendicular to the edge can lead to a notable alteration of the superconducting phase surrounding the vortex core.
One effective way to impose this constraint on the supercurrent amplitude at the edge of the junction is through the method of vortex images. As illustrated in Fig. 6 (a), when considering a single vortex in a superconductor, we can introduce two additional image vortices outside the superconductor, positioned at a specific distance from the edge to ensure that the supercurrent amplitude becomes vanishing at the edge. It is important to note that the image vortices must have the same winding as the vortex within the superconductor because using an image antivortex would not nullify the supercurrent; rather, it would increase the supercurrent at the edge, since both circulations would be moving in the same direction.

V
Conclusions

We have demonstrated that nonreciprocal supercurrents
can be exploited to distinguish physical configurations with vortices having winding number equal to one, coexistence of vortices and antivortices or the occurrence of a giant vortex.
A suitable platform to observe these effects can be based on Josephson junctions made of nanoislands in the presence of an applied magnetic field
Kanda et al. 2004
. It is known that giant vortices can be induced by magnetic field for superconductors with coherence length much smaller than the magnetic penetration depth (e.g. Pb or Nb based nanostructures)
Kanda et al. 2004
.
Then, starting from a low magnetic field configuration with a vortex having, for instance, winding
V^{\mathrm{L}}_{0}=1
nearby the center of the superconducting lead, the transition to a higher winding vortex state by the increase of the magnetic field will be accompanied to a sizable variation of the rectification. Detection of such transitions can be used to probe the high-winding vortex phase.
Along this line, one can also envision a field-free platform
Amundsen et al. 2018
with circular current flow inducing high-winding vortices that concomitantly yield supercurrent rectification.
Moreover, since a generic phase vortex texture can be expanded in harmonics by employing vortex configurations with different windings, then, in principle our results provide a toolkit to design the supercurrent rectification for a wide variety of superconducting phase patterns.

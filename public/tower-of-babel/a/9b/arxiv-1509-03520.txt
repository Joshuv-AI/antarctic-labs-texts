Finite degrees of freedom for the refined blow-up profile of the semilinear heat equation
V. T. Nguyen Department of Mathematics, New York University in Abu Dhabi, Saadiyat Island, Abu Dhabi, United Arab Emirates. H. Zaag Université Paris 13, Sorbonne Paris Cité, LAGA, CNRS (UMR 7539), F-93430, Villetaneuse, France.

Abstract
We refine the asymptotic behavior of solutions to the semilinear heat equation with Sobolev subcritical power nonlinearity which blow up in some finite time at a blow-up point where the (supposed to be generic) profile holds. In order to obtain this refinement, we have to abandon the explicit profile function as a first order approximation, and take a non explicit function as a first order description of the singular behavior. This non explicit function is in fact a special solution which we construct, obeying some refined prescribed behavior. The construction relies on the reduction of the problem to a finite dimensional one and the use of a topological argument based on index theory to conclude. Surprisingly, the new non explicit profiles which we construct make a family with finite degrees of freedom, namely ( N + 1 ) ​ N 2 \frac{(N+1)N}{2} if N N is the dimension of the space. Keywords: Finite-time blow-up, single point blow-up, blow-up profile, stability, semilinear heat equations. Mathematics Subject Classification: 35K58, 35K55 (Primary); 35B40, 35B44 (Secondary).

Introduction.

We are interested in the following semilinear heat equation:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}u_{t}&=&\Delta u+|u|^{p-1}u,\\
u(0)&=&u_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\end{array}\right.

(1)

where
u(t):x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\to u(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}
,
\Delta
denotes the Laplacian in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, and

p>1\quad\text{or}\quad 1<p<\frac{N+2}{N-2}\quad\text{if}\quad N\geq 3.

Equation (
1
) is a simple model for a large class of nonlinear parabolic equations. In fact, it captures features common to a whole range of blow-up problems arsing in various physical situations, particularly it highlights the role of scaling and self-similarity. Among related equations, we would like nonetheless to mention: the solid fuel ignition model (Bebernes, Bressan and Eberly
[
2
]
), the thermal explosion (Bebernes and Kassoy
[
4
]
, Kassoy and Poland
[
24
]
),
[
25
]
), surface diffusion (Bernoff, Bertozzi and Witelski
[
3
]
), the motion by mean curvature (Soner and Souganidis
[
40
]
), vortex dynamics in superconductors (Chapman, Hunton and Ockendon
[
8
]
, Merle and Zaag
[
28
]
).

By standard results, the problem (
1
) has a unique classical solution
u(x,t)
continuous in time with values in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, which exists at least for small times. The solution
u(x,t)
may develop singularities in some finite time (see Kaplan
[
23
]
, Fujita
[
14
]
, Levine
[
26
]
, Ball
[
1
]
, Weissler
[
45
]
for the existence of finite-time blow-up solutions to (
1
)). In this case, we say that
u(x,t)
blows up in a finite time
T<+\infty
in the sense that

\lim_{t\to T}\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=+\infty.

Here we call
T
the blow-up time of
u(x,t)
. In such a blow-up case, we say that
\hat{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a blow-up point of
u
if
u
is not locally bounded in the neighborhood of
(\hat{a},T)
, this means that there exists
(x_{n},t_{n})\to(\hat{a},T)
such that
|u(x_{n},t_{n})|\to+\infty
when
n\to+\infty
.

Let us consider
u(t)
a solution of (
1
) which blows up in finite time
T
at only one blow-up point
\hat{a}
. From the translation invariance of (
1
), we may assume that
\hat{a}=0
. Studying the solution
u(x,t)
near the singularity
(0,T)
is based on the following
similarity variables
(see
[
18
,
19
]
):

\mathscr{T}[u](y,s)=(T-t)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}u(x,t),\quad y=\frac{x}{\sqrt{T-t}},\quad s=-\log(T-t),

(2)

and
w=\mathscr{T}[u]
solves a new parabolic equation in
(y,s)
,

\partial_{s}w=\mathcal{L}w-\frac{p}{p-1}w+|w|^{p-1}w,\quad(y,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[-\log T,+\infty),

(3)

where

\mathcal{L}=\Delta-\frac{y}{2}\cdot\nabla+1.

(4)

In view of (
2
), the study of
u(x,t)
as
(x,t)\to(0,T)
is then equivalent to the study of
\mathscr{T}[u](y,s)
as
s\to+\infty
, and each result for
u
has an equivalent formulation in term of
\mathscr{T}[u]
.

According to Giga and Kohn in
[
19
]
(see also
[
17
,
18
]
), we know that:

If
\hat{a}
is a blow-up point of
u
, then

\lim_{t\to T}(T-t)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}u(\hat{a}+y\sqrt{T-t},t)=\lim_{s\to+\infty}\mathscr{T}[u](y,s)=\pm\kappa,

(5)

uniformly on compact sets
|y|\leq R
, where
\kappa=(p-1)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}
.

The estimate (
5
) has been refined until the higher order by Filippas, Kohn and Liu
[
11
]
,
[
12
]
, Herrero and Velázquez
[
20
]
,
[
22
]
,
[
41
]
,
[
43
]
,
[
42
]
. More precisely, they classified the behavior of
\mathscr{T}[u](y,s)
for
|y|
bounded, and showed that one of the following cases occurs (up to replacing
u
by
-u
if necessary),

-
Case 1 (non-degenerate rate of blow-up): There exists
\ell\in\{1,\cdots,N\}
, and up to an orthogonal transformation of space coordinates,

\forall R>0,\;\sup_{|y|\leq R}\left|\mathscr{T}[u](y,s)-\left[\kappa+\frac{\kappa}{4ps}\left(2\ell-\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}|y_{i}|^{2}\right)\right]\right|=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\log s}{s^{2}}\right).

(6)

-
Case 2 (degenerate rate of blow-up): There exists
\mu>0
such that

\forall R>0,\;\sup_{|y|\leq R}\left|\mathscr{T}[u](y,s)-\kappa\right|=\mathcal{O}(e^{-\mu s}),

(7)

(this exponential convergence has been refined up to the order 1 by Herrero and Velázquez, but we omit that description since we choose in this work to concentrate on the non-degenerate rate of blow-up mentioned in the case 1 above).

If
\ell=N
, then
\hat{a}=0
is an isolated blow-up point from Velázquez
[
41
]
. Merle and Zaag
[
30
,
31
,
32
]
(with no sign condition), and Herrero and Velázquez
[
41
,
22
]
(in the positive case) established the following blow-up profile in the variable
\xi=\frac{y}{\sqrt{s}}
(which is the intermediate scale that separates the regular and singular parts in the non-degenerate case):

\forall R>0,\quad\sup_{|\xi|\leq R}\left|\mathscr{T}[u](\xi\sqrt{s},s)-f(\xi)\right|\to 0\quad\text{as $s\to+\infty$},

(8)

where

f(\xi)=\kappa\left(1+\frac{p-1}{4p}|\xi|^{2}\right)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}.

(9)

Herrero and Velázquez
[
21
]
proved that the profile (
9
) is generic in the case
N=1
, and they announced the same for
N\geq 2
, but they never published it.

Merle and Zaag
[
30
]
,
[
31
]
,
[
32
]
derived the limiting profile in the
u(x,t)
variable, in sense that
u(x,t)\to u^{*}(x)
when
t\to T
if
x\neq 0
and
x
is the neighborhood of
0
, with

u^{*}(x)\sim\left[\frac{8p|\log|x||}{(p-1)^{2}|x|^{2}}\right]^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\quad\text{as}\;x\to 0.

(10)

They also showed that all the behaviors (
6
) with
\ell=N
, (
8
) and (
10
) are equivalent.

Bricmont and Kupiainen
[
5
]
, Merle and Zaag in
[
29
]
showed the existence of initial data for (
1
) such that the corresponding solutions blow up in finite time
T
at only one blow-up point
\hat{a}=0
and verify the behavior (
8
). Note that the method of
[
29
]
allows to derive the stability of the blow-up behavior (
8
) with respect to perturbations in the initial data or the nonlinearity (see also Fermanian, Merle and Zaag
[
13
]
,
[
15
]
for other proofs of the stability).

In this work, considering the expansions (
6
) with
\ell=N
, (
8
) and (
10
), we ask whether we can carry on these expansions and obtain lower order estimates. In particular in (
10
), we wonder whether we can obtain the following terms of the expansion, up to bounded functions? In view of the self-similar transformation (
2
), a necessary condition would be to carry on the expansion (
6
) up to the scale of
e^{-\frac{s}{p-1}}=(T-t)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}
. Unfortunately, any attempt to carry on the expansion (
6
) would give bunches of terms in the scale of powers of
\frac{1}{s}=\frac{1}{|\log(T-t)|}
(with possibly
(\log s)^{b}
corrections). This way, instead of reaching the scale of powers of the blow-up variable
(T-t)
, we are trapped in logarithmic scales of that variable, namely
\frac{1}{|\log(T-t)|^{a}}
. Logarithmic scales also arise in some singular perturbation problems such as low Reynolds number fluids and some vibrating membranes studies (see Ward
[
44
]
and the references therein, see also Segur and Kruskal
[
39
]
for a Klein-Gordon equation). Since the logarithmic scales go to zero slowly, infinite logarithmic series may be of only limited practical use in approximating the exact solution. Relevant approximations, i.e., approximations up to lower order terms
(T-t)^{\beta}
for
\beta>0
, lie beyond all logarithmic scales. In order to escape all logarithmic scales, a possible idea would be to abandon expansions around the explicit profile function (
9
), which happens to be only an approximate solution of equation (
3
), and to linearize around a non explicit profile function which is a solution of equation (
3
). This has been done by Fermanian and Zaag
[
15
]
whose work shows that when linearizing around a fixed solution, say
\hat{u}
a radially symmetric and decreasing solution to equation (
1
) which blows up in finite time
T
at only
\hat{a}=0
, they can reach the order
(T-t)^{\beta}
for
\beta>0
through a modulation of the dilation of
\hat{u}
, provided that
N=1
. In this paper, we aim at extending their result to the higher dimensional case.

Let us explain the difficulty raised in
[
15
]
for the case
N\geq 2
. It is convenient to introduce the following definitions:

Definition 1.1
.

For all
(a,T)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}
, we denote by
\mathbb{B}_{a,T}
the set of all solutions to equation (
1
) which blow up in finite time
T
at point
x=a
(not necessary to be unique) and have the stable profile (
8
) (or (
6
) with
\ell=N
or (
10
)). We denote by
\mathbb{B}^{\prime}_{a,T}
the subset of
\mathbb{B}_{a,T}
where
a
is the unique blow-up point and where no blow-up occurs at infinity (in the sense that
|u(x,t)|\leq C
for all
|x|\geq c_{0}
and
t\in[0,T)
for some
C>0
and
c_{0}>0
).

Definition 1.2
.

We denote by
\mathbb{M}_{N}(\mathbb{R})
the set of all symmetric, real
(N\times N)
matrices.

Introducing the following dilation transformation for any
\lambda>0
,

\mathcal{D}_{\lambda}:\;u\mapsto\mathcal{D}_{\lambda}u:\;(x,t)\mapsto\lambda^{\frac{2}{p-1}}u\big(\lambda x,T-\lambda^{2}(T-t)\big),

(11)

we see that
\mathcal{D}_{\lambda}
is one-to-one from
\mathbb{B}_{a,T}
to itself.

Let us consider
\hat{u}
, a radially symmetric and decreasing solution to equation (
1
) in
\mathbb{B}^{\prime}_{0,T}
satisfying

\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left|\mathscr{T}[\hat{u}](y,s)-\left[f\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt{s}}\right)+\frac{N\kappa}{2ps}\right]\right|\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{s}},

(12)

where
f
is defined in (
9
) (see Appendix
A.1
for the justification of the existence of such a solution). The solution
\hat{u}
and
T
will be considered as fixed in the following. Then, we have the following classification from
[
15
]
:

If
u\in\mathbb{B}_{0,T}
, then, two cases arise:

- Case 1: There is a matrix
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{B}(u,\hat{u})\in\mathbb{M}_{N}(\mathbb{R})
(
\mathcal{B}\not\equiv 0
) such that

\mathscr{T}[u](y,s)-\mathscr{T}[\hat{u}](y,s)=\dfrac{1}{s^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{2}y^{T}\mathcal{B}y-tr(\mathcal{B})\right)+o\left(\frac{1}{s^{2}}\right)\quad\text{in}\;\;L^{2}_{\rho}.

(13)

- Case 2: There is a constant
C>0
,

\|\mathscr{T}[u](s)-\mathscr{T}[\hat{u}](s)\|_{L^{2}_{\rho}}\leq\dfrac{Ce^{-s/2}}{s^{3}},

(14)

(see Appendix
A.2
for the justification of this result).

When
N=1
(
\mathcal{B}(u,\hat{u})\in\mathbb{R}
), the authors in
[
15
]
noted the following property when
\lambda>0
and
u=\mathcal{D}_{\lambda}\hat{u}
defined in (
11
):

\mathscr{T}[\mathcal{D}_{\lambda}\hat{u}](y,s)-\mathscr{T}[\hat{u}](y,s)=\frac{\kappa\log\lambda}{ps^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{2}|y|^{2}-1\right)+o\left(\frac{1}{s^{2}}\right)\quad\text{in}\;L^{2}_{\rho},

(15)

hence
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{D}_{\lambda}\hat{u},\hat{u})=\frac{\kappa\log\lambda}{p}
(note that (
15
) is true wherever
N\geq 2
with
-1
replaced by
-N
). This is due to the fact that

\mathscr{T}[\mathcal{D}_{\lambda}\hat{u}](s)=\mathscr{T}[\hat{u}](s+2\log\lambda).

Therefore, choosing
\lambda
such that
\frac{\kappa\log\lambda}{p}=\mathcal{B}(u,\hat{u})
, that is
\lambda=e^{\frac{p}{\kappa}\mathcal{B}(u,\hat{u})}
, we see from (
13
) and (
15
) that

\mathscr{T}[u](y,s)-\mathscr{T}[\mathcal{D}_{\lambda}\hat{u}](y,s)=o\left(\frac{1}{s^{2}}\right)\quad\text{in}\;L^{2}_{\rho}.

Hence, only (
14
) holds and

\|\mathscr{T}[u](s)-\mathscr{T}[\mathcal{D}_{\lambda}\hat{u}](s)\|_{L^{2}_{\rho}}\leq\frac{Ce^{-s/2}}{s^{3}}.

This implies by
[
15
]
that when
p\geq 3
,

\left.\begin{array}[]{lll}&|u(x,t)-\mathcal{D}_{\lambda}\hat{u}(x,t)|\leq C_{0},&\quad\forall|x|\leq\epsilon_{0},\quad\forall t\in\big[t_{0},T\big),\\
&|u(x,t)-\mathcal{D}_{\lambda}\hat{u}(x,t)|\to 0&\quad\text{as}\;\;(x,t)\to(0,T),\end{array}\right\}

(16)

where
\epsilon_{0}>0
and
t_{0}\in[0,T)
.

In view of (
16
), it appears that the non explicit one-parameter family
\mathcal{D}_{\lambda}\hat{u}
serves as a sharp blow-up final profile for any arbitrary
u\in\mathbb{B}_{0,T}
, accurate up to bounded functions. This is to be considered as a refinement of (
10
), since
\mathcal{D}_{\lambda}\hat{u}
encapsulates all singular terms in the expansion of
u(x,t)
near the singularity
(0,T)
. However, there is a price to pay to reach such an accuracy, and the price lays in the fact that
\mathcal{D}_{\lambda}\hat{u}
is not explicit, unlike
u^{*}(x)
in (
10
).

If
N\geq 2
, the matrix
\mathcal{B}(u,\hat{u})
in (
13
) has
\frac{N(N+1)}{2}
real parameters. Applying the dilation trick of
[
15
]
allows to manage only one parameter. Therefore,
\frac{N(N+1)}{2}-1
parameters remain to be handled. This is the major reason preventing the authors in
[
15
]
from having such a striking result in higher dimensions. Trying to apply other transformations which keep the equation and
\mathbb{B}_{0,T}
invariant (rotation, symmetries of space coordinates, …), we could not handle all the remaining
\frac{N(N+1)}{2}-1
parameters. Fortunately, we could overcome this obstacle and construct a
\frac{N(N+1)}{2}
parameters family, which generalizes the
\mathcal{D}_{\lambda}\hat{u}
family and serves as the accurate profile for solutions in
\mathbb{B}_{0,T}
. In the following statement, we construct that family:

Theorem 1

(
Construction of blow-up solutions for equation (
1
) in
\mathbb{B}^{\prime}_{0,T}
with a refined behavior
)
.

For any
\mathcal{A}\in\mathbb{M}_{N}(\mathbb{R})
, there exists
s_{0}(\mathcal{A})>0
such that equation (
1
) has a unique solution
u_{\mathcal{A}}
in
\mathbb{B}^{\prime}_{0,T_{\mathcal{A}}}
with
T_{\mathcal{A}}=e^{-s_{0}(\mathcal{A})}
and the following holds

\mathscr{T}[u_{\mathcal{A}}](y,s)-\mathscr{T}[\hat{u}](y,s)=\dfrac{1}{s^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{2}y^{T}\mathcal{A}y-tr(\mathcal{A})\right)+o\left(\frac{1}{s^{2}}\right)\;\;\text{in}\;\;L^{2}_{\rho}\;\;\text{as $s\to+\infty.$}

(17)

Remark 1.3
.

From (
13
), we see that Theorem
1
remains true if we change
\hat{u}
by any other
\tilde{u}
in
\mathbb{B}^{\prime}_{0,T}
.

Remark 1.4
.

The blow-up time
T_{\mathcal{A}}
goes to zero when
\|\mathcal{A}\|\to+\infty
.

As mentioned earlier, Theorem
1
is a major step in extending (
16
) to the higher dimensional case. More precisely, we have the following result:

Theorem 2

(
A finite parameter family as a sharp profile for solutions of (
1
) having the same profile (
8
)
)
.

Consider
u\in\mathbb{B}_{0,T}
, then there exist a matrix
\mathcal{A}\in\mathbb{M}_{N}(\mathbb{R})
,
\epsilon_{0}>0
and
t_{0}\in[0,T)
such that

(i)\qquad\qquad\qquad\|\mathscr{T}[u](s)-\mathscr{T}[\bar{u}_{\mathcal{A}}](s)\|_{L^{2}_{\rho}}=\mathcal{O}\left(\dfrac{e^{-s/2}}{s^{3}}\right)\quad\text{as}\;\;s\to+\infty,

where
\bar{u}_{\mathcal{A}}(x,t)=u_{\mathcal{A}}(x,t+T_{\mathcal{A}}-T)
and
u_{\mathcal{A}}\in\mathbb{B}^{\prime}_{0,T_{\mathcal{A}}}
is the solution to equation (
1
) constructed in Theorem
1
. The convergence also holds in
L^{\infty}_{loc}
.

(ii)
For all
|x|\leq\epsilon_{0}
and for all
t\in[t_{0},T)
,

|u(x,t)-\bar{u}_{\mathcal{A}}(x,t)\big|\leq CmM\left\{\frac{(T-t)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p-1}}}{|\log(T-t)|^{\frac{3}{2}}},\frac{|x|^{1-\frac{2}{p-1}}}{|\log|x||^{2-\frac{1}{p-1}}}\right\},

(18)

where
mM=\min
if
1<p<3
and
mM=\max
if
p\geq 3
.

With this theorem, we see that if
p\geq 3
, then the difference
u-u_{\mathcal{A}}
is bounded and goes to zero as
t\to T
, up to a good choice of
\mathcal{A}
in
\mathbb{M}_{N}(\mathbb{R})
, although both functions blow up. Therefore, Theorem
2
directly yields the following corollary:

Corollary 1.5

(
The sharp profile encapsulates all singular terms if
p\geq 3
)
.

Assume in addition to Theorem
2
that
p\geq 3
. Then

\big|u(x,t)-u_{\mathcal{A}}(x,t+T_{\mathcal{A}}-T)\big|\leq C_{0},\quad\forall|x|\leq\epsilon_{0},\;\forall t\in[t_{0},T),

(19)

and

\big|u(x,t)-u_{\mathcal{A}}(x,t+T_{\mathcal{A}}-T)\big|\to 0\quad\text{as}\quad(x,t)\to(0,T).

Remark 1.6
.

If we denote by
\hat{\mathbb{B}}
the set of solutions constructed in Theorem
1
, namely

\hat{\mathbb{B}}=\hat{\mathbb{B}}(\hat{u})\triangleq\{u_{\mathcal{A}}\in\mathbb{B}^{\prime}_{0,T_{\mathcal{A}}}\;\text{constructed in Theorem \ref{theo:1} satisfying \eqref{equ:theo1refined}}\big|\mathcal{A}\in\mathbb{M}_{N}(\mathbb{R})\},

(20)

and define from Corollary
1.5
the following equivalence relation
\thicksim
on
\mathbb{B}_{0,T}
for

:

\forall u,v\in\mathbb{B}_{0,T},\quad u\thicksim v\Longleftrightarrow\exists\epsilon_{0}>0,\;\;(u-v)\in L^{\infty}\big(B(0,\epsilon_{0})\times[T-\epsilon_{0},T)\big),

then

u\diagup_{\thicksim}:\;\hat{\mathbb{B}}\thicksim\mathbb{M}_{N}(\mathbb{R}),

and

\mathbb{B}_{0,T}\thicksim\mathbb{M}_{N}(\mathbb{R})\times L^{\infty}_{x,t}.

This says that if we consider the blow-up asymptotic behavior given by (
6
) with
\ell=N
or (
8
) or (
10
) as a first order expansion describing the behavior of

near the singular point
(0,T)
, then the following orders have
\frac{N(N+1)}{2}
degrees of freedom which is the dimension of the set
\mathbb{M}_{N}(\mathbb{R})
, up to bounded functions.

Remark 1.7
.

If
u
blows up at time
T
at some point

with the profile (

), then
u(x-a,t)\in\mathbb{B}_{0,T}
. Thus, from Theorem

and Corollary

, we have a sharp profile for

, hence for

. Note also that if

, then estimates (

) and (

) hold for all

.

Not that Theorem
2
and Corollary
1.5
were already proved in one dimension by Fermanian and Zaag
[
15
]
. Thus, the novelty of our contribution lays in the higher dimensional case.

As in
[
15
]
, we believe that our result is a forward step in the problem of the regularity of the blow-up set, which has been poorly studied in the literature and is challenging. In particular, Zaag in
[
49
]
(see also
[
47
]
and
[
48
]
) used the ideas given in
[
15
]
and proved that under a non-degeneracy condition, the blow-up set is a
\mathcal{C}^{2}
manifold if it is continuous and its dimensional Hausdorff measure is equal to
N-1
. He also derived the first description of the blow-up profile of solutions to (
1
) near a non-isolated blow-up point.

Let us now briefly give the main ideas of the proof of Theorem
1
. The proof is based on techniques developed by Bricmont and Kupiainen in
[
5
]
, Merle and Zaag in
[
29
]
for the construction of a solution to equation (
1
) in
\mathbb{B}^{\prime}_{0,T}
, that is, prescribing only the behavior (
8
). Because we need in addition the estimate (
17
) (note that this estimate is the crucial point in order to obtain Theorem
2
), we need new ideas. Instead of linearizing equation (
3
) around
f\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt{s}}\right)
defined in (
9
) as in
[
5
]
and
[
29
]
, our major idea is to linearize equation (
3
) around
\mathscr{T}[\hat{u}]
, where
\hat{u}
is the given radially symmetric solution to equation (
1
) in
\mathbb{B}^{\prime}_{0,T}
. Although this choice may seem less interesting, given that
\mathscr{T}[\hat{u}]
is not explicit, unlike
f\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt{s}}\right)
, it is in fact much more advantageous, since linearizing around
\mathscr{T}[\hat{u}]
generates no rest term, unlike with
f\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt{s}}\right)
. This way, we are able to reach the order
\frac{1}{s^{2}}
in the expansion of solutions to equation (
3
) (as expected in (
17
)), unlike with
f\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt{s}}\right)
, where we are stuck in the
\frac{\log s}{s^{2}}

2.1
Formulation of the constructive problem.

Consider
s_{0}>0
to be fixed large enough later. Let us introduce the change of function

v_{\mathcal{A}}(y,s)=w_{\mathcal{A}}(y,s)-\hat{w}(y,s),

where
\hat{w}=\mathscr{T}[\hat{u}]
is the solution of (

) which satisfies (

) and
\hat{u}
is the considered radially decreasing solution of (

). Then, from (
3
),
v_{\mathcal{A}}
(or
v
for simplicity) solves the following equation: for all
(y,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[s_{0},+\infty)
,

v_{s}=(\mathcal{L}+\gamma(y,s))v+B(v)=(\mathcal{L}+\alpha(y,s))v+B(v)+(\gamma(y,s)-\alpha(y,s))v,

(28)

where
\mathcal{L}
is given in (
4
) and

\displaystyle\gamma(y,s)

\displaystyle=p\left(|\hat{w}(y,s)|^{p-1}-\kappa^{p-1}\right),

(29)

\displaystyle B(v)

\displaystyle=|\hat{w}+v|^{p-1}(\hat{w}+v)-|\hat{w}|^{p-1}\hat{w}-p|\hat{w}|^{p-1}v,

(30)

\displaystyle\alpha(y,s)

\displaystyle=p\left(|\varphi(y,s)|^{p-1}-\kappa^{p-1}\right),\quad\text{where}\;\;\varphi(y,s)=f\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt{s}}\right)+\frac{N\kappa}{2ps}.

(31)

As mentioned earlier, we do linearize equation (
3
) around
\hat{w}
, instead of the profile
f\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt{s}}\right)
. Doing
v
generates no rest term in equation (
28
) and this is one of the major ideas in this work. Looking at the second version of equation (
28
), the reader may ask why we use the function
\alpha(y,s)
instead of
\gamma(y,s)
as the potential. In fact, the use of the potential
\alpha
is convenient for the two following reasons:

i.

We want to use the same dynamical system formulation given in
[
5
]
and
[
29
]
, and that analysis was already based on the understanding of the linear operator
\mathcal{L}+\alpha
and its related Duhamel formulation, together with some related
a priori
estimates that were already obtained (see Lemma
2.7
below for these estimates).

ii.

In view of (
12
), we see from the definitions of
\alpha
and
\gamma
that they are almost the same in the sense that
\|\alpha(s)-\gamma(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}\to 0
as
s\to+\infty
. Therefore, the term
(\gamma-\alpha)v
in (
28
) is easily controlled (see Lemma
B.3
below).

Satisfying (
25
) reduces to the construction of a function
v
such that

\|v(s)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\to 0\quad\text{as}\;s\to+\infty.

(32)

In fact, we will be more specific and require
v
to satisfy some geometrical property, namely that
v
belongs to some set
V_{A}\subset L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
where
V_{A}(s)
shrinks to
v\equiv 0
as
s\to+\infty
. This set is very similar to that of
[
29
]
, except for the control of the null modes, where we modify the definition of
[
29
]
in a crucial way to handle the requirement given in (
26
). In fact, our new definition covers the one of
[
29
]
. Again, we insist on the fact that this is our second main contribution and novelty in this work, with respect to
[
29
]
(see Definition
2.1
for more clarity, especially condition (
46
) below).

Our analysis uses the Duhamel formulation of equation (
28
): for each
s\geq\sigma\geq s_{0}
, we have

v(s)=\mathcal{K}(s,\sigma)v(\sigma)+\int_{\sigma}^{s}\mathcal{K}(s,\tau)\left[B(v(\tau))+(\gamma(\tau)-\alpha(\tau))v(\tau)\right]d\tau,

(33)

where
\mathcal{K}
is the fundamental solution of the linear operator
\mathcal{L}+\alpha
defined for each
\sigma>0
and
s\geq\sigma
by

\partial_{s}\mathcal{K}(s,\sigma)=(\mathcal{L}+\alpha)\mathcal{K}(s,\sigma),\quad\mathcal{K}(\sigma,\sigma)=Identity.

(34)

The linear operator
\mathcal{L}
is self-adjoint in
L^{2}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, where
L^{2}_{\rho}
is the weighted
L^{2}
space associated with the weight
\rho
defined by

\rho(y)=\prod_{i=1}^{N}\rho_{1}(y_{i})\quad\text{with}\quad\rho_{1}(\xi)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi}}e^{-\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{4}},

(35)

and

spec(\mathcal{L})=\{1-\frac{n}{2},\;n\in\mathbb{N}\}.

For
\beta=(\beta_{1},\cdots,\beta_{N})\in\mathbb{N}^{N}
, the eigenfunctions corresponding to
1-\frac{|\beta|}{2}
(
|\beta|=\beta_{1}+\cdots+\beta_{N}
) are

\phi_{\beta}(y)=\phi_{\beta_{1}}(y_{1})\cdots\phi_{\beta_{N}}(y_{N}),

(36)

where

\phi_{k}(\xi)=\sum_{i=0}^{\left[\frac{k}{2}\right]}\frac{k!}{i!(k-2i)!}(-1)^{i}\xi^{k-2i},\quad k\in\mathbb{N},

(37)

satisfy

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi_{k}(\xi)\phi_{n}(\xi)\rho_{1}(\xi)d\xi=2^{k}k!\delta_{k,n}.

(38)

Note from Lemma
B.1
that the potential
\alpha(y,s)
has two fundamental properties:

i)

\alpha(\cdot,s)\to 0
in
L^{2}_{\rho}
as
s\to+\infty
. In particular, the effect of
\alpha
on the bounded sets or in the "blow-up" region (
|y|\leq K\sqrt{s}
) is regarded as a perturbation of the effect of
\mathcal{L}
.

ii)

outside of the "blow-up" region, we have the following property: for all
\epsilon>0
, there exist
C_{\epsilon}>0
and
s_{\epsilon}
such that

\sup_{s\geq s_{\epsilon},|y|\geq C_{\epsilon}\sqrt{s}}\left|\alpha(y,s)-\left(-\frac{p}{p-1}\right)\right|\leq\epsilon.

(39)

This means that
\mathcal{L}+\alpha
behaves like
\mathcal{L}-\frac{p}{p-1}
in the region
|y|\geq K\sqrt{s}
. Because
1
is the largest eigenvalue of
\mathcal{L}
, the operator
\mathcal{L}-\frac{p}{p-1}
has a purely negative spectrum. Therefore, the control of
v(y,s)
in
L^{\infty}
outside of the "blow-up" region will be done without difficulties.

Since the behavior of
\alpha
inside and outside of the "blow-up" region is different, let us decompose
v
as follows: Let
\chi_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}([0,+\infty))
with
\text{supp}(\chi_{0})\subset[0,2]
and
\chi_{0}\equiv 1
on
[0,1]
. We define

\chi(y,s)=\chi_{0}\left(\frac{|y|}{K\sqrt{s}}\right),

(40)

where
K>0
is to be fixed large enough, and write

v(y,s)=v_{b}(y,s)+v_{e}(y,s),

(41)

where

v_{b}(y,s)=\chi(y,s)v(y,s)\quad\text{and}\quad v_{e}(y,s)=(1-\chi(y,s))v(y,s).

Note that
supp(v_{b}(s))\subset\mathbf{B}(0,2K\sqrt{s})
and
supp(v_{e}(s))\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\mathbf{B}(0,K\sqrt{s})
.

In order to control
v_{b}
, we expand it with respect to the spectrum of
\mathcal{L}
in
L^{2}_{\rho}
since the eigenfunctions of
\mathcal{L}
span the whole space
L^{2}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. More precisely, we write
v
as follows:

\displaystyle v(y,s)=v_{0}(s)+v_{1}(s)\cdot y+\frac{1}{2}y^{T}v_{2}\,y-tr(v_{2})+v_{-}(y,s)+v_{e}(y,s),

(42)

where

v_{0}(s)=P_{0}(v_{b})(y,s)
,
v_{1}(s)\cdot y=P_{1}(v_{b})(y,s)
,
v_{-}(y,s)=P_{-}(v_{b})(y,s)=\sum_{m\geq 3}P_{m}(v_{b})(y,s)
, and
P_{m}
is the projector on the eigenspace corresponding to the eigenvalue
1-\frac{m}{2}
defined by

P_{m}(v_{b})(y,s)=\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{N},|\beta|=m}\frac{\phi_{\beta}(y)}{\|\phi_{\beta}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\rho}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\phi_{\beta}(y)v_{b}(y,s)\rho(y)dy,

(43)

where
\phi_{\beta}
is defined in (
36
), and
v_{2}(s)\in\mathbb{M}_{N}(\mathbb{R})
defined by

v_{2}(s)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}v_{b}(y,s)\mathcal{M}(y)\rho(y)dy,

(44)

where

\mathcal{M}(y)=\left\{\frac{1}{4}y_{i}y_{j}-\frac{1}{2}\delta_{ij}\right\}_{1\leq i,j\leq N}.

(45)

The reader should keep in mind that
v_{m},m=0,1,2
and
v_{-}
are coordinates of
v_{b}
and not those of
v
.

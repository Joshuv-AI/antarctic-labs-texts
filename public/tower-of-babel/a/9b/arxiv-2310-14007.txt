Local smooth convergence of 𝔽 \mathbb{F} -limit flows
Pak-Yeung Chan, Zilu Ma, Yongjia Zhang

Abstract
The metric flow is introduced and extensively studied by Bamler [ Bam20b , Bam20c ] , especially as an 𝔽 \mathbb{F} -limit of a sequence of smooth Ricci flows with uniformly bounded Nash entropy, in which case each regular point on the limit is a point of smooth convergence. In this note, we shall consider the 𝔽 \mathbb{F} -convergence of a sequence of 𝔽 \mathbb{F} -limit flows, and, like Bamler, show that each regular point on the limit is also a point of smooth convergence. The main result will be applied in a forthcoming work of the authors [ CMZ23 ] .

Introduction

The metric flow
[
Bam20b
,
Bam20c
]
is a reasonable generalization of the Ricci flow. A metric flow that arises as an
\mathbb{F}
-limit of smooth Ricci flows with uniformly bounded Nash entropy is in particular worthy of detailed study, since it is a generalization of the classical singularity model, and is essential to extending the Ricci flow beyond singularities. In view of the fact that such metric flow provides an important category for the Ricci flow singularity analysis, it shall be called the

\mathbb{F}
-limit flow
by us, whose definition will be presented shortly.

Because of the massive content of
[
Bam20a
,
Bam20b
,
Bam20c
]
, and of the lengthy definitions of the notions encountered in this note, such as, metric flows,
\mathbb{F}
-convergence, and convergence within a correspondence, we shall not introduce any of them in details, but assume the reader’s familiarity with the definitions and notations in Bamler’s three papers. All notations which we adopt are the same as Bamler’s, and all definitions mentioned can be found in
[
Bam20a
,
Bam20b
,
Bam20c
]
.

Throughout this note, we assume that
I
is a left-open right-closed interval of the form
(-T,S]
, where
T\leq+\infty
,
S<+\infty
. We shall use the capital letter
\mathcal{M}
(sometimes with indices) to represent a Ricci flow, namely, a space-time produce
M\times I
with a Riemannian metric evolving according to the Ricci flow equation. The lowercase letters
x
,
y
, etc., always represent space-time points in a Ricci flow or points in a metric flow. The letter
\mathfrak{t}
stands for the time function. For instance, if
x=(a_{0},t_{0})\in\mathcal{M}=M\times I
, then
\mathfrak{t}(x)=t_{0}
. As
[
Bam20b
]
, the notations
\mathcal{X}_{t}
and
\mathcal{X}_{I_{0}}
represent a time-slice and a time-slab of the metric flow
\mathcal{X}
, respectively; the same notations are also applied to Ricci flows. All smooth Ricci flows under our consideration are complete and have bounded curvature on compact time-intervals.

Definition 1.1

(
\mathbb{F}
-limit flow)
.

A metric flow
\big(\mathcal{X},(\mu_{t})_{t\in I^{\prime}}\big)
over interval
I
, where
I^{\prime}\subset I
and
|I\setminus I^{\prime}|=0
, is called an
n
-dimensional
\mathbb{F}
-limit flow
, if there is a sequence of Ricci flows
\{\mathcal{M}^{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
over
I
and a sequence of points
x^{i}_{0}\in\mathcal{M}^{i}_{\sup I}
, such that

\displaystyle\big(\mathcal{M}^{i},(\nu^{i}_{x^{i}_{0};t})_{t\in I}\big)\xrightarrow[i\to\infty]{\hskip 8.19447pt\mathbb{F}\hskip 8.19447pt}\big(\mathcal{X},(\mu_{t})_{t\in I^{\prime}}\big).

(1.1)

If
I
is unbounded, then the convergence is assumed to be on compact time intervals.

If, in addition to (
1.1
), there are positive numbers
\tau>0
and
Y<+\infty
, such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{x_{0}^{i}}(\tau)\geq-Y,

(1.2)

then
\big(\mathcal{X},(\mu_{t})_{t\in I^{\prime}}\big)
is called
(\tau,Y)
-noncollapsed
.

Remark.
It is to be admitted that our definition of
\mathbb{F}
-limit flow is a bit too restrictive. Indeed, in a more reasonably general definition, each Ricci flow
\mathcal{M}^{i}
in (
1.1
) should live in its own time-interval
I^{i}=(-T^{i},S]
with
T^{i}\to T
, or, even more generally,
I^{i}=(-T^{i},S^{i}]
with
T^{i}\to T
and
S^{i}\to S
. However, since smooth convergence point is a local notion, one may always restrict all flows to a common interval, and Theorem
1.2
still holds with the more general definition. In particular, we remark that this does not affect our application in
[
CMZ23
]
.

According to
[
Bam20b
, §7]
, the space of
n
-dimensional
\mathbb{F}
-limit flows is also sequentially compact with respect to the
\mathbb{F}
-topology. We shall consider a sequence of
\mathbb{F}
-limit flows, which converges in the
\mathbb{F}
-sense to a metric flow. The limit is naturally an
\mathbb{F}
-limit flow. Our main result is that, if the sequence consists of
(\tau,Y)
-noncollapsed
\mathbb{F}
-limit flows, then the convergence is smooth in the sense of
[
Bam20b
, Theorem 9.31]
wherever the limit is smooth.

Theorem 1.2

(Main Theorem)
.

Let
\big(\mathcal{X},(\mu_{t})_{t\in I^{\prime}}\big)
be an
n
-dimensional
\mathbb{F}
-limit flow over interval
I
. Assume that there is a sequence of
n
-dimensional
(\tau,Y)
-noncollapsed
\mathbb{F}
-limit metric flows
\left\{\big(\mathcal{X}^{i},(\mu^{i}_{t})_{t\in I^{{}^{\prime},i}}\big)\right\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
over
I
, where
\tau>0
and
Y<+\infty
are some constants independent of
i
, such that

\displaystyle\big(\mathcal{X}^{i},(\mu^{i}_{t})_{t\in I^{{}^{\prime},i}}\big)\xrightarrow[i\to\infty]{\hskip 8.19447pt\mathbb{F}\hskip 8.19447pt}\big(\mathcal{X},(\mu_{t})_{t\in I^{\prime}}\big).

(1.3)

Then the above convergence is smooth in the sense of
[
Bam20b
, Theorem 9.31]
on
\mathcal{R}_{(-\infty,\sup I)\cap I}\subset\mathcal{X}
. In other words, for each
y\in\mathcal{R}_{(-\infty,\sup I)\cap I}\subset\mathcal{X}
, we have
y\in\mathcal{R}^{*}\subset\mathcal{X}
.

Remark.

In our setting, there are always multiple convergences considered. For instance, the defining convergence (
1.1
) and the convergence (
1.3
) studied in the main theorem may not have the same set of smooth convergence. Whenever necessary, we shall use subindices to indicate the convergence in question. For example,
\mathcal{R}^{*}_{\eqref{the-convergence-in-question}}\subset\mathcal{X}
represents the points of smooth convergence in (
1.3
).

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be an
n
-dimensional Ricci flow over interval
I
.
(\mu_{t})_{t\in I}
is a conjugate heat flow on
\mathcal{M}
. Assume that
\{(\mathcal{X}^{i},(\mu_{t})_{t\in I^{{}^{\prime},i}})\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
is a sequence of
n
-dimensional
(\tau,Y)
-noncollapsed
\mathbb{F}
-limit flows over

(\mathcal{M},(\mu_{t})_{t\in I})
in the

-sense, then the convergence is locally smooth on
\mathcal{M}_{(-\infty,\sup I)\cap I}
. Precisely, there are open sets
U_{i}\subset\mathcal{M}
satisfying
U_{1}\subset U_{2}\subset U_{3}\subset\ldots
and
\cup_{i=1}^{\infty}U_{i}=\mathcal{M}_{(-\infty,\sup I)\cap I}
, time-preserving diffeomorphisms

\displaystyle\psi^{i}:U_{i}\to V_{i}\subset\mathcal{R}^{i}\subset\mathcal{X}^{i},

and a sequence of positive numbers
\varepsilon_{i}\searrow 0

[
Bam20b

Regular points on
\mathbb{F}
-limit flows

The following theorem is a generalization of
[
Bam20a
, Theorem 10.4]
. It says that if the metric flow is regular enough at a point, then the Nash entropy cannot be too far away from zero. Recall that the Nash entropy is defined for a
(\tau,Y)
-noncollapsed
\mathbb{F}
-limit flow since the singular set has zero measure. The Nash entropy is also continuous with respect to
\mathbb{F}
-convergence according to
[
Bam20c
, Theorem 2.10]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

For any
\varepsilon>0
and any
Y<+\infty
, there is a positive number
\delta(Y,\varepsilon)>0
, depending also on the dimension
n
, with the following property. Let
\big(\mathcal{X},(\mu_{t})_{t\in I^{\prime}}\big)
be an
n
-dimensional
(\tau_{0},Y_{0})
-noncollapsed
\mathbb{F}
-limit flow for some
\tau_{0}>0
and
Y_{0}<+\infty
. For any
x\in\mathcal{X}_{(-\infty,\sup I)\cap I}
and any

, if
[\mathfrak{t}(x)-r^{2},\mathfrak{t}(x)]\subset I
,
P^{-}(x;r)\Subset\mathcal{R}
is unscathed, and

\displaystyle|{\Rm}|\leq r^{-2}\quad\text{on}\quad P^{-}(x;r),

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{x}(r^{2})\geq-Y,

then we have

\mathcal{N}_{x}(\delta r^{2})\geq-\varepsilon.

Proof.

This theorem follows from a limiting argument and the continuity of the Nash entropy with respect to the
\mathbb{F}
-convergence
[
Bam20c
, Theorem 2.10]
. Let us assume that the interval
I
has finite length. The more general case follows easily from our argument, and is left to the reader.

For any
\varepsilon>0
,
Y<+\infty
, we pick
\delta=\frac{1}{4}\delta_{0}(\varepsilon,2Y)>0
, where
\delta_{0}(\cdot,\cdot)
is the constant in the statement of
[
Bam20a
, Theorem 10.4]
.
Let
\mathcal{M}^{i}
be a sequence of
n
-dimensional Ricci flows and
x_{0}^{i}\in\mathcal{M}^{i}_{\sup I}
a sequence of points as in (
1.1
), such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{x^{i}_{0}}(\tau_{0})\geq-Y_{0},

\displaystyle\big(\mathcal{M}^{i},(\nu_{x^{i}_{0};t})_{t\in I}\big)\xrightarrow[i\to\infty]{\hskip 8.19447pt\mathbb{F}\hskip 8.19447pt}\big(\mathcal{X},(\mu_{t})_{t\in I^{\prime}}\big).

Since
\mathbb{F}
-convergence is equivalent to convergence within a correspondence (c.f.
[
Bam20b
, §5]
), let us pick a correspondence
\mathfrak{C}=(Z_{t},\varphi^{i}_{t})
over
I
between
\big(\mathcal{M}^{i},(\nu_{x^{i}_{0};t})_{t\in I}\big)
and
\big(\mathcal{X},(\mu_{t})_{t\in I^{\prime}}\big)
, such that

\displaystyle\big(\mathcal{M}^{i},(\nu_{x^{i}_{0};t})_{t\in I}\big)\xrightarrow[i\to\infty]{\hskip 8.19447pt\mathbb{F},\ \mathfrak{C}\hskip 8.19447pt}\big(\mathcal{X},(\mu_{t})_{t\in I^{\prime}}\big).

(2.1)

By
[
Bam20b
, Theorem 6.45]
, there are points
x^{i}\in\mathcal{M}^{i}_{\mathfrak{t}(x)}
, such that

\displaystyle x^{i}\xrightarrow[i\to\infty]{\hskip 8.19447pt\mathfrak{C}\hskip 8.19447pt}x.

On the other hand, since
[
Bam20c
, Theorem 2.5]
implies that
P^{-}(x;r)\Subset\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{R}^{*}_{\eqref{extranonsense10}}
, we can apply
[
Bam20b
, Theorem 9.31]
to find an open set
P^{-}(x;r)\Subset U\subset\mathcal{R}^{*}
and time-preserving diffeomorphisms

\displaystyle\psi_{i}:U\rightarrow V^{i}\subset\mathcal{M}^{i}

with all the properties in
[
Bam20b
, Theorem 9.31]
. In particular, we have
\psi_{i}^{-1}(x^{i})\rightarrow x
and

\displaystyle|{\Rm^{i}}|\leq 4r^{-2}\qquad\text{ on }\qquad P^{-}(x^{i};\tfrac{1}{2}r)

(2.2)

for all
i
large enough.

By the continuity of the Nash entropy
[
Bam20c
, Theorem 2.10]
, we have that
\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\mathcal{N}_{x^{i}}(r^{2})=\mathcal{N}_{x}(r^{2})\geq-Y
. Thus

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{x^{i}}\big(\tfrac{1}{4}r^{2}\big)\geq-2Y

(2.3)

for all
i
large enough.

Applying
[
Bam20a
, Theorem 10.4]
at each
x^{i}
using (
2.2
) (
2.3
), we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{x^{i}}(\tfrac{1}{4}\delta_{0}r^{2})\geq-\varepsilon

for all

large enough, where

is the constant in
[
Bam20a
, Theorem 10.4]
. Finally, from the continuity of the Nash entropy
[
Bam20c
, Theorem 2.10]
again and our choice of
\delta
, the conclusion follows.

\displaystyle d^{\mathfrak{C}_{I\cap(-\infty,t_{0}]}}_{W_{1}}\bigg((\mathcal{X}_{I\cap(-\infty,t_{0}]},\nu_{y;t}),(\mathcal{X}^{i_{k}}_{I\cap(-\infty,t_{0}]},\nu_{y^{i_{k}};t})\bigg)\leq\frac{1}{k}

\displaystyle d^{\mathfrak{C}^{i_{k}}_{I\cap(-\infty,t_{0}]}}_{W_{1}}\bigg((\mathcal{M}^{i_{k}}_{j_{k},I\cap(-\infty,t_{0}]},\nu_{y^{i_{k}}_{j_{k}};t}),(\mathcal{X}^{i_{k}}_{I\cap(-\infty,t_{0}]},\nu_{y^{i_{k}};t})\bigg)\leq\frac{1}{k}
.

The third property above follows from
[
Bam20b
, Theorem 6.40]
.

Next, for each
k
, regarding
\mathfrak{C}^{i_{k}}
,
\mathfrak{C}
as correspondences between
\mathcal{M}^{i_{k}}_{j_{k}}
and
\mathcal{X}^{i_{k}}
,
\mathcal{X}^{i_{k}}
and
\mathcal{X}
, respectively, (and omitting many other flows that are also within these correspondences), we combine them into one correspondence
\tilde{\mathfrak{C}}^{k}
by applying
[
Bam20b
, Lemma 5.15]
. Note that all the correspondence in our consideration are defined on the same time interval
I
, this avoid many nuances in the definition of the
\mathbb{F}
-distances within a correspondence; one can easily carry this to more general cases. It is easy to verify that

\displaystyle d^{\tilde{\mathfrak{C}}^{k}}_{\mathbb{F}}\bigg(\big(\mathcal{M}^{i_{k}}_{j_{k}},\nu_{x^{i_{k}}_{j_{k}};t}\big),(\mathcal{X},\mu_{t})\bigg)\leq

\displaystyle\ d^{\tilde{\mathfrak{C}}^{k}}_{\mathbb{F}}\bigg(\big(\mathcal{M}^{i_{k}}_{j_{k}},\nu_{x^{i_{k}}_{j_{k}};t}\big),(\mathcal{X}^{i_{k}},\mu^{i_{k}}_{t})\bigg)+d^{\tilde{\mathfrak{C}}^{k}}_{\mathbb{F}}\bigg((\mathcal{X}^{i_{k}},\mu^{i_{k}}_{t}),(\mathcal{M},\mu_{t})\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ d^{\mathfrak{C}^{i_{k}}}_{\mathbb{F}}\bigg(\big(\mathcal{M}^{i_{k}}_{j_{k}},\nu_{x^{i_{k}}_{j_{k}};t}\big),(\mathcal{X}^{i_{k}},\mu^{i_{k}}_{t})\bigg)+d^{\mathfrak{C}}_{\mathbb{F}}\bigg((\mathcal{X}^{i_{k}},\mu^{i_{k}}_{t}),(\mathcal{M},\mu_{t})\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \frac{2}{k}.

In like manner, we also have

\displaystyle d^{\tilde{\mathfrak{C}}^{k}_{I\cap(-\infty,t_{0}]}}_{W_{1}}\bigg(\big(\mathcal{M}^{i_{k}}_{j_{k},I\cap(-\infty,t_{0}]},\nu_{y^{i_{k}}_{j_{k}};t}\big),(\mathcal{M}_{I\cap(-\infty,t_{0}]},\nu_{y;t})\bigg)\leq\frac{2}{k}.

Combining all the
\tilde{\mathfrak{C}}^{k}
into one correspondence
\tilde{\mathfrak{C}}
between
\big(\mathcal{M}^{i_{k}}_{j_{k}},\nu_{x^{i_{k}}_{j_{k}};t}\big)
(

) and
(\mathcal{M},\mu_{t})
by way of applying the proof of

Bam20b
, Lemma 2.13]
at each

, we obtain the following convergence within correspondence
\tilde{\mathfrak{C}}
:

\displaystyle\big(\mathcal{M}^{i_{k}}_{j_{k}},\nu_{x^{i_{k}}_{j_{k}};t}\big)\xrightarrow[\hskip 8.19447ptk\to\infty\hskip 8.19447pt]{\mathbb{F},\ \tilde{\mathfrak{C}}}(\mathcal{X},\mu_{t})

\displaystyle y^{i_{k}}_{j_{k}}\xrightarrow[\hskip 8.19447ptk\to\infty\hskip 8.19447pt]{\tilde{\mathfrak{C}}}y.

Finally, by

3.3
Local conjugate heat flow bound along the converging points

By Proposition
3.2
, for each
i
we may pass
j
to a subsequence, such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{y^{i}_{j}}(r^{2})\geq-\varepsilon\qquad\text{for all}\qquad j\in\mathbb{N},

whenever
i
is large enough. Write
d\mu_{t}:=u_{t}dg_{t}
on
\mathcal{R}\subset\mathcal{X}
and
d\nu_{x^{i}_{j};t}:=u^{i}_{j,t}dg_{t}
. Define

\displaystyle c_{0}:=u_{t_{0}}(y)>0.

The following proposition follows from a similar argument as Proposition
3.2
.

Proposition 3.3
.

For each
i
large enough, there are infinitely many
j
, such that

\displaystyle u^{i}_{j,t_{0}}(y^{i}_{j})>\tfrac{1}{2}c_{0}.

Sketch of proof.

Arguing in the same way as the proof of Proposition
3.2
, suppose that we can find a subsequence
\{i_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
, such that for each
i_{k}
, there are infinitely many
j
with

\displaystyle u^{i_{k}}_{j,t_{0}}(y^{i_{k}}_{j})\leq\tfrac{1}{2}c_{0}.

Then we may choose
j_{k}
for each
k
and combine
\mathfrak{C}^{i_{k}}
and
\mathfrak{C}
into a correspondence
\tilde{\mathfrak{C}}
just like the proof of Proposition
3.2
, such that

\displaystyle\big(\mathcal{M}^{i_{k}}_{j_{k}},\nu_{x^{i_{k}}_{j_{k}};t}\big)\xrightarrow[\hskip 8.19447ptk\to\infty\hskip 8.19447pt]{\mathbb{F},\ \tilde{\mathfrak{C}}}(\mathcal{X},\mu_{t})

(3.9)

\displaystyle y^{i_{k}}_{j_{k}}\xrightarrow[\hskip 8.19447ptk\to\infty\hskip 8.19447pt]{\tilde{\mathfrak{C}}}y,

(3.10)

\displaystyle u^{i_{k}}_{j_{k},t_{0}}(y^{i_{k}}_{j_{k}})\leq\tfrac{1}{2}c_{0}.

(3.11)

Since
y\in\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{R}^{*}_{\eqref{extranonsense3}}\subset\mathcal{X}
by
[
Bam20c
, Theorem 2.5]
.
[

); this finishes the proof.
∎

3.4
Conclusion

In summary, applying Proposition
3.2
and Proposition
3.3
successively, for each
i
large enough, we may pass each
\{\big(\mathcal{M}^{i}_{j},\nu_{x^{i}_{j};t}\big)\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
to a subsequence, such that
\mathcal{N}_{y^{i}_{j}}(r^{2})\geq-\varepsilon
and
u^{i}_{j,t_{0}}(y^{i}_{j})>\tfrac{1}{2}c_{0}
at the same time. By our assumption on
\varepsilon
,
[
Bam20a
, Theorem 10.3]
, and
[
Bam20a
, Theorem 6.1]
, we have that whenever
i
is large enough,

\displaystyle|{\Rm^{i}_{j}}|\leq\tfrac{1}{2}r^{-2}\quad\text{on}\quad P(y^{i}_{j};\tfrac{1}{2}r)\qquad\text{ for all }j\in\mathbb{N},

\displaystyle|B(y^{i}_{j},\tfrac{1}{2}r)|\geq c(n)(\tfrac{1}{2}r)^{n}\qquad\text{ for all }j\in\mathbb{N},

\displaystyle u^{i}_{j,t_{0}}(y^{i}_{j})>\tfrac{1}{2}c_{0}\qquad\text{ for all }j\in\mathbb{N}.

For each fixed
i
large enough, we apply
[
Bam20b
, Lemma 9.33]
to the sequences
\{\big(\mathcal{M}^{i}_{j},\nu_{x^{i}_{j};t}\big)\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
and
\{y^{i}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
. Thus, there are positive constant
r^{*}=r^{*}(r,c_{0})>0
and
C=C(r,c_{0})<+\infty
, independent of
i
, such that

1.

\displaystyle P(y^{i};r^{*})\subset\mathcal{R}^{i}
is unscathed,

2.

\displaystyle|B(y^{i},r^{*})|\geq 1/C
,

3.

\displaystyle|{\Rm^{i}}|\leq C
on
\displaystyle P(y^{i};r^{*})
,

where the first conclusion is a direct consequence of
[
Bam20b
, Lemma 9.33]
and the last two are due to the definition of smooth convergence. It follows immediately from
[
Bam20b
, Definition 9.30]
that
y\in\mathcal{X}
is a point of smooth convergence in (
3.4
). This finishes the proof of the main theorem.

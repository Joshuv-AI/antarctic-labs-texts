Jones rational coincidences
Ruth Lawrence, Ori Rosenstein

Abstract




In memory of Vaughan Jones

Abstract:

We investigate coincidences of the (one-variable) Jones polynomial amongst rational knots, what we call ‘Jones rational coincidences’. We provide moves on the continued fraction expansion of the associated rational which we prove do not change the Jones polynomial and conjecture (based on experimental evidence from all rational knots with determinant
<900
) that these moves are sufficient to generate all Jones rational coincidences. These coincidences are generically not mutants, as is verified by checking the HOMFLYPT polynomial. In the process we give a new formula for the Jones polynomial of a rational knot based on a continued fraction expansion of the associated rational, which has significantly fewer terms than other formulae known to us. The paper is based on the second author’s Ph.D. thesis and gives an essentially self-contained account.

Keywords: Jones polynomial, rational knots, rational tangles

Mathematics Subject Classification 2020:
57K10

Introduction

The (one-variable) Jones polynomial was defined in
[
6
]
as an ambient isotopy invariant of links in
S^{3}
. Although it has subsequently been found to be connected with many interesting other mathematical structures (Temperley-Leib algebra, quantum group
U_{q}sl_{2}
, topological quantum field theory etc.), many basic properties remain unknown. For example, it is still unknown whether Jones’ conjecture holds, that
V_{K}
distinguishes the unknot from other knots, that is
V_{K}\equiv 1
for a knot
K
only if
K
is the unknot; see
[
18
]
for numerical evidence up to 22 crossings. It is known that Conway mutation
[
3
]
and various generalisations generate knots with identical Jones polynomial, see
[
15
]
and references therein, as well as
[
8
]
. Kanenobu
[
9
]
constructed a very special infinite family of 3-bridge knots
K_{p,q}
(
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}
) whose Jones polynomial depends only on
p+q
; that is, an infinite family of infinite families each sharing the same Jones polynomial. Further examples are in
[
10
]
. It is still unclear whether coincidences should be considered more the rule than the exception.

In this paper we concentrate on coincidences amongst Jones’ polynomials of rational knots. Since the determinant of a knot is a special value of the Jones polynomial and there are only a finite number of rational knots with given determinant, the number of coincidences is limited and can be checked by direct computation.

In sections 2,3,4 we give an elementary and self-contained account of the evaluation of the Jones polynomial of rational knots as a matrix product (Theorems
4.2
and
4.6
) via the Kauffman bracket vector of
(2,2)
-tangles. Similar matrix formulae were used in
[
4
]
and
[
11
]
for computer computations of the Jones polynomial and other polynomial invariants of rational knots.

By a careful analysis of cancellations of terms in the evaluation of the matrix product, we obtain in section 5 a seemingly new combinatorial formula (Theorem
5.4
) for the Jones polynomial of rational knots in terms of a continued fraction expansion, with significantly fewer terms than other formulae we found in the literature, such as in
[
4
]
.

By analyzing properties of products of the particular family of matrices appearing, we prove in section 6 the invariance of the Jones polynomial of rational knots under a family of moves, Templates I, II (Theorems
6.5
,
6.8
). A third move, a generalization of Template I, is introduced in section 7 (Theorem
7.10
) using the notion of pivoting pairs defined there. On the way a new structural property of Jones polynomials of rational knots is found (Corollary
7.5
). In
[
17
]
it was found that out of 80,317 rational knots with determinant
<900
, there are 223 Jones rational coincidences, three of which are triplets; these are listed in §8. All of these coincidences were accounted for via the moves proved in sections 6,7 and we conjecture that they are indeed generating moves for Jones rational coincidences.

Lemma 3.2
.

Any rational in reduced form

Proof.

Suppose

2m
. Then

|q|
) and even denominator (still

Any rational knot can be written as

5
A combinatorial formula for Jones polynomials of rational knots

In this section we evaluate the matrix product in Theorem
4.6
and carefully investigate cancellations, to arrive at the compact formula for the Jones polynomial of rational knots given in Theorem
5.4
.

Let
K\!=R(2m_{2k},\ldots,2m_{1})^{N}
be a rational knot. Write
[k]=\{1,\ldots,k\}
, not to be confused with the
q

Definition 5.1
.

For
k\in\mathbb{N}
, denote by
{\cal S}_{k}
the set of all subsets
S\subset[k]
(possibly empty) for which

(i)

|S|
has the same parity as
k
;

(ii)

for

S
are listed in ascending order, the parities alternate with the first (smallest) element odd.

For a set

, the complementary set

has an even number of elements, which when written in ascending order will be denoted
a_{1},b_{1},a_{2},b_{2},\ldots
.

Lemma 5.2

{\bf B}_{n_{k}}\ldots{\bf B}_{n_{1}}\begin{pmatrix}1\cr 0\cr\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\sum\limits_{S\in{\cal S}_{k}}i^{k-|S|}\cdot{}u^{\sum_{j}(n_{a_{j}}-n_{b_{j}})}\prod\limits_{r\in{}S}[n_{r}]\cr\sum\limits_{S\in{\cal S}_{k-1}}i^{k-|S|}\cdot{}u^{n_{k}+\sum_{j}(n_{a_{j}}-n_{b_{j}})}\prod\limits_{r\in{}S}[n_{r}]\cr\end{pmatrix}

Proof.

We expand the matrix product by definition as a sum over all intermediate indices of products of matrix entries. Thus the upper entry of the matrix product in the lemma is

\sum\limits_{u_{1},\ldots,u_{k-1}\in\{1,2\}}({\bf B}_{n_{k}})_{u_{k},u_{k-1}}\ldots({\bf B}_{n_{1}})_{u_{1},u_{0}}

[formula]

where the indices
u_{i}
are extended so that
u_{0}=u_{k}=1
. Similarly the lower entry is obtained from the same sum but where the indices are extended so that
u_{0}=1
,
u_{k}=2
.

Recall that
({\bf B}_{n})_{11}=[n]
,
({\bf B}_{n})_{12}=iu^{-n}
,
({\bf B}_{n})_{21}=iu^{n}
while
({\bf B}_{n})_{22}=0
. Sequences

making non-zero contributions to (*) will therefore be ones in which all adjacent pairs are 11, 12 or 21; that is they consist of isolated 2’s separated by strings of 1’s. Such sequences starting and beginning with 1 are in bijection with elements

where

is the set of

for which
u_{j-1}=u_{j}=1
. Then the complementary set
[k]\backslash{}S
when written in ascending order will have form
a_{1},b_{1},a_{2},b_{2},\ldots
where the

denote the positions (index values) of the isolated 2’s in the sequence
u_{i}
and
b_{j}=a_{j}+1
. The matrix element product in (*) corresponding to this particular sequence

is the summand in the upper element given in the lemma.

Similarly for the lower entry in the matrix product, where now

. Removing this last element, relevant sequences
(u_{j})
are seen to be in bijection with
{\cal S}_{k-1}
.∎

6
Templates generating Jones coincidences

In this section we use Theorem

Lemma 6.1

If

and

are two knots whose Jones polynomials differ by at most a sign and multiplication by a power of

Proof.

Since
V_{K}(1)=1
, thus there can be no sign difference.
Suppose otherwise that
V_{K}\not\equiv{}V_{K^{\prime}}
; without loss of generality, assume that

with

. By Proposition 12.5 in

is divisible by

. Hence

is divisible by
(t-1)(t^{3}-1)
. Dividing through by

we see that
(1+t+\cdots+t^{n-1})V_{K}(t)
is divisible by

which contradicts the fact that its evaluation at

is
n
.
∎

Combining with Theorem

, this means that for identification of Jones polynomials of two rational knots, it is sufficient to prove that the corresponding matrix product agrees (even up to a sign and power of

∎

Definition 6.4
.

Suppose that
{\bf n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}
is an integer sequence. Denote by
-{\bf n}
and
{\overset{\leftarrow}{{\bf n}}}
respectively, the sequences obtained from
{\bf n}
by reversing all the signs and by reversing order of the sequence, respectively. Define
{\bf n}^{*}=-\overset{\leftarrow}{{\bf n}}
.

Theorem 6.5

(
Template I).

If two rational knots have integer sequence presentations related by the move

({\bf n}^{\epsilon_{0}},d_{1},{\bf n}^{\epsilon_{1}},\ldots,d_{k},{\bf n}^{\epsilon_{k}})\longrightarrow({\bf n}^{\epsilon_{k}},d_{k},{\bf n}^{\epsilon_{k-1}},\ldots,d_{1},{\bf n}^{\epsilon_{0}})

,

{\bf n}
,

Proof.

Let
{\bf C}={\bf B}_{n_{1}}\cdots{\bf B}_{n_{r}}
where
{\bf n}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{r})
.
By Lemma
6.2
(i),
{\bf C}^{*}=(-1)^{r}{\bf B}_{-n_{r}}\cdots{\bf B}_{-n_{1}}
. The theorem follows from the Key Lemma (2) with

by Lemma
6.2
(ii).
∎

Theorem 6.8

(
Template II).

If two rational knots have integer sequence presentations related by the move

(\overset{\epsilon_{0}}{{\bf n}},m_{0},-\overset{\phi_{0}}{{\bf n}},d_{1},\ldots,d_{k},\overset{\epsilon_{k}}{{\bf n}},m_{k},-\overset{\phi_{k}}{{\bf n}})\longrightarrow(\overset{\epsilon_{0}}{{\bf n}^{*}},m_{0},-\overset{\phi_{0}}{{\bf n}^{*}},d_{1},\ldots,d_{k},\overset{\epsilon_{k}}{{\bf n}^{*}},m_{k},-\overset{\phi_{k}}{{\bf n}^{*}})

m_{0},\ldots,{}m_{k},d_{1},\ldots,d_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\epsilon_{0},\ldots,\epsilon_{k},\phi_{0},\ldots,\phi_{k}\in\{\leftarrow,\rightarrow\}

,

The template follows from the Key Lemma (1) where
{\bf U}_{j}
and
{\bf V}_{j}
are the products of
{\bf B}_{n}
matrices associated with the integer sequences
(\overset{\epsilon_{j}}{{\bf n}},m_{j},-\overset{\phi_{j}}{{\bf n}})

(1). For the first one, either

\begin{pmatrix}1&d\end{pmatrix}{\bf C}{\bf B}_{m}{\bf C}^{-}\begin{pmatrix}1\cr 0\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}1&d\end{pmatrix}{\bf C}^{*}{\bf B}_{m}\overset{\leftarrow}{{\bf C}}\begin{pmatrix}1\cr 0\end{pmatrix}

(v), since the sequences

\epsilon_{j}

\begin{pmatrix}1&d\end{pmatrix}{\bf C}{\bf B}_{m}{\bf C}^{*}\begin{pmatrix}1\cr 0\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}1&d\end{pmatrix}{\bf C}^{*}{\bf B}_{m}{\bf C}\begin{pmatrix}1\cr 0\end{pmatrix}

6.5

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}1&d\end{pmatrix}{\bf B}(\overset{\epsilon}{{\bf n}}){\bf B}_{m}{\bf B}(-\overset{\phi}{{\bf n}})\begin{pmatrix}1\cr-d\end{pmatrix}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}1&0\end{pmatrix}{\bf B}(\overset{\epsilon^{\prime}}{{\bf n}}){\bf B}_{k}{\bf B}(-\overset{\phi^{\prime}}{{\bf n}})\begin{pmatrix}1\cr 0\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}1&d\end{pmatrix}{\bf B}(\overset{\epsilon}{{\bf n}^{*}}){\bf B}_{m}{\bf B}(-\overset{\phi}{{\bf n}^{*}})\begin{pmatrix}1\cr-d\end{pmatrix}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}1&0\end{pmatrix}{\bf B}(\overset{\epsilon^{\prime}}{{\bf n}^{*}}){\bf B}_{k}{\bf B}(-\overset{\phi^{\prime}}{{\bf n}^{*}})\begin{pmatrix}1\cr 0\end{pmatrix}

Remark 7.4
.

Suppose that
{\bf C}
is a product of matrices
{\bf B}_{n}
. Since
\det{\bf B}_{n}=1
thus
\det{\bf C}=1
. Recall that
\overset{\leftarrow}{{\bf C}}={\bf C}^{T}_{u\rightarrow{}u^{-1}}
and so by
Lemma
6.2
(v),(vi),

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\begin{pmatrix}1&d\end{pmatrix}{\bf C}\begin{pmatrix}1\cr 0\end{pmatrix}&=&\begin{pmatrix}1&d\end{pmatrix}{\bf C}^{T}_{u\rightarrow{}u^{-1}}\begin{pmatrix}1\cr 0\end{pmatrix}\cr\begin{pmatrix}1&d\end{pmatrix}{\bf C}\begin{pmatrix}1\cr-d\end{pmatrix}&=&(1-d^{2})\cdot\begin{pmatrix}1&0\end{pmatrix}{\bf C}^{T}_{u\rightarrow{}u^{-1}}\begin{pmatrix}1\cr 0\end{pmatrix}\end{array}

(9)

Matrices
{\bf C}=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&i\beta\cr{}i\gamma&\delta\end{pmatrix}
satisfying (
9
) have

\alpha+id\gamma=\alpha^{\prime}+id\beta^{\prime},\quad\alpha-id\beta+id\gamma-d^{2}\delta=(1-d^{2})\alpha^{\prime}

where the prime denotes the image under
u\rightarrow{}u^{-1}
. It follows that such a matrix is determined by the entries in the first row,
\gamma=\beta^{\prime}+\frac{\alpha^{\prime}-\alpha}{u-u^{-1}}
and
\delta=\alpha^{\prime}+\frac{\beta-\beta^{\prime}}{u-u^{-1}}
.

{\bf C}=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&i\beta\cr i\beta^{\prime}+i\frac{\alpha^{\prime}-\alpha}{u-u^{-1}}&\alpha^{\prime}+\frac{\beta-\beta^{\prime}}{u-u^{-1}}\end{pmatrix}\hbox{ with }\alpha\alpha^{\prime}+\beta\beta^{\prime}+\frac{\alpha^{\prime}\beta-\alpha\beta^{\prime}}{u-u^{-1}}=1

(10)

Matrices of the form (
10
) where
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Z}[u,u^{-1}]
form a group,
{\cal{C}}
, containing as a subgroup
{\cal{B}}
, the set of those matrices which can be written as a product of
{\bf B}_{n}
matrices.
Such matrices which arise as product of
{\bf B}_{n}
matrices will have either
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}[t,t^{-1}]
,
\beta\in{}u\mathbb{Z}[t,t^{-1}]
or
\alpha\in{}u\mathbb{Z}[t,t^{-1}]
,
\beta\in\mathbb{Z}[t,t^{-1}]
, according as the number of even indices (
n
even) in the product is even or odd, respectively. This follows from the same fact about individual
{\bf B}_{n}
matrices and its invariance under matrix product.
Observe that the condition on
\alpha,\beta
in (
10
) can be reformulated as

(\alpha+(u^{-1}-u)\beta)(\alpha^{\prime}+(u-u^{-1})\beta^{\prime})=(u^{2}-1+u^{-2})\alpha\alpha^{\prime}-(u-u^{-1})^{2}

while the second factor on the left hand side is, by Theorem
4.2
, the Jones polynomial of the associated rational knot, up to a sign and power of
u
. Recalling that
t=-u^{-2}
, we arrive at the following corollary, which seems to be new.

Corollary 7.5
.

The Jones polynomial of a rational knot
K
satisfies

V_{K}(t)V_{K}(t^{-1})=(2+t+t^{-1})-(1+t+t^{-1})a(t)a(t^{-1})

where
a(t)\in\mathbb{Z}[t,t^{-1}]
.

Denote by
{\cal{C}}_{0}
those elements of
{\cal{C}}
for which
\alpha=0
.

Lemma 7.6
.

{\cal{C}}_{0}=\{\pm{\bf B}_{0}{\bf B}_{n}{\bf B}_{0}|n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
.

Proof.

In the case of
\alpha=0
, the condition in (
10
) reduces to
\beta\beta^{\prime}=1
. Since
\beta\in\mathbb{Z}[u,u^{-1}]
, thus
\beta=\pm{}u^{n}
for some
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. The matrix
{\bf C}
is now determined by (
10
) to be

{\bf C}=\begin{pmatrix}0&i\beta\cr i\beta^{\prime}&\frac{\beta^{\prime}-\beta}{u-u^{-1}}\end{pmatrix}=\pm\begin{pmatrix}0&iu^{n}\cr iu^{-n}&-[n]\end{pmatrix}=\mp{\bf B}_{0}{\bf B}_{n}{\bf B}_{0}
∎

Definition 7.7
.

A pair of matrices
{\bf C}_{1},{\bf C}_{2}\in{\cal{B}}
are called a pivoting pair if they satisfy

\begin{pmatrix}1&d\end{pmatrix}{\bf C}_{1}\begin{pmatrix}1\cr-d\end{pmatrix}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}1&0\end{pmatrix}{\bf C}_{2}\begin{pmatrix}1\cr 0\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}1&d\end{pmatrix}{\bf C}_{2}\begin{pmatrix}1\cr-d\end{pmatrix}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}1&0\end{pmatrix}{\bf C}_{1}\begin{pmatrix}1\cr 0\end{pmatrix}

Equivalently, by (
9
),
\alpha_{1}^{\prime}\alpha_{2}=\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}^{\prime}
.

It follows immediately from the definition that,

(i)

for any
{\bf C}_{0}\in{\cal{C}}_{0}
and
{\bf C}\in{\cal{B}}
, the pair
{\bf C}_{0},{\bf C}
is a pivoting pair;

(ii)

for pairs of matrices in
{\cal{B}}\backslash{\cal{C}}_{0}
, being a pivoting pair is an equivalence relation, namely it states that
\frac{\alpha_{1}}{\alpha_{1}^{\prime}}=\frac{\alpha_{2}}{\alpha_{2}^{\prime}}
. We denote the equivalence relation on the corresponding integer sequences by
\sim
.

Example 7.8
.

The matrices
{\bf B}_{n}\in{\cal{B}}
all lie in the same equivalence class since
\alpha=[n]=\alpha^{\prime}
. Similarly, any product of
{\bf B}_{n}
matrices corresponding to a palindromic integer sequence will lie in this same equivalence class.

Example 7.9
.

For any
{\bf C}\in{\cal{B}}
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, the pair
{\bf C},{\bf C}{\bf B}_{n}{\bf C}
is a pivoting pair. This follows from the fact that the
(1,1)
matrix entry of
{\bf C}_{2}\equiv{\bf C}{\bf B}_{n}{\bf C}
is

\alpha_{2}=\alpha\left(\frac{u^{n}\alpha-u^{-n}\alpha^{\prime}}{u-u^{-1}}-u^{n}\beta-u^{-n}\beta^{\prime}\right)

where the factor in brackets is invariant under
u\rightarrow{}u^{-1}
. Thus all equivalence classes are infinite.

(

({\bf n}_{0}^{\epsilon_{0}},d_{1},{\bf n}_{1}^{\epsilon_{1}},\ldots,d_{k},{\bf n}_{k}^{\epsilon_{k}})\longrightarrow({\bf n}_{k}^{\epsilon_{k}},d_{k},{\bf n}_{k-1}^{\epsilon_{k-1}},\ldots,d_{1},{\bf n}_{1}^{\epsilon_{0}})

d_{1},\ldots,d_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\epsilon_{0},\ldots,\epsilon_{k}\in\{1,*\}
and integer sequences
{\bf n}_{0}\sim{\bf n}_{1}\sim\ldots\sim{\bf n}_{k}

The proof is as for Theorem
6.5

6.3

6.2
(ii).
∎

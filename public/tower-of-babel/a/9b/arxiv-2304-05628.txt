Bounding the Lagrangian Hofer metric via barcodes Thanks: The author was partially supported by the Swiss National Science Foundation (grant number 200021_204107)
Patricia Dietzsch

Abstract
. We provide an upper bound on the Lagrangian Hofer distance between equators in the cylinder in terms of the barcode of persistent Floer homology. The bound consists of a weighted sum of the lengths of the finite bars and the spectral distance.



1.
Introduction and main results

Let
(M,\omega=-\mathrm{d}\lambda)
be an exact symplectic manifold.
Consider the group
\mathrm{Ham}(M)
of compactly supported Hamiltonian diffeomorphisms.
Any compactly supported Hamiltonian function
H\in C^{\infty}([0,1]\times M)

generates a Hamiltonian flow
\{\phi_{t}^{H}\}
.
The Hofer norm of a Hamiltonian diffeomorphism
\phi\in\mathrm{Ham}(M)

is given by

||\phi||_{H}=\inf\left\{\int_{0}^{1}\max_{x\in M}H_{t}(x)-\min_{x\in M}H_{t}(x)\,\mathrm{d}t\,\big|\,\phi_{1}^{H}=\phi\right\}.

Let
L
and
L^{\prime}
be closed connected Lagrangian submanifolds in
M
that are Hamiltonian isotopic.
The Lagrangian Hofer distance between
L
and
L^{\prime}
is defined by

d_{H}(L,L^{\prime})=\inf\left\{||\phi||_{H}\,\big|\,\phi(L)=L^{\prime}\right\}.

For transversely intersecting and exact Lagrangians
L
and
L^{\prime}
we consider the Floer complex
\mathrm{CF}(L,L^{\prime})
over
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.
A choice of primitives of the exact
1
-forms
\lambda|_{L}
and
\lambda|_{L^{\prime}}

gives rise to an action functional
\mathcal{A}
that induces a filtration on the Floer complex
\mathrm{CF}(L,L^{\prime})
. Therefore, the homology group
\mathrm{HF}(L,L^{\prime})

of
\mathrm{CF}(L,L^{\prime})
becomes a persistence module

\mathrm{HF}^{\leq\bullet}(L,L^{\prime})
. The barcode
\mathcal{B}(L,L^{\prime})
associated with it gives rise to a number of invariants for the pair
(L,L^{\prime})
.
One of them is the Lagrangian spectral metric
\gamma(L,L^{\prime})
, which is the largest distance between two infinite bars in
\mathcal{B}(L,L^{\prime})

[
Vit92
,
KS21
]
. The boundary depth
\beta_{1}(L,L^{\prime})
, studied
in
[
Ush13
]
, is the length of the longest finite bar.
In this paper, we also consider the lengths of the other finite bars.
We denote by

\beta_{1}(L,L^{\prime})\geq\beta_{2}(L,L^{\prime})\geq\dots\geq\beta_{k}(L,L^{\prime})

the lengths of the finite bars ordered by their size.
While the barcode
\mathcal{B}(L,L^{\prime})
actually depends on the choice of primitives
of
\lambda|_{L}
and
\lambda|_{L^{\prime}}
,
the numbers
\gamma(L,L^{\prime})
and
\beta_{i}(L,L^{\prime})
,
i\in\{1,\dots,k\}
, are independent of it.
Kislev-Shelukhin
[
KS21
]
proved the following inequalities

(1)

\displaystyle\beta_{1}(L,L^{\prime})\leq\gamma(L,L^{\prime})\leq d_{H}(L,L^{\prime}).

In this paper we prove a converse inequality for equators in the cylinder.
From now on, we work in
\Sigma:=S^{1}\times(-1,1)
. In local coordinates
(q,p)
, the standard symplectic form on
\Sigma
is
\omega=\mathrm{d}q\wedge\mathrm{d}p=-\mathrm{d}\lambda
for

\lambda=p\mathrm{d}q
. Let
L_{0}=S^{1}\times\{0\}\subset\Sigma
denote the zero-section. We are interested into the set
\mathcal{L}(L_{0})
of all Lagrangians
L\subset\Sigma
which are Hamiltonian isotopic to
L_{0}
.

Our main result is:

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
L,L^{\prime}\in\mathcal{L}(L_{0})
intersect transversely in
2n
points. Then

d_{H}(L,L^{\prime})\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}2^{j}\beta_{j}(L,L^{\prime})+\gamma(L,L^{\prime}).

Using the inequalities (
1
) we get the following bound:

Corollary 1.2
.

For any
L,L^{\prime}
as above

\gamma(L,L^{\prime})\leq d_{H}(L,L^{\prime})\leq 2^{n}\gamma(L,L^{\prime}).

1.2.
Outline of proof

We explain the strategy to prove Theorem
1.1
.
Let
L,L^{\prime}\in\mathcal{L}(L_{0})
be two transverse Lagrangians that intersect in

2n

The action spectrum
\{\mathcal{A}(q)|q\in L\cap L^{\prime}\}
coincides
with the endpoints of the bars in
\mathcal{B}(L,L^{\prime})
. Therefore, the barcode
\mathcal{B}(L,L^{\prime})
consists of
n-1
finite bars and two infinite bars.
We prove the Theorem by induction on the number of intersection points.

Base case:
If there are only two intersection points, say
q
and
p
, then Hofer’s distance is equal to the area of one of the Floer strips connecting
q
and
p
. This is also the difference between the action values of the two infinite bars in
\mathcal{B}(L,L^{\prime})

Filtration

Any

is exact, meaning that the

\lambda|_{L}\in\Omega^{1}(L)
is exact. We call a function

of

h_{L}
is unique up to an additive constant because

is connected.
The filtration on
\mathrm{CF}(L,L^{\prime})
we introduce below will depend on a choice
of markings of
L
and
L^{\prime}
.

Fix two markings
h_{L}
and
h_{L^{\prime}}
of
L
and
L^{\prime}
respectively.
Consider the space of paths from

L^{\prime}

\Omega_{L,L^{\prime}}:=\{\gamma\in C^{\infty}([0,1])\,|\,\gamma(0)\in L,\gamma(1)\in L^{\prime}\}.

We identify tangent vectors
\xi

with vector fields
\xi(t)\in T_{\gamma(t)}\Sigma
along
\gamma

The action functional
\mathcal{A}\colon\Omega_{L,L^{\prime}}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
is defined by

\mathcal{A}(\gamma)=h_{L}(\gamma(1))-h_{L^{\prime}}(\gamma(0))-\int_{0}^{1}\lambda_{\gamma(t)}(\dot{\gamma}(t))\,\mathrm{d}t.

The exterior derivative of
\mathcal{A}

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}(\xi)=\int_{0}^{1}\omega(\dot{\gamma}(t),\xi(t))\,\mathrm{d}t

for any
\xi\in T_{\gamma}\Omega_{L,L^{\prime}}
.

2.3.
Persistent Floer homology and its barcode

For an overview of the theory of persistent homology and its use in symplectic topology see

[
PRSZ20
]
. We follow closely chapters 1 and 2 from

[
PRSZ20
]
.

4
4

4

The definitions here differ slightly from those in
[
PRSZ20
]
in the convention for semicontinuity.
Since we consider
\mathrm{CF}^{\leq\alpha}
and not
\mathrm{CF}^{<\alpha}
we need to work
with intervals of the form
[a,b)
and not
(a,b]
.

A
persistence module
over
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
consists of an
\mathbb{R}
-indexed
family of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-vector spaces
\{V_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
and
linear maps
f_{s,t}\colon V_{s}\longrightarrow V_{t}
for any
s\leq t
satisfying

(1)

f_{t,t}=\mathrm{id}
for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

(2)

f_{s,t}\circ f_{r,s}=f_{r,t}
for any
r\leq s\leq t
.

Any filtered chain complex gives rise to a
persistence module by taking homology of the subcomplexes.
In our case, we consider the family

\left\{\mathrm{HF}^{\leq\alpha}((L,h_{L}),(L^{\prime},h_{L^{\prime}}))\right\}_{\alpha\in\mathbb{R}}=\left\{\mathrm{H}_{*}\left(\mathrm{CF}^{\leq\alpha}((L,h_{L}),(L^{\prime},h_{L^{\prime}}))\right)\right\}_{\alpha\in\mathbb{R}}

of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-vector spaces
together with the maps

i_{\alpha,\beta}\colon\mathrm{HF}^{\leq\alpha}((L,h_{L}),(L^{\prime},h_{L^{\prime}}))\longrightarrow\mathrm{HF}^{\leq\beta}((L,h_{L}),(L^{\prime},h_{L^{\prime}}))

induced by inclusion for any
\alpha\leq\beta
.
We refer to this persistence module as

\mathrm{HF}^{\leq\bullet}((L,h_{L}),(L^{\prime},h_{L^{\prime}})).

It is a persistence module of finite type, in the following sense.
A persistence module
V
is
of finite type
if

(1)

For any
t\in\mathbb{R}
, there exists
\epsilon>0
such that

f_{s,t}
is an isomorphism for any

s\in[t,t+\epsilon)
.

(2)

There exist
t_{1},\dots,t_{m}\in\mathbb{R}
such that for all
t\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{t_{1},\dots,t_{m}\}
, there exists
\epsilon>0
such that

f_{s,r}
is an isomorphism for any
s\leq r\in(t-\epsilon,t+\epsilon)
.

(3)

There exists
s_{-}
such that
V_{s}=0
for any
s<s_{-}
.

Isomorphism classes of persistence modules of finite type can be classified by their barcodes.
A
barcode of finite type

\mathcal{B}=\{I_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
is a finite multiset of intervals
I_{j}
of two possible types:

(1)

Finite bars:
I_{j}=[a_{j},b_{j})
, where
a_{j}<b_{j}
are real numbers.

(2)

Infinite bars:
I_{j}=[c_{j},\infty)
, where
c_{j}
is a real number.

A bar
[a,b)
corresponds to the persistence module
V^{[a,b)}
defined by

\displaystyle V^{[a,b)}_{t}=\begin{cases}\mathbb{Z}_{2}\qquad\text{ if }t\in[a,b),\\
0\qquad\quad\text{else},\end{cases}

with internal maps
f_{s,t}\colon V_{s}^{[a,b)}\longrightarrow V_{t}^{[a,b)}
being the identity for
a\leq s\leq t<b
and
0
for any other pair
s\leq t
.

Theorem 2.4

(Structure Theorem,
[
ZC05
]
)
.

For a persistence module

This is a bijection

.

Let

be the two infinite bars of

.

and

and

.

.

be the permutation that

Then

where the first inequality comes from the fact, that the

of a sequence of
numbers is the monotone one (

).
It follows that

\displaystyle a(v)+\epsilon\geq|\beta_{k}-\alpha_{k}^{\prime}|\geq|\beta_{k}-\alpha_{k}^{\prime}|\geq\beta_{k}-\alpha_{k}^{\prime}\geq\beta_{k}-\beta_{k}^{\prime}.

By symmetry we conclude that
|\beta_{k}-\beta_{k}^{\prime}|\leq a(v)+\epsilon
.
This shows how Proposition
3.5
implies the inequalities
(

) and (

).

.

We only show it for Case 1. The other case is similar.

4.
The shortest bar.

The goal of this section is to show Proposition
1.5
.
As in the previous section, we only consider the pair
(L_{0},L)

for
L\in\mathcal{L}_{0}
. We assume that
L_{0}
and
L
intersect in at least

2n\geq 4
points. Moreover, the action values
\mathcal{A}(q)
,

q\in L_{0}\cap L
, are assumed to be distinct.
Let
[a,b)
be the shortest (finite) bar in
\mathcal{B}(L_{0},L)
and let
\bar{q},\bar{p}\in L_{0}\cap L
be the intersection points satisfying
\mathcal{A}(\bar{q})=b
and
\mathcal{A}(\bar{p})=a
.
The goal is to show that there is a leaf from
\bar{q}
to
\bar{p}
.
The proof is based on the following two lemmas whose proofs will be given shortly after.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
x,y\in L_{0}\cap L
such that
n(x,y)=1
.
Then
\mathcal{A}(x)-\mathcal{A}(y)\geq b-a.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
u\colon\mathbb{D}\longrightarrow\Sigma

be a smooth lune for
(L_{0},L)
.
Then there exists a leaf
v\colon\mathbb{D}\longrightarrow\Sigma

such that
\mathrm{Im}(v)\subseteq\mathrm{Im}(u)
.

Let
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathrm{CF}(L_{0},L)
be a Jordan basis for
\partial

(see the definition in section
2.3
).
We explain how Proposition
1.5
follows assuming the previous two lemmas.

Proof of Proposition
1.5
.

As a first step towards the proof, we show that
n(\bar{q},\bar{p})=1
:
Let

e=\bar{q}+\sum_{\mathcal{A}(q)<\mathcal{A}(\bar{q})}n_{q}q\in\mathcal{S}

be the basis element with
\mathcal{A}(e)=b
and

f=\bar{p}+\sum_{\mathcal{A}(p)<\mathcal{A}(\bar{p})}m_{p}p\in\mathcal{S}

be the basis element with
\mathcal{A}(f)=a
.
Since
\partial e=f
, there exists
q^{\prime}\in L_{0}\cap L
with
\mathcal{A}(q^{\prime})\leq\mathcal{A}(\bar{q})=b
such that
n(q^{\prime},\bar{p})=1
. By Lemma
4.1
it follows that
\mathcal{A}(q^{\prime})=\mathcal{A}(\bar{q})
. Under the assumption that all the action values are distinct, we conclude that
\bar{q}=q^{\prime}
, hence
n(\bar{q},\bar{p})=n(q^{\prime},\bar{p})=1
.
In particular, there exists a unique smooth lune
u_{\bar{q},\bar{p}}
from
\bar{q}
to
\bar{p}
.

Suppose by contradiction that the smooth lune
u_{\bar{q},\bar{p}}
is not a leaf. By Lemma
4.2
, there exists a leaf, say from
x
to
y
, whose image is strictly contained in
\mathrm{Im}(u_{\bar{q},\bar{p}})
. If
n(x,y)=1
, then the inequality

\mathcal{A}(x)-\mathcal{A}(y)<\mathcal{A}(\bar{q})-\mathcal{A}(\bar{p})=b-a

is a contradiction to Lemma
4.1
. If
n(x,y)=0
, then there are two smooth lunes from

to
y
, one of them being a leaf.
It follows from Lemma

that the only possibility for such a situation is when

. This is not the case here because there are at least
4

We conclude that

is a leaf from
\bar{q}
to
\bar{p}
.
∎

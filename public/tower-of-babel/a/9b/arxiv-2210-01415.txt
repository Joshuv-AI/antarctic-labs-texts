Late-time correlation functions in dS
3

Abstract
We compute the late-time correlation functions on three-dimensional de Sitter spacetime for a higher-spin gravity theory. For this, we elaborate on the formulation to obtain the wave functional of universe from a dual conformal field theory, which is used to compute the late-time correlation functions. We argue that the relation to direct bulk Feynman diagram computations in the in-in formulation. We furthermore provide a precise prescription to construct a higher-spin dS 3 holography as an analytic continuation of Gaberdiel-Gopakumar duality for AdS 3 . Part of results here were already reported in an earlier letter. We explain the details of their derivations and extend the analysis to more generic cases in this paper. Previously, we have examined two- and three-point functions and a simple four-point correlator at the leading order in Newton constant. Here we also evaluate more complicated four-point correlators. Finally, we study late-time correlators in an alternative limit of dS 3 /CFT 2 with critical level coset, such as, two-point correlator on conical defect geometry. We also examine one-loop corrections to two-point correlator on dS 3 .

Introduction and summary

Our universe experienced an inflationary era at the early time, during which its geometry can be approximated by de Sitter (dS) spacetime. In order to understand the beginning of our universe, it is important to formulate quantum gravity on dS spacetime, which has been understood very little so far. In the case of anti-de Sitter (AdS) spacetime, the situation is quite different mainly due to AdS/CFT correspondence proposed by Maldacena
[

Recently, the late-time correlation functions on three-dimensional dS (dS

In

We see that the two formulations are consistent with each other. As an advantage, our formalism would allow us to compute non-trivial correlators, which are difficult to compute directly from the bulk theory due to, e.g., the lack of knowledge of quantum aspects on the (higher-spin) gravity theory.

General strategy

The main purpose of this work is to compute correlation functions of higher-spin gravity on dS
3
at late time from dual CFT
2
. For this, we utilize the wave functional prescription in
[
6
]
, which will be explained to some details in this section.
Before focusing on an explicit setting, we consider a bulk theory defined on
(d+1)
-dimensional dS spacetime and including massive scalar fields and (massive) symmetric tensor fields in order to make argument as generic as possible.
We assume that dual CFT exists as well.

2.1
Analytic continuation from AdS
d+1
to dS
d+1

We start from the physical quantities on Euclidean AdS space and then discuss the map to those on Lorentzian dS spacetime.
In the bulk theory on Euclidean AdS
d+1
, we include at least one scalar field
\phi^{\text{AdS}}
and symmetric tensor fields
\sigma_{i_{1}\cdots i_{s}}^{\text{AdS}}
.
We denote the dual scalar operator by
\mathcal{O}_{\text{AdS}}
and the dual symmetric traceless tensor by
J^{i_{1}\cdots i_{s}}_{\text{AdS}}
. We denote the mass of a bulk field as
m
, then the conformal dimension of dual operator is
\Delta=\Delta_{\pm}
depending on the boundary condition assigned to the given bulk field.
Here
\Delta_{\pm}
satisfy

\displaystyle\ell_{\text{AdS}}^{2}\,m^{2}=-(\Delta_{+}\Delta_{-}+s)\,,\qquad\Delta_{-}=d-\Delta_{+}

(2.1)

with the AdS radius
\ell_{\text{AdS}}
.
We represent the spin of the field by
s
and set
s=0
for the scalar field.
We will later consider the case where the dual symmetric tensor currents are conserved, and in that case
\Delta_{+}=s+d-2
. For the metric of Euclidean AdS
d+1
, we adopt the Poincaré coordinates as

\displaystyle ds^{2}=\ell_{\text{AdS}}^{2}\,\frac{dy^{2}+d\vec{x}^{2}}{y^{2}}

(2.2)

with
y\geq 0
. The boundary of AdS space is located at
y=0
.
The bulk fields on AdS
d+1
behave near the boundary
y\to+0
as

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \phi^{\text{AdS}}(y,\vec{x})&\sim\phi_{+}^{\text{AdS}}(\vec{x})\,y^{\Delta_{+}}+\phi_{-}^{\text{AdS}}(\vec{x})\,y^{\Delta_{-}}\,,\\
\sigma_{i_{1}\cdots i_{s}}^{\text{AdS}}(y,\vec{x})&\sim\sigma_{i_{1}\cdots i_{s}}^{+,\text{AdS}}(\vec{x})\,y^{\Delta_{+}-s}+\sigma_{i_{1}\cdots i_{s}}^{-,\text{AdS}}(\vec{x})\,y^{\Delta_{-}-s}\ .\end{aligned}

(2.3)

It is convenient to define the
d
-dimensional boundary metric as

\displaystyle ds^{2}=d\vec{x}^{2}\,,

(2.4)

such as to be independent of the AdS radius
\ell_{\text{AdS}}
.
With the boundary metric above, the boundary couplings between the bulk fields and the CFT operators are given by

\displaystyle\ell^{d}_{\text{AdS}}\int d^{d}\vec{x}\,\phi^{\text{AdS}}_{\pm}\,\mathcal{O}^{\pm}_{\text{AdS}}\,,\quad\ell^{d}_{\text{AdS}}\int d^{d}\vec{x}\,\sigma^{\pm,\text{AdS}}_{i_{1}\cdots i_{s}}\,J^{i_{1}\cdots i_{s}}_{\pm,\text{AdS}}\,.

(2.5)

We then consider the bulk theory on Lorentzian dS spacetime including at least a scalar field
\phi
and symmetric tensor fields
\sigma_{i_{1}\cdots i_{s}}
as in the case of Euclidean AdS space. We also denote the dual scalar operator by
\mathcal{O}
and the dual symmetric tensor operators by
J^{i_{1}\cdots i_{s}}
.
The conformal dimension of dual operator is given by:

\displaystyle\ell^{2}\,m^{2}=\Delta_{+}\Delta_{-}+s\,,\qquad\Delta_{-}=d-\Delta_{+}\,,

(2.6)

where
m
corresponds to the mass of bulk field.
We express the Poincaré patch of Lorentzian dS
d+1
with the metric

\displaystyle ds^{2}=\ell^{2}\,\frac{-d\eta^{2}+d\vec{x}^{2}}{\eta^{2}}\,,

(2.7)

where we consider the region with
-\infty<\eta\leq 0
.
The future infinity is located at
\eta\to-0
.
We may relate the dS metric to the AdS one given in (
2.2
) by:

\displaystyle y=i\eta\ ,\qquad\ell_{\text{AdS}}=-i\ell\,.

(2.8)

In the following, we will see how such an analytic continuation acts on bulk fields and CFT operators.

We begin with the simpler case with scalar field and then move to more complicated case with symmetric tensor fields.
Near the future infinity
\eta\to-0
, the bulk scalar field behaves as

\displaystyle\phi(\eta,\vec{x})\sim\phi_{+}(\vec{x})\,(-\eta)^{\Delta_{+}}+\phi_{-}(\vec{x})\,(-\eta)^{\Delta_{-}}\,.

(2.9)

As mentioned in
[
6
]
,
\phi^{\text{AdS}}
may be identified with
\phi
under the map (
2.8
), which leads to

\displaystyle\phi_{\pm}=e^{-i\frac{\pi}{2}\Delta_{\pm}}\,\phi_{\pm}^{\text{AdS}}\,.

(2.10)

In AdS/CFT correspondence, the boundary couplings between bulk fields and CFT operators are given by (
2.5
). On the other hand, in dS/CFT correspondence, we have the boundary coupling as in (
1.5
) instead. The two types of boundary couplings can be identified if we relate the corresponding dual CFT operators as:

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{\pm}=e^{i\frac{\pi}{2}(\Delta_{\pm}-d)}\,\mathcal{O}_{\pm}^{\text{AdS}}\,.

(2.11)

In the case of spin-
s
tensor fields, their behaviors near the future infinity are given by

\displaystyle\sigma_{i_{1}\cdots i_{s}}(\eta,\vec{x})\sim\sigma^{+}_{i_{1}\cdots i_{s}}(\vec{x})\,(-\eta)^{\Delta_{+}-s}+\sigma^{-}_{i_{1}\cdots i_{s}}(\vec{x})\,(-\eta)^{\Delta_{-}-s}\,.

(2.12)

Here notice that the indices of bulk fields are lowered and raised by the bulk metric
g_{\mu\nu}\sim\ell^{2}
and
g^{\mu\nu}\sim\ell^{-2}
. On the other hand, the boundary metric is defined as in (
2.4
), which is independent of
\ell
.
It is thus convenient to define boundary operators, whose indices are raised and lowered by the boundary metric independent of
\ell
, see, e.g.,
[
11
]
for a similar argument.
Therefore, we assign

\displaystyle\ell^{s}\,\sigma^{\pm}_{i_{1}\cdots i_{s}}(\vec{x})=(-i)^{\Delta_{\pm}-s}\,\ell_{\text{AdS}}^{s}\,\sigma^{\pm,\text{AdS}}_{i_{1}\cdots i_{s}}(\vec{x})\,,

(2.13)

which leads to

\displaystyle\sigma^{\pm}_{i_{1}\cdots i_{s}}(\vec{x})=e^{-i\frac{\pi}{2}\Delta_{\pm}}\,\sigma^{\pm,\text{AdS}}_{i_{1}\cdots i_{s}}(\vec{x})\,,\qquad J^{\pm}_{i_{1}\cdots i_{s}}(\vec{x})=e^{i\frac{\pi}{2}(\Delta_{\pm}-d)}\,J^{\pm,\text{AdS}}_{i_{1}\cdots i_{s}}(\vec{x})\,.

(2.14)

For instance, a spin-
s
conserved current have conformal dimension
\Delta_{+}=s+d-2
, and hence symmetric tensor currents are related as

\displaystyle J^{+}_{i_{1}\cdots i_{s}}(\vec{x})=e^{i\frac{\pi}{2}(s-2)}\,J^{+,\text{AdS}}_{i_{1}\cdots i_{s}}(\vec{x})\,.

(2.15)

In particular, the energy momentum tensor with
s=2
does not receive any phase factor and the standard convention of energy momentum tensor is preserved by this prescription.

2.2
Late-time correlators from the wave functional of universe

In the previous subsection, we obtained the phase factors for operators dual to bulk fields by making use of the map from AdS to dS as in (
2.8
). In this subsection, we explain how to compute correlation functions of the bulk fields at late time from these dual operators, see, e.g., appendix A of
[
10
]
as well. As explained in the introduction, we utilize the wave functional of universe to compute the correlation functions on dS at late time. The proposal of
[
6
]
is that the wave functional can be evaluated from a certain dual CFT as in (
1.5
).
In AdS/CFT correspondence, the boundary values of bulk fields are fixed, and they can be regarded as the sources in dual CFT
[
2
,
3
]
.
However, in dS/CFT correspondence, boundary values
\psi_{j}^{0}
at late time
t=t_{\infty}
are not constants but path-integral variables. Therefore, it is natural to expand the wave functional by the boundary fields
\psi^{0}_{j}
as:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \Psi[\psi_{l}^{0}]&=\exp\left[\sum_{m\geq 2}\int d^{d}\vec{x}_{1}\cdots d^{d}\vec{x}_{m}C_{m}(\{\vec{x}_{l}\})\prod_{j=1}^{m}\psi_{j}^{0}(\vec{x}_{j})\right]\,.\end{aligned}

(2.16)

Thus, it is better to state that the coefficient functions
C_{m}(\{\vec{x}_{l}\})
could be evaluated by the dual CFT living on the late boundary of dS.
We will specify the precise relation between
C_{m}(\{\vec{x}_{l}\})
and the
m
-point CFT correlation function in the concrete example considered later.
The correlation functions of
\psi_{j}^{0}
are computed as expectation values as in (
1.6
).

There are a lot of computations of late-time correlation functions of bulk theory such as in
[
6
,
8
,
9
,
10
]
, see also
[
37
]
. They are however analyzed usually in momentum basis. This is because their prime interest is to deduce the information after the inflation ends, where scale invariance is broken and only translation invariance remains, thus the momentum representation is useful. Generically, CFT correlation functions in momentum basis are much more difficult to treat than those in coordinate basis. In order to avoid unnecessary complication, we mainly work in coordinate basis in this paper. If one wishes, the expressions obtained here could be mapped to those in momentum basis by performing Fourier transformations in principle.

3
at late time by applying the method explained above. We will systematically consider the order-by-order expansion of these correlation functions in the Newton constant
G_{N}
(see (

) below for the relation with other parameters).
In this subsection, we concentrate on the leading order contributions. We will discuss the higher order effects in subsection
2.4
.
At the leading non-trivial order in

, bulk correlators can be expressed by CFT correlators as follows.
The two-point function of scalar field is obtained through functional inverse as:

\displaystyle\langle\,\phi(\vec{x}_{1})\,\phi(\vec{x}_{2})\,\rangle=-\frac{1}{2\,\text{Re}\,\langle\,\mathcal{O}_{i}(\vec{x}_{1})\,\mathcal{O}_{i}(\vec{x}_{2})\,\rangle}\,.

For symmetric tensor fields, they are written as

\displaystyle\langle\,\sigma_{i_{1}\cdots i_{s}}(\vec{x}_{1})\,\sigma^{j_{1}\cdots j_{s}}(\vec{x}_{2})\,\rangle=-\frac{\Pi^{j_{1}\cdots j_{s}}_{i_{1}\cdots i_{s}}(\hat{x}_{12})}{2\,\text{Re}\,\langle\,J_{s}(\vec{x}_{1})\,J_{s}(\vec{x}_{2})\,\rangle}\,.

Similarly, the three-point function we will use is

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\langle\,\phi_{1}(\vec{x}_{1})\,\phi_{2}(\vec{x}_{2})\,\sigma_{i_{1}\cdots i_{s}}(\vec{x}_{3})\,\rangle\\

(2.20)

\displaystyle\langle\,\phi_{1}(\vec{x}_{1})\,\phi_{2}(\vec{x}_{2})\,\phi_{3}(\vec{x}_{3})\,\phi_{4}(\vec{x}_{4})\,\rangle=\int\prod_{l=1}^{4}d^{d}\vec{x}_{l}^{\prime}\frac{\langle\mathcal{O}^{4}\rangle_{c}+\langle\mathcal{O}^{4}\rangle_{d}}{\prod_{i=1}^{4}(-2\,\text{Re}\,\langle\,\mathcal{O}_{i}(\vec{x}_{i})\,\mathcal{O}_{i}(\vec{x}_{i}^{\prime})\,\rangle)}\ .

(2.21)

Here we have split the contribution in the following:

\displaystyle\langle\,\mathcal{O}^{4}\,\rangle_{c}

\displaystyle=2\,\text{Re}\,\langle\,\mathcal{O}_{1}(\vec{x}_{1}^{\prime})\,\mathcal{O}_{2}(\vec{x}_{2}^{\prime})\,\mathcal{O}_{3}(\vec{x}_{3}^{\prime})\,\mathcal{O}_{4}(\vec{x}_{4}^{\prime})\,\rangle_{c}\,,

(2.22)

\displaystyle\langle\,\mathcal{O}^{4}\,\rangle_{d}

\displaystyle=\sum\int d^{d}\vec{y}_{1}d^{d}\vec{y}_{2}

(2.23)

\displaystyle\times\frac{2\text{Re}\,\langle\,\mathcal{O}_{1}(\vec{x}_{1}^{\prime})\,\mathcal{O}_{2}(\vec{x}_{2}^{\prime})\,J_{j_{1}\cdots j_{s}}(\vec{y}_{1})\,\rangle\,\Pi^{j_{1}\cdots j_{s}}_{i_{1}\cdots i_{s}}(\vec{y}_{12})\,2\text{Re}\,\langle\,J^{i_{1}\cdots i_{s}}(\vec{y}_{2})\,\mathcal{O}_{3}(\vec{x}_{3}^{\prime})\,\mathcal{O}_{4}(\vec{x}_{4}^{\prime})\,\rangle}{2\text{Re}\,\langle\,J_{s}(\vec{y}_{1})\,J_{s}(\vec{y}_{2})\,\rangle}+\text{perm}\,.

The term
\langle\,\mathcal{O}^{4}\,\rangle_{c}

On the other hand for

It should be noted that only appropriate real combinations appear here as we take the product of the wave functional
\Psi[\psi_{l}^{0}]
and its complex conjugate
\bar{\Psi}[\psi_{l}^{0}]
when extracting bulk correlation functions.

Let us remark here on a subtlety to define the coefficient functions
C_{m}(\{\vec{x_{l}}\})
by an analytic continuation from correlation functions of CFT
d
dual to gravity theory on AdS
d+1
. Here we assume that the CFT has a large
N
structure.
We consider a four-point function of scalar operators

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{4}\equiv\left\langle\mathcal{O}_{1}^{\text{AdS}}(\vec{x}_{1})\mathcal{O}_{2}^{\text{AdS}}(\vec{x}_{2})\mathcal{O}_{3}^{\text{AdS}}(\vec{x}_{3})\mathcal{O}_{4}^{\text{AdS}}(\vec{x}_{4})\right\rangle\,.

(2.24)

Its generalization to other correlation functions is straightforward.
We expand the four-point function by
1/N
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{4}=\mathcal{A}^{(0)}_{4}+\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{4}+\cdots\,.

(2.25)

The leading order contribution comes from the products of two-point functions

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{(0)}_{4}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\mathcal{O}_{1}^{\text{AdS}}(\vec{x}_{1})\mathcal{O}_{2}^{\text{AdS}}(\vec{x}_{2})\right\rangle\left\langle\mathcal{O}_{3}^{\text{AdS}}(\vec{x}_{3})\mathcal{O}_{4}^{\text{AdS}}(\vec{x}_{4})\right\rangle+\text{perm}\,.

(2.26)

This is a kind of definition of large
N
structure. We would like to read off coefficient functions
C_{4}(\{\vec{x}_{l}\})
in (
2.16
) from the computation of four-point function
\mathcal{A}_{4}
. However, the bulk correlators are computed via (
1.6
), and the products of two-point functions can already be obtained from two-point coefficients
C_{2}(\{\vec{x}_{l}\})
in (
2.16
).
Therefore, in order to obtain
C_{4}(\{\vec{x}_{l}\})
from an analytic continuation of
\mathcal{A}_{4}
, the leading order contribution
\mathcal{A}_{N}^{(0)}
should be extracted beforehand. The next leading order can decomposed as

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{4}=\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{4,c}+\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{4,d}\,.

(2.27)

Here we define
\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{4,d}
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{4,d}

\displaystyle=\sum\int d^{d}\vec{y}_{1}d^{d}\vec{y}_{2}

(2.28)

\displaystyle\times\frac{\langle\,\mathcal{O}_{1}^{\text{AdS}}(\vec{x}_{1})\,\mathcal{O}_{2}^{\text{AdS}}(\vec{x}_{2})\,J^{\text{AdS}}_{j_{1}\cdots j_{s}}(\vec{y}_{1})\,\rangle\,\Pi^{j_{1}\cdots j_{s}}_{i_{1}\cdots i_{s}}(\vec{y}_{12})\langle\,J^{i_{1}\cdots i_{s}}_{\text{AdS}}(\vec{y}_{2})\,\mathcal{O}_{3}^{\text{AdS}}(\vec{x}_{3})\,\mathcal{O}_{4}^{\text{AdS}}(\vec{x}_{4})\,\rangle}{\langle\,J_{s}^{\text{AdS}}(\vec{y}_{1})\,J_{s}^{\text{AdS}}(\vec{y}_{2})\,\rangle}+\text{perm.}\,,

where the sum is over all intermediate operators. As expressed in (
2.23
),
\langle\,\mathcal{O}^{4}\,\rangle_{d}
can be computed only with two- and three-point coefficient functions. Therefore, the four-point coefficient functions
C_{4}(\{\vec{x}_{l}\})
should be obtained by an analytic continuation of the rest
\mathcal{A}_{4,c}^{(1)}
.

:
Bulk interpretation for the decompositions of four-point functions.

From the bulk viewpoints, the above procedure may be explained as follows. As seen in fig.
1
, the four-point function can be computed by the sum of Witten diagrams. A type of diagrams consists of the product of two bulk propagators, which correspond to the product of two-point functions. Another type consists of the exchange diagrams of spin-
s
fields, which roughly correspond to the conformal partial waves of spin-
s
current exchange. The rest is the four-point contact diagram, which basically corresponds to the term we just extracted. The precise relation can be read off from the decomposition of conformal partial waves for bulk exchange diagrams as explained in appendix
B.1
.

2.3
Relation to the in-in formulation

In the previous subsection, we explain our method to utilize the wave functional of universe for evaluating correlation functions on bulk dS at late time from dual CFT.
Recently, there are developments on a different approach to obtain correlation functions by making use of bulk Feynman diagram computations
[
22
,
23
]
, see also
[
24
]
. In their analysis, they compute bulk correlation functions in the in-in formulation, whose good review may be found in
[
38
]
.
They put an identical theory on AdS and dS and compare the contributions coming from corresponding Feynman diagrams. In particular, they concluded that only a phase factor should be associated with each contribution along with the analytic continuation.

In this subsection, we compare the two formulations in order to clarify their similarity and difference.
In the in-in formulation, we compute correlation function by

\displaystyle\langle\Omega(t_{0})|\,\bar{T}\left(e^{i\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{\infty}}\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}H(t^{\prime})}\right)\psi_{1}^{0}(x_{1})\cdots\psi_{m}^{0}(x_{n})\,T\left(e^{-i\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{\infty}}\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}H(t^{\prime})}\right)\,|\Omega(t_{0})\rangle\,.

(2.29)

Here
T
and
\bar{T}
denote time and anti-time orderings. Moreover, correlators are among bulk fields at the late time
t=t_{\infty}
and the Bunch-Davies boundary condition is assigned at the early time
t=t_{0}
. The vacuum corresponding to the boundary condition is denoted as
\Omega(t_{0})
.
Note that there are two types of interactions corresponding to time and anti-time orderings.
On the other hand, the formalism adopted in this paper utilize the wave functional of universe as explained in subsection
2.2
.
The point here is that we have to sum over the both contributions from the coefficient functions of
\Psi[\psi_{l}^{0}]
and
\bar{\Psi}[\psi_{l}^{0}]
.

We begin with three-point function at the tree level.
In this case, the correspondence between the two formalisms is rather clear. In the in-in formulation, there are only two types of contact interactions, where one comes in the anti-time ordered insertions and the other comes in the time ordered insertions, see fig.
2
.

indicates the (anti-)time ordering of inserted interaction terms in the Hamiltonian.

As in
[
23
]
, we put

or

in order to distinguish them. In the formulation with wave functional, there are two types of contributions from
\Psi
and
\bar{\Psi}
as mentioned above. In other words, a diagram in fig.

corresponds to
C_{3}(\{\vec{x}\})
, and the other corresponds to the complex conjugate of
C_{3}(\{\vec{x}\})
. Precisely speaking, in order to match with the scalar correlators on dS at late time, we have to multiply the inverse of two-point functions, see subsection

.

Interesting phenomena arise from four-point functions; here we only consider four-point functions of external scalar operators and ignore the contributions coming from the products of two-point functions.
The sums of Feynman diagrams in the first line of fig.

2.4
Higher order contributions in
G_{N}

So far, we have restricted ourselves to the leading order contributions in the Newton constant
G_{N}
to bulk correlation functions. However, the holographic formulation with the wave functional of universe can be applied to higher order contributions as well. Note that, as in the case of four-point functions, careful treatments have to be performed, which is explained in this section with the example of one-loop corrections to two-point bulk correlator. In subsection
6.1
, we will evaluate it for an explicit example.

Let us compute two-point correlator of scalar field
\phi
on dS
d+1
at late time by applying (
1.6
) with the wave functional of universe (
2.16
).
At the leading order in
G_{N}
, the two-point function is given by (
2.17
). There could be several types of one-loop contributions to two-point correlator, but here we only focus on one particular type.
We define the following integral as

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\mathcal{F}_{2}(x_{1}^{\prime},x_{2}^{\prime})=\sum_{s=2}^{\infty}\int d^{d}\vec{y}_{1}d^{d}\vec{y}_{2}d^{d}\vec{y}_{3}d^{d}\vec{y}_{4}\\
&\quad\times\frac{2\,\text{Re}\,\langle\,\mathcal{O}(\vec{x}_{1}^{\prime})\,\mathcal{O}(\vec{y}_{1})\,J_{j_{1}\cdots j_{s}}(\vec{y}_{2})\,\rangle\,\Pi^{j_{1}\cdots j_{s}}_{i_{1}\cdots i_{s}}(\vec{y}_{23})\,2\,\text{Re}\,\langle\,J^{i_{1}\cdots i_{s}}(\vec{y}_{3})\,\mathcal{O}_{3}(\vec{y}_{4})\,\mathcal{O}_{4}(\vec{x}_{2}^{\prime})\,\rangle}{(-2\,\text{Re}\,\langle\,J_{s}(\vec{y}_{2})\,J_{s}(\vec{y}_{3})\,\rangle)(-2\,\text{Re}\,\langle\,\mathcal{O}(\vec{y}_{1})\,\mathcal{O}(\vec{y}_{4})\,\rangle)}\,.\end{aligned}

(2.30)

This integral naturally arises when considering so-called split representation of one-loop correction to the bulk to bulk scalar propagator.
The particular type of contribution to the two-point function considered here can be evaluated from the inversion formula:

\displaystyle\int d^{d}\vec{x}_{1}^{\prime}d^{d}\vec{x}_{2}^{\prime}\frac{\mathcal{F}_{2}(x_{1}^{\prime},x_{2}^{\prime})}{(-2\,\text{Re}\,\langle\,\mathcal{O}(\vec{x}_{1})\,\mathcal{O}(\vec{x}_{1}^{\prime})\,\rangle)(-2\,\text{Re}\,\langle\,\mathcal{O}(\vec{x}_{2})\,\mathcal{O}(\vec{x}_{2}^{\prime})\,\rangle)}\,.

(2.31)

From (
1.6
) with (
2.16
), we can then read off other types of contributions.

The above expression implies another subtlety when
C_{2}(\{\vec{x}_{l}\})
is obtained from an analytic continuation of AdS case.
For CFT
d
dual to AdS
d+1
, we may be able to obtain an exact expression of two-point function

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{O}^{\text{AdS}}(\vec{x}_{1})\mathcal{O}^{\text{AdS}}(\vec{x}_{2})\rangle

(2.32)

in terms of
N
, which would be related to
1/N^{2}\propto G_{N}
(or
1/N\propto G_{N}
in a higher-spin holography as our case, see (
3.3
)). However, a part of contributions at the next leading order can be computed by

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\sum_{s=2}^{\infty}\int d^{d}\vec{y}_{1}d^{d}\vec{y}_{2}d^{d}\vec{y}_{3}d^{d}\vec{y}_{4}\\
&\quad\times\frac{\langle\,\mathcal{O}^{\text{AdS}}(\vec{x}_{1}^{\prime})\,\mathcal{O}^{\text{AdS}}(\vec{y}_{1})\,J_{j_{1}\cdots j_{s}}^{\text{AdS}}(\vec{y}_{2})\,\rangle\,\Pi^{j_{1}\cdots j_{s}}_{i_{1}\cdots i_{s}}(\vec{y}_{23})\langle\,J^{i_{1}\cdots i_{s}}_{\text{AdS}}(\vec{y}_{3})\,\mathcal{O}_{3}(\vec{y}_{4})\,\mathcal{O}_{4}(\vec{x}_{2}^{\prime})\,\rangle}{\langle\,J_{s}^{\text{AdS}}(\vec{y}_{2})\,J_{s}^{\text{AdS}}(\vec{y}_{3})\,\rangle\langle\,\mathcal{O}^{\text{AdS}}(\vec{y}_{1})\,\mathcal{O}^{\text{AdS}}(\vec{y}_{4})\,\rangle}\,,\end{aligned}

(2.33)

which corresponds to evaluating the Witten diagram in fig.
4
.

Figure 4
:
Split representation of a contribution to one-loop corrections in two-point function.

If we define
C_{2}(\{\vec{x}_{l}\})
from (
2.32
) with higher order corrections in
1/N
, then the contribution in (
2.33
) would be double-counted in (
2.31
) with (
2.30
). Thus we have to remove the contribution in (
2.33
) from (
2.32
) to define
C_{2}(\{\vec{x}_{l}\})
via an analytic continuation. We can see that the situation is quite analogous to the case of four-point correlator discussed above. In the same way, we can see that all higher order contributions in
1/N
to the two-point function (
2.32
) have to be removed in order to avoid double-counting.

\text{dS}_{3}/\text{CFT}_{2}

In the previous section, we explained the method to compute bulk correlation functions at late-time from dual CFT using the formalism with the wave functional of universe. In this section, we introduce a prescription to construct a concrete example of dS
3
/CFT
2
correspondence as an analytic continuation of Gaberdiel-Gopakumar duality
[
19
]
. In the next subsection, we review the known results on the higher-spin AdS
3
holography. In subsection

, we explain our prescription to perform the analytic continuation from the AdS

We also examine two- and three-point correlators as simple examples.
In the succeeding sections, we examine more interesting examples including four-point correlators.

3.1
Review of Gaberdiel-Gopakumar duality

It was proposed in
[
19
]
that classical higher-spin gravity on AdS
3
is dual to the coset model (
1.2
) at the ’t Hooft limit (
1.3
). The higher-spin theory is the one constructed by Prokushkin and Vasiliev in
[
20
]
, which includes higher-spin gauge fields with spin
s\,(=2,3,\ldots,\infty)
and two massive complex scalar fields. The gauge sector can be described by a Chern-Simons theory with the action

\displaystyle S=S_{\text{CS}}[A]-S_{\text{CS}}[\bar{A}]\,,\quad S_{\text{CS}}=\frac{k_{\text{CS}}}{4\pi}\int\text{tr}\left(A\wedge dA+\frac{2}{3}A\wedge A\wedge A\right)\,.

(3.1)

Here the
gauge fields
A
and
\bar{A}
are holomorphic and anti-holomorphic one-forms and they take values in higher-spin gauge algebra
\mathfrak{hs}[\hat{\lambda}]
. The gauge algebra is characterized such as to be truncated to
\mathfrak{sl}(N^{\prime})
if we set
\hat{\lambda}=\pm N^{\prime}
.
We denote the generators by
V^{s}_{m}
. The index
s
is related to the spin of higher-spin gauge field as
s=2,3,\ldots
. The other index
m
runs over
m=-s+1,-s+2,\ldots,s-1
. In particular, they satisfy

\displaystyle[V^{2}_{m},V^{s}_{n}]=(m(s-1)-n)V^{s}_{m+n}\,,

(3.2)

see, e.g.,
[
39
]
for other commutation relations.
The coupling is given by the Chern-Simons level
k_{\text{CS}}
, and it is related to the gravity parameters as

\displaystyle k_{\text{CS}}=\frac{\ell_{\text{AdS}}}{4G_{N}}\,.

(3.3)

The asymptotic symmetry near the AdS boundary was examined in
[
39
,
40
,
41
,
42
]
.
The symmetry algebra is identified with an infinite dimensional higher-spin algebra denoted by
W_{\infty}[\hat{\lambda}]
with the central charge

\displaystyle c=6k_{\text{CS}}=\frac{3\ell_{\text{AdS}}}{2G_{N}}\,,

(3.4)

which is the same as the famous Brown-Henneaux value
[
43
]
.
The parameter
\hat{\lambda}
is the same as the one in (
1.1
), and the algebra can be truncated to
W_{N^{\prime}}
-algebra with currents of spin
s\,(=2,3,\ldots,N^{\prime})
if we set
\hat{\lambda}=\pm N^{\prime}
.
The massive sector of the Prokushkin-Vasiliev theory consists of two scalar fields
\phi_{\pm}
with the same mass given in (
1.1
) as

\ell^{2}_{\text{AdS}}\,m^{2}=-1+{\hat{\lambda}}^{2}
.
Here we assign
0<\hat{\lambda}<1
.
The coupling to the gauge fields is described by the equations of motion
[
20
]
, see
[
44
]
for a good review.

The CFT dual to the higher-spin theory on AdS
3
was proposed in
[
19
]
to be the coset CFT (
1.2
), which describes a unitary minimal model with respect to
W_{N}
-algebra. The central charge of the model is given by

\displaystyle c=(N-1)\left(1-\frac{N(N+1)}{(N+k)(N+k+1)}\right)\,.

(3.5)

The classical limit of higher-spin gravity corresponds to the ’t Hooft limit of the CFT given by (
1.3
).
The coset CFT (
1.2
) at the ’t Hooft limit is known to possess the symmetry of
W_{\infty}[\lambda]
-algebra with the central charge (
3.5
)
[
39
,
45
]
.
In such a limit the central charge becomes:

\lim_{\rm{}^{\prime}tHooft}c\to N(1-\lambda^{2})+{\cal O}(N^{0})\,,\quad\lambda=\frac{N}{N+k}\to\text{fixed}\,.

(3.6)

The ’t Hooft parameter
\lambda
defined above is identified with
\hat{\lambda}
appearing in the higher-spin theory, and in the following we only use
\lambda
instead of
\hat{\lambda}
.
We will thus take (
3.6
) to be the defining relation between the central charge
c
and
N
in the ’t Hooft limit (
1.3
) and we will mostly be working in this limit throughout unless otherwise stated. Furthermore using (
3.4
), we can directly relate the large
c
/large
N
expansion with the perturbative expansion in
G_{N}
in AdS
3
.

The states of the coset CFT (
1.2
) are labeled by three Young diagrams
(\Lambda_{+},\omega;\Lambda_{-})
. There are selection rules and field identifications, and not all labels are allowed, see
[
46
]
for more details. In this case,
\omega
is uniquely fixed by the other two labels
(\Lambda_{+};\Lambda_{-})
. The state labeled by
(\Lambda_{+};\Lambda_{-})
has conformal weight

\displaystyle h_{(\Lambda_{+};\Lambda_{-})}=n+h^{(k)}_{\Lambda_{+}}+h^{(1)}_{\omega}-h^{(k+1)}_{\Lambda_{-}}\,,\quad h_{\Lambda}^{(k)}=\frac{C_{2}(\Lambda)}{k+N}\,,\quad C_{2}(\Lambda)=\frac{1}{2}(\Lambda,\Lambda+2\rho)\,.

(3.7)

Here the positive integer
n
represents how the denominator representation is embedded into the numerator one. Moreover,
C_{2}(\Lambda)
is the eigenvalue of the quadratic Casimir operator and
\rho
is the Weyl vector.
The fundamental states are given by those labeled by
(\square;0)
and
(0;\square)
(and their conjugate representations) and other states may be generated by fusing these fundamental states. We denote the corresponding complex scalar operators by

\mathcal{O}_{\pm}(z,\bar{z})
, whose conformal dimensions are

\displaystyle\Delta_{\pm}^{(0)}=2h_{\pm}=1\pm\lambda

(3.8)

at the ’t Hooft limit (
1.3
).
They are dual to bulk scalar fields with standard
(+)
and alternative
(-)
boundary conditions (see, e.g.,
[
44
]
).
The states labeled by generic Young diagrams
(\Lambda_{+};\Lambda_{-})
are interpreted as bound states of these scalar fields.
More precisely, to compare CFT results with gravity computations later, let us introduce the expansion of the scaling dimensions in terms of the inverse central charge
1/c
:

\Delta_{\pm}=\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\Delta_{\pm}^{(r)}c^{-r}\,,\quad c\to\infty\,.

(3.9)

As explained earlier, this is equivalent to an expansion in
G_{N}
hence (
3.8
) should strictly be taken as the leading classical value. We will apply such an expansion when expanding the higher-point correlation functions in the next section.

The coset CFT (
1.2
) has the symmetry of
W_{N}
-algebra as proven in
[
47
]
and in the ’t Hooft limit, the algebra is generated by conserved currents
J_{(s)}^{\text{AdS}}(z)
(and anti-holomorphic currents
\bar{J}_{(s)}^{\text{AdS}}(\bar{z})
) with
s=2,3,\ldots,\infty
. These conserved currents are dual to higher-spin gauge fields
A
(and
\bar{A}
) in the bulk theory.
The gauge configuration corresponding to the AdS background is given by

\displaystyle A=e^{\rho}\,V_{1}^{2}\,dz+V^{2}_{0}\,d\rho\,,\qquad\bar{A}=e^{\rho}\,V_{-1}^{2}\,dz-V^{2}_{0}\,d\rho\,.

(3.10)

Here
\rho
relates to the radial coordinate
y
in the Poincare metric (
2.2
) via
y=e^{-\rho}
and
(z,\bar{z})
are the complex coordinates for the plane parallel to the boundary.
We consider a small fluctuation from the holomorphic AdS background gauge field
A
, while keeping anti-holomorphic
\bar{A}
fixed as (see (3.20) of
[
44
]
)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &A_{z}=e^{\rho}\,V_{1}^{2}+\frac{1}{cB^{(s)}}\,J_{(s)}^{\text{AdS}}(z)\,e^{-(s-1)\rho}\,V^{s}_{-(s-1)}\,,\\
&A_{\bar{z}}=-\mu_{(s)}^{\text{AdS}}(z)\,e^{(s-1)\rho}\,V^{s}_{s-1}+\cdots\,.\end{aligned}

(3.11)

Here we use the convention with

\displaystyle\qquad B^{(s)}=\frac{1}{2^{2s}\pi^{\frac{5}{2}}}\,\frac{\sin(\pi\lambda)}{\lambda(1-\lambda^{2})}\,\frac{\Gamma(s)\,\Gamma(s-\lambda)\,\Gamma(s+\lambda)}{\Gamma\left(s-\frac{1}{2}\right)}\,,

(3.12)

where
\mu_{(s)}^{\rm AdS}(z)
acts as the source term for
J_{(s)}^{\rm AdS}(z)
.
The generators of
W_{\infty}[\lambda]
near the AdS boundary are given by
J_{(s)}^{\text{AdS}}
, which can be identified with the generators of symmetry algebra in the dual CFT.

Solving the bulk equations of motion, the authors in
[
44
]

obtained three-point functions of scalar-scalar-higher spin current as

\displaystyle\langle\,\mathcal{O}_{\pm}^{\text{AdS}}(z_{1})\,\bar{\mathcal{O}}_{\pm}^{\text{AdS}}(z_{2})\,J_{(s)}^{\text{AdS}}(z_{3})\,\rangle=C_{\pm}^{(s)}\left(\frac{z_{12}}{z_{13}z_{23}}\right)^{s}\,\langle\,\mathcal{O}_{\pm}^{\text{AdS}}(z_{1})\,\bar{\mathcal{O}}_{\pm}^{\text{AdS}}(z_{2})\,\rangle

(3.13)

with

\displaystyle C_{\pm}^{(s)}=\frac{\eta^{s}_{\pm}}{2\pi}\,\frac{\Gamma(s)^{2}}{\Gamma(2s-1)}\frac{\Gamma(s\pm\lambda)}{\Gamma(1\pm\lambda)}\,.

(3.14)

The phase factors can be chosen arbitrary but here we set
\eta^{s}_{+}=1
and
\eta^{s}_{-}=(-1)^{s}
as in
[
48
]
.
The three-point function with
\bar{J}_{(s)}^{\text{AdS}}
has an extra factor
(-1)^{s}
along with
z
replaced by
\bar{z}
. The higher-spin currents are normalized as

\displaystyle\langle\,J_{(s)}^{\text{AdS}}(z)\,J_{(s)}^{\text{AdS}}(0)\,\rangle=\frac{cB^{(s)}}{z^{2s}}\,,\quad\langle\,\bar{J}_{(s)}^{\text{AdS}}(\bar{z})\,\bar{J}_{(s)}^{\text{AdS}}(0)\,\rangle=\frac{cB^{(s)}}{\bar{z}^{2s}}\,.

(3.15)

They are evaluated by tree-level computations in the bulk theory, thus they are valid at the leading order in
1/c
. It is also possible to confirm them from the dual CFT viewpoints
[
44
,
48
]
, see also appendix
A.2
.

We should also mention about the two-point functions of scalar operators. The coordinate dependence is fixed by conformal symmetry and the overall normalization is just a convention. However, as in the case of two-point functions of higher-spin currents (
3.15
), the canonical convention in the bulk theory leads to

\displaystyle\langle\,\mathcal{O}_{\pm}^{\text{AdS}}(z_{1})\,\bar{\mathcal{O}}_{\pm}^{\text{AdS}}(z_{2})\,\rangle\sim\frac{c}{|z_{12}|^{4h_{\pm}}}\ ,

(3.16)

up to an multiplication of real number. We will use a different normalization as defined below, but the original factor is quite important to perform an analytic continuation from AdS

to dS

, see
[

]
for a similar argument for higher-spin dS

Analytic continuation from AdS

We would like to introduce a dS

We use

.
As mentioned above in the ’t Hooft limit, the symmetry organizing the CFT reduces to
W_{\infty}[\lambda]
and has two parameters
\lambda
and
c
.
The central charge is now taken to be purely imaginary value
c=-ic^{(g)}
with
c^{(g)}\in\mathbb{R}
.
The original coset is labeled by two parameters
N,k
, and these parameters can be written in terms of
\lambda,c
instead via (
1.3
) and (
3.5
).
In practice, we compute physical quantities by the coset CFT (or its dual description of Toda field theory as derived in
[
50
]
), which may be expressed even with finite
N,k
. We then rewrite them in terms of
\lambda,c
via (
1.3
) and (
3.5
) and expand them in terms of
1/c
.

3.2.1
Two-point correlators

We would like to begin by computing the two-point bulk correlators on dS
3
at late time. We will first consider the scalar fields and then move to the symmetric tensor fields. For this, we use the formulas given in (
2.17
) and (
2.18
). In the coordinate basis, taking the inverse of two-point function is actually quite non-trivial.
We first define the inverse function
K_{h,\bar{h}}(x,y)
which satisfies

\displaystyle\int d^{2}y\,K_{h,\bar{h}}(x,y)\,\frac{1}{(y-z)^{2h}(\bar{y}-\bar{z})^{2\bar{h}}}=\delta^{(2)}(x-z)\,.

(3.20)

Explicitly we have,

\displaystyle K_{h,\bar{h}}(x,y)\equiv\frac{1}{\pi}k_{h,\bar{h}}\frac{1}{(x-y)^{2-2h}(\bar{x}-\bar{y})^{2-2h}}\,,\quad k_{h,\bar{h}}=\frac{\Gamma(2-2\bar{h})}{\Gamma(2h-1)}=(-1)^{2(h-\bar{h})}\frac{\Gamma(2-2h)}{\Gamma(2\bar{h}-1)}\,,

(3.21)

see, e.g.,
[
51
]
for more extensive definitions. We have followed the notations there.
Notice that we can equivalently view
K_{h,\bar{h}}(x,y)
as the two-point correlation function of the so-called shadow operator, see (
4.10
) below.

Let us consider the two-point correlation functions of scalar fields at late time in dS
3
.
As discussed around (
2.11
), the scalar operators dual to the bulk dS
3
fields are related to those dual to the bulk AdS
3
fields as

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{\pm}=e^{i\frac{\pi}{2}(2h_{\pm}-2)}\mathcal{O}_{\pm}^{\text{AdS}}\,,\qquad\tilde{\mathcal{O}}_{\pm}=e^{i\frac{\pi}{2}(2h_{\pm}-2)}\bar{\mathcal{O}}_{\pm}^{\text{AdS}}\,,

(3.22)

where we have set
\Delta_{\pm}^{(0)}=2h_{\pm}=1\pm\lambda
and
d=2
.
Note that
\bar{\mathcal{O}}_{\pm}^{\text{AdS}}
is complex conjugate of
\mathcal{O}_{\pm}^{\text{AdS}}
, while
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}_{\pm}
is not complex conjugate of
\mathcal{O}_{\pm}
.
The relations (
3.22
) and the canonical normalization (
3.16
) imply that the two-point coefficient functions are given by

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \langle\,\mathcal{O}_{\pm}(z_{1})\,\tilde{\mathcal{O}}_{\pm}(z_{2})\,\rangle&=\left.e^{2i\pi h_{\pm}}\,\langle\,\mathcal{O}_{\pm}^{\text{AdS}}(z_{1})\,\bar{\mathcal{O}}_{\pm}^{\text{AdS}}(z_{2})\,\rangle\right|_{c\to-ic^{(g)}}\\
&=-\frac{ic^{(g)}}{c}\,e^{2i\pi h_{\pm}}\,\langle\,\mathcal{O}_{\pm}^{\text{AdS}}(z_{1})\,\bar{\mathcal{O}}_{\pm}^{\text{AdS}}(z_{2})\,\rangle\,.\end{aligned}

(3.23)

In the following, we will set the overall normalization factors of the AdS/dS two-point correlation functions to absorb
c/c^{(g)}
respectively, such that we are keeping only the overall phase after the analytic continuations (
3.22
):

\displaystyle\langle\,\mathcal{O}_{\pm}^{\text{AdS}}(z_{1})\,\bar{\mathcal{O}}_{\pm}^{\text{AdS}}(z_{2})\,\rangle=\frac{1}{|z_{12}|^{4h_{\pm}}}\,,\quad\langle\,\mathcal{O}_{\pm}(z_{1})\,\tilde{\mathcal{O}}_{\pm}(z_{2})\,\rangle=-ie^{2i\pi h_{\pm}}\,\frac{1}{|z_{12}|^{4h_{\pm}}}\,.

(3.24)

Now we can obtain the bulk two-point correlators at late time from the wave functional formalism explicitly by applying the inversion formula (
2.17
). Here the second expression in (
3.24
) plays the role of
C_{2}(\{\vec{x}_{l}\})
, and we obtain

\displaystyle\langle\,\phi_{\pm}(z_{1})\,\bar{\phi}_{\pm}(z_{2})\,\rangle=\frac{1}{2\sin(2\pi h_{\pm})}K_{h_{\pm},h_{\pm}}(z_{1},z_{2})=a_{h_{\pm}}\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{\Gamma(2-2h_{\pm})}{\Gamma(2h_{\pm}-1)}\frac{1}{|z_{12}|^{4-4h_{\pm}}\,}\,.

(3.25)

Here we have used
K_{h_{\pm},h_{\pm}}(z_{1},z_{2})
defined in (
3.21
).
Compared with pure AdS
3
case, we have extra phase factor

\displaystyle a_{h_{\pm}}=\frac{1}{2\sin(2\pi h_{\pm})}\,.

(3.26)

The factor of
2\sin(2\pi h_{\pm})
in the denominator of (
3.26
) arises as we need to include the contributions from
\Psi[\psi_{l}^{0}]
and its complex conjugate when extracting the bulk two-point correlators after the analytic continuation.
Note that the same factor
a_{h_{\pm}}=c_{2h_{\pm}}^{\text{dS-AdS}}
was inserted in order to relate bulk propagators on AdS
d+1
and dS
d+1
as in (2.15) of
[
23
]
. In
[
23
]
, the authors included the factor such that the both two-point propagators have the correct normalization at small distance limit. We also remark that the identical factor
c_{\Delta}^{\text{dS-AdS}}
can be used also for symmetric tensor fields with
\Delta
and spin
s
.

We next move to the case of higher-spin gauge fields.
The gauge configuration corresponds to dS
3
background is given by

\displaystyle A=i\eta^{-1}(V_{1}^{2}dz+iV_{0}^{2}d\eta)\,,\qquad\bar{A}=i\eta^{-1}(V_{-1}^{2}d\bar{z}-iV_{0}^{2}d\eta)\,,

(3.27)

where we have set
y=e^{-\rho}
and change as
y=i\eta
in (
3.10
).
A small fluctuation corresponding to the inclusion of holomorphic higher spin fields can be expressed as

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &A_{z}=i\eta^{-1}V_{1}^{2}+\left.\frac{i}{cB^{(s)}}\,J_{(s)}(z)\eta^{s-1}V^{s}_{-(s-1)}\right|_{c\to-ic^{(g)}}=i\eta^{-1}V_{1}^{2}-\frac{1}{c^{(g)}B^{(s)}}\,J_{(s)}(z)\eta^{s-1}V^{s}_{-(s-1)}\,,\\
&A_{\bar{z}}=-i\mu_{(s)}(z)\,\eta^{-(s-1)}\,V^{s}_{s-1}+\cdots\,.\end{aligned}

(3.28)

Assuming the identification of
A_{z}
under the map from AdS
3
to dS
3
, we have the relation

\displaystyle J_{(s)}(z)=e^{i\frac{\pi}{2}(s-2)}J_{(s)}^{\text{AdS}}(z)\,.

(3.29)

The result is consistent with the one in (
2.15
) based on generic argument. In a similar manner, we can obtain

\displaystyle\tilde{J}_{(s)}(\bar{z})=e^{i\frac{\pi}{2}(s-2)}\bar{J}_{(s)}^{\text{AdS}}(\bar{z})\,.

(3.30)

We again remark that while
\bar{J}_{(s)}^{\text{AdS}}(\bar{z})
is complex conjugate of
J^{\text{AdS}}_{(s)}(z)
but
\tilde{J}_{(s)}(\bar{z})
is not complex conjugate of
J_{(s)}(z)
due to the same phase assignments for the both.

Using the relation (
3.29
), the two-point coefficient functions are written as

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \langle\,J_{(s)}(z_{1})\,J_{(s)}(z_{2})\,\rangle&=\left.(-1)^{s}\,\langle\,J_{(s)}^{\text{AdS}}(z_{1})\,J_{(s)}^{\text{AdS}}(z_{2})\,\rangle\right|_{c\to-ic^{(g)}}\\
&=(-1)^{s+1}\frac{ic^{(g)}}{c}\,\,\langle\,J_{(s)}^{\text{AdS}}(z_{1})\,J_{(s)}^{\text{AdS}}(z_{2})\,\rangle\,.\end{aligned}

(3.31)

We should add also the complex conjugate of the two-point function of
\tilde{J}_{s}(\bar{z})
:

\displaystyle\langle\,\tilde{J}_{(s)}(\bar{z}_{1})\,\tilde{J}_{(s)}(\bar{z}_{2})\,\rangle=(-1)^{s}\frac{ic^{(g)}}{c}\,\langle\,\bar{J}_{(s)}^{\text{AdS}}(\bar{z}_{1})\,\bar{J}_{(s)}^{\text{AdS}}(\bar{z}_{2})\,\rangle\,,

(3.32)

where we have used (
3.30
).
In contrast with the scalar two-point functions, we do not change the normalization factors here to absorb
c/c^{(g)}
.
Applying the formula (
2.18
) and (
3.21
), the bulk correlators of the higher-spin fields at late time are obtained as

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \langle\mu_{(s)}(z_{1})\mu_{(s)}(z_{2})\rangle&=(-1)^{s+1}\frac{i}{2}\frac{1}{c^{(g)}B^{(s)}}K_{s,0}(z_{1},z_{2})\\
&=a_{(s)}\frac{1}{c^{(g)}B^{(s)}\pi\Gamma(2s-1)}\frac{1}{(z_{12})^{2-2s}(\bar{z}_{12})^{2}}\,.\end{aligned}

(3.33)

The factor

\displaystyle a_{(s)}=(-1)^{s+1}\frac{i}{2}

(3.34)

arises due to our prescription of analytic continuation as in the case of scalar field. It does not match with the generic expression (
3.26
) since the factor diverges at the massless limit with
\Delta=s+d-2=s\in\mathbb{N}
for
d=2
. This is however not a contradiction, since the massless limit is quite delicate. For instance, let us consider a spin-one massive field on Lorentzian dS
d+1
spacetime. Its bulk two-point function may be found in (3.18)-(3.20) of
[
52
]
, but it diverges at the massless limit with
m\to 0
. In order to obtain correct two-point function of massless spin-one gauge field, we may have to work with the action of massless gauge field from the beginning as in section 4 of
[
52
]
.
It is natural to expect that the factor (
3.34
) can be obtained by working with massless higher-spin gauge fields from the beginning.
It is an important future problem to reproduce the factor (
3.34
) from the method of
[
22
,
23
]
for the case as well.

3.2.2
Three-point correlators

As in the AdS
3
case, here
we consider the basic three-point correlators in dS
3
at the late time consisting of two scalar fields and one higher-spin gauge field.
Using the maps (
3.22
) and (
3.29
), the three-point coefficient functions are related to (
3.13
) as:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \langle\,\mathcal{O}_{\pm}(z_{1})\,\tilde{\mathcal{O}}_{\pm}(z_{2})\,J_{(s)}(z_{3})\,\rangle&=-\left.e^{i\frac{\pi}{2}(4h_{\pm}+s)}\langle\,\mathcal{O}_{\pm}^{\text{AdS}}(z_{1})\,\bar{\mathcal{O}}_{\pm}^{\text{AdS}}(z_{2})\,J_{(s)}^{\text{AdS}}(z_{3})\,\rangle\right|_{c\to-ic^{(g)}}\\
&=i\,e^{i\frac{\pi}{2}(4h_{\pm}+s)}C_{\pm}^{(s)}\left(\frac{z_{12}}{z_{13}z_{23}}\right)^{s}\frac{1}{|z_{12}|^{4h_{\pm}}}\,.\end{aligned}

(3.35)

In the same way, we find

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \langle\,\tilde{\mathcal{O}}_{\pm}(z_{1})\,\mathcal{O}_{\pm}(z_{2})\,\tilde{J}_{(s)}(\bar{z}_{3})\,\rangle=i\,e^{i\frac{\pi}{2}(4h_{\pm}+s)}C_{\pm}^{(s)}\left(\frac{\bar{z}_{12}}{\bar{z}_{13}\bar{z}_{23}}\right)^{s}\frac{1}{|z_{12}|^{4h_{\pm}}}\end{aligned}

(3.36)

from the maps (
3.22
) and (
3.30
).
The sum of (
3.35
) and the complex conjugate of (
3.36
) yields

\displaystyle 2\sin\left((4h_{\pm}+s)\frac{\pi}{2}\right)C_{\pm}^{(s)}\left(\frac{z_{12}}{z_{13}z_{23}}\right)^{s}\frac{1}{|z_{12}|^{4h_{\pm}}}\,.

(3.37)

Notice that the complex conjugation of (
3.36
) only depend on the holomorphic coordinates
z_{ij}
up to the scalar two-point function just like (
3.35
).

In order to obtain the corresponding dS bulk correlators from the coefficient function
C_{3}(\{\vec{x}_{l}\})
, which is proportional to (
3.35
), we will use the expression (
2.20
).
For this, it is convenient to first consider the inversion formula
[
51
]

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \frac{k_{h,\bar{h}}}{\pi}\int d^{2}y\frac{1}{(z-y)^{2-2h}(\bar{z}-\bar{y})^{2-2\bar{h}}}\mathcal{F}^{h,\bar{h}}_{12}(y,\bar{y})=\frac{\Gamma(1-h-h_{12})\Gamma(1-\bar{h}+\bar{h}_{12})}{\Gamma(\bar{h}+\bar{h}_{12})\Gamma(h-h_{12})}\mathcal{F}^{1-h,1-\bar{h}}_{12}(y,\bar{y})\,,\end{aligned}

(3.38)

with

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{h,\bar{h}}_{12}(z,\bar{z})=\frac{1}{z_{12}^{h_{1}+h_{2}-h}(z_{1}-z)^{h+h_{12}}(z_{2}-z)^{h-h_{12}}}\frac{1}{\bar{z}_{12}^{\bar{h}_{1}+\bar{h}_{2}-\bar{h}}(\bar{z}_{1}-\bar{z})^{\bar{h}+\bar{h}_{12}}(\bar{z}_{2}-\bar{z})^{\bar{h}-\bar{h}_{12}}}\,.

(3.39)

Here we set
h_{12}=h_{1}-h_{2}
and so on.
Substituting kinematic dependence in (
3.37
), we can now perform its inversion in turns. First we apply the two-point inversion formula:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\int d^{2}z_{1}^{\prime}d^{2}z_{2}^{\prime}K_{h_{+},h_{+}}(z_{1},z_{1}^{\prime})K_{h_{+},h_{+}}(z_{2},z_{2}^{\prime})\left(\frac{z_{1^{\prime}2^{\prime}}}{z_{1^{\prime}3^{\prime}}z_{2^{\prime}3^{\prime}}}\right)^{s}\frac{1}{|z_{1^{\prime}2^{\prime}}|^{4h_{\pm}}}\\
&\qquad\qquad=(-1)^{s+1}\frac{\Gamma(\mp\lambda)\Gamma(1\mp\lambda)}{\Gamma(1-s\pm\lambda)\Gamma(s\pm\lambda)}\left(\frac{z_{12}}{z_{13^{\prime}}z_{23^{\prime}}}\right)^{s}\frac{1}{|z_{12}|^{4h_{\mp}}}\,,\end{aligned}

(3.40)

and next we evaluate

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int d^{2}z_{3}^{\prime}K_{s,0}(z_{3},z_{3}^{\prime})\left(\frac{z_{12}}{z_{13^{\prime}}z_{23^{\prime}}}\right)^{s}\frac{1}{|z_{12}|^{4h_{\mp}}}=\frac{(-1)^{s+1}}{\Gamma(s)^{2}}\left(\frac{z_{12}}{z_{13}z_{23}}\right)^{1-s}\left(\frac{\bar{z}_{12}}{\bar{z}_{13}\bar{z}_{23}}\right)\frac{1}{|z_{12}|^{4h_{\mp}}}\,.\end{aligned}

(3.41)

Restoring the pre-factors in (
3.37
), as well as the normalization factors in two-point functions (
3.26
) and (
3.34
), we finally obtain

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\langle\,\phi_{\pm}(z_{1})\,\bar{\phi}_{\pm}(z_{2})\,\mu_{(s)}(z_{3})\rangle\\
&\quad=\lambda_{h_{\pm},h_{\pm},s}\frac{\Gamma(\mp\lambda)\Gamma(1\mp\lambda)}{\Gamma(s)^{2}\Gamma(1-s\pm\lambda)\Gamma(s\pm\lambda)}\frac{C_{\pm}^{(s)}}{c^{(g)}B^{(s)}}\left(\frac{z_{12}}{z_{13}z_{23}}\right)^{1-s}\left(\frac{\bar{z}_{12}}{\bar{z}_{13}\bar{z}_{23}}\right)\frac{1}{|z_{12}|^{4h_{\mp}}}\end{aligned}

(3.42)

with

\displaystyle\lambda_{h_{\pm},h_{\pm},s}=2a_{h_{\pm}}^{2}a_{(s)}\sin\left((4h_{\pm}+s)\frac{\pi}{2}\right)\,.

(3.43)

Almost the same factor appears in (3.24) of
[
23
]
up to the subtlety associated to the massless limit of higher-spin fields mentioned above, our result here thus serves as a confirmation in an explicit model.

4
Four-point correlators dual to AdS
3
and dS
3

We next turn to computations of the bulk four-point correlation functions of the following types:

\displaystyle\langle\,\phi_{-}(\infty)\,\phi_{+}(1)\,\bar{\phi}_{+}(z)\,\bar{\phi}_{-}(0)\,\rangle\,,\quad\langle\,\phi_{\pm}(\infty)\,\bar{\phi}_{\pm}(1)\,\phi_{\pm}(z)\,\bar{\phi}_{\pm}(0)\,\rangle

(4.1)

on dS
3
at the late time.
More precisely, we first consider the corresponding four-point correlation functions computed exactly in the 2d coset CFTs dual to higher-spin AdS
3
gravity, and we then obtain their leading non-trivial
1/c
-order contribution. In 2d CFTs, we normally consider the expansions of correlation functions in terms of Virasoro conformal blocks, and in the limit
c\to\infty
while keeping fixed all the external scaling dimensions (sometimes called as “LLLL” limit), it is well-known that these can be replaced by global conformal blocks, see
[
53
,
54
]
for recent progress. As mentioned earlier, this also corresponds to the expansion in
G_{N}
for the bulk correlation functions, such that at the leading order we only need to include tree-level interactions. We then perform the suitable analytic continuations to obtain the corresponding results in dS
3
.

4.1
The case dual to AdS
3

4.1.1
Four-point function
G_{-+}(z)

Let us start with the first correlator in (
4.1
) which is of simpler structure.
This case was already analyzed in
[
7
]
, but here we explain the analysis in more details.
We utilized the four-point function of operators dual to the bulk scalars
\phi_{+}^{\text{AdS}}
and
\phi_{-}^{\text{AdS}}
on AdS
3
. They are evaluated explicitly in
[
55
]
as

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} G_{-+}(z)&=\langle\,\mathcal{O}^{\text{AdS}}_{-}(\infty)\,\mathcal{O}^{\text{AdS}}_{+}(1)\,\bar{\mathcal{O}}^{\text{AdS}}_{+}(z)\,\bar{\mathcal{O}}^{\text{AdS}}_{-}(0)\,\rangle\\
&=\frac{|z|^{\frac{2}{N}}}{|1-z|^{2\Delta_{+}}}\left|1+\frac{1-z}{Nz}\right|^{2}\,,\end{aligned}

(4.2)

and the expression is exact for all finite
k,N
. Here we also list the exact expressions for
\Delta_{\pm}
:

\displaystyle\Delta_{+}=\frac{(N-1)(2N+1+k)}{N(N+k)}\,,\quad\Delta_{-}=\frac{(N-1)k}{N(N+k+1)}\,.

(4.3)

Let us now consider the systematic
1/c
-expansion of
G_{-+}(z)
.
As explained above, the symmetry organizing the CFT is
W_{\infty}[\lambda]
, which has two parameters
\lambda
and
c
.
As mentioned above, the coset (
1.2
) has two parameters
N,k
,
and we rewrite physical quantities instead in terms
\lambda,c
.
Explicitly we have

\displaystyle N=\frac{c}{1-\lambda^{2}}+\frac{1+\lambda+\lambda^{2}}{1+\lambda}+\mathcal{O}(c^{-1})\,.

(4.4)

Therefore, taking
N
large but keeping
\lambda=N/(N+k)
finite and fixed in the ’t Hooft limit, the
1/N
-expansion coincides with
1/c
-expansion at the leading order, but there will be corrections at higher order expansions as we will illustrate. The conformal dimensions
\Delta_{\pm}
can be also expanded in the form of (
3.9
) as:

\displaystyle\Delta_{+}

\displaystyle=1+\lambda+\frac{\lambda^{2}-1}{c}+\mathcal{O}(c^{-2})=\Delta_{+}^{(0)}+\frac{\Delta^{(1)}_{+}}{c}+\mathcal{O}(c^{-2})\,,

(4.5)

\displaystyle\Delta_{-}

\displaystyle=1-\lambda-\frac{(\lambda^{2}-1)^{2}}{c}+\mathcal{O}(c^{-2})=\Delta_{-}^{(0)}+\frac{\Delta^{(1)}_{-}}{c}+\mathcal{O}(c^{-2})\,.

(4.6)

The leading
1/c
-expansion of
G_{-+}(z)
(
4.2
) was obtained in
[
48
]
(see also appendix
A.2
).
In terms of global conformal block expansion, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} G_{-+}(z)&=\frac{1}{|1-z|^{2\Delta_{+}^{(0)}+2\Delta_{+}^{(1)}/c}}\\
&+\frac{1}{c}\frac{1}{|1-z|^{2\Delta_{+}^{(0)}}}\left[\sum_{s=2}^{\infty}(-1)^{s}\,\frac{C^{(s)}_{-}C^{(s)}_{+}}{B^{(s)}}\,(1-z)^{s}\,{}_{2}F_{1}(s,s;2s;1-z)+\text{c.c.}\right]+\mathcal{O}(c^{-2})\,.\end{aligned}

(4.7)

Note that
the hypergeometric function
{}_{2}F_{1}(s,s;2s,1-z)
(and its complex conjugate) corresponds to the global conformal block with intermediate operator
J_{(s)}
(and
\bar{J}_{(s)}
) which are conserved tensors saturating the unitarity bound
\Delta=s
, as the result we have the simplification or holomorphic factorization.
This is also precisely what the Virasoro blocks reduce to at this order in
1/c
-expansion.

As explained in subsection
2.2
, we need to extract contributions which can be obtained from lower-point functions. The first term in
(
4.7
)
corresponds to the contribution from the products of disconnected two-point functions,

\displaystyle\langle\,\mathcal{O}_{-}^{\text{AdS}}(\infty)\bar{\mathcal{O}}_{-}^{\text{AdS}}(0)\,\rangle\langle\,\mathcal{O}_{+}^{\text{AdS}}(1)\bar{\mathcal{O}}_{+}^{\text{AdS}}(z)\,\rangle\,.

(4.8)

More precisely at this order in
1/c
-expansion, the scaling dimension now consists of the tree level
\Delta_{+}^{(0)}
and the extra one-loop correction
\Delta^{(1)}_{+}/c
. From the next line in (
4.7
), we can extract the contribution of the form (
2.28
). For this, we evaluate

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\frac{1}{|1-z|^{2(1+\lambda)}}\hat{\mathcal{I}}_{s,0}(1-z,1-\bar{z})\\
&\quad=\frac{k_{1-s,1}k_{s,0}}{\pi^{2}B^{(s)}}\int d^{2}x\,d^{2}y\,\frac{\langle\,\mathcal{O}_{-}^{\text{AdS}}(\infty)\,\bar{\mathcal{O}}_{-}^{\text{AdS}}(0)\,J_{(s)}^{\text{AdS}}(x)\,\rangle\langle\,J_{(s)}^{\text{AdS}}(y)\,\mathcal{O}_{+}^{\text{AdS}}(1)\,\bar{\mathcal{O}}_{+}^{\text{AdS}}(z)\,\rangle}{{(x-y)^{2-2s}(\bar{x}-\bar{y})^{2}}}\,,\end{aligned}

(4.9)

where we have used the fact that the bi-tensorial projector simplifies in two dimensions.
As in
[
56
]
, it is convenient to define a shadow operator
\tilde{\Phi}_{1-h,1-\bar{h}}
for a primary operator
\Phi_{h,\bar{h}}
by

\displaystyle\tilde{\Phi}_{1-h,1-\bar{h}}(x)=\frac{k_{h,\bar{h}}}{\pi}\int d^{2}y\,\frac{1}{(x-y)^{2-2h}(\bar{x}-\bar{y})^{2-2\bar{h}}}\,\Phi_{h,\bar{h}}(y)\,.

(4.10)

The shadow operator
\tilde{\Phi}_{1-h,1-\bar{h}}
satisfies the normalization condition:

\displaystyle\langle\,\tilde{\Phi}_{1-h,1-\bar{h}}(x)\,\Phi_{h,\bar{h}}(y)\,\rangle=\delta^{(2)}(x-y)

(4.11)

for
\langle\Phi_{h,\bar{h}}(z)\Phi_{h,\bar{h}}(0)\rangle=z^{-2h}\bar{z}^{-2\bar{h}}
and
k_{h,\bar{h}}
defined in (
3.21
).
In terms of the shadow operator, the integral (
4.9
) can be written as:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\frac{1}{|1-z|^{2(1+\lambda)}}\hat{\mathcal{I}}_{s,0}(1-z,1-\bar{z})\\
&\quad=\frac{\Gamma(2s)}{\pi B^{(s)}}\int d^{2}x\,\langle\,\mathcal{O}_{-}^{\text{AdS}}(\infty)\,\bar{\mathcal{O}}_{-}^{\text{AdS}}(0)\,J_{(s)}^{\text{AdS}}(x)\,\rangle\,\,\langle\,\tilde{J}_{(s)}^{\text{AdS}}(x)\,\mathcal{O}_{+}^{\text{AdS}}(1)\,\bar{\mathcal{O}}_{+}^{\text{AdS}}(z)\,\rangle\,.\end{aligned}

(4.12)

This is nothing but the definition of conformal partial wave up to an overall factor given as:

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{I}}_{s,0}(1-z,1-\bar{z})=(-1)^{s}\frac{C^{(s)}_{-}C^{(s)}_{+}}{B^{(s)}}\,\mathcal{I}_{s,0}(1-z,1-\bar{z})\,.

(4.13)

The conformal partial wave
\mathcal{I}_{h,\bar{h}}(z,\bar{z})
is introduced in (
A.27
), and in the current case it is written explicitly as

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \frac{\Gamma(s)^{2}}{\Gamma(2s)}\mathcal{I}_{s,0}(z,\bar{z})&=\frac{\Gamma(s)^{2}}{\Gamma(2s)}z^{s}{}_{2}F_{1}(s,s;2s;z)\\
&+\frac{\Gamma(2s-1)}{\Gamma(s)^{2}}z^{1-s}{}_{2}F_{1}(1-s,1-s;2-2s;z)\bar{z}{}_{2}F_{1}(1,1;2;\bar{z})\,.\end{aligned}

(4.14)

Replacing the conformal blocks by the conformal partial waves, we have

\displaystyle G_{-+}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|1-z|^{2\Delta_{+}^{(0)}+2\Delta_{+}^{(1)}/c}}

(4.15)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{c}\frac{1}{|1-z|^{2{\Delta_{+}^{(0)}}}}\left[\sum_{s=2}^{\infty}(-1)^{s}\,\frac{C^{(s)}_{-}C^{(s)}_{+}}{B^{(s)}}\,\mathcal{I}_{s,0}(1-z,1-\bar{z})+(\lambda^{2}-1)\ln|z|^{2}\right]+\mathcal{O}(c^{-2})\,,

see appendix
A.3
for its derivation.
The term proportional to
\ln|z|^{2}
is the contribution which cannot be obtained from lower-point functions, i.e., four-point contact diagrams.

4.1.2
Four-point functions
G_{\pm\pm}(z)

We next move to the other correlators listed in (
4.1
).
These have not been dealt with in
[

\displaystyle Q_{-+}^{(1)}(1-z)

\displaystyle Q_{-+}^{(2)}(1-z)

We can now comment on the identifications of some of the terms listed in (

3

The first term in (

corrected up to two-loop order.
As shown in section

It is thus natural to expect that the second term in (

Finally the last term would be attributed to certain combination of the exchange diagrams now with one-loop corrected three-point vertex, or with one-loop corrected bulk to bulk exchange propagator. The appearance of

can perhaps be explained by the fact that all of these diagrams can be constructed by fusing a pair of tree level four-point exchange diagrams using the split representation of the AdS Harmonic function. However to make the identification precise at this level, it would be necessary to combine the result from the higher order
1/c
expansion of Virasoro conformal block, see e.g.
[
54
]
, as well as the actual AdS loop computations.

We then examine more complicated examples of

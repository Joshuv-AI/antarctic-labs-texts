A free boundary problem in thermal insulation with a prescribed heat source
P. Acampora, E. Cristoforoni, C. Nitsch, C. Trombetti

Abstract
We study the thermal insulation of a bounded body Ω ⊂ ℝ n \Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n} , under a prescribed heat source f > 0 f>0 , via a bulk layer of insulating material. We consider a model of heat transfer between the insulated body and the environment determined by convection; this corresponds to Robin boundary conditions on the free boundary of the layer. We show that a minimal configuration exists and that it satisfies uniform density estimates. MSC 2020: 35R35, 35J25, 35A01. Keywords: Robin, thermal insulation, free boundary, heat source

Introduction

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open bounded set with smooth boundary, let
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
be a positive function and let
\beta,\,C_{0}
be positive constants. We consider the following energy functional

\mathcal{F}(A,v)=\int_{A}\lvert\nabla v\rvert^{2}\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}+\beta\int_{\partial A}v^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}-2\int_{\Omega}fv\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}+C_{0}\mathcal{L}^{n}(A\setminus\Omega),

(1.1)

and the variational problem

\inf\Set{\mathcal{F}(A,v)}{\begin{aligned} &A\supseteq\Omega\text{ open, bounded and Lipschitz}\\
&v\in W^{1,2}(A),\>v\geq 0\,\text{in }A\end{aligned}}.

(1.2)

This problem is related to the following thermal insulation problem: for a given heat source
f
distributed in a conductor
\Omega
, find the best possible configuration of insulating material surrounding
\Omega
. A similar problem has been studied in
[
2
]
and
[
8
]
for a thin insulating layer, and in
[
6
]
,
[
4
]
and
[
5
]
for a prescribed temperature in
\Omega
.

For a fixed open set
A
with Lipschitz boundary, we have, via the direct methods of the calculus of variations, that there exists
u_{A}\in W^{1,2}(A)
such that

\mathcal{F}(A,u_{A})\leq\mathcal{F}(A,v),

for all
v\in W^{1,2}(A)
, with
v\geq 0
in
A
. Furthermore
u_{A}
solves the following stationary problem, with Robin boundary condition on
\partial A
. Precisely

\begin{cases}-\Delta u_{A}=f&\text{in }\Omega,\\[3.0pt]
\dfrac{\partial u_{A}^{+}}{\partial\nu\hphantom{\scriptstyle{+}}}=\dfrac{\partial u_{A}^{-}}{\partial\nu\hphantom{\scriptstyle{+}}}&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\\[6.0pt]
\Delta u_{A}=0&\text{in }A\setminus\Omega,\\[3.0pt]
\dfrac{\partial u_{A}}{\partial\nu}+\beta u_{A}=0&\text{on }\partial A,\end{cases}

where
u_{A}^{-}
and
u_{A}^{+}
denote the traces of
u_{A}
on
\partial\Omega
in
\Omega
and in
A\setminus\Omega
respectively. That is

\int_{A}\nabla u_{A}\cdot\nabla\varphi\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}+\beta\int_{\partial A}u_{A}\varphi\,\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=\int_{\Omega}f\varphi\,d\mathcal{L}^{n},

(1.3)

for all
\varphi\in W^{1,2}(A)
. The Robin boundary condition represents the case when the heat transfer with the environment is conveyed by convection.

If for any couple
(A,v)
with
A
an open bounded set with Lipschitz boundary containing
\Omega
and
v\in W^{1,2}(A)
,
v\geq 0
in
A
, we identify
v
with
v\chi_{A}
, where
\chi_{A}
is the characteristic function of
A
, and the set
A
with the support of
v
, then the energy functional (
1.1
) becomes

\mathcal{F}(v)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\lvert\nabla v\rvert^{2}\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}+\beta\int_{J_{v}}\left(\overline{v}^{2}+\underline{v}^{2}\right)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}-2\int_{\Omega}fv\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}+C_{0}\mathcal{L}^{n}(\set{v>0}\setminus\Omega),

(1.4)

and the minimization problem (
1.2
) becomes

\inf\Set{\mathcal{F}(v)}{v\in\sbv^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap W^{1,2}(\Omega)},

(1.5)

where
\overline{v}
and
\underline{v}
are respectively the approximate upper and lower limits of
v
,
J_{v}
is the jump set and
\nabla v
is the absolutely continuous part of the derivative of
v
. See
Section 2
for the definitions.

We state the main results of this paper in the two following theorems.

.

, let

)

Definition 2.2
.

Let

Definition 2.3

(Approximate upper and lower limits)
.

Let

be a measurable function. We define the

of

\overline{u}(x)=\inf\Set{t\in\mathbb{R}}{\limsup_{r\to 0^{+}}\dfrac{\mathcal{L}^{n}(B_{r}(x)\cap\set{u>t})}{\mathcal{L}^{n}(B_{r}(x))}=0},

We define the

of

J_{u}=\Set{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\underline{u}(x)<\overline{u}(x)}.

We denote by
K_{u}
the closure of

.

If
\overline{u}(x)=\underline{u}(x)=l
, we say that

is the approximate limit of
u
as

tends to

, and we have that, for any

\limsup_{r\to 0^{+}}\dfrac{\mathcal{L}^{n}(B_{r}(x)\cap\set{\lvert u-l\rvert\geq\varepsilon)}}{\mathcal{L}^{n}(B_{r}(x))}=0.

If

, the jump set

is a
(n-1)
-rectifiable set, i.e.
{J_{u}\subseteq\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}M_{i}}

and

H^{-}_{\nu_{u}}=\set{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\nu_{u}(x)\cdot(y-x)\leq 0}

Definition 2.4
.

Let

be a measurable set and let

See for instance

Lemma 3.1

Let

\lVert v\rVert_{2}\leq C.

(3.2)

Proof.

Let

\begin{split}0\geq\mathcal{F}(v)&\geq\,\lambda_{\beta}\left(B^{\prime}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v^{2}\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}-2\int_{\Omega}fv\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}\\

By

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
g:[0,t_{1}]\to[0,+\infty)
be an increasing, absolutely continuous function such that

Precisely,

Proof.

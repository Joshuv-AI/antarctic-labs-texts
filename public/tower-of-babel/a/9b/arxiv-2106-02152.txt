Many-body excitations in trapped Bose gas: A non-Hermitian view Thanks: The work of the second and third authors (DM and SS) was partially supported by Grant No. 1517162 of the Division of Mathematical Sciences (DMS) of the National Science Foundation (NSF).
Manoussos G. Grillakis, Dionisios Margetis, Stephen Sorokanich

Abstract
We provide the analysis of a physically motivated model for a trapped dilute Bose gas with repulsive pairwise atomic interactions at zero temperature. Our goal is to describe aspects of the excited many-body quantum states by accounting for the scattering of atoms in pairs from the macroscopic state (condensate). We formally construct a many-body Hamiltonian, ℋ app \mathcal{H}_{\text{app}} , that is quadratic in the Boson field operators for noncondensate atoms. This ℋ app \mathcal{H}_{\text{app}} conserves the total number of atoms. Inspired by Wu ( J. Math. Phys. , 2:105–123, 1961), we apply a non-unitary transformation to ℋ app \mathcal{H}_{\text{app}} . Key in this non-Hermitian view is the pair-excitation kernel, which in operator form obeys a Riccati equation. In the stationary case, we develop an existence theory for solutions to this operator equation by a variational approach. We connect this theory to the one-particle excitation wave functions heuristically derived by Fetter ( Ann. Phys. , 70:67–101, 1972). These functions solve an eigenvalue problem for a J J -self-adjoint operator. From the non-Hermitian Hamiltonian, we derive a one-particle nonlocal equation for low-lying excitations, describe its solutions, and recover Fetter’s excitation spectrum. Our approach leads to a description of the excited eigenstates of the reduced Hamiltonian in the N N -particle sector of Fock space.

Introduction

In Bose-Einstein condensation (BEC) integer-spin particles (Bosons) occupy en masse a single-particle macroscopic quantum state, known as the ‘condensate’, at extremely low temperatures. The first experimental realizations of BEC in trapped atomic gases in 1995
Anderson1995
;
Ketterle1995

Cornell-rev2002

Ketterle-rev2002

Dalfovoetal1999

Fetter2009

The dilute atomic gas is amenable to a systematic analysis mainly because of the length scale separation inherent to this system

, of each atom. For many experimental situations, it is reasonable to assume that

. If a trapping potential is applied externally, another length scale is the linear size of the trap, which can be of the same order as or larger than
\ell_{\text{dB}}

, and a macroscopic quantum state may exist if
\ell\lesssim\ell_{\text{dB}}

The realization of BEC in atomic gases has sparked various investigations in the modeling and analysis of nonlinear dynamics and out-of-equilibrium phenomena in Boson systems

In this context, the usual mean field approach

1.1
Motivation: Why a non-Hermitian view?

The reader may raise the following question: What is our motivation for focusing on the non-Hermitian approach of
wu61
;
wu98
? After all, non-unitary transformations are often deemed as mathematically hard to deal with. Our motivation is twofold.

First, from a physics perspective, it can be argued that the formalism involving the pair-excitation kernel is a natural extension of the systematic treatment by Lee, Huang and Yang for the setting with translation invariance and periodic boundary conditions
leehuangyang
. In their case, the eigenvectors of the many-body Hamiltonian can be approximately expressed in terms of the action of a non-unitary operator,
e^{\mathcal{K}}
, on finite superpositions of tensor products of one-particle momentum (
\bm{p}
) states
leehuangyang
. The exponent
\mathcal{K}
is of the form
leehuangyang
;
wu61

\mathcal{K}=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\bm{p}\neq 0}\alpha(\bm{p})\,a_{\bm{p}}^{*}a_{-\bm{p}}^{*}~,

where
a_{\bm{p}}
(
a_{\bm{p}}^{*}
) is the annihilation (creation) operator at one-particle momentum
\bm{p}
,
\bm{p}\in(2\pi/L)\mathbb{Z}^{3}
and
L
is the linear size of the periodic box. The function
\alpha(\bm{p})
, where
\alpha:(2\pi/L)\mathbb{Z}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
, yields the phonon spectrum. In the above, the operator
a_{0}
was replaced by
\sqrt{N}
times the identity operator, which amounts to the Bogoliubov approximation in the periodic setting
Seiringer
. Each term in the series for
\mathcal{K}
describes the excitation of particles from the condensate to pairs of opposite momenta.

Adopting Wu’s extension of the above treatment to non-translation invariant settings
wu61
;
wu98
, in the case of stationary states we consider a Hamiltonian that conserves the total number of atoms. In addition, we replace the exponent
\mathcal{K}
by an integral,
\mathcal{W}
, over
\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
. This integral involves the pair excitation kernel,
k
, a symmetric function of two spatial variables (
k:\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
), viz.,

\mathcal{W}=-(2N)^{-1}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y\ a_{x}^{\ast}a_{y}^{\ast}\,k(x,y)\,a(\overline{\phi})^{2}.

Here,
a_{x}^{\ast}
is the Boson field creation operator at position
x
,
\phi
denotes the condensate wave function (
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
),
a(\bar{\phi})
is the Boson field annihilation operator for the single-particle state
\phi
, and
\phi
is assumed to be orthogonal to
k
; see Sect.
1.3
. The kernel
k
is found to obey a nonlocal and nonlinear PDE
wu61
;
wu98
. In this paper, we rigorously study the existence of stationary solutions to this PDE and explore possible implications. Note that
a(\overline{\phi})
is not treated as a
c
-number within our approach.

We show that the formalism based on the operator
\mathcal{W}
yields an excitation energy spectrum for the Bose gas in agreement with the one derived by Fetter
fetter72
. His approach invokes a Bogoliubov-type rotation of Boson field operators in the space orthogonal to
\phi
which keeps intact the Hermiticity of the many-body Hamiltonian. Fetter’s Hamiltonian does not commute with the particle number operator. Here, we place emphasis on the pair-excitation kernel,
k
, in the context of a non-Hermitian Hamiltonian that conserves the total number of particles. We
explicitly construct the excited many-body eigenstates in the appropriate (
N
-particle) sector of Fock space
.

Another reason for our choice of the non-Hermitian view is that this illustrates, and exploits, previously unnoticed connections of abstract operator theory to phonon-like excitations in Boson dynamics. In our analysis we identify the governing equation for
k
with an operator Riccati equation. The latter has been studied extensively by Albeverio and coworkers in an abstract context
AlbeverioMotovilov2019
;
Albeverio2009
;
AlbeverioMotovilov2010
; see also
Tretter2016
;
Kostrykin03-chap
;
Tretter-book
. Our existence theory for this equation, based on a variational approach, seems to differ from existence proofs found in these works. Since we focus on
k
, our formalism has a different flavor from the variational approaches for operator matrices in
Tretter-book
. The Riccati equation for
k
here is inherent to the non-Hermitian formalism for the Boson system
wu61
. We rigorously establish that: The excitation spectrum by Fetter’s approach
fetter72
comes from the eigenvalue problem for a
J
-self-adjoint operator intimately connected to the equation for
k
from Wu’s treatment
wu61
;
wu98
. In our analysis, we use a regularized interaction potential in the place of the delta-function potential used in
fetter72
;
wu61
;
wu98
. Our findings for the excitation spectrum are independent of the particle-conserving (or not) character of the quadratic Hamiltonian. In contrast, the construction of the many-body eigenstates relies on the particle number conservation.

On related past works

The quantum dynamics of the Bose gas has been the subject of numerous studies. It is impossible to exhaustively list this bibliography. Here, we make an attempt to place our work in the appropriate context of the existing literature. For a broad view on Boson dynamics, the interested reader may consult, e.g.,

Hence, vectors in
\mathbb{F}
are described as sequences
\{u^{n}\}
of
n
-particle wave functions where
u^{n}\in L^{2}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3n})
,
n\geq 0
. The inner product of
|u\rangle=\{u^{n}\},|w\rangle=\{w^{n}\}\in\mathbb{F}
is

\langle u,w\rangle_{\mathbb{F}}:=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\langle\overline{u}^{n},w^{n}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3n})}}~,

which induces the norm
\||u\rangle\|=\sqrt{\langle u,u\rangle_{\mathbb{F}}}
.
We employ the bra-ket notation for Schrödinger state vectors in
\mathbb{F}
to distinguish them from wave functions in
L^{2}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3n})
. We often write the inner product of
|u\rangle
with
\mathcal{A}|w\rangle
(
\mathcal{A}:\mathbb{F}\mapsto\mathbb{F}
) as
\langle u|\mathcal{A}|w\rangle
. The vacuum state in
\mathbb{F}
is
|vac\rangle:={\{1,0,0,\dots\}}
, where the unity is placed in the zeroth slot. A symmetric
N
-particle wave function,
\psi_{N}\in L^{2}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3N})
, has a natural embedding into
\mathbb{F}
given by
|\psi\rangle_{N}=\{0,0,\dots,\psi_{N}(x),0,\dots\}
, where
\psi_{N}(x)
is in the
N
-th slot. The set of such state vectors
|\psi\rangle_{N}
is the ‘
N
-th fiber’ (
N
-particle sector) of
\mathbb{F}
, denoted
\mathbb{F}_{N}
. We sometimes omit the subscript ‘
N
’ in
|\psi\rangle_{N}
, simply writing
|\psi\rangle
.

\bullet

A Hamiltonian on
L_{s}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3N})
admits an extension to an operator on
\mathbb{F}
. This extension is carried out via the Bosonic field operator
a_{x}
and its adjoint,
a_{x}^{\ast}
, which are indexed by the spatial coordinate
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
. To define these field operators, first consider the annihilation and creation operators for a one-particle state
f\in\mathfrak{h}
, denoted by
a(\overline{f})
and
a^{\ast}(f)
. These operators act on
|u\rangle=\{u^{n}\}\in\mathbb{F}
according to

\big(a(\overline{f})|u\rangle\big)^{n}:=\sqrt{n+1}\int{{\rm d}x\,\overline{f(x)}\,u^{n+1}(x,x_{2},\dots,x_{n})}~,

\big(a^{\ast}(f)|u\rangle\big)^{n}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{j\leq n}{f(x_{j})\,u^{n-1}(x_{1},\dots,x_{j-1},x_{j+1},\dots,x_{n})}~.

We often use the symbols
a_{\overline{f}}:=a(\overline{f})
and
a^{*}_{f}:=a^{*}(f)
. Also, given an orthonormal basis,
\{e_{j}(x)\}_{j}\subset\mathfrak{h}
, we will write
a_{j}^{\ast}
in place of
a^{\ast}(e_{j})
and
a_{j}
in place of
a(\overline{e_{j}})
.

\bullet

The Boson field operators
a_{x}^{\ast},\,a_{x}
are now implicitly defined via the integrals

a^{\ast}_{f}=\int{{\rm d}x\,\big\{f(x)\,a_{x}^{\ast}\big\}}~,\quad a_{\overline{f}}=\int{{\rm d}x\,\big\{\overline{f(x)}\,a_{x}\big\}}~.

By the orthonormal basis
\{e_{j}(x)\}_{j}
, the field operators are expressed by

a^{\ast}_{x}=\sum_{j}{e_{j}(x)\,a^{\ast}_{j}}~,\quad a_{x}=\sum_{j}{\overline{e_{j}(x)}\,a_{j}}~.

The canonical commutation relations

[a_{x},a_{y}^{\ast}]=\delta(x-y)
,
[a_{x},a_{y}]=0
then follow.

\bullet

The Boson field operators orthogonal to the condensate
\phi\in\mathfrak{h}
are defined by

a_{\perp,x}=\int{\rm d}y\,\left\{\widehat{\delta}(x,y)a_{y}\right\}=\int{\rm d}y\,\left\{a_{y}\widehat{\delta}^{T}(y,x)\right\}~,

a^{\ast}_{\perp,x}=\int{\rm d}y\,\left\{\widehat{\delta}^{T}(x,y)a^{\ast}_{y}\right\}=\int{\rm d}y\,\left\{a^{\ast}_{y}\widehat{\delta}(y,x)\right\}~.

We can decompose the Boson field operators according to the equations

a_{x}=a_{\perp,x}+\phi(x)a_{\overline{\phi}}~,\quad a^{\ast}_{x}=a_{\perp,x}^{\ast}+\overline{\phi(x)}a^{\ast}_{\phi}~.

(1)

It is worthwhile to notice the commutation relations

\big[a_{\perp,x},a^{\ast}_{\perp,y}\big]=\widehat{\delta}(x,y)~,\ \big[a^{\ast}_{\perp,x},a_{\perp,y}\big]=-\widehat{\delta}^{T}(x,y)~,\ \big[a_{\overline{\phi}},a^{\ast}_{\perp,x}\big]=\big[a_{\perp,x},a^{\ast}_{\phi}\big]=0~.

\bullet

Fock space operators such as the full many-body Hamiltonian,
\mathcal{H}
, are primarily denoted by calligraphic letters. Some exceptions pertain to annihilation and creation operators including the field operators
a_{x},\,a^{\ast}_{x},\,a_{\perp,x},\,a_{\perp,x}^{\ast}
as well as the operators
a_{\overline{\phi}},a^{*}_{\phi}
and
a_{j},a_{j}^{\ast}
associated with the basis
\{e_{j}(x)\}_{j}\subset\mathfrak{h}
.

\bullet

Functionals on Banach spaces are often denoted also by calligraphic letters. (Their distinction from Fock space operators is self evident.)

2
Hamiltonian model, main results, and methodology

In this section, we introduce the full many-body Hamiltonian, and summarize our results and approach. The more precise statements of results along with derivations or proofs can be found in the corresponding sections, as specified below.

The starting point is the many-body Hamiltonian in Fock space, viz.,

\mathcal{H}=\int{\rm d}x{\rm d}y\,\left\{a^{\ast}_{x}\epsilon(x,y)a_{y}+\frac{1}{2}a^{\ast}_{x}a^{\ast}_{y}\upsilon(x-y)a_{x}a_{y}\right\}~,

(2)

where
\epsilon(x,y)=\left\{-\Delta_{x}+V(x)\right\}\delta(x,y)
is the kinetic part,
\upsilon(x)
is the pairwise interaction potential, and
V(x)
is the trapping potential. We assume that
\upsilon(x)
is positive, symmetric, integrable and bounded on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. The trapping potential
V(x)
is positive and such that the one-particle Schrödinger operator
-\Delta+V
has discrete spectrum.

2.1
Reduced Hamiltonian and operator Riccati equation for
k

Section
3
describes Wu’s approach
wu98
in a language closer to operator theory, which serves our objectives.
By heuristics, we reduce Hamiltonian (
2
) to the quadratic form

\mathcal{H}_{\text{app}}=NE_{\text{H}}+h(a_{\perp}^{\ast},a_{\perp})+\frac{1}{2N}f_{\phi}(a_{\perp}^{\ast},a_{\perp}^{\ast})a_{\overline{\phi}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2N}\overline{f_{\phi}}(a_{\perp},a_{\perp}){a_{\phi}^{\ast}}^{2}~,

where
E_{H}
is the (mean field) Hartree energy per particle,
h(a_{\perp}^{\ast},a_{\perp})
and
\overline{f_{\phi}}(a_{\perp},a_{\perp})
are operators of the form
\int{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y\,\{a_{\perp,x}^{\ast}h(x,y)a_{\perp,y}\}
and
\int{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y\,\{a_{\perp,x}\overline{f_{\phi}(x,y)}a_{\perp,y}\}
for suitable kernels
h(x,y)
and
f_{\phi}(x,y)
, and
\phi
is the condensate wave function; see Sect.
3.1
. Our derivation of the reduced Hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}_{\text{app}}
relies on (
1
) and the conservation of the particle number. This
\mathcal{H}_{\text{app}}
provides a simple model for pair excitation. Our goal is to solve the eigenvalue problem
\mathcal{H}_{\text{app}}|\psi\rangle=E_{N}|\psi\rangle
.

Subsequently, we transform
\mathcal{H}_{\text{app}}
non-unitarily according to
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{app}}:=e^{\mathcal{W}}\mathcal{H}_{\text{app}}e^{-\mathcal{W}}
where the operator
\mathcal{W}
is of the form
-(2N)^{-1}\int{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y\ \{k(x,y)\,a_{\perp,x}^{\ast}a_{\perp,y}^{\ast}\}a_{\overline{\phi}}^{2}
which conserves the total number of particles; see Sect.
3.2
.
The Riccati equation for kernel
k
is extracted via the requirement that the non-Hermitian operator
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{app}}
does
not
contain any terms with the product
a_{\perp}^{\ast}a_{\perp}^{\ast}
; see Sect.
3.3
.
If
k(x,\overline{\phi})=0
, the Riccati equation for
k
reads

h\circ k+k\circ h^{T}+f_{\phi}+k\circ\overline{f_{\phi}}\circ k=\lambda\otimes_{\text{s}}\phi~,

where the Lagrange multiplier
\lambda
is determined self-consistently.

2.2
Existence theory for
k

In Sect.
4
, we introduce a functional of
\overline{k}
and
k
by use of which we develop an existence theory for
k
. This functional,
\mathcal{E}[\overline{k},k]:\mathrm{dom}(\mathcal{E})\to\mathbb{R}
, reads

\mathcal{E}[\overline{k},k]:=\mathrm{tr}\Big\{\big(\delta-\overline{k}\circ k\big)^{-1}\circ\Big(\overline{k}\circ h\circ k+\frac{1}{2}\overline{k}\circ f_{\phi}+\frac{1}{2}\overline{f_{\phi}}\circ k\Big)\Big\}~;

see Sect.
4.1
for the definition of
\mathrm{dom}(\mathcal{E})
. Setting the functional derivative of
\mathcal{E}[\overline{k},k]
with respect to
\overline{k}
equal to zero yields the Riccati equation for
k
.

We prove the existence of solutions to the Riccati equation for
k
by assuming that

\displaystyle h(\overline{e},e)-\big|f_{\phi}(\overline{e},\overline{e})\big|\geq c\|e\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\quad\quad\forall e\in\phi^{\perp}=\big\{e\in\mathfrak{h}^{1}_{V}\ \big|\ e\perp\phi\big\}~.

In particular, this condition is satisfied if
\phi
is a minimizer of the Hartree energy,
E_{H}
.
The aforementioned inequality is employed as a hypothesis in the main existence theorem, Theorem
4.1
(Sect.
4.2
). In fact, Theorem
4.1
states that the above inequality and the property that
f_{\phi}
is Hilbert-Schmidt imply that the functional
\mathcal{E}
restricted to
\mathrm{dom}(\mathcal{E})_{\perp}=\mathrm{dom}(\mathcal{E})\cap\{k\in\mathfrak{B}_{2}(\mathfrak{h}^{1}_{V})\,\big|\,k(x,\overline{\phi})=0\}
attains a minimum for some
k\in\mathrm{dom}(\mathcal{E})_{\perp}
which is a weak solution to the operator Riccati equation. We emphasize that
\phi
does not need to be a minimizer of the Hartree energy. Our proof makes use of a basis of
\phi^{\perp}
, the theory of complex (
\mathcal{C}
-) symmetric operators and a variational principle based on functional
\mathcal{E}
. In Sect.
4.3
, we discuss an implication of our existence theory, namely, the non-uniqueness of solutions to the Riccati equation.

2.3
Spectrum and eigenvectors of reduced non-Hermitian Hamiltonian

In Sect.
5
, we study the eigenvectors and spectrum of the non-unitarily transformed Hamiltonian
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{app}}
, under the assumptions of Theorem
4.1
for
k
. A highlight of our analysis is the explicit construction of these eigenvectors in
\mathbb{F}_{N}
by Fock space techniques. We write
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{app}}=NE_{H}+\mathcal{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm ph}
where

\displaystyle\mathcal{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm ph}

\displaystyle:=h_{\rm ph}\big(a^{\ast}_{\perp},a_{\perp}\big)+\frac{1}{N}(a^{\ast}_{\phi})^{2}\overline{f_{\phi}}\big(a_{\perp},a_{\perp}\big)~;

\displaystyle h_{\rm ph}\big(a^{\ast}_{\perp},a_{\perp}\big)

\displaystyle:=\int{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y\ \{a_{\perp,x}^{\ast}(h+k\circ\overline{f_{\phi}})(x,y)a_{\perp,y}\}~.

Evidently,
h_{\rm ph}(a^{\ast}_{\perp},a_{\perp})
forms the diagonal part of
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{app}}-NE_{H}
. We show that
h_{\rm ph}
is responsible for the discrete phonon-like excitation spectrum of the trapped Bose gas.

The main result is captured by a theorem (Theorem
5.1
), according to which the following equality of spectra holds:

\sigma\left(\mathcal{H}_{\rm ph}\big|_{\mathbb{F}_{N}}\right)=\sigma\left(h_{\rm ph}(a^{\ast}_{\perp},a_{\perp})\big|_{\mathbb{F}_{N}}\right)~.

Furthermore, in this theorem we show that for every eigenvector of
h_{\text{ph}}(a_{\perp}^{\ast},a_{\perp})
with eigenvalue
E
there is a unique eigenvector of
\mathcal{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm ph}
with the same eigenvalue,
E
.

Our analysis is based on the following steps. First, we provide a formalism for the decomposition of
\mathbb{F}_{N}
into appropriate orthogonal subspaces (Sect.
5.1
). Our technique is similar to that in the construction by Lewin, Nam, Serfaty and Solovej
Lewin2014
. However, here we consider the eigenvectors of a Hamiltonian that conserves the number,
N
, of particles as opposed to the Bogoliubov Hamiltonian studied in
Lewin2014
. Second, we show that by the restriction
\|k\|_{\text{op}}<1
, the spectrum of the one-particle Schrödinger-type operator
h_{\text{ph}}
is positive and discrete, and the corresponding eigenfunctions form a non-orthogonal Riesz basis of
\phi^{\perp}
(Sect.
5.2
). The proof of the main theorem (Theorem
5.1
) relies on the above steps to show that the eigenvalue problem for
\mathcal{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm ph}
can be reduced to a finite-dimensional system of equations that has an upper triangular form (Sect.
5.3
).

2.4
Connection of non-Hermitian and Hermitian approaches

In Sect.
6
, we compare our approach to Fetter’s formalism
fetter72
. In particular, we prove the existence of solutions to a PDE system for one-particle excitations, which reduces to Fetter’s system
fetter72
when the interaction potential
\upsilon
is replaced by
g\delta
for some constant
g>0
. To this end, we assume that a solution to the operator Riccati equation exists. In this vein, we discuss the connection of the Riccati equation for
k
to the theory of
J
-self-adjoint matrix operators by Albeverio and coworkers
AlbeverioMotovilov2019
;
Albeverio2009
;
AlbeverioMotovilov2010
.

Starting with the relevant Bogoliubov Hamiltonian
fetter72
, we indicate that its diagonalization via “quasiparticle” operators (in Fetter’s terminology) leads to the PDE system (
j=1,\,2,\,\ldots
)

\begin{pmatrix}h^{T}_{\perp}&-{f_{\phi}}_{\perp}\\
\overline{{f_{\phi}}}_{\perp}&-h_{\perp}\end{pmatrix}\circ\begin{pmatrix}u_{j}(x)\\
v_{j}(x)\end{pmatrix}=E_{j}\begin{pmatrix}u_{j}(x)\\
v_{j}(x)\end{pmatrix}

for the one-particle wave functions
u_{j}
and
v_{j}
and respective eigenvalues
E_{j}
(Sect.
6.1
). Here,
q_{\perp}
(
q=h,\,f_{\phi}
) is the projection of operator
q
on space
\phi^{\perp}
. Notably, we show that the existence of solutions to the Riccati equation for
k
implies the solvability of the above system for
(u_{j},v_{j})
; see Sect.
6.2
. We also prove that the completeness relations between
u_{j}
and
v_{j}
, previously posed by Fetter
fetter72
, directly follow from our approach. In Sect.
6.3
, we invoke ideas from
J
-self-adjoint operator theory to show that the restriction
\|k\|_{\text{op}}<1
yields a positive spectrum
\{E_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
for the symplectic matrix involved in the system for
(u_{j},v_{j})
.

3
Construction of quadratic many-body Hamiltonian

In this section, we formally construct a quadratic (Hermitian) Hamiltonian and transform it non-unitarily. A core ingredient of this approach is that the number of atoms is strictly conserved. We follow the treatment of Wu
wu61
;
wu98
but replace his delta-function potential for repulsive pairwise atomic interactions by a smooth potential.

Section
3.1
focuses on heuristic approximations in the Hermitian setting, where we expand the Hamiltonian in powers of Boson field operators for noncondensate particles. Section
3.2
concerns the non-unitary transformation of the quadratic Hermitian Hamiltonian. In Sect.
3.3
, we derive a Riccati equation for the pair excitation kernel of the transformation. Section
3.4
provides some discussion on the procedure.

3.1
Reduction of Hamiltonian in Hermitian setting

In this subsection, we formally reduce the many-body Hamiltonian to a quadratic Hermitian operator in Fock space. The total number of particles is conserved. Our main result is described by (
3a
)–(
3e
) below.

We start with Hamiltonian (
2
).
Let
\phi
denote the (one-particle) condensate wave function, which has
L^{2}
-norm
\|\phi\|_{2}=1
. Recall decomposition (
1
) for the Boson field operators
a_{x}
,
a_{x}^{\ast}
. The particle number operator,
\mathcal{N}
, on
\mathbb{F}
can thus be decomposed as

\mathcal{N}=\int{\rm d}x\ \{a_{x}^{\ast}a_{x}\}=a^{\ast}_{\phi}a_{\overline{\phi}}+\int{\rm d}x\ \{a_{\perp,x}^{\ast}a_{\perp,x}\}=:\mathcal{N}_{\phi}+\mathcal{N}_{\perp}~,

where
\mathcal{N}_{\phi}:=a^{\ast}_{\phi}a_{\overline{\phi}}
is the number operator for condensate atoms;
\mathcal{N}_{\phi}
and
\mathcal{N}_{\perp}
commute, and
\mathcal{H}
commutes with
\mathcal{N}
, viz.,
[\mathcal{H},\mathcal{N}]=\mathcal{H}\mathcal{N}-\mathcal{N}\mathcal{H}=0
. We use the
N
-th fiber,
\mathbb{F}_{N}
, of the Bosonic Fock space, considering state vectors
|\psi\rangle_{N}
that satisfy

\mathcal{N}|\psi\rangle_{N}=N|\psi\rangle_{N}~;\qquad\||\psi\rangle_{N}\|=1~.

Following Wu
wu61
, we first expand
\mathcal{H}
is powers of
a_{\perp,x}
,
a_{\perp,x}^{\ast}
by applying decomposition (
1
) for
a_{x}
,
a_{x}^{\ast}
. The Hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}
reads

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y\ \left\{\overline{\phi(x)}\epsilon(x,y)\phi(y)+\frac{1}{2}(\mathcal{N}_{\phi}-1)|\phi(x)|^{2}\upsilon(x-y)|\phi(y)|^{2}\right\}\mathcal{N}_{\phi}

\displaystyle\mbox{}+\int{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y\ \left\{a_{\perp,x}^{\ast}\Big(\epsilon(x,y)\phi(y)+(\mathcal{N}_{\phi}-1)\phi(x)\upsilon(x-y)|\phi(y)|^{2}\Big)a_{\overline{\phi}}\right\}

\displaystyle\mbox{}+\int{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y\ \left\{a^{\ast}_{\phi}\Big(\overline{\phi(x)}\epsilon(x,y)+(\mathcal{N}_{\phi}-1)\overline{\phi(y)}\upsilon(x-y)|\phi(x)|^{2}\Big)a_{\perp,y}\right\}

\displaystyle\mbox{}+\int{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y\ \left\{a_{\perp,x}^{\ast}\Big(\epsilon(x,y)+\mathcal{N}_{\phi}\,(\upsilon\ast|\phi|^{2})(x)\,\delta(x,y)+\mathcal{N}_{\phi}\phi(x)\upsilon(x-y)\overline{\phi(y)}\Big)a_{\perp,y}\right\}

\displaystyle\mbox{}+\frac{1}{2}\int{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y\ \left\{a_{\perp,x}^{\ast}a^{\ast}_{\perp,y}\phi(x)\upsilon(x-y)\phi(y)a_{\overline{\phi}}^{2}+{a^{\ast}_{\phi}}^{2}\overline{\phi(x)}\upsilon(x-y)\overline{\phi(y)}a_{\perp,x}a_{\perp,y}\right\}

\displaystyle\mbox{}+\int{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y\ \left\{a_{\perp,x}^{\ast}a^{\ast}_{\perp,y}\upsilon(x-y)\phi(y)a_{\perp,x}a_{\overline{\phi}}+a^{\ast}_{\phi}a_{\perp,x}^{\ast}\overline{\phi(y)}\upsilon(x-y)a_{\perp,x}a_{\perp,y}\right\}

\displaystyle\mbox{}+\frac{1}{2}\int{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y\ \left\{a_{\perp,x}^{\ast}a^{\ast}_{\perp,y}\upsilon(x-y)a_{\perp,x}a_{\perp,y}\right\}~.

Recall that
\epsilon(x,y)=\left\{-\Delta_{x}+V(x)\right\}\delta(x,y)
.

The next step is to reduce
\mathcal{H}
to a Hermitian operator quadratic in
a_{\perp}
,
a_{\perp}^{\ast}
. First, we drop the terms that are cubic or quartic in
a_{\perp}
,
a^{\ast}_{\perp}
. Second, we make the substitution
\mathcal{N}_{\phi}=\mathcal{N}-\mathcal{N}_{\perp}
and replace
\mathcal{N}
by
N
(
\mathcal{N}\mapsto N
with
N\gg 1
) because
|\psi\rangle\in\mathbb{F}_{N}
. We then drop the term
\mathcal{N}_{\perp}^{2}
. We take
N-1\simeq N
and
apply a Hartree-type equation
for the condensate wave function
\phi
which we write as

\int{\rm d}y\ \left\{\epsilon(x,y)\phi(y)+N\phi(x)\upsilon(x-y)|\phi(y)|^{2}\right\}-\mu\phi(x)=0~.

This results in the elimination of terms linear in
a_{\perp}
,
a_{\perp}^{\ast}
in the Hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}
. The multiplier
\mu
enables us to impose the normalization constraint
\|\phi\|_{2}=1
; thus,

\mu=\int{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y\ \left\{\overline{\phi(x)}\epsilon(x,y)\phi(y)+N|\phi(x)|^{2}\upsilon(x-y)|\phi(y)|^{2}\right\}~.

The PDE for
\phi
formally becomes the Gross-Pitaevskii equation
Gross61
;
Pitaevskii61
if
\upsilon
is replaced by
g\delta
for some constant
g>0
.

Consequently, the original Hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}
is reduced to the quadratic form

\mathcal{H}_{\text{app}}=NE_{\text{H}}+h(a_{\perp}^{\ast},a_{\perp})+\frac{1}{2N}f_{\phi}(a_{\perp}^{\ast},a_{\perp}^{\ast})a_{\overline{\phi}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2N}\overline{f_{\phi}}(a_{\perp},a_{\perp}){a_{\phi}^{\ast}}^{2}

(3a)

where, abusing notation slightly, we define the operators

\displaystyle h(a_{\perp}^{\ast},a_{\perp})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\int{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y\ \left\{a_{\perp,x}^{\ast}h(x,y)a_{\perp,y}\right\}~,

(3b)

\displaystyle f_{\phi}(a_{\perp}^{\ast},a_{\perp}^{\ast})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\int{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y\ \left\{a_{\perp,x}^{\ast}f_{\phi}(x,y)a_{\perp,y}^{\ast}\right\}~,

(3c)

along with the corresponding kernels

\displaystyle h(x,y)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\epsilon(x,y)+N(\upsilon\ast|\phi|^{2})(x)\,\delta(x,y)+N\gamma(x,y)-\mu~,

(3d)

\displaystyle f_{\phi}(x,y)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle N\phi(x)\upsilon(x-y)\phi(y)~,\quad\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\gamma(x,y):=\phi(x)\upsilon(x-y)\overline{\phi(y)}~.

(3e)

In the above, the Hartree energy functional,
E_{\text{H}}
, is defined by

E_{\text{H}}=\int{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y\ \left\{\overline{\phi(x)}\epsilon(x,y)\phi(y)+\frac{N}{2}|\phi(x)|^{2}\upsilon(x-y)|\phi(y)|^{2}\right\}~.

Equation (
3a
) is the desired quadratic Hamiltonian. Note the key property

[\mathcal{H}_{\text{app}},\mathcal{N}]=0~.

3.2
Non-unitary transformation of quadratic Hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}_{\text{app}}

In this subsection, we transform
\mathcal{H}_{\text{app}}
non-unitarily by use of the pair-excitation kernel,
k
. The main result is given by (
5a
) and (
5b
) below.

For this purpose, we invoke the following quadratic operator:

\mathcal{K}:=-\frac{1}{2}\int{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y\ \left\{k(x,y)a^{\ast}_{\perp,x}a^{\ast}_{\perp,y}\right\}~,

(4a)

where
k=k^{T}
. This
\mathcal{K}
does not conserve the number of particles (
[\mathcal{K},\mathcal{N}]\neq 0
). In addition, following Wu
wu61
, we introduce the operator

\mathcal{W}:=-\frac{1}{2N}\int{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y\ \{k(x,y)a_{\perp,x}^{\ast}a_{\perp,y}^{\ast}\}(a_{\overline{\phi}})^{2}=\frac{1}{N}\mathcal{K}(a_{\overline{\phi}})^{2}~.

(4b)

The kernel
k
is not known at this stage, but must satisfy certain consistency conditions (see Sect.
3.3
). We refrain from specifying the function space of
k
now. A salient point of this formalism is the identity
[\mathcal{W},\mathcal{N}]=0
. Consequently, the operator
e^{\mathcal{W}}
, which is used to define the non-unitary transformation of
\mathcal{H}_{\text{app}}
below, leaves
\mathbb{F}_{N}
invariant, i.e.,
e^{\mathcal{W}}:\mathbb{F}_{N}\mapsto\mathbb{F}_{N}
. (However,
e^{\mathcal{W}}
does not respect the Fock space norm.) Our goal here is to describe the non-Hermitian operator
e^{\mathcal{W}}\mathcal{H}_{\text{app}}e^{-\mathcal{W}}
.

The main idea concerning the proposed non-unitary transformation of
\mathcal{H}_{\text{app}}
can be described as follows. Assume that
|\psi\rangle_{N}=|\psi\rangle
(
|\psi\rangle\in\mathbb{F}_{N}
) is an eigenvector of the (Hermitian) Hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}_{\text{app}}
with eigenvalue
E
, viz.,

\mathcal{H}_{\text{app}}|\psi\rangle=E|\psi\rangle
. Then, we have

\left\{e^{\mathcal{W}}\mathcal{H}_{\text{app}}e^{-\mathcal{W}}\right\}\big(e^{\mathcal{W}}|\psi\rangle\big)=E\big(e^{\mathcal{W}}|\psi\rangle\big)~.

Hence, the non-Hermitian, non-unitarily transformed, operator
e^{\mathcal{W}}\mathcal{H}_{\text{app}}e^{-\mathcal{W}}
has eigenvalue
E
and eigenvector
e^{\mathcal{W}}|\psi\rangle
. It turns out that it is more tractable (in a certain sense, as shown below) to describe the transformed eigenvector
e^{\mathcal{W}}|\psi\rangle
in
\mathbb{F}_{N}
than the original vector
|\psi\rangle
by exploiting spectral properties of
e^{\mathcal{W}}\mathcal{H}_{\text{app}}e^{-\mathcal{W}}
. A price that one must pay for this option is that the pair-excitation kernel
k
must satisfy the operator Riccati equation. One of our major goals here is to motivate the equation obeyed by
k
through the computation of the non-Hermitian operator
e^{\mathcal{W}}\mathcal{H}_{\text{app}}e^{-\mathcal{W}}
.

Next, we organize our calculation. First, we readily compute the conjugation

e^{\mathcal{W}}a_{\perp,x}e^{-\mathcal{W}}=a_{\perp,x}+\frac{1}{N}\widehat{k}^{T}(a^{\ast}_{\perp},x)\big(a_{\overline{\phi}}\big)^{2}~,

where (abusing notation) we define

\displaystyle\widehat{k}^{T}(x,y)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\int{\rm d}z\ \left\{k(x,z)\widehat{\delta}^{T}(z,y)\right\}~,

\displaystyle\widehat{k}^{T}(a^{\ast}_{\perp},x)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\int{\rm d}y\,{\rm d}z\ \left\{a^{\ast}_{\perp,y}k(y,z)\widehat{\delta}^{T}(z,x)\right\}~.

In a similar vein, by virtue of (
4a
) we compute

e^{\mathcal{W}}a_{\phi}^{\ast}e^{-\mathcal{W}}=a_{\phi}^{\ast}+\frac{2}{N}\mathcal{K}a_{\overline{\phi}}~.

In order to obtain a symmetric equation in the end, we symmetrize
h(a_{\perp}^{\ast},a_{\perp})
as

h(a_{\perp}^{\ast},a_{\perp})=\frac{1}{2}\left\{h(a_{\perp}^{\ast},a_{\perp})+h^{T}(a_{\perp},a_{\perp}^{\ast})\right\}+c_{\infty}~,

where
c_{\infty}
is an (infinite) immaterial constant. This constant is harmless since it is added and subtracted. In fact, we remove this
c_{\infty}
after we perform the calculation.

We proceed to carry out the computation of
e^{\mathcal{W}}\mathcal{H}_{\text{app}}e^{-\mathcal{W}}
. To avoid overly cumbersome expressions, we only display the manipulation of key terms of
\mathcal{H}_{\text{app}}
, for illustration purposes. We refrain from presenting the explicit computation of all terms.

The main term that we need to compute reads

\displaystyle\int{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y\ \left\{e^{\mathcal{W}}\Big((a^{\ast}_{\phi})^{2}a_{\perp,x}a_{\perp,y}\Big)e^{-\mathcal{W}}\frac{1}{N}\overline{f_{\phi}(x,y)}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{(a^{\ast}_{\phi})^{2}+\frac{2}{N}\mathcal{K}(2\mathcal{N}_{\phi}-1)+\frac{4}{N^{2}}\mathcal{K}^{2}(a_{\overline{\phi}})^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle\times\int{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y\ \left(a_{\perp,x}+\frac{1}{N}\widehat{k}^{T}(a^{\ast}_{\perp},x)(a_{\overline{\phi}})^{2}\right)\frac{1}{N}\overline{f_{\phi}(x,y)}\left(a_{\perp,y}+\frac{1}{N}\widehat{k}(y,a^{\ast}_{\perp})(a_{\overline{\phi}})^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{(a^{\ast}_{\phi})^{2}+\frac{2}{N}\mathcal{K}(2\mathcal{N}_{\phi}-1)+\frac{4}{N^{2}}\mathcal{K}^{2}(a_{\overline{\phi}})^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle\times\left\{\frac{1}{N}\overline{f_{\phi}}(a_{\perp},a_{\perp})+\frac{1}{N^{2}}\left((\widehat{k}^{T}\circ\overline{f_{\phi}})(a^{\ast}_{\perp},a_{\perp})+(\overline{f_{\phi}}\circ\widehat{k})(a_{\perp},a^{\ast}_{\perp})\right)(a_{\overline{\phi}})^{2}\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\left.+\frac{1}{N^{3}}(\widehat{k}^{T}\circ\overline{f}\circ\widehat{k})(a^{\ast}_{\perp},a^{\ast}_{\perp})(a_{\overline{\phi}})^{4}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a^{\ast}_{\phi})^{2}\frac{1}{N}\overline{f_{\phi}}(a_{\perp},a_{\perp})+\frac{1}{N^{2}}\left\{(\widehat{k}^{T}\circ\overline{f_{\phi}})(a^{\ast}_{\perp},a_{\perp})+(\overline{f_{\phi}}\circ\widehat{k})(a_{\perp},a^{\ast}_{\perp})\right\}\mathcal{N}_{\phi}(\mathcal{N}_{\phi}-1)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{N^{3}}(\widehat{k}^{T}\circ\overline{f_{\phi}}\circ\widehat{k})(a^{\ast}_{\perp},a^{\ast}_{\perp})(a_{\overline{\phi}})^{2}(\mathcal{N}_{\phi}-2)(\mathcal{N}_{\phi}-3)

\displaystyle\qquad+{\rm higher\ order\ terms\ in\ }a_{\perp},\,a_{\perp}^{\ast}~.

The above Fock space operator can be further simplified, without distortion of its commutability with
\mathcal{N}
, via the replacement
\mathcal{N}_{\phi}=\mathcal{N}-\mathcal{N}_{\perp}\mapsto N-\mathcal{N}_{\perp}
. Subsequently, we drop terms higher than quadratic in
a_{\perp}
,
a_{\perp}^{\ast}
; and treat
N
as large so that
N-l\simeq N
if
l
is fixed. The other relevant computations are

\displaystyle e^{\mathcal{W}}h(a_{\perp}^{\ast},a_{\perp})e^{-\mathcal{W}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h(a_{\perp}^{\ast},a_{\perp})+\frac{1}{N}\big(h\circ\widehat{k}\big)(a_{\perp}^{\ast},a_{\perp}^{\ast})\,(a_{\overline{\phi}})^{2}~,

\displaystyle e^{\mathcal{W}}h^{T}(a_{\perp},a_{\perp}^{\ast})e^{-\mathcal{W}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h^{T}(a_{\perp},a_{\perp}^{\ast})+\frac{1}{N}\big(\widehat{k}^{T}\circ h^{T}\big)(a_{\perp}^{\ast},a_{\perp}^{\ast})\,(a_{\overline{\phi}})^{2}~.

Accordingly, we obtain the non-Hermitian quadratic operator

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{app}}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle e^{\mathcal{W}}\mathcal{H}_{\text{app}}e^{-\mathcal{W}}=NE_{\text{H}}+\big(h+\widehat{k}^{T}\circ\overline{f_{\phi}}\big)(a^{\ast}_{\perp},a_{\perp})+\big(h^{T}+\overline{f_{\phi}}\circ\widehat{k}\big)(a_{\perp},a^{\ast}_{\perp})

(5a)

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{N}\text{Ric}(a_{\perp}^{\ast},a_{\perp}^{\ast})\,(a_{\overline{\phi}})^{2}+\frac{1}{N}(a_{\phi}^{\ast})^{2}\,\overline{f_{\phi}}(a_{\perp},a_{\perp})~.

In the formal limit
\upsilon\to\delta
, i.e., when the interaction potential becomes a delta function, this
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{app}}
becomes the reduced transformed Hamiltonian derived in
wu98
.
The ‘Riccati kernel’ is defined by

\text{Ric}(x,y):=h\circ\widehat{\delta}\circ k+k\circ\widehat{\delta}^{T}\circ h^{T}+f_{\phi}+k\circ\widehat{\delta}^{T}\circ\overline{f_{\phi}}\circ\widehat{\delta}\circ k~.

(5b)

Recall that the kernel
h(x,y)
is defined by (
3d
) with (
3e
), viz.,

h(x,y)=\{-\Delta_{x}+V(x)\}\delta(x,y)+N(\upsilon\ast|\phi|^{2})(x)\delta(x,y)+N\phi(x)\upsilon(x-y)\overline{\phi(y)}-\mu~.

The operator
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{app}}
is the focus of our analysis. As we anticipated, we have the identity
[\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{app}},\mathcal{N}]=0
, which enables us to seek eigenvectors of
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{app}}
in
\mathbb{F}_{N}
.

3.3
Riccati equation for
k

Next, we heuristically outline the rationale for the derivation of an equation for
k
, in the spirit of Wu
wu61
;
wu98
. This equation is described by (
6b
) below. In Sects.
4
–
6
, we rigorously study properties and implications of solutions to this equation.

By inspection of (
5a
), we see that
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{app}}-NE_{H}
consists of two types of terms: (i) Terms that contain both
a_{\perp}^{\ast}
and
a_{\perp}
, and no
a_{\overline{\phi}}
and
a_{\phi}^{\ast}
. The sum of these terms forms the ‘diagonal part’ of
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{app}}
, and can be described by use of a (nonlocal) one-particle Schrödinger operator. In the periodic setting
leehuangyang
, the use of this operator yields the phonon spectrum. (ii) Terms that contain
a_{\perp}^{\ast}
and
a_{\overline{\phi}}
, or
a_{\perp}
and
a_{\phi}^{\ast}
. In the periodic setting, it can be effortlessly argued that this second part does
not
affect the phonon spectrum
provided

\text{Ric}(a_{\perp}^{\ast},a_{\perp}^{\ast})=0
. Following Wu
wu61
;
wu98
, we require that

\text{Ric}=\lambda\otimes_{\text{s}}\phi~,

where
\otimes_{\text{s}}
denotes the symmetrized tensor product. In view of (
5b
), we thus have an equation for
k
. Here,
\lambda(x)
is arbitrary and can be chosen to satisfy a prescribed constraint involving the inner product
k(x,\overline{\phi})
. Notably, the operator
\mathcal{W}
is invariant under changes of this constraint. In other words, physical predictions are not affected by the choice of
k(x,\overline{\phi})
. For example, we can impose
k(x,\overline{\phi})=0

wu61
;
wu98
. This condition removes
\widehat{\delta}
,
\widehat{\delta}^{T}
from the related equations, which is natural since

\int{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y\ \left\{\text{Ric}(x,y)a_{\perp,x}^{\ast}a_{\perp,y}^{\ast}\right\}=\int{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y\ \left\{\big(\widehat{\delta}\circ\text{Ric}\circ\widehat{\delta}^{T}\big)(x,y)\ a_{x}^{\ast}a_{y}^{\ast}\right\}~.

The expression for
\text{Ric}(x,y)
becomes

\text{Ric}(x,y)=h\circ k+k\circ h^{T}+f_{\phi}+k\circ\overline{f_{\phi}}\circ k~.

(6a)

Consequently, the equation for
k
reads

h\circ k+k\circ h^{T}+f_{\phi}+k\circ\overline{f_{\phi}}\circ k=\lambda\otimes_{\text{s}}\phi=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\lambda\otimes\phi+\phi\otimes\lambda)~,

(6b)

where
\lambda
should be determined self-consistently. In fact,
\lambda(x)
obeys the equation

\lambda(x)=C_{1}\phi(x)+\sqrt{2}(h\circ k+k\circ h^{T}+f_{\phi}+k\circ\overline{f_{\phi}}\circ k)(x,\overline{\phi})=C_{1}\phi(x)+\sqrt{2}(k\circ h^{T}+f_{\phi})(x,\overline{\phi})

with
C_{1}=-\langle\overline{\phi},\lambda\rangle
; see Sect.
4.2
. We refer to (
6b
) as the ‘operator Riccati equation’ for
k
. In Sect.
5
, we show that this equation leads to an excitation spectrum that is identical to the one from Fetter’s formalism
fetter72
. By virtue of (
6b
), the transformed approximate Hamiltonian becomes

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{app}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle NE_{\text{H}}+\big(h+\widehat{k}^{T}\circ\overline{f_{\phi}}\big)(a^{\ast}_{\perp},a_{\perp})+\big(h^{T}+\overline{f_{\phi}}\circ\widehat{k}\big)(a_{\perp},a^{\ast}_{\perp})+\frac{1}{N}(a_{\phi}^{\ast})^{2}\,\overline{f_{\phi}}(a_{\perp},a_{\perp})~.

3.4
A few comments

It is worthwhile to comment on aspects of our heuristic procedure. First, in hindsight, it is of some interest to discuss how (
6b
) can be motivated more transparently. The main observation is that, in regard to
\mathcal{H}_{\text{app}}
, we can consider the quadratic matrix form

\int{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y\ \big(a_{\perp,x}\ ,\ a^{\ast}_{\perp,x}\big)\left(\begin{matrix}-h^{T}(x,y)&N^{-1}(a^{\ast}_{\phi})^{2}\overline{f_{\phi}}(x,y)\\
-N^{-1}f_{\phi}(x,y)(a_{\overline{\phi}})^{2}&h(x,y)\end{matrix}\right)\left(\begin{matrix}-a^{\ast}_{\perp,y}\\
a_{\perp,y}\end{matrix}\right)~.

In view of the commutability of
a_{\perp},\,a_{\perp}^{\ast}
with
a_{\overline{\phi}},\,a^{\ast}_{\phi}
we can perform the following conjugation of the above
2\times 2
matrix, assuming for simplicity that
k(x,\overline{\phi})=0
:

\displaystyle\left(\begin{matrix}\delta&0\\
N^{-1}k(a_{\overline{\phi}})^{2}&\delta\end{matrix}\right)\circ\left(\begin{matrix}-h^{T}&N^{-1}(a^{\ast}_{\phi})^{2}\overline{f_{\phi}}\\
-N^{-1}f_{\phi}(a_{\overline{\phi}})^{2}&h\end{matrix}\right)\circ\left(\begin{matrix}\delta&0\\
-N^{-1}k(a_{\overline{\phi}})^{2}&\delta\end{matrix}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{matrix}-h^{T}-N^{-2}\overline{f_{\phi}}\circ k\mathcal{N}_{\phi}(\mathcal{N}_{\phi}-1)&N^{-1}(a^{\ast}_{\phi})^{2}\overline{f_{\phi}}\\
-N^{-1}\widetilde{\rm Ric}(a_{\overline{\phi}})^{2}&h+N^{-2}k\circ\overline{f_{\phi}}\mathcal{N}_{\phi}(\mathcal{N}_{\phi}-1)\end{matrix}\right)

where

\widetilde{\rm Ric}=h\circ k+k\circ h^{T}+f_{\phi}+\frac{1}{N^{2}}k\circ\overline{f_{\phi}}\circ k\,\mathcal{N}_{\phi}(\mathcal{N}_{\phi}-1)~.

Now replace
\mathcal{N}_{\phi}
with
N
in the last expression and take
N-1\simeq N
; thus,
\widetilde{\rm Ric}
is reduced to
Ric
(with the
\widehat{\delta}
and
\widehat{\delta}^{T}
removed).
Equation (
6b
) then results from the requirement that the transformed
2\times 2
matrix is upper triangular.
We will show that this property implies that the excitation spectrum of
\mathcal{H}_{\text{app}}
coincides with the one of the diagonal part of
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{app}}
, and is identical to the spectrum of Fetter’s approach
fetter72
; see Sect.
5
.

A second comment concerns the Hartree-type equation for
\phi
, which becomes the Gross-Pitaevskii equation if
\upsilon
is replaced by
g\delta
for some constant
g>0
. We write the relevant PDE as
\mathbb{H}_{\text{H}}\phi=\mu\phi
where

\mathbb{H}_{\text{H}}:=-\Delta_{x}+V(x)+N\big(\upsilon\ast|\phi|^{2}\big)(x)

(7)

is a one-particle Hartree operator. In our analysis, we will consider the interaction potential,
\upsilon(x)
, to be positive, integrable and smooth. For a
trapping
potential
V(x)
, where
V(x)\to\infty
as
|x|\to\infty
, the condensate wave function
\phi(x)
is bounded and decays exponentially as
|x|\to\infty
.

We are tempted to loosely comment on the assumptions underlying the uncontrolled approximations for the many-body Hamiltonian in this section. We expect that the simplifications leading to the reduced Hamiltonian
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{app}}
make sense provided

\frac{\langle\psi|\mathcal{N}_{\perp}^{l}|\psi\rangle}{N^{l}}\ll 1\quad\forall\,|\psi\rangle\in\mathbb{F}_{N}~;\quad l=1,\,2,\,3,\,4~.

4
Existence theory for operator Riccati equation: Variational approach

In this section, we address the existence of solutions to (
6b
). Our analysis is partly inspired by works of Albeverio, Tretter and coworkers, e.g.,
AlbeverioMotovilov2019
;
Albeverio2009
;
AlbeverioMotovilov2010
;
Tretter2016
, who rigorously connected the operator Riccati equation to the spectral theory of
J
-self-adjoint operators. In our work, we view an existence proof for
k
as a necessary step towards ensuring the self-consistency of the approximation and non-unitary transform for the Bosonic many-body Hamiltonian. The existence proof for
k
paves the way to establishing the connection of pair excitation to the phonon spectrum in a trap (Sect.
5
).

Our theory invokes an appropriate functional,
\mathcal{E}[\overline{k},k]
, and two related lemmas (Sect.
4.1
).
A highlight is Theorem
4.1
on the existence of
k
(Sect.
4.2
). We stress that our existence proof differs significantly from the approach found in
AlbeverioMotovilov2019
;
Albeverio2009
;
AlbeverioMotovilov2010
. First, we utilize a variational approach by seeking stationary points of the functional
\mathcal{E}[\overline{k},k]
on a Hilbert space, instead of applying the fixed-point argument of
Albeverio2009
. Note that the fixed-point argument in
Albeverio2009
makes use of operator estimates that are not expected to hold for the operator
\mathrm{Ric}
of (
5b
). The variational approach developed here is amenable to constraints inherent to our problem; thus, the term
\lambda\otimes_{\text{s}}\phi
of (
6b
) emerges as a Lagrange multiplier. Alternative approaches of variational character for block operator matrices (not for the Riccati equation per se) are described in
Tretter-book
.

Second, our variational approach reveals that Riccati equation (
6b
) may in principle
not
have a unique solution. Our existence proof indicates how one can construct an infinite number of solutions for
k
. These correspond to saddle points of the underlying functional,
\mathcal{E}
. This lack of uniqueness can pose a challenge in the subsequent analysis of the phonon spectrum (Sect.
5
). As a remedy to this issue, we point out that a restriction on the norm of
k
, i.e.,
\|k\|_{\mathrm{op}}<1
, warrants uniqueness (see also
AlbeverioMotovilov2010
). By this restriction, the
k
that solves Riccati equation (
6b
) is in fact a minimizer of
\mathcal{E}
.

4.1
Functional
\mathcal{E}[\overline{k},k]
and useful lemmas

Next, we define the relevant Hilbert space and the functional
\mathcal{E}[\overline{k},k]
which yields (
6b
). We also prove two lemmas needed for our existence theory.

Definition 1

Let
\mathfrak{h}^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})
be the space of functions
k(x,x^{\prime})
such that

\iint{{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}x^{\prime}\ \Big\{|\nabla_{x}k(x,x^{\prime})|^{2}+|\nabla_{x^{\prime}}k(x,x^{\prime})|^{2}+\big(V(x)+V(x^{\prime})\big)|k(x,x^{\prime})|^{2}\Big\}}<\infty~.

The energy functional
\mathcal{E}[\overline{k},k]:\mathrm{dom}(\mathcal{E})\to\mathbb{R}
is defined by

\mathcal{E}[\overline{k},k]:=\mathrm{tr}\Big\{\big(\delta-\overline{k}\circ k\big)^{-1}\circ\Big(\overline{k}\circ h\circ k+\frac{1}{2}\overline{k}\circ f_{\phi}+\frac{1}{2}\overline{f_{\phi}}\circ k\Big)\Big\}

(8a)

where

\mathrm{dom}(\mathcal{E}):=\Big\{k\in\mathfrak{B}_{2}(\mathfrak{h}^{1}_{V})\big|\,k^{T}=k\,\,\mathrm{and}\,\,\|k\|_{\rm op}<1\Big\}\subset\mathfrak{B}_{2}(\mathfrak{h}^{1}_{V})~.

(8b)

Remark 1

The space
\mathfrak{h}^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})
is the same as the space
\mathfrak{B}_{2}\big(\mathfrak{h}^{1}_{V}\big)
. If
k\in\mathfrak{B}_{2}(\mathfrak{h}^{1}_{V})
and
\|k\|_{2}<1
then

(\delta-\overline{k}\circ k)^{-1}\in\mathfrak{B}_{2}(\mathfrak{h}^{1}_{V})
. Thus,
\mathrm{dom}(\mathcal{E})
is nonempty. The inequality
\|k\|_{2}<1
implies
\|k\|_{\mathrm{op}}<1
. Further remarks on
\|k\|_{\mathrm{op}}<1
are deferred to Sect.
4.3
.

The first lemma can be stated as follows.

Lemma 1

The functional derivative of
\mathcal{E}[\overline{k},k]
with respect to symmetric variations of
\overline{k}
in
\mathfrak{h}^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})
, denoted by
\delta\mathcal{E}/\delta\overline{k}
where
\delta\mathcal{E}/\delta\overline{k}\in\mathfrak{B}_{2}^{\ast}(\mathfrak{h}^{1}_{V})=\mathfrak{B}_{2}(\mathfrak{h}^{1}_{V})
, is

\frac{\delta\mathcal{E}[\overline{k},k]}{\delta\overline{k}}=\frac{1}{2}(\delta-k\circ\overline{k})^{-1}\circ\big\{h\circ k+k\circ h^{T}+f_{\phi}+k\circ\overline{f_{\phi}}\circ k\big\}\circ(\delta-\overline{k}\circ k)^{-1}~.

Proof

Consider the arbitrary symmetric perturbation
\ell(x,x^{\prime})
.
It suffices to show that

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}s}\mathcal{E}[\overline{k}+s\overline{\ell},k]\Big)\Big|_{s=0}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}x^{\prime}\ \left\{\overline{\ell}(x,x^{\prime})(\delta-k\circ\overline{k})^{-1}\circ\big\{h\circ k+k\circ h^{T}+f_{\phi}+k\circ\overline{f_{\phi}}\circ k\big\}\circ(\delta-\overline{k}\circ k)^{-1}(x,x^{\prime})\right\}\ .

First, by differentiating the formal identity

\delta=(\delta-\overline{k}\circ k)^{-1}\circ(\delta-\overline{k}\circ k)
we obtain

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}s}\big\{\delta-(\overline{k}+s\overline{\ell})\circ k\big\}^{-1}\Big)\Big|_{s=0}=\big(\delta-\overline{k}\circ k\big)^{-1}\circ\overline{\ell}\circ k\circ\big(\delta-\overline{k}\circ k\big)^{-1}\ .

Using, e.g., the Neumann series for
(\delta-\overline{k}\circ k)^{-1}
, we realize that

\displaystyle k\circ\big(\delta-\overline{k}\circ k\big)^{-1}=\big(\delta-k\circ\overline{k}\big)^{-1}\circ k~.

Hence, we also obtain the identity

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}s}\big\{\big(\delta-(\overline{k}+s\overline{\ell})\circ k\big)^{-1}\circ(\overline{k}+s\overline{\ell})\big\}\Big)\Big|_{s=0}=\big(\delta-\overline{k}\circ k\big)^{-1}\circ\overline{\ell}\circ\big(\delta-k\circ\overline{k}\big)^{-1}~.

Now express
\mathcal{E}
as the sum

\mathcal{E}=\mathrm{tr}\big\{(\delta-\overline{k}\circ k)^{-1}\circ\overline{k}\circ\big(h\circ k+{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}f_{\phi}\big)\big\}+\mathrm{tr}\big\{\big(\delta-\overline{k}\circ k\big)^{-1}\circ{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}(\overline{f_{\phi}}\circ k)\big\}=:\mathcal{E}_{1}+\mathcal{E}_{2}~.

The use of the cyclic property of the trace along with
\ell^{T}=\ell
and
(\overline{k}\circ k)^{T}=k\circ\overline{k}
yield

\begin{split}\Big(\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}s}\mathcal{E}_{1}[\overline{k}+s\overline{\ell},k]\Big)\Big|_{s=0}&=\mathrm{tr}\big\{(\delta-\overline{k}\circ k)^{-1}\circ\overline{\ell}\circ(\delta-k\circ\overline{k})^{-1}\circ(h\circ k+{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}f_{\phi})\big\}\\
&=\mathrm{tr}\big\{\overline{\ell}\circ(\delta-k\circ\overline{k})^{-1}\circ(h\circ k+{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}f_{\phi})\circ(\delta-\overline{k}\circ k)^{-1}\big\}\end{split}

and

\begin{split}\Big(\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}s}\mathcal{E}_{2}[\overline{k}+s\overline{\ell},k]\Big)\Big|_{s=0}&=\mathrm{tr}\big\{{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}(\delta-\overline{k}\circ k\big)^{-1}\circ\overline{\ell}\circ k\circ\big(\delta-\overline{k}\circ k\big)^{-1}\circ(\overline{f_{\phi}}\circ k)\big\}\\
&=\mathrm{tr}\big\{{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\overline{\ell}\circ k\circ\big(\delta-\overline{k}\circ k\big)^{-1}\circ(\overline{f_{\phi}}\circ k)\circ(\delta-\overline{k}\circ k\big)^{-1}\big\}\\
&=\mathrm{tr}\big\{{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\overline{\ell}\circ\big(\delta-k\circ\overline{k}\big)^{-1}\circ(k\circ\overline{f_{\phi}}\circ k)\circ(\delta-\overline{k}\circ k\big)^{-1}\big\}~.\end{split}

Now combine the above results to obtain the expression

\Big(\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}s}\mathcal{E}[\overline{k}+s\overline{\ell},k]\Big)\Big|_{s=0}=\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{tr}\Big\{\overline{\ell}\circ\Big(\big(\delta-k\circ\overline{k}\big)^{-1}\circ\text{Ric}\circ\big(\delta-\overline{k}\circ k\big)^{-1}\Big)\Big\}~,

where
\rm{Ric}
is defined by (
6a
).
Note that
{\rm Ric}
is manifestly symmetric
if
k
is symmetric.
This observation completes the proof of Lemma
1
.
\square

Remark 2

The notion of the weak solution as the critical point of the functional
\mathcal{E}[\overline{k},k]
is relevant to our existence theorem (Theorem
4.1
). Consider the space
\phi^{\perp}=\big\{e\in\mathfrak{h}^{1}_{V}\ \big|\ e\perp\phi\big\}
. We remind the reader that a bounded operator
k\in\mathfrak{B}(\phi^{\perp},\phi^{\perp})
has a weak solution to the Riccati equation

k\circ h_{\perp}^{T}+h_{\perp}\circ k+k\circ\overline{f_{\phi}}\circ k+f_{\phi}=0

provided

\langle k\circ h_{\perp}^{T}p,r\rangle+\langle kp,h^{T}_{\perp}r\rangle+\langle k\circ\overline{f_{\phi}}\circ kp,r\rangle=\langle-f_{\phi}p,r\rangle.\quad\forall p,\,r\in\mathrm{dom}\{h^{T}_{\perp}\}~,

where
h_{\perp}
is the projection of operator
h
on space
\phi^{\perp}
.

Before stating the second lemma, we remark on the condensate wave function,
\phi
.

Remark 3

By Sect.
3
, recall that
\phi
satisfies
\mathbb{H}_{\rm H}\phi(x)=\mu\phi(x)
where the one-particle Hartree operator
\mathbb{H}_{\rm H}
is defined in (
7
). We now state a few assumptions, which primarily concern the interaction potential
\upsilon(x)
and the trapping potential
V(x)
.
First, let us assume that
\upsilon(x)
is positive, symmetric, integrable, and smooth. If the equation for
\phi
comes from minimizing the Hartree energy functional,
E_{H}
, viz.,

E_{H}(\phi):=\int{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y\ \left\{\overline{\phi(x)}\epsilon(x,y)\phi(y)+\frac{N}{2}|\phi(x)|^{2}\upsilon(x-y)|\phi(y)|^{2}\right\}~,

with
\|\phi\|_{2}=1
then
\mu
is
the lowest eigenvalue of the linear operator that results from fixing
\phi
in
\mathbb{H}_{\rm H}
. The existence theorem (Theorem
4.1
) is stated and proved for a condensate
\phi
that is not necessarily a minimizer of
E_{H}
. In fact, we replace the assumption of
\phi
being such a minimizer by a less restrictive hypothesis (see Lemma
2
). We assume that the potential
V
is such that
-\Delta+V
has
discrete spectrum; for example,
V(x)=c|x|^{2}
(
c>0
). The spectrum of

\mathbb{H}_{\rm H}
is also discrete since
\mathbb{H}_{\rm H}
is a compact perturbation of
-\Delta+V
.

Lemma 2

If
\upsilon(x)
has positive Fourier transform
\widehat{\upsilon}(\xi)
,
\widehat{\upsilon}(\xi)\geq 0
, and
\phi
is a minimizer of the functional
E_{H}(\phi)
, then for some
c>0

the following inequality holds:

h(\overline{e},e)-\big|f_{\phi}(\overline{e},\overline{e})\big|\geq c\|e\|_{2}\quad\forall e\in\phi^{\perp}=\big\{e\in\mathfrak{h}_{V}^{1}\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\ \big|\ e\perp\phi\big\}

where
h(\cdot,\cdot)
and
f_{\phi}(\cdot,\cdot)
are defined from (
3b
)–(
3e
).

Proof

Define
g(x):=\overline{\phi(x)}\,e(x)
. Parseval’s identity yields

\iint{{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y\ \{\overline{e(x)}\phi(x)N\upsilon(x-y)\overline{\phi}(y)e(y)\}}=\int{{\rm d}\xi\ \{N\widehat{\upsilon}(\xi)|\widehat{g}(\xi)|^{2}\}}~,

which dominates the integral

\iint{{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y\ \{\overline{e}(x)f_{\phi}(x,y)\overline{e}(y)\}}=\int{d\xi\{N\widehat{\upsilon}(\xi)\big(\overline{\widehat{g}(\xi)}\big)^{2}}\}~.

Since
\phi
is the minimizer of the Hartree functional,
E_{H}(\phi)
, we can assert that

\phi
is the eigenfunction with the lowest eigenvalue of
the operator
\mathbb{H}_{\rm H}
and is therefore simple.
If
e\perp\phi
then
\big<\overline{e},\mathbb{H}_{H}e\big>\geq c\|e\|_{L^{2}}

for some
c>0
because the spectrum of the Hartree operator
\mathbb{H}_{H}
is discrete (see Remark
3
).
\square

Lemma
2
motivates the inequality involving
h
and
f_{\phi}
as a key assumption of Theorem
4.1
, which replaces the requirement that
\phi
is a minimizer of
E_{H}(\cdot)
.

4.2
Existence theorem and proof

The existence theorem can be stated as follows:

Theorem 4.1

Suppose that the kernels
h(x,y)
and
f_{\phi}(x,y)
satisfy the inequality

\displaystyle h(\overline{e},e)-\big|f_{\phi}(\overline{e},\overline{e})\big|\geq c\|e\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\quad\quad\forall e\in\phi^{\perp}=\big\{e\in\mathfrak{h}^{1}_{V}\ \big|\ e\perp\phi\big\}~,

(9)

for some constant
c>0
. Moreover, let us assume that
f_{\phi}
is Hilbert-Schmidt.

Consider the functional
\mathcal{E}[\overline{k},k]
, defined in (
8a
),
with domain

\mathrm{dom}(\mathcal{E})_{\perp}:=\mathrm{dom}(\mathcal{E})\cap\left\{k\in\mathfrak{B}_{2}(\mathfrak{h}^{1}_{V})\ \big|\ k(x,\overline{\phi})=0\right\}

which consists of the compact
\mathcal{C}
-symmetric Hilbert-Schmidt operators
k\in\mathfrak{B}_{2}(\mathfrak{h}^{1}_{V})
satisfying
k(x,\overline{\phi})=0
.

Then the functional
\mathcal{E}
restricted to
\mathrm{dom}(\mathcal{E})_{\perp}
attains a minimum for some
k\in\mathrm{dom}(\mathcal{E})_{\perp}
which is a weak solution of the operator Riccati equation (
6b
).
The function
\lambda(x)
entering this equation is a Lagrange multiplier due to the restriction and equals

\lambda(x)=\sqrt{2}\left\{\big(k\circ\gamma\big)\big(x,\overline{\phi}\big)+f_{\phi}\big(x,\overline{\phi}\big)-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}f_{\phi}(\overline{\phi},\overline{\phi})\phi(x)\right\}~.

(10)

At this stage, two remarks are in order.

Remark 4

We will seek stationary points of
\mathcal{E}[\overline{k},k]
under the constraint
k^{T}=k
. We now describe a generalization of the spectral theorem for compact operators with
symmetric
kernels which is invoked in the proof of Theorem
4.1
. Let
\mathcal{C}
denote the operator of complex conjugation on
\mathfrak{h}
where

\mathcal{C}f(x)=\overline{f(x)}\qquad\forall\,f\in\mathfrak{h}~.

An operator
\mathcal{T}
on
\mathfrak{h}
is called complex-symmetric (“
\mathcal{C}
-symmetric”) if it satisfies

\mathcal{C}\mathcal{T}=\mathcal{T}^{\ast}\mathcal{C}~,

where
\mathcal{T}^{\ast}
is the Hermitian conjugate of
\mathcal{T}
(
\mathcal{T}^{\ast}(x,y)=\overline{\mathcal{T}(y,x)}
). Clearly, integral operators whose kernels are symmetric in their arguments are
\mathcal{C}
-symmetric.
An important property is that any compact complex-symmetric operator
\mathcal{T}
such that
\mathcal{T}^{\ast}\circ\mathcal{T}
has
simple spectrum
admits the decomposition

\mathcal{T}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{a_{n}(u_{n}\otimes\mathcal{C}u_{n})}~,

(11)

where
a_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
converge to zero as
n\to\infty
and
\{u_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is an orthonormal basis of
\mathfrak{h}
. This property comes from the identity
(\mathcal{C}\mathcal{T})\circ(\mathcal{C}\mathcal{T})=\mathcal{T}^{\ast}\circ\mathcal{T}
, which implies the commutation relation

[\mathcal{C}\mathcal{T},\mathcal{T}^{\ast}\circ\mathcal{T}]=0
.
In particular,
\mathcal{C}\mathcal{T}
commutes with the spectral measure (and any eigenprojector) of the positive operator
\mathcal{T}^{\ast}\circ\mathcal{T}
. Since the latter operator has simple spectrum, it follows that these two operators have the same eigenspace. This fact allows us to pass from the eigenvalue equation
(\mathcal{T}^{\ast}\circ\mathcal{T})(u_{n},x)=c_{n}u_{n}(x)
to the eigenvalue equation
\mathcal{C}\mathcal{T}(u_{n},x)=a_{n}u_{n}(x)
;
thus,
|a_{n}|^{2}=c_{n}
.
It can be directly shown that all
\mathcal{C}
-symmetric tensor products
u\otimes v
must have
v(x)=\mathcal{C}u(x)
. Hence, we can also pass from the spectral representation

\mathcal{T}^{\ast}\circ\mathcal{T}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{c_{n}(u_{n}\otimes u_{n})}

to expression (
11
). This result amounts to a version of the spectral theorem for compact
\mathcal{C}
-symmetric operators; see, e.g.,
Garcia2005
.

Remark 5

The reader should compare (
10
), regarding the Lagrange multiplier
\lambda
, with equation (3.24) in Wu’s paper
wu98
, which employs a delta-function interaction potential. The respective formulas for
\lambda(x)
differ by a factor of
\sqrt{2}
because of our choice of a different normalization factor for
\lambda\otimes_{\mathrm{s}}\phi
.

We can now proceed to prove Theorem
4.1
. Notably, we consider a condensate
\phi
that is not necessarily a minimizer of the Hartree energy,
E_{H}
.

Proof

We split the proof of Theorem
4.1
into three main steps.

Step 1.
We now express the functional
\mathcal{E}
in terms of a suitable basis and describe critical points, by taking into account the theory of
\mathcal{C}
-symmetric operators. By Remark
4
, any
k
satisfying our assumptions admits the decomposition

k(x,x^{\prime})=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}{z_{j}e_{j}(x)e_{j}(x^{\prime})}\ ,\quad e_{j}\in\phi^{\perp}~,

where
\{e_{j}(x)\}_{j}\subset\mathfrak{h}
is an orthonormal basis and the coefficients
\{z_{j}\}\subset\mathbb{C}
are such that
z_{j}\to 0
as
j\to\infty
. For the moment, we assume
|z_{j}|\not=1
for all
j
so that

(\delta-\overline{k}\circ k)^{-1}(x,x^{\prime})=\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}{\Big(\frac{1}{1-|z_{j}|^{2}}\Big)\overline{e}_{j}(x)e_{j}(x^{\prime})}~.

The substitution of the two preceding expressions into (
8a
) for the energy furnishes

\mathcal{E}\Big(\{e_{j}\},\{z_{i}\}\Big)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}{\frac{1}{1-|z_{j}|^{2}}\left\{h(\overline{e}_{j},e_{j})|z_{j}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\Big(f_{\phi}(\overline{e}_{j},\overline{e}_{j})z_{j}+\overline{f_{\phi}}(e_{j},e_{j})\overline{z}_{j}\Big)\right\}}~,

where
\overline{f_{\phi}}(e_{j},e_{j})=\overline{f_{\phi}(\overline{e}_{j},\overline{e}_{j})}
. The derivative of
\mathcal{E}\big(\{e_{j}\},\{z_{j}\}\big)
with respect to
\overline{z_{j}}
reads

\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline{z}_{j}}\mathcal{E}\Big(\{e_{j}\},\{z_{j}\}\Big)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{2h(\overline{e}_{j},e_{j})z_{j}+\overline{f_{\phi}}(e_{j},e_{j})+f_{\phi}(\overline{e}_{j},\overline{e}_{j})z_{j}^{2}}{(1-|z_{j}|^{2})^{2}}~.\color[rgb]{0,0,0}

Setting
\partial\mathcal{E}/\partial\overline{z}_{j}=0
gives two roots, viz.,

z^{\pm}_{j}=\frac{-h(\overline{e}_{j},e_{j})\pm\sqrt{h^{2}(\overline{e}_{j},e_{j})-|f_{\phi}(\overline{e}_{j},\overline{e}_{j})|^{2}}}{f_{\phi}(\overline{e}_{j},\overline{e}_{j})}~.

(12)

The assumption stated by (
9
)
guarantees that
|z^{\pm}_{j}|\not=1
, provided
e_{j}
is a member of the function space
\phi^{\perp}
; in fact,
|z_{j}^{+}|<1
and
|z_{j}^{-}|>1
. Regarding
\mathcal{E}(\{e_{j}\},\{z_{j}\})
, notice that the summand (for fixed
j
and
e_{j}=e
) is described by the function

f(z;e):=\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\frac{2h(\overline{e},e)|z|^{2}+f_{\phi}(\overline{e},\overline{e})\overline{z}+\overline{f_{\phi}}(e,e)z}{1-|z|^{2}}

which takes real values with
f(0;e)=0
, while

\displaystyle\lim_{|z|\to 1^{-}}f(z;e)=+\infty~.

Thus, the function
f(z;e_{j})

attains a minimum at
z=z_{j}^{+}
. On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{|z|\to\infty}f(z;e)=-2h(\overline{e},e)~,

and
f(z_{j}^{-};e_{j})=-h(\overline{e}_{j},e_{j})-\sqrt{h^{2}(\overline{e}_{j},e_{j})-|f_{\phi}(\overline{e}_{j},\overline{e}_{j})|^{2}}

which implies that
f(z;e_{j})
has a maximum at
z=z_{j}^{-}
in view of

\lim_{|z|\to 1^{+}}f(z;e)=-\infty~.

By (
12
) the evaluation of
\mathcal{E}
with the roots
z^{\pm}_{j}
yields

\mathcal{E}\Big(\{(z^{\pm}_{j}\},\{e_{j}\}\Big)=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}{\Big\{h(\overline{e}_{j},e_{j})\mp\sqrt{h^{2}(\overline{e}_{j},e_{j})-|f_{\phi}(\overline{e}_{j},\overline{e}_{j}|^{2}}\Big\}}~.

In light of the preceding discussion, we choose the root
z^{+}_{j}
where
|z^{+}_{j}|<1
and define

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(e):=h(\overline{e},e)-\sqrt{h^{2}(\overline{e},e)-|f_{\phi}(\overline{e},\overline{e})|^{2}}~,

so that the value of the functional
\mathcal{E}
reads

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}\big(\{z_{j}^{+}\},\{e_{j}\}\big)=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{F}(e_{j})\ .

Step 2.

So far, the minimization problem over compact symmetric operators has been converted into the following problem:

\displaystyle\min_{\{e_{j}\},{\rm orthonormal}}\left\{-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{F}(e_{j})\right\}=-\frac{1}{2}\max_{\{e_{j}\},{\rm orthonormal}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{F}(e_{j})~.

Next, we prove that the minimum is attained. The construction of the orthonormal set
\{e_{j}(x)\}_{j=1}^{\infty}

can be carried out inductively via a standard procedure,
so we skip the details here.
For simplicity, we describe the first
step. The remaining steps are similar. Our task is to maximize
\mathcal{F}(e)
, focusing on

\displaystyle\max_{\|e\|_{L^{2}}=1,e\in\phi^{\perp}}\mathcal{F}(e)~.

Consider a maximizing sequence
\{e_{n}\}\in\phi^{\perp}
with
\|e_{n}\|_{L^{2}}=1

such that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{F}(e_{n})=:\mathcal{F}_{\rm max}~.\color[rgb]{0,0,0}

We can assert that
\mathcal{F}_{\rm max}
is finite, which follows from the
observation

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(e)=\frac{|f_{\phi}(\overline{e},\overline{e})|^{2}}{h(\overline{e},e)+\sqrt{h^{2}(\overline{e},e)-|f_{\phi}(\overline{e},\overline{e})|^{2}}}\leq|f_{\phi}(\overline{e},\overline{e})|\leq\|f_{\phi}\|_{L^{2}}\|e\|_{L^{2}}^{2}~.

We can assume without loss of generality that
\mathcal{F}_{\rm max}>0
,
for if
\mathcal{F}_{\rm max}=0
then

f_{\phi}(\overline{e},\overline{e})
vanishes identically on the set on which
we try to maximize, namely

\phi^{\perp}
.
We know that
f_{\phi}(\overline{e}_{n},\overline{e}_{n})
is bounded and from the
expression of
\mathcal{F}(e_{n})
we conclude that

h(\overline{e}_{n},e_{n})
is also bounded. Since

\{e_{n}\}\subset\phi^{\perp}
we can find a subsequence
(again denoted by
\{e_{n}\}
) which converges weakly in
\phi^{\perp}

to some
e_{1}\in\phi^{\perp}
. Because
\phi^{\perp}
is compactly
embedded in
\mathfrak{h}=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
we conclude that

\{e_{n}\}
converges strongly in
\mathfrak{h}
.
Up to this subsequence, we therefore have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{F}(e_{n})=\mathcal{F}(e_{1})=\mathcal{F}_{\rm max}~.

Furthermore, we can assert that

h(\overline{e}_{n},e_{n})\to h(\overline{e}_{1},e_{1})~.

The reason is that
\mathcal{F}(e)
is a decreasing
function of
h(\overline{e},e)
. Thus, if

h(\overline{e}_{n},e_{n})\to\widetilde{h}<h(\overline{e}_{1},e_{1})

then
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{F}(e_{n})
will not be the maximum,
which leads to a contradiction.
In conclusion, the subsequence
\{e_{n}\}
converges
strongly in
\phi^{\perp}
and

\mathcal{F}(e_{1})=\mathcal{F}_{\rm max}
.

Next, we check that the overall minimum is finite. Condition (
9
) implies that
h(\overline{e},e)
is bounded below. This
means that the the overall minimum is finite provided

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{F}(e_{j})

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|f_{\phi}(\overline{e}_{j},e_{j})|^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left|\ \int{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y\,\left\{\overline{e}_{j}(x)f_{\phi}(x,y)\overline{e}_{j}(y)\right\}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int{\rm d}x\,\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left|\ \int{\rm d}y\,\left\{f_{\phi}(x,y)\overline{e}_{j}(y)\right\}\right|^{2}\leq\int{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y\,\left\{|f_{\phi}(x,y)|^{2}\right\}<\infty~.

The last condition indeed holds. Note that
z_{j}^{+}
is recast to the expression

\displaystyle z_{j}^{+}=\frac{f_{\phi}(\overline{e}_{j},\overline{e}_{j})}{h(\overline{e}_{j},e_{j})+\sqrt{h^{2}(\overline{e}_{j},e_{j})-|f_{\phi}(\overline{e}_{j},\overline{e}_{j})|^{2}}}~.

Accordingly, we see that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|z_{j}^{+}|^{2}\leq C\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|f_{\phi}(\overline{e}_{j},\overline{e}_{j})|^{2}\leq\|f_{\phi}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}~.

Thus,
k
is Hilbert-Schmidt.

Step 3.
So far, we showed that the minimum is attained in
\mathrm{dom}(\mathcal{E})_{\perp}
. We must now take into account the constraint

k(x,\overline{\phi})=0
via a Lagrange multiplier. We introduce the
Lagrange multiplier as an operator with
symmetric kernel
\ell(x,y)
where

\displaystyle\ell(x,y)=\big(\lambda\otimes_{\rm s}\phi\big)(x,y):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left\{\lambda(x)\phi(y)+\lambda(y)\phi(x)\right\}~.

In the above,
\phi
is the condensate wave function and
\lambda(x)
is to be
determined.
Hence, the modified energy functional to be minimized has the form

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{E}}[\overline{k},k]:=\mathcal{E}[\overline{k},k]-{\rm tr}\left\{\overline{\ell}\circ k+\overline{k}\circ\ell\right\}~.

In view of Lemma
1
, setting equal to zero the functional derivative of
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}
with respect to
\overline{k}
yields Riccati equation (
6b
).
Given that
h=\mathbb{H}_{\rm H}\delta+N\gamma-\mu
and

\mathbb{H}_{\rm H}\phi=\mu\phi
, we compute
\lambda
by
contracting the above equation for
k
with
\overline{\phi}
. Thus, we obtain (
10
).

Note on
k
as a weak solution.
We conclude the proof by showing that
k
satisfies the definition of a weak solution (Remark
2
). The condition that
\mathcal{E}
is minimized implies that the first variation with respect to
\overline{k}
vanishes, i.e.,

\mathrm{tr}\Big[\overline{k}_{1}\circ(\delta-k\circ\overline{k})^{-1}\circ\Big\{h_{\perp}\circ k+k\circ h^{T}_{\perp}+f_{\phi}+k\circ\overline{f_{\phi}}\circ k\Big\}\circ(\delta-\overline{k}\circ k)^{-1}\Big]=0~,

for all
\overline{k}_{1}\in\mathrm{dom}(\mathcal{E})
. Without loss of generality, we can make the substitution
\overline{k}_{1}\mapsto(\delta-\overline{k}\circ k)^{-1}\circ\overline{k}_{1}\circ(\delta-\overline{k}\circ k)^{-1}
so that the equation for vanishing first variation reads

\mathrm{tr}\Big[\overline{k}_{1}\circ\Big\{h_{\perp}\circ k+k\circ h^{T}_{\perp}+f_{\phi}+k\circ\overline{f_{\phi}}\circ k\Big\}\Big]=\mathrm{tr}\Big[\overline{k}_{1}\circ\mathrm{Ric}\Big]=0~.

If
\overline{k}_{1}:=p\otimes_{\mathrm{s}}r
for
p,\,r\in\mathrm{dom}(h_{\perp})
, the condition
\mathrm{tr}\Big[\overline{k}_{1}\circ\mathrm{Ric}\Big]=0
translates to

\begin{split}\mathrm{tr}\Big[p\otimes_{\mathrm{s}}r\circ\mathrm{Ric}\Big]&=\mathrm{tr}\Big[(k\circ p\otimes_{\mathrm{s}}r)\circ h_{\perp}\Big]+\mathrm{tr}\Big[(p\otimes_{\mathrm{s}}r)\circ k\circ h_{\perp}\Big]\\
&+\mathrm{tr}\Big[(p\otimes_{\mathrm{s}}r)\circ\Big(f_{\phi}+k\circ\overline{f_{\phi}}\circ k\Big)\Big]\\
&=\sqrt{2}\big(\langle kp,h_{\perp}r\rangle+\langle p,(k\circ h_{\perp})r\rangle+\langle p,\big(f_{\phi}+k\circ\overline{f_{\phi}}\circ k\big)r\rangle\big)~.\end{split}

Observe that this expression is defined for any
p,\,r\in\mathrm{dom}(h_{\perp})
, which is the space of test functions for the weak formulation of the Riccati equation.

\square

4.3
On the non-uniqueness of solution for
k

Next, we discuss the important issue of the non-uniqueness of solutions for
k
by our variational approach. The energy functional
\mathcal{E}[\overline{k},k]
has infinitely many critical points which correspond to choosing one of the two roots
z_{j}^{\pm}
, at every index
j
, in the proof of Theorem
4.1
. We can show that these different choices correspond to minimax points. To this end, pick an
arbitrary
e_{1}(x)
, normalized so that
\|e_{1}\|_{\mathfrak{h}}=1
, and
let

\displaystyle X^{\perp}(\phi,e_{1}):=\left\{e(x)\ \big|\ e\perp\{\phi,e_{1}\}\right\}~.

Also, consider the subspace

X(e_{1}):={\rm span}(e_{1})=\left\{z_{1}e_{1}(x)\in\mathfrak{h}\ |\ z_{1}\in{\mathbb{C}}\right\}
.
Accordingly, we set up the min-max problem expressed by

\displaystyle\max_{e\in X(e_{1}),|z_{1}|>1}\left\{\min_{\|k|_{X^{\perp}(\phi,e_{1})}\|_{\rm op}<1}\mathcal{E}(\overline{k},k)\right\}~.

By repetition of the above argument (proof of Theorem
4.1
), this min-max problem generates the (saddle type) critical point of the functional
\mathcal{E}(\overline{k},k)
. More generally, the maximum can be taken over any finite collection of
\{(e_{j_{k}},z_{j_{k}})\}_{k}
, producing a unique solution for every distinct sequence.

Evidently, the only solution
k
that obeys
\|k\|_{\mathrm{op}}<1
is the one given in the proof of Theorem
4.1
. Thus, we single out the choice
\{z_{j}=z_{j}^{+}\}
with
|z_{j}^{+}|<1
for all
j
as the one yielding the unique pair-excitation kernel for our model.

Remark 6

If
z_{j}
is chosen in (
12
) such that
z_{j}=z_{j}^{+}
for all
j>0
(i.e.,
\|k\|_{\mathrm{op}}<1
) then

\begin{split}(h+k\circ\overline{f_{\phi}})(\overline{e}_{j},e_{j})&=h(\overline{e}_{j},e_{j})+(k\overline{e}_{j},\overline{f_{\phi}}e_{j})=h(\overline{e}_{j},e_{j})+(z_{j}^{+})\overline{f_{\phi}}(e_{j},e_{j})\\

5.2
).

5
Spectrum and eigenvectors of reduced Hamiltonian

In this section, we describe the spectrum and eigenvectors of the reduced transformed Hamiltonian
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{app}}
in the
N
-th sector of Fock space,
\mathbb{F}_{N}
(see Sect.
3.3
). For this purpose, we decompose
\mathbb{F}_{N}
into suitable orthogonal subspaces. A similar technique is used in
Lewin2014
in connection to the Bogoliubov Hamiltonian which does not conserve the particle number.

We start by writing the transformed, quadratic non-Hermitian Hamiltonian as

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{app}}=NE_{H}+\mathcal{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm ph}~,\quad\mathcal{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm ph}:=h_{\rm ph}\big(a^{\ast}_{\perp},a_{\perp}\big)+\frac{1}{N}(a^{\ast}_{\phi})^{2}\,\overline{f_{\phi}}\big(a_{\perp},a_{\perp}\big)~

(13a)

where
f_{\phi}(a_{\perp}^{\ast},a_{\perp}^{\ast})
(and thus
\overline{f_{\phi}}(a_{\perp},a_{\perp})
) is defined by (
3b
) with (
3d
), and

\displaystyle h_{\rm ph}:=h+k\circ\overline{f_{\phi}}~.

(13b)

The operator
h_{\rm ph}\big(a^{\ast}_{\perp},a_{\perp}\big)
forms the diagonal part of
\mathcal{H}_{\rm ph}
and is non-Hermitian.

The main result of this section is expressed by the following theorem.

Theorem 5.1

Consider the operators
\mathcal{H}_{\rm ph}
and
h_{\rm ph}(a^{\ast}_{\perp},a_{\perp})

restricted on
\mathbb{F}_{N}
. Then

\displaystyle\sigma\left(\mathcal{H}_{\rm ph}\big|_{\mathbb{F}_{N}}\right)=\sigma\left(h_{\rm ph}(a^{\ast}_{\perp},a_{\perp})\big|_{\mathbb{F}_{N}}\right)~.

Moreover, for each eigenvector
|\Omega\rangle_{N}\in\mathbb{F}_{N}

of
h_{\rm ph}(a^{\ast}_{\perp},a_{\perp})
with eigenvalue
E
there exists
a unique eigenvector
|\Psi(\Omega)\rangle_{N}\in\mathbb{F}_{N}
of

\mathcal{H}_{\rm ph}
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\rm ph}|\Psi(\Omega)\rangle_{N}=E|\Psi(\Omega)\rangle_{N}~.

Before we give a proof of Theorem
5.1
, we need to provide a few useful results. In Sect.
5.1
, we develop a formalism for the decomposition of
\mathbb{F}_{N}
into orthogonal subspaces. A key ingredient of our approach is the use of Fock space techniques. In Sect.
5.2
, we show that the spectrum of
h_{\rm ph}
is discrete. In Sect.
5.3
, we use this machinery (theory of Sects.
5.1
and
5.2
) to prove Theorem
5.1
. In our proof, we describe an explicit construction of the
eigenvectors of
\mathcal{H}_{\rm ph}
restricted on
\mathbb{F}_{N}
in terms of eigenvectors of
h_{\rm ph}(a_{\perp}^{\ast},a_{\perp})
. This construction invokes the discrete spectrum of
h_{\rm ph}
.

5.1
Decomposition of
\mathbb{F}_{N}
and two related lemmas

Next, we set the stage for the proof of Theorem
5.1
. We use the symbol
|\psi\rangle_{n}
to denote the
vector of
\mathbb{F}
with entry
\psi_{n}(x_{1},\,\ldots,\,x_{n})
in the
n
-th slot and zero elsewhere. Let us also introduce the projection

\mathcal{P}_{N}\ :\ \mathbb{F}\mapsto\mathbb{F}_{N}
.

The vector
\psi_{N}(x_{1},\,\ldots,\,x_{N})
can be decomposed
as a direct sum according to

\displaystyle\psi_{N}=\sum_{n=0}^{N}\psi_{N,n}\otimes_{\rm s}\big(\otimes^{N-n}\phi\big)=:\sum_{n=0}^{N}\psi^{\phi}_{N,n}~,\qquad\otimes^{p}\phi:=\prod_{j=1}^{p}\phi(x_{j})~,

where the vectors
\psi_{N,n}(x_{1},\,\ldots\,,x_{n})

satisfy the orthogonality relations (
n=1,2\ldots N
)

\displaystyle\int{\rm d}x\ \left\{\overline{\phi}(x)\psi_{N,n}(x,\,x_{2},\,\ldots\,,x_{n})\right\}=0~.

We also define
|\psi^{\phi}_{N,n}\rangle_{N}:=\big(0,\,\ldots,\,0,\,\psi^{\phi}_{N,n},\,\ldots\big)\in\mathbb{F}_{N}
.
Hence, the vector
|\psi^{\perp}\rangle:=\big(\psi_{N,0},\,\psi_{N,1},\,\ldots,\,\psi_{N,N},\,0,\,\ldots\big)

describes fluctuations around the tensor product

\otimes^{N}\phi
(pure condensate). This means that we can decompose the space
\mathbb{F}_{N}
into the following direct sum
of orthogonal subspaces:

\mathbb{F}_{N}=\oplus_{n=0}^{N}\mathbb{F}_{N,n}~;\qquad\mathbb{F}_{N,n}={\rm span}_{\psi_{n}\perp\phi}\big(0,\,\ldots,\,0,\,\psi_{n}\otimes_{\mathrm{s}}(\otimes^{N-n}\phi),\,0\ldots\big)~.

To describe this decomposition, we consider the number operator
\mathcal{N}_{\phi}=a^{*}_{\phi}a_{\bar{\phi}}
for the condensate. We have

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\phi}\big|_{\mathbb{F}_{N,n}}=(N-n)\mathcal{I}\big|_{\mathbb{F}_{N,n}}\quad,\quad 0\leq n\leq N~,

where
\mathcal{I}
is the identity operator on
\mathbb{F}
.
In other words,
\mathbb{F}_{N,n}
are
the eigenspaces of
\mathcal{N}_{\phi}
restricted to
\mathbb{F}_{N}
.
To see how the decomposition works, we invoke the identity

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{N}=\sum_{n=0}^{N}\frac{(-1)^{N-n}}{(N-n)!n!}\prod_{p=0\atop p\not=N-n}^{N}\big(\mathcal{N}_{\phi}-p\mathcal{I}\big)\mathcal{P}_{N}~,

which can be understood as a resolution of the identity on
\mathbb{F}_{N}
.
By introducing the projection operator (projection on
\mathbb{F}_{n}
)
via the polynomial

P_{n}(z):=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n!}\prod_{j=1}^{n}(z-j)~,

we define

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{n,n}:=P_{n}(\mathcal{N}_{\phi})=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n!}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\big(\mathcal{N}_{\phi}-j\mathcal{I}\big)~.

Thus,
\mathcal{P}_{n,n}
is the projection
\mathcal{P}_{n,n}:\mathbb{F}_{n}\mapsto\mathbb{F}_{n,n}
.

At this stage, we can state the first lemma of this section as follows.

Lemma 3

The operator
\mathcal{P}_{n,n}=P_{n}(\mathcal{N}_{\phi})
satisfies the factorization

\sum_{n=0}^{N}\frac{(-1)^{N}}{(N-n)!}\,{a^{\ast}_{\phi}}^{N-n}\mathcal{P}_{n,n}a_{\overline{\phi}}^{N-n}=\sum_{n=0}^{N}\frac{(-1)^{N-n}}{(N-n)!\,n!}\prod_{p=0\atop p\not=N-n}^{N}\big(\mathcal{N}_{\phi}-p\mathcal{I}\big)

which, restricted to
\mathbb{F}_{N}
,
describes a resolution of
\mathbb{F}_{N}
into orthogonal
subspaces.

Proof

First, we note the useful identities

\displaystyle{a_{\overline{\phi}}}^{p}\mathcal{N}_{\phi}=\big(\mathcal{N}_{\phi}+p\mathcal{I}\big){a_{\overline{\phi}}}^{p}~,\quad{a^{\ast}_{\phi}}^{p}\mathcal{N}_{\phi}=\big(\mathcal{N}_{\phi}-k\mathcal{I}\big){a^{\ast}_{\phi}}^{p}\qquad(p=0,\,1,\,\ldots N)~.

Subsequently, for any polynomial
P(z)
we can assert that

\displaystyle P(\mathcal{N}_{\phi}){a_{\overline{\phi}}}^{p}={a_{\overline{\phi}}}^{p}P\big(\mathcal{N}_{\phi}-p\mathcal{I}\big)~,\quad P(\mathcal{N}_{\phi}){a^{\ast}_{\phi}}^{p}={a^{\ast}_{\phi}}^{p}P\big(\mathcal{N}_{\phi}+p\mathcal{I}\big)~.

Moreover, we have the following formulas:

\displaystyle a_{\overline{\phi}}^{n}{a^{\ast}_{\phi}}^{n}=\prod_{p=1}^{n}\big(\mathcal{N}_{\phi}+p\mathcal{I}\big)~,\quad{a^{\ast}_{\phi}}^{n}a_{\overline{\phi}}^{n}=\prod_{p=0}^{n-1}\big(\mathcal{N}_{\phi}-p\mathcal{I}\big)~.

By using the above relations, we write

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{N}\frac{(-1)^{N}}{(N-n)!}{a^{\ast}_{\phi}}^{N-n}P_{n}(\mathcal{N}_{\phi})a_{\overline{\phi}}^{N-n}=\sum_{n=0}^{N}\frac{(-1)^{N}}{(N-n)!}{a^{\ast}_{\phi}}^{N-n}{a_{\overline{\phi}}}^{N-n}P_{n}\big(\mathcal{N}_{\phi}-(N-n)\mathcal{I}\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{N}\frac{(-1)^{N}(-1)^{n}}{(N-n)!n!}\prod_{j=0}^{N-n-1}\big(\mathcal{N}_{\phi}-j\mathcal{I}\big)\prod_{p=1}^{n}\big(\mathcal{N}_{\phi}+(n-p-N)\mathcal{I}\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{N}\frac{(-1)^{N-n}}{(N-n)!\,n!}\prod_{j=0\atop j\not=N-n}^{N}\big(\mathcal{N}_{\phi}-j\mathcal{I}\big)=\mathcal{P}_{N}~.

We can show that
\mathcal{P}_{n,n}
is a projection. Indeed, notice that

\mathcal{N}_{\phi}P_{n}(\mathcal{N}_{\phi})\big|_{\mathbb{F}_{n}}=0
,
and if
|\psi\rangle_{n}\in\mathbb{F}_{n}
then

\mathcal{P}_{n,n}|\psi\rangle_{n}=|\psi_{n,n}\rangle_{n}
.
If we consider the decomposition

\displaystyle\psi_{n}=\sum_{p=0}^{n}\psi_{n,p}\otimes_{s}\big(\otimes^{n-p}\phi\big)=\sum_{p=0}^{n}\psi^{\phi}_{n,p}

then
\mathcal{P}_{n,n}
applied to
\big|\psi\big>_{n}

produces a vector in
\mathbb{F}_{n,n}
, where
\mathbb{F}_{n,p}
is the decomposition of
\mathbb{F}_{n}
for

p=0,1\ldots n
.
\square

For later algebraic convenience, we give the following definition (cf.
Lewin2014
).

Definition 2

Consider the operators
\mathcal{U}_{n},\,\mathcal{U}^{\ast}_{n}:\mathbb{F}_{N}\mapsto\mathbb{F}_{N}

given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{n}:=\mathcal{P}_{n,n}\frac{a_{\overline{\phi}}^{N-n}}{\sqrt{(N-n)!}}~,\quad\mathcal{U}_{n}^{\ast}:=\frac{{a^{\ast}_{\phi}}^{N-n}}{\sqrt{(N-n)!}}\mathcal{P}_{n,n}~;\quad n=0,\,1,\,\ldots,\,N~.

By Definition
2
, the result of Lemma
3
implies that

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{N}=\sum_{n=0}^{N}\frac{(-1)^{N}}{(N-n)!}{a^{\ast}_{\phi}}^{N-n}\mathcal{P}_{n,n}\mathcal{P}_{n.n}{a_{\overline{\phi}}}^{N-n}\mathcal{P}_{N}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N}\frac{{a^{\ast}_{\phi}}^{N-n}\mathcal{P}_{n,n}}{\sqrt{(N-n)!}}\frac{\mathcal{P}_{n,n}{a_{\overline{\phi}}}^{N-n}}{\sqrt{N-n)!}}\mathcal{P}_{N}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N}\mathcal{U}_{n}^{\ast}\mathcal{U}_{n}\Big|_{\mathbb{F}_{N}}~.

The key relations following from this decomposition are

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{n}|\psi\rangle_{N}=|\psi_{N,n}\rangle_{n}~,\quad\mathcal{U}^{\ast}_{n}|\psi_{N,n}\rangle_{n}=|\psi_{N,n}^{\phi}\rangle_{N}\quad{\rm and}\quad\sum_{n=0}^{N}|\psi^{\phi}_{N,n}\rangle_{N}=|\psi\rangle_{N}~.

Hence, we have
\mathcal{U}_{n}:\mathbb{F}_{N}\mapsto\mathbb{F}_{n,n}
and
\mathcal{U}^{\ast}_{n}:\mathbb{F}_{n}\mapsto\mathbb{F}_{N,n}
.

Lemma 4

The operators
\{\mathcal{U}_{n}\}_{n=0}^{N}
(see Definition
2
) satisfy the relation

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{m}\mathcal{U}^{\ast}_{n}=\delta_{m,n}\mathcal{P}_{m,m}\mathcal{P}_{n,n}~.

Proof

First, we make the observation that

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{m}\mathcal{U}^{\ast}_{n}=\mathcal{P}_{m,m}\frac{{a_{\overline{\phi}}}^{N-m}}{\sqrt{(N-m)!}}\frac{{a^{\ast}_{\phi}}^{N-n}}{\sqrt{(N-n)!}}\mathcal{P}_{n,n}~.

If
m<n
(thus,
N-m>N-n
), in view of the property
a_{\overline{\phi}}\mathcal{P}_{n,n}\big|_{\mathbb{F}_{n}}=0
we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{m}\mathcal{U}^{\ast}_{n}=\frac{\prod_{j=n-m}^{N-m-1}\big(\mathcal{N}_{\phi}+j\mathcal{I}\big)}{\sqrt{(N-m)!(N-n)!}}\mathcal{P}_{m,m}{a_{\overline{\phi}}}^{n-m}\mathcal{P}_{n,n}=0~.

In this vein, if
n<m
then
\mathcal{U}_{m}\mathcal{U}^{\ast}_{n}=0
.
By
\mathcal{N}_{\phi}\mathcal{P}_{n,n}=0
we assert that if
m=n
then

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{n}\mathcal{U}^{\ast}_{n}=\mathcal{P}_{n,n}\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{N-n}\big(\mathcal{N}_{\phi}+j\mathcal{I}\big)}{(N-n)!}\mathcal{P}_{n,n}=\mathcal{P}_{n,n}~.\hskip 144.54pt\square

5.2
On the spectrum of
h_{\rm ph}

Next, we discuss key properties of the diagonal part,
h_{\rm ph}(a_{\perp}^{*},a_{\perp})
, of the reduced Hamiltonian. Interestingly,
h_{\rm ph}(x,y)
is similar to a self-adjoint operator. As such, many important spectral properties of self-adjoint operators carry over to
h_{\rm ph}
.

Lemma 5

Assume that the pair-excitation kernel
k
solves the operator Riccati equation (
6b
) with
\|k\|_{\mathrm{op}}<1
.

(i)
Then the spectrum of
h_{\mathrm{ph}}\,:\,\mathfrak{h}^{1}_{V}\cap\phi^{\perp}\mapsto\mathfrak{h}
is real and discrete. The corresponding eigenfunctions
\omega_{j}(x)
, which satisfy
h_{\mathrm{ph}}(x,\omega_{j})=E_{j}\omega_{j}(x)
where
E_{j}>0
are the eigenvalues (for
j=1,\,\dots
),
form a non-orthogonal Riesz basis of
\phi^{\perp}
.

(ii)
Also, suppose that the functions
u_{j}(x)
solve the adjoint problem, i.e.,

h_{\rm ph}^{\ast}(x,u_{j})=E_{j}u_{j}(x)
on
\phi^{\perp}
(for
j=1,2,\dots
).
Then the following completeness relation holds:

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}{\overline{\omega_{j}(x)}u_{j}(y)}=\widehat{\delta}(x,y)~.

(14)

Proof

The following relation holds on
\phi^{\perp}
by use of the Riccati equation (
6b
):

(h+k\circ\overline{f_{\phi}})\circ(\widehat{\delta}-k\circ\overline{k})=(\widehat{\delta}-k\circ\overline{k})\circ(h+f_{\phi}\circ\overline{k})~.

(15)

If
\|k\|_{\mathrm{op}}<1
then
(\widehat{\delta}-k\circ\overline{k})^{-1}
and
(\widehat{\delta}-k\circ\overline{k})^{1/2}
exist and are bounded operators on
\phi^{\perp}
.
By (
15
), the operator
\varkappa:=(\widehat{\delta}-k\circ\overline{k})^{-1/2}\circ(h+k\circ\overline{f_{\phi}})\circ(\widehat{\delta}-k\circ\overline{k})^{1/2}
is self-adjoint. Recall that
h
has discrete spectrum; thus,
h+k\circ\overline{f_{\phi}}
has discrete spectrum because
k\circ\overline{f_{\phi}}
is compact. Moreover, the eigenvalues of
h+k\circ\overline{f_{\phi}}
are positive (see Remark
6
).
By the spectral theorem, the eigenvalues of
\varkappa
are then positive and discrete, and the respective eigenvectors form an orthonormal basis of
\phi^{\perp}
.

(i)
Let the eigenvalues of
\varkappa
be
\{E_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
, with eigenvectors
\{\eta_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
.
Since the mapping
(h+k\circ\overline{f_{\phi}})\mapsto(\widehat{\delta}-k\circ\overline{k})^{-1/2}(h+k\circ\overline{f_{\phi}})(\widehat{\delta}-k\circ\overline{k})^{1/2}
is a similarity transformation, the operator
h_{\rm ph}
also has real spectrum
\{E_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
. From the relation

\omega_{j}(x)=(\widehat{\delta}-k\circ\overline{k})^{1/2}(x,\eta_{j})~,

(16a)

we conclude that
\{\omega_{j}(x)\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
forms a Riesz basis as a bounded perturbation of an orthonormal basis of
\phi^{\perp}
.

(ii)
In a similar vein, the family
\{u_{j}(x)\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
defined by

u_{j}(x):=(\widehat{\delta}-k\circ\overline{k})^{-1/2}(x,\eta_{j})

(16b)

forms a Riesz basis for the adjoint problem on
\phi^{\perp}
.

The resolution of the identity by the eigenvectors
\eta_{j}
of the operator
\varkappa
reads

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}{\eta_{j}(x)\overline{\eta_{j}(y)}}=\widehat{\delta}(x,y)~.

This equation yields completeness relation (
14
), by use of (
16
).
\square

5.3
Proof of Theorem
5.1

We are now in position to prove Theorem
5.1
.
Our argument for the construction of eigenvectors of
\mathcal{H}_{\rm ph}
relies on the fact the

h_{\rm ph}
has discrete spectrum. Let
|\psi\rangle:=|\psi\rangle_{N}
.

Proof

Step 1.
We decompose
\mathcal{H}_{\rm ph}\big|\psi\big>
, where

\mathcal{H}_{\rm ph}
is given in (
13
). We show that

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{N}\mathcal{U}^{\ast}_{m}\mathcal{U}_{m}\mathcal{H}_{\rm ph}\sum_{n=0}^{N}\mathcal{U}^{\ast}_{n}\mathcal{U}_{n}|\psi\rangle=\sum_{m,n=0}^{N}\mathcal{U}^{\ast}_{m}\left\{\mathcal{U}_{m}\mathcal{H}_{\rm ph}\mathcal{U}^{\ast}_{n}\right\}\left(\mathcal{U}_{n}\big|\psi\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{N}\mathcal{U}^{\ast}_{n}h_{\rm ph}(a^{\ast}_{\perp},a_{\perp})\mathcal{U}_{n}|\psi\rangle+\sum_{n=0}^{N-2}b_{N,n}\mathcal{U}^{\ast}_{n}\overline{f_{\phi}}(a_{\perp},a_{\perp})\mathcal{U}_{n+2}|\psi\rangle~.

(17)

Here,
b_{N,n}
is a numerical constant. Regarding the operators
\mathcal{U}_{n}
, see Definition
2
.

In order to derive (
Proof
), we invoke Lemma
4
. In this vein, we notice the relations

\displaystyle h_{\rm ph}(a_{\perp}^{\ast},a_{\perp})|\psi\rangle=\sum_{n,m=0}^{N}\mathcal{U}^{\ast}_{n}\mathcal{U}_{n}h_{\rm ph}(a^{\ast}_{\perp},a_{\perp})\mathcal{U}^{\ast}_{m}\mathcal{U}_{m}|\psi\rangle=\sum_{n=0}^{N}\mathcal{U}_{n}^{\ast}h_{\rm ph}(a_{\perp}^{\ast},a_{\perp})\mathcal{U}_{n}|\psi\rangle~,\color[rgb]{0,0,0}

\displaystyle\frac{{a^{\ast}_{\phi}}^{2}}{N}\overline{f_{\phi}}(a_{\perp},a_{\perp})|\psi\rangle=\sum_{n,m=0}^{N}\mathcal{U}^{\ast}_{n}\mathcal{U}_{n}\frac{{a^{\ast}_{\phi}}^{2}}{N}\overline{f_{\phi}}(a_{\perp},a_{\perp})\mathcal{U}^{\ast}_{m}\mathcal{U}_{m}|\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{n,m=0}^{N}\mathcal{U}^{\ast}_{n}\mathcal{P}_{n,n}\frac{{a_{\overline{\phi}}}^{N-n}}{\sqrt{(N-n)!}}\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\frac{{a^{\ast}_{\phi}}^{2}}{N}\frac{{a^{\ast}_{\phi}}^{N-m}}{\sqrt{(N-m)!}}\mathcal{P}_{m,m}\overline{f_{\phi}}(a_{\perp},a_{\perp})\mathcal{U}_{m}|\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{N-2}b_{N,n}\mathcal{U}^{\ast}_{n}\overline{f_{\phi}}(a_{\perp},a_{\perp})\mathcal{U}_{n+2}|\psi\rangle~,\quad b_{N,n}=\frac{\sqrt{(N-n)(N-n+1)}}{N}~,

since only the terms with
n=m-2
survive in the last double sum.

Step 2.
Next, we describe the finite system that results from the above decomposition.
The first observation is that
\mathcal{U}_{n}|\psi\rangle=|\psi_{N,n}\rangle_{n}

where
\psi_{N,n}(x_{1},\,\ldots,\,x_{n})
is a function orthogonal to the condensate. Let

\psi_{n}:=\psi_{N,n}
, a function of

n
variables where
n=0,\,1,\,\ldots,\,N
. The operator
h_{\rm ph}
acts on each of these functions
\psi_{n}

for
n=1,\,2,\,\ldots,\,N
by preserving the number of variables.
On the other hand, the operator
\overline{f_{\phi}}(a_{\perp},a_{\perp})

maps
\psi_{n+2}
to
\psi_{n}
. Denote the first action by

h_{\rm ph}\circ\psi_{n}
and the second one
by
\overline{f_{\phi}}:\psi_{n+2}
.

We elaborate on these actions. For a symmetric function

\psi_{n}(x_{1},\,\ldots,\,x_{n})
, we have

\displaystyle h_{\rm ph}\circ\psi_{n}

\displaystyle=d_{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int{\rm d}y\ \left\{h_{\rm ph}(x_{j},y)\psi(x_{1},\,\ldots,\,x_{j-1},\,y,\,x_{j+1},\,\ldots,\,x_{n})\right\}~.

Similarly,
\overline{f_{\phi}}
acts on
\psi_{n+2}(x_{1}\ldots x_{n+2})

as follows:

\displaystyle\overline{f_{\phi}}:\psi_{n+2}

\displaystyle=b_{n}\int{\rm d}y_{1}\,{\rm d}y_{2}\ \left\{\overline{f_{\phi}}(y_{1},y_{2})\psi_{n+2}(y_{1},\,y_{2},\,x_{1},\,\ldots,\,x_{n})\right\}~.

In the above,
d_{n}
and
b_{n}
are some (immaterial) numerical constants.

Hence, the eigenvalue equation

\mathcal{H}_{\rm ph}|\psi\rangle=E|\psi\rangle

reduces to a finite system, viz.,

\displaystyle h_{\rm ph}\circ\psi_{N,N}

\displaystyle=E\psi_{N,N}~,

(18a)

\displaystyle h_{\rm ph}\circ\psi_{N,N-2}+b_{2}\overline{f_{\phi}}:\psi_{N,N}

\displaystyle=E\psi_{N,N-2}~,

(18b)

\displaystyle h_{\rm ph}\circ\psi_{N,N-4}+b_{4}\overline{f_{\phi}}:\psi_{N,N-2}

\displaystyle=E\psi_{N,N-4}~,\,\ldots\ .

(18c)

This system has upper triangular form and manifests the effect of pair excitation, since the number of non-condensate particles is reduced in pairs.
The even and odd values of
N
should be considered separately. These equations describe how to compute the
fluctuation vector
|\psi_{\perp}\rangle=\big(\psi_{N,0},\,\psi_{N,1},\,\ldots,\,\psi_{N,N},\,0,\,\ldots\big)
.

Notably, (
18a
) implies the equality of the spectra,

\sigma\left(\mathcal{H}_{\rm ph}\right)=\sigma\left(h_{\rm ph}(a^{\ast}_{\perp},a_{\perp})\right)
, on
\mathbb{F}_{N}
.
Indeed, if (
18a
)
has only the trivial solution then all the subsequent equations
have trivial solutions. The upper triangular form suggests that
we can construct the eigenvalues explicitly. Note that the top equation
has infinitely many possible solutions corresponding to the spectrum of
h_{\rm ph}
– but choosing one of them results in a finite system of equations.

We now give the relevant construction, which serves as a proof of existence for system (
18
). For example, start with (see Lemma
5
)

\displaystyle\Omega_{N}=\prod_{p=1}^{N}\omega_{j_{p}}(x_{p})

for given
j_{p}
(
p=0,\,1,\,\ldots,\,N
) so that
\Omega_{N}
is an eigenvector of
h_{\rm ph}(a_{\perp}^{\ast},a_{\perp})
, viz.,

\displaystyle h_{\rm ph}\circ\Omega_{N}=\left(\sum_{p=0}^{N}E_{j_{p}}\right)\Omega_{N}~.

The action of
\overline{f_{\phi}}
on the state
\Omega_{N}
produces the collection of states

\displaystyle\Omega_{l,m}:=\prod_{p=0\atop p\not=l,m}^{N}\omega_{j_{p}}(x_{p})~;\qquad l,\,m=0,\,1,\,\ldots,\,N~.

We can determine
\Omega_{N-2}:=\sum_{l,m}c_{l,m}\Omega_{l,m}
which plays the role of
\psi_{N,N-2}
.
By substituting into (
18b
), we obtain the system

c_{l,m}\big(E_{j_{l}}+E_{j_{m}}\big)=b_{2}\overline{f_{\phi}}(\omega_{j_{l}},\omega_{j_{m}})
which yields
c_{l,m}
.
The next state,
\Omega_{N-4}
, is a linear combination
of
\omega_{j_{k}}
where four terms have been removed from the
original collection. The idea of computation is similar. One can proceed until all non-condensate particles are removed.
This argument concludes our explicit construction of the eigenvectors of

\mathcal{H}_{\rm ph}
in terms of eigenvectors of
h_{\rm ph}
.
\square

It is of some interest to observe that the eigenvectors of

\mathcal{H}_{\rm ph}
contain (in part) the condensate wave function,
in contrast to the eigenvectors of
h_{\rm ph}(a^{\ast}_{\perp},a_{\perp})
.

6
Connections to a Hermitian approach and
J
-self-adjoint system

In this section, we focus on how our existence theory for kernel
k
is connected to another approach, namely, the use of a (Hermitian) Hamiltonian that does not conserve the number of particles
fetter72
;
Lewin2014
. This Hamiltonian results from the Bogoliubov approximation and has the same spectrum as our non-Hermitian
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{app}}
when restricted to
\mathbb{F}_{N}
. Our analysis reveals a connection between (unitary) Bogoliubov-type rotations of quadratic Hamiltonians, the Riccati equation for
k
, and the theory of
J
-self-adjoint operators developed by Albeverio and coworkers
AlbeverioMotovilov2019
;
Albeverio2009
;
AlbeverioMotovilov2010
(see also
Tretter2016
;
Tretter-book
). These works, however, appear not to address the possible presence of infinitely many solutions to the Riccati equation which is suggested by our existence theory. We also point out that our results so far imply the existence of solutions to the eigenvalue problem for Boson excitations formulated by Fetter, if his delta-function interaction potential is regularized
fetter72
.

6.1
On a reduced Hamiltonian via Bogoliubov approximation

Recall our reduced Hamiltonian with a smooth interaction potential (Sect.
3.1
), viz.,

\mathcal{H}_{\text{app}}=NE_{\rm H}+h(a^{\ast}_{\perp},a_{\perp})+\frac{1}{2N}f_{\phi}(a^{\ast}_{\perp},a^{\ast}_{\perp})a_{\overline{\phi}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2N}\overline{f_{\phi}}(a_{\perp},a_{\perp}){a^{\ast}_{\phi}}^{2}~.

Let us now apply the Bogoliubov approximation to this
\mathcal{H}_{\text{app}}
by formally replacing the operators
a_{\overline{\phi}},\,a^{\ast}_{\overline{\phi}}
with
\sqrt{N}
. This results in the Hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{Bog}}:\mathbb{F}\mapsto\mathbb{F}
where

\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{Bog}}:=NE_{\mathrm{H}}+h(a^{\ast}_{\perp},a_{\perp})+\frac{1}{2}{f_{\phi}}(a^{\ast}_{\perp},a^{\ast}_{\perp})+\frac{1}{2}\overline{{f_{\phi}}}(a_{\perp},a_{\perp})~,

(19)

which does not commute with the number operator
\mathcal{N}
.

Next, we discuss the diagonalization of
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{Bog}}
by using eigenstates of the operator
h_{\mathrm{ph}}:\mathfrak{h}^{1}_{V}\cap\phi^{\perp}\to\mathfrak{h}
defined by (
13b
) (Sect.
5
). We proceed in the spirit of Fetter
fetter72
, who diagonalizes
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{Bog}}-NE_{H}
via (unitary) Bogoliubov-type rotations of the Boson field operators in the space orthogonal to
\phi
; see equation (2.14) for a delta-function interaction potential in
fetter72
. In this vein, let us consider Fetter’s “quasiparticle” operators
\gamma_{j},\gamma_{j}^{\ast}
which are defined as follows
fetter72
.

Definition 3

The operators
\gamma_{j},\,\gamma^{\ast}_{j}:\mathbb{F}\mapsto\mathbb{F}
(
j=1,\,2,\,\dots
) are defined by

\gamma_{j}:=\int{\rm d}x\ \{\overline{u_{j}(x)}a_{\perp,x}+\overline{v_{j}(x)}a^{\ast}_{\perp,x}\}~,\quad\gamma_{j}^{\ast}:=\int{\rm d}x\ \{u_{j}(x)a^{\ast}_{\perp,x}+v_{j}(x)a_{\perp,x}\}~.

In the above,
\{u_{j}(x)\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
is a Riesz basis of
\phi^{\perp}
, and
\{v_{j}(x)\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
are chosen such that
\gamma_{j}
and
\gamma_{j}^{\ast}
satisfy the canonical commutation relations.

One can verify that
\gamma_{j},\gamma^{\ast}_{j}
satisfy the canonical commutation relations provided

\begin{split}\int{\rm d}x\ \{u_{j}(x)v_{j^{\prime}}(x)-v_{j}(x)u_{j^{\prime}}(x)\}&=0~,\\
\int{\rm d}x\ \{u_{j}(x)\overline{u_{j^{\prime}}(x)}-v_{j}(x)\overline{v_{j^{\prime}}(x)}\}&=\delta_{jj^{\prime}}~.\end{split}

(20)

We proceed to show that the diagonalization of
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{Bog}}
in terms of
\gamma_{j}
and
\gamma_{j}^{*}
implies that
\{u_{j}(x),v_{j}(x)\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
must solve a linear system of PDEs. Following Fetter’s procedure
fetter72
, let us momentarily assume the following completeness relations:

\begin{split}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}{\{u_{j}(x)\overline{u_{j}(x^{\prime})}-\overline{v_{j}(x)}v_{j}(x^{\prime})\}}&=\widehat{\delta}(x,x^{\prime})~,\\
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}{\{u_{j}(x)\overline{v_{j}(x^{\prime})}-\overline{v_{j}(x)}u_{j}(x^{\prime})\}}&=0,\quad\forall x,\,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}~.\end{split}

(21)

In conjunction with Definition
3
, these relations allow us to decompose the Boson field operators
a_{\perp,x}
and
a^{\ast}_{\perp,x}
as

a_{\perp,x}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}{\{u_{j}(x)\gamma_{j}-\overline{v_{j}(x)}\gamma_{j}^{\ast}\}}~,\quad a^{\ast}_{\perp,x}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}{\{\overline{u_{j}(x)}\gamma_{j}^{\ast}-v_{j}(x)\gamma_{j}\}}~.

These two relations together with Definition
3
amount to a Bogoliubov-type (unitary) transformation in the space orthogonal to the condensate
\phi
.
The substitution of these expressions into (
19
) along with the requirement that the terms proportional to
\gamma_{j}\gamma_{l}
and
\gamma_{j}^{\ast}\gamma_{l}^{\ast}
vanish (for all
j,\,l=1,\,2,\,\ldots
) yields the following eigenvalue problem involving a symplectic matrix:

\begin{pmatrix}h^{T}_{\perp}&-{f_{\phi}}_{\perp}\\
\overline{{f_{\phi}}}_{\perp}&-h_{\perp}\end{pmatrix}\circ\begin{pmatrix}u_{j}(x)\\
v_{j}(x)\end{pmatrix}=E_{j}\begin{pmatrix}u_{j}(x)\\
v_{j}(x)\end{pmatrix}~;\quad j=1,\,2,\,\ldots\,.

(22)

In the above,
h_{\perp}
and
{f_{\phi}}_{\perp}
are the projections of operators
h
and
f_{\phi}
on space
\phi^{\perp}
. System (
22
) should be compared to equations (2.21a,b) in
fetter72
. We alert the reader that the notation for
u_{j}
and
E_{j}
here is the same as the one used for the eigenvectors and eigenvalues of
h_{\rm ph}^{\ast}
in Sect.
5.2
. In fact, the corresponding quantities turn out to be identical in the two eigenvalue problems, as we discuss below.

6.2
On the existence of solutions to eigenvalue problem for
(u_{j},\,v_{j})

Let us recall the spectral theory for
h_{\rm ph}
, particularly Lemma
5
(Sect.
5.2
). We should also add that this theory relies on the existence of solutions to the Riccati equation for
k
, Theorem
4.1
(Sect.
4
). To make a connection to system (
22
), consider the solutions
\omega_{j}
and
u_{j}
(
j=1,\,2,\,\ldots
) to the eigenvalue problem for
h_{\rm ph}
and its adjoint. This
problem is expressed by the equations

\begin{split}h_{\mathrm{ph}}(x,\omega_{j})=(h+k\circ\overline{f_{\phi}})(x,\omega_{j})&=E_{j}\omega_{j}(x)~,\\
h^{\ast}_{\mathrm{ph}}(x,u_{j})=(h^{T}+f_{\phi}\circ\overline{k})(x,u_{j})&=E_{j}u_{j}(x)\qquad(j=1,\,2,\,\ldots\,)~.\end{split}

Notice that if we define
v_{j}(x):=-\overline{k}(x,u_{j})

then the (adjoint) equation for
u_{j}(x)
here immediately takes the form of the first equation in system (
22
). We can show that this definition for
v_{j}
also gives the second equation in system (
22
) by employing the conjugate Riccati equation (for
\overline{k}
). Indeed, notice that

\begin{split}-h_{\perp}(x,v_{j})&=(h_{\perp}\circ\overline{k})(x,u_{j})=(-\overline{k}\circ h_{\perp}^{T}-\overline{f_{\phi}}-\overline{k}\circ f_{\phi}\circ\overline{k})(x,u_{j})\\
&=-\overline{k}\circ\big[E_{j}u_{j}(x)-f_{\phi}\circ\overline{k}(x,u_{j})\big]-\overline{f_{\phi}}(x,u_{j})-(\overline{k}\circ f_{\phi}\circ\overline{k})(x,u_{j})\\
&=-E_{j}v_{j}(x)-\overline{f_{\phi}}(x,u_{j})~.\end{split}

Hence, the existence of eigenvectors
\{\omega_{j},u_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
and spectrum
\{E_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
in regard to
h_{\rm ph}
entails the existence of solutions to system (
22
).

Remark 7

(i)
We showed an intimate connection of the eigenvalue problem for
h_{\rm ph}
and its adjoint, based on the Riccati equation for kernel
k
, to PDE system (
22
) coming from Fetter’s Hermitian view. A direct comparison to the results in
fetter72
is meaningful if Fetter’s delta-function interaction is appropriately regularized. This connection is in fact a manifestation of a deeper theory which links the Riccati equation to
J
-self-adjoint matrix operators
AlbeverioMotovilov2019
;
Albeverio2009
;
AlbeverioMotovilov2010
. We briefly discuss aspects of this theory in Sect.
6.3
.

(ii)
In Fetter’s paper
fetter72
, an ansatz for the many-body ground state,
|\psi_{0}\rangle
, of the quadratic Hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{Bog}}
on
\mathbb{F}
is

|\psi_{0}\rangle=Ze^{\mathcal{G}}\{{a_{\overline{\phi}}^{\ast}}^{N}\}|vac\rangle~;\quad\mathcal{G}:=\frac{1}{2}\int{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y\ b(x,y)\,a^{\ast}_{\perp,x}a^{\ast}_{\perp,y}~.

However, a single governing equation for the associated kernel
b(x,y)
is not given in
fetter72
. Instead, the condition
\gamma_{j}|\psi_{0}\rangle=0
is applied for all
j=1,2,\dots
, which yields the following system of integral relations:

\int{\rm d}y\ \big\{b(x,y)\overline{u}_{j}(y)\big\}=-\overline{v_{j}(x)}\qquad(j=1,\,2,\,\ldots)~.

Evidently, by comparison of this formalism to our approach, we realize that kernel
b
coincides with
k
, and the above integral relations are already a consequence of our solution for
k
. In fact, in
fetter72
Fetter uses the above integral system to define the kernel

b(x,y)=k(x,y)
when
\{u_{j}(x),v_{j}(x)\}
solve the matrix eigenvalue problem (
22
) (under a delta-function interaction). Our existence proof for
k
furnished in the context of Theorem
4.1
shows that the ground state
|\psi_{0}\rangle
is self-consistent, in the sense that the integral system stemming from
|\psi_{0}\rangle
and (
22
) is well-posed if a solution to the Riccati equation for
k
exists.

At this stage, we find it compelling to give the following corollary for system (
22
).

Corollary 1

For
\{u_{j}(x),v_{j}(x)\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
that solve (
22
), completeness relations (
21
) and orthogonality relations (
20
) hold.

Proof

We resort to the spectral theory of operator
h_{\rm ph}
on space
\phi^{\perp}
, particularly the proof of Lemma
5
(Sect.
5.2
).
Recall the completeness relation for the basis
\{\eta_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
of
\phi^{\perp}
, as well as the relation
\eta_{j}(x)=(\widehat{\delta}-k\circ\overline{k})^{1/2}(x,u_{j})
.

Hence, on
\phi^{\perp}
we have

\begin{split}\widehat{\delta}(x,x^{\prime})&=(\widehat{\delta}-k\circ\overline{k})^{1/2}\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}{\eta_{j}(x)\overline{\eta_{j}(x^{\prime})}}\right\}(\widehat{\delta}-k\circ\overline{k})^{-1/2}\\
&=(\widehat{\delta}-k\circ\overline{k})\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}{u_{j}(x)\overline{\eta_{j}(x^{\prime})}}\right\}(\widehat{\delta}-k\circ\overline{k})^{-1/2}=(\widehat{\delta}-k\circ\overline{k})\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}{u_{j}(x)\overline{u_{j}(x^{\prime})}}~.\end{split}

Thus, we obtain

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}{u_{j}(x)\overline{u_{j}(x^{\prime})}}=\frac{\widehat{\delta}(x,x^{\prime})}{\widehat{\delta}-k\circ\overline{k}}~.

By use of the relation
v_{j}(x)=-\overline{k}(x,u_{j})
, we can therefore assert that

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}{\overline{v_{j}(x)}v_{j}(x^{\prime})}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}k(\overline{u_{j}},x){\overline{k}(x^{\prime},u_{j})}=\frac{k\circ\overline{k}}{\widehat{\delta}-k\circ\overline{k}}~.

The last two equations entail the first relation of (
21
).

Next, we invoke the equation just derived to write

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}{u_{j}(x)\overline{v_{j}(x^{\prime})}}=-\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}u_{j}(x)k(x^{\prime},\overline{u_{j}})=-(\widehat{\delta}-k\circ\overline{k})^{-1}\circ k~.

Alternatively, we have

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}{\overline{v_{j}(x)}u_{j}(x^{\prime})}=-\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}{k(x,\overline{u_{j}})u_{j}(x^{\prime})}=-k\circ(\widehat{\delta}-\overline{k}\circ k)^{-1}~.

Thus, we obtain the second completeness relation of (
21
) by using the identity
(\widehat{\delta}-k\circ\overline{k})^{-1}\circ k=k\circ(\widehat{\delta}-\overline{k}\circ k)^{-1}
.

Regarding orthogonality relations (
20
), the manipulation of system (
22
) yields the following equations:

\begin{split}(E_{j}-\overline{E_{j^{\prime}}})\int{\rm d}x\ \{u_{j}(x)\overline{u_{j^{\prime}}(x)}\}&=\int{\rm d}x\ \{-\overline{u_{j^{\prime}}(x)}f_{\phi}(x,v_{j})+u_{j}(x)\overline{f_{\phi}}(x,\overline{v_{j^{\prime}}})\}~,\\
(E_{j}-\overline{E_{j^{\prime}}})\int{\rm d}x\ \{v_{j}(x)\overline{v_{j^{\prime}}(x)}\}&=\int{\rm d}x\ \{\overline{v_{j^{\prime}}(x)}\,\overline{f_{\phi}}(x,u_{j})-v_{j}(x)f_{\phi}(x,\overline{u_{j^{\prime}}})\}~.\end{split}

By subtracting the second equation from the first one, we can obtain the second orthogonality relation of (
20
), if
\|u_{j}\|^{2}_{2}-\|v_{j}\|^{2}_{2}\neq 0
and this normalization for
u_{j}
and
v_{j}
is chosen to give unity. The first orthogonality relation of (
20
) follows by a similar procedure which we omit here.
\square

6.3
On the
J
-self-adjoint system

Next, we discuss in more detail the connection between Riccati equation (
6b
) and main ideas from the theory of
J
-self-adjoint operators found in, e.g.,
AlbeverioMotovilov2019
;
Albeverio2009
;
AlbeverioMotovilov2010
. A link between these two theories is suggested by the eigenvalue problem (
22
), which involves the symplectic matrix

M:=\begin{pmatrix}h^{T}_{\perp}&-{f_{\phi}}_{\perp}\\
\overline{f_{\phi}}_{\perp}&-h_{\perp}\end{pmatrix};\quad\mathrm{dom}(M):=\mathfrak{h}^{1}_{V}\oplus\mathfrak{h}^{1}_{V}~.

(23)

Note the matrix

\widetilde{M}:=\begin{pmatrix}h^{T}&-{f_{\phi}}\\
\overline{f_{\phi}}&-h\end{pmatrix}

has the zero eigenvalue with eigenvector
(\phi,\overline{\phi})
.

Suppose that
\phi(x)
,
h(x,y)
and
{f_{\phi}}(x,y)
satisfy the assumptions of Theorem
4.1
(Sect.
4.2
). Let
k
be the unique solution to the Riccati equation with
\|k\|_{\mathrm{op}}<1
. Then the operator matrix

W:=\begin{pmatrix}\widehat{\delta}&\quad k\\
\overline{k}&\quad\widehat{\delta}\end{pmatrix}:\phi^{\perp}\oplus\phi^{\perp}\mapsto\phi^{\perp}\oplus\phi^{\perp}

is boundedly invertible
Albeverio2009
, with inverse

W^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}(\widehat{\delta}-k\circ\overline{k})^{-1}&-k\circ(\widehat{\delta}-\overline{k}\circ k)^{-1}\\
-\overline{k}\circ(\widehat{\delta}-k\circ\overline{k})^{-1}&(\widehat{\delta}-\overline{k}\circ k)^{-1}\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\end{pmatrix}~.

Now let us consider the diagonal matrix

D:=\begin{pmatrix}h^{T}_{\perp}+k\circ\overline{f_{\phi}}_{\perp}&0\\
0&-h_{\perp}-\overline{k}\circ{f_{\phi}}_{\perp}\end{pmatrix}~.

The spectrum of
D
is
\sigma(\overline{h_{\mathrm{ph}}})\cup\sigma(-h_{\mathrm{ph}})
, which under the assumptions of Theorem
4.1
consists of two disjoint parts.
Since
k
obeys the Riccati equation on
\phi^{\perp}
, we have

DW=\begin{pmatrix}h^{T}_{\perp}+k\circ\overline{f_{\phi}}_{\perp}&\quad-k\circ h_{\perp}-{f_{\phi}}_{\perp}\\
\overline{k}\circ h^{T}_{\perp}+\overline{f_{\phi}}_{\perp}&\quad-h_{\perp}-\overline{k}\circ{f_{\phi}}_{\perp}\end{pmatrix}=WM~,

where
M
is defined by (
23
). Thus, the matrix
M
is similar to the diagonal matrix
D
.

We proceed to describe implications of this similarity relation. Eigenvectors of the diagonal operator matrix
D
are of two types. One type is of the form
\big({\omega}_{j}(x),0\big)
where
{\omega}_{j}(x)
is an eigenvector of
{h_{\mathrm{ph}}}
, and another type is of the form
\big(0,\overline{\omega_{j}(x)}\big)
(see Sect.
5.2
). This fact yields two types of eigenvectors for
M
after transformation by
W^{-1}
, viz.,

W^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}{\omega}_{j}(x)\\
0\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}(\widehat{\delta}-k\circ\overline{k})^{-1}(x,{\omega}_{j})\\
-\overline{k}\circ(\widehat{\delta}-k\circ\overline{k})^{-1}(x,\omega_{j})\end{pmatrix}~,

and

W^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\overline{\omega_{j}(x)}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}-k\circ(\widehat{\delta}-\overline{k}\circ k)^{-1}(x,\overline{\omega_{j}})\\
(\widehat{\delta}-\overline{k}\circ k)^{-1}(x,\overline{\omega_{j}})\end{pmatrix}~.

For the second type of eigenvector, we make the identifications

u_{j}(x):=(\widehat{\delta}-k\circ\overline{k})^{-1}(x,\omega_{j})\quad\mbox{and}\quad v_{j}(x):=-\overline{k}\circ(\widehat{\delta}-k\circ\overline{k})^{-1}(x,\omega_{j})=-\overline{k}(x,u_{j})~.

The eigenvectors of the second type should be excluded because they yield a negative spectrum.

Remark 8

So far, we assumed that the Riccati equation for
k
is satisfied (and solutions to this equation exist by Theorem
4.1
). Conversely, if we assume that the integral system
v_{j}=-\overline{k}(x,u_{j})
as well as PDE system (
22
) hold then
k
must obey the Riccati equation. This claim can be proved by use of the methods that we already developed.

7
Conclusion

In concluding this paper, we stress the intimate, and perhaps surprising, mathematical connection between two apparently disparate approaches (those of Fetter
fetter72
and Wu
wu61
) to the problem of Boson excitations via the theory of
J
-self-adjoint operators
Albeverio2009
. The results presented here form an application of its powerful machinery to a physics-inspired problem with interesting implications.

Notably, the similarity relation
WMW^{-1}=D
, discussed in Sect.
6.3
, shows that the spectrum of
h_{\mathrm{ph}}
can change for different solutions to the Riccati equation, but in a predictable way. In particular, since the spectrum
\sigma(M)=\sigma(D)=\sigma(h_{\mathrm{ph}})\cup\sigma(-h_{\mathrm{ph}})
, the (double) spectrum
\sigma(h_{\mathrm{ph}})\cup\sigma(-h_{\mathrm{ph}})
is unaffected by the choice of
k
solving the Riccati equation. However, the spectrum
\sigma(h_{\mathrm{ph}})
will change under different choices of solutions for
k
. In light of our analysis, the only possible change induced by
\sigma(h_{\mathrm{ph}}(k))\mapsto\sigma(h_{\mathrm{ph}}(k^{\prime}))
for two different solutions
k
and
k^{\prime}
(
k^{\prime}\not=k
) is such that a finite collection of eigenvalues
\{E_{j}\}\subset\sigma(h_{\mathrm{phon}}(k))
is mapped to
\{-E_{j}\}\subset\sigma(h_{\mathrm{phon}}(k^{\prime}))
while the rest of the eigenvalues remain unchanged.

We are tempted to mention a few open problems motivated by our work. For example, given the existence of the kernel
k
with
\|k\|_{\text{op}}<1
, it is of interest to study the existence of the Boson pair correlation function in a trap at zero temperature. Another possible extension is to consider the effect of a non-unitary transformation analogous to
e^{\mathcal{W}}
by including contributions from higher-order (cubic and quartic terms) in the reduced many-body Hermitian Hamiltonian. This consideration would plausibly require the introduction of additional kernels, which must satisfy several consistency conditions. Finally, it is conceivable that the non-Hermitian approach involving
k
can be extended to the setting of finite (positive) temperatures below the phase transition in the presence of a trapping potential. In the spirit of the periodic case
leeyang
, we could construct an effective quadratic Hamiltonian that involves a parameter expressing the average fraction of particles at the condensate, and subsequently transform it non-unitarily. Alternatively, one may use a Hermitian approach at finite temperatures akin to Fetter’s formalism, e.g., the approach of
Griffin1996
.

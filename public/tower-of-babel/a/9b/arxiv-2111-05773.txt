Alexandrov-Fenchel inequality for convex hypersurfaces with capillary boundary in a ball Thanks: 2010 Mathematics Subject Classification. 53C21, 53C44, 35K96, 52A40
Liangjun Weng and Chao Xia

Abstract
. In this paper, we first introduce the quermassintegrals for convex hypersurfaces with capillary boundary in the unit Euclidean ball 𝔹 n + 1 \mathbb{B}^{n+1} and derive its first variational formula. Then by using a locally constrained nonlinear curvature flow, which preserves the n n -th quermassintegral and non-decreases the k k -th quermassintegral, we obtain the Alexandrov-Fenchel inequality for convex hypersurfaces with capillary boundary in 𝔹 n + 1 \mathbb{B}^{n+1} . This generalizes the result in [ 42 ] for convex hypersurfaces with free boundary in 𝔹 n + 1 \mathbb{B}^{n+1} .



1.
Introduction

Isoperimetric inequality is one of the fundamental topics in differential geometry. The classical isoperimetric inequality in the Euclidean space says that among all bounded domains in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with fixed enclosed volume, the minimum of the area functional is achieved by round balls.
The higher order generalizations of isoperimetric inequality in convex geometry are the Alexandrov-Fenchel inequalities for quermassintegrals, which say that for closed convex hypersurfaces
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, it holds that

\int_{\Sigma}H_{k}dA\geq\omega_{n}^{\frac{k-l}{n-l}}\left(\int_{\Sigma}H_{l}dA\right)^{\frac{n-k}{n-l}},

with equality holding on the round spheres.
Here
H_{k}
is the normalized
k
-th mean curvature of
\Sigma
and
\omega_{n}
denotes the surface area of
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. See
[
8
,
38
,
39
]
for instance.

Fix a domain
B
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, whose boundary
\partial B
is named support hypersurface, the partitioning problem or the relative isoperimetric problem is to find hypersurfaces
\Sigma
in
B
with least area functional among all hypersurfaces with divide
B
into two components with prescribed ratio. When
B
is
\mathbb{B}^{n+1}
, the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, the solution to this problem is given by all spherical caps or totally geodesic balls with free boundary, cf.
[
6
,
7
,
49
]
. Here a hypersurface with free boundary means that it intersects with
\partial\mathbb{B}^{n+1}=\mathbb{S}^{n}
orthogonally.
The higher order generalization of relative isoperimetric inequality – the relative Alexandrov-Fenchel inequalities in
\mathbb{B}^{n+1}
have been considered by Scheuer-Wang-Xia in
[
42
]
. They gave the definition of quermassintegral in
\mathbb{B}^{n+1}
from the viewpoint of the first variational formula, and they proved the highest order Alexandrov-Fenchel inequalities and the Gauss-Bonnet-Chern formula for convex hypersurfaces with free boundary in
\mathbb{B}^{n+1}
. Willmore type geometric inequalities for convex hypersurface with free boundary in a ball have been obtained in
[
30
,
42
]
.

Motivated by the study of the equilibrium shapes of a liquid confined in a given container, there is a more general relative isoperimetric problem in a fixed domain
B
, where the energy functional

(1.1)

\displaystyle|\partial\Omega\cap B|-\cos\theta|\partial B\cap\Omega|

is involved. Here
\Omega\subset B
and
\theta\in(0,\pi)
,
|\partial B\cap\Omega|
is the so-called wetting energy. For more physical background, we refer to Finn’s book
[
17
]
.
It is known that among all hypersurfaces which divide
\mathbb{B}^{n+1}
into two components with prescribed energy, the ones with least energy functional are all spherical caps or totally geodesic balls which intersects at the constant angle
\theta
, cf.
[
49
]
. We shall call such boundary condition the
\theta
-capillary boundary.

In the same spirit of
[
42
]
, we are interested in finding the quermassintegral for convex hypersurfaces with
\theta
-capillary boundary in
\mathbb{B}^{n+1}
, which should be the right higher order generalization of the energy functional. Moreover, in this paper, we shall study the Alexandrov-Fenchel inequalities and the Gauss-Bonnet-Chern formula for such hypersurfaces.

Let
n\geq 2
. Let
\mathbb{B}^{n}
and
\mathbb{B}^{n+1}
be the
n
-dimensional and
(n+1)
-dimensional open Euclidean unit ball centered at the origin, respectively. Let
\mathbb{S}^{n}=\partial\mathbb{B}^{n+1}
and
\bar{N}
denote the unit outward normal of
\mathbb{S}^{n}
.
Let
\Sigma\subset\overline{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}
be a smooth, orientable, compact hypersurface with boundary, given by an isometric embedding

x:\overline{\mathbb{B}}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}
.
\Sigma
is said to be properly embedded, if

\Sigma=x(\mathbb{B}^{n})\subset{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1},\quad\partial\Sigma=x(\partial\mathbb{B}^{n})\subset\mathbb{S}^{n}.

We are concerned with the case when
\Sigma
is a convex hypersurface with capillary boundary. We choose
\nu
to be the unit normal of
\Sigma
such that
\Sigma
is convex in the sense that the second fundamental form defined by
h(X,Y)=-\langle D_{X}Y,\nu\rangle
is non-negative definite, where
D
is the Euclidean connection.

Definition 1.1
.

For
\theta\in(0,\pi)
,
\Sigma
is said to be with a
\theta
-capillary boundary, if
\Sigma
intersects
\mathbb{S}^{n}
at the constant angle
\theta
, that is,

\displaystyle\langle\nu,\overline{N}\circ x\rangle=-\cos\theta,\hbox{ along }\partial\mathbb{B}^{n}.

In particular, if
\theta=\frac{\pi}{2}
, or equivalently say that
\Sigma
intersects
\partial\mathbb{B}^{n+1}
orthogonally, we say that
\Sigma
is with free boundary.

The model examples of properly embedded hypersurfaces with a
\theta
-capillary boundary are
the spherical cap of radius
r\in(0,\infty)
around some constant unit vector field
e
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with
\theta
-capillary boundary
, given by

(1.2)

\displaystyle C_{\theta,r}(e):=\{x\in\overline{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}:\left|x-\sqrt{r^{2}+2r\cos\theta+1}e\right|=r\},

and
the flat ball around
e\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
with
\theta
-capillary boundary
, given by

(1.3)

\displaystyle C_{\theta,\infty}(e):=\{x\in\overline{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}:\langle x,e\rangle=\cos\theta\}.

We shall drop the argument
e
, in cases where it is not relevant.

For our purpose, we always assume
\Sigma
is a convex hypersurface with
\theta
-capillary boundary for
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
.
Then
\partial\Sigma\subset\mathbb{S}^{n}
is a strictly convex hypersurface in
\mathbb{S}^{n}
(see Proposition
2.5
) and it bounds a strictly convex body in
\mathbb{S}^{n}
, which we denote by
\widehat{\partial\Sigma}
, cf.
[
13
,
22
]
. Denote by
\widehat{\Sigma}
the domain enclosed by
\Sigma
in
\mathbb{B}^{n+1}
which contains
\widehat{\partial\Sigma}
.
Let
\sigma_{k}
denote the
k
-th elementary symmetric polynomial, evaluated at the principal curvatures of
\Sigma
, and

H_{k}=\frac{1}{\binom{n}{k}}\sigma_{k},\quad 1\leq k\leq n.

We make the convention that

\sigma_{0}=H_{0}=1\quad\sigma_{n+1}=H_{n+1}=0.

Now we define the following geometric functionals for convex hypersurfaces with
\theta
-capillary boundary in
\mathbb{B}^{n+1}
as follows, which we expect to be the correct counterparts to the quermassintegrals for closed convex hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
:

\displaystyle W_{0,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma}):=|\widehat{\Sigma}|,

(1.4)

\displaystyle W_{1,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma}):=\frac{1}{n+1}\left(|\Sigma|-\cos\theta W_{0}^{\mathbb{S}^{n}}(\widehat{\partial\Sigma})\right),

(1.5)

\displaystyle W_{n+1,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma}):=\frac{1}{n+1}\left[\int_{\Sigma}H_{n}dA-\sum_{l=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{n+l}\binom{n}{l}\cos^{n-1-l}\theta\sin^{l}\theta W_{l}^{\mathbb{S}^{n}}(\widehat{\partial\Sigma})\right],

and for
1\leq k\leq n-1
,

(1.6)

\displaystyle W_{k+1,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma}):=\frac{1}{n+1}\Bigg\{\int_{\Sigma}H_{k}dA-\cos\theta\sin^{k}\theta W_{k}^{\mathbb{S}^{n}}(\widehat{\partial\Sigma})

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}\frac{(-1)^{k+l}}{n-l}\binom{k}{l}\Big[(n-k)\cos^{2}\theta+(k-l)\Big]\cos^{k-1-l}\theta\sin^{l}\theta W_{l}^{\mathbb{S}^{n}}(\widehat{\partial\Sigma})\Bigg\}.

Here for a
k
-dimensional submanifold
M\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
(with or without boundary),
|M|
always denotes the
k
-dimensional Hausdorff measure of
M
.
W_{k}^{\mathbb{S}^{n}}
denotes the
k
-th quermassintegral of the closed convex hypersurface
\partial\Sigma\subset\mathbb{S}^{n}
, see Section
2.4
for more discussion.
We remark that
W_{1,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})
is just a multiple of the energy functional (
1.1
) we mentioned before, or see e.g.
[
37
,
46
,
49
]
.
When
\theta=\frac{\pi}{2}
,
W_{k,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})
is exactly the quermassintegrals defined in
[
42
]
.

The reason to define
W_{k+1,\theta}
to be the quermassintegral for convex hypersurfaces with
\theta
-capillary boundary in
\overline{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}
is the following first variational formula.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Sigma_{t}\subset\overline{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}
be a family of smooth, properly embedded hypersurfaces with
\theta
-capillary boundary, given by
x(\cdot,t):\overline{\mathbb{B}}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}
, such that

(\partial_{t}x)^{\perp}=f\nu,

for some normal speed function
f
. Then for
1\leq k\leq n
,

(1.7)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}W_{k,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma_{t}})=\frac{n+1-k}{n+1}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}H_{k}fdA_{t},

and

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}W_{n+1,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma_{t}})=0.

We obtain the following Alexandrov-Fenchel type inequalities and Gauss-Bonnet-Chern formula for convex hypersurfaces with
\theta
-capillary boundary in
\overline{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
and
\Sigma\subset\overline{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}
be a convex hypersurface with
\theta
-capillary boundary.
Then

(1.8)

\displaystyle W_{n+1,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})=\frac{\omega_{n}}{2(n+1)}I_{\sin^{2}\theta}(\frac{n}{2},\frac{1}{2}),

where
I_{s}(\frac{n}{2},\frac{1}{2})
is the regularized incomplete beta function given by

I_{s}(\frac{n}{2},\frac{1}{2})=\frac{\int_{0}^{s}t^{\frac{n}{2}-1}(1-t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}dt}{\int_{0}^{1}t^{\frac{n}{2}-1}(1-t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}dt}.

We also have that for
0\leq k\leq n-1
,

(1.9)

\displaystyle W_{n,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})\geq(f_{n}\circ f_{k}^{-1})(W_{k,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})),

where
f_{k}:=f_{k}(r)
is the strictly increasing real function given by

\displaystyle f_{k}(r):=W_{k,\theta}(\widehat{C_{\theta,r}}),

where
C_{\theta,r}
is the spherical cap given by (
1.2
). Moreover, equality holds if and only if
\Sigma
is a spherical cap or a flat ball with
\theta
-capillary boundary.

In particular, for
n=2
, we obtain a Minkowski type inequality for convex surfaces in
\overline{\mathbb{B}}^{3}
with
\theta
-capillary boundary.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
and
\Sigma\subset\overline{\mathbb{B}}^{3}
be a convex surface with
\theta
-capillary boundary. Then

\displaystyle 3W_{3,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})=\int_{\Sigma}H_{2}dA-\cos\theta|\widehat{\partial\Sigma}|+\sin\theta|\partial\Sigma|={2\pi}(1-\cos\theta),

and

(1.10)

\displaystyle W_{2,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{6}\left(\int_{\Sigma}HdA-\sin\theta\cos\theta|\partial\Sigma|+(1+\cos^{2}\theta)|\widehat{\partial\Sigma}|\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(f_{2}\circ f_{1}^{-1})\left(\frac{1}{3}(|\Sigma|-\cos\theta|\widehat{\partial\Sigma}|)\right).

Moreover, equality holds if and only if
\Sigma
is a spherical cap or a flat disk with
\theta
-capillary boundary.

We remark that, when
\theta=\frac{\pi}{2}
, Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
have been proved in
[
42
]
.

The method of our proof follows the classical method for proving geometric inequalities by employing monotonicity properties along and convergence of a suitable curvature flow. We will use the locally constrained inverse type curvature flow with capillary boundary in this paper to achieve the inequality (
1.9
) in Theorem
1.3
. Such locally constrained flow has been first considered by Guan-Li
[
24
]
, and used in
[
5
,
11
,
24
,
25
,
26
,
28
]
for closed hypersurfaces in space forms, and in
[
42
,
50
]
for hypersurfaces with free boundary in balls to prove various geometric inequalities. The Minkowski formulas play an essential role to design these flows. We refer to
[
5
,
42
,
43
]
for more description to such locally constrained flows.
We also refer to
[
2
,
3
,
12
,
14
,
19
,
27
,
31
,
33
,
48
]
and references therein for the studying of the Alexandrov-Fenchel type inequalities for closed hypersurfaces in space forms which used various types of curvature flow.
In addition to the curvature flow approach, there are also many other interesting methods to study the Alexandrov-Fenchel inequalities for closed hypersurfaces, see e.g.
[
9
,
10
,
34
,
35
,
44
]
. Besides, we could establish the Alexandrov-Fenchel inequalities for capillary hypersurfaces in the half-space in a forthcoming paper
[
47
]
.

For our purpose, we first generalize the Minkowski formula for hypersufaces with capillary boundary in
[
49
,
46
]
to higher order mean curvatures,

(1.11)

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}H_{k-1}\left(\langle x,e\rangle+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle\right)dA=\int_{\Sigma}H_{k}\langle X_{e},\nu\rangle dA,

where
e
is a constant unit vector field in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and
X_{e}
is defined in (
2.3
), which is a conformal Killing vector field parallel to
\mathbb{S}^{n}
.
Then we consider the flow

(1.12)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}&\partial_{t}x(\cdot,t)=f(\cdot,t)\nu(\cdot,t)+T(\cdot,t),&\hbox{ in }\mathbb{\mathbb{B}}^{n}\times[0,T),\\
&\langle\nu(\cdot,t),\overline{N}\circ x(\cdot,t)\rangle=-\cos\theta&\hbox{ on }\partial\mathbb{\mathbb{B}}^{n}\times[0,T),\end{array}\right.

where
T(\cdot,t)
is the tangential component of
\partial_{t}x
and

f=\frac{\langle x+\cos\theta\nu,e\rangle}{n\sigma_{n}/\sigma_{n-1}}-\langle X_{e},\nu\rangle.

By the first variational formula (
1.7
) and (
1.11
), it is easy to see that
under flow (
1.12
),
W_{n,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})
is preserved and
W_{k,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma}),1\leq k\leq n-1
is non-decreasing.

The remaining task is to prove the convergence of the flow to a spherical cap with capillary boundary. For this aim, we need to make a choice of
e
, which depends only on the initial hypersurface
x(\overline{\mathbb{B}}^{n},0)=\Sigma_{0}
, so that
\langle x+\cos\theta\nu,e\rangle>0
for
\Sigma_{0}
, due to the strict convexity and its
\theta-
capillary boundary of
\Sigma_{0}
. This will be done in Proposition
2.16
. Under this choice, since we have barriers for the flow hypersurfaces, given by spherical caps around
e
with
\theta
-capillary boundary, we are able to prove that under the flow,
\langle x+\cos\theta\nu,e\rangle
is always bounded below by a uniform constant. On the other hand, we can prove the quantity
\langle X_{e},\nu\rangle
is bounded below by a uniform constant, which enable us to write the flow equation to be a scalar equation of graph functions by using Möbius transformations, cf.
[
29
,
46
,
50
]
. Moreover, we prove that the flow preserves the strict convexity and we have the uniform curvature estimates, which implies the long-time existence. Finally, by using the monotonicity of
W_{k,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})
along the flow, we obtain the convergence of the flow to a spherical cap around
e
.

This article is structured as follows.
In Section
2
, we give some preliminaries for
\theta
-capillary hypersurfaces, and prove a new Minkowksi type formula (
2.7
). Besides, we introduce the definition of quermassintegral for hypersurfaces with
\theta
-capillary boundary and derive its first variational formula, i.e. Theorem
1.2
. In Section
3
, we introduce a locally constrained type curvature flow (
3.1
), derive the evolution equations for various geometric quantities, and obtain the uniform curvature estimates for convex hypersurfaces with
\theta
-capillary boundary in a ball along this flow, which follows the long-time existence and convergence of our flow (
3.1
). The last part is devoted to prove the Alexandrov-Fenchel inequalities for convex hypersurfaces with
\theta
-capillary boundary, i.e. Theorem
1.3
.

2.
Convex hypersurfaces with
\theta
-capillary boundary

2.1.
Notation and preliminaries

Let
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a smooth embedded, orientable hypersurface. Denote
D
be the Levi-Civita connection of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with respect to the Euclidean metric
\delta
, and
\nabla
be the Levi-Civita connection on
\Sigma
with respect to induced metric
g
from the embedding
x
. We denote
{\rm div},\Delta,\nabla^{2}
be the divergence, Laplacian, Hessian operator on
\Sigma
respectively.
The second fundamental form of
x
is given by the Gaussian formula

D_{X}Y=\nabla_{X}Y-h(X,Y)\nu.

The Weingarten operator is defined via

g(\mathcal{W}(X),Y)=h(X,Y),

and the Weingarten equation says that

\displaystyle\bar{\nabla}_{X}\nu=\mathcal{W}(X).

The Gauss-Codazzi equation says that

\displaystyle\Rm(X,Y,Z,W)=h(Y,Z)h(X,W)-h(Y,W)h(X,Z),

\displaystyle(\nabla_{Z}h)(X,Y)=(\nabla_{Y}h)(X,Z).

where our convention for the Riemannian curvature tensor
\Rm
is

\Rm(X,Y,Z,W)=g(\Rm(X,Y)Z,W)=g(\nabla_{X}\nabla_{Y}Z-\nabla_{Y}\nabla_{X}Z-\nabla_{[X,Y]}Z,W).

Remark 2.1
.

We will simplify the notation by using the following shortcuts occasionally:

(1)

When dealing with complicated evolution equations of tensors, we will use a local frame to express tensors with the help of their components, i.e. for a tensor field
T\in\mathcal{T}^{k,l}(\Sigma)
, the expression
T^{i_{1}\dots i_{k}}_{j_{1}\dots j_{l}}
denotes

\displaystyle T^{i_{1}\dots i_{k}}_{j_{1}\dots j_{l}}=T(e_{j_{1}},\dots,e_{j_{l}},\epsilon^{i_{1}},\dots\epsilon^{i_{k}}),

where
(e_{i})
is a local frame and
(\epsilon^{i})
its dual coframe.

(2)

The coordinate expression for the
m
-th covariant derivative of a
(k,l)
-tensor field
T
is

\displaystyle\nabla^{m}T=\left(T^{i_{1}\dots i_{k}}_{j_{1}\dots j_{l};j_{l+1}\dots j_{l+m}}\right),

where indices appearing after the semi-colon denote the covariant derivatives.

(3)

We shall use the convention of Einstein summation. For convenience the components of the Weingarten map
\mathcal{W}
are denoted by
(h^{i}_{j})=(g^{ik}h_{kj})
, and
|h|^{2}
be the norm square of the second fundamental form, that is
|h|^{2}=g^{ik}h_{kl}h_{ij}g^{jl}
, where
(g^{ij})
is the inverse of
(g_{ij})
. We use the metric tensor
(g_{ij})
and its inverse
(g^{ij})
to lower down and raise up the indices of tensor fields on
\Sigma
.

Let
\sigma_{k}(\kappa),1\leq k\leq n,
be the
k
-th elementary symmetric polynomial for
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
H_{k}(\kappa)
be its normalization
H_{k}(\kappa)=\frac{1}{\binom{n}{k}}\sigma_{k}(\kappa)
. Denote by
\sigma_{k}(\kappa|i)
the symmetric function
\sigma_{k}(\kappa)
with
\kappa_{i}=0
. We shall use the following basic properties in this paper.

Proposition 2.2
.

(1)

\sigma_{k}(\kappa)=\sigma_{k}(\kappa|i)+\kappa_{i}\sigma_{k-1}(\kappa|i),\quad\forall 1\leq i\leq n.

(2)

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}{\sigma_{k}(\kappa|i)}=(n-k)\sigma_{k}(\kappa).

(3)

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}{\kappa_{i}\sigma_{k-1}(\kappa|i)}=k\sigma_{k}(\kappa)
.

(4)

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}{\kappa_{i}^{2}\sigma_{k-1}(\kappa|i)}=\sigma_{1}(\kappa)\sigma_{k}(\kappa)-(k+1)\sigma_{k+1}(\kappa)
.

Proposition 2.3
.

For
1\leq k<l\leq n
, for
\kappa\in\Gamma_{+}:=\{\kappa\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\kappa_{i}>0,1\leq i\leq n\}
, we have

(2.1)

\displaystyle H_{k}H_{l}\geq H_{k-1}H_{l+1},

with equality holding if and only if
\kappa=c(1,\cdots,1)
for some
c>0
.

Proposition 2.4
.

For
1\leq k\leq n
,
F(\kappa)=\frac{\sigma_{k}}{\sigma_{k-1}}(\kappa)
is concave in
\Gamma_{+}
.

For a proof of Propositions
2.2
-
2.4
, one can refer to
[
32
, Chapter XV, Section 4]
and
[
40
, Lemma 2.10, Theorem 2.11]
respectively.

2.2.
Basic properties for
\theta
-capillary hypersurfaces

Let
\Sigma\subset\overline{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}
be a smooth, properly embedded, orientable hypersurfaces with
\theta
-capillary boundary, given by the embedding
x:\overline{\mathbb{B}}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}
.
Let
\mu
be the unit outward co-normal of
\partial\Sigma
in
\Sigma
and
\overline{\nu}
be the unit normal to
\partial\Sigma
in
\mathbb{S}^{n}
such that
\{\nu,\mu\}
and
\{\overline{\nu},\overline{N}\circ x\}
have the same orientation in the normal bundle of
\partial\Sigma\subset\overline{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}
. From Definition
1.1
, it follows that

(2.2)

\begin{cases}\overline{N}\circ x=\sin\theta\mu-\cos\theta\nu,\\
\overline{\nu}=\cos\theta\mu+\sin\theta\nu.\end{cases}

\partial\Sigma
can be viewed as a smooth closed hypersurface in
\mathbb{S}^{n}
, which bounds
\widehat{\partial\Sigma}
inside
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. By our convention,
\bar{\nu}
is the unit outward normal of
\partial\Sigma
in
\widehat{\partial\Sigma}\subset\mathbb{S}^{n}
. The second fundamental form of
\partial\Sigma
in
\mathbb{S}^{n}
is given by

\widehat{h}(X,Y):=-\langle\nabla^{\mathbb{S}}_{X}Y,\bar{\nu}\rangle=-\langle D_{X}Y,\bar{\nu}\rangle,\quad X,Y\in T(\partial\Sigma).

The second equality holds since
\langle\bar{\nu},\bar{N}\circ x\rangle=0
.
The second fundamental form of
\partial\Sigma
in
\Sigma
is given by

\tilde{h}(X,Y):=-\langle\nabla_{X}Y,\mu\rangle=-\langle D_{X}Y,\mu\rangle,\quad X,Y\in T(\partial\Sigma).

The second equality holds since
\langle\nu,\mu\rangle=0
.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
\Sigma\subset\overline{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}
be with
\theta
-capillary boundary. Let
\{e_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha=1}^{n-1}
be an orthonormal frame of
\partial\Sigma
. Then along
\partial\Sigma
,

(1)

\mu
is a principal direction of
\Sigma
, that is,
h(\mu,e_{\alpha})=0
.

(2)

h_{\alpha\beta}=\sin\theta\widehat{h}_{\alpha\beta}-\cos\theta\delta_{\alpha\beta}.

(3)

\tilde{h}_{\alpha\beta}=\cos\theta\widehat{h}_{\alpha\beta}+\sin\theta\delta_{\alpha\beta}=\frac{1}{\sin\theta}\delta_{\alpha\beta}+\cot\theta h_{\alpha\beta}.

(4)

h_{\alpha\beta;\mu}=\tilde{h}_{\beta\gamma}(h_{\mu\mu}\delta_{\alpha\gamma}-h_{\alpha\gamma})

Proof.

The first assertion is well-known, cf.
[
49
, Proposition 2.1]
.
For (2), we see

\displaystyle h_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=-\langle D_{e_{\alpha}}e_{\beta},\nu\rangle=\langle\widehat{h}_{\alpha\beta}\overline{\nu}+\delta_{\alpha\beta}\overline{N},\nu\rangle=\sin\theta\widehat{h}_{\alpha\beta}-\cos\theta\delta_{\alpha\beta}.

For (3), we see

\displaystyle\tilde{h}_{\alpha\beta}=-\langle D_{e_{\alpha}}e_{\beta},\mu\rangle=\langle\widehat{h}_{\alpha\beta}\overline{\nu}+\delta_{\alpha\beta}\overline{N},\mu\rangle=\cos\theta\widehat{h}_{\alpha\beta}+\sin\theta\delta_{\alpha\beta}.

For (4), by taking derivative of
h(\mu,e_{\alpha})=0
with respect to
e_{\beta}
, using the Codazzi equation and the fact (1), we get

\displaystyle 0=e_{\beta}(h(\mu,e_{\alpha}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h_{\alpha\mu;\beta}+h(\nabla_{e_{\beta}}e_{\alpha},\mu)+h(\nabla_{e_{\beta}}\mu,e_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h_{\alpha\beta;\mu}+\langle\nabla_{e_{\beta}}e_{\alpha},\mu\rangle h_{\mu\mu}+\langle\nabla_{e_{\beta}}\mu,e_{\gamma}\rangle h_{\alpha\gamma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h_{\alpha\beta;\mu}-\tilde{h}_{\beta\gamma}(h_{\mu\mu}\delta_{\alpha\gamma}-h_{\alpha\gamma}).

∎

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
\Sigma\subset\overline{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}
be with
\theta
-capillary boundary for
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
. If
\Sigma
is convex (strictly convex resp.), in the sense
h
is nonnegative definite (positive definite resp.), then
\partial\Sigma
is convex (strictly convex resp.) in both
\mathbb{S}^{n}
and
\Sigma
.

Proof.

It follows directly from Proposition
2.5
(2) and (3) that
\widehat{h}
and
\tilde{h}
are nonnegative definite (positive definite resp.) provided
h
is so.∎

2.3.
Minkowski formula

For a constant unit vector field
e\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, denote

(2.3)

\displaystyle X_{e}:=\langle x,e\rangle x-\frac{1}{2}(|x|^{2}+1)e.

It has been observed in
[
49
, Proposition 3.1]
that
X_{e}
is a conformal Killing vector field such that

(2.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{X_{e}}\delta=\langle x,e\rangle\delta,

and

\langle X_{e},\bar{N}\rangle=0\hbox{ along }\mathbb{S}^{n}.

By using such property of
X_{e}
, the following Minkowski type formula has been proved in
[
49
, Proposition 3.2]
: for a properly embedded hypersurface in
\overline{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}
with
\theta
-capillary boundary,

(2.5)

\displaystyle n\int_{\Sigma}\big(\langle x,e\rangle+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle\big)dA=\int_{\Sigma}H\langle X_{e},\nu\rangle dA.

Remark 2.7
.

From a direct calculation, we see that
C_{\theta,r}(e)
satisfies

(2.6)

\displaystyle\langle x,e\rangle+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle=\frac{1}{r}\langle X_{e},\nu\rangle.

For our purpose, we generalize (
2.5
) to higher order Minkowski type formulas.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
\Sigma\subset\overline{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}
be a smooth, properly embedded, orientable hypersurfaces with
\theta
-capillary boundary, given by the embedding
x:\overline{\mathbb{B}}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}
.
For
1\leq k\leq n
, it holds

(2.7)

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}H_{k-1}\left(\langle x,e\rangle+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle\right)dA=\int_{\Sigma}H_{k}\langle X_{e},\nu\rangle dA.

Proof.

Let
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
be an orthonormal frame on
\Sigma
.
Define

P_{e}:=\langle\nu,e\rangle x-\langle x,\nu\rangle e.

Let
X_{e}^{T}
and
P_{e}^{T}
be the tangential projection of
X_{e}
and
P_{e}
on
\Sigma
respectively.
From (
2.4
), we have

(2.8)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\nabla_{i}(X_{e}^{T})_{j}+\nabla_{j}(X_{e}^{T})_{i})=\langle x,e\rangle g_{ij}-h_{ij}\langle X_{e},\nu\rangle.

On the other hand, since
\langle P_{e},\nu\rangle=0
, by a direct computation, we know

(2.9)

\displaystyle\nabla_{i}(P_{e}^{T})_{j}=D_{i}(P_{e})_{j}=\langle\nu,e\rangle g_{ij}+h_{il}\langle e,e_{l}\rangle\langle x,e_{j}\rangle-h_{il}\langle x,e_{l}\rangle\langle e,e_{j}\rangle.

By multiplying
\sigma_{k-1}^{ij}
into above equations (
2.8
) and (
2.9
), and combining with Proposition
2.2
, it follows that

\displaystyle\sigma^{ij}_{k-1}\nabla_{i}(X_{e}^{T}+\cos\theta P_{e}^{T})_{j}=(n-k+1)\sigma_{k-1}\left(\langle x,e\rangle+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle\right)-k\sigma_{k}\langle X_{e},\nu\rangle.

We need to show

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\sigma^{ij}_{k-1}\nabla_{i}(X_{e}^{T}+\cos\theta P_{e}^{T})_{j}dA=0,

In fact, by integration by parts, and using the fact that
\nabla_{i}\sigma^{ij}_{k-1}=0
and
\mu
is a principal direction of
\Sigma
along
\partial\Sigma
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\sigma^{ij}_{k-1}\nabla_{i}(X_{e}^{T}+\cos\theta P_{e}^{T})_{j}dA=\int_{\partial\Sigma}\sigma_{k-1}^{\mu\mu}\langle X_{e}+\cos\theta P_{e},\mu\rangle ds.

From (
2.2
), on
\partial\Sigma
, we see that

\displaystyle\langle X_{e},\mu\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle X_{e},\sin\theta\overline{N}+\cos\theta\overline{\nu}\rangle=\cos\theta\langle X_{e},\overline{\nu}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cos\theta\left(\langle x,e\rangle\langle x,\overline{\nu}\rangle-\langle e,\overline{\nu}\rangle\right)=-\cos\theta\langle e,\overline{\nu}\rangle,

and

\displaystyle\langle P_{e},\mu\rangle=\langle\nu,e\rangle\langle x,\mu\rangle-\langle x,\nu\rangle\langle e,\mu\rangle=\langle\sin\theta\nu+\cos\theta\mu,e\rangle=\langle\overline{\nu},e\rangle,

which follows that

\langle X_{e}+\cos\theta P_{e},\mu\rangle=0.

We complete the proof.
∎

2.4.
Quermassintegrals and first variational formula

Recall that given a convex body
K\subset\mathbb{S}^{n}
with non-empty smooth boundary
\partial K
, its quermassintegrals
W^{\mathbb{S}^{n}}_{k}
are defined by

\displaystyle W_{0}^{\mathbb{S}^{n}}(K):=|K|,\quad\quad W_{1}^{\mathbb{S}^{n}}(K):=\frac{|\partial K|}{n},

and for
2\leq k\leq n
,

\displaystyle W_{k}^{\mathbb{S}^{n}}(K)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{n}\int_{\partial K}H_{k-1}^{\mathbb{S}^{n}}dA+\frac{k-1}{n-k+2}W^{\mathbb{S}^{n}}_{k-2}(K),

where

H_{k}^{\mathbb{S}^{n}}=\frac{1}{\binom{n-1}{k}}\sigma_{k}^{\mathbb{S}^{n}}
and
\sigma_{k}^{\mathbb{S}^{n}}
denote the
k
-th elementary symmetric polynomials, which evaluated at the
(n-1)
-principal curvatures of the hypersurface
\partial K\subset\mathbb{S}^{n}
, and we use the convention that
H_{0}^{\mathbb{S}^{n}}=1
,
H_{n}^{\mathbb{S}^{n}}=0
.
In particular,

W_{n}^{\mathbb{S}^{n}}(K)=\frac{\omega_{n-1}}{n},

due to the spherical Gauss-Bonnet-Chern’s Theorem, cf.
[
41
]
.
The following first variational formula of
W^{\mathbb{S}^{n}}_{k}
is proved by Reilly
[
36
, Section 3]
, see also Barbosa-Colares
[
4
, Section 4]
.

Proposition 2.9
.

Consider a family of convex bodies
\{K_{t}\}
whose boundary
\partial K_{t}
evolving by a normal speed function
f
, then for
0\leq k\leq n
,

(2.10)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}W_{k}^{\mathbb{S}^{n}}(K_{t})=\frac{1}{\binom{n}{k}}\int_{\partial K_{t}}\sigma_{k}^{\mathbb{S}^{n}}fdA_{t}=\frac{n-k}{n}\int_{\partial K_{t}}H_{k}^{\mathbb{S}^{n}}fdA_{t}.

Next we define the geometric quantities
{W}_{k,\theta}
for smooth, properly embedded, convex hypersurfaces in
\overline{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}
with
\theta
-capillary boundary.
Let
\Sigma\subset\overline{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}
be such a hypersurface.
Denote
\widehat{\Sigma}
be the enclosed bounded convex domain by
\Sigma
inside
\mathbb{B}^{n+1}
and
\widehat{\partial\Sigma}\subset\mathbb{S}^{n}
be the enclosed convex domain by
\partial\Sigma\subset\mathbb{S}^{n}
. We define the
W_{k,\theta}
for
\widehat{\Sigma}\subset\overline{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}
as in (
1
), (
1.5
) and (
1.6
).
In particular,

\displaystyle W_{2,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})=\frac{1}{n(n+1)}\Big\{\int_{\Sigma}HdA-\sin\theta\cos\theta|\partial\Sigma|+\big[1+(n-1)\cos^{2}\theta\big]|\widehat{\partial\Sigma}|\Big\}.

We have the following first variational formula for
W_{k,\theta}
.

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
\Sigma_{t}\subset\overline{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}
be a family of smooth, properly embedded hypersurfaces with
\theta
-capillary boundary, given by
x(\cdot,t):\overline{\mathbb{B}}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}
, such that

(2.11)

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}x)^{\perp}=f\nu,

for some normal speed function
f
. Then for
0\leq k\leq n-1
,

(2.12)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}W_{k+1,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma_{t}})=\frac{n-k}{n+1}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}H_{k+1}fdA_{t},

and

(2.13)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}W_{n+1,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma_{t}})=0.

Before proving Theorem
2.10
, we study the evolution equations for several geometric quantities under the flow

(2.14)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}x=f\nu+T,

where
T\in T\Sigma_{t}
.

Proposition 2.11
.

Along the general flow (
2.14
), it holds that

(1)

\partial_{t}g_{ij}=2fh_{ij}+\nabla_{i}T_{j}+\nabla_{j}T_{i}
.

(2)

\partial_{t}dA_{t}=\left(fH+{\rm div}(T)\right)dA_{t}.

(3)

\partial_{t}\nu=-\nabla f+h(e_{i},T)e_{i}
.

(4)

\partial_{t}h_{ij}=-\nabla^{2}_{ij}f+fh_{ik}h_{j}^{k}+\nabla_{T}h_{ij}+h_{j}^{k}\nabla_{i}T_{k}+h_{i}^{k}\nabla_{j}T_{k}.

(5)

\partial_{t}h^{i}_{j}=-\nabla^{i}\nabla_{j}f-fh_{j}^{k}h^{i}_{k}+\nabla_{T}h^{i}_{j}.

(6)

\partial_{t}H=-\Delta f-|h|^{2}f+\nabla_{T}H

(7)

\partial_{t}F=-F^{j}_{i}\nabla^{i}\nabla_{j}f-fF^{j}_{i}h_{j}^{k}h^{i}_{k}+\nabla_{T}F

F=F(h^{j}_{i})
, where
F^{i}_{j}:=\frac{\partial F}{\partial h^{j}_{i}}

(8)

Proof.

Due to the tangential part appearance in flow (
2.14

[
20

or
[
16

We choose an orthonormal frame
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
around some point

\displaystyle D_{e_{i}}x=e_{i},\quad D_{e_{i}}e_{j}=\nabla_{e_{i}}e_{j}-h_{ij}\nu,\quad D_{e_{i}}\nu=h_{ik}e_{k}.

We compute

\displaystyle\partial_{t}g_{ij}

\displaystyle 2fh_{ij}+\nabla_{i}T_{j}+\nabla_{j}T_{i}.

It follows that

Since

where in the last equality we have used

\displaystyle e_{j}(h(e_{i},T))+h(e_{k},T)\langle e_{i},\nabla_{e_{j}}e_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\nabla^{2}_{ij}f-fh_{ik}h_{kj}+\nabla_{T}h_{ij}+h_{ik}\nabla_{j}T_{k}-h_{jk}\nabla_{k}T_{i}.

The last three assertions (6)-(8) follow directly from (5), just noticing that

and the fact

which follows from Proposition
2.2
(4).

2.5.
Estimates for convex hypersurfaces with
\theta

To begin with, we state a theorem, due to Ghomi
[
21
]
, about global convexity of a compact, connected, immersed
C^{2}
-hypersurface with positive curvature.

Theorem 2.13

(Ghomi
[
21
]
, Theorem 1.2.5)
.

Let
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a compact, connected, immersed
C^{2}
-hypersurface with positive curvature. Then
\Sigma
may be extended to a
C^{2}
-ovaloid if and only if for any boundary component
\Gamma
and any
p\in\Gamma
,
\Gamma\cap T_{p}\Sigma=\{p\}
.

Recall that a closed immersed hypersurface
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is called an ovaloid if for any
p\in\Sigma
,
\Sigma
lies on one side of
T_{p}\Sigma
. The classical Hadamard theorem says that a closed embedded hypersurface with positive curvature must be an ovaloid. Ghomi’s theorem can be viewed as a generalization of Hadamard’s theorem on hypersurface with boundary.

Proposition 2.14
.

For any

(\widehat{h}_{\alpha\beta})>\cot\theta(\delta_{\alpha\beta})\geq 0.

(2.19)

\displaystyle\max_{y\in\partial\Sigma}{\rm dist}_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}(y,\partial\Sigma)<\theta.

the geodesic sphere in

of radius

\displaystyle\widehat{\partial\Sigma}\subset\widehat{S_{p,\theta}}\hbox{ with }\partial\Sigma\cap S_{p,\theta}=\{x(p)\}.

The following facts about strictly convex hypersurface with

Proposition 2.16
.

Let
\Sigma\subset\overline{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}
be a strictly convex hypersurface with
\theta
-capillary boundary for
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
, which is given by the embedding
x:\overline{\mathbb{B}}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}
. Then there exists
e_{0}\in{\rm int}(\widehat{\partial\Sigma})
and some
\delta_{0}>0
, depending on
\Sigma
, such that the following properties hold:

(1)

\langle x,e_{0}\rangle\geq\cos\theta+\delta_{0}
.

(2)

\langle\nu,e_{0}\rangle<-\cos^{2}\theta
.

(3)

\langle\mu,e_{0}\rangle>0
on
\partial{\mathbb{B}}^{n}
.

(4)

\langle x+\cos\theta\nu,e_{0}\rangle\geq\delta_{0}>0
.

Proof.

The conclusion for
\theta=\frac{\pi}{2}
has been proved by Lambert-Scheuer
[
29
, Section 4]
. Hence we prove the case for
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
.

(1)
Step 1.
Define

\phi(p):=-\left<x(p),\nu(p)\right>,\quad p\in\overline{\mathbb{B}}^{n}.

Note that
\phi|_{\partial\mathbb{B}^{n}}\equiv\cos\theta>0
. On the other hand, since
\mu
is a principal direction and
\Sigma
is strictly convex,

D_{\mu}\phi=-h_{\mu\mu}\langle x,\mu\rangle=-h_{\mu\mu}\sin\theta<0,\quad\hbox{ along }\partial\mathbb{B}^{n},

which implies that
\phi
cannot attain its maximum on
\partial\mathbb{B}^{n}
. Thus,
\phi
attains its maximum at some interior point, say
p_{0}\in\mathbb{B}^{n}
. Therefore

\phi(p_{0})>\phi\big|_{\partial\mathbb{B}^{n}}=\cos\theta,

and at
p_{0}
, we have

0=D_{e_{i}}\phi(p_{0})=-h_{ij}(p_{0})\left<x(p_{0}),e_{j}(p_{0})\right>,

for any
e_{i}\in T_{p_{0}}\Sigma
.
Since
(h_{ij}(p_{0}))
is positive definite, we get
\left<x(p_{0}),e_{j}(p_{0})\right>=0
for any
e_{j}\in T_{p_{0}}\Sigma
. This implies
x(p_{0})\parallel\nu(p_{0})
. Since
\phi(p_{0})>\cos\theta>0,
for
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
, we obtain

-\left<x(p_{0}),\nu(p_{0})\right>=|x(p_{0})|>\cos\theta.

From Proposition
2.15
, we see

\left<x(p)-x(p_{0}),\nu(p_{0})\right>\leq 0,\hbox{ for }p\in\overline{\mathbb{B}}^{n}.

Define
e_{0}:=-\nu(p_{0})
, it follows

\left<x(p),e_{0}\right>=\left<x(p),-\nu(p_{0})\right>\geq-\left<x(p_{0}),\nu(p_{0})\right>>\cos\theta,

for all
p\in\overline{\mathbb{B}}^{n}
. See Figure 2.

Figure 2

Next, we claim that
e_{0}
must be contained in
{\rm int}(\widehat{\partial\Sigma})
. In fact, if not, there exists some
\tilde{p}\in\partial\mathbb{B}^{n}
such that

and
{\rm dist}_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}(e_{0},x(\tilde{p}))={\rm dist}_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}(e_{0},\partial\Sigma)\geq 0
. Using Proposition
2.15
, we see that
\Sigma
lies on one side of
T_{\tilde{p}}\Sigma
, say
T_{\tilde{p}}\Sigma^{+}
. By the fact
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
, we see that
T_{\tilde{p}}\Sigma^{+}
does not contain the line segment
l
through
e_{0}
and the origin. However,

goes through
x(p_{0})\in\Sigma
since
e_{0}=-\nu(p_{0})=\frac{x(p_{0})}{|x(p_{0})|}
. This leads to a contradiction and we get the claim.

Step 2.
Denote the tangent hyperplane
T_{p_{0}}\Sigma:=\mathcal{H}
, where
p_{0}
is determined in Step 1. It is known that
\mathcal{H}
intersects
\mathbb{S}^{n}
at the angle
\theta_{1}<\theta
, where
\theta_{1}
is given by

\cos\theta_{1}=|x(p_{0})|>\cos\theta.

Now we parallel transport
\mathcal{H}
downwards along the direction
-e_{0}
until it achieves a position
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
which satisfies that
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
intersects with
\mathbb{S}^{n}
at the angle
\theta
, see Figure 3.

Figure 3

We construct the foliation
C_{\theta,s}(e_{0}),s\geq 0
so that
C_{\theta,\infty}(e_{0}):=\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
. Recall
C_{\theta,s}(e_{0}),s>0
is the spherical cap of radius
s
around
e_{0}
with
\theta
-capillary boundary, see (
1.2
).

Since
C_{\theta,0}(e_{0})=\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
does not touch
\Sigma
and lies below
\Sigma
, there exists some
s_{0}>0
, depending on
\theta_{1}
, such that
C_{\theta,s_{0}}(e_{0})
touches
\Sigma
at the first time. Hence

\left<x,e_{0}\right>\geq\min_{x\in C_{\theta,0}(e_{0})}\left<x,e_{0}\right>:=\cos\theta+\delta_{0},

for some
\delta_{0}>0
. From above, it is clear that
\delta_{0}
depends on
s_{0}
and in turn on
\Sigma
.

Hence we complete the proof of statement (
1
).

(2) From (1), we know that

\Sigma\subset H_{e_{0}}:=\{\langle x,e_{0}\rangle>0\}.

Since
e_{0}\in{\rm int}(\widehat{\partial\Sigma})
, we see that

\widehat{\partial\Sigma}\subset H_{e_{0}}.

On the other hand, we see from Corollary
2.6
that
\partial\Sigma
is a strictly convex hypersurface in
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. It follows from
[

,

]
that, there exist two disjoint open connected components
A
and

\mathbb{S}^{n}\setminus\bar{\nu}(\partial\mathbb{B}^{n})=A\cup B,

which
\bar{\nu}(\partial\mathbb{B}^{n})
bounds.
Since
\widehat{\partial\Sigma}\subset H_{e_{0}}
, we have

\partial A\subset H_{-e_{0}},

which implies

\left<\bar{\nu}(p),e_{0}\right>\leq 0,\quad p\in\partial\mathbb{B}^{n}.

Using (
2.2

\left<\nu(p)+\cos\theta x(p),e_{0}\right>\leq 0,\quad p\in\partial\mathbb{B}^{n}.

Combining with
\left<x(p),e_{0}\right>>\cos\theta
from (

(2.21)

\displaystyle\left<\nu(p),e_{0}\right>\leq-\cos\theta\left<x(p),e_{0}\right><-\cos^{2}\theta,\quad p\in\partial\mathbb{B}^{n}.

On the other hand, assume

\mathbb{S}^{n}\setminus\nu(\partial\mathbb{B}^{n})=\tilde{A}\cup\tilde{B},

is the component which contains

. However,

\left<\nu(p),e_{0}\right><-\cos^{2}\theta,\quad p\in\overline{\mathbb{B}}^{n}.

(3) Using equation (

) and the statement (

\displaystyle\langle\mu(p),e_{0}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sin\theta}\langle x(p)+\cos\theta\nu(p),e_{0}\rangle>\frac{1}{\sin\theta}\left(\cos\theta+\cos\theta\langle\nu(p),e_{0}\rangle\right)>0,

for all

.

(4) This follows directly from (1).

Proposition 2.17
.

Let
\Sigma\subset\overline{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}
be a strictly convex hypersurface with
\theta
-capillary boundary for
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
and
e_{0}
is given in Proposition
2.16
. Then there exists
\epsilon>0
depending on
\Sigma
, such that

\displaystyle\langle X_{e_{0}},\nu\rangle\geq\epsilon\cos^{2}\theta.

Remark 2.18
.

In the case
\theta=\frac{\pi}{2}
, Lambert-Scheuer
[
29
]
showed that for strictly convex hypersurface with free boundary and
e_{0}\in{\rm int}(\widehat{\partial\Sigma})
such that
\widehat{\partial\Sigma}\subset H_{e_{0}}
,
there exists some constant
\epsilon>0
depending on
\Sigma
such that
\langle X_{e_{0}},\nu\rangle\geq\epsilon
. Our estimate in Proposition
2.17
is weaker in the case
\theta=\frac{\pi}{2}
. Nevertheless, it is enough for use in the case
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
.

Proof.

Since
\Sigma
is strictly convex and
e_{0}\in\widehat{\partial\Sigma}
, then

(2.22)

\displaystyle\langle x-e_{0},\nu\rangle\geq 0.

Since
\Sigma
is compact and
e_{0}\not\in\Sigma
, there exists
\epsilon>0
such that
\frac{1}{2}|x-e_{0}|^{2}\geq\epsilon
on
\Sigma
.
Recall that
X_{e_{0}}=\langle x,e_{0}\rangle x-\frac{|x|^{2}+1}{2}e_{0}
. Thus

\displaystyle\langle X_{e_{0}},\nu\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle x,e_{0}\rangle\langle x,\nu\rangle-\frac{|x|^{2}+1}{2}\langle\nu,e_{0}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle x,e_{0}\rangle\langle x-e_{0},\nu\rangle-\frac{|x-e_{0}|^{2}}{2}\langle\nu,e_{0}\rangle\geq\epsilon\cos^{2}\theta.

where the last inequality follows from (
2.22
) and Proposition
2.16
(2).
∎

3.
Locally constrained curvature flow

In this section, we study the flow. Let
\Sigma_{0}:=\Sigma\subset\overline{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}
be a smooth, properly embedded, strictly convex hypersurface with
\theta
-capillary boundary, given by the embedding
x_{0}:\overline{\mathbb{B}}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}
. Choose
e_{0}
as in Proposition
2.16
, which depends only on
\Sigma_{0}
. For simplicity of notation, we omit the subscription and write
e=e_{0}
.
Let
\Sigma_{t}\subset\overline{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}
be a family of smooth, properly embedded, strictly convex hypersurfaces with
\theta
-capillary boundary, given by
x(\cdot,t):\overline{\mathbb{B}}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}

(3.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}&\partial_{t}x(\cdot,t)=f(\cdot,t)\nu(\cdot,t)+T(\cdot,t),&\hbox{ in }\mathbb{\mathbb{B}}^{n}\times[0,T),\\
&\langle\nu(\cdot,t),\overline{N}\circ x(\cdot,t)\rangle=-\cos\theta&\hbox{ on }\partial\mathbb{\mathbb{B}}^{n}\times[0,T),\\
&x(\cdot,0)=x_{0}(\cdot)&\text{ in }\overline{\mathbb{B}}^{n}.\end{array}\right.

where

(3.2)

\displaystyle f=\frac{\langle x+\cos\theta\nu,e\rangle}{F}-\langle X_{e},\nu\rangle,

with

The following property of the flow (

Proposition 3.1
.

As long as
\Sigma_{t}
is strictly convex and the flow (
3.1
) exists,
W_{n,\theta}
is preserved and
W_{k,\theta}
is non-decreasing for
1\leq k\leq n-1
.

Proof.

From Proposition
2.8
and Theorem
2.10
, we know that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}W_{n,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma_{t}})=\frac{1}{n+1}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\Big[H_{n-1}\left(\langle x,e\rangle+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle\right)-H_{n}\langle X_{e},\nu\rangle\Big]=0,

and for
1\leq k\leq n-1

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}W_{k,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma_{t}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{n+1-k}{n+1}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}H_{k}\Big[\frac{H_{n-1}}{H_{n}}\left(\langle x,e\rangle+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle\right)-\langle X_{e},\nu\rangle\Big]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{n+1-k}{n+1}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\Big[H_{k-1}\left(\langle x,e\rangle+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle\right)-H_{k}\langle X_{e},\nu\rangle\Big]=0,

where we have also used the Newton-MacLaurin inequality (
2.1
) and Proposition Proposition
2.16
(4).
∎

The aim of this section is the following existence and convergence result for the flow (
3.1
).

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\Sigma_{0}\subset\overline{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}
be a smooth, properly embedded, strictly convex hypersurface with
\theta
-capillary boundary for some
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
, given by the embedding
x_{0}:\overline{\mathbb{B}}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}
. Choose
e_{0}
as in Proposition
2.16
. Then
x(\cdot,t)
to the flow (
3.1
) exists for

. Moreover,
x(\cdot,t)
converges smoothly to the spherical cap around
a
with
\theta
-capillary boundary, which has the same

as
\Sigma_{0}
.

3.1.
Barriers

First of all, we have the short time existence for the flow (
3.1
). This is because the strict convexity and the
\theta
-capillary boundary of
\Sigma_{0}
imply that
\Sigma_{0}
satisfies

[

]
or
[
42

there exists some

depending on

such that

Thus the scalar equation is a strictly parabolic equation. Then the short time existence follows from the standard parabolic theory.

Let
T^{*}
be the maximal time of smooth existence of a solution to (
3.1
). The positivity of
F
implies that
\Sigma_{t}
is strictly convex up to

for flow (

From Proposition
2.16
(1),
It follows that

\Sigma_{0}\subset\widehat{C_{\theta,\infty}(e)}=\{\langle x,e\rangle>\cos\theta\}.

The family of

forms natural barriers of (

\Sigma_{t},t\in[0,T^{*})

Recall that

satisfies (

3.2.
Evolution equations

In order to avoid confusion with tensor indices, we abbreviate

For

(3.5)

Proposition 3.5
.

Along the flow (

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\langle x,e\rangle)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\langle x,e\rangle)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{t}x,e\rangle-\frac{\langle x+\cos\theta\nu,e\rangle}{F^{2}}F^{ij}\nabla^{2}_{ij}\langle x,e\rangle-\left<X-\frac{\cos\theta}{F}e+T,\nabla\langle x,e\rangle\right>

\displaystyle 2F^{-1}\langle x+\cos\theta\nu,e\rangle\langle\nu,e\rangle+\cos\theta F^{-1}|e^{T}|^{2}-\langle X,e\rangle.

Proposition 3.6
.

Along the flow (

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}F

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2F^{ij}}{F^{2}}\langle x+\cos\theta\nu,e\rangle_{;i}F_{;j}

\displaystyle+\frac{\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle}{F^{2}}F^{ij}F_{;ij}+\cos\theta\frac{2F^{ij}\langle\nu,e\rangle_{;i}F_{;j}}{F^{2}}

\displaystyle+F^{ij}\langle X,\nu\rangle_{;ij}.

Similarly, by conducting a direct computation, (one can also refer to

\displaystyle\langle X,\nu\rangle_{;ij}

\displaystyle-fF^{ij}h^{k}_{i}h_{kj}=-\left(\frac{\langle x+\cos\theta\nu,e\rangle}{F}-\langle X,\nu\rangle\right)F^{ij}h^{k}_{i}h_{kj}.

\displaystyle\partial_{t}F

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big[\frac{\langle x+\cos\theta\nu,e\rangle}{F^{2}}F^{ij}F_{;ij}+F_{;k}\langle X,e_{k}\rangle\Big]+\frac{2F^{ij}F_{;j}}{F^{2}}\left(\langle x,e\rangle_{;i}+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle_{;i}\right)

\displaystyle\frac{2F^{ij}}{F^{2}}\langle x+\cos\theta\nu,e\rangle_{;i}F_{;j}-\frac{2\langle x+\cos\theta\nu,e\rangle}{F^{3}}F^{ij}F_{;i}F_{;j}

From the definition (

Proposition 3.7
.

Along the flow (
3.1

(3.10)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}H

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H\langle x,e\rangle+\left(HF^{-1}-n+\cos\theta|h|^{2}F^{-1}\right)\langle\nu,e\rangle

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}H\leq 0\hbox{ along }\partial\Sigma_{t}.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.11

(3.12)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}H

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\Delta f-|h|^{2}f+\nabla_{T}H=-\Delta\Big[\frac{\langle x+\cos\theta\nu,e\rangle}{F}-\langle X_{e},\nu\rangle\Big]

\displaystyle-|h|^{2}\left(\frac{\langle x+\cos\theta\nu,e\rangle}{F}-\langle X_{e},\nu\rangle\right)+\nabla_{T}H.

Proposition 3.12

satisfies

(3.18)

\displaystyle 2\langle x,e\rangle\sqrt{r^{2}+2r\cos\theta+1}=|x|^{2}+2r\cos\theta+1.

By taking the time derivative for (
3.18
), we get

\displaystyle\left(\frac{(r+\cos\theta)\langle x,e\rangle}{\sqrt{r^{2}+2r\cos\theta+1}}-\cos\theta\right)\partial_{t}r

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{t}x,x-\sqrt{r^{2}+2r\cos\theta+1}e\rangle=\langle f\nu+T,x-\sqrt{r^{2}+2r\cos\theta+1}e\rangle.

We evaluate at
(\xi_{t},t)
. Since
\Sigma_{t}
is tangential to
C_{\theta,r}(e)
at
x(\xi_{t},t)
,
we have

\nu_{\Sigma_{t}}(\xi_{t},t)=\nu_{\partial C_{\theta,r}(e)}(\xi_{t},t)=\frac{1}{r}\left(x-\sqrt{r^{2}+2r\cos\theta+1}e\right).

Thus we deduce

\displaystyle\left(\frac{(r_{\max}+\cos\theta)\langle x,e\rangle}{\sqrt{r_{\max}^{2}+2r_{\max}\cos\theta+1}}-\cos\theta\right)\frac{d}{dt}r_{\max}=r\left(\frac{\langle x+\cos\theta\nu,e\rangle}{F}-\langle X_{e},\nu\rangle\right).

(3.19)

We first claim that there exists
\delta_{1}>0
such that

(3.20)

\displaystyle\frac{(r_{\max}+\cos\theta)\langle x,e\rangle}{\sqrt{r_{\max}^{2}+2r_{\max}\cos\theta+1}}-\cos\theta\geq\delta_{1}.

This follows directly from (
3.18
). In fact, from (
3.18
), we see

(3.21)

\displaystyle\langle x,e\rangle^{2}(r^{2}+2r\cos\theta+1)=\frac{(|x|^{2}+1)^{2}}{4}+r\cos\theta(|x|^{2}+1)+r^{2}\cos^{2}\theta.

By using (
3.18
) again and (
3.21
), we have

\displaystyle(r+\cos\theta)\langle x,e\rangle-\cos\theta\sqrt{r^{2}+2r\cos\theta+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(r+\cos\theta)\langle x,e\rangle-\cos\theta\frac{|x|^{2}+2r\cos\theta+1}{2\langle x,e\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r\langle x,e\rangle}\left[r(r+\cos\theta)\langle x,e\rangle^{2}-r^{2}\cos^{2}\theta-r\cos\theta\frac{|x|^{2}+1}{2}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r\langle x,e\rangle}\left[\frac{(|x|^{2}+1)^{2}}{4}+r\cos\theta(|x|^{2}+1)-r\cos\theta\langle x,e\rangle^{2}-\langle x,e\rangle^{2}-r\cos\theta\frac{|x|^{2}+1}{2}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r\langle x,e\rangle}\left[\frac{(|x-e||x+e|)^{2}}{4}+r\cos\theta\frac{|x-e|^{2}}{2}\right].

This yields (
3.20
).

Since the spherical caps
C_{\theta,r_{\max}}(e)
are the static solution to (
3.1
) and
x(\cdot,t)
is tangential to
C_{\theta,r_{\max}}(e)
at
x(\xi_{t},t)
, we see from (
2.6
)

(3.22)

\displaystyle\frac{\langle x+\cos\theta\nu,e\rangle}{\langle X_{e},\nu\rangle}\Big|_{x(\xi_{t},t)}=\frac{\langle x+\cos\theta\nu,e\rangle}{\langle X_{e},\nu\rangle}\Big|_{C_{\theta,r_{\max}}(e)}=\frac{1}{r_{\max}(t)}.

Since
x(\cdot,t)
converges to
C_{\theta,r_{\infty}}(e_{\infty})
and
r_{\infty}
is uniquely determined, we have

\displaystyle F=\frac{n\sigma_{n}}{\sigma_{n-1}}\to\frac{1}{r_{\infty}}\quad\text{uniformly},

as
t\to+\infty
.
Thus there exists
T_{0}>0
such that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{F}-{r_{\infty}}<\frac{\epsilon}{2},

and hence

\displaystyle\frac{1}{F}-{r_{\max}(t)}<-\frac{\epsilon}{2},

for all
t>T_{0}
.
Taking into account of (
3.22
), we see

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{F}-\frac{\langle x+\cos\theta\nu,e\rangle}{\langle X_{e},\nu\rangle}\right)\Big|_{x(t,\xi_{t})}<-\frac{\epsilon}{2},

for all
t>T_{0}
.
Finally, we conclude from (
3.19
) that there exists some
C>0
such that

\frac{d}{dt}r_{\max}\leq-C\epsilon.

This is a contradiction to the fact that
\lim\limits_{t\to+\infty}\frac{d}{dt}r_{\max}=0
, hence the claim (
3.16
) is true. Similarly, we can obtain that

(3.23)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to+\infty}r_{\min}(t)=r_{\infty}.

Combining this with claim (
3.16
), we know that
\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}r(t,\cdot)=r_{\infty}
, which is a constant. This implies any limit of a convergent subsequence is the spherical cap arounnd
e
, the claimed uniqueness. We complete the proof of Proposition
3.15
.
∎

Combining Propositions
3.14
and
3.15
, we get the assertion in Theorem
3.2
.

3.5.
Proof of Theorem
1.3

With the preparations above, using the same approach as in
[
42
, Section 4]
, we can prove the main result, i.e. Theorem
1.3
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Firstly, for given
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
, we claim that

\displaystyle{f_{k}}(r):=W_{k}(\widehat{C_{\theta,r}(e)}),

is strictly increasing with respect to
r>0
.
To prove this claim, given
r_{0}>0
, consider the hypersurface flow
y:\overline{\mathbb{B}}^{n}\times[0,+\infty)\to\overline{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}
given by

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}y=X_{e},\quad\text{ on }\overline{\mathbb{B}}^{n}\times[0,+\infty)\\
y(\cdot,0)=C_{\theta,r_{0}}(e)\quad\text{ on }\overline{\mathbb{B}}^{n}.\end{cases}

The flow hypersurfaces of this flow are
C_{\theta,r(t)}(e)
, where the radius
r(t)
is some strictly increasing function. In fact,
r(t)
satisfies

\displaystyle r^{\prime}(t)=r\sqrt{r^{2}+2r\cos\theta+1},

with
r(0)=r_{0}
.
Hence from Theorem
2.10
, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}W_{k,\theta}(\widehat{{C}_{\theta,r(t)}(e)})=\frac{k}{n+1}\binom{n}{k-1}^{-1}\int_{C_{\theta,r(t)}(e)}\sigma_{k}\langle X_{e},\nu\rangle dA_{t}>0,

where the last inequality follows from the strictly convex of the spherical caps, hence it yields that
f_{k}(r)
are strictly increasing functions, which follows the claim.

Assume that
\Sigma
is strictly convex. By evolving along the flow (
3.1
) with initial strictly hypersurface
\Sigma
, we denote its convergence limit be the spherical cap given by
C_{\theta,r_{\infty}}(e)
, then we have

\displaystyle W_{n,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle W_{n,\theta}(\widehat{C_{\theta,r_{\infty}}(e)})=f_{n}(r_{\infty})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{n}\circ f^{-1}_{k}\circ f_{k}(r_{\infty})\geq f_{n}\circ f^{-1}_{k}(W_{k,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})),

moreover, equality holds iff
\Sigma
is a spherical cap. Also,

\displaystyle W_{n+1,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle W_{n+1,\theta}(\widehat{C_{\theta,r_{\infty}}(e)}).

Note that when
r\to 0
,
C_{\theta,r}(e)
is more and more close to the cap
C^{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}_{\theta,r}
of radius
r
in the half space
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
with
\theta
-capillary boundary.
Then

(n+1)\lim_{r\to 0}W_{n+1,\theta}(\widehat{C_{\theta,r}(e)})=\lim_{r\to 0}\int_{C^{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}_{\theta,r}}H_{n}=\frac{\omega_{n}}{2}I_{\sin^{2}\theta}(\frac{n}{2},\frac{1}{2}).

The second equality follows from the simple fact that

\left|C^{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}_{\theta,r}\right|=\left|C^{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}_{\theta,1}\right|r^{n}=\frac{\omega_{n}}{2}I_{\sin^{2}\theta}(\frac{n}{2},\frac{1}{2})r^{n}.

When
\Sigma
is convex but not strictly convex, the inequality (
1.9
) and the equality (
1.8
) follows by approximation. The equality characterization in (
1.9
) can be proved similar to
[
42
]
Section 4, by using an argument of
[
23
]
. We omit the details here.
∎

In particular, we have following simplified expansion formulas for a convex hypersurface
\Sigma
with
\theta
-capillary boundary in
\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}
.

(1)

For
n=2
,

(3.24)

\displaystyle 3W_{3,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})=\int_{\Sigma}H_{2}dA-\cos\theta|\widehat{\partial\Sigma}|+\sin\theta|\partial\Sigma|={2\pi}(1-\cos\theta),

due to
I_{\sin^{2}\theta}(1,\frac{1}{2})=1-\cos\theta.

(2)

For
n=3
,

\displaystyle 4W_{4,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma}H_{3}dA+\sin^{2}\theta\int_{\partial\Sigma}H_{1}^{\mathbb{S}^{3}}+|\widehat{\partial\Sigma}|-\sin\theta\cos\theta|\partial\Sigma|

\displaystyle={2\pi}(\theta-\sin\theta\cos\theta),

due to
I_{\sin^{2}\theta}(\frac{3}{2},\frac{1}{2})=\frac{2\theta-\sin(2\theta)}{\pi}.

(3)

For
n=4
,

\displaystyle 5W_{5,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}H_{4}dA+\sin^{3}\theta\int_{\partial\Sigma}H_{2}^{\mathbb{S}^{4}}-\frac{3}{2}\cos\theta\sin^{2}\theta\int_{\partial\Sigma}H_{1}^{\mathbb{S}^{4}}

\displaystyle-\cos\theta(1+\frac{1}{2}\sin^{2}\theta)|\widehat{\partial\Sigma}|+\sin\theta(1-\frac{\sin^{2}\theta}{3})|\partial\Sigma|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi^{2}}{3}(2-2\cos\theta-\cos\theta\sin^{2}\theta).

Remark 3.16
.

For
n=2
, we can use another viewpoint to show (
3.24
) directly. From Gauss-Bonnet formula, we see

\displaystyle 2\pi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}KdA+\int_{\partial\Sigma}\kappa_{g}ds=\int_{\Sigma}KdA+\int_{\partial\Sigma}(\cos\theta\widehat{\kappa}+\sin\theta)ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}KdA+\sin\theta|\partial\Sigma|+\cos\theta\big(2\pi-|\widehat{\partial\Sigma}|\big),

where
K=H_{2}
is the Gauss curvature,
\kappa_{g}
and
\widehat{\kappa}
denote the geodesic curvature of
\partial\Sigma
in
\Sigma
and
\widehat{\partial\Sigma}
respectively. From
\mu=\cos\theta\overline{\nu}+\sin\theta\overline{N}
implies
\kappa_{g}=\cos\theta\widehat{\kappa}+\sin\theta
.
Hence it follows

\displaystyle 3W_{3,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})=\int_{\Sigma}KdA-\cos\theta|\widehat{\partial\Sigma}|+\sin\theta|\partial\Sigma|=2\pi(1-\cos\theta).

Yet another proof of the strong law of large numbers
Nicolas Curien

Abstract
We give a short proof of the strong law of large numbers based on duality for random walk.



Let
X_{1},X_{2},\dots
be i.i.d. random variables with finite expectation and let
S_{n}=X_{1}+\dots+X_{n}
for
n\geqslant 0
be the corresponding random walk. Kolmogorov’s strong law of large numbers says that
n^{-1}S_{n}\to\mathbb{E}[X]
almost surely as
n\to\infty
. Clearly, it is a consequence of the lemma:

Lemma 1
.

Let
X_{1},X_{2},\dots
be i.i.d. r.v. with
\mathbb{E}[X]>0
. Then
\inf_{n\geqslant 0}(X_{1}+\cdots+X_{n})
is finite a.s.

Proof.

Step 1. Bounding the increments from above.
Choose
C>0
large enough so that by dominated convergence
\mathbb{E}[X\mathbf{1}_{X<C}]>0
. We will show that the random walk
\tilde{S}_{n}=X_{1}\mathbf{1}_{X_{1}<C}+\dots+X_{n}\mathbf{1}_{X_{n}<C}
is a.s. bounded from below which is sufficient to prove the lemma.

Step 2. Duality.
For every
n\geqslant 0
we have the equality in law
(0=\tilde{S}_{0},\tilde{S}_{1},\dots,\tilde{S}_{n}){=}(\tilde{S}_{n}-\tilde{S}_{n},\tilde{S}_{n}-\tilde{S}_{n-1},\dots,\tilde{S}_{n}-\tilde{S}_{1},\tilde{S}_{n}-\tilde{S}_{0}).

Let
T=\inf\{i\geqslant 0:\tilde{S}_{i}>0\}
be the first hitting time of the positive axis by the walk and recall that a time
n\geqslant 0
is a weak descending record time if and only if
\tilde{S}_{n}=\min_{0\leqslant k\leqslant n}\tilde{S}_{k}.
By applying the above equality in law we deduce (see Figure
1
) that

\mbox{ for all }n\geqslant 0,\quad\mathbb{P}(T>n)=\mathbb{P}(n\mbox{ is a weak descending record time}).

Figure 1:
Time and space reversal shows that
\mathbb{P}(T>n)=\mathbb{P}(n\mbox{ is a descending record time}).

Summing over
n\geqslant 0
, we get that
\mathbb{E}[T]=\mathbb{E}[\#\mbox{ weak descending record times}]
and the proof is complete if we prove that
\mathbb{E}[T]<\infty
since this implies that almost surely there is a finite number of weak descending records for
\tilde{S}
, hence the walk is bounded from below a.s.

Step 3. Optional sampling theorem.
To prove
\mathbb{E}[T]<\infty
, consider the standard martingale

M_{n}\penalty\ =\penalty\ \tilde{S}_{n}-\mathbb{E}[X\mathbf{1}_{X<C}]n,\quad\mbox{ for }n\geqslant 0

(for the filtration generated by the
X_{i}
’s) and apply the optional sampling theorem to the stopping time
n\wedge T
to deduce that

0=\mathbb{E}[M_{n\wedge T}]\quad\mbox{ or in other words }\quad\mathbb{E}[X\mathbf{1}_{X<C}]\mathbb{E}[n\wedge T]=\mathbb{E}[\tilde{S}_{n\wedge T}].

Since the increments of
\tilde{S}
are bounded above by
C
, the right-hand side of the last display is bounded by
C
as well. Letting
n\to\infty
, by monotone convergence we deduce that the expectation of
T
is finite. Et voilà. ∎

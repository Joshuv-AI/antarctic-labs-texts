More infinite classes of APN-like Power Functions
Longjiang Qu, Kangquan Li

Abstract
In the literature, there are many APN-like functions that generalize the APN properties or are similar to APN functions, e.g. locally-APN functions, 0-APN functions or those with boomerang uniformity 2. In this paper, we study the problem of constructing infinite classes of APN-like but not APN power functions. For one thing, we find two infinite classes of locally-APN but not APN power functions over 𝔽 2 2 ​ m {\mathbb{F}}_{2^{2m}} with m m even, i.e., ℱ 1 ​ ( x ) = x j ⁡ ( 2 m − 1 ) \mathcal{F}_{1}(x)=x^{j(2^{m}-1)} with gcd ⁡ ( j , 2 m + 1 ) = 1 \gcd(j,2^{m}+1)=1 and ℱ 2 ​ ( x ) = x j ⁡ ( 2 m − 1 ) + 1 \mathcal{F}_{2}(x)=x^{j(2^{m}-1)+1} with j = 2 m + 2 3 j=\frac{2^{m}+2}{3} . As far as the authors know, our infinite classes of locally-APN but not APN functions are the only two discovered in the last eleven years. Moreover, we also prove that this infinite class ℱ 1 \mathcal{F}_{1} is not only with the optimal boomerang uniformity 2 2 , but also has an interesting property that its differential uniformity is strictly greater than its boomerang uniformity. For another thing, using the multivariate method, including the above infinite class ℱ 1 \mathcal{F}_{1} , we construct seven new infinite classes of 0-APN but not APN power functions.

In addition to APN functions, there are many concepts that generalize the APN properties or are similar to APN functions. In 2011, while working on the differential properties of the functions
x\mapsto x^{2^{t}-1}
, Blondeau et al.
[

For a power function
F

F(x+1)+F(x)=b

{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}

. Obviously, if
F
is an APN power function,

is locally-APN. In
[
2
]
, Blondeau et al. obtained an infinite class of locally-APN but not APN functions, i.e.,
\mathcal{B}(x)=x^{2^{m}-1}

x^{2^{n-1}-1}
with
n
even and
x^{2^{m+1}-1}
with
n=2m
and
m
even, from
x^{3}
and
\mathcal{B}(x)
.

Obviously, if

-APN for some

-APN for some

A concept closely related to differential uniformity is the so-called boomerang uniformity, which was proposed by Cid et al.
[
8
]
, Boura and Canteaut
[
3
]
. The definition of boomerang uniformity in
[
8
]
and
[
3
]
is only suitable for permutations over
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
. In 2019, Li et al.
[
17
]
provided an equivalent formula as follows to compute the boomerang uniformity. In particular, it removes the permutation condition. For a function
F
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
, its
boomerang uniformity

[
17
]
, denoted by
\Delta_{F}
, equals the maximal number of solutions in
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}\times{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
of the equation system

\begin{cases}F(x+a)+F(y+a)=b\\
F(x)+F(y)=b,\end{cases}

for any
a,b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}^{*}
. Similarly, when
F
is power, it suffices to consider the above equation system with
a=1
. It was shown that the boomerang uniformity of any permutation over
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
is greater than
or equal to its differential uniformity, and that the lowest possible boomerang uniformity
2

is achieved by APN functions. However, recently, Hasan et al.
[
14
]
considered the boomerang uniformity of the unique locally-APN power function up to now, i.e.,
\mathcal{B}(x)=x^{2^{m}-1}
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
with
n=2m
and found that this class of power functions is with boomerang uniformity
2
when
m
is even although its differential uniformity equals
2^{m}-2
. This is also the only known infinite class of functions whose differential uniformity is strictly greater than its boomerang uniformity.

In this paper, we refer to these functions which are locally-APN, 0-APN or with boomerang uniformity 2 collectively as
APN-like
functions. Moreover, we study the problem of constructing more APN-like power functions. Specifically, on one hand, we find two infinite classes of locally-APN but not APN power functions over
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{2m}}
with
m
even, i.e.,
\mathcal{F}_{1}(x)=x^{j(2^{m}-1)}
with
\gcd(j,2^{m}+1)=1
, which generalizes the first class (
j=1
) proposed by Blondeau et al.
[
2
]
in 2011, and
\mathcal{F}_{2}(x)=x^{j(2^{m}-1)+1}
with
j=\frac{2^{m}+2}{3}
, see Theorems
2.4
and
2.6
, respectively. It is interesting that the proof of Theorem
2.4
depends on some basic properties of the Dickson polynomial of the first kind. As far as the authors know, our infinite classes of locally-APN but not APN functions are the only two discovered in the last eleven years. The experimental results show that
\mathcal{F}_{1}
and
\mathcal{F}_{2}
can explain most of locally-APN but not APN power instances over
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
for
n\leq 12
. The only example that cannot be explained is
x^{219}
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{10}}
.
Moreover, we also prove that this infinite class
\mathcal{F}_{1}
is another one with the optimal boomerang uniformity
2
, which also generalizes the result of Hasan et al.
[
14
]
. Particularly, the differential uniformity of
\mathcal{F}_{1}
is strictly greater than its boomerang uniformity, see Proposition
2.5
. Furthermore, the experimental results show that
\mathcal{F}_{1}
explains all (not APN) power instances with boomerang uniformity 2 over
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
for
n\leq 12
.
On the other hand, we try to construct more infinite classes of 0-APN but not APN power functions since there are many such power instances over
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
for
n\leq 10
. Using the multivariate method
introduced by Dobbertin
[
12
]
and the resultant of polynomials, we construct seven new infinite classes of 0-APN but not APN power functions, see Theorems
3.1
and
3.2
.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. Section
2
provides two new infinite classes of locally-APN but not APN power functions, one of which is also with boomerang uniformity
2
. Next, some infinite classes of 0-APN but not APN functions are obtained in Section
3
. Finally, Section
4
concludes the work of this paper.

2
Two infinite classes of locally-APN but not APN power functions

In this section, we first give an infinite class of locally-APN but not APN power functions, where some properties of the Dickson polynomials of the first kind will be used. Thus we now recall these important results.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
18
]

Let
\mathbb{R}
be a commutative ring with identity. The Dickson polynomial
D_{k}(x,a)
of the first kind of degree
k
in the indeterminate
x
and with parameter
a\in\mathbb{R}
is given as

D_{k}(x,a)=\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor\frac{k}{2}\rfloor}\frac{k}{k-j}\begin{pmatrix}k-j\\
j\end{pmatrix}(-a)^{j}x^{k-2j}.

Then the following statements hold.

1.

D_{k}(x_{1}+x_{2},x_{1}x_{2})=x_{1}^{k}+x_{2}^{k}
, where
x_{1},x_{2}
are two indeterminates;

2.

b^{k}D_{k}(x,a)=D_{k}\left(bx,b^{2}a\right)
;

3.

if
\mathbb{R}={\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
, then for any
a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}^{*}
,
D_{k}(x,a)
permutes
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
if and only if
\gcd(k,2^{2n}-1)=1
;

4.

if
\mathbb{R}={\mathbb{F}}_{2}
, then the map
x\mapsto D_{k}(x,1)
induces a permutation of
T_{1}=\{x:~x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{m}},~{\rm Tr}_{m}(1/x)=1\}
if and only if
\gcd(k,2^{m}+1)=1
, where
{\rm Tr}_{m}
is the absolutely trace function on
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{m}}
.

In addition, the characterization of the number of solutions of quadratic equations is also useful.

Lemma 2.2
.

[
19
]

Let
\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{m}}
,
\alpha\neq 0
. Then the quadratic equation
x^{2}+\alpha x+\beta=0
has solutions in
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{m}}
if and only if
{\rm Tr}_{m}\left(\frac{\beta}{\alpha^{2}}\right)=0
.

Remark 2.3
.

Since for any
\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{m}}
with
\alpha\neq 0
,
{\rm Tr}_{2m}\left(\frac{\beta}{\alpha^{2}}\right)=0
must hold, by Lemma
2.2
, the quadratic equation
x^{2}+\alpha x+\beta=0
has solutions
x_{0},x_{1}
in
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{2m}}
. Moreover, it is trivial that
x_{0}+x_{1}=\alpha
and
x_{0}x_{1}=\beta
. If the solutions
x_{0},x_{1}
belong to
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{2m}}\backslash{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{m}}
, then
x_{1}=x_{0}^{2^{m}}
and
{\rm Tr}_{m}\left(\frac{\beta}{\alpha^{2}}\right)=1
. Conversely, for any element
x_{0}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{2m}}\backslash{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{m}}
, let
x_{0}+x_{0}^{2^{m}}=\alpha
and
x_{0}^{2^{m}+1}=\beta
. Then
x_{0},x_{0}^{2^{m}}
are solutions of the equation
x^{2}+\alpha x+\beta=0
in
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{2m}}
and
{\rm Tr}_{m}\left(\frac{\beta}{\alpha^{2}}\right)=1
.

The first class of locally-APN power functions in this section is as follows.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
n=2m
with
m
even and
j
be a positive integer satisfying
\gcd(j,2^{m}+1)=1
. Then
\mathcal{F}_{1}(x)=x^{j\cdot(2^{m}-1)}
is locally-APN over
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
.

Proof.

According to definition, it suffices to show that the equation

(x+1)^{j\cdot(2^{m}-1)}+x^{j\cdot(2^{m}-1)}=b

(1)

has zero or two solutions in
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
for any
b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}\backslash\{0,1\}
. Actually, we can prove the statement for
b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}^{*}
.

First of all, it is trivial that
x\in\{0,1\}
are solutions of Eq. (
1
) only for
b=1
. Next, when
x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}\backslash\{0,1\}
, Eq. (
1
) is equivalent to

\frac{\left(x^{2^{m}+1}+x\right)^{j}+\left(x^{2^{m}+1}+x^{2^{m}}\right)^{j}}{\left(x^{2}+x\right)^{j}}=b.

(2)

In addition, if
x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{m}}\backslash\{0,1\}
, then
(x+1)^{j\cdot(2^{m}-1)}+x^{j\cdot(2^{m}-1)}=0
. Thus any element
x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{m}}\backslash\{0,1\}
is not a solution of Eq. (
1
) for any
b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}^{*}
. In the following, we consider the solutions
x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}\backslash{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{m}}
and the proof is divided into two parts: (i)
b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{m}}^{*}
; (ii)
b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}\backslash{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{m}}
.

(i) When
b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{m}}^{*}
, by Eq. (
2
), we know that

(x^{2}+x)^{j}=\frac{\left(x^{2^{m}+1}+x\right)^{j}+\left(x^{2^{m}+1}+x^{2^{m}}\right)^{j}}{b}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{m}}^{*}.

Since
\gcd(j,2^{m}+1)=1
, we have
x^{2}+x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{m}}^{*}
, denoted by
\beta
. Then by Remark
2.3
, we have
{\rm Tr}_{m}(\beta)=1
,
x+x^{2^{m}}=1
and
x^{2^{m}+1}=\beta
. Moreover, the left part of Eq. (
2

\frac{\left(x^{2^{m}+1}+x\right)^{j}+\left(x^{2^{m}+1}+x^{2^{m}}\right)^{j}}{\left(x^{2}+x\right)^{j}}=\frac{(\beta+x)^{j}+(\beta+x+1)^{j}}{\beta^{j}}=\frac{D_{j}(1,\beta^{2})}{\beta^{j}}=D_{j}\left(\frac{1}{\beta},1\right),

(3)

where the second and last equalities hold due to Lemma
2.1
1) and 2), respectively.

Let
T_{1}=\{x:~x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{m}},~{\rm Tr}_{m}(1/x)=1\}
. Then
1\not\in T_{1}
since
m
is even. By Lemma
2.1
4), when
\gcd(j,2^{m}+1)=1
, the map
u\mapsto D_{j}(u,1)
induces a permutation of

and thus

. Hence Eq. (

{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}\backslash{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{m}}
for

. Namely, when

, Eq. (
1
) has only two solutions
x\in\{0,1\}
in
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
.

We now assume that for a given
b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{m}}\backslash\{0,1\}
, there exist two different elements
x_{1},x_{2}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}\backslash{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{m}}
such that
x_{1},x_{2}
are both solutions of Eq. (
1
). Let
x_{1}^{2}+x_{1}=\beta_{1}
and
x_{2}^{2}+x_{2}=\beta_{2}
. Then
\beta_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{m}}^{*}
,
{\rm Tr}_{m}(\beta_{i})=1
for

and by Eq. (
3
), we have

D_{j}\left(u_{1},1\right)=D_{j}\left(u_{2},1\right),

(4)

where
u_{i}=\frac{1}{\beta_{i}}
for

.
Since
{\rm Tr}_{m}\left(\frac{1}{u_{i}}\right)={\rm Tr}_{m}(\beta_{i})=1
,
u_{i}\in T_{1}
for

. Together with

. Thus by Eq. (

. Hence

and then

or

.

Therefore, Eq. (

) has zero or two solutions in
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
for any

.

(ii) Now we consider the case
b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}\backslash{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{m}}
.
Raising Eq. (
2
) into its
2^{m}
-th power, we get

\frac{\left(x^{2^{m}+1}+x\right)^{j}+\left(x^{2^{m}+1}+x^{2^{m}}\right)^{j}}{\left(x^{2^{m+1}}+x^{2^{m}}\right)^{j}}=b^{2^{m}}.

Together with Eqs. (

\displaystyle b+b^{2^{m}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\left(x^{2^{m}+1}+x\right)^{j}+\left(x^{2^{m}+1}+x^{2^{m}}\right)^{j}\right]\left[\frac{1}{\left(x^{2}+x\right)^{j}}+\frac{1}{\left(x^{2^{m+1}}+x^{2^{m}}\right)^{j}}\right]

\displaystyle\left[\left(x^{2^{m}+1}+x\right)^{j}+\left(x^{2^{m}+1}+x^{2^{m}}\right)^{j}\right]\frac{\left(x^{2}+x\right)^{j}+\left(x^{2^{m+1}}+x^{2^{m}}\right)^{j}}{\left(x^{2}+x\right)^{j}\left(x^{2^{m+1}}+x^{2^{m}}\right)^{j}}

b+b^{2^{m}}=D_{j}(\alpha,\beta^{2}+\beta+\alpha\beta)\cdot\frac{D_{j}(\alpha^{2}+\alpha,\beta^{2}+\beta+\alpha\beta)}{(\beta^{2}+\beta+\alpha\beta)^{j}}

(6)

(7)

Since

Thanks to Lemma

In the following, we show that

For one thing, since

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}_{m}\left(\frac{\sqrt{\beta^{2}+\beta+\alpha\beta}}{\alpha^{2}+\alpha}\right)

3

In this section, we construct six explicit infinite classes of 0-APN power functions. Before that,
since the resultant of polynomials will be used in our proof, we now recall some basic facts about it. Given two polynomials

\begin{pmatrix}a_{m}&a_{m-1}&\cdots&a_{0}&0&&\cdots&0\\

\vdots&&\ddots&&&&&\vdots\\
0&\cdots&0&a_{m}&a_{m-1}&&\cdots&a_{0}\\
b_{n}&b_{n-1}&\cdots&&b_{0}&0&\cdots&0\\
0&b_{n}&b_{n-1}&\cdots&&b_{0}&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&&\ddots&&&&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&\cdots&0&b_{n}&b_{n-1}&&\cdots&b_{0}\\
\end{pmatrix}.

For a field
K
and two polynomials
F(x,y),G(x,y)\in K[x,y]

to denote the resultant of
F
and

with respect to

and

belongs to the ideal generated by
F
and
G
. It is known that
F(x,y)=0
and
G(x,y)=0
has a common solution
(x,y)
if and only if

x
is a solution of
\mathrm{Res}_{y}(F,G)(x)=0
(see
[
19
, p. 36]
).

By the proof of Theorem
2.4
, we can see that the power function

in Theorem
2.4
is also 0-APN. In addition to this, we construct six infinite classes of 0-APN but not APN power functions over

d=2^{2m-1}-2^{m}-1
, let
d^{{}^{\prime}}=2d=2^{2m}-2^{m+1}-2
. Then Eq. (
27
) is equivalent to

(x+1)^{d^{{}^{\prime}}}+x^{d^{{}^{\prime}}}+1=0,

i.e.,

(x+1)^{-2^{m+1}-1}+x^{-2^{m+1}-1}+1=0.

(28)

Since we only consider the solutions to Eq. (
28
) in
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}\backslash\{0,1\}
, Eq. (
28
) can be written as

\frac{1}{(x+1)^{2^{m+1}+1}}+\frac{1}{x^{2^{m+1}+1}}+1=0,

namely,

x^{2^{m+2}+2}+x^{2^{m+2}+1}+x^{2^{m+1}+2}+x^{2^{m+1}+1}+x^{2^{m+1}}+x+1=0.

(29)

Let
y=x^{2^{m}}
. Then
y^{2^{m}}=x^{2^{2m}}=x
and Eq. (
29
) becomes

x^{2}y^{4}+xy^{4}+x^{2}y^{2}+xy^{2}+y^{2}+x+1=0.

(30)

Raising Eq. (
30
) into its
2^{m}
-th power, we get

y^{2}x^{4}+yx^{4}+y^{2}x^{2}+yx^{2}+x^{2}+y+1=0.

(31)

Let polynomials
F_{1}(x,y),F_{2}(x,y)\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}[x,y]
be defined by

F_{1}(x,y)=x^{2}y^{4}+xy^{4}+x^{2}y^{2}+xy^{2}+y^{2}+x+1

and

F_{2}(x,y)=y^{2}x^{4}+yx^{4}+y^{2}x^{2}+yx^{2}+x^{2}+y+1,

respectively. With the help of MAGMA, we obtain the resultant of
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
with respect to
y
as follows

\mathrm{Res}_{y}(F_{1},F_{2})(x)=x^{2}(x+1)^{2}(x^{2}+x+1)^{3}(x^{3}+x+1)(x^{3}+x^{2}+1).

(32)

Together with Eqs. (
30
), (
31
) and (
32
), we have

x^{2}(x+1)^{2}(x^{2}+x+1)^{3}(x^{3}+x+1)(x^{3}+x^{2}+1)=0.

Then
x^{2}+x+1=0
or
x^{3}+x+1=0
or
x^{3}+x^{2}+1=0
. If
x^{2}+x+1=0
, then
x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{2}}\backslash\{0,1\}
and
(x+1)^{d^{{}^{\prime}}}+x^{d^{{}^{\prime}}}=0
since
3\mid(2^{m+1}+1)
due to
m
even. Thus any element
x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{2}}\backslash\{0,1\}
is not the solution to Eq. (
28
). If
x^{3}+x+1=0
or
x^{3}+x^{2}+1=0
, then
3\mid 2m
, which contradicts the condition
3\nmid m
.

Therefore, Eq. (
28
) exactly has no solution in
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}\backslash\{0,1\}
.

2) When
d=2^{2m-1}-2^{m-1}-1
, let
d^{{}^{\prime}}=2d=2^{2m}-2^{m}-2
. Then Eq. (
27
) is equivalent to

(x+1)^{d^{{}^{\prime}}}+x^{d^{{}^{\prime}}}+1=0,

i.e.,

(x+1)^{-2^{m}-1}+x^{-2^{m}-1}+1=0.

(33)

Since we only consider the solutions to Eq. (
33
) in
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}\backslash\{0,1\}
, Eq. (
33
) can be written as

\frac{1}{(x+1)^{2^{m}+1}}+\frac{1}{x^{2^{m}+1}}+1=0,

namely,

x^{2^{m+1}+2}+x^{2^{m+1}+1}+x^{2^{m}+2}+x^{2^{m}+1}+x^{2^{m}}+x+1=0.

(34)

If
x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{m}}\backslash\{0,1\},
then Eq. (
34
) becomes
x^{4}+x^{2}+1=0
, i.e.,
x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{2}}\backslash\{0,1\}
. However, in this case,
(x+1)^{d^{{}^{\prime}}}+x^{d^{{}^{\prime}}}=0
since
3\mid(2^{m}+1)
due to
m
odd. Thus any element
x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{2}}\backslash\{0,1\}
is not the solution to Eq. (
33
).

If
x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}\backslash{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{m}}
, let
x+x^{2^{m}}=\alpha
and
x^{2^{m}+1}=\beta
. Then by Remark
2.3
,
\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{m}}^{*}
and
{\rm Tr}_{m}\left(\frac{\beta}{\alpha^{2}}\right)=1
. Moreover, Eq. (
34
) can be written as

\beta^{2}+\alpha\beta+\beta+\alpha+1=0,

i.e.,

\frac{\beta^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}+\frac{\beta}{\alpha}+\frac{\beta}{\alpha^{2}}+\frac{1}{\alpha}+\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}}=0.

Therefore, we have

0={\rm Tr}_{m}\left(\frac{\beta^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}+\frac{\beta}{\alpha}+\frac{\beta}{\alpha^{2}}+\frac{1}{\alpha}+\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}}\right)={\rm Tr}_{m}\left(\frac{\beta}{\alpha^{2}}\right)=1,

which is wrong. Thus Eq. (
33
) has no solution in
x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}\backslash{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{m}}
.

All in all, Eq. (
33
) has no solution in
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}\backslash\{0,1\}
.

3) When
d=2^{3k}-2^{2k}+2^{k}-1
, since we only consider the solutions in
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}\backslash\{0,1\}
, Eq. (
27
) is equivalent to

\frac{(x+1)^{2^{3k}+2^{k}}}{(x+1)^{2^{2k}+1}}+\frac{x^{2^{3k}+2^{k}}}{x^{2^{2k}+1}}+1=0,

i.e.,

(x+1)^{2^{3k}+2^{k}}x^{2^{2k}+1}+(x+1)^{2^{2k}+1}x^{2^{3k}+2^{k}}+(x+1)^{2^{2k}+1}x^{2^{2k}+1}=0.

(35)

If
x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{m}}\backslash\{0,1\}
, then Eq. (
35
) becomes

(x+1)^{2^{k+1}}x^{2}+(x+1)^{2}x^{2^{k+1}}+(x+1)^{2}x^{2}=0,

namely,

x^{2^{k+1}}+x^{4}=0.

Thus
x^{2^{k-1}}=x
. Since
\gcd(k-1,2k)=1
due to
k
even, Eq. (
35
) has no solution in
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{m}}\backslash\{0,1\}.

If
x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}\backslash{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{m}}
, let
x+x^{2^{m}}=\alpha
and
x^{2^{m}+1}=\beta
. Then
\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{m}}^{*}
and
{\rm Tr}_{m}\left(\frac{\beta}{\alpha^{2}}\right)=1
. Moreover,

(x+1)^{2^{m}+1}=x^{2^{m}+1}+x^{2^{m}}+x+1=\beta+\alpha+1

and then Eq. (
35
) becomes

\left(\beta^{2^{k}}+\alpha^{2^{k}}+1\right)\beta+(\beta+\alpha+1)\beta^{2^{k}}+(\beta+\alpha+1)\beta=0,

i.e.,

\alpha^{2^{k}}\beta+\alpha\beta^{2^{k}}+\beta^{2^{k}}+\beta^{2}+\alpha\beta=0.

(36)

If
\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{k}}^{*}
, then
x^{2^{m}+1}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{k}}^{*}
. That is to say
(x^{2^{2k}+1})^{2^{k}}=x^{2^{2k}+1}
and then
x^{2^{3k}-2^{2k}+2^{k}-1}=x^{d}=1
. Thus
(x+1)^{d}=0
, which is impossible. Thus
\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{m}}\backslash{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{k}}
.

Now raising Eq. (
36
) into its
2^{k}
-th power, we get

\alpha\beta^{2^{k}}+\alpha^{2^{k}}\beta+\beta+\beta^{2^{k+1}}+\alpha^{2^{k}}\beta^{2^{k}}=0.

(37)

To eliminate
\alpha^{2^{k}}
, we compute the summation of the left part of Eq. (
36
) multiplied by
(\beta+\beta^{2^{k}})
and the multiplication of the left part of Eq. (
37
) and
\beta
, getting

\alpha\left(\beta^{2^{k+1}}+\beta^{2}+\beta^{2^{k}+1}\right)+\left(\beta^{2^{k}}+\beta^{2}\right)\left(\beta^{2^{k}}+\beta\right)+\beta^{2^{k+1}+1}+\beta^{2}=0.

After further simplifying, we have

(\alpha+\beta+1)\left(\beta^{2^{k+1}}+\beta^{2}+\beta^{2^{k}+1}\right)=0.

Then
\beta=\alpha+1
or
\beta^{2^{k+1}}+\beta^{2}+\beta^{2^{k}+1}=0
. If
\beta=\alpha+1
, then

{\rm Tr}_{m}\left(\frac{\beta}{\alpha^{2}}\right)={\rm Tr}_{m}\left(\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha^{2}}\right)={\rm Tr}_{m}\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}+\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}}\right)=0,

which contradicts
{\rm Tr}_{m}\left(\frac{\beta}{\alpha^{2}}\right)=1
. If
\beta^{2^{k+1}}+\beta^{2}+\beta^{2^{k}+1}=0
, then
\beta^{2^{k+1}-2}+\beta^{2^{k}-1}+1=0
. Thus
\beta^{3(2^{k}-1)}=1
. Since
\gcd(3(2^{k}-1),2^{2k}-1)=(2^{k}-1)\gcd(3,2^{k}+1)=2^{k}-1
due to
k
even, we have
\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{k}}
, which is also impossible.

Therefore, Eq. (
35
) has no solution in
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}\backslash\{0,1\}
.
∎

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
n=2m+1
. Then
F(x)=x^{d}
is 0-APN over
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
when one of the following statements holds:

1.

m\not\equiv 1\pmod{3}
and
d=2^{2m}-2^{m}-1
;

2.

d=2^{2m-1}-2^{m-1}-1
;

3.

d=2^{2m-1}-2^{m}-1
;

Proof.

It suffices to show that the equation

(x+1)^{d}+x^{d}+1=0

(38)

has no solution in
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}\backslash\{0,1\}
.

1) When
d=2^{2m}-2^{m}-1
, let
d^{{}^{\prime}}=2d=2^{2m+1}-2^{m+1}-2
. Then Eq. (
38
) is equivalent to

(x+1)^{d^{{}^{\prime}}}+x^{d^{{}^{\prime}}}+1=0,

i.e.,

(x+1)^{-2^{m+1}-1}+x^{-2^{m+1}-1}+1=0.

(39)

Since we only consider the solutions to Eq. (
39
) in
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}\backslash\{0,1\}
, Eq. (
39
) can be written as

\frac{1}{(x+1)^{2^{m+1}+1}}+\frac{1}{x^{2^{m+1}+1}}+1=0,

namely,

x^{2^{m+2}+2}+x^{2^{m+2}+1}+x^{2^{m+1}+2}+x^{2^{m+1}+1}+x^{2^{m+1}}+x+1=0.

(40)

Let
y=x^{2^{m+1}}
. Then
y^{2^{m+1}}=x^{2}
and Eq. (
40
) becomes

x^{2}y^{2}+xy^{2}+x^{2}y+xy+y+x+1=0.

(41)

Raising Eq. (
41
) into its
2^{m+1}
-th power, we get

y^{2}x^{4}+yx^{4}+y^{2}x^{2}+yx^{2}+x^{2}+y+1=0.

(42)

Let
F_{1}(x,y),F_{2}(x,y)\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}[x,y]
be polynomials defined by

F_{1}(x,y)=x^{2}y^{2}+xy^{2}+x^{2}y+xy+y+x+1

and

F_{2}(x,y)=y^{2}x^{4}+yx^{4}+y^{2}x^{2}+yx^{2}+x^{2}+y+1,

respectively. With the help of MAGMA, we obtain the resultant of
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
with respect to
y
as follows

\mathrm{Res}_{y}(F_{1},F_{2})(x)=x^{2}(x+1)^{2}(x^{3}+x+1)(x^{3}+x^{2}+1).

(43)

Together with Eqs. (
41
), (
42
) and (
43
), we have

x^{2}(x+1)^{2}(x^{3}+x+1)(x^{3}+x^{2}+1)=0,

which does not hold for any
x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}\backslash\{0,1\}
since
3\nmid n
due to
m\not\equiv 1\pmod{3}
.

Therefore, Eq. (
38
) has no solution in
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}\backslash\{0,1\}
.

2) When
d=2^{2m-1}-2^{m-1}-1
, let
d^{{}^{\prime}}=4d=2^{2m+1}-2^{m+1}-4
. Then Eq. (
38
) is equivalent to

(x+1)^{-2^{m+1}-3}+x^{-2^{m+1}-3}+1=0,

i.e.,

\frac{1}{(x+1)^{2^{m+1}+3}}+\frac{1}{x^{2^{m+1}+3}}+1=0

(44)

since we only consider the solutions in
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}\backslash\{0,1\}
. After further simplifying, Eq. (
44
) is actually

(x+1)^{2^{m+1}+3}+x^{2^{m+1}+3}+(x^{2}+x)^{2^{m+1}+3}=0.

Let
y=x^{2^{m+1}}
. Then
y^{2^{m+1}}=x^{2}
and the above equation becomes

x^{3}y+(y+1)(x+1)^{3}+(y^{2}+y)(x^{2}+x)^{3}=0.

(45)

Raising Eq. (
45
) into its
2^{m+1}
-th power, we get

y^{3}x^{2}+(x^{2}+1)(y+1)^{3}+(x^{4}+x^{2})(y^{2}+y)^{3}=0.

(46)

Let
F_{1}(x,y),F_{2}(x,y)\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}[x,y]
be polynomials defined by

F_{1}(x,y)=x^{3}y+(y+1)(x+1)^{3}+(y^{2}+y)(x^{2}+x)^{3}

and

F_{2}(x,y)=y^{3}x^{2}+(x^{2}+1)(y+1)^{3}+(x^{4}+x^{2})(y^{2}+y)^{3},

F_{1}
and
F_{2}

as follows

\mathrm{Res}_{y}(F_{1},F_{2})(x)=x^{4}(x+1)^{4}(x^{2}+x+1)^{4}(x^{4}+x+1)^{2}(x^{4}+x^{3}+1)^{2}(x^{4}+x^{3}+x^{2}+x+1)^{2}.

(47)

Together with Eqs. (
45

46
) and (
47
), we have

x^{4}(x+1)^{4}(x^{2}+x+1)^{4}(x^{4}+x+1)^{2}(x^{4}+x^{3}+1)^{2}(x^{4}+x^{3}+x^{2}+x+1)^{2}=0.

Since
n
is odd, for any
x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
, we have

x^{2}+x+1\neq 0,x^{4}+x+1\neq 0,x^{4}+x^{3}+1\neq 0,x^{4}+x^{3}+x^{2}+x+1\neq 0.

Therefore, Eq. (
44
) has no solution in
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}\backslash\{0,1\}
and then
F
is 0-APN over
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
.

4
Conclusion

In this paper, using some basic properties of the Dickson polynomial of the first kind and the multivariate method, we obtained two infinite classes of locally-APN but not APN power functions over
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{2m}}
with
m
even, i.e.,
\mathcal{F}_{1}(x)=x^{j(2^{m}-1)}
with
\gcd(j,2^{m}+1)=1
and
\mathcal{F}_{2}(x)=x^{j(2^{m}-1)+1}
with
j=\frac{2^{m}+2}{3}
, and several infinite classes of 0-APN but not APN power functions. Particularly, the family
\mathcal{F}_{1}
is also with the optimal boomerang uniformity
2
and has an interesting property that its differential uniformity is strictly greater than its boomerang uniformity.

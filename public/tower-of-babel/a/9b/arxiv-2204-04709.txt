Recurrence relations of coefficients involving hypergeometric function with an application
Zhen-Hang Yang

Abstract
. For a , b , p ∈ ℝ a,b,p\in\mathbb{R} , − c ∉ ℕ ∪ { 0 } -c\notin\mathbb{N\cup}\left\{0\right\} and θ ∈ [ − 1 , 1 ] \theta\in\left[-1,1\right] , let U θ ​ ( x ) = ( 1 − θ ​ x ) p ​ F ​ ( a , b , c , x ) = ∑ n = 0 ∞ u n ​ ( θ ) ​ x n . U_{\theta}\left(x\right)=\left(1-\theta x\right)^{p}F\left(a,b;c;x\right)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}u_{n}\left(\theta\right)x^{n}. In this paper, we prove that the coefficients u n ​ ( θ ) u_{n}\left(\theta\right) for n ≥ 0 n\geq 0 satisfies a 3-order recurrence relation. In particular, u n ​ ( 1 ) u_{n}\left(1\right) satisfies a 2-order recurrence relation. These offer a new way to study for hypergeometric function. As an example, we present the necessary and sufficient conditions such that a hypergeometric mean value is Schur m-power convex or concave on ℝ + 2 \mathbb{R}_{+}^{2} .

1.
Introduction

For real numbers
a,b
, and
c
with
c\neq 0,-1,-2,...
, the Gaussian
hypergeometric function is defined by

F\left(a,b;c;x\right)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left(a\right)_{n}\left(b\right)_{n}}{\left(c\right)_{n}}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}

for
x\in\left(-1,1\right)
, where
\left(a\right)_{n}
denotes
Pochhammer symbol defined by

\left(a\right)_{n}=a\left(a+1\right)\cdot\cdot\cdot\left(a+n-1\right)=\frac{\Gamma\left(n+a\right)}{\Gamma\left(a\right)},

for
n=1,2,...
, and
(a)_{0}=1
for
a\neq 0
, here
\Gamma(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{x-1}e^{-t}dt

(x>0)
is the gamma function.

The hypergeometric function
F(a;b;c;x)
has the simple differentiation
formulas

(1.1)

F^{\prime}\left(a,b;c;x\right)=\frac{ab}{c}F\left(a+1,b+1;c+1;x\right).

The behavior of the hypergeometric function near
x=1
in the three cases
a+b<c
,
a+b=c
, and
a+b>c
,
a,b,c>0
, is given by

(1.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lc}\vskip 12.0pt plus 4.0pt minus 4.0ptF\left(a,b;c;1\right)=\dfrac{\Gamma\left(c\right)\Gamma\left(c-a-b\right)}{\Gamma\left(c-a\right)\Gamma\left(c-b\right)}&\text{if }c>a+b\text{,}\\
\vskip 12.0pt plus 4.0pt minus 4.0ptF\left(a,b;c;x\right)=\dfrac{R\left(a,b\right)-\ln\left(1-x\right)}{B\left(a,b\right)}+O\left(\left(1-x\right)\ln\left(1-x\right)\right)&\text{if }c=a+b\text{,}\\
F\left(a,b;c;x\right)=(1-x)^{c-a-b}F(c-a,c-b;c;x)&\text{if }c<a+b\text{,}\end{array}\right.

where

B\left(z,w\right)=\frac{\Gamma\left(z\right)\Gamma\left(w\right)}{\Gamma\left(z+w\right)}\text{, \ }\mathop{\mathrm{R}e}\left(z\right)>0\text{, }\mathop{\mathrm{R}e}\left(w\right)>0

is the classical beta function,

(1.3)

R\equiv R\left(a,b\right)=-2\gamma-\psi\left(a\right)-\psi\left(b\right)\text{,}

here
\psi\left(z\right)=\Gamma^{\prime}\left(z\right)/\Gamma\left(z\right)
,
\mathop{\mathrm{R}e}\left(z\right)>0
is the psi function and
\gamma

is the Euler-Mascheroni constant. It should be noted that
F\left(a,b;a+b;x\right)
is called zero-balanced function.

In 2018, Yang
[
1
, Lemma 2]
established two recurrence
relations of coefficients of
\left(r^{\prime}\right)^{p}K\left(r\right)
and
\left(r^{\prime}\right)^{p}E\left(r\right)
, where and in what
follows,
r^{\prime}=\sqrt{1-r^{2}}
.

Proposition Y1
.
We have

(1.4)

\left(r^{\prime}\right)^{p}K\left(r\right)=\frac{\pi}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}r^{2n},

where
a_{0}=1
,
a_{1}=1/4-p/2
and for
n\geq 2
,

(1.5)

a_{n}=\frac{8n^{2}-4\left(p+3\right)n+\left(2p+5\right)}{4n^{2}}a_{n-1}-\frac{\left(p-2n+3\right)^{2}}{4n^{2}}a_{n-2};

(1.6)

\left(r^{\prime}\right)^{p}E\left(r\right)=\frac{\pi}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}r^{2n},

where
b_{0}=1,b_{1}=-p/2-1/4
and for
n\geq 1

(1.7)

b_{n+1}=\frac{1}{4}\frac{8n^{2}-4pn-2p-1}{\left(n+1\right)^{2}}b_{n}-\frac{1}{4}\frac{\left(2n-p-1\right)\left(2n-p-3\right)}{\left(n+1\right)^{2}}b_{n-1}.

In 2021, Yand and Tian
[
2
, Proposition 3]
presented
another recurrence relation of those coefficients of
\left(1-x\right)^{-q}F\left(-1/2,-1/2;2;x\right)
.

Proposition Y2
.
Let
q\in\mathbb{R}
.
We
have

(1.8)

\left(1-x\right)^{-q}F\left(-\frac{1}{2},-\frac{1}{2};2;x\right)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}u_{n}x^{n}

with
u_{0}=1
,
u_{1}=q-1/8
and for
n\geq 1
,

(1.9)

u_{n+1}=\frac{2n^{2}+\left(2q+1\right)n+2q-1/4}{\left(n+1\right)\left(n+2\right)}u_{n}-\frac{\left(n+q-1/2\right)\left(n+q-3/2\right)}{\left(n+1\right)\left(n+2\right)}u_{n-1}.

Inspired the above results, the aim of this paper is to establish more
general recurrence relation of coefficients
u_{n}
given by

U_{\theta}\left(x\right)=\left(1-\theta x\right)^{p}F\left(a,b;c;x\right)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}u_{n}\left(\theta\right)x^{n}.

As consequences, we also present recurrence relations of coefficients
u_{n}\left(\theta\right)
for
\theta=-1,1
and
v_{n}
given by

V\left(x\right)=\ln\left(1-x\right)\times F\left(a,b;c;x\right)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}v_{n}x^{n}.

As an application, we obtain necessary and sufficient conditions for which a
hypergeometric mean is Schur m-power convex or concave on
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
.

2.
Main results

We start with the useful lemma.

Lemma 1
.

Let

\begin{array}[]{ccccc}F\equiv F\left(a,b;c,z\right),&&F_{a-}\equiv F\left(a-1,b;c,z\right),&&F_{a+}\equiv F\left(a+1,b;c,z\right)\end{array}.

Then

(2.1)

\displaystyle\frac{dF}{dz}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left(c-a\right)F_{a-}+\left(a-c+bz\right)F}{z\left(1-z\right)},

(2.2)

\displaystyle\frac{dF_{a-}}{dz}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{a-1}{z}\left(F-F_{a-}\right)

Proof.

It was shown in
[
3
, p. 51–52, (7) and (18)]
that

(2.3)

\displaystyle z\frac{dF}{dz}+aF

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle aF_{a+},

(2.4)

\displaystyle z\frac{dF_{a-}}{dz}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(a-1\right)F-\left(a-1\right)F_{a-}.

These in combination with Gauss’ contiguous function relation

\left(c-a\right)F_{a-}+\left(2a-c-az+bz\right)F+a\left(z-1\right)F_{a+}=0

see
[
4
, p. 558, (15.2.10)]
give the desired
formulas.

We now are in position to state and prove the general recurrence relation.

Theorem 1
.

Let
a,b,p\in\mathbb{R}
,
-c\notin\mathbb{N\cup}\left\{0\right\}
and
\theta\in\left[-1,1\right]
. Then we have

(2.5)

U_{\theta}\left(x\right)=\left(1-\theta x\right)^{p}F\left(a,b;c;x\right)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}u_{n}x^{n},

with
u_{0}=1
,
u_{1}=ab/c-p\theta
,

(2.6)

u_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\theta^{2}p\left(p-1\right)-\theta p\frac{ab}{c}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{ab\left(b+1\right)\left(a+1\right)}{c\left(c+1\right)},

and for
n\geq 2
,

(2.7)

u_{n+1}=\frac{\xi_{n,p,\theta}\left(a,b,c\right)}{\left(n+1\right)\left(n+c\right)}u_{n}-\theta\dfrac{\eta_{n,p,\theta}\left(a,b,c\right)}{\left(n+1\right)\left(n+c\right)}u_{n-1}+\theta^{2}\frac{\lambda_{n,p}\left(a,b\right)}{\left(n+1\right)\left(n+c\right)}u_{n-2},

where

\displaystyle\xi_{n,p,\theta}\left(a,b,c\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(n+a\right)\left(n+b\right)+\theta\left[2n^{2}-2n\left(p-c+1\right)-cp\right],

\displaystyle\eta_{n,p,\theta}\left(a,b,c\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2n^{2}+2\left(a+b-p-2\right)n-\left(a+b-1\right)p+2\left(a-1\right)\left(b-1\right)

\displaystyle+\theta\left(n-p-1\right)\left(n-p+c-2\right),

\displaystyle\lambda_{n,p}\left(a,b\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(n+a-p-2\right)\left(n+b-p-2\right).

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Let

(2.8)

Differentiating for
U_{\theta}
yields

-p\theta\left(1-\theta x\right)^{p-1}F+\left(1-\theta x\right)^{p}F^{\prime}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}nu_{n}x^{n-1}.

Substituting (

then multiplying by
x\left(1-x\right)\left(1-\theta x\right)

(2.9)

\begin{array}[]{c}-p\theta x\left(1-x\right)U_{\theta}\left(x\right)+\left(c-a\right)\left(1-\theta x\right)U_{\theta}^{\ast}\left(x\right)+\left(a-c+bx\right)\left(1-\theta x\right)U_{\theta}\left(x\right)\\
=\left(1-x\right)\left(1-\theta x\right)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}nu_{n}x^{n}.\end{array}

Expanding in power series yields

\displaystyle-p\theta\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}u_{n-1}x^{n}-\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}u_{n-2}x^{n}\right)+\left(c-a\right)\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}u_{n}^{\ast}x^{n}-\theta\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}u_{n-1}^{\ast}x^{n}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}nu_{n}x^{n}-\left(\theta+1\right)\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\left(n-1\right)u_{n-1}x^{n}+\theta\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\left(n-2\right)u_{n-2}x^{n},

which can be arranged as

\displaystyle\left(c-a\right)\left(u_{0}^{\ast}-u_{0}\right)+\left[-p\theta u_{0}+\left(c-a\right)\left(u_{1}^{\ast}-\theta u_{0}^{\ast}\right)+\left(a-c\right)u_{1}+\left(b-\theta\left(a-c\right)\right)u_{0}\right]x

\displaystyle+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\left[\left(c-a\right)\left(u_{n}^{\ast}-\theta u_{n-1}^{\ast}\right)+\left(a-c\right)u_{n}+\left(b-\theta\left(a-c+p\right)\right)u_{n-1}+\left(p-b\right)\theta u_{n-2}\right]x^{n}

\displaystyle u_{1}x+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\left[nu_{n}-\left(\theta+1\right)\left(n-1\right)u_{n-1}+\theta\left(n-2\right)u_{n-2}\right]x^{n}.

Comparing coefficients of

gives

\displaystyle\left(c-a\right)\left(u_{n}^{\ast}-\theta u_{n-1}^{\ast}\right)+\left(p-b\right)\theta u_{n-2}+\left(b-\theta\left(a-c+p\right)\right)u_{n-1}+\left(a-c\right)u_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta\left(n-2\right)u_{n-2}-\left(\theta+1\right)\left(n-1\right)u_{n-1}+nu_{n}\text{ for }n\geq 2\text{,}

U_{\theta}^{\ast}

Proof of Corollary

Notice that

Schur convexity was introduced by Schur in 1923

, and it has many important applications in

,

, linear regression
[

, graphs and matrices
[

]
, combinatorial optimization
[

, information-theoretic topics
[

,

We need to recall several notions.

Definition 1
.

Let
\boldsymbol{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
and
\boldsymbol{y}=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

(i)
\boldsymbol{x}
is said to be majorized by
\boldsymbol{y}
(in symbols

\boldsymbol{x}\prec\boldsymbol{y}
) if
\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{[i]}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}y_{[i]}
for
k=1,2,\ldots,n-1
and
\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}
, where
x_{[1]}\geq\dotsm\geq x_{[n]}
and
y_{[1]}\geq\dotsm\geq y_{[n]}
are rearrangements of
\boldsymbol{x}
and
\boldsymbol{y}
in a descending order.

(ii)
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is called a convex set if
(\alpha x_{1}+\beta y_{1},\dotsc,\alpha x_{n}+\beta y_{n})\in\Omega
for any
\boldsymbol{x}
and
\boldsymbol{y}\in\Omega
, where
\alpha
and
\beta\in[0,1]
with
\alpha+\beta=1
.

(iii) Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\varphi
:
\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is said to be a Schur-convex
(
Schur-concave
)
function on
\Omega
if
\boldsymbol{x}\prec\boldsymbol{y}
on
\Omega
implies
\varphi\left(\boldsymbol{x}\right)\leq(\geq)

\varphi\left(\boldsymbol{y}\right)
.

Definition 2
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}(n\geq 2)
be a set with nonempty
interior. Then
\mathbb{\phi}:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is called
Schur convex on
\Omega
if
\mathbb{\phi}(\boldsymbol{x})\leq\mathbb{\phi}(\boldsymbol{y})
for each two
n
-tuples
\boldsymbol{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n})
and
\boldsymbol{y}=(y_{1},y_{2},...,y_{n})
of
\Omega
, such that
\boldsymbol{x}\prec\boldsymbol{y}
holds. The
relationship of majorization
\boldsymbol{x}\prec\boldsymbol{y}
means that

\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{[i]}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}y_{[i]},\text{ \ \ }\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{[i]}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{[i]},\text{\ }

where
1\leq k\leq n-1
, and
x_{[i]}
denotes the
i
-th largest component
of
\boldsymbol{x}
.
\mathbb{\phi}
is called Schur concave if
-\mathbb{\phi}
is Schur convex.

The following well-known result was proved by Marshall and Olkin
[
5
]
.

Theorem M-O.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be
a symmetric convex set with nonempty interior
\Omega
and
\phi:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a continuous and symmetric function on
\Omega
.
If
\phi
is differentiable on
\Omega
, then
\phi
is Schur convex
(Schur concave) on
\Omega
if and only if

(y-x)\left(\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial y}-\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x}\right)>(<)0

for all
(x,y)\in\Omega
with
x\neq y
.

In 2012, Yang

Definition 3

on
\Omega

\varphi(\boldsymbol{x})\leq(\geq)\varphi(\boldsymbol{y})

Theorem Y.
Let

(3.1)

\displaystyle\frac{x_{i}^{m}-x_{j}^{m}}{m}\left(x_{i}^{1-m}\frac{\partial\phi\left(\boldsymbol{x}\right)}{\partial x_{i}}-x_{j}^{1-m}\frac{\partial\phi\left(\boldsymbol{x}\right)}{\partial x_{j}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(\leq)0\text{

(3.2)

\displaystyle(\ln x_{i}-\ln x_{j})\left(x_{i}\frac{\partial\phi\left(\boldsymbol{x}\right)}{\partial x_{i}}-x_{j}\frac{\partial\phi\left(\boldsymbol{x}\right)}{\partial x_{j}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(\leq)0\text{ if }m=0

(x_{i}-x_{j})\left(x_{i}^{1-m}\frac{\partial\phi\left(\boldsymbol{x}\right)}{\partial x_{i}}-x_{j}^{1-m}\frac{\partial\phi\left(\boldsymbol{x}\right)}{\partial x_{j}}\right)\geq(\leq)0.

As an application of main results, we next investigate the m-power Schur

To this end, we first note that

is a symmetric and
homogeneous mean of positive numbers

and

y\geq x>0
, then

where

, we have

\displaystyle M^{a}\left(x,y\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{y^{a}}{B\left(b,b\right)}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left(-a\right)_{n}}{n!}\left(\int_{0}^{1}s^{n+b-1}\left(1-s\right)^{b-1}ds\right)t^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{y^{a}}{B\left(b,b\right)}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left(-a\right)_{n}}{n!}B\left(n+b,b\right)t^{n}=y^{a}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left(-a\right)_{n}\left(b\right)_{n}}{n!\left(2b\right)_{n}}t^{n},

which implies (

To obtain necessary and sufficient conditions for which

Lemma 2

Let

Since
\left(\ln\Gamma\left(x\right)\right)^{\prime\prime}=\psi^{\prime}\left(x\right)>0

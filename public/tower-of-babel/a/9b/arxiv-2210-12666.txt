Introduction

Throughout this paper,

with the maximal ideal

for all

denote the left derived functor of the
\mathfrak{a}
-adic completion
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(-)
. The concept of sequentially Cohen- Macaulay was defined by combinatorial commutative algebraists
[

to answer a basic question to find a non-pure
generalization of the concept of a Cohen-Macaulay module so that the face ring of a shellable (non-pure) simplicial
complex has this property. This concept was then applied by commutative algebraists to study some algebraic invariants or
special algebras that come from graphs. Recall that a finitely generated
R
-module

over a local ring

is called sequentially Cohen-Macaulay if there exists a finite filtration

0=M_{0}\subset M_{1}\subset\dots\subset M_{r-1}\subset M_{r}=M
of

by submodules of

such that each quotient module
M_{i}/M_{i-1}
is a Cohen-Macaulay

-module and that

( For more details see
[
19

There are some nice characterizations of the concept of sequentially Cohen-Macaulay modules which one of them is the following basic theorem of J. Herzog and D. Popescu in
[
8
, Theorem 2.4]
:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a
d
-dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local ring with the canonical

-module

and

be a finitely generated
R
-module. Then, the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

M
is sequentially Cohen-Macaulay.

(ii)

\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{d-i}_{R}(M,\omega_{R})
is zero or Cohen-Macaulay of dimension
i
for all
i\geq 0.

The author, in
[
14

as a natural generalization of the concept of Cohen-Macaulay modules over a local ring. Indeed, we say that an

-module

-relative Cohen-Macaulay if there is precisely one non-vanishing local cohomology module of

\mathfrak{a}

[

in the case where

is a relative Cohen-Macaulay local ring with respect to
\mathfrak{a}
and
c=\mbox{ht}\,_{R}\mathfrak{a}
. Indeed, we showed that these modules behave like the canonical modules over Cohen-Macaulay local rings. On the other hand, as a generalization of the notion of sequentially Cohen-Macaulay, the concept of relative sequentially Cohen-Macaulay was defined and studied by some authors ( see
[
1
]
,
[
13
]
,
[

]
and for definition see

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be an
\mathfrak{a}
-relative Cohen-Macaulay local ring with
\operatorname{\mathsf{cd}}(\mathfrak{a},R)=c
. Then, the following statements hold:

(i)

If

-relative sequentially Cohen-Macaulay, then

R. N. Roberts, in

-module

On the other hand, the concept of co-regular sequence for Artinian

. Hence, in section 4, as a generalization of this concept our main aim is to study the

Prerequisites

An
R
-complex

. The derived category of

. Any

0
in its other spots. We denote the
full subcategory of homologically left (resp. right) bounded complexes by
\mathrm{D}_{\sqsubset}(R)
(resp.

\mathrm{D}_{\sqsupset}(R)

and/or

The left-derived tensor product complex of

and

in
\mathrm{D}(R)
is

{X}\otimes_{R}^{{\bf L}}{Y}\simeq{F}\otimes_{R}{Y}\simeq{X}\otimes_{R}{F}^{{}^{\prime}}\simeq{F}\otimes_{R}{F},^{{}^{\prime}}

where

and

and
{Y}
, respectively.
Also, let
{X}\in\mathrm{D}(R)
and/or
{Y}\in\mathrm{D}(R)
. The right derived homomorphism complex of
{X}
and
{Y}
in

\mathrm{D}(R)
is denoted by

{\bf R}\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{R}({X},{Y})\simeq\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{R}({P},{Y})\simeq\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{R}({X},{I})\simeq\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{R}({P},I),

\mbox{H}_{l}(\operatorname{\mathsf{T}}(X))\cong\operatorname{\mathsf{T}}(\mbox{H}_{-l}(X))
for all

. For any

R
-module
M
and ideal
\mathfrak{a}
of

\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(M):=\{x\in M|~\Supp_{R}Rx\subseteq\mbox{V}(\mathfrak{a})\}.

, the right derived functor of the functor

\operatorname{\mathsf{cd}}(\mathfrak{a},M)=1
and
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)\neq 0
; and so
M
is not
\mathfrak{a}
-relative Cohen-Macaulay. But,
0\subset\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)\subset M
is a finite filtration such that
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)
and
M/\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)
are
\mathfrak{a}
-relative Cohen-Macaulay
R
-modules with cohomological dimension
0
and
1
, respectively. Therefore
M
is a finite
\mathfrak{a}
-relative sequentially Cohen-Macaulay
R
-module which is not finite
\mathfrak{a}
-relative Cohen-Macaulay.

3.
Relative Sequentially Cohen-Macaulay and relative Canonical modules

The starting point of this section is the next two lemmas, which will play an essential role in proving our main result
3.3
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be an
\mathfrak{a}
-relative Cohen-Macaulay local ring with
\operatorname{\mathsf{cd}}(\mathfrak{a},R)=c
and suppose that
M
is a finite
\mathfrak{a}
-relative sequentially Cohen-Macaulay module with the filtration
0=M_{0}\subset M_{1}\subset\dots\subset M_{r-1}\subset M_{r}=M
and set
\operatorname{\mathsf{cd}}(\mathfrak{a},M_{i}/M_{i-1}):=c_{i}
. Then
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{c-j}_{R}(M,\mbox{D}_{\mathfrak{a}})=0
for all
j\notin\{c_{1},\dots,c_{r}\}
and
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{c-c_{i}}_{R}(M,\mbox{D}_{\mathfrak{a}})
is
\mathfrak{a}
-relative Cohen-Macaulay of cohomological dimension
c_{i}
for all
i=1,\dots,r.

Proof.

We use induction on
r
to prove the result. To do this, let
r=1
, then by our assumption
M
is finite
\mathfrak{a}
-relative Cohen-Macaulay with
\operatorname{\mathsf{cd}}(\mathfrak{a},M)=c_{1}
. So, by Theorem
2.2
, one has
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}_{R}^{c-i}(M,\mbox{D}_{\mathfrak{a}})=0
for all
i\neq c_{1}
and
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}_{R}^{c-c_{1}}(M,\mbox{D}_{\mathfrak{a}})
is an
\mathfrak{a}
-relative Cohen-Macaulay module with
\operatorname{\mathsf{cd}}(\mathfrak{a},\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}_{R}^{c-c_{1}}(M,\mbox{D}_{\mathfrak{a}}))=c_{1}
. Now, assume that
r>1
and the result has been proved for all finite
\mathfrak{a}
-relative sequentially Cohen-Macaulay modules with a finite filtration of length
r-1
. Notice that
M/M_{1}
is a finite
\mathfrak{a}
-relative sequentially Cohen-Macaulay
R
-module with the following finite filtration of length
r-1
:

0=M_{1}/M_{1}\subset M_{2}/M_{1}\subset\dots\subset M_{r}/M_{1}=M/M_{1}.

Therefore, by induction hypothesis one has
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}_{R}^{c-j}(M/M_{1},\mbox{D}_{\mathfrak{a}})=0
for all
j\notin\{c_{2},\dots,c_{r}\}
and
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{c-c_{i}}_{R}(M/M_{1},\mbox{D}_{\mathfrak{a}})
is
\mathfrak{a}
-relative Cohen-Macaulay of cohomological dimension
c_{i}
for all
i=2,\dots,r.

Now consider the short exact sequence
0\longrightarrow M_{1}\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow M/M_{1}\longrightarrow 0
and the induced long exact sequence

\begin{array}[]{rl}\cdots\longrightarrow\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}_{R}^{c-i}(M/M_{1},\mbox{D}_{\mathfrak{a}})\longrightarrow\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}_{R}^{c-i}(M,\mbox{D}_{\mathfrak{a}})\longrightarrow\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}_{R}^{c-i}(M_{1},\mbox{D}_{\mathfrak{a}})\\
\longrightarrow\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}_{R}^{c-i+1}(M/M_{1},\mbox{D}_{\mathfrak{a}})\longrightarrow\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}_{R}^{c-i+1}(M,\mbox{D}_{\mathfrak{a}})\longrightarrow\cdots.\end{array}

Notice that, by Theorem
2.2
,
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}_{R}^{c-j}(M_{1},\mbox{D}_{\mathfrak{a}})=0
for all
j\neq c_{1}
and
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}_{R}^{c-c_{1}}(M_{1},\mbox{D}_{\mathfrak{a}})
is
\mathfrak{a}
-relative Cohen-Macaulay with
\operatorname{\mathsf{cd}}(\mathfrak{a},\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}_{R}^{c-c_{1}}(M_{1},\mbox{D}_{\mathfrak{a}}))=c_{1}.
Therefore, by the above induced sequence one has the isomorphisms
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}_{R}^{c-j}(M,\mbox{D}_{\mathfrak{a}})\cong\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}_{R}^{c-j}(M/M_{1},\mbox{D}_{\mathfrak{a}})
for
j\neq c_{1},c_{1}-1
and the following exact sequence:

\begin{array}[]{rl}0\longrightarrow\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}_{R}^{c-c_{1}}(M/M_{1},\mbox{D}_{\mathfrak{a}})\longrightarrow\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}_{R}^{c-c_{1}}(M,\mbox{D}_{\mathfrak{a}})\longrightarrow\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}_{R}^{c-c_{1}}(M_{1},\mbox{D}_{\mathfrak{a}})\\
\longrightarrow\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}_{R}^{c-c_{1}+1}(M/M_{1},\mbox{D}_{\mathfrak{a}})\longrightarrow\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}_{R}^{c-c_{1}+1}(M,\mbox{D}_{\mathfrak{a}})\longrightarrow 0.\end{array}

Since
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}_{R}^{c-j}(M/M_{1},\mbox{D}_{\mathfrak{a}})=0
for
j=c_{1},c_{1}-1
, one has
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}_{R}^{c-c_{1}}(M,\mbox{D}_{\mathfrak{a}})\cong\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}_{R}^{c-c_{1}}(M_{1},\mbox{D}_{\mathfrak{a}})
and
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}_{R}^{c-c_{1}+1}(M,\mbox{D}_{\mathfrak{a}})=0
, as required.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{a}
be an ideal of
R
with
\operatorname{\mathsf{cd}}(\mathfrak{a},R)=c
,
M
be a finitely generated
R
-module and
D
be an
R
-module which the following conditions are established:

(i)

\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{R}(D,D)
is flat and
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}_{R}^{i}(D,D)=0
for all
i>0
.

(ii)

For all
i
, we have
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}_{R}^{c-i}(M,D)
is zero, or

(a)

\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}_{R}^{c-j}(\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}_{R}^{c-i}(M,D),D)=0
if and only if
j\neq i
, and

(b)

\mbox{H}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{j}(\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}_{R}^{c-i}(\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}_{R}^{c-i}(M,D),D))=0
if and only if
j\neq i
.

Then
M\otimes_{R}\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{R}(D,D)
is
\mathfrak{a}
-sequentially Cohen-Macaulay.

Proof.

Let
P_{\bullet}:\cdots\longrightarrow P_{1}\longrightarrow P_{0}\longrightarrow 0
be a deleted free resolution with finitely generated terms for
M
and
I_{\bullet}:0\longrightarrow I^{0}\longrightarrow I^{1}\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow I^{c}\longrightarrow\cdots
be a deleted injective resolution for
D
. Now, consider the first quadrant double complex
M_{p,q}:=\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{R}(\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{R}(P_{p},D),I^{c-q})
. Let
{}^{I}E
(resp.
{}^{II}E
) denote the vertical (resp. horizontal) spectral sequence
associated to the double complex
\mathcal{M}=\{M_{p,q}\}
. Now, with the notation of
[
18
]
,
\mbox{E}^{1}
is the bigraded module whose
(p,q)
term is
\mbox{H}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{q}(M_{p,*})
, the q-th homology of the p-th column. Therefore,
\mbox{H}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{q}(M_{p,*})=\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}_{R}^{c-q}(\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{R}(P_{p},D),D)
, and so in view of
(i)
one has

{}^{I}E_{p,q}^{1}=\mbox{H}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{q}(M_{p,*})=\begin{cases}0&\text{if $q\neq c$}\\
\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{R}(\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{R}(P_{p},D),D)&\text{if $q=c$},\end{cases}

As
P_{p}
is finite free module for all
p
, then in view of
[
3
, Theorem 2.5.6]
, one has
\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{R}(\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{R}(P_{\bullet},D),D)\simeq P_{\bullet}\otimes_{R}\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{R}(D,D)
.
So, we have

{}^{I}E_{p,q}^{2}=\mbox{H}^{{}^{\prime}}_{p}\mbox{H}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{q}(\mathcal{M})=\begin{cases}0&\text{if $q\neq c$}\\
\operatorname{\mathsf{Tor}}^{R}_{p}(M,\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{R}(D,D))&\text{if $q=c$}.\end{cases}

Hence, in view of
(i)
,
{}^{I}E_{p,q}^{2}=0
for all
p>0
and so the spectral sequence collapses on the
q
-axis. Therefore, by
[
18
, Proposition 10.21]
, one has
{}^{I}E_{0,c}^{2}=M\otimes_{R}\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{R}(D,D)\cong\mbox{H}_{c}(\mbox{Tot}\,(M))
.

A similar argument applies to the second iterated homology, using the fact that each
I_{c-q}
is injective, yields

{}^{II}E_{p,q}^{2}=\mbox{H}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{p}\mbox{H}^{{}^{\prime}}_{q}(\mathcal{M})=\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}_{R}^{c-p}(\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}_{R}^{q}(M,D),D)
and that
{}^{II}E_{p,q}^{2}\underset{p}{\Longrightarrow}\mbox{H}_{p+q}(\mbox{Tot}\,(\mathcal{M}))
. Thus, for all
p,q
such that
c=p+q
, there is the following filtration

{0}=\Phi^{-1}H_{c}\subseteq\Phi^{0}H_{c}\subseteq\ldots\subseteq\Phi^{c-1}H_{c}\subseteq\Phi^{c}H_{c}=H_{c}(\mbox{Tot}\,(M))=M\otimes_{R}\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{R}(D,D)

such that
E_{p,c-p}^{\infty}:=^{II}E_{p,c-p}^{\infty}\cong\Phi^{p}H_{c}/\Phi^{p-1}H_{c}
.

On the other hand, by our assumption
(ii)
, one has
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}_{R}^{c-p}(\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}_{R}^{q}(M,D),D)=0
for all
p\neq c-q
and so

\mbox{E}^{2}_{p,q}=0
for all
p\neq c-q
. Next, for each
r\geq 2
and
p\geq 0
, let

Z^{r}_{p,c-p}:=\mbox{Ker}\,(\mbox{E}^{r}_{p,c-p}\longrightarrow\mbox{E}^{r}_{p-r,c-p+r-1})

and

B^{r}_{p,c-p}:=\mbox{Im}\,(\mbox{E}^{r}_{p+r,c-p-r+1}\longrightarrow\mbox{E}^{r}_{p,c-p}).

By using the assumption,
\mbox{E}^{r}_{p-r,c-p+r-1}=\mbox{E}^{r}_{p+r,c-p-r+1}=0
for all
r\geq 2
and
p\geq 0
, because for all
p
,
\mbox{E}^{r}_{p,c-p}
is subquotients of
\mbox{E}^{2}_{p,c-p}
, and so
\mbox{E}^{r}_{p,c-p}=Z^{r}_{p,c-p}
and
B^{r}_{p,c-p}=0
. On the other hand, one has
\mbox{E}^{r+1}_{p,c-p}=\frac{Z^{r}_{p,c-p}}{B^{r}_{p,c-p}}
, and thus
\mbox{E}^{2}_{p,c-p}\cong\mbox{E}^{3}_{p,c-p}\cong\dots\cong\mbox{E}_{p,c-p}^{\infty}
for all
p\geq 0
. Therefore, by
our assumption
(iii)
for all
p
,
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{c-p}_{R}(\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{c-p}_{R}(M,D),D)
is
\mathfrak{a}
-relative Cohen-Macaulay of cohomological dimension
p
. Therefore,
\Phi^{p}H_{c}/\Phi^{p-1}H_{c}
is
\mathfrak{a}
-relative Cohen-Macaulay of cohomological dimension
p
for all
0\leq p\leq c
, and so the above filtration is a
\mathfrak{a}
-relative Cohen-Macaulay filtration for
M\otimes_{R}\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{R}(D,D)
, as required.

∎

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be an
\mathfrak{a}
-relative Cohen-Macaulay local ring with
\operatorname{\mathsf{cd}}(\mathfrak{a},R)=c
. Then the following statements hold:

(i)

If
M
is finite
\mathfrak{a}
-relative sequentially Cohen-Macaulay, then
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{c-i}_{R}(M,\mbox{D}_{\mathfrak{a}})
is zero or is
\mathfrak{a}
-relative Cohen-Macaulay of cohomological dimension
i
for all
i\geq 0
.

(ii)

If for all
i\geq 0
,
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{c-i}_{R}(M,\mbox{D}_{\mathfrak{a}})
is zero or is
\mathfrak{a}
-relative Cohen-Macaulay of cohomological dimension
i
and
\mbox{H}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{j}(\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{c-i}_{R}(\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{c-i}_{R}(M,\mbox{D}_{\mathfrak{a}}),\mbox{D}_{\mathfrak{a}}))=0
for all
j>i
, then
M\otimes_{R}\widehat{R}
is
\mathfrak{a}
-relative sequentially Cohen-Macaulay.

Proof.

The statement
(i)
is an immediate consequence of Lemma
3.1
. For the statement
(ii)
, first in view of Theorem
2.2
(iii) one has
\widehat{R}\cong\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{R}(\mbox{D}_{\mathfrak{a}},\mbox{D}_{\mathfrak{a}})
and
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}_{R}^{i}(\mbox{D}_{\mathfrak{a}},\mbox{D}_{\mathfrak{a}})=0
for all
i>0
. Also, by our assumption and
[
15
, Corollary 3.2]
, we have
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}_{R}^{c-j}(\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}_{R}^{c-i}(M,\mbox{D}_{\mathfrak{a}}),\mbox{D}_{\mathfrak{a}})=0
if and only if
j\neq i
.

Now, we show that for all
i
,
\mbox{H}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{j}(\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{c-i}_{R}(\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{c-i}_{R}(M,\mbox{D}_{\mathfrak{a}}),\mbox{D}_{\mathfrak{a}}))=0
for all
j<i
, which to do this it is enough to show that
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}_{R}^{j}(R/\mathfrak{a},\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{c-i}_{R}(\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{c-i}_{R}(M,\mbox{D}_{\mathfrak{a}}),\mbox{D}_{\mathfrak{a}}))=0
for all
j<i
. To do this, notice that since
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{c-i}_{R}(M,\mbox{D}_{\mathfrak{a}})
is
\mathfrak{a}
-relative Cohen-Macaulay of cohomological dimension
i
, in view of
[
15
, Theorem 3.1(iii)]
, we have
\operatorname{\mathsf{Tor}}_{t-i}^{R}(R/\mathfrak{a},\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{c-i}_{R}(M,\mbox{D}_{\mathfrak{a}}))\cong\operatorname{\mathsf{Tor}}_{t}^{R}(R/\mathfrak{a},\mbox{H}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{i}(\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{c-i}_{R}(M,\mbox{D}_{\mathfrak{a}})))
for all
t
. Therefore, one can use
[
15
, Corollary 3.2]
to get the following isomorphisms

\begin{array}[]{rl}\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}_{R}^{j}(R/\mathfrak{a},\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{c-i}_{R}(\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{c-i}_{R}(M,\mbox{D}_{\mathfrak{a}}),\mbox{D}_{\mathfrak{a}}))&\cong(\operatorname{\mathsf{Tor}}_{j}^{R}(R/\mathfrak{a},\mbox{H}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{i}(\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{c-i}_{R}(M,\mbox{D}_{\mathfrak{a}}))))^{\vee}\\
&\cong(\operatorname{\mathsf{Tor}}_{j-i}^{R}(R/\mathfrak{a},\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{c-i}_{R}(M,\mbox{D}_{\mathfrak{a}})))^{\vee},\end{array}

which implies that
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}_{R}^{j}(R/\mathfrak{a},\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{c-i}_{R}(\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{c-i}_{R}(M,\mbox{D}_{\mathfrak{a}}),\mbox{D}_{\mathfrak{a}}))=0
for all
j<i
, as required. Therefore, by
our assumption we can see that
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{c-i}_{R}(\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{c-i}_{R}(M,\mbox{D}_{\mathfrak{a}}),\mbox{D}_{\mathfrak{a}})
is
\mathfrak{a}
-relative Cohen-Macaulay of cohomological dimension
i
, for all
i
. Therefore, the assertion is done by Lemma
3.2
.
∎

The following result is a consequence of Lemma
3.2
which has already been proved by J. Herzog and D. Popescu in
[
8
, Theorem 2.4]
.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a
d
-dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local ring with the canonical
R
-module
\omega_{R}
and
M
be a finitely generated
R
-module. Then, the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

M
is sequentially Cohen-Macaulay.

(ii)

\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{d-i}_{R}(M,\omega_{R})
is zero or Cohen-Macaulay of dimension
i
for all
i\geq 0.

Proof.

First notice that for all finitely generated
R
-modules
X
we have
\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}_{R}(X)=\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}_{\widehat{R}}(X\otimes_{R}\widehat{R})
,
\operatorname{\mathsf{depth}}_{R}(X)=\operatorname{\mathsf{depth}}_{\widehat{R}}(X\otimes_{R}\widehat{R})
and
\operatorname{\mathsf{id}}_{R}(X)=\operatorname{\mathsf{id}}_{\widehat{R}}(X\otimes_{R}\widehat{R})=\operatorname{\mathsf{depth}}R
. On the other hand, , it is clear to see that

\begin{array}[]{rl}(1)~~~\omega_{\widehat{R}}&\cong\omega_{R}\otimes_{R}{\widehat{R}}\\
&\cong\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{R}(\mbox{H}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{d}(R),\mbox{E}_{R}(R/\mathfrak{m}))\\
&\cong\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{\widehat{R}}(\mbox{H}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{d}(R)\otimes_{R}\widehat{R},\mbox{E}_{R}(R/\mathfrak{m}))\\
&\cong\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{\widehat{R}}(\mbox{H}_{\mathfrak{m}\widehat{R}}^{d}(\widehat{R}),\mbox{E}_{\widehat{R}}(\widehat{R}/\mathfrak{m}\widehat{R}))\\
&=\mbox{D}_{{\mathfrak{m}}\widehat{R}}.\end{array}

Hence, for all integers
i
, one has

\begin{array}[]{rl}(2)~~~\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{d-i}_{\widehat{R}}(M\otimes_{R}{\widehat{R}},\mbox{D}_{{\mathfrak{m}}\widehat{R}})&\cong\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{d-i}_{\widehat{R}}(M\otimes_{R}\widehat{R},\omega_{\widehat{R}})\\
&\cong\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{d-i}_{R}(M,\omega_{R})\otimes_{R}\widehat{R}.\end{array}

Therefore, for the implication
(ii)\Longrightarrow(i)
, in view of Theorem
2.2
and the fact that
\widehat{R}
is faithfully flat, one can deduce that

\operatorname{\mathsf{id}}_{R}(\omega_{R})=\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}R
,
R\cong\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{R}(\omega_{R},\omega_{R})
and
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}_{R}^{i}(\omega_{R},\omega_{R})=0
for all
i>0
. Also, by our assumption and Theorem
3.3
(iv), for all
i=0,1,\ldots,\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}R
, the
i
-dimensional finitely generated Cohen-Macaulay
R
-modules
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}_{R}^{d-i}(M,\omega_{R})
, have the following conditions:

(a)

\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}_{R}^{d-j}(\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}_{R}^{d-i}(M,\omega_{R}),\omega_{R})=0
if and only if
j\neq i
.

(b)

\mbox{H}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{j}(\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}_{R}^{d-i}(\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}_{R}^{d-i}(M,\omega_{R}),\omega_{R}))=0
if and only if
j\neq i
.

Therefore, one can use Lemma
3.2
to complete the proof. Note that, by use of definition, one can check that
M\otimes_{R}\widehat{R}
is a sequentially Cohen-Macaulay
\widehat{R}
-module whenever
M
is a sequentially Cohen-Macaulay
R
-module. Hence, the implication
(i)\Longrightarrow(ii)
follows from Theorem
3.3
(i) and the above two isomorphisms
(1)
and
(2)
.
∎

4.
Relative sequentially Cohen-Macaulay and local homology

Based on the discussion made in the introduction, an Artinain
R
-module
M
is co-Cohen-Macaulay if and only if
\mbox{H}^{\mathfrak{m}}_{i}(M)=0
for all
i\neq\mbox{Ndim}\,M
.
Hence, in this section, as a generalization of this concept, our main aim is to study the
R
-modules
X
such that its local homology modules concerning an arbitrary ideal
\mathfrak{a}
,
\mbox{H}_{i}({\mathbf{L}}\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(X))
, has just one non-vanishing point. Indeed, in this section, we are going to provide some characterizations for relative Cohen-Macaulay and relative sequentially Cohen-Macaulay modules.

The starting point of this section is the next two lemmas which play an essential role in the proof of the next main theorem.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
X_{\bullet}:=\cdots\longrightarrow X_{l+1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d_{l+1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}X_{l}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d_{l}}}{{\longrightarrow}}X_{l-1\longrightarrow\cdots}
be an
R
-complex such that
H_{i}(X_{\bullet})=0
for all
i\neq c
, then there exists the isomorphism
\Sigma^{c}\mbox{H}_{c}(X_{\bullet})\simeq X_{\bullet}
in derived category
\mbox{D}(R)
.

Proof.

Consider the following quasi-isomorphisms:

\begin{CD}\Sigma^{c}\mbox{H}_{c}(X_{\bullet}):\cdots @>{}>{}>0@>{}>{}>0@>{}>{}>\mbox{H}_{c}(X_{\bullet})@>{}>{}>0@>{}>{}>0@>{}>{}>\cdots\\
@V{0}V{}V@V{}V{0}V@V{}V{i}V@V{0}V{}V@V{0}V{}V\\
\subset_{c}X_{\bullet}:\cdots @>{}>{}>0@>{}>{}>0@>{}>{}>\frac{X_{c}}{\mbox{Im}\,d_{c+1}}@>{\overline{d_{c}}}>{}>X_{c-1}@>{d_{c-1}}>{}>X_{c-2}@>{}>{}>\cdots\\
@A{0}A{}A@A{0}A{}A@A{\pi}A{}A\Big\|\Big\|\\
X_{\bullet}:\cdots @>{}>{}>X_{c+2}@>{d_{c+2}}>{}>X_{c+1}@>{d_{c+1}}>{}>X_{c}@>{d_{c}}>{}>X_{c-1}@>{d_{c-1}}>{}>X_{c-2}@>{}>{}>\cdots,\end{CD}

and

\begin{CD}X_{\bullet}:\cdots @>{}>{}>X_{c+2}@>{d_{c+2}}>{}>X_{c+1}@>{d_{c+1}}>{}>X_{c}@>{d_{c}}>{}>X_{c-1}@>{}>{}>\cdots\\
\Big\|\Big\|@A{i}A{}A@A{0}A{}A\\
{X_{\bullet}}_{c}\supset:\cdots @>{}>{}>X_{c+2}@>{d_{c+2}}>{}>X_{c+1}@>{iod_{c+1}}>{}>\ker d_{c}@>{}>{}>0@>{}>{}>\cdots\\
@V{0}V{}V@V{}V{}V@V{\pi}V{}V@V{}V{0}V\\
\Sigma^{c}\mbox{H}_{c}(X_{\bullet}):\cdots @>{}>{}>0@>{}>{}>0@>{}>{}>\mbox{H}_{c}(X_{\bullet})@>{}>{}>0@>{}>{}>\cdots\\
\end{CD}

Therefore, there exists the quasi-isomorphisms

\begin{cases}\Sigma^{c}\mbox{H}_{c}(X_{\bullet})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\longrightarrow}}\subset_{c}X_{\bullet}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\longleftarrow}}X_{\bullet}&\text{and}\\
\Sigma^{c}\mbox{H}_{c}(X_{\bullet})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\longleftarrow}}{X_{\bullet}}_{c}\supset\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\longrightarrow}}X_{\bullet}&,\end{cases}

where
\subset_{c}X_{\bullet}
and
{X_{\bullet}}_{c}\supset
are the soft left truncation and the soft right truncation
of the complex
X_{\bullet}
, respectively, for more detail see
[
2
, A.1.14]
. Therefore, by the definition of isomorphism in derived category
\mbox{D}(R)
one has
\Sigma^{-c}\mbox{H}^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)
and
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(I^{\bullet})
are isomorphic in derived category modules
\mbox{D}(R)
.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
M
be an
R
-module,
\mathfrak{a}
be an ideal of
R
such that for a fix integer
c
,
\mbox{H}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{c}(M)\neq 0
and
\mbox{H}_{i}({\mathbf{L}}\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(\mbox{H}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{c}(M)))=0
for all
i\neq c
. Then,
\mbox{H}_{c}({\mathbf{L}}\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(\mbox{H}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{c}(M)))
is a nonzero
\mathfrak{a}
-relative Cohen-Macaulay
R
-module of cohomological dimension
c
.

Proof.

First notice that by our assumption and Lemma
4.1
we have the isomorphism
\Sigma^{c}\mbox{H}_{c}({\mathbf{L}}\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(\mbox{H}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{c}(M)))\simeq{\mathbf{L}}\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(\mbox{H}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{c}(M))
, and so
\mbox{H}_{c}({\mathbf{L}}\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(\mbox{H}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{c}(M)))\simeq\Sigma^{-c}{\mathbf{L}}\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(\mbox{H}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{c}(M)).

Next, consider the following isomorphisms in derived category
\mbox{D}(R)
:

i
. Hence
\mbox{H}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{c}(M)=0
. So, in the statement
(i)
of the previous theorem, there exists at least one integer
i
such that
\operatorname{\mathsf{T}}^{i}(\mbox{H}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{i}(M))
is nonzero.

The following result is an immediate consequence of Theorem
4.7
for the case where
\mathfrak{a}=\mathfrak{m}
.

Corollary 4.8
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a local ring and
M
be a finitely generated
R
-module. Then, the following statements hold:

(i)

If
M
is sequentially Cohen-Macaulay, then
\mbox{H}_{j}({\mathbf{L}}\Lambda_{\mathfrak{m}}(\mbox{H}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{t}(M)))=0
for all
0\leq t\leq\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}M
and for all
j\neq t
.

(ii)

If
\mbox{H}_{j}({\mathbf{L}}\Lambda_{\mathfrak{m}}(\mbox{H}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{t}(M)))=0
for all
0\leq t\leq\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}M
and for all
j\neq t
, then
\widehat{M}
is a sequentially Cohen-Macaulay
\widehat{R}
-module.

Definition and Remarks 4.9
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a local ring of dimension
d
and
M
be a finitely generated
R
-module with
n=\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}_{R}M
. For an integer
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, define
K_{M}^{i}:=\mbox{H}^{i}(\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{R}(M,D_{R}^{\bullet})

where
D_{R}^{\bullet}
is a normalaized dualizing complex for
R
.
The module
K_{M}:=K_{M}^{n}
is called the canonical module of
M
. For
i\neq n
the modules

K_{M}^{i}
are called the modules of deficiency of
M
. By the local duality theorem, for all
i
there are the following canonical isomorphisms:

\mbox{H}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{i}(M)\cong\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{R}(K_{M}^{i},\mbox{E}_{R}(R/\mathfrak{m})).

Recall that all of the
K_{M}^{i}
,
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, are finitely generated
R
-modules. Moreover
M
is a Cohen-Macaulay
module if and only if
K_{M}^{i}=0
for all
i\neq n
. Whence the modules of deficiencies of
M

measure the deviation of
M
from being a Cohen-Macaulay module. In the case of
(R,\mathfrak{m})

the quotient of a local Gorenstein ring
(S,\mathfrak{n})
there are the following isomorphisms

K_{M}^{i}=\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{s-i}_{S}(M,S),s=\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}S
, for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
. Therefore, one has
K_{\widehat{M}}^{i}\cong\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{R}(\mbox{H}^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M),\mbox{E}_{R}(R/\mathfrak{m}))\cong\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{\widehat{R}}(\mbox{H}^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}\widehat{R}}(\widehat{M}),\mbox{E}_{\widehat{R}}(\widehat{R}/\mathfrak{m}\widehat{R}))
and is finitely generated
\widehat{R}
-module for all
i
.

Proposition 4.10
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a local ring with a dualizing complex and
M
be a finitely generated
R
-module. Then, for any integer
t
, the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

K_{M}^{t}
is zero or a Cohen-Macaulay
R
-module of dimension
t
.

(ii)

\mbox{H}_{j}({\mathbf{L}}\Lambda_{\mathfrak{m}}(\mbox{H}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{t}(M)))=0
for all
j\neq t
.

Proof.

First, consider the following isomorphisms:

\begin{array}[]{rl}\mbox{H}_{j}({\mathbf{L}}\Lambda_{\mathfrak{m}}(\mbox{H}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{t}(M)))&\cong\mbox{H}_{j}(\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{R}(\check{L}_{x},\mbox{H}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{t}(M))\\
&\cong\mbox{H}_{j}(\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{R}(\check{L}_{x},\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{R}(K_{M}^{t},\mbox{E}_{R}(R/\mathfrak{m}))))\\
&\cong\mbox{H}_{j}(\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{R}(\check{L}_{x}\otimes_{R}K_{M}^{t},\mbox{E}_{R}(R/\mathfrak{m}))\\
&\cong\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{R}(\mbox{H}^{j}(\check{L}_{x}\otimes_{R}K_{M}^{t}),\mbox{E}_{R}(R/\mathfrak{m}))\\
&\cong\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{R}(\mbox{H}^{j}_{\mathfrak{m}}(K_{M}^{t}),\mbox{E}_{R}(R/\mathfrak{m})).\end{array}

Here, the last isomorphism follows from
[
21
, Theorem 7.4.4]
. Therefore,
K_{M}^{t}
is a Cohen-Macaulay if and only if
\mbox{H}_{j}({\mathbf{L}}\Lambda_{\mathfrak{m}}(\mbox{H}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{t}(M)))=0
for all
j\neq t
, as required.
∎

It is well-known that a complete local ring
(R,\mathfrak{m})
with respect to
\mathfrak{m}
-adic completion has a dualizing complex. Therefore, we provide the following result as an immediate consequence of Corollary
4.8
and Proposition
4.10
.

Corollary 4.11
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a complete local ring with respect to
\mathfrak{m}
-adic completion and
M
be a finitely generated
R
-module. Then, the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

M
is a sequentially Cohen-Macaulay
R
-module.

(ii)

K_{M}^{t}
is zero or a Cohen-Macaulay
R
-module of dimension
t
for all
0\leq t\leq\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}M
.

In
[
20
, Corollary 5.4]
, for a
d
-dimensional finitely generated
R
-module
M
over the local ring
(R,\mathfrak{m})
it is proven that
K_{\widehat{M}}^{d}
is a Cohen-Macaulay
\widehat{R}
-module if and only if
\mbox{H}_{j}({\mathbf{L}}\Lambda_{\mathfrak{m}}(\mbox{H}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{d}(M)))=0
for all
j\neq d
. So, the following corollary, which is a consequence of Proposition
4.10
, is a generalization of
[
20
, Corollary 5.4]
.

Corollary 4.12
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a local ring and
M
be a finitely generated
R
-module. Then, for any
0\leq t\leq\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}M
the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

K_{\widehat{M}}^{t}
is zero or a Cohen-Macaulay
\widehat{R}
-module of dimension
t
.

(ii)

\mbox{H}_{j}({\mathbf{L}}\Lambda_{\mathfrak{m}}(\mbox{H}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{t}(M)))=0
for all
j\neq t
.

Proof.

First notice that, by
[
21
, Corollary 2.2.5]
,
\mbox{H}_{j}({\mathbf{L}}\Lambda_{\mathfrak{m}}(\mbox{H}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{t}(M)))\cong\mbox{H}_{j}({\mathbf{L}}\Lambda_{\mathfrak{m}\widehat{R}}(\mbox{H}_{\mathfrak{m}\widehat{R}}^{t}(\widehat{M})))
for all
j
. Hence we may and do assume that
R
is a complete local ring, and so it has a dualizing complex. Therefore, the proof is complete by Proposition
4.10
.
∎

In
[
21
, Theorem 10.5.9]
, it is shown that
R
is a
d
-dimensional Gorenstein ring if and only if
\mbox{H}_{i}({\mathbf{L}}\Lambda_{\mathfrak{m}}(\mbox{E}_{R}(k)))=0
for all
i\neq d
and
\mbox{H}_{d}({\mathbf{L}}\Lambda_{\mathfrak{m}}(\mbox{E}_{R}(k)))\cong\widehat{R}^{\mathfrak{m}}.
So, in this direction we provide the following result:

Theorem 4.13
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a local ring of dimension
d
. and let
\mathfrak{a}
be an ideal of
R
. Then, the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

R
is Gorenstein

(ii)

\mbox{H}_{i}({\mathbf{L}}\Lambda_{\mathfrak{m}}(\mbox{E}_{R}(k)))=0
for all
i\neq d
and
\mbox{H}_{d}({\mathbf{L}}\Lambda_{\mathfrak{m}}(\mbox{E}_{R}(k)))\cong\widehat{R}^{\mathfrak{m}}.

(iii)

There exist an ideal
\mathfrak{a}
of
R
with
\operatorname{\mathsf{cd}}(\mathfrak{a},R)=c
such that
\mbox{H}_{i}({\mathbf{L}}\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(\mbox{E}_{R}(k)))=0
for all
i\neq c
and
\operatorname{\mathsf{fd}}_{R}(\mbox{H}_{c}({\mathbf{L}}\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(\mbox{E}_{R}(k))))<\infty.

Proof.

The implication
(i)\Longrightarrow(ii)
follows from Corollary
4.4
and the fact that
R
is Cohen-Macaulay and
\mbox{H}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{d}(R)\cong\mbox{E}_{R}(k)
.
The implication
(ii)\Longrightarrow(iii)
is obvious. For the implication
(iii)\Longrightarrow(i)
, in view of
[
21
, Corollary 9.2.5]
one has
\mbox{H}_{i}({\mathbf{L}}\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(\mbox{E}_{R}(k)))\cong\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{R}(\mbox{H}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{i}(R),\mbox{E}_{R}(k))
for all
i
. Therefore,
\mbox{H}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{i}(R)=0
for all
i\neq c
, and also
\mbox{H}_{c}({\mathbf{L}}\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(\mbox{E}_{R}(k)))\cong\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{R}(\mbox{H}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{c}(R),\mbox{E}_{R}(k))
. Hence, by our assumption
\operatorname{\mathsf{fd}}_{R}(\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{R}(\mbox{H}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{c}(R),\mbox{E}_{R}(k)))
is finite. On the there hand, by
[
15
, Theorem 3.4]
, we have
\mbox{H}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{i}(\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{R}(\mbox{H}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{c}(R),\mbox{E}_{R}(k)))=0
for all
i\neq c
and also by
[
15
, Lemma 3.5]
one has
\mbox{H}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{c}(\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{R}(\mbox{H}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{c}(R),\mbox{E}_{R}(k)))\cong\mbox{E}_{R}(k)
. Therefore, by
[
16
, Theorem 3.2]
,
\operatorname{\mathsf{fd}}_{R}(\mbox{E}_{R}(k))
is finite which it follows that
R
is Gorenstein, as required.
∎

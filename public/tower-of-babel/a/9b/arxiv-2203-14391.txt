Bailey pairs and strange identities
Jeremy Lovejoy

Abstract
. Zagier introduced the term “strange identity” to describe an asymptotic relation between a certain q q -hypergeometric series and a partial theta function at roots of unity. We show that behind Zagier’s strange identity lies a statement about Bailey pairs. Using the iterative machinery of Bailey pairs then leads to many families of multisum strange identities, including Hikami’s generalization of Zagier’s identity.

1.
Introduction and Statement of Results

A
Bailey pair
relative to
(a,q)
is a pair of sequences
(\alpha_{n},\beta_{n})_{n\geq 0}
satisfying

\beta_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{\alpha_{k}}{(q)_{n-k}(aq)_{n+k}}.

(1.1)

Here we have used the standard
q
-hypergeometric notation,

(a)_{n}=(a;q)_{n}=\prod_{k=1}^{n}(1-aq^{k-1}),

defined for integers
n\geq 0
and in the limit as
n\to\infty
. Bailey pairs are one of the principal structural elements in the theory of
q
-hypergeometric series. Much of their power comes from the fact that Bailey pairs give rise to new Bailey pairs, and they do so in many different ways. This leads to an iterative “machinery” that produces infinite families of identities starting from a single identity. For example, once one understands how to use Bailey pairs to prove the Rogers-Ramanujan identities,

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{q^{n^{2}}}{(q)_{n}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{n\equiv 1,4\pmod{5}}\frac{1}{1-q^{n}}

and

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{q^{n^{2}+n}}{(q)_{n}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{n\equiv 2,3\pmod{5}}\frac{1}{1-q^{n}},

the Bailey machinery produces the entire family of Andrews-Gordon identities,

\sum_{n_{k-1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{1}\geq 0}\frac{q^{n_{1}^{2}+\cdots+n_{k-1}^{2}+n_{1}+\cdots+n_{k-i}}}{(q)_{n_{k-1}-n_{k-2}}\cdots(q)_{n_{2}-n_{1}}(q)_{n_{1}}}=\prod_{n\not\equiv 0,\pm i\pmod{2k+1}}\frac{1}{1-q^{n}}.

(1.2)

Here
1\leq i\leq k
.
As a bonus, such identities often arise naturally in number theory, combinatorics, algebra, physics, or knot theory. For more on Bailey pairs and their applications, see
[
4
,
5
,
6
,
21
,
24
]

Here we consider the role of Bailey pairs in certain
q
-series identities which are not really identities at all. The term
strange identity
was first used by Zagier
[
25
]
to describe the fact that

\sum_{n\geq 0}(q)_{n}``="-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n\geq 1}n\left(\frac{12}{n}\right)q^{(n^{2}-1)/24}.

(1.3)

What the symbol
``="
means here is not that the identity holds in any classical sense, but that both sides agree to infinite order at any root of unity
\zeta
. Somewhat more specifically, replacing
q
by
\zeta e^{-t}
and letting
t\to 0^{+}
, the right-hand side has an asymptotic expansion as a power series in
t
, and this power series is given by the left-hand side at
q=\zeta e^{-t}
. See
[
2

[
25

Hikami
[

gave an elegant generalization of Zagier’s strange identity. To state it,
recall the

-binomial coefficient defined by

\begin{bmatrix}n\\

0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(1.4)

Then for
0\leq a\leq k-1
Hikami showed that

\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1},\dots,n_{k}\geq 0}

\displaystyle(q)_{n_{k}}q^{n_{1}^{2}+\cdots+n_{k-1}^{2}+n_{a+1}+\cdots+n_{k-1}}\prod_{i=1}^{k-1}\begin{bmatrix}n_{i+1}+\delta_{i,a}\\
n_{i}\end{bmatrix}

(1.5)

\displaystyle``="-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n\geq 0}n\chi_{8k+4}^{(a)}(n)q^{\frac{n^{2}-(2k-2a-1)^{2}}{8(2k+1)}},

where
\chi_{8k+4}^{(a)}(n)
is the even periodic function modulo
8k+4
defined by

\chi_{8k+4}^{(a)}(n)=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if $n\equiv 2k-2a-1$ or $6k+2a+5\pmod{8k+4}$},\\
-1,&\text{if $n\equiv 2k+2a+3$ or $6k-2a+1\pmod{8k+4}$},\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(1.6)

2.
Bailey pairs And Zagier’s identity

Recall the definition of a Bailey pair from the introduction. From now on we shall say “Bailey pair relative to
a
” instead of “Bailey pair relative to
(a,q)
” unless the second parameter is something other than
q
. The
Bailey lemma
says that if
(\alpha_{n},\beta_{n})
is a Bailey pair relative to
a
, then so is
(\alpha_{n}^{\prime},\beta_{n}^{\prime})
, where

\alpha^{\prime}_{n}=\frac{(\rho_{1})_{n}(\rho_{2})_{n}(aq/\rho_{1}\rho_{2})^{n}}{(aq/\rho_{1})_{n}(aq/\rho_{2})_{n}}\alpha_{n}

(2.1)

and

\beta^{\prime}_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{(\rho_{1})_{k}(\rho_{2})_{k}(aq/\rho_{1}\rho_{2})_{n-k}(aq/\rho_{1}\rho_{2})^{k}}{(aq/\rho_{1})_{n}(aq/\rho_{2})_{n}(q)_{n-k}}\beta_{k}.

(2.2)

A useful limiting form of the Bailey lemma is found by putting (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) into (
1.1
) and letting
n\to\infty
, giving

\sum_{n\geq 0}(\rho_{1})_{n}(\rho_{2})_{n}(aq/\rho_{1}\rho_{2})^{n}\beta_{n}=\frac{(aq/\rho_{1})_{\infty}(aq/\rho_{2})_{\infty}}{(aq)_{\infty}(aq/\rho_{1}\rho_{2})_{\infty}}\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{(\rho_{1})_{n}(\rho_{2})_{n}(aq/\rho_{1}\rho_{2})^{n}}{(aq/\rho_{1})_{n}(aq/\rho_{2})_{n}}\alpha_{n},

(2.3)

provided both sides converge absolutely. The case
(a,\rho_{1},\rho_{2})=(x^{2}q,xq,q)
of (
2.3
) is recorded as the following lemma, which will play a key role throughout the paper.

Lemma 2.1
.

If
(\alpha_{n},\beta_{n})
is a Bailey pair relative to
x^{2}q
, then we have

(1-x)\sum_{n\geq 0}(xq)_{n}(q)_{n}x^{n}\beta_{n}=(1-x^{2}q)\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{(q)_{n}}{(x^{2}q)_{n}}x^{n}\alpha_{n}.

We are now ready to prove Zagier’s strange identity, along with the base cases of the families of identities in Theorem
1.1
. In each case we make use of a specialization of Slater’s Bailey pair relative to
a

[
22
]
,

\alpha_{n}=\frac{(a)_{n}(1-aq^{2n})(-1)^{n}q^{\binom{n}{2}}(b)_{n}(c)_{n}\left(\frac{aq}{bc}\right)^{n}}{(q)_{n}(1-a)(\frac{aq}{b})_{n}(\frac{aq}{c})_{n}}

(2.4)

and

\beta_{n}=\frac{(\frac{aq}{bc})_{n}}{(q)_{n}(\frac{aq}{b})_{n}(\frac{aq}{c})_{n}}.

(2.5)

Proof of (
1.3
).

Take
a=x^{2}q
and
b,c\to\infty
in (
2.4
) and (
2.5
) to obtain the Bailey pair relative to
x^{2}q
,

\alpha_{n}=\frac{(x^{2}q)_{n}(1-x^{2}q^{2n+1})(-1)^{n}x^{2n}q^{n(3n+1)/2}}{(q)_{n}(1-x^{2}q)}

(2.6)

and

\beta_{n}=\frac{1}{(q)_{n}}.

(2.7)

Using this in Lemma
2.1
we obtain

(1-x)\sum_{n\geq 0}(xq)_{n}x^{n}=\sum_{n\geq 0}(-1)^{n}x^{3n}q^{n(3n+1)/2}(1-x^{2}q^{2n+1}).

Note that for
|q|<1
, the right-hand side converges for all
x\in\mathbb{C}
while the left-hand side requires
|x|<1
. To remedy this, we follow Zagier and add and subtract
(x)_{\infty}
on the left-hand side to obtain

(1-x)\sum_{n\geq 0}\left((xq)_{n}-(xq)_{\infty}\right)x^{n}+(xq)_{\infty}=\sum_{n\geq 0}(-1)^{n}x^{3n}q^{n(3n+1)/2}(1-x^{2}q^{2n+1}).

Now replace
x
by
x^{2}
on both sides, multiply by
x
, and take
\frac{d}{dx}|_{x=1}
. The result is the “sum of tails” identity,

2\sum_{n\geq 0}\left((q)_{n}-(q)_{\infty}\right)+(q)_{\infty}\left(-1+2\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{q^{n}}{1-q^{n}}\right)=-\sum_{n\geq 1}n\left(\frac{12}{n}\right)q^{(n^{2}-1)/24}.

Letting
q
approach a root of unity, we obtain Zagier’s strange identity, since
(q)_{\infty}
vanishes to infinite order.
∎

3.
More on Bailey pairs

Our goal in this section is to prove Lemma

Lemma 4.3

We have

\sum_{n\geq 0}x^{n}\begin{bmatrix}n\\

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}x^{n+k}\begin{bmatrix}n+k\\

\displaystyle=\frac{x^{k}}{(x)_{k+1}},

by

4.4
).

For (

), the case

is clear. If

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}x^{n}\begin{bmatrix}n+1\\

k\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=x^{-1}\sum_{n\geq 1}x^{n}\begin{bmatrix}n\\
k\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=x^{-1}\sum_{n\geq 0}x^{n}\begin{bmatrix}n\\

6.
Conclusion

We conclude with a few remarks. First comparing (

) gives a “quantum

-series identity” in the sense of
[

]
,

\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1},\dots,n_{2k-1}\geq 0}

\displaystyle(q)_{n_{2k-1}}\frac{q^{n_{1}^{2}+\cdots+n_{2k-2}^{2}+n_{1}+\cdots+n_{2k-2}}}{(-q)_{n_{1}}}\prod_{i=1}^{2k-2}\begin{bmatrix}n_{i+1}\\

n_{i}\end{bmatrix}_{q^{2}}.

The symbol

means that the two sides are equal at any odd root of unity (in which case the sums become finite) but not as functions inside the unit disk (where neither series converges, anyway). The case
k=1
reads

\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{(q)_{n}}{(-q)_{n}}=_{q}2\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{(q)_{2n}}{(-q)_{2n+1}}.

Second, it would be nice to have strange identities for other classes of partial theta series of the form

\sum_{n\geq 0}n^{\nu}\chi(n)q^{\frac{n^{2}-a}{b}},

where
\nu\in\{0,1\}
,

and

are integers, and

is an appropriate periodic function. One especially interesting class would be

\sum_{n\geq 0}n\chi_{2st}(n)q^{\frac{n^{2}-(st-s-t)^{2}}{4st}},

where
\chi_{2st}(n)
is the even periodic function modulo
2st
defined by

\chi_{2st}(n)=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if $n\equiv st-s-t$ or $st+s+t\pmod{2st}$},\\
-1,&\text{if $n\equiv st-s+t$ or $st+s-t\pmod{2st}$},\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

As explained in
[
16
,
17
]
, this series captures the values of the colored Jones polynomial for the torus knot

at roots of unity. The case
T_{2,2k+1}
corresponds to Hikami’s strange identities (
1.5
) when
a=0
. The case of torus knots

is studied in
[
9
]
, though the strange identities found there do not appear to be simple consequences of the Bailey pair framework presented here.

Finally, as is often the case when identities are understood in the context of Bailey pairs, we have only scratched the surface of what is possible. For instance, in establishing families of Bailey pairs like those in (
4.1
) and (
4.2
), we have iterated the Bailey Lemma in (

) and (
2.2
) only in the special case

. This typically leads to the most elegant results, but there are other possibilities. For example, if we follow the proof of (
4.1
) and (
4.2
) but use

,

n_{i}\end{bmatrix}

Abstract
. We study the minimal homogeneous generating sets of the Eulerian ideal associated with a simple graph and its maximal generating degree. We show that the Eulerian ideal is a lattice ideal and use this to give a characterization of binomials belonging to a minimal homogeneous generating set. In this way, we obtain an explicit minimal homogeneous generating set. We find an upper bound for the maximal generating degree in terms of the graph. This invariant is half the number of edges of a largest Eulerian subgraph of even cardinality without even-chords. We show that for bipartite graphs this invariant is the maximal generating degree. In particular, we prove that if the graph is bipartite, the Eulerian ideal is generated in degree 2 2 if and only if the graph is chordal. Furthermore, we show that the maximal generating degree is also 2 2 when the graph is a complete graph.



1.
Introduction

Currently, one of the most popular research areas in commutative algebra is the
study of properties of ideals, of the ring of polynomials over a field, associated with graphs.
This originated with the famous paper
[
14
]
and has
motivated the study of several other ideals associated with graphs.
Among these, the Eulerian ideal is a recent addition and the focus of this paper.
It was defined in
[
8
]
, by the first author, together with Vaz Pinto and Villarreal,
inspired by the vanishing ideal parameterized by a graph,
introduced in
[
12
]
. The Eulerian ideal is a binomial ideal of a polynomial
ring whose variables are indexed by the edges of a simple graph.
It is known that this ideal has a generating set that contains a set
of binomials canonically associated with the Eulerian subgraphs of the graph —
see Subsection
2.1
— and that, moreover, this ideal contains
the toric ideal associated with the graph.

Several properties of Eulerian ideals have been studied.
In
[
8
]
, the quotient of the polynomial ring by it was
shown to be a Cohen-Macaulay graded ring of dimension
1
; in
[
7
]
, a Gröbner basis for the ideal was given and
a combinatorial characterization of its Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity and degree was derived; in
[
10
]
, the ideal and the above results were generalized for
k
-uniform hypergraphs
and the Hilbert function of the quotient was characterized by
a combinatorial formula and computed in some cases; finally, in
[
9
]
,
a monomial basis of the socle of the quotient and a description of the socle degrees was found.

Through all of these results the graph invariant that appears in common, and that translates the properties of the Eulerian ideal, is the notion of
T
-join. Given a subset,
T
, of vertices of the graph,
a
T
-join is a subset of edges,
J
, such that, in the subgraph with edge set
J
,
T
is the set of odd degree vertices. We will focus on
T
-joins with cardinality of a fixed parity. For more on
T
-joins, see
[
6
]
and Subsection
2.2
, below.

This work has two main goals; to characterize minimal binomial generating sets of the Eulerian ideal and to study its
maximal generating degree. In the case of the toric ideal of a graph, an explicit description of the minimal generating sets, in terms of the closed even walks of the graph, was obtained in
[
13
, Theorem 4.13]
. For the Eulerian ideal,
we have found that a minimal generating set consists of binomials that identify
T
-joins that are not equivalent under a certain equivalence relation — see
Definition
3.2
and
Theorem
3.3
.
The equivalence relation is an adaptation of the notion of Markov basis, as introduced by Diaconis and Sturmfels in
[
3
]
and used in
[
2
, Theorem 4.12]
to obtain a description of all minimal generating sets of binomials
of a lattice ideal. Since, as we show in Proposition
2.7
,
the Eulerian ideal is a lattice ideal, in Theorem
3.5
,
we use this result to obtain an explicit minimal generating set for the Eulerian ideal.

Regarding the maximal generating degree, we start by showing that this invariant
is
2
for complete graphs — see Theorem
4.5
.
Then, in Theorem
4.6
, by showing that the binomials associated with
even cycles with an even-chord or with edge-disjoint unions of two odd cycles with at most
one vertex in common that possess an even-chord are not needed in a minimal generating set of the Eulerian ideal, we
obtain an upper bound for the maximal generating degree.
This result is analogous to the results
[
11
, Lemmas 3.1, 3.2 and Theorem 1.2]
for the toric ideal.
As an application, in Corollary
4.7
, we show that if the graph is bipartite, then
the Eulerian ideal is generated in degree
2
if and only if the graph is chordal.

This paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
, we define the ideal, the graph theoretical notions that we need, exhibit the lattice associated with the Eulerian ideal and recall two background results: the characterization of its binomials and the description of a Gröbner basis.
In Section
3
, we characterize when a binomial in the
Eulerian ideal is in the ideal generated by binomials of lower degree
and we describe a minimal generating set.
In Section
4
, we give an upper bound for the maximal generating degree of the Eulerian ideal and show that this invariant is
2
for bipartite chordal graphs and complete graphs.

Preliminaries

Let
G=(V_{G},E_{G})

V_{G}=\{1,\dots,n\}
and that

s=|E_{G}|>0
. Let

consider the polynomial rings
\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
and

\mathbb{K}[E_{G}]=\mathbb{K}[t_{e}:e\in E_{G}]
. Throughout, we use the multi-index notation for monomials in a polynomial
ring, so that, for example, if
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{E_{G}}
,

\textstyle\mathbf{t}^{\alpha}=\prod\limits_{e\in E_{G}}t_{e}^{\alpha(e)}.

Also, if
J\subseteq E_{G}
, we denote by
\mathbf{t}_{J}
the product of all
variables

, with

.

2.1.
The ideal

Let
\varphi
be the ring homomorphism
\mathbb{K}[E_{G}]\to\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
that maps
t_{e}\mapsto x_{i}x_{j}
, for every
e=\{i,j\}
in
E_{G}
.

Definition 2.1
.

The Eulerian ideal of
G
is the ideal of
\mathbb{K}[E_{G}]
defined by

I(G)=\varphi^{-1}(x_{i}^{2}-x_{j}^{2}:i,j\in V_{G}).

Given two edges of
G
,
e=\{i,j\}
and
\ell=\{u,v\}
, it is easy to see that the binomial

t_{e}^{2}-t_{\ell}^{2}
is in
I(G)
,

\textstyle\varphi(t_{e}^{2}-t_{\ell}^{2})=x_{i}^{2}x_{j}^{2}-x_{u}^{2}x_{v}^{2}=x_{i}^{2}(x_{j}^{2}-x_{u}^{2})+x_{u}^{2}(x_{i}^{2}-x_{v}^{2}).

Therefore the Eulerian ideal is trivial if and only
G
has only one edge.
From now on let us assume that
|E_{G}|\geq 2
.

To describe a generating set of
I(G)
, let us fix some terminology.
For a set of edges
J\subseteq E_{G}
and a vertex
i\in V_{G}
, we define the degree
of
i
in
J
as

\textstyle\deg_{J}(i)=\sum\limits_{e\in J}|e\cap\{i\}|.

We say that a subset of edges
C\subseteq E_{G}
is Eulerian if
\deg_{C}(i)
is even, for every
i\in V_{G}
.
Given an Eulerian subset
C
of even cardinality and a partition
C=J\operatorname{\mathbin{\dot{\cup}}}K
into
sets of same cardinality, we form a homogeneous binomial in
\mathbb{K}[E_{G}]
,

\textstyle\mathbf{t}_{J}-\mathbf{t}_{K}.

Binomials obtained in this way are called Eulerian binomials associated with
C
.
Let us denote by
\mathscr{E}
the set of all Eulerian binomials, associated with
all such
C
and choices of partitions. We recall from
[
7
, Theorem 3.3]
the following result.

Theorem 2.2
.

The set

\{t_{e}^{2}-t_{\ell}^{2}:e,\ell\in E_{G}\}\cup\mathscr{E}

is a Gröbner basis for
I(G)
, with respect to
the graded reverse lexicographic order on
\mathbb{K}[E_{G}]
, associated with any choice
of ordering of
E_{G}
.

2.2.
Joins

Given
T\subseteq V_{G}
, a
T
-join is a subset of edges,
J\subseteq E_{G}
, such that

T=\{v\in V_{G}:\deg_{J}(v)\text{ is odd}\}.

If

, we say that

is a
(T,p)
-join whenever
|J|+2\mathbb{Z}=p
.
Throughout, we abbreviate
0+2\mathbb{Z}
and

by

It is clear that, given any subset of edges

, there is a unique pair

(T,p)
such that

is a
(T,p)
-join. For example, an Eulerian subset of even cardinality
is an
(\emptyset,0)
-join. However, given a pair
(T,p)
, there may or may not exist a
(T,p)
-join.
For one, by
[
6
, Proposition 12.7]
, if a
(T,p)
-join exists,
then the intersection of

with the vertex set of each connected component of the graph must have even cardinality.

Let us denote by

2.3.
The lattice property

Fix
e_{1},\dots,e_{s}
an ordering of the edge set
E_{G}
.
The incidence matrix
of
G
is the
n\times s
matrix
B
, where each entry
B_{ij}
equals
1
if the vertex
i
is in the edge
e_{j}
, and
0
otherwise.
To identify the lattice associated with
I(G)
, let us use
the induced identification of
\mathbb{N}^{E_{G}}
with
\mathbb{N}^{s}
.

Proposition 2.7
.

I(G)
is the lattice ideal associated with the lattice

\textstyle\mathscr{L}=\{\theta\in\mathbb{Z}^{s}:B\theta\in(2\mathbb{Z})^{n}\text{ and }\sum_{e\in E_{G}}\theta(e)=0\}.

Proof.

Recall that, given
\mathscr{L}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{s}
a lattice,
the lattice ideal associated with
\mathscr{L}
, which we denote by

I_{\mathscr{L}}
, is the ideal generated by

\mathbf{t}^{\alpha}-\mathbf{t}^{\beta}
, where
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{s}
and
\alpha-\beta\in\mathscr{L}
.
Given
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{E_{G}}\cong\mathbb{N}^{s}
, let

\mu,\nu\in\mathbb{N}^{E_{G}}
and
J,K\subseteq E_{G}
be such that

\mathbf{t}^{\alpha}=\mathbf{t}^{2\mu}\mathbf{t}_{J}
and
\mathbf{t}^{\beta}=\mathbf{t}^{2\nu}\mathbf{t}_{K}
.
By Proposition
2.4
, the Eulerian ideal is generated by the
set of binomials
\mathbf{t}^{\alpha}-\mathbf{t}^{\beta}
such that

(1)

\textstyle\deg(\mathbf{t}^{\alpha})=\deg(\mathbf{t}^{\beta})\iff\sum\limits_{e\in E_{G}}\bigl(\alpha(e)-\beta(e)\bigr)=0

and such that
J
and
K
are
(T,p)
-joins, for some
(T,p)\in\mathscr{T}_{G}
.
Let us show that this set coincides with the generating set of
I_{\mathscr{L}}
.
We have

\textstyle(B\alpha)_{i}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{s}B_{ij}\cdot\alpha(e_{j})=\sum\limits_{j:\ i\in e_{j}}\alpha(e_{j})=\sum\limits_{j:\ i\in e_{j}}|\{e_{j}\}\cap J|+2\mu(e_{j}).

Since, for each
i\in V_{G}
, the sum
\sum_{j:\ i\in e_{j}}|\{e_{j}\}\cap J|
counts
the number of edges in
J
that contain
i
, which is equal to
\deg_{J}(i)
, we get

(B\alpha)_{i}\equiv_{2}\deg_{J}(i)
. Hence

(2)

\textstyle(B(\alpha-\beta))_{i}=(B\alpha)_{i}-(B\beta)_{i}\equiv_{2}\deg_{J}(i)-\deg_{K}(i).

Now, if
\mathbf{t}^{\alpha}=\mathbf{t}^{2\mu}\mathbf{t}_{J}
and
\mathbf{t}^{\beta}=\mathbf{t}^{2\nu}\mathbf{t}_{K}
are such that
(
1
) is satisfied and
J
and
K
are
(T,p)
-joins then,
by (
2
),
\bigl(B(\alpha-\beta)\bigr)_{i}
is even,
for every
i\in V_{G}
, and hence
\alpha-\beta\in\mathscr{L}
. Conversely, if
\alpha-\beta\in\mathscr{L}

then, if
J
and
K
are obtained as above, by (
1
) they
have cardinalities of the same parity and moreover, using (
2
), we deduce that
there exists
(T,p)\in\mathscr{T}_{G}
such that both
J
and
K
are
(T,p)
-joins.
∎

Thus
\operatorname{d}(I(G))\leq d.

Now, if
G

.
Set

.
Let

Then

Take the binomial

.
If
\operatorname{d}(I(G))<d,
then
\mathbf{t}_{J}-\mathbf{t}_{K}
is a
\mathbb{K}[E_{G}]
-linear combination of binomials of lower degree.
It follows that there is an even cycle

and a binomial
\mathbf{t}_{A}-\mathbf{t}_{D\setminus A}\in I(G)

.
Then, since no edges of

Therefore, as

C
and every edge in

C

We conclude that
\operatorname{d}(I(G))=d.

∎

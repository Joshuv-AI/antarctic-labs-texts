Analysis of the gradient for the stochastic fractional heat equation with spatially-colored noise in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d}
Ran Wang

Abstract




Abstract:
Consider the stochastic partial differential equation

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}u_{t}(\boldsymbol{x})=-(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}u_{t}(\boldsymbol{x})+b\left(u_{t}(\boldsymbol{x})\right)+\sigma\left(u_{t}(\boldsymbol{x})\right)\dot{F}(t,\boldsymbol{x}),\ \ \ t\geq 0,\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

where
-(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}
denotes the fractional Laplacian with the power
\alpha/2\in(1/2,1]
, and the driving noise
\dot{F}
is a centered Gaussian random field which is white in time and with a spatial homogeneous covariance given by the Riesz kernel. We study the detailed behavior of the approximation spatial gradient
u_{t}(\boldsymbol{x})-u_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}-{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{e})
at any fixed time
t>0
, as
{\varepsilon}\downarrow 0
, where
\boldsymbol{e}
is a unit vector in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. As applications, we deduce the law of iterated logarithm and the behavior of the
q
-variations of the solution in space.

Keyword:
Stochastic heat equation; Fractional Brownian motion; Fractional Laplacian; Gradient estimates.

MSC:
60H15; 60G17.

1.
Introduction

Consider the stochastic fractional heat equation

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}u_{t}(\boldsymbol{x})=-(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}u_{t}(\boldsymbol{x})+b(u_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}))+\sigma(u_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}))\dot{F}(t,\boldsymbol{x}),\ \ \ t\geq 0,\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

(1.1)

where
b
and
\sigma
are assumed to be Lipschitz continuous functions,
\alpha\in(1,2]
is a fixed “spatial scaling” parameter,
-(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}
denotes the fractional Laplacian with the power
\alpha/2
, and the driving noise
\dot{F}
is a centered Gaussian field which is white in time and with a spatial homogeneous covariance given by the Riesz kernel
f(x)=c_{1,1}\|x\|^{-(d-\gamma)}
of the form:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\dot{F}(t,\boldsymbol{x})\dot{F}(s,\boldsymbol{y})\right]=c_{1,1}\delta_{0}(t-s)\|\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{y}\|^{-(d-\gamma)},\ \ 0<\gamma<d,

(1.2)

where
\delta_{0}
denotes the Dirac delta function and

\displaystyle c_{1,1}=2^{d-\gamma}\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}\Gamma\left((d-\gamma)/2\right)/\Gamma\left(\gamma/2\right).

(1.3)

Throughout this paper, we assume that

Condition 1.1
.

(a)

\alpha\in(1,2]
,
d\geq 1
and
\gamma\in((d-\alpha)_{+},d)
.

(b)

Let
u_{0}=\{u_{0}(\boldsymbol{x})\}_{\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}
be a random field. Assume that there exist real numbers
k_{0}>\max\{2,\,2/(2+d-\alpha-\gamma)\}
,
\eta_{0}\in(\frac{\alpha-d+\gamma}{\alpha},1]
and
c_{1,2}>0
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{E}\left[|u_{0}(\boldsymbol{x})|^{k_{0}}\right]<\infty

(1.4)

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[|u_{0}(\boldsymbol{x})-u_{0}(\boldsymbol{x}+\boldsymbol{h})|^{k_{0}}\right]\leq c_{1,2}\|\boldsymbol{h}\|^{\eta_{0}k_{0}},

(1.5)

uniformly for all
\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{h}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

(c)

The functions
b
and
\sigma:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
are Lipschitz continuous, that is,

\displaystyle|b(r)-b(v)|\leq L_{b}|r-v|,\,\,\,|\sigma(r)-\sigma(v)|\leq L_{\sigma}|r-v|\ \ \ \text{for }r,v\in\mathbb{R},

(1.6)

where
L_{b}
and
L_{\sigma}
are some finite positive constants.

Under Condition
1.1
, by the theory of Dalang
[
6
]
, there exists a unique continuous solution of (
1.1
), satisfying that for any
T>0,k\in[2,k_{0}]
,

\displaystyle\sup_{(t,\boldsymbol{x})\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{E}\left[|u_{t}(\boldsymbol{x})|^{k}\right]<+\infty.

(1.7)

See, for instance,
[
2
]
and references therein for details.

When
d=1
and
F
is the space-time white noise, Foondun et al.
[
12
]
utilized an approximation approximation approach to study the local and variational properties of the spatial processes
\{u_{t}(\boldsymbol{x})\}_{\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}}
, where
t>0
is fixed. Their key idea in
[
12
]
is to show that, as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
,

u_{t}(\boldsymbol{x})-u_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}-{\varepsilon})\approx c_{1,3}\sigma(u_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}))\left[B^{H_{0}}(\boldsymbol{x})-B^{H_{0}}(\boldsymbol{x}-{\varepsilon})\right]\ \ \ \text{in certain sense},

where
c_{1,3}=(2\Gamma(\alpha)|\cos(\alpha\pi/2)|)^{-1/2}
, and
B^{H_{0}}
denotes a fractional Brownian motion (fBm, for short) with Hurst index

H_{0}=(\alpha-1)/2\in(0,1/2]
. They were able to quantify the size of the approximation error by controlling its moments. Consequently, some of the local properties of

\boldsymbol{x}\mapsto u_{t}(\boldsymbol{x})
can be derived from those of fBm
B^{H_{0}}
.

In this paper, we consider the stochastic fractional heat equation (1.1) with the time-white and space-colored Gaussian noise and extend the approximation approach in
[
12
]
to the more general setting. In this case, the solution will be related to the isotropic multiparameter fractional Brownian motion (also known as the Lévy fBm)
\{B^{H}(\boldsymbol{x})\}_{\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}
, which is defined as a centered Gaussian process, starting from zero, with covariance function

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[B^{H}(\boldsymbol{x})B^{H}(\boldsymbol{y})\right]=\frac{1}{2}\left(\|\boldsymbol{x}\|^{2H}+\|\boldsymbol{y}\|^{2H}-\|\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{y}\|^{2H}\right)\ \ \ \ (\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}).

(1.8)

Here, the Hurst index is
H=(\alpha-d+\gamma)/{2}\in(0,1)
, which will be used throughout the whole of this paper.

Our main result is the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

Under Condition
1.1
,
for every fixed
t>0
, there exists an isotropic multiparameter fBm
\{B^{H}(\boldsymbol{x})\}_{\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}
, with Hurst index
H=(\alpha-d+\gamma)/2
such that for all
\lambda>0
,

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\sup_{\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sup_{\|\boldsymbol{e}\|=1}\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\frac{u_{t}(\boldsymbol{x})-u_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}-\varepsilon\boldsymbol{e})}{B^{H}(\boldsymbol{x})-B^{H}(\boldsymbol{x}-\varepsilon\boldsymbol{e})}-c_{\alpha,\gamma,d}\sigma(u_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}))\right|>\lambda\right)=0,

(1.9)

where
c_{\alpha,\gamma,d}
is the following numerical constant:

\displaystyle c_{\alpha,\gamma,d}=(2\pi)^{-d/2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\|\boldsymbol{w}\|^{-(\alpha+\gamma)}\big(1-\cos(\boldsymbol{w}\cdot\boldsymbol{e})\big)d\boldsymbol{w}\right)^{1/2},

(1.10)

with
\boldsymbol{e}
being a unit vector in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

By using the arguments in the proofs of Corollaries 1.2, 1.3 and 1.5 in
[
12
]
, we can obtain the following results.

Corollary 1.3
.

Assume that Condition
1.1
holds.
Choose and fix
t>0
,
\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and a unit vector
\boldsymbol{e}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then with probability one,

\begin{split}\limsup_{{\varepsilon}\downarrow 0}\frac{u_{t}(\boldsymbol{x})-u_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}-{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{e})}{{\varepsilon}^{H}\sqrt{2\log\log(1/{\varepsilon})}}=c_{\alpha,\gamma,d}|\sigma(u_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}))|.\end{split}

(1.11)

.

Assume that Condition
1.1
holds.
Choose and fix
t>0
,
\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and a unit vector
\boldsymbol{e}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

\lim_{{\varepsilon}\downarrow 0}\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{u_{t}(\boldsymbol{x})-u_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}-{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{e})}{{\varepsilon}^{H}}\leq\theta\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(\sigma(u_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}))\times\mathcal{N}\leq\frac{\theta}{c_{\alpha,\gamma,d}}\right),

.

(1.13)

where

See, e.g.,
[

.

For any

.

Assume that Condition
1.1

\varphi:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is Lipschitz continuous, then for all non random reals
A_{2}>A_{1}
and

,

\begin{split}&\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{A_{1}2^{n}\leq j\leq A_{2}2^{n}}\varphi(u_{t}({\boldsymbol{x}}_{j/2^{n}}))\left|u_{t}({\boldsymbol{x}}_{(j+1)/2^{n}})-u_{t}({\boldsymbol{x}}_{j/2^{n}})\right|^{1/H}\\

(1.14)

(1.15)

at any fixed

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Stochastic integral

We first define precisely the driving noise that appears in (
1.1
), which is borrowed from
[
8
]
.
Let
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be the space of
C^{\infty}
-test functions with compact support. Then
F=\{F(\phi),\,\phi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})\}
is an
L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
-valued mean zero Gaussian process with covariance

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[F(\phi)F(\psi)\right]=c_{1,1}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}dr\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}d\boldsymbol{y}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}d\boldsymbol{z}\,\phi(r,\boldsymbol{y})\|\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{z}\|^{-(d-\gamma)}\psi(r,\boldsymbol{z}).

(2.1)

Using elementary properties of the Fourier transform (see
[
6
]
), this covariance can also be written as

\mathbb{E}\left[F(\phi)F(\psi)\right]=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}dr\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}d\xi\,\|\xi\|^{-\gamma}\mathcal{F}\phi(r,\cdot)(\xi)\overline{\mathcal{F}\psi(r,\cdot)}(\xi),

(2.2)

where
\mathcal{F}f(\cdot)(\xi)
denotes the Fourier transform of
f
, that is,

\mathcal{F}f(\cdot)(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{2\pi i\xi\cdot\boldsymbol{x}}f(\boldsymbol{x})d\boldsymbol{x}.

Following Walsh
[
21
]
and Dalang
[
6
]
, a rigorous formulation of (
1.1
) through the notion of mild solution as follows. Let
M=\{M_{t}(A),\,t\geq 0,A\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\}
be the worthy martingale measure obtained as an extension of the process
\dot{F}
as in Dalang and Frangos
[
7
]
(also see Dalang and Quer-Sardanyons
[
9
]
). Then a mild solution of (
1.1
) is jointly measurable
\mathbb{R}
-valued process
u=\{u(t,\boldsymbol{x})\}_{t\geq 0,\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}
, adapted to the natural filtration generated by
M
, such that

\begin{split}u_{t}(\boldsymbol{x})=&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G^{\alpha}_{t}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})u_{0}(\boldsymbol{y})d\boldsymbol{y}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G^{\alpha}_{t-s}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})b(u_{s}(\boldsymbol{y}))dsd\boldsymbol{y}\\
&\,+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G^{\alpha}_{t-s}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})\sigma(u_{s}(\boldsymbol{y}))M(ds,d\boldsymbol{y}),\end{split}

(2.3)

where
G^{\alpha}_{t}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}):=G^{\alpha}_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{y})
is the Green kernel associated to the operator
-(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, which is defined via its Fourier transform

\displaystyle(\mathcal{F}G^{\alpha}_{t})(\cdot)(\xi)=e^{-t\|\xi\|^{\alpha}},\ \ \ \xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

(2.4)

for
\alpha\in(1,2]
, and the stochastic integral is interpreted in the sense of Walsh
[
21
]
.
We note that the covariance measure
M
is

Q([0,t]\times A\times B):=\langle M(A),M(B)\rangle_{t}=t\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}d\boldsymbol{x}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}d\boldsymbol{y}\,1_{A}(\boldsymbol{x})\|\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{y}\|^{-(d-\gamma)}1_{B}(\boldsymbol{y})

and its dominating measure
K\equiv Q
. In particular, by (
2.1
), (
2.2
) and (
2.4
), we have

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G^{\alpha}_{t-s}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})M(ds,d\boldsymbol{y})\right)^{2}\right]\\
=&\,c_{1,1}\int_{0}^{t}ds\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}d\boldsymbol{y}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}d\boldsymbol{z}\,G^{\alpha}_{t-s}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})\|\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{z}\|^{-(d-\gamma)}G^{\alpha}_{t-s}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{z})\\
=&\,\int_{0}^{t}ds\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}d\xi\,\|\xi\|^{-\gamma}\,|\mathcal{F}G^{\alpha}_{t-s}(\cdot)(\xi)|^{2}\\
=&\,\int_{0}^{t}ds\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}d\xi\,\|\xi\|^{-\gamma}\,e^{-2(t-s)\|\xi\|^{\alpha}}\\
=&\,c_{2,1}\int_{0}^{t}s^{-(d-\gamma)/\alpha}ds,\end{split}

(2.5)

where
c_{2,1}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\|\xi\|^{-\gamma}\,e^{-2\|\xi\|^{\alpha}}d\xi<\infty
. The integral
ds
in the last term of (
2.5
) is finite if and only if
d-\gamma<\alpha
.

For any random variable
\zeta\in L^{p}(\Omega)
with
p\geq 1
, let
\|\zeta\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}:=\left(\mathbb{E}|\zeta|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}
.
For any
\phi,\psi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, let

\begin{split}\langle\phi,\psi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}:=&\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}d\xi\,\|\xi\|^{-\gamma}\mathcal{F}\phi(\cdot)(\xi)\overline{\mathcal{F}\psi(\cdot)}(\xi)\\
=&\,c_{1,1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}d\boldsymbol{y}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}d\boldsymbol{z}\,\phi(\boldsymbol{y})\|\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{z}\|^{-(d-\gamma)}\psi(\boldsymbol{z}).\end{split}

(2.6)

Denote by
\mathcal{H}
the Hilbert space obtained by the completion of
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with respect to the inner product
\langle\phi,\,\psi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}
defined by (
2.6
).

According to the proof of Lemma 5.4 in
[

]

(2.7)

2.2.
The fractional Green kernel

Let us recall some useful properties of the kernel
G^{\alpha}_{t}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})=G^{\alpha}_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{y})
defined through (
2.4
). For details, we refer to
[
2
,
5
]
.

It is well known that
G^{\alpha}_{t}(\cdot)
is the probability transition density function of a rotationally invariant
d
-dimensional stable process
\{L_{t}^{(\alpha)}\}_{t\geq 0}
. By the scaling property of
L_{t}^{(\alpha)}\overset{d}{=}t^{1/\alpha}L_{1}^{(\alpha)}
, it is easy to see that

G^{\alpha}_{t}(\boldsymbol{x})=t^{-\frac{d}{\alpha}}G^{\alpha}_{1}\left(t^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}\boldsymbol{x}\right)\ \ \ \ (t>0,\,\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}).

(2.8)

When
\alpha=2
,
L_{t}^{(\alpha)}
is the standard Brownian motion, and

G^{\alpha}_{t}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})=\frac{1}{(2\pi t)^{d/2}}\exp\left\{-\frac{\|\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{y}\|^{2}}{2t}\right\}.

For simplicity of presentation, we assume that
\alpha\in(1,2)
throughout the rest of this paper. The proof in the case of
\alpha=2
is relatively simple, which is omitted here.

When
\alpha\in(1,2)
, by
[
4
, Theorem 2.1]
, there exist some finite constants
0<K_{\alpha}^{\prime}<K_{\alpha}<\infty
such that for all
t>0,\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

\displaystyle K_{\alpha}^{\prime}\frac{t}{\left(t^{1/\alpha}+\|\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{y}\|\right)^{d+\alpha}}\leq G^{\alpha}_{t}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})\leq K_{\alpha}\frac{t}{\left(t^{1/\alpha}+\|\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{y}\|\right)^{d+\alpha}}.

(2.9)

By
[
5
, Lemma 2.2]
, one has that for every
T>0
, there exists a constant
c_{2,2}>0
such that for all
0<t\leq T
and
\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y},\boldsymbol{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\left|G^{\alpha}_{t}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})-G^{\alpha}_{t}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{z})\right|\leq c_{2,2}\left(\frac{\|\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{z}\|}{t^{1/\alpha}}\wedge 1\right)\times\big(G^{\alpha}_{t}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})+G^{\alpha}_{t}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{z})\big).

(2.10)

2.3.
Linearization of the stochastic heat equation

Let us consider the following linearization of the stochastic heat equation:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}Z_{t}(\boldsymbol{x})=-(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}Z_{t}(\boldsymbol{x})+\dot{F}(t,\boldsymbol{x}),

subject to

\sup_{t\in[0,T],\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{E}\left[|Z_{t}(\boldsymbol{x})|^{p}\right]<+\infty.

3.

For any
{\varepsilon}>0
and any unit vector
\boldsymbol{e}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, define

\displaystyle(\nabla_{{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{e}}f)(\boldsymbol{x}):=f(\boldsymbol{x})-f(\boldsymbol{x}-{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{e})\ \ \ \ \ (\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}),

(3.1)

as a substitute for the approximate spatial gradient of the function

Let us recall the solution
Z
to Eq. (

Consider the approximate gradient operator

\displaystyle(\nabla_{{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{e}}Z_{t})(\boldsymbol{x}):=Z_{t}(\boldsymbol{x})-Z_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}-{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{e})=\int_{(0,t)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\nabla_{{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{e}}G^{\alpha}_{t-s}\right)(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})M(ds,d\boldsymbol{y}).

(3.2)

Throughout, let us choose and fix parameters

\displaystyle\beta>1\ \ \ \ \text{and }\ \ \ \ \delta:=1+\beta^{1+\frac{1}{H}}.

(3.3)

Then, we define a family of space-time boxes as follows: For every
t\geq 0,\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\varepsilon>0

\displaystyle\mathbf{B}_{\beta}(\boldsymbol{x},t;{\varepsilon})=[t-\beta{\varepsilon}^{\alpha},t]\times\mathbf{B}(\boldsymbol{x};{\varepsilon}\delta),

(3.4)

where
\mathbf{B}(\boldsymbol{x};{\varepsilon}\delta)=\{\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\,\|\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{y}\|\leq{\varepsilon}\delta\}
.

The following is a generalization of Proposition 4.1 in
[
12
]
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Choose and fix
T>0
. There exists a positive constant
c_{3,1}
such that

\left\|(\nabla_{{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{e}}Z_{t})(\boldsymbol{x})-\int_{\mathbf{B}_{\beta}(\boldsymbol{x},t;{\varepsilon})}\left(\nabla_{{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{e}}G^{\alpha}_{t-s}\right)(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})M(ds,d\boldsymbol{y})\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq c_{3,1}{\varepsilon}^{H}\beta^{-\frac{d+2-\alpha-\gamma}{2\alpha}},

simultaneously for all
\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
t\in[0,T]
,
{\varepsilon}\in(0,1)
,
\beta>1
and the unit vector
\boldsymbol{e}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Proof.

By the stationary of
\{Z_{t}(\boldsymbol{x})\}_{\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}
and the independence of
\sigma\{M(s,\boldsymbol{x});s\in[0,t],\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\}
and
\sigma\{M(s,\boldsymbol{x})-M(t,\boldsymbol{x});s\geq t,\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\}

\begin{split}Q:=&\,\mathbb{E}\left[\left|(\nabla_{{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{e}}Z_{t})(\boldsymbol{x})-\int_{\mathbf{B}_{\beta}(\boldsymbol{x},t;{\varepsilon})}\left(\nabla_{{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{e}}G^{\alpha}_{t-s}\right)(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})M(ds,d\boldsymbol{y})\right|^{2}\right]\\

Because
\mathcal{F}\left(\nabla_{{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{e}}G^{\alpha}_{t-s}\right)(\cdot)(\xi)=e^{-(t-s)\|\xi\|^{\alpha}}\left(1-e^{-2\pi{\varepsilon}i\xi\cdot\boldsymbol{e}}\right)
, (
2.5
) implies that

\begin{split}Q_{1}=&\,\int_{\beta{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}}^{t}ds\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}d\xi\,e^{-2s\|\xi\|^{\alpha}}\left[1-\cos\left(2\pi{\varepsilon}\xi\cdot\boldsymbol{e}\right)\right]\|\xi\|^{-\gamma}\\

=&\,\pi^{2}{\varepsilon}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-2\beta{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}\|\xi\|^{\alpha}}\|\xi\|^{2-\alpha-\gamma}d\xi\\
=&\ \pi^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\|\xi\|^{\alpha}}\|\xi\|^{2-\alpha-\gamma}d\xi\cdot(2\beta)^{-\frac{d+2-\alpha-\gamma}{\alpha}}{\varepsilon}^{\alpha-d+\gamma},\end{split}

(3.6)

where
1-\cos(a)\leq a^{2}/2
(
a\in\mathbb{R}
) has been used in the second step.

Let us estimate
Q_{2}
with a litter more effort. First, by the Hölder inequality, we have that for any
p>\max\left\{\frac{2\alpha}{\alpha-d+\gamma},\frac{d}{\gamma}\right\}

\begin{split}Q_{2}=&\,\int_{0}^{\beta{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}}ds\int_{\|\boldsymbol{y}\|>{\varepsilon}\delta}d\boldsymbol{y}\int_{\|\boldsymbol{z}\|>{\varepsilon}\delta}d\boldsymbol{z}\left(\nabla_{{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{e}}G^{\alpha}_{s}\right)(\boldsymbol{y})\|\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{z}\|^{-d+\gamma}\left(\nabla_{{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{e}}G^{\alpha}_{s}\right)(\boldsymbol{z})\\

=:&\,Q_{2,1}\cdot Q_{2,2}.\end{split}

(3.7)

Next, we use the argument in the proof of (4.15) in
[
12
]
to estimate

.
Notice that

\begin{split}\int_{\|\boldsymbol{y}\|>{\varepsilon}\delta}\left|\left(\nabla_{{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{e}}G^{\alpha}_{s}\right)(\boldsymbol{y})\right|d\boldsymbol{y}\leq\,\int_{\|\boldsymbol{y}\|>{\varepsilon}\delta}\left|G^{\alpha}_{s}(\boldsymbol{y})\right|d\boldsymbol{y}+\int_{\|\boldsymbol{y}\|>{\varepsilon}\delta}\left|G^{\alpha}_{s}(\boldsymbol{y}-{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{e})\right|d\boldsymbol{y}.\end{split}

(3.8)

Let

\alpha

If

(3.9)

Since

[see (

)], by the triangle inequality and the scaling of

\begin{split}\sup_{0\leq\eta\leq 1}\int_{\|\boldsymbol{y}\|>{\varepsilon}\delta}\left|G^{\alpha}_{s}(\boldsymbol{y}-\eta{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{e})\right|d\boldsymbol{y}\leq&\,\mathbb{P}\left\{|L^{(\alpha)}_{s}|\geq{\varepsilon}\beta^{1+\frac{1}{H}}\right\}\\

\mathbb{P}\left(\left\|L^{(\alpha)}_{1}\right\|>\lambda\right)\leq\text{const}\cdot\lambda^{-\alpha}\ \ \ \text{for all }\ \lambda>0.

See, e.g.,
[
1
]

\begin{split}Q_{21}\leq\,\left(3^{-1}{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}\beta^{-\left(2\alpha\left(1+\frac{1}{H}\right)-3\right)}\right)^{1/p}.\end{split}

\displaystyle 2|\langle f,g\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}|\leq\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+\|g\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2},

\begin{split}Q_{22}=&\left\{\int_{0}^{\beta{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}}ds\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}d\boldsymbol{y}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}d\boldsymbol{z}\left|\left(\nabla_{{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{e}}G^{\alpha}_{s}\right)(\boldsymbol{y})\right|\|\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{z}\|^{-\frac{p}{p-1}(d-\gamma)}\left|\left(\nabla_{{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{e}}G^{\alpha}_{s}\right)(\boldsymbol{z})\right|\right\}^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\\

\begin{split}Q:=&\,\left\|(\nabla_{{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{e}}X_{t})(\boldsymbol{x})-\int_{\mathbf{B}_{\beta}(\boldsymbol{x},t;{\varepsilon})}\left(\nabla_{{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{e}}G^{\alpha}_{t-s}\right)(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})\sigma(u_{s}(\boldsymbol{y}))M(ds,d\boldsymbol{y})\right\|_{L^{k}(\Omega)}^{2}\\
=&\,\left\|\int_{[(0,t)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}]\backslash\mathbf{B}_{\beta}(0,t;{\varepsilon})}\left(\nabla_{{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{e}}G^{\alpha}_{t-s}\right)(\boldsymbol{y})\sigma(u_{s}(\boldsymbol{y}))M(ds,d\boldsymbol{y})\right\|_{L^{k}(\Omega)}^{2}\\
\leq&\,\left\|\int_{0}^{t-\beta{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\nabla_{{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{e}}G^{\alpha}_{t-s}\right)(\boldsymbol{y})\sigma(u_{s}(\boldsymbol{y}))M(ds,d\boldsymbol{y})\right\|_{L^{k}(\Omega)}^{2}\\
&\,+\left\|\int_{t-\beta{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}}^{t}\int_{\|\boldsymbol{y}\|>{\varepsilon}\delta}\left(\nabla_{{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{e}}G^{\alpha}_{t-s}\right)(\boldsymbol{y})\sigma(u_{s}(\boldsymbol{y}))M(ds,d\boldsymbol{y})\right\|_{L^{k}(\Omega)}^{2}\\
=:&\,Q_{1}+Q_{2}.\end{split}

By using (
1.7
) and using the same argument in the proof of (
3.14
), we can obtain that

\displaystyle Q_{2}\leq c_{3,5}{\varepsilon}^{\alpha-d+\gamma}\beta^{-\frac{2+d-\alpha-\gamma}{\alpha}},

(3.18)

where
c_{3,5}>0
.

By using the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality (
2.7
), (
1.6
), (
1.7
), (
2.10
) and (
3.12
),
we have

\begin{split}Q_{1}\leq&\,4k\int_{0}^{t-\beta{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}}ds\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}d\boldsymbol{y}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}d\boldsymbol{z}\left|\left(\nabla_{{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{e}}G^{\alpha}_{t-s}\right)(\boldsymbol{y})\right|\left\|\sigma(u_{s}(\boldsymbol{y}))\right\|_{L^{k}(\Omega)}\\
&\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\cdot\|\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{z}\|^{-(d-\gamma)}\cdot\left|\left(\nabla_{{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{e}}G^{\alpha}_{t-s}\right)(\boldsymbol{z})\right|\left\|\sigma(u_{s}(\boldsymbol{z}))\right\|_{L^{k}(\Omega)}\\
\leq&\,c_{3,6}{\varepsilon}^{2}\int_{0}^{t-\beta{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}}(t-s)^{-\frac{2}{\alpha}}ds\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}d\boldsymbol{y}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}d\boldsymbol{z}\left(G^{\alpha}_{t-s}(\boldsymbol{y})+G^{\alpha}_{t-s}(\boldsymbol{y}-{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{e})\right)\\
&\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\cdot\|\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{z}\|^{-(d-\gamma)}\cdot\left(G^{\alpha}_{t-s}(\boldsymbol{z})+G^{\alpha}_{t-s}(\boldsymbol{z}-{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{e})\right)\\
\leq&\,4c_{3,6}{\varepsilon}^{2}\int_{0}^{t-\beta{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}}(t-s)^{-\frac{2}{\alpha}}ds\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}d\boldsymbol{y}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}d\boldsymbol{z}\,G^{\alpha}_{t-s}(\boldsymbol{y})\cdot\|\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{z}\|^{-(d-\gamma)}\cdot G^{\alpha}_{t-s}(\boldsymbol{z})\\
=&\,4c_{1,1}^{-1}c_{3,6}{\varepsilon}^{2}\int_{0}^{t-\beta{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}}(t-s)^{-\frac{2}{\alpha}}ds\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}d\xi\,\|\xi\|^{-\gamma}\,e^{-2(t-s)\|\xi\|^{\alpha}}\\
\leq&\,4c_{1,1}^{-1}c_{3,6}{\varepsilon}^{2}\int_{\beta{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}}^{\infty}s^{-\frac{2+d-\gamma}{\alpha}}ds\cdot\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\|\xi\|^{-\gamma}\,e^{-2\|\xi\|^{\alpha}}d\xi\\
=&\,\Big(4c_{1,1}^{-1}c_{3,6}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\|\xi\|^{-\gamma}\,e^{-2\|\xi\|^{\alpha}}d\xi\Big){\varepsilon}^{\alpha-d+\gamma}\beta^{-\frac{2+d-\alpha-\gamma}{\alpha}},\end{split}

(3.19)

where
c_{3,6}>0
.
Putting (
3.18
) and (
3.19
) together and using the Minkowski inequality, we obtain (

).
The proof is complete. ∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Choose and fix
T>0
,
k\in[2,k_{0}]
and
\beta:={\varepsilon}^{-l}
for some
l>0
. Then, there exists a constant
c_{3,7}>0
such that

\begin{split}&\left\|\int_{\mathbf{B}_{\beta}(\boldsymbol{x},t;{\varepsilon})}\left(\nabla_{{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{e}}G^{\alpha}_{t-s}\right)(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})\left[\sigma(u_{s}(\boldsymbol{y}))-{\sigma(u_{t}(\tilde{\boldsymbol{x}}))}\right]M(ds,d\boldsymbol{y})\right\|_{L^{k}(\Omega)}\\
\leq&\,c_{3,7}{\varepsilon}^{2H}\beta^{1+\alpha-d+\gamma},\end{split}

(3.20)

simultaneously for all
\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
t\in[0,T]
,
{\varepsilon}\in(0,1)
,
l>0
,
\tilde{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathbf{B}(\boldsymbol{x};{\varepsilon}\delta)
and the unit vector
\boldsymbol{e}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Proof.

Let
t\in[0,T]
and
\tilde{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathbf{B}(\boldsymbol{x};{\varepsilon}\delta)
.
We first consider a related quantity
Q_{1}
, where

\begin{split}Q_{1}:=\left\|\int_{\mathbf{B}_{\beta}(\boldsymbol{x},t;{\varepsilon})}\left(\nabla_{{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{e}}G^{\alpha}_{t-s}\right)(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})\left[\sigma(u_{s}(\boldsymbol{y}))-\sigma(u_{t-\beta{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}}(\tilde{\boldsymbol{x}}))\right]M(ds,d\boldsymbol{y})\right\|_{L^{k}(\Omega)}^{2}.\end{split}

(3.21)

Since
u_{t-\beta{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}}(\tilde{\boldsymbol{x}})
is measurable with respect to the time-white noise of
[0,t-\beta{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, it is independent of the time-white noise of
\mathbf{B}_{\beta}(\boldsymbol{x},t;{\varepsilon})
. Therefore, by the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality (
2.7
), we have

\displaystyle Q_{1}\leq

\displaystyle\,2k\int_{t-\beta{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}}^{t}ds\int_{\mathbf{B}(\boldsymbol{x};{\varepsilon}\delta)}d\boldsymbol{y}\int_{\mathbf{B}(\boldsymbol{x};{\varepsilon}\delta)}d\boldsymbol{z}\left(\nabla_{{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{e}}G^{\alpha}_{t-s}\right)(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})\left\|\sigma(u_{s}(\boldsymbol{y}))-\sigma(u_{t-\beta{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}}(\tilde{\boldsymbol{x}}))\right\|_{L^{k}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\cdot\|\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{z}\|^{-(d-\gamma)}\cdot\left(\nabla_{{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{e}}G^{\alpha}_{t-s}\right)(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{z})\left\|\sigma(u_{s}(\boldsymbol{z}))-\sigma(u_{t-\beta{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}}(\tilde{\boldsymbol{x}}))\right\|_{L^{k}(\Omega)}.

By Hölder’s inequality, we have that for any
p>\max\{\frac{d}{\gamma},\frac{\alpha^{2}}{\alpha-d+\gamma}\}
,

\displaystyle Q_{1}\leq

\displaystyle\,2k\int_{t-\beta{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}}^{t}ds\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}d\boldsymbol{y}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}d\boldsymbol{z}\left|\left(\nabla_{{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{e}}G^{\alpha}_{t-s}\right)(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})\right|\cdot\left|\left(\nabla_{{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{e}}G^{\alpha}_{t-s}\right)(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{z})\right|\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\cdot\sup_{(s,\boldsymbol{y})\in\mathbf{B}_{\beta}(\boldsymbol{x},t;{\varepsilon})}\left\|\sigma(u_{s}(\boldsymbol{y}))-\sigma(u_{t-\beta{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}}(\tilde{\boldsymbol{x}}))\right\|_{L^{k}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\cdot\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}d\boldsymbol{y}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}d\boldsymbol{z}\left|\left(\nabla_{{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{e}}G^{\alpha}_{t-s}\right)(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})\right|\cdot\|\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{z}\|^{-\frac{p}{p-1}(d-\gamma)}\cdot\left|\left(\nabla_{{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{e}}G^{\alpha}_{t-s}\right)(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{z})\right|\right]^{\frac{p}{p-1}}.

By (
2.10
) and (
3.12
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}d\boldsymbol{y}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}d\boldsymbol{z}\left|\left(\nabla_{{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{e}}G^{\alpha}_{t-s}\right)(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})\right|\cdot\left|\left(\nabla_{{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{e}}G^{\alpha}_{t-s}\right)(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{z})\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,c_{2,2}\frac{{\varepsilon}}{(t-s)^{\alpha}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}d\boldsymbol{y}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}d\boldsymbol{z}\left(G^{\alpha}_{t-s}(\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{y})+G^{\alpha}_{t-{\varepsilon}-s}(\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{y})\right)\left(G^{\alpha}_{t-s}(\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{z})+G^{\alpha}_{t-{\varepsilon}-s}(\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{z})\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,4c_{2,2}\frac{{\varepsilon}}{(t-s)^{\alpha}}.

By Proposition
2.6
, we have that for

\displaystyle\sup_{(s,\boldsymbol{y})\in\mathbf{B}_{\beta}(\boldsymbol{x},t;{\varepsilon})}\left\|\sigma(u_{s}(\boldsymbol{y}))-\sigma(u_{t-\beta{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}}(\tilde{\boldsymbol{x}}))\right\|_{L^{k}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq c_{2,8}{\varepsilon}^{2H}\left[\beta^{H/\alpha}+(2\delta)^{H}\right]^{2}.

By (
3.12
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}d\boldsymbol{y}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}d\boldsymbol{z}\left|\left(\nabla_{{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{e}}G^{\alpha}_{t-s}\right)(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})\right|\cdot\|\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{z}\|^{-\frac{p}{p-1}(d-\gamma)}\cdot\left|\left(\nabla_{{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{e}}G^{\alpha}_{t-s}\right)(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{z})\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,4\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}d\boldsymbol{y}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}d\boldsymbol{z}\,G^{\alpha}_{t-s}(\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{y})\|\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{z}\|^{-\frac{p}{p-1}(d-\gamma)}G^{\alpha}_{t-s}(\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{z})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,c_{3,8}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}d\xi\,\|\xi\|^{-\frac{1}{p-1}(p\gamma-d)}\,e^{-2(t-s)\|\xi\|^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\left(c_{3,8}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\,\|\xi\|^{-\frac{p\gamma-d}{p-1}}\,e^{-2\|\xi\|^{\alpha}}d\xi\right)\cdot(t-s)^{-\frac{p(d-\gamma)}{(p-1)\alpha}},

where
c_{3,8}\in(0,\infty)
, the integral
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\,\|\xi\|^{-\frac{1}{p-1}(p\gamma-d)}\,e^{-2\|\xi\|^{\alpha}}d\xi
is finite because
\frac{p\gamma-d}{p-1}<d
.
Thus,

\begin{split}Q_{1}\leq&\,c_{3,9}{\varepsilon}^{2H+\frac{1}{p}}\left[\beta^{H/\alpha}+(2\delta)^{H}\right]^{2}\int_{t-\beta{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}}^{t}\,(t-s)^{-\frac{\alpha}{p}-\frac{d-\gamma}{\alpha}}ds\\
=&\,c_{3,9}\left(1-\frac{\alpha}{p}-\frac{d-\gamma}{\alpha}\right)^{-1}\cdot{\varepsilon}^{2(\alpha-d+\gamma)}\left[\beta^{H/\alpha}+(2\delta)^{H}\right]^{2}\cdot\beta^{1-\frac{d-\gamma}{\alpha}-\frac{\alpha}{p}}{\varepsilon}^{-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{p}+\frac{1}{p}}.\end{split}

(3.22)

Next, we estimate the cost of estimating
u_{t}(\tilde{\boldsymbol{x}})
by
u_{t-\beta{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}}(\tilde{\boldsymbol{x}})
. By the Hölder inequality, (
2.20
) and Lemma
2.2
(a), we have

\begin{split}Q_{2}:=&\,\left\|\left[\sigma\left(u_{t}(\tilde{\boldsymbol{x}})\right)-\sigma\left(u_{t-\beta{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}}(\tilde{\boldsymbol{x}})\right)\right]\int_{\mathbf{B}_{\beta}(\boldsymbol{x},t;{\varepsilon})}\left(\nabla_{{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{e}}G^{\alpha}_{t-s}\right)(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})M(ds,d\boldsymbol{y})\right\|_{k}^{2}\\

\leq&\,c_{2,7}^{2}L_{\sigma}^{2}\left(\beta{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}\right)^{2H/\alpha}\cdot{\varepsilon}^{\alpha-d+\gamma}=c_{2,7}^{2}L_{\sigma}^{2}{\varepsilon}^{2(\alpha-d+\gamma)}\beta^{\frac{\alpha-d+\gamma}{\alpha}}.\end{split}

(3.23)

Since
\delta=1+\beta^{1+\frac{1}{H}}\leq 2\beta^{1+\frac{1}{H}}
, we can conclude from (

) and (

\displaystyle\sqrt{Q_{1}}+\sqrt{Q_{2}}\leq c_{3,10}{\varepsilon}^{\alpha-d+\gamma}\beta^{1+\alpha-d+\gamma}\cdot\left[\beta^{-\frac{(\alpha-1)(\alpha-d+\gamma)}{2\alpha}-\frac{\alpha}{2p}}{\varepsilon}^{-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2p}+\frac{1}{2p}}+\beta^{-1-(\alpha-d+\gamma)+\frac{\alpha-d+\gamma}{2\alpha})}\right],

where
c_{3,10}\in(0,\infty)
. Since

\displaystyle\sqrt{Q_{1}}+\sqrt{Q_{2}}\leq 2c_{3,10}{\varepsilon}^{\alpha-d+\gamma}\beta^{1+\alpha-d+\gamma}.

This and Minkowski’s inequality together imply this lemma. ∎

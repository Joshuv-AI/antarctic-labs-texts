Perturbative Calculations of Gravitational Form Factors at Large Momentum Transfer
Xuan-Bo Tong, Jian-Ping Ma, Feng Yuan

Abstract
We perform a perturbative QCD analysis of the gravitational form factors (GFFs) of nucleon at large momentum transfer. We derive the explicit factorization formula of the GFFs in terms of twist-3 and twist-4 light-cone distribution amplitudes of nucleon. Power behaviors for these GFFs are obtained from the leading order calculations. Numeric results of the quark and gluon contributions to various GFFs are presented with model assumptions for the distribution amplitudes in the literature. We also present the perturbative calculations of the scalar form factor ⟨ P ′ | F 2 | P ⟩ \langle P^{\prime}|F^{2}|P\rangle for pion and proton at large momentum transfer.

The EMT of a field theory in the Minkowski space is derived from the space-time translation invariance of the action by the Noether theorem
Noether 1918

\displaystyle T^{\mu\nu}(x)=\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\delta S_{M}}{\delta g_{\mu\nu}(x)}\ ,

(1)

;
Belinfante 1940

To explain the implication of GFFs, we can consider a hadron system in the classical gravitational background.
When the curvature of the gravitational field is weak enough, we can expand the metric field around the Minkowski metric

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}(x)=\eta_{\mu\nu}+h_{\mu\nu}(x)\ .

(2)

\displaystyle\int d^{4}x~h_{\mu\nu}(x)T^{\mu\nu}_{QCD}(x)~.

(3)

N

\displaystyle\langle N(P^{\prime})|T^{\mu\nu}_{QCD}|N(P)\rangle\ .

II
Gravitational form factors

In this section, we will first present the explicit expressions of the symmetric QCD EMT and discuss some important features of it.

II.1
Energy-Momentum Tensor

The symmetric QCD energy momentum tensor is defined as

\displaystyle T^{\mu\nu}_{QCD}=T^{\mu\nu}_{q}+T^{\mu\nu}_{g}\ .

(5)

The quark and gluon sectors are given by

\displaystyle T^{\mu\nu}_{q}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\bar{\psi}_{f}\left(-i\overleftarrow{D}^{\mu}\gamma^{\nu}-i\overleftarrow{D}^{\nu}\gamma^{\mu}+i\overrightarrow{D}^{\mu}\gamma^{\nu}+i\overrightarrow{D}^{\nu}\gamma^{\mu}\right)\psi_{f}~,

\displaystyle T^{\mu\nu}_{g}

\displaystyle=F^{a,\mu\lambda}F_{\lambda}^{a,\nu}+\frac{1}{4}g^{\mu\nu}F^{a,\sigma\rho}F^{a}_{\sigma\rho}~,

(6)

where
\psi_{f}
is a quark field of flavor
f
, and
F^{a}_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}A^{a}_{\nu}-\partial^{\nu}A^{a}_{\mu}-g_{s}f^{abc}A^{b}_{\mu}A^{c}_{\nu}
is the strength tensor of the gluon field
A^{a,\mu}
, and the covariant derivatives are defined as

\displaystyle\overrightarrow{D}_{\mu}=\overrightarrow{\partial}_{\mu}+ig_{s}A^{a}_{\mu}T^{a},\quad\overleftarrow{D}_{\mu}=\overleftarrow{\partial}_{\mu}-ig_{s}A^{a}_{\mu}T^{a}\ .

(7)

The sum of the quark flavors and colors are implied.

There are several features that we would like to emphasize. First, the total EMT in Eq. (
5
) is conserved due to the space-time translation invariance, i.e.
\partial_{\nu}T^{\mu\nu}_{QCD}=0
. However, the gluon or quark part of the EMT is not conserved individually. Instead, the derivative of the EMT operators are related to the twist-four operators
Braun and Lenz 2004
;
Tanaka 2018
,

\displaystyle\partial_{\nu}T_{q}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\bar{\psi}_{f}gF^{\mu\nu}\gamma_{\nu}\psi_{f}~,

(8)

\displaystyle\partial_{\nu}T_{g}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=F_{\nu}^{\ \mu}D_{\alpha}F^{\alpha\nu}\ .

(9)

The above equations are derived by applying the equations of motion for the quark and gluon fields:
(i\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rightarrow}}{{\not{D}}}-m_{f})\psi_{f}=0
and
\partial^{\mu}F^{a}_{\mu\nu}-gf^{abc}A^{b\mu}F^{c}_{\mu\nu}=g\bar{\psi}\gamma_{\nu}T^{a}\psi
. With the EOM of the gluon strength tensor, one can also find the right sides of Eqs.(
8
) and (
9
) cancel out each other. This confirms the conservation of the total EMT operator explicitly. Therefore, the total EMT operator is UV finite and scale-independent
Freedman et al. 1974
;
Nielsen 1975
;
Nielsen 1977
;
Collins et al. 1977
while the quark or gluon sector of the EMT is divergent and needs regularization and renormalization
Hatta et al. 2018
;
Tanaka 2019
. The renormalization of the EMT is closely related to the trace of this operator. Classically, the quark or gluon EMT is traceless if the quark masses are neglected:

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}T^{\mu\nu}_{q,g}=0~,

(10)

which is also the consequence of the conformal symmetry in the massless QCD. This feature will be broken at the quantum loop level, called trace anomaly
Freedman et al. 1974
;
Nielsen 1975
;
Nielsen 1977
;
Collins et al. 1977
;
Tarrach 1982
;
Ji 1995b
:

\displaystyle T^{\mu}_{\mu}=\frac{\beta(g_{s})}{2g_{s}}F^{a,\mu\nu}F^{a}_{\mu\nu}~,

(11)

where
\beta(g_{s})
is the QCD beta function and the terms
(1+\gamma_{m})m_{f}\bar{\psi}_{f}\psi_{f}
should be added in the massive case.

Second, in the covariant quantization of QCD, the EMT operators in Eqs. (
5
,
6
) are not completed rigorously. For example, in the Faddeev-Popov quantization procedure for the gauge theory one has to introduce the ghost and gauge-fixing terms in the Lagrangian, thus there are corresponding terms in the QCD EMT
Freedman et al. 1974
. However, these terms are ensured to be absent in the physical matrix elements by the BRST symmetry
Joglekar and Lee 1976
and hence have no observable effects. Therefore, we have excluded them in our study of the GFFs.

II.2
Nucleon Gravitational Form Factors

Nucleon is a spin-
1/2
particle, and the quark or gluon gravitational form factors can be parameterized as
Ji 1997a
:

\displaystyle\langle P^{\prime},s^{\prime}|T^{\mu\nu}_{a}(0)|P,s\rangle=\bar{u}_{s}(P^{\prime})\Bigg[A_{a}(t)\gamma^{(\mu}\bar{P}^{\nu)}

\displaystyle+B_{a}(t)\frac{i\bar{P}^{(\mu}\sigma^{\nu)\rho}\Delta_{\rho}}{2M}+C_{a}(t)\frac{\Delta^{\mu}\Delta^{\nu}-g^{\mu\nu}\Delta^{2}}{M}

\displaystyle+\overline{C}_{a}(t)Mg^{\mu\nu}\Bigg]u_{s}(P)\ ,

(12)

where
a=q,g
and the brace for the Lorentz indices
\mu,\nu
denote the symmetrization known as
A^{(\mu}B^{\nu)}\equiv(A^{\mu}B^{\nu}+A^{\nu}B^{\mu})/2
.
P
and
P^{\prime}
are the momenta of the initial and final particle respectively, satisfying the on-shell condition
P^{2}={P^{\prime}}^{2}=M^{2}
, where
M
is the nucleon mass.
\bar{P}
is the average momentum,
\Delta
is the momentum transfer and
t
is the momentum transfer squared:

\displaystyle\bar{P}=(P+P^{\prime})/2,\quad\Delta=P^{\prime}-P,\quad t=\Delta^{2}\ .

(13)

The nucleon state is covariant normalized,
\langle P^{\prime}|P\rangle=2P^{0}(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{3}(\vec{P}^{\prime}-\vec{P})
.
u_{s}(P)
is the Dirac spinor of nucleon normalized as
\bar{u}_{s^{\prime}}(P)u_{s}(P)=2M\delta_{ss^{\prime}}
.
One can utilize the well-known Gorden identity for the spinor,

\displaystyle 2M\bar{u}_{s^{\prime}}(P^{\prime})\gamma^{\alpha}u_{s}(P)=\bar{u}_{s^{\prime}}(P^{\prime})(2\bar{P}^{\alpha}+i\sigma^{\alpha\kappa}\Delta_{\kappa})u_{s}(P)~,

(14)

to obtain another parametrization for the GFFs:

\displaystyle\langle P^{\prime},s^{\prime}|T^{\mu\nu}_{a}(0)|P,s\rangle=\bar{u}_{s^{\prime}}(P^{\prime})\Bigg[2J_{a}(t)\gamma^{(\mu}\bar{P}^{\nu)}

\displaystyle-B_{a}(t)\frac{\bar{P}^{\mu}\bar{P}^{\nu}}{M}+C_{a}(t)\frac{\Delta^{\mu}\Delta^{\nu}-g^{\mu\nu}\Delta^{2}}{M}

\displaystyle+\overline{C}_{a}(t)Mg^{\mu\nu}\Bigg]u_{s}(P)\ ,

(15)

where
J_{a}(t)=[A_{a}(t)+B_{a}(t)]/2
. Here we follow the notations in Refs.
Ji 1997a
;
Ji 1997b
for the
C
form factors. They have also been referred as
D
or
d_{1}
form factors in Refs.
Polyakov 2003
;
Polyakov and Schweitzer 2018
;
Burkert et al. 2018
;
Shanahan and Detmold 2019a
;
Kumerički 2019
with different normalizations:
D(t)=4/5d_{1}(t)=4C(t)
.

From the above expressions, one can observe the quark and gluon GFFs are the Lorentz invariants of the momentum transfer squared
t
. As shown in Eqs. (
8
,
9
), the quark and gluon parts of EMT are not conserved and require renormalization, and the corresponding GFFs should also depend on the renormalization scale

;
Tanaka 2019
. For each form factor, we can also define the total GFF, e.g.
C(t)=\sum_{a=q,g}C_{a}(t)
. They are, on the other hand, renormalization scale independent due to the conservation of the total EMT. This feature has further implication on the GFFs:

\displaystyle\langle P^{\prime}|\partial_{\nu}T_{QCD}^{\mu\nu}|P\rangle=

\displaystyle\langle P^{\prime}|i\Big[\hat{P}_{\nu},T_{QCD}^{\mu\nu}\Big]|P\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\Delta_{\nu}\langle P^{\prime}|T_{QCD}^{\mu\nu}|P\rangle=0\ ,

(16)

where the Hesienberg equation of the EMT operator is applied. The last equality yields

\displaystyle\Delta_{\nu}\langle P^{\prime},s^{\prime}|T_{QCD}^{\mu\nu}|P,s\rangle=\overline{C}(t)M\Delta^{\mu}\bar{u}_{s^{\prime}}(P^{\prime})u_{s}(P)=0\ ,

(17)

where the terms related to the
A,B,C
-form factors vanish automatically from its own tensor structure or the on-shell condition of the Dirac spinor,
\not{P}u(P)=Mu(P)
. Therefore, an important constraint on the
\overline{C}
-GFFs is obtained:

\displaystyle\overline{C}(t)=\sum_{a=q,g}\overline{C}_{a}(t)=0\ .

(18)

This constraint works regardless of the value of the momentum transfer squared
t
and will serve as an important consistent check on our perturbative calculations of the quark and gluon GFFs at large momentum transfer. The details will be presented in Sec.
III.6
.

The GFFs
\overline{C}_{q,g}(t)
play an important role in the nucleon mass reconstruction since it is closely related to the trace anomaly
F^{2}
and its value at

determines the contributions of the so-called quantum anomalous energy

;
Ji 1995b
;
Ji 2021
;
Ji et al. 2021a
;
Hatta et al. 2018
;
Boussarie and Hatta 2020
.
The

form factors in Eqs. (

,

) are proposed to describe the mechanical properties such as pressure and shear forces distribution inside the nucleon

;
Polyakov and Schweitzer 2018
;
Panteleeva and Polyakov 2021
;

. This interpretation uniquely requires the whole knowledge of

C
-form factor.

In addition, the
A_{q,g}
form factor at zero momentum transfer can be interpreted as the longitudinal momentum fraction that the parton carries inside the nucleon in the infinite momentum frame with
\sum_{a=q,g}A_{a}(0)=1

III
Perturbative Analysis at large momentum transfer

In general, the nucleon GFFs are non-perturbative due to the color confinement.
Nonetheless, in the large momentum transfer limit, there are indeed perturbative calculable effects in the GFFs. The physics behind is that the highly virtual graviton has short enough wavelength to resolve the structure of the nucleon by the short space-time interactions. The partons participated in this interaction are weakly coupled but endure with hard gluon exchanges to pass the large momentum transfer from the initial nucleon to the final nucleon. These effects can be calculated using perturbation theory due to the asymptotic freedom of QCD. On the other hand, the interactions among those active partons and the other spectator partons inside the nucleon are still of long range and hence strong. In this section, we will demonstrate that this short-range and long-range physics can be factorized at lowest order perturbation theory, leading to the factorization theorem of the GFFs in terms of twist-3 or twist-4 nucleon light-cone distribution amplitudes. Our derivations follow the ERBL formalism
Lepage and Brodsky 1979
;
Brodsky and Lepage 1981
;
Efremov and Radyushkin 1980
;
Chernyak and Zhitnitsky 1977
;
Chernyak and Zhitnitsky 1980
;
Chernyak and Zhitnitsky 1984a
;
Belitsky et al. 2003
with development on the nucleon light-front wave function involving zero or one unit of orbital angular momentum
Ji et al. 2003a
;
Ji et al. 2004
;
Belitsky et al. 2003
.

We will first introduce the helicity-amplitude with appropriate tensor projection to isolate each GFF, and choose a reference frame. Later, we will review the three-quark Fock state of the nucleon in the literature.
Based on these materials, we will carry out the perturbative analysis of the GFFs through the helicity-conserved and helicity-flip amplitudes, respectively. Finally, we summarize the factorization results and perform consistent checks.

III.1
Helicity Amplitude of EMT

Since the nucleon is a spin-1/2 particle, the parametrization of the EMT amplitude involves the Dirac structures as well as the tensor structures. The corresponding form factors can be extracted from the nucleon helicity amplitude with appropriate tensor projections. In the high energy, we will find that the helicity-conserved amplitudes yield the
A
-form factors, and the helicity-flip amplitudes lead to other GFFs.

As presented in Eq. (
15
), there are two Dirac structures involved in the EMT amplitude:
\bar{u}_{s^{\prime}}\gamma^{\mu}u_{s}
and
\bar{u}_{s^{\prime}}u_{s}
, corresponding to the chiral-odd and chiral-even Dirac bilinear spinors. In the large momentum transfer limit, the nucleon mass can be neglected, and hence the chirality becomes a good quantum number to identify the nucleon state, i.e.,

\displaystyle\gamma_{5}u_{\uparrow/\downarrow}(P)=\pm u_{\uparrow/\downarrow}(P)~,

(19)

where
u_{\uparrow/\downarrow}(P)
is the chirality eigenstates, named as right- or left-handed spinors. Besides, for a massless spin-1/2 particle, its chirality is known to be equivalent to its helicity, where helicity is defined by the projection of the spin along the three-momentum direction. We will use helicity as the terminology in the following paragraphs. Now we choose the appropriate helicity eigenstates for the initial and final nucleons along with tensor projections to extract the GFFs.

Helicity-conserved Amplitude

A
-form factor can be extracted form the helicity-conserved amplitude:

\displaystyle\langle P^{\prime}_{\uparrow}|T^{\mu\nu}_{a}|P_{\uparrow}\rangle=A_{a}(t)\bar{u}_{\uparrow}(P^{\prime})\gamma^{(\mu}\bar{P}^{\nu)}u_{\uparrow}(P)\ ,

(20)

where
B
-form factor are power-suppressed as shown later and hence neglected in the above equation. Since only
A
-form factor is relevant at the leading power, we do not need to assign the tensor projection. Here we used the amplitude with positive helicity. The amplitude for the negative helicity can also be used and leads to the same result.

Helicity-flip Amplitude

On the other hand, helicity-flip amplitude can be used to extract the GFFs
{B}_{a}
,
{C}_{a}
and
\overline{C}_{a}
:

\displaystyle\langle P^{\prime}_{\uparrow}|T^{\mu\nu}_{a}|P_{\downarrow}\rangle=\bar{u}_{\uparrow}(P^{\prime})\left[-\frac{B_{a}(t)}{M}\bar{P}^{\mu}\bar{P}^{\nu}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{C_{a}(t)}{M}(\Delta^{\mu}\Delta^{\nu}-g^{\mu\nu}\Delta^{2})+\overline{C}_{a}(t)Mg^{\mu\nu}\right]u_{\downarrow}(P)\ .

(21)

To further isolate the GFFs, we can define the following projection tensors,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{1}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\bar{P}^{\mu}\bar{P}^{\nu}-\bar{P}^{2}g^{\mu\nu}\ ,

(22)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{2}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta^{\mu}\Delta^{\nu}-\Delta^{2}g^{\mu\nu}/4\ .

(23)

Notice that
g^{\mu\nu}\Gamma_{1,2}^{\mu\nu}=0
in the massless limit. Applying the above, we can derive

\displaystyle{B}_{a}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2M\left(\Gamma_{2}^{\mu\nu}-3\Gamma_{1}^{\mu\nu}\right)\langle P^{\prime}_{\uparrow}|T^{\mu\nu}_{a}|P_{\downarrow}\rangle}{(\Delta^{2})^{2}\bar{u}_{\uparrow}(P^{\prime})u_{\downarrow}(P)}\ ,

(24)

\displaystyle{C}_{a}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{M\left(3\Gamma_{2}^{\mu\nu}-\Gamma_{1}^{\mu\nu}\right)\langle P^{\prime}_{\uparrow}|T^{\mu\nu}_{a}|P_{\downarrow}\rangle}{2(\Delta^{2})^{2}\bar{u}_{\uparrow}(P^{\prime})u_{\downarrow}(P)}\ .

(25)

From the traceless feature of the EMT in our calculations at this order, we can also derive
\overline{C}
form factor:

\displaystyle\overline{C}_{a}(t)=

\displaystyle\frac{-t}{16M^{2}}\big[B_{a}(t)-12C_{a}(t)\big].

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma_{2}^{\mu\nu}\langle P^{\prime}_{\uparrow}|T^{\mu\nu}_{a}|P_{\downarrow}\rangle}{M(\Delta^{2})\bar{u}_{\uparrow}(P^{\prime})u_{\downarrow}(P)}\ .

(26)

As mentioned above, due to the conservation of the EMT,
the total
\overline{C}
-form factor should vanish. That renders

\displaystyle\Gamma_{2}^{\mu\nu}\langle P^{\prime}_{\uparrow}|T^{\mu\nu}_{QCD}|P_{\downarrow}\rangle=0\ .

(27)

Furthermore, the total contributions to the
B
and
C
form factors follow

\displaystyle B(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{6M\Gamma_{1}^{\mu\nu}\langle P^{\prime}_{\uparrow}|T^{\mu\nu}_{QCD}|P_{\downarrow}\rangle}{(\Delta^{2})^{2}\bar{u}_{\uparrow}(P^{\prime})u_{\downarrow}(P)}\ ,

(28)

\displaystyle C(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{M\Gamma_{1}^{\mu\nu}\langle P^{\prime}_{\uparrow}|T^{\mu\nu}_{QCD}|P_{\downarrow}\rangle}{2(\Delta^{2})^{2}\bar{u}_{\uparrow}(P^{\prime})u_{\downarrow}(P)}\ .

(29)

As a consequence, we find that there is a nontrivial relation between the total
B
- and
C
-form factors at large momentum transfer at the leading order perturbation theory:

\displaystyle B(t)=12C(t)\ .

(30)

This relation along with the constraint
\overline{C}(t)=0
will serve as the consistent checks of our calculations.

III.2
Breit Frame

In order to facilitate the computation, we need to fix the reference frame explicitly. Since the GFFs are the Lorentz invariant, one can choose any frame in principle. Particularly, we use the so-called Breit frame in the calculation where the initial nucleon is along the
z
-axes with high energy and the final nucleon is along the opposite direction:

\displaystyle P^{\mu}=(P^{+},P^{-},\boldsymbol{P}_{\perp})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\sqrt{-t},\ 0,\ \boldsymbol{0}_{\perp}\right),

\displaystyle P^{\prime\mu}=(P^{\prime+},P^{\prime-},\boldsymbol{P}^{\prime}_{\perp})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(0,\ \sqrt{-t},\ \boldsymbol{0}_{\perp}\right),

\displaystyle\Delta^{\mu}=(\Delta^{+},\Delta^{-},\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\perp})=\left(-\sqrt{-t},\ \sqrt{-t},\ \boldsymbol{0}_{\perp}\right).

(31)

Here the large momentum transfer limit
-t\gg M^{2}
is applied and the light-cone coordinates are used where
a^{+}=(a^{0}+a^{3})/\sqrt{2}
,
a^{-}=(a^{0}-a^{3})/\sqrt{2}
and
a_{\perp}^{2}=-\boldsymbol{a}_{\perp}^{2}
. In the Breit frame, the momenta of the initial state and final state nucleons as well as the momentum transfer contain only the longitudinal components. Hence, all the large scales in the partonic subprocesses must come from the longitudinal momentum and make the power counting of the perturbative part more transparent.

III.3
Three-valence Quark Fock State of the Nucleon

In the Breit frame, both the initial and final nucleons are traveling along the longitudinal direction and carrying large momentum.
In this frame, one can apply the light-front Fock state expansion
Brodsky et al. 1998
.
In general, there are infinite number of Fock states with light-front wave functions in this expansion. However, for an exclusive process at the large momentum transfer, one can conclude from the general power counting rule that the leading contributions come from the terms with minimal numbers of partons and the fewest orbital angular momentum (OAM) content
Brodsky and Farrar 1973
;
Matveev et al. 1973
;
Ji et al. 2003b
. In the following, we will review the three-valence quark Fock state of the nucleon
Ji et al. 2003a
;
Ji et al. 2004
.

For the nucleon, the leading Fock state is made of three valence quarks, i.e.
u,u,d
. Each quark can carries the helicity
\lambda_{i}=\pm 1/2
.
The total helicity of the valence quarks has the value as
3/2,1/2,-1/2,-3/2
and the corresponding Fock states can be denoted

\displaystyle\big|P_{\uparrow}\big\rangle=\big|P_{\uparrow}\big\rangle^{l_{z}=2}_{-3/2}+\big|P_{\uparrow}\big\rangle^{l_{z}=1}_{-1/2}+\big|P_{\uparrow}\big\rangle^{l_{z}=0}_{1/2}+\big|P_{\uparrow}\big\rangle^{l_{z}=-1}_{3/2}.

(32)

where the upper subscript represents the total quark helicity
\lambda=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}
and the superscript stands for the projection of OAM along the
z
direction determined from the angular momentum sum rule
l_{z}=\Lambda-\lambda
. Similarly, the left-handed proton can be expressed as

\displaystyle\big|P_{\downarrow}\big\rangle=\big|P_{\downarrow}\big\rangle^{l_{z}=1}_{-3/2}+\big|P_{\downarrow}\big\rangle^{l_{z}=0}_{-1/2}+\big|P_{\downarrow}\big\rangle^{l_{z}=-1}_{1/2}+\big|P_{\downarrow}\big\rangle^{l_{z}=2}_{3/2}\ .

(33)

Therefore, the proton helicity amplitude of EMT

\displaystyle\langle P^{\prime},\Lambda^{\prime}|T^{\mu\nu}_{a}|P,\Lambda\rangle\ ,

(34)

can be expressed with the above Fock state components.

Then the nucleon EMT amplitudes are factorized into the partonic EMT amplitudes multiplied by the light-front wave function from the initial and final state nucleons. In the high energy scattering, the light quark mass can be neglected, and the parton helicity is conserved.
That means the nucleon helicity-flip amplitude can only be obtained by involving a non-zero OAM from either initial or final state Fock components.
In fact, this OAM along the longitudinal direction is achieved by the parton’s intrinsic transverse motion and hence introduces some transverse momentum factors in the phase space integral which normally scale as
\Lambda_{QCD}
. In the end, this factor will be compensated by the large momentum scale (
-t
in our case) and lead to power suppression. Therefore, more OAM involved, more power suppression will be obtained
Ji et al. 2003b
.

For the nucleon helicity-conserved amplitude in Eq.(
20
), we have

\displaystyle\langle P^{\prime}_{\uparrow}|{T^{\mu\nu}_{a}}|P_{\uparrow}\rangle=\langle P^{\prime}_{\uparrow}|^{l^{\prime}_{z}=0}_{1/2}{T^{\mu\nu}_{a}}|P_{\uparrow}\rangle^{l_{z}=0}_{1/2}\ ,

(35)

where the power suppressed contributions from
\Delta l_{z}=l^{\prime}_{z}-l_{z}\neq 0
are neglected. However, for the nucleon helicity flip amplitude in Eq. (
21
), one unit of OAM is needed to achieve the spin flip. Hence, the leading contributions come from the components with
\Delta l_{z}=1
,

\displaystyle\langle P^{\prime}_{\uparrow}|{T_{a}^{\mu\nu}}|P_{\downarrow}\rangle=

\displaystyle\langle P^{\prime}_{\uparrow}|^{l^{\prime}_{z}=0}_{1/2}{T_{g}^{\mu\nu}}|P_{\downarrow}\rangle^{l_{z}=-1}_{1/2}

\displaystyle+\langle P^{\prime}_{\uparrow}|^{l^{\prime}_{z}=1}_{-1/2}{T_{a}^{\mu\nu}}|P_{\downarrow}\rangle^{l_{z}=0}_{-1/2}\ ,

(36)

where the helicity flip for the first term is from the initial state OAM and the second one from the final state OAM. From the parity and time reversal invariance symmetry, one can show that they in fact have the same contributions to the GFFs at large
-t
. Here we only focus on the first term.

In the followings, the expressions of the relevant Fock state components involved in our evaluation for the nucleon helicity amplitude are given
Ji et al. 2003a
;
Ji et al. 2004
:

\displaystyle\big|P_{\uparrow}\big\rangle_{1/2}^{l_{z}=0}=

\displaystyle\int\ \frac{[dx][d^{2}\boldsymbol{k}]}{\sqrt{24x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}}}\psi_{1}(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2},\kappa_{3})

\displaystyle\times\ \big|\{x_{i}P+\boldsymbol{k}_{i}\}\big\rangle_{1/2},

(37)

\displaystyle\big|P_{\downarrow}\big\rangle_{1/2}^{l_{z}=-1}=

\displaystyle\int\ \frac{[dx][d^{2}\boldsymbol{k}]}{\sqrt{24x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}}}\big[k_{1L}\psi_{3}+k_{2L}\psi_{4}\big](\kappa_{2},\kappa_{1},\kappa_{3})

\displaystyle\times\ \big|\{x_{i}P+\boldsymbol{k}_{i}\}\big\rangle_{1/2}~,

(38)

where the three-valence-quark state is defined by

\displaystyle\big|\{p_{i}\}\big\rangle_{1/2}=

\displaystyle\frac{\epsilon_{abc}}{\sqrt{6}}\hat{u}_{a,\uparrow}^{\dagger}(p_{1})\big[\hat{u}_{b,\downarrow}^{\dagger}(p_{2})\hat{d}_{c,\uparrow}^{\dagger}(p_{3})

\displaystyle\ -\hat{d}_{b,\downarrow}^{\dagger}(p_{2})\hat{u}_{c,\uparrow}^{\dagger}(p_{3})\big]\big|0\big\rangle~.

(39)

In the above expressions,
\hat{u}^{\dagger}_{a,\lambda}
and
\hat{d}^{\dagger}_{a,\lambda}
are the creation operators of the
u,d
quarks with the helicity
\lambda
and color indices
a
in the fundamental representation of SU(3) group, normalized as
\{\hat{u}_{a,\lambda}(k),\hat{u}^{\dagger}_{b,\lambda^{\prime}}(k^{\prime})\}=\delta_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}\delta_{ab}2k^{+}\delta(k^{+}-k^{\prime+})\delta^{(2)}(\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}-\boldsymbol{k})
.
\psi_{1},\psi_{3},\psi_{4}
are the light-front wave functions of the proton depending on
\kappa_{i}\equiv(x_{i},\boldsymbol{k}_{i})
:
x_{i}
is the longitudinal momentum fraction of the parton with
\sum_{i}x_{i}=1
;
\boldsymbol{k}_{i}
is the intrinsic transverse momentum of the parton with
\sum_{i}\boldsymbol{k}_{i}=0
.
The integral measures for the phase space are defined as

\displaystyle[dx]=dx_{1}dx_{2}dx_{3}\ \delta(1-x_{1}-x_{2}-x_{3})\ ,\quad

\displaystyle[d^{2}k]=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{6}}d^{2}\boldsymbol{k}_{1}d^{2}\boldsymbol{k}_{2}d^{2}\boldsymbol{k}_{3}\delta^{(2)}(\boldsymbol{k}_{1}+\boldsymbol{k}_{2}+\boldsymbol{k}_{3})\ .

(40)

The momentum factor
k_{L}\equiv k^{x}-ik^{y}
is the explicit representation of quark OAM in the proton.

III.4
A
-Form Factor and Helicity-conserved Amplitude at Large
-t

As introduced in the Sec.
III.1
, the
A
-GFFs are related to the nucleon helicity-conserved amplitude.
The dominated contributions come from three-quark light-cone wave function with zero orbital angular momentum:

\displaystyle\langle P^{\prime}_{\uparrow}|_{1/2}T^{\mu\nu}_{a}|P_{\uparrow}\rangle_{1/2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\ \frac{[dx][dy][d^{2}\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}][d^{2}\boldsymbol{k}]}{24(x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}y_{1}y_{2}y_{3})^{1/2}}\psi_{1}^{*}(\kappa^{\prime}_{1},\kappa^{\prime}_{2},\kappa^{\prime}_{3})\psi_{1}(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2},\kappa_{3})

\displaystyle\times\big\langle\{y_{i}P^{\prime}+\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}_{i}\}\big|_{1/2}{T^{\mu\nu}_{a}}\big|\{x_{i}P+\boldsymbol{k}_{i}\}\big\rangle_{1/2}\ .

(41)

At large momentum transfer, the transverse momenta of the partons in the above amplitude can be neglected.
Therefore,

\displaystyle A_{a}(t)\bar{u}_{\uparrow}(P^{\prime})\gamma^{(\mu}\bar{P}^{\nu)}u_{\uparrow}(P)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\ \frac{[dx][dy]}{96(x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}y_{1}y_{2}y_{3})^{1/2}}\Phi_{3}^{*}(y_{1},y_{2},y_{3})\Phi_{3}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})

\displaystyle\times\big\langle uud-udu,\{y_{i}P^{\prime}\}\big|{T^{\mu\nu}_{a}}\big|uud-udu,\{x_{i}P\}\big\rangle\ ,

(42)

where
\Phi_{3}
is the twist-three light-cone amplitude of proton
Braun et al. 1999
:

\displaystyle\Phi_{3}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})=2\int[d^{2}\boldsymbol{k}]\psi_{1}(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2},\kappa_{3})~.

(43)

The label
uud
and
udd
denote the relevant Fock state configurations in Eq. (
39
):

\displaystyle|uud,\{p_{i}\}\rangle=\frac{\epsilon_{abc}}{\sqrt{6}}\hat{u}_{a,\uparrow}^{\dagger}(p_{1})\hat{u}_{b,\downarrow}^{\dagger}(p_{2})\hat{d}_{c,\uparrow}^{\dagger}(p_{3})\big|0\big\rangle~,

\displaystyle|udu,\{p_{i}\}\rangle=\frac{\epsilon_{abc}}{\sqrt{6}}\hat{u}_{a,\uparrow}^{\dagger}(p_{1})\hat{d}_{b,\downarrow}^{\dagger}(p_{2})\hat{u}_{c,\uparrow}^{\dagger}(p_{3})\big|0\big\rangle~.

(44)

Since the helicity and flavor of a massless quark are conserved in the fermion line, there is no interaction between these two configurations. Both of them share the same kind of diagrams without any quark line crossing, as presented in Fig.
1
.
The second configuration in Eq. (
44
) have additional diagrams with the final
u
quarks exchanged from Fig.
1
. Therefore, the hard partonic part for the EMT amplitude can be expressed as

\displaystyle H_{a}^{\mu\nu}=2{\cal H}_{a}^{\mu\nu}+{{\cal H}^{\prime}}^{\mu\nu}_{a},

(45)

where

\displaystyle{\cal H}_{a}^{\mu\nu}(\{p_{i}\})=\big\langle uud,\{y_{i}P^{\prime}\}\big|{T^{\mu\nu}_{a}}\big|uud,\{x_{i}P\}\big\rangle\ ,

(46)

and
{\cal H}^{\prime}_{a}
is obtained from
{\cal H}_{a}
by interchanging
y_{1}
and
y_{3}
.

As shown in Fig.
1
, for the quark contribution, the numbered places represent the insertion of the quark EMT operator. Similarly, we can have gluon contribution, where the insertion only appears on the two gluon propagators. The total contribution to the GFF comes from the sum of all these diagrams with all possible insertions. The diagrams in Fig.
1
represent the two different configurations: (1) two gluons attach to the spin-down quark; (2) two gluons attach to the spin-up quark. All other diagrams can be obtained by an exchange of the quark lines
(1\leftrightarrow 3)
.

Figure 1:
Generic diagrams for gravitational form factor calculations. The numbered places represent the insertion of the energy-momentum tensor operator. The three quark lines denote the leading light-cone wave function configuration for the proton state.

Since the partons are approximately on-shell, we use the covariant perturbation theory in Feynman gauge to compute the partonic amplitudes.
The technical part of the computation is to evaluate the Dirac structure which involve three fermion lines. In general, they have the following forms:

\displaystyle\bar{u}_{\uparrow}(p^{\prime}_{1})\Gamma_{1}u_{\uparrow}(p_{1})\ ,

\displaystyle\bar{u}_{\downarrow}(p^{\prime}_{2})\Gamma_{2}u_{\downarrow}(p_{2})\ ,

\displaystyle\bar{u}_{\uparrow}(p^{\prime}_{3})\Gamma_{3}u_{\uparrow}(p_{3})\ .

(47)

Since we only concentrate on the Dirac structure, all irrelevant factors are included in the
\Gamma_{1,2,3}
, which denote the product of
\gamma
-matrix with odd number from three fermion lines presented in the Fig.
1
. All the diagrams share the same color factor,

\displaystyle C_{B}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{6}\epsilon_{ijk}\epsilon_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}k^{\prime}}(T^{a})_{i^{\prime}i}(T^{a}T^{b})_{j^{\prime}j}(T^{b})_{k^{\prime}k}=\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{2}\ ,

(48)

where
T^{a}
is the generator in the fundamental representation.
Note that the helicity amplitudes in Eq. (
47
) have a pair of quark lines with zero total helicity. One can apply the following strategy to combine them into a Dirac trace. First, we move out the momentum faction inside the spinor by
u_{\lambda}(x_{i}P)=\sqrt{x_{i}}u_{\lambda}(P)
, and similarly for
\bar{u}_{\lambda}(y_{i}P^{\prime})
. The next step is to utilize the identity

\displaystyle\bar{u}_{\uparrow/\downarrow}(P^{\prime})\Gamma u_{\uparrow/\downarrow}(P)=\bar{u}_{\downarrow/\uparrow}(P)\Gamma_{R}u_{\downarrow/\uparrow}(P^{\prime})\ ,

(49)

where
\Gamma_{R}
is obtained from
\Gamma
by reversing the order of the
\gamma
-matrix. Then we can use the spin density matrix for massless spinor

\displaystyle u_{\uparrow/\downarrow}(P)\bar{u}_{\uparrow/\downarrow}(P)=\frac{1}{2}(1\pm\gamma_{5})\not{P}\ ,

(50)

which will turn two fermion lines with opposite helicity of Eq. (
47
) into a Dirac trace. After that, Eq. (
47
) becomes

\displaystyle\text{Tr}\bigg[\frac{1}{2}(1+\gamma_{5})\not{P}^{\prime}\Gamma_{1}\frac{1}{2}(1+\gamma_{5})\not{P}\Gamma_{2R}\bigg]\ \bar{u}_{\uparrow}(P^{\prime})\Gamma_{3}u_{\uparrow}(P)\ .

(51)

Evaluating the trace, contracting the Lorentz indices, and applying the on-shell condition for the spinor
\not{P}u_{\lambda}(P)=0,\ \bar{u}_{\lambda}(P^{\prime})\not{P}^{\prime}=0
, we arrive at the following four Dirac bilinear:

\displaystyle E_{1}\bar{u}_{\uparrow}(P^{\prime})\bar{P}^{(\mu}\gamma^{\nu)}u_{\uparrow}(P)+E_{2}i\bar{P}^{(\mu}\epsilon^{\nu)\sigma PP^{\prime}}\bar{u}_{\uparrow}(P^{\prime})\gamma_{\sigma}u_{\uparrow}(P)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle E_{3}\bar{u}_{\uparrow}(P^{\prime})\Delta^{(\mu}\gamma^{\nu)}u_{\uparrow}(P)+E_{4}i\Delta^{(\mu}\epsilon^{\nu)\sigma PP^{\prime}}\bar{u}_{\uparrow}(P^{\prime})\gamma_{\sigma}u_{\uparrow}(P)\ ,

(52)

where
E_{i}\equiv E_{i}(\{x_{i}\},\{y_{i}\})
. In particular, from the explicit calculation, one can find that
E_{3}
and
E_{4}
have the form as
x_{i}-y_{i}
, and hence the second line with
\Delta
factor should vanish due to the symmetry between the initial and final state. Furthermore, one can eliminate the Levi-Civita tensor by the identity:

\displaystyle\bar{u}_{\uparrow}(P^{\prime})\gamma^{\mu}u_{\uparrow}(P)=\frac{2i}{-t}\epsilon^{\mu\nu PP^{\prime}}\bar{u}_{\uparrow}(P^{\prime})\gamma_{\nu}u_{\uparrow}(P)\ ,

(53)

which can be verified by using the explicit expression of the Dirac spinors given in Eq. (
A
).
Therefore, one finally obtain the Dirac structure in the proton helicity-conserved amplitude as expected:

\displaystyle E(\{x_{i}\},\{y_{i}\})\bar{u}_{\uparrow}(P^{\prime})\gamma^{(\mu}\bar{P}^{\nu)}u_{\uparrow}(P)\ .

(54)

The hard coefficients for
A_{a}(t)
are straightforward to find out.
After summarizing the results, the
A_{a}(t)
GFF for the nucleon has the following factorization formula at large momentum transfer:

\displaystyle A_{a}(t)=

\displaystyle\int[dx][dy]\Phi_{3}^{*}(y_{1},y_{2},y_{3})\Phi_{3}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})

\displaystyle\times\ {\cal A}_{a}(\{x\},\{y\})\ ,

(55)

where
\{x\}=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
,
[dx]=dx_{1}dx_{2}dx_{3}\delta(1-x_{1}-x_{2}-x_{3})
, and
\Phi_{3}(x_{i})
is the twist-three light-cone amplitude of the proton
Braun et al. 1999
. The hard function can be written as

\displaystyle{\cal A}_{a}(\{x\},\{y\})=\frac{4\pi^{2}\alpha_{s}^{2}C_{B}^{2}}{3t^{2}}\left(2{\cal A}_{a}+{\cal A}^{\prime}_{a}\right),

(56)

where
{\cal A}^{\prime}_{a}
is obtained from
{\cal A}_{a}
by interchanging
y_{1}
and
y_{3}
.
C_{B}=2/3
is the color factor. For the gluon part,
{\cal A}_{g}
can be written as

\displaystyle{\cal A}_{g}=

\displaystyle\frac{x_{1}+y_{1}}{\bar{x}_{1}\bar{y}_{1}x_{1}x_{3}y_{1}y_{3}}+\frac{x_{1}+y_{1}}{\bar{x}_{1}\bar{y}_{1}x_{1}x_{2}y_{1}y_{2}}+(1\leftrightarrow 3).

(57)

For the quark part, the hard coefficient can be expressed as

\displaystyle{\cal A}_{q}=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{x_{3}y_{3}\bar{x}_{1}^{2}\bar{y}_{1}^{2}}+\frac{2}{x_{2}y_{2}\bar{x}_{3}^{2}\bar{y}_{3}^{2}}+\frac{2(\bar{y}_{3}+\bar{x}_{1})-1}{x_{1}x_{3}y_{1}y_{3}\bar{x}_{1}\bar{y}_{3}}

\displaystyle+\frac{\bar{x}_{1}-x_{1}}{x_{2}x_{3}y_{2}y_{3}\bar{x}_{1}\bar{y}_{3}}+\frac{\bar{y}_{1}-y_{1}}{x_{2}x_{3}y_{2}y_{3}\bar{x}_{3}\bar{y}_{1}}+(1\leftrightarrow 3)\ .

(58)

In summary, the quark and gluon
A
-form factors have the same power behavior at large momentum transfer.

III.5
Helicity-flip Amplitude at Large
-t

Now we turn to the computation of
B,C
and
\overline{C}
form factors at large
-t
. As shown in Eq. (
25
), they can be extracted respectively from the proton helicity-flip amplitude of EMT with appropriate tensor projections.
The dominant contributions come from the amplitude components with one unit of OAM from either initial or final state nucleons. Since they yield the same contribution, we only focus on one of them:

\displaystyle\langle P^{\prime}_{\uparrow}|^{l^{\prime}_{z}=0}_{1/2}T^{\mu\nu}_{a}|P_{\downarrow}\rangle_{1/2}^{l_{z}=-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\ \frac{[dx][dy][d^{2}\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}][d^{2}\boldsymbol{k}]}{24(x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}y_{1}y_{2}y_{3})^{1/2}}\big[k_{1L}\psi_{3}+k_{2L}\psi_{4}\big](\kappa_{2},\kappa_{1},\kappa_{3})

\displaystyle\times\psi_{1}^{*}(\kappa^{\prime}_{1},\kappa^{\prime}_{2},\kappa^{\prime}_{3})\big\langle\{y_{i}P^{\prime}+\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}_{i}\}\big|_{1/2}{T^{\mu\nu}_{a}}\big|\{x_{i}P+\boldsymbol{k}_{i}\}\big\rangle_{1/2}\ ,

(59)

where the factors
k_{L}=k^{x}-ik^{y}
are manifestations of the quark OAM with
l_{z}=-1
from the initial state proton. The partonic amplitudes have the same structure as that in Eq. (
45
) except that the transverse momenta of the initial state partons can not be ignored.
Otherwise, the amplitudes will vanish due to the OAM factor
k_{L}
in the phase-space integral. Instead, we need to evaluate the following partonic amplitude:

\displaystyle\big\langle uud,\{y_{i}P^{\prime}\}\big|\Gamma_{\mu\nu}T^{\mu\nu}_{a}\big|uud,\{x_{i}P+\boldsymbol{k}_{i}\}\big\rangle,

(60)

where we have used the label
\Gamma_{\mu\nu}
to represent the tensor projection for the
B,C,
or
\overline{C}
form factors in Eq. (
25
). The partonic state
|uud\rangle
has been defined in Eq. (
44
). Similar to the
A
-form factor, there are three fermion lines responsible for the three-valence-quark configuration for each diagram in Fig.
1
. These perturbative diagrams share the same color factor as that in Eq. (
48

47

49
-
51
) and Eq. (
53

In order to obtain the leading contribution at large
-t
, we should expand the transverse momenta in terms of
\boldsymbol{k}_{i}/\sqrt{-t}
. The linear terms of
\boldsymbol{k}_{i}
will contribute at this order. In particular, one can apply the following formula for the spinor:

\displaystyle u_{\lambda}(x_{i}P+\boldsymbol{k}_{i})

\displaystyle\approx u_{\lambda}(x_{i}P)+\frac{\not{k}_{i}\not{P}^{\prime}}{x_{i}P^{\prime}\cdot P}u_{\lambda}(x_{i}P)\ ,

(61)

which leads to a linear expansion term.

Applying the transverse momentum expansion of the partonic scattering, we obtain the following expression for the linear expansion term,

\displaystyle{\cal H}_{1}(\{x_{i}\},\{y_{i}\})\bar{u}_{\uparrow}(P^{\prime})\not{\boldsymbol{k}}_{1}u_{\uparrow}(P)

\displaystyle+{\cal H}_{3}(\{x_{i}\},\{y_{i}\})\bar{u}_{\uparrow}(P^{\prime})\not{\boldsymbol{k}}_{3}u_{\uparrow}(P)\ ,

(62)

where
\boldsymbol{k}_{2}=-\boldsymbol{k}_{1}-\boldsymbol{k}_{3}
has been used to simplify the final results. To obtain the helicity-flip like structure, one can use the identity

\gamma^{i}u_{\uparrow}(p)=(\delta^{ix}+i\delta^{iy})u_{\downarrow}(p)
for
i=x,y
,
and hence the bilinear spinor can be further reduced as

\bar{u}_{\uparrow}(P^{\prime})\not{\boldsymbol{k}}_{i}u_{\uparrow}(P)=k_{iR}\bar{u}_{\uparrow}(P^{\prime})u_{\downarrow}(P)
.
Therefore, the GFFs can be expressed as

\displaystyle\langle P^{\prime}_{\uparrow}|^{l^{\prime}_{z}=0}_{1/2}\Gamma_{\mu\nu}T^{\mu\nu}_{a}|P_{\downarrow}\rangle_{1/2}^{l_{z}=-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{u}_{\uparrow}(P^{\prime})u_{\downarrow}(P)\int[dx][dy][d^{2}\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}][d^{2}\boldsymbol{k}]\psi_{1}^{*}(\kappa^{\prime}_{1},\kappa^{\prime}_{2},\kappa^{\prime}_{3})

\displaystyle\times\big[k_{2L}\psi_{3}(\kappa_{2},\kappa_{1},\kappa_{3})+k_{1L}\psi_{4}(\kappa_{2},\kappa_{1},\kappa_{3})\big]

\displaystyle\times[k_{1R}{H}_{1}(\{x_{i}\},\{y_{i}\})+k_{3R}{H}_{3}(\{x_{i}\},\{y_{i}\})]

(63)

where
k_{R/L}=k^{x}\pm ik^{y}
. The hard parts have the form as
H_{i}=2{\cal H}_{i}+{\cal H}^{\prime}_{i}
and
{\cal H}^{\prime}_{i}={\cal H}_{i}(y_{1}\leftrightarrow y_{3})
where the
x_{i},y_{i}
-dependences from the phase integral are also included. On the other hand, from the property of the transverse-momentum integral, one has
\int[d^{2}\boldsymbol{k}]k_{iL}k_{jR}f(\boldsymbol{k}_{i}\cdot\boldsymbol{k}_{j})=\int[d^{2}\boldsymbol{k}]\ \boldsymbol{k}_{i}\cdot\boldsymbol{k}_{j}f(\boldsymbol{k}_{i}\cdot\boldsymbol{k}_{j})

Belitsky et al. 2003

\displaystyle\Phi_{4}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})=

\displaystyle 2\int\frac{[d^{2}\boldsymbol{k}]}{Mx_{3}}\ \boldsymbol{k}_{3}\cdot\big[\boldsymbol{k}_{1}\psi_{3}(\kappa_{2},\kappa_{1},\kappa_{3})

\displaystyle\qquad+\boldsymbol{k}_{2}\psi_{4}(\kappa_{2},\kappa_{1},\kappa_{3})\big]\ ,

\displaystyle\Psi_{4}(x_{2},x_{1},x_{3})=

\displaystyle 2\int\frac{[d^{2}\boldsymbol{k}]}{Mx_{1}}\ \boldsymbol{k}_{1}\cdot\big[\boldsymbol{k}_{2}\psi_{3}(\kappa_{2},\kappa_{1},\kappa_{3})

\displaystyle\qquad+\boldsymbol{k}_{1}\psi_{4}(\kappa_{2},\kappa_{1},\kappa_{3})\big]\ .

(64)

Braun et al. 2000

\displaystyle\langle P^{\prime}_{\uparrow}|^{l^{\prime}_{z}=0}_{1/2}\Gamma_{\mu\nu}T^{\mu\nu}_{a}|P_{\downarrow}\rangle_{1/2}^{l_{z}=-1}

\displaystyle=\int[dx][dy]\left\{x_{3}\Phi_{4}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})H_{\Phi a}(\{x\},\{y\})\right.

\displaystyle\left.+x_{1}\Psi_{4}(x_{2},x_{1},x_{3})H_{\Psi a}(\{x\},\{y\})\right\}\ \Phi_{3}(y_{1},y_{2},y_{3})\ ,

(65)

where
\Phi_{3}
is the twist-3 distribution amplitude and
\Phi_{4},\Psi_{4}
are the twist-4 distribution amplitudes defined above. They embrace the non-perturbative effects of the GFFs at large
-t
.
H_{\Phi a},H_{\Psi a}
are the hard coefficients and can be calculated order by order in principle.

\ln^{2}(-t/\Lambda^{2}_{c})/(-t)^{2}

and play an important role in describing the scaling behavior of

at the large momentum transfer as compared to the experimental data
Belitsky et al. 2003
;
Puckett et al. 2010
.

V
Scalar Form Factors

The scalar form factors(FFs), defined as the hadron form factors of the
F^{2}
operator, are also important in GFFs physics, since it has the close connection to the trace anomaly, as shown in Eq. (
11
). Following the discussion in the previous sections, similar perturbative analysis can be applied to the scalar FFs at large
-t
. We will first study the pion case, which will be simple and interesting, and then turn back to the proton case.

V.1
Pion

The scalar FF for pion,
G_{\pi}(t)
, is defined from the parameterization of the
F^{2}
transition matrix:

\displaystyle\langle P^{\prime}|F^{a}_{\mu\nu}(0)F^{a\mu\nu}(0)|P\rangle=m_{\pi}^{2}G_{\pi}(t)~.

(105)

At large
(-t)
, the factorization analysis can be applied.
At the leading order of
\alpha_{s}
, there is only one diagram that contributes, see, for example, Ref.
Tong et al. 2021
. Therefore,

\displaystyle m_{\pi}^{2}

\displaystyle G_{\pi}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int dxdy\ \phi^{*}(y)\phi(x)\frac{\text{tr}[t^{a}t^{a}]}{3}\ \frac{g_{s}^{2}\text{Tr}[\not{P}^{\prime}\gamma_{\sigma}\not{P}\gamma_{\rho}]}{4(q_{1}^{2}+i\epsilon)(q_{2}^{2}+i\epsilon)}

\displaystyle\Big[(-q_{1}^{\mu}g^{\nu\sigma}+q_{1}^{\nu}g^{\mu\sigma})(-q_{2\mu}g_{\nu}^{\ \rho}+q_{2\nu}g_{\mu}^{\ \rho})+(\sigma\leftrightarrow\rho)\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int dxdy\ \phi^{*}(y)\phi(x)\ 8\pi\alpha_{s}C_{F}\left(\frac{1}{x\bar{y}}+\frac{1}{\bar{x}y}\right)+{\cal O}(t^{-1})~,

(106)

where
q_{1}=xP-yP^{\prime},\ q_{2}=\bar{x}P-\bar{y}P^{\prime}
are the momenta that flow into the
F^{2}
operator.
\phi(x)
is the pion twist-2 light-cone distribution amplitude, defined as

\displaystyle\phi(x)i\sqrt{6}P^{+}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\frac{d\xi^{-}}{2\pi}e^{ixP^{+}\xi^{-}}\langle 0|\bar{d}(0)[0,\xi^{-}]\gamma^{+}\gamma_{5}u(\xi^{-})|\pi(P)\rangle~,

(107)

with the normalization

\int^{1}_{0}dx\ \phi(x)=\frac{f_{\pi}}{\sqrt{6}},

where
f_{\pi}\approx 92.3
MeV is the pion decay constant.

From the above results, it is interesting to see that the pion scalar form factor has no power behavior with respect to
-t
, which is different from the pion
A,C
form factor with
A^{\pi}_{g,q}\sim C^{\pi}_{g,q}\sim 1/(-t)

Tong et al. 2021
.
It is also expected that higher order contributions will not change the power behavior.
At leading order perturbation theory, the possible
(-t)
-dependence only comes from the running of the strong coupling
\alpha_{s}
with
\mu^{2}
taken as
-t
.
There are also evolution effects hidden in the pion DAs. Hence, to make a sensible numerical estimate on the large
-t
behavior of the pion scalar form factor, more details on the pion DAs are needed.

Similar to the proton DAs introduced in the previous sections, the pion DA can also be expressed as the series in the the eigenfunctions of the leading order evolution equation (e.g.
Fagundes et al. 2020
):

\displaystyle\phi(x,\mu^{2})=

\displaystyle\sqrt{6}f_{\pi}x\bar{x}\Big[1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{2n}(\mu^{2}_{0})L^{\gamma^{(0)}_{2n}/b}_{2n}(\mu^{2})C_{2n}^{3/2}(x-\bar{x})\Big]~,

(108)

where
C_{n}^{\alpha}(x)
are the Gegenbauer polynomials.
L_{n}(\mu^{2})
has been given in Eq. (
103
) and represents the logarithmic factor for the one-loop evolution from the default scale
\mu_{0}^{2}
to the varied scale
\mu^{2}
.
\gamma_{n}
is the anomalous dimension given by

\displaystyle\gamma^{(0)}_{n}=4C_{F}\left(\psi(n+2)+\gamma_{E}-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{1}{2(n+1)(n+2)}\right)~,

(109)

where
\psi(n)
is the digamma function and
b=11-2/3n_{f}
. When
\mu^{2}
approaches to
+\infty
, all the logarithmic factors are suppressed, and the first term determines the asymptotic form of the DA, i.e.,
\phi^{asy}(x)=\sqrt{6}f_{\pi}x(1-x)
. The coefficients
a_{2n}
, known as Gegenbauer moments, describe the deviation from the asymptotic DA.

Following Eq. (
11
), it is convenient to express the pion transition matrix element of the trace anomaly in terms of the pion scalar form factor in massless-quark limit. With the Gegenbauer expansion of the pion DAs in Eq. (
106
) and
\mu^{2}=-t
, a simple expression can be obtained

\displaystyle\langle P^{\prime}|\frac{\beta(g)}{2g}F^{2}|P\rangle=

\displaystyle-3\alpha_{s}^{2}(-t)C_{F}\left(11-\frac{2n_{f}}{3}\right)f_{\pi}^{2}

\displaystyle\times\Big[1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{2n}(\mu_{0}^{2})L^{\gamma_{2n}/b}_{2n}(-t)\Big]^{2}\ ,

(110)

where only the sum of the Gegenbauer moments is relevant. The above result shows that the pion trace anomaly matrix element should be negative in the large
-t
limit. However, it is known that at zero momentum transfer, the pion trace anomaly should be positive
Ji 1995a
;
He et al. 2021
.
The above results reveal the fact that the trace anomaly matrix element should change sign from the large
(-t)
to small
(-t)
.
Preliminary lattice simulation also indicates this behavior
Liu
. Sign changes have also been observed in the gluon
C
form factor for both pion and proton. Later we will also show the proton scalar form factor experiences the sign change.

With a truncation on the Gegenbauer-polynomial expansions and the explicit setting on the values of the coefficients
a_{2n}
at the default scale
\mu_{0}^{2}
, several models on the pion DA can be obtained. Here we follow the models adopted in
Gao et al. 2022
. In Model I
Cheng et al. 2020
, the coefficients are set as

\displaystyle\left\{a_{2},a_{4},a_{6},a_{8}\right\}\left(\mu_{0}^{2}\right)\approx\{0.269,0.185,0.141,0.049\}~,

(111)

where
\mu_{0}=1
GeV and the uncertainties are neglected here. These values are fitted from the QCD light-cone-sum-rule results for the pion electromagnetic form factor to its measured data
Cheng et al. 2020
. In model II
Bakulev et al. 2001
;
Mikhailov et al. 2016
;
Stefanis 2020
, the coefficients are set as

\displaystyle\left\{a_{2},a_{4}\right\}\left(\mu_{0}^{2}\right)\approx\left\{0.203,-0.143\right\}

(112)

with
\mu_{0}=1
GeV. The values are taken from an estimation from QCD sum rules with nonlocal condensate
Mikhailov and Radyushkin 1992
;
Bakulev et al. 2001
;
Mikhailov et al. 2016
;
Stefanis 2020
. Besides the Gegenbauer-type models, the Ads/QCD inspired model(Model III) are also applied
Brodsky and de Teramond 2008
;
Gao et al. 2022
:

\displaystyle\phi\left(x,\mu^{2}_{0}\right)=\frac{f_{\pi}}{\sqrt{2N_{c}}}\frac{\Gamma\left(2+2\alpha_{\pi}\right)}{\Gamma^{2}\left(1+\alpha_{\pi}\right)}(x\bar{x})^{\alpha_{\pi}}

(113)

with
\alpha_{\pi}\left(\mu_{0}\right)\approx 0.422
.

V.2
Nucleon

Now we turn to the nucleon case. The nucleon scalar form factor
G_{p}(t)
is defined by

\displaystyle\langle P^{\prime},s^{\prime}|F^{a}_{\mu\nu}(0)F^{a\mu\nu}(0)|P,s\rangle=G_{p}(t)M\bar{u}_{s^{\prime}}(P^{\prime})u_{s}(P)~,

(114)

where
M
is the nucleon mass and
u_{s}(P)
is the Dirac spinor with spin
s
. From the Dirac structure,
G_{p}(t)
is relevant to the nucleon helicity-flip amplitude at the high energy. Hence, we can apply the same methodology introduced in Sec.
III.5
to investigate the large momentum transfer behavior of
G_{p}(t)
. It results in a factorization formula as follows:

\displaystyle G_{p}(t)=

\displaystyle\int[dx][dy]\{x_{3}\Phi_{4}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}){\cal G}_{\Phi}(\{x\},\{y\})

\displaystyle+x_{1}\Psi_{4}(x_{2},x_{1},x_{3}){\cal G}_{\Psi}(\{x\},\{y\})\}\Phi_{3}(y_{1},y_{2},y_{3})~,

(115)

where the hard function has the following structure

\displaystyle{\cal G}=2{\cal H}+{\cal H}^{\prime}

(116)

and
H^{\prime}=H(y_{1}\leftrightarrow y_{3})
.
Explicitly, we have

\displaystyle{\cal H}_{\Psi}=

\displaystyle\frac{C_{B}^{2}M_{p}}{6t^{2}}(4\pi\alpha_{s})^{2}\Big[x_{3}\left(\left(y_{1}-y_{3}\right)\bar{x}_{1}+x_{1}y_{2}\right)T_{1}-T_{3}

\displaystyle+\bar{x}_{3}\left(y_{3}\bar{x}_{3}+x_{3}\bar{y}_{3}\right)\tilde{T}_{1}+x_{3}\left(y_{2}\bar{x}_{2}+x_{2}\bar{y}_{2}\right)(\tilde{T}_{2}-T_{2})

\displaystyle+x_{3}\left(y_{1}-\bar{y}_{1}\right)(T_{4}+T_{5})+\left(y_{3}\bar{x}_{3}+x_{3}\bar{y}_{3}\right)(\tilde{T}_{4}+\tilde{T}_{5})\Big]~,

where
{\cal H}_{\Phi}={\cal H}_{\Psi}(1\leftrightarrow 3)
and the functions
T_{i}
are defined by

\displaystyle T_{1}=\frac{1}{x_{1}x_{3}^{2}y_{1}y_{3}^{2}\bar{x}_{1}^{2}\bar{y}_{1}}~,\quad T_{2}=\frac{1}{x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}^{2}y_{2}y_{3}^{2}\bar{x}_{2}\bar{y}_{2}}~,\quad

\displaystyle T_{3}=\frac{1}{x_{1}x_{2}y_{1}y_{2}\bar{x}_{1}^{2}\bar{y}_{1}}~,\quad T_{4}=\frac{1}{x_{1}x_{3}^{2}y_{1}y_{3}\bar{x}_{1}\bar{y}_{1}^{2}}~,

\displaystyle T_{5}=\frac{1}{x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}y_{1}y_{2}\bar{x}_{1}\bar{y}_{1}^{2}}~,\quad\tilde{T}_{i}=T_{i}(1\leftrightarrow 3)~.

(118)

The above calculation reveals that the nucleon scalar form factor has the same power behavior as
\overline{C}_{g,q}
, i.e.,
G_{p}(t)\sim 1/(-t)^{2}
and hence

VI
Conclusion

In this paper, we have performed a detailed perturbative analysis of the gravitation form factors for nucleon at large momentum transfer, which results in the factorizations formulas of the GFFs in terms of the twist-3 or twist-4 nucleon distribution amplitude. We derived the hard coefficients at the leading order explicitly.

For
A_{q,g}
form factors, we have demonstrated that they are related to the helicity-conserved amplitude and the quark and gluon contributions both scale
1/(-t)^{2}
. We have also computed
B_{q,g},C_{q,g},\overline{C}_{q,g}
form factors separately from the helicity-flip amplitude. Multiple consistent checks have been performed. We found that
C_{q,g},B_{g,q}
are power suppressed as compared to the
A_{q,g}^{p}
.
\overline{C}_{q,g}
scale as
A_{q,g}^{p}
due to parameterization and the total
\overline{C}
vanishes as expected. Because of the end point singularities, the
B_{q,g},C_{q,g},\overline{C}_{q,g}
form factors receive additional logarithmic contributions.

We have also computed the scalar form factors
\langle P^{\prime}|\frac{\beta(g)}{2g}F^{2}|P\rangle
for pion and proton and found out that both are negative at large
(-t)
. It is understood that they are related to the hadron masses at zero momentum transfer. Therefore, there must be a sign change at lower
(-t)
. This may lead to a nontrivial mass distributions of hadrons.

Based on the models for the twist-3 and twist-4 distribution amplitudes, we have performed numeric estimates for the quark and gluon GFFs in the perturbative region of large momentum transfer. It will be interesting to test these predictions in future experiments.

Two-timescale weighted sum-rate maximization for large cellular and cell-free massive MIMO
Lorenzo Miretti 1 , Emil Björnson 2 , Sławomir Stańczak 1

Abstract
We reconsider the problem of joint power control and beamforming design to maximize the weighted sum rate in large and potentially cell-free massive MIMO networks. In contrast to the available short-term methods, where an iterative algorithm is run for every instantaneous channel realization, we derive an iterative algorithm that can be run only sporadically leveraging known channel statistics, with minor performance loss. In addition, our algorithm also applies to the design of non-trivial cooperative beamforming schemes subject to limited sharing of instantaneous channel state information. Furthermore, our algorithm generalizes and outperforms the competing long-term methods from the massive MIMO literature, which are restricted to long-term power control only or to long-term joint power control and large-scale fading decoding design.

I
Introduction

This study introduces and addresses a novel two-timescale formulation of the classical weighted sum-rate maximization problem in multi-user multiple-input multiple-output (MU-MIMO) wireless systems. By focusing on the uplink of a system with
K
single-antenna users and a base station equipped with
M
antennas, and by assuming standard linear receiver processing and single-user coding schemes that treat interference as noise, this classical problem is commonly formulated as

\displaystyle\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{p}\in\mathbbmss{R}_{+}^{K}\\
\bm{v}_{1},\ldots,\bm{v}_{K}\in\mathbbmss{C}^{M}\end{subarray}}{\text{maximize}}

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\omega_{k}\log(1+\mathsf{SINR}^{\mathsf{inst}}_{k}(\bm{H},\bm{v}_{k},\bm{p}))

(1)

\displaystyle\text{subject to}

\displaystyle(\forall k\in\mathcal{K})~p_{k}\leq P,

where
\mathcal{K}=\{1,\ldots,K\}
is the set of user indexes,
(\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{K})\in\mathbbmss{R}_{+}^{K}
and
P\in\mathbbmss{R}_{+}
are problem parameters, and
\mathsf{SINR}^{\mathsf{inst}}_{k}
denotes the instanteous SINR of user
k
for given MU-MIMO channel matrix
\bm{H}\in\mathbbmss{C}^{K\times M}
, power control coefficients
\bm{p}=(p_{1},\ldots,p_{K})\in\mathbbmss{R}_{+}^{K}
, and receive beamformers
\bm{v}_{1},\ldots,\bm{v}_{K}\in\mathbbmss{C}^{M}
. Although computing an optimal solution to Problem (
1
) is notoriously challenging, efficient stationary solutions can be obtained using the many iterative algorithms developed in the last two decades
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
]
. These methods apply also to the many variants of (
1
) covering, e.g., downlink operations and imperfect channel state information (CSI).

The main working assumption of this study, which is also ubiquitous in the cellular and cell-free massive MIMO literature
[
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
]
, is that the above algorithms for addressing (
1
) do not scale well to modern wireless systems, such as those building on the cell-free
[
8
,
9
]
or ultra-large massive MIMO concept
[
10
]
. The first challenge is the excessive computational complexity associated with running an iterative algorithm
for every channel realization
. The second challenge is the excessive control signaling overhead for collecting the necessary algorithm input
\bm{H}
at a central processing unit and for distributing the output
\bm{p},\bm{v}_{1},\ldots,\bm{v}_{K}
to the appropriate network entities,
for every channel realization
. Importantly, note that all the above operations must be completed under strict latency constraints, since the optimized powers
\bm{p}
must be conveyed to the users within the channel coherence time.

I-A

Two-timescale formulation

To mitigate the abovementioned scalability issues of the approach in (
1
), which we refer to as the
short-term
approach, one possibility is to replace the instantaneous rate expressions in the objective with ergodic rate expressions of the type

\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\omega_{k}\mathsf{E}[\log(1+\mathsf{SINR}^{\mathsf{inst}}_{k}(\mathbbm{H},\mathbbm{v}_{k},\bm{p})],

(2)

where
\mathbbm{H}
is a random matrix,
\bm{p}
is a deterministic power vector, and the beamformers
\mathbbm{v}_{k}
are
functions
of the available instantaneous CSI (hence, they are random vectors). A key benefit of this two-timescale formulation, which we refer to as the
long-term
approach, is that the powers are optimized sporadically
for many channel realizations
, based on relatively slowly-varying channel statistics. The same benefit applies to the optimization of the beamformers, or, more precisely, of the
functions
mapping instantaneous CSI to beamforming weights. In addition, the long-term approach facilitates the introduction of functional constraints as in
[
11
]

[
12
]
to enforce distributed beamforming architectures with limited instantaneous CSI sharing, which are particularly relevant for cell-free systems since they allow to distribute the processing load and potentially reduce the fronthaul overhead.

However, the main disadvantage of long-term approaches based on (
2
) is that, mostly due to the expectation outside the logarithm, they typically lead to intractable optimization problems. Hence, to derive practical long-term power control algorithms, most studies in the cellular and cell-free massive MIMO literature replace (
2
) with the so-called use-and-then-forget (UatF) lower bound on the achievable ergodic rates
[
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
13
,
14
]
. For example, by fixing the beamformers,
[
9
, Sect. 3.4.2]
provides an iterative long-term power control algorithm for sum-rate maximization building on the weighted minimum mean-square error (W-MMSE) technique in
[
2
]
. The same technique is applied in
[
13
]
to perform long-term joint power control and large-scale fading decoding (LSFD) design, i.e., the design of a statistical combining stage. However, none of the available studies provide long-term joint power control and beamforming design algorithms for sum-rate maximization, i.e., a more general two-timescale variant of (
1
).

I-B

Contributions of this study

In this study we close the above gap in the literature by providing a long-term joint power control and beamforming design algorithm for sum-rate maximization. Our algorithm extends the state-of-the-art in
[
9
, Algorithm 3.4.2]
and
[
13
]
to include beamforming design, i.e., the optimization of the functions mapping instantaneous CSI to beamforming weights. Notably, our algorithm can be applied to both centralized and distributed beamforming architectures. Our derivation builds on the UatF bound and the W-MMSE technique
[
2
]
as in
[
9
,
13
]
, and on a recently identified general MMSE-SINR relation for the UatF bound
[
11
,
12
]
, reminiscent of the well-known MMSE-SINR relation for (
2
) and restricted to centralized beamforming. The distributed beamforming case is covered using the
team MMSE
framework recently developed in
[
11
]
, where power control was left as an open problem. To illustrate the application of our findings, we provide an updated numerical comparison between short-term and long-term approaches, and between small-cells and cell-free networks.

II
System model and problem statement

We consider the uplink of a large-scale MU-MIMO wireless system composed by
K
single-antenna users indexed by
\mathcal{K}:=\{1,\ldots,K\}
, and a radio access infrastructure equipped with a total number of antennas
M
indexed by
\mathcal{M}:=\{1,\ldots,M\}
. The
M
infrastructure antennas may be split among many geographically distributed access points equipped with a relatively low number of antennas as in the cell-free massive MIMO concept
[

,

, or colocated as in the ultra-large massive MIMO concept
[
10

. As customary in the massive MIMO literature, we assume for each time-frequency resource a standard synchronous

II-A

Ergodic achievable rates

For performance evaluation, we consider uplink ergodic achievable rates of the type in (
2
). More specifically, for each user
k\in\mathcal{K}
, we consider the classical expression given by
[
15
]

R_{k}(\mathbbm{v}_{k},\bm{p}):=\mathsf{E}\Bigg[\log\Bigg(1+\dfrac{p_{k}|\mathbbm{h}_{k}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbbm{v}_{k}|^{2}}{\underset{j\neq k}{\sum}p_{j}|\mathbbm{h}_{j}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbbm{v}_{k}|^{2}+\|\mathbbm{v}_{k}\|_{2}^{2}}\Bigg)\Bigg],

(3)

where
\bm{p}:=(p_{1},\ldots,p_{K})\in\mathbbmss{R}_{+}^{K}
collects the transmit powers,
\mathbbm{h}_{k}\in\mathcal{H}^{M}
is a random vector modeling the (noise normalized, without loss of generality) fading state between user
k
and all
M
infrastructure antennas, and
\mathbbm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{H}^{M}
models the beamforming vector which is applied by the infrastructure to process the received signals of potentially all
M
antennas to obtain a soft estimate of the transmit signal of user
k
. We stress that
\mathbbm{v}_{k}
is generally a function of the instantaneous CSI realizations, and hence it is denoted as a random vector. Since (
3
) is not well-defined for
\mathbbm{v}_{k}=\bm{0}
a.s., we let
(\forall\bm{p}\in\mathbbmss{R}_{+}^{K})

R_{k}(\bm{0},\bm{p}):=0
. For convenience, we also define the global channel matrix
\mathbbm{H}:=[\mathbbm{h}_{1},\ldots,\mathbbm{h}_{K}]
.

Furthermore, similar to
[
9
,
13
]
, we consider for optimization purposes the more tractable UatF bound
[
7
,
15
]

(\forall k\in\mathcal{K})(\forall\bm{p}\in\mathbbmss{R}_{+}^{K})(\forall\mathbbm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{H}^{M})

R_{k}^{\mathsf{UatF}}(\mathbbm{v}_{k},\bm{p}):=\log(1+\mathsf{SINR}_{k}(\mathbbm{v}_{k},\bm{p})),

(4)

where

\mathsf{SINR}_{k}(\mathbbm{v}_{k},\bm{p}):=\resizebox{15600900}{}{$\dfrac{p_{k}|\mathsf{E}[\mathbbm{h}_{k}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbbm{v}_{k}]|^{2}}{p_{k}\mathsf{V}(\mathbbm{h}_{k}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbbm{v}_{k})+\underset{j\neq k}{\sum}p_{j}\mathsf{E}[|\mathbbm{h}_{j}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbbm{v}_{k}|^{2}]+\mathsf{E}[\|\mathbbm{v}_{k}\|^{2}]}$}

if
\mathbbm{v}_{k}\neq\bm{0}
, and
\mathsf{SINR}_{k}(\bm{0},\bm{p}):=0
otherwise.

Remark 1
.

The UatF bound (
4
) is a lower bound on (
3
), i.e., it satisfies
[
15
]

(\forall k\in\mathcal{K})(\forall\bm{p}\in\mathbbmss{R}^{K}_{+})(\forall\mathbbm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{H}^{M})

~R_{k}^{\mathsf{UatF}}(\mathbbm{v}_{k},\bm{p})\leq R_{k}(\mathbbm{v}_{k},\bm{p}).

II-B

Weighted sum-rate maximization

Our target is to jointly optimize the uplink transmit powers and the receive beamformers according to a long-term variant of the weighted sum-rate criterion in (
1
). More precisely, under consideration is the following optimization problem:

\displaystyle\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{p}\in\mathbbmss{R}_{+}^{K}\\
\mathbbm{v}_{1},\ldots,\mathbbm{v}_{K}\in\mathcal{H}^{M}\end{subarray}}{\text{maximize}}

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\omega_{k}R_{k}^{\mathsf{UatF}}(\mathbbm{v}_{k},\bm{p})

(5)

\displaystyle\text{subject to}

\displaystyle(\forall k\in\mathcal{K})~p_{k}\leq P,

\displaystyle(\forall k\in\mathcal{K})~\mathbbm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k},

where
(\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{K})\in\mathbbmss{R}_{+}^{K}
is a given vector of positive weights,
R_{k}^{\mathsf{UatF}}(\mathbbm{v}_{k},\bm{p})
is given by (
4
), and where
\mathcal{V}_{k}\subseteq\mathcal{H}^{M}
denotes a given
information
constraint which can be used to limit the dependency of each entry of
\mathbbm{v}_{k}
to some given (and potentially different) instantaneous CSI. We point out that, if
\mathcal{V}_{k}=\mathcal{H}^{M}
, then
\mathbbm{v}_{k}
can be essentially any function of
\mathbbm{H}
, which is not practical (perfect CSI). Additional details are given in the next section. Note that a similar problem to (
5
) focusing on the max-min criterion is fully solved in
[
16
,
17
]
.

Remark 2
.

A well-known challenge in long-term approaches is the need to identify, estimate, and track some form of channel statistics that can be used to produce effective solutions. In contrast, short-term approaches can be often implemented based on minimal statistical information. However, the main working assumption of this study is that short-term approaches are impractical for large-scale systems, thus making long-term approaches not really an option but rather a necessity.

II-C

Information constraints

We model constraints related to practical centralized and distributed beamforming architectures in a unified way, following the approach introduced in
[
11
,
12
]
. We first partition the
M=NL
infrastructure antennas into
L
groups of
N
antennas, and assume that the corresponding entries of
\mathbbm{v}_{k}
are computed by
L
separate processing units. In the context of cell-free massive MIMO, these processing units can be directly mapped to access points. We further let each user
k\in\mathcal{K}
be jointly served by a subset
\mathcal{L}_{k}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,L\}
of these units. Given an arbitrarily distributed tuple
(\mathbbm{H},S_{1},\ldots,S_{L})
, where
S_{l}
is the instantaneous CSI at the
l
th processing unit (e.g., noisy measurements of portions of
\mathbbm{H}
), we then let
(\forall k\in\mathcal{K})

\mathcal{V}_{k}:=\mathcal{V}_{1,k}\times\ldots\times\mathcal{V}_{L,k},\quad\mathcal{V}_{l,k}:=\begin{cases}\mathcal{H}_{l}^{N}&\text{if }l\in\mathcal{L}_{k},\\
\{\bm{0}\}&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

(6)

where
\mathcal{H}_{l}^{N}\subseteq\mathcal{H}^{N}
denotes the set of
N
-tuples of
\Sigma_{l}
-measurable functions
\Omega\to\mathbbmss{C}^{N}
satisfying
(\forall\mathbbm{x}\in\mathcal{H}_{l}^{N})

\mathsf{E}[\|\mathbbm{x}\|^{2}]<\infty
, and where
\Sigma_{l}\subseteq\Sigma
is the sub-
\sigma
-algebra induced by the CSI
S_{l}
, which is also called the
information subfield
of the
l
th processing unit. The interested reader is referred to
[
12
]
for additional details on the above notions. However, we stress that these notions are by no means required for understanding the key results of this study. The crucial point is that, informally, the constraint set
\mathcal{V}_{k}
enforces the subvector of
\mathbbm{v}_{k}
applied by the
l
th processing unit to be a function of
S_{l}
only. Importantly, the above constraints cover all common models in the user-centric cell-free massive MIMO literature, including the case of (clustered) centralized beamforming architectures based on global CSI, and the case of (clustered) distributed beamforming architectures based on local CSI
[
9
]
.

III
Approximate solution

We now derive an iterative algorithm for computing a suboptimal solution to the long-term power control and beamforming design problem (
5
). The key technical novelty is the derivation of an equivalent problem formulation which extends the approaches in
[
9
, Sect. 3.4.2]
and
[
13
]
(based on the W-MMSE technique in
[
2
]
) to beamforming design. Our extension is enabled by a general MMSE-SINR relation recently identified in
[
11
,

III-A

SINR maximization via MSE minimization

Let us consider the MSE between the transmit signal
x_{k}\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,1)
of a user
k\in\mathcal{K}
and its soft estimate
\hat{x}_{k}:=\mathbbm{v}_{k}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbbm{y}
obtained from the received signal
\mathbbm{y}:=\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\sqrt{p_{k}}\mathbbm{h}_{k}x_{k}+\mathbbm{n}
with noise
\mathbbm{n}\sim\mathcal{CN}(\bm{0},\bm{I})
, where the noise and all user signals are mutually independent and independent of
(\mathbbm{H},\mathbbm{v}_{1},\ldots,\mathbbm{v}_{K})
. Specifically, let
(\forall k\in\mathcal{K})(\forall\mathbbm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k})(\forall\bm{p}\in\mathbbmss{R}_{+}^{K})

\mathsf{MSE}_{k}(\mathbbm{v}_{k},\bm{p}):=

\mathsf{E}[|x_{k}-\hat{x}_{k}|^{2}]\\
=\mathsf{E}\left[\|\bm{P}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbbm{H}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbbm{v}_{k}-\bm{e}_{k}\|_{2}^{2}\right]+\mathsf{E}\left[\|\mathbbm{v}_{k}\|^{2}\right],

(7)

where
\bm{P}:=\mathrm{diag}(\bm{p})
, and where the second equality can be verified via simple manipulations. We then have the following.

Proposition 1
.

For all
k\in\mathcal{K}
and
\bm{p}\in\mathbbmss{R}_{+}^{K}
,

1+\sup_{\mathbbm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}}\mathsf{SINR}_{k}(\mathbbm{v}_{k},\bm{p})=\frac{1}{\inf_{\mathbbm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}}\mathsf{MSE}_{k}(\mathbbm{v}_{k},\bm{p})}.

Furthermore,
\exists!\mathbbm{v}_{k}^{\star}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}
attaining
\inf_{\mathbbm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}}\mathsf{MSE}_{k}(\mathbbm{v}_{k},\bm{p})
. Moreover, this
\mathbbm{v}_{k}^{\star}
also attains
\sup_{\mathbbm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}}\mathsf{SINR}_{k}(\mathbbm{v}_{k},\bm{p})
.

Proof.

Similar to
[
12
]
, and reported in the appendix.∎

Remark 3
.

Proposition
1
should not be confused with the well-known MMSE-SINR relation for instantaneous rate expressions
[
2
]
, such as the argument of the expectation in (
3
) or in similar expressions covering noisy CSI
[
7
, Sect. 4.1]
, which applies to centralized beamforming architectures only. In contrast, the relation in Proposition
1
holds for the UatF bound (
2
), and it applies to arbitrary beamforming architectures, i.e., to arbitrary information constraints
\mathcal{V}_{k}
.

III-B

Equivalent problem formulation

Equipped with Proposition
1
, we now consider the following optimization problem:

\displaystyle\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{d}\in\mathbbmss{R}_{++}^{K},\;\bm{p}\in\mathbbmss{R}_{+}^{K}\\
\mathbbm{v}_{1},\ldots,\mathbbm{v}_{K}\in\mathcal{H}^{M}\end{subarray}}{\text{maximize}}

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\omega_{k}\left(\log(d_{k})-d_{k}\mathsf{MSE}_{k}(\mathbbm{v}_{k},\bm{p})\right)

(8)

\displaystyle\text{subject to}

\displaystyle(\forall k\in\mathcal{K})~p_{k}\leq P,

\displaystyle(\forall k\in\mathcal{K})~\mathbbm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k},

where
\mathsf{MSE}_{k}(\mathbbm{v}_{k},\bm{p})
is defined in (
7
). The above problem is equivalent to Problem (
5
), in the sense specified next.

Proposition 2
.

If a solution
(\bm{d}^{\star},\bm{p}^{\star},\mathbbm{v}_{1}^{\star},\ldots,\mathbbm{v}_{K}^{\star})
to Problem (
8
) exists, then
(\bm{p}^{\star},\mathbbm{v}_{1}^{\star},\ldots,\mathbbm{v}_{K}^{\star})
is also a solution to Problem (
5
).

Proof.

Assume that
(\bm{d}^{\star},\bm{p}^{\star},\mathbbm{v}_{1}^{\star},\ldots,\mathbbm{v}_{K}^{\star})
is a solution to Problem (
8
). First, we notice that
(\forall k\in\mathcal{K})~\mathsf{MSE}_{k}(\mathbbm{v}_{k}^{\star},\bm{p}^{\star})=\inf_{\mathbbm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}}\mathsf{MSE}_{k}(\mathbbm{v}_{k},\bm{p}^{\star})
holds by construction. Since the objective of (
8
) is concave and continuously differentiable in
\bm{d}
, by the first order optimality condition we obtain

\displaystyle(\forall k\in\mathcal{K})~d_{k}^{\star}=\frac{1}{\mathsf{MSE}_{k}(\mathbbm{v}_{k}^{\star},\bm{p}^{\star})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\inf_{\mathbbm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}}\mathsf{MSE}_{k}(\mathbbm{v}_{k},\bm{p}^{\star})}

\displaystyle=1+\sup_{\mathbbm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}}\mathsf{SINR}_{k}(\mathbbm{v}_{k},\bm{p}^{\star})

\displaystyle=1+\mathsf{SINR}_{k}(\mathbbm{v}_{k}^{\star},\bm{p}^{\star})>0,

where the last two equalities follow from Proposition
1
.
Hence, the optimum of Problem (
8
) can be rewritten as

\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\omega_{k}\log(1+\mathsf{SINR}_{k}(\mathbbm{v}_{k}^{\star},\bm{p}^{\star}))-\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\omega_{k}.

This shows that the optimum of Problem (
5
) differs from the optimum of Problem (
8
) only by a constant offset
\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\omega_{k}
, and that it is attained by
(\bm{p}^{\star},\mathbbm{v}_{1}^{\star},\ldots,\mathbbm{v}_{K}^{\star})
.
∎

III-C

Block coordinate ascent algorithm

Following similar arguments as in
[
2
,
9
,
13
]
, we propose to compute a suboptimal solution to Problem (
5
) using a block coordinate ascent algorithm for the equivalent problem (
8
). The pseudocode of the proposed algorithm is reported at the top of the next page.

1:

\bm{p}\in\mathbbmss{R}_{+}^{K}

2:

repeat

3:

(\forall k\in\mathcal{K})~\mathbbm{v}_{k}\leftarrow\arg\min_{\mathbbm{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{k}}\mathsf{MSE}_{k}(\mathbbm{v}_{k},\bm{p})

4:

(\forall k\in\mathcal{K})~d_{k}\leftarrow\frac{1}{\mathsf{MSE}_{k}(\mathbbm{v}_{k},\bm{p})}

5:

(\forall k\in\mathcal{K})~p_{k}=\min\Big\{\Big(\frac{\omega_{k}d_{k}|\mathsf{E}[\mathbbm{h}_{k}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbbm{v}_{k}]|}{\sum_{j=1}^{K}\omega_{j}d_{j}\mathsf{E}[|\mathbbm{h}^{\mathsf{H}}_{k}\mathbbm{v}_{j}|^{2}]}\Big)^{2},P\Big\}

6:

until
no significant progress is observed.

Additional details on the proposed algorithm are given below.

•

The update rule for
(\mathbbm{v}_{1},\ldots,\mathbbm{v}_{K})
involves solving
K
MSE minimization problems under information constraints
[
11
]
. For the ideal case
\mathcal{V}_{k}=\mathcal{H}^{M}
(fully centralized processing with perfect CSI), the solution is readily given by the familiar MMSE beamformers
\mathbbm{v}_{k}=\left(\mathbbm{H}\bm{P}\mathbbm{H}^{\mathsf{H}}+\bm{I}\right)^{-1}\mathbbm{h}_{k}\sqrt{p_{k}}
. Similar expressions can also be obtained for many other practical cases, as we will see in the simulation section. We remark once more that
\mathbbm{v}_{k}
is a function of the CSI, and not a deterministic vector. Hence, storing
\mathbbm{v}_{k}
during the algorithm execution means storing its long-term parameters (e.g., the regularization factor in a channel inversion block).

•

The update rule for
\bm{d}
follows from the first order optimality condition as in the proof of Proposition
2
.

•

The update rule for
\bm{p}
is based on the problem

\underset{(\forall k\in\mathcal{K})~0\leq p_{k}\leq P}{\text{minimize}}\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\omega_{k}d_{k}\mathsf{MSE}_{k}(\mathbbm{v}_{k},\bm{p}).

It can be shown that, if the beamformers are obtained from a previous MSE minimization step, then the above problem admits a closed-form solution as in
[
9
, Eq. (7.9)]
.

Our algorithm enjoys the following monotonicity property:

Proposition 3
.

Let
(\bm{d}^{(i)},\bm{p}^{(i)},\mathbbm{v}_{1}^{(i)},\ldots,\mathbbm{v}_{K}^{(i)})_{i\in\mathbbmss{N}}
be the sequence of variables produced by the proposed algorithm. Then, the sequence of objectives
(f^{(i)})_{i\in\mathbbmss{N}}
, where

(\forall i\in\mathbbmss{N})~f^{(i)}:=\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\omega_{k}\log(1+\mathsf{SINR}_{k}(\mathbbm{v}_{k}^{(i)},\bm{p}^{(i)})),

is monotonically increasing and convergent.

Proof.

The proof follows similar arguments as in the proof of Proposition
2
. It is omitted due to space limitations.
∎

In fact, the related studies based on
[
2
]
(such as
[
9
, Sect. 3.4.2]
and
[
13
]
) typically advertise convergence not only of the objectives, but also of the variables (to a stationary solution). However, since in this work we are dealing with a functional optimization problem, the notions of convergence and stationarity must be taken with particular care. Hence, we leave additional convergence analysis of our proposed algorithm to an extended version of this study in preparation.

. Note that, in contrast to the standard (cell-free) massive MIMO regime with
M=NL\gg K
, here we consider a critically loaded system with
M\approx K
. We use the same simulation environment as in
[

,
12
]
, which in turn uses the same channel model as in
[

On Moment Functionals with Signed Representing Measures
Konrad Schmüdgen

Abstract
. Suppose that 𝖠 \mathsf{A} is a finitely generated commutative unital real algebra and K K is a closed subset of the set A ^ \hat{A} of characters of 𝖠 \mathsf{A} . We study the following problem: When is each linear functional L : 𝖠 → ℝ L:{\mathsf{A}}\to\mathds{R} an integral with respect to some signed Radon measure on 𝖠 ^ \hat{\mathsf{A}} supported by the set K K ? A complete characterization of the sets K K and algebras 𝖠 \mathsf{A} by necessary and sufficient conditions is given. The result is applied to the polynomial algebra ℝ ⁡ [ x 1 , … , x d ] \mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{d}] and subsets K K of ℝ d \mathds{R}^{d} .

1.
Introduction

R. P. Boas
[
B39
]
showed that
each
real sequence is the moment sequence of some
signed
Radon measure on
\mathds{R}
, see also
[
ST43
, Theorem 3.11]
for a proof. In terms of functionals this means that each linear functional on the polynomial algebra
\mathds{R}[x]
can be represented as an integral by some signed Radon measure on
\mathds{R}
. This result was sharpened by G. Polya
[
P38
]
who proved that the measure can be chosen such that its support is any real sequence without finite cluster points. A. Duran
[
D89
]
studied the case when the signed measure has a Schwartz space density. T. Sherman
[
Sh64
]
generalized Boas’ theorem to
d
-sequences on
\mathds{R}^{d}
and on
[0,+\infty)^{d}
for
d\in\mathds{N}
.

In this paper we investigate the following problem:

Which closed subsets
K
of
\mathds{R}^{d}
have the property that each real
d
-sequence is a moment sequence with a signed representing measure whose support is contained in
K
?

As it is common, moment problems are studied in terms of functionals rather than moment sequences. To each
d
-sequence
s=(s_{n})_{n\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{d}}
one associates a linear functional

L_{s}
, called the Riesz functional of
s
, on the polynomial algebra
\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{d}]
by
L_{s}(x^{n})=s_{n},n\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{d}
. Then moment
d
-sequences correspond to moment functionals on
\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{d}]
and the above problem can be rephrased by asking when is each linear functional
L:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{d}]\to\mathds{R}
a moment functional with signed representing measure supported by
K
.

The corresponding general problem for an arbitrary commutative finitely generated real unital algebra is completely settled in Theorem
2
by giving necessary and sufficient conditions. The case of polynomial algebras is obtained as an application of Theorem
2
and is stated in Theorem
5
.

This paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
we introduce
the necessary terminology and state our main result (Theorem
2
). In Section
3
we give the corresponding applications to polynomial algebras (Theorem
5
) and develop some corollaries and examples

Sections
4
and
5
are devoted to the proof of Theorem
2
. The crucial technical ingredient is a result about topologies on unbounded operator algebras which was proved in
[
S80
]
. Note that Sherman’s theorem
[
Sh64
]
(even more, a generalization to enveloping algebras of Lie algebras) has been derived in
[
S78
]
in a similar manner.

2.
Terminology and Main Results

Throughout this paper,
\mathsf{A}
denotes a
commutative finitely generated real unital algebra
and
\{a_{1},\dots,a_{d}\}
is a fixed set of algebra generators of
\mathsf{A}
. The unit element of
\mathsf{A}
is denoted by
1
. Our guiding examples of algebras
\mathsf{A}
are the polynomial algebras
\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{d}]
for
d\in\mathds{N}
.

A
character
of
\mathsf{A}
is an algebra homomorphism
\chi:\mathsf{A}\mapsto\mathds{R}
such that
\chi(1)=1
. Let
\hat{\mathsf{A}}
denote the set of characters of
\mathsf{A}
.

Since
\{a_{1},\dots,a_{d}\}
is a set of algebra generators, there exists a unique surjective unital algebra homomorphism
\pi:\mathds{R}_{d}[x]\to{\mathsf{A}}
such that
\pi(x_{j})=d_{j},

j=1,\dots,d
. If
\mathcal{J}\,
denotes the kernel of
\pi
, then
\mathcal{J}\,
is an ideal of
\mathds{R}_{d}[x]
and
{\mathsf{A}}
is isomorphic to the quotient algebra
\mathds{R}_{d}[x]/\mathcal{J}\,,
that is,

{\mathsf{A}}\cong\mathds{R}_{d}[x]/\mathcal{J}\,
.
Each character
\chi
of
{\mathsf{A}}
is uniquely determined by the point
x_{\chi}:=(\chi(a_{1}),\dots,\chi(a_{d}))
of
\mathds{R}^{d}
. We identify
\chi
with
x_{\chi}
and write
f(x_{\chi}):=\chi(f)
for
f\in{\mathsf{A}}
. That is,
f(x)
always denotes the values of the character
x\in\hat{\mathsf{A}}
at
f\in\mathsf{A}
. Under this identification,
\hat{{\mathsf{A}}}
becomes the real algebraic set

(1)

\displaystyle\hat{{\mathsf{A}}}=\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{J}\,):=\{x\in\mathds{R}^{d}:p(x)=0\penalty\ {\rm for}\penalty\ p\in\mathcal{J}\,\}.

Since
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{J}\,)
is closed in
\mathds{R}^{d}
,
\hat{{\mathsf{A}}}
is a locally compact Hausdorff space in the induced topology of
\mathds{R}^{d}
. The elements of
{\mathsf{A}}
are real polynomials
p(a_{1},\dots,a_{d})
in the generators
a_{1},\dots,a_{d}
and they are continuous functions on
\hat{\mathsf{A}}.
Note that in the case
{\mathsf{A}}=\mathds{R}_{d}[x]
we can take
a_{1}=x_{1},\dots,a_{d}=x_{d}
and obtain
\hat{{\mathsf{A}}}=\mathds{R}^{d}
.

Let
M_{+}(\hat{\mathsf{A}})
denote the set of Radon measures
\mu
on the locally compact Hausdorff space
\hat{\mathsf{A}}
such that all
f\in\mathsf{A}
are
\mu
-integrable. Since
1\in\mathsf{A}
, all measures of
M_{+}(\hat{\mathsf{A}})
are finite. Let
M(\hat{\mathsf{A}})
be the set of differences
\mu=\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}
of Radon measures
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in M_{+}(\hat{\mathsf{A}})
. The elements of
M(\hat{\mathsf{A}})
are
signed
Radon measures. We say that
\mu
is supported by a subset
K
of
\hat{\mathsf{A}}
if
\mathrm{supp}\,\mu_{1}\subseteq K
and
\mathrm{supp}\,\mu_{2}\subseteq K
.

Definition 1
.

Let
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in M_{+}(\hat{\mathsf{A}})
. The linear functional
L
on
\mathsf{A}
defined by

\displaystyle L(f)=\int f(x)\,d\mu_{1}(x)-\int f(x)\,d\mu_{2}(x),\penalty\ \penalty\ f\in\mathsf{A},

is called a
general moment functional
and
\mu:=\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}
is called a representing signed measure of
L
.

We want to study and solve the following problem:

Given a closed subset
K
of
\hat{\mathsf{A}}
, when is each linear functional on
\mathsf{A}
a general moment functional with representing signed measure supported by
K
?

This question is settled by Theorem
2
which is also the main result of this paper. For this the following linear subspaces
\mathcal{N}_{n}(K)
of
\mathsf{A}
are needed:
Suppose
K
is a subset of
\hat{\mathsf{A}}
. For
n\in\mathds{N}_{0}
we define

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{n}(K):=\{a\in

\displaystyle\mathsf{A}:\textrm{ There exists a number}\penalty\ \penalty\ C_{a}>0\penalty\ \textrm{such that}

\displaystyle|a(x)|\leq C_{a}(1+a_{1}(x)^{2}+\cdots+a_{d}(x)^{2})^{n}\penalty\ \penalty\ \textrm{for all}\penalty\ \penalty\ x\in K\,\}.

Further, we say that
K

separates the points of
\mathsf{A}
if
a(x)=0
for all
x\in K
and for some
a\in{\mathsf{A}}
implies that
a=0
.

Theorem 2
.

Suppose that
\{a_{1},\dots,a_{d}\}
,
d\in\mathds{N}_{0}
, is a set of generators of the commutative unital real algebra
\mathsf{A}
and
K
is a closed subset of
\hat{\mathsf{A}}
. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

Each linear functional on
\mathsf{A}
is a general moment functional with representing measure supported by
K
.

(ii)

K
separates the points of
\mathsf{A}
and the linear space
\mathcal{N}_{n}(K)
is finite-dimensional for each
n\in\mathds{N}_{0}
.

The proof of Theorem
2
will be given in Section
5
.

The following simple example shows that the separation assumption in Theorem
2
(ii) cannot be omitted.

Example 3
.

Let
\mathsf{A}
be the quotient algebra of
\mathds{R}[x]
by the ideal generated by
x^{2}
. That is,
\mathsf{A}
is the vector space
\{a+bx;a,b\in\mathds{R}\}
with multiplication rule
(a+bx)(c+dx)=ac+(ad+bc)x
. Then
\mathsf{A}
has only one character which is given by
\chi(f)=f(0)
and each general moment functional is of the form
L(f)=cf(0)
for some
c\in\mathds{R}
. Note that
\hat{\mathsf{A}}
does not separate the points of
\mathsf{A}
and the linear functional
L_{1}(f)=f(1)
,
f\in{\mathsf{A}}
, cannot be represented by some signed Radon measure on
\hat{\mathsf{A}}
.

Remark 4
.

The considerations and results of this paper extend easily to each finitely generated commutative unital complex
*
-algebra
B
, with involution
b\to b^{*}
, and linear functionals
L:B\mapsto\mathds{C}
.
It suffices to apply the results for the commutative real algebra
{\mathsf{A}}:=\{b\in B:b=b^{*}\}
. The corresponding representing measures are then complex measures of the form
\mu=\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}+\mathsf{i}(\mu_{3}-\mu_{4})
, where
\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\mu_{3},\mu_{4}
are (positive) Radon measures.

3.
Application to polynomial algebras

In this section we turn to the special case where
\mathsf{A}
is the polynomial algebra
\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{d}]
and
a_{1}=x_{1},\dots,a_{d}=x_{d}
. As noted above,
\hat{\mathsf{A}}=\mathds{R}^{d}
.
For a subset
K
of
\mathds{R}^{d}
and
n\in\mathds{N}_{0}
, we define

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{n}(K):=\{p\in

\displaystyle\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{d}]:\textrm{ There exists a number}\penalty\ \penalty\ \lambda_{p}>0\penalty\ \textrm{such that}

\displaystyle|p(x)|\leq\lambda_{p}\,(1+x_{1}^{2}+\cdots+x_{d}^{2})^{n}\penalty\ \penalty\ \textrm{for all}\penalty\ \penalty\ x\in K\}.

Recall that a subset
K
of
\mathds{R}^{d}
is called
Zariski dense
if it is not contained in the zero set of a polynomial
p\in\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{d}],p\neq 0
. Clearly,
K
is Zariski dense if and only the point evaluations at
K
separate polynomials of
\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{d}],p\neq 0
.

The following theorem restates Theorem
2
in the present setting.

Theorem 5
.

Suppose that
K
is a closed subset of
\mathds{R}^{d}
. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

Each linear functional on
\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{d}]
is a general moment functional with support contained in
K
.

(ii)

K
is Zariski dense and the linear space
\mathcal{N}_{n}(K)
is finite-dimensional for each
n\in\mathds{N}_{0}
.

Next we give applications of Theorem
2
.

The case
d=1
is settled completely by the following corollary. It is in fact Polya’s theorem
[
P38
]
.

Corollary 6
.

Let
K
be closed subset of

\mathds{R}
. Then each linear functional on
\mathds{R}[x]
is a general moment functional with support contained in
K
if and only if
K
is unbounded.

Proof.

If
K
is bounded, then obviously
\mathds{R}[x]\subseteq\mathcal{N}_{0}(K)
, so condition (ii) in Theorem
5
is not true. If
K
is not bounded, then
K
is infinite, hence Zariski dense in
\mathds{R}
, and it is easily checked that
\mathcal{N}_{n}(K)=\{p\in\mathds{R}[x]:\deg p\leq 2n\}
. Thus, condition (ii) Theorem
5
is fulfilled.
∎

In view of Theorem
5
it might be of interest to characterize the subsets
K
of
\mathds{R}^{d}
for which all linear subspaces
\mathcal{N}_{n}(K)
,
n\in\mathds{N}_{0}
, of
\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{d}]
are finite-dimensional. Similarly, if
\mathsf{A}
is the coordinate algebra of some real algebraic variety, when are all spaces
\mathcal{N}_{n}(K)
, as defined in Section
2
, finite-dimensional?

Now we suppose that
d\geq 2
. Then a simple sufficient condition is the following:

(*)
For each
j=1,\dots,d
there exists a Zariski dense subset
M_{j}
of
\mathds{R}^{d-1}
such that for each
y=(y_{1},\dots,y_{j-1},y_{j+1},\dots,y_{d})\in M_{j}
there exists a real sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathds{N}}
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}|x_{n}|=+\infty
and

\displaystyle x_{n}(y):=(y_{1},\dots,y_{j-1},x_{n},y_{j+1},\dots,y_{d})\in K\penalty\ \penalty\ \textrm{for}\penalty\ \penalty\ n\in\mathds{N}.

Roughly speaking, condition (*) means that the set
K
is ”unbounded in all directions”.

Corollary 7
.

Suppose
d\geq 2
. If
K
is Zariski dense and condition (*) holds, then each linear functional on
\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{d}]
is a general moment functional with signed representing measure whose support is contained in
K
.

Proof.

Suppose that (*) is satisfied. Let
m\in\mathds{N}_{0}
and
p\in\mathcal{N}_{m}(K)
. Fix
j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
and
y\in M_{j}
. We write
p
as a sum of terms

(2)

\displaystyle p(x)\equiv p(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{d})=\sum_{i=0}^{k_{i}}p_{i}(x_{1},\dots,x_{j-1},x_{j+1},\dots,x_{d})x_{j}^{i}

with polynomials
p_{j}\in\mathds{R}[t_{1},t_{2},\dots,t_{d-1}]
.

We show that
p_{i}=0
for
i>2m
. Assume the contrary. Let
i
be the largest number such that
p_{i}\neq 0
. Since
M_{j}
is Zarsiki dense in
\mathds{R}^{d-1}
, we can choose
y\in M_{j}
such that
p_{i}(y)\neq 0
. Now we insert the sequence of elements
x_{n}(y)\in K
from condition (*) for
x
. Since
p\in\mathcal{N}_{m}(K)
, there exists
\lambda_{p}>0
such that

(3)

\displaystyle|p(x_{n}(y))|\leq\lambda_{p}(1+y_{1}^{2}+\cdots+y_{j-1}^{2}+x_{n}^{2}+y_{j+1}^{2}+\dots+y_{d}^{2})^{m}

Now we divide both sides by
x_{n}^{2m}
and pass to the limit
n\to\infty
. Since
\lim_{n\to\infty}|x_{n}|=+\infty
, the left-hand side gives
|p_{i}(y)|
and the right-hand side gives zero, a contradiction.
This proves that
p_{i}=0
for
i>2m
. That is, the degree of
p
with respect to
x_{j}
is at most
2m
. Therefore,
\dim\mathcal{N}_{m}(K)\leq 2dm
for all
m\in\mathds{N}_{0}
and Theorem
5
yields the assertion.
∎

In particular, condition (*) is fulfilled for
K=\mathds{R}^{d}
and
K=[0,+\infty)^{d}
. In this special case Corollary
7
gives Sherman’s theorem
[
Sh64
]
. Other simple applications are sets of the form
K=\mathds{R}^{d}\backslash M
for compact sets
M
.

Note that for
d=2
the requirement that
M_{j}
is Zariski dense in
\mathds{R}
is very simple: It suffices to assume that both sets
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
are infinite.

Example 8
.

Let
d=2
. If
K
contains a
2
-dimensional affine cone of
\mathds{R}^{2}
, then Corollary
7
applies, so each linear functional on
\mathds{R}[x_{1},x_{2}]
is a general moment functional with signed representing measure supported by
K
.

One may also take an infinite grid: If
(x_{k})_{k\in\mathds{N}}
and
(y_{n})_{n\in\mathds{N}}
are unbounded real sequences, then Corollary
7
applies to the set
K:=\{(x_{k},y_{n}):k,n\in\mathds{N}\}
.

Corollary 9
.

If there is a non-constant polynomial
p\in\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{d}]
which is bounded on
K
, then there exist a linear functional on
\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{d}]
that is not a general moment functional with support contained in
K
.

Proof.

All powers
p^{m}
for
m\in\mathds{N}
are in
\mathcal{N}_{0}(K)
, so that
\mathcal{N}_{0}(K)
has infinite dimension and the assertion follows from Theorem
5
.
∎

Corollary
9
applies (for instance) if
K
is contained in a strip
[a,b]\times\mathds{R}
in
\mathds{R}^{2}
, where
a,b\in\mathds{R},a\leq b.

4.
Preliminaries to the proof of Theorem
2

The crucial step of the proof of the main implication (ii)
\to
(i) of Theorem 2 is a combination of a result on the uniform topology of an unbounded operator algebra with a result on normal cones of ordered topological vector spaces.
In this section we recall the corresponding notions and results.

We begin with unbounded operator algebras; proofs and more details can be found in
[
S90
]
and
[
S20
]
.

Suppose that

(\mathcal{D}\,,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
is a complex inner product space. Let
\|\cdot\|
denote the norm on
\mathcal{D}\,
defined by
\|\varphi\|:=\langle\varphi,\varphi\rangle^{1/2}
,
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}\,
. An
O^{*}
-algebra
on
\mathcal{D}\,
is a subalgebra
\mathcal{A}
of the algebra
L(\mathcal{D}\,)
of linear mappings of
\mathcal{D}\,
into itself such that the identity
map
I_{\mathcal{D}}\,
is in
\mathcal{A}
and for each
a\in\mathcal{A}
there exists
b\in\mathcal{A}
satisfying

\displaystyle\langle a\varphi,\psi\rangle=\langle\varphi,b\psi\rangle\quad\text{for}\penalty\ \penalty\ \varphi,\psi\in\mathcal{D}\,.

In this case,
b
is uniquely determined by
a
and denoted by
a^{+}
. Then
\mathcal{A}
is a complex unital
*
-algebra with involution
a\mapsto a^{+}
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an
O^{\ast}
-algebra on
\mathcal{D}\,
. For
a\in\mathcal{A}
we define a seminorm
\|\cdot\|_{a}
on
\mathcal{D}\,
by

\|\varphi\|_{a}:=\|a\varphi\|
,
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}\,
.
The
graph topology

t_{\mathcal{A}}
is the locally convex topology on
\mathcal{D}\,
determined by the family of seminorms
\|\cdot\|_{a}
,
a\in\mathcal{A}
.
For a bounded subset
M
of the locally convex space
\mathcal{D}\,[t_{\mathcal{A}}]
, let
p_{M}
be the seminorm on
\mathcal{A}
defined by

\displaystyle p_{M}(a):=\sup_{\varphi,\psi\in M}\penalty\ |\langle a\varphi,\psi\rangle|,\penalty\ \penalty\ a\in\mathcal{A}.

The locally convex topology on
\mathcal{A}
defined by the family of such seminorms
p_{M}
is called the
uniform topology
and denoted by
\tau_{\mathcal{D}}\,
.

The uniform topology was introduced by G. Lassner
[
L73
]
; it was extensively studied in the monograph
[
S90
]
. Note that if all operators
a\in\mathcal{A}
are bounded, then the graph topology
t_{\mathcal{A}}
is the norm topology of the norm
\|\cdot\|
on
\mathcal{D}\,
and the uniform topology
\tau_{\mathcal{D}}\,
is given by the operator norm.

From the polarization identity

\displaystyle 4\langle a\varphi,\psi\rangle=

\displaystyle\langle a(\varphi+\psi),\varphi+\psi\rangle-\langle a(\varphi-\psi),\varphi-\psi\rangle

\displaystyle+{\rm i}\langle a(\varphi+{\rm i}\psi),\varphi+{\rm i}\psi\rangle-{\rm i}\langle a(\varphi-{\rm i}\psi),\varphi-{\rm i}\psi\rangle

for
a\in\mathcal{A}
and
\varphi,\psi\in\mathcal{D}\,
it follows that the uniform topology
\tau_{\mathcal{D}}\,
is also generated by the family of seminorms

(4)

\displaystyle p^{\prime}_{M}(a):=\sup_{\varphi\in M}\penalty\ |\langle a\varphi,\varphi\rangle|,\penalty\ \penalty\ a\in\mathcal{A}.

The crucial technical result for our approach is the following.

Proposition 10
.

Suppose
\mathcal{A}
is an
O^{*}
-algebras on
\mathcal{D}\,
which is countably generated as a
*
-algebra. Then the uniform topology
\tau_{\mathcal{D}}\,
coincides with the finest locally convex topology
\tau_{st}
on the vector space
\mathcal{A}
if and only if
for each
a\in\mathcal{A}
the vector space

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}\,_{a}:=\{b\in\mathcal{A}:

\displaystyle\textrm{There exists a number}\penalty\ \penalty\ \lambda_{b}>0\penalty\ \textrm{such that}

\displaystyle\penalty\ |\langle b\varphi,\varphi\rangle|\leq\lambda_{b}\|a\varphi\|^{2}\penalty\ \textrm{for all}\penalty\ \varphi\in\mathcal{D}\,\}

is finite-dimensional.

Proof.

[
S80
, Theorem 1]
, see also
[
S90
, Theorem 4.5.4]
.
∎

Next we turn to ordered vector spaces, see
[
Sch99
, Chapter V]
for a detailed treatment. Suppose that
E
is a real vector space. By a
cone
in
E
we mean a non-empty subset
C
of
E
such that
\lambda x\in C
and
x+y\in C
for all
x,y\in C
and
\lambda\in[0,+\infty)
. A linear functional
L:E\to\mathds{R}
is said to be
C
-positive
if
L(x)\geq 0
for all
x\in C
.

Let
\tau
be a locally convex topology on
E
. The vector space of continuous linear functionals
L:E\to\mathds{R}
is denoted by
E[\tau]^{\prime}
. A cone
C
of
E
is called
\tau
-normal
if there exists a generating family
\{p_{j};j\in J\}
of
C
-monotone seminorms for
\tau
, that is,
p_{j}(x)\leq p_{j}(x+y)
for all
x,y\in C
and
j\in J
(see e.g.
[
Sch99
, Chapter V, 3.1]
).
In the terminology of ordered vector spaces the next proposition
says that normal cones are weakly normal. This is the second technical ingredient of our proof.

Proposition 11
.

Suppose that
\tau
is a locally convex topology on
E
and
C
is a
\tau
-normal cone in
E
. Then for each linear functional
L\in E[\tau]^{\prime}
there exist
C
-positive linear functionals
L_{1},L_{2}\in E[\tau]^{\prime}
such that
L=L_{1}-L_{2}
.

Proof.

[
Sch99
, Corollary 3.1 Chapter V]
.
∎

Now we bring both topics together. Suppose
\mathcal{A}
is an
O^{*}
- algebra on
\mathcal{D}\,
. Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{+}:=\{a\in\mathcal{A}:\langle a\varphi,\varphi\rangle\geq 0\quad\textrm{for all}\penalty\ \penalty\ \varphi\in\mathcal{D}\,\}

is a cone in the real vector space

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{h}:=\{b\in\mathcal{A}:b^{+}=b\}.

For each bounded subset
M
of
\mathcal{D}\,[t_{\mathcal{A}}]
and
a\in\mathcal{A}_{+}
, we have

p^{\prime}_{M}(a)=\sup_{\varphi\in M}\penalty\ \langle a\varphi,\varphi\rangle.

This obviously implies that each seminorm
p^{\prime}_{M}
is
\mathcal{A}_{+}
-monotone. Since the family of seminorms
p_{M}^{\prime}
defined by (
4
) generates the topology
\tau_{\mathcal{D}}\,
as well, we conclude that the cone
\mathcal{A}_{+}
is normal with respect to the uniform topology
\tau_{\mathcal{D}}\,
on

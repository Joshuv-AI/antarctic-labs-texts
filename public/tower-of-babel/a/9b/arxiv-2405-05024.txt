On some intrinsic differentiability properties for Absolutely Continuous functions between Carnot groups and the Area formula
Andrea Pinamonti, Francesco Serra Cassano, Kilian Zambanini

Abstract
. We discuss Q − Q- absolutely continuous functions between Carnot groups, following Malý’s definition for maps of several variables ( [ 43 ] ). Such maps enjoy nice regularity properties, like continuity, Pansu differentiability a.e., weak differentiability and an area formula. Furthermore, we extend Stein’s result concerning the sharp condition for continuity and differentiability a.e. of a Sobolev map in terms of the integrability of the weak gradient: more precisely, we prove that a Sobolev map between Carnot groups with horizontal gradient of its sections uniformly bounded in L Q , 1 L^{Q,1} admits a representative which is Q Q -absolutely continuous.

1.
Introduction

In this paper we study Sobolev regularity, continuity and Pansu differentiability for maps
f:\Omega\subset\mathbb{G}_{1}\to\mathbb{G}_{2}
satisfying suitable intrinsic notions of absolute continuity or bounded variation, called
Q
-absolute continuity
and
bounded
Q
-variation
, where
\mathbb{G}_{1}
and
\mathbb{G}_{2}
are Carnot groups and
\Omega
is an open set in
\mathbb{G}_{1}
. In particular, we will apply such a study for extending in this setting an analogous of the remarkable Euclidean Stein’s result
[
56
]
concerning the sharp condition providing continuity and differentiability a.e. in terms of integrability of the weak gradient, thus paving the way to the study of the gradient regularity of solutions to the sub-Laplace Poisson equation, see e.g.
[
34
]
.
Moreover we will prove an area formula for
Q
-absolutely continuous maps between Carnot group.

The definition and main properties of a Carnot group are collected in section
2
. Let us quickly recall here that a
Carnot group

\mathbb{G}
is a simply connected nilpotent Lie group which can be represented as
\mathbb{G}\equiv\,(\mathbb{R}^{n},\cdot)
equipped with:

•

a family of
intrinsic dilations

(\delta_{\lambda})_{\lambda>0}\colon\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{G}
, which are automorphisms of
\mathbb{G}
;

•

a subbundle
H\mathbb{G}
of the tangent bundle
T\mathbb{G}
, called
horizontal subbundle
, which generates by commutation the whole
T\mathbb{G}
;

•

a
Carnot-Carathéodory
(or, also,
control
)
metric

d_{c}
associated to the horizontal subbundle
H\mathbb{G}
, which is left-invariant and homogeneous with respect to the intrinsic dilations.

The Haar measure of
\mathbb{G}
turns out to be the
n
-dimensional Lebesgue measure
\mathcal{L}^{n}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and the metric (Hausdorff) dimension of
(\mathbb{G},d_{c})
coincides with its homogeneous dimension
Q
. If
\mathbb{G}
is commutative, then
\mathbb{G}\equiv\mathbb{R}^{n}
as Euclidean vector space; if
\mathbb{G}
is not commutative, then
Q>\,n
. In particular, we will see that the homogeneous dimension
Q
will play the same role of the topological dimension
n
in the Euclidean setting.

A Carnot group is a sub-Riemannian structure, where several classical differential notions and results of the Euclidean/Riemannian geometry do not apply (see
[
25
,
26
]
). Thus new intrinsic notions are needed in this setting, such as, for instance, the ones of differentiable and Sobolev regular function between Carnot groups (see Definitions
2.5
and
2.18
, respectively). Nevertheless, from the intrinsic point of view, some important analogous results to Euclidean setting still hold for maps between Carnot groups, such as a Rademacher differentiability result due to Pansu (see Theorem
2.6
) and a Sobolev-Morrey embedding theorem (see, Theorem
2.21
).

Let us stress that the intrinsic notion of differentiability for mappings between Carnot groups, and the related results, cannot be always recovered within the theory of differentiation for mappings between metric spaces (see Section
7
).

The notions of
n
-dimensional absolutely continuous function
and
n
-variation
for a map
f:\,\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
were initially introduced by Malý in
[
43
]
. They have been studied in several papers (see, for instance,
[
13
,
29
,
30
,
31
]
). An historical account as far as the concept of absolute continuity for functions can be found in
[
43
]
and the references therein.

These notions can also be introduced in a metric measure setting, thus it is immediate to extend them to maps between Carnot groups. In the following the balls involved are the ones with respect to the CC distance
d_{c}
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\mathbb{G}_{1}=\,(\mathbb{R}^{n},\cdot,d_{1})
and
\mathbb{G}_{2}=\,(\mathbb{R}^{m},*,d_{2})
be two Carnot groups, let
Q
be the homogeneous dimension of
\mathbb{G}_{1}
(see (
1
)) and let
f:\,\Omega\subset\mathbb{G}_{1}\to\mathbb{G}_{2}
, with
\Omega
open set in
\mathbb{G}_{1}
.

(i)

f
is said to be
Q
-absolutely continuous if for each
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\delta=\,\delta(\varepsilon)>\,0
such that, for each finite disjoint family of open balls
U_{1},\dots,U_{k}
contained in
\Omega
,

\text{if }\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{L}^{n}(U_{i})<\delta\text{ then }\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left({\rm osc}_{U_{i}}f\right)^{Q}<\,\varepsilon

where

{\rm osc}_{U}f:=\,{\rm diam}_{\mathbb{G}_{2}}(f(U))\,.

(ii)

The
Q
-variation of
f
is defined as

V_{Q}(f,\Omega):=\,\sup\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left({\rm osc}_{U_{i}}f\right)^{Q}:\{U_{1},\dots,U_{k}\}\text{ disjoint family of balls in }\Omega\,\right\}\,.

We say that
f
has bounded
Q
-variation if
V_{Q}(f,\Omega)<\,\infty
.

We will denote by
BV^{Q}(\Omega,\mathbb{G}_{2})
the class of functions
f:\,\Omega\subset\mathbb{G}_{1}\to\mathbb{G}_{2}
with bounded
Q
-variation and with

AC^{Q}(\Omega,\mathbb{G}_{2})
the class of
Q
-absolutely continuous functions. We use the notation
BV_{\rm loc}^{Q}(\Omega,\mathbb{G}_{2})
for functions which are locally of bounded
Q
-variation and
AC_{\rm loc}^{Q}(\Omega,\mathbb{G}_{2})
for the family of functions which are locally
Q
-absolutely continuous. It actually holds that
AC^{Q}(\Omega.\mathbb{G}_{2})\subset BV^{Q}(\Omega,\mathbb{G}_{2})
if
\Omega\subset\mathbb{G}_{1}
is a bounded open set (see Proposition
3.1
).

The study of absolutely continuous functions has been extended from the Euclidean framework to the one of metric measures spaces: firstly, the study of real-valued functions defined on measure metric spaces (see, for instance,
[
51
,
52
]
), and then the one of metric space-valued maps (see, for instance,
[
62
,
63
,
45
,
35
]
). We are going to compare later our results with the previous ones.

Note that also in this context the homogeneous dimension
Q
of the Carnot group
\mathbb{G}_{1}
plays the same role of the Euclidean dimension. Indeed, we will see that, roughly speaking, the class
AC_{\rm{loc}}^{Q}(\Omega,\mathbb{G}_{2})
locally lies between the class of Sobolev maps which admit weak horizontal gradient with summability
Q
, that is the class
W_{\rm loc}^{1,Q}(\Omega,\mathbb{G}_{2})
(see Definition

2.18
), and the union over
p>Q
of the spaces
W_{\rm loc}^{1,p}(\Omega,\mathbb{G}_{2})
. Moreover it shares good properties with the maps in
W_{\rm loc}^{1,p}(\Omega,\mathbb{G}_{2})
with
p>Q
.

In section
3
we begin our study by stressing some simple classes of functions contained in
AC^{Q}(\Omega,\mathbb{G}_{2})
. In particular we will see that, if
f
satisfies the so-called (RR) condition, then
f\in AC^{Q}(\Omega,\mathbb{G}_{2})
and we will show that, if
f
is a Sobolev map with exponent
p>Q
, that is
f\in W^{1,p}(\Omega,\mathbb{G}_{2})
, then
f\in AC^{Q}(\Omega,\mathbb{G}_{2})
(see Theorem
3.6
). Moreover we will prove that maps with finite
Q
-variation between Carnot groups, that is maps in
BV^{Q}(\Omega,\mathbb{G}_{2})
, are Pansu-differentiable a.e. and admit weak horizontal gradient (see Theorems
3.7
and
3.10
). As a consequence, since
AC^{Q}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{G}_{2})\subset BV^{Q}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{G}_{2})
, also functions in
AC^{Q}(\Omega,\mathbb{G}_{2})
share the same properties.

In section
4
we will prove that a Sobolev function
f:\Omega\subset\mathbb{G}_{1}\to\mathbb{G}_{2}
admitting weak gradient in the Lorenz space
L^{Q,1}(\Omega)
admits a representative in
AC^{Q}(\Omega,\mathbb{G}_{2})
(Theorem
4.2
), extending in this way Stein’s result on Euclidean spaces. Moreover we will discuss this result also for maps in a suitable class of Orlicz-Sobolev spaces between Carnot groups (see Appendix
6
and in particular Theorem
6.7
).

In section
5
we will show an area formula for functions in
AC^{Q}(\Omega,\mathbb{G}_{2})
(see Theorem
5.7
and Corollary
5.8
), which extends the one proved independently in
[
39
]
and in
[
60
]
for Lipschitz functions.

Eventually in Appendix
7
we will collect, for sake of completeness, some relevant notions and results about the differentiability and Sobolev regularity of metric spaces-valued maps defined on a metric measure space, in order to compare our results with the previuos ones.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Carnot groups

A
Carnot group

(\mathbb{G},\cdot)
is a simply connected and nilpotent Lie group whose Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of left-invariant vector fields has dimension
n
and admits a stratification of step
s\in\mathbb{N}
, that is

\mathfrak{g}=V_{1}\oplus V_{2}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{s},

where the vector spaces
V_{1},\dots,V_{s}\subset\mathfrak{g}
satisfy

V_{i}=[V_{1},V_{i-1}]\quad\text{for}\ i=2,\ldots,s,\quad[V_{1},V_{s}]=\{0\}.

We set
m_{i}=\dim(V_{i})
and
h_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{i}m_{j}
for
i=1,\dots,s
.
We fix an
adapted basis
of
\mathfrak{g}
, i.e. a basis
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
such that

X_{h_{i-1}+1},\dots,X_{h_{i}}\ \text{is a basis of}\ V_{i},\quad i=1,\dots,s.

The subspace
V_{1}
of
\mathfrak{g}
is also called
horizontal layer
, while
X_{1},\dots,X_{m_{1}}
are the
generating vector fields
of the group.
We endow the algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with the left-invariant Riemannian metric
\langle{\cdot,\cdot}\rangle
that makes the basis
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
orthonormal.
Exploiting the exponential identification
p=\exp\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}X_{i}\right)
, we can identify
\mathbb{G}
with
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, endowed with the group law determined by the Campbell–Hausdorff formula.
In particular, the identity
e\in\mathbb{G}
corresponds to
0\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and the
inversion map
becomes
\iota(p)=p^{-1}=-p
for any
p\in\mathbb{G}
.
Moreover, it is not restrictive to assume that
X_{i}(0)=\mathrm{e}_{i}
for any
i=1,\dots,n
.
Therefore, by left-invariance, we get

X_{i}(p)=d\tau_{p}\mathrm{e}_{i},\quad i=1,\dots,n,

where
\tau_{p}\colon\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{G}
is the
left-translation
by
p\in\mathbb{G}
, i.e.
\tau_{p}(q)=p\cdot q
for any
q\in\mathbb{G}
.

The subbundle of the tangent bundle
T\mathbb{G}
that is spanned by the
vector fields
X_{1},\dots,X_{m_{1}}
plays a particularly important
role in the theory and it is called the
horizontal bundle

H\mathbb{G}
; the fibers of
H\mathbb{G}
are

H\mathbb{G}_{x}=\mbox{span
}\{X_{1}(x),\dots,X_{m_{1}}(x)\},\qquad x\in\mathbb{G}.

For any
i=1,\dots,n
, the
degree

d(i)\in\{1,\dots,s\}
of the basis vector field
X_{i}
is
d(i)=j
if and only if
X_{i}\in V_{j}
.
The group
dilations

(\delta_{\lambda})_{\lambda\geq 0}\colon\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{G}
are hence given by

\delta_{\lambda}(p)=\delta_{\lambda}(p_{1},\dots,p_{n})=(\lambda p_{1},\dots,\lambda^{d(i)}p_{i},\dots,\lambda^{s}p_{n})\quad\text{for all}\ p\in\mathbb{G}.

The Haar measure of the group
\mathbb{G}
coincides with the
n
-dimensional Lebesgue measure
\mathcal{L}^{n}
.
The homogeneity property
\mathcal{L}^{n}(\delta_{\lambda}(E))=\lambda^{Q}\mathcal{L}^{n}(E)
holds for any measurable set
E\subset\mathbb{G}
, where

(1)

Q:=\sum_{i=1}^{s}i\dim(V_{i})\in\mathbb{N}

is the
homogeneous dimension
of
\mathbb{G}
.
For notational convenience, we will use also the shorthand
|E|=\mathcal{L}^{n}(E)
.

In the following proposition (see
[
20
]
) we collect some fairly
elementary properties of the group operation.

Proposition 2.1
.

The group product has the form

(2)

x\cdot y=x+y+\mathcal{Q}(x,y),\qquad\forall x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

where
\mathcal{Q}=(\mathcal{Q}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{Q}_{n}):\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
and each
\mathcal{Q}_{i}
is a homogeneous
polynomial of degree
d(i)
i.e.

\mathcal{Q}_{i}(\delta_{\lambda}x,\delta_{\lambda}y)=\lambda^{d(i)}\mathcal{Q}_{i}(x,y),\quad\forall x,y\in\mathbb{G}.

Moreover
\mathcal{Q}
is anti-symmetric, which means

\mathcal{Q}_{j}(x,y)=-\mathcal{Q}_{j}(-y,-x),

and also

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{1}(x,y)=...=\mathcal{Q}_{m_{1}}(x,y)=0,

(3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{j}(x,0)=\mathcal{Q}_{j}(0,y)=0,

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{j}(x,x)=\mathcal{Q}_{j}(x,-x)=\mathcal{Q}_{j}(-x,x)=0,\quad\text{for}\;\;m_{1}<j\leq n,

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{j}(x,y)=\mathcal{Q}_{j}(x_{1},\dots,x_{h_{i-1}},y_{1},\dots,y_{h_{i-1}}),\quad\text{if}\quad 1<i\leq s\quad\text{and}\quad j\leq h_{i}.

Definition 2.2
.

An absolutely continuous curve
\gamma\colon[a,b]\to\mathbb{G}
is horizontal if there exist
u_{1},\dots,u_{m_{1}}\in L^{1}[a,b]
such that

\gamma^{\prime}(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{m_{1}}u_{j}(t)X_{j}(\gamma(t))\quad\mbox{for a.e.}\ t\in[a,b].

We define the
horizontal length
of a horizontal curve
\gamma
by
L(\gamma)=\int_{a}^{b}|u(t)|dt
, where
u=(u_{1},\dots,u_{m_{1}})
and
|\cdot|
denotes the Euclidean norm on
\mathbb{R}^{m_{1}}
.

Chow-Rashevskii Theorem asserts that any two points of
\mathbb{G}
can be connected by horizontal curves
[
7
, Theorem 9.1.3]
. Actually, any two points can be connected by a geodesic, despite such a curve can be not unique
[
46
, Theorem 1.4.4]
. This allows us to define the
Carnot-Carathéodory distance (CC distance)
as follows.

Definition 2.3
.

The
CC distance
between
x,y\in\mathbb{G}
is

d_{c}(x,y):=\inf\{L(\gamma)|\,\gamma\colon[0,1]\to\mathbb{G}\mbox{ horizontal joining }x\mbox{ to }y\}.

This CC metric induces the Euclidean topology and it is an
invariant distance
, since it satisfies
d_{c}(zx,zy)=d_{c}(x,y)
(left translation invariance) and
d_{c}(\delta_{r}(x),\delta_{r}(y))=rd_{c}(x,y)
(homogeneity) for
x,y,z\in\mathbb{G}
and
r>0
. Every invariant distance
d
induces a
homogeneous norm
, simply defining
\|x\|:=d(x,0)
.
We denote the CC open ball with center
x
and radius
r
by
U_{r}(x)
or by
U(x,r)
and in particular we use the shorthand
U_{1}=U(0,1)
. When convenient, we will consider the closed CC ball of center
x
and radius
r
, denoted by
B(x,r)
. It can be proved that
\mathcal{L}^{n}(U_{r}(x))=r^{Q}\mathcal{L}^{n}(U_{1}(0))
, where
Q
is the homogeneous dimension of
\mathbb{G}
(see (
1
)). Therefore
Q
turns out to be the Hausdorff dimension of the metric measure space
(\mathbb{G},d_{c},\mathcal{L}^{n})
. In particular note that, if the step of
\mathbb{G}
is
s\geq\,2
, then
Q>\,n
, which means that the Hausdorff metric dimension of
\mathbb{G}
is larger than its topological dimension
n
.

Following
[
20
, Thm. 5.1]
, we also introduce the left-invariant and homogeneous distance
d_{\infty}(p,q)=d_{\infty}(q^{-1}\cdot p,0)
for
p,q\in\mathbb{G}
, where, identifying
\mathbb{G}=\mathbb{R}^{n}=\mathbb{R}^{m_{1}}\times\dots\times\mathbb{R}^{m_{s}}
and letting
\pi_{\mathbb{R}^{m_{i}}}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{m_{i}}
to be the canonical projection for
i=1,\dots,s
,

(4)

d_{\infty}(p,0)=\max\{\varepsilon_{i}|\pi_{\mathbb{R}^{m_{i}}}(p)|_{\mathbb{R}^{m_{i}}}^{1/i}:i=1,\dots,s\}\quad\text{for all}\ p\in\mathbb{G},

with constants
\varepsilon_{1}=1
and
\varepsilon_{i}\in(0,1)
for all
i=2,\dots,s
depending on the structure of
\mathbb{G}
.
We use the shorthand
\|p\|_{\infty}=d_{\infty}(p,0)
for
p\in\mathbb{G}
.
Consequently, for
p\in\mathbb{G}
and
r>0
, we define the open and closed balls with respect to this metric as

U_{\infty}(p,r)=\{q\in\mathbb{G}:d_{\infty}(q,p)<r\},\qquad B_{\infty}(p,r)=\{q\in\mathbb{G}:d_{\infty}(q,p)\leq r\}.

2.2.
Differential Calculus on Carnot Groups

This section is taken from
[
20
]
and
[
38
, Chapter 3]
; we refer to
[
40
]
for a generalization of these contents.
The notion of
P-differentiability
for functions acting between Carnot groups was introduced by Pansu in
[
48
]
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\mathbb{G}_{1},\mathbb{G}_{2}
be Carnot groups, with homogeneous
norms
\|\cdot\|_{1}
,
\|\cdot\|_{2}
and dilations
\delta^{1}_{\lambda}
,
\delta^{2}_{\lambda}
. We say that

L:\mathbb{G}_{1}\to\mathbb{G}_{2}
is

H-linear
, or
is a
homogeneous homomorphism
, if
L
is a group
homomorphism such that

L(\delta^{1}_{\lambda}g)=\delta^{2}_{\lambda}L(g),\quad\text{for all }g\in\mathbb{G}_{1}\text{ and }\lambda>0.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{G}_{1}
be open.
We say that
f:\Omega\to\mathbb{G}_{2}
is
P-differentiable
at
x_{0}\in\Omega
if
there is a
H
-linear function
d_{P}f(x_{0}):\mathbb{G}_{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{G}_{2}
such that

\|\left(d_{P}f(x_{0})(x_{0}^{-1}\cdot x)\right)^{-1}\cdot f(x_{0})^{-1}\cdot f(x)\|_{2}=o\big(\|x_{0}^{-1}\cdot x\|_{1}\big),\quad\text{as $\|x_{0}^{-1}\cdot x\|_{1}\to 0$}

where
o(t)/t\to 0
as
t\to 0^{+}
.
The
H
-linear map
d_{P}f(x_{0})
is called the
Pansu’s differential
of
f
at
x_{0}
.

The fundamental result where P-differentiability applies is the following generalization of Rademacher theorem and it is due to P. Pansu
[
48
]
. Other possible proofs of this result were obtained by S.K. Vodopyanov
[
60
]
and by V. Magnani
[
39
]
, see also
[
46
]
. Pansu’s theorem was later extended by Magnani in
[
40
]
, in which a general homogeneous group as target space is considered. In
[
42
]
the authors proved the result for Banach space valued mappings (with the Radon-Nikodym property on the first layer); see also the subsequent work
[
41
]
.

Theorem 2.6

(Pansu
[
48
]
)
.

Let
\mathbb{G}_{1}=(\mathbb{R}^{n},\cdot)
and
\mathbb{G}_{2}=(\mathbb{R}^{m},*)
be two Carnot groups equipped with two CC metrics denoted, respectively, by
d_{1}
and
d_{2}
. Let
f:\,\Omega\subset\mathbb{G}_{1}\to\mathbb{G}_{2}
be a Lipschitz continuous function with
\Omega
an open set. Then
f
is P-differentiable at
\mathcal{L}^{n}
-a.e. in
\Omega
.

A version of the classical Stepanov’s theorem
[
57
]
holds for maps between Carnot groups, which extends Pansu-Rademacher theorem (see
[
60
, Theorem 3.1]
).

Theorem 2.7

(Stepanov’s theorem for Carnot groups)
.

Let
\mathbb{G}_{1}=(\mathbb{R}^{n},\cdot)
and
\mathbb{G}_{2}=(\mathbb{R}^{m},*)
be two Carnot groups equipped with two CC metrics denoted, respectively, by
d_{1}
and
d_{2}
. Let
f:\,\Omega\subset\mathbb{G}_{1}\to\mathbb{G}_{2}
and assume that

(5)

{\rm Lip}(f)(p):=\,\limsup_{q\to p}\frac{d_{2}(f(q),f(p))}{d_{1}(q,p)}<\infty\text{ for $\mathcal{L}^{n}$-a.e. }p\in\Omega.

Then
f
is P-differentiable at
\mathcal{L}^{n}
-a.e.
p\in\Omega
.

Let
X_{1},\dots,X_{m_{1}}
be a generating family of vector fields. For
j=1,\dots,m_{1}
, we say that
f:\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{R}
is
differentiable along

X_{j}
at a point
x_{0}
if the map
\lambda\mapsto f(\tau_{x_{0}}(\delta_{\lambda}e_{j}))\,=f(\exp(\lambda X_{j})(x_{0}))

is differentiable at
\lambda=0
, where
e_{j}
is the

j
-th vector of the canonical basis of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
In other words,
f
is differentiable along
X_{j}
at
x_{0}
if there exists

X_{j}f(x_{0}):=\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{f(\exp(tX_{j})(x_{0}))-f(x_{0})}{t}.

Even if
f
is not differentiable, we can still define the discrete difference quotients:

Definition 2.8
.

Let
f:\Omega\to\mathbb{R},\,i=1,\dots,m_{1}
. We define the difference quotient at
x
in direction
X_{i}
as

D^{h}_{i}f(x):=\frac{f(\exp(hX_{i})(x))-f(x)}{h}

defined for
x\in\Omega^{\prime}\subset\subset\Omega
with
0<|h|<\mbox{dist}(\Omega^{\prime},\partial\Omega)
.

Notice that, if
f:\Omega\subset\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{R}
is P-differentiable at a point
x_{0}
, then it is also differentiable along
X_{j}
at
x_{0}
and

X_{j}f(x_{0})=d_{P}f(x_{0})(e_{j}).

For any function
f:\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{R}
for which the partial derivatives

X_{j}f
exist, we
define the
horizontal gradient
of
f
, denoted by

\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}f
, as

\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}f:=\sum_{i=1}^{m_{1}}(X_{i}f)X_{i}.

We will represent the horizontal gradient in coordinates
\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}f=(X_{1}f,\dots,X_{m_{1}}f).

We conclude this section by recalling the notion of convolution in Carnot groups; we address the interested reader to
[
16
, Proposition 1.20]
for the proof.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
\mathbb{G}=(\mathbb{R}^{n},\cdot)
be a Carnot group and let
\varrho\in{\mathbf{C}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be such that
0\leq\varrho\leq 1
,
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\varrho\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}=1
,
{\mathrm{spt}}\varrho\subset U_{1}
and
\varrho(x^{-1})=\varrho(-x)=\varrho(x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let us denote

(6)

\varrho_{\varepsilon}(x):=\varepsilon^{-Q}\varrho\left(\delta_{1/\varepsilon}(x)\right)\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,;

(7)

(f\star\varrho_{\varepsilon})(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\varrho_{\varepsilon}(x\cdot\,y^{-1})\,f(y)\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}(y)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\varrho_{\varepsilon}(y)\,f(y^{-1}\cdot\,x)\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}(y)

Then

(i)

if
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\ 1\leq p<\infty
, then
f\star\varrho_{\varepsilon}\in{\mathbf{C}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
f\star\varrho_{\varepsilon}\to f
in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
as
\varepsilon\to 0
;

(ii)

{\mathrm{spt}}\ f\star\varrho_{\varepsilon}\subset{\mathrm{spt}}\ f\cdot B(0,\varepsilon)
;

(iii)

X_{j}(\varphi\star\varrho_{\varepsilon})=X_{j}\varphi\star\varrho_{\varepsilon}
for any
\varphi\in{\mathbf{C}}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and each
j=1,\dots,m_{1}
;

(iv)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(f\star\varrho_{\varepsilon})\,g\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(g\star\varrho_{\varepsilon})\,f\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}
for every
f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
;

(v)

if
f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap{\mathbf{C}}^{0}(\Omega)
for a suitable open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
then
f\star\varrho_{\varepsilon}\to f
uniformly on compact subsets of
\Omega
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

2.3.
Lorentz space

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{G}\equiv\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open set, let
Q
denote the homogeneous dimension of
\mathbb{G}
and let
A:=\,\mathcal{L}^{n}(\Omega)
. If
g:\Omega\to\bar{\mathbb{R}}
is a measurable function,
\mathcal{L}^{n}
-a.e. finite, we define its
distribution function

\lambda_{g}:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty]
as

\lambda_{g}(s):=\,\mathcal{L}^{n}\left(\left\{x\in\Omega:\,|g(x)|>\,s\right\}\right)

and the
nonincreasing rearrangement

g^{*}:\,[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty]
of
g
as

g^{*}(t):=\,\inf\left\{s\geq\,0:\,\lambda_{g}(s)\leq\,t\right\}\,.

It is well-known that
\lambda_{g}
and
g^{*}
are nonincreasing and right-continuous. Moreover
g^{*}
is the nonincreasing function which is equimeasurable with
|g|
, namely

\mathcal{L}^{n}(\{x\in\Omega:|g(x)|>s\})=\mathcal{L}^{1}(\{t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}:g^{*}(t)>s\})\qquad\text{for all }s>0.

The
Lorentz space

L^{Q,1}(\Omega)
is defined as the class of all measurable functions
g:\Omega\to\bar{\mathbb{R}}
,
\mathcal{L}^{n}
-a.e. finite, for which the quasi-norm

\|g\|_{L^{Q,1}(\Omega)}:=\,\int_{0}^{A}t^{\frac{1-Q}{Q}}\,g^{*}(t)\,dt

is finite. Note that it is not a norm since the triangle inequality may fail. A systematic study of Lorentz spaces is carried out in
[
55
]
whereas a simpler recent account can be found in
[
31
]
.

Let us recall some properties of the Lorentz space
L^{Q,1}(\Omega)
whose proof can be found in
[
6
]
and
[
31
]
.

Proposition 2.10
.

1

(i)

For any measurable function
g:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
,

\|g\|_{L^{Q,1}(\Omega)}=\,Q\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda_{g}(s)^{1/Q}\,ds\,.

(ii)

L^{Q,1}(\Omega)\subset L^{Q}(\Omega)
; moreover, if
|\Omega|<\,\infty
, it holds that
L^{p}(\Omega)\subset L^{Q,1}(\Omega)
for each
p>Q
.

(iii)

If
|g_{1}|\leq\,|g_{2}|
a.e. in
\Omega
, then

\|g_{1}\|_{L^{Q,1}(\Omega)}\leq\,\|g_{2}\|_{L^{Q,1}(\Omega)}\,.

Notice that, in view of point (ii) of the previous Proposition, the Lorentz space
L^{Q,1}(\Omega)
is, locally, an intermediate space between
L^{Q}(\Omega)
and
L^{p}(\Omega)
for all
p>Q
.

Let us also recall the following remarkable characterization of
L^{Q,1}(\Omega)
in terms of Orlicz integrability conditions, contained in
[
31
, Corollary 2.4]
, which we will need later.

Theorem 2.11
.

Let
g
be a measurable function on
\Omega
. Then the following
properties are equivalent:

(i)

g\in L^{Q,1}(\Omega)
.

(ii)

There is a positive nonincreasing function
\varphi\in L^{1/Q}((0,\infty))
such that

(8)

\int_{\Omega}F_{\varphi}(|g|)\,dx<\,\infty\,,

where

(9)

F_{\varphi}(s):=\begin{cases}s\,\varphi(s)^{\frac{1}{Q}-1}&\text{ if }s>\,0\\
0&\text{ if }s=\,0.\end{cases}

2.4.
Sobolev spaces on Carnot groups.

In this section we introduce and study
L^{p}
and Sobolev spaces for maps between Carnot groups.

2.4.1.
Sobolev functions from a Carnot group to
\mathbb{R}

Sobolev spaces for scalar functions defined on a Carnot group
\mathbb{G}
are constructed in the usual way by considering distributional derivatives along the basis
X_{1},\dots,X_{m_{1}}
of the horizontal layer of
\mathbb{G}
:
a function
g\in L^{1}_{loc}(\Omega)
is the
weak (or distributional) derivative of
f
along
X_{i}
if

\int_{\Omega}g\varphi\,dx=-\int_{\Omega}fX_{i}\varphi\,dx\qquad\text{for all }\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega).

As usual we will write
X_{i}f
also to denote the weak derivative of
f
along
X_{i}
and
\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}f=(X_{1}f,\dots,X_{m_{1}}f)
for the
weak horizontal gradient
.

Definition 2.12
.

Let
\Omega
be an open subset of
\mathbb{G}\equiv\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
1\leq p\leq\infty
.
We define the (horizontal) Sobolev space
W^{1,p}_{\mathbb{G}}(\Omega)
as the space of all the functions with finite norm

(10)

\|u\|_{W^{1,p}_{\mathbb{G}}}:=\|u\|_{p}+\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u\|_{p},

where
\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u
denotes the horizontal distributional gradient of
u
.

The following result is well-known, see e.g.
[
16
]
,
[
19
]
,
[
23
]
.

Proposition 2.13
.

For any
1\leq p\leq\infty
,
(W^{1,p}_{\mathbb{G}}(\Omega),\|\cdot\|_{W^{1,p}_{\mathbb{G}}})
is a Banach space, reflexive if
1<p<\infty
.
Moreover,
W^{1,2}_{\mathbb{G}}(\Omega)
is a Hilbert space.

Another way to define the
W^{1,p}_{\mathbb{G}}
with
1\leq p<\infty

is to take the closure of

C^{\infty}
functions w.r.t the norm (
10
). As in the Euclidean
case, the two approaches are equivalent. This
was obtained independently in

[
19
, Theorem 1.2]
and
[
23
, Theorem A.2]
. However, the
method goes back to the classical result by Friedrichs
[
22
]
. The result can be stated as follows.

Theorem 2.14
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
. We have

\mathbf{C}^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,p}_{\mathbb{G}}(\Omega)\mbox{ is dense in }W^{1,p}_{\mathbb{G}}(\Omega).

For functions in
W^{1,p}_{\mathbb{G}}(\Omega)
, the difference quotients are uniformly bounded by the
L^{p}
norm of the weak gradient. This is expressed by the following Lemma, whose Euclidean counterpart is a classical result (see for instance
[
24
]
).

Lemma 2.15
.

Let
f\in W^{1,p}_{\mathbb{G}}(\Omega)
. Then, for every
i=1,\dots,m_{1}
and for every
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\subset\Omega
it holds

\|D_{i}^{h}f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega^{\prime})}\leq\|X_{i}f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

for every
0<|h|<\mbox{dist}(\Omega^{\prime},\partial\Omega)
.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.14
, it suffices to prove the statement for
f\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,p}_{\mathbb{G}}(\Omega)
.
Fix
i=1,\dots,m_{1}
,
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\subset\Omega
and
0<|h|<\mbox{dist}(\Omega^{\prime},\partial\Omega).

Consider
x\in\Omega^{\prime}
and define

\gamma_{x}(s):=\exp(hsX_{i})(x).

Then
\gamma_{x}
is a horizontal curve satisfying
\gamma_{x}(0)=x,\,\gamma_{x}(1)=\exp(hX_{i})(x)
and, by construction,
\dot{\gamma}_{x}(s)=hX_{i}(\gamma_{x}(s))
. Then the result follows by a direct computation:

\displaystyle\|D_{i}^{h}f\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\left|\frac{f(\exp(hX_{i})(x))-f(x)}{h}\right|^{p}dx=\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\left|\frac{f(\gamma_{x}(1))-f(\gamma_{x}(0))}{h}\right|^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|h|^{p}}\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\left|\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d}{ds}(f\circ\gamma_{x})(s)\,ds\right|^{p}dx=\frac{1}{|h|^{p}}\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\left|\int_{0}^{1}\big\langle\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}f(\gamma_{x}(s)),\dot{\gamma}_{x}(s)\big\rangle_{\gamma_{x}(s)}\,ds\right|^{p}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|h|^{p}}\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\left|\int_{0}^{1}hX_{i}f(\gamma_{x}(s))\,ds\right|^{p}dx\leq\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\int_{0}^{1}\left|X_{i}f(\gamma_{x}(s))\right|^{p}ds\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\left|X_{i}f(\gamma_{x}(s))\right|^{p}dx\,ds=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Omega^{\prime}\cdot\delta_{hs}(e_{i})}\left|X_{i}f(z)\right|^{p}dz\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Omega}\left|X_{i}f(z)\right|^{p}dz\,ds=\|X_{i}f\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.\qed

Proposition 2.16
.

Let
1\leq p<+\infty
. Let
f:\,\Omega\subset\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{R}
and suppose that

(i)

f
is P-differentiable a.e. in
\Omega
;

(ii)

f\in W^{1,p}_{\mathbb{G},\rm loc}(\Omega)
.

Then

X_{i}f(x)=\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{f\left(\exp(tX_{i})(x)\right)-f(x)}{t}\text{ for a.e. }x\in\Omega\,

for each
i=1,\dots,m_{1}
, where
X_{i}f
denotes the distributional derivative of
f
along the vector field
X_{i}
.

Proof.

Let us first show that, since
f\in W^{1,p}_{\mathbb{G},\rm loc}(\Omega)
, then

D_{i}^{h}f\to X_{i}f\quad\text{in }L^{p}_{\rm loc}(\Omega).

Let
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\subset\Omega
and
u\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,p}_{\mathbb{G}}(\Omega)
. We write

D_{i}^{h}f-X_{i}f=D_{i}^{h}(f-u)+D_{i}^{h}u-X_{i}u+X_{i}(u-f)

and using the previous lemma for
h
sufficiently small

\|D_{i}^{h}f-X_{i}f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega^{\prime})}\leq\|D_{i}^{h}u-X_{i}u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega^{\prime})}+2\|X_{i}(u-f)\|_{{L^{p}}(\Omega)}.

Now the claim follows by Theorem
2.14
, since for
u\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)
it is well known that
D_{i}^{h}u\to X_{i}u
(which coincides with the classical directional derivative)
uniformly
on compact sets.

On the other hand, by assumption (i),
D_{i}^{h}f
converges pointwise almost everywhere to the classical derivative of
f
along the vector field
X_{i}
. Hence the conclusion follows.
∎

We conclude this section by recalling a well-known estimate on the Riesz potential that will be helpful later on. The proof easily follows using the Poincaré inequality in Carnot groups
[
28
]
,
[
18
]
and
[
21
]
and we omit it.

Theorem 2.17
.

Let
U=U(x,r)
and let
f:\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{R}
be a
continuously differentiable function. Then there exists
C>0
, independent of
U
and
f
, such that

(11)

|f(x)-f_{U}|\leq C\int_{U}\frac{|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}f(y)|}{\;\;\;\;d(x,y)^{Q-1}}\;dy

for all
x\in U
, where
f_{U}:={\mathchoice{\kern 4.30554pt\vbox{\hrule height=0.4pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}\kern-9.7pt}{\kern 4.30554pt\vbox{\hrule height=0.4pt,width=4.3pt,depth=0.0pt}\kern-7.0pt}{}{}}\int_{U}f\,dx
. Moreover, if
f\in W^{1,1}_{\mathbb{G},\rm loc}(U)
, then (
11
) holds for a.e.
x\in U
and
\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}f(y)
denotes the weak horizontal gradient of
f
at
y
.

2.4.2.
Sobolev functions between Carnot groups.

We are now going to introduce the notions of
L^{p}
functions and Sobolev functions with values in a Carnot group. These concepts actually work for mappings between general metric spaces and can be introduced in several different ways (see Section
7
). A possible approach relies on
upper gradients
(this is for instance the case discussed in
[
35
]
), while here we use the definition by
sections of mappings
. These notions go back to Ambrosio
[
3
]
and Reshetnyak
[
53
]
for functions defined on open sets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
or a Riemannian manifold and have been extended by Vodop’yanov
[
59
]
to the setting of Carnot groups. A recent account containing different approaches to this argument is due to Kleiner, Müller and Xie
[
32
,
33
]
.

Definition 2.18
.

Let
\mathbb{G}_{1}=(\mathbb{R}^{n},\cdot,d_{1})
and
\mathbb{G}_{2}=(\mathbb{R}^{m},\star,d_{2})
be two Carnot groups and let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{G}_{1}
be an open set.

(i)

We say that a map
f:\,\Omega\to\mathbb{G}_{2}
belongs to
L^{p}(\Omega,\mathbb{G}_{2})
, and we will write
f\in L^{p}(\Omega,\mathbb{G}_{2})
, if
f:\,\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
is measurable and the map
\mathbb{R}^{n}\ni x\mapsto d_{2}(0,f(x))
is in
L^{p}(\Omega)
.

(ii)

We say that a map
f:\,\Omega\to\mathbb{G}_{2}
belongs to the Sobolev space
W^{1,p}(\Omega,\mathbb{G}_{2})
, and we will write
f\in W^{1,p}(\Omega,\mathbb{G}_{2})
, if

(
\bullet
)
f\in L^{p}(\Omega,\mathbb{G}_{2})
;

(
\bullet
) for all
z\in\mathbb{G}_{2}
the functions
u_{z}:\,\Omega\to\mathbb{R}

(12)

u_{z}(x):=\,d_{2}(z,f(x))\text{ if }x\in\Omega\,,

are in
W^{1,p}_{\mathbb{G}_{1},\rm loc}(\Omega)
;

(
\bullet
) there exists a nonnegative function
g\in L^{p}(\Omega)
(independent of
z
) such that

(13)

|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}_{1}}u_{z}|\leq\,g\text{ a.e. in $\Omega$, for each $z\in\mathbb{G}_{2}$}\,.

In the following we will refer to a function
u_{z}
as a section of
f
.

As usual, we will denote by
W^{1,p}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{G}_{2})
the set of functions
f:\Omega\to\mathbb{G}_{2}
such that
f\in W^{1,p}(\Omega^{\prime},\mathbb{G}_{2})
for each
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\subset\Omega
.

Remark 2.19
.

The stability condition requested in (
13
) with respect to the family of
1
-Lipschitz maps
\varphi:\,\mathbb{G}_{2}\to\mathbb{R}
,
\varphi(y):=\,d_{2}(z,y)
, can be extended to all Lipschitz maps between
\mathbb{G}_{2}
and
\mathbb{R}
. Indeed the following characterization holds (see
[
59
, Proposition 1]
and
[
32
, Proposition 2.31]
, see also Lemma
7.4
):
f\in W^{1,p}(\Omega,\mathbb{G}_{2})
if and only if there exists a nonnegative function
g\in L^{p}(\Omega)
such that, for each Lipschitz function
\varphi:\,\mathbb{G}_{2}\to\mathbb{R}
, the composition map
\varphi\circ f:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
is in
W^{1,p}_{\mathbb{G}_{1},\rm loc}(\Omega)
and
|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}_{1}}(\varphi\circ f)|\leq\,{\rm Lip}(\varphi)\,g
a.e. in
\Omega
.

Notice that, exploiting this remark, it is easy to see that Definition
2.18
does not depend on the specific distance
d_{2}
on
\mathbb{G}_{2}
which appears in (
12
). In fact, suppose
\tilde{d}_{2}
is another invariant distance on
\mathbb{G}_{2}
. Then
d_{2}
and
\tilde{d}_{2}
determine the same Sobolev spaces. Indeed, since
d_{2}
and
\tilde{d}_{2}
are both invariant, they are equivalent as distances (see
[
7
, Corollary 5.1.5]
), namely there exists a constant
L>1
such that

(14)

L^{-1}d_{2}(x,y)\leq\tilde{d}_{2}(x,y)\leq Ld_{2}(x,y)

for every
x,y\in\mathbb{G}_{2}
. Suppose now
f\in W^{1,p}(\Omega,\mathbb{G}_{2})
according to the distance
d_{2}
. Then

•

The map
\mathbb{R}^{n}\ni x\mapsto\tilde{d}_{2}(0,f(x))
is in
L^{p}(\Omega)
by (
14
);

•

For every
z\in\mathbb{G}_{2}
, the map
\varphi_{z}:(\mathbb{G}_{2},d_{2})\to(\mathbb{R},|\cdot|)
defined by
\varphi_{z}(y):=\tilde{d}_{2}(z,y)
is
L
-Lipschitz since

|\varphi_{z}(x)-\varphi_{z}(y)|=|\tilde{d}_{2}(z,x)-\tilde{d}_{2}(z,y)|\leq\tilde{d}_{2}(x,y)\leq Ld_{2}(x,y).

Hence we can apply Remark
2.19
and deduce that
the composition
\tilde{d}_{2}(z,f(\cdot))=\varphi_{z}\circ f\in W^{1,p}_{\mathbb{G}_{1},\rm loc}(\Omega)
;

•

Again by the previous considerations and Remark
2.19
, we conclude that
|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}_{1}}(\varphi_{z}\circ f)|\leq Lg
a.e. in
\Omega
for each
z\in\mathbb{G}_{2}
.

Thus
f\in W^{1,p}(\Omega,\mathbb{G}_{2})
with respect to
\tilde{d}_{2}
: this shows that Definition
2.18
does not depend on the specific invariant distance on
\mathbb{G}_{2}
.

P-differentiability and Morrey embedding theorem for real-valued Sobolev spaces on Carnot groups when
p>Q
(see, for instance,
[
47
]
and
[
23
, Theorem 1.11]
) can be extended to Sobolev maps between Carnot groups. Here
Q
always denotes the homogeneous dimension of
\mathbb{G}_{1}
.

Theorem 2.20

(P-differentiability with
p>Q
,
[
61
, Corollary 2]
)
.

Let
f\in W^{1,p}(\Omega,\mathbb{G}_{2})
with
p>\,Q
. Then
f
is P-differentiable a.e. in
\Omega
.

The following theorem can be found in
[
33
, Lemma 2.7]
.

Theorem 2.21

(Morrey-Sobolev embedding with
p>Q
)
.

Let
f:U(x,r)\subset\mathbb{G}_{1}\to\mathbb{G}_{2}
and
p>\,Q
. There is a positive constant
C_{p}
(depending on
p
) such that, if
f\in W^{1,p}(U(x,r),\mathbb{G}_{2})
and
g
is as in (
13
), then
f
has a continuous representative and for each
y\in U(x,r)
it satisfies

(15)

d_{2}(f(x),f(y))\leq\,C_{p}\,r^{1-\frac{Q}{p}}\,\left(\int_{U(x,r)}g^{p}\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}\right)^{1/p}.

3.
Absolutely continuous functions between Carnot groups and some properties

Let us collect in this section some interesting properties of
Q-
absolutely continuous functions and functions of bounded
Q-
variation.

Let us begin to observe that, on bounded open sets (with finite measure would suffice), the class of
Q
-absolutely functions is contained in the one of bounded
Q
-variation functions.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{G}_{1}
be a bounded open set. Then
AC^{Q}(\Omega,\mathbb{G}_{2})\subset BV^{Q}(\Omega,\mathbb{G}_{2})
.

Proof.

The strategy of the proof is inspired by
[
35
, Lemma 2.16]
, where the authors prove a local version of this result for abstract metric measure spaces.
Let’s denote by
A:=\mathcal{L}^{n}(\Omega)<+\infty
and let
f\in AC^{Q}(\Omega,\mathbb{G}_{2})
. Fix
\varepsilon=1
: by
Q-
absolute continuity, there exists
\delta>0
such that, for each finite disjoint family of open balls
U_{1},\dots,U_{k}
contained in
\Omega
,

(16)

\text{if }\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{L}^{n}(U_{i})<2\delta\,\text{ then }\,\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left({\rm osc}_{U_{i}}f\right)^{Q}<\,1.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
be sufficiently large so that
A<n\delta
.
Let now
\mathcal{F}=\{U_{1},\dots,U_{k}\}
be a disjoint family of balls in
\Omega

and write
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}_{1}\cup\mathcal{F}_{2}
, where

\mathcal{F}_{1}=\{U_{j}\in\mathcal{F}:\mathcal{L}^{n}(U_{j})\geq\delta\},

\mathcal{F}_{2}=\{U_{j}\in\mathcal{F}:\mathcal{L}^{n}(U_{j})<\delta\}.

Notice that the elements in
\mathcal{F}_{1}
are at most
n

and their radii are equibounded.
For any
U=U(x,r)\in\mathcal{F}_{1}
and any
y\in U
consider a geodesic joining
x
and
y
. On such a curve pick points
x=x_{0},\,x_{1},\dots,\,x_{N-1},\,x_{N}=y
such that
x_{i}
and
x_{i+1}
live in a common small ball (contained in
U
) of measure less than
2\delta
.
Note that it is possible to find
N
independent of
y
and
U
since all the elements in
\mathcal{F}_{1}
have equibounded radii. By (
16
) we can now estimate

\begin{split}d_{2}(f(x),f(y))^{Q}&\leq(d_{2}(f(x),f(x_{1}))+\dots+d_{2}(f(x_{N-1}),f(y)))^{Q}\\
&\leq C(Q)(d_{2}(f(x),f(x_{1}))^{Q}+\dots+d_{2}(f(x_{N-1}),f(y))^{Q})\leq C(Q)N:=K.\end{split}

By the triangular inequality the same estimate holds (with a greater constant
\widetilde{K}
) for any
y,z\in U
.
We conclude that
\sum_{U\in\mathcal{F}_{1}}(\text{osc}_{U}f)^{Q}\leq n\widetilde{K}.

Collect instead the elements of
\mathcal{F}_{2}
into at most
n
sets
\mathcal{F}_{2,1},\dots\mathcal{F}_{2,n}
with
\sum_{U\in\mathcal{F}_{2,j}}\mathcal{L}^{n}(U)<2\delta
, so that by (
16
)

\sum_{U\in\mathcal{F}_{2}}(\text{osc}_{U}f)^{Q}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{U\in\mathcal{F}_{2,j}}(\text{osc}_{U}f)^{Q}<n

Hence
V_{Q}(f,\Omega)<n(\widetilde{K}+1)
and the thesis follows.
∎

It is clear that a
Q
-absolutely continuous function is actually continuous; here we provide a first example of a class of
Q
-absolutely continuous maps.

Remark 3.2
.

Let
f:\,\Omega\subset\mathbb{G}_{1}\to\mathbb{G}_{2}
be Lipschitz continuous. Then
f
turns out to be
Q
-absolutely continuous. Indeed,

{\rm osc}_{U_{r}(x)}(f):={\rm diam}_{\mathbb{G}_{2}}(f(U_{r}(x)))\leq\mathrm{Lip}(f)\,{\rm diam}_{\mathbb{G}_{1}}(U_{r}(x))

for each
x\in\mathbb{G}_{1}

Definition 3.3

A function

satisfies the condition (RR) if there is a nonnegative function

, called weight, such that

for every ball
U_{r}(x)\subset\Omega
, being

the homogeneous dimension of
\mathbb{G}_{1}
.

Remark 3.4
.

The acronym (RR) stands for Rado and Reichelderfer

, who introduced a similar condition for proving an area formula in

Remark 3.5

It is immediate to check that if

As observed in Remark

absolutely continuous. Also Sobolev functions with a high order of summability share the same property. This result was shown in

By the following Corollary

We are now interested in studying some differentiability properties of absolutely continuous maps between Carnot groups. We start with the following

Theorem 3.7

(Differentiability a.e.)

Let

(ii)

Remark 3.8

Note that Pansu’s differentiability for maps in

does not follow from differentiability result à la Cheeger-Kleiner proved in
[

, Proposition 1.3]
for maps between metric spaces, of which the target metric space is biLipschitz embedded into a Banach space satisfying the Radon- Nikodym property (RNP). Indeed it is well-known that Carnot groups may not be biLipschitz embedded into a Banach space satisfying (RNP) (see Appendix
7.1
)

Proof.

We can follow the same proof of
[
43
, Thm. 3.3]
with suitable changes. For the sake of completeness, we collect here the main steps. Let
g:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
be a nonnegative upper semicontinuous function such that

0\leq g(x)\leq[Lip(f)(x)]^{Q}\qquad\text{for all }x\in\Omega.

For any
x\in\{g>0\}
, there exists a unique integer
k=k(x)
such that
2^{k}\leq g(x)<2^{k+1}
. Furthermore, since
g
is upper semicontinuous, there exists
0<r=r(x)<1
such that
U(x,5r)\subset\{g<2^{k+1}\}
. In addition, from the fact that
g(x)\leq[Lip(f)(x)]^{Q}
, we can assume, up to reduce

(19)

2^{k}r^{Q}\leq g(x)r^{Q}<2(\underset{U(x,r)}{\text{osc}}f)^{Q}.

Applying Vitali’s covering Lemma, we can extract a disjoint (hence at most countable) family
\{U(x_{i},r_{i})\}_{i}\subset\{U(x,r(x))\}
of balls such that

\{g>0\}\subset\bigcup_{x\in\{g>0\}}U(x,r(x))\subset\bigcup_{i}U(x_{i},5r_{i}).

We define, for all

I_{k}:=\{i:k(x_{i})\geq k\}
and
E_{k}:=\{x\in\Omega:2^{k}\leq g(x)<2^{k+1}\}
.

Notice that
E_{k}\subset\bigcup_{i\in I_{k}}U(x_{i},5r_{i})
. In fact, let

. In particular
g(x)>0
, hence
x\in U(x_{i},5r_{i})
for some
i
. If it were
i\not\in I_{k}
, then
k(x_{i})<k
, which implies
x\in U(x_{i},5r_{i})\subset\{g<2^{k}\}
, contradicting
x\in E_{k}
. Thus we get

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}^{n}(E_{k})&\leq\sum_{i\in I_{k}}\mathcal{L}^{n}(U(x_{i},5r_{i}))\leq C\sum_{i\in I_{k}}r_{i}^{Q}\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{esti})}}{{\leq}}C\sum_{i\in I_{k}}2^{-k(x_{i})}(\underset{U(x_{i},r_{i})}{\text{osc}}f)^{Q}.\end{split}

Finally we use this estimate to bound

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}g(x)\,dx&\leq\sum_{k=-\infty}^{+\infty}\int_{E_{k}}g(x)\,dx\leq\sum_{k=-\infty}^{+\infty}2^{k+1}\mathcal{L}^{n}(E_{k})\\
&\leq C\sum_{k=-\infty}^{+\infty}\sum_{i\in I_{k}}2^{k+1-k(x_{i})}(\underset{U(x_{i},r_{i})}{\text{osc}}f)^{Q}\\
&=C\sum_{i=1}^{+\infty}\sum_{k\leq k(x_{i})}2^{k+1-k(x_{i})}(\underset{U(x_{i},r_{i})}{\text{osc}}f)^{Q}\\
&=4C\sum_{i=1}^{+\infty}(\underset{U(x_{i},r_{i})}{\text{osc}}f)^{Q}\leq CV_{Q}(f,\Omega),\end{split}

where we exploited the properties of the
Q-
variation and the fact that
\sum_{k\leq k(x_{i})}2^{k}=2^{k(x_{i})+1}
.
It follows that

\int_{\Omega}[Lip(f)(x)]^{Q}\,dx=\sup\Big\{\int_{\Omega}g(x)\,dx:g\text{ u.s.c., }0\leq g\leq Lip(f)^{Q}\Big\}\leq CV_{Q}(f,\Omega).

Thus (ii) follows and also that
{\rm Lip}(f)(x)<\infty
for a.e.

. From Theorem
2.7
, (i) follows, too.

Lemma 3.12

(i)

For every

Proof.

(i) Since

\{U(z\cdot y,\sigma_{0}/3k):z\in\mathbf{Z}_{k}\}
is disjoint. In addition, up to increase
\sigma_{1}
, we can assume
\sigma_{1}\geq\sigma_{0}
.

Let
(\varrho_{k})_{k}
be the sequence of mollifiers defined in (
6
), where
\varepsilon=1/k
,
\varrho_{k}\equiv\varrho_{1/k}
and suppose that
{\mathrm{spt}}(\varrho)\subset B(0,\sigma_{0}/6)

(36)

\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\varrho_{k}(x)=\,k^{Q+1}\,\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\varrho(\delta_{k}(x))\text{ for each }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,k\in\mathbb{N}\,,

(37)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}X_{i}\varrho_{k}(r_{x}(y))\,dy=\,0\text{ for each }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,i=1,\dots,m_{1}\,,

(38)

\begin{split}\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}(g\star\varrho_{k})(x)&=\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\varrho_{k}(r_{x}(y^{-1}))\,g(y)\,dy\\
&=\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\varrho_{k}(w^{-1})\,g(\ell_{x}(w))\,dw\\
&\text{ for each }g\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,.\end{split}

Indeed (
36
) follows by the definition of
\varrho_{k}
and homogeneity of intrinsic gradient
\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}:=(X_{1},\dots,X_{m_{1}})
;(
37
) follows by the properties of
\varrho_{k}
, the structure of the horizontal vector fields
X_{i}
and the right-invariance of
n
-dimensional Lebesgue measure
\mathcal{L}^{n}
;(

) follows by the definition of convolution and by the left invariance of the horizontal vector fields.

Thus , given
y\in B(0,2\sigma_{1}/k)
, by (
37
),(
38
) and the fact that the family
\{U(z\cdot y,\sigma_{0}/3k):z\in\mathbf{Z}_{k}\}
is disjoint,

\begin{split}&\sum_{z\in{\bf Z}_{k}}\int_{U(z\cdot y,\sigma_{0}/6k)}|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}(f\star\varrho_{k})(x)|^{Q}\,dx=\\
&=\,\sum_{z\in{\bf Z}_{k}}\int_{U(z\cdot y,\sigma_{0}/6k)}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\varrho_{k}(w^{-1})\,f(\tau_{x}(w))\,dw\right|^{Q}\,dx\\
&=\,\sum_{z\in{\bf Z}_{k}}\int_{U(z\cdot y,\sigma_{0}/6k)}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\varrho_{k}(w^{-1})\,(f(\tau_{x}(w))-f(x))\,dw\right|^{Q}\,dx\\
&=\,\sum_{z\in{\bf Z}_{k}}\int_{U(z\cdot y,\sigma_{0}/6k)}\left|k^{Q+1}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\varrho(\delta_{k}(w^{-1}))\,(f(\tau_{x}(w))-f(x))\,dw\right|^{Q}\,dx\\
&=\,\sum_{z\in{\bf Z}_{k}}\int_{U(z\cdot y,\sigma_{0}/6k)}\left|k^{Q+1}\,\int_{B(0,\sigma_{0}/6k)}\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\varrho(\delta_{k}(w^{-1}))\,(f(\tau_{x}(w))-f(x))\,dw\right|^{Q}\,dx\\
&\leq\,c_{1}\,\sum_{z\in{\bf Z}_{k}}({\rm osc}_{U(z\cdot y,\sigma_{0}/3k)}f)^{Q}\leq\,c_{1}\,V_{Q}(f,\mathbb{R}^{n})\,.\end{split}

By integrating the previous inequality with respect to
y\in B(0,2\sigma_{1}/k)
, it follows that there exists a positive constant
c_{2}>\,0
such that

(39)

\begin{split}&k^{Q}\,\int_{B(0,2\sigma_{1}/k)}\left(\sum_{z\in{\bf Z}_{k}}\int_{U(z\cdot y,\sigma_{0}/6k)}|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}(f\star\varrho_{k})(x)|^{Q}\,dx\right)\,dy\\
&\leq\,c_{2}\,V_{Q}(f,\mathbb{R}^{n})\text{ for each positive integer }k\,.\end{split}

Notice also that, writing
g(x)=|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}(f\star\varrho_{k})(x)|^{Q}
, we get, for each
z\in\mathbb{G},

\begin{split}\int_{U(z,\sigma_{1}/k)}g(x)\,dx&=\int_{U(0,\sigma_{1}/k)}g(z\cdot x)\,dx\\
&=c_{3}k^{Q}\int_{U(0,\sigma_{0}/6k)}\left(\int_{U(0,\sigma_{1}/k)}g(z\cdot x)\,dx\right)dy\\
&=c_{3}k^{Q}\int_{U(0,\sigma_{0}/6k)}\left(\int_{U(0,\sigma_{1}/k)\cdot y^{-1}}g(z\cdot x\cdot y)\,dx\right)dy\\
&\leq c_{3}k^{Q}\int_{U(0,\sigma_{0}/6k)}\left(\int_{B(0,2\sigma_{1}/k)}g(z\cdot x\cdot y)\,dx\right)dy\\
&=c_{3}k^{Q}\int_{B(0,2\sigma_{1}/k)}\left(\int_{U(0,\sigma_{0}/6k)}g(z\cdot x\cdot y)\,dy\right)dx\\
&=c_{3}k^{Q}\int_{B(0,2\sigma_{1}/k)}\left(\int_{U(z\cdot x,\sigma_{0}/6k)}g(y)\,dy\right)dx.\end{split}

In these passages we performed some changes of variables (which are allowed being the
n
-dimensional Lebesgue measure both left and right invariant), we noticed that
U(0,\sigma_{1}/k)\cdot y^{-1}\subset B(0,2\sigma_{1}/k)
for every
y\in U(0,\sigma_{0}/6k)
and applied Fubini-Tonelli theorem.

Let us now notice that, by applying Lemma
3.12
(ii) and the previous estimates, it follows that

(40)

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}(f\star\varrho_{k})(x)|^{Q}\,dx\leq\,\sum_{z\in{\bf Z}_{k}}\int_{U(z,\sigma_{1}/k)}|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}(f\star\varrho_{k})(x)|^{Q}\,dx\\
&\leq\,c_{3}\,k^{Q}\,\int_{B(0,2\sigma_{1}/k)}\left(\sum_{z\in{\bf Z}_{k}}\int_{U(z\cdot y,\sigma_{0}/6k)}|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}(f\star\varrho_{k})(x)|^{Q}\,dx\right)\,dy\,.\end{split}

From (
39
) and (
40
), we get that there exists
c_{4}>\,0
such that

(41)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}(f\star\varrho_{k})(x)|^{Q}\,dx\leq\,c_{4}\,V_{Q}(f,\mathbb{R}^{n})<\,\infty,\,\forall\,k\in\mathbb{N}\,.

Since
W^{1,Q}_{\mathbb{G}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a reflexive space and
f\star\varrho_{k}\to f
in
L^{Q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, as
k\to\infty
, we can infer that
f\in W^{1,Q}_{\mathbb{G}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. In the general case
f\in BV^{Q}_{\mathbb{G};\rm loc}(\Omega)
, it is sufficient to show that
f\,\varphi\in W_{\mathbb{G}}^{1,Q}(\Omega)
for each
\varphi\in{\mathbf{C}}^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
which is a cutoff function in a small neighbourhood of a point. Using zero-extension, since
f\,\varphi\in BV^{Q}_{\mathbb{G}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{Q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, by the previous argument,
f\,\varphi\in W_{\mathbb{G}}^{1,Q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and we are done.

2nd step.
Let us now show the conclusion in the general case. We assume
f\in BV^{Q}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{G}_{2})
and prove that
f\in W^{1,Q}(\Omega^{\prime},\mathbb{G}_{2})
for every
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\subset\Omega.
Let
u_{z}:\,\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
be the function in (
12
). Then, by the triangle inequality for distances, it follows that

{\rm osc}_{U_{d_{1}}(x,r)}(u_{z})\leq\,{\rm osc}_{U_{d_{1}}(x,r)}(f)

for each ball
U_{d_{1}}(x,r)\subset\Omega
and
z\in\mathbb{G}_{2}
. In particular,
u_{z}\in BV^{Q}_{\mathbb{G};\rm loc}(\Omega)
and

V_{Q}(u_{z},\Omega)\leq\,V_{Q}(f,\Omega)\text{ for each }z\in\mathbb{G}_{2}\,.

From Theorem
3.7
and the previous step,
u_{z}:\,\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
is P-differentiable a.e. and
u_{z}\in W_{\mathbb{G}_{1},\rm loc}^{1,Q}(\Omega)
(implying
u_{z}\in W^{1,Q}_{\mathbb{G}_{1}}(\Omega^{\prime})
). Thus, by Proposition
2.16
,

\begin{split}\left|X_{i}u_{z}(x)\right|&=\,\left|\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{u_{z}\left(\exp(tX_{i})(x)\right)-u_{z}(x)}{t}\right|\\
&\leq\,{\rm Lip}(u_{z})(x)\leq\,\,{\rm Lip}(f)(x)\end{split}

for a.e.
x\in\Omega
and for each
z\in\mathbb{G}_{2}
. Since
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\subset\Omega
and
f\in BV^{Q}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{G}_{2})
, by Theorem
3.7
it follows that
{\rm Lip}(f)\in L^{Q}(\Omega^{\prime})
. To conclude notice that
f\in L^{Q}(\Omega^{\prime},\mathbb{G}_{2})
: indeed
f\in BV^{Q}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{G}_{2})
, so it is locally bounded and
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\subset\Omega
.
Hence

according to Definition
2.18
.

∎

4.
Sobolev maps between Carnot groups with horizontal gradient of sections in
L^{Q,1}

In this section we deal with the sharp condition on the summability of the weak gradient of a Sobolev function which ensures continuity and differentiability almost everywhere.
It is well known that these properties hold for maps in the Sobolev space
W^{1,p}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
provided that
p>n
(in the limit case
p=n
the answer is negative, see also Remark
4.3
). Such a result has been extended to the case of Carnot groups (see Theorem
2.20
and Theorem
3.6
). In his article
[
56
]
, Elias M. Stein sharpened the previous result on Euclidean spaces: if a weakly differentiable function admits weak gradient in the Lorentz space
L^{n,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then it is continuous and differentiable a.e. This is a remarkable improvement since, by Proposition
2.10
,
L^{n,1}
is locally an intermediate space between
L^{n}
and
L^{p}
(for all
p>n
). The approach of Stein makes use of the machinery of harmonic analysis, while later Kauhanen, Koskela and Malý
[
31
]
obtained an alternative proof exploiting tools of real analysis and, in particular, the already discussed notion of
n-
absolutely continuous functions. Next we will follow this last strategy in order to discuss Stein’s result for maps between Carnot groups.

Continuity and differentiability have been already studied also for real valued Sobolev functions on metric measure spaces with upper gradient in the Lorentz space
L^{Q,1}
, where
Q
denotes the Hausdorff dimension of the metric space (see, for instance,
[
52
,
51
,
64
,
62
,
63
]
). Here we are going to prove that a Sobolev map
f:\,\Omega\subset\mathbb{G}_{1}\to\mathbb{G}_{2}
, with horizontal gradients of its sections
u_{z}:\,\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
uniformly bounded in
L^{Q,1}(\Omega)
(see (
12
)), actually satisfies the condition (RR), if
Q
denotes the homogeneous dimension of
\mathbb{G}_{1}
.

Let us first recall an estimate of the Riesz potential in Carnot groups in terms of the Orlicz conditions, which extends the Euclidean one first introduced in
[
31
, Theorem 3.1]
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\mathbb{G}\equiv\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a Carnot group,
d
the associated CC distance,
Q
its homogeneous dimension and
\Omega\subset\mathbb{G}
. Let
g
be a nonnegative measurable function on
\Omega
and let
\varphi
be a nonincreasing
positive function on
(0,\infty)
. If
F_{\varphi}(s)
is defined as in (
9
), then for any
z\in\Omega
and any measurable set
E\subset\Omega
, we have the inequality

\left(\int_{E}d(x,z)^{1-Q}g(x)\,dx\right)^{Q}\leq\,C_{Q}\,\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi^{\frac{1}{Q}}ds\right)^{Q-1}\int_{E}F_{\varphi}(g)\,dx

where
C_{Q}:=\,\left(1+|\partial U(0,1)|_{\mathbb{G}}(\mathbb{G})\right)^{Q}
and
|\partial U(0,1)|_{\mathbb{G}}(\mathbb{G})
denotes the
\mathbb{G}
-perimeter of the CC open ball
U(0,1)
.

Proof.

The proof follows the Euclidean one in
[
31
, Theorem 3.1]
, with suitable changes. We provide here the details for the reader’s convenience.

Up to a left translation, we can assume
z=0
. Moreover, we can suppose the integral on the left-hand side to be positive, since otherwise there is nothing to prove. For notational simplicity, let’s write

(42)

I_{1}=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\varphi^{\frac{1}{Q}}(s)\,ds,\qquad\qquad I_{2}=\int_{E}F_{\varphi}(g(x))\,dx.

Similarly as before, we will assume
I_{1},I_{2}<+\infty
, otherwise the proof is over.

Let
0<c<+\infty
be an arbitrary value such that
c\leq\int_{E}\|x\|^{1-Q}g(x)\,dx
, where
\|\cdot\|
denotes the homogeneous norm associated to the distance
d
.

Let
S:=\{(x,s)\in E\times\mathbb{R}:0<s<g(x)\}
. We can write, by Fubini-Tonelli theorem

\int_{E}\|x\|^{1-Q}g(x)\,dx=\int_{S}\|x\|^{1-Q}\,dx\,ds.

Define

\lambda=\frac{I_{2}^{1/Q}}{c\,I_{1}^{1/Q}}

and

S^{\prime}=\left\{(x,s)\in S:\varphi(s)<\left(\frac{\|x\|}{c\lambda}\right)^{Q}\right\}\qquad\quad S^{\prime\prime}=S\setminus S^{\prime}.

If
(x,s)\in S^{\prime}
, then
\|x\|^{Q}>c^{Q}\lambda^{Q}\varphi(s)\geq c^{Q}\lambda^{Q}\varphi(g(x))
since
\varphi
is nonincreasing.

Hence we estimate

(43)

\begin{split}\int_{S^{\prime}}\|x\|^{1-Q}dx\,ds&\leq c^{1-Q}\lambda^{1-Q}\int_{S^{\prime}}\varphi^{\frac{1-Q}{Q}}(g(x))\,dx\,ds\\
&\leq c^{1-Q}\lambda^{1-Q}\int_{E}\left(\int_{0}^{g(x)}ds\right)\varphi^{\frac{1-Q}{Q}}(g(x))\,dx\\
&=c^{1-Q}\lambda^{1-Q}\int_{E}F_{\varphi}(g(x))\,dx=c^{1-Q}\lambda^{1-Q}I_{2}.\end{split}

Now we bound the integral over
S^{\prime\prime}
by using Fubini-Tonelli theorem and the integration formula in polar coordinates in Carnot groups (see, for instance,
[
47
, Corollary 4.6]
):

(44)

\begin{split}\int_{S^{\prime\prime}}\|x\|^{1-Q}dx\,ds&\leq\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left(\int_{B(0,c\lambda\varphi^{1/Q}(s))}\|x\|^{1-Q}\,dx\right)\,ds\\
&=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{c\lambda\varphi^{1/Q}(s)}\left(\int_{\partial U(0,1)}\|\delta_{r}(y)\|^{1-Q}r^{Q-1}d|\partial U(0,1)|_{\mathbb{G}}(y)\right)dr\right)ds\\
&\leq|\partial U(0,1)|_{\mathbb{G}}(\mathbb{G})\int_{0}^{+\infty}c\lambda\varphi^{\frac{1}{Q}}(s)\,ds=|\partial U(0,1)|_{\mathbb{G}}(\mathbb{G})\,c\lambda I_{1}.\end{split}

Combining (
43
) and (
44
), we get

\begin{split}c\leq\int_{E}\|x\|^{1-Q}g(x)\,dx&=\int_{S^{\prime}}\|x\|^{1-Q}\,dx\,ds+\int_{S^{\prime\prime}}\|x\|^{1-Q}\,dx\,ds\\
&\leq c^{1-Q}\lambda^{1-Q}I_{2}+|\partial U(0,1)|_{\mathbb{G}}(\mathbb{G})\,c\lambda I_{1}=(1+|\partial U(0,1)|_{\mathbb{G}}(\mathbb{G}))I_{1}^{\frac{Q-1}{Q}}I_{2}^{\frac{1}{Q}},\end{split}

which implies

c^{Q}\leq C_{Q}I_{1}^{Q-1}I_{2}=C_{Q}\bigg(\int_{0}^{+\infty}\varphi^{\frac{1}{Q}}(s)\,ds\bigg)^{Q-1}\int_{E}F_{\varphi}(g(x))\,dx.

Since this inequality holds for every
c\leq\int_{E}\|x\|^{1-Q}g(x)\,dx
, we get the conclusion.
∎

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\mathbb{G}_{1}\equiv\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\mathbb{G}_{2}\equiv\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
\Omega\subset\mathbb{G}_{1}
be an open set and let
Q
denote the homogeneous dimension of
\mathbb{G}_{1}
. Assume that
f\in W^{1,1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{G}_{2})
and the function
g
in (
13
) belongs to
L^{Q,1}(\Omega)
. Then, a suitable representative of
f
is continuous and satisfies the condition (RR). In particular
f\in AC^{Q}(\Omega,\mathbb{G}_{2})
and then it is P-differentiable a.e. in
\Omega
.

Proof.

1st step.
First assume
\mathbb{G}_{1}=\mathbb{G}
,
\mathbb{G}_{2}=\mathbb{R}
with
f\in W^{1,1}_{\mathbb{G},\rm loc}(\Omega)
and
|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}f|\in L^{Q,1}(\Omega)
. We stress that this case was already studied in the general case of measure metric spaces (see, for instance,
[
62
, Thm. 6.6]
) by extending the strategy exploited in
[
31
]
. However we are going to repeat this strategy since we will use it in the second step, which is still open to our knowledge.

First of all notice that we can apply Theorem
2.11
inferring that, since
|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}f|\in L^{Q,1}(\Omega)
, there exists a nonnegative nonincreasing function
\varphi:(0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
such that

(45)

\int_{0}^{+\infty}\varphi^{\frac{1}{Q}}(s)\,ds<+\infty,

(46)

\int_{\Omega}F_{\varphi}(|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}f(x)|)\,dx<+\infty.

Let’s apply now Theorem
2.17
, finding a negligible set
N
such that

(47)

|f(z)-f_{U}|\leq C\int_{U}\frac{|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}f(y)|}{d(z,y)^{Q-1}}\,dy

for each CC open ball
U\subset\Omega
and
z\in U\setminus N
. This implies that, for every CC open ball
U\subset\Omega
,

(48)

(\text{osc}_{U\setminus N}f)^{Q}\leq\int_{U}w(x)\,dx,

where

w(x)=2^{Q}C^{Q}C_{Q}\left(\int_{0}^{+\infty}\varphi^{\frac{1}{Q}}(s)\,ds\right)^{Q-1}F_{\varphi}(|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}f(x)|).

In order to prove (
48
) we can assume the left-hand side to be positive, and choose
0\leq c<\text{osc}_{U\setminus N}f
.
By definition of oscillation and the triangular inequality, there exists
z\in U\setminus N
such that
c\leq 2|f(z)-f_{U}|
. Combining this fact with (
47
) and Theorem
4.1
, we infer that

\begin{split}c^{Q}&\leq 2^{Q}C^{Q}\left(\int_{U}\frac{|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}f(y)|}{d(z,y)^{Q-1}}\,dy\right)^{Q}\\
&\leq 2^{Q}C^{Q}C_{Q}\,\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi^{\frac{1}{Q}}ds\right)^{Q-1}\int_{U}F_{\varphi}(|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}f(x)|)\,dx\\
&=\int_{U}w(x)\,dx.\end{split}

By arbitrariness of
0\leq c<
osc
{}_{U\setminus N}u
, we conclude that (
48
) is satisfied.
As a consequence,
f
is locally uniformly continuous on
\Omega\setminus N
and can be extended to a continuous function
\tilde{f}
on the whole space
\Omega
which satisfies the (RR) condition with weight
w
.

2nd step.
Let us now prove the general case. For given
z\in\mathbb{G}_{2}\equiv\mathbb{R}^{m}
let
u_{z}:\,\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
denote a section of
f
, that is, the function defined in (
12
). By definition
u_{z}\in\ W^{1,1}_{\mathbb{G}_{1},\rm loc}(\Omega)
and satisfies (
13
) with
g\in L^{Q,1}(\Omega)
.
Thus, by the 1st step, there exists a continuous function
\tilde{u}_{z}:\,\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
and a negligible set
N_{z}\subset\Omega
, found as in (
47
), such that

(49)

\tilde{u}_{z}(x)=\,u_{z}(x)=\,d_{2}(z,f(x))\text{ for each }x\in\Omega\setminus N_{z}\,.

Now we proceed again as in the 1st step: since
g\in L^{Q,1}(\Omega)
, we know that there exists a nonnegative nonincreasing function
\varphi:(0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
such that

(50)

\int_{0}^{+\infty}\varphi^{\frac{1}{Q}}(s)\,ds<+\infty,

(51)

\int_{\Omega}F_{\varphi}(g(x))\,dx<+\infty.

We want to show that

(52)

(\text{osc}_{U\setminus N_{z}}\tilde{u}_{z})^{Q}\leq\widetilde{C}\left(\int_{0}^{+\infty}\varphi^{\frac{1}{Q}}(s)\,ds\right)^{Q-1}\int_{U}F_{\varphi}(g(x))\,dx

for each CC open ball
U\subset\Omega
and each
z\in\mathbb{G}_{2}
.
If the left-hand side is 0, clearly there is nothing to prove. Otherwise pick any
0\leq c<\text{osc}_{U\setminus N_{z}}\tilde{u}_{z}
. This implies that there exists
x\in U\setminus N_{z}
such that

c\leq 2|\tilde{u}_{z}(x)-(\tilde{u}_{z})_{U}|
.
Hence, using Theorem
4.1
,

\begin{split}c^{Q}&\,\leq\,\,2^{Q}|\tilde{u}_{z}(x)-(\tilde{u}_{z})_{U}|^{Q}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{rieszformula})}}{{\leq}}2^{Q}C^{Q}\left(\int_{U}\frac{|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}_{1}}\tilde{u}_{z}(y)|}{d_{1}(x,y)^{Q-1}}\,dy\right)^{Q}\\
&\,\leq\,\,2^{Q}C^{Q}\left(\int_{U}\frac{g(y)}{d_{1}(x,y)^{Q-1}}\,dy\right)^{Q}\\
&\,\leq 2^{Q}C^{Q}C_{Q}\left(\int_{0}^{+\infty}\varphi^{\frac{1}{Q}}(s)\,ds\right)^{Q-1}\int_{U}F_{\varphi}(g(x))\,dx.\end{split}

Since this estimate holds for every
0\leq c<\text{osc}_{U\setminus N_{z}}\tilde{u}_{z}
, we get (
52
).

From the fact that
\tilde{u}_{z}
is continuous on
\Omega
, the same estimate holds replacing osc
{}_{U\setminus N_{z}}\tilde{u}_{z}
with osc
{}_{U}\,\tilde{u}_{z}
.
Let
N=\,\cup_{z\in\mathbb{Q}^{m}}N_{z}
. Then
N
is still negligible and, by (
49
), it follows that

(53)

\begin{split}&|d_{2}(z,f(x))-d_{2}(z,f(y))|^{Q}\leq\,({\rm osc}_{U}\tilde{u}_{z})^{Q}\\
&\leq\,\widetilde{C}\left(\int_{0}^{+\infty}\varphi^{\frac{1}{Q}}(s)\,ds\right)^{Q-1}\int_{U}F_{\varphi}(g(x))\,dx\end{split}

for each
x,\,y\in U\setminus N
and
z\in\mathbb{Q}^{m}
, for each CC open ball
U\subset\Omega
. Now observe that, by the triangle inequality and density of
\mathbb{Q}^{m}
in
\mathbb{G}_{2}\equiv\mathbb{R}^{m}
, it follows that

(54)

\sup_{z\in\mathbb{Q}^{m}}|d_{2}(z,f(x))-d_{2}(z,f(y))|=\,d_{2}(f(x),f(y))

for each
x,\,y\in U\setminus N
. Thus, from (
53
) and (
54
), it follows that

(55)

d_{2}(f(x),f(y))^{Q}\leq\widetilde{C}\left(\int_{0}^{+\infty}\varphi^{\frac{1}{Q}}(s)\,ds\right)^{Q-1}\int_{U}F_{\varphi}(g(x))\,dx,

for each
x,\,y\in U\setminus N
and each CC open ball
U\subset\Omega
. This implies that
f:\,\Omega\setminus N\to\mathbb{G}_{2}
is locally uniformly continuous and, since
\Omega\setminus N
is dense in
\Omega
, it can be extended to a (unique) continuous function
f:\Omega\to\mathbb{G}_{2}
. Moreover, from (
55
), it follows also that

({\rm osc}_{U}f)^{Q}\leq\,\widetilde{C}\left(\int_{0}^{+\infty}\varphi^{\frac{1}{Q}}(s)\,ds\right)^{Q-1}\int_{U}F_{\varphi}(g(x))\,dx,

for each CC open ball
U\subset\Omega
.
This means that
f
satisfies the condition (RR) with weight

w(x)=\widetilde{C}\left(\int_{0}^{+\infty}\varphi^{\frac{1}{Q}}(s)\,ds\right)^{Q-1}F_{\varphi}(g(x)).\qed

Remark 4.3
.

Let us recall that Theorem
4.2
is sharp already in the Euclidean case. Indeed , Stein
[
56
]
pointed out that, if
\nabla f\notin L^{n,1}
, then both continuity and differentiability of
f
may fail.
For instance, an explicit example is given by the function
u(x):=\log(\log(1/|x|))
on
B(0,1/3)
extended in a smooth way on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with compact support. By a direct computation it is easy to prove that
u\in W^{1,n}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and moreover
|\nabla u|\in L^{n,q}
for every
q>1
(see
[
55
]
for the definition of general Lorentz spaces).
If
\{q_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is an enumeration of the rational points in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we set
f_{k}(x)=u(x-q_{k})
and define

f(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}2^{-k}f_{k}(x).

Then such a map
f
, and its weak gradient, have the same summability properties of
u
, but it is essentially unbounded (thus discontinuous and not differentiable) around any point. Hence Theorem
4.2
is sharp also in the refined class of Lorentz spaces.

Remark 4.4
.

Note that Theorem
4.2
is not invertible, in the following sense: even though
\Omega
is a bounded open set of an Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, it is well known that a function
f\in AC^{n}(\Omega)
need not admit weak gradient
\nabla f
in
L^{n,1}(\Omega)
(see, for instance,
[
30
, Theorem 3.7]
).

5.
Area formula and change of variables for absolutely continuous functions between Carnot groups

An important property of Lipschitz functions between Euclidean spaces is the so called area formula, relating the Hausdorff measure of the image of a Lipschitz map (counted with multiplicity) to the integral of the Jacobian on the domain. Such a formula holds also in the setting of Carnot groups (Theorem
5.3
); we will extend this result to the case of absolutely continuous functions (see Theorem
5.7
).

5.1.
Area formula between Carnot groups

The content of this section mainly relies on
[
39
]
.

An important tool in Euclidean geometric measure theory is the so-called
area formula
for Lipschitz maps, which turns to be true also for Lipschitz maps between Carnot groups.

Definition 5.1
.

[H-Jacobian] Given two Carnot groups
(\mathbb{G}_{i},d_{i})
,
i=1,2
, endowed with an invariant distance
d_{i}
, let
L:\,\mathbb{G}_{1}\to\mathbb{G}_{2}
be a
H
- linear map. Let
Q
denote the homogeneous dimension of
\mathbb{G}_{1}
. The
horizontal jacobian
of
L
is defined by

J_{Q}(L):=\frac{\mathcal{H}_{d_{2}}^{Q}(L(B_{1}))}{\mathcal{H}_{d_{1}}^{Q}(B_{1})}

where
B_{1}
denotes the unit open ball in
(\mathbb{G}_{1},d_{1})
and for
i=1,2
,
\mathcal{H}_{d_{i}}^{Q}
denotes the Hausdorff measure w.r.t.
d_{i}
.

Remark 5.2
.

In the previous definition it is possible to replace
B_{1}
with any other measurable subset of
\mathbb{G}_{1}
of positive finite measure, by the
H
-linearity of the map
L
.

The following Theorem is taken from
[
39
, Theorem 4.4]
.

Theorem 5.3
.

(Area formula for Lipschitz maps)
Let
f:\,E\subset(\mathbb{G}_{1},d_{1})\to(\mathbb{G}_{2},d_{2})
be a Lipschitz continuous function with
E
measurable set. Then

\int_{E}J_{Q}(d_{P}f(x))\,d\mathcal{H}^{Q}_{d_{1}}(x)=\,\int_{\mathbb{G}_{2}}N(f,E,y)\,d\mathcal{H}^{Q}_{d_{2}}(y)

where
N(f,E,\cdot)
denotes the multiplicity function of
f
relatively to
E
, that is
N(f,E,y)=\#\{f^{-1}(y)\cap E\}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{+\infty\}
.

Remark 5.4
.

The original definition of P-differentiability (cf. Definition
2.5
) requires
E
to be an open subset of a Carnot group
\mathbb{G}_{1}
. However, it is possible to extend this notion to the set of density points of a general measurable set. In particular, the Pansu’s differential, if it exists, is uniquely determined at a density point of
E
. Moreover, a generalization of Theorem
2.6
holds: it has been proved by Magnani in
[
39
, Theorem 3.9]
that a Lipschitz map
f:E\to\mathbb{G}_{2}
is
\mathcal{H}^{Q}_{d_{1}}
a.e. P-differentiable, where
E\subset\mathbb{G}_{1}
is measurable, while
Q
and
d_{1}
denote the homogeneous dimension and the CC distance of
\mathbb{G}_{1}
respectively.

Aim of this chapter is to extend Theorem
5.3
to the case of maps in
AC^{Q}(\Omega,\mathbb{G}_{2})
, which are not necessarily Lipschitz continuous.
The following useful result is proved in
[
35
, Lemma 2.12]
in the general setting of metric measure spaces.

Proposition 5.5

(Lusin’s (N) condition for
AC^{Q}
)
.

Let
\mathbb{G}_{1}\equiv\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mathbb{G}_{2}\equiv\mathbb{R}^{m}
be Carnot groups and let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{G}_{1}
be an open set. Let
f\in AC^{Q}(\Omega,\mathbb{G}_{2})
, where
Q
denotes the homogeneous dimension of
\mathbb{G}_{1}
. Then, for every measurable subset
E\subset\Omega
, it holds

\mathcal{L}^{n}(E)=0\;\Longrightarrow\;\mathcal{H}^{Q}(f(E))=0.

The next result is inspired by Theorem 3.1.9 in
[
15
]
and adapted to our setting.

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{G}_{1}\equiv\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open set and
A\subset\Omega
be measurable. If
f:\Omega\to\mathbb{G}_{2}\equiv\mathbb{R}^{m}
has the property that

\limsup_{y\to x}\frac{d_{2}(f(y),f(x))}{d_{1}(y,x)}<+\infty\qquad\text{for all $x\in A$,}

then there exists a countable measurable partition
\{A_{k}\}_{k}
of
A
such that
f_{\upharpoonright A_{k}}
is Lipschitz continuous.

Proof.

For every
j\in\mathbb{N}
, we define

(56)

C_{j}:=\{z\in\Omega:d_{2}(f(y),f(z))\leq jd_{1}(y,z)\quad\text{for every }y\in U_{d_{1}}(z,1/j)\}.

It follows that
A
is contained in
\cup_{j}C_{j}
.
Indeed, let
a\in A,
so that

\limsup_{y\to a}\frac{d_{2}(f(y),f(a))}{d_{1}(y,a)}<+\infty.

This implies that there exists
j\in\mathbb{N}
and
\bar{r}>0
sufficiently small such that
\frac{d_{2}(f(y),f(a))}{d_{1}(y,a)}<j
for every
y\in U_{d_{1}}(a,\bar{r})
. Hence, up to increase
j
, we deduce that the previous inequality holds for every
y\in U_{d_{1}}(a,\frac{1}{j})
, which means that
a\in C_{j}
.

Notice that each
C_{j}
is closed in
\Omega
. In fact, let
x_{n}\in C_{j}
which converge to
x\in\Omega
as
n\to\infty
and let
y\in U_{d_{1}}(x,\frac{1}{j})
. If
n\in\mathbb{N}
is chosen sufficiently large, we can assume
\{x,y\}\subset U_{d_{1}}(x_{n},1/j)
. Hence

\begin{split}d_{2}(f(y),f(x))&\leq d_{2}(f(y),f(x_{n}))+d_{2}(f(x_{n}),f(x))\\
&\leq jd_{1}(y,x_{n})+jd_{1}(x_{n},x)\end{split}

and, passing to the limit as
n\to\infty
, we get
d_{2}(f(y),f(x))\leq jd_{1}(y,x)
, so that
x\in C_{j}
.

Exploiting the Lindelöf property of
\mathbb{G}_{1}\equiv\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we can write
C_{j}
as a countable union of closed sets
C_{j}^{i}
, for
i\in\mathbb{N}
, each of which of diameter less than
\frac{1}{j}
. If we relabel
\{C_{k}\}_{k}=\{C_{j}^{i}\}_{j,i}
, it follows that
f_{\upharpoonright C_{k}}
is Lipschitz by (
56
). To conclude, define
A_{k}:=C_{k}\cap A
which are still measurable and notice that we can suppose the sets
A_{k}
to be disjoint (hence a partition of
A
) by the usual differences procedure.
∎

We now combine Theorem
5.6
and Theorem
5.3
to get an area formula for absolutely continuous maps between Carnot groups.

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{G}_{1}
be open and
f\in AC^{Q}(\Omega,\mathbb{G}_{2})
. For every measurable subset
E
of
\Omega
, we have

(57)

\int_{E}J_{Q}(d_{P}f(x))\,d\mathcal{H}^{Q}_{d_{1}}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{G}_{2}}N(f,E,y)\,d\mathcal{H}^{Q}_{d_{2}}(y)

where
J_{Q}
is the Jacobian introduced in Definition
5.1
, while
N(f,E,\cdot)
denotes the multiplicity function of
f
relatively to
E
.

Proof.

We note that, by Theorem
3.7
, Lip
(f)
is finite almost everywhere, which means

\text{Lip}(f)(x):=\limsup_{y\to x}\frac{d_{2}(f(y),f(x))}{d_{1}(y,x)}<+\infty\quad for\,\,\mathcal{L}^{n}-a.e.\,x\in\Omega.

We can therefore apply Theorem
5.6
and write
\Omega
as a disjoint union of measurable subsets

\Omega=A_{0}\cup\bigcup_{k=1}^{+\infty}A_{k},

with
\mathcal{L}^{n}(A_{0})=0
and
f_{\upharpoonright A_{k}}
Lipschitz.

Moreover we know, again by Theorem
3.7
, that
f
is P-differentiable almost everywhere. In the negligible set where
f
does not satisfy this condition, we can define
d_{P}f(x)
to be for instance the zero map.

Let now
E
be a measurable subset of
\Omega
and denote
E_{k}:=E\cap A_{k}
. Applying Theorem
5.3
we get, for every
k\geq 1
,

\int_{E_{k}}J_{Q}(d_{P}f_{\upharpoonright E_{k}}(x))\,d\mathcal{H}^{Q}_{d_{1}}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{G}_{2}}N(f,E_{k},y)\,d\mathcal{H}^{Q}_{d_{2}}(y).

Moreover this identity trivially holds for
k=0
in view of Lemma
5.5
. Note that, if
x
is a density point of
E_{k}
and
f:\Omega\to\mathbb{G}_{2}
is P-differentiable at
x\in E_{k}
, then also
f_{\upharpoonright E_{k}}
is P-differentiable at
x
in the sense of Remark
5.4
and the two differentials coincide. Hence, summing over
k\in\mathbb{N}
we find, by a simple application of monotone convergence,

\begin{split}&\int_{E}J_{Q}(d_{P}f(x))\,d\mathcal{H}^{Q}_{d_{1}}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\int_{E_{k}}J_{Q}(d_{P}f_{\upharpoonright E_{k}}(x))\,d\mathcal{H}^{Q}_{d_{1}}(x)\\
&=\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{G}_{2}}N(f,E_{k},y)\,d\mathcal{H}^{Q}_{d_{2}}(y)=\int_{\mathbb{G}_{2}}N(f,E,y)\,d\mathcal{H}^{Q}_{d_{2}}(y).\qed\end{split}

Through standard arguments of approximation by simple functions, it is easy to extend formula (
57
) to a more general form:

Corollary 5.8
.

If
f\in AC^{Q}(\Omega,\mathbb{G}_{2})
, then for any measurable subset
E
of
\Omega
and each summable function
u:E\to\mathbb{R}
, it holds

\int_{E}u(x)J_{Q}(d_{P}f(x))\,d\mathcal{H}^{Q}_{d_{1}}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{G}_{2}}\sum_{x\in f^{-1}(y)}u(x)\,d\mathcal{H}^{Q}_{d_{2}}(y).

As a straightforward consequence of Theorem
4.2
and Theorem
5.7
, we get the following

Corollary 5.9
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{G}_{1}
be an open set and let
f\in W^{1,1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{G}_{2})
such that the function
g
in (
13
) belongs to
L^{Q,1}(\Omega)
. Then a suitable representative of
f
satisfies

\int_{E}J_{Q}(d_{P}f(x))\,d\mathcal{H}^{Q}_{d_{1}}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{G}_{2}}N(f,E,y)\,d\mathcal{H}^{Q}_{d_{2}}(y)

for every measurable subset
E\subset\Omega
. Moreover, for every
u:E\to\mathbb{R}
summable,

\int_{E}u(x)J_{Q}(d_{P}f(x))\,d\mathcal{H}^{Q}_{d_{1}}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{G}_{2}}\sum_{x\in f^{-1}(y)}u(x)\,d\mathcal{H}^{Q}_{d_{2}}(y).

6.
Appendix: Orlicz-Sobolev spaces on Carnot groups

Orlicz spaces arise naturally in many applications in real, harmonic or functional analysis. Such spaces were introduced by the Polish mathematician Władysław Orlicz in 1932 and are generalizations of the classical
L^{p}
spaces. Indeed, the role played by the function
t^{p}
is replaced by more general convex maps, called
N
-functions.

We recall now the main definitions and basic properties of Orlicz spaces: see for instance
[
1
]
,
[
36
]
or
[
37
]
for an exhaustive treatment.

Definition 6.1
.

A map
A:[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
is called an N-function if it is convex, vanishes only at
0
and has the following limit properties:

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{A(t)}{t}=0,\qquad\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{A(t)}{t}=+\infty.

It is easy to verify that a map satisfying these properties is actually continuous on
[0,+\infty)
and strictly increasing.
Given an
N
-function
A:[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
, its
Young conjugate

\widetilde{A}
is defined as the
N
-function

\widetilde{A}(t):=\sup_{s>0}\,\{st-A(s)\}.

Examples of
N
-functions and associated conjugates are for instance

\begin{split}A(t)=\frac{t^{p}}{p},&\qquad\widetilde{A}(t)=\frac{t^{p^{\prime}}}{p^{\prime}}\qquad\left(1<p<+\infty,\quad p^{\prime}=\frac{p}{p-1}\right);\\
A(t)=e^{t}-&t-1,\qquad\widetilde{A}(t)=(1+t)\log(1+t)-t.\end{split}

Definition 6.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be measurable and let
A
be an
N-
function. For every measurable map
u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
, we define its Luxemburg norm as

\|u\|_{L^{A}(\Omega)}:=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\int_{\Omega}A\left(\frac{|u(x)|}{\lambda}\right)\,dx\leq 1\right\}.

The Orlicz space associated to the
N
- function
A
is defined as

L^{A}(\Omega):=\{u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}\text{ measurable}:\|u\|_{L^{A}(\Omega)}<+\infty\}.

Equipped with
\|\cdot\|_{L^{A}(\Omega)}
, the Orlicz space
L^{A}(\Omega)
is a Banach space. Moreover,
an interesting relation between an
N-
function and its Young conjugate is expressed by the following Hölder-type inequality:

\int_{\Omega}u(x)v(x)\,dx\leq 2\|u\|_{L^{A}(\Omega)}\|v\|_{L^{\widetilde{A}}(\Omega)}.

Notice that, when considering
A(t):=t^{p}
, for
1<p<+\infty
, we get that
L^{A}(\Omega)
coincides with the classical
L^{p}(\Omega)
space. Moreover in this case we get
\|\cdot\|_{L^{A}(\Omega)}=p^{-1/p}\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}
.

In order to establish an inclusion relation similar to the classical one for Lebesgue spaces on domain with finite measure, we introduce two new concepts: if
A
and
B
are
N
-functions, we will say that
B
dominates
A
globally
if there exists a positive constant
k
such that
A(t)\leq B(kt)
for all
t\geq 0
. If this estimate holds only for large values of
t
, we will say that
B
dominates
A
near infinity
.
An interesting result is that the embedding
L^{B}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{A}(\Omega)
holds if and only if either
B
dominates
A
globally or
B
dominates
A
near infinity and
|\Omega|<+\infty.

Hence functions which are globally equivalent, i.e. each one dominates the other globally, determine the same Orlicz space (and clearly the same holds if we require that they are equivalent near infinity and
|\Omega|<+\infty
).

Note that, if
1<p<q<+\infty
, then the map
B(t):=t^{q}
dominates the function
A(t):=t^{p}
near infinity, so that
L^{q}(\Omega)\subset L^{p}(\Omega)
if
\Omega
has finite measure.

In the same spirit of Sobolev space, it is now straightforward to define Orlicz-Sobolev functions by means of Orlicz integrability conditions on the weak derivatives. We give this definition directly in the general setting of Carnot groups:

Definition 6.3
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{G}\equiv\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open and let
A
be an
N
-function.
We define the Orlicz-Sobolev space

W^{1,A}_{\mathbb{G}}(\Omega):=\{u\in L^{A}(\Omega):\exists X_{i}u\in L^{A}(\Omega)\quad i=1,\dots,m_{1}\},

where
X_{i}u
are the distributional derivatives of
u
computed along the generating vector fields of the group.
We also set

\|u\|_{W^{1,A}_{\mathbb{G}}(\Omega)}:=\|u\|_{L^{A}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u\|_{L^{A}(\Omega)}.

Equipped with such a norm, it is easy to see that
(W^{1,A}_{\mathbb{G}}(\Omega),\|\cdot\|_{W^{1,A}_{\mathbb{G}}(\Omega)})
is a Banach space. If
\mathbb{G}
is the Euclidean
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we will use the notation
W^{1,A}(\Omega)
.

The classical embedding theorems for Sobolev spaces have been extended also to Orlicz-Sobolev spaces in the Euclidean setting. In particular, the condition
p>n
has to be replaced by a more general requirement on the growth of the
N
-function which induces the space. In
[
1
]
or
[
14
]
it has been proven that, if
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is sufficiently regular and
A
is an
N
-function, then
W^{1,A}(\Omega)
is embedded in the space of continuous and bounded maps provided that the condition

(58)

\int_{1}^{+\infty}\frac{A^{-1}(t)}{t^{1+1/n}}\,dt<+\infty

is satisfied.
In the case of
A(t)=t^{p}
, we recover the classical embedding theorem for
p>n
.
However, as remarked in
[
11
]
, this condition is not sharp, since it can be improved by requiring

(59)

\int_{1}^{+\infty}\frac{\widetilde{A}(t)}{t^{1+n^{\prime}}}\,dt<+\infty,

which is a weaker assumption than (
58
) (see
[
58
]
). Indeed, G. Talenti proved in the same work that (
59
) is a sufficient condition for boundedness (see also
[
12
]
), while A. Cianchi showed in
[
11
]
that it is also the sharp condition for continuity. Moreover the same author proved in
[
2
]
that this condition is also the correct one to ensure differentiability almost everywhere.

The strategy of Cianchi relies on rearrangement tools, while in the following we will provide an alternative way to extend this result in the setting of Carnot groups, following the approach introduced by Kauhanen, Koskela and Malý in
[
31
]
, in the same spirit of Theorem
4.2
.

In order to reach this result, we need first to extend Definition
6.3
to the case of group-valued maps, inspired by Definition
2.18
. This generalization is not straightforward as for Euclidean spaces (in which it suffices to work in components), since a Carnot group cannot be seen in general as direct product of subgroups.

Definition 6.4
.

Let
\mathbb{G}_{1}\equiv\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{G}_{2}\equiv\mathbb{R}^{m}
be Carnot groups. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{G}_{1}
be open and let
A
be an
N
-function.

(i)

We say that a function
u:\Omega\to\mathbb{G}_{2}
belongs to the space
L^{A}(\Omega,\mathbb{G}_{2})
if
u:\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
is measurable and the map
u_{0}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
defined by
u_{0}(x)=d_{2}(0,u(x))
is in
L^{A}(\Omega)
;

(ii)

We say that a function
u:\Omega\to\mathbb{G}_{2}
belongs to the Orlicz-Sobolev space
W^{1,A}(\Omega,\mathbb{G}_{2})
if

•

u\in L^{A}(\Omega,\mathbb{G}_{2})
;

•

for all
z\in\mathbb{G}_{2}
, the functions
u_{z}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

(60)

u_{z}(x):=d_{2}(z,u(x))\quad\text{if }x\in\Omega

are in
W^{1,A}_{\mathbb{G}_{1},\rm loc}(\Omega)
(according to Definition
6.3
);

•

There exists a nonnegative function
g\in L^{A}(\Omega)
(which does not depend on
z
) such that

|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}_{1}}u_{z}|\leq g\quad\text{a.e. in }\Omega,\;\;\text{for each }z\in\mathbb{G}_{2}.

We provide now the natural extension of Theorem
3.6
and Theorem
4.2
to the case of Orlicz-Sobolev maps: under a sufficient growth condition on
A
, functions in the space
W^{1,A}(\Omega,\mathbb{G}_{2})
are
Q
-absolutely continuous and so enjoy nice regularity properties.

The following Lemma represents the key step in order to apply the strategy proposed by the authors in
[
31
]
also to the case of Orlicz-Sobolev spaces.

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{G}\equiv\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open with
\mathcal{L}^{n}(\Omega)<+\infty
.
Let
A
be an N-function satisfying

(61)

\int_{1}^{+\infty}\left(\frac{t}{A(t)}\right)^{\frac{1}{Q-1}}\,dt<+\infty,

and let
g
be a nonnegative function in the space
L^{A}(\Omega)
.
Then there exists a nonincreasing function
\varphi:(0,+\infty)\to(0,+\infty)
such that

(62)

\int_{\Omega}F_{\varphi}(g(x))\,dx<+\infty

and

(63)

\int_{0}^{+\infty}\varphi^{1/Q}(t)\,dt<+\infty.

Proof.

Notice first that we can assume that

(64)

\int_{0}^{1}\left(\frac{t}{A(t)}\right)^{\frac{1}{Q-1}}\,dt<+\infty,

since it is always possible to replace
A
with another
N-
function which is equivalent near infinity (so that it determines the same Orlicz space) and decays sufficiently slow to 0.

Since
g\in L^{A}(\Omega)
, there exists for sure
\bar{\lambda}>0
such that
\int_{\Omega}A(\bar{\lambda}g(x))\,dx<+\infty
.
Define
\varphi:(0,+\infty)\to(0,+\infty)
as

\varphi(t):=\left(\frac{A(\bar{\lambda}t)}{t}\right)^{\frac{Q}{1-Q}}.

By definition
\varphi
is positive and also nonincreasing. In fact, by convexity of
A
,
we know that
A(\lambda t_{1}+(1-\lambda)t_{2})\leq\lambda A(t_{1})+(1-\lambda)A(t_{2})
for every
\lambda\in(0,1),\,t_{1},t_{2}\in[0,+\infty).

Consider
0<t<s
and apply this inequality with
t_{1}=\bar{\lambda}s,\,t_{2}=0,\,\lambda=\frac{t}{s}
.
We get
A(\bar{\lambda}t)\leq\frac{t}{s}A(\bar{\lambda}s)
and, by simple rearrangements,
\varphi(t)\geq\varphi(s)
.
Moreover we can calculate

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}F_{\varphi}(g(x))\,dx&=\int_{\{g>0\}}g(x)\left[\left(\frac{A(\bar{\lambda}g(x))}{g(x)}\right)^{\frac{Q}{1-Q}}\right]^{\frac{1-Q}{Q}}\,dx\\
&=\int_{\Omega}A(\bar{\lambda}g(x))\,dx<+\infty\end{split}

and also, exploiting (
61
) and (
64
),

\int_{0}^{+\infty}\varphi^{1/Q}(t)\,dt=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left(\frac{A(\bar{\lambda}t)}{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{1-Q}}dt=\bar{\lambda}^{\frac{Q}{1-Q}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left(\frac{t}{A(t)}\right)^{\frac{1}{Q-1}}\,dt<+\infty.\qed

Remark 6.6
.

It is proved in
[
12
]
that condition (
61
) is indeed equivalent to the following:

(65)

\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{\widetilde{A}(t)}{t^{1+Q^{\prime}}}\,dt<+\infty,

which clearly extends (
59
) in the case of Carnot groups of homogeneous dimension
Q
.

Under condition (
61
), or equivalently (
65
), functions in the space
W^{1,A}(\Omega,\mathbb{G}_{2})
are
Q-
absolutely continuous. Actually, only an Orlicz bound on the weak horizontal gradient of the sections is needed. This is expressed by the following result.

Theorem 6.7
.

Let
\mathbb{G}_{1}\equiv\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\mathbb{G}_{2}\equiv\mathbb{R}^{m}
be Carnot groups and let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{G}_{1}
open. Suppose also that
\mathcal{L}^{n}(\Omega)<+\infty
and denote by
Q
the homogeneous dimension of
\mathbb{G}_{1}
. Let
A
be an N-function satisfying (
61
).
If
f\in W^{1,1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{G}_{2})
and there exists a function
g\in L^{A}(\Omega)
such that

|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u_{z}|\leq g\qquad\text{for all }z\in\mathbb{G}_{2},

where
u_{z}
are defined as in (
60
),
then there exists a representative of
f
which is continuous and satisfies the (RR) condition. In particular
f\in AC^{Q}(\Omega,\mathbb{G}_{2})
and is P-differentiable a.e. in

7.
Appendix: Differentiation for maps between metric spaces and metric space-valued Sobolev maps

7.1.
Differentiation for maps between metric spaces:

In this section we will assume that the metric measure space
X\equiv(X,d_{X},\mu)
admits a
p
-Poincaré inquality with
p\geq\,1
, the ambient measure
\mu
is doubling and
Y\equiv(Y,d_{Y})
is a (separable) metric space.

Let us recall that a general differentiation theory has been carried out for maps
f:X\to Y
, whenever
Y
can be isometrically embedded into a Banach space
V
satisfying the
Radon-Nikodym property
(RNP) (see
[
9
,
10
]
). We recall that a Banach space
V
sarisfies (RNP) if each Lipschitz function
f:\,\mathbb{R}\to V
is differentiable a.e. The first pioneering result is due to Cheeger
[
8
]
when
Y=\,\mathbb{R}
, who was able to endow the metric measure space
X
by a so-called
Cheeger differentiable structure
and proved a Rademacher differentiability theorem for Lipschitz function in this context. This result was remarkably extended by Cheeger and Kleiner to maps with target space
Y=V
satisfying (RNP).
Then, whenever the target space
Y
can be bi-Lipschitz embedded into a Banach space
V
satisfying (RNP), a differentiation theory can be carried out also for maps
f:\Omega\subset X\to Y\subset V
and one can get a Stepanov’s type theorem (see, for instance,
[
63
]
). Moreover metric space-valued Sobolev functions can be introduced by means of the notion of
upper gradient
in this setting as in
[
27
, Chap. 7]
).

This approach may not work, as concern the differentiability issue, if the target space is a Carnot group. Indeed, as showed in
[
9
, Theorem 6.1]
, the simplest Carnot group
\mathbb{H}^{1}
does not admit a bi-Lipschitz embedding into any Banach space satisfying (RNP).

7.2.
Metric space-valued Sobolev maps:

As far as the notion of metric space-valued Sobolev mapping is concerned, different equivalent approaches can be introduced. Let us point out here two ones related to our arguments: the approach by
embedding of the target space
and the one by
sections of mappings
. From now on we can weaken the assumptions on the involved metric spaces. More precisely, we assume that
X\equiv(X,d_{X},\mu)
is a metric measure space with ambient measure
\mu

finite on bounded sets
, that is
\mu(E)<\,\infty
for each bounded
E\subset X
, and
Y\equiv(Y,d_{Y},y_{0})
is a pointed complete and separable metric space. Moreover, we will denote by
B(x,r)
and
U(x,r)
the closed and open ball of
X
centered at
x
with radius
r
, respectively.

Approach by embedding of the target metric space:
This approach extends the one due to Shanmugalingam
[
54
]

who introduced the notion of
Newtonian Sobolev space

N^{1,p}(X)
for real-valued functions defined on
X
and it was carried out in
[
27
, Chap. 7]
. Let us briefly recall here the main notions and results. We first introduce vector-valued Sobolev functions on metric spaces. If
V
is a Banach space and
1\leq\,p<\infty
, we say that a mapping
f:X\to V
belongs to the
Dirichlet space

D^{1,p}(X:V)
if
f
is measurable and it possesses a
p
-integrable
p
-weak
upper gradient
(see
[
27
, Section 6.2]
). When
V=\mathbb{R}
, we simply denote
D^{1,p}(X):=\,D^{1,p}(X:\mathbb{R})
. It is clear that
D^{1,p}(X:V)
is a vector space.

We denote as
Sobolev space of
V
-valued functions on
X
the space
N^{1,p}(X:V):=\,D^{1,p}(X:V)\cap L^{p}(X,V)
and
N^{1,p}(X:V)
turns out to be a Banach space with respect to the norm

\|u\|_{N^{1,p}(X:V)}:=\,\|u\|_{L^{p}(X,V)}+\|\varrho_{u}\|_{L^{p}(X)},

where
\varrho_{u}
denotes the minimal
p
-weak upper gradient of
u
guaranteed by
[
27
, Chap. 6 and Theorem 6.3.20]
.

Consider now the
Kuratowski isometric embedding
of
Y
into
V:=\,\ell^{\infty}(Y)
, with respect to the base point
y_{0}
. More precisely, let
\ell^{\infty}(Y)
denote the Banach space of bounded functions from
Y
to
\mathbb{R}
, equipped with the norm

\|\varphi\|_{\ell^{\infty}(Y)}:=\,\sup_{y\in Y}|\varphi(y)|\quad\text{ if }\varphi\in\ell^{\infty}(Y)\,.

Then the mapping

\begin{split}Y\ni y\mapsto\,&\varphi_{y}\in\ell^{\infty}(Y)\\
&\varphi_{y}(z):=\,d(z,y)-d(y_{0},z)\quad z\in Y\end{split}

is an isometric embedding (see
[
27
, Theorem 4.1]
).
According to
[
27
]
, we can introduce the Sobolev and Dirichlet classes of mappings between metric spaces
X
to
Y
(depending on the base point
y_{0}
) as follows:

N^{1,p}(X:Y):=\left\{f\in N^{1,p}(X:\ell^{\infty}(Y)):f(x)\in Y\,\mu\text{-a.e. }x\in X\right\}

and similarly

D^{1,p}(X:Y):=\left\{f\in D^{1,p}(X:\ell^{\infty}(Y)):f(x)\in Y\,\mu\text{-a.e. }x\in X\right\}\,.

Moreover we denote as
N_{\rm loc}^{1,p}(X:Y)
(
D_{\rm loc}^{1,p}(X:Y)
, respectively) the class of all maps
f:\,X\to\ell^{\infty}(Y)
such that each
x\in X
has a small radius
r>\,0
for which
f|_{U(x,r)}\in N^{1,p}(U(x,r):Y)
(
f|_{U(x,r)}\in D^{1,p}(U(x,r):Y)
, respectively). Eventually we denote as
L^{p}_{\rm loc}(X,\mu)
the class of measurable maps
f\to\bar{\mathbb{R}}
such that
\int_{E}|f|^{p}\,d\mu<\infty
for each bounded set
E\subset X
.
Let us recall the following characterizations of
N^{1,p}(X:Y)
and
D^{1,p}(X:Y)
.

Theorem 7.1
.

(
[
27
, Theorem 7.1.20]
) Let
V
be a Banach space and let
f:\,X\to V
be a measurable function. The the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

f
has a
\mu
-representative in the Dirichlet class
D^{1,p}(X:V)
;

(ii)

there esists a
p
-integrable Borel function
\varrho:\,X\to[0,\infty]
with the following property: for each
1
-Lipschitz function
\varphi:V\to\mathbb{R}
there esists a
\mu
-representative
f_{\varphi}
of the function
\varphi\circ f
in
D^{1,p}(X)
so that the minimal upper gradient
\varrho_{f_{\varphi}}
of
f_{\varphi}
satisfies
\varrho_{f_{\varphi}}\leq\,\varrho

\mu
-a.e. in
X
.

Theorem 7.2
.

(
[
27
, Proposition 7.1.38]
) A measurable map
f:X\to Y
is in
D^{1,p}(X:Y)
if and only if there is a non-negative
p
-integrable Borel function
\varrho
on
X
such that whenever
\gamma:\,[0,\ell(\gamma)]\to X
is a non constant rectifiable curve in
X

d_{Y}(f\left(\gamma(\ell(\gamma))\right),f\left(\gamma(0)\right))\leq\,\int_{\gamma}\varrho\,ds\,,

where
\ell(\gamma)
denotes the length of
\gamma
and

\int_{\gamma}\varrho\,ds:=\,\int_{0}^{\ell(\gamma)}\varrho(\gamma(t))\,dt\,.

Moreover, if
\mu(X)
is finite, then
f\in N^{1,p}(X:Y)
if and only if
f\in D^{1,p}(X:Y)
and the function
X\ni x\mapsto d_{Y}(y_{0},f(x))
is
p
-integrable for some (and hence every )
y_{0}\in Y
.

Theorem
7.2
shows that there is an intrinsic way of determining

D^{1,p}(X:Y)
and

that is independent of the

into a Banach space. Instead, membership in

(as well as in

depends on the specific embedding and, in particular, on the base point

.

Approach by sections of mappings:

It is an extension of the one exploited in Definition
2.18
, first introduced by Ambrosio
[
3
]
, for the case of
BV
functions, and then by Reshetnyak
[
53
]
, for the case of Sobolev functions. More precisely, first one provides the notion of a suitable real-valued Sobolev space
W^{1,p}(X)
and, for each
u\in W^{1,p}(X)
a notion of modulus of the “weak gradient”
|\nabla_{X}u|
is introduced. For instance, by using the previous Newtonian Sobolev space
N^{1,p}(X)
, the minimal
p
-weak upper gradient
\varrho_{u}
plays the role of
|\nabla_{X}u|
(see, for instance,
[
4
,
5
]
). In the following we assume that
W^{1,p}(X)=\,N^{1,p}(X)
. Then, given a mapping
f:X\to Y
, we say that
f\in W^{1,p}(X,Y)
if

•

the map
X\ni x\mapsto d_{Y}(y_{0},f(x))
belongs to
L^{p}(X,\mu)
;

•

for each
y\in Y
, the section
u_{y}:\,X\to\mathbb{R}
,

(66)

u_{y}(x):=\,d_{Y}(y,f(x))\text{ if }x\in X\text{ belongs to $D^{1,p}(X)$}

and there exists
g\in L^{p}(X,\mu)
such that

(67)

\varrho_{u_{y}}\leq g\,\,\mu\text{-a.e. in $X$, for each $y\in Y$}\,,

denoting by
\varrho_{u}
the minimal
p
-weak upper gradient of
u\in D^{1,p}(X)
. A function

as in (
67
) will be called a
p
-weak upper gradient of

.

As usual, let us denote by
W^{1,p}_{\rm loc}(X,Y)
the class of all maps
f:\,X\to Y
such that each

has a bounded open neighborhood

for which

.

As a consequence of Theorem
7.1
, for fixed

, we get the following coincidence of spaces:

Theorem 7.3
.

Let

. Then

W^{1,p}(X,Y)=\,N^{1,p}(X:Y)
.

Before the proof of Theorem

, we will introduce a preliminary result, which is an extension of
[

, Proposition 2.31]
to metric spaces.

Lemma 7.4
.

Let
1<\,p<\,\infty
,
f\in W^{1,p}(X,Y)
and let
\varphi:\,Y\to\mathbb{R}
be Lipschitz. Then

(i)

X\ni x\mapsto\varphi(f(x))-\varphi(y_{0})
is in
L^{p}(X,\mu)
.

(ii)

If
f_{\varphi}:=\,\varphi\circ f
, then
f_{\varphi}-\varphi(y_{0})\in N^{1,p}(X)
and
f_{\varphi}
admits a minimal
p
-weak upper gradient
\varrho_{f_{\varphi}}\leq L\,g

\mu
-a.e. in
X
, being

the function in (
67
) and
L:=\,Lip(\varphi)
.

Proof.

(i) By definition of
W^{1,p}(X,Y)
, the map
X\ni x\mapsto d_{Y}(y_{0},f(x))
is in
L^{p}(X,\mu)
. Being
\varphi
Lipschitz,

|\varphi(f(x))-\varphi(y_{0})|\leq\,L\,d_{Y}(y_{0},f(x))\text{ for each }x\in X\,,

and we get the desired conclusion.

(ii) Let
Z:=\,\{z_{i}:\,i\in\mathbb{N}\}\subset Y
be a dense set. Let us denote

\tilde{f}_{\varphi}:=\,f_{\varphi}-\varphi(y_{0}):\,X\to\mathbb{R}\text{ and }u_{i}:=\varphi(z_{i})+L\,u_{z_{i}}:\,X\to\mathbb{R}\,,

where
u_{z}
is the section of
f
defined in (
66
).
Note that we can represent
f_{\varphi}
as

(68)

\varphi(f(x))=\,\inf_{i\in\mathbb{N}}u_{i}(x)\text{ for each }x\in X\,.

Indeed, being
\varphi

|\varphi(f(x))-\varphi(z_{i})|\leq L\,d_{Y}(z_{i},f(x)).

This means that

\varphi(f(x))\leq\varphi(z_{i})+L\,d_{Y}(z_{i},f(x))=\varphi(z_{i})+L\,u_{z_{i}}(x)=u_{i}(x).

Moreover, consider a sequence
(i_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
such that

. We get

\begin{split}|u_{i_{k}}(x)-\varphi(f(x))|&=|\varphi(z_{i_{k}})+L\,d_{Y}(z_{i_{k}},f(x))-\varphi(f(x))|\\
&\leq|\varphi(z_{i_{k}})-\varphi(f(x))|+|L\,d_{Y}(z_{i_{k}},f(x))|\leq 2L\,d_{Y}(z_{i_{k}},f(x))\to 0,\end{split}

which implies (
68
).
Thus, from (
68
), it also follows that

(69)

\tilde{f}_{\varphi}(x)=\,\varphi(f(x))-\varphi(y_{0})=\,\inf_{i\in\mathbb{N}}(\tilde{u}_{i}(x))\,.

where
\tilde{u}_{i}:=\,u_{i}-\varphi(y_{0})
. Note also that
\tilde{u}_{i}\in D^{1,p}(X)\cap L^{p}_{\rm loc}(X,\mu)
with

(70)

\varrho_{\tilde{u}_{i}}\leq Lg\,\mu\text{-a.e. in $X$ for each $i\in\mathbb{N}$}

where
\varrho_{\tilde{u}_{i}}
denotes the minimal
p
-weak upper gradient of
\tilde{u}_{i}
and
g
a
p
-weak upper gradient of
f
, that is a function satisfying in (
67
).
Let
v_{j}:=\,\inf_{1\leq i\leq j}\tilde{u}_{i}
. From
[

),

is a lattice, that is

for each

and

(71)

\varrho_{v_{j}}\leq L\,g\quad\mu\text{-a.e. in $X$, for each $j$}\,.

Now observe that, since

(72)

\tilde{f}_{\varphi}\leq\,v_{j}\leq\,v_{1}\quad\text{for each $j$}\,,

from (
69
) and (
72
), by applying Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem, it follows that

(73)

v_{j}\to\tilde{f}_{\varphi}\text{ in }L^{p}_{\rm loc}(X,\mu)\text{ as $j\to\infty$.}

Let
x_{0}\in X
and
\Omega_{n}:=\,U(x_{0},n)
, if

. Then

(74)

and
\hat{f}
admits a

-weak upper gradient
\varrho_{\infty}
in

satisfying

(75)

\varrho_{\infty}\leq\,L\,g\quad\mu\text{-a.e. in }X.

By (

) and (

\tilde{f}_{\varphi}
admits a
\mu
-representative
\hat{f}\in N^{1,p}(X)
with minimal

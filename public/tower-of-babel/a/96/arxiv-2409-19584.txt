Adaptive sampling accelerates the hybrid deviational particle simulations
Zhengyang Lei, Sihong Shao

Abstract
To avoid ineffective collisions between the equilibrium states, the hybrid method with deviational particles (HDP) has been proposed to integrate the Vlasov-Poisson-Landau system, while leaving a new issue in sampling deviational particles from the high-dimensional source term. In this paper, we present an adaptive sampling (AS) strategy that first adaptively reconstructs a piecewise constant approximation of the source term based on sequential clustering via discrepancy estimation, and then samples deviational particles directly from the resulting adaptive piecewise constant function without rejection. The mixture discrepancy, which can be easily calculated thanks to its explicit analytical expression, is employed as a measure of uniformity instead of the star discrepancy the calculation of which is NP-hard. The resulting method, dubbed the HDP-AS method, samples deviational particles through adaptive sampling instead of the acceptance-rejection method in the original HDP method. In the Landau damping, two stream instability, bump on tail and Rosenbluth’s test problems, the HDP-AS method runs approximately ten times faster than the HDP method while keeping the same accuracy.

Introduction

As a fundamental mathematical model for the kinetic theory, the Vlasov–Poisson–Landau (VPL) system

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f+\boldsymbol{v}\cdot\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}f-\boldsymbol{E}\cdot\nabla_{\boldsymbol{v}}f

\displaystyle=Q(f,f),

(1)

\displaystyle-\nabla_{\bm{x}}\cdot\boldsymbol{E}

\displaystyle=\rho(\bm{x},t),

describes the evolution of the charged particles in phase space
(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{v})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}

[
1
,
2
]
, where
f=f(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{v},t)
gives the time-dependent distribution function,
\rho(\bm{x},t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{v},t)\textup{d}\bm{v}
the spatial density and
Q(f,f)
the Landau collision operator. The form of the VPL system (
1
) adopted in this paper is consistent with that presented in
[
3
]
. Numerical resolution of the VPL system encounters some fundamental obstacles due to the notorious curse of dimensionality and thus particle-based methods play a prevalent role benefiting from their (possible) dimension independence.
The DSMC-PIC method, an often-used particle method, which exploits the operator splitting technique and combines the direct simulation Monte Carlo (DSMC) (such as the TA method by Takizuka and Abe
[
4
]
and the Nanbu method
[
5
]
) for the collision step

\partial_{t}f=Q(f,f),

(2)

and the particle in cell (PIC)
[
6
,
7
]
for the advection step

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f+\boldsymbol{v}\cdot\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}f-\boldsymbol{E}\cdot\nabla_{\boldsymbol{v}}f

\displaystyle=0,

(3)

\displaystyle-\nabla_{\bm{x}}\cdot\boldsymbol{E}

\displaystyle=\rho(\bm{x},t),

has been widely recognized in the literature. Accordingly, a key challenge arises in the collision step (
2
) due to the high-dimensionality and nonlinearity of
Q(f,f)
. The DSMC may become inefficient when the distribution function
f(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{v},t)
approaches the equilibrium state, denoted by
m(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{v},t)
(see Eq. (
5
)), as the collisions between equilibrium parts satisfy
Q(m,m)\equiv 0
which results in no net effect.
To address this, a class of hybrid methods has been proposed to improve the efficiency via explicitly avoiding sampling from the ineffective collisions
Q(m,m)
by the decomposition

f(\bm{x},\bm{v},t)=m(\bm{x},\bm{v},t)+f_{d}(\bm{x},\bm{v},t),

e.g., the micro-macro approach for the BGK operator
[
8

, the thermalization/dethermalization method for the spatial homogeneous Coulomb collisions
[
9
]
and the low-variance deviational simulation Monte Carlo methods for rarefied gas
[
10

11

. For the VPL system (
1

to be negative
[
12
,
3

. Namely, the HDP method decomposes the distribution as follows

f(\bm{x},\bm{v},t)=m(\bm{x},\bm{v},t)+f_{p}(\bm{x},\bm{v},t)-f_{n}(\bm{x},\bm{v},t),

(4)

where
f_{p}(\bm{x},\bm{v},t)\geq 0
and
f_{n}(\bm{x},\bm{v},t)\geq 0
denote the positive part and negative part of the difference
f(\bm{x},\bm{v},t)-m(\bm{x},\bm{v},t)
, respectively.
After that, how to sample new deviational particles from the high-dimensional source term
Q(f_{p}-f_{n},m)
at each collision step (see Eq. (
11
)) becomes a new
issue. The source term is a 5-D integral with singularity. HDP ingeniously approximates it as a 1-D integral and samples new deviational particles from it using the acceptance-rejection method
[
12
]
. Nevertheless, this part remains very time-consuming in actual numerical simulations as a result of the low acceptance rate (see Figure
4
) and the time spent on it accounts for, e.g., roughly 90% of the total wall time (see Figure
1

[
13
]
, can be used to sample from general distribution. However, it is hard to find a high acceptance rate upper bound that is convenient to sample for the acceptance-rejection method and the Markov Chain Monte Carlo method may get stuck in configuration space when the proposal variance is too high
[
14
]
. This work aims to solve this high-dimensional sampling problem from
Q(f_{p}-f_{n},m)
at the collision step.

We propose an HDP method based on adaptive sampling (AS) for solving the VPL system (
1
). The resulting method, dubbed the HDP-AS method, reconstructs an adaptive piecewise constant approximation of
Q(f_{p}-f_{n},m)
from the point distribution, which can be readily sampled from (see Figure
2
for a quick demonstration), and the wall time consumed by sampling new deviational particles is significantly reduced (see Figure
1
).
This two-phase architecture — first constructing a piecewise constant approximation of the target distribution and then sampling from the resulting piecewise constant approximation —
can trace its lineage to the 1960s ziggurat algorithm
[
15
,
16
]

and dramatically enhance the sampling efficiency. Moreover, HDP-AS partially borrows the idea from the discrepancy-based density estimation
[
17
]
, which employs the star discrepancy to measure irregularity of points distribution and then constructs an adaptive approximation of high-dimensional unknown function.
Considering the fact that the complexity of calculating the star discrepancy is NP-hard
[
18
]
and a high computational cost may be inevitable for a large sample sizes, HDP-AS replaces the star discrepancy with the mixture discrepancy which can be calculated explicitly
[
19
]
.
The only difference between HDP and HDP-AS lies in that HDP samples new deviational particles by the acceptance-rejection method, whereas HDP-AS employs adaptive sampling. The other steps remain the same for both methods.
Numerical results for the Landau damping, two stream instability, bump on tail and Rosenbluth’s test problem demonstrates that HDP-AS achieves about ten times acceleration compared with HDP while maintaining the same accuracy.

2
, we first present some preliminaries of the VPL system, and then introduce related numerical attempts, including the TA method and the HDP method, which lay the groundwork for the proposed HDP-AS method. Section
3
details the HDP-AS method. Numerical experiments are conducted in Section
4
for comparing HDP-AS with HDP. The paper is concluded in Section
5
.
This paper addresses sampling new deviational particles from the source term in the collision step of the HDP method. Other components like the advection step’s macro-micro decomposition and collision step’s particle annihilation remain identical between HDP-AS and HDP and are thus omitted here for brevity.
The interested readers can refer to
[
12
,
3
]
for more details.

Preliminaries and background

The Landau collision operator in Eq. (
1
) modules the binary collisions governed by the long range Coulomb interaction and has the form

Q(f,f)(\bm{x},\bm{v},t)=\nabla_{\bm{v}}\cdot\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Phi\left(\bm{v}-\bm{v}^{*}\right)[\nabla_{\bm{v}}f(\bm{x},\bm{v},t)f\left(\bm{x},\bm{v}^{*},t\right)-\nabla_{\bm{v}^{*}}f\left(\bm{x},\bm{v}^{*},t\right)f(\bm{x},\bm{v},t)]\textup{d}\bm{v}^{*},

where
\Phi(\bm{v})=\frac{\boldsymbol{I}}{|\bm{v}|}-\frac{\bm{v}\otimes\bm{v}}{|\bm{v}|^{3}}
with
\boldsymbol{I}
being the
3\times 3
identity matrix.
For the equilibrium state given by the Maxwellian form

m(\bm{x},\bm{v},t)=\frac{\rho(\bm{x},t)}{(2\pi T(\bm{x},t))^{3/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{|\bm{v}-\boldsymbol{u}(\bm{x},t)|^{2}}{2T(\bm{x},t)}\right),

(5)

it can be easily verified that
Q(m,m)\equiv 0
, where the macroscopic velocity
\boldsymbol{u}
and temperature
T
are defined by

\boldsymbol{u}=\frac{1}{\rho}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f\bm{v}\textup{d}\bm{v},\quad T=\frac{1}{3\rho}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f|\bm{v}-\boldsymbol{u}|^{2}\textup{d}\bm{v}.

(6)

A hybrid representation of the distribution function, which involves both grids and particles, is widely used to integrate the VPL system (
1
). In the spatial direction, uniform grids
\mathcal{C}_{k},\,k=1,\dots,N_{x}
are constructed with
\bm{x}_{k}
being the center point
of
\mathcal{C}_{k}
. The macroscopic quantities
\rho
,
\boldsymbol{u}
and
T
depend only on the position, and thus are defined on the spatial grids. In the velocity direction, the particle representation is employed. The positive part
f_{p}
and negative part
f_{n}
are represented by particles called P-particles and N-particles respectively.
Denote the sets of these particles at
\bm{x}_{k}
and time
t
as
\mathbb{P}_{k}(t)
and
\mathbb{N}_{k}(t)
, the size of which is
N_{p,k}
and
N_{n,k}
, respectively.

To solve the VPL system (
1
), we also start with an operator splitting between the collision part (
2
) and the advection part (
3
).
In the HDP-AS method, the simulation of advection part employs the identical micro-macro decomposition framework
[
20
]
as that utilized in HDP, so it will not be repeated here. Below we illustrate how the TA
[
4
]
and HDP
[
3
]
methods simulate collisions in one time step
\Delta t
, which lay the groundwork for the proposed HDP-AS method.

2.1
The TA method

The Landau operator
Q(g,h)
describes the changes in
h
resulting from collisions with
g
and we refer to it as G-H collisions. The particles with the distribution
g
and that with the distribution
h
are respectively named as G-particles and H-particles. Denote the sets of G-particles and H-particles at grid point
\bm{x}_{k}
and time
t
as
\mathbb{G}_{k}(t)
and
\mathbb{H}_{k}(t)
, whose size is
N_{g,k}
and
N_{h,k}
, respectively.
It is required that
N_{g,k}\geq N_{h,k}
. The TA method randomly matches the H-particles with G-particles to simulate binary collisions.
Suppose the current velocities of a certain pair of particles are
\bm{v}^{g}
and
\bm{v}^{h}
, and the velocities after the collision are
\widetilde{\bm{v}}^{g}
and
\widetilde{\bm{v}}^{h}
. When the particle masses are equal, the conservation of momentum and energy yields

\displaystyle\bm{v}^{g}+\bm{v}^{h}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\bm{v}}^{g}+\widetilde{\bm{v}}^{h},

(7)

\displaystyle|\bm{v}^{g}|^{2}+|\bm{v}^{h}|^{2}

\displaystyle=|\widetilde{\bm{v}}^{g}|^{2}+|\widetilde{\bm{v}}^{h}|^{2},

(8)

and thus

\displaystyle\widetilde{\bm{v}}^{g}

\displaystyle=\bm{v}^{g}+\frac{1}{2}\Delta\boldsymbol{u},

(9)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\bm{v}}^{h}

\displaystyle=\bm{v}^{h}-\frac{1}{2}\Delta\boldsymbol{u},

where
\Delta\boldsymbol{u}=(\widetilde{\bm{v}}^{h}-\widetilde{\bm{v}}^{g})-(\bm{v}^{h}-\bm{v}^{g})
is the change of relative velocity. During the collision process, the magnitude of the relative velocity remains unchanged, but its direction is altered by the scattering angle
\Theta
and azimuthal angle
\Phi
, which characterize the nature of the Coulomb collisions. In the TA method,
\Phi
is chosen randomly with a uniform distribution in
\left[0,2\pi\right]
and
\Theta=2\arctan(\delta)
,
where
\delta
is sampled from the normal distribution
\mathcal{N}(0,C\Delta t)
and
C
is determined by some physical constants as well as the magnitude of the relative velocity (see
[
4
]
for more details). Let
\bm{v}^{h}-\bm{v}^{g}:=(u_{x},u_{y},u_{z})
and
u:=\sqrt{u_{x}^{2}+u_{y}^{2}+u_{z}^{2}}
. The change of relative velocity
\Delta\boldsymbol{u}:=(\Delta u_{x},\Delta u_{y},\Delta u_{z})
can be computed by

\displaystyle\Delta u_{x}

\displaystyle=\frac{u_{x}}{\sqrt{u_{x}^{2}+u_{y}^{2}}}u_{z}\sin\Theta\cos\Phi-\frac{u_{y}}{\sqrt{u_{x}^{2}+u_{y}^{2}}}u\sin\Theta\sin\Phi-u_{x}(1-\cos\Theta),

(10)

\displaystyle\Delta u_{y}

\displaystyle=\frac{u_{x}}{\sqrt{u_{x}^{2}+u_{y}^{2}}}u_{z}\sin\Theta\cos\Phi+\frac{u_{x}}{\sqrt{u_{x}^{2}+u_{y}^{2}}}u\sin\Theta\sin\Phi-u_{y}(1-\cos\Theta),

\displaystyle\Delta u_{z}

\displaystyle=-\sqrt{u_{x}^{2}+u_{y}^{2}}\sin\Theta\cos\Phi-u_{z}(1-\cos\Theta).

Once
\Delta\boldsymbol{u}
is determined, the velocities after the collision
\widetilde{\bm{v}}^{g}
and
\widetilde{\bm{v}}^{h}
can be obtained according to Eq. (

2.2
The HDP method

The TA method may become inefficient when the distribution
f(\bm{x},\bm{v},t)
approaches the equilibrium state
m(\bm{x},\bm{v},t)
, as the collisions between equilibrium parts do not produce actual effect. According to the decomposition (
4
), the HDP method solves the following system of equations
[
12
,
3
]

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}\tilde{f}=Q(\tilde{f},\tilde{f}),\\
\partial_{t}m=0,\\
\partial_{t}f_{p}=Q(\tilde{f},f_{p})+\left(Q\left(f_{p},m\right)-Q\left(f_{n},m\right)\right)_{+},\\
\partial_{t}f_{n}=Q(\tilde{f},f_{n})+\left(Q\left(f_{p},m\right)-Q\left(f_{n},m\right)\right)_{-},\end{array}\right.

(11)

explicitly removing the collisions
Q(m,m)
of the equilibrium parts, where
h_{\pm}=\frac{1}{2}\left(|h|\pm h\right)
gives the positive/negative part. Here the notation
\tilde{f}
is used to distinguish it from the desired solution
f=m+f_{p}-f_{n}
in Eq. (
4
). It is introduced in HDP as a coarse approximation of
f
and used in simulating collisions
Q(\tilde{f},f_{p})
and
Q(\tilde{f},f_{n})
. The distribution
\tilde{f}
is represented by F-particles and the set of these particles at
\bm{x}_{k}
and time
t
is denoted as
\mathbb{F}_{k}(t)
, the size of which is
N_{f,k}
. The three parts
\tilde{f},f_{p},f_{n}
are presented respectively by the F-particles, P-particles and N-particles, i.e.,
\forall\,\varphi(\boldsymbol{v})\in\mathbb{H}_{0}^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{3}\right)
,
\forall\,k\in\{1,\dots,N_{x}\}
,

\displaystyle\langle\varphi,\tilde{f}(\bm{x}_{k},\bm{v},t)\rangle

\displaystyle\approx N_{\text{eff }}^{F}\sum_{\bm{v}_{\tilde{f}}\in\mathbb{F}_{k}(t)}\varphi(\bm{v}_{\tilde{f}}),

(12)

\displaystyle\left\langle\varphi,f_{p}(\bm{x}_{k},\bm{v},t)\right\rangle

\displaystyle\approx N_{\text{eff }}\sum_{\bm{v}_{f_{p}}\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(t)}\varphi(\bm{v}_{f_{p}}),

\displaystyle\left\langle\varphi,f_{n}(\bm{x}_{k},\bm{v},t)\right\rangle

\displaystyle\approx N_{\text{eff }}\sum_{\bm{v}_{f_{n}}\in\mathbb{N}_{k}(t)}\varphi(\bm{v}_{f_{n}}),

where the standard inner product
\langle g,h\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}gh\textup{d}\bm{v}
is adopted. The effective number
N_{\text{eff }}^{F}
and
N_{\text{eff }}
represent the physical densities per numerical particle and satisfy

\displaystyle N_{f,k}N_{\text{eff }}^{F}\approx\rho(\bm{x}_{k},t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\tilde{f}(\bm{x}_{k},\bm{v},t)\textup{d}\bm{v},

(13)

\displaystyle N_{p,k}N_{\text{eff }}\approx\rho_{p}(\bm{x}_{k},t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{p}(\bm{x}_{k},\bm{v},t)\textup{d}\bm{v},

\displaystyle N_{n,k}N_{\text{eff }}\approx\rho_{n}(\bm{x}_{k},t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{n}(\bm{x}_{k},\bm{v},t)\textup{d}\bm{v}.

Eq. (
13
) can be obtained by taking
\varphi\equiv 1
in Eq. (
12
).
A key ingredient is that F-particles are not necessary to be a very accurate approximation of
f
, and one only needs
N_{f,k}\geq N_{p,k}+N_{n,k}
to ensure there are enough F-particles to simulate collisions
Q(\tilde{f},f_{p})
and
Q(\tilde{f},f_{n})
. An error analysis shows that F-particles introduces a negligible error (see Section 3.3 in
[
12
]
). Eq. (
11
) guides the evolution of the F-particles, P-particles and N-particles in
a prediction–correction manner.
The F-particles act as a prediction of
f
, and they are still simulated by the TA method.
As corrections, P-particles and N-particles contain two parts in their evolution: One is collisions with F particles, simulated using the TA method; the other is sampling new particles from the source term

\Delta m_{k}(\bm{v}):=\Delta t\left(Q\left(f_{p}(\bm{x}_{k},\bm{v},t),m(\bm{x}_{k},\bm{v},t)\right)-Q\left(f_{n}(\bm{x}_{k},\bm{v},t),m(\bm{x}_{k},\bm{v},t)\right)\right).

(14)

3
Adaptive sampling

Different from the acceptance-rejection sampling in the HDP method
[
12
,
3
]
, HDP-AS proposes an adaptive high-dimensional sampling approach to sample from
\Delta m_{k}(\bm{v})_{\pm}
required by Algorithm
2
.
There exist two steps in the HDP-AS method: (a) Provide an adaptive piecewise constant approximation of the function
\Delta m_{k}(\bm{v})
,
denoted by
\overline{\Delta m_{k}}(\bm{v})
, and (b) sample new deviational particles directly from
\overline{\Delta m_{k}}(\bm{v})

instead of
\Delta m_{k}(\bm{v})
. In contrast to the acceptance-rejection sampling, the implementation of sampling from a piecewise constant function in (b) is much more straightforward and without rejection. Moreover, we employ adaptive piecewise constants instead of uniform ones to alleviate the curse of dimensionality. As the implementation of (b) is straightforward, we focus exclusively on how to achieve (a) in an efficient manner in the following.

It involves two processes:
(a1) Simulate the Coulomb collisions
Q(f_{p},m)
and
Q(f_{n},m)
in the function
\Delta m_{k}(\bm{v})
via the TA method in Algorithm
1
and obtain the point distribution
W_{k}(\bm{v})
(see Eq. (
15
)), approximating
Q\left(f_{p},m\right)-Q\left(f_{n},m\right)
in the weak sense, and (a2) adaptively reconstruct
\overline{\Delta m_{k}}(\bm{v})
from the point distribution
W_{k}(\bm{v})
.
Denote the particles with the equilibrium distribution
m
as M-particles, which are not tracked and only sampled from (
5
) whenever needed. In
\Delta m_{k}(\bm{v})
,
Q(f_{p},m)
and
Q(f_{n},m)
represent that P-M collisions and N-M collisions respectively, both of which can be simulated by the TA method. Let
\mathbb{M}^{+}_{k}
and
\mathbb{M}^{-}_{k}
collect all the M-particles after P-M collisions and N-M collisions at grid point
\bm{x}_{k}
and time
t
, respectively. They can form a point distribution

of

(18)

(19)

\Omega_{l}

\displaystyle c_{l}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{1}{\mu\left(\Omega_{l}\right)}\int_{\Omega_{l}}(Q\left(f_{p}(\bm{x}_{k},\bm{v},t),m(\bm{x}_{k},\bm{v},t)\right)-Q\left(f_{n}(\bm{x}_{k},\bm{v},t),m(\bm{x}_{k},\bm{v},t)\right))\textup{d}\bm{v}

where

gives the Lebesgue measure of
\Omega_{l}
. A simple idea for the domain decomposition in Eq. (

\Omega_{l}

[
17
]
. It seeks an adaptive partition of
\Omega
via a recursive binary splitting until the particles inside each sub-domain
\Omega_{l}
are close to uniform distribution, which means that
\Delta m_{k}(\bm{v})
can be approximately considered as a constant within
\Omega_{l}
. Algorithm

and
\Omega_{1}=\Omega
. At level

is generated by splitting one of the regions in
\mathcal{P}_{L}
along one coordinate and then merging both resulting sub-domains with the remaining regions in
\mathcal{P}_{L}
.

Two key issues remains to be specified in the recursive binary splitting: Where to split and whether to split.

1.

Where to split
: Suppose
\Omega_{l}=\left[a_{1}^{(l)},b_{1}^{(l)}\right]\times\left[a_{2}^{(l)},b_{2}^{(l)}\right]\times\left[a_{3}^{(l)},b_{3}^{(l)}\right]
and
s_{i_{0},j_{0}}^{(l)}=a_{j_{0}}^{(l)}+\frac{i_{0}}{q}\left(b_{i_{0}}^{(l)}-a_{i_{0}}^{(l)}\right)
are some partition points to be selected where
i_{0}=1,2,\ldots,q-1
,

. To further divide the non-uniform sub-domains, the (sub)-optimal split points, denoted by
\widetilde{s}_{i_{0},j_{0}}^{(l)}

\widetilde{s}_{i_{0},j_{0}}^{(l)}=\underset{s_{i_{0},j_{0}}^{(l)}}{\arg\max}\left|\frac{N_{+}^{<}+N_{-}^{<}}{N_{+}+N_{-}}-\frac{i_{0}}{q}\right|,

(21)

where
\Omega_{l}
is divided into
\Omega_{l}^{(1)}
and
\Omega_{l}^{(2)}
,

\displaystyle\Omega_{l}^{(1)}=\prod_{j=1}^{j_{0}-1}\left[a_{j}^{(l)},b_{j}^{(l)}\right]\times\left[a_{j_{0}}^{(l)},s_{i_{0},j_{0}}^{(l)}\right]\times\prod_{j=j_{0}+1}^{3}\left[a_{j}^{(l)},b_{j}^{(l)}\right],

(22)

\displaystyle\Omega_{l}^{(2)}=\Omega_{l}\backslash\Omega_{l}^{(1)},

and
N_{+}^{<}
and
N_{-}^{<}
count the number of particles in
\mathbb{M}^{+}_{\text{cur}}
and
\mathbb{M}^{-}_{\text{cur}}
(see Lines
8
and
9
in Algorithm

) that fall within
\Omega_{l}^{(1)}
, respectively. The intuition behind Eq. (

) is to dig out the most non-uniform sub-domain after fixing the selectable partition points.

2.

Whether to split
: We use the discrepancies
D(\mathbb{M}^{+}_{\text{cur}})
and
D(\mathbb{M}^{-}_{\text{cur}})
as indicators to measure the uniformity of the points distribution. Small discrepancies indicate a relatively uniform particle distribution and no further partitioning is necessary. Discrepancy is a broad category and contains various specific definitions
[
21
,
22
]
. The star discrepancy is used in
[
17
]
, the definition of which for point set
\left(\bm{v}_{1},\dots,\bm{v}_{n}\right)\subset\left[0,1\right]^{3n}
is stated as follows

D^{*}\left(\bm{v}_{1},\dots,\bm{v}_{n}\right)=\sup_{\boldsymbol{u}\in[0,1]^{3}}\left|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbbm{1}_{[\mathbf{0},\boldsymbol{u})}\left(\bm{v}_{i}\right)-\operatorname{vol}([\mathbf{0},\boldsymbol{u}))\right|.

(23)

The complexity of calculating the star discrepancy is NP-hard. Although there are heuristic algorithms
[
18
,
23
]
, a high computational cost is likely to be unavoidable when the sample sizes are large.
To ensure efficient partitioning, we employ the mixture discrepancy (take
D
in Algorithm
4
as
D^{\text{mix}}
in Eq. (
24
)) to substitute the star discrepancy as the uniformity measure, which possesses an explicit expression
[
19
]

\displaystyle D^{\text{mix}}\left(\bm{v}_{1},\dots,\bm{v}_{n}\right)=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{19}{12}\right)^{3}-\frac{2}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{3}\left(\frac{5}{3}-\frac{1}{4}\left|v_{ij}-\frac{1}{2}\right|-\frac{1}{4}\left|v_{ij}-\frac{1}{2}\right|^{2}\right)

(24)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{3}\left(\frac{15}{8}-\frac{1}{4}\left|v_{ij}-\frac{1}{2}\right|-\frac{1}{4}\left|v_{kj}-\frac{1}{2}\right|\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\frac{3}{4}\left|v_{ij}-v_{kj}\right|+\frac{1}{2}\left|v_{ij}-v_{kj}\right|^{2}\right),

where
\bm{v}_{i}=(v_{i1},v_{i2},v_{i3})
. Let
\mathbb{M}^{+}_{\text{cur}}=\{\bm{v}_{1}^{+},\dots,\bm{v}_{N_{+}}^{+}\}
,
\mathbb{M}^{-}_{\text{cur}}=\{\bm{v}_{1}^{-},\dots,\bm{v}_{N_{-}}^{-}\}
. The discrepancy, for two sequences
\mathbb{M}^{+}_{\text{cur}}
and
\mathbb{M}^{-}_{\text{cur}}
in
\Omega_{l}
, can be defined by a linear scaling
\iota_{l}:\Omega_{l}\rightarrow\left[0,1\right]^{3}
,

\displaystyle D^{\text{mix}}(\mathbb{M}^{+}_{\text{cur}})

\displaystyle=D^{\text{mix}}(\iota_{l}(\bm{v}_{1}^{+}),\dots,\iota_{l}(\bm{v}_{N_{+}}^{+})),

(25)

\displaystyle D^{\text{mix}}(\mathbb{M}^{-}_{\text{cur}})

\displaystyle=D^{\text{mix}}(\iota_{l}(\bm{v}_{1}^{-}),\dots,\iota_{l}(\bm{v}_{N_{-}}^{-})),

with

\iota_{l}(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3})=\left(\frac{v_{1}-a_{1}^{(l)}}{b_{1}^{(l)}-a_{1}^{(l)}},\frac{v_{2}-a_{2}^{(l)}}{b_{2}^{(l)}-a_{2}^{(l)}},\frac{v_{3}-a_{3}^{(l)}}{b_{3}^{(l)}-a_{3}^{(l)}}\right).

(26)

The specified stopping criterion is

D^{\text{mix}}(\mathbb{M}^{+}_{\text{cur}})\leq\frac{\vartheta\sqrt{N}}{\max(N_{+},N_{-})},\quad D^{\text{mix}}(\mathbb{M}^{-}_{\text{cur}})\leq\frac{\vartheta\sqrt{N}}{\max(N_{+},N_{-})},

(27)

where the parameter
\vartheta
governs the depth of partition,
and
N,N_{\pm}
are given in Lines
4
,
10
and
11
in Algorithm
4
. A smaller
\vartheta
leads to a finer partitioning. The above criterion (
27
) treats the P-particles and N-particles on the same footing and has been successfully applied into an efficient stochastic particle simulation of the Wigner-Coulomb quantum dynamics
[
24
]
.

In contrast to the low acceptance rate of the acceptance-rejection method employed by HDP (see Figure
4
), sampling from the piecewise constant functions
\overline{\Delta m_{k}}(\bm{v})

Using this improvement, the main time-consuming steps of HDP-AS become simulating collisions and advection, while sampling new deviational particles is no longer time-consuming now (see Figure

Remark 1
.

The iconic ziggurat algorithm
[
15
,
16
]
efficiently generates samples
by partitioning the desired distribution functions into equal-area segments. Knuth praises it as “a very pretty example of mathematical theory intimately interwoven with programming ingenuity—a fine illustration of the art of computer programming!” in his famous book
[
25
]
. This work inherits the idea of the ziggurat algorithm to solve the sampling problem in the HDP method. The main challenges in current applications lie in the desired distribution
\Delta m_{k}(\bm{v})
is a three-dimensional function and we need efficiently construct a piecewise constant approximation from its point cloud form (
15
). Our HDP-AS method addresses these specific challenges through an adaptive domain partitioning based on the mixture discrepancy.

5
Conclusion and discussion

The main contributions of this work include two parts:

1.

Proposing an alternative adaptive strategy for sampling from
\Delta m_{k}(\bm{v})_{\pm}
in the HDP method. It constructs an adaptive piecewise constant approximation of
\Delta m_{k}(\bm{v})_{\pm}
at first, and then the sampling is conducted directly from the piecewise constant function without rejection.

2.

Employing the mixture discrepancy in place of the star discrepancy as the uniformity measure accelerates the
reconstruction of the adaptive piecewise constant approximation in
[
17
]
. The mixture discrepancy has an explicit expression and no external solver is required.

Owing to the above two improvements, the proposed HDP-AS method successfully reduces the computational cost of the HDP method. The same idea could be applied to other sampling tasks, provided that each part of the function to be sampled has already been represented by multiple particle sets (e.g., in this work, the
Q(f_{p},m)
and
Q(f_{n},m)
in
\Delta m_{k}(\bm{v})_{\pm}
are represented by particle sets
\mathbb{M}^{+}_{k}
and
\mathbb{M}^{-}_{k}
in Eq. (
15
)).
There remain some aspects to be explored, such as
the error analysis of HDP-AS, the application to practical problems in higher dimensions,
and whether more efficient uniformity measure manner can further accelerate the reconstruction of the adaptive piecewise constant approximation.

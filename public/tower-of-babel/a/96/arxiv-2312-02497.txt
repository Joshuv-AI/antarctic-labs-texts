On a variational model for the continuous mechanics exhibiting hexagonal to square Phase Transitions
Senping Luo, Juncheng Wei

Abstract
. Inspired by Conti and Zanzotto [ 7 ] , we reformulate a simple variational model for reconstructive phase transitions in crystals arising in continuum mechanics in the framework of Landau’s theory of phase transition(with slight modification). We provide and prove that this class of modular invariant functions admit exactly hexagonal-square lattices minimizers without passing through rhombic lattices, being the first rigorous result in this regard. Our result gives an affirmative answer to an open problem by in [ 7 ] . In addition, our result has independent interest from number theory.

We start with some basic analysis in continuum mechanics. For more details, see

2

\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{y}(\mathbf{x})

Among all deformations that map a Bravais lattice into itself, the strain energy density

\displaystyle\phi(\mathbf{C})=\phi(\mathbf{m}^{\prime}\mathbf{C}\mathbf{m}),

(1.1)

where
\mathbf{m}

\hbox{GL}(n,\mathbb{Z})

\det(\mathbf{m})=\pm 1

We are interested in dimension

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbf{C}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\tilde{\mathbf{C}}_{11}&\tilde{\mathbf{C}}_{12}\\

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}=\{z=x+iy\;\hbox{or}\;(x,y)\in\mathbb{C}:y>0\},\;\;ds^{2}=\frac{dx^{2}+dy^{2}}{y^{2}}

\displaystyle\phi(\gamma(z))=\phi(z),\;\;\gamma\in\hbox{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z}),

\displaystyle z\mapsto-\frac{1}{z};\;\;z\mapsto z+1;.

The fundamental domain associated to the group

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{0}:=\{z\in\mathbb{H}:|z|\geq 1,\;|\operatorname{Re}(z)|\leq\frac{1}{2}\}.

A mathematical theory to explain hexagonal to square lattice phase transitions is to find a strain energy function with a parameter such that

This problem is initiated by Landau in 1936 and justified by many mathematicians and physicists.

1.1

\displaystyle\hbox{for}\;\;\alpha\geq 1\;\;\min_{z\in\mathbb{H}}M(\alpha,z)\;\;\hbox{is achieved at}\;\;i.

The cases of

in

\displaystyle\min_{z\in\mathbb{H}}M(\alpha,z)\;\;\hbox{for}\;\;\alpha\in(0,1]\;\;\Leftrightarrow\;\;\min_{z\in\mathbb{H}}\Big(\theta(\alpha,z)-\pi\alpha M(\alpha,z)\Big)\;\;\hbox{for}\;\;\alpha\in[1,\infty).

This is achieved by an observed duality relation

\displaystyle M(\frac{1}{\alpha},z)=\frac{\alpha^{2}}{\pi}\Big(\theta(\alpha,z)-\pi\alpha M(\alpha,z)\Big).

in Lemma
3.1
.
Thanks to this duality relation, by Propositions
3.1
and
4.1
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\min_{z\in\mathbb{H}}M(\alpha,z)=\min_{z\in\Gamma}M(\alpha,z)\;\;\hbox{for}\;\;\alpha\in(0,1],

(7.70)

here
\Gamma=\Gamma_{a}\cup\Gamma_{b}
. (

) reduces the location of the minimizers onto a curve
\Gamma
.

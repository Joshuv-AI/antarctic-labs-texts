Axion production via trapped misalignment from Peccei-Quinn symmetry breaking
Luca Di Luzio a, Philip Sørensen a,b

Abstract
The Peccei-Quinn (PQ) symmetry does not need to be exact, and even a tiny source of PQ breaking not aligned with the QCD anomaly might have significant phenomenological implications. In this study, we examine the effects of a general class of PQ-breaking operators on the axion cosmological production via misalignment, focussing on both temperature-dependent and independent PQ-breaking potentials. In particular, we show that a variant of the trapped misalignment mechanism can delay the onset of axion oscillation, leading to an axion dark matter window with m a ≫ 10 − 5 m_{a}\gg 10^{-5} eV. This scenario is testable through various experimental approaches, including standard axion haloscopes and helioscopes, as well as searches for electric dipole moments and axion-mediated forces.



1
Introduction

The Peccei-Quinn (PQ) solution to the strong CP problem

[
1
,
2
]

requires a global
{\rm U}(1)_{\rm PQ}
symmetry, that is
explicitly broken by the
Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD) anomaly.
The axion field, which arises as the Goldstone mode
of the spontaneously broken
{\rm U}(1)_{\rm PQ}

[
3
,
4
]
,
also
provides
an excellent dark matter (DM) candidate,
based e.g. on the so-called axion misalignment
production mechanism
[
5
,
6
,
7
]
.
The

{\rm U}(1)_{\rm PQ}
,
being a global symmetry,
does not need to be exact.
In fact, it is explicitly broken by the QCD anomaly,
and it is expected to be broken further
by ultraviolet (UV) physics,
at the very least by quantum gravity effects

[
8
,
9
,
10
]
.
While the explicit breaking from QCD is guaranteed to generate a minimum which conserves CP
[
11
]
,
other sources of PQ breaking would in general not be aligned to this minimum.
This implies that the axion
vacuum expectation value (VEV)
could be displaced from the minimum at zero.
However,
a large axion VEV would reintroduce the CP-violating effects that the axion was introduced to resolve and it is strongly constrained by the
non-observation of the
electric dipole moment (EDM) of the neutron (nEDM)
[
12
]
. Therefore,
PQ-breaking
potentials must
be strongly subdominant
today with respect
to the axion potential from QCD.

However, a source of PQ breaking might still leave an
imprint in axion cosmology. As it was studied in Ref.
[
13
]
,
PQ-breaking effects,
assumed to be temperature-independent,
become relevant at high temperatures,

T\gg T_{c}\approx 150
MeV, for which the standard QCD axion potential is suppressed.
Hence, new PQ-breaking sources
beyond QCD
may affect the axion field evolution
in the early universe and modify
(by either delaying or expediting)
the time
when the axion starts to oscillate,
thus
affecting
(by either enhancing or suppressing)
the axion relic
abundance from
misalignment.
In Ref.
[
13
]
it was found that the QCD axion could account for all the observed DM with masses well above the range expected from the standard misalignment mechanism, i.e.
m_{a}\gg 10^{-5}
eV. This was achieved with PQ-breaking effects which delay damping by realizing the so-called trapped misalignment
[
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
,
18
]
.
However, in the framework presented there, a tuning of at least
10^{-3}
in the alignment of the PQ-breaking potential was necessary to remain compatible with nEDM constraints. Fundamentally, this need arises because the dimensionless axion VEV today can be at most
10^{-10}
and the QCD axion potential at the epoch relevant for
misalignment is suppressed only by a factor of about
10^{-7}
, leaving the residual factor of
10^{-3}
unaccounted for.

In this work, we
extend the analysis of Ref.
[
13
]

by taking into account a new class of PQ-breaking operators,
recently considered in Refs.
[
19
,
20
]
,
in which the
axion
couples directly
to Standard Model (SM) fields.
Since the latter belong to the
early universe thermal bath,
a remarkable consequence of those
PQ-breaking operators
is the generation of a temperature-dependent
axion potential which
grows
with
T
,
thus becoming
even more relevant at high temperatures,
when compared to the suppressed QCD axion potential.
The additional temperature dependence of the
PQ-breaking potential can then overcome the residual hierarchy faced in Ref.
[
13
]
and realize axion DM for
m_{a}\gg 10^{-5}
eV without tuning.

Our paper is structured as follows: In section
2
, we motivate the relevance of PQ-breaking effects for misalignment and highlight the relevant hierarchies and temperature scales. In section
3
, we briefly review the results of
[
13
]
and study trapped misalignment from a temperature-independent
PQ-breaking potential. In section
4
, we go beyond
[
13
]
by
analyzing the effects of the PQ-breaking potentials
introduced
in
[
19
,
20
]
and
showing how trapped misalignment can be realized without tuning. In this section, we also discuss relevant constraints and
how
such a
relatively heavy axion DM
scenario, with
m_{a}\lesssim 0.1
eV,
might be testable in future
experiments,
including standard axion haloscopes and helioscopes,
as wells as
searches for
EDMs and axion-mediated forces.

2
Relevance of PQ breaking for misalignment

In the following, we will refer
to the
standard QCD axion and discuss how
general sources of
PQ breaking
might allow for interesting effects at the temperatures relevant for misalignment.
First, consider the temperature dependence of the QCD axion potential, which can be written as

\displaystyle V_{\rm QCD}(T)=-\chi_{\rm QCD}(T)\cos(\theta)\,,

(1)

where
\chi_{\rm QCD}(T)=f_{a}^{2}m_{a}^{2}(T)
is the topological susceptibility at finite temperature.
The temperature dependence of the axion mass can be extracted from lattice QCD simulations (see e.g.
[
21
]
), and for our purposes it can be approximated
as

\displaystyle m_{a}^{2}(T)=m_{a}^{2}\,\min\left[\left(\frac{T}{T_{c}}\right)^{-2b},\ 1\right]~,

(2)

where
T_{c}=150
MeV and
b=3.92
. In our notation,
m_{a}
and
\chi_{\rm QCD}
refer to their zero-temperature values when written without explicit
T
-dependence.
In the early universe,
m_{a}(T)
competes against an effective friction term arising from the cosmological expansion, and the equation of motion (EOM)
of the axion field
in the harmonic approximation
reads

\displaystyle\ddot{a}+3H\dot{a}+m_{a}^{2}(T)a=0\,,

(3)

where
H
is the Hubble parameter. At early times, when the friction term (referred to as Hubble friction) dominates, the system is over-damped and the axion is effectively frozen at some initial value
a_{\rm ini}=\theta_{\rm ini}f_{a}
, where
\theta_{\rm ini}\in[-\pi,\pi)
is the initial misalignment angle.
If the spontaneous breaking of the PQ symmetry takes place after inflation, then each patch of the universe may select a different value and
we expect
a_{\rm ini}
to take an averaged value.
In such post-inflationary scenarios, topological defects
such as axion strings and domain walls
also play a major role. However, if PQ breaking takes place before the end of inflation, then inflation generically selects a single randomly chosen value
for the initial condition, such that we naturally expect
\theta_{\rm ini}\sim\mathcal{O}(1)
. In this work, we focus on such pre-inflationary scenarios in which topological defects are absent.

Eventually, at some temperature
T_{\rm osc}
the axion starts to oscillate once
V_{\rm QCD}
overcomes Hubble friction, i.e. when

\displaystyle m_{a}(T_{\rm osc})\approx 3H(T_{\rm osc})\,.

(4)

After the mass term dominates, the axion EOM becomes a damped harmonic oscillator which behaves as DM. In this limit, the EOM eq. (
3
) can be solved by a WKB approximation which yields
a\propto m_{a}^{-1/2}(T)\ R^{-3/2}
, where
R
is the scale factor. Assuming that this WKB evolution takes effect immediately after
T_{\rm osc}
, oscillations starting with an amplitude of
a_{\rm ini}
lead to a present-day DM relic of the form

\displaystyle\rho_{a,\,\rm today}\approx\frac{1}{2}\chi_{\rm QCD}\theta_{\rm ini}^{2}\frac{m_{a}(T_{\rm osc})}{m_{a}}\frac{g_{*s}(T_{0})}{g_{*s}(T_{\rm osc})}\left(\frac{T_{0}}{T_{\rm osc}}\right)^{3}~,

(5)

where
g_{*s}(T)
is the number of relativistic degrees of freedom in entropy and
T_{0}
is the present-day temperature. Anharmonic effects may contribute an
\mathcal{O}(1)
factor which we neglect here for simplicity.
Because
\chi_{\rm QCD}=f_{a}^{2}m_{a}^{2}\approx\left(76\text{ MeV}\right)^{4}
is fixed for any QCD axion, the present-day observed DM density is found for a specific amount of redshift corresponding to a unique oscillation temperature. For the axion to make up a fraction
r_{\rm DM}
of the observed DM abundance, the axion must start to oscillate at

\displaystyle T_{\rm osc}\approx 950\text{ MeV }r_{\rm DM}^{\frac{-1}{3+b}}\theta_{\rm ini}^{\frac{2}{3+b}}.

(6)

In the conventional scenario, in which the condition eq. (
4
) controls
T_{\rm osc}
,
\theta_{\rm ini}
must be tuned to compensate for the failure to hit
T_{\rm osc}\sim 950
MeV if the axion mass deviates significantly from
m_{a}\sim 10^{-5}
eV. However, if we introduce additional dynamics which can modify the starting temperature of axion oscillations, then such an additional degree of freedom can be used to fix the condition eq. (
6
) even for axion masses far from
m_{a}\sim 10^{-5}
eV. This is the underlying principle of approaches such as
trapped misalignment

[
14
,
15
,
17
,
18
]
and
kinetic misalignment

[
22
,
23
]
, which have been recently discussed in the literature.

The central question of this work is how PQ-breaking potentials
can implement trapped misalignment and thus be used to motivate axion DM across a non-standard parameter space. Naively, one might expect this not to be possible given that any source of PQ breaking must be small enough not to generate an axion VEV,

\theta_{\rm eff}\equiv\langle a\rangle/f_{a}
,
that overshoots the nEDM limit
of
|\theta_{\rm eff}|\lesssim 10^{-10}
.
This means that any PQ-breaking potential must be far subdominant to
V_{\rm QCD}
today. However, because of the temperature dependence of the QCD axion potential, eq. (
2
), this need not always have been the case.

In particular, the hierarchy between
T_{c}\sim 150
MeV and
T_{\rm osc}\sim 950
MeV generates a suppression of the QCD potential of

\displaystyle\frac{V_{\rm QCD}(T_{\rm osc})}{V_{\rm QCD}(T=0)}\approx\left(\frac{T_{c}}{T_{\rm osc}}\right)^{2b}\sim 5\times 10^{-7},

(7)

which makes it much easier for PQ-breaking potentials to compete near
T_{\rm osc}
. Nevertheless, as we will show explicitly in section
3.3
,
this implies that a temperature-independent PQ-breaking potential
in the absence of tuning
would not be able to compete at
T_{\rm osc}\sim 950
MeV, as this would require a suppression of
10^{-10}
in order to overcome nEDM limits.

Which temperature then is high enough for PQ breaking to dominate?
If we give up on the assumption that the axion makes up all of the observed DM, then we can consider misalignment at higher temperatures where the QCD potential is further suppressed.
Because the misalignment temperature
T_{\rm osc}
is controlled by
m_{a}
through the condition
m_{a}(T_{\rm osc})\approx 3H(T_{\rm osc})
, pushing
T_{\rm osc}
to higher temperatures requires us to consider larger axion masses.
In order for the QCD potential to be suppressed by a factor of
10^{-10}
, such that constant-temperature PQ-breaking can dominate, requires axion masses of

\displaystyle m_{a}\gtrsim 5\times 10^{-3}\text{ eV}~.

(8)

Of course, if misalignment takes place at such temperatures, then DM would be under-produced according to eq. (
6
). Nevertheless, the fact that PQ-breaking effects can play a role means that interesting phenomenology might arise. We review this constant-temperature scenario in the next section. Furthermore, it is clear that the competitiveness of the PQ-breaking potential can be drastically improved if this grows with
T
,
a case that will be discussed
in section
4
.

3
Axion DM production from
T
-independent PQ-breaking potentials

We now discuss the scenario of a temperature-independent PQ-breaking potential in more detail.
To keep the discussion general, we will parameterize the potential as

\displaystyle V_{\rm\cancel{\rm PQ}}=-\Lambda_{\rm\cancel{\rm PQ}}^{4}\cos(n\theta+\deltaPQV)~,

(9)

where
\theta=a/f_{a}
is the adimensional axion field,

\Lambda_{\rm\cancel{\rm PQ}}
describes the amplitude of the
PQ-breaking potential,
n
is an integer and

\delta_{\rm\cancel{\rm PQ}}
is a generic phase that parametrizes the misalignment of the minimum from that of the QCD axion potential.

A potential of the form in eq. (
9
) can be generated from
UV sources of PQ-breaking,
possibly related to quantum gravity effects
(see e.g.
[
24
]
).
Such effects can be encoded
through
an effective operator of the type

\displaystyle V\supset-e^{i\delta_{\rm\cancel{\rm PQ}}}\frac{\phi^{n}}{\Lambda_{\rm UV}^{n-4}}+\text{h.c.}\,,

(10)

with
n>4
.
Here,

\Lambda_{\rm UV}
is a UV scale
and
\phi=\frac{f_{a}}{\sqrt{2}}e^{i\theta}
is a complex scalar,
with the radial mode integrated out and
the angular mode
given by the
adimensional axion field.
When written in terms of
\theta
, an operator of the form in eq. (
9
) arises with an amplitude of

\Lambda_{\rm\cancel{\rm PQ}}^{4}=\frac{1}{2^{n/2-1}}\frac{f_{a}^{n}}{\Lambda_{\rm UV}^{n-4}}\,.

(11)

While
this provides a canonical example,
in this work we remain agnostic about the source of PQ breaking and work with the generic potential in eq. (
9
).
The impact of such a
T
-independent PQ-breaking potential was investigated in Ref.
[
13
]
.
If the PQ-breaking potential dominates
with respect to
the QCD axion potential prior to misalignment, which in the absence of tuning in the CP-violating phase

\delta_{\rm\cancel{\rm PQ}}
is subject to the condition eq. (
8
), then the PQ-breaking potential modifies the resulting relic density.
As pointed out by the authors of Ref.
[
13
]
, the impact of such a PQ-breaking potential can be classified into two distinct regimes:

\displaystyle\text{Smooth shift regime:}\qquad\absolutevalue{n\thetaini+ \deltaPQV}

\displaystyle<\pi~,

(12)

\displaystyle\text{Trapping regime:}\qquad\absolutevalue{n\thetaini+ \deltaPQV}

\displaystyle>\pi~.

(13)

These conditions are visualized in figure
1
.
In the first regime, smooth shift, the main impact of the PQ-breaking potential is to trigger oscillations earlier than what would have been achieved by
V_{\rm QCD}
alone. This results in increased redshift and therefore a lower present-day DM abundance.
Such a scenario takes place when
\theta_{\rm ini}
selects the minimum of
V_{\rm\cancel{\rm PQ}}
closest to the minimum of
V_{\rm QCD}
, which permits an adiabatic transition between the two minima. However, if
\theta_{\rm ini}
selects a minimum of
V_{\rm\cancel{\rm PQ}}
further from the minimum of
V_{\rm QCD}
, then a potential barrier separates the two minima. This potential barrier prevents an adiabatic transition and results in trapped misalignment. In this scenario, the field first settles into and tracks the initial, false minima of
V_{\rm\cancel{\rm PQ}}
. The oscillations in this initial misalignment process are damped out by Hubble friction, and we can consider the field to be at rest at the time-dependent minimum. Then, at some transition temperature,
T_{\rm release}
, the potential barrier eventually disappears and the field suddenly starts oscillating around the late-time minimum of
V_{\rm QCD}
,
\theta=0
. This essentially restarts oscillations at a temperature below the temperature at which oscillations would have taken place given only
V_{\rm QCD}
, resulting in less redshift and a larger present-day DM abundance.

Figure 1:
Illustration of the two principal regimes defined by eqs. (
12
) and (
13
). If
\theta_{\rm ini}
lies in the minimum of
V_{\rm\cancel{\rm PQ}}
closest to the origin (defined by the minimum of
V_{\rm QCD}
), then
\theta
will adiabatically shift towards zero. This defines the smooth shift regime. On the contrary, if
\theta_{\rm ini}
lies in any other minimum, then
\theta
is separated from the origin by a potential barrier resulting in a trapped scenario.

As discussed in the previous section, general arguments lead us to conclude that temperature-independent PQ-breaking effects can only become relevant
in the regime in which the misalignment mechanism
underproduces axion DM, see eq. (
8
). Therefore, we expect more interesting phenomenology from the trapped regime, which we focus on in the remainder of this work.

3.1
Trapping and release

If the axion starts oscillating in a local minimum that is not the closest one to the CP-conserving origin, then there will be a potential barrier which prevents the transition from taking place in an adiabatic way. Instead, the axion will be trapped in the false minimum until the potential barrier disappears.
The disappearance of the potential barrier requires that

\displaystyle\partialderivative{V}{\theta}=0\quad\quad\mbox{and}\quad\quad\partialderivative[2]{V}{\theta}=0\,,

(14)

where
V=V_{\rm QCD}+V_{\rm\cancel{\rm PQ}}
. With the PQ-breaking potential given by
eq. (
9
), these two conditions can be rewritten into the form

\displaystyle\tan{\thetaTrap}=\frac{1}{n}\tan(n\thetaTrap+\deltaPQV)\,,

(15)

\displaystyle\frac{T_{\rm release}}{T_{c}}=\left(\frac{-\chi_{\rm QCD}\sin\theta_{\rm release}}{n\Lambda_{\rm\cancel{\rm PQ}}^{4}\sin(n \thetaTrap+\deltaPQV)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2b}}\,.

(16)

Taken in isolation, eq. (
15
) has
2(n-1)
solutions.
n-1
of these solutions also solve (
16
), corresponding to transitions from the
n-1
minima of
V_{\rm\cancel{\rm PQ}}
with trapping solutions.
As it was pointed out in
[
13
]
,

\theta_{\rm release}
does not depend on the amplitude of the PQ-breaking potential.

Eq. (
16
), given a suitable solution of eq. (
15
), governs the temperature at which the transition takes place. These equations do not have exact analytical solutions, but by discarding the angular dependence in eq. (
16
) we can approximate
T_{\rm release}
as

\displaystyle T_{\rm release}\approx T_{c}\left(\frac{\chi_{\rm QCD}}{n\Lambda_{\rm\cancel{\rm PQ}}^{4}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2b}}=1.3\text{ GeV }\left(\frac{n^{1/4}\Lambda_{\rm\cancel{\rm PQ}}}{10^{-3}\text{ GeV}}\right)^{-0.51}.

(17)

In the absence of tuning, the error arising from discarding the angular dependence corresponds to an
\mathcal{O}(1)
factor in
T_{\rm release}
. Interestingly, this simplified solution can be also
obtained by comparing
m_{a}(T)
with an effective mass defined as

\displaystyle m_{a,\rm\,\cancel{\rm PQ}}^{2}\equiv\frac{1}{a}\partialderivative{\VPQV}{a}\,,

(18)

with
m_{a}(T)
and
m_{a,\rm\,\cancel{\rm PQ}}
being evaluated in the minima of their respective potentials. These effective masses are defined such that the EOM,
\ddot{a}+3H\dot{a}+V^{\prime}=0
, matches the standard form
\ddot{a}+3H\dot{a}+m_{a,\rm\,\cancel{\rm PQ}}^{2}\,a=0
.
The simplified solution eq. (
17
) is then approximated by comparing the masses as
m_{a}(T_{\rm release})\approx m_{a,\rm\,\cancel{\rm PQ}}
and then solving for
T_{\rm release}
, which
allows us to better understand the relative importance of the two potentials.

3.2
Axion DM relic abundance

After the axion is released from the false minimum at
T_{\rm release}
, the field oscillates in
V_{\rm QCD}
as it would in standard misalignment. As before, we can then assume an instantaneous entry to the WKB regime to obtain the present-day axion relic abundance from eq. (
5
), with
T_{\rm release}
replacing
T_{\rm osc}
. To simplify the expression, we approximate the
g_{*s}(T_{\rm release})\approx g_{*s}(1\,\text{GeV})\approx 69
, which yields

\displaystyle\rho_{a,\,\rm today}\approx 2.9\times 10^{-2}\chi_{\rm QCD}\left(\frac{T_{c}}{T_{\rm release}}\right)^{b}\left(\frac{T_{0}}{T_{\rm release}}\right)^{3}\theta_{\rm release}^{2}\,.

(19)

Applying the approximate solution for
T_{\rm release}
, the present-day relic takes the form

\displaystyle\rho_{a,\,\rm today}\approx 2.9\times 10^{-2}(\chi_{\rm QCD})^{1-\frac{3}{b}}\left(\frac{T_{0}}{T_{c}}\right)^{3}n^{\frac{3+b}{2b}}\theta_{\rm release}^{2}\Lambda_{\rm\cancel{\rm PQ}}^{2+\frac{6}{b}}\,.

(20)

As it is clear from the above discussion, the effect of the PQ-breaking potential is to delay oscillations. This reduces the amount of redshift the DM relic experiences between
T_{\rm release}
and today and thus increases the predicted DM relic density. The effect grows with increasing
\Lambda_{\rm\cancel{\rm PQ}}
.

3.3
nEDM limits and tuned PQ-breaking scenario

The size of
\Lambda_{\rm\cancel{\rm PQ}}
is ultimately limited by nEDM constraints.
Unless the misalignment angle
\delta_{\rm\cancel{\rm PQ}}
is tuned to be small, this requires
\Lambda_{\rm\cancel{\rm PQ}}^{4}\ll\chi_{\rm QCD}
. Specifically,
V_{\rm\cancel{\rm PQ}}
generates a VEV of size

\displaystyle\theta_{\rm eff}\approx-\frac{n\Lambda_{\rm\cancel{\rm PQ}}^{4}}{\chi_{\rm QCD}}\sin\delta_{\rm\cancel{\rm PQ}}\,,

(21)

where we expanded in the limit
\theta_{\rm eff}\ll 1
and
\Lambda_{\rm\cancel{\rm PQ}}^{4}\ll\chi_{\rm QCD}
. nEDM constraints restrict this VEV to
|\theta_{\rm eff}|\lesssim 10^{-10}
, which implies an upper bound of

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\rm\cancel{\rm PQ}}\lesssim 2.4\times 10^{-4}\text{ GeV }\left(n\sin\delta_{\rm\cancel{\rm PQ}}\right)^{-1/4}\,.

(22)

Plugging this into eq. (
20
) reveals that this limit restricts the axion DM relic to at most

\displaystyle\frac{\rho_{a,\,\rm today}}{\rho_{\rm DM,\,today}}\lesssim 5\times 10^{-4}\frac{\theta_{\rm ini}^{2}}{\sin^{0.88}(\delta_{\rm\cancel{\rm PQ}})}\,,

(23)

which rules out the possibility of trapped misalignment with a temperature-independent potential to generate the observed DM abundance, unless
the PQ-breaking phase is tuned down to
\delta_{\rm\cancel{\rm PQ}}\lesssim 10^{-3}
.

3.4
Numerical analysis

We have implemented a numerical solution to confirm the
analytic approximation in eq. (
23
).
The numerical analysis,
exemplified in figure
2
,
shows that once
\Lambda_{\rm\cancel{\rm PQ}}
is assumed to saturate the nEDM limit then the axion relic density begins to significantly deviate from the standard result for
m_{a}\gg 10^{-3}
eV, which confirms the expectation from eq. (
8
). Furthermore, for
m_{a}\gg 10^{-3}
eV the relic abundance enters a regime in which the relic abundance becomes independent of
m_{a}
. The relic abundance in this regime agrees with our analytic estimate for
\rho_{a,\,\rm today}
up to an
\mathcal{O}(1)
factor, which can be attributed to anharmonic effects and the failure of oscillations to instantaneously enter the WKB regime after the release at
T_{\rm release}
.

\Lambda_{\rm\cancel{\rm PQ}}

)) and an

) to the asymptotic behaviour of the numerical result. The model parameters used in this example are
n=3
,

and

.

4
Axion DM production from
T
-dependent
PQ-breaking
potentials

In the previous section, we have seen how the rapid decay of
V_{\rm QCD}
for
T\gtrsim T_{c}
allows for a
T
-independent PQ-breaking potential to
impact the misalignment mechanism. However, although a trapping mechanism allows
V_{\rm\cancel{\rm PQ}}
to dominate and postpone the onset of oscillations, the effect is insufficient to account for axion DM in the trapping regime.
The main cause for this limitation is that the suppression of
V_{\rm QCD}
is insufficient,
barring a tuning of
\delta_{\rm\cancel{\rm PQ}}
,
to overturn the nEDM-enforced hierarchy between
V_{\rm QCD}
and
V_{\rm\cancel{\rm PQ}}
if the latter potential does not depend on
T
. To escape this limitation, we will now investigate a class of PQ-breaking operators
recently considered in
[
19
,
20
]
:

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\phi}{\Lambda_{\rm UV}}\right)^{n}\mathcal{O}_{\rm SM}\,,

(24)

where
\mathcal{O}_{\rm SM}
is a renormalizable SM
operator. The advantage of such a term is that it generates a temperature-dependent thermal potential, so that

V_{\rm\cancel{\rm PQ}}
grows with temperature.
In
[
19
,
20
]
the operator (
24
) was considered with only
n=1
. We have here generalized eq. (
24
) to
n\geq 1
, since
n=1
only offers the smooth shift solution which would result in a strongly suppressed DM relic.

In this section, we investigate the possibilities
offered by the above scenario. First, we proceed to generalize the results of section
3
to a general, temperature-dependent potential. Then, we present specific implementations of
eq. (
24
). Finally, we discuss the axion VEV,
the CP-violating axion interactions with SM matter fields
and the associated constraints,
before we summarize our results.

4.1
Trapped misalignment
in the presence of
T
-dependent PQ-breaking potentials

Before coming to specific examples of axion potentials
growing with
T
,
let us discuss some general features of
trapped misalignment for
such thermal potentials.
We will parameterize these as

\displaystyle V_{\rm th}=-\Lambda_{\rm\cancel{\rm PQ}}^{4}(T)\cos(n\theta+ \deltaPQV)\,,\quad\quad\mbox{where}\quad\quad\Lambda_{\rm\cancel{\rm PQ}}^{4}(T)=\lambda_{\rm\cancel{\rm PQ}}^{4}\left(\frac{f_{a}}{\sqrt{2}\Lambda_{\rm UV}}\right)^{n}T^{q}\,,

(25)

and
q
is a positive power.
At high temperatures, such a potential can dominate with respect to the QCD potential. With the replacement
\Lambda_{\rm\cancel{\rm PQ}}\to\Lambda_{\rm\cancel{\rm PQ}}(T)
many of the results from the previous section can be reapplied. In particular, for
n>1
it is possible to have trapped solutions with the release conditions
set by eqs. (
15
)-(
16
).
It is also possible to generalize the approximate solution in eq. (
17
), which becomes

\displaystyle T_{\rm release}\approx T_{c}\left(\frac{\chi_{\rm QCD}}{n\Lambda_{\rm\cancel{\rm PQ}}^{4}(T_{\rm release})}\right)^{\frac{1}{2b}}.

(26)

By solving (
26
) for
T_{\rm release}
, we can then find the release temperature for a thermal potential with arbitrary
T
dependence. If we factor out the
T
dependence as in

\displaystyle T_{\rm release}\approx\left(2^{\frac{n}{4b}}n^{-\frac{1}{2b}}\lambda_{\rm\cancel{\rm PQ}}^{-2/b}m_{a}^{1/b}f_{a}^{-\frac{n-2}{2b}}T_{c}\Lambda_{\rm UV}^{\frac{n}{2b}}\right)^{\frac{2b}{2b+q}}.

(27)

The present-day axion relic is then found by plugging eq. (
27
) into eq. (
19
). To produce
an axion abundance
\rho_{a,\,\rm today}=r_{\rm DM}\,\rho_{\rm DM,\,today}
requires a specific value of
\Lambda_{\rm UV}
, which is

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\rm UV}\approx\frac{0.60^{\frac{2b+q}{n}}}{\sqrt{2}}f_{a}n^{1/n}\lambda_{\rm\cancel{\rm PQ}}^{4/n}T_{c}^{\frac{b(q-6)}{(b+3)n}}T_{0}^{\frac{3(2b+q)}{(b+3)n}}\chi_{\rm QCD}^{\frac{b+q-3}{(b+3)n}}(r_{\rm DM}\,\rho_{\rm DM,\,today})^{-\frac{2b+q}{bn+3n}}\absolutevalue{\thetaini}^{\frac{2(2b+q)}{(b+3)n}}.

(28)

Hence, given an operator which gives rise to a potential of the form of eq. (
25
), we can find a solution for the value of the scale
\Lambda_{\rm UV}
which reproduces DM simply by reading the parameter
\lambda_{\rm\cancel{\rm PQ}}
from the definition in eq. (
25
).

Interestingly, regardless of the origin of PQ breaking,
once the DM abundance is fixed according to eq. (
28
), the amplitude of the
PQ-breaking potential is fixed
by the condition
V_{\rm\cancel{\rm PQ}}(T_{\rm release})=V_{\rm QCD}(T_{\rm release})
.
Because the overall amplitude at
T_{\rm release}
is fixed by the DM requirement, the evolution of the PQ-breaking potential depends just on the scaling power
q
(and implicitly on
m_{a}
through
T_{\rm release}
).
To better understand this evolution, it is convenient to consider the effective mass:

\displaystyle m_{a,\rm th}^{2}=\frac{1}{a}\partialderivative{\VThermalgeneral}{a}\Big|_{\rm min}\,.

(29)

This effective mass allows us to understand the cosmological evolution by comparing
it
with
m_{a}(T)
and
H(T)
. In figure
3
, we can see how the effective masses (dashed lines)
dominate over
m_{a}(T)
until
T\sim 950
MeV. Furthermore, both effective masses dominate over
H(T)
before trapping.
While the former is true by construction, the second feature is a condition which is necessary for the self-consistency of the scenario.
At the very least, it is necessary that the effective masses dominate Hubble immediately prior to the transition at
T_{\rm release}
.
Saturating the inequality
m_{a,\rm th}(T_{\rm osc})>H(T_{\rm osc})
, we find that it is satisfied in the same regime in which the standard misalignment mechanism underproduces DM. This is because
m_{a,\rm th}(T_{\rm release})\sim H(T_{\rm release})
implies
m_{a}(T_{\rm release})\sim H(T_{\rm release})
.
This means that Hubble is subdominant to
m_{a}(T_{\rm release})
and
m_{a,\rm th}(T_{\rm release})
in the mass range

\displaystyle m_{a}\gg 10^{-5}\text{ eV}\,.

(30)

In this regime, the thermal potential can dominate at least for a short time before
T_{\rm release}
, which is required by the consistency of the trapping scenario. If
q=4
then
m_{a,\rm th}\propto T^{2}
and the mass remains dominant with respect to
H(T)\propto T^{2}
for all
T\gtrsim T_{\rm release}
. However, if
q<4

can dominate at higher temperatures, and we must ask whether the field has sufficient time to settle down to the minimum of the PQ-breaking potential. The numerical results presented in Ref.
[

suggest that a short duration of oscillations prior to

is sufficient for

to relax to the false minimum, meaning that even for
q<4
eq. (

4.2
Thermal potentials from PQ-breaking operators coupled to SM fields

4.3
Induced axion VEV

Because the PQ-breaking operators in eqs. (
32
)-(
33
) violate CP in the presence of a non-zero phase
\delta_{\rm\cancel{\rm PQ}}
they also drive a non-zero axion VEV.
This can either be generated radiatively or via long-distance QCD effects. We here discuss which of these effects dominate the
axion VEV for each of the operators in eqs. (
32
) and (
33
).

Consider first the fermion Yukawa operator in eq. (
32
). As discussed in Refs.
[
19
,
20
]
,
this operator is responsible for a radiatively induced axion VEV.
Here, we follow the approach of Ref.
[
28
]

to compute the axion tadpole,
V_{\rm\cancel{\rm PQ}}=-\sigma a+\ldots
at one loop.
After setting
\langle H\rangle=(0\ \ v/\sqrt{2})^{T}
,
in the leading-log approximation we find

\sigma(\mu)=-\frac{n\,n_{\rm col}}{2\pi^{2}}\left(\frac{f_{a}}{\sqrt{2}\Lambda_{\rm UV}}\right)^{\!n}\frac{m_{f}^{4}}{f_{a}}\sin\delta_{\rm\cancel{\rm PQ}}\ln\left(\frac{v}{\mu}\right),

(38)

where
\mu
denotes the renormalization scale.
Including the
contribution of the QCD axion potential,
we obtain the induced axion VEV

\displaystyle\theta_{\rm eff}

\displaystyle\approx-\frac{n\,n_{\rm col}}{2\pi^{2}}\left(\frac{f_{a}}{\sqrt{2}\Lambda_{\rm UV}}\right)^{\!n}\!\frac{m_{f}^{4}}{\chi_{\rm QCD}}\sin\delta_{\rm\cancel{\rm PQ}}\,\ln\left(\frac{v}{1\,\text{GeV}}\right)\,,

(39)

where we took
\mu=1
GeV
to assess the
nEDM bound.
This VEV is generated by all the fermion Yukawa operators in eq. (
32
), but it is particularly relevant for leptons.

In the case of quarks, the operator in
eq. (
32
) also yields a long-distance
QCD contribution to the axion potential

\displaystyle V^{qq}_{\text{long-dis.}}\approx-\left(\frac{f_{a}}{\sqrt{2}\Lambda_{\rm UV}}\right)^{n}m_{q}\,\langle\overline{q}q\rangle\cos(n\theta+\deltaPQV)\,,

(40)

where
\langle\overline{q}q\rangle\approx(240\,\text{MeV})^{3}
,
for
q=u,d,s
,
is the quark condensate evaluated at the renormalization scale
of 1 GeV. Upon minimizing
V_{\rm QCD}+V^{qq}_{\text{long-dis.}}
, the induced axion VEV is

\displaystyle\theta_{\rm eff}\approx-n\left(\frac{f_{a}}{\sqrt{2}\Lambda_{\rm UV}}\right)^{n}\frac{m_{q}\langle\overline{q}q\rangle}{\chi_{\rm QCD}}\sin\delta_{\rm\cancel{\rm PQ}}\,.

(41)

Note that in the case of quarks the VEV in
eq. (
41
) dominates,
by several orders of magnitude,
with respect to the radiative one
in eq. (
39
).
Therefore, eq. (
41
) is relevant for quark operators
while eq. (
39
) yields the dominant contribution in the case of leptons.

Next, we consider the axion potential induced by the
axion coupling to gluons, due to the PQ-breaking operator in
eq. (
33
). In this case, the axion potential
is dominated by the long-distance QCD contribution, which reads

\displaystyle V^{GG}_{\text{long-dis.}}\approx-\left(\frac{f_{a}}{\sqrt{2}\Lambda_{\rm UV}}\right)^{n}2\pi\,\expectationvalue{\frac{\alphaStrong}{\pi} G G}\cos(n\theta+\deltaPQV)\,,

(42)

where the gluon condensate is

\langle\frac{\alpha_{\rm s}}{\pi}GG\rangle\approx\left(330\text{ MeV}\right)^{4}

[
29
]
.
Upon minimizing
V_{\rm QCD}+V^{GG}_{\text{long-dis.}}
,
one obtains the axion VEV
[
19
]

\theta_{\rm eff}\approx-2\pi n\frac{\expectationvalue{{\frac{\alphaStrong}{\pi} GG}}}{\chi_{\rm QCD}}\left(\frac{f_{a}}{\sqrt{2}\Lambda_{\rm UV}}\right)^{n}\sin\delta_{\rm\cancel{\rm PQ}}\,.

(43)

4.4
Direct and indirect scalar
axion
interactions

The PQ-breaking operators we are considering here lead to scalar interaction terms of the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\supset g_{a\overline{f}f}a\overline{f}f\,.

(44)

In our framework, there are two ways such an interaction can arise. The first is directly from
the fermion interaction in eq. (
32
), which
contains a term of the form of eq. (
44
) with

\displaystyle g_{a\overline{f}f}^{\text{direct}(\overline{f}f)}=n\frac{m_{f}}{f_{a}}\left(\frac{f_{a}}{\sqrt{2}\Lambda_{\rm UV}}\right)^{n}\sin\delta_{\rm\cancel{\rm PQ}}\,.

(45)

This coupling arises both from the lepton and the quark Yukawa operators. At low energies, through the matrix element
\bra{N}m_{q}\overline{q}q\ket{q}=f_{q}m_{N}\bar{\psi}_{N}\psi_{N}

[
20
,
30
]
the latter operator gives rise to a scalar axion-nucleon interaction of the form

\displaystyle g_{a\overline{N}N}^{\text{direct}(\overline{q}q)}=n\frac{f_{q}m_{N}}{f_{a}}\left(\frac{f_{a}}{\sqrt{2}\Lambda_{\rm UV}}\right)^{n}\sin\delta_{\rm\cancel{\rm PQ}}\,,

(46)

where we use the values
f_{q}
provided in
[
30
]
.
For the
GG
coupling, a direct contribution to the axion-nucleon coupling is also generated by the interaction with the gluonic component of the nucleons. To evaluate this, we use the matrix element
m_{N}\bar{\psi}_{N}\psi_{N}\approx-\frac{9}{8\pi}\bra{N}\alpha_{\rm s}GG\ket{N}

[
29
]
to identify the coupling

\displaystyle g_{a\overline{N}N}^{\text{direct}(GG)}=n\frac{16\pi}{9}\frac{m_{N}}{f_{a}}\left(\frac{f_{a}}{\sqrt{2}\Lambda_{\rm UV}}\right)^{n}\sin\delta_{\rm\cancel{\rm PQ}}\,.

(47)

Alternatively, a scalar axion-nucleon coupling can also arise in an indirect way through the axion VEV. In particular, if the axion has a VEV
\theta_{\rm eff}\neq 0
, then the latter generates a scalar axion-nucleon interaction
given by
the standard isospin-symmetric formula of Moody and Wilczek
[
31
]
,
with the correct extra 1/2 factor
[
32
]

\displaystyle g_{a\overline{N}N}^{\text{indirect}(\theta_{\rm eff})}=\frac{\theta_{\rm eff}}{f_{a}}\frac{m_{u}m_{d}}{m_{u}+m_{d}}\frac{\bra{N}\bar{u}u+\bar{d}d\ket{N}}{2}\approx\theta_{\rm eff}\frac{f_{s}}{f_{a}}\,,

(48)

where
f_{s}\approx 13\text{ MeV}
and we ignored isospin breaking effects which lead to corrections at the 10% level
[
32
]
. To evaluate this indirect contribution, we plug in the relevant
\theta_{\rm eff}
contribution determined
in section
4.3

for the interactions in eq. (
32
) and (
33
).

For quark-Yukawa and
GG
operators, both of which generate direct axion-nucleon couplings, the direct and indirect contributions are comparable, although the indirect contribution is slightly dominant.
Lepton-Yukawa operators do not generate a direct axion-nucleon interaction. Nevertheless, as we will see shortly, all of the above scalar interactions give rise to long-range forces.
Therefore, even though
g_{a\overline{e}e}^{\rm direct}
and
g_{a\overline{N}N}^{\rm indirect}
naively correspond to different observables, it will become useful to compare the amplitude of the two couplings:

\displaystyle\frac{g_{a\overline{e}e}^{\text{direct}(\overline{e}e)}}{g_{a\overline{N}N}^{\text{indirect}(\theta_{\rm eff})}}\approx\frac{2\pi^{2}}{\ln(v/\mu)}\frac{\chi_{\rm QCD}}{f_{s}m_{e}^{3}}\approx 7\times 10^{7}.

(49)

For leptons, we will see that this implies a stronger constraint from the direct axion-lepton coupling.

4.5
Constraints

Now that we have a set of candidate PQ-breaking operators, we know which parameters reproduce the DM
relic density, and we know the implied axion VEVs and couplings,
we will discuss
constraints on the parameter space of those PQ-breaking scenarios.
We will consider constraints stemming from the EDM of the neutron,
CP-violating axion interactions
in the form of
monopole-monopole and monopole-dipole forces
(for a recent review, see
[
33
]
),
as well as astrophysical bounds.

4.5.1
nEDM

As discussed above, the nEDM constrains the axion VEV to
|\theta_{\rm eff}|\lesssim 10^{-10}
.
2
2

2

Other
contributions to EDMs
stemming from direct axion exchange,
recently computed in Refs.
[
34
,

]
,
turn out to yield subleading constraints on the interactions
of eqs. (

since they are associated to

featuring an extra

Applying this condition to

For the quark Yukawa, once we assume the value of
\Lambda_{\rm UV}
required for trapped misalignment to account for DM,
eq. (
28

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{\thetaVEV}\approx 3.2\times 10^{-7}\left(\frac{r_{\rm DM}}{1.0}\right)^{\frac{2(\beta+1)}{\beta+3}}\left(\frac{m_{s}}{m_{q}}\right)\frac{\sin\delta_{\rm\cancel{\rm PQ}}}{\absolutevalue{\thetaTrap}^{\frac{4(\beta+1)}{\beta+3}}}\,,

(50)

where we normalized the quark mass
m_{q}
to the strange quark mass
m_{s}
because this yields the smallest
\theta_{\rm eff}
for the operators considered here. It is evident that a coupling to either of the quarks cannot be viable because it predicts an nEDM larger than the observational bound.

On the other hand, the leptons receive only the radiative contribution to
\theta_{\rm eff}
. Therefore, the value of
\theta_{\rm eff}
implied by the DM solution is much smaller
and can be compatible with the nEDM bound:

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{\thetaVEV}\approx 2.4\times 10^{-13}\left(\frac{r_{\rm DM}}{1.0}\right)^{\frac{2(\beta+1)}{\beta+3}}\left(\frac{m_{\ell}}{m_{e}}\right)^{2}\frac{\sin\delta_{\rm\cancel{\rm PQ}}}{\absolutevalue{\thetaTrap}^{\frac{4(\beta+1)}{\beta+3}}}\,,

(51)

where
m_{\ell}
(with
\ell=e,\mu
) is the lepton mass. Since
\theta_{\rm eff}
scales with
m_{\ell}^{2}
, only scenarios with an electron coupling are compatible with nEDM constraints. If we consider
for example
an axion coupling to the muon Yukawa then the enhancement
(m_{\mu}/m_{e})^{2}\approx 4\times 10^{4}
pushes well
\theta_{\rm eff}
above
10^{-10}
.

Interestingly, if the axion is coupled to
GG

\theta_{\rm eff}
are very close to the observational bound:

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{\thetaVEV}\approx 1.2\times 10^{-10}\left(\frac{r_{\rm DM}}{1.0}\right)^{\frac{2(\beta+2)}{\beta+3}}\frac{\sin\delta_{\rm\cancel{\rm PQ}}}{\absolutevalue{\thetaTrap}^{\frac{4(\beta+2)}{\beta+3}}}\,.

(52)

If we assume that the axion makes up all of the DM, i.e.
r_{\rm DM}=1
, then for the gluon scenario the compatibility with the nEDM is controlled by the last term in eq. (
52

\mathcal{O}(1)
in the absence of tuning, which means that while the gluon scenario is compatible with nEDM in an
\mathcal{O}(1)
fraction of the parameter space (in
\theta_{\rm ini}
and
\delta_{\rm\cancel{\rm PQ}})
, it generically predicts an observable nEDM just beyond the current bound.

Since for the gluon operator, the axion-nucleon interaction is dominantly generated by the axion VEV, this corresponds to the usual picture in which the nEDM is the most important constraint.
For the lepton Yukawa, we have seen that the scalar interactions generated directly by eq. (
32
) dominate by far the
one due to
contribution from the axion VEV, see eq. (
49

lepton-Yukawa PQ-breaking operators.

4.6
Experimental prospects and testability

We have evaluated each of the above scenarios,
implementing a PQ-breaking axion-SM coupling either through a lepton Yukawa, a quark Yukawa or through

Starting with nEDM constraints, see section

, we can immediately narrow down the list of candidate operators to only the axion-electron scenario and the axion-gluon one. Both of these scenarios exhibit interesting phenomenology.

In the axion-electron scenario, we only have a contribution to
\theta_{\rm eff}
at the loop level, which allows us to consider stronger couplings (lower
\Lambda_{\rm UV}
) without violating nEDM constraints. These stronger couplings give rise to a direct scalar interaction, eq. (
45

eV. This means that if the QCD axion is implemented as in conventional KSVZ or DFSZ models,
the astrophysical upper limits on
m_{a}
exclude the remaining parameter space. However, in

PQ-breaking scenario features trapped misalignment DM solutions in the mass range
2\times 10^{-2}
eV
\lesssim m_{a}\lesssim 2\times 10^{-1}
eV. This class of solutions is especially interesting because it is testable with a moderate increase in the sensitivity of fifth force constraints or monopole-dipole forces. For long-range forces to be sensitive to this scenario, improvements have to be made on deviations from gravity on length scales of
\sim 5\,\mu
m. Furthermore, assuming a photon coupling for an axion with

5
Conclusions

In this work, we have studied how much room there is for PQ-breaking potentials to modify the misalignment
mechanism for axion DM
within the constraints set by the nEDM and axion-mediated forces. We have focused on the pre-inflationary scenario in which the axion field is initially homogeneous in space.

In the absence of tuning of the CP-violating phase
\delta_{\rm\cancel{\rm PQ}}
, sources of PQ breaking which are constant in temperature can at most limit the degree to which DM is under-produced by misalignment at large axion masses, i.e.
m_{a}\gtrsim 10^{-3}
eV, in agreement with the existing literature
[
13
]
.
Also, in this regime we do not get a fraction of DM
that is larger than
\Omega_{a}/\Omega_{\rm DM}\lesssim\text{few}\times 10^{-4}
.

However, by employing a particular class of
PQ-breaking operators involving also SM fields
[
19
,
20
]
, we find that the
extra
temperature dependence
of the axion potential
allows for a significant modification of the axion DM abundance from misalignment.
In particular, the same type of trapped misalignment realized for constant temperature potentials in Ref.
[
13
]
,
with the temperature-dependent potentials of Refs.
[
19
,
20
]
, allows for solutions in which the QCD axion makes up all the observed DM at masses much larger than what is conventionally expected, i.e. in the regime
10^{-5}
eV
\lesssim m_{a}\lesssim 10^{-1}
eV. This is realized by coupling the axion to SM fields through a
\phi^{n}
operator with
n\geq 2
. We have studied the scalar Yukawa interactions, axion VEVs and long-range forces implied by such couplings. In this respect, we extended the work of
Refs.
[
19
,
20
]
by considering additional contributions to the axion VEV, taking the indirect contribution to the scalar interactions into account, updating bounds from searches for fifth forces and
EP violation, and by extending
them to both monopole-monopole and monopole-dipole forces. We find that, among the PQ-breaking terms coupled
to SM operators, the electron Yukawa and the
GG
terms allow for a viable phenomenology while sizeably
affecting the production of axion DM from misalignment. SM particles heavier than
\mathcal{O}
(GeV) are not relevant because their contribution to the
thermal axion potential is suppressed at the
temperatures where the axion starts to
oscillate,
while the muon and light quark Yukawa operators predict an nEDM that exceeds the experimental bound.

For the electron Yukawa scenario (cf. top panel of figure
4
), we find that the associated PQ-breaking operator generates a scalar axion coupling
to electrons which significantly constrains this scenario through long-range forces. These
limits, in particular searches for fifth forces,
imply that all of the observed DM can only be accounted for in the regime
m_{a}\gtrsim 2\times 10^{-2}
eV, which requires non-standard axion models to
evade astrophysical limits on axion couplings to nucleons and electrons, also known as astrophobic axion models.
This scenario is testable with a moderate improvement in fifth forces searches on length scales on the order of 5
\mu
m.

For the
GG
scenario (cf. bottom panel of figure
4
), we find that the axion scalar
interaction with nucleons is (barely) dominated by the indirect contribution from
\theta_{\rm eff}
, which places this scenario in the standard regime in which the nEDM is the most sensitive probe. Therefore, long-range forces play no major role in this case.
This scenario motivates QCD axion DM in the entire parameter range
10^{-5}
eV
\lesssim m_{a}\lesssim 10^{-1}
eV, if an astrophobic model is considered, or
10^{-5}
eV
\lesssim m_{a}\lesssim 10^{-2}
eV for a conventional KSVZ or DFSZ model.
Interestingly, although an
\mathcal{O}(1)
part of the parameter space for the angles
\theta_{\rm ini}
and
\delta_{\rm\cancel{\rm PQ}}
is compatible with nEDM constraints,
one generically predicts a signal in nEDM just beyond the current observational bound. This scenario is therefore also testable with a moderate improvement in the nEDM sensitivity.

Both of these realizations of trapped misalignment motivate axion DM with
m_{a}
in the range of both high-mass haloscopes such as BREAD
[
75
]
, WISPFI
[
76
]
and LAMPOST
[
77
]
, as well as the helioscope IAXO
[
78
]
. The reach of these experiments is indicated in figure
4
.

Finally, we comment on avenues in which this study may be extended. Firstly, while our results predict QCD axion DM solutions tantalizingly close to current observational bounds, for the gluon scenario we rely on QCD dynamics at temperatures
T\sim 1
GeV near the QCD confinement scale.
Our results are only to be trusted at the
\mathcal{O}(1)
level, and it would be
worth to improve on this.
Furthermore, in this study, we left out the photon coupling
\left(\phi/\Lambda_{\rm UV}\right)^{n}FF
. It would be interesting to extend this study with a careful treatment of the (2-loop) thermal effects
and the associated constraints on this scenario.
Finally, our setup relies on terms that couple the axion to a single SM operator without introducing a
\phi^{n}/\Lambda_{\rm UV}^{n-4}
operator. We have taken a bottom-up approach and have not attempted to find a UV completion for this scenario. Clearly, it would be relevant to identify UV structures that naturally realize such a term.

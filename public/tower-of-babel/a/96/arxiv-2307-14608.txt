Smooth modules over the N=1 Bondi-Metzner-Sachs superalgebra Thanks: Mathematics Subject Classification: 17B65, 17B68, 17B69,17B70, 81R10.
Dong Liu, Yufeng Pei, Limeng Xia, Kaiming Zhao

Abstract
. In this paper, we present a determinant formula for the contravariant form on Verma modules over the N=1 Bondi-Metzner-Sachs (BMS) superalgebra. This formula establishes a necessary and sufficient condition for the irreducibility of the Verma modules. We then introduce and characterize a class of simple smooth modules that generalize both Verma and Whittaker modules over the N=1 BMS superalgebra. We also utilize the Heisenberg-Clifford vertex superalgebra to construct a free field realization for the N=1 BMS superalgebra. This free field realization allows us to obtain a family of natural smooth modules over the N=1 BMS superalgebra, which includes Fock modules and certain Whittaker modules.

Definition 2.1

(

The

\displaystyle{[L_{m},L_{n}]}=(m-n)L_{m+n}+{1\over 12}\delta_{m+n,0}(m^{3}-m){\bf c}_{1},

\displaystyle{[L_{m},M_{n}]}=(m-n)M_{m+n}+{1\over 12}\delta_{m+n,0}(m^{3}-m){\bf c}_{2},

\displaystyle{[Q_{r},Q_{s}]}=2M_{r+s}+{1\over 3}\delta_{r+s,0}\left(r^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\right){\bf c}_{2},

\displaystyle{[L_{m},Q_{r}]}=\left(\frac{m}{2}-r\right)Q_{m+r},

\displaystyle{[M_{m},M_{n}]}=[M_{n},Q_{r}]=0,

\displaystyle[{\bf c}_{1},\mathfrak{g}]=[{\bf c}_{2},\mathfrak{g}]=0,

Note that the even part

The N=1 BMS superalgebra

has a close relation with the N=1 Neveu-Schwarz algebra

\mathfrak{ns}=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}L_{n}\oplus\bigoplus_{r\in\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}}\mathbb{C}G_{r}\oplus\mathbb{C}\mathbf{c}

\displaystyle{\left[L_{m},L_{n}\right]}

\displaystyle=(m-n)L_{m+n}+\delta_{m,-n}\frac{m^{3}-m}{12}\mathbf{c},

\displaystyle{\left[L_{m},G_{r}\right]}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{m}{2}-r\right)G_{m+r},

\displaystyle{\left[G_{r},G_{s}\right]}

\displaystyle=2L_{r+s}+\frac{1}{3}\delta_{r+s,0}\left(r^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\right)\mathbf{c},

\displaystyle{[\mathfrak{ns},\mathbf{c}]}

\displaystyle=0,

for all

\left|L_{n}\right|=\overline{0},\quad\left|G_{r}\right|=\overline{1},\quad|\mathbf{c}|=\overline{0}.

Let

\displaystyle L_{m}

\displaystyle\mapsto L_{m}\otimes 1,

\displaystyle M_{m}

The N=1 BMS superalgebra

has an anti-involution

\displaystyle\overline{L_{n}}=L_{-n},\quad\overline{M_{n}}=M_{-n},\quad\overline{Q_{r}}=Q_{-r},\quad\overline{{\bf c}_{1}}={\bf c}_{1},\quad\overline{{\bf c}_{2}}={\bf c}_{2}

Moreover,

It follows that

where

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}_{\pm}

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\mathbb{C}L_{\pm n}\oplus\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\mathbb{C}M_{\pm n}\oplus\bigoplus_{r\in\mathbb{N}+\frac{1}{2}}\mathbb{C}Q_{\pm r},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}_{0}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{C}L_{0}\oplus\mathbb{C}M_{0}\oplus\mathbb{C}{{\bf c}_{1}}\oplus\mathbb{C}{{\bf c}_{2}}.

For any

3.1.
Contravariant forms on Verma modules

For
h_{1},h_{2},{c_{1},c_{2}}\in\mathbb{C}
, let
\mathbb{C}
be a one-dimensional
{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}
-module defined by

L_{0}1=h_{1}1,\quad M_{0}1=h_{2}1,\quad{\bf c}_{1}1=c_{1}1,\quad{\bf c}_{2}1=c_{2}1.

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}_{+}
act trivially on
1
, making
\mathbb{C}
be a
({\mathfrak{g}}_{0}\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}_{+})
-module.
The Verma module
M_{\mathfrak{g}}(h_{1},h_{2},c_{1},c_{2})
over
\mathfrak{g}
is defined by

M_{\mathfrak{g}}(h_{1},h_{2},c_{1},c_{2})=U(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{g}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{+})}\mathbb{C}\cong U(\mathfrak{g}_{-}){\bf 1},\quad(\text{as vector spaces}),

where
{\bf 1}=1\otimes 1
. Moreover,

M_{\mathfrak{g}}(h_{1},h_{2},c_{1},c_{2})=\bigoplus_{n\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{N}}M_{\mathfrak{g}}(h_{1},h_{2},c_{1},c_{2})_{n},

where

M_{\mathfrak{g}}(h_{1},h_{2},c_{1},c_{2})_{n}=\{v\in M_{\mathfrak{g}}(h_{1},h_{2},c_{1},c_{2})\,|\,L_{0}v=(h_{1}+n)v\}.

According to the Poincaré-Birkhoff-Witt (
\mathrm{PBW}
) theorem, every element
v
of
M_{\mathfrak{g}}(h_{1},h_{2},c_{1},c_{2})
can be uniquely written in
the following form

v=\sum_{\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j}\in\mathbb{M},\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{M}_{1}}a_{\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j},\mathbf{k}}Q^{\mathbf{k}}M^{\mathbf{j}}L^{\mathbf{i}}{\bf 1},

(3.1)

where
a_{\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j},\mathbf{k}}\in\mathbb{C}
and only finitely many of them are nonzero. For any
v\in M_{\mathfrak{g}}(h_{1},h_{2},c_{1},c_{2})
as in (
3.1
), we denote by
\mathrm{supp}(v)
the set of all
(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j},\mathbf{k})\in\mathbb{M}_{1}\times\mathbb{M}^{2}
such that
a_{\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j},\mathbf{k}}\neq 0
.

The Verma module
M_{\mathfrak{g}}(h_{1},h_{2},c_{1},c_{2})
has
a unique maximal submodule
J(h_{1},h_{2},c_{1},c_{2})
and the factor module

L_{\mathfrak{g}}(h_{1},h_{2},c_{1},c_{2})=M_{\mathfrak{g}}(h_{1},h_{2},c_{1},c_{2})/J(h_{1},h_{2},c_{1},c_{2})

is simple.

Let
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
be a
\mathbb{C}
-value bilinear form on
M_{\mathfrak{g}}(h_{1},h_{2},c_{1},c_{2})
defined by

\displaystyle\langle a{\bf 1},b{\bf 1}\rangle=\langle{\bf 1},{\rm P}(\bar{a}b){\bf 1}\rangle,\quad\langle{\bf 1},{\bf 1}\rangle=1,\quad\forall\ a,b\in U(\mathfrak{g}_{-}),

where
{\rm P}:U(\mathfrak{g})\to U(\mathfrak{g}_{0})
be the Harish-Chandra projection, i.e.,
a projection along the decomposition

\displaystyle U(\mathfrak{g})=U(\mathfrak{g}_{0})\oplus(\mathfrak{g}_{-}U(\mathfrak{g})+U(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{g}_{+}).

Since
{\rm P}(\overline{a}b)={\rm P}(\overline{b}a)
, we know that the bilinear form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is symmetric, i.e.

\langle a{\bf 1},b{\bf 1}\rangle=\langle b{\bf 1},a{\bf 1}\rangle,\quad\forall\ a,b\in U(\mathfrak{g}_{-}).

(3.2)

Also, the form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is contravariant, i.e.,
for
m\in\mathbb{Z},r\in\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}
,
u,v\in M_{\mathfrak{g}}(h_{1},h_{2},c_{1},c_{2})
, we have

\langle L_{m}u,v\rangle=\langle u,L_{-m}v\rangle,\quad\langle M_{m}u,v\rangle=\langle u,M_{-m}v\rangle,\quad\langle Q_{r}u,v\rangle=\langle u,Q_{-r}v\rangle.

The simplicity of the Verma module
M_{\mathfrak{g}}(h_{1},h_{2},c_{1},c_{2})
is encoded in the determinant of the contravariant form. Since distinct weight spaces of
M_{\mathfrak{g}}(h_{1},h_{2},c_{1},c_{2})
are orthogonal with respect to
\langle\;,\;\rangle
, the Verma module
M_{\mathfrak{g}}(h_{1},h_{2},c_{1},c_{2})
is simple if and only if the restricted forms

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{n}:M_{\mathfrak{g}}(h_{1},h_{2},c_{1},c_{2})_{n}\times M_{\mathfrak{g}}(h_{1},h_{2},c_{1},c_{2})_{n}\to\mathbb{C}

are nondegenerate for all
n\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{N}
.

For
n\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{N}
, let

S_{n}=\{Q^{\mathbf{k}}M^{\mathbf{j}}L^{\mathbf{i}}{\bf 1}:{\rm w}(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j},\mathbf{k})=n,\forall\ \mathbf{i},\mathbf{j}\in\mathbb{M},\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{M}_{1}\}.

From the PBW theorem,
S_{n}
is a basis of
M_{\mathfrak{g}}(h_{1},h_{2},c_{1},c_{2})_{n}
. Hence

|S_{n}|=p(n):=\#\left\{(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j},\mathbf{k})\in\mathbb{M}^{2}\times\mathbb{M}_{1}:{\rm w}(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j},\mathbf{k})=n\right\},

where
p(n)
can be counted by the generating series

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{N}}p(n)q^{n}=\frac{\prod_{k\in\mathbb{N}+\frac{1}{2}}(1+q^{k})}{\prod_{k\geq 1}(1-q^{k})^{2}}.

Denote by
>
the
lexicographical total order
on
\mathbb{M}
, defined as follows: for any
\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j}\in\mathbb{M}
, set

\mathbf{i}>\mathbf{j}\ \Leftrightarrow\ \mathrm{there\ exists}\ r\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\ \mathrm{such\ that}\ i_{r}>j_{r}\ \mathrm{and}\ i_{s}=j_{s},\ \forall\;s>r.

Now we can induce a principal total order
>
on
S_{n}
:

Q^{\mathbf{k}}M^{\mathbf{j}}L^{\mathbf{i}}{\bf 1}>Q^{\mathbf{k}_{1}}M^{\mathbf{j}_{1}}L^{\mathbf{i}_{1}}{\bf 1}

if and only if one of the following conditions is satisfied:

(i)

|\mathbf{j}|>|\mathbf{j}_{1}|;

(ii)

|\mathbf{j}|=|\mathbf{j}_{1}|\ \mbox{and}\ \mathbf{j}_{1}>\mathbf{j}
;

(iii)

\mathbf{j}=\mathbf{j}_{1},\ |\mathbf{k}|>|\mathbf{k}_{1}|;

(iv)

\mathbf{j}=\mathbf{j}_{1},\ |\mathbf{k}|=|\mathbf{k}_{1}|\ \mbox{and}\ \mathbf{k}>\mathbf{k}_{1};

(v)

\mathbf{j}=\mathbf{j}_{1},\ \mathbf{k}=\mathbf{k}_{1},\ \mbox{and}\ \mathbf{i}>\mathbf{i}_{1}.

Sort the elements in
S_{n}
by
>
, for example,

\displaystyle S_{0}

\displaystyle=\{{\bf 1}\},

\displaystyle S_{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\{Q_{-\frac{1}{2}}{\bf 1}\},

\displaystyle S_{1}

\displaystyle=\{M_{-1}{\bf 1},L_{-1}{\bf 1}\},

\displaystyle S_{\frac{3}{2}}

\displaystyle=\left\{Q_{-\frac{1}{2}}M_{-1}{\bf 1},Q_{-\frac{3}{2}}{\bf 1},Q_{-\frac{1}{2}}L_{-1}{\bf 1}\right\},

\displaystyle S_{2}

\displaystyle=\left\{M_{-1}^{2}{\bf 1},M_{-2}{\bf 1},M_{-1}L_{-1}{\bf 1},Q_{-\frac{3}{2}}Q_{-\frac{1}{2}}{\bf 1},L_{-2}{\bf 1},L_{-1}^{2}{\bf 1}\right\}.

3.2.
Simplicity of Verma modules

Let
G_{n}
be the Gram matrix of the form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{n}
defined by

\langle Q^{\mathbf{k}}M^{\mathbf{j}}L^{\mathbf{i}}{\bf 1},Q^{\mathbf{k}_{1}}M^{\mathbf{j}_{1}}L^{\mathbf{i}_{1}}{\bf 1}\rangle,

where
{\rm w}(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j},\mathbf{k})={\rm w}(\mathbf{i}_{1},\mathbf{j}_{1},\mathbf{k}_{1})=n
.

For
u=Q^{\mathbf{k}}M^{\mathbf{j}}L^{\mathbf{i}}{\bf 1}\in S_{n},

we define

\displaystyle u^{*}=Q^{\mathbf{k}}M^{\mathbf{i}}L^{\mathbf{j}}{\bf 1}.

Then

S_{n}^{*}=\{u^{*}\mid u\in S_{n}\}

is also a basis of
M_{\mathfrak{g}}(h_{1},h_{2},c_{1},c_{2})_{n}
. It is clear that

\displaystyle S^{*}_{0}

\displaystyle=\{{\bf 1}\},

\displaystyle S^{*}_{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\{Q_{-\frac{1}{2}}{\bf 1}\},

\displaystyle S^{*}_{1}

\displaystyle=\{L_{-1}{\bf 1},M_{-1}{\bf 1}\},

\displaystyle S^{*}_{\frac{3}{2}}

\displaystyle=\left\{Q_{-\frac{1}{2}}L_{-1}{\bf 1},Q_{-\frac{3}{2}}{\bf 1},Q_{-\frac{1}{2}}M_{-1}{\bf 1}\right\},

\displaystyle S^{*}_{2}

\displaystyle=\left\{L_{-1}^{2}{\bf 1},L_{-2}{\bf 1},L_{-1}M_{-1}{\bf 1},Q_{-\frac{3}{2}}Q_{-\frac{1}{2}}{\bf 1},M_{-2}{\bf 1},M_{-1}^{2}{\bf 1}\right\}.

Let
D_{n}=(d_{ij})
, where
d_{ij}=\langle b_{i},b_{j}^{*}\rangle
for
i,j=1,\dots p(n)
. Then

D_{\frac{1}{2}}=(2h_{2}),\quad D_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}2h_{2}&0\\
2h_{1}&2h_{2}\\
\end{pmatrix},\quad D_{\frac{3}{2}}=\begin{pmatrix}4h_{2}^{2}&0&0\\
*&2h_{2}+\frac{2}{3}c_{2}&0\\
*&*&4h_{2}^{2}\\
\end{pmatrix},

D_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}8h_{2}^{2}&0&0&0&0&0\\
*&4h_{2}+\frac{1}{2}c_{2}&0&0&0&0\\
*&*&4h_{2}^{2}&0&0&0\\
*&*&*&2h_{2}(2h_{2}+\frac{2}{3}c_{2})&0&0\\
*&*&*&*&4h_{2}+\frac{1}{2}c_{2}&0\\
*&*&*&*&*&8h_{2}^{2}\\
\end{pmatrix}.

If
\mathbf{j}\in\mathbb{M}
,
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
with
m\geq k
for all
k\in{\rm supp}(\mathbf{j})
, then

L_{m}M^{\mathbf{j}}{\bf 1}=\left(2mh_{2}+\frac{m^{3}-m}{12}c_{2}\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial M_{-m}}M^{\mathbf{j}}{\bf 1},

where
\frac{\partial}{\partial M_{-m}}
is the operation of formal partial derivative defined by the Leibniz rule and

\frac{\partial}{\partial M_{-m}}(M_{-n})=\delta_{m,n},\quad\frac{\partial}{\partial M_{-m}}({\bf 1})=0.

It follows immediately that if
\mathbf{i}>\mathbf{j}
,

\left\langle L^{\mathbf{i}}{\bf 1},M^{\mathbf{j}}{\bf 1}\right\rangle=L_{1}^{i_{1}}L_{2}^{i_{2}}\cdots M_{-2}^{j_{2}}M_{-1}^{j_{1}}{\bf 1}=0.

(3.3)

If
\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{M}_{1}
,
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and
k_{t}=1,k_{t+i}=0
, then

Q_{t-\frac{1}{2}}Q^{\mathbf{k}}{\bf 1}=\left(2h_{2}+\frac{4t^{2}-1}{12}c_{2}\right)Q^{\mathbf{k}^{\prime\prime}}{\bf 1}.

It follows immediately that if
\mathbf{i}>\mathbf{i}_{1}
,

\left\langle Q^{\mathbf{i}}{\bf 1},Q^{\mathbf{i}_{1}}{\bf 1}\right\rangle=0.

(3.4)

Lemma 3.1
.

The matrix
D_{n}
is lower triangular for any
n\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. Moreover, the diagonal entries of
D_{n}
are

d_{ii}=\prod_{r\in{\rm supp}(\mathbf{i})}r\left(2h_{2}+\frac{r^{2}-1}{12}c_{2}\right)^{i_{r}}\prod_{t\in{\rm supp}(\mathbf{k})}\left(2h_{2}+\frac{4t^{2}-1}{12}c_{2}\right)^{k_{t}}\prod_{s\in{\rm supp}(\mathbf{j})}s\left(2h_{2}+\frac{s^{2}-1}{12}c_{2}\right)^{j_{s}},

where
b_{i}=Q^{\mathbf{k}}M^{\mathbf{j}}L^{\mathbf{i}}{\bf 1}\in S_{n}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
b_{i}=Q^{\mathbf{k}}M^{\mathbf{j}}L^{\mathbf{i}}{\bf 1}>Q^{\mathbf{k}_{1}}M^{\mathbf{j}_{1}}L^{\mathbf{i}_{1}}{\bf 1}=b_{j}
with
Q^{\mathbf{k}}M^{\mathbf{j}}L^{\mathbf{i}}{\bf 1},Q^{\mathbf{k}_{1}}M^{\mathbf{j}_{1}}L^{\mathbf{i}_{1}}{\bf 1}\in S_{n}.
It suffices to show

\left\langle b_{i},b_{j}^{*}\right\rangle=\left\langle Q^{\mathbf{k}}M^{\mathbf{j}}L^{\mathbf{i}}{\bf 1},\left(Q^{\mathbf{k}_{1}}M^{\mathbf{j}_{1}}L^{\mathbf{i}_{1}}{\bf 1}\right)^{*}\right\rangle=0.

•

If
|\mathbf{j}|>|\mathbf{j}_{1}|
, then

0=\left\langle M^{\mathbf{j}}{\bf 1},L^{\mathbf{j}_{1}}{\bf 1}\right\rangle=\left\langle{\bf 1},\overline{M^{\mathbf{j}}}L^{\mathbf{j}_{1}}{\bf 1}\right\rangle\Rightarrow\overline{M^{\mathbf{j}}}L^{\mathbf{j}_{1}}{\bf 1}=0.

It follows that

\displaystyle d_{ij}

\displaystyle=\left\langle Q^{\mathbf{k}}M^{\mathbf{j}}L^{\mathbf{i}}{\bf 1},Q^{\mathbf{k}_{1}}M^{\mathbf{i}_{1}}L^{\mathbf{j}_{1}}{\bf 1}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle{\bf 1},\overline{L^{\mathbf{i}}}\cdot\overline{M^{\mathbf{j}}}\cdot\overline{Q^{\mathbf{k}}}\cdot Q^{\mathbf{k}_{1}}M^{\mathbf{i}_{1}}L^{\mathbf{j}_{1}}{\bf 1}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle{\bf 1},\overline{L^{\mathbf{i}}}\cdot\overline{Q^{\mathbf{k}}}\cdot Q^{\mathbf{k}_{1}}M^{\mathbf{i}_{1}}\overline{M^{\mathbf{j}}}L^{\mathbf{j}_{1}}{\bf 1}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=0.

•

If
|\mathbf{j}|=|\mathbf{j}_{1}|\ \mbox{and}\ \mathbf{j}<\mathbf{j}_{1}
, it follows from (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) that
\overline{M^{\mathbf{j}}}L^{\mathbf{j}_{1}}{\bf 1}=\overline{L^{\mathbf{j}_{1}}}M^{\mathbf{j}}{\bf 1}=0
. Then

\displaystyle d_{ij}

\displaystyle=\left\langle Q^{\mathbf{k}}M^{\mathbf{j}}L^{\mathbf{i}}{\bf 1},Q^{\mathbf{k}_{1}}M^{\mathbf{i}_{1}}L^{\mathbf{j}_{1}}{\bf 1}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle{\bf 1},\overline{L^{\mathbf{i}}}\cdot\overline{M^{\mathbf{j}}}\cdot\overline{Q^{\mathbf{k}}}\cdot Q^{\mathbf{k}_{1}}M^{\mathbf{i}_{1}}L^{\mathbf{j}_{1}}{\bf 1}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle{\bf 1},\overline{L^{\mathbf{i}}}\cdot\overline{Q^{\mathbf{k}}}\cdot Q^{\mathbf{k}_{1}}M^{\mathbf{i}_{1}}\overline{M^{\mathbf{j}}}L^{\mathbf{j}_{1}}{\bf 1}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=0.

•

If

\mathbf{j}=\mathbf{j}_{1},\ |\mathbf{k}|>|\mathbf{k}_{1}|
or
|\mathbf{k}|=|\mathbf{k}_{1}|,\mathbf{k}>\mathbf{k}_{1}
, it is clear that
\overline{Q^{\mathbf{k}}}Q^{\mathbf{k}_{1}}{\bf 1}=0
. It follows that

\displaystyle d_{ij}

\displaystyle=\left\langle Q^{\mathbf{k}}M^{\mathbf{j}}L^{\mathbf{i}}{\bf 1},Q^{\mathbf{k}_{1}}M^{\mathbf{i}_{1}}L^{\mathbf{j}_{1}}{\bf 1}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle{\bf 1},\overline{L^{\mathbf{i}}}M^{\mathbf{i}_{1}}\overline{Q^{\mathbf{k}}}Q^{\mathbf{k}_{1}}{\bf 1}\right\rangle\left\langle{\bf 1},\overline{M^{\mathbf{j}}}L^{\mathbf{j}_{1}}{\bf 1}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=0.

•

If
\mathbf{j}=\mathbf{j}_{1},\ \mathbf{k}=\mathbf{k}_{1},\ \mbox{and}\ \mathbf{i}>\mathbf{i}_{1}
, it follows from (
3.3
) that
\overline{L^{\mathbf{i}}}M^{\mathbf{i}_{1}}{\bf 1}=0
. Then

\displaystyle d_{ij}

\displaystyle=\left\langle Q^{\mathbf{k}}M^{\mathbf{j}}L^{\mathbf{i}}{\bf 1},Q^{\mathbf{k}_{1}}M^{\mathbf{i}_{1}}L^{\mathbf{j}_{1}}{\bf 1}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle{\bf 1},\overline{L^{\mathbf{i}}}\cdot\overline{M^{\mathbf{j}}}\cdot\overline{Q^{\mathbf{k}}}\cdot Q^{\mathbf{k}_{1}}M^{\mathbf{i}_{1}}L^{\mathbf{j}_{1}}{\bf 1}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle{\bf 1},\overline{L^{\mathbf{i}}}M^{\mathbf{i}_{1}}{\bf 1}\right\rangle\left\langle{\bf 1},\overline{Q^{\mathbf{k}}}Q^{\mathbf{k}_{1}}{\bf 1}\right\rangle\left\langle{\bf 1},\overline{M^{\mathbf{j}}}L^{\mathbf{j}_{1}}{\bf 1}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=0.

Moreover,

\displaystyle d_{ii}

\displaystyle=\left\langle{\bf 1},\overline{L^{\mathbf{i}}}M^{\mathbf{i}}\right\rangle\left\langle{\bf 1},\overline{Q^{\mathbf{k}}}Q^{\mathbf{k}}{\bf 1}\right\rangle\left\langle{\bf 1},\overline{M^{\mathbf{j}}}L^{\mathbf{j}}{\bf 1}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\prod_{r\in{\rm supp}(\mathbf{i})}r\left(2h_{2}+\frac{r^{2}-1}{12}c_{2}\right)^{i_{r}}\prod_{t\in{\rm supp}(\mathbf{k})}\left(2h_{2}+\frac{4t^{2}-1}{12}c_{2}\right)^{k_{t}}\prod_{s\in{\rm supp}(\mathbf{j})}s\left(2h_{2}+\frac{s^{2}-1}{12}c_{2}\right)^{j_{s}}

for
i=1,2,\dots p(n)
.
∎

We are now in the position to state the main result in this section:

Theorem 3.2
.

The Verma module
M_{\mathfrak{g}}(h_{1},h_{2},c_{1},c_{2})
is simple if and only if

h_{2}+\frac{i^{2}-1}{24}c_{2}\neq 0,\quad\forall\ i\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}.

Proof.

For all
n\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{N}
, the restricted form

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{n}:M_{\mathfrak{g}}(h_{1},h_{2},c_{1},c_{2})_{n}\times M_{\mathfrak{g}}(h_{1},h_{2},c_{1},c_{2})_{n}\to\mathbb{C}

is nondegenerate if and only if the determinant of the Gram matrix
G_{n}
is nonzero, if and only if
{\rm det}\,D_{n}\neq 0
. By applying Lemma
3.1
, we can directly conclude that
{\rm det}\,D_{n}=\prod_{i=1}^{p(n)}d_{ii}\neq 0
if and only if

h_{2}+\frac{i^{2}-1}{24}c_{2}\neq 0,\quad\forall\ i\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}.

∎

As a corollary of Theorem
3.2
, we know that
M_{\mathfrak{g}}(0,0,c_{1},c_{2})
is not simple. In fact,
Q_{-\frac{1}{2}}{\bf 1}
is a singular vector of
M(0,0,c_{1},c_{2})
, and then
L_{-1}{\bf 1}
is a subsingular vector.
The
vaccum module
for
\mathfrak{g}
is
the quotient module

V_{\mathfrak{g}}(c_{1},c_{2})=M_{\mathfrak{g}}(0,0,c_{1},c_{2})/J,

where
J
is the submodule of
V_{\mathfrak{g}}(0,0,c_{1},c_{2})
generated by
L_{-1}{\bf 1}
. Denote by
{\mathds{1}}:={\bf 1}+J
the vacuum vector of
V_{\mathfrak{g}}(c_{1},c_{2})
. Using a similar argument as Theorem
3.2
, one obtains the following criterion of
simplicity of the vacuum module:

Theorem 3.3
.

The vaccum module
V_{\mathfrak{g}}(c_{1},c_{2})
is simple if and only if
c_{2}\neq 0
.

4.
Classifications of simple smooth
\mathfrak{g}
-modules

In this section, we construct and classify all simple smooth
\mathfrak{g}
-modules of central charge
(c_{1},c_{2})
under certain conditions.

4.1.
Constructing simple smooth
\mathfrak{g}
-modules

For any
m\in\mathbb{N},n\in\mathbb{Z}
with
n\leq 1
, let

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}^{(m,n)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{i\geq m}\mathbb{C}L_{i}\oplus\bigoplus_{j\geq n}\mathbb{C}M_{j}\oplus\bigoplus_{k\geq 1}\mathbb{C}Q_{k-\frac{1}{2}}\oplus\mathbb{C}{\bf c}_{1}\oplus\mathbb{C}{\bf c}_{2}.

Note that
\mathfrak{g}^{(m,n)}
is a subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{g}^{(0,0)}=\mathfrak{g}_{+}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{0}
.

Given
q\in\mathbb{N}
and
a
\mathfrak{g}^{(0,-q)}
-module
V
of central charge
(c_{1},c_{2})
. We consider the following induced
\mathfrak{g}
-module

{\rm Ind}_{q}(V)={\rm Ind}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{g}^{(0,-q)}}V.

According to the PBW theorem, every element
v
of
{\rm Ind}_{q}(V)
can be uniquely written in the following form

v=\sum_{\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j}\in\mathbb{M},\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{M}_{1}}Q^{\mathbf{k}}M^{\mathbf{j}}L^{\mathbf{i}}v_{\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j},\mathbf{k}},

(4.1)

where all
v_{\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j},\mathbf{k}}\in V
and only finitely many of them are nonzero.

Denote by
\prec
the
reverse lexicographical total order
on
\mathbb{M}
, defined as follows: for any
\mathbf{i},\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{M}
, set

\mathbf{i}\prec\mathbf{j}\ \Leftrightarrow\ \mathrm{there\ exists}\ r\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\ \mathrm{such\ that}\ i_{r}<j_{r}\ \mathrm{and}\ i_{s}=j_{s},\ \forall\;1\leq s<r.

Next we introduce a principal total order on
\mathbb{M}\times\mathbb{M}_{1}
as follows.

Denoted by
\prec
:

(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{k})\prec(\mathbf{j}_{1},\mathbf{k}_{1})
if and only if one of the following conditions is satisfied:

(1)
\mathbf{k}<\mathbf{k}_{1}
;

(2)
\ \mathbf{k}=\mathbf{k}_{1}\ \mathrm{and}\ \mathbf{j}<\mathbf{j}_{1}
.

Now we can induce a principal total order on
\mathbb{M}^{2}\times\mathbb{M}_{1}
, still denoted by
\prec
:

(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j},\mathbf{k})\prec(\mathbf{i}_{1},\mathbf{j}_{1},\mathbf{k}_{1})
if and only if one of the following conditions is satisfied:

(1)
{\rm w}(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j},\mathbf{k})<{\rm w}(\mathbf{i}_{1},\mathbf{j}_{1},\mathbf{k}_{1})
;

(2)
{\rm w}(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j},\mathbf{k})={\rm w}(\mathbf{i}_{1},\mathbf{j}_{1},\mathbf{k}_{1})
and
\mathbf{i}_{1}\prec\mathbf{i}
;

(3)
{\rm w}(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j},\mathbf{k})={\rm w}(\mathbf{i}_{1},\mathbf{j}_{1},\mathbf{k}_{1})
and
\mathbf{i}=\mathbf{i}_{1}
and
(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{k})\prec(\mathbf{j}_{1},\mathbf{k}_{1})
.

For any
v\in{\rm Ind}_{q}(V)
as in (
3.1
), we denote by
\mathrm{supp}(v)
the set of all
(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j},\mathbf{k})\in\mathbb{M}^{2}\times\mathbb{M}_{1}
such that
v_{\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j},\mathbf{k}}\neq 0
.
For a nonzero
v\in{\rm Ind}_{q}(V)
, we write
\mathrm{deg}(v)
the maximal (with respect to the principal total order on
\mathbb{M}^{2}\times\mathbb{M}_{1}
) element in
\mathrm{supp}(v)
, called the degree of
v
. Note that here and later
we make the
convention that
\mathrm{deg}(v)
is only for
v\neq 0
.

Theorem 4.1
.

For
q\in\mathbb{N}
,
c_{1},c_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
, let
V
be
a simple
\mathfrak{g}^{(0,-q)}
-module of central charge
(c_{1},c_{2})
.
Assume that there exists
t\in\mathbb{N}
satisfying the following two conditions:

(a)

the action of
M_{t}
on
V
is injective if
t>0
or the action of
M_{0}+\frac{1}{24}(n^{2}-1){\bf c}_{2}
on
V
is injective for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{*}
if
t=0
;

(b)

M_{i}V=0
for all
i>t
and
L_{j}V=0
for all
j>t+q
.

Then

(i)

Q_{i-\frac{1}{2}}V=0
for all
i>t
;

(ii)

The induced module
{\rm Ind}_{q}(V)
is a simple module in
\mathcal{R}_{\mathfrak{g}}(c_{1},c_{2})
.

Proof.

(i) For
i>t\geq 0
, we have
Q_{i-\frac{1}{2}}^{2}V=M_{2i-1}V=0
by (b). If
Q_{j-\frac{1}{2}}V=0
we are done. Otherwise,
W=Q_{j-\frac{1}{2}}V
is a proper subspace of
V
. For
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, we have

Q_{j-\frac{1}{2}}W=Q_{j-\frac{1}{2}}Q_{i-\frac{1}{2}}V=2M_{i+j-1}V-Q_{i-\frac{1}{2}}Q_{j-\frac{1}{2}}V\subset Q_{i-\frac{1}{2}}V=W.

Clearly
L_{n}W\subset W
and
M_{m}W\subset W
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
m\in\mathbb{Z}
with
m\geq-q
.
It follows that
W
is a proper
\mathfrak{g}^{(0,-q)}
-submodule of
V
. Then
W=Q_{i-\frac{1}{2}}V=0
for
i>t
since
V
is simple.

(ii)
Since the actions of
L_{i},M_{i},Q_{i-\frac{1}{2}}
on
{\rm Ind}_{q}(V)
for all
i>t+q
are locally nilpotent,

{\rm Ind}_{q}(V)
is an object in
\mathcal{R}_{\mathfrak{g}}(c_{1},c_{2})
.

Suppose that
W
is a nonzero proper
\mathfrak{g}
-submodule of
{\rm Ind}_{q}(V)
. Now we are going to deduce some contradictions. Take an element
v\in W\setminus V
such that
\mathrm{deg}(v)=(\underline{\mathbf{i}},\underline{\mathbf{j}},\underline{\mathbf{k}})
is minimal. Write

v=\sum_{\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j}\in\mathbb{M},\,\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{M}_{1}}Q^{\mathbf{k}}M^{\mathbf{j}}L^{\mathbf{i}}v_{\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j},\mathbf{k}},

(4.2)

where all
v_{\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j},\mathbf{k}}\in V
and only finitely many of them are nonzero.

Note that there exists
t\in\mathbb{N}
satisfying the following condition:
the action of
M_{t}
on
V
is injective if
t>0
or the action of
M_{0}+\frac{1}{24}(n^{2}-1){\bf c}_{2}
on
V
is injective for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{*}
if
t=0
.

Claim 1.

\underline{\mathbf{i}}=\mathbf{0}
.

If
\underline{\mathbf{i}}\neq\mathbf{0}
, then
\hat{\underline{i}}={\rm min}\{s\mid i_{s}\neq 0\}>0
, we shall prove that
\mathrm{deg}(M_{{\hat{\underline{i}}}+t}v)\prec(\underline{\mathbf{i}},\underline{\mathbf{j}},\underline{\mathbf{k}})
. It contradicts to the choice of
v
. Note that
M_{\hat{\underline{i}}+t}v_{\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j},\mathbf{k}}=0
for any
(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j},\mathbf{k})\in\mathrm{supp}(v)
.

Case 1.

t>0
. One can easily check that

\deg(M_{\hat{\underline{i}}+t}Q^{\underline{\mathbf{k}}}M^{\underline{\mathbf{j}}}L^{\underline{\mathbf{i}}}v_{\underline{\mathbf{i}},\underline{\mathbf{j}},\underline{\mathbf{k}}})=\deg(Q^{\mathbf{k}}M^{\underline{\mathbf{j}}}[M_{\hat{\underline{i}}+t},L^{\underline{\mathbf{i}}}]v_{\underline{\mathbf{i}},\underline{\mathbf{j}},\mathbf{k}})=(\underline{\mathbf{i}}^{\prime},\underline{\mathbf{j}},\underline{\mathbf{k}}).

Here
M_{t}v_{\underline{\mathbf{i}},\underline{\mathbf{j}},\underline{\mathbf{k}}}\neq 0
by (a).

For
(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j},\mathbf{k})\in{\rm supp}(v)
with
(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j},\mathbf{k})\prec(\underline{\mathbf{i}},\underline{\mathbf{j}},\underline{\mathbf{k}})
, if

{\rm w}(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j},\mathbf{k})<{\rm w}(\underline{\mathbf{i}},\underline{\mathbf{j}},\underline{\mathbf{k}}),

then

\deg M_{\hat{\underline{i}}+t}Q^{\mathbf{k}}M^{\mathbf{j}}L^{\mathbf{i}}v_{\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j},\mathbf{k}}\prec(\underline{\mathbf{i}}^{\prime},\underline{\mathbf{j}},\underline{\mathbf{k}}).

Now we suppose that
{\rm w}(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j},\mathbf{k})={\rm w}(\underline{\mathbf{i}},\underline{\mathbf{j}},\underline{\mathbf{k}})

and
\mathbf{i}\prec\underline{\mathbf{i}}
.

If
\hat{i}>\hat{\underline{i}}
, then
{\rm w}(\mathbf{i}^{\prime},\mathbf{j},\mathbf{k})<{\rm w}(\underline{\mathbf{i}}^{\prime},\underline{\mathbf{j}},\underline{\mathbf{k}})
. Hence

\mathrm{deg}(M_{\hat{\underline{i}}+t}Q^{\mathbf{k}}M^{\mathbf{j}}L^{\mathbf{i}}v_{\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j},\mathbf{k}})\prec(\mathbf{i}^{\prime},\mathbf{j},\mathbf{k})\prec(\underline{\mathbf{i}}^{\prime},\underline{\mathbf{j}},\underline{\mathbf{k}}).

If
\hat{i}=\hat{\underline{i}}
, then

\mathrm{deg}(M_{\hat{\underline{i}}+t}Q^{\mathbf{k}}M^{\mathbf{j}}L^{\mathbf{i}}v_{\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j},\mathbf{k}})=(\mathbf{i}^{\prime},\mathbf{j},\mathbf{k})\prec(\underline{\mathbf{i}}^{\prime},\underline{\mathbf{j}},\underline{\mathbf{k}}).

If
{\rm w}(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j},\mathbf{k})={\rm w}(\underline{\mathbf{i}},\underline{\mathbf{j}},\underline{\mathbf{k}})
and
\mathbf{i}=\underline{\mathbf{i}}
, it is easy to see that

\deg(M_{\hat{\underline{i}}+t}Q^{\mathbf{k}}M^{\mathbf{j}}L^{\mathbf{i}}v_{\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j},\mathbf{k}})=(\mathbf{i}^{\prime},\mathbf{j},\mathbf{k})\preceq(\underline{\mathbf{i}}^{\prime},\underline{\mathbf{j}},\underline{\mathbf{k}}).

So Claim 1 holds in this case.

Case 2
:
t=0
.

Set
|v|_{\rm Vir}={\rm max}\{|\mathbf{i}|:(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j},\mathbf{k})\in{\rm supp}(v)\}
and
\ell={\rm max}\{\ell(\mathbf{i}):(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j},\mathbf{k})\in{\rm supp}(v),|\mathbf{i}|=|v|_{\rm Vir}\}
. Now
we rewrite
v
as

v=\sum_{|\mathbf{i}|=|v|_{\rm Vir}}L^{\mathbf{i}}u_{\mathbf{i}}+u,

(4.3)

where
|u|_{\rm Vir}<|v|_{\rm Vir}
and
u_{\mathbf{i}}\in U(\mathfrak{b})V
,
\mathfrak{b}

is a specified vector called

,

is an endomorphism of
V
, and

is a linear map

\displaystyle V\to(\mbox{End}\,V)[[z,z^{-1}]],

\displaystyle v\mapsto Y(v,z)=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}v_{n}z^{-n-1}\ \ \ \ (v_{n}\in({\mathrm{End}}\,V)_{\tilde{v}}),

and

for

(V2)

(V3)

(V4)

\frac{d}{dz}Y(v,z)=Y(Dv,z)=[D,Y(v,z)]

(V5)

For
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-homogeneous
u,v\in V
, the following Jacobi identity holds:

\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle{z^{-1}_{0}\delta\left(\frac{z_{1}-z_{2}}{z_{0}}\right)Y(u,z_{1})Y(v,z_{2})-(-1)^{{|u|}{|v|}}z^{-1}_{0}\delta\left(\frac{z_{2}-z_{1}}{-z_{0}}\right)Y(v,z_{2})Y(u,z_{1})}\\

where

is
expanded as a formal power series in

A homomorphism from a vertex superalgebra

For

\circ\atop\circ

\circ\atop\circ
of vertex operators as follows:

\mbox{\,$\circ\atop\circ$}\,Y(u,z_{1})Y(v,z_{2})\mbox{\,$\circ\atop\circ$}\,=Y^{+}(u,z_{1})Y(v,z_{2})+(-1)^{|u||v|}Y(v,z_{2})Y^{-}(u,z_{1}),

where

Y(u,z)=Y^{-}(u,z)+Y^{+}(u,z)=\sum_{n\geq 0}u_{n}z^{-n-1}+\sum_{n<0}u_{n}z^{-n-1}.

Let
V
be a vertex superalgebra. A

-module is a triple

, where

is a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded vector space,

\begin{array}[]{l}V\to({\mathrm{End}}\,M)[[z,z^{-1}]],\\

which satisfies that for all
u,v\in V,

w\in M,

(M1)

u_{n}w=0

(M2)

(M3)

(M4)

For
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-homogeneous
u,v
, the following Jacobi identity holds:

\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle{z^{-1}_{0}\delta\left(\frac{z_{1}-z_{2}}{z_{0}}\right)Y_{M}(u,z_{1})Y_{M}(v,z_{2})-(-1)^{|{u}||{v}|}z^{-1}_{0}\delta\left(\frac{z_{2}-z_{1}}{-z_{0}}\right)Y_{M}(v,z_{2})Y_{M}(u,z_{1})}\\

We shall see that there is

Lemma 5.1
.

Set

L(z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}L_{n}z^{-n-2},\ M(z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}M_{n}z^{-n-2},\ Q(z)=\sum_{r\in\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}}Q_{r}z^{-r-\frac{3}{2}}\in{\rm End}(V_{\mathfrak{g}}(c_{1},c_{2}))[[z,z^{-1}]].

\mathfrak{g}
are equivalent to the following operator product expansions (OPEs).

\displaystyle L(z_{1})L(z_{2})

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{\frac{c_{1}}{2}}{(z_{1}-z_{2})^{4}}+\frac{2L(z_{2})}{(z_{1}-z_{2})^{2}}+\frac{L^{\prime}(z_{2})}{z_{1}-z_{2}},

\displaystyle{L(z_{1})M(z_{2})}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{\frac{c_{2}}{2}}{(z_{1}-z_{2})^{4}}+\frac{2M(z_{2})}{(z_{1}-z_{2})^{2}}+\frac{M^{\prime}(z_{2})}{z_{1}-z_{2}},

\displaystyle{L(z_{1})Q(z_{2})}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{\frac{3}{2}Q(z_{2})}{(z_{1}-z_{2})^{2}}+\frac{Q^{\prime}(z_{2})}{z_{1}-z_{2}},

\displaystyle{Q(z_{1})Q(z_{2})}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{\frac{2c_{2}}{3}}{(z_{1}-z_{2})^{3}}+\frac{2M(z_{2})}{z_{1}-z_{2}}.

\{L(z),M(z),Q(z)\}
is a set of mutually local homogeneous vertex operators on every smooth module over the N=1 BMS superalgebra

Proof.

The following operator product expansions (OPEs) can be verified directly.

Corollary 5.9
.

The Fock-Whittaker
\mathfrak{g}
-module
\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\phi,\rho)

\phi(b_{1})\neq 0

Proof.

\displaystyle L_{1}w_{\phi}

\mathfrak{g}

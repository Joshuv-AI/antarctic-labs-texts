Comparison theorem for infinite-dimensional linear impulsive systems*
Vladyslav Bivziuk, Sergey Dashkovskiy ✉, Vitalii Slynko

Abstract
We consider a linear impulsive system in an infinite-dimensional Banach space. It is assumed that the moments of impulsive action satisfy the averaged dwell-time condition and the linear operator on the right side of the differential equation generates an analytic semigroup in the state space. Using commutator identities, we prove a comparison theorem that reduces the problem of asymptotic stability of the original system to the study of a simpler system with constant dwell-times. An illustrative example of a linear impulsive system of parabolic type in which the continuous and discrete dynamics are both unstable is given.

Introduction

The study of the stability of hybrid systems
[
1

plays an important role in control theory. The class of hybrid systems usually includes impulsive systems
[

,
3
]

[

. Lie algebraic methods for stability investigations of finite-dimensional switching systems were previously used in
[
5

,
8

[

. The ISS property and stability of coupled nonlinear impulsive infinite-dimensional input systems were also studied in
[
12
,
13
,
14
,
15

.

The aim of this paper is to extend the results of
[
9
]
to a wider class of linear impulsive systems which includes partial differential equations for which the influence of impulsive disturbances is not well understood. The main contribution of this article is the substantiation of the comparison principle which reduces the problem of the stability of a linear impulsive system for which the sequence of moments of impulsive action satisfies the averaged dwell-time (ADT) condition to the study of the stability of a linear impulsive system with constant dwell-time. This problem is much simpler and can be solved on the basis of the Lyapunov function method from the class of piecewise time-differentiable functions.

In order to derive the main result we apply the Hadamard’s commutator formula from
[
16
]
, which is extended here to the case of analytic semi-groups.

The work consists of six sections. In the second section, we state the problem; in the third section, we prove an auxiliary result which extends the Hadamar’s formula for some classes of unbounded operators. In the fourth section, the main result is proved, and in the fifth section, some examples are given. The sixth section contains conclusions.

II
Problem statement

To state the problem we use the following notation.
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
denotes non-negative integers. Let
X
be a Banach space, by
L(X)
we denote the set of linear bonded operators from
X
to
X
. By
B_{r}(0)
we denote the ball of radius

around the origin. The commutator of

is defined by
[D,C]:=DC-CD
. For symmetric square matrices
\mathbb{P},\mathbb{Q}
we write
\mathbb{P}\prec\mathbb{Q}

\mathbb{Q}-\mathbb{P}

We consider the linear impulsive system

\begin{gathered}\dot{x}(t)=Ax(t),\quad t\neq\tau_{k},\quad x(\tau_{0}^{+})=x_{0}\in\mathrm{D}(A),\\
x(t^{+})=Bx(t),\quad t=\tau_{k},\end{gathered}

(1)

where
x\in X
is a state vector,
A
is a closed densely defined linear operator with domain

\mathrm{D}(A)
that generates an analytic semigroup
(T_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}\subset L(X)
in the space
X
.
Assume that
B
is a closed linear operator from
\mathrm{D}(B)
to
X
. Since we are considering classical solutions of (
1
), we assume that
B\mathrm{D}(A)\subseteq\mathrm{D}(A)
.
Here,
\{\tau_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
is a sequence of moments of impulsive action which is assumed to be increasing and having a single accumulation point at infinity. For this sequence, we assume that the ADT condition is satisfied in the following form: there are constants
\theta>0

\chi_{\max}\in[0,\theta)

\begin{gathered}|\tau_{k}-\tau_{0}-k\theta|\leq\chi_{\max}.\end{gathered}

(2)

We recall the well-known fact that a closed densely defined linear operator

is a generator of an analytic semigroup if and only if it is sectorial in the sense of the following definition.
Let
R_{A}(\lambda)
be the resolvent of the operator
A
and
\rho(A)

Definition 1

(
[
17
]
)
.

A closed linear operator
A
with
\overline{\mathrm{D}(A)}=X

a\in\mathbb{R}
and
\phi\in(\frac{\pi}{2},\pi)
it holds that

\begin{gathered}\Sigma_{a,\phi}:=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{a\}\,|\,|\arg(\lambda-a)|<\phi\}\subset\rho(A),\end{gathered}

and there is a positive constant
K
such that for all
\lambda\in\Sigma_{a,\phi},

the following inequality holds

\begin{gathered}\|R_{A}(\lambda)\|_{L(X)}\leq\frac{K}{|\lambda-a|}.\end{gathered}

(3)

III
Commutators

It is known that linear bounded operators
D,C\in L(X)
satisfy the identity (Hadamard’s formula from
[
16
]
)

\begin{gathered}De^{tC}=e^{tC}\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{m}}{m!}\{D,C^{m}\}.\end{gathered}

(4)

Here,
\{D,C^{m}\}
,
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
is a sequence of nested commutators defined recurrently

\begin{gathered}\{D,C^{0}\}:=D,\quad\{D,C^{m+1}\}:=[\{D,C^{m}\},C],\quad m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}.\end{gathered}

We define an extension of the operator
\{B,A^{m}\}
,
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
for the case of unbounded operators
A
and
B
inductively. Let
\{B,A^{0}\}:=A
. Let a linear operator
\{B,A^{m}\}
with domain
\mathrm{D}(\{B,A^{m}\})
be already defined for some
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. We denote

\begin{gathered}\widehat{\mathrm{D}}:=\{x\in X\,\,|\,Ax\in\mathrm{D}(\{B,A^{m}\}),\quad\{B,A^{m}\}x\in\mathrm{D}(A)\,\}.\end{gathered}

Let
\widehat{\mathrm{D}}\supseteq\mathrm{D}(A)
and a linear operator with domain
\widehat{\mathrm{D}}
acting by the rule

\widehat{\mathrm{D}}\ni x\mapsto\{B,A^{m}\}Ax-A\{B,A^{m}\}x.

be closable and its closure is denoted by
\{B,A^{m+1}\}
.

We note that by definition
\mathrm{D}(\{B,A^{m}\})\supseteq\mathrm{D}(A)
.

Lemma 1
.

Assume that ,
the operators
\{B,A^{m}\}
are defined for all
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, satify the condition
\{B,A^{m}\}\mathrm{D}(A)\subseteq\mathrm{D}(A)
and

\|\{B,A^{m}\}R_{A}(\lambda)\|_{L(X)}\leq\frac{K_{1}\eta^{m}}{|\lambda-a|}

(5)

for some constants
\eta>0
,
K_{1}>0
and all
\lambda\in\Sigma_{a,\phi}
.

Then for all
x\in\mathrm{D}(A)
,
the following equality holds

BT_{t}x=T_{t}\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{m}}{m!}\{B,A^{m}\}x.

(6)

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we can assume that
a=0
.
First of all, using the method of mathematical induction, we show that for any
N\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
the identity

\begin{gathered}BT_{t}x=T_{t}\sum\limits_{m=0}^{N}\frac{t^{m}}{m!}\{B,A^{m}\}x\\
+\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{\Gamma}e^{\lambda t}R_{A}^{N+1}(\lambda)\{B,A^{N+1}\}R_{A}(\lambda)x\,d\lambda.\end{gathered}

(7)

holds for all
x\in\mathrm{D}(A)
. Here and further we denote,

\int\limits_{\Gamma}F(\lambda)x\,d\lambda:=\lim\limits_{R\to+\infty}\int\limits_{\Gamma_{R}}F(\lambda)x\,d\lambda,

where
F\,:\,\rho(A)\to L(X)
,
\Gamma=\Gamma(r,\psi)=\Gamma_{1}\cup\Gamma_{2}\cup\Gamma_{3}
,

\Gamma_{R}=\Gamma\cap\overline{B_{R}(0)}
,
\psi\in(\frac{\pi}{2},\phi)
,
r>0
,
R>0
,

\begin{gathered}\Gamma_{1}=\Gamma_{1}(r,\psi)=\{\lambda=-se^{i\psi}\,|\,s\in(-\infty,-r]\},\\
\Gamma_{2}=\Gamma_{2}(r,\psi)=\{\lambda=re^{i\alpha}\,|\,\alpha\in[-\psi,\psi]\},\\
\Gamma_{3}=\Gamma_{3}(r,\psi)=\{\lambda=se^{i\psi}\,|\,s\in(r,\infty)\},\\
\end{gathered}

Indeed, using the Dunford–Taylor formula
[
17
]
, we obtain

T_{t}x=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{\Gamma}e^{\lambda t}R_{A}(\lambda)x\,d\lambda.

(8)

Since
T_{t}x\in\mathrm{D}(A)\subset\mathrm{D}(B)
and the operator
B
is closed, we have

BT_{t}x=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{\Gamma}e^{\lambda t}BR_{A}(\lambda)x\,d\lambda.

Taking into account the assumption
B\mathrm{D}(A)\subseteq\mathrm{D}(A)
, we get for all
x\in\mathrm{D}(A)
that

\begin{gathered}BR_{A}(\lambda)x-R_{A}(\lambda)Bx=R_{A}(\lambda)(\lambda\operatorname{id\,}-A)BR_{A}(\lambda)x\\
-R_{A}(\lambda)B(\lambda\operatorname{id\,}-A)R_{A}(\lambda)x=R_{A}(\lambda)((\lambda\operatorname{id\,}-A)B\\
-B(\lambda\operatorname{id\,}-A))R_{A}(\lambda)x=R_{A}(\lambda)[B,A]R_{A}(\lambda)x.\end{gathered}

Substituting the expression for
BR_{A}(\lambda)x
into (
8
), we get

\begin{gathered}BT_{t}x=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{\Gamma}e^{\lambda t}R_{A}(\lambda)Bx\,d\lambda\\
+\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{\Gamma}e^{\lambda t}R_{A}(\lambda)[B,A]R_{A}(\lambda)x\,d\lambda\\
=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{\Gamma}e^{\lambda t}R_{A}(\lambda)\,d\lambda Bx\\
+\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{\Gamma}e^{\lambda t}R_{A}(\lambda)[B,A]R_{A}(\lambda)x\,d\lambda\\
=T_{t}Bx+\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{\Gamma}e^{\lambda t}R_{A}(\lambda)[B,A]R_{A}(\lambda)x\,d\lambda.\end{gathered}

Therefore, the formula (
7
) is proven for
N=0
.

Assume that the formula (
7
) is valid for
N=p
.
Then, given that
\mathrm{D}(\{B,A^{p+1}\})\supseteq\mathrm{D}(A)
and
\{B,A^{p+1}\}\mathrm{D}(A)\subseteq\mathrm{D}(A)
for
x\in\mathrm{D}(A)
, we get that

\begin{gathered}\{B,A^{p+1}\}R_{A}(\lambda)x-R_{A}(\lambda)\{B,A^{p+1}\}x\\
=R_{A}(\lambda)(\lambda\operatorname{id\,}-A)\{B,A^{p+1}\}R_{A}(\lambda)x\\
-R_{A}(\lambda)\{B,A^{p+1}\}(\lambda\operatorname{id\,}-A)R_{A}(\lambda)x\\
=R_{A}(\lambda)\{B,A^{p+2}\}R_{A}(\lambda)x.\end{gathered}

Expressing
\{B,A^{p+1}\}R_{A}(\lambda)x
from here and substituting into the formula (
7
) for
N=p
, we get

\begin{gathered}BT_{t}x=T_{t}\sum\limits_{m=0}^{p}\frac{t^{m}}{m!}\{B,A^{m}\}x\\
+\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{\Gamma}e^{\lambda t}R_{A}^{p+1}(\lambda)\{B,A^{p+1}\}R_{A}(\lambda)x\,d\lambda\\
=T_{t}\sum\limits_{m=0}^{p}\frac{t^{m}}{m!}\{B,A^{m}\}x+\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{\Gamma}e^{\lambda t}R_{A}^{p+2}(\lambda)\{B,A^{p+1}\}x\,d\lambda\\
+\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{\Gamma}e^{\lambda t}R_{A}^{p+2}(\lambda)\{B,A^{p+2}\}R_{A}(\lambda)x\,d\lambda\\
=T_{t}\sum\limits_{m=0}^{p}\frac{t^{m}}{m!}\{B,A^{m}\}x+\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{\Gamma}e^{\lambda t}R_{A}^{p+2}(\lambda)\,d\lambda\{B,A^{p+1}\}x\\
+\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{\Gamma}e^{\lambda t}R_{A}^{p+2}(\lambda)\{B,A^{p+2}\}R_{A}(\lambda)x\,d\lambda.\end{gathered}

(9)

Using the identity (5.22) from
[
17
]
:

\begin{gathered}R_{A}^{p+2}(\lambda)=\frac{(-1)^{p+1}}{(p+1)!}\frac{d^{p+1}}{d\lambda^{p+1}}R_{A}(\lambda)\end{gathered}

and applying integration by parts
p+1
times (taking into account that
|e^{\lambda t}|\to 0
as
\operatorname{Re}\lambda\to-\infty
and (
3
)), we obtain

\begin{gathered}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{\Gamma}e^{\lambda t}R_{A}^{p+2}(\lambda)\,d\lambda\\
=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\frac{(-1)^{p+1}}{(p+1)!}\int\limits_{\Gamma}e^{\lambda t}\frac{d^{p+1}}{d\lambda^{p+1}}R_{A}(\lambda)\,d\lambda\\
=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\frac{(-1)^{p+1}}{(p+1)!}(-1)^{p+1}\int\limits_{\Gamma}\frac{d^{p+1}}{d\lambda^{p+1}}(e^{\lambda t})R_{A}(\lambda)\,d\lambda\\
=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\frac{t^{p+1}}{(p+1)!}\int\limits_{\Gamma}e^{\lambda t}R_{A}(\lambda)\,d\lambda=\frac{t^{p+1}}{(p+1)!}T_{t}.\end{gathered}

which completes the proof of (
7
).

We now show that (
7
) implies (
6
).
To do this, we estimate the integral in (
7
) taking (
3
) and (
5
) into account:

\begin{gathered}\Big\|\int\limits_{\Gamma_{R}}e^{\lambda t}R_{A}^{N+1}(\lambda)\{B,A^{N+1}\}R_{A}(\lambda)\,d\lambda\Big\|\\
\leq\int\limits_{r}^{R}\frac{K_{1}K^{N+1}\eta^{N+1}\exp(ts\operatorname{Re}e^{-i\psi})}{|se^{-i\psi}|^{N+2}}|d(se^{-i\psi})|\\
+\int\limits_{-\psi}^{\psi}\frac{K_{1}K^{N+1}\eta^{N+1}\exp(tr\operatorname{Re}e^{i\alpha})}{|re^{i\alpha}|^{N+2}}|d(re^{i\alpha})|\\
+\int\limits_{r}^{R}\frac{K_{1}K^{N+1}\eta^{N+1}\exp(ts\operatorname{Re}e^{i\psi})}{|se^{i\psi}|^{N+2}}|d(se^{i\psi})|\\
\leq 2\int\limits_{r}^{\infty}\frac{K_{1}K^{N+1}\eta^{N+1}\exp(ts\cos(\psi))}{s^{N+1}}\,ds\\
+\int\limits_{-\psi}^{\psi}\frac{K_{1}K^{N+1}\eta^{N+1}\exp(tr\cos\alpha)}{r^{N+1}}d\alpha\\
=K_{1}K^{N+1}\eta^{N+1}\Big(2\int\limits_{r}^{\infty}\frac{\exp(ts\cos(\psi))}{s^{N+1}}\,ds\\
+\int\limits_{-\psi}^{\psi}\frac{\exp(tr\cos\alpha)}{r^{N+1}}d\alpha\Big)\end{gathered}

Applying the change of variables
y=-st\cos(\psi)
, we find

\begin{gathered}\int\limits_{r}^{\infty}\frac{\exp(ts\cos(\psi))}{s^{N+1}}\,ds\\
=(t|\cos(\psi)|)^{N}\int\limits_{rt|\cos(\psi)|}^{\infty}\frac{\exp(-y)}{y^{N+1}}\,dy\\
\leq(t|\cos(\psi)|)^{N}\frac{e^{-rt|\cos(\psi)|}}{N}(rt|\cos(\psi)|)^{-N}\\
=\frac{r^{-N}e^{-rt|\cos(\psi)|}}{N}.\end{gathered}

Using also the estimate

\begin{gathered}\int\limits_{-\psi}^{\psi}\frac{\exp(tr\cos\alpha)}{r^{N+1}}d\alpha\leq\frac{2\psi e^{rt}}{r^{N+1}},\end{gathered}

we get

\begin{gathered}\Big\|\int\limits_{\Gamma_{R}}e^{\lambda t}R_{A}^{N+1}(\lambda)\{B,A^{N+1}\}R_{A}(\lambda)\,d\lambda\Big\|\\
\leq K_{1}K\eta(K\eta r^{-1})^{N}\Big(2\frac{e^{-rt|\cos(\psi)|}}{N}+\frac{2\psi e^{rt}}{r}\Big)\end{gathered}

Let
r>K\eta
. Then,

\begin{gathered}\Big\|\int\limits_{\Gamma_{R}}e^{\lambda t}R_{A}^{N+1}(\lambda)\{B,A^{N+1}\}R_{A}(\lambda)\,d\lambda\Big\|\to 0\end{gathered}

as
N\to\infty
uniformly in
R\geq R_{0}
, where
R_{0}
is a sufficiently large positive number. Therefore, in the formula (
7
), one can pass to the limit
N\to\infty
and obtain the formula (
6
). The lemma is proven.
∎

IV
Main result

Along with the original impulsive system (
1
), consider the following impulsive system with constant dwell-time (comparison system)

\begin{gathered}\dot{z}(t)=Az(t),\quad t\neq k\theta,\quad z(0^{+})=z_{0}\in\mathrm{D}(A),\\
z(t^{+})=Bz(t)+\sum\limits_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{(\chi_{\max}+\chi_{k+1})^{m}}{m!}\{B,A^{m}\}z(t),\,t=k\theta,\end{gathered}

(10)

where
z\in D(A)
and
\chi_{k}:=\tau_{k}-\tau_{0}-k\theta\leq\chi_{\max}
, see (
2
).

The main theorem reduces the problem of stability of the initial impulsive system (
1
) to the study of the comparison system (
10
).

Theorem 1
.

Let the linear operator
A
be sectorial, the linear operator
B
be closed, and for all
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
the linear operators
\{B,A^{m}\}
be defined as above and such that
\{B,A^{m}\}\mathrm{D}(A)\subseteq\mathrm{D}(A)
. Assume that inequality (
5
) holds and
\{B,A^{m}\}T_{\theta-\chi_{\max}}\in L(X)
for all
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
as well as

\begin{gathered}2e\cdot\chi_{\max}\lim\sup\limits_{m\to\infty}\frac{\|\{B,A^{m}\}T_{\theta-\chi_{\max}}\|^{1/m}}{m}<1.\end{gathered}

(11)

Then, the asymptotic stability of the linear impulsive system (
10
) implies the asymptotic stability of the linear impulsive system (
1
).

Proof.

Let
x_{0}\in\mathrm{D}(A)
. Then,
x(\tau_{1})=T_{\tau_{1}-\tau_{0}}x_{0}
. Therefore, applying the Lemma 1 and the semigroup property, we obtain

\begin{gathered}x(\tau_{1}^{+})=BT_{\tau_{1}-\tau_{0}}x_{0}=BT_{\tau_{1}-\tau_{0}-\theta+\chi_{\max}}T_{\theta-\chi_{\max}}x_{0}\\
=T_{\tau_{1}-\tau_{0}-\theta+\chi_{\max}}\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\chi_{1}+\chi_{\max})^{m}}{m!}\{B,A^{m}\}T_{\theta-\chi_{\max}}x_{0}.\end{gathered}

Let

z_{0}:=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\chi_{1}+\chi_{\max})^{m}}{m!}\{B,A^{m}\}T_{\theta-\chi_{\max}}x_{0}.

Then, using the semigroup property, we have

\begin{gathered}x(\tau_{2})=T_{\tau_{2}-\tau_{1}}x(\tau_{1}^{+})=T_{\tau_{2}-\tau_{1}}T_{\tau_{1}-\tau_{0}-\theta+\chi_{\max}}z_{0}\\
=T_{\chi_{2}+\chi_{\max}}T_{\theta}z_{0}.\end{gathered}

Applying the Lemma 1 again, we get

\begin{gathered}x(\tau_{2}^{+})=BT_{\chi_{2}+\chi_{\max}}T_{\theta}z_{0}\\
=T_{\chi_{2}+\chi_{\max}}\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\chi_{2}+\chi_{\max})^{m}}{m!}\{B,A^{m}\}T_{\theta}z_{0}.\end{gathered}

We denote by
\widehat{z}(t)
the solution to the Cauchy problem for the linear impulsive system (
10
) with the initial condition
\widehat{z}(0)=z_{0}
. Then,
x(\tau_{2}^{+})=T_{\chi_{2}+\chi_{\max}}\widehat{z}(\theta^{+})
.
Using the method of mathematical induction, we prove that

\begin{gathered}x(\tau_{k}^{+})=T_{\chi_{k}+\chi_{\max}}\widehat{z}((k-1)\theta^{+}),\quad k\geq 2.\end{gathered}

(12)

For
k=2
, this has already been proven. Let (
12
) be valid for
k=p
. Then,

\begin{gathered}x(\tau_{p+1}^{+})=Bx(\tau_{p+1})=BT_{\tau_{p+1}-\tau_{p}}x(\tau_{p}^{+})\\
=BT_{\tau_{p+1}-\tau_{p}}T_{\chi_{p}+\chi_{\max}}\widehat{z}((p-1)\theta^{+})\\
=BT_{\chi_{p+1}+\chi_{\max}}T_{\theta}\widehat{z}((p-1)\theta^{+})\\
=T_{\chi_{p+1}{+}\chi_{\max}}\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\chi_{p+1}{+}\chi_{\max})^{m}}{m!}\{B,A^{m}\}T_{\theta}\widehat{z}((p-1)\theta^{+})\\
=T_{\chi_{p+1}+\chi_{\max}}\widehat{z}(p\theta^{+}).\end{gathered}

Therefore, (
12
) is valid for
k=p+1
.

From the definition of
z_{0}
by the triangle inequality for norms we estimate

\begin{gathered}\|z_{0}\|\leq\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(2\chi_{\max})^{m}}{m!}\|\{B,A^{m}\}T_{\theta-\chi_{\max}}\|\|x_{0}\|=:\mu\|x_{0}\|.\end{gathered}

The convergence of the series on the right-hand side follows from the Cauchy criterion, the Stirling formula, and the condition (
11
).
For the dwell-time, the estimate
\tau_{k+1}-\tau_{k}\leq\theta+2\chi_{\max}
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
follows from (
2
).
We denote

M:=\sup\{\|T_{t}\|\,\,|\,\,t\in[0,\theta+2\chi_{\max}]\}.

Then, (
12
) implies the estimate

\begin{gathered}\|x(t)\|=\|T_{t-\tau_{k}}x(\tau_{k}^{+})\|\leq\|T_{t-\tau_{k}}\|\|x(\tau_{k}^{+})\|\\
\leq M\|T_{\chi_{k}+\chi_{\max}}\widehat{z}((k-1)\theta^{+})\|\\
\leq M\|T_{\chi_{k}+\chi_{\max}}\|\cdot\|\widehat{z}((k-1)\theta^{+})\|\end{gathered}

for
t\in(\tau_{k},\tau_{k+1}]
. Since
0\leq\chi_{k}+\chi_{\max}\leq 2\chi_{\max}
, we have

\|T_{\chi_{k}+\chi_{\max}}\|\leq M
, and therefore,

\|x(t)\|\leq M^{2}\|\widehat{z}((k-1)\theta^{+})\|.

Let
\varepsilon>0
. Then, it follows from the stability of the linear impulse system (
10
) that for some
\delta=\delta(\varepsilon)
for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
the inequality
\|z(t)\|<\varepsilon(\mu M^{2})^{-1}
holds.
Then,
\|x(t)\|<\varepsilon
is satisfied for all
t\geq 0
which proves the stability of the original linear impulsive system (
1
).

The asymptotic stability of the linear impulsive system (
10
) implies that for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
k_{0}(\varepsilon)\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
such that

\|\widehat{z}(k\theta^{+})\|<\varepsilon M^{-2},\quad k\geq k_{0}(\varepsilon).

Then, for
t>\tau_{k+1}
, the inequality
\|x(t)\|<\varepsilon
is satisfied. The theorem is proven.
∎

V
Example

For
\ell>0
and
\mu>0
we consider the linear impulsive system of parabolic equations

\begin{gathered}\partial_{t}x(y,t)=\mu^{2}\partial^{2}_{yy}x(y,t)+\mathbb{A}x(y,t),\quad t\neq\tau_{k},\\
x(y,t^{+})=\mathbb{B}x(y,t),\quad t=\tau_{k}\end{gathered}

(14)

in the state space
X=L^{2}((0,\ell);\mathbb{R}^{n})
, where

x\in C([0,\infty),L^{2}((0,\ell);\mathbb{R}^{n}))\cap C^{1}((0,\infty),L^{2}((0,\ell);\mathbb{R}^{n}))
,

y\in[0,\ell]
,
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
,

\mathbb{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
and
\mathbb{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
. The initial and boundary conditions are given by

\begin{gathered}x(y,0)=x_{0}(y),\quad x(0,t)=x(\ell,t)=0,\end{gathered}

(15)

where
x_{0}\in H^{2}(0,\ell)\cap H_{0}^{1}(0,\ell)
. Let
\mathrm{D}(A)=H^{2}(0,\ell)\cap H_{0}^{1}(0,\ell)
,
\mathrm{D}(B)=X

and

\begin{gathered}(Ax)(y):=\mu^{2}\partial^{2}_{yy}x(y)+\mathbb{A}x(y),\quad(Bx)(y)=\mathbb{B}x(y).\end{gathered}

It follows from Theorem 1 that for the asymptotic stability of the linear impulsive system (
14
)-(
15
), it suffices to check the asymptotic stability of the comparison system

\begin{gathered}\partial_{t}z(y,t)=\mu^{2}\partial^{2}_{yy}z(y,t)+\mathbb{A}z(y,t),\quad t\neq k\theta,\\
z(y,t^{+})=\mathbb{B}z(y,t)+\mathbb{G}_{k}z(y,t),\quad t=k\theta,\end{gathered}

(16)

where
z\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+},X)
,
y\in[0,\ell]
,
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
,

\mathbb{G}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
,
\|\mathbb{G}_{k}\|\leq\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{(2\chi_{\max})^{m}}{m!}\|\{\mathbb{B},\mathbb{A}^{m}\}\|:=\omega
. The initial and boundary conditions are given by

\begin{gathered}z(y,0)=z_{0}(y),\quad z(0,t)=z(\ell,t)=0,\end{gathered}

(17)

where
z_{0}\in H^{2}(0,\ell)\cap H_{0}^{1}(0,\ell)
.
To study the stability of the comparison system (
16
)—(
17
), we define a candidate Lyapunov function by

\begin{gathered}V(t,z):=\int\limits_{0}^{\ell}z^{\operatorname{T\,}}(y)\mathbb{P}(t)z(y)\,dy.\end{gathered}

(18)

Here,
\mathbb{P}\,:\,\,\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
is a piece-wise continuous and differentiable on the set
\mathbb{R}_{+}\setminus\theta\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and
\theta
-periodic map
with values

P(t)
in the set of symmetric positive-definite matrices.

The total derivative of this function along the semiflow generated by (
16
)—(
17
) is

\begin{gathered}\dot{V}(t,z):=2\mu^{2}\int\limits_{0}^{\ell}z^{\operatorname{T\,}}(y)\mathbb{P}(t)\partial_{yy}^{2}z(y)\,dy\\
+\int\limits_{0}^{\ell}z^{\operatorname{T\,}}(y)(\dot{\mathbb{P}}(t)+\mathbb{A}^{\operatorname{T\,}}\mathbb{P}(t)+\mathbb{P}(t)\mathbb{A})z(y)\,dy.\end{gathered}

(19)

Applying integration by parts and the Friedrich’s inequality, we obtain

\begin{gathered}\int\limits_{0}^{\ell}z^{\operatorname{T\,}}(y)\mathbb{P}(t)\partial_{yy}^{2}z(y)\,dy=-\int\limits_{0}^{\ell}(\partial_{y}z(y))^{\operatorname{T\,}}\mathbb{P}(t)\partial_{y}z(y)\,dy\\
=-\int\limits_{0}^{\ell}\|\mathbb{P}^{1/2}(t)\partial_{y}z(y)\|^{2}\,dy\leq-\frac{\pi^{2}}{\ell^{2}}\int\limits_{0}^{\ell}\|\mathbb{P}^{1/2}(t)z(y)\|^{2}\,dy\\
=-\frac{\pi^{2}}{\ell^{2}}\int\limits_{0}^{\ell}z^{\operatorname{T\,}}(y)\mathbb{P}(t)z(y)\,dy\end{gathered}

Therefore,

\begin{gathered}\dot{V}(t,z)\leq\int\limits_{0}^{\ell}z^{\operatorname{T\,}}(y)(\dot{\mathbb{P}}(t)+(\mathbb{A}-\frac{\pi^{2}\mu^{2}}{\ell^{2}}\operatorname{id\,})^{\operatorname{T\,}}\mathbb{P}(t)\\
+\mathbb{P}(t)(\mathbb{A}-\frac{\pi^{2}\mu^{2}}{\ell^{2}}\operatorname{id\,}))z(y)\,dy.\end{gathered}

We choose
\mathbb{P}(t)
so that for
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\setminus\theta\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, the equality

\begin{gathered}\dot{\mathbb{P}}(t)+(\mathbb{A}-\frac{\pi^{2}\mu^{2}}{\ell^{2}}\operatorname{id\,})^{\operatorname{T\,}}\mathbb{P}(t)+\mathbb{P}(t)(\mathbb{A}-\frac{\pi^{2}\mu^{2}}{\ell^{2}}\operatorname{id\,})=0\end{gathered}

is satisfied. Then,

\mathbb{P}(t)=e^{\frac{2\pi^{2}\mu^{2}(t-k\theta)}{\ell^{2}}}e^{-\mathbb{A}^{\operatorname{T\,}}(t-k\theta)}\mathbb{P}_{0}e^{-\mathbb{A}(t-k\theta)}

for
t\in(k\theta,(k+1)\theta]
.
Assume that there exists a positive-definite matrix
\mathbb{P}_{0}
that satisfies the matrix inequality

\begin{gathered}e^{\frac{-2\pi^{2}\mu^{2}\theta}{\ell^{2}}}\Phi^{\operatorname{T\,}}\mathbb{P}_{0}\Phi+e^{\frac{-2\pi^{2}\mu^{2}\theta}{\ell^{2}}}(2\omega\|\mathbb{B}\mathbb{P}_{0}\|\\
+\omega^{2}\|\mathbb{P}_{0}\|)e^{\mathbb{A}^{\operatorname{T\,}}\theta}e^{\mathbb{A}\theta}\prec\mathbb{P}_{0},\end{gathered}

(20)

where
\Phi=\mathbb{B}e^{\mathbb{A}\theta}
.

At the moments of jumps
t=(k+1)\theta
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,

\begin{gathered}V((k+1)\theta^{+},z^{+})=\int\limits_{0}^{\ell}(\mathbb{B}z(y))^{\operatorname{T\,}}\mathbb{P}_{0}(\mathbb{B}z)(y)\,dy\\
+2\int\limits_{0}^{\ell}(\mathbb{B}z(y))^{\operatorname{T\,}}\mathbb{P}_{0}(\mathbb{G}_{k}z)(y)\,dy\\
+\int\limits_{0}^{\ell}(\mathbb{G}_{k}z(y))^{\operatorname{T\,}}\mathbb{P}_{0}(\mathbb{G}_{k}z)(y)\,dy\\
\leq\int\limits_{0}^{\ell}(z(y))^{\operatorname{T\,}}\mathbb{B}^{\operatorname{T\,}}\mathbb{P}_{0}\mathbb{B}z(y)\,dy\\
+2\int\limits_{0}^{\ell}\|\mathbb{B}^{\operatorname{T\,}}\mathbb{P}_{0}\|\omega(z(y))^{\operatorname{T\,}}z(y)\,dy\\
+\int\limits_{0}^{\ell}\omega^{2}\|\mathbb{P}_{0}\|(z(y))^{\operatorname{T\,}}z(y)\,dy\\
=\int\limits_{0}^{\ell}(z(y))^{\operatorname{T\,}}(\mathbb{B}^{\operatorname{T\,}}\mathbb{P}_{0}\mathbb{B}+(2\omega\|\mathbb{B}^{\operatorname{T\,}}\mathbb{P}_{0}\|+\omega^{2}\|\mathbb{P}_{0}\|)\operatorname{id\,})z(y)\,dy\\
<\int\limits_{0}^{\ell}(z(y))^{\operatorname{T\,}}\mathbb{P}(\theta)z(y)\,dy=V((k+1)\theta,z).\end{gathered}

Therefore,
V(t,z)
is a Lyapunov function for the linear impulsive system (
16
)-(
17
), hence this system is asymptotically stable. From Theorem 1 we obtain sufficient conditions for the asymptotic stability of the original system (
14
)-(
15
):

Proposition 1
.

Let the sequence of moments of impulsive action
\{\tau_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
satisfy the ADT condition (
2
),
\Phi=\mathbb{B}e^{\mathbb{A}\theta}
,
\omega=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{(2\chi_{\max})^{m}}{m!}\|\{\mathbb{B},\mathbb{A}^{m}\}\|

r_{\sigma}(\Phi)<e^{\pi^{2}\mu^{2}\theta/\ell^{2}}
and for some positive-definite matrix
\mathbb{P}_{0}

) holds.
Then system (

)–(

) is asymptotically stable.

We consider a numerical example setting
\ell=\pi
,
\mu=1
,

,
\chi_{\max}=0.1

\begin{gathered}\mathbb{A}=\begin{pmatrix}1.2&0.1\\

VI
Conclusion

The main theorem allows one to study wide classes of infinite-dimensional systems, for example, systems of parabolic partial differential equations, integro-differential partial differential equations and others. For the comparison system, the problem of construction of a Lyapunov function is much simpler than for the original system since dwell-times are constant. We also note that the obtained stability conditions have a wide range of applicability since they are applicable when the continuous and discrete dynamics are both unstable.
It is of interest to extend these results to the case when the operator
A
generates a
C_{0}
– semigroup as well as to relax the assumptions about the operators
A
,
\,B
and the sequence of commutators
\{B,A^{m}\}
that we have applied in Theorem 1.

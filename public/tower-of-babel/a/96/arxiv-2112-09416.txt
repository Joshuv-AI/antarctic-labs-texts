Abstract
. It is well known that the completeness theorem for L ω 1 ​ ω \mathrm{L}_{\omega_{1}\omega} fails with respect to Tarski semantics. Mansfield showed that it holds for L ∞ ​ ∞ \mathrm{L}_{\infty\infty} if one replaces Tarski semantics with Boolean valued semantics. We use forcing to improve his result in order to obtain a stronger form of Boolean completeness (but only for L ∞ ​ ω \mathrm{L}_{\infty\omega} ). Leveraging on our completeness result, we establish the Craig interpolation property and a strong version of the omitting types theorem for L ∞ ​ ω \mathrm{L}_{\infty\omega} with respect to Boolean valued semantics. We also show that a weak version of these results holds for L ∞ ​ ∞ \mathrm{L}_{\infty\infty} (if one leverages instead on Mansfield’s completeness theorem). Furthermore we bring to light (or in some cases just revive) several connections between the infinitary logic L ∞ ​ ω \mathrm{L}_{\infty\omega} and the forcing method in set theory.

The infinitary logics
\mathrm{L}_{\kappa\lambda}

Remark 2.3

Remark 2.15
.

It needs to be noted that with this deduction system the completeness theorem for
\mathrm{L}_{\infty\infty}
(even for
\mathrm{L}_{\omega_{2}\omega}
) fails for the usual semantics given by Tarski structures.

Remark first that our proof system is forcing invariant: the existence of a proof for a certain sentence is described by a
\Sigma_{1}
statement in parameter the sequent to be proved; if the proof exists in
V
it exists in any further extension of
V
.

Consider now a set of
\kappa
constants
\{c_{\alpha}:\alpha<\kappa\}
for
\kappa>\omega
and the sentence

\psi:=\bigg(\bigwedge_{\omega\leq\alpha\neq\beta}c_{\alpha}\neq c_{\beta}\bigg)\Rightarrow\exists v\bigg(\bigwedge_{n<\omega}v\neq c_{n}\bigg).

The sentence
\psi
is valid in the usual Tarski semantics but it cannot be proved (in our deduction system or in any forcing invariant system) since the sentence is no longer valid when moving to
V[G]
for
G
a
V
-generic filter for
\Coll(\omega,\kappa)
.

Malitz
[
9
, Thm. 3.2.4]
showed also that the above formula is a counterexample to Craig’s interpolation property for Tarski semantics in
\mathrm{L}_{\infty\omega}
.

Our opinion is that a proof system should not depend on the model of set theory in which one is working, which is the case for the proof system we present here at least when restricted to
\mathrm{L}_{\infty\omega}
.
In contrast with our point of view, one finds a complete proof system for Tarski semantics on
\mathrm{L}_{\infty\infty}
in Malitz’s thesis
[
9
, Thm. 3.3.1]
, however this proof system (which by the way is due to Karp
[
5
, Ch. 11]
) is not forcing invariant e.g. a proof of some sequent in some model of set theory may not be anymore a proof of that same sequent in some forcing extension.

4.
Consistency properties for relational
\omega
-signatures

Consistency properties are partial approximations to the construction of a model of an infinitary theory.
In first order logic the main tool for constructing Tarski models of a theory is the compactness theorem. However, this technique is not suited for the infinitary logics
\mathrm{L}_{\kappa\lambda}
since it fails even at the simplest case
\mathrm{L}_{\omega_{1}\omega}
. Actually, a cardinal
\kappa
is (weakly) compact if and only if the (weak) compactness theorem holds for the logic
\mathrm{L}_{\kappa\kappa}
. Thus a new recipe for constructing models is needed. This is given by the notion of consistency property.
Our aim is to show that by means of consistency properties one gets a powerful tool to produce boolean valued models of infinitary logic.
We follow (generalizing it) the approach of Keisler’s book
[
6
]
to consistency properties for

\mathrm{L}_{\omega_{1}\omega}
.

First of all it is convenient to reduce the satisfaction problem to formulae where negations occur only in atomic formulae.
We use the abbreviation
\vec{v}
to denote a sequence of variables.
Similarly
\vec{c}
denotes a string of constants.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\phi
be a
\mathrm{L}_{\infty\infty}
-formula.
We define
\phi\neg
(moving a negation inside) by induction on the complexity of formulae:

•

If
\phi
is an atomic formula
\varphi
,
\phi\neg
is
\neg\varphi
.

•

If
\phi
is
\neg\varphi
,
\phi\neg
is
\varphi
.

•

If
\phi
is
\bigwedge\Phi
,
\phi\neg
is
\bigvee\{\neg\varphi:\varphi\in\Phi\}
.

•

If
\phi
is
\bigvee\Phi
,
\phi\neg
is
\bigwedge\{\neg\varphi:\varphi\in\Phi\}
.

•

If
\phi
is
\forall\vec{v}\varphi(\vec{v})
,
\phi\neg
is
\exists\vec{v}\neg\varphi(\vec{v})
.

•

If
\phi
is

,

It is easily checked that

Now let us give a few examples of consistency properties for

Any of the previous cases where the Tarski structures in

5.
Forcing with consistency properties

In this section we assume that
\mathrm{L}
denotes a set-sized
\omega
-relational signature,
\mathcal{C}
is a set of fresh constants, and
S\subset\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{L}(\mathcal{C})_{\infty\omega})
is a set-sized consistency property.

We start by noting the following:

Remark 5.1
.

If
S
is a consistency property, so is

\{s\subset\mathrm{L}(\mathcal{C})_{\infty\omega}:\exists s_{0}\in S\,s\subseteq s_{0}\}
.

Definition 5.2
.

Let
S
be a consistency property over
\mathrm{L}(\mathcal{C})_{\infty\omega}
for a set of constants
\mathcal{C}
and a relational
\omega
-signature
\mathrm{L}
.
The forcing notion
\mathbb{P}_{S}
is given by:

•

domain:
\{s\subset\mathrm{L}(\mathcal{C})_{\infty\omega}:\exists s_{0}\in S\,(s\subseteq s_{0})\}
;

•

order:
p\leq q
if and only if
q\subseteq p
.

Given a filter
F
on
\mathbb{P}_{S}
,
\Sigma_{F}=\bigcup F
.

The proof of the Model Existence Theorem for
\mathrm{L}_{\omega_{1}\omega}
as given in
[
6
]
, corresponds naturally to the construction for a given consistency property
S
of a suitable filter
G
on
\mathbb{P}_{S}
generic over countably many dense sets.
The clauses of a consistency property are naturally attached to dense sets a maximal filter
G
on
\mathbb{P}_{S}
needs to meet in order to produce a Tarski model of the formulae
\phi\in\bigcup G
.
For example, suppose
\bigvee\Phi\in s_{0}\in S
. Clause
(Ind.4)
together with Remark
5.1
states that the set
\{s\in S:\,\Phi\cap s\neq\emptyset\}
is dense below
s_{0}
. In Keisler’s case the elements of a consistency property are countable and each
\mathrm{L}(\mathcal{C})_{\omega_{1}\omega}
-formula has countably many subformulae. So, one can take an enumeration of all the dense sets at issue and diagonalize.
In the general case for
\mathrm{L}_{\infty\omega}
one deals with many more dense sets, hence a filter meeting all the relevant dense sets may not exists. However we can translate Keisler’s argument using forcing and produce a Boolean valued model for the associated consistency property.

For the rest of this section we work with consistency properties made up from finite sets of sentences. The reader familiar with Keisler’s book
[
6
]
will find this restriction natural.

We split our generalization of Keisler’s result in two pieces. The first piece shows how far one can go in proving the Model Existence Theorem assuming only the existence of a maximal filter on a consistency property
S
. The second one shows how genericity fills the missing gaps.

Fact 5.3
.

Let
S
be a consistency property for
\mathrm{L}(\mathcal{C})_{\infty\omega}
for a set of constants
\mathcal{C}
. Assume
S
consists only of finite sets of formulae.
Suppose
F\subseteq\mathbb{P}_{S}
is a filter. Then
[\Sigma_{F}]^{<\omega}=F
.

Proof.

The inclusion
F\subset[\Sigma_{F}]^{<\omega}
follows by definition of
\Sigma_{F}
. We now prove
[\Sigma_{F}]^{<\omega}\subseteq F
. Suppose
p=\left\{\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{n}\right\}\in[\Sigma_{F}]^{<\omega}
. Then there exist
s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}\in F
with each
\phi_{i}\in s_{i}
. Hence
p\subseteq\bigcup_{i\leq n}s_{i}
. Since
F
is a filter, we have
\bigcup_{i\leq n}s_{i}\in F\subseteq\mathbb{P}_{S}
. The set
p
is a condition in
\mathbb{P}_{S}
since
\mathbb{P}_{S}
is closed under subsets. Finally,
\bigcup_{i\leq n}s_{i}\leq p
and
\bigcup_{i\leq n}s_{i}\in F
imply
p\in F
.
∎

Definition 5.4
.

Given a relational
\omega
-signature
\mathrm{L}=\mathcal{R}\cup\mathcal{D}
, a set of fresh constants
\mathcal{C}
, and a consistency property
S
for
\mathrm{L}(\mathcal{C})_{\infty\omega}
, let
F
be
a maximal filter for
\mathbb{P}_{S}
.

\mathcal{A}_{F}=(A_{F},R_{F}:R\in\mathcal{R},d_{F}:d\in\mathcal{D})
is the following string of objects:

•

A_{F}
is the set of equivalence classes on
\mathcal{C}\cup\mathcal{D}
for the equivalence relation

c\cong_{F}d
if and only if
(c=d)\in\Sigma_{F}
,

•

for
R\in\mathcal{D}

n
-ary relation symbol and
c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}\in\mathcal{C}\cup\mathcal{D}
,
R_{F}([c_{1}]_{F},\dots,[c_{n}]_{F})
holds if and only if
R(c_{1},\dots,c_{n})\in\Sigma_{F}
,

•

d_{F}=[d]_{F}
for any

.

Consistency properties are so designed that
\mathcal{A}_{F}
is a Tarski structure for
\mathrm{L}(\mathcal{C})
:

Fact 5.5
.

Let
\mathrm{L}=\mathcal{R}\cup\mathcal{D}
be a relational
\omega
-signature,

\mathcal{C}
a fresh set of constants,

S
a consistency property for
\mathrm{L}(\mathcal{C})_{\infty\omega}
,

a maximal filter for
\mathbb{P}_{S}
. Then
\mathcal{A}_{F}
is a Tarski structure for
\mathrm{L}(\mathcal{C})
.

Proof.

We need to check that the definition of
A_{F}
and of
R_{F}
does not depend on the chosen representatives
c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}
. Suppose
c_{1}=d_{1},\ldots,c_{n}=d_{n},R(c_{1}\ldots c_{n})\in\Sigma_{F}
.
By the previous Fact
\{c_{1}=d_{1},\ldots,c_{n}=d_{n},R(c_{1}\ldots c_{n})\}\in F
. Hence by Clause

4.2
(Ind2) for any
p\supseteq\{c_{1}=d_{1},\ldots,c_{n}=d_{n},R(c_{1}\ldots c_{n})\}
in

\mathbb{P}_{S}
,

p\cup\left\{R(d_{1},\dots,d_{n})\right\}\in\mathbb{P}_{S}
. This combined with Clause
4.2
(Con) gives that no
p\in\mathbb{P}_{S}
can contain

\{c_{1}=d_{1},\ldots,c_{n}=d_{n},R(c_{1}\ldots c_{n}),\neg R(d_{1},\dots,d_{n})\}

Hence by maximality of

,

\{c_{1}=d_{1},\ldots,c_{n}=d_{n},R(c_{1}\ldots c_{n}),R(d_{1},\dots,d_{n})\}\in F

∎

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
\mathrm{L}
be a relational
\omega
-signature and
\mathcal{C}
an infinite set of constants disjoint from
\mathrm{L}
.
Assume
S\subset[\mathrm{L}(\mathcal{C})_{\infty\infty}]^{<\omega}
is a consistency property.
Let
F\subseteq\mathbb{P}_{S}
be a maximal filter on
\mathbb{P}_{S}
.
Consider
\Sigma^{\prime}_{F}\subset\Sigma_{F}
the set of (quantifier free) formulae
\psi\in\Sigma_{F}
which are either atomic, negated atomic, or such that any subformula of
\psi
which is neither atomic nor negated atomic contains just the logical constant
\bigwedge
. Then
\mathcal{A}_{F}\vDash\Sigma^{\prime}_{F}
.

Proof.

We do it by induction on the complexity of
\psi\in\Sigma^{\prime}_{F}
.
First note that
\mathbb{P}_{S}
is a consistency property of which
S
is a dense subset.
The atomic case follows by Def.
5.4
. For the remaining inductive clauses we proceed as follows:

\neg
:

Suppose
\psi=\neg\phi\in\Sigma^{\prime}_{F}
with
\phi
an atomic formula. Let’s see that

\mathcal{A}_{F}\nvDash\phi.

Since
\phi
is atomic it is enough to check
\phi\notin\Sigma^{\prime}_{F}
. Suppose otherwise. Then there exists
p\in F
with
\phi\in p
. Also
\psi\in q
for some
q\in F
. By compatibility of filters there exists
r\leq p,q
. But
\phi,\neg\phi\in r
contradicts clause
4.2
(Con) for
\mathbb{P}_{S}
. Therefore

\mathcal{A}_{F}\vDash\psi.

\bigwedge
:

Suppose
\psi=\bigwedge\Phi
is in
\Sigma_{F}^{\prime}
. One needs to check

\mathcal{A}_{F}\vDash\phi

for any
\phi\in\Phi
. Fix such a
\phi\in\Phi
. We start by showing that if
\bigwedge\Phi\in\Sigma^{\prime}_{F}
,
\phi
is also in
\Sigma^{\prime}_{F}
. It is enough to check
\phi\in\Sigma_{F}
, and then apply the inductive assumptions on
\phi\in\Sigma^{\prime}_{F}
, to get that
\mathcal{A}_{F}\models\phi
.
Towards this aim we note the following:

For any
q\in\mathbb{P}_{S}
with
\bigwedge\Phi\in q
,

q\cup\left\{\phi\right\}\in\mathbb{P}_{S}
, while
q\cup\mathbb{\neg\phi}\not\in\mathbb{P}_{S}
.

Proof.

Take
q
in
\mathbb{P}_{S}
with
\bigwedge\Phi\in q
.
By Clause
4.2
(Ind.2),
q\cup\{\phi\}\in\mathbb{P}_{S}
.
Assume now that
\neg\phi\in q
. Then
q\cup\{\phi\}\in\mathbb{P}_{S}
would contradict
Clause
4.2
(Ind.2) for
\mathbb{P}_{S}
. The thesis follows.
∎

By maximality of
F
if some
q\in F
is such that
\bigwedge\Phi\in q
, then

q\cup\left\{\phi\right\}\in F
as well, yielding that
\phi\in\Sigma_{F}
as was to be shown.

∎

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
\mathrm{L}
be a relational
\omega
-signature,
\mathcal{C}
an infinite set of constants disjoint from
\mathrm{L}
, and
S
be a consistency property consisting of
\mathrm{L}(\mathcal{C})_{\infty\omega}
-sentences.

Assume that
F
is a
V
-generic filter for
\mathbb{P}_{S}
. Then in

it holds that:

(1)

The domain of

\mathcal{A}_{F}
is exactly given by
\left\{[c]_{F}:c\in\mathcal{C}\right\}
.

(2)

For any
\mathrm{L}(\mathcal{C})_{\infty\omega}
-sentence
\psi

\mathcal{A}_{F}\vDash\psi\text{ if }\psi\in\Sigma_{F}.

Note the following apparently trivial corollary of the above Theorem:

Corollary 5.8
.

Assume
S
is a consistency property on
\mathrm{L}(\mathcal{C})_{\infty\omega}
satisfying the assumptions of Thm.
5.7
. Then for any

s\in S

s\not\vdash\emptyset
.

Remark 5.9
.

We note that essentially the same Theorem and Corollary have been proved independently by Ben De Bondt and Boban Velickovic (using the language of forcing via partial orders to formulate them).

Proof.

Assume
s\vdash\emptyset
for some
s\in S
. Note that if
F
is
V
-generic for
\mathbb{P}_{S}
with
s\in F
, the same proof existing in
V
of
s\vdash\emptyset
is a proof of the same sequent in
V[F]
.
By Thm.
5.7

\mathcal{A}_{F}\models\bigwedge s
holds in
V[F]
.
Hence by the soundness of Tarski semantics for
\vdash
in
V[F]
, we would get that

\mathcal{A}_{F}\models\psi\wedge\neg\psi
for some
\psi
holds in
V[F]
. This is clearly a contradictory statement for
V[F]
.
∎

We now prove Thm.
5.7
:

Proof.

Let (in
V[F]
)
\mathcal{A}_{F}
be the structure obtained from
F
as in Def.
5.4
.
Since

is a dense subset of
\mathbb{P}_{S}
,

is a generic filter for
(S,\supseteq)
as well.
By Clause
4.2
(Str.3)

D_{d}=\{p\in S:\exists c\in\mathcal{C},c=d\in p\}

is dense in
\mathbb{P}_{S}
for any

. Let
p\in F\cap D_{d}
. Then for some
c\in C
,
d=c\in p\subset\Sigma_{F}
and
[d]_{F}=[c]_{F}
. This proves part
1
of the Theorem.

We now establish part
2
. We have to handle only the cases for
\neg
,
\bigvee
,
\exists
,
\forall
formulae, since the atomic case and the case
\bigwedge
can be treated exactly as we did in Fact
5.5
and Lemma
5.6
. We continue the induction as follows:

\bigvee
:

Suppose
\bigvee\Phi\in\Sigma_{F}
. Let
p_{0}\in F
be such that
\bigvee\Phi\in p_{0}
. By Clause

4.2
(Ind.4)

D_{\bigvee\Phi}=\{p\in S:\exists\phi\in\Phi,\phi\in p\}

is dense below
p_{0}
. Since
F
is
V
-generic over
\mathbb{P}_{S}
and

, there exists

\phi\in\Phi

\phi\in p\subset\Sigma_{F}
and

\mathcal{A}_{F}\vDash\phi,

proving

\exists

Suppose

. Let
p_{0}\in F
such that

By Clause

(Ind.5)

D_{\exists v\phi(\vec{v})}=\{p\in S:\exists\vec{c}\in\mathcal{C}^{\vec{v}},\phi(\vec{c})\in p\}

. Since

is

hence

\mathcal{A}_{F}\vDash\exists\vec{v}\phi(\vec{v}).

\forall

Suppose

One needs to check

. Then

if and only if there are

Remark 5.10
.

One may wonder why the Theorem is proved just for consistency properties for

and not for arbitrary consistency properties on
\mathrm{L}_{\infty\infty}

as computed in

is a strict superset of
\mathcal{E}^{<\lambda}
as computed in

. In this case there is no reason to expect that

when

but

.

Note that it may occur that for some

-sentence
\psi
, neither
\psi
nor
\neg\psi
belongs to any

, hence for some

-generic filter

for

it can be the case that

while

. For example this occurs because
S
is a set and there are class many

-sentence

.

We can prove a partial converse of the second conclusion of Thm.

which requires a slight strengthening of the notion of consistency property:

.

Let
\mathrm{L}=\mathcal{R}\cup\mathcal{D}
,
\mathcal{D}

\mathrm{L}(\mathcal{C})_{\kappa\lambda}
-sentences and

satisfies the following clause:

(S-Max)

Remark 6.2
.

We note that a key assumption for Mansfield’s result is that
\mathrm{L}
is a first order signature.
This is crucially used in the proof that

holds for the relation symbols of
\mathrm{L}
in the structure
\mathcal{M}
: since these relation symbols are finitary, in the proof above we just had to distribute finitely many infinite disjunctions; this distributive law holds for any complete Boolean algebra.
If we dealt with a relational
\omega_{1}
-signature we might have had to distribute countably many infinitary disjunctions to establish

\mathbb{P}_{S}
.

We do not know whether this result can be established for arbitrary
\lambda

8.
Forcing notions as consistency properties

By the results of Section
5
a consistency property
S
for
\mathrm{L}_{\kappa\omega}
can be naturally seen as a forcing notion
\mathbb{P}_{S}
; then, using the forcing machinery on
\mathbb{P}_{S}
, we can produce a Boolean valued model with the mixing property of
\bigwedge p
for any
p\in S
. In this section we show that it is possible to go the other way round: we prove that any forcing notion
\mathbb{P}
has a consistency property
S_{\mathbb{P}}
associated to it, so that it is equivalent to force with
\mathbb{P}
or with
\mathbb{P}_{S_{\mathbb{P}}}
.

From now on we deal with forcing notions given both by partial orders or by complete Boolean algebras.

Given a complete Boolean algebra
\mathsf{B}
,
we show that for some regular
\kappa
large enough in
V
,
\mathsf{B}
is forcing equivalent to a consistency property describing the
\in
-theory of
H_{\kappa}
as computed in a
V
-generic extension by

\mathsf{B}
.

Notation 8.1
.

Let
\mathsf{B}
be a complete Boolean algebra of cardinality
\kappa
.
The signature
\mathrm{L}
is
\left\{\in\right\}
.

\mathrm{L}(\mathcal{C})_{\infty\omega}
is produced by the set of constants
\mathcal{C}=V^{\mathsf{B}}\cap H_{\kappa^{+}}
. We use

to denote that all quantifiers from
\phi
are restricted to
H_{\kappa^{+}}
.
We write
\left\llbracket\phi\right\rrbracket_{\mathsf{B}}
rather than
\left\llbracket\phi\right\rrbracket^{V^{\mathsf{B}}}_{\mathsf{B}}
.

Theorem 8.2
.

For any complete Boolean algebra
\mathsf{B}
of size less or equal than
\kappa
and any regular cardinal
\lambda
the following holds:

(i)

S_{\mathsf{B}}=\{s\in[\mathrm{L}(\mathcal{C})_{\lambda\omega}]^{<\omega}:\left\llbracket(\bigwedge s)^{H_{\check{\kappa}^{+}}}\right\rrbracket_{\mathsf{B}}>0_{\mathsf{B}}\}
is a consistency property,

(ii)

the map

\displaystyle\pi_{\mathsf{B}}:(S_{\mathsf{B}},\leq)

\displaystyle\rightarrow(\mathsf{B}^{+},\leq_{\mathsf{B}})

\displaystyle s

\displaystyle\mapsto\left\llbracket(\bigwedge s)^{H_{\check{\kappa}^{+}}}\right\rrbracket_{\mathsf{B}}

In particular

Proof.

We first prove
(ii)
.

•

If

•

We have
p\perp q\Leftrightarrow p\cup q\notin S_{\mathsf{B}}\Leftrightarrow\left\llbracket\bigwedge(p\cup q)\right\rrbracket_{\mathsf{B}}=0_{\mathsf{B}}\Leftrightarrow\left\llbracket\bigwedge p\right\rrbracket_{\mathsf{B}}\wedge\left\llbracket\bigwedge q\right\rrbracket_{\mathsf{B}}=0_{\mathsf{B}}\Leftrightarrow\pi(p)\perp\pi(q)

•

\left\llbracket d\neq c_{0}\right\rrbracket^{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathsf{C}}=\neg\left\llbracket d=c_{1}\right\rrbracket^{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathsf{C}}

in all
\mathsf{C}
-valued models
\mathcal{N}
of

c_{0}\neq c_{1}\wedge\bigvee_{m\in 2}d=c_{m}
. Hence we cannot have that

\left\llbracket d\neq c_{0}\right\rrbracket^{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathsf{C}}=\left\llbracket d_{n}\neq c_{1}\right\rrbracket^{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathsf{C}}=1_{\mathsf{C}}

in any boolean valued model model of the other axioms of
T
.
∎

We conclude this part noting that boolean satisfiability is the correct generalization to
\mathrm{L}_{\infty\omega}
of Tarski satisfiability:

Fact 9.4
.

Assume
T
is a first order theory. Then
T
is boolean
satisfiable if and only if
T
is Tarski satisfiable.

Proof.

By Thm.
3.1
any boolean satisfiable first order theory
is realized in a
\mathsf{B}
-valued model

\mathcal{M}
with the mixing property. If
G
is a ultrafilter on
\mathsf{B}
,
\mathcal{M}/_{G}
models
T
by Proposition
2.9
and Thm.
2.12
.
∎

9.2.
Proof of Fact
2.6
and Proposition
2.9

We first prove Fact
2.6
.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on the complexity of
\phi(x_{i}:i<\alpha)
. The Fact holds by definition for atomic formulae.
Assume the Fact for all proper subformulae of
\phi(x_{i}:i<\alpha)
.

Now note that the desired inequality entails that for all
\beta
and

(\sigma_{i}:i<\beta)
,
(\tau_{i}:i<\beta)
in

\mathcal{M}^{\beta}
and all
\psi(x_{i}:i<\beta)
proper subformula of
\phi(x_{i}:i<\alpha)
,

\bigg(\bigwedge_{i\in\beta}\left\llbracket\tau_{i}=\sigma_{i}\right\rrbracket_{\mathsf{B}}\bigg)\wedge\left\llbracket\psi(\tau_{i}:\,i<\beta)\right\rrbracket_{\mathsf{B}}=\bigg(\bigwedge_{i\in\beta}\left\llbracket\tau_{i}=\sigma_{i}\right\rrbracket_{\mathsf{B}}\bigg)\wedge\left\llbracket\psi(\sigma_{i}:\,i<\beta)\right\rrbracket_{\mathsf{B}}.

Now if
\phi=\neg\psi
the above equality is extended to
\phi
. It is also preserved if
\phi=\bigvee\Phi
since

\displaystyle\bigg(\bigwedge_{i\in\alpha}\left\llbracket\tau_{i}=\sigma_{i}\right\rrbracket_{\mathsf{B}}\bigg)\wedge\left\llbracket\bigvee\Phi(\tau_{i}:\,i<\alpha)\right\rrbracket_{\mathsf{B}}=

\displaystyle=\bigg(\bigwedge_{i\in\alpha}\left\llbracket\tau_{i}=\sigma_{i}\right\rrbracket_{\mathsf{B}}\bigg)\wedge\bigvee_{\psi\in\Phi}\left\llbracket\psi(\tau_{i}:\,i<\alpha)\right\rrbracket_{\mathsf{B}}=

\displaystyle=\bigvee_{\psi\in\Phi}\bigg(\bigwedge_{i\in\alpha}\left\llbracket\tau_{i}=\sigma_{i}\right\rrbracket_{\mathsf{B}}\wedge\left\llbracket\psi(\tau_{i}:\,i<\alpha)\right\rrbracket_{\mathsf{B}}\bigg)=

\displaystyle=\bigvee_{\psi\in\Phi}\bigg(\bigwedge_{i\in\alpha}\left\llbracket\tau_{i}=\sigma_{i}\right\rrbracket_{\mathsf{B}}\wedge\left\llbracket\psi(\sigma_{i}:\,i<\alpha)\right\rrbracket_{\mathsf{B}}\bigg)=

\displaystyle=\bigg(\bigwedge_{i\in\alpha}\left\llbracket\tau_{i}=\sigma_{i}\right\rrbracket_{\mathsf{B}}\bigg)\wedge\bigvee_{\phi\in\Phi}\left\llbracket\psi(\sigma_{i}:\,i<\alpha)\right\rrbracket_{\mathsf{B}}=

\displaystyle=\bigg(\bigwedge_{i\in\alpha}\left\llbracket\tau_{i}=\sigma_{i}\right\rrbracket_{\mathsf{B}}\bigg)\wedge\left\llbracket\bigvee\Phi(\sigma_{i}:\,i<\alpha)\right\rrbracket_{\mathsf{B}}.

Similarly

\displaystyle\bigg(\bigwedge_{i\in\alpha}\left\llbracket\tau_{i}=\sigma_{i}\right\rrbracket_{\mathsf{B}}\bigg)\wedge\left\llbracket\exists(y_{j}:j\in\beta)\psi(\tau_{i}:\,i<\alpha,y_{j}:j\in\beta)\right\rrbracket_{\mathsf{B}}=

\displaystyle=\bigg(\bigwedge_{i\in\alpha}\left\llbracket\tau_{i}=\sigma_{i}\right\rrbracket_{\mathsf{B}}\bigg)\wedge\bigvee_{(\eta_{j}:j\in\beta)\in\mathcal{M}^{\beta}}\left\llbracket\psi(\tau_{i}:\,i<\alpha,\eta_{j}:j\in\beta)\right\rrbracket_{\mathsf{B}}=

\displaystyle=\bigvee_{(\eta_{j}:j\in\beta)\in\mathcal{M}^{\beta}}\bigg(\bigwedge_{i\in\alpha}\left\llbracket\tau_{i}=\sigma_{i}\right\rrbracket_{\mathsf{B}}\wedge\left\llbracket\psi(\tau_{i}:\,i<\alpha,\eta_{j}:j\in\beta)\right\rrbracket_{\mathsf{B}}\bigg)=

\displaystyle=\bigvee_{(\eta_{j}:j\in\beta)\in\mathcal{M}^{\beta}}\bigg(\bigwedge_{i\in\alpha}\left\llbracket\tau_{i}=\sigma_{i}\right\rrbracket_{\mathsf{B}}\wedge\left\llbracket\psi(\sigma_{i}:\,i<\alpha,\eta_{j}:j\in\beta)\right\rrbracket_{\mathsf{B}}\bigg)=

\displaystyle=\bigg(\bigwedge_{i\in\alpha}\left\llbracket\tau_{i}=\sigma_{i}\right\rrbracket_{\mathsf{B}}\bigg)\wedge\bigvee_{(\eta_{j}:j\in\beta)\in\mathcal{M}^{\beta}}\left\llbracket\psi(\sigma_{i}:\,i<\alpha,\eta_{j}:j\in\beta)\right\rrbracket_{\mathsf{B}}=

\displaystyle=\bigg(\bigwedge_{i\in\alpha}\left\llbracket\tau_{i}=\sigma_{i}\right\rrbracket_{\mathsf{B}}\bigg)\wedge\left\llbracket\exists(y_{j}:j\in\beta)\psi(\sigma_{i}:\,i<\alpha,y_{j}:j\in\beta)\right\rrbracket_{\mathsf{B}}.

The cases of
\bigwedge,\forall
are handled similarly.
∎

We can now prove Proposition
2.9
.

Proof.

Let
\exists\overline{v}\phi(\overline{v})
be a
\mathrm{L}_{\infty\infty}
-sentence. Fix a maximal antichain
A
among

\left\{b\in\mathsf{B}:b\leq\left\llbracket\phi(\overline{c_{b}})\right\rrbracket\text{ for some }\overline{c}\in M^{|\overline{v}|}\right\}.

For each
b\in A
let
\overline{c_{b}}=(c_{i,b}:i\in I)
. The mixing property in
\mathcal{M}
gives
c_{i}
for each
i\in I
such that
\left\llbracket c_{i}=c_{i,b}\right\rrbracket_{\mathsf{B}}\geq b
for all
b\in A
. Let
\overline{c}=(c_{i}:i\in I)
. Then

\displaystyle\left\llbracket\exists\overline{v}\phi(\overline{v})\right\rrbracket=\bigvee A=\bigvee_{b\in A}\left\llbracket\phi(\overline{c_{b}})\right\rrbracket=\bigvee_{b\in A}(b\wedge\left\llbracket\phi(\overline{c_{b}})\right\rrbracket\wedge\bigwedge_{i\in I}\left\llbracket c_{i}=c_{i,b}\right\rrbracket_{\mathsf{B}})\leq

\displaystyle\bigvee_{b\in A}(b\wedge\left\llbracket\phi(\overline{c})\right\rrbracket_{\mathsf{B}})=\left\llbracket\phi(\overline{c})\right\rrbracket_{\mathsf{B}}.

∎

Concluding remarks

Mansfield’s completeness theorem follows from his proof that if
S
is a consistency property for

\mathrm{L}_{\infty\infty}
, there is a
\RO(\mathbb{P}_{S})
-valued model
\mathcal{M}_{S}

such that
\left\llbracket\bigwedge s\right\rrbracket=\text{Reg}\left(\left\{s\right\}\right)
for any
s\in S
. However there is no reason to expect that the model
\mathcal{M}_{S}
produced in Mansfield’s proof is full.
We conjecture it is not, at least for some
S
.

We also conjecture that if
S
is a consistency property for
\mathrm{L}_{\infty\infty}
,
our model
\mathcal{A}_{S}
may not satisfy
\left\llbracket\psi\right\rrbracket=\text{Reg}\left(\left\{r\in S:\psi\in r\right\}\right)
for some
formula
\psi
of
\mathrm{L}_{\infty\infty}
.
The key point is that the
\mathrm{L}_{\infty\infty}
-semantics of existential quantifiers over infinite strings is not forcing invariant: if one forces the addition of a new countable sequence to some
\mathsf{B}
-valued model
\mathcal{M}
in
V
, it may be the case that
\exists\vec{v}\psi
gets Boolean value
0_{\mathsf{B}}
in
V
and positive
\RO^{V[G]}(\mathsf{B})
-Boolean value in the generic extension
V[G]
. This makes our proof of Thm.
5.7
break down when handling the existential quantifier clause for
\mathrm{L}_{\infty\infty}
over an infinite string.

We dare the following:

Conjecture 9.5
.

Assume
\mathrm{L}
is a relational
\omega
-signature.
There are Boolean satisfiable
\mathrm{L}_{\infty\infty}
-theories which do not have a Boolean valued
model with the mixing property.

Another point to be clarified on the completeness of Boolean valued semantics for
\mathrm{L}_{\infty\infty}
is the following:

Question 9.6
.

Assume
\mathrm{L}
is a relational
\lambda
-signature for
\lambda>\omega
. Does the completeness theorem for consistent
(according to the
\mathrm{L}_{\infty\infty}
-Gentzen’s calculus)

\mathrm{L}_{\infty\infty}
-theories holds?

Mansfield’s model existence theorem does not apply to such theories as the proof of (
2
) for the model obtained in Mansfield’s proof breaks down for the obvious modification of the notion of consistency property required in order to deal with Boolean valued models for
\lambda
-signatures (e.g. one should replace clause (Str.2) of a consistency property with the stronger:
“If
\left\{\bigwedge_{i\in I}c_{i}=d_{i},\phi(c_{i}:i\in I)\right\}\in s\in S
, then
\left\{\phi(d_{i}:i\in I)\right\}\cup s\in S
”
.) In the tentative proof of (
2
) for the model obtained in Mansfield’s proof one should replace our argument with one requiring that a distributivity law for infinite conjunctions of infinite disjunctions holds.
The latter may not hold for
\mathsf{B}_{S}
.

Note finally that while Boolean compactness fails for
\mathrm{L}_{\infty\omega}
, one can prove the following curious form of weak Boolean compactness:

Fact 9.7
.

Assume
T
is a family of Boolean satisfiable
\mathrm{L}_{\infty\omega}
-sentences.
Then
T
is weakly Boolean satisfiable.

This holds noticing that if
S_{\psi}
is a consistency property that witnesses that
\psi
is Boolean consistent,

S=\bigcup_{\psi\in T}S_{\psi}
is a consistency property such that
\mathcal{A}_{S}
assigns a positive Boolean value to any
\psi\in T
.

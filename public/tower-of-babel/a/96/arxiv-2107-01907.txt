About the value of the two dimensional Levy’s constant
Yitwah Cheung, Nicolas Chevallier

Abstract
We give a numerical approximation of the Lévy constant on the growth of the denominators of the best Diophantine approximations in dimension 2 with respect to the euclidean norm. This constant is expressed as an integral on a surface of dimension 7. We reduce the computation of this integral to a triple integral, whose numerical evaluation was carried out in [ 3 ] .



1
Introduction

In 1936, Aleksandr Khintchin showed that there exists a constant
K
such that the denominators
(q_{n})_{n\geq 0}

of the convergents of the continued fraction expansions of almost all real
numbers
\theta
satisfy

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\ln q_{n}=K

Soon afterward, Paul Lévy gave the explicit value
of the constant,

K=\frac{\pi^{2}}{12\ln 2}.

In
[
2
]
, this result is extended to the denominators of best Diophantine approximations to vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and even to matrices in
M_{d,c}(\mathbb{R})
. The value of the limit is given by an integral
\int_{S}d\mu_{S}
over a codimension one submanifold
S
in the space of lattices
\SL(d+1,\mathbb{R})/\SL(d+1,\mathbb{Z})
(see Section
2.2
below). However, apart from the case of
d=1
, this integral is very difficult to calculate. The aim of this document is to give a numerical
approximation of the integral associated with best Diophantine approximations to vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

This document is organized as follows. We first give the definition of the submanifold
S
together with a parametrization of
S
. Then we give an explicit formula for the measure
\mu_{S}
induced by the flow. The two difficult parts of the work are the explicit description of the domain of integration, i.e., the subset of parameters corresponding to
S
, and the calculation of the integral
\int_{S}d\mu_{S}
. This is done in the two last sections.
For more details on best Diophantine approximation we refer the reader to
[
2
]
.

4
Domain of integration

The suspension of the transversal gives a fundamental domain for the action of
\SL(3,\mathbb{Z})
that is invariant under is
\SO(2)

\SO(3)

transversal by the circle action is a 6 dimensional space that we shall realize as a fiber bundle over the 3 dimensional base
\Omega_{2}\times[0,1[

\Omega_{2}=\{a=a_{1}+ia_{2}\in\mathbb{C}:|a|<1,|a-1|\geq 1\}

parametrizes the possible configurations of

and

on the boundary of the standard unit cylinder
C_{0}=C(1)

S
we rescale and rotate so that the systole cylinder is given by
C_{0}
with the vectors
u
and
v
on its
\partial_{+}
- and
\partial_{-}
-faces. Ambiguity in the normal form can be ignored since it occurs on a set of positive codimension. The lattice is determined by specifying the third vector
w=(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3})
that forms an integral basis for the lattice, positively oriented by requiring
c_{2}<0

w

The space of possibilities for

is a connected component of the complement of the union of
C_{0}
with all its translates under the action of the discrete subgroup
\mathbb{Z}u+\mathbb{Z}v

where this surface meets
\partial C_{0}
. The orthogonal projection of
G(a,b)
onto the
(c_{1},c_{3})
-plane is a rectilinear domain that we will denote by

F(a,b).

The choice of

is such that its orthogonal projection lies in
F(a,b)
;

(c_{1},c_{3})\in F(a,b)\quad\text{ and }\quad-c_{2}\geq\sqrt{1-c_{1}^{2}}.

F(a,b)
is rectilinear since curved arcs on
\partial G(a,b)
map to horizontal segments.

Remark: it can be shown that
\Xi>1/24

\Xi:=(1-a_{1}b)\sqrt{1-c_{1}^{2}}-a_{2}(bc_{1}-c_{3}).

The substitution

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{A+B\tau}{1+\tau^{2}}+\frac{C(a_{2}b+(1-a_{1}b-a_{2}c_{3})\tau)+D}{1-a_{1}b+a_{2}c_{3}-2a_{2}b\tau-(1-a_{1}b-a_{2}c_{3})\tau^{2}}\right)d\tau

Evolving convex curves by a generalized length-preserving flow
Laiyuan Gao Shengliang Pan

Abstract




Abstract
This paper deals with a generalized length-preserving flow for convex curves in the plane.
It is shown that the flow exists globally and deforms smooth initial curves into circles as time tends to infinity.

Keywords
convex curve, length-preserving flow, quasilinear parabolic equation.

Mathematics Subject Classification (2020)
53A04, 53E10, 35K15, 35K59

1
Introduction

Let
X(\phi,t):S^{1}\times[0,\omega)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a family of smooth and closed curves in the plane.
Denote by
\kappa(\phi,t)
the (relative) curvature with respect to the Frenet frame
\{T(\phi,t),N(\phi,t)\}
at a point

(\phi,t)
, where
T(\phi,t)
and
N(\phi,t)
are the unit tangent and inward normal vectors, respectively.
An embedded closed
C^{2}
curve is called convex if its curvature is positive everywhere.
Suppose
X_{0}(\phi)
is a convex curve in the plane.
This paper focuses on a general nonlocal curvature flow for convex curves:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{\partial X}{\partial t}(\phi,t)=\left[F(\kappa)-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{L}F(\kappa)\kappa ds\right]N(\phi,t)\ \ \textup{in}~S^{1}\times(0,\omega),\\
\\
X(\phi,0)=X_{0}(\phi)\ \ \textup{on}~S^{1},\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

where
s
represents the arc-length parameter,
L=L(t)
the length of
X(\cdot,t)
and
F(\cdot)
a smooth
real-valued function on
(0,+\infty)
.

In recent decades, the curvature flow of curves has attracted extensive attention since 1980s. Besides the well known curve
shortening flow (see
[
8
,
9
]
,
[
11
]
and
[
17
]
)
and its generalizations (see
[
2
,
3
]
), there are also nonlocal flows, such as length-preserving flows by
Pan-Yang
[
28
]
, Ma-Zhu
[
23
]
and area-preserving flows by Gage
[
10
]
, Ma-Cheng
[
22
]
,
Mao-Pan-Wang
[
26
]
. Lin and Tsai
[
20
]
summarised previews length-preserving
or area-preserving flows into the model (
1.1
). Let
\alpha>0
be a constant.
They especially studied the asymptotic behavior of
\kappa^{\alpha}
-type and
1/\kappa^{\alpha}
-type nonlocal flows
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
, provided that the initial curve is convex and the flow exists globally.
Subsequently, Wang and Tsai
[
30
]
demonstrated that
\kappa^{\alpha}
-type length-preserving or area-preserving flows for convex curves
exist globally and drive the evolving curve into circles as
t\rightarrow+\infty
.
The
\alpha
-homogeneity of
F
plays an essential role in Wang-Tsai’s research. The higher
dimensional similar model with
\alpha
-homogeneity assumption of
F
has been treated by Andrews and McCoy
[
5
]
.
Recently, Gao, Pan and Tsai
[
12
,
13
,
14
]
studied
1/\kappa^{\alpha}
type nonlocal flows for convex curves.
A number of blow-up examples of this kind of nonlocal flows have been discovered, and all global flows are proven convergent as time tends to infinity.

Both the
\kappa^{\alpha}
-type and
1/\kappa^{\alpha}
-type nonlocal flows are special cases of the flow (
1.1
). Thus, the research
of this generalized model is a continuation of previous studies. The flow (
1.1
) with some concrete
F
can be defined for general embedded curves,
but it may experience a blow-up in a finite time if the function
F
or the initial convex curve is not appropriately chosen. One can find
some blow-up examples in
[
10
,
25
]
(
X_{0}
is embedded but not convex) and in
[
13
,
33
]
(
X_{0}
is convex).
To obtain favorable results of the flow, including its global existence and convergence, certain restrictions on the function
F
should be imposed.

From this point forward, we assume the function
F
, defined in the interval
(0,+\infty)
, is smooth and satisfies the following three conditions for
u>0
:

(i)
F
is strictly increasing on
(0,+\infty)
, i.e.
F^{\prime}(u)>0
and

\lim\limits_{u\rightarrow 0^{+}}F^{\prime}(u)\cdot u=0;

(ii)
F(u)
is positive for all
u>0
and

\lim\limits_{u\rightarrow+\infty}F(u)=+\infty,~~~~F(0):=\lim\limits_{u\rightarrow 0^{+}}F(u)=0;

(iii)
F(u)
and
F^{\prime}(u)
satisfy two asymptotic properties

\displaystyle\lim_{u\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{F^{\prime}(u)\cdot u^{2}}{F(u)}=+\infty,~~~~\lim_{u\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{F^{\prime}(u)\cdot u^{2}}{F(u)}=0.

Before the statement of our main result, two remarks are in order.
First, in contrast to the evolution problem of hypersurfaces (see
[
1
,
4
,
16
]
, etc.),
there are no additional convexity conditions on the function
F
.
Along with the
\alpha
-homogeneity case of
F
studied by Wang-Tsai
[
30
]
,
there are numerous other examples of
F
satisfying above conditions (i)-(iii), such as

F(\kappa)=\ln(1+\kappa),~e^{\kappa}-1,~2\kappa+\sin(\kappa),~\kappa^{2}\ln\kappa+\kappa

and so on.
Second, the parabolic property
F^{\prime}>0
and the positivity condition
F>0
imply the limit given in the condition (i)
(See Remark
3.1
), if this limit exists.
Therefore, the conditions (i)-(iii) can be summarized as the parabolic property, positivity and five limits of
F
.
The main result of this paper is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
X_{0}
be a smooth and convex plane curve. If
F
satisfies
the above conditions (i)-(iii) then the flow (
1.1
) exists on time interval
[0,+\infty)
,
keeps the convexity of the evolving curve, preserves its length and deforms
X(\cdot,t)
into a finite circle
as time tends to infinity.

It is extremely challenging to obtain good convergence results of the flow (
1.1
) for generic initial convex curves, if
any one of the three conditions of
F
is omitted.
The condition (i) is used to ensure the parabolicity of the evolution equation.
In condition (ii), we assume
F
is a positive function.
Tsai
[
33
]
discovered the blow up phenomenon of a convex curve evolving under
(
1.1
) when
F(\kappa)=-1/\kappa
.
The same phenomenon also occurs in some other similar nonlocal models with
F<0

[
12
,
13
,
14
]
.
Hence, without condition (ii), this flow may blow up a convex curve in finite time.
The third condition can be used to uniformly bound the curvature both above and below.
The proof depends on a delicate application of the maximum principle for two geometric auxiliary functions,
which are inspired by Chou’s early work
[
6
]
and its developments
[
5
]
,

[
15
]
and
[
30
]
.
Without the uniform bounds of curvature, the convergence of the flow cannot be expected.

Lin and Tsai in
[
20
]
proposed that one might consider the flow (1.1) under the assumption that
F(u)/u
is increasing if
u>0
.
This condition implies the parabolicity condition
F^{\prime}>0
when
F(u)>0
for all positive
u
. It remains an open question whether or not Lin-Tsai’s
condition leads to the convergence of the flow (
1.1
).
For flows of other type of curves, one can consult Wang-Kong
[
29
]
, Wang-Wo
[
31
]
, Wang-Wo-Yang
[
32
]
.
One can read the books
[
7
,
24
,
34
]

and references therein for more information about the curvature flows of curves and some higher dimensional analogues.

The global existence of the flow (
1.1
) is proven in Section
2
.
The convergence of the curvature is demonstrated in Section
3
and the convergence of the curve is presented in Section

4
.

2
Global existence

In this section, the global existence of the flow (
1.1
) is proven.
It is shown that the evolving curve keeps convex and its curvature has a uniform upper bound,
which is independent of time. So the flow can be extended on time interval
[0,+\infty)
.

2.1
Short time existence

Let
\theta=\theta(\varphi,t)
be the tangent angle of the evolving curve. Under the flow (
1.1
),

\theta
varies with respect to time. One can make it independent of time by adding a proper tangent component to the
original flow to obtain

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{\partial\widetilde{X}}{\partial t}=-\frac{1}{\kappa}\frac{\partial F}{\partial s}T+(F-\lambda)N\ \ \textup{in}~S^{1}\times(0,\omega),\\
\\
\widetilde{X}(\varphi,0)=X_{0}(\varphi)\ \ \textup{on}~S^{1}.\end{array}\right.

(2.1)

If there is a family of convex curves evolving under the flow (
1.1
), then (
2.1
) also has a
solution and it differs from
X(\cdot,t)
by a reparameterization and a translation
[
7
]
.
By straightforward computations (see for example,
[
6
]
), the tangent angle under the flow (
2.1
) satisfies that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial t}=\left(-\frac{1}{\kappa}\frac{\partial F}{\partial s}\right)\kappa+\frac{\partial}{\partial s}(F-\lambda)=0.

So
\theta
is independent of time. It can be used as the parameter of the evolving curve
X(\cdot,t)
.
Due to this reason, we consider the flow (
2.1
) from now on.

Since the curvature determines the curve up to a transformation via the Euclidean group, the short time existence of the flow (
2.1
)
can be reduced to the evolution equation of the curvature
\kappa
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\kappa}{\partial t}=\kappa^{2}\left[F^{\prime}(\kappa)\frac{\partial^{2}\kappa}{\partial\theta^{2}}+F^{\prime\prime}(\kappa)\left(\frac{\partial\kappa}{\partial\theta}\right)^{2}+F(\kappa)-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}F(\kappa)d\theta\right],

(2.2)

where the initial curvature
\kappa_{0}(\theta)=\kappa(\theta,0)
satisfies the closing condition

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{e^{i\theta}}{\kappa_{0}(\theta)}d\theta=0.

(2.3)

The equation (
2.2
) is a quasilinear parabolic equation. Its linearization at a smooth function
u(\theta,t)=\kappa_{0}(\theta)+tu_{0}(\theta)
is as follows

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\kappa}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u^{2}F^{\prime}(u)\frac{\partial^{2}\kappa}{\partial\theta^{2}}+2u^{2}F^{\prime\prime}(u)\frac{\partial u}{\partial\theta}\frac{\partial\kappa}{\partial\theta}

\displaystyle+2u\left[F^{\prime}(u)\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial\theta^{2}}+F^{\prime\prime}(u)\left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial\theta}\right)^{2}+F(u)\right]\kappa

\displaystyle+u^{2}\left[F^{\prime\prime}(u)\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial\theta^{2}}+F^{\prime\prime\prime}(u)\left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial\theta}\right)^{2}+F^{\prime}(u)\right]\kappa

\displaystyle-\frac{u}{\pi}\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}F(u)d\theta\right)\kappa-\frac{u^{2}}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}F^{\prime}(u)\kappa d\theta.

The smooth function
\kappa_{0}(\theta)
is positive everywhere, so the linearization of (
2.2
) at
u_{0}
with small
t>0
is uniformly parabolic.
It follows from the linearization method
[
7
,
15
]
that the Cauchy problem (
2.2
)
with initial value
\kappa_{0}
has a unique smooth solution
\kappa(\theta,t)
which satisfies the closing condition (
2.3
) on some time interval.
Thus the flow (
2.1
) and also the flow (
1.1
) exist on a short time interval.

Lemma 2.1
.

There is a unique family of smooth convex curves evolving under the flow (
1.1
) on a time interval

[0,t)
, where
t
is positive.

Using the maximum principle, one can show that
\kappa
is always positive under the flow.
So the flow (
2.1
) preserves the convexity of the evolving curve.

Lemma 2.2
.

Under the flow (
2.1
), the evolving curve is convex.

Proof.

Suppose that the flow (
2.1
) exits on time interval
[0,\omega)
.
By the continuity of the evolving curve, it is strictly convex in a small time interval.
Suppose there is a positive
t_{0}<\omega
such that
\min_{\theta}\kappa(\theta)(t_{0})=0
but
X(\cdot,t)
is strictly convex when
t\in[0,t_{0})
.

By the continuity of
\kappa
on
[0,2\pi]\times[0,t_{0}]
, there exists a positive constant
M
such that
\max\{\kappa(\theta,t)|\theta\in[0,2\pi],t\in[0,t_{0}]\}\leq M
.
Define a function on the interval
[0,\omega)
as follows

\displaystyle f(t)=\frac{\min\limits_{\theta}\kappa_{0}(\theta)}{F(M)\min\limits_{\theta}\kappa_{0}(\theta)t+1}.

The positive function
f
satisfies that
f(0)=\min\limits_{\theta}\kappa_{0}(\theta)\leq\kappa_{0}(\theta)
and
f^{\prime}(t)=-F(M)f^{2}(t)
.
Define
h(\theta,t)=\kappa(\theta,t)-f(t)+A\varepsilon t+\varepsilon
on the domain
[0,2\pi]\times[0,t_{0})
,
where
\varepsilon>0
and
A=F(M)(M+\min\limits_{\theta}\kappa_{0}(\theta))
. By the evolution equation of
\kappa
,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial h}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\kappa^{2}\left[F^{\prime}(\kappa)\frac{\partial^{2}h}{\partial\theta^{2}}+F^{\prime\prime}(\kappa)\left(\frac{\partial h}{\partial\theta}\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle+\kappa^{2}F(\kappa)-\kappa^{2}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}F(\kappa)d\theta+F(M)f^{2}(t)+A\varepsilon.

Next, we prove that
h(\theta,t)>\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
holds for all
(\theta,t)\in[0,2\pi]\times[0,t_{0})
. Suppose there is a smallest
t_{1}\in(0,t_{0})
such
that
\min\limits_{\theta}h(\theta,t_{1})=\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
and
h(\theta,t)>\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
holds for all
(\theta,t)\in[0,2\pi]\times[0,t_{1})
.
By the continuity of
h
, there exists
\theta_{1}\in[0,2\pi]
such that
h(\theta_{1},t_{1})=\min\limits_{\theta}h(\theta,t_{1})
. At the point
(\theta_{1},t_{1})
, we have

\frac{\partial^{2}h}{\partial\theta^{2}}(\theta_{1},t_{1})\geq 0,\frac{\partial h}{\partial\theta}(\theta_{1},t_{1})=0
and

\frac{\partial h}{\partial t}(\theta_{1},t_{1})=\lim_{t\rightarrow t_{1}^{-}}\frac{h(\theta_{1},t)-h(\theta_{1},t_{1})}{t-t_{1}}\leq 0.

However, at the same point
(\theta_{1},t_{1})
, the R.H.S. of the evolution equation of
h
satisfies

\displaystyle\kappa^{2}F(\kappa)-\kappa^{2}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}F(\kappa)d\theta+F(M)f^{2}(t_{1})+A\varepsilon

\displaystyle\geq 0-\kappa^{2}F(M)+F(M)f^{2}(t_{1})+A\varepsilon

\displaystyle=-F(M)(\kappa+f(t_{1}))(h-A\varepsilon t_{1}-\varepsilon)+A\varepsilon

\displaystyle=-F(M)(\kappa+f(t_{1}))\frac{\varepsilon}{2}+A\varepsilon+F(M)(\kappa+f(t_{1}))(A\varepsilon t_{1}+\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\geq-F(M)(M+\min_{\theta}\kappa_{0}(\theta))\frac{\varepsilon}{2}+A\varepsilon>0.

This is contradict to the previous inequality
\frac{\partial h}{\partial t}(\theta_{1},t_{1})\leq 0
.
Therefore, we have proven the claim of
h
, which implies
\kappa(\theta,t)-f(t)+A\varepsilon t>\varepsilon/2
, if
(\theta,t)\in[0,2\pi]\times[0,t_{0})
.
By the arbitrariness of
\varepsilon
, one obtains that
\kappa(\theta,t)\geq f(t)
holds on the domain
[0,2\pi]\times[0,t_{0})
.
The continuity of
\kappa
tells us

\kappa(\theta,t_{0})\geq f(t_{0})=\frac{\min\limits_{\theta}\kappa_{0}(\theta)}{F(M)\min\limits_{\theta}\kappa_{0}(\theta)t_{0}+1}>0.

So the original assumption that
\kappa
attains 0 at the time
t_{0}
is not correct. The curvature is always positive for
t\in[0,\omega)
.
∎

The proof of Lemma
2.2
is inspired by the previous work
[
18
,
26
]
. Since the evolving curve is always
uniformly convex under the flow (
2.1
), from now on, one can use the tangent angle
\theta
to parameterize the curve
\widetilde{X}(\cdot,t)
.

2.2
Uniform bounds of the support function

Denote by
p:=-\langle\widetilde{X},N\rangle
the support function of the evolving curve. This function
plays an essential role in the proof of the global existence of the flow (
1.1
).

Lemma 2.3
.

Under the flow (
2.1
), the length of
\widetilde{X}(\cdot,t)
is preserved and the
bounded area
A(t)
is increasing.

Proof.

The proof is a direct computation (see also Remark 2.13 in the paper
[
20
]
). Under the flow (
2.1
), the length of the
evolving curve and its bounded area evolve according to next equations respectively.

\displaystyle\frac{dL}{dt}=-\int_{0}^{2\pi}(F(\kappa)-\lambda(t))d\theta=-\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left[F(\kappa)-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}F(\kappa)d\theta\right]d\theta\equiv 0,

\displaystyle\frac{dA}{dt}=-\int_{0}^{L}(F(\kappa)-\lambda(t))ds=-\int_{0}^{L}F(\kappa)ds+\frac{L}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{L}F(\kappa)\kappa ds.

Since
F
is strictly increasing, Andrews’ inequality (Lemma I3.3 of the paper
[
2
]
) implies that the
area
A(t)
is increasing.
∎

Let
I
be the isoperimetric ratio of a convex curve, i.e.,
I=\frac{L^{2}}{4\pi A}
. For this curve,
Bonnesen’s inequality
[
27
]
says

\displaystyle rL-A-\pi r^{2}\geq 0,~~~r\in[r_{in},r_{out}],

where
r_{in}
and
r_{out}
are the inradius and outradius
(circumradius) of the convex domain bounded by
X
, respectively.
It follows from Bonnesen’s inequality,

\displaystyle\frac{L-\sqrt{L^{2}-4\pi A}}{2\pi}\leq r_{in}\leq r_{out}\leq\frac{L+\sqrt{L^{2}-4\pi A}}{2\pi},

which implies

\displaystyle\frac{r_{out}}{r_{in}}\leq\frac{L+\sqrt{L^{2}-4\pi A}}{L-\sqrt{L^{2}-4\pi A}}=\left(\sqrt{I}+\sqrt{I-1}\right)^{2}.

Lemma
2.3
tells us that the function
I(t)
is decreasing under the flow (
1.1
).
In particular, we have

\displaystyle\frac{r_{out}(t)}{r_{in}(t)}\leq\left(\sqrt{I(0)}+\sqrt{I(0)-1}\right)^{2},~~t\in[0,\omega).

Noticing that
r_{out}(t)\geq\frac{L}{2\pi}
, we obtain a uniform lower bound of
r_{in}(t)
as follows

\displaystyle r_{in}(t)\geq\frac{L}{2\pi}\left(\sqrt{I(0)}+\sqrt{I(0)-1}\right)^{-2}>0,~~t\in[0,\omega).

(2.4)

Let
O
be the center of a maximum inscribed circle of
X_{0}
. Let
E_{0}
be a circle with fixed radius defined by

r_{0}:=\frac{L}{2\pi}\left(\sqrt{I(0)}+\sqrt{I(0)-1}\right)^{-2}

and centred at
O
. Shrink
E_{0}
via next flow

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{\partial Y}{\partial t}=F(\widetilde{\kappa})N_{in},\\
\\
Y(\cdot,0)=E_{0},\end{array}\right.

(2.5)

where
\widetilde{\kappa}
is the curvature of
Y
. By the maximum principle,
Y(\cdot,t)
is a family of circles.
Its radius
r=r(t)
satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{dr}{dt}=-F\left(\frac{1}{r}\right).

If
r(t)
is positive then
F\left(\frac{1}{r(t)}\right)>0
. By the evolution equation of
r(t)
, this function is strictly decreasing.
Define

T_{1}:=r_{0}\bigg/\left[2F\left(\frac{2}{r_{0}}\right)\right].

(2.6)

We will show that, if
t\in[0,T_{1})
, then

\displaystyle r(t)>\frac{r_{0}}{2}.

Assume that there is a positive
\widetilde{t}\in(0,T_{1})
such that
r(t)>\frac{r_{0}}{2}
holds
for
t\in(0,\widetilde{t})
and
r(\widetilde{t})=\frac{r_{0}}{2}
. Since
F
is increasing,
for time
t\in(0,\widetilde{t})
, one obtains

\displaystyle F\left(\frac{1}{r(t)}\right)<F\left(\frac{1}{\frac{r_{0}}{2}}\right)=F\left(\frac{2}{r_{0}}\right).

So, on the time interval
[0,\widetilde{t})
, the radius function satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{dr}{dt}>-F\left(\frac{2}{r_{0}}\right).

Integrating this inequality on time interval
[0,t)
gives us

\displaystyle r(t)\geq r_{0}-F\left(\frac{2}{r_{0}}\right)t.

(2.7)

Let
t
tend to
\widetilde{t}
. By the continuity of
r(t)
and the definitions of
\widetilde{t}
and
T_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle r(\widetilde{t})\geq r_{0}-F\left(\frac{2}{r_{0}}\right)\widetilde{t}>r_{0}-F\left(\frac{2}{r_{0}}\right)T_{1}=\frac{r_{0}}{2}.

This conflicts the existence of
\widetilde{t}
. Therefore,
r(t)\geq\frac{r_{0}}{2}

if
t\in[0,T_{1}]
.

Since
X(\cdot,t)
contains
Y(\cdot,t)
for each
t\in\left[0,\min\left\{T_{1},\omega\right\}\right)
, the support function has a lower bound

\displaystyle p(\theta,t)\geq r(t)\geq r(T_{1})\geq\frac{r_{0}}{2}

in the same time interval. Setting
\delta=\frac{r_{0}}{4}>0
and by the definition of the support function, we have, on the domain
[0,2\pi]\times\big[0,\min\left\{T_{1},\omega\right\}\big)
,
next estimate

\displaystyle 2\delta\leq p(\theta,t)\leq\frac{L}{2},

(2.8)

where the constants
\delta
and
L
only rely on the initial curve
X_{0}
.

2.3
Extending the flow globally

We argue by contradiction. Suppose the flow (
1.1
) exists on the maximal time interval
[0,\omega)
and
\omega
is finite.
Let
n
be the unique nonnegative integer such that
nT_{1}<\omega\leq(n+1)T_{1}
, where
T_{1}
is defined in the equation (
2.6
).
On each time interval

[0,T_{1}),[T_{1},2T_{1}),\cdots,[nT_{1},\omega),

one can choose an original point
O
such that the support function
p(\theta,t)
with respect to
O
satisfying (
2.8
).

On the above each time interval, let us consider the function

\varphi(\theta,t):=\frac{F(\kappa(\theta,t))}{p(\theta,t)-\delta}.

By some straightforward calculations, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial\theta}=\frac{1}{p-\delta}\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}-\frac{F}{(p-\delta)^{2}}\frac{\partial p}{\partial\theta},

and

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}\varphi}{\partial\theta^{2}}=\frac{1}{p-\delta}\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial\theta^{2}}-\frac{2}{(p-\delta)^{2}}\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}\frac{\partial p}{\partial\theta}-\frac{F}{(p-\delta)^{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}p}{\partial\theta^{2}}+\frac{2F}{(p-\delta)^{3}}\left(\frac{\partial p}{\partial\theta}\right)^{2}.

By the evolution equation of
\kappa
, the function
F
evolves according to

\displaystyle\frac{\partial F}{\partial t}=F^{\prime}\kappa^{2}\left[\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial\theta^{2}}+F-\lambda(t)\right].

(2.9)

It follows from the definition of the support function and the flow equation (
2.1
), the support function satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{\partial p}{\partial t}=\lambda(t)-F.

(2.10)

Direct calculations can give us the evolution equation of
\varphi(\theta,t)
:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p-\delta}\frac{\partial F}{\partial t}-\frac{F}{(p-\delta)^{2}}\frac{\partial p}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{F^{\prime}\kappa^{2}}{p-\delta}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial\theta^{2}}+F-\lambda(t)\right)-\frac{F}{(p-\delta)^{2}}(\lambda(t)-F)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F^{\prime}\kappa^{2}\left[\frac{\partial^{2}\varphi}{\partial\theta^{2}}+\frac{2}{(p-\delta)^{2}}\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}\frac{\partial p}{\partial\theta}+\frac{F}{(p-\delta)^{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}p}{\partial\theta^{2}}-\frac{2F}{(p-\delta)^{3}}\left(\frac{\partial p}{\partial\theta}\right)^{2}\right]

Lemma 2.4
.

There is a positive constant
M
independent of time
t

\theta

Consider the time interval

In the evolution equation of

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F^{\prime}\kappa^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}\varphi}{\partial\theta^{2}}+\frac{2F^{\prime}\kappa^{2}}{p-\delta}\frac{\partial p}{\partial\theta}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial\theta}-\frac{\lambda F^{\prime}\kappa^{2}}{p-\delta}-\frac{\lambda\varphi}{p-\delta}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}\right)^{5}\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial\theta\partial t}d\theta=-5\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}\right)^{4}\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial\theta^{2}}\frac{\partial F}{\partial t}d\theta

\displaystyle-5\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}\right)^{4}\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial\theta^{2}}F^{\prime}\kappa^{2}\left[\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial\theta^{2}}+F-\lambda(t)\right]d\theta

Remark 3.1

In the condition (i), we assume that

Suppose that the lower limit is positive.
There exist constants

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{\partial F}{\partial t}\left(\frac{1}{F^{\prime}(\kappa)\kappa^{2}}\frac{\partial F}{\partial t}-F+\lambda\right)d\theta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{1}{F^{\prime}(\kappa)\kappa^{2}}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial t}\right)^{2}d\theta+\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{2\pi}F^{2}d\theta-\lambda\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{2\pi}Fd\theta.

M_{i}
independent of time such that

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\partial^{i}\kappa}{\partial\theta^{i}}(\theta,t)\right|\leq M_{i},~~(\theta,t)\in[0,2\pi]\times(0,+\infty),~~i=1,2,3,\cdots.

(3.12)

From (
2.17
), (
3.10
), (
3.11
), (
3.12
) and the limit (
3.8
), one can conclude

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\partial^{i}\kappa}{\partial\theta^{i}}(\theta,t)=\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\partial^{i}F}{\partial\theta^{i}}(\theta,t)=0,~~i=1,2,\cdots.

(3.13)

This is the
C^{\infty}
convergence of the curvature.

4
Convergence of the evolving curve

Until now, the evolving curve has been proved asymptotically circular by the limit (
3.8
). In order to prove that the flow (
2.1
)
can efficiently evolve
X(\cdot,t)
into a circle, one needs to show that the evolving curve indeed has a limit as time
goes to infinity. In fact, if the speed of the flow (
2.1
) exponentially decays
then the evolving curve
X(\cdot,t)
converges to a fixed limiting curve as time goes to infinity.

By the evolution equation of
F
(see (
2.8
)), one may compute

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}\right)^{2}d\theta\ =\ -\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial\theta^{2}}\frac{\partial F}{\partial t}d\theta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial\theta^{2}}F^{\prime}\kappa^{2}\left[\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial\theta^{2}}+F-\lambda(t)\right]d\theta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{2\pi}F^{\prime}\kappa^{2}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial\theta^{2}}\right)^{2}d\theta+\int_{0}^{2\pi}F^{\prime}\kappa^{2}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}\right)^{2}d\theta

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{F^{\prime\prime}}{F^{\prime}}\kappa^{2}(F-\lambda)\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}\right)^{2}d\theta+2\int_{0}^{2\pi}\kappa(F-\lambda)\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}\right)^{2}d\theta.

The limit (
3.8
) says that the curvature converges to
2\pi/L
. So one obtians

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}F^{\prime}(\kappa)\cdot\kappa^{2}=F^{\prime}\left(\frac{2\pi}{L}\right)\cdot\left(\frac{2\pi}{L}\right)^{2}

and

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}(F(\kappa)-\lambda(t))=\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\left(F(\kappa)-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}F(\kappa)d\theta\right)=0.

For any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
T_{0}>0
such that, if
t>T_{0}
then
|F-\lambda|<\varepsilon
and

\displaystyle F^{\prime}\left(\frac{2\pi}{L}\right)\cdot\left(\frac{2\pi}{L}\right)^{2}-\varepsilon\leq F^{\prime}(\kappa)\cdot\kappa^{2}\leq F^{\prime}\left(\frac{2\pi}{L}\right)\cdot\left(\frac{2\pi}{L}\right)^{2}+\varepsilon.

So, one has the following estimate for large
t>T_{0}
,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}\right)^{2}d\theta

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\left(F^{\prime}\left(\frac{2\pi}{L}\right)\cdot\left(\frac{2\pi}{L}\right)^{2}-\varepsilon\right)\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial\theta^{2}}\right)^{2}d\theta

(4.1)

\displaystyle+\left(F^{\prime}\left(\frac{2\pi}{L}\right)\cdot\left(\frac{2\pi}{L}\right)^{2}+\varepsilon\right)\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}\right)^{2}d\theta

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{C_{2}}{c_{1}}M^{2}\varepsilon+2M\varepsilon\right)\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}\right)^{2}d\theta.

Since

\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}=F^{\prime}(\kappa)\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\left(\frac{1}{\rho}\right)=F^{\prime}(\kappa)\left(-\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial\theta}\right)

and

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial\theta^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F^{\prime\prime}(\kappa)\left(\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial\theta}\right)^{2}-F^{\prime}(\kappa)\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}\rho}{\partial\theta^{2}}+F^{\prime}(\kappa)\frac{2}{\rho^{3}}\left(\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial\theta}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\rho^{3}}\left(\frac{F^{\prime\prime}}{\rho}+2F^{\prime}\right)\left(\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial\theta}\right)^{2}-F^{\prime}(\kappa)\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}\rho}{\partial\theta^{2}},

one may compute

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial\theta^{2}}\right)^{2}d\theta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{1}{\rho^{6}}\left(\frac{F^{\prime\prime}}{\rho}+2F^{\prime}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial\theta}\right)^{4}d\theta+\int_{0}^{2\pi}(F^{\prime})^{2}\frac{1}{\rho^{4}}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}\rho}{\partial\theta^{2}}\right)^{2}d\theta

\displaystyle-2\int_{0}^{2\pi}F^{\prime}\frac{1}{\rho^{5}}\left(\frac{F^{\prime\prime}}{\rho}+2F^{\prime}\right)\left(\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial\theta}\right)^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}\rho}{\partial\theta^{2}}d\theta.

It follows from the convergence of
\kappa
that one has the convergence

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial\theta}=\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\partial^{2}\rho}{\partial\theta^{2}}=0.

So, there exists
\widetilde{\varepsilon}\in(0,\varepsilon)
such that, at large time,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial\theta^{2}}\right)^{2}d\theta

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left[\left(F^{\prime}\left(\frac{2\pi}{L}\right)\right)^{2}\left(\frac{2\pi}{L}\right)^{4}-\widetilde{\varepsilon}\right]\left(\frac{\partial^{2}\rho}{\partial\theta^{2}}\right)^{2}d\theta

\displaystyle-\widetilde{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial\theta}\right)^{2}d\theta.

Using next Wirtinger type inequality,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}\rho}{\partial\theta^{2}}\right)^{2}d\theta\geq 4\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial\theta}\right)^{2}d\theta

and considering at large time, one could estimate

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial\theta^{2}}\right)^{2}d\theta

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 4\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left[\left(F^{\prime}\left(\frac{2\pi}{L}\right)\right)^{2}\left(\frac{2\pi}{L}\right)^{4}-\frac{5\widetilde{\varepsilon}}{4}\right]\left(\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial\theta}\right)^{2}d\theta

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 4\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left[(F^{\prime}(\kappa))^{2}\frac{1}{\rho^{4}}-2\widetilde{\varepsilon}\right]\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial\theta}\right)^{2}d\theta.

Since
(F^{\prime}(\kappa))^{2}\frac{1}{\rho^{4}}
has uniform lower and upper bounds independent of time, one may choose
\widetilde{\varepsilon}

small enough such that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial\theta^{2}}\right)^{2}d\theta\geq 2\int_{0}^{2\pi}(F^{\prime}(\kappa))^{2}\frac{1}{\rho^{4}}\left(\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial\theta}\right)^{2}d\theta=2\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}\right)^{2}d\theta.

(4.2)

Substituting (
4.2
) into (
4.1
), one obtains

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}\right)^{2}d\theta\leq\left(-F^{\prime}\left(\frac{2\pi}{L}\right)\left(\frac{2\pi}{L}\right)^{2}+3\varepsilon+\frac{C_{2}}{c_{1}}M^{2}\varepsilon+2M\varepsilon\right)\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}\right)^{2}d\theta.

Let
\varepsilon>0
be small enough such that

3\varepsilon+\frac{C_{2}}{c_{1}}M^{2}\varepsilon+2M\varepsilon<\frac{1}{2}F^{\prime}(\frac{2\pi}{L})\left(\frac{2\pi}{L}\right)^{2}
. Then
one gets for large
t
,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}\right)^{2}d\theta\leq-\frac{1}{2}F^{\prime}\left(\frac{2\pi}{L}\right)\cdot\left(\frac{2\pi}{L}\right)^{2}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}\right)^{2}d\theta.

(4.3)

Thus
\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}\right)^{2}d\theta

decays exponentially.

Lemma 4.1
.

If
t
is large enough then the speed of the flow (
2.1
) decays exponentially.

Proof.

It follows from the classical Sobolev’s inequality, one has

\displaystyle|F-\lambda|\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(F-\lambda\right)^{2}d\theta\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\sqrt{2\pi}\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}\right)^{2}d\theta\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(4.4)

Since
\int_{0}^{2\pi}(F-\lambda)d\theta=0
, Wirtinger’s inequality implies that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}(F-\lambda)^{2}d\theta\leq\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}\right)^{2}d\theta.

(4.5)

Substituting (
4.5
) into (
4.4
) yields

\displaystyle|F-\lambda|\leq\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}+\sqrt{2\pi}\right)\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}\right)^{2}d\theta\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Since
\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}\right)^{2}d\theta

exponentially decays when
t>T_{0}
, one has shown that
|F-\lambda|
also has exponential decay.
∎

Theorem 4.2
.

Under the flow (
2.1
), the evolving curve converges to a limiting circle as time
goes to infinity.

Proof.

Since the speed
|F-\lambda|
of the flow decays exponentially if
t
is large enough, one could claim that the limit

\int_{0}^{t}(F-\lambda)d\tau
exists as
t\rightarrow+\infty
. Therefore, integrating the flow equation, one gets the the limit of the evolving curves as follows

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\widetilde{X}(\cdot,t)=X_{0}(\cdot)+\int_{0}^{+\infty}(F-\lambda)dt:=X_{\infty}(\cdot),

(4.6)

where
X_{0}
is the initial curve of the flow. Since the flow preserves the length of the evolving curve and the curvature
\kappa(\cdot,t)
converges to the
constant
\frac{2\pi}{L}
, the limiting curve
X_{\infty}
is a circle with radius
\frac{L}{2\pi}
.
∎

Until now, we have proved that both the curvature and the evolving curve converge as
t\rightarrow+\infty
.
So the proof of Theorem
1.1
is a combination of Theorem
2.5
,
Lemma
3.4
and Theorem
4.2
. Once the speed of the flow is proved decaying exponentially, the
C^{\infty}
convergence
of the flow is a straightforward computation as did in the papers
[
11
,
21
]
. For the sake of brevity, the related details are omitted.

5
Appendix: uniform gradient estimate

Let
X(\cdot,t):[0,2\pi]\times[0,+\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a global solution to the flow (
2.1
) with convex
initial curve
X_{0}
. By (
2.11
) and (
3.4
), the curvature
\kappa(\cdot,t)
has lower and upper
bounds independent of time, i.e.,

0<m\leq\kappa\leq M,~~~~(\theta,t)\in[0,2\pi]\times[0,+\infty).

Recalling (
3.5
) and (
3.6
), one has bounds of
F^{\prime}(\kappa)
as follows

0<c_{1}\leq F^{\prime}(\kappa)\leq C_{1},~~~~(\theta,t)\in[0,2\pi]\times[0,+\infty).

In this appendix, we show that there exists a constant
\widetilde{M}_{1}
independent of time such that

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}(\theta,t)\right|\leq\widetilde{M}_{1},~~(\theta,t)\in[0,2\pi]\times(0,+\infty).

Comparing to Lieberman’s theory of gradient estimate
[
19
]
, the method used here is more elementary.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
p\geq 2
be an integer. Under the flow (
2.1
), we have the estimate

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\frac{1}{2p}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}\right)^{2p}d\theta

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\frac{(2p-1)c_{1}m^{2}}{2p^{2}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}\right)^{2p}d\theta

(5.1)

\displaystyle+\frac{(2p-1)c_{1}m^{2}}{4\pi p^{2}}\left[\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}\right)^{p}d\theta\right]^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{(2p-1)C_{1}M^{2}F(M)^{2}}{2}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}\right)^{2p-2}d\theta.

Proof.

We compute by the evolution equation of
F(\kappa(\theta,t))
(see (
2.9
)),

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\frac{1}{2p}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}\right)^{2p}d\theta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}\right)^{2p-1}\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial\theta\partial t}d\theta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(2p-1)\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}\right)^{2p-2}\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial\theta^{2}}\frac{\partial F}{\partial t}d\theta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(2p-1)\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}\right)^{2p-2}\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial\theta^{2}}F^{\prime}\kappa^{2}\left[\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial\theta^{2}}+F-\lambda(t)\right]d\theta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(2p-1)\int_{0}^{2\pi}F^{\prime}\kappa^{2}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}\right)^{2p-2}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial\theta^{2}}\right)^{2}d\theta

\displaystyle-(2p-1)\int_{0}^{2\pi}F^{\prime}\kappa^{2}(F-\lambda(t))\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}\right)^{2p-2}\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial\theta^{2}}d\theta.

Applying Cauchy-Schwarz Inequality, one gets

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\frac{1}{2p}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}\right)^{2p}d\theta

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\frac{2p-1}{2}\int_{0}^{2\pi}F^{\prime}\kappa^{2}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}\right)^{2p-2}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial\theta^{2}}\right)^{2}d\theta

(5.2)

\displaystyle+\frac{(2p-1)}{2}\int_{0}^{2\pi}F^{\prime}\kappa^{2}(F-\lambda(t))^{2}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}\right)^{2p-2}d\theta.

Using the lower bounds of
\kappa
and
F^{\prime}(\kappa)
, one may estimate the first integral in the R.H.S. of (
5.2
) as

\displaystyle-\frac{2p-1}{2}\int_{0}^{2\pi}F^{\prime}\kappa^{2}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}\right)^{2p-2}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial\theta^{2}}\right)^{2}d\theta

\displaystyle~~=-\frac{2p-1}{2p^{2}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}F^{\prime}\kappa^{2}\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}\right)^{p}\right]^{2}d\theta

\displaystyle~~\leq-\frac{(2p-1)c_{1}m^{2}}{2p^{2}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}\right)^{p}\right]^{2}d\theta.

Let
f=f(\theta)
be a periodic and
C^{1}
function on
[0,2\pi]
. We have another Wirtinger’s inequality for the function
f
:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial\theta}\right)^{2}d\theta

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}f^{2}d\theta-\frac{1}{2\pi}\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}fd\theta\right)^{2}.

(5.3)

By using (
5.3
), one may continue to estimate

\displaystyle-\frac{2p-1}{2}\int_{0}^{2\pi}F^{\prime}\kappa^{2}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}\right)^{2p-2}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial\theta^{2}}\right)^{2}d\theta

\displaystyle~~\leq-\frac{(2p-1)c_{1}m^{2}}{2p^{2}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}\right)^{2p}d\theta

\displaystyle~~~~+\frac{(2p-1)c_{1}m^{2}}{4\pi p^{2}}\left[\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}\right)^{p}d\theta\right]^{2}.

(5.4)

Substituting (
5.3
) and (
5.4
) into (
5.2
) yields (
5.1
).
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Both
\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}\right)^{4}d\theta
and
\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}\right)^{6}d\theta

have uniform upper bounds independent of time.

Proof.

Set
p=2
in the equation (
5.1
). Then one has

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}\right)^{4}d\theta

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\frac{3c_{1}m^{2}}{8}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}\right)^{4}d\theta

(5.5)

\displaystyle+\frac{3c_{1}m^{2}}{16\pi}\left[\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}\right)^{2}d\theta\right]^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{3C_{1}M^{2}F(M)^{2}}{2}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}\right)^{2}d\theta.

Since
\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}\right)^{2}d\theta
is bounded uniformly on time interval
[0,+\infty)

(see (
3.2
)), the inequality (
5.5
) implies that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}\right)^{4}d\theta\leq-\frac{3c_{1}m^{2}}{8}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}\right)^{4}d\theta+A_{0},

where
A_{0}
is a constant independent of time. Integrating this inequality gives us

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}\right)^{4}d\theta\leq\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial F_{0}}{\partial\theta}\right)^{4}d\theta+\frac{8A_{0}}{3c_{1}m^{2}}.

The integral
\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}\right)^{4}d\theta
has a uniformly upper bound independent of time.

Set
p=3
in the equation (
5.1
). One gets

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\frac{1}{6}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}\right)^{6}d\theta

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\frac{5c_{1}m^{2}}{18}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}\right)^{6}d\theta

(5.6)

\displaystyle+\frac{5c_{1}m^{2}}{36\pi}\left[\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}\right)^{3}d\theta\right]^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{5C_{1}M^{2}F(M)^{2}}{2}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}\right)^{4}d\theta.

By Holder’s inequality, one may estimate

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}\right)^{3}d\theta\leq(2\pi)^{1/4}\left[\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}\right)^{4}d\theta\right]^{3/4}.

So the integral
\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}\right)^{3}d\theta
also has a uniformly upper bound independent of time.
Now (
5.6
) implies

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\frac{1}{6}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}\right)^{6}d\theta\leq-\frac{5c_{1}m^{2}}{18}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}\right)^{6}d\theta+B_{0},

(5.7)

where
B_{0}
is a constant independent of time. Integrating this inequality gives us a uniformly upper bound of
the integral
\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}\right)^{6}d\theta
.
∎

Theorem 5.3
.

Under the global flow (
2.1
), the derivative
|\frac{\partial F(\kappa(\theta,t))}{\partial\theta}|
has an upper bound on the domain
[0,2\pi]\times(0,+\infty)
.

Proof.

Recalling in the Subsection
2.4
, we have shown that, for small
\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial\theta^{2}}\right)^{2}d\theta

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(A_{1}\varepsilon-F^{\prime}\kappa^{2}\right)\left(\frac{\partial^{3}F}{\partial\theta^{3}}\right)^{2}d\theta+A_{2}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}\right)^{6}d\theta

\displaystyle+A_{3}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}\right)^{2}d\theta,

where
A_{1}=A_{1}(c_{1},C_{1},C_{2}),A_{2}=A_{2}(\varepsilon,c_{1},C_{1},C_{2})
and
A_{3}=A_{3}(\varepsilon,c_{1},C_{1},C_{2})
are constants independent of time.

Applying Wirtinger’s inequality (
5.3
), one gets

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial^{3}F}{\partial\theta^{3}}\right)^{2}d\theta\geq\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial\theta^{2}}\right)^{2}d\theta.

Choosing
\varepsilon
small enough, one obtains from Lemma
5.2
,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial\theta^{2}}\right)^{2}d\theta

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\frac{c_{1}m^{2}}{2}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial\theta^{2}}\right)^{2}d\theta+B_{1},

where
B_{1}
is a constant independent of time. Integrating this inequality yields an upper bound of

\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial\theta^{2}}\right)^{2}d\theta
on time interval
(0,+\infty)
.

Since both the integrals
\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}\right)^{2}d\theta
and
\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial\theta^{2}}\right)^{2}d\theta

are bounded for
t\in[0,+\infty)
, Sobolev’s inequality tells us that the derivative

|\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}|
is also uniformly bounded.
∎

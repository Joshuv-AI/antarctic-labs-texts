1.
Introduction

In this paper, we estimate small ball probabilities for the solution of the stochastic heat equation of the type:

(1.1)

\partial_{t}u(t,x)=\partial^{2}_{x}u(t,x)+\sigma(t,x,u)\dot{F}(t,x)

where
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
x\in\mathbb{T}:=\left[-1,1\right]
, which denotes the 1-dimensional torus in the usual way. The centered Gaussian noise
\dot{F}
is white in time and homogeneous in space, i.e.,

\mathbb{E}\left[\dot{F}(t,x)\dot{F}(s,y)\right]=\delta_{0}(t-s)\Lambda(x-y),

where
\delta_{0}
is the Dirac delta generalized function and
\Lambda:\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is a non-negative generalized function whose Fourier series is given by

(1.2)

\Lambda(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}q_{n}\exp(n\pi ix).

In this article, we focus on an analogue of the Riesz kernel on
\mathbb{R}
. To be more precise, for
\gamma\in(0,1)
, there exist two positive constants
C
and
C^{\prime}
such that

(1.3)

q_{n}=\begin{cases}C&\text{if~$n=0$};\\
C^{\prime}|n|^{-(1-\gamma)}&\text{if~$n\neq 0$}.\end{cases}

Moreover,
[
Bro83
]
, Lemma 2.9, proved that there exists a constant
C(\gamma)>0
such that the spatial covariance function
\Lambda(x)
admits the following estimate:

(1.4)

\Lambda(x)\leq C(\gamma)|x|^{-\gamma},\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{T}.

Small ball problems have a long history, and one can see
[
LS01
]
for more surveys. In short, we are interested in the probability that a stochastic process
X_{t}
starting at 0 stays in a small ball for a long time period, i.e.,

P\left(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|X_{t}|<\varepsilon\right),

where
\varepsilon>0
is small. A recent paper
[
AJM21
]
studied small ball probabilities for the solution to this type of stochastic heat equation driven by a space-time white noise and another paper
[
FJK22
]
considered small ball probabilities for the solution to the same stochastic heat equation but under a Hölder semi-norm. The objective of this paper is to continue this topic with a white-in-time but colored-in-space noise.

Thanks to the Da Prato–Kwapien–Zabczyk’s factorization and the assumption (
2.1
) and (
2.2
), we may estimate the regularities of the stochastic term driven by a spatially homogeneous noise, which allows us to prove the critical tail bounds on the noise term as shown in Lemma
4.4
.

Regarding the temporal coefficient
c_{0}
in
[
AJM21
]
, it was demonstrated that it attains a uniform bound due to the exponential decay of the correlation between two points on the spatial interval as they separate. The correlation decays much slower in the case of spatially homogeneous noise. We therefore cannot bound
c_{0}
uniformly, but we could control
c_{0}
in terms of
\varepsilon
indicated in (
3.1
).

2.
Main Result

We write
u_{0}(x):=u(0,x)
and assume that
\sigma
is Lipschitz continuous in the third variable, i.e., there exists a constant
\mathcal{D}>0
such that for all
t\geq 0
,
x\in\mathbb{T}
,
u,v\in\mathbb{R}
,

(2.1)

|\sigma(t,x,u)-\sigma(t,x,v)|\leq\mathcal{D}|u-v|,

and
\sigma
is uniformly bounded, i.e., there exist constants
\mathcal{C}_{1}
,
\mathcal{C}_{2}>0
such that for all
t\geq 0
,
x\in\mathbb{T}
,
u\in\mathbb{R}
,

(2.2)

\mathcal{C}_{1}\leq\sigma(t,x,u)\leq\mathcal{C}_{2}.

Assumption (
2.2
) assures that
\sigma
is non-zero, which is helpful for us to control the variance of noise terms. In fact, (
1.1
) is not well-posed since the solution
u
is not differentiable and
\dot{F}
exists as a generalized function. However, the existence and uniqueness of the mild solution
u(t,x)
to (1.1) under the above assumptions are well known as follow,

(2.3)

\begin{split}u(t,x)&=\int_{\mathbb{T}}G(t,x-y)u_{0}(y)dy\\
&\hskip 85.35826pt+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}G(t-s,x-y)\sigma(s,y,u(s,y))F(dyds),\end{split}

where
G:\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{R}
is the fundamental solution of the heat equation on
\mathbb{T}
,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}

\displaystyle G(t,x)=\partial_{x}^{2}G(t,x),

\displaystyle G(0,x)=\delta_{0}(x).

See for example
[
Wal86
]
,
[
Dal99
]
,
[
DKM+09
]
, and
[
TV99
]
for the proofs and various other properties.
Now we are ready to state the main theorem.

Theorem 2.1
.

Under the assumptions (
2.1
) and (
2.2
), and a given parameter
\gamma
in spatial covariance function defined in (
1.2
) and (
1.3
), if
u(t,x)
is the solution to (
1.1
) with
u_{0}(x)\equiv 0
, then there are positive constants
\textbf{C}_{0},\textbf{C}_{1},\textbf{C}_{2},\textbf{C}_{3},\mathcal{D}_{0}
and
\varepsilon_{0}
depending only on
\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}
and
\gamma
, such that for all
\mathcal{D}<\mathcal{D}_{0},0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
and
T>1
, we have

\textbf{C}_{0}\exp\left(-\frac{\textbf{C}_{1}T}{\varepsilon^{\frac{8-2\gamma}{2-\gamma}}}\right)\leq P\left(\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq t\leq T\\
x\in\mathbb{T}\end{subarray}}|u(t,x)|\leq\varepsilon\right)\leq\textbf{C}_{2}\exp\left(-\frac{\textbf{C}_{3}T}{\varepsilon^{\frac{2\gamma+4}{2-\gamma}}}\right).

Here we make a couple of open problems. These could be of independent interests.

(a)

When
\gamma=1
,
\Lambda(x)=\delta_{0}(x)
and
\dot{F}
becomes the space-time white noise. One reference paper
[
Bez16
]
has proved the weak convergence of a probability measure corresponding to the solution of the nonlinear stochastic heat equation with colored noise. Therefore the result from this paper can be converted to the white noise case in
[
AJM21
]
as
\gamma\uparrow 1
. The open problem is the remaining part where
\gamma\downarrow 0
. When
\gamma=0
and
x\neq 0
,
\Lambda(x)
is a constant and
\dot{F}
becomes a smooth noise. Can we also get this case by taking a limit as
\gamma\downarrow 0
?

(b)

When
\gamma=1
, both lower and upper bounds include a term
\varepsilon^{-6}
in the exponent. However, in other cases, there exists a discrepancy between the exponents of
\varepsilon
in both lower and upper bounds, due to the fact that
c_{0}
depends on
\varepsilon
, as defined in (
3.1
).

(c)

One may extend this result to other white-in-time and colored-in-space noises whose correlation kernel differs from
\Lambda(x)
. However, the problem gets more complicated if the noise becomes fractional in time since we can not apply the Markov property, which plays an essential role in the proof.

For the upper bound of Theorem
2.1
, we use a perturbation argument to approximate the solution
u
by a Gaussian random field in a small region, accompanied by the proof of the bounds of a Gaussian process. In addition, we apply the Gaussian correlation inequality and a change of measure argument similar to
[
AJM21
]
to determine the lower bound for a Gaussian process. Furthermore, we demonstrate that the error in the approximation can be effectively controlled if the time interval where the coefficient is frozen is appropriately selected. We also make key use of the Markov property of (
1.1
) regarding the time parameter
t
. Thanks to this property, we can reduce our analysis to the behavior of this non-Gaussian solution in tiny time intervals.

Here is the organization of the rest of this paper. In section
3
, we present Proposition
3.1
and its connection to Theorem
2.1
. We provide some useful estimates in section
4
, and the proof of Proposition
3.1
in section
5
completes the paper.

Throughout the entire article,
C
and
C^{\prime}
denote positive constants whose values may vary from line to line. The dependence of constants on parameters will be denoted by mentioning the parameters in parentheses.

3.
Key Proposition

We decompose
[-1,1]
into subintervals of length
\varepsilon^{2}
and divide
[0,T]
into intervals of length
c_{0}\varepsilon^{4}
where
c_{0}
denotes the temporal coefficient satisfying

(3.1)

c_{0}=\mathcal{C}_{0}\varepsilon^{\frac{4-4\gamma}{\gamma}},

where
0<\mathcal{C}_{0}<\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{C}
is specified in the proof of Lemma
5.2
.

Remark 3.1
.

Unlike the space-time white noise case in
[
AJM21
]
,
c_{0}
needs to be selected depending on
\varepsilon
due to the large correlation between pairs of points.
c_{0}
does not appear in either the upper bound nor lower bound for small ball probabilities.

We define
t_{i}=ic_{0}\varepsilon^{4},x_{j}=j\varepsilon^{2}
where
i\in\mathbb{N}
and
j\in\mathbb{Z}
, and

n_{1}:=\min\left\{n\in\mathbb{Z}:n\varepsilon^{2}>1\right\}.

It is clear that
x_{n_{1}}>1
and
x_{n_{1}-1}\leq 1
, therefore, by symmetry, the point
x_{j}
lies in
\left[-1,1\right]
if

(3.2)

-n_{1}+1\leq j\leq n_{1}-1.

For
n\geq 0
, we define the following sequence of events to determine the upper bound in Theorem
2.1
,

(3.3)

F_{n}=\left\{|u(t_{n},x_{j})|\leq t_{1}^{\frac{2-\gamma}{4}}\text{~for all $-n_{1}+1\leq j\leq n_{1}-1$}\right\},

which is the event that the magnitude of the solution is small (recall that
t_{1}=c_{0}\varepsilon^{4}
) at time
t_{n}
for each grid point.
In addition, let
E_{-1}=\Omega
. For
n\geq 0
, we define the following sequence of events to determine the lower bound in Theorem
2.1
,

(3.4)

E_{n}=\left\{|u(t_{n+1},x)|\leq\frac{\mathcal{C}_{3}}{3}t_{1}^{\frac{2-\gamma}{4}}~\text{and}~|u(t,x)|\leq\mathcal{C}_{3}t_{1}^{\frac{2-\gamma}{4}}~\forall t\in[t_{n},t_{n+1}],x\in[-1,1]\right\},

which is the event that the magnitude of the solution is small at any time in
[t_{n},t_{n+1}]
for any points within
[-1,1]
and even smaller at terminal time
t_{n+1}
.
\mathcal{C}_{3}
is a positive constant such that

(3.5)

\mathcal{C}_{3}>6\mathcal{C}_{2}\sqrt{\frac{\ln C_{5}}{C_{6}}},

where
\mathcal{C}_{2}
is the uniform bound of
\sigma
in (
2.2
) and
C_{5},C_{6}
are positive constants defined in Lemma
4.4
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Consider the solution to (
1.1
) with
u_{0}(x)\equiv 0
. Then, there exist positive constants
\textbf{C}_{4},\textbf{C}_{5},\textbf{C}_{6},\textbf{C}_{7},\mathcal{D}_{0}
and
\varepsilon_{1}
depending solely on
\mathcal{C}_{1}
,
\mathcal{C}_{2}
and
\gamma
such that for any
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{1}
and
\mathcal{D}<\mathcal{D}_{0}
, we have

(a)

P\left(F_{n}\bigg|\bigcap_{k=0}^{n-1}F_{k}\right)\leq{\bf C_{4}}\exp\left(-\frac{{\bf C_{5}}}{t_{1}^{\gamma/2}}\right),

(b)

P\left(E_{n}\bigg|\bigcap_{k=-1}^{n-1}E_{k}\right)\geq{\bf C_{6}}\exp\left(-\frac{{\bf C_{7}}}{t_{1}^{1-\gamma/2}}\right).

We then demonstrate how Theorem
2.1
results from Proposition
3.1
.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
:
The event
F_{n}
in
(
3.3
)
deals with the behavior of
u(t,x)
at time
t_{n}
, thus combining these events together indicates

F:=\bigcap_{n=0}^{\left\lfloor\frac{T}{t_{1}}\right\rfloor}F_{n}\supset\left\{|u(t,x)|\leq t_{1}^{\frac{2-\gamma}{4}},t\in[0,T],x\in\mathbb{T}\right\},

and

P(F)=P\left(\bigcap_{n=0}^{\left\lfloor\frac{T}{t_{1}}\right\rfloor}F_{n}\right)=P(F_{0})\prod_{n=1}^{\left\lfloor\frac{T}{t_{1}}\right\rfloor}P\left(F_{n}\bigg|\bigcap_{k=0}^{n-1}F_{k}\right).

With
u_{0}(x)\equiv 0
,
F_{0}=\Omega
, and Proposition
3.1
(a) immediately yields

\displaystyle P(F)

\displaystyle\leq\left[\textbf{C}_{4}\exp\left(-\frac{\textbf{C}_{5}}{t_{1}^{\gamma/2}}\right)\right]^{\left\lfloor\frac{T}{t_{1}}\right\rfloor}\leq\textbf{C}_{2}\exp\left(-\frac{\textbf{C}_{3}T}{t_{1}^{1+\gamma/2}}\right).

Therefore we have

P\left(\left\{|u(t,x)|\leq t_{1}^{\frac{2-\gamma}{4}},t\in[0,T],x\in\mathbb{T}\right\}\right)\leq\textbf{C}_{2}\exp\left(-\frac{\textbf{C}_{3}T}{t_{1}^{1+\gamma/2}}\right),

and replacing
t_{1}^{\frac{2-\gamma}{4}}
with
\varepsilon_{2}
gives

(3.6)

P\left(\left\{|u(t,x)|\leq\varepsilon_{2},t\in[0,T],x\in\mathbb{T}\right\}\right)\leq\textbf{C}_{2}\exp\left(-\frac{\textbf{C}_{3}T}{\varepsilon_{2}^{\frac{4+2\gamma}{2-\gamma}}}\right).

We derive that, from (
3.1
),

\varepsilon_{2}=t_{1}^{\frac{2-\gamma}{4}}=\left(c_{0}\varepsilon^{4}\right)^{\frac{2-\gamma}{4}}=\left(\mathcal{C}_{0}\varepsilon^{\frac{4}{\gamma}}\right)^{\frac{2-\gamma}{4}}<\left(\mathcal{C}_{0}\varepsilon_{1}^{\frac{4}{\gamma}}\right)^{\frac{2-\gamma}{4}},

so (
3.6
) provides the upper bound in Theorem
2.1
whenever
\varepsilon_{0}<\left(\mathcal{C}_{0}\varepsilon_{1}^{\frac{4}{\gamma}}\right)^{\frac{2-\gamma}{4}}
.

On the other hand, the event
E_{n}
in
(
3.4
)
deals with the behavior of
u(t,x)
at any time between
t_{n}
and
t_{n+1}
, thus combining these events together indicates

E:=\bigcap_{n=-1}^{\left\lfloor\frac{T}{t_{1}}\right\rfloor}E_{n}\subset\left\{|u(t,x)|\leq\mathcal{C}_{3}t_{1}^{\frac{2-\gamma}{4}},t\in[0,T],x\in\mathbb{T}\right\},

and

P(E)=P\left(\bigcap_{n=-1}^{\left\lfloor\frac{T}{t_{1}}\right\rfloor}E_{n}\right)=P(E_{-1})\prod_{n=0}^{\left\lfloor\frac{T}{t_{1}}\right\rfloor}P\left(E_{n}\bigg|\bigcap_{k=-1}^{n-1}E_{k}\right).

With
u_{0}(x)\equiv 0
and
E_{-1}=\Omega
, Proposition
3.1
(b) immediately yields

P(E)\geq\left[\textbf{C}_{6}\exp\left(-\frac{\textbf{C}_{7}}{t_{1}^{1-\gamma/2}}\right)\right]^{\left\lfloor\frac{T}{t_{1}}\right\rfloor+1}\geq\textbf{C}_{0}\exp\left(-\frac{\textbf{C}_{1}T}{t_{1}^{2-\gamma/2}}\right).

Therefore we have

P\left(\left\{|u(t,x)|\leq\mathcal{C}_{3}t_{1}^{\frac{2-\gamma}{4}},t\in[0,T],x\in\mathbb{T}\right\}\right)\geq\textbf{C}_{0}\exp\left(-\frac{\textbf{C}_{1}T}{t_{1}^{2-\gamma/2}}\right),

and replacing
\mathcal{C}_{3}t_{1}^{\frac{2-\gamma}{4}}
with
\varepsilon_{3}
gives

(3.7)

P\left(\left\{|u(t,x)|\leq\varepsilon_{3},t\in[0,T],x\in\mathbb{T}\right\}\right)\geq\textbf{C}_{0}\exp\left(-\frac{\textbf{C}_{1}T}{\varepsilon_{3}^{\frac{8-2\gamma}{(2-\gamma)}}}\right).

We derive that, from (
3.1
),

\varepsilon_{3}=\mathcal{C}_{3}t_{1}^{\frac{2-\gamma}{4}}=\mathcal{C}_{3}\left(c_{0}\varepsilon^{4}\right)^{\frac{2-\gamma}{4}}=\mathcal{C}_{3}\left(\mathcal{C}_{0}\varepsilon^{\frac{4}{\gamma}}\right)^{\frac{2-\gamma}{4}}<\mathcal{C}_{3}\left(\mathcal{C}_{0}\varepsilon_{1}^{\frac{4}{\gamma}}\right)^{\frac{2-\gamma}{4}},

so (
3.7
) provides the lower bound in Theorem
2.1
whenever
\varepsilon_{0}<\mathcal{C}_{3}\left(\mathcal{C}_{0}\varepsilon_{1}^{\frac{4}{\gamma}}\right)^{\frac{2-\gamma}{4}}
.

With such an
\varepsilon_{0}<\left(\mathcal{C}_{0}\varepsilon_{1}^{\frac{4}{\gamma}}\right)^{\frac{2-\gamma}{4}}\cdot\min\left\{\mathcal{C}_{3},1\right\}
, we conclude Theorem
2.1
from Proposition
3.1
, and the rest of this paper is devoted to the proof of Proposition
3.1
.

4.
Preliminaries

In this section, we provide some preliminary results that are used to prove Proposition
3.1
.

4.1.
Heat Kernel Estimates

In this subsection, we prove some results related to the heat kernel
G(t,x)
. To simplify our computation, we write
G_{t}(x):=G(t,x)
and recall that the explicit form for the heat kernel on
\mathbb{T}
is given by

G_{t}(x)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(4\pi t)^{-1/2}\exp\left(-\frac{(x+2n)^{2}}{4t}\right).

The following statement has been proven for
x\in\mathbb{R}
in the paper
[
SSS02
]
. Here we prove for the case when
x\in\mathbb{T}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

There exist positive constants
C(\alpha,\xi)
and
C(\alpha,\zeta)
such that for all
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
\xi\in(0,2\alpha\wedge 1)
,
\zeta\in(0,\alpha)
,
0<s<t\leq 1
and
x,y\in\mathbb{T}
, we have

(4.1)

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{-1}^{1}|G_{t-r}(x-z)-G_{t-r}(y-z)|(t-r)^{\alpha-1}dzdr\leq C(\alpha,\xi)|x-y|^{\xi},

(4.2)

\int_{0}^{s}\int_{-1}^{1}\left|G_{t-r}(x-z)(t-r)^{\alpha-1}-G_{s-r}(x-z)(s-r)^{\alpha-1}\right|dzdr\leq C(\alpha,\zeta)|t-s|^{\zeta}.

Proof.

For any
\xi\in(0,2\alpha\wedge 1)
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}|G_{t-r}(x-z)-G_{t-r}(y-z)|(t-r)^{\alpha-1}dz

\displaystyle\leq 2^{1-\xi}(t-r)^{\alpha-1-\frac{\xi}{2}}\left(\int_{-1}^{1}\left|\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left[\exp\left(-\frac{(x+2n-z)^{2}}{4(t-r)}\right)\right.\right.\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 199.16928pt\left.\left.\left.-\exp\left(-\frac{(y+2n-z)^{2}}{4(t-r)}\right)\right]\right|dz\right)^{\xi}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{1-\xi}(t-r)^{\alpha-1-\frac{\xi}{2}}\left(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{-1}^{1}\left|\exp\left(-\frac{(x+2n-z)^{2}}{4(t-r)}\right)\right.\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 199.16928pt\left.\left.-\exp\left(-\frac{(y+2n-z)^{2}}{4(t-r)}\right)\right|dz\right)^{\xi}

\displaystyle=2^{1-\xi}(t-r)^{\alpha-1-\frac{\xi}{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\exp\left(-\frac{(x-z)^{2}}{4(t-r)}\right)-\exp\left(-\frac{(y-z)^{2}}{4(t-r)}\right)\right|dz\right)^{\xi}

\displaystyle\leq C(\xi)(t-r)^{\alpha-1-\frac{\xi}{2}}|x-y|^{\xi}.

The first inequality follows that the integral
\int_{-1}^{1}|G_{t-r}(x-z)-G_{t-r}(y-z)|dz
is bounded above by
2
. The last inequality is obtained by applying the Mean Value theorem to the function
x\to\exp\left(-\frac{(x-z)^{2}}{4(t-r)}\right)
, so there exists
x^{\prime}\in(x,y)
such that

\left|\exp\left(-\frac{(x-z)^{2}}{4(t-r)}\right)-\exp\left(-\frac{(y-z)^{2}}{4(t-r)}\right)\right|=\frac{1}{2}|x-y|\exp\left(-\frac{(x^{\prime}-z)^{2}}{4(t-r)}\right)\cdot\frac{|x^{\prime}-z|}{(t-r)}.

Because
x\leq\exp\left(\frac{x^{2}}{2}\right)
for
\forall x\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\frac{|x^{\prime}-z|}{2(t-r)}=\frac{|x^{\prime}-z|}{2(t-r)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\cdot\frac{1}{(t-r)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\leq\exp\left(\frac{(x^{\prime}-z)^{2}}{8(t-r)}\right)\cdot\frac{1}{(t-r)^{\frac{1}{2}}},

which implies

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\exp\left(-\frac{(x-z)^{2}}{4(t-r)}\right)-\exp\left(-\frac{(y-z)^{2}}{4(t-r)}\right)\right|dz\\
&\hskip 113.81102pt=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|x-y|\exp\left(-\frac{(x^{\prime}-z)^{2}}{4(t-r)}\right)\cdot\frac{|x^{\prime}-z|}{2(t-r)}dz\\
&\hskip 113.81102pt\leq|x-y|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\exp\left(-\frac{(x^{\prime}-z)^{2}}{8(t-r)}\right)\cdot\frac{1}{(t-r)^{\frac{1}{2}}}dz=C|x-y|.\end{split}

Therefore, we derive

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{-1}^{1}|G_{t-r}(x-z)-G_{t-r}(y-z)|(t-r)^{\alpha-1}dzdr\\
\leq\int_{0}^{t}C(\xi)(t-r)^{\alpha-1-\frac{\xi}{2}}|x-y|^{\xi}dr\leq C(\alpha,\xi)t^{\alpha-\frac{\xi}{2}}|x-y|^{\xi}\leq C(\alpha,\xi)|x-y|^{\xi},

which completes the proof of (
4.1
).

For (
4.2
), we decompose the integral into two parts,

(4.3)

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{s}\int_{-1}^{1}\left|\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left[\exp\left(-\frac{(x+2n-z)^{2}}{4(t-r)}\right)(t-r)^{\alpha-1-\frac{1}{2}}\right.\right.\\
&\hskip 170.71652pt\left.\left.-\exp\left(-\frac{(x+2n-z)^{2}}{4(s-r)}\right)(s-r)^{\alpha-1-\frac{1}{2}}\right]\right|dzdr\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\exp\left(-\frac{(x-z)^{2}}{4(t-r)}\right)(t-r)^{\alpha-1-\frac{1}{2}}-\exp\left(-\frac{(x-z)^{2}}{4(s-r)}\right)(s-r)^{\alpha-1-\frac{1}{2}}\right|dzdr\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\exp\left(-\frac{(x-z)^{2}}{4(t-r)}\right)(t-r)^{\alpha-1-\frac{1}{2}}-\exp\left(-\frac{(x-z)^{2}}{4(s-r)}\right)(t-r)^{\alpha-1-\frac{1}{2}}\right|dzdr\\
&+\int_{0}^{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\exp\left(-\frac{(x-z)^{2}}{4(s-r)}\right)(t-r)^{\alpha-1-\frac{1}{2}}-\exp\left(-\frac{(x-z)^{2}}{4(s-r)}\right)(s-r)^{\alpha-1-\frac{1}{2}}\right|dzdr.\end{split}

For any
\zeta\in(0,\alpha)
, we have

(4.4)

\begin{split}&\left|\exp\left(-\frac{(x-z)^{2}}{4(t-r)}\right)-\exp\left(-\frac{(x-z)^{2}}{4(s-r)}\right)\right|\\
&\hskip 142.26378pt\leq\left|\exp\left(-\frac{(x-z)^{2}}{4(t-r)}\right)-\exp\left(-\frac{(x-z)^{2}}{4(s-r)}\right)\right|^{\zeta}.\end{split}

Apply the Mean Value theorem to function
t\to\exp\left(-\frac{(x-z)^{2}}{4(t-r)}\right)
, and there exists
t^{\prime}\in(s,t)
such that

\displaystyle\left|\exp\left(-\frac{(x-z)^{2}}{4(t-r)}\right)-\exp\left(-\frac{(x-z)^{2}}{4(s-r)}\right)\right|=(t-s)\exp\left(-\frac{(x-z)^{2}}{4(t^{\prime}-r)}\right)\cdot\frac{(x-z)^{2}}{4(t^{\prime}-r)^{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt=2(t-s)(t^{\prime}-r)^{-1}\exp\left(-\frac{(x-z)^{2}}{4(t^{\prime}-r)}\right)\cdot\frac{(x-z)^{2}}{8(t^{\prime}-r)}

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt\leq 2(t-s)(t^{\prime}-r)^{-1}\exp\left(-\frac{(x-z)^{2}}{8(t^{\prime}-r)}\right).

The last inequality follows from the property
x\leq\exp(x)
for
\forall x\in\mathbb{R}
. Plug the above into the RHS of (
4.4
) to parametrize the upper bound in term of
\zeta
, thus we have

\left|\exp\left(-\frac{(x-z)^{2}}{4(t-r)}\right)-\exp\left(-\frac{(x-z)^{2}}{4(s-r)}\right)\right|\leq C(\zeta)(t-s)^{\zeta}(s-r)^{-\zeta}\exp\left(-\zeta\frac{(x-z)^{2}}{8(t-r)}\right).

As a result,

(4.5)

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\exp\left(-\frac{(x-z)^{2}}{4(t-r)}\right)(t-r)^{\alpha-1-\frac{1}{2}}-\exp\left(-\frac{(x-z)^{2}}{4(s-r)}\right)(t-r)^{\alpha-1-\frac{1}{2}}\right|dzdr\\
&\leq C(\zeta)(t-s)^{\zeta}\int_{0}^{s}(s-r)^{-\zeta}(t-r)^{\alpha-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\exp\left(-\zeta\frac{(x-z)^{2}}{8(t-r)}\right)(t-r)^{-\frac{1}{2}}dzdr\\
&\leq C(\zeta)(t-s)^{\zeta}\int_{0}^{s}(s-r)^{\alpha-1-\zeta}dr\leq C_{\alpha,\zeta}s^{\alpha-\zeta}(t-s)^{\zeta}\leq C_{\alpha,\zeta}(t-s)^{\zeta}.\end{split}

Similarly, for any
\zeta\in(0,\alpha),r<s\leq t
, we have

\left|(t-r)^{\alpha-1-\frac{1}{2}}-(s-r)^{\alpha-1-\frac{1}{2}}\right|^{1-\zeta}\leq 2(s-r)^{(\alpha-1-\frac{1}{2})(1-\zeta)}.

If we multiply
\left|(t-r)^{\alpha-1-\frac{1}{2}}-(s-r)^{\alpha-1-\frac{1}{2}}\right|^{\zeta}
to both sides, we obtain

(4.6)

\left|(t-r)^{\alpha-1-\frac{1}{2}}-(s-r)^{\alpha-1-\frac{1}{2}}\right|\leq 2(s-r)^{(\alpha-1-\frac{1}{2})(1-\zeta)}\left|(t-r)^{\alpha-1-\frac{1}{2}}-(s-r)^{\alpha-1-\frac{1}{2}}\right|^{\zeta}.

Applying the Mean Value theorem to the function of
x\to(x-r)^{\alpha-1-\frac{1}{2}}
yields

\left|(t-r)^{\alpha-1-\frac{1}{2}}-(s-r)^{\alpha-1-\frac{1}{2}}\right|=C(\alpha)(t-s)(t^{\prime}-r)^{\alpha-2-\frac{1}{2}}

for some
t^{\prime}\in(s,t)
. Plug the above into the RHS of (
4.6
) to parametrize the upper bound in term of
\zeta
, thus we have

\displaystyle\left|(t-r)^{\alpha-1-\frac{1}{2}}-(s-r)^{\alpha-1-\frac{1}{2}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C(\alpha,\zeta)(s-r)^{(\alpha-1-\frac{1}{2})(1-\zeta)+(\alpha-2-\frac{1}{2})\zeta}(t-s)^{\zeta}

\displaystyle=C(\alpha,\zeta)(s-r)^{(\alpha-1-\frac{1}{2}-\zeta)}(t-s)^{\zeta}.

Consequently,

(4.7)

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\exp\left(-\frac{(x-z)^{2}}{4(s-r)}\right)(t-r)^{\alpha-1-\frac{1}{2}}-\exp\left(-\frac{(x-z)^{2}}{4(s-r)}\right)(s-r)^{\alpha-1-\frac{1}{2}}\right|dzdr\\
&\leq C(\alpha,\zeta)(t-s)^{\zeta}\int_{0}^{s}(s-r)^{(\alpha-1-\zeta)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(s-r)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{(x-z)^{2}}{4(s-r)}\right)dzdr\\
&\leq C(\alpha,\zeta)s^{\alpha-\zeta}(t-s)^{\zeta}\leq C(\alpha,\zeta)(t-s)^{\zeta}.\end{split}

Combining (
4.3
), (
4.5
) and (
4.7
) completes the proof of (
4.2
).
∎

4.2.
Noise Term Estimates

Define the second integral of (
2.3
), i.e. noise term, by

(4.8)

N(t,x):=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{-1}^{1}G_{t-s}(x-y)\sigma(s,y,u(s,y))F(dyds).

When
\sigma\equiv 1
, we may express
N(t,x)
in terms of random Fourier series decomposition as

(4.9)

\begin{split}&N(t,x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sqrt{q_{n}}\left(\cos(\pi nx)\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\pi^{2}n^{2}(t-s)}\beta_{n}(ds)\right.\\
&\hskip 142.26378pt\left.+\sin(\pi nx)\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\pi^{2}n^{2}(t-s)}\tilde{\beta}_{n}(ds)\right),\end{split}

where the sequence
\{\beta_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
and
\{\tilde{\beta}_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
are independent and each formed of independent 1-dimensional real-valued
(\mathcal{F}_{t})
-adapted Brownian motions.
[
TTV03
]
Corollary 1 shows that the necessary and sufficient condition for existence of (
4.9
) in
L^{2}(\Omega\times[0,T]\times[-1,1])
is

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{q_{n}}{n^{2}}<\infty.

We could apply a factorization method (see
[
DPZ14
]
Theorem 5.10) to
N(t,x)
under assumptions (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) of
\sigma
. By the semigroup property of
G
and a stochastic Fubini’s theorem (see
[
Wal86
]
Theorem 2.6), for all
\alpha\in(0,1)
, we have

N(t,x)=\frac{\sin(\pi\alpha)}{\pi}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{-1}^{1}G_{t-r}(x-z)(t-r)^{\alpha-1}Y_{\alpha}(r,z)dzdr,

where

Y_{\alpha}(r,z)=\int_{0}^{r}\int_{-1}^{1}G_{r-s}(z-y)\sigma(s,y,u(s,y))(r-s)^{-\alpha}F(dyds).

Thanks to this factorization, we could estimate the noise term. Before that, we prove the following lemma which is similar to
[
SSS02
]
Lemma 2.2.

Lemma 4.2
.

For any
T\geq 0
,
\alpha\in\left(0,\frac{2-\gamma}{4}\right)
,

\sup_{0\leq r\leq T}\sup_{z\in\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{\alpha}^{2}(r,z)\right]<+\infty.

Proof.

By assumption (
2.2
) of
\sigma
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{\alpha}^{2}(r,z)\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{r}\int_{-1}^{1}G_{r-s}(z-y)\sigma(s,y,u(s,y))(r-s)^{-\alpha}F(dyds)\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{s,y}\mathbb{E}\left[\sigma^{2}(s,y,u(s,y))\right]\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{r}\int_{-1}^{1}G_{r-s}(z-y)(r-s)^{-\alpha}F(dyds)\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sqrt{q_{n}}\left(\cos(n\pi z)\int_{0}^{r}(r-s)^{-\alpha}e^{-\pi^{2}n^{2}(r-s)}\beta_{n}(ds)\right.\right.\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt\left.\left.\left.+\sin(n\pi z)\int_{0}^{r}(r-s)^{-\alpha}e^{-\pi^{2}n^{2}(r-s)}\tilde{\beta}_{n}(ds)\right)\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}q_{n}\left(\sin^{2}(n\pi z)+\cos^{2}(n\pi z)\right)\int_{0}^{r}(r-s)^{-2\alpha}e^{-2\pi^{2}n^{2}(r-s)}ds

\displaystyle=\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}q_{n}\int_{0}^{r}(r-s)^{-2\alpha}e^{-2\pi^{2}n^{2}(r-s)}ds

\displaystyle\leq C\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}\left[q_{0}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}q_{n}n^{4\alpha-2}\Gamma(1-2\alpha)\right]

\displaystyle<C+C^{\prime}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{4\alpha+\gamma-3}<\infty

The second inequality follows the Fourier series decomposition of a spatial convolution, and we substitute
2\pi^{2}n^{2}(r-s)
in the last inequality to get an Euler gamma function. Recall
q_{n}
in (
1.3
), and the summation converges so that the lemma follows.
∎

We will now estimate the spatial regularity of
N(t,x)
with
t\leq 1
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[(N(t,x)-N(t,y))^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=C(\alpha)\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{-1}^{1}[G_{t-r}(x-z)-G_{t-r}(y-z)](t-r)^{\alpha-1}Y_{\alpha}(r,z)dzdr\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C(\alpha)\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{-1}^{1}\left|[G_{t-r}(x-z)-G_{t-r}(y-z)](t-r)^{\alpha-1}Y_{\alpha}(r,z)\right|dzdr\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C(\alpha)\sup_{r,z}\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{\alpha}^{2}(r,z)\right]\left(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{-1}^{1}\left|[G_{t-r}(x-z)-G_{t-r}(y-z)](t-r)^{\alpha-1}\right|dzdr\right)^{2}.

By Lemma
4.1
and Lemma
4.2
, we conclude that there exists a constant
C>0
depending only on
\alpha,\gamma
and
\xi
with
\xi\in(0,2\alpha)
and
\alpha\in\left(0,\frac{2-\gamma}{4}\right)
such that

(4.10)

\mathbb{E}\left[(N(t,x)-N(t,y))^{2}\right]\leq C\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}|x-y|^{2\xi}.

For temporal regularity,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[(N(t,x)-N(s,x))^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=C(\alpha)\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{s}\int_{-1}^{1}\left[G_{t-r}(x-z)(t-r)^{\alpha-1}-G_{s-r}(x-z)(s-r)^{\alpha-1}\right]Y_{\alpha}(r,z)dzdr\right.\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt\left.\left.+\int_{s}^{t}\int_{-1}^{1}G_{t-r}(x-z)(t-r)^{\alpha-1}Y_{\alpha}(r,z)dzdr\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=C(\alpha)\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{s}\int_{-1}^{1}\left[G_{t-r}(x-z)(t-r)^{\alpha-1}-G_{s-r}(x-z)(s-r)^{\alpha-1}\right]Y_{\alpha}(r,z)dzdr\right)^{2}\right]\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt\left.+\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{s}^{t}\int_{-1}^{1}G_{t-r}(x-z)(t-r)^{\alpha-1}Y_{\alpha}(r,z)dzdr\right)^{2}\right]\right)

\displaystyle\leq C(\alpha)\sup_{r,z}\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{\alpha}^{2}(r,z)\right]\left(I_{1}^{2}+I_{2}^{2}\right),

where

I_{1}=\int_{0}^{s}\int_{-1}^{1}\left|G_{t-r}(x-z)(t-r)^{\alpha-1}-G_{s-r}(x-z)(s-r)^{\alpha-1}\right|dzdr,

and

(4.11)

I_{2}=\int_{s}^{t}\int_{-1}^{1}\left|G_{t-r}(x-z)(t-r)^{\alpha-1}\right|dzdr=C_{\alpha}(t-s)^{\alpha}<C_{\alpha}(t-s)^{\zeta}.

By Lemma
4.1
and for
0\leq s<t\leq 1
, we derive

(4.12)

I_{1}\leq C_{\alpha,\zeta}(t-s)^{\zeta}.

Therefore, we conclude that there exists a constant
C>0
depending only on
\alpha,\gamma
and
\zeta
with
\zeta\in(0,\alpha)
and
\alpha\in\left(0,\frac{2-\gamma}{4}\right)
such that

\mathbb{E}\left[(N(t,x)-N(s,x))^{2}\right]\leq C\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}(t-s)^{2\zeta}.

Lemma 4.3
.

There exist positive constants
C_{1},C_{2}
depending only on
\alpha,\gamma
and
\xi
, and positive constants
C_{3},C_{4}
depending only on
\alpha,\gamma
and
\zeta
such that for all
0\leq s<t\leq 1
,
x,y\in\mathbb{T}
,
\alpha\in\left(0,\frac{2-\gamma}{4}\right)
,
\xi\in(0,2\alpha),\zeta\in(0,\alpha)
, and
\lambda>0
, we have

(4.13)

P(|N(t,x)-N(t,y)|>\lambda)\leq C_{1}\exp\left(-\frac{C_{2}\lambda^{2}}{\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}|x-y|^{2\xi}}\right),

(4.14)

P(|N(t,x)-N(s,x)|>\lambda)\leq C_{3}\exp\left(-\frac{C_{4}\lambda^{2}}{\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}|t-s|^{2\zeta}}\right).

Proof.

Define

N_{t}(s,x):=\int_{0}^{s}\int_{-1}^{1}G_{t-r}(x-y)\sigma(r,y,u(r,y))F(dydr).

Note that
N_{t}(t,x)=N(t,x)
and
N_{t}(s,x)
is a continuous
\mathcal{F}_{s}^{F}
-adapted martingale in
s
since the integrand does not depend on
s
. Consider

M_{s}:=N_{t}(s,x)-N_{t}(s,y)=\int_{0}^{s}\int_{-1}^{1}[G_{t-r}(x-z)-G_{t-r}(y-z)]\sigma(r,z,u(r,z))F(dzdr),

so that
M_{t}=N(t,x)-N(t,y)
. As
M_{s}
is a continuous
\mathcal{F}_{s}^{F}
-adapted martingale with
M_{0}=0
, it is a time changed Brownian motion. In particular, we have

M_{t}=B_{\langle M\rangle_{t}},

and (
4.10
) gives an upper bound on the time change as

\langle M\rangle_{t}\leq C\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}|x-y|^{2\xi}.

Therefore, by the reflection principle for the Brownian motion
B_{\langle M\rangle_{t}}
,

\displaystyle P(N(t,x)-N(t,y)>\lambda)

\displaystyle=P(M_{t}>\lambda)=P\left(B_{\langle M\rangle_{t}}>\lambda\right)

\displaystyle\leq P\left(\sup_{s\leq C\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}|x-y|^{2\xi}}B_{s}>\lambda\right)=2P\left(B_{C\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}|x-y|^{2\xi}}>\lambda\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\exp\left(-\frac{C_{2}\lambda^{2}}{\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}|x-y|^{2\xi}}\right).

Switching
x
and
y
gives

\displaystyle P(-N(t,x)+N(t,y)>\lambda)

\displaystyle=P(M_{t}<-\lambda)\leq 2P\left(B_{C\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}|x-y|^{2\xi}}<-\lambda\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\exp\left(-\frac{C_{2}\lambda^{2}}{\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}|x-y|^{2\xi}}\right).

Consequently, for
\forall\xi\in(0,2\alpha)
,

P(|N(t,x)-N(t,y)|>\lambda)\leq C_{1}\exp\left(-\frac{C_{2}\lambda^{2}}{\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}|x-y|^{2\xi}}\right),

which completes the proof of (
4.13
).

Define

R_{q_{1}}=\int_{0}^{q_{1}}\int_{-1}^{1}[G_{t-r}(x-y)-G_{s-r}(x-y)]\sigma(r,y,u(r,y))F(dydr),

where
0\leq q_{1}\leq s
. Note
R_{q_{1}}
is a continuous
\mathcal{F}_{q_{1}}^{F}
-adapted martingale with
R_{0}=0
. Also define

S_{q_{2}}=\int_{s}^{s+{q_{2}}}\int_{-1}^{1}G_{t-r}(x-y)\sigma(r,y,u(r,y))F(dydr),

where
0\leq q_{2}\leq t-s
. Note
S_{q_{2}}
is a continuous
\mathcal{F}_{q_{2}}^{F}
-adapted martingale with
S_{0}=0
. Thus, both
R_{q_{1}}
and
S_{q_{2}}
are time changed Brownian motions, i.e.,

R_{s}=B_{\langle R\rangle_{s}}\quad\text{and}\quad S_{t-s}=B^{\prime}_{\langle S\rangle_{t-s}},

where
B
,
B^{\prime}
are two different Brownian motions. Note that
N(t,x)-N(s,x)=R_{s}+S_{t-s}
, then

P(N(t,x)-N(s,x)>\lambda)\leq P(R_{s}>\lambda/2)+P(S_{t-s}>\lambda/2).

(
4.11
) and (
4.12
) give an upper bound on the time changes as

\langle R\rangle_{s}\leq C\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}(t-s)^{2\zeta}\quad\text{and}\quad\langle S\rangle_{t-s}\leq C\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}(t-s)^{2\zeta}.

By the reflection principle for the Brownian motions
B_{\langle R\rangle_{s}}
and
B^{\prime}_{\langle S\rangle_{t-s}}
,

\displaystyle P(N(t,x)-N(s,x)>\lambda)

\displaystyle\leq P\left(B_{\langle R\rangle_{s}}>\lambda/2\right)+P\left(B^{\prime}_{\langle S\rangle_{t-s}}>\lambda/2\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2P\left(\sup_{r\leq C\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}|t-s|^{2\zeta}}B_{r}>\frac{\lambda}{2}\right)=4P\left(B_{C\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}|t-s|^{2\zeta}}>\frac{\lambda}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{3}\exp\left(-\frac{C_{4}\lambda^{2}}{\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}|t-s|^{2\zeta}}\right).

In addition,

\displaystyle P(-N(t,x)+N(s,x)>\lambda)

\displaystyle\leq P(R_{s}<-\lambda/2)+P(S_{t-s}<-\lambda/2)

\displaystyle\leq 4P\left(B_{C\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}|t-s|^{2\zeta}}<-\frac{\lambda}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{3}\exp\left(-\frac{C_{4}\lambda^{2}}{\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}|t-s|^{2\zeta}}\right).

Consequently, for
\forall\zeta\in(0,\alpha)
,

P(|N(t,x)-N(s,x)|>\lambda)\leq C_{3}\exp\left(-\frac{C_{4}\lambda^{2}}{\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}|t-s|^{2\zeta}}\right),

which completes the proof of (
4.14
).
∎

Definition 4.1
.

Given a grid

\mathbb{G}_{n}=\left\{\left(\frac{j}{2^{2n}},\frac{k}{2^{n}}\right):0\leq j\leq 2^{2n},0\leq k\leq 2^{n},j,k\in\mathbb{Z}\right\},

and we write

\left(t_{j}^{(n)},x_{k}^{(n)}\right)=\left(\frac{j}{2^{2n}},\frac{k}{2^{n}}\right).

Two points
\left(t_{j_{i}}^{(n)},x_{k_{i}}^{(n)}\right):i=1,2
are called
nearest neighbors
if either

j_{1}=j_{2}\quad\text{and}\quad|k_{1}-k_{2}|=1\quad\textbf{or}\quad|j_{1}-j_{2}|=1\quad\text{and}\quad k_{1}=k_{2}.

The following lemma is analogous to Lemma 3.4 in
[
AJM21
]
and Lemma 2.5 in
[
FJK22
]
, which is crucial for estimating small ball probabilities.

Lemma 4.4
.

There exist constants
C_{5},C_{6}>0
depending on
\gamma
such that for all
\beta,\lambda,\varepsilon>0
and
\beta\varepsilon^{4}\leq 1
, we have

P\left(\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq t\leq\beta\varepsilon^{4}\\
x\in\left[0,\varepsilon^{2}\right]\end{subarray}}|N(t,x)|>\lambda\varepsilon^{2-\gamma}\right)\leq\frac{C_{5}}{1\wedge\sqrt{\beta}}\exp\left(-\frac{C_{6}\lambda^{2}}{\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}\beta^{\frac{2-\gamma}{2}}}\right).

Proof.

Let us first consider the case
\beta\geq 1
. For
n\geq 0
, consider the grid

\mathbb{G}_{n}=\left\{\left(\frac{j}{2^{2n}},\frac{k}{2^{n}}\right):0\leq j\leq\beta\varepsilon^{4}2^{2n},0\leq k\leq\varepsilon^{2}2^{n},j,k\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}.

Let

(4.15)

n_{0}=\left\lceil\log_{2}\left(\beta^{-1/2}\varepsilon^{-2}\right)\right\rceil,

and for
n<n_{0}
,
\mathbb{G}_{n}
contains only the origin. For
n\geq n_{0}
, the grid
\mathbb{G}_{n}
has at most
\left(\beta\varepsilon^{4}2^{2n}+1\right)\cdot\left(\varepsilon^{2}2^{n}+1\right)\leq 2^{5}\cdot 2^{3(n-n_{0})}
many points. Because
4\zeta\wedge 2\xi<4\alpha<2-\gamma
, we can choose two parameters
0<\delta_{1}(\gamma)<\delta_{2}(\gamma)<\frac{2-\gamma}{2}
, and define

\delta:=\delta_{2}-\delta_{1},

which satisfies the following constraint

(4.16)

4\zeta\wedge 2\xi=2-\gamma-2\delta.

We now define another constant

(4.17)

\mathcal{M}=\frac{1-2^{-\delta_{1}}}{4\cdot 2^{\delta n_{0}}},

and consider the event

A(n,\lambda)=\left\{|N(p)-N(q)|\leq\lambda\mathcal{M}\varepsilon^{2-\gamma}2^{-\delta_{1}n}2^{\delta_{2}n_{0}}\text{ for all nearest neighbors $p,q\in\mathbb{G}_{n}$}\right\}.

If
p,q\in\mathbb{G}_{n}
have different spatial variables, (
4.13
) implies

P\left(|N(p)-N(q)|>\lambda\mathcal{M}\varepsilon^{2-\gamma}2^{-\delta_{1}n}2^{\delta_{2}n_{0}}\right)\leq C_{1}\exp\left(-\frac{C_{2}\lambda^{2}\mathcal{M}^{2}\varepsilon^{4-2\gamma}}{2^{-2n\xi}\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}}2^{-2\delta_{1}n}2^{2\delta_{2}n_{0}}\right).

If
p,q\in\mathbb{G}_{n}
have different time variables, (
4.14
) implies

P\left(|N(p)-N(q)|>\lambda\mathcal{M}\varepsilon^{2-\gamma}2^{-\delta_{1}n}2^{\delta_{2}n_{0}}\right)\leq C_{3}\exp\left(-\frac{C_{4}\lambda^{2}\mathcal{M}^{2}\varepsilon^{4-2\gamma}}{2^{-4n\zeta}\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}}2^{-2\delta_{1}n}2^{2\delta_{2}n_{0}}\right).

Therefore, a union bound gives

\displaystyle P(A^{c}(n,\lambda))\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p,q\in\mathbb{G}_{n}\\
\text{nearest neighbors}\end{subarray}}P\left(|N(p)-N(q)|>\lambda\mathcal{M}\varepsilon^{2-\gamma}2^{-\delta_{1}n}2^{\delta_{2}n_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C2^{3(n-n_{0})}\exp\left(-\frac{C^{\prime}\lambda^{2}\mathcal{M}^{2}\varepsilon^{4-2\gamma}}{\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}}2^{n(4\zeta\wedge 2\xi)}2^{-2\delta_{1}n}2^{2\delta_{2}n_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle=C2^{3(n-n_{0})}\exp\left(-\frac{C^{\prime}\lambda^{2}\mathcal{M}^{2}}{\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}}\left(\varepsilon^{4-2\gamma}2^{n_{0}(2-\gamma)}\right)2^{n(4\zeta\wedge 2\xi)}2^{-2\delta_{1}n}2^{-n_{0}(2-\gamma)}2^{2\delta_{2}n_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C2^{3(n-n_{0})}\exp\left(-\frac{C^{\prime}\lambda^{2}\mathcal{M}^{2}}{\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}\beta^{\frac{2-\gamma}{2}}}2^{(4\zeta\wedge 2\xi-2\delta_{1})(n-n_{0})}\right),

where
C,C^{\prime}
are positive constants depending only on
\gamma
. The last inequality follows from that
\varepsilon^{4-2\gamma}2^{n_{0}(2-\gamma)}\geq\beta^{-\frac{(2-\gamma)}{2}}
by the definition of
n_{0}
in (
4.15
), and our choices of
\delta_{1},\delta_{2}
and
\delta
in (
4.16
).

Let
A(\lambda)=\bigcap_{n\geq n_{0}}A(n,\lambda)
and we can bound
P(A^{c}(\lambda))
by summing
P(A^{c}(n,\lambda))
for all
n\geq n_{0}
,

\displaystyle P\left(A^{c}(\lambda)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n\geq n_{0}}P\left(A^{c}(n,\lambda)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n\geq n_{0}}C2^{3(n-n_{0})}\exp\left(-\frac{C^{\prime}\lambda^{2}\mathcal{M}^{2}}{\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}\beta^{\frac{2-\gamma}{2}}}2^{(4\zeta\wedge 2\xi-2\delta_{1})(n-n_{0})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{5}\exp\left(-\frac{C^{\prime}\lambda^{2}\mathcal{M}^{2}}{\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}\beta^{\frac{2-\gamma}{2}}}\right).

From definitions (
4.15
) and (
4.17
), we have

\displaystyle P\left(A^{c}(\lambda)\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{5}\exp\left(-\frac{C^{\prime}\lambda^{2}2^{-2\delta n_{0}}}{\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}\beta^{\frac{2-\gamma}{2}}}\right)

\displaystyle P\left(A^{c}(\lambda)\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{5}\exp\left(-\frac{C_{6}\lambda^{2}}{\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}\beta^{\frac{2-\gamma}{2}}}\right),

where both
C_{5}
and
C_{6}
depend on
\gamma.

Now we consider a point

for some

\displaystyle|N(t,x)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}|N(p_{j})-N(p_{j+1})|\leq 4\sum_{n\geq n_{0}}\lambda\mathcal{M}\varepsilon^{2-\gamma}2^{-\delta_{1}n}2^{\delta_{2}n_{0}}

\displaystyle=4\lambda\varepsilon^{2-\gamma}2^{\delta_{2}n_{0}}\frac{1-2^{-\delta_{1}}}{4\cdot 2^{\delta n_{0}}}\sum_{n\geq n_{0}}2^{-\delta_{1}n}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\varepsilon^{2-\gamma}.

∎

Lemma 5.1

Consider noise terms

∎

where the last equality follows by the definition of

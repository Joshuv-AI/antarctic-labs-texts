Online learning with exponential weights in metric spaces
Quentin Paris

Abstract
This paper addresses the problem of online learning in metric spaces using exponential weights. We extend the analysis of the exponentially weighted average forecaster, traditionally studied in a Euclidean settings, to a more abstract framework. Our results rely on the notion of barycenters, a suitable version of Jensen’s inequality and a synthetic notion of lower curvature bound in metric spaces known as the measure contraction property. We also adapt the online-to-batch conversion principle to apply our results to a statistical learning framework.



1  Introduction

The problem of online convex optimization
(
Cesa-Bianchi and Lugosi 2006
,
Shalev-Shwartz 2012
,
Hazan 2016
)
has become a strandard model of online learning. Its simple and flexible formulation as a repeated game, devoid of distributional assumptions on the data, has proven effective in framing theoretically a number of online prediction tasks including online recommendation systems, online portfolio selection or network routing problems. Traditionally studied in the context of Euclidean spaces, less seems to be known when the decision space is a more general metric space, with potentially no linear structure. In this paper, we extend the analysis of the exponentially weighted average (
ewa
) forecaster to some geodesic metric spaces.

Motivations for this level of generality arise, for example, when the decision space is a smooth manifold. Such a scenario is routinely encountered in directional or shape statistics
(
Mardia 1999
)
where observations take values in spheres, projective spaces or shape spaces. Computer vision and medical imaging deal with spaces of transformations that carry a Lie group structure
(
Younes 2019
)
. The space of real symmetric positive-definite matrices with Bures metric
(
Bures 1969
)
, Log-Euclidean metric
(
Arsigny et al. 2007
)
or Log-Cholesky metric
(
Lin 2019
)
have found important applications in quantum information theory or diffusion tensor imaging. Recent works have also demonstrated the use of hyperbolic spaces for applications in natural language processing
(
Nickel and Kiela 2017
)
. Other situations of interest involve spaces which cannot be endowed with a formal Riemannian structure such as the
2
-Wasserstein space
(
Villani 2009
,
Santambrogio 2015
)
, the Billera-Holmes-Vogtmann space of phylogenetic trees
(
Billera et al. 2001
)
or the Gromov-Wasserstein space
(
Mémoli 2011
,
Sturm 2012
)
. For a detailed survey on data analysis in non-standard spaces, we also refer the reader to
Huckemann and Eltzner 2020
.

All the spaces mentioned above share the characteristic of being geodesic spaces
(
Bridson and Haefliger 1999
,
Burago et al. 2001
,
Alexander et al. 2019b
,
Alexander et al. 2019a
)
, a structure that turns out to be rich enough to formulate and study the theoretical properties of a generalized version of the
ewa
forecaster, expanding the range of potential applications for online prediction strategies.

From a technical pespective, the definition of our learning strategy, called the exponentially weighted barycentric (
ewb
) forecaster, relies on the central notion of barycenters of probability measures. Its analysis is then based on an adapted version of Jensen’s inequality along with a general geometric property referred to as the measure contraction property that may be understood as a general definition of a lower Ricci curvature bound, in spaces that do not have a formal Riemannian structure.

The use the measure contraction property in the Euclidean setting, can be traced back to
Blum and Kalai 1999
that use the scale invariance of the Lebesgue measure to analyse the performance of the universal rebalanced porfolio algorithm introduced by

Cover 1991
. Later, the same argument was leveraged by
Hazan et al. 2007
to show that the
ewa
forecaster achieves logarithmic regret for general exponentially concave losses in Euclidean spaces. The argument we develop builds upon these original ideas, extending them to a more abstract framework.

Proofs are reported to Section

2.2  Geodesic spaces and convexity

Let

Bounded and locally compact geodesic spaces

Complete and connected Riemannian manifolds of dimension

3.1  The
ewb
forecaster

We are now in position to define a learning strategy we call the Exponentially Weighted Barycentric (
ewb
) forecaster.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
(\beta_{t})_{t\geq 1}
be a sequence of positive tuning parameters. Then, for
t\geq 1
, we define
x_{t}
as a barycenter of
m_{t}
, i.e.,

x_{t}\in\underset{x\in M}{\arg\min}\int_{M}d^{2}(x,y)\,m_{t}(\mathrm{d}y),

(3.1)

where, as in the classical setting,
m_{1}:=m
and, for all
t\geq 1
,

\mathrm{d}m_{t+1}(x):=\frac{e^{-\beta_{t+1}\ell_{t}(x)}}{\int_{M}e^{-\beta_{t+1}\ell_{t}}\,\mathrm{d}m_{t}}\,\mathrm{d}m_{t}(x).

3.2  Regret bounds

Throughout the rest of the section, we denote

\psi(r):=(r\coth r)\exp(-r\coth r),

for

, set
\psi(0)=e^{-1}
and define, for all
x\in M
, all

and all

\int_{M}\psi\left(d(x,y)\sqrt{\frac{-\kappa}{p-1}}\right)\,m(\mathrm{d}y)&\mbox{if }\kappa<0.\end{array}\right.

3.3  Online to batch conversion

The principle of online-to-batch conversion is a classical way to exploit algorithms, developed for sequential prediction, in the context of statistical learning. This section presents a simple adaptation of this procedure in the context of metric spaces.

Consider the following statistical learning problem. Let
(M,d)
be a metric space, let
\mathcal{Z}
be an arbitrary measurable space and let
\ell:M\times\mathcal{Z}\to\mathbb{R}
be a fixed loss function. Suppose given a collection (or batch)
\{Z_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
of independent and identically distributed
\mathcal{Z}
-valued random variables with same distribution as (and independent from) a generic random variable
Z
. Finally, consider the task of constructing
\hat{\theta}_{n}\in M
based on
\{Z_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
and such that the excess risk

\mathbb{E}[\ell(\hat{\theta}_{n},Z)]-\inf_{\theta\in M}\mathbb{E}[\ell(\theta,Z)],

is as small as possible.

To that aim, consider first the problem of online optimization studied so far with decision space
M
and loss functions
\mathcal{L}=\{\ell(.,z):z\in\mathcal{Z}\}
. In this setting, at each round
t\geq 1
the player first chooses a point
\theta_{t}\in M
, the environment then reveals a point
z_{t}
, the player incurs loss
\ell(\theta_{t},z_{t})
and moves on to round
t+1
. Here, the players strategy
(\theta_{t})_{t\geq 1}
can be formally associated to a sequence of measurable maps
(\vartheta_{t})_{t\geq 1}
where
\vartheta_{1}
is constantly equal to
\theta_{1}
and, for
t\geq 2
,
\vartheta_{t}:\mathcal{Z}^{t-1}\to M
is such that

\theta_{t}=\vartheta_{t}(z_{1},\dots,z_{t-1}).

Now, suppose given such a prediction strategy
(\vartheta_{t})_{t\geq 1}
and suppose that, for all
n\geq 1
, there exists
B_{n}>0
satisfying

\sum_{t=1}^{n}\ell(\theta_{t},z_{t})-\inf_{\theta\in M}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\ell(\theta,z_{t})\leq B_{n},

(3.5)

uniformly over the outcome sequence
(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})\in\mathcal{Z}^{n}
.

Then, coming back to the statistical learning problem, consider
\hat{\theta}_{n}
to be a barycenter of the (random) probability measure

\frac{1}{n+1}\delta_{\vartheta_{1}}+\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{t=2}^{n+1}\delta_{\vartheta_{t}(Z_{1},\dots,Z_{t-1})},

on
M
, i.e.,

\hat{\theta}_{n}\in\underset{\theta\in M}{\arg\min}\left\{d^{2}(\theta,\vartheta_{1})+\sum_{t=2}^{n+1}d^{2}(\theta,\vartheta_{t}(Z_{1},\dots,Z_{t-1}))\right\}.

(3.6)

Then we have the following result.

Theorem 3.6
.

Suppose that the metric space
(M,d)
satisfies Assumptions
(A1)
and
(A2)
. Suppose that (
3.5
) holds uniformly over the outcome sequence
(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})\in\mathcal{Z}^{n}
and that, for all
z\in\mathcal{Z}
, the function
\ell(.,z):M\to\mathbb{R}
is geodesically convex.
Then, for all
n\geq 1
,

\mathbb{E}[\ell(\hat{\theta}_{n},Z)]-\inf_{\theta\in M}\mathbb{E}[\ell(\theta,Z)]\leq\frac{B_{n+1}}{n+1}.

Combining Theorems
3.2
and
3.6
, we readily obtain the following result. A similar adaptation of Theorem
3.5
is left to the reader.

Corollary 3.7
.

Suppose that
(M,d)
satisfies Assumptions
(A1)
and
(A2)
. Let
m
be a prior distribution over
M
such that
(M,d,m)
satisfies Assumption
(A3)
and condition (
3.3
) if
\kappa<0
. Suppose that there exists
\beta>0
such that, for all
z\in\mathcal{Z}
, the function
\ell(.,z):M\to\mathbb{R}
is geodesically
\beta
-expconcave. Let
\hat{\theta}_{n}
be as in (
3.6
) where
\vartheta_{1}
is a barycenter of
m
and, for
2\leq t\leq n+1
,
\vartheta_{t}(Z_{1},\dots,Z_{t-1})
is a barycenter of the (random) probability measure
m_{t}
defined by

\mathrm{d}m_{t}(\theta)=\frac{e^{-\beta\ell(\theta,Z_{t-1})}}{\int_{M}e^{-\beta\ell(.,Z_{t-1})}\mathrm{d}m_{t-1}}\mathrm{d}m_{t-1}(\theta),

where
m_{1}:=m
. Then, for all
n\geq 2
,

\mathbb{E}[\ell(\hat{\theta}_{n},Z)]-\inf_{\theta\in M}\mathbb{E}[\ell(\theta,Z)]\leq\frac{2+\ln(\inf_{M}c_{\kappa,p})^{-1}}{\beta(n+1)}+\frac{p\ln(n+1)}{\beta(n+1)}.

3.4  Example: The
2
-Wasserstein space over
\mathbb{R}^{d}

Consider the set
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
of all square integrable (Borel) probability measures over
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Given
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, denote
\Pi_{\mu,\nu}
the set of couplings between
\mu
and

\pi

\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}

. The

on

W^{2}_{2}(\mu,\nu):=\inf_{\pi\in\Pi_{\mu,\nu}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\|x-y\|^{2}_{2}\,{\rm d}\pi(x,y).

The metric space
(\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),W_{2})
is called the
2
-Wasserstein space over
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. It is complete, separable, geodesic and has non-negative curvature in the sense of Definition
2.4

(
Sturm 2006a
, Proposition 2.10)
. The existence and uniqueness of barycenters of probability measures on

have been established under general conditions. For instance
Le Gouic and Loubes 2017
show that, for any
P\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
,

•

P
admits a barycenter,

•

if there exists a Borel subset
B\subset\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
P(B)>0
and such that every
\mu\in B
has a density with respect to the Lebesgue measure, then this barycenter is unique.

Assumptions
(A1)
-
(A3)
are therefore verified for a subset

equipped with the Wasserstein metric provided the following conditions are satisfied:

(1)

is a geodesically convex subset of
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
in the sense that for any
\mu,\nu\in M
and any geodesic
\gamma:[0,1]\to\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
connecting
\mu
to
\nu
, the image of
\gamma
is included in
M
,

(2)

M
has finite Hausdorff dimension
p

We end with a result of special interest for online learning with the
W_{2}
loss in the
2
-Wasserstein space. Recall first that, according to the Kantorovich dual representation of

, for all
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{D})

W^{2}_{2}(\mu,\nu)=\sup_{\varphi}\int(\|.\|^{2}-2\varphi)\,{\rm d}\mu+\int(\|.\|^{2}-2\varphi^{*})\,{\rm d}\nu,

where

ranges over the set of proper and lower semi-continuous convex functions and

Lemma 3.8
.

Let

and suppose

. Let
\mu^{*}
be a barycenter of

\mu^{*}
is unique and, for all
\mu

we have

W^{2}_{2}(\mu^{*},\mu)\leq-\frac{C^{-1}_{\rm var}}{\beta}\ln\int\exp(-\beta W^{2}_{2}(\mu,.))\,\mathrm{d}P,

(3.7)

where

4.1  Proof of Lemma
2.3

(1)
Suppose that
f
is
\beta
-expconcave for some
\beta>0
. Then, since the logarithm is increasing and concave, we get that, for any geodesic
\gamma:[0,1]\to M
and all
t\in[0,1]
,

\displaystyle f(\gamma_{t})

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\beta}\ln e^{-\beta f(\gamma_{t})}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{\beta}\ln((1-t)e^{-\beta f(\gamma_{0})}+te^{-\beta f(\gamma_{1})})

\displaystyle\leq(1-t)f(\gamma_{0})+tf(\gamma_{1}).

(2)
Suppose
f:M\to\mathbb{R}
is geodesically
\alpha
-convex and
L
-Lipchitz for some
\alpha,L>0
. Using the fact that
M
is complete, it is enough to show that for every
0\leq\beta\leq\alpha/L^{2}
and every geodesic
\gamma:[0,1]\to M

\frac{1}{2}e^{-\beta f(\gamma_{0})}+\frac{1}{2}e^{-\beta f(\gamma_{1})}\leq e^{-\beta f(\gamma_{1/2})},

or, equivalently, that

\frac{1}{2}e^{\beta(f(z)-f(x))}+\frac{1}{2}e^{\beta(f(z)-f(y))}\leq 1,

(4.1)

where
x=\gamma_{0},y=\gamma_{1}
and

\displaystyle\geq\varepsilon f(r)\exp(-f(r)),

where the last inequality uses the fact that
\varepsilon\leq 1/2
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.2
.

Fix
n\geq 2
and

x^{*}_{n}\in\underset{x\in M}{\arg\min}\,L_{n}(x).

Let
h:M\times[0,1]\to M
be a geodesic homothety with center
x^{*}_{n}
for which the measure contraction property
(A3)
holds. For
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, denote

M_{\varepsilon}:=M^{\varepsilon}_{x^{*}_{n}}=\{h(y,\varepsilon):y\in M\},

and introduce the probability measure

m_{\varepsilon}(.):=\frac{m(.\cap M_{\varepsilon})}{m(M_{\varepsilon})}.

According to Lemma
4.2
we deduce that

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{n}\ell_{t}(x_{t})

\displaystyle\leq\int_{M}L_{n}\mathrm{d}m_{\varepsilon}+\frac{\mathcal{E}(m_{\varepsilon}|m)}{\beta}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m(M_{\varepsilon})}\int_{M_{\varepsilon}}L_{n}\mathrm{d}m+\frac{1}{\beta}\ln\left(\frac{1}{m(M_{\varepsilon})}\right).

(4.4)

Then, since
h(y,.):[0,1]\to M
is a geodesic connecting
x^{*}_{n}
to
y
, the
\beta
-expconcavity of the losses implies that

e^{-\beta\ell_{t}(h(y,\varepsilon))}\geq(1-\varepsilon)e^{-\beta\ell_{t}(x^{*}_{n})}+\varepsilon e^{-\beta\ell_{t}(y)}\geq(1-\varepsilon)e^{-\beta\ell_{t}(x^{*}_{n})},

and therefore

e^{-\beta L_{n}(h(y,\varepsilon))}\geq(1-\varepsilon)^{n}e^{-\beta L_{n}(x^{*}_{n})},

which implies

L_{n}(h(y,\varepsilon))\leq\frac{n}{\beta}\ln\left(\frac{1}{1-\varepsilon}\right)+L_{n}(x^{*}_{n}).

Combining this inequality with (
4.4
), we obtain

R_{n}\leq\frac{n}{\beta}\ln\left(\frac{1}{1-\varepsilon}\right)+\frac{1}{\beta}\ln\left(\frac{1}{m(M_{\varepsilon})}\right).

It remains to bound
m(M_{\varepsilon})
from below. According to
(A3)
we have

m(M_{\varepsilon})\geq\int_{M}\varepsilon\left(\frac{s_{\kappa}(\varepsilon d(x^{*}_{n},y)/\sqrt{p-1})}{s_{\kappa}(d(x^{*}_{n},y)/\sqrt{p-1})}\right)^{p-1}\,m(\mathrm{d}y).

To make this lower bound more explicit, we consider three cases.

\bullet
Suppose
\kappa>0
. Then, according to the Bonnet-Myers Theorem, valid under the
mcp
property
(
Ohta 2007
, Theorem 4.3)
, the diameter of
M
is at most
\pi\sqrt{(p-1)/\kappa}
. Hence, it follows from Lemma
4.3
point
(1)
, and the definition of
s_{\kappa}
in display (
2.8
), that for all
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
,
m(M_{\varepsilon})\geq\varepsilon^{p}
which implies that

R_{n}\leq\frac{n}{\beta}\ln\left(\frac{1}{1-\varepsilon}\right)+\frac{p}{\beta}\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}.

Taking
\varepsilon=1/n
, and using the standard inequality
\ln(1+x)\leq x
, we obtain in particular

R_{n}\leq\frac{n}{\beta}\ln\left(\frac{n}{n-1}\right)+\frac{p\ln n}{\beta}\leq\frac{n}{\beta(n-1)}+\frac{p\ln n}{\beta},

as desired.

\bullet
Suppose
\kappa=0
. Then since
s_{\kappa}(r)=r
, the
mcp
property reads in this case
m(M_{\varepsilon})\geq\varepsilon^{p}
and we conclude as above.

\bullet
Suppose
\kappa<0
. Then Lemma
4.2
point
(2)
implies that, for all
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2]

\displaystyle m(M_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\geq\varepsilon^{p}\int_{M}\psi\left(d(x^{*}_{n},y)\sqrt{\frac{-\kappa}{p-1}}\right)\,m(\mathrm{d}y)

\displaystyle=c_{\kappa,p}(x^{*}_{n})\varepsilon^{p}.

Hence, for all
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2]
,

R_{n}\leq\frac{n}{\beta}\ln\left(\frac{1}{1-\varepsilon}\right)+\frac{1}{\beta}\ln\frac{1}{c_{\kappa,p}(x^{*}_{n})}+\frac{p}{\beta}\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon}.

Hence taking
\varepsilon=1/n
allows to conclude as in the previous cases.
∎

4.3  Proof of Proposition
3.3

Consider

x^{*}_{n}\in\underset{x\in M}{\arg\min}\,L_{n}(x).

For
r\in(0,r_{0}]
, define the probability measure

\mu_{r}(.):=\frac{m(.\cap B(x^{*}_{n},r))}{m(B(x^{*}_{n},r))}.

According to Lemma
4.2
we deduce that

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{n}\ell_{t}(x_{t})

\displaystyle\leq\int_{M}L_{n}\mathrm{d}\mu_{r}+\frac{\mathcal{E}(\mu_{r}|m)}{\beta}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m(B(x^{*}_{n},r))}\int_{B(x^{*}_{n},r)}L_{n}\mathrm{d}m+\frac{1}{\beta}\ln\left(\frac{1}{m(B(x^{*}_{n},r))}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{m(B(x^{*}_{n},r))}\int_{B(x^{*}_{n},r)}L_{n}\mathrm{d}m+\frac{1}{\beta}\ln\frac{1}{c(x^{*}_{n})}+\frac{p}{\beta}\ln\frac{1}{r},

by Assumption (
3.4
). Substracting
L_{n}(x^{*}_{n})
on both sides and using the Lipschitz property of the losses, we obtain, for all
r\in(0,r_{0}]
,

\displaystyle R_{n}

\displaystyle\leq nLr+\frac{1}{\beta}\ln\frac{1}{c(x^{*}_{n})}+\frac{p}{\beta}\ln\frac{1}{r}.

Taking finally
r=1/n
implies the desired result.

4.4  Proof of Proposition
3.4

According to
Ohta 2007
, for all
0<r\leq r_{0}
(and
r_{0}\leq\pi\sqrt{(p-1)/\kappa}
if
\kappa>0
), we have, for all
x\in M

\frac{m(B(x,r))}{m(B(x,r_{0}))}\geq\frac{r}{r_{0}}\inf_{\varepsilon\in[0,1]}\left(\frac{s_{\kappa}(\varepsilon r/\sqrt{p-1})}{s_{\kappa}(\varepsilon r_{0}/\sqrt{p-1})}\right)^{p-1}.

In particular, for all

4.5  Proof of Theorem
3.6

First, since inequality (
3.5
) holds for any outcome sequence, we have (almost surely)

\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{t=1}^{n+1}\ell(\theta_{t},Z_{t})-\inf_{\theta\in M}\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{t=1}^{n+1}\ell(\theta,Z_{t})\leq\frac{B_{n+1}}{n+1}.

Taking the expectation on both sides we deduce that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{t=1}^{n+1}\mathbb{E}[\ell(\theta_{t},Z_{t})]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}[\inf_{\theta\in M}\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{t=1}^{n+1}\ell(\theta,Z_{t})]+\frac{B_{n+1}}{n+1}

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{\theta\in M}\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{t=1}^{n+1}\mathbb{E}[\ell(\theta,Z_{t})]+\frac{B_{n+1}}{n+1}

\displaystyle=\inf_{\theta\in M}\mathbb{E}[\ell(\theta,Z)]+\frac{B_{n+1}}{n+1}.

(4.5)

Assumption
(A2)
and the definition of
\hat{\theta}_{n}
imply that, for all
z\in\mathcal{Z}
,

\ell(\hat{\theta}_{n},z)\leq\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{t=1}^{n+1}\ell(\theta_{t},z).

In particular,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\ell(\hat{\theta}_{n},Z)]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{t=1}^{n+1}\mathbb{E}[\ell(\theta_{t},Z)]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{t=1}^{n+1}\mathbb{E}[\ell(\theta_{t},Z_{t})],

(4.6)

where the last identity holds since
\theta_{t}=\vartheta_{t}(Z_{1},\dots,Z_{t-1})
and
Z_{t}
are independent for all
t\geq 1
. The proof then follows by combining (
4.5
) and (
4.6
).

4.6  Proof of Lemma
3.8

We use the following technical Lemma due to
Chewi et al. 2020
, refining previous results from
Ahidar-Coutrix et al. 2019
and
Le Gouic et al. 2019
. Recall that the variance of
P\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
is defined by

\mathcal{V}^{*}_{P}:=\inf_{\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\int W^{2}_{2}(\mu,.)\,\mathrm{d}P.

Lemma 4.4

(
Chewi et al. 2020
, Theorem 6)
.

Let
P\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
and
\mu^{*}
be a barycenter of
P
. Suppose that, for
P
-almost all
\nu
,
\varphi_{\mu^{*}\to\nu}
is
\alpha(\nu)
-strongly convex for a measurable
\alpha:\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Then
\mu^{*}
is unique and, for all
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

C_{\rm var}W^{2}_{2}(\mu^{*},\mu)\leq\int W^{2}_{2}(\mu,.)\,\mathrm{d}P-\mathcal{V}^{*}_{P},

for
C_{\rm var}:=\int\alpha(\nu)\,\mathrm{d}P(\nu)
.

Lemma
4.4
is remarkable since it provides, for all
\mu
, a distribution dependent analog of Jensen’s inequality for
W^{2}_{2}(\mu,.)
(see Lemma
2.10
) while this function isn’t geodesically convex. Next, we show how this result implies directly Lemma
3.8
under slightly more general conditions. For readability, we use notation

\mathcal{E}_{\mu}(\nu):=W^{2}_{2}(\mu,\nu)-\int W^{2}_{2}(\mu,.)\mathrm{d}P.

Lemma 4.5
.

Under the assumptions of Lemma
4.4
, for all
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{D})
, and all
\beta>0
satisfying

\frac{1}{\beta}\ln\int\exp(-\beta\mathcal{E}_{\mu})\,\mathrm{d}P\leq\mathcal{V}^{*}_{P},

(4.7)

we have

W^{2}_{2}(\mu^{*},\mu)\leq-\frac{C^{-1}_{\rm var}}{\beta}\ln\int\exp(-\beta W^{2}_{2}(\mu,.))\,\mathrm{d}P.

(4.8)

Proof of Lemma
4.5
.

It suffices to notice that, according to Lemma
4.4
, we obtain

\displaystyle\ln\int\exp(-\beta W^{2}_{2}(\mu,.))\,\mathrm{d}P

\displaystyle=-\beta\int W^{2}_{2}(\mu,.)\mathrm{d}P+\ln\int\exp(-\beta\mathcal{E}_{\mu})\mathrm{d}P

\displaystyle\leq-\beta C_{\rm var}W^{2}_{2}(\mu^{*},\mu)-\beta\mathcal{V}^{*}_{P}+\ln\int\exp(-\beta\mathcal{E}_{\mu})\mathrm{d}P,

which implies the claim.
∎

To prove Lemma
3.8
, it remains to observe that, if
P
has bounded support, then by Hoeffding’s Lemma we have, for any
\beta>0
,

\frac{1}{\beta}\ln\int\exp(-\beta\mathcal{E}_{\mu})\,\mathrm{d}P\leq\frac{\beta\Delta^{4}}{8}.

A.1  Proof of Theorem

The proof follows the same strategy as that of Theorem
3.2
. We only need to slightly adapt Lemma
4.2
.

Lemma A.1
.

Assume that
(A1)
and
(A2)
hold. Suppose that every
\ell\in\mathcal{L}
is geodesically convex. Then the cumulative loss of the
ewb
forecaster, with
0<\beta_{t+1}<\beta_{t}
for all
t\geq 1
, satisfies

\sum_{t=1}^{n}\ell_{t}(x_{t})\leq\inf_{\mu}\left\{\int_{M}L_{n}\,\mathrm{d}\mu+\frac{\mathcal{E}(\mu|m)}{\beta_{n+1}}\right\}+\sum_{t=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\beta_{t}}\ln\left(\int_{M}e^{\beta_{t}(\bar{\ell}_{t}-\ell_{t})}\mathrm{d}m_{t}\right),

where
\bar{\ell}_{t}:=\int_{M}\ell_{t}\mathrm{d}m_{t}
, where

and where the inf runs over all probability measures
\mu
on
M
.

Proof of Lemma
4.2
.

By geodesic convexity of
\ell_{t}
and Assumption
(A2)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\ell_{t}(x_{t})-\frac{1}{\beta_{t}}\ln\int e^{\beta_{t}(\bar{\ell}_{t}-\ell_{t})}\mathrm{d}m_{t}

\displaystyle\leq\bar{\ell}_{t}-\frac{1}{\beta_{t}}\ln\int e^{\beta_{t}(\bar{\ell}_{t}-\ell_{t})}\mathrm{d}m_{t}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\beta_{t}}\ln\int e^{-\beta_{t}\ell_{t}}\mathrm{d}m_{t}.

Summing over
1\leq t\leq n
and using Lemma
4.1
, we obtain

\sum_{t=1}^{n}\ell_{t}(x_{t})\leq-\frac{1}{\beta_{n+1}}\ln\left(\int_{M}e^{-\beta_{n+1}L_{n}}\,\mathrm{d}m\right)+\sum_{t=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\beta_{t}}\ln\left(\int_{M}e^{\beta_{t}(\bar{\ell}_{t}-\ell_{t})}\mathrm{d}m_{t}\right).

We conclude, as in Lemma
4.2
, by using the Gibbs variational principle.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.5
.

Using Lemma
A.1
, and letting
m_{\varepsilon}
be as in the proof of Theorem
3.2
, we obtain for all
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{n}\ell_{t}(x_{t})

\displaystyle\leq\int_{M}L_{n}\mathrm{d}m_{\varepsilon}+\frac{\mathcal{E}(m_{\varepsilon}|m)}{\beta_{n+1}}+\sum_{t=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\beta_{t}}\ln\left(\int_{M}e^{\beta_{t}(\bar{\ell}_{t}-\ell_{t})}\mathrm{d}m_{t}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m(M_{\varepsilon})}\int_{M_{\varepsilon}}L_{n}\mathrm{d}m+\frac{1}{\beta_{n+1}}\ln\left(\frac{1}{m(M_{\varepsilon})}\right)+\sum_{t=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\beta_{t}}\ln\left(\int_{M}e^{\beta_{t}(\bar{\ell}_{t}-\ell_{t})}\mathrm{d}m_{t}\right).

By convexity and boundedness of the losses, we deduce that, for all
y\in M

\displaystyle L_{n}(h(y,\varepsilon))

\displaystyle\leq(1-\varepsilon)L_{n}(x^{*}_{n})+\varepsilon L_{n}(y)

\displaystyle\leq L_{n}(x^{*}_{n})+\varepsilon n(b-a).

Combining this inequality with the above, we obtain

\displaystyle R_{n}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon n(b-a)+\frac{1}{\beta_{n+1}}\ln\left(\frac{1}{m(M_{\varepsilon})}\right)+\sum_{t=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\beta_{t}}\ln\left(\int_{M}e^{\beta_{t}(\bar{\ell}_{t}-\ell_{t})}\mathrm{d}m_{t}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon n(b-a)+\frac{1}{\beta_{n+1}}\ln\left(\frac{1}{m(M_{\varepsilon})}\right)+\frac{(b-a)^{2}}{8}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\beta_{t},

where the last inequality follows from Hoeffding’s Lemma. Now taking
\varepsilon=1/n
, we deduce as in the proof of Theorem
3.2
that

R_{n}\leq(b-a)+\frac{1}{\beta_{n+1}}\ln\frac{1}{c_{\kappa,p}(x^{*}_{n})}+\frac{p\ln n}{\beta_{n+1}}+\frac{(b-a)^{2}}{8}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\beta_{t}.

Hence, for
c>0
, taking

\beta_{t}=\frac{c}{(b-a)}\sqrt{\frac{p\ln(t\lor 2)}{t}},

we obtain, for all

\sum_{t=1}^{n}\beta_{t}\leq\frac{c}{(b-a)}\sqrt{p\ln n}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\leq\frac{2c\sqrt{pn\ln n}}{(b-a)}.

Using the fact that
(n+1)/\ln(n+1)\leq(3/2)(n/\ln n)
for all

, and taking
c=2(3/2)^{1/4}
, so that the two leading terms coincide, we obtain

R_{n}\leq(b-a)\left[1+\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}\left(\ln\frac{1}{c_{\kappa,p}(x^{*}_{n})}\sqrt{\frac{n}{p\ln n}}+\sqrt{pn\ln n}\right)\right],

which completes the proof.
∎

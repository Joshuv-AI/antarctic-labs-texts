Toric and tropical Bertini theorems in positive characteristic
Francesca Gandini, Milena Hering, Diane Maclagan, Fatemeh Mohammadi, Jenna Rajchgot, Ashley K. Wheeler, Josephine Yu

Abstract
. We generalize the toric Bertini theorem of Fuchs, Mantova, and Zannier [ 11 ] to positive characteristic. A key part of the proof is a new algebraically closed field containing the field 𝕜 ⁡ ( t 1 , … , t d ) \mathbbm{k}(t_{1},\dots,t_{d}) of rational functions over an algebraically closed field 𝕜 \mathbbm{k} of prime characteristic. As a corollary, we extend the tropical Bertini theorem of Maclagan and Yu [ 17 ] to arbitrary characteristic, which removes the characteristic dependence from the d d -connectivity result for tropical varieties from that paper.



1.
Introduction

Bertini’s theorem, which states that a general hyperplane section of
an irreducible variety is again irreducible, is a basic result in
algebraic geometry. This has been generalized in many different ways,
most notably for this paper by Fuchs, Mantova, and Zannier
[
11
]
,
who replace hyperplane sections by certain subtori when the variety is
a subvariety of an algebraic torus in characteristic zero. Our main result
removes this characteristic assumption, at the expense of some precision.

Theorem 1.1

(Toric Bertini)
.

Let
\mathbbm{k}
be an algebraically closed field of arbitrary characteristic.
Let
X
be a
d
-dimensional irreducible subvariety of
(\mathbbm{k}^{*})^{n}

with
d\geq 2
, and let
\pi\colon(\mathbbm{k}^{*})^{n}\rightarrow(\mathbbm{k}^{*})^{d}
be a
morphism with
\pi|_{X}
dominant and finite. Suppose that the pullback of
\pi|_{X}

along any isogeny
\mu\colon(\mathbbm{k}^{*})^{d}\rightarrow(\mathbbm{k}^{*})^{d}
is irreducible.
Then for every
1\leq r\leq d-1
the set of

r
-dimensional subtori
T\subseteq(\mathbbm{k}^{*})^{d}
with
\pi^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\theta}\cdot T)\cap X
irreducible for all
\boldsymbol{\theta}\in(\mathbbm{k}^{*})^{d}
is dense in the Grassmannian

\Gr(r,d)
.

In characteristic zero
[
11
]
show that the conclusion holds for
subtori
T
in a generic (Zariski open) set, rather than just in a dense
set. More precisely, there are a finite number of exceptional
subtori that
T
must avoid, and any other
T
will have irreducible
preimage. A similar “generic” conclusion may hold in
characteristic
p
; we are not aware of a counterexample, though our
current techniques are insufficient for a proof. The techniques of

[
11
]
cannot be extended to characteristic
p
as they rely on
results that are false in characteristic
p
.

The condition on the irreducibility of pullbacks along isogenies appears already in

[
11
]
and is necessary for a generic result;
for example, for the projection
\pi:\Var(x-y^{2}z^{2})\rightarrow(\mathbbm{k}^{*})^{2}
onto the first two coordinates, the preimage of any subtorus
of
(\mathbbm{k}^{*})^{2}
of the form
(t^{2a},t^{b})
is reducible. Thus the set of desired subtori cannot be open. However the denseness conclusion may still hold without this pullback hypothesis. We do not have a counterexample; see Remark
4.8
.

One consequence of Theorem
1.1
is that we can remove the
characteristic assumption in the Tropical Bertini theorem of Maclagan
and Yu
[
17
]
.

Theorem 1.2

(Tropical Bertini)
.

Let
X\subset(\mathbbm{k}^{*})^{n}
be an irreducible
d
-dimensional variety,
with
d\geq 2
, over an algebraically closed valued field
\mathbbm{k}
with
\mathbb{Q}

contained in the value group. The set of rational affine hyperplanes

H
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for which the intersection
\trop(X)\cap H
is
the tropicalization of an irreducible variety is dense in the
Euclidean topology on
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\mathbb{Q}}
.

The characteristic zero case of the Tropical Bertini theorem was originally introduced in
[
17
]
to prove a
higher connectivity result for tropicalizations of irreducible
varieties
[
17
]
*Theorem 1. Theorem
1.2
removes the
characteristic assumption from that connectivity theorem.

The key ingredient in the proof of Theorem
1.1
is a new field
K_{\mathbf{w}}
that contains the algebraic
closure of
\mathbbm{k}(t_{1},\dots,t_{d})
. This field is smaller, in some crucial
aspects, than previously constructed
algebraically closed fields containing
\mathbbm{k}(t_{1},\dots,t_{d})
when
\mathbbm{k}
has characteristic
p
.

The use for this field is best illustrated by the case when the
variety
X
of Theorem
1.1
is a hypersurface, the
map
\pi\colon X\rightarrow(\mathbbm{k}^{*})^{d}
is projection onto the
first
d
coordinates, and the subtorus
T\subseteq(\mathbbm{k}^{*})^{d}
is
one-dimensional:
T=\{(t^{n_{1}},\dots,t^{n_{d}}):t\in\mathbbm{k}^{*}\}

for some
\mathbf{n}=(n_{1},\dots,n_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. This is, in
fact, the core case of the proof. Given an irreducible
f\in\mathbbm{k}[t_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,t_{d}^{\pm 1},y]
, we wish to show
that the set of
\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, for which the substitution
g\in\mathbbm{k}[x^{\pm 1},y]
given by

g(x,y)=f(x^{n_{1}},\dots,x^{n_{d}},y)

remains irreducible, is dense
in
\mathbb{P}^{d-1}_{\mathbb{Q}}
. The key idea is to regard
f
as a polynomial in
y
with coefficients in
\mathbbm{k}(t_{1},\dots,t_{d})
. The polynomial
f
then factors as

f=\prod_{i=1}^{s}(y-\alpha_{i}),

where
\alpha_{i}
are in the
algebraic closure of
\mathbbm{k}(t_{1},\dots,t_{d})
. A precise description of
the algebraic closure is still unknown but several fields
containing it are known; see
[
9
,
14
,
15
,
2
,
18
,
12
,
4
,
5
,
22
]
. When
d=1
and
\charr(\mathbbm{k})=0
, the
algebraic closure of
\mathbbm{k}(t_{1})
is contained in the field of Puiseux
series
\mathbbm{k}\{\!\{t_{1}\}\!\}=\bigcup_{n\geq 1}\mathbbm{k}(\!(t_{1}^{1/n})\!)
. The
exponents appearing in a particular Puiseux series all have a fixed
common denominator. In characteristic
p
this is relaxed to allow
arbitrary powers of
p
in the denominator, subject to the
requirement, which is automatic for Puiseux series, that the set
of all exponents is well ordered. As we recall in
Section
2
, there are also multivariate
generalizations of this, so we may regard the roots
\alpha_{i}
of
f

as multivariate Laurent series with fractional exponents.

Given this description of the roots of
f
, the natural expectation is
that the roots of
g
as a polynomial in
y
are obtained from the
series
\alpha_{i}
by the same specialization
t_{i}=x^{n_{i}}
. To show
that
g
remains irreducible for most
\mathbf{n}
it then suffices to
show that none of these specializations of
\alpha_{i}
s, or the elementary symmetric
polynomials in them, which are the coefficients of any factors of
g
, are polynomials in
x
, as opposed to generalized Puiseux series.

However it is far from clear that this specialization map is well
defined. For example, given the multivariate series

\alpha=\sum_{j\geq 1}t_{1}^{1-1/2^{j}}t_{2}^{1/2^{j}}

over a field of
characteristic
2
, we cannot make the substitution
t_{1}=t_{2}=x
. A
contribution of this paper is to define a field
K_{\mathbf{w}}

that contains the algebraic closure of
\mathbbm{k}(t_{1},\dots,t_{d})
, and has
natural subrings on which this specialization map is a well-defined
ring homomorphism, so this plan of attack goes through.

The structure of the paper is as follows. The new field
K_{\mathbf{w}}
is
introduced in Section
2
, and it is shown to be
algebraically closed in Section
3
.
Theorem
1.1
is proved in Section
4
, while
Theorem
1.2
is proved in
Section
5
.

\mathbb{R}^{d}
we mean a convex set closed under multiplication by

pointed
if its closure does not contain
a line. For a cone
C
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the
dual cone

is
\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{y}\geq 0\text{ for all }\mathbf{y}\in C\}
. We denote by

\intt(C)

2.
Field Families and
p
-discreteness

In this section we construct the field
K_{\mathbf{w}}
containing

\mathbbm{k}(t_{1},\dots,t_{d})
that plays a key role in the proof of
Theorem
1.1
. We will prove in
Section
3
that
K_{\mathbf{w}}
is algebraically closed.

2.1.
Field families

We first recall previous constructions of algebraically closed fields
containing
\mathbbm{k}(t_{1},\dots,t_{d})
.

When
\charr(\mathbbm{k})=0
and
d=1
, the field of Puiseux series
\mathbbm{k}\{\!\{t\}\!\}:=\bigcup_{n\geq 0}\mathbbm{k}(\!(t^{1/n})\!)
is algebraically closed and contains

\mathbbm{k}(t)
.
Puiseux series are not algebraically closed when
\charr(\mathbbm{k})=p>0
.
This was first observed by Chevalley
[
8
]
, who observed that
the Artin–Schreier polynomial
x^{p}-x-t^{-1}
has no Puiseux series root.
Abhyankar
[
1
]
showed that the series
\sum_{j\geq 0}t^{1/p^{j}}
is a root. This was generalized by Rayner

[
21
]
, who showed that the collection

(1)

\left\{\sum c_{\alpha}t^{\alpha}:\{\alpha:c_{\alpha}\neq 0\}\text{ is well ordered, and there is }N>0\text{ with }\alpha\in\bigcup_{j\geq 0}\frac{1}{Np^{j}}\mathbb{Z}\text{ for all }\alpha\right\}

forms an algebraically closed field.
Rayner introduced the idea of a

field family
, which gives conditions on the possibilities of
supports for power series of this form. We now recall this in our setting.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a family of subsets of
an ordered abelian group
(\Gamma,\preceq)
.
For
A\in\mathcal{A}
, let
S(A)
denote the semigroup generated by
A
under addition in
\Gamma
.
Then
\mathcal{A}
is called a
field family
with respect to

\Gamma
if the following axioms hold:

(1)

Every
A\in\mathcal{A}
is well ordered, i.e., every subset has a least element with respect to
\preceq
.

(2)

The elements of members of
\mathcal{A}
generate
\Gamma
.

(3)

If
A,B\in\mathcal{A}
then
A\cup B\in\mathcal{A}
.

(4)

If
A\in\mathcal{A}
and
B\subseteq A
then
B\in\mathcal{A}
.

(5)

Given
A\in\mathcal{A}
and
\gamma\in\Gamma
, then
\gamma+A:=\{\gamma+a:\ a\in A\}\in\mathcal{A}
.

(6)

If
A\in\mathcal{A}
,
with
a\succeq 0

for all
a\in A
, then
S(A)\in\mathcal{A}
.

Given any field
\mathbbm{k}
, let
\mathbbm{k}^{\Gamma}
denote the set of all mappings
\varphi:\Gamma\to\mathbbm{k}
. The
support
of
\varphi\in\mathbbm{k}^{\Gamma}
is the set
\supp{\varphi}=\{\gamma\in\Gamma:\ \varphi(\gamma)\neq 0\}
.
For a family
\mathcal{A}
of subsets of
\Gamma
define

\mathbbm{k}^{\Gamma}(\mathcal{A})=\{\varphi\in\mathbbm{k}^{\Gamma}:\ \supp{\varphi}\in\mathcal{A}\}.

We think of elements in
\mathbbm{k}^{\Gamma}(\mathcal{A})
as formal power series,

\varphi=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\varphi(\gamma)\mathbf{t}^{\gamma}\in\mathbbm{k}^{\Gamma}(\mathcal{A}).

For
\varphi,\psi\in\mathbbm{k}^{\Gamma}(\mathcal{A})
and
\gamma\in\Gamma
, addition is defined as

(\varphi+\psi)(\gamma)=\varphi(\gamma)+\psi(\gamma)

and multiplication is defined as

(\varphi\cdot\psi)(\gamma)=\sum_{\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}=\gamma}\varphi(\gamma_{1})\psi(\gamma_{2}).

Theorem

(
[
21
]
*Theorem 1)
.

If
\mathcal{A}
is a field family then
\mathbbm{k}^{\Gamma}(\mathcal{A})
is a field.

The field in (
1
) is the case when
\Gamma=\mathbb{Q}
and

\mathcal{A}
consists of all well-ordered subsets of
\mathbb{Q}
that lie in
\bigcup_{j\geq 0}\frac{1}{Np^{j}}\mathbb{Z}
for some
N\in\mathbb{N}
.

When
\Gamma=\mathbb{Q}^{d}
the field elements can be regarded as
generalized Puiseux series in
d
variables
t_{1},\dots,t_{d}
.
For
\charr(\mathbbm{k})=0
, in
[
18
]
McDonald constructed multivariate Puiseux series
solutions
g
for equations of the form
f(t_{1},\dots,t_{d},g)=0
where
f\in\mathbbm{k}[t_{1},\dots,t_{d},y]
. The supports of the solution series are contained in sets
of the form
\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{Z}^{d}\cap C
where
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
C
is a pointed
cone.

Using Rayner’s field family formalism
Aroca and Ilardi constructed algebraically closed fields
containing
\mathbbm{k}(t_{1},\dots,t_{d})
and McDonald’s series
[
4
]
.
For
\charr(\mathbbm{k})=p>0
Saavedra gave an analogous construction of
algebraically closed fields containing
\mathbbm{k}(t_{1},\dots,t_{d})

[
22
]
. The supports of Saavedra’s series are contained in sets
of the form
\bigcup_{j\geq 0}\frac{1}{Np^{j}}\mathbb{Z}^{d}\cap C
where
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
C
is a pointed
cone.

All of these fields contain elements that are not algebraic over

\mathbbm{k}(t_{1},\dots,t_{d})
. While there is some work on characterizing the
algebraic closure of
\mathbbm{k}(t_{1},\dots,t_{d})

[
2
,
5
,
9
,
14
,
15
]

there is currently no complete answer in multiple variables and
arbitrary characteristic.

2.2.
The field of
p
-discrete series

We now use the formalism of Definition
2.1
to
construct a field containing
\mathbbm{k}(t_{1},\dots,t_{d})
but contained in
Saavedra’s field. Let
\mathbb{R}^{d}_{li}
denote the set of vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{d}

whose coordinates are linearly independent over
\mathbb{Q}
. For
\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}_{li}

we define an ordering
\preceq_{\mathbf{w}}
on the abelian group
\mathbb{Q}^{d}

as follows:

\mathbf{u}\preceq_{\mathbf{w}}\mathbf{v}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ when }\hskip 10.00002pt\mathbf{w}\cdot\mathbf{u}\leq\mathbf{w}\cdot\mathbf{v}.

The condition that the entries of
\mathbf{w}
are linearly independent over the rationals ensures that
\preceq_{\mathbf{w}}
is a total order.

Definition 2.2

(
p
-discreteness)
.

For a subset
A\subseteq\mathbb{Q}^{d}
,
\boldsymbol{\gamma}\in\mathbb{Q}^{d}
,
and

\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}_{li}
, set

A^{+}_{\boldsymbol{\gamma},\mathbf{w}}=\{\mathbf{a}\in A:\mathbf{w}\cdot\mathbf{a}>\mathbf{w}\cdot\boldsymbol{\gamma}\}\ \ \text{and}\ \ A^{-}_{\boldsymbol{\gamma},\mathbf{w}}=\{\mathbf{a}\in A:\mathbf{w}\cdot\mathbf{a}<\mathbf{w}\cdot\boldsymbol{\gamma}\}.

We say that a set
A\subseteq\mathbb{Q}^{d}
is
p
-discrete with
respect to
\mathbf{w}
if the following conditions are satisfied.

(1)

There exists an open cone
\sigma
containing
\mathbf{w}
for which the set
\{\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\cdot\mathbf{a}:\mathbf{a}\in A\}
is well ordered when
\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\in\sigma\cap\mathbb{R}^{d}_{li}
.

(2)

There is
N>0
,
\boldsymbol{\gamma}\in\mathbb{Q}^{d}
, a pointed cone
C
with
\mathbf{w}\in\intt(C^{\vee})
such that

A\subseteq(\boldsymbol{\gamma}+C)\cap\left(\bigcup_{j\geq 0}\frac{1}{Np^{j}}\mathbb{Z}^{d}\right).

(3)

For any sequence
\{\mathbf{a}_{i}\}
in
A
, if the sequence
\{\mathbf{w}\cdot\mathbf{a}_{i}\}
converges in
\mathbb{R}
then

\{\mathbf{a}_{i}\}
converges to a point of
\mathbb{Q}^{d}
.

(4)

For all
\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Q}^{d}
there
is an open cone
\sigma_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{\prime}}
containing
\mathbf{w}
for which for
all
\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\in\sigma_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{\prime}}\cap\mathbb{R}^{d}_{li}
, we have

A^{+}_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{\prime},\mathbf{w}^{\prime}}=A^{+}_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{\prime},\mathbf{w}}
and

A^{-}_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{\prime},\mathbf{w}^{\prime}}=A^{-}_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{\prime},\mathbf{w}}
.

For a given
p
-discrete set
A
we may choose the closure of
\sigma
in
condition
1
and the dual cone
C^{\vee}
of
C
in
condition
2
to coincide, at the expense of at least one
of them not being the largest possible, since
\sigma
can be replaced
with a smaller open cone and
C
can be replaced with a larger pointed
cone. In condition
4
it is
possible that a different
\sigma_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{\prime}}
is needed for each

\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{\prime}
. Note that condition
4
is equivalent
to the existence of open cones
\sigma_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{\prime}}
containing
\mathbf{w}
such that that

A^{-}_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{\prime},\mathbf{w}}\subseteq\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{\prime}-\sigma_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{\prime}}^{\vee}
, and
A^{+}_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{\prime},\mathbf{w}}\subseteq\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{\prime}+\sigma_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{\prime}}^{\vee}
.

Definition 2.3
.

For fixed
\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}_{li}
, we define
\mathcal{A}_{\mathbf{w}}
to be the
set

(
*
)

\mathcal{A}_{\mathbf{w}}=\{A\subset\mathbb{Q}^{d}:A\text{ is }p\text{-discrete with respect to }\mathbf{w}\}.

.

, where the well ordering is

Lemma 2.5
.

Fix
\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}_{li}
, and
\boldsymbol{\gamma}\in\mathbb{Q}^{d}
. Suppose
A\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathbf{w}}
with
\mathbf{w}\cdot\mathbf{a}\geq 0
for all
\mathbf{a}\in A
.
Let
S(A)
be the semigroup generated by
A
under addition in
\mathbb{Q}^{d}
and let
S(A)^{-}_{\boldsymbol{\gamma},\mathbf{w}}
be as in Definition
2.2
. Then there is
M>0
such that for any nonzero
\mathbf{s}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathbf{a}_{i}\in S(A)^{-}_{\boldsymbol{\gamma},\mathbf{w}}
, where
\mathbf{a}_{i}\in A\setminus\{0\}
, the number
m
of summands is at most
M
.

Proof.

We have
\mathbf{w}\cdot\mathbf{s}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathbf{w}\cdot\mathbf{a}_{i}
, so

0<\min_{i}\{\mathbf{w}\cdot\mathbf{a}_{i}\}\leq\frac{\mathbf{w}\cdot\mathbf{s}}{m}<\frac{\mathbf{w}\cdot\boldsymbol{\gamma}}{m},

where the last inequality holds because
\mathbf{s}\in S(A)^{-}_{\boldsymbol{\gamma},\mathbf{w}}
. If for all
m>0
there is

\mathbf{s}\in S(A)^{-}_{\boldsymbol{\gamma},\mathbf{w}}
that is the sum of at least
m
nonzero terms, we would
get a contradiction to well-ordering of the set
\{\mathbf{w}\cdot\mathbf{a}\colon\mathbf{a}\in A\}
given in condition
1
.
∎

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
A\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathbf{w}}
and let
S(A)
be the semigroup generated by elements of
A
under addition. Suppose that
\mathbf{w}\cdot\mathbf{a}\geq 0
for all
\mathbf{a}\in A
, and
conditions
1
,
2
,

3
of

-discreteness hold for
S(A)
.
Then
S(A)
also satisfies
condition
4
, so

is in
\mathcal{A}_{\mathbf{w}}
.

Proof.

Fix
\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Q}^{d}
. It suffices to show there are open cones

\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
containing
\mathbf{w}
for which

S(A)^{+}_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{\prime},\mathbf{w}}\subseteq S(A)^{+}_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{\prime},\mathbf{w}^{\prime}}
for all

\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\in\sigma_{1}\cap\mathbb{R}^{d}_{li}
and

S(A)^{-}_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{\prime},\mathbf{w}}\subseteq S(A)^{-}_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{\prime},\mathbf{w}^{\prime}}

for all

\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\in\sigma_{2}\cap\mathbb{R}^{d}_{li}
.

We first show the existence of
\sigma_{1}
. Since
\{\mathbf{w}\cdot\mathbf{a}:\mathbf{a}\in S(A)^{+}_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{\prime},\mathbf{w}}\}

is
well ordered by
1
there is
\mathbf{a}\in S(A)^{+}_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{\prime},\mathbf{w}}
achieving the minimum value of

\mathbf{w}\cdot\mathbf{a}
, so

\delta:=\min\{\mathbf{w}\cdot\mathbf{a}:\mathbf{a}\in S(A)^{+}_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{\prime},\mathbf{w}}\}-\mathbf{w}\cdot\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{\prime}

is positive. By condition
2
, we have
S(A)\subseteq\boldsymbol{\gamma}+C
for a pointed cone
C
with
\mathbf{w}\in\intt(C^{\vee})
and
\boldsymbol{\gamma}\in\mathbb{Q}^{d}
. If
\mathbf{w}\cdot\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{\prime}\leq\mathbf{w}\cdot\boldsymbol{\gamma}
then we may take
\sigma_{1}

to be the interior of
\{\mathbf{x}:\mathbf{x}\cdot\boldsymbol{\gamma}\geq\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\cdot\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{\prime}\}\cap C^{\vee}
. We now suppose
that
\mathbf{w}\cdot\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{\prime}>\mathbf{w}\cdot\boldsymbol{\gamma}
. We may
assume that
C
is full-dimensional here, so there is a rational point

\tilde{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}
in
\boldsymbol{\gamma}+C\cap\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\colon\mathbf{w}\cdot\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{\prime}<\mathbf{w}\cdot\mathbf{x}<\mathbf{w}\cdot\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{\prime}+\delta\}
. Let
C^{\prime}
be the closure of
the cone generated by
\{\mathbf{a}-\tilde{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}:\mathbf{a}\in S(A)^{+}_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{\prime},\mathbf{w}}\}
. The cone
C^{\prime}
is pointed, as it is
contained in the cone with vertex
\tilde{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}
over the

\boldsymbol{\gamma}+C\cap\{\mathbf{x}:\mathbf{w}\cdot\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{w}\cdot\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{\prime}+\delta\}
, which is bounded. It also
contains
\mathbf{w}
in its dual. Thus any open cone
\sigma_{1}

containing
\mathbf{w}
and contained in
\intt({C^{\prime}}^{\vee})\cap\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\colon\mathbf{x}\cdot(\tilde{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}-\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{\prime})\geq 0\}
will have the property that

S(A)^{+}_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{\prime},\mathbf{w}}\subseteq S(A)^{+}_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{\prime},\mathbf{w}^{\prime}}
for
all
\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\in\sigma_{1}
.

We next show the existence of
\sigma_{2}
.

By Lemma
2.5
there is a bound
M
on the number of summands
m
in any
element
\mathbf{a}\in S(A)^{-}_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{\prime},\mathbf{w}}

Let

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\alpha\in K_{C}
be an element with infinite but bounded support. Then there
exists an open cone
C^{\prime}\subseteq C
containing
\mathbf{w}
and a sublattice
H\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
such that for any integer vector

\mathbf{n}\in(C^{\prime}\setminus H)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, the substitution

\phi^{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{\mathbf{n}}(\alpha)
is not a polynomial. Moreover, the sublattice

H
can be chosen to have an arbitrarily large index in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.

Proof.

The support of
\alpha
must contain points in
\mathbb{Q}^{d}
with coordinates
whose denominators (in reduced form) are arbitrarily large because
there are only finitely many elements in
\mathbb{Q}^{d}
in a bounded region
with denominators smaller than a given bound. Let
\mathbf{v}
be an
element of
\supp(\alpha)
with a non-integer coordinate. By
axiom
4
of
p
-discreteness, there is an open cone
C^{\prime}\subseteq C

containing
\mathbf{w}
for which for any
\mathbf{n}\in C^{\prime}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}
the term
\mathbf{v}
is the only preimage of the
map (
6
), so does not get cancelled by the
substitution
\phi^{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{\mathbf{n}}
.
Let
H=\{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\mid\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{Z}\}
. For all integer vectors
\mathbf{n}\in C^{\prime}

not in
H
, the map
\phi_{\mathbf{n}}^{\boldsymbol{\theta}}

sends the term
\mathbf{t^{v}}

in
\alpha
to the term
\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\mathbf{v}}x^{\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{v}}
in
\phi^{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{\mathbf{n}}(\alpha)
with
non-integer exponent, so
\phi^{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{\mathbf{n}}(\alpha)
is not a polynomial.
The lattice
H
is a sublattice of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
but is not all of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
because
if the denominator (in reduced form) of

th coordinate of

\mathbf{v}
is

, then
\mathbf{e}_{j},2\mathbf{e}_{j},\dots,(M-1)\mathbf{e}_{j}
are not
in

, so

has index at least

in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.
The element

\mathbf{v}\in\supp(\alpha)
can be chosen to have a coordinate with
an arbitrarily large denominator, so the sublattice

to avoid can
be chosen to have an arbitrarily large index.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let

be irreducible and monic in
y
.
Suppose that the projection of
\Var(f)\subseteq(\mathbbm{k}^{*})^{d}\times\mathbb{A}^{1}

onto the first
d
coordinates
has the PB property.
Fix
\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}_{li}
.
Then there exists an open cone
C
containing

\mathbf{w}
and finitely many sublattices
H_{1},\dots,H_{r}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
such that for all
vectors
\mathbf{n}\in(C\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d})\setminus(H_{1}\cup\cdots\cup H_{r})
, and all
\boldsymbol{\theta}\in(\mathbbm{k}^{*})^{d}
the Laurent polynomial

f(\theta_{1}x^{n_{1}},\dots,\theta_{d}x^{n_{d}},y)\in\mathbbm{k}[x^{\pm 1},y]
is irreducible. Moreover, the sublattices can be
chosen to be of arbitrarily high index.

Proof.

Consider
f
as a polynomial in
K_{\mathbf{w}}[y]
. Since

K_{\mathbf{w}}
is algebraically closed, we can write

f=\prod_{i=1}^{s}(y-\alpha_{i})

where
\alpha_{i}\in K_{\mathbf{w}}
.
By the construction of
K_{\mathbf{w}}
, there is
N>0
for which every denominator of an
exponent appearing in some
\alpha_{i}
can be put in the form
Np^{j}

for some
j\geq 0
, where
p=1
if
\charr(\mathbbm{k})=0
. The isogeny
\mu\colon(\mathbbm{k}^{*})^{d}\rightarrow(\mathbbm{k}^{*})^{d}
given by
t_{i}\mapsto t_{i}^{N}

extends to an inclusion of fields
\mu\colon K_{\mathbf{w}}\rightarrow K_{\mathbf{w}}
, and a map

K_{\mathbf{w}}[y]\rightarrow K_{\mathbf{w}}[y]
, sending
y
to
y
.
We then have
g:=\mu(f)=\prod_{i=1}^{s}(y-\mu(\alpha_{i}))
. It
suffices to prove the theorem for
g
, as

\phi^{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{\mathbf{n}}(g)=\phi_{N\mathbf{n}}^{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(f)
, and
if
\phi_{N\mathbf{n}}^{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(f)
is irreducible, so is

\phi^{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{\mathbf{n}}(f)
. We thus henceforth assume that
N=1
.

Since
N=1
,
all denominators are powers of
p
, so by axioms
1
and
2
of
p
-discreteness there is an open cone
C
with
\alpha_{i}\in K_{C}

for all
1\leq i\leq s
. Thus for all
\mathbf{n}\in C\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and all
\boldsymbol{\theta}\in(\mathbbm{k}^{*})^{d}
we have

\phi_{\mathbf{n}}^{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(f)=\prod_{i=1}^{s}(y-\phi_{\mathbf{n}}^{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\alpha_{i})),

so all roots of
\phi_{\mathbf{n}}^{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(f)
have the form
\phi_{\mathbf{n}}^{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\alpha_{i})
for a root
\alpha_{i}
of
f
.
Since
\phi^{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{\mathbf{n}}(f)
is monic, if it is not irreducible, then it can be factored into monic polynomials, and monic factors of
\phi^{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{\mathbf{n}}(f)
are images under
\phi^{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{\mathbf{n}}
of monic factors of
f\in K_{\mathbf{w}}[y]
. Thus we can consider all of the finitely many ways to factor
f
into
two monic polynomials in
K_{\mathbf{w}}[y]
and show that “most” substitutions of

\mathbf{n}
do not make the factors into polynomials in
\mathbbm{k}[x^{\pm 1},y]
.
Since

f
has
\deg_{y}(f)
roots in
K_{\mathbf{w}}
, there are
m:=2^{\deg_{y}(f)-1}-1
ways to factor
f
into two monic polynomials.

Since the projection
\pi:\Var(f)\to(\mathbbm{k}^{*})^{d}
onto the first
d

coordinates has the PB property,
f
cannot be factored into
polynomials in
K_{\mathbf{w}}[y]
whose coefficients in
K_{\mathbf{w}}
have finite
support. If it did have such a factorization, then the isogeny

\lambda:t_{i}\mapsto t_{i}^{N^{\prime}}
would clear the common denominator
N^{\prime}
of
the exponents of the coefficients in the factorization, violating the
PB property. If a factorization of
f
in
K_{\mathbf{w}}[y]
involves a
coefficient with unbounded support, then by
Lemma
4.3
there is an open cone containing

\mathbf{w}
such that any integer vector
\mathbf{n}
in this cone gives
a substitution that is not a polynomial factorization. If a
factorization involves a coefficient whose support is infinite but
bounded, by Lemma
4.4
we can choose a
sublattice of index greater than
m
for
\mathbf{n}
to avoid. Since
the union of
m
lattices with index greater than
m
cannot cover all
of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, the conclusion follows.
∎

Remark 4.7
.

The use of the field
K_{\mathbf{w}}
, instead of one of the larger fields of
generalized Puiseux series such as the one constructed in

[
22
]
, was crucial for Theorem

. The fact
that the polynomial

factors completely is a consequence of the

is algebraically closed
(Theorem

), which used

of Definition
2.2
. The
fact that the roots of the transformed polynomial

all live in

for some open cone

is a consequence of
axiom

of Definition

.
Without this reduction, the specialization homomorphism

might not be well defined.

We are now ready to prove the main theorem of this paper. Recall that
a morphism
\psi\colon(\mathbbm{k}^{*})^{r}\rightarrow(\mathbbm{k}^{*})^{d}
is given in
coordinates by
\psi(t_{1},\dots,t_{r})=(c_{1}\mathbf{t}^{\mathbf{a}_{1}},\dots,c_{d}\mathbf{t}^{\mathbf{a}_{d}})

for all

is an embedding of tori.
Let

be the

matrix with columns the

The morphism
\psi
is an embedding if the matrix

has rank

and the greatest common divisor of the

correspond to row

correspond to rational points in the Grassmannian

.

\boldsymbol{\theta}\in(\mathbbm{k}^{*})^{d}
, so
{\pi^{\prime}}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\theta}\cdot T)\cap\Var(f)
is irreducible, and thus
\pi|_{X}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\theta}\cdot T)
is irreducible for all such
\boldsymbol{\theta}
. As
T
is close to

T_{0}
in
\Gr(r,d)
, this proves the theorem.
∎

Remark 4.8
.

Theorem
1.1
differs from the version in

[
11
]
, in that we only show that the set of exceptional tori are
the complement of a dense set, rather than essentially finite. As
already discussed, the finiteness is not achievable without the PB
condition we, and
[
11
]
, impose. We do not know whether the PB
condition is necessary for the conclusion of
Theorem
1.1
. In our proof the main use is to
guarantee that the sublattices
H_{1},\dots,H_{r}
to avoid do not cover

\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, so the “genericity” condition for
\mathbf{n}
is
nonempty. However we do not know an example of a projection
\pi

failing PB where the union of the lattices is all of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.
In addition, it is possible that the stronger finiteness conclusion
of
[
11
]
holds when we assume PB. To prove this using our
techniques, we would need a deeper understanding of the structure of
the cones
C
used in Theorem
4.6
. In characteristic
zero, under mild hypotheses, McDonald
[
18
]
relates these cones
to the normal fan of the fiber polytope of a certain projection of
the Newton polytope of the polynomial
f
. It would be interesting
to understand to what extent that can be generalized to arbitrary
characteristic.

5.
Tropical Bertini Theorem

One application of Theorem
1.1
is that the tropical Bertini theorem of
[
17
]
holds in arbitrary characteristic, and thus the
d
-connectivity of the tropicalization of
d
-dimensional irreducible varieties holds in arbitrary characteristic.

The
tropicalization
of a variety
X\subseteq(\mathbbm{k}^{*})^{n}
, where
\mathbbm{k}
is a valued field with valuation
\val\colon\mathbbm{k}^{*}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, is

\trop(X)=\cl((\val(x_{1}),\dots,\val(x_{n})):(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in X(L)),

where
L/\mathbbm{k}
is a nontrivially valued algebraically closed
field extension, and
\cl()
is the closure in the usual Euclidean
topology on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. By the Structure Theorem (see, for
example,
[
16
]
*Chapter 3, or
[
6
]
)

\trop(X)
is the support of a connected polyhedral complex.

In
[
17
]
Maclagan and Yu proved a tropical Bertini theorem in characteristic zero. We now remove the characteristic assumption.

We identify rational affine hyperplanes in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with points
of
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\mathbb{Q}}
.

Theorem
1.2

(Tropical Bertini)
.

Let
X\subset(\mathbbm{k}^{*})^{n}
be an irreducible
d
-dimensional variety,
with
d\geq 2
, over an algebraically closed field
\mathbbm{k}
with
\mathbb{Q}

contained in the value group. The set of rational affine hyperplanes

H
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for which the intersection
\trop(X)\cap H
is
the tropicalization of an irreducible variety is dense in the
Euclidean topology on
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\mathbb{Q}}
.

Proof.

We only need to replace a few sentences from the proof of

[
17
]
*Theorem 5. Explicitly, the proof holds
essentially verbatim (with the forward reference to Theorem 8 replaced
by a forward reference to our Theorem
1.1
) until
the sentence of
[
17
]
*Theorem 5 “We can now
apply Theorem 8 to
\rho|_{Y}\colon Y\rightarrow(\mathbbm{k}^{*})^{d}
”, where “Theorem 8” should again be replaced by Theorem
1.1
of this paper. The proof then continues as follows.

By Theorem
1.1
, since
\rho|_{Y}
satisfies PB, and
is dominant and finite, the set of
(d-1)
-dimensional subtori
T^{\prime}\subseteq(\mathbbm{k}^{*})^{d}
such that
\pi_{X}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\theta}\cdot T^{\prime})
is irreducible
for all
\boldsymbol{\theta}\in(\mathbbm{k}^{*})^{d}
is dense in
\Gr(d-1,d)\cong\mathbb{P}^{d-1}_{\mathbb{Q}}
. By definition, this means that the set of
affine hyperplanes
\overline{H}=\trop(\boldsymbol{\theta}\cdot T^{\prime})
for such
\boldsymbol{\theta},T^{\prime}
is dense in

\mathbb{P}^{d}_{\mathbb{Q}}
.

Fix one such
\overline{H}=\trop(\boldsymbol{\theta}\cdot T^{\prime})
, and let
H
be the
hyperplane in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
defined by
H=(\trop(\rho)^{-1}(\overline{H}))
. Since
\rho
is a monomial map, we have
H=\trop(\rho^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\theta}\cdot T^{\prime}))
.
As
\trop(\rho)
is injective on every maximal face of
\trop(Y)
, the intersection
H\cap\trop(Y)
is transverse, so by the Transverse Intersection Lemma
[
20
]
*Theorem 1.1,
[
7
]
*Lemma 15,

[
16
]
*Theorem 3.4.12,

H\cap\trop(Y)=\trop(\rho^{-1}|_{Y}(\boldsymbol{\theta}\cdot T^{\prime})).

Thus
H\cap\trop(Y)
is the tropicalization of the irreducible variety

\rho|_{Y}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\theta}\cdot T^{\prime})
. Note that

\trop(\mu^{\prime}(\rho|_{Y}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\theta}\cdot T^{\prime})))=\trop(\mu^{\prime})(H\cap\trop(Y))=\trop(\mu^{\prime})(H)\cap\trop(X)=\trop(\pi)^{-1}(\trop(\mu)(\overline{H})).

Since
\trop(\mu)\in\GL(n,\mathbb{Q})
, and the set of
\overline{H}
is dense in
\mathbb{P}^{d}_{\mathbb{Q}}
, the set of
\trop(\mu)(\overline{H})
is dense in
\mathbb{P}^{d}_{\mathbb{Q}}
as required.
∎

In
[
17
]
Theorem
1.2
is used to prove
a higher connectivity theorem for tropical varieties. While the
statement of
[
17
]
*Theorem 1 assumes that the field
\mathbbm{k}

has characteristic zero, as observed in
[
17
]
*Remark 11
this is only needed to apply the tropical Bertini theorem, so in light
of Theorem
1.2
we have the following
generalization of
[
17
]
*Theorem 1. A pure polyhedral
complex
\Sigma
of dimension
d
is
d
-connected through codimension
one if it is still connected after removing any
d-1
closed facets.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\mathbbm{k}
be a field that is either algebraically closed, complete, or
real closed with convex valuation ring. Let
X
be a
d
-dimensional
irreducible subvariety of
(\mathbbm{k}^{*})^{n}
.
Let
\Sigma
be a pure

d
-dimensional rational polyhedral complex with support
|\Sigma|=\trop(X)
. Write
\ell
for the dimension of the lineality space of

\Sigma
. Then
\Sigma
is
(d-\ell)
-connected through codimension
one.

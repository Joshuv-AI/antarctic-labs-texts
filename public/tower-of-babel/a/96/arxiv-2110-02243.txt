The Quadrupole Moment of Compact Binaries to the Fourth post-Newtonian Order II. Dimensional Regularization and Renormalization
François Larrouturou, Luc Blanchet, Quentin Henry, Guillaume Faye

Abstract
The regularization and renormalization of the radiative mass-type quadrupole moment of inspiralling compact binaries (without spins) is investigated at the fourth post-Newtonian (4PN) approximation of general relativity. As clear from the conservative 4PN equations of motion, a dimensional regularization has to be implemented in order to properly treat the non-linear interactions experienced by gravitational waves during their propagation toward future null infinity. By implementing such procedure, we show that the poles coming from the source moment (computed in a companion paper) are exactly cancelled in the radiative moment, as expected for a physical quantity. We thus define and obtain a “ renormalized ” source quadrupole, three-dimensional by nature, which is an important step towards the computation of the gravitational-wave flux with 4PN accuracy. Furthermore, we explicitly prove the equivalence between the dimensional regularization and the previously used Hadamard partie finie scheme up to the 3PN order.

I
Introduction

The mass quadrupole moment of compact binary systems is a crucial ingredient toward the definition of accurate gravitational wave (GW) templates in the post-Newtonian (PN) approximation. Such PN templates represent the main technique for detection and analysis of binary neutron star signals in the current network of detectors on ground, and they are at the basis of effective phenomenological methods such as EOB (effective-one-body) and IMR (inspiral-merger-ringdown), able to describe the late inspiral and merger of binary black holes (see more details in the reviews
Maggiore 2008
;
Blanchet 2014
;
Buonanno and Sathyaprakash 2015
;
Porto 2016
).

Previous computations of the quadrupole moment in the case of non-spinning compact binaries achieved the 2PN order
Blanchet et al. 1995a
;
Blanchet et al. 1995b
;
Will and Wiseman 1996
;
Leibovich et al. 2020
, then, the 3PN order
Blanchet et al. 2002
;
Blanchet and Iyer 2004
;
Blanchet et al. 2004
;
Blanchet et al. 2005
, and the state-of-the-art is currently the 4PN order. A preliminary calculation was done at the 4PN order
Marchand et al. 2020
where all the terms were computed using a series of techniques (extensively documented in
Marchand et al. 2020
); however, as recognized in this paper, the computation was incomplete because the infra-red (IR) divergences appearing at the 4PN order were regularized thanks to the Hadamard partie finie regularization instead of the required dimensional regularization. By contrast, the ultra-violet (UV) divergences, due to the point-like nature of the source (model of compact objects by point masses without internal structure) were correctly treated with dimensional regularization
Marchand et al. 2020
.

Recently, we analyzed in Ref.
Larrouturou et al. 2021
the IR divergences of the (source type) mass quadrupole moment of compact binaries at 4PN order by means of dimensional regularization. This study led to the presence of poles in the dimension,
i.e.

\propto\varepsilon^{-1}\equiv(d-3)^{-1}
, arising already from the 3PN order, and, of course, also contributing at the 4PN order. The thorough computation of IR poles and the finite terms following the poles was done in
Larrouturou et al. 2021
.

Furthermore, we also obtained
Larrouturou et al. 2021
the contribution of propagating GW tails in the source quadrupole itself. This effect is due to retarded correlations over arbitrarily large time spans in the dynamics of the source
Blanchet and Damour 1988
. It is exactly the analogue of the GW tail effect which adds a non-local piece to the conservative dynamics (equations of motion and Lagrangian/Hamiltonian) at the 4PN order
Damour et al. 2014
;
Damour et al. 2016
;
Bernard et al. 2016
;
Bernard et al. 2017
;
Marchand et al. 2018
;
Foffa and Sturani 2012
;
Galley et al. 2016
;
Foffa and Sturani 2019
;
Foffa et al. 2019
and beyond
Blümlein et al. 2021
;
Bini et al. 2021
. As such, it induces a non-locality in time in the mass quadrupole moment at the 4PN order.

The present work is the follow-up of the companion paper
Larrouturou et al. 2021
. We prove that the IR poles are cancelled by UV poles coming from the contributions of non-linear effects, essentially the “tails-of-tails” and the “tails-of-memory”, arising in the radiative type quadrupole moment, which constitutes the physical observable at future null infinity.

More precisely, at the 3PN order, the non-linearities are made of the tail-of-tails due to cubic interaction between two masses and the quadrupole
Blanchet 1998a
, and the interactions between one mass and two mass dipoles (the latter naturally vanishes in the center-of-mass frame). Taking into account the pole term and the finite term beyond the pole, we find that the contributions from dimensional regularization in the radiative moment exactly cancel out at this order. This proves that at 3PN order the quadrupole moment can be computed using the Hadamard regularization for the IR divergences (but the dimensional regularization for the UV) and, therefore, we totally confirm the previous calculations of the 3PN quadrupole moment
Blanchet et al. 2002
;
Blanchet and Iyer 2004
;
Blanchet et al. 2004
;
Blanchet et al. 2005
.

Furthermore, we prove that the same cancellation holds for the 3PN current quadrupole moment, thus supporting the recent computation of this moment at 3PN order using the Hadamard regularization for the IR
Henry et al. 2021
, and for the 3PN mass octupole moment, also confirming the previous calculation done in
Faye et al. 2015
.

Finally, we demonstrate that all the poles cancel in the radiative type quadrupole moment up to 4PN order. At the 4PN order, in addition to tails-of-tails, the main non-linear effects to consider in the radiative moment are the so-called tails-of-memory, which are cubic interations between one mass monopole and two quadrupole moments. However, at the 4PN order, the net contribution of the dimensional regularization is not vanishing and, thus, we determine some finite contributions beyond the poles which are crucial to include into the final expression of the mass quadrupole moment.

To control the occurrence of poles from the non-linear multipole interactions, we have to obtain a general expression for the retarded integral of some extended source in
d
dimensions up to cubic order, and compute from it the difference up to order

between the dimensional regularization or, more precisely, the so-called
B\varepsilon
regularization which we systematically employ
Bernard et al. 2017
;
Marchand et al. 2018
;
Larrouturou et al. 2021
, and the previously used Hadamard regularization.

, but this time a retarded solution, therefore regular at infinity, when
r\to+\infty
. This is that solution
h_{L}^{\infty}
which will be responsible for the contribution of far zone tails in the difference between the dimensional and Hadamard regularizations, as we will show in the next section.

III
The difference between two regularization schemes

Up to vanishingly small terms when
\varepsilon\to 0
, the difference between the dimensional regularization and the Hadamard one comes only from the bound
{r^{\prime}}=0
in the general formula for the retarded integral,
i.e.
it comes only from the part of the integral
h_{L}^{<}
given by (
16
). This is clear because only the bound
{r^{\prime}}=0
can give a pole in dimensional regularization, and any integration over an interval excluding
{r^{\prime}}=0
will have a finite limit when
\varepsilon\to 0
, and so cancels out between the two regularization in the limit
\varepsilon\to 0
. This further means that for the computation of the difference we can replace the integral from 0 to
r
in (
16
) by the integral from 0 up to
+\infty
(or any finite constant),
i.e.
we can just consider the homogeneous retarded solution
h_{L}^{\infty}
given by (
20
).

Thus our statement is that modulo terms of order
\varepsilon
the difference is

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}h_{L}=\mathcal{D}h^{\infty}_{L}+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)\,,

(21)

where the difference operator
\mathcal{D}
is formally defined as the commutator between the three-dimensional limit (
viz.

\lim_{3d}
) and the PF operator:

\mathcal{D}A\equiv\left[\lim_{3d}\,,\mathop{\mathrm{PF}}_{B=0}\right]\,A=\lim_{3d}\left\{\mathop{\mathrm{PF}}_{B=0}\,A\right\}-\mathop{\mathrm{PF}}_{B=0}\left\{\lim_{3d}\,A\right\}=A^{B\varepsilon}-A^{\text{Had}}\,.

(22)

Here, by “3d” limit, we really mean the expansion
\varepsilon\to 0
, while crucially keeping all the divergent poles
\varepsilon^{-1}
, and neglecting terms
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)
. By contrast,
\mathop{\mathrm{PF}}_{B=0}
discards the poles
B^{-1}
, so the difference
\mathcal{D}A
is actually a function of the poles
\varepsilon^{-1}
but does not depend on
B
.

Since
h^{\infty}_{L}
is a retarded homogeneous solution of the wave equation in
d
dimensions, it can be written in multipolar form as

\displaystyle h_{L}^{\infty}=\hat{\partial}_{L}\,\biggl[\frac{\tilde{k}}{r^{d-2}}\,\int_{1}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}y\,\gamma_{\frac{1-d}{2}}(y)\,H(t-yr)\biggr]\,,

(23)

where we pose

\displaystyle H(t)

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{\ell}\int_{1}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}z\,\gamma_{\frac{1-d}{2}-\ell}(z)\int_{0}^{+\infty}\!\mathrm{d}{r^{\prime}}\,{r^{\prime}}^{B-\ell+1}\,\left[\widehat{N}_{\varepsilon}^{(-2\ell-1)}\bigl({r^{\prime}},t-z{r^{\prime}}\bigr)-\widehat{N}_{\varepsilon}^{(-2\ell-1)}\bigl({r^{\prime}},t+z{r^{\prime}}\bigr)\right]\,.

(24)

We recall that the “
\varepsilon
-transform” operation is defined by (
12
).

As we consider only the limit
{r^{\prime}}\to 0
we insert into (
24
) the near-zone expansion of the source term
N(r,t)
in
d
dimensions, which will turn out to be always of the generic type (as shown in Sec.
IV
)

\displaystyle N(r,t)=\sum_{p,q}r^{-p-q\varepsilon}f_{p,q}(t)\,,

(25)

where the coefficients
f_{p,q}(t)
may be complicated non-local integrals involving poles, and the singularity is not essential,
i.e.
the maximal divergence when
r\to 0
is finite, say
p\leqslant p_{0}
. Furthermore, we have explicitly checked that
q
takes values in a finite range, say
q_{0}\leqslant q\leqslant q_{1}
. The
d
-dimensional coefficients
f_{p,q}(t)
match the corresponding three-dimensional coefficients when
\varepsilon\to 0
, in the sense that

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\,\sum_{q}r^{-q\varepsilon}\,f_{p,q}(t)=f^{(3d)}_{p}(t)+f^{(3d,\text{ln})}_{p}(t)\ln\left(\frac{r}{\ell_{0}}\right)\,.

(26)

The three-dimensional logarithm comes from possible poles in
f_{p,q}
, see also Eq. (3.9) of
Larrouturou et al. 2021
, but in our practical calculations the limit (
26
) is finite. Furthermore, in our case, the source does not bear double poles so that only simple logarithms show up in the three-dimensional limit. By inserting (
25
) into (
24
) we obtain

\displaystyle H(t)\!

\displaystyle=\!\sum_{p,q}\Gamma_{\ell}\!\int_{1}^{+\infty}\!\!\mathrm{d}z\,\gamma_{-1-\ell-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}(z)\!\int_{0}^{+\infty}\!\!\mathrm{d}{r^{\prime}}{r^{\prime}}^{B-\ell+1-p-q\varepsilon}\,\biggl[{}_{\varepsilon}\widehat{f}_{p,q}^{(-2\ell-1)}\bigl(t-z{r^{\prime}}\bigr)\!-\!{}_{\varepsilon}\widehat{f}_{p,q}^{(-2\ell-1)}\bigl(t+z{r^{\prime}}\bigr)\biggr]\,,

(27)

and by posing
\rho=zr^{\prime}
, we find that the integral over
z
factorizes out and can nicely be computed in closed analytic form.
6
6

6

For that we use

\int_{1}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}z\,z^{\alpha}\,\gamma_{s}(z)=\sqrt{\pi}\frac{\Gamma(-s-\frac{\alpha+1}{2})}{\Gamma(-s-\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(\frac{1-\alpha}{2})}.

Finally, we are able to perform a series of integrations by parts (with all integrated terms at infinity vanishing) to arrive at

\displaystyle H(t)\!

\displaystyle=\!-2^{\ell-1+\varepsilon}\pi^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}\sum_{p,q}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\!\!\mathrm{d}\rho\,Q_{\ell}^{p,q}\bigl(B,\varepsilon,\rho\bigr)\,\biggl[(-)^{\ell+p}{}_{\varepsilon}\widehat{f}_{p,q}^{(-\ell-2+p)}\bigl(t-\rho\bigr)+{}_{\varepsilon}\widehat{f}_{p,q}^{(-\ell-2+p)}\bigl(t+\rho\bigr)\biggr]\,,

(28)

in which we have inserted
\Gamma_{\ell}\equiv 2^{\ell+d-4}\pi^{\frac{d}{2}-2}\Gamma(\frac{d}{2}+\ell-1)
, and posed

\displaystyle Q_{\ell}^{p,q}\bigl(B,\varepsilon,\rho\bigr)\equiv\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{B+\ell+3-p-(q-1)\varepsilon}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\text{$-B+q\varepsilon$}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{B-\ell+3-p-q\varepsilon}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(-B+\ell-1+p+q\varepsilon\right)}\,\rho^{B-q\varepsilon}\,.

(29)

With this result we straightforwardly obtain the difference
\mathcal{D}H
corresponding to the calculation with the
B\varepsilon
method minus the Hadamard regularization which is just the finite part at
B=0
in three dimensions. Of course we use the link between
d
dimensions and 3 dimensions saying that the functions
f_{p,q}(t)
match their counterparts in 3 dimensions, see (
26
). From (
28
), it is clear that the difference reads as

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}H(t)

\displaystyle=-2^{\ell-1+\varepsilon}\pi^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}\sum_{p,q}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\!\mathrm{d}\rho\,\mathcal{D}Q_{\ell}^{p,q}\bigl(\varepsilon,\rho\bigr)\,\biggl[(-)^{\ell+p}{}_{\varepsilon}\widehat{f}_{p,q}^{(-\ell-2+p)}\bigl(t-\rho\bigr)+{}_{\varepsilon}\widehat{f}_{p,q}^{(-\ell-2+p)}\bigl(t+\rho\bigr)\biggr]\,,

(30)

where we recall that
\mathcal{D}Q_{\ell}^{p,q}\bigl(\varepsilon,\rho\bigr)
denotes the difference of the quantities (
29
) evaluated with the two regularizations, and given explicitly by (
22
) as

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}Q_{\ell}^{p,q}\bigl(\varepsilon,\rho\bigr)\equiv\lim_{3d}\left\{\mathop{\mathrm{PF}}_{B=0}\bigl[Q_{\ell}^{p,q}\bigl(B,\varepsilon,\rho\bigr)\bigr]\right\}-\mathop{\mathrm{PF}}_{B=0}\bigl[Q_{\ell}^{p,q}\bigl(B,0,\rho\bigr)\bigr]\,.

(31)

Finally it is a straightforward (although fairly tedious) matter to evaluate explicitly the difference (
31
) neglecting vanishing terms in the limit
\varepsilon\to 0
. We find that there are two cases where the difference is not zero:

1.

When
p=\ell+2i+3
where
i\in\mathbb{N}
and
q\neq 1
, in which case we have

\displaystyle\hskip-8.5359pt\mathcal{D}Q_{\ell}^{p,q}=\frac{(-)^{\ell}(\ell+i)!}{i!(2\ell+2i+1)!}\frac{1}{q-1}\biggl[\frac{1}{\varepsilon}-\sum_{k=0}^{\ell+i}\frac{1}{2k+1}-\frac{1}{2}\gamma_{\text{E}}-\ln\left(\frac{\rho\,r_{0}^{q-1}}{\ell_{0}^{q}}\right)\biggr]+\mathcal{O}\left(\varepsilon\right)\,.

(32)

Note the important point that the exclusion of the
q=1
is not an artificial requirement to ensure that the formula is well-defined. Indeed, when
q=1
the difference
\mathcal{D}Q_{\ell}^{p,1}
is exactly vanishing; this point will be of uttermost importance in Sec.
IV
.

2.

When
p=-\ell+2i+2
where
i\in\mathbb{N}
and
q\neq 0
, where we have

\displaystyle\hskip-8.5359pt\mathcal{D}Q_{\ell}^{p,q}=(-)^{i}\frac{(2\ell-2i-1)!!}{2^{\ell+i}i!}\frac{1}{q}\biggl[\frac{1}{\varepsilon}+\sum_{k=0}^{|\ell-i|-1}\frac{1}{2k+1}-\frac{1}{2}\gamma_{\text{E}}+\ln\left(\frac{\ell_{0}^{q}}{2r_{0}^{q}}\right)\biggr]+\mathcal{O}\left(\varepsilon\right)\,.

(33)

with the convention that
(-2n-1)!!(2n+1)!!\equiv(-)^{n}(2n+1)
for any integer
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

All other cases give zero (no difference). In case 2, since (
33
) does not depend on
\rho
the difference is local, and turns out to be vanishing. Indeed, due to the even nature of
p+\ell
, the two integrals corresponding to the two terms in the square brackets of (
30
) exactly cancel out. On the other hand, the case 1 seems to be non-local as there is a
\ln\rho
remaining and therefore a non-local integral associated. However we find that this non-local integral exactly cancels out the one present in the coefficients
{}_{\varepsilon}\widehat{f}_{p,q}
up to order
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)
,
cf.
Eq. (
12
) of the
\varepsilon
-transform, so that the final result does not contain a remaining integral over
\rho
. Note that we have not considered the
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)
term, as we have explicitly verified that none of the contributing
f_{p,q}
(
i.e.
such that
p-\ell-3\in 2\mathbb{N}
) bears poles. If it were the case, the difference would turn out to be non-local. Fortunately this is not our case and thus we are able to write down our final result as (with
\bar{q}=4\pi\mathrm{e}^{\gamma_{\text{E}}}
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}H(t)

\displaystyle=(-2)^{\ell}\sum_{i=0}^{+\infty}\frac{(i+\ell)!}{i!(2\ell+2i+1)!}\sum_{p,q\not=1}\frac{1}{q-1}\biggl[\frac{1}{\varepsilon}-\sum_{k=0}^{\ell+i}\frac{1}{2k+1}+\ln\left(\sqrt{\bar{q}}\,\frac{\ell_{0}^{q-1}}{r_{0}^{q-1}}\right)\biggr]\frac{f_{p,q}^{(2i)}(t)}{c^{2i}}\,,

(34)

plus the neglected remainder
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)
. It is understood that the sum over
i
is actually finite because the singularity in the source term (
25
) is finite (
i.e.

p\leqslant p_{0}
for some
p_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
). In (
34
) we have restored the relevant Hadamard regularization scale
r_{0}
and dimensional regularization scale
\ell_{0}
. Again we emphasize that this result is local, as long as the
f_{p,q}(t)
are themselves local. As presented in the Sec.
IV
, this is always the case, and thus the complicated difference between the two regularization schemes of non-linear interactions is local. This appears to be a non-trivial result, considering that the tails and their iterations are by essence non-local.

To summarize, we have computed the difference between regularization schemes in the metric in harmonic coordinates. From (
21
) and (
23
) we have expressed this difference, for each component of the metric and each multipolarity
\ell
, see (
6
), in the form of a retarded solution of the d’Alembertian equation in
d
dimensions,

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}h_{L}=\hat{\partial}_{L}\,\biggl[\frac{\tilde{k}}{r^{d-2}}\,\int_{1}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}y\,\gamma_{\frac{1-d}{2}}(y)\,\mathcal{D}H(t-yr)\biggr]\,,

(35)

where the function
\mathcal{D}H
is given by (
34
) for any source term of the form (
25
)

IV
The MPM algorithm for non-linear interactions

The goal of this work is to apply the formulas derived in the previous section to the case of the non-linear interactions entering the mass quadrupole moment at 4PN. A quick dimensional analysis shows that such interactions are at most cubic in
G
. Thus, we need to compute the cubic source term entering the right-hand side of the wave equation (
1
). To this end, we rely on the so-called Multipolar-Post-Minkowskian (MPM) algorithm
Blanchet and Damour 1986
;
Blanchet 1998b
.

The MPM algorithm builds the sources (and solutions) of the wave equation (
1
) in an iterative fashion, orders by orders in
G
, hence the name.
Its starting point is the so-called canonical metric, which is a generalization in
d
dimensions of Thorne’s linearized metric
Thorne 1980
. The recent calculation in
d
dimensions
Henry et al. 2021
writes it as

\displaystyle h^{00}_{\text{can\,1}}=-\frac{4}{c^{2}}\sum_{\ell\geqslant 0}\frac{(-)^{\ell}}{\ell!}\,\hat{\partial}_{L}\,\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{L}\,,

(36a)

\displaystyle h^{0i}_{\text{can\,1}}=\frac{4}{c^{3}}\sum_{\ell\geqslant 1}\frac{(-)^{\ell}}{\ell!}\,\left[\hat{\partial}_{L-1}\,\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{iL-1}^{(1)}+\frac{\ell}{\ell+1}\hat{\partial}_{L}\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{i|L}\right]\,,

(36b)

\displaystyle h^{ij}_{\text{can\,1}}=-\frac{4}{c^{4}}\sum_{\ell\geqslant 2}\frac{(-)^{\ell}}{\ell!}\,\left[\hat{\partial}_{L-2}\,\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{ijL-2}^{(2)}+\frac{2\ell}{\ell+1}\hat{\partial}_{L-1}\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}^{(1)}_{(i|j)L-1}+\frac{\ell-1}{\ell+1}\hat{\partial}_{L}\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{ij|L}\right]\,,

(36c)

where we have defined the “tilded” versions of the moments as,
e.g.
,

\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{L}(r,t)\equiv\frac{\tilde{k}}{r^{d-2}}\int_{1}^{+\infty}\!\!\mathrm{d}y\,\gamma_{\frac{1-d}{2}}(y)\,M_{L}\left(t-\frac{yr}{c}\right)\,,

(37)

and where
M_{L}
and
S_{i|L}
denote the mass and current canonical moments, together with the additional moment
K_{ij|L}
, which does not exist in 3 dimensions.

We let the reader refer to
Blanchet 2014
for a comprehensive review of the MPM formalism.
7
7

7

Note that the procedure is formally similar in three-dimensions (as presented in
Blanchet 2014
) and in
d
-dimensions (as should be implemented here); however the three-dimensional moments have to be replaced by their
d
-dimensional tilded counterparts,
e.g.

M_{L}(t-r/c)/r\longrightarrow\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{L}(r,t)
as defined in (
37
).
We will only note that, due to its iterative nature, we need the knowledge of the quadratic solution to the wave equation in order to build up the cubic source. However, let us recall that the difference between regularization schemes is a near zone effect [as only the
{r^{\prime}}\to 0
limit of the integral (
20
) plays a role], and so we can focus on the near zone behaviour of the source only. This notably implies that we do not require the knowledge of the whole quadratic interactions to build up the cubic source, but only their near zone behaviour, which we can iterate following the MPM procedure.

We discuss the near zone expansion of a quadratic metric, taking first the example of the quadratic interaction
M\times M_{ij}
. Later we will say a word on the more difficult interaction
M_{ij}\times M_{ij}
, but the generalization to other interactions is almost straightforward. The source of a
d
-dimensional quadratic interaction like
M\times M_{ij}
(for which the monopole
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}=\tilde{k}Mr^{2-d}
is static) takes the generic form

N(\mathbf{x},t)=\hat{n}_{L}\,\frac{\ell_{0}^{q\varepsilon}}{r^{p+q\varepsilon}}\int_{1}^{+\infty}\!\!\mathrm{d}z\,\gamma_{\frac{1-d}{2}}(z)\,z^{k}\,F\left(t-\frac{zr}{c}\right)\,,

(38)

and
F
involves some time derivative of
M_{ij}
. The exact prescription to be applied would naturally be the propagator (
2
), as we discussed in details in Sec.
II
. Nevertheless, we do not need to use it for the quadratic interactions, as we only seek for the near zone behaviour. Following
Bernard et al. 2017
, the formal PN expansion of the quadratic solution is made of two terms,

\displaystyle\overline{h}_{L}=\text{PF}\,\Box_{\text{R}}^{-1}\overline{N}-\mathop{\mathrm{PF}}_{B=0}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{(-)^{m}}{m!}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\Delta^{-j}\hat{x}_{M}\int\!\mathrm{d}^{d}\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\left(\frac{r^{\prime}}{r_{0}}\right)^{B}\hat{\partial}_{M}^{\prime}\left[\frac{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}^{(2j)}(\mathbf{y},t-zr^{\prime}/c)}{c^{2j}\,r^{\prime}{}^{d-2}}\right]_{\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{x}^{\prime}}\,,

(39)

where the “tilde” operation (with respect to variables
r^{\prime},t
) is defined by (
37
) and we denoted

\Delta^{-j}\hat{x}_{L}\equiv\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{d}{2}+\ell\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{d}{2}+\ell+j\right)}\frac{r^{2j}\hat{x}_{L}}{2^{2j}j!}\,.

(40)

The first term is a particular solution built up (in the sense of the PF) from the formal PN expansion
\overline{N}
of the source term (
38
). Introducing
\tau=zr/c
and using the asymptotic expansion when
z\to+\infty
of the function
\gamma_{\frac{1-d}{2}}
given by (
3
),
i.e.

\gamma_{\frac{1-d}{2}}(z)\underset{z\to+\infty}{\sim}\ \sum_{j=0}^{+\infty}\frac{(-)^{j}}{j!}\frac{2\sqrt{\pi}\,z^{-2-2j-\varepsilon}}{\Gamma\left(-j-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{1+\varepsilon}{2}\right)}\,,

(41)

we can decompose
\overline{N}
into “even” and “odd” parts given by

\displaystyle\overline{N}^{\text{even}}=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}A_{j}^{k}\,\frac{r^{2j+\kappa-p-q\varepsilon}}{c^{2j+\kappa}}\,F^{(2j+\kappa)}(t)\,\hat{n}_{L}\,,

(42a)

\displaystyle\overline{N}^{\text{odd}}=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}B_{j}^{k}\,\frac{r^{1+2j-k-p+(1-q)\varepsilon}}{c^{1+2j-k+\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\mathrm{d}\tau\,\tau^{-\varepsilon}\,F^{(2j+2-k)}(t-\tau)\,\hat{n}_{L}\,,

(42b)

where
\kappa\equiv k-2[\frac{k}{2}]
,
i.e.

\kappa=0
if
k
is even and
1
if
k
is odd, and the coefficients that enter those expressions are

\displaystyle A_{j}^{k}\equiv\frac{(-)^{j}}{2^{2j+\kappa}j!}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}-j-\kappa\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1+\varepsilon}{2}\right)}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1-\kappa-k+\varepsilon}{2}-j\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1-\kappa-k}{2}-j\right)}\,,

(43a)

\displaystyle B_{j}^{k}\equiv\frac{(-)^{k}}{2^{2j}j!}\frac{2\sqrt{\pi}\,\Gamma\left(2-k+\varepsilon\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1+\varepsilon}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(1-\varepsilon\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{-\varepsilon}{2}\right)}\frac{\Gamma\left(1+j+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(1+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)}\frac{\Gamma\left(1-\frac{k}{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(1+j-\frac{k}{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{3-k+\varepsilon}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(j+\frac{3-k+\varepsilon}{2}\right)}\,.

(43b)

The expressions (
42
)–(
43
) generalize those of
Bernard et al. 2017
in the case of a non-vanishing
k
.

Once integrated with the PN-expanded propagator, using the “Matthieu” formula,
i.e.
Eq. (3.16) in
Larrouturou et al. 2021
, we find that Eqs. (
42
) yield poles. Fortunately, the three-dimensional limit is non-pathological, since those poles then disappear by mutual cancellation. They simply generate logarithmic terms as emphasized by (
26
). Moreover, such poles do not contribute to the difference between regularization schemes in the iterated cubic solution, as already advertized (they do not enter the terms bearing
p-\ell-3\in 2\mathbb{N}
).

As for the homogeneous solution, which is the second term of (
39
), after a series of transformations following
Bernard et al. 2017
, it becomes

\displaystyle\overline{h}_{L}^{\text{hom}}=\frac{(-)^{p+\ell}}{d+2\ell-2}\mathop{\mathrm{PF}}_{B=0}\,\frac{\Gamma\left(q\varepsilon-B\right)}{\Gamma\left(p+\ell-1+q\varepsilon-B\right)}\,C^{k,p,q}_{\ell}\,\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\Delta^{-j}\hat{x}_{L}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\mathrm{d}\tau\frac{\tau^{B-q\varepsilon}}{r_{0}^{B}}\frac{F^{(2j+\ell+p-1)}(t-\tau)}{c^{2j+\ell+p+q\varepsilon-B}}\,,

(44)

where we have denoted

C^{k,p,q}_{\ell}\equiv\int_{1}^{+\infty}\!\!\mathrm{d}y\,\gamma_{\frac{1-d}{2}-\ell}(y)\int_{1}^{+\infty}\!\!\mathrm{d}z\,\gamma_{\frac{1-d}{2}}(z)\,z^{k}(y+z)^{\ell-2+p+q\varepsilon-B}\,.

(45)

The coefficients
C^{k,p,q}_{\ell}
are generalizations for
q\in\mathbb{Z}
of the ones introduced in
Bernard et al. 2017
. They can be computed by methods similar to those described in the App. D of
Bernard et al. 2017
.

The three types of terms we have met: integrated “even” and “odd” PN expansions, as well as the homogeneous solutions, are to be inserted into the cubic source which, again, can be PN-expanded and will take the same general form as in (
42
), which confirms our claim on the structure of the cubic source (
25
).

An important point to note is that only the “even” contributions (
42a
) will contribute to the final result (
34
). Indeed we have explicitly checked that, due to the particular powers of
r
, the “odd” terms given by (
42b
) will bear
p-\ell-3=2i+1
,
i\in\mathbb{N}
, thus not contributing to the final difference. Concerning the homogeneous solution, it is apparent from Eqs. (
44
) and (
40
) that it bears
q=0
. As the linear metric bears
q=1
, the homogeneous solution, once inserted into the cubic source, will produce only terms with
q=1
, thus evading the condition for a term to contribute to the difference. This crucial remark that only “even” terms contribute makes the cubic source become local in the end, once expanded in the near zone. Therefore, our final result (
34
) will itself be local, as claimed at the end of Sec.
III
.

The only quadratic interaction that does not fall within the previous investigation is the memory type one
M_{ij}\times M_{ij}
, which is needed to compute the cubic source of the 4PN tail-of-memory (see Sec.
VI.1
). This interaction involves two time-dependent moments, so that its source reads

N(\mathbf{x},t)=\hat{n}_{L}\,\frac{\ell_{0}^{q\varepsilon}}{r^{p+q\varepsilon}}\int_{1}^{+\infty}\!\!\mathrm{d}y\,\gamma_{\frac{1-d}{2}}(y)\,y^{s}\int_{1}^{+\infty}\!\!\mathrm{d}z\,\gamma_{\frac{1-d}{2}}(z)\,z^{k}\,F\left(t-\frac{yr}{c}\right)\,G\left(t-\frac{zr}{c}\right)\,.

(46)

In this case, the PN expansion of the solution involves five types of terms: the squares “even-even” and “odd-odd”, the cross products “even-odd” and “odd-even”, and the homogeneous solution, which keeps the same structure as Eq. (
44
). Nevertheless, by arguments similar as for the tail interaction, only the “even-even” terms contribute to the final result. Note that, although the “odd-odd” terms could contribute as they contain terms with
p-\ell-3\in 2\mathbb{N}
, it turns out that they have
q=0
. Therefore, the same argument as the one used to discard the contribution of the homogeneous solution applies. From (
42a
), we see again that the result for the difference at cubic order will be purely local.

Finally, applying the formula (
35
) on each terms of the (PN-expanded) cubic source, we obtain the difference between regularizations in the form of a piece
\mathcal{D}h^{\mu\nu}
in the metric to the cubic order,

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}h^{\mu\nu}=\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}\hat{\partial}_{L}\,\biggl[\frac{\tilde{k}}{r^{d-2}}\,\int_{1}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}y\,\gamma_{\frac{1-d}{2}}(y)\,\mathcal{D}H^{\mu\nu}\left(t-\frac{yr}{c}\right)\biggr]\,.

(47)

We are almost done but, while this piece clearly obeys
\Box\mathcal{D}h^{\mu\nu}=0
, it does not
a priori
satisfy the harmonic coordinate condition by itself,
\partial_{\nu}\mathcal{D}h^{\mu\nu}\not=0
. Next, we compute from it another object,
\mathcal{D}h^{\mu\nu}_{\text{gen}}
, which happens to be a
general
solution of the linearized Einstein field equations in vacuum (and
d
dimensions),
i.e.
which satisfies at once
\Box\mathcal{D}h^{\mu\nu}_{\text{gen}}=0
and
\partial_{\nu}\mathcal{D}h^{\mu\nu}_{\text{gen}}=0
. In doing so, we are exactly following the MPM procedure
Blanchet and Damour 1986
;
Blanchet 1998b
,
which can be summarized as

\displaystyle u^{\mu\nu}\equiv\mathcal{D}h^{\mu\nu}~\longrightarrow~w^{\mu}=\partial_{\nu}u^{\mu\nu}~\longrightarrow~v^{\mu\nu}=\mathcal{H}(w^{\mu})~\longrightarrow~\mathcal{D}h^{\mu\nu}_{\text{gen}}\equiv u^{\mu\nu}+v^{\mu\nu}\,,

(48)

v^{\mu\nu}
being here a homogeneous solution such that
\partial_{\nu}v^{\mu\nu}=-w^{\mu}
, built from

V
Regularization of non-linear interactions at 3PN order

When computing the radiative mass quadrupole moment at 3PN order
Blanchet et al. 2005
, a Hadamard regularization scheme for the IR sector has been used for the source moment and the non-linear interactions have been computed in ordinary 3 dimensions. However, the crucial need for a dimensional regularization scheme in the 4PN equations of motion
Bernard et al. 2017
implies that one should trade the Hadamard regularization for dimensional regularization when computing the mass quadrupole at the 4PN order. This has been achieved in the companion paper
Larrouturou et al. 2021
. It was found that, when using a dimensional regularization scheme, the source quadrupole moment receives corrections including poles
\propto\varepsilon^{-1}
already starting at the 3PN order.

To describe the poles, it is convenient to “dress” them into the particular combination

\Pi_{\varepsilon}\equiv-\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}+\ln\left(\frac{r_{0}\sqrt{\bar{q}}}{\ell_{0}}\right)\,,\quad\text{with}\quad\bar{q}\equiv 4\pi\mathrm{e}^{\gamma_{E}}\,.

(53)

Here
r_{0}
is the scale associated to the Partie Finie and
\ell_{0}
the one associated to the dimensional regularization. Now, the detailed calculations in
Larrouturou et al. 2021
conclude that, at the 3PN order, the dimensional regularization corrections can be recast as

\mathcal{D}I_{ij}^{\text{3PN}}=\frac{214}{105}\,\frac{G^{2}M^{2}}{c^{6}}\left(\Pi_{\varepsilon}+\frac{246\,299}{44\,940}\right)\,I_{ij}^{(2)}-\frac{428}{105}\,\frac{G^{2}M}{c^{6}}\left(\Pi_{\varepsilon}+\frac{252\,599}{44\,940}\right)\,P_{\langle i}P_{j\rangle}\,,

(54)

where
I_{ij}
is the source quadrupole moment and
P_{i}
is the conserved linear momentum,
i.e.

P_{i}=I^{(1)}_{i}
, with
I_{i}
being the linearly varying mass dipole. Note that this formula is valid in an arbitrary frame, not necessarily the center-of-mass (CoM) frame for which
P_{i}=0
. Obviously,
I_{ij}
and
P_{i}
are merely Newtonian with the 3PN accuracy of (
54
) but will contain crucial 1PN corrections when this equation is applied to 4PN order.

We recognize
\beta_{I}=-\frac{214}{105}
in the coefficient of the first term in (
54
). This coefficient is well known from “tail-of-tail” calculations
Blanchet 1998a
. It can be interpreted as the beta function coefficient associated with the logarithmic renormalization of the mass quadrupole moment
Goldberger and Ross 2010
.

The 3PN correction to the source quadrupole (
54
) bears a pole, which looks dreadful. Fortunately, the source quadrupole is not a physical quantity, contrary to the radiative quadrupole defined at infinity. We thus have to ensure that the correction (
54
) is exactly compensated by the regularization of the 3PN non-linear interactions in the radiative moment. Otherwise, it would show that the formalism used previously at 3PN order is inconsistent with dimensional regularization.

A trivial dimensional analysis shows that only two interactions can arise at 3PN order: (i) the tail-of-tail
M\times M\times M_{ij}
, where
M
is the constant ADM mass and
M_{ij}
the canonical moment (which agrees with the source moment to 2PN order), and (ii) the interaction
M\times M_{i}\times M_{i}
where
M_{i}
is the mass dipole moment, varying linearly with time and vanishing in the CoM frame. As we have proved in Sec.
III
[see (
34
)], the regularization of the non-linear interactions linking the canonical and radiative quadrupoles yields purely local corrections. Moreover, since in general relativity the propagation of GW is described entirely by the two types of multipole moments
M_{L}
and
S_{i|L}
(called the canonical moments in our terminology) we have to express the non-linear terms as corrections in the canonical moments, as discussed in Sec.
IV
[see (
50
)].

Implementing the non-linear iteration of the requested multipole interactions, as described in Sec.
IV
, we computed the 3PN correction to the canonical quadrupole:

\mathcal{D}M_{ij}^{\text{3PN}}=-\frac{214}{105}\,\frac{G^{2}M^{2}}{c^{6}}\left(\Pi_{\varepsilon}+\frac{246\,299}{44\,940}\right)\,M_{ij}^{(2)}+\frac{428}{105}\,\frac{G^{2}M}{c^{6}}\left(\Pi_{\varepsilon}+\frac{252\,599}{44\,940}\right)\,M^{(1)}_{\langle i}M^{(1)}_{j\rangle}\,.

(55)

As the canonical and source moments are equivalent at Newtonian order, we see that the contribution of the non-linear interactions exactly cancels the correction of the source quadrupole given by Eq. (
54
). Again, this result is valid in a general frame, not necessarily in the CoM frame, and it is true not only for the pole part, but also for the finite part following the pole.

We conclude that the corrections due to dimensional IR regularization exactly vanish in the radiative quadrupole to 3PN order; so, using Hadamard or dimensional regularization for the IR is equivalent at that order.
A priori
, the same cancellation is not expected to happen at 4PN order, because we found for the conservative equations of motion that the dimensional and Hadamard regularization schemes are no more equivalent
Bernard et al. 2017
. Correcting the quadrupole moment to account for dimensional regularization at 4PN order is the main motivation for this work.

Two other radiative moments are very important as they have to be computed to 3PN order in order to control the 4PN flux: these are the mass octupole moment, obtained in
Faye et al. 2015
, and the current quadrupole moment, computed in
Henry et al. 2021
. We have to verify the equivalence of both regularization schemes for these 3PN moments too. Following the methods described in
Larrouturou et al. 2021
, we have computed the correction due to the change in regularization schemes for both the source mass octupole

and the (dual of the) current quadrupole

VI
The renormalized mass quadrupole moment at 4PN order

VI.1
Regularization of non-linear interactions at 4PN order

Having proved that Hadamard and dimensional (IR) regularizations are equivalent at the 3PN order, we now turn to the main goal of this work, namely the IR regularization of the radiative mass quadrupole up to 4PN.

In addition to the 3PN interaction (
55
) which has now to be considered up to 4PN, five additional interactions can show up: the “tail-of-memory”
M\times M_{ij}\times M_{ij}
, the interaction
M\times M_{ij}\times S_{i|j}
involving the constant angular momentum
S_{i|j}
, and three interactions
M\times M_{i}\times M_{ijk}
,
M_{i}\times M_{i}\times M_{ij}
and
M\times M_{i}\times S_{i|jk}
involving the mass dipole
M_{i}
. As discussed earlier, the difference between the regularizations of the non-linear interactions are local and can be expressed as corrections to the canonical moments
M_{L}
and
S_{i|L}
.

For the sake of simplicity, we will work in the CoM frame, where
M_{i}
vanishes. Indeed, the main application of the present work for PN templates can be performed in such frame. Only three interactions survive, yielding the following corrections in the canonical moment:

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}M_{ij}^{\text{CoM, 4PN}}=\,

\displaystyle-\frac{214}{105}\,\frac{G^{2}M^{2}}{c^{6}}\left(\Pi_{\varepsilon}+\frac{246\,299}{44\,940}\right)\,M_{ij}^{(2)}

(58)

\displaystyle+\frac{G^{2}M}{c^{8}}\Biggl[\frac{12}{7}\left(\Pi_{\varepsilon}+\frac{3581}{7560}\right)\,M_{a\langle i}^{(2)}\,M_{j\rangle a}^{(2)}-\frac{24}{7}\left(\Pi_{\varepsilon}-\frac{338}{2835}\right)\,M_{a\langle i}^{(1)}\,M_{j\rangle a}^{(3)}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\frac{4}{7}\left(\Pi_{\varepsilon}-\frac{1447}{216}\right)\,M_{a\langle i}\,M_{j\rangle a}^{(4)}+\frac{4}{3}\left(\Pi_{\varepsilon}+\frac{11\,243}{7560}\right)M_{a\langle i}^{(3)}S_{j\rangle|a}\Biggl]\,,

where we recall that the pole has been “dressed-up” according to (
53
).

As we have discussed in Sec.
V
the tail-of-tail contribution given by the first line of (
58
) exactly cancels the correction due to dimensional regularization in the source moment at the 3PN order. Note that this keeps being true up to the 4PN order because the canonical and source moments are equivalent at the 1PN order. Now, we discover that the coefficients of the remaining poles entering the non-linear interactions in the purely 4PN terms of (
58
) exactly cancel those coming from the IR regularization of the source mass quadrupole, given by Eq. (4.4) of the companion paper
Larrouturou et al. 2021
.

Therefore, we arrive at the main result of this paper, namely that the radiative mass quadrupole moment is free of poles up to the 4PN level. This important feature (but expected for a physical quantity) naturally leads us to define a “
renormalized mass quadrupole
” as the sum of the properly regularized source mass quadrupole, which was computed in
Marchand et al. 2020
;
Larrouturou et al. 2021
, and augmented by the contributions from the dimensional regularization of the cubic non-linear interactions, as displayed in Eqs. (
58
),
including
the finite corrections beyond the poles explicitly found in (
58
). The interest of such quantity is that it is finite when
\varepsilon\to 0
, so that it can be considered and manipulated in ordinary three dimensions. This notably allows us to express it in the CoM frame and, then, for quasi-circular orbits using the usual three-dimensional reduction procedure.

Recalling the work conducted in
Larrouturou et al. 2021
, the renormalized quadrupole is thus defined as

I^{\text{renorm}}_{ij}\equiv I_{ij}+\mathcal{D}M_{ij}=I_{ij}^{\text{Had}}+I_{ij}^{\text{non-loc}}+\mathcal{D}I_{ij}+\delta_{\chi}I_{ij}+\mathcal{D}M_{ij}\,,

(59)

where
I_{ij}^{\text{Had}}
denotes the result of
Marchand et al. 2020
,
i.e.
the local source quadrupole computed with a UV dimensional regularization and an IR Hadamard one;
I_{ij}^{\text{non-loc}}
is the non-local effect of the
source
quadrupole, coming from the 4PN tail effect in the conservative sector (equations of motion and Fokker Lagrangian), given in Eq. (2.14) of
Larrouturou et al. 2021
;
\mathcal{D}I_{ij}
is the correction due to dimensional regularization in the source quadrupole (
\mathcal{D}I_{ij}
is itself the sum of four contributions in Eq. (3.1) of
Larrouturou et al. 2021
);
\delta_{\chi}I_{ij}
is the effect of the local part of the IR shift applied in the conservative sector
Larrouturou et al. 2021
;
Bernard et al. 2017
; and, finally,
\mathcal{D}M_{ij}
is the correction due to dimensional regularization of the non-linear interactions, computed in the present work.

The non-local tail term in the source quadrupole found in
Larrouturou et al. 2021
can be restated as

\displaystyle I_{ij}^{\text{non-loc}}=I_{ij}^{\text{inst}}+\hat{I}_{ij}^{\text{non-loc}}\,.

(60)

The first term corresponds to instantaneous (non-tail) contributions given by

\displaystyle\hat{I}_{ij}^{\text{inst}}=\frac{24}{7}\frac{G^{2}M}{c^{8}}\left(\Pi_{\varepsilon}+\frac{74}{105}\right)\,I_{a\langle i}\,I_{j\rangle a}^{(4)}\,,

(61)

and which combine with (
58
) and the other terms in (
59
) to cancel the pole. The second term is, properly speaking, the purely non-local tail contribution which reads as

\displaystyle\hat{I}_{ij}^{\text{non-loc}}=\frac{24}{7}\frac{G^{2}M}{c^{8}}\,I_{k\langle i}(t)\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\,\ln\left(\frac{c\tau}{2r_{0}}\right)\,I^{(5)}_{j\rangle k}(t-\tau)\,.

(62)

Such contribution will naturally combine with the non-localities induced by the non-linear interactions in the radiative quadrupole. The non-local tail term contains both conservative and dissipative effects,

\hat{I}_{ij}^{\text{non-loc}}=\hat{I}_{ij}^{\text{non-loc}}\bigg|_{\text{cons}}+\hat{I}_{ij}^{\text{non-loc}}\bigg|_{\text{diss}}\,,

(63)

where the conservative sector is given by the time-symmetrized integral

\displaystyle\hat{I}_{ij}^{\text{non-loc}}\bigg|_{\text{cons}}=\frac{12}{7}\frac{G^{2}M}{c^{8}}\,I_{k\langle i}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\,\ln\left(\frac{c\tau}{2r_{0}}\right)\left[I^{(5)}_{j\rangle k}(t-\tau)-I^{(5)}_{j\rangle k}(t+\tau)\right]\,,

(64)

or can also be expressed in the form of the Hadamard partie finie (Pf) integral

\displaystyle\hat{I}_{ij}^{\text{non-loc}}\bigg|_{\text{cons}}=\frac{12}{7}\frac{G^{2}M}{c^{8}}\,I_{k\langle i}\,\text{Pf}_{2r_{0}/c}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}}{|t-t^{\prime}|}\,I^{(4)}_{j\rangle k}(t^{\prime})\,,

(65)

with associated time scale
2r_{0}/c

The dissipative effect is naturally encoded in the complementary time-antisymmetrized integral

\displaystyle\hat{I}_{ij}^{\text{non-loc}}\bigg|_{\text{diss}}=\frac{12}{7}\frac{G^{2}M}{c^{8}}\,I_{k\langle i}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\,\ln\left(\frac{c\tau}{2r_{0}}\right)\left[I^{(5)}_{j\rangle k}(t-\tau)+I^{(5)}_{j\rangle k}(t+\tau)\right]\,.

(66)

Note that the notion of renormalized mass quadrupole applies to the source type moment, hence we appropriately called it
I^{\text{renorm}}_{ij}
and not
M^{\text{renorm}}_{ij}
. In order to obtain the physical radiative moment, we still have to add to the renormalized source moment the corrections coming from (i) the relation between the canonical quadrupole on the one hand, and the source and gauge moments on the other hand (see
Blanchet 1998b
for discussion), and (ii) the non-linear tails, tails-of-tails, tails-of-memory,
etc.
multipole interactions. However, the interest of having defined such renormalized source quadrupole moment
I^{\text{renorm}}_{ij}
is that the latter corrections (i) and (ii) can be computed in ordinary three dimensions using the standard MPM algorithm, as all dimensional regularization contributions have already been included. Many of the corrections (i)-(ii) are already known (see notably
Blanchet et al. 2008
;
Faye et al. 2012

M\times M_{ij}\times M_{ij}
will still have to be computed in 3 dimensions, which is left for future work.

\displaystyle\quad+\gamma^{2}\biggl(-\frac{357761}{19800}+\frac{428}{105}\ln\biggl(\frac{r}{r_{0}{}}\biggr)-\frac{92339}{5544}\nu+\frac{35759}{924}\nu^{2}+\frac{457}{5544}\nu^{3}\biggr)

\displaystyle\quad+\gamma^{3}\biggl(\frac{23006898527}{1589187600}-\frac{4922}{2205}\ln\biggl(\frac{r}{r_{0}{}}\biggr)+\biggl[\frac{8431514969}{529729200}+\frac{143}{192}\pi^{2}-\frac{32}{7}\ln\left(16\gamma\mathrm{e}^{2\gamma_{\text{E}}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad-\frac{1266}{49}\ln\biggl(\frac{r}{r_{0}{}}\biggr)-\frac{968}{63}\ln\biggl(\frac{r}{r^{\prime}_{0}}\biggr)\biggr]\nu

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\biggl[\frac{351838141}{5045040}-\frac{41}{24}\pi^{2}+\frac{968}{21}\ln\biggl(\frac{r}{r^{\prime}_{0}}\biggr)\biggr]\nu^{2}-\frac{1774615}{81081}\nu^{3}-\frac{3053}{432432}\nu^{4}\biggl)\,,

(69b)

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle=\frac{48}{7}+\gamma\left(-\frac{4096}{315}-\frac{24512}{945}\nu\right)-\frac{32}{7}\pi\,\gamma^{3/2}\,.

(69c)

The term
C
corresponds to time-odd contributions. The 2.5PN and 3.5PN terms were computed in
Faye et al. 2012
, whereas the new 4PN term
-\frac{32}{7}\pi\,\gamma^{3/2}
comes from the dissipative contribution of the non-local term (
66
). As for the conservative contribution of the tail term (
65
), it yields the logarithmic dependences
\ln(16\gamma\mathrm{e}^{2\gamma_{\text{E}}})
in the time-even coefficients
A
and
B
.

Finally, the two constants
r_{0}
and
r_{0}^{\prime}
entering the coefficients (
69
) are associated with the Partie Finie and the dimensional UV regularization schemes, respectively (see
Marchand et al. 2020
). As unphysical scales, they are expected to be exactly compensated in the radiative moment, by virtue of non-linear effects and application of the time derivatives. This will be checked in future work.

Appendix A
Check with the
d=3
limit of the retarded integral

In three dimensions, the function
\gamma_{\frac{1-d}{2}}(z)
defined by (
3
) takes the distributional form
\gamma_{-1}(z)=\delta(z-1)
, where
\delta(z-1)
means the Dirac function at point
z=1
. More generally, we have for any
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
(see App. C of
Bernard et al. 2017
)

\displaystyle\gamma_{-1-\ell}(z)=\frac{1}{(2\ell-1)!!}\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}\beta_{i}^{\ell}\,\delta^{(i)}(z-1)\,,

(70)

where
\delta^{(i)}
denotes the
i
-th derivative of the Dirac function. The numerical coefficients
\beta_{i}^{\ell}\equiv 2^{i-\ell}\frac{(2\ell-i)!}{i!(\ell-i)!}
are such that the multipolar retarded or advanced homogeneous wave in 3 dimensions reads

\displaystyle\hat{\partial}_{L}\!\left(\frac{F(t\mp r)}{r}\right)

\displaystyle=(-)^{\ell}\frac{\hat{n}_{L}}{r^{\ell+1}}\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}\beta_{i}^{\ell}\,(\pm r)^{i}\,F^{(i)}(t\mp r)\,,

(71)

We compute the 3-dimensional limit of our final
d
-dimensional result (
18
). In this limit the two integrations over variables
y
and
z
can be effected explicitly thanks to Eq. (
70
), while the integration over
\tau
follows from the limit (
14
) which just amounts to replacing the source term
\widehat{N}_{\varepsilon}
by
N
. Thus, we obtain in 3 dimensions

\displaystyle h_{L}=\frac{(-)^{\ell}}{2}\,\hat{n}_{L}\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\beta_{i}^{\ell}\,\beta_{j}^{\ell}\,r^{-1-\ell+j}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\Biggl\{\int_{0}^{+\infty}\!\mathrm{d}{r^{\prime}}\,{r^{\prime}}^{B-\ell+1+i}N^{(-2\ell-1+i+j)}({r^{\prime}},t-r-{r^{\prime}})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-(-)^{j}\int_{r}^{+\infty}\!\mathrm{d}{r^{\prime}}\,{r^{\prime}}^{B-\ell+1+i}N^{(-2\ell-1+i+j)}({r^{\prime}},t+r-{r^{\prime}})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-(-)^{i}\int_{0}^{r}\!\mathrm{d}{r^{\prime}}\,{r^{\prime}}^{B-\ell+1+i}N^{(-2\ell-1+i+j)}({r^{\prime}},t-r+{r^{\prime}})\Biggr\}\,.

(72)

After inserting the well-known formula for the multiple time anti-derivative (valid for the source term becoming zero when
{t^{\prime}}\to-\infty
)

\displaystyle N^{(-2\ell-1+i+j)}({r^{\prime}},t^{\prime})=\int_{-\infty}^{t^{\prime}-r^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}s\,\frac{(t^{\prime}-r^{\prime}-s)^{2\ell-i-j}}{(2\ell-i-j)!}N({r^{\prime}},s+{r^{\prime}})\,,

(73)

and inverting the summations over
{r^{\prime}}
and
s
, we can re-express the result (
A
) into the form

\displaystyle h_{L}=\int_{-\infty}^{t-r}\mathrm{d}s\,\Biggl\{\int_{0}^{\frac{t-r-s}{2}}\!\mathrm{d}{r^{\prime}}\,{r^{\prime}}^{B-\ell+1}N({r^{\prime}},s+{r^{\prime}})\,\mathcal{A}_{L}

(74)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad-\int_{r}^{\frac{t+r-s}{2}}\!\mathrm{d}{r^{\prime}}\,{r^{\prime}}^{B-\ell+1}N({r^{\prime}},s+{r^{\prime}})\,\mathcal{B}_{L}-\int_{0}^{r}\!\mathrm{d}{r^{\prime}}\,{r^{\prime}}^{B-\ell+1}N({r^{\prime}},s+{r^{\prime}})\,\mathcal{C}_{L}\Biggr\}\,.

Here, we have posed as intermediate notation

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{L}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-)^{\ell}}{2}\frac{\hat{n}_{L}}{r^{1+\ell}}\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\beta_{i}^{\ell}\,\beta_{j}^{\ell}\,r^{j}{r^{\prime}}^{i}\frac{(t-r-s-2{r^{\prime}})^{2\ell-i-j}}{(2\ell-i-j)!}\,,

(75a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{L}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-)^{\ell}}{2}\frac{\hat{n}_{L}}{r^{1+\ell}}\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\beta_{i}^{\ell}\,\beta_{j}^{\ell}\,(-r)^{j}{r^{\prime}}^{i}\frac{(t+r-s-2{r^{\prime}})^{2\ell-i-j}}{(2\ell-i-j)!}\,,

(75b)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{L}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-)^{\ell}}{2}\frac{\hat{n}_{L}}{r^{1+\ell}}\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\beta_{i}^{\ell}\,\beta_{j}^{\ell}\,r^{j}(-{r^{\prime}})^{i}\frac{(t-r-s)^{2\ell-i-j}}{(2\ell-i-j)!}\,.

(75c)

All the point is now to rewrite these quantities into a more convenient form. For this we dispose of the following formula, valid for any
\rho
and
{r^{\prime}}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}\beta_{i}^{\ell}\,\frac{{r^{\prime}}^{i}(2\rho-2{r^{\prime}})^{2\ell-i-j}}{(2\ell-i-j)!}=\frac{2^{\ell-j}}{\ell!}\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\rho}\right)^{j}\Bigl[\rho^{\ell}(\rho-{r^{\prime}})^{\ell}\Bigr]\,,

(76)

together with the fact that the coefficients
\beta_{i}^{\ell}
also enter the formula (
71
), which can alternatively be further written as

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\right)^{\ell}\left[\frac{F(t\mp r)}{r}\right]=\frac{(-)^{\ell}}{r^{2\ell+1}}\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}\beta_{i}^{\ell}\,(\pm r)^{i}\,F^{(i)}(t\mp r)\,.

(77)

These two facts permit proving that the three terms defined in (
75
) are actually all identical:

\mathcal{A}_{L}=\mathcal{B}_{L}=\mathcal{C}_{L}\quad\text{(for any $t$, $s$, $r$, ${r^{\prime}}$)}\,,

(78)

so that we may glue together the three integrals in (
74
) and arrive at

\displaystyle h_{L}=\int_{-\infty}^{t-r}\mathrm{d}s\,\Biggl\{\int_{0}^{\frac{t-r-s}{2}}\!\mathrm{d}{r^{\prime}}\,{r^{\prime}}^{B-\ell+1}N({r^{\prime}},s+{r^{\prime}})\,\mathcal{A}_{L}-\int_{0}^{\frac{t+r-s}{2}}\!\mathrm{d}{r^{\prime}}\,{r^{\prime}}^{B-\ell+1}N({r^{\prime}},s+{r^{\prime}})\,\mathcal{B}_{L}\Biggr\}\,,

(79)

together with the following summed-up expressions

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{L}

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{\ell-1}}{\ell!}\hat{\partial}_{L}\!\left[\frac{1}{r}\left(\frac{t-r-s}{2}\right)^{\ell}\left(\frac{t-r-s}{2}-{r^{\prime}}\right)^{\ell}\right]\,,

(80a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{L}

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{\ell-1}}{\ell!}\hat{\partial}_{L}\!\left[\frac{1}{r}\left(\frac{t+r-s}{2}\right)^{\ell}\left(\frac{t+r-s}{2}-{r^{\prime}}\right)^{\ell}\right]\,.

(80b)

The fact that
\mathcal{A}_{L}
and
\mathcal{B}_{L}
are actually identical follows immediately from Eq. (A36) in
Blanchet and Damour 1986
. At last, we can commute the multi-spatial derivative operator
\hat{\partial}_{L}
in
\mathcal{A}_{L}
and
\mathcal{B}_{L}
with the integration over
{r^{\prime}}
in (
79
). Indeed, the contributions coming from the differentiation of the bound
(t\mp r-s)/2
are seen to be zero from the structure of
\mathcal{A}_{L}
and
\mathcal{B}_{L}
respectively. So, finally, posing

\displaystyle R(\rho,s)=\rho^{\ell}\int_{0}^{\rho}\mathrm{d}{r^{\prime}}\frac{(\rho-{r^{\prime}})^{\ell}}{\ell!}\left(\frac{2}{{r^{\prime}}}\right)^{\ell-1}\!{r^{\prime}}^{B}N({r^{\prime}},s+{r^{\prime}})\,,

(81)

we get our final result in 3 dimensions,

\displaystyle h_{L}=\int_{-\infty}^{t-r}\mathrm{d}s\,\hat{\partial}_{L}\left[\frac{R\bigl(\frac{t-r-s}{2},s\bigr)-R\bigl(\frac{t+r-s}{2},s\bigr)}{r}\right]\,.

(82)

This is exactly the result given by Eq. (6.4) in
Blanchet and Damour 1986
.

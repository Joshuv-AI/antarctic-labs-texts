On the analyticity of the Dirichlet-Neumann operator and Stokes waves
Massimiliano Berti, Alberto Maspero, Paolo Ventura

Abstract




To the memory of Antonio Ambrosetti

Abstract:
We prove an analyticity result for the Dirichlet-Neumann operator
under space periodic boundary conditions in any dimension in an unbounded domain
with infinite depth.
We derive an analytic bifurcation result of analytic
Stokes waves –i.e. space periodic traveling solutions– of
the water waves equations in deep water.

MSC 2020:
76B15, 35B32, 35J05.

1
Introduction and main results

The Dirichlet-Neumann operator plays an important role in fluid mechanics, for example in the Craig-Sulem-Zhakarov
[
19
,
42
]

formulation of the water waves equations (cfr. Section
1.2
),
and in several other branches of analysis, as in
the theory of inverse problems. Roughly speaking it is defined as the linear
operator which maps
the Dirichlet datum of a harmonic function in a
given domain into the normal derivative at its boundary
(Neumann datum).
The Dirichlet-Neumann operator is nonlinear with respect to the boundary of the domain.
In view of many applications it is important
to determine
its regularity in different function spaces.

Several results about the analyticity of the Dirichlet-Neumann operator
acting between Sobolev spaces, with respect to the variation of the boundary,
have been proved, starting with the pioneering works of Coifmann-Meyer
[
16
]
, Craig-Schanz-Sulem

[
18
]
, Craig-Nicholls
[
17
]
and Lannes
[
26
]
where we
refer for an extended bibliography.
We also mention the deep analysis of the Dirichlet-Neumann operator in
[
5
,
4
,
3
,
40
]
, on which we will comment later.

The major
aim of this paper is to prove a further analyticity result for the Dirichlet-Neumann operator

G(\eta)
defined in (
1.3
)
on the unbounded domain
\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\{y\leq\eta(x)\}
, where

\mathbb{T}^{d}:=(\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z})^{d}
is the standard
d
-dimensional flat
torus, in any space dimension
d\geq 1
.
Assuming that
\eta(x)
is analytic, we prove in
Theorem
1.2
the analyticity of the
map
\eta\mapsto G(\eta)
acting between suitable spaces of analytic
periodic functions. The delicate point of this result
is that
\eta
and
\psi
are assumed to
have the
same
regularity (if

\eta
is more regular than
\psi
the result is simpler).
Following Lannes
[
25
,
26
]
and Alazard-Burq-Zuily
[
1
]

we make use of a regularizing diffeomorphism
to flatten the domain to the half cylinder, in which the transformed
harmonic function solves a perturbed elliptic equation. Then
the proof relies on a perturbative approach to invert the transformed Laplacian
over suitable spaces of functions
u(x,y)
which are analytic in
x
, with Sobolev
regularity in
y
and
decay to zero as
y\to-\infty
, cfr. (
2.7
).
The key step is obtain linear elliptic regularity estimates for the
Poisson equation in these spaces, see Lemma
2.10
.
Then the elliptic estimates for the modified problem are obtained
by a perturbative argument differently from
[
1
]
.

As a consequence of Theorem
1.2
,
we derive an analytic bifurcation result of analytic Stokes waves –i.e. space periodic traveling solutions, which look stationary in a moving frame with constant speed– of the pure gravity water waves equations in infinite depth, see Theorem
1.3
.
Existence of traveling waves which are constant in one space dimension, i.e.
are
1
-dimensional waves,
dates back to classical works of
Levi-Civita
[
27
]
, Nekrasov
[
31
]
and Struik
[
37
]
,
in the twenties of the last century. Then Lewy
[
28
]
proved
that a traveling wave which is at least
C^{1}

is actually analytic. Theorem
1.3

proves in addition that small amplitude Stokes waves depend
analytically on the amplitude taking values in a space of analytic functions.
In finite depth and with surface tension, a result of this kind
is proved in Nicholls-Reitich
[
33
]
by a power series expansion approach.

In this paper we deduce Theorem
1.3
by the analytic
Crandall-Rabinowitz
bifurcation theorem from a simple eigenvalue, as presented in
the book of Ambrosetti-Prodi
[
6
]
, thanks to the analytic estimates
of the Dirichlet-Neumann operator obtained in Theorem

1.2
.

In addition to their interests per se –traveling waves have
fundamental importance in fluid mechanics–,
these results have been used in the study of the
Benjiamin-Feir instability of the Stokes waves in
[
13
]
.

We now state precisely our results. Along this paper we use the following notation.
We denote the spatial variables by

(x,y)\in\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}
,
d\geq 1
, where
\mathbb{T}^{d}:=(\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z})^{d}

is the standard flat torus.
The symbol
\nabla
denotes the gradient

\nabla:=(\partial_{x_{j}})_{j=1,\ldots,d}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Delta:=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\partial_{x_{j}}^{2}\,,\quad\Delta_{x,y}:=\Delta+\partial_{y}^{2}\,.

A dot will denote the standard scalar product in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Moreover

\mathbb{N}:=\{1,2,\ldots\}
and
\mathbb{N}_{0}=\{0\}\cup\mathbb{N}
.

1.1
Dirichlet-Neumann operator

We consider the cylindrical domain

{\mathcal{D}}_{\eta}:=\big\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}\ :\ y<\eta(x)\big\}\,,\quad d\geq 1\,,

(1.1)

delimited by the graph
\partial{\mathcal{D}}_{\eta}=\{y=\eta(x)\}
of a periodic function
\eta(x)
, and, given
a periodic Dirichlet datum

\psi(x)
, we consider the unique harmonic function
\Phi(x,y)

solving the system

\begin{cases}\Delta_{x,y}\Phi=0&\mbox{ in }{\mathcal{D}}_{\eta}\\
\Phi(x,y)=\psi(x)&\mbox{ at }y=\eta(x)\\
\partial_{y}\Phi(x,y)\to 0&\mbox{ as }y\to-\infty\,.\end{cases}

(1.2)

The Dirichlet-Neumann operator
G(\eta)
is then defined as the linear operator

\displaystyle(x)

\displaystyle:=\sqrt{1+|\nabla\eta|^{2}}\,\partial_{n}\Phi_{|y=\eta(x)}

(1.3)

\displaystyle=(\partial_{y}\Phi)(x,\eta(x))-\nabla\eta(x)\cdot(\nabla\Phi)(x,\eta(x))

where
n
denotes the exterior normal

n:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla\eta|^{2}}}\left[\footnotesize{\begin{matrix}-\nabla\eta\\
1\end{matrix}}\right]\,,\quad\partial_{n}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla\eta|^{2}}}(\partial_{y}-\nabla\eta\cdot\nabla)\,.

The reason of the name
“Dirichlet-Neumann” is that the operator
G(\eta)
maps the Dirichlet datum
\psi(x)
of
the harmonic function
\Phi(x,y)
into the (normalized) normal derivative

\partial_{n}\Phi

at the
boundary
\partial{\mathcal{D}}_{\eta}=\{y=\eta(x)\}
(Neumann datum).

Remark 1.1
.

In (
1.2
) it is equivalent to require the boundary condition

\nabla\Phi(x,y)\to 0

as
y\to-\infty
, see Remark
2.8
. Actually

\nabla\Phi(x,y)
decays to zero exponentially fast as
y\to-\infty
.

Simple algebraic properties of the Dirichlet-Neumann operator are recalled in Appendix
A
.

Since Calderon it is known that
the Dirichlet-Neumann operator
G(\eta)
is, if
\eta
is a
C^{\infty}
function,
a classical pseudo-differential operator, elliptic of order
1
, with an asymptotic expansion
in classical decreasing symbols.
For the flat surface
\eta(x)=0
, the Dirichlet-Neumann operator is the Fourier multiplier

G(0)=|D|=(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}

as follows by the elementary calculus (
2.25
).
In space dimension
d=1

the Dirichlet-Neumann operator is equal to
|D|
up to infinitely many
times regularizing operators, see e.g.
[
14
,
8
]
.
If
\eta(x)
has a finite smoothness, Lannes
[
25
,
26
]

proved an analogous expansion in symbols with finite smothness.

The Dirichlet-Neumann operator is a nonlinear map with respect to the boundary
of the domain

\partial{\mathcal{D}}_{\eta}
.
The analytic dependence with respect to
\eta

of the Dirichlet-Neumann operator
\eta\mapsto G(\eta)

has been first established in the two dimensional setting by Coifman-Meyer

[
16
]
, and in the three dimensional setting by
Craig, Schanz and Sulem
[
18
]
, showing
that, if
\eta\in C^{k+1}
,
\psi\in H^{k+1}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, then

G(\eta)[\psi]\in H^{k}
is analytic in
C^{k+1}\cap\{\|\eta\|_{C^{1}}<r\}
for
r
sufficiently small.

In view of of application to water waves
Craig-Nicholls
[
17
]
, Wu

[
40
,
41
]
, and Lannes
[
25
,
26
]
proved,
with different approaches, that if

\eta,\psi
have the same Sobolev regularity
H^{s}

then
G(\eta)[\psi]\in H^{s-1}
.
In particular Lannes proved tame estimates using
regularizing
diffeomorphisms
to straighten the domain.

The paralinearization of
G(\eta)\psi
, which enables to prove optimal
estimates for the action of the Dirichlet-Neumann operator, has been obtained in
Alazard-Metivier
[
5
]
,
Alazard-Delort
[
4
]
,
and Alazard-Burq-Zuily
[
2
,
3
]

in rough domains, using a variational analysis to construct the solution
and applying elliptic regularity theory.
The paralinearization of the Dirichlet-Neumann operator in
d=1

with a multilinear expansion in
\eta
is proved in Berti-Delort
[
10
]
, by using a
paradifferential parametrix à la Boutet de Monvel.

Finally we mention the work of Alazard-Burq-Zuily
[
1
]

for the study of the Dirichlet-Neumann operator acting in analytic function spaces,
making use of a regularizing diffeomorphism as in
[
25
]
, variational methods and
elliptic regularity analysis.

In this paper we prove an analyticity result (Theorem
1.2
)
for the Dirichlet-Neumann map
\eta\mapsto G(\eta)\psi
in
the cylindrical domain
{\mathcal{D}}_{\eta}
defined in (
1.1
),
acting between spaces of periodic analytic functions defined in (
1.4
) below.
We suppose that the functions
\eta
and
\psi

belong to the spaces of periodic
functions

H^{\sigma,s}:=H^{\sigma,s}(\mathbb{T}^{d}):=\Big\{u(x)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}u_{k}e^{\mathrm{i}\,k\cdot x}\,:\ \ \|u\|_{H^{\sigma,s}}^{2}:=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}e^{2\sigma|k|_{1}}\langle k\rangle^{2s}\,|u_{k}|^{2}<\infty\Big\}

(1.4)

where, for any
k=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, we set

|k|_{1}:=|k_{1}|+\dots+|k_{d}|\,,\quad\langle k\rangle:=\max(1,|k|)\,,\quad|k|:=\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{d}k_{j}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\,.

Clearly, if the dimension
d=1
then
|k|=|k|_{1}
.

If
\sigma=0
the space
H^{0,s}
is the usual Sobolev space
H^{s}
.
If
\sigma>0
, a periodic function
u(x)
belongs to

H^{\sigma,s}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
, if and only if
it admits an analytic extension in the strip
|y|_{\infty}:=\max\{|y_{1}|,\ldots,|y_{d}|\}<\sigma
and
the traces at the boundaries
u(\cdot+\mathrm{i}\,y)
,
|y|_{\infty}=\sigma
,
belong to the Sobolev space
H^{s}:=H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
.
In Appendix

B.1
we prove this characterization, together with the property that
the spaces
H^{\sigma,s}

form, for
s>d/2
, an algebra with respect to the product of functions
and satisfy tame estimates.

The main result of this section is the following theorem.

Let
B^{\sigma,s}(r)
denote the open ball in

H^{\sigma,s}
of center
0
and radius
r>0
.

Theorem 1.2
.

(Dirichlet-Neumann operator)

Let
\sigma\geq 0
and
s
,
s_{0}

such that
s+\frac{1}{2},\,s_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
,
and
s-\frac{3}{2}\geq s_{0}>\frac{d+1}{2}
. Then there exists
\epsilon_{0}:=\epsilon_{0}(s)>0
such that
the Dirichlet-Neumann operator map
1
1

1

H^{\sigma,s}\cap B^{\sigma,s_{0}}(\epsilon_{0})
is an open set in the
H^{\sigma,s}
topology.

\eta\mapsto G(\eta)\,,\quad H^{\sigma,s}\cap B^{\sigma,s_{0}+\frac{3}{2}}(\epsilon_{0})\to\mathcal{L}(H^{\sigma,s},H^{\sigma,s-1})\,,

is analytic and fulfills the tame estimate

\|G(\eta)\psi\|_{H^{\sigma,s-1}}\leq C(s)\big(\|\psi\|_{H^{\sigma,s}}+\|\eta\|_{H^{\sigma,s}}\|\psi\|_{H^{\sigma,s_{0}+\frac{3}{2}}}\big)\,.

(1.5)

We remark that, in Theorem

The proof of such result, given in Section

1.2
Stokes waves

As an application of Theorem
1.2
we prove that

1
-dimensional Stokes waves solutions of the pure gravity
water waves equations in deep water
are analytic functions belonging to the spaces
H^{\sigma,s}
,
and moreover depend analytically with respect to
the amplitude parameter.
Clearly
1
-dimensional traveling waves are also
2
d-traveling waves
which are constant in one space direction, so it extends to higher dimensional Stokes waves.
We first present the
water waves equations.

The pure gravity water waves equations.

We consider the Euler equations for a bi-dimensional incompressible,
inviscid, irrotational fluid under the action of gravity,
filling the
region
{\mathcal{D}}_{\eta}
defined in (
1.1
) with
d=1
,

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\Phi+\frac{1}{2}\big((\partial_{x}\Phi)^{2}+(\partial_{y}\Phi)^{2}\big)+g\eta=0\ \ \qquad\text{ at}\ y=\eta(x)\cr\partial_{t}\eta=\partial_{y}\Phi-(\partial_{x}\eta)\,(\partial_{x}\Phi)\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\ \,\text{at}\ y=\eta(x)\cr\Delta_{x,y}\Phi=0\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\ \qquad\qquad\ \quad\text{in}\ {\mathcal{D}}_{\eta}\cr\partial_{y}\Phi\to 0\qquad\qquad\ \ \,\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\ \text{ as}\ y\to-\infty\,,\end{cases}

(1.6)

where
g>0
is the acceleration of gravity.
The irrotational velocity field is the gradient
of the harmonic scalar potential
\Phi=\Phi(t,x,y)
,
determined by its trace
\psi(t,x)=\Phi(t,x,\eta(t,x))
at the free surface

y=\eta(t,x)
.
Actually
\Phi(t,\cdot)
is
the unique solution of the elliptic equation (
1.2
).
The time evolution of the fluid is determined by the first
two boundary conditions in (
1.6
) at the free surface.
The first states that the pressure of the fluid
is equal, at the free surface, to the constant atmospheric pressure (dynamic boundary condition) and the second one
that the fluid particles remain, along the evolution, on the free surface (kinematic
boundary condition).

As shown by Zakharov
[
42
]
and Craig-Sulem
[
19
]
,
the evolutionary system (
1.6
) amounts to
the following equations for the unknowns
(\eta(t,x),\psi(t,x))
,

\eta_{t}=G(\eta)\psi\,,\quad\psi_{t}=-g\eta-\dfrac{\psi_{x}^{2}}{2}+\dfrac{1}{2(1+\eta_{x}^{2})}\big(G(\eta)\psi+\eta_{x}\psi_{x}\big)^{2}\,,

(1.7)

where
G(\eta)
is
the Dirichlet-Neumann operator in (
1.3
).
In addition the equations (
1.7
) are the Hamiltonian system

\partial_{t}\eta=\nabla_{\psi}\mathcal{H}\,,\quad\partial_{t}\psi=-\nabla_{\eta}\mathcal{H}\,,

(1.8)

where
\nabla_{\eta},\nabla_{\psi}

denote the
L^{2}
-gradients of the Hamiltonian

\mathcal{H}(\eta,\psi):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left(\psi\,G(\eta)\psi+g\eta^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}x\,,

which is the sum of the kinetic energy (cfr. (
A.1
))
and potential gravitational energy of the fluid.
Actually, as proved in
[
19
,
42
]
, the
L^{2}
-gradient with respect to

\eta
of the kinetic energy

K(\eta,\psi):=\frac{1}{2}(\psi,G(\eta)\psi)_{L^{2}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{DNSP}}}{{=}}\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathcal{D}}_{\eta}}|\nabla\Phi|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,,

(1.9)

is equal to

\nabla_{\eta}K(\eta,\psi)=-\frac{1}{2}\psi_{x}^{2}+\dfrac{1}{2(1+\eta_{x}^{2})}\big(G(\eta)\psi+\eta_{x}\psi_{x}\big)^{2}\,,

(1.10)

yielding the equivalence between (
1.8
) and (
1.7
).

We also remark that the water waves equations (
1.7
)
are invariant under space translations namely, by
(
A.2
),

{\mathcal{H}}\circ\tau_{\theta}={\mathcal{H}}\,,\quad\forall\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,.

In addition, the water waves equations
are reversible with respect to the involution

\rho\left[\footnotesize{\begin{matrix}\eta(x)\\
\psi(x)\end{matrix}}\right]:=\left[\footnotesize{\begin{matrix}\eta(-x)\\
-\psi(-x)\end{matrix}}\right],\quad\text{i.e. }\mathcal{H}\circ\rho=\mathcal{H}\,,

as a consequence of (
A.3
).

The Stokes waves.

Noteworthy solutions of (
1.7
) are the so-called Stokes waves, namely traveling
solutions of the form

\eta(t,x)=\breve{\eta}(x-ct)\,,\quad\psi(t,x)=\breve{\psi}(x-ct)\,,

(1.11)

for some real
c
(the speed) and
2\pi
-periodic functions
(\breve{\eta}(x),\breve{\psi}(x))
(the profiles).
In a reference frame in translational motion with constant speed
c
,
the water waves equations (
1.7
) then become,
by using the translation invariance property
(
A.2
),

\eta_{t}=c\eta_{x}+G(\eta)\psi\,,\quad\psi_{t}=c\psi_{x}-g\eta-\dfrac{\psi_{x}^{2}}{2}+\dfrac{1}{2(1+\eta_{x}^{2})}\big(G(\eta)\psi+\eta_{x}\psi_{x}\big)^{2}\,.

(1.12)

The Stokes waves profiles
(\breve{\eta},\breve{\psi})
in (
1.11
)
are then equilibrium steady solutions
of (
1.12
), namely solve the system

c\eta_{x}+G(\eta)\psi=0\,,\quad c\psi_{x}-g\eta-\dfrac{\psi_{x}^{2}}{2}+\dfrac{1}{2(1+\eta_{x}^{2})}\big(G(\eta)\psi+\eta_{x}\psi_{x}\big)^{2}=0\,.

(1.13)

The next theorem is the main bifurcation result of small amplitude Stokes waves
proved in this paper.
We denote by
B(r):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}\colon\ |x|<r\}
the real ball with center 0 and radius
r
.

Theorem 1.3
.

(Stokes waves)
For any
\sigma\geq 0
,

s>5/2
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
\epsilon_{0}:=\epsilon_{0}(\sigma,s,k)>0

and a unique family of solutions

(\eta_{\epsilon}(x),\psi_{\epsilon}(x),c_{\epsilon})\in H^{\sigma,s}(\mathbb{T})\times H^{\sigma,s}(\mathbb{T})\times\mathbb{R}

of the system (
1.13
),
parameterized by

|\epsilon|\leq\epsilon_{0}
,
such that

1.

the map

\epsilon\mapsto(\eta_{\epsilon},\psi_{\epsilon},c_{\epsilon})
,

B(\epsilon_{0})\to H^{\sigma,s}(\mathbb{T})\times H^{\sigma,s}(\mathbb{T})\times\mathbb{R}

is analytic;

2.

\eta_{\epsilon}(x)
is even,
\eta_{\epsilon}(x)
has zero average,

\psi_{\epsilon}(x)
is odd;

3.

the solutions
(\eta_{\epsilon}(x),\psi_{\epsilon}(x),c_{\epsilon})
have the expansion

(\eta_{\epsilon}(x),\psi_{\epsilon}(x))=\epsilon(\sqrt{k}\cos(kx),\sqrt{g}\sin(kx))+O(\epsilon^{2})\,,\quad c_{\epsilon}\to\sqrt{\frac{g}{k}}\ \ \text{as}\ \ \epsilon\to 0\,.

(1.14)

Theorem
1.3
is proved in Section
3
.
Let us make some comments on the result.

1. As already mentioned in the introduction, the first rigorous bifurcation
proof of small amplitude Stokes waves for pure
gravity water waves goes back to Levi-Civita
[
27
]
and
Nekrasov
[
31
]
in deep water, and Struik
[
37
]
in finite depth.
We refer to the monographs of Ambrosetti-Prodi
[
6
]

and Buffoni-Toland
[
15
]
for a complete presentation.
Concerning regularity, it is known since Lewy
[
28
]
that a Stokes wave which is at least
C^{1}

is actually analytic. Theorem
1.3

proves in addition that the Stokes waves
(\eta_{\epsilon}(x),\psi_{\epsilon}(x))
depend
analytically on the amplitude
\epsilon
taking values in a space of analytic functions
H^{\sigma,s}\times H^{\sigma,s}
.
In finite depth and in presence of surface tension, an analyticity result of this kind
is proved in Nicholls-Reitich
[
33
]
, by a power series expansion.
We also mention Plotnikov-Toland
[
35
]
for related results about analytic continuation of Stokes waves.

Existence of traveling water waves has been also proved by
Zeidler
[
43
]
under the effect of capillary forces and
Martin
[
30
]
, Walhén
[
39
]
also for constant vorticity flows.
We expect that, thanks to Theorem
1.2
,
an analyticity result for the Stokes waves, analogous to Theorem
1.3
,
holds also in these cases.

2. Higher order Taylor expansions of the Stokes waves in
\epsilon
are known, see e.g.

[
20
]
,
[
32
]
,
[
33
]
.
We remark that Theorem
1.3
proves the convergence of the Taylor
series of the Stokes waves in
\epsilon
, taking values in spaces of analytic periodic functions.

3.
Quasi-periodic traveling waves.

More general
1
d time quasi-periodic traveling Stokes waves
have been recently obtained in Berti-Franzoi-Maspero
[
11
,
12
]
, with or without
surface tension, and Feola-Giuliani
[
21
]
, by means
of a Nash-Moser implicit function iterative scheme.
We remark that these solutions are not steady in any moving frame. This implies
a small divisor problem.

4.
Higher space dimension: existence.

For three dimensional fluids, in addition to Stokes waves,
also traveling wave solutions which are
nontrivially periodic in both spatial directions are known,
for example forming hexagonal patterns.
Their existence was first proved in Craig-Nicholls

[
17
]
for gravity-capillary water waves, by applying
variational bifurcation arguments à la Weinstein-Moser, exploiting the Hamiltonian
nature (
1.8
) of the water waves equations.
The surface tension allows to apply, in the bifurcation analysis,
the standard implicit function theorem.
On the other hand the existence of

2
d pure gravity doubly-periodic traveling wave solutions is a small divisor problem.
In this case, solutions with Sobolev regularity were constructed by
Iooss-Plotinkov

[
22
,
23
]
by means of a Nash-Moser implicit function theorem,
requiring suitable Diophantine conditions on the speed vector.

5.
Regularity.

In higher space dimensions a regularity result à la Lewy
[
28
]
, i.e.
a traveling wave surface which is at least
C^{1}

is actually analytic,
has been proved for gravity-capillary water waves
by Craig-Matei
[
16
]
.
For
pure gravity waves, a result of this kind is false,
because the system is no more elliptic. This feature is the counterpart of the small divisor problem arising in the existence proof of Iooss-Plotinkov
[
22
,
23
]
.
Assuming Diophantine conditions on the speed vector,
Alazard-Metivier
[
5
]
proved that the periodic traveling waves
constructed in
[
22
,
23
]
, which have Sobolev regularity,
are indeed
C^{\infty}
.

6. We finally note that, for larger values of the amplitude

\epsilon
, the regularity of the traveling wave
solutions
may break down. Indeed it is well known that
large traveling waves have cusps, as proved in the celebrated works about the Stokes
conjecture of Amick, Fraenkel, Toland
[
7
]
and Plotinkov
[
34
]
.

2
Analyticity of the Dirichlet-Neumann operator

In this section we prove Theorem
1.2
concerning the analyticity of the Dirichlet-Neumann operator.
The first step is to straighten the free surface.

Regularizing diffeomorphism.

Following
[
25
,
1
]
we apply the regularizing
change of variables

x=x^{\prime}\,,\qquad y=\rho(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\,,\quad\rho(x^{\prime},y^{\prime}):=y^{\prime}+e^{y^{\prime}|D|}\eta(x^{\prime})\,,

(2.1)

where
e^{y|D|}
is the Fourier multiplier

\left(e^{y|D|}g\right)(x):=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}g_{k}\,e^{y|k|}\,e^{\mathrm{i}\,k\cdot x}\,,\quad\forall\ g(x)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}g_{k}\,e^{\mathrm{i}\,k\cdot x}\,.

It is based on the application of the analytic Crandall-Rabinowitz Theorem

Theorem 3.1

(Crandall-Rabinowitz bifurcation Theorem)
.

Let
X,Y
be Banach spaces and
U\subset X
be an open neighbourhood of 0. Let
F:U\times\mathbb{R}\to Y
,
F(u,c)
, be an analytic map satisfying

F(0,c)=0
for any
c\in\mathbb{R}
. Let
c^{*}
be such that

L:=\mathrm{d}_{u}F(0,c^{*})\in{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y)
is not invertible and

1.

\text{Ker}(L)=\textup{span}\langle u^{*}\rangle
,
u^{*}\in X
, is
1
-dimensional;

2.

the range
R:=\text{Rng}(L)
is closed and
\textup{codim }R=1
;

3.

(transversality)

\;\partial_{c}\mathrm{d}_{u}F(0,c^{*})[u^{*}]\notin R\,.

Then there exist
\epsilon_{*}>0
and an analytic function

(-\epsilon_{*},\epsilon_{*})\to U\times\mathbb{R},\ \ \epsilon\mapsto(u_{\epsilon},c_{\epsilon})\,,\ \quad\quad u_{\epsilon}=\epsilon u^{*}+O(\epsilon^{2})\,,\ \ c_{\epsilon}=c^{*}+O(\epsilon)\,,

such that

F(u_{\epsilon},c_{\epsilon})=0
for any
|\epsilon|<\epsilon_{*}
.

Theorem
1.3
is proved by applying Theorem
3.1
to
the nonlinear operator

\displaystyle F:\;\big(H^{\sigma,s}_{\mathtt{ev}_{0}}\cap B^{\sigma,s_{0}}(\epsilon_{0})\big)\times H^{\sigma,s}_{\mathtt{odd}}\times\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow H^{\sigma,s-1}_{\mathtt{odd}}\times H^{\sigma,s-1}_{\mathtt{ev}_{0}}\,,\ \sigma\geq 0\,,\ s>5/2\,,

(3.1)

\displaystyle F(\eta,\psi,c):=\Big(c\eta_{x}+G(\eta)\psi\ ,\ c\psi_{x}-g\eta-\dfrac{\psi_{x}^{2}}{2}+\dfrac{1}{2(1+\eta_{x}^{2})}\big(G(\eta)\psi+\eta_{x}\psi_{x}\big)^{2}\Big)

where

, respectively

, denote the space of even, respectively odd, and average-free real valued functions in
H^{\sigma,s}
defined in (
1.4
),
and
\epsilon_{0}:=\epsilon_{0}(\sigma,s,s_{0})>0
is provided by Theorem
1.2
. Note that a
real function

admits a Fourier series expansion

\left[\footnotesize{\begin{matrix}\eta(x)\\
\psi(x)\end{matrix}}\right]=\sum_{k\geq 1}\left[\footnotesize{\begin{matrix}\eta_{k}\cos(kx)\\
\psi_{k}\sin(kx)\end{matrix}}\right]\quad\text{with norm}\quad\|(\eta,\psi)\|_{H^{\sigma,s}}^{2}\simeq\sum_{k\geq 1}e^{2\sigma|k|}\langle k\rangle^{2s}(\eta_{k}^{2}+\psi_{k}^{2})\,.

(3.2)

The fact that the nonlinear operator

in (

) maps

which are odd/even in

into a pair of functions which are even/odd in

). Moreover, the second component of

has zero average thanks to the following lemma.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let

be the Dirichlet-Neumann operator defined in (

Proof.

In view of (

∎

We now start verifying the assumptions of the Crandall-Rabinowitz Theorem

First, by Theorem

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{(\eta,\psi)}F(0,0,c):\;H^{\sigma,s}_{\mathtt{ev}_{0}}\times H^{\sigma,s}_{\mathtt{odd}}\to

Lemma 3.3
.

(Bifurcation speeds)

The kernel of

(3.5)

For any

, the Kernel of

,
where we set

Proof.

By the Fourier expansion

In addition, a vector

Appendix A
Basic properties of the Dirichlet-Neumann operator

The linear Dirichlet-Neumann operator
G(\eta)
defined in (
1.3
)
is self-adjoint with respect to the
L^{2}
scalar product,

\big(G(\eta)\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\big)_{L^{2}}=\int_{{\mathcal{D}}_{\eta}}\nabla\Phi_{1}\cdot\nabla\Phi_{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=\big(G(\eta)\psi_{2},\psi_{1}\big)_{L^{2}}\,,

where
\Phi_{1}
and
\Phi_{2}
are the harmonic functions associated to
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}
as in (
1.2
).
Thus
G(\eta)
is semi-positive definite

\big(G(\eta)\psi,\psi\big)_{L^{2}}=\int_{{\mathcal{D}}_{\eta}}|\nabla\Phi|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\geq 0\,,

(A.1)

and its kernel contains only the constant functions,
G(\eta)[1]=0
.
In particular
(
A.1
) implies also the unicity of the solutions of (
1.2
).

We list other classical algebraic properties of the Dirichlet-Neumann used in the paper.

Lemma A.1
.

The Dirichlet-Neumann
G(\eta)
in (
1.3
) is:

(i) invariant under space translations

\tau_{\theta}G(\eta)\psi=G(\tau_{\theta}\eta)[\tau_{\theta}\psi]\,,\quad\tau_{\theta}u(x):=u(x+\theta)\,,\ \ \forall\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,;

(A.2)

(ii) invariant under the reflection at the origin, namely

G(\eta^{\vee})[\psi^{\vee}]=\left(G(\eta)[\psi]\right)^{\vee}\quad\text{where}\quad f^{\vee}(x):=f(-x)\,;

(A.3)

(iii) constant along vertical translations, i.e.

G(\eta+m)=G(\eta)\,,\quad\forall m\in\mathbb{R}\,.

(A.4)

Proof.

Let us prove (
A.2
).
Let
\Phi
be the solution of (
1.2
).
For any
\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
the harmonic function

\Phi_{\theta}(x,y):=\Phi(x+\theta,y)\quad\forall(x,y)\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\tau_{\theta}\eta}=\{y<\eta(x+\theta)\}

solves

\Delta_{x,y}\Phi_{\theta}=0\ \text{in}\ {\mathcal{D}}_{\tau_{\theta}\eta}\,,\quad\Phi_{\theta}(x,\tau_{\theta}\eta(x))=\tau_{\theta}\psi(x)\,,\quad\partial_{y}\Phi_{\theta}(x,y)\to 0\mbox{ as }y\to-\infty\,.

Therefore, by (
1.3
),

\displaystyle G(\tau_{\theta}\eta)[\tau_{\theta}\psi]

\displaystyle=(\partial_{y}\Phi_{\theta})(x,\tau_{\theta}\eta)-(\nabla\tau_{\theta}\eta)(x)\cdot(\nabla\Phi_{\theta})(x,\tau_{\theta}\eta)

\displaystyle=(\partial_{y}\Phi)(x+\theta,\eta(x+\theta))-(\nabla\eta)(x+\theta)\cdot(\nabla\Phi)(x+\theta,\eta(x+\theta))=\tau_{\theta}G(\eta)[\psi]

proving (
A.2
). To prove (
A.3
), consider the harmonic function

\Phi^{\vee}(x,y):=\Phi(-x,y)\quad\forall(x,y)\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\eta^{\vee}}=\{y<\eta^{\vee}(x)\}

which solves

\Delta_{x,y}\Phi^{\vee}=0\ \text{in}\ {\mathcal{D}}^{\vee}_{\eta}\,,\quad\Phi^{\vee}(x,\eta^{\vee}(x))=\psi^{\vee}(x)\,,\quad\partial_{y}\Phi^{\vee}(x,y)\to 0\mbox{ as }y\to-\infty\,.

Therefore (
A.3
) follows by

\displaystyle G(\eta^{\vee})[\psi^{\vee}]

\displaystyle=(\partial_{y}\Phi^{\vee})(x,\eta^{\vee}(x))-(\nabla\eta^{\vee})(x)\cdot(\nabla\Phi^{\vee})(x,\eta^{\vee}(x))

\displaystyle=(\partial_{y}\Phi)(-x,\eta(-x))-(\nabla\eta)(-x)\cdot(\nabla\Phi)(-x,\eta(-x))=G(\eta)[\psi](-x)\,.

For any
m\in\mathbb{R}
the harmonic function

\Phi_{m}(x,y):=\Phi(x,y-m)\quad\forall(x,y)\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\eta+m}=\{y<\eta(x)+m\}

solves

\Delta_{x,y}\Phi_{m}=0\ \text{in}\ {\mathcal{D}}_{\eta+m}\,,\quad\Phi_{m}(x,\eta(x)+m)=\psi(x)\,,\quad\partial_{y}\Phi_{m}(x,y)\to 0\mbox{ as }y\to-\infty\,.

Therefore, by (
1.3
),

\displaystyle G(\eta+m)[\psi]

\displaystyle=(\partial_{y}\Phi_{m})(x,\eta(x)+m)-(\nabla\eta)(x)\cdot(\nabla\Phi_{m})(x,\eta(x)+m)

\displaystyle=(\partial_{y}\Phi)(x,\eta(x))-(\nabla\eta)(x)\cdot(\nabla\Phi)(x,\eta(x))=G(\eta)[\psi]

proving (
A.4
).
∎

B.1
The spaces
H^{\sigma,s}

We first note the following characterization of the spaces
H^{\sigma,s}
.

Lemma B.1
.

(Characterization of
H^{\sigma,s}
)

The space

H^{\sigma,s}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
,
\sigma>0
, coincides with the periodic functions

u(x)

which admit an extension
u(z)
in the complex strip

\mathbb{T}^{d}_{\sigma}:=\mathbb{T}^{d}+\mathrm{i}\,[-\sigma,\sigma]^{d}=\Big\{z=x+\mathrm{i}\,y\,\colon\,\,x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}\,,\;y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,\ |y|_{\infty}:=\max\{|y_{1}|,\ldots,|y_{d}|\}\leq\sigma\Big\}\,,

which is analytic in
|y|_{\infty}<\sigma
, and
whose traces at the boundaries
u(\cdot+\mathrm{i}\,y)
,
|y|_{\infty}=\sigma
,
belong to the Sobolev space
H^{s}:=H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
, with equivalence of the norms

\|u\|_{H^{\sigma,s}}\simeq_{d}\,\sup_{|y|_{\infty}\leq\sigma}\Big\{\|u(\cdot+\mathrm{i}\,y)\|_{H^{s}}\Big\}\,.

(B.1)

Proof.

Let
u(x)
be a function in
H^{\sigma,s}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
.
For any
z\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
,
z=x+\mathrm{i}\,y
,
|y|_{\infty}\leq\sigma
,
we define its extension

u(z):=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}u_{k}\,e^{\mathrm{i}\,k\cdot z}

which is analytic for
|y|_{\infty}<\sigma
.
For any
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
|y|_{\infty}\leq\sigma
,
the Sobolev norm
\|\ \|_{H^{s}}
of the periodic function

x\mapsto u^{(y)}(x):=u(x+\mathrm{i}\,y)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}u_{k}\,e^{-k\cdot y}e^{\mathrm{i}\,k\cdot x}

(B.2)

is bounded by

\displaystyle\|u^{(y)}\|_{H^{s}}^{2}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|u_{k}|^{2}e^{-2k\cdot y}\langle k\rangle^{2s}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|u_{k}|^{2}e^{2|y|_{\infty}|k|_{1}}\langle k\rangle^{2s}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|u_{k}|^{2}e^{2\sigma|k|_{1}}\langle k\rangle^{2s}=\|u\|_{H^{\sigma,s}}^{2}\,.

Thus
u^{(y)}(\cdot)
belongs to
H^{s}
and

\|u^{(y)}\|_{H^{s}}\leq\|u\|_{H^{\sigma,s}}
.

In order to prove the equivalence (
B.1
), consider the partition of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,

\mathbb{Z}^{d}=\bigcup_{\vec{\epsilon}\in\{\pm 1\}^{d}}\mathbb{Z}^{d}_{\vec{\epsilon}}\,,\quad\mathbb{Z}^{d}_{\vec{\epsilon}}:=\left\{k=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\,:\,\begin{cases}k_{j}>0\,,\quad\text{if}\ \epsilon_{j}=-1\,,\\
k_{j}\leq 0\,,\quad\text{if}\ \epsilon_{j}=1\,,\end{cases}\forall j=1,\ldots,d\right\}\,.

For any
\vec{\epsilon}=(\epsilon_{1},\ldots,\epsilon_{d})\in\{\pm 1\}^{d}
, the function

u^{(\sigma\vec{\epsilon})}
defined as in (
B.2
) satisfies

\displaystyle\|u^{(\sigma\vec{\epsilon})}\|_{H^{s}}^{2}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|u_{k}|^{2}e^{-2\sigma k\cdot\vec{\epsilon}}\langle k\rangle^{2s}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}_{\vec{\epsilon}}}|u_{k}|^{2}\langle k\rangle^{2s}e^{2\sigma(|k_{1}|+\ldots+|k_{d}|)}

and therefore

\|u\|_{H^{\sigma,s}}^{2}=\sum_{\vec{\epsilon}\in\{\pm 1\}^{d}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}_{\vec{\epsilon}}}|u_{k}|^{2}\langle k\rangle^{2s}e^{2\sigma|k|_{1}}\leq 2^{d}\sup_{|y|_{\infty}=\sigma}\|u^{(y)}\|_{H^{s}}^{2}\,.

The equivalence (
B.1
) is proved.
∎

s>d/2

and
the following more general tame estimates hold.

Lemma B.2

(B.3)

\|f^{j}\|_{H^{\sigma,s}}\leq(2C_{s,\mathfrak{s}_{0}}\|f\|_{H^{\sigma,\mathfrak{s}_{0}}})^{j-1}\|f\|_{H^{\sigma,s}}\,.

(B.4)

Proof.

The classical proof follows adapting the proof of Lemma 4.5.1 in

9

B.2
The spaces
\mathcal{H}^{\sigma,s,a}

Proof of Lemma
2.3
.

For any
u\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{\leq 0})
, any
y_{0}\leq 0
,
we have the inequality

|u_{k}(y_{0})|^{2}\leq 2\int_{-\infty}^{0}|\partial_{y}u_{k}(y)|\,|u_{k}(y)|\,\mathrm{d}y\,.

Multiplying by
\langle k\rangle^{2s}
and using the elementary
inequality

2\langle k\rangle^{2s}ab\leq\langle k\rangle^{2s-1}a^{2}+\langle k\rangle^{2s+1}b^{2}
, for any
a,b\geq 0
,
we get that

\displaystyle\|u(\cdot,y_{0})\|_{H^{\sigma,s}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}e^{2\sigma|k|_{1}}\langle k\rangle^{2s}|u_{k}(y_{0})|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}e^{2\sigma|k|_{1}}\int_{-\infty}^{0}2\langle k\rangle^{2s}|\partial_{y}u_{k}(y)|\,|u_{k}(y)|\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq\int_{-\infty}^{0}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}e^{2\sigma|k|_{1}}\langle k\rangle^{2s-1}|\partial_{y}u_{k}(y)|^{2}\mathrm{d}y+\int_{-\infty}^{0}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}e^{2\sigma|k|_{1}}\langle k\rangle^{2s+1}|u_{k}(y)|^{2}\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{\leq 0},H^{\sigma,s+\frac{1}{2}})}^{2}+\|\partial_{y}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{\leq 0},H^{\sigma,s-\frac{1}{2}})}^{2}

which proves (
2.12
) for smooth functions with compact support and then by density for all functions. Finally, recalling the definition of the norm

in (

2.14

Proof of Lemma
2.5
.

In view of (

\displaystyle\|e^{y|D|}g-g_{0}\|_{\sigma,s+\frac{1}{2},a}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{s+\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\}}e^{2\sigma|k|_{1}}\,\langle k\rangle^{2(s+\frac{1}{2}-j)}\,|g_{k}|^{2}\int_{-\infty}^{0}|\partial_{y}^{j}e^{|k|y}|^{2}\,e^{-2ay}\mathrm{d}y

Proof of Proposition
2.4
.

We define

\mathcal{H}^{\sigma,s,a}_{\mathbb{R}}:=\left\{u\;:\;\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}\;:\;\ \ \|u\|_{\sigma,s,a,\mathbb{R}}<\infty\right\}

endowed with the norm

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\sigma,s,a,\mathbb{R}}^{2}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{j=0}^{s}\|\partial_{y}^{j}u\|_{L^{2,a}(\mathbb{R},H^{\sigma,s-j})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\,e^{2\sigma|k|_{1}}\,\langle k\rangle^{2(s-j)}\,|\partial_{y}^{j}u_{k}(y)|^{2}e^{2a|y|}\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{s}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}e^{2\sigma|k|_{1}}\,\langle k\rangle^{2(s-j)}\,\|\partial_{y}^{j}u_{k}\|_{L^{2,a}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}

(B.5)

where, given a Hilbert space
X
, we have used the notation

\|u\|_{L^{2,a}(\mathbb{R},X)}^{2}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|u(y)\|_{X}^{2}e^{2a|y|}\mathrm{d}y\,.

By adapting the method in
[
29
]
of “extension by reflection” we have the following lemma.

Lemma B.3
.

(Extension operator)
There exists a linear bounded extension operator

{\mathcal{E}}_{s}:\mathcal{H}^{\sigma,s,a}\to\mathcal{H}^{\sigma,s,a}_{\mathbb{R}}

such that
{\mathcal{E}}_{s}u=u
a.e. on
(-\infty,0)
. Thus

\|u\|_{\sigma,s,a}\leq\|{\mathcal{E}}_{s}u\|_{\sigma,s,a,\mathbb{R}}\lesssim_{s}\|u\|_{\sigma,s,a}\,.

(B.6)

Proof.

We follow
[
29
]
.
For any
u\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R},H^{\sigma,s})
we define

(\mathcal{E}_{s}u)(y):=\begin{cases}u(y),\quad y\leq 0\,,\\
\alpha_{0}^{(s)}u(-y)+\dots+\alpha_{s}^{(s)}u(-(s+1)y)\,,\quad y>0\,,\end{cases}

(B.7)

where the coefficients
\alpha_{j}^{(s)}
,
j=0,\dots,s
are to be chosen in order to have

\partial_{y}^{j}({\mathcal{E}}_{s}u)(0)=(\partial_{y}^{j}u)(0),\quad\forall j=0,\dots,s\,,

namely solve the linear system

\begin{pmatrix}1&1&\ \dots&1\\
-1&-2&\dots&-s-1\\
\vdots&\ \vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\ \vdots\\
(-1)^{s}&(-2)^{s}&\ \dots&(-s-1)^{s}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\alpha_{0}^{(s)}\\
\vdots\\
\vdots\\
\vdots\\
\alpha_{s}^{(s)}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}1\\
\vdots\\
\vdots\\
\vdots\\
1\end{pmatrix}.

The above Vandermonde matrix is invertible
and thus the coefficients
\alpha_{0}^{(s)},\dots,\alpha_{s}^{(s)}
are uniquely
well-defined. Then by (
B.7
)

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{E}}_{s}u\|_{\sigma,s,a,\mathbb{R}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|u\|^{2}_{\sigma,s,a}+C_{s}\sum_{j=0}^{s}\sum_{i=0}^{s}\int_{0}^{\infty}\|\partial_{y}^{j}u(-(i+1)y)\|_{H^{\sigma,s-j}}^{2}e^{2a|y|}\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq C_{s}^{\prime}\sum_{i,j=0}^{s}\int_{-\infty}^{0}\|\partial_{y}^{j}u(z)\|_{H^{\sigma,s-j}}^{2}e^{\frac{2a|z|}{i+1}}\mathrm{d}z\lesssim_{s}\|u\|^{2}_{\sigma,s,a}\,.

By density the operator
u\mapsto{\mathcal{E}}_{s}u
admits a bounded linear extension to

{\mathcal{E}}_{s}:\mathcal{H}^{\sigma,s,a}\to\mathcal{H}^{\sigma,s,a}_{\mathbb{R}}
and the lemma follows.
∎

In order to analyze the space
\mathcal{H}^{\sigma,s,a}_{\mathbb{R}}
we can
use the Fourier transform in the variable
y
.
Given a function
y\mapsto u(y)
in

L^{2}(\mathbb{R},X)
, where
X
is a Hilbert space, we denote its Fourier transform

\widehat{u}(\xi):=({\mathcal{F}}u)(\xi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{u}(y)e^{-\mathrm{i}\,\xi\,y}\mathrm{d}y

and, by the inverse Fourier transform formula,

u(y)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{u}(\xi)e^{\mathrm{i}\,\xi\,y}\mathrm{\dj}\xi\,\,,\quad\mathrm{\dj}:=\frac{1}{2\pi}\mathrm{d}\,.

When
X=H^{\sigma,s}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
we may also Fourier expand in
x
writing

u(y,x)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}u_{k}(y)e^{\mathrm{i}\,k\cdot x}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{u}_{k}(\xi)e^{\mathrm{i}\,(\xi y+k\cdot x)}\mathrm{\dj}\xi\,.

(B.8)

In the sequel we shall also denote
\widehat{u}_{k}(\xi)=\widehat{u}(k,\xi)
.

The following lemma characterizes the space
\mathcal{H}^{\sigma,s,a}_{\mathbb{R}}
.

Lemma B.4
.

(Characterization of
\mathcal{H}^{\sigma,s,a}_{\mathbb{R}}
)

The space

\mathcal{H}^{\sigma,s,a}_{\mathbb{R}}
,
a>0
, coincides with the functions

y\mapsto u(y)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},H^{\sigma,s})
whose Fourier transform
\xi\mapsto\widehat{u}(\xi)

admits an extension
\widehat{u}(\zeta)
in the complex strip

\{\zeta=\xi+\mathrm{i}\,\eta\,\colon\,\,\xi\in\mathbb{R}\,,\;\eta\in\mathbb{R},\,\ |\eta|\leq a\}
, analytic in
|\eta|<a
,
whose traces at the boundaries
\widehat{u}(\cdot+\varsigma\mathrm{i}\,a)
,
belong to
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},H^{\sigma,s})
and

\langle\xi\rangle^{j}\widehat{u}(\xi+\varsigma\mathrm{i}\,a)\in{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},H^{\sigma,s-j})}
, for any
j=0,\dots,s
,
\varsigma=\pm 1
,
with equivalence of the norms

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\sigma,s,a,\mathbb{R}}^{2}

\displaystyle\simeq_{s,a}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=0,\dots,s\\
\varsigma=\pm 1\end{subarray}}\big\|\langle\xi\rangle^{j}\widehat{u}(\xi+\varsigma\mathrm{i}\,a)\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},H^{\sigma,s-j})}^{2}

(B.9)

\displaystyle\simeq_{s,a}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=0,\dots,s\\
\varsigma=\pm 1\end{subarray}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\langle\xi\rangle^{2j}\langle k\rangle^{2(s-j)}e^{2\sigma|k|_{1}}|\widehat{u}_{k}(\xi+\varsigma\mathrm{i}\,a)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle\simeq_{s,a}\max_{\varsigma=\pm 1}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\langle k,\xi\rangle^{2s}e^{2\sigma|k|_{1}}|\widehat{u}_{k}(\xi+\varsigma\mathrm{i}\,a)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi

(B.10)

where
\langle k,\xi\rangle:=\sqrt{1+|k|^{2}+|\xi|^{2}}
.

Proof.

For any
|\eta|\leq a
, integrating by parts,

\displaystyle(-\mathrm{i}\,\xi)^{j}\widehat{u}(\xi\pm\mathrm{i}\,\eta)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}u(y)e^{\mp\eta y}(-\mathrm{i}\,\xi)^{j}e^{-\mathrm{i}\,\xi y}\mathrm{d}y=\int_{\mathbb{R}}u(y)e^{\mp\eta y}\partial_{y}^{j}(e^{-\mathrm{i}\,\xi y})\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=(-1)^{j}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{y}^{j}\big(u(y)e^{\mp\eta y}\big)e^{-\mathrm{i}\,\xi y}\mathrm{d}y=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big(\sum_{p=0}^{j}{j\choose p}(\mp\eta)^{j-p}e^{\mp\eta y}\partial_{y}^{p}u(y)\Big)e^{-\mathrm{i}\,\xi y}\mathrm{d}y\,.

By Plancherel theorem
we have

\|\xi^{j}\widehat{u}(\xi\pm\mathrm{i}\,\eta)\|_{L^{2}_{\xi}(\mathbb{R},H^{\sigma,s-j})}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{p=0}^{j}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|\partial_{y}^{p}u(y)\|_{H^{\sigma,s-j}}^{2}e^{2|y|a}\mathrm{d}y\lesssim\|u\|_{\sigma,s,a,\mathbb{R}}^{2}\,.

(B.11)

Conversely, for any
0\leq j\leq s
, we have

\displaystyle\|e^{a|y|}\partial_{y}^{j}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},H^{\sigma,s-j})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{2ay}\|\partial_{y}^{j}u(y)\|_{H^{\sigma,s-j}}^{2}\mathrm{d}y+\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{-2ay}\|\partial_{y}^{j}u(y)\|_{H^{\sigma,s-j}}^{2}\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq\|e^{ay}\partial_{y}^{j}u(y)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},H^{\sigma,s-j})}^{2}+\|e^{-ay}\partial_{y}^{j}u(y)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},H^{\sigma,s-j})}^{2}\,.

(B.12)

Now

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}\big(e^{\pm ay}\partial_{y}^{j}u(y)\big)(\xi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-\mathrm{i}\,\xi y}e^{\pm ay}\partial_{y}^{j}u(y)\mathrm{d}y=(-1)^{j}\sum_{p=0}^{j}{j\choose p}(\pm a)^{j-p}(-\mathrm{i}\,\xi)^{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-\mathrm{i}\,\xi y}e^{\pm ay}u(y)\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\sum_{p=0}^{j}{j\choose p}(\mp a)^{j-p}(\mathrm{i}\,\xi)^{p}\widehat{u}(\xi\mp\mathrm{i}\,a)\,,

and thus, by Plancharel theorem,

\displaystyle\|e^{\pm ay}\partial_{y}^{j}u(y)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},H^{\sigma,s-j})}^{2}

\displaystyle\simeq\|{\mathcal{F}}\big(e^{\pm ay}\partial_{y}^{j}u(y)\big)(\xi)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},H^{\sigma,s-j})}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{a,j}\|\langle\xi\rangle^{j}\widehat{u}(\xi\mp\mathrm{i}\,a)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},H^{\sigma,s-j})}^{2}\,.

(B.13)

We deduce by (
B.12
) and (
B.2
) that, for any
j=0,\ldots,s
,

\|e^{a|y|}\partial_{y}^{j}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},H^{\sigma,s-j})}^{2}\lesssim_{a,s}\max_{\varsigma=\pm}\|\langle\xi\rangle^{j}\widehat{u}(\xi+\varsigma\mathrm{i}\,a)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},H^{\sigma,s-j})}^{2}\,.

(B.14)

The estimates (
B.11
) and (
B.14
) prove (
B.9
).

The last equivalence in (
B.10
) follows by Young’s inequality

\langle\xi\rangle^{2j}\langle k\rangle^{2(s-j)}\leq\langle\xi\rangle^{2s}+\langle k\rangle^{2s}
.

We now prove the tame estimate for the product of two functions in
\mathcal{H}^{\sigma,s,a}_{\mathbb{R}}

Lemma B.5

(
Tame).

Let
\sigma,a\geq 0
,
s,s_{0}\in\mathbb{N}

such that

s\geq s_{0}>\frac{d+1}{2}

\|uv\|_{\sigma,s,a,\mathbb{R}}\leq C_{s}\,\|u\|_{\sigma,s,0,\mathbb{R}}\|v\|_{\sigma,s_{0},a,\mathbb{R}}+C_{s}\,\|u\|_{\sigma,s_{0},0,\mathbb{R}}\|v\|_{\sigma,s,a,\mathbb{R}}\,.

(B.15)

Proof.

The product of the functions (cfr. (
B.8
))

u(x,y)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{u}(k,\xi)e^{\mathrm{i}\,(\xi y+k\cdot x)}\mathrm{\dj}\xi\,,\quad v(x,y)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{v}(k,\xi)e^{\mathrm{i}\,(\xi y+k\cdot x)}\mathrm{\dj}\xi\,,

is

uv=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{uv}(m,\eta)e^{\mathrm{i}\,\eta y}\,e^{\mathrm{i}\,mx}\mathrm{\dj}\eta

with

\widehat{uv}(m,\eta)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{u}(k,\xi)\,\widehat{v}(m-k,\eta-\xi)\mathrm{\dj}\xi\,.

(B.16)

By (
B.10
), (
B.16
)
we have that

\displaystyle\|uv\|_{\sigma,s,a,\mathbb{R}}^{2}

\displaystyle\simeq\max_{\varsigma\in\{\pm 1\}}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\widehat{uv}(m,\eta+\varsigma\mathrm{i}\,a)|^{2}e^{2\sigma|m|_{1}}\langle m,\eta\rangle^{2s}\mathrm{\dj}\eta

(B.17)

\displaystyle\leq\max_{\varsigma\in\{\pm 1\}}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\widehat{u}(k,\xi)|\,|\widehat{v}(m-k,\eta-\xi+\varsigma\mathrm{i}\,a)|e^{\sigma|m|_{1}}\langle m,\eta\rangle^{s}\mathrm{\dj}\xi\Big)^{2}\!\mathrm{\dj}\eta.

We split the frequency space into

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle:=\big\{(m,\eta,k,\xi)\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}\;:\;\langle m,\eta\rangle\leq 2\langle k,\xi\rangle\big\},

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle:=\big\{(m,\eta,k,\xi)\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}\;:\;\langle m,\eta\rangle>2\langle k,\xi\rangle\big\}

and, since
e^{\sigma|m|_{1}}\leq e^{\sigma|k|_{1}}e^{\sigma|m-k|_{1}}
,
we estimate (
B.17
) as

\|uv\|_{\sigma,s,a,\mathbb{R}}^{2}\leq I_{1}+I_{2}

(B.18)

where

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big(\!\!\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\!\int_{\mathbb{R}_{A}}\!\!\!\!\!e^{\sigma|k|_{1}}|\widehat{u}(k,\xi)|\langle k,\xi\rangle^{s}\,e^{\sigma|m-k|_{1}}|\widehat{v}(m-k,\eta-\xi+\varsigma\mathrm{i}\,a)|\langle m-k,\eta-\xi\rangle^{s_{0}}\frac{\langle m,\eta\rangle^{s}}{\langle k,\xi\rangle^{s}\langle m-k,\eta-\xi\rangle^{s_{0}}}\mathrm{\dj}\xi\Big)^{2}\!\!\!\mathrm{\dj}\eta,

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big(\!\!\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\!\int_{\mathbb{R}_{B}}\!\!\!\!\!e^{\sigma|k|_{1}}|\widehat{u}(k,\xi)|\langle k,\xi\rangle^{s_{0}}\,e^{\sigma|m-k|_{1}}|\widehat{v}(m-k,\eta-\xi+\varsigma\mathrm{i}\,a)|\langle m-k,\eta-\xi\rangle^{s}\frac{\langle m,\eta\rangle^{s}}{\langle k,\xi\rangle^{s_{0}}\langle m-k,\eta-\xi\rangle^{s}}\mathrm{\dj}\xi\Big)^{2}\!\!\!\mathrm{\dj}\eta,

where, given
m,\,k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
\eta\in\mathbb{R}
, we denoted

\mathbb{R}_{A}:=\Big\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}\;:\;(m,\eta,k,\xi)\in A\Big\},\qquad\mathbb{R}_{B}:=\Big\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}\;:\;(m,\eta,k,\xi)\in B\Big\}\,.

Using that, if
(m,\eta,k,\xi)\in A
then
\langle k,\xi\rangle>\frac{1}{2}\langle m,\eta\rangle
, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and exchanging the order of integration we get

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{s}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{2\sigma|k|_{1}}|\widehat{u}(k,\xi)|^{2}\langle k,\xi\rangle^{2s}\,e^{2\sigma|m-k|_{1}}|\widehat{v}(m-k,\eta-\xi+\varsigma\mathrm{i}\,a)|^{2}\langle m-k,\eta-\xi\rangle^{2s_{0}}\mathrm{\dj}\xi\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\Big(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{A}}\frac{1}{\langle m-k,\eta-\xi\rangle^{2s_{0}}}\mathrm{\dj}\xi\Big)\mathrm{\dj}\eta

\displaystyle\lesssim_{s}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{2\sigma|k|_{1}}|\widehat{u}(k,\xi)|^{2}\langle k,\xi\rangle^{2s}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\,e^{2\sigma|m-k|_{1}}|\widehat{v}(m-k,\eta-\xi+\varsigma\mathrm{i}\,a)|^{2}\langle m-k,\eta-\xi\rangle^{2s_{0}}\mathrm{\dj}\eta\Big)\mathrm{\dj}\xi

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{normequiv2}}}{{\lesssim_{s}}}\|u\|_{\sigma,s,0,\mathbb{R}}^{2}\|v\|_{\sigma,s_{0},a,\mathbb{R}}^{2}\,.

(B.19)

Note that since
s_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
,
s_{0}>(d+1)/2

then
s_{0}\geq\frac{d+1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}
.
If
(m,\eta,k,\xi)\in B
, i.e.
\langle k,\xi\rangle<\frac{1}{2}\langle m,\eta\rangle
, then

\langle m-k,\eta-\xi\rangle>\frac{1}{2}\langle m,\eta\rangle
, and one deduces
similarly that

I_{2}\lesssim_{s,s_{0}}\|u\|_{\sigma,s_{0},0,\mathbb{R}}^{2}\|v\|_{\sigma,s,a,\mathbb{R}}^{2}\,,

proving, in view of (
B.18
), the tame estimate (
B.15
).
∎

We now prove (
2.15
). Given

u\in\mathcal{H}^{\sigma,s,0}
and
v\in\mathcal{H}^{\sigma,s,a}
, we consider their extensions

{\mathcal{E}}_{s}u
and
{\mathcal{E}}_{s}v
obtained by Lemma
B.3
.
Since the product
{\mathcal{E}}_{s}u{\mathcal{E}}_{s}v
is an extension of
uv

we have that

\|uv\|_{\sigma,s,a}\leq\|{\mathcal{E}}_{s}u{\mathcal{E}}_{s}v\|_{\sigma,s,a,\mathbb{R}}
.
Thus the tame estimate (
B.15
) and
the equivalence of the norms
in
(
B.6
),
imply (
2.15
).

The proof of (
2.16
) follows by induction on
j
. For
j=1
it is trivial
and if it holds for
j
then

\displaystyle\|u^{j+1}\|_{\sigma,s,a}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{algebrassa}}}{{\leq}}C_{s}\big(\|u\|_{\sigma,s,a}\|u^{j}\|_{\sigma,s_{0},a}+\|u\|_{\sigma,s_{0},a}\|u^{j}\|_{\sigma,s,a}\big)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{potenze}_{j}}}{{\leq}}C_{s}\big(\|u\|_{\sigma,s,a}(2C_{s_{0}})^{j-1}\|u\|_{\sigma,s_{0},a}^{j}+\|u\|_{\sigma,s_{0},a}(2C_{s}\|u\|_{\sigma,s_{0},a})^{j-1}\|u\|_{\sigma,s,a}\big)

\displaystyle\leq(2C_{s})^{j}\|u\|_{\sigma,s_{0},a}^{j}\|u\|_{\sigma,s,a}

proving (
2.16
) at the step
j+1
.

Appendix C
Proof of the elliptic regularity Lemma
2.10

We define the weighted
L^{2}
-space of functions (cfr. (
2.10
))

L^{2,a}:=\left\{p\;\colon\;\mathbb{R}_{\leq 0}\to\mathbb{C}\ \ \ :\ \ \|p\|_{L^{2,a}}^{2}:=\int_{-\infty}^{0}\,|p(y)|^{2}e^{-2ay}\mathrm{d}y<\infty\right\}\,.

(C.1)

For any
\lambda\geq 0
, define also the integral operators

(T_{\lambda}p)(y):=\int_{-\infty}^{y}e^{\lambda(z-y)}\,p(z)\mathrm{d}z\,,\quad(\widetilde{T}_{\lambda}p)(y):=\int_{y}^{0}e^{\lambda(y-z)}\,p(z)\mathrm{d}z\,,\quad\forall y\leq 0\,.

(C.2)

The next technical lemma shows that the operators
T_{\lambda},\widetilde{T}_{\lambda}
extend to bounded operators on
L^{2,a}
.

Lemma C.1
.

Let
s\geq 0,a>0
. For any
\lambda\geq 0
one has

\displaystyle\|T_{\lambda}p\|_{L^{2,a}}\leq\frac{1}{\lambda+a}\,\|p\|_{L^{2,a}}\,,

(C.3)

\displaystyle|(T_{\lambda}p)(y)|\leq\frac{e^{ay}}{\sqrt{2(\lambda+a)}}\|p\|_{L^{2,a}}\,,\quad\forall y\leq 0\,,

(C.4)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{y}^{j}(T_{\lambda}p)\|_{L^{2,a}}\leq C_{a}\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\langle\lambda\rangle^{j-i-1}\|\partial_{y}^{i}p\|_{L^{2,a}}\,,\quad\forall j\geq 1\,,

(C.5)

where
\langle\lambda\rangle=\max\{1,|\lambda|\}
. For any
\lambda>a
, one has

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{T}_{\lambda}p\|_{L^{2,a}}\leq\frac{1}{\lambda-a}\,\|p\|_{L^{2,a}}\,,

(C.6)

\displaystyle|(\widetilde{T}_{\lambda}p)(y)|\leq\Big(\frac{e^{2ay}-e^{2\lambda y}}{2(\lambda-a)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|p\|_{L^{2,a}}\,,\quad\forall y\leq 0\,,

(C.7)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{y}^{j}(\widetilde{T}_{\lambda}p)\|_{L^{2,a}}\leq C_{a}\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\langle\lambda\rangle^{j-i-1}\|\partial_{y}^{i}p\|_{L^{2,a}}\,,\quad\forall j\geq 1\,.

(C.8)

Proof.

We consider first the operator
T_{\lambda}
.
Recalling (
C.2
) we have

\displaystyle\|T_{\lambda}p\|_{L^{2,a}}^{2}=\int_{-\infty}^{0}\left|\int_{-\infty}^{y}e^{\lambda(z-y)}p(z)\mathrm{d}z\right|^{2}e^{-2ay}\,\mathrm{d}y\leq\int_{-\infty}^{0}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{y}e^{(\lambda+a)(z-y)}|p(z)|e^{-az}\mathrm{d}z\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}y\,.

Since
\int_{-\infty}^{y}e^{(\lambda+a)(z-y)}\mathrm{d}z=\frac{1}{\lambda+a}
,
the measure

(\lambda+a)e^{(\lambda+a)(z-y)}\mathrm{d}z
is normalized on the domain
(-\infty,y)
, and Jensen inequality and exchanging the order of integration implies

\displaystyle\|T_{\lambda}p\|_{L^{2,a}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(\lambda+a)^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{0}\int_{-\infty}^{y}(\lambda+a)e^{(\lambda+a)(z-y)}\,|p(z)|^{2}e^{-2az}\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda+a}\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{-2az}\,|p(z)|^{2}e^{(\lambda+a)z}\left(\int_{z}^{0}e^{-(\lambda+a)y}\mathrm{d}y\right)\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(\lambda+a)^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{-2az}|p(z)|^{2}\left(1-e^{(\lambda+a)z}\right)\mathrm{d}z\leq\frac{1}{(\lambda+a)^{2}}\|p\|_{L^{2,a}}^{2}

as
1-e^{(\lambda+a)z}\leq 1
for any
z\leq 0
.
This proves (
C.3
). Estimate (
C.4
) descends, recalling (
C.2
),
(
C.1
), and applying Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

\displaystyle|(T_{\lambda}p)(y)|

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\lambda y}\int_{-\infty}^{y}e^{\lambda z}|p(z)|\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\lambda y}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{y}e^{2(\lambda+a)z}\mathrm{d}z\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\Big(\int_{-\infty}^{0}\,|p(z)|^{2}e^{-2az}\mathrm{d}z\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{e^{ay}}{\sqrt{2(\lambda+a)}}\|p\|_{L^{2,a}}\,.

In order to prove the estimate (
C.5
) for
\partial_{y}^{j}(T_{\lambda}p)
, we first note
the following inductive formula

\partial_{y}^{j}\,(T_{\lambda}p)=\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}(-\lambda)^{j-i-1}\,\partial_{y}^{i}p+(-\lambda)^{j}T_{\lambda}p\,,\quad\forall j\geq 1\,.

Then (
C.5
) follows estimating
\|T_{\lambda}p\|_{L^{2,a}}
by (
C.3
).

Now we consider the operator
\widetilde{T}_{\lambda}
in (
C.2
). Since
\int_{y}^{0}e^{(\lambda-a)(y-z)}\mathrm{d}z=\frac{1-e^{(\lambda-a)y}}{\lambda-a}
, then, for any
\lambda>a
,
the measure

\frac{\lambda-a}{1-e^{(\lambda-a)y}}e^{(\lambda-a)(y-z)}\mathrm{d}z
is normalized on the domain
(y,0)
, and,
by Jensen inequality and exchanging the order of integration,

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{T}_{\lambda}p\|_{L^{2,a}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{-\infty}^{0}\frac{1-e^{(\lambda-a)y}}{\lambda-a}\int_{y}^{0}|p(z)|^{2}e^{-2az}e^{(\lambda-a)(y-z)}\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda-a}\int_{-\infty}^{0}|p(z)|^{2}e^{-2az}e^{-(\lambda-a)z}\int_{-\infty}^{z}\big(e^{(\lambda-a)y}-e^{2(\lambda-a)y}\big)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(\lambda-a)^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{0}|p(z)|^{2}e^{-2az}\big(1-\frac{1}{2}e^{(\lambda-a)z}\big)\mathrm{d}y\leq\frac{1}{(\lambda-a)^{2}}\|p\|_{L^{2,a}}^{2}

as
1-\frac{1}{2}e^{(\lambda-a)z}\leq 1
for any
z\leq 0
. This proves (
C.6
). The estimate (
C.7
) follows similarly to (
C.4
). Finally (
C.8
)
descends from the identity

\partial_{y}^{j}\,(\widetilde{T}_{\lambda}p)=-\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\lambda^{j-i-1}\,\partial_{y}^{i}p+\lambda^{j}{\widetilde{T}}_{\lambda}p\,,\quad\forall j\geq 1\,,

together with the estimate for
{\widetilde{T}}_{\lambda}p
in (
C.6
).
∎

Proof of Lemma
2.10
.

Writing
u(x,y)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}u_{k}(y)e^{\mathrm{i}\,k\cdot x}
,
we expand (
2.28
) in Fourier in the
x
variables, obtaining for any
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
the second order system for
u_{k}(y)
,

\begin{cases}-|k|^{2}u_{k}(y)+\partial_{y}^{2}u_{k}(y)=g_{k}(y)\\
u_{k}(0)=0,\quad\partial_{y}u_{k}(y)\to 0\mbox{ as }y\to-\infty\,.\end{cases}

(C.9)

Case
k=0
:

The solution of (
C.9
) is, for
k=0
,

u_{0}(y)=\int_{-\infty}^{y}\int_{-\infty}^{y^{\prime}}g_{0}(z)\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}-\int_{-\infty}^{0}\int_{-\infty}^{y^{\prime}}g_{0}(z)\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{Tk}}}{{=}}\underbrace{(T_{0}^{2}g_{0})(y)}_{=\Pi u_{0}}\underbrace{-(T_{0}^{2}g_{0})(0)}_{=u_{0}-\Pi u_{0}}\,.

(C.10)

First note that, since
g\in\mathcal{H}^{\sigma,s,a}
, then, by (
2.9
),

\|\partial_{y}^{j}g_{0}\|_{L^{2,a}}\leq\|g\|_{\sigma,s,a}\,,\quad\forall j=0,\ldots,s\,.

(C.11)

By
(
C.3
), (
C.4
),
the function
\Pi u_{0}=T_{0}^{2}g_{0}
and the constant

u_{0}-\Pi u_{0}=-(T_{0}^{2}g_{0})(0)
satisfy

\displaystyle\|\Pi u_{0}\|_{L^{2,a}}\leq a^{-2}\|g_{0}\|_{L^{2,a}}\leq a^{-2}\|g\|_{\sigma,s,a}\,,\ |u_{0}-\Pi u_{0}|\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}a^{3/2}}\,\|g_{0}\|_{L^{2,a}}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}\,a^{3/2}}\|g\|_{\sigma,s,a}\ .

(C.12)

Thus
u_{0}\in\mathbb{C}\oplus L^{2,a}
.
Moreover
\partial_{y}u_{0}=T_{0}g_{0}
and (
C.4
), (
C.3
) imply that

|(\partial_{y}u_{0})(y)|\leq\frac{e^{ay}}{\sqrt{2a}}\|g_{0}\|_{L^{2,a}}\,,\quad\|\partial_{y}u_{0}\|_{L^{2,a}}\leq a^{-1}\|g_{0}\|_{L^{2,a}}\leq a^{-1}\|g\|_{\sigma,s,a}\,.

(C.13)

In addition, since
\partial_{y}^{2}u_{0}=g_{0}
,
we get
\partial_{y}^{j}u_{0}=\partial_{y}^{j-2}g_{0}
, for any
j\geq 2
, and then, by (
C.11
),

\|\partial_{y}^{j}u_{0}\|_{L^{2,a}}=\|\partial_{y}^{j-2}g_{0}\|_{L^{2,a}}\leq\|g\|_{\sigma,s,a}\,,\quad\forall 2\leq j\leq s+2\,.

(C.14)

The bounds
(
C.12
),
(
C.13
), (
C.14
), and recalling (
2.9
), imply that

|u_{0}-\Pi u_{0}|^{2}+\|\Pi u_{0}\|_{L^{2,a}}^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{s+2}\|\partial_{y}^{j}u_{0}\|_{L^{2,a}}^{2}\leq C_{a}\|g\|_{\sigma,s,a}^{2}\,.

(C.15)

Case
k\neq 0
:

The solution of the linear equation (
C.9
)
is (by the variation of constants method)

\displaystyle u_{k}(y)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2|k|}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{y}e^{|k|(z-y)}\,g_{k}(z)\,\mathrm{d}z-\frac{1}{2|k|}\int\limits_{y}^{0}e^{|k|(y-z)}g_{k}(z)\,\mathrm{d}z+\frac{e^{|k|y}}{2|k|}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{0}g_{k}(z)e^{|k|z}\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{Tk}}}{{=}}-\frac{1}{2|k|}(T_{|k|}g_{k})(y)-\frac{1}{2|k|}(\widetilde{T}_{|k|}g_{k})(y)+\frac{e^{|k|y}}{2|k|}(T_{|k|}g_{k})(0)\,.

(C.16)

By (
2.9
), each

g_{k}\in L^{2,a}
and
\|g_{k}\|_{L^{2,a}}\leq\|g\|_{\sigma,s,a}
.
Thus by
\|e^{|k|y}\|_{L^{2,a}}=1/\sqrt{2(|k|-a)}
, Lemma
C.1
,
and recalling that
a\in(0,1)
,
we bound (
C.16
) for any
|k|\geq 1
, as

\|u_{k}\|_{L^{2,a}}\leq\frac{1}{2|k|}\|T_{|k|}g_{k}\|_{L^{2,a}}+\frac{1}{2|k|}\|\widetilde{T}_{|k|}g_{k}\|_{L^{2,a}}+\frac{|(T_{|k|}g_{k})(0)|}{2|k|\sqrt{2(|k|-a)}}|\lesssim_{a}\frac{1}{|k|^{2}}\|g_{k}\|_{L^{2,a}}\,.

(C.17)

Thus each
u_{k}\in L^{2,a}
,
k\neq 0
.
Note also that

\partial_{y}u_{k}(y)=\frac{1}{2}(T_{|k|}g_{k})(y)-\frac{1}{2}(\widetilde{T}_{|k|}g_{k})(y)+\frac{e^{|k|y}}{2}(T_{|k|}g_{k})(0)

satisfies, by (
C.4
) and (
C.6
),

\displaystyle|\partial_{y}u_{k}(y)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}|(T_{|k|}g_{k})(y)|+\frac{1}{2}|(\widetilde{T}_{|k|}g_{k})(y)|+\frac{e^{|k|y}}{2}|(T_{|k|}g_{k})(0)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\frac{e^{ay}}{\sqrt{2(|k|+a)}}\|g_{k}\|_{L^{2,a}}+\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{e^{2ay}-e^{2|k|y}}{2(|k|-a)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|g_{k}\|_{L^{2,a}}+\frac{e^{|k|y}}{2}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2(|k|+a)}}\|g_{k}\|_{L^{2,a}}

thus tends to
0
as
y\to-\infty
.

By (
C.17
) and recalling (
2.9
) we deduce that

\sum_{k\neq 0}e^{2\sigma|k|_{1}}\,\langle k\rangle^{2(s+2)}\,\|u_{k}\|_{L^{2,a}}^{2}\lesssim_{a}\|g\|_{\sigma,s,a}^{2}

(C.18)

and we conclude that

u=u_{0}(y)+\sum_{k\neq 0}u_{k}(y)e^{\mathrm{i}\,k\cdot x}

is in
\mathbb{C}\oplus L^{2,a}(\mathbb{R}_{\leq 0},H^{\sigma,s+2})

with

\|\Pi u\|_{L^{2,a}(\mathbb{R}_{\leq 0},H^{\sigma,s+2})}^{2}=\|\Pi u_{0}\|_{L^{2,a}}^{2}+\sum_{k\neq 0}e^{2\sigma|k|_{1}}\,\langle k\rangle^{2(s+2)}\,\|u_{k}\|_{L^{2,a}}^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{stimau0},\eqref{parzap}}}{{\leq}}C_{a}\|g\|_{\sigma,s,a}^{2}\,.

(C.19)

Now we estimate the derivatives
\partial_{y}^{j}u
,
j\geq 1
.
Differentiating (
C.16
) we get, for any
j\geq 1
,

\partial_{y}^{j}u_{k}(y)=-\frac{1}{2|k|}\partial_{y}^{j}(T_{|k|}g_{k})(y)-\frac{1}{2|k|}\partial_{y}^{j}(\widetilde{T}_{|k|}g_{k})+\,\frac{1}{2}|k|^{j-1}\,e^{|k|y}(T_{|k|}g_{k})(0)

and, using
\|e^{|k|y}\|_{L^{2,a}}=1/\sqrt{2(|k|-a)}
, Lemma
C.1
,
a\in(0,1)
, we get, for any
|k|\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\|\partial_{y}^{j}u_{k}\|_{L^{2,a}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2|k|}\|\partial_{y}^{j}T_{|k|}g_{k}\|_{L^{2,a}}+\frac{1}{2|k|}\|\partial_{y}^{j}\widetilde{T}_{|k|}g_{k}\|_{L^{2,a}}+C_{a}|k|^{j-\frac{3}{2}}\,|(T_{|k|}g_{k})(0)|

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{Tn.est2},\eqref{TB8},\eqref{Tn.est1bis}}}{{\lesssim_{a}}}\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\langle k\rangle^{j-i-2}\,\|\partial_{y}^{i}g_{k}\|_{L^{2,a}}\,.

(C.20)

By (
C.20
) we conclude that, for any
1\leq j\leq s+1
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k\neq 0}e^{2\sigma|k|_{1}}\langle k\rangle^{2(s+2-j)}\|\partial_{y}^{j}u_{k}\|_{L^{2,a}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{a}\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\sum_{k\neq 0}e^{2\sigma|k|_{1}}\,\langle k\rangle^{2(s+2-j)}\langle k\rangle^{2(j-i-2)}\|\partial_{y}^{i}g_{k}\|_{L^{2,a}}^{2}

(C.21)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{a}\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\sum_{k\neq 0}e^{2\sigma|k|_{1}}\,\langle k\rangle^{2(s-i)}\|\partial_{y}^{i}g_{k}\|_{L^{2,a}}^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{migliore4}}}{{\leq}}C_{a}\|g\|_{\sigma,s,a}^{2}\,.

We finally estimate the last derivative
\partial_{y}^{s+2}u_{k}
.
Differentiating (
C.9
) with respect to
\partial_{y}^{s}
, we get

\partial_{y}^{s+2}u_{k}(y)=\partial_{y}^{s}g_{k}(y)+|k|^{2}\partial_{y}^{s}u_{k}(y)

and then

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}e^{2\sigma|k|_{1}}\|\partial_{y}^{s+2}u_{k}\|_{L^{2,a}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}e^{2\sigma|k|_{1}}\Big(\|\partial_{y}^{s}g_{k}\|_{L^{2,a}}^{2}+|k|^{4}\|\partial_{y}^{s}u_{k}\|_{L^{2,a}}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{migliore4},\eqref{pauj}}}{{\lesssim_{a}}}\|g\|_{\sigma,s,a}^{2}+\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}e^{2\sigma|k|_{1}}\langle k\rangle^{4}\sum_{i=0}^{s-1}\langle k\rangle^{2(s-i-2)}\,\|\partial_{y}^{i}g_{k}\|_{L^{2,a}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{a}\|g\|_{\sigma,s,a}^{2}+\sum_{i=0}^{s-1}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}e^{2\sigma|k|_{1}}\langle k\rangle^{2(s-i)}\,\|\partial_{y}^{i}g_{k}\|_{L^{2,a}}^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{migliore4}}}{{\leq}}C_{a}\|g\|_{\sigma,s,a}^{2}\,.

(C.22)

Recalling (
2.8
), summing the estimates (

),
(

) and (

),

u\in\mathbb{C}\oplus\mathcal{H}^{\sigma,s+2,a}

.

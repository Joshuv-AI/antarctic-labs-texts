Exterior algebras in matroid theory
Manoel Jarra

Abstract
. Ordered blueprints are algebraic objects that generalize monoids and ordered semirings, and 𝔽 1 ± \mathbb{F}_{1}^{\pm} -algebras are ordered blueprints that have an element ϵ \myepsilon that acts as − 1 -1 . In this work we introduce an analogue of the exterior algebra for 𝔽 1 ± \mathbb{F}_{1}^{\pm} -algebras that provides a new cryptomorphism for matroids. We also show how to recover the usual exterior algebra if the 𝔽 1 ± \mathbb{F}_{1}^{\pm} -algebra comes from a ring, and the Giansiracusa Grassmann algebra if the 𝔽 1 ± \mathbb{F}_{1}^{\pm} -algebra comes from an idempotent semifield.



Introduction

It is a classical theme that
d
-dimensional linear subspaces of the vector space
K^{n}
over
a field
K
correspond to certain elements of the exterior algebra
\Lambda K^{n\choose d}
, which are well-defined
up to scalar multiples in
K^{\times}
.

The combinatorial counterpart of such linear subspaces are matroids. Baker and Bowler streamline in
[
BB19
]
this analogy in a broad sense by the theory of matroids with coefficients in so-called tracts. Fields, semifields and more generally hyperfields, can be seen as examples of tracts.

Jeffrey and Noah Giansiracusa introduce in
[
GG18
]
an exterior algebra for idempotent semifields
S
and exhibit a ‘cryptomorphic’ description of
S
-matroids in terms of the exterior algebra, in a formal analogy to the description of
K
-matroids, or linear subspaces of
K^{n}
, in the case of a field
K
.

Somewhat puzzling, however, is that Giansiracusas’ definition of the exterior algebra for idempotent semifields makes explicit use of the idempotency in the sense that for a free module with basis
\{e_{1},\dotsc,e_{n}\}
, one has that
e_{i}\otimes e_{i}=0
, for
i=1,\dotsc,n
, are the only defining relations, in contrast to the larger set of relations for fields.

In this paper, we give a unified approach to both the classical theory over fields and Giansiracusas’ theory for idempotent semifields, which is based on Lorscheid’s theory of ordered blueprints (cf.
[
Lor18
]
). Both fields and idempotent semifields can be realized as ordered blueprints in terms of faithful functors:

(-)^{\textup{mon}}:\Rings\longrightarrow\OBlpr\qquad\text{and}\qquad(-)^{\textup{mon}}:\textup{IdempSFields}\longrightarrow\OBlpr

which have respective left inverses

(-)^{\textup{hull}}:\OBlpr\longrightarrow\Rings\qquad\text{and}\qquad(-)^{\textup{idem}}:\OBlpr\longrightarrow\textup{IdempSFields}.

We define an exterior algebra for ordered blueprints and show that it recovers both classical exterior algebra over rings as well as Giansiracusas’ exterior algebra over idempotent semifields in terms of these functors. Moreover, we give a cryptomorphic description of matroids over
{\mathbb{F}}^{\pm}_{1}
-algebras, as introduced by Baker and Lorscheid in
[
BL21
]
, by using elements of the exterior algebra, which recovers the classical viewpoint on linear subspaces of
K^{n}
and Giansiracusas’ interpretation of matroids over idempotent semifields.

Description of results

Let
B
be an
{{\mathbb{F}}_{1}^{\pm}}
-algebra and
n
be an integer. The exterior algebra
\Lambda B^{n}=\underset{i\geqslant 0}{\bigoplus}\Lambda^{i}B^{n}
of
B^{n}
is a
B
-module whose underlying semigroup is a (typically non-commutative)
B^{+}
-algebra. This exterior algebra bears properties analogous to the classical exterior algebra.

Theorem A
.

There are
B
-linear isomorphisms of ordered blue
B
-modules

B\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\ \Lambda^{0}B^{n},\qquad B^{n}\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\ \Lambda^{1}B^{n},\qquad 0\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\ \Lambda^{k}B^{n}\kern 5.0pt\text{for}\kern 5.0ptk\geqslant n+1,

and
(\Lambda B^{n})^{+}
is generated by
(\Lambda^{1}B^{n})^{+}
as a
B^{+}
-algebra.

This unifies and generalizes the classical and Giansircusas’ exterior algebra in the following sense:

Theorem B
.

(i)

Let
R
be a ring and
B=R^{\textup{mon}}
. Then
\Lambda R^{n}
is canonically isomorphic to
(\Lambda B^{n})^{\textup{hull},+}
as an
R
-algebra.

(ii)

Let
S
be an idempotent semiring and
B=S^{\textup{mon}}
. Then the Giansiracusa exterior algebra
\Lambda S^{n}
is canonically isomorphic to
(\Lambda B^{n})^{\textup{idem},+}
as an
S
-algebra.

Let
[n]=\{1,\dotsc,n\}
and
\Gamma=\binom{[n]}{d}
be the family of
d
-subsets of
[n]
. We define a
B
-matroid of rank
d
on
[n]
as the
B^{\times}
-class
[\mynu]
of an element
\mynu=(\mynu_{I})_{I\in\Gamma}
of
\Lambda^{d}B^{\Gamma}
that satisfies a certain system of relations (see
3
) and such that
\mynu_{I}\in B^{\times}
for some
I\in\Gamma
. This recovers the aforementioned concepts of matroids in the following sense:

Theorem C
.

Consider
0\leqslant d\leqslant n
.

(i)

Let
K
be a field and
B=K^{\textup{mon}}
. Then the isomorphism
\Lambda K^{\binom{[n]}{d}}\simeq(\Lambda B^{\binom{[n]}{d}})^{\textup{hull},+}
induces a bijection between
K
-matroids (as in
[
BB19
]
) and
B
-matroids.

(ii)

Let
S
be an idempotent semifield and
B=S^{\textup{mon}}
. Then the isomorphism
\Lambda S^{\binom{[n]}{d}}\simeq(\Lambda B^{\binom{[n]}{d}})^{\textup{idem},+}
induces a bijection between tropical Plücker vectors (as in
[
GG18
]
) and Grassmann-Plücker functions with coefficients in
B
in the sense of this text.

(iii)

Let
B
be an ordered blueprint. Then there is a canonical bijection between
B
-matroids in the sense of
[
BL21
]
and classes of
B
-Plücker vectors (as defined in
3
).

Remark

We draw the reader’s attention to the fact that the functors
(-)^{\textup{mon}}
,
(-)^{\textup{hull},+}
and
(-)^{\textup{idem},+}
play the same role as in Lorscheid’s approach to tropicalization as a base change from a field to the tropical hyperfield in
[
Lor22
]
. This indicates that our theory is part of a larger picture that puts classical theory and idempotent analysis on an equal footing.

1.
Algebraic background

In this section, we review some background around
\mathbb{F}_{1}^{\pm}
-algebras, following
[
Lor18
]
.

If
\mytau
is a preorder on a set
X
, viewed as a subset of
X\times X
, we will use
x\leqslant_{\mytau}y
to denote that
(x,y)
is in
\mytau
and
a\equiv_{\mytau}b
to denote that both
(a,b)
and
(b,a)
are in
\mytau
. Note that
\equiv_{\mytau}
is an equivalence relation. If the context is clear, we will denote
\leqslant_{\mytau}
simply by
\leqslant
.

1.1.
Monoids and
M
-sets

A
monoid
is a unital semigroup. A monoid
M
is called
pointed
if it has an
absorbing element
,
i.e.
, an element
0
such that
0.m=0=m.0
for all
m
in
M
. The neutral and the absorbing element (if they exist) are always unique. For the rest of this text, unless otherwise stated, every monoid is supposed to be commutative.

A
submonoid
of a monoid
M
is monoid
N
that is a subset of
M
, contains
1_{M}
and whose operation
\cdot_{N}
is the restriction of
\cdot_{M}
. If
M
is pointed and its absorbing element is in
N
, we say that
N
is a pointed submonoid of
M
.

If
M
and
W
are monoids, a map
f:M\to W
is called a
morphism of monoids
if
f(1_{M})=1_{W}
and
f(x).f(y)=f(xy)
for all
x,y
in
M
. If
M
and
W
are pointed and
f
carries the absorbing element of
M
to the absorbing element of
W
, we say that
f
is a
morphism of pointed monoids
. The category of pointed monoids will be denoted by
\Mon_{*}
.

A preorder
\mathfrak{r}
on
M
is called
multiplicative
(or
additive
, depending on the operation of
M
) if, for all elements
m
,
n
and
x
in
M
, one has
mx\leqslant_{\mathfrak{r}}nx
whenever
m\leqslant_{\mathfrak{r}}n
. A
congruence
is a multiplicative preorder that is symmetric (thus an equivalence relation). If
\mathfrak{r}
is a multiplicative preorder on
M
, the set
\mathfrak{c}_{\mathfrak{r}}:=\{(m,n)\in M\times M|\,m\equiv_{\mathfrak{r}}n\}
is a congruence. The quotient set
M/\mathfrak{c}_{\mathfrak{r}}
is a monoid, with operation
[m].[n]=[m.n]
, and has an induced multiplicative partial order
\overline{\mathfrak{r}}:=\{([a],[b])|\,a\leqslant_{\mathfrak{r}}b\}
.

If
M
is a monoid, an
M
-set
is a set
X
equipped with a map

\begin{array}[]{ccc}M\times X&\longrightarrow&X\\
(m,x)&\longmapsto&m.x\end{array}

that satisfies:

\;\quad(i)\;(m.n).x=m.(n.x)\;\quad\text{and}\;\quad(ii)\;1.x=x,

for all
m,n
in
M
and
x
in
X
. An
M
-subset
of
X
is an
M
-set
Z
such that
Z
is a subset of
X
and whose map
M\times Z\to Z
is the restriction of the map
M\times X\to X
.

If
M
is a pointed monoid with absorbing element
0
, a
pointed
M
-set
is a pointed set
(X,p)
equipped with a map
M\times X\to X
that makes
X
an
M
-set and satisfies:

\;\quad(iii)\;0.x=p\;\quad\text{and}\;\quad(iv)\;m.p=p,

for all
m
in
M
and
x
in
X
. A
pointed
M
-subset
of
(X,p)
is a pointed
M
-set
(Z,q)
such that
Z
is an
M
-subset of
X
and
p=q
.

If
\{(X_{i},p_{i})|\,i\in I\}
is a family of pointed
M
-sets, its
coproduct
is given by
\underset{i\in I}{\bigvee}X_{i}:=\bigg(\underset{i\in I}{\bigsqcup}X_{i}\bigg)\big/\sim
, where
\sim:=\{(p_{i},p_{j})|\,i,j\in I\}
, equipped with the
M
-action

\begin{array}[]{ccc}M\times\bigvee X_{i}&\longrightarrow&\bigvee X_{i}\\
(m,[x_{j}])&\longmapsto&[m.x_{j}]\end{array}

where
[y_{j}]
denotes the class, in
\underset{i\in I}{\bigvee}X_{i}
, of
y_{j}\in X_{j}
.

If
X_{1}
,
X_{2}
and
W
are
M
-sets, a map
\myvarphi:X_{1}\times X_{2}\to W
is called
M
-bilinear
if

\myvarphi(-,x_{2}):X_{1}\to W\qquad\text{and}\qquad\myvarphi(x_{1},-):X_{2}\to W

are morphims of
M
-sets for all
x_{1}
in
X_{1}
and
x_{2}
in
X_{2}
. We denote the set of
M
-bilinear maps
X_{1}\times X_{2}\to W
by
\textup{Bil}_{M}(X_{1}\times X_{2},W)
.

We construct the
tensor product
X_{1}\otimes_{M}X_{2}
of
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
as the quotient of
X_{1}\times X_{2}
by the equivalence relation generated by
\{(m.x_{1},x_{2})\sim(x_{1},m.x_{2})|\;x_{i}\in X_{i}\text{ and }m\in M\}
, and denote the class of
(x_{1},x_{2})
by
x_{1}\otimes x_{2}
.

The map

\begin{array}[]{ccc}M\times(X_{1}\otimes_{M}X_{2})&\longrightarrow&X_{1}\otimes_{M}X_{2}\\
(m,x_{1}\otimes x_{2})&\longmapsto&(m.x_{1})\otimes x_{2}=x_{1}\otimes(m.x_{2})\end{array}

turns
X_{1}\otimes_{M}X_{2}
into an
M
-set.

If
\myvarphi:X_{1}\times X_{2}\to W
is an
M
-bilinear map, then

\begin{array}[]{cccc}\overline{\myvarphi}:&X_{1}\otimes_{M}X_{2}&\longrightarrow&W\\
&x_{1}\otimes x_{2}&\longmapsto&\myvarphi(x_{1},x_{2})\end{array}

defines a morphism of
M
-sets.

The universal property of
X_{1}\otimes_{M}X_{2}
can be expressed as the fact that
\myvarphi\mapsto\overline{\myvarphi}
is a bijection from
\mbox{Bil}_{M}(X_{1}\times X_{2},W)
to
\Hom_{M}(X_{1}\otimes_{M}X_{2},W)
.

1.2.
Semirings and modules

A
semiring
is a triple
(S,+,\cdot)
, where
(S,+)
is a commutative monoid with identity
0
,
(S,\cdot)
is a (non-necessarily commutative) pointed monoid with identity
1
and absorbing element
0
, and satisfying
(a+b).c=(a.c)+(b.c)
and
c.(a+b)=(c.a)+(c.b)
for all
a,b
and
c
in
S
. We also use
ab
to denote
a.b
. A semiring is called
commutative
if
(S,\cdot)
is commutative. If the operations are clear, we use
S
to denote the semiring
(S,+,\cdot)
. For the rest of this text, unless otherwise stated, every semiring is supposed to be commutative.

If
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
are semirings, a map
f:S_{1}\to S_{2}
is called a
morphism of semirings
if it is, at the same time, a morphism of the underlying monoids
f:(S_{1},+)\to(S_{2},+)
and
f:(S_{1},\cdot)\to(S_{2},\cdot)
. The category of semirings will be denoted by
\SRings
.

A preorder
\mathfrak{r}
on a semiring
S
is called
additive and multiplicative
if
a+z\leqslant_{\mathfrak{r}}b+z
and
az\leqslant_{\mathfrak{r}}bz
whenever
(a,b)
is in
\mathfrak{r}
and
z
is in
S
.

An
ordered semiring
is a pair
(S,\leqslant)
, where
S
is a semiring and
\leqslant
is an additive and multiplicative partial order on
S
. A
morphism of ordered semirings
is a morphism of semirings that is order-preserving.

A
congruence
on a semiring
S
is an additive and multiplicative preorder that is symmetric (thus an equivalence relation). If
\mathfrak{c}
is a congruence on a semiring
S
, the quotient set
S/\mathfrak{c}
is naturally a semiring, with operations defined by
[a]+[b]:=[a+b]
and
[a].[b]:=[ab]
.

If
\mathfrak{r}
is an additive and multiplicative preorder on a semiring
S
, the set
\mathfrak{c}_{\mathfrak{r}}:=\{(a,b)\in S\times S|\,a\equiv_{\mathfrak{r}}b\}
is a congruence and the quotient semiring
S/\mathfrak{c}_{\mathfrak{r}}
has an induced additive and multiplicative partial order
\overline{\mathfrak{r}}:=\{([a],[b])|\,a\leqslant_{\mathfrak{r}}b\}
.

An
S
-module
is a pointed monoid
(Y,+)
with identity
0
equipped with a map

\begin{array}[]{cccc}\mylambda:&S\times Y&\longrightarrow&Y\\
&(s,y)&\longmapsto&s.y\end{array}

that makes
Y
an
(S,\cdot)
-set and satisfies

(s+r).y=(s.y)+(r.y)\;\quad\text{and}\;\quad s.(y+z)=(s.y)+(s.z)

for all
s,r
in
S
and
y,z
in
Y
. A map
f:Y_{1}\to Y_{2}
is called a
morphism of
S
-modules
if
f(y+z)=f(y)+f(z)
and
f(s.y)=s.f(y)
, for all
y,z
in
Y_{1}
and
s
in
S
.

If
Y_{1}
,
Y_{2}
and
Z
are
S
-modules, a map
\myvarphi:Y_{1}\times Y_{2}\to Z
is called
S
-bilinear
if

\myvarphi(-,y_{2}):Y_{1}\to Z\qquad\text{and}\qquad\myvarphi(y_{1},-):Y_{2}\to Z

are morphims of
S
-modules for all
y_{1}
in
Y_{1}
and
y_{2}
in
Y_{2}
. We denote the set of
S
-bilinear maps
Y_{1}\times Y_{2}\to Z
by
\textup{Bil}_{S}(Y_{1}\times Y_{2},Z)
.

We construct the
tensor product
Y_{1}\otimes_{S}Y_{2}
as the quotient of
S^{Y_{1}\times Y_{2}}
by the congruence generated by
\{e_{(s.y_{1},y_{2})}\equiv s.e_{(y_{1},y_{2})}\equiv e_{(y_{1},s.y_{2})}|\;s\in S\text{ and }y_{i}\in Y_{i}\}
(where
e_{(h_{1},h_{2})}
is the element of
S^{Y_{1}\times Y_{2}}
that has
1
in the entry correponding to
(h_{1},h_{2})
and
0
on the others). We denote the class of
e_{(y_{1},y_{2})}
by
y_{1}\otimes y_{2}
.

If
\myvarphi:Y_{1}\times Y_{2}\to Z
is an
S
-bilinear map, one has a morphism of
S
-modules characterized by

\begin{array}[]{cccc}\overline{\myvarphi}:&Y_{1}\otimes_{S}Y_{2}&\longrightarrow&Z\\
&y_{1}\otimes y_{2}&\longmapsto&\myvarphi(y_{1},y_{2}).\end{array}

The association
\myvarphi\mapsto\overline{\myvarphi}
is a bijection from
\textup{Bil}_{S}(Y_{1}\times Y_{2},Z)
to
\Hom_{S}(Y_{1}\otimes_{S}Y_{2},Z)
. For a number
n\geqslant 1
and an
S
-module
Y
, we will denote the
n
-fold tensor product
Y\otimes\dotsc\otimes Y
by
Y^{\otimes n}
, and define
Y^{\otimes 0}
as
S
.

For the
S
-module
TY:=\underset{l=0}{\overset{\infty}{\bigoplus}}(Y)^{\otimes l}
, the map

(y_{1}\otimes\dotsc\otimes y_{i},z_{1}\otimes\dotsc\otimes z_{j})\longmapsto y_{1}\otimes\dotsc\otimes y_{i}\otimes z_{1}\otimes\dotsc\otimes z_{j}

extends, by linearity, to a product of
TY
that makes it a (typically non-commutative)
S
-algebra.

1.3.
Ordered blueprints

An
ordered blueprint
is a triple
(B^{\bullet},B^{+},\leqslant)
such that:

(i)

(B^{+},\leqslant)
is an ordered semiring;

(ii)

B^{\bullet}
is a pointed submonoid of
(B^{+},\cdot)
;

(iii)

B^{\bullet}
generates
B^{+}
as a semiring,
i.e.
, every element of
B^{+}
is a finite sum of elements in
B^{\bullet}
.

We call
B^{\bullet}
the
underlying monoid
and
B^{+}
the
ambient semiring
of
B
, and use
B^{\times}
to denote the set of invertible elements of
B^{\bullet}
. A
morphism of ordered blueprints
\myvarphi:B\to C
is an order-preserving morphism
\myvarphi:B^{+}\to C^{+}
of semirings that satisfies
\myvarphi(B^{\bullet})\subseteq C^{\bullet}
.

If
B
is an ordered blueprint and
\mathfrak{r}
is an additive and multiplicative preorder on
B^{+}
containing
\leqslant
, one has the
quotient ordered blueprint

B\mathfrak{\!}\sslash\!r:=\{\overline{B^{\bullet}},B^{+}/\mathfrak{c}_{\mathfrak{r}},\overline{\mathfrak{r}}\}
, where
\mathfrak{c}_{\mathfrak{r}}
and
\overline{\mathfrak{r}}
are the congruence and the partial order induced by
\mathfrak{r}
, respectively, and
\overline{B^{\bullet}}
is the multiplicative submonoid of
B^{+}/\mathfrak{c}_{\mathfrak{r}}
whose elements are the classes of elements in
B^{\bullet}
.

For a subset
H
of
B^{+}\times B^{+}
, we define the preorder generated by
H
as

\langle H\rangle:=\bigcap\{\text{additive and multiplicative preorders on }B^{+}\text{ containing }H\text{ and }\leqslant\}.

If
H=\{(a_{i},b_{i})|\,i\in I\}
, we write
\langle a_{i}\leqslant b_{i}|\,i\in I\rangle
to denote
\langle H\rangle
, and
\langle a_{i}\equiv b_{i}|\,i\in I\rangle
to denote
\langle a_{i}\leqslant b_{i}\mbox{ and }b_{i}\leqslant a_{i}|\,i\in I\rangle
.

For an ordered blueprint
B
, let
\mathfrak{f}:=\langle a\equiv b|\,a\leqslant b\rangle
and define the
algebraic hull
of
B
as the ordered blueprint
B^{\textup{hull}}:=B\big/\penalty\!\!\!\big/\penalty\mathfrak{f}
. Note that the partial order of
B^{\textup{hull}}
is trivial.

If
(D,\cdot)
is a pointed monoid with absorbing element
0_{D}
, one has the
semiring-algebra

\widetilde{D}:=\mathbb{N}[D]\big/\penalty\!\langle 0\equiv 1.0_{D}\rangle
, whose elements can be seen as formal finite sums of non-zero elements of
D
, with operations

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\sum n_{d}.d+\sum m_{d}.d&=&\sum(n_{d}+m_{d}).d\\
\big(\sum n_{d}.d\big)\big(\sum m_{d}.d\big)&=&\underset{d\in D\backslash\{0_{D}\}}{\sum}\bigg(\underset{a.b=d}{\sum}n_{a}m_{b}\bigg)d.\end{array}

If
H\subseteq\widetilde{D}\times\widetilde{D}
, we use
D\big/\penalty\!\!\!\big/\penalty\!\big\langle\,{H}\,\big\rangle
to denote the ordered blueprint
(D,\widetilde{D},=)\big/\penalty\!\!\!\big/\penalty\!\big\langle\,{H}\,\big\rangle
.

There are two canonical functors
(-)^{\bullet}
and
(-)^{+}
that sends an ordered blueprint to its underlying monoid and ambient semiring, respectively.

1.4.
Ordered blue modules

Let
B=(B^{\bullet},B^{+},\leqslant_{B})
be an ordered blueprint. An
ordered blue
B
-module
, or simply
B
-module
, is a triple
M=(M^{\bullet},M^{+},\leqslant_{M})
such that:

(i)

M^{+}
is a
B^{+}
-module;

(ii)

\leqslant_{M}
is an additive partial order on
M^{+}
such that
(b_{1}.m_{1})\leqslant_{M}(b_{2}.m_{2})
whenever
b_{1}\leqslant_{B}b_{2}
and
m_{1}\leqslant_{M}m_{2}
;

(iii)

M^{\bullet}
is a pointed
B^{\bullet}
-subset of
M^{+}
, where the map
B^{\bullet}\times M^{+}\to M^{+}
is the restriction of
B^{+}\times M^{+}\to M^{+}
;

(iv)

M^{\bullet}
generates
M^{+}
as a semigroup,
i.e.
, every element of
M^{+}
is a finite sum of elements in
M^{\bullet}
.

We call
M^{\bullet}
the
underlying
B^{\bullet}
-set
and
M^{+}
the
ambient
B^{+}
-module
of
M
. A
morphism of
B
-modules
f:M\to N
is an order-preserving morphism
f:M^{+}\to N^{+}
of
B^{+}
-modules such that
f(M^{\bullet})\subseteq N^{\bullet}
. The category of
B
-modules will be denoted by
B\text{-}\textup{Mod}
.

An
additive
B
-preorder
on
M
is an additive preorder
\mathfrak{r}
on the monoid
M^{+}
that contains
\leqslant_{M}
and satisfies
b_{1}.m_{1}\leqslant_{\mathfrak{r}}b_{2}.m_{2}
whenever
b_{1}\leqslant_{B}b_{2}
and
m_{1}\leqslant_{\mathfrak{r}}m_{2}
. A
congruence
is a
B
-preorder that is symmetric. If
\mathfrak{r}
is a
B
-preorder on
M
, the set
\mathfrak{c}_{\mathfrak{r}}:=\{(m,n)|\,m\equiv_{\mathfrak{r}}n\}
is a congruence. In this case, we have the
quotient
B
-module

M/\!\!\!/\mathfrak{r}:=(\overline{M^{\bullet}},M^{+}/\mathfrak{c}_{\mathfrak{r}},\overline{\mathfrak{r}})
, where
M^{+}/\mathfrak{c}_{\mathfrak{r}}
is the quotient
B^{+}
-module,
\overline{M^{\bullet}}:=\{[m]|\,m\in M^{\bullet}\}

Proposition 1.1
.

The map

\begin{array}[]{cccc}\Phi:&\textup{Bil}_{B}(M_{1}\times M_{2},N)&\longrightarrow&\Hom_{B}(M_{1}\otimes M_{2},N)\\

c+b=c=c+a
and
c+c=a+c
. Thus, in
\mathcal{S}^{+}
, one has
1.c\leqslant 1.a+1.b
and
1.a\leqslant 2.c
. Analogously,
1.b\leqslant 2.c
. Therefore,
1.a+1.b\leqslant 4.c
in
\mathcal{S}^{+}
, which implies
1.c\leqslant 1.a+1.b\leqslant 4.c=1.c
. Thus,
1.c=1.a+1.b
in
\mathcal{S}^{\textup{idem},+}
. As a consequence, we obtain that
\myvarphi
is injective.

Next we observe that
\big(\bigwedge^{d}\mathcal{S}^{n}\big)^{+}\simeq\bigg(\mathcal{S}^{[n]\choose d}\bigg)^{+}\simeq\big(\mathcal{S}^{+}\big)^{[n]\choose d}\mbox{ as }\mathcal{S}^{+}\mbox{-modules}
, which implies

\begin{array}[]{c}\big(\bigwedge^{d}\mathcal{S}^{n}\big)^{\textup{idem},+}\simeq\bigg(\mathcal{S}^{[n]\choose d}\bigg)^{\textup{idem},+}\simeq\big(\mathcal{S}^{\textup{idem},+}\big)^{[n]\choose d}\simeq S^{[n]\choose d}\simeq\bigwedge^{d}S^{n}\end{array}

as
S
-modules.

Thus
\big(\bigwedge\mathcal{S}^{n}\big)^{\textup{idem},+}\simeq\underset{d=0}{\overset{n}{\bigoplus}}\big(\bigwedge^{d}\mathcal{S}^{n}\big)^{+}\simeq\underset{d=0}{\overset{n}{\bigoplus}}\big(\bigwedge^{d}S^{n}\big)\simeq\bigwedge S^{n}
, via

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\big(\bigwedge\mathcal{S}^{n}\big)^{\textup{idem},+}&\longrightarrow&\bigwedge S^{n}\\
[e_{I}]&\longmapsto&e_{I}.\end{array}

As this map is a morphism of
S
-algebras, we have the result.
∎

3.
Matroids

In this section we will focus on matroids over blueprints and prove
Theorem C
.

Definition 3.1
.

A
Grassmann-Plücker function of rank
d
on
[n]
with coefficients in
B
is a function
\Delta:{[n]\choose d}\to B^{\bullet}
such that
\Delta(J)
is in
B^{\times}
for some
J
and satisfies the Plücker relations

\displaystyle\underset{i_{k}\notin X}{\sum}\myepsilon^{k}\Delta(X\cup\{i_{k}\})\Delta(Y\backslash\{i_{k}\})\geqslant 0

whenever
X\in{[n]\choose d-1}
and
Y=\{i_{1},\dotsc,i_{d+1}\}\in{[n]\choose d+1}
, with
i_{1}<\dotsc<i_{d+1}
.

We say that two Grassmann-Plücker functions
\Delta
and
\Delta^{\prime}
are equivalent, and write
\Delta\sim\Delta^{\prime}
, if there is some
a
in
B^{\times}
such that
\Delta(I)=a.\Delta^{\prime}(I)
for all
I
. A
B
-matroid (of rank
d
on
[n]
)
is an equivalence class of Grassmann-Plücker functions (of rank
d
on
[n]
) with coefficients in
B
(cf.
[
BL21
, Definition 5.1]
).

Remark 3.2
.

Besides other applications, the order in blueprint theory is used to describe relations analogous to algebraic equations. In particular, the
positive cone

\{b\in B^{+}|\,b\geqslant 0\}
in Baker and Lorscheid’s theory of
{\mathbb{F}}^{\pm}_{1}
-algebras plays the same role as of the null set in Baker and Bowler’s theory of tracts, generalizing previous concepts of zero elements and sets (cf.
[
BL21
, Section 1.2.4]
and
[
BB19
, Section 2.3]
).

For
X\in{[n]\choose d-1}
and
Y=\{i_{1},\dotsc,i_{d+1}\}\in{[n]\choose d+1}
with
i_{1}<\dotsc<i_{d+1}
, there exists a (unique) morphism of
B
-modules
\myvarphi_{X,Y}:\bigwedge^{d}B^{n}\otimes\bigwedge^{d}B^{n}\to B
that sends
e_{I}\otimes e_{J}
to
\myepsilon^{k}
if there is
k\in[d+1]
such that
I=X\cup\{i_{k}\}
and
J=Y\backslash\{i_{k}\}
, and sends
e_{I}\otimes e_{J}
to
0
otherwise.

Definition 3.3
.

A rank
d

B
-Plücker vector is a
v\in H_{d,n}\backslash K_{d,n}
satisfying
\myvarphi_{X,Y}(v\otimes v)\geqslant 0
, i.e., a
v=\displaystyle\underset{I\in{[n]\choose d}}{\sum}v_{I}e_{I}\in H_{d,n}
such that there exists a
J
with
v_{J}\in B^{\times}
and, for all
X\in{[n]\choose d-1}
and
Y\in{[n]\choose d+1}
, one has

\underset{i_{k}\notin X}{\sum}\myepsilon^{k}v_{X\cup\{i_{k}\}}v_{Y\backslash\{i_{k}\}}\geqslant 0,

where
Y=\{i_{1},\dotsc,i_{d+1}\}
with
i_{1}<\dotsc<i_{d+1}
.

Note that
B^{\times}
acts on the set of
B
-Plücker vectors, and more generally on
H_{d,n}
, by usual multiplication. So we can take the equivalence class of a
B
-Plücker vector in
H_{d,n}/B^{\times}
.

Theorem 3.4
.

There is a bijection

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\{\text{rank d $B$-Pl\"{u}cker vectors}\}&\longrightarrow&\{\text{Grassmann-Pl\"{u}cker functions }\Delta:{[n]\choose d}\rightarrow B^{\bullet}\}\\
v=\displaystyle\underset{I\in{[n]\choose d}}{\sum}v_{I}e_{I}&\longmapsto&\Delta:I\longmapsto v_{I}\end{array}

Proof.

It follows from
subsection 2.2
and the definitions above.
∎

The next corollaries prove
Theorem C
.

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
K
be a field and
B:=K^{\textup{mon}}
. Then there exists a bijection between
B
-matroids of rank
d
on
[n]
and
K
-matroids of rank
d
on
[n]
.

Proof.

Due to the description of
K
-matroids via Grassmann-Plücker functions (cf.
[
BB19
, p. 841]
), the bijection is reached by composing the Grassmann-Plücker functions presented in
Theorem 3.4
with the functor
(-)^{\textup{hull},+}
and taking equivalence classes on both sides.
∎

The following is a reformulation of Definition 4.1.1 from
[
GG18
]
.

Definition 3.6
.

Let
S
be an indempotent semifield. A
rank
d
tropical
S
-Plücker vector
is a nonzero
v=\sum v_{I}e_{I}\in\bigwedge^{d}S^{n}
satisfying, for all subsets
A\in{[n]\choose d+1}
and

\underset{i\in A\backslash X}{\sum}v_{a-i}v_{X+i}=\underset{i\in A\backslash X,\;i\neq p}{\sum}v_{a-i}v_{X+i}

in

, for all

.

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
S
be an idempotent semifield and
B:=S^{\textup{mon}}
. Then there exists a bijection between rank
d

B
-Plücker vectors and rank
d
tropical

Note that, due to the description of the relations

Corollary 3.8
.

There is a bijection between rank

-matroids and classes of rank

We just need to take the equivalence class (by the action of

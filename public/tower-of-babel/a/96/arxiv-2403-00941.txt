Abstract
. We prove a number of results involving the kernel of the identity minus the monodromy on the vanishing cycles.

1.
Introduction

Suppose that
{\mathcal{U}}
is a non-empty open subset of
{\mathbb{C}}^{n+1}
and
f:{\mathcal{U}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
is a nowhere locally constant, reduced, complex analytic function such that
f^{-1}(0)
is non-empty. Then
V(f)=f^{-1}(0)
is an affine hypersurface inside
{\mathcal{U}}
.

In Theorem 2.5 of
[
7
]
, we proved that we have a short exact sequence in the abelian category of perverse sheaves on
V(f)
(using
{\mathbb{Z}}
as our base ring):

[formula]

0\to\ker\{\operatorname{id}-\widetilde{T}_{f}\}\to{\mathbb{Z}}^{\bullet}_{V(f)}[n]\rightarrow\mathbf{I}^{\bullet}_{V(f)}\to 0,

where
\mathbf{I}^{\bullet}_{V(f)}
is the intersection cohomology complex on
V(f)
(with constant
{\mathbb{Z}}
coefficients) and
\widetilde{T}_{f}
is the vanishing cycle monodromy operator.

In Theorem 2.6 of
[
7
]
, we note that the dual statement is also true. That dual result is, letting
j:V(f)\rightarrow{\mathcal{U}}
denote the inclusion, there exists a short exact sequence

[formula]

0\to\mathbf{I}^{\bullet}_{V(f)}\to j^{!}[1]{\mathbb{Z}}_{\mathcal{U}}^{\bullet}[n+1]\to{\operatorname{coker}}\{\operatorname{id}-\widetilde{T}_{f}\}\to 0,

in
\operatorname{Perv}(V(f))
.

In this paper, we will prove a number of other results involving
\ker\{\operatorname{id}-\widetilde{T}_{f}\}
, which is the perverse eigenspace of one for the vanishing cycle monodromy; in particular, in Theorem
5.5
, we prove generalizations of (
\dagger
) and (
\ddagger
).

For

The vanishing triangle

\rightarrow j^{*}[-1]\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{comp}}\psi_{f}[-1]\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{can}}\phi_{f}[-1]\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle[1]}}{{\longrightarrow}};

\operatorname{var}\circ\operatorname{can}=\operatorname{id}-T_{f}

\operatorname{can}\circ\operatorname{var}=\operatorname{id}-\widetilde{T}_{f}

3.
A Fundamental Exact Sequence

Suppose that
\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}\in\operatorname{Perv}(X)
. Then, applying perverse cohomology to the vanishing and dual vanishing triangles, we immediately conclude:

Proposition 3.1
.

{}^{\mu}\hskip-1.4457ptH^{k}(j^{!}[1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet})
is possibly non-zero only for
k=-1,0
,
{}^{\mu}\hskip-1.4457ptH^{k}(j^{*}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet})
is possibly non-zero only for
k=0,1
, and there are exact sequences

0\rightarrow{}^{\mu}\hskip-1.4457ptH^{0}(j^{*}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet})\xrightarrow{{}^{\mu}\hskip-1.4457ptH^{0}(\operatorname{comp})}\psi_{f}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{can}}\phi_{f}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}\rightarrow{}^{\mu}\hskip-1.4457ptH^{1}(j^{*}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet})\rightarrow 0;

and

0\rightarrow{}^{\mu}\hskip-1.4457ptH^{-1}(j^{!}[1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet})\rightarrow\phi_{f}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{var}}\psi_{f}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}\xrightarrow{{}^{\mu}\hskip-1.4457ptH^{0}(\operatorname{pmoc})}{}^{\mu}\hskip-1.4457ptH^{0}(j^{!}[1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet})\rightarrow 0.

Thus,
{}^{\mu}\hskip-1.4457ptH^{0}(j^{*}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet})\cong\ker\{\operatorname{can}\}
,
{}^{\mu}\hskip-1.4457ptH^{1}(j^{*}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet})\cong{\operatorname{coker}}\{\operatorname{can}\}
,
{}^{\mu}\hskip-1.4457ptH^{-1}(j^{!}[1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet})\cong\ker\{\operatorname{var}\}
, and
{}^{\mu}\hskip-1.4457ptH^{0}(j^{!}[1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet})\cong{\operatorname{coker}}\{\operatorname{var}\}
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Suppose that
\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}
is a perverse sheaf on
X
. Then, there is an exact sequence in
\operatorname{Perv}(V(f))
:

0\rightarrow\frac{\ker\{{\operatorname{id}}-\widetilde{T}_{f}\}}{\ker\{\operatorname{var}\}}\longrightarrow{}^{\mu}\hskip-1.4457ptH^{0}(j^{*}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet})\xlongrightarrow{{}^{\mu}\hskip-1.4457ptH^{0}(\omega_{f})}{}^{\mu}\hskip-1.4457ptH^{0}(j^{!}[1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet})\longrightarrow\frac{\operatorname{im}\{\operatorname{can}\}}{\operatorname{im}\{{\operatorname{id}}-\widetilde{T}_{f}\}}\rightarrow 0,

where we view
\ker\{\operatorname{var}\}
as a sub-perverse sheaf of
\ker\{{\operatorname{id}}-\widetilde{T}_{f}\}
by the canonical injection
\ker\{\operatorname{var}\}\hookrightarrow\ker\{\operatorname{can}\circ\operatorname{var}\}=\ker\{{\operatorname{id}}-\widetilde{T}_{f}\}
, and
we view
\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits\{{\operatorname{id}}-\widetilde{T}_{f}\}
as a sub-perverse sheaf of
\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits\{\operatorname{can}\}
by the canonical injection
\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits\{\operatorname{{\operatorname{id}}-\widetilde{T}_{f}}\}=\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits\{\operatorname{can}\circ\operatorname{var}\}\hookrightarrow\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits\{\operatorname{can}\}
.

Proof.

One easily verifies that there is an exact sequence

0\rightarrow\ker\{\operatorname{var}\}\rightarrow\ker\{\operatorname{can}\circ\operatorname{var}\}\rightarrow\ker\{\operatorname{can}\}\rightarrow\hfill

\hfill{\operatorname{coker}}\{\operatorname{var}\}\rightarrow{\operatorname{coker}}\{\operatorname{can}\circ\operatorname{var}\}\rightarrow{\operatorname{coker}}\{\operatorname{can}\}\rightarrow 0,

where

•

the second arrow from the left is the canonical injection,

•

the third arrow is induced by
\operatorname{var}
,

•

the fourth arrow is the composition of the canonical injection of
\ker\{\operatorname{can}\}
into
\psi_{f}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}
with the canonical surjection from
\psi_{f}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}
onto
{\operatorname{coker}}\{\operatorname{var}\}
,

•

the fifth arrow is induced by
\operatorname{can}
, and

•

the sixth arrow is the canonical surjection.

Now the exact sequence in the statement of the theorem follows immediately from Proposition
3.1
.
∎

4.
Splitting

We have the following easy result which tells us when the vanishing cycles are a direct summand of the nearby cycles.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}
be a perverse sheaf on
X
. Then, the following are equivalent:

(1)

{\operatorname{id}}-\widetilde{T}_{f}
is an isomorphism;

(2)

\ker\{{\operatorname{id}}-\widetilde{T}_{f}\}=0
and
{\operatorname{coker}}\{{\operatorname{id}}-\widetilde{T}_{f}\}=0
;

(3)

{}^{\mu}\hskip-1.4457ptH^{-1}(j^{!}[1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet})=0
,
{}^{\mu}\hskip-1.4457ptH^{1}(j^{*}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet})=0
, and
{}^{\mu}\hskip-1.4457ptH^{0}(\omega_{f})
is an isomorphism;

(4)

j^{!}[1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}
and
j^{*}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}
are perverse, and
\omega_{f}:j^{*}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}\rightarrow j^{!}[1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}
is an isomorphism;

(5)

\omega_{f}:j^{*}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}\rightarrow j^{!}[1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}
is an isomorphism, and
\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}
is the intermediate extension of
i^{*}\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}=i^{!}\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}
to
V(f)
.

Furthermore, when these equivalent conditions hold, the vanishing and dual vanishing triangles are short exact sequences in
\operatorname{Perv}(V(f))
which split in a manner compatible with the monodromy automorphisms; thus we have isomorphisms

\psi_{f}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}\cong\phi_{f}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}\oplus j^{*}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}\cong\phi_{f}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}\oplus j^{!}[1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}

and, via these isomorphisms,
T_{f}
is identified with
(\widetilde{T}_{f},{\operatorname{id}})
in each case.

Proof.

Given Proposition
3.1
and Theorem
3.2
, the equivalences of (1), (2), and (3) are utterly trivial. Clearly (4) implies (3), and (3) together with Theorem
3.2
implies (4), since having zero perverse cohomology outside of degree 0 is equivalent to being perverse (see 1.3.7 of
[
1
]
or Proposition 5.1.7 of
[
3
]
).

We need to show the equivalence of (4) and (5). Recall the standard results (i) and (ii) from Section
2
, which tell us that
j^{*}[-1]
sends
{}^{{}^{\mu}}\mathbb{D}^{{}^{\geqslant 0}}(X)
to
{}^{{}^{\mu}}\mathbb{D}^{{}^{\geqslant 0}}(V(f))
and
j^{!}[1]
sends
{}^{{}^{\mu}}\mathbb{D}^{{}^{\leqslant 0}}(X)
to
{}^{{}^{\mu}}\mathbb{D}^{{}^{\leqslant 0}}(V(f))
; therefore,
j^{*}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}\in{}^{{}^{\mu}}\mathbb{D}^{{}^{\geqslant 0}}(V(f))
and
j^{!}[1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}\in{}^{{}^{\mu}}\mathbb{D}^{{}^{\leqslant 0}}(V(f))
. But one of the equivalent definitions/characterizations of
\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}
being the intermediate extension of
i^{*}\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}=i^{!}\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}
is that
j^{*}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}\in{}^{{}^{\mu}}\mathbb{D}^{{}^{\leqslant 0}}(V(f))
and
j^{!}[1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}\in{}^{{}^{\mu}}\mathbb{D}^{{}^{\geqslant 0}}(V(f))
(see, for instance,
[
3
]
Definition 5.2.6). Thus (4) and (5) are equivalent.

We need to show that (1)-(5) imply the splittings exist. Assuming (1)-(5), we have short exact sequences in
\operatorname{Perv}(X)
:

0\rightarrow j^{*}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{comp}}\psi_{f}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{can}}\phi_{f}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}\rightarrow 0

and

0\rightarrow\phi_{f}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{var}}\psi_{f}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{pmoc}}j^{!}[1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}\rightarrow 0,

where
\operatorname{can}\circ\operatorname{var}=\operatorname{id}-\widetilde{T}_{f}
.

Consider
\operatorname{var}\circ(\operatorname{id}-\widetilde{T}_{f})^{-1}
from
\phi_{f}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}
to
\psi_{f}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}
, and
(\operatorname{id}-\widetilde{T}_{f})^{-1}\circ\operatorname{can}
from
\psi_{f}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}
to
\phi_{f}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}
. Then
\operatorname{can}\circ[\operatorname{var}\circ(\operatorname{id}-\widetilde{T}_{f})^{-1}]=\operatorname{id}
and
[(\operatorname{id}-\widetilde{T}_{f})^{-1}\circ\operatorname{can}]\circ\operatorname{var}=\operatorname{id}
. The first equality shows that the first short exact sequence splits, and the second equality shows that the second short exact sequence splits.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

Throughout this paper, we use
{\mathbb{Z}}
as our base ring because we care about torsion. However, we could use any base ring,
R
, which is a commutative, regular, Noetherian ring, with finite Krull dimension (e.g.,
{\mathbb{Z}}
,
{\mathbb{Q}}
, or
{\mathbb{C}}
). In particular, if we use a base ring which is a field in Theorem
4.1
, then
\ker\{{\operatorname{id}}-\widetilde{T}_{f}\}=0
if an only if
{\operatorname{coker}}\{{\operatorname{id}}-\widetilde{T}_{f}\}=0
; so, in the field case,
\ker\{{\operatorname{id}}-\widetilde{T}_{f}\}=0
if and only if
{\operatorname{id}}-\widetilde{T}_{f}
is an isomorphism.

to
X
follows immediately from (1), since one of the equivalent characterizations of the intermediate extension is that
j^{*}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}\in{}^{{}^{\mu}}\mathbf{D}^{{}^{\leqslant{0}}}(V(f))
and
j^{!}[1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}\in{}^{{}^{\mu}}\mathbf{D}^{{}^{\geqslant{0}}}(V(f))
. See, for instance,
[
3
]
, Definition 5.2.6.

Proof of (3): This follows immediately from (2).

Proof of (4): Suppose that
\Sigma_{f}
contains an irreducible component
C
of
\operatorname{supp}j^{*}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}
or
\operatorname{supp}j^{!}[1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}
, where we know that
\operatorname{supp}j^{*}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}
and
\operatorname{supp}j^{!}[1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}
are perverse by (1). Then, restricting to an open dense subset of
C
either
m^{*}j^{*}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}
or
m^{!}j^{!}[1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}
would be perverse and non-zero. But this would contradict either
{}^{\mu}\hskip-1.4457ptH^{-1}(\hat{m}^{*}\mathbf{P}^{\bullet})=0
or
{}^{\mu}\hskip-1.4457ptH^{1}(\hat{m}^{!}\mathbf{P}^{\bullet})=0
.

Proof of (5): By (1), Proposition
3.1
, and Theorem
3.2
, we have an exact sequence in
\operatorname{Perv}(V(f))
:

0\rightarrow\ker\{{\operatorname{id}}-\widetilde{T}_{f}\}\rightarrow j^{*}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}\xlongrightarrow{\omega_{f}}j^{!}[1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}\rightarrow{\operatorname{coker}}\{{\operatorname{id}}-\widetilde{T}_{f}\}\rightarrow 0.

Let
\mathbf{J}^{\bullet}:=\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits\{\omega_{f}\}
. Then we have two short exact sequences in
\operatorname{Perv}(V(f))
:

0\rightarrow\ker\{{\operatorname{id}}-\widetilde{T}_{f}\}\rightarrow j^{*}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}\rightarrow\mathbf{J}^{\bullet}\rightarrow 0.

and

0\rightarrow\mathbf{J}^{\bullet}\rightarrow j^{!}[1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}\rightarrow{\operatorname{coker}}\{{\operatorname{id}}-\widetilde{T}_{f}\}\rightarrow 0.

We claim that
\mathbf{J}^{\bullet}\cong\mathbf{I}^{\bullet}_{V(f)}
.

First, by applying
\ell^{*}=\ell^{!}
to the short exact sequences, we see that
\mathbf{J}^{\bullet}
is an extension of
\ell^{*}j^{*}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}\cong\ell^{!}j^{!}[1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}

m^{*}\mathbf{J}^{\bullet}\in{}^{{}^{\mu}}\mathbf{D}^{{}^{\leqslant{-1}}}(\Sigma_{f})
and
m^{!}\mathbf{J}^{\bullet}\in{}^{{}^{\mu}}\mathbf{D}^{{}^{\geqslant{1}}}(\Sigma_{f})

{}^{\mu}\hskip-1.4457ptH^{0}(\hat{m}^{*}\mathbf{J}^{\bullet})=0
and
{}^{\mu}\hskip-1.4457ptH^{0}(\hat{m}^{!}\mathbf{J}^{\bullet})=0
.

Apply
m^{*}
to the first short exact sequence/distinguished triangle above, apply
m!
to the second short exact sequence/distinguished triangle, and take the long exact sequences on perverse cohomology. We obtain exact sequences

\rightarrow{}^{\mu}\hskip-1.4457ptH^{0}(m^{*}j^{*}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet})\rightarrow{}^{\mu}\hskip-1.4457ptH^{0}(m^{*}\mathbf{J}^{\bullet})\rightarrow{}^{\mu}\hskip-1.4457ptH^{1}(m^{*}\ker\{{\operatorname{id}}-\widetilde{T}_{f}\})\rightarrow

and

{}^{\mu}\hskip-1.4457ptH^{1}(m^{*}\ker\{{\operatorname{id}}-\widetilde{T}_{f}\})=0
and
{}^{\mu}\hskip-1.4457ptH^{-1}(m^{!}{\operatorname{coker}}\{{\operatorname{id}}-\widetilde{T}_{f}\})=0
because

and

are perverse since they are restrictions/upper-shrieks of perverse sheaves to sets containing the supports of the initial perverse sheaves. Furthermore,

{}^{\mu}\hskip-1.4457ptH^{0}(m^{*}j^{*}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet})={}^{\mu}\hskip-1.4457ptH^{-1}(\hat{m}^{*}\mathbf{P}^{\bullet})=0

{}^{\mu}\hskip-1.4457ptH^{0}(m^{!}j^{!}[1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet})={}^{\mu}\hskip-1.4457ptH^{1}(\hat{m}^{!}\mathbf{P}^{\bullet})=0

6.
The constant sheaf on affine space

Let us consider the classical case from the introduction, where we replace
X
with
{\mathcal{U}}
, a non-empty open subset of
{\mathbb{C}}^{n+1}
, and replace
\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}
with
{\mathbb{Z}}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{U}}[n+1]
.

We begin with an easy, but fundamental, lemma. This lemma is well-known, but the proof is short, so we include it.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
Y
be a closed complex analytic subspace of
{\mathcal{U}}
, which we do not assume is pure-dimensional. Denote by
c
the codimension of
Y
in
{\mathcal{U}}
, i.e., let
c:=n+1-\dim Y
. Let
r:Y\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{U}}
be the inclusion.

Then,

r^{*}[-c]{\mathbb{Z}}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{U}}[n+1]\in{}^{{}^{\mu}}\mathbf{D}^{{}^{\leqslant{0}}}(Y)\hskip 14.45377pt\textnormal{ and }\hskip 14.45377ptr^{!}[c]{\mathbb{Z}}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{U}}[n+1]\in{}^{{}^{\mu}}\mathbf{D}^{{}^{\geqslant{0}}}(Y).

Proof.

First, we will show that

r^{*}[-c]{\mathbb{Z}}^{\bullet}_{Y}[n+1]\cong{\mathbb{Z}}^{\bullet}_{Y}[\dim Y]\in{}^{{}^{\mu}}\mathbf{D}^{{}^{\leqslant{0}}}(\Sigma),

which says that
r^{*}[-c]{\mathbb{Z}}^{\bullet}_{Y}[n+1]
satisfies the support condition. This argument is simple.

Let
p
be an integer. We need to show that
\dim\operatorname{supp}^{-p}\big({\mathbb{Z}}^{\bullet}_{Y}[\dim Y]\big)\leq p
. We have

\operatorname{supp}^{-p}\big({\mathbb{Z}}^{\bullet}_{Y}[\dim Y]\big)=\overline{\{x\in Y\,|\,H^{\dim Y-p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{\bullet}_{Y})_{x}\neq 0\}}.

Now
H^{\dim Y-p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{\bullet}_{Y})_{x}=0
unless
p=\dim Y
. Thus the support condition is satisfied.

Now we will show that

r^{!}[c]{\mathbb{Z}}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{U}}[n+1]\in{}^{{}^{\mu}}\mathbf{D}^{{}^{\geqslant{0}}}(Y),

which says that
r^{!}[c]{\mathbb{Z}}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{U}}[n+1]
satisfies the cosupport condition. Let
p
be an integer. We need to show that
\dim\operatorname{cosupp}^{p}\big(r^{!}[c]{\mathbb{Z}}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{U}}[n+1]\big)\leq p
.

For all
x\in Y
, let
v_{x}:\{x\}\hookrightarrow Y
denote the inclusion, and let
\hat{v}_{x}:=r\circ v_{x}
; hence,
\hat{v}_{x}
is the inclusion of
\{x\}
into
{\mathcal{U}}
. Then, we have

\operatorname{cosupp}^{p}\big(r^{!}[c]{\mathbb{Z}}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{U}}[n+1]\big)=\overline{\{x\in Y\,|\,H^{p}(v_{x}^{!}r^{!}[c]{\mathbb{Z}}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{U}}[n+1])\neq 0\}}=

\overline{\{x\in Y\,|\,H^{n+1+c+p}(\hat{v}_{x}^{!}{\mathbb{Z}}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{U}})\neq 0\}}=\overline{\{x\in Y\,|\,H^{n+1+c+p}(B^{\circ}_{\epsilon}(x),B^{\circ}_{\epsilon}(x)\backslash\{x\};\,{\mathbb{Z}})\neq 0\}}=

\overline{\{x\in Y\,|\,\widetilde{H}^{n+c+p}(S^{2n+1};\,{\mathbb{Z}})\neq 0\}}.

where
B^{\circ}_{\epsilon}(x)
again denotes a open ball of (small) radius
\epsilon>0
, centered at
x
,
S^{2n+1}
denotes a sphere in
{\mathcal{U}}
of real dimension
2n+1
(its center and radius are irrelevant), and
\widetilde{H}
denotes reduced cohomology. Now
\widetilde{H}^{n+c+p}(S^{2n+1};\,{\mathbb{Z}})=0
unless
p=n+1-c=\dim Y
. Thus the cosupport condition is satisfied.
∎

Now, as before, let
f:{\mathcal{U}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
be a nowhere locally constant complex analytic function such that
V(f)
is non-empty. Note that we have
not
assumed that
f
is reduced. Let
\Sigma_{f}:=\operatorname{supp}\phi_{f}[-1]{\mathbb{Z}}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{U}}[n+1]
.

There is a notion of the singular set of the analytic set
V(f)
; it is the set of points at which
V(f)
fails to be an analytic submanifold of
{\mathcal{U}}
(which, using results about Milnor fibrations, is also the set of points where
V(f)
fails to be even a topological submanifold of
{\mathcal{U}}
). We denote this singular set by
\Sigma V(f)
, and note that it always has dimension at most
n-1
.

If
f
is not reduced, then
\Sigma_{f}
will contain an irreducible component of
V(f)
, while
\Sigma V(f)
will not. However, if
f
is reduced, then
\Sigma_{f}=\Sigma V(f)
, and so the intermediate extension to
V(f)
of the shifted constant sheaf on
V(f)\backslash\Sigma V(f)
– the intersection cohomology on
V(f)
– is the same as the intermediate extension to
V(f)
of the shifted constant sheaf on
V(f)\backslash\Sigma_{f}
, which is how we defined
\mathbf{I}^{\bullet}_{V(f)}
in Definition
5.4
.

As in the general case, we let
j:V(f)\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{U}}
and
\hat{m}:\Sigma_{f}\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{U}}
denote the inclusions.

The following lemma explains our terminology in Definition
5.3
.

Lemma 6.2
.

The function
f
is reduced (in the algebraic sense) if and only if
f
is
{\mathbb{Z}}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{U}}[n+1]
-reduced, i.e., if and only if

\hat{m}^{*}{\mathbb{Z}}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{U}}[n+1]\in{}^{{}^{\mu}}\mathbf{D}^{{}^{\leqslant{-2}}}(\Sigma_{f})\hskip 14.45377pt\textnormal{ and }\hskip 14.45377pt\hat{m}^{!}{\mathbb{Z}}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{U}}[n+1]\in{}^{{}^{\mu}}\mathbf{D}^{{}^{\geqslant{2}}}(\Sigma_{f}).

Proof.

Suppose that

\hat{m}^{*}{\mathbb{Z}}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{U}}[n+1]\in{}^{{}^{\mu}}\mathbf{D}^{{}^{\leqslant{-2}}}(\Sigma_{f})\hskip 14.45377pt\textnormal{ and }\hskip 14.45377pt\hat{m}^{!}{\mathbb{Z}}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{U}}[n+1]\in{}^{{}^{\mu}}\mathbf{D}^{{}^{\geqslant{2}}}(\Sigma_{f}).

Then, Item (4) of Theorem
5.5
tells us that
\Sigma_{f}
does not contain an irreducible component of
V(f)
, i.e.,
f
is reduced.

Now we must prove the converse. Let
c
be the codimension of
\Sigma f
in
{\mathcal{U}}
, i.e.,
c=n+1-\dim\Sigma_{f}
. Assume that
f
is reduced, so that
\dim\Sigma_{f}\leq n-1
and so
c\geq 2
.

By Lemma
6.1
, we have that

\hat{m}^{*}[-c]{\mathbb{Z}}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{U}}[n+1]\in{}^{{}^{\mu}}\mathbf{D}^{{}^{\leqslant{0}}}(\Sigma_{f})\hskip 14.45377pt\textnormal{ and }\hskip 14.45377pt\hat{m}^{!}[c]{\mathbb{Z}}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{U}}[n+1]\in{}^{{}^{\mu}}\mathbf{D}^{{}^{\geqslant{0}}}(\Sigma_{f}),

that is

\hat{m}^{*}{\mathbb{Z}}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{U}}[n+1]\in{}^{{}^{\mu}}\mathbf{D}^{{}^{\leqslant{-c}}}(\Sigma_{f})\hskip 14.45377pt\textnormal{ and }\hskip 14.45377pt\hat{m}^{!}{\mathbb{Z}}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{U}}[n+1]\in{}^{{}^{\mu}}\mathbf{D}^{{}^{\geqslant{c}}}(\Sigma_{f}).

As
c\geq 2
, we are finished.

∎

From this lemma and Theorem
5.5
, we immediately conclude a new proof of (
\dagger
) and (
\ddagger
) from the introduction, which we state here as:

Theorem 6.3
.

Suppose that
f
is reduced. Then, there are short exact sequences in the abelian category of perverse sheaves on
V(f)
:

0\to\ker\{\operatorname{id}-\widetilde{T}_{f}\}\to{\mathbb{Z}}^{\bullet}_{V(f)}[n]\rightarrow\mathbf{I}^{\bullet}_{V(f)}\to 0,

and

0\to\mathbf{I}^{\bullet}_{V(f)}\to j^{!}[1]{\mathbb{Z}}_{\mathcal{U}}^{\bullet}[n+1]\to{\operatorname{coker}}\{\operatorname{id}-\widetilde{T}_{f}\}\to 0,

where
\mathbf{I}^{\bullet}_{V(f)}
is the intersection cohomology complex on
V(f)
.

As our final result, we will prove a theorem about integral cohomology (homology) manifolds. First, we need a lemma.

Lemma 6.4
.

For all
x\in V(f)
, let
E_{f,x}
be the total space of the Milnor fibration of
f
at
x
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

j^{!}[1]{\mathbb{Z}}_{\mathcal{U}}^{\bullet}[n+1]
has stalk cohomology isomorphic to that of
j^{*}[-1]{\mathbb{Z}}_{\mathcal{U}}^{\bullet}[n+1]\cong{\mathbb{Z}}_{V(f)}^{\bullet}[n]
, and

(2)

for all
x\in V(f)
,

H^{k}(E_{f,x};{\mathbb{Z}})\cong\begin{cases}{\mathbb{Z}},\ \textnormal{ if }k=1,0;\\
0,\ \textnormal{ if }k\neq 1,0.\end{cases}

Proof.

Item (1) means that

H^{p}(j^{!}[1]{\mathbb{Z}}_{\mathcal{U}}^{\bullet}[n+1])_{x}\cong\begin{cases}{\mathbb{Z}},\ \textnormal{ if }p=-n;\\
0,\ \textnormal{ if }p\neq-n.\end{cases}

Now,

H^{p}(j^{!}[1]{\mathbb{Z}}_{\mathcal{U}}^{\bullet}[n+1])_{x}\cong{\mathbb{H}}^{n+p+2}(B_{\epsilon}^{\circ}(x),\,B_{\epsilon}^{\circ}(x)\backslash V(f);{\mathbb{Z}})\cong\widetilde{H}^{n+p+1}(B_{\epsilon}^{\circ}(x)\backslash V(f);{\mathbb{Z}}),

where
B_{\epsilon}^{\circ}(x)
is a small open ball, centered at
x
, and
B_{\epsilon}^{\circ}(x)\backslash V(f)
is homotopy-equivalent to
E_{f,x}
, the total space of the Milnor fibration of
f
at
x
.

Thus, Item (1) is equivalent to

\widetilde{H}^{k}(E_{f,x};{\mathbb{Z}})\cong\begin{cases}{\mathbb{Z}},\ \textnormal{ if }k=1;\\
0,\ \textnormal{ if }k\neq 1,\end{cases}

or, equivalently,

H^{k}(E_{f,x};{\mathbb{Z}})\cong\begin{cases}{\mathbb{Z}},\ \textnormal{ if }k=1,0;\\
0,\ \textnormal{ if }k\neq 1,0.\end{cases}

∎

Theorem 6.5
.

Suppose that
f
is reduced, and that
j^{!}[1]{\mathbb{Z}}_{\mathcal{U}}^{\bullet}[n+1]
has stalk cohomology isomorphic to that of
j^{*}[-1]{\mathbb{Z}}_{\mathcal{U}}^{\bullet}[n+1]
. Then,

(1)

{\operatorname{id}}-\widetilde{T}_{f}
is an isomorphism,

(2)

j^{*}[-1]{\mathbb{Z}}_{\mathcal{U}}^{\bullet}[n+1]
and
j^{!}[1]{\mathbb{Z}}_{\mathcal{U}}^{\bullet}[n+1]
are perverse sheaves,

(3)

\omega_{f}:j^{*}[-1]{\mathbb{Z}}_{\mathcal{U}}^{\bullet}[n+1]\rightarrow j^{!}[1]{\mathbb{Z}}_{\mathcal{U}}^{\bullet}[n+1]
is an isomorphism, and both of these complexes are isomorphic to
\mathbf{I}^{\bullet}_{V(f)}
,

(4)

V(f)
is an integral cohomology/homology manifold, and

(5)

there is an isomorphism

\psi_{f}[-1]{\mathbb{Z}}_{\mathcal{U}}^{\bullet}[n+1]\cong\phi_{f}[-1]{\mathbb{Z}}_{\mathcal{U}}^{\bullet}[n+1]\oplus\mathbf{I}^{\bullet}_{V(f)}.

Proof.

The non-zero part of the cohomological version of the Wang sequence from Lemma 8.4 in
[
9
]
begins as follows:

0\rightarrow H^{0}(E_{f,x};\,{\mathbb{Z}})\rightarrow H^{0}(F_{f,x};\,{\mathbb{Z}})\xrightarrow{(\operatorname{id}-T_{f})^{-n}_{x}}H^{0}(F_{f,x};\,{\mathbb{Z}})\rightarrow

H^{1}(E_{f,x};\,{\mathbb{Z}})\rightarrow H^{1}(F_{f,x};\,{\mathbb{Z}})\xrightarrow{(\operatorname{id}-T_{f})^{-n+1}_{x}}H^{1}(F_{f,x};\,{\mathbb{Z}})\rightarrow

H^{2}(E_{f,x};\,{\mathbb{Z}})\rightarrow H^{2}(F_{f,x};\,{\mathbb{Z}})\xrightarrow{(\operatorname{id}-T_{f})^{-n+2}_{x}}H^{2}(F_{f,x};\,{\mathbb{Z}})\rightarrow,

where
F_{f,x}
is the Milnor fiber of
f
at
x
,
T_{f}
is the monodromy automorphism on the
nearby
cycles, and the subscript
k
in
(\operatorname{id}-T_{f})^{k}_{x}
denotes the degree (not exponentiation).

By Lemma
6.4
, we know that

H^{k}(E_{f,x};{\mathbb{Z}})\cong\begin{cases}{\mathbb{Z}},\ \textnormal{ if }k=1,0;\\
0,\ \textnormal{ if }p\neq 1,0.\end{cases}

Since

is reduced,
F_{f,x}
and
E_{f,x}
are path-connected. We also know that
H^{1}(F_{f,x};\,{\mathbb{Z}})
is torsion-free by the Universal Coefficient Theorem. Therefore we obtain the exact sequence

0\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}\xrightarrow{(\operatorname{id}-T_{f})^{-n}_{x}}{\mathbb{Z}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}\rightarrow H^{1}(F_{f,x};\,{\mathbb{Z}})\xrightarrow{(\operatorname{id}-T_{f})^{-n+1}_{x}}H^{1}(F_{f,x};\,{\mathbb{Z}})\rightarrow

where the first map from

We continue with all of our previous notation.

Theorem
3.2
and the definition of the intermediate extension motivate the following definition.

Definition 7.1
.

We define the
intermediate Wang restriction
of
\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}
to be the (perverse) image

\mathbf{J}^{\bullet}_{f}:=\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits\Big\{{}^{\mu}\hskip-1.4457ptH^{0}(j^{*}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet})\xlongrightarrow{{}^{\mu}\hskip-1.4457ptH^{0}(\omega_{f})}{}^{\mu}\hskip-1.4457ptH^{0}(j^{!}[1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet})\Big\}.

The point is that now Theorem
3.2
and Theorem
5.5
tell us immediately that we have:

Proposition 7.2
.

There are exact sequences in
\operatorname{Perv}(V(f))

Of course, the big question is:

Question 7.3
.

Does

have any interesting properties in general, even when it is not isomorphic to the intermediate extension

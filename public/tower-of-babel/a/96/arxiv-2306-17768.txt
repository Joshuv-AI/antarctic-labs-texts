Asymptotically quasiconvex functionals with general growth conditions.


Abstract
. We establish local regularity results for minimizers of autonomous vectorial integrals of Calculus of Variations, assuming ψ \psi -growth conditions and imposing φ \varphi -quasiconvexity only in an asymptotic sense, both in the sub-quadratic and super-quadratic case. In particular, we obtain C 1 , α C^{1,\alpha} regularity at points x 0 x_{0} where D ​ u Du is sufficiently large in a neighborhood of x 0 x_{0} , as well as Lipschitz regularity on a dense set. The results hold for all pairs of Young functions ( φ , ψ ) (\varphi,\psi) satisfying the Δ 2 \Delta_{2} condition.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we study multidimensional variational integrals of the form

\mathcal{F}(u)=\int_{\Omega}f(Du(x))\,dx\quad\text{ for }u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{N}

where
\Omega
is an open bounded subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
n\geq 2
and
N\geq 1
.

We consider Young functions
\varphi
and
\psi
of class
C^{1}([0,+\infty))\cap C^{2}(0,+\infty)
such that, satisfy the following conditions:

(H.0)

(1)

\varphi,\psi\in\Bigg\{h\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}):\quad\forall t\quad\exists\quad p_{1}(h),q_{1}(h)>0\quad p_{1}(h)\frac{h^{\prime}(t)}{t}\leq h^{\prime\prime}(t)\leq q_{1}(h)\frac{h^{\prime}(t)}{t}\Bigg\}

Where the context makes it clear, we will omit the explicit dependence on
h
.

(H.1)

Regularity
-
f\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{nN},\mathbb{R})
.

(H.2)

Asymptotic
W^{1,\varphi}
-quasiconvexity

[
12
]
- There exist
M\gg 0
,
\gamma>0
, and a continuous function
g\in W^{1,\varphi}(\mathbb{R}^{nN},\mathbb{R})
such that

f(z)=g(z),\quad\forall z:|z|>M

and
g
is strictly
W^{1,\varphi}
-quasiconvex, meaning that it satisfies

\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B_{1}}g(z+D\phi)\geq g(z)+\gamma\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B_{1}}\varphi_{1+|z|}(|D\phi|),\quad\forall z,\forall\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{1},\mathbb{R}^{N})

where
\varphi_{a}(t)
is defined for any
a>0
by

\varphi^{\prime}_{a}(t)=\varphi^{\prime}(a+t)\frac{t}{a+t},\quad\varphi_{a}(0)=0

and it was shown in
[
26
]
to satisfy

\varphi_{a}(t)\sim t^{2}\varphi^{\prime\prime}(a+t).

(H.3)

Growth conditions
- The following inequalities hold:

\Gamma^{\prime}\varphi(|z|)\leq f(z)\leq\Gamma^{\prime\prime}(1+\psi(|z|))

|D^{2}f(z)|\leq\Gamma^{\prime\prime}(1+\psi^{\prime\prime}(|z|))

for all
z\in\mathbb{R}^{nN}
, for some positive constants
\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma^{\prime\prime}>0
.

(H.4)

Range of anisotropy
- For any
a>M
, the function
\mathcal{N}_{a}=\phi_{a}\circ(\psi_{a}^{\prime})^{-1}
is a Young function, and its complementary Young function
\mathcal{N}_{a}^{*}
satisfies

[\mathcal{N}_{a}]^{*}(t)\leq c\varphi_{a}^{\beta}(t),\quad\forall t\gg 1

for some
1\leq\beta<\frac{n}{n-1}
.

From condition
(H.4)
, we deduce the inequality

\psi(t)\leq c\varphi^{\beta}(t),\quad\forall t\gg 1.

In the special case
\varphi(t)=t^{p}
and
\psi(t)=t^{q}
, condition
(H.4)
is equivalent to
q<p+\frac{1}{n}
.

In
[
26
]
, it was proven that condition
(H.2)
is equivalent to:

(H.2’)

There exist
M\gg 0
,
\gamma>0
such that

\forall z:|z|>M

\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B_{1}}f(z+D\phi)\geq f(z)+\gamma\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B_{1}}\varphi_{1+|z|}(|D\phi|),\quad\forall\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{1},\mathbb{R}^{N}).

This follows from the fact that

We study local
W^{1,\varphi}
-minimizers of

In the case of a globally quasiconvex functional and under the super-quadratic hypothesis

(see also

[

More precisely, we establish the following result:

Theorem 1
.

Let
f,\varphi,\psi
satisfy hypotheses
(H.0)
,
(H.1)
,
(H.2)
,
(H.3)
, and
(H.4)
, and let
u
be a local minimizer of the corresponding functional
\mathcal{F}
. Suppose that
z_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{nN}
satisfies
|z_{0}|>M+1
and that there exists
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

\lim\limits_{\rho\to 0^{+}}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}\left|V(Du(x))-V(z_{0})\right|^{2}=0,

then

, where
\Reg(u)=\{x\in\Omega:\ u\text{ is locally }C^{1,\alpha}(\Omega)\text{ around }x\}
, and
V(z)
is defined in Section
3
.

This theorem leads to the following interesting corollary:

Corollary 2
.

Under the hypotheses and notation of Theorem
1
, the set of points where
u
is locally Lipschitz continuous is a dense open subset of
\Omega
.

In the power case, the common approach in this context is to use the blow-up argument. However, in the general growth case, homogeneity is absent, which is crucial in the blow-up method. Therefore, we employ the so-called
\mathcal{A}
-harmonic approximation, as proved in
[
12
]
. This approach enables us to compare the solutions of our problem with those of the regularized problem in terms of gradient closeness.

A partial regularity result for our type of integrals (i.e., the minimizers are Lipschitz continuous on an open and dense subset of
\Omega
) was previously obtained in
[
32
]
.
Our result is optimal in the following sense: it is not possible to establish regularity outside a negligible set. Thus, the fact that our minimizers are
C^{1,\alpha}
on a dense subset of
\Omega
is optimal in this respect.

2.
Young Functions and Their Properties

Definition
.

A real function
\varphi:[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
is called a
Young function
if it satisfies the following conditions:

(1)

\varphi(0)=0
.

(2)

\varphi
is differentiable, and its derivative
\varphi^{\prime}
is right-continuous, non-decreasing, and satisfies

\varphi^{\prime}(0)=0,\quad\varphi^{\prime}(t)>0\text{ for all }t>0.

(3)

\lim\limits_{t\to+\infty}\varphi^{\prime}(t)=+\infty
.

In this paper, we also assume that Young functions are
C^{2}(0,\infty)\cap C^{1}([0,+\infty))
, which is not a particularly restrictive assumption. It is easy to see from the definition that a Young function is necessarily convex.

Definition
.

A Young function
\varphi
satisfies the
\Delta_{2}
condition if there exists a positive constant
k_{1}>0
such that, for all
t>0
, the following holds:

\varphi(2t)\leq k_{1}\varphi(t),

and we define the
\Delta_{2}
-constant
of
\varphi
as:

\Delta_{2}(\varphi):=\sup\limits_{t>0}\frac{\varphi(2t)}{\varphi(t)}.

If
\varphi
satisfies the
\Delta_{2}
condition, then for any
a>1
, we have the asymptotic equivalence
\varphi(at)\sim\varphi(t)
.

Definition
.

The space
L^{\varphi}
of functions is defined by

L^{\varphi}(\Omega):=\{f:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}\text{ measurable such that }\int_{\Omega}\varphi(|f|)\,dx<+\infty\}.

This space is called the
Orlicz space
associated with the Young function
\varphi
.

The
Orlicz-Sobolev space

W^{1,\varphi}
is the space of functions in
L^{\varphi}
whose weak derivative is also in
L^{\varphi}
.

Moreover, by
W_{0}^{1,\varphi}
we denote the closure in
W^{1,\varphi}
of the space of
C^{\infty}
functions with compact support.

For any Young function
\varphi
, since
\varphi^{\prime}
is non-decreasing, the generalized inverse is well-defined:

(\varphi^{\prime})^{-1}(t):=\sup\{s\in[0,+\infty):\varphi^{\prime}(s)\leq t\},

which allows us to define the complementary function
\varphi^{*}
, also a Young function, implicitly by:

\varphi^{*}(t)^{\prime}=(\varphi^{\prime})^{-1},\quad\varphi^{*}(0)=0.

2.1.
Examples

An example of a family of functions
f
that satisfy hypotheses
(H.0)
through
(H.4)
is given by:

f(z):=\tilde{f}(|z|),\quad\text{where}\quad\tilde{f}(t):=t^{p}\log^{\alpha}(1+t)[\sin^{2}(t)+\cos^{2}(t)t^{\beta}],

with
0\leq\alpha,0\leq\beta<\frac{1}{n}
, and the choice of
\varphi
and
\psi
as:

\varphi:=t^{p}\log^{\alpha}(1+t),\quad\psi:=t^{p+\beta}\log^{\alpha}(1+t),

which reduces to simpler examples if either
\alpha=0
or
\beta=0
.

For more examples of Orlicz spaces, particularly those generated by functions satisfying the
\Delta_{2}
condition, see
[
3
]
.

Technical Lemmas and Definitions

The following lemma is a technical tool used to prove the Caccioppoli estimate (for the proof, see
[
20
]
):

Lemma 3
.

Let
-\infty<r<s<+\infty
, and let
\Xi:[r,s]\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous, non-decreasing function. Then there exist
\tilde{r}\in[r,\frac{2r+s}{3}]
and
\tilde{s}\in[\frac{r+2s}{3},s]
such that:

\frac{\Xi(t)-\Xi(\tilde{r})}{t-\tilde{r}}\leq 3\frac{\Xi(s)-\Xi(r)}{s-r},

and

\frac{\Xi(\tilde{s})-\Xi(t)}{\tilde{s}-t}\leq 3\frac{\Xi(s)-\Xi(r)}{s-r},

for every
t\in(\tilde{r},\tilde{s})
.

In particular, we have:

\frac{s-r}{3}\leq\tilde{s}-\tilde{r}\leq s-r.

The following lemma concerns Young functions satisfying hypothesis
(H.0)
:

Lemma 4
.

Let
h
be a Young function satisfying
(H.0)
. Then the following hold:

(a)

h
satisfies
\Delta_{2}(h)<+\infty
and
\Delta_{2}(h^{*})<+\infty
.

(b)

For all
t>0
, the following inequality holds:

h(1)(t^{p}-1)\leq h(t)\leq h(1)(t^{q}+1),

where
p=p_{1}(h)+1
and
q=q_{1}(h)+1
.

(c)

For all
t>0
, the expression
h^{\prime}(t)t
is equivalent to
h(t)
.

For the proof, see Lemma 3.1 in
[
17
]
.

Definition

(Excess)
.

For any
z\in\mathbb{R}^{nN}
, we define the quantity

V(z):=\sqrt{\frac{\varphi^{\prime}(|z|)}{|z|}}z,

and note that, under our hypotheses, we have

|V(z_{1})-V(z_{2})|^{2}\simeq\varphi_{|z_{1}|}(|z_{1}-z_{2}|).

We also define the excess function

\Phi_{\varphi}(u,x_{0},\rho,z):=\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}|V(Du)-V(z)|^{2}\,dx

and

\Phi_{\varphi}(u,x_{0},\rho):=\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}|V(Du)-V[(Du)_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}]|^{2}\,dx,

where by placing a set as a subscript to a function, we refer to the integral average of the function over the set. That is,

[V(Du)]_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}=\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}V(Du)\,dx.

We immediately observe that

(2)

\Phi_{\varphi}(u,x_{0},\rho,z)\simeq\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int\varphi_{|z|}(|Du-z|)\,dx.

.

Under hypothesis
(H.3)
, assumptions

and

Outline of the Proof of the Main Theorem and Preliminary Lemmas

In
[
5
]
, D. Breit and A. Verde proved that if
u
is a
W^{1,\varphi}
-minimizer of
\mathcal{F}
on
B_{\rho}(x_{0})
, for all
L>0
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
, there exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that if

\Phi_{\varphi}(u,x_{0},\rho)\leq\varepsilon_{0}\quad\text{and}\quad\left|\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}Du\right|\leq\frac{L}{2},

then
u\in C^{1,\alpha}_{\text{loc}}(B_{\rho}(x_{0});\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

We will now replicate their reasoning under the weaker hypothesis of asymptotic
\varphi
-quasiconvexity and without assuming the superquadratic growth behavior of
\varphi
.

Once we have proved the Caccioppoli inequality and used
\mathcal{A}
-harmonic approximation, it is essential to obtain growth estimates for the excess, because the perturbation terms from the Caccioppoli inequality can be controlled using the smallness of the excess.

Using Campanato’s integral characterization of Hölder continuity (see
[
6
]
), we will be able to prove the main result.

Let us begin by proving the following preliminary lemma:

Lemma 5
.

If there exists
z_{0}
with
|z_{0}|>M+1
and
x_{0}
such that

\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}\left|V(Du)-V(z_{0})\right|^{2}\to 0\quad\text{as}\quad\rho\to 0^{+},

then there exists
r_{1}=r_{1}(x_{0},z_{0})
such that for all
r<r_{1}
,

\left|\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}Du\right|>M+1.

Proof.

Let
|z_{0}|=M+1+\varepsilon
. Then, by the definition of the limit, there exists a
r_{1}
(depending on
x_{0}
and
z_{0}
) such that for all
r<r_{1}
, we have, thanks to (
2
), that

\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\varphi_{|z_{0}|}(|Du-z_{0}|)\leq\varphi_{|z_{0}|}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right),\quad\forall r<r_{1}.

Using Jensen’s inequality, we also obtain that

\left|\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}Du-z_{0}\right|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2},\quad\forall r<r_{1},

which gives

\left|\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}Du\right|\geq|z_{0}|-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}=M+1+\varepsilon-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}>M+1,\quad\forall r<r_{1}.

∎

Now, we state Lemma 2.5 from
[
5
]
, which generalizes the extension operator from
[
20
]
to Orlicz spaces. This is a useful tool for us as well:

Lemma 6
.

Let
0<r<s
and
\alpha\geq p
. Then there exists a linear operator

T_{r,s}:W^{1,\varphi}(\Omega)\to W^{1,\varphi}(\Omega),

defined as

T_{r,s}u(x)=\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B_{1}(0)}u(x+\xi(x)y)\,dy,\quad\text{where}\quad\xi(x):=\frac{\max\{0,\min\{|x|-r,s-|x|\}\}}{2},

such that the following properties hold:

(a)

T_{r,s}u=u
on
B_{r}
and outside
\overline{B_{s}}
;

(b)

T_{r,s}u\in u+W_{0}^{1,\varphi}(B_{s}\setminus\overline{B_{r}},\mathbb{R}^{n})
;

(c)

|DT_{r,s}u|\leq cT_{r,s}|Du|
;

(d)

The following estimates hold:

\displaystyle\int_{B_{s}\setminus B_{r}}\varphi(|T_{r,s}u|)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{B_{s}\setminus B_{r}}\varphi(|u|)\,dx,

\displaystyle\int_{B_{s}\setminus B_{r}}\varphi(|DT_{r,s}u|)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{B_{s}\setminus B_{r}}\varphi(|Du|)\,dx,

\displaystyle\int_{B_{s}\setminus B_{r}}\varphi^{\beta}(|T_{r,s}u|)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq c(s-r)^{-n\beta+n+\beta}\left[\sup_{r\leq t\leq s}\frac{\theta(t)-\theta(r)}{t-r}+\sup_{r\leq t\leq s}\frac{\theta(s)-\theta(t)}{s-t}\right],

\displaystyle\int_{B_{s}\setminus B_{r}}\varphi^{\beta}(|DT_{r,s}u|)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq c(s-r)^{-n\beta+n+\beta}\left[\sup_{r\leq t\leq s}\frac{\Theta(t)-\Theta(r)}{t-r}+\sup_{r\leq t\leq s}\frac{\Theta(s)-\Theta(t)}{s-t}\right].

where

\theta(t):=\int_{B_{t}}\varphi(|u|)\,dx,\quad\Theta(t):=\int_{B_{t}}\varphi(|Du|)\,dx.

The final preliminary lemma provides an estimate that will be useful in proving the Caccioppoli inequality for both subquadratic and superquadratic cases:

Lemma 7
.

Let
L
be any positive constant larger than
M
, and let
a
be a real number in the interval
(M,L)
. Then, for any
t>0
, we have:

\psi_{a}(t)\leq K\cdot H(\varphi_{a}(t)),

where
K=K(M,L,\beta,\psi,\varphi)
is a positive constant depending on
L
, and
H(t):=t+t^{\beta}
.

Proof.

We begin with the case
t\leq 1
. In this case, by property
(H.2)
, we have:

\psi_{a}(t)\simeq\psi^{\prime\prime}(a+t)t^{2}\simeq\psi(a+t)\frac{t^{2}}{(a+t)^{2}}\leq\max\limits_{[M,L+1]}\psi\cdot\frac{t^{2}}{(a+t)^{2}}\leq K_{1}\min\limits_{[M,L+1]}\varphi\cdot\frac{t^{2}}{(a+t)^{2}}\leq K_{1}\varphi(a+t)\frac{t^{2}}{(a+t)^{2}}\simeq K_{1}\varphi_{a}(t),

where

K_{1}=\frac{\max\limits_{[M,L+1]}\psi}{\min\limits_{[M,L+1]}\varphi}\in(0,+\infty),

which depends only on
M
,
L
,
\psi
, and
\varphi
.

On the other hand, if
t>1
, we obtain:

\psi_{a}(t)\simeq\psi^{\prime\prime}(a+t)t^{2}\simeq\psi(a+t)\frac{t^{2}}{(a+t)^{2}}\leq\varphi^{\beta}(a+t)\left(\frac{t^{2}}{(a+t)^{2}}\right)^{\beta}\left(1+\frac{a}{t}\right)^{2\beta-2}\leq K_{2}\left[\frac{\varphi(a+t)t^{2}}{(a+t)^{2}}\right]^{\beta}\simeq K_{2}\varphi_{a}(t)^{\beta},

where
K_{2}=(1+L)^{2\beta-2}
.

The thesis follows with
K=\max\{K_{1},K_{2}\}
.
∎

5.
Caccioppoli Inequality

We are now ready to prove the Caccioppoli inequality. This section is dedicated to the Caccioppoli inequality, which is the main tool used to prove partial regularity of solutions for this type of problem:

Lemma 8
.

Let the assumptions
(H.0)-(H.4)
hold for a given
M
. Choose any positive constants
L>M>0
, and let
u\in W^{1,\varphi}(B_{\rho}(x_{0});\mathbb{R}^{N})
be a minimizer of
\mathcal{F}
on the ball
B_{\rho}(x_{0})
contained in
\Omega
. Then, for all
z\in\mathbb{R}^{nN}
with
M<|z|<L+1
, let
q(x)
be an affine function with gradient
z
and
v(x)=u(x)-q(x)
. We have:

(3)

\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B_{\frac{\rho}{2}}}\varphi_{|z|}(|Dv|)\,dx\leq c\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B_{\rho}}\varphi_{|z|}\left(\frac{|v|}{\rho}\right)\,dx+c\left\{\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B_{\rho}}\left[\varphi_{|z|}(|Dv|)+\varphi_{|z|}\left(\frac{|v|}{\rho}\right)\right]\,dx\right\}^{\beta}.

Proof.

Let us assume, for simplicity, that
x_{0}=0
, and let
\frac{\rho}{2}\leq r<s\leq\rho
. Define:

\Xi(t):=\int_{B_{t}}\left[\varphi_{|z|}(|Dv|)+\varphi_{|z|}\left(\left|\frac{v}{\tilde{s}-\tilde{r}}\right|\right)\right]\,dx.

We choose
r\leq\tilde{r}<\tilde{s}\leq s
as in Lemma
3
. Let
\eta
denote a smooth cutoff function with support in
B_{\tilde{s}}
, satisfying
\eta\equiv 1
in
\overline{B_{\tilde{r}}}
and
0\leq\eta\leq 1
, with
|\nabla\eta|\leq\frac{2}{\tilde{s}-\tilde{r}}
on
B_{\rho}
. Using the extension operator from Lemma
6
, we set:

\zeta:=T_{\tilde{r},\tilde{s}}[(1-\eta)v]\quad\text{and}\quad\xi:=v-\zeta.

By
W^{1,\varphi}
-quasiconvexity, we have:

\gamma\int_{B_{\tilde{s}}}\varphi_{|z|}(|D\xi|)\leq\int_{B_{\tilde{s}}}f(z+D\xi)-f(z)=\int_{B_{\tilde{s}}}f(z+D\xi)-f(Du)+f(Du)-f(Du-D\xi)+f(Du-D\xi)-f(z).

Since
f(Du)-f(Du-D\xi)\leq 0
and
Du=z+D\xi+D\zeta
, we obtain:

\int_{B_{\tilde{s}}}f(z+D\xi)-f(Du)+f(Du)-f(Du-D\xi)+f(Du-D\xi)-f(z)\leq\int_{B_{\tilde{s}}}f(z+D\xi)-f(z+D\xi+D\zeta)+\int_{B_{\tilde{s}}}f(z+D\xi)-f(z).

Let us begin reasoning on
\mathcal{I}_{1}
, recalling our growth hypothesis
(H.3)
and also making use of Lemma 3.2 from
[
26
]
. We deduce:

\mathcal{I}_{1}\leq\int_{B_{\tilde{s}}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}|D^{2}f(tz+(1-t)(z+\theta D\zeta))||\theta D\zeta|D\zeta|\,dt\,d\theta,dx\leq\\
\leq\Gamma^{\prime\prime}\int_{B_{\tilde{s}}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}|\psi^{\prime\prime}(|tz+(1-t)(z+\theta D\zeta|))||D\zeta|^{2}\,dt\,d\theta,dx\leq\\
\leq c\int_{B_{\tilde{s}}}\frac{\psi^{\prime}(2|z|+|z+D\zeta|)}{2|z|+|z+D\zeta|}\,dx\leq\\
\leq c\int_{B_{\tilde{s}}}\psi_{|z|}(|D\zeta|)\,dx.

Regarding
\mathcal{I}_{2}
, we deduce:

\mathcal{I}_{2}\leq\int_{B_{\tilde{s}}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}|D^{2}f(t(z+D\xi+\theta D\zeta)+(1-t)z)||D\xi+\theta D\zeta||D\zeta|\,dt\,d\theta\,dx\leq\\
\leq c\int_{B_{\tilde{s}}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}|\psi^{\prime\prime}(t(z+D\xi+\theta D\zeta)+(1-t)z)||D\xi+\theta D\zeta||D\zeta|\,dt\,d\theta\,dx\leq\\
\leq c\int_{B_{\tilde{s}}}\psi^{\prime\prime}(|z|+|D\xi|+|D\zeta|)(|D\xi|+|D\zeta|)|D\zeta|\,dx\leq\\
\leq\int_{B_{\tilde{s}}}\psi^{\prime}_{|z|}(|D\xi|+|D\zeta|)|D\zeta|\,dx\leq\\
\leq c\int_{B_{\tilde{s}}}\psi^{\prime}_{|z|}(|D\xi|)|D\zeta|+c\int_{B_{\tilde{s}}}\psi^{\prime}_{|z|}(|D\zeta|)|D\zeta|\leq\\
\leq c\int_{B_{\tilde{s}}}\psi^{\prime}_{|z|}(|D\xi|)|D\zeta|+c\int_{B_{\tilde{s}}}\psi_{|z|}(|D\zeta|)\,dx.

By combining our estimates, we obtain:

\gamma\int_{B_{\tilde{s}}}\varphi_{|z|}(|D\xi|)\leq c\int_{B_{\tilde{s}}}\psi^{\prime}_{|z|}(|D\xi|)|D\zeta|+c\int_{B_{\tilde{s}}\setminus B_{\tilde{r}}}\psi_{|z|}(|D\zeta|)\,dx.

Applying our anisotropy assumption
(H.3)
and Lemma
7
, we derive the following estimate:

\gamma\int_{B_{\tilde{s}}}\varphi_{|z|}(|D\xi|)\leq c\int_{B_{\tilde{s}}}H[\varphi_{|z|}(|D\zeta|)]\,dx+\\
+c\left[\int_{B_{\tilde{s}}\setminus B_{\tilde{r}}}\varphi_{|z|}(|D\xi|)\,dx+\int_{B_{\tilde{s}}\setminus B_{\tilde{r}}}\varphi_{|z|}^{\beta}(|D\zeta|)\,dx\right]\leq\\
\leq c\left[\int_{B_{\tilde{s}}\setminus B_{\tilde{r}}}\varphi_{|z|}(|D\zeta|)\,dx+\int_{B_{\tilde{s}}\setminus B_{\tilde{r}}}\varphi_{|z|}^{\beta}(|D\zeta|)\,dx+\int_{B_{\tilde{s}}\setminus B_{\tilde{r}}}\varphi_{|z|}(|D\xi|)\right]=\\
=\int_{B_{\tilde{s}}\setminus B_{\tilde{r}}}\varphi_{|z|}(|DT_{\tilde{r},\tilde{s}}[(1-\eta)v]|)\,dx+\int_{B_{\tilde{s}}\setminus B_{\tilde{r}}}\varphi_{|z|}^{\beta}(|DT_{\tilde{r},\tilde{s}}[(1-\eta)v]|)+\\
+c\int_{B_{\tilde{s}}\setminus B_{\tilde{r}}}\phi_{|z|}(|Dv|)\leq\\
\leq c\int_{B_{\tilde{s}}\setminus B_{\tilde{r}}}\varphi_{|z|}(|D\eta||v|+|Dv|)+c(\tilde{s}-\tilde{r})^{-n\beta+n+\beta}\left[\sup\limits_{[\tilde{r},\tilde{s}]}\frac{\Xi(t)-\Xi(\tilde{r})}{t-\tilde{r}}+\sup\limits_{[\tilde{r},\tilde{s}]}\frac{\Xi(\tilde{s})-\Xi(t)}{\tilde{s}-t}\right]^{\beta}+\\
+c\int_{B_{\tilde{s}}\setminus B_{\tilde{r}}}\phi_{|z|}(|Dv|)\,dx\leq\\
\leq c^{\prime}\int_{B_{\tilde{s}}\setminus B_{\tilde{r}}}\phi_{|z|}\left(\left|\frac{v}{\tilde{s}-\tilde{r}}\right|\right)+\varphi_{|z|}(|Dv|)\,dx+\\
+c(s-r)^{-n\beta+n}[\Xi(s)-\Xi(r)]^{\beta}.

Here, we have applied point (d) of Lemma
6
and Lemma
3
.

Now, proceeding from the left-hand side again, we obtain:

\int_{B_{r}}\varphi_{|z|}(|Dv|)\,dx\leq c\int_{B_{\rho}}\varphi_{|z|}\left(\frac{|v|}{\tilde{s}-\tilde{r}}\right)+\\
+c^{\prime}\int_{B_{s}\setminus B_{r}}\varphi_{|z|}(|Dv|)\,dx+c(s-r)^{n}\left[\frac{\Xi(\rho)}{(s-r)^{n}}\right]^{\beta}.

Using the hole-filling method, we get:

\int_{B_{r}}\varphi_{|z|}(|Dv|)\,dx\leq\frac{c^{\prime}}{1+c^{\prime}}\int_{B_{s}}\varphi_{|z|}(|Dv|)+\\
+c(s-r)^{n}\left[(s-r)^{-n}\int_{B_{\rho}}\varphi_{|z|}(|Dv|)+\varphi_{|z|}\left(\frac{|v|}{\tilde{s}-\tilde{r}}\right)\right]^{\beta}+\\
+c\int_{B_{\rho}}\varphi_{|z|}\left(\frac{|v|}{s-r}\right)\,dx.

A well-known lemma by Giaquinta (see
[
22
]
, Chapter V, Lemma 3.1) concludes the proof.

∎

6.
\mathcal{A}
-harmonicity

Let us consider a bilinear form
\mathcal{A}
on
\mathbb{R}^{nN}
and assume that the upper bound

(4)

|\mathcal{A}|\leq\Lambda

holds for some constant
\Lambda>0
, and that the Legendre-Hadamard condition

(5)

\mathcal{A}(yx^{T},yx^{T})\geq\lambda|x|^{2}|y|^{2}\quad\text{for all}\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

is satisfied with ellipticity constant
\lambda>0
. We say that
h\in W^{1,1}_{loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N})
is
\mathcal{A}
-harmonic on
\Omega
if

\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{A}(Dh,D\phi)\,dx=0

holds for all smooth
\phi:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
with compact support in
\Omega
.

The following lemma guarantees that for large
z
, the bilinear form
\mathcal{A}=D^{2}f(z)
satisfies the Legendre-Hadamard condition.

Lemma 9
.

Let
f
satisfy
(H.0)
and
(H.2^{\prime})
for a given
M>0
. Then, for any
z
such that
|z|>M
, the bilinear form
\mathcal{A}=D^{2}f(z)
satisfies the Legendre-Hadamard condition:

\mathcal{A}(\zeta x^{T},\zeta x^{T})\geq\lambda|x|^{2}|\zeta|^{2}\quad\text{for all}\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\quad\text{and}\quad\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

with ellipticity constant
\lambda=2\gamma
.

Proof.

Let
u
be the affine function
u(x)=zx
with
z
such that
|z|>M
.
W^{1,\varphi}
-quasiconvexity in
z
ensures that
u
is a
W^{1,\varphi}
-minimizer of the functional
\mathcal{F}
induced by
f
, and the function

G(t)=G_{\Phi}(t):=\mathcal{F}_{B_{1}}(u+t\Phi)-\gamma\int_{B_{1}}\varphi_{1+|z|}(|tD\Phi|)\,dx

has a minimum at
t=0
for any
\phi\in W_{0}^{1,\varphi}(B_{1},\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Following the same steps as in (
[
24
]
, Prop. 5.2), from
G_{\Phi}^{\prime}(0)=0
and
G_{\Phi}^{\prime\prime}(0)\geq 0
, the Legendre-Hadamard condition follows.

In fact, from
G^{\prime\prime}(0)\geq 0
, we obtain:

(6)

\int_{B_{1}}\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial z_{k}^{\alpha}\partial z_{j}^{\beta}}(z_{0})D_{k}\phi^{\alpha}D_{j}\phi^{\beta}\,dx\geq 2\gamma\int_{B_{1}}|D\phi|^{2}\,dx

for every
\phi\in C_{c}^{1}(B_{1},\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

Let us choose
\phi=\nu+i\mu
and write (
6
) separately for
\nu
and
\mu
, obtaining:

(7)

\int_{B_{1}}\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial z_{k}^{\alpha}\partial z_{j}^{\beta}}(z_{0})D_{k}\nu^{\alpha}D_{j}\nu^{\beta}\,dx\geq 2\gamma\int_{B_{1}}|D\nu|^{2}\,dx,

and

(8)

\int_{B_{1}}\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial z_{k}^{\alpha}\partial z_{j}^{\beta}}(z_{0})D_{k}\mu^{\alpha}D_{j}\mu^{\beta}\,dx\geq 2\gamma\int_{B_{1}}|D\mu|^{2}\,dx.

Adding these inequalities, we obtain:

(9)

\int_{B_{1}}\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial z_{k}^{\alpha}\partial z_{j}^{\beta}}(z_{0})\left[D_{k}\nu^{\alpha}D_{j}\nu^{\beta}+D_{k}\mu^{\alpha}D_{j}\mu^{\beta}\right]\,dx\geq 2\gamma\int_{B_{1}}(|D\nu|^{2}+|D\mu|^{2})\,dx.

Thus, we get:

\text{Re}\int_{B_{1}}\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial z_{k}^{\alpha}\partial z_{j}^{\beta}}(z_{0})D_{k}\phi^{\alpha}D_{j}\overline{\phi}^{\beta}\,dx\geq 2\gamma\int_{B_{1}}|D\phi|^{2}\,dx.

Now, let us consider any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
, and
\varPsi(x)\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{1},\mathbb{R})
, and define
\phi
as

\phi(x)=\eta e^{i\tau(\xi\cdot x)}\varPsi(x).

Since
\phi^{\alpha}(x)=\eta^{\alpha}\varPsi(x)e^{i\tau\xi\cdot x}
, we obtain

\int_{B_{1}}\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial z_{k}^{\alpha}\partial z_{j}^{\beta}}(z_{0})\eta^{\alpha}\eta^{\beta}[\tau^{2}\xi_{k}\xi_{j}\varPsi^{2}+D_{k}\varPsi D_{j}\varPsi]\,dx\geq 2\gamma|\eta|^{2}\int_{B_{1}}(|D\varPsi|^{2}+\tau^{2}|\xi|^{2}|\varPsi(x)|^{2})\,dx.

Dividing by
\tau^{2}
and letting
\tau\to\infty
, we deduce:

\int_{B_{1}}\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial z_{k}^{\alpha}\partial z_{j}^{\beta}}(z_{0})\xi_{k}\xi_{j}\eta^{\alpha}\eta^{\beta}\varPsi^{2}(x)\,dx\geq 2\gamma|\eta|^{2}|\xi|^{2}\int_{B_{1}}\varPsi^{2}(x)\,dx.

Since this holds for all
\varPsi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{1},\mathbb{R})
, the proposition is proved.

∎

Remark 2
.

If
f\in C^{2}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{nN})
, for each
L>0
, there exists a modulus of continuity
\omega_{L}:[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
satisfying
\lim_{z\to 0}\omega_{L}(z)=0
such that for all
z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{nN}
, we have:

|z_{1}|\leq L,\quad|z_{2}|\leq L+1\quad\Rightarrow\quad|D^{2}f(z_{1})-D^{2}f(z_{2})|\leq\omega_{L}(|z_{1}-z_{2}|^{2}).

Moreover,
\omega_{L}
can be chosen such that the following properties hold:

(1)

\omega_{L}
is non-decreasing,

(2)

\omega_{L}^{2}
is concave,

(3)

\omega_{L}^{2}(z)\geq z
for all
z\geq 0
.

This lemma essentially proves that if
Du
is close to
z
, subtracting an affine function with gradient
z
from
u
results in a function that is "almost"
D^{2}f(z)
-harmonic.

Lemma 10
.

Let
f
satisfy conditions
(H.0)
through
(H.4)
for a given
M>0
. Let
L>M>0
and take
u\in W^{1,\varphi}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{N})
to be a
W^{1,\varphi}
-minimizer of
\mathcal{F}
on some ball
B_{\rho}(x_{0})
. Then, for all
z
such that
M<|z|\leq L
and
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{\rho}(x_{0}))
, we have the following estimate:

(10)

\left|\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}D^{2}f(z)(Du-z,D\phi)\,dx\right|\leq c\sqrt{\Phi_{\varphi}}\omega_{L}(\Phi_{\varphi})\sup\limits_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}|D\phi|.

where
\Phi_{\varphi}:=\Phi_{\varphi}(u,x_{0},\rho,z)
, the constant
c
depends only on
n
,
N
,
\Gamma^{\prime}
,
\Gamma^{\prime\prime}
,
L
, and
\omega_{L}
is the modulus of continuity from Remark
2
(see also
[
33
]
).

Proof.

Let
v(x):=u(x)-zx
. The Euler equation of
\mathcal{F}
gives:

\left|\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B_{\rho}}D^{2}f(z)(Dv,D\phi)\,dx\right|=\left|\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B_{\rho}}\left[D^{2}f(z)(Dv,D\phi)+Df(z)D\phi-Df(Du)D\phi\right]\,dx\right|.

If
|Dv|\leq 1
, we have:

|D^{2}f(z)(Dv,D\phi)+Df(z)D\phi-Df(Du)D\phi|\leq\int_{0}^{1}\left|D^{2}f(z)-D^{2}f(z+tDv)\right|\,dt|Dv|\|D\phi\|_{\infty}\leq\omega_{L}(|Dv|^{2})|Dv|\|D\phi\|_{\infty}.

Since
\omega_{L}
is a modulus of continuity, we get:

\leq c\omega_{L}(\varphi_{|z|}(|Dv|))\varphi_{|z|}(|Dv|)\|D\phi\|_{\infty},

where we used that
|Dv|^{2}\leq\inf_{t\in[M,L+1]}\varphi^{\prime\prime}(t)|Dv|^{2}\leq\varphi^{\prime\prime}(|z|+|Dv|)|Dv|^{2}\simeq\varphi_{|z|}(|Dv|)
from
(H.1)
.
If, instead,
|Dv|>1
, then since
M\leq|z|\leq L
, the condition
(H.1)
implies
\psi^{\prime}(t)>ct
for
t>1
and
(H.3)
holds, so we obtain:

|D^{2}f(z)(Dv,D\phi)+Df(z)D\phi-Df(Du)D\phi|\leq c\left(|Dv|+|Dv|\int_{0}^{1}D^{2}f(|z+t(Du-z)|)\,dt\right)\|D\phi\|_{\infty}.

This can be bounded by:

\leq c\left(|Dv|+\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\psi^{\prime}(|z+t(Du-z)|)}{|z+t(Du-z)|}\,dt\right)\|D\phi\|_{\infty}\leq c\left[\psi^{\prime}(|Dv|)+\frac{\psi^{\prime}(2|z|)}{|z|}+\frac{\psi^{\prime}(2|Du-z|)}{2|Du-z|}\right]\|D\phi\|_{\infty}.

We can bound this further:

\leq c\left[\psi^{\prime}(|Dv|)+1\right]\|D\phi\|_{\infty}\leq c\varphi(|Dv|)\|D\phi\|_{\infty}\leq c\varphi_{|z|}(|Dv|)\|D\phi\|_{\infty}.

Now, since
\omega_{L}^{2}(t)\geq t
for
t\geq 0
, we get:

\left|\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B_{\rho}}D^{2}f(z)(Dv,D\phi)\,dx\right|\leq c\|D\phi\|_{\infty}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B_{\rho}}\omega_{L}(\varphi_{|z|}(|Dv|))\sqrt{\varphi_{|z|}(|Dv|)}\,dx.

Since
\omega_{L}
is non-decreasing, by using the Cauchy-Schwarz and Jensen inequalities, we obtain:

\left|\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B_{\rho}}D^{2}f(z)(Dv,D\phi)\,dx\right|\leq c\sqrt{\Phi_{\varphi}}\omega_{L}(\Phi_{\varphi})\sup_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}|D\phi|,

which concludes the proof.
∎

7.
Excess Decay Estimate

Proposition 12
.

Let
z_{0}
be such that
|z_{0}|>M+1
and
x_{0}
be such that:

\lim_{\rho\to 0}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}\left|V(Du(x))-V(z_{0})\right|^{2}\,dx=0.

Then:

\Phi_{p}(u,x_{0},\rho)\to 0\quad\text{as}\quad\rho\to 0.

Proof.

Let
(Du)_{\rho}:=\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}|Du|
. By the triangle inequality, we have:

\Phi_{p}(u,x_{0},\rho)=\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}|V(Du(x))-V((Du)_{\rho})|^{2}\,dx\leq c\left[\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}\left|V(Du(x))-V(z_{0})\right|^{2}\,dx+\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}\left|V(Du_{\rho})-V(z_{0})\right|^{2}\,dx\right].

The first summand tends to 0 by hypothesis. The second summand is equivalent to:

\varphi_{|z_{0}|}\left(\left|\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}Du(x)-z_{0}\,dx\right|\right).

By Jensen’s inequality, we obtain:

\varphi_{|z_{0}|}\left(\left|\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}Du(x)-z_{0}\,dx\right|\right)\leq\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}\varphi_{|z_{0}|}\left(|Du(x)-z_{0}|\right)\,dx.

The second member of this inequality is equivalent to:

\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}\left|V(Du(x))-V(z_{0})\right|^{2},

which vanishes by hypothesis.
∎

Finally, we can prove the excess decay:

Proposition 13
.

Let us assume
f
,
\varphi
, and
\psi
satisfy hypotheses
(H.0)
through
(H.4)
for given
p_{1},q_{1}
, and
M

L>M+1>0
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
, and
z_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{nN}
such that
L>|z_{0}|>M+1
. Then, there exist constants
\varepsilon_{0}>0
,
\theta\in(0,1)
, and a radius
\rho^{*}>0
depending on
n,N,L,p_{1},q_{1},\Gamma,\alpha,\gamma,x_{0},z_{0}
, and
\Lambda_{L}:=\max_{B_{L+2}}|D^{2}f|
, and with
\varepsilon_{0}
also depending on
\omega_{L}
, such that the following result holds.

Let us consider
u

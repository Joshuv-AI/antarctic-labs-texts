Abstract
. It is known that the identity component of the automorphism group of a projective algebraic variety is an algebraic group. This is not true in general for quasi-projective varieties. In this note we address the question: given an affine algebraic surface Y Y , as to when the identity component Aut ∘ ⁡ ( Y ) \operatorname{Aut}^{\circ}(Y) of the automorphism group Aut ⁡ ( Y ) \operatorname{Aut}(Y) is an algebraic group? We show that this occurs if and only if Y Y admits no effective action of the additive group 𝔾 a {\mathbb{G}}_{\rm a} of the field. In the latter case, Aut ∘ ⁡ ( Y ) \operatorname{Aut}^{\circ}(Y) is an algebraic torus of rank ≤ 2 \leq 2 .

2.
NC-pairs

2.1.
Birational transformations of NC-pairs

Definition 2.1.1
.

Let
X
be a normal projective surface over
{\mathbb{K}}

and
D
be a reduced effective divisor on
X
.
The pair
(X,D)
is called an
NC
-pair
if
D

is a normal crossing divisor (i.e., the only singularities of

D
are nodes) contained in the smooth locus of
X
.
An NC-pair
(X,D)
is called an
SNC-pair
if
D

is a simple normal crossing divisor, i.e., each component
of
D
is smooth and any two components of
D

either are disjoint or intersect at a single point.
An NC-pair
(X,D)
is called
minimal

if the contraction of a
(-1)
-curve on
X
that is
a component of
D
does not lead to a new NC-pair.

Clearly, an NC-pair
(X,D)
is not an SNC-pair
if and only if
D
has either a nodal component
or a pair of components with more than one point
in common. As an example, one can consider the pair

({\mathbb{P}}^{2},C)
, where
C\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
is a nodal cubic curve.
Any NC-pair is dominated by an SNC-pair
via a birational morphism.
For instance, in the example above such a domination
is obtained after blowing
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
up in the node of
C

and performing another blowup in a node of the resulting curve,
cf. Example
3.5.2
.

Definition 2.1.2
.

Let
(X,D)
and
(X^{\prime},D^{\prime})
be NC-pairs. We denote by

(1)

\operatorname{Bir}((X,D),(X^{\prime},D^{\prime}))
the set of
birational transformations

X\dashrightarrow X^{\prime}
that restrict
to biregular isomorphisms of the complements

X\setminus\operatorname{supp}D\xlongrightarrow{\simeq}X^{\prime}\setminus\operatorname{supp}D^{\prime}
;

(2)

\operatorname{Bir}(X,D)
the group
\operatorname{Bir}((X,D),(X,D))
;

(3)

\operatorname{Aut}(X,D)
the group of biregular
automorphisms of
X
that preserve
D
;

(4)

\operatorname{Aut}^{\natural}(X,D)

the subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}(X,D)
of automorphisms
preserving every component and
every node of
D
;

(5)

\operatorname{Aut}^{\circ}(X,D)
the identity component
of
\operatorname{Aut}(X,D)
.

Remark 2.1.3
.

The group
\operatorname{Bir}(X,D)
is naturally isomorphic
to the automorphism
group
\operatorname{Aut}(Y)
of the complement

Y=X\setminus\operatorname{supp}D
.
Indeed, for
g\in\operatorname{Bir}(X,D)
the restriction
g|_{Y}

is an automorphism of
Y
, and any

f\in\operatorname{Aut}(Y)

extends to a unique birational transformation

(X,D)\dasharrow(X,D)
.

Thus, the group
\operatorname{Bir}(X,D)=\operatorname{Aut}(Y)

does not depend on the choice of
an NC-completion
(X,D)

of
Y=X\setminus\operatorname{supp}D
.
However, in general,
the group
\operatorname{Aut}(X,D)
depends on the chosen
NC-completion. For instance,
for the completion
{\mathbb{A}}^{2}\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{2}

of the affine plane we have

\operatorname{Aut}({\mathbb{P}}^{2},{\mathbb{P}}^{1})\simeq\operatorname{Aff}({\mathbb{A}}^{2})
, whereas
for the completion
{\mathbb{A}}^{2}\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
we have

\operatorname{Aut}({\mathbb{P}}^{1}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1},{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\times\{\infty\}\cup\{\infty\}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1})\cong(\operatorname{Aff}({\mathbb{A}}^{1}))^{2}\rtimes{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}.

2.2.
Inner transformations

Definition 2.2.1
.

A blowup of an NC-pair
(X,D)
with center
x\in\operatorname{supp}D

gives again an NC-pair. Both pairs provide
NC-completions of
Y=X\setminus\operatorname{supp}D
.
Such a blowup is called
inner

if
x
is a node of
D
and
outer
otherwise.
We say that a blowdown of a component of
D
,
producing a new NC-pair, is
inner

(resp.
outer
) if the inverse blowup
is inner (resp. outer).

Definition 2.2.2
.

A birational transformation

(X,D)\dashrightarrow(X^{\prime},D^{\prime})
between two NC-pairs
is called
inner

if it can be decomposed into a sequence of inner blowups,
inner blowdowns and isomorphisms of NC-pairs.
The subset of inner birational transformations
forms a subgroup
\operatorname{Inn}(X,D)\subset\operatorname{Bir}(X,D)=\operatorname{Aut}(Y)
.

Lemma 2.2.3
.

We have

\operatorname{Aut}^{\circ}(X,D)\subset\operatorname{Aut}^{\natural}(X,D)\subset\operatorname{Aut}(X,D)\subset\operatorname{Inn}(X,D)\subset\operatorname{Bir}(X,D)=\operatorname{Aut}(Y).

Proof.

This is immediate from our definitions.
∎

Lemma 2.2.4
.

\operatorname{Aut}^{\circ}(X,D)=(\operatorname{Aut}^{\natural}(X,D))^{\circ}

is a (connected) algebraic group.

Proof.

It is well known that
\operatorname{Aut}^{\circ}(X)

is a connected algebraic group, see e.g.
[
35
,
36
,
42
]
,
and so is its closed connected subgroup
\operatorname{Aut}^{\circ}(X,D)
.
∎

Lemma 2.2.5
.

Every
\phi\in\operatorname{Inn}(X,D)
can be written as

(1)

\phi=\alpha\sigma_{n}^{\pm 1}\cdots\sigma_{1}^{\pm 1}={\sigma^{\prime}}_{n}^{\pm 1}\cdots{\sigma^{\prime}}_{1}^{\pm 1}\beta

where
\alpha,\beta\in\operatorname{Aut}(X,D)
and the

\sigma_{i},\sigma_{i}^{\prime}
are inner blowdowns.

Proof.

Consider a commutative diagram

(X_{1},D_{1})
(X^{\prime}_{1},D^{\prime}_{1})
(X_{2},D_{2})
(X^{\prime}_{2},D^{\prime}_{2})
\sigma^{\pm 1}
\alpha_{1}
\simeq
{\sigma^{\prime}}^{\pm 1}
\alpha_{2}
\simeq

where
\sigma
and
\sigma^{\prime}
are inner blowdowns.
Given a pair
(\sigma^{\prime},\alpha_{1})
there exists
a unique pair
(\sigma,\alpha_{2})

fitting in the diagram, and vice versa.
Now the claim follows.
∎

Lemma 2.2.6
.

Every inner birational transformation

\phi\colon(X,D)\dashrightarrow(X^{\prime},D^{\prime})

of NC-pairs
induces an isomorphism

\phi_{*}\colon\operatorname{Aut}^{\natural}(X,D)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\longrightarrow}}\operatorname{Aut}^{\natural}(X^{\prime},D^{\prime}),\quad\alpha\mapsto\phi\circ\alpha\circ\phi^{-1}.

In particular,
\operatorname{Aut}^{\natural}(X,D)
is a normal
subgroup of
\operatorname{Inn}(X,D)
.

Proof.

The statement is definitely
true for inner blowups and blowdowns.
Hence, it holds in general.
∎

Remark 2.2.7
.

The group
\operatorname{Aut}^{\natural}(X,D)
is the kernel
of the natural homomorphism

\operatorname{Aut}(X,D)\to\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma(D))
, where
\Gamma(D)

is the dual graph of
D
, see Definition
3.5.1
.

Proposition 2.2.8
.

The group
\operatorname{Inn}(X,D)/\operatorname{Aut}^{\natural}(X,D)
is countable.

Proof.

Since
\operatorname{Aut}(X,D)/\operatorname{Aut}^{\natural}(X,D)

is a finite group, it suffices to show that
the collection
\operatorname{Inn}(X,D)/\operatorname{Aut}(X,D)

of left cosets is countable.

By Lemma
2.2.5
any left coset in
\operatorname{Inn}(X,D)/\operatorname{Aut}(X,D)

has the form
\psi\cdot\operatorname{Aut}(X,D)
for some

\psi={\sigma^{\prime}}_{n}^{\pm 1}\cdots{\sigma^{\prime}}_{1}^{\pm 1}\in\operatorname{Inn}(X,D)
.
These cosets form a countable set.
∎

2.3.
The ind-structure on
\operatorname{Bir}(X,D)

Let an NC-pair
(X,D)
be a completion of
a normal affine surface

Y=X\setminus\operatorname{supp}D
.
By Goodman’s criterion of affiness for surfaces

[
25
, Theorem 2]

we can find an ample divisor
H\subset X
such that

\operatorname{supp}H=\operatorname{supp}D
. This defines
an ample polarization on
X
.
Replacing
H
by its suitable
high multiple, the very ample
linear system
|H|
defines an
embedding
\phi_{|H|}\colon X\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{n}

that sends the divisor
H

to a hyperplane section of
X
.
The restriction of
\phi_{|H|}
to
Y

yields a proper embedding

Y=X\setminus\operatorname{supp}H\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{A}}^{n}={\mathbb{P}}^{n}\setminus{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}
.

Definition 2.3.1

(cf. e.g.
[
7
, (1.1)]
)
.

For
\Phi\in\operatorname{Bir}(X,D)
we define its degree as

\deg\Phi=H\cdot\Phi^{*}(H)
.
For
\Phi\in\operatorname{Aut}(Y)
we understand
\deg\Phi

as the degree of the natural extension
\hat{\Phi}\in\operatorname{Bir}(X,D)
.

Remarks 2.3.2
.

1.
The degree function
\deg\Phi
on
\operatorname{Bir}(X,D)

coincides
with the usual degree of
\Phi|_{Y}\in\operatorname{Aut}(Y)
viewed as
an automorphism of the closed subvariety

Y\subset{\mathbb{A}}^{n}
.
Indeed, let
\Phi
be written in homogeneous coordinates
on
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
as

\Phi=(p_{0}\colon\ldots\colon p_{n})|_{X}

where the
p_{i}
are homogeneous polynomials in
n+1

variables of the same degree
d
,
that do not belong simultaneously to the homogeneous
ideal of
X
.
Then
\deg\Phi=\min\{d\}

where the minimum is taken over all such presentations.

2. We have
\operatorname{Bir}(X,D)=\varinjlim\operatorname{Bir}(X,D)_{\leq d}
where

\operatorname{Bir}(X,D)_{\leq d}=\big\{\Phi\in\operatorname{Bir}(X,D)\,|\,\max\{\deg\Phi,\,\deg\Phi^{-1}\}\leq d\big\}

is an affine algebraic variety and

\operatorname{Bir}(X,D)_{\leq d}\subset\operatorname{Bir}(X,D)_{\leq d+1}

is a closed embedding.
This yields an affine ind-structure on

\operatorname{Bir}(X,D)=\operatorname{Aut}(Y)
.
It is easily seen that this ind-structure coincides
with the usual ind-structure on
\operatorname{Aut}(Y)
defined via
the induced closed embedding
Y\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{A}}^{n}
,
see e.g.
[
21
, Section 5.1]
or
[
31
, Section 2.1]
.
On the other hand, in the case of
a rational surface
X
the group
\operatorname{Bir}(X)

carries no natural ind-structure, see
[
4
]
.

3. The very ample linear system
|H|

is stable under
the action of
\operatorname{Aut}^{\natural}(X,D)
on
(X,D)
.
Hence the embedding

\phi_{|H|}\colon(X,\operatorname{supp}D)=(X,\operatorname{supp}H)\hookrightarrow({\mathbb{P}}^{n},{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1})

induces an embedding of

\operatorname{Aut}^{\natural}(X,D)

onto a closed subgroup of

\operatorname{Aut}({\mathbb{P}}^{n},{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1})
.
Therefore,
\operatorname{Aut}^{\natural}(X,D)

is an affine algebraic group.

Recall the following definitions; see e.g.
[
21
]
.
One says that
a subgroup
G\subset\operatorname{Bir}(X,D)
is connected
in the Zariski ind-topology if every
element
g\in G
belongs to a connected algebraic
subset of
G

that contains the identity.
The identity component
G^{\circ}
of a subgroup

G\subset\operatorname{Bir}(X,D)
is the maximal connected
subgroup of
G
.

Proposition 2.3.3
.

We have
\operatorname{Inn}^{\circ}(X,D)=\operatorname{Aut}^{\circ}(X,D)
.
In particular,
\operatorname{Inn}^{\circ}(X,D)
is
a connected affine algebraic group.

Proof.

Assume first that
the ground field
{\mathbb{K}}
is uncountable.
Recall that
\operatorname{Aut}^{\natural}(X,D)
is
a normal affine algebraic subgroup
of
\operatorname{Inn}(X,D)
of countable index closed
in
\operatorname{Bir}(X,D)
, see Lemma
2.2.6

and Proposition
2.2.8
.
Therefore,
\operatorname{Inn}(X,D)

is a countable union
of mutually disjoint closed algebraic subvarieties
of
\operatorname{Bir}(X,D)
of the form

h_{k}\circ\operatorname{Aut}^{\natural}(X,D)

with
h_{k}\in\operatorname{Inn}(X,D)
for
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Let
A
be an irreducible algebraic subvariety of

\operatorname{Bir}(X,D)=\operatorname{Aut}(Y)
of positive dimension contained in

\operatorname{Inn}(X,D)
. Since the set of
{\mathbb{K}}
-points of
A

is uncountable,

A
is actually contained in
one of the left cosets
h_{k}\circ\operatorname{Aut}^{\natural}(X,D)
,
see
[
21
, Lemma 1.3.1]
.
If
{\mathrm{id}}_{(X,D)}\in A
then
A\subset(\operatorname{Aut}^{\natural}(X,D))^{\circ}=\operatorname{Aut}^{\circ}(X,D)
. Now
the equality
\operatorname{Inn}^{\circ}(X,D)=\operatorname{Aut}^{\circ}(X,D)
follows.

Suppose now that
{\mathbb{K}}
is countable.
We embed
{\mathbb{K}}
in
the uncountable algebraically closed field
{\mathbb{L}}
; for instance,
one can take for
{\mathbb{L}}
the algebraic closure of the function field

{\mathbb{K}}(x_{t}\,|\,t\in{\mathbb{R}})
. Let
{\rm Gal}={\rm Gal}({\mathbb{L}}/{\mathbb{K}})

stand for the Galois group
of the field extension

{\mathbb{K}}\subset{\mathbb{L}}
. Then
{\mathbb{K}}={\mathbb{L}}^{\rm Gal}
. Moreover,
for any algebraic
{\mathbb{K}}
-variety
Z
we have

Z=Z({\mathbb{L}})^{\rm Gal}
and
Z({\mathbb{L}})
is irreducible
if and only if
Z
is,
see
[
21
, Sec. 1.11]
and

[
45
,
Lemma 037K
]
.
In particular,

(X,D)=(X({\mathbb{L}}),D({\mathbb{L}}))^{\rm Gal}
. Furthermore,

\operatorname{Aut}^{\natural}(X({\mathbb{L}}),D({\mathbb{L}}))^{\rm Gal}=(\operatorname{Aut}^{\natural}(X,D)({\mathbb{L}}))^{\rm Gal}=\operatorname{Aut}^{\natural}(X,D),

and likewise for
\operatorname{Aut}^{\circ}(X,D)=(\operatorname{Aut}^{\natural}(X,D))^{\circ}
.
Since
\operatorname{Inn}(X,D)=\bigcup_{k}h_{k}\circ\operatorname{Aut}^{\natural}(X,D)

we have
\operatorname{Inn}(X({\mathbb{L}}),D({\mathbb{L}}))^{\rm Gal}=\operatorname{Inn}(X,D)
.

Applying the first part of the proof we get

\operatorname{Inn}^{\circ}(X({\mathbb{L}}),D({\mathbb{L}}))=\operatorname{Aut}^{\circ}(X({\mathbb{L}}),D({\mathbb{L}}))
.
Passing to the
{\rm Gal}
-fixed point loci in the latter equality
we obtain
\operatorname{Inn}^{\circ}(X,D)=\operatorname{Aut}^{\circ}(X,D)
.
∎

3.
Birational geometry
of weighted graphs

The birational geometry of NC-pairs has its
combinatorial counterpart, namely, the
birational geometry of weighted graphs,
see the references in the Introduction.

3.1.
Weighted graphs

Definition 3.1.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a (nonempty) finite
weighted graph with the set of vertices

\operatorname{Vert}(\Gamma)

and the set of edges
\operatorname{Edge}(\Gamma)
,
where vertices are weighted by integer numbers,
and let
\operatorname{Ratio}(\Gamma)\subseteq\operatorname{Vert}(\Gamma)

be a distinguished subset of vertices
which we call
rational vertices
.
A rational vertex of weight
-1

(resp. of weight
0
) is called
(
-1
)
-vertex

(resp. (
0
)
-vertex
).
The graph
\Gamma
may be disconnected
and may contain loops,
cycles and multiple edges, that is, several
edges joining the same pair of vertices.

Notation 3.1.2
.

Following
[
14
]

we let
[[a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}]]
be
a linear weighted graph
with
n
ordered
vertices of weights
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}
.
We also let
((a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}))
be
a circular weighted graph
with
n
cyclically ordered
vertices of weights
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}
.

Definition 3.1.3
.

•

Given a weighted graph
\Gamma
,
the
degree

\deg_{\Gamma}(v)
of a vertex

v\in\operatorname{Vert}(\Gamma)
is the
number of incident edges of
\Gamma
at
v
,
where every loop
[v,v]
is counted
twice. We simply write
\deg(v)

when the graph

\Gamma
has been fixed.

•

A rational vertex
v
of
\Gamma

is called a
tip
or an

end vertex
if
\deg(v)=1

and

at most linear vertex
if
\deg(v)\leq 2

and no loop of
\Gamma
is incident with
v
.
We let
\operatorname{Tip}(\Gamma)
be the set
of tips of
\Gamma
.

•

A vertex
v
is called
a
branch vertex

if either
v
is non-rational or
\deg(v)\geq 3
.
We let
{\mathrm{Br}}(\Gamma)
be the set of
all branch vertices of
\Gamma
.
Thus,
\operatorname{Vert}(\Gamma)\setminus\operatorname{Ratio}(\Gamma)\subseteq{\mathrm{Br}}(\Gamma)
.

•

For a proper subset
V
of
\operatorname{Vert}(\Gamma)
we
let
\Gamma\ominus V

be the subgraph of
\Gamma
obtained by deleting

V
and all its incident edges,
including the incident loops.

•

Given a vertex
v\in\Gamma
,
the connected components of

\Gamma\ominus\{v\}
linked to
v

are the
branches

of
\Gamma
at
v
. A branch linked to
v

via exactly one edge
is called
simple
.

Definition 3.1.4
.

•

In the case where
V={\mathrm{Br}}(\Gamma)
is the set of
branch vertices of
\Gamma
,
the connected
components of
\Gamma\ominus{\mathrm{Br}}(\Gamma)

are called

segments
.
A connected graph
with no branch vertex coincides with its only segment.

•

A segment can be either linear or circular.
A circular segment
is a cycle in
\Gamma
which contains
no branch vertex; it is a connected component
of
\Gamma
.
A linear segment can consist of
a single vertex. For example, this is
the case for a vertex of degree 2 linked
to a branch point
by two edges.

•

A linear segment
L
is called

extremal
if
L
contains one or two
tips of

\Gamma
and

inner
if
L
contains no
tip of
\Gamma
.

Definition 3.1.5
.

Let
\Gamma
be a weighted graph
with vertices
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}
.
The
intersection form

I(\Gamma)

is a bilinear form on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
given by
the symmetric square matrix
A(\Gamma)=(a_{i,j})
,
where
a_{i,i}
is the weight of
v_{i}
and for

j\neq i
,
a_{i,j}
equals the number of
edges joining
v_{i}
and
v_{j}
.
The
discriminant

{\rm discr}(\Gamma)

is the determinant
\det(-A(\Gamma))
.
We let
i_{+}(\Gamma),i_{-}(\Gamma)
and

i_{0}(\Gamma)
be the inertia indices of
the associate quadratic form
I(\Gamma)
.

Definition 3.1.6
.

Let
[v_{1},v_{2}]
(where possibly
v_{1}=v_{2}
)
be an edge of

\Gamma
.
The
inner blowup
in
[v_{1},v_{2}]
consists
in adding a new (
-1
)-vertex
v
,
replacing the edge
[v_{1},v_{2}]
by two new edges

[v_{1},v]
and
[v,v_{2}]
and
decreasing the weights of
v_{1}
and
v_{2}

by
1
if
v_{1}\neq v_{2}
and by
4
if

v_{1}=v_{2}
(i.e.
[v_{1},v_{2}]
is a loop).

The
outer blowup
at
a vertex
v
in
\Gamma
consists in
introducing a new (
-1
)-vertex

v^{\prime}
along with a new edge

[v,v^{\prime}]
and decreasing
the weight of
v
by
1
.

The modifications inverse to inner and
outer blowups are called

inner
and
outer blowdowns
, respectively.

Remark 3.1.7
.

The definition of inner birational transformations
of weighted graphs in

[
14
, Definitions 2.3 and 2.8]

has a broader meaning. Indeed,
the inverse of an outer blowup is
considered in
loc.cit.
to be inner.

Proposition 3.1.8

(cf.
[
38
, Proposition 1.1]
,

[
44
, [Proposition 1.14]
,
[
14
, Lemma 4.6]
)
.

A blowup of a weighted graph
\Gamma
adds
1
to

i_{-}(\Gamma)
, while
i_{+}(\Gamma)
and

i_{0}(\Gamma)
remain unchanged.

Proof.

Letting
\operatorname{Vert}(\Gamma)=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}

consider the
{\mathbb{R}}
-vector space
V=\bigoplus_{i}{\mathbb{R}}v_{i}

with the inner product given by
the intersection form
I(\Gamma)
.
The symmetric matrix
M_{n}
of
I(\Gamma)
is diagonal
in a suitable orthogonal basis
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\}
of
V
.

A blowup
\delta\colon\Gamma^{\prime}\dashrightarrow\Gamma

creating a vertex

v_{n+1}
induces a map
\delta^{*}\colon V\hookrightarrow V^{\prime}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n+1}{\mathbb{R}}v_{i}
defined by

\delta^{*}\colon v\mapsto v+I(\Gamma^{\prime})(v,v_{n+1})v_{n+1}.

The vector
v_{n+1}
is an eigenvector of the matrix

M_{n+1}
of
I(\Gamma^{\prime})
with eigenvalue
-1
.
Indeed, one can check that
I(\Gamma^{\prime})
restricted on

\delta^{*}(V)\subset V^{\prime}

coincides with
\delta^{*}I(\Gamma)

and
v_{n+1}
is orthogonal to
\delta^{*}(V)
.
The orthogonal basis in
V^{\prime}
of the eigenvectors

\delta^{*}(e_{1}),\ldots,\delta^{*}(e_{n}),v_{n+1}

is diagonalizing for
M_{n+1}
, which proves the claim.
∎

Remark 3.1.9
.

In the case where
\Gamma=\Gamma(D)

is the dual graph of an NC-divisor
D
on
a smooth surface
X
and
\delta\colon X^{\prime}\to X

is the inverse of the blowup
of a point on
D
, the homomorphism
\delta^{*}

sends an
{\mathbb{R}}
-divisor
N
supported on
\operatorname{supp}D

to its total transform
\delta^{*}(N)
on
X^{\prime}
, and

v_{n+1}
represents the exceptional
(-1)
-curve of
\delta
.
The properties of
\delta^{*}

used in the proof follow in this case from the Projection Formula.

Definition 3.1.10
.

A
birational transformation of weighted graphs

\phi:\Gamma\dashrightarrow\Gamma^{\prime}

is a finite sequence of blowups, blowdowns
and isomorphisms.
If all these blowups and blowdowns are inner,
then
\phi
is called
inner
.
Two weighted graphs are called

birationally equivalent
if one can
be transformed into the other by means of
a birational transformation.

From Proposition
3.1.8

we deduce the following

Corollary 3.1.11
.

The inertia indices
i_{+}(\Gamma)
and

i_{0}(\Gamma)
are birational invariants.

Remarks 3.1.12
.

1.
Consider a weighted graph
\Gamma

and a sequence
\phi
of blowups, blowdowns
and isomorphisms resulting in a final graph
\Gamma^{\prime}
.
If, besides isomorphisms,
\phi
includes also
blowups or blowdowns, then one can ignore
the isomorphisms that participate in
\phi

by simply renaming the created graphs, including
the final one. Anyway, one can reduce
\phi
to a sequence
of blowups and blowdowns preceded or followed by
a single isomorphism,
see formula (
1
) in the proof of Proposition

2.2.8
.

2. A birational transformation of weighted graphs

\phi\colon\Gamma\dasharrow\Gamma^{\prime}

is not a mapping of graphs:
in general, it is not well defined neither
on the sets of vertices nor on the sets of edges.
Nonetheless, given a second birational transformation

\phi^{\prime}\colon\Gamma^{\prime}\dasharrow\Gamma^{\prime\prime}
the composition

\phi^{\prime}\circ\phi\colon\Gamma\dasharrow\Gamma^{\prime\prime}

is a well defined birational transformation of weighted graphs.
Also the identity
{\rm id}\colon\Gamma\to\Gamma

and the inverse birational transformation

\phi^{-1}\colon\Gamma^{\prime}\dasharrow\Gamma
are well defined.

3.2.
Contractible graphs

Definition 3.2.1
.

A birational transformation

\phi:\Gamma\dasharrow\Gamma^{\prime}
is
a
birational morphism
(or
a domination
)
if no blowup participates in
\phi
.
In this case we write
\phi:\Gamma\to\Gamma^{\prime}
.
If, moreover,
\phi
is composed only of blowdowns
then we say that
\phi
is a
contraction
.
So,
contractions are birational morphisms, but an isomorphism
of graphs is not a contraction.

Remark 3.2.2
.

The composition of birational morphisms (resp., contractions)
of weighted graphs is a birational morphism (resp., a contraction).

Definition 3.2.3
.

A weighted graph
\Gamma
is called
minimal

if any birational morphism
\Gamma\to\Gamma^{\prime}

is in fact an isomorphism. A graph
\Gamma

is minimal if and only if it has no
at most linear
(-1)
-vertex. Clearly, any weighted
graph
\Gamma
dominates a minimal one called
a
minimal model
of
\Gamma
.

A simple example of a minimal weighted graph is
a graph consisting of a single vertex
v
and a loop,
cf. Example
3.5.2
.
Indeed, an at most linear
(-1)
-vertex has
no incident loop, see Definition
3.1.3
.

Definition 3.2.4
.

A connected weighted graph
\Gamma

with at least two vertices is said
to be
contractible
if there exists a contraction of

\Gamma

to a graph
\Gamma^{\prime}
that consists of
a single
(-1)
-vertex with
no incident loop; the latter graph
\Gamma^{\prime}

also is considered to be contractible.

The following lemma is well known.

Lemma 3.2.5
.

Let
\Gamma
be a weighted graph.

(a)

If
\Gamma
is contractible,
then
\Gamma
is a tree with only rational vertices.
Furthermore, the intersection form
I(\Gamma)

is negative definite of discriminant 1.

(b)

Conversely, if
\Gamma
is a tree with
only rational vertices and negative definite
intersection form
I(\Gamma)
of discriminant 1,
then
\Gamma
is contractible.

(c)

Contracting an at most linear
(-1)
-vertex
of a contractible
graph with at least two vertices yields a contractible
graph and so does a blowup of a contractible graph.
Thus, the contractibility is a birational invariant.

Proof.

To show the first assertion in (a)
it suffices to look at the reconstruction process
of blowups
starting from the graph which consists of
a single isolated
(-1)
-vertex.
The second assertion follows from
[
27
, Section 3,
Proposition and Theorem]
.
We address
[
44
, Proposition 1.20]

and
[
14
, Remark 4.8, formula (17)]

for statement (b).
Statement (c) follows e.g. from (b) due to

[
14
, Remark 4.8, formula (17)]
;
cf. also

[
27
, Section 3, Proposition]
.
∎

The next corollary is immediate.

Corollary 3.2.6
.

For a contractible weighted graph
\Gamma

the following hold.

(a)

The weight of every vertex
v

of
\Gamma
is negative.

(b)

No two
(-1)
-vertices
of
\Gamma
are neighbors.

(c)

Every
(-1)
-vertex
v
of
\Gamma

is at most linear.

(d)

If
\Gamma
contains at least two vertices,
then every

(-1)
-vertex of
\Gamma
is contracted in
any process of contraction
of
\Gamma
to a graph that consists of
a single
(-1)
-vertex
with no incident loop.

3.3.
Contraction of a subgraph

We use below the following notions.
Let
\Gamma
be a weighted graph and
\Delta

be a proper
induced subgraph
of
\Gamma
.
The latter means that every edge of

\Gamma
linking two vertices of
\Delta

is an edge of
\Delta
.

Definition 3.3.1
.

Let
\Delta
be an induced subgraph of
\Gamma

and
p\colon\Gamma\to\Gamma^{\prime}
be a contraction.
We say that
p

contracts

\Delta

if every vertex of
\Delta
is contracted under
p
.
We say that
p
is a
contraction of

\Delta
if
p
contracts every vertex of
\Delta

and no other vertices.

Remarks 3.3.2
.

1.
The fact that
p
contracts

\Delta
does not imply,
in general, that
\Delta
is contractible,
see e.g. Example
4.1.6
. On the other hand,
if
\Delta
is contractible, this does not imply,
in general,
that there exists a contraction

p\colon\Gamma\to\Gamma^{\prime}

that contracts
\Delta
.
For instance, a
(-1)
-vertex
v
of
a minimal graph
\Gamma

survives every contraction
\Gamma\to\Gamma^{\prime}
.
Indeed, a minimal graph
admits no contraction, and so
v
cannot be
an at most linear vertex.

2. It follows by recursion from Lemma

3.2.5
(c)
that a contraction of a proper induced subgraph
of a contractible graph results in a contractible graph.

Definition 3.3.3
.

Consider the blowdown

\delta\colon\Gamma\to\Gamma^{\prime}
of
an at most linear
vertex
v_{0}\in\operatorname{Vert}(\Gamma)
.
The image
\delta(\Gamma\ominus\{v_{0}\})
in

\Gamma^{\prime}
is a well defined subgraph isomorphic to

\Gamma\ominus\{v_{0}\}

up to a change of weights.
Likewise, there is a well defined image
of any subgraph
\Delta
of
\Gamma\ominus\{v_{0}\}
.
For a connected subgraph
\Delta
of
\Gamma
such that

v_{0}\in\operatorname{Vert}(\Delta)
and
\operatorname{Vert}(\Delta)\neq\{v_{0}\}
,
the image of
\Delta
in

\Gamma^{\prime}
coincides with
\delta(\Delta\ominus\{v_{0}\})

if
v_{0}
is a tip of
\Delta
;
otherwise
\delta(\Delta)=\delta(\Delta\ominus\{v_{0}\})\cup[\delta(u),\delta(v)]

where
u
and
v
are the vertices of
\Delta

linked to
v_{0}
in
\Delta

(it is possible that
u=v
).

We extend by recursion the notion of image
of a subgraph to
any birational morphism
\phi\colon\Gamma\to\Gamma^{\prime}
.

Remark 3.3.4
.

If
\delta
is an inner blowdown,
then
v_{0}
has in
\Gamma
degree 2
and exactly two incident edges, say,

[u,v_{0}]
and
[v_{0},v]

that are replaced by a single edge

e^{\prime}=[\delta(u),\delta(v)]
of
\Gamma^{\prime}
,
see Definition
3.1.6
.
If
\delta
is outer, then
v_{0}

is a tip of
\Gamma
, and
the unique edge

[v_{0},v]
of
\Gamma
at
v_{0}

disappears under
\delta
.

For a birational morphism

\delta\colon\Gamma\to\Gamma^{\prime}
and
an induced subgraph

\Delta^{\prime}\subset\Gamma^{\prime}
we define
the partial preimage

\delta^{-1}(\Delta^{\prime})\subset\Gamma

and the total preimage

\delta^{*}(\Delta^{\prime})\subset\Gamma
as follows.

Definition 3.3.5
.

Let
\Delta^{\prime}\subset\Gamma^{\prime}

be an induced subgraph and

\delta\colon\Gamma\to\Gamma^{\prime}

be the blowdown of an at most linear
vertex
v_{0}\in\operatorname{Vert}(\Gamma)
.
If
\delta

is inner (resp. outer) and
v_{0}
is linked
to vertices
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
of
\Gamma

(resp. to a vertex
v_{1}\in\operatorname{Vert}(\Gamma)
),
then we define the

partial preimage

\delta^{-1}(\Delta^{\prime}):=\delta_{0}^{-1}(\Delta^{\prime}\setminus[v_{1},v_{2}])\quad(\text{resp. }\delta^{-1}(\Delta^{\prime}):=\delta_{0}^{-1}(\Delta^{\prime}))

where

\delta_{0}\colon\Gamma\ominus\{v_{0}\}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\longrightarrow}}\Gamma^{\prime}\setminus[v_{1},v_{2}]\quad(\text{resp. }\delta_{0}\colon\Gamma\ominus\{v_{0}\}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\longrightarrow}}\Gamma^{\prime})

stands for the induced isomorphism of
unweighted graphs, cf. Definition
3.3.3
.

If
\delta
is inner (resp. outer) then
we define the
total preimage

\delta^{*}(\Delta^{\prime}):=\begin{cases}\delta^{-1}(\Delta^{\prime})\cup[v_{1},v_{0}]\cup\{v_{0}\}\cup[v_{0},v_{2}]&\text{if }[v_{1},v_{2}]\in\operatorname{Edge}(\Delta^{\prime}),\\
\delta^{-1}(\Delta^{\prime})\cup\{v_{0}\}\cup[v_{1},v_{0}]&\text{if }v_{1}\in\operatorname{Vert}(\Delta^{\prime})\text{ and }v_{2}\notin\operatorname{Vert}(\Delta^{\prime}),\\
\delta^{-1}(\Delta^{\prime})&\text{if }v_{1},v_{2}\notin\operatorname{Vert}(\Delta^{\prime}),\end{cases}

resp.

\delta^{*}(\Delta^{\prime}):=\begin{cases}\delta^{-1}(\Delta^{\prime})\cup[v_{1},v_{0}]\cup\{v_{0}\}&\text{if }v_{1}\in\operatorname{Vert}(\Delta^{\prime}),\\
\delta^{-1}(\Delta^{\prime})&\text{if }v_{1}\notin\operatorname{Vert}(\Delta^{\prime}).\end{cases}

We extend by recursion the notions of partial and
total preimages of an induced subgraph
to any birational morphism

\phi\colon\Gamma\to\Gamma^{\prime}
.

Remark 3.3.6
.

For a vertex
v\in\operatorname{Vert}(\Gamma^{\prime})

its partial preimage
\delta^{-1}(v)

is a vertex of
\Gamma

that is often denoted by the same letter
v
.
Given an induced subgraph
\Delta^{\prime}\subset\Gamma^{\prime}
,
the subgraph
\delta^{-1}(\Delta^{\prime})

is not necessarily induced,
while
\delta^{*}(\Delta^{\prime})
is. So,
\delta^{*}(\Delta^{\prime})

is uniquely defined by the set of its vertices,
that is, by the subset

\operatorname{Vert}(\delta^{-1}(\Delta^{\prime}))\subset\operatorname{Vert}(\Gamma)

plus the vertex
v_{0}
in certain cases.

If
\Delta^{\prime}
is connected, then also

\delta^{*}(\Delta^{\prime})
is. Moreover,
\delta^{*}(\Delta^{\prime})

is a connected component of the subgraph

\Gamma\ominus\bigcup_{v\in\operatorname{Vert}(\Gamma^{\prime})\setminus\operatorname{Vert}(\Delta^{\prime})}\{\delta^{-1}(v)\}
.

We will usually apply Definitions
3.3.3

and
3.3.5

to induced subgraphs of
\Gamma
.

3.3.7
.

Convention.

Given a birational morphism

\phi\colon\Gamma\to\Gamma^{\prime}

we consider
\operatorname{Vert}(\Gamma^{\prime})

as a subset of
\operatorname{Vert}(\Gamma)

identifying
v^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Vert}(\Gamma^{\prime})

with
\phi^{-1}(v^{\prime})\in\operatorname{Vert}(\Gamma)
.

Remark 3.3.8
.

A path
\gamma
in
\Gamma
is
called
simple

if
\gamma
contains no loop and has
no self-intersection.
It is easily seen that, under a birational morphism

p\colon\Gamma\to\Gamma^{\prime}
, the partial preimage of
a simple path
\gamma

connecting vertices
u^{\prime},v^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Vert}(\Gamma^{\prime})

is a simple path in
\Gamma

connecting
p^{-1}(u^{\prime})
and
p^{-1}(v^{\prime})
.
Moreover, the partial preimage
p^{-1}(\Delta^{\prime})

of a subgraph
\Delta^{\prime}\subset\Gamma^{\prime}

is homeomorphic to
\Delta^{\prime}
,
i.e. the associated simplicial
1
-complexes
viewed as topological spaces
are homeomorphic. Also,
\Delta^{\prime}
and
the total preimage
p^{*}(\Delta^{\prime})
are
homotopically equivalent,
cf.
[
14
, Sec. 2.2]
.

Definition 3.3.9
.

Given a weighted graph
\Gamma
we say
that two birational morphisms

p_{i}\colon\Gamma\to\Gamma_{i}
,
i=1,2
,
are
equivalent
if
there is a (uniquely defined) isomorphism
of weighted graphs

\iota\colon\Gamma_{1}\to\Gamma_{2}

such that
p_{1}^{-1}(\Delta)=p_{2}^{-1}(\iota(\Delta))

for any subgraph
\Delta\subset\Gamma_{1}
.
If this holds for
\Gamma_{1}=\Gamma_{2}

with
\iota={\mathrm{id}}
then we say that
p_{1}

and
p_{2}
are
equal

and we write
p_{1}=p_{2}
.
We say that a diagram of weighted graphs

\Gamma
\Gamma_{1}
\Gamma_{2}
p_{1}
p_{3}
p_{2}

where the
p_{i}
are birational morphisms of graphs,
commutes if
p_{3}\circ p_{1}=p_{2}
.
In the latter case the diagram

\Gamma
\Gamma_{2}

3.5.
Dual graphs of NC-pairs

Let us recall the correspondence between
NC-pairs and weighted graphs.

Definition 3.5.1
.

Given an NC-pair
(X,D)
the
dual graph

\Gamma(D)
is a weighted graph
whose vertices are in bijection with the irreducible
components of
D

and edges are in bijection with the nodes of
D
.
The loops of
\Gamma(D)
at a vertex

are in bijection with the self-intersection points
of the component
C
of
D
, and
the edges joining two different vertices
C_{1}

and
C_{2}
of
\Gamma(D)
are in bijection with
the points of
C_{1}\cap C_{2}
.
The weight of
C
in
\Gamma(D)
is
the self-intersection index
C^{2}
of
the component
C
in
X
.
The rational vertices in
\operatorname{Ratio}(\Gamma(D))

correspond to the rational components of
D
.

An NC-pair
(X,D)
is
an SNC-pair if and only if

\Gamma(D)
contains no loops and multiple edges,
cf. Definition
2.1.1
.
Clearly, any NC-pair is dominated by an SNC-pair.
An NC-pair
(X,D)
is minimal
if the dual graph
\Gamma(D)
is minimal.

The notions of inner and outer blowups of
an NC-pair are consistent with the notions
of inner and outer blowups
of the dual graph
\Gamma(D)
, respectively.

Example 3.5.2
.

Recall that any non-complete normal
algebraic surface
Y
admits an SNC-completion
(X,D)
,
where

Y=X\setminus\operatorname{supp}D
,
and an NC-completion with a minimal dual graph.

For example, if
X={\mathbb{P}}^{2}
and
D
is a nodal cubic,
then
\Gamma(D)
is minimal and consists
of a single vertex of weight
9
and a loop.
So the pair
({\mathbb{P}}^{2},D)
is an NC-completion
of the affine surface

Y={\mathbb{P}}^{2}\setminus D
with a minimal dual graph.
There exists a non-minimal SNC-completion
(X,D_{1})

whose dual graph
\Gamma(D_{1})
is a cycle
with three rational vertices and a cyclically ordered
sequence of weights
((-2,-1,4))
.
Moreover, there exists a minimal such completion
with the sequence of weights
((0,0,-2,-2,-2,-2,-3))
.
Thus, two minimal cyclic graphs with rational vertices
and the cyclically ordered sequences of weights
((9))

and
((0,0,-2,-2,-2,-2,-3))
, respectively,
are birationally equivalent.

Similarly, one can show that for any

the minimal linear graphs with sequences of weights

[[a]],\quad[[\underbrace{-2,-2,\ldots,-2,0,0}_{a+1}]]\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,[[\underbrace{-2,\ldots,-2,-3}_{a-1},\underbrace{-2,\ldots,-2,0,1}_{b+1}]]

are birationally equivalent, cf. Theorem
7.0.8
.

The assumption that the surface

is affine imposes severe restrictions
on the dual graph
\Gamma(D)
.

Lemma 3.5.3
.

Let an NC-pair
(X,D)
be a completion
of a normal affine surface

(a)

\operatorname{supp}D
is connected
and supports an effective ample divisor.

(b)

The dual graph
\Gamma(D)
is connected,
the intersection form

is not seminegative definite,
and

and
\Gamma(D)

are not contractible.

(c)

If

then

If, moreover,

According to the Goodman criterion
of affiness for surfaces

(cf. also
[

an ample divisor

The connectedness of

from the Lefschetz hyperplane section theorem,

[

or
[

.
Hence the graph
\Gamma(D)
is connected.
This proves statement (a).

Since

,
the intersection form

cannot be seminegative definite.
By Lemma
3.2.5

the dual graph
\Gamma(D)
is not contractible,
hence also

Assume now that
\operatorname{supp}D=C

4.2.

Definition 4.2.1
.

A minimal weighted graph

(
3
)
with a minimal graph

\Gamma_{2}

\operatorname{Vert}(\Gamma_{1})\subset\operatorname{Vert}(\Gamma)

is contracted in
\Gamma_{2}
.
In other words,

\operatorname{Vert}(\Gamma_{1})\subset\operatorname{Vert}(\Gamma_{2})
in
\Gamma
.

We say that
\Gamma_{1}
is

if all its segments are admissible,
see Definition
4.1.1
.

Examples 4.2.2
.

The graph

\Gamma_{1}=[[0,0]]

is neither admissible, nor
birationally rigid, see Examples
4.1.5

and
4.1.6
.
The same holds for the circular graph

\Gamma_{1}=((3))

with a single vertex
v
of weight
3
and a loop.
Indeed, there is
a relatively minimal diagram
(
3
), where
\Gamma
is
the circular graph
((-1,-1))
with
two vertices
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
.
It dominates, for

,
the minimal graph

\Gamma_{i}=((3))
with a single vertex

v_{3-i}

via the blowdown

of

.

Lemma 4.2.3
.

A minimal weighted
graph
\Gamma_{1}
is
birationally rigid if and only if for
every relatively minimal diagram
(
3
)
with a minimal graph
\Gamma_{2}
,
the birational morphisms
p_{1}
and
p_{2}

are isomorphisms.

Proof.

The ‘‘if’’ part is immediate. Let us prove the ‘‘only if’’ part.
Suppose on the contrary that
\Gamma_{1}
is birationally rigid
and there exists a
(-1)
-vertex

v\in\operatorname{Vert}(\Gamma)\setminus\operatorname{Vert}(\Gamma_{2})
.
By the assumption of birational rigidity we have

.
It follows that

is blown down by
both
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
.
The latter contradicts the relative

Thus,

is an isomorphism, and so

is minimal.
Therefore,
p_{1}

is an isomorphism too.
∎

We also have the following criterion
of birational rigidity;
cf.
[

, Corollaries A.3 and A.4]
.
Notice that our birationally rigid graphs correspond
to absolutely minimal graphs of

]

For the reader’s convenience we provide a proof.

Proposition 4.2.4
.

A minimal weighted graph

is birationally rigid

Proof.

Assume to the contrary that

\Gamma_{1}

a
(-1)

.

Assume

(
3
) with minimal weighted

\Gamma_{1}
and
\Gamma_{2}
dominated
by a graph

.
Then the following hold.

1.

The birational map

is inner and the

Statement 1(a) follows from Graph

(

).

(

) resp. from

To prove 1(d) we have to show that a relatively

5.
{\mathbb{G}}_{\rm a}
-actions, affine rulings and dual graphs

The following lemma is well known.

Lemma 5.0.1

(
[
18
, Corollary 1.2]
)
.

If an affine variety
Y
of dimension
\geq 2
admits
a nontrivial
{\mathbb{G}}_{\rm a}
-action, then

\operatorname{Aut}(Y)
contains a connected infinite-dimensional
commutative unipotent ind-subgroup.
In particular, it contains algebraic unipotent subgroups
of arbitrarily large dimensions.

Proof.

Indeed, let
H\subset\operatorname{Aut}^{\circ}(Y)

be the one-parameter unipotent subgroup
which corresponds to the given
{\mathbb{G}}_{\rm a}
-action.
Then
H
can be written as
H=\exp({\mathbb{K}}\partial)
,
where
\partial
is a nonzero locally nilpotent derivation
of the algebra
\mathcal{O}(Y)
, see
[
19
]
.
The ring of invariants
\ker\partial=\mathcal{O}(Y)^{H}

is an infinite dimensional vector space, and

\exp((\ker\partial)\partial)
is
a connected infinite-dimensional
commutative unipotent subgroup
of
\operatorname{Aut}^{\circ}(Y)
. For any finite-dimensional
subspace
V\subset\ker\partial
,
\exp(V\partial)
is
an algebraic unipotent subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}^{\circ}(Y)
.
∎

Remark 5.0.2
.

The assumption in Lemma

5.0.1
that
Y
is affine is important.
Indeed, an open surface
Y={\mathbb{P}}^{1}\times{\mathbb{A}}^{1}

admits an effective
{\mathbb{G}}_{\rm a}
-action,
and at the same time

\operatorname{Aut}(Y)
is an algebraic group;
see
[
33
]

for a description of
\operatorname{Aut}(Y)
.

Recall that an
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
-fibration
, or an

affine ruling
, on a normal affine
surface

is a morphism
Y\to B
to a smooth
curve with a general fiber
isomorphic to the affine line
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
.
The following lemma is well known, see e.g.

[
16
, Lemma 1.6]
; cf.
the proof of Proposition
5.0.4
.

Lemma 5.0.3
.

Let

be a normal affine surface.
Given an
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
-fibration

of

with the following properties:

•

\pi
extends to a
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
-fibration

of

•

of

•

the
(0)

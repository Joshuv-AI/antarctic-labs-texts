Null Infinity as a Weakly Isolated Horizon
Abhay Ashtekar, Simone Speziale

Abstract
Null infinity, ℐ + \mathscr{I}^{+} , arises as a boundary of the Penrose conformal completion ( M ^ , g ^ a ​ b ) (\hat{M},\hat{g}_{ab}) of an asymptotically flat physical space-time ( M , g a ​ b ) (M,g_{ab}) . We first note that ℐ + \mathscr{I}^{+} is a weakly isolated horizon (WIH) in ( M ^ , g ^ a ​ b ) (\hat{M},\hat{g}_{ab}) , and then show that its familiar geometric properties can be derived from the general WIH framework afk ; abl1 ; akkl1 ; akkl2 ; akrev ; boothrev ; Gourgoulhon:2005ng ; Jaramillo:2011zw . This seems quite surprising because physics associated with black hole (and cosmological) WIHs Δ \Delta is very different from that extracted at ℐ + \mathscr{I}^{+} . We show that these differences can be directly traced back to the fact that ℐ + \mathscr{I}^{+} is a WIH in the conformal completion rather than the physical space-time. In particular, the BMS group at ℐ + \mathscr{I}^{+} stems from the symmetry group of WIHs akkl1 . In a companion paper aass2 , we obtain fluxes and charges associated with symmetries associated with ℐ + \mathscr{I}^{+} and Δ \Delta using a new Hamiltonian framework. The fact that is there is a single mathematical framework underlying Δ \Delta and ℐ + \mathscr{I}^{+} paves the way to explore the relation between horizon dynamics in the strong field region and waveforms at infinity. It should also be useful in the analysis of black hole evaporation in quantum gravity.

Using a new perspective, these papers discussed the universal structure and symmetry group

Therefore WIHs have had the connotation of representing geometries of boundaries that are in equilibrium; indeed WIHs are often regarded as being ‘only slightly weaker than Killing horizons’.

Goal of this paper is to resolve this apparent tension. In a nutshell, we will show that the difference can be traced back to the fact that, whereas black hole (or cosmological) WIHs

, but the time-dependence is now encoded in Bondi-news –
a field with two degrees of freedom per point in 3 dimensions!
Thus, in sharp contrast to what we found in section

for

. While the connection

\Delta

\mathscr{I}^{+}
because the curvature of

contains the Bondi news

which can be freely specified on entire
\mathscr{I}^{+}

To summarize,

are WIHs and therefore they both inherit the rich structure discussed in section

11

different physics because complementary terms of Eq. (

Remark:
There is a conceptual point about the notion of symmetries that one has to keep in mind. Symmetries of a given space-time are generated by a Killing vector and a generic metric

\mathscr{I}^{+}

Let us begin by recalling a key feature of general WIHs. As discussed in section

(M,g_{ab})
and the space-time extension of a general BMS symmetry does not preserve the given
\hat{g}_{ab}
even at
\mathscr{I}^{+}
, but only the conformal class of intrinsic metrics. Thus, for the metric the situation at
\mathscr{I}^{+}
is the same as that for
\Delta
. The only difference is that the rescaling of the metric and the null normal are not as tightly intertwined at
\Delta
as they are at
\mathscr{I}^{+}
.

In both cases, to arrive at charges and fluxes associated with symmetries, we have to work with a phase space which carries a well-defined action of the symmetry group –i.e., all space-times that admit the desired WIH as a boundary. As we will see in there companion paper
aass2
, if the phase space includes a
(\hat{M},\hat{g}_{ab})
that arises from a divergence-free conformal completion of a physical space-time
(M,g_{ab})
, it will include all other divergence-free completions as well.

IV
Discussion

The fact that null infinity is a WIH seems very surprising at first because WIHs are commonly associated with black hole (and cosmological) horizons
\Delta
. Indeed,
\mathscr{I}^{+}
and
\Delta
have almost the opposite connotations.
\mathscr{I}^{+}
lies in the asymptotic, weak curvature region, while
\Delta
lies in a strong field region.
\mathscr{I}^{+}
is the arena for discussing gravitational waves, while
\Delta
is generally used to discuss the Coulombic properties of black holes in equilibrium. The goal of this paper was to probe this tension. We began in section
II
by showing that the resolution lies in three facts that are rather subtle:

•

First, the general notion of a weakly isolated horizon
\mathfrak{h}
refers only to a metric, say
\bar{g}_{ab}
, of signature -,+,+,+ on a manifold
\bar{M}
. The notion does not require
\bar{g}_{ab}
to satisfy either Einstein’s equations or conformal Einstein’s equations. The only condition on the Ricci tensor
\bar{R}_{a}{}^{b}
is that, if
{\bar{k}}^{a}
is a null normal to
\mathfrak{h}
, then
\bar{R}_{a}{}^{b}{\bar{k}}^{a}
be proportional to
{\bar{k}}^{b}
at points of
\mathfrak{h}
.
As a consequence, the existence of commonly used geometrical fields on
\mathfrak{h}
and their basic properties –investigated extensively in, e.g.
afk
;
abl1
;
akkl1
– do not use Einstein’s or conformal Einstein’s equations. Each WIH comes equipped with a degenerate metric
\bar{q}_{ab}
and a (torsion-free) connection
\bar{D}
defined intrinsically on
\mathfrak{h}
, satisfying
\bar{D}_{a}\bar{q}_{bc}\,\hat{=}\,0
. Because
\bar{q}_{ab}
is degenerate, it does
not
determine
\bar{D}
; the connection has extra information.

As we saw, the condition on the Ricci tensor is satisfied on both:
(i) the standard black hole and cosmological WIHs where
(\bar{M},\bar{g}_{ab})
is the physical space-time
(M,g_{ab})
and
\mathfrak{h}
is denoted by
\Delta
, and,
(ii) at null infinity with the standard fall-off of matter stress-energy, where now
(\bar{M},\bar{g}_{ab})
is the conformal completion
(\hat{M},\hat{g}_{ab})
of the physical space-time and
\mathfrak{h}
is denoted by
\mathscr{I}^{+}
.
Therefore both
\Delta
and
\mathscr{I}
are WIHs, and each is equipped with an intrinsically defined degenerate metric and a connection.

•

Second, in sharp contrast to ‘stationary horizons’ discussed in the literature (e.g. in
cfp
), the intrinsically defined
\bar{D}
on WIHs is generically
time-dependent
! At
\mathscr{I}^{+}
, the time derivative of this connection –now denoted by
\hat{D}
– has direct physical significance: it determines the Bondi news
aa-radiativemodes
;
aams
;
ashtekar1987asymptotic
. Consequently,
\hat{D}
encodes the radiative modes of the gravitational field –two per point of 3-dimensional
\mathscr{I}^{+}
. Hence there can be flux of gravitational waves carrying BMS-momentum across
\mathscr{I}^{+}
.

•

Third, a subtle but key difference arises for generic black hole and cosmological WIHs
\Delta
because they lie in
physical space-times

(M,\,g_{ab})
: even though the time derivative of the intrinsic connection
D
is
again non-zero
, there is no flux of gravitational radiation across
\Delta
. We saw that the vacuum Einstein’s equations
R_{ab}=0
imply that the first time derivative of
D
can be expressed entirely in terms of geometrical fields which are themselves ‘time independent’. Consequently, the ‘freely specifiable data’ on
\Delta
consists of fields that can be specified on a 2-dimensional cross section; there are
n
o volume degrees of freedom in
D
. Gravitational waves on the other hand correspond to geometric fields that carry 3-dimensional, local degrees of freedom. Because these degrees of freedom are absent, there is no flux of gravitational waves across
\Delta
. Finally, if there are matter fields, then Einstein’s equation at
\Delta
guarantee that no matter flux falls across
\Delta
either. By contrast, the metric
\hat{g}_{ab}
at
\mathscr{I}^{+}
satisfies
conformal
Einstein’s equations and they allow the the intrinsic derivative
\hat{D}
to carry 2 degrees of freedom per point of 3-d
\mathscr{I}^{+}
,
even though
\hat{g}_{ab}
endows
\mathscr{I}^{+}
with a WIH structure.
Note also that it is critical that while
\mathscr{I}^{+}
is a WIH, it is not an IH. Had it been an IH then the connection
\hat{D}
would have been so constrained that its News tensor
\hat{N}_{ab}
would have been forced to vanish. Thus, the difference between WIH and IH structures –that may seem like a minor technicality– makes a profound difference to physics at
\mathscr{I}^{+}
.

In section
III
we examined the symmetry groups on WIHs. The group
\mathfrak{G}
on a general
\mathfrak{h}
is a 1-dimensional extension of the BMS group by dilations. This extension is essential because, in stationary, non-extremal black hole solutions, for example, the restriction of the Killing field to the horizon is a dilation. The presence of this dilation is directly related to the fact that a generic WIH is naturally equipped with a null normal that is unique only up to a constant rescaling whence a symmetry vector field can rescale this normal by a constant. On the other hand
\mathscr{I}^{+}
is an WIH in a conformal completion of the physical space-time and each completion endows
\mathscr{I}^{+}
with a specific null normal without the rescaling freedom. Because of this extra structure, the symmetry group
\mathfrak{G}
reduces to the
\mathfrak{B}
group.

In the companion paper
aass2
, we will introduce a new Hamiltonian framework tailored to degrees of freedom that reside in 3-d open regions and apply it to regions
\mathcal{R}
of
\Delta
and
\hat{R}
of
\mathscr{I}^{+}
to obtain expressions of fluxes across these regions associated with respective symmetries. Again we will find that while one and the same framework can be applied to both types of WIHs, it leads to strikingly different results in the two cases: At
\mathscr{I}^{+}
we obtain the standard BMS fluxes while at
\Delta
all fluxes vanish. The origin of this difference can be traced back to the fact that at
\mathscr{I}^{+}
we have 3-d (radiative) degrees of freedom, while at
\Delta
we only have 2-d (Coulombic) degrees of freedom. Moreover, in contrast to other approaches, the BMS fluxes (and charges) at
\mathscr{I}^{+}
are obtained without having to extend the BMS vector fields
\xi^{a}
to the space-time interior. We will compare and contrast the relative merits of this method vis a vis those that have been widely used in the recent literature. Our Hamiltonian framework could be useful also other contexts. For example, it is well-suited to the future space-like infinity in asymptotically de Sitter space-times.

This unified treatment of black hole (and cosmological) horizons
\Delta
and null infinity
\mathscr{I}^{+}
may open new directions for further research both in classical general relativity and quantum gravity. For example, it was shown in
akkl2
that the expressions of fluxes on
perturbed
black hole and cosmological horizons
\Delta
are completely analogous to those of perturbations of stationary black holes at
\mathscr{I}^{+}
. This finding paves the way to gravitational tomography at late times in compact binary mergers

;
aank
, enabling one to read off the late time horizon dynamics from the waveform at
\mathscr{I}^{+}
, even though the dynamical horizon is causally inaccessible to observers in the asymptotic region. Similarly, in the analysis of isolated gravitating systems
in presence of a positive cosmological constant
, one can use certain cosmological horizons as ‘local
\mathscr{I}^{\pm}
’
Ashtekar_2017
;
Ashtekar_2019
. The present unified treatment suggests concrete avenues to develop a framework to extract the physics of gravitational waves emitted by these systems. Finally, the availability of a single framework encompassing black hole horizons and null infinity is likely to be useful also in quantum gravity. Specifically, it offers sharper tools to analyze correlations between horizon dynamics and quantum radiation at
\mathscr{I}^{+}
during the long semi-classical phase
Ashtekar_2020
of black hole evaporation
aa-loops24

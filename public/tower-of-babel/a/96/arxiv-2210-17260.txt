Joint BS-RIS-User Association and Beamforming Design for RIS-assisted Cellular Networks
Sifan Liu, Rang Liu, Ming Li, Yang Liu, Qian Liu

Abstract
Reconfigurable intelligent surface (RIS) is a revolutionary technology for sixth-generation (6G) networks owing to its ability to manipulate wireless environments. As a frequency-selective device, RIS can only effectively shape the propagation of signals within a certain frequency band. Due to this frequency-selective property, the deployment of RIS in cellular networks will introduce a complicated base station (BS)-RIS-user association issue since adjacent BSs operate at different frequency bands. In this paper, with the consideration of the frequency-selective characteristics of RIS, we aim to jointly optimize BS-RIS-user association, active beamforming at BSs, and passive beamforming of RIS to maximize the sum-rate of a RIS-assisted cellular network. We first leverage l 0 l_{0} -norm to efficiently integrate BS-RIS-user association with active and passive beamforming. Then, we adopt fractional programming (FP) and block coordinate descent (BCD) methods to deal with logarithmic and fractional parts and decouple the joint association and beamforming design problem into several sub-problems. Efficient algorithms which combine l 0 l_{0} -norm approximation, majorization-minimization (MM), and alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM) are developed to alternately solve the sub-problems. Extensive simulation results illustrate the importance of BS-RIS-user association optimization in RIS-assisted cellular networks and verify the effectiveness of the proposed joint association and beamforming design algorithm.

Introduction

Recently, with the rapid deployment of commercial fifth-generation (5G) wireless networks worldwide, researchers shift their interests to future sixth-generation (6G) systems, which require higher data rate and network capacity.
However, the existing technologies in 5G, e.g., massive multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO), millimeter wave (mmWave) communications, and ultra-dense heterogeneous network have reached their inherent limitations on transmission rate, spectrum/energy efficiency, etc. Thereby, innovative technologies are needed to achieve radical changes.

In conventional networks, the wireless propagation environment is regarded as a randomly varied uncontrollable factor, which cannot be optimized in the network design.
Recently, an innovative reconfigurable intelligent surface (RIS) technology is introduced to radically change this conventional perception.

Most existing works on RIS-assisted systems consider relatively simple scenarios with only one transmitter or base station (BS).
In order to accelerate the deployment of RIS in realistic cellular networks, the design of RIS in multi-cell systems is also a worth studying problem.
Moreover, two special and crucial issues are needed to be considered for the RIS-assisted cellular network.
Firstly, some existing works illustrated the frequency-selective property of RIS. By considering the hardware complexity and cost in practical implementations, this property can be approximately modeled as: Each element of RIS can only effectively provide tunable phase-shift for signals within a certain frequency band, while generates almost fixed phase-shift for signals at other frequency bands

This fact means that the BS-RIS association problem deserves to be carefully studied in the RIS-assisted multi-cell system and effective association algorithms need to be developed for this new BS-RIS association problem.
Secondly, the deployment of RIS introduces a different and more complicated BS-user association problem than conventional multi-cell systems, because the BS-user link relies not only on the direct channels, but also on the BS-RIS-user cascaded channels.
Moreover, since RIS can reflect the signals from all associated/un-associated BSs, the BS-user association in all cells will be affected.
This fact reveals that the traditional user association algorithms cannot be directly adopted in RIS-assisted systems, which inspires us to investigate new BS-user association algorithms.

III
Association and Beamforming Design

In this section, we present the proposed FP-MM-ADMM based joint association and beamforming design algorithm.
To be specific, we utilize the FP method to convert the logarithmic and fractional objective function into a more tractable form.
Then, we adopt the BCD method to decompose the coupled variables and alternately solve the three resulting sub-problems, i.e., 1) the update of auxiliary variables, 2) the joint BS-user association and active beamforming optimization problem, and 3) the joint BS-RIS association and passive beamforming optimization problem.

(10b)

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\left\|\left\|\mathbf{w}_{j,k}\right\|_{2}^{2}\right\|_{0}=1,~\forall k,

(10c)

in which we define

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\Upsilon}_{j}

\displaystyle\triangleq\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}|q_{j,k}|^{2}\widetilde{\mathbf{h}}_{j,k}\widetilde{\mathbf{h}}_{j,k}^{H},

(11a)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{j,k}

\displaystyle\triangleq(2\sqrt{1+\tau_{j,k}}q_{j,k}^{*}\widetilde{\mathbf{h}}_{j,k}^{H})^{H},

(11b)

\displaystyle\varsigma_{j,k}

\displaystyle\triangleq|q_{j,k}|^{2}\sigma_{k}^{2},

(11c)

to re-arrange the objective function into a more compact form.
We clearly see that the objective function (
10a
) is concave with respect to
\mathbf{w}_{j,k}
, constraint (
10b
) is convex. Thus, the non-convex constraint (
10c
) is the major difficulty that requires sophisticated derivations as follows.

Notice that constraint (
10c
) is a non-smooth non-convex constraint due to the property of
l_{0}
-norm.
To make this constraint (
10c
) more tractable, we approximate the
l_{0}
-norm, i.e.,
\|x\|_{0}
, with a continuous smooth and concave function
[
46
]

g_{\delta}(x)\triangleq 1-\exp\left(-\frac{x}{\delta}\right),

(12)

where
\delta>0
is a parameter that controls the smoothness of the approximation.
With the decreasing of
\delta
, function (
12
) becomes more approximate to
l_{0}
-norm with worse smoothness.
By utilizing the
l_{0}
-norm approximation method, constraint (
10c
) can be relaxed as

n_{1}\overset{\text{(c)}}{\leq}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}g_{\delta}(\|\mathbf{w}_{j,k}\|_{2}^{2})\overset{\text{(d)}}{\leq}n_{2},~\forall k,

(13)

where

g_{\delta}(\|\mathbf{w}_{j,k}\|_{2}^{2})=1-\exp\big(-\frac{\|\mathbf{w}_{j,k}\|_{2}^{2}}{\delta}\big).

(14)

In (
13
),
n_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
and
n_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
are numbers slightly less and greater than one, respectively.
Since
g_{\delta}(\|\mathbf{w}_{j,k}\|_{2}^{2})
is a non-concave function with respect to
\mathbf{w}_{j,k}
, the inequalities (c) and (d) in (
13
) cannot be solved directly.
Thus, we use the MM method to deal with these two intractable constraints.
In brief, the essence of MM method is to construct an upper-bound surrogate function that locally approximates the objective function in each iteration, in order to make the optimization problem more tractable
[
47
]
.
The details of constructing surrogate functions for inequalities (c) and (d) are presented as follows.

Inequality (c):

To better handle the inequality (c) in (
13
), we first convert it into a more standard form in convex optimization as

-\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}g_{\delta}(\|\mathbf{w}_{j,k}\|_{2}^{2})\leq-n_{1},~\forall k.

(15)

Then, by using the second-order Taylor expansion, a convex upper-bound
\widetilde{g}_{\delta}(\mathbf{w}_{j,k}|\mathbf{w}_{j,k}^{(t-1)})
of
-g_{\delta}(\|\mathbf{w}_{j,k}\|_{2}^{2})
can be constructed as shown in the following proposition, whose proof is provided in the Appendix A.

Proposition 1:
The convex upper-bound
\widetilde{g}_{\delta}(\mathbf{w}_{j,k}|\mathbf{w}_{j,k}^{(t-1)})
of
-g_{\delta}(\|\mathbf{w}_{j,k}\|_{2}^{2})
can be constructed as

\displaystyle\widetilde{g}_{\delta}(\mathbf{w}_{j,k}|\mathbf{w}_{j,k}^{(t-1)})=

\displaystyle\mathfrak{Re}\{\mathbf{w}_{j,k}^{H}\mathbf{M}_{j,k}\mathbf{w}_{j,k}\}+\mathfrak{Re}\{\boldsymbol{\iota}_{j,k}^{H}\mathbf{w}_{j,k}\}

(16)

\displaystyle+\varpi_{j,k},~\forall j,~\forall k,

in which

\displaystyle\mathbf{M}_{j,k}\triangleq

\displaystyle\max{\{0,\lambda_{j,k}\}}\cdot\mathbf{I}_{M},~\forall j,\forall k,

(17a)

\displaystyle\beta_{j,k}^{(t-1)}\triangleq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\delta}\exp\Big(-\frac{\big\|\mathbf{w}_{j,k}^{(t-1)}\big\|_{2}^{2}}{\delta}\Big),~\forall j,\forall k,

(17b)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\iota}_{j,k}^{H}\triangleq

\displaystyle-\hskip-2.84544pt2(\mathbf{w}_{j,k}^{(t-1)})^{H}\mathbf{M}_{j,k}\hskip-2.84544pt-\hskip-2.84544pt2\beta_{j,k}^{(t-1)}(\mathbf{w}_{j,k}^{(t-1)})^{H},~\forall j,\forall k,

(17c)

\displaystyle\varpi_{j,k}\triangleq

\displaystyle-h_{\delta}(\|\mathbf{w}_{j,k}^{(t-1)}\|_{2}^{2})+\mathfrak{Re}\{(\mathbf{w}_{j,k}^{(t-1)})^{H}\mathbf{M}_{j,k}\mathbf{w}_{j,k}^{(t-1)}\}\hskip-1.42271pt

(17d)

\displaystyle+\hskip-1.42271pt2\mathfrak{Re}\{\beta_{j,k}^{(t-1)}(\mathbf{w}_{j,k}^{(t-1)})^{H}\mathbf{w}_{j,k}^{(t-1)}\},\forall j,\forall k.

In (
17
),
\mathbf{w}_{j,k}^{(t-1)}
is the solution obtained in the previous iteration and
\lambda_{j,k}
is the maximum eigenvalue of the complex Hessian matrix of
-g_{\delta}(\|\mathbf{w}_{j,k}\|_{2}^{2})
.
\blacksquare

Thus,
\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\widetilde{g}_{\delta}(\mathbf{w}_{j,k}|\mathbf{w}_{j,k}^{(t-1)})
can be used as the surrogate upper-bound function of
-\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}g_{\delta}(\|\mathbf{w}_{j,k}\|_{2}^{2})
, and the inequality constraint (
15
) is transformed into

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\widetilde{g}_{\delta}(\mathbf{w}_{j,k}|\mathbf{w}_{j,k}^{(t-1)})\leq-n_{1},~\forall k,

(18)

which is convex and can be easily handled.

Inequality (d):

For inequality (d) in (
13
), our goal is also finding a convex upper-bound function of
g_{\delta}(\|\mathbf{w}_{j,k}\|_{2}^{2})
to convert it into a convex constraint.
It is obvious that function
g_{\delta}(\cdot)
is strictly monotone increasing.
Thus, by introducing auxiliary variable
\mathbf{u}\triangleq[u_{1,1},...,u_{j,k},...,u_{J,K}]^{T}
which satisfies

\left\|\mathbf{w}_{j,k}\right\|_{2}^{2}-u_{j,k}\leq 0,~\forall j\in\mathcal{J},~\forall k\in\mathcal{K},

(19)

we can easily obtain
g_{\delta}(u_{j,k})
as an upper-bound of
g_{\delta}(\|\mathbf{w}_{j,k}\|_{2}^{2})
, i.e., the following inequality

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69046ptg_{\delta}(\|\mathbf{w}_{j,k}\|_{2}^{2})\leq g_{\delta}\left(u_{j,k}\right)=1-\exp\left(-\frac{u_{j,k}}{\delta}\right),~\forall j,~\forall k,

(20)

is satisfied. Then, we can utilize the surrogate function
g_{\delta}\left(u_{j,k}\right)
in the inequality (d) as

\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}g_{\delta}(u_{j,k})\leq n_{2},~\forall k.

(21)

Since (
21
) is still a non-convex constraint,
the first-order Taylor expansion is adopted to construct a convex upper-bound function of
g_{\delta}\left(u_{j,k}\right)
as

\displaystyle\widehat{g}_{\delta}(u_{j,k}|u_{j,k}^{(t-1)})=

\displaystyle\nabla g_{\delta}\big(u_{j,k}^{(t-1)}\big)\big(u_{j,k}-u_{j,k}^{(t-1)}\big)

(22)

\displaystyle+g_{\delta}\big(u_{j,k}^{(t-1)}\big),~\forall j,~\forall k,

where
u_{j,k}^{(t-1)}
is the result obtained in the previous iteration,
and the gradient
\nabla g_{\delta}\left(u_{j,k}\right)
is obtained by

\displaystyle\nabla g_{\delta}\left(u_{j,k}\right)=\frac{1}{\delta}\exp\Big(-\frac{u_{j,k}}{\delta}\Big),~\forall j,~\forall k.

(23)

Then, by using the convex surrogate function
\widehat{g}_{\delta}(u_{j,k}|u_{j,k}^{(t-1)})
, inequality (d) in (
13
) can be converted into

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\widehat{g}_{\delta}(u_{j,k}|u_{j,k}^{(t-1)})\leq n_{2},~\forall k.

(24)

In summary, based on the above derivations, the active beamforming design problem at the
t
-th iteration can be reformulated as

\displaystyle\mathop{\max}\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{w},\mathbf{u}\end{subarray}}\quad

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}(-\mathbf{w}_{j,k}^{H}\boldsymbol{\Upsilon}_{j}\mathbf{w}_{j,k}+\mathfrak{Re}\{\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{j,k}^{H}\mathbf{w}_{j,k}\})

(25a)

s.t.

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}u_{j,k}\leq P_{j},~\forall j,

(25b)

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{w}_{j,k}\right\|_{2}^{2}-u_{j,k}\leq 0,~\forall j,~\forall k,

(25c)

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\widetilde{g}_{\delta}(\mathbf{w}_{j,k}|\mathbf{w}_{j,k}^{(t-1)})\leq-n_{1},~\forall k,

(25d)

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\widehat{g}_{\delta}(u_{j,k}|u_{j,k}^{(t-1)})\leq n_{2},~\forall k,

(25e)

which is convex and can be solved by many existing methods or convex optimization solvers, such as CVX
[
48
]
.

III-D

Update Passive Beamforming
\boldsymbol{\varphi}

In this subsection, we present the algorithm for optimizing the passive beamforming of RIS, which also indicates the BS-RIS association.
With fixed
\boldsymbol{\tau}
,
\mathbf{q}
, and
\mathbf{w}
, the reformulated objective function (
7
) can be concisely re-arranged as the following form with respect to
\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}(-\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}^{H}\mathbf{D}_{j}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}+2\mathfrak{Re}\{\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}^{H}\mathbf{v}_{j}\}+c_{j}),

(26)

in which

\displaystyle\mathbf{a}_{j,i,k}\triangleq

\displaystyle(\operatorname{diag}\{\mathbf{h}_{\text{r},k}^{H}\}\mathbf{G}_{j}\mathbf{w}_{j,i})^{*},~b_{j,i,k}\triangleq(\mathbf{h}_{\text{d},j,k}^{H}\mathbf{w}_{j,i})^{*},

(27a)

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}_{j}\triangleq

\displaystyle\hskip-2.84544pt\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\left|q_{j,k}\right|^{2}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{K}}\mathbf{a}_{j,i,k}\mathbf{a}_{j,i,k}^{H},

(27b)

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}_{j}\triangleq

\displaystyle\hskip-2.84544pt\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}(\sqrt{1+\tau_{j,k}}q_{j,k}^{*}\mathbf{a}_{j,k,k}\hskip-2.84544pt-\hskip-2.84544pt\left|q_{j,k}\right|^{2}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{K}}b_{j,i,k}^{*}\mathbf{a}_{j,i,k}),

(27c)

\displaystyle c_{j}\triangleq

\displaystyle\hskip-2.84544pt\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}2\sqrt{1+\tau_{j,k}}\mathfrak{Re}\left\{q_{j,k}^{*}b_{j,k,k}\right\}

(27d)

\displaystyle-\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\left|q_{j,k}\right|^{2}(\sigma^{2}_{k}+\sum_{i\in\mathcal{K}}\left|b_{j,i,k}\right|^{2}).

Thus, by removing the irrelevant constant term
c_{j}
, the passive beamforming optimization problem can be formulated as

\displaystyle\hskip-2.84544pt\mathop{\max}\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\end{subarray}}~

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}(-\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}^{H}\mathbf{D}_{j}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}+2\mathfrak{Re}\{\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}^{H}\mathbf{v}_{j}\})

(28a)

s.t.

\displaystyle|\varphi_{j,n}|=1,~\forall j,~\forall n,

(28b)

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\left\|\left\|\ln(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j})\right\|_{2}^{2}\right\|_{0}=1,

(28c)

which is still difficult to solve due to the non-convex unit modulus constraints and non-smooth non-convex
l_{0}
-norm constraints. Here, the ADMM method
[
49
]
is adopted to solve this problem.
We first deal with the unit modulus constraints (
28b
) by introducing auxiliary variables
\boldsymbol{\psi}_{j}\triangleq[{\psi}_{j,1},...,{\psi}_{j,N}]^{T}\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times 1},\forall j
, and reformulate problem (
28
) as

\displaystyle\mathop{\max}\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j},\boldsymbol{\psi}_{j},\forall j\end{subarray}}\;

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}(-\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}^{H}\mathbf{D}_{j}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}+2\mathfrak{Re}\{\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}^{H}\mathbf{v}_{j}\})

(29a)

s.t.

\displaystyle|\varphi_{j,n}|\leq 1,~\forall j,~\forall n,

(29b)

\displaystyle|\psi_{j,n}|=1,~\forall j,~\forall n,

(29c)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}=\boldsymbol{\psi}_{j},~\forall j,

(29d)

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\left\|\big\|\ln(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j})\big\|_{2}^{2}\right\|_{0}=1,

(29e)

whose optimal solution can be obtained by solving its augmented Lagrangian (AL) problem.
Thus, by introducing dual variables
\boldsymbol{\xi}_{j}\triangleq[{\xi}_{j,1},...,{\xi}_{j,N}]\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times 1},~\forall j
and penalty coefficient
\rho>0
, the AL problem of (
29
) is written as

\displaystyle\mathop{\max}\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j},\boldsymbol{\psi}_{j},\boldsymbol{\xi}_{j},\forall j\end{subarray}}\quad

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}(-\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}^{H}\mathbf{D}_{j}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}+2\mathfrak{Re}\{\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}^{H}\mathbf{v}_{j}\}

(30a)

\displaystyle-\mathfrak{Re}\{\boldsymbol{\xi}_{j}^{H}(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}-\boldsymbol{\psi}_{j})\}-\frac{\rho}{2}\|\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}-\boldsymbol{\psi}_{j}\|_{2}^{2})

s.t.

\displaystyle|\varphi_{j,n}|\leq 1,~\forall j,~\forall n,

(30b)

\displaystyle|\psi_{j,n}|=1,~\forall j,~\forall n,

(30c)

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\left\|\big\|\ln(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j})\big\|_{2}^{2}\right\|_{0}=1,

(30d)

which is more tractable than (
29
) after removing the equality constraint (
29d
)
and can be solved by alternately updating
\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}
,
\boldsymbol{\psi}_{j}
, and
\boldsymbol{\xi}_{j}
, as introduced in the following.

III-D
.1
Update
\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}

With fixed
\boldsymbol{\psi}_{j}
and
\boldsymbol{\xi}_{j}
, the problem of optimizing
\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}
can be written as

\displaystyle\mathop{\max}\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j},\forall j\end{subarray}}\quad

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}(-\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}^{H}\mathbf{D}_{j}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}+2\mathfrak{Re}\{\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}^{H}\mathbf{v}_{j}\}

(31a)

\displaystyle~~~~-\mathfrak{Re}\{\boldsymbol{\xi}_{j}^{H}(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}-\boldsymbol{\psi}_{j})\}-\frac{\rho}{2}\|\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}-\boldsymbol{\psi}_{j}\|_{2}^{2})

s.t.

\displaystyle|\varphi_{j,n}|\leq 1,~\forall j,~\forall n,

(31b)

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\left\|\big\|\ln(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j})\big\|_{2}^{2}\right\|_{0}=1.

(31c)

It is obvious that the main difficulty to solve this problem is how to deal with the non-smooth non-convex
l_{0}
-norm constraint.
Similar to the active beamforming optimization algorithm introduced in the previous subsection, we utilize the
l_{0}
-norm approximation and MM based method to solve this problem.
By adopting the same concave function
g_{\delta}(\cdot)
to approximate the non-smooth
l_{0}
-norm, constraint (
31c
) can be relaxed as

n_{3}\overset{\text{(e)}}{\leq}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}g_{\delta}(\|\ln(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j})\|_{2}^{2})\overset{\text{(f)}}{\leq}n_{4},

(32)

in which

g_{\delta}(\|\ln(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j})\|_{2}^{2})=1-\exp\Big(-\frac{\|\ln(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j})\|_{2}^{2}}{\delta}\Big),

(33)

and
n_{3}\in\mathbb{R}
and
n_{4}\in\mathbb{R}
are numbers slightly less and greater than one, respectively.
Then, we adopt the MM method to convert these two non-convex constraints, i.e., inequalities (e) and (f) in (
32
), into more tractable forms.

Inequality (e):
We first transform inequality (e) in (
32
) into a more standard form as

-\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}g_{\delta}(\|\ln(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j})\|_{2}^{2})\leq-n_{3}.

(34)

The convex upper-bound function
\overline{g}_{\delta}(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}|\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}^{(t-1)})
of
-g_{\delta}(\|\ln(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j})\|_{2}^{2})
can be constructed by second-order Taylor expansion as shown in the following
proposition, whose proof is presented in Appendix B.

Proposition 2:

The convex upper-bound
\overline{g}_{\delta}(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}|\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}^{(t-1)})
of
-g_{\delta}(\|\ln(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j})\|_{2}^{2})
is written as

\displaystyle\hskip-8.5359pt\overline{g}_{\delta}(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}|\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}^{(t-1)})=\mathfrak{Re}\{\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}^{H}\boldsymbol{\Xi}_{j}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}\}\hskip-1.42271pt+\hskip-1.42271pt\mathfrak{Re}\{\boldsymbol{\kappa}_{j}^{H}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}\}\hskip-1.42271pt+\hskip-1.42271pt\mu_{j},\forall j,

(35)

in which

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt\mathbf{\Xi}_{j}\triangleq

\displaystyle\max{\{0,\lambda_{j}\}}\cdot\mathbf{I}_{N},~\forall j,

(36a)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\kappa}_{j}\triangleq

\displaystyle-2(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}^{(t-1)})^{H}\boldsymbol{\Xi}_{j}-2\varepsilon_{j}^{(t-1)}(\boldsymbol{\phi}_{j}^{(t-1)})^{H},~\forall j,

(36b)

\displaystyle\mu_{j}\triangleq

\displaystyle-g_{\delta}(\|\ln(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}^{(t-1)})\|_{2}^{2})\hskip-1.42271pt+\hskip-1.42271pt\mathfrak{Re}\{(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}^{(t-1)})^{H}\boldsymbol{\Xi}_{j}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}^{(t-1)}\}\hskip-1.42271pt

(36c)

\displaystyle+\hskip-1.42271pt2\mathfrak{Re}\{\varepsilon_{j}^{(t-1)}(\boldsymbol{\phi}_{j}^{(t-1)})^{H}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}^{(t-1)}\},~\forall j.

Here, for brevity we define

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{j}^{(t-1)}\triangleq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\delta}\exp\Big(-\frac{\big\|\ln(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}^{(t-1)})\big\|_{2}^{2}}{\delta}\Big),~\forall j,

(37a)

\displaystyle(\boldsymbol{\phi}_{j}^{(t-1)})^{H}\triangleq

\displaystyle\ln({\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}^{(t-1)})}^{H}\oslash{(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}^{(t-1)})}^{T},~\forall j.

(37b)

In (
36
),
\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}^{(t-1)}
and
\lambda_{j}
are the optimal solution obtained in the previous iteration and the maximum eigenvalue of the complex Hessian matrix of
-g_{\delta}\big(\|\ln(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j})\|_{2}^{2}\big)
, respectively.
\blacksquare

Then, with the convex surrogate function
\overline{g}_{\delta}(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}|\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}^{(t-1)})
, the inequality (e) in (
32
) can be transformed into

\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\overline{g}_{\delta}(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}|\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}^{(t-1)})\leq-n_{3},

(38)

which satisfies the original constraint and is more tractable.

Inequality (f):
The second-order Taylor expansion is utilized to construct the upper-bound function
\breve{g}_{\delta}(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}|\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}^{(t-1)})
for the non-convex function
g_{\delta}(\|\ln(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j})\|_{2}^{2})
as presented in the following proposition, whose proof is shown in Appendix C.

Proposition 3:

The convex upper-bound
\breve{g}_{\delta}(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}|\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}^{(t-1)})
of
g_{\delta}(\|\ln(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j})\|_{2}^{2})
is written as

\displaystyle\hskip-8.5359pt\breve{g}_{\delta}(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}|\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}^{(t-1)})=\mathfrak{Re}\{\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}^{H}\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{j}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}\}+\mathfrak{Re}\{\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{j}^{H}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}\}+\eta_{j},\forall j,

(39)

where

\displaystyle\mathbf{\Gamma}_{j}\triangleq

\displaystyle\max{\{0,\lambda_{j}\}}\cdot\mathbf{I}_{N},~\forall j,

(40a)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{j}^{H}\triangleq

\displaystyle-2(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}^{(t-1)})^{H}\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{j}+2\varepsilon_{j}^{(t-1)}(\boldsymbol{\phi}_{j}^{(t-1)})^{H},~\forall j,

(40b)

\displaystyle\eta_{j}\triangleq

\displaystyle g_{\delta}(\|\ln(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}^{(t-1)})\|_{2}^{2})+\mathfrak{Re}\{(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}^{(t-1)})^{H}\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{j}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}^{(t-1)}\}

(40c)

\displaystyle-2\mathfrak{Re}\{\varepsilon_{j}^{(t-1)}(\boldsymbol{\phi}_{j}^{(t-1)})^{H}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}^{(t-1)}\},~\forall j.

Here,
\lambda_{j}
is the maximum eigenvalue of the complex Hessian matrix of
g_{\delta}(\|\ln(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j})\|_{2}^{2})
.
\blacksquare

Thus, by utilizing the convex upper-bound surrogate function
\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\breve{g}_{\delta}(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}|\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}^{(t-1)})
, the non-convex inequality (f) in (
32
) can be converted into

\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\breve{g}_{\delta}(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}|\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}^{(t-1)})\leq n_{4}.

(41)

In summary, the optimization problem of passive beamforming at the
t
-th iteration can be written as

\displaystyle\mathop{\max}\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j},\forall j\end{subarray}}\quad

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}(-\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}^{H}\mathbf{D}_{j}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}+2\mathfrak{Re}\{\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}^{H}\mathbf{v}_{j}\}

(42a)

\displaystyle~~~~-\mathfrak{Re}\{\boldsymbol{\xi}_{j}^{H}(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}-\boldsymbol{\psi}_{j})\}-\frac{\rho}{2}\|\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}-\boldsymbol{\psi}_{j}\|_{2}^{2})

s.t.

\displaystyle|\varphi_{j,n}|\leq 1,~\forall j,~\forall n,

(42b)

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\overline{g}_{\delta}(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}|\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}^{(t-1)})\leq-n_{3},

(42c)

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\breve{g}_{\delta}(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}|\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}^{(t-1)})\leq n_{4},

(42d)

which is a convex problem and can be easily solved by CVX.

III-D
.2
Update
\boldsymbol{\psi}_{j}

With fixed
\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}
and
\boldsymbol{\xi}_{j}
, the problem of optimizing
\boldsymbol{\psi}_{j}
can be written as

\displaystyle\mathop{\max}\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\boldsymbol{\psi}_{j},\forall j\end{subarray}}\;

\displaystyle-\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\mathfrak{Re}\{\boldsymbol{\xi}_{j}^{H}(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}-\boldsymbol{\psi}_{j})\}-\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\frac{\rho}{2}\|\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}-\boldsymbol{\psi}_{j}\|_{2}^{2}

(43a)

s.t.

\displaystyle|\psi_{j,n}|=1,~\forall j,~\forall n.

(43b)

Then, to facilitate algorithm development, we rewrite the objective function (43a) as

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\mathfrak{Re}

\displaystyle\{(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{j}+{\rho}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j})^{H}\boldsymbol{\psi}_{j}\}-\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\frac{{\rho}}{2}\|\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}\|_{2}^{2}

(44)

\displaystyle-\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\frac{{\rho}}{2}\|\boldsymbol{\psi}_{j}\|_{2}^{2}-\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\mathfrak{Re}\{\boldsymbol{\xi}_{j}^{H}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}\}.

Since
\psi_{j,n}
has unit modulus, the quadratic term
\|\boldsymbol{\psi}_{j}\|_{2}^{2}
equals to
N
.
Hence, by removing the constant parts, the equivalent problem of (43) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\mathop{\max}\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\boldsymbol{\psi}_{j},\forall j\end{subarray}}\quad

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\mathfrak{Re}\{(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{j}+{\rho}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j})^{H}\boldsymbol{\psi}_{j}\}

(45a)

s.t.

\displaystyle|\psi_{j,n}|=1,~\forall j,~\forall n,

(45b)

whose optimal solution of
\boldsymbol{\psi}_{j}
can be obtained by

\boldsymbol{\psi}_{j}^{\star}=e^{\jmath\angle(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{j}+{\rho}\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j})}.

(46)

III-D
.3
Update
\boldsymbol{\xi}_{j}

With fixed
\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}
and
\boldsymbol{\psi}_{j}
, we can update the dual variable
\boldsymbol{\xi}_{j}
by

\boldsymbol{\xi}_{j}:=\boldsymbol{\xi}_{j}+\rho(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}-\boldsymbol{\psi}_{j}),

(47)

which is introduced in the standard ADMM method
[
49
]
.

Finally, the ADMM based passive beamforming design algorithm is summarized in Algorithm
1
. We can obtain the solution of problem (
30
) in an iterative manner by alternately updating
\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}
,
\boldsymbol{\psi}_{j}
, and
\boldsymbol{\xi}_{j}
until convergence.

V
Conclusion

In this paper, we considered a practical RIS-assisted multi-cell wireless network and investigated to maximize the sum-rate by jointly optimizing the BS-RIS-user association, the active beamforming at BSs, as well as the passive beamforming of RIS.
An efficient joint association and beamforming optimization algorithm exploiting FP, MM and ADMM methods was developed to solve this complicated non-convex problem.
Simulation results demonstrated the effectiveness and the performance improvement of our proposed algorithm.
To be specific, the deployment of RIS with the proposed joint association and beamforming design algorithm can expand the cell coverage, facilitate the load balancing between BSs, and consequently promotes the performance of cellular network.
For the future studies, it would be significative to extend the proposed joint BS-RIS-user association and beamforming design architecture to multi-RIS assisted multi-cell multi-user systems. Moreover, the fairness among users is also a worth studying issue as the extended research directions.

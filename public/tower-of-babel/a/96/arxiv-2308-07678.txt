The infimum values of the probability functions for some infinitely divisible distributions motivated by Chvátal’s theorem

Abstract

1
Introduction

Let
B(n,p)
denote a binomial random variable with parameters
n
and
p
. Janson
[
6
]
introduced the following conjecture suggested by Vašk Chvátal.

Conjecture 1
(Chvátal). For any fixed
n\geq 2
, as
m
ranges over
\{0,\ldots,n\}
, the probability
q_{m}:=P(B(n,m/n)\leq m)
is the smallest when

m
is closest to
\frac{2n}{3}
.

Chvátal’s conjecture has applications in machine learning (see Doerr
[
2
]
, Greenberg and Mohri
[
3
]
and the references therein). Janson
[
6
]
showed that Conjecture 1 holds for large
n
. Barabesi et al.
[
1
]
and Sun
[
11
]
proved that Conjecture 1 is true for general
n\geq 2
. Hereafter, we call Conjecture 1 by Chvátal’s theorem.

Motivated by Chvátal’s theorem, Li et al.
[
9
]
considered the infimum value problem on the probability that a random variable is not more than its expectation, when its distribution is the Poisson distribution, the geometric distribution or the Pascal distribution, where as to the Pascal distribution, only some partial results have been obtained. Sun et al.
[
12
]
investigated the corresponding infimum value problem for the Gamma distribution among other things. Li et al.
[
8
]
studied the infimum value problem for the Weibull distribution and the Pareto distribution. Guo et al.
[
4
]
considered the infimum value of the probability
P(X\leq E[X])
, where
X
is a negative binomial random variable, and gave an affirmative answer to the conjecture on the Pascal distribution posed in
[
9
]
.

In this paper, we consider the infimum value of the probability
P(X\leq\kappa E[X])
, where
\kappa
is a positive real number, and
X
is a random variable whose distribution belongs to some infinitely divisible distributions including the inverse Gaussian, log-normal, Gumbel and logistic distributions in Section 2 and Section 3. Before presenting the main results, we give a remark.

Remark 1.1
Let
X_{\alpha}
be a random variable with finite expectation
E[X_{\alpha}]
, where
\alpha
stands for some parameter in the distribution of
X_{\alpha}
, and it is a real number or a vector in
\mathbf{R}^{n}
for some positive integer
n\geq 2
. We have the following two motivations to study
\inf_{\alpha}P(X_{\alpha}\leq\kappa E[X_{\alpha}])
:

•

From
\inf_{\alpha}P(X_{\alpha}\leq\kappa E[X_{\alpha}])
, we can get
\sup_{\alpha}P(X_{\alpha}>\kappa E[X_{\alpha}])
. Obviously, if we wish the probability
P(X_{\alpha}>\kappa E[X_{\alpha}])
is as large as possible, we should consider
\sup_{\alpha}P(X_{\alpha}>\kappa E[X_{\alpha}])
or equivalently
\inf_{\alpha}P(X_{\alpha}\leq\kappa E[X_{\alpha}])
. Based on this observation, we wish that our work on this topic may find some applications in machine learning, statistics, finance and economics etc.

•

Assume that
X_{\alpha}
is nonnegative and denote by
\mu_{\alpha}
the distribution of
X_{\alpha}
. If
\inf_{\alpha}P(X_{\alpha}\leq\kappa E[X_{\alpha}])=\beta>0
, then for any
\alpha
, we have

\displaystyle\mu_{\alpha}([0,\kappa E[X_{\alpha}])\geq\beta,

which tells us that the family of the distributions
\{\mu_{\alpha}\}
possesses a kind of measure concentration phenomenon.

2
Inverse Gaussian distribution

Let
X_{\mu,\lambda}
be an inverse Gaussian random variable with parameters
\mu
and
\lambda

(\mu>0,\lambda>0).
By
[
7
, Chapter 27]
, we know that
the density function of
X_{\mu,\lambda}
is given by

f_{\mu,\lambda}(x)=\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{2\pi x^{3}}}\exp\left(-\frac{\lambda(x-\mu)^{2}}{2{\mu}^{2}x}\right),\ \ x>0,

and
E[X_{\mu,\lambda}]=\mu
. Then, for any given real number
\kappa>0
, by
[
7
, Chapter 27]
, we know that

\displaystyle P(X_{\mu,\lambda}\leq\kappa E[X_{\mu,\lambda}])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\kappa\mu}\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{2\pi x^{3}}}\exp\left(-\frac{\lambda(x-\mu)^{2}}{2{\mu}^{2}x}\right)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Phi\left(\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{\kappa\mu}}(\kappa-1)\right)+\exp\left(\frac{2\lambda}{\mu}\right)\Phi\left(-\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{\kappa\mu}}(\kappa+1)\right).

Hereafter,
\Phi(\cdot)
stands for the distribution function of the standard normal distribution.

Denote
x:=\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{\mu}}
. Then

P(X_{\mu,\lambda}\leq\kappa E[X_{\mu,\lambda}])=\Phi\left(\sqrt{\frac{1}{\kappa}}(\kappa-1)x\right)+e^{2x^{2}}\Phi\left(-\sqrt{\frac{1}{\kappa}}(\kappa+1)x\right).

Define a function

\displaystyle g_{\kappa}(x):=\Phi\left(\sqrt{\frac{1}{\kappa}}(\kappa-1)x\right)+e^{2x^{2}}\Phi\left(-\sqrt{\frac{1}{\kappa}}(\kappa+1)x\right),\ x>0.

(2.1)

The main result of this section is

Theorem 2.1

(i)
If
\kappa\leq 1
, then

\displaystyle\inf\limits_{x\in\left(0,\infty\right)}g_{\kappa}\left(x\right)=\lim_{x\to\infty}g_{\kappa}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}0,&\mbox{if}\ \kappa<1,\\
\frac{1}{2},&\mbox{if}\ \kappa=1.\end{array}\right.

(ii)
If
\kappa>1
, then

\displaystyle\min_{x\in\left(0,\infty\right)}g_{\kappa}\left(x\right)=g_{\kappa}\left(x_{0}(\kappa)\right),

where
x_{0}(\kappa)
is the unique zero point of the function

\displaystyle h_{\kappa}(x):=2\int^{\infty}_{\sqrt{\frac{1}{\kappa}}(\kappa+1)x}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}t^{2}\right)dt-\sqrt{\frac{1}{\kappa}}\frac{\exp(-\frac{{(\kappa+1)}^{2}}{2\kappa}x^{2})}{x},\ x\in(0,\infty).

(2.3)

Proof.
By taking the derivative of the function
g_{\kappa}(x)
defined in (
2.1
) and in virtue of (
2.3
), we have

\displaystyle g_{\kappa}^{{}^{\prime}}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{1}{\kappa}}(\kappa-1)\Phi^{\prime}\left(\sqrt{\frac{1}{\kappa}}(\kappa-1)x\right)+4xe^{2x^{2}}\Phi\left(-\sqrt{\frac{1}{\kappa}}(\kappa+1)x\right)

(2.4)

\displaystyle-\sqrt{\frac{1}{\kappa}}(\kappa+1)e^{2x^{2}}\Phi^{\prime}\left(-\sqrt{\frac{1}{\kappa}}(\kappa+1)x\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4xe^{2x^{2}}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\sqrt{\frac{1}{\kappa}}(\kappa+1)x}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}t^{2}\right)dt-2\sqrt{\frac{1}{2\pi\kappa}}\exp\left(-\frac{{(\kappa-1)}^{2}}{2\kappa}x^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2xe^{2x^{2}}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\left[2\int_{\sqrt{\frac{1}{\kappa}}(\kappa+1)x}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}t^{2}\right)dt-\sqrt{\frac{1}{\kappa}}\frac{\exp\left(-\frac{{(\kappa+1)}^{2}}{2\kappa}x^{2}\right)}{x}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2xe^{2x^{2}}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}h_{\kappa}(x).

By taking the derivative of the function
h_{\kappa}(x),

we get that

\displaystyle h_{\kappa}^{{}^{\prime}}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{1}{\kappa}}\exp\left(-\frac{(\kappa+1)^{2}}{2\kappa}x^{2}\right)\left(\frac{1}{\kappa}-\kappa+\frac{1}{x^{2}}\right)

(2.5)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{1}{\kappa}}\exp\left(-\frac{(\kappa+1)^{2}}{2\kappa}x^{2}\right)\varphi_{\kappa}(x),

where
\varphi_{\kappa}(x)=\frac{1}{\kappa}-\kappa+\frac{1}{x^{2}}.

(i)
If
\kappa\leq 1
, then
\varphi_{\kappa}(x)\geq\frac{1}{x^{2}}>0
for any
x\in\left(0,\infty\right)
. Thus, we have
h_{\kappa}^{{}^{\prime}}(x)>0,\ \ \forall x\in(0,\infty),
which implies that the function
h_{\kappa}(x)
is strictly increasing on interval
(0,\infty)
. We have

\displaystyle\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}h_{\kappa}\left(x\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\left(2\int_{\sqrt{\frac{1}{\kappa}}(\kappa+1)x}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}t^{2}\right)dt-\sqrt{\frac{1}{\kappa}}\frac{\exp(-\frac{{(\kappa+1)}^{2}}{2\kappa}x^{2})}{x}\right)=0.

Thus for any
x\in(0,\infty)
,

h_{\kappa}(x)<0
. Then by (
2.4
), we get

g_{\kappa}^{{}^{\prime}}\left(x\right)<0,\ \forall x\in(0,\infty).

Therefore,
g_{\kappa}\left(x\right)
is a strictly decreasing function on interval
\left(0,\infty\right)
and thus

\displaystyle\inf_{x\in(0,\infty)}g_{\kappa}(x)=\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}g_{\kappa}(x)

\displaystyle=\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\Phi\left(\sqrt{\frac{1}{\kappa}}(\kappa-1)x\right)+\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}e^{2x^{2}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\sqrt{\frac{1}{\kappa}}(\kappa+1)x}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{t^{2}}{2}\right)dt

\displaystyle=\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\Phi\left(\sqrt{\frac{1}{\kappa}}(\kappa-1)x\right)+\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{2x^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{{\left(u+\sqrt{\frac{1}{\kappa}}(\kappa+1)x\right)}^{2}}{2}\right)du

\displaystyle=\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\Phi\left(\sqrt{\frac{1}{\kappa}}(\kappa-1)x\right)+\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\left(u^{2}+\frac{2(\kappa+1)}{\sqrt{\kappa}}xu+\frac{({\kappa-1})^{2}}{\kappa}x^{2}\right)\right)du

\displaystyle=\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\Phi\left(\sqrt{\frac{1}{\kappa}}(\kappa-1)x\right)

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&\mbox{if}\ \kappa<1,\\
\frac{1}{2},&\mbox{if}\ \kappa=1.\end{array}\right.

(ii)
If
\kappa>1
, then by
h^{{}^{\prime}}_{\kappa}(x)=0
, i.e.,
\varphi_{\kappa}(x)=\frac{1}{\kappa}-\kappa+\frac{1}{x^{2}}=0
, we get
x=\sqrt{\frac{\kappa}{\kappa^{2}-1}}.
Then, by (
2.5
), we know that

h^{{}^{\prime}}_{\kappa}(x)>0
for any
x\in(0,\sqrt{\frac{\kappa}{\kappa^{2}-1}})
and
h^{{}^{\prime}}_{\kappa}(x)<0
for any
x\in(\sqrt{\frac{\kappa}{\kappa^{2}-1}},\infty)
. It follows that the function
h_{\kappa}(x)
is strictly increasing on
(0,\sqrt{\frac{\kappa}{\kappa^{2}-1}})
and strictly decreasing on
(\sqrt{\frac{\kappa}{\kappa^{2}-1}},\infty)
. Furthermore, by the fact that
\lim_{x\rightarrow 0+}h_{\kappa}(x)=-\infty<0
and
\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}h_{\kappa}(x)=0
, we know that the continuous function
h_{\kappa}(x)
has a unique zero point
x_{0}(\kappa)\in(0,\sqrt{\frac{\kappa}{\kappa^{2}-1}})
.
It follows that
h_{\kappa}(x)<0
for any
x\in(0,x_{0}(\kappa))
and
h_{\kappa}(x)>0
for any
x\in(x_{0}(\kappa),\infty)
.

Hence, by (
2.4
), we get that
g^{{}^{\prime}}_{\kappa}(x)<0
on
(0,x_{0}(\kappa))
and
g^{{}^{\prime}}_{\kappa}(x)>0
on
(x_{0}(\kappa),\infty)
. It follows that

\min_{x\in(0,\infty)}g_{\kappa}(x)=g_{\kappa}(x_{0}(\kappa)).

The proof is complete.

Remark 2.2

(i) For any
\kappa>1,\mu>0,\lambda>0
, it is easy to know that

P(X_{\mu,\lambda}\leq\kappa E[X_{\mu,\lambda}])>P(X_{\mu,\lambda}\leq E[X_{\mu,\lambda}])>\frac{1}{2},

where the second inequality follows from the proof of Theorem
2.1
. Then
for any
\kappa>1
, we have

\displaystyle\min_{x\in\left(0,\infty\right)}g_{\kappa}\left(x\right)=g_{\kappa}\left(x_{0}(\kappa)\right)>\frac{1}{2}.

(ii) The above analysis shows that there is an interesting phase transition phenomenon
in the infimum value problem for the inverse Gaussian distribution. The critical point is
\kappa=1
.

3
Log-normal, Gumbel and Logistic distributions

3.1
Log-normal distribution

Let
X_{\mu,\sigma}
be a log-normal random variable with parameters
\mu
and
\sigma

(\mu\in\mathbb{R},\sigma>0).
By
[
7
, Chapter 22]
, we know that the density function of
X_{\mu,\sigma}
is

f_{\mu,\sigma}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma x}\exp\left(-\frac{(\ln x-\mu)^{2}}{2{\sigma}^{2}}\right),\ \ x>0,

and the expectation is
E[X_{\mu,\sigma}]=\exp(\mu+\frac{{\sigma}^{2}}{2})
.
Then, for any given real number
\kappa>0,
by
[
7
, (22.1.2)]
, we have

\displaystyle P(X_{\mu,\sigma}\leq\kappa E[X_{\mu,\sigma}])=\Phi\left(\frac{\ln\left(\kappa e^{\mu+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}}\right)-\mu}{\sigma}\right)=\Phi\left(\frac{\ln\kappa}{\sigma}+\frac{\sigma}{2}\right),

which shows that
P(X_{\mu,\sigma}\leq\kappa E[X_{\mu,\sigma}])
is independent of
\mu.

Define a function

g_{\kappa}(\sigma):=\Phi\left(\frac{\ln\kappa}{\sigma}+\frac{\sigma}{2}\right),\ \sigma>0.

The main result of this section is

Proposition 3.1

(i)
If
\kappa\leq 1
, then

\displaystyle\inf\limits_{\sigma\in(0,\infty)}g_{\kappa}(\sigma)=\lim_{\sigma\to 0+}g_{\kappa}(\sigma)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}0,&\mbox{if}\ \kappa<1,\\
\frac{1}{2},&\mbox{if}\ \kappa=1;\end{array}\right.

(ii)
If
\kappa>1
, then

\displaystyle\min_{\sigma\in\left(0,\infty\right)}g_{\kappa}\left(\sigma\right)=g_{\kappa}\left(\sqrt{2\ln\kappa}\right)=\Phi(\sqrt{2\ln\kappa})>\frac{1}{2}.

(3.4)

Proof.
Define a function

\displaystyle h_{\kappa}(\sigma):=\frac{\ln\kappa}{\sigma}+\frac{\sigma}{2},\ \sigma>0.

(3.5)

Then
g_{\kappa}(\sigma)=\Phi(h_{\kappa}(\sigma)).
Therefore, in order to prove this proposition, it is enough to investigate the value of
h_{\kappa}(\sigma)
.

If
\kappa\leq 1,
then
\ln\kappa\leq 0.
Thus, by (
3.5
), we have
h^{{}^{\prime}}_{\kappa}(\sigma)=-\frac{\ln\kappa}{\sigma^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}>0
, which implies that
h_{\kappa}(\sigma)
is a strictly increasing function of
\sigma
. Thus,

\displaystyle\inf\limits_{\sigma\in\left(0,\infty\right)}h_{\kappa}\left(\sigma\right)=\lim_{\sigma\to 0+}h_{\kappa}(\alpha)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}-\infty,&\mbox{if}\ \kappa<1,\\
0,&\mbox{if}\ \kappa=1,\end{array}\right.

It follows that (
3.1
) holds.

If
\kappa>1,
then
\ln\kappa>0.
If
h^{{}^{\prime}}_{\kappa}(\sigma)=-\frac{\ln\kappa}{\sigma^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}=0
, then
\sigma=\sqrt{2\ln\kappa}.
It is easy to check that the function
h_{\kappa}(\sigma)
is strictly decreasing on
(0,\sqrt{2\ln\kappa})
and strictly increasing on
(\sqrt{2\ln\kappa},\infty)
. Thus,

\min_{\sigma\in(0,\infty)}h_{\kappa}(\sigma)=h_{\kappa}(\sqrt{2\ln\kappa})=\sqrt{2\ln\kappa}.

It follows that (
3.4
) holds. The proof is complete.

3.2
Gumbel distribution

Let
X_{\mu,\beta}
be a Gumbel random variable with parameters
\mu
and
\beta

(\mu\in\mathbb{R},\beta>0)
. By
[
10
]
, the density function of
X_{\mu,\beta}
is given by

f_{\mu,\beta}(x)=\frac{1}{\beta}e^{-z-e^{-z}},\ z=\frac{x-\mu}{\beta},\ x\in\mathbb{R}

and its expectation is
E[X_{\mu,\beta}]=\mu+\beta\gamma,
where
\gamma
is the Euler’s constant. Then, for any given real number
\kappa>0,
we have

P(X_{\mu,\beta}\leq\kappa E[X_{\mu,\beta}])=e^{-e^{-\frac{\kappa{(\mu+\beta\gamma)}-\mu}{\beta}}}=e^{-e^{-\frac{(\kappa-1)\mu}{\beta}-\kappa\gamma}}.

Let
x:=\frac{\mu}{\beta}
. Then

P(X_{\mu,\beta}\leq\kappa E[X_{\mu,\beta}])=e^{-e^{-[(\kappa-1)x+\kappa\gamma]}}.

Define a function

\displaystyle g_{\kappa}(x):=e^{-e^{-[(\kappa-1)x+\kappa\gamma]}},\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}.

(3.1)

The main result of this section is

Proposition 3.2

(i)
If
\kappa<1
, then

\displaystyle\inf\limits_{x\in\mathbb{R}}g_{\kappa}\left(x\right)=\lim_{x\to\infty}g_{\kappa}(x)=0;

(ii)
If
\kappa>1
, then

\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}}g_{\kappa}\left(x\right)=\lim_{x\to-\infty}g_{\kappa}(x)=0;

(iii)
If
\kappa=1,
then

g_{\kappa}(x)\equiv e^{-e^{-\gamma}}>\frac{1}{2}.

Proof.

Define a function

\displaystyle h_{\kappa}(x):=(\kappa-1)x+\kappa\gamma,\ x\in\mathbb{R}.

Then

g_{\kappa}(x)=e^{-e^{-h_{\kappa}(x)}},\ x\in\mathbb{R}.

By taking the derivative of
g_{\kappa}(x)
, we have

\displaystyle g^{{}^{\prime}}_{\kappa}(x)=(\kappa-1)e^{-h_{\kappa}(x)}e^{-e^{-h_{\kappa}(x)}}.

If
\kappa<1,
then
g^{{}^{\prime}}_{\kappa}(x)<0,
which implies that
g_{\kappa}(x)
is a strictly decreasing function of
x.
Then

\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}}g_{\kappa}\left(x\right)=\lim_{x\to-\infty}g_{\kappa}(x)=0.

If
\kappa>1,
then
g^{{}^{\prime}}_{\kappa}(x)>0.
Thus,
g_{\kappa}(x)
is a strictly increasing function of
x
and

\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}}g_{\kappa}(x)=\lim_{x\rightarrow-\infty}g_{\kappa}(x)=0.

If
\kappa=1,
then by (
3.1
), we get that
g_{\kappa}(x)\equiv e^{-e^{-\gamma}}>\frac{1}{2}
. The proof is complete.

3.3
Logistic distribution

Let
X_{\mu,\beta}
be a logistic random variable with parameters
\mu
and
\beta

(\mu\in\mathbb{R},\beta>0)
. By
[
7
, Chapter 21]
, we know that the distribution function of
X_{\mu,\beta}
is given by

P(X\leq x)=\frac{1}{1+e^{-\frac{x-\mu}{\beta}}},\ x\in\mathbb{R},

and the expectation of
X_{\mu,\beta}
is
E[X]=\mu
. Then, for any given real number
\kappa>0,
we have

P(X_{\mu,\beta}\leq\kappa E[X_{\mu,\beta}])=\frac{1}{1+e^{-\frac{\kappa\mu-\mu}{\beta}}}.

Let
y:=\frac{\mu}{\beta}
. Then

P(X_{\mu,\beta}\leq\kappa E[X_{\mu,\beta}])=\frac{1}{1+e^{-(\kappa-1)y}}.

Define a function

\displaystyle g_{\kappa}(y):=\frac{1}{1+e^{-(\kappa-1)y}},\ \ y\in\mathbb{R}.

(3.1)

The main result of this section is

Proposition 3.3

(i)
If
\kappa<1
, then

\displaystyle\inf\limits_{y\in\mathbb{R}}g_{\kappa}\left(y\right)=\lim_{y\to\infty}g_{\kappa}(y)=0;

(ii)
If
\kappa>1
, then

\inf_{y\in\mathbb{R}}g_{\kappa}\left(y\right)=\lim_{y\to-\infty}g_{\kappa}(y)=0;

(iii)
If
\kappa=1,
then

g_{\kappa}(y)\equiv\frac{1}{2}.

Proof.
By the definition
g_{\kappa}(y)
in (
3.1
), we have

g^{{}^{\prime}}_{\kappa}(y)=\frac{(\kappa-1)e^{-(\kappa-1)y}}{(1+e^{-(\kappa-1)y})^{2}},\ \ y\in\mathbb{R}.

If
\kappa<1,
then
g^{{}^{\prime}}_{\kappa}(y)<0
, which implies that the function
g_{\kappa}(y)
is strictly decreasing. Then

\inf_{y\in\mathbb{R}}g_{\kappa}(y)=\lim_{y\rightarrow\infty}g_{\kappa}(y)=0.

If
\kappa>1,
then
g^{{}^{\prime}}_{\kappa}(y)>0.
Thus,
g_{\kappa}(y)
is a strictly increasing function of
y
and

\inf_{y\in\mathbb{R}}g_{\kappa}(y)=\lim_{y\rightarrow-\infty}g_{\kappa}(y)=0.

If
\kappa=1,
then
g_{\kappa}(y)\equiv\frac{1}{2}
by (
3.1
). The proof is complete.

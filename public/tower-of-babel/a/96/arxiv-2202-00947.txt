1.
Introduction

The Hahn–Banach extension theorem is one of the most long-standing topics in functional analysis. Many illustrious mathematicians including Lawrence Narici, Edward Beckenstein, A. R. Halbrook, Mart Poldvere, Dirk Werner, and Eve Oja have contributed to the understanding of this continuously-discussed theorem. The reader can follow the link in
[
MO
]
to obtain an overview of the importance of this theorem.
This paper follows the roadmap of the unique existence of norm-preserving extensions. For a subspace
Y
of
X

X
is unique for every
y^{*}\in Y^{*}
, the subspace is said to have
property-
(U)
(see
[
P
]
). A slightly weaker property than that stated above is given as follows.

We say
Y
has
property-
(wU)
whenever
y^{*}\in Y^{*}
satisfying
\|y^{*}\|=y^{*}(y_{0})
for some
y_{0}\in S_{Y}
has a unique norm-preserving extension to
X
. The property-
(wU)
is known as the
weak Hahn–Banach extension property
(see
[
LA
]
) in the literature; we maintain similarities with this property while referring to the other properties along with it. We continue to study the uniqueness of the Hahn–Banach extension.
In a recent paper (
[
DPR
]
), the property-
(SU)
was extensively studied. A potpourri presentation of the said property led us to further strengthen this property. In this paper, we focus on property-
(wU)
, property-
(HB)
, and property-
(k-U)
.

1.2.
A short background of this work

The smoothness of a (non-zero) vector
x
in a Banach space is related to the uniqueness of the functional in its state space
S(X^{*},x)
. Property-
(U)
(and property-
(wU)
) extends this notion to subspaces. If there exists a linear Hahn–Banach extension operator
S:Y^{*}\rightarrow X^{*}
(i.e.,
Y
is an ideal in
X
), property-
(U)
ensures the uniqueness of the existence of such an
S
and also establishes an embedding of
Y^{*}
into
X^{*}
; in this case,
X^{*}=Y^{\#}\bigoplus Y^{\perp}
, a vector space decomposition. Note that property-
(HB)
ensures that the corresponding projection is bi-contractive. It is relevant that property-
(U)
is not invariant under isometry.

We also extend our study to cases in which a subspace
Y
fails to have property-
(U)
, but the dimension of the set
HB(y^{*})
has a finite upper bound for
y^{*}\in Y^{*}
, known as property-
(k-U)
, as defined in Definition
1.1.4
. If
Y
is a subspace of
X
, then

Y
has property-
(U)
in
X

\Longleftrightarrow

Y^{\perp}
is a Chebyshev subspace of
X^{*}

[
P
]
.

X^{*}
is a strictly convex
\Longleftrightarrow
every subspace of
X
has property-
(U)

[
T
,
F
]
.

The
k
-valued analogues of these cases are as follows.

Theorem 1.2.1
.

[
X
]

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
Y
be a subspace of
X
.

(a)

Y
has property-
(k-U)
in
X
if and only if
Y^{\perp}
is a
k
-Chebyshev subspace of

.

(b)

X^{*}
is

-strictly convex if and only if every subspace of

has property-
(k-U)
in

.

In this case, (property-

), the corresponding notion of smoothness is multismooth, as discussed in
[

]
.
It is clear from the definition that an

has property-

in its bi-dual. By contrast, no subspace of

2.
On weakly Hahn–Banach smoothness

In this section, we study property-
(wU)
. We begin with some characterizations of property-
(wU)
. Let us recall that for
x^{*}\in X^{*}
, a hyperplane
(x^{*})^{-1}\left(0\right)
is proximinal if and only if
x^{*}
attains its norm in the unit sphere of
X
.

2.1.
Characterizations

Theorem 2.1.1
.

Let
Y
be a subspace of the Banach space
X
. Then, the following are equivalent.

(a)

Y
has property-
(wU)
in
X
.

(b)

Every proximinal subspace
Z\subseteq Y
,
Y/Z
has property-
(wU)
in
X/Z
.

(c)

P_{Y^{\perp}}(x^{*})=\{0\}
for
x^{*}\in X^{*}
satisfying
x^{*}(y)=\|x^{*}\|
, for some
y\in S_{Y}
.

Proof.

(a)\Rightarrow(b)
. Suppose
Y
has property-
(wU)
in
X
, and
Z
is proximinal in
Y
. Let
F\in(Y/Z)^{*}
be a norm attaining functional. That is, there exists
y+Z\in Y/Z,\|y+Z\|=1
such that
F(y+Z)=\|F\|
. Considering
F
as an element in
Z^{\perp}_{Y^{*}}
,
y^{*}
, say, is clearly norm-attaining at some
y+z\in S_{X}
, for some
z\in Z
. Now,
y^{*}
has a unique norm-preserving extension to
X
; let us call it
x^{*}
. Since
x^{*}\in Z^{\perp}_{X^{*}}
, we may identify
x^{*}
to some
\widetilde{F}\in(X/Z)^{*}
with
\|F\|=\|y^{*}\|=\|x^{*}\|=\|\widetilde{F}\|
. To prove the uniqueness of
\widetilde{F}
, let us assume
G\in(X/Z)^{*}
such that
G|_{Y/Z}=F
. Clearly,
G\in Z^{\perp}_{X^{*}}
, which we call
w^{*}
. Then,
w^{*}|_{Y}=y^{*}
and
\|w^{*}\|=\|y^{*}\|
. Hence, we have
w^{*}=x^{*}
, which proves the necessity of the condition.

(b)\Rightarrow(a)
. Let us assume that
Y
does not have property-
(wU)
in
X
. Then, there exists
y^{*}\in S_{Y^{*}}
such that
y^{*}
attains its norm on
B_{Y}
and distinct
x_{1}^{*},x_{2}^{*}\in HB(y^{*})
. Let
Z=\ker(y^{*})
; then,
Z
is proximinal in
Y
. Suppose
y_{0}\in B_{Y}
is such that
y^{*}(y_{0})=\|y^{*}\|
, then
\|y_{0}+Z\|=d(y_{0},Z)=|y^{*}(y_{0})|=1
. We can now identify
y^{*},x_{1}^{*},x_{2}^{*}
in the corresponding quotient spaces, i.e.,
Z^{\perp}_{Y^{*}}
and
Z^{\perp}_{X^{*}}
, respectively. If we define
F=y^{*}
and
G_{1},G_{2}
as
x_{1}^{*},x_{2}^{*}
, respectively, then clearly it is a contradiction to the assumption that
Y/Z
has property-
(wU)
in
X/Z
.

(a)\Rightarrow(c).
Let
0\neq y^{\perp}\in P_{Y^{\perp}}(x^{*})
for some
x^{*}\in X^{*}
, as stated in
(c)
. Then,
x^{*},x^{*}-y^{\perp}\in HB(x^{*}|_{Y})
. This contradicts the fact that
Y
has property-
(wU)
in
X
.

(c)\Rightarrow(a).
If
y^{*}\in S_{Y^{*}}
such that
|y^{*}(y)|=1
, for some
y\in S_{Y}
, then, any
x^{*}\in HB(y^{*})
, we have that
x^{*}(y)=1
. Hence, for distinct
x_{1}^{*},x_{2}^{*}\in HB(y^{*})
, we obtain
x_{1}^{*}-x_{2}^{*}\in P_{Y^{\perp}}(x_{1}^{*})
, which contradicts our assumption on
(c)
.
∎

2.2.
A variant of the 3-space problem

We now focus on the main result of this section. Let us recall the characterization
[
LA
, Theorem 2.2]
for property-
(wU)
of a subspace
Y
in
X
.

Theorem 2.2.1
.

Let
Y,Z
be the subspaces of
X
such that
Z\subseteq Y\subseteq X
. If
Z
is a semi-M-ideal in
X
and
Y/Z
has property-
(wU)
in
X/Z
, then
Y
has property-
(wU)
in
X
.

Proof.

Let us choose
x_{0}\in S_{X}
,
y_{0}\in S_{Y}
and
\varepsilon>0
.

Claim:

\exists~R\geq 1
such that
Y\cap B(x_{0}+Ry_{0},R+\varepsilon)\cap B(x_{0}-Ry_{0},R+\varepsilon)\neq\emptyset.

Now, if
x_{0}\in Z
, then the aforementioned intersection holds true for any
R>0
and clearly contains
x_{0}
. Moreover, if
y_{0}\in Z
, then the aforementioned intersection is non-empty for
R=1
(see
[
LA
, Pg.98]
). Hence, without loss of generality, we assume that
x_{0},y_{0}\notin Z
.

Define
p=\frac{1}{\|x_{0}+Z\|}
,
q=\frac{1}{\|y_{0}+Z\|}
and choose
\eta>0
such that
\eta<\frac{\varepsilon p}{q+2p}
.

From
[
LA
, Theorem 2.2]
, there exists
r\geq 1
such that,

Y/Z\cap B(px_{0}+Z+rqy_{0}+Z,r+\eta)\cap B(px_{0}+Z-rqy_{0}+Z,r+\eta)\neq\phi.

That is,

Y/Z\cap B(px_{0}+rqy_{0}+Z,r+\eta)\cap B(px_{0}-rqy_{0}+Z,r+\eta)\neq\phi.

Let
r^{\prime}>\max\{\frac{p}{q},r\}
. Since
r^{\prime}>r
, we have

Y/Z\cap B(px_{0}+r^{\prime}qy_{0}+Z,r^{\prime}+\eta)\cap B(px_{0}-r^{\prime}qy_{0}+Z,r^{\prime}+\eta)\neq\phi.

Hence,
Y/Z\cap B(x_{0}+\frac{r^{\prime}q}{p}y_{0}+Z,\frac{r^{\prime}}{p}+\frac{\eta}{p})\cap B(x_{0}-\frac{r^{\prime}q}{p}y_{0}+Z,\frac{r^{\prime}}{p}+\frac{\eta}{p})\neq\phi
.

As
q\geq 1
, we have

Y/Z\cap B(x_{0}+\frac{r^{\prime}q}{p}y_{0}+Z,\frac{qr^{\prime}}{p}+\frac{q\eta}{p})\cap B(x_{0}-\frac{r^{\prime}q}{p}y_{0}+Z,\frac{qr^{\prime}}{p}+\frac{q\eta}{p})\neq\phi
.

Let
y+Z\in Y/Z\cap B(x_{0}+\frac{r^{\prime}q}{p}y_{0}+Z,\frac{qr^{\prime}}{p}+\frac{q\eta}{p})\cap B(x_{0}-\frac{r^{\prime}q}{p}y_{0}+Z,\frac{qr^{\prime}}{p}+\frac{q\eta}{p})
, i.e.,
\|x_{0}\pm\frac{r^{\prime}q}{p}y_{0}-y+Z\|\leq\frac{qr^{\prime}}{p}+\frac{q\eta}{p}
. Let
z_{i}\in Z
,
i=1,2,
such that
\|x_{0}\pm\frac{r^{\prime}q}{p}y_{0}-y+z_{i}\|\leq\frac{qr^{\prime}}{p}+\frac{q\eta}{p}+\eta
.

Consider the balls
B_{1}=B(x_{0}+\frac{r^{\prime}q}{p}y_{0}-y,\frac{qr^{\prime}}{p}+\frac{q\eta}{p}+\eta)
and

B_{2}=B(x_{0}-\frac{r^{\prime}q}{p}y_{0}-y,\frac{qr^{\prime}}{p}+\frac{q\eta}{p}+\eta)
.

Because
B_{1}\cap B_{2}\neq\phi
and
B_{i}\cap Z\neq\phi

(i=1,2)
, from the
2
-ball property of
Z
, we obtain

\exists z\in Z\cap B\left(x_{0}+\frac{r^{\prime}q}{p}y_{0}-y,\frac{qr^{\prime}}{p}+\frac{q\eta}{p}+2\eta\right)\cap B\left(x_{0}-\frac{r^{\prime}q}{p}y_{0}-y,\frac{qr^{\prime}}{p}+\frac{q\eta}{p}+2\eta\right).

Thus,
y+z\in Y\cap B(x_{0}+\frac{r^{\prime}q}{p}y_{0},\frac{qr^{\prime}}{p}+\frac{q\eta}{p}+2\eta)\cap B(x_{0}-\frac{r^{\prime}q}{p}y_{0},\frac{qr^{\prime}}{p}+\frac{q\eta}{p}+2\eta)
. As
\frac{r^{\prime}q}{p}>1
and
\frac{q\eta}{p}+2\eta<\varepsilon
, the
claim
follows for
R\geq\frac{r^{\prime}q}{p}
.

This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 3.1.1

Let

(b)

\ell_{\infty}
.

Our next theorem characterizes property-
(SU)
in Banach spaces.

Theorem 3.1.3
.

Let
Y
be a subspace of
X
: then, the following are equivalent:

(a)

Y
has property-
(SU)
in
X
.

(b)

Y
has property-
(U)
in
X
and
Y
is an ideal in
X
.

(c)

Y^{\perp}
is Chebyshev in
X^{*}
, and the metric projection
P_{Y^{\perp}}:X^{*}\rightarrow Y^{\perp}
is a linear projection.

Proof.

(a)\Longleftrightarrow(b)
follows from
[
O
]
.

(a)\Rightarrow(c)
. Let us recall from
[
O
]
that,
(a)
holds if and only if
Y^{\#}
is a linear subspace.
Let
x_{1}^{*},x_{2}^{*}\in X^{*}
. It is sufficient to show that
P_{Y^{\perp}}(x_{1}^{*}+x_{2}^{*})=P_{Y^{\perp}}(x_{1}^{*})+P_{Y^{\perp}}(x_{2}^{*})
. Let
y_{1}^{\perp},y_{2}^{\perp}\in Y^{\perp}
such that
P_{Y^{\perp}}(x_{1}^{*})=y_{1}^{\perp}
and
P_{Y^{\perp}}(x_{2}^{*})=y_{2}^{\perp}
. Consequently, for
i=1,2

\|x_{i}^{*}-y_{i}^{\perp}\|=d(x_{i}^{*},Y^{\perp})=\|x_{i}^{*}|_{Y}\|=\|(x_{i}-y_{i}^{\perp})|_{Y}\|
. Thus,
x_{1}^{*}-y_{1}^{\perp},x_{2}^{*}-y_{2}^{\perp}\in Y^{\#}
and hence by
(a)
,
x_{1}^{*}+x_{2}^{*}-y_{1}^{\perp}-y_{2}^{\perp}\in Y^{\#}
and
\|x_{1}^{*}+x_{2}^{*}-y_{1}^{\perp}-y_{2}^{\perp}\|\leq\|x_{1}^{*}+x_{2}^{*}-y_{1}^{\perp}-y_{2}^{\perp}+y^{\perp}\|
for all
y^{\perp}\in Y^{\perp}
. Hence,
\|x_{1}^{*}+x_{2}^{*}-y_{1}^{\perp}-y_{2}^{\perp}\|\leq d(x_{1}^{*}+x_{2}^{*}-y_{1}^{\perp}-y_{2}^{\perp},Y^{\perp})=d(x_{1}^{*}+x_{2}^{*},Y^{\perp})
. Therefore,
P_{Y^{\perp}}(x_{1}^{*}+x_{2}^{*})=y_{1}^{\perp}+y_{2}^{\perp}=P_{Y^{\perp}}(x_{1}^{*})+P_{Y^{\perp}}(x_{2}^{*})
.

To prove
(c)\Rightarrow(b)
, we show that
Y^{\perp}
is the kernel of a contraction. The proof of
\|I-P_{Y^{\perp}}\|=1
remains. Let
x^{*}\in X^{*}
and
x^{*}=y^{\perp}+z^{*}
. Then,
\|z^{*}\|=\|x^{*}-y^{\perp}\|=d(x^{*},Y^{\perp})\leq\|x^{*}\|
, and hence, the result follows.
∎

Focusing on property-
(HB)
, we have the following.

Theorem 3.1.4
.

Let
Y
be a subspace of
X
. Then, the following are equivalent.

(a)

Y
has property-
(HB)
in
X
.

(b)

Y
has property-
(U)
in
X
, and there exists a projection
P:X^{*}\rightarrow Y^{\perp}
such that
\|P\|=1=\|I-P\|
.

(c)

Y^{\perp}
is Chebyshev in
X^{*}
, and the metric projection
P_{Y^{\perp}}:X^{*}\rightarrow Y^{\perp}
is a linear projection of norm-
1
.

Proof.

(a)\Longleftrightarrow(b)
. Clearly, we have
(a)\Rightarrow(b)
. By contrast, it is sufficient to show that
\|(I-P)(x^{*})\|<\|x^{*}\|
whenever
(I-P)(x^{*})\neq x^{*}
. Suppose that is not the case, then there exists
x_{0}^{*}\in X^{*}
such that
(I-P)(x_{0}^{*})\neq x_{0}^{*}
and
\|(I-P)(x_{0}^{*})\|=\|x_{0}^{*}\|
. Clearly,
\|x_{0}^{*}|_{Y}\|=\|(I-P)x_{0}^{*}|_{Y}\|\leq\|(I-P)x_{0}^{*}\|
. Let
\widetilde{x_{0}^{*}}
be a norm-preserving extension of
x_{0}^{*}|_{Y}
. Then
x_{0}^{*}-\widetilde{x_{0}^{*}}\in Y^{\perp}
; hence,
(I-P)x_{0}^{*}=(I-P)\widetilde{x_{0}^{*}}
. Consequently,
\|(I-P)x_{0}^{*}\|=\|(I-P)\widetilde{x_{0}^{*}}\|\leq\|\widetilde{x_{0}^{*}}\|=\|x_{0}^{*}|_{Y}\|
. Thus
(I-P)(x_{0}^{*}),x_{0}^{*}\in HB(x_{0}^{*}|_{Y})
, which is a contradiction.

(c)\Rightarrow(b).
If
(c)
holds, then for any
x^{*}\in X^{*}
, there exists
y^{\perp}\in Y^{\perp}
and
y^{*}\in X^{*}
such that
x^{*}=y^{\perp}+y^{*}
. Now, we have
\|y^{*}\|=\|x^{*}-y^{\perp}\|=d(x^{*},Y^{\perp})\leq\|x^{*}\|
. Hence, both
P_{Y^{\perp}}
and
I-P_{Y^{\perp}}
are of norm-
1
and
(b)
follows.

(a)\Rightarrow(c).
Clearly,
Y
has property-
(U)
in
X
. Now, if
(a)
holds, then
X^{*}=Y^{\perp}\bigoplus Y^{\#}
. Now for every
x^{*}\in X^{*}
, there exists a unique
y^{\perp}\in Y^{\perp}
and
y^{\#}\in Y^{\#}
such that
x^{*}=y^{\perp}+y^{\#}
where
\|y^{\perp}\|\leq\|x^{*}\|
. Hence,
\|x^{*}-y^{\perp}\|=\|y^{\#}\|=\|y^{\#}|_{Y}\|=\|x^{*}|_{Y}\|=d(x^{*},Y^{\perp})
. Therefore,
y^{\perp}=P_{Y^{\perp}}(x^{*})
, which completes the proof.
∎

Our next observation serves a sufficient condition for property-
(HB)
.

Proposition 3.1.5
.

Let
Y
be a subspace of
X
. If
Y^{\perp\perp}
has property-
(HB)
in
X^{**}
, then
Y
has property-
(HB)
in
X
.

Proof.

Clearly,
Y
has property-
(U)
in
X
if
Y^{\perp\perp}
has property-
(U)
in
X^{**}
. In fact, if
y^{*}\in Y^{*}
and distinct
x_{1}^{*},x_{2}^{*}\in HB(y^{*})
, then distinct
J_{X^{*}}(x_{1}^{*}),J_{X^{*}}(x_{2}^{*})\in HB(J_{Y^{*}}(y^{*}))
, where
J_{X^{*}}:X^{*}\hookrightarrow X^{***}
is the canonical embedding. It has been shown in
[
DPR
, pg. 11]
that
Y
is ideal in
X
if and only if
Y^{\perp\perp}
is ideal in
X^{**}
. Hence,
Y
has property-
(SU)
in

if
Y^{\perp\perp}
has property-
(HB)
in
X^{**}
, and we have the decomposition

.

Owing to the property-
(HB)
of
Y^{\perp\perp}
in

, there exists
Q:X^{***}\rightarrow X^{***}
, which is a bi-contractive projection with
\ker(Q)=Y^{\perp\perp\perp}
. As

has property-
(SU)
in

, there exists a projection

with
\|P\|=1
. Clearly,

.

Now, if there exists

∎

3.2.
On subspaces of tensor product spaces

We now identify a few cases mainly in the spaces of vector valued functions and tensor product spaces, where property-
(HB)
remains stable.
By
(\Omega,\Sigma,\mu)
we mean a probability space, and let
L_{p}(\mu,X)
be the Banach space consisting of all
p
th Bochner integrable functions (see
[
DU
]
for details) as defined in Section 2. It is well known that
L_{p}(\mu,X)^{*}\cong L_{q}(\mu,X^{*})
,
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
if and only if
X^{*}
has the RNP.

A routine verification of the property-
(HB)
in the proofs of
[
DPR
, Theorem 3.4, 3.6]
allows us to prove the following Theorems
3.2.1
and
3.2.2
.

Theorem 3.2.1
.

Let
Y
be a subspace of
X
, and let
X^{*}
has the RNP. Then,
L_{p}(\mu,Y)
has property-
(HB)
in
L_{p}(\mu,X)
if and only if

has property-
(HB)
in
X
, for
1<p<\infty
.

We denote the injective (projective) tensor product for Banach spaces
X
and
Z
by
X\widehat{\otimes}_{\varepsilon}Z
(
X\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}Z
) (see
[
R
]
for details). We denote the injective (projective) norm of
\varphi\in X\widehat{\otimes}_{\varepsilon}Z
(
\varphi\in X\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}Z
) by
\varepsilon(\varphi)
(
\pi(\varphi)
).
It is well known that if one of the spaces

has the RNP and one of them has the

,
\left(X\widehat{\otimes}_{\varepsilon}Z\right)^{*}\cong X^{*}\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}Z^{*}
(see
[
R
, Pg. 114]
). In the case of the projective tensor product, we have
(X\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}Z)^{*}\cong\mathcal{L}(X,Z^{*})
: the space of all bounded linear operators from

to

(see
[
R
, Pg.24]
).
Let us also recall that if

is a subspace of

, then
X\widehat{\otimes}_{\varepsilon}Y
is a subspace of
X\widehat{\otimes}_{\varepsilon}Z
; by contrast,
X\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}Y
is not necessarily a subspace of

in general.
X\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}Y
is a subspace of
X\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}Z
if

is an ideal in

(see
[

, Theorem 1.(i)]
).

A routine generalization for property-
(HB)
of
[

, Theorem 3.6]
leads to the following.

Theorem 3.2.2
.

Let

be a Banach space such that

has the RNP.
Let

be an
L_{1}
-predual space and let

be a subspace of

. Then,
E\widehat{\otimes}_{\varepsilon}Y
has property-
(HB)
in

if and only if

has property-
(HB)
in

.

It is well known that
C(K,X)\cong C(K)\widehat{\otimes}_{\varepsilon}X
for any Banach space

.

Corollary 3.2.3
.

Let

be a subspace of

, and let

has the RNP. Then,

has property-
(HB)
in

C(K,Y)
has property-
(HB)
in

.

Remark 3.2.4
.

As stated earlier, if
Y
is an ideal of a Banach space
Z
, then the projective tensor project
Y\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}X
is a subspace of
Z\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}X
. In addition, if
X
is a Banach space, then
L_{1}(\mu)\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}X\cong L_{1}(\mu,X)
(see
[
R
, Example 2.19]
). Along with
[
BR
, Example 2.3]
, one can conclude that property-
(U)
may not be stable under a projective tensor product.

However, we can identify a class of functionals in
(X\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}Y)^{*}
that still have unique norm-preserving extensions over
X\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}Z
.

Theorem 3.2.5
.

Let
X
and
Z
be Banach spaces, and
Y
be a subspace of
Z
. If
Y
has property-
(SU)
in
Z
, then every isometry in
\mathcal{L}(X,Y^{*})\cong(X\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}Y)^{*}
has a unique norm-preserving extension to
(X\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}Z)
.

Proof.

Let
S\in\mathcal{L}(X,Y^{*})
be an isometry and
T_{1},T_{2}
be two distinct norm-preserving extensions of
S
to
(X\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}Z)
. Let
x_{0}\in S_{X}
such that
T_{1}x_{0}\neq T_{2}x_{0}
. As
T_{1}|_{(X\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}Y)}=S=T_{1}|_{(X\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}Y)}
, we have
T_{1}(x_{0}\otimes y)=S(x_{0}\otimes y)=T_{2}(x_{0}\otimes y)
, that is,
T_{1}x_{0}(y)=Sx_{0}(y)=T_{2}x_{0}(y)
for all
y\in Y
. Hence,
T_{1}x_{0}
and
T_{2}x_{0}
are two linear extensions of
Sx_{0}
. Because
S
is an isometry, we have
\|Sx_{0}\|=\|x_{0}\|=1
. Now,
\|S\|=\|T_{i}\|\geq\|T_{i}x_{0}\|\geq\|Sx_{0}\|=\|x_{0}\|=1=\|S\|
,
i=1,2
. Consequently,
\|T_{1}x_{0}\|=\|T_{2}x_{0}\|=\|Sx_{0}\|
. Hence,
T_{1}x_{0}
and
T_{2}x_{0}
are two distinct norm-preserving extensions of
Sx_{0}
.
∎

We have an affirmative answer to the converse of Theorem
3.2.5
.

Theorem 3.2.6
.

Let
X
and
Z
be Banach spaces, and
Y
be a subspace of
Z
such that
(X\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}Y)
is a subspace of
(X\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}Z)
. If
(X\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}Y)
has property-
(U)
in
(X\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}Z)
, then
Y
has property-
(U)
in
Z
.

Proof.

Let
y^{*}\in S_{Y^{*}}
and distinct
z_{1}^{*},z_{2}^{*}\in HB(y^{*})
. Choose
x^{*}\in S_{X^{*}}
and define the following maps:

S(x)=x^{*}(x)y^{*}\text{ for }x\in X\mbox{~and}

T_{1}(x)=x^{*}(x)z_{1}^{*},~T_{2}(x)=x^{*}(x)z_{2}^{*}\text{ for }x\in X.

One can check that
S\in\mathcal{L}(X,Y^{*}),T_{1},T_{2}\in\mathcal{L}(X,Z^{*})
and
\|S\|=\|T_{1}\|=\|T_{2}\|
. We consider
S\in(X\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}Y)^{*}
and
T_{1},T_{2}\in(X\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}Z)^{*}
. For any

, we have

, and we obtain

. If
D
is the linear span of all simple tensors in

, then
T_{1}|_{D}=T_{2}|_{D}=S
. Based on the density of

in
(X\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}Y)
, we have
T_{1}|_{(X\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}Y)}=T_{2}|_{(X\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}Y)}=S
. Hence, distinct
T_{1},T_{2}\in HB(S)
.
∎

3.3.
On quotient spaces and higher duals of Banach spaces

Let
W,Y
be subspaces of

such that

, where

has property-
(SU)
in

; then, it is shown in

]

has property-
(SU)
in

(Y/W)^{\#}=\{w^{\perp}\in W^{\perp}_{X^{*}}:\|w^{\perp}|_{Y}\|=\|w^{\perp}\|\}
.

Theorem 3.3.1
.

Let
W,Y
be subspaces of

where

has property-
(HB)
in

, then

has property-
(HB)
in

We have that

has property-

in

and

, where

and

. It remains to show

. Since

and

, we have

. Moreover,

; we can view

, where

and

are obtained from

and

, respectively. Hence,
\|x^{*}\|\geq\|y^{\perp}\|
as
Y
has property-
(HB)
in

.
∎

The converse of Theorem
3.3.1
is true under the assumption that

is an

-ideal in

.

Theorem 3.3.2
.

-ideal in

4.
On property-
(k-U)
in Banach spaces

4.1.
Characterization

We derive a characterization for reflexive subspaces with property-
(k-U)
in Theorem
4.1.5
. Let
x\in X\setminus\{0\}
and
x^{*}\in X^{*}\setminus\{0\}
. Let us recall from Section 1 that

S(X^{*},x)=\{x^{*}\in S_{X^{*}}:x^{*}(x)=\|x\|\}
; we also define

S(X,x^{*})=\{x\in S_{X}:x^{*}(x)=\|x^{*}\|\}
.

Proposition 4.1.1
.

Let
Y
be a subspace of
X
. If
Y
is a
k
-Chebyshev subspace of
X
, then
\dim(\{S(X,y^{\perp})-x\}\cap Y)<k
for all
x\in S(X,y^{\perp})
, for all
y^{\perp}\in S_{Y^{\perp}}
.

Proof.

If possible, let
y^{\perp}\in S_{Y^{\perp}}
such that
\dim(\{S(X,y^{\perp})-x\}\cap Y)\geq k
for some
x\in S(X,y^{\perp})
. Let
y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{k}\in\{S(X,y^{\perp})-x\}\cap Y
be
k
linearly independent elements. Since
x,y_{i}+x\in S(X,y^{\perp})
,
1\leq i\leq k
, we have
\|y_{i}+x\|=1=\|x\|
. Hence, for
y\in Y
,
\|x-y\|\geq y^{\perp}(x-y)=y^{\perp}(x)=\|y^{\perp}\|=1=\|x\|
, which is true for all

. Consequently,
d(x,Y)=\|x\|=\|x+y_{i}\|
, that is,
0,-y_{i}\in P_{Y}(x)
for

. Therefore,
Y
is not a
k
-Chebyshev subspace of
X
.
∎

The converse of the aforementioned theorem is true for the proximal subspace of
X
. We first prove a similar result for the hyperplanes in
X
.

Proposition 4.1.2
.

Let
Y=\ker(x^{*})
be a proximinal hyperplane of
X
for some
x^{*}\in S_{X^{*}}
. If
Y
is not a
k
-Chebyshev subspace of
X
, then
\dim(\{S(X,x^{*})-x\}\cap Y)\geq k
for some
x\in S(X,x^{*})
.

Proof.

Let
Y
not be a
k
-Chebyshev subspace of
X
. Let
x\in X
be such that
d(x,Y)=1=\|x\|=x^{*}(x)
and
\dim(P_{Y}(x))\geq k
.

Let

be
k
linearly independent elements such that
\|y_{i}-x\|=d(x,Y)=1
,

. Then, we have
-y_{i}\in S(X,x^{*})-x
, where

. Hence, the result follows.
∎

We now generalize the aforementioned proposition for proximinal subspaces of
X
.

Proposition 4.1.3
.

Let
Y
be a proximinal subspace of
X
. If
Y
is not a
k
-Chebyshev subspace of
X
, then
\dim(\{S(X,y^{\perp})-x\}\cap Y)\geq k
for some
x\in S(X,y^{\perp})
and for some
y^{\perp}\in S_{Y^{\perp}}
.

Proof.

If
Y
is not a
k
-Chebyshev subspace of
X
, then there exists
x_{0}\in X\setminus Y
such that
Y
is not a
k
-Chebyshev subspace of

. Clearly,

is proximinal in

, and there exists
z^{*}\in S_{Z^{*}}
such that
Y=\ker(z^{*})
. From Proposition
4.1.2
, we have
\dim(\{S(Z,z^{*})-z\}\cap Y)\geq k
for some
z\in S(Z,z^{*})
. If
x^{*}
is a Hahn–Banach extension of

over

, then
\dim(\{S(X,x^{*})-z\}\cap Y)\geq k
, and the result follows.
∎

Hence, we have the following.

Theorem 4.1.4
.

Let
Y
be a proximinal subspace of
X
.
Y
is a
k
-Chebyshev subspace of
X
if and only if
\dim(\{S(X,y^{\perp})-x\}\cap Y)<k
for all
x\in S(X,y^{\perp})
and for all
y^{\perp}\in S_{Y^{\perp}}
.

We now state Theorem
1.2.1
in terms of the characterization derived in Theorem

.

Theorem 4.1.5
.

Let

be a subspace of

(b)

(a)\Longleftrightarrow(b)

.

such that
z^{*}(z_{0})=1
and
x_{i}^{*}(z_{0})=1
,
1\leq i\leq k+1
. Hence,
\dim(S(X^{*},z_{0}))\geq k
, that is,
z_{0}
is not a
k
-smooth point.
∎

In particular, we have the following.

Proposition 4.3.3
.

Let
F
be a finite-dimensional subspace of
X
. Suppose that every
x\in\emph{ext}(B_{F})
is a
k
-smooth point in

. Then, any subspace
Z
of
F
has property-
(k-U)
in

.

Proof.

Let
x\in S_{F}
, then
(x_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\subseteq\textrm{ext}(B_{F})
such that
x=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}x_{i}
for some
(\lambda_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
with
\lambda_{i}\geq 0
and
\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}=1
. Clearly, we have
S(X^{*},x)\subseteq\cap_{i=1}^{n}S(X^{*},x_{i})
. Hence, every
x\in S_{F}
is a
k
-smooth point in
X
. The result follows from Theorem
4.3.2
.
∎

We now identify some subspaces of

(k-U)
.

Theorem 4.3.4
.

Let

Proof.

Because

, we have that
\textrm{ext}(B_{F})

B_{F}=\textrm{conv}(\textrm{ext}(B_{F}))
. Let

and

|x_{i}(j)|=1

.
It follows that there exists a large

x_{i}^{\prime}

k
-smooth. Proposition
4.3.3

∎

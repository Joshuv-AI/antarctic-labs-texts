A new kind of uniqueness theorems for inverse Sturm-Liouville problems
Yuri Ashrafyan

Abstract
We prove Marchenko-type uniqueness theorems for inverse Sturm-Liouville problems. Moreover, we prove a generalization of Ambarzumyan theorem.



Keywords:

Inverse problem, Sturm-Liouville operator, uniqueness theorem, Ambarzumyan theorem

Introduction

Let us denote by
L(q,\alpha,\beta)
the Sturm-Liouville boundary value problem

\displaystyle-y^{\prime\prime}+q(x)y=\mu y,\quad x\in(0,\pi),\ \mu\in\mathbb{C},

(1.1)

\displaystyle y(0)\cot\alpha+y^{\prime}(0)=0,\qquad\alpha\in(0,\pi),

(1.2)

\displaystyle y(\pi)\cot\beta+y^{\prime}(\pi)=0,\qquad\beta\in(0,\pi),

(1.3)

where
q
is a real-valued, summable function,
q\in L^{1}_{\mathbb{R}}(0,\pi)
.
At the same time,
L(q,\alpha,\beta)
denotes the self-adjoint operator, generated by
problem (
1.1
)-(
1.3
) (see, e.g.
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
).
It is known, that under the above conditions the spectrum of operator

L(q,\alpha,\beta)
is discrete and consists of real, simple eigenvalues (see, e.g.
[
2
]
,
[
4
]
), which we denote by
\mu_{n}=\mu_{n}(q,\alpha,\beta)
,
n\geq 0
, emphasizing the dependence of
\mu_{n}
on
q
,
\alpha

and
\beta
. We assume that eigenvalues are enumerated in the increasing order, i.e.,

\mu_{0}(q,\alpha,\beta)<\mu_{1}(q,\alpha,\beta)<\dots<\mu_{n}(q,\alpha,\beta)<\dots.

Let
\varphi(x,\mu)
be a solution of equation (
1.1
), which satisfies the initial conditions

\varphi(0,\mu)=1,\quad\varphi^{\prime}(0,\mu)=-\cot\alpha.

(1.4)

The eigenvalues
\mu_{n}=\mu_{n}(q,\alpha,\beta)
,
n\geq 0
, of

L(q,\alpha,\beta)
are the solutions of equation

\varphi(\pi,\mu)\cot\beta+\varphi^{\prime}(\pi,\mu)=0.

It is easy to see that the functions
\varphi(x,\mu_{n})
,
n\geq 0
, are the eigenfunctions, corresponding to the eigenvalue
\mu_{n}
.
The squares of the
L^{2}
-norm of these eigenfunctions:

a_{n}=a_{n}(q,\alpha,\beta):=\int_{0}^{\pi}|\varphi(x,\mu_{n})|^{2}dx,\quad n\geq 0,

are called norming constants.
The eigenvalues and norming constants are called spectral data (besides these, there are other quantities, which are also called spectral data).
The inverse Sturm-Liouville problem is to reconstruct the quantities
q,\ \alpha,\ \beta
by some spectral data.

Let
L=L(q,\alpha,\beta)
and
L_{0}=L(q_{0},\alpha_{0},\beta_{0})
be two operators.
The following assertion is usually called uniqueness theorem of Marchenko

This is an exception, as in general additional information is needed in order to reconstruct the potential

Our generalization of Ambarzumyan’s theorem is as follows.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
q^{\prime}\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}(0,\pi)
.

If
\mu_{n}(q,\alpha,\pi-\alpha)=\mu_{n}(0,\alpha,\pi-\alpha)
, for all

q(x)\equiv 0

We think that Theorem

2
Preliminaries

Two operators
L=L(q,\alpha,\beta)
and
L_{0}=L(q_{0},\alpha_{0},\beta_{0})
are called isospectral, if they have the same spectra, i.e.
\mu_{n}(q,\alpha,\beta)=\mu_{n}(q_{0},\alpha_{0},\beta_{0}),\ n\geq 0
. In what follows, if a certain symbol
\gamma
denotes an object related to
L
, then
\gamma_{0}
(or
\gamma^{0}
, depending on situation) will denote a similar object related to
L_{0}
.

The problem of describing all the operators
L
isospectral with
L_{0}
first was considered by Trubowitz et al. (see
[
19
,
20
,
21
,
22
]
) for
q\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}(0,\pi)
.
The same problem was considered by Jodeit and Levitan in
[
9
]
for
q
, such that
q^{\prime}\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}(0,\pi)
.
For this aim the Gelfand-Levitan integral equation and transformation operators were used in
[
9
]
.
They construct the kernel
F(x,y)
of the integral equation as follows.
Let
c_{n},\ n\geq 0
, be arbitrary real numbers, converging to zero, as
n\rightarrow\infty
, so rapidly, that the function

F(x,y)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}c_{n}\varphi_{0}(x,\mu_{n}^{0})\varphi_{0}(y,\mu_{n}^{0})

(2.1)

is continuous and all the second order partial derivatives are also continuous.
The integral equation

K(x,y)+F(x,y)+\int^{x}_{0}K(x,t)F(t,y)dt=0,\qquad 0\leq y\leq x\leq\pi,

(2.2)

is called Gelfand-Levitan integral equation
2
2

2

Here
F(x,y)
is a kernel of integral equation (
2.2
), where
x
is a parameter,
F(x,y)
is known function and
K(x,y)
is unknown function, as functions of
y
.
.

They proved, that if
1+c_{n}a_{n}^{0}>0
, for all
n\geq 0
, then the integral equation (
2.2
) has a unique solution
K(x,y)
and the function

\varphi(x,\mu)=\varphi_{0}(x,\mu)+\int_{0}^{x}K(x,t)\varphi_{0}(t,\mu)dt

is a solution of the differential equation (
1.1
), with potential function

q(x)=q_{0}(x)+2\cfrac{d}{dx}K(x,x),

(2.3)

and
\varphi(x,\mu)
satisfies the initial conditions

\varphi(0,\mu)=1,\qquad\varphi^{\prime}(0,\mu)=-\cot\alpha,

where

\cot\alpha=\cot\alpha_{0}+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}c_{n}.

(2.4)

It means, that the function
\varphi(x,\mu)
satisfies the boundary condition (
1.2
) for all
\mu\in\mathbb{C}
.

Find
\beta\in(0,\pi)
, such that
\mu_{n}(q,\alpha,\beta)=\mu_{n}(q_{0},\alpha_{0},\beta_{0})
, for all
n\geq 0
, i.e.
\varphi(x,\mu)
should satisfy, at the point
x=\pi
, the boundary condition (
1.3
)

\varphi(\pi,\mu_{n}^{0})\cot\beta+\varphi^{\prime}(\pi,\mu_{n}^{0})=0,

for this
\beta\in(0,\pi)
.
Such
\beta
(in
[
9
]
) is being defined from the following relation

\cot\beta=\cot\beta_{0}+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\cfrac{c_{n}\varphi_{0}^{2}(\pi,\mu_{n}^{0})}{1+c_{n}a_{n}^{0}}.

(2.5)

Thus Jodeit and Levitan showed, that each admissible sequence
\{c_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
generate an isospectral operator

, where
q,\ \alpha
and
\beta
are given by the formulae (
2.3
), (
2.4
) and (
2.5

In this way they obtained all the potentials
q
, with

, having a given spectrum
\mu_{n}^{0}=\mu_{n}(q_{0},\alpha_{0},\beta_{0}),\ n\geq 0

3
Proof of Theorem
1.2

Consider operators
L_{0}=L(q_{0},\alpha_{0},\beta_{0})
and
L=L(q,\alpha_{0},\beta)
, with the set of norming constants
a_{n}^{0}=a_{n}(q_{0},\alpha_{0},\beta_{0})
and
a_{n}=a_{n}(q,\alpha_{0},\beta)
,
n\geq 0
, respectively.
It is known (see, e.g.
[
9
]
), that in this case the kernel
F(x,y)
of the integral equation (
2.2
) is

F(x,y)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\cfrac{1}{a_{n}}-\cfrac{1}{a_{n}^{0}}\right)\varphi_{0}(x,\mu_{n}^{0})\varphi_{0}(y,\mu_{n}^{0}).

(3.1)

Since by the condition of Theorem
1.2
the operators
L
and
L_{0}
are isospectral, then the formulae (
2.3
)–(
2.5
) are held.
If we compare the kernels (
2.1
) and (
3.1
), we’ll refer, that
c_{n}=\cfrac{1}{a_{n}}-\cfrac{1}{a_{n}^{0}}
.
So the formulae (
2.4
) and (
2.5
) will become

\cot\alpha=\cot\alpha_{0}+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\cfrac{1}{a_{n}}-\cfrac{1}{a_{n}^{0}}\right),

(3.2)

\cot\beta=\cot\beta_{0}+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(a_{n}^{0}-a_{n})\cfrac{\varphi^{2}_{0}(\pi,\mu_{n}^{0})}{(a_{n}^{0})^{2}}.

(3.3)

Thus, we have all the operators
L(q,\alpha,\beta)
isospectral with
L(q_{0},\alpha_{0},\beta_{0})
.

We supposed, that
\alpha=\alpha_{0}
, then by formula (
3.2
) we have

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\cfrac{1}{a_{n}}-\cfrac{1}{a_{n}^{0}}\right)=0.

(3.4)

Since
a_{n}\geq a_{n}^{0}
, for all
n\geq 0
, thus from the equation (
3.4
) it refers that
a_{n}=a_{n}^{0}
, for all
n\geq 0
.
Thus, from Marchenko uniqueness theorem
1.1
we obtain
q(x)\equiv q_{0}(x)
and
\beta=\beta_{0}
.

This completes the proof.

Remark 2.
From the equation (
3.4
) it follows, that the condition

can be changed with
a_{n}\leq a_{n}^{0}
.
From the relation (

, with the condition

or

and then we will also obtain

and

Appendix. Analogues of Theorem

Consider
L(q,\alpha,\beta)

Let

The eigenvalues
\mu_{n}=\mu_{n}(q,\alpha,\beta)
,

\Phi(\mu):=\varphi(\pi,\mu)\cot\beta+\varphi^{\prime}(\pi,\mu)=0,

\Phi(\mu)

In

It is easy to see that the functions

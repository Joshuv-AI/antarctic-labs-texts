Continued-fraction characterization of Stieltjes moment sequences with support in [ ξ , ∞ ) [\xi,\infty)
Alan D. Sokal and James Walrad

Abstract
We give a continued-fraction characterization of Stieltjes moment sequences for which there exists a representing measure with support in [ ξ , ∞ ) [\xi,\infty) . The proof is elementary.



Key Words:
Classical moment problem, moment sequence,
Stieltjes moment sequence, binomial transform,
continued fraction.

44A60 (Primary); 05A15, 30B70, 30E05 (Secondary).

we call

A Hamburger (resp. Stieltjes) moment sequence is called

otherwise it is called H-indeterminate (resp. S-indeterminate).

One fundamental characterization of Stieltjes moment sequences
was found by Stieltjes
[
22
]
in 1894
(see also
[
26

A sequence
{\bm{a}}=(a_{n})_{n\geq 0}
of real numbers
is a Stieltjes moment sequence if and only if
there exist real numbers
c\geq 0
and
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\ldots\geq 0

such that

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}t^{n}\;=\;\cfrac{c}{1-\cfrac{\alpha_{1}t}{1-\cfrac{\alpha_{2}t}{1-\cdots}}}

(1)

in the sense of formal power series.
(That is, the ordinary generating function

f(t)=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}t^{n}

can be represented as a Stieltjes-type continued fraction
with nonnegative coefficients.)
Moreover, when
c\neq 0

the coefficients
{\bm{\alpha}}=(\alpha_{i})_{i\geq 1}
are unique
if we make the convention that
\alpha_{i}=0
implies

\alpha_{j}=0
for all

;
we shall call such a sequence
{\bm{\alpha}}

standard
.

If
K

[0,\infty)
,
we denote by

the set of Stieltjes moment sequences
for which there exists a representing measure

with support in

1

We shall need two elementary facts about these continued fractions:

1) The contraction formula: We have

\cfrac{1}{1-\cfrac{\alpha_{1}t}{1-\cfrac{\alpha_{2}t}{1-\cfrac{\alpha_{3}t}{1-\cdots}}}}\;\;=\;\;\cfrac{1}{1-\alpha_{1}t-\cfrac{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}t^{2}}{1-(\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3})t-\cfrac{\alpha_{3}\alpha_{4}t^{2}}{1-(\alpha_{4}+\alpha_{5})t-\cfrac{\alpha_{5}\alpha_{6}t^{2}}{1-\cdots}}}}

(5)

as an identity between formal power series.
In other words, an S-fraction with coefficients
{\bm{\alpha}}

is equal to a J-fraction with coefficients
{\bm{\gamma}}
and
{\bm{\beta}}
, where

\gamma_{0}&=\alpha_{1}{}\\
\gamma_{n}=\alpha_{2n}+\alpha_{2n+1}\quad\hbox{for $n\geq 1$}{}\\
\beta_{n}=\alpha_{2n-1}\,\alpha_{2n}{}

(6)

See
[
26
, pp. 20–22]
for the classic algebraic proof of
the contraction formula (
5
);
see
[

[

for a very simple variant algebraic proof;

[

for an enlightening combinatorial proof,
based on Flajolet’s
[
11
]
combinatorial interpretation
of S-fractions (resp. J-fractions)
as generating functions for Dyck (resp. Motzkin) paths
with height-dependent weights.

2) Binomial transform: Fix a real number
\xi

and let
{\bm{a}}=(a_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be a sequence of real numbers.
Then the
\xi

is defined to be the sequence

b_{n}\;=\;\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\,a_{k}\,\xi^{n-k}\;.

(7)

Note that if

,
then
b_{n}=\int(x+\xi)^{n}\,d\mu(x)
.
In other words, if

with representing measure

Now suppose that the ordinary generating function of
{\bm{a}}

is given by a J-fraction:

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}t^{n}\;=\;\cfrac{1}{1-\gamma_{0}t-\cfrac{\beta_{1}t^{2}}{1-\gamma_{1}t-\cfrac{\beta_{2}t^{2}}{1-\cdots}}}\;.

(8)

Then the
\xi

of
{\bm{a}}
is given by a J-fraction

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}t^{n}\;=\;\cfrac{1}{1-(\gamma_{0}+\xi)t-\cfrac{\beta_{1}t^{2}}{1-(\gamma_{1}+\xi)t-\cfrac{\beta_{2}t^{2}}{1-\cdots}}}\;.

(9)

See
[

[

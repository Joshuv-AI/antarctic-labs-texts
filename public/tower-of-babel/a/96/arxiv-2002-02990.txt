On the Number of 𝝉 \boldsymbol{\tau} -Tilting Modules over Nakayama Algebras This paper is a contribution to the Special Issue on Cluster Algebras. The full collection is available at https://www.emis.de/journals/SIGMA/cluster-algebras.html
Hanpeng GAO † and Ralf SCHIFFLER ‡

Abstract
Let Λ n r \Lambda^{r}_{n} be the path algebra of the linearly oriented quiver of type 𝔸 \mathbb{A} with n n vertices modulo the r r -th power of the radical, and let Λ ~ n r \widetilde{\Lambda}^{r}_{n} be the path algebra of the cyclically oriented quiver of type 𝔸 ~ \widetilde{\mathbb{A}} with n n vertices modulo the r r -th power of the radical. Adachi gave a recurrence relation for the number of τ \tau -tilting modules over Λ n r \Lambda^{r}_{n} . In this paper, we show that the same recurrence relation also holds for the number of τ \tau -tilting modules over Λ ~ n r \widetilde{\Lambda}^{r}_{n} . As an application, we give a new proof for a result by Asai on recurrence formulae for the number of support τ \tau -tilting modules over Λ n r \Lambda^{r}_{n} and Λ ~ n r \widetilde{\Lambda}^{r}_{n} .

Introduction

The starting point of
tilting theory
was the introduction of tilting modules over a hereditary algebra by Happel and Ringel in
[
10
]
. Ever since, the study of tilting modules and their endomorphism algebras has been an important branch of representation theory.

About 25 years later,
cluster-tilting theory
, introduced in
[
7
]
(and
[
8
]
in type
\mathbb{A}
), brought a new perspective to the subject by replacing the hereditary algebra by its cluster category, and a direct relation between tilted and cluster-tilted algebras was then established in
[
4
]
.

In 2014, Adachi, Iyama and Reiten
[
2
]
introduced
\tau
-tilting theory
replacing the rigidity condition
\mathop{\rm Ext}\nolimits^{1}_{\Lambda}(T,T)=0
for a tilting module by the weaker condition
\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits_{\Lambda}(T,\tau_{\Lambda}T)=0
for a
\tau
-tilting module, where
\tau
denotes the Auslander–Reiten translation, and
\Lambda
is any finite-dimensional algebra. They showed that, in contrast to tilting modules but in agreement with cluster-tilting objects, it is always possible to exchange a given indecomposable summand of a support
\tau
-tilting module for a unique other indecomposable and obtain a new support
\tau
-tilting module. This process, called mutation, is essential in cluster theory.

In the same paper, the authors also showed that the support
\tau
-tilting modules are in bijection with several other important classes in representation theory including functorially finite torsion classes introduced in
[
6
]
, 2-term silting complexes introduced in
[
11
]
, and cluster-tilting objects in the cluster category when the algebra
\Lambda
is hereditary, or, more generally, cluster-tilted.

Therefore, it is natural to ask what is the number of support
\tau
-tilting modules over a given algebra.

For a hereditary algebra, the support
\tau
-tilting modules are exactly the support tilting modules. Moreover they are in bijection with the cluster-tilting objects in the cluster category and hence with the clusters in the corresponding cluster algebra. For Dynkin type, these numbers were first calculated in
[
9
]
via cluster algebras, and later in
[
12
]
via representation theory. In particular, over a hereditary algebra of type
\mathbb{A}_{n}
, the number of tilting modules is
C_{n}
, and the number of support tilting modules is
C_{n+1}
where
C_{i}
denotes the
i
-th Catalan number
\frac{1}{i+1}\binom{2i}{i}
.

In this paper, we study this question over finite-dimensional Nakayama algebras. Recall that a finite-dimensional
K
-algebra is said to be a
Nakayama algebra
if every indecomposable projective module and every indecomposable injective module has a unique composition series. Nakayama algebras come in two types, in fact, a finite-dimensional algebra is Nakayama if and only if its quiver is one of the following

\textstyle{A_{n}\colon}
\textstyle{1\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{2\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{3\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\cdots\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{n,}
\textstyle{\widetilde{A}_{n}\colon}
\textstyle{1\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{2\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{3\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\cdots\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{n,\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

see
[
5
, Section V.3.2]
. Throughout the paper, we use the following notation

\displaystyle\Lambda_{n}^{r}=KA_{n}/\mathop{\rm rad}\nolimits^{r}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\widetilde{\Lambda}_{n}^{r}=K\widetilde{A}_{n}/\mathop{\rm rad}\nolimits^{r}.

Moreover, we let
t_{r}(n)
and
\tilde{t}_{r}(n)
denote the number of
\tau
-tilting modules over
\Lambda^{r}_{n}
and
\widetilde{\Lambda}^{r}_{n}
, and let
s_{r}(n)
and
\tilde{s}_{r}(n)
denote the number of support
\tau
-tilting modules over
\Lambda^{r}_{n}
and
\widetilde{\Lambda}^{r}_{n}
, respectively. We also set
t_{r}(n):=0
and
s_{r}(n):=0
for
n<0
.

Adachi classified
\tau
-tilting modules over Nakayama algebras in
[
1
]
. Under the assumption that the Loewy length of every indecomposable projective module is at least
n
, Adachi showed that the number of
\tau
-tilting modules is exactly
\binom{2n-1}{n-1}
and the number of support
\tau
-tilting modules is
\binom{2n}{n}
. Moreover, he also gave the following recurrence relation for the number
t_{r}(n)
of
\tau
-tilting modules over
\Lambda^{r}_{n}

\displaystyle t_{r}(n)=\sum^{r}_{i=1}C_{i-1}\cdot t_{r}(n-i).

The aim of this paper is study the number of
\tau
-tilting modules over
\widetilde{\Lambda}^{r}_{n}
. We show that there is a close relationship between the number
\tilde{t}_{r}(n)
of
\tau
-tilting
\widetilde{\Lambda}_{n}^{r}
-modules and the number
t_{r}(n)
of
\tau
-tilting
\Lambda_{n}^{r}
-modules.

Proposition 1.1

(see Proposition
3.7
)
.

\displaystyle\tilde{t}_{r}(n)=\sum^{r}_{i=1}i\cdot C_{i-1}\cdot t_{r}(n-i).

Next, we prove that the functions
t
and
\tilde{t}
satisfy the same recurrence relation.

Theorem 1.2

(see Theorem
3.8
)
.

We have the following recurrence relation

\displaystyle\tilde{t}_{r}(n)=\sum\limits^{r}_{i=1}C_{i-1}\cdot\tilde{t}_{r}(n-i).

As an application, we obtain a new proof for the following result by Asai on the numbers of support
\tau
-tilting modules
s_{r}(n)
and
\tilde{s}_{r}(n).

Theorem 1.3

(
[
3
, Theorem 4.1]
)
.

(1)

s_{r}(n)=2s_{r}(n-1)+\sum\limits^{r}_{i=2}C_{i-1}\cdot s_{r}(n-i).

(2)

\tilde{s}_{r}(n)=2\tilde{s}_{r}(n-1)+\sum\limits^{r}_{i=2}C_{i-1}\cdot\tilde{s}_{r}(n-i).

Asai used a bijection between support
\tau
-tilting modules and semibricks to obtain his result in the context of semibricks. Our proof is combinatorial.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we fix the notation and recall several results of Adachi that are relevant to this paper. We study the Nakayama algebras of type
{A}_{n}
and
\widetilde{A}_{n}
and prove Theorem
1.2
in Section
3
. Theorem
1.3
is proved in Section
4
. We include tables of the numbers of
\tau
-tilting and support
\tau
-tilting modules in Section
5
.

2
Preliminaries

Throughout this paper, all algebras will be basic, connected, finite-dimensional algebras over an algebraically closed field
K

and all modules will be finitely generated right modules. For an algebra
\Lambda
, we denote by
\mathop{\rm mod}\nolimits\Lambda
the category of finitely generated right
\Lambda
-modules and by
\tau_{\Lambda}
the Auslander–Reiten translation of
\Lambda
. Let
\{e_{1},e_{2},\dots,e_{n}\}
be a complete set of primitive orthogonal idempotents of
\Lambda
. We put
P_{i}=e_{i}\Lambda
the indecomposable projective module and
S_{i}=P_{i}/\operatorname{rad}P_{i}
the simple module of
\Lambda
for
i=1,2,\dots,n
. For
M\in\mathop{\rm mod}\nolimits\Lambda
, we denote by
\ell(M)
the Loewy length of
M
and by
|M|
the number of pairwise nonisomorphic indecomposable summands of
M
. For a finite set
X
, we denote by
|X|
the cardinality of
X
.
For details on representation theory of finite-dimensional algebras we refer to
[
5
,
13
]
.

Let
\Lambda
be an algebra. In this section, we recall results about support
\tau
-tilting modules that are needed later.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
M\in\mathop{\rm mod}\nolimits\Lambda
.

(1)

M
is called
\tau
-rigid
if
\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits_{\Lambda}(M,\tau_{\Lambda}M)=0
.

(2)

M
is called
\tau
-tilting
if it is
\tau
-rigid and
|M|=|\Lambda|
.

(3)

M
is called
support
\tau
-tilting
if it is a
\tau
-tilting
\Lambda/\Lambda e\Lambda
-module for some idempotent
e
of
\Lambda
.

(4)

M
is called
proper support
\tau
-tilting
if it is a support
\tau
-tilting but not a
\tau
-tilting
\Lambda
-module.

Recall that
M\in\mathop{\rm mod}\nolimits\Lambda
is called
sincere
if every simple
\Lambda
-module appears as a composition factor in
M
. It is well-known that the
\tau
-tilting modules are exactly the sincere support
\tau
-tilting modules
[
2
, Proposition 2.2(a)]
.

We will denote by
\mathop{\rm\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits\Lambda
(respectively,
\mathop{\rm s\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits\Lambda
,
\mathop{\rm ps\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits\Lambda
) the set of isomorphism classes of basic
\tau
-tilting (respectively, support
\tau
-tilting, proper support
\tau
-tilting)
\Lambda
-modules. Obviously, we have
|\mathop{\rm s\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits\Lambda|=|\mathop{\rm\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits\Lambda|+|\mathop{\rm ps\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits\Lambda|
.

Let
\mathop{\rm ps\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits_{np}\Lambda:=\{M\in\mathop{\rm ps\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits\Lambda\,|\,M\ \text{has no projective direct summands}\}
. We recall the following results proved by Adachi in
[
1
]
.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
1
, Theorem 2.6]
)
.

Let
\Lambda
be a Nakayama algebra. There is a bijection between
\mathop{\rm\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits\Lambda
and
\mathop{\rm ps\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits_{np}\Lambda
.

The following result is very useful.

Proposition 2.3

(
[
1
, Proposition 2.32]
)
.

Let
\Lambda
be a Nakayama algebra of type
A_{n}
. Then each
\tau
-tilting
\Lambda
-module has
P_{1}
as a direct summand.

The following recurrence relations are very useful to calculate the number of
\tau
-tilting modules over Nakayama algebras of type
A_{n}
.

Lemma 2.4

(
[
1
, Corollary 2.34]
)
.

Let
\Lambda
be a Nakayama algebra of type
A_{n}
. Then

\displaystyle|\mathop{\rm\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits\Lambda|=\sum^{\ell(P_{1})}_{i=1}C_{i-1}\cdot|\mathop{\rm\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits(\Lambda/\langle e_{\leqslant i}\rangle)|,

where
e_{\leqslant i}:=e_{1}+e_{2}+\dots+e_{i}
. In particular, for
\Lambda=\Lambda_{n}^{r}
, we have

\displaystyle t_{r}(n)=\sum^{r}_{i=1}C_{i-1}\cdot t_{r}(n-i),

where
t_{r}(n)
is the number of
\tau
-tilting modules over
\Lambda^{r}_{n}
.

See Table
1
in Section
5
for explicit values of
t_{r}(n)
.

Remark 2.5
.

If
r\geq n
then
\Lambda^{r}_{n}
is hereditary, and we have
t_{r}(n)=C_{n}.
On the other hand, Lemma
2.4
yields the equation
t_{n}(n)=\sum\limits^{n}_{i=1}C_{i-1}\cdot t_{n}(n-i)
. Hence we recover the well-known combinatorial identity

\displaystyle C_{n}=\sum^{n}_{i=1}C_{i-1}\cdot C_{n-i}.

In this section,
\Lambda

We denote by

Lemma 3.1
.

|W_{i}|=|\mathop{\rm\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits\Lambda_{<i}|\cdot|\mathop{\rm s\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits\Lambda_{>i}|

Proof.

Since the quiver of
\Lambda
is tree, we have
\Lambda/\langle e_{i}\rangle\cong\Lambda_{<i}\times\Lambda_{>i}

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits\Lambda_{<i}\times\mathop{\rm s\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits\Lambda_{>i}\longrightarrow W_{i}

given by
(N_{1},N_{2})\mapsto N_{1}\oplus N_{2}
where
N_{1}
is a
\tau
-tilting
\Lambda_{<i}
-module and
N_{2}
is a support
\tau
-tilting
\Lambda_{>i}
-module. Hence
|W_{i}|=|\mathop{\rm\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits\Lambda_{<i}|\cdot|\mathop{\rm s\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits\Lambda_{>i}|
.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\Lambda
be a Nakayama algebra of type
A_{n}
. We have

(1)

|\mathop{\rm ps\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits\Lambda|=\sum\limits^{n}_{i=1}|\mathop{\rm\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits\Lambda_{<i}|\cdot|\mathop{\rm s\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits\Lambda_{>i}|
,

(2)

|\mathop{\rm s\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits\Lambda|=\sum\limits^{n}_{i=1}|\mathop{\rm\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits\Lambda_{<i}|\cdot|\mathop{\rm s\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits\Lambda_{>i}|+\sum\limits^{\ell(P_{1})}_{i=1}C_{i-1}\cdot|\mathop{\rm\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits\Lambda_{>i}|
,

(3)

|\mathop{\rm ps\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits\Lambda|=\sum\limits^{n}_{i=1}|\mathop{\rm s\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits\Lambda_{<i}|\cdot|\mathop{\rm\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits\Lambda_{>i}|
,

(4)

|\mathop{\rm s\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits\Lambda|=\sum\limits^{n}_{i=1}|\mathop{\rm s\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits\Lambda_{<i}|\cdot|\mathop{\rm\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits\Lambda_{>i}|+\sum\limits^{\ell(P_{1})}_{i=1}C_{i-1}\cdot|\mathop{\rm\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits\Lambda_{>i}|
.

Proof.

(1)
Since
\mathop{\rm ps\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits\Lambda=\bigcup\limits^{n}_{i=1}W_{i}
, we have

\displaystyle|\mathop{\rm ps\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits\Lambda|=\sum\limits^{n}_{i=1}|W_{i}|=\sum\limits^{n}_{i=1}|\mathop{\rm\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits\Lambda_{<i}|\cdot|\mathop{\rm s\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits\Lambda_{>i}|

by Lemma
3.1
.

(2) Since
|\mathop{\rm s\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits\Lambda|=|\mathop{\rm ps\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits\Lambda|+|\mathop{\rm\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits\Lambda|
, the statement follows from Lemma
2.4
.

(3) is similar to (1) and
(4) follows from (3).
∎

Corollary 3.4
.

Proposition 3.5

\big|\mathop{\rm ps\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits\widetilde{\Lambda}^{r}_{n}\big|=\sum\limits^{n-1}_{i=1}i\cdot t_{r}(i-1)\cdot s_{r}({n-i-1})+n\cdot t_{r}(n-1).

\displaystyle K_{n,0}=\big\{N\in\mathop{\rm s\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits\widetilde{\Lambda}^{r}_{n}\,|\,N~\text{does not contain $S_{n}$ as composition factor}\big\}.

Note that

\textstyle{n-\ell+1\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\textstyle{n\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\textstyle{2\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\cdots\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{n-\ell-1}

With the above notions, we have

(2)

|Y_{n,\ell}|=\begin{cases}\sum\limits^{r}_{i=\ell+1}C_{i-1}\cdot t_{r}({n-i+1})&\text{if $\ell\leqslant r-1$},\\
0&\text{if $\ell\geqslant r$}.\end{cases}

Proof.

(1) Write
\Lambda=\Lambda^{r}_{n}
, we have

\Lambda/\langle e_{i}\rangle\cong\Lambda_{<i}\times\Lambda_{>i}
for a given
i
.
Let
Z_{1}
be the set of all support
\tau
-tilting
\Lambda_{<i}
-modules which have
S_{1},S_{2},\dots,S_{i-1}
as composition factor (they are exactly
\tau
-tilting
\Lambda_{<i}
-modules)
and they don’t have
P_{1},P_{2},\dots,P_{n-r+1}
as direct summands.

If
i\leqslant r
, then
Z_{1}=\mathop{\rm\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits\Lambda_{<i}
.

If
i\geqslant r+1
, then all
\tau
-tilting
\Lambda_{<i}
-modules have
P_{1}
as direct summand by Proposition
2.3
, and hence
Z_{1}=\varnothing
.

Let
Z_{2}
be the set of all support
\tau
-tilting
\Lambda_{>i}
-modules who do not have
P_{1},P_{2},\dots,P_{n-r+1}
as direct summands. Then
Z_{2}
consists of exactly all support
\tau
-tilting
\Lambda_{>i}
-modules which do not contain

P_{i+1},P_{i+2},\dots,P_{n-r+1}
as direct summands.
Denoted by
X_{n,i}\subseteq X_{n}

(i=1,2,\dots,n)
the subset of all support
\tau
-tilting modules having
S_{1},S_{2},\dots,S_{i-1}
as composition factor but not
S_{i}
and by
X_{n,n+1}\subseteq X_{n}
the subset of all support
\tau
-tilting modules having
S_{1},S_{2},\dots,S_{n}
as composition factor (hence, they are exactly
\tau
-tilting). We have
|Z_{2}|=|X_{n-i}|
, since
\Lambda_{>i}\cong\Lambda^{r}_{n-i}
.
There is a bijection between
Z_{1}\times Z_{2}
and
X_{n,i}
given
by
(N_{1},N_{2})\to N_{1}\oplus N_{2}
where
N_{1}\in Z_{1}
and
N_{2}\in Z_{2}
.
Therefore,
|X_{n,i}|=|Z_{1}|\cdot|Z_{2}|
.
Thus, we have

\displaystyle|X_{n,i}|=\begin{cases}|\mathop{\rm\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits\Lambda_{<i}|\cdot|X_{n-i}|&\text{if}\ i\leqslant r,\\
0&\text{if}\ i\geqslant r+1.\end{cases}

If
r\leqslant n
, we have

\displaystyle|X_{n}|=\sum^{n+1}_{i=1}|X_{n,i}|=\sum^{r}_{i=1}|\mathop{\rm\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits\Lambda_{<i}|\cdot|X_{n-i}|=\sum^{r}_{i=1}C_{i-1}\cdot|X_{n-i}|.

Note that, if
r\geq n+1
then
X_{n}=\mathop{\rm s\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits\Lambda^{r}_{n}
and hence

|X_{n}|=C_{n+1}
since
\Lambda^{r}_{n}
is hereditary, we get
|X_{n}|=t_{r}(n+1)
.
On the other hand, Lemma
2.4
says that
t_{r}(n)
and
|X_{n}|
satisfy the same recursive formula. Thus
we have
|X_{n}|=t_{r}(n+1)
for all
n
, by induction.

(2) follows from (1) and the fact
Y_{n,\ell}=\bigcup\limits^{n+1}_{i=\ell+1}X_{n,i}
.
∎

As a result of Lemma
3.6
, we have the following relationship between
\tilde{t}_{r}(n)
and
t_{r}(n)
.

Proposition 3.7
.

We have

\displaystyle\tilde{t}_{r}(n)=\sum^{r}_{i=1}i\cdot C_{i-1}\cdot t_{r}({n-i}).

Proof.

Considering the set
K^{np}_{n,\ell}
consisting of all modules in
K_{n,\ell}
which do not have projective
\widetilde{\Lambda}^{r}_{n}
-modules as direct summands.

Note that the indecomposable projective
\widetilde{\Lambda}^{r}_{n}/\langle e_{n-\ell}\rangle
-modules corresponding to the last
r-1
points (the length of them is at most
r-1
) are not projective
\widetilde{\Lambda}^{r}_{n}
-modules, we have

\big|K^{np}_{n,\ell}\big|=|Y_{n-1,\ell}|
since
\widetilde{\Lambda}^{r}_{n}/\langle e_{n-\ell}\rangle\cong\Lambda^{r}_{n-1}
.
Thus,

\displaystyle\big|\mathop{\rm ps\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits_{np}\widetilde{\Lambda}^{r}_{n}\big|

\displaystyle=\sum\limits^{n-1}_{\ell=0}\big|K^{np}_{n,\ell}\big|=\sum\limits^{n-1}_{\ell=0}|Y_{n-1,\ell}|

\displaystyle=\sum\limits^{r-1}_{\ell=0}\sum\limits^{r}_{i=l+1}C_{i-1}\cdot t_{r}(n-i)\quad\text{(by~Lemma~\ref{3.6})}

\displaystyle=\sum^{r}_{i=1}i\cdot C_{i-1}\cdot t_{r}(n-i).

Therefore, the assertion follows from Theorem
2.2
.
∎

Now, we are ready to prove our main result of this section.

Theorem 3.8
.

We have

\displaystyle\tilde{t}_{r}(n)=\sum\limits^{r}_{i=1}C_{i-1}\cdot\tilde{t}_{r}(n-i).

Proof.

By Proposition
3.7
, we have
\tilde{t}_{r}(n)=\sum\limits^{r}_{\ell=1}\ell\cdot C_{\ell-1}\cdot t_{r}(n-\ell)
.
Thus,

\displaystyle\tilde{t}_{r}(n)-\sum\limits^{r}_{i=1}C_{i-1}\cdot\tilde{t}_{r}(n-i)=\sum\limits^{r}_{\ell=1}\ell\cdot C_{\ell-1}\cdot t_{r}(n-\ell)

\displaystyle\hphantom{\tilde{t}_r(n)-\sum\limits^r_{i=1}C_{i-1}\cdot\tilde{t}_r(n-i)=}{}-\sum\limits^{r}_{i=1}C_{i-1}\cdot\left(\sum\limits^{r}_{\ell=1}\ell\cdot C_{\ell-1}\cdot t_{r}(n-i-\ell)\right)

\displaystyle\hphantom{\tilde{t}_r(n)-\sum\limits^r_{i=1}C_{i-1}\cdot\tilde{t}_r(n-i)}{}=\sum\limits^{r}_{\ell=1}\ell\cdot C_{\ell-1}\cdot\left(t_{r}(n-\ell)-\sum\limits^{r}_{i=1}C_{i-1}\cdot t_{r}(n-\ell-i)\right)

\displaystyle\hphantom{\tilde{t}_r(n)-\sum\limits^r_{i=1}C_{i-1}\cdot\tilde{t}_r(n-i)}{}=0.\quad\text{(by~Lemma~\ref{2.4})}

Hence,
\tilde{t}_{r}(n)=\sum\limits^{r}_{i=1}C_{i-1}\cdot\tilde{t}_{r}(n-i)
.∎

The following proposition and its proof are similar to
[
3
, Theorem 4.1 (3)]
. For convenience, we include a proof here. We shall use the following notation.

For every positive integer
r
, let
F_{r}(X)=\sum\limits^{r}_{i=0}c_{i}\cdot X^{r-i}
where
c_{0}=1
and
c_{i}=-C_{i-1}
for
i=1,2,\dots,r
. Let

\displaystyle{\bf E}_{n}(X_{1},X_{2},\dots,X_{r})=\sum_{J\subseteq\{1,2,\dots,r\},|J|=n}\prod_{j\in J}X_{j}

be the
n
-th elementary symmetric polynomial,
n=0,1,2,\dots,r.
Let

\displaystyle{\bf H}_{n}(X_{1},X_{2},\dots,X_{r})=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}t_{1},t_{2},\dots,t_{r}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}\\
t_{1}+t_{2}+\dots+t_{r}=n\end{subarray}}X^{t_{1}}_{1}X^{t_{2}}_{2}\cdots X^{t_{r}}_{r}\qquad\text{for all }n\in\mathbb{Z},

\displaystyle{\bf P}_{n}(X_{1},X_{2},\dots,X_{r})=\sum\limits^{r}_{i=1}X_{i}^{n}\quad\text{for all }n\geqslant 1.

In particular, we have
{\bf E}_{0}=1
,
{\bf H}_{0}=1
, and
{\bf H}_{n}=0
for
n<0
.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\dots,\xi_{r}
be the roots
(
not necessarily distinct
)
of the polynomial
F_{r}(X)
.
Then we have

(1)

{t}_{r}(n)=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}t_{1},t_{2},\dots,t_{r}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}\\
t_{1}+t_{2}+\dots+t_{r}=n\end{subarray}}\xi^{t_{1}}_{1}\xi^{t_{2}}_{2}\cdots\xi^{t_{r}}_{r}
,

(2)

\widetilde{t}_{r}(n)=\sum\limits^{r}_{i=1}\xi^{n}_{i}
.

Proof.

Using Vieta’s formula on symmetric polynomials, we have

\displaystyle{\bf E}_{i}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\dots,\xi_{r})=(-1)^{i}c_{i}

for
i=0,1,2,\dots,r
. By
[
3
, Lemma 4.8]
, we have

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=0}^{r}c_{i}{\bf H}_{n-i}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\dots,\xi_{r})=0.

On the other hand, Lemma
2.4
yields
t_{r}(n)=\sum\limits^{r}_{i=1}C_{i-1}\cdot t_{r}(n-i)
which implies

\displaystyle\sum^{r}_{i=0}c_{i}\cdot t_{r}(n-i)=0.

Therefore

\displaystyle\sum^{r}_{i=0}c_{i}\cdot(t_{r}(n-i)-{\bf H}_{n-i}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\dots,\xi_{r}))=0.

(3.1)

Note that
t_{r}(0)=1={\bf H}_{0}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\dots,\xi_{r})
and
t_{r}(n)=0={\bf H}_{n}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\dots,\xi_{r})
for
n<0
.
Therefore, using induction and equation (
3.1
), we see that for all
n

\displaystyle t_{r}(n)={\bf H}_{n}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\dots,\xi_{r})=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}t_{1},t_{2},\dots,t_{r}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}\\
t_{1}+t_{2}+\cdots+t_{r}=n\end{subarray}}\xi^{t_{1}}_{1}\xi^{t_{2}}_{2}\cdots\xi^{t_{r}}_{r}.

In Proposition
3.7
, we have shown the following relation

\displaystyle\tilde{t}_{r}(n)=\sum^{r}_{i=1}-i\cdot c_{i}\cdot t_{r}({n-i}).

Hence,

\displaystyle\tilde{t}_{r}(n)

\displaystyle=\sum^{r}_{i=1}-i\cdot c_{i}\cdot t_{r}({n-i})=\sum^{r}_{i=1}-i\cdot c_{i}\cdot{\bf H}_{n-i}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\dots,\xi_{r})

\displaystyle=\sum^{r}_{i=1}-i\cdot(-1)^{i}\cdot{\bf E}_{i}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\dots,\xi_{r})\cdot{\bf H}_{n-i}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\dots,\xi_{r})

\displaystyle=\sum^{r}_{i=1}(-1)^{i-1}\cdot i\cdot{\bf E}_{i}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\dots,\xi_{r})\cdot{\bf H}_{n-i}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\dots,\xi_{r})

\displaystyle={\bf P}_{n}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\dots,\xi_{r})\quad(\text{by~\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{S2018}{}{}, Lemma 4.8]}})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits^{r}_{i=1}\xi^{n}_{i}.

∎

For

, we obtain the Fibonacci recurrence
\tilde{t}_{2}(n)=\tilde{t}_{2}(n-1)+\tilde{t}_{2}(n-2)
; however, with different initial conditions. Thus we obtain Lucas numbers and we have the following formula.

Corollary 3.10
.

\tilde{t}_{2}(n)=\big(\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}\big)^{n}+\big(\frac{1-\sqrt{5}}{2}\big)^{n}

Applying Proposition
3.2
, we obtain the following recurrence relation for the number of support
\tau
-tilting modules over

. See Table

in Section

for explicit values of

Proposition 4.1

(
[
3
, Theorem 4.1(1)]
)
.

\displaystyle s_{r}(n)=2s_{r}(n-1)+\sum^{r}_{i=2}C_{i-1}\cdot s_{r}(n-i).

Proof.

Since
\Lambda=\Lambda^{r}_{n}
, we have
\Lambda_{<i}\cong\Lambda^{r}_{i-1}
and
\Lambda_{>i}\cong\Lambda^{r}_{n-i}
. Thus Proposition
3.2
(1) yields

s_{r}(n)=\sum\limits^{n}_{i=1}t_{r}(i-1)\cdot s_{r}(n-i)+t_{r}(n).

Therefore,

\displaystyle s_{r}(n)-s_{r}(n-1)=\sum\limits^{n}_{i=1}t_{r}(i-1)\cdot s_{r}(n-i)+t_{r}(n)

\displaystyle\hphantom{s_r(n)-s_r(n-1)=}{}-\left(\sum\limits^{n-1}_{i=1}t_{r}(i-1)\cdot s_{r}(n-1-i)+t_{r}(n-1)\right)

\displaystyle\hphantom{s_r(n)-s_r(n-1)}{}=s_{r}(n-1)+\sum\limits^{n-1}_{i=1}(t_{r}(i)-t_{r}(i-1))\cdot s_{r}(n-1-i)+(t_{r}(n)-t_{r}(n-1)).

By Lemma
2.4
, for all
i
we have

t_{r}(i)=t_{r}(i-1)+\sum\limits^{r}_{\ell=2}C_{\ell-1}\cdot t_{r}(i-\ell).

Using this equality in the identity above, we get

\displaystyle s_{r}(n)-s_{r}(n-1)=s_{r}(n-1)+\sum\limits^{n-1}_{i=1}\sum^{r}_{\ell=2}C_{\ell-1}\cdot t_{r}(i-\ell)\cdot s_{r}(n-1-i)

\displaystyle\hphantom{s_r(n)-s_r(n-1)=}{}+\sum^{r}_{\ell=2}C_{\ell-1}\cdot t_{r}(n-\ell)

and since
s_{r}(n-\ell-i)=0
whenever
i>n-\ell
, we obtain

\displaystyle s_{r}(n)-s_{r}(n-1)=s_{r}(n-1)+\sum^{r}_{\ell=2}C_{\ell-1}\cdot\sum^{n-\ell}_{i=1}t_{r}(i-1)\cdot s_{r}(n-\ell-i)

\displaystyle\hphantom{s_r(n)-s_r(n-1)=}{}+\sum^{r}_{\ell=2}C_{\ell-1}\cdot t_{r}(n-\ell)

\displaystyle\hphantom{s_r(n)-s_r(n-1)}{}=s_{r}(n-1)+\sum^{r}_{\ell=2}C_{\ell-1}\cdot\left(\sum^{n-\ell}_{i=1}t_{r}(i-1)\cdot s_{r}(n-\ell-i)+t_{r}(n-\ell)\right)

\displaystyle\hphantom{s_r(n)-s_r(n-1)}{}=s_{r}(n-1)+\sum^{r}_{\ell=2}C_{\ell-1}\cdot s_{r}(n-\ell)\quad\text{by Proposition \ref{3.2}(1)}.

Hence,
s_{r}(n)=2s_{r}(n-1)+\sum\limits^{r}_{i=2}C_{i-1}\cdot s_{r}(n-i).

∎

Remark 4.2
.

If
r\geq n
then
\Lambda_{n}^{r}
is a hereditary algebra of Dynkin type
\mathbb{A}_{n}
and the support
\tau
-tilting modules are in bijection with the clusters in the corresponding cluster algebra. In particular,
s_{r}(n)=C_{n+1}
.
This fact also can be obtained directly from Proposition
4.1
and the identity

C_{n}=\sum\limits^{n}_{i=1}C_{i-1}\cdot C_{n-i}
(see Remark
2.5
). Indeed, the following equation

\displaystyle s_{n}(n)-C_{n+1}=2s_{n}({n-1})+\sum^{n}_{i=2}C_{i-1}\cdot s_{n}({n-i})-\left(\sum^{n+1}_{i=1}C_{i-1}\cdot C_{n+1-i}\right)

\displaystyle\hphantom{s_n(n)-C_{n+1}}{}=C_{0}\cdot s_{n}({n-1})+\sum^{n}_{i=2}C_{i-1}\cdot s_{n}({n-i})-\left(\sum^{n}_{i=1}C_{i-1}\cdot C_{n+1-i}\right)

\displaystyle\hphantom{s_n(n)-C_{n+1}=}{}+s_{n}({n-1})-C_{n}\cdot C_{0}

\displaystyle\hphantom{s_n(n)-C_{n+1}}{}=\sum^{n}_{i=1}C_{i-1}\cdot(s_{n}({n-i})-C_{n+1-i})+(s_{n}({n-1})-C_{n}\cdot C_{0})

\displaystyle\hphantom{s_n(n)-C_{n+1}}{}=\sum^{n}_{i=1}C_{i-1}\cdot(s_{n-i}({n-i})-C_{n+1-i})+(s_{n-1}({n-1})-C_{n})

implies
s_{n}(n)=C_{n+1}
by induction.

3.8
, we obtain the following recurrence relation for the number of support
\tau
-tilting modules over
\widetilde{\Lambda}^{r}_{n}
. See Table
4
in Section
5
for explicit values of

.

Proposition 4.3

(
[
3
, Theorem 4.1(2)]
)
.

\displaystyle\tilde{s}_{r}(n)=2\tilde{s}_{r}(n-1)+\sum\limits^{r}_{i=2}C_{i-1}\cdot\tilde{s}_{r}(n-i).

Proof.

We need to show

\tilde{s}_{r}(n)-2\tilde{s}_{r}(n-1)-\sum\limits^{r}_{i=2}C_{i-1}\cdot\tilde{s}_{r}(n-i)=0.

Using Proposition
3.5
and the equation
\tilde{s}_{r}(n)=\big|\mathop{\rm ps\tau\makebox{-}tilt}\nolimits\widetilde{\Lambda}^{r}_{n}\big|+\tilde{t}_{r}(n)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\tilde{s}_{r}(n)=\sum\limits^{n-1}_{\ell=1}\ell\cdot t_{r}(\ell-1)\cdot s_{r}(n-\ell-1)+n\cdot t_{r}(n-1)+\tilde{t}_{r}(n).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\tilde{s}_{r}(n)-2\tilde{s}_{r}(n-1)-\sum^{r}_{i=2}C_{i-1}\cdot\tilde{s}_{r}(n-i)

\displaystyle{}=\sum\limits^{n-1}_{\ell=1}\ell\cdot t_{r}(\ell-1)\cdot s_{r}(n-\ell-1)+n\cdot t_{r}(n-1)+\tilde{t}_{r}(n)

\displaystyle\quad{}-2\left(\sum\limits^{n-2}_{\ell=1}\ell\cdot t_{r}(\ell-1)\cdot s_{r}(n-\ell-2)+(n-1)\cdot t_{r}(n-2)+\tilde{t}_{r}(n-1)\right)

\displaystyle\quad{}-\sum^{r}_{i=2}C_{i-1}\cdot\!\left(\sum\limits^{n-i-1}_{\ell=1}\ell\cdot t_{r}(\ell-1)\cdot s_{r}(n-i-\ell-1)+(n-i)\cdot t_{r}(n-i-1)+\tilde{t}_{r}(n-i)\right).

Note that every term in the summation

\sum\limits^{r}_{i=2}C_{i-1}\sum\limits^{n-2}_{\ell=n-i}\ell\cdot t_{r}(\ell-1)\cdot s_{r}(n-i-\ell-1)
is zero, and therefore the whole expression is equal to

\displaystyle=\sum\limits^{n-2}_{\ell=1}\ell\cdot t_{r}(\ell-1)\cdot\left(s_{r}(n-\ell-1)-2s_{r}(n-\ell-2)-\sum^{r}_{i=2}C_{r-1}\cdot s_{r}(n-\ell-1-i)\right)

\displaystyle\quad{}+n\cdot t_{r}(n-1)+\big((n-1)\cdot t_{r}(n-2)-2(n-1)\cdot t_{r}(n-2)\big)

\displaystyle\quad{}-\sum^{r}_{i=2}(n-i)\cdot C_{i-1}\cdot t_{r}(n-i-1)+\left(\tilde{t}_{r}(n)-2\tilde{t}_{r}(n-1)-\sum^{r}_{i=2}C_{i-1}\cdot\tilde{t}_{r}(n-i)\right).

Now, the parenthesis in the first sum is zero, by Proposition
4.1
, the large parenthesis in the second row can be included in the first sum of the third row as the
i=1
term, and the parenthesis in the third row is equal to
-\tilde{t}_{r}(n-1)
by Theorem
3.8
. So the whole expression is equal to

\displaystyle=n\cdot t_{r}(n-1)-\sum^{r}_{i=1}(n-i)\cdot C_{i-1}\cdot t_{r}(n-i-1)-\tilde{t}_{r}(n-1)

\displaystyle=n\cdot\left(t_{r}(n-1)-\sum^{r}_{i=1}C_{i-1}\cdot t_{r}(n-i-1)\right)+\sum^{r}_{i=1}i\cdot C_{i-1}\cdot t_{r}(n-i-1)-\tilde{t}_{r}(n-1)

\displaystyle=\sum^{r}_{i=1}i\cdot C_{i-1}\cdot t_{r}(n-i-1)-\tilde{t}_{r}(n-1)\quad\text{(by~Lemma~\ref{2.4})}

\displaystyle=\tilde{t}_{r}(n-1)-\tilde{t}_{r}(n-1)\quad\text{(by~Proposition \ref{3.7})}

\displaystyle=0.

∎

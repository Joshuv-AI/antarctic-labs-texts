For a graph

Let

. The distance of

and

\lceil\frac{d_{i}+1}{3}\rceil

[

[

]

The first three authors of this article in

The article is organized as follows. In section

Let

Set

of

of
G
on

. For a vertex

A

of length
(n-1)
in a graph
G
is an alternating sequence of vertices and edges
w=\{v_{1},z_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{n-1},z_{n-1},v_{n}\}
, where
z_{i}=\{v_{i},v_{i+1}\}

and

. A walk is closed if

. A walk may also be denoted

of length

is a

. A

is a walk with all the points distinct. For simplicity, a

with

, is a walk with

and edge set

, and the length of

.

In the sequel, let

be the unique graded maximal ideal of the standard

. The local cohomology modules of a finitely generated

Definition 2.1
.

Let
M
be a finitely generated graded
S
-module.

(a)

The
depth
of
M
is defined as

\operatorname{depth}(M):=\min\{i:H_{S_{+}}^{i}(M)\neq 0\}.

(1)

For

, the
i

a
-invariant
of

a_{i}(M):=\max\{t:(H_{S_{+}}^{i}(M))_{t}\neq 0\}

with the convention that
\max\emptyset=-\infty
.

(2)

The
Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity
of
M
is defined as

\operatorname{reg}(M):=\max\{a_{i}(M)+i:0\leq i\leq\dim(M)\}.

A graph
G
is called Cohen-Macaulay (abbreviated as C-M) if the quotient ring
S/I_{G}
is Cohen-Macaulay, i.e.,
\operatorname{depth}(S/I_{G})=\dim(S/I_{G})
.
For a proper non-zero homogeneous ideal

in

, it is known that
\operatorname{reg}(S/I)=\operatorname{reg}(I)-1
.

The following lemmas are often used to compute the depth and regularity of a module.
In particular, since the facts in Lemma
2.2
are well-known, they will be used implicitly in this paper.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let

be two finitely generated graded

-modules. Then,

(1)

\operatorname{depth}(M\oplus N)=\min\{\operatorname{depth}(M),\operatorname{depth}(N)\}
, and

(2)

\operatorname{reg}(M\oplus N)=\max\{\operatorname{reg}(M),\operatorname{reg}(N)\}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

(
[

)

Let

(1)

\operatorname{depth}\,(M)\geq\min\{\operatorname{depth}\,(N),\operatorname{depth}\,(P)+1\}
, the equality holds if
\operatorname{depth}\,(N)\\
\neq\operatorname{depth}\,(P)
.

(2)

\operatorname{reg}\,(M)\leq\max\{\operatorname{reg}\,(N),\operatorname{reg}\,(P)+1\}

Lemma 2.4

(
[

For a proper ideal

Lemma 2.8

Let

3.
Study of bipartite graphs

In this section, we will study the depth and regularity of a bipartite graph obtained from a Cohen-Macaulay bipartite graph by deleting its one leaf.
We give some exact formulas for the depth and regularity of the edge ideal of such a graph.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
G
be a C-M bipartite graph with a leaf
u
. Let
N_{G}(u)=\{v\}
with
\deg_{G}(v)\geq 2
. Then

\operatorname{depth}(S_{G\backslash u}/I_{G\backslash u})=\operatorname{depth}(S_{G}/I_{G})-1.

Proof.

Let
V(G)=\{x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}\}\sqcup\{y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{n}\}

u=x_{i}
for some
i\in[n]

v=y_{i}
with
\deg_{G}(y_{i})\geq 2
. Thus
n\geq 2

We prove the claimed formula by induction on
n

If
n=2

G
and
G\backslash u
are paths with
4
and
3

2.9

In the following, we assume that

N_{G_{1}}(u)=\{v_{1}\}
.
Then

\operatorname{depth}(S_{G}/I_{G})=\operatorname{depth}(S_{G_{1}}/I_{G_{1}})

Proof.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
G=(G_{1},u_{1})*(G_{2},u_{2})
, where each
G_{i}
is a C-M bipartite graph with a leaf
u_{i}
. Let
N_{G_{i}}(u_{i})=\{v_{i}\}
. Then

\operatorname{depth}(S_{G}/I_{G})=\operatorname{depth}(S_{G_{1}}/I_{G_{1}})+\operatorname{depth}(S_{G_{2}}/I_{G_{2}})-1,

Proof.

For any
i\in[2]
, let
V(G_{i})=X_{i}\sqcup Y_{i}
with
X_{i}=\{x_{i,1},\dots,x_{i,n_{i}}\}
,
Y_{i}=\{y_{i,1},\dots,y_{i,n_{i}}\}
and
u_{i}=x_{i,j_{i}}
for some
j_{i}\in[n_{i}]
. Then

N_{G_{i}}(u_{i})=\{y_{i,j_{i}}\}
. Let
v_{i}=y_{i,j_{i}}
and
N_{G_{1}}(v_{1})=\{x_{1,k_{1}},\dots,x_{1,k_{t}}\}
with
1\leq k_{1}<\dots<k_{t}=j_{1}
. We divide into the following two cases:

(1) If
\deg_{G_{i}}(v_{i})=1
for some
i\in[2]
, then we assume
\deg_{G_{2}}(v_{2})=1
by symmetry. Then
G_{2}=P_{2}\sqcup(G_{2}\backslash\{u_{2},v_{2}\})
, which implies
G=(G_{1}\cup_{u_{1}}P_{2})\sqcup(G_{2}\backslash\{u_{2},v_{2}\})
. By Lemmas
2.4
(
2
),
2.8
(
1
), and
4.6
, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{depth}(S_{G}/I_{G})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{depth}(S_{G_{1}\cup_{u_{1}}P_{2}}/I_{G_{1}\cup_{u_{1}}P_{2}})+\operatorname{depth}(S_{G_{2}\backslash\{u_{2},v_{2}\}}/I_{G_{2}\backslash\{u_{2},v_{2}\}})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{depth}(S_{G_{1}}/I_{G_{1}})+\operatorname{depth}(S_{G_{2}}/I_{G_{2}})-1.

(2) If
\deg_{G_{i}}(v_{i})\geq 2
for all
i\in[2]
, then we choose
J=(N_{G}(v_{1}))+I_{G\backslash N_{G}[v_{1}]}
,
K=(v_{1})+I_{G\backslash v_{1}}
. In this case,
G\backslash v_{1}
is the disjoint union of
G_{2}
and
G_{1}\backslash\{u_{1},v_{1}\}
. Thus by Lemma
2.8
(
1
), we get

\displaystyle\operatorname{depth}(S_{G}/K)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{depth}(S_{G_{2}}/I_{G_{2}})+\operatorname{depth}(S_{G_{1}\backslash\{u_{1},v_{1}\}}/I_{G_{1}\backslash\{u_{1},v_{1}\}})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{depth}(S_{G_{2}}/I_{G_{2}})+\operatorname{depth}(S_{G_{1}}/I_{G_{1}})-1.

In order to compute the depth of
S_{G}/J
, we distinguish into the following two cases:

(i) If
N_{G}(v_{1})=X_{1}
, then
G\backslash N_{G}[v_{1}]
is the disjoint union of
G_{2}\backslash u_{2}
and isolated set
Y_{1}\backslash v_{1}
. By Lemmas
2.4
(
2
),
2.8
(
1
) and
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{depth}(S_{G}/J)

\displaystyle=1+\operatorname{depth}(S_{G\backslash N_{G}[v_{1}]}/I_{G\backslash N_{G}[v_{1}]})

\displaystyle=1+\operatorname{depth}(S_{G_{2}\backslash u_{2}}/I_{G_{2}\backslash u_{2}})+(n_{1}-1)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{depth}(S_{G_{1}}/I_{G_{1}})+\operatorname{depth}(S_{G_{2}}/I_{G_{2}})-1.

(ii) If
N_{G}(v_{1})\subsetneq X_{1}
, then
G\backslash N_{G}[v_{1}]
is the disjoint union of
G_{1}\backslash\{x_{1,k_{1}},y_{1,k_{1}},\dots,\\
x_{1,k_{t}},y_{1,k_{t}}\}
,
G_{2}\backslash u_{2}
, and isolated set
\{y_{1,k_{1}},y_{1,k_{2}},\dots,y_{1,k_{t-1}}\}
. Note that
H=G_{1}\backslash\{x_{1,k_{1}},\\
y_{1,k_{1}},\dots,x_{1,k_{t}},y_{1,k_{t}}\}
is a C-M bipartite graph by Lemma
2.7
(
2
).
Thus, by Lemmas
2.8
(
1
) and

3.1
, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{depth}(S_{G}/J)

\displaystyle=1+\operatorname{depth}(S_{G\backslash N_{G}[v_{1}]}/I_{G\backslash N_{G}[v_{1}]})

\displaystyle=1+\operatorname{depth}(S_{H}/I_{H})+\operatorname{depth}(S_{G_{2}\backslash u_{2}}/I_{G_{2}\backslash u_{2}})+(t-1)

\displaystyle=1+\frac{|V(G_{1})|-2t}{2}+[\operatorname{depth}(S_{G_{2}}/I_{G_{2}})-1]+(t-1)

\displaystyle=\frac{|V(G_{1})|}{2}+\operatorname{depth}(S_{G_{2}}/I_{G_{2}})-1

\displaystyle=\operatorname{depth}(S_{G_{1}}/I_{G_{1}})+\operatorname{depth}(S_{G_{2}}/I_{G_{2}})-1.

Applying Lemmas
2.2
,
2.3
(
1
) and
2.5
to the exact sequence (
2
), we obtain that

\operatorname{depth}(S_{G}/I_{G})=\operatorname{depth}(S_{G_{1}}/I_{G_{1}})+\operatorname{depth}(S_{G_{2}}/I_{G_{2}})-1
. We finish the proof.

∎

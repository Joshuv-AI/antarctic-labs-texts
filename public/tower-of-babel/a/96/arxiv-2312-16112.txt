Smooth Blowups: Global vs. Local Perspectives
Aleksey Zinger

Abstract
We show that the global and local constructions of three types of blowup of a smooth manifold along a closed submanifold in differential topology are equivalent.



1
Introduction

Blowups are surgery constructions that replace a closed submanifold
Y
in a smooth manifold
X

by a fiber bundle over
Y
.
They play prominent roles in algebraic geometry, symplectic geometry, and differential topology.
In algebraic geometry, blowups are constructed using coordinate charts; see
[
7
, p603]
.
In symplectic geometry, blowups are usually constructed via a tubular neighborhood identification;
see
[
8
, Section 8]
.
In differential topology, they can be constructed using either coordinate charts,
as in the proof of
[
2
, Lemma 2.1]
and
[
3
, Section 3]
, or
a tubular neighborhood identification, as at the bottom of
[
1
, p111]
.
The global approach via a tubular neighborhood identification
is quicker, less cumbersome, and more convenient
for studying the changes in the topology under blowups,
as in
[
9
, Section 3]
and
[
4
, Section 3.2]
.
However, the local approach via coordinate charts
may be more suitable in settings with natural collections of
coordinate charts, as is the case in
[
5
, Sections 4,5]
, for example.
It also applies in the real analytic category and leads to functorial properties
for the three kinds of blowups in the spirit of
[
3
, Sections 4,5]
.
In this paper, we show that the global and local approaches to blowup constructions
in differential topology are equivalent.

Throughout this paper,
X
denotes a smooth manifold and
Y\!\subset\!X

a closed submanifold of (real or complex) codimension
\mathfrak{c}

(
Y
is closed as a topological subspace of
X
, is without boundary,
but may be non-compact if
X
is not compact).
We consider three types of blowups of
X
along
Y
,

\pi\!:\mathbb{BL}^{\!\!\mathbb{R}}_{Y}X\longrightarrow X,\qquad\pi\!:\mathbb{BL}^{\!\!\mathbb{C}}_{Y}X\longrightarrow X,\qquad\hbox{and}\qquad\pi\!:\mathbb{BL}_{\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{c}_{1}}}X\longrightarrow X,

(1.1)

which we call the
real
,
complex
, and
\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{c}_{1}}
-augmented

(or just
\mathfrak{c}_{1}
-augmented) blowups, respectively.
The last two blowups involve a choice of complex structure on the normal bundle
\mathcal{N}_{X}Y

of
Y
in
X
and a subbundle
\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{c}_{1}}\!\subset\!\mathcal{N}_{X}Y
of corank
\mathfrak{c}_{1}\!<\!\mathfrak{c}
,
respectively.
The three maps in (
1.1
) are surjective, proper, and smooth.
The restrictions

\displaystyle\pi\!:\mathbb{BL}^{\!\!\mathbb{R}}_{Y}X\!-\!\pi^{-1}(Y)\longrightarrow X\!-\!Y,\qquad\pi\!:\mathbb{BL}^{\!\!\mathbb{C}}_{Y}X\!-\!\pi^{-1}(Y)\longrightarrow X\!-\!Y,

(1.2)

\displaystyle\hbox{and}\qquad\pi\!:\mathbb{BL}_{\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{c}_{1}}}X\!-\!\pi^{-1}(Y)\longrightarrow X\!-\!Y

(1.3)

are diffeomorphisms.
The local constructions of the complex and augmented blowups depend
on the choice of a compatible collection of local charts for
X
that cover
Y
.
Such a collection determines a complex structure on
\mathcal{N}_{X}Y
in the complex case
and a distinguished subbundle
\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{c}_{1}}\!\subset\!\mathcal{N}_{X}Y

in the augmented case.
The global constructions of these two blowups depend
on the choice of a tubular neighborhood identification.
The global construction of an augmented blowup is actually two real blowups,
first along
Y
and then along
\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{c}_{1}}\!\subset\!\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{N}_{X}Y)

followed by a real blowdown in a different direction.

The restrictions of
\pi
to the
exceptional loci

in the first two cases
are isomorphic to the real and complex projectivizations,

\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{N}_{X}Y)

The normal bundles to the exceptional loci in these two cases are

(1.4)

For

be the trivial rank

In the last case in (

\big(\mathbb{E}^{0}_{\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{c}_{1}}}X,\mathbb{E}^{0}_{\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{c}_{1}}}X\!\cap\!\mathbb{E}^{-}_{\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{c}_{1}}}X\big)\approx\big(\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}\big(\mathcal{N}_{Y}X/\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{c}_{1}}\!\oplus\!\tau_{Y}^{1}\big),\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}\big(\mathcal{N}_{Y}X/\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{c}_{1}}\!\oplus\!\tau_{Y}^{0}\big)\big)

Theorem

Let

be a smooth manifold and
Y\!\subset\!X
be a closed submanifold of codimension
\mathfrak{c}
.

(1)

The global construction of real and complex blowups in
Section
2.1
is equivalent to the local construction in Section
2.2
.

(2)

If in addition
\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{c}_{1}}\!\subset\!\mathcal{N}_{X}Y
is a subbundle
of corank
\mathfrak{c}_{1}\!<\!\mathfrak{c}
,
the global construction of
\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{c}_{1}}
-augmented blowup in
Section
3.1
is equivalent to the local construction in Section
3.2
.

It is straightforward to cut up a tubular neighborhood identification (with additional structure)
used to construct a blowup

as in Section
2.1
or
3.1
into a compatible collection
of coordinate charts (with additional structure) that determines
a blowup
\pi^{\prime}\!:\widetilde{X}^{\prime}\!\longrightarrow\!X

as in Section
2.2
or
3.2
, respectively.
Lemma
2.1
or
3.1
, as appropriate, then provides a diffeomorphism

\widetilde{\Psi}\!:\widetilde{X}\longrightarrow\widetilde{X}^{\prime}\qquad\hbox{s.t.}\quad\pi\!=\!\pi^{\prime}\!\circ\!\widetilde{\Psi}\!:\widetilde{X}\longrightarrow X\,,

(1.5)

i.e. the two blowups are
isomorphic
.
It is more technical to assemble a compatible collection of coordinate charts used to construct
a blowup
\pi^{\prime}\!:\widetilde{X}^{\prime}\!\longrightarrow\!X
in Section
2.2
or
3.2

into a tubular neighborhood identification
suitable for constructing a blowup
\pi\!:\widetilde{X}\!\longrightarrow\!X
;
this is done via Proposition
2.2
or
3.2
, as appropriate.
A tubular neighborhood identification obtained in this way can be cut up into
coordinate charts that are compatible with the original charts and thus determine
a blowup isomorphic to
\pi^{\prime}\!:\widetilde{X}^{\prime}\!\longrightarrow\!X

By Lemma
2.1
or
3.1
, the blowup determined by the new charts is also
isomorphic to
\pi\!:\widetilde{X}\!\longrightarrow\!X
.
This establishes the theorem.
Sections

and
3.2

are essentially identical to the main parts of
Sections 3.1 and 3.2, respectively, in
[

]
;
we include them for the ease of use.

3

\displaystyle\big\langle\!\{h_{\theta}(v)\!\}(w),\{h_{\theta}(v)\!\}(w)\!\big\rangle

\displaystyle=f(v)\langle w,w\rangle

\displaystyle\forall~v\!\in\!W_{\cap}^{\prime},\,w\!\in\!\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{c}_{1}}\big|_{\pi_{\mathcal{N}_{X}Y}(v)}.

By (

), the subset

is still an open neighborhood of

in

.
Define

),

(3.15)

As in the proof of Lemma

, we assume that

Define

\displaystyle h_{\eta}\in\Gamma\big(W_{\cap}^{\prime\prime},\pi_{\mathcal{N}_{X}Y}^{*}\textnormal{End}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathcal{N}_{X}Y)\!\big)\qquad\hbox{by}

\displaystyle\big\{\!h_{\eta}(v)\!\big\}(w)=\begin{cases}w\!+\!\eta\big(\pi_{\mathcal{N}_{X}Y}(v)\!\big)\big(\{h_{\theta}(v)\!\}(w)\!-\!w\big),&\hbox{if}~w\!\in\!\pi_{\mathcal{N}_{X}Y}^{*}\mathcal{N}^{\prime};\\

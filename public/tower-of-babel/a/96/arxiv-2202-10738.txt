Irrationality exponents of semi-regular continued fractions
Daniel Duverney, Iekata Shiokawa

Abstract
. We prove that the formula giving the exact value of the irrationality exponent of regular continued fractions remains valid for semi-regular continued fractions satisfying certain conditions.



1991 Mathematics Subject Classification
11A55, 11J70, 11J82

1.
Introduction

An infinite continued fraction

(1.1)

\alpha:=b_{0}+\frac{a_{1}}{b_{1}}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{}}{+}\frac{a_{2}}{b_{2}}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{}}{+\cdots+}\frac{a_{n}}{b_{n}}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{}}{+\cdots}

is called
semi-regular
if
b_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
and

(1.2)

a_{n}\in\left\{-1,1\right\},\qquad b_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0},\qquad b_{n}+a_{n+1}\geq 1\qquad\left(n\geq 1\right),

with the additional condition

(1.3)

b_{n}+a_{n+1}\geq 2\qquad\text{infinitely often.}

It is known that semi-regular continued fractions (SRCF) are well defined and
convergent by Tietze Theorem
[
24
]
. Examples of SRCF (among others) are:

(i) The
regular
continued fractions (RCF), where
a_{n}=1
for every

n\geq 1.

(ii) The
negative
continued fractions (NCF), also known as

backward
continued fractions, where
a_{n}=-1
for every
n\geq 1

[
4
]
.

(iii) The
nearest integer
continued fractions (NICF), where

b_{n}+a_{n+1}\geq 2
and
b_{n}\geq 2
for every
n\geq 1

[
17
]
.

(iv) The
singular
continued fractions (SCF), where
b_{n}+a_{n}\geq 2

and
b_{n}\geq 2
for every
n\geq 1

[
17
]
.

(v) The
Lehner
continued fractions (LCF), where
(b_{n},a_{n+1})=(1,1)
or
(2,-1)
for every
n\geq 0
(
[
5
]
,
[
15
]
).

It should be noted that the condition (
1.3
) is always realized if
there exist infinitely many
n
such that
a_{n}=1.
So (
1.3
) is
always realized, except if there exists
n
such that

(1.4)

x_{n}:=\frac{a_{n+1}}{b_{n+1}}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{}}{+}\frac{a_{n+2}}{b_{n+2}}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{}}{+}\frac{a_{n+3}}{b_{n+3}}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{}}{+\cdots}\qquad\left(n\geq 0\right)

is a NCF and
b_{k}=2
for all large
k
(see Remark
1
below).
Hence in the definition of SRCF the condition (
1.3
) can be replaced
by

If
a_{n}=-1
for all large
n,
then

b_{n}\geq 3
infinitely often.

This shows that NCF are of special importance among SRCF.

The story of SRCF seems to begin with Lagrange, who mentioned in

[
14
, Sections 6 and 7]
the possibility of introducing signs in the
partial numerators of regular continued fractions and gave the way for
transforming such continued fractions into regular ones. Tietze
[
24
]

proved the convergence of SRCF and the irrationality of their values. Chapter
5 of Perron’s classical book
[
17
]
is entirely devoted to SRCF. Holzbaur
and Riederle
[
10
]
characterized SRCF which represent quadratic
irrationals. Pinner
[
18
]
computed certain inhomogeneous approximation
constants by using NCF expansions. Sarma and Kushwaha
[
21
]
examined
precisely the algorithm to convert a SRCF into regular, even or odd continued fractions.

SRCF have also been widely discussed from the metric theoretical point of view
(see
[
5
]
and
[
13
]
). In particular, the map attached to NCF defined
by Rényi
[
19
]
has been studied by many authors (see
[
2
]
and
the recent papers
[
11
]
,
[
23
]
).

Let
\alpha
be the SRCF defined by (
1.1
). As usual, we define for

n\geq 1

(1.5)

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{lll}p_{-1}=1,&p_{0}=b_{0},&p_{n}=b_{n}p_{n-1}+a_{n}p_{n-2},\\
q_{-1}=0,&q_{0}=1,&q_{n}=b_{n}q_{n-1}+a_{n}q_{n-2}.\end{array}\right.

It is well known (
[
7
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
17
]
) that

(1.6)

b_{0}+\frac{a_{1}}{b_{1}}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{}}{+}\frac{a_{2}}{b_{2}}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{}}{+\cdots+}\frac{a_{n}}{b_{n}}=\frac{p_{n}}{q_{n}}\qquad\left(n\geq 1\right),

and an easy induction using (
1.5
) shows that

(1.7)

p_{n}q_{n-1}-p_{n-1}q_{n}=\left(-1\right)^{n-1}a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{n}\qquad\left(n\geq 1\right),

which yields immediately

(1.8)

\frac{p_{n}}{q_{n}}=b_{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{k-1}a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{k}}{q_{k-1}q_{k}}.

By (
1.7
), we observe that
p_{n}
and
q_{n}
are coprime for all

n\geq 1.\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt

For a real number
\alpha,
the irrationality exponent
\mu\left(\alpha\right)
is defined by the infimum of the set of numbers
\mu
for
which the inequality

(1.9)

\left|\alpha-\frac{p}{q}\right|<\frac{1}{q^{\mu}}

has only finitely many rational solutions
p/q
, or equivalently the supremum
of the set of numbers
\mu
for which the inequality (
1.9
) has
infinitely many solutions. If
\alpha
is irrational, then
\mu\left(\alpha\right)\geq 2
. If
\alpha
is a real algebraic irrational number, then

\mu\left(\alpha\right)=2
by Roth’s theorem
[
20
]
. If
\mu\left(\alpha\right)=\infty,
then
\alpha
is called a Liouville number.

When
\alpha
defined by (
1.1
) is a regular continued fraction, that is
when
a_{n}=1
for all
n\geq 1,
it is known that

(1.10)

\mu\left(\alpha\right)=1+\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log q_{n+1}}{\log q_{n}}=2+\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log b_{n+1}}{\log q_{n}}

(see for example
[
22
, Theorem 1]
). The main purpose of this paper is
to examine to what extent this formula applies to SRCF. Our main result is

Theorem 1
.

Let
\alpha
be the SRCF defined by (
1.1
) with (
1.2
)
and (
1.3
). Assume that one of the following conditions holds:

(A) The number of consecutive
n
such that
a_{n}=-1
is bounded.

(B)
b_{n}\geq 2
for all large
n
and the number of consecutive
n
such that

b_{n}=2
is bounded.

(C)
b_{n}+a_{n}\geq 2
for all large
n.

(D)
b_{n}\geq 2
and
b_{n}+a_{n+1}\geq 2
for all large
n.

Then (
1.10
) holds.

By
(C)
and
(D)
, we see that (
1.10
) holds for all SCF and NICF
respectively, but it is not valid in general for LCF. Indeed, (
1.10
)
implies that
\mu\left(\alpha\right)=2
as soon as
b_{n}
is bounded
since
q_{n}\rightarrow\infty
by Tietze Theorem. As any irrational number

\alpha\in\left]1,2\right[
can be expanded uniquely in a LCF
[
15
]
,
(
1.10
) is false for LCF if
\mu\left(\alpha\right)>2.
On the other
hand,
(A)
implies that
\mu\left(\alpha\right)=2
for all LCF for which
the number of consecutive
n
such that
\left(b_{n},a_{n+1}\right)=\left(2,-1\right)
is bounded.

Formula (
1.10
) is not valid either for all NCF (see Section
3

below). However,
(B)
implies that (
1.10
) holds for all NCF for which
the number of consecutive
n
such that
b_{n}=2
is bounded.

By adding a condition, we can deduce from Theorem
1
an expression of
the irrationality exponent of
\alpha
in terms of the
b_{n}
’s.

Corollary 1
.

Let
\alpha
be as in Theorem
1
. Assume that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n}{\log\left(b_{1}b_{2}\cdots b_{n}\right)}=0.

Then

(1.11)

\mu\left(\alpha\right)=2+\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log b_{n+1}}{\log(b_{1}b_{2}\cdots b_{n})}.

Note that (
1.11
) also results from
[
8
, Corollary 4]
when the
series
\sum

\left(b_{n}b_{n+1}\right)^{-1}
is convergent and, in the
case of regular continued fractions, from
[
22
, Corollary 2]
.

In order to prove Theorem
1
, we will need to present in some details
the bases of the theory of SRCF. This theory is exposed in Perron’s book

[
17
]
and, to our knowledge, it has not been updated since then. Besides,
some proofs can be simplified, as we are interested here only in infinite
SRCF. Thus, we will study first some basic properties of infinite SRCF in
Section
2
. In Sections
3
and
4
respectively
we will prove formulas for the irrationality exponents of NCF and LCF by
transforming them into RCF. In particular, we will give in Theorem

2
examples of NCF for which (
1.10
) is not valid. In Section

5
, we will state and prove Theorem
4
, which gives
a rather general formula allowing to compute irrationality exponents. In
Section
6
we will prove Theorem
1
and Corollary

1
. Finally, we will give some examples of application of Corollary

1
in Section
7
.

2.
Convergence and irrationality of infinite SRCF

In this section we recall some basic results on infinite SRCF.

Proposition 1
.

Let
q_{n}
be defined by (
1.5
). We have for
n\geq 0

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{l}q_{n}\geq 1\\
q_{n}+a_{n+1}q_{n-1}\geq 1\\
q_{n+1}>q_{n}\quad\text{if}\quad b_{n+1}\geq 2\text{ or }a_{n+1}=1\\
q_{n+2}>q_{n}>q_{n+1}\quad\text{if}\quad b_{n+1}=1\text{ and }a_{n+1}=-1.\end{array}\right.

Proof.

We prove first by induction that

(2.1)

q_{n}\geq 1\quad\text{and}\quad q_{n}+a_{n+1}q_{n-1}\geq 1

by following Offer
[
16
]
. When
n=0,

q_{0}+a_{1}q_{-1}=q_{0}=1
by
(
1.5
). Assume that (
2.1
) holds for some
n\geq 0.
Then by
(
1.1
), (
1.2
) and (
1.5
)

q_{n+1}=b_{n+1}q_{n}+a_{n+1}q_{n-1}\geq q_{n}+a_{n+1}q_{n-1}\geq 1

and
q_{n+1}+a_{n+2}q_{n}=\left(b_{n+1}+a_{n+2}\right)q_{n}+a_{n+1}q_{n-1}\geq q_{n}+a_{n+1}q_{n-1}\geq 1,
which proves (
2.1
). If

b_{n+1}\geq 2,
then by (
2.1
)

q_{n+1}\geq 2q_{n}+a_{n+1}q_{n-1}\geq q_{n}+1,

and the same inequation holds evidently if
a_{n+1}=1.
Finally, if

b_{n+1}=1
and
a_{n+1}=-1,
then
a_{n+2}=1
by (
1.2
), and so

q_{n+2}=b_{n+2}q_{n+1}+q_{n}>q_{n}
by (
2.1
), which completes the
proof of Proposition
1
.
∎

Proposition
1
shows that the the convergents of the infinite SRCF
defined by (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) are well defined since
q_{n}\geq 1

for all
n\geq 0.
It also shows that the sequence
q_{n}
is not always
increasing, for example in the case of the Lehner continued fraction

1+\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}=1+\frac{1}{2}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{}}{-}\frac{1}{1}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{}}{+}\frac{1}{2}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{}}{-}\frac{1}{1}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{}}{+}\frac{1}{2}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{}}{-}\frac{1}{1}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{}}{+\cdots}.

However, the sequence
q_{n}
is increasing in the cases of regular, negative,
nearest integer and singular SRCF.

Proposition 2
.

(Tietze Theorem) The infinite SRCF defined by (
1.1
) and
(
1.2
) is convergent and
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}q_{n}=+\infty.

The proof of Tietze Theorem in the general case uses continuants (
[
17
]
,

[
16
]
). It is rather long and we don’t reproduce it here. However, when
the sequence
q_{n}
is increasing, the convergence of
p_{n}/q_{n}
follows
immediately from (
1.8
).

Proposition 3
.

For
n\geq 0,
let
x_{n}
be defined by (
1.4
). Then for

n\geq 0

0<x_{n}\leq 1\text{ if }a_{n+1}=1,\qquad-1\leq x_{n}<0\text{ if }a_{n+1}=-1.

Proof.

Define for
n\geq 1
and
k\geq 0

(2.2)

x_{n,k}:=\frac{a_{n+1}}{b_{n+1}}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{}}{+}\frac{a_{n+2}}{b_{n+2}}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{}}{+\cdots+}\frac{a_{n+k}}{b_{n+k}}.

As
x_{n}=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}x_{n,k}
by Proposition
2
, we
have to prove that for
n\geq 0
and
k\geq 1,

(2.3)

0<x_{n,k}\leq 1\text{ if }a_{n+1}=1,\qquad-1\leq x_{n,k}<0\text{ if }a_{n+1}=-1.

The proof is by induction on
k.
If
k=1,
then
x_{n,1}=a_{n+1}/b_{n+1}
and
so (
1.2
) implies (
2.3
) for
k=1
and any
n\geq 0.
Assume that
(
2.3
) holds for some
k\geq 1
and any
n\geq 0.
Then by (
2.2
) we have

(2.4)

x_{n,k+1}=\frac{a_{n+1}}{b_{n+1}+x_{n+1,k}},

where for any
n\geq 0

(2.5)

0<x_{n+1,k}\leq 1\text{ if }a_{n+2}=1,\qquad-1\leq x_{n+1,k}<0\text{ if
}a_{n+2}=-1.

If
b_{n+1}\geq 2,
then by (
2.5
)

(2.6)

b_{n+1}+x_{n+1,k}\geq 1.

Otherwise, we have
b_{n+1}=1
and therefore
a_{n+2}=1
by (
1.2
), and
so we find again (
2.6
) by the first inequality in (
2.5
).
Hence (
2.4
) and (
2.6
) imply

0<x_{n,k+1}\leq 1\text{ if }a_{n+1}=1,\qquad-1\leq x_{n,k+1}<0\text{ if
}a_{n+1}=-1,

which proves (
2.3
) by induction, and Proposition
3
follows.
∎

Proposition 4
.

The value of the infinite SRCF
\alpha
defined by (
1.1
)
with (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) is irrational.

Proof.

Let
x_{n}
be defined in (
1.4
). It is well known, and easy to check by
induction, that for all
n\geq-1

\alpha=\frac{p_{n+1}+x_{n+1}p_{n}}{q_{n+1}+x_{n+1}q_{n}}.

This yields immediately

(2.7)

\left|\alpha-\frac{p_{n}}{q_{n}}\right|=\frac{1}{q_{n}\left|\xi_{n+1}\right|},

where
\xi_{n+1}:=q_{n+1}+x_{n+1}q_{n}

\left(n\geq 0\right).
Assume
first that there exist infinitely many
n
such that
a_{n}=1.
Then by
Proposition
3
there are infinitely many
n
such that

x_{n+1}>0
and therefore by (
2.7
)

(2.8)

0<\left|\alpha-\frac{p_{n}}{q_{n}}\right|\leq\frac{1}{q_{n}q_{n+1}}\leq\frac{2}{q_{n}q_{n+1}}

for infinitely many
n.
Now if
a_{n}=-1
for all large
n,
there is no loss
of generality in assuming that
a_{n}=-1
for all
n\geq 1.
In this case

x_{n}
is a negative continued fraction and there exist infinitely many

n\geq N
such that
b_{n+1}\geq 3,
in which case

q_{n+1}=b_{n+1}q_{n}-q_{n-1}\geq 2q_{n}+q_{n}-q_{n-1}>2q_{n},

since
q_{n}>q_{n-1}
by Proposition
1
. As
-1<x_{n+1}\leq 0
by
Proposition
3
, we get

\xi_{n+1}=q_{n+1}+x_{n+1}q_{n}>q_{n+1}-q_{n}\geq\frac{1}{2}q_{n+1}

and therefore (
2.8
) holds again for infinitely many
n.
Now, assume
that
\alpha
is a rational number,
\alpha=c/d
with
d>0.
Then by
(
2.8
) there exist infinitely many
n
such that
1\leq\left|cq_{n}-dp_{n}\right|\leq 2d/q_{n+1}
since
cq_{n}-dp_{n}
is a non-zero
integer. This yields a contradiction for
n
large because
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}q_{n+1}=+\infty.

∎

Remark 1
.

The continued fraction

(2.9)

\omega:=\frac{1}{2}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{}}{-}\frac{1}{2}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{}}{-\cdots-}\frac{1}{2}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{}}{-\cdots}

is convergent by Proposition
2
, and clearly
\omega=1/(2-\omega),

whence
\omega=1.
This explains the condition (
1.3
) in the definition
of SRCF.

3.
Negative continued fractions

In this section, we study in more detail negative continued
fractions (NCF), in order to show that formula (
1.10
) may not hold in
this case. We will use the following notation:

b_{0}-\frac{1}{b_{1}}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{}}{-}\frac{1}{b_{2}}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{}}{-\cdots-}\frac{1}{b_{n}}=\left[b_{0};b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{n}\right]^{-},

which is similar to that for regular continued fractions (RCF):

b_{0}+\frac{1}{b_{1}}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{}}{+}\frac{1}{b_{2}}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{}}{+\cdots+}\frac{1}{b_{n}}=\left[b_{0};b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{n}\right].

As already observed, NCF are of special importance among SRCF. They present
two characteristics:

(a)
b_{n}\geq 3

infinitely often, in order for NCF to be irrational
(Remark
1
).

(b) For all
n\geq 0,
the convergent
p_{n}/q_{n}
is an approximation by
excess of the infinite NCF
\alpha
defined in (
1.1
). This results
immediately from (
1.8
).

A constructive process, which goes back to Lagrange
[
14
]
, allows to
transform any NCF into a RCF by using the following two elementary formulas,
which are easy to prove. The first one is

(3.1)

x-\frac{1}{y}=x-1+\frac{1}{1}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{}}{+}\frac{1}{y-1}.

The second one is

(3.2)

x+\frac{1}{0}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{}}{+}\frac{1}{y}=x+y.

Assume first that
b_{0},b_{1},\ldots,b_{n}
are indeterminates, and let

T_{n}:=\left[b_{0};b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{n}\right]^{-}.

By using (
3.1
), we get easily

(3.3)

T_{n}=\left[b_{0}-1;\underline{1,b_{1}-2},\underline{1,b_{2}-2},\ldots,\underline{1,b_{n}-2},1\right].

Now assume that
b_{0}
is a rational integer and that
b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{n},\ldots
are rational integers with
b_{n}\geq 2
for all
n\geq 1

and
b_{n}\geq 3
for infinitely many
n\geq 1.
Let
n_{k}
be defined by

n_{0}=0
and for all
k\geq 1

(3.4)

b_{n_{k}}\geq 3\quad\text{and}\quad b_{n}=2\text{ if }n_{k-1}<n<n_{k}.

Then (
3.3
) yields by using (
3.2
)

(3.5)

T_{n_{k}}=\left[b_{0}-1;\underline{n_{1}-n_{0},b_{n_{1}}-2},\underline{n_{2}-n_{1},b_{n_{2}}-2},\ldots,\underline{n_{k}-n_{k-1},b_{n_{k}}-2},1\right].

Consider the infinite RCF

\displaystyle\beta

\displaystyle=\left[b_{0}-1;\underline{n_{1}-n_{0},b_{n_{1}}-2},\underline{n_{2}-n_{1},b_{n_{2}}-2},\ldots,\underline{n_{k}-n_{k-1},b_{n_{k}}-2},\ldots\right]

\displaystyle=\left[c_{0};c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4},\ldots,c_{2k-1},c_{2k},\ldots\right],

where
c_{0}=b_{0}-1
and for
k\geq 1

(3.6)

c_{2k-1}=n_{k}-n_{k-1},\quad c_{2k}=b_{n_{k}}-2

with
n_{k}
defined in (
3.4
). We observe by (
3.5
) that

T_{n_{k}}=\left[c_{0};c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4},\ldots,c_{2k-1},c_{2k},1\right].

Let
P_{n}/Q_{n}
be the
n
-th convergent of
\beta.
Then

\left|T_{n_{k}}-\frac{P_{2k}}{Q_{2k}}\right|=\left|\frac{P_{2k}+P_{2k-1}}{Q_{2k}+Q_{2k-1}}-\frac{P_{2k}}{Q_{2k}}\right|=\frac{1}{Q_{2k}\left(Q_{2k}+Q_{2k-1}\right)}.

Hence
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\left(T_{n_{k}}-P_{2k}/Q_{2k}\right)=0,

which proves that

(3.7)

\left[b_{0};b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{n},\ldots\right]^{-}=\left[c_{0};c_{1},c_{2},\ldots,c_{n},\ldots\right],

where the
c_{n}
are defined by
c_{0}=b_{0}-1
and (
3.6
). This formula
allows to transform any infinite NCF into an infinite RCF.

Remark 2
.

Formula (
3.7
) is equivalent to Formula (6) in

[
11
]
(see also
[
5
, Proposition 2]
), which allows to transform any
RCF into a NCF:

\left[0;a_{1},a_{2},\ldots\right]=[1;\underset{\left(a_{1}-1\right)\text{ {times}}}{\underline{2,\ldots,2}},a_{2}+2,\underset{\left(a_{3}-1\right)\text{ {times}}}{\underline{2,\ldots,2}},a_{4}+2,\ldots]^{-}.

Remark 3
.

More generally, any SRCF can be transformed into a regular one
by using (
3.1
) and (
3.2
), as shown in
[
17
, p. 159]
. The
converse is also true
[
5
]
.

Now we can show that (
1.10
) doesn’t hold in general for NCF. For this,
we consider an arbitrary increasing sequence
n_{k}
with
n_{0}=0
and define

b_{n}
by

(3.8)

b_{n_{k}}=3,\qquad b_{n}=2\text{\quad if\quad}n_{k-1}<n<n_{k}.

Let
\alpha
be the infinite NCF defined by (
3.8
). Assume that
(
1.10
) is true for every NCF. Then
\mu\left(\alpha\right)=2
since

b_{n}
is bounded. However we can show that, for every
s\geq 2,
it is
possible to construct a sequence
n_{k}
such that
\mu\left(\alpha\right)=s.
Indeed, we know that the expansion in RCF of

5.
A general formula for the irrationality exponent

In this section, we prove the following theorem, which allows
to compute the irrationality exponent of a real number
\alpha
by knowing
suitable rational approximations.

Theorem 4
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}.
Let
p_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}
and

q_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
be coprime for all
n\geq 0,
with
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}q_{n}=+\infty.
Assume that there exist positive
constants
\rho,

\sigma
and
\tau
such that
q_{n+1}\geq\rho q_{n}
and

(5.1)

\frac{\sigma}{q_{n}q_{n+1}}\leq\left|\alpha-\frac{p_{n}}{q_{n}}\right|\leq\frac{\tau}{q_{n}q_{n+1}}

for all large
n
. Then
\alpha
is irrational and its irrationality exponent
is

(5.2)

\mu\left(\alpha\right)=1+\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log q_{n+1}}{\log q_{n}}.

Proof.

There is no loss of generality in assuming that
q_{n+1}\geq\rho q_{n}
and
(
5.1
) hold for every
n\geq 0.
Let
\lambda_{n}:=\log q_{n+1}/\log q_{n}
and
\lambda:=\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\lambda_{n}.
Let

\varepsilon>0.
By (
5.1
), we have

\left|\alpha-\frac{p_{n}}{q_{n}}\right|\leq\frac{\tau}{q_{n}^{\lambda_{n}+1}}\leq\frac{1}{q_{n}^{\lambda_{n}+1-\varepsilon/2}}

for all large
n
since
q_{n}\rightarrow+\infty.
If
\lambda=\infty,
then

\mu\left(\alpha\right)=\infty
and (
5.2
) holds. So we may
assume that
\lambda<\infty.
As
\lambda_{n}\geq\lambda-\varepsilon/2
for
infinitely many
n,
the inequality

(5.3)

\left|\alpha-\frac{p_{n}}{q_{n}}\right|\leq\frac{1}{q_{n}^{\lambda+1-\varepsilon}}

holds for infinitely many
n.
Hence the inequality

(5.4)

\left|\alpha-\frac{p}{q}\right|\leq\frac{1}{q^{\lambda+1-\varepsilon}}

has infinitely many solutions
p/q
because
p_{n}
and
q_{n}
are coprime
and
q_{n}\rightarrow+\infty.
This proves that
\mu\left(\alpha\right)\geq\lambda+1.
We prove that
\lambda+1\geq\mu\left(\alpha\right).
Let
again
\varepsilon>0.
We consider the inequality

(5.5)

\left|\alpha-\frac{p}{q}\right|\leq\frac{1}{q^{\lambda+1+\varepsilon}}.

Our aim is to prove that this inequality has only finitely many solutions

p/q.
Without loss of generality, we can assume that
p
and
q>0
are
coprime. We distinguish two cases.

First case
: there exists
n
such that
p/q=p_{n}/q_{n}.
Then

p_{n}=p
and
q_{n}=q
since
p/q
and
p_{n}/q_{n}
are both irreducible. By
(
5.1
), we have

\left|\alpha-\frac{p}{q}\right|=\left|\alpha-\frac{p_{n}}{q_{n}}\right|\geq\frac{\sigma}{q_{n}^{\lambda_{n}+1}}=\frac{\sigma}{q^{\lambda_{n}+1}}=\frac{\sigma q^{\varepsilon/2}}{q^{\lambda_{n}+1+\varepsilon/2}}.

For large
q,
we have
\sigma q^{\varepsilon/2}\geq 1
and
\lambda_{n}+1+\varepsilon/2\leq\lambda+1+\varepsilon
since
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}q_{n}=+\infty.
Hence

(5.6)

\left|\alpha-\frac{p}{q}\right|\geq\frac{1}{q^{\lambda+1+\varepsilon}}.

Second case
: for all
n,
we have
pq_{n}-qp_{n}\neq 0.
Fix a
positive integer
Q
such that
2\tau Q>\rho q_{1}
and assume that
q\geq Q.

Let
n=n(q)\geq 1
be the least integer such that

(5.7)

\frac{\tau}{\rho q_{k}}q<\frac{1}{2}

for all
k\geq n+1,
which exists since
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}q_{k}=+\infty.
Then

(5.8)

q_{n}\leq\frac{2\tau}{\rho}q.

As
pq_{n+1}-qp_{n+1}\neq 0,
we can write by (
5.1
) and (
5.7
):

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle\leq\left|pq_{n+1}-qp_{n+1}\right|\leq q\left|q_{n+1}\alpha-p_{n+1}\right|+q_{n+1}\left|q\alpha-p\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\tau}{q_{n+2}}q+q_{n+1}\left|q\alpha-p\right|\leq\frac{\tau}{\rho q_{n+1}}q+q_{n+1}\left|q\alpha-p\right|<\frac{1}{2}+qq_{n+1}\left|\alpha-\frac{p}{q}\right|.

By using (
5.8
), this implies

\left|\alpha-\frac{p}{q}\right|>\frac{1}{2qq_{n+1}}=\frac{1}{2qq_{n}^{\lambda_{n}}}\geq\frac{\rho^{\lambda_{n}}}{2^{\lambda_{n}+1}\tau^{\lambda_{n}}q^{\lambda_{n}+1}}=\frac{\rho^{\lambda_{n}}q^{\varepsilon/2}}{2^{\lambda_{n}+1}\tau^{\lambda_{n}}q^{\lambda_{n}+1+\varepsilon/2}}.

However there exist two constants
C
and
C^{\prime}
such that
C\leq\lambda_{n}\leq C^{\prime},
because
\lambda
is finite,
q_{n+1}\geq\rho q_{n}
and
q_{n}\rightarrow+\infty.
So there exists
C^{\prime\prime}>0

such that

\left|\alpha-\frac{p}{q}\right|>\frac{C^{\prime\prime}q^{\varepsilon/2}}{q^{\lambda_{n}+1+\varepsilon/2}}.

Now
n=n\left(q\right)
tends to infinity with
q,
and therefore for

q\geq Q^{\prime}>Q
we have
C^{\prime\prime}q^{\varepsilon/2}\geq 1
and

\lambda_{n}\leq\lambda+\varepsilon/2.
This yields for large

(5.9)

\left|\alpha-\frac{p}{q}\right|>\frac{1}{q^{\lambda+1+\varepsilon}}.

By (
5.6
) and (
5.9
) we conclude that the inequality
(
5.5
) has only finitely many solutions for every
\varepsilon>0.

The proof of Theorem
4
is complete.
∎

We give here examples of semi-regular continued fractions and

Example 1.
For
c\neq 0,-1,-2,\ldots,
the modified Bessel function is

{}_{0}F_{1}\left(c;z\right):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n}}{\left(c\right)_{n}n!},

and
(c)_{n}=c\left(c+1\right)\cdots\left(c+n-1\right)
for

It is known that

\frac{{}_{0}F_{1}\left(1;z\right)}{{}_{0}F_{1}\left(2;z\right)}=1+\frac{z}{2}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{}}{+}\frac{z}{3}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{}}{+}\frac{z}{4}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{}}{+}\frac{z}{5}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{}}{+\cdots}

(cf.
[
12
, Formula (6.1.51)]
). Corollary
1

(B),

(C)
or

(D)
applies and we get

\mu\left(1+\frac{a_{1}}{2}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{}}{+}\frac{a_{2}}{3}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{}}{+}\frac{a_{3}}{4}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{}}{+}\frac{a_{4}}{5}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{}}{+}\frac{a_{5}}{6}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{}}{+}\frac{a_{6}}{7}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{}}{+\cdots}\right)=2

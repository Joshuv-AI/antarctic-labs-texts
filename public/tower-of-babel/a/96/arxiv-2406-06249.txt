Hierarchical cubes: Gibbs measures and decay of correlations
Sabine Jansen, Jan Philipp Neumann

Abstract
. We study a hierarchical model of non-overlapping cubes of sidelengths 2 j 2^{j} , j ∈ ℤ j\in\mathbb{Z} . The model allows for cubes of arbitrarily small size and the activities need not be translationally invariant. It can also be recast as a spin system on a tree with a long-range hard-core interaction. We prove necessary and sufficient conditions for the existence and uniqueness of Gibbs measures, discuss fragmentation and condensation, and prove bounds on the decay of two-point correlation functions. Mathematics Subject Classification : 82B20 Keywords : existence and uniqueness of Gibbs measures; exponential decay of correlations; condensation; hierarchical model



1.
Introduction

The present article is an addendum to the article
[
13
]
. There, the first author introduced a discrete polymer model of non-overlapping cubes of unbounded size. At every scale
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, one places a tiling of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
consisting of translates of the prototypical
j
-block
\{1,\ldots,2^{j}\}^{d}
in such a way that different blocks only overlap if one is contained in the other. The model is inspired by hierarchical structures in the theory of the renormalisation group
[
8
,
3
]
.

Here we complement the analysis from
[
13
]
by addressing infinite-volume Gibbs measures and decay of correlations. In addition, we extend the model in two directions: (a) the activities need not be scale-wise constant (same-size cubes may have different activities); (b) we allow for arbitrarily small cubes of side-lengths
2^{-j}
,
j\in\mathbb{N}
. The second extension is motivated by Mandelbrot’s fractal percolation model, see e.g.
[
17
,
4
,
15
,
16
]
, and the question whether the random sets in that model can be constructed with Gibbs measures.

Our central result, Theorem
2.3
, gives necessary and sufficient criteria for the existence of a Gibbs measure and says that, if a Gibbs measure exists, it is unique. The criteria are formulated as summability conditions on (effective) activities. Propositions
2.5
and
2.7
link these conditions to the physical phenomena of fragmentation and condensation in which mass escapes to microscopic cubes (fragmentation) or macroscopic cubes (condensation). Roughly, an infinite-volume Gibbs measure exists if and only if there is neither fragmentation nor condensation. In particular, at the point
\mu=\mu_{c}
of first-order phase transitions in
[
13
, Section 5]
, the Gibbs measure is unique – non-differentiability of the pressure and non-uniqueness of Gibbs measures are not equivalent, contrary to the Ising model, see
[
9
, Theorem 3.34]
.

An interesting subtlety shows up for small cubes and inhomogeneous activities: Theorem
2.3
covers situations in which some finite-volume partition functions are infinite but nevertheless a Gibbs measure exists. Clearly, “finite volumes”, as in bounded regions of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, generally contain an infinitude of small cubes and may have infinite partition function but we can still define a Gibbs measure with the GNZ conditions from the theory of Gibbs point processes (
[
18
, Theorems 2 and 2’]
,
[
2
, Equation (1)]
), see Definition
2.1
. (An alternative point of view, based on a reinterpretation of our model as a spin system on a tree, is sketched below.) With this definition already the existence of finite-volume Gibbs measures becomes a non-trivial issue.

As a minor observation, Mandelbrot’s fractal percolation is not given by one of our Gibbs measures (Proposition
2.4
).

Turning back to the scale-wise homogeneous setting, we investigate decay of correlations (Theorems
2.8
and
2.9
). This is motivated by standard results for spin systems on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, where we expect exponential decay of truncated correlations away from phase transitions and slower decay at the point of phase transition, e.g. algebraic at critical points (
[
9
,
7
,
1
]
); “exponential” refers to bounds of the type
C\exp(-\gamma||i-j||)
as the Euclidean distance
||i-j||
between the sites indexing spins
\sigma_{i}
,
\sigma_{j}
goes to infinity. In our model the Euclidean distance is replaced by an ultrametric distance
D(B,B^{\prime})=2^{d\,\mathrm{lcs}(B,B^{\prime})}
between blocks, with
\mathrm{lcs}(B,B^{\prime})\in\mathbb{Z}
the scale of the smallest block containing both
B
and
B^{\prime}
. In the gas phase, correlations decay as
\exp(-\mathrm{const}\,D(B,B^{\prime}))
(Theorem
2.8
); for a concrete class of models with a first-order phase transition, the decay at the point of phase transition is instead subexponentially bounded by
\exp(-\mathrm{const}\,D(B,B^{\prime})^{\alpha})
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
, (Theorem
2.9
).

To conclude we highlight an alternative point of view on our model as a spin system on a tree. The set of admissible cubes has a natural tree structure. By drawing edges from any block to those it contains one scale below, our cube set becomes a regular
2^{d}
-ary tree (also called Cayley tree or Bethe lattice of degree/branching number
2^{d}
) whose edges we prefer to think of as oriented toward decreasing scales, see Figure
1
(cf. also
[
14
]
with regard to this tree structure arising via the successively refined tiling of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with cubes). Each vertex of the tree comes with a
\{0,1\}
-valued spin variable. The interaction is a long-range hard-core interaction: if a vertex (block) is occupied (spin
1
), then all descendants and ancestors in the tree must be unoccupied (spin
0
).

There is an extensive body of literature related to the theory of Gibbs measures on trees and their relationship with tree-indexed Markov chains, the fundamentals of which can be found in Georgii
[
11
, Chapter 12]
. More recent developments include the articles
[
5
,
6
]
by Coquille, Külske and Le Ny. The general theory is not applicable to our context because of the interaction’s long range. Our Gibbs measures are not described as tree-indexed Markov chains, instead we provide a concrete characterisation as
hierarchical measures
(defined in Lemma
3.3
).

Our results easily generalise to similar trees. The most obvious modification simply replaces the cube sidelength base/subdivision parameter
M=2
by any larger integer.
All our results then carry over mutatis mutandis and, in particular, the relationship of scaling limits and fractal/Mandelbrot percolation with its Galton-Watson construction remains intact.

The remainder of this article is organised as follows. In Section
2
we first give the formal definition of our model and then formulate our main result, Theorem
2.3
, and the observation on Mandelbrot percolation not being representable via one of our Gibbs measures (Subsection
2.1
). Next we explain the relation with condensation and fragmentation in Subsection
2.2
and analyse decay of correlations in Subsection
2.3
. Proofs are given in Section
3
.

2.
Main results

2.1.
Existence and uniqueness of infinite-volume Gibbs measures

Fix a dimension
d\in\mathbb{N}
. We define the set of admissible blocks to be the union
\mathbb{B}=\bigcup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{B}_{j}

where

\mathbb{B}_{j}:=\bigl\{\mathbf{k}+[0,2^{j})^{d}\mid\mathbf{k}\in 2^{j}\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}\bigr\}

denotes the set of blocks of scale
j
or simply
j
-blocks. Thus
\mathbb{B}_{j}
consists of non-overlapping cubes of sidelength
2^{j}
all contained in the non-negative orthant
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}=[0,\infty)^{d}
. Allowing for
\mathbf{k}\in 2^{j}\mathbb{Z}^{d}
instead results in a model on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
in which different orthants do not interact. Cubes may be arbitrarily small or large – we do not impose any bounds on
j
.

Blocks interact through a hard-core potential that forbids overlap. Thus
B\in\mathbb{B}
cannot occur alongside any additional block
B^{\prime}
from

\mathbb{I}_{B}:=\{B^{\prime}\in\mathbb{B}\mid B^{\prime}\cap B\neq\varnothing\},

the set of blocks that intersect
B
. Prohibiting, in particular, block multiplicities other than
0
or
1
, our configuration space
\Omega=\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{B})
consists of the collection of all subsets
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathbb{B}
. It is equipped with the
\sigma
-algebra generated by the sets
\{\mathcal{B}\in\Omega\mid\mathcal{B}\ni B\}
,
B\in\mathbb{B}
.

Figure 1.
A hierarchical cube configuration in dimension
d=1
. The top part shows a truncated portion of the set
\mathbb{B}
of admissible cubes (intervals) with mid-point nodes and dashed lines emphasising the implicit binary tree structure. Occupied cubes are highlighted in scale-dependent colours, both in the tree structure (top) and on the real line (bottom). The shaded areas visualise excluded volume effects.

Figure 2.
A hierarchical cube configuration in dimension
d=2
consisting of occupied cubes (squares) on finitely many colour-coded scales. The small points in the squares’ bottom left corners mark the respective
\mathbf{k}
-parameters from the definition of
\mathbb{B}
, the squares’ closed sides (bottom and left) are emphasised with darker lines.

In the following, the symbol
\omega
stands not only for elements
\omega\in\Omega
but also for the configuration-valued random variable given by the identity map
\omega\mapsto\omega
. This allows us to use notational shorthand from probability theory, e.g., the above generating events are written as
\{\omega\ni B\}
.

We define infinite-volume Gibbs measures through the GNZ equation (
[
18
, Theorem 2, Eq. (3.3) or Theorem 2, Cond. (3)]
,
[
2
, Eq. (1)]
), borrowed from the theory of Gibbs point processes rather than the usual DLR conditions. We discuss the relation between the definitions toward the end of this subsection.

Definition 2.1
.

A probability measure
\mathbb{P}
on
\Omega
is a Gibbs measure for the activity
z:\mathbb{B}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
if

\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\sum_{B\in\omega}F(B,\,\omega)\Bigr]=\sum_{B\in\mathbb{B}}z(B)\,\mathbb{E}\Bigl[{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{\{\omega\cap\mathbb{I}_{B}=\varnothing\}}\,F(B,\,\omega\cup\{B\})\Bigr].

for all measurable
F:\mathbb{B}\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
. The set of Gibbs measures is denoted
\mathcal{G}(z)
.

By standard arguments, it is enough to consider functions
F
that are indicators of measurable sets of the form
\{B_{0}\}\times\mathcal{A}\subset\mathbb{B}\times\Omega
. Moreover,
F
is only ever evaluated on pairs
(B,\,\mathcal{B})
with
B\in\mathcal{B}
so
\mathcal{A}
can be chosen to only depend on the configuration outside
\{B_{0}\}
. Thus,
\mathbb{P}
is in
\mathcal{G}(z)
if and only if

\mathbb{P}\bigl(\omega\ni B,\,\omega\setminus\{B\}\in\mathcal{A}\bigr)=z(B)\,\mathbb{P}\bigl(\omega\cap\mathbb{I}_{B}=\varnothing,\,\omega\setminus\{B\}\in\mathcal{A}\bigr)

(1)

for all
B\in\mathbb{B}
and all measurable subsets
\mathcal{A}\subset\Omega
. Of course, every Gibbs measure is supported on the non-overlap event

\Delta:=\{\mathcal{B}\in\Omega\mid\forall B,B^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}:\,B\neq B^{\prime}\Rightarrow B\cap B^{\prime}=\varnothing\}.

The grand-canonical
partition function
of a cube
\Lambda\in\mathbb{B}
is a sum over configurations boxed in by
\Lambda
, i.e., elements of the space

\Omega_{\Lambda}:=\{\mathcal{B}\in\Omega\mid\forall B\in\mathcal{B}:B\subset\Lambda\}.

Accounting for the activity and the non-overlap prescription, it is given by

\Xi_{\Lambda}(z):=\sum_{\mathcal{B}\in\Omega_{\Lambda}}{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{\Delta}(\mathcal{B})\prod_{B\in\mathcal{B}}z(B).

The empty product is
1
by convention. In view of

1+\sum_{B\subset\Lambda}z(B)\leq\Xi_{\Lambda}(z)\leq\prod_{B\subset\Lambda}\bigl(1+z(B)\bigr),

(2)

the partition function of
\Lambda
is finite if and only if
\sum_{B\subset\Lambda}z(B)<\infty
. This also allows restricting the sum defining
\Xi_{\Lambda}(z)
to the (countably many) finite configurations in
\Omega_{\Lambda}
, a fact we use without further comment.

Given a fixed block
B\in\mathbb{B}
of scale
j\in\mathbb{Z}
, its
effective activity
, cf.
[
13
, Section 3]
, is

\widehat{z}(B):=\frac{z(B)}{\prod_{B^{\prime}\in\mathbb{B}_{j-1}:B^{\prime}\subset B}\Xi_{B^{\prime}}(z)}.

(3)

Here and throughout the rest of the paper, we employ the convention that fractions with infinite denominators are zero.

If, on every individual scale
j\in\mathbb{Z}
, the activity assigns the same value to all
j
-blocks, we call the activity
homogeneous
or
scale-wise constant
and write
z_{j}
and
\widehat{z}_{j}
rather than
z(B)
and
\widehat{z}(B)
for
B\in\mathbb{B}_{j}
,
j\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Theorem 2.2
.

If the activity is homogeneous, then
\mathcal{G}(z)\neq\varnothing
if and only if

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}2^{dj}z_{-j}<\infty\quad\text{ and }\quad\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\widehat{z}_{j}<\infty.

If these conditions are satisfied, the Gibbs measure is unique:
|\mathcal{G}(z)|=1
.

The second summability condition in Theorem
2.2
and its relation to the phenomenon of condensation was discussed at length in
[
13
]
. There, negative scales were excluded, which amounts to
z_{j}=0
for all
j<0
. The first summability condition, which simply ensures that every block has finite partition function, is trivial in that situation.

Noting that “anchoring” the summability conditions of Theorem
2.2
at scales other than
0
changes nothing, said theorem is an immediate consequence of the following general statement. Indeed, the first summability condition above, implying
\Xi_{\Lambda}(z)<\infty
for all
\Lambda\in\mathbb{B}
, makes Condition (i) below vacuously true while the second summability condition above translates effortlessly into the subsequent Condition (ii).

Theorem 2.3
.

\mathcal{G}(z)\neq\varnothing
if and only if the following two conditions hold true:

(i)

For all
\Lambda\in\mathbb{B}
with
\Xi_{\Lambda}(z)=\infty
,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}B\subset\Lambda:\\
\Xi_{B}(z)<\infty\end{subarray}}z(B)=\infty.

(ii)

For every
B\in\mathbb{B}
,

\sum_{B^{\prime}\supset B}\widehat{z}(B^{\prime})<\infty.

If these conditions are satisfied, the Gibbs measure is unique:
|\mathcal{G}(z)|=1
.

Condition (i) is obviously satisfied when all finite-volume partition functions
\Xi_{\Lambda}(z)
,
\Lambda\in\mathbb{B}
, are finite. Perhaps surprisingly, however, Condition (i) does allow for
\Xi_{\Lambda}(z)=\infty
, as long as the subcubes of
\Lambda
with finite partition function carry enough mass. In the proof of Lemma
3.8
, we show that this happens precisely when
\Lambda
has disjoint subcubes
\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2},\ldots
with finite partition functions
\Xi_{\Lambda_{j}}(z)<\infty
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
but infinite product
\prod_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\Xi_{\Lambda_{j}}(z)=\infty
.

Regarding Condition (ii), note that its summability assertion only needs to be checked for a single
B\in\mathbb{B}
since the set
\{B^{\prime}\in\mathbb{B}\mid B^{\prime}\supset B\}
changes only by finitely many blocks when switching between any two choices for
B
. This is because we work on
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}
. On
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we would need to check the condition for
2^{d}
blocks, one for each orthant. In particular, Condition (ii) is automatically satisfied whenever some block
\Lambda\in\mathbb{B}
has infinite partition function as one then has
\widehat{z}(B)=0
for all blocks
B\supset\Lambda
. Thus, no activity can fail both Conditions (i) and (ii) in Theorem
2.3
.

Example
.

Let
d=1
. Partition the unit interval as

[0,1)=\Bigl[0,\frac{1}{2}\Bigr)\cup\Bigl[\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{4}\Bigr)\cup\cdots=\bigcup_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}[s_{j},s_{j+1})

with
s_{j}=\sum_{k=1}^{j}2^{-k}
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Fixing some sequence
(\lambda_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
in
\mathbb{R}_{+}
, let
\mathbb{P}
be the measure on
\Omega
under which the events
\{\omega\ni B\}
,
B\in\mathbb{B}
, are independent with

\mathbb{P}\bigl(\omega\ni[s_{j},s_{j+1})\bigr)=\frac{\lambda_{j}}{1+\lambda_{j}}

for all
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, and
\mathbb{P}(\omega\ni B)=0
for all other blocks. Then
\mathbb{P}
is a Gibbs measure for the activity

z(B)=\begin{cases}\lambda_{j}&\text{if }B=[s_{j},s_{j+1}),\,j\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\\
0&\text{else}.\end{cases}

In particular,
\mathcal{G}(z)\neq\varnothing
even if
\Xi_{[0,1)}(z)=\prod_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}(1+\lambda_{j})=\infty
. In the latter case the activity
z
is not uniquely determined by the measure
\mathbb{P}
: Changing
z
in an arbitrary way on the cubes
B
from
\{[s_{j},1)\mid j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}\cup\{[0,2^{j})\mid j\in\mathbb{N}\}
– i.e., those with infinite partition function
\Xi_{B}(z)=\infty
– results in a new activity
z^{\prime}
for which
\mathbb{P}\in\mathcal{G}(z^{\prime})
as well. Of course, similar constructions are possible in any dimension
d\in\mathbb{N}
.

Remark
.

When the infinite-volume Gibbs measure exists, volumes with infinite partition function may be identified via the chain of equivalences

\displaystyle\Xi_{\Lambda}(z)=\infty

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\mathbb{P}\bigl(\{B\in\omega\mid B\subset\Lambda\}=\varnothing\bigr)=0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\mathbb{P}\bigl(|\{B\in\omega\mid B\subset\Lambda\}|<\infty\bigr)<1

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\mathbb{P}\bigl(|\{B\in\omega\mid B\subset\Lambda\}|=\infty\bigr)=1

that we mention without proof. The first equivalence is interesting because
1/\Xi_{\Lambda}(z)
is often thought of as an emptiness probability, thus infinite partition functions correspond to zero probability for being empty. The last equivalence says that in fact such blocks then automatically contain infinitely many blocks.

Let us briefly comment on our definition of Gibbs measures. Definition
2.1
corresponds to the GNZ equation for Gibbs point processes. As noted in the introduction, they can be formulated even when some finite-volume partition function
\Xi_{\Lambda}(z)
is infinite, in which case a naive use of the usual DLR conditions would be problematic.

However, the DLR conditions do make sense if we change perspectives, identify a configuration
\mathcal{B}\in\Omega
with its indicator function
{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{\mathcal{B}}\in\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{B}}
, and view our model as a spin system on a lattice. “Finite volume” then no longer refers to bounded regions in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}
but instead to finite subsets
\mathcal{L}\subset\mathbb{B}
. The DLR condition, see e.g.
[
11
, Definitions (1.23) and (2.9)]
, associated to a singleton reference volume
\mathcal{L}=\{B\}
boils down to the following requirement:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigl(\omega\ni B,\,\omega\setminus\{B\}\in\mathcal{A}\bigr)

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{z(B)}{1+z(B)}\,\mathbb{P}\bigl(\omega\cap\mathbb{I}_{B}\setminus\{B\}=\varnothing,\,\omega\setminus\{B\}\in\mathcal{A}\bigr)

(4)

for all
B\in\mathbb{B}
and all measurable
\mathcal{A}\subset\Omega
, where we note that the denominator
Z_{\{B\}}=1+z(B)
is just the (finite) partition function in finite (lattice) volume
\mathcal{L}=\{B\}
(with empty boundary condition). This is easily seen to be equivalent to Eq. (
1
): Given
\mathbb{P}
,
z
,
B
,
\mathcal{A}
, and substituting the latter by

\{\omega\in\Omega\mid\omega\cap\mathbb{I}_{B}=\varnothing\}\cap\mathcal{A},

Eq. (
1
) implies

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigl(\omega\cap\mathbb{I}_{B}\setminus\{B\}=\varnothing,\,\omega\setminus\{B\}\in\mathcal{A}\bigr)

\displaystyle\qquad=(1+z(B))\,\mathbb{P}\bigl(\omega\cap\mathbb{I}_{B}=\varnothing,\,\omega\setminus\{B\}\in\mathcal{A}\bigr)

while, conversely, Eq. (
4
) yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigl(\omega\cap\mathbb{I}_{B}=\varnothing,\,\omega\setminus\{B\}\in\mathcal{A}\bigr)

\displaystyle\qquad=\left(1-\frac{z(B)}{1+z(B)}\right)\,\mathbb{P}\bigl(\omega\cap\mathbb{I}_{B}\setminus\{B\}=\varnothing,\,\omega\setminus\{B\}\in\mathcal{A}\bigr)

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{1}{1+z(B)}\,\mathbb{P}\bigl(\omega\cap\mathbb{I}_{B}\setminus\{B\}=\varnothing,\,\omega\setminus\{B\}\in\mathcal{A}\bigr).

Thus, Definition
2.1
is equivalent to the single-site DLR conditions for the model treated as a lattice spin system. It is known in some cases that the single-site DLR conditions already imply the full DLR conditions, see
[
11
, Theorem 1.33]
(that general theorem is not applicable here, nevertheless our measures do satisfy the full set of DLR equations). Interestingly Georgii presented the GNZ characterisation as a continuum analogue to that fact, see the first sentence of
[
10
, Section 3]
.

We conclude this section with an example that is not a Gibbs measure. The example is related to Mandelbrot’s percolation process, cf.
[
17
,
4
,
15
,
16
]
, and it was proposed as a candidate scaling limit for finite-volume Gibbs measures in the context of continuous phase transitions in
[
13
, Section 5.3]
. Let
p\in(0,1]
. Define a measure on configurations of blocks contained in
[0,1)^{d}
as follows: Pick the top-level block
[0,1)^{d}
with probability
p
. If the block has been chosen, stop. Otherwise, for each of the
2^{d}
blocks
B\in\mathbb{B}_{-1}
contained in
[0,1)^{d}
, decide independently for each of them whether to keep them or not, and then move to the next scale below. After
n
iterations, we obtain a random collection
\mathcal{B}_{n}\subset\mathbb{B}_{0}\cup\cdots\cup\mathbb{B}_{-n}
. We let
\mathcal{B}:=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\mathcal{B}_{n}
and let
\mathbb{P}_{p}
be the distribution of
\mathcal{B}
.

Proposition 2.4
.

There is no activity
z:\mathbb{B}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that
\mathbb{P}_{p}\in\mathcal{G}(z)
.

Proof.

The GNZ Eq. (
1
) with
\mathcal{A}=\Omega
reads

\mathbb{P}(\omega\ni B)=z(B)\,\mathbb{P}(\omega\cap\mathbb{I}_{B}=\varnothing)

which, upon choosing
\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{P}_{p}
and
B=[0,1)^{d}
, becomes

p=z\bigl([0,1)^{d}\bigr)\,\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}B^{\prime}\in\mathbb{B}:\\
B^{\prime}\subset[0,1)^{d}\end{subarray}}(1-p).

Since
p\in(0,1]
, the former identity absurdly states
p=0
, no matter the choice of
z
.
∎

The case
p=0
is excluded because
\mathbb{P}_{0}(\omega=\varnothing)=1
and, hence,
\mathbb{P}_{0}
is trivially in
\mathcal{G}(0)
.

2.2.
Fragmentation and condensation

The standard procedure for constructing Gibbs measures is to consider finite-volume Gibbs measures (or specification kernels) and pass to the limit.
In this spirit, we may start from a finite volume
\Lambda\in\mathbb{B}
and consider an upward truncation of the activity

z_{\Lambda}(B):=\begin{cases}z(B)&\text{if }B\subset\Lambda,\\
0&\text{else},\end{cases}

as well as downward truncations
z^{(n)}
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, given by

z^{(n)}(B):=\begin{cases}z(B)&\text{if }B\in\bigcup_{j\geq-n}\mathbb{B}_{j},\\
0&\text{else}.\end{cases}

By standard arguments, the (essentially finite-volume) Gibbs measure for the two-sidedly truncated activity
z_{\Lambda}^{(n)}
is

\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda}^{(n)}\bigl(\omega=\mathcal{B}\bigr)={\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{\Delta}(\mathcal{B})\,\frac{\prod_{B\in\mathcal{B}}z^{(n)}(B)}{\Xi_{\Lambda}(z^{(n)})},\quad\mathcal{B}\in\Omega_{\Lambda}.

Our first result in this section addresses the limit
n\to\infty
at fixed
\Lambda
when Condition (i) in Theorem
2.3
fails.

Proposition 2.5
.

If the activity
z
violates Condition (i) in Theorem
2.3
, then there exists an infinite set
\mathbb{V}(z)\subset\mathbb{B}
such that, for all
\Lambda,B\in\mathbb{V}(z)
with
B\subset\Lambda
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda}^{(n)}(\exists B^{\prime}\subset B:\omega\ni B^{\prime})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda}^{(n)}(\exists B^{\prime}\subset B:\omega\cap\mathbb{V}(z)\ni B^{\prime})=1

but also

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda}^{(n)}(\omega\ni B)=0,\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda}^{(n)}(\exists B^{\prime}\subset B:\omega\setminus\mathbb{V}(z)\ni B^{\prime})<1.

Roughly, if Condition (i) in Theorem
2.3
fails, then a positive fraction of mass is lost to “infinitely small blocks” along
\mathbb{V}(z)
whose individual contributions all vanish in the limit. The most extreme instances of this phenomenon occur when the latter set contains all blocks
B\subset\Lambda
. In fact, this case always applies to homogeneous activities violating the first summability condition in Theorem
2.2
.

Corollary 2.6
.

Suppose the activity
z
is scale-wise constant, given by
(z_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
, such that
\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}2^{dj}z_{-j}=\infty
. Then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda}^{(n)}(\omega\ni B)=0,\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda}^{(n)}(\exists B^{\prime}\subset B:\omega\ni B^{\prime})=1

for every
\Lambda\in\mathbb{B}
and all blocks
B\subset\Lambda
.

Our second result in this section is about the limit
\Lambda\uparrow\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}
when Condition (ii) in Theorem
2.3
fails, assuming Condition (i) holds true. It follows from Theorem
2.3
that, for every
\Lambda\in\mathbb{B}
, there is a uniquely defined Gibbs measure
\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda}\in\mathcal{G}(z_{\Lambda})
– no need to assume
\Xi_{\Lambda}(z)<\infty
or to downward truncate the activity.

In the following, the limit
\Lambda\uparrow\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}
refers to limits along growing sequences
\Lambda_{1}\subset\Lambda_{2}\subset\ldots
in
\mathbb{B}
with
\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\Lambda_{n}=\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}
, and the limit statements hold true for every such sequence
(\Lambda_{n})
.

Proposition 2.7
.

If the activity satisfies Condition (i) but not Condition (ii) in Theorem
2.3
, then, for every block
B\in\mathbb{B}
,

\lim_{\Lambda\uparrow\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}}\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda}(\omega\ni B)=0,\quad\lim_{\Lambda\uparrow\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}}\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda}(\exists B^{\prime}\supset B:\omega\ni B^{\prime})=1.

The interpretation is that all mass escapes to ever larger cubes and no mass at all remains with finite-size cubes. Moreover, as our configurations do not allow for infinite blocks, we see that
\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda}
at best converges to the measure concentrated on the empty configuration. This measure is not in
\mathcal{G}(z)
because the failure of Condition (ii) from Theorem
2.3
excludes the trivial case
z=0
.

2.3.
Decay of correlations

In this subsection, we take a look at how occurrence events for pairs of distinct blocks correlate under a Gibbs measure when the blocks are, in a certain sense, far from each other.

For the sake of concreteness, we consider the homogeneous case only and fix an activity
z
via a sequence
(z_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
. By Theorem
2.2
, the existence of a (necessarily unique) Gibbs measure for
z
also implies that both the
pressure

p:=\lim_{j\to\infty}2^{-dj}\log\Xi_{[0,2^{j})^{d}}(z)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-dj}\log(1+\widehat{z}_{j})

and the
stability threshold

\theta^{*}:=\limsup_{j\to\infty}2^{-dj}\log z_{j}

are finite with
\theta^{*}\leq p<\infty
, cf.
[
13
, Theorem 3.1]
. (The proofs from
[
13
]
, written for models without small scales, can be adapted to the present setup.)

For
B,B^{\prime}\in\mathbb{B}
, let

\mathrm{lsc}(B,B^{\prime}):=\min\{j\in\mathbb{Z}\mid\exists B^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{B}_{j}:B^{\prime\prime}\supset B,B^{\prime\prime}\supset B^{\prime}\}

denote the lowest scale at which there is a block covering both
B
and
B^{\prime}
, and set

D(B,B^{\prime}):=2^{d\,\mathrm{lsc}(B,B^{\prime})}{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{\{B\neq B^{\prime}\}}.

We view
D(B,B^{\prime})
as an (ultra-)metric on the set of blocks and call it the
hierarchical distance
(or metric).

Theorem 2.8
.

Suppose that
z
admits a Gibbs measure
\mathbb{P}
. Then
\theta^{*}\leq p<\infty
and, for all disjoint
B,B^{\prime}\in\mathbb{B}
,

\mathbb{P}(\omega\supset\{B,B^{\prime}\})-\mathbb{P}(\omega\ni B)\,\mathbb{P}(\omega\ni B^{\prime})=\mathbb{P}(\omega\ni B)\,\mathbb{P}(\omega\ni B^{\prime})\,R_{\mathrm{lcs}(B,B^{\prime})}

where
R_{j}=\prod_{k\geq j}(1+\widehat{z}_{k})-1
,
j\in\mathbb{Z}
, satisfies
R_{j}\downarrow 0
as
j\uparrow\infty
as well as

\limsup_{j\to\infty}2^{-dj}\,\log(R_{j})=\limsup_{j\to\infty}2^{-dj}\,\log(\widehat{z}_{j})=\theta^{*}-p\in[-\infty,0].

Note that the conclusions of the above Theorem
2.8
in particular yield an exponential decorrelation of the form

|\mathbb{P}(\omega\supset\{B,B^{\prime}\})-\mathbb{P}(\omega\ni B)\,\mathbb{P}(\omega\ni B^{\prime})|\leq C_{\mathrm{lcs}(B,B^{\prime})}(\theta)\,\mathrm{e}^{-(p-\theta)\,D(B,B^{\prime})}

for all
\theta\in(\theta^{*},p)
and disjoint
B,B^{\prime}\in\mathbb{B}
with
\lim_{j\to\infty}C_{j}(\theta)\to 0
. Assuming now
\theta^{*}>-\infty
, for
\theta=\theta^{*}
, i.e. for the supremal rate
p-\theta^{*}\geq 0
, we can only guarantee

\limsup_{j\to\infty}2^{-dj}\,\log(C_{j}(\theta^{*}))=0

but we have no generally available bound on
\limsup_{j\to\infty}C_{j}(\theta^{*})
.

Exponential decay of correlations usually involves some Euclidean distance between lattice sites (for spin systems) or, in stochastic geometry, a Hausdorff distance on the set of non-empty compact subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(“particles” or “grains”), cf.
[
1
, Subsection 3.3]
. Our theorem instead works with the hierarchical distance. Note that if the Euclidean or Hausdorff metric between
B
and
B^{\prime}
is large then so is the hierarchical distance,

2^{\mathrm{lcs}(B,B^{\prime})}\geq\sup_{\mathbf{x}\in B,\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\in B^{\prime}}\max\{|x_{k}-x^{\prime}_{k}|\mid k\in\{1,\ldots,d\}\},

but the converse is false; at fixed (positive) Hausdorff distance,
\mathrm{lcs}(B,B^{\prime})
can be made arbitrarily large and, for prescribed
\mathrm{lcs}(B,B^{\prime})
, the blocks can be chosen to have arbitrarily small Hausdorff distance.

The strict inequality
\theta^{*}<p
, implicitly needed for the described exponential decay, characterises the gas phase in the original paper, cf.
[
13
, Proposition 4.3]
in particular. While the condensed phase is marked by the absence of Gibbs measures, the existence of a Gibbs measure with
\theta^{*}=p<\infty
defines the coexistence region.

A situation that naturally lends itself to a refinement of the above analysis is the one encountered in
[
13
, Subsection 5.4]
where the activity, tuned via a
chemical potential
, yields a parametrised system possibly undergoing a
phase transition
at a critical chemical potential that may or may not admit a Gibbs measure with
\theta^{*}=p<\infty
. For simplicity and concreteness, we henceforth only consider parametric homogeneous activities
z=z(\mu,J,\alpha)
given by

z_{j}(\mu,J,\alpha)=\begin{cases}\exp\left(2^{dj}\,\mu-2^{\alpha dj}\,J\right)&\text{if }j\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\\
0&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

with chemical potential
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
, coupling constant
J\in\mathbb{R}
and fractional exponent
\alpha\in(0,1)
. The fractional exponent reflects surface corrections and should be thought of as
\alpha=(d-1)/d
(if
d\geq 2
), see
[
13
, Example 5.8]
.

By
[
13
, Lemma 5.1(b)]
and Theorem
2.2
, if
J
is sufficiently large, the system undergoes a first-order phase transition as
\mu
increases. Precisely, the following holds true: There exists a unique
\mu_{c}=\mu_{c}(J,\alpha)\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
(necessarily positive in fact) such that

(a)

for all
\mu<\mu_{c}
,
z
admits a Gibbs measure with

\mu=\theta^{*}(\mu,J,\alpha)=\theta^{*}<p=p(\mu,J,\alpha)<\infty,

(b)

for
\mu=\mu_{c}
,
z
satisfies
\mu=\theta^{*}=p<\infty
,

(c)

for all
\mu>\mu_{c}
,
z
satisfies
\mu=\theta^{*}=p<\infty
and
\mathcal{G}(z)=\varnothing
.

Furthermore, there exist finite thresholds
J_{1}=J_{1}(\alpha)\geq 0
and
J_{2}=J_{2}(\alpha)\geq J_{1}
such that

(1)

for all
J<J_{1}
(and for
J=0
),
\mu_{c}=\infty
(cf.
[
13
, Theorem 5.3, Corollary 5.4]
),

(2)

for all
J\in(J_{1},J_{2})
,
\mu_{c}<\infty
with
\mathcal{G}(z(\mu_{c},J,\alpha))=\varnothing
,

(3)

for all
J>J_{2}
,
\mu_{c}<\infty
with
\mathcal{G}(z(\mu_{c},J,\alpha))\neq\varnothing
(cf.
[
13
, Theorem 5.6, Corollary 5.7(b)]
).

In case (2) the parametric system undergoes a
continuous
phase transition while, in case (3), the phase transition is of
first order
(see
[
13
, Section 5]
). (We do not address the questions of whether or not
J_{1}>0
or
J_{2}>J_{1}
and what happens at
J=J_{1}
or
J=J_{2}
.)

Theorem 2.9
.

Let
J\in\mathbb{R}
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Suppose that
\mu\leq\mu_{c}
is such that
z
admits a Gibbs measure
\mathbb{P}
. Then the conclusions of Theorem
2.8
hold true and, if additionally
J>0
, then

\lim_{j\to\infty}\left(\log(R_{j})+2^{dj}\,(p-\theta^{*})+2^{\alpha dj}\,J\right)=0.

If
J
and
\alpha
are such that
z
admits a Gibbs measure at
\mu=\mu_{c}<\infty
(in particular
J>0
, see above), then, for disjoint
B,B^{\prime}
,

\displaystyle|\mathbb{P}(\omega\supset\{B,B^{\prime}\})-\mathbb{P}(\omega\ni B)\mathbb{P}(\omega\ni B^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\begin{cases}C_{\mathrm{lcs}(B,B^{\prime})}(\theta^{*})\,\exp\left(-(p-\theta^{*})\,D(B,B^{\prime})\right)&\text{if }\mu<\mu_{c},\\
C^{\prime}_{\mathrm{lcs}(B,B^{\prime})}\,\exp\left(-J\,D(B,B^{\prime})^{\alpha}\right)&\text{if }\mu=\mu_{c},\\
\end{cases}

with
\lim_{j\to\infty}C_{j}(\theta^{*})=0
and
\lim_{j\to\infty}C^{\prime}_{j}=1
. Note that the previously problematic supremal rate
p-\theta^{*}
is now achieved with constants
C_{j}(\theta^{*})
that are not only bounded but exhibit an explicit subexponential decay.

The theorem illustrates that the decay of correlations at the point of phase transition can be slower than in the gas phase. In this example, we end up with stretched exponential decay of the order of
\exp(-\mathrm{const}D(B,B^{\prime})^{\alpha})
.

3.1.
Proof of Theorem
2.3

For the proofs, we introduce some additional notation. The set
\mathbb{I}_{B}
of blocks intersecting
B
is the union of the sets

\mathbb{A}_{B}:=\{B^{\prime}\in\mathbb{B}\mid B^{\prime}\supset B\}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{B}_{B}:=\{B^{\prime}\in\mathbb{B}\mid B^{\prime}\subset B\}

of blocks above and, respectively, below
B
, including
B
itself. Starred sets exclude the block
B
,

\mathbb{I}_{B}^{*}:=\mathbb{I}_{B}\setminus\{B\},\quad\mathbb{A}_{B}^{*}:=\mathbb{A}_{B}\setminus\{B\},\quad\mathbb{B}_{B}^{*}:=\mathbb{B}_{B}\setminus\{B\}.

We extend the notation to subsets
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathbb{B}
by

\mathbb{I}_{\mathcal{B}}:=\bigcup_{B\in\mathcal{B}}\mathbb{I}_{B},\quad\mathbb{I}_{\mathcal{B}}^{*}:=\bigcup_{B\in\mathcal{B}}\mathbb{I}_{B}^{*},\quad\text{ etc}.

Note that the event of non-overlap between blocks can be written as

\Delta=\{\mathcal{B}\in\Omega\mid\mathcal{B}\cap\mathbb{I}_{\mathcal{B}}^{*}=\varnothing\}=\{\omega\cap\mathbb{I}_{\mathcal{B}}^{*}=\varnothing\}

and that the same characterisation holds true when replacing
\mathbb{I}_{\mathcal{B}}^{*}
by either
\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{B}}^{*}
or
\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{B}}^{*}
. For
\zeta:\mathbb{B}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathbb{B}
we set

\zeta^{\mathcal{B}}:=\prod_{B\in\mathcal{B}}\zeta(B),

the empty product is
1
and infinite products are defined as the obvious limits which are going to exist, possibly infinite, in all subsequent uses of this notation.

Next we record a few simple facts. For
\mathbb{P}\in\mathcal{G}(z)
, the univariate Eq. (
1
) immediately implies the bound
\mathbb{P}(\omega\ni B)\leq z(B)
and, via a straightforward induction, also the multivariate equation, cf.
[
2
, Lemma 2.1]
,

\mathbb{P}(\omega\supset\mathcal{B},\,\omega\setminus\mathcal{B}\in\mathcal{A})={\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{\Delta}(\mathcal{B})\,z^{\mathcal{B}}\,\mathbb{P}(\omega\cap\mathbb{I}_{\mathcal{B}}=\varnothing,\,\omega\setminus\mathcal{B}\in\mathcal{A}),

(5)

valid for all finite subsets
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathbb{B}
and all measurable
\mathcal{A}\subset\Omega
.

The partition functions satisfy a recurrence relation, cf.
[
13
, Eq. (3.1)]
: for
\Lambda\in\mathbb{B}_{j}
,

\Xi_{\Lambda}(z)=z(\Lambda)+\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Lambda^{\prime}\in\mathbb{B}_{j-1}:\\
\Lambda^{\prime}\subset\Lambda\end{subarray}}\Xi_{\Lambda^{\prime}}(z)=\bigl(1+\widehat{z}(\Lambda)\bigr)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Lambda^{\prime}\in\mathbb{B}_{j-1}:\\
\Lambda^{\prime}\subset\Lambda\end{subarray}}\Xi_{\Lambda^{\prime}}(z).

(6)

The products are over the
2^{d}
subblocks of
\Lambda
that are exactly one scale below
\Lambda
. Set

\widehat{\rho}_{z}(B):=\frac{\widehat{z}(B)}{1+\widehat{z}(B)},

cf.
[
13
, Lemma 4.5(i)]
, and note that the recurrence relation (
6
) yields the alternative expression

\widehat{\rho}_{z}(B)=\frac{z(B)}{\Xi_{B}(z)}.

(7)

Gibbs measures satisfy a property that reflects a top-down construction implicit in the proof of
[
13
, Theorem 3.2]
: Conditional on the configuration at scales
k\geq j+1
, the configuration at scale
j
is that of blocks placed independently with probabilities
\widehat{\rho}_{z}(B)
or zero, depending on the compatibility with higher-up scales. We formulate a slightly different version that relates the conditional probability of seeing a fixed block, given the state of all blocks that do not lie beneath it. (Cf. also the definition of
\mathbb{P}_{p}
in Proposition
2.4
.)

Given
\widehat{\rho}:\mathbb{B}\to[0,1]
, we say that a measure
\mathbb{P}
satisfies the
top-down condition
for
\widehat{\rho}
if

\mathbb{P}(\omega\ni B,\,\omega\setminus\mathbb{B}_{B}\in\mathcal{A})=\widehat{\rho}(B)\,\mathbb{P}(\omega\cap\mathbb{A}_{B}^{*}=\varnothing,\,\omega\setminus\mathbb{B}_{B}\in\mathcal{A})

(8)

for all
B\in\mathbb{B}
and all measurable
\mathcal{A}\subset\Omega
.

Comparing Eq. (
8
) to Eq. (
1
), the former seems to look like a version of the latter where the void probability on
\mathbb{B}_{B}=\mathbb{I}_{B}\setminus\mathbb{A}_{B}^{*}
was somehow absorbed into the prefactor. Let us make rigorous sense out of this intuition.

Lemma 3.1
.

Every
\mathbb{P}\in\mathcal{G}(z)
satisfies the top-down condition for
\widehat{\rho}_{z}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathbb{P}\in\mathcal{G}(z)
,
B\in\mathbb{B}
and
\mathcal{A}\subset\Omega
measurable. By the GNZ Eq. (
1
),

\mathbb{P}(\omega\ni B,\,\omega\setminus\mathbb{B}_{B}\in\mathcal{A})=z(B)\,\mathbb{P}(\omega\cap\mathbb{I}_{B}=\varnothing,\,\omega\setminus\mathbb{B}_{B}\in\mathcal{A})

so, using Eq. (
7
), it suffices to prove

\mathbb{P}(\omega\cap\mathbb{I}_{B}=\varnothing,\,\omega\setminus\mathbb{B}_{B}\in\mathcal{A})=\frac{1}{\Xi_{B}(z)}\,\mathbb{P}(\omega\cap\mathbb{A}_{B}^{*}=\varnothing,\,\omega\setminus\mathbb{B}_{B}\in\mathcal{A}).

(9)

Essentially choosing
\mathcal{A}=\Omega
, we henceforth omit the specification
\omega\setminus\mathbb{B}_{B}\in\mathcal{A}
for better readability but the same argument goes through with the latter event reinserted everywhere. Write

\mathbb{P}\bigl(\omega\cap\mathbb{A}_{B}^{*}=\varnothing,\,|\omega\cap\mathbb{B}_{B}|<\infty\bigr)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{B}\subset\mathbb{B}_{B}:\\
|\mathcal{B}|<\infty\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}\bigl(\omega\cap\mathbb{A}_{B}^{*}=\varnothing,\,\omega\cap\mathbb{B}_{B}=\mathcal{B}\bigr)

and use the multivariate GNZ Eq. (
5
) to express each summand as

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigl(\omega\cap\mathbb{A}_{B}^{*}=\varnothing,\,\omega\cap\mathbb{B}_{B}=\mathcal{B}\bigr)

\displaystyle\qquad={\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{\Delta}(\mathcal{B})\,z^{\mathcal{B}}\,\mathbb{P}\bigl(\omega\cap\mathbb{A}_{B}^{*}=\omega\cap\mathbb{I}_{\mathcal{B}}=\omega\cap\mathbb{B}_{B}\setminus\mathcal{B}=\varnothing\bigr)

\displaystyle\qquad={\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{\Delta}(\mathcal{B})\,z^{\mathcal{B}}\,\mathbb{P}(\omega\cap\mathbb{I}_{B}=\varnothing).

Thus, we get

\mathbb{P}\bigl(\omega\cap\mathbb{A}_{B}^{*}=\varnothing,\,|\omega\cap\mathbb{B}_{B}|<\infty\bigr)=\Xi_{B}(z)\,\mathbb{P}(\omega\cap\mathbb{I}_{B}=\varnothing).

(10)

If
\Xi_{B}(z)
is infinite, then the probability on the right side must be zero because, otherwise, the probability on the left would be infinite. Thus, both sides in Eq. (
9
) vanish and the equation holds true.

If
\Xi_{B}(z)
is finite, then we may omit the requirement
|\omega\cap\mathbb{B}_{B}|<\infty
on the left side in Eq. (
10
). Indeed, as seen in Eq. (
2
),
\Xi_{B}(z)<\infty
implies
\sum_{B^{\prime}\in\mathbb{B}_{B}}z(B^{\prime})<\infty
and hence also

\sum_{B^{\prime}\in\mathbb{B}_{B}}\mathbb{P}(\omega\ni B^{\prime})<\infty.

By the Borel-Cantelli lemma, said finiteness condition holds
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely.
Therefore, we may omit it from Eq. (
10
), then divide by the (finite) partition function on both sides and obtain Eq. (
9
).
∎

Remark
.

Note that, in the case
\Xi_{B}(z)=\infty
, Eq. (
10
) actually necessitates

\mathbb{P}\bigl(\omega\cap\mathbb{A}_{B}^{*}=\varnothing,\,|\omega\cap\mathbb{B}_{B}|=\infty\bigr)=\mathbb{P}\bigl(\omega\cap\mathbb{A}_{B}^{*}=\varnothing\bigl).

and, if the right-hand side is positive,
\sum_{B^{\prime}\in\mathbb{B}_{B}}\mathbb{P}(\omega\ni B^{\prime})=\infty
by the Borel–Cantelli lemma. If
\mathbb{P}\in\mathcal{G}(z)
, the latter sum is bounded from above by

\sum_{B^{\prime}\in\mathbb{B}_{B}}\widehat{\rho}_{z}(B^{\prime})\leq\sum_{B^{\prime}\in\mathbb{B}_{B}}\widehat{z}(B^{\prime})\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}B^{\prime}\in\mathbb{B}_{B}:\\
\Xi_{B^{\prime}}(z)<\infty\end{subarray}}z(B^{\prime}),

where we used Lemma
3.1
and Eq. (
8
), followed by the definitions of
\widehat{\rho}_{z}
and
\widehat{z}
. In view of the upcoming Lemma
3.2
, applied to
\widehat{\rho}=\widehat{\rho}_{z}<1
, we see that both Conditions (i) and (ii) in Theorem
2.3
are indeed necessary for
\mathcal{G}(z)\neq\varnothing
.

Naturally, the next question is that of existence of solutions to Eq. (
8
). We start by deducing necessary conditions.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\mathbb{P}
satisfy the top-down condition for
\widehat{\rho}:\mathbb{B}\to[0,1]
.
Then

\mathbb{P}(\omega\cap\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{B}}=\varnothing)=(1-\widehat{\rho})^{\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{B}}}

and
\sum_{B\in\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{B}}}\widehat{\rho}(B)<\infty
for all finite
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathbb{B}
.

Proof.

Fix a finite
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathbb{B}
. Without loss, assume that
\mathcal{B}\in\Delta
since one may anyway pass to

\min(\mathcal{B}):=\{B\in\mathcal{B}\mid\forall B^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}:B^{\prime}\subset B\Leftrightarrow B^{\prime}=B\}=\mathcal{B}\setminus\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{B}}^{*}\in\Delta,

the subset of the minimal cubes in
\mathcal{B}
with respect to inclusion.
Now, for any
B\in\mathcal{B}=\min(\mathcal{B})
, one has
\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{B}}\cap\mathbb{B}_{B}=\{B\}
and Eq. (
8
) yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\omega\cap\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{B}}=\varnothing)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}(\omega\cap\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{B}}\setminus\{B\}=\varnothing)-\mathbb{P}(\omega\ni B,\,\omega\cap\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{B}}\setminus\{B\}=\varnothing)

\displaystyle=(1-\widehat{\rho}(B))\,\mathbb{P}(\omega\cap\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{B}}\setminus\{B\}=\varnothing)

\displaystyle=(1-\widehat{\rho}(B))\,\mathbb{P}(\omega\cap\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{B}^{\prime}}=\varnothing)

with
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}=\min(\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{B}}\setminus\{B\})
differing from
\mathcal{B}\setminus\{B\}
by at most the unique block in
\min(\mathbb{A}_{B}^{*})
. Inductively, we obtain

\mathbb{P}(\omega\cap\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{B}}=\varnothing)=(1-\widehat{\rho})^{\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{B}}\setminus\mathbb{A}_{B}}\,\mathbb{P}(\omega\cap\mathbb{A}_{B}=\varnothing)

for all
B\in\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{B}}
since
\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{B}}\setminus\mathbb{A}_{B}
is finite.

Hence, it remains to consider the case
\mathcal{B}=\{B\}
for a fixed block
B\in\mathbb{B}_{j}
. Denote by
B_{n}
the unique element of
\mathbb{A}_{B}\cap\mathbb{B}_{j+n}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Then we have

\mathbb{P}(\omega\cap\mathbb{A}_{B_{m}}=\varnothing)=(1-\widehat{\rho})^{\mathbb{A}_{B_{m}}\setminus\mathbb{A}_{B_{n}}}\,\mathbb{P}(\omega\cap\mathbb{A}_{B_{n}}=\varnothing)

for all
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
with
m\leq n
and

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(\omega\cap\mathbb{A}_{B_{n}}=\varnothing)=1

because the (increasing) union of the above events contains the event

\bigcap_{B^{\prime}\in\mathbb{B}}\{\omega\ni B^{\prime}\Rightarrow\omega\cap\mathbb{A}_{B^{\prime}}^{*}=\varnothing\}=\{\omega\cap\mathbb{A}_{\omega}^{*}=\varnothing\}=\Delta

of
\mathbb{P}
-measure one, cf. Eq. (
8
). Thus,

\mathbb{P}(\omega\cap\mathbb{A}_{B}=\varnothing)=(1-\widehat{\rho})^{\mathbb{A}_{B}}

and
\lim_{n\to\infty}(1-\widehat{\rho})^{\mathbb{A}_{B_{n}}}=1
, yielding

\sum_{B^{\prime}\in\mathbb{A}_{B}}\widehat{\rho}(B^{\prime})=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\widehat{\rho}(B_{n})<\infty.

The lemma follows from combining the latter results with the initial argument for arbitrary finite
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathbb{B}
.
∎

Combining Lemmas
3.1
and
3.2
and the remark in between, we see that, for any Gibbs measure to exist,
\widehat{\rho}_{z}
must be summable over all sets
\mathbb{A}_{B}
,
B\in\mathbb{B}
, which is in fact equivalent to Condition (ii) in Theorem
2.3
, and Condition (i) must also hold true. Furthermore, the first part of the latter lemma actually limits our options for candidate Gibbs states down to one of the following type.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\widehat{\rho}:\mathbb{B}\to[0,1]
. There is a uniquely defined probability measure
\mathbb{H}_{\widehat{\rho}}
such that

\mathbb{H}_{\widehat{\rho}}\bigl(\omega\supset\mathcal{B}\bigr)={\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{\Delta}(\mathcal{B})\,\widehat{\rho}^{\mathcal{B}}\,(1-\widehat{\rho})^{\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{B}}^{*}}

(11)

for all finite sets
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathbb{B}
.

We call the measure
\mathbb{H}_{\widehat{\rho}}
the
hierarchical measure
associated with
\widehat{\rho}
.

Proof.

First off, Eq. (
11
) determines the probabilities of the events of the form
\{\omega\supset\mathcal{B}\}
where
\mathcal{B}
runs through the finite subsets of
\mathbb{B}
. These events form a generating
\pi
-system of the
\sigma
-field of
\Omega
; the
\pi
–
\lambda
theorem implies uniqueness.

Let
\mathbb{P}_{\widehat{\rho}}^{\mathrm{Ber}}
be the probability measure on
\Omega
under which the occupation numbers
\mathcal{B}\mapsto{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{\{\mathcal{B}\ni B\}}
are independent Bernoulli random variables with

\mathbb{P}_{\widehat{\rho}}^{\mathrm{Ber}}(\omega\ni B)=\widehat{\rho}(B).

The Bernoulli measure
\mathbb{P}_{\widehat{\rho}}^{\mathrm{Ber}}
allows for configurations with overlapping blocks but we keep only some of them: For
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathbb{B}
, let

\max(\mathcal{B}):=\{B\in\mathcal{B}\mid\forall B^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}:B^{\prime}\supset B\Leftrightarrow B^{\prime}=B\}=\mathcal{B}\setminus\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{B}}^{*}\in\Delta

be the set of cubes that are maximal in
\mathcal{B}
with respect to the partial order of inclusion. Then

\mathbb{P}_{\widehat{\rho}}^{\mathrm{Ber}}\bigl(\max(\omega)\supset\mathcal{B}\bigr)=\mathbb{P}_{\widehat{\rho}}^{\mathrm{Ber}}\bigl(\omega\supset\mathcal{B},\,\omega\cap\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{B}}^{*}=\varnothing\bigr)={\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{\Delta}(\mathcal{B})\,\widehat{\rho}^{\mathcal{B}}\,(1-\widehat{\rho})^{\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{B}}^{*}}

for all finite
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathbb{B}
. Therefore the image of
\mathbb{P}_{\widehat{\rho}}^{\mathrm{Ber}}
under the map
\mathcal{B}\mapsto\max(\mathcal{B})
is the unique choice for
\mathbb{H}_{\widehat{\rho}}
that satisfies Eq. (
11
).
∎

Remark
.

Note that the measure
\mathbb{P}_{p}
from Prop.
2.4
is just the hierarchical measure associated with
\widehat{\rho}=p\,{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{\mathbb{B}_{[0,1)^{d}}}
.

If any measure satisfies the top-down condition, it is the hierarchical one.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\widehat{\rho}:\mathbb{B}\to[0,1]
with
\sum_{B^{\prime}\in\mathbb{A}_{B}}\widehat{\rho}(B^{\prime})<\infty
for all
B\in\mathbb{B}
. Then
\mathbb{H}_{\widehat{\rho}}
is the unique solution to the top-down equations for
\widehat{\rho}
.

Proof.

We begin by showing that
\mathbb{H}_{\widehat{\rho}}
, by its construction in the proof of Lemma
3.3
, indeed satisfies the top-down condition. For fixed
B\in\mathbb{B}
and
\mathcal{A}\subset\Omega
measurable, we first note that, for all
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathbb{B}
, the set

\max(\mathcal{B})\setminus\mathbb{B}_{B}=\mathcal{B}\setminus\bigl(\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{B}}^{*}\cup\mathbb{B}_{B}\bigr)=\bigl(\mathcal{B}\setminus\{B\}\bigr)\setminus\bigl(\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{B}\setminus\{B\}}^{*}\cup\mathbb{B}_{B}^{*}\bigr)

is unaffected by whether or not
B\in\mathcal{B}
. Therefore, the left-hand side of Eq. (
8
) for
\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{H}_{\widehat{\rho}}
reads

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}_{\widehat{\rho}}(\omega\ni B,\,\omega\setminus\mathbb{B}_{B}\in\mathcal{A})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{\widehat{\rho}}^{\mathrm{Ber}}(\omega\ni B,\,\omega\cap\mathbb{A}_{B}^{*}=\varnothing,\,\max(\omega)\setminus\mathbb{B}_{B}\in\mathcal{A})

\displaystyle=\widehat{\rho}(B)\,P_{\widehat{\rho}}^{\mathrm{Ber}}(\omega\cap\mathbb{A}_{B}^{*}=\varnothing,\,\max(\omega)\setminus\mathbb{B}_{B}\in\mathcal{A}).

On the other hand, for any
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathbb{B}
, we have
\max(\mathcal{B})\cap\mathbb{A}_{B}^{*}=\varnothing
if and only if
\mathcal{B}\cap\mathbb{A}_{B}^{*}
is either empty or infinite. Since
\sum_{B^{\prime}\in\mathbb{A}_{B}^{*}}\widehat{\rho}(B^{\prime})<\infty
, the Borel-Cantelli lemma yields

\mathbb{H}_{\widehat{\rho}}(\omega\cap\mathbb{A}_{B}^{*}=\varnothing,\,\omega\setminus\mathbb{B}_{B}\in\mathcal{A})=\mathbb{P}_{\widehat{\rho}}^{\mathrm{Ber}}(\omega\cap\mathbb{A}_{B}^{*}=\varnothing,\,\max(\omega)\setminus\mathbb{B}_{B}\in\mathcal{A}).

Hence,
\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{H}_{\widehat{\rho}}
satisfies Eq. (
8
).

Conversely, let
\mathbb{P}
solve the top-down equations for
\widehat{\rho}
. As noted in the proof of Lemma
3.2
,
\mathbb{P}(\Delta)=1
and, for every finite
\mathcal{B}\in\Delta
and any
B\in\mathcal{B}
,

\mathbb{P}(\omega\supset\mathcal{B})=\widehat{\rho}(B)\,\mathbb{P}(\omega\cap\mathbb{A}_{B}^{*}=\varnothing,\,\omega\supset\mathcal{B}\setminus\{B\}).

By induction,

\mathbb{P}(\omega\supset\mathcal{B})={\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{\Delta}(\mathcal{B})\,\widehat{\rho}^{\mathcal{B}}\,\mathbb{P}(\omega\cap\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{B}}^{*}=\varnothing)

for all finite
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathbb{B}
and, by Lemma
3.2
, the latter expression equals the right-hand side of Eq. (
11
). Lemma
3.3
then yields
\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{H}_{\widehat{\rho}}
.
∎

Remark
.

If
\sum_{B^{\prime}\in\mathbb{A}_{B}}\widehat{\rho}(B^{\prime})=\infty
for some (and hence all choices of the) block
B\in\mathbb{B}
, the hierarchical measure is a Dirac on the empty configuration,
\mathbb{H}_{\widehat{\rho}}=\delta_{\varnothing}
, and it does not satisfy the top-down equations. Indeed, for
\mathbb{P}=\delta_{\varnothing}
the top-down equations with
\mathcal{A}=\Omega
yield
0=\widehat{\rho}(B)\cdot 1
for all
B\in\mathbb{B}
, contradicting the initial assumption.

By now, we know that, if
\mathcal{G}(z)\neq\varnothing
, then both Conditions (i) and (ii) in Theorem
2.3
must be satisfied and
\mathcal{G}(z)=\{\mathbb{H}_{\widehat{\rho}_{z}}\}
. We now turn to the question of when a hierarchical measure is actually Gibbsian.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\widehat{\rho}:\mathbb{B}\to[0,1]
with
\sum_{B^{\prime}\in\mathbb{A}_{B}}\widehat{\rho}(B^{\prime})<\infty
for all
B\in\mathbb{B}
. Then
\mathbb{H}_{\widehat{\rho}}\in\mathcal{G}(z)
if and only if

\widehat{\rho}(B)=z(B)\,(1-\widehat{\rho})^{\mathbb{B}_{B}}

(12)

for all
B\in\mathbb{B}
.

Proof.

In view of the proofs of Lemmas
3.3
and
3.4
and a standard extension argument via the
\pi
–
\lambda
theorem,
\mathbb{H}_{\widehat{\rho}}
satisfies the GNZ equation if and only if, for all
B\in\mathbb{B}
and every finite
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathbb{B}\setminus\{B\}
, the terms

\mathbb{H}_{\widehat{\rho}}(\omega\supset\{B\}\cup\mathcal{B})={\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{\Delta}(\{B\}\cup\mathcal{B})\,\widehat{\rho}^{\{B\}\cup\mathcal{B}}\,(1-\widehat{\rho})^{\mathbb{A}_{B}^{*}\cup\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{B}}^{*}}

and

z(B)\,\mathbb{H}_{\widehat{\rho}}(\omega\cap\mathbb{I}_{B}=\varnothing,\,\omega\supset\mathcal{B})=z(B)\,{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{\Delta}(\{B\}\cup\mathcal{B})\,\widehat{\rho}^{\mathcal{B}}\,(1-\widehat{\rho})^{\mathbb{I}_{B}\cup\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{B}}^{*}}

coincide. Note that the conjunction of
\mathcal{B}\cap\mathbb{I}_{B}=\varnothing
and
\mathcal{B}\in\Delta
is indeed equivalent to
\{B\}\cup\mathcal{B}\in\Delta
, which also implies
\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{B}}^{*}\setminus\mathbb{A}_{B}^{*}=\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{B}}^{*}\setminus\mathbb{I}_{B}
. It hence suffices to compare the above terms in the case
\mathcal{B}=\varnothing
, that is,
\mathbb{H}_{\widehat{\rho}}\in\mathcal{G}(z)
if and only if Eq. (
12
) holds true for all
B\in\mathbb{B}
such that

(1-\widehat{\rho})^{\mathbb{A}_{B}^{*}}=\mathbb{H}_{\widehat{\rho}}(\omega\cap\mathbb{A}_{B}^{*}=\varnothing)>0.

Of course, for any given
B\in\mathbb{B}
, Eq. (
12
) implies
\widehat{\rho}(B)<1
while the summability condition on
\widehat{\rho}
yields the existence of some
B^{\prime}\in\mathbb{A}_{B}^{*}
with
(1-\widehat{\rho})^{\mathbb{A}_{B^{\prime}}}>0
. Combining these observations, one inductively obtains

(1-\widehat{\rho})^{\mathbb{A}_{B}^{*}}=(1-\widehat{\rho})^{\mathbb{A}_{B^{\prime}}}(1-\widehat{\rho})^{\mathbb{A}_{B}^{*}\setminus\mathbb{A}_{B^{\prime}}}>0

whenever
\mathbb{H}_{\widehat{\rho}}
is a Gibbs measure. The lemma now follows.
∎

Remark
.

Note that Lemma
3.5
generalises Proposition
2.4
. The proof of the latter also proceeds by inserting the measure
\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{P}_{p}
into the GNZ Eq. (
1
) (with
B=[0,1)^{d}
and
\mathcal{A}=\Omega
). Since
\mathbb{P}_{p}=\mathbb{H}_{\widehat{\rho}}
for
\widehat{\rho}=p\,{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{\mathbb{B}_{[0,1)^{d}}}
, the identity that fails in said proof is precisely Eq. (
12
).

By plugging
\widehat{\rho}_{z}
into Lemma
3.5
, we are set on the path to deriving the sufficiency of Conditions (i) and (ii) in Theorem
2.3
.

Lemma 3.6
.

The map
\widehat{\rho}=\widehat{\rho}_{z}:\mathbb{B}\to[0,1]
satisfies Eq. (
12
) for all
B\in\mathbb{B}
if and only if

\Xi_{B}(z)=(1+\widehat{z})^{\mathbb{B}_{B}}

(13)

for all
B\in\mathbb{B}
.

Proof.

By definition,
1-\widehat{\rho}_{z}=1/(1+\widehat{z})
. Given any
B\in\mathbb{B}
, applying the former together with Eq. (
7
) turns Eq. (
12
) into

\frac{z(B)}{\Xi_{B}(z)}=\frac{z(B)}{(1+\widehat{z})^{\mathbb{B}_{B}}},

which certainly holds true whenever Eq. (
13
) does.

Conversely, suppose that Eq. (
12
) holds true for all
B\in\mathbb{B}
. Fix some
\Lambda\in\mathbb{B}
and observe that Eq. (
12
) still holds true for all
B\in\mathbb{B}
when
z
,
\widehat{z}
and
\widehat{\rho}=\widehat{\rho}_{z}
are replaced by

z_{\Lambda}=z\,{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda}},\quad\widehat{(z_{\Lambda})}=\widehat{z}\,{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda}}\quad\text{and}\quad\widehat{\rho}_{z_{\Lambda}}=\widehat{\rho}_{z}\,{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda}},

respectively. Hence, given any
B\in\mathbb{B}
, we may assume without loss that
\sum_{B^{\prime}\in\mathbb{A}_{B}^{*}}\widehat{\rho}_{z}(B^{\prime})=0
and that, by Lemma
3.5
,
\mathbb{H}_{\widehat{\rho}_{z}}
is a Gibbs measure for which Eq. (
9
) with

\mathcal{A}=\Omega
reads

\frac{1}{(1+\widehat{z})^{\mathbb{B}_{B}}}=\frac{1}{\Xi_{B}(z)}

since
\widehat{z}
and
\widehat{\rho}_{z}
are assumed to vanish on
\mathbb{A}_{B}^{*}
. The latter identity is obviously just Eq. (
13
).
∎

Cubes with finite partition function always satisfy Condition (
13
).

Lemma 3.7
.

For all
\Lambda\in\mathbb{B}
with
\Xi_{\Lambda}(z)<\infty
, one has
\Xi_{\Lambda}(z)=(1+\widehat{z})^{\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda}}
.

Proof.

For
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, set

\mathbb{B}^{(n)}:=\bigcup_{j\geq-n}\mathbb{B}_{j},\quad\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda}^{(n)}:=\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda}\cap\mathbb{B}^{(n)}.

By the recurrence relation (
6
) for partition functions, for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
with
-n-1
at most the scale of
\Lambda
,

\Xi_{\Lambda}(z)=(1+\widehat{z})^{\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda}^{(n)}}\prod_{B\in\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda}\cap\mathbb{B}_{-n-1}}\Xi_{B}(z).

When
z
vanishes for blocks at scale below
-n
, the partition functions on the right side are equal to
1
and can be omitted from the previous equation. Thus, let us consider the truncated activity

z^{(n)}(B):=\begin{cases}z(B)&\text{if }B\in\mathbb{B}^{(n)},\\
0&\text{else},\end{cases}

and write
\widehat{z}^{(n)}(B)
for the associated effective activity. Then

\Xi_{\Lambda}\bigl(z^{(n)}\bigr)=\bigl(1+\widehat{z}^{(n)}\bigr)^{\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda}}

(14)

and only finitely many terms on the right side, namely those for
B\in\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda}^{(n)}
, may differ from
1
. The left side converges monotonically to
\Xi_{\Lambda}(z)
as
n\to\infty
. For the right side, we note that
\widehat{z}^{(n)}(B)\to\widehat{z}(B)
for each
B\in\mathbb{B}
and

\widehat{z}^{(n)}(B)\leq z^{(n)}(B)\leq z(B)
with

\sum_{B\in\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda}}\log\bigl(1+z(B)\bigr)\leq\sum_{B\in\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda}}z(B)\leq\Xi_{\Lambda}(z)<\infty.

Dominated convergence allows us to pass to the limit on the right side of Eq. (
14
).
∎

This leaves blocks with infinite partition function, for which Eq. (
13
) turns out to be equivalent to Condition (i) in Theorem
2.3
.

Lemma 3.8
.

If
\Lambda\in\mathbb{B}
such that
\Xi_{\Lambda}(z)=\infty
, then
\Xi_{\Lambda}(z)=(1+\widehat{z})^{\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda}}
holds true if and only if
\sum_{B\in\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda}:\Xi_{B}(z)<\infty}z(B)=\infty
.

Proof.

If
\Xi_{\Lambda}(z)=(1+\widehat{z})^{\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda}}=\infty
, then we must have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}B\in\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda}:\\
\Xi_{B}(z)<\infty\end{subarray}}z(B)\geq\sum_{B\in\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda}}\widehat{z}(B)=\infty.

Conversely, suppose that
\sum_{B\in\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda}:\Xi_{B}(z)<\infty}z(B)=\infty
holds true. Let

\mathbb{F}_{\Lambda}(z):=\{B\in\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda}\mid\Xi_{B}(z)<\infty\}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{M}_{\Lambda}(z):=\max(\mathbb{F}_{\Lambda}(z))

be the relevant set of blocks below
\Lambda
with finite partition function and the set of its maximal elements with respect to inclusion, respectively, cf. the proof of Lemma
3.3
. Writing

\mathbb{F}_{\Lambda}(z)=\mathbb{B}_{\mathbb{M}_{\Lambda}(z)}=\bigcup_{M\in\mathbb{M}_{\Lambda}(z)}\mathbb{B}_{M},

where the last union is over mutually disjoint sets of cubes, we obtain

(1+\widehat{z})^{\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda}}=(1+\widehat{z})^{\mathbb{F}_{\Lambda}(z)}=\prod_{M\in\mathbb{M}_{\Lambda}(z)}(1+\widehat{z})^{\mathbb{B}_{M}}=\prod_{M\in\mathbb{M}_{\Lambda}(z)}\Xi_{M}(z).

In the last line we have applied Lemma
3.7
to the blocks
M\in\mathbb{M}_{\Lambda}(z)
. The product of partition functions is bounded from below by

\prod_{M\in\mathbb{M}_{\Lambda}(z)}\Bigl(1+\sum_{B\in\mathbb{B}_{M}}z(B)\Bigr)\geq 1+\sum_{B\in\mathbb{F}_{\Lambda}(z)}z(B)=\infty.

Thus
\prod_{M\in\mathbb{M}_{\Lambda}(z)}\Xi_{M}(z)
is infinite and so is
(1+\widehat{z})^{\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda}}
.
∎

At this point, the proof of our main result on the existence and uniqueness of Gibbs measures comes down to collecting the partial results derived so far.

Proof of Theorem
2.3
.

By Lemmas
3.1
,
3.2
and
3.4
, any
\mathbb{P}\in\mathcal{G}(z)
satisfies the top-down condition for
\widehat{\rho}_{z}
and is hence equal to
\mathbb{H}_{\widehat{\rho}_{z}}
with

\sum_{B^{\prime}\in\mathbb{A}_{B}}\widehat{\rho}_{z}(B^{\prime})<\infty

for all
B\in\mathbb{B}
. This covers both the uniqueness of the Gibbs measure as well as the necessity of Condition (ii) in Theorem
2.3
, the latter being equivalent to the above summability assertion for
\widehat{\rho}_{z}=\widehat{z}/(1+\widehat{z})
.

As previously noted, the necessity of Condition (i) in Theorem
2.3
also follows from the arguments proving Lemmas
3.1
and
3.2
. Nevertheless, it now suffices to prove the following: given that the above summability condition on
\widehat{\rho}_{z}
(i.e. Condition (ii) in Theorem
2.3
) holds true, one has
\mathbb{H}_{\widehat{\rho}_{z}}\in\mathcal{G}(z)
if and only if Condition (i) in Theorem
2.3
is met. But this is essentially the content of Lemmas
3.5
through
3.8
.
∎

This concludes the proof of our main result. The explicit representation of Gibbs states as hierarchical measures is of interest in its own right and very useful for subsequent proofs.

3.2.
Proofs of Propositions
2.5
and
2.7

Recall that, given an activity
z:\mathbb{B}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}

the finite-volume restriction of

to blocks below
\Lambda
and by
\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda}\in\mathcal{G}(z_{\Lambda})
the corresponding (unique) finite-volume Gibbs-measure whenever it exists. If it does exist, then, by Lemmas

3.2

\mathbb{H}_{\widehat{\rho}_{z_{\Lambda}}}

\widehat{\rho}_{z_{\Lambda}}=\frac{\widehat{z}}{1+\widehat{z}}\,{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda}}

.

For
n\in\mathbb{Z}

z_{\Lambda}^{(n)}=z\,{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda}^{(n)}}

and

to the mix, the corresponding Gibbs-measure
\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda}^{(n)}

Lemma 3.9
.

One has

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda}^{(n)}(\omega=\varnothing)=\frac{1}{\Xi_{\Lambda}(z)}\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda}^{(n)}(\omega\supset\mathcal{B})=\mathbb{H}_{\widehat{\rho}_{z_{\Lambda}}}(\omega\supset\mathcal{B})

for all finite
\mathcal{B}\in\mathbb{B}
.

Proof.

Since, by the preceding discussion,

\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda}^{(n)}(\omega=\varnothing)=\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda}^{(n)}\bigl(\omega\cap\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda}^{(n)}=\varnothing\bigr)=\frac{1}{\bigl(1+\widehat{z}_{\Lambda}^{(n)}\bigr)^{\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda}^{(n)}}}=\frac{1}{\Xi_{\Lambda}\bigl(z_{\Lambda}^{(n)}\bigr)}

for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, the first statement follows by monotone convergence. As an immediate consequence,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\widehat{\rho}_{z_{\Lambda}^{(n)}}(B)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{z_{\Lambda}^{(n)}(B)}{\Xi_{B}\bigl(z_{\Lambda}^{(n)}\bigr)}=\frac{z_{\Lambda}(B)}{\Xi_{B}(z_{\Lambda})}=\widehat{\rho}_{z_{\Lambda}}

for all
B\in\mathbb{B}
so we also get

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda}^{(n)}(\omega\supset\mathcal{B})

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{\Delta}(\mathcal{B})\,\widehat{\rho}_{z_{\Lambda}^{(n)}}^{\mathcal{B}}\,(1-\widehat{\rho}_{z_{\Lambda}^{(n)}})^{\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{B}}^{*}}

\displaystyle={\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{\Delta}(\mathcal{B})\,\widehat{\rho}_{z_{\Lambda}}^{\mathcal{B}}\,(1-\widehat{\rho}_{z_{\Lambda}})^{\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{B}}^{*}}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{H}_{\widehat{\rho}_{z_{\Lambda}}}(\omega\supset\mathcal{B}).

for all finite
\mathcal{B}\in\mathbb{B}
where we note that effectively only finitely many blocks, namely those in
\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{B}}\cap\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda}
, contribute to the above products.
∎

Remark
.

When equipping
\Omega\cong\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{B}}
with the product topology, with each factor viewed as discrete, the second part of Lemma
3.9
obtains a new meaning: it says that
\mathbb{H}_{\widehat{\rho}_{z_{\Lambda}}}
, the unique candidate for the finite-volume Gibbs measure
\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda}
, is always the weak limit of the truncated finite-volume Gibbs measures
\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda}^{(n)}
.

Next, recall the set
\mathbb{F}_{\Lambda}(z)=\{B\in\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda}\mid\Xi_{B}(z)<\infty\}
from the proof of Lemma
3.8
. The implications of the representation

\mathbb{F}_{\Lambda}(z)=\mathbb{B}_{\mathbb{M}_{\Lambda}(z)}=\bigcup_{M\in\mathbb{M}_{\Lambda}(z)}\mathbb{B}_{M}

(15)

with
\mathbb{M}_{\Lambda}(z)=\max(\mathbb{F}_{\Lambda}(z))
are particularly relevant to the last parts of the proof of the next lemma.

Lemma 3.10
.

One has

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda}^{(n)}(\omega\cap\mathbb{F}_{\Lambda}(z)=\varnothing)=\frac{1}{(1+\widehat{z})^{\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda}}}.

If
\Xi_{\Lambda}(z)=\infty
and
\sum_{B\in\mathbb{F}_{\Lambda}(z)}z(B)<\infty
, one additionally has

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda}^{(n)}(\omega\setminus\mathbb{F}_{\Lambda}(z)=\varnothing)=0.

Proof.

We prove the first statement in two steps. On the one hand, one trivially has

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda}^{(n)}(\omega\cap\mathbb{F}_{\Lambda}(z)=\varnothing)\leq\inf_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda}^{(n)}\bigl(\omega\cap\mathbb{F}_{\Lambda}(z)\cap\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda}^{(m)}=\varnothing\bigr).

In view of Lemma
3.9
,
\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda}^{(m)}
being finite and
\widehat{z}
vanishing on
\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{\Lambda}(z)
, the probability in the upper bound actually converges to

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda}^{(n)}\bigl(\omega\cap\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda}^{(m)}=\varnothing\bigr)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\bigl(1+\widehat{z}^{(n)}\bigr)^{\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda}^{(m)}}}=\frac{1}{(1+\widehat{z})^{\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda}^{(m)}}}

whose infimum over
m\in\mathbb{Z}
is just
1/(1+\widehat{z})^{\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda}}
.

On the other hand, we obtain a lower bound via

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda}^{(n)}(\omega\cap\mathbb{F}_{\Lambda}(z)=\varnothing)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{B}\subset\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda}^{(n)}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{\Lambda}(z):\\
\mathcal{B}\in\Delta\end{subarray}}\widehat{\rho}_{z^{(n)}}^{\mathcal{B}}\,\bigl(1-\widehat{\rho}_{z^{(n)}}\bigr)^{\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda}^{(n)}\setminus\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{B}}}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{B}\subset\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda}^{(n)}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{\Lambda}(z):\\
\mathcal{B}\in\Delta\end{subarray}}\frac{\widehat{\rho}_{z^{(n)}}^{\mathcal{B}}\,\bigl(1-\widehat{\rho}_{z^{(n)}}\bigr)^{\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda}^{(n)}\setminus(\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{B}}\cup\mathbb{F}_{\Lambda}(z))}}{\bigl(1+\widehat{z}^{(n)}\bigr)^{\mathbb{F}_{\Lambda}(z)}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\bigl(1+\widehat{z}^{(n)}\bigr)^{\mathbb{F}_{\Lambda}(z)}}.

While the summands in the first sum are hierarchical probabilities of the events
\{\omega\cap\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda}^{(n)}=\mathcal{B}\}
, the last equality comes about because, by Eq. (
15
), the numerators in the second sum are the corresponding hierarchical probabilities of the events
\{\omega\cap\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda}^{(n)}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{\Lambda}(z)=\mathcal{B}\}
. Lemmas
3.7
and
3.8
, together with monotone convergence, yield

\bigl(1+\widehat{z}^{(n)}\bigr)^{\mathbb{F}_{\Lambda}(z)}=\Xi_{\Lambda}\bigl(z^{(n)}\,{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{\mathbb{F}_{\Lambda}(z)}\bigr)\to\Xi_{\Lambda}\bigl(z\,{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{\mathbb{F}_{\Lambda}(z)}\bigr)=(1+\widehat{z})^{\mathbb{F}_{\Lambda}(z)}

as
n\to\infty
(even if the limiting terms on the right are infinite) so we now observe

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda}^{(n)}(\omega\cap\mathbb{F}_{\Lambda}(z)=\varnothing)\geq\frac{1}{(1+\widehat{z})^{\mathbb{F}_{\Lambda}(z)}}=\frac{1}{(1+\widehat{z})^{\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda}}},

the term on the right coinciding with our upper bound.

For the additional claim of the lemma, we write

\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda}^{(n)}(\omega\setminus\mathbb{F}_{\Lambda}(z)=\varnothing)=\frac{1}{\bigl(1+\widehat{z}^{(n)}\bigr)^{\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{\Lambda}(z)}}=\frac{\bigl(1+\widehat{z}^{(n)}\bigr)^{\mathbb{F}_{\Lambda}(z)}}{\bigl(1+\widehat{z}^{(n)}\bigr)^{\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda}}},

cf. again Eq. (
15
). Building on previous arguments, the numerator and denominator on the right respectively converge to
(1+\widehat{z})^{\mathbb{F}_{\Lambda}(z)}
and
\Xi_{\Lambda}(z)
and the additional claim follows.
∎

Having completed our preparation, Proposition
2.5
is easy to deal with.

Proof of Proposition
2.5
.

Unsurprisingly, we choose

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}(z)

\displaystyle=\left\{\Lambda\in\mathbb{B}\,\Bigg|\,\Xi_{\Lambda}(z)=\infty,\sum_{B\in\mathbb{F}_{\Lambda}(z)}z(B)<\infty\right\}

\displaystyle=\bigl\{\Lambda\in\mathbb{B}\mid\Xi_{\Lambda}(z)=\infty,\,(1+\widehat{z})^{\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda}(z)}<\infty\bigr\}

and assume Condition (i) in Theorem
2.3
to fail, i.e.
\mathbb{V}(z)\neq\varnothing
. Fix
\Lambda\in\mathbb{V}(z)
, observe that

\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda}\cap\mathbb{V}(z)=\{B\in\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda}\mid\Xi_{B}(z)=\infty\}=\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{\Lambda}(z)

and that this intersection must be infinite by virtue of Eq. (
6
), cf. also the beginning of the proof of Lemma
3.7
.

From Lemmas
3.9
and
3.10
, we already obtain

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda}^{(n)}(\omega\ni B)=\mathbb{H}_{\widehat{\rho}_{z_{\Lambda}}}(\omega\ni B)\leq\widehat{\rho}_{z}(B)=\frac{z(B)}{\Xi_{B}(z)}=0

for all
B\in\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda}\cap\mathbb{V}(z)
and, specifically for
B=\Lambda
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda}^{(n)}(\omega\cap\mathbb{B}_{B}\neq\varnothing)=1-\frac{1}{\Xi_{\Lambda}(z)}=1=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda}^{(n)}(\omega\cap\mathbb{B}_{B}\cap\mathbb{V}(z)\neq\varnothing)

as well as

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda}^{(n)}(\omega\cap\mathbb{B}_{B}\setminus\mathbb{V}(z)\neq\varnothing)=1-\frac{1}{(1+\widehat{z})^{\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda}}}<1.

The latter restriction is easily overcome by writing

\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda}^{(n)}(\omega\cap\mathbb{B}_{B}\cap\mathbb{X}\neq\varnothing)=\bigl(1-\widehat{\rho}_{z^{(n)}}\bigr)^{\mathbb{A}_{B}^{*}\cap\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda}}\,\mathbb{P}_{B}^{(n)}(\omega\cap\mathbb{B}_{B}\cap\mathbb{X}\neq\varnothing)

with
\mathbb{X}\in\{\mathbb{B},\mathbb{V}(z),\mathbb{B}\setminus\mathbb{V}(z)\}
and noticing that the product over the finite set
\mathbb{A}_{B}^{*}\cap\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda}
converges to
1
whenever
B\in\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda}\cap\mathbb{V}(z)
.
∎

With fragmentation out of the way, we turn to condensation. There is no additional preparation needed but we reiterate that taking limits as
\Lambda\uparrow\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}
simply means taking limits as
\Lambda
runs through an increasing sequence
\Lambda_{1}\subset\Lambda_{2}\subset\ldots
of cubes in
\mathbb{B}
with
\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\Lambda=\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}
.

Proof of Proposition
2.7
.

Suppose that
z
satisfies Condition (i) in Theorem
2.3
. Then, for any given
\Lambda\in\mathbb{B}
,
\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda}
exists and is just the hierarchical measure associated with
\widehat{\rho}_{z_{\Lambda}}=\widehat{\rho}_{z}\,{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda}}
. In particular, one has

\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda}(\omega\ni B)={\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda}}(B)\,\widehat{\rho}_{z}(B)\,(1-\widehat{\rho}_{z})^{\mathbb{A}_{B}^{*}\cap\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda}}={\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda}}(B)\,\frac{\widehat{z}(B)}{(1+\widehat{z})^{\mathbb{A}_{B}\cap\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda}}}

and

\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda}(\omega\cap\mathbb{A}_{B}\neq\varnothing)=1-(1-\widehat{\rho}_{z})^{\mathbb{A}_{B}\cap\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda}}=1-\frac{1}{(1+\widehat{z})^{\mathbb{A}_{B}\cap\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda}}}

for all
\Lambda,B\in\mathbb{B}
, cf. also Lemma
3.2
. Since Condition (ii) in Theorem
2.3
can be equivalently phrased as

\frac{1}{(1+\widehat{z})^{\mathbb{A}_{B}}}=\lim_{\Lambda\uparrow\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}}\frac{1}{(1+\widehat{z})^{\mathbb{A}_{B}\cap\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda}}}>0

for all (or, equivalently, some)
B\in\mathbb{B}
, the proposition follows.
∎

Remark
.

In analogy with the remark following the proof of Lemma
3.9
, it is easy to show that, provided their existence, the finite-volume Gibbs measures
\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda}
always converge weakly to the unique candidate Gibbs state
\mathbb{H}_{\widehat{\rho}_{z}}
as
\Lambda\uparrow\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}
. The crux of the issue is yet again the extension of this convergence to appropriate void probabilities.

3.3.
Proofs of Theorems
2.8
and
2.9

Finally, let us tackle the exponential decay of block covariances. Although we formulated these results for homogeneous activities, the factorisation they are based on holds for all our Gibbs measures.

Lemma 3.11
.

Suppose that
z
admits a Gibbs measure
\mathbb{P}
. Then, for all distinct
B,B^{\prime}\in\mathbb{B}
,

\mathbb{P}(\omega\supset\{B,B^{\prime}\})={\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{\Delta}(\{B,B^{\prime}\})\,\mathbb{P}(\omega\ni B)\,\mathbb{P}(\omega\ni B^{\prime})\,(1+R(B^{\prime\prime}))

where
\{B^{\prime\prime}\}=\min(\mathbb{A}_{B}^{*}\cap\mathbb{A}_{B^{\prime}}^{*})
and
R(B^{\prime\prime})=(1+\widehat{z})^{\mathbb{A}_{B^{\prime\prime}}}-1\downarrow 0
as
B^{\prime\prime}\uparrow\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}
.

Proof.

Fix two blocks
B,B^{\prime}\in\mathbb{B}
. By the results of Subsection
3.1
,
\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{H}_{\widehat{\rho}_{z}}
and the relevant probabilities are given by

\mathbb{P}(\omega\supset\{B,B^{\prime}\})={\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{\Delta}(\{B,B^{\prime}\})\,\widehat{\rho}_{z}(B)\,\widehat{\rho}_{z}(B^{\prime})\,(1-\widehat{\rho}_{z})^{\mathbb{A}_{\{B,B^{\prime}\}}^{*}}

as well as

\mathbb{P}(\omega\ni\mathcal{B})=\widehat{\rho}_{z}(B)\,(1-\widehat{\rho}_{z})^{\mathbb{A}_{B}^{*}}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{P}(\omega\ni B^{\prime})=\widehat{\rho}_{z}(B^{\prime})\,(1-\widehat{\rho}_{z})^{\mathbb{A}_{B^{\prime}}^{*}}.

The result follows by inserting

(1-\widehat{\rho}_{z})^{\mathbb{X}}=\frac{1}{(1+\widehat{z})^{\mathbb{X}}},\quad\mathbb{X}\in\{\mathbb{A}_{B}^{*},\,\mathbb{A}_{B^{\prime}}^{*},\,\mathbb{A}_{\{B,B^{\prime}\}}^{*}=\mathbb{A}_{B}^{*}\cup\mathbb{A}_{B^{\prime}}^{*}\},

and observing that Condition (ii) in Theorem
2.3
yields
(1+\widehat{z})^{\mathbb{A}_{B}}\downarrow 1
as
B\uparrow\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}
.
∎

What is left in the proofs of Theorems
2.8
and
2.9
is mainly the asymptotic analysis of the factor
R(B)
as
B\uparrow\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}
. Clearly,
R(B)=(1+\widehat{z})^{\mathbb{A}_{B}}-1
has the same (multiplicative) asymptotics as
\sum_{B^{\prime}\in\mathbb{A}_{B}}\widehat{z}(B^{\prime})
whenever either expression is finite but we can be far more precise than that. The following simple analytical observation will prove helpful.

Lemma 3.12
.

Let
r>0
and
b>1
. Then

\mathrm{e}^{-r\,b^{j}}\leq\sum_{k\geq j}\mathrm{e}^{-r\,b^{k}}\leq\mathrm{e}^{-r\,b^{j}}\left(1+\frac{1}{r\,\log(b)\,b^{j}}\right)

for all
j\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

Fix some arbitrary
j\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then

\sum_{k\geq j+1}\mathrm{e}^{-r\,b^{k}}\leq\int_{j}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{e}^{r\,b^{x}}}=\int_{r\,b^{j}}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\log(b)\,y\,\mathrm{e}^{y}}\leq\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-r\,b^{j}}}{r\,\log(b)\,b^{j}}

and the lemma follows.
∎

Recall that we are considering homogeneous activities and may write

R(B)=R_{j}=\prod_{k\geq j}(1+\widehat{z}_{k})-1

for
B\in\mathbb{B}_{j}
,
j\in\mathbb{Z}
. Note also that we may write

\widehat{z}_{j}=z_{j}\,\mathrm{e}^{-2^{dj}\,p_{j-1}}\quad\text{with}\quad p_{j}=\sum_{k\leq j}2^{-dk}\log(1+\widehat{z}_{k})

for all
j\in\mathbb{Z}
, cf.
[
13
, Theorem 3.1]
.

Lemma 3.13
.

Suppose that
z
admits a Gibbs measure
\mathbb{P}
. Then
\theta^{*}\leq p<\infty
,
\lim_{j\to\infty}2^{dj}\,(p-p_{j-1})=0
and

\limsup_{j\to\infty}2^{-dj}\,\log(R_{j})=\limsup_{j\to\infty}2^{-dj}\,\log(\widehat{z}_{j})=\theta^{*}-p\in[-\infty,0].

Proof.

Theorem
2.2
readily implies

0\leq p=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-dj}\,\log(1+\widehat{z}_{j})\leq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}2^{dj}\,z_{-j}+\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\widehat{z}_{j}<\infty

as well as

0\leq 2^{dj}\,(p-p_{j-1})=\sum_{k\geq j}2^{d(j-k)}\,\log(1+\widehat{z}_{k})\leq\sum_{k\geq j}\widehat{z}_{k}\searrow 0

as
j\uparrow\infty
. Furthermore,

\displaystyle-\infty\leq\theta^{*}

\displaystyle=\limsup_{j\to\infty}2^{-dj}\,\log(z_{j})

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{j\to\infty}2^{-dj}\,\log\left(\Xi_{[0,2^{j})^{d}}(z)\right)

\displaystyle=p,

see also Lemma
3.7
. Thus, we have

\limsup_{j\to\infty}2^{-dj}\,\log(\widehat{z}_{j})=\limsup_{j\to\infty}2^{-dj}\,\log(z_{j})-p=\theta^{*}-p\in[-\infty,0].

Finally, observe that
\lim_{j\to\infty}R_{j}=0
clearly entails

\limsup_{j\to\infty}2^{-dj}\,\log(R_{j})\leq 0

and that the latter superior limit is equal to
\limsup_{j\to\infty}2^{-dj}\,\log(\widehat{z}_{j})
by applying Lemma
3.12
with
b=2^{d}
and the bounds

\widehat{z}_{j}\leq\sum_{k\geq j}\widehat{z}_{k}\leq R_{j}=\prod_{k\geq j}(1+\widehat{z}_{k})-1\leq(1+R_{j})\,\sum_{k\geq j}\widehat{z}_{k},

valid for all
j\in\mathbb{Z}
.
∎

This concludes the proof of our first decorrelation theorem.

Proof of Theorem
2.8
.

Simply combine Lemmas
3.11
and
3.13
.
∎

The proof of our second decorrelation theorem is essentially an extension of the final arguments above, using the specific choice of
z
.

Proof of Theorem
2.9
.

For what remains to be shown, we fix
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
,
J>0
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
such that
\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\widehat{z}_{j}<\infty
with

\widehat{z}_{j}=\widehat{z}_{j}(\mu,J,\alpha)=\exp\left(2^{dj}\,(\mu-p_{j-1})-2^{\alpha dj}\,J\right)\,{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{\{j\geq 0\}}

for all
j\in\mathbb{Z}
. Recall that
\theta^{*}=\mu
in the present setting. Adapting the arguments from the end of the proof of Lemma
3.13
, we have

\widehat{z}_{j}\,\mathrm{e}^{2^{dj}\,(p-\theta^{*})}\leq R_{j}\,\mathrm{e}^{2^{dj}\,(p-\theta^{*})}\leq(1+R_{j})\,\sum_{k\geq j}\widehat{z}_{k}\,\mathrm{e}^{2^{dk}\,(p-\theta^{*})},

where we used
p-\theta^{*}\geq 0
in the second bound. Note that, for all
j\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\widehat{z}_{j}\,\mathrm{e}^{2^{dj}\,(p-\theta^{*})}=\exp\left(2^{dj}\,(p-p_{j-1})-2^{\alpha dj}\,J\right)

and that, again, Lemmas
3.11
and
3.13
respectively yield

\lim_{j\to\infty}R_{j}=0\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{j\to\infty}2^{dj}\,(p-p_{j-1})=0.

In conclusion, we obtain

\lim_{j\to\infty}\left(\log(R_{j})+2^{dj}\,(p-\theta^{*})+2^{\alpha dj}\,J\right)=\lim_{j\to\infty}2^{dj}\,(p-p_{j-1})=0

from another application of Lemma
3.12
with
r=J
and
b=2^{\alpha d}
.
∎

At the very end, let us briefly indicate how to extend the exponential decay from covariances of blocks to covariances of (finite sub-)configurations. Some readers might expect an exponential mixing statement resembling
[
12
, Corollary 7.9, Theorem 7.12, Definition 9.5]
but we stick with a formulation that is more in line with the theory of Gibbsian point processes.

Most of Lemma
3.11
is easily generalised to the following statement: For all
\mathbb{P}\in\mathcal{G}(z)
and all disjoint finite subsets
\mathcal{B},\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{B}
,

\mathbb{P}(\omega\supset\mathcal{B}\cup\mathcal{B}^{\prime})={\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{\Delta}(\mathcal{B}\cup\mathcal{B}^{\prime})\,\mathbb{P}(\omega\supset\mathcal{B})\,\mathbb{P}(\omega\supset\mathcal{B}^{\prime})\,(1+R(\mathcal{B}^{\prime\prime}))

where
\mathcal{B}^{\prime\prime}=\min(\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{B}}^{*}\cap\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{B}^{\prime}}^{*})
and
R(\mathcal{B}^{\prime\prime})=(1+\widehat{z})^{\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{B}^{\prime\prime}}}-1
with

\sum_{B\in\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{B}^{\prime\prime}}}\widehat{z}(B)\leq R(\mathcal{B}^{\prime\prime})\leq(1+R(\mathcal{B}^{\prime\prime}))\,\sum_{B\in\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{B}^{\prime\prime}}}\widehat{z}(B),

\sum_{B\in\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{B}^{\prime\prime}}}\widehat{z}(B)\leq|\mathcal{B}^{\prime\prime}|\max_{B\in\mathcal{B}^{\prime\prime}}\sum_{B^{\prime}\in\mathbb{A}_{B}}\widehat{z}(B^{\prime}),\quad 1\leq|\mathcal{B}^{\prime\prime}|\leq\min\{|\mathcal{B}|,\,|\mathcal{B}^{\prime}|\},

and

1\leq(1+R(\mathcal{B}^{\prime\prime}))\leq\prod_{B\in\mathcal{B}^{\prime\prime}}(1+R(B))\leq\max_{B\in\mathcal{B}^{\prime\prime}}(1+R(B))^{|\mathcal{B}^{\prime\prime}|}.

Assuming homogeneous
z
and
\mathcal{B}\cup\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\in\Delta
, the analysis yielding the proof of Theorem
2.8
then yields

\displaystyle|\mathbb{P}(\omega\supset\mathcal{B}\cup\mathcal{B}^{\prime})-\mathbb{P}(\omega\supset\mathcal{B})\,\mathbb{P}(\omega\supset\mathcal{B}^{\prime})|\leq

\displaystyle\qquad\mathbb{P}(\omega\supset\mathcal{B})\,\mathbb{P}(\omega\supset\mathcal{B}^{\prime})\,\min\{|\mathcal{B}|,\,|\mathcal{B}^{\prime}|\}\,c_{\mathcal{B},\mathcal{B}^{\prime}}(\theta)\,\mathrm{e}^{-(p-\theta)\,D(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})}

for all finite
\theta\in[\theta^{*},p)
where, writing
\mathrm{lcs}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})=\min_{(B,B^{\prime})\in\mathcal{B}\times\mathcal{B}^{\prime}}\mathrm{lcs}(B,B^{\prime})
,

D(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})=2^{d\,\mathrm{lcs}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})}\,{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{\{\mathcal{B}\cap\mathcal{B}^{\prime}=\varnothing\}}=\min_{(B,B^{\prime})\in\mathcal{B}\times\mathcal{B}^{\prime}}D(B,B^{\prime})

is just the hierarchical distance between the sets
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
and

c_{\mathcal{B},\mathcal{B}^{\prime}}(\theta)\leq(1+R_{\mathrm{lcs}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})})^{\min\{|\mathcal{B}|,\,|\mathcal{B}^{\prime}|\}-1}\,c_{\mathrm{lcs}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})}(\theta)

with
\limsup_{j\to\infty}2^{-dj}\,\log(c_{j}(\theta))=\theta^{*}-\theta\in[-\infty,0]
.

The above decorrelation result should be seen as an analogue to
[
1
, Theorem 2]
. The rigorous link consists in the fact that, in our setting of simple point processes on the discrete space
\mathbb{B}
, implicitly equipped with the counting measure, the sequence of point process theoretic factorial moment measures
(\alpha_{p})_{p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
and their densities
(\rho_{p})_{p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
, customarily called correlation functions, can be identified with

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{B}\subset\mathbb{B}:\\
|\mathcal{B}|<\infty\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}(\omega\supset\mathcal{B})\,\delta_{\mathcal{B}}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{B}\mapsto\mathbb{P}(\omega\supset\mathcal{B}),

respectively. In our model, the latter objects are supported on finite configurations in
\Delta
.

Introduction

Let
P
be a log-concave probability distribution in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with density
e^{-V},

V:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}
being a convex function and let
P_{\theta}
,
\theta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

be a location family generated by
P:

P_{\theta}(dx)=p_{\theta}(x)dx=e^{-V(x-\theta)}dx
,
\theta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.
In other words, a random variable
X\sim P_{\theta}
could be represented as
X=\theta+\xi,

where
\theta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is a location parameter and
\xi\sim P
is a random noise with log-concave distribution. Without loss of generality, one can assume that
{\mathbb{E}}\xi=0

(otherwise, one can replace function
V(\cdot)
by
V(\cdot+{\mathbb{E}}\xi)
).
We will also assume that function
V
is known and
\theta
is an unknown parameter of the model to be estimated based on i.i.d. observations
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
of
X.

We will refer to this statistical model as a
log-concave location family
. Our main goal is to study the estimation of
f(\theta)
for a given smooth functional
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}.

A natural estimator of location parameter is the maximum likelihood estimator (MLE) defined as

\displaystyle\hat{\theta}:=\operatorname{argmax}_{\theta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\limits\prod_{j=1}^{n}p_{\theta}(X_{j})=\operatorname{argmin}_{\theta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\limits\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}V(X_{j}-\theta).

Note that, by Lemma 2.2.1 in
[
7
]
for a log-concave density
e^{-V},V:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}
there exist constants
A,B>0
such that
e^{-V(x)}\leq Ae^{-B\|x\|}
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},
implying that
V(x)\to\infty
as
\|x\|\to\infty.

It is easy to conclude from this fact that MLE does exist.
Moreover, it is unique if
V
is strictly convex (this condition is assumed in what follows).
In addition, MLE
\hat{\theta}
is an equivariant estimator with respect to the translation group in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:

\displaystyle\hat{\theta}(X_{1}+u,\dots,X_{n}+u)=\hat{\theta}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})+u,u\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

Also note that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{\theta}V(X-\theta^{\prime})-{\mathbb{E}}_{\theta}V(X-\theta)=K(P_{\theta}\|P_{\theta^{\prime}}),

where
K(P_{\theta}\|P_{\theta^{\prime}})
is the Kullback-Leibler divergence between
P_{\theta}
and
P_{\theta^{\prime}},

implying that
\theta
is the unique minimal point
of
\theta^{\prime}\mapsto{\mathbb{E}}_{\theta}V(X-\theta^{\prime}).

The uniqueness follows from the identifiability of parameter
\theta:
if
\theta
were not identifiable, we would have

V(x)=V(x+h),x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
for some
h\neq 0,
which would contradict the assumption that
V(x)\to\infty
as
\|x\|\to\infty.
Assuming some regularity (including differentiability of
V
) one can also argue as follows : using strict convexity and a necessary condition of extremum
{\mathbb{E}}_{\theta}V^{\prime}(X-\theta)=0,
we can conclude that
\mathbb{E}_{\theta}(V(X-\theta^{\prime})-V(X-\theta))>\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\langle V^{\prime}(X-\theta),\theta-\theta^{\prime}\rangle=0
for every
\theta^{\prime}\neq\theta
.

Moreover, for differentiable
V,
the score function of location family is
\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\log p_{\theta}(X)=-V^{\prime}(X-\theta),

and, under some regularity,

{\mathbb{E}}_{\theta}V^{\prime}(X-\theta)=0.

In addition, the Fisher information matrix of such a log-concave location family is well defined, does not depend on
\theta
and is given by

\displaystyle{\mathcal{I}}

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}_{\theta}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\log p_{\theta}(X)\otimes\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\log p_{\theta}(X)={\mathbb{E}}_{\theta}V^{\prime}(X-\theta)\otimes V^{\prime}(X-\theta)

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}V^{\prime}(\xi)\otimes V^{\prime}(\xi)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}V^{\prime}(x)\otimes V^{\prime}(x)e^{-V(x)}dx

(provided that the integral in the right hand side exists).
Under further regularity, for twice differentiable
V,
we also have (via integration by parts)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{I}}={\mathbb{E}}V^{\prime\prime}(\xi)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}V^{\prime\prime}(x)e^{-V(x)}dx.

Finally, if Fisher information
{\mathcal{I}}
is non-singular, then, for a fixed
d
and
n\to\infty,
MLE
\hat{\theta}
is an asymptotically normal estimator of
\theta
with limit covariance
{\mathcal{I}}^{-1}:

\displaystyle\sqrt{n}(\hat{\theta}-\theta)\overset{d}{\to}N(0;{\mathcal{I}}^{-1})\ {\rm as}\ n\to\infty.

Assumption
1
below suffices for all the above
properties to hold.

It seems natural to estimate
f(\theta)
by the plug-in estimator

f(\hat{\theta}),
where
\hat{\theta}
is the MLE. Such an approach yields asymptotically efficient estimators for regular statistical models in the case of fixed dimension
d
and
n\to\infty.
In particular, for our location family, we have by a standard application
of the delta method that

\displaystyle\sqrt{n}(f(\hat{\theta})-f(\theta))\overset{d}{\to}N(0;\sigma^{2}_{f}(\theta)),

where
\sigma^{2}_{f}(\theta):=\langle{\mathcal{I}}^{-1}f^{\prime}(\theta),f^{\prime}(\theta)\rangle.

However, it is well known that plug-in estimators are sub-optimal in high-dimensional problems mainly due to their large bias
and, often, non-trivial bias reduction methods are needed to achieve an optimal error rate. This has been one of the difficulties
in the problem of estimation of functionals of parameters of high-dimensional and infinite-dimensional models for a number of
years
[
21
,
22
,
11
,
4
,
12
,
25
,
5
,
26
]
.

One approach to this problem is based
on replacing
f
by another function
g
for which the bias of estimator
g(\hat{\theta})
is small. To find such a function
g,

one has to solve approximately the “bias equation”
{\mathbb{E}}_{\theta}g(\hat{\theta})=f(\theta),\theta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

This equation can be written as
{\mathcal{T}}g=f,
where

\displaystyle({\mathcal{T}}g)(\theta):={\mathbb{E}}_{\theta}g(\hat{\theta})=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}g(u)P(\theta;du),\theta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

and
P(\theta;A),\theta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},A\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is a Markov kernel on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

(or, more generally, on the parameter space
\Theta
of statistical model), providing the distribution of estimator
\hat{\theta}.

Denoting
{\mathcal{B}}:={\mathcal{T}}-{\mathcal{I}},
where
{\mathcal{I}}
is the identity operator in the space of bounded functions on
{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
(not to be confused with the Fisher information also denoted by
{\mathcal{I}}
), and assuming that
\hat{\theta}

is close to
\theta
and, as a consequence, operator
{\mathcal{B}}
is “small”, one can view
{\mathcal{T}}={\mathcal{I}}+{\mathcal{B}}
as a small perturbation of identity.
In such cases, one can try to solve the equation
{\mathcal{T}}g=f
in terms of Neumann series
g=({\mathcal{I}}-{\mathcal{B}}+{\mathcal{B}}^{2}-\dots)f.
In what follows, we denote by

\displaystyle f_{k}(\theta):=\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{j}({\mathcal{B}}^{j}f)(\theta),\theta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

the partial sum of this series and we will use
f_{k}(\hat{\theta})
(for a suitable choice of
k
depending on smoothness of functional
f
) as an estimator of
f(\theta).
It is easy to see that its bias is

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{\theta}f_{k}(\hat{\theta})-f(\theta)

\displaystyle=(\mathcal{B}f_{k})(\theta)+f_{k}(\theta)-f(\theta)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k}({\mathcal{B}}^{k+1}f)(\theta),\theta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

If
{\mathcal{B}}
is “small” and
k
is sufficiently large, one can hope to achieve a bias reduction through estimator
f_{k}(\theta).

Another way to explain this approach is in terms of iterative bias reduction: since the bias of plug-in estimator
f(\hat{\theta})
is equal to
({\mathcal{B}}f)(\theta),
one can estimate the bias by
({\mathcal{B}}f)(\hat{\theta})
and the first order bias reduction yields
the estimator
f_{1}(\hat{\theta})=f(\hat{\theta})-({\mathcal{B}}f)(\hat{\theta}).
Its bias is equal to
-({\mathcal{B}}^{2}f)(\theta)

and the second order bias reduction yields the estimator
f_{2}(\hat{\theta})=f(\hat{\theta})-({\mathcal{B}}f)(\hat{\theta})+({\mathcal{B}}^{2}f)(\hat{\theta}),
etc. This is close to the idea of iterative bootstrap bias reduction
[
10
,
9
,
13
]
.

Let
\{\hat{\theta}^{(k)}:k\geq 0\}
be the Markov chain with
\hat{\theta}^{(0)}=\theta
and with transition probability kernel
P(\theta;A),\theta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},A\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.
This chain can be viewed as an output of iterative application of parametric bootstrap to estimator
\hat{\theta}
in the model
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
i.i.d.
\sim P_{\theta},\theta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:
at the first iteration, the data is sampled from the distribution
with parameter
\hat{\theta}^{(0)}=\theta
and estimator
\hat{\theta}^{(1)}=\hat{\theta}
is computed; at the second iteration, the data is sampled
from the distribution
P_{\hat{\theta}}
(conditionally on the value of
\hat{\theta}
) and bootstrap estimator
\hat{\theta}^{(2)}
is computed,
and so on. We will call
\{\hat{\theta}^{(k)}:k\geq 0\}
the bootstrap chain of estimator
\hat{\theta}.

Clearly,
({\mathcal{T}}^{k}f)(\theta)={\mathbb{E}}_{\theta}f(\hat{\theta}^{(k)})
and, by Newton’s binomial formula, we also have

\displaystyle({\mathcal{B}}^{k}f)(\theta)

\displaystyle=(({\mathcal{T}}-{\mathcal{I}})^{k}f)(\theta)=\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{k-j}\binom{k}{j}({\mathcal{T}}^{j}f)(\theta)

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}_{\theta}\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{k-j}\binom{k}{j}f(\hat{\theta}^{(j)}).

(1.1)

This means that
({\mathcal{B}}^{k}f)(\theta)
is the expectation of the
k
-th order difference of function
f

along the bootstrap chain
\{\hat{\theta}^{(j)}:j\geq 0\}.
In the case when
\|\hat{\theta}-\theta\|\lesssim\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}

with a high probability, the same bound also holds for the increments
\hat{\theta}^{(j+1)}-\hat{\theta}^{(j)}
(conditionally on
\hat{\theta}^{(j)}
).
If functional
f
is
k
times differentiable and
d
is small comparing with
n,
one could therefore expect that
({\mathcal{B}}^{k}f)(\theta)\lesssim\bigl(\frac{d}{n}\bigr)^{k/2}

(based on the analogy with
the behavior of
k
-th order differences of
k
times differentiable functions in the real line).
The justification of this heuristic for general parametric models could be rather involved (see
[
14
,
15
,
18
,
16
]
), but it will be shown below that it is much simpler in the case of equivariant
estimators
\hat{\theta}
(such as the MLE) (see also
[
17
,
19
]
).

Inserting representation (
1.1
) into the definition of
f_{k}
and using a simple combinatorial identity, we obtain the following useful representation
of function
f_{k}(\theta):

\displaystyle f_{k}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{j}\binom{k+1}{j+1}f(\hat{\theta}^{(j)}).

(1.2)

The following notations will be used throughout the paper (and some of them have been already used). For two variables
A,B\geq 0,

A\lesssim B
means that there exists an absolute constant
C>0
such that
A\leq CB.
The notation
A\gtrsim B
means that

B\lesssim A
and
A\asymp B
means that
A\lesssim B
and
B\lesssim A.
If the constants in the relationships
\lesssim,\gtrsim,\asymp

depend on some parameter(s), say, on
\gamma,
this parameter will be used as a subscript of the relationship, say,
A\lesssim_{\gamma}B.

Given two square matrices
A
and
B,

A\preceq B
means that
B-A
is positively semi-definite and
A\succeq B
means that
B\preceq A.

The norm notation
\|\cdot\|
(without further subscripts or superscripts) will be used by default in certain spaces. For instance, it will always
denote the canonical Euclidean norm of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d},
the operator norm of matrices (linear transformations) and the operator norm
of multilinear forms. In some other cases, in particular for functional spaces
L_{\infty},C^{s},
etc, the corresponding subscripts will be used.

2
Main results

Recall that, for a convex non-decreasing function
\psi:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
with
\psi(0)=0,

the Orlicz
\psi
-norm
of a r.v.
\eta
is defined as

\displaystyle\|\eta\|_{\psi}:=\inf\Bigl\{c\geq 0:{\mathbb{E}}\psi\Bigl(\frac{|\eta|}{c}\Bigr)\leq 1\Bigr\}.

The Banach space of all r.v. on a probability space
(\Omega,\Sigma,{\mathbb{P}})
with finite
\psi
-norm is denoted by
L_{\psi}({\mathbb{P}}).

If
\psi(u)=u^{p},u\geq 0,p\geq 1,
then the
\psi
-norm coincides with the
L_{p}
-norm. Another important
choice is
\psi_{\alpha}(u)=e^{u^{\alpha}}-1,u\geq 0,\alpha\geq 1.
In particular, for
\alpha=1,

L_{\psi_{1}}
is
the space of sub-exponential r.v. and, for
\alpha=2,

L_{\psi_{2}}
is the space of sub-gaussian r.v.
It is also well known that the
\psi_{\alpha}
-norm is equivalent to the following norm defined in terms of moments
(or the
L_{p}
-norms):

\displaystyle\|\eta\|_{\psi_{\alpha}}\asymp\sup_{p\geq 1}p^{-1/\alpha}{\mathbb{E}}^{1/p}|\eta|^{p},\alpha\geq 1.

(2.1)

Note that the right hand side defines a norm for
0<\alpha<1,
too, whereas the left hand side is not a norm in this case since function

\psi_{\alpha}
is not convex for
0<\alpha<1.
Relationship (
2.1
) still holds for
0<\alpha<1,
but with constants depending
on
\alpha
as
\alpha
approaches
0.

With a slight abuse of notations, we will define
\|\eta\|_{\psi_{\alpha}}
by the right hand side of (
2.1
) for all
\alpha>0.

We will use the following definition of Hölder
C^{s}
-norms of functions
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}.
For
j\geq 0,

f^{(j)}
denotes the
j
-th Fréchet
derivative of
f.
For
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},

f^{(j)}(x)
is a
j
-linear form on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and the space of such forms will be equipped with the operator norm.
Clearly,
f^{(0)}=f
and
f^{(1)}=f^{\prime}
coincides with the gradient
\nabla f.

If
f
is
l
times differentiable and
s=l+\rho,

\rho\in(0,1],
define

\displaystyle\|f\|_{C^{s}}:=\max_{0\leq j\leq l}\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\|f^{(j)}(x)\|\vee\sup_{x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},x\neq y}\frac{\|f^{(l)}(x)-f^{(l)}(y)\|}{\|x-y\|^{\rho}}.

We will also frequently use
L_{\infty}
and Lipschitz norms of functions and their derivatives. For instance,

\|f^{(j)}\|_{L_{\infty}}=\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\|f^{(j)}(x)\|
and
\|f^{(j)}\|_{\rm Lip}=\sup_{x,x^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},x\neq x^{\prime}}\frac{\|f^{(j)}(x)-f^{(j)}(x^{\prime})\|}{\|x-x^{\prime}\|}.

In what follows, we will use some facts related to isoperimetry and concentration
properties
of log-concave measures. Given a Borel probability measure
\mu
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d},
let

\displaystyle\mu^{+}(A):=\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\mu(A_{\varepsilon})-\mu(A)}{\varepsilon},\ A\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),

where
A_{\varepsilon}
denotes the
\varepsilon
-neighborhood of
A
and
{\mathcal{B}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

is the Borel
\sigma
-algebra in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

The so called Cheeger isoperimetric constant of
\mu
is defined as

\displaystyle I_{C}(\mu):=\inf_{A\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\frac{\mu^{+}(A)}{\mu(A)\wedge(1-\mu(A))}.

According to the well known Kannan-Lovàsz-Simonovits (KLS) conjecture, for a log-concave probability measure

\mu(dx)=e^{-V(x)}dx
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with covariance operator
\Sigma,

I_{C}(\mu)\gtrsim\|\Sigma\|^{-1/2}

with a dimension-free constant.
This conjecture remains open, but the following deep recent result by Chen
[
8
]

provides a lower bound on
I_{C}(\mu)
that is almost dimension-free.

Theorem 2.1
.

There exists a constant
b>0
such that, for all
d\geq 3
and for all log-concave distributions
\mu(dx)=e^{-V(x)}dx
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with covariance
\Sigma,

\displaystyle I_{C}(\mu)\geq\|\Sigma\|^{-1/2}d^{-b(\frac{\log\log d}{\log d})^{1/2}}.

Isoperimetric constants
I_{C}(\mu)
are known to be closely related to important functional inequalities,
in particular, to Poincaré inequality and its generalizations
(see, e.g.,
[
6
,
23
]
).
It is said that Poincaré inequality holds for a r.v.
\xi
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
iff, for some constant
C>0
and for all locally Lipschitz functions
g:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}

(which, by Rademacher theorem, are differentiable almost everywhere),

\displaystyle{\rm Var}(g(\xi))\leq C{\mathbb{E}}\|\nabla g(\xi)\|^{2}.

The smallest value
c(\xi)
of constant
C
in the above inequality is called the Poincaré constant of
\xi
(clearly, it depends only on the distribution of
\xi
). The following property of Poincaré constant will be frequently used: if r.v.
\xi=(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n})
has independent
components (with
\xi_{j}
being a r.v. in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{j}}
), then
c(\xi)=\max_{1\leq j\leq n}c(\xi_{j})
(see
[
20
]
, Corollary 5.7).

If now
\xi\sim\mu
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d},
then the following Cheeger’s inequality holds (see, e.g.,
[
23
]
, Theorem 1.1):

\displaystyle c(\xi)\leq\frac{4}{I_{C}^{2}(\mu)}.

Moreover, the following
L_{p}
-version of Poincaré inequality holds for all
p\geq 1

and for all locally Lipschitz functions
g:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}
(see
[
6
]
, Theorem 3.1)

\displaystyle\|g(\xi)-{\mathbb{E}}g(\xi)\|_{L_{p}}\lesssim\frac{p}{I_{C}(\mu)}\|\|\nabla g(\xi)\|\|_{L_{p}}.

(2.2)

Remark 2.1
.

Note that, if
\xi\sim\mu
and
\mu(dx)=e^{-V(x)}dx
is log-concave, then (see
[
23
]
, Theorem 1.5)

\displaystyle c(\xi)\asymp\frac{1}{I_{C}^{2}(\mu)}

and, by Theorem
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle c(\xi)\leq\|\Sigma\|\ d^{2b(\frac{\log\log d}{\log d})^{1/2}}.

(2.3)

In what follows, we denote the Poincaré constant
c(\xi)
of r.v.
\xi\sim\mu

with log-concave distribution
\mu(dx)=e^{-V(x)}dx
by
c(V).
Bound (
2.3
)
implies that
c(V)\lesssim_{\epsilon}\|\Sigma\|d^{\epsilon}
for all
\epsilon>0.

Also, with this notation, we can rewrite the
L_{p}
-version (
2.2
) of Poincaré inequality as follows:

\displaystyle\|g(\xi)-{\mathbb{E}}g(\xi)\|_{L_{p}}\lesssim\sqrt{c(V)}p\ \|\|\nabla g(\xi)\|\|_{L_{p}}.

(2.4)

This concentration bound will be our main tool in Section
4
. It will be convenient for our purposes to express it in terms of local Lipschitz
constants of
g
defined as follows:

\displaystyle(Lg)(x):=\inf_{U\ni x}\sup_{x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime}\in U,x^{\prime}\neq x^{\prime\prime}}\frac{|g(x^{\prime})-g(x^{\prime\prime})|}{\|x^{\prime}-x^{\prime\prime}\|},x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

where the infimum is taken over all the balls
U
centered at
x.
Similar definition could be also used for vector valued
functions
g.
Clearly,
\|\nabla g(x)\|\leq(Lg)(x),x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and (
2.4
) implies
that

\displaystyle\|g(\xi)-{\mathbb{E}}g(\xi)\|_{L_{p}}\lesssim\sqrt{c(V)}p\ \|(Lg)(\xi)\|_{L_{p}}.

(2.5)

The following assumptions on
V
will be used throughout the paper.

Assumption 1
.

Suppose that

(i)

V
is strictly convex and twice continuously differentiable such that,
for some constants
M,L>0,

\|V^{\prime\prime}\|_{L_{\infty}}\leq M
and
\|V^{\prime\prime}\|_{\rm Lip}\leq L.

(ii)

For some constant
m>0,

{\mathcal{I}}\succeq mI_{d}.

Under Assumption
1
, we have
{\mathcal{I}}=\mathbb{E}V^{\prime\prime}(\xi)\preceq MI_{d}
and thus
m\leq M
.

Remark 2.2
.

Obviously, Assumption
1
holds in the Gaussian case, when

V(x)=c_{1}+c_{2}\|x\|^{2},x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.
In this case,

V^{\prime\prime}(x)=mI_{d},x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
for
m=2c_{2}>0,

which is much stronger than Assumption
1
, (ii).
Assumption
1
also holds, for instance, for
V(x)=\varphi(\|x\|^{2}),x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},
where
\varphi
is a
C^{\infty}
function
in
{\mathbb{R}}
such that
\varphi^{\prime\prime}
is supported in
[0,1],

\varphi^{\prime\prime}(t)\geq 0,t\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
\varphi^{\prime}(0)=0.

Of course, in this case, the condition

V^{\prime\prime}(x)\succeq mI_{d}
does not hold
uniformly in
x
for any positive
m,
but Assumption
1
, (ii) holds.
If
V(x)=c_{1}+\|x\|^{2p},x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
for some
p\geq 1/2,

it is easy to check that Assumption
1
holds only
for
p=1.

We are now ready to state our main result.

Theorem 2.2
.

Suppose Assumption
1
holds and
d\leq\gamma n,
where

\displaystyle\gamma:=c\Bigl(\frac{m}{M}\wedge\frac{m^{2}}{L\sqrt{M}}\Bigr)^{2}

with a small enough constant
c>0.

Let
f\in C^{s}
for some
s=k+1+\rho,

k\geq 0,

\rho\in(0,1].

Then

\displaystyle\sup_{\theta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\Bigl\|f_{k}(\hat{\theta})-f(\theta)-n^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\langle V^{\prime}(\xi_{j}),{\mathcal{I}}^{-1}f^{\prime}(\theta)\rangle\Bigr\|_{L_{\psi_{2/3}}({\mathbb{P}}_{\theta})}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{L,M,m,s}\|f\|_{C^{s}}\Bigl[\sqrt{\frac{c(V)}{n}}\Bigl(\frac{d}{n}\Bigr)^{\rho/2}+\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}\Bigr)^{s}\Bigr].

3
Error bounds for the MLE

Our main goal in this section is to obtain upper bounds on the error
\|\hat{\theta}-\theta\|
of MLE
\hat{\theta}.

Namely, the following result will be proved.

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose Assumption
1
holds and let
t\geq 1.

If
d\vee t\leq\gamma n
for

\displaystyle\gamma:=c\Bigl(\frac{m}{M}\wedge\frac{m^{2}}{L\sqrt{M}}\Bigr)^{2}

(3.1)

with a small enough constant
c>0
, then, with probability at least
1-e^{-t}
,

\displaystyle\|\hat{\theta}-\theta\|\lesssim\frac{\sqrt{M}}{m}\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}\vee\sqrt{\frac{t}{n}}\Bigr).

Several simple facts will be used in the proof.

For a differentiable function
g:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\mapsto{\mathbb{R}},
define the remainder of its first order Taylor expansion

\displaystyle S_{g}(x;h):=g(x+h)-g(x)-\langle g^{\prime}(x),h\rangle,x,h\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

The next proposition is straightforward.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
g
be twice differentiable.
Then, for all
x,y,h,h^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},
the following properties hold:

(i)

|S_{g}(x;h)|\leq\frac{1}{2}\|g^{\prime\prime}\|_{L_{\infty}}\|h\|^{2}.

(ii)

|S_{g}(x;h)-\frac{1}{2}\langle g^{\prime\prime}(x)h,h\rangle|\leq\frac{1}{6}\|g^{\prime\prime}\|_{\rm Lip}\|h\|^{3}.

(iii)

|S_{g}(x;h)-S_{g}(x;h^{\prime})|\leq\frac{1}{2}\|g^{\prime\prime}\|_{L_{\infty}}\|h-h^{\prime}\|^{2}+\|g^{\prime\prime}\|_{L_{\infty}}\|h\|\|h-h^{\prime}\|.

(iv)

|S_{g}(x;h)-S_{g}(y;h)|\leq\frac{1}{4}\|g^{\prime\prime}\|_{\rm Lip}\|h\|^{2}\|x-y\|.

If
g\in C^{s}
for
s=1+\rho,

\rho\in(0,1],
then

(v)

|S_{g}(x;h)-S_{g}(x;h^{\prime})|\lesssim\|g\|_{C^{s}}(\|h\|^{\rho}\vee\|h^{\prime}\|^{\rho})\|h-h^{\prime}\|.

Let
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n}
be i.i.d. copies of
\xi
(that is,
\xi_{j}:=X_{j}-\theta
).
Define the following convex functions:

\displaystyle g(h):={\mathbb{E}}V(\xi+h),

\displaystyle g_{n}(h):=n^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}V(\xi_{j}+h),h\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

(3.2)

Note that
{\mathbb{E}}g_{n}(h)=g(h)
and
g^{\prime\prime}(0)={\mathcal{I}}.

We will need simple probabilistic bounds for r.v.

g_{n}^{\prime}(0)=n^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}V^{\prime}(\xi_{j})

and

g_{n}^{\prime\prime}(0)=n^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}V^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{j}).

We start with the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

For all
u\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},

\langle V^{\prime}(\xi),u\rangle
is a subgaussian r.v. with

\displaystyle\|\langle V^{\prime}(\xi),u\rangle\|_{\psi_{2}}\lesssim\sqrt{M}\|u\|.

proof.

For all
k\geq 1,
we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\langle V^{\prime}(\xi),u\rangle^{2k}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\langle V^{\prime}(x),u\rangle^{2k}e^{-V(x)}dx.

By Lemma 2.2.1 in
[
7
]
, there are constants
A,B>0
such that
e^{-V(x)}\leq Ae^{-B\|x\|}
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Moreover, by Assumption
1
,
V^{\prime}
is
M
-Lipschitz which implies that
\|V^{\prime}(x)\|\leq\|V^{\prime}(0)\|+M\|x\|
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Combining these two facts, the above integral is finite for all
k\geq 1
and we obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\langle V^{\prime}(x),u\rangle^{2k}e^{-V(x)}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\langle V^{\prime}(x),u\rangle^{2k-1}\langle V^{\prime}(x),u\rangle e^{-V(x)}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}(2k-1)\langle V^{\prime}(x),u\rangle^{2k-2}\langle V^{\prime\prime}(x)u,u\rangle e^{-V(x)}dx,

where we used integration by parts in the last equality.
Therefore,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\langle V^{\prime}(\xi),u\rangle^{2k}

\displaystyle\leq(2k-1)M\|u\|^{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\langle V^{\prime}(x),u\rangle^{2k-2}e^{-V(x)}dx

\displaystyle=(2k-1)M\|u\|^{2}{\mathbb{E}}\langle V^{\prime}(\xi),u\rangle^{2(k-1)}.

It follows by induction that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\langle V^{\prime}(\xi),u\rangle^{2k}\leq(2k-1)!!M^{k}\|u\|^{2k}.

It is easy to conclude that, for all
p\geq 1,

\displaystyle\|\langle V^{\prime}(\xi),u\rangle\|_{L_{p}}\lesssim\sqrt{p}\sqrt{M}\|u\|,

implying the claim.

∎

An immediate consequence is the following corollary.

Corollary 3.1
.

For all
t\geq 1,
with probability at least
1-e^{-t}

\displaystyle\|g_{n}^{\prime}(0)\|\lesssim\sqrt{M}\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}\vee\sqrt{\frac{t}{n}}\Bigr).

proof.

Let
S^{d-1}
be the unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and let

be a
1/2
-net with

Then

\displaystyle\|g_{n}^{\prime}(0)\|=\sup_{u\in S^{d-1}}\langle g_{n}^{\prime}(0),u\rangle\leq 2\max_{u\in A}\langle g_{n}^{\prime}(0),u\rangle.

By Lemma
3.1
, we have for all

\displaystyle\|\langle g_{n}^{\prime}(0),u\rangle\|_{\psi_{2}}=\Bigl\|n^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\langle V^{\prime}(\xi_{j}),u\rangle\Bigr\|_{\psi_{2}}\lesssim\frac{\sqrt{M}}{\sqrt{n}},

implying that with probability at least

\displaystyle|\langle g_{n}^{\prime}(0),u\rangle|\lesssim\sqrt{M}\sqrt{\frac{t}{n}}.

It remains to use the union bound and to replace

by

.

∎

Proposition 3.2
.

For all
t\geq 1,
with probability at least

\displaystyle\|g_{n}^{\prime\prime}(0)-{\mathcal{I}}\|\lesssim M\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}\vee\sqrt{\frac{t}{n}}\Bigr).

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\|g_{n}^{\prime\prime}(0)-{\mathcal{I}}\|\Bigr\|_{\psi_{2}}\lesssim M\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}.

Similarly to the proof of Corollary
3.1
, one can use the fact that
\|\langle V^{\prime\prime}(\xi)u,v\rangle\|_{\psi_{2}}\lesssim\|\langle V^{\prime\prime}(\xi)u,v\rangle\|_{L_{\infty}}\lesssim M,u,v\in S^{d-1}
and discretization of the unit sphere to prove the first bound.

Moreover, using that
t,d\geq 1
, the first bound implies that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\biggl\{\|g_{n}^{\prime\prime}(0)-{\mathcal{I}}\|\geq C_{1}M\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}\sqrt{t}\biggr\}\leq e^{-t},t\geq 1,

which is equivalent to the second bound.

∎

We now turn to the proof of Theorem
3.1
.

proof.

Note that the minimum of convex function
g
from (

) is attained at

\displaystyle\hat{h}:=\operatorname{argmin}_{h\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\limits g_{n}(h)=\theta-\hat{\theta},

We will use the following elementary lemma.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
q:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}
be a convex function attaining its minimum at
\bar{x}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.
For all
x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

and
\delta>0,
the condition

implies that

\inf_{\|x-x_{0}\|=\delta}q(x)-q(x_{0})\leq 0.

Indeed, assume that
\|\bar{x}-x_{0}\|\geq\delta.
Clearly,
q(\bar{x})-q(x_{0})\leq 0.

Let

x^{\ast}=\lambda\bar{x}+(1-\lambda)x_{0}
with
\lambda:=\frac{\delta}{\|\bar{x}-x_{0}\|}.

Then,
\|x^{\ast}-x_{0}\|=\delta
and,
by convexity of
q,

q(x^{\ast})\leq\lambda q(\bar{x})+(1-\lambda)q(x_{0}),

implying that
q(x^{\ast})-q(x_{0})\leq\lambda(q(\bar{x})-q(x_{0}))\leq 0.

∎

If
\|\hat{h}\|\geq\delta,
then, by Lemma
3.2
,

\displaystyle\inf_{\|h\|=\delta}g_{n}(h)-g_{n}(0)\leq 0.

(3.4)

Note that

\displaystyle g_{n}(h)-g_{n}(0)

\displaystyle=\langle g_{n}^{\prime}(0),h\rangle+S_{g_{n}}(0;h)

\displaystyle=\langle g_{n}^{\prime}(0),h\rangle+S_{g_{n}}(0;h)-\frac{1}{2}\langle g_{n}^{\prime\prime}(0)h,h\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\langle g_{n}^{\prime\prime}(0)h,h\rangle.

(3.5)

For
\|h\|=\delta,
we have, by Assumption
1
, (ii),

\displaystyle\langle g_{n}^{\prime\prime}(0)h,h\rangle=\langle g_{n}^{\prime\prime}(0)h,h\rangle-\langle{\mathcal{I}}h,h\rangle+\langle{\mathcal{I}}h,h\rangle\geq m\delta^{2}-\delta^{2}\|g_{n}^{\prime\prime}(0)-\mathcal{I}\|

and, by Proposition
3.1
, (ii),

\displaystyle S_{g_{n}}(0;h)-\frac{1}{2}\langle g_{n}^{\prime\prime}(0)h,h\rangle\geq-\frac{L}{2}\delta^{3}.

Inserting these inequalities into (
3.5
) and using (
3.4
) we can conclude that if
\|\hat{h}\|\geq\delta
, then

\displaystyle\|g_{n}^{\prime}(0)\|\delta+\frac{\delta^{2}}{2}\|g_{n}^{\prime\prime}(0)-\mathcal{I}\|\geq\frac{m}{2}\delta^{2}-\frac{L}{2}\delta^{3}.

(3.6)

To complete the proof, assume that the bound of Corollary
3.1
holds with constant
C_{1}\geq 1
and the bound of Proposition
3.2
holds with constant
C_{2}\geq 1.

If constant
c
in the definition of
\gamma
is small enough, then
the condition
d\vee t\leq\gamma n
implies that

\displaystyle C\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}\vee\sqrt{\frac{t}{n}}\Bigr)\leq\frac{m}{M}\wedge\frac{m^{2}}{L\sqrt{M}}

with
C:=(16C_{1})\vee(4C_{2}).
Moreover, let

\displaystyle\delta:=4C_{1}\frac{\sqrt{M}}{m}\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}\vee\sqrt{\frac{t}{n}}\Bigr)

Then,
\delta\leq\frac{m}{4L}
and, on the event

\displaystyle E:=\Bigl\{\|g_{n}^{\prime\prime}(0)-\mathcal{I}\|\leq C_{2}M\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}\vee\sqrt{\frac{t}{n}}\Bigr)\Bigr\},

bound (
3.6
) implies that

\delta\leq\frac{4}{m}\|g_{n}^{\prime}(0)\|.

Note also that, by Proposition
3.2
,
{\mathbb{P}}(E^{c})\leq e^{-t}
. By Corollary
3.1
, the event
\{\delta\leq\frac{4}{m}\|g_{n}^{\prime}(0)\|\}
occurs with probability at most
e^{-t}.

Recall that bound (
3.6
) follows from
\|\hat{\theta}-\theta\|=\|\hat{h}\|\geq\delta.

Thus, with probability at least

\|\hat{\theta}-\theta\|\leq\delta.

It remains to adjust the constants in order to replace the probability bound

with

∎

Corollary 3.2
.

Suppose Assumption

where

Then

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\|\hat{\theta}-\theta\|\wedge\frac{m}{12L}\Bigr\|_{\psi_{2}}\lesssim\frac{\sqrt{M}}{m}\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}+\frac{m}{L\sqrt{\gamma}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}.

First, for

, Theorem

∎

4
Concentration bounds

In this section, we prove concentration inequalities for
f(\hat{\theta}),
where
f
is a smooth function on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

Namely, we will prove the following result.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
f\in C^{s}
for some
s=1+\rho,

\rho\in(0,1].

Suppose that
d\leq\gamma n,
where
\gamma:=c\bigl(\frac{m}{M}\wedge\frac{m^{2}}{L\sqrt{M}}\bigr)^{2}
with a small enough
c>0.

Then

\displaystyle\sup_{\theta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\Bigl\|f(\hat{\theta})-{\mathbb{E}}_{\theta}f(\hat{\theta})-n^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\langle V^{\prime}(\xi_{j}),{\mathcal{I}}^{-1}f^{\prime}(\theta)\rangle\Bigr\|_{L_{\psi_{2/3}}({\mathbb{P}}_{\theta})}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{M,L,m}\sqrt{c(V)}\|f\|_{C^{s}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\Bigl(\frac{d}{n}\Bigr)^{\rho/2}.

To derive concentration bounds for
f(\hat{\theta}),
we need to bound local Lipschitz constants of estimator

\hat{\theta}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
as a function of its variables. A good place to start is to show the continuity
of this function. The following fact is, probably, well known. We give its proof for completeness.

Proposition 4.1
.

Suppose that
V
is strictly convex.
Then, MLE
\hat{\theta}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
exists and is unique
for all
(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times\dots\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and the function

{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times\dots\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\ni(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\mapsto\hat{\theta}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

is continuous.

proof.

Let
(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times\dots\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d},(x_{1}^{(k)},\dots,x_{n}^{(k)})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times\dots\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d},k\geq 1

and
(x_{1}^{(k)},\dots,x_{n}^{(k)})\to(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
as
k\to\infty.
Define

\displaystyle p(\theta):=n^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}V(x_{j}-\theta),\ p_{k}(\theta):=n^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}V(x_{j}^{(k)}-\theta),\theta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},k\geq 1.

By continuity of
V,

p_{k}(\theta)\to p(\theta)
as
k\to\infty
for all
\theta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.
Since
p_{k}
and
p
are convex,
this implies the uniform convergence on all compact subsets of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

If
\|\hat{\theta}(x_{1}^{(k)},\dots,x_{n}^{(k)})-\hat{\theta}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\|\geq\delta,
then, by Lemma
3.2
,

\displaystyle\inf_{\|\theta-\hat{\theta}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\|=\delta}p_{k}(\theta)-p_{k}(\hat{\theta}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}))\leq 0

By the uniform convergence of
p_{k}
to
p
on compact sets,

\displaystyle\inf_{\|\theta-\hat{\theta}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\|=\delta}p_{k}(\theta)-p_{k}(\hat{\theta}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}))\to\inf_{\|\theta-\hat{\theta}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\|=\delta}p(\theta)-p(\hat{\theta}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}))

as
k\to\infty.
Due to strict convexity, the minimum
\hat{\theta}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
of
p(\theta)
exists and is unique (see the argument in the introduction), and

\displaystyle\inf_{\|\theta-\hat{\theta}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\|=\delta}p(\theta)-p(\hat{\theta}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}))>0,

implying that
\|\hat{\theta}(x_{1}^{(k)},\dots,x_{n}^{(k)})-\hat{\theta}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\|<\delta
for all large enough
k
and thus
\hat{\theta}(x_{1}^{(k)},\dots,x_{n}^{(k)})\to\hat{\theta}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})

as
k\to\infty.

∎

Note that the continuity of
\hat{\theta}
also follows from the implicit function theorem in the case when
V
is twice differentiable with
V^{\prime\prime}
being positively definite throughout
\mathbb{R}^{d}.

We will now study Lipschitz continuity properties of
\hat{\theta}
as a function of the data
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
needed to prove concentration inequalities.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let

\displaystyle A_{1}:=\Bigl\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times\dots\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:\|\hat{\theta}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})-\theta\|\leq\frac{m}{12L}\Bigr\}

and

\displaystyle A_{2}:=\Bigl\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times\dots\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:\Bigl\|n^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}V^{\prime\prime}(x_{j}-\theta)-\mathcal{I}\Bigr\|\leq\frac{m}{4}\Bigr\}

and let
A:=A_{1}\cap A_{2}.

Then the function
A\ni(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\mapsto\hat{\theta}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
is Lipschitz with constant
\frac{4M}{m\sqrt{n}}:

for all
(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}),(\tilde{x}_{1},\dots,\tilde{x}_{n})\in A,

\displaystyle\|\hat{\theta}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})-\hat{\theta}(\tilde{x}_{1},\dots,\tilde{x}_{n})\|\leq\frac{4M}{m\sqrt{n}}\Bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|x_{j}-\tilde{x}_{j}\|^{2}\Bigr)^{1/2}.

proof.

Due to equivariance, we have

\displaystyle\hat{\theta}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})-\hat{\theta}(\tilde{x}_{1},\dots,\tilde{x}_{n})=\hat{\theta}(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n})-\hat{\theta}(\tilde{\xi}_{1},\dots,\tilde{\xi}_{n})

with
\xi_{j}=x_{j}-\theta
and
\tilde{\xi}_{j}=\tilde{x}_{j}-\theta.
Hence, if we abbreviate
\hat{h}=\theta-\hat{\theta}(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n})
and
\tilde{h}=\theta-\hat{\theta}(\tilde{\xi}_{1},\dots,\tilde{\xi}_{n})
, then we have
g_{n}^{\prime}(\hat{h})=0
with
g_{n}
from (
3.2
) and
\tilde{g}_{n}^{\prime}(\tilde{h})=0
with

\displaystyle\tilde{g}_{n}(h):=n^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}V(\tilde{\xi}_{j}+h),h\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

Recall that
g^{\prime}(0)=0
and
g^{\prime\prime}(0)={\mathcal{I}}
. By the first order Taylor expansion for function
g^{\prime},

g^{\prime}(h)={\mathcal{I}}h+r(h),

where

\displaystyle r(h):=\int_{0}^{1}(g^{\prime\prime}(\lambda h)-g^{\prime\prime}(0))d\lambda\ h

is the remainder. Therefore,

\displaystyle g^{\prime}(\hat{h})={\mathcal{I}}\hat{h}+r(\hat{h}),

implying that

\displaystyle\hat{h}

\displaystyle={\mathcal{I}}^{-1}(g^{\prime}(\hat{h})-g_{n}^{\prime}(\hat{h}))-{\mathcal{I}}^{-1}r(\hat{h})

\displaystyle={\mathcal{I}}^{-1}(g^{\prime}(0)-g_{n}^{\prime}(0))+{\mathcal{I}}^{-1}q_{n}(\hat{h})-{\mathcal{I}}^{-1}r(\hat{h})

\displaystyle=-{\mathcal{I}}^{-1}g_{n}^{\prime}(0)+{\mathcal{I}}^{-1}q_{n}(\hat{h})-{\mathcal{I}}^{-1}r(\hat{h}),

(4.1)

where

\displaystyle q_{n}(h):=(g_{n}-g)^{\prime}(h)-(g_{n}-g)^{\prime}(0)=\int_{0}^{1}(g_{n}^{\prime\prime}-g^{\prime\prime})(\lambda h)d\lambda\ h.

Similarly, we have
\tilde{h}=-{\mathcal{I}}^{-1}\tilde{g}_{n}^{\prime}(0)+{\mathcal{I}}^{-1}\tilde{q}_{n}(\tilde{h})-{\mathcal{I}}^{-1}r(\tilde{h})
with
\tilde{q}_{n}(h):=(\tilde{g}_{n}-g)^{\prime}(h)-(\tilde{g}_{n}-g)^{\prime}(0).
Using these representations we now bound the difference between
\hat{h}
and
\tilde{h}.

First note that

\displaystyle\|g_{n}^{\prime}(0)-\tilde{g}_{n}^{\prime}(0)\|\leq n^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|V^{\prime}(\xi_{j})-V^{\prime}(\tilde{\xi}_{j})\|

\displaystyle\leq Mn^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\xi_{j}-\tilde{\xi}_{j}\|\leq\frac{M}{\sqrt{n}}\Bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|x_{j}-\tilde{x}_{j}\|^{2}\Bigr)^{1/2}.

(4.2)

Also,

\displaystyle q_{n}(\hat{h})-\tilde{q}_{n}(\tilde{h})

\displaystyle=((g_{n}^{\prime\prime}-g^{\prime\prime})(0))(\hat{h}-\tilde{h})

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{1}((g_{n}^{\prime\prime}-g^{\prime\prime})(\lambda\hat{h})-(g_{n}^{\prime\prime}-g^{\prime\prime})(0))d\lambda\ (\hat{h}-\tilde{h})

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{1}[(g_{n}^{\prime\prime}-g^{\prime\prime})(\lambda\hat{h})-(g_{n}^{\prime\prime}-g^{\prime\prime})(\lambda\tilde{h})]d\lambda\ \tilde{h}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{1}(g_{n}^{\prime\prime}-\tilde{g}_{n}^{\prime\prime})(\lambda\tilde{h})d\lambda\ \tilde{h}.

Since, by Assumption
1
,
V^{\prime\prime}
is Lipschitz with constant
L,
the function
h\mapsto g^{\prime\prime}(h)={\mathbb{E}}V^{\prime\prime}(\xi+h)
satisfies
the Lipschitz condition with the same constant
L
and the function
h\mapsto(g_{n}^{\prime\prime}-g^{\prime\prime})(h)
is Lipschitz with constant at most
2L.

In addition,

\displaystyle\|(g_{n}^{\prime\prime}-\tilde{g}_{n}^{\prime\prime})(\lambda\tilde{h})\|

\displaystyle\leq n^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|V^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{j}+\lambda\tilde{h})-V^{\prime\prime}(\tilde{\xi}_{j}+\lambda\tilde{h})\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{L}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\xi_{j}-\tilde{\xi}_{j}\|\leq\frac{L}{\sqrt{n}}\biggl(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|x_{j}-\tilde{x}_{j}\|^{2}\biggr)^{1/2}.

Therefore, we easily get

\displaystyle\|q_{n}(\hat{h})-\tilde{q}_{n}(\tilde{h})\|

\displaystyle\leq\|g_{n}^{\prime\prime}(0)-g^{\prime\prime}(0)\|\|\hat{h}-\tilde{h}\|+L(\|\hat{h}\|+\|\tilde{h}\|)\|\hat{h}-\tilde{h}\|

\displaystyle+\frac{L}{\sqrt{n}}\|\tilde{h}\|\biggl(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|x_{j}-\tilde{x}_{j}\|^{2}\biggr)^{1/2}.

(4.3)

Similarly, note that

\displaystyle r(\hat{h})-r(\tilde{h})=\int_{0}^{1}(g^{\prime\prime}(\lambda\hat{h})-g^{\prime\prime}(\lambda\tilde{h}))d\lambda\ \hat{h}+\int_{0}^{1}(g^{\prime\prime}(\lambda\tilde{h})-g^{\prime\prime}(0))d\lambda\ (\hat{h}-\tilde{h})

which implies the following bound:

\displaystyle\|r(\hat{h})-r(\tilde{h})\|\leq\frac{L}{2}(\|\hat{h}\|+\|\tilde{h}\|)\|\hat{h}-\tilde{h}\|.

(4.4)

It follows from (
4
), (
4
) and (
4.4
) that

\displaystyle\|\hat{h}-\tilde{h}\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{m}\biggl(\Bigl(\frac{M}{\sqrt{n}}+\frac{L}{\sqrt{n}}\|\tilde{h}\|\Bigr)\Bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|x_{j}-\tilde{x}_{j}\|^{2}\Bigr)^{1/2}

\displaystyle+\|g_{n}^{\prime\prime}(0)-g^{\prime\prime}(0)\|\|\hat{h}-\tilde{h}\|+\frac{3}{2}L(\|\hat{h}\|+\|\tilde{h}\|)\|\hat{h}-\tilde{h}\|\biggr).

If
\|g_{n}^{\prime\prime}(0)-g^{\prime\prime}(0)\|\leq\frac{m}{4}
and
\|\hat{h}\|\vee\|\tilde{h}\|\leq\frac{m}{12L},
we easily conclude that

\displaystyle\|\hat{h}-\tilde{h}\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4M}{m\sqrt{n}}\Bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|x_{j}-\tilde{x}_{j}\|^{2}\Bigr)^{1/2},

which completes the proof.

∎

Since the Lipschitz condition holds for
\hat{\theta}
only on set
A,
it will be convenient for our purposes to replace
\hat{\theta}
with its “smoothed truncated”
version
\check{\theta}
that is Lipschitz in the whole space. To this end, let
\phi:{\mathbb{R}}\mapsto[0,1]
be defined as follows:
\phi(s)=1,s\leq 1,

\phi(s)=0,s\geq 2

and
\phi(s)=2-s,s\in(1,2).
Clearly,
\phi
is Lipschitz with constant
1.
By Theorem
3.1
,
\|\hat{\theta}-\theta\|\leq\frac{m}{24L}

with probability at least
1-e^{-\gamma n},
where
\gamma:=c\bigl(\frac{m}{M}\wedge\frac{m^{2}}{L\sqrt{M}}\bigr)^{2}
with a small enough constant
c>0
and it is assumed that
d\leq\gamma n.
Similarly, it follows from Proposition
3.2
that
\|g_{n}^{\prime\prime}(0)-g^{\prime\prime}(0)\|\leq\frac{m}{8}
with probability at least

1-e^{-\beta n},
where
\beta=c\bigl(\frac{m}{M}\bigr)^{2}
for a small enough constant
c>0
(and under the assumption that
d\leq\beta n.

Clearly, we can assume that
\beta\geq\gamma,
so, both properties hold with probability at least
1-2e^{-\gamma n}
provided that
d\leq\gamma n.

Define

\displaystyle\varphi(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}):=\phi\Bigl(\frac{24L}{m}\|\hat{\theta}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})-\theta\|\Bigr),

\displaystyle\psi(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}):=\phi\Bigl(\frac{8}{m}\|g_{n}^{\prime\prime}(0)(x_{1}-\theta,\dots,x_{n}-\theta)-g^{\prime\prime}(0)\|\Bigr).

and let

\displaystyle\check{\theta}:=(1-\varphi\psi)\theta+\varphi\psi\hat{\theta}.

Note that
\check{\theta}-\theta=(\hat{\theta}-\theta)\varphi\psi
and
\check{\theta}=\hat{\theta}
on the event
\{\varphi=\psi=1\}

of probability at least
1-2e^{-\gamma n}.

Proposition 4.3
.

If
d\leq\gamma n,
then

\displaystyle\|{\mathbb{E}}_{\theta}\check{\theta}-\theta\|\lesssim_{M,L,m}\frac{d}{n}\qquad\text{and}\qquad{\mathbb{E}}_{\theta}^{1/2}\|\check{\theta}-\theta\|^{2}\lesssim_{M,L,m}\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}.

proof.

By representation (
4
) and the fact that
g^{\prime}(0)=0
,

\displaystyle\check{\theta}-\theta

\displaystyle=(\hat{\theta}-\theta)\varphi\psi=-\hat{h}\varphi\psi

\displaystyle={\mathcal{I}}^{-1}g_{n}^{\prime}(0)-{\mathcal{I}}^{-1}g_{n}^{\prime}(0)(1-\varphi\psi)-{\mathcal{I}}^{-1}q_{n}(\hat{h})\varphi\psi+{\mathcal{I}}^{-1}r(\hat{h})\varphi\psi.

(4.5)

Using Corollary
3.1
, we get

\displaystyle\|\|{\mathcal{I}}^{-1}g_{n}^{\prime}(0)(1-\varphi\psi)\|\|_{L_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\|{\mathcal{I}}^{-1}g_{n}^{\prime}(0)\|\|_{L_{4}}\|I(\varphi\psi\neq 1)\|_{L_{4}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{m}\|\|g_{n}^{\prime}(0)\|\|_{L_{4}}e^{-\gamma n/4}\lesssim\frac{\sqrt{M}}{m}\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}e^{-\gamma n/4}.

Note also that

\displaystyle\|q_{n}(\hat{h})\|=\Bigl\|\int_{0}^{1}(g_{n}^{\prime\prime}-g^{\prime\prime})(\lambda\hat{h})d\lambda\ \hat{h}\Bigr\|\leq\|g_{n}^{\prime\prime}(0)-g^{\prime\prime}(0)\|\|\hat{h}\|+L\|\hat{h}\|^{2},

such that we also have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}^{1/2}\|{\mathcal{I}}^{-1}q_{n}(\hat{h})\varphi\psi\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{m}{\mathbb{E}}^{1/2}\|g_{n}^{\prime\prime}(0)-g^{\prime\prime}(0)\|^{2}\Bigl(\|\hat{h}\|\wedge\frac{m}{12L}\Bigr)^{2}+\frac{L}{m}{\mathbb{E}}^{1/2}\Bigl(\|\hat{h}\|\wedge\frac{m}{12L}\Bigr)^{4}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{m}{\mathbb{E}}^{1/4}\|g_{n}^{\prime\prime}(0)-g^{\prime\prime}(0)\|^{4}{\mathbb{E}}^{1/4}\Bigl(\|\hat{h}\|\wedge\frac{m}{12L}\Bigr)^{4}+\frac{L}{m}{\mathbb{E}}^{1/2}\Bigl(\|\hat{h}\|\wedge\frac{m}{12L}\Bigr)^{4}.

Using the second bound of Proposition
3.2
and the bound of Corollary
3.2
,
we get

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}^{1/2}\|{\mathcal{I}}^{-1}q_{n}(\hat{h})\varphi\psi\|^{2}\lesssim_{M,L,m}\frac{d}{n}.

Similarly, we can show that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}^{1/2}\|{\mathcal{I}}^{-1}r(\hat{h})\varphi\psi\|^{2}\lesssim_{M,L,m}\frac{d}{n},

using the fact that

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{I}}^{-1}r(\hat{h})\varphi\psi\|\leq\frac{L}{2m}\Bigl(\|\hat{h}\|\wedge\frac{m}{12L}\Bigr)^{2}

and the bound of Corollary
3.2
.

The above bounds and representation (
4
) imply that

\displaystyle\|{\mathbb{E}}_{\theta}\check{\theta}-\theta\|\lesssim_{M,L,m}\frac{d}{n}.

Using also Corollary
3.1
, we get

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{\theta}^{1/2}\|\check{\theta}-\theta\|^{2}\lesssim_{M,L,m}\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}.

∎

Proposition 4.4
.

The function
(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\mapsto\check{\theta}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
is Lipschitz with constant
\lesssim\frac{M}{m\sqrt{n}}:

for all
(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}),(\tilde{x}_{1},\dots,\tilde{x}_{n})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times\dots\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d},

\displaystyle\|\check{\theta}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})-\check{\theta}(\tilde{x}_{1},\dots,\tilde{x}_{n})\|\lesssim\frac{M}{m\sqrt{n}}\Bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|x_{j}-\tilde{x}_{j}\|^{2}\Bigr)^{1/2}.

proof.

By Proposition
4.2
, on the set
A,

\hat{\theta}
is Lipschitz with constant
\frac{4M}{m\sqrt{n}}.

This implies that function
\varphi
is also Lipschitz on the same set with constant
\frac{24L}{m}\frac{4M}{m\sqrt{n}}.

Note also that

\displaystyle\|g_{n}^{\prime\prime}(0)-\tilde{g}_{n}^{\prime\prime}(0)\|\leq\frac{L}{\sqrt{n}}\biggl(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|x_{j}-\tilde{x}_{j}\|^{2}\biggr)^{1/2},

implying that
\psi
is a Lipschitz function (on the whole space) with constant
\frac{8}{m}\frac{L}{\sqrt{n}}.

Using also the fact that
\varphi
and
\psi
are both bounded by
1
and
\|\hat{\theta}-\theta\|\leq\frac{m}{12L}
on the
set
\{\varphi\neq 0\},
it is easy to conclude that
\check{\theta}
is Lipschitz on
A
with constant

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{4M}{m\sqrt{n}}+\frac{m}{12L}\frac{24L}{m}\frac{4M}{m\sqrt{n}}+\frac{m}{12L}\frac{8}{m}\frac{L}{\sqrt{n}}\lesssim\frac{M}{m\sqrt{n}}.

It remains to consider the case when
\varphi(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in A
and
\varphi(\tilde{x}_{1},\dots,\tilde{x}_{n})\in A^{c}

(the case when both points are in
A^{c}
is
trivial). In this case, define
x_{j}^{\lambda}=\lambda x_{j}+(1-\lambda)\tilde{x}_{j},\lambda\in[0,1],j=1,\dots,n.

Note that
A
is a closed set (by continuity of both
\hat{\theta}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
and
n^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}V^{\prime\prime}(x_{j}-\theta)
).
If
\bar{\lambda}
denotes the supremum of those
\lambda
for which
(x_{1}^{\lambda},\dots,x_{n}^{\lambda})\in A,

then
(x_{1}^{\bar{\lambda}},\dots,x_{n}^{\bar{\lambda}})\in\partial A,

(\varphi\psi)(x_{1}^{\bar{\lambda}},\dots,x_{n}^{\bar{\lambda}})=0

and
\check{\theta}(x_{1}^{\bar{\lambda}},\dots,x_{n}^{\bar{\lambda}})=0=\check{\theta}(\tilde{x}_{1},\dots,\tilde{x}_{1}).

\displaystyle|{\mathbb{E}}_{\theta}\check{f}_{k}(\check{\theta})-f(\theta)|\lesssim\|f\|_{C^{s}}({\mathbb{E}}\|\check{\vartheta}\|)^{k}(\|{\mathbb{E}}\check{\vartheta}\|+{\mathbb{E}}\|\check{\vartheta}\|^{1+\rho}).

Moreover,

\displaystyle|{\mathbb{E}}_{\theta}\check{f}_{k}(\check{\theta})-f(\theta)|\lesssim_{M,L,m}\|f\|_{C^{s}}\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}\Bigr)^{s}.

proof.

Note that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{\theta}\check{f}_{k}(\check{\theta})-f(\theta)=(-1)^{k}(\check{\mathcal{B}}^{k+1}f)(\theta).

We also have

\displaystyle(\check{\mathcal{B}}^{k+1}f)(\theta)

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}_{\theta}(\check{\mathcal{B}}^{k}f)(\check{\theta})-(\check{\mathcal{B}}^{k}f)(\theta)

\displaystyle=\langle(\check{\mathcal{B}}^{k}f)^{\prime}(\theta),{\mathbb{E}}\check{\vartheta}\rangle+{\mathbb{E}}S_{\check{\mathcal{B}}^{k}f}(\theta;\check{\vartheta}).

Using bounds of Proposition
5.2

and of Proposition

\displaystyle|(\check{\mathcal{B}}^{k+1}f)(\theta)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|(\check{\mathcal{B}}^{k}f)^{\prime}\|\|{\mathbb{E}}\check{\vartheta}\|+\|\check{\mathcal{B}}^{k}f\|_{C^{1+\rho}}{\mathbb{E}}\|\check{\vartheta}\|^{1+\rho}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{C^{s}}({\mathbb{E}}\|\check{\vartheta}\|)^{k}(\|{\mathbb{E}}\check{\vartheta}\|+{\mathbb{E}}\|\check{\vartheta}\|^{1+\rho}).

Using also Proposition

\displaystyle|{\mathbb{E}}_{\theta}\check{f}_{k}(\check{\theta})-f(\theta)|\lesssim_{M,L,m}\|f\|_{C^{s}}\Bigl(\frac{d}{n}\Bigr)^{k/2}\Bigl(\frac{d}{n}+\Bigl(\frac{d}{n}\Bigr)^{(1+\rho)/2}\Bigr)

which allows to complete the proof.

\displaystyle|{\mathbb{E}}_{\theta}f_{k}(\hat{\theta})-f(\theta)|\lesssim_{M,L,m,s}\|f\|_{C^{s}}\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}\Bigr)^{s}.

(5.5)

It is now easy to prove Theorem

proof.

proof.

Recall that, for

\displaystyle g_{n}^{\prime}(0)=g_{n}^{\prime}(0)-g_{n}^{\prime}(\hat{h})=-\int_{0}^{1}g_{n}^{\prime\prime}(\lambda\hat{h})d\lambda\ \hat{h}.

g_{n}^{\prime}(\hat{h})=0

\displaystyle\tilde{g}_{n}^{\prime}(\tilde{h})-g_{n}^{\prime}(\tilde{h})=g_{n}^{\prime}(\hat{h})-g_{n}^{\prime}(\tilde{h})=\int_{0}^{1}g_{n}^{\prime\prime}(\tilde{h}+\lambda(\hat{h}-\tilde{h}))d\lambda\ (\hat{h}-\tilde{h}).

6
Minimax lower bounds

In this section, we provide lower bounds for the estimation of the location parameter and functionals thereof
that match the upper bounds obtained in the previous sections up to constants.

We start with a comment on the proof of Proposition
2.2
.

proof.

It follows the same line of arguments as in the proof of Theorem 2.2 in
[
18
]
. It is based on a construction of a set
{\mathcal{F}}
of smooth functionals such that the existence of estimators
of
f(\theta)
for all
f\in{\mathcal{F}}
with some error rate would allow one to design an estimator of parameter
\theta
itself with a certain
error rate. This rate is then compared with a minimax lower bound

\inf_{\hat{\theta}}\max_{\theta\in\Theta}\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\|\hat{\theta}-\theta\|^{2}
in the parameter estimation problem,
where
\Theta
is a maximal
\varepsilon
-net of the unit sphere (for a suitable
\varepsilon
), yielding as a result a minimax lower bound in the
functional estimation. Minimax lower bound in the parameter estimation problem can be deduced in a standard way from Theorem 2.5 of
[
28
]
using KL-divergence (Fano’s type argument).
In fact, while in the Gaussian location model KL-divergence coincides with
1/2
times the squared Euclidean distance, a similar property also holds for our log-concave location models:

\displaystyle K(P_{\theta}\|P_{\theta^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}(V(\xi+\theta-\theta^{\prime})-V(\xi))

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}(V(\xi+\theta-\theta^{\prime})-V(\xi)-\langle V^{\prime}(\xi),\theta-\theta^{\prime}\rangle)\leq M\|\theta-\theta^{\prime}\|^{2}/2,

where we used Proposition
3.1
and Assumption
1
in the inequality.

∎

Our next goal is to provide the proof of the local minimax lower bound of
Proposition
2.3
.
It will be based on Bayes risk lower bounds for the estimation of location parameter as well as functionals thereof that might be of independent interest.

Let
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{F},(\mathbb{P}_{\theta})_{\theta\in\Theta})
be a statistical model and let
G
be a topological group acting on
the measurable space
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{F})
and
\Theta.
Let
\Theta
be such that
(\mathbb{P}_{\theta})_{\theta\in\Theta}
is
G
-equivariant,
i.e.,
\mathbb{P}_{g\theta}(gA)=\mathbb{P}_{\theta}(A)
for all
g\in G,\theta\in\Theta
and
A\in\mathcal{F}.

Suppose that
g\mapsto\mathbb{P}_{g\theta}(A)
is measurable for every
A\in\mathcal{F}
,
\theta\in\Theta
.

Recall that, for two probability measures
\mu,\nu
on an arbitrary measurable space with
\mu
being absolutely continuous w.r.t.

\nu,
the
\chi^{2}
-divergence
\chi^{2}(\mu,\nu)
is defined as

\displaystyle\chi^{2}(\mu,\nu):=\int\Bigl(\frac{d\mu}{d\nu}-1\Bigr)^{2}d\nu=\int\Bigl(\frac{d\mu}{d\nu}\Bigr)^{2}d\nu-1.

The key ingredient in our proofs is the following version of an equivariant van Trees type inequality established in
[
30
]
, Proposition 1.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
\Pi
be a Borel probability measure on
G,
let
\psi:\Theta\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a derived parameter such that
\int_{G}\|\psi(g\theta)\|^{2}\Pi(dg)<\infty
for all
\theta\in\Theta
and let

\hat{\psi}:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
be an estimator of
\psi(\theta),

based on an observation

X\sim{\mathbb{P}}_{\theta},\theta\in\Theta.

Then, for all
\theta\in\Theta
and all
h_{1},\dots,h_{m}\in G
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{G}\mathbb{E}_{g\theta}\|\hat{\psi}(X)-\psi(g\theta)\|^{2}\,\Pi(dg)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\big(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\int_{G}(\psi_{j}(gh_{j}^{-1}\theta)-\psi_{j}(g\theta))\,\Pi(dg)\big)^{2}}{\sum_{j=1}^{m}\big(\chi^{2}(\mathbb{P}_{h_{j}\theta},\mathbb{P}_{\theta})+\chi^{2}(\Pi\circ R_{h_{j}},\Pi)+\chi^{2}(\mathbb{P}_{h_{j}\theta},\mathbb{P}_{\theta})\chi^{2}(\Pi\circ R_{h_{j}},\Pi)\big)},

with
\Pi\circ R_{h_{j}}
defined by
(\Pi\circ R_{h_{j}})(B)=\Pi(Bh_{j})
for all Borel sets
B\subset G.

This lemma will be applied to our log-concave location model with
G
being the group of all translations of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

The Bayes risk lower bound will be formulated for the class of all prior density functions
\pi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
with respect to the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfying one of the following two conditions:

(P1)

\pi=e^{-W}
with
W:\mathbb{R}^{d}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
being twice differentiable such that
\|W^{\prime\prime}\|_{L_{\infty}}
and
\|W^{\prime\prime}\|_{\operatorname{Lip}}
are finite,

(P2)

\pi
has bounded support and is twice differentiable such that
\|\pi^{(j)}\|_{L_{\infty}}
and
\|\pi^{(j)}\|_{\operatorname{Lip}}
are finite for
j=0,1,2
(actually,
it suffices to assume it only for
j=2
since the support is bounded).

Our first result deals with the estimation of the location parameter itself. We assume that i.i.d. observations
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}

are sampled from a distribution belonging to a log-concave location family
e^{-V(x-\theta)}dx,\theta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

with convex function
V
satisfying Assumption
1
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
\Pi
be a probability measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with density
\pi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
with respect to the Lebesgue measure satisfying either (P1) or (P2). Suppose that
\pi
has finite Fisher information matrix

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\pi}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\pi^{\prime}(\theta)\otimes\pi^{\prime}(\theta)}{\pi(\theta)}\,d\theta.

Then, for all
\delta>0
, we have

\displaystyle\inf_{\hat{\theta}_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\|\hat{\theta}_{n}-\theta\|^{2}\,\Pi_{\delta}(d\theta)\geq\frac{1}{n}\operatorname{tr}\Big(\Big(\mathcal{I}+\frac{1}{\delta^{2}n}\mathcal{J}_{\pi}\Big)^{-1}\Big),

(6.1)

where
\Pi_{\delta}
is the prior measure with density
\pi_{\delta}(\theta)=\delta^{-d}\pi(\delta^{-1}\theta)
,
\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

and
\hat{\theta}_{n}=\hat{\theta}_{n}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
is an arbitrary estimator based on
(X_{1},\dots,X_{n}).

Let us discuss two simple implications. First, if we choose
\pi=e^{-V}
that satisfies (P1) (in view of Assumption
1
) and let
\delta\to\infty,
then Theorem
6.1
implies

\displaystyle\inf_{\hat{\theta}_{n}}\sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\|\hat{\theta}_{n}-\theta\|^{2}\geq\frac{1}{n}\operatorname{tr}(\mathcal{I}^{-1}).

Secondly, if we choose

\displaystyle\pi(\theta)=\prod_{j=1}^{d}\frac{3}{4}\cos^{3}(\theta_{j})I_{[-\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2}]}(\theta_{j}),

that satisfies (P2), then we have, by an easy
computation,
\mathcal{J}_{\pi}=\frac{9}{2}I_{d}.

Moreover, for
\delta=\frac{2c}{\pi\sqrt{n}}
,
c>0
, the prior
\pi_{\delta}
has support in
\{\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\max_{j}|\theta_{j}|\leq\frac{c}{\sqrt{n}}\}\subseteq\{\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\|\theta\|\leq c\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}\}
, and Theorem
6.1
implies

\displaystyle\inf_{\hat{\theta}_{n}}\sup_{\|\theta\|\leq c\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}}\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\|\hat{\theta}_{n}-\theta\|^{2}\geq\frac{1}{n}\operatorname{tr}\Big(\Big(\mathcal{I}+\frac{9\pi^{2}}{8c^{2}}I_{d}\Big)^{-1}\Big).

The proof of Theorem
6.1
will be based on the following lemma.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
\pi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
be a probability density function with respect to the Lebesgue measure
\lambda
satisfying (P2) and
\int\frac{\|\pi^{\prime}\|^{2}}{\pi}\,d\lambda<\infty
. Moreover, let
p=e^{-W}
,
W:\mathbb{R}^{d}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
, be a probability density function with respect
\lambda
satisfying (P1). Suppose that
p
is constant on the support of
\pi
and that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\|\theta\|^{2}\,p(\theta)d\theta<\infty
. For
\epsilon>0
, set

\displaystyle\pi_{\epsilon}:=\frac{q+\epsilon}{1+\epsilon}\ p,\qquad q:=\frac{\pi}{\int\pi p\,d\lambda}.

(6.2)

Then, for every
\epsilon>0
,
\pi_{\epsilon}
is a probability density function with respect to
\lambda
satisfying (P1) and
\int\frac{\|\pi_{\epsilon}^{\prime}\|^{2}}{\pi_{\epsilon}}\,d\lambda<\infty
. Moreover, as
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\pi_{\epsilon}}=\int\frac{\pi_{\epsilon}^{\prime}\otimes\pi_{\epsilon}^{\prime}}{\pi_{\epsilon}}\,d\lambda\to\int\frac{\pi^{\prime}\otimes\pi^{\prime}}{\pi}\,d\lambda=\mathcal{J}_{\pi}.

proof.

To see that
\pi_{\epsilon}
satisfies (P1), we have to show that
W_{\epsilon}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
defined by
\pi_{\epsilon}=e^{-W_{\epsilon}}
is twice differentiable with
\|W_{\epsilon}^{\prime\prime}\|_{L_{\infty}},\|W_{\epsilon}^{\prime\prime}\|_{\operatorname{Lip}}<\infty
. Write
q_{\epsilon}=\frac{q+\epsilon}{1+\epsilon}
such that
W_{\epsilon}=-\log q_{\epsilon}+W
. Hence,

\displaystyle W^{\prime\prime}_{\epsilon}=W^{\prime\prime}-\frac{q_{\epsilon}^{\prime\prime}}{q_{\epsilon}}+\frac{q^{\prime}_{\epsilon}\otimes q^{\prime}_{\epsilon}}{q_{\epsilon}^{2}}=W^{\prime\prime}-\frac{q^{\prime\prime}}{q+\epsilon}+\frac{q^{\prime}\otimes q^{\prime}}{(q+\epsilon)^{2}}.

Using the fact that
q+\epsilon
is lower bounded by
\epsilon
and that all involved functions
W^{\prime\prime},q^{\prime},q^{\prime\prime}
and
q+\epsilon
are bounded and have bounded Lipschitz constant, it follows that
\pi_{\epsilon}
satisfies (P1). Moreover, by the assumptions on
\pi
and
W
, we get

\displaystyle\pi^{\prime}_{\epsilon}=\frac{q^{\prime}e^{-W}+(q+\epsilon)W^{\prime}e^{-W}}{1+\epsilon}=\frac{q^{\prime}e^{-W}+\epsilon W^{\prime}e^{-W}}{1+\epsilon},

and

\displaystyle\int\frac{\|\pi_{\epsilon}^{\prime}\|^{2}}{\pi_{\epsilon}}\,d\lambda\leq\frac{2}{1+\epsilon}\int\Big(\frac{\|q^{\prime}\|^{2}e^{-W}}{q+\epsilon}+\|W^{\prime}\|^{2}e^{-W}\epsilon\Big)\,d\lambda<\infty.

Moreover, using again that
W
is constant on the support of
\pi
, we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\pi_{\epsilon}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1+\epsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big(\frac{(q^{\prime}\otimes q^{\prime})e^{-W}}{q+\epsilon}+\epsilon(W^{\prime}\otimes W^{\prime})e^{-W}\Big)\,d\lambda

\displaystyle\to\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{(q^{\prime}\otimes q^{\prime})e^{-W}}{q}\,d\lambda=\int\frac{\pi^{\prime}\otimes\pi^{\prime}}{\pi}\,d\lambda=\mathcal{J}_{\pi}\qquad\text{as }\epsilon\rightarrow 0,

where we applied the dominated convergence theorem in the last step.∎

We are now ready to prove Theorem
6.1
.

proof.

We first consider the case where
\pi
satisfies (P1). We assume that the Bayes risk on the left-hand side in (
6.1
) is finite because otherwise the result is trivial. Since the location model is an example of an equivariant statistical model (with translation group acting on parameter space and sample space), we can apply Lemma
6.1
to
\psi(\theta)=\theta
,
which yields that for any
\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{d}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\inf_{\hat{\theta}_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\|\hat{\theta}_{n}-\theta\|^{2}\,\Pi_{\delta}(d\theta)

(6.3)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\big(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\langle e_{j},\theta_{j}\rangle\big)^{2}}{\sum_{j=1}^{d}\big(\chi^{2}(P^{\otimes n}_{\theta_{j}},P^{\otimes n}_{0})+\chi^{2}(\Pi_{\delta,\theta_{j}},\Pi_{\delta})+\chi^{2}(P^{\otimes n}_{\theta_{j}},P^{\otimes n}_{0})\chi^{2}(\Pi_{\delta,\theta_{j}},\Pi_{\delta})\big)},

where
e_{1},\dots,e_{d}
is the standard basis in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\Pi_{\delta,\theta_{j}}
is the probability measure with density
\delta^{-d}\pi(\delta^{-1}(\theta+\theta_{j}))
,
\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Let us now apply a limiting argument. First, we have

\displaystyle\chi^{2}(P_{\theta_{j}},P_{0})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}e^{2V(\xi)-2V(\xi-\theta_{j})}-1

\displaystyle\leq e^{L\|\theta_{j}\|^{3}}\mathbb{E}e^{2\langle V^{\prime}(\xi),\theta_{j}\rangle-\langle V^{\prime\prime}(\xi)\theta_{j},\theta_{j}\rangle}-1,

(6.4)

where we used Proposition
3.1
in the inequality. If we set
\theta_{j}=th_{j}
with
t>0
and
h_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and then combine (
6.4
) with Lemma
3.1
and Assumption
1
, we get

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{t^{2}}\chi^{2}(P_{th_{j}},P_{0})\leq-\mathbb{E}\langle V^{\prime\prime}(\xi)h_{j},h_{j}\rangle+2\mathbb{E}\langle V^{\prime}(\xi),h_{j}\rangle^{2}=\langle h_{j},\mathcal{I}h_{j}\rangle

and thus also

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{t^{2}}\chi^{2}(P^{\otimes n}_{th_{j}},P^{\otimes n}_{0})\leq n\langle h_{j},\mathcal{I}h_{j}\rangle.

Moreover, since the prior density
\pi
satisfies (P1), Proposition
3.1
and Lemma
3.1
are still applicable, and we also have

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{t^{2}}\chi^{2}(\Pi_{\delta,th_{j}},\Pi_{\delta})\leq\langle h_{j},\delta^{-2}\mathcal{J}_{\pi}h_{j}\rangle.

Substituting these formulas into (
6.3
), we get for every
h_{1},\dots,h_{d}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\inf_{\hat{\theta}_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\|\hat{\theta}_{n}-\theta\|^{2}\,\Pi_{\delta}(d\theta)\geq\frac{\big(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\langle e_{j},h_{j}\rangle\big)^{2}}{\sum_{j=1}^{d}\langle h_{j},(n\mathcal{I}+\delta^{-2}\mathcal{J}_{\pi})h_{j}\rangle}.

Setting
h_{j}=(n\mathcal{I}+\delta^{-2}\mathcal{J}_{\pi})^{-1}e_{j}
,
j\leq d
, we arrive at

\displaystyle\inf_{\hat{\theta}_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{E}_{\delta\theta}\|\hat{\theta}_{n}-\delta\theta\|^{2}\,\pi(\theta)d\theta=\inf_{\hat{\theta}_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\|\hat{\theta}_{n}-\theta\|^{2}\,\Pi_{\delta}(d\theta)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{j=1}^{d}\langle e_{j},(n\mathcal{I}+\delta^{-2}\mathcal{J}_{\pi})^{-1}e_{j}\rangle=\operatorname{tr}((n\mathcal{I}+\delta^{-2}\mathcal{J}_{\pi})^{-1}).

(6.5)

It remains to extend (
6.5
) to all densities
\pi
satisfying (P2). To this end, we apply Lemma
6.2
to get
\pi_{\epsilon}
,
\epsilon>0
, from (
6.2
) satisfying (P1) and
\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\mathcal{J}_{\pi_{\epsilon}}=\mathcal{J}_{\pi}
. Note also that
qp=\pi.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\inf_{\hat{\theta}_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{E}_{\delta\theta}\|\hat{\theta}_{n}-\delta\theta\|^{2}\,\pi(\theta)d\theta=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\inf_{\hat{\theta}_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{E}_{\delta\theta}\|\hat{\theta}_{n}-\delta\theta\|^{2}\,(\pi(\theta)+\epsilon p(\theta))d\theta

\displaystyle=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{1+\epsilon}\inf_{\hat{\theta}_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{E}_{\delta\theta}\|\hat{\theta}_{n}-\delta\theta\|^{2}\,(q(\theta)p(\theta)+\epsilon p(\theta))d\theta

\displaystyle=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\inf_{\hat{\theta}_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{E}_{\delta\theta}\|\hat{\theta}_{n}-\delta\theta\|^{2}\,\pi_{\epsilon}(\theta)d\theta\geq\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\operatorname{tr}((n\mathcal{I}+\delta^{-2}\mathcal{J}_{\pi_{\epsilon}})^{-1})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{tr}((n\mathcal{I}+\delta^{-2}\mathcal{J}_{\pi})^{-1}),

where we applied (
6.5
) to
\pi_{\epsilon}.

∎

We now turn to the estimation of functionals of the location parameter.
For a continuous function
g:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the local continuity modulus of
g
at point
x_{0}
is defined by

\displaystyle\omega_{g}(x_{0},\delta)=\sup_{\|x-x_{0}\|\leq\delta}\|g(x)-g(x_{0})\|,\ \delta\geq 0.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
be a continuously differentiable function and let
\theta_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Let
\pi:\mathbb{R}\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
be a probability density function satisfying (P2) for
d=1
. Suppose that

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\pi}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{(\pi^{\prime}(s))^{2}}{\pi(s)}\,ds<\infty.

Then there exists
v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
\|v\|=1
, such that, for every
\delta>0
,

\displaystyle\inf_{\hat{T}_{n}}

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}n\mathbb{E}_{\theta_{0}+sv}(\hat{T}_{n}-f(\theta_{0}+sv))^{2}\,\Pi_{\delta}(ds)\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\geq\|\mathcal{I}^{-1/2}f^{\prime}(\theta_{0})\|-\sqrt{\frac{\mathcal{J}_{\pi}}{\delta^{2}n}}\|\mathcal{I}^{-1}f^{\prime}(\theta_{0})\|-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\omega_{\mathcal{I}^{-1/2}f^{\prime}}(\theta_{0},|s|)\,\Pi_{\delta}(ds),

where
\Pi_{\delta}
is the prior distribution with density
\delta^{-1}\pi(\delta^{-1}s)
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
\hat{T}_{n}=\hat{T}_{n}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})

is an arbitrary estimator based on
(X_{1},\dots,X_{n}).

proof.

Without loss of generality we may assume that
\theta_{0}=0.

Our goal is to apply Lemma
6.1
to
\psi=f
and to the one-dimensional subgroup
G=\mathbb{R}v=\{sv:s\in\mathbb{R}\}
with direction
v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\|v\|=1
, to be determined later.
As in the proof of Theorem
6.1
, we first establish a lower bound for densities
\pi=e^{-W}
satisfying (P1) with
d=1
and for the special case that
f
and
f^{\prime}
are bounded on
\mathbb{R}v.

In this case, applying Lemma
6.1
, we get for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\inf_{\hat{T}_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{E}_{sv}(\hat{T}_{n}-f(sv))^{2}\,\Pi_{\delta}(ds)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}(f((s-t)v)-f(sv))\,\Pi_{\delta}(ds)\big)^{2}}{\chi^{2}(P^{\otimes n}_{tv},P^{\otimes n}_{0})+\chi^{2}(\Pi_{\delta,t},\Pi_{\delta})+\chi^{2}(P^{\otimes n}_{tv},P^{\otimes n}_{0})\chi^{2}(\Pi_{\delta,t},\Pi_{\delta})},

(6.6)

where
\Pi_{\delta,t}
is the probability measure with density
\pi_{\delta,t}(s)=\delta^{-1}\pi(\delta^{-1}(s+t))
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Now, using that
\pi
satisfies (P1), we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{t^{2}}\chi^{2}(P^{\otimes n}_{tv},P^{\otimes n}_{0})\leq n\langle v,\mathcal{I}v\rangle,

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{t^{2}}\chi^{2}(\Pi_{\delta,t},\Pi_{\delta})\leq\delta^{-2}\mathcal{J}_{\pi}=\delta^{-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}W^{\prime\prime}(s)e^{-W(s)}\,ds,

as shown in the proof of Theorem
6.1
. Moreover, using that
f
and
f^{\prime}
are bounded on
\mathbb{R}v,
standard results on the differentiation of integrals where the integrand depends on a real parameter (e.g.
[
3
, Corollary 5.9]
) yield

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(f((s-t)v)-f(sv))\,\Pi_{\delta}(ds)=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\langle f^{\prime}(sv),v\rangle\,\Pi_{\delta}(ds).

Substituting these formulas into (
6.3
) and letting
t
go to zero, we get

\displaystyle\inf_{\hat{T}_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{E}_{sv}(\hat{T}_{n}-f(sv))^{2}\,\Pi_{\delta}(ds)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\langle f^{\prime}(sv),v\rangle\,\Pi_{\delta}(ds)\big)^{2}}{n\langle v,\mathcal{I}v\rangle+\delta^{-2}\mathcal{J}_{\pi}}.

(6.7)

While this inequality holds for all densities
\pi
satisfying (P1), we can apply Lemma
6.2
to extend this inequality to all probability densities satisfying (P2) (see the proof of Theorem
6.1
for the detailed argument). Moreover, for densities
\pi
with bounded support, we can also drop the boundedness conditions on
f
and
f^{\prime}
. In fact, the latter can be achieved by applying (
6.7
) to a functional
g
with
g,g^{\prime}
bounded on
\mathbb{R}v
and
g=f
on the support of
\Pi_{\delta}
(times
v
). As a consequence, under the assumptions of Theorem
6.2
, we have for every
v\in\mathbb{R}
,

\|v\|=1
,

\displaystyle\inf_{\hat{T}_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{E}_{sv}(\hat{T}_{n}-f(sv))^{2}\,\Pi_{\delta}(ds)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\langle f^{\prime}(sv),v\rangle\,\Pi_{\delta}(ds)\big)^{2}}{n\langle v,\mathcal{I}v\rangle+\delta^{-2}\mathcal{J}_{\pi}}=\frac{1}{n}\frac{\big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\langle f^{\prime}(sv),v\rangle\,\Pi_{\delta}(ds)\big)^{2}}{\langle v,(\mathcal{I}+\frac{\mathcal{J}_{\pi}}{\delta^{2}n}I_{d})v\rangle}.

Choosing

\displaystyle v=\frac{A^{-1}f^{\prime}(0)}{\|A^{-1}f^{\prime}(0)\|},\qquad A=\mathcal{I}+\frac{\mathcal{J}_{\pi}}{\delta^{2}n}I_{d},

we obtain

\displaystyle\inf_{\hat{T}_{n}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}n\mathbb{E}_{sv}(\hat{T}_{n}-f(sv))^{2}\,\Pi_{\delta}(ds)\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\langle A^{-1/2}f^{\prime}(sv),A^{-1/2}f^{\prime}(0)\rangle\,\Pi_{\delta}(ds)|}{\|A^{-1/2}f^{\prime}(0)\|}.

Using the inequality

\displaystyle|\langle A^{-1/2}f^{\prime}(sv),A^{-1/2}f^{\prime}(0)\rangle|

\displaystyle\geq\|A^{-1/2}f^{\prime}(0)\|^{2}-\|A^{-1/2}f^{\prime}(0)\|\|A^{-1/2}f^{\prime}(0)-A^{-1/2}f^{\prime}(sv)\|

\displaystyle\geq\|A^{-1/2}f^{\prime}(0)\|(\|A^{-1/2}f^{\prime}(0)\|-\omega_{A^{-1/2}f^{\prime}}(0,|s|)),

we arrive at

\displaystyle\inf_{\hat{T}_{n}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}n\mathbb{E}_{sv}(\hat{T}_{n}-f(sv))^{2}\,\Pi_{\delta}(ds)\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\geq\|A^{-1/2}f^{\prime}(0)\|-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\omega_{A^{-1/2}f^{\prime}}(0,|s|)\,d\Pi_{\delta}(s).

(6.8)

Since
A^{-1}=\mathcal{I}^{-1}-\frac{\mathcal{J}_{\pi}}{\delta^{2}n}A^{-1}\mathcal{I}^{-1}\succeq 0
and

{\mathcal{I}}^{-2}\succeq A^{-1}\mathcal{I}^{-1},

we have

\displaystyle\|A^{-1/2}f^{\prime}(0)\|

\displaystyle=\langle A^{-1}f^{\prime}(0),f^{\prime}(0)\rangle^{1/2}

\displaystyle=(\langle{\mathcal{I}}^{-1}f^{\prime}(0),f^{\prime}(0)\rangle-\langle\frac{\mathcal{J}_{\pi}}{\delta^{2}n}A^{-1}\mathcal{I}^{-1}f^{\prime}(0),f^{\prime}(0)\rangle)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\geq\langle{\mathcal{I}}^{-1}f^{\prime}(0),f^{\prime}(0)\rangle^{1/2}-\langle\frac{\mathcal{J}_{\pi}}{\delta^{2}n}A^{-1}\mathcal{I}^{-1}f^{\prime}(0),f^{\prime}(0)\rangle^{1/2}

\displaystyle\geq\|\mathcal{I}^{-1/2}f^{\prime}(0)\|-\sqrt{\frac{\mathcal{J}_{\pi}}{\delta^{2}n}}\|\mathcal{I}^{-1}f^{\prime}(0)\|.

Note also that
A^{-1}\preceq{\mathcal{I}}^{-1},
implying
\|A^{-1}u\|\leq\|{\mathcal{I}}^{-1}u\|,u\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

and, as a consequence,
\omega_{A^{-1/2}f^{\prime}}(0,|s|)\leq\omega_{\mathcal{I}^{-1/2}f^{\prime}}(0,|s|).

These bounds along with (
6.8
) imply the claim of the theorem.
∎

Finally, we prove Proposition
2.3
.

proof.
Let us choose
\pi(s)=\frac{3}{4}\cos^{3}(\theta)I_{[-\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2}]}(\theta)
in which case (P2) holds and
\mathcal{J}_{\pi}=\frac{9}{2}
.
Choosing additionally
\delta=\frac{2c}{\pi\sqrt{n}}
,
c>0
, Theorem
6.2
yields

\displaystyle\inf_{\hat{T}_{n}}\sup_{\|\theta-\theta_{0}\|\leq\frac{c}{\sqrt{n}}}(n\mathbb{E}_{\theta}(\hat{T}_{n}-f(\theta))^{2})^{1/2}

\displaystyle\geq\|\mathcal{I}^{-1/2}f^{\prime}(\theta_{0})\|-\frac{3\pi}{\sqrt{8}c}\|\mathcal{I}^{-1}f^{\prime}(\theta_{0})\|-\omega_{\mathcal{I}^{-1/2}f^{\prime}}\Big(\theta_{0},\frac{c}{\sqrt{n}}\Big).

(6.9)

Under Assumption
1
,
\|\mathcal{I}^{-1}f^{\prime}(\theta_{0})\|\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}}\|\mathcal{I}^{-1/2}f^{\prime}(\theta_{0})\|.

In addition, for
f\in C^{s},
where
s=1+\rho,

\rho\in(0,1],

\displaystyle\omega_{\mathcal{I}^{-1/2}f^{\prime}}\Big(\theta_{0},\frac{c}{\sqrt{n}}\Big)\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}}\omega_{f^{\prime}}\Big(\theta_{0},\frac{c}{\sqrt{n}}\Big)\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}}\|f\|_{C^{s}}\Bigl(\frac{c}{\sqrt{n}}\Bigr)^{\rho}.

Recalling that
\|\mathcal{I}^{-1/2}f^{\prime}(\theta_{0})\|=\sigma_{f}(\theta_{0}),
bound (
6
) implies

\displaystyle\inf_{\hat{T}_{n}}\sup_{\|\theta-\theta_{0}\|\leq\frac{c}{\sqrt{n}}}\frac{\sqrt{n}\|\hat{T}_{n}-f(\theta)\|_{L_{2}({\mathbb{P}}_{\theta})}}{\sigma_{f}(\theta_{0})}

\displaystyle\geq 1-\frac{3\pi}{\sqrt{8m}c}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}}\frac{\|f\|_{C^{s}}}{\sigma_{f}(\theta_{0})}\Bigl(\frac{c}{\sqrt{n}}\Bigr)^{\rho}.

(6.10)

Note that, for all
\theta
satisfying
\|\theta-\theta_{0}\|\leq\frac{c}{\sqrt{n}},

\displaystyle|\sigma_{f}(\theta)-\sigma_{f}(\theta_{0})|=|\|\mathcal{I}^{-1/2}f^{\prime}(\theta)\|-\|\mathcal{I}^{-1/2}f^{\prime}(\theta_{0})\||

\displaystyle\leq\omega_{\mathcal{I}^{-1/2}f^{\prime}}\Big(\theta_{0},\frac{c}{\sqrt{n}}\Big)\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}}\|f\|_{C^{s}}\Bigl(\frac{c}{\sqrt{n}}\Bigr)^{\rho}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\sup_{\|\theta-\theta_{0}\|\leq\frac{c}{\sqrt{n}}}\frac{\sqrt{n}\|\hat{T}_{n}-f(\theta)\|_{L_{2}({\mathbb{P}}_{\theta})}}{\sigma_{f}(\theta_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\|\theta-\theta_{0}\|\leq\frac{c}{\sqrt{n}}}\frac{\sqrt{n}\|\hat{T}_{n}-f(\theta)\|_{L_{2}({\mathbb{P}}_{\theta})}}{\sigma_{f}(\theta)}\sup_{\|\theta-\theta_{0}\|\leq\frac{c}{\sqrt{n}}}\frac{\sigma_{f}(\theta)}{\sigma_{f}(\theta_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\|\theta-\theta_{0}\|\leq\frac{c}{\sqrt{n}}}\frac{\sqrt{n}\|\hat{T}_{n}-f(\theta)\|_{L_{2}({\mathbb{P}}_{\theta})}}{\sigma_{f}(\theta)}\Bigl(1+\sup_{\|\theta-\theta_{0}\|\leq\frac{c}{\sqrt{n}}}\frac{|\sigma_{f}(\theta)-\sigma_{f}(\theta_{0})|}{\sigma_{f}(\theta_{0})}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\|\theta-\theta_{0}\|\leq\frac{c}{\sqrt{n}}}\frac{\sqrt{n}\|\hat{T}_{n}-f(\theta)\|_{L_{2}({\mathbb{P}}_{\theta})}}{\sigma_{f}(\theta)}\Bigl(1+\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}}\frac{\|f\|_{C^{s}}}{\sigma_{f}(\theta_{0})}\Bigl(\frac{c}{\sqrt{n}}\Bigr)^{\rho}\Bigr).

Using this bound together with (
6
) easily yields

\displaystyle\sup_{\|\theta-\theta_{0}\|\leq\frac{c}{\sqrt{n}}}\frac{\sqrt{n}\|\hat{T}_{n}-f(\theta)\|_{L_{2}({\mathbb{P}}_{\theta})}}{\sigma_{f}(\theta)}\geq 1-\frac{3\pi}{\sqrt{8m}c}-\frac{2}{\sqrt{m}}\frac{\|f\|_{C^{s}}}{\sigma_{f}(\theta_{0})}\Bigl(\frac{c}{\sqrt{n}}\Bigr)^{\rho}.

∎

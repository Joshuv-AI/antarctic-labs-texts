Strong Nonlocal-to-Local Convergence of the Cahn-Hilliard Equation and its Operator
Helmut Abels, Christoph Hurm

Abstract
We prove convergence of a sequence of weak solutions of the nonlocal Cahn-Hilliard equation to the strong solution of the corresponding local Cahn-Hilliard equation. The analysis is done in the case of sufficiently smooth bounded domains with Neumann boundary condition and a W 1,1 W^{1,1} -kernel. The proof is based on the relative entropy method. Additionally, we prove the strong L 2 L^{2} -convergence of the nonlocal operator to the negative Laplacian together with a rate of convergence.



Key words:
Cahn-Hilliard equation, nonlocal Cahn-Hilliard equation, nonlocal operators, nonlocal-to-local convergence, singular limit

35B40, 35K25, 35K55, 35D30, 45K05

\displaystyle=m\Delta\mu\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\text{in}\;\Omega_{T},

(1)

\displaystyle\mu

\displaystyle=-\Delta c+f^{\prime}(c)\;\;\;\;\;\;\text{in}\;\Omega_{T},

(2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{NL}_{\varepsilon}(c):=\frac{1}{4}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}J_{\varepsilon}(x-y)\big|c(x)-c(y)\big|^{2}\>\text{d}y\text{d}x+\int_{\Omega}f(c(x))\>\text{d}x.

\displaystyle\partial_{t}c

\displaystyle=m\Delta\mu\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\text{in}\;\Omega_{T},

\displaystyle c|_{t=0}=c_{0}\;\;\;\;\;\;\text{in }\Omega

\displaystyle\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\mu=0\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\text{on }\partial\Omega\times(0,T).

\displaystyle\rho_{\varepsilon}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow[0,\infty),\;\;\;\rho_{\varepsilon}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}),\;\;\;\rho_{\varepsilon}(r)=\rho_{\varepsilon}(-r)\;\;\;\text{for all }r\in\mathbb{R},\;\varepsilon>0,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\rho_{\varepsilon}(r)\>r^{n-1}\>\text{d}r=\frac{2}{C_{n}}\;\;\;\text{for all }\varepsilon>0,

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}\int_{\delta}^{\infty}\rho_{\varepsilon}(r)\>r^{n-1}\>\text{d}r=0\;\;\;\text{for all }\delta>0,

Preliminaries

In this section, we recollect some preliminary results, which we need throughout the paper. First, we briefly recall the Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(f)(\xi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-ix\cdot\xi}f(x)\>\text{d}x.

We observe that this map is well-defined and defines an isometric automorphism on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, cf. Plancherel‘s Theorem.
Further, we recall that the inverse of the negative Laplacian
-\Delta
with Neumann boundary condition is a well-defined isomorphism

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{-1}:\{c\in(H^{1}(\Omega))^{\prime}:c_{\Omega}=0\}\rightarrow\{c\in H^{1}(\Omega):c_{\Omega}=0\}.

For
c\in(H^{1}(\Omega))^{\prime}
, we define
c_{\Omega}=\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\langle c,1\rangle
.
Next, we state some important inequalities.

Remark 2.3
.

The space
\{c\in C^{k}_{c}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}):\partial_{\mathbf{n}}c|_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}=0\}
is dense in

\{c\in H^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}):\partial_{\mathbf{n}}c|_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}=0\}
. If
k=3
, the proof is based on the following idea:

Since
C^{\infty}_{0}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}})
is dense in
H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
, for any
c\in H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
there exists a sequence
(\tilde{c}_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset C^{\infty}_{0}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}})
such that
\tilde{c}_{j}\rightarrow c
in
H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
as
j\rightarrow\infty
. Next, we consider the auxiliary problem

\displaystyle(1-\Delta)w_{j}

\displaystyle=0\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\>\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},

\displaystyle\mathbf{n}\cdot\nabla w_{j}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{n}\cdot\nabla\tilde{c}_{j}\;\;\;\text{on }\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}

for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
. Then, by linear elliptic theory, there exists a solution
w_{j}\in C^{3}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}})
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
. Finally, the sequence
c_{j}:=\tilde{c}_{j}-w_{j}
,
j\in\mathbb{N}
, has the desired properties.

Next, we consider the case of the bent half-space.
Let
\gamma\in C^{k}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})
be given. Then, the space
\{c\in C^{k-1,1}_{c}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma}}):\partial_{\mathbf{n}}c|_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma}}=0\}
is a dense subset of
\{c\in H^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma}):\partial_{\mathbf{n}}c|_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma}}=0\}
. The proof is based on the following idea:

Since
\gamma\in C^{k}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})
, there exists a
C^{k-1,1}
-diffeomorphism
F_{\gamma}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
F_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})=\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma}
,
F_{\gamma}(x^{\prime},0)=(x^{\prime},\gamma(x^{\prime}))
and

, where

denotes the exterior unit normal on

, cf.
[

. Let
c\in\{c\in H^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma}):\partial_{\mathbf{n}}c|_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma}}=0\}

. Thanks to the first part, there exists a sequence

such that

as

. Finally, the sequence
c_{j}:=\tilde{c}_{j}\circ F_{\gamma}^{-1}\in C^{k-1,1}_{c}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma}})
,

, has the desired properties.

3
Convergence of the Nonlocal to the Local Operator

In this section, we prove the strong
L^{2}-
convergence of the nonlocal operator
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}
to
-\Delta
. In the first case, we study the convergence on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
c\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then, it holds

\displaystyle\Big\|\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathbb{R}^{n}}c+\Delta c\Big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\rightarrow 0\;\;\;\text{as }\varepsilon\searrow 0.

In addition, if
c\in H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we even have

\displaystyle\Big\|\mathcal{L}^{\mathbb{R}^{n}}_{\varepsilon}c+\Delta c\Big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq K\varepsilon\|c\|_{H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Beweis.

Thanks to Plancherel‘s Theorem, it suffices to prove

\displaystyle\Big\|\widehat{\mathcal{L}^{\mathbb{R}^{n}}_{\varepsilon}c}+\widehat{\Delta c}\Big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\rightarrow 0\;\;\;\text{as }\varepsilon\searrow 0.

By definition, we have

\displaystyle\Big\|\widehat{\mathcal{L}^{\mathbb{R}^{n}}_{\varepsilon}c}+\widehat{\Delta c}\Big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big|\big(-\mathcal{F}(J_{\varepsilon})(\xi)+\mathcal{F}(J_{\varepsilon})(0)-|\xi|^{2}\big)\mathcal{F}(c)(\xi)\big|^{2}\text{d}\xi,

where we used that

\displaystyle(J_{\varepsilon}*1)(x)=\mathcal{F}(J_{\varepsilon})(0)

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Next, we prove the pointwise convergence

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(J_{\varepsilon})(0)-\mathcal{F}(J_{\varepsilon})(\xi)\rightarrow|\xi|^{2}\;\;\;\text{for all }\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

as
\varepsilon\searrow 0
. Defining the functions
f_{\xi}(x):=-e^{-ix\cdot\xi}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(J_{\varepsilon})(0)-\mathcal{F}(J_{\varepsilon})(\xi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}J_{\varepsilon}(|x|)(f_{\xi}(x)-f_{\xi}(0))\>\text{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}J_{\varepsilon}(|x|)\big(f_{\xi}(x)-f_{\xi}(0)-\frac{1}{2}x^{T}D^{2}f_{\xi}(0)x\big)\>\text{d}x

\displaystyle\;\;\;+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}J_{\varepsilon}(|x|)\frac{1}{2}x^{T}D^{2}f_{\xi}(0)x\>\text{d}x

\displaystyle=:I_{\varepsilon}^{1}+I_{\varepsilon}^{2}.

Now, we analyze these terms separately.

Ad
I_{\varepsilon}^{1}
: Since the function
J_{\varepsilon}(|x|)x_{i}
is odd for all
i=1,\ldots,n
, it holds

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}J_{\varepsilon}(|x|)x_{i}\>\text{d}x=0

(14)

for all
i=1,\ldots,n
. Therefore, multiplying (
14
) by
\partial_{i}f_{\xi}(0)
and summing over all
i=1,\ldots,n
, gives

\displaystyle\big|I_{\varepsilon}^{1}\big|=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}J_{\varepsilon}(|x|)\big(f_{\xi}(x)-f_{\xi}(0)-\nabla f_{\xi}(0)\cdot x-\frac{1}{2}x^{T}D^{2}f_{\xi}(0)x\big)\>\text{d}x\right|.

Using Taylor‘s Theorem, we have
|f_{\xi}(x)-f_{\xi}(0)-\nabla f_{\xi}(0)\cdot x-\frac{1}{2}x^{T}D^{2}f_{\xi}(0)x|\leq\tilde{\varepsilon}C_{d,f}|x|^{2}
for all
|x|<\delta
. Therefore, it holds

\displaystyle\big|I_{\varepsilon}^{1}\big|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}J_{\varepsilon}(|x|)\Big|f_{\xi}(x)-f_{\xi}(0)-\nabla f_{\xi}(0)\cdot x-\frac{1}{2}x^{T}D^{2}f_{\xi}(0)x\Big|\>\text{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{\delta}(0)}\tilde{\varepsilon}C_{d,f}\rho_{\varepsilon}(|x|)\>\text{d}x+\int_{B_{\delta}(0)^{c}}C\rho_{\varepsilon}(|x|)\>\text{d}x.

Since we can choose
\tilde{\varepsilon}>0
arbitrarily small and since
\int_{B_{\delta}(0)}\rho_{\varepsilon}(|x|)\>\text{d}x\leq K
, the first term in the last line is arbitrarily small. In the second integral, the properties of
\rho_{\varepsilon}
imply that this integral vanishes as
\varepsilon\searrow 0
. Altogether, this shows
I_{\varepsilon}^{1}\rightarrow 0
as
\varepsilon\searrow 0
.

Ad
I_{\varepsilon}^{2}
: We compute

\displaystyle I_{\varepsilon}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}J_{\varepsilon}(|x|)\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l,m=1}^{n}x_{l}\partial_{l}\partial_{m}f_{\xi}(0)x_{m}\>\text{d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l,m=1}^{n}\xi_{l}\xi_{m}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}J_{\varepsilon}(|x|)x_{l}x_{m}\>\text{d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m=1}^{n}\xi_{m}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}J_{\varepsilon}(|x|)x_{m}^{2}\>\text{d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m=1}^{n}\xi_{m}^{2}\frac{1}{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}J_{\varepsilon}(|x|)\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}^{2}\>\text{d}x=|\xi|^{2}.

Here, we used the facts

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}J_{\varepsilon}(|x|)x_{l}x_{m}\>\text{d}x=0

for all
m\neq l
, and

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}J_{\varepsilon}(|x|)x_{m}^{2}\>\text{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}J_{\varepsilon}(|x|)x_{1}^{2}\>\text{d}x

for all
m=1,\ldots,n
. In the last step, we used our assumptions on
\rho_{\varepsilon}
to compute

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(|x|)\>\text{d}x=\frac{1}{2n}\>\omega_{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}\rho(r)r^{n-1}\>\text{d}r=\frac{\omega_{n}}{n}\frac{1}{C_{n}}=1,

where we used
\omega_{n}=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
and that (for any
j=1,\ldots,n
) it holds

\displaystyle C_{n}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|e_{1}\cdot\sigma|^{2}\>\text{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\sigma)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|e_{j}\cdot\sigma|^{2}\>\text{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\sigma)=\frac{1}{n}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\sigma|^{2}\>\text{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\sigma)=\frac{\omega_{n}}{n}.

Altogether, this shows the pointwise convergence

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(J_{\varepsilon})(\xi)-\mathcal{F}(J_{\varepsilon})(0)\rightarrow|\xi|^{2}\;\;\;\text{as }\varepsilon\searrow 0

for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Again, using Taylor‘s Theorem, we observe that

\displaystyle\Big|\widehat{J_{\varepsilon}}(0)-\widehat{J_{\varepsilon}}(\xi)-|\xi|^{2}\Big|

\displaystyle=\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}J_{\varepsilon}(|x|)\big(f_{\xi}(x)-f_{\xi}(0)-\nabla f_{\xi}(0)\cdot x-\frac{1}{2}x^{T}D^{2}f_{\xi}(0)x\big)\>\text{d}x\Big|

\displaystyle\leq C(1+|\xi|^{2})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(|x|)\>\text{d}x\leq C(1+|\xi|^{2}),

(15)

which implies

\displaystyle\big|\big(\mathcal{F}(J_{\varepsilon})(0)-\mathcal{F}(J_{\varepsilon})(\xi)-|\xi|^{2}\big)\mathcal{F}(c)(\xi)\big|^{2}\leq C\big(|\mathcal{F}(c)(\xi)|^{2}+|\xi|^{2}|\mathcal{F}(c)(\xi)|^{2}\big)

for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. As
c\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the right-hand side is integrable. Therefore, we use Lebesgue‘s dominated convergence theorem to conclude the proof for
c\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Now, let
c\in H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then, we even have

\displaystyle\Big|\widehat{J_{\varepsilon}}(0)-\widehat{J_{\varepsilon}}(\xi)-|\xi|^{2}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}J_{\varepsilon}(|x|)\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|D^{3}f_{\xi}(y)||x|^{3}\>\text{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C|\xi|^{3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(|x|)|x|\>\text{d}x\leq C\varepsilon|\xi|^{3}

for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
using a third-order Taylor expansion. Consequently, it holds

\displaystyle\Big\|\mathcal{L}^{\mathbb{R}^{n}}_{\varepsilon}c+\Delta c\Big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|(\mathcal{F}(J_{\varepsilon})(\xi)-\mathcal{F}(J_{\varepsilon})(0)-|\xi|^{2}|)\mathcal{F}(c)(\xi)|^{2}\>\text{d}\mathbf{\xi}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C\varepsilon^{2}}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big||\xi|^{3}\mathcal{F}(c)(\xi)\big|^{2}\text{d}\mathbf{\xi}=C\varepsilon^{2}\|c\|_{H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}.

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\Big\|\mathcal{L}^{\mathbb{R}^{n}}_{\varepsilon}c+\Delta c\Big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\varepsilon\|c\|_{H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

which concludes the proof.
∎

In the next lemma, we study the situation, where
x
and
y
have positive distance, i.e., no singularity appears. In fact, this will play an important role for the following proofs.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open and let
\Omega^{\prime}\subseteq\Omega
such that
\textup{dist}(\overline{\Omega^{\prime}},\partial\Omega)>0
. Then, for every
c\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, it holds

\displaystyle\big\|\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}c\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime})}\leq K\>\varepsilon\|c\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

where
K>0
only depends on
\textup{dist}(\Omega^{c},\Omega^{\prime})
. Here, we defined

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}c(x):=\int_{\Omega^{c}}J_{\varepsilon}(|x-y|)\big(c(x)-\tilde{c}(y)\big)\>\text{d}y

for all
c\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and almost all
x\in\Omega
. Here,
\tilde{c}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is an extension of
c
to the whole of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\|\tilde{c}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq K\|c\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
.

Beweis.

First of all, we observe

\displaystyle\big\|\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}c\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq K\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\left|\int_{\Omega^{c}}J_{\varepsilon}(|x-y|)c(x)\>\text{d}y\right|^{2}\>\text{d}x+K\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\left|\int_{\Omega^{c}}J_{\varepsilon}(|x-y|)\tilde{c}(y)\>\text{d}y\right|^{2}\>\text{d}x

\displaystyle=:K\big(I_{\varepsilon}^{1}+I_{\varepsilon}^{2}\big).

Now, we estimate these terms separately.

Ad
I_{\varepsilon}^{1}
: Since
\textup{dist}(\overline{\Omega^{\prime}},\partial\Omega)>0
, it holds
|x-y|\geq\delta:=\text{dist}(\Omega^{c},\Omega^{\prime})>0
for all
x\in\Omega^{c}
,
y\in\Omega^{\prime}
. This yields

\displaystyle I_{\varepsilon}^{1}\leq K_{\delta}\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}|c(x)|^{2}\left(\int_{\Omega^{c}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(|x-y|)|x-y|\>\text{d}y\right)^{2}\>\text{d}x\leq K_{\delta}\>\varepsilon^{2}\|c\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Ad
I_{\varepsilon}^{2}
: Here, we estimate

\displaystyle I_{\varepsilon}^{2}\leq\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\left(\int_{\Omega^{c}}J_{\varepsilon}(|x-y|)|\tilde{c}(y)|\>\text{d}y\right)^{2}\>\text{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\left(\int_{\Omega^{c}}J_{\varepsilon}(|x-y|)\>\text{d}y\right)\left(\int_{\Omega^{c}}J_{\varepsilon}(|x-y|)|\tilde{c}(y)|^{2}\>\text{d}y\right)\>\text{d}x

\displaystyle\leq K_{\delta}\>\varepsilon\int_{\Omega^{c}}|\tilde{c}(y)|^{2}\left(\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(|x-y|)|x-y|\>\text{d}x\right)\>\text{d}y

\displaystyle\leq K_{\delta}\>\varepsilon^{2}\|c\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2},

where we used
|x-y|\geq\delta:=\text{dist}(\Omega^{c},\Omega^{\prime})>0
and Fubini‘s Theorem. Altogether, we obtain

\displaystyle\big\|\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}c\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime})}\leq K_{\delta}\>\varepsilon\|c\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

This concludes the proof.
∎

In the proof of Theorem
4.1
, we want to use a localization argument. To this end, we now consider the upper half-space
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
c\in H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
with
\partial_{\mathbf{n}}c=0
on
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
. Then, it holds

\displaystyle\Big\|\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}c+\Delta c\Big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}\leq K\sqrt{\varepsilon}\|c\|_{H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}.

Beweis.

Denote
\tilde{c}\in H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
an extension of
c
to the whole of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\|\tilde{c}\|_{H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq K\|c\|_{H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}
. Then, we observe

\displaystyle\Big\|\mathcal{L}^{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}_{\varepsilon}c+\Delta c\Big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}

\displaystyle\leq\Big\|\mathcal{L}^{\mathbb{R}^{n}}_{\varepsilon}\tilde{c}+\Delta\tilde{c}\Big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}\tilde{c}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}

\displaystyle\leq K\varepsilon\|c\|_{H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}+\|\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}\tilde{c}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})},

where the error term
\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}c(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{-}}J_{\varepsilon}(|x-y|)(c(x)-\tilde{c}(y))\>\text{d}y

(16)

for a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
.
We want to prove that
\|\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}\tilde{c}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}\rightarrow 0
as
\varepsilon\searrow 0
. To this end, we first prove the statement for functions
c\in C^{3}_{0}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}})
with
\partial_{\mathbf{n}}c=0
on
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
and use a density argument afterwards to conclude the proof.

Using the transformation
y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n-1},y_{n})\mapsto(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n-1},-y_{n})=:\hat{y}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}c(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{-}}J_{\varepsilon}(|x-y|)(c(x)-\tilde{c}(y))\text{d}y=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}J_{\varepsilon}(|x-\hat{y}|)(c(x)-c(\hat{y}))\text{d}y

for a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
. For
\delta>0
, we obtain

\displaystyle|\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}c(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\Big|\int_{B_{\delta}(x^{\prime})\times(0,\delta)}J_{\varepsilon}(|x-\hat{y}|)(c(x)-c(\hat{y}))\text{d}y\Big|+\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\setminus\big(B_{\delta}(x^{\prime})\times(0,\delta)\big)}J_{\varepsilon}(|x-\hat{y}|)(c(x)-c(\hat{y}))\text{d}y\Big|

(17)

for a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
. In the second integral on the right-hand side, we use the calculations in the proof of Lemma
3.2
to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\setminus\big(B_{\delta}(x^{\prime})\times(0,\delta)\big)}J_{\varepsilon}(|x-\hat{y}|)(c(x)-c(\hat{y}))\text{d}y\Big|^{2}\text{d}x\leq K_{\delta}\>\varepsilon^{2}\|c\|_{H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}^{2},

since it holds
\text{dist}(x,\hat{y})\geq\delta>0
for all
\hat{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\setminus\big(B_{\delta}(x^{\prime})\times(0,\delta)\big)
. Thus, it suffices to consider the first integral on the right-hand side. Here, a first order Taylor expansion yields

\displaystyle c(x)-c(\hat{y})

\displaystyle=\nabla c(x)\cdot(x-\hat{y})+R_{2}(x,\hat{y})

\displaystyle=\nabla_{x^{\prime}}c(x)\cdot(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime})+\partial_{x_{n}}c(x)(x_{n}+y_{n})+R_{2}(x,\hat{y}),

where
x:=(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
and

\displaystyle R_{2}(x,\hat{y}):=\sum_{|\beta|=2}\frac{2}{\beta!}\left(\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)\text{D}^{\beta}c(\hat{y}+t(x-\hat{y}))\text{d}t\right)(x-\hat{y})^{\beta}.

Inserting this, we end up with

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{B_{\delta}(x^{\prime})\times(0,\delta)}J_{\varepsilon}(|x-\hat{y}|)(c(x)-c(\hat{y}))\text{d}y\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\Big|\int_{B_{\delta}(x^{\prime})\times(0,\delta)}J_{\varepsilon}(|x-\hat{y}|)\nabla_{x^{\prime}}c(x)\cdot(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime})\;\text{d}y\Big|

\displaystyle\;\;\;+\Big|\int_{B_{\delta}(x^{\prime})\times(0,\delta)}J_{\varepsilon}(|x-\hat{y}|)\partial_{x_{n}}c(x)(x_{n}+y_{n})\;\text{d}y\Big|

\displaystyle\;\;\;+\Big|\int_{B_{\delta}(x^{\prime})\times(0,\delta)}J_{\varepsilon}(|x-\hat{y}|)R_{2}(x,\hat{y})\;\text{d}y\Big|

\displaystyle=:I_{\varepsilon,\delta}^{1}+I_{\varepsilon,\delta}^{2}+I_{\varepsilon,\delta}^{3}.

(18)

Now, we estimate these integrals separately.

Ad
I_{\varepsilon,\delta}^{1}
: We observe that the integrand
J_{\varepsilon}(|x-\hat{y}|)\nabla_{x^{\prime}}c(x)\cdot(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime})
is odd with respect to
x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}
. Therefore, it holds

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\delta}(x^{\prime})}J_{\varepsilon}(|x-\hat{y}|)\nabla_{x^{\prime}}c(x)\cdot(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime})\text{d}y^{\prime}=0,

which then implies
I_{\varepsilon,\delta}^{1}=0
.

Ad
I_{\varepsilon,\delta}^{2}
: First of all, the properties of
c
and the fundamental theorem of calculus imply

\displaystyle\partial_{x_{n}}c(x^{\prime},x_{n})=\partial_{x_{n}}c(x^{\prime},x_{n})-\partial_{x_{n}}c(x^{\prime},0)=\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{x_{n}}^{2}c(x^{\prime},tx_{n})x_{n}\>\text{d}t.

This yields

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\>\Big|\int_{B_{\delta}(x^{\prime})\times(0,\delta)}J_{\varepsilon}(|x-\hat{y}|)\partial_{x_{n}}c(x)(x_{n}+y_{n})\text{d}y\Big|

\displaystyle=\Big|\int_{B_{\delta}(x^{\prime})\times(0,\delta)}\int_{0}^{1}J_{\varepsilon}(|x-\hat{y}|)\partial_{x_{n}}^{2}c(x^{\prime},tx_{n})x_{n}(x_{n}+y_{n})\>\text{d}t\>\text{d}y\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}\big|\partial_{x_{n}}^{2}c(x^{\prime},tx_{n})\big|\Big(\int_{B_{\delta}(x^{\prime})\times(0,\delta)}J_{\varepsilon}(|x-\hat{y}|)(|x_{n}|+|y_{n}|)|x_{n}+y_{n}|\text{d}y\Big)\text{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}\big|\partial_{x_{n}}^{2}c(x^{\prime},tx_{n})\big|a_{\varepsilon}(x_{n})\>\text{d}t,

where we defined

\displaystyle a_{\varepsilon}(x_{n}):=\int_{B_{\delta}(x^{\prime})\times(0,\delta)}\rho_{\varepsilon}(|x-\hat{y}|)\text{d}y.

By construction and due to the properties of
\rho_{\varepsilon}
, the function
a_{\varepsilon}
is in fact independent of
x^{\prime}
. Computing the
L^{2}
-norm of
a_{\varepsilon}
, we get

\displaystyle\|a_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{B_{\delta}(x^{\prime})\times(0,\delta)}\rho_{\varepsilon}(|x-\hat{y}|)\>\text{d}y\right)^{2}\text{d}x_{n}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{B_{\delta}(x^{\prime})\times(0,\delta)}\varepsilon^{-n}\rho\Big(\Big|\frac{x-\hat{y}}{\varepsilon}\Big|\Big)\>\text{d}y\right)^{2}\text{d}x_{n}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\rho(|x-\hat{y}|)\>\text{d}y^{\prime}\text{d}y_{n}\right)^{2}\text{d}x_{n}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\int_{0}^{R}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\rho(|x-\hat{y}|)\>\text{d}y^{\prime}\text{d}y_{n}\right)^{2}\text{d}x_{n}\leq R\varepsilon.

(19)

Here, we applied the transformations
y\mapsto\varepsilon y
and
x_{n}\mapsto\varepsilon x_{n}
and we used that
\text{supp}\rho\subset B_{R}(0)
. This then implies

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\Big|\int_{B_{\delta}(x^{\prime})\times(0,\delta)}J_{\varepsilon}(|x-\hat{y}|)\partial_{x_{n}}c(x)(x_{n}+y_{n})\text{d}y\Big|^{2}\>\text{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|a_{\varepsilon}(x_{n})|^{2}\left(\frac{1}{x_{n}}\int_{0}^{x_{n}}\|\partial_{x_{n}}^{2}c(x^{\prime},.)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}\text{d}z_{n}\right)^{2}\text{d}x

\displaystyle\leq K\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}|a_{\varepsilon}(x_{n})|^{2}\|\partial_{x_{n}}^{2}c(x^{\prime},.)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}^{2}\text{d}x_{n}\text{d}x^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq K\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\|a_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}^{2}\|\partial_{x_{n}}^{2}c(x^{\prime},.)\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}^{2}\text{d}x^{\prime}\leq K\varepsilon\|c\|_{H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}^{2},

where we used the embedding
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
.

Ad
I_{\varepsilon,\delta}^{3}
: Here, it holds

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\Big|\int_{B_{\delta}(x^{\prime})\times(0,\delta)}J_{\varepsilon}(|x-\hat{y}|)R_{2}(x,\hat{y})\text{d}y\Big|^{2}\text{d}x

\displaystyle\leq K\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\left(\int_{B_{\delta}(x^{\prime})\times(0,\delta)}\int_{0}^{1}\rho_{\varepsilon}(|x-\hat{y}|)|D^{2}c(\hat{y}+t(x-\hat{y}))|\>\text{d}t\text{d}y\right)^{2}\text{d}x

\displaystyle\leq K\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\left(\int_{B_{\delta}(x^{\prime})\times(0,\delta)}\rho_{\varepsilon}(|x-\hat{y}|)\>\text{d}y\right)\left(\int_{0}^{1}\int_{B_{\delta}(x^{\prime})\times(0,\delta)}\rho_{\varepsilon}(|x-\hat{y}|)|D^{2}c(\hat{y}+t(x-\hat{y}))|^{2}\>\text{d}y\text{d}t\right)\>\text{d}x

\displaystyle\leq K\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}a_{\varepsilon}(x_{n})\left(\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(|x-\hat{y}|)\|D^{2}c(\hat{y}^{\prime}+t(x^{\prime}-\hat{y}^{\prime}),.)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}^{2}\>\text{d}y\text{d}t\right)\>\text{d}x

\displaystyle\leq K\int_{0}^{R}a_{\varepsilon}(x_{n})\text{d}x_{n}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\|\rho_{\varepsilon}(|x^{\prime}-\hat{y}^{\prime},.|)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}\|D^{2}c(\hat{y}^{\prime}+t(x^{\prime}-\hat{y}^{\prime}),.)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}^{2}\>\text{d}y\text{d}x^{\prime}\text{d}t

\displaystyle\leq K\left(\int_{0}^{R}a_{\varepsilon}(x_{n})\>\text{d}x_{n}\right)\|c\|^{2}_{H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}\leq K\varepsilon\|c\|^{2}_{H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})},

(20)

where we first used the inequality of Cauchy-Schwarz and then Lemma
2.2
together with the embedding
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
in the fourth step. In the last step, we used
\text{supp}\rho\subset B_{R}(0)
and applied the transformations
y\mapsto\varepsilon y
and
x_{n}\mapsto\varepsilon x_{n}
.
Altogether, this shows

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}c\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}\leq K\sqrt{\varepsilon}\|c\|_{H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}

for all
c\in C^{3}_{0}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}})
with
\partial_{\mathbf{n}}c=0
on
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
.

In the next step, we want to use a denseness argument, in order conclude the proof. Let
c\in H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
with
\partial_{\mathbf{n}}c=0
on
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
be arbitrary. Since the space
\{c\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}):\partial_{\mathbf{n}}c=0\text{ on }\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\}
is dense in
\{c\in H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}):\partial_{\mathbf{n}}c=0\text{ on }\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\}
, cf. Remark
2.3
, there exists a sequence
(c_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset C^{\infty}_{0}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}})
with
\partial_{\mathbf{n}}c=0
on
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
such that
c_{k}\rightarrow c
in
H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
. Thanks to our results so far, the sequence

\displaystyle\Big(\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}c_{k}+\Delta c_{k}\Big)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})

is bounded. Thus, there exists a subsequence, which is again denoted by
\Big(\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}c_{k}+\Delta c_{k}\Big)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
, such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}c_{k}+\Delta c_{k}\rightharpoonup w\;\;\;\text{in }L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\;\;\;\text{as }k\rightarrow\infty

for some
w\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
. Since
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}
is linear and continuous, and therefore weakly continuous, it follows that
w=\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}c+\Delta c
. Then, the weak lower semi-continuity of norms implies

\displaystyle\Big\|\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}c+\Delta c\Big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}\leq\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\Big\|\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}c_{k}+\Delta c_{k}\Big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}\leq\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}K\sqrt{\varepsilon}\|c_{k}\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}\leq K\sqrt{\varepsilon}

for all
c\in H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
with
\partial_{\mathbf{n}}c=0
on
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
with
\|c\|_{H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}\leq 1
. In particular, this concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

The rate of convergence obtained in Lemma
3.3
is optimal. Even in the simplest case, where
n=1
and
c\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\overline{\mathbb{R}_{+}})
, we do not gain a better rate of convergence in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
unless
\partial_{\mathbf{n}}^{2}c=0
on
\partial\mathbb{R}_{+}
. This shows the following calculation:
Let
x>0
. Then, we obtain for the error term

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}c(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}J_{\varepsilon}(|x-\hat{y}|)(c(x)-c(\hat{y}))\>\textup{d}y

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{\delta}(x)\cap\mathbb{R}_{+}}J_{\varepsilon}(|x-\hat{y}|)(c(x)-c(\hat{y}))\>\textup{d}y+\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}\setminus{B_{\delta}(x)}}J_{\varepsilon}(|x-\hat{y}|)(c(x)-c(\hat{y}))\>\textup{d}y

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{\delta}(x)\cap\mathbb{R}_{+}}J_{\varepsilon}(|x-\hat{y}|)\Big(\frac{1}{2}c^{\prime\prime}(0)(x-\hat{y})^{2}+R_{3}(x,\hat{y})\Big)\textup{d}y+\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}\setminus{B_{\delta}(x)}}J_{\varepsilon}(|x-\hat{y}|)(c(x)-c(\hat{y}))\>\textup{d}y

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}c^{\prime\prime}(0)\int_{B_{\delta}(x)\cap\mathbb{R}_{+}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(|x-\hat{y}|)\textup{d}y+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon),

where
\int_{B_{\delta}(x)\cap\mathbb{R}_{+}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(|x-\hat{y}|)\textup{d}y=:a_{\varepsilon}(x)
and
\|a_{\varepsilon}(x)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}\geq K\sqrt{\varepsilon}
for
\varepsilon>0
small enough similar as in (
3
).
In the third step, we used a Taylor expansion. In the last step, we then applied Lemma
3.2
. Here, the term
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)
is measured with respect to the
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
-norm.

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
c\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
with
\partial_{\mathbf{n}}c=0
on
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
. Then, it holds

\displaystyle\Big\|\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}c+\Delta c\Big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}\rightarrow 0\;\;\;\text{as }\varepsilon\searrow 0.

Beweis.

Thanks to Lemma
3.3
, it suffices to prove that
\big\|\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}c+\Delta c\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}
is bounded uniformly in
\varepsilon>0
for all
c\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
with
\partial_{\mathbf{n}}c=0
on
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
. Then, the Banach-Steinhaus Theorem concludes the proof.

In fact, we only need to estimate the error term (
17
) in a suitable way. First of all, we prove the assertion for functions
c\in C^{2}_{0}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}})
with
\partial_{\mathbf{n}}c=0
on
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
and use a density argument afterwards to conclude the proof.
In the term

\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\setminus\big(B_{\delta}(x^{\prime})\times(0,\delta)\big)}J_{\varepsilon}(|x-\hat{y}|)(c(x)-c(\hat{y}))\text{d}y\Big|,

we can apply Lemma
3.2
, again.
Thus, it suffices to consider the first part of (
17
).
As in the proof of Lemma
3.3
, it holds
I^{1}_{\varepsilon,\delta}=0
. In
I^{2}_{\varepsilon,\delta}
, we use the properties of
c
and the fundamental theorem of calculus to obtain

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\>\Big|\int_{B_{\delta}(x^{\prime})\times(0,\delta)}J_{\varepsilon}(|x-\hat{y}|)\partial_{x_{n}}c(x)(x_{n}+y_{n})\text{d}y\Big|

\displaystyle=\Big|\int_{B_{\delta}(x^{\prime})\times(0,\delta)}\int_{0}^{1}J_{\varepsilon}(|x-\hat{y}|)\partial_{x_{n}}^{2}c(x^{\prime},tx_{n})x_{n}(x_{n}+y_{n})\>\text{d}t\text{d}y\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{\delta}(x^{\prime})\times(0,\delta)}\int_{0}^{1}J_{\varepsilon}(|x-\hat{y}|)|\partial_{x_{n}}^{2}c(x^{\prime},tx_{n})||x_{n}||x_{n}+y_{n}|\>\text{d}t\text{d}y

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}|\partial_{x_{n}}^{2}c(x^{\prime},tx_{n})|\Big(\int_{B_{\delta}(x^{\prime})\times(0,\delta)}J_{\varepsilon}(|x-\hat{y}|)(|x_{n}|+|y_{n}|)|x_{n}+y_{n}|\text{d}y\Big)\text{d}t.

In the inner integral, it holds

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\delta}(x^{\prime})\times(0,\delta)}J_{\varepsilon}(|x-\hat{y}|)(|x_{n}|+|y_{n}|)|x_{n}+y_{n}|\text{d}y

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{\delta}(x^{\prime})\times(0,\delta)}J_{\varepsilon}(|x-\hat{y}|)K(|x_{n}|^{2}+|y_{n}|^{2})\text{d}y

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{\delta}(x^{\prime})\times(0,\delta)}K\rho_{\varepsilon}(|x-\hat{y}|)\text{d}y\leq K.

This implies

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{B_{\delta}(x^{\prime})\times(0,\delta)}J_{\varepsilon}(|x-\hat{y}|)\partial_{x_{n}}c(x)(x_{n}+y_{n})\text{d}y\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}K|\partial_{x_{n}}^{2}c(x^{\prime},tx_{n})|\text{d}t

\displaystyle=K\frac{1}{x_{n}}\int_{0}^{x_{n}}|\partial_{x_{n}}^{2}c(x^{\prime},z_{n})|\text{d}z_{n},

where we changed variables as
z_{n}=tx_{n}
in the last step. This implies

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\Big|\int_{B_{\delta}(x^{\prime})\times(0,\delta)}J_{\varepsilon}(|x-\hat{y}|)\partial_{x_{n}}c(x)(x_{n}+y_{n})\text{d}y\Big|^{2}\text{d}x

\displaystyle\leq K\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\Big(\frac{1}{x_{n}}\int_{0}^{x_{n}}|\partial_{x_{n}}^{2}c(x^{\prime},z_{n})|\text{d}z_{n}\Big)^{2}\text{d}x

\displaystyle\leq K\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}|\partial_{x_{n}}^{2}c(x^{\prime},x_{n})|^{2}\text{d}x_{n}\text{d}x^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq K\|c\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}^{2},

(21)

where we applied Hardy‘s inequality.

Ad
I_{\varepsilon,\delta}^{3}
: Here, we use Fubini‘s Theorem and Lemma
2.2
to get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\Big|\int_{B_{\delta}(x^{\prime})\times(0,\delta)}J_{\varepsilon}(|x-\hat{y}|)R_{2}(x,\hat{y})\text{d}y\Big|^{2}\text{d}x

\displaystyle\leq K\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\int_{B_{\delta}(x^{\prime})\times(0,\delta)}\int_{0}^{1}\rho_{\varepsilon}(|x-\hat{y}|)|D^{2}c(\hat{y}+t(x-\hat{y}))|^{2}\text{d}t\text{d}y\text{d}x

\displaystyle\leq K\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\|\rho_{\varepsilon}(|(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime},.)|)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\|D^{2}c((1-t)y^{\prime}+tx^{\prime},.)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}^{2}\text{d}y^{\prime}\text{d}x^{\prime}\text{d}t

\displaystyle\leq K\|c\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}^{2}.

(22)

Altogether, we get the following estimate

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}\tilde{c}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}\leq K_{\delta}\|c\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}+K\|c\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}

for all
c\in C^{2}_{0}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}})
with
\partial_{x_{n}}c_{\big|\{x_{n}=0\}}=0
. Finally, a density argument as in the proof of Lemma
3.3
finishes the proof.
∎

In the next step, we prove convergence on the bent half-space.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\gamma\in C^{3}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})
with
\|\gamma\|_{C^{3}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}
sufficiently small and
c\in H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma})
with
\partial_{\mathbf{n}}c=0
on
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma}
. Then, it holds

\displaystyle\Big\|\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma}}c+\Delta c\Big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma})}\leq K\sqrt{\varepsilon}\|c\|_{H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma})}.

Beweis.

Let
\tilde{c}\in H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
denote an extension of
c
to the whole of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\|\tilde{c}\|_{H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq K\|c\|_{H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma})}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\Big\|\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma}}c+\Delta c\Big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma})}

\displaystyle\leq\Big\|\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\tilde{c}+\Delta\tilde{c}\Big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}\tilde{c}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma})}

\displaystyle\leq K\varepsilon\|c\|_{H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma})}+\|\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}\tilde{c}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma})},

where we used Lemma
3.3
and defined the error term

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}\tilde{c}(x):=\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma})^{c}}J_{\varepsilon}(|x-y|)\big(c(x)-\tilde{c}(y)\big)\;\text{d}y

(23)

for a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma}
. Thus, it suffices to consider the error term.

Since
\gamma\in C^{3}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})
, there exists a
C^{2,1}-
diffeomorphism
F_{\gamma}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfying
F_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})=\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma}
,
F_{\gamma}(x^{\prime},0)=(x^{\prime},\gamma(x^{\prime}))
and
-\partial_{x_{n}}F_{\gamma}(x)|_{x_{n}=0}=\mathbf{n}(x^{\prime},\gamma(x^{\prime}))
, where
\mathbf{n}
denotes the exterior unit normal on
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma}
, cf.
[
21
, Lemma 2.1]
.

In the following, we assume

\displaystyle\sup_{\hat{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\text{D}F_{\gamma}(\hat{x})-\text{Id}|\leq\alpha

(24)

for some
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{3})
. Using the diffeomorphism
F_{\gamma}
and reflection afterwards, we compute

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}\tilde{c}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{-}}J_{\varepsilon}(|F_{\gamma}(\hat{x})-F_{\gamma}(\hat{y})|)\big(c(F_{\gamma}(\hat{x}))-\tilde{c}(F_{\gamma}(\hat{y}))\big)\big|\det(\text{D}F_{\gamma}(\hat{y}))\big|\>\text{d}y

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}J_{\varepsilon}(|A_{\gamma}(\hat{x},\bar{y})(\hat{x}-\bar{y})|)\big(u(\hat{x})-u(\bar{y})\big)\big|\det(\text{D}F_{\gamma}(\bar{y}))\big|\>\text{d}y

for almost all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma}
. Here, we defined
u:=c\circ F_{\gamma}:\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,
F_{\gamma}(\hat{x})=x
,
\bar{y}=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n-1},-y_{n})
and we used

\displaystyle F_{\gamma}(\hat{x})-F_{\gamma}(\bar{y})=\int_{0}^{1}\text{D}F_{\gamma}(\bar{y}+t(\hat{x}-\bar{y}))(\hat{x}-\bar{y})\>\text{d}t=:A_{\gamma}(\hat{x},\bar{y})(\hat{x}-\bar{y}).

Next, we rewrite the error term
\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}c(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{\delta}(\hat{x}^{\prime})\times(0,\delta)}J_{\varepsilon}(|A_{\gamma}(\hat{x},\hat{x})(\hat{x}-\bar{y})|)\big(u(\hat{x})-u(\bar{y})\big)\big|\det\text{D}F_{\gamma}(\hat{x})\big|\>\text{d}y

\displaystyle+\int_{B_{\delta}(\hat{x}^{\prime})\times(0,\delta)}K_{\varepsilon}(|A_{\gamma}(\hat{x},\hat{x})(\hat{x}-\bar{y})|)\big(u(\hat{x})-u(\bar{y})\big)\>\text{d}y

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\setminus\big(B_{\delta}(\hat{x}^{\prime})\times(0,\delta)\big)}J_{\varepsilon}(|A_{\gamma}(\hat{x},\bar{y})(\hat{x}-\bar{y})|)\big(u(\hat{x})-u(\bar{y})\big)\big|\det(\text{D}F_{\gamma}(\bar{y}))\big|\>\text{d}y

\displaystyle=:I_{\varepsilon}^{1}(x)+I_{\varepsilon}^{2}(x)+I_{\varepsilon}^{3}(x),

(25)

where
K_{\varepsilon}(|A_{\gamma}(\hat{x},\hat{x})(\hat{x}-\bar{y})|)
is defined by

\displaystyle K_{\varepsilon}(|A_{\gamma}(\hat{x},\hat{x})(\hat{x}-\bar{y})|):=J_{\varepsilon}(|A_{\gamma}(\hat{x},\hat{y})(\hat{x}-\bar{y})|)\big|\det\text{D}F_{\gamma}(\bar{y})\big|-J_{\varepsilon}(|A_{\gamma}(\hat{x},\hat{x})(\hat{x}-\bar{y})|)\big|\det\text{D}F_{\gamma}(\hat{x})\big|

for almost all
\hat{x},\bar{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
.
In
I_{\varepsilon}^{3}(x)
, we can apply Lemma
3.2
, since it holds

\displaystyle|A_{\gamma}(\hat{x},\bar{y})(\hat{x}-\bar{y})|\geq K|\hat{x}-\bar{y}|\geq K\delta.

In the next step, we estimate the terms
I_{\varepsilon}^{1}(x)
and
I_{\varepsilon}^{2}(x)
separately. In the following, let
c\in C^{2,1}_{0}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma}})
with
\partial_{\mathbf{n}}c=0
on
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma}
.

Ad
I_{\varepsilon}^{1}(x)
: First, we use a Taylor expansion for
u
to get

\displaystyle I_{\varepsilon}^{1}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{\delta}(\hat{x}^{\prime})\times(0,\delta)}J_{\varepsilon}(|A_{\gamma}(\hat{x},\hat{x})(\hat{x}-\bar{y})|)\nabla_{x^{\prime}}u(\hat{x})\cdot(\hat{x}^{\prime}-\bar{y}^{\prime})\big|\det\text{D}F_{\gamma}(\hat{x})\big|\;\text{d}y

\displaystyle+\int_{B_{\delta}(\hat{x}^{\prime})\times(0,\delta)}J_{\varepsilon}(|A_{\gamma}(\hat{x},\hat{x})(\hat{x}-\bar{y})|)\partial_{x_{n}}u(\hat{x})(\hat{x}_{n}+\bar{y}_{n})\big|\det\text{D}F_{\gamma}(\hat{x})\big|\;\text{d}y

\displaystyle+\int_{B_{\delta}(\hat{x}^{\prime})\times(0,\delta)}J_{\varepsilon}(|A_{\gamma}(\hat{x},\hat{x})(\hat{x}-\bar{y})|)R_{2}(\hat{x},\bar{y})\big|\det\text{D}F_{\gamma}(\hat{x})\big|\;\text{d}y,

where the error term
R_{2}(\hat{x},\bar{y})
is defined by

\displaystyle R_{2}(\hat{x},\bar{y}):=\sum_{|\beta|=2}\frac{2}{\beta!}\left(\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)\text{D}^{\beta}c(\bar{y}+t(\hat{x}-\bar{y}))\text{d}t\right)(\hat{x}-\bar{y})^{\beta}.

Observe that by our choice of
F_{\gamma}
, the term
A_{\gamma}(\hat{x},\hat{x})
is given by

\displaystyle A_{\gamma}(\hat{x},\hat{x})=\text{D}F_{\gamma}(\hat{x})=U(\hat{x})\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc|c}&&&0\\
&A^{\prime}(\hat{x})&&\vdots\\
&&&0\\
\hline\cr 0&\ldots&0&1\end{array}\right),

where
U(\hat{x})\in\text{SO}(n)
and
A^{\prime}(\hat{x})\in\mathbb{R}^{(n-1)\times(n-1)}
, cf.
[
2
]
for details. This implies

\displaystyle|A_{\gamma}(\hat{x},\hat{x})(\hat{x}-\bar{y})|^{2}=|A^{\prime}(\hat{x})(\hat{x}^{\prime}-y^{\prime})|^{2}+|\hat{x}_{n}+y_{n}|^{2},

and therefore it follows that the integrand in the first term of
I_{\varepsilon}^{1}(x)
is odd with respect to
\hat{x}^{\prime}-\bar{y}^{\prime}
. Consequently,

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\delta}(\hat{x}^{\prime})\times(0,\delta)}J_{\varepsilon}(|A_{\gamma}(\hat{x},\hat{x})(\hat{x}-\bar{y})|)\nabla_{x^{\prime}}u(\hat{x})\cdot(\hat{x}^{\prime}-\bar{y}^{\prime})\big|\det\text{D}F_{\gamma}(\hat{x})\big|\;\text{d}y=0.

Furthermore,
F_{\gamma}
satisfies

\displaystyle\partial_{x_{n}}u(\hat{x})_{\big|\{x_{n}=0\}}=\partial_{x_{n}}\big(c\circ F_{\gamma}\big)(\hat{x})_{\big|\{x_{n}=0\}}=\nabla c(x^{\prime},\gamma(x^{\prime}))\cdot(-\mathbf{n}(x^{\prime},\gamma(x^{\prime})))=0,

since
\partial_{\mathbf{n}}c=0
on
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma}
. Thus, it holds

\displaystyle\partial_{x_{n}}u(\hat{x})=\partial_{x_{n}}u(\hat{x})-\partial_{x_{n}}u(\hat{x}^{\prime},0)=\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{x_{n}}^{2}u(\hat{x}^{\prime},tx_{n})\>\text{d}t\Big)x_{n}.

Hence, we obtain in the second term of
I_{\varepsilon}^{1}(x)

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{B_{\delta}(\hat{x}^{\prime})\times(0,\delta)}J_{\varepsilon}(|A_{\gamma}(\hat{x},\hat{x})(\hat{x}-\bar{y})|)\partial_{x_{n}}u(\hat{x})(\hat{x}_{n}+\bar{y}_{n})\big|\det\text{D}F_{\gamma}(\hat{x})\big|\;\text{d}y\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}a_{\varepsilon}(x_{n})|\partial_{x_{n}}^{2}u(\hat{x}^{\prime},t\hat{x}_{n})|\>\text{d}t,

where we used the inequalities
|A_{\gamma}(\hat{x},\bar{y})(\hat{x}-\bar{y})|\geq K|\hat{x}-\bar{y}|
for all
\hat{x},\bar{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\big|\det\text{D}F_{\gamma}(\hat{x})\big|\leq K
for all
\hat{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Here, we defined

\displaystyle a_{\varepsilon}(x_{n}):=\sup_{x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\int_{B_{\delta}(\hat{x}^{\prime})\times(0,\delta)}\rho_{\varepsilon}(|A_{\gamma}(\hat{x},\hat{x})(\hat{x}-\bar{y})|)\;\text{d}y.

Note that it holds
a_{\varepsilon}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
and
\|a_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}\leq K\sqrt{\varepsilon}
. This follows from the same calculation as in the proof of Lemma
3.3
.
Computing the
L^{2}
-norm, we then obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\Big|\int_{B_{\delta}(\hat{x}^{\prime})\times(0,\delta)}J_{\varepsilon}(|A_{\gamma}(\hat{x},\hat{x})(\hat{x}-\bar{y})|)\partial_{x_{n}}u(\hat{x})(\hat{x}_{n}+y_{n})\big|\det\text{D}F_{\gamma}(\hat{x})\big|\;\text{d}y\Big|^{2}\text{d}\hat{x}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|a_{\varepsilon}(\hat{x}_{n})|^{2}\Big(\frac{1}{\hat{x}_{n}}\int_{0}^{\hat{x}_{n}}|\partial_{\hat{x}_{n}}u(\hat{x}^{\prime},z_{n})|\text{d}z_{n}\Big)^{2}\text{d}\hat{x}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}|a_{\varepsilon}(\hat{x}_{n})|^{2}\|\partial_{\hat{x}_{n}}u(\hat{x}^{\prime},.)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}\text{d}\hat{x}_{n}\text{d}\hat{x}^{\prime}\leq K\varepsilon\|u\|_{H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}^{2}.

In the third term of
I_{\varepsilon}^{1}(x)
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\Big|\int_{B_{\delta}(\hat{x}^{\prime})\times(0,\delta)}J_{\varepsilon}(|A_{\gamma}(\hat{x},\hat{x})(\hat{x}-\bar{y})|)R_{2}(\hat{x},\bar{y})\big|\det\text{D}F_{\gamma}(\hat{x})\big|\;\text{d}y\Big|^{2}\>\text{d}\hat{x}

\displaystyle\leq K\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}a_{\varepsilon}(\hat{x}_{n})\left(\int_{0}^{1}\int_{B_{\delta}(\hat{x}^{\prime})\times(0,\delta)}\rho_{\varepsilon}(|A_{\gamma}(\hat{x},\hat{x})(\hat{x}-\bar{y})|)|D^{2}u(\bar{y}+t(\hat{x}-\bar{y}))|^{2}\>\text{d}y\text{d}t\right)\text{d}\hat{x}

\displaystyle\leq K\left(\int_{0}^{R}a_{\varepsilon}(\hat{x}_{n})\>\text{d}\hat{x}_{n}\right)\|u\|_{H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}^{2}\leq K\varepsilon\|u\|_{H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}^{2},

(26)

where we used the calculations as in (
20
) and Lemma
2.2
.
Altogether, we end up with

\displaystyle\big\|I^{1}_{\varepsilon}\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma})}\leq K\sqrt{\varepsilon}\|c\|_{H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma})},

where we used the same estimates as in the proof of Lemma
3.3
.

Ad
I_{\varepsilon}^{2}(x)
: Here, we first rewrite the term
K_{\varepsilon}(|A_{\gamma}(\hat{x},\hat{x})(\hat{x}-\hat{y})|)
in the following way:

\displaystyle K_{\varepsilon}(|A_{\gamma}(\hat{x},\hat{x})(\hat{x}-\bar{y})|)

\displaystyle=J_{\varepsilon}(|A_{\gamma}(\hat{x},\bar{y})(\hat{x}-\bar{y})|)\big(\big|\det\text{D}F_{\gamma}(\bar{y})\big|-\big|\det\text{D}F_{\gamma}(\hat{x})\big|\big)

\displaystyle+\Big(J_{\varepsilon}(|A_{\gamma}(\hat{x},\bar{y})(\hat{x}-\bar{y})|)-J_{\varepsilon}(|A_{\gamma}(\hat{x},\hat{x})(\hat{x}-\bar{y})|)\Big)\big|\det\text{D}F_{\gamma}(\hat{x})\big|.

Using this identity, we then get

\displaystyle|I_{\varepsilon}^{2}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\Big|\int_{B_{\delta}(\hat{x}^{\prime})\times(0,\delta)}J_{\varepsilon}(|A_{\gamma}(\hat{x},\bar{y})(\hat{x}-\bar{y})|)\big(\big|\det\text{D}F_{\gamma}(\bar{y})\big|-\big|\det\text{D}F_{\gamma}(\hat{x})\big|\big)\big(u(\hat{x})-u(\bar{y})\big)\;\text{d}y\Big|

\displaystyle+\Big|\int_{B_{\delta}(\hat{x}^{\prime})\times(0,\delta)}\Big(J_{\varepsilon}(|A_{\gamma}(\hat{x},\bar{y})(\hat{x}-\bar{y})|)-J_{\varepsilon}(|A_{\gamma}(\hat{x},\hat{x})(\hat{x}-\bar{y})|)\Big)\big|\det\text{D}F_{\gamma}(\hat{x})\big|\big(u(\hat{x})-u(\bar{y})\big)\;\text{d}y\Big|.

In the first term on the right-hand side, we use the continuity of

, the fundamental theorem of calculus as well as the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the calculations in
I^{1}_{\varepsilon}(x)
to estimate the
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
-norm

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\Big|\int_{B_{\delta}(\hat{x}^{\prime})\times(0,\delta)}J_{\varepsilon}(|A_{\gamma}(\hat{x},\bar{y})(\hat{x}-\bar{y})|)\big(\big|\det\text{D}F_{\gamma}(\bar{y})\big|-\big|\det\text{D}F_{\gamma}(\hat{x})\big|\big)\big(u(\hat{x})-u(\bar{y})\big)\;\text{d}y\Big|^{2}\text{d}\hat{x}

where we used the same arguments as in (
26
) in the last step.
In the second term on the right-hand side, we use the mean value theorem to conclude

\displaystyle\big|J_{\varepsilon}(|A_{\gamma}(\hat{x},\bar{y})(\hat{x}-\bar{y})|)-J_{\varepsilon}(|A_{\gamma}(\hat{x},\hat{x})(\hat{x}-\bar{y})|)\big|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{0}^{1}\nabla J_{\varepsilon}(|z|)_{\big|z=z_{t}}\cdot\big(A_{\gamma}(\hat{x},\bar{y})-A_{\gamma}(\hat{x},\hat{x})\big)(\hat{x}-\bar{y})\>\text{d}t\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\frac{\rho_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(|z|)}{|z|^{2}}_{\big|z=z_{t}}\>K|\hat{x}-\bar{y}|^{2}\>\text{d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\rho_{\varepsilon}(|z|)}{|z|^{3}}_{\big|z=z_{t}}\>K|\hat{x}-\bar{y}|^{2}\>\text{d}t,

(27)

where we defined

\displaystyle z_{t}

\displaystyle:=M_{t}(\hat{x},\bar{y})(\hat{x}-\bar{y}),

\displaystyle M_{t}(\hat{x},\bar{y})

\displaystyle:=A_{\gamma}(\hat{x},\hat{x})+t\big(A_{\gamma}(\hat{x},\bar{y})-A_{\gamma}(\hat{x},\hat{x})\big)

for all
\hat{x},\bar{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and all
t\in[0,1]
. Thanks to assumption (
24
), it follows that
|M_{t}(\hat{x},\bar{y})-\text{Id}|\leq 3\alpha
for all
\hat{x},\bar{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and all
t\in[0,1]
. Therefore,
M_{t}(\hat{x},\bar{y})^{-1}
exists for all
\hat{x},\bar{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and all
t\in[0,1]
and satisfies the inequality

\displaystyle|M_{t}(\hat{x},\bar{y})^{-1}|\leq\frac{1}{1-|M_{t}(\hat{x},\bar{y})-\text{Id}|}<\frac{1}{1-3\alpha}

for all
\hat{x},\bar{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and all
t\in[0,1]
. Using (
27
), we then obtain for the second term of
I_{\varepsilon}^{2}(x)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\Big|\int_{B_{\delta}(\hat{x}^{\prime})\times(0,\delta)}\Big(J_{\varepsilon}(|A_{\gamma}(\hat{x},\bar{y})(\hat{x}-\bar{y})|)-J_{\varepsilon}(|A_{\gamma}(\hat{x},\hat{x})(\hat{x}-\bar{y})|)\Big)\big|\det\text{D}F_{\gamma}(\hat{x})\big|\big(u(\hat{x})-u(\bar{y})\big)\;\text{d}y\Big|^{2}\>\text{d}\hat{x}

\displaystyle\leq K\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\int_{B_{\delta}(\hat{x}^{\prime})\times(0,\delta)}\int_{0}^{1}\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\frac{\rho_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(|z_{t}|)}{|z_{t}|^{2}}+\frac{\rho_{\varepsilon}(|z_{t}|)}{|z_{t}|^{3}}\right)\big|Du(\bar{y}+s(\hat{x}-\bar{y}))\big||\hat{x}-\bar{y}|^{3}\>\text{d}s\text{d}y\text{d}t\right)^{2}\text{d}\hat{x}

\displaystyle\leq K\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\int_{B_{\delta}(\hat{x}^{\prime})\times(0,\delta)}\int_{0}^{1}\Big(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\rho_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(|z_{t}|)|\hat{x}-\bar{y}|+\rho_{\varepsilon}(|z_{t}|)\Big)\big|Du(\bar{y}+s(\hat{x}-\bar{y}))\big|\>\text{d}s\text{d}y\text{d}t\right)^{2}\text{d}\hat{x}

\displaystyle\leq K\varepsilon\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\Big(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\rho_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(|z_{t}|)|\hat{x}-\bar{y}|+\rho_{\varepsilon}(|z_{t}|)\Big)\big|Du(\bar{y}+s(\hat{x}-\bar{y}))\big|^{2}\>\text{d}y\text{d}\hat{x}\text{d}s\text{d}t\leq K\varepsilon\|u\|_{H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}^{2},

where we used the same arguments as above.
Then, we conclude

\displaystyle\big\|I^{2}_{\varepsilon}\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma})}\leq K\sqrt{\varepsilon}\|c\|_{H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma})}.

In particular, this implies

\displaystyle\Big\|\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma}}c+\Delta c\Big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma})}\leq K\sqrt{\varepsilon}\|c\|_{H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma})}

for all
c\in C^{2,1}_{0}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma}})
with
\partial_{\mathbf{n}}c=0
on
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma}
. Finally, a density argument, cf. Lemma
3.3
, yields

\displaystyle\Big\|\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma}}c+\Delta c\Big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma})}\leq K\sqrt{\varepsilon}\|c\|_{H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma})}

for all
c\in H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma})
with
\partial_{\mathbf{n}}c=0
on
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma}
. This concludes the proof.
∎

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
c\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma})
with
\partial_{\mathbf{n}}c=0
on
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma}
. Then, it holds

\displaystyle\Big\|\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma}}c+\Delta c\Big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma})}\rightarrow 0\;\;\;\text{as }\varepsilon\searrow 0.

Beweis.

Due to Lemma
3.6
, it suffices to show that
\big\|\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma}}c+\Delta c\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma})}
is bounded uniformly in
\varepsilon>0
for all
c\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma})
with
\partial_{\mathbf{n}}c=0
on
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma}
. Then, we can apply the Banach Steinhaus Theorem, which implies the assertion.

In fact, it suffices to bound the error term (
23
) in a suitable way. Using the same methods as in the proof of Lemma
3.6
, we can rewrite
\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}
as in (
25
). First of all, we prove the assertion for functions
c\in C^{1,1}_{0}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma}})
with
\partial_{\mathbf{n}}c=0
on
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma}
and use a density argument afterwards to conclude the proof. Observe that it suffices to integrate in
\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}
only over
B_{\delta}(\hat{x}^{\prime})\times(0,\delta)
, since otherwise we can employ Lemma
3.2
, cf. proof of Lemma
3.6
.

Ad
I^{1}_{\varepsilon}(x)
: Using a Taylor expansion, we obtain

\displaystyle I_{\varepsilon}^{1}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{\delta}(\hat{x}^{\prime})\times(0,\delta)}J_{\varepsilon}(|A_{\gamma}(\hat{x},\hat{x})(\hat{x}-\bar{y})|)\nabla_{x^{\prime}}u(\hat{x})\cdot(\hat{x}^{\prime}-\bar{y}^{\prime})\big|\det\text{D}F_{\gamma}(\hat{x})\big|\;\text{d}y

\displaystyle+\int_{B_{\delta}(\hat{x}^{\prime})\times(0,\delta)}J_{\varepsilon}(|A_{\gamma}(\hat{x},\hat{x})(\hat{x}-\bar{y})|)\partial_{x_{n}}u(\hat{x})(\hat{x}_{n}+\bar{y}_{n})\big|\det\text{D}F_{\gamma}(\hat{x})\big|\;\text{d}y

\displaystyle+\int_{B_{\delta}(\hat{x}^{\prime})\times(0,\delta)}J_{\varepsilon}(|A_{\gamma}(\hat{x},\hat{x})(\hat{x}-\bar{y})|)R_{2}(\hat{x},\bar{y})\big|\det\text{D}F_{\gamma}(\hat{x})\big|\;\text{d}y.

As in the proof before, the first term on the right-hand side vanishes, since the integrand is odd with respect to
\hat{x}^{\prime}-\bar{y}^{\prime}
. In the second term, the properties of
F_{\gamma}
imply

\displaystyle\partial_{x_{n}}u(\hat{x})_{\big|\{x_{n}=0\}}=\partial_{x_{n}}\big(c\circ F_{\gamma}\big)(\hat{x})_{\big|\{x_{n}=0\}}=\nabla c(x^{\prime},\gamma(x^{\prime}))\cdot(-\mathbf{n}(x^{\prime},\gamma(x^{\prime})))=0,

since
\partial_{\mathbf{n}}c=0
on
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma}
, and therefore

\displaystyle\partial_{x_{n}}u(\hat{x})=\partial_{x_{n}}u(\hat{x})-\partial_{x_{n}}u(\hat{x}^{\prime},0)=\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{x_{n}}^{2}u(\hat{x}^{\prime},tx_{n})\>\text{d}t\Big)x_{n}.

This yields

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{B_{\delta}(\hat{x}^{\prime})\times(0,\delta)}J_{\varepsilon}(|A_{\gamma}(\hat{x},\hat{x})(\hat{x}-\bar{y})|)\partial_{x_{n}}u(\hat{x})(\hat{x}_{n}+\bar{y}_{n})\big|\det\text{D}F_{\gamma}(\hat{x})\big|\;\text{d}y\Big|

\displaystyle\leq K\int_{0}^{1}|\partial_{x_{n}}^{2}u(\hat{x}^{\prime},t\hat{x}_{n})|\Big(\int_{B_{\delta}(\hat{x}^{\prime})\times(0,\delta)}\rho_{\varepsilon}(|A_{\gamma}(\hat{x},\hat{x})(\hat{x}-\bar{y})|)\;\text{d}y\Big)\>\text{d}t,

\displaystyle\leq K\Big(\frac{1}{\hat{x}_{n}}\int_{0}^{\hat{x}_{n}}|\partial_{x_{n}}^{2}u(\hat{x}^{\prime},z_{n})|\;\text{d}z_{n}\Big)\Big(\int_{B_{\delta}(\hat{x}^{\prime})\times(0,\delta)}\rho_{\varepsilon}(|A_{\gamma}(\hat{x},\hat{x})(\hat{x}-\bar{y})|)\;\text{d}y\Big)

where we used the inequalities
|A_{\gamma}(\hat{x},\bar{y})(\hat{x}-\bar{y})|\geq C|\hat{x}-\bar{y}|
for all
\hat{x},\bar{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\big|\det\text{D}F_{\gamma}(\hat{x})\big|\leq C
for all
\hat{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Computing the
L^{2}
-norm, we then get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\Big|\int_{B_{\delta}(\hat{x}^{\prime})\times(0,\delta)}J_{\varepsilon}(|A_{\gamma}(\hat{x},\hat{x})(\hat{x}-\bar{y})|)\partial_{x_{n}}u(\hat{x})(\hat{x}_{n}+\bar{y}_{n})\big|\det\text{D}F_{\gamma}(\hat{x})\big|\;\text{d}y\Big|^{2}\text{d}\hat{x}\leq K\|u\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})},

where we used the same ideas as in Corollary
3.5
, cf. (
21
).
In the third term, it holds

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\delta}(\hat{x}^{\prime})\times(0,\delta)}J_{\varepsilon}(|A_{\gamma}(\hat{x},\hat{x})(\hat{x}-\bar{y})|)R_{2}(\hat{x},\bar{y})\big|\det\text{D}F_{\gamma}(\hat{x})\big|\;\text{d}y

\displaystyle\leq K\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\int_{B_{\delta}(\hat{x}^{\prime})\times(0,\delta)}\int_{0}^{1}\rho_{\varepsilon}(|A_{\gamma}(\hat{x},\hat{x})(\hat{x}-\bar{y})|)|D^{2}u(\bar{y}+t(\hat{x}-\bar{y}))|^{2}\>\text{d}t\text{d}y\text{d}\hat{x}

\displaystyle\leq K\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\|\rho_{\varepsilon}(|A_{\gamma}((\hat{x}^{\prime},.),(\hat{x}^{\prime},.))(\hat{x}^{\prime}-\bar{y}^{\prime},.)|)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\|D^{2}u(\bar{y}^{\prime}+t(\hat{x}^{\prime}-\bar{y}^{\prime}),.)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}\>\text{d}\hat{x}^{\prime}\text{d}y^{\prime}\text{d}t

\displaystyle\leq K\|u\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma})}^{2}.

Here, we used similar arguments as before, cf. (
22
) and (
26
).
Altogether, we have

\displaystyle\|I^{1}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma})}\leq K\|c\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma})}.

Ad
I^{2}_{\varepsilon}(x)
: Here, we have

\displaystyle|I_{\varepsilon}^{2}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\Big|\int_{B_{\delta}(\hat{x}^{\prime})\times(0,\delta)}J_{\varepsilon}(|A_{\gamma}(\hat{x},\bar{y})(\hat{x}-\bar{y})|)\big(\big|\det\text{D}F_{\gamma}(\bar{y})\big|-\big|\det\text{D}F_{\gamma}(\hat{x})\big|\big)\big(u(\hat{x})-u(\bar{y})\big)\;\text{d}y\Big|

\displaystyle+\Big|\int_{B_{\delta}(\hat{x}^{\prime})\times(0,\delta)}\Big(J_{\varepsilon}(|A_{\gamma}(\hat{x},\bar{y})(\hat{x}-\bar{y})|)-J_{\varepsilon}(|A_{\gamma}(\hat{x},\hat{x})(\hat{x}-\bar{y})|)\Big)\big|\det\text{D}F_{\gamma}(\hat{x})\big|\big(u(\hat{x})-u(\bar{y})\big)\;\text{d}y\Big|,

where we used the same identity as in the proof of Lemma
3.6
. In the first term on the right-hand side, we observe that

\displaystyle\Big|\big(\big|\det\text{D}F_{\gamma}(\bar{y})\big|-\big|\det\text{D}F_{\gamma}(\hat{x})\big|\big)\big(u(\hat{x})-u(\bar{y})\big)\Big|\leq K\Big(\int_{0}^{1}|Du(\bar{y}+t(\hat{x}-\bar{y}))|\text{d}t\Big)|\hat{x}-\bar{y}|^{2},

which yields

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\Big|\int_{B_{\delta}(\hat{x}^{\prime})\times(0,\delta)}J_{\varepsilon}(|A_{\gamma}(\hat{x},\bar{y})(\hat{x}-\bar{y})|)\big(\big|\det\text{D}F_{\gamma}(\bar{y})\big|-\big|\det\text{D}F_{\gamma}(\hat{x})\big|\big)\big(u(\hat{x})-u(\bar{y})\big)\;\text{d}y\Big|^{2}\text{d}\hat{x}

\displaystyle\leq K\|u\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}^{2}.

Here, we concluded with the same arguments as in the proof before.
In the second term, we again use the mean value theorem, cf. Lemma
3.6
, to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\Big|\int_{B_{\delta}(\hat{x}^{\prime})\times(0,\delta)}\Big(J_{\varepsilon}(|A_{\gamma}(\hat{x},\bar{y})(\hat{x}-\bar{y})|)-J_{\varepsilon}(|A_{\gamma}(\hat{x},\hat{x})(\hat{x}-\bar{y})|)\Big)\big|\det\text{D}F_{\gamma}(\hat{x})\big|\big(u(\hat{x})-u(\bar{y})\big)\;\text{d}y\Big|^{2}\text{d}\hat{x}

\displaystyle\leq K\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\int_{B_{\delta}(\hat{x}^{\prime})\times(0,\delta)}\int_{0}^{1}\Big(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\rho_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(|z_{t}|)|\hat{x}-\bar{y}|+\rho_{\varepsilon}(|z_{t}|)\Big)\big|Du(\bar{y}+s(\hat{x}-\bar{y}))\big|\>\text{d}s\text{d}y\text{d}t\right)^{2}\text{d}\hat{x}

\displaystyle\leq K\|u\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}^{2},

where we defined

\displaystyle z_{t}

\displaystyle:=M_{t}(\hat{x},\bar{y})(\hat{x}-\bar{y}),

\displaystyle M_{t}(\hat{x},\bar{y})

\displaystyle:=A_{\gamma}(\hat{x},\hat{x})+t\big(A_{\gamma}(\hat{x},\bar{y})-A_{\gamma}(\hat{x},\hat{x})\big)

for all
\hat{x},\bar{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and all
t\in[0,1]
. In fact, this estimate follows by the same methods as in the proof of Lemma
3.6
. Altogether, it follows that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}\tilde{c}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma})}\leq K\|c\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma})}

for all
c\in C^{1,1}_{0}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma}})
with
\partial_{\mathbf{n}}c=0
on
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma}
, and therefore

\displaystyle\Big\|\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma}}c+\Delta c\Big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma})}\leq K\|c\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma})}

for all
c\in C^{1,1}_{0}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma}})
with
\partial_{\mathbf{n}}c=0
on
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma}
. Finally, a denseness argument, cf. Lemma
3.6
, yields that this estimate also holds true for all
c\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma})
with
\partial_{\mathbf{n}}c=0
on
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma}
. In the last step, we employ the Banach Steinhaus Theorem. Then, the claim follows.
∎

4
Main Result

In this section, we want to state and prove the main result on the strong convergence of the nonlocal operator
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}
on bounded smooth domains
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n=2,3
, be a bounded domain with
C^{2}
-boundary. Then, for every
c\in H^{2}(\Omega)
with
\partial_{\mathbf{n}}c=0
on
\partial\Omega
, it holds

\displaystyle\Big\|\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}^{\Omega}c+\Delta c\Big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\rightarrow 0\;\;\;\text{as }\varepsilon\searrow 0.

(28)

Beweis.

Since
\partial\Omega
is compact, there exist open sets
U_{1},\ldots,U_{N}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{N}\in C^{2}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})
such that, up to a rotation,
\Omega\cap U_{j}=\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma_{j}}\cap U_{j}
for all
j=1,\ldots,N
and
\partial\Omega\subseteq\bigcup_{j=1}^{N}U_{j}
. Since also
\Omega\setminus\big(\bigcup_{j=1}^{N}U_{j}\big)
is compact, there exists an open, bounded set
U_{0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\Omega\setminus\big(\bigcup_{j=1}^{N}U_{j}\big)\subset U_{0}
and
\overline{U_{0}}\subset\Omega
. Then, we choose some
\gamma_{0}\in C^{2}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})
such that
\overline{U_{0}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma_{0}}
. Altogether, we have

\displaystyle\overline{\Omega}\subseteq\bigcup_{j=0}^{N}U_{j}\;\;\;\text{and}\;\;\;\Omega\cap U_{j}=\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma_{j}}\cap U_{j}\;\;\;\text{for all }j=0,\ldots,N.

Next, we choose a partition of unity
\varphi_{j}
,
j=0,\ldots,N
, on
\overline{\Omega}
. Without loss of generality, we can assume that
\partial_{\mathbf{n}_{j}}{\varphi_{j}}=0
on
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma_{j}}
for all
j=0,\ldots,N
, where
\mathbf{n}_{j}
denotes the outer unit normal to
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma_{j}}
. Otherwise,
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma_{j}}
admits a tubular neighborhood
U_{a_{j}}
of width
a_{j}>0
, cf.
[
20
, Section 2.3]
. Then, the restriction of
\varphi_{j}
to
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma_{j}}
can be extended to a function
\hat{\varphi_{j}}
on
U_{a_{j}}
, which is constant in the direction of
\mathbf{n}_{j}
by setting
\hat{\varphi_{j}}(x):=\varphi_{j}(\pi_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma_{j}}}(x))
for
x\in U_{a_{j}}
, where
\pi_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma_{j}}}:U_{a_{j}}\rightarrow\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma_{j}}
denotes the projection on
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma_{j}}
.

Finally, we choose functions
\psi_{j}\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
\text{supp }\psi_{j}\subseteq U_{j}
,
\psi_{j}\geq 0
and
\psi_{j}\equiv 1
in
\text{supp }\varphi_{j}
for all
j=0,\ldots,N
.

Now, let
c\in H^{2}(\Omega)
with
\partial_{\mathbf{n}}c=0
on
\partial\Omega
be arbitrary. Using the identity
c=\sum_{j=0}^{N}\varphi_{j}c
, we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{\Omega}_{\varepsilon}c+\Delta c

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}^{\Omega}_{\varepsilon}\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{N}\varphi_{j}c\Big)+\Delta\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{N}\varphi_{j}c\Big)=\sum_{j=0}^{N}\Big(\mathcal{L}^{\Omega}_{\varepsilon}\big(\varphi_{j}c\big)+\Delta\big(\varphi_{j}c\big)\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{N}\Big(\psi_{j}\mathcal{L}^{\Omega}_{\varepsilon}\big(\varphi_{j}c\big)+(1-\psi_{j})\mathcal{L}^{\Omega}_{\varepsilon}\big(\varphi_{j}c\big)+\Delta\big(\varphi_{j}c\big)\Big)

\displaystyle=:\sum_{j=0}^{N}\Big(I_{\varepsilon,j}^{1}+I_{\varepsilon,j}^{2}+\Delta\big(\varphi_{j}c\big)\Big).

In the next step, we analyze these terms separately.

Ad
I_{\varepsilon,j}^{1}
: For almost all
x\in\Omega
and all
j=0,\ldots,N
it holds:

\displaystyle\psi_{j}(x)\mathcal{L}^{\Omega}_{\varepsilon}\big(\varphi_{j}c\big)(x)

\displaystyle=\psi_{j}(x)\int_{\Omega}J_{\varepsilon}(|x-y|)\big(\varphi_{j}(x)c(x)-\varphi_{j}(y)c(y)\big)\;\text{d}y

\displaystyle=\psi_{j}(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma_{j}}}J_{\varepsilon}(|x-y|)\big(\varphi_{j}(x)c(x)-\varphi_{j}(y)c(y)\big)\;\text{d}y

\displaystyle\;\;\;+\psi_{j}(x)\int_{\Omega\setminus U_{j}}J_{\varepsilon}(|x-y|)\varphi_{j}(x)c(x)\;\text{d}y-\psi_{j}(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma_{j}}\setminus U_{j}}J_{\varepsilon}(|x-y|)\varphi_{j}(x)c(x)\;\text{d}y

\displaystyle=:\hat{I}_{\varepsilon,j}^{1}+\hat{I}_{\varepsilon,j}^{2}-\hat{I}_{\varepsilon,j}^{3}.

In
\hat{I}_{\varepsilon,j}^{2}
, we observe that
\delta_{2,j}:=\text{dist}(\text{supp }\psi_{j},\Omega\setminus U_{j})>0
and therefore
|x-y|\geq\delta_{2,j}
. Hence,

\|\hat{I}_{\varepsilon,j}^{2}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\rightarrow 0
as
\varepsilon\searrow 0
for all
j=0,\ldots,N
by Lemma
3.2
.

In
\hat{I}_{\varepsilon,j}^{3}
, it holds
\delta_{3,j}:=\text{dist}(\text{supp }\psi_{j},\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma_{j}}\setminus U_{j})>0
. This implies
\|\hat{I}_{\varepsilon,j}^{3}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\rightarrow 0
as
\varepsilon\searrow 0
for all
j=0,\ldots,N
again by Lemma
3.2
.

In the next step, we consider
\hat{I}_{\varepsilon,j}^{1}+\Delta\big(\varphi_{j}c\big)
. Since
\text{supp }\psi_{j}\subseteq U_{j}
, we have

\displaystyle\hat{I}_{\varepsilon,j}^{1}+\Delta\big(\varphi_{j}c\big)=\psi_{j}\mathcal{L}^{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma_{j}}}_{\varepsilon}\big(\varphi_{j}c\big)+\Delta\big(\varphi_{j}c\big)=\psi_{j}\Big(\mathcal{L}^{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma_{j}}}_{\varepsilon}\big(\varphi_{j}c\big)+\Delta\big(\varphi_{j}c\big)\Big).

(29)

Due to the properties of
\varphi_{j}
, it holds
\varphi_{j}c\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma_{j}})
and
\partial_{\mathbf{n}}(\varphi_{j}c)=0
on
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\gamma_{j}}
. Therefore, (
29
) vanishes as
\varepsilon\searrow 0
by Corollary
3.7
.

Ad
I_{\varepsilon,j}^{2}
: Due to our choice of the functions
\psi_{j}
, it holds
\text{supp}(1-\psi_{j})\cap\text{supp }\varphi_{j}=\emptyset
. Thus, we have
\delta_{j}:=\text{dist}(\text{supp}(1-\psi_{j}),\text{supp }\varphi_{j})>0
. Therefore,
\|I_{\varepsilon,j}^{2}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\rightarrow 0
as
\varepsilon\searrow 0
for all
j=0,\ldots,N
by Lemma
3.2
. This concludes the proof.
∎

Corollary 4.2
.

In addition, if
c\in H^{3}(\Omega)
and
\Omega
is of class
C^{3}
, it even holds

\displaystyle\Big\|\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}^{\Omega}c+\Delta c\Big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq K\sqrt{\varepsilon}\|c\|_{H^{3}(\Omega)}.

Beweis.

This can be proven analogously as Theorem
4.1
using Lemma
3.6
instead of Corollary
3.7
.

5
Nonlocal-to-Local Convergence of the Cahn-Hilliard Equation

Theorem 5.1
.

Let the initial data
c_{0,\varepsilon}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
satisfy
c_{0,\varepsilon}\rightarrow c_{0}
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
at rate
\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{\varepsilon})
as
\varepsilon\searrow 0
for
c_{0}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
.
Let the weak solution

c\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(\Omega))
with
f^{\prime}(c)\in L^{2}(\Omega_{T})

of the local Cahn-Hilliard equation satisfy

c\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{3}(\Omega))
.
Then, the weak solution
c_{\varepsilon}
of the nonlocal Cahn-Hilliard equation (
7
)-(
8
) converges strongly to the strong solution of the local Cahn-Hilliard equation (
1
)-(
2
) in
L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{-1}(\Omega))\cap L^{2}(\Omega_{T})
at rate
\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{\varepsilon})
as
\varepsilon\searrow 0
.

Beweis.

Let us define the functions
u:=c_{\varepsilon}-c
and
w:=\mu_{\varepsilon}-\mu
. Then,
(u,w)
solves the system

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u

\displaystyle=\Delta w,

(30)

\displaystyle w

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}^{\Omega}c_{\varepsilon}+\Delta c+f^{\prime}(c_{\varepsilon})-f^{\prime}(c).

(31)

In the next step, we test (
30
) by
(-\Delta_{N})^{-1}u
.
Here,
v:=(-\Delta_{N})^{-1}u\in H^{1}(\Omega)\cap L^{2}_{(0)}(\Omega)
denotes the unique solution of

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla v\cdot\nabla\varphi\>\text{d}x=\int_{\Omega}u\varphi\>\text{d}x

for all
\varphi\in H^{1}(\Omega)\cap L^{2}_{(0)}(\Omega)
.
This yields

\displaystyle\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}t}\frac{1}{2}\|u\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}^{2}=\int_{\Omega}\Delta w(-\Delta_{N})^{-1}u\>\text{d}x=-\int_{\Omega}wu\>\text{d}x.

(32)

Here, we also used the following calculation

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}u(-\Delta_{N})^{-1}u\>\text{d}x=\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}t}\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}\big|(-\Delta_{N})^{-1/2}u\big|^{2}\>\text{d}x=\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}t}\frac{1}{2}\|u\|^{2}_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}.

Furthermore, we multiply (
31
) by
u
and integrate over
\Omega
. Hence,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}wu\>\text{d}x=\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{L}^{\Omega}_{\varepsilon}c_{\varepsilon}u\>\text{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\Delta cu\>\text{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\big(f^{\prime}(c_{\varepsilon})-f^{\prime}(c)\big)u\>\text{d}x.

Using the assumptions on
f
, we can estimate the last integral on the right-hand side from below by

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\big(f^{\prime}(c_{\varepsilon})-f^{\prime}(c)\big)u\>\text{d}x\geq-\int_{\Omega}\alpha(c_{\varepsilon}-c)u\>\text{d}x=-\int_{\Omega}\alpha|u|^{2}\>\text{d}x.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}wu\>\text{d}x

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{L}^{\Omega}_{\varepsilon}uu\>\text{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\big(\mathcal{L}^{\Omega}_{\varepsilon}c+\Delta c\big)u\>\text{d}x-\alpha\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2}\>\text{d}x

\displaystyle=\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}(u)+\int_{\Omega}\big(\mathcal{L}^{\Omega}_{\varepsilon}c+\Delta c\big)u\>\text{d}x-\alpha\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2}\>\text{d}x,

(33)

where we used

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{L}^{\Omega}_{\varepsilon}uu\>\text{d}x=\frac{1}{4}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}J_{\varepsilon}(x-y)\big|u(x)-u(y)\big|^{2}\>\text{d}x\>\text{d}y=\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}(u)

(34)

in the last step. Now, combining (
32
) and (
33
) yields

\displaystyle\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}t}\frac{1}{2}\|u\|^{2}_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}+\frac{1}{2}\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}(u)

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\alpha+\frac{1}{2}\Big)\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}-\int_{\Omega}\big(\mathcal{L}^{\Omega}_{\varepsilon}c+\Delta c\big)u\>\text{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\alpha+1\Big)\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\frac{1}{2}\Big\|\mathcal{L}^{\Omega}_{\varepsilon}c+\Delta c\Big\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Employing inequality (
12
) from Lemma
2.1
with
\delta=\frac{1}{2(\alpha+1)}
, we then obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}t}\frac{1}{2}\|u\|^{2}_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}+\frac{1}{2}\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}(u)\leq C\|u\|^{2}_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}+C\Big\|\mathcal{L}^{\Omega}_{\varepsilon}c+\Delta c\Big\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

(35)

Finally, Gronwall‘s inequality yields

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}(u)(\tau)\>\text{d}\tau\leq K\sqrt{\varepsilon}\Big(1+\int_{0}^{t}\|c(\tau)\|_{H^{3}(\Omega)}^{2}\>\text{d}\tau\Big)\exp\Big(\int_{0}^{t}K\>\text{d}\tau\Big)

for almost all
t\in(0,T)
, where we used Corollary
4.2
and the assumptions on the initial data on the right-hand side. Using (
13
), we then conclude the proof.
∎

Corollary 5.2
.

Under the assumptions of Corollary
5.1
, it even holds

\displaystyle c_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow c\;\;\;\text{in }L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{s}(\Omega))\;\;\;\text{ as }\varepsilon\searrow 0

for all
s\in(-1,0)
and furthermore,

\displaystyle c_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow c\;\;\;\text{in }L^{2}(0,T;L^{p}(\Omega))\;\;\;\text{ as }\varepsilon\searrow 0

for all
p\in[2,6)
.

Beweis.

In Theorem
5.1
, we have already shown that

\displaystyle c_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow c\;\;\;\text{in }L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{-1}(\Omega))

as
\varepsilon\searrow 0
. Since the sequence
(c_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}\subset L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
is bounded, cf.
[
8
, Theorem 2.3]
, we get

\displaystyle\|c_{\varepsilon}-c\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{s}(\Omega))}\leq K\|c_{\varepsilon}-c\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}^{\theta}\;\|c_{\varepsilon}-c\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{-1}(\Omega))}^{1-\theta}

for
s=\theta-1
,
\theta\in(0,1)
and thus the first assertion follows.

In the second assertion, we use that
(c_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}\subset L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))\hookrightarrow L^{2}(0,T;L^{6}(\Omega))
is bounded, cf.
[
8
, Theorem 2.2]
. Then, an interpolation yields

\displaystyle\|c_{\varepsilon}-c\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{p}(\Omega))}\leq K\|c_{\varepsilon}-c\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}^{\theta}\;\|c_{\varepsilon}-c\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{6}(\Omega))}^{1-\theta}

for all
p=\frac{6}{1+2\theta}
, where
\theta\in(0,1]
. This finishes the proof.
∎

Remark 5.3
.

Lastly, we want to mention that our methods from Section
5
can also be used to prove nonlocal-to-local convergence for the Allen-Cahn equation. The Allen-Cahn equation is given by

\displaystyle\partial_{t}c=\Delta c-f^{\prime}(c)\;\;\;\text{in }\Omega_{T}

(36)

together with boundary and initial conditions

\displaystyle\partial_{\mathbf{n}}c

\displaystyle=0\;\;\;\;\text{on }\partial\Omega\times(0,T),

(37)

\displaystyle c|_{t=0}

\displaystyle=c_{0}\;\;\;\text{in }\Omega.

(38)

The nonlocal version is given by

\displaystyle\partial_{t}c=-\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}c-f^{\prime}(c)\;\;\;\text{in }\Omega_{T}

(39)

with initial condition

\displaystyle c|_{t=0}

\displaystyle=c_{0}\;\;\;\text{in }\Omega.

(40)

Observe that we do not need to impose any boundary condition for the nonlocal Allen-Cahn equation. Here, we use the same notation and the same prerequisites as before, cf. Section
1
. Then, we can prove the following assertion.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let the initial data
c_{0,\varepsilon}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
satisfy
c_{0,\varepsilon}\rightarrow c_{0}
at rate
\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{\varepsilon})
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
as
\varepsilon\searrow 0
for
c_{0}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
.
Let the weak solution

c\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(\Omega))
with
f^{\prime}(c)\in L^{2}(\Omega_{T})

of the local Allen-Cahn equation satisfy
c\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{3}(\Omega))
.
Then, the weak solution
c_{\varepsilon}
of the nonlocal Allen-Cahn equation (
39
)-(
40
) converges strongly to the strong solution of the local Allen-Cahn equation (
36
)-(
38
) in
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
at rate
\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{\varepsilon})
as
\varepsilon\searrow 0
.

Beweis.

First of all, we define
u:=c_{\varepsilon}-c
. Then,
u
is a solution of

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u=-\mathcal{L}^{\Omega}_{\varepsilon}c_{\varepsilon}-f^{\prime}(c_{\varepsilon})-\Delta c+f^{\prime}(c).

(41)

Testing (
41
) with
u
, yields

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}uu\;\text{d}x

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}\big(\mathcal{L}^{\Omega}_{\varepsilon}c_{\varepsilon}+\Delta c\big)u\;\text{d}x-\int_{\Omega}\big(f^{\prime}(c_{\varepsilon})-f^{\prime}(c)\big)u\;\text{d}x

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}\big(\mathcal{L}^{\Omega}_{\varepsilon}c+\Delta c\big)u\;\text{d}x-\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{L}^{\Omega}_{\varepsilon}uu\;\text{d}x-\int_{\Omega}\big(f^{\prime}(c_{\varepsilon})-f^{\prime}(c)\big)u\;\text{d}x,

where the left-hand side also satisfies

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}uu\;\text{d}x=\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}t}\frac{1}{2}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Employing the properties of
f
and (
34
), we then end up with

\displaystyle\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}t}\frac{1}{2}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}(u)\leq\Big(\alpha+\frac{1}{2}\Big)\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\frac{1}{2}\Big\|\mathcal{L}^{\Omega}_{\varepsilon}c+\Delta c\Big\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

In the last step, we use inequality (
12
) from Lemma
2.1
with
\delta=\frac{1}{2(\alpha+\frac{1}{2})}
, Gronwall‘s inequality as well as Theorem
4.1
and (
13
). Then, the claim follows.
∎

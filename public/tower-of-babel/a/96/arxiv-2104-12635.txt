Stochastic behavior of outcome of Schur-Weyl duality measurement
Masahito Hayashi, Akihito Hora, Shintarou Yanagida

Abstract
We focus on the measurement defined by the decomposition based on Schur-Weyl duality on n n qubits. As the first setting, we discuss the asymptotic behavior of the measurement outcome when the state is given as the permutation mixture ρ m ​ i ​ x , n , l \rho_{mix,n,l} of the state | 1 l ​ 0 n − l ⟩ := | 1 ⟩ ⊗ l ⊗ | 0 ⟩ ⊗ ( n − l ) \ket{1^{l}\,0^{n-l}}:=\ket{1}^{\otimes l}\otimes\ket{0}^{\otimes(n-l)} . In contrast, when the state is given as the Dicke state | Ξ n , l ⟩ \ket{\Xi_{n,l}} , the measurement outcome takes one deterministic value. These two cases have completely different behaviors. As the second setting, we study the case when the state is given as the tensor product of the permutation mixture ρ m ​ i ​ x , k , l \rho_{mix,k,l} and the Dicke state | Ξ n − k , m − l ⟩ \ket{\Xi_{n-k,m-l}} . We derive various types of asymptotic distribution including a kind of central limit theorem when n n goes to infinity.

Introduction

Schur-Weyl duality plays a central role in quantum information,
and it introduces direct sum decomposition on the
n
-tensor product system.
This decomposition makes a decomposition of the identity operator, and
can be regarded as a projection measurement over the corresponding quantum system,
so called Schur-Weyl duality measurement.
This measurement is used for the estimation for the eigenvalue of the density matrix,
universal quantum data compression, and
universal distortion-free entanglement concentration.
When we focus on the

n
-tensor product of the qubit system,
each component of the decomposition is characterized by Young index
(n-x,x)

with
x=0,1,\ldots,\lfloor n/2\rfloor
.
The outcome
X
of the measurement
is given by the second index
x
.
When the state is prepared as the
n
-tensor product of a state with
eigenvalues
(1-p,p)
under the condition
0<p<\frac{1}{2}
,
the variable
\frac{X}{n}
converges to
p

and the normalized difference

\frac{X-np}{\sqrt{n}}
is asymptotically subject to the normal distribution
with variance
p(1-p)

[
40
, (55)]
[
39
]
[
11
, Section 6.3]
.
In contrast, when the state is prepared as the
n
-tensor product
of the completely mixed state,
the variable
\frac{X}{n}
converges to
1/2

and the normalized difference

\frac{X-n/2}{\sqrt{n}}
is asymptotically subject to
the chi-square distribution
[
38
, Section 13.5]
.

For a further study, we remember that
any
n
-tensor product state can be written as a mixture of
equal weight states.
Once we fix our basis to the computation basis
\ket{0},\ket{1}
,
each equal weight state is given as
the permutation mixture
\rho_{mix,n,l}

of the state
\ket{1^{l}\,0^{n-l}}\coloneqq\ket{1}^{\otimes l}\otimes\ket{0}^{\otimes(n-l)}
.
Notice that
the outcome
X
of Schur-Weyl duality measurement
has the same behavior with the state

\left|1^{l}\,0^{n-l}\right>\!\left<1^{l}\,0^{n-l}\right|
and its permutation mixture
\rho_{mix,n,l}
.
The first question is the asymptotic behavior of
the outcome
X
when the state is
the permutation mixture
\rho_{mix,n,l}

of a state
\left|1^{l}\,0^{n-l}\right>\!\left<1^{l}\,0^{n-l}\right|
.
When
l=np
with
0<p<1/2
,
we show that
the variable
\frac{X}{n}
converges to
p

and the normalized difference

\frac{X-np}{\sqrt{n}}
converges to zero.
To see the behavior of the difference
\frac{X}{n}-p
,
we need to enlarge it with larger scaling.
As the result, we show that
X-np

is asymptotically subject to the geometric normal distribution.
In contrast, when
l=n/2
,
we show that
the variable
\frac{X}{n}
converges to
1/2
,
but the normalized difference

\frac{X-n/2}{\sqrt{n}}
does not converges to zero
and is asymptotically subject to the Rayleigh distribution.

On the other hand, when the state is the superposition

\ket{\Xi_{n,l}}

of the permutations of the state
\ket{1^{l}\,0^{n-l}}
,
the outcome
X
is always
0

because such a state belongs to the symmetric subspace.
Such a state is called the Dicke state
[
7
]
, and
it has been playing an important roles in
calculation of entanglement measures
[
34
,
13
,
35
,
33
,
36
]
,
quantum communication and quantum networking
[
22
,
27
]
. Some typical Dicke states have been realized in trapped atomic ions
[
18
]
.
Recently, the multi-qubit Dicke state with half-excitations
has been employed to implement a scalable quantum search based on
Grover’s algorithm by using adiabatic techniques
[
19
]
.

Now, we arise the following question.
How can we characterize the stochastic behavior of
the distribution
P_{n,m,k,l}
of the outcome
X
of
the Schur-Weyl duality measurement
when the state is given as the tensor product of

\rho_{mix,k,l}
and
\left|\Xi_{n-k,m-l}\right>\!\left<\Xi_{n-k,m-l}\right|
?
When
k=l=0
, the state belongs to the symmetric subspace
and the variable

\frac{X}{n}
takes the value
0
.
When
k=n,l=m
, the variable

\frac{X}{n}
asymptotically converges to
m/n
in probability.
Therefore, we expect that
the variable
\frac{X}{n}
takes values between
0
and
m/n
.
Interestingly, as shown in the paper
[
16
]
,
the probability mass function (pmf)
p(x\,|\,n,m,k,l)
of
the distribution
P_{n,m,k,l}

can be expressed by using Hahn and Racah polynomials, which are

{}_{3}F_{2}
- and
{}_{4}F_{3}
-hypergeometric orthogonal polynomials.
Since the numerical calculation method of these functions
has been well established,
this method yields the explicit calculations of the distribution
P_{n,m,k,l}
.
Further, this method enables us to derive a useful recursion formula for
the probability mass function (pmf)
p(x\,|\,n,m,k,l)
of
the distribution
P_{n,m,k,l}
.

However, although these formulas give us the exact values of
the pmf
p(x\,|\,n,m,k,l)
,
they did not directly present the trend of
the distribution
P_{n,m,k,l}
when
n
is sufficiently large.
To extract their trend, we discuss
their asymptotic behavior under two types of limit, i.e., Type I and Type II limits.
Type I limit assumes that
k
and
l
are fixed and
m
is linear for
n
,
i.e.,
m=\xi n
with fixed ratio
\xi
, under
n\to\infty
.
That is, the mixed state
\rho_{mix,k,l}
is fixed and the size of the Dicke state
\ket{\Xi_{n-k,m-l}}
increases.
Type II limit does that
k,l
and
m
are linear for
n
under
n\to\infty
.
That is, the sizes of the mixed state
\rho_{mix,k,l}
and the Dicke state
\ket{\Xi_{n-k,m-l}}
increase.
Type I limit corresponds to the situation of the law of small numbers,
and Type II limit corresponds to the situation of the central limit theorem.
Under Type I limit, we show that
the distribution
P_{n,m,k,l}
converges to
the convolution of two binomial distributions.

In Type II limit, i.e., the limit
n\to\infty
with
k,l
and
m
linear for
n
,
we need to treat many ratios and parameters,
which are summarized in Table
1
.
While
the fixed ratios 1 and 2 are natural,
the fixed ratio 3 is useful for our purpose.

fixed ratio set 1

\alpha=\frac{l}{n}

\beta=\frac{m-l}{n}

\gamma=\frac{k-l}{n}

\delta=\frac{n-m-k+l}{n}

fixed ratio set 2

\xi=\frac{m}{n}

\kappa=\frac{k}{n}
-l

\tau=\frac{l}{n}-\xi\kappa

fixed ratio set 3

\beta=\frac{m-l}{n}

\delta=\frac{n-m-k+l}{n}
-l

\xi=\frac{m}{n}

limit pdf parameters

\mu\coloneqq\frac{1-\sqrt{D}}{2}

\sigma^{2}\coloneqq\frac{(1-\beta-\delta)\beta\delta}{D}

D\coloneqq 4\beta\delta+(2\xi-1)^{2}

Table 1:
Ratios and parameters for Type II limit

Under this limit, we show that

\frac{X}{n}
asymptotically converges to
\mu
in probability,
where
\mu
is defined in Table
1
.
We also derive a kind of central limit theorem
by using the above mentioned recursion formula.
That is, the normalized difference

\frac{X-np}{\sqrt{n}}
is asymptotically subject to the normal distribution
with the variance
\sigma
defined in Table
1

except for the case

\beta\delta=0
nor
\alpha=\gamma=0
.
That is,
denoting the probability for the distribution
P_{n,m,k,l}

by
\Prb_{n}
,
we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\Prb_{n}\Bigl[t\leq\frac{X-n\mu}{\sqrt{n}\sigma}\leq u\Bigr]=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{t}^{u}e^{-s^{2}/2}\,ds

(1)

for any real
t<u
.

shows some examples comparing the pmf
p(x\,|\,n,m,k,l)

and the normal distribution.

As shown in this paper,
the variance
\sigma
is well defined and has non-zero value
except for these two cases.
When
\beta\delta=0
,
the state
\left|\Xi_{n-k,m-l}\right>\!\left<\Xi_{n-k,m-l}\right|
is

\rho_{n-k,0}
or
\rho_{n-k,n-k}
.
In this case the outcome
X
of Schur-Weyl duality measurement
has the same behavior with the state

\left|1^{l}\,0^{n-l}\right>\!\left<1^{l}\,0^{n-l}\right|

or
\left|1^{l+n-k}\,0^{k-l}\right>\!\left<1^{l+n-k}\,0^{k-l}\right|
.
This case has been discussed in the above discussion.
When
\alpha=\gamma=0
,
the state of the system to be measured is
the state
\left|\Xi_{n-k,m-l}\right>\!\left<\Xi_{n-k,m-l}\right|
so that
the variable
\frac{X}{n}
takes the value
0
.
Therefore, it is sufficient to discuss the case except for these two cases.
The above type of central limit theorem is the main contribution of this paper.
This analysis shows the intermediate case
between the two cases mentioned the above.

Although several related preceding studies exist,
our obtained central limit theorem is different from theirs in the following way.
Several studies
[

,

,

,

]
discussed

although their distributions do not depend on the state of the quantum system

In the context of quantum information, various studies

[

,

However, since their analysis assumes the independent and identical tensor product structure,

In fact, Wigner 3j and 6j symbols are described by

{}_{3}F_{2}
- and
{}_{4}F_{3}
-hypergeometric orthogonal polynomials
[
3
,

]
.
However, their description and our description are different in the following points.
First,
{}_{3}F_{2}
- and

Second, these polynomials are directly linked to the pmf in our case.
But, in the case of Wigner 3j and 6j symbols,

Therefore, it is not easy to derive a recursion formula for pmfs related to

2
First setting

This paper focuses the
n
-fold tensor product

\mathcal{H}=(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes n}
of the qubit system
\mathbb{C}^{2}=\mathbb{C}\ket{0}\oplus\mathbb{C}\ket{1}
,
and consider it as the representation space of the permutation group
G=\mathfrak{S}_{n}

acting by permutation of tensor factors as

\displaystyle\pi(g)(\ket{i_{1}\dotsm i_{n}})\coloneqq\ket{i_{g^{-1}(1)}\dotsm i_{g^{-1}(n)}},\quad g\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}.

(2)

Also, we consider the representation of
\SU(2)
as the product representation.

Our main focus is the
\SU(2)
-
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
Schur-Weyl duality
on the tensor product system
\mathcal{H}=(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes n}
depicted as

\displaystyle\SU(2)\curvearrowright(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes n}\curvearrowleft\mathfrak{S}_{n}.

(3)

The Schur-Weyl duality claims that we have the following decomposition of

(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes n}
into irreducible representations (irreps for short):

\displaystyle(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes n}=\bigoplus_{x=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\mathcal{U}_{(n-x,x)}\boxtimes\mathcal{V}_{(n-x,x)}.

(4)

Here
\boxtimes
denotes the tensor product of linear spaces, equipped with

\SU(2)
-action on the left and
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-action on the right factor,

\mathcal{V}_{(n-x,x)}
denotes the
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-irrep corresponding to the partition
(n-x,x)
,
and
\mathcal{U}_{(n-x,x)}
denotes the highest weight
\SU(2)
-irrep of dimension
n-2x+1
.
Using the hook formula (see
[
24
, I.5, Example 2; I.7, (7.6)]
for example),
we can compute the dimension of
\mathcal{V}_{(n-x,x)}
as

\displaystyle\dim\mathcal{V}_{(n-x,x)}=\frac{k!}{\prod_{\square\in(n-x,x)}h(\square)}=\frac{n-2x+1}{n-x+1}\binom{n}{x}=\binom{n}{x}-\binom{n}{x-1},

(5)

where
\square\in(n-x,x)
denotes a box in the Young diagram
corresponding to the partition
(n-x,x)
.
Using this formula, we have the completely mixed state

\rho_{\text{mix},\mathcal{V}_{(n-x,x)}}=\frac{1}{\dim\mathcal{V}_{(n-x,x)}}\id_{\mathcal{V}_{(n-x,x)}}
.
The highest weight
\SU(2)
-irrep of dimension
2j+1
has the standard basis

\{\ket{j,m}\mid m=-j,-j+1,\dotsc,j\}
, where

j
and
m
express the total and z-direction angular momenta, respectively.

In our analysis,
the projector
\mathsf{P}_{x}
onto the isotypical component
in the decomposition (
4
)
plays a key role, and is defined as

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}_{x}\colon(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes n}\relbar\joinrel\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{U}_{(n-x,x)}\boxtimes\mathcal{V}_{(n-x,x)},

(6)

where
x
takes a value in
\{0,1,\ldots,\lfloor n/2\rfloor\}
.
As the set of the projectors

\{\mathsf{P}_{x}\}_{x}
forms a projective measurement,
and is called
Schur-Weyl duality measurement.
The aim of this paper is to investigate the behavior of
the measurement outcome
X
.

First, we assume that the state is given as
the state
\left|1^{l}\,0^{n-l}\right>\!\left<1^{l}\,0^{n-l}\right|
or
its permutation mixture
\rho_{mix,n,l}
with
l\leq n/2
,
and they are defined as

\displaystyle\ket{1^{l}\,0^{n-l}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq|\overbrace{1\dotsm 1}^{l}\,\overbrace{0\dotsm 0}^{k-l}\rangle=\ket{1}^{\otimes l}\otimes\ket{0}^{\otimes(n-l)}\in(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes n},

(7)

\displaystyle\rho_{mix,n,l}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\tbinom{n}{l}^{-1}\bigl(\left|1^{l}\,0^{n-l}\right>\!\left<1^{l}\,0^{n-l}\right|+\text{permuted terms}\bigr).

(8)

Since the projection
\mathsf{P}_{x}
is permutation invariant,
we have

\displaystyle p(x|n,l)\coloneqq\tr\mathsf{P}_{x}\left|1^{l}\,0^{n-l}\right>\!\left<1^{l}\,0^{n-l}\right|=\tr\mathsf{P}_{x}\rho_{mix,n,l}.

(9)

This probability can be calculated as

\displaystyle p(x|n,l)=\frac{\binom{n}{x}-\binom{n}{x-1}}{\binom{n}{l}}=\frac{\binom{n}{x}}{\binom{n}{l}}\frac{n-2x+1}{n-x+1}

(10)

for
x=0,1,\dotsc,l
as follows.
The projection
\binom{n}{l}\rho_{mix,n,l}
commutes with
\mathsf{P}_{x}
and
satisfies the relation

\displaystyle\rank\Big(\sum_{x=0}^{x^{\prime}}\mathsf{P}_{x}\Big)\binom{n}{l}\rho_{mix,n,l}\Big(\sum_{x=0}^{x^{\prime}}\mathsf{P}_{x}\Big)=\binom{n}{x^{\prime}}

(11)

for
x^{\prime}=0,1,\dotsc,l
. Then, (
11
) implies (
10
).

Next, we discuss the asymptotic behavior when
the ratio
\frac{l}{n}
is fixed to
\mu
.
Let us denote by
\Prb_{n}
the probability for the distribution
defined in (
10
).

Theorem 1
.

When
\mu<\frac{1}{2}
,
we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\Prb_{n}\bigl[X=n\mu-i\bigr]=\Bigl(\frac{\mu}{1-\mu}\Bigr)^{i}\,\frac{1-2\mu}{1-\mu}.

(12)

That is, the variable
n\mu-X
asymptotically obeys
the geometric distribution with parameter
\frac{\mu}{1-\mu}
.
We also have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[X]=n\mu-\frac{\mu}{1-2\mu}+o(n^{0}),\quad\mathbb{V}[X]=\frac{\mu(1-\mu)}{(1-2\mu)^{2}}+o(n^{0}).

(13)

Proof.

First, we consider the case with
\mu<\frac{1}{2}
, i.e.,
l<n-l
.
The probability (
10
) is rewritten as

\displaystyle p(l-i)=\frac{l(l-1)\cdots(l-i+1)}{(n-l+1)(n-l+2)\cdots(n-l+i)}\frac{n-2l+2i+1}{n-l+i+1}.

(14)

Taking the limit
n\to\infty
, we obtain (
12
).
To prove (
13
), it is enough to show

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[X-n\mu+\tfrac{\mu}{1-2\mu}]=0,\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\bigl(X-n\mu+\tfrac{\mu}{1-2\mu}\bigl)^{2}\bigr]=\frac{\mu(1-\mu)}{(1-2\mu)^{2}}.

(15)

Denoting
\phi\coloneqq\frac{\mu}{1-\mu}
, we have
0\leq\phi<1
, and can rewrite
(
12
) as
\lim_{n\to\infty}p(n\mu-i)=\phi^{i}(1-\phi)
.
Then, we can deduce the following relations:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lim_{R\to\infty}\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{i=0}^{R}\bigl(i-\tfrac{\mu}{1-2\mu}\bigr)p(n\mu-i)&=\lim_{R\to\infty}-(R+1)\phi^{R+1}=0,\\
\lim_{R\to\infty}\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{i=0}^{R}\bigl(i-\tfrac{\mu}{1-2\mu}\bigr)^{2}p(n\mu-i)&=\lim_{R\to\infty}\Bigl(\tfrac{\phi(1-\phi^{R+1})}{(1-\phi)^{2}}-(R+1)^{2}\phi^{R+1}\Bigr)\\
&=\tfrac{\phi}{(1-\phi)^{2}}=\tfrac{\mu(1-\mu)}{(1-2\mu)^{2}}.\end{split}

(16)

On the other hand, using (
14
), we have
p(l-i)\leq\phi^{i}
. Hence,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lim_{R\to\infty}\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{i=R+1}^{n}\left|i-\tfrac{\mu}{1-2\mu}\right|p(n\mu-i)&\leq\lim_{R\to\infty}\sum_{i=R+1}^{\infty}\left|i-\tfrac{\phi}{1-\phi}\right|\phi^{i}=0,\\
\lim_{R\to\infty}\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{i=R+1}^{n}\bigl(i-\tfrac{\mu}{1-2\mu}\bigr)^{2}p(n\mu-i)&=\lim_{R\to\infty}\sum_{i=R+1}^{\infty}\bigl(i-\tfrac{\mu}{1-2\mu}\bigr)^{2}\bigl(\tfrac{\mu}{1-\mu}\bigr)^{i}=0.\end{split}

(17)

Therefore, combining (
16
) and (
17
),
we obtain (
15
).
∎

Theorem 2
.

When
\mu=\frac{1}{2}
,
we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\Prb_{n}\Bigl[-u\leq\frac{X-n\mu}{\sqrt{n}}\leq-t\Bigr]=\int_{t}^{u}4se^{-2s^{2}}\,ds

(18)

for any real
t<u
.
That is,
\frac{X-n\mu}{\sqrt{n}}
asymptotically obeys the Rayleigh distribution.
We also have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[X]=n\mu-\sqrt{n}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{8}}+o(\sqrt{n}),\quad\mathbb{V}[X]=n\Bigl(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\pi}{8}\Bigr)+o(n).

(19)

Proof.

Similarly as (
14
), we can rewrite the probability (
10
) as

\displaystyle p(n\mu-\sqrt{n}R)=\frac{2\sqrt{n}R+1}{\frac{n}{2}+\sqrt{n}R+1}\prod_{i=1}^{n\sqrt{R}}\Bigl(\frac{1-\frac{2(i-1)}{n}}{1+\frac{2i}{n}}\Bigr).

(20)

Hence, using
\log\frac{1-x/n}{1+y/n}=x+y+y^{2}+o(n^{-2})
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\log\bigl(\sqrt{n}p(n\mu-\sqrt{n}R)\bigr)=(\log 4R)\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{R\sqrt{n}}\log\frac{1-\frac{2(i-1)}{n}}{1+\frac{2i}{n}}

(21)

\displaystyle=(\log 4R)\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{R\sqrt{n}}\Bigl(-\frac{2(i-1)}{\sqrt{n}}-\frac{2i}{\sqrt{n}}\Bigr)=(\log 4R)\int_{0}^{R}(-4s)\,ds=-2R^{2}(\log 4R).

(22)

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\Prb_{n}\bigl[-u\leq\tfrac{X-n\mu}{\sqrt{n}}\leq-t\bigr]

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{i=t\sqrt{n}}^{u\sqrt{n}}p(n\mu-i)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{i=t\sqrt{n}}^{u\sqrt{n}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\cdot\frac{4i}{\sqrt{n}}e^{-2(i/\sqrt{n})^{2}}

\displaystyle=\int_{t}^{u}4se^{-2s^{2}}\,ds,

(23)

which implies (
18
).
The distribution defined by (
23
) is the Rayleigh distribution
with mean
\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{8}}
and variance
\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\pi}{8}
.
Then, due to the same reason as the proof of (
13
) in
Theorem
1
, it is sufficient
for the proof of (
19
) to show the following:

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{i=\sqrt{n}R}^{n}\left|\tfrac{i-\sqrt{n}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{8}}}{\sqrt{n}}\right|\,p(n\mu+i)=0,\quad\lim_{R\to\infty}\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{i=\sqrt{n}R}^{n}\Big(\tfrac{i-\sqrt{n}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{8}}}{\sqrt{n}}\Big)^{2}p(n\mu+i)=0.

Since the second statement implies the first, we will show only the second one.

Since
x-x^{2}\leq\log(1+x)\leq x
for
x>0
,
we have
\log\frac{1-x}{1+y}\leq x+y+y^{2}
for
x,y>0

Hence, (

) implies

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{\frac{n}{2}+\sqrt{n}R+1}{2\sqrt{n}R+1}\log p(n\mu-j)\leq&\sum_{i=1}^{j}\Bigl(-\frac{2(i-1)}{n}-\frac{2i}{n}+\Bigl(\frac{2i}{n}\Bigr)^{2}\Bigr)\\
&=-\frac{2j^{2}}{n}+\frac{2j(j+1)(2j+1)}{3n^{2}}.\end{split}

(25)

Then, as for the second statement in (
24

\displaystyle\sum_{i=\sqrt{n}R}^{n}\Bigl(\frac{i-\sqrt{n}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{8}}}{\sqrt{n}}\Bigr)^{2}p(n\mu+i)\leq\sum_{i=\sqrt{n}R}^{n}\frac{2i+1}{\frac{n}{2}+i+1}\Big(\frac{i-\sqrt{n}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{8}}}{\sqrt{n}}\Big)^{2}e^{-\frac{2j^{2}}{n}+\frac{2j(j+1)(2j+1)}{3n^{2}}}.

(26)

Hence,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{i=\sqrt{n}R}^{n}\Bigl(\frac{i-\sqrt{n}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{8}}}{\sqrt{n}}\Bigr)^{2}p(n\mu+i)\leq\int_{R}^{\infty}4s\Bigl(s-\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{8}}\Bigr)^{2}e^{-2s^{2}}\,ds<\infty,

which implies the second statement in (

Thus the proof is completed.

Second setting

3.1
Formulation

Next, we focus on a Dicke state, which is a typical invariant state because
it is given as the permutation invariant state
with fixed weights
N
and
M
as

\displaystyle\ket{\Xi_{N+M,M}}\coloneqq\tbinom{N+M}{M}^{-1/2}\bigl(\ket{1^{M}\,0^{N}}+\text{permuted terms}\bigr)\in(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes(N+M)}.

(32)

When the state is
\ket{\Xi_{n,l}}
,
the outcome
X
of Schur-Weyl duality measurement
is always zero due to the symmetry.
Then, we discuss the behavior of the outcome
X
when
the state is given as the tensor product state

\rho_{mix,k,l}\otimes\left|\Xi_{n-k,m-l}\right>\!\left<\Xi_{n-k,m-l}\right|
.
For brevity, we denote the range of the parameters
n,m,k,l
by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{N}\coloneqq\{(n,m,k,l)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{4}\mid m,k\leq n,\ m+k-n\leq l\leq m\wedge k\}.

(33)

Our focus is the pmf defined as

\displaystyle p(x\,|\,n,m,k,l)\coloneqq

\displaystyle\tr\mathsf{P}_{x}\rho_{mix,k,l}\otimes\left|\Xi_{n-k,m-l}\right>\!\left<\Xi_{n-k,m-l}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tr\mathsf{P}_{x}\left|1^{l}\,0^{k-l}\right>\!\left<1^{l}\,0^{k-l}\right|\otimes\left|\Xi_{n-k,m-l}\right>\!\left<\Xi_{n-k,m-l}\right|.

(34)

By using the state

\displaystyle\ket{\Xi_{n,m|k,l}}\coloneqq\ket{1^{l}\,0^{k-l}}\otimes\ket{\Xi_{n-k,m-l}}\in(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes n},

(35)

the pmf

has the form

\displaystyle p(x\,|\,n,m,k,l)=\bra{\Xi_{n,m|k,l}}\mathsf{P}_{(n-x,x)}\ket{\Xi_{n,m|k,l}}.

(36)

The setting implies a few but basic properties of
p(x)
.
First, we can switch
\ket{0}\leftrightarrow\ket{1}
in the system,
and
p(x)
inherits the symmetry

\displaystyle p(x\,|\,n,m,k,l)=p(x\,|\,n,n-m,k,k-l).

(37)

Second, since
p(x)
comes from the decomposition (
4
)
of unitary representation,
p(x)
is a probability mass function
for a discrete probability distribution
\{0,1,\dotsc,\lfloor n/2\rfloor\}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
.

When
m=l
, the Dicke state

\ket{\Xi_{n,m|k,l}}
is
\ket{0^{n-k}}
. We have

\displaystyle p(x\,|\,n,m,k,l)=

\displaystyle\tr\mathsf{P}_{x}\left|1^{l}\,0^{k-l}\right>\!\left<1^{l}\,0^{k-l}\right|\otimes\left|0^{n-k}\right>\!\left<0^{n-k}\right|=p(x\,|\,n,l),

(38)

which does not depend on
k
.
Similarly, when
n-m-k+l=0
, the Dicke state
\ket{\Xi_{n,m|k,l}}
is
\ket{1^{k-l}}
. We have

\displaystyle p(x\,|\,n,m,k,l)=

\displaystyle\tr\mathsf{P}_{x}\left|1^{l}\,0^{k-l}\right>\!\left<1^{l}\,0^{k-l}\right|\otimes\left|1^{k-l}\right>\!\left<1^{k-l}\right|=p(x\,|\,n,k),

(39)

which does not depend on
m,l
.
Hence, these two cases are reduced to the first setting,
and has redundancy for indices.
Also, when

, our state is the Dicke state

\ket{\Xi_{n,m|k,l}}
so that the random variable

takes

with probability

4
Asymptotics analysis on distribution

We study the asymptotics of our distribution

under the limit

. We study the following two cases.

1.

The limit
n\to\infty
with
k,l
and
m/n
fixed.
That is, the mixed state
\rho_{mix,k,l}
is fixed, and the size of the Dicke state
\ket{\Xi_{n-k,m-l}}
increases.

2.

The limit
n\to\infty
with
m/n
,
k/n
and
l/n
fixed.
That is, the sizes of the mixed state
\rho_{mix,k,l}
and the Dicke state
\ket{\Xi_{n-k,m-l}}

We call them Type I and Type II limit, respectively.
The details are given in Subsection

and
Subsections

. Hereafter we denote by

the probability for the distribution

, and by

4.1
Asymptotic analysis of Type I limit

To address Type I limit, we employ the binomial distribution
B_{\xi,j}
with
j

with successful probability
0\leq\xi\leq 1
whose pmf is given by
x\mapsto\binom{j}{x}\xi^{x}(1-\xi)^{j-x}
.
Then, we introduce the distribution

P_{R|\xi;k,l}\coloneqq B_{\xi,k-l}*B_{1-\xi,l}

where

denotes the convolution of probability distributions.
Its pmf is denoted by

and written as

\displaystyle q(x\,|\,\xi;k,l)=\xi^{l-x}(1-\xi)^{k-l-x}\sum_{u=0\vee(x-k+l)}^{x\wedge l}\binom{k-l}{x-u}\binom{l}{u}\xi^{2(x-u)}(1-\xi)^{2u}.

(49)

Then, as shown in Section
8
, we have the following theorem.

Theorem 8
.

In the limit
n\to\infty
with
k,l
and
\xi=m/n
fixed,
the distribution
P_{n,m,k,l}
is approximated to
the above defined distribution

.
In particular, the asymptotic expectation is given by

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[X]=(k-l)\xi+l(1-\xi).

(50)

In fact, it is known as the law of small numbers
that the distribution

converges to a Poisson distribution

In Type I limit,

and

That is, we can consider In Type I limit as

The aim of this section is the proof of Theorem

Consider Type I limit.
Let us denote by

\{0,1,\dotsc,j\}\to\mathbb{R}

and

Theorem 13
.

In the limit

with

and

\displaystyle P_{n,n\xi,k,l}\sim P_{R|\xi;k,l}\coloneqq B_{\xi,k-l}*B_{1-\xi,l}.

(75)

The limit distribution

(k-l)\xi+l(1-\xi)
and

, respectively.

We denote by

the pmf of the limit distribution

By definition of the convolution

\displaystyle q(x\,|\,\xi;k,l)=\xi^{l-x}(1-\xi)^{k-l-x}\sum_{u=0\vee(x-k+l)}^{x\wedge l}\binom{k-l}{x-u}\binom{l}{u}\xi^{2(x-u)}(1-\xi)^{2u}.

(76)

Note that the limit distribution

inherits the system symmetry (

) of the distribution
P_{n,m,k,l}
,
and is invariant under the switch
(\xi,k-l,l)\leftrightarrow(1-\xi,l,k-l)
,
which can be checked by the definition (
75
)
and also by the expression (

) of limit pmf

.

Now, we show Theorem
13
. The proof consists of two steps.

The first step of the proof of Theorem
13
.

By the symmetry remarked after (

),
it is enough to consider the case

.
Thus, we can use Hahn presentation of the pmf

in Theorem

. Let us show it again: For

, we have

\displaystyle p(x)=\frac{\binom{n}{x}}{\binom{n}{m}}\frac{n-2x+1}{n-x+1}\sum_{i\geq 0}\frac{\binom{M}{i}\binom{N}{i}}{\binom{m}{i}\binom{n-m}{i}}\sum_{r=0}^{i}(-1)^{r}\binom{x}{r}\binom{m-x}{i-r}\binom{n-m-x}{i-r}

(77)

with
M\coloneqq m-l
and
N\coloneqq n-m-k+l
.
In this first step of the proof,
we transform the formula (
77
) without taking limit. The result is:

Proposition 14
.

The formula (
77
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle p(x)=\frac{\binom{n-k}{m-l}}{\binom{n}{m}}\frac{n-2x+1}{n-x+1}\frac{n^{\downarrow x}}{m^{\downarrow x}(n-m)^{\downarrow x}}\frac{1}{x!}w(x),

(78)

\displaystyle w(x)\coloneqq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq u,v\leq x\\
u+v\geq x\end{subarray}}l^{\downarrow u}(k-l)^{\downarrow v}\frac{(M^{\downarrow x-u})^{2}(N^{\downarrow x-v})^{2}}{(M+N)^{\downarrow 2x-u-v}}\frac{x!}{(u+v-x)!(x-u)!(x-v)!}.

(79)

Proof.

Let us set
T\coloneqq\binom{n}{m}^{-1}\binom{n}{x}\frac{n-2x+1}{n-x+1}
and

\displaystyle W(x)\coloneqq\binom{n-k}{m-l}^{-1}\sum_{i=0}^{M\wedge N}\frac{\binom{M}{i}\binom{N}{i}}{\binom{m}{i}\binom{n-m}{i}}\,\sum_{r=0}^{i\wedge x}(-1)^{r}\binom{x}{r}\binom{m-x}{i-r}\binom{n-m-x}{i-r}.

(80)

Thus, the formula (
77
) is
p(x)=T\cdot\binom{n-k}{m-l}\cdot W(x)
,
and the consequence will be obtained if we show

\displaystyle W(x)=\frac{1}{m^{\downarrow x}(n-m)^{\downarrow x}}\sum_{u,v=0}^{x}l^{\downarrow u}(k-l)^{\downarrow v}\frac{(M^{\downarrow x-u})^{2}(N^{\downarrow x-v})^{2}}{(M+N)^{\downarrow 2x-u-v}}\binom{x}{u+v-x}\binom{2x-u-v}{x-u}.

(81)

We start with

\displaystyle\frac{\binom{m-x}{i-r}}{\binom{m}{i}}=\frac{i^{\downarrow r}(m-i)^{\downarrow x-r}}{m^{\downarrow x}},\quad\frac{\binom{n-m-x}{i-r}}{\binom{n-m}{i}}=\frac{i^{\downarrow r}(n-m-i)^{\downarrow x-r}}{(n-m)^{\downarrow x}}.

(82)

Thus, we have

\displaystyle W(x)\coloneqq I(x)/\bigl(m^{\downarrow x}(n-m)^{\downarrow x}\bigr),

(83)

\displaystyle I(x)\coloneqq\binom{M+N}{N}^{-1}\sum_{i=0}^{M\wedge N}\sum_{r=0}^{i\wedge x}\binom{M}{i}\binom{N}{i}(-1)^{r}\binom{x}{r}i^{\downarrow r}(m-i)^{\downarrow x-r}i^{\downarrow r}(n-m-i)^{\downarrow x-r}.

(84)

Next, using the identity

\displaystyle(y+z)^{\downarrow j}=\sum_{U=0}^{j}y^{\downarrow U}z^{\downarrow j-U}\binom{j}{U}

(85)

for commuting letters
x,y
and
j\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}&i^{\downarrow r}\binom{M}{i}\cdot(m-i)^{\downarrow x-r}=\frac{M^{\downarrow i}}{(i-r)!}\cdot\sum_{U=0}^{x-r}(M-i)^{\downarrow U}l^{\downarrow x-r-U}\binom{x-r}{U}\\
&=\sum_{U=0}^{x-r}\frac{M^{\downarrow i+U}}{(i-r)!}l^{\downarrow x-r-U}\binom{x-r}{U}=\sum_{U=0}^{x-r}M^{\downarrow r+U}\binom{M-r-U}{i-r}l^{\downarrow x-r-U}\binom{x-r}{U}.\end{split}

(86)

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle i^{\downarrow r}\binom{N}{i}\cdot(n-m-i)^{\downarrow x-r}=\sum_{V=0}^{x-r}N^{\downarrow r+V}\binom{N-r-V}{i-r}(k-l)^{\downarrow x-r-V}\binom{x-r}{V}.

(87)

Then, changing the order of summations, we can rewrite

as

\displaystyle\begin{split}I(x)=&\sum_{U,V=0}^{x}\sum_{r=0}^{x}(-1)^{r}\binom{x}{r}l^{\downarrow a-r-U}\binom{x-r}{U}(k-l)^{\downarrow x-r-V}\binom{x-r}{V}M^{\downarrow r+U}N^{\downarrow r+V}\\
&\cdot\binom{M+N}{N}^{-1}\sum_{i=0}^{M\wedge N}\binom{M-r-U}{i-r}\binom{N-r-V}{i-r}.\end{split}

(88)

Using the Chu-Vandermonde formula

\displaystyle\sum_{r\geq 0}\binom{A}{a-r}\binom{B}{r}=\binom{A+B}{a},

(89)

we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{i=0}^{M\wedge N}\binom{M-r-U}{i-r}\binom{N-r-V}{i-r}&=\binom{M+N-2r-U-V}{N-r-V}\\
&=\binom{M+N}{N}\frac{M^{\downarrow r+U}N^{\downarrow r+V}}{(M+N)^{\downarrow 2r+U+V}}.\end{split}

(90)

Replacing
u\coloneqq x-r-U
and
v\coloneqq x-r-V
, we can further rewrite
I(x)
as

\displaystyle\begin{split}&I(x)=\sum_{u,v=0}^{x}l^{\downarrow u}(k-l)^{\downarrow v}\frac{(M^{\downarrow x-u})^{2}(N^{\downarrow a-v})^{2}}{(M+N)^{\downarrow 2x-u-v}}J(u,v),\\
&J(u,v)\coloneqq\sum_{r=0}^{x}(-1)^{r}\binom{x}{r}\binom{x-r}{u}\binom{x-r}{v}.\end{split}

(91)

By (
83
) and (
91
),
in order to prove (
81
), it remains to show

\displaystyle J(u,v)=\begin{cases}0&(u+v<x)\\
\frac{x!}{(u+v-x)!(x-u)!(x-v)!}&(u+v\geq x)\end{cases}.

(92)

Note that

is equal to the coefficient of
A^{u}B^{v}

in
\bigl((1+A)(1+B)-1)^{x}
for commuting letters
A
and
B
.
Thus, it is also equal to the coefficient of

in
(AB+A+B)^{x}
, and we have the above equality (
92
).
∎

This is the end of the first step.
∎

The second step of the proof of Theorem

.

Next, as the second step,
we take Type I limit
n\to\infty
of Proposition

Proposition 15
.

In the limit
n\to\infty
with

and

in Proposition

\displaystyle\frac{n^{\downarrow x}}{m^{\downarrow x}(n-m)^{\downarrow x}}\frac{1}{x!}h(x)\sim\xi^{-x}(1-\xi)^{-x}\sum_{u=0}^{x}\binom{l}{u}\binom{k-l}{x-u}\xi^{2(x-u)}(1-\xi)^{2u}\quad(n\to\infty).

Proof.

We find from the expression of

that
the terms with

Then, we can estimate the left hand side of the statement as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{n^{\downarrow x}}{m^{\downarrow x}(n-m)^{\downarrow x}}\frac{1}{x!}h(x)\sim&\frac{n^{x}}{m^{x}(n-m)^{x}}\frac{1}{x!}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq u,v\leq x\\
u+v=x\end{subarray}}l^{\downarrow u}(k-l)^{\downarrow v}\frac{m^{2(x-u)}(n-m)^{2(x-v)}}{n^{2x-u-v}}\binom{x}{u}\\

∎

By Proposition

\zeta_{1,x}&\coloneqq\frac{\theta_{x}-\sqrt{\theta_{x}^{2}-4\eta_{1,x}}}{2},\quad\zeta_{2,x}\coloneqq\frac{\theta_{x}+\sqrt{\theta_{x}^{2}-4\eta_{1,x}}}{2},\quad\theta_{x}\coloneqq 1+\eta_{1,x}+\eta_{2,x}.\end{split}

(154)

In the limit
n\to\infty
, we have (
127
):

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\eta_{1,nt}=\frac{(1-t)(t-\kappa)}{t(1-\kappa-t)},\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}\eta_{2,nt}=\frac{(t-\mu)(t-\nu)(1-2t)^{2}}{(\xi-t)(1-\xi-t)t(1-\kappa-t)}.

(155)

Proof of Proposition
22
.

Let us discuss the former probability
\Prb_{n}[Y>R]
in the statement.
Let
R>0
and set
R_{1}\coloneqq R\sigma
.
Using
\epsilon>0
, we divide the probability into two parts as

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\Prb_{n}[Y>R]=\Prb_{n}\bigl[X>n\mu+\sqrt{n}R_{1}\bigr]\\
&=\Prb_{n}\bigl[X>n(\mu+\epsilon)+\sqrt{n}R_{1}\bigr]+\Prb_{n}\bigl[n(\mu+\epsilon)+\sqrt{n}R_{1}\geq X>n\mu+\sqrt{n}R_{1}\bigr].\end{split}

(156)

By the law of large numbers for
X/n
(Theorem
20
), we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\Prb_{n}\bigl[X>n(\mu+\epsilon)+\sqrt{n}R_{1}\bigr]=0
.
So it is enough to treat the remaining probability.

For brevity, we denote
x_{1,n}\coloneqq n\mu+\sqrt{n}R_{1}
and

x_{2,n}\coloneqq n(\mu+\epsilon)+\sqrt{n}R_{1}
.
Recalling the recursion (
153
), we set

\displaystyle\zeta_{2,\max}\coloneqq\max_{x\in[x_{1,n},x_{2,n}]}\zeta_{2,x}\in(0,1).

(157)

The inequalities
0<\zeta_{2,\max}<1
follows from Lemma
19
(3),
since we have
0<\frac{x}{n}-\mu<1-\kappa-\mu
in the range concerned
(recall (
149
)).
The same argument shows
\zeta_{1,x}<0
for
x\in[x_{1,n},x_{2,n}]
.

If
\epsilon>0
is small enough, then
\zeta_{1,x}
is either
monotonically increasing or decreasing in the range
x\in[x_{1,n},x_{2,n}]
.
Hereafter until (
166
),
we assume that
\left|\zeta_{1,x}\right|
is decreasing. It yields

\displaystyle p_{n}(x+1+i)+\left|\zeta_{1,x+i}\right|p_{n}(x+i)\leq p_{n}(x+1+i)+\left|\zeta_{1,x+i-1}\right|p_{n}(x+i).

(158)

Using the recursion (
153
) iteratively, we have

\displaystyle p_{n}(x+1+i)+\left|\zeta_{1,x+i}\right|p_{n}(x+i)\leq\zeta_{2,\max}^{i}\bigl(p_{n}(x)+\left|\zeta_{1,x}\right|p_{n}(x-1)\bigr).

(159)

By summation, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\sum_{j=0}^{\sqrt{n}}\bigl(p_{n}(x+1+i+j)+\left|\zeta_{1,x+i+j}\right|p_{n}(x+i+j)\bigr)\\
&\leq\zeta_{2,\max}^{i}\sum_{j=0}^{\sqrt{n}}\bigl(p_{n}(x+j)+\left|\zeta_{1,x+j}\right|p_{n}(x-1+j)\bigr).\end{split}

(160)

We will use this inequality to estimate the remaining part

\Prb_{n}\bigl[x_{2,n}\geq X>x_{1,n}\bigr]=\sum_{x=x_{1,n}+1}^{x_{2,n}}p_{n}(x)
.
The idea is to consider the range
[x_{1,n},x_{2,n}]
modulo
\sqrt{n}
.
Replacing
i
in (
160
) by
\epsilon i^{\prime}
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\Prb_{n}\bigl[x_{2,n}\geq X>x_{1,n}\bigr]=\sum_{x=x_{1,n}+1}^{x_{2,n}}p_{n}(x)\leq\sum_{i^{\prime}=0}^{\epsilon\sqrt{n}}\sum_{j=0}^{\sqrt{n}}p_{n}(x_{1,n}+1+\sqrt{n}i^{\prime}+j)\\
&\leq\sum_{i^{\prime}=0}^{\epsilon\sqrt{n}}\sum_{j=0}^{\sqrt{n}}\bigl(p_{n}(x_{1,n}+1+\sqrt{n}i^{\prime}+j)+\left|\zeta_{1,x_{1,n}+\sqrt{n}i^{\prime}+j}\right|p_{n}(x_{1,n}+\sqrt{n}i^{\prime}+j)\bigr)\\
&\leq\sum_{i^{\prime}=0}^{\epsilon\sqrt{n}}\zeta_{2,\max}^{\sqrt{n}i^{\prime}}\sum_{j=0}^{\sqrt{n}}\bigl(p_{n}(x_{1,n}+j)+\left|\zeta_{1,x_{1,n}+j}\right|p_{n}(x_{1,n}-1+j)\bigr)\\
&\leq(1-\zeta_{2,\max}^{\sqrt{n}})^{-1}\sum_{j=0}^{\sqrt{n}}\bigl(p_{n}(x_{1,n}+j)+\left|\zeta_{1,x_{1,n}}\right|p_{n}(x_{1,n}-1+j)\bigr).\end{split}

(161)

In the last inequality we used
0<\zeta_{2,\max}<1
in (
157
).

Now, we want to take the limit
n\to\infty
of the inequality (
161
).
Set
c_{1}\coloneqq\lim_{n\to\infty}\left|\zeta_{1,x_{1,n}}\right|
which surely exists.
As for the limit of
\zeta_{2,\max}
, let us observe that, since
\frac{x}{n}\to\mu
,

\eta_{2,x}
is small by the expression of
\overline{\eta}_{2}
in (
155
).
Since
\eta_{1,x}\sim\eta_{1,\mu}<0
in the limit, we have

\displaystyle\zeta_{2,x}=\frac{1+\eta_{1,x}+\eta_{2,x}+\sqrt{(1-\eta_{1,x})^{2}+\eta_{2,x}^{2}+2(1+\eta_{1,x})\eta_{2,x}}}{2}\sim 1+\frac{\eta_{2,x}}{1-\eta_{1,x}}.

(162)

A direct calculation using (
162
) yields that

\zeta_{2,\max}=\zeta_{2,x_{1,n}}
and

\displaystyle c_{2}\coloneqq\lim_{n\to\infty}\sqrt{n}(1-\zeta_{2,\max})=\frac{R_{1}(\nu-\mu)(1-2\mu)}{\kappa(\xi-\mu)(1-\xi-\mu)}>0.

(163)

Then, in (
161
), we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\zeta_{2,\max}^{\sqrt{n}}=\lim_{n\to\infty}(1-\frac{c_{2}}{\sqrt{n}})^{\sqrt{n}}=e^{-c_{2}}
and

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{\sqrt{n}}\bigl(p_{n}(x_{1,n}+j)+\left|\zeta_{1,x_{1,n}}\right|p_{n}(x_{1,n}-1+j)\bigr)\\
&\leq(1+c_{1})\bigl(F(\tfrac{R_{1}+1}{\sigma}+0)-F(\tfrac{R_{1}}{\sigma}-0)\bigr)=(1+c_{1})\bigl(F(R+\tfrac{1}{\sigma}+0)-F(R-0)\bigr).\end{split}

(164)

Thus, the limit of the inequality (

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\Prb_{n}\bigl[x_{2,n}\geq X>x_{1,n}\bigr]\leq(1-e^{-c_{0}})^{-1}(1+c_{0,1})\bigl(F(R+\tfrac{1}{\sigma}+0)-F(R-0)\bigr).

(165)

Note that
c_{2}>0
by (

),
so that the term
(1-e^{-c_{0}})^{-1}
makes sense.
Since
F
is the limit function obtained by Helly lemma (Fact
21
),
we have
\lim_{R\to\infty}\bigl(F(R+\tfrac{1}{\sigma}+0)-F(R-0)\bigr)=0
.
Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\Prb_{n}\bigl[x_{2,n}\geq X>x_{1,n}\bigr]=0.

(166)

We can similarly treat the case when
|\zeta_{1,x}|
is increasing
in the range
x\in[x_{1,n}\sqrt{n},x_{2,n}]
.
In fact, dividing the recursion (

) by
\left|\zeta_{1,x}\right|
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\zeta_{1,x}\right|^{-1}p_{n}(x+1)+p_{n}(x)=\zeta_{2,x}\bigl(\left|\zeta_{1,x}\right|^{-1}p_{n}(x)+p_{n}(x-1)\bigr).

(167)

Since
\left|\zeta_{1,x}\right|^{-1}

Therefore, we have (
166
) in either case,

\lim_{R\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\Pr_{n}[Y>R]=0
.

Finally, we treat the latter

in the statement.
For

,
we consider the rewritten form of the recursion (

\displaystyle p_{n}(x)+\left|\zeta_{1,x}\right|p_{n}(x-1)=\zeta_{2,x}^{-1}\bigl(p_{n}(x+1)+\left|\zeta_{1,x}\right|p_{n}(x)\bigr).

(168)

From (

) and

,

.

a similar argument as before works, and it yields the desired consequence

∎

8.2
The limit distribution

Going back to the beginning of §
8.1
,
we consider the random variable
Y\coloneqq\frac{X-n\mu}{\sqrt{n}\sigma}
,
the cdf
F_{n}(y)
and the limit function
F
.
By Proposition
22
, we can focus on the interval

\displaystyle n\mu-\sqrt{n}\sigma R\leq X\leq n\mu+\sqrt{n}\sigma R\iff-R\leq Y\leq R.

(169)

We continue to use the recursion of
p_{n}(x)=p(x\,|\,n,m,k,l)
as the main tool.
Let us write it again:

\displaystyle\bigl(p_{n}(x+1)-p_{n}(x)\bigr)-\eta_{1,x}\bigl(p_{n}(x)-p_{n}(x-1)\bigr)=\eta_{2,x}p_{n}(x).

(170)

Let us study the behavior of
p_{n}(x)
around
x=n\mu
.

Lemma 23
.

If
\{x_{n}\}_{n}
is a sequence in
I\coloneqq[n\mu-\sqrt{n}\sigma R,n\mu+\sqrt{n}\sigma R]

such that
\{\frac{x_{n}-n\mu}{\sqrt{n}}\}_{n}
converges, then we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}p_{n}(x_{n})=0
.

Proof.

We assume that
p_{n}(x_{n})
converges to a positive value,
and prove the statement by contradiction.
We can also assume that
p_{n}(x_{n}-1)
converges
(if not, then take a converging subsequence
\{n_{j}\}_{j}
).
Then, for every
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq-1}
, we have the limit

a_{i}\coloneqq\lim_{n\to\infty}p_{n}(x_{n}+i)
.
In particular, we have
a_{0}>0
.
Since
\lim_{n\to\infty}\eta_{2,x}=0
for
x
in the range concerned,
the recursion (
170
) yields

\displaystyle a_{i+1}-a_{i}=-\lambda(a_{i}-a_{i-1}),

(171)

with
\lambda\coloneqq-\lim_{n\to\infty}\eta_{1,x}>0
.
On the other hand, we have

\sum_{i=0}^{N}a_{i}\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\sum_{x\in I}p_{n}(x)

for any
N\in\mathbb{N}
, which yields

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_{i}\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\sum_{x\in I}p_{n}(x).

(172)

If
a_{0}=a_{-1}
, then
a_{i}=a_{0}>0
for any
i
, and
the summation
\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_{i}
diverges, which is a contradiction.
Thus, we can assume
a_{0}\neq a_{-1}
.
If
\lambda\geq 1
, then (
171
) implies that

\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_{i}
diverges, which is a contradiction.
If
\lambda<1
, then
a_{i}
converges to a point
a

in the open interval
(a_{0},a_{-1})
or
(a_{-1},a_{0})
.
Then,
a>0
, and
\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_{i}
diverges, which is a contradiction.
∎

Hereafter we fix
y\in\mathbb{R}
and set

\displaystyle x_{0}\coloneqq n\mu+\sqrt{n}\sigma y,\quad x_{\pm}\coloneqq x_{0}\pm\sqrt{n}{\sigma}\epsilon.

(173)

We only treat the case
x\in[x_{0},x_{+}]
,
when
\eta_{2,x}=O(1/\sqrt{n})
and has the same sign as
y
.

For
x\in[x_{0},x_{+}]
, the function
\eta_{1,x}
of
x
is
either monotonically increasing or decreasing.
We assume it is increasing, and set

\displaystyle\eta_{1}^{o}\coloneqq\eta_{1,x_{0}},\quad d(x)\coloneqq\eta_{1,x}-\eta_{1}^{o}.

(174)

We can see from (
155
) that
d(x)=O(\epsilon/\sqrt{n})
and
d(x)\geq 0
.
The decreasing case can be treated similarly by setting
\eta_{1}^{o}\coloneqq\eta_{1,x_{+}}
,
so we omit the detail.

In the limit
n\to\infty
, the quantity
\eta_{1}^{o}
converges to
a constant independent of
y
and
\epsilon
. We denote it by

\displaystyle\overline{\eta}_{1}^{o}\coloneqq\lim_{n\to\infty}\eta_{1}^{o}.

(175)

Using these quantities, we have:

Lemma 24
.

Define
c_{1}^{\pm}
and
c_{2}
by

\displaystyle c_{1}^{+}\coloneqq\lim_{n\to\infty}\sqrt{n}\sigma\left(\max_{x^{\prime}}\eta_{2,x^{\prime}}\right),\ c_{1}^{-}\coloneqq\lim_{n\to\infty}\sqrt{n}\sigma\left(\min_{x^{\prime}}\eta_{2,x^{\prime}}\right),\ c_{2}\coloneqq\lim_{n\to\infty}\sqrt{n}\sigma\left(\max_{x^{\prime}}d(x^{\prime})\right),

(176)

where
\min_{x^{\prime}},\max_{x^{\prime}}
are taken in the interval
[x_{-},x_{+}]
.
Then, for any
0<\varepsilon^{\prime}<\varepsilon
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}&(c_{1}^{-}-c_{2})\int_{0}^{\epsilon^{\prime}}\bigl(F(y+\epsilon-t)-F(y-t)\bigr)\,dt\\
&\qquad\leq(1-\overline{\eta}_{1}^{o})\bigl(F(y+\epsilon)-F(y+\epsilon-\epsilon^{\prime})-F(y)+F(y-\epsilon^{\prime})\bigr)\\
&\qquad\qquad\leq(c_{1}^{+}+c_{2})\int_{0}^{\epsilon^{\prime}}\bigl(F(y+\epsilon-t)-F(y-t)\bigr)\,dt.\end{split}

(177)

Proof.

The recursion (
170
) can be written as

\displaystyle\bigl(p_{n}(x+1)-p_{n}(x)\bigr)-\eta_{1}^{o}\bigl(p_{n}(x)-p_{n}(x-1)\bigr)=\eta_{2,x}p_{n}(x)+d(x)\bigl(p_{n}(x)-p_{n}(x-1)\bigr).

(178)

It yields the inequalities

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left(\min_{x^{\prime}}\eta_{2,x^{\prime}}\right)p_{n}(x)-\left(\max_{x^{\prime}}d(x)\right)p_{n}(x-1)\\
&\leq\bigl(p_{n}(x+1)-p_{n}(x)\bigr)-\eta_{1}^{o}\bigl(p_{n}(x)-p_{n}(x-1)\bigr)\leq\max_{x^{\prime}}\bigl(\eta_{2,x^{\prime}}+d(x^{\prime})\bigr)p_{n}(x),\end{split}

(179)

where
\min_{x^{\prime}},\max_{x^{\prime}}
are taken in the interval
[x_{-},x_{+}]
.
Applying the second inequality of (
179
) on the summation

p_{n}(x_{+})-p_{n}(x_{0})=\sum_{i=1}^{\sqrt{n}\sigma\epsilon}\bigl(p_{n}(x_{0}+i)-p_{n}(x_{0}+i-1)\bigr)
,
we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}&p_{n}(x_{+})-p_{n}(x_{0})-\eta_{1}^{o}\bigl(p_{n}(x_{+}-1)-p_{n}(x_{0}-1)\bigr)\\
&\leq\max_{x^{\prime}}\bigl(\eta_{2,x^{\prime}}+d(x^{\prime})\bigr)\sum_{i=1}^{\sqrt{n}\sigma\epsilon}p_{n}(x_{0}+i-1)\\
&\quad=\max_{x^{\prime}}\bigl(\eta_{2,x^{\prime}}+d(x^{\prime})\bigr)\bigl(F_{n}(y+\epsilon-\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{n}\sigma})-F_{n}(y)\bigr).\end{split}

(180)

Take any
0<\epsilon^{\prime}<\epsilon
as in the statement.
Then, for
j\in[0,\sqrt{n}\sigma\epsilon^{\prime}]
, the same argument yields

\displaystyle\begin{split}p_{n}(x_{+}-j)-p_{n}(x_{0}-j)&-\eta_{1}^{o}\bigl(p_{n}(x_{+}-j-1)-p_{n}(x_{0}-j-1)\bigr)\\
&\leq\max_{x^{\prime}}\bigl(\eta_{2,x^{\prime}}+d(x^{\prime})\bigr)\bigl(F_{n}(y+\epsilon-\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{n}\sigma}-j)-F_{n}(y-j)\bigr).\end{split}

(181)

Summation over
j
yields

\displaystyle\begin{split}&F_{n}(y+\epsilon)-F_{n}(y+\epsilon-\epsilon^{\prime})-F_{n}(y)+F_{n}(y-\epsilon)\\
&-\eta_{1}^{o}\Bigl(F_{n}(y+\epsilon-\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{n}\sigma})-F_{n}(x+\epsilon-\epsilon^{\prime}-\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{n}\sigma})-F_{n}(y-\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{n}\sigma})+F_{n}(y-\epsilon^{\prime}-\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{n}\sigma})\Bigr)\\
&\leq\max_{x^{\prime}}\bigl(\eta_{2,x^{\prime}}+d(x^{\prime})\bigr)\sum_{j=0}^{\sqrt{n}\sigma\epsilon^{\prime}}\bigl(F_{n}(y+\epsilon-\tfrac{j+1}{\sqrt{n}\sigma})-F_{n}(y-\tfrac{j}{\sqrt{n}\sigma})\bigr).\end{split}

(182)

Let us admit the following for a while:

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}\sigma}\sum_{j=0}^{\sqrt{n}\sigma\epsilon}F_{n}(y-\tfrac{j}{\sqrt{n}\sigma})=\int_{0}^{\epsilon}F(y-t)\,dt.

(183)

Then, taking
n\to\infty
of the inequality (
182
), we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}(1-\overline{\eta}_{1}^{o})&\bigl(F(y+\epsilon)-F(y+\epsilon-\epsilon^{\prime})-F(y)+F(y-\epsilon)\bigr)\\
&\leq(c_{1}^{+}+c_{2})\int_{0}^{\epsilon^{\prime}}\bigl(F(y+\epsilon-t)-F(y-t)\bigr)\,dt,\end{split}

(184)

where
\overline{\eta}_{1}^{o}
is given in (
175
),
and
c_{1}^{+},c_{2}
are given in the statement.
This is the second of the desired inequalities (
177
).

Now, we show (
183
).
Divide the interval
[0,\epsilon]
by the points
\{\tfrac{j}{\sqrt{n}\sigma}\}_{j}
.
We also consider another coarser division by
\{\tfrac{i}{N}\}_{i}

which has the first division as a refinement.
Since
F_{n}(y-t)
is a decreasing function of
t
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=0}^{N\epsilon}F_{n}(y-\tfrac{i+1}{N})&\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}\sigma}\sum_{j=0}^{\sqrt{n}\sigma\epsilon}F_{n}(y-\tfrac{j+1}{\sqrt{n}\sigma})\\
&\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}\sigma}\sum_{j=0}^{\sqrt{n}\sigma\epsilon}F_{n}(y-\tfrac{j}{\sqrt{n}\sigma})\leq\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=0}^{N\epsilon}F_{n}(y-\tfrac{i}{N}).\end{split}

(185)

Now, fixing
N
, we take
n\to\infty
.
Since it has been already shown that
F
is continuous, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=0}^{N\epsilon}F(y-\tfrac{i+1}{N})&\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}\sigma}\sum_{j=0}^{\sqrt{n}\sigma\epsilon}F_{n}(y-\tfrac{j}{\sqrt{n}\sigma})\\
&\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}\sigma}\sum_{j=0}^{\sqrt{n}\sigma\epsilon}F_{n}(y-\tfrac{j}{\sqrt{n}\sigma})\leq\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=0}^{N\epsilon}F(y-\tfrac{i}{N}).\end{split}

(186)

Next we take
N\to\infty
.
Then, the leftmost and rightmost sides of (
186
) converge to

\int_{0}^{\epsilon}F(y-t)\,dt
,
so that both middle limits are equal to the same value,
which is the desired (
183
).
Therefore, we finished the proof of the second inequality in (
177
).

As for the first inequality in (
177
),
since
\overline{\eta_{1}}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\eta_{1,x_{+}}
,
a quite similar argument works using the first inequality in (
179
).
We omit the detail.
∎

Now, we can show the central limit theorem, Theorem
10
.

Proof of Theorem
10
.

Take any
0<\epsilon^{\prime}<\epsilon
, and
consider the the inequalities (
177
) in Lemma
23
.
Dividing them by
\epsilon^{\prime}
and taking the limit
\epsilon^{\prime}\to 0
,
we find that the the derivative
f
of
F
exists and

\displaystyle\begin{split}(c_{1}^{-}-c_{2})\bigl(F(y+\epsilon-t)-F(y-t)\bigr)&\leq(1-\overline{\eta}_{1}^{o})\bigl(f(y+\epsilon)-f(y)\bigr)\\
&\leq(c_{1}^{+}+c_{2})\bigl(F(y+\epsilon-t)-F(y-t)\bigr).\end{split}

(187)

As noted right after (
174
),
we have
d(x)=\eta_{1,x}-\eta_{1}^{o}=O(\epsilon/\sqrt{n})
,
which implies
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}c_{2}=0
.
A direct calculation using (
155
) shows

\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}c_{1}^{+}=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}c_{1}^{-}=yd_{1},\quad d_{1}\coloneqq\frac{\sigma^{2}(\mu-\nu)}{(\xi-\mu)(1-\xi-\mu)}\frac{(1-2\mu)^{2}}{\mu(1-\kappa-\mu)}.

(188)

Thus, dividing (
187
) by
\epsilon
and taking
\epsilon\to 0
,
we find that
f
is differentiable and
d_{1}yf(y)=(1-\overline{\eta}_{1}^{o})f^{\prime}(y)
, i.e.,

\displaystyle f^{\prime}(y)=\frac{d_{1}y}{1-\overline{\eta}_{1}^{o}}f(y).

(189)

Finally, recalling the calculation in (
114
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{d_{1}}{1-\overline{\eta}_{1}^{o}}=-\sigma^{2}\frac{(\nu-\mu)(1-2\mu)}{\kappa(\xi-\mu)(1-\xi-\mu)}=-1,

(190)

which means that
f
satisfies
the same differential equation of the standard normal distribution.
Then, by Proposition
22
,
we find that
f
is a probability distribution function, i.e.,
having the standard normalization factor
1/\sqrt{2\pi}
.
The proof is now finished.
∎

9
Asymptotic analysis beyond central limit theorem

In this subsection, we give an asymptotic analysis
beyond the central limit theorem, Theorem
10
.
We consider the Type II limit with the condition (
65
).
Then,
\eta<1/4
,
D\coloneqq 1-4\eta>0
and the following theorem holds.

Theorem 25
.

In Type II limit with the condition (
65
), we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{V}\Bigl[\frac{X}{\sqrt{n}}\Bigr]=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\Big(\frac{X-n\mu}{\sqrt{n}}\Big)^{2}\Bigr]=\sigma^{2}\coloneqq\frac{(\alpha+\gamma)\beta\delta}{D}.

(191)

with
\mu\coloneqq(1-\sqrt{D})/2
. Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[X]=n\mu+\phi+o(n^{0}),\quad\phi\coloneqq\frac{\sigma^{2}-\mu}{1-2\mu}.

(192)

The main tool of the proof is Theorem
7
, where we derived the relation of
the expectation
\mathbb{E}[X]
and the variance
\mathbb{V}[X]
:

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{V}[X]}{n^{2}}=\frac{\mathbb{E}[X]}{n}\left(1-\frac{\mathbb{E}[X]}{n}\right)+\frac{\mathbb{E}[X]}{n^{2}}-\eta,\quad\eta\coloneqq\alpha\gamma+\alpha\delta+\beta\gamma.

(193)

Further, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\Big(\frac{X-n\mu}{\sqrt{n}}\Big)^{j}\Bigr]

\displaystyle<\infty

(194)

for
j=3,4,\ldots.

The proof consists of two steps.
In the first step, we show the first equality in (
191
)
and the estimation (
192
) using (
193
) under a certain assumption.
In the second step, we show the assumption.

Step 1 of Proof of Theorem
25

12
Conclusion and discussion

We have discussed the asymptotic behavior of the outcome of
Schur-Weyl duality measurement under
two kinds of settings.
The first setting addresses the case when the state is given as
the permutation mixture

\rho_{mix,n,l}

of the state
\left|1^{l}\,0^{n-l}\right>\!\left<1^{l}\,0^{n-l}\right|
.
The second setting addresses the case when the state is given as
the tensor product
of the permutation mixture
\rho_{mix,k,l}

and the Dicke state
\ket{\Xi_{n-k,m-l}}

and the distribution of the outcome is denoted by
P_{n,m,k,l}
.
We have derived various types of asymptotic distribution including
a kind of central limit theorem when
n
goes to infinity.

For our analysis on the distribution
P_{n,m,k,l}
,
we have employed the fact that the pmf
p(x\,|\,n,m,k,l)
of
the distribution
P_{n,m,k,l}
is described by
Hahn and Racah polynomials, which are

{}_{3}F_{2}
- and
{}_{4}F_{3}
-hypergeometric orthogonal polynomials.
Since the distribution
P_{n,m,k,l}
has a highly complicated form,
this fact shows the usefulness of
Hahn and Racah polynomials.
The relation between these polynomials and
the distribution
P_{n,m,k,l}
is based on the
the Schur-Weyl duality, which is a key structure in quantum information.
Therefore, we can expect that
these polynomials can be used in other topics in quantum information.
In particular,
for the above derivation of the central limit theorem under Type II limit,
we have employed the recurrence relation among three probabilities

p(x),(x+1),p(x+2)
that was obtained
from the formulas for hypergeometric function in the paper
[
16
]
.
This fact shows that such a recurrence relation
is useful for the central limit theorem
Therefore, it is expected that a similar
recurrence relation derives the central limit theorem.
Seeking such a possibility is another future topic.

Although Section
4
has presented
the asymptotic analysis under Type II limit under the assumption that

\beta\delta>0
,
the analysis on this case already has been presented as
the first setting.
Therefore,
under Type II limit, we have obtained three types of distributions,
normal distributions, Rayleigh distributions, and geometric distributions
as the limit distribution
dependently of the parameters
\alpha,\beta,\delta,\gamma
.
That is, the limit distribution changes
discontinuously for these parameters.
This fact means that the convergence is not uniform
around the discontinuous points.
For example,
when
\xi(=\alpha+\beta)=\frac{1}{2}
and
\beta\delta=0
,
the limit distribution is a Rayleigh distribution.
Hence, when
\beta=n^{-s}
with parameter
0<s<1

and
\xi=\frac{1}{2}
,
we can expect that the limiting distribution is an intermediate distribution
between normal distributions and Rayleigh distributions.
As an example of such distributions, we can list
the Rayleigh-normal distribution, which was introduced in
the reference
[
23
]
, and
is a one-parameter family of distributions connecting normal and Rayleigh distributions.
Investigating the limit behavior of the distribution
in this region is an interesting open problem.

Further,
when
\xi\neq\frac{1}{2}
and
\beta\delta=0
,
the limit distribution is a geometric distribution,
i.e., the limit distribution is a discrete distribution due to the following reason.
In this case, since the variable
\frac{X-n\mu}{\sqrt{n}}
converges to
0
in probability,
we need to focus on the variable
{X-n\mu}
that has a different scaling.
Hence, it is expected that the variable has
a different scaling in intermediate cases, e.g.,
when

\beta\delta=0
and

\xi=\frac{1}{2}+n^{-s}
with parameter
0<s<1

or when
\beta=n^{-s}
with parameter
0<s<1

and
\xi\neq\frac{1}{2}
.
It is another interesting open problem to clarify the limit behavior in these cases.

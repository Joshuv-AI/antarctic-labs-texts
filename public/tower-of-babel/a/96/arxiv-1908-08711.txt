Structures and bimodules of simple Hom-alternative algebras
Sylvain Attan

Abstract
. This paper is mainly devoted to a structure study of Hom-alternative algebras . Equivalent conditions for Hom-alternative algebras being solvable, simple and semi-simple are displayed. Moreover some results about Hom-alternative bimodule are found.

Questions on the structure of simple algebras in this or that variety

Representations (or bimodules) and deformations are important tools in most parts of Mathematics and Physics. By means of the representation theory, we would be more aware of the corresponding algebras.
The study of bimodule of Jordan algebras was initiated by N. Jacobson

Definition 2.1
.

A Hom-module is a pair
(M,\alpha_{M})
consisting of a
\mathbb{K}
-module
M
and
a linear self-map
\alpha_{M}:M\longrightarrow M.
A morphism

f:(M,\alpha_{M})\longrightarrow(N,\alpha_{N})
of Hom-modules is a linear map

f:M\longrightarrow N
such that
f\circ\alpha_{M}=\alpha_{N}\circ f.

Definition 2.2
.

(
[
15
]
,
[
22
]
) A Hom-algebra is a triple
(A,\mu,\alpha)
in which
(A,\alpha)
is a Hom-module,
\mu:A^{\otimes 2}\longrightarrow A
is a linear map.
The Hom-algebra
(A,\mu,\alpha)
is said to be multiplicative if
\alpha\circ\mu=\mu\circ\alpha^{\otimes 2}
(multiplicativity). A morphism

f:(A,\mu_{A},\alpha_{A})\longrightarrow(B,\mu_{B},\alpha_{B})
of Hom-algebras is a morphism of the underlying Hom-modules such that
f\circ\mu_{A}=\mu_{B}\circ f^{\otimes 2}.

In this paper, we will only consider multiplicative Hom-algebras.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
(A,\mu,\alpha)
be a Hom-algebra.

(1) The Hom-associator of
A
is the linear map
as_{A}:A^{\otimes 3}\longrightarrow A
defined as
as_{A}=\mu\circ(\mu\otimes\alpha-\alpha\otimes\mu).
A multiplicative Hom-algebra
(A,\mu,\alpha)
is said to be Hom-associative algebra if
as_{A}=0

(2) A Hom-alternative algebra
[
14
]
is a multiplicative Hom-algebra
(A,\mu,\alpha)
that satisfies both

(1)

\displaystyle as_{A}(x,x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0\mbox{ (left Hom-alternativity)}\mbox{ and }

(2)

\displaystyle as_{A}(x,y,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0\mbox{ (right Hom-alternativity)}

for all
x,y\in A.

(3) Let
(A,\mu,\alpha)
be a Hom-alternative algebra. A Hom-subalgebra of
(A,\mu,\alpha)
is a linear subspace
H
of
A,
which is closed for the multiplication
\mu
and invariant by
\alpha,
that is,
\mu(x,y)\in H
and
\alpha(x)\in H
for all
x,y\in H.
If furthermore
\mu(a,b)\in H
and
\mu(b,a)\in H
for all
(a,b)\in A\times H,
then
H
is called a two-sided Hom-ideal of
A.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
(A,\mu,\alpha)
be a Hom-alternative algebra. If there is an alternative algebra
(A,\mu^{\prime})
such that
\mu=\alpha\circ\mu^{\prime},
we say that

It is remarked

that a Hom-alternative algebra with an invertible twisting map has the compatible alternative algebra. More precisely, we get:

Corollary 2.7
.

Let
(A,\mu,\alpha)
be a Hom-alternative algebra where
\alpha
is invertible then
(A,\mu^{\prime}=\alpha^{-1}\circ\mu)
is an alternative algebra and
\alpha
is an automorphism with respect to
\mu^{\prime}.
Hence
(A,\mu,\alpha)
is of alternative type and
(A,\mu^{\prime}=\alpha^{-1}\circ\mu)
is its compatible alternative algebra.

Proof.
Let denote by
as
the associator of
(A,\mu^{\prime}=\alpha^{-1}\circ\mu).
We prove that
(A,\mu^{\prime}=\alpha^{-1}\circ\mu)
is an alternative algebra. Indeed,

\displaystyle as(x,x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu^{\prime}(\mu^{\prime}(x,x),y)-\mu^{\prime}(x,\mu^{\prime}(x,y))=\alpha^{-1}\circ\mu(\alpha^{-1}\circ\mu(x,x),y)-\alpha^{-1}\circ\mu(x,\alpha^{-1}\circ\mu(x,y))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha^{-1}\circ\mu(\alpha^{-1}\circ\mu(x,x),\alpha^{-1}\circ\alpha(y))-\alpha^{-1}\circ\mu(\alpha^{-1}\circ\alpha(x),\alpha^{-1}\circ\mu(x,y))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha^{-2}(\mu(\mu(x,x),\alpha(y))-\mu(\alpha(x),\mu(x,y)))=\alpha^{-2}\circ as_{A}(x,x,y)=\alpha^{-2}(0)=0.

Similarly, we get
as(x,y,y)=0.
Moreover,
\alpha
is an automorphism with respect to
\mu^{\prime}
. Indeed,

\displaystyle\mu^{\prime}(\alpha(x),\alpha(y))=\alpha^{-1}\circ\mu(\alpha(x),\alpha(y))=\alpha^{-1}\circ\alpha\circ\mu(x,y)=\alpha\circ\mu^{\prime}(x,y).

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
(A_{1},\mu_{1},\alpha_{1})
and
(A_{2},\mu_{2},\alpha_{2})
be two Hom-alternative algebras and
\varphi:
be an invertible morphism of Hom-algebras. If
(A_{1},\mu_{1},\alpha_{1})
is of alternative type and
(A_{1},\mu_{1}^{\prime})
is its compatible alternative algebra then
(A_{2},\mu_{2},\alpha_{2})
is of alternative type with compatible alternative algebra
(A_{2},\mu_{2}^{\prime}=\varphi\circ\mu_{1}^{\prime}\circ(\varphi^{-1}\otimes\varphi^{-1}))
such that
\varphi:(A_{1},\mu_{1}^{\prime})\longrightarrow(A_{2},\mu_{2}^{\prime})

\alpha_{2}\circ\varphi=\varphi\circ\alpha_{1}

\mu_{2}

\mu_{2}\circ\varphi^{\otimes 2}=\varphi\circ\mu_{1}

to
(A_{2},\mu_{2},\alpha_{2}).
It is easy to show that to check that
(A_{2},\mu_{2}^{\prime})
is an alternative algebra. Furthermore

\displaystyle\mu_{2}\circ\varphi^{\otimes 2}=\varphi\circ\mu_{1}=\varphi\circ\alpha_{1}\circ\mu_{1}^{\prime}=\alpha_{2}\circ\varphi\circ\mu_{1}^{\prime}=\alpha_{2}\circ\mu_{2}^{\prime}\circ\varphi^{\otimes 2}

We show that

\mu_{2}^{\prime}=\varphi\circ\mu_{1}^{\prime}\circ(\varphi^{-1}\otimes\varphi^{-1}).
Let denote by

and

respectively. Then

\displaystyle as_{2}(u,u,v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{2}^{\prime}(\mu_{2}^{\prime}(u,u),v)-\mu_{2}^{\prime}(u,\mu_{2}^{\prime}(u,v))=\varphi\circ\mu_{1}^{\prime}(\varphi^{-1}\circ\varphi\circ\mu_{1}^{\prime}(\varphi^{-1}(u),\varphi^{-1}(u)),\varphi^{-1}(v))

\displaystyle-\varphi\circ\mu_{1}^{\prime}(\varphi^{-1}(u),\varphi^{-1}\circ\varphi\circ\mu_{1}^{\prime}(\varphi^{-1}(u),\varphi^{-1}(v)))=\varphi\circ\mu_{1}^{\prime}(\mu_{1}^{\prime}(\varphi^{-1}(u),\varphi^{-1}(u)),\varphi^{-1}(v))

Similarly, we prove that
as_{2}(u,v,v)=0.

Proposition 2.9
.

Given two Hom-alternative algebras
(A,\mu_{A},\alpha)
and
(B,\mu_{B},\beta),
there is a Hom-alternative algebra
(A\oplus B,\mu_{A\oplus B},\alpha+\beta),
where the bilinear map
\mu_{A\oplus B}:(A\oplus B)^{\times 2}\longrightarrow(A\oplus B)
is given by

\displaystyle\mu_{A\oplus B}(a_{1}+b_{1},a_{2}+b_{2})=\mu_{A}(a_{1},a_{2})+\mu_{B}(b_{1},b_{2}),\forall\ a_{1},a_{2}\in A,\ \forall\ b_{1},b_{2}\in B,

and the linear map
(\alpha+\beta):(A\oplus B)\longrightarrow(A\oplus B)
is given by

(3)

\displaystyle(\alpha+\beta)(a+b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\alpha(a)+\beta(b))\ \forall\ (a,b)\in A\times B.

Proof.

First,
(\alpha+\beta)
is multiplicative with respect to
\mu_{A\oplus B}.
Indeed,

\displaystyle(\alpha+\beta)\circ(\mu_{A\oplus B})(a_{1}+b_{1},a_{2}+b_{2})=(\alpha+\beta)(\mu_{A}(a_{1},a_{2})+\mu_{B}(b_{1},b_{2}))

\displaystyle=\alpha\circ\mu_{A}(a_{1},a_{2})+\beta\circ\mu_{B}(b_{1},b_{2})=\mu_{A}(\alpha(a_{1}),\alpha(a_{2}))+\mu_{B}(\beta(b_{1}),\beta(b_{2})

\displaystyle=\mu_{A\oplus B}(\alpha(a_{1})+\beta(b_{1}),\alpha(a_{2})+\beta(b_{2}))=\mu_{A\oplus B}((\alpha+\beta)(a_{1}+b_{1}),(\alpha+\beta)(a_{2}+b_{2}))

Secondly we prove the left Hom-alternativity (
1
) for

as follows

\displaystyle as_{A\oplus B}(a_{1}+b_{1},a_{1}+b_{1},a_{2}+b_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{A\oplus B}(\mu_{A\oplus B}(a_{1}+b_{1},a_{1}+b_{1}),(\alpha+\beta)(a_{2}+b_{2}))

\displaystyle-\mu_{A\oplus B}((\alpha+\beta)(a_{1}+b_{1}),\mu_{A\oplus B}(a_{1}+b_{1},a_{2}+b_{2}))

\displaystyle=\mu_{A\oplus B}(\mu_{A}(a_{1},a_{1})+\mu_{B}(b_{1},b_{1}),(\alpha(a_{2})+\beta(b_{2}))

\displaystyle-\mu_{A\oplus B}(\alpha(a_{1})+\beta(b_{1}),\mu_{A}(a_{1},a_{2})+\mu_{(}b_{1},b_{2}))

\displaystyle\mu_{A}(\mu_{A}(a_{1},a_{1}),\alpha(a_{2}))+\mu_{B}(\mu_{B}(b_{1},b_{1}),\beta(b_{2}))

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
(A,\mu_{A},\alpha)
and
(B,\mu_{B},\beta)
be two Hom-alternative algebras and
\varphi:A\rightarrow B
be a linear map. Denote
by
\Gamma_{\varphi}\subset A\oplus B,
the graph of
\varphi.
Then

\varphi
is a morphism from the Hom-alternative
(A,\mu_{A},\alpha)
to the Hom-alternative algebra
(B,\mu_{B},\beta)
if and only if its graph

\Gamma_{\varphi}
is a Hom-subalgebra of
(A\oplus B,\mu_{A\oplus B},\alpha+\beta).

Proof

Let
\phi:(A,\mu_{A},\alpha)\longrightarrow(B,\mu_{B},\beta)
be a morphism of Hom-alternative algebras.

Then we have for all
u,v\in A,

\mu_{A\oplus B}((u,\varphi(u)),(v,\varphi(v)))=(\mu_{A}(u,v),\mu_{B}(\varphi(u),\varphi(v)))=(\mu_{A}(u,v),\varphi(\mu_{A}(u,v))).

Thus the graph
\Gamma_{\varphi}
is closed under the multiplication
\mu_{A\oplus B}.

Furthermore since
\varphi\circ\alpha=\beta\circ\varphi
, we have
(\alpha\oplus\beta)(u,\varphi(u))=(\alpha(u),\beta\circ\varphi(u))=(\alpha(u),\varphi\circ\alpha(u)),

which implies that
\Gamma_{\varphi}
is closed under
\alpha\oplus\beta.
Thus
\Gamma_{\varphi}
is a Hom-subalgebra of

(A\oplus B,\mu_{A\oplus B},\alpha\oplus\beta).

Conversely, if the graph
\Gamma_{\varphi}\subset A\oplus B
is a
Hom-subalgebra of

(A\oplus B,\mu_{A\oplus B},\alpha\oplus\beta),
then we have

\mu_{A\oplus B}((u,\varphi(u)),(v,\varphi(v)))=(\mu_{A}(u,v),\mu_{B}(\varphi(u),\varphi(v)))\in\Gamma_{\varphi},

which implies that

\mu_{B}(\varphi(u),\varphi(v))=\varphi(\mu_{A}(u,v)).

Furthermore,
(\alpha\oplus\beta)(\Gamma_{\varphi})\subset\Gamma_{\varphi}
implies

(\alpha\oplus\beta)(u,\varphi(u))=(\alpha(u),\beta\circ\varphi(u))\in\Gamma_{\varphi},

which is equivalent to the condition
\beta\circ\phi(u)=\phi\circ\alpha(u),
i.e.
\beta\circ\varphi=\varphi\circ\alpha.
Therefore,
\varphi
is a
morphism of Hom-alternative algebras.
\square

Definition 3.1
.

Let
(A,\mu,\alpha)
be a Hom-alternative algebra. Define its derived sequences as follow:

A^{(0)}=A,\ A^{(1)}=\mu(A,A),\ A^{(2)}=\mu(A^{(1)},A^{(1)}),\cdots,A^{(k)}=\mu(A^{(k-1)},A^{(k-1)}),\cdots

The following elementary result will be very useful.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
(A,\mu,\alpha)
be a Hom-alternative algebra and
k\in\mathbb{N}.
Then

\cdots\subseteq A^{(k+1)}\subseteq A^{(k)}\subseteq A^{(k-1)}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq A^{(2)}\subseteq A^{(1)}\subseteq A

and
A^{(k)}
is a two-sided Hom-ideal of
(A,\mu,\alpha).

Proof.
First, it is clear that
A^{(1)}\subseteq A^{(0)}=A.
Next let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and assume that
A^{(k)}\subseteq A^{(k-1)}.
Then, we have

A^{(k+1)}=\mu(A^{(k)},A^{(k)})\subseteq\mu(A^{(k-1)},A^{(k-1)})=A^{(k)}

To prove that
A^{(k)}
is a two-sided Hom-ideal of
(A,\mu,\alpha)
for every
k\in\mathbb{N},
by the inclusion condition, it suffices to prove the case
n=1.
If note that
A^{(1)}\subseteq A,
we have first

\alpha(A^{(1)})=\alpha(\mu(A,A))=\mu(\alpha(A),\alpha(A))\subseteq\mu(A,A)=A^{(1)}

and next

\mu(A^{(1)},A)\subseteq\mu(A,A)=A^{(1)}\mbox{ and }\mu(A,A^{(1)})\subseteq\mu(A,A)=A^{(1)}.

Thus
A^{(1)}
is a two-sided Hom-ideal of
(A,\mu,\alpha).

Definition 3.3
.

Let
(A,\mu,\alpha)
be a Hom-alternative algebra. Then
(A,\mu,\alpha)
is said to be solvable if
there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
such that
A^{(n)}=\{0\}.

We get the following example of solvable and non solvable Hom-alternative algebras respectively.

Consider the

(2)

and it has no proper two-sided Hom-ideal.

(2)

(A,\mu,\alpha)
is said to be a semi-simple Hom-alternative algebra if

A=A_{1}\oplus A_{2}\oplus A_{2}\oplus\cdots\oplus A_{p}

where

(

) are simple two-sided Hom-ideals of

Let give the following example of non simple Hom-alternative algebra.

Example 3.6
.

Consider the three-dimensional Hom-alternative algebra

over

with basis

\mu(e_{1},e_{1})=e_{1},\ \mu(e_{2},e_{2})=e_{2},\ \mu(e_{3},e_{3})=e_{1},\ \mu(e_{1},e_{3})=\mu(e_{3},e_{1})=-e_{3}

We have the following elementary result which will be used in next sections.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
(A,\mu,\alpha)
be a simple Hom-alternative algebra. Then for each

k\in\mathbb{N},

A^{(k)}=A.

Proof.
Thank to the definition of
A^{(k)},
it suffices to prove that
A^{(1)}=A.

By the simpleness of
(A,\mu,\alpha),
we have
A^{(1)}\neq\{0\}.
Moreover by Lemma
3.2
,
A^{(1)}
is a two-sided Hom-ideal of

The following lemma is useful for next results.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
(A,\mu,\alpha)
be a Hom-alternative algebra. Then

(Ker(\alpha),\mu,\alpha)
is a two-sided Hom-ideal of
(A,\mu,\alpha),
.

Proof.
Obvious,
\alpha(x)=0\in Ker(\alpha)
for all
x\in Ker(\alpha).
Next, let
x,z\in A
and
y\in Ker(\alpha).
Then

\alpha(\mu(x,y))=\mu(\alpha(x),\alpha(y))=\mu(\alpha(x),0)=0
and

\alpha(\mu(y,z))=\mu(\alpha(y),\alpha(z))=\mu(0,\alpha(z))=0.
Thus

\mu(x,y)\in Ker(\alpha)
and
\mu(y,z)\in Ker(\alpha)
and it follows that
(Ker(\alpha),\mu,\alpha)
is a two-sided Hom-ideal.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
(A,\mu,\alpha)
be a finite dimensional simple Hom-alternative algebra. Then the Hom-alternative algebra is of alternative type and

\alpha
is an automorphism of both
(A,\mu,\alpha)
and its induced algebra.

Prof.
By Lemma
3.8
,
Ker(\alpha)
is a two-sided Hom-ideal of the simple Hom-alternative algebra
(A,\mu,\alpha).
Therefore
Ker(\alpha)=\{0\}
or

Ker(\alpha)=A.
Since the Hom-alternative algebra is non trivial, it follows that
Ker(\alpha)=\{0\}
and
\alpha
is an automorphism.
Thus,
A
is of alternative type( see corollary
2.7
).

Let
(A,\mu^{\prime}=\alpha^{-1}\circ\mu)
be the induced (the compatible) alternative algebra of the simple Hom-alternative algebra
(A,\mu,\alpha).
We have

\alpha\circ\mu^{\prime}=\alpha\circ\alpha^{-1}\circ\mu=\alpha^{-1}\circ\mu\circ\alpha^{\otimes 2}=\mu^{\prime}\circ\alpha^{\otimes 2}

i.e.
\alpha
is both automorphism of
(A,\mu^{\prime})
and
(A,\mu,\alpha).

As Hom-associative algebras case
[
25
]
, by the above proposition, there exists an induced alternative algebra of any simple Hom-alternative algebra
(A,\mu,\alpha)
and
\alpha
is an automorphism of the induced alternative algebra. Moreover, their products are mutually determined.

Theorem 3.10
.

Two finite dimensional simple Hom-alternative algebras
(A_{1},\mu_{1},\alpha)
and
(A_{2},\mu_{2},\beta)
are isomorphic if and only if there exists an alternative algebra
isomorphism
\varphi:A_{1}\rightarrow A_{2}
(between their induced alternative algebras) which renders conjugate the two alternative algebra automorphisms
\alpha
and
\beta
that is

\varphi\circ\alpha=\beta\circ\varphi.

Proof.
Since
(A_{1},\mu_{1},\alpha)
and
(A_{2},\mu_{2},\beta)
are finite dimensional simple Hom-alternative algebras, they are of alternative-type. Let
(A_{1},\mu_{1}^{\prime})
and
(A_{2},\mu_{2}^{\prime})
be their induced alternative algebras respectively. Suppose that
\varphi:(A_{1},\mu_{1},\alpha)\rightarrow(A_{2},\mu_{2},\beta)
is an isomorphism of Hom-alternative algebras, then
\varphi\circ\alpha=\beta\circ\varphi,
thus

\beta^{-1}\circ\varphi=\varphi\circ\alpha^{-1}.
Moreover,

\varphi\circ\mu_{1}^{\prime}=\varphi\circ\alpha^{-1}\circ\alpha\circ\mu_{1}^{\prime}=\varphi\circ\alpha^{-1}\circ\mu_{1}=\beta^{-1}\circ\varphi\circ\mu_{1}=\beta^{-1}\circ\mu_{2}\circ\varphi^{\otimes 2}=\mu_{2}^{\prime}\circ\varphi^{\otimes 2}.

So,
\varphi
is an isomorphism between the induced Hom-alternative algebras.

On the other hand, if there exists an
isomorphism between the induced alternative algebras satisfying

\varphi\circ\alpha=\beta\circ\varphi,
then

\varphi\circ\mu_{1}=\varphi\circ\alpha\circ\mu_{1}^{\prime}=\beta\circ\varphi\circ\mu_{1}^{\prime}=\beta\circ\mu_{2}^{\prime}\circ\varphi^{\otimes 2}=\mu_{2}\circ\varphi^{\otimes 2}.

Structures of Hom-alternative algebras

In this section, we discuss about necessary and sufficient conditions for
Hom-alternative algebras to be solvable, simple and semi-simple. This study is inspired by the one done in
[

]
.
In the classical case, we already know that every simple alternative
algebra is either an associative algebra or a Cayley-Dickson algebra over its center
[
17
]
,
[

. As it turns out, there is one nonassociative simple alternative algebras. Recall that the Cayley-Dickson algebras are a sequence

of non-associative

The first three Cayley-Dickson algebras are associative and and

Proposition 4.1
.

[

]
.
Let

two-sided Hom-ideal of

\mu

Next, pick

Then the left alternativity
(
1

is proved as follows

\displaystyle as_{A/I}(\bar{x},\bar{x},\bar{y})

\displaystyle\bar{u}(\bar{u}(\bar{x},\bar{x}),\bar{\alpha}(\bar{y}))-\bar{\mu}(\bar{\alpha}(\bar{x}),\bar{\mu}(\bar{x},\bar{y})

\displaystyle\overline{\mu(\mu(x,x)\alpha(y))-\mu(\alpha(x),\mu(x,y))}=\overline{as_{A}(x,x,y))}=\bar{0}.

Hence we get (
1
) for
(A/I,\bar{\mu},\bar{\alpha}).
Similarly, we get (
2

\bullet

(1)

(2)

Let

Proof.

(1)

,
\alpha
is both automorphism

4.3

3.7

and thus,

is semi-simple.

Now, by the semi-simpleness of

In other words,

(2)

By Proposition
2.5

Assume that

\alpha_{1}((\bar{x},h)):=(\overline{\alpha(x)},0).
Then one can show that
(A_{1},\mu_{1},\alpha_{1})
is a Hom-alternative algebra which is a decomposition of direct sum of alternative algebras. Next, let
y\in Ker(\alpha)\cap Im(\alpha).
Then, there exists
x\in A
such that
y=\alpha(x).
Moreover, we have

0=\alpha(y)=\alpha^{2}(x)=\alpha(x)=y.

It follows that
Ker(\alpha)\cap Im(\alpha)=\{0\}
and then

A=Ker(\alpha)\oplus Im(\alpha)
since for any
x\in A
we have
x=(x-\alpha(x))+\alpha(x)
with
x-\alpha(x)\in Ker(\alpha)
and
\alpha(x)\in Im(\alpha).

Now, let show that
(Im(\alpha),\mu,\alpha)\cong(V/Ker(\alpha),\bar{\mu},\bar{\alpha}).
Note that, it is clear that
(Im(\alpha),\mu,\alpha)
is a Hom-alternative algebra. Define

\varphi:V:Ker(\alpha)\rightarrow Im(\alpha)
by
\varphi(\bar{x})=\alpha(x)
for all
\bar{x}\in A/Ker(\alpha).
Clearly,

\varphi
is bijective and for all
\bar{x},\bar{y}\in A/Ker(\alpha),
we have

\varphi(\bar{\mu}(\bar{x},\bar{y}))=\varphi(\overline{\mu(x,u)})=\alpha(\mu(x,y))=\mu(\alpha(x),\alpha(y))=\mu(\varphi(\bar{x}),\varphi(\bar{y}))

\varphi(\bar{\alpha}(\bar{x}))=\varphi(\overline{\alpha(x)})=\alpha^{2}(x)=\alpha(\varphi(\bar{x})),

i.e.
\varphi\circ\bar{\mu}=\bar{\mu}\circ\varphi^{\otimes 2}
and

\varphi\circ\bar{\alpha}=\alpha\circ\varphi.
It follows that

(Im(\alpha),\mu,\alpha)\cong(V/Ker(\alpha),\bar{\mu},\bar{\alpha})
and therefore
V=Ker(\alpha)\oplus Im(\alpha)\cong(A/Ker(\alpha))\oplus Ker(\alpha).

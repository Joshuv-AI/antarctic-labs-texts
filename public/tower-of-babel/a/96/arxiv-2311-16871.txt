Improvements on dimension growth results and effective Hilbert’s irreducibility theorem
Raf Cluckers, Pierre Dèbes, Yotam I. Hendel, Kien Huu Nguyen, Floris Vermeulen

Abstract
. We sharpen and generalize the dimension growth bounds for the number of points of bounded height lying on an irreducible algebraic variety of degree d d , over any global field. In particular, we focus on the the affine hypersurface situation by relaxing the condition on the top degree homogeneous part of the polynomial describing the affine hypersurface. Our work sharpens the dependence on the degree in the bounds, compared to [ CCDN20 ] . We also formulate a conjecture about plane curves which gives a conjectural approach to the uniform degree 3 3 case (the only case which remains open). For induction on dimension, we develop a higher dimensional effective version of Hilbert’s irreducibility theorem.

1.1.
Improvements to affine dimension growth

If
X\subset{\mathbb{A}}^{n}_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
is an affine variety, define

N_{\rm aff}(X,B)

as the number of points
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
on
X({\mathbb{Z}})
satisfying
|x_{i}|\leq B
for

. Also write
N_{\rm aff}(f,B)
for

when

is a hypersurface given by a polynomial

In previous results about affine dimension growth for a hypersurface given by
f=0
of degree
d\geq 2
, one typically assume that the degree
d
part
f_{d}
of

is absolutely irreducible, see
[
BHBS06
,
Sal15
,
Sal23a
,

,
PS22b
]
. We relax the conditions on

, see

Our first variant of affine dimension growth uses a condition on

which we coin NCC, for ‘not cylindrical over a curve’. This condition means that

depends non-trivially on at least three variables in any affine coordinate system over our base field, which is
{\mathbb{Q}}
up to

.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
f
be a polynomial over
{\mathbb{Q}}
in
n\geq 3

the affine hypersurface cut by

. Say that

is
cylindrical over a curve
if there exists a
{\mathbb{Q}}
-linear map
\ell:{\mathbb{A}}^{n}_{\mathbb{Q}}\to{\mathbb{A}}^{2}_{\mathbb{Q}}
and a curve

in
{\mathbb{A}}^{2}_{\mathbb{Q}}
such that

. We abbreviate

by NCC and say that

is NCC if

Note that

is cylindrical over a curve if and only if there exist a polynomial

and linear forms
\ell(x),\ell^{\prime}(x)
over
{\mathbb{Q}}
such that
f(x)=g(\ell(x),\ell^{\prime}(x))

Definition 1.4
.

Let

be a polynomial over
{\mathbb{Q}}
and let

is

if

does not have any factors of degree
\leq r
over
{\mathbb{Q}}

The following is our main result on affine dimension growth, for NCC hypersurfaces.

Theorem 1.5

(Affine dimension growth)
.

Given
n\geq 3
an integer, there exist constants
c=c(n),e=e(n)
such that for all polynomials
f
in
{\mathbb{Z}}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]
of degree

so that

is irreducible over

and NCC, and whose homogeneous degree

is

\displaystyle N_{\rm aff}(f,B)

\displaystyle\leq cd^{7}B^{n-2},

\displaystyle\text{ if }d\geq 5,

\displaystyle N_{\rm aff}(f,B)

\displaystyle\leq cB^{n-2}(\log B)^{e},

\displaystyle\text{ if }d=4,

\displaystyle N_{\rm aff}(f,B)

\displaystyle\leq cB^{n-3+2\sqrt{3}}(\log B)^{e},

\displaystyle\text{ if }d=3.

If
f_{d}
is merely
1
-irreducible, then we still have that

\displaystyle N_{\rm aff}(f,B)

\displaystyle\leq cd^{7}B^{n-2}(\log B)^{e},

\displaystyle\text{ if }d\geq 4,

\displaystyle N_{\rm aff}(f,B)

\displaystyle\leq cB^{n-3+2\sqrt{3}}(\log B)^{e},

\displaystyle\text{ if }d=3.

Remark 1.6

If

1.2.
A conjectural approach

Our second affine variant is given by

For curves, recall the following variant of a classical finiteness result, which follows from Siegel’s theorem as stated in e.g.

Theorem 1.7

(Siegel)
.

Given

in
{\mathbb{Z}}[x,y]
whose homogeneous part of highest degree is
2

Also recall a conjecture by W. M. Schmidt, just below (1.4) in

Motivated by the finiteness result of

Conjecture 1.8

Fix

and

. Then there exists a constant

such that for all

in
{\mathbb{Z}}[x,y]
of degree

is

The case of Pell or Thue equations forms some evidence for Conjecture

We obtain affine dimension growth results for those values

Theorem 1.9

(Affine dimension growth, assuming

)
.

Fix

and

and suppose that

holds for this

1.3.
Higher dimensional effective variants of Hilbert’s irreducibility theorem

Given a polynomial
f
over
{\mathbb{Q}}
, let
||f||
denote the height of its tuple of coefficients in projective space. Theorems
1.10
and
1.12
are our main results on higher dimensional effective Hilbert’s irreducibility theorem

Theorem 1.10
.

Let
F\in{\mathbb{Z}}[T,Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{n}]
be irreducible in
{\mathbb{Q}}[T,Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{n}]
such that
d=\deg_{Y}F\geq 1
. For an integer
B^{\prime}\geq 0
denote by
S_{T}(F,B^{\prime})
the number of
t\in{\mathbb{Z}}
with
|t|\leq B^{\prime}
for which
F(t,Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{n})
is reducible in
{\mathbb{Q}}[Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{n}]
. Then

S_{T}(F,B^{\prime})\ll_{n}2^{e(n)d}d^{e(n)}(\log||F||+1)^{e(n)}(B^{\prime})^{1/2}.

where
e=e(n)
is a constant depending only on
n
.

As a corollary, we obtain an effective version of Hilbert’s irreducibility theorem. Since we will need it for multiple polynomials at the same time, we formulate it in a slightly more general way than the usual irreducibility theorem.

Corollary 1.11
.

Let
F_{1},\ldots,F_{r}
be irreducible polynomials in
{\mathbb{Q}}[T_{1},Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{n}]
of degree
d
with
n\geq 1
. Suppose that each
F_{i}
remains irreducible in
{\mathbb{Q}}(T)[Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{n}]
. Then there exists an integer
t_{1}
of height at most polynomial in
\log||F_{1}||,\ldots,\log||F_{r}||
, with degrees depending on
n
, and coefficients depending on
d,n,r
, such that for each
i=1,\ldots,r
, the polynomial
F(t_{1},Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{n})
is irreducible over
{\mathbb{Q}}
.

The proof of
1.10
is based on the base case with
n=1
from
[
Wal05
]
,
[
PS22a
]
and on the use of Kronecker transforms. The planar case with

[

]
,
[
DW08
]
,
[
SZ95
]

[

]

Finally, we also obtain the following result resembling both Bertini and effective Hilbert’s irreducibility theorem, which does not include dependence on the height of

Theorem 1.12

Let

such that if

The proof of

Proposition 2.5

We prove this result by induction, where the base case is

where

, and

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{|k|\leq d^{2}(d-1)B}N_{\rm aff}\left(X\cap\{\ell_{a}(x)=k\},B\right)

Write

. If

We also have

Let

and fix

. We count the number of lines in

over

]

(3.3)

At the same time, for each

By our discussion,

It is now left to bound

, for each

.

\sum_{i=1}^{d_{\ell}}n_{\ell,i}\dfrac{4B}{i}\ll\sum_{i=1}^{d_{\ell}}d_{\ell}i\dfrac{4B}{i}\ll\sum_{i=1}^{d_{\ell}}d_{\ell}B\leq d_{\ell}^{2}B.

(3.5)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=d_{\ell}}^{2B}n_{\ell,i}\dfrac{4B}{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=d_{\ell}}^{2B-1}\left(\sum_{i=d_{\ell}}^{k}n_{\ell,i}\right)\left(\dfrac{4B}{k}-\dfrac{4B}{k+1}\right)+\left(\sum_{i=d_{\ell}}^{2B}n_{\ell,i}\right)\dfrac{4B}{2B}

\displaystyle\ll d_{\ell}^{2}\left(\left(\sum_{k=d_{\ell}}^{2B-1}k^{\frac{2}{d_{\ell}}}\log(k)^{O(1)}\dfrac{B}{k(k+1)}\right)+B^{\frac{2}{d_{\ell}}}\log(B)^{O(1)}\right).

.
We now consider two cases, depending on whether

Assume first that

is universally bounded, hence

is a (possibly reducible) curve of degree at most

\ll d^{9/2}.

Now assume that

\displaystyle\ll\deg(C)(\log B)^{3}B^{1/2}(\log(B)+\log B)

\displaystyle\ll B^{1/\sqrt{d}}B^{1/2}(\log B)^{O(1)}.

Distinguishing between

\displaystyle\ll B,

\displaystyle\text{ if }d\geq 5,

\displaystyle\ll B\log(B)^{O(1)},

Proposition 3.4

We first need several lemmas.

Lemma 3.5

Let

Let

In the situation of the proposition, we say that

is
(n-2)
-ruled

is the union of all

-planes contained in it. Equivalently,

contains infinitely many
(n-2)
-planes. We first bound the number of
(n-2)
-planes on

which do not come from an
(n-2)
-ruling of
X
. Let
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
be a hypersurface and consider the Fano variety

F_{n-2}(X):=\{L\in{\rm Gr}(n-1,n+1)\mid L\subset X\}

of

We first bound the number of

, which are the

-planes on

which do not belong to an

-ruling of

. We will call such a plane an

]

As usual, the product formula shows that this is well-defined. If

For a polynomial

S_{T}(F,B^{\prime})\ll_{K,n}2^{O_{n}(d)}d^{O_{n}(1)}(\log H_{K}(F)+1)^{O_{n}(1)}(B^{\prime})^{1/2}.

Proof.

Put
a=1+\max_{i}\deg_{Y_{i}}F
and consider the Kronecker transform of
F

\Kr(F)=F(T,Y,Y^{a},\ldots,Y^{a^{n-1}})\in{\mathcal{O}}_{K}[T,Y].

Note that
H_{K}(\Kr(F))=H_{K}(F)
, that
\deg_{T}(\Kr(F))=\deg_{T}(F)
, and that

\deg_{Y}(\Kr(F))\leq(a-1)+(a-1)a+\ldots(a-1)a^{n-1}=a^{n}-1.

Let

\Kr(F)=\prod_{i=1}^{m}Q_{i}(T,Y)

be an irreducible factorization of
\Kr(F)
in
K[T,Y]
. We have that
m\leq\deg_{Y}(\Kr(F))
and for each
i
that

\deg_{T}(Q_{i})\leq\deg_{T}(\Kr(F)),\quad\deg_{Y}(Q_{i})\leq\deg_{Y}(\Kr(F)).

Also, by combining
[
Lan83
, Ch.3, Prop. 2.4]
and
[
PS22a
, Lemma 2.1]

we have

H_{K}(Q_{i})\ll_{K,n}H_{K}(Q_{i})H_{K}(\prod_{j\not=i}Q_{j})\ll_{K,n}4^{d_{K}^{2}(\deg Kr(F))^{2}}H_{K}(Kr(F)).

We now follow the proof of
[
FJ23
, Lem. 13.1.3]
(see the note right after
[
FJ23
, Equation (13.2)]
),
which gives for each non-empty proper subset
I\subset\{1,\ldots,m\}
a non-zero polynomial
c_{I}(T)\in K[T]
such that
\deg c_{I}\leq\deg_{T}F

with the following property: let
t_{0}\in K
and assume that for each
i=1,\ldots,m
, the polynomial
Q_{i}(t_{0},Y)
is irreducible in
K[Y]
, and for each non-empty proper subset
I\subset\{1,\ldots,m\}
,
c_{I}(t_{0})\neq 0
, then
F(t_{0},Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{n})
is irreducible in
K[Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{n}]
.
In other words, we have that

S_{T}(F,B^{\prime})\leq\sum_{i}S_{T}(Q_{i},B^{\prime})+\sum_{I}\deg c_{I}.

We conclude by using
[
PS22a
, Thm. 1.1]
to bound
S_{T}(Q_{i},B^{\prime})
and the discussion above to bound
\deg c_{I}
.
∎

for global fields.

Proof of
1.11

By the previous theorem, the number of

Theorem 4.6
.

Let

Remark 4.7
.

In particular, if

, we can deduce a version (up to a change of variables) of Hilbert’s irreducibilty theorem for

with

Factor

into absolutely irreducible (homogeneous) factors and let

be the variety cut by

in
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
. By Lemma

, for each

of degree at most

such that if

, then

, then

.

be an irreducible component of

4.3.
The NCC condition

In this section we show that the condition of being NCC is preserved when cutting with a well-chosen hyperplane, and with most of its translates. This allows us to use induction in Section
4.4
. In fact, using the theory of Hilbert schemes it is easy to prove such a non-effective result in characteristic zero, see e.g.
[
Sal
]
. However, proving an effective variant which also holds in positive characteristic is much more technical.

Fix an algebraic closure
\overline{K}
of
K
, and a separable closure
K^{\operatorname{sep}}\subset\overline{K}
. Recall the condition of being NCC is defined as follows.

Definition 4.8
.

Let
f\in K[x_{1},...,x_{n}]
for some
n\geq 3
. We say that
f
is NCC over
K
if there does not exist a
K
-linear map
\ell:{\mathbb{A}}_{K}^{n}\to{\mathbb{A}}_{K}^{2}
and a curve
C
in
{\mathbb{A}}_{K}^{2}
such that
V(f)=\ell^{-1}(C)
.

Equivalently,
f\in K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
is NCC over
K
if there do not exist
K
-linear forms
\ell_{1}(x),\ell_{2}(x)
on
K^{n}
and a polynomial
g\in K[y_{1},y_{2}]
such that
f(x)=g(\ell_{1}(x),\ell_{2}(x))
.

We denote by
({\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}_{K})^{*}
the dual space of
{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}_{K}
, which parametrizes hyperplanes in
{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}_{K}
, or in
{\mathbb{A}}^{n}_{K}
through the origin. We identify points of
({\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}_{K})^{*}(K)
and linear forms in
n
variables over
K
.
If
S
is a finite subset of
({\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}_{K})^{*}(\overline{K})
, then we define

H_{S}=\bigcup_{\ell\in S}V(\ell)\subset{\mathbb{A}}^{n},

which is the union of all hyperplanes in
{\mathbb{A}}^{n}
defined by a linear form in
S
(we always take
H_{S}
to be reduced).

We begin by restating the NCC condition, and show that being NCC over
K
is equivalent to being NCC over
K^{\operatorname{sep}}
.

Notation 4.9
.

Let
f\in K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be a non-constant polynomial and let
f_{i}
denote the homogenous degree
i
part of
f
. Let
I:=\{i:f_{i}\neq 0\}
. We set,

W(f):=\bigcup_{i\in I}V(f_{i})_{\operatorname{red}}\subset{\mathbb{A}}^{n}_{K},

where
V(f_{i})_{\operatorname{red}}
denotes the reduced scheme cut by the radical of
(f_{i})
in
{\mathbb{A}}_{K}^{n}
.

Note that
W(f)
is always a union of cones through the origin, since we consider
W(f)
in affine space.

Proposition 4.10
.

Let
n\geq 3
, and let
f\in K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be a non-constant polynomial.
Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

f
is not NCC over
K
.

(2)

There exists a line
L\subset({\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}_{K})^{*}
defined over
K
and a finite subset

S\subset L(\overline{K})

such that
W(f)=H_{S}
.

Proof.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(2): Suppose that
f
is not NCC. Then there exist distinct elements
P=(a_{1}:\ldots:a_{n}),Q=(b_{1}:\ldots:b_{n})
in
({\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}_{K})^{*}(K)
corresponding to
K
-linear forms
\ell_{1}(x):=\sum_{m}a_{m}x_{m}
and
\ell_{2}(x):=\sum_{m}b_{m}x_{m}
, and a polynomial
g
in
K[y_{1},y_{2}]
such that

f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=g(\ell_{1}(x),\ell_{2}(x)).

Let
L
be the line in
({\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}_{K})^{*}
through
P
and
Q
. Write
f=f_{0}+f_{1}+\ldots+f_{d}
where
f_{i}
is the homogeneous degree
i
part of
f
, and similarly for
g_{i}
. Then
f_{i}(x)=g_{i}(\ell_{1}(x),\ell_{2}(x))
,
for every
i=0,\ldots,d
. Since every
homogeneous polynomial in two variables splits over
\overline{K}
, each
g_{i}
splits over
\overline{K}
, and we have that if
f_{i}
is non-constant then

V(f_{i})_{\operatorname{red}}=V(g_{i}(\ell_{1}(x),\ell_{2}(x)))_{\operatorname{red}}=H_{S_{i}},

where
S_{i}
is some finite subset of
L(\overline{K})
. Hence
W(f)=H_{\cup_{i}S_{i}}
.

(2)
\Rightarrow
(1): For the converse, suppose that (2) holds with
P=(a_{1}:\ldots:a_{n}),Q=(b_{1}:\ldots:b_{n})\in({\mathbb{P}}_{K}^{n-1})^{*}(K)
on the line
L
, and
S\subset L(\overline{K})
a finite set. Let
\ell_{1}(x):=\sum_{m}a_{m}x_{m}
and
\ell_{2}(x):=\sum_{m}b_{m}x_{m}
denote the corresponding
K
-linear forms.

Writing
f=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{d}f_{i}
where
f_{i}
is homogeneous of degree
i
as before, it is enough to show that for each
i
, we have
f_{i}=g_{i}(\ell_{1}(x),\ell_{2}(x))
where
g_{i}\in K[y_{1},y_{2}]
.
Fix
i
such that
f_{i}
is non-constant. Each
\ell\in S
can be written as a
\overline{K}
-linear combination of
\ell_{1},\ell_{2}
. Therefore, since

V(f_{i})_{\operatorname{red}}\subseteq W(f)=H_{S}=\bigcup\limits_{\ell\in S}V(\ell),
there exists a polynomial

h_{i}\in\overline{K}[y_{1},y_{2}]
such that
f_{i}(x)=h_{i}(\ell_{1}(x),\ell_{2}(x))
.
Write

f_{i}(x)=h_{i}\left(\ell_{1}(x),\ell_{2}(x)\right)=\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}h_{ij}\left(\ell_{1}(x),\ell_{2}(x)\right),

where
\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j}
forms a
K
-basis for
\overline{K}
,
\lambda_{1}=1
, and
h_{ij}\in K[y_{1},y_{2}]
.
Since
f_{i}
is defined over
K
, and
h_{ij}\left(\ell_{1}(x),\ell_{2}(x)\right)\in K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
, we get by the linear independence of
\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j}
that
h_{ij}=0
unless
j=1
.
Therefore
h_{i}(y_{1},y_{2})=h_{i1}(y_{1},y_{2})
is defined over
K
as required.
∎

We now move to showing that a hypersurface is NCC over
K
if and only if it is NCC over the separable closure
K^{\operatorname{sep}}
. We first prove a proposition which characterizes the dimension of the smallest
K
-linear space containing a given
\overline{K}
-point
P
in
\mathbb{P}^{m}_{K}(\overline{K})
.

Proposition 4.11
.

Let
P=(a_{1}:\ldots:a_{m+1})\in\mathbb{P}^{m}(\overline{K})
be a point, and define

\delta_{K}(P):=\dim\mathrm{span}_{K}\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{m+1}\}
. Then
P
lies on a unique
K
-linear space of dimension
\delta_{K}(P)-1
, and is not contained in any
K
-linear space of smaller dimension.

Proof.

First note that
\delta_{K}(P)
is well defined, since for any non-zero
\lambda\in\overline{K}
the collection
\{a_{i_{j}}\}_{j}
is linearly independent if and only if
\{\lambda a_{i_{j}}\}_{j}
is linearly independent.
For
\delta_{K}(P)=1
the statement is clear;
\delta_{K}(P)=1
if and only if
P
is defined over
K
. Let
s>1
and assume by induction that the statement holds for every point
P^{\prime}
with
\delta_{K}(P^{\prime})<s
.

If
\delta_{K}(P)=s
, we may assume without loss of generality that
\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{s}\}
forms a
K
-basis for
\mathrm{span}_{K}\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{m+1}\}
, and write
a_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{s}a_{i}b_{ij}
for each
j
, and some
b_{ij}\in K
. We get
K
-points
Q_{i}=(b_{i1}:\ldots:b_{i\,m+1})\in\mathbb{P}^{m}(K)
, where
1\leq i\leq s
.
Note that
b_{ij}=1
if
i=j
and
b_{ij}=0
if
i\neq j
and
j\leq s
. Therefore
\{Q_{i}\}_{i=1}^{s}
are linearly independent over
K
, and the
K
-linear space
L\subseteq{\mathbb{P}}^{m}
spanned by
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{s}
has dimension
s-1
, and contains
P
.
If
P
is contained in a different
K
-linear space
L^{\prime}
of dimension
s-1
, then we have linearly independent points
Q^{\prime}_{i}=(b^{\prime}_{i1}:\ldots:b^{\prime}_{i\,m+1})\in L^{\prime}(K)
and we may write
a_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{s}\lambda_{i}b^{\prime}_{ij}

for each
j
and some
\lambda_{i}\in\overline{K}
. Since
\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{s}\}\subset\mathrm{span}_{K}\{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{s}\}
is a
K
-basis, we may write
\lambda_{j}=\sum_{k=1}^{s}a_{k}\alpha_{jk}
where
\alpha_{jk}\in K
. Rewriting, we get for each
j
,

\sum_{i=1}^{s}a_{i}b_{ij}=a_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{s}\lambda_{i}b^{\prime}_{ij}=a_{1}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{s}\alpha_{k1}b^{\prime}_{kj}\right)+\ldots+a_{s}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{s}\alpha_{ks}b^{\prime}_{kj}\right).

Since
\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{s}\}
is a
K
-basis, we get

b_{ij}=\sum_{k=1}^{s}\alpha_{ki}b^{\prime}_{kj}

for all
i,j,k
, implying that
\{Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{s}\}\subset L^{\prime}
. It follows that
L=L^{\prime}
is unique, and that
P
is not contained in any
K
-linear space of dimension smaller than
s-1
, as that would contradict the uniqueness of
L
.

If
P
is contained in a unique
K
-linear space
L
of dimension
s-1
, then
\delta_{K}(P)\geq s
since otherwise there exists by induction a
K
-linear space of dimension smaller than
s-1
containing
P
, and therefore there are infinitely many
K
-linear spaces of dimension
s-1
on which
P
lies. Since
L
is defined over
K
,
there exist points
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{s}\in L(K)
, with
Q_{i}=(b_{i1}:\ldots:b_{i\,m+1})
where we may assume
b_{ij}\in K
for all
i,j
, and scalars
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{s}\in\overline{K}
such that

P=\lambda_{1}Q_{1}+\ldots+\lambda_{s}Q_{s}.

We get
a_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{s}\lambda_{i}b_{ij}
for each
j
, implying
\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{m+1}\}\subset\mathrm{span}_{K}\{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{s}\}
,
and therefore
\delta_{K}(P)\leq s
, implying
\delta_{K}(P)=s
.
∎

Proposition 4.12
.

Let
n\geq 3
, and let
f\in K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be a non-constant polynomial. Then
f
is NCC over
K
if and only if
f
is NCC over
K^{\operatorname{sep}}
.

Proof.

If
f
is not NCC over
K
, then clearly it is not NCC over
K^{\operatorname{sep}}
.

If
f
is not NCC over
K^{\operatorname{sep}}
, by Proposition
4.10
there exists a line
L\subset({\mathbb{P}}^{n-1})^{*}
defined over
K^{\operatorname{sep}}

and a finite set
S\subset L(\overline{K})

such that
W(f)=H_{S}
.
Since
f
is defined over
K
, we have that

\sigma(W(f))=W(f)
for all
\sigma\in{\rm Aut}(\overline{K}/K)
and therefore
\sigma(S)=S
for all
\sigma\in{\rm Aut}(\overline{K}/K)
.

If
|S|\geq 2
, since
S
consists of points on the line
L
, then the automorphism group
{\rm Aut}(\overline{K}/K)
must preserve
L
.
Since
L
is defined over
K^{\operatorname{sep}}
, this implies that
\sigma(L)=L
for every
\sigma\in{\rm Gal}(K^{\operatorname{sep}}/K)
. Hence
4.4
implies that
L
can be defined over
K
, which proves the required statement using Proposition
4.10
.

We may thus assume that
S=\{\ell\}
is a singleton. Then we have that
\sigma(\ell)=\ell
for every
\sigma\in{\rm Aut}(\overline{K}/K)
. Write
\ell=(c_{1}:\ldots:c_{n})
, then without loss of generality we may assume that
c_{1}=1
. It follows that
\sigma(c_{i})=c_{i}
for every
\sigma\in{\rm Aut}(\overline{K}/K)
.

Consider now
\delta_{K^{\operatorname{sep}}}(\ell)
as in Proposition
4.11
. Since
\ell
lies on the
K^{\operatorname{sep}}
-line
L
, then
\delta_{K^{\operatorname{sep}}}(\ell)\leq 2
. If
\delta_{K^{\operatorname{sep}}}(\ell)=1
, then
\ell
is defined over
K^{\operatorname{sep}}
, and therefore
\ell
is defined over
K
, since every element of
{\rm Gal}(K^{\operatorname{sep}}/K)
fixes
\ell
. We may thus take any line defined over
K
which passes through
\ell
to deduce Condition (2) of Proposition
4.10
.

Assume
\delta_{K^{\operatorname{sep}}}(\ell)=2
. By Proposition
4.11
, there exists a unique line
L
defined over
K^{\operatorname{sep}}
which contains
\ell
. Since for each
\sigma\in{\rm Aut}(\overline{K}/K)
we have

\sigma(\ell)=\ell
, the line

\sigma(L)
is defined over

and
\ell^{\prime}
with
W^{\prime}
has at most
O_{n}(d^{C_{K}})
points. It follows there are at most
O_{n}(d^{C_{K}})
values
t\in K
for which
f_{d}|_{V(\ell+t\ell^{\prime})}
is not
r
-irreducible.
The claim now follows using

4.14
(2),(3).
∎

Proof of
4.1
.

Recall that we are given an irreducible
f\in{\mathcal{O}}_{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
of degree
d\geq 3
whose highest degree part is
2
-irreducible (or
1
-irreducible) over
K
and we wish to bound the number of
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
-points on
X=V(f)
of height at most
B
. As usual, we can assume that
f
is absolutely irreducible. We follow the same proof strategy as for
1.5
.

Let us first assume that
d>\kappa
, for some constant
\kappa=O_{K,n}(1)
. We use
[
PS22b
, Thm. 5.14]
to find an auxiliary polynomial
g
of degree at most

\ll_{K,n}d^{e_{1}}B^{\frac{1}{d^{n-1}}}

which vanishes on all integral points of
X
of height at most
B
, where
e_{1}=4
in characteristic
0
and
e_{1}=7
in positive characteristic. We then count integral points on all irreducible components of
X\cap V(g)
as in the proof of
1.5
. The components of degree
1
contribute at most
O_{K,n}(d^{e}B^{n-2})
if
f_{d}
is
2
-irreducible, and at most
O_{K,n}(d^{e}B^{n-2}\log(B)^{O_{K},n(1)})
if
f_{d}
is
1
-irreducible, where
e=7
in characteristic
0
and
e=11
in positive characteristic. This larger exponent for positive characteristic comes from the fact that in
3.6
one uses Noether forms, which are of degree
O(d^{6})
in positive characteristic
[
Kal95
]
rather than of degree
d^{2}

[
Rup86
]
, see
2.7
. For the components of higher degree, one reasons exactly as in the proof of
1.5
.

Assume now that
d\leq\kappa=O_{K,n}(1)
. If
f_{d}
is
1
-irreducible, or
f_{d}
is
2
-irreducible and its absolutely irreducible factors can be defined over

, we follow the proof of
1.5
by using induction,
4.18
and the Schwartz–Zippel bounds.
If
f_{d}
is
2
-irreducible and it has an absolutely irreducible factor which cannot be defined over

, the induction argument stays the same, where we now use
4.3
to preserve
2
-irreducibility (together with
4.14
to preserve NCC), instead of using
4.18
which is based on
4.6
.
We note that in the latter case (with

) we may assume
\log H_{K}(f)\ll_{K,n}\log B
, since otherwise we may use arguments similar to

[

, Theorem 4]
to get

.
∎

Theorem 4.19

(Uniform dimension growth for projective varieties)
.

Let
K
be a global field. Let
e=7
if
\charac K=0
and
e=11
if
\charac K>0
. Given
n\geq 3
an integer, there exist constants
c=c(n,K)
and
\kappa=\kappa(n,K)
such that for any integral hypersurface
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{K}^{n}
, for all
B\geq 2
that

\displaystyle N(X,B)

\displaystyle\leq cd^{e}B^{d_{K}(n-1)},

\displaystyle\text{ if }d\geq 5,

\displaystyle N(X,B)

\displaystyle\leq cB^{d_{K}(n-1)}(\log B)^{\kappa},

\displaystyle\text{ if }d=4,

\displaystyle N(X,B)

\displaystyle\leq cB^{d_{K}(n-2+2\sqrt{3})}(\log B)^{\kappa},

\displaystyle\text{ if }d=3.

Proof.

Follows from
4.1
.
∎

4.1
, where we change the conditions on
f_{d}
to being
2

Theorem 4.20
.

Let
K
be a global field with ring of integers
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
. Let
e=7
if
\charac K=0
and
e=11
if
\charac K>0
. Given
n\geq 3
an integer, there exist constants
c=c(n)
and
\kappa=\kappa(n)
such that for all polynomials
f
in
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]
of degree
d\geq 3
such that
f
is irreducible over
K
and NCC, and, whose homogeneous degree
d
part
f_{d}
contains at least one absolutely irreducible factor of degree
>2
, one has for all
B\geq 2
that

\displaystyle N_{\rm aff}(f,B)

\displaystyle\leq cd^{e}B^{n-2},

\displaystyle\text{ if }d\geq 5,

\displaystyle N_{\rm aff}(f,B)

\displaystyle\leq cB^{n-2}(\log B)^{\kappa},

\displaystyle\text{ if }d=4,

\displaystyle N_{\rm aff}(f,B)

\displaystyle\leq cB^{n-3+2\sqrt{3}}(\log B)^{\kappa},

\displaystyle\text{ if }d=3.

If
f_{d}
merely has at least one absolutely irreducible factor degree
>1
, then

\displaystyle N_{\rm aff}(f,B)

\displaystyle\leq cd^{e}B^{n-2}(\log B)^{\kappa},

\displaystyle\text{ if }d\geq 4,

\displaystyle N_{\rm aff}(f,B)

\displaystyle\leq cB^{n-3+2\sqrt{3}}(\log B)^{\kappa},

\displaystyle\text{ if }d=3.

Proof.

We follow the proof of
4.1
, where the key point is that
3.4
, about counting on
(n-2)
-planes, still holds. So let us prove this proposition under these weaker assumptions on
f_{d}
.

As usual, we may assume that
f
is absolutely irreducible. Let
X\subset{\mathbb{A}}^{n}_{K}
be the affine variety
V(f)
, and let
X_{\infty}\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}_{K}
be the intersection of
X
with the hyperplane at infinity. By assumption there exists a geometrically irreducible component
Y
of
X_{\infty}
of degree at least
3
. If
f_{d}
contains an irreducible factor of degree
1
or
2
, then it also has an absolutely irreducible factor of degree
1
or
2
, let
Z\subset X_{\infty}
be the corresponding geometrically irreducible component.
We claim that there are only finitely many
(n-2)
-planes on
X
which intersect
Z
. Suppose towards a contradiction that this is not true. Since
X
is geometrically irreducible,
X
is the union of the infinitely many
(n-2)
-planes contained in it which intersect
Z
.
In particular, by considering the closure of
X
in
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}_{K}
this holds for the points of
Y
. So through every point of
Y
there is an
(n-2)
-plane contained in
X
intersecting
Z
. But then because
\deg Y\geq 3
and
Y\neq Z
, it would follow that the entire plane at infinity is contained in
X
, contradiction. Now, in the proof of
3.4
we no longer have to consider rulings of
X
coming from components of
X_{\infty}
of degree
1
or
2
. We can then simply follow the proof of
3.4
in the same way to conclude.

If
f_{d}
has at least one absolutely irreducible factor of degree at least
2
, the above proof goes through to show that we can disregard linear components of
X_{\infty}
.
∎

1.8
and Theorem
1.9
can be generalized correspondingly, namely, with the
1
-irreducibility being changed to
f_{d}

having at least one absolutely irreducible factor of degree
>1
, while assuming

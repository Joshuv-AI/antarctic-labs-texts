Uncertainty-Based Knowing How Logic
Carlos Areces 1,2 Raul Fervari 1,2,3 Andrés R. Saravia 1,2 Fernando R. Velázquez-Quesada 4

Abstract
We introduce a novel semantics for a multi-agent epistemic operator of knowing how , based on an indistinguishability relation between plans. Our proposal is, arguably, closer to the standard presentation of knowing that modalities in classical epistemic logic. We study the relationship between this new semantics and previous approaches, showing that our setting is general enough to capture them. We also study the logical properties of the new semantics. First, we define a sound and complete axiomatization. Second, we define a suitable notion of bisimulation and prove correspondence theorems. Finally, we investigate the computational complexity of the model checking and satisfiability problems for the new logic.



1
Introduction

Epistemic logic (EL;
[
28
,
13
]
) is a logical formalism tailored
for reasoning about the knowledge of abstract autonomous entities commonly
called agents (e.g., a human being, a robot, a vehicle). It has contributed to the formal study of complex multi-agent epistemic notions not only in philosophy
[
25
]
but also in computer science
[
13
,
41
]
and economics
[
47
]
.

Standard epistemic logics deal with an agent’s knowledge about the truth-value
of propositions (the notion of
knowing that
). Thus, they focus
on the study of sentences like
“the agent knows that it is sunny in
Paris”
or
“the robot knows that it is standing next to a
wall”
. For doing so, at the semantic level, EL formulas are typically interpreted over
relational models
[
9
,
10
]
: essentially, labeled
directed graphs. The elements of the domain (called
states
or

worlds
) represent different possible situations, and they fix the facts an agent might or might not know. Then, the knowledge of each agent is given by her
epistemic
indistinguishability
relation, used to represent her uncertainty about the truth: related states are considered indistinguishable for the
agent. Finally, an agent is said to know that a proposition

\varphi
is true at a given state
s
if and only if
\varphi
holds
in all states she cannot distinguish from
s
(i.e., in all states accessible from
s
). In order to capture properly the properties of knowledge, it is typically assumed
that the indistinguishability relation is an equivalence relation.

In spite of its simplicity, this indistinguishability-based
representation of knowledge has several advantages. First, it captures the agent’s
high-order
knowledge (knowledge about
her own knowledge and that of other agents). Moreover, due to its generality, it opens the way to study other epistemic notions, such as the notion of
belief

[
28
]
. Finally,
it allows a very natural
representation of actions through which knowledge changes
[
53
,
50
]
.

In recent years, other forms of knowledge have been studied (see the discussion in
[
58
]
). Some authors have studied knowledge of propositions using rather the notion of
knowing whether

[
23
,
14
]
; some others have focused in the reasons/justifications for propositional knowledge, exploring the notion of
knowing why

[
5
,
60
]
; some more have looked at more general scenarios, proposing logics for
knowing the value

[
20
,
6
,
54
]
. A further and particularly interesting form of knowledge, motivated by different scenarios in philosophy and AI, is one that focuses rather on the agent’s abilities: the notion of
knowing how

[
15
]
. Intuitively, an agent knows how to achieve
\varphi
given
\psi
if she has the
ability
to guarantee that
\varphi
will be the case whenever she is in a situation in which
\psi
holds. Arguably, this notion is particularly important as it provides the formal foundations of automated planning and strategic reasoning within AI.

Historically, the concept of knowing how has been considered different from knowing that, as posed e.g. in
[
48
]
. Knowing how is often seen as a reflection of actions or abilities that agents may take, in an intelligent manner, in order to achieve a certain goal. In turn,
there is a large literature connecting
knowing how
with logics of knowledge and action (see, e.g.,
[
39
,
42
,
31
,
52
,
27
]
). However, the way in which these proposals represent
knowing how
has been the target of criticisms. The main issue is that a simple combination of standard operators expressing
knowing that
and
ability
(see, e.g.,
[
51
]
) does not seem to lead to a natural notion of
knowing how
(see
[
29
,
26
]
for a discussion).

Taking these considerations into account,
[
57
,
58
,
59
]
introduced a novel framework based on a binary
knowing how
modality that is not defined in terms of
knowing that
. At the semantic level, this language is also interpreted over relational models — called in this context labeled transition systems (LTSs). Yet, relations do not represent
indistinguishability anymore; they rather describe the actions the agent has at her disposal (similar to what is done in, e.g., propositional dynamic logic
[
22
]
). Indeed, an edge labeled
a
from state
w
to state
u
indicates now that the agent can execute action
a
to transform state
w
into
u
. In the proposed semantics, the new modality
\mathsf{Kh}(\psi,\varphi)
holds if and only if there is a “plan” — a sequence of actions satisfying a constraint called strong executability (SE) — leading from
\psi
-states to
\varphi
-states. Intuitively, SE implies that the plan is “fail-proof” in the LTS; it unerringly leads from every
\psi
-state only to
\varphi
-states. Other variants of this
knowing how
operator follow a similar approach (see
[
32
,
34
,
16
,
56
]
). Further motivation for these semantics can be found in the referred papers.

It is interesting to notice how LTSs have no epistemic component: their relations are interpreted as actions, and then the abilities of an agent are defined only in terms what these actions can achieve. This is in sharp contrast with standard EL, where relational models provide two kinds of information: ontic facts about the given situation (the model’s evaluation point) and the particular way an agent ‘sees’ this situation (both the possible states available in the model and the agent’s indistinguishability relation among them). In particular, in a multi-agent scenario, all agents share the same ontic information, and differ on their
epistemic interpretation
of it. If one wants to mirror the situation in EL, it seems natural that
knowing how
should be defined in terms of some kind of indistinguishability over the actual situation. Such an extended model would then be able to capture both the abilities of an agent as given by her available actions (the ontic information) as well as the knowledge (or lack of it) that arises from her uncertainty (the epistemic information).

This paper investigates a new semantics for
\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\psi,\varphi)
, a multi-agent version of the
knowing how
modality, first presented in
[
3
]
.
These semantics introduces two ideas. The first, the crucial, is the use of a notion of
epistemic indistinguishability over plans
, in the spirit of the
strategy indistinguishability
of, e.g.,
[
30
,
8
]
. The intuition behind it is that, under the original
\mathrm{LTS}
semantics, the only reason why an agent might not know how to achieve a goal is because there is no adequate action available. However, one can think of scenarios in which the lack of knowledge arises for a different reason: the agent might have an adequate plan available (she has the ability to do something), and yet she might not be able to distinguish it from a non-adequate one, in the sense of not being able to tell that, in general, these plans produce different outcomes.
Section 4
provides a deeper discussion on this. In this way, these
uncertainty-based

{\mathrm{LTS}}s
reintroduce the notion of epistemic indistinguishability.

Now, although indistinguishability over plans is the main idea behind the new semantics, this proposal incorporates a second insight. One can also think of scenarios in which some of the actions, despite being
ontically
available, are not
epistemically
accessible to the agent. There might be several reasons for this, but an appealing one is that the agent might not be
aware of
all available actions. In such cases, the epistemically inaccessible actions are then not even under consideration when the agent looks for a plan to reach a goal. The idea of awareness is not new in the EL literature: it has been used for dealing with the EL problem of logical omniscience
[
55
,
49
,
21
]
by allowing the agent not to be aware of all involved atoms/formulas, thus bringing it closer to what a ‘real’ resource-bounded agent is (see
[
12
]
).

Notice that, the ideas discussed above are in line with a reading that has a consensus among the literature (see, e.g.,
[
24
]
): knowing how of an agent entails her ability (i.e., the capacity of actually doing it); but ability does not necessarily entail knowing how.
It is equally important to notice that, in the new semantics, the agent does not
need
to be incapable of distinguishing certain actions, and she does not
need
to be unaware of some of them. As it will be proved, the new semantics are a generalisation of the original ones in
[
57
,
59
]
. Thus, an agent in the new semantics who does not have uncertainty among plans and has full awareness of all of them is, knowledge-wise, exactly as an agent in the original semantics.

Our contributions.

Our work aims to shed new light on knowing how logics. In particular, we investigate a new multi-agent semantics for capturing the notion of knowing how, generalizing previous proposals
[
57
,
58
,
59
,
3
]
.
Herein, we establish a distinction between ontic information shared by the agents (or abilities), and epistemic information for each individual agent (or awareness), at the level of models. In our semantics, knowing how is given by the latter, instead of by the former, as in existing approaches
[
57
,
58
,
59
]
.
Moreover, we present a thorough study of the metalogical properties of the new logic, and compare it with previous approaches.
Our contributions can be summarized as follows:

(1)

We introduce a new semantics for
\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\psi,\varphi)
(for
i
an agent) that reintroduces the notion of epistemic indistinguishability from classical EL. This dimension captures the awareness for each particular agent over the available abilities in the real world.

(2)

We introduce a suitable notion of bisimulation for the new semantics, based on ideas from
[
17
,
18
]
. We prove an invariance result, and a Hennessy-Milner style theorem over finite models.

(3)

We show that the logic obtained is strictly weaker (and this is an advantage, as we will discuss) than the logic from
[
57
,
58
,
59
]
. Still, the new semantics is general enough to capture the original proposal by imposing adequate conditions on the class of models. Apart from the direct correspondence between models of each framework established already in
[
3
]
, we introduce a new general class of models that also does the job.

(4)

We present a sound and complete axiomatization for the logic over the class of all models.

(5)

We study the computational properties of our logic. First, we provide a finite model property via filtrations. I.e., we show how, given an arbitrary model, it is possible to obtain a finite model satisfying the same set of formulas. A more careful selection argument can be used to prove that the satisfiability problem for the new logic is
NP
-complete, whereas model checking is in
P
.

This paper extends the work presented in
[
3
]
. Herein, we provide detailed discussions and motivations, and full proofs. Moreover, the results about bisimulations, expressive power and finite models via filtrations, are novel with respect to
[
3
]
.

recalls the framework
of

, including its axiom

,

), and the

logic (
Subsection 8.2
). We conclude in

with some conclusions and future lines of research.

2
A short review of the literature

The ideas discussed in the previous section concerning the notion of
knowing how
introduced in
[
57
,
58
,
59
]
have been successful, and have lead to different works in the literature. An earlier one is
[
34
]
, which considers a ternary modality
\mathsf{Kh}(\psi,\chi,\varphi)
asking for a plan whose intermediate states satisfy
\chi
. Then,
[
32
]
introduces a weaker binary modality
\mathsf{Kh}^{\sf w}(\psi,\varphi)
that allows plans that abort, and in which the states reached by these aborted executions should also satisfy the goal
\varphi
. Finally,
[
56
]
uses a semantics under which intermediate actions in a given plan may be skipped.

The respective works introducing these variants also provide an axiom system (interestingly, the logic for the modality with skippable plans is the same as the logic for the original modality). Regarding computational behaviour, the satisfiability problem has been proved to be decidable (in their respective papers) for the basic system, the one allowing aborted executions and the one with skippable plans. However, no complexity bounds for any of the systems have been given. Finally, suitable notions of bisimulation for these systems can be found in
[
17
,
18
]
(for all but the one with skippable plans) and
[
56
]
(for the one with skippable plans). These bisimilarity tools have been useful to investigate the systems’ relative expressive power. It has been shown that the original binary modality
\mathsf{Kh}(\psi,\varphi)
is strictly less expressive than the one with intermediate steps (
\mathsf{Kh}(\psi,\chi,\varphi)
), and that they are both incomparable with the modality with aborted executions
\mathsf{Kh}^{\sf w}(\psi,\varphi)
. Moreover, in
[
11
]
the computational complexity of the model-checking problem for different knowing how logics is characterized. In particular, it is established that model-checking for the basic knowing how logic from
[
57
,
58
,
59
]
is
PSpace
-complete, whereas for a variant with budget constraints is
ExpSpace
-hard. Other constraints over plans are also studied therein, concretely the variant of
[
3
]
(the one studied in this paper) with regularity constraints and budgets, for which model-checking is in
P
. More recently, in
[
2
]
, the framework of knowing how is extended to a deontic setting, formalizing the notion of
knowingly complying
.

Further proposals explore new features. For instance, a natural extension is considering the interaction between
knowing how
and standard
knowing that
modalities. In
[
16
]
, a single-agent logic with the two modalities is introduced. The knowing how operator is, unlike previous approaches, a unary local modality
\mathsf{Kh}(\varphi)
, and its interpretation allows branching plans. The interaction between both kinds of knowledge is studied via an axiom system, and it is proved that its satisfiability problem is decidable. The decidability result has been recently refined in
[
33
]
, where
PSpace
-completeness is proved for the satisfiability problem, via a tableau-based procedure. In
[
37
]
a neighbourhood semantics is provided for the
knowing how
modality, as an alternative to the standard relational semantics.

Other papers incorporate multi-agent behaviour for
knowing how
and
knowing that
modalities. For instance, in
[
43
,
45
]
this is explored in the context of coalitions, i.e., the logic is used to describe different notions of collective knowledge. It is known that a fragment of this logic is incomparable in expressive power with the logic from
[
16
]
(the proof uses bisimulation, and it is presented in
[
18
]
). Other variants of this logic have been explored, including those relying on
second-order knowing how
strategies
[
44
]
, and
knowing how
with degrees of uncertainty
[
46
]
. Axiom systems are presented for each logic.

Finally, a multi-agent knowing how logic describing the behaviour of epistemic planning is investigated in
[
35
]
. The main peculiarity is that the execution of an action is represented by an update in the model via epistemic action models
[
7
]
. The logic obtained is strictly weaker than the one in
[
16
]
. Again, its satisfiability problem is decidable. This work is extended in
[
38
]
, which provides a unified approach for planning-based knowing how. More remarkably, the work in
[
36
]
establishes a connection between planning and knowing how, not just from the perspective of
planning-based
know how, but also the other way around: a planning problem based on know how goals. To do so, the authors introduce a model checking algorithm running in
P
time.

A logic of knowing how

This section recalls the basics of the

Syntax and semantics.
Throughout the text, let
{\rm\sf Prop}
be a countable non-empty set of propositional symbols.

Definition 3.1
.

Formulas of the language
\mathsf{L}_{\mathsf{Kh}}

\varphi::=p\mid\neg\varphi\mid\varphi\vee\varphi\mid\mathsf{Kh}(\varphi,\varphi)
,

“when

.

\dashv

In
[
57
,
58
,

]
(and variations like
[
34
,
32
]

\mathsf{L}_{\mathsf{Kh}}
are interpreted over
labeled transition systems
: relational models in which the relations describe the state-transitions available to the agent. Throughout the text, let
{\rm\sf Act}

Definition 3.2
(Actions and plans).

Let

(note:

\dashv

A

),

\mathrm{R}=\{\mathrm{R}_{a}\subseteq\mathrm{W}\times\mathrm{W}\mid a\in A,\text{ for some }A\subseteq{\rm\sf Act}\}

collection of binary relations on

,

1

Thus,
\mathrm{R}_{a}

Definition 3.4
.

Let
\{\mathrm{R}_{a}\subseteq\mathrm{W}\times\mathrm{W}\mid a\in A,\text{ for some }A\subseteq{\rm\sf Act}\}

\mathrm{R}_{\epsilon}:=\{(w,w)\mid w\in\mathrm{W}\}

\sigma\in{\rm\sf Act}^{*}

\mathrm{R}_{\sigma{a}}:=\{(w,u)\in\mathrm{W}\times\mathrm{W}\mid\exists v\in\mathrm{W}\text{ s.t. }(w,v)\in\mathrm{R}_{\sigma}\text{ and }(v,u)\in\mathrm{R}_{a}\}

\dashv

The idea in

Definition 3.5
(Strong executability).

Let
\{\mathrm{R}_{a}\subseteq\mathrm{W}\times\mathrm{W}\mid a\in A,\text{ for some }A\subseteq{\rm\sf Act}\}

\sigma\in{\rm\sf Act}^{*}

\mathrm{SE}(\sigma):=\{w\in\mathrm{W}\mid\sigma\mbox{ is SE at }w\}

\dashv

Thus, strong executability asks for

partial execution of the plan (including
\epsilon

4
Uncertainty-based semantics

The
\mathrm{LTS}
-based semantics provides a reasonable representation of an
agent’s abilities: the agent knows how to achieve
\varphi
given

\psi
if and only if there is a plan that, when executed at any

\psi
-state, will always complete every partial execution, ending
unerringly in states satisfying
\varphi
. Still, one could argue that
this representation involves a certain level of idealization.

Take an agent that
lacks
a certain ability. In the
\mathrm{LTS}
-based
semantics, this can only happen when the environment does not provide the required
(sequence of) action(s). Still, there are situations in which an adequate plan
exists, and yet the agent lacks the ability for a different reason. Indeed, she might

fail to distinguish
an adequate plan from a non-adequate one, in the
sense of not being able to tell that, in general, those plans produce
different outcomes. Consider, for example, an agent baking a
cake. She might have the ability to do the
nine different mixing methods
2
2

2

https://www.perfectlypastry.com/the-importance-of-the-mixing-method/
(beating, blending, creaming, cutting,
folding, kneading, sifting, stirring, whipping), and she might even
recognize them as different actions. However, she might not be able to
perfectly distinguish one from the others: she might not recognize that,
sometimes, they produce different results. In such cases, one would say that the
agent does not know how to bake a cake: sometimes she gets good
outcomes (when she uses the adequate mixing method) and sometimes she
does not.

Indistinguishability among
basic
actions can account for the
example above (with each mixing method a basic action). Still, one can also
think of situations in which a more general form of indistinguishability, one
among plans
, is involved. Consider the baking agent
again. It is reasonable to assume that she can tell the difference
between “adding milk” and “adding flour”, but perhaps she does not
realize the effect that
the order
of these actions might
have in the final result. Here, the issue is not that she cannot
distinguish between basic actions; rather, two plans are
indistinguishable because the order of their actions is being
considered irrelevant. For a last possibility, the agent might
not know that, while opening the oven once to check whether the baking
goods are done is reasonable, this must not be done in excess. In this
case, the problem consists in not being able to tell the difference between
the effect of executing an action once and executing it multiple times.
Thus, plans of
different lengths

might be considered equivalent for the task at hand, for such an agent.

The previous examples suggest that one can devise a more general
representation of an agent’s abilities. This involves
taking into account not only
the plans she has available (the
\mathrm{LTS}
structure), but also her skills
for telling two different plans apart (a form of

indistinguishability among plans
).
As we will see, this (in)ability for distinguishing plans will also let us define a natural model for a multi-agent
scenario. In this setting, agents share the same set of
affordances
(provided
by the actual environment), but still have different
abilities
depending
on and how well they can tell these affordances apart, or even which of these affordances they have available.
To drive this last point home notice that, in principle, an agent does not need to have ‘epistemic access’ to every available plan. Some might be so foreign to the agent, or so complex, that she might not be
aware of
them. Such plans are, then, out of the agent’s reach, not in the sense that she cannot distinguish them from others, but in that she does not even take them into consideration. This is similar to what
[
12
]
proposed for the epistemic notion of
knowing that
: the agent might not be aware of (i.e., she might not entertain) every formula of the language, and thus she does not need to know that these formulas are indeed the case.

.

Take

It is easy to see that the converse of
Theorem 2
does not hold over arbitrary models: in fact, the counterexample provided in
[
18
, Section 2]
serves also here to make our point. To satisfy the converse, we usually need to restrict ourselves to some particular classes of models, that are in general known as Hennessy-Milner classes.
In many modal logics, one typically works only with image-finite models: those in which, at every state, every basic relation has only finitely many successors. For languages in which the global universal modality
\mathsf{A}
is definable, as
\mathsf{L}_{\mathsf{Kh}_{i}}
, this requirement needs to be strengthened, as every state can reach (via the relation underlying
\mathsf{A}
) every other state. For instance, we can take the class of models with a finite domain. Precisely, here we show that the class of finite models (taken as those with a finite domain) forms a Hennessy-Milner class. Note that we do not impose any restriction over the uncertainty relation of the models (or the sets
\mathrm{S}_{i}
).

\mathsf{L}_{\mathsf{Kh}_{i}}

\Delta\in\bm{\Upphi}
, define

\begin{array}[]{r@{\;:=\;}l@{\qquad\qquad}r@{\;:=\;}l}\Delta|_{\mathsf{Kh}_{i}}&\{\xi\in\Delta\mid\xi\;\text{is of the form}\;\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\psi,\varphi)\},&\Delta|_{\mathsf{Kh}}&\bigcup_{i\in{\rm\sf Agt}}\Delta|_{\mathsf{Kh}_{i}}.\\
\Delta|_{\lnot\mathsf{Kh}_{i}}&\{\xi\in\Delta\mid\xi\;\text{is of the form}\;\lnot\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\psi,\varphi)\},&\Delta|_{\lnot\mathsf{Kh}}&\bigcup_{i\in{\rm\sf Agt}}\Delta|_{\lnot\mathsf{Kh}_{i}}.\\
\end{array}

Let
\Gamma
be a set in
\bm{\Upphi}
; we will define a structure satisfying its formulas. Define a set of basic actions
{\rm\sf Act}^{\Gamma}_{i}:=\{\langle\psi,\varphi\rangle\mid\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\psi,\varphi)\in\Gamma\}
associated to each agent
i\in{\rm\sf Agt}
, and then their union
{\rm\sf Act}^{\Gamma}:=\bigcup_{i\in{\rm\sf Agt}}{\rm\sf Act}^{\Gamma}_{i}
. Notice that
\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\bot,\varphi)\in\Gamma
for every
i\in{\rm\sf Agt}
and every
\varphi\in\mathsf{L}_{\mathsf{Kh}_{i}}
(by
\mathpzc{COND}
); since
{\rm\sf Agt}
is finite and non-empty, this implies that
{\rm\sf Act}^{\Gamma}
is denumerable, and thus it is an adequate set of actions for building a model. It is worth noticing that
{\rm\sf Act}^{\Gamma}
fixes a new signature. However, since the operators of the language cannot talk explicitly about the names of the actions, we can define a mapping from
{\rm\sf Act}^{\Gamma}
to any particular
{\rm\sf Act}
, to preserve the original signature, provided that the cardinalities match.

Then, the structure
\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}=\langle\mathrm{W}^{\Gamma},\mathrm{R}^{\Gamma},\{\mathrm{S}^{\Gamma}_{i}\}_{i\in{\rm\sf Agt}},\mathrm{V}^{\Gamma}\rangle
over
{\rm\sf Act}^{\Gamma}
,
{\rm\sf Agt}
and
{\rm\sf Prop}
is defined as follows.

•

\mathrm{W}^{\Gamma}:=\{\Delta\in\bm{\Upphi}\mid\Delta|_{\mathsf{Kh}}=\Gamma|_{\mathsf{Kh}}\}
.

•

\mathrm{R}^{\Gamma}_{\langle\psi,\varphi\rangle}:=\bigcup_{i\in{\rm\sf Agt}}\mathrm{R}^{\Gamma}_{\langle\psi,\varphi\rangle^{i}}
, with

\mathrm{R}^{\Gamma}_{\langle\psi,\varphi\rangle^{i}}:=\{(\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2})\in\mathrm{W}^{\Gamma}\times\mathrm{W}^{\Gamma}\mid\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\psi,\varphi)\in\Gamma,\psi\in\Delta_{1},\varphi\in\Delta_{2}\}
.

•

\mathrm{S}^{\Gamma}_{i}:=\left\{\{\langle\psi,\varphi\rangle\}\mid\langle\psi,\varphi\rangle\in{\rm\sf Act}^{\Gamma}_{i}\right\}
.

•

\mathrm{V}^{\Gamma}(\Delta):=\{p\in{\rm\sf Prop}\mid p\in\Delta\}
.

\dashv

Since
\Gamma\in\bm{\Upphi}
, the structure
\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}
is of the required type, as the following proposition states.

Proposition 5
.

The structure
\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}=\langle\mathrm{W}^{\Gamma},\mathrm{R}^{\Gamma},\{\mathrm{S}^{\Gamma}_{i}\}_{i\in{\rm\sf Agt}},\mathrm{V}^{\Gamma}\rangle
is an
\mathrm{LTS}^{U}
.

Proof.
.

It is enough to show that each
\mathrm{S}^{\Gamma}_{i}
defines a partition over a non-empty subset of
2^{({\rm\sf Act}^{*})}
. First,
\mathpzc{COND}
implies
\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\bot,\bot)\in\Gamma
, so
\langle\bot,\bot\rangle\in{\rm\sf Act}^{\Gamma}_{i}
and hence
\{\langle\bot,\bot\rangle\}\in\mathrm{S}^{\Gamma}_{i}
; thus,
\bigcup_{\uppi\in\mathrm{S}_{i}}\uppi\neq\varnothing
. Then,
\mathrm{S}_{i}
indeed defines a partition over
\bigcup_{\uppi\in\mathrm{S}_{i}}\uppi
: its elements are mutually disjoint (they are singletons with different elements), collective exhaustiveness is immediate and, finally,
\varnothing\notin\mathrm{S}^{\Gamma}_{i}
.

\blacksquare

\triangleleft

Let
\Gamma\in\bm{\Upphi}
; the following properties of
\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}
will be useful (proofs are similar to the ones in

]
).

Proposition 6
.

For any
\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2}\in\mathrm{W}^{\Gamma}
we have
\Delta_{1}|_{\mathsf{Kh}}=\Delta_{2}|_{\mathsf{Kh}}
.

Proof.
.

Straightforward from the definition of
\mathrm{W}^{\Gamma}
.

\blacksquare

Proposition 7
.

Take
\Delta\in\mathrm{W}^{\Gamma}
. If
\Delta
has a
\mathrm{R}^{\Gamma}_{\langle\psi,\varphi\rangle}
-successor, then every
\Delta^{\prime}\in\mathrm{W}^{\Gamma}
with
\varphi\in\Delta^{\prime}
can be
\mathrm{R}^{\Gamma}_{\langle\psi,\varphi\rangle}
-reached from
\Delta
.

Proof.
.

If
\Delta
has a
\mathrm{R}^{\Gamma}_{\langle\psi,\varphi\rangle}
-successor, then it has a
\mathrm{R}^{\Gamma}_{\langle\psi,\varphi\rangle^{i}}
-successor for some
i\in{\rm\sf Agt}
; thus,
\psi\in\Delta
and
\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\psi,\varphi)\in\Gamma
. Hence, every
\Delta^{\prime}\in\mathrm{W}^{\Gamma}
with
\varphi\in\Delta^{\prime}
is such that
(\Delta,\Delta^{\prime})\in\mathrm{R}^{\Gamma}_{\langle\psi,\varphi\rangle^{i}}
, and thus such that
(\Delta,\Delta^{\prime})\in\mathrm{R}^{\Gamma}_{\langle\psi,\varphi\rangle}
.

\blacksquare

\triangleleft

Proposition 8
.

Let
\varphi
be an
\mathsf{L}_{\mathsf{Kh}_{i}}
-formula. If
\varphi\in\Delta
for every
\Delta\in\mathrm{W}^{\Gamma}
, then
\mathsf{A}\varphi\in\Delta
for every
\Delta\in\mathrm{W}^{\Gamma}
.

Proof.
.

First, some facts for any
\Delta
in
\mathrm{W}^{\Gamma}\subseteq\bm{\Upphi}
. By definition,
\Delta|_{\mathsf{Kh}}\cup\Delta|_{\lnot\mathsf{Kh}}
is a subset of
\Delta
, and therefore it is consistent. Moreover: any maximally consistent extension of
\Delta|_{\mathsf{Kh}}\cup\Delta|_{\lnot\mathsf{Kh}}
, say
\Delta^{\prime}
, should satisfy
\Delta|_{\mathsf{Kh}}=\Delta^{\prime}|_{\mathsf{Kh}}
. For
\bm{(\subseteq)}
, note that
\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\psi,\varphi)\in\Delta|_{\mathsf{Kh}}
implies
\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\psi,\varphi)\in(\Delta|_{\mathsf{Kh}}\cup\Delta|_{\lnot\mathsf{Kh}})
, and thus
\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\psi,\varphi)\in\Delta^{\prime}
, i.e.,
\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\psi,\varphi)\in\Delta^{\prime}|_{\mathsf{Kh}}
. For
\bm{(\supseteq)}
, use the contrapositive. If
\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\psi,\varphi)\not\in\Delta|_{\mathsf{Kh}}
then
\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\psi,\varphi)\not\in\Delta
, so
\lnot\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\psi,\varphi)\in\Delta
(as
\Delta
is an MCS). Thus,
\lnot\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\psi,\varphi)\in(\Delta|_{\mathsf{Kh}}\cup\Delta|_{\lnot\mathsf{Kh}})
and hence
\lnot\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\psi,\varphi)\in\Delta^{\prime}
; therefore,
\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\psi,\varphi)\not\in\Delta^{\prime}
(as
\Delta
is consistent) and thus
\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\psi,\varphi)\not\in\Delta^{\prime}|_{\mathsf{Kh}}
.

For the proof of the proposition, suppose
\varphi\in\Delta
for every
\Delta\in\mathrm{W}^{\Gamma}
. Take any
\Delta\in\mathrm{W}^{\Gamma}
, and note how
\Delta|_{\mathsf{Kh}}=\Gamma|_{\mathsf{Kh}}
. Then, the set
\Delta|_{\mathsf{Kh}}\cup\Delta|_{\lnot\mathsf{Kh}}\cup\{\lnot\varphi\}
is inconsistent. Otherwise it could be extended into an MCS
\Delta^{\prime}\in\bm{\Upphi}
. By the result in the previous paragraph, this would imply
\Delta^{\prime}|_{\mathsf{Kh}}=\Delta|_{\mathsf{Kh}}
, so
\Delta^{\prime}|_{\mathsf{Kh}}=\Gamma|_{\mathsf{Kh}}
and therefore
\Delta^{\prime}\in\mathrm{W}^{\Gamma}
. But then, by the assumption,
\varphi\in\Delta^{\prime}
, and by construction,
\lnot\varphi\in\Delta^{\prime}
. This would make
\Delta^{\prime}
inconsistent, a contradiction.

Thus, given that
\Delta|_{\mathsf{Kh}}\cup\Delta|_{\lnot\mathsf{Kh}}\cup\{\lnot\varphi\}
is inconsistent, there should be sets
\{\mathsf{Kh}_{b_{1}}(\psi_{1},\varphi_{1}),\ldots,\mathsf{Kh}_{b_{n}}(\psi_{n},\varphi_{n})\}\subseteq\Delta|_{\mathsf{Kh}}
and
\{\lnot\mathsf{Kh}_{b^{\prime}_{1}}(\psi^{\prime}_{1},\varphi^{\prime}_{1}),\ldots,\lnot\mathsf{Kh}_{b^{\prime}_{m}}(\psi^{\prime}_{m},\varphi^{\prime}_{m})\}\subseteq\Delta|_{\lnot\mathsf{Kh}}
such that

\vdash\displaystyle\left(\bigwedge_{k=1}^{n}\mathsf{Kh}_{b_{k}}(\psi_{k},\varphi_{k})\land\bigwedge_{k=1}^{m}\lnot\mathsf{Kh}_{b^{\prime}_{k}}(\psi^{\prime}_{k},\varphi^{\prime}_{k})\right)\rightarrow\varphi
.

Hence, by
\mathpzc{NECA}
,

\vdash\displaystyle\mathsf{A}\left(\left(\bigwedge_{k=1}^{n}\mathsf{Kh}_{b_{k}}(\psi_{k},\varphi_{k})\land\bigwedge_{k=1}^{m}\lnot\mathsf{Kh}_{b^{\prime}_{k}}(\psi^{\prime}_{k},\varphi^{\prime}_{k})\right)\rightarrow\varphi\right)

and then, by
\mathpzc{DISTA}
and
\mathpzc{MP}
,

\vdash\displaystyle\mathsf{A}\left(\bigwedge_{k=1}^{n}\mathsf{Kh}_{b_{k}}(\psi_{k},\varphi_{k})\land\bigwedge_{k=1}^{m}\lnot\mathsf{Kh}_{b^{\prime}_{k}}(\psi^{\prime}_{k},\varphi^{\prime}_{k})\right)\rightarrow\mathsf{A}\varphi
.

Now,
\mathsf{Kh}_{b_{k}}(\psi_{k},\varphi_{k})\in\Delta|_{\mathsf{Kh}}
implies (
\mathpzc{4KhA}
and
\mathpzc{MP}
) that
\mathsf{A}\mathsf{Kh}_{b_{k}}(\psi_{k},\varphi_{k})\in\Delta
(for each
k\in[1\mathbin{\ldotp\!\ldotp}n]
). Similarly,
\lnot\mathsf{Kh}_{b^{\prime}_{k}}(\psi^{\prime}_{k},\varphi^{\prime}_{k})\in\Delta|_{\mathsf{Kh}}
implies (
\mathpzc{5KhA}
and
\mathpzc{MP}
) that
\mathsf{A}\lnot\mathsf{Kh}_{b^{\prime}_{k}}(\psi^{\prime}_{k},\varphi^{\prime}_{k})\in\Delta
(for each
k\in[1\mathbin{\ldotp\!\ldotp}m]
). Thus,

\displaystyle\bigwedge_{k=1}^{n}\mathsf{A}\mathsf{Kh}_{b_{k}}(\psi_{k},\varphi_{k})\in\Delta\hskip 18.49988pt\text{and}\hskip 18.49988pt\bigwedge_{k=1}^{m}\mathsf{A}\lnot\mathsf{Kh}_{b^{\prime}_{k}}(\psi^{\prime}_{k},\varphi^{\prime}_{k})\in\Delta

and hence

\displaystyle\bigwedge_{k=1}^{n}\mathsf{A}\mathsf{Kh}_{b_{k}}(\psi_{k},\varphi_{k})\land\bigwedge_{k=1}^{m}\mathsf{A}\lnot\mathsf{Kh}_{b^{\prime}_{k}}(\psi^{\prime}_{k},\varphi^{\prime}_{k})\in\Delta,\,\text{so}\,\mathsf{A}\left(\bigwedge_{k=1}^{n}\mathsf{Kh}_{b_{k}}(\psi_{k},\varphi_{k})\land\bigwedge_{k=1}^{m}\lnot\mathsf{Kh}_{b^{\prime}_{k}}(\psi^{\prime}_{k},\varphi^{\prime}_{k})\right)\in\Delta

and therefore
\mathsf{A}\varphi\in\Delta
.

\blacksquare

\triangleleft

Proposition 9
.

Take
\psi,\psi^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime}
in
\mathsf{L}_{\mathsf{Kh}_{i}}
. Suppose that every
\Delta\in\mathrm{W}^{\Gamma}
with
\psi\in\Delta
has a
\mathrm{R}^{\Gamma}_{\langle\psi^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime}\rangle}
-successor. Then,
\mathsf{A}(\psi\rightarrow\psi^{\prime})\in\Delta
for all
\Delta\in\mathrm{W}^{\Gamma}
.

Proof.
.

Take any
\Delta\in\mathrm{W}^{\Gamma}
. On the one hand, if
\psi\in\Delta
then, by the supposition,
(\Delta,\Delta^{\prime})\in\mathrm{R}^{\Gamma}_{\langle\psi^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime}\rangle}
for some
\Delta^{\prime}
. Hence, from
\mathrm{R}^{\Gamma}_{\langle\psi^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime}\rangle}
’s definition,
\psi^{\prime}\in\Delta
and thus (maximal consistency)
\psi\rightarrow\psi^{\prime}\in\Delta
. On the other hand, if
\psi\not\in\Delta
then
\lnot\psi\in\Delta
(again, maximal consistency) and thus
\psi\rightarrow\psi^{\prime}\in\Delta
. Thus,
\psi\rightarrow\psi^{\prime}\in\Delta
for every
\Delta\in\mathrm{W}^{\Gamma}
; then, by
Proposition 8
,
\mathsf{A}(\psi\rightarrow\psi^{\prime})\in\Delta
for every
\Delta\in\mathrm{W}^{\Gamma}
.

\blacksquare

With these properties at hand, we can prove the truth lemma for
\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}
.

Lemma 1
(Truth lemma for
\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}
).

Given
\Gamma\!\in\!\bm{\Upphi}
, take
\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}=\langle\mathrm{W}^{\Gamma},\mathrm{R}^{\Gamma},\{\mathrm{S}^{\Gamma}_{i}\}_{i\in{\rm\sf Agt}},\mathrm{V}^{\Gamma}\rangle
. Then, for every
\Theta\in\mathrm{W}^{\Gamma}
and every
\varphi\in\mathsf{L}_{\mathsf{Kh}_{i}}
,

\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma},\Theta\models\varphi

if and only if

\varphi\in\Theta
.

Proof.
.

The proof is by induction on
\varphi
. The atom and Boolean cases as usual, so we focus on the
knowing how
case.

1

Case
\bm{\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\psi,\varphi)}
.

\bm{(\Rightarrow)}
Suppose
\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma},\Theta\models\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\psi,\varphi)
, and consider two cases.

•

\bm{\llbracket\psi\rrbracket^{\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}}=\varnothing}
. Then, each
\Delta\in\mathrm{W}^{\Gamma}
is such that
\Delta\not\in\llbracket\psi\rrbracket^{\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}}
, which implies
\psi\not\in\Delta
(by IH) and thus
\lnot\psi\in\Delta
(by maximal consistency). Hence, by
Proposition 8
,
\mathsf{A}\lnot\psi\in\Delta
for every
\Delta\in\mathrm{W}^{\Gamma}
. In particular,
\mathsf{A}\lnot\psi\in\Theta
and thus, by
\mathpzc{SCOND}
and
\mathpzc{MP}
,
\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\psi,\varphi)\in\Theta
.

•

\bm{\llbracket\psi\rrbracket^{\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}}\neq\varnothing}
. From
\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma},\Theta\models\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\psi,\varphi)
, there is
\{\langle\psi^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime}\rangle\}\in\mathrm{S}^{\Gamma}_{i}
such that

(
\bm{\mathsf{Kh}}
-1)

\llbracket\psi\rrbracket^{\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}}\subseteq\mathrm{SE}(\{\langle\psi^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime}\rangle\})
and

(
\bm{\mathsf{Kh}}
-2)

\mathrm{R}^{\Gamma}_{\{\langle\psi^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime}\rangle\}}(\llbracket\psi\rrbracket^{\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}})\subseteq\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket^{\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}}

.

In other words, there is
\langle\psi^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime}\rangle\in{\rm\sf Act}^{\Gamma}_{a}
such that

(
\bm{\mathsf{Kh}}
-1)

for all
\Delta\in\mathrm{W}^{\Gamma}
, if
\Delta\in\llbracket\psi\rrbracket^{\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}}
then
\Delta\in\mathrm{SE}(\{\langle\psi^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime}\rangle\})
, so
\Delta\in\mathrm{SE}(\langle\psi^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime}\rangle)
and therefore
\Delta
has a
\mathrm{R}^{\Gamma}_{\langle\psi^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime}\rangle}
-successor.

(
\bm{\mathsf{Kh}}
-2)

for all
\Delta^{\prime}\in\mathrm{W}^{\Gamma}
, if
\Delta^{\prime}\in\mathrm{R}^{\Gamma}_{\{\langle\psi^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime}\rangle\}}(\llbracket\psi\rrbracket^{\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}})
then
\Delta^{\prime}\in\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket^{\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}}
.

This case requires three pieces.

(1)

Take any
\Delta\in\mathrm{W}^{\Gamma}
with
\psi\in\Delta
. Then, by IH,
\Delta\in\llbracket\psi\rrbracket^{\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}}
and thus, by
(

Kh

-1)
,
\Delta
has a
\mathrm{R}^{\Gamma}_{\langle\psi^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime}\rangle}
-successor. Thus, every
\Delta\in\mathrm{W}^{\Gamma}
with
\psi\in\Delta
has such successor; then (
Proposition 9
), it follows that
\mathsf{A}(\psi\rightarrow\psi^{\prime})\in\Delta
for every
\Delta\in\mathrm{W}^{\Gamma}
. In particular,
\mathsf{A}(\psi\rightarrow\psi^{\prime})\in\Theta
.

(2)

From
\langle\psi^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime}\rangle\in{\rm\sf Act}^{\Gamma}_{i}
it follows that
\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\psi^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime})\in\Gamma
. But
\Theta\in\mathrm{W}^{\Gamma}
, so
\Theta|_{\mathsf{Kh}}=\Gamma|_{\mathsf{Kh}}
(by definition of
\mathrm{W}^{\Gamma}
). Hence,
\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\psi^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime})\in\Theta
.

(3)

Since
\llbracket\psi\rrbracket^{\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}}\neq\varnothing
, there is
\Delta\in\llbracket\psi\rrbracket^{\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}}
. By
(

Kh

-1)
,
\Delta
should have at least one
\mathrm{R}^{\Gamma}_{\langle\psi^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime}\rangle}
-successor. Then, by
Proposition 7
, every
\Delta^{\prime}\in\mathrm{W}^{\Gamma}
satisfying
\varphi^{\prime}\in\Delta^{\prime}
can be
\mathrm{R}^{\Gamma}_{\langle\psi^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime}\rangle}
-reached from
\Delta
; in other words, every
\Delta^{\prime}\in\mathrm{W}^{\Gamma}
satisfying
\varphi^{\prime}\in\Delta^{\prime}
is in
\mathrm{R}^{\Gamma}_{\langle\psi^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime}\rangle}(\Delta)
. But
\Delta\in\llbracket\psi\rrbracket^{\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}}
, so every
\Delta^{\prime}\in\mathrm{W}^{\Gamma}
satisfying
\varphi^{\prime}\in\Delta^{\prime}
is in
\mathrm{R}^{\Gamma}_{\langle\psi^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime}\rangle}(\llbracket\psi\rrbracket^{\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}})
. Then, by
(

Kh

-2)
, every
\Delta^{\prime}\in\mathrm{W}^{\Gamma}
satisfying
\varphi^{\prime}\in\Delta^{\prime}
is in
\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket^{\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}}
. By IH on the latter part, every
\Delta^{\prime}\in\mathrm{W}^{\Gamma}
satisfying
\varphi^{\prime}\in\Delta^{\prime}
is such that
\varphi\in\Delta^{\prime}
. Thus,
\varphi^{\prime}\rightarrow\varphi\in\Delta^{\prime}
for every
\Delta^{\prime}\in\mathrm{W}^{\Gamma}
, and hence (
Proposition 8
)
\mathsf{A}(\varphi^{\prime}\rightarrow\varphi)\in\Delta^{\prime}
for every
\Delta^{\prime}\in\mathrm{W}^{\Gamma}
. In particular,
\mathsf{A}(\varphi^{\prime}\rightarrow\varphi)\in\Theta
.

Thus,
\{\mathsf{A}(\psi\rightarrow\psi^{\prime}),\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\psi^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime}),\mathsf{A}(\varphi^{\prime}\rightarrow\varphi)\}\subset\Theta
. Therefore, by
\mathpzc{KhA}
and
\mathpzc{MP}
,
\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\psi,\varphi)\in\Theta
.

\bm{(\Leftarrow)}
Suppose
\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\psi,\varphi)\in\Theta
. Thus (
Proposition 6
),
\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\psi,\varphi)\in\Gamma
, so
\langle\psi,\varphi\rangle\in{\rm\sf Act}^{\Gamma}_{i}
and therefore
\{\langle\psi,\varphi\rangle\}\in\mathrm{S}^{\Gamma}_{i}
. The rest of the proof is split into two cases.

•

Suppose there is no
\Delta_{\psi}\in\mathrm{W}^{\Gamma}
with
\psi\in\Delta
. Then, by IH, there is no
\Delta_{\psi}\in\mathrm{W}^{\Gamma}
with
\Delta_{\psi}\in\llbracket\psi\rrbracket^{\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}}
, that is,
\llbracket\lnot\psi\rrbracket^{\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}}=\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{W}^{\Gamma}}
. Since
\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}
is an
\mathrm{LTS}^{U}
(
Proposition 5
), the latter yields
(\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma},\Delta)\models\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\psi,\chi)
for any
i\in{\rm\sf Agt}
,
\chi\in\mathsf{L}_{\mathsf{Kh}_{i}}
and
\Delta\in\mathrm{W}^{\Gamma}
(cf.
Proposition 2
); hence,
(\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma},\Theta)\models\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\psi,\varphi)
.

•

Suppose there is
\Delta_{\psi}\in\mathrm{W}^{\Gamma}
with
\psi\in\Delta_{\psi}
. It will be shown that the set of plans
\{\langle\psi,\varphi\rangle\}\in\mathrm{S}^{\Gamma}_{i}
satisfies the requirements.

(
\bm{\mathsf{Kh}}
-1)

Take any
\Delta\in\llbracket\psi\rrbracket^{\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}}
. By IH,
\psi\in\Delta
. Moreover, from
\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\psi,\varphi)\in\Theta
and
Proposition 6
it follows that
\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\psi,\varphi)\in\Delta
. Then, from
\mathrm{R}^{\Gamma}_{\langle\psi,\varphi\rangle^{i}}
’s definition, every
\Delta^{\prime}\in\mathrm{W}^{\Gamma}
with
\varphi\in\Delta^{\prime}
is such that
(\Delta,\Delta^{\prime})\in\mathrm{R}^{\Gamma}_{\langle\psi,\varphi\rangle^{i}}
, and therefore such that
(\Delta,\Delta^{\prime})\in\mathrm{R}^{\Gamma}_{\langle\psi,\varphi\rangle}
. Now note how, since there is
\Delta_{\psi}\in\mathrm{W}^{\Gamma}
with
\psi\in\Delta_{\psi}
, there should be
\Delta_{\varphi}\in\mathrm{W}^{\Gamma}
with
\varphi\in\Delta_{\varphi}
. Suppose otherwise, i.e., suppose there is no
\Delta^{\prime\prime}\in\mathrm{W}^{\Gamma}
with
\varphi\in\Delta^{\prime\prime}
. Then,
\lnot\varphi\in\Delta^{\prime\prime}
for every
\Delta^{\prime\prime}\in\mathrm{W}^{\Gamma}
, and hence (
Proposition 8
)
\mathsf{A}\lnot\varphi\in\Delta^{\prime\prime}
for every
\Delta^{\prime\prime}\in\mathrm{W}^{\Gamma}
. In particular,
\mathsf{A}\lnot\varphi\in\Delta_{\psi}
. Moreover, from
\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\psi,\varphi)\in\Theta
and
Proposition 6
it follows that
\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\psi,\varphi)\in\Delta_{\psi}
. Then,
\mathpzc{KhE}
(written as
\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\psi,\varphi)\rightarrow(\mathsf{A}\lnot\varphi\rightarrow\mathsf{A}\lnot\psi)
) and
\mathpzc{MP}
yield

, and thus (axiom

)
\lnot\psi\in\Delta_{\psi}
. Hence,

, contradicting
\Delta_{\psi}
’s consistency.

\Delta_{\varphi}\in\mathrm{W}^{\Gamma}
with

implies that

and thus, since

\Delta\in\mathrm{SE}(\langle\psi,\varphi\rangle)
, and so

. Since

is an arbitrary state in

, the required

follows.

(
\bm{\mathsf{Kh}}
-2)

Take any
\Delta^{\prime}\in\mathrm{R}^{\Gamma}_{\{\langle\psi,\varphi\rangle\}}(\llbracket\psi\rrbracket^{\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}})
. Then, there is
\Delta\in\llbracket\psi\rrbracket^{\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}}
such that

. By definition of
\mathrm{R}^{\Gamma}
, it follows that
\varphi\in\Delta^{\prime}
so, by IH,
\Delta^{\prime}\in\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket^{\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}}
. Since
\Delta^{\prime}
is an arbitrary state in
\mathrm{R}^{\Gamma}_{\{\langle\psi,\varphi\rangle\}}(\llbracket\psi\rrbracket^{\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}})
, the required
\mathrm{R}^{\Gamma}_{\{\langle\psi,\varphi\rangle\}}(\llbracket\psi\rrbracket^{\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}})\subseteq\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket^{\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}}
follows.

\blacksquare

Finally, we present the intended result.

Theorem 4
.

The axiom system
\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{LTS}^{U}}_{\mathsf{Kh}_{i}}
(
Table 2
) is sound and strongly complete for
\mathsf{L}_{\mathsf{Kh}_{i}}
w.r.t. the class of all
{\mathrm{LTS}^{U}}s
.

Proof.
.

For soundness, it is enough to show that the system’s axioms are valid and that its rules preserve validity (which, as mentioned before, is straightforward). For completeness, take any
\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{LTS}^{U}}_{\mathsf{Kh}_{i}}
-consistent set of formulas
\Gamma^{\prime}\subseteq\mathsf{L}_{\mathsf{Kh}_{i}}
. Since
\mathsf{L}_{\mathsf{Kh}_{i}}
is enumerable,
\Gamma^{\prime}
can be extended into a maximally
\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{LTS}^{U}}_{\mathsf{Kh}_{i}}
-consistent set

by a standard Lindenbaum’s construction (see, e.g.,
[
9
, Lemma 4.17]
). By

,
\Gamma^{\prime}
is satisfiable in
\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}
at
\Gamma
. The fact that
\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}
is an
\mathrm{LTS}^{U}
(
Proposition 5
) completes the proof.

\blacksquare

\triangleleft

One detail in the construction of the canonical model might be surprising: each set of indistinguishable plans for a given agent is a singleton set. Hence, the logic is also complete with respect to this particular class of models. On the other hand,
{\mathrm{LTS}^{U}}s
are a more general and accurate representation from a conceptual point of view.
We could, for instance, extend the language so that this representation is also reflected by the logic (i.e., the language can explicitly refer to plans), or define public announcements-like modalities to refine the indistinguishability relation of each agent (see, e.g.,
[
4
]
).

The provided axiom system can be used to compare the notion of

Proposition 10
.

Proof.
.

Proof.
.

Consider the

With respect to

7.1
A very simple class of
\mathrm{LTS}^{U}
s

Still, the uncertainty-based framework is general enough to capture the
\mathrm{LTS}
semantics. Given the discussion in

, there is an obvious class of

and

\mathrm{LTS}^{U}

Definition 7.1
.

Define the class of models:
\bm{\mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{NU}}}:=\{\mathcal{M}\mid\mathcal{M}\mbox{ is an }\mathrm{LTS}^{U}\mbox{ and }\mathrm{S}=\{\{\sigma\}\mid\sigma\in{\rm\sf Act}^{*}\}\}
.

\dashv

Indeed, for models in
\bm{\mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{NU}}}

\epsilon

\mathpzc{EMP}
) and from
\{\sigma_{1}\}\in\mathrm{S}

\{\sigma_{2}\}\in\mathrm{S}

\mathrm{LTS}^{U}

Proposition 12
.

The following properties hold.

(1)

Given a model
\mathcal{M}=\langle\mathrm{W},\mathrm{R},\mathrm{S},\mathrm{V}\rangle
in
\bm{\mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{NU}}}

\mathrm{LTS}

\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{M}}=\langle\mathrm{W},\mathrm{R},\mathrm{V}\rangle

.

(2)

Given an
\mathrm{LTS}

with

\bm{\mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{NU}}}
and is such that
\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket^{\mathcal{S}}=\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket^{\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{S}}}
for every

.

\triangleleft

\mathrm{LTS}

.

.

To prove that

is strongly complete over the class

Let

7.2
Active and
\mathrm{SE}
-compositional
\mathrm{LTS}^{U}
s

We presented above a very simple class of models that enables us to establish a direct relation between both semantics. However, the result is somewhat trivial:
\mathrm{LTS}^{U}
s generalize
\mathrm{LTS}
s by adding uncertainty among plans, and the class
\bm{\mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{NU}}}
contains those
\mathrm{LTS}^{U}
s in which the agent does not have uncertainty. The rest of this section will discuss a larger and very general class (with very weak constraints) for which the same correspondence holds.

Let us start by introducing some preliminary definitions.

Definition 7.2
.

Let
\mathcal{M}=\langle\mathrm{W},\mathrm{R},\mathrm{S},\mathrm{V}\rangle
be an
\mathrm{LTS}^{U}
.

•

The
composition
of
\uppi_{1},\uppi_{2}\in 2^{{\rm\sf Act}^{*}}
is the set of plans
\uppi_{1}\uppi_{2}\in 2^{{\rm\sf Act}^{*}}
given by

\uppi_{1}\uppi_{2}:=\{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\in{\rm\sf Act}^{*}\mid\sigma_{1}\in\uppi_{1}\text{ and }\sigma_{2}\in\uppi_{2}\}.

•

The
\mathrm{SE}
-composition
of
\uppi_{1},\uppi_{2}\in 2^{{\rm\sf Act}^{*}}

in
\mathcal{M}
is the set of plans
\uppi_{1}\mathbin{\bm{;}}\uppi_{2}\in 2^{{\rm\sf Act}^{*}}
given by

\uppi_{1}\mathbin{\bm{;}}\uppi_{2}:=\begin{cases}\uppi_{1}\uppi_{2}&if $\mathrm{SE}(\uppi_{1})\neq\varnothing$ and $\mathrm{R}_{\uppi_{1}}(\mathrm{SE}(\uppi_{1}))\subseteq\mathrm{SE}(\uppi_{2})$\\
\varnothing&otherwise.\end{cases}

\dashv

Thus, the
\mathrm{SE}
-composition
\uppi_{1}\mathbin{\bm{;}}\uppi_{2}
is the sequential composition of
\uppi_{1}
and then
\uppi_{2}
(i.e.,
\uppi_{1}\uppi_{2}
) when
\uppi_{1}
is strongly executable somewhere in the model and
\uppi_{2}
is strongly executable at all the states that are reachable via
\uppi_{1}
from states where
\uppi_{1}
is strongly executable. Otherwise,
\uppi_{1}\mathbin{\bm{;}}\uppi_{2}=\varnothing
. This guarantees that
\uppi_{1}\mathbin{\bm{;}}\uppi_{2}
contains only suitable plans. For multiple sets of plans
\uppi_{1},\dots,\uppi_{k}\in\mathrm{S}
, the
\mathrm{SE}
-composition
\uppi_{1}\mathbin{\bm{;}}\cdots\mathbin{\bm{;}}\uppi_{k}
is the set of plans
\uppi_{1}\cdots\uppi_{k}
if and only if
\mathrm{SE}(\uppi_{1})\neq\varnothing
and
\mathrm{R}_{\uppi_{i}}(\mathrm{SE}(\uppi_{i}))\subseteq\mathrm{SE}(\uppi_{i+1})
for all
i=1,\dots,k-1
, and
\varnothing
otherwise.

The following lemma establishes important properties of the just defined
\mathrm{SE}
-composition. They will be helpful in the rest of the section.

Lemma 2
.

Let
\mathcal{M}=\langle\mathrm{W},\mathrm{R},\mathrm{S},\mathrm{V}\rangle
be an
\mathrm{LTS}^{U}
with
\uppi_{1},\dots,\uppi_{k}\in\mathrm{S}
. Then,

(1)

\uppi_{1}\mathbin{\bm{;}}\cdots\mathbin{\bm{;}}\uppi_{k}\neq\varnothing
if and only if
\uppi_{i}\mathbin{\bm{;}}\uppi_{i+1}\neq\varnothing
for all
i=1,\dots,k-1
.

(2)

\uppi_{1}\mathbin{\bm{;}}\cdots\mathbin{\bm{;}}\uppi_{k}\neq\varnothing
implies
\mathrm{SE}(\uppi_{1})=\mathrm{SE}(\uppi_{1}\mathbin{\bm{;}}\dots\mathbin{\bm{;}}\uppi_{k})
.

Proof.
.

For the first, consider the left-to-right direction. If
\uppi_{1}\mathbin{\bm{;}}\cdots\mathbin{\bm{;}}\uppi_{k}\neq\varnothing
, then
\mathrm{SE}(\uppi_{1})\neq\varnothing
. Moreover, for all
i=1,\dots,k-1
, if
\mathrm{SE}(\uppi_{i})\neq\varnothing
, then
\mathrm{SE}(\uppi_{i+1})\neq\varnothing
(because
\mathrm{R}_{\uppi_{i}}(\mathrm{SE}(\uppi_{i}))\subseteq\mathrm{SE}(\uppi_{i+1})
). Therefore,
\mathrm{SE}(\uppi_{i})\neq\varnothing
for all
i=1,\dots,k-1
. Using
Definition 7.2
, for all
i=1,\dots,k-1
we have
\uppi_{i}\mathbin{\bm{;}}\uppi_{i+1}\neq\varnothing
. The other direction is direct.

For the second, suppose
\uppi_{1}\mathbin{\bm{;}}\cdots\mathbin{\bm{;}}\uppi_{k}\neq\varnothing
. For
\bm{(\subseteq)}
, proceed by contradiction: assume there is
w\in\mathrm{SE}(\uppi_{1})
with
w\not\in\mathrm{SE}(\uppi_{1}\mathbin{\bm{;}}\cdots\mathbin{\bm{;}}\uppi_{k})
. From the latter,
w\not\in\mathrm{SE}(\sigma)
for some
\sigma=\sigma_{1}\dots\sigma_{k}\in\uppi_{1}\mathbin{\bm{;}}\cdots\mathbin{\bm{;}}\uppi_{k}
. Thus, there are
d<k
and
w=v_{1},\dots,v_{d+1}=v
such that
v_{i+1}\in\mathrm{R}_{\sigma_{i}}(v_{i})
and
v\not\in\mathrm{SE}(\sigma_{d+1})
. By hypothesis,
v_{1}=w\in\mathrm{SE}(\uppi_{1})
and for all
i=1,\dots,d
, if
v_{i}\in\mathrm{SE}(\uppi_{i})
, then we have
v_{i+1}\in\mathrm{SE}(\uppi_{i+1})
(since
v_{i+1}\in\mathrm{R}_{\uppi_{i}}(v_{i})
and
\mathrm{R}_{\uppi_{i}}(\mathrm{SE}(\uppi_{i}))\subseteq\mathrm{SE}(\uppi_{i+1})
). Hence,
v_{d+1}=v\in\mathrm{SE}(\uppi_{d+1})
and therefore
v\in\mathrm{SE}(\sigma_{d+1})
, a contradiction. Thus,
w\in\mathrm{SE}(\uppi_{1}\mathbin{\bm{;}}\dots\mathbin{\bm{;}}\uppi_{k})
, and therefore
\mathrm{SE}(\uppi_{1})\subseteq\mathrm{SE}(\uppi_{1}\mathbin{\bm{;}}\dots\mathbin{\bm{;}}\uppi_{k})
. The direction
\bm{(\supseteq)}
is rather immediate.

\blacksquare

\triangleleft

Now, here are the crucial properties we will require of
\mathrm{LTS}^{U}
, to establish the intended correspondence with
\mathrm{LTS}
s.

Definition 7.3
.

We say that an
\mathrm{LTS}^{U}

\mathcal{M}=\langle\mathrm{W},\mathrm{R},\mathrm{S},\mathrm{V}\rangle
is:

•

active
if and only if there exists
\uppi\in\mathrm{S}
such that

\mathrm{SE}(\uppi)=\mathrm{W}
and, for all
u,v\in\mathrm{W}
,

v\in\mathrm{R}_{\uppi}(u)
implies
\mathcal{M},u\mathrel{\,\raisebox{2.15277pt}{\small$\underline{\!\leftrightarrow\!}$}\,}\mathcal{M},v
.

•

\mathrm{SE}
-compositional
if and only if for all
\uppi_{1},\uppi_{2}\in\mathrm{S}
with
\uppi_{1}\mathbin{\bm{;}}\uppi_{2}\neq\varnothing
there exists
\uppi\in\mathrm{S}
such that:

(1)

\mathrm{R}_{\uppi_{1}\mathbin{\bm{;}}\uppi_{2}}\subseteq\mathrm{R}_{\uppi}
,

(2)

\mathrm{SE}(\uppi_{1}\mathbin{\bm{;}}\uppi_{2})\subseteq\mathrm{SE}(\uppi)
, and

(3)

for all
(w,v)\in\mathrm{R}_{\uppi}
there exists
(w^{\prime},v^{\prime})\in\mathrm{R}_{\uppi_{1}\mathbin{\bm{;}}\uppi_{2}}
such that
\mathcal{M},w\mathrel{\,\raisebox{2.15277pt}{\small$\underline{\!\leftrightarrow\!}$}\,}\mathcal{M},w^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{M},v\mathrel{\,\raisebox{2.15277pt}{\small$\underline{\!\leftrightarrow\!}$}\,}\mathcal{M},v^{\prime}
.

We define the class
\bm{\mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{AC}}}:=\{\mathcal{M}\mid\mathcal{M}\mbox{ is active and $\mathrm{SE}$-compositional}\}
.

\dashv

While activeness ensures that there is a set of plans doing what the empty plan
\epsilon
does in an
\mathrm{LTS}
,
\mathrm{SE}
-compositionality ensures that
\mathrm{S}
is closed under a suitable notion of composition of sets of plans. The use of bisimilarity gives us a slightly more general class of models.

The next lemma establishes that the requirements for
\mathrm{SE}
-compositionality generalize to an arbitrary number of sets of plans.

Lemma 3
.

Let
\mathcal{M}=\langle\mathrm{W},\mathrm{R},\mathrm{S},\mathrm{V}\rangle
be an
\mathrm{SE}
-compositional
\mathrm{LTS}^{U}
, and take
\uppi_{1},\dots,\uppi_{k}\in\mathrm{S}
(with
k\geq 2
) such that
\uppi_{1}\mathbin{\bm{;}}\cdots\mathbin{\bm{;}}\uppi_{k}\neq\varnothing
. Then, there is
\uppi\in\mathrm{S}
such that:

(1)

\mathrm{R}_{\uppi_{1}\mathbin{\bm{;}}\dots\mathbin{\bm{;}}\uppi_{k}}\subseteq\mathrm{R}_{\uppi}
,

(2)

\mathrm{SE}(\uppi_{1}\mathbin{\bm{;}}\dots\mathbin{\bm{;}}\uppi_{k})\subseteq\mathrm{SE}(\uppi)
, and

(3)

for all
(w,v)\in\mathrm{R}_{\uppi}
, there exists
(w^{\prime},v^{\prime})\in\mathrm{R}_{\uppi_{1}\mathbin{\bm{;}}\dots\mathbin{\bm{;}}\uppi_{k}}
such that
\mathcal{M},w\mathrel{\,\raisebox{2.15277pt}{\small$\underline{\!\leftrightarrow\!}$}\,}\mathcal{M},w^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{M},v\mathrel{\,\raisebox{2.15277pt}{\small$\underline{\!\leftrightarrow\!}$}\,}\mathcal{M},v^{\prime}
.

Proof.
.

We prove the existence of
\uppi
by induction on
k\geq 2
; then we will show that this witness does the work. The base case
k=2
follows from the definition, so take sets of plans in
\mathrm{S}
such that
\uppi_{1}\mathbin{\bm{;}}\cdots\mathbin{\bm{;}}\uppi_{k}\mathbin{\bm{;}}\uppi_{k+1}\neq\varnothing
. By
Lemma 2
,
\uppi_{2}\mathbin{\bm{;}}\cdots\mathbin{\bm{;}}\uppi_{k}\mathbin{\bm{;}}\uppi_{k+1}\neq\varnothing
and thus, by inductive hypothesis, there is a
\uppi^{\prime}\in\mathrm{S}
such that

(1)

\mathrm{R}_{\uppi_{2}\mathbin{\bm{;}}\dots\mathbin{\bm{;}}\uppi_{k+1}}\subseteq\mathrm{R}_{\uppi^{\prime}}
,

(2)

\mathrm{SE}(\uppi_{2}\mathbin{\bm{;}}\dots\mathbin{\bm{;}}\uppi_{k+1})\subseteq\mathrm{SE}(\uppi^{\prime})
, and

(3)

for all
(w,v)\in\mathrm{R}_{\uppi^{\prime}}
there exists
(w^{\prime},v^{\prime})\in\mathrm{R}_{\uppi_{2}\mathbin{\bm{;}}\dots\mathbin{\bm{;}}\uppi_{k+1}}
such that
\mathcal{M},w\mathrel{\,\raisebox{2.15277pt}{\small$\underline{\!\leftrightarrow\!}$}\,}\mathcal{M},w^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{M},v\mathrel{\,\raisebox{2.15277pt}{\small$\underline{\!\leftrightarrow\!}$}\,}\mathcal{M},v^{\prime}
.

Note also how
\mathrm{SE}(\uppi_{1})\neq\varnothing
and
\mathrm{R}_{\uppi_{1}}(\mathrm{SE}(\uppi_{1}))\subseteq\mathrm{SE}(\uppi_{2})=\mathrm{SE}(\uppi_{2}\mathbin{\bm{;}}\dots\mathbin{\bm{;}}\uppi_{k+1})\subseteq\mathrm{SE}(\uppi^{\prime})
(by definition of
\mathrm{SE}
-composition,
Lemma 2
and the second property of
\uppi^{\prime}
). Thus,
\uppi_{1}\mathbin{\bm{;}}\uppi^{\prime}\neq\varnothing
and hence there is a
\uppi\in\mathrm{S}
such that

(1)

\mathrm{R}_{\uppi_{1}\mathbin{\bm{;}}\uppi^{\prime}}\subseteq\mathrm{R}_{\uppi}
,

(2)

\mathrm{SE}(\uppi_{1}\mathbin{\bm{;}}\uppi^{\prime})\subseteq\mathrm{SE}(\uppi)
, and

(3)

for all
(w,v)\in\mathrm{R}_{\uppi}
there exists
(w^{\prime},v^{\prime})\in\mathrm{R}_{\uppi_{1}\mathbin{\bm{;}}\uppi^{\prime}}
such that
\mathcal{M},w\mathrel{\,\raisebox{2.15277pt}{\small$\underline{\!\leftrightarrow\!}$}\,}\mathcal{M},w^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{M},v\mathrel{\,\raisebox{2.15277pt}{\small$\underline{\!\leftrightarrow\!}$}\,}\mathcal{M},v^{\prime}
.

We will prove that
\uppi
is the witness we are looking for.

For
Item (1)
, take
(w,v)\in\mathrm{R}_{\uppi_{1}\mathbin{\bm{;}}\dots\mathbin{\bm{;}}\uppi_{k+1}}
. Then, there exists
u\in\mathrm{W}
such that
(w,u)\in\mathrm{R}_{\uppi_{1}}
and
(u,v)\in\mathrm{R}_{\uppi_{2}\mathbin{\bm{;}}\dots\mathbin{\bm{;}}\uppi_{k+1}}
. Hence, we have
(w,u)\in\mathrm{R}_{\uppi_{1}}
and
(u,v)\in\mathrm{R}_{\uppi^{\prime}}
, so
(w,v)\in\mathrm{R}_{\uppi_{1}\mathbin{\bm{;}}\uppi^{\prime}}
and therefore
(w,v)\in\mathrm{R}_{\uppi}
.

For
Item (2)
, take
w\in\mathrm{SE}(\uppi_{1}\mathbin{\bm{;}}\dots\mathbin{\bm{;}}\uppi_{k+1})
. By
Lemma 2
,
w\in\mathrm{SE}(\uppi_{1})
and, by the same lemma,
w\in\mathrm{SE}(\uppi_{1}\mathbin{\bm{;}}\uppi^{\prime})
. Hence,
w\in\mathrm{SE}(\uppi)
.

For
Item (3)
, take
(w,v)\in\mathrm{R}_{\uppi}
. Then, there exists
(w^{\prime},v^{\prime})\in\mathrm{R}_{\uppi_{1}\mathbin{\bm{;}}\uppi^{\prime}}
such that
\mathcal{M},w\mathrel{\,\raisebox{2.15277pt}{\small$\underline{\!\leftrightarrow\!}$}\,}\mathcal{M},w^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{M},v\mathrel{\,\raisebox{2.15277pt}{\small$\underline{\!\leftrightarrow\!}$}\,}\mathcal{M},v^{\prime}
. Thus,
(w^{\prime},u^{\prime})\in\mathrm{R}_{\uppi_{1}}
and
(u^{\prime},v^{\prime})\in\mathrm{R}_{\uppi^{\prime}}
for some
u^{\prime}\in\mathrm{W}
. Again, since
(u^{\prime},v^{\prime})\in\mathrm{R}_{\uppi^{\prime}}
, there exists
(u^{\prime\prime},v^{\prime\prime})\in\mathrm{R}_{\uppi_{2}\mathbin{\bm{;}}\dots\mathbin{\bm{;}}\uppi_{k+1}}
such that
\mathcal{M},u^{\prime}\mathrel{\,\raisebox{2.15277pt}{\small$\underline{\!\leftrightarrow\!}$}\,}\mathcal{M},u^{\prime\prime}
and
\mathcal{M},v^{\prime}\mathrel{\,\raisebox{2.15277pt}{\small$\underline{\!\leftrightarrow\!}$}\,}\mathcal{M},v^{\prime\prime}
. Using that
\mathrel{\,\raisebox{2.15277pt}{\small$\underline{\!\leftrightarrow\!}$}\,}
is transitive, there exists
(w^{\prime},v^{\prime\prime})\in\mathrm{R}_{\uppi_{1}\mathbin{\bm{;}}\dots\mathbin{\bm{;}}\uppi_{k+1}}

8
Finite model property and complexity

This section is devoted to the study of the computational complexity of the logic
\mathsf{L}_{\mathsf{Kh}_{i}}
over
\mathrm{LTS}^{U}
s. To do so, we will use two standard tools from modal logic: filtration and selection (see, e.g.,
[
9
]
for details). First, we define a notion of filtration that, given an arbitrary model and a formula, allows us to obtain a finite model that satisfies the formula if and only if the original model satisfies it. This proves that the satisfiability problem for
\mathsf{L}_{\mathsf{Kh}_{i}}
is decidable. Then, we define a (more specialized) selection function which, from a canonical model, enables us to extract a polynomial-size model. Thus, we show that the satisfiability problem for
\mathsf{L}_{\mathsf{Kh}_{i}}
is
NP
-complete (given that we provide a model checking algorithm running in
P
).

8.1
Finite model property via filtrations

We start by introducing two relations that will be crucial to define a proper notion of filtration, given a set of formulas
\Sigma
and a model
\mathcal{M}
.

Definition 8.1
(
\Sigma
-equivalence).

Let
\mathcal{M}=\langle\mathrm{W},\mathrm{R},\{\mathrm{S}_{i}\}_{i\in{\rm\sf Agt}},\mathrm{V}\rangle
be an
\mathrm{LTS}^{U}
and let
\Sigma
be a set of
\mathsf{L}_{\mathsf{Kh}_{i}}
-formulas closed under subformulas. Define the relations
{\mathrel{\leftrightsquigarrow}_{\Sigma}}\subseteq\mathrm{W}\times\mathrm{W}
and
{\mathrel{\small{\leftrightarrows}}_{\Sigma}}\subseteq\mathrm{S}_{\rm\sf Agt}\times\mathrm{S}_{\rm\sf Agt}
(with
\mathrm{S}_{\rm\sf Agt}:=\bigcup_{i\in{\rm\sf Agt}}\mathrm{S}_{i}
) as:

w\mathrel{\leftrightsquigarrow}_{\Sigma}v

\mbox{\it iff}_{\mbox{\tiny\it def}}

for all
\psi\in\Sigma
,
\mathcal{M},w\models\psi
iff
\mathcal{M},v\models\psi
,

\uppi\mathrel{\small{\leftrightarrows}}_{\Sigma}\uppi^{\prime}

\mbox{\it iff}_{\mbox{\tiny\it def}}

for all
i\in{\rm\sf Agt}
and
\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\psi,\varphi)\in\Sigma
,
\uppi
is a witness

for
\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\psi,\varphi)
in
\mathcal{M}
iff
\uppi^{\prime}
is a witness for
\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\psi,\varphi)
in
\mathcal{M}
.

Notice that
\mathrel{\leftrightsquigarrow}_{\Sigma}
(a generalisation of
\mathrel{\leftrightsquigarrow}
in
Definition 5.3
to a given set of formulas) and
\mathrel{\small{\leftrightarrows}}_{\Sigma}
are equivalence relations over
\mathrm{W}
and
\mathrm{S}_{\rm\sf Agt}
, respectively. For
w\in\mathrm{W}
(resp.,
\uppi\in 2^{({\rm\sf Act}^{*})}
), we use
[w]_{\Sigma}
(resp.,
[\uppi]_{\Sigma}
) to denote
w
’s (resp.,
\uppi
’s)
\Sigma
-equivalence class; i.e.,

[w]_{\Sigma}:=\{v\in\mathrm{W}\mid w\mathrel{\leftrightsquigarrow}_{\Sigma}v\}
;
[\uppi]_{\Sigma}:=\{\uppi^{\prime}\in 2^{({\rm\sf Act}^{*})}\mid\uppi\mathrel{\small{\leftrightarrows}}_{\Sigma}\uppi^{\prime}\}
.

\dashv

Although the notation
[\_]_{\Sigma}
is overloaded, its argument will always disambiguate its use.

Definition 8.2
.

Let
\mathcal{M}=\langle\mathrm{W},\mathrm{R},\{\mathrm{S}_{i}\}_{i\in{\rm\sf Agt}},\mathrm{V}\rangle
be an
\mathrm{LTS}^{U}
and let
\Sigma
be a set of
\mathsf{L}_{\mathsf{Kh}_{i}}
-formulas that is closed under subformulas. For
i\in{\rm\sf Agt}
define
{\rm\sf Act}^{\Sigma}_{i}:=\{a_{[\uppi]_{\Sigma}}\mid\uppi\in\mathrm{S}_{i}\text{ is a witness of some }\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\psi,\varphi)\in\Sigma\text{ in }\mathcal{M}\}
;
and
{\rm\sf Act}^{\Sigma}:=\bigcup_{i\in{\rm\sf Agt}}{\rm\sf Act}^{\Sigma}_{i}
.

\dashv

The idea behind the definition of
{\rm\sf Act}^{\Sigma}
is that, for each
\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\psi,\varphi)\in\Sigma
that is true at
\mathcal{M}
, we consider an action mimicking the behaviour of those sets of plans
\uppi
that witness the satisfiability of
\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\psi,\varphi)
in
\mathcal{M}
.

Now, we are in position of defining the notion of filtration.

Definition 8.3
(Filtration of
\mathcal{M}
through
\Sigma
).

Let
\mathcal{M}=\langle\mathrm{W},\mathrm{R},\{\mathrm{S}_{i}\}_{i\in{\rm\sf Agt}},\mathrm{V}\rangle
be an
\mathrm{LTS}^{U}
; let
\Sigma
be a set of
\mathsf{L}_{\mathsf{Kh}_{i}}
-formulas that is closed under subformulas. An
\mathrm{LTS}^{U}

\mathcal{M}^{f}=\langle\mathrm{W}^{f},\mathrm{R}^{f},\{\mathrm{S}^{f}_{i}\}_{i\in{\rm\sf Agt}},\mathrm{V}^{f}\rangle
is
a filtration of
\mathcal{M}
through
\Sigma
if and only if it satisfies the following conditions:

(1)

\mathrm{W}^{f}:=\{[w]_{\Sigma}\mid w\in\mathrm{W}\}
;

(2)

\mathrm{V}^{f}([w]_{\Sigma}):=\{p\in\Sigma\mid\mathcal{M},w\models p\}
;

(3)

for all
i\in{\rm\sf Agt}
,
a_{[\uppi]_{\Sigma}}\in{\rm\sf Act}^{\Sigma}_{i}
implies
\{a_{[\uppi]_{\Sigma}}\}\in\mathrm{S}^{f}_{i}
;

(4)

\mathrm{S}_{i}^{f}
is finite and well-defined (as per
Remark 1
), and each
\uppi\in\mathrm{S}^{f}_{i}
is finite;

(5)

for all
\uppi\in\mathrm{S}^{f}_{i}
, if
\uppi
is a witness of some
\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\psi,\varphi)\in\Sigma
in
\mathcal{M}^{f}
, then there is
\uppi^{\prime}\in\mathrm{S}_{i}
such that
\uppi^{\prime}
is a witness of
\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\psi,\varphi)
in
\mathcal{M}
;

(6)

if
(w,v)\in\mathrm{R}_{\uppi}
and
a_{[\uppi]_{\Sigma}}\in{\rm\sf Act}^{\Sigma}_{i}
, then
([w]_{\Sigma},[v]_{\Sigma})\in\mathrm{R}^{f}_{a_{[\uppi]_{\Sigma}}}
;

(7)

if
([w]_{\Sigma},[v]_{\Sigma})\in\mathrm{R}^{f}_{a_{[\uppi]_{\Sigma}}}
, and
\uppi
is a witness of some
\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\psi,\varphi)\in\Sigma
in
\mathcal{M}
, then
w\in\llbracket\psi\rrbracket^{\mathcal{M}}
implies
v\in\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket^{\mathcal{M}}
.

\dashv

Note that
\mathrm{V}^{f}
is well-defined: given
p\in\Sigma
, if
[w]_{\Sigma}=[v]_{\Sigma}
and
\mathcal{M},w\models p
, then
\mathcal{M},v\models p
. Also, as
\mathrm{S}^{f}_{i}
is well-defined (by definition), we have that
\mathcal{M}^{f}
is an
\mathrm{LTS}^{U}
over
{\rm\sf Act}^{\Sigma}
,
{\rm\sf Prop}
and
{\rm\sf Agt}
. Also, note that if
(5)
,
(6)
and
(7)
above are turned into if and only if conditions, they always define an
\mathrm{LTS}^{U}
which is a filtration.

Definition 8.3
deserves further comments. Notice that, for the
\mathrm{LTS}
part, the filtration is defined similarly as for the basic modal logic (see, e.g.,
[
9
]
). The most significant difference is the change in the labelling of the relations, since we now use
{\rm\sf Act}^{\Sigma}
as the set of action names, instead of
{\rm\sf Act}
. But this has a consequence on the definition of
\mathrm{S}^{f}_{i}
. The relation
\mathrel{\small{\leftrightarrows}}_{\Sigma}
enables us to obtain a finite set of witnesses for the formulas
\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\psi,\varphi)\in\Sigma
, from which we also get that
{\rm\sf Act}^{\Sigma}
and (together with
\mathrel{\leftrightsquigarrow}_{\Sigma}
)
\mathrm{W}^{f}
are finite. However, the new set
\mathrm{S}^{f}_{i}
is defined in terms of a new set of action names, so there are potentially infinite new available plans to consider. Thus, we need to state that
\mathrm{S}^{f}_{i}
is any finite set, satisfying the minimum and maximum conditions, whose members are also finite, and that is well-defined. Finally,
{\rm\sf Act}^{\Sigma}
may be finite, unlike the original set of actions
{\rm\sf Act}
which is infinite by definition. However, this poses no problem in the construction, as
{\rm\sf Act}^{\Sigma}
can be extended to an infinite set, without breaking the finiteness of the filtration (recall that the accessibility relation is defined over a, potentially finite, subset of the set of actions).

Theorem 7
.

Let
\mathcal{M}=\langle\mathrm{W},\mathrm{R},\{\mathrm{S}_{i}\}_{i\in{\rm\sf Agt}},\mathrm{V}\rangle
be an
\mathrm{LTS}^{U}
and
let
\Sigma
be a set of
\mathsf{L}_{\mathsf{Kh}_{i}}
-formulas that is closed under subformulas. Then, for all
\psi\in\Sigma
and
w\in\mathrm{W}
,
\mathcal{M},w\models\psi
iff
\mathcal{M}^{f},[w]_{\Sigma}\models\psi
. Moreover, if
\Sigma
is finite then
\mathcal{M}^{f}
is a finite model.

Proof.
.

Boolean cases work as expected. So, we will only show that
\mathcal{M},w\models\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\psi,\varphi)
iff
\mathcal{M}^{f},[w]_{\Sigma}\models\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\psi,\varphi)
.

\bm{(\Rightarrow)}
Suppose that
\mathcal{M}\models\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\psi,\varphi)
: let
\uppi\in\mathrm{S}_{i}
be such that
\llbracket\psi\rrbracket^{\mathcal{M}}\subseteq\mathrm{SE}(\uppi)
and
\mathrm{R}_{\uppi}(\llbracket\psi\rrbracket^{\mathcal{M}})\subseteq\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket^{\mathcal{M}}
.
By definition,
a_{[\uppi]_{\Sigma}}\in{\rm\sf Act}^{\Sigma}_{i}
and therefore,
\{a_{[\uppi]_{\Sigma}}\}\in\mathrm{S}^{f}_{i}
. If
\llbracket\psi\rrbracket^{\mathcal{M}^{f}}=\varnothing
, the result trivially follows. Otherwise, let
[w]_{\Sigma}\in\llbracket\psi\rrbracket^{\mathcal{M}^{f}}
.
By IH,
w\in\llbracket\psi\rrbracket^{\mathcal{M}}
, and since
\uppi
is SE at
w
,
\mathrm{R}_{\uppi}(w)\neq\varnothing
.
Since
\mathcal{M}^{f}
is a filtration, we have that
\mathrm{R}_{a_{[\uppi]_{\Sigma}}}(w)\neq\varnothing
and
\{a_{[\uppi]_{\Sigma}}\}
is SE at
[w]_{\Sigma}
. Thus,
\llbracket\psi\rrbracket^{\mathcal{M}^{f}}\subseteq\mathrm{SE}(\{a_{[\uppi]_{\Sigma}}\})
.

Let
([w]_{\Sigma},[v]_{\Sigma})\in\mathrm{R}^{f}_{a_{[\uppi]_{\Sigma}}}
be such that
[w]_{\Sigma}\in\llbracket\psi\rrbracket^{\mathcal{M}^{f}}
. By IH,
w\in\llbracket\psi\rrbracket^{\mathcal{M}}
. Since
\uppi
is a witness of
\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\psi,\varphi)\in\Sigma
in
\mathcal{M}
(by assumption), by the definition of
\mathcal{M}^{f}
we get
v\in\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket^{\mathcal{M}}
. Again, by IH,
[v]_{\Sigma}\in\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket^{\mathcal{M}}
.
Thus,
\mathrm{R}^{f}_{\{a_{[\uppi]_{\Sigma}}\}}(\llbracket\psi\rrbracket^{\mathcal{M}})\subseteq\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket^{\mathcal{M}}
. Therefore,
\mathcal{M}^{f}\models\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\psi,\varphi)
.

\bm{(\Leftarrow)}
Suppose that
\mathcal{M}^{f}\models\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\psi,\varphi)
: let
\uppi\in\mathrm{S}^{f}_{i}
be such that
\llbracket\psi\rrbracket^{\mathcal{M}^{f}}\subseteq\mathrm{SE}(\uppi)
and
\mathrm{R}_{\uppi}(\llbracket\psi\rrbracket^{\mathcal{M}^{f}})\subseteq\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket^{\mathcal{M}^{f}}
.
By definition of
\mathcal{M}^{f}
, since
\uppi
is a witness of
\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\psi,\varphi)\in\Sigma
in
\mathcal{M}^{f}
, we have that there is
\uppi^{\prime}\in\mathrm{S}_{i}
such that
\uppi^{\prime}
is a witness of
\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\psi,\varphi)
in
\mathcal{M}
.
Thus,
\mathcal{M}\models\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\psi,\varphi)
.

It remains to show that
\mathcal{M}^{f}
is finite. First, note that the number of elements in
\mathrm{W}^{f}
is
2^{m}
, with
m
being the number of formulas in
\Sigma
. By definition, for all
i\in{\rm\sf Agt}
,
{\rm\sf Act}^{\Sigma}_{i}
is at most the number of
\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\psi,\varphi)\in\Sigma
, since if there are two groups of witnesses
[\uppi]_{\Sigma}
and
[\uppi^{\prime}]_{\Sigma}
for some
\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\psi,\varphi)
,
[\uppi]_{\Sigma}=[\uppi^{\prime}]_{\Sigma}
. Hence,
{\rm\sf Act}^{\Sigma}
is polynomial in the number of
\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\psi,\varphi)
in
\Sigma
. Finally, by definition,
\mathrm{S}^{f}_{i}
is finite. Thus,
\mathcal{M}^{f}
is finite.

\blacksquare

\triangleleft

The last theorem states that every satisfiable formula of
\mathsf{L}_{\mathsf{Kh}_{i}}
, is satisfiable in a finite model. As a consequence, the satisfiability problem for
\mathsf{L}_{\mathsf{Kh}_{i}}
is decidable. In the next section we will refine this result and provide exact complexity bounds.

8.2
Complexity via selection

Here we investigate the computational complexity of the satisfiability
problem of
\mathsf{L}_{\mathsf{Kh}_{i}}
under the
\mathrm{LTS}^{U}
-based semantics. We will
establish membership in
NP
by showing a polynomial-size model property.

Given a formula, we will show that it is possible to select just a
piece of the canonical model which is relevant for its evaluation. The
selected model will preserve satisfiability, and moreover, its size
will be polynomial w.r.t. the size of the input formula.

Definition 8.4
(Selection function).

Let

\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}=\langle\mathrm{W}^{\Gamma},\mathrm{R}^{\Gamma},\{\mathrm{S}_{i}^{\Gamma}\}_{i\in{\rm\sf Agt}},\mathrm{V}^{\Gamma}\rangle

be a canonical model for an MCS
\Gamma
(see

Definition 6.1
); take
w\in\mathrm{W}^{\Gamma}
and a formula

\varphi\in\mathsf{L}_{\mathsf{Kh}_{i}}
. Define

{\rm\sf Act}_{\varphi}:=\{\langle\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\rangle\in{\rm\sf Act}^{\Gamma}\mid\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})\text{ is a subformula of }\varphi\}
. A

canonical selection function

\mathsf{sel}^{\varphi}_{w}
is a
function that takes
\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}
,
w
and
\varphi
as input, returns a
set
\mathrm{W}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathrm{W}^{\Gamma}
, and is such that:

(1)

\mathsf{sel}^{\varphi}_{w}(p)=\{w\}
;

(2)

\mathsf{sel}^{\varphi}_{w}(\neg\varphi_{1})=\mathsf{sel}^{\varphi}_{w}(\varphi_{1})

(3)

\mathsf{sel}^{\varphi}_{w}(\varphi_{1}\vee\varphi_{2})=\mathsf{sel}^{\varphi}_{w}(\varphi_{1})\cup\mathsf{sel}^{\varphi}_{w}(\varphi_{2})
;

(4)

If

\llbracket\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})\rrbracket^{\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}}\neq\varnothing\text{ and }\llbracket\varphi_{1}\rrbracket^{\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}}=\varnothing
:

\mathsf{sel}^{\varphi}_{w}(\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}))=\{w\}
;

(5)

If

\llbracket\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})\rrbracket^{\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}}\neq\varnothing\text{ and }\llbracket\varphi_{1}\rrbracket^{\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}}\neq\varnothing
:

\mathsf{sel}^{\varphi}_{w}(\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}))=\{w_{1},w_{2}\}\cup\mathsf{sel}^{\varphi}_{w_{1}}(\varphi_{1})\cup\mathsf{sel}^{\varphi}_{w_{2}}(\varphi_{2})
,
where
w_{1}
,
w_{2}
are
s.t.
(w_{1},w_{2})\in\mathrm{R}^{\Gamma}_{\langle\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\rangle}
;

(6)

If

\llbracket\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})\rrbracket^{\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}}=\varnothing
(note
that
\llbracket\varphi_{1}\rrbracket^{\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}}\neq\varnothing
):

For all set of plans
\uppi
, either

\llbracket\varphi_{1}\rrbracket^{\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}}\not\subseteq\mathrm{SE}(\uppi)

or

\mathrm{R}_{\uppi}^{\Gamma}(\llbracket\varphi_{1}\rrbracket^{\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}})\not\subseteq\llbracket\varphi_{2}\rrbracket^{\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}}
. For each
a\in{\rm\sf Act}_{\varphi}
:

(a)

if

\llbracket\varphi_{1}\rrbracket^{\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}}\not\subseteq\mathrm{SE}(\{a\})
:
we add
\{w_{1}\}\cup\mathsf{sel}^{\varphi}_{w_{1}}(\varphi_{1})
to

\mathsf{sel}^{\varphi}_{w}(\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}))
, where

w_{1}\in\llbracket\varphi_{1}\rrbracket^{\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}}
and

w_{1}\notin\mathrm{SE}(\{a\})
;

(b)

if

\mathrm{R}_{\uppi}^{\Gamma}(\llbracket\varphi_{1}\rrbracket^{\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}})\not\subseteq\llbracket\varphi_{2}\rrbracket^{\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}}
we add

\{w_{1},w_{2}\}\cup\mathsf{sel}^{\varphi}_{w_{1}}(\varphi_{1})\cup\mathsf{sel}^{\varphi}_{w_{2}}(\varphi_{2})
to

\mathsf{sel}^{\varphi}_{w}(\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}))
, where

w_{1}\in\llbracket\varphi_{1}\rrbracket^{\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}}
,

w_{2}\in\mathrm{R}^{\Gamma}_{a}(w_{1})
and

w_{2}\notin\llbracket\varphi_{2}\rrbracket^{\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}}
.

\dashv

We can now select a small model which preserves the
satisfiability of a given formula.

Definition 8.5
(Selected model).

Let
\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}
be the canonical model for an MCS
\Gamma
,
w
a
state in
\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}
, and
\varphi
an
\mathsf{L}_{\mathsf{Kh}_{i}}
-formula. Let

\mathsf{sel}^{\varphi}_{w}
be a selection function, we define the

\mathsf{sel}^{\varphi}_{w}
as

\mathcal{M}^{\varphi}_{w}=\langle\mathrm{W}^{\varphi}_{w},\mathrm{R}^{\varphi}_{w},\{(\mathrm{S}^{\varphi}_{w})_{i}\}_{i\in{\rm\sf Agt}},\mathrm{V}^{\varphi}_{w}\rangle
,
where

•

\mathrm{W}^{\varphi}_{w}:=\mathsf{sel}^{\varphi}_{w}(\varphi)
;

•

(\mathrm{R}^{\varphi}_{w})_{\langle\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\rangle}:=\mathrm{R}^{\Gamma}_{\langle\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\rangle}\cap(\mathrm{W}^{\varphi}_{w})^{2}
for each
\langle\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\rangle\in{\rm\sf Act}_{\varphi}

•

(and
(\mathrm{R}^{\varphi}_{w})_{\langle\bot,\top\rangle}:=\varnothing

•

\mathrm{V}^{\varphi}_{w}
is the restriction of
\mathrm{V}^{\Gamma}
to
\mathrm{W}^{\varphi}_{w}

\dashv

Note that, although

can be an empty set, each collection of sets of plans

is not. Moreover,
{\rm\sf Act}_{\varphi}
can be extended to an infinite set of actions, to be defined over a proper signature. Therefore,
\mathcal{M}^{\varphi}_{w}
is an
\mathrm{LTS}^{U}
.

Proposition 15
.

Let
\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}
be a canonical model,
w
a state in
\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}
and
\varphi
an
\mathsf{L}_{\mathsf{Kh}_{i}}
-formula. Let
\mathcal{M}^{\varphi}_{w}
be the selected model by a selection function
\mathsf{sel}^{\varphi}_{w}
.
Then,
\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma},w\models\varphi
implies that
for all
\psi
subformula of
\varphi
, and for all
v\in\mathrm{W}^{\varphi}_{w}
we have that

\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma},v\models\psi
iff
\mathcal{M}^{\varphi}_{w},v\models\psi
.
Moreover,
\mathcal{M}^{\varphi}_{w}
is polynomial on the size of
\varphi
.

Proof.
.

The proof proceeds by induction in the size of the formula:

Case
\psi=p
: if
\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma},v\models p
, then
p\in\mathrm{V}^{\Gamma}(v)
. Given that
v\in\mathrm{W}^{\varphi}_{w}
, we have
p\in\mathrm{V}^{\varphi}_{w}(v)
and therefore
\mathcal{M}^{\varphi}_{w},v\models p
. The other direction is similar.

Case
\psi=\neg\psi_{1}
: if
\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma},w\models\neg\psi_{1}
, then
\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma},w\not\models\psi_{1}
. By IH,
\mathcal{M}^{\varphi}_{w},w\not\models\psi_{1}
and therefore
\mathcal{M}^{\varphi}_{w},w\models\neg\psi_{1}
. The other direction is similar.

Case
\psi=\psi_{1}\vee\psi_{2}
: if
\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma},v\models\psi_{1}\vee\psi_{2}
, then
\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma},v\models\psi_{1}
or
\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma},v\models\psi_{2}
. By IH,
\mathcal{M}^{\varphi}_{w},v\models\psi_{1}
or
\mathcal{M}^{\varphi}_{w},v\models\psi_{2}
and therefore
\mathcal{M}^{\varphi}_{w},v\models\psi_{1}\vee\psi_{2}
. The other direction is similar.

Case
\psi=\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})
:
Suppose that
\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma},v\models\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})
. We consider two possibilities:

•

In order to prove that the satisfiability problem of
\mathsf{L}_{\mathsf{Kh}_{i}}

is in
NP

P
.

Proposition 16
.

The model checking problem for
\mathsf{L}_{\mathsf{Kh}_{i}}
is in
P
.

Proof.
.

Given a pointed
\mathrm{LTS}^{U}

\mathcal{M},w
and a formula
\varphi
, we define
a bottom-up labeling algorithm running in polynomial time which checks whether

\mathcal{M},w\models\varphi
. We follow
the same ideas as for the basic modal logic
K
(see
e.g.,
[
10
]
). Below we introduce the case for formulas
of the shape
\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\psi,\varphi)
, over an
\mathrm{LTS}^{U}

\mathcal{M}=\langle\mathrm{W},\mathrm{R},\mathrm{S},\mathrm{V}\rangle
:

Procedure
ModelChecking(
(\mathcal{M},w)
,
\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\psi,\varphi))

lab(\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\psi,\varphi))\leftarrow\varnothing
;

for all

\uppi\in\mathrm{S}_{i}

do

kh\leftarrow True
;

for all

\sigma\in\uppi

do

for all

v\in lab(\psi)

do

kh\leftarrow(kh\ \&\ v\in\mathrm{SE}(\sigma)\ \&\ \mathrm{R}_{\sigma}(v)\subseteq lab(\varphi))
;

end

for

end

for

if

kh

then

lab(\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\psi,\varphi))\leftarrow\mathrm{W}
;

end

if

end

for

As
\mathrm{S}_{i}
and each
\uppi\in\mathrm{S}_{i}
are not empty, the first two

for
loops are necessarily executed. If

lab(\psi)=\varnothing
, then the formula
\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\psi,\varphi)
is
trivially true. Otherwise,
kh
will remain true only if the
appropriate conditions for the satisfiability of

\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\psi,\varphi))
hold. If no
\uppi
succeeds, then the

be overwritten, as it should be. Both

and

\mathrm{R}_{\sigma}
can be verified in polynomial time. Hence, the model

.

\blacksquare

The intended result for satisfiability now follows.

.

The satisfiability problem for
\mathsf{L}_{\mathsf{Kh}_{i}}
over
{\mathrm{LTS}^{U}}s

-complete.

Proof.
.

Hardness follows from

\mathsf{L}_{\mathsf{Kh}_{i}}

By

\blacksquare

9
Final remarks

In this article, we introduce a new semantics for the
knowing
how
modality from
[
57
,
58
,
59
]
, for multiple
agents. It is defined in terms of
uncertainty-based labeled
transition systems (
\mathrm{LTS}^{U}
)
. The novelty in our proposal is that

{\mathrm{LTS}^{U}}s
are equipped with an indistinguishability relation among
plans. In this way, the epistemic notion of uncertainty of an agent
–which in turn defines her epistemic state– is reintroduced, bringing the notion of
knowing how
closer to the notion of
knowing that
from classical epistemic
logics. We believe that the semantics based on
\mathrm{LTS}^{U}
can represent properly the
situation of a shared, objective description of the affordances of a
given situation, together with the different, subjective and personal
abilities of a group of agents; this seems difficult to achieve using
a semantics based on LTSs alone.

We show that the logic of
[
57
,
58
,
59
]
can be obtained
by imposing particular conditions over
\mathrm{LTS}^{U}
; thus, the new semantics is more general.
In particular, it provides counter-examples to
\mathpzc{EMP}
and
\mathpzc{COMP}
, which directly link the knowing how modality
\mathsf{Kh}
to
properties of the universal modality.
Indeed, consider
\mathpzc{EMP}
: even though
\mathsf{A}(\psi\rightarrow\varphi)
objectively holds in the underlying
LTS of an
\mathrm{LTS}^{U}
, it could be argued that an agent might not be aware of actions or plans to turn those facts into knowledge, resulting in
\mathsf{Kh}(\psi,\varphi)
failing in the
model.

To characterize validities in this language over
\mathrm{LTS}^{U}
s, we introduce a sound and strongly complete axiom system.

We also define a suitable notion of bisimulation over
{\mathrm{LTS}^{U}}s
, following
ideas introduced in
[
17
,
18
]
. We show that bisimilarity implies formula equivalence, and
that finite models form a Hennessy-Milner class (i.e., that formula equivalence implies
bisimilarity over finite models).

Finally, we prove that the satisfiability problem for our multi-agent knowing
how logic over the
\mathrm{LTS}^{U}
-based semantics is
NP
-complete. The proof relies on a selection argument on the canonical model, and on the fact that the model checking problem is polynomial. We also provide a filtration technique that, given an arbitrary model satisfying
\varphi
, returns a finite model that satisfies
\varphi
.

Future work.

There are several interesting lines of research to explore in the
future. First, our framework easily accommodates other notions of
executability. For instance, one could require only some of the plans in
a set
\uppi
to be strongly executable, or weaken the condition
of
strong
executability, etc. We can also explore the
effects of imposing different restrictions
on the construction of the indistinguishability relation between plans.
It would be interesting to investigate which logics we
obtain in these cases, and their relations with the
\mathrm{LTS}
semantics.

Second, to our knowledge, the exact complexity of
the satisfiability problem for knowing how over
\mathrm{LTS}
s is open.
It would be interesting to solve this problem, for instance, by following and adapting ideas from
[
40
]
.

Third, the
\mathrm{LTS}^{U}
semantics, in the multi-agent
setting, leads to natural definitions of concepts of
collective
knowing how, in the spirit of
[
13
]
.
For instance, one can easily define a notion of
general knowing how
as

\mathsf{EKh}_{G}(\psi,\varphi):=\bigwedge_{i\in G}\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\psi,\varphi)
, whose reading is
“everyone in the group
G
knows how to achieve
\varphi
given
\psi
”
; and

“somebody in the group
G
knows how to achieve
\varphi
given
\psi
”
,
as
\mathsf{SKh}_{G}(\psi,\varphi):=\bigvee_{i\in G}\mathsf{Kh}_{i}(\psi,\varphi)
(see, e.g.,
[
1
]
for a similar approach in standard epistemic logic).
Other, more complex notions such as

distributed
and
common knowing how
,
deserve further exploration.

Finally, dynamic modalities capturing epistemic updates can be defined
via operations that modify the indistinguishability relation among
plans (as is done with other dynamic epistemic
operators, see, e.g.,
[
53
]
). This would allow to express
different forms of communication, such as
public
,

private
and
semi-private
announcements concerning (sets of) plans.
Some preliminary results have been presented in
[
4
]
.

Hodge Theoretic invariants Detecting Tautological Cycles in Moduli Space of Curves
Mohammad Reza Rahmati

Abstract
. We apply the method of higher Abel-Jacobi invariants and cycle classes of Hodge theory to special tautological classes in the Chow ring of moduli space of curves with marked points. Specifically, based on the result of Green and Griffiths of non-triviallity of Faber-Pandharipande cycle in self product of a curve of genus g g , we show certain classes of tautological cycles that we call higher FP-cycles have non-trivial AJ-invariants.

2^{\text{th}}
Faber-Pandharipande cycle

(69)

FP_{2}=\pi_{1}^{\times 2}(\Delta_{12}^{2}.\psi_{1})\in CH^{3}(C_{g}^{2})

We should already assume
\dim(C_{g}^{2})=2g-2+2=2g\geq 3=\text{codim}_{{C_{g}^{2}}}FP_{2}
. The cycle
FP_{2}
has codimension
3
in
C_{g}^{2}
. The second Faber-Pandharipande cycle is a nontrivial algebraic cycle in the tautological ring of
C_{g}^{2}
when
g
is big enough. We will give a simple proof of this based on the behavior of higher Abel-Jacobi invariants for zero cycles in the product varieties, i.e proposition 3.2 and Theorem 3.3. We proceed to check out the AJ-invariants for the restriction of
{FP}_{2}
over a generic family
\mathcal{C}\times_{S}\mathcal{C}
where
\dim(S)=1
.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\to S
be a generic family of smooth curves of genus
g
, and
\dim(S)=2
. Then the restriction of the algebraic cycle
FP_{2}
over
\mathcal{C}\times_{S}\mathcal{C}
is
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{rat}}}{{\neq}}0
when
g\geq 7
.

Proof.

Step 1:
First we show that the restriction of the cycle
FP_{1}\times FP_{1}
over
\mathcal{C}_{S}^{2}\times_{S}\mathcal{C}_{S}^{2}
is
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{rat}}}{{\neq}}0.
In fact this follows from the Theorem 3.2 and the invariants of the cycle
Z_{K}
listed above. It follows that

(70)

cl_{\mathcal{C}_{S}^{2}\times_{S}\mathcal{C}_{S}^{2}}^{4}(\mathfrak{Z}_{K}\times\mathfrak{Z}_{K})=0,\qquad AJ_{\mathcal{C}_{S}^{2}\times_{S}\mathcal{C}_{S}^{2}}^{3}(\mathfrak{Z}_{K}\times\mathfrak{Z}_{K})\neq 0

This tells that
\mathfrak{Z}_{K}\times\mathfrak{Z}_{K}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{rat}}}{{\neq}}0
and
\mathfrak{Z}_{K}\times\mathfrak{Z}_{K}\in L^{4}CH_{S}^{4}(\mathcal{C}_{S}^{2}\times_{S}\mathcal{C}_{S}^{2})
.

Step 2:
Next we note that
\mathfrak{Z}_{K}.\mathfrak{Z}_{K}={(\imath_{\Delta_{S}})}^{*}(\mathfrak{Z}_{K}\times\mathfrak{Z}_{K})\in CH^{4}(\mathcal{C}_{S}^{2})
where
\imath_{\Delta_{S}}:\mathcal{C}_{S}^{2}\to\mathcal{C}_{S}^{2}\times_{S}\mathcal{C}_{S}^{2}
is the relative diagonal. By properties of the filtration
L^{\bullet}
it follows that

\mathfrak{Z}_{K}.\mathfrak{Z}_{K}\in L^{4}CH^{4}(\mathcal{C}_{S}^{2})
is
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{rat}}}{{\neq}}0
.

Step 3:
Let
r:S\hookrightarrow M_{g}
as in the Theorem. The cycle
\mathfrak{Z}=r^{*}FP_{1}=\pi_{1}^{\times 2}\circ r^{*}(\Delta_{12}).r^{*}(\psi_{1})
is given by the similar formula on the product family
\mathcal{C}_{S}^{2}
. Set
\mathfrak{W}=r^{*}(FP_{2})
be the restriction of the second FP-cycle on
\mathcal{C}_{S}^{2}
. It does as
\mathfrak{Z}=\mathfrak{Z}_{K}
does for
FP_{1}
. Assume
\mathfrak{W}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{rat}}}{{=}}0
. We show it causes a contradiction. Let

(71)

\imath_{\Delta_{S}^{r}}:\mathcal{C}_{S}^{2}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{C}_{S}^{2}\times_{S}...\times_{S}\mathcal{C}_{S}^{2}

be the diagonal. Then we have

(72)

(\imath_{\Delta_{\mathcal{C}_{S}^{2}}})^{*}\circ\{(\pi_{1}^{\times 2})^{\text{t}}\times(\pi_{1}^{\times 2})^{\text{t}}\}(\mathfrak{W}^{\times 2})=(\imath_{\Delta_{\mathcal{C}_{S}^{3}}})^{*}\circ\{(\pi_{1}^{\times 2})^{\text{t}}\times(\pi_{1}^{\times 2})^{\text{t}}\times(\pi_{1}^{\times 2})^{\text{t}}\}(\mathfrak{Z}^{\times 3})

where they equal
r^{*}(\Delta_{12}^{4}.\psi_{1}^{2})=r^{*}(\Delta_{12}^{3}.\psi_{1}^{3})
. The operators used in equation (72) are obviously algebraic correspondences. Therefore the assumption
\mathfrak{W}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{rat}}}{{=}}0
implies
\mathfrak{Z}^{\times 3}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{rat}}}{{=}}0
and hence
\mathfrak{Z}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{rat}}}{{=}}0
by projecting onto one of the factors. This is a contradiction.

Step 4:
The proof in steps 1, 2 and 3 is based on the non-triviality proof of
\mathfrak{Z}
and that of the method by Green-Griffiths in
[
8
]
we briefly mentioned above. There, the genus condition that is
g\geq 4
is coming from the embedding dimension of the canonical curve in
\mathbb{P}^{r}
, where the genus condition translates into
r\geq 3
. The method of the proof in step 3 shows that one need to consider the canonical embedding compatible through a diagonal map of type

(73)

\mathcal{C}_{S}^{2}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{C}_{S}^{2}\times\mathcal{C}_{S}^{2}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}_{S}^{2r}

which by the above consideration requires
2r\geq 6
, which is equivalent to
g\geq 7
.
∎

The codimension
3
cycle
FP_{2}
lies lower down the Leray filtration. Specifically:
FP_{2}
is a codimension
3
cycle in
C^{2}_{g}
over
M_{g}
.
The Leray spectral sequence degenerates at
E_{2}
by the Deligne theorem. So the associated graded of the Leray filtration
L^{\bullet}
are

(74)

H^{6}(C_{g}^{2},\mathbb{C})=\bigoplus_{t}H^{6-t}(M_{g},R^{t}f_{*}\mathbb{Q})

The class of the cycle
FP_{2}
will determine a
class in
H^{6}(C_{g}^{2})
. If it lies in
L^{s}H^{6}(C_{g}^{2})
, it will determine a
class in
H^{s}(M_{g},R^{6-s}f_{*}\mathbb{Q})
. Then by the above proof
FP_{2}
lies in
L^{3}
or

L^{4}
, so that it is detected by a class in
H^{3}(M_{g},R^{3})
or
H^{4}(M_{g},R^{2})
.

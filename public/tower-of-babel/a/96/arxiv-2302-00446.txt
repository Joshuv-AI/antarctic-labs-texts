Chevalley involutions for Lie tori and extended affine Lie algebras Thanks: This work is based upon research funded by Iran National Science Foundation (INSF) under project No. 4001480. Thanks: This research was in part carried out in IPM-Isfahan Branch.
Saeid Azam, Mehdi Izadi Farhadi

Abstract
. In finite-dimensional simple Lie algebras and affine Kac-Moody Lie algebras, Chevalley involutions are crucial ingredients of the modular theory. Towards establishing the modular theory for extended affine Lie algebras, we investigate the existence of “Chevalley involutions” for Lie tori and extended affine Lie algebras. We first discuss how to lift a Chevalley involution from the centerless core which is characterized to be a centerless Lie torus to the core and then to the entire extended affine Lie algebra. We then prove by a type-dependent argument the existence of Chevalley involutions for centerless Lie tori.

1.
Introduction

The main goal of this study is to investigate the existence of Chevalley involutions for extended affine Lie algebras (EALAs).
Extended affine Lie algebras are a class of mostly infinite dimensional Lie algebras that generalize the class of finite and affine Lie algebras.
This class has been under intensive investigation for the past few decades.
For the basic
structure theory of extended affine Lie algebras, we refer the interested reader to
[
1
]
and
[
24
]
.

This work is a continuation of
[
5
]
in which
the concept of modular theory for extended affine Lie algebras was initiated. By a modular theory, we mean a higher nullity version of the seminal procedure in finite dimensional complex simple Lie algebras due to C. Chevalley which provides a framework for passing from complex numbers to any field of positive characteristic, leading to the Chevalley groups. The main ingredients of this procedure are Chevalley involutions and Chevalley bases. Roughly speaking, by means of a Chevalley involution which acts on the Cartan subalgebra as minus the identity, one constructs a Chevalley basis whose
{\mathbb{Z}}
-span provides a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-form (integral form) for the ground complex Lie algebra. Then by tensoring the provided
{\mathbb{Z}}
-form with any field of positive characteristic one obtains a modular Lie algebra
that is independent of the choice of the Chevalley base. One then proceeds to get an integral form for the corresponding universal enveloping algebra which is of great importance in the representation theory of Chevalley groups, see
[
17
]
and
[
14
]
.

The existence of Chevalley involutions is a focal point in modular theory. Naively, such involutions permit the lift of arguments that are valid locally in certain subalgebras to arguments that are valid globally. In
[
5
]
, the authors construct a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-form for the core of the considered extended affine Lie algebra, by assuming that it admits a Chevalley involution. In finite and affine theory, the existence of such involutions is guaranteed by Serre-type presentations. However, the situation for general extended affine Lie algebras is not clear at all. In this work, we investigate the existence of Chevalley involutions for (tame) extended affine Lie algebras.

H. Garland extended the theory of integral forms to untwisted affine Lie algebras by providing Chevalley bases for these algebras and presenting integral forms of corresponding enveloping algebras along with explicit
{\mathbb{Z}}
-bases of them. Also he constructed Chevalley groups associated to standard representations of untwisted affine Lie algebras, see
[
12
]
,
[
13
]
. In
[
20
]
, D. Mitzman gives, by a type-dependent approach, a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-basis for the universal enveloping algebra corresponding to a twisted affine Lie algebra. The construction of
{\mathbb{Z}}
-forms for the class of all Kac-Moody Lie algebras is due to J. Tits
[
26
]
,
[
27
]
.

We recall that an extended affine Lie algebra is a triple
(E,(\cdot,\cdot),{\mathcal{H}})
consisting of a
complex Lie algebra
E
, a Cartan subalgebra
{\mathcal{H}}
of
E
, and a symmetric invariant non-degenerate bilinear form
(\cdot,\cdot)
on
E
, satisfying certain natural axioms, see Definition
2.1.1
. Let
R
be the set of roots of
E
with respect to
{\mathcal{H}}
. Then we have
R=R^{\times}\cup R^{0}
where
R^{\times}
is the set of “non-isotropic” roots of
E
and
R^{0}
is the set of “isotropic roots” of
E
.
To explain our strategy in investigating Chevalley involutions for
E
, we need to first recall a few terms.
The “core”
E_{c}
of

is by definition the subalgebra of
E
generated by non-isotropic root spaces
E_{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in R^{\times}
.
The centerless core
E_{cc}
is the quotient Lie algebra
E_{c}/Z(E_{c})
, where
Z(E_{c})
is the center of
E_{c}
.
It is very well understood that the centerless core plays a central role in the classification and realization of extended affine Lie algebras as well as other parts of the theory, see
[
4
]
. The centerless core is characterized as a “centerless Lie torus”, see Definition
2.2.1
. Centerless Lie tori are defined by Y. Yoshii
[
32
]
.
In brief, the centerless core of an extended affine Lie algebra is a centerless Lie torus. Conversely, starting from a centerless Lie torus
\mathcal{L}
there is a prescribed way due to E. Neher
[
22
]
to construct an extended affine Lie algebra whose centerless core is
\mathcal{L}
. We explain this construction briefly here.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a centerless Lie torus equipped with a symmetric invariant non-degenerate form
(\cdot,\cdot)
; see
[
32
, Theorem 5.2]
. Let
D
be a “permissible subalgebra” of skew centroidal derivations
\mathrm{SCDer}(\mathcal{L})
of
\mathcal{L}
, and
{D^{gr}}^{\star}
be its graded dual, viewed as a
D
-module via the contragredient action, see
2.3.6
and Definition
2.4.2
for details. Let
\kappa
be an “affine cocycle” on
D
, see
2.4.1
. Consider the vector space
E=E(\mathcal{L},D,\kappa)=\mathcal{L}\oplus{D^{gr}}^{\star}\oplus D
.
Then one endows
E
with a natural Lie bracket induced by the Lie bracket on
\mathcal{L}
, the commutator bracket on
D
, the action of
D
on
{D^{gr}}^{\star}
, the natural action of
D
on
\mathcal{L}
as derivations, the affine cocycle
\kappa
, and an involved
2
-cocycle invoked by
D
and
(\cdot,\cdot)
, see
2.4.5
. One also endows
E
with a bilinear form
(\cdot,\cdot)
, induced by the form on
\mathcal{L}
and the dual paring of
D
and
{D^{gr}}^{\star}.
set
{\mathcal{H}}=\mathcal{L}^{0}_{0}\oplus{D^{0}}^{\star}\oplus D^{0}
.
According to
[
22
, Theorem 16]
,
(E,(\cdot,\cdot),{\mathcal{H}})
is an extended affine Lie algebra with
E_{c}=\mathcal{L}\oplus{D^{gr}}^{\star}
, conversely any extended affine Lie algebra is graded-isomorphic to
E(\mathcal{L},D,\kappa)
for some centerless Lie torus
\mathcal{L}
, some permissible subalgebra
D
, and an affine cocycle
\kappa
.

We now return to the question of the existence of Chevalley involutions for an extended affine Lie algebra
E(\mathcal{L},D,\kappa)
. Suppose first that
\mathcal{L}
is equipped with such an involution
\tau
, see Definition
3.1.1
. We extend
\tau
to an involution
\bar{\tau}
for the vector space
\mathcal{L}\oplus\mathrm{End}(\mathcal{L})^{\star}\oplus\mathrm{End}(\mathcal{L})
in a natural manner. Then
\bar{\tau}
restricts to an involution
\bar{\tau}
of
\mathcal{L}\oplus{\mathrm{SCDer}(\mathcal{L})^{gr}}^{\star}\oplus\mathrm{SCDer}(\mathcal{L})
as a Lie algebra. Now the question is if
\bar{\tau}
preserves the permissible subalgebra
D
and its graded dual. The answer in general is negative, see Example
3.2.5
(ii). To get a better picture of the situation, we set
D_{\tau}:=\bar{\tau}(D)
and we define the bilinear map

\kappa_{\tau}:D_{\tau}\times D_{\tau}\rightarrow{({D_{\tau}^{0}})^{\star}}
by
\kappa_{\tau}=\bar{\tau}\circ\kappa\circ(\bar{\tau}\times\bar{\tau}).

It turns out that
D_{\tau}
is a permissible subalgebra of
\mathrm{SCDer}(\mathcal{L})
and
\kappa_{\tau}
is an affine cocycle on
D_{\tau}
, see Lemma
3.2.1
. Therefore the Chevalley involution
\tau
for
\mathcal{L}
leads us to a Lie algebra isomorphism
\bar{\tau}
from

E(\mathcal{L},D,\kappa)
onto
E^{\tau}:=E(\mathcal{L},D_{\tau},\kappa_{\tau})
which maps the core of
E
onto the core of
E^{\tau}
. Moreover, if
D
is
\tau
-invariant, namely
D_{\tau}=D
, then
\bar{\tau}
restricts to a Chevalley involution of
E_{c}
.
Furthermore, if the pair
(D,\kappa)
is
\tau
-invariant, meaning
(D_{\tau},\kappa_{\tau})=(D,\kappa)
, then

\bar{\tau}
restricts to a Chevalley involution of
E
, see Theorem
3.3.2
. In Lemma
3.2.4
, we give a necessary and sufficient condition for a permissible subalgebra

to be
\tau
-invariant. It turns out that the special cases
D=D^{0}
or
D=\mathrm{SCDer}(\mathcal{L})
are both
\tau
-invariant, and so the existence of involution

on
E_{c}
is guaranteed.
As far as we know, these two cases cover all specific examples of extended affine Lie algebras appeared in the literature, see remark
3.3.4
, except the ones we construct in Example
3.2.5
. The question; “Which affine cocycles are invariant?” is more subtle since we don’t have enough examples and our information about affine cocycles is limited.
Fortunately, the known examples are
\tau
-invariant with respect to any Chevalley involution
\tau
for
\mathcal{L}
, see Remark
3.3.4
(ii).

We now discuss the existence of Chevalley involutions for centerless Lie tori of reduced types. Fortunately, due to the works of
[
9
]
,
[
8
]
,
[
30
]
and
[
4
]
the structures and coordinate algebras of centerless Lie tori of reduced types are very well understood.
In fact a centerless Lie torus
\mathcal{L}
is coordinatized by a unital algebra that is a Jordan algebra, an alternative algebra or an associative algebra, depending on its type
\Delta
, see Section
4
. In simply laced cases, types
A_{\ell}(\ell\geq 3)
,
A_{2}
and
A_{1}
can be constructed using
\mathfrak{sl}_{\ell+1}
,
\mathfrak{psl}_{3}
and Tits-Kantor-Koecher (TKK) construction, respectively, and types
D,E
are untwisted centerless Lie tori. In non-simply laced cases, types
B_{\ell}(\ell\geq 3)
,
F_{4}
and
G_{2}
can be constructed using generalized Tits construction and type
C_{\ell}(\ell\geq 2)
can be constructed using Tits-Kantor-Koecher construction, see Section
5
. We, therefore, start by showing that the coordinate algebra of
\mathcal{L}
admits a pre-Chevalley involution, meaning that it admits an involution that reverses the grading, see Section
4
. We then, by a type-dependent procedure, show that this involution can be lifted to a Chevalley involution for the corresponding tensor structure,
\mathfrak{sl}_{\ell+1}
structure,
\mathfrak{psl}_{3}
structure,
TKK structure, or generalized Tits structure, depending on the type.
This completes the problem of the existence of Chevalley involutions for the considered centerless Lie tori, see Theorems
4.0.2
and
5.0.1
. The results mentioned above covers the content of Sections 2-5.

In Section
6
, we discuss the existence of Chevalley involutions for Lie tori that are finitely generated over their centroid (fgc Lie tori).
Any centerless Lie torus of a type different from
A_{\ell}
is fgc, and any such algebra is a multi-loop algebra, see Section
6
for details and definitions. In Subsection
6.2
, the existence of Chevalley involutions for multi-loop algebras is considered.

We conclude the introduction by mentioning that the study of involutions is closely related to the study of compact forms of involutive Lie algebras. In
[
11

a uniform approach is considered for the study of involutive Lie algebras graded by finite root systems of simply laced types.

2.1.
Extended affine Lie algebras

We begin by recalling the definition of an “extended affine Lie algebra”. Since in this work, we are interested in “tame” extended affine Lie algebras, the tameness condition is included in the definition. For details, we refer the reader to
[
1
]
and
[
24
]
.

Definition 2.1.1
.

An
extended affine Lie algebra
is a triple

(E,(\cdot,\cdot),{\mathcal{H}})
consisting of a Lie algebra
E
,
a non-trivial subalgebra
{\mathcal{H}}
and a bilinear form
(\cdot,\cdot):E\times E\rightarrow{\mathbb{K}}
satisfying the following six axioms:

(A1)
(\cdot,\cdot)
is invariant and non-degenerate on
E
,

(A2)
{\mathcal{H}}
is finite-dimensional and we have the root space decomposition

E=\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathcal{H}}^{\star}}E_{\alpha}
with

E_{\alpha}=\{x\in E\mid[h,x]=\alpha(h)x\hbox{ for all }h\in{\mathcal{H}}\},

and
E_{0}={\mathcal{H}}.

To give the next axioms, we denote the root system of
E
with respect to
{\mathcal{H}}
by
R
, namely
R=\{\alpha\in{\mathcal{H}}^{\star}\mid E_{\alpha}\neq\{0\}\}
.
From (A1)-(A2), it follows that the form
(\cdot,\cdot)
on
E
restricted to
{\mathcal{H}}
is non-degenerate and so it transfers
to
{\mathcal{H}}^{\star}
by
(\alpha,\beta):=(t_{\alpha},t_{\beta})
where
t_{\alpha}\in{\mathcal{H}}

is the unique element satisfying
\alpha(h)=(h,t_{\alpha})
,
h\in{\mathcal{H}}
. Then a root
\alpha
is called
isotropic
if

(\alpha,\alpha)=0
and
non-isotropic
otherwise.
We denote by
R^{0}
and
R^{\times}
the set of isotropic and non-isotropic roots of
R
, respectively. The subalgebra
E_{c}
of
E
generated by non-isotropic root spaces is called the
core
of
E
.

(A3) For
\alpha\in R^{\times}
and
x\in E_{\alpha}
,
\hbox{ad}(x)
is locally nilpotent on
E
.

(A4)
E
is
tame
, namely the centralizer of
E_{c}
in
E
is contained in
E_{c}
.

(A5) The
{\mathbb{Z}}
-span of
R
in
{\mathcal{H}}^{\star}
is a free abelian group of finite rank.

(A6)
R^{\times}
is indecomposable in the sense that if
R^{\times}=R_{1}\cup R_{2}
with
(R_{1},R_{2})=0
, then
R_{1}=\emptyset
or
R_{2}=\emptyset.

2.1.2
.

Let
(E,(\cdot,\cdot),{\mathcal{H}})
, or simply
E
, be an extended affine Lie algebra with root system
R
. Set
{\mathcal{V}}=\hbox{span}_{{\mathbb{R}}}R
and
{\mathcal{V}}^{0}=\hbox{span}_{{\mathbb{R}}}R^{0}
. Let
\bar{\;}:{\mathcal{V}}\rightarrow\bar{\mathcal{V}}:={\mathcal{V}}/{\mathcal{V}}^{0}
.
It turns out that the image
\bar{R}
of
R
in
\bar{\mathcal{V}}
is an irreducible finite root system in
\bar{\mathcal{V}}
. One can find an isomorphic preimage
\dot{R}
of
\bar{R}
in
{\mathcal{V}}
, under
\bar{\;}
, such that
\dot{R}
is an irreducible finite root system in its real span. Then
R\subseteq\dot{R}+\Lambda
, where
\Lambda=\langle R^{0}\rangle
is a free abelian group of rank equal to
\dim{\mathcal{V}}^{0}
. The
type
and the
rank
of
R
(or
E
) is by definition the type and the rank of
\bar{R}
, respectively.

The quotient Lie algebra

E_{cc}:=E_{c}/Z(E_{c})
is called the
centerless core
of
E
, where here
Z(E_{c})
denotes the center of
E_{c}
. Most structural properties of an extended affine Lie algebra is encoded in its centerless core. Centerless core of an extended affine Lie algebra is characterized as a “Lie torus”.

2.2.
Lie tori

Lie tori were introduced by Y. Yoshii
in
[
32
]
to give a characterization of extended affine Lie algebras and were further studied by E. Neher in
[
23
]
. Here we recall the definition of a Lie torus.

Let
\Lambda
be a free abelian group of finite rank and let
\Delta
be a finite irreducible root
system with root lattice
Q=\hbox{span}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\Delta)
. For
\alpha,\beta\in\Delta,\alpha^{\vee}
is the
coroot of
\alpha
, and
\langle\beta,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle
is the corresponding Cartan integer. We denote by
\Delta_{\hbox{ind}}=\{0\}\cup\{\alpha\in\Delta^{\times}\mid(\alpha/2)\notin\Delta\}
the subsystem of “indivisible” roots of

\Delta
.

Definition 2.2.1
.

A Lie
\Lambda
-torus of type
\Delta
is a Lie algebra
\mathcal{L}
over
{\mathbb{K}}
satisfying the following conditions (LT1)-(LT4):

(
LT1
)
\mathcal{L}
is a
Q\times\Lambda
-graded Lie algebra;

\mathcal{L}=\bigoplus_{(\alpha,\lambda)\in Q\times\Lambda}\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}^{\lambda}\quad\text{with}\quad\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}^{\lambda}=0\;\text{if}\;\alpha\notin\Delta,

and

[\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}^{\lambda},\mathcal{L}_{\beta}^{\mu}]\subset\mathcal{L}_{\alpha+\beta}^{\lambda+\mu}.

(
LT2
) For
\alpha\in\Delta^{\times}
and
\lambda\in\Lambda
we have

(i)

\dim\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}^{\lambda}\leq 1
, with
\dim\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}^{0}=1
if
\alpha\in\Delta_{\hbox{ind}}
,

(ii)

if
\dim\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}^{\lambda}=1
then there exist elements
e_{\alpha}^{\lambda}\in\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}^{\lambda}
and
f_{\alpha}^{\lambda}\in\mathcal{L}_{-\alpha}^{-\lambda}
such that

\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}^{\lambda}={\mathbb{K}}e_{\alpha}^{\lambda},\quad\mathcal{L}_{-\alpha}^{-\lambda}={\mathbb{K}}f_{\alpha}^{\lambda},

and

[[e_{\alpha}^{\lambda},f_{\alpha}^{\lambda}],x^{\mu}_{\beta}]=\langle\beta,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle x_{\beta}^{\mu}

for
\beta\in\Delta,

\mu\in\Lambda
,
x_{\beta}^{\mu}\in\mathcal{L}_{\beta}^{\mu}.

(
LT3
) For
\lambda\in\Lambda
we have
\mathcal{L}_{0}^{\lambda}=\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta^{\times},\mu\in\Lambda}[\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}^{\mu},\mathcal{L}_{-\alpha}^{\lambda-\mu}]
.

(
LT4
)
\Lambda=\langle\hbox{supp}_{\Lambda}(\mathcal{L})\rangle
, where
\hbox{supp}_{\Lambda}(\mathcal{L})=\{\lambda\in\Lambda\mid\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}^{\lambda}\neq 0\;\text{for some}\;\alpha\in\Delta\}
.

The Lie torus
\mathcal{L}
is called
centreless
if
\mathcal{L}
has trivial center, and is called
invariant
, if
\mathcal{L}
has an invariant non-degenerate symmetric
bilinear form
(\cdot,\cdot)
which is graded, meaning that

(\mathcal{L}^{\lambda}_{\alpha},\mathcal{L}^{\mu}_{\beta})=0\quad\hbox{unless}\quad(\alpha,\lambda)=(-\beta,-\mu).

The rank of
\Lambda
is called the
nullity
of
\mathcal{L}
. When
\Delta
and
\Lambda
are fixed, we simply say that
\mathcal{L}
is a Lie torus. For
\lambda\in\Lambda
and
\alpha\in Q
, we set

\mathcal{L}^{\lambda}:=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in Q}\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}^{\lambda}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}:=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}^{\lambda},

then
\mathcal{L}=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\mathcal{L}^{\lambda}
is
\Lambda
-graded and
\mathcal{L}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in Q}\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}
is
Q
-graded.

2.2.2
.

Assume that
E
is an extended affine Lie algebra with root system
R
. Consider the finite root system
\dot{R}
, and the free abelian group
\Lambda
as in

. We denote the type of

Proposition 2.2.3
.

[
22

[
4

The core of

is a Lie

-torus of type

with

-grading
E_{c}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\dot{R},\lambda\in\Lambda}(E_{c})_{\alpha}^{\lambda}

2.3.
Centroidal and skew centroidal derivations

We describe a construction that associates to a centerless Lie torus
\mathcal{L}
a class of extended affine Lie algebras
E(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{D},\kappa)
, where
D
is a graded subalgebra of derivation algebra of
\mathcal{L}
and
\kappa
is a so called an “affine cocycle”. As a vector space, we have
E(\mathcal{L},D,\kappa)=\mathcal{L}\oplus{D^{gr}}^{\star}\oplus D
, where
{D^{gr}}^{\star}
is the graded dual of
D
. Below, we will describe the details of this construction.

Definition 2.3.1
.

The
centroid
of a Lie algebra
\mathcal{L}
over any commutative ring
R
, denoted
\mathrm{Cent}_{R}(\mathcal{L})
, is the set of all
R
-linear endomorphisms of
\mathcal{L}
that commute with left and right multiplication by elements of
\mathcal{L}
, i.e.

\mathrm{Cent}_{R}(\mathcal{L})=\{\chi\in\mathrm{End}_{R}(\mathcal{L})\mid\chi([x,y])=[\chi(x),y]=[x,\chi(y)],\forall x,y\in\mathcal{L}\}.

Then
\mathcal{L}
can be naturally viewed as a module over
\mathrm{Cent}_{R}(\mathcal{L})
via
\chi.x=\chi(x)
for all
x\in\mathcal{L},\chi\in\mathrm{Cent}_{R}(\mathcal{L})
.

Definition 2.3.2
.

An EALA is called
fgc
if its centerless core is fgc, i.e. the centerless core is finitely generated as a module over its centroid.

2.3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a Lie
\Lambda
-torus of type
\Delta
over
{\mathbb{K}}
of nullity
n
. Then its centroid
\mathrm{Cent}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathcal{L})
is also
\Lambda
-graded;

\mathrm{Cent}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathcal{L})=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\mathrm{Cent}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathcal{L})^{\lambda}\quad\text{with}\quad\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\mathrm{Cent}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathcal{L})^{\lambda}\leq 1,

where
\mathrm{Cent}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathcal{L})^{\lambda}=\{\chi\in\mathrm{Cent}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathcal{L})\mid\chi(\mathcal{L}^{\mu})\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{\lambda+\mu},\;\text{for all}\;\mu\in\Lambda\}
. Now let
\Gamma=\{\lambda\in\Lambda\mid\mathrm{Cent}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathcal{L})^{\lambda}\neq 0\}
. The following result from
[
22
]
justifies to call
\Gamma
the
centroidal grading group
of
\mathcal{L}
.

Theorem 2.3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a centerless Lie
\Lambda
-torus of type

\Delta
. Then

(a)

\Gamma
is a subgroup of
\Lambda
, and
\mathrm{Cent}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathcal{L})
is isomorphic to the group ring
{\mathbb{K}}[\Gamma]
, hence
to a Laurent polynomial ring in finitely many variables.

(b)

\mathcal{L}
is a free
\mathrm{Cent}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathcal{L})
-module, and if
\Delta\neq A_{\ell}
, then
\mathcal{L}
has finite rank as
a
\mathrm{Cent}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathcal{L})
-module.

2.3.5
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a centerless Lie
\Lambda
-torus of type
\Delta
of nullity
n
, with centroidal grading group
\Gamma
. Thus we can write

\mathrm{Cent}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathcal{L})=\bigoplus_{\mu\in\Gamma}{\mathbb{K}}\chi^{\mu},

where
\chi^{\mu}

\mathcal{L}
as an endomorphism of degree

\chi^{\mu}\chi^{\nu}=\chi^{\mu+\nu}

For any
\theta\in\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\Lambda,{\mathbb{K}})

\partial_{\theta}

\partial_{\theta}(x^{\lambda})=\theta(\lambda)x^{\lambda}\;\;\text{for}\;\lambda\in\Lambda,x^{\lambda}\in\mathcal{L}^{\lambda}.

The derivation
\partial_{\theta}
is called a
degree derivation
of
\mathcal{L}
. Put

\mathcal{D}:=\{\partial_{\theta}\mid\theta\in\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\Lambda,{\mathbb{K}})\},

the set of all degree derivations. Denote by

\mathrm{CDer}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathcal{L}):=\mathrm{Cent}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathcal{L})\mathcal{D}=\bigoplus_{\mu\in\Gamma}\chi^{\mu}\mathcal{D},

called the
centroidal derivations
of
\mathcal{L}
. It follows that
\mathrm{CDer}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathcal{L})
is a
\Gamma
-graded subalgebra of the derivation algebra

\mathrm{Der}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathcal{L})
of
\mathcal{L}
with

(2.1)

[\chi^{\mu}\partial_{\theta},\chi^{\nu}\partial_{\psi}]=\chi^{\mu+\nu}(\theta(\nu)\partial_{\psi}-\psi(\mu)\partial_{\theta}).

2.3.6
.

Fix a non-degenerate invariant
\Lambda
-graded bilinear form
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\mathcal{L}}
on
\mathcal{L}
(see
[
32
, Theorem 5.2]
). A derivation
d
satisfying
(d(x),x)_{\mathcal{L}}=0
for all
x\in\mathcal{L}
, or equivalently
(d(x),y)=-(x,d(y))
for all
x,y\in\mathcal{L}
, is called a
skew derivation
of
\mathcal{L}
. Set

\displaystyle\mathrm{SCDer}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathcal{L})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\{d\in\mathrm{CDer}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathcal{L})\mid(d(x),x)_{L}=0\;\text{for all}\;x\in\mathcal{L}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{\mu\in\Gamma}\mathrm{SCDer}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathcal{L})^{\mu}=\bigoplus_{\mu\in\Gamma}\chi^{\mu}\{\partial_{\theta}\in\mathcal{D}\mid\theta(\mu)=0\}.

Then
\mathrm{SCDer}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathcal{L})
is a
\Gamma
-graded subalgebra of
\mathrm{CDer}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathcal{L})
, called the
skew centroidal derivations
of
\mathcal{L}
. Note that
\mathrm{SCDer}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathcal{L})^{0}=\mathcal{D}.

For a graded subalgebra
D=\sum_{\mu\in\Gamma}D^{\mu}
of

\mathrm{SCDer}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathcal{L})
, we denote its graded dual with

D^{gr^{\star}}=\sum_{\mu\in\Gamma}(D^{\mu})^{\star}
with grading

(D^{gr^{\star}})^{\mu}=(D^{-\mu})^{\star}
, and we consider it as a
D
-module by the contragredient action, i.e.

(d.\varphi)(d^{{}^{\prime}})=\varphi([d^{{}^{\prime}},d])\>\text{for}\;d^{{}^{\prime}},d\in D,\varphi\in D^{gr*},

where
\varphi\in(D^{\mu})^{\star}
is viewed as a linear form on
D
by
\varphi|_{D^{\nu}}=0
for
\nu\neq\mu
.

2.4.
Construction of extended affine Lie algebras

We are ready now to give a general construction of extended affine Lie algebras starting from a centerless Lie torus, see
[
24
]
. We begin with the following definition.

2.4.1
.

Let
D=\bigoplus_{\mu\in\Gamma}D^{\mu}
be a
\Gamma
-graded subalgebra of
\mathrm{SCDer}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathcal{L})
, and
\kappa:D\times D\rightarrow D^{gr*}
be a bilinear map. Assume that
D
satisfies the following two conditions:

(i) the canonical evaluation map
\text{ev}:\Lambda\rightarrow(D^{0})^{\star}
defined by

\text{ev}(\lambda)(\partial_{\theta})=\theta(\lambda),\;\lambda\in\Lambda,

is injective and has discrete image. Note that, we conclude from (i) that
D^{0}
is an ad-diagonalizable subalgebra of
D
with weight spaces
D^{\mu}
,
\mu\in\Gamma
.
We also note that
D^{0}\subseteq\mathrm{SCDer}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathcal{L})^{0}=\mathcal{D}
.

(ii)
\kappa
is an “affine cocycle” which is graded and invariant. This means that,
\kappa
is a bilinear map satisfying

\begin{array}[]{c}\kappa(d,d)=0,\;\;{\sum_{(i,j,k)\circlearrowleft}\kappa([d_{i},d_{j}],d_{k})=\sum_{(i,j,k)\circlearrowleft}d_{i}\cdot\kappa(d_{j},d_{k})},\\
\kappa(D^{\mu_{1}},D^{\mu_{2}})\subseteq(D^{-\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}})^{\star}\;\;\text{and}\;\;\kappa(d_{1},d_{2})(d_{3})=\kappa(d_{2},d_{3})(d_{1}),\\
\kappa(D^{0},D)=0,\end{array}

for
d,d_{1},d_{2},d_{3}\in\ D
.
Note that by
(i,j,k)\circlearrowleft
, we mean
(i,j,k)
is a cyclic permutation of
(1,2,3)
.

Definition 2.4.2
.

We call a subalgebra
D
of
\mathrm{SCDer}(\mathcal{L})

permissible
, if it satisfies
2.4.1
(i).

Remark 2.4.3
.

Extended affine Lie algebras are defined originally over the field of complex numbers. This notion, later was generalized by E. Neher to fields of characteristic 0, see
[
22
, Definition 11]
. In this general case, the canonical evaluation map doesn’t need to have discrete image.

Example 2.4.4
.

(i) Let
({\mathcal{U}},{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime},\Gamma^{\prime})
be a triple consisting of
{\mathbb{K}}
-subspaces
{\mathcal{U}},{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}
of
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\Lambda,{\mathbb{K}})
and a subgroup
\Gamma^{\prime}
of
\Gamma
. We set

{({\mathcal{U}},{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime},\Gamma^{\prime})_{\mu}}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\mathcal{U}}&\hbox{if }\mu=0,\\
\{\theta\in{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}\mid\theta(\mu)=0\}&\hbox{if }\mu\in\Gamma^{\prime}\setminus\{0\},\\
\{0\}&\hbox{otherwise,}\end{array}\right.

and

D^{\mu}:=\chi^{\mu}\{\partial_{\theta}\mid\theta\in{({\mathcal{U}},{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime},\Gamma^{\prime})_{\mu}}\},\qquad(\mu\in\Gamma).

Then one can see from (
2.1
) that

D_{({\mathcal{U}},{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime},\Gamma^{\prime})}:=D^{0}\oplus\sum_{0\neq\mu\in\Gamma^{\prime}}D^{\mu}

is a
\Gamma
-graded subalgebra of
\mathrm{SCDer}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathcal{L})
.

(ii) Note that if
{\mathcal{U}}
in part (i) separates points of
\Lambda
, then the evaluation map
\hbox{\bf ev}:\Lambda\rightarrow{(D^{0})^{\star}=(\{\partial_{\theta}\mid\theta\in{\mathcal{U}}\})^{\star}}
is injective. If moreover the image of
ev
is discrete, then
D_{({\mathcal{U}},{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime},\Gamma^{\prime})}
is permissible.

(iii) Here we give an example of a subspace
{\mathcal{U}}
of
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\Lambda,{\mathbb{K}})
which separates points of
\Lambda
.
Assume that
\Lambda=\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\mathbb{Z}}\sigma_{i}
, then
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\Lambda,{\mathbb{K}})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\mathbb{K}}\theta_{i}
, where

\theta_{i}(\sigma_{j})=\delta_{i,j}
. If

p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}
are any
{\mathbb{Z}}
-linearly independent elements of
{\mathbb{K}}
, then
the one dimensional space
{\mathbb{K}}(p_{1}\theta_{1}+\cdots+p_{n}\theta_{n})
separates points of
\Lambda
.

(iv) Let
{\mathcal{U}}=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\Lambda,{\mathbb{K}})
. Then
\text{ev}:\Lambda\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{\star}
is a discrete embedding. Thus
D_{({\mathcal{U}},{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime},\Gamma^{\prime})}
is a permissible subalgebra of
\mathrm{SCDer}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathcal{L})
. In particular,
D_{({\mathcal{U}},{\mathcal{U}},\Gamma)}=\mathrm{SCDer}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathcal{L})
and
D_{({\mathcal{U}},\{0\},\{0\})}=\mathcal{D}
are permissible subalgebras of
\mathrm{SCDer}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathcal{L})
.

2.4.5
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a centerless Lie torus,
D
be a permissible subalgebra of

\mathrm{SCDer}(\mathcal{L})
and
\kappa
be an affine cocycle on
D
, see
2.3.6
. Let

E=E(\mathcal{L},D,\kappa):=\mathcal{L}\oplus D^{gr*}\oplus D

be the Lie algebra with the Lie bracket

\displaystyle[x_{1}+c_{1}+d_{1},x_{2}+c_{2}+d_{2}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle([x_{1},x_{2}]_{\mathcal{L}}+d_{1}(x_{2})-d_{2}(x_{1}))

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(\sigma_{D}(x_{1},x_{2})+d_{1}.c_{2}-d_{2}.c_{1}+\kappa(d_{1},d_{2}))

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle[d_{1},d_{2}]

for
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathcal{L},c_{1},c_{2}\in D^{gr*},d_{1},d_{2}\in D
, where
[\>,\>]_{\mathcal{L}}
denotes the Lie bracket of
\mathcal{L}
,
[d_{1},d_{2}]=d_{1}d_{2}-d_{2}d_{1}
, and
\sigma_{D}:\mathcal{L}\times\mathcal{L}\rightarrow D^{gr*}
is defined by

\sigma_{D}(x,y)(d)=(d(x)|y)\;\text{for all}\;x,y\in\mathcal{L},d\in D.

In fact
\sigma_{D}
is a
2
-cocycle for
\mathcal{L}
whose values are in the trivial
\mathcal{L}
-module
D^{gr*}
. Also
\sigma_{D}
respects the gradings of
\mathcal{L}
and
D^{gr*}
. It follows that
E
has a non-degenerate invariant symmetric bilinear form
(\cdot,\cdot)
given by

(2.2)

(x_{1}+c_{1}+d_{1},x_{2}+c_{2}+d_{2})=(x_{1},x_{2})_{\mathcal{L}}+c_{1}(d_{2})+c_{2}(d_{1}).

Let

(2.3)

H:=\mathfrak{h}\oplus(D^{0})^{\star}\oplus D^{0},\hbox{ where }\mathfrak{h}:=\mathcal{L}_{0}^{0},

see Definition
2.2.1
. Then we have the following.

Theorem 2.4.6
.

[
22
, Theorem 16]

(a) The algebra
E(\mathcal{L},D,\kappa)
constructed in
2.4.5
is an extended affine Lie algebra of nullity
n
with respect to the form (
2.2
) and the Cartan subalgebra
H
given in (
2.3
). Moreover,
E_{c}=\mathcal{L}\oplus{D^{gr}}^{\star}
.

(b) Conversely, let
E
be an extended affine Lie algebra, and let
\mathcal{L}=E_{cc}
be its centerless core of type
(\Lambda,\Delta)
. Then there exists a unique permissible subalgebra
D\subseteq\mathrm{SCDer}(\mathcal{L})
and an affine cocycle
\kappa
on
D
such that
D
induces the
\Lambda
-grading of
\mathcal{L}
, and
E\cong E(\mathcal{L},D,\kappa)
.

3.
Chevalley and Pre-Chevalley involutions

We discuss the possibility of lifting a Chevalley involution on a centerless Lie torus
\mathcal{L}
to the extended affine Lie algebra
E(\mathcal{L},D,\kappa),
where

D
is a permissible subalgebra of skew centroidal derivations of
\mathcal{L}
and
\kappa
is an affine cocycle.
Let
\mathcal{L}
be a Lie
\Lambda
-torus of type
\Delta
as in Definition
2.2.1
.

3.1.
Induced pre-Chevalley involutions

We begin with the formal definition of a Chevalley involution for a centerless Lie torus.

Definition 3.1.1
.

(i) We call an involution (an automorphism of order
2
)
\tau
of a
\Lambda
-graded algebra
\mathcal{A}
,
a
pre-Chevalley involution
if
\tau(\mathcal{A}^{\lambda})=\mathcal{A}^{-\lambda}
for all
\lambda\in\Lambda.

(ii) A pre-Chevalley involution
\tau
of
\mathcal{L}
is called a
Chevalley involution
if
\tau(h)=-h
for
h\in\mathcal{L}^{0}_{0}
.

Lemma 3.1.2
.

Assume that
\tau
is a Chevalley involution for
\mathcal{L}
. Then
\tau(\mathcal{L}_{\alpha})=\mathcal{L}_{-\alpha}
for
\alpha\in\Delta
.

Proof.
First consider the root-grading pair
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
for
\mathcal{L}
where
\mathfrak{g}
is the subalgebra of
\mathcal{L}
generated by
\{\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}^{0}\}_{\alpha\in\Delta^{\times}}
and
\mathfrak{h}=\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta^{\times}}[\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}^{0},\mathcal{L}_{-\alpha}^{0}]
. Then

\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}=\{x\in\mathcal{L}\mid[h,x]={\alpha}(h)x\;\text{for}\;h\in\mathfrak{h}\},

for
\alpha\in Q
, where
\alpha
is identified in the natural way as an element of
\mathfrak{h}^{\star}
, see
[
2
, Proposition 1.2.2]
. Note that
\mathfrak{h}\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{0}^{0}
and since
\tau_{|_{\mathcal{L}_{0}^{0}}}=-\hbox{id}
we have

[h,\tau(x)]=\tau([-h,x])=-{\alpha}(h)\tau(x),

for
\alpha\in Q,x\in\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}
and
h\in\mathfrak{h}
. So
\tau(\mathcal{L}_{\alpha})=\mathcal{L}_{-\alpha}
, for
\alpha\in Q
.
\Box

Remark 3.1.3
.

Assume that
\tau
is a pre-Chevalley involution for
\mathcal{L}
. It is easy to see that
\tau\partial_{\theta}=-\partial_{\theta}\tau

for
\theta\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\Lambda,{\mathbb{K}})
.

Suppose that
\tau
is a pre-Chevalley involution for
\mathcal{L}
.
We consider associative algebra automorphisms (both denoted by
\bar{\tau}
for the sake of notation),

(3.1)

\begin{array}[]{c}\bar{\tau}:\mathrm{End}(\mathcal{L})\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(\mathcal{L})\\
\phi\mapsto\tau\phi\tau^{-1}\end{array}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\begin{array}[]{c}\bar{\tau}:\mathrm{End}(\mathcal{L})^{\star}\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(\mathcal{L})^{\star}\\
\chi\mapsto\bar{\tau}(\chi):\phi\mapsto{\chi(\tau^{-1}\phi\tau)}\end{array}

for
\phi\in\mathrm{End}(\mathcal{L})
and
\chi\in\mathrm{End}(\mathcal{L})^{\star}
. Then
\bar{\tau}
can also be considered as an involutions on
\mathfrak{g}\ell(\mathcal{L})
, or
\mathfrak{g}\ell(\mathcal{L})^{\star}
.

The
\Lambda
-grading of
\mathcal{L}
induces a
\Lambda
-gradings on

\mathrm{gr}\mathrm{End}(\mathcal{L}):=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\mathrm{End}(\mathcal{L})^{\lambda}

where

\mathrm{End}(\mathcal{L})^{\lambda}=\{\phi\in\mathrm{End}(\mathcal{L})\mid\phi(\mathcal{L}^{\mu})\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{\lambda+\mu},\;\text{for all}\;\mu\in\Lambda\}.

We also consider the graded dual
\mathrm{gr}{\mathrm{End}(\mathcal{L})^{gr}}^{\star}=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}(\mathrm{End}(\mathcal{L})^{\lambda})^{\star}
of
\mathrm{gr}\mathrm{End}(\mathcal{L})
as a
\Lambda
-graded algebra with

(\mathrm{gr}{\mathrm{End}(\mathcal{L})^{gr}}^{\star})^{\lambda}=(\mathrm{End}(\mathcal{L})^{-\lambda})^{\star},

for
\lambda\in\Lambda
. Under the commutator bracket, the resulting
\Lambda
-graded Lie algebras will be denoted by

\mathrm{gr}\mathfrak{g}\ell(\mathcal{L})
and
\mathrm{gr}{\mathfrak{g}\ell(\mathcal{L})^{gr}}^{\star}
, respectively.

Next, for
\mu\in\Gamma
and
\lambda\in\Lambda
, we define

(3.2)

c^{(\mu)}_{\lambda}(\chi^{-\mu}\partial_{\theta}):=\theta(\lambda),

and extend it to an element of
{\mathrm{SCDer}(\mathcal{L})^{gr}}^{\star}
by
c^{(\mu)}_{\lambda}(\chi^{\nu}\partial_{\gamma})=0
for
\nu\not=-\mu.

Using these facts, now we have the following result.

Lemma 3.1.4
.

Let
\tau
be a pre-Chevalley involution on
\mathcal{L}
, and consider the automorphism
\bar{\tau}
as in (
3.1
).

(i)
\bar{\tau}
restricts to pre-Chevalley involutions on
\mathrm{gr}\mathrm{End}(\mathcal{L})
and
\mathrm{gr}{\mathrm{End}(\mathcal{L})^{gr}}^{\star}
.

(ii)
\bar{\tau}
restricts to a pre-Chevalley involution on
\mathrm{Cent}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathcal{L})
. In particular,
\mathrm{Cent}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathcal{L})
has a basis
\{\chi^{\mu}\mid\mu\in\Gamma\}
shch that
\bar{\tau}(\chi^{\mu})=\chi^{-\mu}
,
\mu\in\Gamma
.

Proof.
(i) For
\phi\in\mathrm{End}(\mathcal{L})^{\lambda}
, we have

\bar{\tau}(\phi)(\mathcal{L}^{\mu})=\tau\phi\tau^{-1}(\mathcal{L}^{\mu})=\tau\phi(\mathcal{L}^{-\mu})\subseteq\tau(\mathcal{L}^{\lambda-\mu})=\mathcal{L}^{-\lambda+\mu},

for all
\mu\in\Lambda
. So
\bar{\tau}(\phi)\in\mathrm{End}(\mathcal{L})^{-\lambda}
. This shows that
\bar{\tau}
is a pre-Chevalley involution for
\mathrm{gr}\mathrm{End}(\mathcal{L})
. Similarly
\bar{\tau}({(\mathrm{End}(\mathcal{L})^{\lambda})}^{\star})={(\mathrm{End}(\mathcal{L})^{-\lambda})}^{\star}
, for all
\lambda\in\Lambda
. Thus
\bar{\tau}
is a pre-Chevalley involution for
\mathrm{gr}\mathrm{End}(\mathcal{L})^{\star}
.

(ii) Let
\chi\in\mathrm{Cent}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathcal{L})
, then we have

\tau\chi\tau^{-1}([x,y])=[x,\tau\chi\tau^{-1}(y)]=[\tau\chi\tau^{-1}(x),y],\;\forall x,y\in\mathcal{L}.

So
\bar{\tau}(\chi)=\tau\chi\tau^{-1}\in\mathrm{Cent}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathcal{L})
. Also note that
\mathrm{Cent}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathcal{L})^{\lambda}=\mathrm{End}(\mathcal{L})^{\lambda}\cap\mathrm{Cent}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathcal{L})
. Thus
\bar{\tau}
restricts to a a pre-Chevalley involution for
\mathrm{Cent}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathcal{L})
.

Now recall that

\mathrm{Cent}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathcal{L})=\bigoplus_{\mu\in\Gamma}{\mathbb{K}}\chi^{\mu},

where
\chi^{\mu}
acts on
\mathcal{L}
as an endomorphism of degree
\mu
, and
\Gamma
is the central grading group of
\mathcal{L}
. So
\bar{\tau}(\chi^{\mu})=\eta_{\mu}\chi^{-\mu}
, for some
\eta_{\mu}\in{\mathbb{K}}^{\times}
.
Since
\bar{\tau}
has order
2
,
\eta_{\mu}\eta_{-\mu}=1.
Then
for each
\mu
,

\bar{\tau}(\eta_{\mu}^{\frac{-1}{2}}\chi^{\mu})=\eta_{\mu}\eta_{\mu}^{\frac{-1}{2}}\chi^{-\mu}={\eta_{\mu}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\chi^{-\mu}=\eta_{-\mu}^{\frac{-1}{2}}\chi^{-\mu}.

Thus replacing each
\chi^{\mu}
by
\eta_{\mu}^{\frac{-1}{2}}\chi^{\mu}
, we may assume that
\bar{\tau}(\chi^{\mu})=\chi^{-\mu}
.
\Box

Using Lemma
3.1.4
,
we assume from now on
that

(3.3)

\mathrm{Cent}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathcal{L})=\sum_{\mu\in\Gamma}{\mathbb{K}}\chi^{\mu}\hbox{ with }\bar{\tau}(\chi^{\mu})=\chi^{-\mu}.

Lemma 3.1.5
.

Let
\tau
be a pre-Chevalley involution on
\mathcal{L}
, and
\bar{\tau}
be as in (
3.1
).

(i)
\bar{\tau}
induces a pre-Chevalley involution on
\mathrm{gr}\mathfrak{g}\ell(\mathcal{L})
. Moreover
\bar{\tau}(\chi^{\mu}\partial_{\theta})=\chi^{-\mu}\partial_{-\theta}
for each
\mu
and
\theta
. In particular,
\bar{\tau}(\chi^{\mu}\mathcal{D})=\chi^{-\mu}\mathcal{D}
,
\mu\in\Gamma
, and
\bar{\tau}
restricts to a pre-Chevalley involution on
\mathrm{SCDer}(\mathcal{L})
.

(ii)

\bar{\tau}
induces a pre-Chevalley involution on
\mathrm{gr}{\mathfrak{g}\ell(\mathcal{L})^{gr}}^{\star}
. Moreover
\bar{\tau}(c^{(\mu)}_{\lambda})=c^{(-\mu)}_{-\lambda}
for each
\mu
and
\lambda
. In particular,
\bar{\tau}
restricts to a pre-Chevalley involution on

{\mathrm{SCDer}(\mathcal{L})^{gr}}^{\star}
.

Proof.
(i) For
\theta\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\Lambda,{\mathbb{K}})
and
x^{\lambda}\in\mathcal{L}^{\lambda}
, we have

\bar{\tau}(\partial_{\theta})(x^{\lambda})=\tau\partial_{\theta}\tau(x^{\lambda})=-\theta(\lambda)\tau^{2}(x^{\lambda})=\partial_{-\theta}(x^{\lambda}).

Thus
\bar{\tau}(\partial_{\theta})=\partial_{-\theta}.

Now the statement follows from this and Lemma
3.1.4
.

(ii)
It follows that
\{c^{(\mu)}_{\lambda}\mid\mu\in\Gamma,\;\lambda\in\Lambda\}
spans

{\mathrm{SCDer}(\mathcal{L})^{gr}}^{\star}
, see
[
21
, Proposition 5.2.4]
.
Now for any
\theta
and
\nu
,

\displaystyle\bar{\tau}(c_{\lambda}^{(\mu)})(\chi^{\nu}\partial_{\theta})=c_{\lambda}^{(\mu)}(\bar{\tau}(\chi^{\nu}\partial_{\theta}))=c_{\lambda}^{(\mu)}(\chi^{-\nu}\partial_{-\theta})=c_{-\lambda}^{(-\mu)}(\chi^{\nu}\partial_{\theta}).

Thus

\bar{\tau}(c^{(\mu)}_{\lambda})=c^{(-\mu)}_{-\lambda}\in({\mathrm{SCDer}(\mathcal{L})^{gr}}^{\star})^{-\mu}.

That is,

\bar{\tau}
maps
({\mathrm{SCDer}(\mathcal{L})^{gr}}^{\star})^{\mu}
onto
({\mathrm{SCDer}(\mathcal{L})^{gr}}^{\star})^{-\mu}
, and
\bar{\tau}
restricts to a pre-Chevalley involution for
{\mathrm{SCDer}(\mathcal{L})^{gr}}^{\star}
, as a subalgebra of
\mathrm{gr}{\mathfrak{g}\ell(\mathcal{L})^{gr}}^{\star}
.
\Box

Remark 3.1.6
.

Let
D=\sum_{\mu\in\Gamma}D^{\mu}
be a permissible subalgebra of
\mathrm{SCDer}(\mathcal{L})
and
C={D^{gr}}^{\star}
.
Consider the maps
c^{(\mu)}_{\lambda}
,
\mu\in\Gamma
, given in (
3.2
).
For simplicity of notation, we denote
the restriction of
c_{\lambda}^{(\mu)}
to
D^{-\mu}
by
c^{(\mu)}_{\lambda}
, again. Then it follows that
C^{\mu}=\hbox{span}_{{\mathbb{K}}}\{c^{(\mu)}_{\lambda}\mid\lambda\in\Lambda\}
.

3.2.
Induced affine cocycles

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a centerless Lie torus,

D=\bigoplus_{\mu\in\Gamma}D^{\mu}
be a permissible subalgebra of
\mathrm{SCDer}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathcal{L})
, and
\kappa:D\times D\rightarrow D^{gr*}
be an affine cocycle. Assume that
\tau
is a pre-Chevalley involution for the Lie torus
\mathcal{L}
.
We set

(3.4)

D_{\tau}:=\bar{\tau}(D)\quad\hbox{and}\quad D^{\mu}_{\tau}:=\bar{\tau}(D^{\mu}),\;\mu\in\Gamma.

Then we get the natural identification
\bar{\tau}(D^{gr^{\star}})=D_{\tau}^{gr^{\star}}=\sum_{\mu\in\Gamma}{D_{\tau}^{\mu}}^{\star}.
We next set

(3.5)

\begin{array}[]{c}\kappa_{\tau}:D_{\tau}\times D_{\tau}\rightarrow D_{\tau}^{gr^{\star}}\\
\kappa_{\tau}(\bar{\tau}(d),\bar{\tau}(d^{\prime}))=\bar{\tau}\kappa(d,d^{\prime}).\end{array}

Lemma 3.2.1
.

(i)
D_{\tau}
is a permissible subalgebra of
\mathrm{SCDer}(\mathcal{L})
.

(ii)
The map
\kappa_{\tau}

defines an affine cocycle on
D_{\tau}
.

Proof.
(i) is clear as
\bar{\tau}(\partial_{\theta})=-\partial_{\theta}
and so

D_{\tau}^{0}=D^{0}.

(ii) We first check the
\circlearrowleft
part in the definition of an affine cocycle.
For
d_{1},d_{2},d_{3}\in D
,

\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j,k)\circlearrowleft}\kappa_{\tau}([\bar{\tau}(d_{i}),\bar{\tau}(d_{j})],\bar{\tau}(d_{k}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j,k)\circlearrowleft}\bar{\tau}(\kappa([d_{i},d_{j}],d_{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{\tau}(\sum_{(i,j,k)\circlearrowleft}\kappa([d_{i},d_{j}],d_{k}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{\tau}(\sum_{(i,j,k)\circlearrowleft}d_{i}\cdot\kappa(d_{j},d_{k}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j,k)\circlearrowleft}\bar{\tau}(d_{i}\cdot\kappa(d_{j},d_{k})).

Now for
d\in D
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j,k)\circlearrowleft}\bar{\tau}(d_{i}\cdot\kappa(d_{j},d_{k}))(d)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j,k)\circlearrowleft}d_{i}\cdot\kappa(d_{j},d_{k})(\tau^{-1}d\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j,k)\circlearrowleft}\kappa(d_{j},d_{k})([\tau^{-1}d\tau,d_{i}])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j,k)\circlearrowleft}\kappa(d_{j},d_{k})(\tau^{-1}[d,\tau d_{i}\tau^{-1}]\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j,k)\circlearrowleft}\bar{\tau}(\kappa(d_{j},d_{k}))([d,\bar{\tau}(d_{i})])

\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j,k)\circlearrowleft}\bar{\tau}(d_{i})\cdot\bar{\tau}(\kappa(d_{j},d_{k}))(d).

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j,k)\circlearrowleft}\bar{\tau}(d_{i})\cdot\bar{\tau}(\kappa(d_{j},d_{k}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j,k)\circlearrowleft}\bar{\tau}(d_{i})\cdot\kappa_{\tau}(\bar{\tau}(d_{j}),\bar{\tau}(d_{k})),

so the proof of
\circlearrowleft
is completed.

Next, for

To check that the remaining properties of an affine cocycle hold for

is straightforward.

\Box

3.3.

We proceed with the same notation and assumptions as in the preceding sections. In particular,

Definition 3.3.1
.

Theorem 3.3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{L}

-torus of type

be a permissible subalgebra of
\mathrm{SCDer}(\mathcal{L})

, and

be the induced vector space isomorphism given by

\begin{array}[]{c}\bar{\tau}:\mathcal{L}\oplus\mathrm{End}(\mathcal{L})\oplus\mathrm{End}(\mathcal{L})^{\star}{\longrightarrow\mathcal{L}\oplus\mathrm{End}(\mathcal{L})\oplus\mathrm{End}(\mathcal{L})^{\star}}\\
x+\phi+\Psi\mapsto\tau(x)+\bar{\tau}(\phi)+\bar{\tau}(\Psi),\end{array}

see (
3.1
). Let
E=E(\mathcal{L},D,\kappa)
and
E^{\tau}:=E(\mathcal{L},D_{\tau},\kappa_{\tau})
. Then

(i)
\bar{\tau}
restricts to a Lie algebra isomorphism from

onto

. In particular if

is
\tau
-invariant, then

restricts to a pre-Chevalley involution for

.

(ii)
\bar{\tau}
restricts to a Lie algebra isomorphism from
E
onto
E^{\tau}
. In particular if
(D,\kappa)
is
\tau
-invariant, then
\bar{\tau}
restricts to a pre-Chevalley involution for
E
.

If in addition
\tau
is a Chevalley involution, then so is

Proof.
We first note that by Lemma
3.2.1
,
E^{\tau}
is an extended affine Lie algebra. We only need to show that

is a Lie algebra homomorphism.
Let

,

.
Then

\begin{split}\bar{\tau}[x_{1}+c_{1}+d_{1},x_{2}+c_{2}+d_{2}]=&[\tau(x_{1}),\tau(x_{2})]_{\mathcal{L}}+\tau(d_{1}(x_{2}))-\tau(d_{2}(x_{1}))\\
+&\bar{\tau}(\sigma_{D}(x_{1},x_{2})+\bar{\tau}(d_{1}.c_{2})-\bar{\tau}(d_{2}.c_{1})+\bar{\tau}\kappa(d_{1},d_{2}))\\
+&[\bar{\tau}(d_{1}),\bar{\tau}(d_{2})].\end{split}

Now
\tau(d_{1}(x_{2}))=\tau d_{1}\tau^{-1}(\tau(x_{2}))=\bar{\tau}(d_{1})(\tau(x_{2}))
. Similarly, we have
\tau(d_{2}(x_{1}))=\tau d_{2}\tau^{-1}(\tau(x_{1}))=\bar{\tau}(d_{2})(\tau(x_{1}))
. Also for

,

\displaystyle\bar{\tau}\sigma_{D}(x_{1},x_{2})(d)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma_{D}(x_{1},x_{2})(\tau^{-1}d\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\tau^{-1}d\tau(x_{1}),x_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(d(\tau(x_{1}),\tau(x_{2}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma_{D}(\tau(x_{1}),\tau(x_{2}))(d).

Therefore
\bar{\tau}\sigma_{D}(x_{1},x_{2})=\sigma_{D}(\tau(x_{1}),\tau(x_{2})).
Next for

,

\displaystyle\bar{\tau}(d_{1}\cdot c_{2})(d)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d_{1}\cdot c_{2}(\tau^{-1}d\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{2}[\tau^{-1}d\tau,d_{1}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{2}\tau^{-1}[d,\tau d_{1}\tau^{-1}]\tau

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{\tau}(d_{1})\cdot\bar{\tau}(c_{2})(d).

Thus

\bar{\tau}(d_{1}\cdot c_{2})=\bar{\tau}(d_{1})\cdot\bar{\tau}(c_{2}).

Also
\bar{\tau}\kappa(d_{1},d_{2})=\kappa_{\tau}(\bar{\tau}(d_{1}),\bar{\tau}(d_{2}))
for
d_{1},d_{2}\in D
. So
\bar{\tau}
is a Lie algebra isomorphism from

onto

\bar{\tau}
restricts to a Lie algebra isomorphism from

.

Next, if
(D,\kappa)
is
\tau
-invariant, i.e.,

and

\bar{\tau}
is a pre-Chevalley involution for

. In particular, if

is
\tau
-invariant, i.e.,

, then

is a pre-Chevalley involution for

4.
Pre-Chevalley involution for
\Lambda
-Tori

The notion of a
\Lambda
-torus arises in the study of Lie tori. Roughly speaking, a centerless Lie
\Lambda
-torus can be constructed as a matrix Lie algebra coordinatized by a
\Lambda
-torus. Depending on the type, it is a unital associative, Jordan or alternative algebra. We begin in this section by recalling the definition of a
\Lambda
-torus. Then we review the classification results of
\Lambda
-tori and using theses results we show that any
\Lambda
-torus admits a pre-Chevalley involution. Throughout the section,
\Lambda
is a free abelian group of rank
n
and by an algebra over
{\mathbb{K}}
we mean a unital Jordan algebra, alternative algebra or associative algebra over
{\mathbb{K}}
. Assuming
\mathcal{A}
and
{\mathcal{B}}
are
\Lambda
-graded algebras, then we say that
\mathcal{A}
and
{\mathcal{B}}
are graded-isomorphic, if there is an algebra isomorphism
\varphi:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow{\mathcal{B}}
that preserves the
\Lambda
-grading.

Definition 4.0.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\mathcal{A}^{\lambda}
be a
\Lambda
-graded algebra over
{\mathbb{K}}
.
\mathcal{A}
is called a
\Lambda
-torus
or
n
-torus
if:

(i) all
0\neq x\in\mathcal{A}^{\lambda}
are invertible and
\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\mathcal{A}^{\lambda}\leq 1
, for all
\lambda\in\Lambda
,

(ii)
\hbox{supp}_{\Lambda}(\mathcal{A})
generates
\Lambda
, where
\hbox{supp}_{\Lambda}(\mathcal{A})=\{\lambda\in\Lambda\mid\mathcal{A}^{\lambda}\neq\{0\}\}
.

If a
\Lambda
-torus
\mathcal{A}
is a Jordan algebra, alternative algebra or associative algebra then
\mathcal{A}
is called a
Jordan, alternative, or associative
\Lambda
-torus (
n
-torus)
, respectively.

To have a pre-Chevalley involution
\tau
of a
\Lambda
-torus
\mathcal{A}=\sum_{\alpha\in\hbox{supp}(\mathcal{A})}\mathcal{A}^{\lambda}
, it looks natural to first consider an appropriate basis
\{x^{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\hbox{supp}(\mathcal{A})}
for
\mathcal{A}
,
x^{\lambda}\in\mathcal{A}^{\lambda}
, and then define
\tau(x^{\lambda})=x^{-\lambda}
,
\lambda\in\hbox{supp}(\mathcal{A})
. This gives a vector space isomorphism which maps
\mathcal{A}^{\lambda}
onto
\mathcal{A}^{-\lambda}
. Therefore, we get a pre-Chevalley involution for the
\Lambda
-torus
\mathcal{A}
, if we show that
k(\alpha,\beta)=k(-\alpha,-\beta)
for
\alpha,\beta\in\Lambda
, where

x^{\alpha}x^{\beta}=k(\alpha,\beta)x^{\alpha+\beta}
,
k(\alpha,\beta)\in{\mathbb{K}}^{\times}.

Theorem 4.0.2
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a Jordan, alternative, or associative
\Lambda
-torus. Then
\mathcal{A}
admits a pre-Chevalley involution.

As explained above to give a proof, we need to have an explicit description of structure constants of the algebra product in each case with respect to a properly chosen basis. We do it in what follows. Then Propositions
4.1.2
,
4.2.4
and
4.3.4
below all together give the proof of Theorem
4.0.2
.

4.1.
Quantum tori

We begin by recalling the definition of a quantum torus from
[
9
]
.

Definition 4.1.1
.

Let

-matrix satisfying

\mathbb{K}

n

be a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-basis of
\Lambda

\Lambda

{\mathbb{K}}_{\bf q}=\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda}{\mathbb{K}}x^{\alpha}

x^{\alpha}:=x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}
. This
\Lambda
-grading makes
\mathbb{K}_{\bf q}
into an associative
\Lambda
-torus. When
n=2
,
the matrix
{\bf q}
is determined by a unique

and so in this case we write
{\mathbb{K}}_{\bf q}={\mathbb{K}}_{q}
with

Proposition 4.1.2
.

Any associative
\Lambda
-torus admits a pre-Chevalley involution.

Proof.
Any associative
\Lambda
-torus is graded-isomorphic to a quantum torus

for some

. Furthermore, any commutative associative

-torus is graded-isomorphic to the algebra of Laurent polynomials over

in
n
-variables, see
[

.
Now, one easily sees that the assignment

.

\Box

An
anti-involution
of
\mathcal{A}
is an order-2 anti-automorphism. If it also preserves the homogeneous spaces, it is called a

-
grading anti-involution

Remark 4.2.2

The octonion
3
-torus
{\mathcal{C}}
, also called the
Cayley torus
, has the following more concrete description
[
3
, Example 9.2]
:

Let
x\in{\mathcal{C}}
. Then one may uniquely write
x=\sum_{\alpha}k_{\alpha}x^{\alpha}
where
x^{\alpha}=(x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}x_{2}^{\alpha_{2}})x_{3}^{\alpha_{3}},\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}
and
k_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{K}
. Define
\epsilon:{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}\times{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}
by
\epsilon(\alpha,\beta)=\alpha_{3}\beta_{1}+\alpha_{2}\beta_{1}+\alpha_{3}\beta_{2}+\alpha_{1}\beta_{2}\beta_{3}+\alpha_{2}\beta_{1}\beta_{3}+\alpha_{3}\beta_{1}\beta_{2}
. Then the multiplication rule of
{\mathcal{C}}
is given by

x^{\alpha}x^{\beta}=(-1)^{\epsilon(\alpha,\beta)}x^{\alpha+\beta}.

The following theorem gives the classification of alternative tori over
\mathbb{K}
.

Theorem 4.2.3
.

[
8
, Theorem 1.25]
[
31
, Corollary 5.13]

Any alternative
\Lambda
-torus over
\mathbb{K}
is graded-isomorphic to either a quantum torus or the octonion
n
-torus
\mathbb{O}
.

Proposition 4.2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\mathcal{A}^{\lambda}
be an alternative
\Lambda
-torus over
{\mathbb{K}}
. Then
\mathcal{A}
admits a pre-Chevalley involution.

Proof.
By Theorem
4.2.3
, we can assume that
\mathcal{A}={\mathbb{K}}_{\bf q}
for some
{\bf q}
, or
\mathcal{A}=\mathbb{O}
, the octonion
n
-torus. First, if
\mathcal{A}={\mathbb{K}}_{\bf q}
then by Remark
4.2.5
(ii) our claim is proved. Next let
\mathcal{A}=\mathbb{O}={\mathcal{C}}\otimes\mathbb{K}[x_{4}^{\pm 1},\ldots,x_{n}^{\pm 1}]
. Then the assignment
x^{\alpha}\mapsto x^{-\alpha},\alpha\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}
defines a pre-Chevalley involution
\tau
on
{\mathcal{C}}
, since
(-1)^{\epsilon(\alpha,\beta)}=(-1)^{\epsilon(-\alpha,-\beta)}
, see Remark
4.2.2
. Now we extend
\tau
to
\mathbb{O}
by
x\otimes x^{\gamma}\mapsto\tau(x)\otimes x^{-\gamma}
, where
x\in{\mathcal{C}},x^{\gamma}=x_{4}^{\gamma_{4}}\cdots x_{n}^{\gamma_{n}}\in\mathbb{K}[x_{4}^{\pm 1},\ldots,x_{n}^{\pm 1}]
. It defines a pre-Chevalley involution for
\mathbb{O}
; this completes our proof.
\Box

Remark 4.2.5
.

Let
\mathbb{O}=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\mathbb{O}^{\lambda}
be the octonion
n
-torus. Then there exists an anti-involution
\sigma
on
\mathbb{O}
such that
\sigma(x)=\pm x
for all
x\in\mathbb{O}^{\lambda}
and for all
\lambda\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
, see
[
8
, Lemma 1.20]
. It is called the
standard anti-involution
on
\mathbb{O}
.

4.3.
Jordan tori

In Example
4.3.2
we briefly describe four families of Jordan
\Lambda
-tori. In particular, we specify a basis
\{x^{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}
and the multiplication rule in each family. For more details, see
[
30
]
.

Definition 4.3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a unital commutative associative algebra,
M
be a left
\mathcal{A}
-module and
f:M\times M\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
be a symmetric
\mathcal{A}
-bilinear form of
M
. Set
\mathrm{Cliff}(f):=\mathcal{A}1\oplus M
. Then
\mathrm{Cliff}(f)
together with the multiplication given by

(a1,x)\cdot(b1,y)=ab1+f(x,y)1+ay+bx

is a Jordan algebra. It is called the
Clifford Jordan algebra of

f
or just the
Jordan algebra of

f
(see
[
19
]
).

Example 4.3.2
.

(i) First let
{\mathbb{K}}_{\bf q}={\mathbb{K}}_{\bf q}[x_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,x_{n}^{\pm 1}]
be the quantum torus based on a quantum matrix
{\bf q}
with the
\Lambda
-grading as in Definition
4.1.1
. Now consider a new multiplication
\cdot
on
{\mathbb{K}}_{\bf q}
defined by
x\cdot y=\frac{1}{2}(xy+yx)
. Then
({\mathbb{K}}_{\bf q},\cdot)
is a Jordan algebra denoted by
{\mathbb{K}}_{\bf q}^{+}
and called the
plus algebra
of
{\mathbb{K}}_{\bf q}
. The
\Lambda
-graded algebra
{\mathbb{K}}_{\bf q}^{+}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Lambda}{\mathbb{K}}x^{\alpha}
is a Jordan
\Lambda
-torus and we have

(4.1)

x^{\alpha}\cdot x^{\beta}=\frac{1}{2}\prod_{i<j}q_{ij}^{\alpha_{j}\beta_{i}}\left(1+\prod_{i,j}q_{ij}^{\alpha_{i}\beta_{j}}\right)x^{\alpha+\beta},

for
\alpha,\beta\in\Lambda
(see
[
30
, Example 3.2]
).

(ii) Assume that
{\bf e}=(e_{ij})
is an elementary quantum matrix i.e.,
{\bf e}
is a quantum matrix such that
e_{ij}=1
or
-1
for all
i,j
. Now let
{\mathbb{K}}_{\bf e}={\mathbb{K}}_{\bf e}[x_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,x_{n}^{\pm 1}]
be the quantum torus determined by
{\bf e}
with the unique involution
-
such that
\overline{x_{i}}=x_{i}
for all
i
. Then the subspace of symmetric elements
H({\mathbb{K}}_{\bf e},-)=\{x\in{\mathbb{K}}_{\bf e}\mid\overline{x}=x\}
is a Jordan subalgebra of
{\mathbb{K}}_{\bf e}^{+}
. In fact the
\Lambda
-graded algebra
H({\mathbb{K}}_{\bf e},-)=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Lambda}\left({\mathbb{K}}x^{\alpha}\cap H({\mathbb{K}}_{\bf e},-)\right)
is a Jordan
\Lambda
-torus (see
[
30
, Example 4.3 (2)]
).

(iii) Suppose that
\Lambda
is of rank
n\geq 2
and let
2\leq m\leq n
.
Choose free abelian subgroups
\Lambda_{m}
and
\Lambda_{n-m}
with

\Lambda=\Lambda_{m}\oplus\Lambda_{n-m}
. Let
S
be a semilattice in
\Lambda_{m}
. Next set
\Gamma=2\Lambda_{m}\oplus\Lambda_{n-m}
and let
Z:={\mathbb{K}}[\Gamma]=\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Gamma}{\mathbb{K}}z^{\gamma}
be the group algebra of
\Gamma
over
{\mathbb{K}}
. Now let
I
be a set of coset representatives of
\{s+2\Lambda_{m}\mid s\in S\}\setminus\{2\Lambda_{m}\}
. Next let
V
be a free
Z
-module with basis
\{t_{\epsilon}\}_{\epsilon\in I}
and define a
Z
-bilinear form
f:V\times V\rightarrow Z
by

f(t_{\epsilon},t_{\eta})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}z^{2\epsilon}&\hbox{if }\epsilon=\eta\\
0&\hbox{otherwise}\end{array}\right.

for all
\epsilon,\eta\in I
. Let
{\mathcal{J}}_{S}:=Z\oplus V
be the Jordan algebra over
Z
of
f
, see Definition
4.3.1
. Note that if
\alpha\in S\oplus\Lambda_{n-m}
then there exist unique
\alpha^{\prime}\in\Gamma
and
\epsilon_{\alpha}\in I\cup\{0\}
such that
\alpha=\alpha^{\prime}+\epsilon_{\alpha}
. Set
t_{0}:=1
and

x^{\alpha}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}z^{\alpha^{\prime}}t_{\epsilon_{\alpha}}&\hbox{if }\alpha\in S\oplus\Lambda_{n-m}\\
0&\hbox{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

Then
{\mathcal{J}}_{S}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Lambda}{\mathbb{K}}x^{\alpha}
. In fact
{\mathcal{J}}_{S}
is a Jordan
\Lambda
-torus over
{\mathbb{K}}
which is called the
standard Clifford torus determined by

S
. For
\alpha,\beta\in S\oplus\Lambda_{n-m}
the multiplication rule is given by (see
[
30
, Example 5.2)]
:

(4.2)

x^{\alpha}x^{\beta}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}x^{\alpha+\beta}&\hbox{if }\epsilon_{\alpha}=\epsilon_{b}\neq 0\\
x^{\alpha+\beta}&\hbox{if }\epsilon_{\alpha}=0\hbox{ or }\epsilon_{\beta}=0\\
0&\hbox{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

(iv) Assume that
\Lambda
is of rank
n\geq 3
. Let
\omega
be a primitive third root of unity and let
w=(w_{ij})
be the quantum
n\times n
matrix with
w_{12}=\omega
,
w_{21}=\omega^{-1}
and all other entries equal to
1
. Next let
{\mathbb{K}}_{w}=\mathbb{K}_{w}[u_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,u_{n}^{\pm 1}]
be the quantum torus determined by
w
. Also let
Z=Z({\mathbb{K}}_{w})
be the center of
{\mathbb{K}}_{w}
. Then
Z=\mathbb{K}[u_{1}^{\pm 3},u_{2}^{\pm 3},u_{3}^{\pm 1},\ldots,u_{n}^{\pm 1}]
. Now consider the first Tits construction
\mathbb{A}_{t}=({\mathbb{K}}_{w},u_{3})={\mathbb{K}}_{w}\oplus{\mathbb{K}}_{w}\oplus{\mathbb{K}}_{w}
over
Z
, for details see
[
30
, Section 6]
. Next let
\{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}\}
be a basis of
\Lambda
and set
\Delta:={\mathbb{Z}}\lambda_{1}+{\mathbb{Z}}\lambda_{2}+3{\mathbb{Z}}\lambda_{3}+{\mathbb{Z}}\lambda_{4}+\cdots+{\mathbb{Z}}\lambda_{n}
. Define
\deg(u^{\delta}=u_{1}^{\delta_{1}}u_{2}^{\delta_{2}}u_{3}^{\delta_{3}}\cdots u_{n}^{\delta_{n}})=\delta
for
\delta=\delta_{1}\lambda_{1}+\delta_{2}\lambda_{2}+3\delta_{3}\lambda_{3}+\delta_{4}\lambda_{4}+\cdots+\delta_{n}\lambda_{n}\in\Delta
. This equips
{\mathbb{K}}_{w}=\bigoplus_{\delta\in\Delta}{\mathbb{K}}u^{\delta}
with a
\Delta
-grading. Finally for
\alpha=\alpha_{1}\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{n}\lambda_{n}\in\Lambda
, set

x^{\alpha}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(u^{\alpha},0,0)&\hbox{if }\alpha_{3}\equiv 0\quad(\hbox{mod}\;3)\\
(0,u^{\alpha-\lambda_{3}},0)&\hbox{if }\alpha_{3}\equiv 1\quad(\hbox{mod}\;3)\\
(0,0,u^{\alpha+\lambda_{3}})&\hbox{if }\alpha_{3}\equiv 2\quad(\hbox{mod}\;3).\\
\end{array}\right.

Then
\mathbb{A}_{t}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Lambda}{\mathbb{K}}x^{\alpha}
is a Jordan
\Lambda
-torus over
{\mathbb{K}}
which is called the
Albert torus
. For
(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2}),(y_{0},y_{1},y_{2})\in\mathbb{A}_{t}
the multiplication is given by

(4.3)

\begin{split}(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2})(y_{0},y_{1},y_{2})=&(x_{0}\cdot y_{0}+\overline{x_{1}y_{2}}+\overline{y_{1}x_{2}},\\
&\overline{x_{0}}y_{1}+\overline{y_{0}}x_{1}+u_{3}^{-1}x_{2}\times y_{2},\\
&y_{2}\overline{x_{0}}+x_{2}\overline{y_{0}}+u_{3}x_{1}\times y_{1}),\end{split}

where
x\cdot y=\frac{1}{2}(xy+yx),

x\times y=x\cdot y-\frac{1}{2}\hbox{tr}(x)y-\frac{1}{2}\hbox{tr}(y)x+\frac{1}{2}(\hbox{tr}(x)\hbox{tr}(y)-\hbox{tr}(x\cdot y))1,

\overline{x}=x\times 1
for
x,y\in{\mathbb{K}}_{w}
, and
tr
is the generic trace of the central closure
\overline{{\mathbb{K}}}_{w}
.

Y. Yoshii classified Jordan tori over any field of characteristic
\neq 2
, see
[
30
, Theorem 2]
. The following is the classification of Jordan tori over
{\mathbb{K}}
.

Theorem 4.3.3
.

[
30
, Corollary 7.2]

Any Jordan
\Lambda
-torus over
{\mathbb{K}}
is graded-isomorphic to one of the four Jordan tori

{\mathbb{K}}_{\bf q}^{+},H({\mathbb{K}}_{\bf e},-),{\mathcal{J}}_{S},\mathbb{A}_{t}.

Proposition 4.3.4
.

Assume that
\mathcal{A}=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\mathcal{A}^{\lambda}
is a Jordan
\Lambda
-torus over
{\mathbb{K}}
. Then
\mathcal{A}
admits a pre-Chevalley involution.

Proof.
By Theorem
4.3.3
, it is enough to prove the statement for Jordan tori
{\mathbb{K}}_{\bf q}^{+}
,
H({\mathbb{K}}_{\bf e},-)
,
{\mathcal{J}}_{S}
, and
\mathbb{A}_{t}
. So let
\mathcal{A}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Lambda}{\mathbb{K}}x^{\alpha}
be one of these four families of Jordan
\Lambda
-tori and let
\{x^{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}
be the
{\mathbb{K}}
-basis of
\mathcal{A}
described in Example
4.3.2
. We define
\tau:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
by
{\mathbb{K}}
-linear extension of
\tau(x^{\alpha})=x^{-\alpha}
for all
\alpha\in\Lambda
. Hence
\tau
is of order
2
and
\tau(\mathcal{A}^{\alpha})=\mathcal{A}^{-\alpha}
for all
\alpha\in\Lambda
. It remains to show that
\tau
is an automorphism of
\mathcal{A}
. Let
\alpha,\beta\in\Lambda
then
x^{\alpha}x^{\beta}=k(\alpha,\beta)x^{\alpha+\beta}
with
k(\alpha,\beta)\in{\mathbb{K}}
. For
{\mathbb{K}}_{\bf q}^{+}
,
H({\mathbb{K}}_{\bf e},-)
and
{\mathcal{J}}_{S}
, it is easy to see from the multiplication rules
4.1
and
4.2
that
k(\alpha,\beta)=k(-\alpha,-\beta)
for all
\alpha,\beta\in\Lambda
. Now let
\mathcal{A}=\mathbb{A}_{t}
and let
u^{\alpha}\in\mathbb{K}_{w}
. Then
u^{\alpha}=zu_{1}^{i_{1}}u_{2}^{i_{2}}
for some
z\in Z=Z(\mathbb{K}_{w})
. We first note that since
tr
is
Z
-linear then
\hbox{tr}(u^{\alpha})=z\hbox{tr}(u_{1}^{i_{1}}u_{2}^{i_{2}})
. On the other hand
\hbox{tr}(u_{1}^{i_{1}}u_{2}^{i_{2}})=0
if
i_{1}\not\equiv 0
or
i_{2}\not\equiv 0

(\hbox{mod}\;3)
, see
[
30
, Proposition 6.7]
. Hence

\hbox{tr}(u^{-\alpha})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0&\hbox{if }i_{1}\not\equiv 0\hbox{ or }i_{2}\not\equiv 0\;(\hbox{mod}\;3)\\
u^{-\alpha}\hbox{tr}(1)&\hbox{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

Considering this fact, now it is easy to check from
4.3
that
k(\alpha,\beta)=k(-\alpha,-\beta)
for all
\alpha,\beta\in\Lambda
. So
\tau
is an involution of
\mathcal{A}
and this proves our claim.
\Box

5.
Chevalley involutions for centerless Lie tori

It is well known that a Lie torus is a root graded Lie algebra. Based on the structure theory of root graded Lie algebras
[
7
]
,
[
6
]
, the coordinatization theorems for centerless Lie tori of reduced types have been proved in
[
9
]
,
[
8
]
,
[
30
]
and
[
4
]
. Using these coordinatization theorems, we investigate the existence of Chevalley involutions for centerless Lie tori of reduced types in this section. We proceed with a type-dependent argument.

Theorem 5.0.1
.

Suppose
\mathcal{L}
is a centerless Lie torus of reduced type where if it is of type
A_{\ell}
,
\ell\geq 2
, its coordinate algebra is equipped with a
\Lambda
-grading anti-involution. Then
\mathcal{L}
admits a Chevalley involution.

The proof is provided in Propositions
5.1.3
,
5.2.3
,
5.3.4
,
5.3.8
,
5.4.3
,
5.4.5
and
5.4.7
below.

5.1.
Simply laced centerless Lie tori of rank
\mathbf{>2}

Suppose in this part that
\Delta
is the finite root system of type
A_{\ell}
(
\ell\geq 3
),
D_{\ell}
(
\ell\geq 4
) or
E_{6,7,8}
and let
Q=Q(\Delta)
be the root lattice of
\Delta
. For type
A_{\ell}
we let

\Delta=\{0\}\cup\{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}\mid 1\leq i\neq j\leq\ell+1\}

where
\epsilon_{1},\ldots,\epsilon_{\ell+1}
is a basis for a vector space containing
\Delta
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\mathcal{A}^{\lambda}
be an associative
\Lambda
-torus and
[\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}]
be the Lie algebra of commutators in
\mathcal{A}
. Here we recall the definition of
special linear
Lie algebra over
\mathcal{A}
.

Definition 5.1.1
.

We denote by
M_{\ell+1}(\mathcal{A})
, the algebra of
(\ell+1)\times(\ell+1)
-matrices with entries from the associative
\Lambda
-torus
\mathcal{A}
, and by
\mathfrak{gl}(\mathcal{A})
the corresponding Lie algebra under the commutator bracket. We set

\mathfrak{sl}_{\ell+1}(\mathcal{A}):=\{A\in M_{\ell+1}(\mathcal{A})\mid\hbox{tr}(A)\in[\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}]\}

which is a Lie subalgebra of
\mathfrak{gl}(\mathcal{A})
called the
(\ell+1)\times(\ell+1)
-special linear Lie algebra over
\mathcal{A}
. When
\mathcal{A}
is commutative, we usually identify
\mathfrak{sl}_{\ell+1}(\mathcal{A})
with

\mathfrak{sl}_{\ell+1}({\mathbb{K}})\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}\mathcal{A}
. We note that

M_{\ell+1}(\mathcal{A})
, and consequently
\mathfrak{gl}(\mathcal{A})
, inherits the
\Lambda
-grading of
\mathcal{A}
. In fact, we have
\mathfrak{gl}_{\ell+1}(\mathcal{A})=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\mathfrak{gl}_{\ell+1}(\mathcal{A})^{\lambda}
with
\mathfrak{gl}_{\ell+1}(\mathcal{A})^{\lambda}=M_{\ell+1}(\mathcal{A}^{\lambda})
. Finally,
\mathfrak{sl}_{\ell+1}(\mathcal{A})
is a
\Lambda
-graded subalgebra of
\mathfrak{gl}_{\ell+1}(\mathcal{A})
.

Let
\mathcal{L}=\mathfrak{sl}_{\ell+1}(\mathcal{A})
or
\mathcal{L}=\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{A}_{[n]}
where
\mathfrak{g}
is the finite dimensional simple Lie algebra of type
D_{\ell}
(
\ell\geq 4
) or
E_{6,7,8}
. Then
\mathcal{L}
is a
Q\times\Lambda
-graded Lie algebra. If
\mathcal{L}=\mathfrak{sl}_{\ell+1}(\mathcal{A})
then

(5.1)

\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}}^{\lambda}=\mathcal{A}^{\lambda}E_{ij},\quad\mathcal{L}_{0}^{\lambda}=\{\Sigma_{i=1}^{\ell+1}x_{i}E_{ii}\mid x_{i}\in\mathcal{A}^{\lambda},\Sigma_{i=1}^{\ell+1}x_{i}\in[\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}]\},

for
i\neq j,\lambda\in\Lambda
, where
E_{ij}
denotes the matrix with
1
in
(i,j)
-th entry and zero elsewhere, and
\mathcal{L}_{\beta}^{\lambda}=\{0\}
for
\lambda\in\Lambda
,
\beta\in Q\setminus\Delta
. Also if
\mathcal{L}=\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{A}_{[n]}
then

(5.2)

\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}^{\lambda}=\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}\otimes\mathcal{A}_{[n]}^{\lambda},\quad\mathcal{L}_{\beta}^{\lambda}=\{0\},

for
\alpha\in\Delta,\beta\in Q\setminus\Delta,\lambda\in\Lambda
.
In fact
\mathcal{L}
is a centerless
\Lambda
-torus of type
\Delta
. Moreover, S. Berman, Y. Gao, and Y. Krylyuk proved the following coordinatization theorem.

Theorem 5.1.2
.

[
9
, Theorem 2.65, Theorem 1.37]

(i) Any centerless Lie
\Lambda
-torus of type
A_{\ell}
(
\ell\geq 3
) is graded-isomorphic to
\mathfrak{sl}_{\ell+1}(\mathcal{A})
where
\mathcal{A}
is an associative
\Lambda
-torus.

(ii) Any centerless Lie
\Lambda
-torus of type
D_{\ell}
(
\ell\geq 4
) or
E_{6,7,8}
is graded-isomorphic to
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{A}_{[n]}
where
\mathfrak{g}
is the finite dimensional simple Lie algebra of type
D_{\ell}
(
\ell\geq 4
) or
E_{6,7,8}
, respectively.

Proposition 5.1.3
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a centerless Lie
\Lambda
-torus of type
A_{\ell}
, (
\ell\geq 3
),
D_{\ell}
(
\ell\geq 4
) or
E_{6,7,8}
. Further for type
A_{\ell}
assume that the coordinate algebra of
\mathcal{L}
is equipped with a
\Lambda
-grading anti-involution. Then
\mathcal{L}
admits a Chevalley involution.

Proof.
First let
\mathcal{L}
be a centerless Lie
\Lambda
-torus of type
A_{\ell},

\ell\geq 3
whose coordinate algebra has a
\Lambda
-grading anti-involution. From Theorem
5.1.2
(i) we see that
\mathcal{L}\cong\mathfrak{sl}_{\ell+1}(\mathcal{A})
where
\mathcal{A}
, the coordinate algebra of
\mathcal{L}
, is an associative
\Lambda
-torus with a
\Lambda
-grading anti-involution
\sigma
. Thus we may assume that
\mathcal{L}=\mathfrak{sl}_{\ell+1}(\mathcal{A})
. We consider the
Q\times\Lambda
-grading of
\mathcal{L}=\sum_{\alpha\in Q,\lambda\in\Lambda}\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}^{\lambda}
as in (
5.1
).

Next let
\tau
be a pre-Chevalley involution of
\mathcal{A}
, see Proposition
4.1.2
. We define
\bar{\tau}:\mathcal{L}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}
by
\bar{\tau}:=\hat{\tau}\circ\theta
, where
\theta
is the involution of
\mathfrak{sl}_{\ell+1}(\mathcal{A})
given by

\theta(A)=-{\sigma(A^{t})},\quad A\in\mathfrak{sl}_{\ell+1}(\mathcal{A}),

with
\sigma(X):=(\sigma(x_{ij}))
for
X=(x_{ij})\in\mathrm{Mat}_{\ell}(\mathcal{A})
,
and
\hat{\tau}
is the involution of
\mathfrak{sl}_{\ell+1}(\mathcal{A})
induced by
\tau\in\hbox{Aut}(\mathcal{A})
. In fact
\hat{\tau}
is given by

{\hat{\tau}(xE_{ij})=\tau(x)E_{ij},\quad x\in\mathcal{A},\;1\leq i,j\leq\ell+1.}

Then clearly
\bar{\tau}(\mathcal{L}^{\lambda})=\mathcal{L}^{-\lambda}
for
\lambda\in\Lambda
and
\bar{\tau}(h)=-h
for
h\in\mathcal{L}^{0}_{0}
. So
\bar{\tau}
is a Chevalley involution for
\mathcal{L}={\mathfrak{sl}_{\ell+1}(\mathcal{A})}
.

Finally, let
\mathcal{L}
be a centerless Lie
\Lambda
-torus of type
\Delta=D_{\ell},\ell\geq 4
or
\Delta=E_{\ell},\ell=6,7,8
. By Theorem
5.1.2
(ii), we may assume
\mathcal{L}=\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{A}
, where
\mathfrak{g}
is the finite dimensional simple Lie algebra of type
\Delta
and
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}_{[n]}
. Replacing
\theta
above with
\theta_{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes 1_{\mathcal{A}}
, where
\theta_{\mathfrak{g}}
is a Chevalley involution for
\mathfrak{g}
, one sees that the discussion above is valid also for this case (the involution
\hat{\tau}
can be expressed as
\hat{\tau}(a\otimes x)=a\otimes\hat{\tau}(x)
for
a\in\mathfrak{g}
and
x\in\mathcal{A}
).
\Box

In order to realize the compact forms of intersection matrix Lie algebras, in
[
11
]
the author introduces a notion of an involution for a
\Delta
-graded Lie algebra which is compatible with the
\Delta
-grading in the sense of
[
11
, Definition 2.4]
. It is worth mentioning that any Chevalley involution of a Lie torus of type
\Delta
, considered as a
\Delta
-graded Lie algebra, is compatible with its
\Delta
-grading.
The involution
\theta
is the proof of Proposition
5.1.3
coincides with the involution given in
[
11
, Example 2.5]
, while the involution
\theta_{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes 1_{\mathcal{A}}
coincides with one given in
[
11
, Example 2.6]
.

5.2.
Lie tori of type
\mathbf{A_{2}}

We first recall definition of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{psl}_{3}(\mathcal{A})
for an alternative algebra
\mathcal{A}
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an alternative algebra and let
L_{a}
and
R_{a}
denote the left and the right multiplication by
a\in\mathcal{A}
, respectively. For
a,b\in\mathcal{A},
let
D_{a,b}:=[L_{a},L_{b}]+[R_{a},R_{b}]+[L_{a},R_{b}]
which is called the
inner derivation of
\mathcal{A}
determined by elements
a,b
. Then, set
D_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}}:=\hbox{span}_{{\mathbb{K}}}\{D_{a,b}\mid a,b\in\mathcal{A}\}
. In fact
D_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}}
is an ideal of the Lie algebra
\mathrm{Der}(\mathcal{A})
of derivations of
\mathcal{A}
.

Definition 5.2.1
.

Assume that
\mathcal{A}
is an alternative algebra over
\mathbb{K}
. Set

\mathfrak{psl}_{3}(\mathcal{A}):=(\mathfrak{sl}_{3}(\mathbb{K})\otimes\mathcal{A})\oplus D_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}}.

Then
\mathfrak{psl}_{3}(\mathcal{A})
is a Lie algebra under the bracket defined by

\begin{split}[x\otimes a,y\otimes b]=&[x,y]\otimes\frac{ab+ba}{2}+\frac{1}{3}\hbox{tr}(xy)D_{a,b}\\
+&(xy+yx-\frac{2}{3}\hbox{tr}(xy)I)\otimes\frac{ab-ba}{2},\\
[D_{a,b},x\otimes c]=&x\otimes D_{a,b}(c)=-[x\otimes c,D_{a,b}],\\
[D_{a,b},D_{c,d}]=&D_{D_{a,b}(c),d}+D_{c,D_{a,b}(d)},\end{split}

for
a,b,c,d\in\mathcal{A},x,y\in\mathfrak{sl}_{3}(\mathbb{K})
where
I
is the
3\times 3
identity matrix and
\hbox{tr}(x)
denotes the trace of a matrix
x
, see
[
8
, Section 2]
.

Let
\mathfrak{h}
be the Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}(\mathbb{K})
consisting of diagonal matrices and for
i=1,2,3
, let
\epsilon_{i}:\mathfrak{h}\rightarrow\mathbb{K}
be the projection onto
(i,i)
entry. Let
\Delta:=\{0\}\cup\{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}\mid 1\leq i\neq j\leq 3\}
. Then
\Delta
can be realized as the root system of type
A_{2}
with
\{\alpha_{1}=\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{2},\alpha_{2}=\epsilon_{2}-\epsilon_{3}\}

as a basis of
\Delta
. Now let
\mathcal{A}=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\mathcal{A}^{\lambda}
be an alternative
\Lambda
-torus and set
\mathcal{L}=\mathfrak{psl}_{3}(\mathcal{A})
. Then
\mathcal{L}
is a
Q\times\Lambda
-graded algebra with

(5.3)

\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}}^{\lambda}=E_{ij}\otimes\mathcal{A}^{\lambda},\quad\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}^{\lambda}=\{0\},\quad\mathcal{L}_{0}^{\lambda}=(\mathfrak{h}\otimes\mathcal{A}^{\lambda})\oplus\sum_{\mu+\nu=\lambda}D_{\mathcal{A}^{\mu},\mathcal{A}^{\nu}},

for
i\neq j,\lambda\in\Lambda
and
\alpha\in Q\setminus\Delta
, where
E_{ij}
denotes the matrix with
1
in
(i,j)
-th entry and zero elsewhere. In fact
\mathcal{L}
is a centerless
\Lambda
-torus of type
A_{2}
, see
[
8
]
,
[
31
]
. The following theorem determines the coordinate algebra of EALAs of type
A_{2}
.

Theorem 5.2.2
.

[
8
, Lemma 3.25]
[
31
, Proposition 6.3]

Any centerless Lie
\Lambda
-torus of type
A_{2}
is graded-isomorphic to
\mathfrak{psl}_{3}(\mathcal{A})
where
\mathcal{A}
is an alternative
\Lambda
-torus.

Proposition 5.2.3
.

Suppose
\mathcal{L}
is a centerless
\Lambda
-torus of type
A_{2}
and that its coordinate algebra is equipped with a
\Lambda
-grading anti-involution. Then
\mathcal{L}
admits a Chevalley involution.

Proof.
Using Theorem
5.2.2
, it is enough to show the existence of a Chevalley involution for
\mathcal{L}=\mathfrak{psl}_{3}(\mathcal{A})
where
\mathcal{A}=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\mathcal{A}^{\lambda}
is an alternative
\Lambda
-torus with a
\Lambda
-grading anti-involution
\bar{}
.
First recall from Proposition
4.2.4
that
\mathcal{A}
admits a pre-Chevalley involution
\tau
. Set
\bar{\tau}:=\tau\circ\bar{}
. Then
\bar{\tau}
is an anti-automorphism of
\mathcal{A}
. So
\bar{\tau}
induces a Lie algebra anti-automorphism
\hat{\tau}:\mathrm{Der}(\mathcal{A})\rightarrow\mathrm{Der}(\mathcal{A})
by
\hat{\tau}(D)=\bar{\tau}D\bar{\tau}^{-1}
, for
D\in\mathrm{Der}(\mathcal{A})
. Since
\hat{\tau}(D_{a,b})=D_{\tau(\overline{a}),\tau(\overline{b})}
for
a,b\in\mathcal{A}
, we see that
\hat{\tau}
restricts to a Lie algebra anti-automorphism of
D_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}}
. Next, we define
\tilde{\tau}
on
\mathfrak{psl}_{3}(\mathcal{A})=(\mathfrak{sl}_{3}(\mathbb{K})\otimes\mathcal{A})\oplus D_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}}
by

\tilde{\tau}(x\otimes a+D_{b,c})=-x^{t}\otimes\tau(\overline{a})+\hat{\tau}(D_{b,c}),

for
x\in\mathfrak{sl}_{3}(\mathbb{K}),a,b,c\in\mathcal{A}
, where
x^{t}
denotes the transpose of
x
. Our claim is that
\tilde{\tau}
is a Chevalley involution for
\mathfrak{psl}_{3}(\mathcal{A})
. To see this, let
x,y\in\mathfrak{sl}_{3}(\mathbb{K}),
and
a,b\in\mathcal{A}
. Then

\begin{split}\tilde{\tau}[x\otimes a,y\otimes b]=&-[x,y]^{t}\otimes\frac{\tau(\overline{ab+ba})}{2}+\frac{1}{3}\hbox{tr}(xy)\hat{\tau}(D_{a,b})\\
-&(xy+yx-\frac{2}{3}\hbox{tr}(xy)I)^{t}\otimes\frac{\tau(\overline{ab-ba})}{2}.\end{split}

Now we have

\begin{split}-[x,y]^{t}\otimes\frac{\tau(\overline{ab+ba})}{2}=&[x^{t},y^{t}]\otimes\frac{\tau(\overline{a})\tau(\overline{b})+\tau(\overline{b})\tau(\overline{a})}{2},\\
\hbox{tr}(xy)\hat{\tau}(D_{a,b})=&\hbox{tr}(x^{t}y^{t})D_{\tau(\overline{a}),\tau(\overline{b})},\end{split}

and

\begin{split}&-(xy+yx-\frac{2}{3}\hbox{tr}(xy)I)^{t}\otimes\frac{\tau(\overline{ab-ba})}{2}=\\
&(x^{t}y^{t}+y^{t}x^{t}-\frac{2}{3}\hbox{tr}(x^{t}y^{t})I)\otimes\frac{\tau(\overline{a})\tau(\overline{b})-\tau(\overline{b})\tau(\overline{a})}{2}.\end{split}

So
\tilde{\tau}[x\otimes a,y\otimes b]=[\tilde{\tau}(x\otimes a),\tilde{\tau}(y\otimes b)]
. Since

(\tau(\overline{b}),\tau(\overline{c}),\tau(\overline{a}))=-(\tau(\overline{a}),\tau(\overline{c}),\tau(\overline{b})),\;a,b,c\in\mathcal{A}

where
(\tau(\overline{b}),\tau(\overline{c}),\tau(\overline{a}))
is the associator of
\tau(\overline{b}),\tau(\overline{c}),\tau(\overline{a})
, we get
\tau(\overline{D_{a,b}(c)})=D_{\tau(\overline{a}),\tau(\overline{b})}(\tau(\overline{c}))
. This gives
\tilde{\tau}[D_{a,b},x\otimes c]=[\tilde{\tau}(D_{a,b}),\tilde{\tau}(x\otimes c)]
and
\tilde{\tau}[D_{a,b},D_{c,d}]=[\tilde{\tau}(D_{a,b}),\tilde{\tau}(D_{c,d})]
. So
\tilde{\tau}
is a Lie algebra involution of
\mathcal{L}=\mathfrak{psl}_{3}(\mathcal{A})
.

To see that
\tilde{\tau}
is a pre-Chevalley involution, fix
\lambda\in\Lambda
, then by (
5.3
),

\mathcal{L}^{\lambda}=(\mathfrak{h}\otimes\mathcal{A}^{\lambda})\oplus\sum_{\mu+\nu=\lambda}D_{\mathcal{A}^{\mu},\mathcal{A}^{\nu}}\oplus\left(\bigoplus_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}\in\Delta}E_{ij}\otimes\mathcal{A}^{\lambda}\right).

So
\tilde{\tau}(\mathcal{L}^{\lambda})=\mathcal{L}^{-\lambda}
. Also we note that
\mathcal{L}_{0}^{0}=(\mathfrak{h}\otimes\mathbb{K}1)\oplus\sum_{\mu\in\Lambda}\mathbb{K}D_{x^{\mu},\tau(x^{\mu})}
, where
0\neq x^{\mu}\in\mathcal{A}^{\mu}
. Since
D_{a,b}+D_{b,a}=0
(see
[
8
, Section 1]
) and
D_{a,b}=D_{-a,-b}
for all
a,b\in\mathcal{A}
, we have
\tilde{\tau}(D_{x^{\mu},\tau(x^{\mu})})=D_{\tau(x^{\mu}),x^{\mu}}=-D_{x^{\mu},\tau(x^{\mu})}
. This shows that
\tilde{\tau}_{|_{\mathcal{L}_{0}^{0}}}=-\hbox{id}
. So
\tilde{\tau}
is a Chevalley involution for
\mathfrak{psl}_{3}(\mathcal{A})
and this completes the proof.
\Box

5.3.
Centerless Lie tori of type
\mathbf{A_{1}}
and
\mathbf{C_{\ell}(\ell\geq 2)}

We begin by recalling from
[
15
, Chapter VIII, Section 5]
the Tits-Kantor-Koecher (
\mathrm{TKK}
) construction of a Lie algebra from a Jordan algebra. We apply the
\mathrm{TKK}
construction to a
\Lambda
-graded Jordan algebra, to construct centreless
\Lambda
-tori of type
A_{1}
and
C_{l}
.

5.3.1
.

(
\mathrm{TKK}
Construction)
Let
{\mathcal{J}}
be a Jordan algebra and let
L_{x}
be the operator on
{\mathcal{J}}
defined by
L_{x}y=xy
, for
x,y\in{\mathcal{J}}
. Set

\mathrm{Instrl}({\mathcal{J}}):=L_{\mathcal{J}}\oplus\mathrm{Inder}({\mathcal{J}})

where
\mathrm{Inder}({\mathcal{J}})
is the Lie algebra of inner derivations
\{\sum_{i}[L_{x_{i}},L_{y_{i}}]\mid x_{i},y_{i}\in{\mathcal{J}}\}
. Together with the bracket defined by

[L_{x}+C,L_{y}+D]=[L_{x},L_{y}]+L_{Cy}-L_{Dx}+[C,D],

for
x,y\in{\mathcal{J}},C,D\in\mathrm{Inder}({\mathcal{J}})
,
\mathrm{Instrl}({\mathcal{J}})
is a subalgebra of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}\ell({\mathcal{J}})
which is called the
inner structure Lie algebra
of
{\mathcal{J}}
. Define
\bar{}:\mathrm{Instrl}({\mathcal{J}})\rightarrow\mathrm{Instrl}({\mathcal{J}})
by
\overline{L_{a}+D}=-L_{a}+D
. It is an involution of
\mathrm{Instrl}({\mathcal{J}})
. Now put
\mathrm{TKK}({\mathcal{J}}):={\mathcal{J}}\oplus\mathrm{Instrl}({\mathcal{J}})\oplus\bar{{\mathcal{J}}}
where
\bar{{\mathcal{J}}}
is isomorphic to
{\mathcal{J}}
under a linear map
x\mapsto\bar{x}
. Then
\mathrm{TKK}({\mathcal{J}})
is a Lie algebra, called
TKK Lie algebra
of
{\mathcal{J}}
, under the bracket defined by

\displaystyle[x_{1}+\bar{y}_{1}+E_{1},x_{2}+\bar{y}_{2}+E_{2}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-E_{2}x_{1}+E_{1}x_{2}-\overline{\bar{E}_{2}b_{1}}+\overline{\bar{E}_{1}b_{2}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle x_{1}\triangle y_{2}-x_{2}\triangle y_{1}+[E_{1},E_{2}],

for
x_{i}\in{\mathcal{J}},\bar{y}_{i}\in\bar{{\mathcal{J}}}
, and
E_{i}\in\mathrm{Instrl}({\mathcal{J}})
, where
x\triangle y=L_{xy}+[L_{x},L_{y}]
.

Let
{\mathcal{J}}=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda}{\mathcal{J}}^{\lambda}
be a
\Lambda
-graded Jordan algebra. Then
\mathrm{TKK}({\mathcal{J}})
is
\Lambda
-graded with

(5.4)

\mathrm{TKK}({\mathcal{J}})^{\lambda}={\mathcal{J}}^{\lambda}\oplus\mathrm{Instrl}({\mathcal{J}})^{\lambda}\oplus\overline{{\mathcal{J}}^{\lambda}},

for
\lambda\in\Lambda
, where
\mathrm{Instrl}({\mathcal{J}})^{\lambda}=L_{{\mathcal{J}}^{\lambda}}\oplus\sum_{\mu+\nu=\lambda}[L_{{\mathcal{J}}^{\mu}},L_{{\mathcal{J}}^{\nu}}]
(for more details see
[
1
, Chapter III,
\lx@sectionsign 2
]
).

Proposition 5.3.2
.

Assume that
{\mathcal{J}}=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda}{\mathcal{J}}^{\lambda}
is a
\Lambda
-graded Jordan algebra with a pre-Chevalley involution. Then
\mathrm{TKK}({\mathcal{J}})
admits a pre-Chevalley involution.

Proof.
Let
\tau:{\mathcal{J}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{J}}
be a pre-Chevalley involution on
{\mathcal{J}}
. We first note that
\tau
induces a Lie algebra involution
\hat{\tau}
of
\mathrm{Der}({\mathcal{J}})
by
\hat{\tau}(D)=\tau D\tau^{-1}
, for
D\in\mathrm{Der}({\mathcal{J}})
. Now we have

\hat{\tau}(\sum_{i}[L_{x_{i}},L_{y_{i}}])=\sum_{i}[L_{\tau(x_{i})},L_{\tau(y_{i})}],

where
\;x_{i},y_{i}\in{\mathcal{J}}
. So
\hat{\tau}
restricts to a Lie algebra involution of
\mathrm{Inder}({\mathcal{J}})
. Next, we extend
\hat{\tau}
to
\mathrm{Instrl}({\mathcal{J}})
by
\hat{\tau}(L_{x}+D):=L_{\tau(x)}+\hat{\tau}(D)
, for
x\in{\mathcal{J}}
and
D\in\mathrm{Inder}({\mathcal{J}})
. Then
\hat{\tau}
is a Lie algebra involution of
\mathrm{Instrl}({\mathcal{J}})
, since
\hat{\tau}(L_{Ex})=L_{\hat{\tau}(E)(\tau(x))},x\in{\mathcal{J}},E\in\mathrm{Inder}({\mathcal{J}})
. Now we define
\bar{\tau}
on
\mathrm{TKK}({\mathcal{J}})=J\oplus\mathrm{Instrl}({\mathcal{J}})\oplus\bar{{\mathcal{J}}}
by

(5.5)

\bar{\tau}(x+\bar{y}+E)=\overline{\tau(x)}+\tau(y)+\overline{\hat{\tau}(E)},\;x\in{\mathcal{J}},\bar{y}\in\bar{{\mathcal{J}}},E\in\mathrm{Instrl}({\mathcal{J}}).

To show that
\bar{\tau}
is a Lie algebra homomorphism of
\mathrm{TKK}({\mathcal{J}})
, let
x_{1},x_{2}\in{\mathcal{J}},\bar{y}_{1},\bar{y}_{2}\in\bar{{\mathcal{J}}},E_{1},E_{2}\in\mathrm{Instrl}({\mathcal{J}})
. Then

\displaystyle\bar{\tau}[x_{1}+\bar{y}_{1}+E_{1},x_{2}+\bar{y}_{2}+E_{2}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\overline{\tau(E_{2}x_{1})}+\overline{\tau(E_{1}x_{2})}-\tau(\bar{E}_{2}y_{1})+\tau(\bar{E}_{1}y_{2})

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\overline{\hat{\tau}(x_{1}\triangle y_{2})}-\overline{\hat{\tau}(x_{2}\triangle y_{1})}+[\overline{\hat{\tau}(E_{1})},\overline{\hat{\tau}(E_{2})}].

We have
\overline{\tau(E_{2}x_{1})}=\overline{\hat{\tau}(E_{2})\tau(x_{1})}
. Similarly
\overline{\tau(E_{1}x_{2})}=\overline{\hat{\tau}(E_{1})\tau(x_{2})},\tau(\bar{E}_{2}y_{1})=\overline{\hat{\tau}(E_{2})}\tau(y_{1})
and
\tau(\bar{E}_{1}y_{2})=\overline{\hat{\tau}(E_{1})}\tau(y_{2})
. On the other hand

\displaystyle\overline{\hat{\tau}(x_{1}\triangle y_{2})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-L_{\tau(x_{1}y_{2})}+[L_{\tau(x_{1})},L_{\tau(y_{2})}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(L_{\tau(y_{2}x_{1})}+[L_{\tau(y_{2})},L_{\tau(x_{1})}])=-\tau(y_{2})\triangle\tau(x_{1}).

Similarly
\overline{\hat{\tau}(x_{2}\triangle y_{1})}=-\tau(y_{1})\triangle\tau(x_{2})
. So
\bar{\tau}
is an involution of
\mathrm{TKK}({\mathcal{J}})
. Finally, note that
\bar{\tau}(\mathrm{TKK}({\mathcal{J}})^{\lambda})=\mathrm{TKK}({\mathcal{J}})^{-\lambda}
for all
\lambda\in\Lambda
, see (
5.4
). Thus
\bar{\tau}
is a Chevalley involution for
\mathcal{L}=\mathrm{TKK}({\mathcal{J}})
and this proves our claim.

\Box

\mathbf{A_{1}}

Next assume that

for

-grading (

), then
\mathcal{L}

.

[

Proposition 5.3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a centerless
\Lambda

A_{1}
. Then
\mathcal{L}
admits a Chevalley involution.

Proof.
By Theorem
5.3.3
, we can assume that
\mathcal{L}=\mathrm{TKK}({\mathcal{J}})
where
{\mathcal{J}}

\Lambda

\mathrm{TKK}({\mathcal{J}})

5.3.2.
Type
\mathbf{C_{\ell}(\ell\geq 2)}

Assume that

\Delta=\{0\}\cup\{\pm 2\epsilon_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq l\}\cup\{\pm(\epsilon_{i}\pm\epsilon_{j})\mid 1\leq i<j\leq\ell\}

is the finite root system of type
C_{\ell}
and let
Q=Q(\Delta)
be the root lattice of
\Delta
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an algebra over
{\mathbb{K}}
and let
\sigma
be an anti-involution of
\mathcal{A}
. For
\ell\geq 1
, set

H_{\ell}(\mathcal{A},\sigma):=\{X\in\mathrm{Mat}_{\ell}(\mathcal{A})\mid\sigma(X^{t})=X\}

where
\sigma(X):=(\sigma(x_{ij}))
for
X=(x_{ij})\in\mathrm{Mat}_{\ell}(\mathcal{A})
. Then
H_{l}(\mathcal{A},\sigma)
is a subalgebra of
\mathrm{Mat}_{\ell}(\mathcal{A})^{+}
, the plus algebra of
\mathrm{Mat}_{\ell}(\mathcal{A})
. So
H_{l}(\mathcal{A},\sigma)
is an algebra with multiplication
X\cdot Y=\frac{1}{2}(XY+YX)
. Note that if
\mathcal{A}
is
\Lambda
-graded, then the
\Lambda
-grading of
\mathcal{A}
induces a
\Lambda
-grading on
H_{\ell}(\mathcal{A},\sigma)
such that

H_{\ell}(\mathcal{A},\sigma)^{\lambda}=\{X=(x_{ij})\in\mathrm{Mat}_{\ell}(\mathcal{A})\mid x_{ij}\in\mathcal{A}^{\lambda},\text{for all }1\leq i,j\leq\ell\},

for
\lambda\in\Lambda
.

Lemma 5.3.5
.

(i) Let
\mathcal{A}
be an algebra with an anti-involution
\sigma
and let
\tau
be an automorphism of
\mathcal{A}
. If
\sigma\tau=\tau\sigma
, then
\tau
induces an automorphism
\tau:H_{\ell}(\mathcal{A},\sigma)\rightarrow H_{\ell}(\mathcal{A},\sigma)
by
\tau(X):=(\tau(x_{ij}))
for
X=(x_{ij})\in H_{\ell}(\mathcal{A},\sigma)
.

(ii) Consider the quantum torus
(\mathcal{A},\sigma)=({\mathbb{K}}_{\bf q},\sigma_{\bf e})
with anti-involution determined by some
{\bf e}
and
{\bf q}
satisfying the conditions of Remark
4.1.3
, or consider the octonion torus
(\mathcal{A},\sigma)=(\mathbb{O},\sigma)
with standard anti-involution. Then
H_{\ell}(\mathcal{A},\sigma)
admits a pre-Chevalley involution.

Proof.
(i) Clearly
\tau:\mathrm{Mat}_{\ell}(\mathcal{A})\rightarrow\mathrm{Mat}_{\ell}(\mathcal{A})
with
\tau(X):=(\tau(x_{ij}))
is an automorphism of
(\mathrm{Mat}_{\ell}(\mathcal{A}),\cdot)
. Since
\sigma\tau=\tau\sigma
, we have
\sigma(\tau(X)^{t})=\sigma(\tau(X^{t}))=\tau(\sigma(X^{t}))=\tau(X)
. So
\tau:\mathrm{Mat}_{\ell}(\mathcal{A})\rightarrow\mathrm{Mat}_{\ell}(\mathcal{A})
restricts to an automorphism of
H_{\ell}(\mathcal{A},\sigma)
.

(ii) By Theorem
4.0.2
,
\mathcal{A}
admits a pre-Chevalley involution
\tau
. Considering Remark
4.1.3
and Remark
4.2.5
, one can easily check that
\sigma\tau=\tau\sigma
. Then by part (i),
\tau:H_{\ell}(\mathcal{A},\sigma)\rightarrow H_{\ell}(\mathcal{A},\sigma)
is an automorphism. Also we have
\tau(H_{l}(\mathcal{A},\sigma)^{\lambda})=H_{\ell}(\mathcal{A},\sigma)^{-\lambda}
, for all
\lambda\in\Lambda
. So
\tau:H_{\ell}(\mathcal{A},\sigma)\rightarrow H_{\ell}(\mathcal{A},\sigma)
is a pre-Chevalley involution.

\Box

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a commutative associative algebra,
M
be a left
\mathcal{A}
-module and
f:M\times M\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
be a symmetric
\mathcal{A}
-bilinear form of
M
. Set
\mathrm{RedCliff}(f):=\mathcal{A}\oplus\mathcal{A}\oplus M
. Then
\mathrm{RedCliff}(f)
together with the multiplication given by

\begin{split}(a_{1},a_{2},x)\cdot(b_{1},b_{2},y)=\left(a_{1}b_{1}+f(x,y),a_{2}b_{2}+f(x,y),\frac{1}{2}(a_{1}y+a_{2}y+b_{1}x+b_{2}x)\right)\end{split}

is a Jordan algebra. It is called the
reduced Clifford Jordan algebra of

f
(see
[
19
]
).

Now assume that
\mathcal{A}_{[n]}
is the ring of Laurent polynomials in
n
variables and
\mathcal{A}_{[n]}^{m}
is the free
\mathcal{A}_{[n]}
-module with basis
v_{1},\ldots,v_{m}
. Fix
\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{m}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
,
m\geq 2
, such that
\tau_{1}=0
and
\tau_{i}\not\equiv\tau_{j}(\hbox{mod}\;2{\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
for
i\neq j
. Define
h:\mathcal{A}_{[n]}^{m}\times\mathcal{A}_{[n]}^{m}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{[n]}
by

(5.7)

h\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}v_{i},\sum_{i=1}^{m}b_{i}v_{i}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}b_{i}x^{\tau_{i}},

Then

(5.8)

\deg(x^{\lambda},0,0)=\deg(0,x^{\lambda},0)=2\lambda\quad\quad\text{and}\quad\quad\deg(0,0,x^{\lambda}v_{i})=2\lambda+\tau_{i},

where

]
).

Lemma 5.3.6
.

Let

be as in (

Proof.
Let

(x^{\lambda},0,0)\mapsto(x^{-\lambda},0,0),\;\;(0,x^{\lambda},0)\mapsto(0,x^{-\lambda},0),\;\;(0,0,x^{\lambda}v_{i})\mapsto(0,0,x^{-\lambda-\tau_{i}}v_{i}),

for

and

, for all

. One checks that

is an automorphism of

.

\Box

Let
{\mathcal{J}}=H_{\ell}(\mathcal{A},\sigma)
, where

is as in Lemma

(ii), or let

\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon_{i}+\epsilon_{j}}={\mathcal{J}}_{ij},\quad\mathcal{L}_{-\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}}=\overline{{\mathcal{J}}_{ij}},\quad 1\leq i\leq j\leq\ell.

[

Any centerless Lie
\Lambda
-torus of type

(ii)

(iii)

{\mathcal{J}}=\mathrm{RedCliff}(h)

Proposition 5.3.8
.

Assume that
\mathcal{L}
is a centerless
\Lambda
-torus of type
C_{\ell},\ell\geq 2
. Then
\mathcal{L}
admits a Chevalley involution.

Proof.
We know that
\mathcal{L}
is graded-isomorphic to
\mathrm{TKK}({\mathcal{J}})
with
{\mathcal{J}}
as in part (i), (ii) or (iii) of Theorem
5.3.7

admits a pre-Chevalley involution

defined by (

5.4.
Centerless Lie tori of type
\mathbf{B_{\ell}(\ell\geq 3)}
,
\mathbf{F_{4}}
and
\mathbf{G_{2}}

The generalized Tits construction can be used to construct centerless Lie tori of type
B_{\ell}(\ell\geq 3)
,
F_{4}
and
G_{2}
. Here, we begin by recalling the generalized Tits construction (see
[
25
, pp.120–127]
and
[
16
,
\lx@sectionsign 10
]
for some references of this construction). Then we separate into three parts, one for each of these types.

Assume that
\mathcal{A}
is a unital commutative associative algebra over
\mathbb{K}
. Let
\mathcal{X}
be a unital algebra over
\mathcal{A}
. An
\mathcal{A}
-linear map
T:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
is called a
normalized trace
on
\mathcal{X}
if
T(1)=1
and

T(xx^{\prime})=T(x^{\prime}x),\quad\quad T((xx^{\prime})x^{\prime\prime})=T(x(x^{\prime}x^{\prime\prime}))

for
x,x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{X}
. We
use the same symbol
T
to denote the normalized trace for different algebras. If
T
is a normalized trace on
\mathcal{X}
, then one can write

\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{A}1\oplus\mathcal{X}_{0}

where
\mathcal{X}_{0}=\{x\in\mathcal{X}\mid T(x)=0\}
. Also for
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}
, we have

xx^{\prime}=T(xx^{\prime})1+x*x^{\prime}

where
x*x^{\prime}
denotes the projection of
xx^{\prime}
onto
\mathcal{X}_{0}
. In the following we write
\mathrm{Der}_{A}^{0}(\mathcal{X})
for the Lie subalgebra of the
\mathcal{A}
-derivations of
\mathcal{X}
which send
\mathcal{X}_{0}
to
\mathcal{X}_{0}
.

5.4.1
.

(Generalized Tits Construction)
Let
\mathcal{A},{\mathcal{B}}
be unital commutative associative algebras over
\mathbb{K}
and let
\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}
be unital algebras with normalized traces over
\mathcal{A}
and
{\mathcal{B}}
, respectively. Also let
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{X}),\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{Y})
be subalgebras of
\mathrm{Der}_{A}^{0}(\mathcal{X})
and
\mathrm{Der}_{B}^{0}(\mathcal{Y})
, respectively and assume there are
\mathcal{A}
and
{\mathcal{B}}
-bilinear skew-symmetric maps
(x,x^{\prime})\rightarrow D_{x,x^{\prime}}
and
(y,y^{\prime})\rightarrow D_{y,y^{\prime}}
of
\mathcal{X}_{0}\times\mathcal{X}_{0}
into
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{X})
and
\mathcal{Y}_{0}\times\mathcal{Y}_{0}
into
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{Y})
, respectively, satisfying

[D,D_{x,x^{\prime}}]=D_{Dx,x^{\prime}}+D_{x,Dx^{\prime}},\quad\quad[E,D_{y,y^{\prime}}]=D_{Ey,y^{\prime}}+D_{y,Ey^{\prime}}

for
D\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{X}),x,x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}_{0},E\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{Y}),y,y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Y}_{0}
. Set

\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{X}/\mathcal{A},\mathcal{Y}/{\mathcal{B}}):=(\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{X})\otimes{\mathcal{B}})\oplus(\mathcal{X}_{0}\otimes\mathcal{Y}_{0})\oplus(\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{Y}))

as a
\mathbb{K}
-vector space. Then
\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{X}/A,\mathcal{Y}/{\mathcal{B}})
is an algebra over
\mathbb{K}
with the anticommutative multiplication given by

\displaystyle[D\otimes b,D^{\prime}\otimes b^{\prime}]=[D,D^{\prime}]\otimes bb^{\prime},

\displaystyle[a\otimes E,a^{\prime}\otimes E^{\prime}]=aa^{\prime}\otimes[E,E^{\prime}],

\displaystyle[D\otimes b,a\otimes E]=0,

\displaystyle[D\otimes b,x\otimes y]=Dx\otimes by=-[x\otimes y,D\otimes b],

\displaystyle[a\otimes E,x\otimes y]=ax\otimes Ey=-[x\otimes y,a\otimes E],

\displaystyle[x\otimes y,x^{\prime}\otimes y\prime]=D_{x,x^{\prime}}\otimes T(yy^{\prime})+(x*x^{\prime})\otimes(y*y^{\prime})+T(xx^{\prime})\otimes D_{y,y^{\prime}}

for
D,D^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{X}),b,b^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{B}},a,a^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A},E,E^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{Y}),x,x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}_{0},y,y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Y}_{0}
. If
\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}
are chosen appropriately, then
\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{X}/\mathcal{A},\mathcal{Y}/{\mathcal{B}})
will be a Lie algebra, see
[
6
, Proposition 3.9]
.

5.4.1.
Type
\mathbf{B_{\ell}(\ell\geq 3)}

Assume that

\Delta=\{0\}\cup\{\pm\epsilon_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq\ell\}\cup\{\pm(\epsilon_{i}\pm\epsilon_{j})\mid 1\leq i<j\leq\ell\}

is the finite root system of type
B_{\ell}
with
\Delta_{sh}=\{\pm\epsilon_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq\ell\}
, and
\Delta_{lg}=\{\pm(\epsilon_{i}\pm\epsilon_{j})\mid 1\leq i<j\leq\ell\}
as short and long roots, respectively and let
Q=Q(\Delta)
be the root lattice of
\Delta
.

Let
\mathbb{V}
be a
(2\ell+1)
-dimensional vector space over
\mathbb{K}
with basis
v_{1},\ldots,v_{2\ell+1}
and let
f
be the symmetric bilinear form on
\mathbb{V}
defined by

f(v_{i},v_{j})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\hbox{if }1\leq i\leq\ell,j=\ell+i\\
1&\hbox{if }i=j=2\ell+1\\
0&\hbox{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

Now let
\mathfrak{g}
be the Lie algebra of endomorphisms of
\mathbb{V}
which are skew relative to

. Then
\mathfrak{g}
is the finite dimensional simple Lie algebra of type
B_{\ell}
with the Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}
consisting of elements of
\mathfrak{g}
which are diagonal relative to the basis
v_{1},\ldots,v_{2\ell+1}
. The set of weights of
\mathfrak{h}
-module

is

\{0\}\cup\Delta_{sh}=\{0\}\cup\{\pm\epsilon_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq\ell\}

and its weight spaces are

\mathbb{V}_{\alpha}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathbb{K}v_{i}&\hbox{if }\alpha=\epsilon_{i}\\
\mathbb{K}v_{\ell+i}&\hbox{if }\alpha=-\epsilon_{i}\\
\mathbb{K}v_{2\ell+1}&\hbox{if }\alpha=0.\end{array}\right.

Next let
\mathcal{X}=\mathrm{Cliff}(f)=\mathbb{K}1\oplus\mathbb{V}
be the Clifford Jordan algebra of
f
over
\mathbb{K}
, see Definition
4.3.1
. Then
\mathcal{X}
has a unique normalized trace
T
such that
\mathcal{X}_{0}=\mathbb{V}
and the product
*
on
\mathcal{X}_{0}
is 0. Now put
D_{v,v^{\prime}}=-[L_{v},L_{v^{\prime}}]
, for

.

Next assume that
{\mathcal{B}}=\mathcal{A}_{[n]}
is the ring of Laurent polynomials in
n

\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{A}_{[n]}^{(m-1)}

\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{m}\in{\Lambda:={\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}

\tau_{1}=0\quad\text{and}\quad\tau_{i}\not\equiv\tau_{j}(\hbox{mod}\;2{\mathbb{Z}}^{n})\quad\text{for}\quad i\neq j.

Define

(5.10)

g\left(\sum_{i=2}^{m}a_{i}w_{i},\sum_{i=2}^{m}b_{i}w_{i}\right)=\sum_{i=2}^{m}a_{i}b_{i}x^{\tau_{i}}.

Let

be the Clifford Jordan algebra of

4.3.1

\mathcal{Y}

*

\mathcal{Y}_{0}

Now consider

\mathcal{L}^{\lambda}=(\mathfrak{g}\otimes{\mathcal{B}}^{\lambda})\oplus(\mathbb{V}\otimes\mathcal{W}^{\lambda})\oplus D_{\mathcal{W},\mathcal{W}}^{\lambda}

Theorem 5.4.2
.

[
4
, Theorem 5.29]

Any centerless Lie
\Lambda
-torus of type
B_{\ell},\ell\geq 3
is graded-isomorphic to
\mathcal{T}(\mathrm{Cliff}(f)/\mathbb{K},\mathrm{Cliff}(g)/\mathcal{A}_{[n]})
where

is a nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form on a
(2\ell+1)
-dimensional vector space
\mathbb{V}
over
\mathbb{K}
and

Proposition 5.4.3
.

Assume that
\mathcal{L}
is a centerless
\Lambda
-torus of type
B_{\ell},\ell\geq 3
. Then
\mathcal{L}
admits a Chevalley involution.

Proof.
By Theorem
5.4.2
, it is enough to show the existence of a Chevalley involution for

\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{T}(\mathrm{Cliff}(f)/\mathbb{K},\mathrm{Cliff}(g)/\mathcal{A}_{[n]})=(\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{A}_{[n]})\oplus(\mathbb{V}\otimes\mathcal{W})\oplus D_{\mathcal{W},\mathcal{W}}

where
\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{A}_{[n]}^{(m-1)}
and
f:\mathbb{V}\times\mathbb{V}\rightarrow\mathbb{K}

Let

\overline{v_{i}}=v_{l+i},\;\;i=1,\ldots,\ell,\quad\quad\overline{v_{2\ell+1}}=v_{2\ell+1},

\mathcal{L}

\mathcal{L}_{0}^{0}=\mathfrak{h}\otimes 1\oplus D_{\mathcal{W},\mathcal{W}}^{0}

\bar{\tau}_{|_{\mathcal{L}_{0}^{0}}}=-\hbox{id}

\Box

6.
Chevalley involutions for fgc centerless tori

In this section, we discuss the existence of Chevalley involutions for fgc centerless Lie tori. We recall from Definition
2.3.2
that a centerless Lie torus which is finitely generated as a module over its centroid is called fgc. According to Theorem
2.3.4
(b), all centerless Lie tori of types different from
A_{\ell}
are fgc.
Moreover, according to Theorem
[
2
, Theorem 3.3.1]
(see Theorem
6.1.1
below), any fgc centerless Lie torus is a ”multi-loop algebra”.
Therefore, the existence of Chevalley involutions for centerless Lie tori amounts to the existence of Chevalley involutions for multi-loop algebras.

6.1.
Multi-loop Lie
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
-tori

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a finite dimensional simple Lie algebra and
\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{\nu}
, be commuting finite order automorphisms of
\mathfrak{g}
with periods

m_{1},\ldots,m_{\nu}
respectively. We set
\boldsymbol{\sigma}=(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{\nu})
.
Let
\omega_{i}
be a primitive
m_{i}^{th}
-root of
unity for
1\leq i\leq\nu
. Then

\mathfrak{g}=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\mathfrak{g}^{\bar{\lambda}},

where for
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{\nu})\in\Lambda:={\mathbb{Z}}^{\nu}
,

\bar{\lambda}:=(\bar{\lambda}_{1},\ldots,\bar{\lambda}_{\nu})
with

\bar{\lambda}_{j}:=\lambda_{j}+m_{j}{\mathbb{Z}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{m_{j}}
, and

\mathfrak{g}^{\bar{\lambda}}=\{x\in\mathfrak{g}\mid\sigma_{j}(x)=\omega^{\lambda_{j}}_{j}x\hbox{
for }1\leq j\leq\nu\}.

Let
\pi_{\bar{\lambda}}:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{\bar{\lambda}}
denote the projection onto
\mathfrak{g}^{\bar{\lambda}}
.
Let
\mathcal{A}={\mathbb{K}}[z^{\pm 1}_{1},\ldots,z^{\pm 1}_{\nu}]
be the algebra of Laurent polynomials in
\nu
-variables equipped with the natural
\Lambda
-grading
\mathcal{A}=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}{\mathbb{K}}z^{\lambda}
,
z^{\lambda}=z_{1}^{\lambda_{1}},\ldots,z_{\nu}^{\lambda_{\nu}}
,
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{\nu}).

(i) The subalgebra

(6.1)

M(\mathfrak{g},\boldsymbol{\sigma}):=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\pi_{\bar{\lambda}}(\mathfrak{g})\otimes z^{\lambda}=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\mathfrak{g}^{\bar{\lambda}}\otimes z^{\lambda}

of
L(\mathfrak{g},\mathcal{A}):=\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{A}
is called the
\nu
-step multi-loop
algebra
based on
\boldsymbol{\sigma}
and
\mathfrak{g}
.

(ii) It is known that
\mathfrak{g}^{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}
contains a subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}
which is
\mathfrak{g}
-ad-diagonalizable, see
[
2
, Remark 3.2.2(ii)]
. Let
\Delta^{\prime}
be the set of weights of
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to
\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}
. Set

\hbox{LT}(\mathfrak{g},\boldsymbol{\sigma},\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}):=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda,\alpha^{\prime}\in\Delta^{\prime}}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{\bar{\lambda}}\otimes z^{\lambda},

where
\mathfrak{g}^{\bar{\lambda}}_{\alpha^{\prime}}
is the intersection of
\mathfrak{g}^{\bar{\lambda}}
and the
\alpha^{\prime}
-weight space of
\mathfrak{g}
, with respect to
\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}
. Then
\hbox{LT}(\sigma,\boldsymbol{\sigma},\mathfrak{h}^{\prime})
is a subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{A}

with the compatible
(\langle\Delta^{\prime}\rangle\times\Lambda)
-grading given above.
We note that as
\Lambda
-graded Lie algebras,
\hbox{LT}(\mathfrak{g},\boldsymbol{\sigma},\mathfrak{h}^{\prime})=M(\mathfrak{g},\boldsymbol{\sigma})
.

Theorem 6.1.1
.

[
2
, Theorem 3.3.1]

Let
E(\mathcal{L},D,\kappa)
be a fgc extended affine Lie algebra of nullity
\nu\geq 1
, where

\mathcal{L}
is a centerless Lie torus. Then
\mathcal{L}
is bi-isomorphic to a multi-loop Lie algebra

LT(\mathfrak{g},\boldsymbol{\sigma},\mathfrak{h}^{\prime})=M(\mathfrak{g},\boldsymbol{\sigma})
for some
\mathfrak{g},\boldsymbol{\sigma},\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}
, with

\mathcal{L}^{0}_{0}\cong\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}\otimes 1
.

6.2.
Chevalley involutions for fgc multi-loop algebras

We discuss Chevalley involutions for fgc centerless Lie tori.
In what follows for
\boldsymbol{\sigma}=(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{\nu})
we write
\boldsymbol{\sigma}\tau=\tau\boldsymbol{\sigma}
to indicate that an automorphism
\tau
commutes with all
\sigma_{i}
.

Proposition 6.2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{L}=\hbox{LT}(\mathfrak{g},\boldsymbol{\sigma},\mathfrak{h}^{\prime})=M(\mathfrak{g},\boldsymbol{\sigma})
be a fgc centerless Lie torus. Let
\tau
be a Chevalley involution for
\mathfrak{g}
such that
\tau\boldsymbol{\sigma}=\boldsymbol{\sigma}\tau
and
\tau(h^{\prime})=-h^{\prime}
for
h^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}
.
Assume that
\mathfrak{g}
has an automorphism
\psi
satisfying:

-
\psi(\mathfrak{g}^{\bar{\lambda}})\subseteq\mathfrak{g}^{-\bar{\lambda}}
, for each
\lambda
,

-
\psi_{|_{\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}}}=\hbox{id}_{|_{\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}}}
.

Then the assignment
\bar{\tau}_{\psi}:x\otimes a^{\lambda}\longmapsto\psi\tau(x)\otimes a^{-\lambda},

x\in\mathfrak{g}
,
\lambda\in\Lambda

defines a pre-Chevalley involution
\bar{\tau}_{\psi}
of
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{A}
which restricts to a Chevalley involution for

\mathcal{L}
.

Proof.
Let
\psi
and
\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}
be as in the statement. We have

\mathcal{L}^{0}_{0}=\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}\otimes 1.

Clearly,
\bar{\tau}_{\psi}
is a pre-Chevalley involution on
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{A}
.
Since
\tau
commutes with
\boldsymbol{\sigma}
, we have
\tau(\pi_{\lambda}(\mathfrak{g}))=\pi_{\lambda}(\mathfrak{g})
, for
\lambda\in\Lambda
. This together with
\psi(\mathfrak{g}^{\lambda})\subseteq\mathfrak{g}^{-\lambda}
, gives

\bar{\tau}_{\psi}(\pi_{\lambda}(\mathfrak{g})\otimes a^{\lambda})=\psi\tau(\pi_{\lambda}(\mathfrak{g}))\otimes a^{-\lambda}=\psi(\pi_{\lambda}(\mathfrak{g}))\otimes a^{-\lambda}\subseteq\pi_{-\lambda}(\mathfrak{g})\otimes a^{-\lambda}.

Thus
\bar{\tau}_{\psi}
restricts to an automorphism of
\mathcal{L}
, satisfying

\bar{\tau}_{\psi}(\mathcal{L}^{\lambda})\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{-\lambda}.

Moreover, for
h^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}
,

\bar{\tau}_{\psi}(h^{\prime}\otimes 1)=\psi(\tau(h^{\prime}))\otimes 1=\psi(-h^{\prime})\otimes 1=-h^{\prime}\otimes 1.

Thus
{\bar{\tau}_{\psi}}
is a Chevalley involution for
\mathcal{L}
.
\Box

Corollary 6.2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{L}=\hbox{LT}(\mathfrak{g},\boldsymbol{\sigma},\mathfrak{h}^{\prime})=M(\mathfrak{g},\boldsymbol{\sigma})
be a fgc centerless Lie torus, where all
\sigma_{i}
’s are graph automorphisms.
Assume that
\mathfrak{g}
has an automorphism
\psi
satisfying:

-
\psi(\mathfrak{g}^{\bar{\lambda}})\subseteq\mathfrak{g}^{-\bar{\lambda}}
, for each
\lambda
,

-
\psi_{|_{\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}}}=\hbox{id}_{|_{\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}}}
.

Then
\mathcal{L}
is equipped with a Chevalley involution.
In particular, if each
\sigma_{i}
has order
1
, or
2
, then the statement holds with
\psi=\hbox{id}
.

Proof.
We consider a Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}
of
\mathfrak{g}
containing
\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}
, and a Chevalley involution

\tau
for
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
. Since Chevalley involutions commute with graph automorphisms, we have

\tau\boldsymbol{\sigma}=\boldsymbol{\sigma}\tau
. Thus the first part of the statement follows from Proposition
6.2.1
. If each
\sigma_{i}
has order
1
or
2
, then
\bar{\lambda}=-\bar{\lambda}
, for each
\lambda
, and so the second part of the statement is clear.
\Box

Remark 6.2.3
.

\psi
in Corollary

has order

or

of

Next, we consider the Chevalley involution

\tau:y\leftrightarrow-z
,
h^{\prime}\rightarrow-h^{\prime}
for
\mathfrak{g}
. We have
\tau\sigma=\sigma\tau
. Then
Proportion
6.2.1
, applies with
\psi=\hbox{id}.

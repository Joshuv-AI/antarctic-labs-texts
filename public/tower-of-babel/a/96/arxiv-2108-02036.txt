It is well-known
[

into the analytic function

[

,

,
24
,

.

As a rule, an analytic function of a matrix can be calculated only approximately.
The usual way to approximately calculate an analytic function
f
of a matrix
A
is based on replacing
f
with a polynomial or a rational function. The approximation by a rational function possesses some additional capabilities compared to a polynomial one: it can be more accurate and can approximate an analytic function on an unbounded set. In this paper, we propose estimates (Theorem
11
and its corollaries) of

is a rational function that interpolates

. Similar estimates for polynomial approximation were described in
[
27
,
30
]
.

As an application of these estimates, we consider the estimate of the accuracy of approximation of the impulse response of a dynamical system (
9
) using the Arnoldi reduced-order method (Theorems

and
24
). The rational (Sections
6
and
7

\exp_{t}(\lambda)=e^{\lambda t}
by a rational function

r_{t}
is not calculated explicitly.

In Section
8
we illustrate (Examples

and

) our estimates of the Arnoldi reduced-order approximation using the properties of the numerical range.
In Section

In Sections

3.
Rational interpolation

A
rational function
is a function
r
of a complex variable that can be represented in the form

r(z)=\frac{u(z)}{v(z)}=\frac{a_{0}+a_{1}z+\dots+a_{L}z^{L}}{b_{0}+b_{1}z+\dots+b_{M}z^{M}},

where
u
and
v
are polynomials. We call the pair
[L/M]
the
degree
of
r
.

Let
z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{m}\in\mathbb{C}
be given distinct points called
points
(or
nodes
)
of interpolation
and
n_{1},n_{2},\dots,n_{m}\in\mathbb{N}
be their
multiplicities
. Let
f
be an analytic function defined on a neighbourhood of the points of interpolation. The problem of
rational interpolation
is
[
4
,
36
]
the problem of finding a rational function
r
of degree
[L/M]
or less satisfying the conditions

r^{(j)}(z_{k})=f^{(j)}(z_{k}),\qquad k=1,\dots,m;\;j=0,1,\dots,n_{k}-1.

(5)

Thus (
5
) consists of

N=\sum_{k=1}^{m}n_{k}

conditions. Usually it is assumed that
L+M\leq N-1
. It is also often assumed that the denominator
v
is given. In the latter case, it is reasonable to assume that
L\leq N-1
. If
v(z)\equiv 1
, the problem of the rational interpolation is reduced to the
polynomial
one.

Proposition 7
.

Let the points of interpolation
z_{1}
,
z_{2}
, …,
z_{m}
have multiplicities
n_{1}

n_{2}
, …,
n_{m}
. Let

, and

be analytic functions defined on a neighborhood of the points
z_{1}
,
z_{2}
, …,
z_{m}
;

,

. Then the interpolation conditions

\Bigl(\frac{u}{v}\Bigr)^{(j)}(z_{k})=f^{(j)}(z_{k}),\qquad k=1,\dots,m;\;j=0,1,\dots,n_{k}-1,

(6)

are equivalent to the interpolation conditions

u^{(j)}(z_{k})=(vf)^{(j)}(z_{k}),\qquad k=1,\dots,m;\;j=0,1,\dots,n_{k}-1.

(7)

Proof.

Let conditions (
6
) be satisfied. Then for all
m=0,1,\dots,n_{k}-1
we have (the argument
z_{k}
is omitted for brevity)

u^{(m)}=\Bigl(\frac{u}{v}\cdot v\Bigr)^{(m)}=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\binom{m}{j}\Bigl(\dfrac{u}{v}\Bigr)^{(j)}v^{(m-j)}=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\binom{m}{j}(f)^{(j)}v^{(m-j)}=(vf)^{(m)}.

Conversely, let conditions (
7
) be satisfied. Then for all

we have (the argument

is again omitted for brevity)

\displaystyle\Bigl(\frac{u}{v}\Bigr)^{(m)}

\displaystyle=(uv^{-1})^{(m)}=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\binom{m}{j}u^{(j)}(v^{-1})^{(m-j)}=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\binom{m}{j}(vf)^{(j)}(v^{-1})^{(m-j)}

\displaystyle=\bigl[(vf)v^{-1}\bigr]^{(m)}=f^{(m)}.\qed

4.
The estimate

Theorem 10
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
;

z_{1}
,
z_{2}
, …,

(

+(t_{1}-t_{2})z_{2}\mathbf{1}+\dots+(t_{N-1}-t_{N})z_{N}\mathbf{1}+t_{N}A\bigr)\,dt_{N}dt_{N-1}\dots dt_{1},

Proof.

By Proposition

f(z)-r(z)=\frac{\Omega(z)}{v(z)}\,\bigl(vf\bigr)[z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{N},z].

On the other hand, by Proposition

Or

Remark 1

Proof.

\begin{split}\bigl|\xi\bigl[f(A)&-r(A)\bigr]\bigr|=\biggl|\xi\biggl(\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\dots\int_{0}^{t_{N-1}}\Omega(A)[v(A)]^{-1}\bigl(vf\bigr)^{{(N)}}\bigl((1-t_{1})z_{1}\mathbf{1}+\dots\\

(8)

Proof.

First, we notice that under the assumptions of the proposition
\lVert V\varphi\rVert=\lVert\varphi\rVert
for any

Proposition 27
.

For any matrix
A\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
one has

\mu(A)=\max\{\,\Real\lambda:\,\lambda\in w(A)\,\}.

Proof.

Indeed,

\max\{\,\Real\lambda:\,\lambda\in w(A)\,\}=\max\{\,\Real\langle Az,z\rangle:\,\lVert z\rVert_{2}=1\,\}=\\
=\max\biggl\{\,\Real\Bigl(\Bigl\langle\frac{A+A^{H}}{2}z,z\Bigr\rangle+\Bigl\langle\frac{A-A^{H}}{2}z,z\Bigr\rangle\Bigr):\,\lVert z\rVert_{2}=1\,\biggr\}=\\
=\max\biggl\{\,\Real\Bigl\langle\frac{A+A^{H}}{2}z,z\Bigr\rangle:\,\lVert z\rVert_{2}=1\,\biggr\}=\\
=\max\biggl\{\,\Bigl\langle\frac{A+A^{H}}{2}z,z\Bigr\rangle:\,\lVert z\rVert_{2}=1\,\biggr\}=\mu(A).\qed

Remark 2
.

We recall
[
18
, p. 137]
the algorithm for approximate calculation (more precisely, estimation from without) of the numerical range
w(A)
of
A
. We denote by
q_{\max}(A)
the largest eigenvalue of the (self-adjoint) matrix
\frac{A+A^{H}}{2}
, and we denote by
q_{\min}(A)
the smallest eigenvalue of the matrix
\frac{A+A^{H}}{2}
.
We recall that
q_{\min}(A)
and
q_{\max}(A)
can be calculated by standard tools
[
37
]
.
By definition,

\mu(A)=q_{\max}(A).

Hence, by Proposition
27
,

q_{\max}(A)=\max\{\,\Real\lambda:\,\lambda\in w(A)\,\}.

Therefore,

w(A)\subseteq\{\,\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:\,\Real\lambda\leq q_{\max}(A)\,\}.

Applying this inclusion to
-A
we arrive at

w(A)\subseteq\{\,\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:\,q_{\min}(A)\leq\Real\lambda\leq q_{\max}(A)\,\}.

Further, we take an arbitrary
\varphi\in\mathbb{R}
and consider the matrix
A_{\varphi}=e^{-i\varphi}A
. By Proposition
25
(d),

w(A)=e^{i\varphi}w(A_{\varphi}).

Therefore,

w(A)\subseteq e^{i\varphi}\{\,\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:\,q_{\min}(A_{\varphi})\leq\Real\lambda\leq q_{\max}(A_{\varphi})\,\}.

Taking several
\varphi
, we construct the intersection of the corresponding strips that contain
w(A)
. In fact, already two angles, 0 and
-\pi/2
, give a rectangle that contains
w(A)
.

The following theorem shows that an analytic function of a matrix can be effectively estimated via the values of the function on the numerical range.

Theorem 28

(see
[
2
,
5
,
10
]
and references therein)
.

Let a function
f
be defined and analytic in a neighborhood of the numerical range

of a square matrix

. Then

\lVert f(A)\rVert\leq C\,\max_{\lambda\in w(A)}|f(\lambda)|,

where
C=11.08
. If the neighborhood is an ellipse, then
C=3.16
. If the neighborhood is an disc, then

.

Finite-time input-to-state stability for infinite-dimensional systems
Xiaorong Sun, Jun Zheng, Guchuan Zhu

Abstract
In this paper, we extend the notion of finite-time input-to-state stability (FTISS) for finite-dimensional systems to infinite-dimensional systems. More specifically, we first prove an FTISS Lyapunov theorem for a class of infinite-dimensional systems, namely, the existence of an FTISS Lyapunov functional (FTISS-LF) implies the FTISS of the system, and then, provide a sufficient condition for ensuring the existence of an FTISS-LF for a class of abstract infinite-dimensional systems under the framework of compact semigroup theory and Hilbert spaces. As an application of the FTISS Lyapunov theorem, we verify the FTISS for a class of parabolic PDEs involving sublinear terms and distributed in-domain disturbances. Since the nonlinear terms of the corresponding abstract system are not Lipschitz continuous, the well-posedness is proved based on the application of compact semigroup theory and the FTISS is assessed by using the Lyapunov method with the aid of an interpolation inequality. Numerical simulations are conducted to confirm the theoretical results.

Introduction

Originally introduced by Sontag in 1989

The ISS theory becomes rapidly one of the pillars in the nonlinear and robust control

Roughly speaking, if a system is ISS, then it is asymptotically stable in the absence of external inputs while keeping certain robust properties, such as “bounded-input-bounded-state”, in the presence of external inputs. Especially, the state of the system should be eventually small when the inputs are small.

Extending the ISS theory of finite-dimensional systems to infinite-dimensional systems started around 2010

for the input-to-state stabilization of PDEs under backstepping control, and

It is worth mentioning that in

More specifically, taking into account the properties of FTS and ISS, the FTISS of a system requires that in the absence of external inputs the state of the system should reach equilibrium within a finite time, while in the presence of external inputs the state can reach a given bounded region in finite time

Therefore, the notion of FTISS provides a refined characterizations for the robust stability of finite-dimensional systems, which plays a key role in the study of finite-time stability and stabilization of finite-dimensional nonlinear systems (see

The first attempt to extend the concept of FTISS to infinite-dimensional systems is due to
[
50
]
, where, as a special case of FTISS, the notion of prescribed-time input-to-state stability (PTISS) was extended to infinite-dimensional systems. Moreover, a PTISS Lyapunov theorem was proved and a sufficient condition for the existence of a PTISS Lyapunov functional was provided for a class of infinite-dimensional systems under the framework of Hilbert spaces
[
50
]
. Unlike the FTISS, for which the settling time may depend on the initial data and be unknown in advance, the PTISS indicates that the system can be stabilized within a prescribed finite time, regardless of its initial data. Especially, as addressing the PTISS of finite-dimensional systems
[
51
]
, by introducing a monotonically increasing function
\vartheta(t):=\frac{T}{T-t}
with a prescribed finite time
T
to the structural conditions of FTISS-LFs, it is straightforward to prove the PTISS for a class of infinite-dimensional systems or parabolic PDEs with time-varying reaction coefficients having a form of
\vartheta(t)
by using the Lyapunov method
[
50
]
.

It is worth noting that for infinite-dimensional systems, studying the FTISS in a generic case where the stabilization time is unknown in advance and may be dependent of initial data, i.e., the FTISS without prescribing the settling time, is indeed more challenging compared to the case of PTISS, and no relevant results have yet been reported in the existing literature.
The main obstacle in addressing the FTISS for infinite-dimensional systems may lie in verifying sufficient conditions for the existence of an FTISS-LF. In particular, for specific PDEs, it is difficult to validate the effectiveness of a Lyapunov candidate in the FTISS analysis due to the fact that sublinear terms are usually involved and cannot be easily handled. In addition, compared to the PTISS analysis of infinite-dimensional systems, for which strongly continuous semigroup (
C_{0}
-semigroup) generated by bounded linear operators is often used to ensure the well-posedness, as shown in Section
2.3
and
3
of this paper, even for parabolic PDEs, additional properties of
C_{0}
-semigroup are needed for proving the well-posedness of the corresponding abstract systems due to the appearance of non-Lipschitz continuous terms when the FTISS is considered. This also represents a challenge.

The aim of this work is to study the FTISS for infinite-dimensional systems without prescribing the settling time and provide tools for establishing the FTISS for certain nonlinear infinite-dimensional systems. In particular, as a first attempt in addressing the FTISS for PDEs, we show how to verify the well-posedness based on the application of the compact semigroup theory and to use the interpolation inequality to overcome the difficulties in verifying the structural conditions of Lyapunov functionals for a class of parabolic PDEs with sublinear terms and distributed in-domain disturbances. Overall, the main contribution of this work include:

(i)

extending the notion of FTISS for finite-dimensional systems to infinite-dimensional systems and proving a Lyapunov theorem, which states that the existence of an FTISS-LF implies the FTISS of the system;

(ii)

providing a sufficient condition to guarantee the existence of an FTISS-LF for certain nonlinear infinite-dimensional systems under the framework of compact semigroup theory and Hilbert spaces, thereby providing tools for stability analysis of infinite-dimensional systems;

(iii)

proving an interpolation inequality, which paves the way to assess the FTISS for PDEs, and verifying the sufficient condition for the existence of an FTISS-LF for a class of parabolic PDEs with sublinear terms and distributed in-domain disturbances.

2
FTISS for infinite-dimensional systems

In this section, we present the notion of FTISS and an FTISS Lyapunov theorem for a class of infinite-dimensional systems, which can be generated by PDEs, abstract differential equations in Banach spaces, time-delay systems, etc.

2.1
The notion of FTISS for infinite-dimensional systems

We first recall the notion of a control system, defined below, which comprises ODE and PDE control systems as special cases.

Definition 1
.

[
3
, Definition 6.1, p. 239]

Let the triple
\Sigma=\left(X,U_{c},\phi\right)
consist of the Banach spaces
\left(X,\|\cdot\|_{X}\right)
and
\left(U,\|\cdot\|_{U}\right)
and a normed vector space of inputs
U_{c}\subset\left\{u:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\rightarrow U\right\}
. We assume that the following two axioms hold true:

(i)

for all
u\in U_{c}
and all
\tau\geq 0
the time shift
u(\cdot+\tau)
belongs to
U_{c}
with
\|u\|_{U_{c}}\geq\|u(\cdot+\tau)\|_{U_{c}}
;

(ii)

for all
u_{1},u_{2}\in U_{c}
and for all
t>0
the concatenation of
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
at time
t
, defined by

\displaystyle u_{1}\underset{t}{\diamond}u_{2}(\tau)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{1}(\tau),&\text{if}\tau\in[0,t]\\
u_{2}(\tau-t),&\text{otherwise}\end{array}\right.

belongs to
U_{c}
.

Consider a transition map
\phi:{D_{\phi}}\rightarrow X
with
D_{\phi}\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times X\times U_{c}
. The triple
\Sigma
is called a control system, if it verifies the following properties:

(i)

identity property: for every
(x,u)\in X\times U_{c}
, it holds that
\phi(0,x,u)=x
;

(ii)

causality: for every
(t,x,u)\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times X\times U_{c}
and
\widetilde{u}\in U_{c}
satisfying
\widetilde{u}(\tau)=u(\tau)
for
\tau\in[0,t]
, it holds that
\phi\left(t,x,\widetilde{u}\right)=\phi(t,x,u)
;

(iii)

continuity: for every
(x,u)\in X\times U_{c}
, the mapping
t\mapsto\phi(t,x,u)
is continuous;

(iv)

cocycle property: for every
t,h\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
and every
(x,u)\in X\times U_{c}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\phi\left(h,\phi(t,x,u),u(t+\cdot)\right)=\phi\left(t+h,x,u\right).

The following definition is concerned with the forward complete control systems considered in this paper.

Definition 2
.

[
52
]

The control system
\Sigma=\left(X,U_{c},\phi\right)
is said to be forward-complete if for any
(t,x,u)\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times X\times U_{c}
, the value
\phi(t,x,u)\in X
is well-defined.

The following definition is concerned with the generalized class-
\mathcal{KL}
function (
\mathcal{GKL}
-function) used in this paper. Note that different from the definition adopted in
[
44
]
, the
\mathcal{GKL}
-function is defined in the same way as in
[
45
]
.

Definition 3
.

[
45
]

A continuous mapping
\beta
:
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is called a
\mathcal{GKL}
-function, if it satisfies the following conditions:

(i)

the mapping
s\mapsto\beta(s,0)
is a
\mathcal{K}
-function;

(ii)

for each fixed
s\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
the mapping
t\mapsto\beta(s,t)
is continuous, decreases to zero and there exists a nonnegative and continuous function
T(s)
such that
\beta(s,t)=0
for all
t\geq T(s)
.

Now, in accordance with the notion of FTISS defined in
[
45

for finite-dimensional systems, we provide the definition of FTISS for the system
\Sigma

Definition 4
.

The control system
\Sigma=\left(X,U_{c},\phi\right)
is said to be finite-time input-to-state stable (FTISS), if there exist functions
\beta\in\mathcal{GKL}
and
\gamma\in\mathcal{K}
such that

\displaystyle\|\phi(t,x,u)\|_{X}\leq\beta\left(\|x\|_{X},t\right)+\gamma\left(\|u\|_{U_{c}}\right),\forall(t,x,u)\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times X\times U_{c}.

2.2
The FTISS Lyapunov theorem for infinite-dimensional systems

For a real-valued function
\Psi:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, the right-hand upper Dini derivative at
t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is given by

\displaystyle D^{+}\Psi(t)=\mathop{\lim\sup}\limits_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{\Psi(t+s)-\Psi(t)}{s}.

Let
x\in X
and
V
be a real-valued function defined in a neighborhood of
x
, the Lie derivative of
V
at
x
corresponding to the input
u\in U_{c}
along the trajectory of the system
\Sigma
is defined by

\displaystyle\dot{V}_{u}(x)=

\displaystyle\left.D^{+}V\left(\phi(\cdot,x,u)\right)\right|_{t=0}=\mathop{\lim\sup}\limits_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{V\left(\phi(t,x,u)\right)-V(x)}{t}.

If it is clear from the context what the input for computing the Lie derivative
\dot{V}_{u}(x)
is, then we simply write
\dot{V}(x)
.

We define the FTISS Lyapunov functional for the control system
\Sigma
.

Definition 5
.

A continuous function
V:X\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is called an FTISS Lyapunov functional (FTISS-LF) for the system
\Sigma
, if there exist constants
M\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and
{\sigma_{0}}\in(0,1)
and functions
\alpha_{1}
,
\alpha_{2}\in\mathcal{K_{\infty}}
, and
\chi\in\mathcal{K}
such that

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}\left(\|x\|_{X}\right)\leq V(x)\leq\alpha_{2}\left(\|x\|_{X}\right),\forall x\in X,

(1)

and for any
u\in U_{c}
, the Lie derivative of
V
at
x
with respect to (w.r.t.) the input
u\in U_{c}
along the trajectory satisfies

\displaystyle\|x\|_{X}\geq\chi\left(\|u\|_{U_{c}}\right)\Rightarrow\dot{V}_{u}(x)\leq-MV^{{\sigma_{0}}}(x).

(2)

Throughout this paper, we always impose the following assumptions:

(H1)

U_{c}:=BC\left(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0};U\right)
;

(H2)

\phi=0
is the unique equilibrium point of the control system
\Sigma
;

(H3)

The system
\Sigma=\left(X,U_{c},\phi\right)
is forward-complete.

The following Lyapunov theorem is the first main result of this paper.

Theorem 1

(FTISS Lyapunov theorem)
.

If the control system
\Sigma=\left(X,U_{c},\phi\right)
admits an FTISS-LF, then it is FTISS.

Proof.

Let
V(x)
be an FTISS-LF of the control system
\Sigma
with
\alpha_{1}
,
\alpha_{2}
,
\chi
,
M
, and
{\sigma_{0}}
being the same as in Definition
5
. Take an arbitrary control
u\in U_{c}
and consider the set

\displaystyle\Omega:=\left\{x\in X\left|\right.~V(x)\leq\alpha_{2}\circ\chi\left(\|u\|_{U_{c}}\right)\right\}.

We claim that the set
\Omega
is invariant, namely, as long as
x_{0}\in\Omega
, there must be

\displaystyle x(t):=\phi(t,x_{0},u)\in\Omega,\forall t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}.

First, note that
0\in\Omega
.

If
u\equiv 0
, it follows from
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in\mathcal{K_{\infty}}
,
\chi\in\mathcal{K}
, and (
1
) that

\displaystyle\Omega=

\displaystyle\left\{x\in X\left|\right.~V(x)\leq\alpha_{2}\circ\chi(0)\right\}=\left\{x\in X\left|\right.~V(x)\leq 0\right\}\subset\left\{x\in X\left|\right.~\alpha_{1}\left(\|x\|_{X}\right)\leq 0\right\}=\{0\}.

Therefore,
\Omega=\{0\}
. Since
x=0
is an equilibrium point,
\Omega
is invariant.

If
u\not\equiv 0
, suppose that
\Omega
is not invariant, then, due to continuity of
x
, there exists
t*\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
such that

\displaystyle V(x(t*))=\alpha_{2}\circ\chi\left(\|u\|_{U_{c}}\right),

which, along with (
1
), leads to

\displaystyle\|x(t*)\|_{X}\geq\chi\left(\|u\|_{U_{c}}\right).

Denote by
\hat{u}
the input to the system after
t*
, i.e.,
\hat{u}(\tau)=u(\tau+t*)
for all
\tau\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
. It follows from (
2
) that

\displaystyle\dot{V}_{\hat{u}}(x)\leq

\displaystyle-MV^{{\sigma_{0}}}(x)\leq-M\left(\alpha_{1}\left(\|x\|_{X}\right)\right)^{{\sigma_{0}}}<0,\forall t>t*.

Therefore, the trajectory cannot escape from the set
\Omega
. This is a contradiction. We conclude that
\Omega
is invariant.

Now we consider
x_{0}\notin\Omega
and let

\displaystyle\tilde{t}:=\inf\left\{t\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}|~V(x(t))\leq\alpha_{2}\circ\chi\left(\|u\|_{U_{c}}\right)\right\}.

In view of (
2
), we have

\displaystyle\dot{V}_{u}(x)\leq-MV^{{\sigma_{0}}}(x),\forall t\in\left(0,\tilde{t}\right).

(3)

It is clear that
V(x)\equiv 0
is the solution to (
3
). However,
V(x)\equiv 0
implies that
x(t)\equiv 0
for all
t\in\left(0,\tilde{t}\right)
. By virtue of the continuity of
x
, we have
x_{0}=0\in\Omega
, which leads to a contradiction. Then, we get
V(x)\not\equiv 0
.

Let
T_{0}:=\frac{{V^{1-{\sigma_{0}}}(x_{0})}}{M(1-{\sigma_{0}})}
. If
T_{0}\in\left(0,\tilde{t}\right)
, we deduce from (
3
) that

\displaystyle V(x(t))\leq

\displaystyle\left(V^{1-{\sigma_{0}}}(x_{0})-M(1-\sigma_{0})t\right)^{\frac{1}{1-{\sigma_{0}}}},\forall t\in\left(0,T_{0}\right),

\displaystyle V(x(t))=

\displaystyle 0,\forall t\in\left[T_{0},\tilde{t}\right).

If
T_{0}\geq\tilde{t}
, we have

\displaystyle V(x(t))\leq\left(V^{1-{\sigma_{0}}}(x_{0})-M(1-\sigma_{0})t\right)^{\frac{1}{1-{\sigma_{0}}}},\forall t\in\left(0,\tilde{t}\right).

Define
T(s):=\frac{s^{1-\sigma_{0}}}{M(1-\sigma_{0})}
for
s\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
. Define the following
\mathcal{GKL}
-function:

\displaystyle\beta_{1}(s,t):=\left\{\!\begin{array}[]{ll}\left(s^{1-{\sigma_{0}}}-M(1-{\sigma_{0}})t\right)^{\frac{1}{1-{\sigma_{0}}}}\!,&\!s\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},t\in\left[0,T(s)\right),\\
\!0,&\!s\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},t\in\left[T(s),+\infty\right).\end{array}\right.

Then, for
T_{0}\in\left(0,\tilde{t}\right)
or
T_{0}\geq\tilde{t}
, we always have

\displaystyle V(x(t))\leq\beta_{1}\left(V(x_{0}),t\right),\forall t\in\left(0,\tilde{t}\right),

which implies that

\displaystyle\|x\|_{X}\leq\beta\left(\|x_{0}\|_{X},t\right),\forall t\in\left(0,\tilde{t}\right)

(4)

with

\displaystyle\beta(s,t):=\alpha_{1}^{-1}\circ\beta_{1}\left(\alpha_{2}(s),t\right),\forall s,t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}.

It is clear that
\beta
is a
\mathcal{GKL}
-function.

By the definition of
\tilde{t}
, we have

V\left(x\left(\tilde{t}\right)\right)=\alpha_{2}\circ\chi\left(\|u\|_{U_{c}}\right)
. Therefore,
x\left(\tilde{t}\right)\in\Omega
.

Note that
\Omega
is invariant, we deduce that

\displaystyle\|x\|_{X}\leq\rho\left(\|u\|_{U_{c}}\right),\forall t\in\left[\tilde{t},+\infty\right),

(5)

where
\rho:={\alpha_{1}}^{-1}\circ{\alpha_{2}}\circ\chi\in\mathcal{K}
.

By (
4
) and (
5
), we have

\displaystyle\|x\|_{X}\leq\beta\left(\|x_{0}\|_{X},t\right)+\rho\left(\|u\|_{U_{c}}\right),\forall t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}.

We conclude that the system
\Sigma
is FTISS.
∎

2.3
Constructing FTISS-LFs for a class of infinite-dimensional systems

In this section, we provide a sufficient condition for ensuring the existence of an FTISS-LF for a class of infinite-dimensional systems under the framework of compact semigroup theory and Hilbert spaces. More precisely, letting
X
be a Hilbert space with scalar product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{X}
and norm
\|\cdot\|_{X}:=\sqrt{\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{X}}
and
U\subset X
be a normed linear space, we consider the following system

\displaystyle\dot{x}=Ax+\Xi(t,x,u),t\in\mathbb{R}_{>0},

(6a)

\displaystyle x(0)=x_{0},

(6b)

where
x\in X
is the state,
A:D(A)\rightarrow X
is a linear operator,
\Xi:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times X\times U\rightarrow X
is a nonlinear functional,
u\in U_{c}=C\left(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0};U\right)
, and
x_{0}\in X
denotes the initial datum.
Moreover, we always impose the following conditions:

(H4)

the operator

is the infinitesimal generator of a compact

-semigroup

on
X
for

;

(H5)

Recall that an operator

is said to be positive, if it is self-adjoint and satisfies

\displaystyle\langle Px,x\rangle_{X}>0,\forall x\in X\setminus\{0\}.

An operator

is called coercive, if there exists
\mu_{1}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
such that

\displaystyle\langle Px,x\rangle_{X}\geq\mu_{1}\|x\|^{2}_{X},\forall x\in X.

(7)

).

Theorem 2
.

Let the conditions
(H4)
and
(H5)
be fulfilled. Assume that there exist a coercive and positive operator

, a function
\zeta\in\mathcal{K}
, and constants

and
\tau\in\left(1,2\right)

\displaystyle\langle(PA+A^{*}P)x,x\rangle_{X}+2\langle\Xi(t,x,u),Px\rangle_{X}\leq-b\|x\|^{\tau}_{X}+c\|x\|_{X}\zeta\left(\|u\|_{U_{c}}\right),

(8)

holds true for all
t\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
,
x\in D(A)\subset X
, and
u\in U_{c}
,
where
A^{*}
denotes the adjoint operator of

6
) admits a mild solution

, which is defined by

\displaystyle x(t)=S(t)x_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)\Xi(s,x(s),u(s))\mathrm{d}s,\forall t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}.

(9)

Moreover,
the functional
V(x):=\langle Px,x\rangle_{X}
is an FTISS-LF and hence, system (
6
) is FTISS.

Proof.

Note that under the assumptions
(H4)
and
(H5)
,
[
53
, Corollary 2.3, p.194]
ensures that, for every
x_{0}\in X
and
u\in U_{c}
, system (
6
) admits a mild solution
x\in C\left(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0};X\right)

Now, for the mild solution
x
, we prove that the functional
V(x):=\langle Px,x\rangle_{X}
is an FTISS-LF.

Since
P\in L(X)
is coercive, there exists
\mu_{1}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
such that

\mu_{1}\|x\|^{2}_{X}\leq V(x)\leq\mu_{2}\|x\|^{2}_{X},\forall x\in X,

(10)

where
\mu_{2}:=\|P\|
.

By direct calculation, we have

\displaystyle\dot{V}_{u}(x)=

\displaystyle\langle P\dot{x},x\rangle_{X}+\langle Px,\dot{x}\rangle_{X}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle P(Ax+\Xi(t,x,u)),x\rangle_{X}+\langle Px,Ax+\Xi(t,x,u)\rangle_{X}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle PAx,x\rangle_{X}+\langle P\Xi(t,x,u),x\rangle_{X}+\langle Px,Ax\rangle_{X}+\langle Px,\Xi(t,x,u)\rangle_{X}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle(PA+A^{*}P)x,x\rangle_{X}+\langle\Xi(t,x,u),P^{*}x\rangle_{X}+\langle Px,\Xi(t,x,u)\rangle_{X}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle(PA+A^{*}P)x,x\rangle_{X}+2\langle\Xi(t,x,u),Px\rangle_{X}.

By (
8
), we have

\displaystyle\dot{V}_{u}(x)\leq-b\|x\|^{\tau}_{X}+c\|x\|_{X}\zeta\left(\|u\|_{U_{c}}\right).

(11)

Let
\epsilon_{0}\in(0,b)
be an arbitrary constant. Define the
\mathcal{K}
-function

for any

\displaystyle\|x\|_{X}\geq\chi\left(\|u\|_{U_{c}}\right)\Rightarrow c\|x\|_{X}\zeta\left(\|u\|_{U_{c}}\right)\leq{\epsilon_{0}}\|x\|^{\tau}_{X},

11

\displaystyle\|x\|_{X}\geq\chi\left(\|u\|_{U_{c}}\right)\Rightarrow\dot{V}_{u}(x)\leq-(b-\epsilon_{0})\|x\|^{\tau}_{X}.

(12)

Note that (
10

\displaystyle\|x\|_{X}\geq\mu_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}V^{\frac{1}{2}}(x).

(13)

We infer from (
12
) and (

\displaystyle\|x\|_{X}\geq\chi\left(\|u\|_{U_{c}}\right)\Rightarrow\dot{V}_{u}(x)\leq-MV^{{\sigma_{0}}}(x),

\displaystyle\sigma_{0}:=\frac{\tau}{2}\in(0,1)\quad\text{and}\quad M:={(b-\epsilon_{0})}\mu_{2}^{-\frac{\tau}{2}}>0.

Therefore,
V(x)=\langle Px,x\rangle_{X}
is an FTISS-LF for system (
6
). Furthermore, Theorem

ensures that system (
6

∎

Remark 1
.

The disturbance-free system (
6
), i.e., system (
6
) with
u\equiv 0
, is finite-time stable in the finite time

.
Furthermore, by virtue of the arbitrariness of
\epsilon_{0}\in(0,b)
, the settling time, denoted by

\displaystyle T_{*}\leq

\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon_{0}\to 0^{+}}T=\frac{2\mu^{\frac{\tau}{2}}_{2}}{(2-\tau)b}V^{1-\frac{\tau}{2}}(x_{0}),

which may depend on the initial data and hence, it cannot be prescribed in advance.

Remark 2
.

For finite-dimensional systems containing sublinear terms, it is a relatively easy task to verify the structural condition (
8

[
45
]
. However, for infinite-dimensional systems described by specific PDEs, as will be shown in Section

8

3
FTISS for a class of parabolic PDEs

3.2
FTISS assessment

In this section, we show how to prove the FTISS for system (

Theorem 3

\delta\in\left(0,1\right)
satisfying

\displaystyle\delta\left(\frac{1}{q}+1-\frac{1}{p}\right)=\frac{1}{q},

(29)

the following interpolation inequality holds true

\displaystyle\|v\|_{{L}^{\infty}(0,1)}\leq\frac{1}{{\delta}^{\delta}}\|v_{y}\|_{{L}^{p}(0,1)}^{\delta}\|v\|_{{L}^{q}(0,1)}^{1-\delta}.

(30)

Proof.

Let
p,q\in(1,+\infty)
and
\delta\in\left(0,1\right)
satisfy (
29
).
Let
\alpha:=\frac{1}{\delta}
. For any
s\in\mathbb{R}
, define
G(s):={\left|s\right|}^{\alpha-1}s
. For any
v\in W_{[0]}^{1,p}(0,1)
, we define the function
H(y):=G(v(y))
. It follows that

\displaystyle H_{y}(y)=G^{\prime}(v(y))v_{y}(y)=\alpha\left|v(y)\right|^{\alpha-1}v_{y}(y).

For any
y\in(0,1)
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\left|H(y)\right|=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{y}H_{z}(z)\mathrm{d}z\right|\leq\int_{0}^{y}\left|H_{z}(z)\right|\mathrm{d}z=\int_{0}^{y}\left|\alpha\left|v(z)\right|^{\alpha-1}v_{z}(z)\right|\mathrm{d}z.

(31)

The equality (
29
) ensures that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{\frac{q}{\alpha-1}}=1

with
\frac{q}{\alpha-1}\in\left(1,+\infty\right)
.
Then, for any
y\in(0,1)
, by using the Hölder’s inequality (see
[
54
, Appendix B.2, p.706]
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{y}\left|\alpha\left|v(z)\right|^{\alpha-1}v_{z}(z)\right|\mathrm{d}z\leq

\displaystyle\alpha{\left(\int_{0}^{y}{\left({\left|v(z)\right|}^{\alpha-1}\right)}^{\frac{q}{\alpha-1}}\mathrm{d}z\right)}^{\frac{\alpha-1}{q}}\left(\int_{0}^{y}{{\left|v_{z}(z)\right|}^{p}}\mathrm{d}z\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\alpha{\left(\int_{0}^{1}{\left({\left|v(z)\right|}^{\alpha-1}\right)}^{\frac{q}{\alpha-1}}\mathrm{d}z\right)}^{\frac{\alpha-1}{q}}\left(\int_{0}^{1}{{\left|v_{z}(z)\right|}^{p}}\mathrm{d}z\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha\|v\|_{L^{q}(0,1)}^{\alpha-1}\|v_{y}\|_{L^{p}(0,1)}.

(32)

We infer from (
31
) and (
32
) that

\displaystyle\left|v(y)\right|^{\alpha}\leq\alpha\|v\|_{L^{q}(0,1)}^{\alpha-1}\|v_{y}\|_{L^{p}(0,1)},\forall y\in(0,1).

(33)

Substituting
\alpha=\frac{1}{\delta}
into (
33
), it follows that

\displaystyle\left|v(y)\right|^{\frac{1}{\delta}}\leq\frac{1}{\delta}\|v\|_{L^{q}(0,1)}^{\frac{1-\delta}{\delta}}\|v_{y}\|_{L^{p}(0,1)},\forall y\in(0,1).

(34)

Taking both sides of the inequality (
34
) to the
\delta
-th power, we have

\displaystyle\left|v(y)\right|\leq\frac{1}{\delta^{\delta}}\|v\|_{L^{q}(0,1)}^{1-\delta}\|v_{y}\|_{L^{p}(0,1)}^{\delta},\forall y\in(0,1),

which ensures that (
30
) holds true.
∎

Corollary 1
.

Let
v\in W_{[0]}^{1,2}(0,1)
.
For any
r\in(0,1)
, the following interpolation inequality holds true

\displaystyle\|v\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{\frac{3+r}{2}}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{(3+r)\epsilon}{2}\|v_{y}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}+\frac{3+r}{8\epsilon}\|v\|_{L^{1+r}(0,1)}^{1+r},\forall\epsilon\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}.

(35)

Proof.

For any
r\in(0,1)
, let

\displaystyle\theta:=\frac{3+r}{4},\quad\delta:=\frac{1}{2\theta}=\frac{2}{3+r}\in\left(\frac{1}{2},\frac{2}{3}\right)\subset\left(0,1\right),\quad p:=4\delta\theta=2,\quad q:=4\theta(1-\delta)=1+r.

By direct calculation, we have

\displaystyle\delta\left(\frac{1}{q}+1-\frac{1}{p}\right)=\frac{1}{q}.

Therefore, for
v\in W_{[0]}^{1,2}(0,1)
, by using the inequality (
30
), we have

\displaystyle\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{\delta^{2\delta}}\|v_{y}\|_{L^{p}(0,1)}^{2\delta}\|v\|_{L^{q}(0,1)}^{2-2\delta},

which implies that

\displaystyle\|v\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2\theta}\leq\frac{1}{\delta^{2\delta\theta}}\|v_{y}\|_{L^{p}(0,1)}^{2\delta\theta}\|v\|_{L^{q}(0,1)}^{2\theta-2\delta\theta}.

(36)

For any
r\in(0,1)
, substituting the values of
\theta,\delta,p,q
into (
36
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|v\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{\frac{3+r}{2}}\leq\frac{3+r}{2}\|v_{y}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}\|v\|_{L^{1+r}(0,1)}^{\frac{1+r}{2}}.

Then, by using the Young’s inequality with
\epsilon\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
(see
[
54
, Appendix B.2, p.706]
), we get

\displaystyle\|v\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{\frac{3+r}{2}}\leq\frac{3+r}{2}\cdot\epsilon\|v_{y}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}+\frac{3+r}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{4\epsilon}\|v\|_{L^{1+r}(0,1)}^{1+r},

which gives the interpolation inequality (
35
). ∎

With the aid of the interpolation inequality (
35
), we prove Theorem
3
by verifying the conditions of the FTISS Lyapunov theorem are all fulfilled.

Proof of Theorem
3
.

Let
P:=I
. Then, (
7
) holds true with
\mu_{1}:=1
. We claim that
V(w):=\langle Pw,w\rangle_{X}=\|w\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}
is an FTISS-LF of system (
15
).

Indeed, for any
t\in\mathbb{R}_{>0},w\in D(A)
, and
f\in U_{c}
, by direct calculation and noting that
A^{*}=A

\displaystyle\langle(PA+A^{*}P)w,w\rangle_{X}=2\langle Aw,w\rangle_{L^{2}(0,1)}=2\langle w_{yy},w\rangle_{L^{2}(0,1)}=-2\langle w_{y},w_{y}\rangle_{L^{2}(0,1)}=-2\|w_{y}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2},

(37)

and

\displaystyle\langle\Xi(t,w,f),w\rangle_{X}=

\displaystyle\langle-k|w|^{r-1}w+f,w\rangle_{L^{2}(0,1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle-k|w|^{r-1}w,w\rangle_{L^{2}(0,1)}+\langle f,w\rangle_{L^{2}(0,1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-k\|w\|_{L^{1+r}(0,1)}^{1+r}+\langle f,w\rangle_{L^{2}(0,1)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-k\|w\|_{L^{1+r}(0,1)}^{1+r}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}\|w\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}

\displaystyle-k\|w\|_{L^{1+r}(0,1)}^{1+r}+\|f\|_{U_{c}}\|w\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}.

(38)

It follows from (
37
) and (

\displaystyle\langle(PA,A^{*}P)w,w\rangle_{X}+2\langle\Xi(t,w,f),w\rangle_{X}\leq-2\|w_{y}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}-2k\|w\|_{L^{1+r}(0,1)}^{1+r}+2\|f\|_{U_{c}}\|w\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}.

\displaystyle-2k\|w\|_{L^{1+r}(0,1)}^{1+r}\leq-\frac{2k}{C_{1}}\|w\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{\frac{3+r}{2}}+\frac{2kC_{0}}{C_{1}}\|w_{y}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2},\forall\epsilon\in\mathbb{R}_{>0},

We infer from (

4
conclusion

In this paper, we extended the notion of FTISS to infinite-dimensional systems and provide Lyapunov theory-based tools to establish the FTISS of infinite-dimensional systems. In particular, we demonstrated the construction of Lyapunov functionals tailored for assessing the FTISS for a class of infinite-dimensional nonlinear systems under the framework of compact semigroup theory and Hilbert spaces, and verified the FTISS for parabolic PDEs with sublinear terms by using an interpolation inequality. Numerical results were presented to illustrate the obtained theoretical results. It is worth mentioning that designing feedback controls to achieve the FTISS for parabolic PDEs remains a challenging subject that will be considered in our future work.

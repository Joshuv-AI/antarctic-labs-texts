MMP for algebraically integrable foliations
Paolo Cascini, Calum Spicer

Abstract
. We show that termination of flips for ℚ \mathbb{Q} -factorial klt pairs in dimension r r implies existence of minimal models for algebraically integrable foliations of rank r r with log canonical singularities over a ℚ \mathbb{Q} -factorial klt projective variety.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
14E30, 37F75

1.
Introduction

Over the last few years, several results have appeared on the Minimal Model Program for foliations over a complex variety (e.g. see

[
Men00
,
Bru00
,
McQ04
,
McQ08
,
Spi20
,
CS21
,
CS20
]
) and, in particular, it is known that foliations of rank one over any manifold or foliations of any rank over a three-dimensional manifold either are uniruled or they admit a minimal model.

The aim of this paper is to study the Minimal Model Program for algebraically integrable foliations with log canonical singularities.
In our previous work, in collaboration with F. Ambro and V.V. Shokurov
[
ACSS21
]
, the cone theorem was proved for these foliations, and the MMP
was proven to exist for a special class, namely Property
(*)
foliations.
While Property
(*)
foliations are a very natural class of foliations, for many applications
Property
(*)
is a too restrictive notion. For instance,
Fano foliations never satisfy Property
(*)
, cf.
[
AD13
, Theorem 5.1]
.

Our goal here is to show that, assuming termination of flips for
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial klt pairs in dimension
r
,
the MMP exists for algebraically integrable
foliations with log canonical singularities of rank
r
. We remark that
log canonical singularities form the largest class
of singularities where the MMP can be expected to be run.

The following is our main result:

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume termination of flips for
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial klt pairs in dimension
r
.
Let
X
be a normal
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial projective variety,
let
\mathcal{F}
be an algebraically integrable foliation on
X
of rank
r

and let
\Delta\geq 0
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor such that
(\mathcal{F},\Delta)
has log canonical singularities
and
(X,\Delta)
is klt.

Then we may run a
(K_{\mathcal{F}}+\Delta)
-MMP
\varphi\colon X\dashrightarrow X^{\prime}
such that, if
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
is the foliation induced on
X^{\prime}
and
\Delta^{\prime}=\varphi_{*}\Delta
then either

(1)

(\mathcal{F}^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime})
is a minimal model, i.e.
K_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}+\Delta^{\prime}
is nef; or

(2)

(\mathcal{F}^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime})
admits a Mori fibre space, i.e. a contraction
\eta\colon X^{\prime}\to Z
onto a normal projective variety
Z
such that
\operatorname{dim}Z<\operatorname{dim}X
,
\rho(X/Z)=1
and
-(K_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}+\Delta^{\prime})
is relatively ample.

1.1.
Sketch of the proof

The main difficulty of the Theorem is to prove the existence of the contraction and, possibly, the flip associated to a
(K_{\mathcal{F}}+\Delta)
-negative extremal ray
R
, where
(\mathcal{F},\Delta)
is an algebraically integrable log canonical foliated pair of rank
r
over a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial variety
X
such that
(X,\Delta)
is klt (cf. Theorem
3.2
). To this end, we divide the proof in three steps. We first run a suitable MMP to construct a Property
(*)
-modification
\pi\colon\overline{X}\to X
so that if
\overline{\mathcal{F}}
is the foliation induced on
\overline{X}
and we write
K_{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}+\overline{\Delta}=\pi^{*}(K_{\mathcal{F}}+\Delta)
, then the pair
(\overline{\mathcal{F}},\overline{\Delta})
satisfies Property
(*)
(cf. Theorem
2.4
). Then we run a partial
(K_{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}+\overline{\Delta})
-MMP so that we contract, and possibly flip, a
(K_{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}+\overline{\Delta})
-negative extremal ray
R^{\prime}
on
\overline{X}
induced by
R
. Finally, we run a third MMP which contracts the strict transform of the divisorial part of the exceptional locus of
\pi
. We then show that the output of this third MMP is either the desired divisorial contraction or the flip associated to
R
.
Note that we need termination of flips for
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial klt pairs in dimension
r
only to run the second MMP.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Notations and preliminary results

We work over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero.

If
D
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on a normal variety
X
and
\Sigma
is a prime divisor in
X
, then we denote by
m_{\Sigma}D
the coefficient of
D
along
\Sigma
.
If
D
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor on
X
, then we denote

D^{\perp}\coloneqq\{\xi\in N_{1}(X)\mid D\cdot\xi=0\}.

A morphism
f\colon X\rightarrow Z
between normal varieties is said to be a
contraction
if it is surjective and
f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}=\mathcal{O}_{Z}
.
Given a contraction between normal varieties
f\colon X\rightarrow Z
and an
\mathbb{R}
divisor
B

on
X
, we define its
horizontal part

B^{h}
as the part of
B
supported on prime divisors which dominate
Z
and its
vertical part

B^{v}\coloneqq B-B^{h}
.

Given an equidimensional contraction
f\colon X\rightarrow Z
between normal varieties and a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
B
on
X
, we define the
discriminant
of the pair
(X/Z,B)
to be the
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on
Z
such that for any prime divisor
\Sigma
on
Z
we have

m_{\Sigma}B_{Z}\coloneqq 1-\sup\{t\mid(\mathcal{F},\Delta+tf^{*}\Sigma)\text{ is log canonical above the generic point of }\Sigma\}.

We refer to
[
CS21
, Section 2.6]
for some of the standard definitions in the Minimal Model Program. Let

\varphi\colon X\dashrightarrow Y
be a birational map between normal varieties. The
exceptional locus
of
\varphi
is the closed subset
\operatorname{Exc}\varphi
of
X
where
\varphi
is not an isomorphism. We say that
\varphi
is a
birational contraction
if
\operatorname{Exc}\varphi^{-1}
does not contain any divisor. Let
\varphi\colon X\dashrightarrow Y
be a birational contraction between normal varieties and let
D
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on
X
such that
D_{Y}\coloneqq\varphi_{*}D
is also
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier. Then
\varphi
is called
D
-negative
(resp.
D
-trivial
) if there exist a normal variety
W
and birational morphisms
p\colon W\to X
and
q\colon W\to Y
resolving the indeterminancy locus of
\varphi
such that
p^{*}D=q^{*}D_{Y}+E
where
E\geq 0
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor whose support coincides with the exceptional locus of
q

(resp.
p^{*}D=q^{*}D_{Y}
). Note that by the negativity lemma (cf.
[
KM92
, Lemma 3.39]
), it follows that if
\varphi
is a composition of steps of a
D
-MMP, then
\varphi
is
D
-negative.

Given a divisor
G
on a normal variety
X
, we denote by
{\mathrm{B}s}(G)
the base locus of the linear system
|G|
. If
D
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on
X
, then the
stable base locus
of
D
is

\mathbb{B}(D)\coloneqq\bigcap{\mathrm{B}s}(mD)

where the intersection is taken over any sufficiently divisible positive integer
m
. The
restricted base locus
of
D
is

\mathbb{B}_{-}(D)\coloneqq\bigcup_{\epsilon\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}}\mathbb{B}(D+\epsilon A)

where
A
is a fixed ample divisor on
X
. Note that the definition does not depend on the choice of the ample divisor
A
.

Similarly, we define the
augmented stable base locus
of
D
as

\mathbb{B}_{+}(D)\coloneqq\bigcap_{\epsilon\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}}\mathbb{B}(D-\epsilon A)

where
A
is a fixed ample divisor on
X
. Also in this case, the definition does not depend on the choice of the ample divisor
A
.
By Nakamaye’s theorem (e.g. see
[
Bir17
, Theorem 1.4]
), if
D
is big and nef then the augmented locus of
D
coincides with the
exceptional locus
of
D
, which is
the union of all the subvarieties
V\subset X
of positive dimension such that
D|_{V}
is not big.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\varphi\colon X\dashrightarrow Y
be a birational contraction between normal
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial projective varieties and let
D
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on
X
such that
\varphi
is
D
-negative.

Then
\operatorname{Exc}\varphi\subset\mathbb{B}_{-}(D)
. Moreover, if
\varphi_{*}D
is nef, then the equality holds.

Proof.

Let
D_{Y}\coloneqq\varphi_{*}D
. Since
\varphi
is
D
-negative, there exist a normal variety
W
and birational morphisms
p\colon W\to X
and
q\colon W\to Y
resolving the indeterminancy locus of
\varphi
and such that
p^{*}D=q^{*}D_{Y}+E
, where
E\geq 0
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor whose support coincides with the exceptional locus of
q
. Since
\varphi
is a birational contraction between
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial normal varieties,
we have that
\operatorname{Exc}p\subset\operatorname{Exc}q
. It follows that
\operatorname{Exc}\varphi=p(\operatorname{Exc}q)
.

Let
A
be an ample divisor on
X
and let
A_{Y}\coloneqq\varphi_{*}A
.
Then there exists
\epsilon>0
sufficiently small, such that if we write

p^{*}(D+\epsilon A)=q^{*}(D_{Y}+\epsilon A_{Y})+E^{\prime},

we have that
E^{\prime}\geq 0
and its support coincides with the support of
E
.
Thus, it follows that
\operatorname{Exc}q\subset\mathbb{B}(p^{*}(D+\epsilon A))
and, therefore,
p(\operatorname{Exc}q)\subset\mathbb{B}(D+\epsilon A)
.
Thus,
\operatorname{Exc}\varphi\subset\mathbb{B}_{-}(D)
, as claimed.

Let us assume now that
D_{Y}
is nef. By the negativity lemma (cf.
[
KM92
, Lemma 3.39]
), there exists an ample divisor
A_{W}
on
W
and a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
G\geq 0
, whose support is contained in
\operatorname{Exc}q
and such that

q^{*}A_{Y}=A_{W}+G
. Thus,
\mathbb{B}(q^{*}(D_{Y}+\epsilon A_{Y}))\subset\operatorname{Exc}q
. It follows that
\mathbb{B}(p^{*}(D+\epsilon A))\subset\operatorname{Exc}q
and, therefore,
\mathbb{B}(D+\epsilon A)\subset\operatorname{Exc}\varphi
. Thus, our claim follows.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
X\to U
and
Y\to U
be two projective morphisms of normal quasi-projective varieties. Let
D
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on
X
and let
\varphi\colon X\dashrightarrow Y
be a
D
-trivial birational contraction over
U
such that
\varphi_{*}D
is ample over
U
.

Then
\varphi\colon X\to Y
is a morphism.

Proof.

Set
D_{Y}\coloneqq\varphi_{*}D
and let
p\colon W\to X
and
q\colon W\to Y
be two birational morphisms from a normal variety
W
which resolve the inderminancy locus of
\varphi
and such that
p^{*}D=q^{*}D_{Y}
. Then
p^{*}D
is semi-ample over
U
and
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(mD))\simeq H^{0}(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y}(mD_{Y}))
for any sufficiently divisible positive integer
m
. It follows that also
D
is semiample and that
\varphi
is the morphism induced by
D
.
∎

2.2.
Foliations

Let
X
be a normal veriety. A
foliation of rank
r
on
X
consists of a coherent subsheaf
T_{\mathcal{F}}\subset T_{X}
of rank
r
such that

(1)

T_{\mathcal{F}}
is saturated, i.e.
T_{X}/T_{\mathcal{F}}
is torsion free; and

(2)

T_{\mathcal{F}}
is closed under Lie bracket.

The
co-rank
of
\mathcal{F}
is its co-rank as a subsheaf of
T_{X}
. We define the canonical divisor
K_{\mathcal{F}}
to be a divisor on
X
so that
\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{\mathcal{F}})\cong(\operatorname{det}T_{\mathcal{F}})^{*}

and we define the conormal divisor
K_{[X/\mathcal{F}]}
to be a divisor on
X
so that

\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{[X/\mathcal{F}]})\cong(\operatorname{det}(T_{X}/T_{\mathcal{F}}))^{*}
.
For any positive integer
d
, we denote
\Omega^{[d]}_{X}\coloneqq(\wedge^{d}\Omega^{1}_{X})^{**}
.
Then, the foliation
\mathcal{F}
induces a map

\phi\colon(\Omega^{[r]}_{X}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(-K_{\mathcal{F}}))^{**}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}.

The
singular locus
of
\mathcal{F}
, denoted by
\operatorname{Sing}\mathcal{F}
, is the cosupport of the image of
\phi
.

Let
\sigma\colon Y\dashrightarrow X
be a dominant map between normal varieties and let
\mathcal{F}
be a foliation of rank
r
on
X
. We denote by
\sigma^{-1}\mathcal{F}
the
induced foliation
on
Y
(e.g. see
[
Dru21
, Section 3.2]
).
If
T_{\mathcal{F}}=0
, i.e. if
\mathcal{F}
is the foliation by points on
X
,
then we refer to
\sigma^{-1}\mathcal{F}
as the
foliation induced by
\sigma
and we denote it by
T_{X/Y}
.
In this case, the foliation
\sigma^{-1}\mathcal{F}
is called
algebraically integrable
.

Let
f\colon X\to Z
be a morphism between normal varieties and let
\mathcal{F}
be the induced foliation on
X
.
If
f
is equidimensional,
then we define the
ramification divisor

R(f)
of
f
as

R(f)=\sum_{D}(\phi^{*}D-\phi^{-1}(D))

where the sum runs through all the prime divisors of
Z
. In this case, we have

K_{\mathcal{F}}\sim K_{X/Z}-R(f)

(e.g. see
[
Dru17
, Notation 2.7 and §2.9]
).

Let
X
be a normal variety and let
\mathcal{F}
be a rank
r
foliation on
X
.
Let
S\subset X
be a subvariety. Then
S
is said to be
\mathcal{F}
-invariant
, or
invariant by
\mathcal{F}
, if for any open subset
U\subset X
and any section
\partial\in H^{0}(U,\mathcal{F})
, we have that

\partial(\mathcal{I}_{S\cap U})\subset\mathcal{I}_{S\cap U},

where
\mathcal{I}_{S\cap U}
denotes the ideal sheaf of
S\cap U
.
If
D\subset X
is a prime divisor then we define
\epsilon(D)=1
if
D
is not
\mathcal{F}
-invariant and
\epsilon(D)=0
if it is
\mathcal{F}
-invariant. Note that if
\mathcal{F}
is the foliation induced by a dominant map
\sigma\colon Y\dashrightarrow Z
, then a divisor
D
is
\mathcal{F}
-invariant if and only if it is vertical with respect to
\sigma
.

2.3.
Foliated pairs

Let
X
be a normal variety.
A
foliated pair

(\mathcal{F},\Delta)
on
X
consists of a foliation
\mathcal{F}
on
X
and an
\mathbb{R}
-divisor

\Delta\geq 0

such that
K_{\mathcal{F}}+\Delta
is
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier.
Assuming that
\mathcal{F}
is algebraically integrable, then we refer to
[
ACSS21
, Section 3.2]
for some standard definitions of foliated singularities (e.g. foliated log smooth, log canonical, F-dlt).

Let
(\mathcal{F},\Delta)
be a foliated pair on a normal variety
X
. Then, we say that
(\mathcal{F},\Delta)
satisfies
Property
(*)
if there exist a projective contraction
f\colon X\to Z
and an
\mathcal{F}
-invariant divisor
G\geq 0
on
X

such that
\mathcal{F}
is induced by
f
and
(X/Z,\Delta+G)
is a GLC pair which satisfies Property
(*)
(cf.
[
ACSS21
, Section 2.2 and Definition 2.13]
). In particular, there exists a divisor
\Sigma_{Z}
on
Z
such that
(Z,\Sigma_{Z}
) is log smooth and
G
is the pre-image, as a reduced divisor, of
\Sigma_{Z}
in
X
.
We say that such a divisor
G
is
associated
to
(\mathcal{F},\Delta)
and we refer to
\Sigma_{Z}\subset Z
as the
discriminant
of
(\mathcal{F},\Delta)
. By
[
ACSS21
, Lemma 2.14]
,
\Sigma_{Z}
coincides with the discriminant of the pair
(X/Z,\Delta+G)
defined above.
If
f\colon X\to Z
is equidimensional then

K_{\mathcal{F}}+\Delta\sim_{f,\mathbb{R}}K_{X}+\Delta+G

(cf.
[
ACSS21
, Proposition 3.6]
).

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
X
be a normal projective
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial klt variety and
let
(\mathcal{F},\Delta)
be a log canonical foliated pair on
X
satisfying Property
(*)
. Assume that
\mathcal{F}
is induced by an equidimensional projective contraction
f\colon X\to Z
. Let
R
be a
(K_{\mathcal{F}}+\Delta)
-negative extremal ray.

Then the contraction and, if the contraction is small, the flip associated to
R
exist. Moreover, if
\varphi\colon X\dashrightarrow X^{\prime}
is the step of the MMP associated to
R
,
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
is the induced foliation on
X^{\prime}
and
\Delta^{\prime}=\varphi_{*}\Delta
, then
X^{\prime}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial and klt,
(\mathcal{F}^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime})
satisfies Property
(*)
and
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
is induced by an equidimensional projective contraction
f^{\prime}\colon X^{\prime}\to Z
.

Proof.

We follow the same methods as in
[
ACSS21
, Lemma 3.14]
. By the cone theorem (cf.
[
ACSS21
, Theorem 3.9]
), we may assume that
R
is spanned by a curve
\xi
on
X
which is tangent to
\mathcal{F}
. Let
G\geq 0
be a divisor associated to
(\mathcal{F},\Delta)
. Then
[
ACSS21
, Proposition 3.6]
implies

K_{\mathcal{F}}+\Delta\sim_{f,\mathbb{R}}K_{X}+\Delta+G,

and, by
[
ACSS21
, Lemma 2.14]
, the pair
(X,\Delta+G)
is log canonical. It follows that
R
is
(K_{X}+(1-\epsilon)\Delta+(1-\epsilon)G)
-negative for some sufficiently small
\epsilon>0
such that
(X,(1-\epsilon)\Delta+(1-\epsilon)G)
is klt. Thus,
the existence of the contraction follows from the classic base point free theorem (cf.
[
KM98
, Theorem 3.3]
). Similarly, if the contraction is small, then the flip associated to
R
exists by the classical result on existence of flips. In particular,
X^{\prime}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial and klt.

Finally, the last part of the Proposition follows by
[
ACSS21
, Proposition 2.18]
.
∎

The following is a slightly stronger version of
[
ACSS21
, Theorem 3.10]
:

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
(\mathcal{F},\Delta)
be a log canonical foliated pair on a normal projective pair
X
and assume that
\mathcal{F}
is algebraically integrable.

Then there exists a birational morphism
\pi\colon X^{\prime}\to X
such that if
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
is the foliation induced on
X^{\prime}
and
\Delta^{\prime}\coloneqq\pi_{*}^{-1}\Delta+\sum\epsilon(E)E
, where the sum runs over all the exceptional divisors of
\pi
, then

(1)

X^{\prime}
is klt and
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial;

(2)

\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
is induced by an equidimensional morphism
f\colon X^{\prime}\to Z
;

(3)

(\mathcal{F}^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime})
satisfies Property
(*)
and if
G
is the associated divisor, then every
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
-invariant divisor in the exceptional locus of
\pi
is contained in the support of
G
; and

(4)

K_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}+\Delta^{\prime}=\pi^{*}(K_{\mathcal{F}}+\Delta)
.

The morphism
\pi
is called a
Property
(*)
-modification
.

Proof.

By
[
ACSS21
, Theorem 2.2 and Proposition 2.16]
,
there exists a birational morphism
m\colon W\to X
such that, if
\mathcal{F}_{W}
is the foliation induced on
W
and

\Delta_{W}\coloneqq m^{-1}E+\sum\epsilon(E)E

where the sum runs over all the
m
-exceptional divisors, then
\mathcal{F}_{W}
is induced by an equidimensional contraction
g\colon W\to Z^{\prime}

and
(\mathcal{F}_{W},\Delta_{W})
satisfies Property
(*)
. Let
G_{W}
be its associated divisor. We may assume that the support of
G_{W}
contains every
\mathcal{F}_{W}
-invariant
m
-exceptional divisor. By Proposition
2.3
, we may run a
(K_{\mathcal{F}_{W}}+\Delta_{W})
-MMP over
X
. By
[
ACSS21
, Lemma 3.13]
, every curve contracted by this MMP is also mapped to a point in
Z^{\prime}
and, therefore, every step of the MMP is also a step in a relative
(K_{W}+\Delta_{W}+G_{W})
-MMP over
X
. In particular, the MMP terminates. Call this MMP
\varphi\colon W\dashrightarrow X^{\prime}
and let
\pi\colon X^{\prime}\to X
be the induced birational morphism. Let
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
be the foliation induced on
X^{\prime}
,
\Delta^{\prime}\coloneqq\varphi_{*}\Delta_{W}
and
G\coloneqq\varphi_{*}G_{W}
. Proposition
2.3
implies that
(\mathcal{F}^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime})
satisfies Property
(*)
and if
G
is its associated divisor,
then the support of
G
contains every
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
-invariant divisor in the exceptional locus of
\pi
. Thus, (3) follows. In addition, Proposition
2.3
implies (1) and (2). Finally, by construction,
\varphi
contracts every
m
-exceptional divisor
E
such that
a(E,\mathcal{F},\Delta)>-\epsilon(E)
. Thus, (4) follows.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Assume termination of flips for
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial klt pairs in dimension
r
. Let
X
be a normal projective
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial klt variety and let
(\mathcal{F},\Delta)
be a log canonical foliated pair of rank
r
on
X
satisfying Property
(*)
. Assume that
\mathcal{F}
is induced by an equidimensional projective contraction
f\colon X\to Z
.

Then any sequence of
(K_{\mathcal{F}}+\Delta)
-flips terminates.

Proof.

Assume by contradiction that

X_{1}\coloneqq X\dashrightarrow X_{2}\dashrightarrow\dots

is an infinite sequence of
(K_{\mathcal{F}}+\Delta)
-flips.
By Proposition
2.3
, it follows that each
X_{i}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial and klt. Moreover, if
\mathcal{F}_{i}
denotes the foliation induced on
X_{i}
and
\Delta_{i}
is the strict transform of
\Delta
on
X_{i}
, then
(\mathcal{F}_{i},\Delta_{i})
satisfies Property
(*)
. Let
G_{i}
be the associated divisor to
(\mathcal{F}_{i},\Delta_{i})
.
As in the proof of Proposition
2.3
, it follows that
each map
\varphi_{i}\colon X_{i}\dashrightarrow X_{i+1}
is a
(K_{X_{i}}+\Delta_{i}+G_{i})
-flip over
Z

and the induced morphism
f_{i}\colon X_{i}\to Z
is equidimensional. By Noetherian
induction, there exists a closed point
p\in Z
such that, if we denote by
F_{i}
the fibre
f_{i}^{-1}(p)
, then the induced map
F_{i}\dashrightarrow F_{i+1}
is not an isomorphism for infinitely many
i
. Note that, by assumption,
F_{i}
has dimension
r
. By
[
ACSS21

\overline{X}_{1}\dashrightarrow\overline{X}_{2}\dashrightarrow\dots

are a sequence of steps of a
(K_{\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{1}}+\overline{\Delta}_{1})
-MMP and, therefore, by Lemma
2.5
, it terminates.
Thus, also the sequence of
(K_{\mathcal{F}}+\Delta)
-flips terminates, as claimed.
∎

4.
Some open problems

We conclude with some open problems related to the main results in this paper.
First, we expect the following generalisation of the base point free theorem to be true:

Conjecture 4.1
.

Let
X
be a normal projective variety, let
\mathcal{F}
be an algebraically integrable foliation on
X

and let
\Delta\geq 0
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor so that
(\mathcal{F},\Delta)
has F-dlt singularities
and
(X,\Delta)
is klt. Let
A
be an ample
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on
X
such that
L=K_{\mathcal{F}}+\Delta+A
is a nef
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor.

Then
L
is semi-ample.

In addition, we anticipate that, as with foliations on a three-dimensional variety (e.g. see
[
CS21
, Theorem 1.6 and Theorem 11.3]
), the Minimal Model Program will yield some interesting implications on foliated singularities:

Conjecture 4.2
.

Let
X
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial projective variety, let
\mathcal{F}
be an algebraically integrable foliation on
X

and let
\Delta\geq 0
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor so that
(X,\Delta)
is klt
and one of the following holds:

(1)

(\mathcal{F},\Delta)
is F-dlt, or

(2)

(\mathcal{F},\Delta)
is canonical.

Then there exists a morphism
f\colon X\rightarrow Y
which induces

\mathcal{F}
.

Finally, we expect the following:

Conjecture 4.3
.

Let
X
be a normal projective variety, let
\mathcal{F}
be a foliation on
X

and let
\Delta\geq 0
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on
X
so that
(X,\Delta)
is klt,
(\mathcal{F},\Delta)
admits log canonical singularities and
K_{\mathcal{F}}+\Delta
is not pseudo-effective.

Then we may run a
(K_{\mathcal{F}}+\Delta)
-MMP.

Note that we are not assuming that
\mathcal{F}
is algebraically integrable. On the other hand,
[
CP19
]
implies that if
\mathcal{F}
is a foliation such that
K_{\mathcal{F}}
is not pseudo-effective then the algebraic part of
\mathcal{F}
is non-trivial, i.e. there exist a dominant map
\sigma\colon X\dashrightarrow Y
and a foliation
\mathcal{G}
on
Y
such that
\operatorname{dim}Y<\operatorname{dim}X
and
\mathcal{F}=\sigma^{-1}\mathcal{G}
.

Poisson imbedding meets the Clark-Ocone formula
Caroline Hillairet, Thomas Peyrat, Anthony Réveillac

Abstract
In this paper we develop a representation formula of Clark-Ocone type for any integrable Poisson functionals, which extends the Poisson imbedding for point processes. This representation formula differs from the classical Clark-Ocone formula on three accounts. First the representation holds with respect to the Poisson measure instead of the compensated one; second the representation holds true in L 1 L^{1} and not in L 2 L^{2} ; and finally contrary to the classical Clark-Ocone formula the integrand is defined as a pathwise operator and not as a L 2 L^{2} -limiting object. We make use of Malliavin’s calculus and of the pseudo-chaotic decomposition with uncompensated iteraded integrals to establish this Pseudo-Clark-Ocone representation formula and to characterize the integrand, which turns out to be a predictable integrable process.

Hawkes processes; Poisson imbedding representation; Malliavin’s calculus; Clark-Ocone formula.

Introduction

Martingale representation formulas for Poisson functionals have been widely investigated in the literature (such as Løkka
[
Løk04
]
, Nualart and Schoutens
[
NS00
]
) in relation with stochastic analysis tools such as the chaotic expansion or the Malliavin calculus. At the crossroad of martingale representation and the Malliavin calculus lies the so-called Clark-Ocone

1

also referred as Clark-Ocone-Haussmann formula
formula which allows one to provide a description of the integrand in terms of the Malliavin derivative. More precisely, on the Poisson space, the Clark-Ocone formula gives the following representation of a square-integrable random variable
F
:

F=\mathbb{E}[F]+\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}(D_{t}F)^{p}\;\tilde{N}(dt)

where
\tilde{N}
is the compensated Poisson process and
(D_{t}F)^{p}
is the predictable projection of the Malliavin derivative of
F
. It is important to note that in nature the Clark-Ocone formula and the martingale representation slightly differ as the latter may exist when the former fails to hold (like for instance for the fractional Brownian motion which is not a semimartingale with respect to its natural filtration). In addition the Clark-Ocone formula is restricted to representation of random variables whereas martingale representation provides a dynamic representation of the integrand for a given martingale. Yet in a Poisson framework both representations are intricated.
Various kinds of generalizations have been obtained.
Using the Malliavin integration by parts formula given in Picard
[
Pic96
]
, Zhang
[

]
establishes the following Clark-Ocone formula: for any bounded Poisson functional

F=\mathbb{E}[F]+\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{X}}\left({\mathbb{D}_{(t,x)}F}\right)^{p}\;\tilde{\mathbb{N}}(dt,dx)

where
\tilde{\mathbb{N}}
is the compensated Poisson random measure, and
\left({\mathbb{D}_{(t,x)}F}\right)^{p}
is the predictable projection of the Malliavin derivative of

Last and Penrose
[
LP11b
]
also give a Clark-Ocone type martingale representation formula when the underlying filtration is generated by a Poisson process on a measurable space.
Flint and Torrisi
[

provide a Clark-Ocone formula for point processes on a finite interval possessing a conditional intensity.
Di Nunno and Vives
[
DNV17
]
develop a Malliavin-Skorohod type calculus for additive processes
and obtain a generalization of the Clark-Ocone formula for random variables in
L^{1}
whose integrand operator is supposed to be in
L^{1}
as well.
At the same time, the so-called Poisson imbedding (see Jacod
[
Jac79

or Brémaud and Massoulié
[
BM96
]
) provides also a representation of any point process with respect to an uncompensated Poisson measure. More precisely,
if

can be represented on a probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
supporting a random Poisson measure
\mathbb{N}
on

H_{T}=\int_{(0,T]\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}\textbf{1}_{\{\theta\leq\lambda_{t}\}}\mathbb{N}(dt,d\theta);\quad\forall T>0.

Yet Hillairet and Réveillac
[
HR22
]
investigate the so called pseudo-chaotic expansion which involves also non-compensated iterated integrals. This recent contribution leads the way to the study of a Clark-Ocone representation formula with respect to the non-compensated Poisson measure
\mathbb{N}
, which in a sense will generalize the Poisson imbedding relation (
1.1
).
The aim of this paper is to investigate which Poisson-functionals admit such Pseudo-Clark-Ocone decomposition and to determine the integrand.

More precisely, we consider a Poisson random measure
\mathbb{N}
defined on
(\mathbb{X},\mathfrak{X}):=([0,T]\times\mathbb{X};\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\otimes\mathcal{X})
equipped with a non-atomic
\sigma
-finite measure
\boldsymbol{\rho}:=dt\otimes\pi
, and where
(\mathbb{X},\mathcal{X})
is a complete separable metric space.
The Pseudo-Clark-Ocone formula is stated in Theorem
3.8
for any
F
in
L^{1}(\Omega)

(under the assumption that
\boldsymbol{\rho}(\mathbb{X})<+\infty
) :

F=F(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\emptyset})+\int_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{X}}\mathcal{H}_{(t,x)}F\;\mathbb{N}(dt,dx),\;\mathbb{P}-a.s..

(1.2)

The operator

\mathcal{H}F
is defined as follows:

\mathcal{H}:\begin{array}[]{ll}L^{0}(\Omega)&\to L^{0}(\Omega\times\mathbb{X})\\
F&\mapsto\mathcal{H}_{(t,x)}F:=\mathbb{D}_{(t,x)}F\circ\tau_{t}\end{array}

where
\mathbb{D}_{(t,x)}
is the Malliavin derivative operator and

\tau_{t}:\begin{array}[]{ll}\;\;\;\;\;\;\Omega&\to\;\;\;\;\;\;\Omega\\
\boldsymbol{\omega}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\delta_{{(t_{j},x_{j})}}&\mapsto\boldsymbol{\omega}_{t}:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\delta_{{(t_{j},x_{j})}}\textbf{1}_{\{t_{j}<t\}}.\end{array}

Let us emphasize that this Pseudo-Clark-Ocone formula holds for any
F
in
L^{1}(\Omega)
and its integrand
\mathcal{H}F
is well defined pathwise and is proved to belong to
\mathbb{L}^{1}_{\mathcal{P}}
(see Theorems
3.8
and
3.9
). This is a major difference with the standard Clark-Ocone formula that requires
F\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and for which the integrand
\left({\mathbb{D}F}\right)^{p}
is only defined as a limit in
L^{2}(\Omega\times\mathbb{X})
(see Remark
3.3
).

The paper is organized as follows. Notations and the description of the Poisson space and elements of Malliavin’s calculus are presented in Section
2

3

We first introduce a purely deterministic operator that will be at the core of the pseudo-chaotic expansion.

]
provides the convergence of the series

\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{n}}\left|T_{n}F(\mathbb{x}_{1},\ldots,\mathbb{x}_{n})\right|^{2}\boldsymbol{\rho}^{\otimes n}(d\mathbb{x}_{1},\ldots,d\mathbb{x}_{n}).

The other classical relation on the Poisson space is the Clark-Ocone formula, for which one can find in the literature different variants with different conditions, such as e.g. Privault
[
Pri09
]
, Zhang
[
Zha09
]
, Di Nunno and Vives
[
DNV17
]
. In our setting it takes the following form.

Theorem 3.2

(Clark-Ocone formula)
.

Let
F
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. Then

F=\mathbb{E}[F]+\int_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{X}}\left({\mathbb{D}_{(t,x)}F}\right)^{p}\tilde{\mathbb{N}}(dt,dx)

where the process
\left({\mathbb{D}F}\right)^{p}
belongs to
\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\mathcal{P}}
and is in this context the predictable projection of
\mathbb{D}F
which is properly defined in the Appendix.

Remark 3.3
.

Contrary to what it suggests, the meaning of the integrand in the stochastic integral has to be made precise. According to Definition
2.4
,
\mathbb{D}F
belongs to
L^{0}(\Omega\times\mathbb{X})
whenever
F
belongs to
L^{0}(\Omega)
; however, the definition of the operator
\left({\mathbb{D}F}\right)^{p}
is not guaranteed. For instance
F
in
L^{2}(\Omega\times\mathbb{X})
does not entail
\mathbb{D}F\in L^{2}(\Omega\times\mathbb{X})
. Several sufficient conditions can be found in the literature (see Last
[
Las16
]
). In general as we will make it precise in the proof of Theorem
3.2
(see Section
5
), it is possible to define
\left({\mathbb{D}F}\right)^{p}
in
L^{2}(\Omega\times\mathbb{X})
in a limiting procedure and based on the continuous feature of the mapping
F\mapsto\left(\mathbb{E}_{t-}\left[\mathbb{D}_{(t,x)}F\right]\right)_{(t,x)}
together with the fact that for fixed
(t,x)
,
\mathbb{E}_{t-}\left[\mathbb{D}_{(t,x)}F\right]=\left({\mathbb{D}_{(t,x)}F}\right)^{p}\;\mathbb{P}-a.s.
(see
[
Zha09
]
). For sake of completeness, we reproduce the proof of this result in the Appendix (Section
5
) following
[
Pri09
, Section 3.2]
. Note that even in the case of the Clark-Ocone formula obtained in
[
DNV17
]
where the
L^{2}
assumption on
F
is relaxed to
L^{1}
, the integrand operator is assumed to belong to
L^{1}
as well. In Theorem
3.8
below (Pseudo-Clark-Ocone formula) the integrability of the integrand is a consequence of
F
in
L^{1}
.

3.2
Pseudo-chaotic expansion and Pseudo-Clark-Ocone formula

We recall here the pseudo-chaotic expansion obtained with respect to the non-compensated iterated integrals operators
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{I}}_{n}
.

Theorem 3.4

(See Theorem 2 in
[
HR22
]
)
.

Assume
\boldsymbol{\rho}(\mathbb{X})<+\infty
and
F
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. Then

F=F(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\emptyset})+\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\boldsymbol{\mathcal{I}}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{k}F);\quad\mathbb{P}-a.s.

(3.1)

Inspired by this pseudo-chaotic expansion, we introduce below the operator that will allow us to write a Clark-Ocone formula with respect to the non-compensated Poisson measure (that we called Pseudo-Clark-Ocone formula).

Definition 3.5
.

For fixed
t>0
, we consider the measurable transformation
\tau_{t}

\tau_{t}:\begin{array}[]{ll}\Omega&\to\Omega\\
\boldsymbol{\omega}&\mapsto\boldsymbol{\omega}_{t}\end{array}

where for
\boldsymbol{\omega}
of the form
\boldsymbol{\omega}:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\delta_{{(t_{j},x_{j})}},\;(t_{j},x_{j})\in\mathbb{X},\;j=1,\ldots,n,\;n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{+\infty\}
, we set

\boldsymbol{\omega}_{t}:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\delta_{{(t_{j},x_{j})}}\textbf{1}_{\{t_{j}<t\}}.

We also set
\tau_{0}
as

\tau_{0}(\boldsymbol{\omega}):=\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\emptyset};\quad\boldsymbol{\omega}\in\Omega

where we recall Notation
2.2
.

Roughly speaking,
\tau_{t}
plays the role of a
\mathcal{F}^{\mathbb{N}}_{t^{-}}
-conditional expectation.

Definition 3.6
.

Fix
(t,x)
in
\mathbb{X}
. For
F\in L^{0}(\Omega)
, we define the operator
\mathcal{H}
as :

\mathcal{H}:\begin{array}[]{ll}L^{0}(\Omega)&\to L^{0}(\Omega\times\mathbb{X})\\
F&\mapsto\mathcal{H}_{(t,x)}F:=\mathbb{D}_{(t,x)}F\circ\tau_{t}\end{array}

that is for
\boldsymbol{\omega}\in\Omega

\mathcal{H}_{(t,x)}F(\boldsymbol{\omega}):=\mathbb{D}_{(t,x)}F(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{t})=F(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{t}\circ\varepsilon^{+}_{(t,x)})-F(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{t}).

As highlighted in the following lemma, it turns out that the operator
\mathcal{H}
can be written as the combination of the Malliavin derivative with a very specific Girsanov transformation
L^{T,t}
. This is exactly the (non-equivalent) Girsanov transformation which is at the core of the pseudo-chaotic expansion (see
[
HR22
]
) and under which the Poisson measure
\mathbb{N}
(with intensity
\boldsymbol{\rho}
on
\mathbb{X}
) becomes a Poisson measure with intensity 0 on
[t,T]\times\mathbb{X}
.

Lemma 3.7
.

Assume
\boldsymbol{\rho}(\mathbb{X})<+\infty
and
F\in L^{0}(\Omega)
. Fix
(t,x)\in(0,T)\times\mathbb{X}
. Then the operator
\mathcal{H}_{(t,x)}
re-writes as

\mathcal{H}_{(t,x)}F=\mathbb{E}_{t-}[L^{T,t}\mathbb{D}_{(t,x)}F],\;\mathbb{P}-a.s.

\mbox{ with }\quad L^{T,t}:=\exp((T-t)\pi(\mathbb{X}))\textbf{1}_{\{\mathbb{N}([t,T]\times\mathbb{X})=0\}}.

Proof.

Let
\boldsymbol{\omega}\in\Omega
.

\displaystyle(L^{T,t}\mathbb{D}_{(t,x)}F)(\boldsymbol{\omega})

\displaystyle=\exp((T-t)\pi(\mathbb{X}))\textbf{1}_{\{\boldsymbol{\omega}([t,T]\times\mathbb{X})=0\}}\mathbb{D}_{(t,x)}F(\boldsymbol{\omega})

\displaystyle=\left(\exp((T-t)\pi(\mathbb{X}))\textbf{1}_{\{\mathbb{N}([t,T]\times\mathbb{X})=0\}}\mathbb{D}_{(t,x)}F\circ\tau_{t}\right)(\boldsymbol{\omega}).

Hence since
\mathbb{D}_{(t,x)}F\circ\tau_{t}
is
\mathcal{F}^{\mathbb{N}}_{t-}
-mesurable

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{t-}[L^{T,t}\mathbb{D}_{(t,x)}F]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{D}_{(t,x)}F\circ\tau_{t}\;\exp((T-t)\pi(\mathbb{X}))\mathbb{E}_{t-}[\textbf{1}_{\{\mathbb{N}([t,T]\times\mathbb{X})=0\}}]

\displaystyle=(F(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{t}\circ\varepsilon^{+}_{(t,x)})-F(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{t}))\;\exp((T-t)\pi(\mathbb{X}))\mathbb{E}_{t-}[\textbf{1}_{\{\mathbb{N}([t,T]\times\mathbb{X})=0\}}]

\displaystyle=\mathcal{H}_{(t,x)}F.

∎

We have now all the elements to state the Pseudo-Clark-Ocone formula for any
F
in
L^{1}(\Omega)
, first under the condition that
\boldsymbol{\rho}(\mathbb{X})<+\infty
.

Theorem 3.8
.

Assume
\boldsymbol{\rho}(\mathbb{X})<+\infty
. Let
F
in
L^{1}(\Omega)
. Then
\mathcal{H}F\in\mathbb{L}^{1}_{\mathcal{P}}
and

F=F(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\emptyset})+\int_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{X}}\mathcal{H}_{(t,x)}F\;\mathbb{N}(dt,dx),\;\mathbb{P}-a.s..

In addition this decomposition is unique in the sense that if there exists
(c,Z)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{L}^{1}_{\mathcal{P}}
such that

F=c+\int_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{X}}Z_{(t,x)}\;\mathbb{N}(dt,dx),\;\mathbb{P}-a.s..

(3.2)

then
c=F(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\emptyset})
and for a.-e.
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{X}
,
Z_{(t,x)}=\mathcal{H}_{(t,x)}F
,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s..

Proof.

To highlight the main keys of the proof, some technical lemmata (namely Lemma
4.1
and Lemma
4.2
) are postponed in Section
4
.

Uniqueness:
Assume Relation (
3.2
) holds. Then
note that

F(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\emptyset})=F\textbf{1}_{\{\mathbb{N}(\mathbb{X})=0\}}=c.

Fix
(t,x)\in\mathbb{X}
,
t>0
. We have for
\mathbb{P}
-a.e.
\boldsymbol{\omega}
in
\Omega
, since
Z\in\mathbb{L}^{1}_{\mathcal{P}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{(t,x)}F(\boldsymbol{\omega})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{D}_{(t,x)}F(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{t})

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{X}}(Z_{(s,y)}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{t}+\delta_{(t,x)})-Z_{(s,y)}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{t}))\;\boldsymbol{\omega}_{t}(ds,dy)+Z_{(t,x)}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{t}+\delta_{(t,x)})

\displaystyle=Z_{(t,x)}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{t}+\delta_{(t,x)})=Z_{(t,x)}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{t})=Z_{(t,x)}(\boldsymbol{\omega}).

Existence:
We proceed in several parts.

Step 1.
Since
\boldsymbol{\rho}(\mathbb{X})<+\infty
,
\mathbb{P}[\mathbb{N}(\mathbb{X})=0]>0
. Let
F\in L^{2}(\Omega)
. Thus
\lim_{p\to+\infty}\mathbb{E}[|F-F^{k}|^{2}]=0
with
F^{k}:=\mathbb{E}[F]+\sum_{n=1}^{k}\mathbb{I}_{n}(T_{n}F)
. By Lemma
4.2
and the uniqueness of the pseudo-chaotic expansion, for any
k
,

F^{k}=F^{k}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\emptyset})+\sum_{n=1}^{k}\boldsymbol{\mathcal{I}}_{n}(\mathcal{T}_{n}F^{k}),\mbox{ and }F^{k}=F^{k}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\emptyset})+\int_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{X}}\mathcal{H}_{(t,x)}F^{k}\;\mathbb{N}(dt,dx),

and
\mathcal{H}_{(t,x)}F^{k}
belongs to
\mathbb{L}^{1}_{\mathcal{P}}
.
In addition by Lemma
4.1
,
\mathcal{H}F
is well-defined in
\mathbb{L}^{1}_{\mathcal{P}}
and

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\int_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{X}}\mathcal{H}_{(t,x)}F^{k}\mathbb{N}(dt,dx)-\int_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{X}}\mathcal{H}_{(t,x)}F\mathbb{N}(dt,dx)\right|\right]=0.

Hence

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|F-F(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\emptyset})-\int_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{X}}\mathcal{H}_{(t,x)}F\mathbb{N}(dt,dx)\right|\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\left|F^{k}-F^{k}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\emptyset})-\int_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{X}}\mathcal{H}_{(t,x)}F^{k}\mathbb{N}(dt,dx)\right|\right]

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\int_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{X}}(\mathcal{H}_{(t,x)}F^{k}-\mathcal{H}_{(t,x)}F)\mathbb{N}(dt,dx)\right|\right]

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\left[\left|F-F^{k}\right|\right]+\left|F(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\emptyset})-F^{k}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\emptyset})\right|\quad\to_{k\to+\infty}0

\mbox{ as }\quad F(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\emptyset})-F^{p}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\emptyset})=(\mathbb{P}[\mathbb{N}(\mathbb{X})=0])^{-1}\mathbb{E}[|F-F^{k}|\textbf{1}_{\{\mathbb{N}(\mathbb{X})=0\}}]\to_{p\to+\infty}0.

Step 2.
Assume
F\in L^{1}(\Omega)
. Let for
k\geq 1
,
F^{k}:=-k\vee F\wedge k
. Then by monotone convergence theorem
\lim_{k\to+\infty}\mathbb{E}[|F-F^{k}|]=0
. For any
k
, as
F^{k}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
,
by Step 1,

F^{k}=F^{k}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\emptyset})+\int_{\mathbb{X}}\mathcal{H}_{(t,x)}F^{k}\mathbb{N}(dt,dx).

By definition,

\displaystyle|F^{k}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\emptyset})-F(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\emptyset})|

\displaystyle=((\mathbb{P}[\mathbb{N}(\mathbb{X})=0])^{-1})\mathbb{E}[|F^{k}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\emptyset})-F(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\emptyset})|]

\displaystyle=((\mathbb{P}[\mathbb{N}(\mathbb{X})=0])^{-1})\mathbb{E}[|F(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\emptyset})|\textbf{1}_{\{|F(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\emptyset})|>k\}}]

\displaystyle\to_{k\to+\infty}0.

Finally by Lemma
4.1
,
\mathcal{H}F\in\mathbb{L}^{1}_{\mathcal{P}}
and

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\int_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{X}}\mathcal{H}_{(t,x)}F^{k}\mathbb{N}(dt,dx)-\int_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{X}}\mathcal{H}_{(t,x)}F\mathbb{N}(dt,dx)\right|\right]=0

leading to

\quad F=F(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\emptyset})+\int_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{X}}\mathcal{H}_{(t,x)}F\mathbb{N}(dt,dx).

∎

We now extend the previous result to the case
\boldsymbol{\rho}(\mathbb{X})=+\infty
.

Theorem 3.9
.

Assume
\boldsymbol{\rho}(\mathbb{X})=+\infty
. Let
F
in
L^{1}(\Omega)
. Then

F=F(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\emptyset})+\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{X}}\mathcal{H}_{(t,x)}F\;\mathbb{N}(dt,dx),\;\mathbb{P}-a.s

and for any subset
\mathbf{R}\subset\mathbb{X}
with
\boldsymbol{\rho}(\mathbf{R})<+\infty
,
\mathcal{H}F\textbf{1}_{\{\mathbf{R}\}}\in\mathbb{L}^{1}_{\mathcal{P}}
.
In addition if there exists
Z\in\mathbb{L}^{0}_{\mathcal{P}}
such that

\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{X}}Z_{(t,x)}\;\mathbb{N}(dt,dx)\right|\right]<+\infty

and

F=F(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\emptyset})+\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{X}}Z_{(t,x)}\;\mathbb{N}(dt,dx),\;\mathbb{P}-a.s.

then for a.-e.
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{X}
,
Z_{(t,x)}=\mathcal{H}_{(t,x)}F
,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s..

Proof.

The proof is divided in two parts.

Uniqueness:
First note that contrary to Theorem
3.8
the value of the random variable
F
on the set
\mathbb{N}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{X})=0
is not uniquely defined as
\mathbb{P}[\mathbb{N}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{X})=0]=0
.
The same proof as in Theorem
3.8
gives that for a.e.
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{X}
,
\mathcal{H}_{(t,x)}F=Z_{(t,x)}
,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s..

Existence:

As
\boldsymbol{\rho}
is a non-atomic
\sigma
-finite measure on
(\mathbb{X},\mathfrak{X})
, there exists a family of sets
(\mathbf{R}_{j})_{j}
such that for any
j
,
\boldsymbol{\rho}(\mathbf{R}_{j})<+\infty
,
\mathbf{R}_{j}\subset\mathbf{R}_{j+1}
and
\cup_{j}\mathbf{R}_{j}=\mathbb{X}
. For any
j
consider

\Omega_{j}:=\left\{\boldsymbol{\omega}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\delta_{\mathbb{x}_{j}},\;\mathbb{x}_{j}\in\mathbf{R}_{j},\;j=1,\ldots,n,\;n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{+\infty\}\right\}\subset\Omega

so that
\Omega_{j}\nearrow\Omega
when
j
tends to
+\infty
.
Let
F\in L^{1}(\Omega)
. As
\mathbb{P}[\mathbb{N}(\mathbb{X})=0]=0
, once again we set
F(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\emptyset}):=0
.
Fix
j\geq 1
and consider
F_{j}:=F\textbf{1}_{\{\Omega_{j}\}}
so that
F^{j}\in L^{1}(\Omega)
. Writing
\mathbb{N}^{j}
the projection of
\mathbb{N}
on
\mathbf{R}_{j}
(that is
\mathbb{N}^{j}(A\times B):=\mathbb{N}((A\times B)\cap\mathbf{R}_{j})
) places ourselves in the framework of Theorem
3.8
and thus

F_{j}=F(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\emptyset})+\int_{\mathbf{R}_{j}}\mathcal{H}_{(t,x)}F_{j}\mathbb{N}^{j}(dt,dx),\;\mathbb{P}-a.s.

and
\mathcal{H}F_{j}\textbf{1}_{\{\mathbf{R}_{j}\}}\in\mathbb{L}^{1}_{\mathcal{P}}
.
By definition
\mathbb{N}^{j}
coincides with
\mathbb{N}
on
\mathbf{R}_{j}
and for
(t,x)
in
\mathbf{R}_{j}
we have for any
\boldsymbol{\omega}\in\Omega_{j}

\displaystyle(\mathcal{H}_{(t,x)}F_{j})(\boldsymbol{\omega})

\displaystyle=(\mathbb{D}_{(t,x)}F^{j}\circ\tau_{t})(\boldsymbol{\omega})=F^{j}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{t}+\delta_{(t,x)})-F^{j}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{t})

\displaystyle=F(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{t}+\delta_{(t,x)})\textbf{1}_{\{\boldsymbol{\omega}_{t}+\delta_{(t,x)}\in\Omega_{j}\}}-F(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{t})\textbf{1}_{\{\boldsymbol{\omega}_{t}\in\Omega_{j}\}}

\displaystyle=(\mathbb{D}_{(t,x)}F\circ\tau_{t})(\boldsymbol{\omega})\textbf{1}_{\{\boldsymbol{\omega}_{t}\in\Omega_{j}\}}=(\mathcal{H}_{(t,x)}F)(\boldsymbol{\omega})\textbf{1}_{\{\boldsymbol{\omega}_{t}\in\Omega_{j}\}}.

So
\textbf{1}_{\{\Omega_{j}\}}\mathcal{H}_{(t,x)}F_{j}=\textbf{1}_{\{\Omega_{j}\}}\mathcal{H}_{(t,x)}F

and thus

F_{j}=\int_{\mathbf{R}_{j}}\mathcal{H}_{(t,x)}F\,\mathbb{N}^{j}(dt,dx),\;\mathbb{P}-a.s..

Note also that by definition of
\mathbb{N}^{j}
,

F_{j}=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{X}}\mathcal{H}_{(t,x)}F\,\mathbb{N}^{j}(dt,dx),\;\mathbb{P}-a.s..

By monotone convergence,
F^{j}
converges to
F
in
L^{1}(\Omega)
so

\lim_{j\to+\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{X}}\mathcal{H}_{(t,x)}F\mathbb{N}^{j}(dt,dx)-\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{X}}\mathcal{H}_{(t,x)}F\mathbb{N}(dt,dx)\right|\right]=0

proving that
\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{X}}\mathcal{H}_{(t,x)}F\,\mathbb{N}(dt,dx)\in L^{1}(\Omega)
.

∎

Remark 3.10
.

For the case of point processes, the Pseudo-Clark-Ocone formula stated in Theorem
3.8
coincides with the Poisson imbedding representation (
1.1
). Indeed let

H
be a point process with predictable intensity
\lambda
defined through the thinning procedure

H_{T}=\int_{(0,T]\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}\textbf{1}_{\{\theta\leq\lambda_{t}\}}\mathbb{N}(dt,d\theta);\quad\forall T>0.

Then

H_{T}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\emptyset})=0
, and using the previous representation of
H_{T}
, the difference operator is

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}_{(t,x)}H_{T}

\displaystyle=H_{T}(\cdot+\delta_{(t,x)})-H_{T}(\cdot)

\displaystyle=\textbf{1}_{\{x\leq\lambda_{t}\}}+\int_{t}^{T}(\textbf{1}_{\{\theta\leq\lambda_{r}\circ\varepsilon^{+}_{(t,x)}\}}-\textbf{1}_{\{\theta\leq\lambda_{r}\}}){\mathbb{N}}(dr,d\theta).

Therefore
\mathbb{D}_{(t,x)}H_{T}\circ\tau_{t}=\textbf{1}_{\{x\leq\lambda_{t}\}}
which is
\mathcal{F}_{t^{-}}
-mesurable. Thus (
1.1
) re-writes as

H_{T}=H_{T}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\emptyset})+\int_{[0,T]\times[0,\infty[}\mathcal{H}_{(t,x)}H_{T}\;\mathbb{N}(dt,dx),\;\mathbb{P}-a.s..

Note that the standard Clark-Ocone formula is much more tricky to compute, since one has to compute the conditional expectation (given
\mathcal{F}_{t^{-}}
) of the integral term

\displaystyle H_{T}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}(H_{T})+\int_{[0,T]\times[0,\infty[}\left(\mathbb{E}_{t-}\left[\textbf{1}_{\{x\leq\lambda_{t}\}}+\int_{t}^{T}(\textbf{1}_{\{\theta\leq\lambda_{r}\circ\varepsilon^{+}_{(t,x)}\}}-\textbf{1}_{\{\theta\leq\lambda_{r}\}}){\mathbb{N}}(dr,d\theta)\right]\right)\tilde{\mathbb{N}}(dt,dx)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{[0,T]}\lambda_{t}dt\right)+\int_{[0,T]\times[0,\infty[}\textbf{1}_{\{x\leq\lambda_{t}\}}\tilde{\mathbb{N}}(dt,dx)

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;\;+\int_{[0,T]\times[0,\infty[}\left(\mathbb{E}_{t-}\left[\int_{t}^{T}(\textbf{1}_{\{\theta\leq\lambda_{r}\circ\varepsilon^{+}_{(t,x)}\}}-\textbf{1}_{\{\theta\leq\lambda_{r}\}}){\mathbb{N}}(dr,d\theta)\right]\right)\tilde{\mathbb{N}}(dt,dx).

The following section gathers some technical lemmata.

4
Technical lemmata

We first prove convergence results of the operator
\mathcal{H}_{(t,x)}
. More precisely:

Lemma 4.1
.

Assume
\boldsymbol{\rho}(\mathbb{X})<+\infty
. Let
r\in[1,2]
and
F\in L^{r}(\Omega)
. Assume there exists
(G_{k})_{k}\subset L^{r}(\Omega)
converging in
L^{r}(\Omega)
to
F
such that for any
k
,
\mathcal{H}G_{k}
belongs to
\mathbb{L}^{r}_{\mathcal{P}}
. Then

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\left(\int_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{X}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\mathcal{H}_{(t,x)}G_{k}-\mathcal{H}_{(t,x)}F\right|^{r}\right]dt\pi(dx)\right)^{1/r}=0.

In particular, the stochastic process
\mathcal{H}F
belongs to
\mathbb{L}^{r}_{\mathcal{P}}
and we have the following convergence in
L^{1}

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\int_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{X}}\mathcal{H}_{(t,x)}G_{k}\,\mathbb{N}(dt,dx)-\int_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{X}}\mathcal{H}_{(t,x)}F\,\mathbb{N}(dt,dx)\right|\right]=0.

Proof.

We adapt the proof of
[
LP11a
, Lemma 2.3]
and give only the main arguments. By definition of the Malliavin derivative (see Definition
2.4
) and Lemma
3.7
.

\displaystyle\left(\int_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{X}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\mathcal{H}_{(t,x)}G_{k}-\mathcal{H}_{(t,x)}F\right|^{r}\right]dt\pi(dx)\right)^{1/r}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{X}}\left|\mathbb{E}_{t-}\left[L^{T,t}\mathbb{D}_{(t,x)}G_{k}\right]-\mathbb{E}_{t-}\left[L^{T,t}\mathbb{D}_{(t,x)}F\right]\right|^{r}dt\pi(dx)\right]^{1/r}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{X}}\left|\mathbb{E}_{t-}\left[L^{T,t}(\mathbb{D}_{(t,x)}G_{k}-\mathbb{D}_{(t,x)}F)\right]\right|^{r}dt\pi(dx)\right]^{1/r}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{X}}\left|L^{T,t}(\mathbb{D}_{(t,x)}G_{k}-\mathbb{D}_{(t,x)}F)\right|^{r}dt\pi(dx)\right]^{1/r}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{X}}(L^{T,t})^{r}\left|\mathbb{D}_{(t,x)}G_{k}-\mathbb{D}_{(t,x)}F\right|^{r}dt\pi(dx)\right]^{1/r}

\displaystyle\leq C_{r}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{X}}(L^{T,t})^{r}\left|G_{k}\circ\varepsilon^{+}_{(t,x)}-F\circ\varepsilon^{+}_{(t,x)}\right|^{r}dt\pi(dx)\right]^{1/r}

\displaystyle+C_{r}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{X}}(L^{T,t})^{r}\left|G_{k}-F\right|^{r}dt\pi(dx)\right]^{1/r},

where
C_{r}>0
is a combinatorial constant depending only on
r
.
Using Mecke’s formula (see Lemma
2.9
)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{X}}(L^{T,t})^{r}\left|G_{k}\circ\varepsilon^{+}_{(t,x)}-F\circ\varepsilon^{+}_{(t,x)}\right|^{r}dt\pi(dx)\right]^{1/r}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{X}}\exp(r(T-t)\pi(\mathbb{X}))|F\circ\varepsilon^{+}_{(t,x)}-G_{k}\circ\varepsilon^{+}_{(t,x)}|^{r}\textbf{1}_{\{\mathbb{N}([t,T]\times\mathbb{X})=0\}}dt\pi(dx)\right]^{1/r}

\displaystyle\leq\exp(T\pi(\mathbb{X}))\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{X}}|F\circ\varepsilon^{+}_{(t,x)}-G_{k}\circ\varepsilon^{+}_{(t,x)}|^{r}\textbf{1}_{\{\mathbb{N}((t,T]\times\mathbb{X})=0\}}dt\pi(dx)\right]^{1/r}

\displaystyle=\exp(T\pi(\mathbb{X}))\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{X}}|F\circ\varepsilon^{+}_{(t,x)}-G_{k}\circ\varepsilon^{+}_{(t,x)}|^{r}\textbf{1}_{\{\mathbb{N}((t,T]\times\mathbb{X})=0\}}\circ\varepsilon^{+}_{(t,x)}dt\pi(dx)\right]^{1/r}

\displaystyle=\exp(T\pi(\mathbb{X}))\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{X}}|F-G_{k}|^{r}\textbf{1}_{\{\mathbb{N}((t,T]\times\mathbb{X})=0\}}\mathbb{N}(dt,dx)\right]^{1/r}

\displaystyle=\exp(T\pi(\mathbb{X}))\mathbb{E}\left[|F-G_{k}|^{r}\int_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{X}}\textbf{1}_{\{\mathbb{N}((t,T]\times\mathbb{X})=0\}}\mathbb{N}(dt,dx)\right]^{1/r}.

Fix

,
(t_{j},x_{j})\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{X}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
(as
\pi(\mathbb{X})<+\infty

\boldsymbol{\omega}([0,T]\times\mathbb{X})<+\infty

\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle\leq\exp(T\pi(\mathbb{X}))(T\pi(\mathbb{X}))^{1/r}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|G_{k}-F\right|^{r}\right]^{1/r}\to_{k\to+\infty}0.

\mathbb{L}^{r}_{\mathcal{P}}
.
Furthermore

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\int_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{X}}\mathcal{H}_{(t,x)}G_{k}\mathbb{N}(dt,dx)-\int_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{X}}\mathcal{H}_{(t,x)}F\mathbb{N}(dt,dx)\right|\right]

\displaystyle\leq(T\pi(\mathbb{X}))^{\frac{r-1}{r}}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{X}}\left|\mathcal{H}_{(t,x)}G_{k}-\mathcal{H}_{(t,x)}F\right|^{r}dt\pi(dx)\right]^{1/r}\to_{p\to+\infty}0

using Hölder inequality.

and
F:=\mathbb{I}_{n}(f_{n})

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle=\sum_{J\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}}(-1)^{n-|J|}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{n-|J|}}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{|J|}}f_{n}(\mathbb{x}_{1},\ldots,\mathbb{x}_{n})\;\mathbb{N}^{(|J|)}(d\mathbb{x}_{J})\boldsymbol{\rho}^{\otimes(n-|J|)}(d\mathbb{x}_{J^{c}})

Conclusion

This paper extends to any integrable Poisson-functional the Poisson imbedding that provides a representation of any point process (with intensity
(\lambda_{t})_{t}
) as the integral of
\textbf{1}_{\{\theta\leq\lambda_{t}\}}
with respect to an uncompensated Poisson measure
\mathbb{N}(dt,d\theta)
. More precisely, we provide for any
F\in L^{1}(\Omega)
a Pseudo-Clark-Ocone representation formula with respect to the uncompensated Poisson measure and whose integrand is fully characterized as
\mathcal{H}_{(t,x)}F=\mathbb{D}_{(t,x)}F\circ\tau_{t}
. Besides
\mathcal{H}F
is well defined in
\mathbb{L}^{1}_{\mathcal{P}}
, contrary to the standard Clark-Ocone formula which requires
F\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and whose integrand
(\mathbb{D}_{(t,x)}F)^{p}
is only defined as a limit in
L^{2}(\Omega\times\mathbb{X})
.

Lemma 5.1

[

]
, in which
X
is assumed to be bounded.
Let
X
such that
\int_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{X}}\mathbb{E}[|X_{(t,x)}|]dt\pi(dx)<+\infty
and we set
X^{k}:=-k\vee X\wedge k
for

. For any

\mathcal{P}

,

We now give a concise proof of the standard Clark-Ocone formula for any
F\in L^{2}(\Omega)
(Theorem
3.2
). In particular, following
[
Pri09
, Section 3.2]
, we make precise the definition of the integrand
\left({\mathbb{D}F}\right)^{p}
in
L^{2}(\Omega\times\mathbb{X})
in a limiting procedure and based on the continuous feature of the mapping
F\mapsto\left(\mathbb{E}_{t-}\left[\mathbb{D}_{(t,x)}F\right]\right)_{(t,x)}
together with the fact that for fixed
(t,x)
,
\mathbb{E}_{t-}\left[\mathbb{D}_{(t,x)}F\right]=\left({\mathbb{D}_{(t,x)}F}\right)^{p}\;\mathbb{P}-a.s.
.

Proof.

The chaotic expansion (Theorem
3.1
) entails that
V:=\textrm{Span}\left\{\mathbb{I}_{n}(f_{n});\quad n\in\mathbb{N},\;f_{n}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{X})\right\}
is dense in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. Let
F:=\mathbb{I}_{n}(f_{n})\in V
with
n>0
and without loss of generality
f_{n}
is assumed to be symmetric. We have that

\mathbb{D}_{(t,x)}F=n\mathbb{I}_{n-1}(f_{n}((t,x),\cdot))

and by Lemma
4.2

(\mathbb{D}_{(t,x)}F)^{p}=\mathbb{E}_{t-}[\mathbb{D}_{(t,x)}F]=n\mathbb{I}_{n-1}(f_{n}((t,x),\cdot)\textbf{1}_{\{([0,t)\times\mathbb{X})^{\otimes(n-1)}\}}(\cdot))

so that

F=\int_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{X}}\mathbb{E}_{t-}\left[\mathbb{D}_{(t,x)}F\right]\tilde{\mathbb{N}}(dt,dx),

(5.1)

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{X}}\left|\mathbb{E}_{t-}\left[\mathbb{D}_{(t,x)}F\right]\right|^{2}dt\pi(dx)\right]

\displaystyle=n^{2}\int_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{X}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\mathbb{I}_{n-1}(f_{n}((t,x),\cdot)\textbf{1}_{\{([0,t)\times\mathbb{X})^{\otimes(n-1)}\}}(\cdot))\right|^{2}\right]dt\pi(dx)

\displaystyle=n(n-1)!\int_{([0,T]\times\mathbb{X})^{n}}\left|f_{n}\right|^{2}(dt\pi(dx))^{\otimes n}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[|F|^{2}]<+\infty.

Note that by orthogonality of the operators
\mathbb{I}_{n}
the previous result extends to any element
F
on
V
with
\mathbb{E}[F]=0
, that is for any
F\in V
with
\mathbb{E}[F]=0
,

\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{X}}\left|\mathbb{E}_{t-}\left[\mathbb{D}_{(t,x)}F\right]\right|^{2}dt\pi(dx)\right]=\mathbb{E}[|F|^{2}]<+\infty.

Let the operators
U
and
\tilde{U}
defined as

U:\begin{array}[]{ll}V&\to\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\mathcal{P}}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\\
F&\mapsto\left(\left(\mathbb{E}_{t-}[\mathbb{D}_{(t,x)}F]\right)_{(t,x)};\mathbb{E}[F]^{2}\right).\end{array}

\tilde{U}:\begin{array}[]{ll}V&\to\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)\\
F&\mapsto\int_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{X}}\mathbb{E}_{t-}[\mathbb{D}_{(t,x)}F]\tilde{\mathbb{N}}(dt,dx).\end{array}

For any
F\in V
, we have proved that
F
enjoys Representation (
5.1
) and Relation (
2.2
) implies that

\|U(F)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\mathcal{P}}\times\mathbb{R}_{+})}^{2}=\|F\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2};\quad\|\tilde{U}(F)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=\mathbb{E}[F^{2}]-\mathbb{E}[F]^{2}\leq\|F\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Let
F\in L^{2}(\Omega)
. There exists
(F_{n})_{n}\subset V
such that
\lim_{n\to+\infty}\mathbb{E}[|F_{n}-F|^{2}]=0
. Using the previous isometries the sequences
(U(F_{n}))_{n}
and
(\tilde{U}(F_{n}))_{n}
are Cauchy and thus converging respectively in
L^{2}(\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\mathcal{P}}\times\mathbb{R}_{+})
and
L^{2}(\Omega)
. Let
U(F):=(U^{1}(F),U^{2}(F))
and
\tilde{U}(F))
their limits where
U^{1}
and
U^{2}
denote the two components of the limits. In addition, the
L^{2}(\Omega\times[0,T]\times\mathbb{X})
-convergence of the process
U^{1}(F_{n})
implies that
U^{1}(F)
is
\mathcal{P}
-measurable. Hence the process
t\mapsto\int_{[0,t]\times\mathbb{X}}U^{1}(F)_{(r,x)}\tilde{\mathbb{N}}(dr,dx)
is a martingale and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\int_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{X}}U^{1}(F)_{(r,x)}\tilde{\mathbb{N}}(dr,dx)\right|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{X}}|U^{1}(F)_{(r,x)}|^{2}dr\pi(dx)\right]

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{X}}|U^{1}(F_{n})_{(r,x)}|^{2}dr\pi(dx)\right]

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\mathbb{E}[F_{n}^{2}]-\mathbb{E}[F_{n}]^{2}=\mathbb{E}[F^{2}]-\mathbb{E}[F]^{2}.

Thus

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|F-\mathbb{E}[F]-\int_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{X}}U^{1}(F)_{(r,x)}\tilde{\mathbb{N}}(dr,dx)\right|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{n\to+\infty}(\mathbb{E}\left[\left|F-F_{n}\right|^{2}\right]+|\mathbb{E}[F]-\mathbb{E}[F_{n}]|)

\displaystyle+\lim_{n\to+\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\int_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{X}}U^{1}(F_{n})_{(r,x)}\tilde{\mathbb{N}}(dr,dx)-\int_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{X}}U^{1}(F)_{(r,x)}\tilde{\mathbb{N}}(dr,dx)\right|\right]

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{n\to+\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{X}}|U^{1}(F_{n})_{(r,x)}-U^{1}(F)|^{2}dr\pi(dx)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{n\to+\infty}\|U^{1}(F_{n})_{(r,x)}-U^{1}(F)\|_{L^{2}(L^{2}_{\mathcal{P}}\times\mathbb{R}_{+})}^{2}=0.

The proof is complete.
∎

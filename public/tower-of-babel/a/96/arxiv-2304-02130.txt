Swarming models with specular boundary condition and environmental noise
Razvan C. Fetecau, Hui Huang, Jinniao Qiu

Abstract
. We investigate a general class of models for swarming/self-collective behaviour in domains with boundaries. The model is expressed as a stochastic system of interacting particles subject to both reflecting boundary condition and common environmental noise. We rigorously derive its corresponding macroscopic mean-field equation, which is a new type of stochastic partial differential equation due to the presence of common noise. The approach relies on a compactness argument, in which we first establish the tightness of the empirical measures associated with the particle system and then demonstrate that the time marginal of the limit measure is a solution to the mean-field equation.



Keywords:

Mean-field limit, swarming models, tightness, weak convergence, stochastic partial differential equations

Introduction

Large systems of interacting particles are widely used in mathematical models of collective behaviour (or swarming), an area that has attracted a great deal of attention in recent years. In such models, the particles (also referred to as individuals or agents) can represent a wide variety of entities such as biological organisms (flocks of birds, schools of fish, aggregation of bacteria)
[
19
,
15
]
, nano-particles
[
29
]
, swarming robots
[
32
]
or a group of opinions
[
38
]
. Understanding the emergence of self-collective/coordinated behaviour from basic interaction principles has been a key focus of research in disciplines such as biology, engineering, physics or social sciences
[
44
,
21
,
34
]
.

Aggregation models typically consider one or several of the three basic interaction mechanisms: repulsion, attraction, and orientation (or alignment), where these interactions are assumed to act in different zones with respect to a reference individual; e.g., short-range repulsion, long-range attraction and intermediate range alignment
[
19
,
15
]
. The qualitative behaviour of the solutions and its equilibria depend entirely on the specific interaction forces (and the specific parameters) that are being considered. The various models that have been proposed and studied in recent literature have demonstrated emergence of self-organized behaviours such as flocking, milling or double-milling
[
21
,
28
,
22
,
12
]
.

Particularly relevant to our paper is the interplay between particle-based (Lagrangian) models and mean-field models with (stochastic) partial differential equation descriptions. While for the first class of models the trajectories of all individuals are being tracked, mean-field models simply govern the evolution of a population density field. There exists a vast amount of theoretical and applied research on both classes of models, as well as on the connection between the two via the mean-field limit. We refer to the review articles
[
13
,
14
]
and the references therein for expanded discussions on these aspects.

In this paper we consider both descriptions of aggregation behaviour: a system of stochastic differential equations (SDE) for the particle evolution, as well as its corresponding mean-field model, representing a statistical description of the interacting system as the number of particles approaches infinity. Specifically, we consider a class of the so called second-order aggregation models (derived from Newton’s second law) in domains with boundaries. We note that in spite of the extensive literature on aggregation models in free space, the role of boundaries in collective behaviour has remained largely unaddressed
[
5
,
47
,
11
,
26
,
25
,
23
,
24
,
37
]
. The presence of boundaries is important in many realistic physical/biological applications of swarming models, where the environment involves an obstacle or an impenetrable wall that limits the movement of individuals (e.g., tank walls in experiments on collective behaviour of fish
[
46
]
or the ground in models for locust swarming
[
45
]
).

For the classical particle system of aggregation models, which is only subject to idiosyncratic noises that are independent from one
particle to another, the effect of the idiosyncratic noises averages out, leading to the deterministic nature of the mean-field equation. In addition to such idiosyncratic noises and the specular boundary condition, this paper studies particle systems that also allow for common/environmental noises. In such case, the limiting density function satisfies a stochastic partial differential equation (SPDE) over a bounded domain, which is new to the best of our knowledge. Common environmental noises (such as temperature, light and sound) together with specular boundary conditions are intrinsic to realistic settings such as culturing bacteria. We refer to
[
4
,
9
,
18
,
16
,
30
]
for different models with common noise in the whole space.

We introduce now the stochastic aggregation model investigated in this paper. Throughout this paper, unless stated otherwise, let

\displaystyle\begin{cases}X_{t}^{i,N}=X_{0}^{i,N}+\int_{0}^{t}V_{s}^{i,N}\,\mathrm{d}s,\quad i=1,\cdots,N\,,\\[2.0pt]

The boundary we consider in this paper can be represented as the 0-level set of a signed distance function

The main objective of this paper is to investigate the mean-field limit for system (

(1.4a)

\displaystyle df=\left[(\sigma+\overline{\sigma})\triangle_{v}f-v\cdot\nabla_{x}f-\nabla_{v}\cdot((H\ast f)f)\right]\,dt-\sqrt{2\overline{\sigma}}\nabla_{v}f\,d\overline{W}_{t},\,(t,x,v)\in(0,T]\times D\times\mathbb{R}^{d},

\displaystyle\gamma(f)(t,x,v)=\gamma(f)\big(t,x,v-2(v\cdot n(x))n(x)\big),\quad(x,v)\in\partial D\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\quad t>0,

The approach in our work is based on a tightness argument which is technically different from
[

,
35
,
36
]

Our approach combines certain probabilistic and stochastic arguments with some analysis on (S)PDEs. The main result, which combines Theorems

and

Theorem 1.1
.

Very pertinent to our paper are the works of Bossy and Jabir

7
,

2.1.
Well-posedness for the microscopic particle system (
1.2
)

Generally, there are two different notions of solutions for SDEs: weak solution and strong solution. The main difference between them is that a strong solution satisfies the SDE with a
given
Wiener process on some
given
filtered probability space, whereas for weak solutions we have to choose the Wiener processes and the filtered probability space as part of the solution satisfying the SDE. We refer to
[
33
, Chapter 5]
for detailed discussions on weak and strong solutions.

For the existence and uniqueness of a strong solution to SDE (
1.2
), we need the well-posedness of a sequence of hitting times for the linear confined Langevin process.

Theorem 2.3
.

(
[
7
, Theorem 2.1]
)

Under Assumption
1
, for each
T>0
and
B=B^{i}
,
i=1,2,\dots
, there exists a unique strong solution to the following linear confined Langevin equation

(2.5)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}Y_{t}=y_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}U_{s}\,\mathrm{d}s,\quad U_{t}=u_{0}+\sqrt{2\sigma}B_{t}+\sqrt{2\overline{\sigma}}\,\overline{W}_{t}+\widetilde{K}_{t},\\[2.0pt]
\widetilde{K}_{t}=-\sum\limits_{0<s\leq t}2(U_{s^{-}}\cdot n(Y_{s}))n(Y_{s})\textbf{1}_{\{Y_{s}\in\partial D\}},\quad\forall t\in[0,T],\end{cases}

with any
(y_{0},u_{0})\in(D\times\mathbb{R}^{d})\cup(\Sigma\setminus\Sigma^{0})
. Furthermore the sequence of hitting times

(2.6)

\tau_{n}=\inf\{\tau_{n-1}<t\leq T;\,Y_{t}\in\partial D\},\mbox{ for }n\geq 1,\tau_{0}=0,

is well-defined and grows to infinity with probability 1.

Here, by saying the sequence of hitting times

is well-defined, we mean that the time-valued random variables

are stopping times (i.e, for each

, the event

In particular, it is proved in

We also note that

Remark 2.2

The original work

Proof.

\{(X^{i,N},V^{i,N})\}_{i=1}^{N}
in vectors
(\mathcal{X}^{N},\mathcal{V}^{N})
(see equation (
1.1
)) and consider

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\mathcal{X}_{t}^{N}&=\mathcal{X}_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{V}_{s}^{N}ds\\[2.0pt]
\mathcal{V}_{t}^{N}&=\mathcal{V}_{0}+\sqrt{2\sigma}\mathcal{B}_{t}^{N}+\sqrt{2\overline{\sigma}}\overline{\mathcal{W}}_{t}^{N}+\mathcal{K}_{t}^{N},~\mbox{ with }\mathcal{K}_{t}^{N}:=(K_{t}^{1,N},\dots,K_{t}^{N,N})\,,\end{cases}

where
\mathcal{B}_{t}^{N}:=\{B^{1}_{t},\dots,B^{N}_{t}\}
and
\overline{\mathcal{W}}_{t}^{N}:=\{\overline{W}_{t},\dots,\overline{W}_{t}\}
are of size
Nd\times 1
, and
for which the well-posedness is ensured by Theorem
2.3
. Next we define a
{\mathbb{R}}^{dN}
-valued process
\mathcal{H}^{N}:=(\mathcal{H}^{N}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{H}^{N}_{N})
with

(2.11)

\mathcal{H}^{N}_{i}(\mathcal{X}_{s}^{N},\mathcal{V}_{s}^{N})=\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}H(X_{s}^{i,N}-X_{s}^{j,N},V_{s}^{i,N}-V_{s}^{j,N}),

and set

(2.12)

\mathcal{Y}_{t}^{N}:=\mathcal{B}_{t}^{N}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\sigma}}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{H}^{N}(\mathcal{X}_{s}^{N},\mathcal{V}_{s}^{N})\,\mathrm{d}s.

This implies that
(\mathcal{X}_{t}^{N},\mathcal{V}_{t}^{N},\mathcal{Y}_{t}^{N})
satisfies

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\mathcal{X}_{t}^{N}&=\mathcal{X}_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{V}_{s}^{N}ds\\[2.0pt]
\mathcal{V}_{t}^{N}&=\mathcal{V}_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{H}^{N}(\mathcal{X}_{s}^{N},\mathcal{V}_{s}^{N})ds+\sqrt{2\sigma}\mathcal{Y}_{t}^{N}+\sqrt{2\overline{\sigma}}\overline{\mathcal{W}}_{t}^{N}+\mathcal{K}_{t}^{N},\end{cases}

which will have the same form as (
1.2
) if one can justify that
\mathcal{Y}_{t}^{N}
is a Wiener process. Indeed, define
(Z_{t}^{N})_{t\geq 0}
by

(2.13)

Z_{t}^{N}:=\exp\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\sigma}}\int_{0}^{t}\langle\mathcal{H}^{N}(\mathcal{X}_{s}^{N},\mathcal{V}_{s}^{N}),d\mathcal{B}_{s}^{N}\rangle-\frac{1}{4\sigma}\int_{0}^{t}|\mathcal{H}^{N}(\mathcal{X}_{s}^{N},\mathcal{V}_{s}^{N})|^{2}ds\right).

Clearly,

(2.14)

\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(\frac{1}{4\sigma}\int_{0}^{T}|\mathcal{H}^{N}(\mathcal{X}_{s}^{N},\mathcal{V}_{s}^{N})|^{2}ds\right)\right]\leq\exp(\|H\|_{\infty}^{2}TN/(4\sigma)),

which together with the classical exponential martingale theorem concludes that the process
(Z_{t}^{N})_{t\in[0,T]}
is a positive martingale with respect to
(\mathscr{F}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
and
\mathbb{E}[Z_{t}]=1
for all
t\in[0,T]
. Then by Girsanov’s Theorem
[
39
, Theorem 8.6.4]
, the processes
\mathcal{Y}_{t}^{N}
defined in (
2.12
) is a
dN
-dimensional Wiener process under the new probability measure
\mathbb{Q}
defined by

(2.15)

\mathbb{Q}(A)=\int_{A}Z_{T}^{N}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P},~\mbox{ for }A\in\mathscr{F}_{T}.

This concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 2.4
.

(Uniqueness)
Let Assumption
1
hold. Assume further that the interaction force
H
is uniformly Lipschitz or satisfies a certain kind of so-called weak monotonicity condition as in
[
41
]
. Then the particle system (
1.2
) has pathwise uniqueness and thus admits a unique strong solution; the proof is analogous to that of Remark
2.2
.

3.
Tightness of the empirical measures associated to the particle system (
1.2
)

Let
\mathcal{E}:=\mathcal{C}([0,T];\overline{D})\times{\mathbb{D}}([0,T];{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
be the sample space and we equip
\mathcal{E}
with the Skorokhod topology, so that
\mathcal{E}
is a closed subset of
{\mathbb{D}}([0,T];{\mathbb{R}}^{2d})
. Now we treat
(X_{t}^{i,N},V_{t}^{i,N})_{t\in[0,T]}
as canonical process on
\mathcal{E}
. Let
f^{N}:=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{(X^{i,N},V^{i,N})}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{E})
be the empirical measure associated to (
1.2
), which is a random measure. In the following we shall prove that
\{\mathcal{L}(f^{N})\}_{N\geq 2}
is tight by using Aldous’s Tightness criterion
[
31
, Lemma 2.2]
:

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\{X^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of random variables defined on a probability space
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{P})
and valued in
{\mathbb{D}}([0,T];{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. The sequence
\{\mathcal{L}(X^{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is tight in
{\mathbb{D}}([0,T];{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
if the following hold:

(Con1)
For all
t\in[0,T]
,
\{\mathcal{L}(X^{n}_{t})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is tight in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

(Con2)
For all
\varepsilon>0
,
\eta>0
, there exists
\delta_{0}>0
and
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
n\geq n_{0}
and for all discrete-valued
\sigma(X^{n}_{s};s\in[0,T])
-stopping times
\beta
with
0\leq\beta+\delta_{0}\leq T
, it holds that

(3.1)

\sup_{\delta\in[0,\delta_{0}]}\mathbb{P}\left(|X^{n}_{\beta+\delta}-X^{n}_{\beta}|\geq\eta\right)\leq\varepsilon\,.

Now the tightness comes from justifying
(Con1)
and
(Con2)
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Under Assumption
1
, the sequences
\{\mathcal{L}(f^{N})\}_{N\geq 2}
and
\{\mathcal{L}(X^{1,N},V^{1,N})\}_{N\geq 2}
are tight.

Proof.

As shown in Sznitman
[
42
]
, the exchangeability of particles in the system (
1.2
) induces the equivalence between the tightness of
\{\mathcal{L}(f^{N})\}_{N\geq 2}
and
\{\mathcal{L}((X^{1,N},V^{1,N}))\}_{N\geq 2}
. Next we apply Aldous’s Tightness criterion to
\{(X^{1,N},V^{1,N})\}_{N\geq 2}
, a sequence of random variables valued in
\mathcal{E}
.

We first check
(Con1)
. Applying Itô’s formula for semimartingales
[
43
, Proposition 8.19]
gives us

(3.2)

|X_{t}^{1,N}|^{2}=|X_{0}^{1}|^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}2X_{s}^{1,N}\cdot V_{s}^{1,N}\,{\rm d}s\leq|X_{0}^{1}|^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\left(|X_{s}^{1,N}|^{2}+|V_{s}^{1,N}|^{2}\right)\,{\rm d}s,

for
0\leq t\leq T
, and

\displaystyle|V_{t}^{1,N}|^{2}=|V_{0}^{1}|^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}2V_{s^{-}}^{1,N}\cdot H\ast f_{s}^{N}(X_{s}^{1,N},V_{s}^{1,N})\,{\rm d}s+\sqrt{2\sigma}\int_{0}^{t}2V_{s^{-}}^{1,N}\cdot\,\mathrm{d}B_{s}^{1}+\sqrt{2\overline{\sigma}}\int_{0}^{t}2V_{s^{-}}^{1,N}\cdot\,\mathrm{d}\overline{W}_{s}

\displaystyle+2d(\sigma+\overline{\sigma})t+\sum_{0<s\leq{t}}2V_{s^{-}}^{1,N}\cdot\Delta K_{s}^{1,N}\textbf{1}_{\{X_{s}^{1,N}\in\partial D\}}+\sum_{0<s\leq t}\left(|V_{s}^{1,N}|^{2}-|V_{s^{-}}^{1,N}|^{2}-2V_{s^{-}}^{1,N}\Delta V_{s}^{1,N}\right)\textbf{1}_{\{X_{s}^{1,N}\in\partial D\}}

(3.3)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|V_{0}^{1}|^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}|V_{s}^{1,N}|^{2}\,{\rm d}s+\sqrt{2\sigma}\int_{0}^{t}2V_{s^{-}}^{1,N}\cdot\,\mathrm{d}B_{s}^{1}++\sqrt{2\overline{\sigma}}\int_{0}^{t}2V_{s^{-}}^{1,N}\cdot\,\mathrm{d}\overline{W}_{s}+\left(\left\lVert H\,\right\rVert_{\infty}^{2}+2d(\sigma+\overline{\sigma})\right)t,

where according to (
2.9
) we have used the fact that

\sum_{0<s\leq{t}}2V_{s^{-}}^{1,N}\cdot\Delta K_{s}^{1,N}\textbf{1}_{\{X_{s}^{1,N}\in\partial D\}}=\sum_{0<s\leq{t}}2V_{s^{-}}^{1,N}\cdot\Delta V_{s}^{1,N}\textbf{1}_{\{X_{s}^{1,N}\in\partial D\}}

and

|V_{s}^{1,N}|^{2}=|V_{s^{-}}^{1,N}|^{2}\,.

Notice that the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality gives that

\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,r]}\left|\sqrt{2\sigma}\int_{0}^{t}2V_{s^{-}}^{1,N}\cdot\,\mathrm{d}B_{s}^{1}\right|^{2}\right]\leq 16\sigma\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{r}|V_{s^{-}}^{1,N}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\right],\quad\text{for }r\in[0,T],

and

\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,r]}\left|\sqrt{2\overline{\sigma}}\int_{0}^{t}2V_{s^{-}}^{1,N}\cdot\,\mathrm{d}\overline{W}_{s}\right|^{2}\right]\leq 16\overline{\sigma}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{r}|V_{s^{-}}^{1,N}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\right],\quad\text{for }r\in[0,T],

which together with estimates (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) yield that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,r]}\big(|X_{t}^{1,N}|^{2}+|V_{t}^{1,N}|^{2}\big)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[|X_{0}^{1}|^{2}+|V_{0}^{1}|^{2}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\big(|X_{s}^{1,N}|^{2}+(2+16\sigma+16\overline{\sigma})|V_{s}^{1,N}|^{2}\big)\,{\rm d}s\right]+\left(\left\lVert H\,\right\rVert_{\infty}^{2}+2d(\sigma+\overline{\sigma})\right)r.

Then, by Gronwall’s inequality, we find

\displaystyle\sup_{N\geq 2}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\big(|X_{t}^{1,N}|^{2}+|V_{t}^{1,N}|^{2}\big)\right]

\displaystyle\leq e^{(2+16\sigma+16\overline{\sigma})T}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[|X_{0}^{1}|^{2}+|V_{0}^{1}|^{2}\right]+\left(\left\lVert H\,\right\rVert_{\infty}^{2}+2d(\sigma+\overline{\sigma})\right)T\,\right)

(3.4)

\displaystyle=:C_{1},

and thus,
(Con1)
is fulfilled.

Now we check
(Con2)
. For given
N\geq 2
, let
\beta
be a
\sigma(X^{1,N},V^{1,N})
-stopping time with discrete values such that
\beta+\delta_{0}\leq T
. Then we observe that for all
\delta\in[0,\delta_{0}]
, it holds that

(3.5)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|X_{\beta+\delta}^{1,N}-X_{\beta}^{1,N}|]=\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\int_{\beta}^{\beta+\delta}V_{s}^{1,N}\mathrm{d}s\right|\right]\leq\delta\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|V_{t}^{1,N}|\right]\leq\delta\sqrt{C_{1}}\,.

Moreover, since
\mathbb{E}[|B_{\beta+\delta}^{1}-B_{\beta}^{1}|]=\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}[|B_{\beta+\delta}^{1}-B_{\beta}^{1}|\big|\mathscr{F}_{\beta}]\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}[|B^{1}_{\delta}|\big|\mathscr{F}_{0}]\right]=\mathbb{E}[|B^{1}_{\delta}|]\leq\sqrt{\delta}

it is easy to see that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|V_{\beta+\delta}^{1,N}-V_{\beta}^{1,N}|]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\int_{\beta}^{\beta+\delta}H\ast f^{N}_{s}(X_{s}^{1,N},V_{s}^{1,N})ds\right|\right]+\sqrt{2\sigma}\mathbb{E}[|B_{\beta+\delta}^{1}-B_{\beta}^{1}|]

\displaystyle\quad+\sqrt{2\overline{\sigma}}\mathbb{E}[|\overline{W}_{\beta+\delta}-\overline{W}_{\beta}|]+\mathbb{E}[|K_{\beta+\delta}^{1,N}-K_{\beta}^{1,N}|]

(3.6)

\displaystyle\leq\delta\|H\|_{\infty}+(\sqrt{2\sigma}+\sqrt{2\overline{\sigma}})\sqrt{\delta}+\mathbb{E}[|K_{\beta+\delta}^{1,N}-K_{\beta}^{1,N}|]\,.

Consider the following probability transformation:

(3.7)

\frac{\rm{d}\mathbb{Q}}{\rm{d}\mathbb{P}}=Z_{T}:=\exp\left(-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\sigma}}\int_{0}^{T}H\ast f_{s}^{N}(X_{s}^{1,N},V_{s}^{1,N})\rm{d}B^{1}_{s}-\frac{1}{4\sigma}\int_{0}^{T}\big|H\ast f_{s}^{N}(X_{s}^{1,N},V_{s}^{1,N})\big|^{2}\rm{d}s\right).

Applying Girsanov’s theorem analogously to the proof of Theorem
2.4
, we have the process
(X^{1,N},\,V^{1,N},\,K^{1,N})
under
\mathbb{Q}
and the process
(Y,\,U,\,\widetilde{K})
in (
2.5
) under
\mathbb{P}
being identically distributed. Thus, it follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|K_{\beta+\delta}^{1,N}-K_{\beta}^{1,N}\right|\right]\leq\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\left[\left|K_{\beta+\delta}^{1,N}-K_{\beta}^{1,N}\right|^{2}Z_{T}\right]\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[1/Z_{T}\right]}

(3.8)

\displaystyle\quad\leq C(T,\sigma,\|H\|_{\infty})\sqrt{\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}\left[\left|K_{\beta+\delta}^{1,N}-K_{\beta}^{1,N}\right|^{2}\right]}=C(T,\sigma,\|H\|_{\infty})\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\widetilde{K}_{\beta+\delta}-\widetilde{K}_{\beta}\right|^{2}\right]}\,.

Analogous to (
3.4
), there exists
\widetilde{C}_{1}>0
such that

(3.9)

\displaystyle\sup_{N\geq 2}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\big(|Y_{t}|^{2}+|U_{t}|^{2}\big)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\widetilde{C}_{1},

with
\widetilde{C}_{1}
being independent of
N
.
Notice that for
\tau\in\{\beta,\,\beta+\delta\}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\widetilde{K}_{\tau}\right|\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\left|-\sum_{0<s\leq\tau}2(U_{s^{-}}\cdot n(Y_{s}))n(Y_{s})\textbf{1}_{\{Y_{s}\in\partial D\}}\right|\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\left|U_{\tau}-U_{0}-\int_{0}^{\tau}\sqrt{2\sigma}B_{\tau}+\sqrt{2\overline{\sigma}}\,\overline{W}_{\tau}\right|\right]

(3.10)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(\sqrt{2\sigma}+\sqrt{2\overline{\sigma}})\sqrt{T}+2\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|U_{t}|\right]<\infty\,.

So by the Dominated Convergence Theorem and the fact that
t\to\sum_{0<s\leq t}2(U_{s^{-}}\cdot n(Y_{s}))n(Y_{s})\textbf{1}_{\{Y_{s}\in\partial D\}}
is right-continuous, one immediately gets that

(3.11)

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\to 0}\mathbb{E}[|\widetilde{K}_{\beta+\delta}-\widetilde{K}_{\beta}|]=\lim_{\delta\to 0}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|-\sum_{\beta<s\leq\beta+\delta}2(U_{s^{-}}\cdot n(Y_{s}))n(Y_{s})\textbf{1}_{\{Y_{s}\in\partial D\}}\right|\right]=0\,.

This together with (
3.8
) and (
3.6
) implies that

(3.12)

\lim_{\delta_{0}\rightarrow 0}\sup_{N\geq 2}\sup_{\delta\in[0,\delta_{0}]}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|V_{\beta+\delta}^{1,N}-V_{\beta}^{1,N}\right|\right]=0,

which combined with (
3.5
) justifies
(Con2)
by applying Chebyshev’s inequality directly.
∎

As a consequence of Theorem
3.1
, we obtain the joint tightness of
\{\mathcal{L}(f^{N},X^{N},V^{N})\}_{N\geq 2}
, which implies
the following result by Prokhorov’s theorem:

Corollary 3.2
.

For any fixed
i=1,\dots,N
, there exists a subsequence of
\{(f^{N},X^{i,N},V^{i,N})\}_{N\geq 2}
(denoted w.l.o.g. by itself) and a random triplet
(f,\overline{X}^{i},\overline{V}^{i})\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{E})\times\mathcal{C}([0,T];{\mathbb{R}}^{d})\times{\mathbb{D}}([0,T];{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
such that

(3.13)

(f^{N},X^{i,N},V^{i,N})\rightharpoonup(f,\overline{X}^{i},\overline{V}^{i})\mbox{ in law as }N\to\infty\,.

4.
Identification of the limit measure

First, for each
\psi\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}(D\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, we define the following functional on
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{E})
:

\displaystyle F_{\psi}(\nu)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathcal{E}}\psi({\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{v}}_{t})\,\nu(\mathrm{d}{\mathbf{x}},\mathrm{d}{\mathbf{v}})-\int_{\mathcal{E}}\psi({\mathbf{x}}_{0},{\mathbf{v}}_{0})\,\nu(\mathrm{d}{\mathbf{x}},\mathrm{d}{\mathbf{v}})-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{E}}\nabla_{x}\psi({\mathbf{x}}_{s},{\mathbf{v}}_{s})\cdot{\mathbf{v}}_{s}\,\nu(\mathrm{d}{\mathbf{x}},\mathrm{d}{\mathbf{v}})\,{\rm d}s

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{E}}\left(\nabla_{v}\psi\cdot H\ast\nu({\mathbf{x}}_{s},{\mathbf{v}}_{s})+(\sigma+\overline{\sigma})\Delta_{v}\psi({\mathbf{x}}_{s},{\mathbf{v}}_{s})\right)\,\nu(\mathrm{d}{\mathbf{x}},\mathrm{d}{\mathbf{v}})\,{\rm d}s

\displaystyle\quad-\sqrt{2\overline{\sigma}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{E}}\nabla_{v}\psi({\mathbf{x}}_{s},{\mathbf{v}}_{s})\,\nu(\mathrm{d}{\mathbf{x}},\mathrm{d}{\mathbf{v}})\mathrm{d}\overline{W}_{s}

\displaystyle=\int_{D\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\psi(x,v)\,\nu_{t}(\,{\rm d}x,\,{\rm d}v)-\int_{D\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\psi(x,v)\nu_{0}(\,{\rm d}x,\,{\rm d}v)-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla_{x}\psi(x,v)\cdot v\,\nu_{s}(\,{\rm d}x,\,{\rm d}v)\,{\rm d}s

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\nabla_{v}\psi\cdot H\ast\nu(x,v)+(\sigma+\overline{\sigma})\Delta_{v}\psi(x,v)\right)\nu_{s}(\,{\rm d}x,\,{\rm d}v)\,{\rm d}s

(4.1)

\displaystyle\quad-\sqrt{2\overline{\sigma}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla_{v}\psi(x,v)\,\nu_{s}(\,{\rm d}x,\,{\rm d}v)\cdot\mathrm{d}\overline{W}_{s},

for all
\nu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{E})
.
Then we have the following estimate.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let Assumption
1
hold.
For any
N\geq 2
and
T>0
, assume that
(\mathcal{X}_{t}^{N},\mathcal{V}_{t}^{N})_{t\in[0,T]}
is a solution to the particle system (
1.2
) with
f_{0}^{\otimes N}
-distributed initial data
(\mathcal{X}_{0},\mathcal{V}_{0})
. There exists a constant
C>0
depending only on
\sigma,T
, and
\|\nabla_{v}\psi\|_{\infty}
such that

\mathbb{E}\left[|F_{\psi}(f^{N})|^{2}\right]\leq\frac{C}{N}\,,

where
f^{N}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{(X^{i,N},V^{i,N})}
is the empirical measure associated with (
1.2
).

Proof.

Using the definition of
F_{\psi}
one has

\displaystyle F_{\psi}(f^{N})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\psi(X_{t}^{i,N},V_{t}^{i,N})-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\psi(X_{0}^{i,N},V_{0}^{i,N})-\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\nabla_{x}\psi(X_{s}^{i,N},V_{s}^{i,N})\cdot V_{s}^{i,N}\,{\rm d}s

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\nabla_{v}\psi(X_{s}^{i,N},V_{s}^{i,N})\cdot H\ast f^{N}(X_{s}^{i,N},V_{s}^{i,N})\,{\rm d}s-\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\sigma+\overline{\sigma})\Delta_{v}\psi(X_{s}^{i,N},V_{s}^{i,N})\,{\rm d}s

\displaystyle\quad-\sqrt{2\overline{\sigma}}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\nabla_{v}\psi(X_{s}^{i,N},V_{s}^{i,N})\cdot\mathrm{d}\overline{W}_{s}\,.

For each
i=1,\dots,N
and
\psi\in\mathcal{C}_{c}(D\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, applying Itô’s formula, it gives

\displaystyle\psi(X_{t}^{i,N},V_{t}^{i,N})

\displaystyle=\psi(X_{0}^{i,N},V_{0}^{i,N})+\int_{0}^{t}\nabla_{x}\psi(X_{s}^{i,N},V_{s}^{i,N})\cdot V_{s}^{i,N}\,{\rm d}s

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\nabla_{v}\psi(X_{s}^{i,N},V_{s}^{i,N})\cdot H\ast f_{s}^{N}(X_{s}^{i,N},V_{s}^{i,N})\,{\rm d}s

\displaystyle\quad+\sqrt{2\sigma}\int_{0}^{t}\nabla_{v}\psi(X_{s}^{i,N},V_{s}^{i,N})\cdot\mathrm{d}B_{s}^{i}+\sqrt{2\overline{\sigma}}\int_{0}^{t}\nabla_{v}\psi(X_{s}^{i,N},V_{s}^{i,N})\cdot\mathrm{d}\overline{W}_{s}

(4.2)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}(\sigma+\overline{\sigma})\Delta_{v}\psi(X_{s}^{i,N},V_{s}^{i,N})\,{\rm d}s\,.

This implies that

(4.3)

F_{\psi}(f^{N})=\sqrt{2\sigma}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\nabla_{v}\psi(X_{s}^{i,N},V_{s}^{i,N})\cdot\mathrm{d}B_{s}^{i}\,.

Then using the fact that
B^{i}
is independent of
B^{j}
when
i\neq j
it holds

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[|F_{\psi}(f^{N})|^{2}\right]=\frac{2\sigma}{N^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\nabla_{v}\psi(X_{s}^{i,N},V_{s}^{i,N})\cdot\mathrm{d}B_{s}^{i}\right|^{2}\right]

(4.4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\sigma}{N^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\int_{0}^{t}\nabla_{v}\psi(X_{s}^{i,N},V_{s}^{i,N})\cdot\mathrm{d}B_{s}^{i}\right|^{2}\right]\leq C(\sigma,T,\|\nabla_{v}\psi\|_{\infty})\frac{1}{N}\,.

This completes the proof.
∎

By Skorokhod’s lemma, using Corollary
3.2
, we may find a common probability space, w.l.o.g., still denoted by
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{P})
, on which for any fixed
i
the processes
\{(f^{N},X^{i,N},V^{i,N},B^{i},\overline{W})\}_{N\geq 2}
converge to some process
(f,\overline{X}^{i},\overline{V}^{i},B^{i},\overline{W})
as a random variable valued in
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{E})\times\mathcal{C}([0,T];{\mathbb{R}}^{d})\times{\mathbb{D}}([0,T];{\mathbb{R}}^{d})\times\mathcal{C}([0,T];{\mathbb{R}}^{d})\times\mathcal{C}([0,T];{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
almost surely. In particular, we have that for all
t\in[0,T]
, and
\phi\in\mathcal{C}_{b}(D\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

(4.5)

\displaystyle\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}|X_{t}^{i,N}-\overline{X}_{t}^{i}|+|V_{t}^{i,N}-\overline{V}_{t}^{i}|=0\quad\mbox{ a.s. }

and

(4.6)

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\iint_{D\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\phi(x,v)(f^{N}_{t}-f_{t})(\,{\rm d}x,\,{\rm d}v)=0\quad\mbox{ a.s.},

which also implies

(4.7)

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\iint_{D\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\phi(x,v)(f^{N}_{t}-f_{t})(\,{\rm d}x,\,{\rm d}v)\right|^{2}\right]=0\,.

Then our main result states that the limit process
f
satisfies the mean-field stochastic equation (
1.4
).

Theorem 4.2
.

Assume the same assumptions as in Remark
2.4
. The limit
f

is the unique solution to

(4.34)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}X_{t}^{1}=X_{0}^{1}+\int_{0}^{t}V_{s}^{1}\,{\rm d}s,\\[3.0pt]
V_{t}^{1}=V_{0}^{1}+\sqrt{2\sigma}\widetilde{B}_{t}^{1,N}+\sqrt{2\overline{\sigma}}\,\overline{W}_{t}+K_{t}^{1},\\[3.0pt]
K_{t}^{1}=-\sum\limits_{0<s\leq t}2(V_{s^{-}}^{1}\cdot n(X_{s}^{i}))n(X_{s}^{1})\textbf{1}_{\{X_{s}^{1}\in\partial D\}},\end{cases}

under the new probability measure
\mathbb{P}_{N}^{1}
. Now the interactions of particle 1 are included in the new Wiener process
\widetilde{B}^{1,N}
.

Step 2.
Recall the fact that
(\widetilde{X}^{1},\widetilde{V}^{1})
satisfies

(4.35)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\widetilde{X}_{t}^{1}=\widetilde{X}_{0}^{1}+\int_{0}^{t}\widetilde{V}_{s}^{1}\,\mathrm{d}s,\\[3.0pt]
\widetilde{V}_{t}^{1}=\widetilde{V}_{0}^{1}+\sqrt{2\sigma}B_{t}^{1}+\sqrt{2\overline{\sigma}}\overline{W}_{t}+\widetilde{K}_{t}^{1},\\[3.0pt]
\widetilde{K}_{t}^{1}=-\sum\limits_{0<s\leq t}2(\widetilde{V}_{s^{-}}^{1}\cdot n(\widetilde{X}_{s}^{i}))n(\widetilde{X}_{s}^{1})\textbf{1}_{\{\widetilde{X}_{s}^{1}\in\partial D\}},\end{cases}

under the original probability measure
\mathbb{P}
. Thus for any
\phi\in\mathcal{C}_{b}(D\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
it holds that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\phi(X_{t}^{1,N},V_{t}^{1,N})]

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\phi(X_{t}^{1,N}(\omega),V_{t}^{1,N}(\omega))\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}(\omega)=\int_{\Omega}\phi(X_{t}^{1,N}(\omega),V_{t}^{1,N}(\omega))\frac{1}{M_{T}^{1,N}}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}_{N}^{1}(\omega)

(4.36)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\phi(\widetilde{X}_{t}^{1}(\omega),\widetilde{V}_{t}^{1}(\omega))\frac{1}{\widetilde{M}_{T}^{1,N}}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}(\omega)\,,

where

(4.37)

\widetilde{M}_{t}^{1,N}:=\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\sigma}}\int_{0}^{t}H\ast\widetilde{f}^{N}_{s}(\widetilde{X}_{s}^{1},\widetilde{V}_{s}^{1})\,\mathrm{d}{B}_{s}^{1}+\frac{1}{4\sigma}\int_{0}^{t}|H\ast\widetilde{f}^{N}_{s}(\widetilde{X}_{s}^{1},\widetilde{V}_{s}^{1})|^{2}\,{\rm d}s\right\}\,.

Here we have used the fact that

\displaystyle M_{t}^{1,N}

\displaystyle=\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\sigma}}\int_{0}^{t}H\ast f^{N}_{s}(X_{s}^{1,N},V_{s}^{1,N})\,\mathrm{d}{B}_{s}^{1}-\frac{1}{4\sigma}\int_{0}^{t}|H\ast f^{N}_{s}(X_{s}^{1,N},V_{s}^{1,N})|^{2}\,{\rm d}s\right\}

(4.38)

\displaystyle=\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\sigma}}\int_{0}^{t}H\ast f^{N}_{s}(X_{s}^{1,N},V_{s}^{1,N})\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{B}_{s}^{1,N}+\frac{1}{4\sigma}\int_{0}^{t}|H\ast f^{N}_{s}(X_{s}^{1,N},V_{s}^{1,N})|^{2}\,{\rm d}s\right\}\,,

which under
\mathbb{P}_{N}^{1}
has the same distribution as
\widetilde{M}_{t}^{1,N}
under
\mathbb{P}
.

Next we notice that

\displaystyle|H\ast\widetilde{f}^{N}_{s}(\widetilde{X}_{s}^{1},\widetilde{V}_{s}^{1})-H\ast\widetilde{f}_{s}(\widetilde{X}_{s}^{1},\widetilde{V}_{s}^{1})|

(4.39)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\iint H(\widetilde{X}_{s}^{1}-x,\widetilde{V}_{s}^{1}-v)(\widetilde{f}^{N}_{s}-\widetilde{f}_{s})(\,\mathrm{d}x,\,\mathrm{d}v)\right|\to 0,\quad a.s.\mbox{ as }N\to\infty\,.

This implies that
\widetilde{M}_{T}^{1,N}\to\widetilde{M}_{T}^{1}
,
\mathbb{P}
a.s., where

(4.40)

\widetilde{M}_{t}^{1}:=\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\sigma}}\int_{0}^{t}H\ast\widetilde{f}_{s}(\widetilde{X}_{s}^{1},\widetilde{V}_{s}^{1})\,\mathrm{d}{B}_{s}^{1}+\frac{1}{4\sigma}\int_{0}^{t}|H\ast\widetilde{f}_{s}(\widetilde{X}_{s}^{1},\widetilde{V}_{s}^{1})|^{2}\,{\rm d}s\right\}\,,

which is obviously a martingale under
\mathbb{P}
. Indeed it is easy to see that

(4.41)

\displaystyle\,\mathrm{d}(\widetilde{M}_{t}^{1,N})^{-1}=(\widetilde{M}_{t}^{1,N})^{-1}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\sigma}}H\ast\widetilde{f}^{N}_{t}(\widetilde{X}_{t}^{1},\widetilde{V}_{t}^{1})\,\mathrm{d}{B}_{t}^{1})

and

(4.42)

\,\mathrm{d}(\widetilde{M}_{t}^{1})^{-1}=(\widetilde{M}_{t}^{1})^{-1}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\sigma}}H\ast\widetilde{f}_{t}(\widetilde{X}_{t}^{1},\widetilde{V}_{t}^{1})\,\mathrm{d}{B}_{t}^{1})\,.

This yields that

\displaystyle\,\mathrm{d}((\widetilde{M}_{t}^{1,N})^{-1}-(\widetilde{M}_{t}^{1})^{-1})

\displaystyle=((\widetilde{M}_{t}^{1,N})^{-1}-(\widetilde{M}_{t}^{1})^{-1})\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\sigma}}H\ast\widetilde{f}^{N}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}{B}_{t}^{1}\right)

(4.43)

\displaystyle\quad+(\widetilde{M}_{t}^{1})^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\sigma}}H\ast(\widetilde{f}^{N}_{t}-\widetilde{f}_{t})\,\mathrm{d}{B}_{t}^{1}\right)\,.

Hence it holds that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|(\widetilde{M}_{t}^{1,N})^{-1}-(\widetilde{M}_{t}^{1})^{-1}|^{2}]\leq 2\mathbb{E}[\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{2\sigma}|(\widetilde{M}_{s}^{1,N})^{-1}-(\widetilde{M}_{s}^{1})^{-1}|^{2}|H\ast\widetilde{f}^{N}_{s}|^{2}\,{\rm d}s]

(4.44)

\displaystyle\quad+2\mathbb{E}[\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{2\sigma}|(\widetilde{M}_{s}^{1})^{-1}|^{2}|H\ast(\widetilde{f}^{N}_{s}-\widetilde{f}_{s}^{N})|^{2}\,{\rm d}s]\,,

which combining with (
4
) and using Gronwall’s inequality leads to

(4.45)

\mathbb{E}[|(\widetilde{M}_{T}^{1,N})^{-1}-(\widetilde{M}_{T}^{1})^{-1}|^{2}]\to 0\quad\mbox{ as }N\to\infty\,.

Letting
N\to\infty
in (
4
) yields that

(4.46)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\phi(X_{t}^{1,N},V_{t}^{1,N})]\to\int_{\Omega}\phi(\widetilde{X}_{t}^{1}(\omega),\widetilde{V}_{t}^{1}(\omega))\frac{1}{\widetilde{M}_{T}^{1}}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}(\omega)=\mathbb{E}\left[\phi(\widetilde{X}_{t}^{1},\widetilde{V}_{t}^{1})\frac{1}{\widetilde{M}_{T}^{1}}\right]=\mathbb{E}_{\widehat{\mathbb{P}}}[\phi(\widetilde{X}_{t}^{1},\widetilde{V}_{t}^{1})]\,,

where
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}
satisfies

(4.47)

\widehat{\mathbb{P}}(A)=\int_{A}\frac{1}{\widetilde{M}_{T}^{1}}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}\quad\mbox{ for }A\in\mathscr{F}_{T},

and
(\widetilde{X}_{t}^{1},\widetilde{V}_{t}^{1})
under
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}
satisfies

(4.48)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\widetilde{X}_{t}^{1}=\widetilde{X}_{0}^{1}+\int_{0}^{t}\widetilde{V}_{s}^{1}\,\mathrm{d}s,\\[3.0pt]
\widetilde{V}_{t}^{1}=\widetilde{V}_{0}^{1}+\int_{0}^{T}H\ast\widetilde{f}_{s}(\widetilde{X}_{s}^{1},\widetilde{V}_{s}^{1})ds+\sqrt{2\sigma}\widehat{B}_{t}^{1}+\sqrt{2\overline{\sigma}}\,\overline{W}_{t}+\widetilde{K}_{t}^{1},\\[3.0pt]
\widetilde{K}_{t}^{1}=-\sum\limits_{0<s\leq t}2(\widetilde{V}_{s^{-}}^{1}\cdot n(\widetilde{X}_{s}^{1}))n(\widetilde{X}_{s}^{1})\textbf{1}_{\{\widetilde{X}_{s}^{1}\in\partial D\}}.\end{cases}

Here,

(4.49)

\widehat{B}_{t}^{1}:=B_{t}^{1}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\sigma}}\int_{0}^{t}H\ast\widetilde{f}_{s}(\widetilde{X}_{s}^{1},\widetilde{V}_{s}^{1})\,{\rm d}s

is a Wiener process under the new probability measure
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}
. Namely
(\widetilde{X}_{t}^{1},\widetilde{V}_{t}^{1},\widehat{B}_{t}^{1},\widehat{\mathbb{P}})
is a weak solution to (
4.28
).

Step 3.
On the other hand, by Corollary
3.2
we also have

(4.50)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\phi(X_{t}^{1,N},V_{t}^{1,N})]\to\mathbb{E}[\phi(\overline{X}_{t}^{1},\overline{V}_{t}^{1})],\quad\mbox{ as }N\to\infty.

This means that

(4.51)

\mathbb{E}[\phi(\overline{X}_{t}^{1},\overline{V}_{t}^{1})]=\mathbb{E}_{\widehat{\mathbb{P}}}[\phi(\widetilde{X}_{t}^{1},\widetilde{V}_{t}^{1})].

Namely,
(\overline{X}_{t}^{1},\overline{V}_{t}^{1})
under
\mathbb{P}
has the same distribution as
(\widetilde{X}_{t}^{1},\widetilde{V}_{t}^{1})
under
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}
.

In addition, one has for all
\xi\in L^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathscr{F}_{T}^{\overline{W}};{\mathbb{R}})
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\phi(\overline{X}_{t}^{1},\overline{V}_{t}^{1})\xi]

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}[\phi(X_{t}^{1,N},V_{t}^{1,N})\xi]=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}[\phi(X_{t}^{i,N},V_{t}^{i,N})\xi]

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}[\langle\phi,f^{N}_{t}\rangle\xi]=\mathbb{E}[\langle\phi,f_{t}\rangle\xi]\,,

where we have used (
4.5
)-(
4.6
) and the exchangeability of particles. Furthermore, since
f
is
(\mathscr{F}_{t}^{\overline{W}})_{t\geq 0}
-adapted, we have

(4.52)

\mathbb{E}\left[\phi(\overline{X}_{t}^{1},\overline{V}_{t}^{1})\big|\mathscr{F}_{t}^{\overline{W}}\right]=\mathbb{E}[\langle\phi,f_{t}\rangle|\mathscr{F}_{t}^{\overline{W}}]=\langle\phi,f_{t}\rangle\,,

which implies

(4.53)

f_{t}(x,v)\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}v=\mathbb{E}[(\overline{X}_{t}^{1},\overline{V}_{t}^{1})\in(\,{\rm d}x,\,{\rm d}v)|\mathscr{F}_{t}^{\overline{W}}]=\mathbb{E}_{\widehat{\mathbb{P}}}[(\widetilde{X}_{t}^{1},\widetilde{V}_{t}^{1})\in(\,{\rm d}x,\,{\rm d}v)|\mathscr{F}_{t}^{\overline{W}}]\,.

Namely
f
is the conditional density of
(\widetilde{X}_{t}^{1},\widetilde{V}_{t}^{1})
given
\mathscr{F}_{t}^{\overline{W}}
.
∎

The following result is an analogue to
[
8
, Corollary 2.3]
:

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{\mathscr{F}}(L^{1}(D\times\mathbb{R}^{d}))
be the limit process obtained in Theorem
4.2
. Then its trace function
\gamma(f)
satisfies that for all
0<t\leq T
,

(4.54)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{0<s\leq t}\left(\varphi(s,\widetilde{Y}_{s},\widetilde{U}_{s})-\varphi(s,\widetilde{Y}_{s},\widetilde{U}_{s^{-}})\right)\textbf{1}_{\{\widetilde{Y}_{s}\in\partial D\}}\big|\mathscr{F}_{t}^{\overline{W}}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad=-\int_{\Sigma_{t}}v\cdot n(x)\gamma(f)(s,x,v)\varphi(s,x,v)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda_{\Sigma_{t}}(s,x,v)\quad\mbox{a.s.}

holds for all bounded measurable functions
\varphi
on
\Sigma_{t}
, where the processes
\widetilde{Y}
and
\widetilde{U}
are from Proposition
4.3
.

Proof.

For any
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{2}\left((0,t)\times\overline{D}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\right)
, according to Definition
2.1

we have

\displaystyle-\int_{(0,t)\times\partial D\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}n(x)\cdot v\gamma(f)(s,x,v)\varphi(s,x,v)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda_{\Sigma_{t}}(s,x,v)

(4.55)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{0<\ell(x)\leq\delta}\int_{(0,t)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla\ell(x)\cdot vf_{s}(x,v)\varphi(s,x,v)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}sdv\,,

where we have also used the fact that
\nabla\ell(x)=-n(x)
when
x\in\partial D
.
For a fixed
\delta>0
, we set
\beta_{\delta}(y):=1-(\frac{\ell(y)}{\delta}\wedge 1)
a.e. differentiable on
\mathring{D}=\{y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:~\ell(y)>0\}
. Applying Itô’s formula, it gives that

\displaystyle\beta_{\delta}(\widetilde{Y}_{t})\varphi(t,\widetilde{Y}_{t},\widetilde{U}_{t})=\beta_{\delta}(\widetilde{Y}_{0})\varphi(0,Y_{0},U_{0})+\int_{0}^{t}\beta_{\delta}(\widetilde{Y}_{s})\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\varphi(s,\widetilde{Y}_{s},\widetilde{U}_{s})\,{\rm d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\beta_{\delta}(\widetilde{Y}_{s})\nabla_{x}\varphi(s,\widetilde{Y}_{s},\widetilde{U}_{s^{-}})\cdot\widetilde{U}_{s}\,{\rm d}s

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\beta_{\delta}(\widetilde{Y}_{s})\nabla_{v}\varphi(s,\widetilde{Y}_{s},\widetilde{U}_{s^{-}})\cdot H\ast\widetilde{f}_{s}(\widetilde{Y}_{s},\widetilde{U}_{s})\,{\rm d}s++\sqrt{2\sigma}\int_{0}^{t}\beta_{\delta}(\widetilde{Y}_{s})\nabla_{v}\varphi(s,\widetilde{Y}_{s},\widetilde{U}_{s^{-}})\cdot\,\mathrm{d}B_{s}

\displaystyle+\sqrt{2\overline{\sigma}}\int_{0}^{t}\beta_{\delta}(\widetilde{Y}_{s})\nabla_{v}\varphi(s,\widetilde{Y}_{s},\widetilde{U}_{s^{-}})\cdot\,\mathrm{d}\overline{W}_{s}+\sum_{0<s\leq t}\nabla_{v}\varphi(s,\widetilde{Y}_{s},\widetilde{U}_{s^{-}})\Delta\widetilde{K}_{s}\textbf{1}_{\{\widetilde{Y}_{s}\in\partial D\}}+\int_{0}^{t}(\sigma+\overline{\sigma})\beta_{\delta}(\widetilde{Y}_{s})\triangle_{v}\varphi(s,\widetilde{Y}_{s},\widetilde{U}_{s^{-}})\,{\rm d}s

\displaystyle+\sum_{0<s\leq t}\left(\varphi(s,\widetilde{Y}_{s},\widetilde{U}_{s})-\varphi(s,\widetilde{Y}_{s},\widetilde{U}_{s^{-}})-\nabla_{v}\varphi(s,\widetilde{Y}_{s},\widetilde{U}_{s^{-}})\cdot\Delta\widetilde{U}_{s}\right)\textbf{1}_{\{\widetilde{Y}_{s}\in\partial D\}}

(4.56)

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{\delta}\nabla\ell(\widetilde{Y}_{s})\cdot\widetilde{U}_{s^{-}}\varphi(s,\widetilde{Y}_{s},\widetilde{U}_{s^{-}})\textbf{1}_{\{\ell(\widetilde{Y}_{s})\leq\delta\}}\,{\rm d}s\,.

Moreover, according to (
2.9
), one has

\sum_{0<s\leq t}\nabla_{v}\varphi(s,\widetilde{Y}_{s},\widetilde{U}_{s^{-}})\cdot\Delta\widetilde{K}_{s}\textbf{1}_{\{\widetilde{Y}_{s}\in\partial D\}}=\sum_{0<s\leq t}\nabla_{v}\varphi(s,\widetilde{Y}_{s},\widetilde{U}_{s^{-}})\cdot\Delta\widetilde{U}_{s}\textbf{1}_{\{\widetilde{Y}_{s}\in\partial D\}}\,.

Thus, taking expectation on both side of (
4
), letting
\delta\to 0^{+}
(so
\beta_{\delta}\to 0
) and using (
4
), we conclude:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{0<s\leq t}\left(\varphi(s,\widetilde{Y}_{s},\widetilde{U}_{s})-\varphi(s,\widetilde{Y}_{s},\widetilde{U}_{s^{-}})\right)\textbf{1}_{\{\widetilde{Y}_{s}\in\partial D\}}\big|\mathscr{F}_{t}^{\overline{W}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}[\nabla\ell(\widetilde{Y}_{s})\cdot\widetilde{U}_{s^{-}}\varphi(s,\widetilde{Y}_{s},\widetilde{U}_{s^{-}})\textbf{1}_{\{\ell(\widetilde{Y}_{s})\leq\delta\}}\big|\mathscr{F}_{t}^{\overline{W}}]\,{\rm d}s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{0<\ell(x)\leq\delta}\int_{(0,t)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla\ell(x)\cdot vf_{s}(x,v)\varphi(s,x,v)\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}xdv

(4.57)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{(0,t)\times\partial D\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}n(x)\cdot v\gamma(f)(s,x,v)\varphi(s,x,v)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda_{\Sigma_{t}}(s,x,v)\,,

for any
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{2}\left((0,t)\times\overline{D}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\right)
. Thanks to (
2.8
), the property in (
4
) can be extended to any bounded measurable function
\varphi
on
\Sigma_{t}
.
This completes the proof.
∎

Finally we can show that the limit process
f
obtained in Theorem
4.2
satisfies the reflecting boundary condition as follows.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
f
be the limit process obtained in Theorem
4.2
. Then its trace function
\gamma(f)
satisfies

(4.58)

\gamma(f)(s,x,v)=\gamma(f)(s,x,v-2(v\cdot n(x))n(x)),\quad\,\mathrm{d}\lambda_{\Sigma_{t}}-a.e.\mbox{ on }\Sigma_{t}\quad\mbox{a.s.},

for all
t\in[0,T]
.

Proof.

In (
4.54
) choose
\varphi
of the form

\varphi(s,x,v)=\phi(s,x,v)+\phi(s,x,v-2(v\cdot n(x))n(x))\,.

with
\phi
being any bounded measurable function on
\Sigma_{t}
.
Since

\widetilde{U}_{s}=\widetilde{U}_{s^{-}}-2(\widetilde{U}_{s^{-}}\cdot n(\widetilde{Y}_{s}))n(\widetilde{Y}_{s}),\quad\widetilde{U}_{s^{-}}=\widetilde{U}_{s}-\widetilde{2}(U_{s}\cdot n(\widetilde{Y}_{s}))n(\widetilde{Y}_{s})\,,

one has

\varphi(s,\widetilde{Y}_{s},\widetilde{U}_{s})-\varphi(s,\widetilde{Y}_{s},\widetilde{U}_{s^{-}})=0\,.

Then (
4.54
) implies that

\int_{\Sigma_{t}}v\cdot n(x)\gamma(f)(s,x,v)\left(\phi(s,x,v)+\phi(s,x,v-2(v\cdot n(x))n(x))\right)\,{\rm d}s\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_{\partial D}(x)\,\mathrm{d}v=0\,.

Let us define

\overline{v}:=v-2(v\cdot n(x))n(x)\,,

then it holds that

\overline{v}\cdot n(x)=-v\cdot n(x)\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\det(D\overline{v}(v))=-1.

It follows by a change of variable that

\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\overline{v}\cdot n(x)\left(\gamma(f)(s,x,\overline{v})-\gamma(f)(s,x,\overline{v}-2(\overline{v}\cdot n(x))n(x))\right)\phi(s,x,\overline{v})\,{\rm d}s\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_{\partial D}(x)\,\mathrm{d}\overline{v}=0\quad\mbox{a.s.}\,,

which by vanishing lemma leads to (
4.58
).
∎

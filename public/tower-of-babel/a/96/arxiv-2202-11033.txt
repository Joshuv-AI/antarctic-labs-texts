Characterizing generalized axisymmetric quantum states in d × d d\times d systems
Marcel Seelbach Benkner, Jens Siewert, Otfried Gühne, Gael Sentís

Abstract
We introduce a family of highly symmetric bipartite quantum states in arbitrary dimensions. It consists of all states that are invariant under local phase rotations and local cyclic permutations of the basis. We solve the separability problem for a subspace of these states and show that a sizable part of the family is bound entangled. We also calculate some of the Schmidt numbers for the family in d = 3 d=3 , thereby characterizing the dimensionality of entanglement. Our results allow us to estimate entanglement properties of arbitrary states, as general states can be symmetrized to the considered family by local operations.



I
Introduction

Entanglement remains as one of the most significant features that distinguishes quantum theory from a classical description of our world. It also underpins a wide range of quantum information processing tasks, such as quantum key distribution
Curty et al. 2004
, quantum communication
Horodecki et al. 2009
, and quantum sensing
Pezzé and Smerzi 2009
, where it acts as a resource for increased performance. Detecting, characterizing, and quantifying the various forms of entanglement present in quantum states is therefore of high practical relevance as well as of fundamental interest
Dada et al. 2011
, shedding light onto the principles that build our physical world through the structure of possible correlations between multiple systems.

While detecting entanglement in pure states shared among two parties is trivial, there is no infallible method to decide whether an arbitrary (noisy) bipartite state is entangled
Gühne and Tóth 2009
. Beyond detecting its presence, it is also important to identify its character. For instance, entanglement can be of high dimension, which provides additional advantages such as noise resistance in entanglement distribution protocols
Ecker et al. 2019
or increased capacity of quantum communication
Mirhosseini et al. 2015
.
Consequently, considerable effort is devoted to creating such high-dimensional entangled states in experiments
Erhard et al. 2020
.

Entanglement may also be bound, that is, a form of entanglement that cannot be distilled into its purest form of maximal dimensionality
by local operations and classical communication
Horodecki et al. 1998
, which is the resource required by most standard applications in quantum information. The observation that known bound entangled states typically lie in close proximity to the separable states has led to the belief that bound entanglement is a “weak” form of entanglement, thus useless for quantum information processing. Surprisingly, though, it has been established that bound entangled states may still serve as a resource for certain tasks, e.g., for quantum key distribution and entanglement activation
Horodecki et al. 2005
;
Horodecki et al. 1999
;
Masanes 2006
. The exact relation between
distillability and dimensionality of entanglement is an actively researched open question
Sanpera et al. 2001
;
Szarek et al. 2010
;
Yang et al. 2016
;
Chen et al. 2017
;
Huber et al. 2018
.

In this paper, we explore these two features of entanglement in
families of states that obey certain symmetries.
Characterizing the entanglement properties of symmetric families has two advantages: First, the reduced number of free parameters makes an otherwise daunting task easier, and second, the symmetries that generate the family provide a simple method to obtain lower bounds on the amount of entanglement (measured either quantitatively or by its dimension) of arbitrary mixed states, by “twirling” them into the family
Sentís et al. 2016a
.
In addition, fully characterizing the set of separable states in symmetric families can even help in establishing results on entanglement theory that go beyond that subset of states. A recent example is the superadditivity of genuine multipartite entanglement
Palazuelos and Vicente 2022
, the proof of which relies on having a full characterization of separable isotropic states
Horodecki and Horodecki 1999
.
To detect the undistillability of entangled states, we resort to the positive partial transpose (PPT) criterion, as entangled states which meet the PPT criterion are known to be bound entangled
Horodecki et al. 1996
;
Peres 1996
. We also look at the Schmidt numbers of the states in the family, a paradigmatic measure of entanglement dimensionality
Terhal and Horodecki 2000
.

With these techniques at hand, we study a highly symmetric family of bipartite mixed states with local dimension
d
, introduced in Refs.
Sentís et al. 2016b
;
Baumgartner et al. 2006
;
Bertlmann and Krammer 2008a
;
Bertlmann and Krammer 2008b
;
Bertlmann and Krammer 2008c
;
Bertlmann and Krammer 2009
, fully characterizing the set of separable states in a subset of this family, and thereby providing a complete characterization of all PPT entangled states for arbitrary
d
. We then use fidelity-based Schmidt number witnesses, together with a combination of analytical and numerical methods, to further refine the characterization of the sets of states with varying Schmidt number. We show explicit results for
d=3
and
d=4
, although our methodology is extensible to higher local dimensions. Our results comprise an exceptional example of a rich family of states arising from symmetries where their entanglement properties can be described to a very high degree.

II
Basic notions: entanglement & symmetry

Throughout this paper we denote by
\varrho
a density matrix that describes a quantum state, which is shared between the two local parties Alice and Bob. We only consider scenarios where the dimensions of the local Hilbert spaces are equal and denote this dimension by
d
.
One central question in quantum information theory is whether or not a quantum state
\varrho
is entangled. It is well known that for pure quantum states one can calculate the Schmidt rank to answer this question. Pure quantum states are separable if and only if they have Schmidt rank
1
and thus can be written as
|\psi\rangle=|\psi_{A}\rangle\otimes|\psi_{B}\rangle
. Otherwise they are entangled.

Schmidt numbers:

In Ref.
Terhal and Horodecki 2000
the definition of the Schmidt rank is extended to mixed states and is called the Schmidt number. To determine if the Schmidt number of a state
\varrho
, is smaller than or equal to
K
, one asks for a decomposition of
\varrho
into pure states
|\psi_{i}\rangle
where all Schmidt ranks
\mathcal{S}(|\psi_{i}\rangle)
are smaller than or equal to
K
. To obtain the Schmidt number of
\varrho
we therefore need to solve

\mathcal{S}(\varrho):=\min\limits_{\{p_{i},|\psi_{i}\rangle\}}\max\limits_{i}\mathcal{S}(|\psi_{i}\rangle),

(1)

where
p_{i}
and
|\psi_{i}\rangle
give valid decompositions of
\varrho
. Note that if we insert a pure state
\varrho=|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|
in the above definition we end up with the Schmidt rank
\mathcal{S}(|\psi\rangle)
of that state.

We denote the states that have Schmidt numbers equal to or less than
K
by
S_{K}
.
The set of separable states, for example, can be denoted by
S_{1}
.
In Fig.
1
there is a schematic overview over these sets and the set of states with positive partial transpose, which we will now cover. Note that we depicted the PPT states inside the states with Schmidt number
2
. This is true for dimension
d=3
as it was conjectured in Ref.
Sanpera et al. 2001
and proven in Ref.
Yang et al. 2016
.

Figure 1:

Schematic view of the state space of
3\times 3
systems:
The sets of states with Schmidt number less or equal
K
are convex.
The conjecture from Ref.
Sanpera et al. 2001
that for dimension
d=3
there are no PPT states with Schmidt number
3
was proven in Ref.
Yang et al. 2016
.
S_{1}
is the set of separable states.

PPT criterion and bound entanglement:

The PPT criterion to determine if a quantum state is separable was introduced in Ref.
Peres 1996
.
In Ref.
Horodecki et al. 1996
it was even shown that for
(2\times 2)
- and
(2\times 3)
- dimensional systems the criterion is sufficient and completely solves the question whether a given state of that dimension is entangled or not. In higher dimensions states can be PPT and entangled
Horodecki 1997
.
The PPT criterion states that for a separable state
\varrho=\sum_{i,j,k,l}\varrho_{i,j,k,l}|ij\rangle\langle kl|
the partial transposition

\varrho^{T_{B}}=\sum_{i,j,k,l}\varrho_{i,l,k,j}|ij\rangle\langle kl|.

is positive semi-definite.

PPT entangled states are particularly interesting, since they are bound entangled, i.e., one cannot distill maximally entangled, pure singlet states via local operations and classical communication (LOCC) from them
Horodecki et al. 1998
. Bound entangled states were first described in Ref.
Horodecki et al. 1998
and even today there are a multitude of open problems about their properties. It is, for example, not known if there are bound entangled states with negative partial transpose
Krueger and Werner 2005
;
Horodecki et al. 2022
. Furthermore, it has been researched how these states can be used in typical quantum information theory tasks. Although they cannot be directly used for quantum teleportation, it was shown in Ref.
Horodecki et al. 1999
that they can be
activated
and then perform these tasks in conjunction with other states.

CCNR criterion:

Another important separability criterion is the computable cross norm or realignment (CCNR) criterion
Rudolph 2005
;
Chen and Wu 2003
.
For the set of linear operators in the local Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{A}
there exist local orthogonal bases
G_{k}^{A}
. The mentioned orthogonality is with respect to the Hilbert-Schmidt scalar product, i.e.,

\Tr(G_{k}^{A}G_{j}^{A})=\delta_{k,j}\quad\forall k,j\in\{1,\ldots,d^{2}\},

(2)

where
d=\text{dim}(\mathcal{H}_{A})
.
An example of a local orthogonal basis would be the generators of
{\rm SU}(d)
appropriately normalized, together with the normalized identity matrix.

Now, by performing a singular value decomposition in operator space, any density matrix
\varrho
can be written as

\varrho=\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}G_{k}^{A}\otimes G_{k}^{B}.

(3)

The CCNR criterion states that if
\varrho
is separable, then
\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}\leq 1
.

In the formulation from Ref.
Chen and Wu 2003
and Ref.
Li et al. 2011
the criterion states that separable states
\varrho
obey

\Tr\big[\sqrt{R(\varrho)^{\dagger}R(\varrho)}\big]\leq 1,

(4)

where
R(\varrho)
denotes the realigned matrix given by
R(\varrho)=\sum\limits_{i,j,k,l}\varrho_{ij,kl}|ik\rangle\langle jl|
.
Using this criterion it is often possible to prove that a state is entangled although it is PPT.

Entanglement quantification:

A frequent question in quantum information theory is how strong the entanglement between particles in a certain state is
Gühne and Tóth 2009
. Such entanglement quantification is important, because one often thinks of entanglement as a kind of useful resource for certain tasks in quantum information processing.

Here we concentrate on the entanglement measure named linear entropy
E_{\text{lin}}

Rungta et al. 2001
. For pure states it is defined as

E_{\text{lin}}(|\psi\rangle)=2[1-\Tr(\varrho_{A}^{2})],

(5)

where the second term
\Tr(\varrho_{A}^{2})
is the purity of the reduced state
\varrho_{A}=\Tr_{B}(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|)
. We extend this definition to mixed states with the convex-roof extension

\displaystyle E_{\text{lin}}(\varrho)=

\displaystyle\inf_{\{p_{k},|\psi_{k}\rangle\}}\sum_{k}p_{k}E_{\text{lin}}(|\psi_{k}\rangle\langle\psi_{k}|),

(6)

where the infimum is calculated over all decompositions of
\varrho
. A closely related entanglement measure is the concurrence, where one considers the square root of the expression in Eq. (
5
).
For two qubits there is an analytical formula for the concurrence
Wootters 1998
. For larger Hilbert spaces the computation of the convex-roof expansion is often a difficult task. In Ref.
Tóth et al. 2015
a method based on semidefinite programming was developed to compute these entanglement measures. This was utilized in Ref.
Sentís et al. 2016b
to calculate the linear entropy for a family of bound entangled qutrit states.

For a pure state
|\psi\rangle\in\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}
with
\psi_{jk}:=\langle j\>k|\psi\rangle
the formula

\displaystyle E_{\text{lin}}=\sum_{jklm}|\psi_{jk}\psi_{lm}-\psi_{jm}\psi_{lk}|^{2}

(7)

was derived for the linear entropy in Refs.
Albeverio and Fei 2001
;
Badziag et al. 2002
.

Symmetries:

Consider maps of the form

\varrho\mapsto\sum_{k}p_{k}\big(U_{k}\otimes V_{k}\big)\varrho\big(U_{k}^{\dagger}\otimes V_{k}^{\dagger}\big),

(8)

where
U_{k}
and
V_{k}
are unitaries acting on the respective local Hilbert spaces. This operator defines an LOCC protocol, since the unitaries
U_{k}
and
V_{k}
act locally and with classical communication one can arrange to apply, for example,
U_{j}\otimes V_{j}
with some probability
p_{j}
.
Since this map corresponds to an LOCC protocol, we know that it only can decrease entanglement.
The families of symmetric states we are interested in are states that are invariant under some of the above maps. Then if one has some insight into the entanglement properties for the family of symmetric states, one can obtain a lower bound for the entanglement of a general state. This is because the twirling map

\mathcal{T}(\varrho)=\int_{\mathcal{G}}\text{d}g\text{ }g\varrho g^{\dagger}

(9)

maps general states to states which are invariant under the group of symmetries
\mathcal{G}\subseteq\{U\otimes V|U,V\in\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H})\}
. Twirling can be viewed as averaging over the symmetries
\mathcal{G}
and behaves like the maps in Eq.
(
8
)
.
This discussion shows that to get insight into the entanglement of the whole state space it is a good idea to study various families of symmetric states, where each family is determined by a group of symmetries
\mathcal{G}
. Multiple families of symmetric states have already been researched. The Werner states
Werner 1989
and the isotropic states
Horodecki and Horodecki 1999
are well known examples of such families. Other families that fulfill this property and have been extensively studied include the graph-diagonal states
Hein et al. 2004
;
Kay 2010
;
Gühne 2011
and the Greenberger-Horne-Zeilinger diagonal states
Dür and Cirac 2000
;
Pittenger and Rubin 2000
;
Gühne and Seevinck 2010
;
Nagata 2009
;
Buchholz et al. 2015
.

III
Introduction of the Explored Family of States

Now we consider states with the following symmetries:

(a)

Simultaneous cyclic permutations of the basis elements of both parties, i.e.,
|ij\rangle\langle kl|\mapsto|i\oplus n,j\oplus n\rangle\langle k\oplus n,l\oplus n|
with
n\in\{1,\ldots,d-1\}
. For dimension
d=3
, exemplary matrices
U_{k},V_{k}
from Eq. (
8
) that correspond to this symmetry are

U_{1}\otimes V_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&1\\
1&0&0\\
0&1&0\end{pmatrix}\otimes\begin{pmatrix}0&0&1\\
1&0&0\\
0&1&0\end{pmatrix}.

(10)

(b)

Simultaneous local phase rotations of the form
S(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2},\ldots,\varphi_{d-1})=e^{i\sum_{j}\varphi_{j}\mathfrak{g}_{j}}\otimes e^{-i\sum_{j}\varphi_{j}\mathfrak{g}_{j}}
, where
\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{d-1}
are real parameters and
\mathfrak{g}_{j}
are the generators of SU(
d
) that are diagonal matrices. For
d=3
this yields

\displaystyle U(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})\otimes V(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})=\begin{pmatrix}e^{2\pi i\phi_{1}}&0&0\\
0&e^{2\pi i\phi_{2}}&0\\
0&0&e^{-2\pi i(\phi_{1}+\phi_{2})}\end{pmatrix}

(11)

\displaystyle\otimes\begin{pmatrix}e^{-2\pi i\phi_{1}}&0&0\\
0&e^{-2\pi i\phi_{2}}&0\\
0&0&e^{2\pi i(\phi_{1}+\phi_{2})}\end{pmatrix},

(12)

with real phases
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
. The last diagonal entry is chosen in a way that the determinant is one. This can be done, since global phases have no physical meaning.

These states were introduced in Ref.
Sentís et al. 2016b
and mainly studied for dimension
d=3
.
For a subfamiliy Sentís et al.
Sentís et al. 2016b
explicitly calculated the convex roof extension of the linear entropy as well as the concurrence. This is particularly interesting, because the family contains a sizable part of bound entangled states. The family is obtained by relaxing the symmetries from the axisymmetric states in Ref.
Eltschka and Siewert 2013
. The
3\times 3
family of bound entangled states from Horodecki et al.
Horodecki et al. 1999
are also contained in the family.

To address the components of the density matrices
\varrho^{\diamond}
that are invariant under these symmetries we use the following variables

\displaystyle\varrho^{\diamond}_{kj,kj}=:x_{(j-k\text{ mod }d)+1},

(13)

and for
k\neq j
,

\displaystyle\varrho^{\diamond}_{kk,jj}=:y_{(k-j\text{ mod }d)}.

(14)

For
d=3
we have the matrix

\varrho^{\diamond}=\begin{pNiceMatrix}.

(15)

In the Appendix, Sec.
A.1
, there is a depiction of the matrix for arbitrary dimension.

The matrix has to be Hermitian in order to still be a density matrix. Therefore the parameters
y_{i}
need to fulfill
y_{i}=y_{d-i}^{*}
. From the positive semi-definiteness it follows directly that
x_{i}\geq 0
.

We want to find further conditions for the off-diagonal parameters, so that
\varrho^{\diamond}
is positive semi-definite. For this we change the order of the basis vectors, so that the resulting matrix has a circulant block in the upper left corner and the rest of the matrix is diagonal. For
d=3
we obtain

\varrho^{\diamond}=\begin{pNiceMatrix}.

(16)

In the upper left corner there is a circulant matrix. Its eigenvalues are given by
Horn and Johnson 2012
:

\lambda_{j}=x_{1}+\sum_{k=1}^{d-1}y_{k}\omega^{jk}y_{k}
, where

\omega=\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\pi\rm i}{d}}
is the
d
-th root of unity.
Therefore, since
\lambda_{j}\geq 0
the parameters for the off-diagonal elements need to fulfill the inequality

\displaystyle\lambda_{j}=x_{1}+\sum_{k=1}^{d-1}e^{\frac{2\pi ijk}{d}}y_{k}\geq 0\quad\forall j\in\{0,\dots,d-1\}.

(17)

In the following, we show that the physical states obeying the symmetries form a polytope in the sense that every state that is contained in it
can be written as convex combination of a finite set of extremal states. These extremal states cannot be written as a non-trivial convex combination of other states in the family.
We begin by searching
for these extremal points or vertices.

First we note that states where for a
k\in\{2,\ldots,d\}
the parameter
x_{k}=\frac{1}{d}
and all the other parameters are zero are extremal.
To find all vertices with
x_{1}\neq 0
we have a closer look at the eigenvalues of the circulant block.

With the convention
y_{0}:=x_{1}
we have

\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}e^{\frac{2\pi ijk}{d}}y_{k}=\lambda_{j}\geq 0\quad\text{and }\quad\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\lambda_{j}=dy_{0}.

(18)

Therefore we have a polytope in the space of the possible eigenvalues.
Now we can apply the reverse discrete Fourier transform

y_{k}=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\lambda_{j}e^{\frac{-2\pi ijk}{d}}

(19)

to obtain the off-diagonal parameters of our family again. Since the reverse discrete Fourier transform is just a linear operation we conclude that we have a polytope with a finite set of vertices. For these new vertices we have
\lambda_{j}=dy_{0}=1
for only one
j\in\{0,\ldots,d-1\}
and the other
\lambda_{k}
are zero.
In total we have
d+d-1=2d-1
vertices. Because we have to enforce the normalization condition every state is determined by
2(d-1)
real parameters. This is due to the fact that all introduced vertices are already Hermitian.

One particular vertex corresponds to the maximally entangled state
|\phi^{+}_{d}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{i}|ii\rangle
. There,
\lambda_{0}
is maximal and the other eigenvalues are zero. The values of the off-diagonal parameters
y_{j}
are equal to
x_{1}=\frac{1}{d}
and the other
x_{k}
are zero. In later sections we will restrict ourselves to the facet, where
|\phi^{+}_{d}\rangle\langle\phi^{+}_{d}|
is the only non-diagonal vertex.

PPT and CCNR criteria for the family.

To investigate the entanglement properties of the family
\varrho^{\diamond}
we apply the CCNR and the PPT criterion. The partial transpose of a matrix like
\varrho^{\diamond}
is block diagonal with
2\times 2
blocks after changing the order of the basis.
The PPT criterion states that the determinant of these blocks is, for all
j
and
k
, positive for separable states. So, PPT states need to fulfill:

\displaystyle x_{j-k+1}\times x_{k-j+d+1}-|y_{j-k}|^{2}\geq 0

(20)

\displaystyle\forall j,k\in\{1,\ldots,d\},\quad j>k

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\sqrt{x_{j-k+1}\times x_{k-j+d+1}}\geq|y_{j-k}|

(21)

\displaystyle\forall j,k\in\{1,\ldots,d\},\quad j>k

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\sqrt{x_{i+1}\times x_{d+1-i}}\geq|y_{i}|\quad\forall i\in\{1,\ldots,d-1\}.

(22)

To search for PPT entangled states, we also calculate the CCNR criterion to detect entanglement.
The realigned matrix has non zero entries at the same places as the original density matrix
\varrho^{\diamond}
.
To calculate its eigenvalues we consider again a matrix where the basis vectors are ordered such that in the upper left corner there is a circulant matrix,
for which we have formulas for the eigenvalues. The sum of the absolute value of all the eigenvalues is

\sum\limits_{i}|\eta_{i}|=d\sum\limits_{j}|y_{j}|+\sum\limits_{j=0}^{d-1}\bigg|\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}x_{k+1}e^{\frac{2\pi ikj}{d}}\bigg|

(23)

and the CCNR criterion states that for separable states
\sum\limits_{i}|\eta_{i}|\leq 1
.
In order to obtain explicit results from Eqs. (
22
) and (
23
) we only consider certain facets of the polytope.

IV
Bloch representation of the states in the family

The goal of this section is twofold. On the one hand, we elucidate
the relation between the magic simplex
Baumgartner et al. 2006
;
Bertlmann and Krammer 2008c

and the family of axisymmetric states as well as the facet of the relaxed axisymmetric states that we will study in detail. On the other hand,
we show that there exists a straightforward operator decomposition for
these states, which is important for entanglement detection by means of the CCNR criterion.

IV.1
Axisymmetric states as subset of the magic simplex

The main ingredient in order to discuss the magic simplex is
a basis of maximally entangled states in the
(d\times d)
-dimensional
Hilbert space. Such bases can always be constructed
Vollbrecht and Werner 2000

(discussed below). The magic simplex then is the set of all convex combinations
of these basis states. It is characterized by

d^{2}-1
real parameters,
in contrast to the full state space with
d^{4}-1
parameters.

A basis of maximally entangled states can be found
by starting from the standard Bell state in
d\times d
dimensions,

\displaystyle|\phi^{+}_{d}\rangle\ =\ \frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}|jj\rangle.

(24)

By applying local unitaries to
|\phi^{+}_{d}\rangle
we can achieve a complete
basis. To this end, we introduce the unitary Weyl matrices
Z
and

X
(see, e.g., Refs.
Weyl 1927
;
Vourdas 2004
) that act on

d
-dimensional Hilbert spaces and have the
properties
Z|j\rangle=\omega^{j}|j\rangle
and
X|j\rangle=|j\oplus 1\rangle
. As mentioned before,

\omega=\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\pi\mathrm{i}}{d}}
and the addition has to be understood
modulo
d
. Then, a full orthonormal basis
\{|\phi_{kl}\rangle\}

of maximally entangled states is obtained by defining

\displaystyle|\phi_{kl}\rangle\ =\ Z^{k}\otimes X^{l}\ |\phi^{+}_{d}\rangle\ =\ \frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\omega^{jk}|j\ (j\oplus l)\rangle,

(25)

where
0\leq k,l\leq d-1
. For
k=l=0
we have

|\phi_{00}\rangle\equiv|\phi^{+}_{d}\rangle
.

The magic simplex, i.e., the convex combinations

\sum_{kl}c_{kl}|\phi_{kl}\rangle\langle\phi_{kl}|
with

0\leq c_{kl}\leq 1
and
\sum_{kl}c_{kl}=1
is still a rather complicated object.
Therefore one
may consider peculiar families of highly symmetric states that are subsets
of the magic simplex, but are described by fewer parameters. The first
family we consider here are the axisymmetric states
Eltschka et al. 2015

that generalize the isotropic states
Horodecki and Horodecki 1999
,

\displaystyle\varrho_{\mathrm{axi}}\ =\ p|\phi^{+}_{d}\rangle\langle\phi_{d}^{+}|+q\ \varrho_{0}+(1-p-q)\ \varrho_{(1)},

(26)

with
0\leq p,q\leq 1
where

\displaystyle\varrho_{0}\ =\

\displaystyle\frac{1}{d-1}\ \sum_{k=1}^{d-1}|\phi_{k0}\rangle\langle\phi_{k0}|

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\frac{1}{d-1}\sum_{k=1}^{d-1}|kk\rangle\langle kk|\ -\ \frac{1}{d(d-1)}\sum_{k\neq l}|kk\rangle\langle ll|,

(27)

and

\displaystyle\varrho_{(1)}\ =\

\displaystyle\frac{1}{d(d-1)}\ \sum_{k=0,l=1}^{d-1}|\phi_{kl}\rangle\langle\phi_{kl}|

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\frac{1}{d-1}\sum_{l=1}^{d-1}\frac{1}{d}\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}|k\ (k\oplus l)\rangle\langle k\ (k\oplus l)|.

(28)

The axisymmetric states are all the mixed states that share the symmetries
of the Bell state
|\phi^{+}_{d}\rangle
(simultaneous local phase rotations
with opposite
sign for the two parties, simultaneously exchanging the labels of the levels
of the local
d
-state systems). They are convex combinations of
the three states given above for all local dimensions
d
.
Evidently this family is a subset of the magic simplex.
It was shown
Eltschka et al. 2015
that there are no
bound entangled axisymmetric states, so in order to have such states in the family we have to lower the degree of symmetry.

It turns out that it is sufficient to relax the symmetry under arbitrary simultaneous level permutations of the two parties into a cyclic permutation symmetry in order to allow for bound entanglement. One consequence of this modification compared to the axisymmetric states is that the state
\varrho_{(1)}
in Eq. (
28
) splits up into
d-1
states
\varrho_{l}
,
l=1\ldots d-1
.
Another consequence of the symmetry relaxation is that the offdiagonal elements may have different modulus as well as complex phases, as we explained in the previous section. We restrict our attention to a subfamily of the relaxed axisymmetric states that evidently lies within the magic simplex. The peculiar facet
that we consider here is the convex hull of the standard Bell state,

|\phi^{+}_{d}\rangle
, and the states
\varrho_{l}
that result from splitting up
\varrho_{(1)}
,

\varrho^{\diamond}_{\mathrm{facet}}\ =\ p\ |\phi^{+}_{d}\rangle\langle\phi_{d}^{+}|\ +\ \sum_{l=1}^{d-1}q_{l}\ \varrho_{l},

(29)

where
p+\sum_{l}q_{l}=1
, and

\displaystyle\varrho_{l}\ =\

\displaystyle\frac{1}{d}\ \sum_{k=0}^{d-1}|\phi_{kl}\rangle\langle\phi_{kl}|

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\frac{1}{d}\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}|k\ (k\oplus l)\rangle\langle k\ (k\oplus l)|,\ \ \forall l\in\{1\ldots d-1\}.

(30)

IV.2
Bloch representation for a facet of the relaxed axisymmetric states

Because of their high symmetry it is per se interesting to study what this means for the Bloch representation of the states in the family. It will turn out particularly useful for the application of the CCNR criterion.

As a matrix basis for the Bloch representation we will choose the (unitary) displacement operators
D_{jk}
with their standard definition
Vourdas 2004
,

\displaystyle D_{jk}\ =\ Z^{j}\ X^{k}\ \omega^{-\frac{jk}{2}}.

(31)

An important rule for calculations is

X^{k}Z^{j}=Z^{j}X^{k}\omega^{-jk}
.
Again we start with the standard Bell state whose Bloch representation is given by

\displaystyle|\phi^{+}_{d}\rangle\langle\phi^{+}_{d}|\ =\ \frac{1}{d^{2}}\sum_{a,b=0}^{d-1}D_{ab}\otimes D_{ab}^{*}.

(32)

The validity of this equation is readily seen from
the general representation of the

\mathsf{SWAP}
operator valid for any orthonormal
matrix basis
\{G_{j}\},\ j=1\ldots d^{2},\Tr\big(G_{j}^{\dagger}G_{k}\big)=d\delta_{jk}
, that is,

\mathsf{SWAP}=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{j}G_{j}\otimes G_{j}^{\dagger}
, and the
well-known relation
\mathsf{SWAP}=d|\phi^{+}_{d}\rangle\langle\phi^{+}_{d}|^{T_{B}}

(cf. Ref.
Wolf 2012
).
With the definition (
25
) we obtain also the Bloch representations of the other states of the basis
\{|\phi_{kl}\rangle\}
,

\displaystyle|\phi_{kl}\rangle\langle\phi_{kl}|\ =\ \frac{1}{d^{2}}\sum_{a,b=0}^{d-1}D_{ab}\otimes D_{ab}^{*}\ \omega^{al+bk}.

(33)

It is now straightforward to determine the Bloch decomposition of the remaining extremal states of the facet, Eq. (
29
). We find

\displaystyle\varrho_{l}\ =\

\displaystyle\frac{1}{d^{2}}\ \sum_{a=0}^{d-1}\ D_{a0}\otimes D_{a0}^{*}\ \omega^{al}\ ,\ \forall l\in\{1\ldots d-1\}.

(34)

These results are remarkable because of their simplicity. The most important property is that the Bloch decomposition of the axisymmetric states is diagonal in the Weyl basis, which facilitates direct application of the CCNR criterion: It suffices to use the absolute values of the Bloch coefficients instead of the singular values of the operator Schmidt decomposition (see Sec. II).

Linear entropy as entanglement criterion.

In Ref.
Sentís et al. 2016b
a method was described to calculate the linear entropy of states that are invariant under an entanglement preserving symmetry. It can be applied as follows. The set
\{|\phi_{d}^{+}\rangle\langle\phi_{d}^{+}|,\varrho_{1},\ldots,\varrho_{d-1}\}
consists of states that span the facet we are interested in. They are invariant under the symmetries, which were used to construct these states. We can parametrize an arbitrary state as follows:

\displaystyle\Tr(\varrho\varrho_{k})=x_{k+1}\quad\forall k\in\{1,\ldots,d-1\}

\displaystyle\quad\wedge\quad

\displaystyle\Tr(\varrho|\phi_{d}^{+}\rangle\langle\phi_{d}^{+}|)=dx_{1}.

(37)

A state in the facet
\sigma_{x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}}
is uniquely determined by the parameters
x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}
. With the theorem from Ref.
Sentís et al. 2016b
we can get a formula for the linear entropy. Explicitly we have to do the following steps:

1.

Find a parametrization for all pure states in the span of
\sigma
in dependence of the facet parameters
x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}
and other parameters
\xi
. Denote these pure states by
|\psi_{\sigma}\rangle
. It follows that every decomposition of a state
\sigma
only uses pure states from
|\psi_{\sigma}\rangle
.

2.

Compute the function
\tilde{E}_{\text{lin}}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})=\min_{\xi}E_{\text{lin}}(|\psi_{\sigma}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d},\xi)\rangle)
, where
\xi
are the parameters of
|\psi_{\sigma}\rangle
that are not facet parameters.

3.

\tilde{E}_{\text{lin}}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})
is not necessarily convex. Compute its convexification
\tilde{E}^{c}_{\text{lin}}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})
.

4.

Then the identity
\tilde{E}^{c}_{\text{lin}}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})=E_{\text{lin}}(\sigma_{x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}})
holds.

In the following we construct a subset
\mathcal{K}
of the facet that includes all separable states.
This property would be fulfilled if the following were true

\tilde{E}^{c}_{\text{lin}}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})=0\Longrightarrow\sigma_{x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}}\in\mathcal{K}.

(38)

We know that the zero set of the convexified function
\tilde{E}^{c}_{\text{lin}}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})
is just the convexification of the zero set of the initial function
\tilde{E}_{\text{lin}}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})
:

\displaystyle\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}|\tilde{E}^{c}_{\text{lin}}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})=0\}

\displaystyle=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}|\tilde{E}_{\text{lin}}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})=0\}^{c}.

(39)

For now we search a set that includes all states where
\tilde{E}_{\text{lin}}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})=0
. This can be achieved by finding the states where
\tilde{E}_{\text{lin}}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})>0
, and considering all other states.

Pure states that build a decomposition of a state in the facet are of the form:

|\psi_{\sigma}\rangle=\sqrt{dx_{1}}|\phi_{d}^{+}\rangle+\sum_{k=1}^{d-1}\sqrt{dx_{k+1}}\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\xi_{k,j}|j\;(j\oplus k)\rangle,

(40)

where
\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}|\xi_{k,j}|^{2}=1
and
\xi_{j,k}\in\mathbb{C}
for all
k\in\{1,\ldots,d-1\}\quad
.

Now by using Eq. (
7
) we get the following formula:

\tilde{E}_{\text{lin}}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})=\min_{\xi}\sum_{jklm}|\psi_{jk}\psi_{lm}-\psi_{jm}\psi_{lk}|^{2},

(41)

where
\psi_{lm}:=|\psi(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d},\xi)\rangle_{lm}
.
The minimization over the complex phases is simple. For two complex numbers
r_{1}e^{i\phi_{1}}
and
r_{2}e^{i\phi_{2}}
, the minimum of
|r_{1}e^{i\phi_{1}}+r_{2}e^{i\phi_{2}}|^{2}
is achieved for
\phi_{1}=\phi_{2}+\pi
. Therefore our minimization problem yields the same value if we assume all coefficients
\xi_{k,j}
to be real.
This allows us to conclude that, for
\tilde{E}_{\text{lin}}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})
to be zero, there has to exist a
\xi
so that

\displaystyle|\psi_{jk}\psi_{lm}-\psi_{jm}\psi_{lk}|^{2}=0\quad\forall j,k,l,m\in\{0,\ldots,d-1\}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\psi_{jk}\psi_{lm}=\psi_{jm}\psi_{lk}\quad\forall j,k,l,m\in\{0,\ldots,d-1\}.

(42)

An implication of Eq. (
42
) is the following:

\prod_{l=1}^{d}\psi_{ll}=\prod_{l=1}^{d}\psi_{l\text{ }l\oplus m}\quad\forall m\in\{0,\ldots,d-1\}.

(43)

For example, for
d=3
,
\psi_{00}\psi_{11}\psi_{22}=\psi_{01}\psi_{10}\psi_{22}=\psi_{01}\psi_{12}\psi_{20}
.
Inserting the state coefficients from Eq. (
40
), we obtain

\left(\sqrt{x_{1}}\right)^{d}=\left(\sqrt{dx_{k+1}}\right)^{d}\prod_{j=0}^{d-1}\xi_{k,j}\leq\left(\sqrt{x_{k+1}}\right)^{d}.

(44)

Here the inequality
\prod_{j=0}^{d-1}\xi_{k,j}\leq\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\right)^{d}
was used

Consider the optimisation problem:

\scriptstyle\max_{(a_{1},\ldots,a_{d})\in\mathbb{R_{+}}^{d}}{\Pi}_{i=1}^{d}a_{i}\hskip 8.92513pt\text{subject to}\hskip 8.92513pt{\Sigma}_{i}a_{i}^{2}=1.

For

the solution is straightforward. For higher dimensions one can solve this iteratively. The geometric interpretation is that the hyperrectangle with largest volume that is contained in a hypersphere is a hypersquare.

.

Since squaring and taking the

) cannot be true (regardless of

. But since the inequalities (

we get the true statement

Therefore we know that the set of states where the coefficients have the property

includes all the states with facet parameters so that

\varrho_{\text{sep}},\varrho_{1},\ldots,\varrho_{d-1}
. But since we showed at the beginning that
\varrho_{\text{sep}}
is separable, the converse is also true and we know that all states in the polytope spanned by

PPT and CCNR criteria for the facet.

From Eq. (
22

\sqrt{x_{i+1}\times x_{d+1-i}}\geq|x_{1}|\quad\forall i\in\{1,\ldots,d-1\}.

(46)

Next we look at the CCNR criterion given in Eq. (

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i}|\lambda_{i}|

\displaystyle=d(d-1)x_{1}+\sum\limits_{j=0}^{d-1}\bigg|\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}x_{k+1}e^{\frac{2\pi ikj}{d}}\bigg|.

(47)

Applying the triangle inequality yields:

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i}|\lambda_{i}|

\displaystyle\geq d(d-1)x_{1}+|\sum\limits_{j=0}^{d-1}\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}x_{k+1}e^{\frac{2\pi ikj}{d}}|

\displaystyle=d(d-1)x_{1}+|dx_{1}+\sum_{k=1}^{d-1}x_{k+1}\frac{1-e^{\frac{2\pi ikd}{d}}}{1-e^{\frac{2\pi ik}{d}}}|

\displaystyle=d(d-1)x_{1}+|dx_{1}+\sum_{k=1}^{d-1}x_{k+1}\frac{1-1}{1-e^{\frac{2\pi ik}{d}}}|

\displaystyle=d(d-1)x_{1}+|dx_{1}|

\displaystyle=d^{2}x_{1}.

(48)

Therefore we know that if

the state is entangled according to the CCNR criterion. By its negation, separability implies that
x_{1}\leq\frac{1}{d^{2}}

We now give an example for some PPT entangled states with

\displaystyle x_{1}\leq\frac{1}{d^{2}},\quad\beta\in[0,x_{1}],\quad l\in\{2,\ldots,\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor\}\quad

\displaystyle\forall k\in\{2,\ldots,d\}\setminus\{l,d-l+2\}

\displaystyle\quad x_{k}=x_{1},\quad x_{l}=x_{1}-\beta,\quad x_{d+2-l}=\frac{1}{d}-(d-1)x_{1}+\beta.

(49)

We calculate the CCNR criterion for these states:

\displaystyle\sum|\eta_{i}|

\displaystyle=d(d-1)x_{1}+\frac{1}{d}+\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}|\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}x_{k}e^{\frac{2\pi ijk}{d}}|

\displaystyle=d(d-1)x_{1}+\frac{1}{d}+\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}|-\beta e^{\frac{2\pi ijl}{d}}+(\frac{1}{d}-dx_{1}+\beta)e^{\frac{2\pi ij(d+2-l)}{d}}|

\displaystyle=d(d-1)x_{1}+\frac{1}{d}+\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}|e^{\frac{4\pi ij(1-l)}{d}}(\frac{1}{d}-dx_{1}+\beta)-\beta|

\displaystyle\geq d(d-1)x_{1}+\frac{1}{d}+\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}||e^{\frac{4\pi ij(1-l)}{d}}(\frac{1}{d}-dx_{1}+\beta)|-\beta|

\displaystyle=d(d-1)x_{1}+\frac{1}{d}+(d-1)((\frac{1}{d}-dx_{1}+\beta)-\beta)

\displaystyle=d(d-1)x_{1}+\frac{1}{d}+(d-1)(\frac{1}{d}-dx_{1})=1,

(50)

where we applied the reverse triangle inequality.
Since

is not equal to 1 for all

\displaystyle(x_{1}-\beta)(\frac{1}{d}-dx_{1})-\beta^{2}

VI
Characterizing the dimensionality of entanglement

VI.0.1
Calculation of Schmidt numbers

To get some insight into the dimensionality of entanglement of our states we want to calculate their Schmidt numbers.
In analogy to entanglement witnesses, Schmidt number witnesses play an important role in calculating Schmidt numbers.
In Ref.
Terhal and Horodecki 2000
Schmidt number witnesses were introduced.
An observable
W
is called a Schmidt witness of class
K\in\mathbb{N}
if and only if
\Tr(W\rho)\geq 0
for all
\rho
with Schmidt number smaller than
K
. One also requires that at least one state with Schmidt number
K
is detected, i.e., fulfills
\Tr(W\rho)<0
.
A
K
-Schmidt witness
W_{1}
is called finer than a
K
-Schmidt witness
W_{2}
if
W_{1}
detects the same states as
W_{2}
, and some states in addition.

It was proven in Ref.
Terhal and Horodecki 2000
that
W=\frac{K-1}{d}\mathbb{I}-|\phi_{d}^{+}\rangle\langle\phi_{d}^{+}|
is an optimal
K
-Schmidt witness. We calculate the states in the facet that are detected by this witness:

\displaystyle\Tr(W\varrho)

\displaystyle=\Tr\Big[\Big(\frac{K-1}{d}\mathbb{I}-|\phi_{d}^{+}\rangle\langle\phi_{d}^{+}|\Big)\Big(a|\phi_{d}^{+}\rangle\langle\phi_{d}^{+}|

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{d-1}b_{k}\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}|j\;(j\oplus k)\rangle\langle j\;(j\oplus k)|\Big)\Big]

\displaystyle=\frac{K-1}{d}-\Tr(a|\phi_{d}^{+}\rangle\langle\phi_{d}^{+}|)

\displaystyle=\frac{K-1}{d}-a.

(53)

Since we are interested in the separable states, which have Schmidt number one, we look at the case
K=2
.
This yields

\displaystyle\Tr(W\varrho)

\displaystyle\geq 0\quad\Leftrightarrow\frac{1}{d}-a\geq 0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\frac{1}{d}

\displaystyle\geq dx_{1}\quad\Leftrightarrow\frac{1}{d^{2}}\geq x_{1},

(54)

which is the same bound as the one we already derived above
in Eq. (
48
).

We will now describe a strategy to approximate the border between states with maximal Schmidt number and states with less than maximal Schmidt number. First one searches pure states that do not have full Schmidt rank and are twirled to the facet. Then the resulting
states after the twirling operation cannot have full Schmidt number either since twirling is an LOCC map.

Therefore we start by looking at the full Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{H}
and consider how the pure states are mapped by the twirling operator. Let
\sigma\in\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{T}\sigma=\varrho
. Then,

\displaystyle\varrho_{i\text{ }i\oplus j,i\text{ }i\oplus j}=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{k}\sigma_{k\text{ }k\oplus j,k\text{ }k\oplus j}\quad\forall i,j\in\{0,\ldots,d-1\}

(55)

\displaystyle\varrho_{i\text{ }i,i\oplus j\text{ }i\oplus j}=\frac{1}{2d}\sum_{k}\left(\sigma_{k\text{ }k,k\oplus j\text{ }k\oplus j}+\sigma_{k\oplus j\text{ }k\oplus j,k\text{ }k}\right).

(56)

If the twirling operator maps the state
\sigma
to the state
\varrho
, then we know that the Schmidt number of
\sigma
is greater than or equal to the Schmidt number of
\varrho
, since twirling is an LOCC map. For simplicity, we investigate the problem for dimension
d=3
. In Ref.
Sentís et al. 2016b
we have the following parametrization of the pure states that are mapped in the span of
\rho^{\diamond}

\displaystyle|\psi_{\sigma}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sqrt{z}|\phi_{d}^{+}\rangle+\sqrt{1-z}[\sqrt{\frac{1+\overline{r}}{2}}(a|01\rangle+b|12\rangle

\displaystyle+c|20\rangle)+\sqrt{\frac{1-\overline{r}}{2}}(e|02\rangle+f|10\rangle+g|21\rangle)].

(57)

Here
z
is a facet parameter with respect to
|\phi_{d}^{+}\rangle
,
\overline{r}\in[-1,1]
, and the complex parameters
a
,
b
,
c
,

,

and

fulfill the normalization conditions

|a|^{2}+|b|^{2}+|c|^{2}=1
and
|e|^{2}+|f|^{2}+|g|^{2}=1
. The connection to the previously used parametrization is

\displaystyle x_{1}=\frac{z}{3},

(58)

\displaystyle x_{2}=\frac{(1-z)(1+\overline{r})}{6},

(59)

\displaystyle x_{3}=\frac{(1-z)(1-\overline{r})}{6}.

(60)

In the Appendix, Sec.
A.4

by choosing the parameters

VII
Conclusion

In summary, we have discussed the entanglement properties
of a family of highly symmetric, bipartite, mixed quantum
states in arbitrary dimensions, which can be seen as a generalization of axisymmetric states. We were able to solve
the separability problem for a subfamily, including the
characterization of bound entanglement, and characterized
also the dimensionality of entanglement for some cases.

There are several directions in which our work may be extended.
First, one may study bipartite quantum states with the given symmetry further. Here, it would be desirable to find bound
entangled states far away from the border of separability or
to determine entanglement monotones, such as the entanglement
of formation for the considered family of states.
In addition, the symmetric bound entangled states in our family, defined in arbitrary dimensions, comprise a promising testbed for finding simpler counterexamples to the Peres conjecture, which stated that bound entangled states cannot display nonlocality. The known counterexamples to date
Vértesi and Brunner 2014
;
Moroder et al. 2014
;
Yu and Oh 2017
are constructed
ad hoc
, and are not defined through symmetries. Finding counterexamples that arise more naturally, as our family of states, could shed more light on whether the original statement by Peres is true except for specially handcrafted examples or it is more generally false.
Finally, one
may consider the multipartite scenario, where states with the
given symmetries can also be defined in a straightforward manner.
Then, it would be useful to discuss the different classes of
multiparticle entanglement in this scenario.

Abstract
The observation of a 266.94 GHz feature in the Venus spectrum has been attributed to PH 3 \text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}} in the Venus clouds, suggesting unexpected geological, chemical or even biological processes. Since both PH 3 \text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}} and SO 2 \text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} are spectrally active near 266.94 GHz, the contribution to this line from SO 2 \text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} must be determined before it can be attributed, in whole or part, to PH 3 \text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}} . An undetected SO 2 \text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} reference line, interpreted as an unexpectedly low SO 2 \text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} abundance, suggested that the 266.94 GHz feature could be attributed primarily to PH 3 \text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}} . However, the low SO 2 \text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} and the inference that PH 3 \text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}} was in the cloud deck posed an apparent contradiction. Here we use a radiative transfer model to analyze the PH 3 \text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}} discovery, and explore the detectability of different vertical distributions of PH 3 \text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}} and SO 2 \text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} . We find that the 266.94 GHz line does not originate in the clouds, but above 80 km in the Venus mesosphere. This level of line formation is inconsistent with chemical modeling that assumes generation of PH 3 \text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}} in the Venus clouds. Given the extremely short chemical lifetime of PH 3 \text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}} in the Venus mesosphere, an implausibly high source flux would be needed to maintain the observed value of 20 ± \pm 10 ppb. We find that typical Venus SO 2 \text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} vertical distributions and abundances fit the JCMT 266.94 GHz feature, and the resulting SO 2 \text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} reference line at 267.54 GHz would have remained undetectable in the ALMA data due to line dilution. We conclude that nominal mesospheric SO 2 \text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} is a more plausible explanation for the JCMT and ALMA data than PH 3 \text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}} .



I
Introduction

Greaves et al. 2020a
recently attributed a 266.94 GHz (1.123 mm) line observed in the Venus spectrum to
\sim
20 ppb of phosphine (
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
) absorbing above 56 km altitude, in the upper clouds. In the strongly-oxidizing Venus atmosphere,
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
formation is disfavored and its destruction is enhanced, leading
Greaves et al. 2020a
to argue that its presence in the clouds points to unknown geological, chemical or even biological processes. The discovery team identified no viable abiotic production mechanism for
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
in the Venus atmosphere
(
Greaves et al. 2020a
;
Bains et al. 2020
)
, and so a biological origin was considered.
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
has been proposed as a potential biosignature in terrestrial planet atmospheres
(
Sousa-Silva et al. 2020
)
due to its association with decaying organic matter
(
Glindemann et al. 2005
)
, and significant—presumed biological—fluxes from marine environments on Earth
(
Zhu et al. 2007
)
. However, the specific mode of biological production of
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
remains uncertain and is still vigorously debated
(
Roels & Verstraete 2001
)
, with no known direct metabolic pathway
(
Roels et al. 2005
)
.

The identification of
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
in the Venus clouds was made using multiple observations of a single spectral feature at 266.94 GHz, where both
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
(266.944 GHz) and
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
(266.943 GHz) have absorption lines
(
Greaves et al. 2020a
)
. After the initial detection using coadded spectra from the James Clark Maxwell Telescope (JCMT), which were taken over 5 nights between 2017 June 9–16, follow-up observations were made with the Atacama Large Millimeter Array (ALMA) on 2019 March 5. The latter dataset included simultaneous narrow-band (0.1171875 GHz) and wide-band (1.875 GHz) observations, centered on the Venus rest-frame
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
frequency. The 266.94 GHz line, seen in the JCMT data at a S/N of 4.3 (
Greaves et al. 2020a
; although this detection significance has been subsequently called into question,
Thompson 2020
), was also detected in the ALMA narrowband and wideband datasets at higher significance than in the JCMT data
(
Greaves et al. 2020a
)
, although a subsequent reanalysis of the ALMA data also suggests a less significant detection, with a correspondingly lower inferred abundance of
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}

(
Greaves et al. 2020b
)
. Assuming a uniform mixing ratio for the
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
,
Greaves et al. 2020a
derive an abundance of 20 ppb from the JCMT observations, and calculate an emission weighting function peaked at 56 km. They therefore conclude that the
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
absorption feature was sourced primarily from within the Venus clouds. However, as
Greaves et al. 2020a
point out, with a FWHM of 4–5 km s
-1
, this line could potentially contain contributions from both
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
and
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
, as the
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
line center is only +1.3 km s
-1
from the
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
line center.

Consequently, the
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
line identification is strongly dependent on accurately estimating and excluding a potentially-significant contribution from
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
, which, after the bulk atmospheric gases
\text{CO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
and
\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
, is the third most abundant gas in the Venus atmosphere.
Greaves et al. 2020a
attempted to quantify the
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
contribution to the observed 266.94 GHz feature by searching the ALMA wide-band observations for the nearby, stronger
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
, based on potentially large spectral “ripples”, artifacts in the data induced by interferometric response to Venus as a bright, extended source.

Because the non-detection of
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
by
Greaves et al. 2020a
supports a corresponding low inferred abundance, and a low contamination fraction for the 266.94 GHz line, it is the key piece of evidence supporting the
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
line identification at 266.94 GHz— and so it warrants closer scrutiny. There is an apparent contradiction between the inferred altitudes that the
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
feature probed, and the
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
abundance constraint. If the putative
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
(266.94 GHz) absorption is sensitive to altitudes near 56 km, and thus probes the Venus middle and upper cloud, then the 267.94 GHz
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
reference line should also originate from this altitude range, since it has similar line strength and amount of underlying continuum absorption. Data and modeling estimates place the
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
abundance near 1–5 ppm at 60 km in the upper cloud, which should increase with depth to match the higher
\sim
130 ppm measured below the cloud deck
(
Zasova et al. 1993
;
Krasnopolsky 2012
;
Zhang et al. 2012
;
Belyaev et al. 2012
;
Marcq et al. 2008
;
Arney et al. 2014
;
Encrenaz et al. 2019
)
. Previous measurements therefore suggest that the inferred disk-averaged
<10
ppb of
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
is anomalously low, especially if the observations probe within the clouds. Assuming similar spatial distribution of the two gases, for an inferred
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
abundance at 56 km of 10ppm, and the 10 ppb
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
abundance of
Greaves et al. 2020a
, the
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
contribution to the observed line would exceed that from
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
by two orders of magnitude
(
Krasnopolsky 2020
)
.

If the observations were instead sensitive to the mesospheric levels above the clouds, as is the case for higher frequency ALMA observations
(
Sandor et al. 2010
;
Encrenaz et al. 2015
)
, then the inferred Venus
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
abundance would be closer to, but still lower than previously measured levels
(
Sandor et al. 2010
;
Encrenaz et al. 2015
;
Piccialli et al. 2017
;
Vandaele et al. 2017
)
. While the abundance of
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
above the clouds is known to vary significantly over time
(
Esposito et al. 1988
;
Encrenaz et al. 2012
;
Encrenaz et al. 2019
)
with a minimum observed around 10-100 ppb at
\sim
80 km, the abundances in the mesosphere have been measured to be in the range 10 ppb to 10 ppm
(
Krasnopolsky 2010
;
Belyaev et al. 2012
;
Vandaele et al. 2017
)
. A planet-wide decrease from a higher cloud-top
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
abundance in 2006 to a low in 2014 of 30 ppb was also observed, but more recent observations from 2016 through September 2018, which span the
Greaves et al. 2020a
JCMT observation, show a strong increase to typical cloud-top values of several hundred ppb of
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

(
Encrenaz et al. 2019
)
.

While line absorption occurring predominantly within the mesosphere would make the non-detection and inferred low abundance of
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
more plausible, it would also suggest that the line attributed to
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
was formed at mesospheric levels. Consequently, the 266.94 GHz line would not be sensitive to, and so not able to confirm, the presence of
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
in the Venus clouds—potentially weakening support for a biological origin. The presence of 20 ppb of mesospheric
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
would require an extremely large source flux due to photolysis and reactions with radical species, including Cl and H, that result in a sub-second lifetime for
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
in the Venus mesosphere
(
Bains et al. 2020
, their Fig. 2)
. Indeed, the vertical distribution predicted using photochemical-kinetics studies with a cloud source of
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
indicates a sharply reduced mesospheric abundance of
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
(
<0.001
ppb) alongside significant (
>100
ppb below 95 km)
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
(
Greaves et al. 2020a
, extended data figure 9;
Bains et al. 2020
).

To explore the potential contradictions posed by the
Greaves et al. 2020a

\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
observations, and to verify the source region for the 266.94 GHz absorption, here we use a radiative transfer model of the Venus atmosphere to simulate the impact on the Venus millimeter-wavelength spectrum of different abundances and vertical distributions of
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
and
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
, including those proposed by
Greaves et al. 2020a
and
Bains et al. 2020
.

Methods

To better visualize individual line signal and compare to the published data, we processed our flux spectra to normalize the continuum. Because we are processing noiseless model results, we mask spectral intervals for individual lines and linearly interpolate the continuum across the interval. The line:continuum (l:c) spectra were determined by dividing the original model spectrum by the continuum and subtracting one.

IV
Discussion

The claim that
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
has been detected in the Venus clouds is currently supported by observations of a single absorption line at a frequency that also coincides with absorption from
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
, a known and relatively common Venus gas, and based on an emission weighting function that peaks at 56 km
(
Greaves et al. 2020a
)
. However, our radiative transfer analysis indicates that the line at 266.94 GHz does not measure absorption within the Venus clouds. Our explicit calculation of radiance Jacobians confirms the assessment that both 266.94 GHz
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
and
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
line core absorption would be produced well above the Venus cloud deck at altitudes exceeding 80 km. Arguments for a mesospheric origin for the 266.94 GHz line core, based on the observed narrow width of the line, are also provided in a recent commentary by
Villanueva et al. 2020
. This mesospheric contribution is inconsistent with a vertical abundance profile that concentrates
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
in the middle and upper clouds, as used by
Greaves et al. 2020a
and
Bains et al. 2020
to interpret their discovery. Our spectral simulation using this photochemical
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
profile also shows that it is not consistent with the strength of the observed 266.94 GHz line. However, the presence of
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
in the Venus clouds is not conclusively ruled out either, a point also made by
Greaves et al. 2020b
, because the
Greaves et al. 2020a
observations are not sensitive to absorption at cloud deck altitudes, and so can neither exclude, nor confirm, the presence of
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
in the Venus clouds.

Given that we have shown that the observed 266.94 GHz line predominantly originates high in the mesosphere, attributing it to
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
is less chemically plausible than
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
. At these higher altitudes (
>
80 km)
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
would be destroyed rapidly, while
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
is photochemically regenerated
(
Sandor et al. 2010
;
Belyaev et al. 2012
;
Zhang et al. 2012
)
. Between 82 km and 96 km (70–300 Pa, where the line core absorption originates, Fig.
4
)
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
has a sub-second lifetime, due to the destruction by Cl and H radicals and UV photolysis
(
Bains et al. 2020
)
. To balance this rapid destruction rate and maintain a mesospheric concentration of 20 ppb, an extremely large flux of
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
is required, potentially as large as
3.7\times 10^{15}
molecules cm
-2
s
-1
. For comparison, this production rate is about
\sim
100 times the flux of
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
produced by Earth’s global photosynthetic biosphere
(
Field et al. 1998
)
, the dominant metabolism on our planet.
Greaves et al. 2020a
, assuming the 266.94 GHz absorption was from
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
in the clouds, calculated a significantly smaller production rate of
10^{7}
molecules cm
-2
s
-1
, due to the lower destruction rate within the clouds. However, the assumption of this in-cloud production rate results in a
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
mixing ratio that effectively falls to zero at
>
80 km altitude
(
Greaves et al. 2020a
, Fig. 5b)
, which is inconsistent with our analysis that the observed line is sourced in the mesosphere. Although a recent reanalysis of the ALMA data by
Greaves et al. 2020b
has greatly reduced the significance of the 266.94 GHz line detection, their assignment of 1 ppb of
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
in the mesosphere would still require a production rate significantly higher than the Earth’s photosynthetic biosphere, and the larger 20 ppb
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
value inferred from the JCMT data still stands.

These challenges to mesospheric production rate are not relevant if the observed 266.94 GHz line is instead attributed to
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
, which is known to increase in abundance with altitude in the mesosphere
(
Belyaev et al. 2012
;
Mills et al. 2018
)
. A combination of infrared observations that probe the upper cloud and lower mesosphere, and UV occultation measurements that probe the upper mesosphere, has been used to map the vertical distribution of mesospheric
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

(
Belyaev et al. 2012
;
Belyaev et al. 2017
)
. This distribution drops from the cloud tops to a minimum just below 80 km, but increases substantially from 80–100 km to typically several hundred ppb
(
Belyaev et al. 2012
;
Vandaele et al. 2017
)
.

Assuming that the Venus atmosphere does not contain
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
, we find that a realistic vertical profile for
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
fits the JCMT 266.94 GHz detection. Because the JCMT observations were single dish, any
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
contribution to the 266.94 GHz line would not have been suppressed, as was the case for the ALMA data, and so should be sensitive to the true mesospheric
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
abundance. We used a mesospheric
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
profile that is based on the profile observed in 2007–2008 by
Belyaev et al. 2012
, which is likely a good fit to similar higher values seen in 2016–2018, a time span that includes the
Greaves et al. 2020a
JCMT observation. This profile is also consistent with cloud top values of 200–350 ppb observed in the mid-infrared within a month of the
Greaves et al. 2020a
JCMT observations
(
Encrenaz et al. 2019
)
. The
Encrenaz et al. 2019
observations support the validity of our
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
vertical profile, and suggest that the Venus mesosphere was unlikely to be experiencing a period of anomalously low
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
abundance at the time of the JCMT observations.
Using this vertical abundance profile and a
\text{CO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
-broadened
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
line profile, we can fit the width and shape of the 266.94 GHz line using
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
alone, without needing an additional
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
component. The
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
is also a better fit to the line centroid than the
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
(cf. Fig.
3
A/B, D). This excellent fit counters the argument of
Greaves et al. 2020b
that
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
alone would be too narrow to fit the observed line.
Greaves et al. 2020b
also recently argued that the
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
abundance required to fit the JCMT 266.94 GHz line (evenly-mixed 150 ppb for their fit, and 100 ppb for
Villanueva et al. 2020
) is unrealistically large, given previous mm-wave observations, which have returned lower values for mesospheric
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

(
Sandor et al. 2010
;
Encrenaz et al. 2015
)
. However, mm-wave observations do not have as long, or as well sampled, a baseline as dedicated Venus spacecraft observations of the mesosphere
(
Belyaev et al. 2012
;
Belyaev et al. 2017
;
Vandaele et al. 2017
)
, and mesospheric
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
abundance has been observed to vary by an order of magnitude on daily to yearly timescales, with values at 90–95 km altitude between 10 to 300 ppb. There is also evidence for longer-term secular changes in mesospheric and cloud-top
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
abundances, with maxima in 2007–2008 and 2016–2018, and a minimum in 2012–2014
(
Belyaev et al. 2017
;
Encrenaz et al. 2019
)
. We note that the model that we used to fit the JCMT 266.94 GHz line assuming a higher abundance of
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
also produced an accurate fit to the lower abundance observation of
Encrenaz et al. 2015
(see our Fig.
2
), which was observed near an
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
minimum.

We also find that strong ALMA line dilution allows the vertical abundance profile of
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
that fits the JCMT 266.94 GHz observations to still be consistent with the non-detection of the
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
ALMA reference lines—which are likely poor indicators of the impact of
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
on the JCMT observations. Spectral simulations using our Case D
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
vertical distribution predict
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
lines at 267.54 and 267.72 GHz with l:c ratios that are close to a factor of 10 larger than the nominal ALMA non-detection limit of
-0.0006
given by
Greaves et al. 2020a
. This apparent contradiction can be reconciled by the lack of line-dilution in the JCMT observation of the 266.94 GHz line, as the single-dish integrates flux over all scales, while the telescope configuration and the removal of measurements from the
\leq 33
m ALMA baselines would have likely resulted in at least 90–95% line dilution (factor of 10–20 suppression) for spatially-uniform
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
gas. Therefore, taking the sensitivity of the two telescopes into account, our JCMT fit does not need to be adjusted, but our modeled
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
l:c ratios should be divided by at least
\sim
20, if the
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
is uniform across the disk, to approximate the ALMA detection for that set of baseline configurations. In doing so, our predicted
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
reference line values fall below the “10 ppb” (
-0.0006
) detection threshold (see Figure
5
inset). Consequently, the
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
-only model with up to several hundred ppb of
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
in the mesosphere can fit the JCMT data, and still be consistent with the non-detection of
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
in the ALMA wide-band data. Moreover, this strong line dilution, with the corresponding loss of sensitivity to even high levels of
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
, suggests that the ALMA wide-band
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
reference observations were likely poor indicators that
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
was low enough to be ruled out as a significant source of the JCMT 266.94 GHz line—thereby significantly weakening the argument that this line was instead due primarily to
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
.

In addition to explaining the JCMT single-dish detection of the 266.94 GHz line, and the suppression of the
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
reference lines in the ALMA data, our
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
-only hypothesis would also predict that the 266.94 GHz ALMA line would be, like the
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
reference lines, strongly suppressed by line dilution and potentially non-detectable. While this was not the case in the original
Greaves et al. 2020a
paper, this is now consistent with recent significant challenges to the detection confidence of the 266.94 GHz ALMA line. These include reanalyses of the
Greaves et al. 2020a
narrowband ALMA discovery data by both
Snellen et al. 2020
and
Villanueva et al. 2020
who concluded that the feature attributed to
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
could not be detected with statistical significance. Our own further analysis of the
Greaves et al. 2020a
ALMA data, including testing the robustness of the detection at 266.94 GHz, comes to a similar conclusion, and is presented in
Akins et al. accepted
. Additionally, a recent reanalysis of high-resolution, S/N
\sim
1000 Venus observations taken in 2015 was used to search for a
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
transition near 10.47
\upmu
m, but it was not detected, setting a stringent upper limit of 5 ppb above the Venus clouds
(
Encrenaz et al. 2020
)
. Finally, the recent
Greaves et al. 2020b
communication analyzing a reprocessing of the ALMA data suggests that the 266.94 GHz feature in the narrow-band whole-planet ALMA data is now significantly reduced in detection significance from the original discovery paper (4.8-
\sigma
vs 13.3-
\sigma
), with an l:c of
-2\times 10^{-5}
, consistent with 1 ppb of
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
. However, this much-reduced 266.94 GHz feature would also be consistent with line-diluted
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
, which in our model would have l:c of
-1
to
-2\times 10^{-5}
at this frequency, for line dilution in the range 95–97%—which is likely well within the range of potential line dilution
(
Akins et al. accepted
)
.

Although the
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
hypothesis self-consistently explains our current understanding of the detection and non-detections in the JCMT and ALMA data, additional analyses and observations will be needed to more definitively discriminate between
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
and
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
as the source of the 266.94 GHz JCMT line. Re-observing Venus at 266.94 GHz will likely still be needed to independently confirm the discovery observation, and detection of an additional
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
absorption feature would provide a much stronger case for its presence in the Venus atmosphere. Future observations to confirm the
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}

J=1\leftarrow 0
line detection should incorporate single dish measurements, which would not suffer from line dilution, or observations including the Atacama Compact Array (which includes shorter baseline measurements than the primary ALMA array). Because the
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
abundance is critical to the
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
identification for the ALMA data, we recommend that future attempts to confirm the ALMA
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
observations should also obtain near-simultaneous
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
measurements. The narrowband correlator configuration can be tuned to 266.94 GHz and to the frequencies of two nearby, stronger
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
lines (near 267.54 and 267.72 GHz). To mitigate the spectral ripple features that compromised measurement of the line intensities
(
Greaves et al. 2020a
)
, these observations should occur when the apparent angular diameter of Venus is smaller and therefore less resolved by the ALMA antennas.

Ultimately, the claimed detection of
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
in the atmosphere of Venus has underscored the necessity of identifying and assessing the context of the environment within which we find potential biosignatures. The identification of the 266.94 GHz line as due to
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
, and its plausibility as a potential biosignature, is inextricably intertwined with the physical and chemical environment of the Venus cloud and above-cloud atmosphere. This initial, controversial detection has highlighted just how much we still need to understand about our sister planet, and how important that knowledge is in interpreting this discovery. If the 266.94 GHz line is confirmed, and conclusively attributed to
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
, its presence in the mesosphere would require additional observations to understand potential sources and sinks, and the attendant (and as yet unknown) phosphorous chemistry that enables its persistence at these high altitudes. Moreover, if
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
is being generated abiotically, especially at these high altitudes, this would have negative implications for the robustness of
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
and other reduced gases to serve as biosignatures in oxidizing terrestrial atmospheres. Regardless of the outcome, additional targeted observations will reveal processes on a terrestrial planet that informs our understanding of our own world, and potentially a large number of exoplanets that may share a similar evolutionary path and current environment.

V
Conclusions

We simulated millimeter-wavelength Venus spectra to explore the vertical distribution and detectability of
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
and
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
in the Venus atmosphere. We find that the observations of the 266.94 GHz absorption line are insensitive to the abundance of
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
and
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
within the cloud deck. Instead, the observed absorption at this wavelength originates from the mesosphere at altitudes above 80 km. At these altitudes,
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
would be rapidly destroyed, such that
20\pm 10
ppb of
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
would require a flux of
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
to the Venus mesosphere that is
\sim
100 times higher than the global production rate of photosynthetically-generated
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
on Earth. Because
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
and
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
both absorb within the width of the line detected at 266.94 GHz, we emphasize that the identification of this absorption line as due to
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
in both the ALMA and JCMT data relies heavily on the apparent low abundance of
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
inferred from the non-detection of an
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
reference line at 267.54 GHz in the ALMA data. However, we show that
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
absorption is likely heavily suppressed in the ALMA data. Using
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
vertical profiles within the range of previous observations (from 30 ppb at 78 km to 400
\pm
150 ppb at 100 km)—including
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
observations taken within a month of the JCMT data—our model can fit the depth and width of the 266.94 GHz feature without
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
. We also show that ALMA line dilution suppresses the values for nominal Venus mesospheric
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
to below the corresponding detectability limit set by
Greaves et al. 2020a
. Given the mesospheric altitude range, short chemical lifetime of
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
, and consistency with existing mesospheric
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
abundances observed within a month of the JCMT observations, we argue that
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
provides a more self-consistent explanation for the 266.94 GHz feature than
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
. Single dish observations optimized for Venus and used to assess the
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
detection and
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
abundance in the Venus upper mesosphere should be prioritized to discriminate between
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
or
\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
as the source of the 266.94 GHz line.

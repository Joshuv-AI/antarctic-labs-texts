A reproducing kernel Hilbert space, denoted as

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&K_{w}(z)=\dfrac{1}{(1-\overline{w}z)}\;\mbox{on}\;\mathcal{H}^{2}(\mathbb{D});\\
&K_{w}(z)=\dfrac{1}{(1-\overline{w}z)^{2}}\;\mbox{on}\;\mathcal{A}^{2}(\mathbb{D});\\
&K_{w}(z)=\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\overline{w}z)^{n}}{1+n^{2}},\;\mbox{on}\;\mathcal{S}^{2}(\mathbb{D});\\

With the definition of the derivative Hardy space as our motivation, our aim is to establish an equivalent norm for the space

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{2}_{2}(\mathbb{D})

\displaystyle=\bigg\{f\in\ H(\mathbb{D}):||f||^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}_{2}}=31\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a|^{2}+41\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}n^{2}|a|^{2}+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}n^{2}(n-1)^{2}|a|^{2}

\displaystyle 31\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}\overline{b_{n}}+41\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}n^{2}a_{n}\overline{b_{n}}+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}n^{2}(n-1)^{2}a_{n}\overline{b_{n}}+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{a_{n}\overline{b_{n}}}{n+1}+49\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}na_{n}\overline{b_{n}}

2.
Characterization of the space
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{2}(\mathbb{D})

In this section, we will provide an account of some essential properties of the space
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{2}(\mathbb{D})
that will be drawn upon in later sections.

Proposition 2.1
.

The space
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{2}(\mathbb{D})
satisfies the following:

(a)

\mathcal{H}^{2}_{2}(\mathbb{D})
is a normed space.

(b)

polynomials are contained in
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{2}(\mathbb{D})
.

(c)

\mathcal{H}^{2}_{2}(\mathbb{D})
is a proper subset of
\mathcal{H}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
,

The following example serves as proof of the proper inclusion of part (c) in Proposition
2.1
.

Example 2.1
.

Let
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sqrt{(n+1)/(n+2)^{5}}z^{n}
. Then

\displaystyle||f||_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{n+1}{(n+2)^{5}}=\zeta(4)-\zeta(5)\approxeq 0.04539<\infty.

Since

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(n+2)^{5}}{n+1}|a_{n}|^{2}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(n+2)^{5}}{n+1}\frac{n+1}{(n+2)^{5}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}1=\infty,

so
f\notin\mathcal{H}^{2}_{2}(\mathbb{D})
.
Hence,
f
belongs to
\mathcal{H}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
but it does not belong to
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{2}(\mathbb{D})
.

To gain a comprehensive understanding of any space, it is essential to be familiar with its basis. For instance, when attempting to demonstrate that an operator defined on a given space is a Hilbert-Schmidt operator, it is necessary to have an orthonormal basis for that space. Since space
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{2}(\mathbb{D})
has been recently constructed, understanding its basis is crucial for a thorough comprehension of this new space. The following theorem provides an orthonormal basis for the space
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{2}(\mathbb{D})
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Suppose
e_{n}(z)=\sqrt{(n+1)/(n+2)^{5}}z^{n}
,
z\in\mathbb{D}
and
n
is any non-negative integer. Then the set
\{e_{n}(z):n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}\}
forms an orthonormal basis for
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{2}(\mathbb{D})
.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

For any non-negative integers
m
and
n
, we have

\displaystyle\langle z^{m},z^{n}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{2}_{2}}=31

\displaystyle\langle z^{m},z^{n}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}+41\langle mz^{m-1},nz^{n-1}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}+\langle m(m-1)z^{m-2},n(n-1)z^{n-2}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}

(2.1)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\langle z^{m},z^{n}\rangle_{\mathcal{A}^{2}}+49\langle mz^{m-1},nz^{n-1}\rangle_{\mathcal{A}^{2}}+11\langle m(m-1)z^{m-2},n(n-1)z^{n-2}\rangle_{\mathcal{A}^{2}}.

For
n=m
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\langle z^{m},z^{n}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{2}_{2}}=

\displaystyle 31\cdot 1+41n^{2}+n^{2}(n-1)^{2}+\frac{1}{(n+1)}+49n+11n^{2}(n-1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{n^{5}+10n^{4}+40n^{3}+80n^{2}+80n+32}{(n+1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(n+2)^{5}}{(n+1)}.

Therefore, it is easy to see that

\displaystyle\langle z^{m},z^{n}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{2}_{2}}=\begin{cases}\frac{(n+2)^{5}}{(n+1)}\;\;\;\;\;\mbox{if}\;m=n\\
\;\;0\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\mbox{if}\;m\neq n.\end{cases}

Hence,
\{e_{n}(z):n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}\}=\{\sqrt{(n+1)/(n+2)^{5}}z^{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}\}
forms an orthonormal set in
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{2}(\mathbb{D})
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{H}^{2}_{2}(\mathbb{D})
be such that
f
is orthogonal to
e_{n}(z)
for all
n\geq 0
and let the power series expansion of
f
be given by
f(z)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{m}z^{m}
. Then for
n\geq 0
,
\langle f(z),e_{n}(z)\rangle=\langle\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{m}z^{m},e_{n}(z)\rangle=0
. This gives
a_{m}=0
for all
m\geq 0
. Thus,
f\equiv 0
and hence the set
\{e_{n}(z):n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}\}
forms an orthonormal basis for
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{2}(\mathbb{D})
.
∎

3.
Multiplication operator on the space
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{2}(\mathbb{D})

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a Banach space consisting of holomorphic functions defined on the open unit disk
\mathbb{D}
.Then for any function
f\in\mathcal{B}
, the multiplication operator
M_{f}
is defined as follows:

\displaystyle M_{f}g=fg

for every holomorphic function
g
defined on
\mathbb{D}
. Multiplication operators have a key role to play in the analysis of weighted composition operators in holomorphic function spaces. The aim is to find a connection between the properties of the multiplication operator
M_{f}
and those of its corresponding symbol
f
. In this section, we will examine the circumstances that determine the boundedness of the multiplication operator
M_{f}
on
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{2}(\mathbb{D})
.

In a recent study, Gu and Luo (see
[
8
, Proposition 2.1]
) demonstrated that for functions belonging to
\mathcal{S}^{2}_{1}(\mathbb{D})
, it holds that
||f||{\infty}\leq\sqrt{2}||f||{\mathcal{S}^{2}_{1}}
where the constant
\sqrt{2}
is sharp. Subsequently, Gupta and Malhotra (in Theorem-3.1, as referenced in
[
9
]
) extended this result by establishing that for functions in
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{1}(\mathbb{D})
, the norm
||f||{\infty}\leq\sqrt{\pi^{2}/6-\zeta(3)}||f||{\mathcal{H}^{2}_{1}}
, and it was confirmed that the constant
\sqrt{\pi^{2}/6-\zeta(3)}
is sharp.

In the following result, we will introduce an analogous counterpart to Proposition 2.1 mentioned in
[
8
]
and Theorem 3.1 discussed in
[
9
]
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose
f\in\mathcal{H}^{2}_{2}(\mathbb{D})
. Then
||f||_{\infty}\leq\sqrt{\zeta(4)-\zeta(5)}||f||_{\mathcal{H}^{2}_{2}}
. Furthermore,
\sqrt{\zeta(4)-\zeta(5)}
is the sharp constant(
\zeta
is the Riemann zeta function and
\zeta(4)\approxeq 1.08232
and
\zeta(5)\approxeq 1.03693
).

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Suppose
f\in\mathcal{H}^{2}_{2}(\mathbb{D})
with power series representation
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
. Then

\displaystyle|f(z)|=\bigg|\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\bigg|\leq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}||z|^{n}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sqrt{\frac{(n+2)^{5}}{n+1}}|a_{n}|\sqrt{\frac{n+1}{(n+2)^{5}}}|z|^{n}.

By making use of the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we obtain that

\displaystyle|f(z)|\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{\bigg(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(n+2)^{5}}{n+1}|a_{n}|^{2}\bigg)}\sqrt{\bigg(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{n+1}{(n+2)^{5}}|z|^{2n}\bigg)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\bigg(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{n+1}{(n+2)^{5}}|z|^{2n}\bigg)}||f||_{\mathcal{H}^{2}_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{n+1}{(n+2)^{5}}}||f||_{\mathcal{H}^{2}_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{(\zeta(4)-\zeta(5))}||f||_{\mathcal{H}^{2}_{2}}.

Hence,
||f||_{\infty}\leq\sqrt{\zeta(4)-\zeta(5)}||f||_{\mathcal{H}^{2}_{2}}
.

If we consider the function
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}((n+1)/(n+2)^{5})z^{n}
, then
||f||_{\infty}=f(1)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(n+1)/(n+2)^{5}=\zeta(4)-\zeta(5)
and
||f||_{\mathcal{H}^{2}_{2}}=\sqrt{\zeta(4)-\zeta(5)}
. Hence, we conclude that the constant
\sqrt{\zeta(4)-\zeta(5)}
is sharp. This completes the proof.
∎

Operator algebras constitute a core subject within functional analysis, a mathematical discipline concerned with vector spaces endowed with a topological structure. They offer a structure for comprehending the characteristics of linear operators, with a special focus on operators that are either bounded or unbounded. In 1969, the paper
[
16
]
presented a proof that
D_{\alpha}
is an algebra for
\alpha>1
. Furthermore, it was noted in
[
17
]
that
\mathcal{S}^{2}_{2}(\mathbb{D})
and its analogous counterparts
D_{n}
for integer values of
n\geq 1
are also algebras, with reference to Russian literature. The research presented in
[
1
]
and
[
4
]
established that both
\mathcal{S}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
and
\mathcal{S}^{2}_{2}(\mathbb{D})
possess the characteristics of algebras. Recently, Guo and Luo
[
8
]
demonstrated that
\mathcal{S}^{2}_{1}(\mathbb{D})
is an algebra, and subsequently, Gupta and Malhotra
[
9
]
established that
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{1}(\mathbb{D})
is also an algebra. Here, we will provide proof demonstrating that
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{2}(\mathbb{D})
constitutes an algebra.

Proposition 3.1
.

The space
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{2}(\mathbb{D})
is an algebra.

Proof of Proposition
3.1
.

Suppose
f\in\mathcal{H}^{2}_{2}(\mathbb{D})
with power series representation
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
. By simple computation, we obtain the following inequality

\displaystyle\bigg|f(z)-\sum_{n=0}^{N}a_{n}z^{n}\bigg|\leq

\displaystyle\bigg|\sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty}\sqrt{\frac{(n+2)^{5}}{n+1}}a_{n}\sqrt{\frac{n+1}{(n+2)^{5}}}z^{n}\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty}\sqrt{\frac{(n+2)^{5}}{n+1}}|a_{n}|\sqrt{\frac{n+1}{(n+2)^{5}}}|z|^{n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{\sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty}\frac{(n+2)^{5}}{n+1}|a_{n}|^{2}}\sqrt{\sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty}\frac{n+1}{(n+2)^{5}}|z|^{2n}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle||f||_{\mathcal{H}^{2}_{2}}\sqrt{\sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty}\frac{n+1}{(n+2)^{5}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle||f||_{\mathcal{H}^{2}_{2}}\sqrt{\sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(n+2)^{4}}-\sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(n+2)^{5}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle||f||_{\mathcal{H}^{2}_{2}}\sqrt{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(n+N+3)^{4}}-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(n+N+3)^{5}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle||f||_{\mathcal{H}^{2}_{2}}\sqrt{\frac{1}{6}Polygamma[3,N+3]-(-\frac{1}{24})Polygamma[4,N+3]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle||f||_{\mathcal{H}^{2}_{2}}\sqrt{\frac{1}{24}(4Polygamma[3,N+3]+Polygamma[4,N+3])}

(note that
Polygamma[n,z]
represents the
n^{th}
derivative, denoted as
\Psi^{n}(z)
, of the Digamma function
\Psi(z)
. The Digamma function
\Psi(z)
serves as the logarithmic derivative of the gamma function
\Gamma(z)
, and we can express it as
\Psi(z)=(d/dz)\ln(\Gamma(z))=\Gamma^{\prime}(z)/\Gamma(z)
, where
\Gamma(z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{t-1}e^{-t}dt
. The power series representation of the Polygamma function is as follows:

\displaystyle Polygamma[m,z]:=\Psi^{m}(z)=(-1)^{m+1}m!\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}1/(n+z)^{m+1}.

It should be noted that this representation is valid for values of
z
greater than zero and for all natural numbers
n
.) By utilizing Mathematica software to observe that the limit of the expression

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{1}{24}(4Polygamma[3,N+3]+Polygamma[4,N+3])}

approaches zero as
N
tends to
\infty
, we can conclude that
f(z)
is the uniform limit of its partial sum. Consequently,
f(z)
maintains a continuous extension to the boundary of
\mathbb{D}
. The space
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{2}(\mathbb{D})
is a subset of the disk algebra
A(\mathbb{D})
, where
A(\mathbb{D})
comprises holomorphic functions mapping from
\mathbb{D}
to
\mathbb{C}
that exhibit continuous extensions to the boundary of
\mathbb{D}
. Let
A^{1}(\mathbb{D})=\{f\in A(\mathbb{D}):f^{\prime}(z)\in A(\mathbb{D})\}
. Then,
A^{1}(\mathbb{D})\subseteq\mathcal{H}^{2}_{2}(\mathbb{D})\subseteq A(\mathbb{D})
. Therefore,
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{2}(\mathbb{D})
is an algebra. This completes the proof.
∎

\displaystyle||fg||{\mathcal{S}^{2}_{1}}\leq 2\sqrt{2}||f||{\mathcal{S}^{2}_{1}}||g||{\mathcal{S}^{2}_{1}}

\displaystyle||fg||^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}_{1}}\leq 6(\pi^{2}/6-\zeta(3))||f||^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}_{1}}||g||^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}_{1}}

is valid. By a similar line of reasoning to that employed in Theorem 2.2 from
[
8
]
and Theorem 3.4 from
[
9
]
, our objective in this section is to establish that, for

and
g
in
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{2}(\mathbb{D})
, the inequality

\displaystyle||fg||_{\mathcal{H}^{2}_{2}}\leq 2\sqrt{2}\sqrt{\zeta(4)-\zeta(5)}||f||_{\mathcal{H}^{2}_{2}}||g||_{\mathcal{H}^{2}_{2}}

holds true.

\mathcal{H}^{2}_{2}(\mathbb{D})
which is similar to
[

and
[

, Theorem 3.5]

Theorem 3.2
.

If
f,g\in\mathcal{H}^{2}_{2}(\mathbb{D})
, then
||fg||_{\mathcal{H}^{2}_{2}}\leq 2\sqrt{2}\sqrt{\zeta(4)-\zeta(5)}||f||_{\mathcal{H}^{2}_{2}}||g||_{\mathcal{H}^{2}_{2}}

).

Proof of Theorem
3.2
.

Suppose that

. Then a straightforward computation leads to the following

\displaystyle||fg||^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}_{2}}=

\displaystyle 31||fg||^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}+41||f^{\prime}g+fg^{\prime}||^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}+||(fg)^{\prime\prime}||^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}+||fg||^{2}_{\mathcal{A}^{2}}+49||f^{\prime}g+fg^{\prime}||^{2}_{\mathcal{A}^{2}}

\displaystyle+11||(fg)^{\prime\prime}||^{2}_{\mathcal{A}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 31(||f||^{2}_{\infty}||g||^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}+||f||^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}||g||^{2}_{\infty})+41(2||f||^{2}_{\infty}||g^{\prime}||^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}+2||f^{\prime}||^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}||g||^{2}_{\infty})

\displaystyle+(4||f||^{2}_{\infty}||g^{\prime\prime}||^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}+2||f^{\prime\prime}||^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}||g||^{2}_{\infty}+8||f||^{2}_{\infty}||g^{\prime}||^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}+8||f^{\prime}||^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}||g||^{2}_{\infty})

\displaystyle+(||f||^{2}_{\infty}||g||^{2}_{\mathcal{A}^{2}}+||f||^{2}_{\mathcal{A}^{2}}||g||^{2}_{\infty})+49(2||f||^{2}_{\infty}||g^{\prime}||^{2}_{\mathcal{A}^{2}}+2||f^{\prime}||^{2}_{\mathcal{A}^{2}}||g||^{2}_{\infty})

\displaystyle+11(4||f||^{2}_{\infty}||g^{\prime\prime}||^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}+2||f^{\prime\prime}||^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}||g||^{2}_{\infty}+8||f||^{2}_{\infty}||g^{\prime}||^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}+8||f^{\prime}||^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}||g||^{2}_{\infty})

\displaystyle||f||^{2}_{\infty}(31||g||^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}+41||g^{\prime}||^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}+||g^{\prime\prime}||^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}+||g||^{2}_{\mathcal{A}^{2}}+49||g^{\prime}||^{2}_{\mathcal{A}^{2}}+11||g^{\prime\prime}||^{2}_{\mathcal{A}^{2}})

\displaystyle+||g||^{2}_{\infty}(31||f||^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}+41||f^{\prime}||^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}+||f^{\prime\prime}||^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}+||f||^{2}_{\mathcal{A}^{2}}+49||f^{\prime}||^{2}_{\mathcal{A}^{2}}+11||f^{\prime\prime}||^{2}_{\mathcal{A}^{2}})

\displaystyle+||f||^{2}_{\infty}(49||g^{\prime}||^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}+3||g^{\prime\prime}||^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}+137||g^{\prime}||^{2}_{\mathcal{A}^{2}}+33||g^{\prime\prime}||^{2}_{\mathcal{A}^{2}})+||g||^{2}_{\infty}(49||f^{\prime}||^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}

Theorem 3.3

If

3.3
.

\max\{||f||_{\infty},||f||_{\mathcal{H}^{2}_{2}}\}\leq||M_{f}||

4.
Complex symmetry of weighted composition operators on
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{2}(\mathbb{D})

Let
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be the algebra of all bounded linear operators on a separable complex Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. A mapping
T:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
is said to be anti-linear if

\displaystyle T(ax+by)=\overline{a}T(x)+\overline{b}T(y),

for all
x,y\in\mathcal{H}
and for all
a,b\in\mathbb{C}
.
A conjugation on
\mathcal{H}
is an antilinear operator
\mathcal{C}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
which satisfies
\langle\mathcal{C}x,\mathcal{C}y\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=\langle y,x\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}
for all
x,y\in\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{C}^{2}=I_{\mathcal{H}}
, where
I_{\mathcal{H}}
is the identity operator on
{\mathcal{H}}
. An operator
T\in\mathcal{B}({\mathcal{H}})
is said to be complex symmetric if there exists a conjugation
\mathcal{C}
on
\mathcal{H}
such that
T=\mathcal{C}T^{*}\mathcal{C}
, or equivalently,
T\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}T^{*}
. In this case, we say that
T
is complex symmetric with conjugation
\mathcal{C}
.

Definition 4.1
.

An operator
T
is said to be a Hilbert-Schmidt operator in a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
if there exists an orthonormal basis
\{e_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
for which the series
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}||Te_{n}||^{2}
converges to a finite value.

In
[
9
]
, Gupta and Malhotra have established that if
\phi
belongs to the space
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{1}(\mathbb{D})
and satisfies
||\phi||_{\infty}<1
, the operator
C_{\phi}
is a Hilbert-Schmidt operator. In the next result, it is shown that the composition operator
C_{\phi}
on
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{2}(\mathbb{D})
is a Hilbert-Schmidt operator, and it is compact when
\phi
belongs to
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{2}(\mathbb{D})
and satisfies the condition
||\phi||_{\infty}<1
. The proof for the following result follows a similar line of reasoning as that of
[
9
, Theorem 4.2]
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\phi\in\mathcal{H}^{2}_{2}(\mathbb{D})
be such that
||\phi||_{\infty}<1
. Then the composition operator
C_{\phi}
is a Hilbert-Schmidt operator on
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{2}(\mathbb{D})
.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

In order to establish that the composition operator
C_{\phi}
is a Hilbert-Schmidt operator, it is necessary to demonstrate that the series
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}||C_{\phi}e_{n}(z)||^{2}
has a finite sum. An easy computation shows that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}||C_{\phi}e_{n}(z)||^{2}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg|\bigg|C_{\phi}\bigg(\frac{z^{n}}{||z^{n}||}\bigg)\bigg|\bigg|^{2}=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{||\phi^{n}||^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}_{2}}}{||z^{n}||^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}_{2}}}=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(n+1)||\phi^{n}||^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}_{2}}}{(n+2)^{5}}.

and

\displaystyle||\phi^{n}||^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}_{2}}=

\displaystyle 31||\phi\phi^{n-1}||^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}+41||(\phi^{n})^{\prime}||^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}+||(\phi^{n})^{\prime\prime}||^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}+||\phi\phi^{n-1}||^{2}_{\mathcal{A}^{2}}+49||(\phi^{n})^{\prime}||^{2}_{\mathcal{A}^{2}}

\displaystyle+11||(\phi^{n})^{\prime\prime}||^{2}_{\mathcal{A}^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 31||\phi\phi^{n-1}||^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}+41||n\phi^{n-1}\phi^{\prime}||^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}+||n(n-1)\phi^{n-2}(\phi^{\prime})^{2}+n\phi^{n-1}\phi^{\prime\prime}||^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}

\displaystyle+||\phi\phi^{n-1}||^{2}_{\mathcal{A}^{2}}+49||n\phi^{n-1}\phi^{\prime}||^{2}_{\mathcal{A}^{2}}+11||n(n-1)\phi^{n-2}(\phi^{\prime})^{2}+n\phi^{n-1}\phi^{\prime\prime}||^{2}_{\mathcal{A}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 31||\phi^{n-1}||^{2}_{\infty}||\phi||^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}+41n^{2}||\phi^{n-1}||^{2}_{\infty}||\phi^{\prime}||^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}+2[n^{2}(n-1)^{2}||\phi^{n-1}||^{2}_{\infty}||\phi^{\prime}||^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}

\displaystyle+n^{2}||\phi^{n-1}||^{2}_{\infty}||\phi^{\prime\prime}||^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}]+||\phi^{n-1}||^{2}_{\infty}||\phi||^{2}_{\mathcal{A}^{2}}+49n^{2}||\phi^{n-1}||^{2}_{\infty}||\phi^{\prime}||^{2}_{\mathcal{A}^{2}}

\displaystyle+22[n^{2}(n-1)^{2}||\phi^{n-1}||^{2}_{\infty}||\phi^{\prime}||^{2}_{\mathcal{A}^{2}}+n^{2}||\phi^{n-1}||^{2}_{\infty}||\phi^{\prime\prime}||^{2}_{\mathcal{A}^{2}}]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle||\phi^{n-1}||^{2}_{\infty}[31||\phi||^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}+41n^{2}||\phi^{\prime}||^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}+n^{2}||\phi^{\prime\prime}||^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}+||\phi||^{2}_{\mathcal{A}^{2}}+49n^{2}||\phi^{\prime}||^{2}_{\mathcal{A}^{2}}

\displaystyle+11n^{2}||\phi^{\prime\prime}||^{2}_{\mathcal{A}^{2}}]+||\phi^{n-1}||^{2}_{\infty}[2n^{2}(n-1)^{2}\phi^{\prime}||^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}+n^{2}||\phi^{\prime\prime}||^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}

\displaystyle+22n^{2}(n-1)^{2}||\phi^{\prime}||^{2}_{\mathcal{A}^{2}}+11n^{2}||\phi^{\prime\prime}||^{2}_{\mathcal{A}^{2}}]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle n^{2}||\phi^{n-1}||^{2}_{\infty}[31||\phi||^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}+41||\phi^{\prime}||^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}+||\phi^{\prime\prime}||^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}+||\phi||^{2}_{\mathcal{A}^{2}}+49||\phi^{\prime}||^{2}_{\mathcal{A}^{2}}+11||\phi^{\prime\prime}||^{2}_{\mathcal{A}^{2}}]

\displaystyle+n^{4}||\phi^{n-1}||^{2}_{\infty}[2\phi^{\prime}||^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}+||\phi^{\prime\prime}||^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}+22||\phi^{\prime}||^{2}_{\mathcal{A}^{2}}+11||\phi^{\prime\prime}||^{2}_{\mathcal{A}^{2}}]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2n^{4}||\phi||^{2(n-1)}_{\infty}||\phi||^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}_{2}}.

Thus, it follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg|\bigg|C_{\phi}\bigg(\frac{z^{n}}{||z^{n}||}\bigg)\bigg|\bigg|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{2n^{4}(n+1)||\phi||^{2(n-1)}_{\infty}||\phi||^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}_{2}}}{(n+2)^{5}}

\displaystyle\leq 1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{2n^{4}(n+1)||\phi||^{2(n-1)}_{\infty}||\phi||^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}_{2}}}{(n+2)^{5}}

\displaystyle\leq 1+2||\phi||^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}_{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}||\phi||^{2(n-1)}_{\infty}

\displaystyle=1+2||\phi||^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}_{2}}(1-||\phi||^{2}_{\infty})^{-1}<\infty.

Hence,
C_{\phi}
is a Hilbert-Schmidt operator.
∎

\mathbb{D}
, the function
q(z)/p(z)
is holomorphic, and it satisfies the condition
q(0)=0
. Consequently, we can express
q(z)/p(z)
as an infinite series:

\displaystyle\frac{q(z)}{p(z)}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}c_{i}a^{i-1}_{0}z^{i},

where
c_{1}=1
, and for
i=2,3,\ldots
, the coefficients
c_{i}
are positive real numbers less than one. Thus, (
4.13
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\bigg(

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{n+1}{(n+2)^{5}}a^{n}_{0}z^{n}\bigg)\bigg(\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{m+1}{(m+2)^{5}}\bigg(\sum_{k=0}^{m}\binom{m}{k}(a_{0}w)^{k}\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}a_{1}c_{i}a^{i-1}_{0}z^{i}w\bigg)^{m-k}\bigg)\bigg)

\displaystyle=\bigg(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{n+1}{(n+2)^{5}}a^{n}_{0}w^{n}\bigg)

\displaystyle\times\bigg(\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{m+1}{(m+2)^{5}}\bigg(\sum_{k=0}^{m}\binom{m}{k}(a_{0}z)^{k}\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}a_{1}c_{i}a^{i-1}_{0}w^{i}z\bigg)^{m-k}\bigg)\bigg).

On comparing the coefficients of
z^{3}w
, on L.H.S, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{2}{3^{5}}\bigg(\frac{4}{5^{5}}a^{4}_{0}+\frac{3}{4^{5}}a^{2}_{0}a_{1}c_{1}+\frac{2}{3^{5}}a^{2}_{0}a_{1}c_{2}+\frac{1}{2^{5}}a^{2}_{0}a_{1}c_{3}\bigg),

(4.14)

whereas on R.H.S, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{4}{5^{5}}\bigg(\frac{2}{3^{5}}a^{4}_{0}+\frac{3}{2^{5}}a^{2}_{0}a_{1}c_{1}\bigg).

(4.15)

Consequently, we find that the expressions of (
4.14
) and (
4.15
) will be equal only if
a_{0}
is zero or
a_{1}
is zero or both are zero. With this the proof is completed.
∎

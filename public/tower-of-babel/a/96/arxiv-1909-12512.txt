On families of optimal Hardy-weights for linear second-order elliptic operators
Yehuda Pinchover, Idan Versano

Abstract
. We construct families of optimal Hardy-weights for a subcritical linear second-order elliptic operator using a one-dimensional reduction. More precisely, we first characterize all optimal Hardy-weights with respect to one-dimensional subcritical Sturm-Liouville operators on ( a , b ) (a,b) , ∞ ≤ a < b ≤ ∞ \infty\leq a<b\leq\infty , and then apply this result to obtain families of optimal Hardy inequalities for general linear second-order elliptic operators in higher dimensions. As an application, we prove a new Rellich inequality. 2000 ​ Mathematics Subject Classification. Primary ​ 35B09; Secondary 35J08, 35J20, 47J20, 49J40. Keywords: ground state, Hardy inequality, minimal growth, positive solutions, Rellich inequality, variational inequalities.



1.
Introduction

The classical Hardy inequalities in the continuum and discrete cases were introduced in 1920th, and has been evolved to many versions in different aspects of mathematics, see
[
5
,
11
,
21
,
20
]
for historical reviews. The problem of improving Hardy-type inequalities has engaged many authors till today, see
[
5
,
7
,
9
]
, and reference therein. Fraas, Devyver and Pinchover, established a general method to generate an explicit
optimal
Hardy-weight for a general (not necessarily symmetric) subcritical linear elliptic operators either in divergence form or in non-divergence form
[
12
]
. In some definite sense, an optimal Hardy-weight is
‘as large as possible’
Hardy-weight. Such an optimal weight is by no means unique. In the present paper we construct for a given linear second-order nonnegative elliptic operator
P

families
of optimal Hardy-weights, generalizing the results in
[
12
]
. In fact, for the one-dimensional case, we characterize the set of all optimal Hardy-weights. We then apply this characterization to obtain families of optimal Hardy inequalities for general linear second-order elliptic operators in higher dimensions.

Let us first recall the notion of optimal Hardy-weights and present the main result of
[
12
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(or a noncompact
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold), and consider a second-order linear elliptic operator
P
defined on
\Omega
.

•

We say that
P
is nonnegative in
\Omega
(in short,
P\geq 0
in
\Omega
) if
P
admits a positive global (super)solution of the equation
Pu=0
in
\Omega
.

•

We say that a nonzero nonnegative function
W
is a Hardy-weight for
P
in
\Omega
if the following Hardy-type inequality

P-W\geq 0
in
\Omega
holds true.

•

A nonnegative operator
P
in
\Omega
is said to be subcritical (respect., critical) in
\Omega
if
P
admits (respect., does not admit) a Hardy-weight for
P
in
\Omega
.

It is well-known that
P
is subcritical in
\Omega
if and only if it admits a positive minimal Green function
G_{P}^{\Omega}(x,y)
in
\Omega
. On the other hand,
P
is critical in
\Omega
if and only if the equation
Pu=0
admits (up to a multiplicative constant) a unique positive supersolution
\phi
in
\Omega
. In fact,
\phi
is a positive solution, called the
Agmon ground state
. Clearly,
P
is critical in
\Omega
if and only if
P^{\star}
is critical in
\Omega
, where
P^{\star}
is the formal adjoint of
P
in
L^{2}(\Omega)

[
24
]
.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
P
be nonnegative in
\Omega
. A Hardy-weight
W
is said to be optimal in
\Omega
if
P-W
is critical in
\Omega
, and
\phi\phi^{*}\not\in L^{1}(E,W\,\mathrm{d}x)
, where
\phi
and
\phi^{*}
are respectively, the ground states of
P-W
and
P^{\star}-W
in
\Omega
, and

is any end of
\Omega
. In this case we say that
P-W
is null-critical at each end of
\Omega
with respect to the weight

.

Remark 1.3
.

We say that a Hardy-weight
W
is optimal at infinity in
\Omega
if for any

,

\sup\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\mid P-\lambda W\geq 0\mbox{ in }\Omega\setminus K\}=1.

We note that the original definition of optimal Hardy-weights in
[
12
]
includes the requirement that
W
is optimal at infinity. Recently it was proved in
[
19
, Corollary 3.4]
that the latter property follows, in fact, from the null-criticality of

with respect to
W
.

The main result of Fraas, Devyver and Pinchover (
[
12
, Theorem 4.12]
, see also
[
19
, Theorem 2.15]

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
P
be a subcritical operator in
\Omega
, and let
G_{P}^{\Omega}
be its positive minimal Green function. Let
0\lneqq\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
,
and consider the Green potential

G_{\varphi}(x):=\int_{\Omega}G_{P}^{\Omega}(x,y)\varphi(y)\,\mathrm{d}y,

(1.1)

(so,
PG_{\varphi}=\varphi
in
\Omega
).
Suppose that there exists a positive solution
u
of the equation
P=0
in
\Omega
satisfying

\lim_{x\to\overline{\infty}}\dfrac{G(x)}{u(x)}=0,

(1.2)

where
\overline{\infty}
denotes the ideal point in the one-point compactification of

.

Consider the supersolution

v:=\sqrt{G_{\varphi}u}

of the operator
P
in
\Omega
. Then the associated
Hardy-weight

W:=\dfrac{Pv}{v}

is an optimal Hardy-weight
with respect to
P
in
\Omega.
Moreover,

W=\dfrac{1}{4}\left|\nabla\left(\log\dfrac{G_{\varphi}}{u}\right)\right|_{A}^{2}=\dfrac{1}{4}\dfrac{\left|\nabla\left((G_{\varphi}/u)\right)\right|_{A}^{2}}{(G_{\varphi}/u)^{2}}\qquad\text{in}~\Omega\setminus\mbox{\rm supp}\,\varphi,

where
A
is the diffusion matrix associated with the principal part of the operator
P
,
|\xi|_{A}:=\sqrt{\langle A\xi,\xi\rangle}
, and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the Euclidean inner product on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Remark 1.5
.

Since
G_{\varphi}\asymp G
in a neighborhood of

\lim_{x\to\overline{\infty}}\big(G(x)/u(x)\big)=0
clearly implies
\lim_{x\to\overline{\infty}}\big(G_{\varphi}(x))/u(x)\big)=0
. We recall that if

is symmetric, or more generally quasi-symmetric, then a global positive solution

[
4
]
.

Remark 1.6
.

The above result has been extended to the case of the
p
-Laplace operator by Devyver and Pinchover
[
13
]
and recently to the realm of Schrödinger operators on discrete graphs by Keller, Pinchover, and Pogorzelski
[
17
]
.

The optimal Hardy-weights obtained by Theorem
1.4
are obviously not unique even in the one-dimensional case. The main aim of the present paper is to
characterize
the set of all
optimal
Hardy-weights for Sturm-Liouville operators, and to establish a new construction of families of optimal Hardy-weights for general second-order subcritical linear elliptic operators using a one-dimensional reduction.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce the necessary notation and recall some previously obtained results needed in the present paper. We proceed in Section
3
, with the characterization of the set of all optimal Hardy weights for Sturm-Liouville operators, while in Section
4
, we use the explicit form of the general solution of the (semilinear) Ermakov-Pinney equation to obtain a family of strictly positive Hardy-weights for one-dimensional subcritical Schrödinger operators which are optimal at one end. Applying the above characterization of optimal Hardy-weights for the one-dimensional case, we obtain in Section
5
families of Hardy inequalities in the higher dimensional case. More precisely, in the symmetric case, our construction leads to a family of
optimal
Hardy weights, while in the nonsymmetric, case we are only able to prove that the aforementioned one-dimensional reduction gives rise to critical Hardy-weights that are optimal at infinity. Finally, in Section
6
, we use the Ermakov-Pinney equation to obtain in the nonsymmetric higher-dimensional case, a new family of optimal Hardy-weights which are larger at infinity than the optimal Hardy-weights obtained in
[
12
]
.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
\Omega
be a domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
We consider a second-order linear elliptic operator

with real coefficients defined in
\Omega

in divergence form

Pu:=-\mathrm{div}\,\left[A(x)\nabla u+u{\mathbf{\tilde{b}}}(x)\right]+{\mathbf{\overline{b}}}(x)\cdot\nabla u+c(x)u,

\Omega

{\mathbf{\tilde{b}}},{\mathbf{\overline{b}}},\mathbf{b},
are vector fields.
We also assume that

is locally uniformly elliptic, that is, for any
K\subset\subset\Omega

0<\lambda_{K}\leq\Lambda_{K}
such that for all

\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and

\lambda_{K}|\xi|^{2}\leq|\xi|_{A}^{2}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}a^{ij}(x)\xi_{i}\xi_{j}\leq\Lambda_{K}|\xi|^{2}.

We further assume the following regularity assumptions on the coefficients of

•

If

is of the form (

{\mathbf{\overline{b}}},{\mathbf{\tilde{b}}}\in L^{p}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})

•

If

\Omega
.

For a divergence form operator

is a
(weak) solution

\Omega

B(u,\varphi):=\int_{\Omega}\left(\langle A(x)\nabla u,\nabla\varphi\rangle+u\langle{\mathbf{\tilde{b}}},\nabla\varphi\rangle+\langle{\mathbf{\overline{b}}},\nabla u\rangle\varphi+cu\varphi\right)\!\!\,\mathrm{d}x=0

By elliptic regularity theory, each (weak) solution of

Definition 2.9

3.
Optimal Hardy-inequalities for Sturm-Liouville operators

In the one-dimensional case, optimal Hardy-weights for a general subcritical Sturm-Liouville operator on an interval
(a,b)
are characterized by the following integral conditions:

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\infty\leq a<b\leq\infty
. Consider the Sturm-Liouville operator

L(y):=-(py^{\prime})^{\prime}+qy\qquad\mbox{on }(a,b),

where
q\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(a,b)
,
p\in C^{1,\alpha}(a,b)
,
p>0
.

Then
0\lneqq w\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(a,b)
is an optimal Hardy-weight for
L
in
(a,b)
if and only if
there exists a positive function
f_{w}
satisfying
(L-w)f_{w}=0
in
(a,b)
such that

\int\limits_{a}^{c}\frac{1}{pf_{w}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t=\int\limits_{c}^{b}\frac{1}{pf_{w}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t=\int\limits_{a}^{c}wf_{w}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t=\int\limits_{c}^{b}wf_{w}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t=\infty,

(3.1)

for any
a<c<b
.

Proof.

Assume that
w
is an optimal Hardy-weight of
L
and fix

a<c<b
.
Then
L-w
is critical and the equation
(L-w)y=0
in
(a,b)
admits a unique positive solution (up to a multiplicative constant)
f_{w}
. Moreover,

\int_{a}^{c}\dfrac{1}{pf_{w}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}s=\int_{c}^{b}\dfrac{1}{pf_{w}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}s=\infty,

since otherwise, one of the functions

\quad f_{w}(t)\int\limits_{a}^{t}\dfrac{1}{pf_{w}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}s,\quad f_{w}(t)\int\limits_{t}^{b}\dfrac{1}{pf_{w}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}s,

would be another linearly independent positive solution of the equation
(L-w)y=0
in
(a,b)
.
In addition, the null-criticality of
L-w
with respect to
w
just means that for any
a<c<b
we have

\int\limits_{a}^{c}wf_{w}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t=\int\limits_{c}^{b}wf_{w}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t=\infty.

On the other hand, let
f_{w}
be a positive solution of the equation
(L-w)y=0
in
(a,b)
satisfying (
3.1
). The general solutions of the equation
(L-w)y=0
in
(a,b)
are of the form

\alpha f_{w}+\beta f_{w}\int_{c}^{t}\dfrac{1}{pf_{w}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}s,\quad\text{for some}~\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}~\text{ and }~a<c<b\,.

But by (
3.1
), such a solution with
\beta\neq 0
is negative either near
a
or near
b
.
Hence,
f_{w}
is the unique (up to a constant) positive solution of the equation
(L-w)y=0
in
(a,b)
. But in the one-dimensional case this implies that
L-w
is critical in
(a,b)

[
23
, Appendix 1]
.
Moreover, by definition, (
3.1
) implies the null-criticality of
L-w
with respect to
w
.
∎

Recall that by Remark
1.3
, if
W
is an optimal Hardy-weight for
P
in
\Omega
, then
W
is also optimal at infinity in
\Omega
. In other words, in this case we have
\lambda_{\infty}(P,W,\Omega)=1
. For the one-dimensional case we give a simple alternative proof for this assertion.

Proposition 3.2
.

Consider a nonnegative Sturm-Liouville operator

L(y):=-(py^{\prime})^{\prime}+qy\qquad\mbox{on }(a,b),

where
q\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(a,b)
,
p\in C^{1,\alpha}(a,b)
,
p>0
in
(a,b)
. If
w\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(a,b)
is an optimal Hardy-weight for
L
in
(a,b)
, then
\lambda_{\infty}(L,w,(a,b))=1
.

Proof.

Denote by
f_{w}
the ground state of
L-w
in
(a,b)
. The criticality of
L-w
in
(a,b)
implies that for a fixed
a<c<b
we have

\int_{a}^{c}\dfrac{1}{pf_{w}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{c}^{b}\dfrac{1}{pf_{w}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t=\infty.

Assume by contradiction that there exists
\lambda>0
such that

M:=L-(1+\lambda)w\geq 0
in
(a,b)\setminus K
for some
K\Subset(a,b)
. Note that
-Mf_{w}=\lambda wf_{w}
. Therefore,
the null-criticality with respect to
w
of
L-w
in
(a,b)
implies that

\min\Big\{\!\int_{a}^{c}\!\!f_{w}(-Mf_{w})\,\mathrm{d}t,\int_{c}^{b}\!\!f_{w}(-Mf_{w})\,\mathrm{d}t\!\Big\}=\lambda\min\Big\{\!\!\int_{a}^{c}\!\!wf_{w}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t,\int_{c}^{b}\!\!wf_{w}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\!\Big\}=\infty.

By
[
28
, Theorem 6.4.1]
, the equation

My=0
has oscillatory solutions both near
a
and near
b
. Since the existence of an oscillatory solution contradicts the generalized maximum principle, it follows that
M\not\geq 0
in
(a,b)\setminus K
, but this contradicts our assumption. ∎

4.
Ermakov-Pinney equation and one-dimensional Hardy inequalities

In the present section we introduce a new approach to construct Hardy-type inequalities in one-dimension. We exploit the explicit general solutions of the well known Ermakov-Pinney semilinear ordinary differential equation, to obtain Hardy-type inequalities for one-dimensional Schrödinger operators. Moreover, this method gives rise to an infinite converging series of Hardy-weights in the spirit of
[
7
]
.

Consider the classical one-dimensional Hardy’s inequality

\int_{0}^{\infty}|\phi^{\prime}(t)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\geq\int_{0}^{\infty}\dfrac{|\phi(t)|^{2}}{4t^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t\qquad\forall\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+}).

It is known that
w(t):=1/(4t^{2})
is an optimal Hardy-weight for the Laplacian on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
with a ground state
f_{0}(t):=\sqrt{2t}
.
[
12
]
. Notice the functional relation between
w
and the corresponding ground state

f_{0}(t)=\sqrt{2t}=w(t)^{-1/4}
. Equivalently, the ground state
f_{0}
solves the Ermakov-Pinney semilinear differential equation
[
14
,
27
]

-y^{\prime\prime}=\frac{1}{y^{3}}\qquad\mbox{in }(0,\infty).

Motivating by the above observation, we use the Ermakov-Pinney equation to construct new

strictly positive
Hardy-weights.

Let
q\in L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
, and
k>0.

By
[
14
,
16
,
27
]
, a general positive solution to the Ermakov-Pinney equation

-y^{\prime\prime}+qy=\dfrac{k}{y^{3}}\qquad\mbox{in }(a,b),

(4.1)

is given by

y=\sqrt{\left|c_{1}v_{1}^{2}+c_{2}v_{2}^{2}+2c_{3}v_{1}v_{2}\right|}\,,

where
c_{i},i=1,2,3
are fixed real numbers
such that
c_{3}^{2}-c_{1}c_{2}=k
, and
v_{i}
,
i=1,2
satisfy the linear Schrödinger equation

-u^{\prime\prime}+qu=0,\quad\mbox{ such that }\;\;W(v_{1},v_{2}):=v_{1}v_{2}^{\prime}-v_{2}v_{1}^{\prime}=1\quad\mbox{in }(a,b).

Here
(a,b)
is any subinterval of the sets

\displaystyle\left\{t\in(a,b)\mid\dfrac{v_{1}(t)}{v_{2}(t)}\neq\dfrac{-c_{3}\pm\sqrt{k}}{c_{1}}\right\}

\displaystyle\mbox{if }c_{1}\neq 0,

\displaystyle\left\{t\in(a,b)\mid\dfrac{v_{2}(t)}{v_{1}(t)}\neq\dfrac{-c_{3}\pm\sqrt{k}}{c_{2}}\right\}

\displaystyle\mbox{if }c_{1}=0~\text{and}~c_{2}\neq 0.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
q\in L^{1}_{{\rm loc}}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
. Suppose that the equation

L(y)=-y^{\prime\prime}+qy=0

admits two linearly independent positive solutions,
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
, in
(0,b)
for some
b>0
such that

v_{1}
has minimal growth near
0
, and
W(v_{1},v_{2})=v_{1}^{\prime}v_{2}-v_{1}v_{2}^{\prime}=1
.
Set

f_{w}:=\sqrt{\left|c_{1}v_{1}^{2}+c_{2}v_{2}^{2}+2c_{3}v_{1}v_{2}\right|},

where
c_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
satisfy
c_{3}^{2}-c_{1}c_{2}=1
.
Then there exists
0<m\leq b
such that

f_{w}
is a (well-defined) positive solution of the equation

(L-w)y=0\qquad\mbox{in }(0,m),

where

w:=\frac{L(f_{w})}{f_{w}}=\dfrac{1}{f_{w}^{4}}>0\qquad\mbox{in }(0,m).

Moreover, for any
0<\varepsilon<m

\int_{0}^{m-\varepsilon}\frac{1}{f_{w}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{m-\varepsilon}wf_{w}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t=\infty.

Proof.

Let
0<b_{1}<b
be fixed.
By reduction of order, given
v_{1}
, a second local linearly independent positive solution of the equation
Ly=0
in
(0,b_{1})
is given by

v(t)=v_{1}(t)\int_{t}^{b_{1}}\dfrac{1}{v_{1}(s)^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}s\qquad 0<t<b_{1}.

(4.2)

Since
v(b_{1})=0
it follows from
[
23
, Appendix 1]
that
v(t)
has minimal growth near
b_{1}
, and hence,

v_{2}=\alpha v_{1}+\beta v
, where
\alpha\geq 0
and
\beta>0
.

The assumption that
v_{1}
is a positive solution of minimal growth near
0
of the equation
Ly=0
implies that
v_{1}\leq C_{\varepsilon}v_{2}
and
v_{1}\leq C_{\varepsilon}v
in
(0,\varepsilon)
for any
0<\varepsilon<b_{1}
.
Moreover, since
v_{1}^{\prime}v_{2}-v_{1}v_{2}^{\prime}=1
, it follows that
v_{1}/v_{2}
is monotone increasing. Therefore, the singular points of
f_{w}
are isolated. Consequently,
f_{w}
is well defined in
(0,m),
where

m=\min\{b_{1},t_{0}-\delta\}
. Here
t_{0}
is first zero of
f_{w}
and
0<\delta<t_{0}.

Given
0<\varepsilon<m
let us compute

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{m-\varepsilon}\dfrac{1}{f_{w}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{m-\varepsilon}\dfrac{1}{|c_{1}v_{1}^{2}+c_{2}v_{2}^{2}+2c_{3}v_{1}v_{2}|}\,\mathrm{d}t\geq C\int_{0}^{m-\varepsilon}\dfrac{1}{v_{2}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{m-\varepsilon}\left(\alpha v_{1}+\beta v_{1}\int_{t}^{b_{1}}v_{1}^{-2}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\!\!\!-2}\,\mathrm{d}t\geq\int_{0}^{m-\varepsilon}\left(\alpha_{1}v_{1}+\beta v_{1}\int_{t}^{m-\varepsilon}v_{1}^{-2}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\!\!\!-2}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{m-\varepsilon}\left(\beta_{1}v_{1}\int_{t}^{m-\varepsilon}v_{1}^{-2}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\!\!\!-2}\,\mathrm{d}t,

where
\alpha_{1}\geq\alpha
and
\beta_{1}\geq\beta
.
The change of variable
\tau:=\int_{t}^{m-\varepsilon}v_{1}^{-2}\,\mathrm{d}s
implies that

\int_{0}^{m-\varepsilon}\dfrac{1}{f_{w}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t\geq C\int_{0}^{M}\dfrac{1}{\tau^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\tau=\infty.

Moreover,

\int_{0}^{m-\varepsilon}wf_{w}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{m-\varepsilon}\dfrac{1}{f_{w}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t=\infty.\qquad\qed

Remark 4.2
.

If
c_{1},c_{2}
, and
c_{3}
are nonnegative, then for any
0<m<b

the function
f_{w}
in Lemma
4.1
is well defined in
(0,m)
.

As a corollary of Lemma
4.1
we obtain the following result:

Proposition 4.3
.

Assume that
Ly:=-y^{\prime\prime}+qy
is nonnegative in
\mathbb{R}_{+}
, where
q\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
. Fix
m>0
. Then there exists an infinite sequence of strictly positive functions

\{w_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
(depending on
m
), such that for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
the function

\tilde{w}_{k}:=\sum_{i=1}^{k}{w}_{i}

satisfies:

•

M_{k}:=L-\tilde{w}_{k}\geq 0
in
(0,m)
.

•

There exists a positive solution
y_{k}
of the equation
M_{k}y=0
in
(0,m+1)
such that

\int_{0}^{m}\dfrac{1}{y_{k}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{m}\tilde{w}_{k}y_{k}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t=\infty,

and
for any
0<\varepsilon<m
,
\lambda_{0}(L,\tilde{w}_{k},(0,\varepsilon))=1
.

Proof.

Fix
m\in(0,\infty)
.
Let
v_{1,0}
and
v_{2,0}
be two positive solutions of
Ly=0
in
(0,m+1)
(depending on
m
) such that their Wronskian
W(v_{1,0},v_{2,0})
equals
1
, and such that
v_{1,0}
has minimal growth in a neighborhood of
0
. In particular,
v_{1,0}
is positive and well-defined in
\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Note also that using (
4.2
), it follows that
v_{2,0}
is well-defined and positive in
(0,m+1)
.

Define
w_{1}:=y_{1}^{-4}
, where

y_{1}:=\sqrt{c_{1,1}v_{1,0}^{2}+c_{2,1}v_{2,0}^{2}+2c_{3,1}v_{1,0}v_{2,0}}\,,

and
c_{i,1}
, are positive numbers for
i=1,2,3
satisfying
(c_{3,1})^{2}-c_{1,1}c_{2,1}=1
. By Remark
4.2
,
y_{1}
is a positive solution of the equation

M_{1}(y):=(L-{w}_{1})y=0\qquad\mbox{in }(0,m+1).

In particular,
M_{1}
is subcritical in
(0,m+1-\varepsilon_{1})
, where
\varepsilon_{1}=1/2
.

Repeating the same argument, we obtain in the
j
-th step,
j\geq 2
,

\displaystyle y_{j}:=\!\sqrt{c_{1,j}v_{1,j-1}^{2}\!+c_{2,j}v_{2,j-1}^{2}\!+\!2c_{3,j}v_{1,j-1}v_{2,j-1}}\;\mbox{ in }(0,m+1-\varepsilon_{j-1}),

\displaystyle\mbox{where }c_{i,j}>0,\mbox{and }(c_{3,j})^{2}-c_{1,j}c_{2,j}=1.

\displaystyle{w}_{j}:=(y_{j})^{-4}\quad\mbox{in }(0,m+1-\varepsilon_{j-1}),\;\;\varepsilon_{j}:=\varepsilon_{j-1}+(1/2)^{j}.

\displaystyle v_{1,j}\mbox{ is a positive solution of $M_{j}u=0$ in $(0,m+1-\varepsilon_{j})$ of minimal growth at }0.

\displaystyle v_{2,j}(t):=\!\alpha_{j}v_{1,j}\!+\!\beta_{j}v_{1,j}(t)\!\!\int_{t}^{m+1-\varepsilon_{j}}\!\!\!\frac{1}{v_{1,j}(s)^{2}}\!\,\mathrm{d}s\!>\!0,\;\mbox{ in }(0,m+1-\varepsilon_{j}),W(v_{1,j},v_{2,j})=1,

where
\alpha_{j},\beta_{j}>0
. So,
M_{k}
is subcritical in
(0,m)
.
Moreover, by Lemma
4.1
, we obtain

\int_{0}^{m}y_{k}^{2}{w}_{k}\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{m}\dfrac{1}{y_{k}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t=\infty,

and consequently,

\int_{0}^{m}y_{k}^{2}\tilde{w}_{k}\,\mathrm{d}t=\infty.

Hence, as in Proposition
3.2
, it follows that

\lambda_{0}(L,\tilde{w}_{k},(0,\varepsilon))=1
for all
\varepsilon\in(0,m)
.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

For any
k\in\mathbb{N}
the inequality
L-\tilde{w}_{k}\geq 0
in
(0,m)
in Proposition
4.3
implies the inequality

\int_{0}^{m}\big(|\varphi^{\prime}|^{2}+q\varphi^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t\geq\int_{0}^{m}\tilde{w}_{k}\varphi^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\qquad\forall\in C_{0}^{\infty}(0,m).

Fix
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(0,m)
, it follows that

\int\limits_{0}^{m}\tilde{w}_{k}\varphi^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\leq\int_{0}^{m}\big(|\varphi^{\prime}|^{2}+q\varphi^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t\leq\int_{0}^{m}\big(|\varphi^{\prime}|^{2}+|q|\varphi^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t\leq C.

Since the sequence
\{\tilde{w}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
is increasing in
k
and positive, it follows by the monotone convergence theorem that

\tilde{w}:=\lim_{k\to\infty}\tilde{w}_{k}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}{w}_{k}

is finite almost everywhere in
(0,m)
. Moreover,
\tilde{w}
is a Hardy-weight for
L
in
(0,m)
.

5.
Improved and optimal Hardy-weights in higher dimensions

In the present section we reduce the problem of constructing optimal Hardy-weights in higher dimensions to a one-dimensional problem. Roughly speaking, each optimal Hardy-weight for the one-dimensional Laplacian yields in the symmetric case optimal Hardy-inequalities for subcritical operators in higher dimensions.

Definition 5.1
.

We say that a function
0\leq w\in L^{\infty}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
belongs to
\mathcal{W}_{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
if
w
is an optimal Hardy-weight for the one-dimensional Laplace operator

Ly:=-y^{\prime\prime}\qquad\mbox{on }\mathbb{R}_{+}.

We have.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
0\leq w\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}_{+}).
Then
w\in\mathcal{W}_{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
with ground state
f_{w}
if and only if the following three conditions are satisfied:

(1)

There exists
f_{w}>0
such that
(L-w)f_{w}=0
in
\mathbb{R}_{+}
,

(2)

\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{1}{f_{w}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{1}^{\infty}\dfrac{1}{f_{w}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t=\infty}
,

(3)

\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{1}wf_{w}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{1}^{\infty}wf_{w}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t=\infty}
,

Proof.

Follows immediately from Proposition
3.1
.
∎

Our main result of the the present section is as follows.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, be a domain containing
x_{0}
. Let
P
be a subcritical operator in
\Omega
and let
G(x):=G_{P}^{\Omega}(x,x_{0})
be its positive minimal Green function with singularity at
x_{0}
. Let
0\lneqq\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
and consider the associated Green potential

G_{\varphi}(x)
(given by (
1.1
)).
Assume that there exists
u\in\mathcal{C}_{P}(\Omega)
satisfying

\lim_{x\to\overline{\infty}}\dfrac{G(x)}{u(x)}=0.

(5.1)

Fix
w\in\mathcal{W}_{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
, and let
f_{w}(t)
be the corresponding ground state.
Suppose that
f_{w}^{\prime}\geq 0
on
\{t=G_{\varphi}(x)/u(x)\mid x\in\mbox{\rm supp}\,(\varphi)\}
.
Define

W:=\dfrac{P\left(uf_{w}\left(G_{\varphi}/u\right)\right)}{uf_{w}\left(G_{\varphi}/u\right)}\,.

Then

(1)

W\geq 0
in
\Omega
and
W:=\left|\nabla(G_{\varphi}/u)\right|_{A}^{2}w(G_{\varphi}/u),~\text{in}~\Omega\setminus\mbox{\rm supp}\,(\varphi).

(2)

P-W
is critical in
\Omega
with ground state
uf_{w}(G_{\varphi}/u)
.

(3)

W
is optimal in a neighborhood of infinity in
\Omega
.

(4)

If
P
is symmetric, then
P-W
is null-critical with respect to
W
, and therefore,
W
is an optimal Hardy-weight for
P
in
\Omega
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume that
G_{\varphi}/u<1
in
\Omega
. By Lemma
5.2
, the function

f_{1}(t):=f_{w}(t)\int\limits_{1}^{t}\dfrac{1}{f_{w}^{2}(s)}\,\mathrm{d}s

is a linearly independent solution of the equation
(L-w)y=0
in
\mathbb{R}_{+}
which is positive for
t>1
, negative for
t<1
, and satisfies

\lim_{t\to 0}\dfrac{f_{w}(t)}{f_{1}(t)}=\lim_{t\to\infty}\dfrac{f_{w}(t)}{f_{1}(t)}=0.

(5.2)

Consider the functions
h,v:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
given by

v(x):=u(x)f_{w}\left(\dfrac{G_{\varphi}(x)}{u(x)}\right),\qquad h(x):=u(x)f_{1}\left(\dfrac{G_{\varphi}(x)}{u(x)}\right).

A direct calculation (see (4.13) in
[
12
]
) shows that
for any
u\in\mathcal{C}_{P}(\Omega)
and
g\in\mathcal{S}_{P}(\Omega)

P(uf_{w}(g/u))=-uf_{w}^{\prime\prime}(g/u)|\nabla(g/u)|_{A}^{2}+uf_{w}^{\prime}(g/u)P(g).

(5.3)

In particular,
W\geq 0
in
\Omega
,
and
v\in\mathcal{C}_{P-W}(\Omega)
. Moreover,

\displaystyle P(v)\!=\!-|\nabla(G_{\varphi}\!/u)|_{A}^{2}uf_{w}^{\prime\prime}(G_{\varphi}\!/u)\!=\!|\nabla(G_{\varphi}\!/u)|_{A}^{2}w(G_{\varphi}\!/u)uf_{w}(G_{\varphi}\!/u)\!=\!Wv\text{ in }\Omega\!\setminus\!\mbox{\rm supp}\,(\varphi),

\displaystyle P(h)\!=\!-|\nabla(G_{\varphi}\!/u)|_{A}^{2}uf_{1}^{\prime\prime}(G_{\varphi}\!/u)\!=\!|\nabla(G_{\varphi}\!/u)|_{A}^{2}w(G_{\varphi}\!/u)uf_{1}(G_{\varphi}\!/u)\!=\!Wh\text{ in }\Omega\!\setminus\!\mbox{\rm supp}\,(\varphi).

Hence,
h,v\in\mathcal{C}_{P-W}(\Omega\setminus\mbox{\rm supp}\,(\varphi))
.
Moreover, (
5.1
) and (
5.2
) imply

\lim_{x\to\overline{\infty}}\dfrac{v(x)}{h(x)}=\lim_{x\to\overline{\infty}}\dfrac{u(x)f_{w}\left(\dfrac{G_{\varphi}(x)}{u(x)}\right)}{u(x)f_{1}\left(\dfrac{G_{\varphi}(x)}{u(x)}\right)}=0.

By a Khas’minskiĭ-type criterion
[
12
, Proposition 6.1]
, it follows that
v
has minimal growth in a neighborhood of infinity in
\Omega
, implying that
v
is a ground state of the operator
P-W
in
\Omega
, and by Theorem
2.7
,
P-W
is critical in
\Omega.

Next we prove that
W
is optimal at
\overline{\infty}
(recall that
P
is not necessarily symmetric). Let
\xi>0
, and consider the equation

-y^{\prime\prime}-wy=\xi wy,~\text{in}~\mathbb{R}_{+}.

(5.4)

Lemma
5.2
implies that

\int_{0}^{1}wf_{w}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t=\infty.

By Proposition
3.2
(or
[
28
, Theorem 6.4.1]
), there exists an oscillatory (near
0
) solution
y_{\xi}
to (
5.4
) satisfying
y_{\xi},y_{\xi}^{\prime}\in AC_{\text{loc}}(0,1)
. Therefore,
uy_{\xi}(G_{\varphi}/u)
is an oscillatory solution of the operator
P-(1+\xi)W
in a neighborhood of
\overline{\infty}
. Hence,
P-(1+\xi)W
does not have a positive solution in a neighborhood of
\overline{\infty}
. In other words,
\lambda_{\infty}=\lambda_{0}=1
. Thus,
W
is optimal at
\overline{\infty}.

It remains to prove that when
P
is symmetric,
P-W
is null-critical with respect to
W
.

Take
\alpha>0
sufficiently small such that

\{0<G_{\varphi}/u<\alpha\}\cap\mbox{\rm supp}\,\varphi=\emptyset.

For any
0<\varepsilon<\alpha
, the coarea formula (see (9.4)) in
[
12
]
) implies

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\varepsilon<G_{\varphi}/u<\alpha}v^{2}W\,\mathrm{d}x=\!\!\!\int\limits_{\varepsilon<G_{\varphi}/u<\alpha}\!\!\!u^{2}f_{w}^{2}(G_{\varphi}/u)|\nabla(G_{\varphi}/u)|_{A}^{2}w(G_{\varphi}/u)\,\mathrm{d}x=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}f_{w}^{2}(t)w(t)\int\limits_{G_{\varphi}/u=t}u^{2}|\nabla(G_{\varphi}/u)|_{A}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\,\mathrm{d}t=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}f_{w}^{2}(t)w(t)\int\limits_{G_{\varphi}/u=t}\langle uA\nabla G_{\varphi}-G_{\varphi}A\nabla u,\vec{\sigma}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\,\mathrm{d}t,

(5.5)

where
\vec{\sigma}
is a normal vector (in the metric
|\cdot|_{A}
) to the level set
\{G_{\varphi}/u=t\}.

By Green’s formula for
0<\varepsilon<t_{1}<t_{2}<\alpha
such that
{t_{1}<G_{\varphi}/u<t_{2}}
is a regular domain (see
[
12
]
)), we have

\displaystyle 0=\int\limits_{t_{1}<G_{\varphi}/u<t_{2}}(P(u)G_{\varphi}-P(G_{\varphi})u)\,\mathrm{d}x=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{G_{\varphi}/u=t_{2}}\langle uA\nabla G_{\varphi}-G_{\varphi}A\nabla u,\vec{\sigma}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s-\int\limits_{G_{\varphi}/u=t_{1}}\langle uA\nabla G_{\varphi}-G_{\varphi}A\nabla u,\vec{\sigma}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s.

Hence, there exists
\beta
such that for any
t\in(0,\alpha)

\int_{G_{\varphi}/u=t}\langle uA\nabla G_{\varphi}-G_{\varphi}A\nabla u,\vec{\sigma}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s=\beta.

Moreover, by (
5
), we infer that
\beta>0
. Consequently,

\int_{0<G_{\varphi}/u<\alpha}v^{2}W\,\mathrm{d}x=\beta\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}f_{w}^{2}(t)w(t)\,\mathrm{d}t=\infty.\qquad\qed

Remark 5.4
.

If condition (
5.1
) is satisfied in
\Omega
for some
u\in\mathcal{C}_{P}(\Omega)
, then (
5.1
) is satisfied in
\Omega^{*}:=\Omega\setminus\{x_{0}\}
for any
x_{0}\in\Omega\setminus\mbox{\rm supp}\,(\varphi)
with
\tilde{u}:=u+G
replacing

. Indeed, one may
take the pair
G_{\varphi}
and
\tilde{u}
. In this case, the corresponding (optimal in the symmetric case) Hardy-weight for
P
in
\Omega^{*}
is
W:=\dfrac{P\left(\tilde{u}f_{w}\left(G_{\varphi}/\tilde{u}\right)\right)}{\tilde{u}f_{w}\left(G_{\varphi}/\tilde{u}\right)}
.

As a corollary of Proposition
6.5
and Theorem
5.3
, we obtain the following result which generalizes the results of Barbatis, Filippas and Tertikas
[
6
]
:

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, be a domain containing
x_{0}
. Let
P
a subcritical operator in
\Omega
, and let
G(x):=G_{P}^{\Omega}(x,x_{0})
be its Green function with singularity at
x_{0}
, and let
G_{\varphi}
be a Green potential with density
0\lneqq\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
(see (

)).
Then there exists an infinite sequence of Hardy-weights

\{{W}_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
such that for any
k\in\mathbb{N}

the function

\tilde{W}_{k}:=\sum_{i=1}^{k}{W}_{i}

satisfies
P-\tilde{W}_{k}\geq 0
in
\Omega
and
\lambda_{0}(P,\tilde{W}_{k},\Omega)=\lambda_{\infty}(P,\tilde{W}_{k},\Omega)=1
. Moreover,
\tilde{W}:=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}{W}_{k}
is a Hardy-weight for
P
in
\Omega
.

Proof.

Apply Proposition
4.3
with
q=0
to obtain a sequence
\{w_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
such that

-y^{\prime\prime}-\sum_{i=1}^{k}w_{i}\geq 0~\mbox{in}~(0,\sup\limits_{\Omega}(G_{\varphi}/u)).

By the proof of Theorem
5.3
the function

\tilde{W}_{k}:=|\nabla(G_{\varphi}/u)|_{A}^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{k}{w}_{i}(G_{\varphi}/u)
is a Hardy-weight of

i.e.,
P-\tilde{W}_{k}\geq 0
in
\Omega
. Moreover, as in Remark
4.4
, it follows that
\tilde{W}
is a well defined Hardy-weight for
P
in
\Omega
.

Assume by contradiction that
\lambda_{0}(P,\tilde{W}_{k},\Omega\setminus K)>1
, namely, there exists
K\Subset\Omega
such that

P-(1+\varepsilon^{2})\tilde{W}_{k}\geq 0
in
\Omega\setminus K
for some
\varepsilon>0
. By the same argument as in Lemma
3.2
there exists an oscillatory solution of the equation
-y^{\prime\prime}-(1+\varepsilon^{2})(\sum_{i=1}^{k}{w}_{i})y=0
near
0
, implying that there exists an oscillatory solution of the equation
P-(1+\varepsilon^{2})\tilde{W}_{k}=0
near
\overline{\infty}
. Such a solution contradicts the maximum principle. Hence,

\mathcal{C}_{P-(1+\varepsilon^{2})\tilde{W}_{k}}(\Omega\setminus K)=\emptyset
, and we arrive at a contradiction.
∎

6.
Improved optimal Hardy-weights in the nonsymmetric case

In the previous section we proved for symmetric operators that the Hardy-weights obtained in Theorem
5.3
are optimal, while in the nonsymmetric case we could only prove the criticality and optimality at infinity. In the present section we use the Ermakov-Pinney equation to obtain a family of optimal Hardy-weights for not necessarily symmetric operators.

The following lemma exploits the general positive solutions of the Ermakov-Pinney equation
y^{\prime\prime}(t)=-\dfrac{1}{y^{3}}
in
(0,2/a)
that vanishes at
t=0
, namely,
y_{a}(t):=\sqrt{2t-at^{2}}
to obtain optimal Hardy-weights.
Using Proposition
3.1
we obtain:

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
w(t):=(2t-at^{2})^{-2}
and
f_{w}(t):=\sqrt{2t-at^{2}}
, where
a>0
is fixed. Then

(1)

-f_{w}^{\prime\prime}-wf_{w}=0
, and
f_{w}>0
in
(0,2/a)
.

(2)

\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{1/a}\dfrac{1}{f_{w}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{1/a}^{2/a}\dfrac{1}{f_{w}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t=\infty}
,

(3)

\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{1/a}wf_{w}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{1/a}^{2/a}wf_{w}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t}=\infty
.

In particular,
w
is an optimal Hardy-weight for the operator
Ly=-y^{\prime\prime}
in
(0,2/a)
.

Remark 6.2
.

We exclude the case
a\leq 0
since we are interested in Hardy-weights greater than the classical Hardy-weight
w_{\mathrm{class}}(t)=(2t)^{-2}.

The following proposition is an important ingredient in the construction of an improved optimal weight for the nonsymmetric case.

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
w(t)=(2t-at^{2})^{-2}
. Then for any
\xi,M,a>0
, and
t\in(0,2/a)
the function

u_{\xi}(t):=\sqrt{2t-at^{2}}\cos\left(\dfrac{\xi}{2}\log\left(\dfrac{Mt}{2-at}\right)\right)

satisfies the following properties:

(1)

-u_{\xi}^{\prime\prime}-(1+\xi^{2})wu_{\xi}=0\quad\mbox{in }(2/(M\mathrm{e}^{\pi/\xi}+a),2/(M+a))
,

(2)

The boundary condition:
u_{\xi}^{\prime}(2/(M+a))=\dfrac{M^{2}-a^{2}}{4M}u_{\xi}(2/(M+a))
,

(3)

The boundary condition:
u_{\xi}\!\left(2/(M\mathrm{e}^{\pi/\xi}+a)\right)=u_{3\xi}\!\left(2/(M\mathrm{e}^{\pi/\xi}+a)\right)=0
,

(4)

u_{\xi}
converges pointwise to
\sqrt{2t-at^{2}}
as
\xi\to 0
,

(5)

|u_{\xi}(t)|\leq\sqrt{2t-at^{2}}.

Proof.

The assertions of the proposition can be verified straightforwardly, see Appendix for a method to obtain the solutions
u_{\xi}
.
∎

We proceed to find a family of optimal Hardy-weights in
\Omega
greater (in a neighborhood of
\infty
) than
W_{\mathrm{class}}
, the classical optimal weight obtained in
[
19
]
which satisfies

W_{\mathrm{class}}=\dfrac{|\nabla(G_{\varphi}/u)|_{A}^{2}}{4(G_{\varphi}/u)^{2}}\qquad\mbox{in }\Omega\setminus\mbox{\rm supp}\,(\varphi).

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

. Let
P

(6.1)

Let

Then

W:=\frac{P\left(uf_{w}\left(G_{\varphi}/u\right)\right)}{uf_{w}\left(G_{\varphi}/u\right)}

is an optimal Hardy-weight of

in
\Omega
, and

W=\frac{|\nabla(G_{\varphi}/u)|_{A}^{2}}{(G_{\varphi}/u)^{2}(2-a(G_{\varphi}/u))^{2}}\geq W_{\mathrm{class}}:=\frac{|\nabla(G_{\varphi}/u)|_{A}^{2}}{4(G_{\varphi}/u)^{2}}\quad\text{in }\Omega\setminus\mbox{\rm supp}\,(\varphi).

Proof.

Obviously,
aG_{\varphi}/u<1

therefore, the function

We first prove the criticality of

\Omega

Let

Next we prove the null-criticality of

with respect to

Fix a sufficiently large constant

such that

\left\{x\in\Omega\left|\;0<G_{\varphi}/u<2/M\right.\right\}\cap\mbox{\rm supp}\,(\varphi)=\emptyset.

(6.2)

Let

\begin{cases}(P^{*}-W)u=0&\text{in }\Omega_{\xi},\\[8.53581pt]

(6.3)

Let

\displaystyle\xi^{2}\int_{\Omega_{\xi}}W\phi_{\xi}\phi_{\xi}^{*}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{\xi}}\phi_{\xi}^{*}(P-W)\phi_{\xi}\,\mathrm{d}x=B.T(\phi_{\xi},\phi_{\xi}^{*}),

\displaystyle 9\xi^{2}\int_{\Omega_{\xi}}W\phi_{3\xi}\phi_{\xi}^{*}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{\xi}}\phi_{\xi}^{*}(P-W)\phi_{3\xi}\,\mathrm{d}x=B.T(\phi_{3\xi},\phi_{\xi}^{*}),

where the boundary terms are

\displaystyle B.T(\phi_{\xi},\phi_{\xi}^{*})

\displaystyle=\int_{\Gamma}\,\langle\phi_{\xi}^{*}A\nabla\phi_{\xi}-\phi_{\xi}A\nabla\phi_{\xi}^{*}+\phi_{\xi}\phi_{\xi}^{*}({\mathbf{\overline{b}}}-{\mathbf{\tilde{b}}}),\vec{\sigma}\rangle\text{d}\sigma,

and where

and (

\displaystyle B.T(\phi_{\xi},\phi_{\xi}^{*})\!=\!\int_{\Gamma}\langle\phi_{0}\phi_{0}^{*}A\nabla u\!+\!\frac{M^{2}-a^{2}}{4M}u\phi_{0}\phi_{0}^{*}\nabla(G_{\phi}/u)\!-\!\phi_{0}A\nabla\phi_{\xi}^{*}\!+\!\phi_{0}\phi_{0}^{*}({\mathbf{\overline{b}}}\!-\!{\mathbf{\tilde{b}}}),\vec{\sigma}\rangle\text{d}\sigma

where the last equality follows since the integrand in the above expression is independent of the choice of either

or

Hence, by (

9\xi^{2}\int_{\Omega_{\xi}}W\phi_{3\xi}\phi_{\xi}^{*}\,\mathrm{d}x=\xi^{2}\int_{\Omega_{\xi}}W\phi_{\xi}\phi_{\xi}^{*}\,\mathrm{d}x.

(6.5)

For nonregular
\Omega_{\xi}
, we obtain (

Assume by contradiction that

is positive-critical (i.e.,

Theorem 6.6
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, be a domain containing
x_{0}
. Let
P
be a symmetric subcritical linear operator in
\Omega
and let
G(x):=G_{P}^{\Omega}(x,x_{0})
be its minimal positive Green function with singularity at
x_{0}
. Consider a Green potential
G_{\varphi}
as in (
1.1
) and
u\in\mathcal{C}_{P}(\Omega)
satisfying (
1.2
). Set

0<a<\dfrac{2}{\sup\limits_{x\in\Omega}{\left(G_{\varphi}(x)/u(x)\right)}}\,,

and consider the “classical” Hardy-weight and the Hardy-weight given by Theorem
6.4
, respectively, i.e.,

W_{\mathrm{class}}=\dfrac{|\nabla(G_{\varphi}/u)|_{A}^{2}}{4(G_{\varphi}/u)^{2}}\,,\quad W=\dfrac{|\nabla(G_{\varphi}/u)|_{A}^{2}}{(G_{\varphi}/u)^{2}(2-a(G_{\varphi}/u))^{2}}\,.

Then for any
\psi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega\setminus\mbox{\rm supp}\,(\varphi))
the following Rellich-type inequality holds true

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\setminus\mbox{\rm supp}\,(\varphi)}\dfrac{(P(\psi))^{2}(G_{\varphi}/u)}{W-W_{\mathrm{class}}}\,\mathrm{d}x\geq\int_{\Omega\setminus\mbox{\rm supp}\,(\varphi)}\psi^{2}(W-W_{\mathrm{class}})(G_{\varphi}/u)\,\mathrm{d}x.

Proof.

Assume first that
P({\bf 1})=0
and let
u={\bf 1}
, and assume that
\lim_{x\to\overline{\infty}}G(x)=0
.
Recall that
W>W_{\mathrm{class}}
in
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega\setminus\mbox{\rm supp}\,(\varphi))
. Consequently, for
\psi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega\setminus\mbox{\rm supp}\,(\varphi))
, we have (see (10.9) in
[
12
]
),

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\setminus\mbox{\rm supp}\,(\varphi)}P(\psi)\psi G_{\varphi}\,\mathrm{d}x=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\setminus\mbox{\rm supp}\,(\varphi)}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!P(\psi\sqrt{G_{\varphi}})\psi\!\sqrt{G_{\varphi}}\!\,\mathrm{d}x+\!\frac{1}{2}\!\int_{\Omega\setminus\mbox{\rm supp}\,(\varphi)}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\psi^{2}P(G_{\varphi})\!\,\mathrm{d}x-\!\!\int_{\Omega\setminus\mbox{\rm supp}\,(\varphi)}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!P(\sqrt{G_{\varphi}})\psi^{2}\sqrt{G_{\varphi}}\!\,\mathrm{d}x=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\setminus\mbox{\rm supp}\,(\varphi)}P(\psi\sqrt{G_{\varphi}})\psi\sqrt{G_{\varphi}}\!\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Omega\setminus\mbox{\rm supp}\,(\varphi)}\psi^{2}W_{\mathrm{class}}G_{\varphi}\!\,\mathrm{d}x\geq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\setminus\mbox{\rm supp}\,(\varphi)}\psi^{2}(W-W_{\mathrm{class}})G_{\varphi}\,\mathrm{d}x\geq 0,

where we used the inequality
P-W\geq 0
to derive the fourth line.
The Cauchy-Schwartz inequality implies

\left(\!\int_{\Omega\setminus\mbox{\rm supp}\,(\varphi)}\!\!\!\!P(\psi)\psi G_{\varphi}\!\,\mathrm{d}x\!\!\right)^{\!\!2}\!\!\leq\!\left(\!\int_{\Omega\setminus\mbox{\rm supp}\,(\varphi)}\!\!\dfrac{(P\psi)^{2}G_{\varphi}}{(W\!-\!W_{\mathrm{class}})}\!\,\mathrm{d}x\!\right)\!\!\left(\!\int_{\Omega\setminus\mbox{\rm supp}\,}\!\!\!\!\!\psi^{2}(W\!-\!W_{\mathrm{class}})G_{\varphi}\!\,\mathrm{d}x\!\right)\!,

and therefore,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\setminus\mbox{\rm supp}\,(\varphi)}\dfrac{(P\psi)^{2}G_{\varphi}}{(W-W_{\mathrm{class}})}\,\mathrm{d}x\geq\int_{\Omega\setminus\mbox{\rm supp}\,(\varphi)}\psi^{2}(W-W_{\mathrm{class}})G_{\varphi}\,\mathrm{d}x.

(6.6)

Now let
P
be a general subcritical operator in
\Omega
which is symmetric in

, and consider the operator
P_{u}:={u}^{-1}Pu
which is the ground state transform of the operator
P
(see for example,
[
12
, Section 4.1]

P_{u}
is symmetric in
L^{2}(\Omega,u^{2}\!\,\mathrm{d}x)
, and a Hardy-weight
W
for

corresponds to the Hardy-weight
Wu^{2}
for
P_{u}
.
Moreover, the corresponding positive minimal Green function is given by
G_{P_{u}}^{\Omega}(x,y)=G_{P}^{\Omega}(x,y)u(y)/u(x)
. Hence, for any
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
, we have

(G_{P_{u}}^{\Omega})_{\frac{\varphi}{u}}=(G_{P}^{\Omega})_{\varphi}/u
.

Let
\psi:=u\vartheta
where ,
\vartheta\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Recall that
P_{u}1=0
, consequently, using the obtained Rellich inequality for
P_{u}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\setminus\mbox{\rm supp}\,(\varphi)}\!\!\!\dfrac{(P(\psi))^{2}(G_{\varphi}/u)}{W-W_{\mathrm{class}}}\!\,\mathrm{d}x=\!\!\int_{\Omega\setminus\mbox{\rm supp}\,(\varphi)}\!\!\dfrac{(P_{u}(\vartheta))^{2}(G_{P_{u}}^{\Omega})_{\frac{\varphi}{u}}}{u^{2}(W-W_{\mathrm{class}})}\!\,\mathrm{d}x\geq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\setminus\mbox{\rm supp}\,(\varphi)}\!\!\!(u\vartheta)^{2}(W-W_{\mathrm{class}})(G_{P_{u}}^{\Omega})_{\frac{\varphi}{u}}\!\,\mathrm{d}x=\!\!\int_{\Omega\setminus\mbox{\rm supp}\,(\varphi)}\!\!\!\psi^{2}(W-W_{\mathrm{class}})(G_{\varphi}/u)\!\,\mathrm{d}x.\qed

7.
Examples

We illustrate our results with two explicit examples.

Example 7.1
.

Let
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{n},n\geq 3
, and consider the classical Hardy inequality

\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\geq\left(\dfrac{n-2}{2}\right)^{2}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}\dfrac{1}{4|x|^{2}}u^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\qquad\forall u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}).

Let
G(x,y)=C_{n}|x-y|^{2-n}
be the Green’s function of
-\Delta
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and let
G(x):=G(x,0).
It follows that

\left(\dfrac{n-2}{2}\right)^{2}\dfrac{1}{|x|^{2}}=\dfrac{1}{4}\left|\dfrac{\nabla G(x)}{G(x)}\right|^{2}

is an optimal Hardy weight in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
.

Consider the classical Hardy-weight for
-\Delta
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, namely
W=-\Delta G_{\varphi}/G_{\varphi}
, where
G_{\varphi}
is given by (
1.1
). We have

W=\dfrac{1}{4}\left|\frac{\nabla G_{\varphi}}{G_{\varphi}}\right|^{2}\qquad\mbox{in }\Omega\setminus\mbox{\rm supp}\,(\varphi).

Assume further that
\varphi
is rotationally invariant. Clearly, there exists
C>0
such that

G_{\varphi}(x)=CG(x)
for all
x\notin\mbox{\rm supp}\,(\varphi)
(see for example,
[
22
, Theorem 9.7]
).
Therefore,

\dfrac{1}{4}\left|\frac{\nabla G_{\varphi}(x)}{G_{\varphi}(x)}\right|^{2}=\dfrac{1}{4}\left|\frac{\nabla G(x)}{G(x)}\right|^{2}=\left(\dfrac{n-2}{2}\right)^{2}\dfrac{1}{|x|^{2}}\qquad\mbox{in }\Omega\setminus\mbox{\rm supp}\,(\varphi).

Consequently, Theorem
6.4
implies that there exists
a>0
and an optimal Hardy-weight
W
for
-\Delta
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfying

W(x)=\dfrac{|\nabla G_{\varphi}(x)|^{2}}{(2G_{\varphi}(x)-a(G_{\varphi}(x))^{2})^{2}}>\left(\dfrac{n-2}{2}\right)^{2}\dfrac{1}{|x|^{2}}\qquad\text{in }\Omega\setminus\mbox{\rm supp}\,(\varphi).

Example 7.2
.

Let
c_{1}>0
,
c_{2}\geq 0
and
c_{3}=\sqrt{1+c_{1}c_{2}}\,
, and consider the following positive harmonic functions in
(0,L):

v_{1,0}(t):=\sqrt{2}\,t,\qquad v_{2,0}(t):=\dfrac{L-t}{\sqrt{2}L}.

For all
k\geq 0
, let

v_{1,k+1}=\sqrt{c_{1}v_{1,k}^{2}+c_{2}v_{2,k}^{2}+2c_{3}v_{1,k}v_{2,k}}\,,\qquad v_{2,k+1}=v_{1,k+1}\int\limits_{t}^{L}\dfrac{1}{v_{1,k+1}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}s.

Then

\displaystyle G_{k+1}(t)\!\!

\displaystyle:=\!\!\!\int_{t}^{L}\dfrac{1}{v_{1,k+1}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}s=\!\!\int_{t}^{L}\dfrac{1}{v_{1,k}^{2}(s)}\dfrac{1}{c_{1}+c_{2}\left(\displaystyle{\int_{s}^{L}}\dfrac{1}{v_{1,k}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}z\right)^{\!\!2}+2c_{3}\displaystyle{\int_{s}^{L}}\dfrac{1}{v_{1,k}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}z}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{0}^{G_{k}(t)}\dfrac{1}{c_{1}+c_{2}\tau^{2}+2c_{3}\tau}\text{d}\tau=F(G_{k})-F(0),

where
F(\tau)=\displaystyle{\int}\dfrac{1}{c_{1}+c_{2}\tau^{2}+2c_{3}\tau}\text{d}\tau
and
G_{0}(t):=(L-t)/(2Lt)
. So,

G_{k}(t)=\begin{cases}F\left(G_{k-1}(t)\right)-F(0)&k\geq 1,\\[8.53581pt]
\dfrac{L-t}{2Lt}&k=0.\end{cases}

By Proposition
4.3
the function

w(t):=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{1}{v_{1,k}^{4}}=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}(G_{k}^{\prime})^{2}

is a Hardy-weight for
Ly=-y^{\prime\prime}
in
(0,L).
Furthermore, if
0<c_{1}\leq 1/L
, then

w>w_{\mathrm{class}}=(2t)^{-2}.

Moreover, if the assumptions of Lemma
5.5
are satisfied for a subcritical operator
P
in
\Omega
, then
w(G_{\varphi}/u)|\nabla(G_{\varphi}/u)|_{A}^{2}
is a Hardy-weight for
P
in
\Omega.

We remark that the improved Hardy inequality obtained in
[
15
]
is recovered
with
c_{1}=1/L
,
c_{2}=0
, and
c_{3}=1
.

In this short section we explain how to obtain the generalized eigenfunctions
u_{\xi}
given in Proposition
6.3
. Let
\rho,q\in L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\alpha,\beta),~\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
with
\rho>0
in
(\alpha,\beta)
, and consider the equation:

-y^{\prime\prime}(t)+q(t)y(t)=\lambda\rho(t)y(t)\qquad t\in(\alpha,\beta).

The Liouville’s substitution

y(t)=\frac{w(s)}{\sqrt[4]{\rho}}\,,\quad s(t)=\int_{\alpha}^{t}\sqrt{\rho(\zeta)}\text{d}\zeta,

yields Liouville’s normal form (see for example
[
8
, Chapter 10.9]
)

-w^{\prime\prime}(s)+\hat{q}(s)w(s)=\lambda w(s)\qquad s\in(0,s(\beta)),

where

\hat{q}=-1+\frac{1}{4\rho}\left[\left(\frac{\rho^{\prime}}{\rho}\right)^{\prime}-\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{\rho^{\prime}}{\rho}\right)^{2}\right].

Note that
\hat{q}=0
if and only if the function
v:=\rho^{-1/4}
satisfies the Ermakov-Pinney equation

-v^{\prime\prime}=\frac{1}{v^{3}}\,.

Moreover,
v
is a positive solution of the equation

-y^{\prime\prime}-\dfrac{y}{v^{4}}=0
having minimal growth near
0
if and only if
v(0)=0
, namely,
v(t)=\sqrt{2t-at^{2}}
.

Now consider the equation

-y^{\prime\prime}-\frac{y}{(2t-at^{2})^{2}}=\xi^{2}\frac{y}{(2t-at^{2})^{2}}\qquad t\in(2/(a+M\text{e}^{\pi/\xi}),2/(M+a)).

where
a,M,\xi
are given in Proposition
6.3
.
The Liouville’s substitution

y(t)=w\sqrt{2t-at^{2}},\quad s=\int_{2/(a+M\text{e}^{\pi/\xi})}^{t}(2\zeta-a\zeta^{2})^{-1}\text{d}\zeta=\frac{1}{2}\log\left(\frac{Mt}{2-at}\right)+\frac{\pi}{2\xi}

implies
-w^{\prime\prime}=\xi^{2}w
, and therefore,

w=C_{1}\cos(\xi s)+C_{2}\sin(\xi s)
. The boundary conditions given by
Proposition
6.3
then imply

u=C_{1}\sqrt{2t-at^{2}}\sin\left(\frac{\xi}{2}\log\left(\frac{Mt}{2-at}\right)+\frac{\pi}{2}\right)=C_{1}\sqrt{2t-at^{2}}\cos\left(\frac{\xi}{2}\log\left(\frac{Mt}{2-at}\right)\right).

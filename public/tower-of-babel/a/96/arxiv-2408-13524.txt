Lemma 13
.

Let
a,b,c>0
. Then

a+b-\frac{2ab}{c}\leq\sqrt{(a-b)^{2}+ac}.

Proof.

If
4b\leq c
, then we have
a+b-\frac{2ab}{c}\leq a+b=\sqrt{(a-b)^{2}+4ab}\leq\sqrt{(a-b)^{2}+ac}
.

If
4b>c
, then we consider the function
h\colon[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R},a\mapsto\sqrt{(a-b)^{2}+ac}-a-b+\frac{2ab}{c}
and get

\displaystyle h^{\prime}(a)

\displaystyle=\frac{2(a-b)+c}{2\sqrt{(a-b)^{2}+ac}}+\frac{2b}{c}-1\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle h^{\prime\prime}(a)

\displaystyle=\frac{c(4b-c)}{4\left((a-b)^{2}+ac\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}}

for
a\geq 0
. Since
h(0)=0
,
h^{\prime}(0)=\frac{c}{2b}+\frac{2b}{c}-2=\left(\sqrt{\frac{c}{2b}}-\sqrt{\frac{2b}{c}}\right)^{2}\geq 0
and
h^{\prime\prime}(a)\geq 0
for all
a\geq 0
, we get
h(a)\geq 0
for all
a\geq 0
, which proves the statement.

Proof.

For this proof we use the notation

\kappa_{i,j}(t)\coloneqq\int_{-1}^{t}\int_{t}^{T}\rho^{i,j}(\tau,\hat{\tau})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\hat{\tau}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau+\int_{t}^{T}\int_{-1}^{t}\rho^{i,j}(\tau,\hat{\tau})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\hat{\tau}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau.

First, let
t\geq 0
. Note that by
Lemma

10
, for every
i\in\left\{0,\ldots,N\right\}
and
j\in\{0,\ldots,\hat{N}\}
we have

\kappa_{i,j}(t)\leq\int_{t}^{T}\mathbf{1}_{[0,\hat{t}_{j})}(\hat{\tau})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\hat{\tau}+\int_{t}^{T}\mathbf{1}_{[0,t_{i})}(\tau)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau=(t_{i}-t)^{+}+(\hat{t}_{j}-t)^{+}.

In particular
\kappa_{i,j}(t)=0
for
t\geq t_{i}\lor\hat{t}_{j}
.
We now prove the statement by induction over
(i,j)
. If
j=0
, then we have

\kappa_{i,0}(t)=0+(t_{i}-t)^{+}\leq t_{i}=\sqrt{(t_{i}-\hat{t}_{0})^{2}}.

If
i=0
, we have

\kappa_{0,j}(t)=(\hat{t}_{j}-t)^{+}+0\leq\hat{t}_{j}=\sqrt{(t_{0}-\hat{t}_{j})^{2}}.

Now let us assume that the statement is true for
(i,j)
,
(i+1,j)
and
(i,j+1)
. We first treat the case that
\hat{h}_{j}\leq h_{i}
. Then by the recursive definition of
\rho^{i+1,j+1}
in
Equation

7
we have

\displaystyle\kappa_{i+1,j+1}(t)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\bigg(1-\frac{\hat{h}_{j}}{h_{i}}\bigg)\kappa_{i+1,j}(t)+\frac{\hat{h}_{j}}{h_{i}}\kappa_{i,j}(t)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{h_{i}}\Bigg(\int_{-1}^{t}\int_{t}^{T}\mathbf{1}_{[t_{i},t_{i+1})}(\tau)\mathbf{1}_{[\hat{t}_{j},\hat{t}_{j+1})}(\hat{\tau})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\hat{\tau}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau+\int_{t}^{T}\int_{-1}^{t}\mathbf{1}_{[t_{i},t_{i+1})}(\tau)\mathbf{1}_{[\hat{t}_{j},\hat{t}_{j+1})}(\hat{\tau})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\hat{\tau}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau\Bigg)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\bigg(1-\frac{\hat{h}_{j}}{h_{i}}\bigg)\kappa_{i+1,j}(t)+\frac{\hat{h}_{j}}{h_{i}}\kappa_{i,j}(t)

\displaystyle{}+\frac{1}{h_{i}}\Bigg(((t-t_{i})^{+}\land h_{i})((\hat{t}_{j+1}-t)^{+}\land\hat{h}_{j})+((t_{i+1}-t)^{+}\land h_{i})((t-\hat{t}_{j})^{+}\land\hat{h}_{j})\Bigg).

(10)

If
0\leq t\leq t_{i}\land\hat{t}_{j}
, then we have
(t-t_{i})^{+}=(t-\hat{t}_{j})^{+}=0
, so the last term in
Equation

10
vanishes. Using also the induction hypothesis and the concavity of the square root function, we get

\displaystyle\kappa_{i+1,j+1}(t)

\displaystyle=\bigg(1-\frac{\hat{h}_{j}}{h_{i}}\bigg)\kappa_{i+1,j}(t)+\frac{\hat{h}_{j}}{h_{i}}\kappa_{i,j}(t)

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(1-\frac{\hat{h}_{j}}{h_{i}}\bigg)\sqrt{(t_{i+1}-\hat{t}_{j})^{2}+|\pi_{\theta}|(t_{i+1}-t)+|\pi_{\hat{\theta}}|(\hat{t}_{j}-t)}

\displaystyle\phantom{{}={}}+\frac{\hat{h}_{j}}{h_{i}}\sqrt{(t_{i}-\hat{t}_{j})^{2}+|\pi_{\theta}|(t_{i}-t)+|\pi_{\hat{\theta}}|(\hat{t}_{j}-t)}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\bigg(1-\frac{\hat{h}_{j}}{h_{i}}\bigg)\left((t_{i+1}-\hat{t}_{j})^{2}+|\pi_{\theta}|(t_{i+1}-t)+|\pi_{\hat{\theta}}|(\hat{t}_{j}-t)\right)

\displaystyle\phantom{{}={}}+\frac{\hat{h}_{j}}{h_{i}}\left((t_{i}-\hat{t}_{j})^{2}+|\pi_{\theta}|(t_{i}-t)+|\pi_{\hat{\theta}}|(\hat{t}_{j}-t)\right)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\bigg((t_{i+1}-\hat{t}_{j+1})^{2}+|\pi_{\theta}|(t_{i+1}-t)+|\pi_{\hat{\theta}}|(\hat{t}_{j+1}-t)

\displaystyle\phantom{{}={}}+\bigg(1-\frac{\hat{h}_{j}}{h_{i}}\bigg)\big(2(t_{i+1}-\hat{t}_{j+1})\hat{h}_{j}+\hat{h}_{j}^{2}-|\pi_{\hat{\theta}}|\hat{h}_{j}\big)

\displaystyle\phantom{{}={}}+\frac{\hat{h}_{j}}{h_{i}}\big(2(t_{i+1}-\hat{t}_{j+1})(\hat{h}_{j}-h_{i})+\hat{h}_{j}^{2}-2\hat{h}_{j}h_{i}+h_{i}^{2}-|\pi_{\theta}|h_{i}-|\pi_{\hat{\theta}}|\hat{h}_{j}\big)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{(t_{i+1}-\hat{t}_{j+1})^{2}+|\pi_{\theta}|(t_{i+1}-t)+|\pi_{\hat{\theta}}|(\hat{t}_{j+1}-t)-\hat{h}_{j}(|\pi_{\theta}|-h_{i})-|\pi_{\hat{\theta}}|\hat{h}_{j}-\hat{h}_{j}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{(t_{i+1}-\hat{t}_{j+1})^{2}+|\pi_{\theta}|(t_{i+1}-t)+|\pi_{\hat{\theta}}|(\hat{t}_{j+1}-t)}.

If
t_{i}<t<\hat{t}_{j}
, then we can use
Section

3.2
,
Equation

10
, the induction hypothesis and the preceding calculation to get

\displaystyle\kappa_{i+1,j+1}(t)

\displaystyle=\bigg(1-\frac{\hat{h}_{j}}{h_{i}}\bigg)\kappa_{i+1,j}(t)+\frac{\hat{h}_{j}}{h_{i}}\kappa_{i,j}(t)+\frac{1}{h_{i}}\left((t-t_{i})\land h_{i}\right)\hat{h}_{j}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(1-\frac{\hat{h}_{j}}{h_{i}}\bigg)\kappa_{i+1,j}(t)+\frac{\hat{h}_{j}}{h_{i}}(\hat{t}_{j}-t)+\frac{\hat{h}_{j}}{h_{i}}(t-t_{i})

\displaystyle=\bigg(1-\frac{\hat{h}_{j}}{h_{i}}\bigg)\kappa_{i+1,j}(t)+\frac{\hat{h}_{j}}{h_{i}}(\hat{t}_{j}-t_{i})

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(1-\frac{\hat{h}_{j}}{h_{i}}\bigg)\sqrt{(t_{i+1}-\hat{t}_{j})^{2}+|\pi_{\theta}|(t_{i+1}-t)+|\pi_{\hat{\theta}}|(\hat{t}_{j}-t)}

\displaystyle\phantom{{}={}}+\frac{\hat{h}_{j}}{h_{i}}\sqrt{(t_{i}-\hat{t}_{j})^{2}+|\pi_{\theta}|(t_{i}-t)+|\pi_{\hat{\theta}}|(\hat{t}_{j}-t)}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{(t_{i+1}-\hat{t}_{j+1})^{2}+|\pi_{\theta}|(t_{i+1}-t)+|\pi_{\hat{\theta}}|(\hat{t}_{j+1}-t)}.

If
\hat{t}_{j}<t<t_{i}
, then we can use
Section

3.2
and
Equation

10
to get

\displaystyle\kappa_{i+1,j+1}(t)

\displaystyle=\bigg(1-\frac{\hat{h}_{j}}{h_{i}}\bigg)\kappa_{i+1,j}(t)+\frac{\hat{h}_{j}}{h_{i}}\kappa_{i,j}(t)+\frac{1}{h_{i}}h_{i}\left((t-\hat{t}_{j})\land\hat{h}_{j}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(1-\frac{\hat{h}_{j}}{h_{i}}\bigg)(t_{i+1}-t)+\frac{\hat{h}_{j}}{h_{i}}(t_{i}-t)+t-\hat{t}_{j}

\displaystyle=t_{i+1}-\hat{t}_{j+1}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{(t_{i+1}-\hat{t}_{j+1})^{2}}.

If
t_{i}\lor\hat{t}_{j}\leq t<t_{i+1}\land\hat{t}_{j+1}
, then we can use
Section

3.2
,
Equation

10
and
Lemma

13
to get

\displaystyle\kappa_{i+1,j+1}(t)

\displaystyle=\bigg(1-\frac{\hat{h}_{j}}{h_{i}}\bigg)\kappa_{i+1,j}(t)+\frac{\hat{h}_{j}}{h_{i}}\kappa_{i,j}(t)+\frac{1}{h_{i}}\left((t-t_{i})(\hat{t}_{j+1}-t)+(t_{i+1}-t)(t-\hat{t}_{j})\right)

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(1-\frac{\hat{h}_{j}}{h_{i}}\bigg)(t_{i+1}-t)+\frac{1}{h_{i}}\left((t-t_{i+1}+h_{i})(\hat{t}_{j+1}-t)+(t_{i+1}-t)(t-\hat{t}_{j+1}+\hat{h}_{j})\right)

\displaystyle=t_{i+1}-t+\hat{t}_{j+1}-t-\frac{2}{h_{i}}(t_{i+1}-t)(\hat{t}_{j+1}-t)

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{(t_{i+1}-\hat{t}_{j+1})^{2}+h_{i}(t_{i+1}-t)}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{(t_{i+1}-\hat{t}_{j+1})^{2}+|\pi_{\theta}|(t_{i+1}-t)}.

If
t_{i+1}\lor\hat{t}_{j}\leq t
, then we can use
Section

3.2
and
Equation

10
to get

\displaystyle\kappa_{i+1,j+1}(t)

\displaystyle=\bigg(1-\frac{\hat{h}_{j}}{h_{i}}\bigg)\kappa_{i+1,j}(t)+\frac{\hat{h}_{j}}{h_{i}}\kappa_{i,j}(t)+\frac{1}{h_{i}}\left(h_{i}(\hat{t}_{j+1}-t)^{+}+0\right)

\displaystyle=(\hat{t}_{j+1}-t)^{+}=\sqrt{\left((\hat{t}_{j+1}-t)^{+}\right)^{2}}\leq\sqrt{|\pi_{\hat{\theta}}|(\hat{t}_{j+1}-t)^{+}}.

We now have shown

\kappa_{i+1,j+1}(t)\leq\sqrt{(t_{i+1}-\hat{t}_{j+1})^{2}+|\pi_{\theta}|(t_{i+1}-t)^{+}+|\pi_{\hat{\theta}}|(\hat{t}_{j+1}-t)^{+}}

for every
t\geq 0
. The case
\hat{h}_{j}>h_{i}
can be treated analogously. This concludes the induction.

If
t<0
, then note that by
Lemma

9
,
\rho^{i,j}(\tau,\hat{\tau})=0
for
\tau<0
and
\hat{\tau}<0
and therefore

\displaystyle\kappa_{i,j}(t)

\displaystyle\leq\kappa_{i,j}(0)\leq\sqrt{(t_{i}-\hat{t}_{j})^{2}+|\pi_{\theta}|t_{i}+|\pi_{\hat{\theta}}|\hat{t}_{j}}.

This concludes the proof for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.
∎

Lemma 15
.

Let
g\in BV(-1,T;X)
. Then for
i\in\left\{0,\ldots,N\right\}
and
j\in\{0,\ldots,\hat{N}\}
we have

\int_{-1}^{T}\int_{-1}^{T}\rho^{i,j}(\tau,\hat{\tau})\left\|g(\tau)-g(\hat{\tau})\right\|_{X}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\hat{\tau}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau\leq\sqrt{(t_{i}-\hat{t}_{j})^{2}+|\pi_{\theta}|t_{i}+|\pi_{\hat{\theta}}|\hat{t}_{j}}\cdot\essVar(g).

Proof.

Let
g_{r}\colon[-1,T]\to X
be a representative of
g
and let
\pi\colon-1=\tilde{t}_{0}<\tilde{t}_{1}<\ldots<\tilde{t}_{\tilde{N}}=T
be a partition of
[-1,T]
. We define
g_{\pi}\colon[-1,T]\to X
by

g_{\pi}\coloneqq g_{r}(0)+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(g_{r}(\tilde{t}_{k})-g_{r}(\tilde{t}_{k-1})\right)\mathbf{1}_{[\tilde{t}_{k},T]}.

Then

\displaystyle g_{\pi}(\tau)-g_{\pi}(\hat{\tau})=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(g_{r}(\tilde{t}_{k})-g_{r}(\tilde{t}_{k-1})\right)\left(\mathbf{1}_{[\tilde{t}_{k},T]\times[-1,\tilde{t}_{k})}(\tau,\hat{\tau})-\mathbf{1}_{[-1,\tilde{t}_{k})\times[\tilde{t}_{k},T]}(\tau,\hat{\tau})\right)

for all
\tau,\hat{\tau}\in[-1,T]
. Therefore, by using
Lemma

14
and the notation
\kappa_{i,j}
as defined in
Section

3.2
, we get

\displaystyle\phantom{{}={}}\int_{-1}^{T}\int_{-1}^{T}\rho^{i,j}(\tau,\hat{\tau})\left\|g_{\pi}(\tau)-g_{\pi}(\hat{\tau})\right\|_{X}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\hat{\tau}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left\|g_{r}(\tilde{t}_{k})-g_{r}(\tilde{t}_{k-1})\right\|_{X}\kappa_{i,j}(\tilde{t}_{k})

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{(t_{i}-\hat{t}_{j})^{2}+|\pi_{\theta}|t_{i}+|\pi_{\hat{\theta}}|\hat{t}_{j}}\cdot\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left\|g_{r}(\tilde{t}_{k})-g_{r}(\tilde{t}_{k-1})\right\|_{X}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{(t_{i}-\hat{t}_{j})^{2}+|\pi_{\theta}|t_{i}+|\pi_{\hat{\theta}}|\hat{t}_{j}}\cdot\Var({g_{r}}).

(11)

Note also that by
Lemma

10
and
Remark

11
we have

\int_{-1}^{T}\rho^{i,j}(\tau,\hat{\tau})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\hat{\tau}\leq\hat{t}_{j}\lor 1

for all
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
. Therefore

\displaystyle\phantom{{}={}}\int_{-1}^{T}\int_{-1}^{T}\rho^{i,j}(\tau,\hat{\tau})\left\|g(\tau)-g_{\pi}(\tau)\right\|_{X}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\hat{\tau}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle=\int_{-1}^{T}\int_{-1}^{T}\rho^{i,j}(\tau,\hat{\tau})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\hat{\tau}\left\|g_{r}(\tau)-g_{\pi}(\tau)\right\|_{X}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq(\hat{t}_{j}\lor 1)\int_{-1}^{T}\left\|g_{r}(\tau)-g_{\pi}(\tau)\right\|_{X}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle=(\hat{t}_{j}\lor 1)\sum_{k=1}^{n}\int_{\tilde{t}_{k-1}}^{\tilde{t}_{k}}\left\|g_{r}(\tau)-g_{\pi}(\tau)\right\|_{X}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle=(\hat{t}_{j}\lor 1)\sum_{k=1}^{n}\int_{\tilde{t}_{k-1}}^{\tilde{t}_{k}}\left\|g_{r}(\tau)-g_{r}(\tilde{t}_{k})\right\|_{X}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq(\hat{t}_{j}\lor 1)\sum_{k=1}^{n}\int_{\tilde{t}_{k-1}}^{\tilde{t}_{k}}\Var({g_{r}|_{[\tilde{t}_{k-1},\tilde{t}_{k}]}})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq|\pi|(\hat{t}_{j}\lor 1)\sum_{k=1}^{n}\Var({g_{r}|_{[\tilde{t}_{k-1},\tilde{t}_{k}]}})

\displaystyle=|\pi|(\hat{t}_{j}\lor 1)\Var({g_{r}}).

(12)

and analogously

\int_{-1}^{T}\int_{-1}^{T}\rho^{i,j}(\tau,\hat{\tau})\left\|g(\hat{\tau})-g_{\pi}(\hat{\tau})\right\|_{X}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\hat{\tau}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau\leq|\pi|(t_{i}\lor 1)\Var({g_{r}}).

Combining
Equation

11
,
Equation

12
and
Section

3.2
we get

\displaystyle\phantom{{}={}}\int_{-1}^{T}\int_{-1}^{T}\rho^{i,j}(\tau,\hat{\tau})\left\|g(\tau)-g(\hat{\tau})\right\|_{X}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\hat{\tau}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq\int_{-1}^{T}\int_{-1}^{T}\rho^{i,j}(\tau,\hat{\tau})\left(\left\|g(\tau)-g_{\pi}(\tau)\right\|_{X}+\left\|g_{\pi}(\tau)-g_{\pi}(\hat{\tau})\right\|_{X}+\left\|g_{\pi}(\hat{\tau})-g(\hat{\tau})\right\|_{X}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\hat{\tau}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{(t_{i}-\hat{t}_{j})^{2}+|\pi_{\theta}|t_{i}+|\pi_{\hat{\theta}}|\hat{t}_{j}}\cdot\Var({g_{r}})+(t_{i}\lor 1)|\pi|\Var({g_{r}})+(\hat{t}_{j}\lor 1)|\pi|\Var({g_{r}}).

Taking the infimum over all partitions
\pi
of
[-1,T]
and over all representatives
g_{r}
of
g
completes the proof.
∎

3.3
An explicit upper bound

We are now able to state an upper bound for
\|u_{\theta}(t_{i})-u_{\hat{\theta}}(\hat{t}_{j})\|_{X}
, which only depends on the mesh sizes
|\pi_{\theta}|
,
|\pi_{\hat{\theta}}|
, the given data of the discretizations
u_{\theta}^{0}
,
u_{\hat{\theta}}^{0}
,
f_{\theta}
and
f_{\hat{\theta}}
as well as some arbitrary
(u,v)\in A
and
g\in BV(0,T;X)
.

Theorem 16
.

Let
A\subseteq X\times X
be accretive of type
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
. Let
\theta,\hat{\theta}
be discretizations, let
u_{\theta}\in{C([0,T];X)}
be a solution of the implicit Euler scheme
Equation

E
θ

and let
u_{\hat{\theta}}
be a solution of
(E_{\hat{\theta}})
.
If
(|\pi_{\theta}|\lor|\pi_{\hat{\theta}}|)\omega<1
, then for all
(u,v)\in A
,
g\in BV(0,T;X)
and all
i\in\left\{0,\ldots,N\right\}
and
j\in\{0,\ldots,\hat{N}\}
,

\displaystyle\|u_{\theta}(t_{i})-u_{\hat{\theta}}(\hat{t}_{j})\|_{X}\leq{}

\displaystyle\mathopen{\exp}\left({\varphi((|\pi_{\theta}|\lor|\pi_{\hat{\theta}}|)\omega)(t_{i}+\hat{t}_{j})\omega^{+}}\right)\Bigg(\|u_{\theta}^{0}-u\|_{X}+\|u_{\hat{\theta}}^{0}-u\|_{X}

\displaystyle{}+\int_{0}^{t_{i}}\left\|f_{\theta}(\tau)-g(\tau)\right\|_{X}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau+\int_{0}^{\hat{t}_{j}}\left\|f_{\hat{\theta}}(\hat{\tau})-g(\hat{\tau})\right\|_{X}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\hat{\tau}

\displaystyle{}+\sqrt{(t_{i}-\hat{t}_{j})^{2}+|\pi_{\theta}|t_{i}+|\pi_{\hat{\theta}}|\hat{t}_{j}}\cdot\left(\essVar(g)+\left\|g(0+)-v\right\|_{X}\right)\Bigg).

Proof.

We first apply
Lemma

8
with
\tilde{g}\in BV(-1,T;X)
defined by

\tilde{g}(\tau)=\begin{cases}v&\text{if }\tau<0,\\
g(\tau)&\text{if }\tau\geq 0,\end{cases}

which gives us

\displaystyle a_{i,j}\leq{}

\displaystyle\mathopen{\exp}\left({\varphi((|\pi_{\theta}|\lor|\pi_{\hat{\theta}}|)\omega)(t_{i}+\hat{t}_{j})\omega^{+}}\right)\Bigg(\|u_{\theta}^{0}-u\|_{X}+\|u_{\hat{\theta}}^{0}-u\|_{X}

\displaystyle{}+\int_{0}^{t_{i}}\left\|f_{\theta}(\tau)-g(\tau)\right\|_{X}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau+\int_{0}^{\hat{t}_{j}}\left\|f_{\hat{\theta}}(\hat{\tau})-g(\hat{\tau})\right\|_{X}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\hat{\tau}

\displaystyle+\int_{-1}^{T}\int_{-1}^{T}\rho^{i,j}(\tau,\hat{\tau})\left\|\tilde{g}(\tau)-\tilde{g}(\hat{\tau})\right\|_{X}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\hat{\tau}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau\bigg).

By
Lemma

15
, we have

\int_{-1}^{T}\int_{-1}^{T}\rho^{i,j}(\tau,\hat{\tau})\left\|\tilde{g}(\tau)-\tilde{g}(\hat{\tau})\right\|_{X}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\hat{\tau}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau\leq\sqrt{(t_{i}-\hat{t}_{j})^{2}+|\pi_{\theta}|t_{i}+|\pi_{\hat{\theta}}|\hat{t}_{j}}\cdot\essVar(\tilde{g}).

Since
\essVar(\tilde{g})=\essVar(g)+\left\|g(0+)-v\right\|_{X}
, this completes the proof.
∎

Remark 17

(Properties of
\varphi
)
.

Recall that
\varphi\colon(-\infty,1)\to\mathbb{R}
is given by

\varphi(x)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{-\log(1-x)}{x}&\text{if }x\neq 0,\\
1&\text{if }x=0.\end{cases}

Having the limit

\lim_{x\to 0}\varphi(x)=1

is quite advantageous when the mesh size tends to 0.
Note that for
-1\leq x<1
we have the series representation

\varphi(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{k}}{k+1}=1+\frac{x}{2}+\frac{x^{2}}{3}+\frac{x^{3}}{4}+\cdots.

By using
\varphi(x)\leq 2
for
0\leq x\leq\frac{1}{2}
, we can see, that
Theorem

16
is a generalization of the result by Kobayashi
[
11
, Lemma 2.1]
.

Theorem 18

(Kobayashi 1975)
.

If
(|\pi_{\theta}|\lor|\pi_{\hat{\theta}}|)\omega\leq\frac{1}{2}
, then for all
(u,v)\in A
and all
i\in\left\{0,\ldots,N\right\}
and
j\in\{0,\ldots,\hat{N}\}
, we have

\displaystyle\|u_{\theta}(t_{i})-u_{\hat{\theta}}(\hat{t}_{j})\|_{X}\leq{}

\displaystyle\mathopen{\exp}\left(2(t_{i}+\hat{t}_{j})\omega^{+}\right)\Big(\|u_{\theta}^{0}-u\|_{X}+\|u_{\hat{\theta}}^{0}-u\|_{X}+\int_{0}^{T}\left\|f_{\theta}(\tau)\right\|_{X}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle{}+\int_{0}^{T}\left\|f_{\hat{\theta}}(\hat{\tau})\right\|_{X}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\hat{\tau}+\sqrt{(t_{i}-\hat{t}_{j})^{2}+|\pi_{\theta}|t_{i}+|\pi_{\hat{\theta}}|\hat{t}_{j}}\cdot\left\|v\right\|_{X}\Big).

Proof.

Apply
Theorem

16
with
g=0
and use
\varphi(x)\leq 2
for
0\leq x\leq\frac{1}{2}
.
∎

Remark 19
.

Note that
Theorem

16
only evaluates
u_{\theta}
and
u_{\hat{\theta}}
at time points in the partitions
\pi_{\theta}
and
\pi_{\hat{\theta}}
. Therefore,
Theorem

16
is also true, if the solutions to Euler schemes were defined to be piecewise constant.
The following version of our main result however uses our definition of piecewise affine solutions of Euler schemes to give an upper bound for
\|u_{\theta}(t)-u_{\hat{\theta}}(\hat{t})\|_{X}
for all
t,\hat{t}\in[0,T]
.

Theorem 20
.

Let
A\subseteq X\times X
be accretive of type
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
. Let
\theta,\hat{\theta}
be discretizations, let
u_{\theta}\in{C([0,T];X)}
be a solution of the implicit Euler scheme
Equation

E
θ

and let
u_{\hat{\theta}}
be a solution of
(E_{\hat{\theta}})
.
If
(|\pi_{\theta}|\lor|\pi_{\hat{\theta}}|)\omega<1
, then for all
(u,v)\in A
,
g\in BV(0,T;X)
and all
t,\hat{t}\in[0,T]
we have

\displaystyle\|u_{\theta}(t)-u_{\hat{\theta}}(\hat{t})\|_{X}

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\mathopen{\exp}\left({\varphi((|\pi_{\theta}|\lor|\pi_{\hat{\theta}}|)\omega)(\lceil t\rceil_{\pi_{\theta}}+\lceil\hat{t}\rceil_{\pi_{\hat{\theta}}})\omega^{+}}\right)\Bigg(\|u_{\theta}^{0}-u\|_{X}+\|u_{\hat{\theta}}^{0}-u\|_{X}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\lceil t\rceil_{\pi_{\theta}}}\left\|f_{\theta}(\tau)-g(\tau)\right\|_{X}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau+\int_{0}^{\lceil\hat{t}\rceil_{\pi_{\hat{\theta}}}}\left\|f_{\hat{\theta}}(\hat{\tau})-g(\hat{\tau})\right\|_{X}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\hat{\tau}

\displaystyle{}+\sqrt{(|t-\hat{t}|+|\pi_{\theta}|+|\pi_{\hat{\theta}}|)^{2}+|\pi_{\theta}|t+|\pi_{\hat{\theta}}|\hat{t}}\cdot\left(\essVar(g)+\left\|g(0+)-v\right\|_{X}\right)\Bigg).

Proof.

Choose
i\in\{0,\ldots,N-1\}
and
j\in\{0,\ldots,\hat{N}-1\}
, such that
t\in[t_{i},t_{i+1}]
and
\hat{t}\in[\hat{t}_{j},\hat{t}_{j+1}]
, so there exist
\lambda,\hat{\lambda}\in[0,1]
such that
t=\lambda t_{i}+(1-\lambda)t_{i+1}
and
\hat{t}=\hat{\lambda}\hat{t}_{j}+(1-\hat{\lambda})\hat{t}_{j+1}
. Since
u_{\theta}
and
u_{\hat{\theta}}
are piecewise affine, we get

\displaystyle\|u_{\theta}(t)-u_{\hat{\theta}}(\hat{t})\|_{X}

\displaystyle=\|\lambda u_{\theta}(t_{i})+(1-\lambda)u_{\theta}(t_{i+1})-\hat{\lambda}u_{\hat{\theta}}(\hat{t}_{j})-(1-\hat{\lambda})u_{\hat{\theta}}(\hat{t}_{j+1})\|_{X}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\left(\hat{\lambda}a_{i,j}+(1-\hat{\lambda})a_{i,j+1}\right)+(1-\lambda)\left(\hat{\lambda}a_{i+1,j}+(1-\hat{\lambda})a_{i+1,j+1}\right).

For
k\in\left\{i,i+1\right\}
and
\ell\in\left\{j,j+1\right\}
, we can use
Theorem

16
to get an upper bound for
a_{k,\ell}
.
Note that
t_{k}\leq\lceil t\rceil_{\pi_{\theta}}
and
\hat{t}_{\ell}\leq\lceil\hat{t}\rceil_{\pi_{\hat{\theta}}}
. By using
|t_{k}-\hat{t}_{\ell}|\leq|t-\hat{t}|+|\pi_{\theta}|+|\pi_{\hat{\theta}}|
and the concavity of the square root function, we get

\displaystyle\lambda\hat{\lambda}\sqrt{(t_{i}-\hat{t}_{j})^{2}+|\pi_{\theta}|t_{i}+|\pi_{\hat{\theta}}|\hat{t}_{j}}+\lambda(1-\hat{\lambda})\sqrt{(t_{i}-\hat{t}_{j+1})^{2}+|\pi_{\theta}|t_{i}+|\pi_{\hat{\theta}}|\hat{t}_{j+1}}

\displaystyle+(1-\lambda)\hat{\lambda}\sqrt{(t_{i+1}-\hat{t}_{j})^{2}+|\pi_{\theta}|t_{i+1}+|\pi_{\hat{\theta}}|\hat{t}_{j}}

\displaystyle+(1-\lambda)(1-\hat{\lambda})\sqrt{(t_{i+1}-\hat{t}_{j+1})^{2}+|\pi_{\theta}|t_{i+1}+|\pi_{\hat{\theta}}|\hat{t}_{j+1}}

\displaystyle{}\leq{}

\displaystyle\Big((|t-\hat{t}|+|\pi_{\theta}|+|\pi_{\hat{\theta}}|)^{2}+\lambda\hat{\lambda}(|\pi_{\theta}|t_{i}+|\pi_{\hat{\theta}}|\hat{t}_{j})+\lambda(1-\hat{\lambda})(|\pi_{\theta}|t_{i}+|\pi_{\hat{\theta}}|\hat{t}_{j+1})

\displaystyle+(1-\lambda)\hat{\lambda}(|\pi_{\theta}|t_{i+1}+|\pi_{\hat{\theta}}|\hat{t}_{j})+(1-\lambda)(1-\hat{\lambda})(|\pi_{\theta}|t_{i+1}+|\pi_{\hat{\theta}}|\hat{t}_{j+1})\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle{}={}

\displaystyle\sqrt{(|t-\hat{t}|+|\pi_{\theta}|+|\pi_{\hat{\theta}}|)^{2}+|\pi_{\theta}|t+|\pi_{\hat{\theta}}|\hat{t}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Aplications

In this section we show how our results can be used to establish existence and uniqueness of Euler solutions for the Cauchy problem
Section

1
as well as showing some properties of the Euler solution.

Theorem 22
.

Let
A\subseteq X\times X
be quasi-accretive,
f\in L^{1}(0,T;X)
and
u^{0}\in\overline{\dom A}
. If there exists a sequence of discretizations
(\theta_{n})
of the form
\theta_{n}=(\pi_{\theta_{n}},f_{\theta_{n}},u^{0}_{\theta_{n}})
satisfying

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}|\pi_{\theta_{n}}|=0,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\|f_{\theta_{n}}-f\right\|_{L^{1}(0,T;X)}=0,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\|u^{0}_{\theta_{n}}-u^{0}\|_{X}=0

and if the implicit Euler scheme
(E_{\theta_{n}})
has a solution for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, then there exists a unique Euler solution of the Cauchy problem
Section

1
.

Proof.

Let
A
be accretive of type
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
. Let the sequence
(\theta_{n})
be as in the assumption. For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
let
u_{n}\in{C([0,T];X)}
be a solution of the implicit Euler scheme
(E_{\theta_{n}})
, which exists by assumption.
Since
\lim_{n\to\infty}|\pi_{\theta_{n}}|=0
, there exists an
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
|\pi_{\theta_{n}}|\omega<1
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq n_{0}}
.
By
Corollary

21
, for every
n,m\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq n_{0}}
, every
(\hat{u},\hat{v})\in A
and every
g\in BV(0,T;X)
, we get

\displaystyle\left\|u_{n}-u_{m}\right\|_{C([0,T];X)}

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\mathopen{\exp}{\left(\varphi((|\pi_{\theta_{n}}|\lor|\pi_{\theta_{m}}|)\omega)2T\omega^{+}\right)}\Big(\|u_{\theta_{n}}^{0}-\hat{u}\|_{X}+\|u_{\theta_{m}}^{0}-\hat{u}\|_{X}

\displaystyle+\left\|f_{\theta_{n}}-g\right\|_{L^{1}(0,T;X)}+\left\|f_{\theta_{m}}-g\right\|_{L^{1}(0,T;X)}

\displaystyle{}+\sqrt{(|\pi_{\theta_{n}}|+|\pi_{\theta_{m}}|)^{2}+|\pi_{\theta_{n}}|T+|\pi_{\theta_{m}}|T}\cdot\left(\essVar(g)+\left\|g(0+)-\hat{v}\right\|_{X}\right)\Big).

As a consequence,

\limsup_{n,m\to\infty}\left\|u_{n}-u_{m}\right\|_{C([0,T];X)}\leq 2e^{2T\omega^{+}}\left(\|u^{0}-\hat{u}\|_{X}+\left\|f-g\right\|_{L^{1}(0,T;X)}\right).

Since
\dom A
is dense in its closure and
BV(0,T;X)
is dense in
L^{1}(0,T;X)
, the right hand side can be made arbitrarily small, and it follows that
(u_{n})
is a Cauchy sequence and hence convergent in
C([0,T];X)
. By definition, the limit
u
is an Euler solution of the Cauchy problem
Section

1
, and we have proved existence.

Now let
u,\hat{u}\in{C([0,T];X)}
be two Euler solutions of
Section

1
. Then there are sequences of discretizations
(\theta_{n})_{n}=((\pi_{\theta_{n}},f_{\theta_{n}},u^{0}_{\theta_{n}}))_{n}
and
(\hat{\theta}_{n})_{n}=((\pi_{\hat{\theta}_{n}},f_{\hat{\theta}_{n}},u^{0}_{\hat{\theta}_{n}}))_{n}
with

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}|\pi_{\theta_{n}}|=\lim_{n\to\infty}|\pi_{\hat{\theta}_{n}}|=0,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\|f_{\theta_{n}}-f\right\|_{L^{1}(0,T;X)}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\|f_{\hat{\theta}_{n}}-f\|_{L^{1}(0,T;X)}=0,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\|u^{0}_{\theta_{n}}-u^{0}\|_{X}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\|u^{0}_{\hat{\theta}_{n}}-u^{0}\|_{X}=0

and there are sequences
(u_{n})_{n}
and
(\hat{u}_{n})_{n}
in
{C([0,T];X)}
, such that
u_{n}
is a solution of
(E_{\theta_{n}})
and
\hat{u}_{n}
is a solution of
(E_{\hat{\theta}_{n}})
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and

\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\|u_{n}-u\right\|_{C([0,T];X)}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\|\hat{u}_{n}-\hat{u}\right\|_{C([0,T];X)}=0.

Since
\lim_{n\to\infty}|\pi_{\theta_{n}}|=\lim_{n\to\infty}|\pi_{\hat{\theta}_{n}}|=0
, there is an
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
(|\pi_{\theta_{n}}|\lor|\pi_{\hat{\theta}_{n}}|)\omega<1
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq n_{0}}
. By
Corollary

21
, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq n_{0}}
, every
(\tilde{u},\tilde{v})\in A
and every
g\in BV(0,T;X)
we get

\displaystyle\left\|u_{n}-\hat{u}_{n}\right\|_{C([0,T];X)}

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\mathopen{\exp}{\left(\varphi((|\pi_{\theta_{n}}|\lor|\pi_{\hat{\theta}_{n}}|)\omega)2T\omega^{+}\right)}\Big(\|u_{\theta_{n}}^{0}-\tilde{u}\|_{X}+\|u_{\hat{\theta}_{n}}^{0}-\tilde{u}\|_{X}

\displaystyle+\left\|f_{\theta_{n}}-g\right\|_{L^{1}(0,T;X)}+\|f_{\hat{\theta}_{n}}-g\|_{L^{1}(0,T;X)}

\displaystyle{}+\sqrt{(|\pi_{\theta_{n}}|+|\pi_{\hat{\theta}_{n}}|)^{2}+|\pi_{\theta_{n}}|T+|\pi_{\hat{\theta}_{n}}|T}\cdot\left(\essVar(g)+\left\|g(0+)-\tilde{v}\right\|_{X}\right)\Big).

Therefore

\displaystyle\left\|u-\hat{u}\right\|_{C([0,T];X)}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\|u_{n}-\hat{u}_{n}\right\|_{C([0,T];X)}\leq\mathopen{2e^{2T\omega^{+}}}\left(\|u^{0}-\tilde{u}\|_{X}+\left\|f-g\right\|_{L^{1}(0,T;X)}\right).

Since

is dense in its closure and
BV(0,T;X)
is dense in

, the right hand side can be made arbitrarily small. Thus,

and we have proved uniqueness.
∎

Definition 25
.

For any
B\subseteq X
we define the
set norm

|B|\coloneqq\inf_{x\in B}\|x\|_{X}.

Let
A\subseteq X\times X
. For
x\in X
we define

\left|x\right|_{A}\coloneqq\liminf_{\hat{x}\to x}|A\hat{x}|=\sup_{r>0}\inf_{\hat{x}\in B(x,r)}|A\hat{x}|,

B(x,r)\coloneqq\left\{\hat{x}\in X\colon\|x-\hat{x}\|_{X}\leq r\right\}
.
The set

\generalizeddom A\coloneqq\left\{x\in X\colon\left|x\right|_{A}<\infty\right\}

is called the
generalized domain of
A
.

Remark 26
.

Let
A\subseteq X\times X
. Since
\left|x\right|_{A}=\liminf_{\hat{x}\to x}|A\hat{x}|\leq|Ax|
for all
x\in X
and
\left|x\right|_{A}=\infty
if
x\not\in\overline{\dom A}
, we have

\dom A\subseteq\generalizeddom A\subseteq\overline{\dom A}.

Lemma 27
.

Let
A\subseteq X\times X
and
u,x\in X
. Then
|u|_{A+x}\leq\left|u\right|_{A}+\|x\|_{X}.

Proof.

As a direct consequence of
Definition

25
, we get

\displaystyle|u|_{A+x}

\displaystyle=\liminf_{\hat{x}\to x}|A\hat{x}+x|

\displaystyle=\liminf_{\hat{x}\to x}\inf_{v\in A\hat{x}}\|v+x\|_{X}

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{\hat{x}\to x}\inf_{v\in A\hat{x}}\left(\|v\|_{X}+\|x\|_{X}\right)

\displaystyle=\liminf_{\hat{x}\to x}|A\hat{x}|+\|x\|_{X}

\displaystyle=\left|u\right|_{A}+\|x\|_{X}.

This completes the proof.

,
u
is a strong solution of
Section

1
.

Now let
f\in L^{1}(0,T;X)
,
u^{0}\in\overline{\dom A}
and
u
be the Euler solution of
Section

1
. Since
BV(0,T;X)
is dense in
L^{1}(0,T;X)
and
\generalizeddom A
is dense in
\overline{\dom A}
, there exist sequences
(f_{n})
in
BV(0,T;X)
and
(u^{0}_{n})
in
\generalizeddom A
, such that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\|f_{n}-f\right\|_{L^{1}(0,T;X)}=0\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\|u^{0}_{n}-u^{0}\|_{X}=0.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
let
u_{n}
be the Euler solution of
Section

1
. By
Corollary

32
(a),
u_{n}
is a strong solution of
Section

1
and by
Theorem

23
,

\left\|u_{n}-u\right\|_{C([0,T];X)}\leq e^{T\omega^{+}}\left(\|u^{0}_{n}-u^{0}\|_{X}+\left\|f_{n}-f\right\|_{L^{1}(0,T;X)}\right).

Therefore,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\|u_{n}-u\right\|_{C([0,T];X)}=0

and
u
is a mild solution of
Section

1
.
∎

Appendix A
Functions of bounded variation

In this appendix, we show some well known results concerning functions of bounded variation. Let
X
be a Banach space with norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{X}
and
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
with
a<b
.

Definition 33
.

A function
f\colon[a,b]\to X
is
of bounded pointwise variation
, if

\Var\left(f\right)\coloneqq\sup\left\{\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\left\|f(t_{k+1})-f(t_{k})\right\|_{X}:n\in\mathbb{N},t_{0},\ldots,t_{n}\in[a,b],t_{0}<\ldots<t_{n}\right\}<\infty

and
f
is
of bounded (essential) variation
, if

\essVar(f)\coloneqq\inf\left\{\Var(g)\mid g\colon[a,b]\to X,f(t)=g(t)\text{ for a.e. }t\in I\right\}<\infty.

For
f\in L^{1}(a,b;X)
we define
\essVar(f)\coloneqq\essVar(f_{r})
, where
f_{r}\colon[a,b]\to X
is a representative of
f
. We denote the space of all functions in
L^{1}(a,b;X)
with bounded essential variation by
BV(a,b;X)
and endow it with the norm

\left\|f\right\|_{BV(a,b;X)}\coloneqq\left\|f\right\|_{L^{1}(a,b;X)}+\essVar(f).

Lemma 34
.

If
f\colon[a,b]\to X
is of bounded pointwise variation, then
f
has a limit from the right
f(t+)
at every
t\in[a,b)
and a limit from the left
f(t-)
at every
t\in(a,b]
and

f(t+)=f(t-)=f(t)

for all
t\in(a,b)
except on a countable set.

Proof.

Let us first assume, that there exists a
t\in[a,b)
, such that the limit from the right
f(t+)
does not exist. Then there exists a strictly decreasing sequence
(t_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
(t,b)
, such that
(\|f(t_{n+1})-f(t_{n})\|_{X})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
does not converge to 0. This implies

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\|f(t_{k+1})-f(t_{k})\|_{X}=\infty,

but since
f
is of bounded pointwise variation, we get

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|f(t_{k+1})-f(t_{k})\|_{X}\leq\Var(f)

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
This is a contradiction and therefore
f(t+)
exists for all
t\in[a,b)
. Analogously
f(t-)
exists for all
t\in(a,b]
.

To prove, that
Lemma

34
holds for all
t\in(a,b)
except on a countable set, we define

S_{n}\coloneqq\left\{t\in(a,b)\colon\|f(t-)-f(t)\|_{X}+\|f(t)-f(t+)\|_{X}\geq\frac{1}{n}\right\}

for
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
For every finite collection of elements
t_{1},t_{2},\ldots,t_{N}\in S_{n}
with
t_{1}<t_{2}<\ldots<t_{N}
,
N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we can choose
a<\hat{t}_{1}<\hat{t}_{2}\ldots<\hat{t}_{3N}<b
, such that for all
k\in\left\{1,\ldots,N\right\}

\displaystyle\hat{t}_{3k-1}

\displaystyle=t_{k},

\displaystyle\|f(\hat{t}_{3k-2})-f(\hat{t}_{3k-1})\|_{X}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\|f(t_{k}-)-f(t_{k})\|_{X}\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle\|f(\hat{t}_{3k-1})-f(\hat{t}_{3k})\|_{X}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\|f(t_{k})-f(t_{k}+)\|_{X}.

Then

\displaystyle\frac{N}{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{1}{n}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{N}(\|f(t_{k}-)-f(t_{k})\|_{X}+\|f(t_{k})-f(t_{k}+)\|_{X})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{3N}2(\|f(\hat{t}_{3k-2})-f(\hat{t}_{3k-1})\|_{X}+\|f(\hat{t}_{3k-1})-f(\hat{t}_{3k})\|_{X})

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{k=0}^{3N-1}\|f(\hat{t}_{k+1})-f(\hat{t}_{k})\|_{X}

\displaystyle\leq 2\Var(f).

Therefore
N\leq 2n\Var(f)
, so
S_{n}
is finite for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence

\left\{t\in(a,b)\colon f(t+)\neq f(t)\text{ or }f(t)\neq f(t-)\right\}=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}S_{n}

is a countable union of finite sets and therefore countable.
∎

Lemma 35
.

Let
f\in BV(a,b;X)
. Then

\int_{a}^{b-h}\|f(\tau+h)-f(\tau)\|_{X}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau\leq h\essVar(f)

for all
h>0
.

Proof.

Let
f_{r}
be a representative of
f
and let
h>0
.
For
\tau\geq 0
we set

\displaystyle N_{\tau}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\lfloor\frac{b-a-\tau}{h}\right\rfloor\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle t_{\tau,k}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\tau+a+kh\text{ for }k\in\left\{0,\ldots,N_{\tau}\right\}.

Then

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b-h}\|f(\tau+h)-f(\tau)\|_{X}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{N_{0}}\int_{a+kh}^{(a+(k+1)h)\land b}\|f_{r}(\tau+h)-f_{r}(\tau)\|_{X}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{N_{0}-1}\int_{0}^{h}\|f_{r}(t_{\tau,k+1})-f_{r}(t_{\tau,k})\|_{X}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\phantom{{}={}}{}+\int_{0}^{b-a-N_{0}h}\|f_{r}(t_{\tau,N_{\tau}})-f_{r}(t_{\tau,N_{\tau}-1})\|_{X}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{h}\sum_{k=0}^{N_{\tau}-1}\|f_{r}(t_{\tau,k+1})-f_{r}(t_{\tau,k})\|_{X}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{h}\Var(f_{r})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle=\Var(f_{r})h.

Taking the infimum over all representatives
f_{r}
of
f
gives the result.
∎

Note that if
f\in BV(a,b;X)
, then
f
has a representative
f_{r}
that is of bounded pointwise variation. By
Lemma

34
, for every
t\in[a,b)
the limit from the right
f_{r}(t+)
exists. If
\widehat{f}_{r}
is also a representative of
f
that is of bounded pointwise variation, then
f_{r}-\widehat{f}_{r}
is also of bounded pointwise variation and
f_{r}-\widehat{f}_{r}=0
almost everywhere. Therefore
(f_{r}-\widehat{f}_{r})(t+)=0
and hence

f_{r}(t+)=(f_{r}-\widehat{f}_{r})(t+)+\widehat{f}_{r}(t+)=\widehat{f}_{r}(t+).

We have shown, that
f_{r}(t+)
does not depend on the choice of
f_{r}
. Therefore the following definition is well-defined.

Definition 36
.

Let
f\in BV(a,b;X)
and
t\in[a,b)
. Then

f(t+)\coloneqq f_{r}(t+),

where
f_{r}
is a representative of
f
that is of bounded pointwise variation.

We could replace the
L^{1}
-norm in the definition of the
BV
-norm (see
Definition

33
) by
\|f(a+)\|_{X}
and get an equivalent norm.
More precisely, we obtain the following lemma.

Lemma 37
.

Let
f\in BV(a,b;X)
. Then

\frac{1}{b-a+1}\left\|f\right\|_{BV(a,b;X)}\leq\|f(a+)\|_{X}+\essVar(f)\leq\left(2\lor\frac{1}{b-a}\right)\left\|f\right\|_{BV(a,b;X)}.

Proof.

Let
f\in BV(a,b;X)
. Then

\displaystyle\frac{1}{b-a+1}\left\|f\right\|_{BV(a,b;X)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{b-a+1}\left(\int_{a}^{b}\|f(\tau)\|_{X}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau+\essVar(f)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{b-a+1}\left(\int_{a}^{b}\left(\|f(\tau)-f(a+)\|_{X}+\|f(a+)\|_{X}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau+\essVar(f)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{b-a+1}\left((b-a)\left(\essVar(f)+\|f(a+)\|_{X}\right)+\essVar(f)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\|f(a+)\|_{X}+\essVar(f)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{b-a}\int_{a}^{b}\|f(a+)\|_{X}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau+\essVar(f)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{b-a}\int_{a}^{b}\left(\|f(a+)-f(\tau)\|_{X}+\|f(\tau)\|_{X}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau+\essVar(f)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{b-a}\left((b-a)\essVar(f)+\left\|f\right\|_{L^{1}(a,b;X)}\right)+\essVar(f)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{b-a}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{1}(a,b;X)}+2\essVar(f)

\displaystyle\leq\left(2\lor\frac{1}{b-a}\right)\left\|f\right\|_{BV(a,b;X)}.

This completes the proof.
∎

The following theorem is a result from Camille Jordan, who introduced the notion of bounded variation in
[
10
]
in 1881.

Theorem 38

(Jordan decomposition)
.

A function
f\colon[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
is of bounded pointwise variation if and only if there exist two increasing functions
f_{+},f_{-}\colon[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
, such that

f=f_{+}-f_{-}.

Proof.

If
f\colon[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
is of bounded pointwise variation, then for
t\in[a,b]
we can define

\displaystyle f_{+}(t)

\displaystyle\coloneqq f(t)+\Var(f|_{[a,t]})\quad\text{and}\quad f_{-}(t)\coloneqq\Var(f|_{[a,t]}).

For all
t,\hat{t}\in[a,b]
with
t<\hat{t}
we now have

\displaystyle f_{+}(t)

\displaystyle=f(\hat{t})+f(t)-f(\hat{t})+\Var(f|_{[a,t]})

\displaystyle\leq f(\hat{t})+\Var(f|_{[t,\hat{t}]})+\Var(f|_{[a,t]})

\displaystyle=f_{+}(\hat{t}).

Thus,
f_{+}
is increasing. Also
f_{-}
is increasing and
f=f_{+}+f_{-}
.

To prove the implication in the other direction, note that for any
g,h\colon[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
we have

\Var(g+h)\leq\Var(g)+\Var(h).

If we assume that
f_{+},f_{-}\colon[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
are increasing, then

\Var(f_{+}-f_{-})\leq\Var(f_{+})+\Var(-f_{-})=f_{+}(b)-f_{+}(a)+f_{-}(a)-f_{-}(b)<\infty,

so
f_{+}-f_{-}
is of bounded pointwise variation.
∎

Lemma 39
.

If
f\in C^{1}([a,b])
, then

\Var(f)=\essVar(f)=\int_{a}^{b}|f^{\prime}(\tau)|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau.

Proof.

Since
f^{\prime}
is continuous, the set
\Omega\coloneqq\left\{t\in(a,b)\colon f^{\prime}(t)>0\right\}
is open, so there exists a sequence of intervals
(I_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, such that

\displaystyle I_{2n}\text{ is open for all }

\displaystyle n\in\mathbb{N},

\displaystyle\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}I_{2n}

\displaystyle=\Omega,

\displaystyle I_{2n-1}\text{ is closed for all }

\displaystyle n\in\mathbb{N}\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}I_{2n-1}

\displaystyle=[a,b]\setminus\Omega.

Let
(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(b_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be sequences in
[a,b]
such that

I_{2n}=(a_{2n},b_{2n})\quad\text{and}\quad I_{2n-1}=[a_{2n-1},b_{2n-1}]

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Now,
f|_{I_{n}}
is monotone and
\Var(f|_{I_{n}})=\Var((-f)|_{I_{n}})
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Therefore

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}|f^{\prime}(\tau)|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}f^{\prime}(\tau)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau-\int_{[a,b]\setminus\Omega}f^{\prime}(\tau)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\int_{I_{2n}}f^{\prime}(\tau)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau-\int_{I_{2n-1}}f^{\prime}(\tau)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(f(b_{2n})-f(a_{2n})-(f(b_{2n-1})-f(a_{2n-1}))\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\Var(f|_{I_{2n}})+\Var(f|_{I_{2n-1}})\right)

\displaystyle=\Var(f).

Since
f
is continuous,
\Var(f)=\essVar(f)
.
∎

. We show the continuity property of spectral gaps and complete Logarithmic constants in terms of the jump operators of Lindblad generators in finite dimensional setting. 1.
Introduction

In quantum information theory, open quantum systems are described by Lindblad generators, the generators of a semigroup of completely positive unital (or trace preserving) maps.

In this paper we investigate the mathematical connection between the Lindblad generator
L:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
on finite dimensional space:

L(x)=i[H,x]+\sum_{j}\big[V_{j}^{*}xV_{j}-\frac{1}{2}(xV_{j}^{*}V_{j}+V_{j}^{*}V_{j}x)\big],V_{j}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}),H=H^{*}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}),

and its corresponding
jump map
which is given by

\Psi_{L}(x)=\sum_{j}V_{j}^{*}xV_{j}.

This map is certainly completely positive, but not unital in general. The jump map contains a lot of information of the physical system. For example, in quantum optics, the number
photon emission rate

R(\rho)=\Tr(\Psi(1)\rho)

corresponds to the probability of detecting exactly one photon at time
t=0
spontaneously. The operators
V_{j}
are called
jump operators
, so that

R_{j}(\rho)=\Tr(a_j^*a_j\rho)
is the probability of measuring a photon of type
j
.

We will show that the jump operators can be recovered from the Lindbladian itself. Then the problem of choosing the jump operators turns out to be equivalent to find the Kraus operators of a completely positive map. The main, standard tools in this analysis is the gradient form introduced in
[
15
]
:

2\Gamma_{L}(x,y)=L(x^{*}y)-L(x^{*})y-x^{*}L(y)

Note that the gradient form for the classical heat kernel in
\Gamma(f,g)=(\nabla f,\nabla g)
, called carré du champs by P.A. Meyer
[
17
]
. For Lindblad operators, we find

\Gamma(x,y)=\sum_{j}[V_{j},x]^{*}[V_{j},y].

Note that
\delta_{V}(x)=[V,x]
is the noncommutative version of a differential operator. This geometric point of view is well-accepted and facilitates introducing geometric tools to study the properties of quantum Markov semigroup, see
[
3
,
4
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
14
,
21
,
22
]
and references therein for more information.
Our main contribution is to build the analogy between Lindblad generators and jump maps in the following sense, which generalizes the work of Lindblad
[
15
]

\begin{array}[]{c|c}\text{Lindbladian}&\text{cp map}\\
\hline\cr&\\
\Gamma_{L}(\cdot,\cdot)=\sum_{j}\delta_{V_{j}}(\cdot)^{*}\delta_{V_{j}}(\cdot)&\Psi(\cdot)=\sum_{j}V_{j}^{*}(\cdot)V_{j}\\
\Gamma_{L}\leq_{cp}C\Gamma_{L^{\prime}}&\Psi\leq_{cp}C\Psi^{\prime}\\
\delta_{V_{j}}=\sum_{k}\alpha_{jk}\delta_{\widetilde{V}_{k}}&V_{j}=\sum_{k}\alpha_{jk}\widetilde{V}_{k}\\
\end{array}

The last two lines refer to different kind of comparisons. For a completely positive map a unitary in the environment does not change the map, whereas a linear transformation preserves the cp-order, i.e.
V_{j}=\sum_{k}\alpha_{jk}\widetilde{V}_{k}
is equivalent to

(id_{R}\otimes\Psi)(x^{*}x)\leq C(id_{R}\otimes\Psi^{\prime})(x^{*}x)

for all matrices
x\in\mathbb{M}_{d}(\mathbb{M}_{n}),\dim R=d,\dim\mathcal{H}=n
. The same remains true in the category of derivation operators on matrix algebras, provided we assume in addition that
V_{j}
are matrices of trace
0
. Our main tool is based on the bimodule structure of the derivation space. See section 3-5 for details.

As applications, we show several stability properties based on the above diagram. For open systems with photon emissions, the quantity
g^{(2)}(0)
reflects the probability of two photons being emitted almost simultaneously compared to the square probability of exactly one photon being emitter, and is given by the correlation function
[
19
]
:

g^{2}(0)=g^{2}_{L}(0)=\frac{\Tr(\Psi_L\circ\Psi_L(I_n)\rho)}{\Tr(\Psi_L(I_n)\rho)^{2}}.

The notation
0
in the quantity
g^{2}(0)
comes from a limit procedure.
g^{2}(0)>1
implies superradiance, see
[
5
,
16
]
. The interpretation is intuitive:
g^{2}(L)=1
means that photon emission is stationary.
g^{2}(L)<1
and
g^{2}(L)>1
is sometimes referred to non-classical and classical light.

The following informal statement summerizes the main results discussed above:

Theorem 1.1
.

(Informal) The map
L\to\Psi_{L}
is injective and continuous. In particular, the photon emission rate
R
and
g^{(2)}(0)
depends continuously on
L
.

Preliminaries

2.1.
Quantum Markov semigroup and gradient form

First we recall the general definition of quantum Markov semigroup, which is a family of completely positive maps with certain conditions.

Definition 2.1
.

Suppose
\mathcal{M}\subseteq\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is a subalgebra. We say that
T_{t}:\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}
is a (unital) quantum Markov semigroup if

•

\forall s,t\geq 0
,
T_{s}T_{t}=T_{s+t},T_{0}=I
.

•

\forall t\geq 0
,
T_{t}
is normal, unital, completely positive.

•

\forall x\in\mathcal{M}
,
T_{t}x\rightarrow x
in ultra-weak topology as
t
tends to 0.

In this paper, we will assume

\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}),

where
\mathcal{H}
is a Hilbert space of complex dimension
n\geq 2
. In other words,
\mathcal{M}=\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
for some
n\geq 2
. This will exclude some examples but some of our results are still true in more general settings.
Denote

•

\mathcal{I}_{n}=\{(i,j)|1\leq i,j\leq n\}

The Hilbert-Schimidt inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\text{HS}}
on
\mathcal{M}
is defined as

\langle x,y\rangle_{\text{HS}}:=\tau(x^{*}y)

(2)

Then
\mathcal{M}
is a Hilbert space with respect to Hilbert-Schimidt inner product, denoted as
L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
. Since
\mathcal{M}
is finite dimensional, we actually have norm continuity:

\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}||T_{t}-I||=0.

(3)

Under (
3
), we have a bounded normal linear operator
L:\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}
, which is called the generator of quantum Markov semigroup, such that
T_{t}=e^{tL}
. It is easy to see that
L
is *-preserving:
L(x^{*})=L(x)^{*},\forall x\in\mathcal{M}
and
L(I)=0
. The following theorem characterizes the generator of quantum Markov semigroup satisfying (
3
), which is a combination of the main theorems of
[
1
,
10
,
15
]
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Suppose
L:\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}
is a bounded normal linear operator such that
L
is *-preserving and
L(I)=0
. The following are equivalent:

(1)

\{T_{t}=e^{tL}\}_{t\geq 0}
is a quantum Markov semigroup satisfying (
3
).

(2)

L
has the form

Lx=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\big[2V_{i}^{*}xV_{i}-(V_{i}^{*}V_{i}x+xV_{i}^{*}V_{i})\big]+i[H,x]

(4)

where
H,V_{i}\in\mathcal{M}
and
H
is self-adjoint and
m\geq 0
.

(3)

For any orthonormal basis
\{F_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\mathcal{I}_{n}}
of
L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
, where
\mathcal{I}_{n}=\{(i,j)|1\leq i,j\leq n\}
, with
F_{(1,1)}=I_{n}
,
L
can be written as

Lx=i[H,x]+\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{I}_{n}\backslash\{(1,1)\}}c_{\alpha,\beta}\big[2F_{\alpha}^{*}xF_{\beta}-F_{\alpha}^{*}F_{\beta}x-xF_{\alpha}^{*}F_{\beta}\big]

(5)

where the
(n^{2}-1)\times(n^{2}-1)
matrix
(c_{\alpha,\beta})_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{I}_{n}\backslash\{(1,1)\}}
is a positive matrix.

(4)

The gradient form of the generator is completely positive, the definition of which is given below.

Proof.

The equivalence of
(1),(2),(4)
is given by
[
15
]
. The equivalence of
(1),(3)
is given by
[
10
]
.
∎

Now we present another important definition: Gradient form of a Lindblad generator which is a non-commutative analogue of
carré du champ
:

Definition 2.3
.

For any normal bounded linear map
L:\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}
, the gradient form of
L
is given as
\Gamma_{L}:\mathcal{M}\times\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}
:

\Gamma_{L}(x,y)=L(x^{*}y)-x^{*}L(y)-L(x^{*})y,\ \forall x,y\in\mathcal{M}.

(6)

The gradient form
\Gamma_{L}
is called
completely positive
if

\forall m\geq 1,\forall X_{m}\in\mathbb{M}_{m}(\mathcal{M})=\mathbb{M}_{m}\otimes\mathcal{M},\ \Gamma_{id_{\mathbb{M}_{m}}\otimes L}(X_{m},X_{m})\geq 0

(7)

where

\displaystyle id_{\mathbb{M}_{m}}\otimes L:

\displaystyle\mathbb{M}_{m}\otimes\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\mathbb{M}_{m}\otimes\mathcal{M}

\displaystyle x\otimes y\mapsto x\otimes L(y)

and
\Gamma_{id_{\mathbb{M}_{m}}\otimes L}:\mathbb{M}_{m}(\mathcal{M})\times\mathbb{M}_{m}(\mathcal{M})\rightarrow\mathbb{M}_{m}(\mathcal{M})
is given by:
\forall X_{m},Y_{m}\in\mathbb{M}_{m}(\mathcal{M})
,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{id_{\mathbb{M}_{m}}\otimes L}(X_{m},Y_{m})=(id_{\mathbb{M}_{m}}\otimes L)(X_{m}^{*}Y_{m})-X_{m}^{*}(id_{\mathbb{M}_{m}}\otimes L)(Y_{m})-(id_{\mathbb{M}_{m}}\otimes L)(X_{m}^{*})Y_{m}.

Similar to the idea of Choi matrix of a quantum channel, the complete positivity of gradient form is equivalent to the positivity of the matrix associated to the gradient form:

Proposition 2.4
.

For any bounded linear map
L:\mathbb{M}_{n}\to\mathbb{M}_{n}
, the following are equivalent

(1)

The gradient form
\Gamma_{L}
is completely positive.

(2)

For any
m\geq 1
, and
\{x_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq m}\subset\mathbb{M}_{n}
,
(\Gamma_{L}(x_{i},x_{j}))_{1\leq i,j\leq m}
is a positive matrix.

(3)

m_{L}:=\bigg[\Gamma_{L}(e_{rs},e_{tv})\bigg]_{(r,s),(t,v)}=\sum_{1\leq r,s,t,v\leq n}\ket{rs}\bra{tv}\otimes\Gamma_{L}(e_{rs},e_{tv})

(8)

is a positive matrix.

Proof.

First the equivalence of
(1)\iff(2)
follows from direct calculation. For
(2)\implies(3)
, we choose
m=n^{2}
many matrices
\{e_{rs}\}_{1\leq r,s\leq n}
and we can get
m_{L}
is positive directly.
Finally, we need to show
(3)\implies(2)
. For any
m\geq 1
, and
\{x_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq m}\subset\mathbb{M}_{n}
, we denote

x_{i}=\sum_{1\leq r,s\leq n}\rho_{rs}^{i}e_{rs}.

Then by the bilinear property of
\Gamma_{L}
, we have

\Gamma_{L}(x_{i},x_{j})=\sum_{1\leq r,s,t,v\leq n}\overline{\rho_{rs}^{i}}\rho_{tv}^{j}\Gamma(e_{rs},e_{tv}),\rho_{rs}^{i}\in\mathbb{C}.

(9)

To show that
(\Gamma_{L}(x_{i},x_{j}))_{i,j}
is positive, it is equivalent to show that
for any
y_{i}\in\mathbb{M}_{n},1\leq i\leq m
,

\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq m}y_{i}^{*}\Gamma_{L}(x_{i},x_{j})y_{j}

is positive as an
n
by
n
matrix. We plug (
9
) into the above matrix, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq m}y_{i}^{*}\Gamma_{L}(x_{i},x_{j})y_{j}=\sum_{1\leq r,s,t,v\leq n}\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq m}y_{i}^{*}\overline{\rho_{rs}^{i}}\rho_{tv}^{j}\Gamma_{L}(e_{rs},e_{tv})y_{j}.

If we denote
X_{rs}=\sum_{1\leq i\leq m}\rho_{rs}^{i}y_{i}\in\mathbb{M}_{n}
, the above is equal to

\sum_{1\leq r,s,t,v\leq n}X_{rs}^{*}\Gamma_{L}(e_{rs},e_{tv})X_{tv},

which is positive by the definition of
m_{L}
.
∎

Now recall the definition of the order of the gradient form, first introduced in
[
15
]
:

Definition 2.5
.

For two generators
L,L^{\prime}
of the form (
4

\Gamma_{L}\leq_{cp}\Gamma_{L^{\prime}}\iff\forall m\geq 1,\forall X_{m}\in\mathbb{M}_{m}(\mathcal{M}),\Gamma_{id_{\mathbb{M}_{m}}\otimes L}(X_{m},X_{m})\leq\Gamma_{id_{\mathbb{M}_{m}}\otimes L^{\prime}}(X_{m},X_{m})

(10)

By Proposition
2.4
, we have

\Gamma_{\mathcal{L}}\leq_{cp}C\Gamma_{\mathcal{L}^{\prime}}\iff m_{\mathcal{L}}\leq Cm_{\mathcal{L}^{\prime}},C>0.

Now we introduce a notion called
standard form
of a Lindblad generator. Let us see how to express a Lindblad generator with the form of
(2)
if we know
L
is given by the form of
(3)
:

Lx=i[H,x]+\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{I}_{n}\backslash\{(1,1)\}}c_{\alpha,\beta}\big[2F_{\alpha}^{*}xF_{\beta}-F_{\alpha}^{*}F_{\beta}x-xF_{\alpha}^{*}F_{\beta}\big]

We choose a
(n^{2}-1)\times(n^{2}-1)
unitary matrix
U
such that it diagonalizes the positive matrix
(c_{\alpha,\beta})_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{I}_{n}\backslash\{(1,1)\}}
, i.e.,

c_{\alpha,\beta}=\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{I}_{n}\backslash\{(1,1)\}}(U^{*})_{\alpha,\gamma}c_{\gamma}U_{\gamma,\beta}=\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{I}_{n}\backslash\{(1,1)\}}\overline{U_{\gamma,\alpha}}c_{\gamma}U_{\gamma,\beta},\forall\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{I}_{n}\backslash\{(1,1)\}

(11)

where
c_{\gamma}\geq 0
because
(c_{\alpha,\beta})_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{I}_{n}\backslash\{(1,1)\}}
is a positive matrix. Then if we define for
\gamma\in\mathcal{I}_{n}\backslash\{(1,1)\}
,

V_{\gamma}=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{I}_{n}\backslash\{(1,1)\}}U_{\gamma,\alpha}F_{\alpha}

(12)

we have

Lx=i[H,x]+\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{I}_{n}\backslash\{(1,1)\}}c_{\gamma}\big[2V_{\gamma}^{*}xV_{\gamma}-V_{\gamma}^{*}V_{\gamma}x-xV_{\gamma}^{*}V_{\gamma}\big]

(13)

Moreover,
\big\{I_{n},V_{\gamma};\gamma\in\mathcal{I}_{n}\backslash\{(1,1)\}\big\}
is an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
. This leads to the following definition:

Definition 2.6
.

The standard form of a generator
L
of a quantum Markov semigroup is given by

Lx=i[H,x]+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\big[V_{j}^{*}xV_{j}-\frac{1}{2}(V_{j}^{*}V_{j}x+xV_{j}^{*}V_{j})\big]

(14)

where
\big\{I_{n},V_{j};1\leq j\leq m\big\}
is an orthonormal set of
L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
.

Remark 2.7
.

By the definition of Hilbert Schmidt inner product,
\tau(V_{j})=0
for all

where
\{V_{j}\}
is given by (
14
).

2.2.
Quantum Markov semigroups with
\sigma-
detailed balance condition

For any linear operator
T:\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}
, we define its adjoint
T_{*}
by

\langle T(x),y\rangle_{\text{HS}}=\langle x,T_{*}(y)\rangle_{\text{HS}}

(15)

We have a general class of operators with symmetry assumptions, known as the operator with
\sigma-
detailed balance condition. Denote the density matrix as

•

\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{H}}:=\{\rho\in\mathcal{M}:\rho\geq 0,\text{Tr}(\rho)=1\}

•

\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{H}}^{+}:=\{\rho\in\mathcal{M}:\rho>0,\text{Tr}(\rho)=1\}

Given
\sigma\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{H}}^{+}
, we can define a family of inner products: for
s\in[0,1]

\langle x,y\rangle_{s}=\text{Tr}(\sigma^{s}x^{*}\sigma^{1-s}y)

(16)

More generally, for any measurable function
f:(0,+\infty)\rightarrow(0,+\infty)
, we can define

\langle x,y\rangle_{f}=\text{Tr}(\sigma x^{*}f(\sigma)yf(\sigma)^{-1})

(17)

We have the following special classes of inner product:

•

GNS-inner product:
\langle x,y\rangle_{1}=\text{Tr}(\sigma x^{*}y)
.

•

KMS-inner product:
\langle x,y\rangle_{\frac{1}{2}}=\text{Tr}(\sigma^{\frac{1}{2}}x^{*}\sigma^{\frac{1}{2}}y)
.

•

Bogoliubov-Kubo-Mori(BKM)-inner product:
\langle x,y\rangle_{f_{0}}=\int_{0}^{1}\text{Tr}(\sigma^{1-s}x^{*}\sigma^{s}y)ds
, where
f_{0}(t)=\frac{t-1}{\log t}=\int_{0}^{1}t^{s}ds

Definition 2.8
.

(operator with
\sigma-
detailed balance)

We say a linear operator
T:\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}
satisfies
\sigma-
detailed balance condition if it is self-adjoint with respect to GNS-inner product, i.e.,

\langle Tx,y\rangle_{1}=\langle x,Ty\rangle_{1}.

We say a linear operator
T:\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}
is self-adjoint if
T=T_{*}
.

Remark 2.9
.

It is shown in
[
3
]
that if
T
is linear,

detailed balance(GNS-symmetric operators) form the smallest class among

We will use this notation in later sections.

Another simple implication is that for self-adjoint generator, our
\sigma
is given by
\frac{1}{n}I_{n}
, which means the eigenvalues of
\sigma
are identical to
\frac{1}{n}
, thus all
V_{\gamma}
are self-adjoint. This gives the well-known form of self-adjoint generator:

Corollary 2.13
.

Suppose the quantum Markov semigroup
\{T_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is self-adjoint, then the generator
L
has the following standard form

Lx=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\big[V_{j}xV_{j}-\frac{1}{2}(V_{j}^{2}x+xV_{j}^{2})\big],

(21)

where
V_{j}
are self-adjoint matrices for any
j\in\mathcal{J}
. Moreover,
\{I_{n},V_{j};j\in\mathcal{J}\}
are orthonormal sets and
\mathcal{J}
is an index set with finite elements:
|\mathcal{J}|\leq n^{2}-1
.

3.
Comparison principle for general quantum Markov semigroups

In this section, we will prove the following criteria for comparing two gradient forms given by general Lindblad generators:

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose
L,L^{\prime}
are two generators of the form (
4
), i.e.,

\displaystyle Lx=i[H,x]+\sum_{i=1}^{m}V_{i}^{*}xV_{i}-\frac{1}{2}(V_{i}^{*}V_{i}x+xV_{i}^{*}V_{i}),

\displaystyle L^{\prime}x=i[H^{\prime},x]+\sum_{i=1}^{m^{\prime}}V_{i}^{\prime*}xV_{i}^{\prime}-\frac{1}{2}(V_{i}^{\prime*}V_{i}^{\prime}x+xV_{i}^{\prime*}V_{i}^{\prime})

where
\mathcal{X}=\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m},\mathcal{X^{\prime}}=\{V_{i}^{\prime}\}_{i=1}^{m^{\prime}}
may not be orthonormal sets. Then
\Gamma_{L}\leq C\Gamma_{L^{\prime}}
for some
C>0
if and only if
\text{span}\{I_{n},\mathcal{X}\}\subseteq\text{span}\{I_{n},\mathcal{X^{\prime}}\}
. Moreover, if we express our Lindblad generator as the standard form defined in (
14
), then
\Gamma_{L}\leq C\Gamma_{L^{\prime}}
for some
C>0
if and only if
\text{span}\{\mathcal{X}\}\subseteq\text{span}\{\mathcal{X^{\prime}}\}
.

To prove the theorem, we also need some results in operator algebra theory. Given
m\geq 1
, define the
\mathcal{M}-
bimodule

C_{m}(\mathcal{M}):=\bigg\{\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}\\
a_{2}\\
\cdots\\
a_{m}\end{pmatrix}:a_{i}\in\mathcal{M}\bigg\}

with
\mathcal{M}-
valued inner product

\langle\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}\\
a_{2}\\
\cdots\\
a_{m}\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}b_{1}\\
b_{2}\\
\cdots\\
b_{m}\end{pmatrix}\rangle=\sum_{k=1}^{m}a_{k}^{*}b_{k}\in\mathcal{M}.

Now we can define the derivation
\delta_{L}

\displaystyle\delta_{L}:

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}\rightarrow C_{m}(\mathcal{M})

(22)

\displaystyle x\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}[V_{1},x]\\
[V_{2},x]\\
\cdots\\
[V_{m},x]\end{pmatrix}

By direct calculation, we see that

\Gamma_{L}(x,y)=\sum_{k=1}^{m}[V_{k},x]^{*}[V_{k},y]=\langle\delta_{L}(x),\delta_{L}(y)\rangle

Now define an
\mathcal{M}-
right submodule determined by
L
:

\Omega_{\delta_{L}}:=\{\sum_{j}\delta_{L}(x_{j})y_{j}:\ x_{j},y_{j}\in\mathcal{M}\}\subseteq C_{m}(\mathcal{M})

(23)

with inner product defined by

\langle\sum_{j}\delta_{L}(x_{j})y_{j},\sum_{k}\delta_{L}(x^{\prime}_{k})y^{\prime}_{k}\rangle_{\Omega_{\delta_{L}}}:=\sum_{j,k}y_{j}^{*}\Gamma_{L}(x_{j},x^{\prime}_{k})y^{\prime}_{k}

Using the chain rule
\delta(xy)=\delta(x)y+x\delta(y),x,y\in\mathcal{M}
,
\Omega_{\delta_{L}}
is actually an
\mathcal{M}
-bimodule, given by

\Omega_{\delta_{L}}:=\{\sum_{j}z_{j}\delta_{L}(x_{j})y_{j}:\ x_{j},y_{j},z_{j}\in\mathcal{M}\}\subseteq C_{m}(\mathcal{M})

By replacing
m
by
m^{\prime}
, we can similarly define
\Omega_{\delta_{L^{\prime}}}\subseteq C_{m^{\prime}}(\mathcal{M})
.
Define the map

\displaystyle T:\Omega_{\delta_{L^{\prime}}}\rightarrow\Omega_{\delta_{L}}

(24)

\displaystyle\sum_{j}\delta_{L^{\prime}}(x_{j})y_{j}\mapsto\sum_{j}\delta_{L}(x_{j})y_{j}

By using the fact
\delta(xy)=\delta(x)y+x\delta(y)
and the definition, it is easy to see that
T
is actually a bimodule map.

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose
C>0
is a fixed constant.

\displaystyle\Gamma_{L}\leq C\Gamma_{L^{\prime}}\iff\langle T(\xi),T(\xi)\rangle_{\Omega_{\delta_{L}}}\leq C\langle\xi,\xi\rangle_{\Omega_{\delta_{L^{\prime}}}},\ \forall\xi\in\Omega_{\delta_{L^{\prime}}}

(25)

Proof.

For any
\xi\in\Omega_{\delta_{L^{\prime}}}
, there exist
x_{j},y_{j}\in\mathcal{M},1\leq j\leq l
, such that

\xi=\sum_{j=1}^{l}\delta_{L^{\prime}}(x_{j})y_{j}.

Then
T(\xi)=\sum_{j=1}^{l}\delta_{L}(x_{j})y_{j}
. If
\Gamma_{L}\leq C\Gamma_{L^{\prime}}
, using Proposition
2.4
, we know that

\big(\Gamma_{L}(x_{i},x_{j})\big)_{1\leq i,j\leq l}\leq C\big(\Gamma_{L^{\prime}}(x_{i},x_{j})\big)_{1\leq i,j\leq l},

which implies

\sum_{i,j}y_{i}^{*}\Gamma_{L}(x_{i},x_{j})y_{j}\leq C\sum_{i,j}y_{i}^{*}\Gamma_{L^{\prime}}(x_{i},x_{j})y_{j}.

The necessary part follows easily if we recall that

\langle\sum_{j}\delta_{L}(x_{j})y_{j},\sum_{j}\delta_{L}(x_{j})y_{j}\rangle_{\Omega_{\delta_{L}}}=\sum_{i,j}y_{i}^{*}\Gamma_{L}(x_{i},x_{j})y_{j}.

For the sufficiency part, note that if for any finitely many matrices
x_{j},y_{j}\in\mathcal{M}
,

\sum_{i,j}y_{i}^{*}\Gamma_{L}(x_{i},x_{j})y_{j}\leq C\sum_{i,j}y_{i}^{*}\Gamma_{L^{\prime}}(x_{i},x_{j})y_{j}
implies

\big(\Gamma_{L}(x_{i},x_{j})\big)_{1\leq i,j\leq l}\leq C\big(\Gamma_{L^{\prime}}(x_{i},x_{j})\big)_{1\leq i,j\leq l}.

Applying Proposition
2.4
again, we have
\Gamma_{L}\leq C\Gamma_{L^{\prime}}
.
∎

We can naturally define a norm on the
\mathcal{M}-
bimodule
C_{m}(\mathcal{M})
:
\|\xi\|=\|\langle\xi,\xi\rangle\|
. Now suppose
\Gamma_{L}\leq C\Gamma_{L^{\prime}}
, from the above lemma we know the operator norm of
T
satisfies
||T||\leq\sqrt{C}
. We have the following extension lemma:

Lemma 3.3
.

There exists a bimodule map
\widehat{T}:C_{m^{\prime}}(\mathcal{M})\rightarrow C_{m}(\mathcal{M})
, such that

\widehat{T}|_{\Omega_{\delta^{\prime}}}=T.

Proof.

Since
T:\Omega_{\delta_{L^{\prime}}}\rightarrow C_{m}(\mathcal{M})
is a bounded bimodule map, we can use
[
2
, Proposition 8.2.2]
to get
||T||=||T||_{cb}
. By
[
20
]
,
C_{m}(\mathcal{M})
is injective, which means for any operator space
W\subseteq Z
, and any completely bounded map
T:W\rightarrow C_{m}(\mathcal{M})
, there exists a linear map
\widetilde{T}:Z\rightarrow C_{m}(M)
extending
T
, such that
||T||_{cb}=||\widetilde{T}||_{cb}
. Thus we can get an extension
\widetilde{T}:C_{m^{\prime}}(\mathcal{M})\rightarrow C_{m}(\mathcal{M})
, such that
||\widetilde{T}||_{cb}=||T||_{cb}
. Now take
\mu
to be the normalized Haar measure on the unitary group
U(n)\subseteq\mathcal{M}
, and define

\displaystyle\accentset{\approx}{T}

\displaystyle:C_{m^{\prime}}(\mathcal{M})\rightarrow C_{m}(\mathcal{M})

(26)

\displaystyle\xi\mapsto\int_{U(n)}\widetilde{T}(\xi u)u^{*}\mu(du)

We claim that
\accentset{\approx}{T}\big|_{\Omega_{\delta^{\prime}}}=T
and
\accentset{\approx}{T}
is a right module map. Indeed, for the first one, we have

\accentset{\approx}{T}(x)=\int_{U(n)}\widetilde{T}(xu)u^{*}\mu(du)=\int_{U(n)}T(xu)u^{*}\mu(du)=\int_{U(n)}T(x)\mu(du)=T(x),\forall x\in\Omega_{\delta^{\prime}}

To show
\accentset{\approx}{T}
is a right module map, we only need to show for any
v\in U(n)
,
\accentset{\approx}{T}(\xi v)=\accentset{\approx}{T}(\xi)v
because any element in
\mathcal{M}=\mathbb{M}_{n}
can be written as a linear combination of elements in
U(n)
. Indeed,

\accentset{\approx}{T}(\xi v)=\int_{U(n)}\widetilde{T}(\xi vu)u^{*}\mu(du)=\int_{U(n)}\widetilde{T}(\xi vu)(vu)^{*}v\mu(du)=\int_{U(n)}\widetilde{T}(\xi u)u^{*}v\mu(du)=\accentset{\approx}{T}(\xi)v

Note that for the third equality, we used left invariance of Haar measure.

Now we define
\widehat{T}
:

\displaystyle\widehat{T}

\displaystyle:C_{m^{\prime}}(\mathcal{M})\rightarrow C_{m}(\mathcal{M})

(27)

\displaystyle\xi\mapsto\int_{U(n)}u^{*}\accentset{\approx}{T}(u\xi)\mu(du)

We can check
\widehat{T}\big|_{\Omega_{\delta^{\prime}}}=T
and
\widehat{T}
is a bimodule map, using similar argument as above.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

\displaystyle\{T:C_{m^{\prime}}(\mathcal{M})\rightarrow C_{m}(\mathcal{M})\big|T\ bounded\ linear,T(axb)=aT(x)b,\forall x\in C_{m^{\prime}}(\mathcal{M}),a,b\in\mathcal{M}\}

(28)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{M}_{m\times m^{\prime}}\otimes\mathcal{M}^{\prime}

where
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
is the commutant of
\mathcal{M}
. In our case, since
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}=\{cI_{n}\}
, the above is equal to
\mathbb{M}_{m\times m^{\prime}}\otimes I_{n}
.

Proof.

For any bounded right module map
T:C_{m^{\prime}}(\mathcal{M})\rightarrow C_{m}(\mathcal{M})
, it is adjointable because
L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
is finite dimensional in this case(this means we can take its adjoint). Then it is well-known that
[
13
]

T\in\mathbb{M}_{m\times m^{\prime}}\otimes\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{M})\simeq\mathbb{M}_{m\times m^{\prime}}(\mathcal{M}),

where
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{M})
is all the linear maps on
\mathcal{M}
.

Suppose
T=\big[a_{ij}\big]_{1\leq i\leq m,1\leq j\leq m^{\prime}},\ a_{ij}\in\mathcal{M}
, seen as left multiplication. Then the left module property gives us for all
a\in\mathcal{M}
,

\displaystyle T\bigg(a\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}\\
\cdots\\
x_{m^{\prime}}\end{pmatrix}\bigg)=aT\bigg(\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}\\
\cdots\\
x_{m^{\prime}}\end{pmatrix}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}a_{11}&\cdots&a_{1m^{\prime}}\\
\cdots&\cdots&\cdots\\
a_{m1}&\cdots&a_{mm^{\prime}}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}ax_{1}\\
\cdots\\
ax_{m^{\prime}}\end{pmatrix}=(I_{m}\otimes a)\begin{pmatrix}a_{11}&\cdots&a_{1m^{\prime}}\\
\cdots&\cdots&\cdots\\
a_{m1}&\cdots&a_{mm^{\prime}}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}\\
\cdots\\
x_{m^{\prime}}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle\iff\sum_{j=1}^{m^{\prime}}a_{ij}ax_{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{m^{\prime}}aa_{ij}x_{j},\forall 1\leq i\leq m

where
a,x_{j}\in\mathcal{M},\ 1\leq j\leq m^{\prime}
are arbitrary, which implies
aa_{ij}=a_{ij}a
thus
a_{ij}\in\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.1
:

Sufficiency: We define
D\in\mathbb{M}_{m\times n^{2}}(\mathbb{M}_{n})
:

D_{L}=\bigg([V_{i},e_{rs}]\bigg)_{1\leq i\leq m,1\leq r,s\leq n}.

(29)

By direct calculation, we have

\displaystyle D_{L}^{*}D_{L}=\big(\sum_{k=1}^{m}[V_{k},e_{rs}]^{*}[V_{k},e_{tv}]\big)_{rs,tv}=\big(\Gamma_{L}(e_{rs},e_{tv})\big)_{rs,tv}=m_{L}.

Since
span\{V_{j}\}_{1\leq j\leq m}\subseteq span\{\widetilde{V}_{j},I_{n};1\leq j\leq m^{\prime}\}
, there exists
\alpha_{ij},c_{i}\in\mathbb{C},1\leq i\leq m,1\leq j\leq m^{\prime}
such that

V_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{m^{\prime}}\alpha_{ij}\widetilde{V_{j}}+c_{i}I_{n}

for any
1\leq i\leq m
. Then

\displaystyle D_{L}

\displaystyle=\bigg([V_{i},e_{rs}]\bigg)_{1\leq i\leq m,1\leq r,s\leq n}=\bigg([\sum_{j=1}^{m^{\prime}}\alpha_{ij}\widetilde{V}_{j},e_{rs}]\bigg)_{1\leq i\leq m,1\leq r,s\leq n}

(30)

\displaystyle=\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{m^{\prime}}\alpha_{ij}[\widetilde{V}_{j},e_{rs}]\bigg)_{1\leq i\leq m,1\leq r,s\leq n}

\displaystyle=(A\otimes I_{n})D_{L^{\prime}}

where
A=(\alpha_{ij})_{1\leq i\leq m,1\leq j\leq m^{\prime}}\in\mathbb{M}_{m\times m^{\prime}}
and

D_{L^{\prime}}=\bigg([\widetilde{V}_{i},e_{rs}]\bigg)_{1\leq i\leq m^{\prime},1\leq r,s\leq n}\in\mathbb{M}_{m^{\prime}\times n^{2}}(\mathbb{M}_{n})
.

Thus we have

m_{L}=D_{L}^{*}D_{L}=D_{L^{\prime}}^{*}(A^{*}A\otimes I_{n})D_{L^{\prime}}\leq CD_{L^{\prime}}^{*}D_{L^{\prime}}=Cm_{L^{\prime}}.

(31)

where
C=||A^{*}A\otimes I_{n}||
, which is equivalent to
\Gamma_{L}\leq C\Gamma_{L^{\prime}}
.

Necessity: Our assumption is
\Gamma_{L}\leq C\Gamma_{L^{\prime}}
. Recall we can define the bimodule map

\displaystyle T:\Omega_{\delta_{L^{\prime}}}\rightarrow\Omega_{\delta_{L}}

(32)

\displaystyle\sum_{j}\delta_{L^{\prime}}(x_{j})y_{j}\mapsto\sum_{j}\delta_{L}(x_{j})y_{j}

By the above lemmas, we have for any
x\in\mathcal{M}
,

\displaystyle T(\delta_{L^{\prime}}(x))=\widehat{T}|_{\Omega_{\delta^{\prime}}}(\delta_{L^{\prime}}(x))

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha_{11}I_{n}&\alpha_{12}I_{n}&\cdots&\alpha_{1m^{\prime}}I_{n}\\
\alpha_{21}I_{n}&\alpha_{22}I_{n}&\cdots&\alpha_{2m^{\prime}}I_{n}\\
\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots\\
\alpha_{m1}I_{n}&\alpha_{m2}I_{n}&\cdots&\alpha_{mm^{\prime}}I_{n}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}[\widetilde{V_{1}},x]\\
[\widetilde{V_{2}},x]\\
\cdots\\
[\widetilde{V_{m^{\prime}}},x]\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}[\sum_{j=1}^{m^{\prime}}\alpha_{1j}\widetilde{V}_{j},x]\\
[\sum_{j=1}^{m^{\prime}}\alpha_{2j}\widetilde{V}_{j},x]\\
\cdots\\
[\sum_{j=1}^{m^{\prime}}\alpha_{mj}\widetilde{V}_{j},x]\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}[V_{1},x]\\
[V_{2},x]\\
\cdots\\
[V_{m},x]\end{pmatrix},

which means for any
i=1,2,\cdots,m
,

V_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{m^{\prime}}\alpha_{ij}\widetilde{V_{j}}+c_{i}I_{n}

(33)

i.e.,
span\mathcal{X}\subseteq span\{\mathcal{X}^{\prime},I_{n}\}
. In particular, if
\{V_{i}\}
are trace zero orthonormal sets, we have
V_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{m^{\prime}}\alpha_{ij}\widetilde{V_{j}}
thus
span\mathcal{X}\subseteq span\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
.∎

If we express
L,L^{\prime}
as the standard form.
\{V_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq m},\{\widetilde{V}_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq m^{\prime}}
are the traceless orthonormal jump operators. If
\Gamma_{L}\leq C\Gamma_{L^{\prime}}
for some constant
C>0
, then by our theorem,
span\{V_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq m}\subseteq span\{\widetilde{V}_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq m^{\prime}}
, which implies
m\leq m^{\prime}
. If
m^{\prime}>m
, we assume

span\{V_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq m}=span\{\widetilde{V}_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq m}.

Then we can define the truncation
\widehat{L^{\prime}}
of
L^{\prime}
by

\widehat{L^{\prime}}(x)=i[H^{\prime},x]+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\big[\widetilde{V}_{j}^{*}x\widetilde{V}_{j}-\frac{1}{2}(\widetilde{V}_{j}^{*}\widetilde{V}_{j}x+x\widetilde{V}_{j}^{*}\widetilde{V}_{j})\big].

(34)

We can show that the redundant part of
L^{\prime}
does not affect the comparison of Lindblad generators:

Corollary 3.5
.

For any
C>0,C_{1}\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{L}\leq C\Gamma_{\widehat{L^{\prime}}}+C_{1}\Gamma_{L^{\prime}-\widehat{L^{\prime}}}\iff\Gamma_{L}\leq C\Gamma_{\widehat{L^{\prime}}}.

Proof.

Sufficiency is obvious. We only need to prove the necessity part. If
\Gamma_{L}\leq C\Gamma_{\widehat{L^{\prime}}}+C_{1}\Gamma_{L^{\prime}-\widehat{L^{\prime}}}
, then by Theorem
3.1
and the assumption
span\{V_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq m}=span\{\widetilde{V}_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq m}
, we know that the bimodule map
T:\Omega_{\delta_{L^{\prime}}}\to\Omega_{\delta_{L}}
defined by (
24
) is given by
A\otimes I_{n}
as left multiplication, where
A=(\widehat{A}\ 0)\in\mathbb{M}_{m\times m^{\prime}}
and
\widehat{A}\in\mathbb{M}_{m}
. Then following the same procedure (
30
) as in the proof of Theorem
3.1
, we have
D_{L}=(A\otimes I_{n})D_{L^{\prime}}
and
D_{L}=(\widehat{A}\otimes I_{n})D_{\widehat{L^{\prime}}}
.

\displaystyle D_{L}^{*}D_{L}=D_{L^{\prime}}^{*}(A^{*}A\otimes I_{n})D_{L^{\prime}}=D_{\widehat{L^{\prime}}}^{*}(\widehat{A}^{*}\widehat{A}\otimes I_{n})D_{\widehat{L^{\prime}}}

Then we know that
\Gamma_{L}\leq\|A^{*}A\|\Gamma_{\widehat{L^{\prime}}}.
Thus we only need to prove

C\geq\|A^{*}A\|.

In fact, if
C<\|A^{*}A\|=\|\widehat{A}^{*}\widehat{A}\|
, we know that
CI_{m}-\widehat{A}^{*}\widehat{A}
has at least one negative eigenvalue by the definition
\|A^{*}A\|=\lambda_{max}(A^{*}A)
. Then

\displaystyle C\widehat{L^{\prime}}-L+C_{1}(L^{\prime}-\widehat{L^{\prime}})(x)

\displaystyle=i[\widetilde{H},x]+\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq m}(C\delta_{i,j}-\beta_{ij})\big[\widetilde{V}_{i}^{*}x\widetilde{V}_{j}-\frac{1}{2}(\widetilde{V}_{i}^{*}\widetilde{V}_{j}x+x\widetilde{V}_{i}^{*}\widetilde{V}_{j})\big]

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=m+1}^{m^{\prime}}\big[\widetilde{V}_{j}^{*}x\widetilde{V}_{j}-\frac{1}{2}(\widetilde{V}_{j}^{*}\widetilde{V}_{j}x+x\widetilde{V}_{j}^{*}\widetilde{V}_{j})\big],

where
(\beta_{ij})=\widehat{A}^{*}\widehat{A}
. The coefficient matrix with respect to
\{\widetilde{V}_{j}\}_{1\leq j\leq m^{\prime}}
is given by

\begin{pmatrix}CI_{m}-\widehat{A}^{*}\widehat{A}&0\\
0&C_{1}I_{m^{\prime}-m}\end{pmatrix}

which is not a positive matrix. Thus by Theorem
2.2
,
\Gamma_{C\widehat{L^{\prime}}-L+C_{1}(L^{\prime}-\widehat{L^{\prime}})}
is not completely positive, which contradicts to the assumption.
∎

The proof of the above theorem also shows that the comparison of two Lindblad generators
L,L^{\prime}
is characterized by the comparison of their
jump map

\Psi_{L},\Psi_{L^{\prime}}
:

Corollary 3.6
.

Suppose
L,L^{\prime}
are two Lindblad generators, and
\Psi_{L},\Psi_{L^{\prime}}
are their corresponding jump maps. Then for any constant
C>0
,

\Gamma_{L}\leq C\Gamma_{L^{\prime}}\iff\Psi_{L}\leq C\Psi_{L^{\prime}}

(35)

Proof.

Suppose
\Gamma_{L}\leq C\Gamma_{L^{\prime}}
. Then
span\{V_{j}\}_{1\leq j\leq m}\subseteq span\{\widetilde{V}_{j}\}_{1\leq j\leq m^{\prime}}
by Theorem
3.1
, where
\{V_{j}\}_{1\leq j\leq m},\{\widetilde{V}_{j}\}_{1\leq j\leq m^{\prime}}
are the traceless orthonormal jump operators of
L,L^{\prime}
. Without loss of generality, we assume that (recall
m\leq m^{\prime}
)

span\{V_{j}\}_{1\leq j\leq m}=span\{\widetilde{V}_{j}\}_{1\leq j\leq m}.

Then there exists a full-rank matrix
A=(\alpha_{ij})\in\mathbb{M}_{m}
, such that

V_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\alpha_{ij}\widetilde{V}_{j},\forall 1\leq i\leq m.

(36)

Then we have

\displaystyle m_{L}=D_{L}^{*}D_{L}=D_{\widehat{L^{\prime}}}^{*}(A^{*}A\otimes I_{n})D_{\widehat{L^{\prime}}}\leq\|A^{*}A\|D_{\widehat{L^{\prime}}}^{*}D_{\widehat{L^{\prime}}}\leq\|A^{*}A\|m_{L^{\prime}}.

We can apply the same argument as in Corollary
3.5
to show that
C\geq\|A^{*}A\|
.
On the other hand, recall that for two completely positive map,
\Psi_{L}\leq C\Psi_{L^{\prime}}
if any only if their corresponding Choi matrices
\tau_{\Psi_{L}}\leq C\tau_{\Psi_{L^{\prime}}}
, where
\tau_{\Psi_{L}}=\big(\sum_{k=1}^{m}V_{k}^{*}\ket{r}\bra{s}V_{k}\big)_{1\leq r,s\leq n}\in\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{M}_{n})
. Now define

W_{\Psi_{L}}=\big(\bra{r}V_{i}\big)_{1\leq i\leq m,1\leq r\leq n}\in\mathbb{M}_{m\times n}(\mathbb{M}_{1\times n}).

(37)

Then it is easy to check
\tau_{\Psi_{L}}=W_{\Psi_{L}}^{*}W_{\Psi_{L}}
. Moreover, by (
36
), we have
W_{\Psi_{L}}=AW_{\Psi_{\widehat{L^{\prime}}}}
. Thus

\displaystyle\tau_{\Psi_{L}}=W_{\Psi_{L}}^{*}W_{\Psi_{L}}=W_{\Psi_{\widehat{L^{\prime}}}}^{*}(A^{*}A)W_{\Psi_{\widehat{L^{\prime}}}}\leq\|A^{*}A\|\tau_{\Psi_{L^{\prime}}}\leq C\tau_{\Psi_{L^{\prime}}}.

For the other direction, classical result implies that
span\{V_{j}\}_{1\leq j\leq m}\subseteq span\{\widetilde{V}_{j}\}_{1\leq j\leq m^{\prime}}
and the constant
C\geq\|A^{*}A\|.

∎

Remark 3.7
.

A simple implication is that the gradient form
\Gamma_{L}
uniquely determines the jump map
\Psi_{L}
. In fact, if
\Gamma_{L}=\Gamma_{L^{\prime}}
, then from the above corollary
\Psi_{L}=\Psi_{L^{\prime}}

4.
The structure of
I-E_{\mathcal{N}}

In this section, we characterize the simple operator
-(I-E_{\mathcal{N}})
as a Lindblad generator, where
E_{\mathcal{N}}
is the conditional expectation onto
\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{N}\subseteq\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is a sub-algebra. In fact, since
E_{\mathcal{N}}
is a unital completely positive map, its Kraus representation is given by

E_{\mathcal{N}}(x)=\sum_{j}V_{j}^{*}xV_{j},\sum_{j}V_{j}^{*}V_{j}=I_{n}.

Therefore,
-(I-E_{\mathcal{N}})
is a Lindblad generator. It is well-known that if
\mathcal{N}=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{l}M_{n_{k}}\otimes 1_{r_{k}}\subseteq\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
, where the Hilbert space has the decomposition
\mathcal{H}=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{l}\mathcal{H}_{k}\otimes\mathcal{K}_{k}
, the conditional expectation
E_{\mathcal{N}}
is given by

E_{\mathcal{N}}(x)=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{l}\Tr_{\mathcal{K}_{k}}(P_{k}xP_{k}(1_{n_{k}}\otimes\tau_{k}))\otimes 1_{r_{k}},

(38)

where
P_{k}
is the projection of
\mathcal{H}
onto
\mathcal{H}_{k}\otimes\mathcal{K}_{k}
and
\tau_{k}
is a family of density operators acting on
\mathcal{K}_{k}
determining the conditional expectation. In particular, if
\tau_{k}=\frac{1}{dim\mathcal{K}_{k}}1_{r_{k}}=\frac{1}{r_{k}}1_{r_{k}}
, we denote our conditional expectation as
E_{\mathcal{N},\tau}
, the
trace preserving
conditional expectation.
We will show that as a Lindblad generator, the Kraus operators of
-(I-E_{\mathcal{N},\tau})
are maximal in some sense. The following simple case is a strong indication.

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose
\{I_{n},V_{j};j\in\mathcal{J}\}
is an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
, then

\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\big[V_{i}^{*}xV_{i}-\frac{1}{2}(V_{i}^{*}V_{i}x+xV_{i}^{*}V_{i})\big]=-n^{2}(I-E_{\tau}),

where
E_{\tau}(x)=\tau(x)I_{n}
.

Proof.

Note that
\{\sqrt{n}e_{ij}\}
is an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
, where
e_{ij}=\ket{i}\bra{j}
is the matrix unit. We have by direct calculation,

\displaystyle\widetilde{L}x

\displaystyle:=n\sum_{i,j}\big[e_{ij}^{*}xe_{ij}-\frac{1}{2}(xe_{ij}^{*}e_{ij}+e_{ij}^{*}e_{ij}x)\big]=-n^{2}(I-E_{\tau})(x).

Now we denote
\{E_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\mathcal{I}_{n}}=\{\sqrt{n}e_{ij}\}_{1\leq i\leq n,1\leq j\leq n},\ \{V_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\mathcal{I}_{n}}=\{I_{n},V_{j};j\in\mathcal{J}\}
. Since they are both orthonormal basis of
L_{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
, there exists a unitary
(U_{\alpha,\beta})_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{I}_{n}}
, such that

V_{\alpha}=\sum_{\beta\in\mathcal{I}_{n}}U_{\alpha,\beta}E_{\beta}

Thus

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{I}_{n}}\big[V_{\alpha}^{*}xV_{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}(V_{\alpha}^{*}V_{\alpha}x+xV_{\alpha}^{*}V_{\alpha})\big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mathcal{I}_{n}}\big[\overline{U_{\alpha,\beta}}U_{\alpha,\gamma}E_{\beta}^{*}xE_{\gamma}-\frac{1}{2}(\overline{U_{\alpha,\beta}}U_{\alpha,\gamma}E_{\beta}^{*}E_{\gamma}x+x\overline{U_{\alpha,\beta}}U_{\alpha,\gamma}E_{\beta}^{*}xE_{\gamma}\big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\beta,\gamma\in\mathcal{I}_{n}}\delta_{\beta,\gamma}\big[E_{\beta}^{*}xE_{\gamma}-\frac{1}{2}(xE_{\beta}^{*}E_{\gamma}+E_{\beta}^{*}E_{\gamma}x)\big]

\displaystyle=n\sum_{i,j}\big[e_{ij}^{*}xe_{ij}-\frac{1}{2}(xe_{ij}^{*}e_{ij}+e_{ij}^{*}e_{ij}x)\big]

\displaystyle=-n^{2}(I-E_{\tau})(x).

∎

The above lemma shows that for any Lindblad generator
\mathcal{L}
, we have
\Gamma_{\mathcal{L}}\leq C\Gamma_{-(I-E_{\tau})}
for some
C>0
, by the comparison principle because the Kraus operators of
-(I-E_{\tau})
consist of the whole orthonormal basis. If a Lindblad generator with
\sigma
-detailed balance has fixed point algegra
\mathcal{N}
, we can show a finer result

\Gamma_{\mathcal{L}}\leq C\Gamma_{-(I-E_{\mathcal{N},\tau})},C>0.

To prove the above result, we need some structural analysis of the algebra
\mathcal{N}
:

Proposition 4.2
.

There exist a selfadjoint orthonormal set
\{I_{n},V_{j};j\in\mathcal{J}\}
which spans
\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
(commutator of
\mathcal{N}
), such that

\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\big[V_{j}XV_{j}-\frac{1}{2}(V_{j}^{2}X+XV_{j}^{2})\big]=-(I-E_{\mathcal{N},\tau})(X),X\in\mathbb{M}_{n}.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we can assume the fixed point algebra

\mathcal{N}=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{m}M_{n_{k}}\otimes 1_{r_{k}}\subseteq\mathcal{M}=\mathbb{M}_{n}

Indeed, from the well-known characterization of finite dimensional
C^{*}
-algebra, there exists a unitary
U\in\mathbb{M}_{n}
, such that

\displaystyle U^{*}\mathcal{N}U=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{m}M_{n_{k}}\otimes 1_{r_{k}}

Then from the uniqueness of conditional expectation determined by states, we have

\displaystyle E_{U^{*}\mathcal{N}U,\tau}(X)=U^{*}E_{\mathcal{N},\tau}(UXU^{*})U,X\in\mathbb{M}_{n}.

If we can show that
-(I-E_{U^{*}\mathcal{N}U,\tau})=\sum_{j}\mathcal{L}_{V_{j}}
, where
\{I_{n},V_{j}\}
is a selfadjoint orthonormal set spanning
\mathcal{(}U^{*}NU)^{\prime}
, then for any
X\in\mathbb{M}_{n}
,

\displaystyle-(I-E_{\mathcal{N},\tau})(X)

\displaystyle=-U(I-E_{U^{*}\mathcal{N}U,\tau}(U^{*}XU))U^{*}

\displaystyle=U\big(\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\big[V_{j}(U^{*}XU)V_{j}-\frac{1}{2}(V_{j}^{2}U^{*}XU+U^{*}XUV_{j}^{2})\big]\big)U^{*}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\mathcal{L}_{\widetilde{V}_{j}},

where
\widetilde{V}_{j}=UV_{j}U^{*}
. Since it is easy to see that
\{I_{n},\widetilde{V}_{j};j\in\mathcal{J}\}
form a selfadjoint orthonormal set which spans
\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
, we only need to show the case when
\mathcal{N}
has the form
\bigoplus_{k=1}^{m}M_{n_{k}}\otimes 1_{r_{k}}\subseteq\mathcal{M}=M_{n}
.

Step I:
First suppose
\mathcal{N}=M_{n_{1}}\otimes 1_{r_{1}}
. Then we have
\mathcal{N}^{\prime}=1_{n_{1}}\otimes M_{r_{1}}
. Any orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
is given by
\{1_{n_{1}}\otimes V_{i,r_{1}}\}_{1\leq i\leq r_{1}^{2}}
, where
\{V_{i,r_{1}}\}_{1\leq i\leq r_{1}^{2}}\ \text{is\ an\ orthonormal\ basis\ of\ }\mathbb{M}_{r_{1}}
, which means

\Tr(V_{i,r_1}^*V_{j,r_1})=r_{1}\delta_{i,j}

For any
X\in\mathbb{M}_{n}
, it can be written as the block form:

X=\sum_{1\leq u,v\leq n_{1}}e_{uv}\otimes X_{uv}=\begin{pmatrix}X_{11}&X_{12}&\cdots&X_{1n_{1}}\\
X_{21}&X_{22}&\cdots&X_{2n_{1}}\\
\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots\\
X_{n_{1}1}&X_{n_{1}2}&\cdots&X_{n_{1}n_{1}}\end{pmatrix}

where each
X_{uv}\in\mathbb{M}_{r_{1}}
and
e_{uv}
is the matrix unit of
\mathbb{M}_{n_{1}}
. We denote

\mathcal{L}_{V_{i,r_{1}}}(X_{uv})=V_{i,r_{1}}^{*}X_{uv}V_{i,r_{1}}-\frac{1}{2}(V_{i,r_{1}}^{*}V_{i,r_{1}}X_{uv}+X_{uv}V_{i,r_{1}}^{*}V_{i,r_{1}})

(39)

Then by calculation, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\mathcal{L}_{1_{n_{1}}\otimes V_{i,r_{1}}}(X)

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq u,v\leq n_{1}}e_{uv}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{V_{i,r_{1}}}(X_{uv})=\sum_{1\leq u,v\leq n_{1}}e_{uv}\otimes\big(-r_{1}^{2}(X_{uv}-\frac{1}{r_{1}}\Tr(X_{uv})1_{r_{1}})\big)

\displaystyle=-r_{1}^{2}\big(\sum_{1\leq u,v\leq n_{1}}e_{uv}\otimes X_{uv}-\sum_{1\leq u,v\leq n_{1}}\frac{1}{r_{1}}\Tr(X_{uv})e_{uv}\otimes 1_{r_{1}}\big)

\displaystyle=-r_{1}^{2}(X-\frac{1}{r_{1}}\Tr_{\mathcal{K}_{1}}(X)\otimes 1_{r_{1}})=-r_{1}^{2}(I-E_{\mathcal{N},\tau})(X).

For the second equality, we used Lemma
4.2
, and for the last equality, we used the form of conditional expectation when
\mathcal{N}=\mathbb{M}_{n_{1}}\otimes 1_{r_{j}}
,
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{K}_{1}
.

Step II
: Suppose
\mathcal{N}=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{l}\mathbb{M}_{n_{k}}\otimes 1_{r_{k}}\subseteq\mathcal{M}=\mathbb{M}_{n}
,
l\geq 2
. In this case, the decomposition of the underlying Hilbert space is given by

\mathcal{H}=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{l}\mathcal{H}_{k}\otimes\mathcal{K}_{k}.

The commutator is given by
\mathcal{N}^{\prime}=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{k}1_{n_{k}}\otimes M_{r_{k}}
. Any element
A\in\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
has the following block diagonal form

A=diag(1_{n_{1}}\otimes A_{1},\cdots,1_{n_{k}}\otimes A_{k},\cdots,1_{n_{l}}\otimes A_{l}),

where
A_{r_{k}}\in\mathbb{M}_{r_{k}}
. For each
1\leq k\leq l
, choose a selfadjoint orthonormal basis
\{V_{i,r_{k}}\}_{1\leq i\leq r_{k}^{2}}
of
\mathbb{M}_{r_{k}}
, i.e., they are orthonormal and we have

\Tr(V_{i,r_k}^*V_{i,r_k})=r_{k}

(40)

Now we define

\displaystyle\widehat{V_{i,r_{1}}}:=diag(1_{n_{1}}\otimes V_{i,r_{1}},\cdots,0,\cdots,0)

(41)

\displaystyle\widehat{V_{i,r_{k}}}:=diag(0,\cdots,0,\cdots,1_{n_{k}}\otimes\frac{r_{1}}{r_{k}}V_{i,r_{k}},\cdots,0,\cdots,0),\ 2\leq k\leq m

It is clear that
\{\widehat{V_{i,r_{j}}}\}_{1\leq i\leq r_{k}^{2},1\leq k\leq l}
is a selfadjoint orthonormal set whose linear span is
\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
. We claim that

\sum_{1\leq k\leq l}\sum_{1\leq i\leq r_{k}^{2}}\mathcal{L}_{\widehat{V_{i,r_{k}}}}(X)=-r_{1}^{2}(I-E_{\mathcal{N},\tau})(X),X\in\mathbb{M}_{n}.

(42)

Note that for any
X\in\mathbb{M}_{n}
, it can be written as the following block form:

\displaystyle X=\begin{pmatrix}X_{11}&\cdots&X_{1l}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
X_{l1}&\cdots&X_{ll}\end{pmatrix},X_{uv}\in\mathbb{M}_{n_{u}r_{u}\times n_{v}r_{v}},\ \forall u,v=1,\cdots,l.

Then we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\widehat{V_{i,r_{k}}}}(X)=\widehat{V_{i,r_{j}}}X\widehat{V_{i,r_{j}}}-\frac{1}{2}(\widehat{V_{i,r_{j}}}^{2}X+X\widehat{V_{i,r_{j}}}^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{r_{1}^{2}}{r_{k}^{2}}diag(0,\cdots,0,(1_{n_{k}}\otimes V_{i,r_{k}})X_{kk}(1_{n_{k}}\otimes V_{i,r_{k}}),0,\cdots,0)

\displaystyle-\frac{r_{1}^{2}}{2r_{k}^{2}}\begin{pmatrix}0&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
(1_{n_{k}}\otimes V_{i,r_{k}})^{2}X_{k1}&\cdots&(1_{n_{k}}\otimes V_{i,r_{k}})^{2}X_{kl}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&\cdots&0\end{pmatrix}-\frac{r_{1}^{2}}{2r_{k}^{2}}\begin{pmatrix}0&\cdots&X_{1k}(1_{n_{k}}\otimes V_{i,r_{k}})^{2}&\cdots&0\\
\cdots&\cdots&\cdots\\
0&\cdots&X_{kk}(1_{n_{k}}\otimes V_{i,r_{k}})^{2}&\cdots&0\\
\cdots&\cdots&\cdots\\
0&\cdots&X_{lk}(1_{n_{k}}\otimes V_{i,r_{k}})^{2}&\cdots&0\\
\end{pmatrix}.

Then using the calculation in
Step I
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i\leq r_{k}^{2}}(1_{n_{k}}\otimes V_{i,r_{k}})^{2}=r_{k}^{2}(1_{n_{k}}\otimes 1_{r_{k}}),

and

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i\leq r_{k}^{2}}(1_{n_{k}}\otimes V_{i,r_{k}})X_{kk}(1_{n_{k}}\otimes V_{i,r_{k}})=r_{k}^{2}\frac{1}{r_{k}}\Tr_{\mathcal{K}_{k}}(X_{kk})\otimes 1_{r_{k}}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i\leq r_{k}^{2}}\mathcal{L}_{\widehat{V_{i,r_{k}}}}(X)=-r_{1}^{2}\begin{pmatrix}0&\cdots&\frac{1}{2}X_{1k}&\cdots&0\\
\cdots&\cdots&\cdots\\
\frac{1}{2}X_{k1}&\cdots&(X_{kk}-\frac{1}{r_{k}}\Tr_{\mathcal{K}_{k}}(X_{kk})\otimes 1_{r_{k}})&\cdots&\frac{1}{2}X_{kl}\\
\cdots&\cdots&\cdots\\
0&\cdots&\frac{1}{2}X_{lk}&\cdots&0\\
\end{pmatrix}

Then we sum
k
from
1
to
l
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq k\leq l}\sum_{1\leq i\leq r_{k}^{2}}\mathcal{L}_{\widehat{V_{i,r_{k}}}}(X)

\displaystyle=-r_{1}^{2}\big(X-diag(\frac{1}{r_{1}}\Tr_{\mathcal{K}_{1}}(X_{11})\otimes 1_{r_{1}},\cdots,\frac{1}{r_{k}}\Tr_{\mathcal{K}_{k}}(X_{kk})\otimes 1_{r_{k}},\cdots,\frac{1}{r_{l}}\Tr_{\mathcal{K}_{l}}(X_{ll})\otimes 1_{r_{l}}\big)

\displaystyle=-r_{1}^{2}(I-E_{\mathcal{N},\tau})(X).

∎

Corollary 4.3
.

Suppose
\mathcal{L}
is a Lindblad generator with
\sigma-
detailed balance, the fixed point algebra of which is given by
\mathcal{N}
. Then there exists a constant
C>0
, such that

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\mathcal{L}}\leq C\Gamma_{-(I-E_{\mathcal{N},\tau})}.

Proof.

We can write down the standard form of
\mathcal{L}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\big[V_{j}^{*}xV_{j}-\frac{1}{2}(V_{j}^{*}V_{j}x+xV_{j}^{*}V_{j})\big],

where
V_{j}\in\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
. By Proposition
4.2
, there exists an orthonormal set
\{I_{n},\widetilde{V}_{j};j\in\mathcal{J}\}
such that

-(I-E_{\mathcal{N},\tau})=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\big[\widetilde{V}_{j}^{*}x\widetilde{V}_{j}-\frac{1}{2}(\widetilde{V}_{j}^{*}\widetilde{V}_{j}x+x\widetilde{V}_{j}^{*}\widetilde{V}_{j})\big].

Moreover,
\{I_{n},\widetilde{V}_{j};j\in\mathcal{J}\}
spans
\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle span\{V_{j}:1\leq j\leq m\}\subseteq span\{I_{n},\widetilde{V}_{j};j\in\mathcal{J}\}.

Then from our comparison theorem, we know that there exists
C>0
, such that

\Gamma_{\mathcal{L}}\leq C\Gamma_{-(I-E_{\mathcal{N},\tau})}.

∎

The following corollary is a simple proof of
[
7
, Theorem 5.4]
:

Corollary 4.4
.

The set of Lindblad generators with
\sigma-
detailed balance which satisfy
\Gamma\mathcal{E}
is dense with
\|\cdot\|_{2\to 2}
.

Proof.

Similar to the proof above, for any Lindblad generator
\mathcal{L}
with
\sigma-
detailed balance, the standard form is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\big[V_{j}^{*}xV_{j}-\frac{1}{2}(V_{j}^{*}V_{j}x+xV_{j}^{*}V_{j})\big],

We can extend
\{V_{j}:1\leq j\leq m\}
to
\{V_{j}:1\leq j\leq m+l\}
such that

span\{V_{j}:1\leq j\leq m+l\}=\mathcal{N}^{\prime}.

Then define
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}=\mathcal{L}+\varepsilon\sum_{j=m+1}^{m+l}\mathcal{L}_{V_{j}},\varepsilon>0
.
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}
satisfies
\Gamma_{\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}}\geq C\Gamma_{-(I-E_{\mathcal{N},\tau})}
for some
C>0
by comparison theorem. Moreover, it satisfies
\|\mathcal{L}-\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}\|_{2\to 2}=\varepsilon\|\sum_{j=m+1}^{m+l}\mathcal{L}_{V_{j}}\|_{2\to 2}\lesssim\varepsilon
.
∎

5.
Order relation from norm estimates

In this section, we obtain order relation of two Lindblad generators
\mathcal{L},\mathcal{L}^{\prime}:L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau)\to L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
from the norm
\|\mathcal{L}-\mathcal{L}^{\prime}\|_{2\to 2}
. Our main tool is ordered real vector space with an order unit. First we briefly review the basics of general theory of ordered real vector space, which help us connect the order and the norm. For the whole story, we refer the reader to
[
18
]

5.1.
Ordered real vector space

Definition 5.1
.

Suppose
V
is a real vector space.
\emptyset\neq C\subseteq V
is called a cone if

•

a\cdot v\in C
whenever
a\in[0,\infty)
and
v\in C
.

•

v+w\in C
whenever
v,w\in C
.

An ordered vector space is a pair
(V,V^{+})
consisting of a real vector space
V
and a cone
V^{+}\subseteq V
satisfying
V^{+}\cap-V^{+}=\{0\}
. We say that
V^{+}
is full if
V=V^{+}-V^{+}
.

We can naturally define a partial ordering
\leq
on
V
by

v\leq w\iff w-v\in V^{+}.

(43)

In particular,
v\geq 0
if and only if
v\in V^{+}
. For this reason, we call
V^{+}
the
the cone of positive elements
of
V
.

Definition 5.2
.

Suppose
(V,V^{+})
is an ordered vector space. An element
e
is called an order unit for
V
, if for any
v\in V
, there exists a constant
C=C(v)>0
, such that
v\leq Ce
. For any
v\in V
, define

||v||_{or}=\inf\{r\geq 0:-re\leq v\leq re\}

(44)

The following property is crucial, which is proved in
[
18
, Proposition 2.23]
:

Proposition 5.3
.

||\cdot||_{or}
gives a semi-norm on
V
. Moreover, if
e
is Archimedean, i.e., when
v\in V
with
re+v\geq 0
for all
r>0
, then
v\in V^{+}
, the semi-norm is actually a norm.

The above property enables us to connect the general vector space
V
to our setting. Recall that if
L
is a Lindblad generator, we can define its gradient matrix
m_{L}
by (
8
). For two Lindblad generators
L,L^{\prime}
, we have
L\leq L^{\prime}
if and only if
m_{L}\leq m_{L^{\prime}}
. The first inequality is defined by its gradient form and the second inequality is the usual matrix order.

Example 5.4
.

We define an ordered vector space with full positive cone:

V^{+}:=\{m_{L}\in\mathbb{M}_{n^{2}}(\mathbb{M}_{n}):L\ \text{is\ a\ Lindblad\ generator}:L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau)\to L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau)\}.

(45)

Define
e=m_{-(I-E_{\tau})}
.
e
is an order unit by Lemma
4.1
. Moreover,
e
is an Archimedean order unit.

To see why
e
is Archimedean, suppose
v\in V
, then there exists two Lindblad generators
L,L^{\prime}
such that
v=m_{L}-m_{L^{\prime}}
. By Lemma
4.1
, we can find an orthonormal set
\{V_{j}\}_{j\in\mathcal{J}}
such that
\{I_{n},V_{j};j\in\mathcal{J}\}
spans
\mathcal{M}=\mathbb{M}_{n}
and

-(I-E_{\tau})(x)=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\big[V_{j}^{*}xV_{j}-\frac{1}{2}(V_{j}^{*}V_{j}x+xV_{j}^{*}V_{j})\big].

By Theorem
2.2
(3), we can write
L,L^{\prime}
as

\displaystyle Lx=i[H_{1},x]+\sum_{i,j\in\mathcal{J}}c_{ij}^{1}\big[V_{i}^{*}xV_{j}-\frac{1}{2}(V_{i}^{*}V_{j}x+xV_{i}^{*}V_{j})\big],

\displaystyle L^{\prime}x=i[H_{2},x]+\sum_{i,j\in\mathcal{J}}c_{ij}^{2}\big[V_{i}^{*}xV_{j}-\frac{1}{2}(V_{i}^{*}V_{j}x+xV_{i}^{*}V_{j})\big],

where
(c_{ij}^{1}),(c_{ij}^{2})
are positive matrices. To prove
e
is Archimedean, suppose
v+re\geq 0
for all
r>0
, we have
m_{L-L^{\prime}-r(I-E_{\tau})}\geq 0
, which means

L-L^{\prime}-r(I-E_{\tau})=i[H_{1}-H_{2},x]+\sum_{i,j\in\mathcal{J}}(c_{ij}^{1}-c_{i,j}^{2}+r\delta_{ij})\big[V_{i}^{*}xV_{j}-\frac{1}{2}(V_{i}^{*}V_{j}x+xV_{i}^{*}V_{j})\big]

is a Lindblad generator. Applying Theorem
2.2
(3) again, we know that
(c_{ij}^{1})-(c_{ij}^{2})+rI_{|\mathcal{J}|}
is a positive matrix for all
r>0
. Therefore,
(c_{ij}^{1})-(c_{ij}^{2})
is a positive matrix, which implies
L-L^{\prime}
is a Lindblad generator thus
v=m_{L-L^{\prime}}\in V^{+}
.

Next we consider another example. Fix a Lindblad generator
L
with
\sigma-
detailed balance, the fixed point algebra of which is
\mathcal{N}
. By Corollary
4.3
, we have

\Gamma_{L}\leq C\Gamma_{-(I-E_{\mathcal{N}},\tau)},C>0\iff m_{L}\leq Ce

(46)

where
e=m_{-(I-E_{\mathcal{N},\tau})}
.

Example 5.5
.

Define an ordered vector space
(V,V^{+},e)
where
V=V^{+}-V^{+}
and

V^{+}=\{m_{\widetilde{L}}:\widetilde{L}\text{\ is\ a\ Lindblad\ generator\ with}\ \sigma-\text{detailed\ balance},\ m_{\widetilde{L}}\leq c(\widetilde{L})e,\exists c(\widetilde{L})>0\}.

(47)

Using the same argument as the previous example and Proposition
4.2
, the order unit is Archimedean.

5.2.
Order relation from norm estimates

Given a general Lindblad generator
L
, if another Lindblad generator
L^{\prime}
is close to
L
under
\|\cdot\|_{2\to 2}
norm, we aim to show some order relation if
\|L-L^{\prime}\|_{2\to 2}
is small enough.

Proposition 5.6
.

Suppose
L
is a Lindblad generator. Then
\forall\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\delta=\delta(\varepsilon,L)>0
, such that for any Lindblad generator
L^{\prime}
,

||L-L^{\prime}||_{2\to 2}<\delta\implies(1-\varepsilon)\Gamma_{L}\leq\Gamma_{L^{\prime}}\leq\Gamma_{L}+\varepsilon\Gamma_{-(I-E_{\tau})}.

(48)

Proof.

We use the constructed ordered vector space in Example
5.4
. Since the order unit
e=m_{-(I-E_{\tau})}
is Archimedean, by Proposition
5.3
, the order norm
\|\cdot\|_{or}
is actually a norm. Moreover, since
\dim V<\infty
, any two norms are equivalent, we have

\frac{1}{c_{1}}||v||\leq||v||_{or}\leq c_{1}||v||,\ v\in V,\ c_{1}\geq 1.

Suppose
||L-L^{\prime}||<\delta
, where
\delta
is to be determined. Then by definition of

m_{L}=\big(L(e_{rs}^{*}e_{tv})-L(e_{rs}^{*})e_{tv}-e_{rs}^{*}L(e_{tv})\big)_{rs,tv},

see (
8
), we can find
c_{2}>0
, such that

\|m_{L}-m_{L^{\prime}}\|=\|\big((L-L^{\prime})(e_{rs}^{*}e_{tv})-(L-L^{\prime})(e_{rs}^{*})e_{tv}-e_{rs}^{*}(L-L^{\prime})(e_{tv})\big)_{rs,tv}\|<c_{2}\delta

Then by equivalence of the norms, we have

||m_{L}-m_{L^{\prime}}||_{or}<c_{1}c_{2}\delta

Then by definition of
||\cdot||_{or}
(
44
), we have

m_{L}-c_{1}c_{2}\delta e\leq m_{L^{\prime}}\leq m_{L}+c_{1}c_{2}\delta e.

(49)

Suppose

Lx=i[H,x]+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\big[V_{j}^{*}xV_{j}-\frac{1}{2}(V_{j}^{*}V_{j}x+xV_{j}^{*}V_{j})\big]

is the standard form of Lindblad generator. We also write down the general form of
-(I-E_{\tau})
:

\displaystyle-(I-E_{\tau})(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\big[F_{j}^{*}xF_{j}-\frac{1}{2}(F_{j}^{*}F_{j}x+xF_{j}^{*}F_{j})\big]+\sum_{j=m+1}^{n^{2}-1}\big[F_{j}^{*}xF_{j}-\frac{1}{2}(F_{j}^{*}F_{j}x+xF_{j}^{*}F_{j})\big],

where we assume
span\{F_{j}:1\leq j\leq m\}=span\{V_{j}:1\leq j\leq m\}
.

By (
49
), we have by linearity

\displaystyle m_{L^{\prime}}\geq m_{L_{1}}-c_{1}c_{2}\delta m_{L_{2}},

where

\displaystyle L_{1}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\mathcal{L}_{V_{j}}-c_{1}c_{2}\delta\sum_{j=1}^{m}\mathcal{L}_{F_{j}},\ L_{2}=\sum_{j=m+1}^{n^{2}-1}\mathcal{L}_{F_{j}}.

Since the jump operators of
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
are orthogonal, by Corollary
3.5
, we have
m_{L^{\prime}}\geq m_{L_{1}}
.
Using the fact
span\{F_{j}:1\leq j\leq m\}=span\{V_{j}:1\leq j\leq m\}
, there exists a constant
c(L)>0
, such that

c(L)m_{\sum_{j=1}^{m}\mathcal{L}_{V_{j}}}\geq m_{\sum_{j=1}^{m}\mathcal{L}_{F_{j}}}.

Choosing
0<\delta<\frac{\varepsilon}{c(L)c_{1}c_{2}}
, we have

m_{L^{\prime}}\geq m_{L_{1}}\geq(1-c(L)c_{1}c_{2}\delta)m_{L}\geq(1-\varepsilon)m_{L}.

Then applying Proposition
2.4
, we finish the proof of the first inequality. The other inequality is obvious if we choose
0<\delta<\frac{\varepsilon}{c_{1}c_{2}}
.
∎

If we restrict ourselves to the class of Lindblad generators with
\sigma-
detailed balance, using the same argument as above and Example
5.5
, we have the following order relation:

Proposition 5.7
.

Suppose
L
is a Lindblad generator with
\sigma-
detailed balance.
\mathcal{N}
is its fixed point algebra. Then
\forall\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\delta=\delta(\varepsilon,L)>0
, such that for any Lindblad generator
L^{\prime}
with
\sigma-
detailed balance with the same fixed point algebra
\mathcal{N}
, we have

\|L-L^{\prime}\|_{L_{2}\to L_{2}}<\delta\implies(1-\varepsilon)\Gamma_{L}\leq\Gamma_{L^{\prime}}\leq\Gamma_{L}+\varepsilon\Gamma_{-(I-E_{\mathcal{N},\tau})}.

(50)

6.
Application: Stability property of non-commutative functional inequalites

In this section, we apply the comparison principle of quantum Markov semigroups and the results established in Section 4,5 to show stability properties for two functional inequalities,
Poincaré inequality
and
complete modified Logarithmic Sobolev inequality
.

6.1.
Stability of Poincaré inequality

Suppose
L:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}
is a Lindblad generator with
\sigma-
detailed balance. Its fixed point algebra is
\mathcal{N}
. We say that the quantum Markov semigroup
T_{t}=e^{tL}
satisfies
Poincaré inequality
for some
\lambda>0
if

\text{Var}_{\mathcal{N},\sigma}(T_{t}(X))\leq e^{-\lambda t}\text{Var}_{\mathcal{N},\sigma}(X),\forall X\in\mathcal{M}.

(51)

The variance
\text{Var}_{\mathcal{N},\sigma}
is defined via the BKM-inner product.

\text{Var}_{\mathcal{N},\sigma}(X)=\int_{0}^{1}\Tr\big((X-E_{\mc N,\sigma}(X))^*\sigma^s(X-E_{\mc N,\sigma}(X))\sigma^{1-s}\big)ds.

(52)

By taking the derivative of
\text{Var}_{\mathcal{N},\sigma}(T_{t}(X))
, it is easy to see the inequality (
51
) is equivalent to

\lambda\text{Var}_{\mathcal{N},\sigma}(X)\leq\mathcal{E}_{L}(X).

(53)

We call
\mathcal{E}_{L}
the
Dirichlet form
associated to
L
, which is defined as

\mathcal{E}_{L}(X,Y):=\int_{0}^{1}\Tr(X^* \sigma^s L(Y)\sigma^{1-s})ds

(54)

and we denote
\mathcal{E}_{L}(X,X):=\mathcal{E}_{L}(X)
. We call the largest constant
\lambda(L)
such that (
51
) holds the
spectral gap
of
L
, which is given by

\lambda(L):=\inf_{X}\frac{\mathcal{E}_{L}(X)}{\text{Var}_{N,\sigma}(X)}.

(55)

Then our stability result for
Poincaré inequality
is given as follows:

Theorem 6.1
.

Suppose
L:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}
is a Lindblad generator with
\sigma-
detailed balance. Then for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\delta=\delta(\varepsilon,\mathcal{L})>0
such that for any Lindblad generator
L^{\prime}
with
\sigma
-detailed balance with the same fixed point algebra as
L
, we have

\displaystyle\|L-L^{\prime}\|_{L_{2}\to L_{2}}<\delta\implies(1-\varepsilon)\lambda(L)\leq\lambda(L^{\prime}).

Proof.

Under the assumption above, using Proposition
5.7
, we know that
(1-\varepsilon)\Gamma_{L}\leq\Gamma_{L^{\prime}}
. We claim that we have

(1-\varepsilon)\mathcal{E}_{L}(X)\leq\mathcal{E}_{L^{\prime}}(X),\forall X\in\mathcal{M}.

(56)

Recall that
L=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}e^{-\omega_{j}/2}\mathcal{L}_{V_{j}}
. We rewrite the expression of
L,L^{\prime}
as in (
20
): suppose
\mathcal{J}=\{\mathcal{J}_{1},\cdots,\mathcal{J}_{l}\}
, such that for each
1\leq k\leq l
and
j\in\mathcal{J}_{k},\omega_{j}=\omega_{k}

L=\sum_{k=1}^{l}e^{-\omega_{k}/2}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{k}}\mathcal{L}_{V_{j}},

(57)

where
\{V_{j}\}_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{k}}
are orthonormal eigenvectors of
\Delta_{\sigma}
corresponding to the eigenvalue
e^{-\omega_{k}/2}
. Since
\Gamma_{L}\leq C\Gamma_{L^{\prime}}
and
L^{\prime}
satisfies
\sigma
-detailed balance condition. We can write
L^{\prime}
as

\displaystyle L^{\prime}=\sum_{k=1}^{l}e^{-\omega_{k}/2}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{k}}\mathcal{L}_{\widetilde{V}_{j}}+\text{remainder},

where the remainder term is orthogonal to
L
and
span\{V_{j}\}_{j\in\mathcal{J}}=span\{\widetilde{V}_{j}\}_{j\in\mathcal{J}}
. The corresponding matrix is given by
A\in\mathbb{M}_{m}
,
|\mathcal{J}|=m
:

(V_{j})_{j\in\mathcal{J}}=(A\otimes I_{n})(\widetilde{V}_{j})_{j\in\mathcal{J}}.

Moreover, for each
1\leq k\leq l
we have
A_{k}\in\mathbb{M}_{m_{k}},|\mathcal{J}_{k}|=m_{k}
such that

\displaystyle(V_{j})_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{k}}=(A_{k}\otimes I_{n})(\widetilde{V}_{j})_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{k}}

and
A=diag(A_{1},\cdots,A_{k},\cdots,A_{l})
. Define
\Omega_{\delta_{L_{k}}}=\Omega_{\delta_{\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{k}}\mathcal{L}_{V_{j}}}}
and
\Omega_{\delta_{L_{k}^{\prime}}}=\Omega_{\delta_{\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{k}}\mathcal{L}_{\widetilde{V}_{j}}}}
as in (
23
). Then the module map defined by (
24
)

T_{k}:\Omega_{\delta_{L_{k}^{\prime}}}\to\Omega_{\delta_{L_{k}}}
is given by
A_{k}\otimes I_{n}
. By direct calculation, the Dirichlet form is given by
[
3
]

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{L}(X)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}e^{(\frac{1}{2}-s)\omega_{j}}\Tr\big([V_j,X]^*\sigma^2 [V_j,X]\sigma^{1-s}\big)ds

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{l}\int_{0}^{1}e^{(\frac{1}{2}-s)\omega_{k}}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{k}}\Tr\big((\sigma^{\frac{s}{2}}[V_j,X]\sigma^{\frac{1-s}{2}})^*(\sigma^{\frac{s}{2}}[V_j,X]\sigma^{\frac{1-s}{2}})\big)ds

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{l}C_{k}\int_{0}^{1}e^{(\frac{1}{2}-s)\omega_{k}}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{k}}\Tr\big((\sigma^{\frac{s}{2}}[\wt V_j,X]\sigma^{\frac{1-s}{2}})^*(\sigma^{\frac{s}{2}}[\wt V_j,X]\sigma^{\frac{1-s}{2}})\big)ds

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{1-\varepsilon}\sum_{k=1}^{l}\int_{0}^{1}e^{(\frac{1}{2}-s)\omega_{k}}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{k}}\Tr\big((\sigma^{\frac{s}{2}}[\wt V_j,X]\sigma^{\frac{1-s}{2}})^*(\sigma^{\frac{s}{2}}[\wt V_j,X]\sigma^{\frac{1-s}{2}})\big)ds\leq\frac{1}{1-\varepsilon}\mathcal{E}_{L^{\prime}}(X).

For the first inequality above, we used (
25
):

\displaystyle\Tr\langle T_{k}(\xi),T_{k}(\xi)\rangle_{\Omega_{\delta_{L_{k}}}}\leq C_{k}\Tr\langle\xi,\xi\rangle_{\Omega_{\delta_{L_{k}^{\prime}}}},

for
C_{k}=\|A_{k}^{*}A_{k}\|
and
\xi=\sigma^{s/2}\delta_{L_{k}^{\prime}}(X)\sigma^{(1-s)/2}
. For the second inequality, we used the fact that
C_{k}=\|A_{k}^{*}A_{k}\|\leq\|A^{*}A\|\leq\frac{1}{1-\varepsilon}
. Thus it is easy to see that

\displaystyle\lambda(L)=\inf_{X}\frac{\mathcal{E}_{L}(X)}{\text{Var}_{\mathcal{N},\sigma}(X)}\leq\frac{1}{1-\varepsilon}\inf_{X}\frac{\mathcal{E}_{L^{\prime}}(X)}{\text{Var}_{\mathcal{N},\sigma}(X)}=\frac{1}{1-\varepsilon}\lambda(L^{\prime}).

∎

6.2.
Stability of complete modified Logarithmic Sobolev inequality

Suppose
L:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}
is a Lindblad generator with
\sigma-
detailed balance.
T_{t}=e^{tL}
is the quantum Markov semigroup generated by
L
. Then we say that
L
satisfies
\alpha-
modified Logarithmic Sobolev inequality
for some
\alpha>0
, if for any
\rho\in\mathcal{D}^{+}(\mathcal{H})

D(T_{t*}(\rho)\|\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{N}*}(\rho))\leq e^{-\alpha t}D(\rho\|\mathcal{(}E_{\mathcal{N}})_{*}(\rho)),

(58)

where
D(\cdot\|\cdot)
is the relative entropy and
E_{\mathcal{N}}
is the long time limit of
T_{t}
:
\lim_{t\to\infty}T_{t}=E_{\mathcal{N}}

[
6
]
. A stronger version called
complete modified Logarithmic Sobolev inequality
is introduced in
[
7
]
. We say
L
satisfies
\alpha-
complete modified Logarithmic Sobolev inequality
for some
\widetilde{\alpha}>0
if for any finite dimensional reference system
R
and
\rho\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{R})
,

D((T_{t*}\otimes id_{R})(\rho)\|(\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{N}*}\otimes id_{R})(\rho))\leq e^{-\widetilde{\alpha}t}D(\rho\|(\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{N}*}\otimes id_{R})(\rho)).

(59)

By taking the derivative, the equation (
59
) is equivalent to the following inequality

\widetilde{\alpha}D(\rho\|(\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{N}*}\otimes id_{R})(\rho))\leq\text{EP}_{L\otimes id_{R}}(\rho)

(60)

for any finite dimensional reference system
R
and
\rho\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{R})
.
\text{EP}_{L\otimes id_{R}}(\rho)
is defined by

\displaystyle\text{EP}_{L\otimes id_{R}}(\rho):=\frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{t=0}D((T_{t*}\otimes id_{R})(\rho)\|(\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{N}*}\otimes id_{R})(\rho)).

Suppose
Lx=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}e^{-\omega_{j}/2}\big[V_{j}^{*}xV_{j}-\frac{1}{2}(V_{j}^{*}V_{j}x+xV_{j}^{*}V_{j})\big]
, we define
\partial_{j}(X):=[V_{j},X],j\in\mathcal{J}
, and
\Gamma_{\sigma,\frac{1}{2}}(X):=\sigma^{1/2}X\sigma^{1/2}
for any
X\in\mathcal{M}
. It is shown in
[
11
]
that

\text{EP}_{L}(\rho)=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\big\langle\Gamma_{\sigma,\frac{1}{2}}\circ\partial_{j}\circ\Gamma_{\sigma,\frac{1}{2}}^{-1}(\rho),[\rho]_{\omega_{j}}^{-1}(\Gamma_{\sigma,\frac{1}{2}}\circ\partial_{j}\circ\Gamma_{\sigma,\frac{1}{2}}^{-1}(\rho))\big\rangle_{HS},

where

[\rho]_{\omega_{j}}^{-1}(X):=\int_{0}^{\infty}(r+e^{-\omega_{j}/2}\rho)^{-1}X(r+e^{\omega_{j}/2}\rho)^{-1}dr.

(61)

Now we denote
\text{CMLSI}(L)
as the optimal constant
\widetilde{\alpha}>0
such that (
60
) holds, i.e.,

\text{CMLSI}(L)=\inf_{R}\inf_{\rho\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{R})}\frac{\text{EP}_{L\otimes id_{R}}(\rho)}{D(\rho\|(\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{N}*}\otimes id_{R})(\rho))}

(62)

It is shown in
[
8
,
GR21
]
that
\text{CMLSI}(L)>0
for any
L
with
\sigma-
detailed balance. Applying our comparison theorem, we can show the following stability property of complete Logarithmic Sobolev inequality:

Theorem 6.2
.

Suppose
L:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}
is a Lindblad generator with
\sigma-
detailed balance. Its fixed point algebra is
\mathcal{N}
. Then
\forall\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\delta=\delta(\varepsilon,L)>0
, such that for any Lindblad generator
L^{\prime}
with
\sigma-
detailed balance which has fixed point algebra
\mathcal{N}
, we have

\|L-L^{\prime}\|_{L_{2}\to L_{2}}<\delta\implies(1-\varepsilon)\text{CMLSI}(L)\leq\text{CMLSI}(L^{\prime}).

(63)

Proof.

Similar to the proof of Theorem
6.1
, using Proposition
5.7
, we know that
(1-\varepsilon)\Gamma_{L}\leq\Gamma_{L^{\prime}}
. We claim that we have

(1-\varepsilon)\text{EP}_{L}(\rho)\leq\text{EP}_{L^{\prime}}(\rho),\forall\rho\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}).

(64)

Using the same trick (
57
) as before, we can rewrite
L,L^{\prime}
as in the proof of Theorem
6.1
. Denote
\sigma^{1/2}X\sigma^{1/2}=\rho
,
\text{EP}_{L}(\rho)
can be written as

\displaystyle\text{EP}_{L}(\rho)=\sum_{k=1}^{l}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{k}}\Tr((\sigma^{1/2}[V_j,X]\sigma^{1/2})^{*} [\rho]_{\omega_k}^{-1}(\sigma^{1/2}[V_j,X]\sigma^{1/2})).

Now we use the elementary integral

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}(r+\lambda)^{-1}(r+\mu)^{-1}dr=\frac{\log\lambda-\log\mu}{\lambda-\mu},\lambda,\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{+}

and functional calculus, we know that

\displaystyle[\rho]_{\omega_{k}}^{-1}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}(r+e^{-\omega_{k}/2}L_{\rho})^{-1}(r+e^{\omega_{k}/2}R_{\rho})^{-1}dr

\displaystyle=\sum_{u,v=1}^{n}\frac{\log(e^{-\omega_k/2} \lambda_u)-\log(e^{\omega_k/2} \lambda_v)}{e^{-\omega_{k}/2}\lambda_{u}-e^{\omega_{k}/2}\lambda_{v}}L_{E_{u}}R_{E_{v}},

where
L_{\rho}(X):=\rho X,R_{\rho}(X):=X\rho
and
\rho=\sum_{u=1}^{n}\lambda_{u}E_{u}
is the spectral decomposition, with
E_{u}
being projections.
Then similar to the proof of Theorem
6.1
,

\displaystyle EP_{L}(\rho)=\sum_{u,v=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{l}\frac{\log(e^{-\omega_k/2} \lambda_u)-\log(e^{\omega_k/2} \lambda_v)}{e^{-\omega_{k}/2}\lambda_{u}-e^{\omega_{k}/2}}

\displaystyle\cdot\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{k}}\Tr\big((E_u\sigma^{1/2}[V_j,X]\sigma^{1/2}E_v)^{*} (E_u\sigma^{1/2}[V_{j},X]\sigma^{1/2}E_v)\big)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{u,v=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{l}\frac{\log(e^{-\omega_k/2} \lambda_u)-\log(e^{\omega_k/2} \lambda_v)}{e^{-\omega_{k}/2}\lambda_{u}-e^{\omega_{k}/2}}

\displaystyle\cdot C_{k}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{k}}\Tr\big((E_u\sigma^{1/2}[\wt V_j,X]\sigma^{1/2}E_v)^{*} (E_u\sigma^{1/2}[\wt V_{j},X]\sigma^{1/2}E_v)\big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{1-\varepsilon}EP_{L^{\prime}}(\rho).

The above proof can be adapted easily so that we have

(1-\varepsilon)\text{EP}_{L\otimes id_{R}}(\rho)\leq\text{EP}_{L^{\prime}\otimes id_{R}}(\rho),\forall\rho\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{R}).

Then

\displaystyle\text{CMLSI}(L)

\displaystyle=\inf_{R}\inf_{\rho\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{R})}\frac{\text{EP}_{L\otimes id_{R}}(\rho)}{D(\rho\|(\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{N}*}\otimes id_{R})(\rho))}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{1-\varepsilon}\inf_{R}\inf_{\rho\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{R})}\frac{\text{EP}_{L^{\prime}\otimes id_{R}}(\rho)}{D(\rho\|(\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{N}*}\otimes id_{R})(\rho))}=\frac{1}{1-\varepsilon}\text{CMLSI}(L^{\prime}).

∎

Remark 6.3
.

Note that our stability theorem is different from the following, which is proved in
[
11
]
:

Suppose
L
(resp.
L^{\prime}
) is a generator of quantum Markov semigroup with
\sigma-
(resp.
\sigma^{\prime}-
) detailed balance condition, with the following form

\displaystyle Lx=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}c_{i}\big[V_{i}^{*}xV_{i}-\frac{1}{2}(V_{i}^{*}V_{i}x+xV_{i}^{*}V_{i})\big]

(65)

\displaystyle L^{\prime}x=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}c_{i}^{\prime}\big[V_{i}^{*}xV_{i}-\frac{1}{2}(V_{i}^{*}V_{i}x+xV_{i}^{*}V_{i})\big]

where
c_{i},c_{i}^{\prime}>0
for all
i\in\mathcal{I}
, and
\{V_{i}\}_{i\in\mathcal{I}}
are eigenvectors of both
\Delta_{\sigma}
and
\Delta_{\sigma^{\prime}}
, such that

\{V_{i}\}_{i\in\mathcal{I}}=\{V_{i}^{*}\}_{i\in\mathcal{I}}
,
\tau(V_{i})=0
and
\tau(V_{i}^{*}V_{j})=\delta_{ij}
.

Then

\min_{k,l}\frac{\sigma_{k}}{\sigma_{l}}\min_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\frac{c_{i}}{c_{i}^{\prime}}\text{CLSI}(L^{\prime})\leq\text{CLSI(L)}\leq\text{CLSI}(L^{\prime})\max_{k,l}\frac{\sigma_{k}}{\sigma_{l}}\max_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\frac{c_{i}}{c_{i}^{\prime}}

(66)

In fact, the assumption of the above theorem requires the two generators must have the standard form given by the same
V_{j}
. However, by a perturbation, the standard form of two generators may not be given by the same
V_{j}
.

7.
Application: Stability property of
g^{(2)}
in quantum optics

Starting from a physical system given by the Green’s function of an electric or magnetic field, and the corresponding quantum field, the Lindblad generator is obtained by a number of approximation steps, in particular the Born-Markov approximation. This approximation gives us the evolution of density operators, which is also known as the
master equation
:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\rho(t)=L_{*}(\rho(t)),\ \rho(0)=\rho.

where dual map
L_{*}
is the dual map

L_{*}(\rho)=i[H,\rho]+\sum_{j}\big[V_{j}\rho V_{j}^{*}-\frac{1}{2}(V_{j}^{*}V_{j}\rho+\rho V_{j}^{*}V_{j})\big].

(67)

In the setting of quantum optics, the above equation models the evolution of atomic density matrix
\rho(0)=\rho
. An important quantity named
second order correlation function
is define by

g^{(2)}(0)=\frac{\Tr(\Psi_*\Psi_*(\rho))}{\Tr(\Psi_*(\rho))^{2}}=\frac{\sum_{i,j}\langle V_{j}^{*}V_{i}^{*}V_{i}V_{j}\rangle_{\rho}}{(\sum_{j}\langle V_{j}^{*}V_{j}\rangle_{\rho})^{2}}

(68)

The interpretation of
g^{(2)}(0)
is the probability of finding two photoelectrons without a delay, compared to the squared probability of finding one photoelectron. If
g^{(2)}(0)>1
, a phenomenon called
superradience
happens, see
[
16
]
.
We now show a stability property for the quantity
g^{(2)}(0)
. We denote
g^{(2)}_{L}(0)
to indicate the relation to the underlying Lindblad generator. We assume implicitly that the initial state is given by the same density
\rho
.

Proposition 7.1
.

Suppose
L
is a Lindblad generator with the associated jump map
\Psi_{L}
and
\rho
is a density operator. We assume that
g_{L}^{(2)}(0)>0
. Then for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a constant
\delta=\delta(\varepsilon,L,\rho)>0
, such that for any other Lindblad generator
L^{\prime}
,

\|L-L^{\prime}\|_{2\to 2}\leq\delta\implies(1-\varepsilon)g_{L}^{(2)}(0)\leq g_{L^{\prime}}^{(2)}(0)\leq(1+\varepsilon)g_{L}^{(2)}(0)

(69)

Proof.

By Proposition
5.6
, there exists a small constant
\delta^{\prime}>0
, to be determined, such that

\displaystyle(1-\delta^{\prime})\Gamma_{L}\leq\Gamma_{L^{\prime}}\leq\Gamma_{L}+\delta^{\prime}\Gamma_{-(I-E_{\tau})}.

Then apply Corollary
3.6
, we know that

\displaystyle(1-\delta^{\prime})\Psi_{L}\leq\Psi_{L^{\prime}}\leq\Psi_{L}+\delta^{\prime}\Psi_{e}=\Psi_{L}+\delta^{\prime}E_{\tau}

where
\Psi_{e}
is the jump map of
-(I-E_{\tau})
, which is actually
E_{\tau}
.
Then we calculate the numerator and denominator of
g^{(2)}(0)
separately.

\displaystyle\Tr((\Psi_{L'})_*(\Psi_{L'})_*(\rho))=\Tr(\rho\Psi_{L'}(\Psi_{L'}(I_n)))

\displaystyle\leq\Tr(\rho\Psi_{L}(\Psi_{L}(I_n)))+\delta^{\prime}\Tr(\rho\Psi_{L}(E_{\tau}(I_n)))+\delta^{\prime}\Tr(\rho E_{\tau}(\Psi_{L}(I_n)))+\delta^{\prime 2}\Tr(\rho E_{\tau}(E_{\tau}(I_n)))

\displaystyle=\Tr(\rho\Psi_{L}(\Psi_{L}(I_n)))+\delta^{\prime}(1+\frac{1}{n})\Tr(\rho\Psi_{L}(I_n))+\delta^{\prime 2}

For the denominator, we have

\displaystyle\Tr((\Psi_{L'})_*(\rho))^{2}=\Tr(\rho\Psi_{L'}(I_n))^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\big(\Tr(\rho\Psi_{L}(I_n))+\delta^{\prime}\Tr(\rho E_{\tau}(I_n))\big)^{2}

\displaystyle=\Tr(\rho\Psi_{L}(I_n))^{2}+2\delta^{\prime}\Tr(\rho\Psi_{L}(I_n))+\delta^{\prime 2}.

On the other hand, it is obvious that

\displaystyle\Tr((\Psi_{L'})_*(\Psi_{L'})_*(\rho))\geq(1-\delta^{\prime})^{2}\Tr((\Psi_{L})_*(\Psi_{L})_*(\rho)),\ \Tr((\Psi_{L'})_*(\rho))^{2}\geq(1-\delta^{\prime})^{2}\Tr((\Psi_{L})_*(\rho))^{2}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{\Tr((\Psi_{L})_*(\Psi_{L})_*(\rho))}{\Tr(\rho\Psi_{L}(I_n))^{2}+2\delta^{\prime}\Tr(\rho\Psi_{L}(I_n))+\delta^{\prime 2}}\leq g^{(2)}_{L^{\prime}}(0)\leq\frac{\Tr(\rho\Psi_{L}(\Psi_{L}(I_n)))+\delta^{\prime}(1+\frac{1}{n})\Tr(\rho\Psi_{L}(I_n))+\delta^{\prime 2}}{(1-\delta^{\prime})^{2}\Tr((\Psi_{L})_*(\rho))^{2}}.

If we choose
\delta^{\prime}>0
such that

\displaystyle 2\delta^{\prime}\Tr(\rho\Psi_{L}(I_n))+\delta^{\prime 2}\leq c\varepsilon\min\{\Tr(\rho\Psi_{L}(I_n))^{2},\ \Tr(\rho\Psi_{L}(\Psi_{L}(I_n)))\},c>0,

we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1+c\varepsilon}g^{(2)}(0)\leq g^{(2)}_{L^{\prime}}(0)\leq\frac{1+c\varepsilon}{(1-\delta^{\prime})^{2}}g^{(2)}_{L}(0).

Then choosing
c>0
small enough, we arrive at our result.
∎

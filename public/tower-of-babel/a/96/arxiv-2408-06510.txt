A metric boundary theory for Carnot groups
Nate Fisher

Abstract
In this paper, we study characteristics of horofunction boundaries of Carnot groups. In particular, we show that for Carnot groups, i.e., stratified nilpotent Lie groups equipped with certain left-invariant homogeneous metrics, all horofunctions are piecewise-defined using Pansu derivatives. For higher Heisenberg groups and filiform Lie groups, two families which generalize the standard 3-dimensional real Heisenberg group, we study the dimensions and topologies of their horofunction boundaries. In doing so, we find that filiform Lie groups of dimension n ≥ 8 n\geq 8 provide the first-known examples of Carnot groups G G whose horofunction boundaries are not of dimension dim ( G ) − 1 \dim(G)-1 .



1
Introduction

In geometric group theory, geometric topology, and dynamics, a great amount of work has been done to understand boundaries of groups and of metrics spaces. These boundary theories have proven incredibly useful, such as in the proof of Mostow’s rigidity theorem about the geometry and topology of hyperbolic manifolds. Boundaries have also been used to understand algebraic splittings of groups and the asymptotic behavior of random walks.

Recently, the horofunction boundary, a natural boundary arising from metric compactification, has been increasingly studied as a more suitable boundary for groups of mixed curvature. In particular, for nilpotent groups, Poisson boundaries are trivial, and visual boundaries have been shown to have trivial topologies
[
8
,
1
,
6
]
. This work, building upon results of
[
19
,
20
,
10
]
among others, focuses on examining the horofunction boundaries of nilpotent Lie groups.

We recall the definition of the horofunction boundary. For a metric space
(X,d)
with basepoint
x_{0}\in X
, we embed
(X,d)
into the space
\mathcal{C}(X)
of continuous real-valued functions on
X
via the map
\iota:x\mapsto d(x,\cdot)-d(x,x_{0})
. We then take the closure under the compact-open topology and define the horofunction boundary to be
\partial_{h}(X,d):=\overline{\iota(X)}\setminus\iota(X)
. See Section
2.6
for more detail.

In previous work with Sebastiano Nicolussi Golo, the author found that all horofunctions of the 3-dimensional real Heisenberg group
H({\mathbb{R}})
when equipped with any sub-Finsler metric were piecewise-linear functions of coordinates coming from the first, or horizontal, layer of the Heisenberg grading. Combining results of
[
10
,
19
]
, we also know that the horofunction boundaries of
H(\mathbb{R})
equipped with polygonal sub-Finsler metrics, which arise as the asymptotic cones of word metrics on
H({\mathbb{Z}})
, or with sub-Riemannian metrics are topologically two-dimensional. That is, the horofunction boundaries are of dimension
\dim H(\mathbb{R})-1
. In this paper, we substantially widen the scope of groups under consideration to Carnot groups equipped with what we will call
layered sup norms
: homogeneous norms realized as
L^{\infty}
combinations of norms on the layers. We ask how previous results generalize to these other Carnot groups:

1.

Are horofunctions always piecewise-linear functions of the coordinates coming from the first layer of the grading?

2.

Is the horofunction boundary of a Carnot group
G
always of dimension
\dim(G)-1
?

In this paper, we will say the horofunction boundary has
expected dimension
if its dimension is one less than the dimension of the metric space. Indeed, that is what we see for normed vector spaces
[
12
,
13
,
17
,
14
,
26
,
27
]
and Carnot–Carathéodory metrics on the Heisenberg group
[
19
,
10
]
.

A nilpotent Lie group
G
is Carnot if it is stratified, i.e., if its Lie algebra satisfies a grading criterion, and if it is equipped with a left-invariant metric which, like the Euclidean distance, is homogeneous with respect to a family of dilations. See Sections
2.1
and
2.4
for a more detailed definitions.
We will consider metrics on these stratified groups derived from homogeneous quasi-norms. These homogeneous quasi-norms are of interest to analysts, have been used to understand the large-scale geometry of nilpotent groups, and appear in the visual boundaries of symmetric spaces
[
3
,
7
,
9
,
11
]
. Specifically, in this paper we will consider metrics derived from layered sup quasi-norms, defined by taking the max of norms of projections of an element to each layer of the grading of
G
. In particular, we will consider
polysmooth
layered sup norms, where on each layer we take a norm which is either polyhedral or smooth, as defined in Section
2.4
.

For the class of polysmooth layered sup norms on Carnot groups, we are able to answer the first question from above in the affirmative.

Theorem A
.

If
G
is a stratified group equipped with a layered sup norm built of polyhedral or smooth norms, every horofunction is piecewise-linear and expressed only in terms of coordinates of the first layer of the grading.

To make progress towards answering the second question about the dimension of horofunction boundaries, we considered two infinite families of stratified groups which generalize the 3-dimensional Heisenberg group: 1) higher Heisenberg groups
H_{2n+1}({\mathbb{R}})
of dimension
2n+1
and step 2; and 2) filiform groups
L_{n}
of the first kind of dimension
n
and step
n-1
. For higher Heisenberg groups, we found that the horofunction boundary always has expected dimension. For certain exceptional layered sup norms which exhibit special symmetries, typically disjoint parts of the horofunction boundary have nonempty intersection. We will say that these norms have
non-separated
boundaries (see Section
4.3
), and the topology of the horofunction boundary in these exceptional cases can differ from the typical case.

Theorem B
.

The horofunction boundary of
H_{2n+1}(\mathbb{R})
equipped with a layered sup norm built of polyhedral or smooth norms is
2n
-dimensional. Except for non-separated norms, the horofunction boundary is homeomorphic to a
2n
-dimensional button pillow, that is, a
2n
-dimensional sphere with closed neighborhoods of the north and south poles identified.

On the other hand, for the family of filiform Lie groups, there is a threshold in dimension, and hence in nilpotency class, at which the horofunction boundary no longer has the expected dimension.

Theorem C
.

When equipped with a layered sup norm built of polyhedral or smooth norms, the horofunction boundary of the filiform group
L_{n}
(of the first kind) has dimension
n-1
for
2\leq n\leq 7
and dimension strictly less than
n-1
when
n\geq 8
.

Theorem C provides the first known examples of Carnot groups whose horofunction boundaries are not of the expected dimension. This threshold at dimension 8 is curious and begs for further exploration into how the dimension of the boundary depends on the nilpotency class and the structure of the grading.

Background and notation

We will start by surveying some background material on graded Lie groups, homogeneous metrics, and horofunction boundaries. For more details on graded Lie groups, Carnot groups, and homogeneous structures on nilpotent groups, we refer the reader to
[
9
,
22
]
.

2.1
Graded Lie algebras

Let
G
be a finite-dimensional Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and Lie bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
. We say that
\mathfrak{g}
is
gradable
if admits a
grading
, that is, a vector space decomposition of
\mathfrak{g}
into linear subspaces,

\mathfrak{g}=\oplus_{s>0}V_{s}

such that all but finitely many of the subspaces
V_{s}
are
\{0\}
and such that

[V_{s},V_{r}]:=\mathrm{span}\{[v,w]:v\in V_{s},w\in V_{r}\}\subseteq V_{s+r}\text{ for all }s,r>0.

A Lie algebra equipped with a specific grading is called a
graded Lie algebra
. We call the subspace
V_{s}
the
layer of degree
s
. Note that that a gradable Lie algebra admits infinitely many gradings. Indeed, if
(V_{s})_{s>0}
is a grading of
\mathfrak{g}
, then for any
r>0
we get another grading
(W_{s})_{s>0}
, where
W_{s}=V_{s/r}
. Using this scaling, we could, for example, scale any grading so that the first nonzero layer is the layer of degree 1, allowing for a simpler comparison of the two gradings.
We say that a Lie group is
graded
if it is simply connected and its Lie algebra is graded.

On any graded Lie algebra, we can define a family of dilations
\{\delta_{t}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}\mid t>0\}
where
\delta_{t}X=t^{j}X
for any
X\in V_{j}
. We note that
\delta_{t}
is a Lie algebra morphism—that is,
[\delta_{t}X,\delta_{t}Y]=\delta_{t}[X,Y]
. Additionally, we have
\delta_{t}\circ\delta_{s}=\delta_{ts}
.

Since the exponential map is a global diffeomorphism, by using exponential coordinates, we can define these dilations on the associated Lie group as well. See the next subsection for more on exponential coordinates.

We recall that the
lower central series
of a Lie algebra is a descending series of Lie subalgebras

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{1}\supset\mathfrak{g}_{2}\supset\cdots\supset\mathfrak{g}_{n}\supset\cdots,

where
\mathfrak{g}_{1}=\mathfrak{g}
and where
\mathfrak{g}_{n}=[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}_{n-1}]=\mathrm{span}\{[v,w]:v\in\mathfrak{g},\ w\in\mathfrak{g}_{n-1}\}
. We say that a Lie algebra is
nilpotent of step
s
if
\mathfrak{g}_{s+1}=\{0\}
while
\mathfrak{g}_{s}\neq\{0\}
. One can show that any gradable Lie algebra is nilpotent for some
s
.

A
stratification
of a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is a particular type of grading of the form

\mathfrak{g}=V_{1}\oplus V_{2}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{s},

where
[V_{1},V_{j}]=V_{j+1}
for
1\leq j\leq s
, where
V_{s}\neq\{0\}
, and where
V_{s+1}=\{0\}
. We say that
\mathfrak{g}
is
stratifiable
if it admits a stratification, and
\mathfrak{g}
equipped with a stratification is a
stratified Lie algebra
. Equivalently, a graded Lie algebra is stratified if the first layer of the grading
V_{1}
bracket-generates the entire Lie algebra. We say that a Lie group is
stratified
if it is simply connected and its Lie algebra is stratified.

While every gradable Lie algebra, and hence every stratifiable Lie algebra, is nilpotent, the converse is not true.

Example 2.1
.

(Nilpotent Lie algebra which is not gradable)
Consider the Lie algebra of dimension 7 generated by
X_{1},\ldots,X_{7}
whose only non-trivial bracket relations are

[X_{1},X_{j}]=X_{j+1}\text{ for }j=2,\ldots 6,\hskip 11.74988pt[X_{2},X_{3}]=X_{6},\hskip 11.74988pt[X_{2},X_{4}]=[X_{5},X_{2}]=[X_{3},X_{4}]=X_{7}.

This Lie algebra is nilpotent but admits no grading.

Additionally, it is worth noting that not every gradable Lie algebra is stratifiable. Indeed, we have the following example in dimension 5.

Example 2.2
.

(Gradable Lie algebra which is not stratifiable)
Consider the nilpotent Lie algebra of dimension 5 with basis
X_{1},\ldots,X_{5}
, where the only non-trivial brackets are given by

[X_{1},X_{j}]=X_{j+1}\text{ for }2\leq j\leq 4,\hskip 11.74988pt[X_{2},X_{3}]=X_{5}.

We can define a grading by putting each basis element in its own layer,

\mathfrak{g}=\langle X_{1}\rangle\oplus\langle X_{2}\rangle\oplus\langle X_{3}\rangle\oplus\langle X_{4}\rangle\oplus\langle X_{5}\rangle.

On the other hand, as an exercise, one can see that this Lie algebra does not admit a stratification.

2.2
Exponential coordinates and the Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff formula

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a nilpotent Lie algebra. On
\mathfrak{g}
we can define a group operation via the Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff formula

\displaystyle PQ

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{n}\sum_{\{s_{j}+r_{j}>0:j=1\dots n\}}\frac{[P^{r_{1}}Q^{s_{1}}P^{r_{2}}Q^{s_{2}}\cdots P^{r_{n}}Q^{s_{n}}]}{\sum_{j=1}^{n}(r_{j}+s_{j})\prod_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}!s_{i}!}

\displaystyle=P+Q+\frac{1}{2}[P,Q]+\frac{1}{12}([P,[P,Q]]-[Q,[P,Q]])+\cdots,

where

[P^{r_{1}}Q^{s_{1}}P^{r_{2}}Q^{s_{2}}\cdots P^{r_{n}}Q^{s_{n}}]=\underbrace{[P,[P,\dots,}_{r_{1}\text{ times}}\underbrace{[Q,[Q,\dots,}_{s_{1}\text{ times}}\underbrace{[P,\dots}_{\dots}]\dots]]\dots]].

The sum in the formula above is finite because
\mathfrak{g}
is nilpotent.

Using the exponential map
\exp:\mathfrak{g}\to G
, which is a global diffeomorphism for nilpotent Lie algebras, we get an identification of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and the simply connected nilpotent Lie group
G
.
Once we fix a basis for the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, through the exponential map we can identify
G
with
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with group multiplication determined by the Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff formula. These coordinates on
G
are called
exponential coordinates of the first kind
.
In these coordinates, we note that
p^{-1}=-p
for every
p\in G
and that
e=\exp(0)
is the neutral element of
G
.

2.2.1
Examples

In this paper, we will look at two infinite families of stratified groups which generalize the 3-dimensional real Heisenberg group
H(\mathbb{R})
. In exponential coordinates, we identify
H(\mathbb{R})
with
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
with group multiplication given by

(x,y,z)(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime})=\left(x+x^{\prime},y+y^{\prime},z+z^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}(xy^{\prime}-yx^{\prime})\right).

The first family which generalizes
H(\mathbb{R})
is the family of higher Heisenberg groups. We let
H_{2n+1}(\mathbb{R})
denote the real Heisenberg group of dimension
2n+1
and of step 2. In exponential coordinates,
H_{2n+1}(\mathbb{R})\cong{\mathbb{R}}^{2n+1}
, which we can think of as
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}
, where

(x,y,z)(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime})=\left(x+x^{\prime},y+y^{\prime},z+z^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}(x\cdot y^{\prime}-x^{\prime}\cdot y)\right),

and where
\cdot
above represents the standard inner product on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. On these Heisenberg groups, the standard stratification is

\langle X_{1},\ldots,X_{n},Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{n}\rangle\oplus\langle Z\rangle,

with the associated family of dilations

\delta_{t}(x,y,z)=(tx,ty,t^{2}z).

The second family of stratified groups generalizing
H(\mathbb{R})
is the family of filiform groups. The filiform group of the first kind
L_{n}
is an
n
-dimensional stratifiable Lie group of step
n-1
. We can define
L_{n}
by first defining its Lie algebra. Let
\mathfrak{l}_{n}
be the
n
-dimensional Lie algebra generated by the vectors
X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{n}
where the only nontrivial bracket relations are

[X_{1},X_{j}]=X_{j+1},
for
2\leq j\leq n-1.

This Lie algebra can be stratified as

\mathfrak{g}=\langle X_{1},X_{2}\rangle\oplus\langle X_{3}\rangle\oplus\cdots\oplus\langle X_{n}\rangle.

Note that the first layer of the stratification bracket-generates the whole Lie algebra. To understand the group multiplication in these filiform Lie groups, we can make use of the Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff formula. We note that
L_{3}=H(\mathbb{R})
, and for
L_{4}
, the 4-dimensional, step-3 filiform group, known as the Engel group, we run the computation. For two general vectors
P
and
Q
with

P=xX_{1}+yX_{2}+zX_{3}+wX_{4},\hskip 11.74988pt\text{and}\hskip 11.74988ptQ=x^{\prime}X_{1}+y^{\prime}X_{2}+z^{\prime}X_{3}+w^{\prime}X_{4},

we want to know what the product
PQ
is. Since
\mathfrak{l}_{4}
is nilpotent of step 4, we need only consider terms in the Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff formula with iterated brackets of length 3 or less. Thus we need only compute

PQ=P+Q+\frac{1}{2}[P,Q]+\frac{1}{12}([P,[P,Q]]-[Q,[P,Q]]).

Using the bracket relations for
\mathfrak{l}_{4}
given above, we find that

\displaystyle PQ=\;

\displaystyle(x+x^{\prime})X_{1}+(y+y^{\prime})X_{2}+\Bigl(z+z^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}(xy^{\prime}-yx^{\prime})\Bigr)X_{3}

\displaystyle+\Bigl(w+w^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}(xz^{\prime}-zx^{\prime})+\frac{1}{12}(x-x^{\prime})(xy^{\prime}-yx^{\prime})\Bigr)X_{4}.

In exponential coordinates, then, the group multiplication in
L_{4}
looks like

\displaystyle(x,y,z,w)(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime},w^{\prime})=\Bigl(x+x^{\prime},y+y^{\prime}

\displaystyle,z+z^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}(xy^{\prime}-yx^{\prime}),

\displaystyle w+w^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}(xz^{\prime}-zx^{\prime})+\frac{1}{12}(x-x^{\prime})(xy^{\prime}-yx^{\prime})\Bigr).

We observe that the group multiplication in the first three coordinates is identical to that of the Heisenberg group
L_{3}
. Indeed, for all
k
, the group multiplication in the first
k
coordinates of
L_{k+1}
will be identical to that of
L_{k}
. Following the same method as above, one could determine the group multiplication for each of the filiform groups
L_{n}
, the main obstruction being the complicated nature of the Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff formula for Lie algebras of higher nilpotency class.

2.3
Homogeneous structures on graded groups

Let
g
be a graded group with the dilations
\delta_{t}
associated to that grading. We will call the exponents of
t
in the dilations of each coordinate the
weights
, and we will denote by
\nu_{j}
the dilation weight associated to the coordinate
x_{j}
in the group.

A Lie group homomorphism between two graded groups
F:G\to G^{\prime}
is
homogeneous
if and only if
F\circ\delta_{t}=\delta_{t}^{\prime}\circ F
for all
t>0
, where
\delta^{\prime}_{t}
denotes the dilations in
G^{\prime}
. A scalar function
f
on
G\setminus{e}
is homogeneous of degree
\nu
if
f\circ\delta_{t}=t^{\nu}f
for all
t>0
. For example, the coordinate function
x_{j}
given by

x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\mapsto x_{j}

is homogeneous of degree
\nu_{j}
. For any nonzero multi-index
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{n}\setminus\{\vec{0}\}
, the function
x^{\alpha}:=x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}
is homogeneous of degree
[\alpha]:=\nu_{1}\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\nu_{n}\alpha_{n}
, called the
homogeneous degree
of the multi-index.

Next we state Proposition 3.1.24 from
[
9
]
, which tells us that the group multiplication we get from the Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff formula is polynomial in each coordinate and that the polynomial in the
j
th coordinate must have homogeneous degree equal to
\nu_{j}
.

Proposition 2.3
.

(
[
9
]
Prop. 3.1.24).
For any
j=1,\ldots,n,
we have

(xy)_{j}=x_{j}+y_{j}+\sum_{[\alpha]+[\beta]=\nu_{j}}c_{j,\alpha,\beta}x^{\alpha}y^{\beta},

where
\alpha
and
\beta
are multi-indices in
{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{n}\setminus\{\vec{0}\}
and
c_{j,\alpha,\beta}
are real coefficients.

2.4
Homogeneous distances and quasi-norms

A
homogeneous distance
on
G
is a distance function
d
that is left-invariant and 1-homogeneous with respect to dilations, i.e.,

(i)

d(gx,gy)=d(x,y)
for all
g,x,y\in G
;

(ii)

d(\delta_{t}x,\delta_{t}y)=t\cdot d(x,y)
for all
x,y\in G
and all
t>0
.

When a stratified group
G
is endowed with a homogeneous distance
d
, we call the metric Lie group
(G,d)
a
Carnot group
.
Homogeneous distances induce the manifold topology of
G
, see
[
21
, Proposition 2.26]
, and are bi-Lipschitz equivalent to each other.
Every homogeneous distance defines a
homogeneous norm

d_{e}(\cdot):G\to[0,\infty)
, where
d_{e}(p)=d(e,p)
and
e
is the identity element of
G
.

Relaxing the definition slightly, we can examine
homogeneous quasi-norms

|\cdot|:G\to[0,\infty)
, continuous non-negative functions satisfying

1.

|x^{-1}|=|x|
for all
x\in G
,

2.

|\delta_{t}x|=t|x|
for all
x\in G
and
t>0
,

3.

|x|=0
if and only if
x=e
.

Note that, unlike a norm, a quasi-norm need not satisfy the triangle inequality. For example, on any graded Lie group, we can define the homogeneous
p
-quasi-norms and the homogeneous sup quasi-norm:

\displaystyle|(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})|_{p}

\displaystyle:=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}|x_{j}|^{p/\nu_{j}}\right)^{1/p},\hskip 11.74988pt0<p<\infty,

\displaystyle|(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})|_{\infty}

\displaystyle:=\max_{1\leq j\leq n}\left\{|x_{j}|^{1/\nu_{j}}\right\}.

Suppose that
G
is a graded group. We equip each layer
V_{j}
of the grading with an ordinary norm
\|\cdot\|_{V_{j}}:V_{j}\cong{\mathbb{R}}^{k}\to[0,\infty)
, and let
\pi_{j}
be the projection from
G
to the coordinates in the
j
th layer of the stratification. We can define a homogeneous quasi-norm on
G
via

|x|:=\max_{1\leq j\leq s}\left\{\|\pi_{j}(x)\|_{V_{j}}^{1/j}\right\}.

We will call this a
layered sup quasi-norm
.

To study horofunction boundaries, we will want to consider norms which induce genuine metrics, not quasi-metrics. Fortunately, a lemma of Guivarc’h tells us that as long as we are in a stratified group, we can upgrade this family of layered sup quasi-norms to genuine norms at the cost of introducing some constants.

Lemma 2.4
.

(Guivarc’h,
[
11
]
Lemme II.1)
For any stratified group, up to rescaling each
\|\cdot\|_{V_{j}}
to a proportional norm
\lambda_{j}\,\|\cdot\|_{V_{j}}
if necessary, where
\lambda_{1}=1
, and
0<\lambda_{j+1}\leq\lambda_{j}
for all
j
, the layered sup quasi-norm

\|x\|=\max_{1\leq j\leq s}\left\{\|\pi_{j}(x)\|_{V_{j}}^{1/j}\right\}

is, in fact, a norm satisfying the triangle inequality and hence induces a metric.

We will call these upgraded quasi-norms
layered sup norms
, and we will at times suppress the scalars
\lambda_{j}
, assuming that the ordinary norms
\|\cdot\|_{V_{j}}
have been scaled as necessary. The main focus of this paper will be to study the horofunction boundaries of these norms on families of Carnot groups which generalize the real Heisenberg group
H(\mathbb{R})
.
In particular, we will consider
polysmooth
layered sup norms where each layer norm
\|\cdot\|_{V_{j}}
is either

1.

polyhedral
, i.e., the unit ball
Q_{j}
of
\|\cdot\|_{V_{j}}
is a convex, centrally-symmetric polyhedron, or

2.

smooth
, meaning the norm is continuously differentiable on
V_{j}\setminus\{0\}
and is strictly convex.

We note that the homogeneous sup quasi-norm
|(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})|_{\infty}=\max_{j}\left\{|x_{j}|^{1/\nu_{j}}\right\}
can be realized as a layered sup quasi-norm where we let each
\|\cdot\|_{V_{j}}
be the sup norm on that layer. Thus, we can apply Lemma
2.4
and upgrade
|\cdot|_{\infty}
to a homogeneous norm, which we will call the
homogeneous sup norm
.

2.5
Convex geometry

As we consider layered sup norms on stratified groups, we will want to make use of terminology and results coming from convex geometry. We refer the reader to
[
2
]
,
[
25
]
, and
[
26
]
for more details.

For a subset
S
of a vector space, we let
V(S)
be the smallest vector subspace containing
S
. The affine hull
\mathrm{aff}(S)
is the smallest affine subset containing
S
. We note that every affine subspace is the translation of a unique vector subspace. We call the unique subspace of which
\mathrm{aff}(S)
is a translation the
space of translations
of
\mathrm{aff}(S)
, and denote it by
T(\mathrm{aff}(S))
.

For a subset
S
, we define the
relative interior
of
S
to be the interior of
S
within the affine subspace
\mathrm{aff}(S)
. Then we can define the
relative boundary
of
S
as the closure of
S
minus the relative interior of
S
.

We remind the reader that the unit ball of any normed linear space is a centrally-symmetric convex body with non-empty interior. A
supporting hyperplane
of a convex body
Q
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
is a hyperplane satisfying two properties:

1.

Q
is contained in one of the two closed half spaces defined by the hyperplane, and

2.

Q
has at least one boundary point on the hyperplane.

Throughout this paper we assume that each of the layer norms
\|\cdot\|_{V_{j}}
which define a layered sup norm on
G
is either

1.

polyhedral
, i.e., the unit ball
Q_{j}
of
\|\cdot\|_{V_{j}}
is a convex, centrally-symmetric polyhedron, or

2.

smooth
, i.e., the norm is continuously differentiable on
V_{j}\setminus\{0\}
and
Q_{j}
is strictly convex.

For one of these convex unit balls
Q_{j}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
, a nonempty convex subset
F\subseteq Q_{j}
is a
face
of
Q_{j}
if for any
x\in F
and every closed interval with
I\subseteq Q_{j}
with
x
in the relative interior of
I
, we have
I\subseteq F
. A face of codimension 1 is called a
facet
.

If
Q_{j}
is polyhedral, then it is the intersection of finitely many halfspaces, and each face of
Q_{j}
can be described as the intersection of one or more supporting hyperplanes.

In the smooth case, we restrict our attention to strictly convex norms so that each point in the boundary of the defining convex body
Q_{j}
is an
extreme point
. That is, in the smooth case the supporting hyperplane at each point of the boundary of the unit ball
Q_{j}
is unique and intersects
Q_{j}
only at that point.

For the nonempty convex unit ball
Q_{j}
, the polar dual
Q_{j}^{\circ}

2.6
Horofunction boundary of a metric space

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space and
{\mathcal{C}}(X)
the space of continuous functions
X\to{\mathbb{R}}
endowed with the topology of the uniform convergence on compact sets.
We fix a base point
x_{0}\in X
and define
\iota:X\to{\mathcal{C}}(X)
via

\iota:x\mapsto d(x,\cdot)-d(x,x_{0}).

We define the
horofunction compactification

\overline{X}^{h}
and the
horofunction boundary

\partial_{h}(X)

\overline{X}^{h}=\overline{\iota(X)},\text{ and }\partial_{h}X:=\overline{\iota(X)}\setminus\iota(X).

Under relatively mild conditions, the map
\iota
is an embedding, i.e., a homeomorphism onto its image, making
\overline{X}^{h}
a topological compactification. See
[
4
,
10
]
.

Unlike other geometric boundary theories, the horofunction compactification, or metric compactification, can be used in very general settings without making assumptions on the geometry of the space. Additionally, the horofunction compactification is always Hausdorff, which, for example, is not necessarily true of the visual boundary in nilpotent groups and other spaces of mixed curvature
[
10
]
. Finally, isometries of the metric space extend to homeomorphisms of the horofunction boundary, which has proved useful in the study of the dynamics of isometries and the asymptotic behavior of random walks
[
18
,
15
,
16
,
23
]
.

2.7
Pansu differentiability and horofunctions

Let
G
and
G^{\prime}
be two Carnot groups with homogeneous metrics
d
and
d^{\prime}
, respectively, and let
\Omega\subseteq G
open.
A function
f:\Omega\to G^{\prime}
is
Pansu differentiable at
p\in\Omega
if
there is homogeneous Lie group homomorphism
L

such that

\lim_{x\to p}\frac{d^{\prime}(f(p)^{-1}f(x),L(p^{-1}x))}{d(p,x)}=0.

The map
L
is called
Pansu derivative
of
f
at
p
and it is denoted by
\text{[1]{$\mathrm{P}$}\!\!$\mathrm{D}$}f(p)
or
\text{[1]{$\mathrm{P}$}\!\!$\mathrm{D}$}f|_{p}
.
A function
f:\Omega\to G^{\prime}
is
strictly Pansu differentiable at
p\in\Omega
if
there is a homogeneous Lie group homomorphism
L

such that

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\ \sup\left\{\frac{d^{\prime}(f(y)^{-1}f(x),L(y^{-1}x))}{d(x,y)}:x,y\in B_{d}(p,\epsilon),\ x\neq y\right\}=0,

where
B_{d}(p,\epsilon)
is the open
\epsilon
-ball centered at
p
.
Clearly, in this case
f
is Pansu differentiable at
p
and
L=\text{[1]{$\mathrm{P}$}\!\!$\mathrm{D}$}F|_{p}
.

If we take
d
to be a homogeneous metric on a graded group
G
with identity element
e
, we can make use of Lemma 2.3 from
[
10
]
which states that every horofunction can be realized as a generalized directional derivative of the metric function
d_{e}(x)=d(e,x)
at a point on the unit metric sphere
\partial B
. That is,
if
f\in\partial_{h}(G,d)
, then there is a sequence of pairs
(p_{n},\epsilon_{n})\in\partial B\times(0,+\infty)
such that
p_{n}\to p\in\partial B
,
\epsilon_{n}\to 0
and

f(x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{d_{e}(p_{n}\delta_{\epsilon_{n}}x)-d_{e}(p_{n})}{\epsilon_{n}},\hskip 11.74988pt\text{locally uniformly in $x\in G$.}

On the other hand, if
(p_{n},\epsilon_{n})\in G\times(0,+\infty)
such that
p_{n}\to p\in\partial B
,
\epsilon_{n}\to 0
and
f:G\to{\mathbb{R}}
is the locally uniform limit
f(x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{d_{e}(p_{n}\delta_{\epsilon_{n}}x)-d_{e}(p_{n})}{\epsilon_{n}}
, then
f\in\partial_{h}(G,d)
.
The horofunction
f
is limit of the sequence of points

\iota(q_{n})=\iota(\delta_{1/\epsilon_{n}}p_{n}^{-1}).

Moreover, if
d_{e}
is strictly Pansu differentiable at
p
, then
f=\text{[1]{$\mathrm{P}$}\!\!$\mathrm{D}$}d_{e}|_{p}
;
if
p_{n}\equiv p
and
d_{e}
is Pansu differentiable at
p
, then
f=\text{[1]{$\mathrm{P}$}\!\!$\mathrm{D}$}d_{e}|_{p}
.

This lemma tells us that every horofunction in the boundary of a homogeneous group looks like a directional derivative of the metric at a point on the unit metric sphere. In practice, by describing all possible types of limits at each point on the unit metric sphere, we can give a complete description of the horofunction boundary.

This method of studying the boundary also allows us to associate points on the unit sphere with horofunctions at infinity. Since Carnot groups come equipped with dilations, we can think of points on the unit sphere as a parametrization space for all dilation tracks and, therefore, as a parametrization space of all asymptotic directions. Thus we can give a correspondence between asymptotic directions and points in the horofunction boundary.

2.8
Blow-ups and horofunctions

As we observed above, in graded groups equipped with homogeneous distances, there is a connection between horofunctions in the boundary and generalized directional derivatives of the metric norm at points on the unit sphere
\partial B
. Almost everywhere on the unit sphere, the metric norm is Pansu differentiable, and these Pansu derivatives are horofunctions in the horofunction boundary. At points where the sphere is not Pansu differentiable, we need some more tools to study the generalized directional derivatives.

Here we will outline a few of the main definitions and results (without proofs) about
blow-ups
as defined in
[
10
]
, which make use of the Kuratowski-Painlevé convergence of closed sets. This brief overview will allow us to state new results below, and we refer the reader to Section 3 of
[
10
]
for a more detailed exposition.

Let
(X,d)
be a locally compact metric space and let
\mathtt{CL}(X)
be the family of all closed subsets of
X
.
If
x\in X
and
C\subseteq X
, we set
d(x,C):=\inf\{d(x,y):y\in C\}
.
The
Kuratowski limit inferior
of a sequence
\{C_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset\mathtt{CL}(X)
is defined to be

\displaystyle\Li_{n\to\infty}C_{n}

\displaystyle:=\left\{q\in X:\limsup_{n\to\infty}d(q,C_{n})=0\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{q\in X:\forall n\in{\mathbb{N}}\;\exists x_{n}\in C_{n}\text{ s.t.~}\lim_{n\to\infty}x_{n}=q\right\},

while the
Kuratowski limit superior
is defined to be

\displaystyle\Ls_{n\to\infty}C_{n}

\displaystyle:=\left\{q\in X:\liminf_{n\to\infty}d(q,C_{n})=0\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{q\in X:\exists N\subset{\mathbb{N}}\text{ infinite }\forall k\in N\;\exists x_{k}\in C_{k}\text{ s.t.~}\lim_{k\to\infty}x_{k}=q\right\}.

From the definitions, one can see that
\Li_{n}C_{n}\subseteq\Ls_{n}C_{n}
and that they are both closed.

If
\Li C_{n}=\Ls C_{n}=C
, then we say that the
C
is the
Kuratowski limit
of
\{C_{n}\}_{n}
and we write

C=\operatorname*{K-lim}_{n\to\infty}C_{n}.

Let
G
be a homogeneous group with distance function
d
.
If
\Omega\subseteq G
is closed,
\{p_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subseteq G
and
\{\epsilon_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset(0,+\infty)
are sequences, we define the
blow-up set

\mathtt{BU}(\Omega,\{p_{n}\}_{n},\{\epsilon_{n}\}_{n}):=\operatorname*{K-lim}_{n\to\infty}\delta_{1/\epsilon_{n}}(p_{n}^{-1}\Omega),

if it exists. If the sequence
\{p_{n}\}_{n}
is identically equal to the point
p
and if the limit exists, we call this the
principal blow-up
at
p
.

Proposition 2.5
.

[
10

Let
\Omega\subseteq G
be a nonempty closed set. In the particular case that

1.

If

.

2.

If

.

One takeaway from this proposition is that the interesting examples occur when

3.

If

and

Often times our closed regions

will be defined, at least locally, as the sublevel sets of smooth functions

. In this case, blow-ups are related to the Pansu derivatives of the region-defining functions

.

Proposition 2.6
.

[

Let

Also, for every

(1)

of the function

on

.

[

Let

Looking forward, our horofunctions will be piecewise-linear functions. We will be able to partition our groups into closed dilation cones on which the norm

Theorem 2.8
.

[

Let

.
For each

.

and

sequences with

, the interior of

, and

.

.

Then

and

exists, where

g(x)=\tilde{\chi}(x)\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}\left(\text{[1]{$\mathrm{P}$}\!\!$\mathrm{D}$}f_{Q}|_{p}(x)+c_{Q}\right){\mathds{1}\!}_{R_{Q}}(x),

(2)

with

3
Horofunctions in general Carnot groups

Let
G
be a stratified group equipped with a layered sup metric induced by the norm

\|x\|=\max_{1\leq j\leq s}\left\{\|\pi_{j}(x)\|_{V_{j}}^{1/j}\right\},

as defined in Section
2.4
.
Throughout this paper we assume that the layered sup norm is polysmooth. That is, we assume that each layer norm
\|\cdot\|_{V_{j}}
is either

1.

polyhedral
, i.e., the unit ball
Q_{j}
of
\|\cdot\|_{V_{j}}
is a convex, centrally-symmetric polyhedron, or

2.

smooth
, meaning the norm is continuously differentiable on
V_{j}\setminus\{0\}
and is strictly convex.

One of our goals is to understand what types of functions can arise as horofunctions in the Carnot setting. In previous work with Sebastiano Nicolussi Golo, the author found that all horofunctions of the 3-dimensional real Heisenberg group
H({\mathbb{R}})
when equipped with any sub-Finsler metric were piecewise-linear functions of coordinates coming from the first layer of the Heisenberg grading. We now consider all Carnot groups equipped with layered sup norms and show that an analogous result holds.

Theorem A
.

If
G
is a stratified group equipped with a polysmooth layered sup norm, then all blow-ups of the norm at points
p
on the unit sphere, and hence all horofunctions, are piecewise-linear and are functions of the coordinates of the first layer of the grading.

We will prove Theorem A by first computing the Pansu derivatives of the layered sup norms at certain points on the unit metric spheres. We will then be able to apply the results from
[
10
]
as given in Section
2.8
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Suppose that
p
is a point on the unit metric sphere
\partial B
of a polysmooth layered sup norm on
G
such that
\|\pi_{i}(p)\|_{V_{i}}^{1/i}=1
and such that
\|\pi_{j}(p)\|_{V_{j}}^{1/j}<1
for
j\neq i
. If
\pi_{i}(p)
is in the interior of a facet of the polyhedron
Q_{i}
or if
\|\cdot\|_{V_{i}}
is smooth, then the layered sup norm

\|x\|=\max_{1\leq j\leq s}\left\{\|\pi_{j}(x)\|_{V_{j}}^{1/j}\right\}

is Pansu differentiable at
p
. The principal blow-up or Pansu derivative is a linear function in the coordinates of
x
coming from the first layer
V_{1}
of the stratification.

Proof.

Suppose that the
i
th layer
V_{i}\cong{\mathbb{R}}^{r}
and that
\pi_{i}(x)=(x_{i_{1}},\ldots,x_{i_{r}})
. To check the Pansu differentiability of the norm
\|\cdot\|
at the point
p
as above, we compute

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{\|p\delta_{t}x\|-\|p\|}{t}

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{\|\pi_{i}(p\delta_{t}x)\|_{V_{i}}^{1/i}-1}{t}

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{1/i}{\|\pi_{i}(p\delta_{t}x)\|_{V_{i}}^{(i-1)/i}}\cdot\frac{d}{dt}\|\pi_{i}(p\delta_{t}x)\|_{V_{i}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{i}\cdot\lim_{t\to 0}\;\frac{d}{dt}\|((p\delta_{t}x)_{i_{1}},\ldots,(p\delta_{t}x)_{i_{r}})\|_{V_{i}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{i}\cdot\lim_{t\to 0}\;\sum_{k=1}^{r}\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i_{k}}}\right|_{\pi_{i}(p\delta_{t}x)}\|(x_{i_{1}},\ldots,x_{i_{r}})\|_{V_{i}}\>\frac{d}{dt}(p\delta_{t}x)_{i_{k}}.

By assumption, our layer norms
\|\cdot\|_{V_{j}}
are continuously differentiable in a small neighborhood containing
p
, and hence each partial derivative
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i_{k}}}\|\pi_{i}(p\delta_{t}x)\|_{V_{i}}
exists for
t
sufficiently small. As
t\to 0
, the limit
\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i_{k}}}\|\pi_{i}(p\delta_{t}x)\|_{V_{i}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i_{k}}}\|\pi_{i}(p)\|_{V_{i}}
, which does not depend on
x
.

Since
(p\delta_{t}x)_{i_{k}}
is a polynomial in the coordinates of
p
and
\delta_{t}x
, the limit
\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{d}{dt}(p\delta_{t}x)_{i_{k}}
will pick out the coefficient of the linear term
t
in
(p\delta_{t}x)_{i_{k}}
. From Proposition
2.3
, we know that the terms in the coordinate
(p\delta_{t}x)_{i_{k}}
of the product which are linear in
t
are

\sum_{[\alpha]=i-1,[\beta]=1}c_{k,\alpha,\beta}(p)^{\alpha}(\delta_{t}x)^{\beta}.

Hence the coefficient of
t
in
(p\delta_{t}x)_{i_{k}}
is a linear combination of products of the coordinates of
p
, which are fixed, with linear expressions in coordinates of
x
coming from the first layer of the grading.

We conclude, therefore, that the polysmooth layered sup norm is Pansu differentiable at
p
, and the derivative is a linear function of the coordinates of
x
coming from the first layer of the stratification.

∎

Note that when
G
is a filiform group, since every layer except
V_{1}
is 1-dimensional, any layered sup norm, modulo scaling layer norms by a constant
\lambda_{j}
as in Guivarc’h’s Lemma
2.4
, is identical to the homogeneous sup norm
\|x\|=\max_{j}\left\{|x_{j}|^{1/\nu_{j}}\right\}
except possibly in the first layer. Since we will study the horofunction boundaries of filiform groups in Section

, it will be useful to look at the particular case of Proposition
3.1
for the homogeneous sup norm.

Let
p=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})
and suppose that
|a_{i}|=1
while
|a_{j}|<1
for
j\neq i
. If we let
c_{i,k}
denote the coefficient of
t^{k}
in the

th coordinate fo the product

, then we have

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{\|p\delta_{t}x\|-\|p\|}{t}

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{\left|a_{i}+t^{\nu_{i}}x_{i}+\sum_{[\alpha]+[\beta]=\nu_{i}}c_{i,\alpha,\beta}(p)^{\alpha}(\delta_{t}x)^{\beta}\right|^{\frac{1}{\nu_{i}}}-1}{t}

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{\left|x_{i}t^{\nu_{i}}+c_{i,\nu_{i}-1}t^{(\nu_{i}-1)}+\cdots+c_{i,1}t+a_{i}\right|^{\frac{1}{\nu_{i}}}-1}{t}

\displaystyle=\pm\frac{c_{i,1}}{\nu_{i}|a_{i}|^{(\nu_{i}-1)/\nu_{i}}}=\pm\frac{c_{i,1}}{\nu_{i}},

Proposition 3.2
.

Suppose that

\|\pi_{i}(p)\|_{V_{i}}^{1/i}=1

\pi_{i}(p)
belongs to a face of
Q_{i}
of codimension greater than or equal to 2. In general, the polysmooth layered sup norm
is not Pansu differentiable at
p
, but its principal blow-up is a piecewise-linear function of the coordinates of
x
coming from the first layer
V_{1}
of the stratification.

Proof.

Following the same arguments as in the proof of Proposition
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{\|p\delta_{t}x\|-\|p\|}{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{i}\cdot\lim_{t\to 0}\;\frac{d}{dt}\|\pi_{i}(p\delta_{t}x)\|_{V_{i}}.

Since this norm is polygonal, locally near
\pi_{i}(p)
, the norm
\|\cdot\|_{V_{i}}
is piecewise-linear where each subdomain is a linear cone.
For a fixed
x\in G
, the curve
\{p\delta_{t}x\mid t\geq 0\}
is a smooth curve based at
p
which is polynomial in
t
. Hence for
t
sufficiently small, the projected curve
\pi_{i}(p\delta_{t}x)
must stay in a single linear cone, say
C_{\alpha}
where
\|y\|_{V_{1}}=\alpha\cdot y
for all
y\in C_{\alpha}
. In fact, we could write down conditions on the coordinates

which would guarantee that
\pi_{i}(p\delta_{t}x)\in C_{\alpha}
for

sufficiently small. These conditions will depend on the coefficients of the terms which are linear in
t
in
\pi_{i}(p\delta_{t}x)
, and, therefore, will depend only on coordinates of
x
coming from the first layer of the grading. For such
x\in G
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{i}\cdot\lim_{t\to 0}\;\frac{d}{dt}\|\pi_{i}(p\delta_{t}x)\|_{V_{i}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{i}\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{d}{dt}\;\alpha\cdot\pi_{i}(p\delta_{t}x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{i}\;\alpha\cdot\left(\lim_{t\to 0}\;\frac{d}{dt}\;\pi_{i}(p\delta_{t}x)\right)=\frac{1}{i}\;\alpha\cdot(c_{i_{1},1},\ldots,c_{i_{r},1}),

where again,
c_{i,k}
is the coefficient of
t^{k}
in the
i
th coordinate of

. For the same reasons as above is a linear function in the coordinates of the first layer of the stratification. Since this holds for each of the partial functions and linear cones defining the polygonal norm, our principal blow-up function is piecewise-linear and a function of the coordinates of the first layer of the stratification.

∎

Proof of Theorem A.

Let
p
be a point on the unit sphere
\partial B
of a polysmooth layered sup norm satisfying
\|\pi_{i}(p)\|_{V_{i}}^{1/i}=1
and

is smooth or polyhedral,

tell us that the principal blow-up at

of the stratification. Fisher–Nicolussi Golo’s theorem stated above as Theorem

Finally, once we have the principal blow-ups at all of these points on

\cdot

{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

1.

polyhedral, or

2.

For generic metrics of this type, we are able to give explicit analytic and topological descriptions of the horofunction boundary. There are certain layered sup norms on

.

The horofunction boundary of

described above in Section

4.1
Blow-ups at interior ceiling/floor points

First we consider points in the relative interior of the ceiling and floor of the unit sphere, i.e., points
p=(a,b,c)\in\partial B
for which
\|(a,b)\|_{V_{1}}<1
and
\lambda\sqrt{|c|}=1
.

Lemma 4.1
.

(Blow-ups at ceiling/floor points)
Let
p=(a,b,c)
be a point in the interior of the ceiling or floor of
\partial B
, that is, such that
\|(a,b)\|_{V_{1}}<1
and
c=\pm 1/\lambda^{2}
. If
p
is on the ceiling, then the blow-up of the norm at
p
is given by

\phi^{+}_{(a,b)}(x,y,z)=\frac{\lambda^{2}}{4}(-b\cdot x+a\cdot y).

If
p
is on the floor, then the blow-up of the norm at
p
is given by

\phi^{-}_{(a,b)}(x,y,z)=\frac{\lambda^{2}}{4}(b\cdot x-a\cdot y).

The set of all blow-up functions at interior ceiling is equal to the set of blow-up functions at interior floor points, and is homeomorphic to an open
2n
-dimensional ball.

Proof.

Let
p=(a,b,c)
be a point in the interior of the ceiling or floor of
\partial B
as in the statement of the lemma. To compute the principal blow-up at
p
, we let
q=(x,y,z)
and evaluate

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{\|p\delta_{t}q\|-\|p\|}{t}

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{\max\left\{\|(a+tx,b+ty)\|_{V_{1}},\lambda\sqrt{|\pm\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}+t^{2}z+\frac{1}{2}t(a\cdot y-b\cdot x)|}\right\}-1}{t}

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{\lambda\sqrt{|\pm\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}+t^{2}z+\frac{1}{2}t(a\cdot y-b\cdot x)|}-1}{t}

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{\lambda\sqrt{\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\pm t^{2}z\pm\frac{1}{2}t(a\cdot y-b\cdot x)}-1}{t}=\pm\frac{\lambda^{2}}{4}(-b\cdot x+a\cdot y).

As the principal blow-up function at
p
is linear, this is the unique blow-up of the norm at
p
.
Since we are assuming that the norm
\|\cdot\|_{V_{1}}
is symmetric, we have that the set of interior ceiling point blow-ups
\{\frac{\lambda^{2}}{4}(-b\cdot x+a\cdot y)\mid\|(a,b)\|_{V_{1}}<1\}
is equal to the set of interior floor point blow-ups
\{\frac{\lambda^{2}}{4}(b\cdot x-a\cdot y)\mid\|(a,b)\|_{V_{1}}<1\}
, and this set of functions is homeomorphic to an open
2n
-dimensional ball.
∎

We note that since the horofunction boundary is a closed set, the boundary points of this open
2n
-dimensional ball, that is, the functions
\{\frac{\lambda^{2}}{4}(-b\cdot x+a\cdot y)\mid\|(a,b)\|_{V_{1}}=1\}

also appear in the horofunction boundary. Indeed, these functions will appear as blow-ups of the norm at seam points of the unit sphere
\partial B
, as described in Section
4.4
.

4.2
Blow-ups at interior wall points

The blow-up functions at interior wall points, i.e., points
p=(a,b,c)\in\partial B
for which
\|(a,b)\|_{V_{1}}=1
and
\lambda\sqrt{|c|}<1
, will depend on the geometry of the polyhedral or smooth normed space
({\mathbb{R}}^{2n},\|\cdot\|_{V_{1}})
. Indeed, as we consider a polysmooth layered sup norm on
H_{2n+1}(\mathbb{R})
, our results on the blow-ups of the norm at interior wall points will rely upon the following result of Schilling.

Theorem 4.2

(
[
26
]
, Theorem 3.3.10)
.

The horofunction boundary of
({\mathbb{R}}^{n},\|\cdot\|)
, where
\|\cdot\|
is either polyhedral or smooth, is homeomorphic to
S^{n-1}
.

Corollary 4.3
.

(Blow-ups at wall points)
The collection of all blow-up functions at interior wall points, that is, at points
p=(a,b,c)
such that
\|(a,b)\|_{V_{1}}=1
and
\lambda\sqrt{|c|}<1
, is isomorphic to the boundary
\partial_{h}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n},\|\cdot\|_{V_{1}})
, which is homeomorphic
S^{2n-1}
.

Proof.

Let
p=(a,b,c)
be an interior wall point of
\partial B
as in the statement of the corollary. To compute the principal blow-up at
p
, we let
q=(x,y,z)
and evaluate

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{\|p\delta_{t}q\|-\|p\|}{t}

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{\max\left\{\|(a+tx,b+ty)\|_{V_{1}},\lambda\sqrt{|\pm\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}+t^{2}z+\frac{1}{2}t(a\cdot y-b\cdot x)|}\right\}-1}{t}

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{\|(a+tx,b+ty)\|_{V_{1}}-1}{t}.

We note that this is equal to the principal blow-up of
\|\cdot\|_{V_{1}}
at the point
(a,b)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}
. Our horofunctions are independent of the vertical coordinate
z
, and the result follows from
[
26
]
.
∎

To describe these horofunctions in more detail, we will make use of ideas from convex geometry as defined in Section
2.5
. We remind the reader that the affine hull
\mathrm{aff}(S)
is the smallest affine subset containing
S
, and the unique vector subspace of which
\mathrm{aff}(S)
is a translation is denoted by
T(\mathrm{aff}(S))
, or simply
T(S)
.

Let
Q_{1}
denote the unit ball of
({\mathbb{R}}^{2n},\|\cdot\|_{V_{1}})
. For both polyhedral and smooth norms, in
[
26
]
Schilling gives the horofunction boundary as

\partial_{h}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n},\|\cdot\|_{V_{1}})=\{h_{E,u}\mid E\subseteq Q_{1}^{\circ}\text{ is a proper face, }u\in T(E)^{*}\},

where
h_{E,u}:X\to{\mathbb{R}},\hskip 11.74988pty\mapsto|u-y|_{E}-|u|_{E}
,
and for a convex set
C\subseteq X^{*}
, we have
|x|_{C}:=-\inf_{q\in C}\langle q|x\rangle
. Schilling then goes on to describe a homeomorphism between this set and the boundary of the polar dual of the unit ball
Q_{1}
.

We now reframe these results in terms of blow-ups and Pansu derivatives. If
d_{e}=\|\cdot\|_{V_{1}}
is smooth, then the horofunction boundary of
({\mathbb{R}}^{2n},\|\cdot\|_{V_{1}})
is given by

\Delta=\{\text{[1]{$\mathrm{P}$}\!\!$\mathrm{D}$}d_{e}|_{(a,b)}(x,y)=h_{(a,b)^{\circ}}(x,y)=\alpha\cdot x+\beta\cdot y\mid(\alpha,\beta)=(a,b)^{\circ},(a,b)\in\partial Q_{1}\},

where
(a,b)^{\circ}
denotes the exposed dual of the extreme point
(a,b)
, as defined in Section
2.5
.

When
\|\cdot\|_{V_{1}}
is polygonal, explicitly writing down all of the horofunctions takes a bit more work. Using Schilling’s notation, if we let
\{F_{i}\}_{i}
be the finite set of faces of
Q_{1}
of any dimension to which
(a,b)
belongs, then the set of all blow-ups of the norm at
p=(a,b)
is given by

\mathcal{W}_{(a,b)}=\bigcup_{F_{i}\ni(a,b)}\{h_{F_{i}^{\circ},u}\mid u\in V(F_{i})^{\perp}\}.

We can also describe the horofunction boundary in another way. As stated above, the horofunction boundary is homeomorphic to
\partial Q_{1}^{\circ}
, which is itself a polyhedron. This dual polyhedron can be described by the union of finitely many
(2n-1)
-dimensional faces, each of which is the exposed dual of a vertex of
Q_{1}
. In the horofunction boundary, each of these
(2n-1)
-dimensional faces is the set of all blow-ups, that is, the set of both finite and infinite translates of the principal blow-up, of
d_{e}=\|\cdot\|_{V_{1}}
at a vertex of
Q_{1}
.
The principal blow-up at a vertex will be a piecewise-linear function defined in terms of the codimension-1 facets which intersect at the vertex. Indeed, each facet
F_{i}
of this polyhedron is defined by a linear equation
\alpha\cdot x+\beta\cdot y=1
. The horofunctions in
\partial_{h}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},\|\cdot\|_{V_{1}})
are linear and piecewise-linear functions built out of the finitely-many facet blow-up functions
\{h_{F_{i}^{\circ}}(x,y)=\alpha\cdot x+\beta\cdot y\mid F_{i}\text{ is a facet of }Q_{1}\}.

4.3
Disjointness of ceiling/floor and wall blow-up functions

We’d now like to examine if the two families of horofunctions we have found so far are disjoint or not. As the closure of blow-ups at interior ceiling and floor points, we have

\Phi=\left\{\frac{\lambda^{2}}{4}(-b\cdot x+a\cdot y)\mid\|(a,b)\|_{V_{1}}\leq 1\right\}=\overline{\{\phi^{\pm}_{(a,b)}\mid\|(a,b)\|_{V_{1}}<1\}}.

As the blow-ups at interior wall points, we get
\Delta
, a copy of
\partial_{h}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n},\|\cdot\|_{V_{1}})
. (As discussed in the previous section, it is not as simple to give an explicit description of the functions in
\Delta
.) In the general case, which we will call the
separated
case, these two families are disjoint, and the blow-up functions coming from seam points will join the families to give us the boundary described in Theorem B. There are examples, however, where these families are not disjoint, such as the one described in Section
4.5
. If we fix our norm
\|\cdot\|_{V_{1}}
on the first layer of
H_{2n+1}(\mathbb{R})
, we can always find a open interval’s worth of values positive scalars
\lambda
such that the layered sup quasi-norm
\max\{\|(x,y)\|_{V_{1}},\lambda\sqrt{|z|}\}
induces a genuine metric and whose boundary is separated.

Lemma 4.4
.

For every norm
\|\cdot\|_{V_{1}}
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}
, there exists a positive
\lambda_{0}<1
such that for all positive
\lambda\leq\lambda_{0}
, the layered sup quasi-norm
|(x,y,z)|=\max\{\|(x,y)\|_{V_{1}},\lambda\sqrt{|z|}\}
on
H_{2n+1}(\mathbb{R})
is a genuine norm. Additionally, there exists a positive
\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{0}
such that for all positive
\lambda\leq\lambda_{1}
, the horofunction boundary of
H_{2n+1}(\mathbb{R})
equipped with the layered sup norm is separated.

Proof.

For the first statement, Guivarc’h’s result cited above as Lemma
2.4
tells us that there exists a positive
\lambda_{0}
such that
\max\{\|(x,y)\|_{V_{1}},\lambda_{0}\sqrt{|z|}\}
is a genuine norm on
H_{2n+1}(\mathbb{R})
. The proof of Guivarc’h’s lemma makes clear that any positive
\lambda\leq\lambda_{0}
also suffices.

For the second statement, let
\Phi(\lambda)=\left\{\frac{\lambda^{2}}{4}(-b\cdot x+a\cdot y)\mid\|(a,b)\|_{V_{1}}\leq 1\right\}
, and let
\Delta=\partial_{h}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n},\|\cdot\|_{V_{1}})
. We would like to find a
\lambda_{1}
such that for all positive
\lambda<\lambda_{1}
, we have

\Phi(\lambda)\cap\Delta=\emptyset
. Note that the family
\Phi
is made up entirely of linear functions, and in fact it is a scaled and rotated copy of
Q_{1}
in
({\mathbb{R}}^{2n})^{*}
. On the other hand,
\Delta
is independent of the scalar
\lambda
and will contain nonlinear functions if
\|\cdot\|_{V_{1}}
is polyhedral. If
\|\cdot\|_{V_{1}}
is polyhedral, then there is a finite number of linear functions in
\Delta
, one for each of the codimension-1 faces of the polyhedron
Q_{1}
. If
\|\cdot\|_{V_{1}}
is smooth, then all functions in
\Delta
are linear. Either way, the set of linear functions
\Delta\cap({\mathbb{R}}^{2n})^{*}
is a closed set which does not contain the origin. Hence there exists an open neighborhood
U
of
0\in({\mathbb{R}}^{2n})^{*}
which is disjoint from
\Delta\cap({\mathbb{R}}^{2n})^{*}
. Let
\lambda_{1}^{\prime}>0
be a scalar such that
\Phi(\lambda_{1}^{\prime})\subseteq U
. Then if
\lambda_{1}=\min\{\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1}^{\prime}\}
, then
\Phi(\lambda)\cap\Delta=\emptyset
for all
\lambda\leq\lambda_{1}
.
∎

4.4
Blow-ups at seam points

Finally, we must examine the blow-ups of the polysmooth layered sup norm at points on the seams between the wall regions and the ceiling or floor regions of the unit sphere
\partial B
. So far in the separated case, we have a
2n
-dimensional ball of horofunctions, i.e., blow-ups, corresponding to interior ceiling or floor points and a
(2n-1)
-dimensional sphere of horofunctions corresponding to interior wall points. In the right panel of Figure
3
, these are given by the purple disk and the orange circle, respectively.

Since a seam point
p
is on the border between two regions on which the layered sup norm is measured in different ways, the principal blow-up function of the norm at
p
will be a piecewise-defined function with partial functions corresponding to wall point blow-ups and ceiling point blow-ups.

Lemma 4.5
.

(Blow-ups at ceiling seam points)
The set of blow-up functions at ceiling seam points, that is, at points
p=(a,b,c)
such that
\|(a,b)\|_{V_{1}}=1
and
c=\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}
, is homeomorphic to a closed spherical shell
S^{2n-1}\times I
, for
I
a closed interval. One boundary sphere of the spherical shell is the boundary sphere of
\Phi=\{\frac{\lambda^{2}}{4}(-b\cdot x+a\cdot y)\mid\|(a,b)\|_{V_{1}}\leq 1\}
, and the other boundary sphere is
\Delta=\partial_{h}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n},\|\cdot\|_{V_{1}})
.

Proof.

Suppose
p=(a,b,c)
is a ceiling seam point with
\|(a,b)\|_{V_{1}}=1
and
c=\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}
. Whether
\|\cdot\|_{V_{1}}
is smooth or polyhedral, the principal blow-up of the layered sup norm at
p
will have the ceiling function
\phi^{+}_{(a,b)}
, as defined in Lemma
4.1
, as a partial function. Any two piecewise-defined functions can only be equal if they are defined by the same partial functions. Since
\phi^{+}_{(a,b)}=\phi^{+}_{(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})}
if and only if
(a,b)=(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})
and since we are assuming the boundary is separated, meaning no other partial functions in the blow-up are equal to
\phi^{+}_{(a,b)}
, the principal blow-up functions at points on the ceiling seam are pairwise distinct. Via the same argument, any two finite translates of the principal blow-ups at different ceiling seam points must also be distinct.

If
\|\cdot\|_{V_{1}}
is smooth, then the principal blow-up of the homogeneous norm at
p
is a piecewise-linear function with partial functions
\phi^{+}_{(a,b)}
and
h_{(a,b)^{\circ}}
, where the subdomains of these two partial functions are uniquely determined by requiring that the blow-up function is continuous. Indeed, these linear functions are distinct since

\phi^{+}_{(a,b)}(a,b)=\frac{\lambda^{2}}{4}(-b\cdot a+a\cdot b)=0
, while by the definition of the exposed dual,
h_{(a,b)^{\circ}}(a,b)=1.

Since there are only two partial functions in this principal blow-up function, there is a 1-parameter family of translates of this function, with limiting infinite translates given by
\phi^{+}_{(a,b)}
and
h_{(a,b)^{\circ}}
. Hence we have a one-to-one correspondence between the boundary ceiling blow-up functions
\{\phi^{+}_{(a,b)}\mid\|(a,b)\|_{V_{1}}=1\}
and the wall blow-up functions
\{h_{(a,b)\circ}\mid\|(a,b)\|_{V_{1}}=1\}
, and we get a 1-parameter family of blow-ups connecting each pair. Since
\phi^{+}_{(a,b)}
and
h_{(a,b)\circ}
vary continuously with
a
and
b
, this gives us a set of blow-up functions which is homeomorphic to
S^{2n-1}\times I
.

When
Q_{1}
is polyhedral, the construction of the spherical shell is more complex. Let
F
is the face of
Q_{1}
of minimal dimension to which
(a,b)
belongs with
\dim F=k
. We know from above that the family of wall blow-up functions
\mathcal{W}_{(a,b)}
associated to
(a,b)
is
(2n-1-k)
-dimensional. We want show that in the principal blow-up at our ceiling seam point
p=(a,b,c)
, the subdomain of the partial function corresponding to the ceiling dilation cone is transverse to the subdomains of the partial functions corresponding to the wall dilation cone. Knowing this tells us that the ceiling seam principal blow-up corresponding to
(a,b)
has one more dimension of translates than the corresponding principal wall blow-up.

Indeed, consider the norm
\|\cdot\|_{V_{1}}
locally near the point
(a,b)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}
. It is piecewise-linear with subdomains which are linear cones based at
(a,b)
. That is, the boundaries of these subdomains are half-hyperplanes through the origin which all intersect at
(a,b)
and hence along the line connecting the origin to
(a,b)
. The principal blow-up of
\|\cdot\|_{V_{1}}
at
(a,b)
, therefore, is invariant under translates in the direction of
(a,b)
.

The point
(a,b)
is in intersection of finitely many facets
\{F_{j}\}_{j},
each defined by the unique hyperplane
H_{j}=\{(x,y)\mid\alpha_{j}\cdot x+\beta_{j}\cdot y=1\}
. If we let
G_{j}(x,y,z)=(\alpha_{j}\cdot x+\beta_{j}\cdot y)^{2}-\lambda^{2}z
, then the ceiling dilation cone, i.e., the set of points in the dilation cone of the closed ceiling face of
\partial B
, is defined locally near
p
by the locus
\cap_{j}\{G_{j}\leq 0\}
. By Proposition
2.6
and Theorem
2.8
, the principal blow-up of the homogeneous norm at
p
, then, has the partial function
\phi^{+}_{(a,b)}
defined on the subdomain
\cap_{j}\{q\in H_{2n+1}(\mathbb{R})\mid\text{[1]{$\mathrm{P}$}\!\!$\mathrm{D}$}G_{j}|_{p}(q)\leq 0\}
. Computing these Pansu derivatives, we see

\displaystyle\text{[1]{$\mathrm{P}$}\!\!$\mathrm{D}$}G_{j}|_{p}(x,y,z)

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{(\alpha_{j}(a+tx)+\beta_{j}\cdot(b+ty))^{2}-\lambda^{2}(c+t^{2}z+\frac{1}{2}t(a\cdot y-b\cdot x))}{t}

\displaystyle=\left(2\alpha_{j}+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}b\right)\cdot x+\left(2\beta_{j}-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}a\right)\cdot y,

and evaluated at
-p
, we see
\text{[1]{$\mathrm{P}$}\!\!$\mathrm{D}$}G_{j}|_{p}(-a,-b,-c)=-2
. Hence
-p
is in the interior of the subdomain for the partial function
\phi^{+}_{(a,b)}
. We have, then, the boundaries of the subdomain for this ceiling blow-up function are transverse to the line connecting the origin to
p
, and we, therefore, have a new direction in which to take translates of the principal blow-up.

Again, we were assuming
F
was the face of
Q_{1}
of minimal dimension to which
(a,b)
belongs with
\dim F=k
and that the family of wall blow-up functions
\mathcal{W}_{(a,b)}
associated to
(a,b)
was
(2n-1-k)
-dimensional. We can conclude, therefore, that the set of blow-up functions corresponding to the ceiling seam point
p
is
(2n-k)
-dimensional, which we can think of as the join of
\phi^{+}_{(a,b)}
and the family
\mathcal{W}_{(a,b)}
. As we let
(a,b)
vary along the
k
-dimensional face
F
, we get a
2n
-dimensional family of blow-ups, described as the join of
\{\phi^{+}_{(a,b)}\mid(a,b)\in F\}
and
\mathcal{W}_{(a,b)}
(which is equal to
\mathcal{W}_{(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})}
if
(a,b)
and
(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})
belong to all the same faces). These
2n
-dimensional joins come together to create a
2n
-dimensional spherical shell.

∎

Lemma 4.6
.

(Blow-ups at floor seam points)
The set of blow-up functions at floor seam points, that is, at points
p=(a,b,c)
such that
\|(a,b)\|_{V_{1}}=1
and
c=-\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}
, also is homeomorphic to a closed
2n
-dimensional spherical shell with the same boundary spheres as the spherical shell of blow-ups at ceiling points. The set of blow-ups at floor seam points is disjoint from the set of blow-up functions at ceiling seam points except along their shared boundary spheres.

Proof.

The first statement of this lemma can be proved following a similar argument to the proof of the previous lemma on the blow-ups at ceiling seam points.

It remains to show that the set of principal blow-ups at floor seam points is disjoint from the set of principal blow-ups at ceiling seam points. As above, we consider partial functions involved in the blow-up functions. Note that
\phi^{+}_{(a,b)}=\phi^{-}_{(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})}
if and only if
(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})=(-a,-b)
. Hence we might ask if the principal blow-up at
p=(a,b,1/\lambda^{2})
might be equal to the principal blow-up at
-p=(-a,-b,-1/\lambda^{2})
. We observed in the previous proof that
-p
was in the subdomain of
\phi^{+}_{(a,b)}
in the principal blow-up at
p
, while
p
was not. Similarly, we could show that
p
is in the subdomain of
\phi^{-}_{(-a,-b)}
in the principal blow-up at
-p
, while
-p
is not. Hence these two blow-up functions cannot be equal.
∎

Proof of Theorem B.

We note that the family of all blow-up functions of interior ceiling points and ceiling seam points give a closed ball of dimension
2n
with boundary sphere isomorphic to
\partial_{h}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n},\|\cdot\|_{V_{1}})
. The same is true of the family of all blow-up functions of interior floor points and floor seam points.

When we identify the common boundary spheres of these two
(2n)
-balls, we get a set homeomorphic to
S^{2n}
. Finally, the blow-up functions at interior ceiling and floor give the same family, resulting in an identification of
\{\phi^{+}_{(a,b)}\mid\|(a,b)\|_{V_{1}}\leq 1\}
with
\{\phi^{-}_{(a,b)}\mid\|(a,b)\|_{V_{1}}\leq 1\}
.
∎

4.5
Non-separated example

In this section, we will describe in detail an example of a non-separated horofunction boundary of
H_{2n+1}(\mathbb{R})
. Recall that non-separated here means that the two families of blow-up functions coming from the interior ceiling/floor points and the interior wall points of the metric sphere are not disjoint. This results in a horofunction boundary whose topology is different from the topology described in Theorem B.

Let
\|\cdot\|_{V_{1}}=2\|\cdot\|_{\text{Eucl}}
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. It is not a difficult exercise to show that for all
\lambda\in(0,1]
, the norm
\|(x,y,z)\|_{\lambda}=\max\{\|(x,y)\|_{V_{1}},\lambda\sqrt{|z|}\}
satisfies the triangle inequality.

Hence we observe that the closure of the blow-ups at interior ceiling and floor points
\Phi=\{\frac{\lambda^{2}}{4}(-b\cdot x+a\cdot y)\mid\|(a,b)\|_{V_{1}}\leq 1\}
is the closed disk of radius
1/2
centered at the origin in
({\mathbb{R}}^{2})^{*}
. Since
\|\cdot\|_{V_{1}}
is smooth, we recall that the family of blow-ups at interior wall points
\Delta=\partial_{h}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n},\|\cdot\|_{V_{1}})=\partial Q_{1}^{\circ}
, which is the circle of radius
1/2
in
({\mathbb{R}}^{2})^{*}
. Thus
\Phi
and
\Delta
intersect along the boundary of the disk of radius 1/2.

The spherical shell (i.e., annulus) of ceiling seam blow-up points
S^{1}\times I
, therefore, has its boundary circles identified (with a rotation), giving us a torus of functions in the boundary. The floor blow-up functions contribute another torus, also attached to the disk
\Phi
along its boundary, as in Figure
4
. This results in a horofunction boundary whose topological type is different from what was described in Theorem B and shown in Figure
3
.

Figure 4:
Unit metric sphere (left) and horofunction boundary (right) of
H(\mathbb{R})
where
\|\cdot\|_{V_{1}}=2\|\cdot\|_{\text{Eucl}}
. In this non-separated example, the boundary has a different homeomorphism type.

If we choose
\lambda<1
, the intersection
\Psi\cap\Delta
is empty, and the horofunction boundary of the homogenenous norm
\|(x,y,z)\|=\max\{\|(x,y)\|_{V_{1}},\lambda\sqrt{|z|}\}
on
H_{2n+1}(\mathbb{R})
is separated. That is, for this family of layered sup norms where
\|\cdot\|_{V_{1}}=2\|\cdot\|_{\text{Eucl}}
and
\|\cdot\|_{V_{2}}=\lambda\sqrt{|\cdot|}
, for all but one value of
\lambda\in(0,1]
, the horofunction boundary has the topology described in Theorem B.

For all other families of examples the author has considered, there has either been no value or a unique value of
\lambda
(among those for which the homogenous norm satisfies the triangle inequality) for which the horofunction boundary is non-separated, suggesting that the topology of the boundary described in Theorem B is typical or generic among polysmooth layered sup norms.

5
Horofunction boundaries of filiform Lie groups

In this section we consider the
n
-dimensional, step-
(n-1)
filiform Lie groups
L_{n}
in exponential coordinates of the first kind for
n\geq 3
. As described above in Section
2.2
, we can define
L_{n}
by first defining its Lie algebra. Let
\mathfrak{l}_{n}
be the
n
-dimensional Lie algebra generated by the vectors
X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{n}
, where the only nontrivial bracket relations are

[X_{1},X_{j}]=X_{j+1},\;2\leq j\leq n-1
.
This Lie algebra admits the stratification

\mathfrak{g}=\langle X_{1},X_{2}\rangle\oplus\langle X_{3}\rangle\oplus\cdots\oplus\langle X_{n}\rangle
.

We can view
L_{n}
as
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with the group multiplication defined by the Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff formula. For any
n\geq 3
, the group multiplication in the first three coordinates is identical to that of the Heisenberg group
H(\mathbb{R})=L_{3}
, and the group
L_{4}
is commonly known as the Engel group. As
n
gets large, the Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff formula involves more terms with higher-order commutators, and consequently, the group multiplication becomes quite complicated and difficult to write down. This complexity makes it difficult to give a full analytic description of the horofunction boundary, but we are able to determine the dimension of the horofunction boundary of
L_{n}
equipped with any polysmooth layered sup norm for any
n\geq 3
.

Theorem C
.

The filiform group
L_{n}
when equipped with a polysmooth layered sup norm has a horofunction boundary which is of the expected dimension of
(n-1)
when
n\leq 7
. For
n>7
, the horofunction boundary is of dimension
\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil+2
, which is strictly less than
n-1
.

To the author’s knowledge, this theorem provides the first examples of topological groups
G
whose horofunction boundaries are not of dimension
\dim(G)-1
.

To prove Theorem C, we will first show there are upper bounds on the dimension of the horofunction boundary of
L_{n}
in Section
5.3
. We will then exhibit families of horofunctions of maximal dimension for each
L_{n}
in Section
5.4
. First, though, we will make some observations concerning blow-ups and horofunctions in the boundary of
L_{n}
in Section
5.1
, and in Section
5.2
, we will look at parts of the horofunction boundary of
L_{4}
to provide some concrete examples.

5.1
Observations on blow-ups in
\partial_{h}(L_{n})

Consider
L_{n}
equipped with a polysmooth layered sup norm

\|x\|=\max_{1\leq j\leq s}\left\{\|\pi_{j}(x)\|_{V_{j}}^{1/j}\right\}
. Recall that for the filiform Lie groups

Using Lemma 2.3 from

.

\displaystyle\Omega_{2}

5.3
Upper bounds on the dimension of
\partial_{h}(L_{n})

Lemma 5.7
.

For
n\geq 3
, the horofunction boundary of
L_{n}
equipped with a polysmooth layered sup norm has dimension less than or equal to
n-1
.

Proof.

To prove this lemma, we will show that no face of the unit sphere
\partial B
can have a family of blow-up functions of dimension greater than
n-1
.

Suppose that we have a face
{\mathcal{F}}_{1}
along which all principal blow-ups are linear functions. Then for each point
p\in{\mathcal{F}}_{1}
, the set of blow-ups
\mathtt{BU}(p)
is a single function (with no translates). In
L_{n}
, the unit metric ball has no faces with dimension greater than
n-1
, and so
\dim\mathtt{BU}({\mathcal{F}}_{1})\leq n-1
.

Next, suppose that we have a face or subset of a face
{\mathcal{F}}_{2}
for which the principal blow-up at each point is made up of two partial functions. Then for each
p\in{\mathcal{F}}_{2}
, we have that
\mathtt{BU}(p)
is 1-dimensional since we have a one-dimensional family of translates of the principal blow-up. It remains to be shown that such a face
{\mathcal{F}}_{2}
can have dimension no greater than
n-2
. We consider three cases for points
p=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in{\mathcal{F}}_{2}
.

•

Case 1:
Suppose that points
p\in{\mathcal{F}}_{2}
satisfy
\|\pi_{1}(p)\|_{V_{1}}=1
, that
\|\cdot\|_{V_{1}}
is polygonal, and that
\pi_{1}(p)
is a vertex of the polygonal norm. Since
\pi_{1}(p)
is a vertex, we have
\pi_{1}(p)=(a_{1},a_{2})
is fixed, meaning we have maximum
(n-2)
free parameters in
{\mathcal{F}}_{2}
.

•

Case 2:
Suppose that points
p\in{\mathcal{F}}_{2}
satisfy
\|\pi_{1}(p)\|_{V_{1}}=1
, and that either
\|\cdot\|_{V_{1}}
is smooth or that
\|\cdot\|_{V_{1}}
is polygonal and the points
\pi_{1}(p)
lie along an edge of the polygon. In this case, we have one free parameter in the first layer
V_{1}
, and the blow-up corresponding to the first layer is linear. Since the principal blow-up at
p
is made up of two partial functions, it must be the case that
\|\pi_{i}(p)\|_{V_{i}}=1
for another index
i\in\{2,\ldots,n-1\}
. Thus we have only
(n-2)
free parameters in
{\mathcal{F}}_{2}
.

•

Case 3:
Otherwise, the principal blow-up at
p\in{\mathcal{F}}_{2}
has two partial functions if
\|\pi_{i}(p)\|_{V_{i}}=1
for two different indices in
\{2,\ldots,n-1\}.
Thus two coordinates are fixed, and there are only
(n-2)
free parameters in
{\mathcal{F}}_{2}
.

Finally, suppose we have a face or subset of a face
{\mathcal{F}}_{3}
for which the principal blow-up at each point is made up of at least three partial functions. Then for each
p\in{\mathcal{F}}_{3}
, we have that
\mathtt{BU}(p)
is 2-dimensional since we have a two-dimensional family of translates of the principal blow-up. It remains to be shown that such a face
{\mathcal{F}}_{3}
can have dimension no greater than
n-3
. We similarly consider three cases for points
p=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in{\mathcal{F}}_{3}
.

•

Case 1:
Suppose that points
p\in{\mathcal{F}}_{3}
satisfy
\|\pi_{1}(p)\|_{V_{1}}=1
, that
\|\cdot\|_{V_{1}}
is polygonal, and that
\pi_{1}(p)
is a vertex of the polygonal norm. Then for the principal blow-up at
p
to have at least three partial functions, we need
\|\pi_{i}(p)\|_{V_{i}}=1
for at least one other index
i\in\{2,\ldots,n-1\}
, leaving us with no more than
(n-3)
free parameters.

•

Case 2:
Suppose that points
p\in{\mathcal{F}}_{3}
satisfy
\|\pi_{1}(p)\|_{V_{1}}=1
, and that either
\|\cdot\|_{V_{1}}
is smooth or that
\|\cdot\|_{V_{1}}
is polygonal and the points
\pi_{1}(p)
lie along an edge of the polygon. In this case, we have one free parameter in the first layer
V_{1}
, and the blow-up corresponding to the first layer is linear. Since the principal blow-up at
p
is made up of three partial functions, it must be the case that
\|\pi_{i}(p)\|_{V_{i}}=1
for at least two indices
i\in\{2,\ldots,n-1\}
. Thus we have no more than
(n-3)
free parameters in
{\mathcal{F}}_{3}
.

•

Case 3:
Otherwise the principal blow-up at
p\in{\mathcal{F}}_{3}
has at least partial functions if
\|\pi_{i}(p)\|_{V_{i}}=1
for at least three different indices
i\in\{2,\ldots,n\}.
Thus there are no more than
(n-3)
free parameters in
{\mathcal{F}}_{3}
.

∎

Lemma 5.8
.

For
n\geq 3
, the horofunction boundary of
L_{n}
equipped with a polysmooth layered sup norm has dimension less than or equal to
\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil+2
.

Proof.

To show that the horofunction boundary of
L_{n}
is has dimension less than or equal to
\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil+2
, it suffices to show that for any face
{\mathcal{F}}
of any dimension in the unit metric sphere of the polysmooth layered sup norm, the family of blow-ups
\mathtt{BU}({\mathcal{F}})
has dimension bounded by
\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil+2
.

Suppose that
{\mathcal{F}}
satisfies
\|\pi_{i}(p)\|_{V_{i}}=1
for all
i\in I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
, where the size of the index set
|I|
is
k>0
. Given that
\|\cdot\|_{V_{1}}
could be polygonal or smooth, as in the proof of Lemma
5.7
, there are a few cases to consider.

•

Case 1:
Suppose that points
p=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in{\mathcal{F}}
satisfy
\|\pi_{1}(p)\|_{V_{1}}=1
, that
\|\cdot\|_{V_{1}}
is polygonal, and that
\pi_{1}(p)
is a fixed vertex of the polygonal norm. Then since
|I|=k
, the points in
{\mathcal{F}}
have
k+1
fixed coordinates, meaning the face is
m=(n-k-1)
-dimensional. The principal blow-ups at these points will be made up of
\ell=k+1
partial functions.

•

Case 2:
Combining Cases 2 and 3 from the proof of the previous lemma, suppose that i)
I\subseteq\{2,\ldots,n-1\}
; or ii) points
p=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in{\mathcal{F}}
satsify
\|\pi_{1}(p)\|_{V_{1}}=1
, and that either
\|\cdot\|_{V_{1}}
is smooth or that
\|\cdot\|_{V_{1}}
is polygonal and the points
\pi_{1}(p)
lie along an edge of the polygon. In this case, the points in
{\mathcal{F}}
have
k
fixed coordinates, meaning the face is
m=(n-k)
-dimensional. The principal blow-ups at these points will be made up of
\ell=k
partial functions.

Regardless of which case we are in, we have
m+\ell=n
, where
m
is the number of free parameters in the face
{\mathcal{F}}
and
\ell
is the number of partial functions in the principal blow-up at a point
p\in{\mathcal{F}}
.
Let
{\mathcal{P}}
be list of Pansu derivatives which appear as partial functions of the principal blow-up at points
p\in{\mathcal{F}}
. The list
{\mathcal{P}}
will consist of the Pansu derivatives of the homogeneous coordinate functions in Table
2
for all
i\in I
,
i\geq 2
. If
1\in I
, then depending on
\|\cdot\|_{V_{1}}
, the list
{\mathcal{P}}
will contain one or two other linear functions in the variables
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
with coefficients which depend on
a_{1}
and
a_{2}

[
26
]
.
If we can define an injective map between the
m
free parameters and the set
G
of polynomial coefficients of
x_{1}
or
x_{2}
from the list
{\mathcal{P}}
(such that each free parameter
a_{i}
is sent to a coefficient it appears in nontrivially), then we will have an
m
-dimension family of principal blow-ups associated to
{\mathcal{F}}
.

We note, however, that the coefficients of
x_{2}
in the Pansu derivatives of the coordinate functions seen in Table
2
are always functions solely of
a_{1}
. This obstacle means no free parameter
a_{i}
,
i\neq 1
can be mapped to a coefficient of
x_{2}
. Hence the dimension of the family of principal blow-ups along the face
{\mathcal{F}}
(where each principal blow-up has
\ell
partial functions) is bounded above by
\ell+1
. To achieve this maximal dimension, we would need the number of free parameters
m
to be at least
\ell+1
, and so
(\ell+1)+\ell\leq m+\ell=n
.

Thus to get an
(\ell+1)
-dimensional family of principal blow-ups (before considering translates), we need
\ell\leq\frac{n-1}{2}
. As long as

, there will be a 2-dimensional family of translates for each principal blow-up, and hence the whole family of blow-ups
\mathtt{BU}({\mathcal{F}})
has dimension bounded by

(\ell+1)+2\leq\left\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\right\rfloor+3=\left\lceil\frac{n}{2}\right\rceil+2.\qed

This finding begs for further exploration. It would be interesting to understand and classify which Carnot groups
G
in general have horofunction boundaries of dimension
\dim G-1
. While higher Heisenberg groups of arbitrary dimension have follow our expectations, once we have higher nilpotency class, we see a change. Interestingly, the threshold between dimensions 7 and 8 which appears in the horofunction boundary of filiform Lie groups also appears in the classification of Carnot gradings on nilpotent Lie algebras
[

]
.

Question 5.10
.

For other infinite families of Carnot groups, is there a dimensional threshold at which the horofunction boundary ceases to be of the expected dimension? Is this threshold a function of the nilpotency class, the dimension of the first layer in the grading, and/or other properties of the group?

Introduction

Let
G
be a graph. The vertex and the edge set of
G

V(G)

k

V(H)
and
E(H)

v_{1},h_{1},v_{2},h_{2},\dots,v_{k},h_{k}
where
v_{i},v_{i+1}\in h_{i}
for each

and
v_{k}v_{1}\in h_{k}
. The hypergraph equivalent of
k

k
Berge triangles sharing a common edge. We say that this common edge is the base of the
k
-book.

The maximum number of edges in a triangle-free graph is one of the classical results in extremal graph theory and proved by Mantel in
1907

13

G

n

contains a triangle. Rademacher (unpublished, and simplified later by Erdős in
[
6

\left\lfloor{\frac{n}{2}}\right\rfloor
. Erdős conjectured in 1962
[
7
]
that the size of the largest book in

\frac{n}{6}

and this was proved soon after by Edwards (unpublished, see also Khadźiivanov and Nikiforov
[
11

Theorem 1

(Edwards
[
4

, Khadźiivanov and Nikiforov
[

]
)
.

Every

\frac{n^{2}}{4}

\frac{n}{6}

Both Rademacher’s and Edwards’ results are sharp. In the former, the addition of an edge to one part in the complete balanced bipartite graph (note that in
G

\left\lfloor{\frac{n}{2}}\right\rfloor
triangles) achieves the maximum. In the latter, every known extremal construction of
G
has
\Omega(n^{3})
triangles. For more details on book-free graphs we refer the reader to the following articles
[
2
]

[
14
]
and
[

]

Given a family of hypergraphs

Proof of Theorem
4

Fix
k\geq 2
,
\epsilon>0
and set

n_{1}(k,\epsilon)=\max\left\{18k+12,n_{0}\left(\frac{\epsilon}{6k^{2}-8k}\right)\right\}

where
n_{0}(.)
is from Theorem
3
. Suppose that
n>n_{1}(k,\epsilon)
.
Let
H
be a
B_{k}
-free
3
-uniform hypergraph on
n
vertices. We are interested in the
2
-shadow, i.e., let
G
be a graph with vertex set
V(H)
and

E(G)=\{ab\mid\{a,b\}\subset e\in E(H)\}.

If an edge in
G
lies in more than one hyperedge in
H
, we color it blue. Otherwise, we color it red.
We define hypergraphs
H_{r}
and
H_{b}
in the following way.
V(H_{r})=V(H_{b})=V(H)
,

E(H_{r})=\{e\in E(H)\mid e\textrm{ contains two or three red edges of }G\}

and
E(H_{b})=E(H)\setminus E(H_{r})
. Note that each hyperedge in
H_{b}
contains two or three blue edges of
G
.

Claim 5
.

The number of hyperedges in
H_{r}
is at most
\frac{n^{2}}{8}
.

Proof.

Denote the subgraph of
G
formed by the red colored edges by
G_{r}
. Suppose that
|E(G_{r})|\geq\frac{n^{2}}{4}+1
. By Theorem
1
we have a book of size
\frac{n}{6}
in
G_{r}
. Denote the vertices of the
\frac{n}{6}
-book in
G_{r}
with
u,v
and
x_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq\frac{n}{6}
respectively where
uv
is the base of the book. Denote the third vertex of the hyperedge containing edge
uv
by
w
, set
X=\{x_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq\frac{n}{6}\}
and for each
x_{i}\in X
denote the hyperedge containing
ux_{i}
and
vx_{i}
by
ux_{i}y_{i}
and
vx_{i}z_{i}
respectively.

Set
E^{\prime}:=\emptyset
and
X^{\prime}:=\emptyset
.
Go through the vertices of
X
and perform the following procedure for each of them. At the beginning of the process no vertex is marked.

If the current vertex
x_{i}=w
then mark it.

If
x_{i}
is unmarked then

•

add
x_{i}
to
X^{\prime}
and hyperedges
ux_{i}y_{i}
and
vx_{i}z_{i}
to
E^{\prime}
,

•

if there exists
j>i
such that
y_{i}=x_{j}
then mark
x_{j}
,

•

if there exists
\ell>i
such that
z_{i}=x_{\ell}
then mark
x_{\ell}
.

By definition of red edges and the procedure (i.e. it adds two new hyperedges to
E^{\prime}
forming a Berge triangle with
uvw
at each step handling an unmarked vertex but at most one: when
x_{i}=w
) the set of hyperedges
E^{\prime}\cup\{uvw\}
with vertex set
X^{\prime}\cup\{u,v\}
form a
k^{\prime}
-book with base
uvw
, where
k^{\prime}=|X^{\prime}|
.
Moreover at each step of the procedure whenever an unmarked vertex was added to
X^{\prime}
then at most two more vertices became marked. Each unmarked vertex are in
X^{\prime}
at the end of the procedure, therefore

k^{\prime}\geq\frac{|X\setminus\{w\}|}{3}\geq\frac{n/6-1}{3}

at the end of the procedure and it is at least
k
by the definition of
n_{1}(k,\epsilon)
, but this is a contradiction.

Hence
|E(G_{r})|\leq\frac{n^{2}}{4}
and

|E(H_{r})|\leq\frac{|E(G_{r})|}{2}\leq\frac{n^{2}}{8}

by the definition of red colored edges.
∎

Now let us work on the sub-hypergraph

Claim 6
.

Each pair of vertices is contained in at most

hyperedges of

.

Proof.

Suppose that

is a pair of vertices which is contained in

. Observe that for each

and

is colored blue.

Set
E^{\prime}:=\emptyset
and
X^{\prime}:=\emptyset
. Go through the vertices of
X
and perform the following procedure for each of them. At the beginning of the process no vertex is marked.

If the current vertex
x_{i}=x_{2k-1}
and there is no marked vertex in
X
then do nothing.

Otherwise if
x_{i}
is unmarked then

•

add
x_{i}
to

and add
ux_{i}v
to

,

•

if

is colored blue denote a hyperedge containing it by

ux_{i}y_{i}

,

•

otherwise

is colored blue, so denote a hyperedge containing it by

•

If at the end of the procedure there is no marked vertex in

We now give an upper bound on the number of hyperedges in
H_{b}
.

Claim 7
.

The number of hyperedges in
H_{b}
is at most
\epsilon n^{2}
.

Proof.

Take a hyperedge
xyz
in the sub-hypergraph
H_{b}
. By Claim
6
there are at most
2k-2
hyperedges of
H_{b}
containing each of the pairs of vertices
xy
,
yz
, and
xz
.
If we deleted all such hyperedges barring
xyz
we would delete at most
6k-9
hyperedges. Therefore there is a linear 3-uniform subhypergraph
H^{\prime}_{b}
of
H_{b}
with
V(H_{b}^{\prime})=V(H_{b})=V(H)
and

|E(H_{b}^{\prime})|\geq\frac{|E(H_{b})|}{6k-8}

(i.e. a greedy algorithm can find an appropriate
H_{b}^{\prime}
).

Consider a hyperedge
e
in
H_{b}^{\prime}
. Observe that
H_{b}^{\prime}
is a
B_{k}
-free hypergraph, since it is a subhypergraph of
H
,
therefore the number of Berge triangles sitting on the edge

is at most
k-1
.
Apply the following greedy procedure until all the hyperedges are marked. In a step pick an unmarked hyperedge, mark it and delete an unmarked hyperedge of each Berge triangle containing the current hyperedge. Observe that this marked edge is not an edge of a triangle anymore.
Define
H_{b}^{\prime\prime}
the following way. Let
V(H_{b}^{\prime\prime})=V(H_{b}^{\prime})=V(H)
and
E(H_{b}^{\prime\prime})
contains the remaining hyperedges of
H_{b}^{\prime}
. Observe that at most
k-1
edges were deleted in each step and marked edges were never deleted. Therefore

|E(H_{b}^{\prime\prime})|\geq\frac{|E(H_{b}^{\prime})|}{k}.

Moreover
H_{b}^{\prime\prime}
is a Berge-triangle-free 3-uniform linear hypergraph therefore Theorem
3
can be applied with
\epsilon^{\prime}=\frac{\epsilon}{6k^{2}-8k}
. We get that

\frac{|E(H_{b})|}{6k^{2}-8k}\leq|E(H_{b}^{\prime\prime})|\leq\frac{\epsilon n^{2}}{6k^{2}-8k}.

∎

Proof of Theorem

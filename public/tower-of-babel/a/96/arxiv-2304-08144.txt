C 1 , α C^{1,\alpha} -regularity for functions in solution classes and its application to parabolic normalized p p -Laplace equations Thanks: Se-Chan Lee is supported by Basic Science Research Program through the National Research Foundation of Korea (NRF) funded by the Ministry of Education (2022R1A6A3A01086546).
Se-Chan Lee, Hyungsung Yun

Abstract
. We establish the global C 1 , α C^{1,\alpha} -regularity for functions in solution classes, whenever ellipticity constants are sufficiently close. As an application, we derive the global regularity result concerning the parabolic normalized p p -Laplace equations, provided that p p is close to 2. Our analysis relies on the compactness argument with the iteration procedure.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we are concerned with regularity results for functions
u\in C(Q_{1})
satisfying the following inequalities in the viscosity sense:

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} u_{t}-\mathcal{M}^{-}_{\lambda,\Lambda}(D^{2}u)&\geq-\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}\\
u_{t}-\mathcal{M}^{+}_{\lambda,\Lambda}(D^{2}u)&\leq\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}\end{aligned}\right.\quad\text{in $Q_{1}$},

where
f\in C(Q_{1})\cap L^{\infty}(Q_{1})
and
\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{\pm}
are the Pucci’s extremal operators. Here we define
Pucci’s extremal operators
as follows: for ellipticity constants
0<\lambda\leq\Lambda
and
\mathcal{S}^{n}\coloneqq\{M:\text{$M$ is a $n\times n$ real symmetric matrix\lx@text@rbrace}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{+}(M)\coloneqq\sup_{\lambda I\leq A\leq\Lambda I}\text{tr}(AM)\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{M}^{-}_{\lambda,\Lambda}(M)\coloneqq\inf_{\lambda I\leq A\leq\Lambda I}\text{tr}(AM).

To be precise, we define the
extended solution class

S^{\ast}(\lambda,\Lambda,f)
by the space of functions
u\in C(Q_{1})
satisfying (
1.1
) in the viscosity sense. In a similar way, we define the
solution class

S(\lambda,\Lambda,f)
by the space of functions
u\in C(Q_{1})
satisfying

(1.2)

u_{t}-\mathcal{M}^{+}_{\lambda,\Lambda}(D^{2}u)\leq f\leq u_{t}-\mathcal{M}^{-}_{\lambda,\Lambda}(D^{2}u)\quad\text{in $Q_{1}$}

in the viscosity sense; see
Section

2
for details. We remark that if
u\in C(Q_{1})
is a viscosity solution of

(1.3)

u_{t}-F(D^{2}u,x,t)=f\quad\text{in }Q_{1}

for some
(\lambda,\Lambda)
-elliptic fully nonlinear operator
F
, then we have

u\in S(\lambda,\Lambda,f(x,t)+F(0,x,t))
. On the other hand, there exists a function
u\in C(Q_{1})
which belongs to the solution class
S(\lambda,\Lambda,0)
, but cannot be a viscosity solution of

\displaystyle u_{t}-F(D^{2}u)=0\quad\text{in $Q_{1}$}

for any
(\lambda,\Lambda)
-elliptic operators
F
. See
[
32
, Section 1.7.2]
for the precise construction of such an example.

Before we state our main theorem, we summarize several regularity results concerning the functions in the solution classes for both elliptic and parabolic settings:

(i)

(Interior
W^{2,\delta}
-estimate,
[
9
,
10
,
25
,
35
]
) If
u\in S(\lambda,\Lambda,f)
in
Q_{1}
, then there exists a universal
\delta>0
such that
u\in W^{2,\delta}(Q_{1/2})
with the uniform estimate

\displaystyle\|u\|_{W^{2,\delta}(Q_{1/2})}\leq C\left(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}+\|f\|_{L^{n+1}(Q_{1})}\right).

(ii)

(Interior
C^{\alpha}
-estimate,
[
10
,
18
,
19
,
35
]
) If
u\in S(\lambda,\Lambda,f)
in
Q_{1}
, then
u
is locally Hölder continuous in
Q_{1}
with the uniform estimate

\displaystyle\|u\|_{C^{\alpha}(\overline{Q_{1/2}})}\leq C\left(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}+\|f\|_{L^{n+1}(Q_{1})}\right),

where
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
C>0
are universal constants which depend only on
n
,
\lambda
, and
\Lambda
. The interior Hölder regularity result essentially comes from Harnack inequality.

(iii)

(Counterexamples,
[
27
,
28
,
29
]
)
We consider the elliptic situation with
n\geq 5
, and define the elliptic solution class
S_{\textnormal{ell}}(\lambda,\Lambda,0)
by the space of functions
u\in C(B_{1})
satisfying

\mathcal{M}^{-}_{\lambda,\Lambda}(D^{2}u)\leq 0\leq\mathcal{M}^{+}_{\lambda,\Lambda}(D^{2}u)\quad\text{in $B_{1}$}

in the viscosity sense. For any
\alpha\in(0,1)
, there exist some
(\lambda,\Lambda)
-elliptic fully nonlinear operator
F
and a viscosity solution
u
of
F(D^{2}u)=0
such that
u
does not belong to
C^{1,\alpha}(B_{1})
. Since the difference quotient
(u(x+he)-u(x))/h
for
h>0
and
e\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
|e|=1
is contained in
S_{\textnormal{ell}}(\lambda,\Lambda,0)
, we conclude that functions in
S_{\textnormal{ell}}(\lambda,\Lambda,0)
are in general only Hölder continuous, and this interior Hölder regularity cannot be improved, at least for
n\geq 5
.

(iv)

(Boundary
C^{1,\alpha}
-estimate,
[
1
,
22
,
33
,
36
]
) Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain with
(0,0)\in\partial_{p}\Omega
, and
u\in S(\lambda,\Lambda,f)
in
\Omega\cap Q_{1}
with
u=g
on
\partial_{p}\Omega\cap Q_{1}
. We also let
\alpha\in(0,\overline{\alpha})
for a universal constant
\overline{\alpha}\in(0,1)
. If
g\in C^{1,\alpha}(0,0)
and
\partial_{p}\Omega\cap Q_{1}\in C^{1,\alpha}(0,0)
, then
u\in C^{1,\alpha}(0,0)
with the pointwise estimate

|u(x,t)-L(x)|\leq C(|x|+\sqrt{|t|})^{1+\alpha}\quad\text{for all }(x,t)\in\Omega\cap Q_{1}.

The universal constant
\overline{\alpha}
is strongly related to the boundary regularity of solutions to the particular model problem, which will be specified soon.

Our main goal in this paper is to establish the global
C^{1,\alpha}
-estimate for viscosity solutions
u
of (
1.1
), provided that the ellipticity constants
\lambda
and
\Lambda
are close enough, i.e.,
\Lambda/\lambda-1\ll 1
. Our first main theorem is concerned with the interior Hölder regularity for the gradient.

Theorem 1.1

(Interior
C^{1,\alpha}
-estimate)
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
u
satisfy (
1.1
) with
f\in C(Q_{1})\cap L^{\infty}(Q_{1})
. Then there exists
\delta>0
depending only on
n
and
\alpha
such that if
\Lambda\leq(1+\delta)\lambda
, then
u\in C_{\textnormal{loc}}^{1,\alpha}(Q_{1})
such that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{Q_{1/2}})}\leq C\left(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}\right),

where
C>0
depends only on
n
,
\lambda
, and
\alpha
.

Note that, in view of (ii) and (iii) above, we improved the interior regularity for functions in
S^{\ast}(\lambda,\Lambda,f)
under the appropriate assumptions on the ellipticity constants. Furthermore, our regularity result for solution classes is optimal in the following sense:

For
\delta>0
, let
\lambda=1
,
\Lambda=1+\delta
, and
f\equiv-2
. Then the function

u(x,t)=\begin{cases}x_{n}^{2}&(x_{n}<0)\\
(1+\delta)^{-1}x_{n}^{2}&(x_{n}\geq 0)\end{cases}

belongs to
S(\lambda,\Lambda,f)
, but is not twice differentiable at
\{(x,t):x_{n}=0\}
. Hence, we cannot expect that, in general, the function in the solution classes
S(\lambda,\Lambda,f)
or
S^{\ast}(\lambda,\Lambda,f)
to be more regular than
C^{1,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(Q_{1})
, even though we further assume that
\Lambda
is close enough to
\lambda
. In other words,
Theorem

1.1
is the sharp regularity result that can be obtained for functions in the solution classes
S(\lambda,\Lambda,f)
or
S^{\ast}(\lambda,\Lambda,f)
, when
\Lambda
is close to
\lambda
.

We next state the boundary regularity result for functions in solution classes.

Theorem 1.2

(Boundary
C^{1,\alpha}
-estimate)
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
f\in C(Q_{1})\cap L^{\infty}(Q_{1})
, and
g\in C^{1,\alpha}(x_{0},t_{0})
for some
(x_{0},t_{0})\in\partial_{p}Q_{1}
. Assume that
u
satisfy

(1.4)

\left\{\begin{aligned} u&\in S^{*}(\lambda,\Lambda,f)&&\text{in }Q_{1}\cap Q_{1}(x_{0},t_{0})\\
u&=g&&\text{on }\partial_{p}Q_{1}\cap Q_{1}(x_{0},t_{0}).\end{aligned}\right.

Then there exists
\delta>0
depending only on
n
and
\alpha
such that if
\Lambda\leq(1+\delta)\lambda
, then
u\in C^{1,\alpha}(x_{0},t_{0})
, i.e., there exists a linear function
L
such that

\displaystyle|u(x,t)-L(x)|\leq C(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}+\|g\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(x_{0},t_{0})})(|x-x_{0}|+\sqrt{|t-t_{0}|})^{1+\alpha}

for all
(x,t)\in Q_{1}\cap Q_{1}(x_{0},t_{0})
and

|Du(0)|\leq C(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}+\|g\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(x_{0},t_{0})}),

where
C>0
depends only on
n
,
\lambda
, and
\alpha
.

We would like to illustrate our pointwise boundary estimate in view of the preceding literature such as
[
1
,
3
,
17
,
21
,
22
,
23
,
33
,
36
]
. To be precise, we first consider the simple ‘model’ problem: let
u
belong to
S(\lambda,\Lambda,0)
in
Q_{1}\cap\{x_{n}>0\}
with a zero boundary condition on a flat boundary
Q_{1}\cap\{x_{n}=0\}
. An application of Harnack inequality, Lipschitz estimate, and Hopf principle yields that there exists a universal constant
\overline{\alpha}\in(0,1)
depending only on
n
,
\lambda
, and
\Lambda
such that
u
is
C^{1,\overline{\alpha}}
at boundary points under some structural conditions. We remark that it is difficult to determine the precise value of
\overline{\alpha}
due to the implicit construction of barrier functions in this step. Then by the compactness argument, the pointwise boundary
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity result for the general case can be achieved whenever
\alpha\in(0,\overline{\alpha})
. In this context, our result can be interpreted as follows: the universal constant
\overline{\alpha}
converges to
1
when
\Lambda/\lambda-1
goes to
0
.

We shortly display our strategy to prove
Theorem

1.1
and
Theorem

1.2
. Indeed, the proofs of two theorems are based on the compactness argument together with the iteration method; see
[
15
,
34
,
36
,
37
]
for example. For the interior case, if
\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}
is small enough and
\lambda
,
\Lambda
are close enough, then we can expect the relation “
S^{\ast}(\lambda,\Lambda,f)\approx S^{\ast}(\lambda,\lambda,0)
”. Since
u\in S^{\ast}(\lambda,\lambda,0)
if and only if
u_{t}-\lambda\Delta u=0
in
Q_{1}
, we capture the smooth property of the limit function, and then show the approximation lemma which plays a crucial role in the iteration scheme. For the boundary case, we further take the
C^{1,\alpha}
-norm of
g
and
\partial_{p}\Omega
sufficiently small to arrive at the simple model problem.

We now move our attention to several applications of
Theorem

1.1
and
Theorem

1.2
. We begin with the global estimates for functions in the solution class. In short, this global result can be derived from
Theorem

1.1
and
Theorem

1.2
together with the machinery developed in

Theorem 1.3

(Global
C^{1,\alpha}
-estimate)
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
f\in C(Q_{1})\cap L^{\infty}(Q_{1})
, and
g\in C^{1,\alpha}(\partial_{p}Q_{1})
. Assume that
u\in C(\overline{Q_{1}})
satisfy

(1.5)

\left\{\begin{aligned} u&\in S^{*}(\lambda,\Lambda,f)&&\text{in }Q_{1}\\
u&=g&&\text{on }\partial_{p}Q_{1}.\end{aligned}\right.

Then there exists
\delta>0
depending only on
n
and
\alpha
such that if
\Lambda\leq(1+\delta)\lambda
, then
u\in C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{Q_{1}})
with the uniform estimate

\displaystyle\|u\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{Q_{1}})}\leq C(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}+\|g\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(\partial_{p}Q_{1})}),

where
C>0
is a constant depending only on
n
,
\lambda
, and
\alpha
.

Finally, such global
C^{1,\alpha}
-estimate for functions in solution classes can be applied to describe the regular property for viscosity solutions of the parabolic normalized
p
-Laplace equations given by

(1.6)

\displaystyle u_{t}=\Delta_{p}^{N}u\coloneqq|Du|^{2-p}\,\text{div}(|Du|^{p-2}Du),

where
1<p<\infty
. The equation (
1.6
) arises from tug-of-war-like stochastic games with noise; see
[

,
26
,
30
,
31
]
and references therein. The existence, uniqueness, and regularity for viscosity solutions of (
1.6
) has been widely studied in both elliptic and parabolic settings recently; we refer to
[

,
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
11
,
13
,
14
,
16
]
. In particular, Jin and Silvestre
[

]
developed the interior

-regularity of

for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
and for any
p\in(1,\infty)
. On the other hand, Andrade and Santos proved
[
2
]
the interior
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity of
u
for given
\alpha\in(0,1)
and for
p
close to 2; i.e.,
|p-2|<\varepsilon=\varepsilon(n,\alpha)
. It is noteworthy that
Theorem

1.1
together with the regularization scheme provides a new proof for
[

; see

An application of

1.3
shows the global

\alpha\in(0,1)
when
p
is close to 2.

Theorem 1.4

(Parabolic normalized
p
-Laplace equations)
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
f\in C(Q_{1})\cap L^{\infty}(Q_{1})
, and
g\in C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{Q_{1}})
. Assume that
u\in C(\overline{Q_{1})}
is a viscosity solution of

\left\{\begin{aligned} u_{t}-\Delta_{p}^{N}u&=f&&\text{in }Q_{1}\\
u&=g&&\text{on }\partial_{p}Q_{1}.\end{aligned}\right.

Then there exists
\delta>0
depending only on
n
and
\alpha
such that if
|p-2|<\delta
, then
u\in C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{Q_{1}})
with the uniform estimate

\displaystyle\|u\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{Q_{1}})}\leq C(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}+\|g\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{Q_{1}})}),

where
C>0
is a constant depending only on
n
,
p
, and
\alpha
.

When we investigate the parabolic normalized
p
-Laplace equations (
1.6
), the essential challenge arises from the fact that the coefficient matrix strongly depends on the gradient of viscosity solutions
u
. In particular, if we aim at the
C^{1,\alpha}
-estimate by finding a linear function
L
which approximates
u
, then the key step is to iterate a similar argument for the difference function
u-L
. However, the quasilinear structure of (
1.6
) leads to the translation of the original equation. We overcome such difficulty by understanding
u
as functions in the solution class
S^{\ast}(\lambda,\Lambda,f)
which is invariant under the replacement of
u
by
u-L
. We also expect that this strategy can be widely employed to remove the dependence of
Du
in certain equations, whenever the a priori Lipschitz estimate for
u
is known.

It is noteworthy that our theorems
Theorem

1.1
-
Theorem

1.4
still hold for the elliptic setting, after the essentially same analysis. Moreover, our approach for parabolic normalized
p
-Laplace equations is flexible enough so that it is also available for more general situations. For example, the global
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity result is still valid for parabolic normalized
p(x,t)
-Laplace equations whenever
(x,t)\mapsto p(x,t)
is Lipschitz and
|p(x,t)-2|
is sufficiently small. We note that the proof is similar to the one for
Theorem

1.4
with small modifications; see
Section

4
for details. For related results on parabolic
\Delta_{p(x,t)}^{N}
operators, we refer to the recent paper
[
13
]
, where they showed the interior
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity for small
\alpha\in(0,1)
provided that
p(x,t)
belongs to
C^{1}
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we summarize some basic notations and gather definitions used throughout the paper.

For a point
(x_{0},t_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and
r>0
, we denote the cylinder as

\displaystyle Q_{r}(x_{0},t_{0})=B_{r}(x_{0})\times(t_{0}-r^{2},t_{0}]\quad\text{and}\quad Q_{r}^{+}(x_{0},t_{0})=\{(x,t)\in Q_{r}(x_{0},t_{0}):x_{n}>0\}.

Moreover, we define the parabolic boundary as

\displaystyle\partial_{p}Q_{r}(x_{0},t_{0})

\displaystyle=(\overline{B_{r}(x_{0})}\times\{t=t_{0}-r^{2}\})\cup(\partial B_{r}(x_{0})\times[t_{0}-r^{2},t_{0})).

For convenience, we denote
Q_{r}=Q_{r}(0,0)
and
Q_{r}^{+}=Q_{r}^{+}(0,0)
.

We denote partial derivatives of
u
as subscriptions.

u_{t}=\partial_{t}u=\frac{\partial u}{\partial t},\quad D_{i}u=\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}},\quad\text{and}\quad D_{ij}u=\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}.

2.1.
Hölder spaces

Definition 2.1

(Hölder spaces)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be an open set and
\alpha\in(0,1)
.

(i)

u\in C^{\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
means that there exists
C>0
such that

|u(x,t)-u(y,s)|\leq C(|x-y|+\sqrt{|t-s|})^{\alpha}\quad\text{for all }(x,t),(y,s)\in\Omega.

In other words,
u
is
\frac{\alpha}{2}
-Hölder continuous in
t
and
\alpha
-Hölder continuous in
x
.

(ii)

u\in C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
means that
u
is
\frac{\alpha+1}{2}
-Hölder continuous in
t
and
Du
is
\alpha
-Hölder continuous in
x
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a bounded set and
f:A\to\mathbb{R}
be a function. We say that
f
is
C^{k,\alpha}
at
(x_{0},t_{0})\in A
(denoted by
f\in C^{k,\alpha}(x_{0},t_{0})
), if there exist constant
C>0
,
r>0
, and a polynomial
P(x,t)
with
\deg P\leq k
such that

(2.1)

|f(x,t)-P(x,t)|\leq C(|x-x_{0}|+\sqrt{|t-t_{0}|})^{k+\alpha}\quad\text{for all }(x,t)\in A\cap Q_{r}(x_{0},t_{0}).

We define

\displaystyle[f]_{C^{k,\alpha}(x_{0},t_{0})}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\inf\{C>0:\eqref{cka_f}\text{ holds with }P(x,t)\text{ and }A\},

\displaystyle\|f\|_{C^{k,\alpha}(x_{0},t_{0})}

\displaystyle\coloneqq[f]_{C^{k,\alpha}(x_{0},t_{0})}+\sum_{i=0}^{k}\|D^{i}P(0,0)\|.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain. We say that
\partial_{p}\Omega
is
C^{k,\alpha}
at
(x_{0},t_{0})\in\partial_{p}\Omega
(denoted by
\partial_{p}\Omega\in C^{k,\alpha}(x_{0},t_{0})
), if there exist constant
C>0
,
r>0
, a new coordinate system
\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{n},t\}
, and a polynomial
P(x^{\prime},t)
with
\deg P\leq k
,
P(0,0)=0
,
DP(0,0)=0
such that
(x_{0},t_{0})=(0,0)
in this coordinate system,

(2.2)

\displaystyle Q_{\rho}\cap\{(x^{\prime},x_{n},t):x_{n}>P(x^{\prime},t)+C(|x^{\prime}|+\sqrt{|t|})^{k+\alpha}\}\subset Q_{r}\cap\Omega,

\displaystyle Q_{\rho}\cap\{(x^{\prime},x_{n},t):x_{n}<P(x^{\prime},t)-C(|x^{\prime}|+\sqrt{|t|})^{k+\alpha}\}\subset Q_{r}\cap\Omega^{c}.

We define

\displaystyle[\partial_{p}\Omega]_{C^{k,\alpha}(x_{0},t_{0})}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\inf\{C>0:\eqref{cka_dom}\text{ holds with }P(x^{\prime},t)\text{ and }C\},

\displaystyle\|\partial_{p}\Omega\|_{C^{k,\alpha}(x_{0},t_{0})}

\displaystyle\coloneqq[\partial_{p}\Omega]_{C^{k,\alpha}(x_{0},t_{0})}+\sum_{i=2}^{k}\|D^{i}P(0^{\prime},0)\|.

2.2.
Viscosity solutions and solution classes

Definition 2.4

(Test functions)
.

Let
u
be a continuous function in
Q_{1}
. The function
\varphi:Q_{1}\to\mathbb{R}
is called
test function
if it is
C^{1}
with respect to
t
and
C^{2}
with respect to
x
.

(i)

We say that the test function
\varphi
touches
u
from above at
(x,t)
if there exists an open neighborhood
U
of
(x,t)
such that

u\leq\varphi\quad\mbox{in }U\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad u(x,t)=\varphi(x,t).

(ii)

We say that the test function
\varphi
touches
u
from below at
(x,t)
if there exists an open neighborhood
U
of
(x,t)
such that

u\geq\varphi\quad\mbox{in }U\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad u(x,t)=\varphi(x,t).

Definition 2.5

(Viscosity solutions)
.

Let
u
be a function defined in
Q_{1}
.

(i)

Let
u
be a upper semicontinuous function in
Q_{1}
.
u
is called a
viscosity subsoution
of (
2.3
) in
Q_{1}
when the following condition holds: if for any
(x,t)\in Q_{1}
and any test function
\varphi
touching
u
from above at
(x,t)
, then

\varphi_{t}(x,t)-F(D^{2}\varphi(x,t),x,t)\leq f(x,t).

(ii)

Let
u
be a lower semicontinuous function in
Q_{1}
.
u
is called a
viscosity supersoution
of (
2.3
) in
Q_{1}
when the following condition holds: if for any
(x,t)\in Q_{1}
and any test function
\varphi
touching
u
from below at
(x,t)
, then

\varphi_{t}(x,t)-F(D^{2}\varphi(x,t),x,t)\geq f(x,t).

Definition 2.6

(Solution classes)
.

We define several solutions classes as follows:

\displaystyle\underline{S}(\lambda,\Lambda,f)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{u\mid u_{t}-\mathcal{M}^{+}_{\lambda,\Lambda}(D^{2}u)\leq f\ \text{in the viscosity sense}\},

\displaystyle\overline{S}(\lambda,\Lambda,f)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{u\mid u_{t}-\mathcal{M}^{-}_{\lambda,\Lambda}(D^{2}u)\geq f\ \text{in the viscosity sense}\},

\displaystyle S(\lambda,\Lambda,f)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\underline{S}(\lambda,\Lambda,f)\cap\overline{S}(\lambda,\Lambda,f),

\displaystyle S^{*}(\lambda,\Lambda,f)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\underline{S}(\lambda,\Lambda,\|f\|_{L^{\infty}})\cap\overline{S}(\lambda,\Lambda,-\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}).

In particular,
S(\lambda,\Lambda,f)
is contained in
S^{\ast}(\lambda,\Lambda,f)
.

We next introduce the definition of viscosity solutions of parabolic normalized
p
-Laplace equations

(2.3)

u_{t}-\Delta_{p}^{N}u=f\quad\text{in $Q_{1}$},

where

. We observe that

\displaystyle\Delta_{p}^{N}u

\displaystyle=|Du|^{2-p}\,\mathrm{div}(|Du|^{p-2}Du)=(\delta_{ij}+(p-2)|Du|^{-2}u_{i}u_{j})u_{ij}\eqqcolon a_{ij}(Du)u_{ij}

and

\displaystyle\min\{p-1,1\}I\leq\delta_{ij}+(p-2)q_{i}q_{j}|q|^{-2}\leq\max\{p-1,1\}I\quad\text{for all $q\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}$.}

Note that (
2.3
) is undeﬁned when

, where it has bounded discontinuous coefficients

. This can be resolved by adapting the notion of viscosity solution using the upper and lower semicontinuous envelopes of the normalized
p
-Laplacian.

We denote the maximum and the minimum eigenvalues associated with

as follows:

e_{\textnormal{min}}(M)\coloneqq\min_{|e|=1}(Me\cdot e)\quad\text{and}\quad e_{\textnormal{max}}(M)\coloneqq\max_{|e|=1}(Me\cdot e).

3.

3.1.
Interior
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity

We begin with the following approximation lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any
\alpha\in(0,1)
, there exists
\delta>0
depending only on
n
and
\alpha
such that if
u
satisfies (
1.1
) with

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}\leq 1,\quad\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}\leq\delta,\quad\text{and}\quad\Lambda\leq(1+\delta)\lambda,

then there exists a linear function
L
such that

\displaystyle\|u-L\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{\eta})}\leq\eta^{1+\alpha}

and

\displaystyle|L(0,0)|+|DL|\leq C_{1},

where
C_{1}>1
depends only on
n
and
\lambda
, and
\eta\in(0,1)
depends only on
n
,
\lambda
, and
\alpha
.

Proof.

We prove by contradiction; we suppose the conclusion does not hold. In other words, there exist sequences
u_{k}\in C(Q_{1})
,
f_{k}\in C(Q_{1})\cap L^{\infty}(Q_{1})
, and
\Lambda_{k}\geq\lambda
such that
u_{k}
satisfies

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}u_{k}-\mathcal{M}^{-}_{\lambda,\Lambda_{k}}(D^{2}u_{k})&\geq-\|f_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}\\
\partial_{t}u_{k}-\mathcal{M}^{+}_{\lambda,\Lambda_{k}}(D^{2}u_{k})&\leq\|f_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}\end{aligned}\right.\quad\text{in $Q_{1}$},

\displaystyle\|u_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}\leq 1,\quad\|f_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}\leq 1/k,\quad\text{and}\quad\Lambda_{k}\leq(1+1/k)\lambda.

Moreover, for any linear function
L
satisfying
|L(0,0)|+|DL|\leq C_{1}
, we have

(3.1)

\displaystyle\|u_{k}-L\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{\eta})}>\eta^{1+\alpha},

where
C_{1}>0
and
\eta\in(0,1)
will be determined later.

We first note that
\{u_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{\infty}(Q_{1})
and by the interior Hölder estimate (
[
35
, Theorem 4.19]
),
\{u_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
is equicontinuous in compact sets of
Q_{1}
. Thus, by Arzela-Ascoli theorem, there exist a subsequence
\{u_{k_{j}}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
of
\{u_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
and a limit function
\overline{u}\in C(Q_{1})
such that
u_{k_{j}}\to\overline{u}
uniformly in compact sets of
Q_{1}
as
j\to\infty
. Moreover, since

\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda_{k_{j}}}^{\pm}(M)\to\lambda\,\mathrm{tr}(M)\text{ uniformly in compact sets of }\mathcal{S}^{n}\text{ as }j\to\infty

and
\|f_{k_{j}}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}\to 0
as
j\to\infty
, the stability theorem yields that

\displaystyle\overline{u}_{t}-\lambda\Delta\overline{u}=0\quad\text{in $Q_{1}$}.

We now choose a linear function
\overline{L}
by

\displaystyle\overline{L}(x)\coloneqq\overline{u}(0,0)+D\overline{u}(0,0)\cdot x.

Then, by the interior estimates for the uniformly parabolic equations, there exists
C>0
that depends only on
n
and
\lambda
such that

\displaystyle|\overline{u}(x,t)-\overline{L}(x)|\leq C(|x|+\sqrt{|t|})^{2}\quad\text{for any $(x,t)\in Q_{1/2}$}

and
|\overline{u}(0,0)|+|D\overline{u}(0,0)|\leq C
.
We take
C_{1}\coloneqq 1+C
and
\eta
small enough so that
8C\eta^{1-\alpha}<1/2
. Then we observe that

\displaystyle\|\overline{u}-\overline{L}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{\eta})}\leq 8C\eta^{2}<\eta^{1+\alpha}/2.

On the other hand, by letting
k_{j}\to\infty
in (
3.1
), we have

\displaystyle\|\overline{u}-\overline{L}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{\eta})}\geq\eta^{1+\alpha},

which leads to the contradiction.
∎

We would like to iterate the result in
Lemma

3.1
to find a sequence of linear functions
\{L_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
close to
u
in a discrete way.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\delta>0
be as in
Lemma

3.1
, and
u
satisfy (
1.1
). Suppose that

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}\leq 1,\quad\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}\leq\delta,\quad\text{and}\quad\Lambda\leq(1+\delta)\lambda.

Then there exists a sequence of linear functions

\displaystyle L_{k}(x)=a_{k}+b_{k}\cdot x,\quad k\geq-1

such that for all
k\geq 0
, we have

(3.2)

\displaystyle\|u-L_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{\eta^{k}})}\leq\eta^{k(1+\alpha)},\

and

(3.3)

\displaystyle|a_{k}-a_{k-1}|+\eta^{k}|b_{k}-b_{k-1}|\leq 2C_{1}\eta^{(k-1)(1+\alpha)},

where
C_{1}>1
and
\eta\in(0,1)
are determined in
Lemma

3.1
.

Proof.

We argue by induction. For
k=0
, by setting
L_{-1}=L_{0}=0
, the conditions immediately hold. Suppose that the conclusion holds for
k\geq 0
. We claim that the conclusion also holds for
k+1
.

For this purpose, let
r=\eta^{k}
,
y=x/r
,
s=t/r^{2}
and

\displaystyle v(y,s)\coloneqq\frac{u(x,t)-L_{k}(x)}{r^{1+\alpha}}.

Then we observe that for

,

\displaystyle\partial_{s}v(y,s)-\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{+}(D_{y}^{2}v(y,s))

\displaystyle=r^{1-\alpha}\left(u_{t}(x,t)-\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{+}(D^{2}u(x,t))\right)

\displaystyle\leq r^{1-\alpha}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{r})}.

A similar calculation for
\mathcal{M}^{-}_{\lambda,\Lambda}(D^{2}v)
shows that
v\in S^{\ast}(\lambda,\Lambda,\widetilde{f})
in
Q_{1}
with

\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}\leq 1,\quad\|\widetilde{f}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}\leq\delta,\quad\text{and}\quad\Lambda\leq(1+\delta)\lambda,

where
\widetilde{f}(y,s)\coloneqq r^{1-\alpha}f(x,t)
. Thus, by applying
Lemma

3.1
to
v
, there exists a linear function
\widetilde{L}
such that

(3.4)

\displaystyle\|v-\widetilde{L}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{\eta})}\leq\eta^{1+\alpha}

and

(3.5)

\displaystyle|\widetilde{L}(0,0)|+|D\widetilde{L}|\leq C_{1}.

We now let
L_{k+1}(x)\coloneqq L_{k}(x)+r^{1+\alpha}\widetilde{L}(x/r)
. Then (
3.5
) implies that

\displaystyle|a_{k+1}-a_{k}|+\eta^{k+1}|b_{k+1}-b_{k}|\leq 2C_{1}\eta^{k(1+\alpha)}.

Finally, (
3.4
) shows that

\displaystyle|u(x,t)-L_{k+1}(x)|

\displaystyle=|u(x,t)-L_{k}(x)-r^{1+\alpha}\widetilde{L}(x/r)|

\displaystyle\leq r^{1+\alpha}|v(x/r,t/r^{2})-\widetilde{L}(x/r)|

\displaystyle\leq\eta^{(k+1)(1+\alpha)}\quad\text{for all }(x,t)\in Q_{\eta^{k+1}},

as desired.
∎

We are now ready to prove our first main theorem,

1.1
.

Proof of
Theorem

1.1
.

Let
u
satisfy (

) with

Thus, we can apply

for

to find a sequence of linear function

Theorem 3.4

\Omega
has a smooth lateral boundary, we have

\displaystyle|x_{n}|\leq[\partial_{p}\Omega]_{C^{2}(0,0)}(|x^{\prime}|^{2}+\sqrt{|t|})^{2}\quad\text{for all }(x,t)\in\partial_{p}\Omega\cap Q_{1}.

This implies that

\displaystyle|y_{n}|\leq\rho[\partial_{p}\Omega]_{C^{2}(0,0)}(|y^{\prime}|^{2}+\sqrt{|s|})^{2}\quad\text{for all }(x,t)\in\partial_{p}\widehat{\Omega}\cap Q_{1}.

Finally, for
K\coloneqq\|\widehat{u}\|_{L^{\infty}(\widehat{\Omega}\cap Q_{1})}+\|\widehat{f}\|_{L^{\infty}(\widehat{\Omega}\cap Q_{1})}/\delta+2^{2+\alpha}\|\widehat{g}\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(0,0)}/\delta+1
, by considering

\displaystyle\widetilde{u}(x,t)\coloneqq\widehat{u}(x,t)/K,\quad\widetilde{f}(x,t)\coloneqq\widehat{f}(x,t)/K,\quad\text{and}\quad\widetilde{g}(x,t)\coloneqq\widehat{g}(x,t)/K,

and taking
\rho
small enough (depending only on
n
,
\lambda
,
\alpha
, and
\|\partial_{p}\Omega\|_{C^{2}(0,0)}
), we may assume that

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega\cap Q_{1})}\leq 1,\quad\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega\cap Q_{1})}\leq\delta,\quad\|\partial_{p}\Omega\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(0,0)}\leq\frac{1-\eta^{\alpha}}{2C_{1}}\delta,

and

|g(x,t)|\leq\frac{\delta}{2^{2+\alpha}}(|x|+\sqrt{|t|})^{1+\alpha}\quad\text{for all $(x,t)\in\partial_{p}\Omega\cap Q_{1}$}.

Thus, we can apply
Theorem

3.4
for
u
to find a sequence
\{a_{k}\}_{k=-1}^{\infty}
satisfying (
3.8
) and (
3.9
). Indeed, by standard argument, we obtain limit
a
such that
a_{k}\to a
satisfying

\displaystyle|a_{k}-a|\leq C\eta^{k\alpha}.

Finally, for any
(x,t)\in\Omega\cap Q_{1}
, there exists
j\geq 0
such that
\eta^{j+1}\leq\max\{|x|,\sqrt{|t|}\}<\eta^{j}
. Then we conclude that

\displaystyle|u(x,t)-ax_{n}|\leq|u(x,t)-a_{j}x_{n}|+|a_{j}-a||x_{n}|\leq C(|x|+\sqrt{|t|})^{1+\alpha},

which implies that
u
is
C^{1,\alpha}
at
(0,0)
.
∎

4.
Parabolic normalized
p
-Laplace equations

We begin with the global
C^{1,\alpha}
-estimate by combining the interior estimate
Theorem

1.1
and the pointwise boundary estimate
Theorem

1.2
.

Proof of
Theorem

1.3
.

For any
u\in S^{\ast}(\lambda,\Lambda,f)
and linear function
L
, we know that
u-L
is contained in
S^{\ast}(\lambda,\Lambda,f)
again. A combination of
Theorem

1.1
and
Theorem

1.2
allows us to conclude that solutions of (
1.5
) have global estimate; see
[
36
, Theorem 3.1]
for details.
∎

Let us move on to the regularity theory for parabolic normalized
p
-Laplace equations (
2.3
) for
p\in(1,\infty)
. We recall that

\displaystyle\Delta_{p}^{N}u=(\delta_{ij}+(p-2)|Du|^{-2}u_{i}u_{j})u_{ij}

and

\displaystyle\min\{p-1,1\}I\leq\delta_{ij}+(p-2)q_{i}q_{j}|q|^{-2}\leq\max\{p-1,1\}I\quad\text{for all $q\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}$.}

Roughly speaking, we can expect that if
u
is a viscosity solution of (
2.3
), then
u\in S^{\ast}(\lambda_{p},\Lambda_{p},f)
for
\lambda_{p}\coloneqq\min\{p-1,1\}
and
\Lambda_{p}\coloneqq\max\{p-1,1\}
. However, a singularity arises when
Du=0
and so we should be careful for the points where the gradient vanishes. To be precise, we are going to consider approximated solutions
u^{\varepsilon}
with the following equations:

(4.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u^{\varepsilon}-a_{ij}^{\varepsilon}(Du^{\varepsilon})D_{ij}u^{\varepsilon}=f\quad\text{in $Q_{1}$},

where the coefficient matrix
a_{ij}^{\varepsilon}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathcal{S}^{n}
is defined by

\displaystyle a_{ij}^{\varepsilon}(q)\coloneqq\delta_{ij}+(p-2)\frac{q_{i}q_{j}}{|q|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}}\quad\text{for $q\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$}.

Before we prove the global
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity for solutions of (
2.3
) when
p
is sufficiently close to
2
, we provide several preliminary results for the approximated equations (
4.1
).

We are now ready to prove
Theorem

1.4
.

Proof of
Theorem

1.4
.

Let
\omega
be the modulus of continuity of
g
in
\partial_{p}Q_{1}
. If we define
f^{\varepsilon}
by the standard mollification of
f\in C(Q_{1})
, then
f^{\varepsilon}\to f
uniformly on compact subsets of
Q_{1}
. We approximate
u
by regularizing the equation (
4.1
). More precisely, let
v^{\varepsilon}\in C^{\infty}(Q_{1})\cap C(\overline{Q_{1}})
be the unique solution of the Dirichlet problem

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}v^{\varepsilon}-a_{ij}^{\varepsilon}(Dv^{\varepsilon})D_{ij}v^{\varepsilon}&=f^{\varepsilon}&&\text{in $Q_{1}$}\\
v^{\varepsilon}&=g&&\text{on $\partial_{p}Q_{1}$}.\end{aligned}\right.

The well-posedness of such problems is guaranteed by
[
20
,
24
]
. Then it follows from the maximum principle that

\displaystyle\|v^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}\leq\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial_{p}Q_{1})}+C\|f^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}.

Moreover, by following the proof of global Hölder estimates (
[
10
, Proposition 4.14]
or
[
16
, Proposition 2.5]
), we have

\displaystyle|v^{\varepsilon}(x,t)-v^{\varepsilon}(y,s)|\leq\omega^{\ast}(|x-y|+\sqrt{|s-t|})\quad\text{for all $(x,t),(y,s)\in\overline{Q_{1}}$}.

Therefore, by Arzela-Ascoli theorem, we can extract a subsequence
\{v^{\varepsilon_{k}}\}
such that
v^{\varepsilon_{k}}\to v\in C(\overline{Q_{1}})
uniformly in
\overline{Q_{1}}
. We note that by the stability theorem and the comparison principle, we obtain that
u\equiv v
in
\overline{Q_{1}}
.

On the other hand, we now concentrate on the uniform
C^{1,\alpha}
-estimate for
\{v^{\varepsilon}\}
. Indeed, the smoothness of
v^{\varepsilon}
in
Q_{1}
implies that
|Dv^{\varepsilon}(x,t)|<\infty
for any
(x,t)\in Q_{1}
and so

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} v^{\varepsilon}&\in S^{\ast}(\lambda_{p},\Lambda_{p},f^{\varepsilon})&&\text{in $Q_{1}$}\\
v^{\varepsilon}&=g&&\text{on $\partial_{p}Q_{1}$}\end{aligned}\right.

for
\lambda_{p}=\min\{p-1,1\}
and
\Lambda_{p}=\max\{p-1,1\}
. Then an important observation is that

\displaystyle\Lambda_{p}/\lambda_{p}\to 1\quad\text{when $p\to 2$}.

Hence, in view of
Theorem

1.3
, there exists
\delta>0
depending only on
n
and
\alpha
such that if
|p-2|<\delta
, then we have

\displaystyle\|v^{\varepsilon_{k}}\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{Q_{1}})}\leq C(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}+\|f^{\varepsilon_{k}}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}+\|g\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(\partial_{p}Q_{1})}),

where
C>0
depends only on
n
,
p
, and
\alpha
. Finally, letting
k\to\infty
, we arrive at the desired conclusion.
∎

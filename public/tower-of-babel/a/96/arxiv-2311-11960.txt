Sharp Hausdorff content estimates for accessible boundaries of domains in metric measure spaces of controlled geometry
Sylvester Eriksson-Bique, Ryan Gibara, Riikka Korte, and Nageswari Shanmugalingam

Abstract
. We give a sharp Hausdorff content estimate for the size of the accessible boundary of any domain in a metric measure space of controlled geometry, i.e., a complete metric space equipped with a doubling measure supporting a p p -Poincaré inequality for a fixed 1 ≤ p < ∞ 1\leq p<\infty . This answers a question posed by Jonas Azzam. In the process, we extend the result to every doubling gauge in metric measure spaces which satisfies a codimension one bound.



Key words and phrases
: John domain, visible boundary, accessible boundary, Hausdorff content,
doubling gauge function, pointwise Poincaré inequality.

Mathematics Subject Classification (2020): Primary: 28A78; Secondary: 30L15, 51F30.

1.
Introduction

Consider a domain (that is, an open and connected set)
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and a point
x_{0}\in\Omega
. From both a geometric and potential-theoretic perspective, it is relevant to consider the portion of the boundary
\partial\Omega
which can be reached from
x_{0}
by well behaved curves. Deemed the
accessible boundary
of
\Omega
with center
x_{0}
, for
c\geq 1
the set
\partial_{x_{0}}\Omega(c)
consists of all points
w\in\partial\Omega
for which there exists a curve

\gamma
with end points
x_{0}
and
w
such that whenever
z
is a point in the trajectory
of
\gamma
, we have
\ell(\gamma_{z,w})\leq c\,d(z,\partial\Omega)
. Here,

d(z,\partial\Omega):=\inf\{d(z,y)\,:\,y\in\partial\Omega\}
, and
\gamma_{z,w}
stands in for
every
subcurve of

\gamma
with end points
z,w
. In the literature, this boundary has also been referred to as the
visible boundary
of the domain.

Besides their natural geometric appeal, one is often interested in domains for which
\partial_{x_{0}}\Omega(c)
is large for some
c\geq 1
. In measuring this size, the cardinality of the accessible boundary is not a good choice from the point of view of geometric measure theory. A sharper notion of size of a set
A
is given by Hausdorff content:

(1.1)

\mathcal{H}^{s}_{r}(A):=\inf\left\{\,\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\diam(A_{i})^{s}\,\colon\,A\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}A_{i},\text{ and }\diam(A_{i})\leq r\text{ for each }i\in{\mathbb{N}}\right\}.

Estimates of the accessible boundary in terms of Hausdorff contents are important, for example, in establishing Hardy inequalities on domains
[
16
,
19
,
24
]
.

The present note is concerned with whether a lower bound for the Hausdorff content of
\partial\Omega
implies a lower bound for the Hausdorff content of
\partial_{x_{0}}\Omega(c)
for some
c\geq 1
and for all
x_{0}\in\Omega
. This has been answered in the affirmative by
[
2
,
20
]
with Hausdorff-content exponent
0<s\leq d-1
on
\partial\Omega
and Hausdorff-content exponent

t
with
0<t<s
on
\partial_{x_{0}}\Omega(c)
. When
s=d-1
, then this is known to be the best that one can do – see
[
2
, page 890]
. On the other hand, for
0<s<d-1
it was asked by Azzam in
[
2
]
whether one can improve the Hausdorff-content exponent of
\partial_{x_{0}}\Omega(c)
to
t=s
. Our goal is to prove this sharp result.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
0<s<d-1
. For every
\widehat{C}>1
there exist constants
c,C\geq 1
and a constant
\Delta>0
so that any domain
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and any
\delta\in(0,1)
satisfy the following property.

If for all
x_{0}\in\Omega
we have that

\mathcal{H}^{s}_{\infty}\left(B(x_{0},\widehat{C}d(x_{0},\partial\Omega))\setminus\Omega\right)\geq\delta\,d(x_{0},\partial\Omega)^{s},

then for all
x_{0}\in\Omega
we have that

\mathcal{H}^{s}_{\infty}(\partial_{x_{0}}\Omega(c)\cap B(x_{0},C\,d(x_{0},\partial\Omega)))\geq\Delta\,\delta\,d(x_{0},\partial\Omega)^{s}.

In fact, we prove a much more general version of this theorem for a metric measure space satisfying
minimal assumptions and Hausdorff content that is measured with respect to a gauge function.

In general metric spaces, and for complex sets, we may wish to measure Hausdorff content with respect to a general gauge function. A
gauge function

\xi
associates to each open ball
B=B(x,r)
a number
\xi(B)\in(0,\infty)
.
The Hausdorff content
\mathcal{H}^{\xi}_{\infty}
with respect to a gauge function is defined in Subsection
2.2
. We will assume that
\xi
is
doubling
: there exists a constant
D_{\xi}
so that for any pair of balls
B^{\prime},B
with
B^{\prime}\cap B\neq\emptyset
and
{\rm rad}(B^{\prime})\leq{\rm rad}(B)\leq 2{\rm rad}(B^{\prime})
, we have
D_{\xi}^{-1}\xi(B^{\prime})\leq\xi(B)\leq D_{\xi}\,\xi(B^{\prime})
.
A distinguished doubling gauge is given by the
standard codimension-
p
gauge function
h_{p}(B)=\frac{\mu(B)}{{\rm rad}(B)^{p}}
for some
1\leq p<\infty
.

We say that
\xi
has
codimension at least
Q
, if there is a
constant
K\geq 1
, so that for all balls
B=B(x,r)
and all radii
R\geq r
, we have

(1.3)

\frac{\xi(B(x,R))}{\xi(B(x,r))}\leq K\frac{h_{Q}(B(x,R))}{h_{Q}(B(x,r))}.

Examples of such gauges are given by
\xi=h_{Q+\sigma}
for any
\sigma\geq 0
.
A reader uneasy with this generality may consider only gauges of this form.
Another example is a gauge of the form

\xi(B(x,R))=\frac{\mu(B(x,R))}{R^{Q}\,(\log(e+R))^{q}}

for any
q>0
.

Codimensional Hausdorff measures such as
h_{Q}
,
Q>0
, play a crucial role in
the geometric analysis of subsets of metric measure spaces where the measure is not necessarily Ahlfors regular.
For instance,
h_{1}
plays a key role in the study of measure-theoretic boundaries of sets of finite
perimeter
[
1
,
6
,
15
,
21
]
,
and
h_{\theta}
for certain choices of
\theta>0
plays a key role in understanding traces of Sobolev functions,
as for example in
[
3
,
8
,
9
,
25
]
.
More general gauge functions such as the last example above occur naturally in probability, for instance,
in Brownian motion
[
26
, page 60]
, see
[
17
,
27
]
for more examples.

In
[
7
]
, under the hypothesis of Ahlfors regularity of the measure
\mu
,
a version of the following theorem was obtained, with the Hausdorff content used in the conclusion (
1.6
)
having exponent strictly less than that of the hypothesis (
1.5
), with the gauge-related Hausdorff contents
replaced by Hausdorff contents. Subsequently, in
[
5
]
, the authors improved the results of
[
7
]
by expanding to
the case that
\mu
is doubling but not necessarily Ahlfors regular. The gauge function considered there was

\xi=h_{p}
in (
1.5
), and the conclusion was again weaker in that the gauge function in (
1.6
)
was
h_{q}
for some
q>p
.
As in the Euclidean case, the sharp result with Hausdorff content exponents in both the hypothesis and
conclusion being equal presented a technical obstruction.
In the present paper, we overcome this obstruction
at the expense of a more involved proof than what was done in
[
5
]
,
leading to the following result. This solves the aforementioned
open problem of Azzam, as given in Theorem
1.2
above in the Euclidean case, which now follows
from Theorem
1.4
with the use of the gauge function
\xi=h_{d-s}
.

To prove Theorem
1.4
, we developed a new technique based on studying an avoidance property
for curves, see Lemma
3.4
and Lemma
4.1
; these two
lemmas are also of independent interest. Indeed, similar ideas have been useful elsewhere and can be found in
[
23
, Theorem 1.1]
, and in
[
22
, Section 4]
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a complete metric measure space with
\mu
a doubling measure supporting a

p
-Poincaré inequality for some

1\leq p<\infty
, and let
\xi
be a doubling gauge function which has codimension strictly greater than
p
. For every
\widehat{C}>1
there exist constants
c_{2},C_{2}\geq 1
and a constant
\Delta>0
so that any domain
\Omega\subset X
and any
\delta\in(0,1)
satisfy the following property.

If for all
x_{0}\in\Omega
we have that

(1.5)

\mathcal{H}^{\xi}_{d(x_{0},\partial\Omega)}\left(B(x_{0},\widehat{C}d(x_{0},\partial\Omega))\setminus\Omega\right)\geq\delta\,\xi(B(x_{0},d(x_{0},\partial\Omega))),

then for all
x_{0}\in\Omega
we have that

(1.6)

\mathcal{H}^{\xi}_{d(x_{0},\partial\Omega)}\left(\partial_{x_{0}}\Omega(c_{2})\cap B(x_{0},C_{2}d(x_{0},\partial\Omega))\right)\geq\Delta\,\delta\,\xi(B(x_{0},d(x_{0},\partial\Omega))).

We assume in Theorems
1.2
and
1.4
a content lower bound for the
complement of
\Omega
. In contrast, the paper of Heinonen
[
12
]
and the papers of Väisälä
[
28
,
29
]

consider the question of which domains in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
satisfy the property

\mathcal{H}^{d-1}_{\infty}(\partial\Omega\cap B(x_{0},\widehat{C}d(x_{0},\partial\Omega)))\geq\delta\,d(x_{0},\partial\Omega)^{d-1},

for each
x_{0}\in\Omega
. The hypothesis in the first of our main theorems is equivalent to the above
property when
s=d-1
. Further, since the accessible boundary is a subset of the topological boundary, we also obtain the following non-trivial fact.

Corollary 1.7
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a complete metric measure space with
\mu
a doubling measure supporting a

p
-Poincaré inequality for some

1\leq p<\infty
, and let
\xi
be a doubling gauge function which has codimension strictly greater than
p
. For every
\widehat{C}\geq 1
there exist a constant
C_{2}\geq 1
and a constant
\Delta>0
so that any domain
\Omega\subset X
and any
\delta\in(0,1)
satisfy the following property.

If for all
x_{0}\in\Omega
we have that

\mathcal{H}^{\xi}_{d(x_{0},\partial\Omega)}\left(B(x_{0},\widehat{C}d(x_{0},\partial\Omega))\setminus\Omega\right)\geq\delta\,\xi(B(x_{0},d(x_{0},\partial\Omega))),

then for all
x_{0}\in\Omega
we have that

\mathcal{H}^{\xi}_{d(x_{0},\partial\Omega)}\left(\partial\Omega\cap B(x_{0},C_{2}d(x_{0},\partial\Omega))\right)\geq\Delta\,\delta\,\xi(B(x_{0},d(x_{0},\partial\Omega))).

In fact, this statement has a direct proof which is considerably simpler than that of Theorem
1.4
, avoiding
many of the technicalities. We will present it in Appendix
A
. Thus, a reader may wish to read the paper
in the following order: first read the preparatory material in Sections
2
and
3
followed
by Appendix
A
and only then read Section
4
.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a complete and separable metric measure space equipped with
a Radon measure
\mu
. The open ball in
X
, centered at a point
x\in X
and with
radius
r>0
, is denoted by
B(x,r)=\{y\in X:d(x,y)<r\}
.
Each ball is considered to have an associated radius and center, and thus is formally a triple.
Different radii and centers may define the same
set, but we consider these as different balls.
Given any ball
B=B(x,r)
, we refer to its (unique) radius as
{\rm rad}(B)=r
. The inflation/deflation of a ball is
denoted by
CB:=B(x,Cr)
for
C>0
. Curves are continuous maps
\gamma:I\to X
, where

I=[a,b]\subset{\mathbb{R}}
is a compact interval. The length of rectifiable curves is denoted
\ell(\gamma)
.
The distance between a point
x\in X
and a set
A\subset X
is given by
d(x,A):=\inf_{a\in A}d(x,a)
.

The measure
\mu
is said to be
doubling
if there exists a constant
D_{\mu}\geq 1
so that

0<\mu(B(x,2r))\leq D_{\mu}\mu(B(x,r))<\infty

for every open ball
B(x,r)\subset X
. With
1\leq p<\infty
,
the space
(X,d,\mu)
is said to satisfy a
p
-Poincaré inequality if there exist constants
C_{P},\lambda
so that for all locally integrable functions
f\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(X)
and all upper gradients
g
of
f
we have

(2.1)

\mathchoice{\mathop{\vrule width=6.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.5pt\kern-8.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x,r)}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x,r)}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x,r)}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x,r)}}|f-f_{B(x,r)}|d\mu\leq C_{P}r\left(\mathchoice{\mathop{\vrule width=6.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.5pt\kern-8.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x,\lambda r)}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x,\lambda r)}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x,\lambda r)}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x,\lambda r)}}g^{p}d\mu\right)^{1/p},

where
f_{A}:=\mathchoice{\mathop{\vrule width=6.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.5pt\kern-8.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{A}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{A}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{A}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{A}}fd\mu:=\frac{1}{\mu(A)}\int_{A}fd\mu,
whenever the final integral is defined and
\mu(A)\in(0,\infty)
. A non-negative Borel function
g:X\to[0,\infty]
is an upper gradient for
f
if

|f(\gamma(0))-f(\gamma(1))|\leq\int_{\gamma}g\,ds

for all rectifiable curves
\gamma:[0,1]\to X
. For more background on upper gradients and analysis on metric spaces,
see
[
13
]
.

2.1.
Notational conventions

Given two expressions
A
and
B
, we write
A\lesssim B
if there is a constant
C
so that
A\leq CB
with
C
depending
only on the data associated with the metric measure space and the doubling constant associated with the
gauge functions used in computing Hausdorff contents. We also say that
A\simeq B
if
A\lesssim B
and
B\lesssim A
.

Throughout this paper
(X,d,\mu)
will be a complete metric measure space, where
\mu
is doubling and
X
supports a

p
-Poincaré inequality. Such spaces
X
satisfy two further properties:
quasiconvexity
and

the metric doubling property
, see
for instance
[
14
, Theorem 8.3.2]
. A space
X
is said to be
L
-quasiconvex if for each
x,y\in X

there is a curve
\gamma
with end points
x,y
such that
\ell(\gamma)\leq L\,d(x,y)
. We will repeatedly, and implicitly, use the fact that
d(x,X\setminus\Omega)\leq L\,d(x,\partial\Omega)
for
L
-quasiconvex spaces. We say that
X
is
N_{1}
-metric doubling
for some
N_{1}\geq 1
if for each
x\in X
and
r>0
there are at most
N_{1}
number of points in
B(x,r)
that are
r/2
-separated.
Observe that a metric space equipped with a
D
-doubling measure is necessarily
D^{4}
-metric doubling.

We denote the doubling constants associated with the gauge
\xi
and the measure
\mu
by

D_{\xi},D_{\mu}
respectively.
However, as their precise values are not kept track of in this work, we will replace
D_{\xi}
and
D_{\mu}
with

D=\max\{D_{\xi},D_{\mu}\}
.

2.2.
Hausdorff content

Let
\xi
be a gauge function. If
\mathcal{C}
is a countable collection of open balls in
X
, we set

{\rm rad}(\mathcal{C}):=\sup_{B\in\mathcal{C}}{\rm rad}(B)
.
We also define

\xi(\mathcal{C}):=\sum_{B\in\mathcal{C}}\xi(B),

\bigcup\mathcal{C}:=\bigcup_{B\in\mathcal{C}}B
,
and for
C>0
we set

C\mathcal{C}=\{CB:B\in\mathcal{C}\}
. If
A\subset\bigcup\mathcal{C}
, we say that

\mathcal{C}
is a cover of
A
, or that
\mathcal{C}
covers
A
. We further define the restricted collection
\mathcal{C}|_{A}=\{B\in\mathcal{C}:B\cap A\neq\emptyset\}
.
It is worth noting that the two operations, restriction
\mathcal{C}|_{A}
and inflation
C\mathcal{C}
, do not commute. In our notation we will use parenthesis to indicate the order of operations. Primarily we will use the order
(C\mathcal{C})|_{A}
, that is, we first inflate the balls and then restrict.

Given a subset
A\subset X
and
r\in(0,\infty]
,
we define the Hausdorff content at scale
r
with respect to the gauge
\xi
by

\mathcal{H}^{\xi}_{r}(A):=\inf\{\xi(\mathcal{C})\,\colon\,A\subset\bigcup\mathcal{C},\,{\rm rad}(\mathcal{C})\leq r,\,\text{and }\mathcal{C}\text{ is countable}\}.

The following lemma allows us to compare Hausdorff content at different scales.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\tau\geq 1
and suppose that
\xi
is a doubling gauge function. Then there is a constant

C(\tau)>0
, depending on
D
, such that
for each
K\subset X
and
r>0
we have

\mathcal{H}^{\xi}_{r}(K)\leq C(\tau)\,\mathcal{H}^{\xi}_{\tau r}(K).

Proof.

The argument is similar to
[
5
, Lemma 2.6]
.
Now, let
\mathcal{C}
be a covering of
K
by balls with
{\rm rad}(\mathcal{C})\leq\tau r
. By iterating the metric doubling property, each of the balls in
\mathcal{C}
can be covered by
N^{\prime}
balls of radius
r
, for some fixed
N^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Collect all of the resulting balls into a collection
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
. Then, applying the doubling property of the
\xi
we get a constant
C(\tau)
so that

\mathcal{H}^{\xi}_{r}(K)\leq\xi(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})\leq C(\tau)\xi(\mathcal{C}).

Infimizing over all such covers
\mathcal{C}
yields the claim.
∎

At various points throughout the paper, we will need to compare
\mathcal{H}^{\xi}_{r}
when
\xi
is a doubling gauge function of codimension at least
Q>0
to
Q
-codimensional Hausdorff content, as well as the
Q
-codimensional Hausdorff content to the
p
-codimensional Hausdorff content for
0<p<Q
. The following lemma is the way we obtain these estimates.

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose that
\xi
is a doubling gauge function with codimension at least
Q>p>0
. There exists a constant
K_{1}
so that the following holds. Let
B_{0}=B(x_{0},r_{0})
be any ball, and let
\mathcal{B}
be a collection of balls with
{\rm rad}(\mathcal{B})\leq r_{0}
and so that for every
B^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}
we have
B^{\prime}\cap B(x_{0},r_{0})\neq\emptyset
. It then holds that

(2.4)

K_{1}\frac{\xi(\mathcal{B})}{\xi(B_{0})}\geq\frac{h_{Q}(\mathcal{B})}{h_{Q}(B_{0})}\geq\frac{h_{p}(\mathcal{B})}{h_{p}(B_{0})}\frac{{\rm rad}(B_{0})^{Q-p}}{{\rm rad}(\mathcal{B})^{Q-p}}.

The constant
K_{1}
depends only on the doubling constants of
\xi
and
the measure
\mu
, and the codimensionality constant
K
in (
1.3
).

Proof.

Let
x_{B},r_{B}
be the center and radius of any ball
B
. By the codimension assumption, see (
1.3
), we
have for all
x\in X
and all
R>r>0
that

(2.5)

\frac{\xi(B(x,r))}{\xi(B(x,R))}\geq K^{-1}\frac{h_{Q}(B(x,r))}{h_{Q}(B(x,R))}.

Thus, for any ball
B^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}
we get

(2.6)

\frac{\xi(B^{\prime})}{\xi(B(x_{B^{\prime}},3r_{0}))}\geq K^{-1}\frac{h_{Q}(B^{\prime})}{h_{Q}(B(x_{B^{\prime}},3r_{0})}.

We have
B(x_{0},r_{0})\subset B(x_{B^{\prime}},3r_{0})\subset B(x_{0},5r_{0})
. Thus, by doubling, we have
\xi(B(x_{B^{\prime}},3r_{0}))\geq D^{-2}\xi(B(x_{0},r_{0}))
and
h_{Q}(B(x_{B^{\prime}},3r_{0})\leq D^{3}h_{Q}(B(x_{0},r_{0}))
. Consequently, from these and (
2.6
) we get

(2.7)

\frac{\xi(B^{\prime})}{\xi(B_{0})}\geq K^{-1}D^{-5}\,\frac{h_{Q}(B^{\prime})}{h_{Q}(B_{0})}.

By summing over all the balls
B^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}
and by choosing
K_{1}=KD^{5}
, we obtain the first part of the claimed inequality (
2.4
).

The second part of (
2.4
) follows by noting that

\frac{h_{Q}(B^{\prime})}{h_{Q}(B_{0})}=\frac{h_{p}(B^{\prime})}{h_{p}(B_{0})}\frac{{\rm rad}(B_{0})^{Q-p}}{{\rm rad}(B^{\prime})^{Q-p}}\geq\frac{h_{p}(B^{\prime})}{h_{p}(B_{0})}\frac{{\rm rad}(B_{0})^{Q-p}}{{\rm rad}(\mathcal{B})^{Q-p}}.\qed

2.3.
John domains

Recall that given
z\in\Omega
,
d(z,\partial\Omega)=\inf\{d(z,y)\,:\,y\in\partial\Omega\}
.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
\Omega\subset X
be an open set, and
x\in\Omega
. A
c
-John curve

\gamma
starting at
x
is a rectifiable curve
such that, with
w
the other end point of
\gamma
,
for each point
z
in the trajectory of
\gamma
we have that

\ell(\gamma_{z,w})\leq c\,d(z,\partial\Omega).

Here
\gamma_{z,w}
denotes
each
subcurve of
\gamma
with end points
z,w
.

Given a sequence of points geometrically approaching the boundary, we can patch together a sequence of John curves to obtain a John curve from the initial point to the final point. In the limit, we obtain a John curve from the initial point to the boundary.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
b\in(0,1),c_{1}\geq 1
. Then there exists
c_{2}\geq 1
so that the following holds for all

N\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{\infty\}
.
Let
x_{k}\in\Omega

with
0\leq k<N,k\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
x_{N}\in\overline{\Omega}
, where
\Omega\subset X
is an open set. Suppose that

d(x_{k+1},\partial\Omega)\leq b\,d(x_{k},\partial\Omega)
for every
k=0,1,\dots,N-1
, and further that

x_{\infty}=\lim_{k\to\infty}x_{k}
if
N=\infty
.

If for every
k=0,\dots,N-1
there exists a
c_{1}
-John curve
\gamma_{k}:[0,1]\to X
starting at
x_{k}
with
\gamma_{k}(1)=x_{k+1}
, then there exists a
c_{2}
-John curve
\gamma:[0,1]\to X
starting at

x_{0}
with
\gamma(1)=x_{N}
. This curve is
obtained as the concatenation of the curves
\gamma_{k}
,
k=0,\ldots,N-1
.

Proof.

Let
\gamma
be the concatenation of the curves
\gamma_{k}
,
k=0,\ldots,N-1
. Note that
the length of
\gamma_{k}
satisfies the estimate
\ell(\gamma_{k})\leq c_{1}\,d(x_{k},\partial\Omega)
because of the
c_{1}
-John condition.
Moreover, for each
k,j\in\{0,\ldots,N-1\}
with
k<j
, we have that
d(x_{j},\partial\Omega)\leq b^{j-k}\,d(x_{k},\partial\Omega)
. It follows that if

z
is a point in the trajectory of
\gamma
,
we fix a subcurve
\gamma_{z,x_{N}}
of
\gamma
with terminal points
z
and
x_{N}
, and then choose the largest

k\in\{1,\ldots,N-1\}
such that
\gamma_{z,x_{N}}\subset\bigcup_{j=k}^{N-1}\gamma_{j}
.
If
k=N-1
, then
\gamma_{z,x_{N}}
is a subcurve of the
c_{1}
-John curve
\gamma_{N-1}
, and so we have
directly the inequality
\ell(\gamma_{z,x_{N}})\leq c_{1}\,d(z,\partial\Omega)
.
Now, if
k\leq N-2
, then

\displaystyle\ell(\gamma_{z,x_{N}})\leq\sum_{j=k+1}^{N-1}\ell(\gamma_{j})+\ell((\gamma_{k})_{z,x_{k+1}})

\displaystyle\leq c_{1}\sum_{j=k+1}^{N-1}d(x_{j},\partial\Omega)+\ell((\gamma_{k})_{z,x_{k+1}})

\displaystyle\leq c_{1}\sum_{j=k+1}^{N-1}b^{j-k-1}d(x_{k+1},\partial\Omega)+\ell((\gamma_{k})_{z,x_{k+1}})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c_{1}}{1-b}\,d(x_{k+1},\partial\Omega)+c_{1}\,d(z,\partial\Omega).

If
d(x_{k+1},\partial\Omega)\leq 2c_{1}\,d(z,\partial\Omega)
, then we have

\ell(\gamma_{z,x_{N}})\leq\left[\frac{2c_{1}^{2}}{1-b}+c_{1}\right]\,d(z,\partial\Omega).

If
d(x_{k+1},\partial\Omega)>2c_{1}\,d(z,\partial\Omega)
, then we would have

d(z,\partial\Omega)\geq d(x_{k+1},\partial\Omega)-d(x_{k+1},z)>2c_{1}\,d(z,\partial\Omega)-\ell((\gamma_{k})_{x_{k+1},z})\geq c_{1}\,d(z,\partial\Omega),

which is not possible as
c_{1}\geq 1
. Note here that we have used the
c_{1}
-John condition above as well. It follows that
necessarily
d(x_{k+1},\partial\Omega)\leq 2c_{1}\,d(z,\partial\Omega)
, and so we have the John condition

\ell(\gamma_{z,x_{N}})\leq\left[\frac{2c_{1}^{2}}{1-b}+c_{1}\right]\,d(z,\partial\Omega).

Now taking

c_{2}:=\max\bigg\{\frac{2c_{1}^{2}}{1-b}+c_{1},c_{1}\bigg\}=\frac{2c_{1}^{2}}{1-b}+c_{1}

yields the claim.
∎

2.4.
Weak maximal function bounds

Let
g\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(X)
. For
s>0
and
x\in X
, we define the
restricted maximal function as

M_{s}g(x):=\sup\left\{\mathchoice{\mathop{\vrule width=6.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.5pt\kern-8.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(y,r)}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(y,r)}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(y,r)}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(y,r)}}|g|d\mu:x\in B(y,r),r\in(0,s)\right\}.

We will need the following statement for this maximal function.

Lemma 2.10
.

Suppose that
(X,d,\mu)
is
D
-doubling. There exists a constant
C_{M}>0
so that the following holds.
If
g\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(X)
is non-negative,
x\in X
, and
r,s>0
, then
there exists
y\in B(x,r)
so that

M_{s}g(y)\leq C_{M}\,\mathchoice{\mathop{\vrule width=6.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.5pt\kern-8.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x,r+2s)}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x,r+2s)}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x,r+2s)}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x,r+2s)}}gd\mu.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
\mathchoice{\mathop{\vrule width=6.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.5pt\kern-8.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x,r+2s)}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x,r+2s)}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x,r+2s)}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x,r+2s)}}gd\mu
is finite,
and we set
\Lambda:=\mathchoice{\mathop{\vrule width=6.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.5pt\kern-8.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x,r+2s)}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x,r+2s)}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x,r+2s)}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x,r+2s)}}gd\mu
.
Let
\tilde{g}=g1_{B(x,r+2s)}
. Let
E_{T}=\{y\in X:M_{s}\tilde{g}>T\}
. By the weak maximal function inequality
[
14
, Theorem 3.5.6]
, there exists a constant
C
, depending only on the doubling constant
D

(strictly speaking, on
D_{\mu}
), such that

\mu(E_{T})\leq\frac{C}{T}\,\int_{X}\tilde{g}d\mu=\frac{C}{T}\,\int_{B(x,r+2s)}gd\mu=\frac{C}{T}\,\Lambda\mu(B(x,r+2s)).

If
T=2C\Lambda\,D^{2+\lceil\log_{2}(s/r)\rceil_{+}}
, then we get from measure doubling that

\tfrac{C}{T}\,\Lambda\mu(B(x,r+2s))<\mu(B(x,r))
. So,

\mu(E_{T})<\mu(B(x,r)),

and there exists a
y\in B(x,r)\setminus E_{T}
. For such
y
, we have
M_{s}\tilde{g}(y)\leq 2C\Lambda\,D^{2+\lceil\log_{2}(s/r)\rceil_{+}}
. We have
M_{s}\tilde{g}(y)=M_{s}g(y)
since if
y\in B(z,\rho)

for some
z\in X
and
0<\rho<s
,
then
B(z,\rho)\subset B(x,r+2s)
. The claim thus follows with
C_{M}=2C\,D^{2+\lceil\log_{2}(s/r)\rceil_{+}}
.
∎

2.5.
Pointwise Poincaré inequality

Definition 2.11
.

We say that a space
X
satisfies the pointwise
p
-Poincaré inequality with constants
C_{P},L,\lambda\geq 1
, if for
every
x,y\in X
and every non-negative Borel function
g\in L^{p}(X)
, we have

\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma^{L,\lambda}_{x,y}}\int_{\gamma}gds\leq C_{P}d(x,y)\left(M_{\lambda d(x,y)}g^{p}(x)+M_{\lambda d(x,y)}g^{p}(y)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where the infimum is taken over the collection
\Gamma^{L,\lambda}_{x,y}
which consists of all rectifiable curves
\gamma
connecting
x
to
y
with length at most
Ld(x,y)
which are contained in
B(x,2\lambda d(x,y))
.

The notion of pointwise Poincaré inequality is perhaps not a standard notion in current literature on analysis in nonsmooth spaces,
but the following proposition shows that it is equivalent to a notion that
is
standard.
The proof of the proposition, as stated, can be found in
[
4
, Theorem 1.5]
, and the proof there uses
the Hajłasz-type inequality
[
11
, Theorem 3.2, Theorem 3.3]
.
On geodesic spaces the
\lambda
that appears in the Poincaré inequality (
2.1
) can be chosen to be unity,

\lambda=1
, see
[
10
, Theorem 1]
. This observation together with
[
11
, Theorem 3.2, Theorem 3.3]
yields the
second paragraph of the proposition.

Proposition 2.12
.

Suppose that
(X,d,\mu)
is doubling. The space
X
satisfies a
p
-Poincaré inequality if and only if
(X,d,\mu)
satisfies a pointwise
p
-Poincaré inequality.

Furthermore, if
X
is a complete geodesic space that supports a
p
-Poincaré
inequality, then the pointwise
p
-Poincaré inequality holds with the choice of
\lambda=2
,
at the expense of increasing the constant
C_{P}
.

3.
Preparation for the proof of Theorem
1.4

3.1.
Reduction to geodesic spaces

First, we do a simple reduction of the problem to the geodesic case. A space
X
is said to be geodesic if for every
x,y\in X
there exists a rectifiable curve
\gamma:[0,1]\to X
with
\gamma(0)=x,\gamma(1)=y
and
\ell(\gamma)=d(x,y)
. Every
complete metric space equipped with a doubling measure that supports a
p
-Poincaré inequality
is bi-Lipschitz to a geodesic space, thanks to the fact that they are necessarily quasiconvex, see
for instance
[
14
, Theorem 8.3.2]
. For such spaces, we can remove one parameter from the statement of
Theorem
1.4
and it will simplify the proofs to do so. We now re-state Theorem
1.4
in this context.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a complete geodesic space with
\mu
a doubling measure supporting a

p
-Poincaré inequality. Let
\xi
be a
doubling gauge function which has codimension greater than
p
. There exist constants
c\geq 1
and a constant
\Delta>0
so that any open subset
\Omega\subset X
and
\delta\in(0,1)
satisfy the following property.

If for all
x\in\Omega
it holds that

(3.2)

\mathcal{H}^{\xi}_{d(x,\partial\Omega)}(B(x,2d(x,\partial\Omega))\setminus\Omega)\geq\delta\,\xi(B(x,d(x,\partial\Omega))),

then for all
x\in\Omega
we have

(3.3)

\mathcal{H}^{\xi}_{d(x,\partial\Omega)}(\partial_{x}\Omega(c)\cap B(x,16d(x,\partial\Omega)))\geq\Delta\,\delta\,\xi(B(x,d(x,\partial\Omega))).

Theorem
1.4
follows from this version.
First note that even if
X
is not a geodesic space, by virtue of the doubling property of
\mu

and the support of a Poincaré inequality,
X
is necessarily quasiconvex. Therefore, the length metric on
X
is
biLipschitz equivalent to the original metric, and the length of curves in
X
with respect to this new metric agrees
with the length in the original metric. The balls with respect to the new metric are not necessarily balls in the original
metric, but they are contained in balls of comparable radii in the original metric, and contain balls in the original metric,
also of comparable radii. Thus modifying the metric results in a change of the relevant constants
in Theorem
1.4
. Thus we can reduce the proof of Theorem
1.4
to the case that

X
is a geodesic space.

We now justify our claim that Theorem
1.4
follows from Theorem
3.1
when

X
is a geodesic space. Suppose now that the hypothesis of Theorem
1.4
holds for some

\delta\in(0,1)
and
\widehat{C}
and for each
x\in\Omega
. Let
x_{0}\in\Omega
. Then by the geodesicity and completeness
of
X
there is a nearest point to
x_{0}
on
\partial\Omega
and a geodesic connecting that point to
x_{0}
, and except
for that point, the remaining part of the geodesic lies in
\Omega
. Now we can find a point
x
on this geodesic
that is a distance
d(x_{0},\partial\Omega)[1-1/\widehat{C}]
from
x_{0}
. Note that then
d(x,\partial\Omega)=d(x_{0},\partial\Omega)/\widehat{C}
.
Also,
B(x,\widehat{C}\,d(x,\partial\Omega))\subset B(x_{0},2d(x_{0},\partial\Omega))
. Hence, by the hypothesis,

\mathcal{H}^{\xi}_{d(x,\partial\Omega)}(B(x_{0},2d(x_{0},\partial\Omega)\setminus\Omega)\geq\mathcal{H}^{\xi}_{d(x,\partial\Omega)}(B(x,\widehat{C}\,d(x,\partial\Omega))\setminus\Omega)\geq\delta\,\xi(B(x,d(x,\partial\Omega))).

Now, by the doubling property of
\xi
and by Lemma
2.2
, we have that

\mathcal{H}^{\xi}_{d(x_{0},\partial\Omega)}(B(x_{0},2d(x_{0},\partial\Omega)\setminus\Omega)\geq C(\widehat{C})^{-1}\,\delta\,\xi(B(x,d(x,\partial\Omega)))\geq C(\widehat{C})^{-1}\,D^{-n_{0}}\,\,\delta\,\xi(B(x_{0},d(x_{0},\partial\Omega))),

where
n_{0}\simeq\log_{2}(\widehat{C})
,
from whence the desired conclusion follows from an application of Theorem
3.1
.

3.2.
An avoidance property

Recall that
h_{q}(B)=\frac{\mu(B)}{{\rm rad}(B)^{q}}
for
q>0
. Given the reduction to the geodesic case, we will henceforth only state results for such spaces.
Recall the notational conventions from Subsection
2.2
. In particular, given a collection
\mathcal{F}
of
subsets of
X
, we set
\bigcup\mathcal{F}:=\bigcup_{K\in\mathcal{F}}K
.

The following lemma coins a crucial avoidance property, which underlies our approach. It states that a collection
\mathcal{B}
satisfying
a certain “sparseness” condition, (
3.5
), can be avoided by a rectifiable curve.
Conversely, if the collection of balls cannot be avoided, then (
3.5
) must fail. In fact, our application of Lemma
3.4
will use this contrapositive form.

Lemma 3.4
.

Under the hypotheses of Theorem
3.1
, we denote the constants associated with the pointwise
p
-Poincaré
inequality by
(C_{P},L,2)
.
There exist constants
C_{1},\epsilon_{0}>0
so that the following holds. Suppose that

and
\mathcal{B}
is a
countable collection of balls so that
\bigcup 4\mathcal{B}\cap\{x,y\}=\emptyset
, and so that for all
r\in(0,4d(x,y)]
and

we have

(3.5)

h_{p}(\mathcal{B}|_{B(z,r)})\leq\epsilon_{0}h_{p}(B(z,r)).

Then, there exists a rectifiable curve

connecting

to

which does not intersect

\bigcup\mathcal{B}
and with

\displaystyle\gamma(0)=x,\hskip 18.49988pt

\displaystyle\gamma(1)=y,\hskip 18.49988pt

\displaystyle\gamma\subset B(x,2d(x,y)),\hskip 18.49988pt

\displaystyle\ell(\gamma)\leq C_{1}\,d(x,y),

\displaystyle C_{1}\,d(\gamma(t),x)\geq\ell(\gamma|_{[0,t]}),\hskip 18.49988pt

\displaystyle C_{1}d(\gamma(t),y)\geq\ell(\gamma|_{[t,1]})

for all

Proof.

Let
\epsilon_{0}>0
, and

We will determine the constraints on their values in the course of the proof.
Let

For each

and for

.

Noting that

, from (

) and from the doubling proprty of
\mu

we obtain

\displaystyle\mathchoice{\mathop{\vrule width=6.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.5pt\kern-8.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(z,r)}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(z,r)}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(z,r)}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(z,r)}}g^{p}d\mu=\mathchoice{\mathop{\vrule width=6.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.5pt\kern-8.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(z,r)}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(z,r)}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(z,r)}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(z,r)}}g_{z}^{p}d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(2r)^{p}}{\mu(B(z,2r))}\,\frac{D}{2^{p}r^{p}}\,\sum_{B\in\mathcal{B}\,:\,B(z,r)\cap 2B\neq\emptyset}\frac{\mu(2B\cap B(z,r))}{{\rm rad}(B)^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{h_{p}(B(z,2r))}\,\frac{D^{2}}{2^{p}r^{p}}\,\sum_{B\in\mathcal{B}\,:\,B(z,r)\cap 2B\neq\emptyset}\frac{\mu(B)}{{\rm rad}(B)^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{D^{2}\epsilon_{0}}{2^{p}r^{p}}.

In applying (
3.5
) in the last line, we used the following fact: the non-emptiness of

implies the non-emptiness of

because

.

Let

to

, and so

\mathchoice{\mathop{\vrule width=6.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.5pt\kern-8.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(z,2d(z,x_{i}))}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(z,2d(z,x_{i}))}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(z,2d(z,x_{i}))}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(z,2d(z,x_{i}))}}g^{p}\,d\mu\lesssim\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{[2d(z,x_{i})]^{p}}.

\mathchoice{\mathop{\vrule width=6.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.5pt\kern-8.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x_{i},18r_{i})}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x_{i},18r_{i})}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x_{i},18r_{i})}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x_{i},18r_{i})}}g^{p}\,d\mu\lesssim\left(\frac{2d(z,x_{i})}{18r_{i}}\right)^{Q-p}\,\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{r_{i}^{p}}\lesssim\frac{1}{(1-a)^{Q-p}}\,\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{r_{i}^{p}}\simeq\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{r_{i}^{p}}.

By Lemma

, with the choices of

to be

and

to be

, we know that

M_{8r_{i}}g^{p}(y_{i})\leq M\,\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{r_{i}^{p}}.

4.
Proof of Theorem
3.1

The proof of Theorem
3.1
will be based on a contradiction argument involving iteration.
Let
\mathcal{C}
be a countable collection of open balls. We say that a ball
B(x,r)
, with
x\in\Omega
and
r>0
,
is
\epsilon
-bad with respect to
\mathcal{C}
if

\xi(\mathcal{C}|_{B(x,16r)})=\sum_{B\in\mathcal{C}|_{B(x,16r)}}\xi(B)\leq\epsilon\,\xi(B(x,r)),

and
{\rm rad}(\mathcal{C}|_{B(x,16r)})\leq r
.
In our application of the following lemma to the proof of Theorem

to be a cover of
the accessible boundary of

from

, but the next lemma will be true even with a general choice of collection

The following Lemma is the basic step of our proof by contradiction. We briefly describe its content and how we use it. The lemma states that an
\epsilon
-bad ball within a domain satisfying the assumptions of Theorem
3.1
, with radius equal to the distance to the boundary, will satisfy one of two properties:

(1)

either there exists a smaller
\epsilon
-bad ball, which is accessible by a John curve,
and with radius equal to the distance to the boundary; or

(2)

the collection that we started from failed to cover a certain accessible boundary.

If we start from the assumption that
\mathcal{C}
is a covering of the accessible boundary, both of these are bad in a different way. In the second case, we actually failed to cover the desired boundary, which yields a direct contradiction. In the first case, when applied infinitely many times, we obtain a sequence of bad balls geometrically approaching the boundary, which fail to be well covered by
\mathcal{C}
. The centres are connected by John curves, and Lemma
2.9
yields a limit point in the accessible boundary. The radius constraint on
\mathcal{C}
prevents this collection from covering the limit point.

B\in\mathcal{B}
we have
4B\cap\{x,x_{0}\}
is empty. Hence
by Lemma
3.4
, there is some
z\in\{x,x_{0}\}
and
0<r_{x}<8r_{0}
such that

h_{p}(\mathcal{B}|_{B(z,r_{x})})>\epsilon_{0}\,h_{p}(B(z,r_{x})),

and so by Lemma
2.3
we have that

(A.10)

\xi(\mathcal{B}|_{B(z,r_{x})})>\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{K_{1}}\,\xi(B(z,r_{x})).

If
z=x_{0}
and
\tfrac{r_{0}}{10}<r_{x}<8r_{0}
, then
\xi(B(z,r_{x}))=\xi(B(x_{0},r_{x}))\geq D^{-4}\,\xi(B(x_{0},r_{0}))
,
and so by (
A.10
),

\frac{1}{D^{4}}\,\xi(B(x_{0},r_{0}))\leq\frac{K_{1}}{\epsilon_{0}}\,\xi(\mathcal{B}|_{B(x_{0},r_{x})})\leq\frac{K_{1}}{\epsilon_{0}}\,\xi(\mathcal{B})<\frac{K_{1}}{\epsilon_{0}}\,\epsilon\,\xi(B(x_{0},r_{0})),

which will not be possible if we also ensure
\epsilon
(or, equivalently,
\Delta
) is small enough so that

(A.11)

\frac{D^{4}\,K_{1}}{\epsilon_{0}}\,\epsilon<1.

On the other hand, if
z=x_{0}
and
r_{x}\leq\tfrac{r_{0}}{10}
, then
B(z,r_{x})\subset B(x_{0},r_{0}/10)
, and
so if
B\in\mathcal{B}
such that
B
intersects

B(z,r_{x})
and hence intersects
B(x_{0},r_{0}/10)
, then by the virtue of
B
containing a point in
\partial\Omega
we must have that

2r_{B}>9r_{0}/10
, that is,
r_{B}>9r_{0}/20
. Now
d(x_{B},x_{0})<r_{B}+\tfrac{r_{0}}{10}<\tfrac{11}{9}r_{B}
.
Therefore when
y\in B(x_{0},r_{0})
,
we must have
d(x_{B},y)<\tfrac{11}{9}r_{B}+r_{0}<\tfrac{31}{9}r_{B}
. It follows that
B(x_{0},r_{0})\subset\tfrac{31}{9}B
,
whence we obtain

\xi(B)\geq D^{-4}\,\xi(\tfrac{31}{9}B)\geq D^{-5}\,\xi(B(x_{0},r_{0})).

Therefore we have

\frac{1}{D^{5}}\,\xi(B(x_{0},r_{0}))\leq\xi(B)\leq\xi(\mathcal{B})<\epsilon\,\xi(B(x_{0},r_{0})),

which is also not possible by making
\epsilon
to be small enough so that

(A.12)

D^{5}\,\epsilon<1.

Therefore, for each
x\in B(x_{0},2r_{0})\setminus(\Omega\cup\bigcup\tau\mathcal{B})
we must have
z=x
, together with
some
0<r_{x}<8r_{0}
such that

(A.13)

\xi(\mathcal{B}|_{B(x,r_{x})})\geq\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{K_{1}}\,\xi(B(x,r_{x})).

The collection
\{B(x,2r_{x})\,:\,x\in B(x_{0},2r_{0})\setminus(\Omega\cup\bigcup\tau\mathcal{B})\}
covers

B(x_{0},2r_{0})\setminus(\Omega\cup\bigcup\tau\mathcal{B})
. We apply the
5
-covering theorem to obtain a pairwise-disjoint
subcollection
\{B_{i}\}_{i\in I\subset{\mathbb{N}}}
such that
\{5B_{i}\}_{i\in I}
covers

B(x_{0},2r_{0})\setminus(\Omega\cup\bigcup\tau\mathcal{B})
. Since
{\rm rad}(B_{i})\leq 8r_{i}
for all
i\in I
, we get
from (
A.8
) and Lemma
2.2
that

(A.14)

\frac{3}{4}\,\delta\,\xi(B(x_{0},r_{0}))<\mathcal{H}^{\xi}_{r_{0}}(B(x_{0},2r_{0})\setminus(\Omega\cup\bigcup\tau\mathcal{B}))\leq C(40)\mathcal{H}^{\xi}_{40r_{0}}(B(x_{0},2r_{0})\setminus(\Omega\cup\bigcup\tau\mathcal{B}))\leq\sum_{i\in I}\xi(5B_{i}).

Now, if
B\in\mathcal{B}
is such that
B\cap B(x,r_{x})\neq\emptyset
for some

x\in B(x_{0},2r_{0})\setminus(\Omega\cup\bigcup\tau\mathcal{B})
, then as
d(x,x_{B})\geq\tau r_{B}
, we must have

\tau r_{B}\leq r_{x}+r_{B}
, and so necessarily

r_{x}>(\tau-1)\,r_{B}.

It follows that
B\subset B(x,2r_{x})
. Consequently, if
B_{i},B_{j}
are distinct balls in our covering,
i,j\in I
, then
every pair of balls
B\in\mathcal{B}|_{\tfrac{1}{2}}B_{i}
and
B^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}|_{\tfrac{1}{2}}B_{j}

satisfy
B\subset B_{i}
and
B^{\prime}\subset B_{j}
.
Since
B_{i}\cap B_{j}=\emptyset
, we have that the collections
\mathcal{B}|_{\tfrac{1}{2}}B_{i}
and

\mathcal{B}|_{\tfrac{1}{2}}B_{j}
are disjoint.

Now it follows from (
A.14
) and the above disjointness property that

\frac{3\delta}{4C(40)}\,\xi(B(x_{0},r_{0}))\leq\sum_{i\in I}\xi(5B_{i})\leq D^{4}\,\sum_{i\in I}\xi(\tfrac{1}{2}B_{i})\leq D^{4}\,\frac{K_{1}}{\epsilon_{0}}\,\sum_{i\in I}\xi(\mathcal{B}|_{\tfrac{1}{2}B_{i}})\leq D^{4}\,\frac{K_{1}}{\epsilon_{0}}\,\epsilon\,\xi(B(x_{0},r_{0})),

which again is not possible by choosing
\Delta
to be small enough so that

(A.15)

\frac{4D^{4}C(40)}{3}\,\frac{K_{1}}{\epsilon_{0}}\,\frac{\epsilon}{\delta}=\frac{4D^{4}C(40)}{3}\,\frac{K_{1}}{\epsilon_{0}}\,\Delta<1.

These exhaust all the possibilities, and so
by ensuring that conditions (
A.7
), (
A.9
), (
A.11
),
(
A.12
), (
A.15
)
all hold, (
A.4
) and (
A.5
) cannot simultaneously hold. This completes
the proof of Theorem
A.1
.

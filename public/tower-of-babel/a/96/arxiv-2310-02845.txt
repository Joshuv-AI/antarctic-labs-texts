Yoshiki Nakamura Department of Computer Science Tokyo Institute of Technology nakamura.yoshiki.ny@gmail.com

Abstract
In this note, we give a linear-size translation from formulas of first-order logic into equations of the calculus of relations preserving validity and finite validity. Our translation also gives a linear-size conservative reduction from formulas of first-order logic into formulas of the three-variable fragment of first-order logic.

1
Introduction

The calculus of relations
(CoR, for short)
[
1
]
is an algebraic system with operations on binary relations.
As binary relations appear everywhere in computer science, CoR and relation algebras can be applied to various areas, such as databases and program development and verification
[
2
]
.
W.r.t. binary relations, CoR has the same expressive power as the three-variable fragment of first-order predicate logic with equality (
\mathrm{FO3}_{=}
) (where all predicate symbols are binary)
[
3
]
, so CoR has strictly less expressive power than first-order predicate logic with equality (
\mathrm{FO}_{=}
).
For example, CoR equations cannot characterize the class of structures s.t. “its cardinality is greater than or equal to
4
”, whereas it can be characterized by the
\mathrm{FO}_{=}
formula
\forall x_{1},\forall x_{2},\forall x_{3},\exists y,(\lnot{y=x_{1}})\land(\lnot{y=x_{2}})\land(\lnot{y=x_{3}})
where
x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},y
are pairwise distinct variables.

Nevertheless, there is a recursive translation (total recursive function) from
\mathrm{FO}_{=}
formulas into CoR equations (resp.
\mathrm{FO3}_{=}
formulas) preserving validity
[
3
]
(see also
[
4
,
5
]
).

In this paper, we give another recursive translation from
\mathrm{FO}_{=}
formulas into CoR equations preserving validity, slightly refined in that it satisfies both of the following:

1.

Our translation preserves both validity and finite validity (so, it also gives a
conservative reduction

[
6
, Def. 2.1.35]
).

2.

Our translation is linear-size (i.e., the output size is bounded by a linear function in the input size).

The first refinement is useful, e.g., in finding counter-models (because if there exists a finite counter-model in the pre-translated formula, then there also exists a finite counter-model in the post-translated formula).
Such a translation is already known (e.g.,
[
6
, Cor. 3.1.8 and Thm. 3.1.9]
), but via encodings of Turing-machines and domino problems.
Our translation presents a conservative reduction from
\mathrm{FO}_{=}
formulas to
\mathrm{FO3}_{=}
formulas, directly.
Thanks to this, we also have the second refinement, which shows that the validity (resp. finite validity) problem of
\mathrm{FO}_{=}
formulas and that of CoR equations are equivalent under linear-size translations, as the converse direction immediately follows from the standard translation from CoR equations into
\mathrm{FO3}_{=}

[
1
]
(Prop.
2.2
).

Our translation is not so far from known encodings (e.g.,
[
4
,
5
]
) in that they and our translation use pairing (
2
-tupling) functions, but in our translation, we use
non-nested

k
-tupling functions where
k
is an arbitrary natural number, instead of arbitrarily nested pairing functions.
For constructions using arbitrarily nested pairing functions, we need infinitely many vertices even if the base universe is finite (as there is no surjective function from
X
to
X^{2}
when
\#X
is finite and
\#X\geq 2
).
Thanks to the modification above, our construction preserves both validity and finite validity.
Additionally, to preserve the output size linear in the input size, we apply a cumulative sum technique.

This paper is structured as follows.
In
Section

2
, we give basic definitions of
\mathrm{FO}_{=}
and CoR.
In
Section

3
, we give a translation from
\mathrm{FO}_{=}
formulas into CoR equations preserving validity and finite validity.
In
Section

4
, we additionally give a Tseitin translation for CoR, which is useful for reducing the number of alternations of operations.
Additionally, in
Appendix

A
, we give a direct translation from
\mathrm{FO}_{=}
formulas into
\mathrm{FO3}_{=}
formulas, not via CoR, for explicitly writing a transformed
\mathrm{FO3}_{=}
formulas; the translation is the same as that given in
Section

3
.

2
Preliminaries

We write
{\rm Nature}
for the set of all non-negative integers.
For a set
A
, we write
\#A
for the cardinality of
A
.

A
structure

\mathfrak{A}
over a set
A
is a tuple
\langle|\mathfrak{A}|,\{a^{\mathfrak{A}}\}_{a\in A}\rangle
, where

•

the universe
|\mathfrak{A}|
is a non-empty set of vertices,

•

each
a^{\mathfrak{A}}\subseteq|\mathfrak{A}|^{2}
is a binary relation on
|\mathfrak{A}|
.

We say that a structure
\mathfrak{A}
is
finite
if
|\mathfrak{A}|
is finite.
For structures
\mathfrak{A},\mathfrak{B}
over a set
A
,
we say that
\mathfrak{A}
and
\mathfrak{B}
are
isomorphic
if there is a bijective map
f\colon|\mathfrak{A}|\to|\mathfrak{B}|
such that for all
x,y\in|\mathfrak{A}|
and
a\in A
, we have
\langle x,y\rangle\in a^{\mathfrak{A}}\Longleftrightarrow\langle f(x),f(y)\rangle\in a^{\mathfrak{B}}
.

2.1
First-order logic

Let
\Sigma
be a countably infinite set of binary predicate symbols and
\mathbf{V}
be a countably infinite set of
variables
.
The set of
formulas
in
first-order predicate logic with equality
(
\mathrm{FO}_{=}
) is defined by:

\displaystyle\varphi,\psi,\rho

\displaystyle\quad::=\quad a(x,y)\mid{x=y}\mid\lnot\psi\mid\psi\land\rho\mid\exists x,\psi

(
a\in\Sigma
and
x,y\in\mathbf{V}
)

We write
\mathrm{V}(\varphi)
for the set of free and bound variables occurring in a formula
\varphi
.
For
k\geq 0
, we write
\mathrm{FO}k_{=}
for the set of all formulas
\varphi
s.t.
\#\mathrm{V}(\varphi)\leq k
.
(In this paper,
\mathrm{FO}3_{=}
mostly occurs.)
A
sentence
is a formula not having any free variable.
We use parentheses in ambiguous situations and use the following notations:

\displaystyle\varphi\lor\psi

\displaystyle\;\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-3.8889pt\hbox{$=$}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip-2.7431pt\raisebox{4.66875pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\Delta$}}\hskip-2.7431pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}\;\lnot((\lnot\varphi)\land(\lnot\psi))

\displaystyle\varphi\to\psi

\displaystyle\;\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-3.8889pt\hbox{$=$}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip-2.7431pt\raisebox{4.66875pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\Delta$}}\hskip-2.7431pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}\;(\lnot\varphi)\lor\psi

\displaystyle\mathsf{t}

\displaystyle\;\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-3.8889pt\hbox{$=$}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip-2.7431pt\raisebox{4.66875pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\Delta$}}\hskip-2.7431pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}\;\exists x,{x=x}

\displaystyle\forall x,\psi

\displaystyle\;\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-3.8889pt\hbox{$=$}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip-2.7431pt\raisebox{4.66875pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\Delta$}}\hskip-2.7431pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}\;\lnot\exists x,\lnot\psi

\displaystyle\varphi\leftrightarrow\psi

\displaystyle\;\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-3.8889pt\hbox{$=$}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip-2.7431pt\raisebox{4.66875pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\Delta$}}\hskip-2.7431pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}\;(\varphi\to\psi)\land(\psi\to\varphi)

\displaystyle\mathsf{f}

\displaystyle\;\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-3.8889pt\hbox{$=$}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip-2.7431pt\raisebox{4.66875pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\Delta$}}\hskip-2.7431pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}\;\lnot\mathsf{t}

We write
\bigwedge\Gamma
for the formula
\varphi_{1}\land\dots\land\varphi_{n}

where
\Gamma=\{\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{n}\}
is a finite set (and
\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{n}
are ordered by a total order).
The
size

\|\varphi\|\in{\rm Nature}
of a formula
\varphi
is defined by:

\displaystyle\|a(x,y)\|

\displaystyle\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-3.8889pt\hbox{$=$}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip-2.7431pt\raisebox{4.66875pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\Delta$}}\hskip-2.7431pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}1+2

\displaystyle\|x=y\|

\displaystyle\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-3.8889pt\hbox{$=$}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip-2.7431pt\raisebox{4.66875pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\Delta$}}\hskip-2.7431pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}1+2

\displaystyle\|\lnot\psi\|

\displaystyle\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-3.8889pt\hbox{$=$}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip-2.7431pt\raisebox{4.66875pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\Delta$}}\hskip-2.7431pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}1+\|\psi\|

\displaystyle\|\psi\land\rho\|

\displaystyle\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-3.8889pt\hbox{$=$}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip-2.7431pt\raisebox{4.66875pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\Delta$}}\hskip-2.7431pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}1+\|\psi\|+\|\rho\|

\displaystyle\|\exists x,\psi\|

\displaystyle\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-3.8889pt\hbox{$=$}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip-2.7431pt\raisebox{4.66875pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\Delta$}}\hskip-2.7431pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}1+1+\|\psi\|

For a structure
\mathfrak{A}
over
\Sigma
,
the
semantics

\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{A}}\subseteq|\mathfrak{A}|^{\mathbf{V}}
of a formula
\varphi
over
\mathfrak{A}
is defined as follows (where
|\mathfrak{A}|^{\mathbf{V}}
denotes the set of functions from
\mathbf{V}
to
|\mathfrak{A}|
):

\displaystyle\llbracket a(x,y)\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{A}}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-3.8889pt\hbox{$=$}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip-2.7431pt\raisebox{4.66875pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\Delta$}}\hskip-2.7431pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}\{f\colon\mathbf{V}\to|\mathfrak{A}|\mid\langle f(x),f(y)\rangle\in a^{\mathfrak{A}}\}

\displaystyle\llbracket x=y\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{A}}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-3.8889pt\hbox{$=$}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip-2.7431pt\raisebox{4.66875pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\Delta$}}\hskip-2.7431pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}\{f\colon\mathbf{V}\to|\mathfrak{A}|\mid f(x)=f(y)\}

\displaystyle\llbracket\lnot\psi\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{A}}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-3.8889pt\hbox{$=$}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip-2.7431pt\raisebox{4.66875pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\Delta$}}\hskip-2.7431pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}|\mathfrak{A}|^{\mathbf{V}}\setminus\llbracket\psi\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{A}}

\displaystyle\llbracket\psi\land\rho\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{A}}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-3.8889pt\hbox{$=$}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip-2.7431pt\raisebox{4.66875pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\Delta$}}\hskip-2.7431pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}\llbracket\psi\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{A}}\cap\llbracket\rho\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{A}}

\displaystyle\llbracket\exists x,\psi\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{A}}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-3.8889pt\hbox{$=$}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip-2.7431pt\raisebox{4.66875pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\Delta$}}\hskip-2.7431pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}\{f\colon\mathbf{V}\to|\mathfrak{A}|\mid\mbox{ for some $v\in|\mathfrak{A}|$, $f[v/x]\in\llbracket\psi\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{A}}$}\}

Here,
f[v/x]
denotes the function
f
in which the value of
x
has been replaced with
v
.

For a formula
\varphi
and a structure
\mathfrak{A}
,
we say that
\varphi
is
true
on
\mathfrak{A}
, written
\mathfrak{A}\models\varphi
, if
\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{A}}=|\mathfrak{A}|^{\mathbf{V}}
.
We say that a formula
\varphi
is
valid
(resp.
finitely valid
) if

\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{A}}=|\mathfrak{A}|^{\mathbf{V}}
holds for all structures (resp. all finite structures)
\mathfrak{A}
.
We say that two formulas
\varphi,\psi
are
semantically equivalent
if the formula
\varphi\leftrightarrow\psi
is valid.
Additionally, we say that a formula
\varphi
is
satisfiable
(resp.
finitely satisfiable
) if

\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{A}}\neq\emptyset
holds for some structure (resp. finite structure)
\mathfrak{A}
.

Remark 2.1

Function and constant symbols can be encoded by predicate symbols with functionality axiom (see, e.g.,
[
7
, Sect. 19.4]
)
and each predicate symbol (of arbitrary arity) can be encoded by binary predicate symbols (see, e.g.,
[
7
, Lem. 21.2 (p. 275)]
, which translations each atomic formula
a(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})
into the formula
\exists z,(\bigwedge_{1\leq j\leq k}p_{j}(z,x_{j}))\land a^{\prime}(z,z)
where
z
is a fresh variable,

p_{1},\dots,p_{k}
are fresh binary symbols for expressing projections, and

a^{\prime}
is a fresh binary symbol for expressing the relation
a
, respectively.
For instance, the formula
a(x,y,x)\land a(y,y,x)
is translated into
(\exists z,p_{1}(z,x)\land p_{2}(z,y)\land p_{3}(z,x)\land a^{\prime}(z,z))\land(\exists z,p_{1}(z,y)\land p_{2}(z,y)\land p_{3}(z,x)\land a^{\prime}(z,z))
).
Thus, by well-known facts, we can give a linear-size translation from formulas of first-order logic with predicate and function symbols of arbitrary arity into
\mathrm{FO}_{=}
formulas (above) preserving validity and finite validity.
Here, the size of a
k
-ary atomic formula is defined as
\|a(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})\|=1+k
.
(Note that the translation is not linear-size when the size is defined as
\|a(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})\|=1
and
k
is not bounded.
Hence, this linearity depends on the size definition.)
Hence, we consider the
\mathrm{FO}_{=}
above (equality
=
can also be eliminated, see, e.g.,
[
7
, Sect. 19.4]
, but we introduce it only for convenience).

2.2
The calculus of relations

Let
\Sigma
be a countably infinite set of
(term) variables
.
The set of
terms
in
the calculus of relations
(CoR) is defined by:

\displaystyle t,s,u

\displaystyle\quad::=\quad a\mid\mathsf{I}\mid s^{-}\mid s\cap u\mid s\cdot u\mid s^{\smile}

(
a\in\Sigma
)

We write
\bigcap\Gamma
for the term
t_{1}\cap\dots\cap t_{n}

where
\Gamma=\{t_{1},\dots,t_{n}\}
is a finite set (and
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}
are ordered by a total order).
We use parentheses in ambiguous situations and use the following notations:

\displaystyle t\cup s

\displaystyle\quad\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-3.8889pt\hbox{$=$}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip-2.7431pt\raisebox{4.66875pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\Delta$}}\hskip-2.7431pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}\quad(t^{-}\cap s^{-})^{-}

\displaystyle t\dagger s

\displaystyle\quad\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-3.8889pt\hbox{$=$}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip-2.7431pt\raisebox{4.66875pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\Delta$}}\hskip-2.7431pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}\quad(t^{-}\cdot s^{-})^{-}

\displaystyle\top

\displaystyle\quad\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-3.8889pt\hbox{$=$}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip-2.7431pt\raisebox{4.66875pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\Delta$}}\hskip-2.7431pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}\quad\mathsf{I}\cup\mathsf{I}^{-}

\displaystyle\bot

\displaystyle\quad\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-3.8889pt\hbox{$=$}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip-2.7431pt\raisebox{4.66875pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\Delta$}}\hskip-2.7431pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}\quad\top^{-}

The
size

\|t\|\in{\rm Nature}
of a term
t
is defined by:

\displaystyle\|a\|

\displaystyle\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-3.8889pt\hbox{$=$}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip-2.7431pt\raisebox{4.66875pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\Delta$}}\hskip-2.7431pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}1

\displaystyle\|\mathsf{I}\|

\displaystyle\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-3.8889pt\hbox{$=$}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip-2.7431pt\raisebox{4.66875pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\Delta$}}\hskip-2.7431pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}1

\displaystyle\|s^{-}\|

\displaystyle\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-3.8889pt\hbox{$=$}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip-2.7431pt\raisebox{4.66875pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\Delta$}}\hskip-2.7431pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}1+\|s\|

\displaystyle\|s\cap u\|

\displaystyle\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-3.8889pt\hbox{$=$}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip-2.7431pt\raisebox{4.66875pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\Delta$}}\hskip-2.7431pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}1+\|s\|+\|u\|

\displaystyle\|s\cdot u\|

\displaystyle\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-3.8889pt\hbox{$=$}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip-2.7431pt\raisebox{4.66875pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\Delta$}}\hskip-2.7431pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}1+\|s\|+\|u\|

\displaystyle\|s^{\smile}\|

\displaystyle\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-3.8889pt\hbox{$=$}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip-2.7431pt\raisebox{4.66875pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\Delta$}}\hskip-2.7431pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}1+\|s\|

The
semantics

\llbracket t\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{A}}\subseteq|\mathfrak{A}|^{2}
of a term
t
over a structure
\mathfrak{A}
over
\Sigma
is defined by:

\displaystyle\llbracket a\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{A}}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-3.8889pt\hbox{$=$}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip-2.7431pt\raisebox{4.66875pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\Delta$}}\hskip-2.7431pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}\{\langle v,v^{\prime}\rangle\in|\mathfrak{A}|^{2}\mid\langle v,v^{\prime}\rangle\in a^{\mathfrak{A}}\}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-3.8889pt\hbox{$=$}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip-2.7431pt\raisebox{4.66875pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\Delta$}}\hskip-2.7431pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}\{\langle v,v^{\prime}\rangle\in|\mathfrak{A}|^{2}\mid v=v^{\prime}\}

\displaystyle\llbracket s^{-}\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{A}}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-3.8889pt\hbox{$=$}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip-2.7431pt\raisebox{4.66875pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\Delta$}}\hskip-2.7431pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}|\mathfrak{A}|^{2}\setminus\llbracket s\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{A}}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-3.8889pt\hbox{$=$}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip-2.7431pt\raisebox{4.66875pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\Delta$}}\hskip-2.7431pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}\llbracket s\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{A}}\cap\llbracket u\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{A}}

\displaystyle\mathfrak{A}\models\lnot\psi

\displaystyle\quad\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 5.0pt\hskip-5.0pt\hbox{$\iff$}\hskip-5.0pt\hskip-2.7431pt\raisebox{4.66875pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\Delta$}}\hskip-2.7431pt\hskip 5.0pt}}\quad\mbox{not ($\mathfrak{A}\models\psi$)}

\displaystyle\quad\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 5.0pt\hskip-5.0pt\hbox{$\iff$}\hskip-5.0pt\hskip-2.7431pt\raisebox{4.66875pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\Delta$}}\hskip-2.7431pt\hskip 5.0pt}}\quad(\mathfrak{A}\models\psi)\mbox{ and }(\mathfrak{A}\models\rho)

For a quantifier-free formula
\varphi

\mathfrak{A}\models\varphi

Similarly for
\mathrm{FO}_{=}

we say that a quantifier-free formula
\varphi
is
valid
(resp.

\mathfrak{A}\models\varphi

\mathfrak{A}

We say that two quantifier-free formulas
\varphi,\psi
are
semantically equivalent
if the quantifier-free formula
\varphi\leftrightarrow\psi
is valid.

It is also well-known that we can translate CoR quantifier-free formulas into CoR equations, preserving the semantic equivalence.

Proposition 2.4

(
Schröder-Tarski translation
theorem
[
1
]
)

There is a linear-size translation from a given quantifier-free formula
\varphi
in CoR into a term
t
such that
\varphi
and
(t=\top)
are semantically equivalent.

Proof 2.5

(The proof is from
[
1
]
.)

First, by using
(s=u)\leftrightarrow((s\cap u)\cup(s^{-}\cap u^{-})=\top)
,
we translate a given quantifier-free formula into a quantifier-free formula s.t. each equation is of the form
t=\top
.
Second, by using the following two semantic equivalences, we eliminate logical connectives:

\displaystyle\lnot(s=\top)

\displaystyle\leftrightarrow\top\cdot s^{-}\cdot\top=\top

\displaystyle(s=\top)\land(u=\top)

\displaystyle\leftrightarrow s\cap u=\top

We then have obtained the desired equation of the form
t=\top
.

\displaystyle=|\mathfrak{A}^{(k)}|^{2}\setminus\llbracket\mathrm{T}^{(k)}(\psi)\rrbracket

\displaystyle=|\mathfrak{A}^{(k)}|^{2}\setminus\{\langle\langle v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\rangle,\langle v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\rangle\rangle\mid\{x_{1}\mapsto v_{1},\dots,x_{k}\mapsto v_{k}\}\in\llbracket\psi\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{A}}\restriction X\}

(IH)

\displaystyle=\{\langle\langle v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\rangle,\langle v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\rangle\rangle\mid\{x_{1}\mapsto v_{1},\dots,x_{k}\mapsto v_{k}\}\not\in\llbracket\psi\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{A}}\restriction X\}

\displaystyle=\{\langle\langle v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\rangle,\langle v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\rangle\rangle\mid\{x_{1}\mapsto v_{1},\dots,x_{k}\mapsto v_{k}\}\in\llbracket\lnot\psi\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{A}}\restriction X\}

\displaystyle\llbracket\mathrm{T}^{(k)}(\psi)\cap\mathrm{T}^{(k)}(\rho)\rrbracket

\displaystyle=\{\langle\langle v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\rangle,\langle v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\rangle\rangle\mid\{x_{1}\mapsto v_{1},\dots,x_{k}\mapsto v_{k}\}\in(\llbracket\psi\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{A}}\restriction X)\cap(\llbracket\psi\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{A}}\restriction X)\}

(IH)

\displaystyle=\{\langle\langle v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\rangle,\langle v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\rangle\rangle\mid\{x_{1}\mapsto v_{1},\dots,x_{k}\mapsto v_{k}\}\in\llbracket\psi\land\rho\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{A}}\restriction X\}

\mathrm{T}^{(k)}(\varphi)=(Q_{i}\cdot\mathrm{T}^{(k)}(\psi)\cdot Q_{i}^{\smile})\cap\mathsf{I}

\displaystyle\llbracket(Q_{i}\cdot\mathrm{T}^{(k)}(\psi)\cdot Q_{i}^{\smile})\cap\mathsf{I}\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{A}^{(k)}}

\displaystyle=\{\langle v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\rangle,\langle v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\rangle\mid\langle\langle v_{1}^{\prime},\dots,v_{k}^{\prime}\rangle,\langle v_{1}^{\prime},\dots,v_{k}^{\prime}\rangle\rangle\in\llbracket\mathrm{T}^{(k)}(\psi)\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{A}^{(k)}}

\displaystyle\hskip 70.0001pt\mbox{ for some $v_{1}^{\prime},\dots,v_{k}^{\prime}\in|\mathfrak{A}|$ s.t.\ $v_{j}^{\prime}=v_{j}$ for $1\leq j\leq k$ s.t.\ $j\neq i$}\}

(note that

by IH)

\displaystyle=\{\langle v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\rangle,\langle v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\rangle\mid\{x_{1}\mapsto v_{1}^{\prime},\dots,x_{k}\mapsto v_{k}^{\prime}\}\in\llbracket\psi\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{A}^{(k)}}\restriction X

\displaystyle\hskip 70.0001pt\mbox{ for some $v_{1}^{\prime},\dots,v_{k}^{\prime}\in|\mathfrak{A}|$ s.t.\ $v_{j}^{\prime}=v_{j}$ for $1\leq j\leq k$ s.t.\ $j\neq i$}\}

Hence, this completes the proof.

By a similar argument as the

[

(see also the Plaisted-Greenbaum translation
[

\mathrm{FO2}_{=}

we can translate into CoR terms with bounded alternation of operations.

For each term
t
, we introduce a fresh variable
a_{t}

Then for a term
t
, we define the set of equations
\Gamma_{t}
as follows:

\displaystyle\Gamma_{b}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-3.8889pt\hbox{$=$}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip-2.7431pt\raisebox{4.66875pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\Delta$}}\hskip-2.7431pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}\{a_{b}=b\}

\displaystyle\Gamma_{s^{-}}

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{s}\cup\{a_{s^{-}}=a_{s}^{-}\}

\displaystyle\Gamma_{s\cap u}

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{s}\cup\Gamma_{u}\cup\{a_{s\cap u}=a_{s}\cap a_{u}\}

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\mathsf{I}}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-3.8889pt\hbox{$=$}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip-2.7431pt\raisebox{4.66875pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\Delta$}}\hskip-2.7431pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}\{a_{\mathsf{I}}=\mathsf{I}\}

\displaystyle\Gamma_{s^{\smile}}

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{s}\cup\{a_{s^{\smile}}=a_{s}^{\smile}\}

\displaystyle\Gamma_{s\cdot u}

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{s}\cup\Gamma_{u}\cup\{a_{s\cdot u}=a_{s}\cdot a_{u}\}

Then we have the following:

Lemma 4.1

For all CoR terms
t
, we have:

\mbox{$t=\top$ is [finitely] valid}\quad\iff\quad\mbox{$(\bigwedge\Gamma_{t})\to a_{t}=\top$ is [finitely] valid.}

Proof 4.2

For all structures
\mathfrak{A}
s.t.
\mathfrak{A}\models\bigwedge\Gamma_{t}
,
we have
\mathfrak{A}\models s=a_{s}
for all subterms

s

Thus it suffices to prove that
t=\top
is [finitely] valid
\iff

\bigwedge\Gamma_{t}\to t=\top
is [finitely] valid.
Both directions are shown as follows.

(
\Longrightarrow

Trivial.

(
\Longleftarrow

Since

is not occurring in

we can easily transform a structure

s.t.

Example 4.3

The equation
((b\cdot c)^{-}\cdot d)^{-}=\top
is translated into the following quantifier-free formula preserving validity and finite validity (we omit equations for each variables
b,c,d

\bigwedge\left\{\begin{gathered}a_{b\cdot c}=a_{b}\cdot a_{c},\quad a_{(b\cdot c)^{-}}=a_{b\cdot c}^{-},\\
a_{(b\cdot c)^{-}\cdot d}=a_{(b\cdot c)^{-}}\cdot a_{d},\quad a_{((b\cdot c)^{-}\cdot d)^{-}}=a_{(b\cdot c)^{-}\cdot d}^{-}\end{gathered}\right\}\to a_{((b\cdot c)^{-}\cdot d)^{-}}=\top

This is semantically equivalent to the following equation:

(\top\cdot\bigcup\left\{\begin{aligned} (a_{b\cdot c}\cap(a_{b}^{-}\dagger a_{c}^{-}))&,\ (a_{b\cdot c}^{-}\cap(a_{b}\cdot a_{c})),\\
(a_{(b\cdot c)^{-}}\cap a_{b\cdot c})&,\ (a_{(b\cdot c)^{-}}^{-}\cap a_{b\cdot c}^{-}),\\
(a_{(b\cdot c)^{-}\cdot d}\cap(a_{(b\cdot c)^{-}}^{-}\dagger a_{d}^{-}))&,\ (a_{(b\cdot c)^{-}\cdot d}^{-}\cap(a_{(b\cdot c)^{-}}\cdot a_{d})),\\
(a_{((b\cdot c)^{-}\cdot d)^{-}}\cap a_{(b\cdot c)^{-}\cdot d})&,\ (a_{((b\cdot c)^{-}\cdot d)^{-}}^{-}\cap a_{(b\cdot c)^{-}\cdot d}^{-})\end{aligned}\right\}\cdot\top)\cup a_{((b\cdot c)^{-}\cdot d)^{-}}=\top

By using the translation above (and replacing complemented variables
b^{-}
with fresh variables

and introducing the axiom

), we can translate each CoR equation without
\_^{\smile}
nor
\mathsf{I}
into an equation of the form

\displaystyle b\cap c\qquad b^{-}\cap c^{-}\qquad b\cap(c\dagger d)\qquad b\cap(c\cdot d)\qquad b\cap(c\cap d)

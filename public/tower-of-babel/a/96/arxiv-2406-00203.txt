Quantum phase transition of infrared radiation
Bartosz Biadasiewicz, Wojciech Dybalski

Abstract
We describe a phase transition of infrared radiation, driven by quantum fluctuations, which takes place at the boundary of (the conformal diagram of) Minkowski spacetime. Specifically, we consider a family of states interpolating between the vacuum and the Kraus-Polley-Reents infravacuum. A state from this family can be imagined as a static source emitting flashes of infrared radiation in distant past. The flashes are in suitable squeezed states and the time intervals between them are controlled by a certain parameter r r . For r < 0 r<0 the states are lightcone normal, thus physically indistinguishable from local excitations of the vacuum. They suffer from the usual infrared problems such as disintegration of the Bloch-Nordsieck S S -matrix and rotational symmetry breaking by soft photon clouds. However, for r > 0 r>0 lightcone normality breaks down, the S S -matrix is stabilized by the Kraus-Polley-Reents mechanism and the rotational symmetry is restored. We interpret these two situations as ordered ( r < 0 r<0 ) and disordered ( r > 0 r>0 ) phase of infrared radiation, and show that they can be distinguished by asymptotic fluctuations of the fields. We also determine the singular behaviour of some S S -matrix elements near the critical point r = 0 r=0 .



Keywords:
Phase transition, infrared problems, symmetry breaking.

1.2
Spontaneous symmetry breaking

The transition from the disordered to the ordered phase is typically accompanied by spontaneous symmetry breaking.
This phenomenon can be concisely described in the language of algebraic QFT. This exact mathematical apparatus, proposed long time ago by Haag and Kastler
[

,
Ha

[

\mathfrak{A}
the

1.3
Correlation length

Apart from constructing the Bloch-Nordsieck
S
-matrix

in the disordered phase,
we also obtain detailed information about the behaviour of its matrix elements near the critical point. Specifically, we obtain

\displaystyle\langle\Omega,S_{r}\Omega\rangle\sim\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0&\textrm{for\quad\quad\ \!\!\! {\color[rgb]{0,0,0}$r\leq 0$},}\\
\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{c}{r^{3/2}}}&\textrm{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}for $0<r\leq 1$.}\end{array}\right.

To explain the first line of (

) let us first consider the vacuum case

. Here the standard computation with an infrared
cut-off

gives

\displaystyle\langle\Omega,S_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}r=-\infty}}^{\sigma}\Omega\rangle\sim\frac{\sigma}{\Lambda},

(1.21)

where

is a fixed UV scale introduced by

. This expression vanishes as
\sigma\to 0
and the same is true in the ordered phase
(
r<0
)
, as we show in Theorem

The second line of (

), which we verify in Section

, means, more precisely, that
C_{1}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{c_{1}}{r^{3/2}}}\leq\langle\Omega,S_{r}\Omega\rangle\leq C_{2}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{c_{2}}{r^{3/2}}}

. Consequently
\lim_{r\to{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}0^{+}}}\langle\Omega,S_{r}\Omega\rangle=0
, thus the dependence

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\xi:=}\langle\Omega,S_{r}\Omega\rangle^{-1}
as the correlation length of our phase transition: All photons of larger wavelengths, emitted by the external source

of correlations is not expected. We observe from (

1.4
Phase transition in the future lightcone

Even if
\{\omega_{r}\}_{r\in\mathbb{R}}
tend
s
to the vacuum as

, one can ask how exotic these states
are for small

with tip at zero. Let us denote by

-algebra generated by operators

, where

is quasi-equivalent to the vacuum representation

. In a shorthand notation, we prove that

\displaystyle\pi_{r}|_{\mathfrak{A}(V_{+})}\approx\pi_{\mathrm{vac}}|_{\mathfrak{A}(V_{+})}\quad\textrm{for}\quad r<0.

(1.22)

This property of
lightcone normality
means physically that the observer cannot decide by experiments inside the lightcone if the radiation is in a state from the familiar vacuum sector,
or in the state
\omega_{r}
, on a verge of the phase transition.
In fact, by Definition
6.2
below, the observer can describe the radiation by
a density matrix from the vacuum sector.

As shown in
[

]
, the relevant automorphisms

\displaystyle\pi_{\mathrm{vac}}\circ\alpha|_{\mathfrak{A}(V_{+})}\simeq\pi_{\mathrm{vac}}|_{\mathfrak{A}(V_{+})}.

By combining this with (

), we note that these automorphisms are unitarily implemented on

also in the ordered phase, perhaps

So one may ask, if the phase transition can actually be observed in

are measurable in

\displaystyle\pi_{r}|_{\mathfrak{A}(V_{+})}\not\approx\pi_{\mathrm{vac}}|_{\mathfrak{A}(V_{+})}\quad\textrm{for}\quad{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}r\geq 0}

\zeta_{\beta}(\boldsymbol{k}):=\zeta^{\circ}_{i}(|\boldsymbol{k}|)Y_{\ell m}(\hat{\boldsymbol{k}})
for

, where

are the real valued spherical harmonics.

As explained in Subsection

, we want to create flashes of radiation at times
-\tau^{(r)}_{\beta}:=-\tau^{(r)}_{i}

defined in (
1.1
). Thus we introduce the time-translated KPR-modes

\displaystyle\zeta^{(r)}_{\beta}(\boldsymbol{k}):=\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}|\boldsymbol{k}|\tau^{(r)}_{\beta}}\zeta_{\beta}(\boldsymbol{k}).

(2.2)

A family of
n
flashes of radiation, as considered in Subsection

, is described by the following squeezed state of radiation

\displaystyle\Psi^{(r)}_{n}=\prod_{\beta\in I_{n}}\exp\bigg(-\frac{1}{\hat{b}_{\beta}}a^{*}(\zeta^{(r)}_{\beta})^{2}\bigg)\Omega,

(2.3)

where
\hat{b}_{\beta}:=\frac{b_{i}^{2}+1}{b_{i}^{2}-1}
and we omitted normalization
4
4

4

For

i=1

. Now the states

\displaystyle\frac{\Psi^{(r)}_{n}}{\|\Psi^{(r)}_{n}\|}=\mathcal{U}_{r}(I_{n})\Omega:=\prod_{\beta\in I_{n}}\bigg(\frac{z_{\beta}}{2\pi}\bigg)^{1/2}\int ds_{\beta}\,\mathrm{e}^{-{\frac{1}{4}}(\hat{b}_{\beta}-1)s_{\beta}^{2}}W(\mathrm{i}s_{\beta}\zeta^{(r)}_{\beta})\Omega,

where

the Weyl operators. Inserting this identity

\displaystyle W(\zeta)W(\zeta^{\prime})=\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{i}\mathrm{Im}\langle\zeta,\zeta^{\prime}\rangle}W(\zeta+\zeta^{\prime}),\quad\langle\Omega,W(\zeta)\Omega\rangle=\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{4}\|\zeta\|_{2}^{2}},

\displaystyle\omega_{r}(W(F))=\langle\Omega,W(T_{r}{}F)\Omega\rangle.

Here

\displaystyle T_{r}{}=1+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(b_{i}^{-1}-1)u_{-\tau^{(r)}_{i}}Q_{i}\mathrm{Re}\,u_{\tau^{(r)}_{i}}+\mathrm{i}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(b_{i}-1)u_{-\tau^{(r)}_{i}}Q_{i}\mathrm{Im}\,u_{\tau^{(r)}_{i}},

In the representation (

From formulas (

3
The existence of the
S
-matrix in the disordered phase

The main technical result of this section is the following:

Theorem 3.1
.

T_{r}{}g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
for all
g
as in (
1.14
) and
r>0
.

Here we wrote
g:=g^{\mathrm{out}}
for brevity, and to stress that the general form of these functions is the same in the ‘in’ and ‘out’ case.
Since
g
is not in
\mathcal{L}
and not even in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
due to a singularity at
\boldsymbol{k}=0
, the operation
T_{r}{}g
requires clarification:
We introduce an intermediate infrared cut-off
\sigma>0
and set
g_{\sigma}(\boldsymbol{k}):=\chi_{[\sigma,\infty)}(|\boldsymbol{k}|)g(\boldsymbol{k})
, where
\chi
is the characteristic function.
Then, more precisely, Theorem
3.1
requires that the limit
T_{r}{}g:=\lim_{\sigma\to 0}T_{r}{}\,g_{\sigma}
exists in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
.

Before we prove Theorem
3.1
, let us indicate its relevance to the problem of the existence of the Bloch-Nordsieck
S
-matrix discussed in Subsection
1.2
. By considering relation (
1.15
) in representation
\pi_{r}
we obtain

\displaystyle\pi_{r}\circ\alpha(W(F))

\displaystyle\!\!\!=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\mathrm{Im}\langle g^{\mathrm{out}}-g^{\mathrm{in}},F\rangle}\pi_{r}(W(F))

(3.1)

\displaystyle\!\!\!=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\mathrm{Im}\langle T_{r}{}(g^{\mathrm{out}}-g^{\mathrm{in}}),T_{r}{}F\rangle}\pi_{r}(W(F))

\displaystyle\!\!\!=

\displaystyle\!\!\!W(T_{r}{}(g^{\mathrm{out}}-g^{\mathrm{in}}))\pi_{r}(W(F))W(T_{r}{}(g^{\mathrm{out}}-g^{\mathrm{in}}))^{*},

where in the second step we used that
T_{r}{}
is symplectic and in the last step we exploited the Weyl relations (
2.6
). Thus, by Theorem
3.1
,
the
S
-matrix
S_{r}:=W(T_{r}{}(g^{\mathrm{out}}-g^{\mathrm{in}}))
is well defined. This is how the
S
-matrix is stabilized by the KPR mechanism.
Combining (
3.1
) with (
2.11
) we also obtain

\displaystyle\pi_{r}\circ\alpha\circ\alpha_{\mathcal{R}}(W(F))=S_{r}U_{\mathcal{R}}S_{r}^{*}\,\pi_{r}\circ\alpha(W(F))\,S_{r}U_{\mathcal{R}}^{*}S_{r}^{*},

(3.2)

which confirms (
1.17
).

The first step of the proof of Theorem
3.1
is to check the case
r=\infty
. Then the time translations disappear in (
2.9
) and the
maps
T_{r}{}
become simply the KPR maps
T
. Noting that
g
is purely imaginary, we have

\displaystyle Tg=(1+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(b_{i}-1)Q_{i})g.

(3.3)

The somewhat intricate definition of the KPR states and maps from
[
KPR77
]
is motivated precisely by the requirement that the above
vector is in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. As there is a detailed modern account in
[
CD19
, Proposition 4.2]
, we can be brief here. We can replace
g
from (
1.14
)
with

\displaystyle g^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{k}):=\mathrm{i}q\chi_{[0,1]}(|\boldsymbol{k}|)\,\frac{1}{|\boldsymbol{k}|^{3/2}(1-\hat{\boldsymbol{k}}\cdot\boldsymbol{v})},

(3.4)

where
q
appeared below (
1.2
), since the difference is an infrared regular vector from
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. We have

\displaystyle Tg^{\prime}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}b_{i}Q_{i}g^{\prime}+(g^{\prime}-\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}Q_{i}g^{\prime}).

(3.5)

One can show that the first term on the r.h.s. is in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
using the fact that
b_{i}\to 0
.
As for the second term, we exploit that
g^{\prime}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(|\zeta^{\circ}_{i}\rangle\langle\zeta^{\circ}_{i}|\otimes 1)g^{\prime}

thanks to
\chi_{[0,1]}
in (
3.4
)
. Thus we obtain

\displaystyle(g^{\prime}-\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}Q_{i}g^{\prime})=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\ell>i}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}(|\zeta^{\circ}_{i}\rangle\langle\zeta^{\circ}_{i}|\otimes|Y_{\ell m}\rangle\langle Y_{\ell m}|)g^{\prime}.

(3.6)

Now using
\langle Y_{\ell m},g^{\prime}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\ell(\ell+1)}}\langle Y_{\ell m},\mathrm{L}^{2}g^{\prime}\rangle
and
\ell>i
we
get

sufficiently strong convergence of the sum to conclude that (
3.6
) is also in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. This gives Theorem
3.1
for
r=\infty
.

In order to prove Theorem
3.1
for arbitrary
r>0
, we
come back to definition
(
2.9
) of
T_{r}{}
. Recalling that
g
as in (
1.14
)
is purely imaginary, we have

\displaystyle T_{r}{}g

\displaystyle\!\!\!=

\displaystyle\!\!\!g+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(b_{i}^{-1}-1)u_{-\tau^{(r)}_{i}}Q_{i}(\mathrm{i}\,\sin(\mu\tau^{(r)}_{i})g)+\mathrm{i}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(b_{i}-1)u_{-\tau^{(r)}_{i}}Q_{i}(-\mathrm{i}\cos(\mu\tau^{(r)}_{i})g).

(3.7)

Exploiting (
3.3
), we can write

\displaystyle T_{r}{}g

\displaystyle\!\!\!=

\displaystyle\!\!\!(T_{r}{}-T)g+Tg

(3.8)

\displaystyle\!\!\!=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(b_{i}^{-1}-1)u_{-\tau^{(r)}_{i}}Q_{i}(\mathrm{i}\,\sin(\mu\tau^{(r)}_{i})g)+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(b_{i}-1)[u_{-\tau^{(r)}_{i}}Q_{i}(\cos(\mu\tau^{(r)}_{i})-1)g]

\displaystyle\!\!\!\phantom{444444444444444444444444444444}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}+}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(b_{i}-1)(u_{-\tau^{(r)}_{i}}-1)Q_{i}g+Tg.

(3.9)

We know already that
Tg\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, so it suffices to consider the remaining terms. In the case of (

it is clear that
\sin(\mu\tau^{(r)}_{i})\sim\mu\tau^{(r)}_{i}
and
\cos(\mu\tau^{(r)}_{i})-1\sim(\mu\tau^{(r)}_{i})^{2}
have a regularizing effect
on the
\mu^{-3/2}
singularity of
g
. We note the estimates
on the

-norms
for

\displaystyle\|Q_{i}(\mu\tau^{(r)}_{i})^{l}g\|_{2}\leq(\tau^{(r)}_{i})^{l}\|\chi_{[\varepsilon_{i+1},\varepsilon_{i}]}(\mu)\mu^{l}g\|_{2}\leq c(\tau^{(r)}_{i}\varepsilon_{i})^{l}=c2^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}-r(i-1)l}}

for a constant

of the sums in (

for

.

The regularizing effect of

in (

.
We argue as follows: On the one hand,

\displaystyle\|(u_{-\tau^{(r)}_{i}}-1)Q_{i}\|\leq\tau^{(r)}_{i}\|\mu Q_{i}\|\leq\tau^{(r)}_{i}\|\mu\zeta^{\circ}_{i}\|_{2}\leq c\tau^{(r)}_{i}\varepsilon_{i}=c2^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}-r(i-1)}},

where

On the other hand

\displaystyle\|Q_{i}g\|_{2}\leq\|\chi_{[\varepsilon_{i+1},\varepsilon_{i}]}(\mu)g\|_{2}\leq c.

(3.12)

Using (

.

4
Non-existence of the
S
-matrix in the ordered phase and at the critical point

The main technical result of this section is the following theorem, which relies on the assumption
0\neq q:=\int d^{3}\boldsymbol{x}\,j_{0}(\boldsymbol{x})
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
r\leq 0
. Then
\lim_{\sigma\to 0}\|T_{r}{}(g^{\mathrm{out}}_{\sigma}-g^{\mathrm{in}}_{\sigma})\|_{2}=\infty
for all
g^{\mathrm{in}/\mathrm{out}}
as in (
1.14
) and
\boldsymbol{v}^{\mathrm{in}}\neq\boldsymbol{v}^{\mathrm{out}}
.

Let us first indicate, following
[
Ro70
]
, how the property from Theorem
4.1
prevents the existence of the
S
-matrix.
Coming back to computation (
3.1
), suppose by contradiction that there exists a unitary operator
S_{r}
such that

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\mathrm{Im}\langle T_{r}{}(g^{\mathrm{out}}-g^{\mathrm{in}}),T_{r}{}F\rangle}\pi_{r}(W(F))=S_{r}\pi_{r}(W(F))S_{r}^{*}.

(4.1)

By evaluating both sides on
\langle\Omega,\,\cdot\,\,\Omega\rangle
and using (
2.6
), (
2.8
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\mathrm{Im}\langle T_{r}{}(g^{\mathrm{out}}-g^{\mathrm{in}}),T_{r}{}F\rangle}=\mathrm{e}^{\frac{1}{4}\|T_{r}{}F\|_{2}}\langle\Omega,S_{r}W(T_{r}{}F)S_{r}^{*}\Omega\rangle.

(4.2)

Treating both sides as functions of
T_{r}{}F
we note that the l.h.s. is discontinuous in the topology of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
by Theorem
4.1

and the Schur lemma. However, the r.h.s. is continuous by the continuity of the maps
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\ni\zeta\mapsto W(\zeta)\Psi
,
\Psi\in\mathcal{F}
, between Hilbert spaces. Using that
T_{r}{}:\mathcal{L}\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
has a dense range (cf.
[
Ku98
, Lemma 3.3]
) we obtain a contradiction.

By a similar token we obtain the rotational symmetry breaking in the ordered phase. Let
\alpha_{\boldsymbol{v}}
be the automorphism
\alpha
corresponding to
g
given by (
1.14
)
with
\boldsymbol{v}:=\boldsymbol{v}^{\mathrm{out}}
. Let us assume for simplicity that
\boldsymbol{v}^{\mathrm{in}}=0
. Now suppose that there is a unitary
V_{\mathcal{R}}
such that

\displaystyle\pi_{r}\circ\alpha_{\boldsymbol{v}}\circ\alpha_{0}^{-1}\circ\alpha_{\mathcal{R}}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}A})=V_{\mathcal{R}}\pi_{r}\circ\alpha_{\boldsymbol{v}}\circ\alpha_{0}^{-1}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}A})V_{\mathcal{R}}^{*},\quad{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}A\in\mathfrak{A}}.

(4.3)

Then, by exploiting (
2.11
), noting
\alpha_{\mathcal{R}^{-1}}\circ\alpha_{\boldsymbol{v}}\circ\alpha_{\mathcal{R}}=\alpha_{\mathcal{R}\boldsymbol{v}}

and redefining

\alpha_{0}^{-1}(A)\to A
, we obtain

\displaystyle\pi_{r}\circ\alpha_{\mathcal{R}\boldsymbol{v}}\circ\alpha_{\boldsymbol{v}}^{-1}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}A})=U_{\mathcal{R}}^{*}V_{\mathcal{R}}\pi_{r}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}A})V_{\mathcal{R}}^{*}U_{\mathcal{R}},\quad{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}A\in\mathfrak{A}}.

(4.4)

By evaluating this relation on a Weyl operator we obtain an identity which is analogous to (
4.1
). Thus we arrive at
a contradiction which confirms (
1.16
).

Let us now move on to the proof of Theorem
4.1
. As in the proof of Theorem
3.1
, we can replace
g^{\mathrm{in}/\mathrm{out}}
with

g^{\prime}{}^{\mathrm{in}/\mathrm{out}}
which have the factor
\tilde{j}_{0}(\boldsymbol{k})
replaced with
q\chi_{[0,1]}(\boldsymbol{k})
as the differences are infrared regular vectors in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
.
We set
g=g^{\prime}{}^{\mathrm{out}}-g^{\prime}{}^{\mathrm{in}}
and compute
\|T_{r}{}g_{\sigma}\|_{2}
using (
3.7
)

\displaystyle\|T_{r}{}g_{\sigma}\|_{2}=\bigg\|\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}u_{-\tau^{(r)}_{i}}\bigg\{u_{\tau^{(r)}_{i}}\chi_{\Delta_{i}}g_{\sigma}+(b_{i}^{-1}-1)Q_{i}(\mathrm{i}\,\sin(\mu\tau^{(r)}_{i})g_{\sigma})+\mathrm{i}(b_{i}-1)Q_{i}(-\mathrm{i}\cos(\mu\tau^{(r)}_{i})g_{\sigma})\bigg\}\bigg\|_{2},\,\,

(4.5)

where
\chi_{\Delta_{i}}
are characteristic functions of the concentric shells
\Delta_{i}:=\{\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\,|\,\varepsilon_{i+1}\leq|\boldsymbol{k}|\leq\varepsilon_{i}\,\}
.
Since the vectors in curly brackets are supported in distinct shells, we can drop the action of
u_{-\tau^{(r)}_{i}}
under the norm. Decomposing
these vectors into real and imaginary parts, we obtain a lower bound

\displaystyle\|T_{r}{}g_{\sigma}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\!\!\!\geq

\displaystyle\!\!\!\bigg\|\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\bigg\{\mathrm{i}\sin(\mu\tau^{(r)}_{i})\chi_{\Delta_{i}}g_{\sigma}+(b_{i}^{-1}-1)Q_{i}(\mathrm{i}\,\sin(\mu\tau^{(r)}_{i})g_{\sigma})\bigg\}\bigg\|_{2}.

(4.6)

Hence, noting that the cut-off
\sigma
truncates the sum to some finite
n(\sigma)
, such that
\lim_{\sigma\to 0}n(\sigma)\to\infty
, we have

\displaystyle\|T_{r}{}g_{\sigma}\|^{2}_{2}

\displaystyle\!\!\!\geq

\displaystyle\!\!\!\sum_{i=1}^{n(\sigma)}\langle g_{\sigma},\chi_{\Delta_{i}}\sin^{2}(\mu\tau^{(r)}_{i})g_{\sigma}\rangle+\sum_{i=1}^{n(\sigma)}(b_{i}^{-2}-1)\langle g_{\sigma},\sin(\mu\tau^{(r)}_{i})Q_{i}\sin(\mu\tau^{(r)}_{i})g_{\sigma}\rangle.

(4.7)

Since both sums above are positive, it suffices to show that the first of them blows up in the limit
\sigma\to 0
.
For
r<0
we have

\displaystyle\langle g_{\sigma},\chi_{\Delta_{i}}\sin^{2}(\mu\tau^{(r)}_{i})g_{\sigma}\rangle

\displaystyle\!\!\!=

\displaystyle\!\!\!c\int_{\varepsilon_{i+1}}^{\varepsilon_{i}}\frac{d|\boldsymbol{k}|}{|\boldsymbol{k}|}\sin^{2}(2^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}-r}(i-1)}|\boldsymbol{k}|/\varepsilon_{i})

(4.8)

\displaystyle\!\!\!=

\displaystyle\!\!\!c\int_{1/2}^{1}\frac{d|\boldsymbol{k}|}{|\boldsymbol{k}|}\sin^{2}(2^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}-r}(i-1)}|\boldsymbol{k}|)\underset{i\to\infty}{\to}c\int_{1/2}^{1}\frac{d|\boldsymbol{k}|}{2|\boldsymbol{k}|}.

Here
c>0
is an inessential factor coming from angular integration and the last step follows from

\sin^{2}(x)=\frac{1}{2}(1-\cos(2x))
and the Riemann-Lebesgue lemma. For
r=0
the expression on the l.h.s. of (
4.8
) is independent of
i
and non-zero. Thus the sum in (
4.7
) diverges
as
\sigma\to 0
for
r\leq 0
. This concludes the proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Recall that

denotes the Bloch-Norsieck

For this purpose we obtain
from (

)

\displaystyle\langle\Omega,S_{r}\Omega\rangle=\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{4}\|T_{r}{}g\|^{2}_{2}}

(5.1)

and recall that the quantity

was the subject of our interest in Sections
3
,
4
.
(We allow a modification
of

as in (

In particular,
formula (

\displaystyle\|T_{r}{}g\|_{2}

\displaystyle\!\!\!{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\leq}

\displaystyle\!\!\!{\|\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(b_{i}^{-1}-1)Q_{i}(\mathrm{i}\,\sin(\mu\tau^{(r)}_{i})g)\|_{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}|b_{i}-1|\|Q_{i}(\cos(\mu\tau^{(r)}_{i})-1)g\|_{2}}

(5.2)

\displaystyle\!\!\!\phantom{4444444444444444444444444444}{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}+}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}|b_{i}-1|\|(u_{-\tau^{(r)}_{i}}-1)Q_{i}g\|_{2}+\|Tg\|_{2}}

Now we are looking for a lower bound for this quantity. We observe, that in the reasoning (

)

\displaystyle\|T_{r}{}g\|^{2}_{2}

\displaystyle\!\!\!\geq

\displaystyle\!\!\!\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(b_{i}^{-2}-1)\langle g,\sin(\mu\tau^{(r)}_{i})Q_{i}\sin(\mu\tau^{(r)}_{i})g\rangle.

(5.4)

We used here the fact that we are in the disordered phase and the bounds (

)-(

)
allow us
to take the limit

.
Next, we compute

\displaystyle\langle g,\sin(\mu\tau^{(r)}_{i})Q_{i}\sin(\mu\tau^{(r)}_{i})g\rangle

\displaystyle\!\!\!\geq

\displaystyle\!\!\!c_{i}(2^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}-2r}})^{i-1}\bigg|\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{1}d|\boldsymbol{k}|\frac{\sin(|\boldsymbol{k}|(2^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}-r}})^{i-1})}{|\boldsymbol{k}|(2^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}-r}})^{i-1}}\bigg|^{2}

\displaystyle\!\!\!\geq

\displaystyle\!\!\!c_{i}(2^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}-2r}})^{i-1}\frac{1}{16},

where in the last step we used

for

The constants

arise from angular integration. As they have the form
\langle g_{\mathrm{ang}},\tilde{Q}_{i}g_{\mathrm{ang}}\rangle

g

\tilde{Q}_{i}\nearrow 1_{L^{2}(S^{2})}
is an increasing family of projections, we know
that
c_{i}\geq c_{i_{0}}>0
for some finite
i_{0}
. On the other hand, first few

may vanish for certain choices of
\boldsymbol{v}^{\mathrm{in}}\neq\boldsymbol{v}^{\mathrm{out}}
.
Coming back to (

), we can write

\displaystyle\|T_{r}{}g\|^{2}_{2}

\displaystyle\!\!\!\geq

\displaystyle\!\!\!\frac{c_{i_{0}}}{16}\sum_{i=i_{0}}^{\infty}(i^{2}-1)(2^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}-2r}})^{i-1}\geq\frac{c_{i_{0}}}{16}\frac{2\mathrm{e}^{-2a}}{(1-\mathrm{e}^{-a})^{3}}\mathrm{e}^{-(i_{0}-1)a}|_{a=2|r|\log(2)}\geq\frac{c}{r^{3}}.

(5.6)

Thus we have verified (

6
The absence of lightcone normality in the disordered phase and at the critical point

The main result of this section is the following:

Theorem 6.1
.

The representations
\pi_{r}
are not lightcone normal for
r\geq 0
.

The breakdown of lightcone normality in the disordered phase
and at the critical point
, we alluded to in formula (
1.24
),
is driven by infinite fluctuations of the timelike order parameter
\phi_{R}(f)
, which we stated in (
1.1
).
For future convenience we note the relation
\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\phi_{R}(f)}=W(F_{R})
,
where

\displaystyle F_{R}(\boldsymbol{k}):=R^{3/2}F(\boldsymbol{k}R),\quad F(\boldsymbol{k})=(2\pi)^{-3/2}|\boldsymbol{k}|^{-1/2}\int\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}|\boldsymbol{k}|t-\mathrm{i}\boldsymbol{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{x}}f(t,\boldsymbol{x})dt{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}d^{3}\boldsymbol{x}}

(6.1)

and
f
is supported in the future lightcone
V_{+}
.
Using this notation, we can write

\displaystyle\langle\Omega,\pi_{r}(W(F_{R}))\Omega\rangle=\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{4}\|T_{r}{}F_{R}\|^{2}_{2}}=\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{4}\omega_{r}(\phi_{R}(f)\phi_{R}(f))}

(6.2)

and the last expression tends to zero if timelike fluctuations of the order parameter tend to infinity with
R
.
By Lemma
6.3
below, this property prevents lightcone normality of the representation.

In Subsection
1.4
we defined the property of lightcone normality of a given representation
\pi
as
quasi-equivalence of
\pi
and
\pi_{\mathrm{vac}}
on
\mathfrak{A}(V_{+})

that is
unitary equivalence up to multiplicity.
In this section it is more convenient to use a different (but equivalent) definition
[
BR
, Section 2.4.4.]
:

Definition 6.2
.

If
\pi
is a representation of a
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathfrak{A}
then a state
\omega
of
\mathfrak{A}

is said to be
\pi
-normal if there exists a density matrix
\rho
such that

\displaystyle\omega(A)=\mathrm{Tr}(\rho\pi(A))

(6.3)

for all
A\in\mathfrak{A}
. Two representations
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
of
\mathfrak{A}
are said to be
quasi-equivalent, written
\pi_{1}\approx\pi_{2}
, if each
\pi_{1}
-normal state
is
\pi_{2}
-normal
and conversely.

The following lemma, which is an instance of the method of central sequences,
gives a useful criterion to exclude lightcone normality.
The irreducibility of
\pi_{r}
, which is
stated
as an assumption, is a consequence of the dense range of
T_{r}{}
. For the latter property we refer to

[
Ku98
, Lemma 3.3]
.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
\pi:\mathfrak{A}\to B(\mathcal{F})
be an irreducible representation. Suppose that the following limit exists (possibly along a subsequence) and

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\langle\Omega,\pi(W(F_{R}))\Omega\rangle\neq\langle\Omega,W(F)\Omega\rangle.

(6.4)

Then
\pi
is not lightcone normal.

Proof.
Suppose, by contradiction, that
\pi
is lightcone normal. Then
\omega_{\mathrm{vac}}
is
\pi
-normal,
hence there exists a density matrix
\rho
such that

\displaystyle\langle\Omega,W(F)\Omega\rangle=\langle\Omega,W(F_{R})\Omega\rangle=\mathrm{Tr}\big(\rho\,\pi(W(F_{R}))\big)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}p_{j}\langle\Psi_{j},\pi(W(F_{R}))\Psi_{j}\rangle,

(6.5)

where we expressed
\rho=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}p_{j}|\Psi_{j}\rangle\langle\Psi_{j}|
via an orthonormal system
\{\Psi_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathcal{F}
and
\{p_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}

is a sequence of positive numbers, summable to one.
Now we can write
\Psi_{j}=U_{j}\Omega
for some unitaries
U_{j}\in\pi(\mathfrak{A})
by the irreducibility assumption and the Kadison transitivity theorem
[
KR
, Theorem 10.2.1]
.
We observe that the localization region of
W(F_{R})=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\phi_{R}(f)}
is shifted to timelike infinity as
R\to\infty
. Consequently, by the Huyghens principle, i.e.
commutation of the massless scalar free field at timelike separation of arguments,
\lim_{R\to\infty}[W(F_{R}),A]=0
in norm for any
A\in\mathfrak{A}
. This gives

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\langle\Psi_{j},\pi(W(F_{R}))\Psi_{j}\rangle=\lim_{R\to\infty}\langle\Omega,\pi(W(F_{R}))\Omega\rangle.

(6.6)

Now by taking the limit
R\to\infty
in (
6.5
) and using (
6.4
), we conclude the proof.
\Box

Let us now prove Theorem
6.1
using Lemma
6.3
. Due to (
6.2
) it suffices to show that

\lim_{R\to\infty}\|T_{r}{}F_{R}\|_{2}=\infty
along a subsequence. Coming back to representation (
2.9
), we can write

\displaystyle\|T_{r}{}F_{R}\|_{2}\geq\|\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(b_{i}^{-1}-1)u_{-\tau^{(r)}_{i}}Q_{i}\mathrm{Re}\,u_{\tau^{(r)}_{i}}F_{R}\|_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}2}}+O(1).

(6.7)

Here

O(1)
denotes error terms which are manifestly bounded in
R
, because the respective operators are bounded and

\|F_{R}\|_{2}
is independent of
R
.
Thus it suffices to show that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(b_{i}^{-1}-1)^{2}\langle\mathrm{Re}(u_{\tau^{(r)}_{i}}F_{R_{n}}),Q_{i}\mathrm{Re}(u_{\tau^{(r)}_{i}}F_{R_{n}})\rangle=\infty,

(6.8)

for some subsequence

, tending to infinity.
As we are interested in a bound from below, it suffices to consider the

-th term in the sum over

and the

term
in the sum over the spherical harmonics hidden in
Q_{i}
. Let us pick in (

)

, where

is sufficiently large depending on the support of

, so that

is supported inside the future lightcone.
Furthermore, we choose
\eta
spherically symmetric and such that
\tilde{\eta}
is positive.
The resulting expression reads

\displaystyle(b_{n}^{-1}-1)^{2}|\langle\zeta^{\circ}_{n},\mathrm{Re}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}u_{\tau^{(r)}_{i}}}F_{R_{n}})\rangle|^{2}=c_{0}^{2}(b_{n}^{-1}-1)^{2}\bigg(\int_{\varepsilon_{n+1}R_{n}}^{\varepsilon_{n}R_{n}}d|\tilde{\boldsymbol{k}}|\,\tilde{\eta}(|\tilde{\boldsymbol{k}}|)\cos(|\tilde{\boldsymbol{k}}|R_{n}^{-1}(2^{-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(n-1)}|r|}\varepsilon^{-1}_{n}+\tau))\bigg)^{2}.\,\,

(6.9)

Now we choose
R_{n}:=L/\varepsilon_{n}
for some

. First, suppose that
r>0
and compute

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\varepsilon_{n+1}R_{n}}^{\varepsilon_{n}R_{n}}d|\tilde{\boldsymbol{k}}|\,\tilde{\eta}(\tilde{\boldsymbol{k}})\cos(|\tilde{\boldsymbol{k}}|R_{n}^{-1}(2^{-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(n-1)}|r|}\varepsilon^{-1}_{n}+\tau))=\int_{L/2}^{L}d|\tilde{\boldsymbol{k}}|\,\tilde{\eta}(\tilde{\boldsymbol{k}}),

(6.10)

which is strictly positive. Since

, we obtain (
6.8
). Next, suppose

. Then the integral in (

) reads

\displaystyle\int_{L/2}^{L}d|\tilde{\boldsymbol{k}}|\,\tilde{\eta}(\tilde{\boldsymbol{k}})\cos(|\tilde{\boldsymbol{k}}|/L),

(6.11)

which is also strictly positive. Thus we conclude the proof of Theorem
6.1
.

As we anticipated in (
6.2
), the timelike order parameter diverges
to infinity in the disordered phase and at the critical point. Actually the same is true for the spacelike order parameter.
In fact,

the discussion (

)–(

) above can be immediately adapted to the

and the support of

outside zero. Thus we have verified the second line in (

).

Interestingly
,

.
Coming back to (

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\mathrm{Im}\langle g^{\mathrm{out}}-g^{\mathrm{in}},F_{R}\rangle}\pi_{r}(W(F_{R}))

\displaystyle\!\!\!=

\displaystyle\!\!\!S_{r}\pi_{r}(W(F_{R}))S_{r}^{*},

for a unitary

. We choose here

as in (

We compute

\displaystyle\mathrm{Im}\langle g^{\mathrm{out}}-g^{\mathrm{in}},F_{R}\rangle\underset{R\to\infty}{\to}\tilde{j}_{0}(0)\int{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}d^{3}\boldsymbol{k}}\,\frac{\mathrm{Re}(F(\boldsymbol{k}))}{|\boldsymbol{k}|^{3/2}}\bigg\{\frac{1}{(1-\hat{\boldsymbol{k}}\cdot\boldsymbol{v}^{\mathrm{out}})}-\frac{1}{(1-\hat{\boldsymbol{k}}\cdot\boldsymbol{v}^{\mathrm{in}})}\bigg\},

(6.13)

which can easily be arranged to be different from zero (and multiples of

.
Now arguing as in the proof of Lemma

Unfortunately, such a general argument fails for fluctuations of the timelike order parameter, as (

inevitably zero in this case

]
.
(Unlike the spacelike case,

. Thus for the divergence of the fluctuations of the field in future timelike directions we have to rely on

7

In this section we prove the following theorem:

.

The representations

Our proof is inspired by
[

Now by the Weyl relations and the definition of the vacuum (

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{U}_{r}(I)\Omega,W(F)\mathcal{U}_{r}(I)\Omega\rangle

\displaystyle|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{1}{4}[s_{\beta}s_{\gamma}\mathrm{q}_{\beta,\gamma}+t_{\beta}t_{\gamma}\mathrm{p}_{\beta,\gamma}-2s_{\beta}t_{\gamma}\mathrm{r}_{\beta,\gamma}]}-1|\leq{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|d\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\mathrm{e}^{3\sum_{\beta}(|t_{\beta}|^{2}+|s_{\beta}|^{2})\|d_{\beta}\|_{2}},

and assumed that
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|d\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\leq 1

8
Order parameters in the ordered phase

In this section we determine the timelike and spacelike order parameters in the ordered phase.
Thus we

Since the representation is equivalent to the vacuum, it is not surprising that the timelike asymptotic fluctuations of the field take the vacuum value.

9
Conclusion and outlook

In this paper we described a family of states
\{\omega_{r}\}_{r\in\mathbb{R}}
interpolating between the vacuum at
r\to-\infty
and
the Kraus-Polley-Reents infravacuum at
r\to\infty
. We found a phase transition at
r=0
separating the
ordered phase
(
r<0
)
from the disordered phase
(
r>0
)
. Remarkably, in the ordered phase the state is lightcone normal, that is,
it cannot be distinguished by any experiments in the future lightcone from a state in the vacuum sector. Not surprisingly, this
phase suffers from all the infrared problems familiar from the vacuum sector: the Bloch-Nordsieck
S
-matrix disintegrates and
soft photon clouds break rotational symmetry. However, by an infinitesimal change of the control parameter from
r={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}-\epsilon}
to
r={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}+\epsilon}

we enter the disordered phase, where the
S
-matrix is stabilized by the Kraus-Polley-Reents mechanism and rotational symmetry
is
restored.
The transition can be observed in the future lightcone by measuring the timelike asymptotic fluctuations of the field.
Their abrupt increase prevents the
lightcone normality in the disordered phase.

Our phase transition is quite subtle and
does not easily fit into standard categories.
As states
\omega_{r}
are not ground states,

it is
a non-equilibrium phase transition, which consists in a qualitative change of a certain dynamics. This dynamics is,
in our case, the Bloch-Nordsieck scattering process and the qualitative change consists in
the existence or disintegration of its scattering matrix
.
This transition is
also
visible in the asymptotic fluctuations of the order parameter
stated in (
1.1
), which have a discontinuity at
r=0
. On the one hand, such discontinuity suggests a first order phase transition.
On the other hand,

the behaviour of the correlation length
\langle\Omega,S_{r}\Omega\rangle^{-1}\sim\exp(c/r^{3/2})
, stated in (
1.3
), points to a
continuous transition. It would be interesting to study the disintegration of other quantities near
r=0
, such as the angular momentum in
representations
\pi_{r}\circ\alpha
. It would also be desirable to identify some universal features of the resulting dependencies (‘critical exponents’)
independent of the detailed construction of the states
\omega_{r}
. Finally, the transition is clearly visible in
the boundary
degrees of freedom,
but it may be accompanied by some measurable effects in the bulk. For example, the rate of decay of correlations

\boldsymbol{x}\mapsto\omega_{r}(\phi(0)\phi(\boldsymbol{x}))
might distinguish the two phases. We leave these questions to future research.

The Kraus-Polley-Reents mechanism for curing infrared problems has obvious advantages over the usual infraparticle (or Faddeev-Kulish)
approach. Instead of intricate, velocity dependent dressing transformations of the
S
-matrix, it provides a regularizing background radiation which is
independent of the dynamics. After replacing the vacuum with a KPR infravacuum the
usual Dyson
S
-matrix is meaningful
. This is
clear in the external source situation, but also thinkable in perturbation theory. Apart from the scalar field, the KPR infravacua are available for the electromagnetic field
[
KPR77
,
CD19
]
and we are confident that our findings could be generalized to this case. However, to our knowledge the KPR mechanism has not yet been tested
in the case of massless higher spin particles. It would be interesting to fill this gap, given the difficulties with applying the Faddeev-Kulish approach
in the presence of gravitons
[
PSW22
]
. In the next step one could revisit the approach to the black hole information paradox from
[
HPS16
]
in the
presence of KPR radiation. As such radiation dramatically changes the superselection structure of infrared degrees of freedom,
this
may lead to interesting new insights.

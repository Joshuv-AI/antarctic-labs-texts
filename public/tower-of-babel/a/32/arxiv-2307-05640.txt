A GH-compactification of CAT ( 0 ) (0) -groups via totally disconnected, unimodular actions Thanks: N.Cavallucci is partially supported by the SFB/TRR 191, funded by the DFG
Nicola Cavallucci

Abstract
. We give a detailed description of the possible limits in the equivariant-Gromov-Hausdorff sense of sequences ( X j , G j ) (X_{j},G_{j}) , where the X j X_{j} ’s are proper, geodesically complete, uniformly packed, CAT ( 0 ) (0) -spaces and the G j G_{j} ’s are closed, totally disconnected, unimodular, uniformly cocompact groups of isometries. We show that the class of metric quotients G / X G/X , where X X and G G are as above, is compact under Gromov-Hausdorff convergence. In particular it is a geometric compactification of the class of locally geodesically complete, locally compact, locally CAT ( 0 ) (0) -spaces with uniformly packed universal cover and uniformly bounded diameter.



1.
Introduction

The author, together with A.Sambusetti, studied in
[
CS23
]
the possible Gromov-Hausdorff limits of locally geodesically complete, locally CAT
(0)
-spaces with bounded diameter and with uniformly packed universal cover, with particular attention to the collapsing case, i.e. when the dimension of the limit space is smaller than the dimension of the approximating ones.

In order to synthetize part of the results of
[
CS23
]
we fix some notations. Let
\mathcal{O}\text{-CAT}_{0}^{\text{disc}}(P_{0},r_{0},D_{0})
be the class of quotient metric spaces
M:=\Gamma\backslash X
, where
X
is a proper, geodesically complete,
(P_{0},r_{0})
-packed, CAT
(0)
-space and
\Gamma
is a
discrete
group of isometries of
X
with
\text{diam}(\Gamma\backslash X)\leq D_{0}
. Here
\mathcal{O}
stays for
orbispace
. The numbers
P_{0},r_{0},D_{0}
are structural constants that have to be thought arbitrary, but fixed once for all. The packing condition on
X
is a very weak synthetic lower bound on the curvature of the metric space (cp. Section
2.2
and
[
CS21
]
for more details) and is a necessary and sufficient condition for precompactness under Gromov-Hausdorff convergence (cp. Lemma
6.4
,
[
CS23
]
and
[
CS21
]
). In case
\Gamma
is torsion-free then
M
is actually a compact, locally geodesically complete, locally CAT
(0)
-space of diameter at most
D_{0}
.

One of the main results of
[
CS23
]
is

Theorem A

(
[
CS23
, Theorem G]
)
.

Let
\{M_{j}\}\subseteq\mathcal{O}\textup{-CAT}_{0}^{\textup{disc}}(P_{0},r_{0},D_{0})
be such that
M_{j}
converges to some space
M_{\infty}
in the Gromov-Hausdorff sense. Then
M_{\infty}
is the quotient of a proper, geodesically complete,
(P_{0},r_{0})
-packed,
CAT
(0)
-space by a closed, totally disconnected group of isometries, and
diam
(M_{\infty})\leq D_{0}
.

The proof of Theorem
A
is based on a careful description of the convergence of the group actions
(X_{j},\Gamma_{j})
to a limit group action
(X_{\infty},G_{\infty})
in the equivariant pointed Gromov-Hausdorff sense (cp. Section
6
and
[
CS23
]
), where
M_{j}=\Gamma_{j}\backslash X_{j}
and
M_{\infty}=G_{\infty}\backslash X_{\infty}
. The main issue is that in general
G_{\infty}
is not discrete, so it is not clear a priori the relation between
X_{\infty}
and
M_{\infty}
. The analysis was divided in two cases: the collapsed and the non-collapsed one. The two cases can be summarized in the following equivalences (
[
CS23
, Theorem 7.10, Theorem 7.11, Theorem 7.15]
):

(1)

\displaystyle\text{Non-collapsed}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow G_{\infty}^{o}=\{\textup{id}\}\Leftrightarrow\dim(M_{\infty})=\dim(M_{j})\text{ for }j\gg 0;

\displaystyle\text{Collapsed}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow G_{\infty}^{o}\neq\{\textup{id}\}\Leftrightarrow\dim(M_{\infty})<\dim(M_{j})\text{ for }j\gg 0,

where
G_{\infty}^{o}
is the connected component of the identity of the topological group
G_{\infty}
. From (
1
) we can see the validity of Theorem
A
in the non-collapsed case. The same result in the collapsed case requires a deep understanding of
G_{\infty}^{o}
which is a non trivial result of
[
CS23
]
. We remark that in the generality of Theorem
A
, it can happen that
M_{\infty}
does not belong to
\mathcal{O}\text{-CAT}_{0}^{\text{disc}}(P_{0},r_{0},D_{0})
, i.e. that the group
G_{\infty}
is not discrete, even in the non-collapsed case (cp.
[
CS23
, Example 7.14]
). Therefore totally disconnected, non-discrete, actions appear in this framework as limit case of discrete actions and it is natural to study them in order to find a compactification of the space
\mathcal{O}\text{-CAT}_{0}^{\text{disc}}(P_{0},r_{0},D_{0})
.

With this in mind we denote by
\mathcal{O}\text{-CAT}_{0}^{\text{td,u}}(P_{0},r_{0},D_{0})
the class of quotients
M:=G\backslash X
, where
X
is again a proper, geodesically complete,
(P_{0},r_{0})
-packed, CAT
(0)
-space and
G
is a
closed, totally disconnected and unimodular
group of isometries of
X
with
\text{diam}(G\backslash X)\leq D_{0}
. We refer to Section
2
for the definition of unimodularity. It naturally holds

\mathcal{O}\text{-CAT}_{0}^{\text{disc}}(P_{0},r_{0},D_{0})\subseteq\mathcal{O}\text{-CAT}_{0}^{\text{td,u}}(P_{0},r_{0},D_{0})

since every discrete group is totally disconnected and unimodular. The unimodular conditions is justified by the following result, which is the main theorem of the paper. It extends Theorem
A
to totally disconnected, unimodular actions, that is to sequences belonging to
\mathcal{O}\textup{-CAT}_{0}^{\textup{td-u}}(P_{0},r_{0},D_{0})
.

Theorem B

(Compactification Theorem)
.

The class
\mathcal{O}\textup{-CAT}_{0}^{\textup{td,u}}(P_{0},r_{0},D_{0})
is closed (and compact) under Gromov-Hausdorff convergence. In other words let
\{M_{j}\}\subseteq\mathcal{O}\textup{-CAT}_{0}^{\textup{td,u}}(P_{0},r_{0},D_{0})
be such that
M_{j}
converges to some space
M_{\infty}
in the Gromov-Hausdorff sense. Then
M_{\infty}
is the quotient of a proper, geodesically complete,
(P_{0},r_{0})
-packed,
CAT
(0)
-space by a closed, totally disconnected, unimodular group of isometries, and
diam
(M_{\infty})\leq D_{0}
.

Observe that in particular Theorem
B
shows that the space
M_{\infty}
appearing in Theorem
A
is not only the quotient by a totally disconnected group, but actually by a
unimodular
one. A more important consequence is that the class
\mathcal{O}\textup{-CAT}_{0}^{\textup{td,u}}(P_{0},r_{0},D_{0})
is a compactification of
\mathcal{O}\textup{-CAT}_{0}^{\textup{disc}}(P_{0},r_{0},D_{0})
with respect to the Gromov-Hausdorff topology. This fact is useful in case we want to find uniform geometric estimates holding for all the spaces in
\mathcal{O}\textup{-CAT}_{0}^{\textup{disc}}(P_{0},r_{0},D_{0})
. Examples of such uniform estimates will be proved especially in Section
6.4
and will be used for the proof of Theorem
B
. To clarify this idea we present here a particular case of one of them.

Theorem C
.

Let
P_{0},r_{0},D_{0}>0
. Then there exists
M_{0}=M_{0}(P_{0},r_{0},D_{0})
such that the following holds.
Let
X
be a proper, geodesically complete,
(P_{0},r_{0})
-packed,
CAT
(0)
-space. Let
G<\textup{Isom}(X)
be closed, totally disconnected, unimodular with
diam
(G\backslash X)\leq D_{0}
. Let
g\in G
be a finite order isometry and write its order as
\textup{ord}(g)=p_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots p_{k}^{\alpha_{k}}
, where the
p_{i}
’s are prime numbers.
Then
\max_{i}p_{i}\leq M_{0}
.

It is not difficult to check the validity of this statement on a regular tree. In that case the full isometry group is totally disconnected and unimodular. Theorem
C
says that a similar phenomenon happens for every totally disconnected, unimodular group acting on a space as in the assumptions. The author does not know a proof of Theorem
C
for discrete groups that does not use totally disconnected actions. As remarked after (
1
) any argument by contradiction provides limit groups that can be totally disconnected, or worse, but in general not discrete.

Using the fact that if a group
G
acts cocompactly on a metric space
X
, then
X
is
(P_{0},r_{0})
-packed for some
P_{0},r_{0}>0
(cp.
[
Cav22a
, Lemma 5.4]
), we also get the following, non-uniform, estimate.

Corollary D
.

Let
X
be a proper, geodesically complete,
CAT
(0)
-space and let
G<\textup{Isom}(X)
be closed, totally disconnected, unimodular and cocompact. Then there exists
M\in\mathbb{N}
such that every prime number appearing in the prime decomposition of the order of every finite order isometry of
G
is at most
M
.

The proof of Theorem
B
follows a scheme similar to the one we used in
[
CS23
]
, but with two additional difficulties. First of all we have to deal with the fact that the groups under consideration are no more discrete, but just totally disconnected. This impacts every key argument in
[
CS23
]
: indeed the discussion therein starts from the Margulis Lemma. That is why in Section
3
we prove the following, new version of the Margulis Lemma holding for totally disconnected actions on proper metric spaces. It generalizes
[
BGT11
, Corollary 11.17]
and it is proved by similar techniques.

Theorem E

(Margulis Lemma for totally disconnected group actions.)
.

Let
K\geq 1
. There exists
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(K)
such that the following is true. Let
X
be any proper metric space and let
x\in X
be a point such that
\textup{Cov}(\overline{B}(x,4),1)\leq K
. Let
G
be a closed, totally disconnected group of isometries of
X
. Then the group
G_{\varepsilon}(x)=\langle S_{\varepsilon}(x)\rangle
contains an open, compact, normal subgroup
N
such that
G_{\varepsilon}(x)/N
is discrete, finitely generated and nilpotent.

The expression
\textup{Cov}(\overline{B}(x,4),1)
refers to the minimal cardinality of a
1
-net inside
\overline{B}(x,4)
, see Section
2.2
for a precise definition and its relation with the packing. The set
S_{\varepsilon}(x)
is the set of elements of
G
moving
x
less than
\varepsilon
, sometimes it is called the almost stabilizer of
x
. For more properties of these sets see Section
2.4
. Observe that if
G
is discrete then
N
is finite, and Theorem
E
becomes the known Margulis Lemma
[
BGT11
, Corollary 11.17]
.

In general the information provided by Theorem
E
is not sufficient to get a good geometric description of the action of the almost stabilizers. The situation is different if the space
X
is CAT
(0)
and the group
G
is cocompact. Under these assumptions the group
G_{\varepsilon}(x)/N
is actually abelian and a geometric description of the action of such groups can be provided (cp. Section
3.3
). It resembles the description we gave in
[
CS23
, §2.4]
for virtually abelian groups acting on CAT
(0)
-spaces.

With these tools in our hands we will adapt the proofs of the Splitting Theorem D and the Renormalization Theorem E of
[
CS23
]
to our setting. This will be done in Sections
4
and
5
.
In Section
6
, and in particular in Section
6.3
, we use the unimodularity assumption, that is not used until this point, to attack the proof of Theorem
B
. This is enough to prove (
1
) in this more general setting. To be more precise we can prove that if
M_{\infty}
is the Gromov-Hausdorff limit of spaces
M_{j}\in\mathcal{O}\text{-CAT}_{0}^{\text{td,u}}(P_{0},r_{0},D_{0})
then
M_{\infty}=G_{\infty}^{\prime}\backslash X_{\infty}^{\prime}
for some proper, geodesically complete,
(P_{0},r_{0})
-packed, CAT
(0)
-space
X_{\infty}^{\prime}
and some closed, totally disconnected,
D_{0}
-cocompact group of isometries
G_{\infty}^{\prime}
. Observe that this is exactly the statement of Theorem
A
if the
M_{j}
’s belong to
\mathcal{O}\text{-CAT}_{0}^{\text{disc}}(P_{0},r_{0},D_{0})
.

The second additional difficulty in the proof of Theorem
B
with respect to
[
CS23
]
consists in the last step, namely in proving that the group
G_{\infty}^{\prime}
above is actually
unimodular
. We sketch here the argument in the non-collapsed case, the collapsed one being more involved but conceptually similar. In the non-collapsed case, calling
M_{j}=G_{j}\backslash X_{j}
, we know that
(X_{j},G_{j})
converges in the equivariant Gromov-Hausdorff sense to some
(X_{\infty},G_{\infty})
. Moreover
G_{\infty}
is totally disconnected by the equivalent of (
1
), so
M_{\infty}=G_{\infty}\backslash X_{\infty}
, in particular we can take
X_{\infty}=X_{\infty}^{\prime}
and
G_{\infty}=G_{\infty}^{\prime}
. It is here that the non-collapsed assumption simplifies the argument. We need to prove that
G_{\infty}
is unimodular. First of all we prove that
G_{j}
converges in the pointed Gromov-Hausdorff sense to
G_{\infty}
with respect to suitable metrics (cp. Proposition
6.6
). Then we give a sufficient condition for the convergence of the Haar measures of
G_{j}
to a Haar measure of

along this pointed Gromov-Hausdorff convergence (cp. Proposition
6.8
). Lastly we use uniform quantitative estimates in the spirit of Theorem

to prove that this sufficient condition for the convergence is satisfied.

We end the introduction by highlighting a result of independent interest that we will use in the paper: it is a characterization of cocompact, totally disconnected subgroups of isometries of a proper, geodesically complete, CAT
(0)
-space in terms of the properties of the action (cp. Theorem
2.6
).

Theorem F
.

Let
X
be a proper, geodesically complete,
CAT
(0)
-space and let
G
be a closed, cocompact group of isometries of
X
. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

G
is totally disconnected;

(ii)

G
is semisimple and
\inf\{\ell(g)\text{ s.t. }g\text{ hyperbolic}\}>0
;

(iii)

the orbit
Gx
is discrete for every
x\in X
.

(iv)

the orbit
Gx
is discrete for one point
x\in X
.

We recall that
G
is semisimple if it does not contain parabolic isometries, while the quantity
\ell(g)
appearing in (ii) is the translation length of the isometry
g
, i.e.
\ell(g)=\inf_{x\in X}d(x,gx)
. The theorem is false even for proper, CAT
(0)
-spaces that are not geodesically complete (see Example
2.8
).

2.
Preliminaries

We start recalling several facts we will need along the paper. We start with notions on metric spaces.

2.1.
CAT
(0)
-spaces

Throughout the paper
X
will be a
proper
metric space with distance
d
.
The open (resp. closed) ball in
X
of radius
r
, centered at
x
, will be denoted by
B_{X}(x,r)
(resp.
\overline{B}_{X}(x,r)
); we will often drop the subscript
X
when the space is clear from the context.

A
geodesic
in a metric space
X
is an isometry
c\colon[a,b]\to X
, where
[a,b]
is an interval of
\mathbb{R}
. The
endpoints
of the geodesic
c
are the points
c(a)
and
c(b)
; a geodesic with endpoints
x,y\in X
is also denoted by
[x,y]
. A
geodesic ray
is an isometry
c\colon[0,+\infty)\to X
and a geodesic line is an isometry
c\colon\mathbb{R}\to X
. A metric space
X
is called
geodesic
if for every two points
x,y\in X
there is a geodesic with endpoints
x
and
y
.

A metric space
X
is called CAT
(0)
if it is geodesic and every geodesic triangle
\Delta(x,y,z)
is thinner than its Euclidean comparison triangle
\overline{\Delta}(\bar{x},\bar{y},\bar{z})
: that is, for any couple of points
p\in[x,y]
and
q\in[x,z]
we have
d(p,q)\leq d(\bar{p},\bar{q})
where
\bar{p},\bar{q}
are the corresponding points in
\overline{\Delta}(\bar{x},\bar{y},\bar{z})
(see for instance
[
BH13
]
for the basics of CAT(0)-geometry).
A CAT
(0)
-space is
uniquely geodesic
: for every
x,y\in X
there exists a unique geodesic with endpoints
x
and
y
.

A CAT
(0)
-metric space
X
is
geodesically complete
if any geodesic
c\colon[a,b]\to X
can be extended to a geodesic line.

The
boundary at infinity
of a CAT
(0)
-space
X
(that is, the set of equivalence classes of geodesic rays, modulo the relation of being asymptotic), endowed with the Tits distance, will be denoted by
\partial X
, see
[
BH13
, Chapter II.9]
.

A subset
C
of
X
is said to be
convex
if for all
x,y\in C
the geodesic
[x,y]
is contained in
C
. Given a subset
Y\subseteq X
we denote by
\text{Conv}(Y)
the smallest convex, closed subset of
X
containing
Y
.
If
C
is a convex subset of a CAT
(0)
-space
X
then it is itself CAT
(0)
, and its boundary at infinity
\partial C
naturally and isometrically embeds in
\partial X
.

We will denote by HD
(X)
and TD
(X)
the Hausdorff and the topological dimension of a metric space
X
, respectively.
By
[
LN19
]
we know that if
X
is a proper and geodesically complete CAT
(0)
-space then every point
x\in X
has a well defined integer dimension in the following sense: there exists
n_{x}\in\mathbb{N}
such that every small enough ball around
x
has Hausdorff dimension equal to
n_{x}
. This defines a
stratification of
X
into pieces of different integer dimensions: namely, if
X^{k}
denotes the subset of points of
X
with dimension
k
, then

X=\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}X^{k}.

The
dimension
of
X
is the supremum of the dimensions of its points: it coincides with the
topological dimension
of
X
, cp.
[
LN19
, Theorem 1.1]
.

2.2.
Packing and covering

Let
X
be a metric space and
r>0
.
A subset
Y
of
X
is called
r
-separated
if
d(y,y^{\prime})>r
for all
y,y^{\prime}\in Y
, while it is a
r
-net
if for all
x\in X
there exists
y\in Y
such that
d(x,y)\leq r
.
Given
x\in X
and
0<r\leq R
we denote by Pack
(\overline{B}(x,R),r)
the maximal cardinality of a
2r
-separated subset of
\overline{B}(x,R)
, and by Cov
(\overline{B}(x,R),r)
the minimal cardinality of a
r
-net of
\overline{B}(x,R)
. Moreover we denote by Pack
(R,r)
(resp. Cov
(R,r)
) the supremum of Pack
(\overline{B}(x,R),r)
(resp. Cov
(\overline{B}(x,R),r)
) among all points of
X
. The packing and covering quantities defined above are classically related as follows (cp.
[
CS21
, § 4]
):

(2)

\displaystyle\text{Pack}(\overline{B}(x,R),r)\leq\text{Cov}(\overline{B}(x,R),r)\leq\text{Pack}\left(\overline{B}(x,R),\frac{r}{2}\right),

(3)

\displaystyle\text{Pack}(R,r)\leq\text{Cov}(R,r)\leq\text{Pack}\left(R,\frac{r}{2}\right).

Given
P_{0},r_{0}>0
we say that
X
is
(P_{0},r_{0})
-packed
if Pack
(3r_{0},r_{0})\leq P_{0}
.
The packing condition should be thought as a metric, weak replacement of a Ricci curvature lower bound: for more details and examples see
[
CS21
]
. Also remark that every metric space admitting a cocompact action is packed (for some
P_{0},r_{0}
), see the proof of
[
Cav22a
, Lemma 5.4]
.

The packing condition has many interesting geometric consequences for complete, geodesically complete CAT
(0)
-spaces, as showed in
[
CS20
]
,
[
Cav21
]
and
[
Cav22b
]
. For instance it provides an upper bound on the dimension.

Proposition 2.1

(
[
CS21
, Theorems 4.2, 4.9]
)
.

Let
X
be a complete, geodesically complete,
(P_{0},r_{0})
-packed,
\textup{CAT}(0)
-space. Then
X
is proper and

(i)

\textup{Pack}(R,r)\leq P_{0}(1+P_{0})^{\frac{R}{\min\{r,r_{0}\}}-1}
for all
0<r\leq R
;

(ii)

the dimension of
X
is at most
n_{0}:=P_{0}/2
;

In particular, for a geodesically complete, CAT
(0)
space
X
which is
(P_{0},r_{0})
-packed, the assumptions
complete
and
proper
are interchangeable.

2.3.
Topological groups

We recall now some terminology about topological groups. A topological group is a group
G
endowed with a topology for which the operation and the inverse are continuous. Every topological group we will consider will be locally compact and
\sigma
-compact, in particular second-countable. Any such group
G
admits a left invariant Haar measure, as well as a right invariant Haar measure. By definition they are Radon measures on the Borel
\sigma
-algebra of
G
that are preserved by the multiplication on the left (resp. on the right). The next lemma characterizes Haar measures.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
G
be a locally compact, second countable group. Then a left (resp. right) invariant measure on the Borel
\sigma
-algebra of
G
is a left (resp. right) invariant Haar measure if and only if it is finite on compact subsets and positive on open subsets.

Proof.

The Haar measure of every open set is positive by left (resp. right) invariance. Viceversa since
G
is locally compact and second countable every open set is
\sigma
-compact. Therefore by
[
Rud63
, Theorem 2.18]
every left (resp. right) invariant measure which is finite on compact subsets and positive on open subsets is Radon, and so a Haar measure.
∎

A topological group is called
unimodular
if it admits a
left and right invariant
Haar measure. For instance every discrete group is unimodular.

The connected component of the identity of a topological group
G
will be denoted by
G^{o}
. It is always a normal (actually characteristic), closed subgroup of
G
. The quotient group
G/G^{o}
endowed with the quotient topology is still locally compact and totally disconnected as a topological space. Therefore a topological group
G
is totally disconnected if and only if
G^{o}=\{1\}
.

A topological group
G
is
compactly generated
if there exists a compact subset
S\subseteq G
such that
\langle S\rangle=G
. The notation
\langle S\rangle
stays for the group generated by
S
. For instance a discrete group is compactly generated if and only if it is finitely generated.

2.4.
Isometry groups

For us the main source of topological groups is the full isometry group of a proper metric space.
Let
\text{Isom}(X)
be the group of isometries of
X
, endowed with the compact-open topology: as
X
is proper, it is a topological, locally compact,
\sigma
-compact group.
Let
G
be a closed subgroup of Isom
(X)
. For
x\in X
and
r,R\geq 0
we set

(4)

\displaystyle\overline{S}_{r}(x,R,X)

\displaystyle:=\{g\in G\text{ s.t. }d(y,gy)\leq r\text{ for all }y\in\overline{B}(x,R)\},

\displaystyle S_{r}(x,R,X)

\displaystyle:=\{g\in G\text{ s.t. }d(y,gy)<r\text{ for all }y\in\overline{B}(x,R)\},

\displaystyle\overline{G}_{r}(x,R,X)

\displaystyle:=\langle\overline{S}_{r}(x,R,X)\rangle,\quad G_{r}(x,R,X):=\langle S_{r}(x,R,X)\rangle.

For
R=0
we simply write
\overline{S}_{r}(x,0,X)=:\overline{S}_{r}(x,X)
and similarly for the others. When
r=0
we use the notation
\overline{S}_{0}(x,R,X)=:\textup{Stab}_{G}(x,R)
: it is the pointwise stabilizer of the ball
\overline{B}(x,R)
. In the same way when both
R=r=0
we use tha notation
\overline{S}_{0}(x,0,X)=:\textup{Stab}_{G}(x)
: it is the stabilizer of the point
x
. When the context is clear we will omit to write the dependence on the metric space
X
. The sets
\overline{S}_{r}(x,R)
are compact by Ascolì-Arzelà Theorem, since
X
is proper. In particular the groups
\overline{G}_{r}(x,R)
are compactly generated. Instead the sets
{S}_{r}(x,R)
are open. It follows from the definition that the sets
\{S_{r}(x,R)\}_{r,R>0}
form a neighbourhood basis of the identity.

Let
x\in X
be a fixed base point. The left and right invariant
L^{\infty}
-pseudometrics at scale
R
of center
x
are by definition

(5)

d_{x,R}^{\ell}(g,h):=\max_{y\in\overline{B}(x,R)}d(gy,hy),\quad d_{x,R}^{\textup{r}}(g,h):=\max_{y\in\overline{B}(x,R)}d(g^{-1}y,h^{-1}y).

As the name suggests they are left (resp. right) invariant pseudometrics on
\text{Isom}(X)
. The expressions

(6)

d_{x}^{\ell}(g,h):=\inf_{R>0}\left\{\frac{1}{R}+d_{x,R}^{\ell}(g,h)\right\};\quad d_{x}^{\textup{r}}(g,h):=\inf_{R>0}\left\{\frac{1}{R}+d_{x,R}^{\textup{r}}(g,h)\right\}

define proper, left (resp. right) invariant metrics on
\text{Isom}(X)
inducing the compact-open topology (cp.
[
SRZ22
, §2.5]
). This follows also from the next result whose proof is a direct consequence of the definitions. The closed ball of center
g
and radius
r
with respect to the metric
d_{x}^{\ell}
(resp.
d_{x}^{\text{r}}
) will be denoted by
\overline{B}_{x}^{\ell}(g,r)
(resp.
\overline{B}_{x}^{\text{r}}(g,r)
).

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
X
be a proper metric space,
G<\textup{Isom}(X)
be a closed subgroup,
x\in X
and
r>0
. Then

\overline{S}_{\frac{r}{2}}\left(x,\frac{2}{r}\right)\subseteq\overline{B}_{x}^{\ell}(\textup{id},r),\overline{B}_{x}^{\textup{r}}(\textup{id},r)\subseteq\overline{S}_{r}\left(x,\frac{1}{r}\right).

A subgroup
G
is
totally disconnected
if it is totally disconnected as a subset of Isom
(X)
(with respect to the compact-open topology).
A closed group
G<\textup{Isom}(X)
is said to be
cocompact
if the quotient metric space
G\backslash X
is compact; in this case, we call
codiameter
of
G
the diameter of the quotient, and we will say that
G
is
D_{0}
-cocompact if it has codiameter at most
D_{0}
.
Notice that the codiameter of
G
coincides with

(7)

\inf\{r>0\text{ s.t. }G\cdot\overline{B}(x,r)=X\,\,\,\,\forall x\in X\}.

It is well-known that if
X
is geodesic and
G<\textup{Isom}(X)
is closed and
D_{0}
-cocompact, then
the subset
\overline{S}_{3D_{0}}(x)
is a generating set for
G
,
that is
\overline{G}_{3D_{0}}(x)=G
, for every
x\in X
. If the orbit
Gx
is discrete then the same is true for
2D_{0}
in place of
3D_{0}
.

The
translation length
of
g\in\text{Isom}(X)
is by definition
\ell(g):=\inf_{x\in X}d(x,gx).

When the infimum is realized, the isometry
g
is called
elliptic
if
\ell(g)=0
and
hyperbolic
otherwise. The
minimal set of
g
,
\text{Min}(g)
, is defined as the subset of points of
X
where
g
realizes its translation length; notice that if
g
is elliptic then
\text{Min}(g)
is the subset of points fixed by
g
. An isometry is called
semisimple
if it is either elliptic or hyperbolic; a subgroup
G
of Isom
(X)
is called
semisimple
if all of its elements are semisimple. The
displacement function

d_{g}\colon X\to X
defined by
d_{g}(x)=d(x,gx)
is convex (
[
BH13
, Proposition II.6.2]
). For
\lambda\geq 0
we call the set
M_{\lambda}(g):=d_{g}^{-1}([0,\lambda])
the
\lambda
-level set
of
g
. It is a closed, convex subset of
X
. For instance
M_{\ell(g)}(g)=\text{Min}(g)
.

The
free-systole
of a group
G<\textup{Isom}(X)

at a point

x\in X
is

\text{sys}^{\diamond}(G,x):=\inf_{g\in G\setminus G^{\diamond}}d(x,gx),

where
G^{\diamond}
is the subset of all elliptic isometries of
G
. The
free-systole of

G
is accordingly defined as

\text{sys}^{\diamond}(G,X)=\inf_{x\in X}\text{sys}^{\diamond}(G,x).

Similarly, the
free-diastole of

G
is defined as

\text{dias}^{\diamond}(G,X)=\sup_{x\in X}\text{sys}^{\diamond}(G,x).

By definition, we have the trivial inequality

\text{sys}^{\diamond}(G,X)\leq\text{dias}^{\diamond}(G,X)
.
The following result, which extends
[
CS23
, Theorem 3.1]
to general groups acting on geodesically complete, CAT
(0)
-spaces, shows that the free systole and the free diastole are for small values quantitatively equivalent, provided one knows an a priori bound on the diameter of the quotient. Therefore it is equivalent to consider actions with small free-systole or small free-diastole. In the rest of the paper we will focus on the free-systole.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
X
be a proper, geodesically complete,
(P_{0},r_{0})
-packed,
\textup{CAT}(0)
-space and let
G<\textup{Isom}(X)
be
D_{0}
-cocompact. Then

\textup{sys}^{\diamond}(G,X)\geq\left(1+P_{0}\right)e^{-\frac{(2D_{0}+1)}{\min\{\textup{dias}^{\diamond}(G,X),r_{0}\}}}.

The proof is the same of
[
CS23
, Theorem 3.1]
, we report it for completeness. It is based on the following important fact we will use also later.

Proposition 2.5

(
[
CS23
, Proposition 3.3]
,
[
CS20
, Proposition 4.5]
)
.

Let
P_{0},r_{0},R>0
and
0<\varepsilon\leq r_{0}
. Then there exists
\delta(P_{0},r_{0},R,\varepsilon)>0
with the following property. Let
X
be a proper, geodesically complete,
(P_{0},r_{0})
-packed,
CAT
(0)
-space. If
g
is a non-elliptic isometry of
X
and
x
is a point of
X
such that
d(x,gx)\leq\delta(P_{0},r_{0},R,\varepsilon)
, then for every
y\in X
with
d(x,y)\leq R
there exists
m\in\mathbb{Z}^{*}
such that
d(y,g^{m}y)\leq\varepsilon
.

(We can choose, explicitely,
\delta(P_{0},r_{0},R,\varepsilon)=(1+P_{0})e^{-\frac{(2R+1)}{\varepsilon}}
).

Proof of Proposition
2.4
.

Assume that

\min\{\text{dias}^{\diamond}(G,X),r_{0}\}>\varepsilon
.
By definition there exists
x_{0}\in X
such that for every hyperbolic isometry
g\in G
one has
d(x_{0},gx_{0})>\varepsilon
.
Now, if sys
{}^{\diamond}(G,X)\leq(1+P_{0})e^{-\frac{(2D_{0}+1)}{\varepsilon}}=:\delta
, we could find
x\in X
and a hyperbolic
g\in G
such that
d(x,gx)\leq\delta
.
By Proposition
2.5
, for every
y\in\overline{B}(x,D_{0})
there would exists a non trivial power
g^{m}
satisfying

.
But then, since the action is
D_{0}
-cocompact we could find a conjugate
\gamma
of
g^{m}
(thus, a hyperbolic isometry) such that

, a contradiction. The conclusion follows by the arbitrariness of
\varepsilon
.
∎

2.5.
Totally disconnected, cocompact groups of CAT
(0)
-spaces

We collect here some important properties of cocompact, totally disconnected groups acting on a proper, geodesically complete, CAT
(0)
-space that we will use extensively along the paper. We suggest the reader to consult also
[
Cap09
]
and
[
CM09
]
.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
X
be a proper, geodesically complete,
CAT
(0)
-space and let
G
be a closed, cocompact group of isometries of
X
. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

G
is totally disconnected;

(ii)

G
is semisimple and
\textup{sys}^{\diamond}(G,X)>0
;

(iii)

the orbit
Gx
is discrete for every
x\in X
.

(iv)

the orbit
Gx
is discrete for one point
x\in X
.

Moreover if any of the conditions above hold then:

(a)

the sets
\textup{Stab}_{G}(x,R)
are open for every
x\in X
and every
R\geq 0
. They form a fundamental system of neighbourhoods of
\{\textup{id}\}
.

(b)

The sets
\overline{S}_{r}(x,R)
are open for every
x\in X
and every
r,R\geq 0
.

(c)

The set
\{gx\text{ s.t. }g\in K\}
is finite for every
x\in X
and every precompact
K\subseteq G
.

We need the following fact.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
X
be a geodesically complete,
CAT
(0)
-space and let
G<\textup{Isom}(X)
be a closed, cocompact group. Then
G^{o}
is either the identity or it contains an infinite cyclic discrete subgroup.

Proof.

Every locally compact group either contains a compact open subgroup or an infinite cyclic discrete subgroup. We apply this statement to
G^{o}
: if it contains a compact open subgroup then it must be the whole
G^{o}
since it is connected. Therefore
G^{o}
is a compact, normal subgroup of
G
. In particular
G
stabilizes the non-empty (cp.
[
BH13
, Corollary II.2.8]
) closed, convex set
\text{Fix}(G^{o})
. By minimality of
G
(cp.
[
CM09
, Proposition 1.5]
) we have that
\text{Fix}(G^{o})=X
, so
G^{o}=\{\text{id}\}
. Thus either
G^{o}=\{\textup{id}\}
or
G^{o}
contains an infinite cyclic discrete subgroup.
∎

We will use some basic facts about convergence recalled in Section
6
.

Proof of Theorem
2.6
.

First of all we observe that, given
x\in X
, the orbit
Gx
is discrete if and only if the group
\text{Stab}_{G}(x)
is open. Indeed suppose
Gx
is discrete and take
g_{j}\in G
converging to
g
, with
gx=x
. Since
g_{j}x
converges to
gx=x
and the orbit
Gx
is discrete, then
g_{j}x=x
for
j
big enough. On the other hand, suppose
\text{Stab}_{G}(x)
is open and that
Gx
is not discrete. The set
Gx
is closed and not discrete, so it has an accumulation point
gx
. This means that there are pairwise distinct
g_{j}x
converging to
gx
with
g_{j}\in G
. Up to multiply on the left by
g^{-1}
we can suppose that
g_{j}x
converges to
x
, and it is still composed by pairwise distinct points. The sequence
g_{j}
converges, up to a subsequence, to some
g^{\prime}\in\text{Stab}_{G}(x)
. Since the latter is open then
g_{j}\in\text{Stab}_{G}(x)
for
j
big enough, i.e.
g_{j}x=x
, which is a contradiction.

Now, (i) implies (ii) by
[
CM09
, Corollary 6.3]
, while (i) implies (a) by
[
CM09
, Theorem 6.1]
and so (i) implies also (iii) by the first observation. It is clear that (iii) implies (iv).

Now we show that (iv) implies (i). As in the proof of
[
CM09
, Theorem 6.1]
we denote by
C
the subset of
X
of points whose stabilizer is open. Then
C
is convex (if
x,y\in C
then
\text{Stab}_{G}(x)\cap\text{Stab}_{G}(y)
is an open subgroup that stabilizes
[x,y]
) and
G
-invariant. It is also non-empty because of (iv) and the first observation. Since the action of
G
is minimal (cp.
[
CM09
, Proposition 1.5]
) we conclude that
C
is dense. We use this fact to provide a neighbourhood basis of the identity made of compact open subgroups. This is enough to show that
G
is totally disconnected. We fix a basepoint
x_{0}\in X
,
R\geq 0
and
\varepsilon>0
and we choose a finite subset
\{c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}\}
of
C\cap\overline{B}(x,R)
which is
\varepsilon
-dense in
\overline{B}(x,R)
. We set
U(R,\varepsilon):=\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}\text{Stab}_{G}(c_{i})
. It is clear that
U(R,\varepsilon)
is open and compact, being a finite intersection of open and compact subgroups. We claim that the family
\{U(R,\varepsilon)\}
form a neighbourhood basis of
\{\textup{id}\}
. For that it is enough to show that
\bigcap_{R\geq 0,\varepsilon>0}U(R,\varepsilon)=\{\textup{id}\}
. Let
g
be in this intersection. For any
R\geq 0
and any
\varepsilon>0
we know that
g
acts trivially on a
\varepsilon

\overline{B}(x_{0},R)
, so it displaces every point of
\overline{B}(x_{0},R)
by at most
2\varepsilon
. Since this is true for every

we deduce that
g
fixes pointwise the whole
\overline{B}(x_{0},R)
. By arbitrariness of

we conclude that
g
fixes pointwise every point of

, i.e.

.

It remains to show that (ii) implies (i). We suppose that
G
has only elliptic and hyperbolic elements and that
\text{sys}^{\diamond}(G,X)=:s>0
. We claim that the set of elliptic isometries

is open and closed in
G
. Suppose
g_{j}\in G
converges to

and let

. Then, for

big enough, we have

, so
g_{j}\in G^{\diamond}
. This shows that
G^{\diamond}
is open.
Suppose now to have
g_{j}\in G^{\diamond}
converging to a hyperbolic isometry

. Let
x
be a point such that

. We denote by

the projection of

on the closed, convex set

. Let

be the point along the geodesic
[z_{j},x]
such that

. Observe that by convexity of the displacement function

along the geodesic
[z_{j},x]
we have

. We consider the sequence of isometric actions
(X,y_{n},G)
and we fix a non-principal ultrafilter

(see Section

is cocompact, by Lemma

we have that the ultralimit
(X_{\omega},y_{\omega},G_{\omega})

. Observe that the sequence

defines an element

of

. The condition

, so

is hyperbolic. On the other hand

. This contradicts the assumption
\text{sys}^{\diamond}(G_{\omega},X_{\omega})=\text{sys}^{\diamond}(G,X)=s

is open and closed, implying

2.7

G^{o}=\{\textup{id}\}
or it contains an infinite cyclic discrete subgroup. Every element of

is elliptic, so if it generates a discrete subgroup then it must be of finite order. This forces to have

is totally disconnected.
This concludes the proof of the equivalences (i)-(iv).

These conditions imply (a) by
[
CM09
, Theorem 6.1]

is not open for some

and

. Then
there exists a sequence

converging to

.
Then

converges to

. By (a) it must hold

for

big enough, i.e.

on

. This contradicts the assumption

. Finally if
K
is a compact subset of

then

is a compact subset of

which is also discrete by (iii). Therefore it must be finite.
∎

The equivalences (i)-(iv) are no more true if the space is proper, CAT
(0)
, but not geodesically complete. In the following example we show that (i) does not imply (iii).

Example 2.8
.

We construct a tree in the following iterative way. We start with
X_{1}=[-1,1]
. The set
V_{1}=\{\pm 1\}
is the set of free vertices of
X_{1}
, i.e. those vertices with only one edge issuing from them. To construct
X_{2}
we glue a segment of length
1
at the point
0\in X_{1}
.
X_{2}
is composed by three segments of length
1
glued at a point. Its set of free vertices
V_{2}
has
3
elements and contains
V_{1}
. To construct
X_{3}
we glue segments of length
1/2
to the midpoints of all the segments of length
1
composing
X_{2}
, so that
X_{3}
is composed by
9
segments of length
1/2
. Its set of free vertices is denoted by
V_{3}
: it contains
V_{2}
. Given
X_{j}
, made of
3^{j-1}
-segments of length
2^{-j+2}
, we construct
X_{j+1}
by gluing segments of length
2^{-j+1}
to each midpoint of the segments of
X_{j}
. We denote by
V_{j+1}
the set of free vertices of
X_{j}
: it contains
V_{j}
. We repeat the procedure inductively and we define the space
X_{\infty}
: it is a tree, so a CAT
(0)
-space, which is also compact, so proper, but not geodesically complete. Let us call
V_{\infty}
its set of free vertices. For every
j
and every two free vertices
v,w\in V_{j}
we can find an isometry of
X_{j}
sending
v
to
w
. Such isometry extends naturally to an isometry of
X_{\infty}
, by construction of
X_{\infty}
. Therefore
\text{Isom}(X_{\infty})
acts transitively on the set of free vertices
V_{\infty}
. This implies that its action on
X_{\infty}
is minimal. The same proof of Lemma
2.7
shows that either
\text{Isom}(X_{\infty})
is totally disconnected or it contains an infinite cyclic discrete subgroup. The second possibility cannot occur because
\text{Isom}(X_{\infty})
is compact, so it contains only elliptic isometries and a discrete group generated by an elliptic isometry is finite. This shows that
\text{Isom}(X_{\infty})
is totally disconnected. However the orbit by
\text{Isom}(X_{\infty})
of every free vertex is not discrete.

2.6.
Crystallographic groups in the Euclidean space

We will denote points in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
by a bold letter
v
, and the origin by
\bf O
. Among CAT
(0)
-spaces, the Euclidean space
{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
and its discrete groups play a special role.
A
crystallographic group
is a discrete, cocompact group
\Gamma
of isometries of some
\mathbb{R}^{k}
. The simplest and most important of them, in view of Bieberbach’s Theorem, are
Euclidean lattices
: i.e.
free abelian crystallographic groups.
It is well known that a lattice must act by translations on
\mathbb{R}^{k}
(see for instance
[
Far81
]
); so, alternatively, a lattice
\mathcal{L}
can be seen as the set of linear combinations with integer coefficients of
k
independent vectors
\bf b_{1},\ldots,\bf b_{k}
(we will make no difference between a lattice and this representation).

A
basis

\mathcal{B}=\{\bf b_{1},\ldots,\bf b_{k}\}
of a lattice
\mathcal{L}
is a set of
k
independent vectors that generate
\mathcal{L}
as a group. There are many geometric invariants classically associated to a lattice
\mathcal{L}
, we will need three of them:

– the
covering radius
, which is defined as

\rho(\mathcal{L})=\inf\left\{r>0\text{ s.t. }\bigcup_{\bf v\in\mathcal{L}}\overline{B}({\bf v},r)=\mathbb{R}^{k}\right\};

– the
shortest generating radius
, that is

\lambda(\mathcal{L})=\inf\{r>0\text{ s.t. }\mathcal{L}\text{ contains }k\text{ independent vectors of length}\leq r\};

– the
shortest vector
, that is

\tau(\mathcal{L})=\inf\left\{\|{\bf v}\|\text{ s.t. }{\bf v}\in\mathcal{L}\setminus\{{\bf O}\}\right\}.

Notice that, by the triangle inequality, any lattice
\mathcal{L}
is
2\rho(\mathcal{L})
-cocompact. Moreover
\tau(\mathcal{L})
coincides with the free-systole of
\mathcal{L}
.
It is always possible to find a basis
\mathcal{B}=\{\bf b_{1},\ldots,\bf b_{k}\}
of
\mathcal{L}
such that
\tau(\mathcal{L})=\|\bf b_{1}\|\leq\cdots\leq\|b_{k}\|=\lambda(\mathcal{L})
;
this is called a
shortest basis
of
\mathcal{L}
.
The shortest generating radius and the covering radius are related as follows:

(8)

\rho(\mathcal{L})\leq\frac{\sqrt{k}}{2}\cdot\lambda(\mathcal{L}).

In particular every lattice
\mathcal{L}
is
(\sqrt{k}\cdot\lambda(\mathcal{L}))
-cocompact.
The proof of the Svarc-Milnor lemma in
[
dLH00
, Theorem IV.B.23]
gives the following estimate. We recall that given a generating set
S
of a lattice
\mathcal{L}
then
\ell_{S}({\bf v})
denotes the length of the shortest word in the alphabet
S
needed to write
{\bf v}
.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a lattice of
\mathbb{R}^{k}
. Recall it is
(\sqrt{k}\cdot\lambda(\mathcal{L}))
-cocompact. Let
S=\overline{S}_{2\sqrt{k}\cdot\lambda(\mathcal{L})}({\bf O})
as defined in (
4
) and recall it is a generating set. Then
\ell_{S}({\bf v})\leq\frac{\|{\bf v}\|}{\tau(\mathcal{L})}+1
.

A property we will need in Section
6
is the existence of nice bases.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a lattice of
\mathbb{R}^{k}
. Then there exists a basis
\mathcal{B}=\{\bf b_{1},\ldots,\bf b_{k}\}
of
\mathcal{L}
such that

(i)

\max_{i}\|{\bf b_{i}}\|\leq 2^{k-1}\cdot\lambda(\mathcal{L})
;

(ii)

\angle({\bf b_{i}},\sum_{j\neq i}\mathbb{R}{\bf b_{j}})\geq\vartheta_{k}>0
, where
\vartheta_{k}
depends only on
k
.

The notation
\sum_{j\neq i}\mathbb{R}{\bf b_{j}}
means the
(k-1)
-dimensional subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{k}
generated by
\{{\bf b_{j}}\}_{j\neq i}
.

Proof.

It follows by the combination of
[
LLL82
, §1, and Proposition 1.12]
and
[
Bab86
, Theorem 5.1]
.
∎

A set of vectors
\{{\bf v_{1}},\ldots,{\bf v_{k}}\}
satisfying condition (ii) of Lemma
2.10
for some
\vartheta>0
are called
\vartheta
-linearly independent
.

From any lattice we can always extract a sublattice with controlled geometry.

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a lattice of
\mathbb{R}^{k}
. Then there exists a sublattice
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{L}
such that
\lambda(\mathcal{L}^{\prime})=\lambda(\mathcal{L})
and
\lambda(\mathcal{L}^{\prime})\leq 2\cdot\tau(\mathcal{L}^{\prime})
.

Proof.

We proceed recursively. If
\lambda(\mathcal{L})\leq 2\cdot\tau(\mathcal{L})
there is nothing to prove. Otherwise we take a shortest basis
\{{\bf b_{1}},\ldots,{\bf b_{k}}\}
. In particular
\|{\bf b_{1}}\|=\tau(\mathcal{L})
. Let
\lambda_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
\lambda_{1}\|{\bf b_{1}}\|\leq\lambda(\mathcal{L})\leq 2\lambda_{1}\|{\bf b_{1}}\|
. We replace
\mathcal{L}
with the sublattice
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
generated by
\{{\lambda_{1}\bf b_{1}},\ldots,{\bf b_{k}}\}
. We have
\lambda(\mathcal{L}^{\prime})=\lambda(\mathcal{L})
and
\tau(\mathcal{L}^{\prime})\geq\tau(\mathcal{L})
. We repeat the procedure up to arrive to a lattice satisfying the thesis. The algorithm stops because we need to do at most
N
steps, where
N=\#\{{\bf v}\in\mathcal{L}\text{ s.t. }\|{\bf v}\|<\frac{\lambda(\mathcal{L})}{2}\}<+\infty
.
∎

The content of the famous Bieberbach’s Theorems can be stated as follows.

Proposition 2.12

(Bieberbach’s Theorem)
.

There exists
J(k)
, only depending on
k
, such that the following holds true.
For any crystallographic group
\Gamma
of
\mathbb{R}^{k}

the subgroup
{\mathcal{L}}(\Gamma)=\Gamma\cap\textup{Transl}({\mathbb{R}}^{k})
is a normal subgroup of index at most
J(k)
, in particular a lattice.

Here
\textup{Transl}({\mathbb{R}}^{k})
denotes the normal subgroup of translations of
\textup{Isom}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
.
The subgroup
\mathcal{L}(\Gamma)
is called the
maximal lattice
of
\Gamma
.
As in
[
CS23
]
we will use the next fact in the study of collapsing sequences: it follows directly from Proposition
2.12
.

Lemma 2.13

(
[
CS23
, Lemma 2.7]
)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a crystallographic group of
\mathbb{R}^{k}
, and let
0<r<\sqrt{2\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{J(k)}\right)}
. If
g\in\Gamma
moves all points of
B_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}({\bf O},\frac{1}{r})
less than

, then

is a translation.

3.
Margulis Lemma and almost abelian groups

Our aim is to extend the Margulis’ Lemma to totally disconnected group actions. In order to do so we need to introduce a new terminology.

3.1.
Almost nilpotent and almost abelian groups

A topological group
A
is
almost nilpotent (resp. almost abelian)
if there exist a compact, open, normal subgroup
N\triangleleft A
such that
A/N
is discrete, finitely generated and virtually nilpotent (resp. virtually abelian).
Observe that the existence of a compact, open subgroup
N
implies that
A
is locally compact.
Recall that for locally compact groups it holds the open mapping theorem, and in particular the classical isomorphism theorems provide topological isomorphisms.

The notion of being almost nilpotent or abelian passes to closed subgroups.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
A
be an almost nilpotent (resp. abelian) group. Then any closed subgroup
B<A
is almost nilpotent (resp. abelian).

Proof.

Let
N
be a compact, open, normal subgroup of
A
such that
A/N
is discrete, finitely generated and virtually nilpotent (resp. virtually abelian). The group
N^{\prime}=N\cap B
is open, compact and normal in
B
because
B
is closed. The group
B/N^{\prime}
is topologically isomorphic to a subgroup of
A/N
, thus it is discrete, finitely generated and virtually nilpotent (resp. virtually abelian).
∎

The rank of an almost abelian group is well defined.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
A
be an almost abelian group. Then there exists
k\geq 0
such that for every compact, open, normal subgroup
N\triangleleft A
, the group
A/N
is discrete, finitely generated and virtually abelian of rank
k\geq 0
. Such
k
is called the
rank
of
A
and it is denoted by
\textup{rk}(A)
.

Proof.

Let
N_{0}
be a compact, open, normal subgroup of
A
such that
A/N_{0}
is discrete, finitely generated and virtually abelian. Every virtually abelian, finitely generated group contains a free abelian subgroup of finite index of some rank
k
. Let
A_{0}<A
be the preimage under the projection map
\pi\colon A\to A/N_{0}
of such a free abelian group. Observe that the index of
A_{0}
in
A
is finite and
A_{0}/N_{0}\cap A_{0}
is topologically isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
.

Let
N\triangleleft A
be an arbitrary compact, open, normal subgroup.
The index of
N\cap N_{0}
in both the compact groups
N
and
N_{0}
is finite because it is an open subgroup.
So
A_{0}/N\cap N_{0}\cap A_{0}
is a finite extension of
A_{0}/N_{0}\cap A_{0}\cong\mathbb{Z}^{k}
. Therefore
A_{0}/N\cap N_{0}\cap A_{0}
is discrete, finitely generated and virtually abelian of rank
k
. Since
A_{0}/N\cap A_{0}
is the quotient of
A_{0}/N\cap N_{0}\cap A_{0}
by a finite group, then it is still virtually abelian of same rank
k
. Finally
A/N
contains
A_{0}/N\cap A_{0}
as a finite index group, so it is still discrete, finitely generated and virtually abelian of rank
k
.
∎

We recall that a closed subgroup
H
of a locally compact group
G
is said
cocompact
if the topological space
G/H
is compact. We will use the two definitions of cocompactness, this one and the one for group actions on metric spaces, in different contexts. We hope the reader will not be confused.
Observe that any finite index subgroup is automatically cocompact, and that for discrete groups the two notions coincide. Moreover the following holds.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
G
be a locally compact group and
H,K<G
be closed subgroups.

(i)

If
K<H<G
with
K
cocompact in
H
and
H
cocompact in
G
then
K
is cocompact in
G
;

(ii)

if
H
is cocompact in
G
then
H\cap K
is cocompact in
K
;

(iii)

if
H
and
K
are cocompact in
G
then
H\cap K
is cocompact in
G
.

Proof.

It is classical that if
G
is locally compact then a closed subgroup
H<G
is cocompact if and only if
G=C\cdot H
for some compact subset
C
. Therefore (i) follows. Instead (ii) is straightforward from the definition and (iii) follows by (i) and (ii). Indeed
H\cap K
is cocompact in
K
by (ii), but
K
is cocompact in
G
, so
H\cap K
is cocompact in
G
by (i).
∎

We end this introduction on almost abelian groups with a useful result.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
A
be an almost abelian group and let
B<A
closed. Then
\textup{rk}(B)\leq\textup{rk}(A)
. Moreover
\textup{rk}(B)=\textup{rk}(A)
if and only if
B
is cocompact in
A
.

Proof.

The first inequality is obvious from Lemma
3.2
, so we pass to the second part of the statement. As in the beginning of the proof of Lemma
3.2
we take a compact, open, normal subgroup
N_{0}
of
A
and a finite index subgroup
A_{0}<A
such that
A_{0}/N_{0}
is topologically isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
. Let
\pi\colon A\to A/N_{0}
be the projection map.

Suppose first that
B
is cocompact in
A
. In particular
B_{0}=B\cap A_{0}
is cocompact in
A_{0}
by Lemma
3.3
.(ii). The image of
B_{0}
through
\pi
is therefore cocompact in the image of
A_{0}
. Since the image of
A_{0}
is
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
, then all its cocompact subgroups have finite index. Moreover every finite index subgroup of
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
. Therefore
B_{0}/B_{0}\cap N_{0}
is topologically isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
. By Lemma
3.2
we conclude that
\text{rk}(B)=k=\text{rk}(A)
.

Suppose now
\text{rk}(B)=\text{rk}(A)=k
. Observe that also
B_{0}=B\cap A_{0}
is almost abelian of rank
k
, being a cocompact subgroup of
B
since it has finite index. Lemma
3.2
says that
B_{0}/N\cap B_{0}
is discrete, finitely generated and virtually abelian of rank
k
, and moreover it is topologically isomorphic to a subgroup of
A_{0}/A_{0}\cap N_{0}\cong\mathbb{Z}^{k}
. In particular
B_{0}/B_{0}\cap N_{0}
is free abelian of rank
k
, so it has finite index in
A_{0}/A_{0}\cap N_{0}
. We deduce that
\pi^{-1}(\pi(B_{0}))
has finite index in
A_{0}
. Moreover
\pi^{-1}(\pi(B_{0}))=B_{0}\cdot N_{0}
, so
B_{0}
is cocompact in
\pi^{-1}(\pi(B_{0}))
. Since
\pi^{-1}(\pi(B_{0}))
has finite index in
A_{0}
, so in
A
, we deduce that
B_{0}
, and so
B
, is cocompact in
A
.
∎

3.2.
The Margulis Lemma for totally disconnected group actions

For discrete groups
\Gamma<\textup{Isom}(X)
of a proper metric space
X
the following version of the Margulis’ Lemma, due to Breuillard-Green-Tao, holds.

Proposition 3.5

(
[
BGT11
]
, Corollary 11.17)
.

Let
K\geq 1
. There exists
\varepsilon(K)
such that the following is true. Let
X
be any proper metric space and let
x\in X
be a point such that
\textup{Cov}(\overline{B}(x,4),1)\leq K
. Let
\Gamma<\textup{Isom}(X)
be discrete. Then the almost stabilizer
\overline{\Gamma}_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}(x)
is virtually nilpotent for all
0\leq\varepsilon^{\prime}\leq\varepsilon(K)
.

Our aim is to extend this result to totally disconnected groups of isometries, and in particular to prove Theorem
E
.
The proof of Proposition
3.5
is based on the precise description of the structure of
K
-approximate subgroups of discrete groups developed in
[
BGT11
]
.

Definition 3.6
.

Let
K>1
. A
K
-approximate subgroup of a group
G
is a symmetric subset of
G
containing the identity for which there exists a symmetric subset
X\subseteq G
of cardinality at most
K
such that
A\cdot A\subseteq X\cdot A
.

The more general structure theory for open precompact approximate subgroups of locally compact groups was studied in
[
Car15
]
and partially improved in
[
TT21
]
. We report here a slightly modified version of
[
TT21
, Corollary 6.11]
which adapts better to our situation.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
K\geq 1
. Then there exists
K^{\prime}=K^{\prime}(K)
, depending only on
K
, such that the
following holds. Assume
G
is a totally disconnected, locally compact group generated by a compact symmetric set
S
containing the identity. Let
A
be an open precompact
K
-approximate subgroup of
G
such that
S^{K^{\prime}}\subseteq A
. Then there are subgroups

N^{\prime},L\triangleleft G
with
N^{\prime}\subseteq A^{6}\cap L
such that
N^{\prime}
is compact,
L
is open and has
index
O_{K}(1)
in
G
, and such that
L/N^{\prime}
is a Lie group of dimension at most

O_{K}(1)
.

Here the notation
O_{K}(1)
stays for a quantity which is bounded above by a constant depending only on
K
. It is the same notation used in
[
BGT11
]
,
[
Car15
]
and
[
TT21
]
.
The proof is a straightforward modification of
[
TT21
, Corollary 6.11]
, where one uses
A
in place of
S^{n}
therein.
This gives the analogue of
[
BGT11
, Corollary 11.2]
for totally disconnected, locally compact groups that are compactly generated.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
K>1
. Then there exists
K^{\prime}=K^{\prime}(K)
, depending only on
K
, such
that the following holds true. Assume
G
is a totally disconnected, locally compact group generated by a compact symmetric set
S
containing the identity. Let
A
be an open precompact
K
-approximate subgroup of
G
such that
S^{K^{\prime}}\subseteq A
. Then there exist
G_{1}<G
of index
\leq O_{K}(1)
and a compact, open, normal subgroup
N\triangleleft G
, with
N\subseteq G_{1}
, such that
G_{1}/N
is discrete, finitely generated and nilpotent of rank and step
\leq O_{K}(1)
. In particular
G
is almost nilpotent.

Proof.

Let
K^{\prime}
be the maximum between the constant of Proposition
3.7
and the one provided by
[
BGT11
, Corollary 11.2]
.
By Proposition
3.7
we can find subgroups
N^{\prime},L\triangleleft G
such that

(a)

L
is open and it has finite index in
G
;

(b)

N^{\prime}
is compact and
L/N^{\prime}
is a Lie group.

Condition (a) implies
L
is also closed, so in particular it is totally disconnected. The quotient of a totally disconnected, locally compact group by a closed, normal subgroup is again totally disconnected. Therefore by (b) we deduce that
L/N^{\prime}
is discrete. In particular
N^{\prime}
is open in
L
, so also in
G
, because it is the continuous preimage of the open set
\{1\}\subseteq L/N^{\prime}
.
Let
\pi\colon G\to G/N^{\prime}
be the projection map. The projections of the precompact sets
S
and
A
are precompact, hence finite. Clearly
\pi(S)
generates
G/N^{\prime}
,
\pi(A)
is a
K
-approximate group in the sense of
[
BGT11
]
and
\pi(S)^{K^{\prime}}=\pi(S^{K^{\prime}})\subseteq\pi(A)
. Therefore
G/N^{\prime}
is finitely generated and it satisfies the assumptions of
[
BGT11
, Corollary 11.2]
. Hence we can find
\overline{G_{1}}<G/N^{\prime}
of index at most
O_{K}(1)
and a finite subgroup
\overline{N}\triangleleft G/N^{\prime}
, with
\overline{N}<\overline{G_{1}}
, such that
\overline{G_{1}}/\overline{N}
is nilpotent of rank and step
\leq O_{K}(1)
. In order to get the thesis it is enough to take
G_{1}=\pi^{-1}(\overline{G_{1}})
and
N=\pi^{-1}(\overline{N})
. Indeed
N
is clearly normal, it is open because it contains
N^{\prime}
and it is compact because
N/N^{\prime}\cong\overline{N}
is finite.
∎

Finally we can argue as in
[
BGT11
, Corollary 11.17]
to get the announced version of the Margulis lemma.

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
K\geq 1
. There exists
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(K)
such that the following is true. Let
X
be any proper metric space and let
x\in X
be a point such that
\textup{Cov}(\overline{B}(x,4),1)\leq K
. Let
G<\textup{Isom}(X)
be closed and totally disconnected. Then the open almost stabilizer
G_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}(x)
is almost nilpotent for all
0\leq\varepsilon^{\prime}\leq\varepsilon(K)
.

Proof of Theorem
3.9
.

The set
A:=S_{2}(x)
is open and it is precompact because
G
is closed and
X
is proper.
Take
K
points
x_{i}\in\overline{B}(x,4)
such that
\overline{B}(x,4)\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{K}\overline{B}(x_{i},1)
. Suppose that for
i=1,\ldots,k\leq K
there is
g_{i}\in S_{4}(x)
such that
d(x_{i},g_{i}x)\leq 1
, while this does not happen for
k+1\leq i\leq K
. For every
g\in A^{2}\subseteq S_{4}(x)
we find
i
such that
d(gx,x_{i})\leq 1
, so
d(gx,g_{i}x)\leq 2
, i.e.
g_{i}^{-1}g\in S_{2}(x)
. In other words
A^{2}\subseteq S_{4}(x)\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}g_{i}\cdot S_{2}(x)
. Therefore
A
is an open, precompact,
K
-approximate subgroup of
G
. Now let
K^{\prime}=K^{\prime}(K)
be the constant of Proposition
3.8
and take
\varepsilon(K):=\frac{2}{K^{\prime}}
. The thesis holds for this choice of
\varepsilon(K)
.
∎

The conclusion of Theorem
3.9
can be improved for totally disconnected, cocompact groups acting on geodesically complete, CAT
(0)
-spaces.

Corollary 3.10
.

Let
P_{0},r_{0}>0
. Then there exists
\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}(P_{0},r_{0})>0
such that the following holds. Let
X
be a proper, geodesically complete,
(P_{0},r_{0})
-packed,
CAT
(0)
-space. Let
G
be a closed, totally disconnected, cocompact group of isometries of
X
. Then both the open and closed almost stabilizers
G_{\varepsilon}(x)
and
\overline{G}_{\varepsilon}(x)
are almost abelian, for all
x\in X
and all
0\leq\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}
.

Proof.

First of all
\text{Cov}(4,1)
is bounded in terms of the packing constants by Proposition
2.1
and (
2
). Therefore by Theorem
3.9
we can find
\varepsilon_{0}
depending only on
P_{0},r_{0}
such that every open stabilizer
G_{\varepsilon}(x)
with
0\leq\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}
is almost nilpotent. Moreover the same is true for the closed stabilizers because by Theorem
2.6
.(b) the sets
\overline{S}_{r}(x)
are open and the proof of Theorem
3.9
holds also for these open, compact, approximate subgroups.

Let us prove that the open almost stabilizers above are actually almost abelian. The same proof will apply for the closed ones. The action of
G
is by semisimple isometries by Theorem
2.6
.(ii). Let
N
be a compact, open, normal subgroup of
G_{\varepsilon}(x)
such that
G_{\varepsilon}(x)/N
is discrete, finitely generated and virtually nilpotent. We can find a finite index subgroup
G^{\prime}<G_{\varepsilon}(x)
such that
G^{\prime}/G^{\prime}\cap N
is nilpotent. The subset
\text{Min}(N)
is closed, convex and non-empty because
N
is compact (
[
BH13
, Corollary II.2.8]
): in particular it is a CAT
(0)
-space. The group
G/N
acts by semisimple isometries (
[
BH13
, Proposition II.6.2]
) on the CAT
(0)
-space
\text{Min}(N)
. The kernel of this action is another normal subgroup
N_{0}
of
G
which is open (because it contains
N
) and compact (because it fixes some point of
X
). Moreover
\text{Min}(N)=\text{Min}(N_{0})
. Therefore the group
G/N_{0}
acts freely on
\text{Min}(N_{0})
, in particular so does the group
G^{\prime}/G^{\prime}\cap N_{0}
which is discrete, finitely generated and nilpotent. It is not restrictive, up to take a finite index subgroup of
G^{\prime}
, to suppose that
G^{\prime}/G^{\prime}\cap N_{0}
is torsion-free. By
[
FSY04
, Theorem 5.3.(i)]
we conclude that
G^{\prime}/G^{\prime}\cap N_{0}
is actually abelian. Therefore
G_{\varepsilon}(x)/N_{0}
is discrete, finitely generated and virtually abelian, i.e.
G_{\varepsilon}(x)
is almost abelian.
∎

We will often refer to the constant
\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}(P_{0},r_{0})
as the
Margulis’ constant
.

From the proof of Theorem
3.9
we can extract the following fact that will be used in Section
6
. For simplicity it will be presented for geodesically complete, CAT
(0)
-spaces, but a similar version holds for proper metric spaces.

Corollary 3.11
.

Let
P_{0},r_{0}>0
. Then there exists
M_{0}=M_{0}(P_{0},r_{0})
such that the following holds.
Let
X
be a proper, geodesically complete,
(P_{0},r_{0})
-packed,
CAT
(0)
-space, let
G<\textup{Isom}(X)
be closed and let
\mu
be a left-invariant Haar measure of
G
. Then

\mu(\overline{S}_{r}(x)^{p})\leq M_{0}^{p-1}\cdot\mu(\overline{S}_{r}(x))

for every
x\in X
, every
r\geq 0
and every
p\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

As in the proof of Theorem
3.9
we find that
\overline{S}_{r}(x)^{2}\subseteq\overline{S}_{2r}(x)\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}g_{i}\overline{S}_{r}(x)
, for some
k\leq\textup{Cov}(2r,\frac{r}{2})
. By (
2
) and Proposition
2.1
.(i) we get
k\leq P_{0}(1+P_{0})^{\frac{2r}{\min\{\frac{r}{4},r_{0}\}}-1}=:M_{0}
. By induction on

we have

\overline{S}_{r}(x)^{p}=\overline{S}_{r}(x)^{p-1}\cdot\overline{S}_{r}(x)\subseteq\bigcup_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{p-1}=1}^{k}g_{i_{1}}\cdots g_{i_{p-1}}\overline{S}_{r}(x).

Therefore
\mu(\overline{S}_{r}(x)^{p})\leq M_{0}^{p-1}\cdot\mu(\overline{S}_{r}(x))
.
∎

3.3.
Almost abelian groups acting on CAT
(0)
-spaces

Corollary
3.10
suggests that almost abelian groups acting on CAT
(0)
-spaces play a special role. In this section we will describe the geometric properties of such groups, generalizing
[
CS23
, Section 2.4]
.

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
X
be a proper
CAT
(0)
-space and let
A<\textup{Isom}(X)
be closed, semisimple and almost abelian of rank
k
. Then
k
is the unique integer for which the following holds.

(i)

There exists a closed, convex,
A
-invariant subset
C(A)
of
X
that splits as
W\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
such that

(i.a)

each
g\in A
preserves the product decomposition and acts as the identity on the first component;

(i.b)

the image
A_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}
of
A
under the projection
A\to\textup{Isom}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
is a crystallographic
group;

(i.c)

if
S
is a compact, symmetric, generating set for
A
containing the identity, then there exists a finite subset
\Sigma\subseteq S^{4J(k)+2}
whose projection
\Sigma_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}
on
Isom
(\mathbb{R}^{k})
generates

\mathcal{L}(A_{\mathbb{R}^{k}})
, where
J(k)
is the constant of Proposition
2.12
.

(ii)

There exists
\partial A\subseteq\partial X
, called the trace at infinity of
A
, such that

(ii.a)

\partial A=\partial\textup{Conv}(Ax)
for every
x\in X
;

(ii.b)

\partial A
is closed, convex,
A
-invariant and isometric to
\mathbb{S}^{k-1}
;

(ii.c)

if
g
is an isometry of
X
then
\partial(gAg^{-1})=g\partial A
;

(ii.d)

if
B<A
is closed then
\partial B\subseteq\partial A
with equality if and only if
\textup{rk}(B)=\textup{rk}(A)
.

Proof.

Let
N
be an open, compact, normal subgroup of
A
. The set Min
(N)
is closed, convex, and
A
-invariant because
N
is normal in
A
. It is also non-empty because
N
is compact (cp.
[
BH13
, Corollary II.2.8]
). Let
N_{0}
be the kernel of the action of
A
on Min
(N)
. It is again a compact, open, normal subgroup of
A
, because it contains
N
. Moreover
\text{Min}(N_{0})\subseteq\text{Min}(N)
because
N\subseteq N_{0}
, and by definition we have
\text{Min}(N_{0})=\text{Min}(N)
. It follows that the induced action of
A/N_{0}
on the CAT
(0)
-space
\text{Min}(N_{0})
is faithful. Moreover
A/N_{0}
is a discrete, finitely generated, virtually abelian group of rank
k
by Lemma
3.2
, whose action on
\text{Min}(N_{0})
is by semisimple isometries (
[
BH13
, Proposition II.6.2.(4)]
). Then (i) follows by the classical statement for discrete groups (cp.
[
BH13
, Corollary II.7.2]
,
[
CS23
, Proposition 2.9 and Lemma 2.10]
).

Suppose
h
is another integer for which (i) holds. Let
N
be the kernel of the action of
A
on
C(A)=W\times\mathbb{R}^{h}
. It is clearly compact and normal. It is also open because the action of
A
on
C(A)
is discrete by (i.a) and (i.b). Therefore the group
A/N
must be discrete, finitely generated and virtually abelian of rank

by Lemma
3.2
. However the group
A/N
acts faithfully and cocompactly on
\mathbb{R}^{h}
, so it must have rank

, as a discrete, virtually abelian group. Therefore

.

Let
C(A)=W\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
be as in (i). Fix
w\in W
and call
Z=\{w\}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
: it is again an
A
-invariant, convex subset because of (i.a). We claim that
\partial Z=\partial\text{Conv}(Ax)
for every
x\in X
. Fix
x\in X
and set
R=d(x,Z)
. By
A
-invariance of
Z
we have that
d(ax,Z)=R
for every
a\in A
. So
Ax\subseteq\overline{B}(Z,R)
. The latter is a convex set, so
\text{Conv}(Ax)\subseteq\overline{B}(Z,R)
and
\partial\text{Conv}(Ax)\subseteq\partial\overline{B}(Z,R)
. By convexity of the distance function every geodesic ray of
\overline{B}(Z,R)
must be at constant distance from
Z
, i.e. parallel to it. This shows that
\partial\text{Conv}(Ax)\subseteq\partial\overline{B}(Z,R)=\partial Z
. For the other inclusion we call
z\in Z
the projection of
x
on
Z
. We fix any
\xi\in\partial Z
and we consider the geodesic ray
[z,\xi]
. The action of
A
on
Z
is cocompact by (i.b) so we can find some
D>0
and elements
a_{j}\in A
such that
d(a_{j}z,[z,\xi])\leq D
and
d(z,a_{j}z)
tends to
+\infty
. They belong to
\text{Conv}(Ax)
. Since
d(x,a_{j}x)\geq d(z,a_{j}z)-2R
tends to
+\infty
, then by properness of
X
the segments
[x,a_{j}x]
subconverge to a geodesic ray
[x,\zeta]
. This ray is contained in
\text{Conv}(Ax)
because each segment
[x,a_{j}x]
is. Moreover
d(a_{j}x,[z,\xi])\leq R+D
, so
\xi=\zeta
. This shows that
\partial\text{Conv}(Ax)=\partial Z
. We set
\partial A:=\partial Z=\partial\text{Conv}(Ax)
and we claim it satisfies (ii). The fact that
\partial A
is closed, convex,
A
-invariant and isometric to
\mathbb{S}^{k-1}
is clear since
\partial Z
has these properties.
In order to get (ii.c) we fix
x\in X
and we use twice the characterization in (ii.a) to get

\partial(gAg^{-1})=\partial\text{Conv}((gAg^{-1})gx)=g\partial\text{Conv}(Ax)=g\partial A.

Moreover (ii.a) gives directly the inclusion in (ii.d). The equality case follows again from (ii.a) applied to some
z\in Z\cong\mathbb{R}^{k}
on which both
A
and
B
act as discrete groups.
∎

The following is an immediate consequence of Proposition
3.12
.

4.
The splitting theorem

In this section we will prove the analogue of the splitting Theorem
[
CS23
, Theorem D]
or more precisely of
[
CS23
, Theorem 4.1]
for sufficiently collapsed totally disconnected group actions, i.e. for actions with free-systole sufficiently small. We will prove a slightly improved version that will be useful in Section
6.3
, the improvement is in items (v) and (vi). After our work on the Margulis Lemma and on the properties of almost abelian groups acting on CAT
(0)
-spaces we can essentially mimic the proof of
[
CS23
]
.

To set the notation, recall that for any proper, geodesically complete,
(P_{0},r_{0})
-packed, CAT
(0)
-space

we have the upper bound
\dim(X)\leq n_{0}=P_{0}/2
provided by Proposition
2.1
and the Margulis’ constant
\varepsilon_{0}
(only depending on
P_{0},r_{0}
) given by Proposition
3.10
.
For a totally disconnected subgroup
G<\textup{Isom}(X)

recall the definition (
4
) of the subgroup
\overline{G}_{r}(x)<G
generated by
\overline{S}_{r}(x)
given in Section
2.4
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Given positive constants
P_{0},r_{0},D_{0}
,
there exists a function

\sigma_{P_{0},r_{0},D_{0}}:(0,\varepsilon_{0}]\rightarrow(0,\varepsilon_{0}]
(depending only on the parameters
P_{0},r_{0},D_{0}
) such that the following holds.
Let
X
be a proper, geodesically complete,
(P_{0},r_{0})
-packed,
\textup{CAT}(0)
-space, and
G<\textup{Isom}(X)
be closed, totally disconnected and
D_{0}
-cocompact.
For any chosen
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0}]
, if
\textup{sys}^{\diamond}(G,X)\leq\sigma_{P_{0},r_{0},D_{0}}(\varepsilon)
then:

(i)

the space
X
splits isometrically as
Y\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
, with
k\geq 1
, and this splitting is
G
-invariant;

(ii)

there exists
{\varepsilon^{\ast}}\in(\sigma_{P_{0},r_{0},D_{0}}(\varepsilon),\varepsilon)

such that the rank of the almost abelian subgroups
\overline{G}_{{\varepsilon^{\ast}}}(x)
is exactly
k
, for all
x\in X
;

(iii)

the traces at infinity
\partial\overline{G}_{{\varepsilon^{\ast}}}(x)
equal the boundary
{\mathbb{S}}^{k-1}
of the convex subsets
\{y\}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
, for all
x\in X
and all
y\in Y
;

(iv)

for every
x\in X
there exists
y\in Y
such that
\overline{G}_{\varepsilon^{\ast}}(x)
preserves
\{y\}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
. The closure of the projection of
\overline{G}_{\varepsilon^{\ast}}(x)
on
\textup{Isom}(Y)
is compact;

(v)

the projection
of
\overline{G}_{\varepsilon^{\ast}}(x)
on
\textup{Isom}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
is a crystallographic group, whose maximal lattice
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon^{*}}(x)
satisfies
\lambda(\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon^{*}}(x))\leq\varepsilon^{*}/2\sqrt{n_{0}}
and is
(\varepsilon^{*}/2)
-cocompact;

(vi)

the groups
\overline{G}_{\varepsilon^{*}}(x)
are almost commensurated for all
x\in X
.

Here, by
G
-invariant splitting
we mean that every isometry of
G
preserves the product decomposition. By
[
BH13
, Proposition I.5.3.(4)]
we can see
G
as a subgroup of
\textup{Isom}(Y)\times\textup{Isom}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
. In particular it is meaningful to talk about the projection of
\overline{G}_{\varepsilon^{*}}(x)
on
\text{Isom}(Y)
and
\text{Isom}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
.
We call the integer
1\leq k\leq n_{0}
the
{\varepsilon^{\ast}}
-splitting rank
of
X
. The fact that

is at most

follows by Corollary
3.13
. Observe that we do not need any unimodularity assumption on

. However we will say even more on the projection of
\overline{G}_{\varepsilon^{*}}(x)
on
\text{Isom}(Y)
in Section
6.3
in case

is unimodular. The almost commensurability condition of item (vi) is introduced below: it plays a fundamental role in the proof of Theorem
4.1
.

4.1.
Almost commensurability

Let
G
be a topological group. We say that two subgroups
H,K<G
are
almost commensurable
if
H\cap K
is cocompact in both
H
and
K
. A subgroup
H<G
is
almost commensurated
in
G
if
H
and
gHg^{-1}
are almost commensurable for every
g\in G
. Observe that if
G
is discrete then this notion coincides with the classical notion of commensurated subgroup.

Lemma 4.2
.

Suppose
S
is a generating set for
G
. If
H
and
gHg^{-1}
are almost commensurable for all
g\in S
then
H
is almost commensurated.

Proof.

Let
g=g_{1}\cdots g_{k}
be an element of
G
, with
g_{i}\in S
. We prove that
H
and
gHg^{-1}
are almost commensurable by induction on
k
. The case
k=1
is the assumption. In the general case, by inductive assumption we have

(a)

g_{2}\cdots g_{k}Hg_{k}^{-1}\cdots g_{2}^{-1}\cap H
is cocompact in both
g_{2}\cdots g_{k}Hg_{k}^{-1}\cdots g_{2}^{-1}
and
H
;

(b)

g_{1}Hg_{1}^{-1}\cap H
is cocompact in both
g_{1}Hg_{1}^{-1}
and
H
.

Conjugating (a) by
g_{1}
we also get

(c)

gHg^{-1}\cap g_{1}Hg_{1}^{-1}
is cocompact in both
gHg^{-1}
and
g_{1}Hg_{1}^{-1}
.

Combining (b) and (c) we conclude that
gHg^{-1}\cap g_{1}Hg_{1}^{-1}\cap H
is cocompact in
g_{1}Hg_{1}^{-1}\cap H
and so in
H
, by Lemma
3.3
. A fortiori
gHg^{-1}\cap H
is cocompact in
H
. In a similar way, using again Lemma
3.3
, we also have that
gHg^{-1}\cap H
is cocompact in
gHg^{-1}
.
∎

Lemma
3.3
implies the following property.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
G
be a locally compact group and let
K<H<G
be closed subgroups, with
K
cocompact in
H
. Then
H
is almost commensurated if and only if
K
is.

We will show how an almost abelian subgroup which is almost commensurated provides an associated metric splitting of the space, generalizing the discrete case (cp.
[
CS23
, Proposition 4.5]
,
[
CM19
]
). We just need to recall an additional definition. Given
Z\subseteq\partial X
we say that a subset
Y\subseteq X
is
Z
-boundary-minimal if it is closed, convex,
\partial Y=Z
and
Y
is minimal with these properties. The union of all the
Z
-boundary-minimal sets is denoted by Bd-Min
(Z)
.

Lemma 4.4

(
[
CS23
, Lemma 4.4]
,
[
CM09
, Proposition 3.6]
)
.

Let
X
be a proper
\textup{CAT}(0)
-space and let
Z
be a closed, convex subset of
\partial X
which is isometric to
\mathbb{S}^{k-1}
. Then each
Z
-boundary-minimal subset of
X
is isometric to
\mathbb{R}^{k}
and
Bd-Min
(Z)
is a closed, convex subset of
X
which splits isometrically as
Y\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
. Moreover
Z
coincides with the boundary at infinity of all the slices
\{y\}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
, for
y\in Y
.

We can now generalize
[
CS23
, Proposition 4.5]
.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
X
be a proper, geodesically complete,
\textup{CAT}(0)
-space, and
G<\textup{Isom}(X)
be closed, totally disconnected and cocompact.
If
A<G
is a semisimple, almost abelian, almost commensurated subgroup of
G
of rank
k
then we have
X=\textup{Bd-Min}(\partial A)
and
X
splits isometrically and
G
-invariantly as
Y\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
. Moreover, the projection of
A
on
\textup{Isom}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
is a crystallographic group and the closure of the projection of
A
on
\textup{Isom}(Y)
is compact. The splitting
X=Y\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
satisfies the following properties:

(i)

the trace at infinity
\partial A
is
G
-invariant and coincides with the boundary of each slice
\{y\}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
, for all
y\in Y
;

(ii)

if
A^{\prime}<A
is another almost abelian, almost commensurated subgroup of
G
of rank

then the splittings
X=Y\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
and

associated respectively to

and

are compatible, i.e.
Y^{\prime}
is isometric to
Y\times\mathbb{R}^{k-k^{\prime}}
.

Proof.

The trace at infinity
\partial A
of

is closed, convex and isometric to
\mathbb{S}^{k-1}
, by Proposition
3.12
.(ii.b). We claim it is
G
-invariant. If
g\in G
then
A\cap gAg^{-1}
is closed and cocompact in both
A
and
gAg^{-1}
. Moreover
A\cap gAg^{-1}
is almost abelian by Lemma
3.1
, and
\text{rk}(A\cap gAg^{-1})=\text{rk}(A)=\text{rk}(gAg^{-1})=k
by Lemma
3.4
.
By (ii.c) and (ii.d) of Proposition
3.12
we have

\partial A=\partial(A\cap gAg^{-1})=\partial(gAg^{-1})=g\partial A,

for all
g\in G
.
By Lemma
4.4
applied to

we deduce that
\text{Bd-Min}(\partial A)
is a closed, convex subset of
X
which splits isometrically as
Y\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
, and that
\partial A
coincides with the boundary at infinity of all sets
\{y\}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
. Each element of
G
sends a
\partial A
-boundary-minimal subset into a
\partial A
-boundary-minimal subset because
\partial A
is
G
-invariant, therefore
\text{Bd-Min}(\partial A)
itself is
G
-invariant. Since
G
is cocompact, the action of
G
on
X
is minimal (
[
CM09
, Lemma 3.13]
). We deduce that

, and so

splits isometrically and

-invariantly as

, which proves the first assertion and (i).

The fact that the projection of

on
\textup{Isom}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
is a crystallographic group follows from Proposition
3.12
. Indeed any set
C(A)=W\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
as in
3.12
.(i) is compatible with the splitting we just proved in the sense that

, because
W\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
is union of
\partial A
-boundary minimal sets as follows by (i.a).
Moreover
A
acts as the identity on

. Therefore the projection of
A
on
Y
is precompact since it fixes a point.

Suppose now to have another almost abelian subgroup
A^{\prime}<A
of rank
k^{\prime}
which is commensurated in
G
.
Let
X=Y^{\prime}\times\mathbb{R}^{k^{\prime}}
be the splitting associated to
A^{\prime}
. Let
x\in X
be a point and write it as
(y,{\bf v})\in Y\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
and
(y^{\prime},{\bf v^{\prime}})\in Y^{\prime}\times\mathbb{R}^{k^{\prime}}
. Then, by the first part of the proof and by Proposition
3.12
we have

\partial(\{y^{\prime}\}\times\mathbb{R}^{k^{\prime}})=\partial A^{\prime}\subseteq\partial A=\partial(\{y\}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}).

It follows that
\{y^{\prime}\}\times\mathbb{R}^{k^{\prime}}\subseteq\{y\}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
, so the parallel slices associated to

are contained in the parallel slices associated to

. Decomposing

as the orhogonal sum of
\mathbb{R}^{k^{\prime}}
and
\mathbb{R}^{k-k^{\prime}}
, we also deduce that the sets
\{y\}\times\mathbb{R}^{k-k^{\prime}}
are parallel for all

(since the slices of

are all parallel). Therefore,

is also isometric to
(Y\times\mathbb{R}^{k-k^{\prime}})\times\mathbb{R}^{k^{\prime}}
, which implies that

is isometric to
Y\times\mathbb{R}^{k-k^{\prime}}
and proves (ii).
∎

6.
Convergence and collapsing

In this last section we will finally attack Theorem
B
using the tools developed until now.
First of all, for the reader’s convenience, we briefly recall the notion of equivariant Gromov-Hausdorff convergence and ultralimits.

6.1.
Equivariant Gromov-Hausdorff convergence and ultralimits

An
isometric action
on a pointed space is a triple
(X,x,G)
where
X
is a proper metric space,
x\in X
is a basepoint and
G<\text{Isom}(X)
is a closed subgroup. An
equivariant isometry
between isometric actions of pointed spaces
(X,x,G)
and
(Y,y,H)
is an isometry
F\colon X\to Y
such that

–

F(x)=y
;

–

F_{*}\colon\textup{Isom}(X)\to\textup{Isom}(Y)
defined by
F_{*}(g)=F\circ g\circ F^{-1}
is an isomorphism between
G
and
H
.

The best known notion of convergence for isometric actions of pointed spaces is the
equivariant pointed Gromov-Hausdorff
convergence, as defined by K. Fukaya in
[
Fuk86
]
:
we will write
(X_{j},x_{j},G_{j})\underset{\textup{eq-pGH}}{\longrightarrow}(X_{\infty},x_{\infty},G_{\infty})

for a sequence
(X_{j},x_{j},G_{j})
of isometric actions converging in the equivariant pointed Gromov-Hausdorff sense to an isometric action
(X_{\infty},x_{\infty},G_{\infty})
.

Forgetting about the group actions and considering just pointed metric spaces
(X_{j},x_{j})
, this convergence reduces to the
pointed Gromov-Hausdorff
convergence: we will write
(X_{j},x_{j})\underset{\textup{pGH}}{\longrightarrow}(X_{\infty},x_{\infty})
for a sequence of pointed metric spaces
(X_{j},x_{j})
converging to the pointed metric space
(X_{\infty},x_{\infty})
in this sense. If moreover all the spaces
X_{j}
under consideration are compact we can even drop the basepoints, and we will simply write
X_{j}\underset{\textup{GH}}{\longrightarrow}X_{\infty}
when
X_{j}
converges to the compact metric space
X_{\infty}
.

We do not recall here the definition of equivariant, pointed Gromov-Hausdorff convergence.
An equivalent approach uses ultralimits. We present it in this section, and then we recall the relations with the usual Gromov-Hausdorff convergence, referring to
[
Jan17
]
and
[
Cav22a
]
.

A
non-principal ultrafilter

\omega
is a finitely additive measure on
\mathbb{N}
such that
\omega(A)\in\{0,1\}
for every
A\subseteq\mathbb{N}
and
\omega(A)=0
for every finite subset of
\mathbb{N}
. Accordingly, we will write
\omega
-a.s.
or
for
\omega
-a.e.
(j)

We start by improving

Proposition 6.6
.

Suppose that
(X_{j},x_{j},G_{j})\underset{\textup{eq-pGH}}{\longrightarrow}(X_{\infty},x_{\infty},G_{\infty})
, with
X_{j}
geodesic spaces. Endow

(resp.

and
(G_{j},\textup{id},d_{x_{j}}^{\textup{r}})\underset{\textup{pGH}}{\longrightarrow}(G_{\infty},\textup{id},d_{x_{\infty}}^{\textup{r}})
.

Proof.

We do the proof for the left invariant metric, the other case is similar.
By definition
X_{\infty}
is proper, so
\textup{Isom}(X_{\infty})
is locally compact. Moreover

is closed, so locally compact as well. As recalled in Section
2.4
the metric

is therefore proper. This means that we are in position to apply Proposition
6.1
. In particular the thesis is equivalent to prove that
(G_{\omega},\text{id},d_{x_{\omega}}^{\ell})
is isometric as pointed metric space to
\omega
-
\lim(G_{j},\text{id},d_{x_{j}}^{\ell})
for every non-principal ultrafilter
\omega
. Let us fix a non-principal ultrafilter
\omega
. Let
(g_{j})
be an admissible sequence defining a point of
\omega
-
\lim(G_{j},\text{id},d_{x_{j}}^{\ell})
. This means that
\omega
-
\lim d_{x_{j}}^{\ell}(g_{j},\textup{id})<+\infty
. This implies
\omega
-
\lim d(g_{j}x_{j},x_{j})<+\infty
by the very definition (
6
), i.e. the sequence
g_{j}\in G_{j}
defines a limit isometry
g_{\omega}\in G_{\omega}
. We set
\Phi\colon\omega
-
\lim(G_{j},\text{id},d_{x_{j}}^{\ell})\to G_{\omega}
defined by
(g_{j})\mapsto g_{\omega}
. The distance function on
\omega
-
\lim(G_{j},\text{id},d_{x_{j}}^{\ell})
will be denoted by
\omega
-
\lim d_{x_{j}}^{\ell}
.

Good definition.
Let us take two admissible sequences
(g_{j})
,
(h_{j})
defining the same point of
\omega
-
\lim(G_{j},\text{id},d_{x_{j}}^{\ell})
, i.e.
\omega
-
\lim d_{x_{j}}^{\ell}(g_{j},h_{j})=0
. By definition, for every
\varepsilon>0
we have
d_{x_{j}}^{\ell}(g_{j},h_{j})<\varepsilon
for
\omega
-a.e.
(j)
. By (
6

R>\frac{1}{\varepsilon}
such that
d_{x_{j},R}^{\ell}(g_{j},h_{j})<\varepsilon
for
\omega
-a.e.
(j)
. If we now fix some
R>0
and we let

go to zero we get that

coincides with

on

. Since this is true for every

we conclude that

, so
\Phi
is well defined.

Isometric embedding.
We now prove that
\Phi
is an isometry. By definition

d_{x_{\omega}}^{\ell}(\Phi((g_{j})),\Phi((h_{j})))=d_{x_{\omega}}^{\ell}(g_{\omega},h_{\omega})=\inf_{R>0}\left\{\frac{1}{R}+d_{x_{\omega},R}^{\ell}(g_{\omega},h_{\omega})\right\}.

Recalling (
5

d_{x_{\omega},R}^{\ell}(g_{\omega},h_{\omega})=\max_{y_{\omega}\in\overline{B}(x_{\omega},R)}d(g_{\omega}y_{\omega},h_{\omega}y_{\omega})=\max_{y_{\omega}\in\overline{B}(x_{\omega},R)}\omega\text{-}\lim d(g_{j}y_{j},h_{j}y_{j}).

Using the fact that
\overline{B}(x_{\omega},R)=\omega
-
\lim\overline{B}(x_{j},R)
(see
[
CS21
, Lemma A.8]
) it is straightforward to conclude that

d_{x_{\omega},R}^{\ell}(g_{\omega},h_{\omega})=\omega\text{-}\lim\max_{y_{j}\in\overline{B}(x_{j},R)}d(g_{j}y_{j},h_{j}y_{j})=\omega\text{-}\lim d_{x_{j},R}^{\ell}(g_{j},h_{j}).

Therefore
d_{x_{\omega}}^{\ell}(g_{\omega},h_{\omega})=\inf_{R>0}\left\{\omega\text{-}\lim\left(\frac{1}{R}+d_{x_{j},R}^{\ell}(g_{j},h_{j})\right)\right\}
.
On the other side we know that
d_{x_{j}}^{\ell}(g_{j},h_{j})=\inf_{R>0}\left\{\frac{1}{R}+d_{x_{j},R}^{\ell}(g_{j},h_{j})\right\}
. We obtain

\omega\text{-}\lim d_{x_{j}}^{\ell}((g_{j}),(h_{j})):=\omega\text{-}\lim d_{x_{j}}^{\ell}(g_{j},h_{j})\leq d_{x_{\omega}}^{\ell}(g_{\omega},h_{\omega}).

To prove the other inequality we fix
\varepsilon>0
and for every

we take
R_{j}>0
such that
\frac{1}{R_{j}}+d_{x_{j},R_{j}}^{\ell}(g_{j},h_{j})\leq d_{x_{j}}^{\ell}(g_{j},h_{j})+\varepsilon
. Since we know that
\omega\text{-}\lim d_{x_{j}}^{\ell}(g_{j},h_{j})
is finite we deduce that
\omega
-
\lim R_{j}>0
. If
\omega
-
\lim R_{j}=:R_{\omega}
is finite then we conclude, as before, that
\omega
-
\lim\frac{1}{R_{j}}+d_{x_{j},R_{j}}^{\ell}(g_{j},h_{j})=\frac{1}{R_{\omega}}+d_{x_{\omega},R_{\omega}}^{\ell}(g_{\omega},h_{\omega})
. So

d_{x_{\omega}}^{\ell}(g_{\omega},h_{\omega})\leq\frac{1}{R_{\omega}}+d_{x_{\omega},R_{\omega}}^{\ell}(g_{\omega},h_{\omega})\leq\omega\text{-}\lim d_{x_{j}}^{\ell}(g_{j},h_{j})+\varepsilon.

If
R_{\omega}=+\infty
we proceed as follows. We fix
R>\frac{1}{\varepsilon}
. For
\omega
-a.e.
(j)
we have
R_{j}\geq R
. Therefore

\displaystyle\frac{1}{R}+d_{x_{\omega},R}^{\ell}(g_{\omega},h_{\omega})\leq d_{x_{\omega},R}^{\ell}(g_{\omega},h_{\omega})+\varepsilon

\displaystyle=\omega\text{-}\lim d_{x_{j},R}^{\ell}(g_{j},h_{j})+\varepsilon

\displaystyle\leq\omega\text{-}\lim d_{x_{j},R_{j}}^{\ell}(g_{j},h_{j})+\varepsilon

\displaystyle\leq\omega\text{-}\lim d_{x_{j}}^{\ell}(g_{j},h_{j})+2\varepsilon.

This implies
d_{x_{\omega}}^{\ell}(g_{\omega},h_{\omega})\leq\omega\text{-}\lim d_{x_{j}}^{\ell}(g_{j},h_{j})+2\varepsilon
.
In any case, by arbitrariness of

, we conclude that
d_{x_{\omega}}^{\ell}(g_{\omega},h_{\omega})\leq\omega\text{-}\lim d_{x_{j}}^{\ell}(g_{j},h_{j})

is an isometric embedding.

Surjectivity.
In the last step we just need to show that

is surjective. Indeed if it is the case then it is a surjective isometric embedding between
\omega
-
\lim(G_{j},\text{id},d_{x_{j}}^{\ell})
and

with

∎

In the situation above the groups

are also equipped with Haar measures

(G_{j},\textup{id},d_{x_{j}}^{\ell},\mu_{j})\underset{\textup{pmGH}}{\longrightarrow}(G_{\infty},\textup{id},d_{x_{\infty}}^{\ell},\mu_{\infty})
up to a subsequence if and only if
\sup_{j}\mu_{j}(\overline{B}_{x_{j}}^{\ell}(\textup{id},R))<+\infty
for every
R\geq 0
. If it is the case then
\mu_{\infty}
is left invariant.

(ii)

The measure
\mu_{\infty}
is a left invariant Haar measure of
G_{\infty}
if and only if
\inf_{j}\mu_{j}(\overline{B}_{x_{j}}^{\ell}(\textup{id},r))>0
for every
r>0
.

A similar statement holds for right invariant measures and metrics.

Proof.

We provide the proof only for the left invariant case, the other one being similar.
The first part of (i) follows by
[
Khe20
, Theorem 3.28]
together with
[
ADH13
, Theorem 2.6]
and Proposition
6.6
. We need to check that
\mu_{\infty}
is left invariant. Fix maps
\psi_{j}\colon G_{j}\to G_{\infty}
realizing the pointed measured Gromov-Hausdorff convergence. Fix an element
g_{\infty}\in G_{\infty}
: it is limit of elements
g_{j}\in G_{j}
. Call
L_{g_{j}}
and
L_{g_{\infty}}
the corresponding left multiplication maps. Fix a continuous function
f\colon G_{\infty}\to\mathbb{R}
with compact support, say contained in
\overline{B}_{x_{\infty}}^{\ell}(\textup{id},R)
for some
R>0
.
The continuous function
f
is uniformly continuous.
Using the compact support of
f
, its uniform continuity and (
12
) we obtain the following estimate. For every
\varepsilon>0
we have

\left|\int_{G_{j}}f\circ(\psi_{j}\circ L_{g_{j}})d\mu_{j}-\int_{G_{j}}f\circ L_{g_{\infty}}\circ\psi_{j}d\mu_{j}\right|<\varepsilon\mu_{j}(\overline{B}_{x_{j}}^{\ell}(\textup{id},2R))

for
j
big enough. Since
\mu_{j}(\overline{B}_{x_{j}}^{\ell}(\textup{id},2R))
is uniformly bounded in
j
and
\varepsilon
is arbitrary, we get

\lim_{j\to+\infty}\int_{G_{j}}f\circ(\psi_{j}\circ L_{g_{j}})d\mu_{j}=\lim_{j\to+\infty}\int_{G_{j}}f\circ L_{g_{\infty}}\circ\psi_{j}d\mu_{j}.

The left hand side equals
\int_{G_{\infty}}fd\mu_{\infty}
because
(L_{g_{j}})_{*}\mu_{j}=\mu_{j}
and (
11
). The function
f\circ L_{g_{\infty}}
is still continuous and with bounded support, so again by (
11
) the right hand side coincides with
\int_{G_{\infty}}f\circ L_{g_{\infty}}d\mu_{\infty}
. By the arbitrariness of
f
we get
(L_{g_{\infty}})_{*}\mu_{\infty}=\mu_{\infty}
, i.e.
\mu_{\infty}
is
L_{g_{\infty}}
-invariant. This shows that
\mu_{\infty}
is left invariant.

Clearly
\mu_{\infty}
is finite on compact sets. Moreover it is positive on open sets if and only
\liminf_{j\to+\infty}\mu_{j}(\overline{B}_{x_{j}}^{\ell}(\textup{id},r))>0
for every
r>0
. This follows from the left invariance and from, for instance,
[
PS21
, Remark 11.5]
. In this case
\mu_{\infty}
is a left invariant Haar measure of
G_{\infty}
by Lemma
2.2
.
∎

Corollary 6.9
.

Suppose that
(X_{j},x_{j},G_{j})\underset{\textup{eq-pGH}}{\longrightarrow}(X_{\infty},x_{\infty},G_{\infty})
,

geodesic spaces, and

unimodular. If there exist Haar measures

of

for every

Proof.

The conditions (i) and (ii) imply, and are essentially equivalent to, conditions in (i) and (ii) of Proposition
6.8
by Lemma
2.3
.
∎

.

We just need the following additional fact.

Lemma 6.11

(compare with
[
CS22
, Lemma 3.3]
)
.

Let

be a proper, geodesically complete,
CAT
(0)
-space and let

be closed, totally disconnected and cocompact.
For every

and

\overline{S}_{r}(y)\subseteq\overline{S}_{r}(x)

Proof.

Suppose it is not true. We can find

.(a) we have

big enough. Therefore

Proof of Proposition
6.10
.

Let
\mu
be a bi-invariant Haar measure of
G
. Observe that
0<\mu(\overline{S}_{\varepsilon^{\ast}}(x))<+\infty
for every
x\in X
, because the set
\overline{S}_{\varepsilon^{\ast}}(x)
is open and compact by Theorem
2.6
.(b). The map
x\mapsto\mu(\overline{S}_{\varepsilon^{\ast}}(x))
is upper semicontinuous by Lemma
6.11
and
G
-invariant by unimodularity. Therefore it admits a maximum by cocompactness of
G
. Let
x
be a point where the maximum is realized. Apply Theorem
4.1
.(iv) to the point

: there exists
y_{0}\in Y
such that
\overline{G}_{\varepsilon^{\ast}}(x)
preserves
\{y_{0}\}\!\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
. If

, then we set
x_{0}=x
and there is nothing more to prove.
Otherwise call
x_{0}
the projection of
x
on the closed, convex,
\overline{G}_{\varepsilon^{\ast}}(x)
-invariant subset
\{y_{0}\}\!\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
.
Let
c\colon[0,d(x,x_{0})]\to X
be the geodesic
[x,x_{0}]
and set

T=\sup\{t\in[0,d(x,x_{0})]\text{ s.t. }\overline{S}_{\varepsilon^{\ast}}(c(t))=\overline{S}_{\varepsilon^{\ast}}(x)\}.

By definition

and we claim that
T=d(x,x_{0})
and it is a maximum.

In order to do so we need the following claim.

Claim:
if
\overline{S}_{\varepsilon^{\ast}}(y)\subseteq\overline{S}_{\varepsilon^{\ast}}(y^{\prime})
then the equality holds if and only if
\mu(\overline{S}_{\varepsilon^{\ast}}(y))=\mu(\overline{S}_{\varepsilon^{\ast}}(y^{\prime}))

\mu(\overline{S}_{\varepsilon^{\ast}}(y))=\mu(\overline{S}_{\varepsilon^{\ast}}(y^{\prime}))
. The two sets are both open and closed by Theorem
2.6
.(b). Therefore if
\overline{S}_{\varepsilon^{\ast}}(y)\subsetneq\overline{S}_{\varepsilon^{\ast}}(y^{\prime})
then the set
\overline{S}_{\varepsilon^{\ast}}(y^{\prime})\setminus\overline{S}_{\varepsilon^{\ast}}(y)
would be open and non-empty, so of positive
\mu
-measure, contradicting the assumption.

We can now continue the proof. Assume
\overline{S}_{\varepsilon^{\ast}}(c(t))=\overline{S}_{\varepsilon^{\ast}}(x)
.
Then,
\overline{S}_{\varepsilon^{\ast}}(c(t+t^{\prime}))\subseteq\overline{S}_{\varepsilon^{\ast}}(c(t))=\overline{S}_{\varepsilon^{\ast}}(x)
for all
t^{\prime}>0
small enough, by Lemma
6.11
.
On the other hand, for every
g\in\overline{S}_{\varepsilon^{\ast}}(x)
we have
d(x_{0},gx_{0})\leq d(x,gx)
(since
g
acts on
Y
fixing
y_{0}
).
Therefore, by the convexity of the displacement functions we deduce that
\overline{S}_{\varepsilon^{\ast}}(x)\subseteq\overline{S}_{\varepsilon^{\ast}}(c(t+t^{\prime}))
too. This shows that the supremum defining
T
is not realized, unless
T=d(x,x_{0})
. Let now
0\leq t_{j}<T
such that
t_{j}
tends to
T
, so the points
c(t_{j})
converge to
c(T)
. By Lemma
6.11
we get
\overline{S}_{\varepsilon^{\ast}}(c(t_{j}))\subseteq\overline{S}_{\varepsilon^{\ast}}(c(T))
for all
n
big enough. But
\overline{S}_{\varepsilon^{\ast}}(c(t_{j}))=\overline{S}_{\varepsilon^{\ast}}(x)
has maximal measure among the sets of this form, therefore
\mu(\overline{S}_{\varepsilon^{\ast}}(x))=\mu(\overline{S}_{\varepsilon^{\ast}}(c(t_{j})))=\mu(\overline{S}_{\varepsilon^{\ast}}(c(T)))
for all
j
. The claim implies that
\overline{S}_{\varepsilon^{\ast}}(c(T))=\overline{S}_{\varepsilon^{\ast}}(x)
, i.e.

is actually a maximum and so

.
Hence,
\overline{S}_{\varepsilon^{\ast}}(x_{0})=\overline{S}_{\varepsilon^{\ast}}(x)
,
and

is the point we are looking for.
∎

Corollary 6.12
.

Let

Let

P_{0},r_{0},D_{0}

-controlled if

-packed, CAT

then
h_{j}h_{j}^{\prime}\in\overline{B}_{d_{y_{j},R+1}^{\ell}}(\text{id},2\varepsilon)
. By (i) the element
h_{j}h_{j}^{\prime}
is
\varepsilon
-close to some
g_{j,i}
, so
d_{y_{j},R+1}^{\ell}(\text{id},g_{j,i})=d_{y_{j},R+1}^{\ell}(g_{j,1},g_{j,i})\leq 3\varepsilon
. Now (ii) implies that
g_{j,i}=g_{j,1}=\text{id}
.
The last thing we need to recall is

(iii)

the multiplication on the left by
g_{j,i}
defines a
d_{y_{j},R+1}^{\ell}
-isometry between
\overline{B}_{d_{y_{j},R+1}^{\ell}}(\text{id},\varepsilon)
and
\overline{B}_{d_{y_{j},R+1}^{\ell}}(g_{j,i},\varepsilon)
.

Recall that we are supposing that the image of
\text{Stab}_{G_{j}}(y_{j})\to\text{Isom}(\overline{B}(y_{j},R))
has cardinality bigger than
N+1
for
\omega
-a.e.
(j)
. This means, together with (iii), that the following holds true
\omega
-a.s.: there exists an isometry
h_{j}\in\text{Stab}_{G_{j}}(y_{j})
such that
d_{y_{j},R+1}^{\ell}(h_{j},\text{id})\leq\varepsilon
and
d_{y_{j},R}^{\ell}(h_{j},\text{id})>0
.

We claim that
d_{y_{j},R+1}^{\ell}(h_{j}^{k},\text{id})>1
for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, which is a contradiction to the fact that
\overline{B}_{d_{y_{j},R+1}^{\ell}}(\text{id},\varepsilon)
is a subgroup. We proceed as follows. Since
d_{y_{j},R}^{\ell}(h_{j},\text{id})>0
we can find a point
p_{j}\in\overline{B}(y_{j},R)
which is not fixed by
h_{j}
. Call
z_{j}
the projection of
p_{j}
to
\text{Fix}(h_{j})
and extend the geodesic
[z_{j},p_{j}]
beyond
p_{j}
up to find a point
w_{j}
at distance
R+1
from
x_{j}
.
Three things hold:
z_{j}
is the projection of
w_{j}
to
\text{Fix}(h_{j})
,
d(w_{j},z_{j})>1
and
w_{j}\in\overline{B}(y_{j},R+1)
. If the whole orbit
\langle h_{j}\rangle w_{j}
would be contained in
\overline{B}(w_{j},1)
then the center of this orbit (cp.
[
BH13
, Proposition II.2.7]
) would be a fixed point of
h_{j}
at distance at most
1
from
w_{j}
, which is a contradiction since
d(w_{j},\text{Fix}(h_{j}))>1
.
Therefore there must be some
k\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
d(h_{j}^{k}w_{j},w_{j})>1
, i.e.
d_{y_{j},R+1}^{\ell}(h_{j}^{k},\text{id})>1
for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.
∎

This gives a similar uniform estimate for all unimodular groups.

Corollary 6.16
.

Let
P_{0},r_{0},D_{0},R>0
. There exists a constant
N_{0}^{\textup{u}}(R)=N_{0}^{\textup{u}}(P_{0},r_{0},D_{0},R)>0
such that the following holds true.
Let
X
be a proper, geodesically complete,
(P_{0},r_{0})
-packed,
\textup{CAT}(0)
-space and let
G<\textup{Isom}(X)
be closed, totally disconnected, unimodular and
D_{0}
-cocompact. Then for all
x\in X
the cardinality of the image of the map
\textup{Stab}_{G}(x)\to\textup{Isom}(\overline{B}(x,R))
is at most
N_{0}^{\textup{u}}(R)
.

Proof.

If
\textup{sys}^{\diamond}(G,X)>\sigma_{0}:=\sigma_{P_{0},r_{0},D_{0}}(\varepsilon_{0})
then it is enough to take
N_{0}^{\textup{u}}(R)=N_{0}(\sigma_{0},R)
. Otherwise we apply Theorem
4.1
with
\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{0}
. We have a splitting
X=Y\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
,
k\geq 1
and we write
x=(y,{\bf v})
. Every element of
\textup{Stab}_{G}(x)
is of the form
(g^{\prime},g^{\prime\prime})
, where
g^{\prime}
fixes
y
and
g^{\prime\prime}
is a finite order isometry of a crystallographic group fixing
{\bf v}
. The order of the stabilizer of
{\bf v}
inside this crystallographic group is at most
J_{0}
by Proposition
2.12
. The isometric action
(Y,y,\overline{G_{Y}})
is
\sigma_{0}
-controlled by Corollary
6.12
, see also the beginning of this section. Therefore the cardinality of the image of the map
\text{Stab}_{\overline{G_{Y}}}(y)\to\text{Isom}(\overline{B}(y,R))
is at most
N_{0}(\sigma_{0},R)
. The thesis follows taking
N_{0}^{\textup{u}}(R)=N_{0}(\sigma_{0},R)\cdot J_{0}
.
∎

As a consequence of Corollary
6.16
we have a quantified version of
[
CM09
, Proposition 6.8]
for unimodular groups. A similar statement for
\sigma
-controlled groups with
N_{0}(\sigma,R)
in place of
N_{0}^{\text{u}}(R)
holds, but we do not write it.

Corollary 6.17
.

Let
X
be a proper, geodesically complete,
(P_{0},r_{0})
-packed,
CAT
(0)
-space and let
G<\textup{Isom}(X)
be closed, totally disconnected, unimodular and
D_{0}
-cocompact. Let
g
be an elliptic isometry of
G
,
x\notin\textup{Fix}(g)
and
y
be the projection of
x
on
\textup{Fix}(g)
. Then

(i)

\angle_{y}(x,gx)\geq 2\arcsin(\frac{1}{2N_{0}^{\textup{u}}(1)})
;

(ii)

d(x,gx)\geq\frac{1}{N_{0}^{\textup{u}}(1)}\cdot d(x,\textup{Fix}(g))
.

Proof.

We need to recall the formula
[
BH13
, Proposition II.3.1]
:

(13)

\alpha:=\angle_{y}(x,gx)=\lim_{t\to 0}2\arcsin\left(\frac{d(c(t),gc(t))}{2t}\right),

where
c(t)
is the geodesic
[y,x]
, and where the right hand side is monotone decreasing as
t
goes to
0
. By Corollary
6.16
we know that the order of
g
, when seen as an isometry of
\overline{B}(x,1)
, is at most
N_{0}^{\text{u}}(1)
. Suppose
\alpha<2\arcsin(\frac{1}{2N_{0}^{\textup{u}}(1)})
and choose
\varepsilon>0
so that
\alpha+\varepsilon<2\arcsin(\frac{1}{2N_{0}^{\textup{u}}(1)})
. We can find
t>0
such that

(14)

\alpha+\varepsilon\geq 2\arcsin\left(\frac{d(c(t),gc(t))}{2t}\right),

i.e.
2t\sin(\frac{\alpha+\varepsilon}{2})\geq d(c(t),gc(t))
. If
k\in\{1,\ldots,N_{0}^{\textup{u}}(1)\}
we get

d(c(t),g^{k}c(t))\leq 2kt\sin\left(\frac{\alpha+\varepsilon}{2}\right)<\frac{k}{N_{0}^{\textup{u}}(1)}t\leq t.

Therefore the orbit
\langle g\rangle c(t)
is contained in a ball of center
c(t)
and radius smaller than
t
. The center of this orbit (cp.
[
BH13
, Proposition II.2.7]
) is a point which is fixed by
g
and which is at distance
<t
from
c(t)
, contradicting the fact that
y
is the projection of
c(t)
on
\text{Fix}(g)
.

A direct application of (
13
) gives (ii). Indeed, recalling that the quantity inside the limit in (
13
) is decreasing as
t
goes to
0
, we get

2\arcsin\left(\frac{d(x,gx)}{2d(x,y)}\right)\geq\alpha.

∎

Remark 6.18
.

The proof of (i) is conceptually equivalent to the proof of
[
CM09
, Proposition 6.8]
, just notice that they wrote the wrong inequality, essentially opposite to (
14
). However their proof still works using the limit procedure as we did.

We can also prove Theorem
C
, actually a bit stronger result.

Corollary 6.19
.

Let
X
be a proper, geodesically complete,
(P_{0},r_{0})
-packed,
CAT
(0)
-space. Let
G<\textup{Isom}(X)
be closed, totally disconnected, unimodular and
D_{0}
-cocompact. Let
x\in X
, let
g\in\textup{Stab}_{G}(x)
and let
R\geq 0
. Let
m=p_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots p_{k}^{\alpha_{k}}
be the prime decomposition of the order of
g
restricted to
\textup{Isom}(\overline{B}(x,R))
. Then
\max\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{k}\}\leq N_{0}^{\textup{u}}(1)
.

In particular the maximal prime number appearing in the prime decomposition of the order of any finite order isometry is at most
N_{0}^{\textup{u}}(1)
.

Clearly the
\alpha_{k}
’s are not bounded in general. A similar statement holds in the
\sigma
-controlled case with
N_{0}(\sigma,1)
in place of
N_{0}^{\text{u}}(1)
, but we do not report it here.

Proof.

The thesis is equivalent to bound the order of
g|_{\overline{B}(x,R)}
when it has prime order
p>1
. For all
1\leq j<p
we can find
k\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
kj\equiv 1
modulo
p
, so

(15)

\text{Fix}(g)\subseteq\text{Fix}(g^{j})\subseteq\text{Fix}(g^{kj})=\text{Fix}(g),

forcing the containments to be equalities. Let
y
be a point of
\overline{B}(x,R)
which is not fixed by
g
and let
x^{\prime}
be the closest point to
y
along
[x,y]
which is fixed by
g
. Finally let
y^{\prime}
be a point of
[x^{\prime},y]
at distance at most
1
from
x^{\prime}
and different from
x^{\prime}
. By (
15
) we conclude that the points
g^{j}y^{\prime}
are all distinct for
1\leq j<p
and contained in
\overline{B}(x^{\prime},1)
. Therefore
p\leq N_{0}^{\text{u}}(1)
.
∎

The last important consequence is the following uniform bound, that we explicit in both the unimodular and the
\sigma
-controlled situation.

Corollary 6.20
.

Let
X
be a proper, geodesically complete,
(P_{0},r_{0})
-packed,
CAT
(0)
-space and
x\in X
. Let
G<\textup{Isom}(X)
be closed, totally disconnected, unimodular (resp.
\sigma
-controlled) and
D_{0}
-cocompact. Then for every
R\geq 0
the group
\textup{Stab}_{G}(x,R)
has index at most
N_{0}^{\textup{u}}(R)
(resp.
N_{0}(\sigma,R)
) in
\textup{Stab}_{G}(x)
.

Proof.

Corollary
6.16
(resp. Proposition
6.15
) implies that the cardinality of the group
\text{Stab}_{G}(x)/\text{Stab}_{G}(x,R)
is at most
N_{0}^{\textup{u}}(R)
(resp.
N_{0}(\sigma,R)
), which is the thesis.
∎

6.5.
Convergence without collapsing

We are ready to describe the limit group in the non-collapsed standard setting of convergence.

Theorem 6.21
.

Assume that we are in the standard setting of convergence of

(X_{j},G_{j})\underset{\textup{eq-pGH}}{\longrightarrow}(X_{\infty},G_{\infty})
,
without collapsing. Then the limit group
G_{\infty}
is closed, totally disconnected, unimodular and
D_{0}
-cocompact.

Proof.

Up to pass to a subsequence, which converges to the same limit, we can suppose that there exists
0<\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}
such that

\text{sys}^{\diamond}(G_{j},X_{j})\geq\varepsilon>0
for every
j
. Therefore each
(X_{j},G_{j})
is
\sigma:=\sigma_{P_{0},r_{0},D_{0}}(\varepsilon)
-controlled, as explained at the beginning of Section
6.4
. Proposition
6.14
implies that
G_{\infty}
is closed,
D_{0}
-cocompact and totally disconnected.

Let
x_{j}
be points of
X_{j}
such that
\overline{G}_{\sigma}(x_{j})=\text{Stab}_{G_{j}}(x_{j})
.
Let
\mu_{j}
be bi-invariant Haar measures on
G_{j}
normalized in such a way that
\mu_{j}(\text{Stab}_{G_{j}}(x_{j}))=1
.
We claim that conditions (i) and (ii) of Corollary
6.9
are satisfied, and so that
G_{\infty}
is unimodular. We first show that
\sup_{j}\mu_{j}(\overline{S}_{R}(x_{j}))<+\infty
for every
R\geq 0
. The condition
\overline{G}_{\sigma}(x_{j})=\text{Stab}_{G_{j}}(x_{j})
implies that the set
G_{j}x_{j}
is made of
\sigma/4
-separated points. In particular the distinct points of the set
\overline{S}_{R}(x_{j})x_{j}
are at most
\text{Pack}(R,\frac{\sigma}{8})=:P
, a number that does not depend on
j
by Proposition
2.1
. Let us take elements
g_{1},\ldots,g_{m}\in\overline{S}_{R}(x_{j})
such that
\{g_{i}x_{j}\}_{i=1}^{m}=\overline{S}_{R}(x_{j})x_{j}
. By the discussion above we know that
m\leq P
. Moreover if
g\in\overline{S}_{R}(x_{j})
is arbitrary then there exists
i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
such that
gx_{j}=g_{i}x_{j}
, so
g_{i}^{-1}g\in\text{Stab}_{G_{j}}(x_{j})
. This implies

\overline{S}_{R}(x_{j})=\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}g_{i}\text{Stab}_{G_{j}}(x_{j}).

Therefore the claim is proved since

\mu_{j}(\overline{S}_{R}(x_{j}))\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mu_{j}(g_{i}\text{Stab}_{G_{j}}(x_{j}))=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mu_{j}(\text{Stab}_{G_{j}}(x_{j}))=m\leq P.

On the other hand Corollary
6.20
implies that for every
r>0
we have

\inf_{j}\mu_{j}\left(\overline{S}_{r}\left(x_{j},\frac{1}{r}\right)\right)\geq\inf_{j}\mu_{j}\left(\text{Stab}_{G_{j}}\left(x_{j},\frac{1}{r}\right)\right)\geq\frac{1}{N_{0}^{\text{u}}(\frac{1}{r})}>0.

∎

6.6.
Convergence with collapsing

We now deal with the collapsing case.

Theorem 6.22
.

Assume that we are in the standard setting of convergence of

(X_{j},G_{j})\underset{\textup{eq-pGH}}{\longrightarrow}(X_{\infty},G_{\infty})

with collapsing. Then:

(i)

X_{\infty}
splits isometrically and
G_{\infty}
-invariantly as
X_{\infty}^{\prime}\times\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
, for some
\ell\geq 1
. In particular
X_{\infty}^{\prime}
is a proper, geodesically complete,
(P_{0},r_{0})
-packed,
\textup{CAT}(0)
-space.

(ii)

G_{\infty}^{\circ}=\{\textup{id}\}\times\textup{Transl}(\mathbb{R}^{\ell})
, and

X_{\infty}^{\prime}=G_{\infty}^{\circ}\backslash X_{\infty}
.

(iii)

\ell
is characterized as follows: there exists
\rho^{*}>0
such that for every
0<\rho\leq\rho^{*}
there exists
j_{\rho}
such that
\textup{rk}(\overline{G}_{j,\rho}(z_{j}))=\ell
for all
j\geq j_{\rho}
and all
z_{j}\in X_{j}
. Here
\overline{G}_{j,\rho}(z_{j}):=\overline{(G_{j})}_{\rho}(z_{j})
as defined in (
4
).

(iv)

the group
G_{\infty}
is unimodular.

(v)

the projection
G_{\infty}^{\prime}
of
G_{\infty}
on
\textup{Isom}(X_{\infty}^{\prime})
is closed, totally disconnected, unimodular and
D_{0}
-cocompact.

(vi)

the quotient spaces
M_{j}=G_{j}\backslash X_{j}
converge to
M_{\infty}=G_{\infty}\backslash X_{\infty}
, which is isometric to the quotient of
X_{\infty}^{\prime}
by
G_{\infty}^{\prime}
. In particular
M_{\infty}\in\mathcal{O}\textup{-CAT}_{0}^{\textup{td,u}}(P_{0},r_{0},D_{0})
.

This theorem is stronger than and refines
[
CS23
, Theorem 7.11]
, which is stated for discrete groups. The main, and technically more important, improvement is the characterization of
\ell
given in (iii).
We recall a couple of facts we need.

Lemma 6.23

(
[
CS23
, Lemma 7.12]
)
.

Let
(X_{j},x_{j},G_{j}),(X_{j}^{\prime},x_{j}^{\prime},G_{j}^{\prime})
be two sequences of isometric actions and
\omega
be a non-principal ultrafilter. Then the ultralimit
\omega\text{-}\lim(X_{j}\times X_{j}^{\prime},(x_{j},x_{j}^{\prime}),G_{j}\times G_{j}^{\prime})
is equivariantly isometric to
(X_{\omega}\times X_{\omega}^{\prime},(x_{\omega},x_{\omega}^{\prime}),G_{\omega}\times G_{\omega}^{\prime})
.

Lemma 6.24

(
[
CS23
, Lemma 7.13]
)
.

Let
A
be a group of translations of
\mathbb{R}^{k}
.
Then
A\cong\mathbb{R}^{\ell}\times\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with
\ell+d\leq k
. Moreover, there is a corresponding
A
-invariant metric factorization of
\mathbb{R}^{k}
as
\mathbb{R}^{\ell}\times\mathbb{R}^{k-\ell}
, such that the connected component
A^{\circ}\cong\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
can be identified with
\textup{Transl}(\mathbb{R}^{\ell})
.

Recall again the function
\sigma_{P_{0},r_{0},D_{0}}(\cdot)
provided by Theorem
4.1
, and the value
\sigma_{0}=\sigma_{P_{0},r_{0},D_{0}}(\varepsilon_{0})
.

Proof of Theorem
6.22
.

The sets defined in (
4
) will be denoted by
S_{j,r}(z_{j},R)
,
\overline{S}_{j,r}(z_{j},R)
,
G_{j,r}(z_{j},R)
,
\overline{G}_{j,r}(z_{j},R)
if they refer to the group
G_{j}
.

Proof of (i) and (ii).
We fix a non-principal ultrafilter
\omega
such that
\omega
-
\lim\textup{sys}^{\diamond}(G_{j},X_{j})=0
. By Proposition
6.1
it is enough to show the thesis for the ultralimit
(X_{\omega},G_{\omega})
. By our choice of the ultrafilter
\omega
, we have
\textup{sys}^{\diamond}(G_{j},X_{j})\leq\sigma_{0}
for
\omega
-a.e.
(j)
. Thus
X_{j}
splits as
Y_{j}\times\mathbb{R}^{k_{j}}
with
k_{j}\geq 1
for
\omega
-a.e.
(j)
, by Theorem
4.1
. If
k=\omega
-
\lim k_{j}
, we have
k\leq n_{0}
and
k_{j}=k
for
\omega
-a.e.
(j)
.
By Lemma
6.2
the limit does not depend on the choice of the basepoints, so we can assume that the basepoints are the points
x_{j}=(y_{j},{\bf v_{j}})
provided by Proposition
6.10
applied to
\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{0}
. In particular
\overline{G}_{j,\sigma_{0}}(x_{j})
preserves the slice
\{y_{j}\}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
.

Choose a positive

\eta\leq\frac{1}{2}\min\left\{\sigma_{0},\sqrt{2\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{J(k)}\right)}\right\}.
By Lemma
2.13
, every element of a crystallographic group of
\mathbb{R}^{k}
moving every point of
B_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}({\bf v_{j}},\frac{1}{2\eta})
less than
2\eta
is a translation. The group
G_{\eta}(x_{\omega},1/\eta)
is open, so
G_{\eta}(x_{\omega},1/\eta)\cap G_{\omega}^{\circ}=G_{\omega}^{\circ}
.
Remark that if
g_{\omega}=\omega
-
\lim g_{j}
belongs to
S_{\eta}(x_{\omega},1/\eta)
, then
for
\omega
-a.e.
(j)
the isometry
g_{j}
belongs to
S_{j,2\eta}(x_{j},1/2\eta)
.
Every element
g_{j}\in S_{j,2\eta}(x_{j},1/2\eta)
belongs to
\overline{G}_{j,\sigma_{0}}(x_{j})
since
2\eta\leq\sigma_{0}
, therefore it acts on
X_{j}=Y_{j}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
as
g_{j}=(g_{j}^{\prime},g_{j}^{\prime\prime})
, where
g^{\prime}_{j}
fixes
y_{j}
(because of our choice of basepoints); moreover,
g_{j}^{\prime\prime}
is a global translation of
\mathbb{R}^{k}
, since it moves the points of
B_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}({\bf v_{j}},\frac{1}{2\eta})
less than
2\eta
.
An application of Lemma
6.23
says that also
X_{\omega}
splits isometrically as
Y_{\omega}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
, where
Y_{\omega}
is the ultralimit of the
(Y_{j},y_{j})
’s. Moreover
G_{\omega}
preserves the product decomposition.
Every element of
g_{\omega}\in G_{\omega}^{\circ}
can be written as a product
u_{\omega}(1)\cdots u_{\omega}(n)
, with each
u_{\omega}(i)
in
S_{\eta}(x_{\omega},1/\eta)
.
As
u_{\omega}(i)=\omega
-
\lim u_{j}(i)
with
u_{j}(i)=(u_{j}(i)^{\prime},u_{j}(i)^{\prime\prime})\in S_{j,2\eta}(x_{j},1/2\eta)
, where
u_{j}(i)^{\prime}
fixes
y_{j}
and
u_{j}(i)^{\prime\prime}
is a translation, it follows that also

g_{\omega}
can be written as
(g_{\omega}^{\prime},g_{\omega}^{\prime\prime})\in\text{Isom}(Y_{\omega})\times\text{Isom}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
where
g_{\omega}^{\prime}y_{\omega}=y_{\omega}
and
g_{\omega}^{\prime\prime}
is a global translation (being the ultralimit of Euclidean translations). Let us call
\pi\colon G_{\omega}\to\text{Isom}(Y_{\omega})
the projection map. The group
\pi(G_{\omega}^{\circ})
is normal in
\pi(G_{\omega})
. The set of fixed points
\text{Fix}(\pi(G_{\omega}^{\circ}))
is closed, convex, non-empty and
\pi(G_{\omega})
-invariant (since
\pi(G_{\omega}^{\circ})
is normal in
\pi(G_{\omega}))
. Since
G_{\omega}
is clearly
D_{0}
-cocompact, so it is
\pi(G_{\omega})
. Then the action of
\pi(G_{\omega})
on
Y_{\omega}
is minimal (cp.
[
CM09
, Lemma 3.13]
) which implies that
\text{Fix}(\pi(G_{\omega}^{\circ}))=Y_{\omega}
, that is
\pi(G_{\omega}^{\circ})=\{\text{id}\}
.
We conclude that
G_{\omega}^{\circ}
is a connected subgroup of

\{\text{id}\}\times\text{Transl}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
. By Lemma
6.24
,
\mathbb{R}^{k}
splits isometrically as
\mathbb{R}^{\ell}\times\mathbb{R}^{k-\ell}
and
G_{\omega}^{\circ}
can be identified with the subgroup of translations of the factor
\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
,
for some
\ell\leq k
.
Setting
X_{\omega}^{\prime}:=(Y_{\omega}\times\mathbb{R}^{k_{\omega}-\ell})
, this is still a proper, geodesically complete,
(P_{0},r_{0})
-packed,
\text{CAT}(0)
-space, and clearly
X_{\omega}^{\prime}=G_{\omega}^{\circ}\backslash X_{\omega}
.
Notice that, since
G_{\omega}^{\circ}
is normal in
G_{\omega}
, then the splitting
X_{\omega}=X_{\omega}^{\prime}\times\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
is
G_{\omega}
-invariant. Let us now show that
G_{\omega}^{\circ}
is non-trivial, hence
\ell\geq 1
.
Actually, if
G_{\omega}^{\circ}
was trivial then
G_{\omega}
would be totally disconnected, implying
\text{sys}^{\diamond}(G_{\omega},X_{\omega})>0
by Theorem
2.6
.(ii). However, we are able to exhibit hyperbolic isometries of
G_{\omega}
with arbitrarily small translation length, which will prove that
G_{\omega}^{\circ}
is non-trivial. Indeed, fix any
\lambda>0
and an error
\xi>0
.
By the collapsing assumption we can find hyperbolic isometries
g_{j}\in G_{j}
with
\ell(g_{j})\leq\xi
, for
\omega
-a.e.
(j)
. By

g=(g^{\prime},g^{\prime\prime})\in G_{\infty}
. After a composition with an element of
G_{\infty}^{\circ}=\{\textup{id}\}\times\text{Transl}(\mathbb{R}^{\ell})
we can suppose that
g^{\prime\prime}
fixes
{\bf v_{\infty}}
. In particular
g^{\prime\prime}\overline{S}_{1}({\bf v_{\infty}})=\overline{S}_{1}({\bf v_{\infty}})=\overline{S}_{1}({\bf v_{\infty}})g^{\prime\prime}
.
Therefore we have

(g^{\prime}A)^{*}=g^{\prime}A\times\overline{S}_{1}({\bf v_{\infty}})=g^{\prime}A\times g^{\prime\prime}\overline{S}_{1}({\bf v_{\infty}})=(g^{\prime},g^{\prime\prime})(A\times\overline{S}_{1}({\bf v_{\infty}}))=(g^{\prime},g^{\prime\prime})A^{*}.

This implies

\nu(g^{\prime}A)=\mu((g^{\prime}A)^{*})=\mu((g^{\prime},g^{\prime\prime})A^{*})=\mu(A^{*})=\nu(A).

In the same way we can prove the right invariance of
\nu
, so
G_{\infty}^{\prime}
is unimodular.

The last step is to show that it is totally disconnected. This can be done observing that, with the notation of the previous steps, each
\overline{G_{X_{j}^{\prime}}}
is
\sigma^{*}
-controlled and using Proposition
6.14
. There is also another way to get the conclusion. Indeed let us consider the natural action of the totally disconnected group
G_{\infty}/G_{\infty}^{o}
on
X_{\infty}^{\prime}
. By construction the image of such action is
G_{\infty}^{\prime}
. The kernel is

\{g=(g^{\prime},g^{\prime\prime})\in G_{\infty}/G_{\infty}^{o}\text{ s.t. }g^{\prime}=\textup{id}\}.

Any class in the set above has a representative of the type
(\textup{id},g^{\prime\prime})
, with
g^{\prime\prime}{\bf v_{\infty}}={\bf v_{\infty}}
. Therefore the kernel of the action is a compact, normal subgroup of
G_{\infty}/G_{\infty}^{o}
. Then the thesis follows since the group
G_{\infty}^{\prime}
is the quotient of the locally compact, totally disconnected group
G_{\infty}/G_{\infty}^{o}
by a closed, normal subgroup. Even if it is not necessary, observe that the fact that the kernel of the action is compact shows also that the group
G_{\infty}/G_{\infty}^{o}
is unimodular. The fact that
G_{\infty}^{\prime}
is
D_{0}
-cocompact is obvious.

Proof of (vi).
The quotients
M_{j}=G_{j}\backslash X_{j}
converge to

, by Lemma

. Moreover

6.7.
Characterization of collapsing

If
X\in\textup{CAT}_{0}^{\textup{td,u}}(P_{0},r_{0},D_{0})
then dim
(X)\leq n_{0}=P_{0}/2
, by Proposition
2.1
(ii). Moreover, if
(X_{j},x_{j})\underset{\textup{pGH}}{\longrightarrow}(X_{\infty},x_{\infty})
then, by
[
CS21
, Theorem 6.5]
,

\text{dim}(X_{\infty})\leq\liminf_{j\rightarrow+\infty}\text{dim}(X_{j}).

The following theorem precisely relates the collapsing (as defined in
6.5
) in the standard setting of convergence to the dimension of the limit quotients, and is a direct consequence of the convergence Theorems
6.21
&
6.22
. The proof is the same of
[
CS23
, Theorem 7.15]
.

Theorem 6.25

(Characterization of collapsing)
.

In the standard setting of convergence of

(X_{j},G_{j})\underset{\textup{eq-pGH}}{\longrightarrow}(X_{\infty},G_{\infty})
,
let
M_{j}=G_{j}\backslash X_{j}
and
M_{\infty}=G_{\infty}\backslash X_{\infty}
be the quotient spaces. Then:

(i)

the sequence is non-collapsing iff
\textup{TD}(M_{\infty})=\lim_{j\to+\infty}\textup{TD}(M_{j})
;

(ii)

the sequence is collapsing iff
\textup{TD}(M_{\infty})<\lim_{j\to+\infty}\textup{TD}(M_{j})
.

Moreover, in the above characterization, the topological dimension
TD
can be replaced by the Hausdorff dimension
HD
.

Notice that since we proved that
\lim_{j\to+\infty}\text{TD}(M_{j})
exists, Theorem
6.25
excludes to have sequences
(X_{j},G_{j})
converging with mixed behaviour (that is, such that along some subsequence the convergence is collapsed, and along other subsequences it is non-collapsed). We have already noticed this fact during the proof of Theorem
6.22
. Finally we conclude with the

Proof of Theorem
B
.

Let
M_{j}\in\mathcal{O}\text{-CAT}_{0}^{\text{td,u}}(P_{0},r_{0},D_{0})
and suppose it converges to some

. We are in the standard setting of convergence. Observe that by Lemma

the space

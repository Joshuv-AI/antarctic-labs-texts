Homotopy torsion theories
Sandra Mantovani, Mariano Messora, Enrico M. Vitale

Abstract




Abstract:

In the context of categories equipped with a structure of nullhomotopies, we introduce the notion of homotopy torsion theory.
As special cases, we recover pretorsion theories as well as torsion theories in multi-pointed categories and in pre-pointed categories.
Using the structure of nullhomotopies induced by the canonical string of adjunctions between a category
{\mathcal{A}}
and the category

\mathbf{Arr}({\mathcal{A}})
of arrows, we give a new proof of the correspondence between orthogonal factorization systems in
{\mathcal{A}}
and homotopy
torsion theories in
\mathbf{Arr}({\mathcal{A}}),
avoiding the request on the existence of pullbacks and pushouts in
{\mathcal{A}}.
Moreover, such a correspondence
is extended to weakly orthogonal factorization systems and weak homotopy torsion theories.

Keywords:
nullhomotopy, homotopy kernel, arrow category, torsion theory, factorization system.

2020 MSC:
18A30, 18A32, 18E40, 18N99

Introduction

There is a striking analogy between orthogonal factorization systems and torsion theories. If you have an orthogonal factorization
system in a category, from each arrow you get a pair of composable arrows, and the two arrows lie in two assigned classes of arrows
whose intersection is reduced to the class of isomorphisms. If you have a torsion theory in an abelian category, from each object you
get a pair of objects connected by a short exact sequence, and the two objects lie in two assigned subcategories whose intersection is
reduced to the zero object. This analogy becomes even more strict considering notions of torsion theory adapted to general (not
necessarily abelian) categories.

Since the category
\mathbf{Arr}({\mathcal{A}})
of arrows of a category
{\mathcal{A}}
is the standard way to turn arrows into objects, one expects that a precise relation
between orthogonal factorization systems in
{\mathcal{A}}
and torsion theories in
\mathbf{Arr}({\mathcal{A}})
exists.
A new direction to establish and describe such a relation has been indicated in the unpublished talk
[
14
]
.
More recently, and independently from
[
14
]
, the correspondence between orthogonal factorization systems in
{\mathcal{A}}
and a certain kind of torsion
theories in
\mathbf{Arr}({\mathcal{A}})
has been established in
[
9
]
. The proof of the main result in
[
9
]
depends on previous results on (co)monads
from
[
10
]
and on the reformulation of the definition of orthogonal factorization system given in
[
12
]
.

The notion of torsion theory used in
[
9
]
is based on the notion of pre-pointed category and, more precisely, on pre-(co)kernels
(not to be confused with the prekernels studied in
[
6
,
7
]
: pre-kernels from
[
9
]
are weak prekernels).
Pre-kernels are not defined in terms of a universal property, they are defined via an ad-hoc construction which depends on the
reflective and coreflective character of the subcategory of trivial objects. Nevertheless, pre-kernels have a universal property:
they are a special instance of the so-called homotopy kernels, as pointed out in
[
18
]
in the special case of
\mathbf{Arr}({\mathcal{A}}).

Any adjunction (in fact, any pre-radical and any pre-coradical) induces a structure of nullhomotopies (see Definition
2.1
),
and any structure of nullhomotopies
carries with it a notion of homotopy kernel. Therefore, the idea developed in the present paper will allow us to gather in the same framework
various notions of pretorsion and
torsion theory (in pointed categories, in multi-pointed categories, in pre-pointed categories) appearing in the literature.
We will show that the latter are all special cases of a general notion based on homotopy kernels.
We call such a notion homotopy torsion theory and we formulate its definition in any category
equipped with a structure of nullhomotopies. The main test for our definition is to get a revisited version of the result in
[
9
]
cited above:
we give a self-contained proof that orthogonal factorization systems in a category
{\mathcal{A}}
correspond to homotopy torsion theories in
\mathbf{Arr}({\mathcal{A}})

with respect to the nullhomotopy structure induced by the canonical string of adjunctions between
{\mathcal{A}}
and
\mathbf{Arr}({\mathcal{A}}).

Moreover, basically the same proof allows us to extend this result to a correspondence between weakly orthogonal factorization systems
and weak homotopy torsion theories.

The layout of the paper is as follows.
In order to express the notion of homotopy torsion theory, in Section
2
we recall the notion of category with nullhomotopies.
We give a method to construct examples from pre-(co)radicals and we compare structure of nullhomotopies with ideals of arrows.
The case of pre-pointed categories and, in particular, the example of
\mathbf{Arr}({\mathcal{A}}),
are considered in Section
3
.
In Section
4
and in Section
5
we discuss the standard notion of homotopy kernel: definition,
main properties and conditions for the existence. In Section
6
we characterize pre-pointed categories among categories equipped
with a structure of nullhomotopies. In Section
7
we define homotopy torsion theories with respect to a given structure of nullhomotopies
and, in Section
8
, we compare them with pretorsion theories. It turns out that pretorsion theories coincide with homotopy torsion
theories when the structure of nullhomotopies is reduced to a closed ideal of arrows.
The correspondence
between various types of factorization systems in a category
{\mathcal{A}}
and homotopy torsion theories in
\mathbf{Arr}({\mathcal{A}})
is developed in the rest of
the paper: the case of (weakly) orthogonal factorization systems in Section
9
, the case of quasi-proper and proper orthogonal factorization
systems in Section
10
and in Section
11
, and finally the pointed case in Section
12
.
In order to help the reader, Section
13
gives a global view of the correspondences studied in Sections
9
,

10
and
11
.

Finally, let us mention that large portions of this paper also appear in the Master Thesis
[
15
]
of the second author, written under the
supervision of the first author.

4.3

The homotopy kernel of an arrow is determined by its universal property uniquely up to a unique isomorphism. Moreover, if an arrow has
two (necessarily isomorphic) homotopy kernels and one of them is strong, the other one also is strong.

Consider a homotopy kernel in a category with nullhomotopies

1. If

\Theta
-trivial.

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
({\mathcal{B}},\Theta)
be a category with nullhomotopies.
If, for every object
X\in{\mathcal{B}},
there exists a
\Theta
-kernel

\textstyle{{\mathcal{N}}(\mathrm{id}_{X})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{n_{X}}
\scriptstyle{\nu_{X}\;\Downarrow}
\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathrm{id}}
\textstyle{X}

then

is a pre-radical and the induced structure of nullhomotopies

\Theta_{n}
(see
2.10
.2) coincides with
\Theta.

Proof.

The functor
{\mathcal{N}}\colon{\mathcal{B}}\to{\mathcal{B}}
of the statement sends an object
X\in{\mathcal{B}}
to the object part of the

\Theta
-kernel of the identity on
X.
To extend it to arrows, consider an arrow
g\colon X\to Y.
Since

\nu_{X}\in\Theta(\mathrm{id}_{X}\cdot n_{X})=\Theta(n_{X}),
then
g\circ\nu_{X}\in\Theta(g\cdot n_{X})=\Theta(\mathrm{id}_{Y}\cdot g\cdot n_{X}).

By the universal property of
{\mathcal{N}}(\mathrm{id}_{Y}),
we get a unique arrow
{\mathcal{N}}_{g}\colon{\mathcal{N}}(\mathrm{id}_{X})\to{\mathcal{N}}(\mathrm{id}_{Y})
such that

g\cdot n_{X}=n_{Y}\cdot{\mathcal{N}}_{g}.
This gives at once the definition of the functor
{\mathcal{N}}
on arrows and the naturality of

In order to construct the isomorphism
\Theta_{n}\simeq\Theta,
recall that, for an arrow

we have

\Theta_{n}(g)=\{\psi\colon X\to{\mathcal{N}}(\mathrm{id}_{Y})\mid n_{Y}\cdot\psi=g\}.
Now we put:

-
\Theta_{n}(g)\to\Theta(g)\colon\psi\mapsto\nu_{Y}\circ\psi

-
\Theta(g)\to\Theta_{n}(g)\colon\lambda\mapsto\lambda^{\prime},
where
\lambda^{\prime}\colon X\to{\mathcal{N}}(\mathrm{id}_{Y})
is the unique

and

To check that these maps realize an isomorphism of nullhomotopy structures is easy.
∎

7
Homotopy torsion theories

Torsion theories have been originally introduced in the context of abelian categories by Dickson, see
[
5
]
.
We refer to Chapter 1 in
[

]
for the classical theory.

Definition 7.1
.

Let
({\mathcal{B}},\Theta)
be a category with nullhomotopies.
A
homotopy torsion theory
in
{\mathcal{B}}
relative to the structure
\Theta
(or
\Theta
-torsion theory) is given by two full subcategories

{\mathcal{T}}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}\,,\;{\mathcal{F}}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}

such that

1)

Both subcategories are replete, that is, closed under isomorphisms. (Note that this condition does not depend on the structure
\Theta.
)

2)

For any object
X\in{\mathcal{B}},
there exists a
\Theta
-exact
({\mathcal{T}},{\mathcal{F}})
-presentation, that is, a diagram

\textstyle{T(X)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{t_{X}}
\scriptstyle{\xi_{X}\;\Downarrow}
\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f_{X}}
\textstyle{F(X)}

such that

(a)

T(X)\in{\mathcal{T}}
and the triple
(T(X),t_{X},\xi_{X})
is a
\Theta
-kernel of
f_{X}.

(b)

F(X)\in{\mathcal{F}}
and the triple
(F(X),f_{X},\xi_{X})
is a
\Theta
-cokernel of
t_{X}.

3)

For all
T\in{\mathcal{T}}
and for all
F\in{\mathcal{F}},
we have that
T
is
\Theta
-orthogonal to
F
(see
4.5
).

Here there are some test properties for our definition of homotopy torsion theory.

.

Let
({\mathcal{B}},\Theta)
be a category with nullhomotopies and

a

-torsion theory.

\Theta
-exact

-presentation of an object is essentially unique.

Proof.

In the situation of Definition
7.1
, consider two
\Theta
-exact

-presentations of an object

\textstyle{T(X)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{t_{X}}
\scriptstyle{\xi_{X}\;\Downarrow}
\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f_{X}}
\textstyle{F(X)}

\textstyle{T\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\scriptstyle{\xi\;\Downarrow}
\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f}
\textstyle{F}

Since

and

there exists a unique

By the universal property of the
\Theta
-kernel

there exists a unique arrow

The aim of this section is to establish a complete comparison between pretorsion theories and homotopy torsion theories.
The first step is to compare kernels and cokernels relative to an ideal (called prekernels and precokernels in

7

Definition 8.1

Lemma 8.2
.

Let

be a category with nullhomotopies.

\Theta
-kernels in the sense of

{\mathcal{Z}}_{1}(\Theta)
-kernels in the sense of

,

Remark 8.3
.

Observe that the above fact is no longer true if we start with a structure of nullhomotopies

which is not discrete:

Corollary 9.5
.

Let
({\mathcal{E}},{\mathcal{M}})
be an orthogonal factorization system in a category
{\mathcal{A}}.
Then
{\mathcal{E}}
and
{\mathcal{M}}
contain the identity arrows.

Proof.

Via Proposition
9.4
, we have to prove that the identity arrows of
{\mathcal{A}},
seen as objects of
\mathbf{Arr}({\mathcal{A}}),
are in
{\mathcal{T}}\cap{\mathcal{F}},

where
({\mathcal{T}},{\mathcal{F}})
is the
{\mathcal{H}}({\mathcal{A}})
-torsion theory corresponding to
({\mathcal{E}},{\mathcal{M}}).
By Corollary

, this amounts to showing
that
{\mathcal{H}}({\mathcal{A}})(\mathrm{id}_{(X,\mathrm{id}_{X},X)})=\{\ast\},
which is obvious.
∎

9.6
.

Here we consider the one obtained simply dropping the uniqueness in the condition of orthogonality. Some other intermediate notions, especially
functorial factorizations and algebraic functorial factorizations as in

10

and the corresponding notions of torsion operators, will

10
The quasi-proper case

As a step towards the case of proper orthogonal factorization systems, which will be treated in Section
11
,
we find useful to consider the intermediate notion of quasi-proper orthogonal factorization systems.

Definition 10.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be a category.

1.

A (weakly) orthogonal factorization system
({\mathcal{E}},{\mathcal{M}})
is
quasi-proper
if:

(a)

for every arrow
e\colon E\to E_{0}
in
{\mathcal{E}},
the unit

\gamma_{(E,e,E_{0})}=(e,\mathrm{id}_{E_{0}})\colon(E,e,E_{0})\to(E_{0},\mathrm{id}_{E_{0}},E_{0})

of the adjunction
{\mathcal{C}}\dashv{\mathcal{U}}
is an epimorphism in
\mathbf{Arr}({\mathcal{A}}),

(b)

for every arrow
m\colon M\to M_{0}
in
{\mathcal{M}},
the counit

\beta_{(M,m,M_{0})}=(\mathrm{id}_{M},m)\colon(M,\mathrm{id}_{M},M)\to(M,m,M_{0})

of the adjunction
{\mathcal{U}}\dashv{\mathcal{D}}
is a monomorphism in
\mathbf{Arr}({\mathcal{A}}).

2.

An orthogonal factorization system
({\mathcal{E}},{\mathcal{M}})
is
proper
if:

(a)

every arrow in
{\mathcal{E}}
is an epimorphism,

(b)

every arrow in
{\mathcal{M}}
is a monomorphism.

10.2
.

Observe that the notion of proper weakly orthogonal factorization system is irrelevant because, if the arrows in
{\mathcal{E}}

are epimorphisms or if the arrows in
{\mathcal{M}}
are monomorphisms, then weakly orthogonal implies orthogonal.

10.3
.

In the next lemma, we will use the following simple facts:

1.

If a category
{\mathcal{A}}
has a terminal object
\ast,
then the functor
{\mathcal{D}}\colon\mathbf{Arr}({\mathcal{A}})\to{\mathcal{A}}

has a right adjoint defined by
\Lambda(X)=(X,!\colon X\to\ast,\ast).

2.

If a category
{\mathcal{A}}
has an initial object
\emptyset,
then the functor
{\mathcal{C}}\colon\mathbf{Arr}({\mathcal{A}})\to{\mathcal{A}}

as a left adjoint defined by
\Gamma(X)=(\emptyset,!\colon\emptyset\to X,X).

Lemma 10.4
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be a category.

1.

Every proper orthogonal factorization system in
{\mathcal{A}}
is quasi-proper.

2.

If the category
{\mathcal{A}}
has a terminal object or an initial object, then every quasi-proper weakly
orthogonal factorization system is orthogonal.

3.

If the category
{\mathcal{A}}
has a terminal object and an initial object, then every quasi-proper weakly
orthogonal factorization system is proper and orthogonal.

Proof.

1. If
e\colon E\to E_{0}
is an epimorphism in
{\mathcal{A}},
then
(e,\mathrm{id}_{E_{0}})
is an epimorphism in
\mathbf{Arr}({\mathcal{A}}).

If
m\colon M\to M_{0}
is a monomorphism in
{\mathcal{A}},
then
(\mathrm{id}_{M},m)
is a monomorphism in
\mathbf{Arr}({\mathcal{A}}).

2 and 3. By assumption,
(e,\mathrm{id}_{E_{0}})
is an epimorphism for every
e\colon E\to E_{0}
in
{\mathcal{E}}.
If
{\mathcal{A}}

has a terminal object, then
{\mathcal{D}}\colon\mathbf{Arr}({\mathcal{A}})\to{\mathcal{A}}
has a right adjoint (see
10.3
) and then
it preserves epimorphisms. Therefore,
e
is an epimorphism, which implies that the diagonal in the
orthogonality condition of Definition
9.1
is necessarily unique. The argument if
{\mathcal{A}}
has an initial
object is dual.
∎

The final output of this section (Corollary

Proposition 11.1

({\mathcal{B}},\Theta)

\textstyle{T(X)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

{\mathcal{Z}}_{1}(\Theta)
-exact.
We check the universal property of the
{\mathcal{Z}}_{1}(\Theta)
-kernel, the one for the
{\mathcal{Z}}_{1}(\Theta)
-cokernel is dual.
Let

f_{X}\cdot f\in{\mathcal{Z}}_{1}(\Theta).
This means that there exists a nullhomotopy

Therefore, there exists a unique arrow

If

∎

Equivariant log-concavity of graph matchings
Shiyue Li

Abstract
. For any graph, we show that the graded permutation representation of the graph automorphism group given by matchings is strongly equivariantly log-concave. The proof gives a family of equivariant injections inspired by a combinatorial map of Kratthenthaler and reduces to the hard Lefschetz theorem.



1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a graph. A
matching
on
\mathbb{G}
is a set of pairwise non-adjacent edges. Let
\mathbb{M}_{k}
be the set of matchings on
\mathbb{G}
with
k
edges. It is well-known that the sequence of graph matching numbers is
log-concave
. That is, for positive integers
{k\leq\ell}
,

\left\lvert\mathbb{M}_{k-1}\right\rvert\left\lvert\mathbb{M}_{\ell+1}\right\rvert\leq\left\lvert\mathbb{M}_{k}\right\rvert\left\lvert\mathbb{M}_{\ell}\right\rvert,

The first proofs in
[
HL72
, Theorem 4.2]
and
[
PL86
]
show that the generating polynomial of the graph matching sequence
\sum\left\lvert\mathbb{M}_{k}\right\rvert x^{k}
has only real roots, which implies log-concavity. Later, in
[
Kra96
]
, Kratthenthaler gave a combinatorial proof of this result. However, as we shall see in
Example

2.3
, this combinatorial approach breaks the graph symmetry.

It is interesting to ask whether the graph matching sequence exhibits log-concavity that respects the graph symmetry. The notion of
(strongly) equivariant log-concavity
arises when one considers log-concavity with respect to the symmetry of the underlying objects. It was introduced by Gedeon, Proudfoot and Young
[
GPY17
, Section 5]
in the context of the equivariant Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomial of a matroid. It is recently used to study other log-concave sequences in combinatorics, geometry and topology that involve group actions (see
[
PXY18
,
MMPR21
,
Gui22
,
GX22
]
).

We now recall precise definitions.

Definition 1.1

(
[
GPY17
, Section 5]
)
.

Let
H
be a group.

(1)

A graded
H
-representation

V^{\bullet}=\bigoplus_{k=0}V_{k}

is
H
-equivariantly log-concave
if for all
i\geq 1
, there exists an
H
-equivariant injection

V_{i-1}\otimes V_{i+1}\hookrightarrow V_{i}\otimes V_{i}.

(2)

A graded
H
-representation

V^{\bullet}=\bigoplus_{k=0}V_{k}

is
strongly
H
-equivariantly log-concave
if for all positive integers
k\leq\ell
, there exists an
H
-equivariant injection

V_{k-1}\otimes V_{\ell+1}\hookrightarrow V_{k}\otimes V_{\ell}.

Here, by an
H
-equivariant map, we mean the following: suppose two objects
X
and
Y
each afford an action by a group
H
. If a map
\varphi\colon X\to Y
satisfies that

h\cdot\varphi(x)=\varphi(h\cdot x)

for all
h
in
H
and
x
in
X
, then
\varphi
is
H
-equivariant.

In the case of graph matchings, the graded
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{G})
-representation is the graded permutation representation of
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{G})
over any field
\mathbb{F}
of characteristic
0

V^{\bullet}_{\mathbb{G}}:=\bigoplus_{k}\operatorname{Span}_{\mathbb{F}}\{x_{M}\mid M\in\mathbb{M}_{k}\}.

The aim of this paper is to prove the following theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

The graded permutation representation
V_{\mathbb{G}}^{\bullet}
of
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{G})
given by matchings on
\mathbb{G}
is strongly
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{G})
-equivariantly log-concave.

To prove this, we first construct an
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{G})
-equivariant map from
V_{k-1}\otimes V_{\ell+1}
to
V_{k}\otimes V_{\ell}
, and then decompose the tensor products
V_{k-1}\otimes V_{\ell+1}
and
V_{k}\otimes V_{\ell}
into direct sums with the following properties:

(1)

The direct sum decomposition of
V_{k-1}\otimes V_{\ell-1}
is given by a partition on
\mathbb{M}_{k-1}\times\mathbb{M}_{\ell+1}
.

(2)

The direct sum decomposition of
V_{k}\otimes V_{\ell}
is given by a similar partition on
\mathbb{M}_{k}\times\mathbb{M}_{\ell}
.

(3)

On each direct summand
V(P)\subset V_{k-1}\otimes V_{\ell+1}
associated with a part
P
, the map
\Phi_{k,\ell}
is a hard Lefschetz operator into the direct summand
V(P^{\prime})\subset V_{k}\otimes V_{\ell}
determined by
P
. Injectivity follows from the hard Lefschetz theorem.

Note that taking dimensions of
V_{\mathbb{G}}^{\bullet}
immediately recovers the non-equivariant log-concavity of graph matching numbers.

As a corollary, if
\mathbb{G}
is the graph of
n
disjoint edges, then we obtain an
S_{n}
-equivariant categorification of the log-concavity of the binomial coefficients.

Corollary 1.3
.

Given an integer
n\geq 1
, the graded permutation representation of
S_{n}
given by
all
k
-subsets of
[n]

V^{\bullet}=\bigoplus_{k}\operatorname{Span}_{\mathbb{F}}\{x_{S}\mid S\subseteq[n],\left\lvert S\right\rvert=k\}

is
S_{n}
-equivariantly log-concave.

Remark 1.4
.

We situate both the object
V_{\mathbb{G}}^{\bullet}
and the techniques in the present work in a slightly broader context. The assignment to each graph
\mathbb{G}
of a graded
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{G})
-representation
V_{\mathbb{G}}^{\bullet}
is also a representation of the
graph minor category

\mathcal{G}
(see
[
MPR20
]
). It consists of graphs as objects, and compositions of edge deletions, contractions and automorphisms as morphisms. For each graph
\mathbb{G}
, one can also take the simplicial complex of matchings
\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{G})
on
\mathbb{G}
and study its homology
H(\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{G}))
(see
[
MR20
]
). One connection is that, the differentials that define the homology
H(\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{G}))
also equip
V_{\mathbb{G}}^{\bullet}
with a natural family of differentials that respects the graph minor morphisms. It would be very interesting to see further interactions between
V_{\mathbb{G}}^{\bullet}
and other representations of the category
\mathcal{G}
.

2.
Proof of
1.2

Let
k\leq\ell
be positive integers less than the maximum matching size on
\mathbb{G}
, and let
\mathbb{M}_{k,\ell}=\mathbb{M}_{k}\times\mathbb{M}_{\ell}
.

2.1.
Construction

Our strategy is to assign a subset of
\mathbb{M}_{k,\ell}
to each pair
(M,M^{\prime})
in
\mathbb{M}_{k-1,\ell+1}
. The procedure goes as follows:

(a)

Color
M
with blue and
M^{\prime}
with pink, then each edge has
0,1
, or
2
colors. If we consider only the set of edges with exactly
1
color, then we obtain a subgraph
\mathbb{G}_{M,M^{\prime}}
. That is,
\mathbb{G}_{M,M^{\prime}}
is the symmetric difference of
M
and
M^{\prime}
.

(b)

Since
M,M^{\prime}
are matchings, each vertex in
\mathbb{G}_{M,M^{\prime}}
cannot have degree
>2
. That is, any connected component in
\mathbb{G}_{M,M^{\prime}}
is one of the two types:

(i)

a chain; or

(ii)

a cycle with even number of edges.

Let
\mathbb{C}_{M,M^{\prime}}
denote the set of chains in
\mathbb{G}_{M,M^{\prime}}
with odd length.
By definition, any chain in
\mathbb{C}_{M,M^{\prime}}
must start and end with the same color. If a chain starts and ends with blue (respectively, pink) edges, we call it a blue (respectively, pink) chain.
We write

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}_{M,M^{\prime}}

\displaystyle:=\{\textrm{blue chains in }\mathbb{C}_{M,M^{\prime}}\},

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{M,M^{\prime}}

\displaystyle:=\{\textrm{pink chains in }\mathbb{C}_{M,M^{\prime}}\}.

(c)

Choose any pink chain in
\mathbb{P}_{M,M^{\prime}}
. If we swap the edge colors (blue to pink, pink to blue), then we obtain an additional blue chain, and the number of blue (respectively, pink) edges in
\mathbb{G}_{M,M^{\prime}}
increases (respectively, decreases) by
1
. Therefore, if we record all the blue (respectively, pink) edges in this new edge-colored graph, we obtain a pair
(N,N^{\prime})
in
\mathbb{M}_{k,\ell}
.

(d)

Do (c) for all pink chains in
\mathbb{P}_{M,M^{\prime}}
. By construction, the resulting pairs are all distinct. Therefore, we obtain a subset of
\mathbb{M}_{k,\ell}
and denote it as
\mathbb{N}_{M,M^{\prime}}
.

We highlight some numerical facts.
Before (c), the contributions of blue and pink edges in
\mathbb{G}_{M,M^{\prime}}
from every type of components are as follows.

(i)

An even chain or cycle contributes the same number of blue and pink edges.

(ii)

A blue chain contributes
1
more blue edge than pink edges.

(iii)

A pink chain contributes
1
more pink edge than blue edges.

If an edge is not in
\mathbb{G}_{M,M^{\prime}}
, then it must have no color or be both blue and pink.
Therefore we have that

\left\lvert\mathbb{P}_{M,M^{\prime}}\right\rvert-\left\lvert\mathbb{B}_{M,M^{\prime}}\right\rvert=(\ell+1)-(k-1)=k-\ell+2\geq 2.

The positivity of this difference ensures that there exists a pink chain on which to perform (c).
This, together with the fact that
\left\lvert\mathbb{B}_{M,M^{\prime}}\right\rvert+\left\lvert\mathbb{P}_{M,M^{\prime}}\right\rvert=\left\lvert\mathbb{C}_{M,M^{\prime}}\right\rvert
, implies that

\left\lvert\mathbb{B}_{M,M^{\prime}}\right\rvert\leq\frac{\left\lvert\mathbb{C}_{M,M^{\prime}}\right\rvert}{2}-1.

After (d), we have
\left\lvert\mathbb{N}_{M,M^{\prime}}\right\rvert=\left\lvert\mathbb{P}_{M,M^{\prime}}\right\rvert\geq 2+\left\lvert\mathbb{B}_{M,M^{\prime}}\right\rvert\geq 2
by construction.

2.2.
Proof of equivariance

This section is devoted to show that
\Phi_{k,\ell}
is
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{G})
-equivariant. That is, for any
\sigma
in
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{G})
and
(M,M^{\prime})
in
\mathbb{M}_{k-1,\ell+1}
,

\Phi_{k,\ell}(\sigma\cdot(x_{M}\otimes x_{M^{\prime}}))=\sigma\cdot\Phi_{k,\ell}(x_{M}\otimes x_{M^{\prime}}).

The left hand side is

\Phi_{k,\ell}(\sigma\cdot(x_{M}\otimes x_{M^{\prime}}))=\Phi_{k,\ell}(x_{\sigma(M)}\otimes x_{\sigma(M^{\prime})})=\frac{1}{\left\lvert\mathbb{N}_{\sigma(M),\sigma(M^{\prime})}\right\rvert}\sum_{(N,N^{\prime})}x_{N}\otimes x_{N^{\prime}},

where
(N,N^{\prime})
ranges over all pairs in
\mathbb{N}_{\sigma(M),\sigma(M^{\prime})}
.

The right hand side is

\sigma\cdot\Phi_{k,\ell}(x_{M}\otimes x_{M^{\prime}})=\sigma\cdot\bigg(\frac{1}{\left\lvert\mathbb{N}_{M,M^{\prime}}\right\rvert}\sum_{(N,N^{\prime})}x_{N}\otimes x_{N^{\prime}}\bigg)=\frac{1}{\left\lvert\mathbb{N}_{M,M^{\prime}}\right\rvert}\sum_{(N,N^{\prime})}x_{\sigma(N)}\otimes x_{\sigma(N^{\prime})},

where
(N,N^{\prime})
ranges over all pairs in
\mathbb{N}_{M,M^{\prime}}
.

Crucially, producing the set
\mathbb{N}_{M,M^{\prime}}
and performing
\sigma
on the pair
(M,M^{\prime})
are commutative. In other words, the set equality

\mathbb{N}_{\sigma(M),\sigma(M^{\prime})}=\sigma(\mathbb{N}_{M,M^{\prime}})=\{(\sigma(N),\sigma(N^{\prime}))\colon(N,N^{\prime})\in\mathbb{N}_{M,M^{\prime}}\}

holds. Moreover, by virtue of
\sigma
being a graph automorphism, the numerical equality

\frac{1}{\left\lvert\mathbb{N}_{M,M^{\prime}}\right\rvert}=\frac{1}{\left\lvert\mathbb{N}_{\sigma(M),\sigma(M^{\prime})}\right\rvert}

holds.
These two identities imply that the two sides in the desired equation coincide.

2.3.
Proof of injectivity

This section is devoted to show that
\Phi_{k,\ell}
is injective.
As stated in the introduction, we do so by decomposing the tensor products
V_{k-1}\otimes V_{\ell+1}
and
V_{k}\otimes V_{\ell}
into direct sums with the following properties:

(1)

The direct sum decomposition of
V_{k-1}\otimes V_{\ell-1}
is given by a partition on
\mathbb{M}_{k-1}\times\mathbb{M}_{\ell+1}
.

(2)

The direct sum decomposition of
V_{k}\otimes V_{\ell}
is given by a similar partition on
\mathbb{M}_{k}\times\mathbb{M}_{\ell}
.

(3)

On each direct summand
V(P)\subset V_{k-1}\otimes V_{\ell+1}
associated with a part
P
, the
\Phi_{k,\ell}
is a hard Lefschetz operator into the direct summand
V(P^{\prime})\subset V_{k}\otimes V_{\ell}
determined by
P
. Injectivity follows from the hard Lefschetz theorem.

First, we create a partition
\Pi_{k-1,\ell+1}
on
\mathbb{M}_{k-1,\ell+1}
by giving an equivalence relation: any two pairs
(M_{1},M_{2})
and
(M^{\prime}_{1},M^{\prime}_{2})
in
\mathbb{M}_{k-1,\ell+1}
are equivalent if they form the same subgraph and yield the same coloring outside of odd chains with
1
-colored edges. More precisely,

(i)

M_{1}\cup M_{1}^{\prime}=M_{2}\cup M_{2}^{\prime}
;

(ii)

M_{1}=M_{2}
,
M_{1}^{\prime}=M_{2}^{\prime}
outside of
\mathbb{C}_{M_{1},M_{1}^{\prime}}
. Note that,
\mathbb{C}_{M_{1},M_{1}^{\prime}}=\mathbb{C}_{M_{2},M_{2}^{\prime}}
by (i). The restriction thus makes sense.

Any part
P
of the partition
\Pi_{k-1,\ell+1}
has the following properties: for any
(M_{1},M_{1}^{\prime})
and
(M_{2},M_{2}^{\prime})
in
P
,

(1)

the sets of chains
\mathbb{C}_{M_{1},M_{1}^{\prime}}
and
\mathbb{C}_{M_{2},M_{2}^{\prime}}
are equal as subgraphs; and

(2)

the cardinalities of the blue chains are equal, i.e.,

\left\lvert\mathbb{B}_{M_{1},M_{1}^{\prime}}\right\rvert=\left\lvert\mathbb{B}_{M_{2},M_{2}^{\prime}}\right\rvert.

Indeed, (1) follows from the definition of
P
, and (2) follows for numerical reasons:

\left\lvert\mathbb{B}_{M_{1},M_{1}^{\prime}}\right\rvert+\left\lvert\mathbb{P}_{M_{1},M_{1}^{\prime}}\right\rvert=\left\lvert\mathbb{C}_{M_{1},M_{1}^{\prime}}\right\rvert=\left\lvert\mathbb{C}_{M_{2},M_{2}^{\prime}}\right\rvert=\left\lvert\mathbb{B}_{M_{2},M_{2}^{\prime}}\right\rvert+\left\lvert\mathbb{P}_{M_{2},M_{2}^{\prime}}\right\rvert

and

\left\lvert\mathbb{P}_{M_{1},M_{1}^{\prime}}\right\rvert-\left\lvert\mathbb{B}_{M_{1},M_{1}^{\prime}}\right\rvert=(\ell+1)-(k-1)=\left\lvert\mathbb{P}_{M_{2},M_{2}^{\prime}}\right\rvert-\left\lvert\mathbb{B}_{M_{2},M_{2}^{\prime}}\right\rvert.

Thus we have that

\left\lvert\mathbb{B}_{M_{1},M_{1}^{\prime}}\right\rvert=\left\lvert\mathbb{B}_{M_{2},M_{2}^{\prime}}\right\rvert.

Therefore, we set
\mathbb{C}_{P}:=\mathbb{C}_{M,M^{\prime}}
and
\left\lvert\mathbb{B}_{P}\right\rvert:=\left\lvert\mathbb{B}_{M,M^{\prime}}\right\rvert
for any
(M,M^{\prime})
in
P
.

Now, consider the following vector space for any part
P
in
\Pi_{k-1,\ell+1}

V_{k-1,\ell+1}(P):=\operatorname{Span}_{\mathbb{F}}\{x_{M}\otimes x_{M}\mid(M,M^{\prime})\in P\}.

We now realize
V_{k-1,\ell+1}(P)
as a categorification of the
\left\lvert\mathbb{B}_{P}\right\rvert
th level of the Boolean lattice on
\mathbb{C}_{P}
.
Consider the map

\beta_{P}\colon P\to\binom{\mathbb{C}_{P}}{\left\lvert\mathbb{B}_{P}\right\rvert},\quad(M,M^{\prime})\mapsto\mathbb{B}_{M,M^{\prime}}.

It is well-defined by the construction of
\mathbb{B}_{M,M^{\prime}}
in step (b) of
Section

2.1
.
It is surjective: for each subset of edges with size
\left\lvert\mathbb{B}_{P}\right\rvert
of
\mathbb{C}_{P}
, one can color the present subset as blue chains and reverse the last step in (c). It is injective: if two pairs
(M_{1},M_{1}^{\prime})
and
(M_{2},M_{2}^{\prime})
in
P
give the same set of blue chains, then the fact

\mathbb{B}_{M_{1},M_{1}^{\prime}}\sqcup\mathbb{P}_{M_{2},M_{2}^{\prime}}=\mathbb{C}_{P}=\mathbb{B}_{M_{2},M_{2}^{\prime}}\sqcup\mathbb{P}_{M_{2},M_{2}^{\prime}}

implies that
\mathbb{P}_{M_{1},M_{1}^{\prime}}=\mathbb{P}_{M_{2},M_{2}^{\prime}}
. This further means
M_{1}=M_{2},M_{1}^{\prime}=M_{2}^{\prime}
on
\mathbb{C}_{P}
, and moreover
(M_{1},M_{1}^{\prime})=(M_{2},M_{2}^{\prime})
. Therefore,
\beta_{P}
is a bijection.

Next, we consider the vector space

V_{\mathbb{C}_{P},\left\lvert\mathbb{B}_{P}\right\rvert}:=\operatorname{Span}_{\mathbb{F}}\bigg\{y_{\mathbb{B}}\mid\mathbb{B}\in\binom{\mathbb{C}_{P}}{\left\lvert\mathbb{B}_{P}\right\rvert}\bigg\},

and define

\underline{\beta_{P}}\colon V_{k-1,\ell+1}(P)\to V_{\mathbb{C}_{P},\left\lvert\mathbb{B}_{P}\right\rvert},\quad x_{M}\otimes x_{M^{\prime}}\mapsto y_{\mathbb{B}_{M,M^{\prime}}}.

It is an isomorphism of vector spaces, because
\beta_{P}
is a bijection on the bases.

Then, we do the same procedure for
\mathbb{M}_{k,\ell}
. If we create a partition
\Pi_{k,\ell}
on
\mathbb{M}_{k,\ell}
in the same way, then the set

P^{\prime}=\bigcup_{(M,M^{\prime})\in P}\mathbb{N}_{M,M^{\prime}}

for
P
in
\Pi_{k-1,\ell+1}
is also a part in
\Pi_{k,\ell}
.
Indeed, we need to show that
P^{\prime}
satisfies the following properties.

(1)

Elements in
P^{\prime}
are pairwise equivalent. Indeed, since the procedure (d) does not change edges outside of
\mathbb{C}_{M,M^{\prime}}
, any
(N_{1},N_{1}^{\prime})
and
(N_{2},N_{2}^{\prime})
in
P^{\prime}
must satisfy that
N_{1}\cup N_{1}^{\prime}=N_{2}\cup N_{2}^{\prime}
and
N_{1}=N_{2},N_{1}^{\prime}=N_{2}^{\prime}
outside of
\mathbb{C}_{M,M^{\prime}}
.

(2)

Any pair that is equivalent to a member in
P^{\prime}
must also belong to
P
. That is, if a pair
(M,M^{\prime})
in
\mathbb{M}_{k,\ell}
is equivalent to a pair
(N,N^{\prime})
in
P^{\prime}
, then there exists
(\underline{M},\underline{M}^{\prime})
in
P
such that

(M,M^{\prime})\in\mathbb{N}_{\underline{M},\underline{M}^{\prime}}.

Indeed, such
(\underline{M},\underline{M}^{\prime})
can be constructed by reversing the procedure (c) using any pink chain in
\mathbb{P}_{M,M^{\prime}}
.

We define the map
\beta_{P^{\prime}}
and
\underline{\beta_{P^{\prime}}}
similar to those for
P
. Note that, by construction,

\left\lvert\mathbb{B}_{P^{\prime}}\right\rvert=\left\lvert\mathbb{B}_{P}\right\rvert+1\quad\textrm{and}\quad\mathbb{C}_{P^{\prime}}=\mathbb{C}_{P}.

Finally, for each

\Pi_{k-1,\ell+1}
, define the linear map

L_{P}\colon V_{\mathbb{C}_{P},\left\lvert\mathbb{B}_{P}\right\rvert}\to V_{\mathbb{C}_{P^{\prime}},\left\lvert\mathbb{B}_{P}\right\rvert+1},\quad y_{\mathbb{B}}\mapsto\frac{1}{\left\lvert\mathbb{C}_{P}\right\rvert-\left\lvert\mathbb{B}_{P}\right\rvert}\sum_{\mathbb{B}\subseteq\mathbb{B}^{\prime}\in\binom{\mathbb{C}_{P}}{\left\lvert\mathbb{B}_{P}\right\rvert+1}}y_{\mathbb{B}^{\prime}}.

Crucially,

. This operator and its injectivity on the lower half graded pieces have been studied in various contexts. We invite the reader to see proofs of various flavors:

Remark 2.2
.

Our construction was inspired by Krattenthaler’s combinatorial proof of the non-equivariant log-concavity of graph matchings in
[
Kra96
]
. His proof constructs an injective set map

f_{k,\ell}\colon\mathbb{M}_{k-1,\ell+1}\to\mathbb{M}_{k,\ell}.

This injective map depends on a vertex order on the graph to select only
1
pink chain to convert into a blue chain; for one concrete example, see
[
SW86
, Section 2.2]
.
In general, this set map is not
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{G})
-equivariant. See the following example. Our approach fixes where the map
f_{k,\ell}
breaks the graph symmetry. Interestingly, this fix also reduces the proof of the injectivity to the hard Lefschetz theorem, as mentioned before.

A weighted equivariant promotion

Thanks to the injections, we also obtain an
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{G})
-equivariant promotion of the following theorem.

Theorem 3.1

(
[
Kra96
, Theorem 2]
)
.

Fix a graph
\mathbb{G}
, the
k
th weighted matching number of
\mathbb{G}
is a polynomial

\mathbb{M}_{k}(\mathbb{G},\mathbf{x}):=\sum_{M\in\mathbb{M}_{k}}\prod_{e\in M}x_{e}\in F[x_{e}]_{e\in E(\mathbb{G})}.

Then for positive integers
{k\leq\ell}
less than the maximum matching size, the polynomial

\mathbb{M}_{k}(\mathbb{G},\mathbf{x})\mathbb{M}_{\ell}(\mathbb{G},\mathbf{x})-\mathbb{M}_{k-1}(\mathbb{G},\mathbf{x})\mathbb{M}_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{G},\mathbf{x})

has nonnegative coefficients.

The polynomial ring in variables
\{x_{e}\}_{e\in E(\mathbb{G})}
is naturally a graded
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{G})

\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{G})

\iota_{k,\ell}\colon V_{k}\otimes V_{\ell}\to F[x_{e}]_{e\in E(\mathbb{G})},\quad x_{M}\otimes x_{M^{\prime}}\mapsto\left(\prod_{e\in M}x_{e}\right)\left(\prod_{e^{\prime}\in M^{\prime}}x_{e^{\prime}}\right).

\iota_{k,\ell}

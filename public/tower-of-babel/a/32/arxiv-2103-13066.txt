Additive and multiplicative Sidon sets
Oliver Roche-Newton, Audie Warren

Abstract
. We give a construction of a set A ⊂ ℕ A\subset\mathbb{N} such that any subset A ′ ⊂ A A^{\prime}\subset A with | A ′ | ≫ | A | 2 / 3 |A^{\prime}|\gg|A|^{2/3} is neither an additive nor multiplicative Sidon set. In doing so, we refute a conjecture of Klurman and Pohoata.



1.
Introduction

A set
A\subset\mathbb{R}
is a
Sidon set
if the only solutions to the energy equation

a+b=c+d\text{ such that }a,b,c,d\in A

are the trivial solutions whereby
\{a,b\}=\{c,d\}
. Sidon sets are important and widely studied objects in Additive Combinatorics; see
[
14
]
for background on this topic and
[
7
]
for a thorough survey.

Given an arbitrary finite set
A\subset\mathbb{R}
, we define
s_{+}(A)
to be the size of the largest Sidon set
A^{\prime}
such that
A^{\prime}\subset A
. The problem of understanding the behaviour of
s_{+}([N])
has attracted particular interest, and it is known that
s_{+}([N])=\Theta(N^{1/2})
. See
[
4
]
for a nice introduction to this question.

Komlos, Sulyok and Szemerédi
[
6
]
proved (as part of a much more general result about sets avoiding certain linear configurations) that, up to constant factors,
s_{+}(A)
is minimised among sets of size
N
by the case when
A=\{1,2,\dots,N\}
. That is, they proved the bound

s_{+}(A)\gg|A|^{1/2}

(1.1)

holds for any
1
1

1

In fact, this was proved in
[
6
]
only for sets of integers, but it has later been established that the same result holds over the reals; see for instance
[
9
, Lemma 2.2]
.
finite set
A\subset\mathbb{R}
.

Similarly, a
multiplicative Sidon set
is a set
A\subset\mathbb{R}
such that the only solutions to

ab=cd\text{ such that }a,b,c,d\in A

are the trivial solutions with
\{a,b\}=\{c,d\}
. Given an arbitrary finite set
A\subset\mathbb{R}
, we define
s_{*}(A)
to be the size of the largest multiplicative Sidon set
A^{\prime}
such that
A^{\prime}\subset A
. Applying the estimate (
1.1
) to the set
\log A=\{\log a:a\in A\}
, it follows that

s_{*}(A)\gg|A|^{1/2}

(1.2)

for any finite
A\subset\mathbb{R}
.

In the spirit of the sum-product problem, one may ask the question of whether it is guaranteed that, for a fixed set
A\subset\mathbb{R}
, at least one of the inequalities (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) is improvable. This question was indeed asked by Klurman and Pohoata
[
8
]
, who made the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1.1
.

There exists a constant
c>0
such that for all finite
A\subset\mathbb{R}
,

\max\{s_{+}(A),s_{*}(A)\}\gg|A|^{1/2+c}.

(1.3)

Klurman and Pohoata in fact also made the stronger conjecture that, for all
\epsilon>0
,

\max\{s_{+}(A),s_{*}(A)\}\gg|A|^{1-\epsilon}.

(1.4)

The main result of this note is a construction disproving (
1.4
).

Theorem 1.2
.

For all
N\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a set
A\subset\mathbb{N}
with
|A|\geq N
and such that
s_{+}(A)\ll|A|^{1/2}\log|A|
and
s_{*}(A)\ll|A|^{2/3}
. In particular,

\max\{s_{+}(A),s_{*}(A)\}\ll|A|^{2/3}.

1.1.
Notation

Throughout the paper, the standard notation

\ll,\gg
and respectively
O
and
\Omega
is applied to positive quantities in the usual way. That is,
X\gg Y
,
Y\ll X,

X=\Omega(Y)
and
Y=O(X)
all mean that
X\geq cY
, for some absolute constant
c>0
. If both
X\ll Y
and
Y\ll X
hold we write
X\approx Y
, or equivalently
X=\Theta(Y)
. All logarithms are in base
e
. For a set
A
, the notation
A(\cdot)
is used for its characteristic function.

2.
Construction

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

For a sufficiently large integer
n
, define
P
to be the set of all primes less than or equal to
n
, and define

Q=\left\{n<q\leq\frac{n^{2}}{\log n}:q\text{ prime}\right\}.

The construction is simply the product set

A:=P\cdot Q=\left\{p\cdot q:p,q\text{ prime, }p\leq n<q\leq\frac{n^{2}}{\log n}\right\}.

A
is a fairly dense subset of the set
\{1,2,\dots,n^{3}/\log n\}
, which implies that
A+A
is small and thus
s_{+}(A)
is also small. Indeed, by the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic and the Prime Number Theorem,

|A|=|P||Q|\approx\frac{n^{3}}{\log^{3}n}.

Since
A+A\subset\left\{1,\dots,\frac{2n^{3}}{\log n}\right\}
, it follows that

|A+A|\ll|A|\log^{2}|A|.

(2.1)

Furthermore, since a Sidon set of size
m
contained in
A
gives rise to
\binom{m}{2}+m
distinct sums in
A+A
, it follows that

s_{+}(A)\ll|A+A|^{1/2}\ll|A|^{1/2}\log|A|.

It remains to prove the upper bound

s_{*}(A)\ll\frac{n^{2}}{\log^{2}n}\approx|A|^{2/3}.

(2.2)

To do this, we appeal to an argument Erdős
[
3
]
used in order to establish the existence of a set with very small additive energy but still having fairly small
s_{+}(A)
. What follows can be viewed as an adaptation of this argument in the multiplicative setting.

We view the elements of
A
as edges of a complete bipartite graph
G=(V,A)
with vertex set
V=P\sqcup Q
, and with the edge between
p\in P
and
q\in Q
labelled by the product
pq
.

Suppose that
E\subset A
is a multiplicative Sidon set. Then
E
does not give rise to any copies of the cycle
C_{4}
in
G
. Indeed, suppose that we have four edges in
E
which form a cycle. Then the cycle must be of the form
pq,qp^{\prime},p^{\prime}q^{\prime},q^{\prime}p
. But if all of these four edges are in
E
then we have

(pq)(p^{\prime}q^{\prime})=(p^{\prime}q)(pq^{\prime}),

which contradicts the fact that
E
is a multiplicative Sidon set.

On the other hand, any
E\subset A
which does not contain a copy of
C_{4}
has size
|E|\ll\frac{n^{2}}{\log^{2}n}
by the Kővári-Sós-Turán Theorem. For completeness, we give a quick proof below using an application of Cauchy-Schwarz:

\displaystyle|E|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{q\in Q}\sum_{p\in P}E(pq)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq|Q|\sum_{q\in Q}\left(\sum_{p\in P}E(pq)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{n^{2}}{\log^{2}n}\sum_{p,p^{\prime}\in P}\sum_{q\in Q}E(pq)E(p^{\prime}q)

\displaystyle=\frac{n^{2}}{\log^{2}n}\left(|E|+\sum_{p,p^{\prime}\in P:p\neq p^{\prime}}\sum_{q\in Q}E(pq)E(p^{\prime}q)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n^{2}}{\log^{2}n}\left(|E|+|P|^{2}\right)\ll\frac{n^{2}}{\log^{2}n}\left(|E|+\frac{n^{2}}{\log^{2}n}\right).

If the sum
|E|+\frac{n^{2}}{\log^{2}n}
is dominated by the second term then we are done. Therefore we may assume otherwise, and the last inequality gives

|E|^{2}\ll\frac{n^{2}}{\log^{2}n}\left(|E|+\frac{n^{2}}{\log^{2}n}\right)\ll\frac{n^{2}}{\log^{2}n}|E|.

We have proved that any subset of size greater than
Cn^{2}/\log^{2}n
, for some constant
C
, contains a
C_{4}
, and thus is not a multiplicative Sidon set. This proves (
2.2
) and thus completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

3.
Connections to additive and multiplicative energy

3.1.
The relationship between
s_{+}(A)
and additive energy

The additive energy of
A
, denoted
E_{+}(A)
, is the number of ordered quadruplues
(a,b,c,d)\in A\times A\times A\times A
such that

a+b=c+d.

Note that
E_{+}(A)
also counts the trivial solutions, and so we have
E_{+}(A)\geq|A|^{2}
.
One may suspect that if
A
has small additive energy then
s_{+}(A)
must be large. This is true to some extent, as the following lemma shows.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any finite
A\subset\mathbb{R}

s_{+}(A)\gg\frac{|A|^{4/3}}{(E_{+}(A))^{1/3}}.

The proof of Lemma
3.1
uses a simple probabilistic argument, and is implicit in the work of Alon and Erdős
[
1
]
. The multiplicative analogue of Lemma
3.1
(with
s_{*}(A)
and
E_{*}(A)
in place of
s_{+}(A)
and
E_{+}(A)
respectively) holds via the same reasoning. This lemma overtakes the bound (
1.1
) when
E_{+}(A)\ll|A|^{5/2}
. Combining these two results together, we have

s_{+}(A)\gg\max\left\{\frac{|A|^{4/3}}{(E_{+}(A))^{1/3}},|A|^{1/2}\right\}.

(3.1)

The purpose of this section is to make the observation that the combined bound (
3.1
) is in fact optimal. This is a consequence of the following result of Kohayakawa, Lee and Rödl
[
5
]
(we only state the parts of the result which are relevant to our analysis).

Theorem 3.2

(
[
5
]
, Theorem 1.1)
.

Let
1/3\leq a\leq 1
be a fixed constant and
m=n^{a}(1+o(1))
. Let
B\subseteq[n]
be a random set of size
m
(i.e.
B
is chosen randomly from all sets of size
m
). Then, almost surely,

(1)

if
1/3\leq a\leq 2/3
then
s_{+}(B)=n^{1/3+o(1)}
,

(2)

if
2/3\leq a\leq 1
then
s_{+}(B)=|B|^{1/2+o(1)}
.

The
o(1)
terms in the exponents suppress logarithmic factors. A more precise version of this statement is given in
[
5
, Theorems 2.2-2.4]
.

For a random set
B\subset[n]
of size
m
, the expected size of
E_{+}(B)
is

cn^{3}\frac{\binom{n-4}{m-4}}{\binom{n}{m}}=c^{\prime}\frac{m^{4}}{n}.

(3.2)

Part (2) of Theorem
3.2
implies that, for a random set
B\subset[n]
of size
n^{2/3+o(1)}\leq|B|\leq n
, the estimate
s_{+}(B)\gg|B|^{1/2}
contained in (
3.1
) is tight, up to the
o(1)
factor. By (
3.2
) these sets will typically have far from maximal additive energy, and taking
m
close to
n^{2/3}
yields a set
B
with
E_{+}(B)\approx|B|^{5/2}
and
s_{+}(B)
very small.

Furthermore we have, with high probability,

\frac{|B|^{4/3}}{E_{+}(B)^{1/3}}\approx n^{1/3},

and thus taking
B
to be a random subset of
[n]
with size
n^{a}
,
1/3\leq a\leq 2/3
, the estimate from Lemma
3.1
matches the information
s_{+}(B)=n^{1/3+o(1)}
given by part (1) of Theorem
3.2
, up to the
o(1)
factor.

3.2.
A variant of the Klurman-Pohoata Conjecture

Conjecture
1.1
could be viewed as a possible line of attack for the sum-product problem, since the inequalities

|A+A|\gg s_{+}(A)^{2},\,\,\,\,\,\,|AA|\gg s_{*}(A)^{2}

imply that a positive answer to Conjecture
1.3
would give a non-trivial sum-product bound. Unfortunately, the construction in Theorem
1.2
shows that even the best possible result in this direction would not yield quantitative improvements to known sum-product inequalities.

However, it is not necessary that a set be additively/multiplicatively Sidon in order for it to determine many sums/products. A weaker assumption that the additive or multiplicative energy is small would suffice, in light of the usual Cauchy-Schwarz energy bounds

E_{+}(A)\geq\frac{|A|^{4}}{|A+A|},\,\,\,\,\,\,\,E_{*}(A)\geq\frac{|A|^{4}}{|AA|}.

With this in mind, we propose a variant of Conjecture
1.1
. For a set
A\subset\mathbb{R}
, define
2
2

2

The multiplicative constant
2
in the definition of
t_{+}(A)
is not particularly important, but is chosen so that at least half of the contributions to
E_{+}(A^{\prime})
come from the
|A^{\prime}|^{2}
trivial solutions.

t_{+}(A)
to be the size of the largest subset
A^{\prime}\subset A
such that

E_{+}(A^{\prime})<2|A^{\prime}|^{2}.

Similarly,
t_{*}(A)
denotes the size of the largest
A^{\prime}\subset A
such that
E_{*}(A^{\prime})<2|A^{\prime}|^{2}
.

Question 3.3
.

For what value of
\kappa>0
is it true that any finite set
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}
satisfies

\max\{t_{+}(A),t_{*}(A)\}\geq|A|^{\kappa}.

The bound

\max\{t_{+}(A),t_{*}(A)\}\gg|A|^{1/2}

(3.3)

follows immediately from the result of Komlos, Sulyok and Szemerédi
[
6
]
, and can also be proved by much simpler means. For this question, an improvement of (
3.3
) follows from the Balog-Wooley Theorem. We will use the following result of Rudnev, Shkredov and Stevens
[
10
]
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
A\subset\mathbb{R}
. Then there exists
A^{\prime}\subset A
with
|A^{\prime}|\geq|A|/2
and

\min\{E_{+}(A^{\prime}),E^{*}(A^{\prime})\}\ll|A|^{11/4}.

We prove the following.

Theorem 3.5
.

For any
A\subset\mathbb{R}
,

\max\{t_{+}(A),t_{*}(A)\}\gg|A|^{5/8}.

Proof.

Apply Theorem
3.4
to obtain a subset
A^{\prime}
and suppose that
E_{+}(A^{\prime})\leq C|A|^{11/4}
. Let
A^{\prime\prime}
be a
p
-random subset of
A^{\prime}
, with
p=\frac{1}{100C^{1/2}|A|^{3/8}}
. The expected size of
A^{\prime\prime}
is at least
p|A|/2
. Let
E_{+}^{0}(A^{\prime\prime})
denote the number of non-trivial solutions to

a_{1}+a_{2}=a_{3}+a_{4}\text{ such that }a_{i}\in A^{\prime\prime}.

The expected value of
E_{+}^{0}(A^{\prime\prime})
is at most

Cp^{4}|A|^{11/4}+p^{3}|A|^{2}.

By Markov’s inequality, the probability that

E_{+}^{0}(A^{\prime\prime})\geq\frac{1}{100}p^{2}|A|^{2}

(3.4)

is at most
1/10
. By Chebyshev, the probability that

|A^{\prime\prime}|<\frac{1}{10}p|A|

(3.5)

is at most
1/10
. Therefore, with positive probability both events (
3.4
) and (
3.5
) do not occur. It therefore follows that there exists a set
A^{\prime\prime}\subset A
such that

|A^{\prime\prime}|\gg|A|^{5/8}

and

E_{+}^{0}(A^{\prime\prime})<\frac{1}{100}p^{2}|A|^{2}\leq|A^{\prime\prime}|^{2}.

In particular,
E_{+}(A^{\prime\prime})<2|A^{\prime\prime}|^{2}
, and it follows that

t_{+}(A)\gg|A|^{5/8}.

If instead we have
E^{*}(A^{\prime})\leq C|A|^{11/4}
at the outset, we can run the same argument in the multiplicative setting and conclude that

t_{*}(A)\gg|A|^{5/8}.

∎

Note that a small improvement to this result can be obtained by instead applying a quantitative improvement to Theorem
3.4
, due to Shakan
[
11
]
, but we have avoided this above in order to simplify the presentation. It can be calculated that this change to the proof results in the improved bound

\max\{t_{+}(A),t_{*}(A)\}\gg|A|^{33/52-o(1)}.

Note that
33/52=5/8+1/104
.

The construction in the proof of Theorem
1.2
does not give a non-trivial construction for Question
3.3
. However, the original construction of Balog and Wooley
3.6
does yield the following.

Theorem 3.6
.

There exists a finite set
A\subset\mathbb{R}
such that

\max\{t_{+}(A),t_{*}(A)\}\ll|A|^{5/6}

Proof.

Define

A:=\{(2i-1)2^{j}:i\in[N^{2}],j\in[N]\}.

This set is a union of
N
arithmetic progressions of length
N^{2}
, and since any solution to
(2i-1)2^{j}=(2i^{\prime}-1)2^{j^{\prime}}
implies that
i=i^{\prime}
and
j=j^{\prime}
, we have that
|A|=N^{3}
. Suppose that
|A^{\prime}|\geq C|A|^{5/6}=CN^{5/2}
for a sufficiently large constant
C
. Note that since

AA\subseteq\{(2i-1)2^{j}:i\in[2N^{4}],j\in[2N]\}

we have
|A^{\prime}A^{\prime}|\leq|AA|\leq 4N^{5}
. Applying the Cauchy Schwarz energy bound then yields

E_{*}(A^{\prime})\geq\frac{|A^{\prime}|^{4}}{|A^{\prime}A^{\prime}|}\geq\frac{C^{2}}{4}|A^{\prime}|^{2}.

Therefore, as long as
C
is sufficiently large we must have
t_{*}(A)\leq C|A|^{5/6}
.

To show that
t_{+}(A)\ll|A|^{5/6}
, we define

A_{j}=\{(2i-1)2^{j}:i\in[N^{2}]\}

noting that we have
A=\bigcup_{j\in[N]}A_{j}
, and that each
A_{j}
is an arithmetic progression with
|A_{j}|=N^{2}
. Let us define

I:=\left\{j\in[N]:|A^{\prime}\cap A_{j}|\geq 2|A^{\prime}|^{1/3}N^{2/3}\right\}.

We then have

\displaystyle|A^{\prime}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in[N]}|A^{\prime}\cap A_{j}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in I}|A^{\prime}\cap A_{j}|+\sum_{j\notin I}|A^{\prime}\cap A_{j}|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\in I}|A^{\prime}\cap A_{j}|+2|A^{\prime}|^{1/3}N^{5/3}.

We now bound the energy of
A^{\prime}
.

\displaystyle E(A^{\prime})

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{j\in I}E(A^{\prime}\cap A_{j})

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{j\in I}\frac{|A^{\prime}\cap A_{j}|^{4}}{|A_{j}+A_{j}|}

\displaystyle\gg\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{j\in I}|A^{\prime}\cap A_{j}|^{4}

\displaystyle\geq 8|A^{\prime}|\sum_{j\in I}|A^{\prime}\cap A_{j}|

\displaystyle\geq 8|A^{\prime}|^{2}-16|A^{\prime}|^{4/3}N^{5/3}.

From the final inequality it follows that
E(A^{\prime})\geq 2|A^{\prime}|^{2}
as long as the constant
C
is sufficiently large. Therefore
t_{+}(A)\ll|A|^{5/6}
, proving the result.
∎

Note that the bound
|AA|\leq N^{5}
above is slightly wasteful and can be improved by a logarithmic factor. Using this fact and slightly rebalancing the construction of
A
above gives the small improvement

\max\{t_{+}(A),t_{*}(A)\}\ll\frac{|A|^{5/6}}{\log^{c}|A|}

for some constant
c>0
.

3.3.
Connection to Balog-Wooley decomposition

Conjecture (
1.3
) is also connected to a question of Balog and Wooley
[
2
]
: For which
\kappa>0
is it true that any finite set
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}
can always be partitioned into two subsets
B
and
C
, such that

\max\{E_{+}(B),E_{*}(C)\}\ll|A|^{3-\kappa}.

(3.6)

A construction is given in
[
2
]
(and which was repeated here in the proof of Theorem
3.6
) proving that any such result must have
\kappa<2/3
. Although the authors do not go as far as to conjecture that an exponent of
7/3
is attainable in (
3.6
), a similar conjecture is stated in
[
10
]
, where the authors conjecture that for all finite
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}
, there exists a subset
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
with
|A^{\prime}|\geq\frac{|A|}{2}
such that

\min\{E_{+}(A^{\prime}),E_{*}(A^{\prime})\}\ll|A|^{7/3+o(1)}.

(3.7)

A positive answer to this conjecture would imply a positive answer to Conjecture
1.1
. Indeed, utilising Lemma
3.1
(or possibly its multiplicative analogue), the subset
A^{\prime}
satisfies

|A|^{5/9-o(1)}\ll|A^{\prime}|^{5/9-o(1)}\ll\max\{s_{+}(A^{\prime}),s_{*}(A^{\prime})\}\leq\max\{s_{+}(A),s_{*}(A)\},

thus giving Conjecture
1.1
with
c=1/18-o(1)
. Weaker variants of conjecture (
3.7
) also imply Conjecture
1.1
, as long as the exponent is at most
5/2-\epsilon
. Stating this in the contrapositive, any construction disproving Conjecture
1.1
also disproves strong Balog Wooley type conjectures.

4.
Further remarks

4.1.
Small sum set implies large
s_{*}(A)
?

Another conjecture of Klurman and Pohoata was the following: for all
\epsilon>0
, and any set
A\subset\mathbb{R}

|A+A|\leq K|A|\Rightarrow s_{*}(A)\gg_{K,\epsilon}|A|^{1-\epsilon}.

(4.1)

By combining the multiplicative analogue of Lemma
3.1
with Solymosi’s
[
13
]
bound on the multiplicative energy via sumsets, they proved that

|A+A|\leq K|A|\Rightarrow s_{*}(A)\gg\frac{|A|^{2/3}}{K^{2/3}(\log|A|)^{1/3}}.

(4.2)

The construction given in the proof of Theorem
1.2
disproves the conjecture (
4.1
), at least in the range when
K=\log^{c}|A|
. Indeed, we recorded in
(
2.1
)
that the construction satisfies
|A+A|\ll|A|\log^{2}|A|
. Moreover, since we have
s_{*}(A)\ll|A|^{2/3}
, this construction also shows that the bound (
4.2
) is in fact optimal in this range, up to logarithmic factors.

4.2.
Other constructions

Theorem
1.2
was obtained independently by Green and Peluse (private communication). We thank them for sharing their construction with us. The construction is similar to the set
A
defined in the proof of Theorem
1.2
. Paraphrasing slightly, they define a set

B=\{p_{1}p_{2}p_{3}:1\leq p_{i}\leq N,\text{ $p_{i}$ prime}\}.

The set
B
is a dense enough in
[N^{3}]
to ensure that its sum set is small and thus
s_{+}(B)
is small. On the other hand, and similarly to our proof of Theorem
1.2
, an application of the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality can be used to show that any subset of
B
larger than
C|B|^{2/3}
must contain four elements of the form

pqr,p^{\prime}qr,pq^{\prime}r^{\prime},p^{\prime}q^{\prime}r^{\prime},

which gives rise to a non-trivial multiplicative energy solution
(pqr)(p^{\prime}q^{\prime}r^{\prime})=(p^{\prime}qr)(pq^{\prime}r^{\prime})
.

In a forthcoming paper, Shkredov
[
12
]
gives another construction of a set with
\max\{s_{+}(A),s_{*}(A)\}\ll|A|^{2/3}
. His construction is somewhat different in nature, and comes from taking
A
to be a sum set of carefully chosen geometric progressions.

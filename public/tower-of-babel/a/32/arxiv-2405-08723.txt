Abstract
. If G G is a finite group and p p is a prime number, we investigate the relationship between the p p -modular decomposition numbers of characters of height zero in the principal p p -block of G G and the p p -local structure of G G . In particular we prove that, under certain conditions on the non-abelian composition factors of G G , d χ ​ 1 G ≠ 0 d_{\chi 1_{G}}\neq 0 for all irreducible characters χ \chi of degree prime to p p in the principal p p -block of G G if, and only if, the normaliser of a Sylow p p -subgroup of G G has a normal p p -complement.

1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a finite group,
p
a prime number,
{\operatorname{Irr}}(G)
the set of irreducible
ordinary characters of
G
and
{\operatorname{IBr}}(G)
the set of irreducible
p
-Brauer
characters of
G
. Then the restriction
\chi^{\circ}
of any
\chi\in{\operatorname{Irr}}(G)
to
the set of
p
-regular elements of
G
can be written as

\chi^{\circ}=\sum_{\varphi\in{\operatorname{IBr}}(G)}d_{\chi\varphi}\varphi,

where the
d_{\chi\varphi}
are uniquely determined non-negative integers. These are
called the
p
-modular
decomposition numbers of
G
, and a great deal of
literature is devoted to understand them.

In
[
NT
,
NT2
]
Navarro and Tiep initiated the investigation on relations
between
p
-decomposition numbers and properties of Sylow
p
-normalisers,
considering two different settings. In
[
NT
]
they conjectured that if
p>3

then
d_{\chi 1_{G}}\neq 0
for all
\chi\in{\operatorname{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(G)
, that is, for all
irreducible characters of
G
of degree prime to
p
, if and only if
G
has
self-normalising Sylow
p
-subgroups, and that this happens if and only if

d_{\chi 1_{G}}=1
for all
\chi\in{\operatorname{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(G)
. Note that irreducible characters
of degree prime to
p
lie in
p
-blocks of maximal defect, so this situation
can only happen when the principal block is the only Brauer
p
-block of
maximal defect of
G
. It is then natural to wonder what can be said when this
is not the case and one restricts attention to the principal
p
-block
B_{0}(G)
.
In this sense, in
[
NT2
]
they conjectured that
d_{\chi 1_{G}}\neq 0
for all

\chi\in{\operatorname{Irr}}(B_{0}(G))
if and only if
G
has a normal
p
-complement.
In this paper we consider the intersection of the two conditions in
[
NT
]

and
[
NT2
]
, namely what happens if
d_{\chi 1_{G}}=1
just for all
\chi\in{\operatorname{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(G))
. Our main result is:

Theorem A
.

Let
G
be a finite group and
p>3
be a prime. Assume that all non-abelian
simple composition factors of
G
of order divisible by
p
satisfy
Property
(*)
below. The following are equivalent:

(i)

For every
\chi\in{\operatorname{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(G))
we have
d_{\chi 1_{G}}\neq 0
.

(ii)

For every
\chi\in{\operatorname{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(G))
we have
d_{\chi 1_{G}}=1
.

(iii)

For
P\in{\operatorname{Syl}}_{p}(G)
we have
{\bf N}_{G}(P)=P\times K
for some
K\leq G
.

Moreover, if
G
is
p
-solvable, this equivalence holds for every prime
p
.

Notice that, as pointed out in
[
NT
]
, all irreducible characters of odd
degree of the alternating group
\mathfrak{A}_{5}
contain the trivial character in their
2-modular reduction, and similarly, all irreducible characters of the Ree group

{}^{2}G_{2}(27)
of non-zero 3-defect contain the trivial character in their
3-modular reduction, while the respective Sylow
p
-normalisers have no normal

p
-complement, so the equivalence in Theorem A fails for non-solvable groups
with
p\leq 3
.

Theorem A involves the following property that a finite non-abelian simple group
might, or might not, satisfy:

Property
(*)
.

Let
S
be non-abelian simple and
p>3
a prime dividing
|S|
. Then for
all almost simple groups
H
with socle
S
and
|H:S|
a
p
-power, there
exists
\chi\in{\operatorname{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(H))
such that
d_{\chi 1_{H}}=0
.

We prove Property
(*)
does hold for many simple groups: for sporadic groups,
alternating groups, and simple groups of Lie type in characteristic different
from
p
. We also show it for some groups of Lie type in characteristic
p
.
(The general case of groups of Lie type in their defining characteristic was
also left open in
[
NT
]
and
[
NT2
]
.)
As the knowledge on
p
-decomposition numbers for groups of Lie type in their
own characteristic is too weak at present we refrain from making a general
conjecture and just leave it as a question as to whether Property
(*)
holds
for all non-abelian finite simple groups.

Structure of the paper.

In Section
2
we prove Property
(*)
for
S
a sporadic
simple group, an alternating group, a simple group of Lie type in characteristic
different from
p
as well as for some groups of Lie type in characteristic
p
.
In Section
3
we show that Theorem A holds for
p
-solvable
groups and in Theorem
3.4
we reduce the general case to the
validity of Property (
*
) on composition factors, thus completing the proof
of Theorem A.

2.
Almost simple groups

In this section, we discuss instances of Property
(*)
from the introduction.

2.1.
Alternating and sporadic groups

We start out with simple groups not of Lie type.

Proposition 2.1
.

Property
(*)
holds for
S
a sporadic simple group or the Tits group.

Proof.

Let
S
be as in the assumption and
H\geq S
as in Property
(*)
. Since
p>2

this means
H=S
. If
S
has abelian
Sylow
p
-subgroups, by
[
NT2
, Prop. 3.2]
there exists
\chi\in{\operatorname{Irr}}(B_{0}(H))

such that
d_{\chi 1_{H}}=0
. By one direction of Brauer’s height zero conjecture

[
KM13
]
, we have
\chi\in{\operatorname{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(H))
as required. Else, by
inspection in
[
GAP
]
,
S
has just one
p
-block of maximal defect and then
by
[
NT
, Lemma 3.2]
, there is
\chi\in{\operatorname{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(H)={\operatorname{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(H))
with

d_{\chi 1_{H}}=0
.
∎

Proposition 2.2
.

Property
(*)
holds for
S
an alternating group.

Proof.

Let
S=\mathfrak{A}_{n}
with
n\geq 5
. As
p>2
again we have
H=S
. Set
G:=\mathfrak{S}_{n}
.
Recall that the irreducible characters of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
are naturally labelled by
partitions of
n
. If
p
divides
n
, then let

\chi=\chi^{(n-2,1^{2})}\in{\operatorname{Irr}}(B_{0}(G))
, as in
[
NT2
, Prop. 3.1]
.
Then
\chi^{\circ}
is the sum of two irreducible Brauer characters of degrees

n-2
and
(n-2)(n-3)/2
and hence it is of degree prime to
p
and does not
contain any irreducible Brauer character of degree 1. Now, any
\theta\in{\operatorname{Irr}}(S)

under
\chi
lies in
{\operatorname{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(S))
and
d_{\theta 1_{S}}=0
, as wanted.

So we may assume that
p
does not divide
n
. Let
n=a_{k}p^{k}+\cdots+a_{1}p+a_{0}

be the
p
-adic expansion of
n
. Since
p
does not divide
n
, we have

a_{0}>0
. Suppose first that
a_{0}>1
. Consider
\chi=\chi^{(a_{0},1^{n-a_{0}})}
.
By Peel’s theorem (see
[
Ja78
, Thm 24.1]
),
\chi^{\circ}\in{\operatorname{IBr}}(G)
. Hence,
it is enough to show that
\chi(1)\neq 1
,
\chi(1)
is
p^{\prime}
, and

\chi\in{\operatorname{Irr}}(B_{0}(G))
. By the hook length formula,
\chi
has degree

\chi(1)=\frac{n!}{n\cdot(a_{0}-1)!(n-a_{0})!}=\binom{n-1}{n-a_{0}}.

Then
\chi(1)\neq 1
since
1<a_{0}<n
. Moreover

n-1=a_{k}p^{k}+\cdots+a_{1}p+(a_{0}-1)

and

n-a_{0}=a_{k}p^{k}+\cdots+a_{1}p,

so by Lucas’ theorem we have

\chi(1)=\binom{n-1}{n-a_{0}}\equiv\prod_{i=1}^{k}\binom{a_{i}}{a_{i}}\binom{a_{0}-1}{0}\equiv 1\pmod{p}.

Thus,
\chi(1)
is not divisible by
p
. Finally since the
p
-core of

\lambda=(a_{0},1^{n-a_{0}})
is
(a_{0})
, we have that
\chi
lies in the principal

p
-block by the Nakayama conjecture, as desired. Now take
\theta\in{\operatorname{Irr}}(S)

under
\chi
, so
\theta\in{\operatorname{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(S))
and
d_{\theta 1_{S}}=0
.

Finally, suppose that
a_{0}=1
, so
p
divides
n-1
. In this case consider

\chi=\chi^{(n-3,2,1)}
, so
\chi
lies in
B_{0}(G)
. This is the character in
the proof of
[
NT
, Lemma 3.1(ii)]
, so we are done in this case as well.
∎

2.2.
Groups of Lie type in non-defining characteristic

For groups of Lie type in cross characteristic we consider the following set-up.
Let
{\mathbf{G}}
be a simple linear algebraic group of adjoint type over an
algebraically closed field of characteristic
r
and
F:{\mathbf{G}}\to{\mathbf{G}}
a Steinberg
map, with group of fixed points
G:={\mathbf{G}}^{F}
. It is well-know that any simple
group of Lie type can be obtained as
S=[G,G]
for
{\mathbf{G}},F
chosen suitably.
Moreover, if
{\mathbf{G}}_{\operatorname{sc}}
denotes a simply connected covering of
{\mathbf{G}}
, with
corresponding Steinberg map also denoted
F
, then

S\cong{\mathbf{G}}_{\operatorname{sc}}^{F}/{\mathbf{Z}}({\mathbf{G}}_{\operatorname{sc}}^{F})
, if
S
is not the Tits simple group, which was
already discussed in Proposition
2.1
. Let
({\mathbf{G}}^{*},F)
be dual to

({\mathbf{G}},F)
and
G^{*}:={\mathbf{G}}^{*F}
.

We let
{\mathbf{B}}\leq{\mathbf{G}}
denote an
F
-stable Borel subgroup of
{\mathbf{G}}
, and set

B:={\mathbf{B}}^{F}
.

We recall that automorphisms of prime order
p\geq 5
of simple groups of Lie
type are either field automorphisms, or diagonal automorphisms for groups
of types
{\operatorname{PSL}}_{n}(\epsilon q)
, with
\epsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Property
(*)
holds for
S
as above if
|B|
is prime to
p
.

Proof.

By assumption
p
divides
|S|
, hence also
|G|=|G^{*}|
. Let
1\neq s\in G^{*}
be
a (semisimple)
p
-element in the centre of a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G^{*}
, and
let
\chi\in{\operatorname{Irr}}(G)
be the semisimple character in the Lusztig series

\mathcal{E}(G,s)
, unique since
{\mathbf{G}}
has connected centre, see

[
GM20
, Def. 2.6.9]
. By the degree formula for Jordan
decomposition
[
GM20
, Cor. 2.6.6]
,
\chi(1)
is then prime to
p
, and also

\chi(1)>1
as
p
does not divide
|{\mathbf{Z}}(G^{*})|
and so
C_{G^{*}}(s)<G^{*}
.
Furthermore, by
[
Hi88
, Cor. 3.4]
, the semisimple character
\chi
lies in
the same
p
-block of
G
as the semisimple character in
\mathcal{E}(G,1)
, i.e., the
trivial character, so in the principal
p
-block. Since
p
does not
divide
|B|
, the permutation module
1_{B}^{G}
is projective, and thus
contains the projective cover of the trivial module. But all constituents
of
1_{B}^{G}
are unipotent, so lie in
\mathcal{E}(G,1)
(see
[
GM20
, Exmp. 3.2.6]
).
Hence, by Brauer reciprocity,
1_{G}^{\circ}
does not occur as a constituent
of
\chi^{\circ}
. Taking for
\theta
any character of
S
below
\chi
we see
that
\theta\in{\operatorname{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(S))
and
d_{\theta 1_{S}}=0
.

Next observe that the order of any outer diagonal automorphism of
S
divides
the order of
B
. Thus,
H
is an extension of
S
by a
p
-power order field
automorphism
\gamma
. Note that any such automorphism of
S
extends to
G

and then also induces a dual automorphism on
G^{*}
, which we denote
\gamma^{*}

(see e.g.
[
Tay
, 5.6]
). Now choose
s
more precisely to be also

\gamma^{*}
-invariant (which is possible as
\gamma^{*}
is a
p
-element, so necessarily
has non-trivial fixed points on the centre of a
\gamma^{*}
-stable Sylow

p
-subgroup of
G^{*}
). Then
\chi

is also
\gamma
-invariant by
[
Tay
, Prop. 7.2]
. Since the index
|G:S|
is a
divisor of
|B|
, hence prime to
p
, there then exists a
\gamma
-invariant

\theta\in{\operatorname{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(S))
below
\chi
, with
d_{\theta 1_{S}}=0
. Let

\tilde{\theta}
be an extension of
\theta
to
H=S\langle\gamma\rangle
in

B_{0}(H)
. Then
\tilde{\theta}
is as desired.
∎

Proposition 2.4
.

Property
(*)
holds for
S
as above if
F
is a Frobenius
map with respect to an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-structure and
p
divides
q-1
.

Proof.

Let
W
be the Weyl group of
G
, that is, the
F
-fixed points of the Weyl
group of
{\mathbf{G}}
. As
p
divides
q-1
, the
p
-decomposition matrix of the group
algebra of
W
embeds into the
p
-modular decomposition matrix of
G
(see

[
Di90
, 4.10]
). Let
\epsilon\in{\operatorname{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(W)
be the (linear) sign character.
Then
\epsilon^{\circ}\neq 1_{W}^{\circ}
since
p>2
, whence
d_{\epsilon 1_{W}}=0
. Now
\epsilon

corresponds to the Steinberg character
{\operatorname{St}}
of
G
.
Then
d_{{\operatorname{St}}1_{G}}=d_{\epsilon 1_{W}}=0
,
{\operatorname{St}}
lies in the principal
p
-block of
G

(e.g. by
[
En00
, Thm A]
) and its degree is a power of the
defining characteristic, so prime to
p
. Now note that any
p
-automorphism
of
S
is realised inside the extension of
G
(which induces all diagonal
automorphisms) by a generator
\gamma
of the cyclic group of
p
-power order field
automorphisms, so we may assume
H\leq\tilde{G}:=G\langle\gamma\rangle
. By

[
GM20
, Thm 4.5.11]
,
{\operatorname{St}}
is invariant under
\gamma
. Let
\tilde{\operatorname{St}}
be an
extension of
{\operatorname{St}}
to
\tilde{G}
in
B_{0}(\tilde{G})
, so

d_{\tilde{\operatorname{St}}1_{\tilde{G}}}=0
. Then
\tilde{\operatorname{St}}|_{H}
is irreducible, since
{\operatorname{St}}

restricts irreducibly to
S
, hence lies in
B_{0}(H)
and so is as required.
∎

Theorem 2.5
.

Property
(*)
holds for
S
of Lie type when
p
is not the
defining characteristic.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.3
we may assume that
p
divides the order
of a Borel subgroup
B
of
G
. If
F
is a Frobenius map with respect to an

{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-structure and
p
divides
q-1
, we are done by
Proposition
2.4
. If
G
is a Suzuki or Ree group and
p
divides

|B|
, then
p|(q^{2}-1)
where
F^{2}
defines an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
-structure, and the
exactly same arguments as in the proof of Proposition
2.4
apply.

So we are reduced
to the case that
F
is a Frobenius map with respect to an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-structure
and
p
divides
|B|
but not
q-1
. Since
p
is not the defining prime, this
implies that
G
is a twisted group of Lie type
{}^{2}\!A_{n-1}
,
{}^{2}\!D_{n}
,
or
{}^{2}\!E_{6}
and
p
divides
q+1
, respectively of type
{}^{3}\!D_{4}
and
p

divides
q^{2}+q+1
. Let

in the respective cases. Then the centraliser of

-torus of

is a maximal torus, so has a unique

-cuspidal
unipotent character. Thus, by
[
En00
, Thm A]
there is a unique unipotent

G
of maximal defect, the principal block, which hence contains the

{\operatorname{St}}
of
G
. By
[
Hi90
, Thm B]
we have
d_{{\operatorname{St}}1_{G}}=0

in our case unless
G={\operatorname{PGU}}_{3}(q)
. Except for that latter case, we can now argue
as in the proof of Proposition
2.4
to conclude. For
G={\operatorname{PGU}}_{3}(q)

let
\chi
be the cuspidal unipotent character of degree
q(q-1)
, prime to
p
.
By
[
Ge90
, Thm 4.3(a)]
it lies in
B_{0}(G)
and satisfies

.
Again,
\chi
restricts irreducibly to

and is invariant under all

∎

2.3.
Groups of Lie type in defining characteristic

We do not see how to approach Property
(*)
for groups of Lie type in their
defining characteristic in general. All characters of positive defect lie in the
principal block and decomposition numbers tend to be large and little is known.
The following was shown by Navarro and Tiep:

Proposition 2.6
.

Property
(*)
holds if
S={\operatorname{PSp}}_{2n}(p^{f})
,
n\geq 1
, with either
p>3
or

p=3
and
n
even.

Proof.

In this case the principal block of
S
is the only
p
-block of positive
defect. Let
H
be almost simple with
H/S
a
p
-group. By

[
Na98
, Cor. 9.6]
there is just one
p
-block of
H
covering
B_{0}(S)
,
necessarily the principal block
B_{0}(H)
. Now the irreducible character

\chi\in{\operatorname{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(H)
with
d_{\chi 1_{H}}=0
constructed in
[
NT
, Prop. 3.11]

lies above a character of
S
of positive defect, hence in the principal

p
-block of
H
and we are done.
∎

Observe that this does not extend to
p=3
and
n
odd: The group

H={\operatorname{PSp}}_{2}(3^{3}).3
has no irreducible character
\chi\in{\operatorname{Irr}}_{3^{\prime}}(H)
with
d_{\chi 1_{H}}=0
.

The paper
[
NT2
]
also contains results for special linear and unitary
groups but these are not applicable here as the considered characters are not
of
p^{\prime}
-degree. Nevertheless, we can follow their general approach.

For
G={\operatorname{SL}}_{n}(q)
we let
\tau_{j}
,
j=1,\ldots,q-2
denote the non-unipotent

Weil characters
of degree
(q^{n}-1)/(q-1)
, ordered such that
\tau_{j}
is
trivial on the centre
Z({\operatorname{SL}}_{n}(q))

of order
z:=\gcd(n,q-1)
if and only if
z|j
.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
S={\operatorname{PSL}}_{n}(q)
with
q=p^{f}
,
p\neq 2
and
n\geq 3
.

(a)

If either
\gcd(p-1,(q-1)/\gcd(n,q-1))>1
or
2^{f}<(q-1)/z-1
then there
is
\chi\in{\operatorname{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(S))
such that
d_{\chi 1_{S}}=0
.

(b)

Write
f=p^{a}f^{\prime}
with
\gcd(p,f^{\prime})=1
and set
q^{\prime}:=p^{f^{\prime}}
. If
a=0
or

2^{f}<(q^{\prime}-1)/(q^{\prime}-1,n)-1
then Property
(*)
holds for
S={\operatorname{PSL}}_{n}(q)
.

Proof.

Let
G:={\operatorname{SL}}_{n}(q)
and set
z=\gcd(n,q-1)
. We are interested in the characters

\tau_{j}
of
G
that are trivial on
Z(G)
, that is, for which
j=zj^{\prime}
for some
integer
1\leq j^{\prime}\leq(q-1-z)/z
. By
[
ZS
, Thm 1.11]
the decomposition number

d_{\tau_{j}1_{G}}
equals the number of solutions
x_{s}\in\{0,1\}
of the congruence

n(p-1)\sum_{s=0}^{f-1}x_{s}p^{s}\equiv j\pmod{(q-1)}.

Dividing by
z
, we need to count solutions to

n/z(p-1)\sum_{s=0}^{f-1}x_{s}p^{s}\equiv j^{\prime}\pmod{(q-1)/z}.

If there is a prime
\ell
dividing
p-1
and
(q-1)/z
, then reducing
modulo
\ell
we see there is no solution for
j^{\prime}=1
. Also, the left hand side
can take at most
2^{f}
distinct values. Since there are
(q-1)/z-1
admissible
values for
j^{\prime}
, there is
j^{\prime}
with no solutions whenever
2^{f}<(q-1)/z-1
.
Thus, under either of our assumptions we find
j^{\prime}
with

\tau_{j}=\tau_{zj^{\prime}}\in{\operatorname{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(G)
with
d_{\tau_{j}1_{G}}=0
. Since
G
has a
single
p
-block of positive defect,
\tau_{j}
lies in the principal block.
Furthermore, by construction
Z({\operatorname{SL}}_{n}(q))
lies in the kernel of
\tau_{j}
and
hence
\tau_{j}
deflates to a character of
S={\operatorname{PSL}}_{n}(q)
. Thus we get (a).

(b) Since
p>2
does not divide
q-1
, the
p
-power order automorphisms of
S

are field automorphisms, of order dividing
p^{a}
where
f=p^{a}f^{\prime}
is as in the
statement. If
a=0
Property
(*)
follows from (a) since necessarily
H=S
.
For
a>0
let
\gamma
be a field automorphism of
G
(and hence of
S
) of order

p^{a}
. There are exactly
q^{\prime}-2
Weil characters of
G
invariant under
\gamma
,
which hence extend to
G\langle\gamma\rangle
. Of these,
(q^{\prime}-1)/\gcd(q^{\prime}-1,n)-1

are trivial on
Z(G)
, so define characters in
{\operatorname{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(S)
invariant
under
\gamma
. By the argument above, if this number is bigger than
2^{f}
then

For

Proposition 2.9
.

Let

with

,

and

(a)

If

.

(b)

The argument is very similar to the one for the special linear groups. Let

and set

. Again, we consider characters

trivial on

equals the

in the proof of Proposition

3.
The reduction

In this section we prove Theorem A. We will need the following results, which we collect here for the reader’s convenience.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
N\triangleleft G
and let
\theta\in{\operatorname{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(N))
. Suppose that

\theta
extends to
PN
, where
P\in{\operatorname{Syl}}_{p}(G)
. Then there exists

\chi\in{\operatorname{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(G))
satisfying
[\theta^{G},\chi]\neq 0
.

Proof.

This is
[
Mur
, Lemma 4.3]
.
∎

The following argument is inside the proof of
[
NT
, Thm 2.6]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
G
be a finite group and suppose that
d_{\chi 1_{G}}\neq 0
for every

\chi\in{\operatorname{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(G))
. Let
M\triangleleft G
and let
P\in{\operatorname{Syl}}_{p}(G)
.
Then
d_{\psi 1_{MP}}\neq 0
for every
\psi\in{\operatorname{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(MP))
.

Proof.

Since
MP/M
is a
p
-group, we have that
\psi_{M}=\tau\in{\operatorname{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(M))
.
By Lemma
3.1
there exists
\chi\in{\operatorname{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(G))
lying
over
\tau
. By hypothesis,
d_{\chi 1_{G}}\neq 0
, and then
\chi^{\circ}_{M}

contains
1_{M}
, so
d_{\tau 1_{M}}\neq 0
. Since
MP/M
is a
p
-group, we have
that
(MP)^{\circ}=M^{\circ}
and then
d_{\psi 1_{MP}}\neq 0
, as wanted.
∎

We next prove Theorem A for
p
-solvable groups.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
G
be a
p
-solvable group. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

For every
\chi\in{\operatorname{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(G))
we have
d_{\chi 1_{G}}\neq 0
.

(ii)

For every
\chi\in{\operatorname{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(G))
we have
d_{\chi 1_{G}}=1
.

(iii)

For
P\in{\operatorname{Syl}}_{p}(G)
we have
{\bf N}_{G}(P)=P\times K
for some
K\leq G
.

Proof.

We first prove (iii) implies (ii). By
[
NTV
, Thm 3.2]
we have

K\subseteq{\mathbf{O}}_{p^{\prime}}(G)=:X
. Now, let
\overline{G}=G/X
and
\overline{P}=PX/X
,
then (iii) implies that
{\bf N}_{\overline{G}}(\overline{P})\cong\overline{P}
.
Since
{\operatorname{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(G))={\operatorname{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(G/X))
we know by
[
NT
, Thm B]

that (i) and (ii) hold.

Since (ii) implies (i) trivially, we just need to show that (i) implies (iii).
We proceed by induction
on
|G|
. Let
N={\mathbf{O}}_{p^{\prime}}(G)
and
use the bar notation. Since
{\operatorname{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(G))={\operatorname{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(\bar{G}))
, if

N>1
, we have by induction that
{\bf N}_{\bar{G}}(\bar{P})=\bar{P}\times\bar{K}
.
By
[
NTV
, Thm 3.2]
we have that
\bar{K}\subseteq{\mathbf{O}}_{p^{\prime}}(\bar{G})=1
so

\bar{K}=1
and hence
{\bf N}_{\bar{G}}(\bar{P})=\bar{P}
. This implies that

{\bf N}_{G}(P)=P\times{\bf C}_{N}(P)
and we are done. So we may assume that
N=1
. But
in this case the principal
p
-block is the only
p
-block of
G
, and we are
done by
[
NT
, Thm B]
.
∎

We finally prove Theorem A.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
G
be a finite group. Assume that
p>3
and all non-abelian composition
factors of
G
of order divisible by
p
satisfy Property
(*)
. Then the
following are equivalent:

(i)

For every
\chi\in{\operatorname{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(G))
we have
d_{\chi 1_{G}}\neq 0
.

(ii)

For every
\chi\in{\operatorname{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(G))
we have
d_{\chi 1_{G}}=1
.

(iii)

For
P\in{\operatorname{Syl}}_{p}(G)
we have
{\bf N}_{G}(P)=P\times K
for some
K\leq G
.

Proof.

As in Theorem
3.3
, we just need to show that (i) implies (iii).
We work to prove this implication in a series of steps, proceeding by induction
on
|G|
.

Step 1. We may assume
{\mathbf{O}}_{p^{\prime}}(G)=1
. Indeed, let
N={\mathbf{O}}_{p^{\prime}}(G)
and
use the bar notation. Since
{\operatorname{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(G))={\operatorname{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(\bar{G}))
, if

N>1
, we have by induction that
{\bf N}_{\bar{G}}(\bar{P})=\bar{P}\times\bar{K}
.
By
[
NTV
, Thm 3.2]
we have that
\bar{K}\subseteq{\mathbf{O}}_{p^{\prime}}(\bar{G})=1
so

\bar{K}=1
and hence
{\bf N}_{\bar{G}}(\bar{P})=\bar{P}
. This implies that

{\bf N}_{G}(P)=P\times{\bf C}_{N}(P)
and we are done.

Step 2. If
1<M\lhd G
then
M
is not
p
-solvable. In particular,
if
N
is a minimal normal subgroup of
G
, then
N
is semisimple of order
divisible by
p
.
Let
M\lhd G
be the largest
p
-solvable normal subgroup of
G
. We claim that

M=1
. Let
\bar{G}=G/M
and use the bar notation. Suppose
M>1
.
Since
{\operatorname{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(\bar{G}))\subseteq{\operatorname{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(G))
we have by
induction that
{\bf N}_{\bar{G}}(\bar{P})=\bar{P}\times\bar{K}
.
By
[
NT
, Thm 3.2]
this implies that
\bar{K}\subseteq{\mathbf{O}}_{p^{\prime}}(\bar{G})
.
Since
M
is the largest normal
p
-solvable subgroup of
G
,

{\mathbf{O}}_{p^{\prime}}(\bar{G})
is trivial and hence
{\bf N}_{\bar{G}}(\bar{P})=\bar{P}
.
By
[
GMN
, Thm 1.1]
this forces
\bar{G}
to be solvable. Hence
G
is

p
-solvable and we are done by Theorem
3.3
.

From now on, let
N=S_{1}\times\cdots\times S_{t}
be a minimal normal subgroup of
G
, where
S_{i}\cong S
is a nonabelian simple group of order divisible by
p
.

Step 3. Let
X/N={\mathbf{O}}_{p^{\prime}}(G/N)
. Then

G=XP
.

Since
{\operatorname{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(G/N))\subseteq{\operatorname{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(G))
, by induction we have

{\bf N}_{G}(P)N/N={\bf N}_{G/N}(PN/N)=PN/N\times K/N
for some
K\leq G
. Then

K/N\subseteq X/N
by
[
NTV
, Thm 3.2]
. Write
H=XN{\bf N}_{G}(P)=XPNK=XP
.
By Lemma
3.2
applied to
X
in place of
M
, we obtain
that
H
satisfies (i). Since the non-abelian composition factors of
H
are composition factors of
G
, if
H<G
, by induction we have
{\bf N}_{G}(P)={\bf N}_{H}(P)=P\times L
for some
L
and we are done. Hence we may assume that
H=G
.

Step 4. Let
H=\bigcap{\bf N}_{G}(S_{i})
and let
X
be as in Step 3. Then
G=(H\cap X)P
.

Write
Y=(H\cap X)P
. We claim first that
{\mathbf{O}}_{p^{\prime}}(Y)=1
. Indeed,

{\mathbf{O}}_{p^{\prime}}(Y)\cap(H\cap X)\subseteq{\mathbf{O}}_{p^{\prime}}(H\cap X)=1
by Step 1. Now,

{\mathbf{O}}_{p^{\prime}}(Y)\cong(H\cap X){\mathbf{O}}_{p^{\prime}}(Y)/(H\cap X)
is a
p
-group, so

{\mathbf{O}}_{p^{\prime}}(Y)=1
as wanted. By Lemma
3.2
applied to

H\cap X
in place of
M
, we have that
Y
satisfies (i). Since the non-abelian composition factors of
Y
are composition factors of
G
, if
Y<G
, by
induction this gives
{\bf N}_{Y}(P)=P\times K
for some
K
. But then

K\subseteq{\mathbf{O}}_{p^{\prime}}(Y)
by
[
NTV
, Thm 3.2]
, so
K=1
. This means that

{\bf N}_{Y}(P)=P
and then by
[
GMN
, Thm 1.1]
the group
Y
is solvable.
But then
N
is solvable, a contradiction. Hence
Y=G
as wanted.

Step 5. Final step.

Let
H=\bigcap{\bf N}_{G}(S_{i})
and
X
be as before. Since
G=(H\cap X)P
and
H
acts trivially on
\{S_{1},\ldots,S_{t}\}
,
P
must act transitively on the set
\{S_{1},\ldots,S_{t}\}
. Write
S=S_{1}
and, for
i=2,\ldots,t
, write
S_{i}=S^{x_{i}}
with
x_{i}\in P
. We proceed now as in the proof of
[
NT
, Thm 2.6]
.
Let
R={\bf N}_{P}(S)
. If
SR=G
, then
S=N
and
{\bf C}_{G}(S)
is a normal
p
-subgroup of
G
. By Step 2,
{\bf C}_{G}(S)=1
and hence
G
is almost simple with socle
S
and
|G:S|
a power of
p
. Since
S
satisfies Property
(*)
by
assumption, we have a contradiction. Hence we may assume that
SR<G
.

Let
Q=P\cap N
and let
R_{1}=R\cap S=Q\cap S=P\cap S\in{\operatorname{Syl}}_{p}(S)
. Let
\gamma\in{\operatorname{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(SR))
and notice that
\gamma_{S}=\psi\in{\operatorname{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(S))
since

is a
p
-group. For

, let
\psi_{i}=\psi^{x_{i}}\in{\operatorname{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(S_{i}))
and let
\eta=\psi\times\psi_{2}\times\cdots\times\psi_{t}\in{\operatorname{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(N))
, which is

[
NTT
, Lemma 4.1(ii)]
. Then
\eta

. By Lemma
3.1
and hypothesis we have

. By
[
NT
, Lemma 2.3]
we have that
d_{\psi 1_{S}}\neq 0
and then, since
SR/S
is a

-group, we conclude that
d_{\gamma 1_{SR}}\neq 0
. Since

and

is a composition factor of

, this implies
{\bf N}_{SR}(R)=R\times K
for some

. Then

. But
{\mathbf{O}}_{p^{\prime}}(SR)=1
(as before,
{\mathbf{O}}_{p^{\prime}}(SR)\cap S=1
, so
{\mathbf{O}}_{p^{\prime}}(SR)\cong S{\mathbf{O}}_{p^{\prime}}(SR)/S
is a

and then

. Now by
[
GMN
, Thm 1.1]
,

∎

Isomorphisms and properties of TAR reconfiguration graphs for zero forcing and other X X -set parameters
Novi H. Bong, Joshua Carlson, Bryan Curtis, Ruth Haas, Leslie Hogben

Abstract
An X X -TAR (token addition/removal) reconfiguration graph has as its vertices sets that satisfy some property X X , with an edge between two sets if one is obtained from the other by adding or removing one element. This paper considers the X X -TAR graph for X − X- sets of vertices of a base graph G G where the X X -sets of G G must satisfy certain conditions. Dominating sets, power dominating sets, zero forcing sets, and positive semidefinite zero forcing sets are all examples of X X -sets. For graphs G G and G ′ G^{\prime} with no isolated vertices, it is shown that G G and G ′ G^{\prime} have isomorphic X X -TAR reconfiguration graphs if and only if there is a relabeling of the vertices of G ′ G^{\prime} such that G G and G ′ G^{\prime} have exactly the same X X -sets. The concept of an X X -irrelevant vertex is introduced to facilitate analysis of X X -TAR graph isomorphisms. Furthermore, results related to the connectedness of the zero forcing TAR graph are given. We present families of graphs that exceed known lower bounds for connectedness parameters.

Introduction

The study of reconfiguration examines relationships among solutions to a problem. These solutions are modeled as vertices in a graph called a
reconfiguration graph
. A
reconfiguration rule
describes the adjacency relationship in a reconfiguration graph. When the solutions are sets, the
token addition or removal rule
says that two sets
S
and
S^{\prime}
are adjacent in the
TAR reconfiguration graph
if
S^{\prime}
can be obtained from
S
by adding or removing one element. For our purposes, solutions are sets of vertices of a graph
G
defined by a property, such as being a dominating set, power dominating set, or zero forcing set of
G
, although reconfiguration is studied more broadly. The graph
G
is called the
base graph
.
In
[
4
]
, Bjorkman et al. established various properties of a TAR reconfiguration graph in a universal framework for parameters that we call
X
-set parameters (see Definition
1.1
), and we call the associated TAR reconfiguration graphs
X
-TAR graphs. Examples of
X
-set parameters include the domination number, the power domination number, the zero forcing number, and the positive semidefinite zero forcing number (definitions of these parameters are presented later in this introduction).

It is established in Theorems
2.10
and
2.12
that if
G
and
G^{\prime}
are graphs with no isolated vertices that have isomorphic
X
-TAR reconfiguration graphs, then there is a relabeling of the vertices of
G^{\prime}
such that
G
and
G^{\prime}
have exactly the same
X
-sets. To prove Theorem
2.12
we introduce the concept of a set of irrelevant vertices (or an
X
-irrelevant set).
In Section
2.2
,
X
-irrelevant sets are used to characterize the automorphism group of an
X
-TAR graph. There we also discuss the existence (or nonexistence) of nonempty
X
-irrelevant sets when
X
is the domination number, power domination number, zero forcing number, or positive semidefinite zero forcing number. The study of which
X
-TAR graphs are unique (up to isomorphism of the base graph and with isolated vertices prohibited) is parameter-specific, and we examine this question for zero forcing TAR graphs in Section
2.4
.

Connectedness, that is, whether any one feasible solution can be transformed into any other by the reconfiguration rule, is a fundamental question in the study of reconfiguration. Connectedness properties seem to be very parameter-specific. In Section
3
we present results related to connectedness for zero forcing TAR reconfiguration graphs. This includes exhibiting families of graphs that exceed known bounds for parameters describing connectedness of the zero forcing TAR reconfiguration graphs.

All graphs are simple, undirected, finite, and have nonempty vertex sets. Let
G=(V(G),E(G))

Let

In the context of TAR reconfiguration, “solutions” to zero forcing on a graph

are zero forcing sets.
The

of

11

, where the token exchange zero forcing reconfiguration graph was studied. Note that for the zero forcing token exchange reconfiguration graph, only minimum zero forcing sets are used as vertices and two such sets are adjacent if one can be obtained from the other by exchanging one vertex.

Positive semidefinite zero forcing is a variant of zero forcing defined by the

A

The

Irrelevant sets and TAR graph isomorphisms

The TAR reconfiguration graph of an
X
-set parameter carries a great deal of information about the
X
-sets of

, but the information about

-sets of

does not generally determine
G
; in particular, the information about zero forcing sets of
G
sets does not generally determine
G
.

We show that for graphs

and
G^{\prime}
with no isolated vertices,
\mathscr{X}^{{\tiny\rm TAR}}(G)\cong\mathscr{X}^{{\tiny\rm TAR}}(G^{\prime})
implies there is a relabeling of the vertices of
G^{\prime}
such that
G
and
G^{\prime}
have exactly the same
X
-sets (see Theorem
2.12
). When applied to zero forcing,
\mathscr{Z}^{{\tiny\rm TAR}}(G)\cong\mathscr{Z}^{{\tiny\rm TAR}}(G^{\prime})
implies

and
G^{\prime}
necessarily have the same the zero forcing polynomials (as defined in
[
5
]
, see Section

). Showing that an isomorphism of zero forcing TAR graphs implies that their base graphs have the same the zero forcing polynomial triggered this study of isomorphisms.

Irrelevant sets are introduced in Section

to prove that if

, then there is an isomorphism that preserves the cardinality of

-sets (see Theorem

). The focus of Section

is proving Theorem

, but irrelevant sets are interesting in their own right (and exhibit significant differences among various

-set parameters). These ideas are discussed in Section

.

In Section

we establish that certain zero forcing TAR graphs are unique up to isomorphism of the base graph (assuming the base graph has no isolated vertices), and also show that a cycle and a cycle with one chord have the same zero forcing TAR graphs (see Proposition

).

2.1
Isomorphisms of the TAR graph

There is a natural poset structure on the
vertices of
X
-TAR reconfiguration graphs whose partial ordering is determined by set inclusion. This poset structure can be used to better understand the isomorphisms between
X
-TAR reconfiguration graphs. We begin this subsection with a result showing that the vertices of induced hypercubes of
X
-TAR reconfiguration graphs are intervals in the aforementioned poset. To formally state this result, we recall some basic poset terminology found in
[
13
]
.

Let
Y
be a set and
P=(Y,\leq)
be a poset. An element
u\in Y
is
maximal
(respectively,
minimal
) if
u\not<y
(respectively,
u\not<y
) for each
y\in Y
. Elements
u,v\in Y
are
comparable
provided
u\leq v
or
v\leq u
. If
u\leq v
in
P
, then the set
[u,v]=\{y:u\leq y\leq v\}
is called an
interval
. A
chain
is a poset such that each pair of elements is comparable. The
length
of a chain
C
is
|C|-1
and the length of the interval
[u,v]
is
the maximum length of a chain in
[u,v]
and is denoted by
\ell(u,v)
.

For a set
T
, let
\mathcal{P}(T)
denote the power set of
T
. We remark that
\mathscr{X}^{{\tiny\rm TAR}}(G)
is a join-semilattice contained in
(\mathcal{P}(V(G)),\subseteq)
and
\ell(S,S^{\prime})=\operatorname{dist}(S,S^{\prime})
for any interval
[S,S^{\prime}]
in
\mathscr{X}^{{\tiny\rm TAR}}(G)
since
S
and
S^{\prime}
are comparable (where
\operatorname{dist}(S,S^{\prime})
is the distance in the graph
\mathscr{X}^{{\tiny\rm TAR}}(G)
).

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
X
be an
X
-set parameter, let

be an integer, let
G
be a graph on
n
vertices, and let

be an induced subgraph of
\mathscr{X}^{{\tiny\rm TAR}}(G)
. If

, then

is an interval of length

in the poset
(\mathcal{P}(V(G)),\subseteq)
.

Proof.

The claim is obvious for

. Suppose that

. We begin by showing that
(V(H),\subseteq)
has exactly 1 maximal element.

Assume, to the contrary, that
(V(H),\subseteq)
has at least 2 maximal elements

and
v
. Since
H
is connected, there exists a path from
u
to
v
in
H
. Every path

from
u
to
v

. For each such

, let

to be the smallest index

, where
y_{0}=u
and
y_{i+1}=v
. Note that since

and
v
are maximal in
(V(H),\subseteq)
,

is always defined.

Let
d
be the minimum
d_{P}
amongst all paths
P
from
u
to
v
in
H
. Pick a path
P^{*}
in
H
of the form
ux_{1}\cdots x_{i}v
such that

, and let
x_{0}=u
and
x_{i+1}=v
. Then
x_{d-1}\subseteq x_{d-2}
because

is adjacent to

. Since

and

and

By our choice of

and

or else

.
Hence

.

Thus,

has exactly 1 maximal element

has exactly 1 minimal element

in the subset partial ordering of

∎

Theorem 2.10

-set parameter, let

Proposition 3.4
.

For
r\geq 2
,
\operatorname{Z}(H(r))=\overline{\operatorname{Z}}(H(r))=r+2
and
\underline{z_{0}}(H(r))=z_{0}(H(r))=2r+2=\overline{\operatorname{Z}}(H(r))+r
.

Proof.

Observe that the vertices
r+1,r+2,2r+3
and
2r+4
have degree
r+1
and all other vertices of
H(r)
have degree
r+2
so
\delta(H(r))=r+1
. Let
U_{1}=\{r+1,r+2\}
and
U_{2}=\{2r+3,2r+4\}
. If
v\in V_{i}
, then
|N_{H(r)}(v)\cap V_{i}|=r+1
. Let
S\subset V(H(r))
and let
S_{i}=S\cap V_{i}
for
i=1,2
. Assuming that
|S_{1}|\geq|S_{2}|
, we show that
S
is a zero forcing set of
H(r)
if and only if
|S_{1}|\geq r+1
and
|S_{2}\cap U_{2}|\geq 1
. Once this is established,
\operatorname{Z}(H(r))=\overline{\operatorname{Z}}(H(r))=r+2
with a minimum zero forcing set
S
having (
|S_{1}|=r+1
and
|S_{2}|=1
) or (
|S_{2}|=r+1
and
|S_{1}|=1
). Furthermore,
\mathscr{Z}^{{\tiny\rm TAR}}_{2r+1}(H(r))
has 2 components, one containing zero forcing sets
S
with
|S_{1}|>|S_{2}|
and the other with
|S_{2}|>|S_{1}|
, whereas
\mathscr{Z}^{{\tiny\rm TAR}}_{2r+2}(H(r))
is connected.

Suppose first that
|S_{1}|=r+1
,
|S_{2}|=1
, and
S_{2}\subset U_{2}
(the argument where
|S_{2}|=r+1
is similar). There must be a vertex
u\in U_{1}\cap S_{1}
, and
u
can force the one white vertex of
S_{1}
. Then
i\to r+2+i
for
i=1,\dots,r
. Finally any blue vertex of
V_{2}
can force the one remaining white vertex in
V_{2}
. Thus
S
is a zero forcing set.

Now suppose
S
is a zero forcing set. Let
v
be the vertex that performs the first force, and without loss of generality
v\in V_{1}
. Since
v
and all but one of its neighbors must be in
S
,
S
contains at least
r+1
of the vertices in
S
. Since at most
r
vertices of
V_{2}
can be forced by vertices in
V_{1}
,
S
must contain a vertex of
V_{2}
that has no neighbor in
V_{1}
, i.e.,
S
must contain at least one vertex of
U_{2}
.
∎

A computer search on graphs up to 8 vertices (with no isolated vertices) found exactly 2 graphs
G
such that
z_{0}(G)>\underline{z_{0}}(G)
. For example, the graph
H_{2}
in Figure
3.2
has
z_{0}(H_{2})=7
and
\underline{z_{0}}(H_{2})=5
(because there are minimal zero forcing sets of orders 4 and 6 but not 5, and
\mathscr{Z}^{{\tiny\rm TAR}}_{5}(H_{2})
is connected, see
[
7
]
).

Next we show that the graph
H_{2}
can be used to create an infinite family of graphs
H_{r}
such that
z_{0}(H_{r})>\underline{z_{0}}(H_{r})
. Observe that
N_{H_{2}}(u_{2})=N_{H_{2}}(u_{1})
. Construct
H_{r}
from
H_{2}
by adding vertices
u_{3},\dots,u_{r}
with
N_{H_{r}}(u_{k})=N_{H_{r}}(u_{1})
for
k=3,\dots,r
. In general, vertices
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
in a graph
G
are called
twins
if
N_{G}(u_{1})=N_{G}(u_{2})
(what we define as twins are often called
independent twins
). A
set of twins
is a set
\{u_{1},\dots,u_{k}\}\subseteq V(G)
such that
u_{i}
and
u_{j}
are twins for all
1\leq i<j\leq k
.

Observation 3.5
.

If
G
is a graph with a set of twins
\{u_{1},\dots,u_{r}\}
, then any zero forcing set of
G
must contain at least
r-1
of the vertices
\{u_{1},\dots,u_{r}\}
.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
G
be a graph that has a set of twins
T=\{u_{1},\dots,u_{r}\}
with
r\geq 3
, and let
G_{i}=G-u_{i}
. If
S_{i}
is a zero forcing set of
G_{i}
, then
S=S_{i}\cup\{u_{i}\}
is a zero forcing set of
G
. If
S
is a zero forcing set of
G
and
u_{i}\in S
, then
S_{i}=S\setminus\{u_{i}\}
is a zero forcing set of
G_{i}
. Thus there is a bijection between zero forcing sets of
G_{i}
and zero forcing sets of
G
that contain
u_{i}
, and a zero forcing set

S_{i}
of
G_{i}
is minimal if and only if
S=S_{i}\cup\{u_{i}\}
is a minimal zero forcing set of
G
.

Proof.

It is immediate that
S_{i}
being a zero forcing set of
G_{i}
implies
S=S_{i}\cup\{u_{i}\}
is a zero forcing set of
G
(without any assumption about twins). Let
S
be a zero forcing set of
G
. Since
r\geq 3
,
S
must contain at least two vertices in
T
, say
u_{i}
and
u_{j}
. Choose a set of forces
\mathscr{F}
that color every vertex of
G
blue. We show that at least one of
u_{i}
and
u_{j}
does not perform a force. Neither
u_{i}
nor
u_{j}
can perform a force until all but one of their common set of neighbors are blue. So if
u_{i}
forces the one white neighbor blue, then
u_{j}
cannot perform a force and if
u_{j}
forces, then
u_{i}
can’t (it is possible neither forces). Thus we may assume
u_{i}
does not perform a force. Then
S_{i}=S\setminus\{u_{i}\}
is a zero forcing set of
G_{i}
using the set of forces
\mathscr{F}
.
The last sentence is immediate. ∎

The technique of removing a nonforcing vertex
v
from a zero forcing set
S
of
G
to obtain a zero forcing set
S\setminus\{v\}
is well known (see, for example, the proof of Theorem 2.7 in
[
8
]
). When there are only two twins, it is not true that
S_{i}=S\setminus\{u_{i}\}
must be a zero forcing set of
G_{i}
, because it is possible that only one of
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
is in
S
and that vertex is required to perform a force. This is illustrated in the next example.

Example 3.7
.

Consider the
double star

DS(2,2)
constructed by adding an edge between the centers of two disjoint copies of
K_{1,2}
. If the leaves of one star are
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
, then
\operatorname{Z}(DS(2,2))=2=\operatorname{Z}(DS(2,2)-u_{1})
(see
[
7
]
).

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
G
be a graph that has a set of twins
T=\{u_{1},\dots,u_{r}\}
with
r\geq 3
, and let
G_{i}=G-u_{i}
. If
\mathscr{Z}^{{\tiny\rm TAR}}_{k}(G_{i})
is connected for some
i
, then
\mathscr{Z}^{{\tiny\rm TAR}}_{k+1}(G)
is connected.

Proof.

The graphs
G_{i}
are isomorphic and hence the
\mathscr{Z}^{{\tiny\rm TAR}}_{k}(G_{i})
are isomorphic as well. Thus if
\mathscr{Z}^{{\tiny\rm TAR}}_{k}(G_{i})
is connected for some
i
then
\mathscr{Z}^{{\tiny\rm TAR}}_{k}(G_{i})
is connected for all
i
.

For
S\subseteq V(G)
with
u_{i}\in S
, let
S_{i}=S\setminus\{u_{i}\}
.
Since
r\geq 3
, any zero forcing set
S
of
G
must contain at least two vertices in
T
, say
u_{i}
and
u_{j}
. By Proposition
3.6
, a set
S\subseteq V(G)
that contains
u_{i},u_{j}
is a zero forcing set of
G
if and only if
S\setminus{u_{i}}
is a zero forcing set of
G_{i}
and
S\setminus{u_{j}}
is a zero forcing set of
G_{j}
.

Let
S,S^{\prime}
be two zero forcing sets of
G
of size
k+1
or less. Since
r\geq 3
and each can omit at most one vertex in
T
, their intersection must contain at least one
u_{i}
. Then
S\setminus{u_{i}},S^{\prime}\setminus{u_{i}}
are zero forcing sets for
G_{i}
. Since by assumption
\mathscr{Z}^{{\tiny\rm TAR}}_{k}(G_{i})
is connected, that means that there is a path between
S\setminus{u_{i}}
and
S^{\prime}\setminus{u_{i}}
in
\mathscr{Z}^{{\tiny\rm TAR}}_{k}(G_{i})
and hence a path between
S,S^{\prime}
in
\mathscr{Z}^{{\tiny\rm TAR}}_{k+1}(G)
.
∎

Proposition 3.9
.

For
r\geq 2
,
\operatorname{Z}(H_{r})=r+2
,
\underline{z_{0}}(H_{r})=r+3
, and
z_{0}(H_{r})=r+5
.

Proof.

The proof is by induction. The base case is
H=H_{2}
, which has minimal zero forcing sets of sixes 4 and 6 and has
\mathscr{Z}^{{\tiny\rm TAR}}_{5}(H_{2})
is connected. Apply Proposition
3.6
to show that
H_{r}
has minimal zero forcing sets of sizes
r+2
and
r+4
. Apply Lemma
3.8
to show
\mathscr{Z}^{{\tiny\rm TAR}}_{r+3}(H_{r})
is connected.
∎

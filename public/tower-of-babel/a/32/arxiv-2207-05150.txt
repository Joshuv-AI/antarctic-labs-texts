Breaching the 2 LMP Approximation Barrier for Facility Location with Applications to k k -Median
Vincent Cohen-Addad Viallat, Fabrizio Grandoni, Euiwoong Lee, Chris Schwiegelshohn

Abstract
The Uncapacitated Facility Location (UFL) problem is one of the most fundamental clustering problems: Given a set of clients C C and a set of facilities F F in a metric space ( C ∪ F , d ​ i ​ s ​ t ) (C\cup F,dist) with facility costs open : F → ℝ + \texttt{open}:F\to\mathbb{R}^{+} , the goal is to find a set of facilities S ⊆ F S\subseteq F to minimize the sum of the opening cost open ​ ( S ) \texttt{open}(S) and the connection cost d ⁡ ( S ) := ∑ p ∈ C min c ∈ S ⁡ d ​ i ​ s ​ t ​ ( p , c ) d(S):=\sum_{p\in C}\min_{c\in S}dist(p,c) . An algorithm for UFL is called a Lagrangian Multiplier Preserving (LMP) α \alpha approximation if it outputs a solution S ⊆ F S\subseteq F satisfying open ​ ( S ) + d ⁡ ( S ) ≤ open ​ ( S ∗ ) + α ​ d ​ ( S ∗ ) \texttt{open}(S)+d(S)\leq\texttt{open}(S^{*})+\alpha d(S^{*}) for any S ∗ ⊆ F S^{*}\subseteq F . The best-known LMP approximation ratio for UFL is at most 2 2 by the JMS algorithm of Jain, Mahdian, and Saberi [STOC’02, J.ACM’03] based on the Dual-Fitting technique. The lack of progress on improving the upper bound on α L ​ M ​ P \alpha_{LMP} in the last two decades raised the natural question whether α L ​ M ​ P = 2 \alpha_{LMP}=2 . We answer this question negatively by presenting a (slightly) improved LMP approximation algorithm for UFL. This is achieved by combining the Dual-Fitting technique with Local Search, another popular technique to address clustering problems. In more detail, we use the LMP solution S S produced by JMS to seed a local search algorithm. We show that local search substantially improves S S unless a big fraction of the connection cost of S S is associated with facilities of relatively small opening costs. In the latter case however the analysis of Jain, Mahdian, and Saberi can be improved (i.e., S S is cheaper than expected). To summarize: Either S S is close enough to the optimum, or it must belong to the local neighborhood of a good enough local optimum. From a conceptual viewpoint, our result gives a theoretical evidence that local search can be enhanced so as to avoid bad local optima by choosing the initial feasible solution with LP-based techniques. Our result directly implies a (slightly) improved approximation for the related k k -Median problem, another fundamental clustering problem: Given ( C ∪ F , d ​ i ​ s ​ t ) (C\cup F,dist) as in a UFL instance and an integer k ∈ ℕ k\in\mathbb{N} , find S ⊆ F S\subseteq F with | S | = k |S|=k that minimizes d ⁡ ( S ) d(S) . The current best approximation algorithms for k k -Median are based on the following framework: use an LMP α \alpha approximation algorithm for UFL to build an α \alpha approximate bipoint solution for k k -Median, and then round it with a ρ B ​ R \rho_{BR} approximate bipoint rounding algorithm. This implies an α ⋅ ρ B ​ R \alpha\cdot\rho_{BR} approximation. The current-best value of ρ B ​ R \rho_{BR} is 1.338 1.338 by Byrka, Pensyl, Rybicki, Srinivasan, and Trinh [SODA’15, TALG’17], which yields 2.6742 2.6742 -approximation. Combining their algorithm with our refined LMP algorithm for UFL (replacing JMS) gives a 2.67059 2.67059 -approximation.

1.1
Our Results

Due to the lack of progress on improving
\alpha_{LMP}
in the last two decades, it is natural to ask the following question:

Open Problem 1
.

Is there an LMP
(2-\eta)
approximation for UFL for some constant
\eta>0
?

We answer the above question in the affirmative (though for a rather small
\eta>2.25\cdot 10^{-7}
), even for the general version of UFL where facilities can have different opening costs. The constant
\eta
can be substantially improved for the case of uniform opening costs, which is sufficient in our application to
k
-Median. Indeed, for
k
-Median some other technical refinements are possible, altogether leading to a more substantial (still small) improvement of
k
-Median approximation factor from
2.6742
to
2.67059
2
2

2

We did not insist too much on improving
\eta
, but we made a substantial effort to refine the approximation factor for
k
-Median.
.

While showing (though only qualitatively) that the LMP
2
approximation barrier can be breached is interesting in our opinion, probably the most interesting aspect of our work is at technical level. The dual-fitting LMP 2 approximation by
[
JMS02
]
is tight. In particular, a tight instance can be built via their factor-revealing LP. One might consider also a natural local-search-based algorithm for UFL, where one swaps in and out subsets of up to
\Delta=O(1)
facilities: however (see Claim
3
in Section
G
)
such an algorithm is not an LMP
\alpha_{LMP}
approximation for any constant
\alpha_{LMP}
(in the case of non-uniform opening costs).

Somewhat surprisingly, we show that a combination of dual-fitting and local search is better than each such approach in isolation. Consider first the case of uniform opening costs. We consider the UFL solution
S
produced by the algorithm JMS in
[
JMS02
]
. We seed the above mentioned local search UFL algorithm with
S
(i.e.,
S
is used as the initial feasible solution), hence obtaining a refined solution
S^{\prime}
. The latter solution turns out to be an LMP
2-\eta<2
approximation for the problem. More precisely, we can show that either
S
is already a good enough solution, or it belongs to the local neighbourhood of a good enough solution. Intuitively, the worst case instances for JMS and local search are to a certain
degree mutually exclusive. For general opening costs we augment the standard local-search neighborhood by also considering the solutions obtained by setting to zero the cost of some facilities and running JMS itself on the obtained instance. We believe that our result is conceptually interesting: seeding a local search algorithm with an LP-based approximate solution is a natural heuristic; our result supports this heuristic from a theoretical viewpoint.

Preliminaries

Facility Location and JMS Algorithm.

In the classical (metric uncapacitated) Facility Location problem (UFL) we are given a set of facilities
F
and a set of clients
C
together with a metric
dist(\cdot,\cdot)
over
F\cup C
. Furthermore, we are given opening costs
\texttt{open}(f)\geq 0

f\in F

S\subseteq F

open

\texttt{open}(S):=\sum_{f\in S}\texttt{open}(f)
be the opening cost of the solution, and

be its connection cost, where
dist(j,S):=\min_{f\in S}\{dist(j,f)\}
. Intuitively, we open the facilities in
S
and connect each client
j
to the closest open facility
f\in S
(paying the associated connection cost
dist(j,S)
). Our goal is to minimize the total cost
\texttt{open}(S)+d(S)
. We will also consider the special case of UFL where all opening costs have uniform value
\lambda
. Recall that an algorithm for UFL (producing a solution
S
) is an
(\alpha,\beta)
approximation if, for any feasible solution
S^{\prime}
, one has
\texttt{open}(S)+d(S)\leq\alpha\texttt{open}(S^{\prime})+\beta d(S^{\prime})
. If
\alpha=1
, that algorithm is a
Lagrangian Multiplier Preserving
(LMP)
\beta
approximation for FL.

As mentioned in the introduction, our algorithm performs local search starting from the solution computed by the classical JMS algorithm
[
JMS02
,
JMM+03
]

Roughly speaking, the JMS algorithm works as follows. At any point of time we maintain a subset of
active

. Furthermore, for each

\alpha_{j}\geq 0

,

High-level overview.

At a high-level, pairs of facilities of

Our proof then decouples the matched and lonely facilities. We provide an analysis of the local search solution

This way, for matched facilities

Handling non-uniform facility opening costs via local search requires a new notion of local neighborhood because the disparity in facility costs forces some swap pairs

We start with the following lemma showing that local search can be made polynomial-time.

Lemma 11
.

For any
\varepsilon>0
,
the results of Lemmas
12
and
13
can be obtained in polynomial time up to
losing an additive factor of
O(\varepsilon(opt+d^{\prime}))
.

Proof.

We apply the following standard preprocessing of the graph. Let
\eta
be the connection cost of the optimum solution.
Our algorithm partitions the input metric by creating a graph whose vertex set is the set of points of the input metric space and
whose edge set is the set of all pairs of
points at distance at most
\eta
;
and applies the local search algorithm independently in each subinstance defined by the set of points that are in the same connected component.

Since the optimum connection cost is at most
\eta
, it is immediate that any optimum clustering is such that no two points of different connected
components are in the same cluster, and so, obtaining the approximation guarantees of Lemmas
12
13
for
each subinstance is enough to recover the same approximation guarantees for the whole instance.

Next, for each subinstance of size
n^{\prime}
, let us assume that the minimum distance between any pair of points is
\varepsilon\eta/n^{\prime 2}
– this can
be achieved up to losing a factor
(1+\varepsilon)

in the connection cost of the optimum solution by “contracting” each pair of points at distance smaller than
\varepsilon\eta/n^{\prime 2}
.
Moreover, the maximum distance between any two points of the subinstance
is at most
n^{\prime}\cdot\eta
. Finally, we can assume without loss of generality that the opening cost is at most
2n^{\prime 2}\cdot\eta
(since otherwise
we know that the optimum solution opens at most 1 center and we can solve the instance optimally) and at least
\varepsilon^{2}\eta/n^{\prime 2}

(otherwise, since the minimum
distance between a pair of points is
\varepsilon\eta/n^{\prime 2}
,
we can obtain a
(1+\varepsilon)
-approximate solution by opening a facility for each input client, at the candidate center location that is the closest).

We thus have that for each subinstance on
n^{\prime}
points, the minimum cost is at least
\varepsilon^{2}\eta/n^{\prime 2}
and the maximum is
\eta n^{\prime 2}
.
The local search is run such that a new solution
is picked if its cost improves the cost of the current solution by a factor at least
(1+\varepsilon^{3}/n^{\prime 5})
. Since the initial solution has cost at most

\eta n^{\prime 2}
and the final solution at most
\varepsilon^{2}\eta/n^{\prime 2}
, the number of steps is at most
O(n^{\prime 9}\varepsilon^{-5})
.
Finally, two solutions of cost at least
\varepsilon^{2}\eta/n^{\prime 2}
and at most
\eta n^{\prime 2}
differ in cost by a multiplicative factor at most

(1+\varepsilon^{3}/n^{\prime 5})
which is an additive factor of at most
\varepsilon^{3}/n^{\prime 5}\cdot\eta n^{\prime 2}=\varepsilon^{3}/n^{\prime 3}\leq\varepsilon/n\cdot\varepsilon^{2}\eta/n^{\prime 2}
.
Since the analysis of Lemmas
12
13
uses at most
k

assuming it reaches a local optimum or a solution which is within a factor
(1+\varepsilon^{3}/n^{\prime 5})

\varepsilon\eta

To conclude, the algorithm is run with
\varepsilon^{-1}\log\frac{\text{maximum distance}}{\text{minimum distance}}
different estimates of
\eta

namely, all the powers of
(1+\varepsilon)

minimum distance between any pair of points, and the best solution is returned.

served by a facility
f^{*}\in OPT^{L}
(hence not matched) appears as a
client of type
C_{1},C_{1}^{a}
or
C_{1}^{b}
in exactly one swap (i.e.: the swap
(\{f^{\prime}\},B)
where

f^{*}\in B
), as a client of type
C_{3}
in at most one swap (i.e.: the swap
(\{f^{\prime}\},B)
where

f^{\prime}
is the facility serving it in
S^{\prime}
) and as a type
C_{4}
client in all remaining swaps.
Therefore, its total contribution to LHS of the sum is at most

4opt(c)-d^{\prime}(c)
.

In addition, each client
c
served by a facility
f^{*}\in OPT^{M}
and served by a facility

f^{\prime}\in S^{\prime M}
such that
f^{*}
and
f^{\prime}
are matched appears as a client of type
C_{1}
or
C_{1}^{a}

in exactly one swap and as a client of type
C_{4}
otherwise. Its total contribution is thus
at most
opt(c)-d^{\prime}(c)
.

Each client
c
served by a facility
f^{*}\in OPT^{M}
and served by a facility

f^{\prime}\in S^{\prime}
such that
f^{*}
and
f^{\prime}
are not matched appears as a client of type
C_{2}

in exactly one swap and as a client of type
C_{4}
otherwise. Its total contribution is thus
at most
opt(c)-d^{\prime}(c)+\frac{\sum_{c^{\prime}\in C_{OPT}(OPT(c))}opt(c^{\prime})+d^{\prime}(c^{\prime})}{(1-\delta)|C_{OPT}(OPT(c))|}
.
Observe that for each matched facility
f^{*}
of
OPT^{M}
the number of such clients is at most

\delta|C_{OPT}(OPT(c))|
by the definition of matched. Hence, summing up over all clients served
by
f^{*}
in
OPT
, the above bound is at most

\sum_{c\in C_{OPT}(f^{*})}\big(opt(c)-d^{\prime}(c)\big)+\frac{\delta}{1-\delta}\sum_{c\in C_{OPT}(f^{*})}\big(opt(c)+d^{\prime}(c)\big).

Therefore, the total cost of
S^{\prime}
is as claimed at most

\texttt{open}(OPT)+\frac{\delta}{1-\delta}d^{\prime}+\frac{1}{1-\delta}opt^{M}+4opt^{L}.\qed

G.2
Final Result by Cost Scaling

The result in the above subsection shows that for any Facility Location instance, if
OPT
is the solution minimizing
\texttt{open}(OPT)+(2-\eta)opt
,
S^{\prime}
is a local optimum of the extended local search starting from a JMS solution, and
d^{\prime}\leq 4opt
, then it is already an LMP
(2-\eta)
approximate solution for some
\eta>0
depending only on our choices of
\delta_{1},\delta_{2},\delta^{\prime}_{1},\delta^{\prime}_{2}
.
Though ensuring
d^{\prime}\leq 4opt
in general is a nontrivial task, we show how to get around this bottleneck and get an LMP
(2-\eta/2)
approximation.
Let
OPT
be the feasible solution that minimizes
\texttt{open}(OPT)+(2-\eta/2)opt
.

The idea is to use the concept of bipoint solutions again.
In particular, we multiply each facility cost by a factor
\lambda
, and apply the JMS algorithm and the extended local search to the resulting Facility Location instance. Let
S(\lambda)
be the corresponding solution.

If
\lambda=0
,
S(0)
connects all clients to their closest facilities. If the original facility cost of
S(0)
is at most that of
OPT
, then
S(0)
is already an LMP
1
approximation, so we can assume otherwise. On the other hand, let
\gamma=\min(\min_{f\in F}\texttt{open}(f),\min_{f,f^{\prime}\in F:\texttt{open}(f)\neq\texttt{open}(f^{\prime})}|\texttt{open}(f)-\texttt{open}(f^{\prime})|)
,
and
M=\sum_{c\in C,f\in F}d(c,f)
.
Suppose we scale facility costs by
\lambda=3M/\gamma
, and consider an arbitrary solution
S^{\prime}
that consists of one facility with the lowest opening cost.
By the definition of
\lambda
, for any solution
S^{\prime\prime}
that strictly spends more on the facility cost than
S^{\prime}
, whether it opens two cheapest facilities or opens a facility which is not the cheapest, the difference between the opening costs between
S^{\prime}
and
S^{\prime\prime}
is at least
3M
, which is at least 3 times larger than the connection cost of any solution.
The fact that
S(\lambda)
is also LMP
2
approximate w.r.t.
S^{\prime}
(with respect to scaled facility costs) implies that
S(\lambda)
also opens exactly one cheapest facility, so its facility cost must be at most that of
OPT
(both scaled and original).

We perform a binary search over
\lambda
until we find a value
\lambda^{*}
such that
S_{1}=S(\lambda^{*}+\delta)
has facility cost (with respect to original facility costs)
\texttt{open}(S_{1})\leq\texttt{open}(OPT)
and
S_{2}=S(\lambda^{*})
has facility cost (with respect to original facility costs)
\texttt{open}(S_{2})\geq\texttt{open}(OPT)
. Here
\delta>0
can be chosen to be exponentially small in the input size and we will next neglect it at the cost of an extra factor
(1+\varepsilon)
in the approximation for arbitrarily small constant
\varepsilon>0
.
Also note that the value of
\texttt{open}(OPT)
can be guessed at the cost of an extra
(1+\varepsilon)
in the approximation.

Define
a\in[0,1]
such that
a\texttt{open}(S_{1})+(1-a)\texttt{open}(S_{2})=\texttt{open}(OPT)
. Let
d_{i}=d(S_{i})
. Since
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
are LMP
2
approximate solutions with respect to scaled facility costs, we have

\lambda^{*}\texttt{open}(S_{1})+d_{1}\leq\lambda^{*}\texttt{open}(OPT)+2opt,\quad\lambda^{*}\texttt{open}(S_{2})+d_{2}\leq\lambda^{*}\texttt{open}(OPT)+2opt,

which implies

ad_{1}+(1-a)d_{2}\leq 2opt.

Suppose that
a\geq 1/2
. (The other case is symmetric.)
This implies that
d_{1}\leq 4opt
, so we can Lemma
16
so that
S_{1}
is indeed LMP
(2-\eta)
approximate with respect to scaled facility costs for some
\eta\geq 4.5\cdot 10^{-7}
;
\lambda^{*}\texttt{open}(S_{1})+d_{1}\leq\lambda^{*}\texttt{open}(OPT)+(2-\eta)opt
.
This implies that a random solution choosing
S_{1}
with probability
a
and
S_{2}
with probability
(1-a)
has the expected (original) facility cost

\texttt{open}(OPT)
and the connection cost at most
a(2-\eta)opt+(1-a)2opt\leq(2-\eta/2)opt
. Therefore, one of
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
is an LMP
(2-\eta/2)
approximate solution. This proves Theorem
4
.

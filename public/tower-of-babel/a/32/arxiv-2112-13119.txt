1.
Introduction

One of the most fundamental problems in extremal graph theory is to determine the
Turán number of a graph
H
.
By definition, let
\text{ex}(n,H)
denote the maximal number of edges of an
H
-free
n
-graph.
The cornerstone in this area is the classical
Erdős-Stone-Simonovits theorem
[
8
,
9
]
, which says that

(1.1)

\text{ex}(n,H)=\big(1-\frac{1}{\chi(H)-1}+o(1)\big){n\choose 2},

where
\chi(H)
is the chromatic number of
H
.
However, when
H
is a bipartite graph, the above theorem only gives a crude bound
o(n^{2})
. In fact,
to understand the correct exponent of the extremal number of various bipartite graphs
is one of the central topics in extremal combinatorics. See the recent survey
[
11
]
for more information about this rather huge topic.

In this paper, we will study this problem for the class of
1
-subdivisions of graphs.
For any graph
H
, its 1-subdivision
H^{\text{sub}}
is obtained by replacing each
edge in
E(H)
by a path of length two. Therefore,
H^{\text{sub}}
is a bipartite graph, whose vertex bipartition can be regarded as
V(H)\cup E(H)
.
In response to a conjecture made by Erdős in
[
7
]
,
Conlon and Lee in
[
4
]

showed that the 1-subdivision of a complete graph always has an extremal exponent strictly less than
\frac{3}{2}
. More precisely,

Theorem 1.1

(
[
4
]
)
.

For any integer
t\geq 3
,
\text{ex}(n,K_{t}^{\text{sub}})=O(n^{3/2-1/{6^{t}}})
.

Then, Janzer in
[
13
]
improved this to

(1.2)

\text{ex}(n,K_{t}^{\text{sub}})=O(n^{\frac{3}{2}-\frac{1}{4t-6}}),

which is expected to be
correct exponent. In his proof, Janzer proposed a nice scheme for upper bounding extremal numbers of 1-subdivisions of graphs.

Another related topic involving 1- subdivisions is the following conjecture
made by Kang, Kim and Liu in
[
15
]
:
For any bipartite graph
H
, if
\text{ex}(n,H)=O(n^{1+\alpha})
for some
\alpha>0
,
then
\text{ex}(n,H^{\text{sub}})=O(n^{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}})
.
In the same paper, it was shown that the above conjecture implies the well-known
rational exponent conjecture by Erdős and Simonovits (see
[
6
]
), which states that
for any rational number
r\in[1,2]
, there exists a graph
H
so that
\text{ex}(n,H)=\Theta(n^{r})
.

Influenced by the subdivision conjecture,
Conlon, Lee and Janzer in
[
5
]
obtained the following result.

Theorem 1.2
.

For any integers
2\leq s\leq t
,
\text{ex}(n,K_{s,t}^{\text{sub}})=O(n^{3/2-1/{2s}})
.

When
t
is sufficiently large,
one can combine the recently developed random polynomial method of Bukh and Conlon
to see the exponent indeed gives the correct order (see
[
1
]
,
[
3
]
and
[
2
]
). More precisely, for any integer
s
and for any
t
sufficiently large depending on
s
,

(1.3)

\text{ex}(n,K_{s,t}^{\text{sub}})=\Theta(n^{3/2-1/2s}).

These papers inspired many works in this direction in recent years. However, the current situation is still very unsatisfactory. For example, if the graph
H
itself is not bipartite, then there still exists no conjecture similar to that of Kang, Kim and Liu. In other words, in general, if the chromatic number of
H
is
k\geq 3
, although its Turán number is of order
\Theta(n^{2})
, there is no theory to even predict the Turán number for its
1
-subdivision graph
H^{\text{sub}}
, with a single exceptional case of the complete graph
K_{k}
, for which (
1.2
) provides an upper bound. In this paper, we contribute to this topic by initiating the study of the extremal number for the
1
-subdivisions of non-bipartite (and non-complete) graphs.

Our first result concerns the
1
-subdivision of a special type of tripartite graph, namely
K_{1,2,2}^{\text{sub}}
. In fact, we see it as one of the following family of graphs. Let
C_{k}
be a cycle with
k\geq 3
. Then define
K_{C_{k},1}
to be the graph by connecting an additional vertex to every vertex of the cycle
C_{k}
. Finally, we consider its subdivision
K_{C_{k},1}^{\text{sub}}
. Note that when
k=3
, the graph
K_{C_{3},1}^{\text{sub}}
is just the graph
K_{4}^{\text{sub}}
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Suppose
k\geq 4
. Then,

(1.4)

\text{ex}(n,K_{C_{k},1}^{\text{sub}})=\Theta(n^{4/3}).

Note that when
k=4
, the graph
K_{C_{4},1}
, also known as
K_{1,2,2}
, has one edge less than the graph
K_{1,1,1,2}
. Before Theorem
1.3
, the only previous result of
\text{ex}(n,K_{1,2,2}^{\text{sub}})
is
O(n^{7/5})
, indicated by
\text{ex}(n,K_{1,1,1,2}^{\text{sub}})
in Theorem 4 of
[
13
]
(a generalization of (
1.2
)). We improve the extremal number to matching upper and lower bounds.

We also remark that in
[
17
]
, Naor and Verstraëte gave an extremal number upper bound of even cycles for bipartite graphs with non-equal parts. Then in
[
14
]
, Jiang, Ma and Yepremyan generalized this idea further to deal with more general theta graphs. These results and their proofs are also relevant in our proof of this theorem.

It is very interesting to ask if the same result holds for
K_{1,2,t}^{\text{sub}}
. However, we suspect that at least for some large
t
, the Turán exponent for
K_{1,2,t}^{\text{sub}}
should be strictly larger than
4/3
. But to ask for a better lower bound for this graph seems to be a very difficult problem.

Next we turn to a tripartite graph
K_{1,s,t}
, with
t\geq s\geq 3
. Clearly, the problem will become even harder. So, our next result is about a particular family of graphs.
Suppose we have a copy of complete bipartite graph
K_{s,t}
, with the two vertex parts
\{y_{1},\ldots,y_{s}\}
and
\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{t}\}
.
For any
i=1,\cdots,s
and
j=1,\cdots,t
, we add a new vertex
b_{ij}
which joins both
y_{i}
and
z_{j}
. This procedure gives a copy of 1-subdivision of the
K_{s,t}
which we write
K_{s,t}^{\text{sub}}
. Then, let a bipartite graph be obtained as follows. Firstly, add a distinct vertex
r
to
K_{s,t}^{\text{sub}}
.
Then, one by one for each of the vertices
y_{1},\cdots,y_{s}
,

,
either we choose one of the vertices

defined above, or we add a new vertex

, or we choose some previously used

, then we join this vertex to it and to

. There are clearly finite ways to obtain such a graph. Finally, define
\mathcal{F}_{1,s,t}
to be the family of all bipartite graphs obtained this way. In particular, the
1
-subdivision of the complete tripartite graph

belongs to this family, and is written as

.

We are ready to state the next result of this paper.

Theorem 1.4
.

For any integers
3\leq s\leq t
,

\text{ex}(n,\mathcal{F}_{1,s,t})=O(n^{1+\frac{s^{2}+1}{2s+2s^{2}}})
.

Note that for all
s\geq 3
,
\frac{s}{2s+2}<\frac{s^{2}+1}{2s+2s^{2}}<\frac{s}{2s+1}
,
and
\frac{s}{2s+1}
was the best previous upper bound for this problem,
given by Janzer in
[
13
]
.
Our result improves this upper bound. We suspect the same upper bound is in fact true for the single interesting graph
K_{1,s,t}^{\text{sub}}
in this family,
that is,

Conjecture 1.5
.

For any integers
3\leq s\leq t
,

(1.5)

\text{ex}(n,K_{1,s,t}^{\text{sub}})=O(n^{1+\frac{s^{2}+1}{2s+2s^{2}}}).

In order to achieve this upper bound for a single graph from the family, more ideas are needed. However, next we will look at

-subdivision of a tripartite subgraph of

, for which the same upper bound can be obtained.

Definition 1.6
.

Let us denote by

.

For any positive integers

\text{ex}(n,(K_{s+1,t}^{+})^{\text{sub}})=O(n^{1+\frac{s^{2}+1}{2s+2s^{2}}}).

2.
Preliminary Considerations

2.1.
Standard Reductions

Hereafter, we only consider
n
to be a sufficiently large integer.

Lemma 2.1
.

(a)

Let
G
be a graph with average degree
d_{G}=2e(G)/v(G)
. Then there exists a subgraph of
G
which is bipartite and every vertex has degree at least
d_{G}/4
. As a direct consequence, if
d_{G}=\omega(1)
, an arbitrary tree with some fixed number of vertices embeds into
G
.

(b)

Let
G
be a bipartite graph with vertex bipartition
A\cup B
, write
d_{A}=e(G)/|A|
and
d_{B}=e(G)/|B|
. Then there exists a subgraph
G^{\prime}
of
G
, on some bipartition
A^{\prime}\cup B^{\prime}
, where
A^{\prime}\subset A
and
B^{\prime}\subset B
, so that each vertex
a\in A^{\prime}
has degree at least
d_{A}/4
and each vertex
b\in B^{\prime}
has degree at least
d_{B}/4
, and
e(G^{\prime})\geq e(G)/2
.

Proof.

The proof of this lemma is sort of folklore. For statement (a), we can color all vertices uniformly at random, with the choice of two colors. The expected number of edges whose two endpoints obtain distinct colors is exactly
e(G)/2
. Therefore, there is one realization of the coloring so that one can keep all such edges. This gives a bipartite graph. Then we delete all the vertices whose degree is at most
d_{G}/4
. The deleting process must end, since each step the average degree keeps increasing. When this process ends, the resulting graph
G^{\prime}
satisfies all the conditions.
In the resulting graph
G^{\prime}
,
in order to embed an arbitrary tree of some fixed number of vertices, a standard greedy embedding procedure is enough.

For statement (b), alternatively for parts
A
and
B
, we delete all the vertices which has degree at most
d_{A}/4
and
d_{B}/4
, respectively, until this procedure cannot be done anymore. This process must end because the average degree for each side is always non-decreasing and when it ends, we have deleted at most
e(G)/2
edges. The conclusion follows.
∎

A graph
G
is called
K
-almost regular
,
if every vertex
v
has degree in the interval

[\delta,K\delta]
, where
\delta
is the minimum degree of the graph
G
.
A bipartite graph with bipartition
A\cup B
is called

balanced
, if
|A|/|B|\in[1/2,2]
.
We will also need the following regularity lemma.

Lemma 2.2
.

[Lemma 2.3 in
[
4
]
]
Let
\alpha<1
be a positive constant.
For
n
sufficiently large,
consider any graph
G
on
n
vertices with
e(G)=\omega(n^{1+\alpha})
.
Then
G
contains a
K
-almost regular balanced bipartite subgraph
G^{\prime}

with
v(G^{\prime})\to\infty
as
n
tends to infinity, and
e(G^{\prime})=\omega(v(G^{\prime})^{1+\alpha})
.

With the above lemma, we usually can simply assume the host graph
G
is
K
-almost regular, balanced bipartite graph on the vertex bipartition
A\cup B
, where
|B|=n
and
|A|=\Theta(n)
, and the degree of every vertex lies in the interval
[\delta,K\delta]
. When
e(G)=\omega(n^{1+\alpha})
, we can further assume
\delta=\omega(n^{\alpha})
.

2.2.
Janzer’s Embedding Scheme for Graph Subdivisions

For
v_{1},\cdots,v_{k}\in A
,
let
N_{G}(v_{1},\cdots,v_{k})\subset B
be the common neighborhood of
v_{1},\cdots,v_{k}
in
G
.

Definition 2.3
.

Fix a graph
H
with
e(H)
edges.
Suppose
G
is a bipartite graph on the bipartition
A\cup B
.
Relative to the graph
H
, in the graph
G
,
define a colored graph
G_{c}(A)
on vertex set
A
.
A pair
(u,v)\in A\times A
forms a
red edge

if
|N_{G}(u,v)|\geq e(H)
.
On the other hand, a pair
(u,v)
forms a
blue edge
if
1\leq|N_{G}(u,v)|\leq e(H)-1
.
Finally, we call
(u,v)
an
uncoloured edge
, if
|N_{G}(u,v)|=0
.

Moreover, in the colored graph
G_{c}(A)
, for any
v\in A
,
let
N_{b}(v)\subset A
denote the blue neighborhood of
v
.
Also denote by
N_{b}(v_{1},\cdots,v_{k})
the common blue neighborhood of
v_{1},\cdots,v_{k}\in A
in the colored graph
G_{c}(A)
.

The following definition provides a visual description of the embedding process.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
H_{c}
be a colored copy of
H
in
G_{c}(A)
. We call
H_{c}
proper, if all the edges of
H_{c}
are blue or red, and
one can choose pairwise distinct common neighbors in
B
for it, to successfully embed a copy of
H^{\text{sub}}
into the graph
G
.

Therefore, the embedding of the graph
H^{\text{sub}}
into the graph
G
becomes a matter of finding a proper copy
H_{c}
of
H
in
A
.

The next lemma guarantees enough blue edges in
G_{c}(A)
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Fix a graph
H
and suppose a bipartite graph
G
on the bipartition
A\cup B
is
H^{\text{sub}}
-free with minimum degree
\delta
for
vertices in
A
.
Let
K_{0}
be a constant which is sufficiently large.
Assume
\delta\big|A\big|/\big|B\big|\geq 2K_{0}
, then there exist at least

\Omega(\delta^{2}|A|^{2}/|B|)
blue edges in
A
.
Consequently,
there exists at least one vertex
x\in A
such that
|N_{b}(x)|=\Omega(\delta^{2}|A|/|B|)
.

Proof.

The proof is essentially the same with Lemma 10 in
[
13
]
. We provide a descriptive proof which might be a good alternative exposition of the original computational proof.

Since
\delta
is the minimum degree for vertices in
A
,
the total edge number of
G
is at least
\delta|A|
which is at least
2K_{0}|B|
. On the other hand, the total number of edges incident to a vertex in
B
with degree at most
K_{0}
, is at most
|B|K_{0}
, no more than half of the total edges.
So, we can only look at vertices in
B
with degree at least
K_{0}
, which provides at least half of the total edges.

On the other hand, for any vertex
v\in B
with
N_{G}(v)\geq K_{0}
, every pair of vertices in
N_{G}(v)
either forms a red edge or a blue edge as
v
is the one of the common neighbors. When
K_{0}
is sufficiently large, we apply Turán’s theorem for the extremal number of the complete graph
K_{v(H)}
.
Therefore, the number of red edges in
N_{G}(v)
does not exceed
e(T_{d_{G}(v),v(H)-1)})=(1-\varepsilon_{0}){d_{G}(v)\choose 2}
for small constant
\varepsilon_{0}
, because otherwise one finds a red copy
H_{c}
of
H
which is proper and therefore
H^{\text{sub}}
embeds, a contradiction. So, there are at least
\Omega\left(d_{G}(v)^{2}\right)

blue edges found in
N_{G}(v)
, for any
v\in B
with degree at least
K_{0}
.
Then, this gives a total count of

(2.1)

\displaystyle\Omega\left(\sum_{d_{G}(v)\geq K_{0},v\in B}d_{G}(v)^{2}\right)\geq\Omega\left(\left(\sum_{d_{G}(v)\geq K_{0},v\in B}d_{G}(v)\right)^{2}/|B|\right)\geq\Omega\left(\delta^{2}|A|^{2}/|B|\right),

where the sum is over all
v\in B
with degree at least
K_{0}
. Note the first estimate is by Cauchy-Schwarz inequality.

Since each blue edge can be counted for at most
e(H)-1
times, because it corresponds to at most
e(H)-1
vertices in
B
, there are
\Omega\left(\delta^{2}|A|^{2}/|B|\right)
blue edges in
G_{c}(A)
.
∎

We summarize one more idea from
[
13
]
as a proposition below.
It is usually used to embed an arbitrarily large number of vertices in the final stage of the embedding.
For a fixed graph
H
and a constant integer
t
, let
K_{H,t}
denote the graph formed by a copy of
H
and an independent
vertex set
T
with
|T|=t
, where each pair
(h,t)\in H\times T
joins an edge.
Below red edges and blue edges in
G_{c}(A)
are defined with respect to the graph

.

Proposition 2.6
.

[See Theorem 4 of
[
13
]
]
Suppose now the bipartite graph
G
as in the previous lemma is
K
-almost regular with minimum degree
\delta
.
Suppose in the colored graph
G_{c}(A)
there is a proper blue copy of
H
, with vertex set
V
.
Assume
that every pair of vertices in
V
forms a non-red edge, and the common blue neighborhood

N_{b}(V)
has size
\omega(\delta)
.
Then, we can find
t
vertices
z_{1},\cdots,z_{t}\in N_{b}(V)
, which together with

V
form a proper blue copy of
K_{H,t}
in
G_{c}(A)
.

Proof.

Suppose
|V|=s
,
Z=N_{b}(V)
and write

V=\{y_{1},\cdots,y_{s}\}\subset A
which is a non-red
s
-clique in
G_{c}(A)
,
i.e., for any pair
(y_{i},y_{j})
,
|N_{G}(y_{i},y_{j})|\leq e(K_{H,t})-1
.
Then we observe the union

(2.2)

B^{\prime}:=\bigcup_{1\leq i<j\leq s}N_{G}(y_{i},y_{j}),

which has size at most

{s\choose 2}\times(e(K_{H,t})-1)
. It follows that the
total number of vertices in
Z
which joins some vertex in
B
is at most
O(\delta)
, because
G
is
K
-almost regular.
Therefore, we can delete these vertices from

and obtain

which still has size
\omega(\delta)
.
For any
z_{1}\in Z_{1}
,
the neighborhood
N_{G}(z_{1})
does not intersect
B^{\prime}
.
It follows that the graph obtained by the embedded

with added edges
between
z_{1}
and every vertex

, is proper. In fact, if it is not proper, it means for some choices of two vertices
y_{i}
and
y_{j}
, the pairs
(z_{1},y_{i})
and
(z_{1},y_{j})
must have some common neighbour, say

. It implies

, which is a contradiction.

In other words, we have found a proper blue copy of
K_{H,1}
in
G_{c}(A)
.
Inductively, we can repeat the above argument until we have embedded
t
vertices
z_{1},\cdots,z_{t}
, which means
we can find a proper blue copy of
K_{H,t}
.
∎

Now we state one result from

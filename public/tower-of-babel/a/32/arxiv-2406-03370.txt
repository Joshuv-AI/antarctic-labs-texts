A representation embedding for algebras of infinite type
R. Bautista, E. Pérez, L. Salmerón

Abstract
We show that for any finite-dimensional algebra Λ \Lambda of infinite representation type, over a perfect field, there is a bounded principal ideal domain Γ \Gamma and a representation embedding from Γ ​ - ​ mod \Gamma\hbox{-}{\rm mod} into Λ ​ - ​ mod \Lambda\hbox{-}{\rm mod} . As an application, we prove a variation of the Brauer-Trall Conjecture II: finite-dimensional algebras of infinite-representation type admit infinite families of non-isomorphic finite-dimensional indecomposables with fixed endolength, for infinitely many endolengths.

1
Introduction

In this note,
k
denotes a perfect field, which will act centrally on every algebra or bimodule we consider
. Given any
k
-algebra
\Lambda
, we denote by
\Lambda\hbox{-}{\rm Mod}
(resp.
\Lambda\hbox{-}{\rm mod}
) the category of left
\Lambda
-modules (resp. finite-dimensional left
\Lambda
-modules). We denote by
\Lambda\hbox{-}{\rm mod}_{\cal I}
the full subcategory of
\Lambda\hbox{-}{\rm mod}

formed by the modules without injective direct summands.

Recall that the
endolength
of a
\Lambda
-module
M
, denoted by

\hbox{\rm endol}\,(M)
, is by definition the length of the right
{\rm End}_{\Lambda}(M)^{op}
-module
M
. We say that
\Lambda
is
discrete
(resp.

e
-discrete
) iff for each dimension
d
(resp. each endolength
d
) there is only a finite number of distinct isoclasses of indecomposable finite-dimensional

\Lambda
-modules
M
with
\dim_{k}M=d
(resp. with
\hbox{\rm endol}\,(M)=d
).

In this article, we focus our attention on finite-dimensional algebras
\Lambda
with infinite representation type. Since the ground field is perfect, this is equivalent
to not being
e
-discrete, see
[
2
]
.
So, for such an algebra
\Lambda

there is at least one number
d\in\mathbb{N}
and an infinite family of non-isomorphic indecomposable finite-dimensional
\Lambda
-modules with endolength
d
.

Definition 1.1
.

Given two
k
-algebras
\Gamma
and
\Lambda
,
a
representation embedding from
\Gamma\hbox{-}{\rm mod}
to
\Lambda\hbox{-}{\rm mod}
is a
k
-linear
functor
H:\Gamma\hbox{-}{\rm mod}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}\Lambda\hbox{-}{\rm mod}
which is exact, preserves indecomposability, and reflects isomorphism classes.

Definition 1.2
.

Given full subcategories
{\cal C}
of
\Gamma\hbox{-}{\rm mod}
and
{\cal C^{\prime}}
of
\Lambda\hbox{-}{\rm mod}
we will say that a functor
H:{\cal C}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}{\cal C}^{\prime}

controls endolength of indecomposables
iff there are some constants
c,c^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\hbox{\rm endol}\,(N)\leq c\times\hbox{\rm endol}\,(H(N))\hbox{ \ and \ }\hbox{\rm endol}\,(H(N))\leq c^{\prime}\times\hbox{\rm endol}\,(N),

for any indecomposable
N\in{\cal C}
.

Definition 1.3
.

A
k
-algebra
{\mathtt{Q}}
is called
minimal
iff it is of one of the following two types:

1.

{\mathtt{Q}}=T_{D_{1}\times D_{2}}(V)
, where
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
are finite-dimensional division
k
-algebras and
V
is a
simple
D_{1}\hbox{-}D_{2}
-bimodule.

2.

{\mathtt{Q}}=T_{D}(V)
, where
D
is a finite-dimensional division
k
-algebra and
V
is a simple

D\hbox{-}D
-bimodule.

The
coefficient algebras
of such a minimal algebra
{\mathtt{Q}}
are
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
in the first case, and
D
in the second one.

The
coefficient algebras
of a finite-dimensional algebra
\Lambda
are, by definition, the opposites of the endomorphism algebras of the simple
\Lambda
-modules.
In this note we prove the following result.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\Lambda
be a finite-dimensional
k
-algebra of infinite representation type. Then, there is a functor
which preserves indecomposables, reflects isomorphism classes, and controls endolength of indecomposables of one of the following two types:

1.

H:{\mathtt{Q}}\hbox{-}{\rm mod}_{\cal I}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}\Lambda\hbox{-}{\rm mod}
, where
{\mathtt{Q}}
is a minimal algebra of the first type.

2.

H:{\mathtt{Q}}\hbox{-}{\rm mod}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}\Lambda\hbox{-}{\rm mod}
, where
{\mathtt{Q}}
is a minimal algebra of the second type.

In both cases, the algebra
{\mathtt{Q}}
is of infinite representation type and
with coefficient algebras some of the coefficient algebras of
\Lambda
. Moreover, the functor
H
is of the form
Z\otimes_{\mathtt{Q}}-
, for some
\Lambda\hbox{-}{\mathtt{Q}}
-bimodule
Z
which is finitely generated by the right.

From this, we derive:

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\Lambda
be a finite-dimensional
k
-algebra of infinite representation type. Then, there is a bounded principal ideal domain
\Gamma
which is not
e
-discrete and a functor

G:\Gamma\hbox{-}{\rm mod}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}\Lambda\hbox{-}{\rm mod},

which preserves indecomposables, reflects isomorphism classes, and controls endolength of indecomposables. The functor
G
is of the form
Z\otimes_{\Gamma}-
, for some
\Lambda\hbox{-}\Gamma
-bimodule
Z
which is finitely generated by the right.

Then, we will show:

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
\Lambda
be a finite-dimensional
k
-algebra of infinite representation type. Then, there is a bounded principal ideal domain
\Gamma
which is not
e
-discrete and a
representation embedding which controls endolength of indecomposables

G:\Gamma\hbox{-}{\rm mod}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}\Lambda\hbox{-}{\rm mod}.

Moreover, the functor
G
is of the form
Z\otimes_{\Gamma}-
, for some
\Lambda\hbox{-}\Gamma
-bimodule
Z
which is free of finite rank by the right.

As an application of the preceding theorem, we will prove the following variation of “the second Brauer-Thrall conjecture” involving endolength.

Definition 1.7
.

We say that a
k
-algebra
\Sigma

satisfies EBTII
if there is an infinite sequence of natural numbers
d_{1}<d_{2}<\cdots
such that for each one of these numbers
d_{i}
there is an infinite family
\{M_{j}\}_{j}
of non-isomorphic finite-dimensional indecomposable
\Sigma
-modules with
\hbox{\rm endol}\,(M_{j})=d_{i}
.

Theorem 1.8
.

Any finite-dimensional algebra
\Lambda
of infinite representation type satisfies EBTII.

This result is a consequence of the following. From (
1.6
), we have functors
G:\Gamma\hbox{-}{\rm mod}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}\Lambda\hbox{-}{\rm mod}
which
preserve indecomposables, reflect isomorphism classes, and control endolength of indecomposables, they preserve the property EBTII, by (
7.6
). These algebras
\Gamma
satisfy EBTII, as we shall verify in §
7
.

In view of the previous theorem, it is very natural to ask whether
EBTII holds for a wider class of representation-infinite artin algebras. The problem is open even in the case of finite-dimensional algebras over a non-perfect fields.

The proof of (
1.4
) relies on the theory of differential tensor algebras
(ditalgebras) and reduction functors first developed by the Kiev School of representation theory, see
[
18
]
and
[
16
]
(see also
[
11
]
). For the general
background on ditalgebras and their module categories, we refer the reader to
[
5
]
. The proofs of the results presented here are largely based on our previous work
[
3
]
.

As in
[
13
]
, we study the phenomenon of existence of an infinite family of indecomposables in
\Lambda\hbox{-}{\rm mod}
with fixed endolength by reducing with matrix problems methods to the case of hereditary algebras. In
[
13
]
, Crawley-Boevey uses the more
general techniques of lift pairs which apply to general artin algebras.
Here, as in
[
3
]
, we restrict ourselves to the case of finite-dimensional algebras over perfect fields and use bocses techniques which permit us to exploit the properties of reduction functors.

Representation embeddings have been of great interest in representation theory of algebras, see for instance
[
7
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
12
]
, or
[
20
]
. Suffice it to mention that the definition of wildness requires the existence of such an embedding from
k\langle x,y\rangle\hbox{-}{\rm mod}
. This work was inspired by Bongartz article
[
6
]
, which, under the assumption that the ground field is algebraically closed, shows that for any
finite-dimensional algebra
\Lambda
of infinite representation type, there is a representation embedding
k[x]\hbox{-}{\rm mod}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}\Lambda\hbox{-}{\rm mod}
. Here we show that some version of this holds in the more general situation of perfect fields.

Strategy for the proof of theorem (
1.4
)

We recall from
[
5
]
and
[
3
]
, some basic definitions.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
{\cal A}
be a layered ditalgebra, with layer
(R,W)
, see
[
5
, §4]
. Given

M\in{\cal A}\hbox{-}{\rm Mod}
, denote by
E_{M}:={\rm End}_{\cal A}(M)^{op}
its endomorphism algebra. Then,
M
admits a
structure of
R\hbox{-}E_{M}
-bimodule, where
m\cdot(f^{0},f^{1})=f^{0}(m)
, for
m\in M
and
(f^{0},f^{1})\in E_{M}
. By
definition,
the endolength of
M
, denoted by
\hbox{\rm endol}\,(M)
, is the length of
M
as a right
E_{M}
-module.

As in the case of algebras, the ditalgebra
{\cal A}
is called
e
-discrete
iff, for each endolength
d
, there are only a finite number of distinct isoclasses of finite-dimensional indecomposable
{\cal A}
-modules with endolength
d
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
{\cal A}=(T,\delta)
be a triangular ditalgebra, with layer

(R,W)
, over the field
k
.
Then,
{\cal A}
is called
admissible
iff
R\cong D_{1}\times\cdots\times D_{n}
, for some finite-dimensional division
k
-algebras

D_{1},\ldots,D_{n}
, and
W
is finitely generated as an
R\hbox{-}R
-bimodule.
The coefficient algebras of
{\cal A}
are the algebras
D_{1},\dots,D_{n}
.

Since our ground field

is perfect, any finite-dimensional
k
-algebra
\Lambda
splits over its radical,
thus
\Lambda=S\oplus J
, where
J
is the radical of
\Lambda
. Then, we can consider the Drozd’s ditalgebra

{\cal D}
of
\Lambda
, see
[
5
, (19.1)]
, which is an admissible ditalgebra if and only if
\Lambda
is basic. In this case, the coefficient algebras of
{\cal D}
coincide with those of
\Lambda
.

For the proof of our theorem (
1.4
), we can assume that
\Lambda
is basic, because Morita equivalences are representation embeddings which preserve coefficient algebras and control endolength of indecomposables. The composition of representations embeddings is a representation embedding, see
[
6
]
.

Remark 2.3
.

If
\Lambda
has infinite representation type, as we mentioned before, it is not
e
-discrete, so there is an fixed endolength
d
and an infinite family
\{M_{i}\}_{i\in I}
of finite-dimensional non-isomorphic indecomposable
\Lambda
-modules with
\hbox{\rm endol}\,(M_{i})=d
.

Recall that we have the cokernel functor
{\rm Cok}:{\cal P}(\Lambda)\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}\Lambda\hbox{-}{\rm mod}
and the equivalence
\Xi_{\Lambda}:{\cal D}\hbox{-}{\rm mod}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}{\cal P}^{1}(\Lambda)
, studied for instance in
[
5
, §18]
, whose composition functor permits to transfer many properties from the category
{\cal D}\hbox{-}{\rm mod}
to
\Lambda\hbox{-}{\rm mod}
. Then, for instance by
[
5
, (18.10)(4)]
, there is a family of non-isomorphic indecomposable objects
\{X_{i}\}_{i\in I}
in
{\cal P}^{2}(\Lambda)
such that
{\rm Cok}(X_{i})\cong M_{i}
, for all
i\in I
. So, for each
i\in I
, we can choose
N_{i}\in{\cal D}\hbox{-}{\rm mod}
with
\Xi_{\Lambda}(N_{i})\cong X_{i}
. Thus,
{\rm Cok}\Xi_{\Lambda}(N_{i})\cong M_{i}
and,
from
[
3
, (4.4)]
, there is a natural number
d_{0}
and an infinite subset
I_{0}\subseteq I
such that
\{N_{i}\}_{i\in I_{0}}
is an infinite family of finite-dimensional non-isomorphic indecomposable
{\cal D}
-modules with
\hbox{\rm endol}\,(N_{i})=d_{0}
. So we have that
{\cal D}
is not
e
-discrete.

The strategy of the proof of our theorem (
1.4
) includes first the proof of the following statement for admissible ditalgebras.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
{\cal A}
be an admissible
k
-ditalgebra which is not
e
-discrete. Then, there is a minimal algebra
{\mathtt{Q}}
of infinite representation type and a functor
F:{\mathtt{Q}}\hbox{-}{\rm mod}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}{\cal A}\hbox{-}{\rm mod}
which preserves indecomposables and reflects isomorphism classes. Moreover, the coefficient algebras of
{\mathtt{Q}}
are some of those of
{\cal A}
.

This theorem will be proved by a reduction procedure. Then, we apply it to the Drozd’s ditalgebra
{\cal D}
of
\Lambda
and consider the composition of the functor
F
provided by (
2.4
) with
{\rm Cok}\Xi_{\Lambda}:{\cal D}\hbox{-}{\rm mod}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}\Lambda\hbox{-}{\rm mod}
to obtain the functor we are looking for.

The proof of the last theorem will be done by induction using the notion of
e
-norm of indecomposable finite-dimensional modules discussed in the following.

3
Norms and reduction functors

Suppose that
(R,W)
is the triangular layer of an admissible ditalgebra
{\cal A}
. Assume that
1=\sum_{i=1}^{n}e_{i}
is the decomposition of the unit of
R
as a sum of central primitive orthogonal idempotents. Thus, with the notation of (
2.2
), we have the finite-dimensional division
k
-algebra
D_{i}=e_{i}D_{i}e_{i}
, for
i\in[1,n]
.

In the discussion of
[
3
]
the following notion of norm is relevant.

Definition 3.1
.

Assume that
{\cal A}
is an admissible
k
-ditalgebra with layer
(R,W)
. Then,
for
M\in{\cal A}\hbox{-}{\rm mod}
, its
norm
is the number

||M||=\dim_{k}{\rm Hom}_{R}(W_{0}\otimes_{R}M,M).

Then, the

length of
M
is
\ell_{R}(M)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\ell_{D_{i}}(e_{i}M)
. The
support of
M
is the set of
idempotents
e_{i}
with
e_{i}M\not=0
. The
{\cal A}
-module

M

is called
sincere
if
e_{i}M\not=0
, for all
i\in[1,n]
.

For
M\in{\cal A}\hbox{-}{\rm mod}
, we have

||M||=\sum_{i,j}\dim_{k}(e_{i}W_{0}e_{j})\ell_{D_{i}}(e_{i}M)\ell_{D_{j}}(e_{j}M).

In this note, when dealing with finite-dimensional indecomposable
{\cal A}
-modules with finite endolength, we will use the following modified norm.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
M
be a
k
-finite-dimensional indecomposable
{\cal A}
-module and denote by
E_{M}={\rm End}_{\cal A}(M)^{op}
. Then, the right
E_{M}
-module
M

has the direct sum decomposition
M=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}e_{i}M
, of right
E_{M}
-modules. We get

\hbox{\rm endol}\,(M)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\ell_{E_{M}}(e_{i}M).

From
[
5
, (5.6)]
,
{\cal A}
is a Roiter ditalgebra and, from
[
5
, (5.12)]
, we know that
E_{M}
is a local finite-dimensional
k
-algebra.
Since the field
k
is perfect, there is a division
k
-subalgebra
D_{M}
of
E_{M}
and a
D_{M}\hbox{-}D_{M}
-bimodule decomposition

E_{M}=D_{M}\bigoplus{\rm rad}E_{M}
.

So every finite-length right
E_{M}
-module
N
is a finite-dimensional right
D_{M}
-vector space and we have
\ell_{E_{M}}(N)=\ell_{D_{M}}(N)
. So,

\hbox{\rm endol}\,(M)=\ell_{D_{M}}(M)
and
\ell_{E_{M}}(e_{i}M)=\ell_{D_{M}}(e_{i}M)
, for
i\in[1,n]
. Hence,

\hbox{\rm endol}\,(M)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\ell_{D_{M}}(e_{i}M).

Let
c=c_{\cal A}
be the least common multiple of
d_{1},d_{2},\ldots,d_{n}
, where
d_{i}=\dim_{k}D_{i}
. So, for each
i
, there is a natural number
c_{i}
with
c=c_{i}d_{i}
. Then, define the
e
-norm
of
M
by

||M||_{e}:=\sum_{i,j}c_{i}c_{j}\dim_{k}(e_{i}W_{0}e_{j})\ell_{D_{M}}(e_{i}M)\ell_{D_{M}}(e_{j}M).

Remark 3.3
.

From this definition, we get that
||M||_{e}\leq c^{2}\dim_{k}(W_{0})\hbox{\rm endol}\,(M)^{2}
. So if there is an infinite family of non-isomorphic finite-dimensional indecomposable
{\cal A}
-modules with endolength bounded by
q
, then their
e
-norms are bounded by
c^{2}\dim_{k}(W_{0})q^{2}
.

If
{\cal A}
is any admissible ditalgebra and
q\in\mathbb{N}
, we say that
{\cal A}
is of
infinite
q
-type
iff
{\cal A}
admits an infinite family of non-isomorphic finite-dimensional indecomposable
{\cal A}
-modules
M
with
e
-norm
||M||_{e}=q
.

So, if
{\cal A}
is not
e
-discrete, it is of infinite
q
-type for some
q\in\mathbb{N}
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Keep the notations of (
3.1
) and (
3.2
), for a given finite-dimensional indecomposable
{\cal A}
-module
M
. Moreover, make
d_{M}=\dim_{k}D_{M}
, then we have:

||M||_{e}=\frac{c_{\cal A}^{2}}{d_{M}^{2}}||M||.

Proof.

Recall that
||M||=\sum_{i,j}\dim_{k}(e_{i}W_{0}e_{j})\ell_{D_{i}}(e_{i}M)\ell_{D_{j}}(e_{j}M)
and notice that, for
each
i\in[1,n]
, we have

c\ell_{D_{i}}(e_{i}M)=c_{i}d_{i}\ell_{D_{i}}(e_{i}M)=c_{i}\dim_{k}e_{i}M=c_{i}d_{M}\ell_{D_{M}}(e_{i}M).

∎

In the next statement we describe the behavior of the reduction functors towards the

-norm of finite-dimensional indecomposables.
We say that an algebra

Lemma 3.5
.

Assume that
{\cal A}
is an admissible
k
-ditalgebra with layer
(R,W)
. Suppose that

and
{\cal A}^{z}
is the admissible ditalgebra obtained from
{\cal A}
by one of the basic reductions: deletion of an idempotent (case

, as in
[

, (8.17)]
), regularization (case

, as in
[

, (8.19)]
), or reduction by a

-module

, where

is an initial subalgebra of
{\cal A}
and

is a finite direct sum of pairwise non-isomorphic finite-dimensional indecomposable

-modules
X_{1},\ldots,X_{t}
with
{\rm End}_{B}(X_{i})^{op}/{\rm rad}{\rm End}_{B}(X_{i})^{op}
isomorphic to some of the coefficient algebras of
{\cal A}
(case

, a particular case of
[
3
, (7.3)]
). Consider also the associated full and faithful functor

F^{z}:{\cal A}^{z}\hbox{-}{\rm mod}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}{\cal A}\hbox{-}{\rm mod}
. Then, the following holds for any finite-dimensional indecomposable
N\in{\cal A}^{z}\hbox{-}{\rm mod}
.

1.

||N||_{e}\leq||F^{d}(N)||_{e}
.

2.

||N||_{e}<||F^{r}(N)||_{e}
, whenever
F^{r}(N)
is sincere.

3.

||N||_{e}<||F^{X}(N)||_{e}
, whenever
F^{X}(N)
is sincere and
W^{\prime}_{0}\not=0
.

Proof.

Recall that the layer
(R,W)
of the admissible ditalgebra
{\cal A}
is such that
R=D_{1}\times\cdots\times D_{n}
as in (
2.2
).

In order to show 1, for simplicity, assume that the idempotent to be deleted is

, then the layer
(R^{d},W^{d})
of

is such that

, so clearly

and, since

. Moreover, since

For 2, we notice that the layer
(R^{r},W^{r})
of

is such that

, so

. Again, since

is full and faithful, we get

. Moreover, since
||N||<||F^{r}(N)||
, whenever
F^{r}(N)
is sincere, as before, we have

||N||_{e}=\frac{c^{2}_{{\cal A}^{r}}}{d^{2}_{N}}||N||<\frac{c^{2}_{\cal A}}{d^{2}_{F^{r}(N)}}||F^{r}(N)||=||F^{r}(N)||_{e}.

In the last case, the assumption implies that
c_{{\cal A}^{X}}\leq c_{\cal A}
, and we can proceed as before, using that for

sincere we have
||N||<||F^{X}(N)||
, see
[
3

.
∎

4
Reduction to minimal algebras

In this section, we prove the following theorem (
4.8
), which is a stronger version of (
2.4
).
Its statement and proof use the following notions and preliminary arguments.
As in the case of algebras, we say that a functor
F:{\cal A}^{\prime}\hbox{-}{\rm mod}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}{\cal A}\hbox{-}{\rm mod}
, where
{\cal A}^{\prime}
and
{\cal A}
are layered ditalgebras,
controls endolength of indecomposables
if there are some
c,c^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\hbox{\rm endol}\,(N)\leq c\times\hbox{\rm endol}\,(F(N))
and
\hbox{\rm endol}\,(F(N))\leq c^{\prime}\times\hbox{\rm endol}\,(N)
, for all indecomposables
N\in{\cal A}^{\prime}\hbox{-}{\rm mod}
.

Recall that given any ditalgebra,
{\cal A}=(T,\delta)
, there is a canonical embedding functor
L_{\cal A}:A\hbox{-}{\rm mod}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}{\cal A}\hbox{-}{\rm mod}
, where
A
is the subalgebra of
T
formed by the homogeneous elements of degree zero. It maps every
A
-module to itself and each morphism
f\in{\rm Hom}_{A}(M,N)
is mapped onto
(f,0)\in{\rm Hom}_{\cal A}(M,N)
, see
[
5
, §2]
. Clearly, the functor
L_{\cal A}
reflects indecomposability.

Definition 4.1
.

We say that an admissible ditalgebra

is such that

Remark 4.2
.

Quasi-minimal ditalgebras
{\cal Q}=(T,\delta)
have the following important feature. If
Q
denotes the subalgebra of
T
formed by the homogeneous elements of degree 0, we have the corresponding canonical embedding functor
L_{\cal Q}:Q\hbox{-}{\rm mod}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}{\cal Q}\hbox{-}{\rm mod}
. Consider the minimal algebra
{\mathtt{Q}}
of
{\cal Q}
, the projection morphism of algebras
\pi:Q=T_{R}(W)\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}T_{R}(W^{\prime}_{0})={\mathtt{Q}}
, and the corresponding
extension functor

E:{\mathtt{Q}}\hbox{-}{\rm Mod}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}Q\hbox{-}{\rm Mod}
, which is given by scalar restriction through
\pi
, thus
E\cong E({\mathtt{Q}})\otimes_{{\mathtt{Q}}}-
. Then, since
\delta(W^{\prime}_{0})=0
and
W^{\prime\prime}_{0}E(N)=0
, for any finite-dimensional indecomposable
{\mathtt{Q}}
-module
N
, we have

\mathbb{E}:={\rm End}_{{\cal Q}}(L_{\cal Q}E(N))^{op}=\mathbb{E}_{N}^{0}\oplus\mathbb{E}_{N}^{1},

where
\mathbb{E}_{N}^{0}
and
\mathbb{E}_{N}^{1}
are the subspaces of
\mathbb{E}
formed, respectively, by the endomorphisms of
E(N)
of the form
(f^{0},0)
, and by the endomorphisms of
E(N)
of the form
(0,f^{1})
. Moreover,
\mathbb{E}_{N}^{0}=L_{\cal Q}E({\rm End}_{{\mathtt{Q}}}(N)^{op})
.

Indeed, given any
g^{0}\in{\rm End}_{{\mathtt{Q}}}(N)^{op}
, from the equality
W^{\prime\prime}_{0}E(N)=0
, we have
g^{0}(wm)=0=wg^{0}(m)
, for all
w\in W^{\prime\prime}_{0}
and
m\in E(N)
. Since
Q
is generated by
R
,
W^{\prime}_{0}
, and
W^{\prime\prime}_{0}
, we have that
g^{0}\in{\rm End}_{Q}(E(N))^{op}
, so
L_{\cal Q}E(g^{0})=(g^{0},0)\in\mathbb{E}_{N}^{0}
. Moreover,
given
f=(f^{0},f^{1})\in\mathbb{E}
, the equality
\delta(W^{\prime}_{0})=0
implies that
f^{0}\in{\rm End}_{{\mathtt{Q}}}(N)^{op}
. Thus,
(f^{0},0)\in\mathbb{E}^{0}_{N}
,
(0,f^{1})=(f^{0},f^{1})-(f^{0},0)\in\mathbb{E}
, and
\mathbb{E}_{N}^{0}=L_{\cal Q}E({\rm End}_{{\mathtt{Q}}}(N)^{op})
.

Our ditalgebra
{\cal Q}
is triangular, so, from
[
5
, (5.4)]
,
\mathbb{E}^{1}_{N}
is a nilpotent ideal of
\mathbb{E}
, so we know that
\mathbb{E}_{N}^{1}\subseteq{\rm Rad}\mathbb{E}
.
Since
k
is perfect, we also have

{\rm End}_{{\mathtt{Q}}}(N)^{op}=K_{N}\oplus{\rm Rad}({\rm End}_{{\mathtt{Q}}}(N)^{op})
,
where
K_{N}
is a division algebra. From the preceding facts, we obtain the equality

{\rm End}_{{\cal Q}}(L_{\cal Q}E(N))^{op}=L_{\cal Q}E(K_{N})\oplus{\rm Rad}({\rm End}_{{\cal Q}}(L_{\cal Q}E(N))^{op}).

Thus, the functor
{\mathtt{L}}_{\mathtt{Q}}:=L_{\cal Q}E:{\mathtt{Q}}\hbox{-}{\rm mod}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}{\cal Q}\hbox{-}{\rm mod}
preserves indecomposables. It reflects isomorphism classes because,
{\cal Q}
is a Roiter ditalgebra, see
[
5
, (5.8) and (5.6)]
. We will keep the preceding notation whenever we deal with quasi-minimal ditalgebras.

We have the following elementary statement relating dimension and endolength.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\Sigma
be any
k
-algebra (not necessarily finite-dimensional) and
M
an indecomposable finite-dimensional

\Sigma
-module. Consider the local algebra
E:={\rm End}_{\Sigma}(M)^{op}
and the division algebra
K_{M}:=E/{\rm Rad}E
. Then, we have

\hbox{\rm endol}\,(M)=\dim_{k}M/\dim_{k}K_{M}.

Proof.

Since
M
is finite-dimensional, its length as an
E
-module is finite. Consider a composition series
0=M_{0}\subset M_{1}\subset\cdots\subset M_{l}=M

for the right
E
-module
M
. Then, each simple
E
-module
M_{i}/M_{i-1}
is a simple
K_{M}
-module: a one-dimensional
K_{M}
-vector space. So, we have
\dim_{k}(M_{i}/M_{i-1})=\dim_{k}K_{M}
, for each
i
. Thus,

\dim_{k}M=l\dim_{k}K_{M}
. Our statement follows from this.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

Similarly, given any admissible ditalgebra
{\cal A}
and an indecomposable
M\in{\cal A}\hbox{-}{\rm mod}
, if we consider the local algebra
E:={\rm End}_{\cal A}(M)^{op}
and the division algebra
K_{M}:=E/{\rm Rad}E
, we have

\hbox{\rm endol}\,(M)=\dim_{k}M/\dim_{k}K_{M}
.

Given a quasi-minimal ditalgebra
{\cal Q}
, with the notation of (
4.2
), given an indecomposable finite-dimensional
{\mathtt{Q}}
-module
M
,
since the functor
{\mathtt{L}}_{{\mathtt{Q}}}
preserves dimension and
K_{M}\cong{\mathtt{L}}_{{\mathtt{Q}}}(K_{M})\cong K_{{\mathtt{L}}_{{\mathtt{Q}}}(M)}
, we obtain from (
4.2
) and (
4.3
) that
\hbox{\rm endol}\,({\mathtt{L}}_{{\mathtt{Q}}}(M))=\dim_{k}{\mathtt{L}}_{{\mathtt{Q}}}(M)/\dim_{k}K_{{\mathtt{L}}_{{\mathtt{Q}}}(M)}=\dim_{k}M/\dim_{k}K_{M}=\hbox{\rm endol}\,(M)
. Thus,
{\mathtt{L}}_{{\mathtt{Q}}}:{\mathtt{Q}}\hbox{-}{\rm mod}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}{\cal Q}\hbox{-}{\rm mod}
preserves endolength of indecomposable modules.

Definition 4.5
.

Let
{\cal C}
and
{\cal C^{\prime}}
be full subcategories of finite-dimensional modules over some algebras or some ditalgebras. Then, we say that a functor
F:{\cal C}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}{\cal C}^{\prime}

controls dimension
iff there are constants
c,c^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\dim_{k}M\leq c\times\dim_{k}FM\hbox{ \ and \ }\dim_{k}FM\leq c^{\prime}\times\dim_{k}M,

for all
M\in{\cal C}
.

Remark 4.6
.

From (
4.3
), we know that
any functor
F:{\cal C}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}{\cal C}^{\prime}
which controls dimension and induces isomorphisms
K_{M}\cong K_{FM}
, for all indecomposable
M
, controls endolength of indecomposables.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
{\cal A}
be an admissible ditalgebra. Assume that
{\cal A}^{X}
is the layered ditalgebra obtained from
{\cal A}
by reduction, using the
B
-module
X
, where
B
is an initial subalgebra of
{\cal A}
and
X
is a finite direct sum of non-isomorphic finite-dimensional indecomposable

-modules. Assume that

is a complete admissible triangular

-module and let

be the associated functor, as in

Proof.

The finite-dimensional algebra

admits the splitting

, where

is the radical of

From

5
Reduction to principal ideal domains

In this work, we use the term uniserial subcategory in the following sense.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
{\cal U}
be a full abelian subcategory of the category
\Sigma\hbox{-}{\rm mod}
of finite-dimensional modules for some
k
-algebra
\Sigma
. Then
{\cal U}
is called
a uniserial subcategory of
\Sigma\hbox{-}{\rm mod}
iff every indecomposable module in
{\cal U}
has a unique composition series in
{\cal U}
, and all the simple factors in this composition series are isomorphic to one simple object of the category
{\cal U}
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\Gamma
be a bounded principal ideal domain and let
{\cal P}
be a complete set of representatives of the non-similar atoms of
\Gamma
. Then, the category of finite-length
\Gamma
-modules is a direct sum of uniserial subcategories

\{{\cal U}_{p}\}_{p\in{\cal P}}
, where each
{\cal U}_{p}
contains a unique simple object
E_{1}^{p}
.

Moreover, we have almost split sequences of the form:

\begin{matrix}\xi_{1}:\ E^{p}_{1}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}E^{p}_{2}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}E^{p}_{1},\\
\,\\
\zeta_{n}:\ E^{p}_{n}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}E^{p}_{n+1}\oplus E^{p}_{n-1}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}E^{p}_{n},\hbox{ for }n\geq 2.\\
\end{matrix}

Proof.

The category of finite-length
\Gamma
-modules is well known. For instance, from
[
3
, (6.5)]
, we have the following.
For each atom
p\in\Gamma
, consider the corresponding simple
\Gamma
-module
S_{p}:=\Gamma/\Gamma p
. Then, for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
, up to isomorphism, there is a unique indecomposable
\Gamma
-module
E_{i}^{p}
with length
i
and all composition factors isomorphic to
S_{p}
. The family
\{E_{i}^{p}\mid i\in\mathbb{N},p\in{\cal P}\}
is a complete set of representatives of the isoclasses of the indecomposable
\Gamma
-modules of finite length. They are all finite-dimensional.

For
p\in{\cal P}
, consider the full subcategory
{\cal U}_{p}
of
\Gamma\hbox{-}{\rm mod}
formed by the modules
M\in\Gamma\hbox{-}{\rm mod}
which admit a filtration

0=M_{0}\subset M_{1}\subset\cdots\subset M_{n-1}\subset M_{n}=M,

where
M_{j}/M_{j-1}\cong S_{p}
, for all
j\in[1,n]
. Thus,
\{E_{i}^{p}\mid i\in\mathbb{N}\}
are the representatives of the indecomposables in
{\cal U}_{p}
. Moreover, in the proof of
[
3
, (6.5)]
, it is shown that
{\rm Hom}_{\Gamma}(E_{i}^{p},E_{j}^{q})=0
, whenever
p
and
q
are not similar atoms, that is whenever
p\not=q
in
{\cal P}
.

It remains to show that
{\cal U}_{p}
is uniserial. Let
M
be an indecomposable
\Gamma
-module in
{\cal U}_{p}
. Then, it has a composition series

0=M_{0}\subset M_{1}\subset\cdots\subset M_{n-1}\subset M_{n}=M,

where
M_{i}/M_{i-1}\cong S_{p}
.
Here
\ell_{\Gamma}(M_{i})=i
, for all
i\in[1,n]
.

We will show that
M_{j}
is the unique submodule of
M
with length
j
.

We proceed by induction on the length
n=\ell_{\Gamma}(M)
.

If
n=1
, we have that
M=M_{1}
is simple and our claim is obvious.
Assume that our claim holds for indecomposable modules in
{\cal U}_{p}
with length
s
such that
s<n
.
We have an exact sequence

0\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}M_{n-1}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits^{f}}M_{n}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits^{g}}S_{p}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}0.

Let
S
be a simple submodule of
M
and
h:S\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}M_{n}
the corresponding inclusion. Since
M
is indecomposable, we know that
gh:S\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}S_{p}
is not an isomorphism, so
gh=0
and
S\subseteq M_{n-1}
. Similarly, working with the given filtration of
M_{n-1}
, we show that
S\subseteq M_{n-2}
. In a finite number of steps, we get
S\subseteq M_{1}
. Hence
S=M_{1}
. We have verified that the socle of
M
is the simple
M_{1}
.

Consider now a submodule
N
of
M_{n}
with length
j>1
. Since
{\rm soc}(M)=M_{1}
, we know that
M_{1}={\rm soc}(N)\subset N
. In particular, the submodules
M_{1},\ldots,M_{n-1}
are all indecomposable. We have the quotient
\eta:M_{n}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}M_{n}/M_{1}
,
and the submodules
\eta(M_{j})=M_{j}/M_{1}
and
\eta(N)=N/M_{1}
with length
j-1
of the module
M/M_{1}\in{\cal U}_{p}
, which has length
n-1
.

From
[
3
, (6.5)]
, we know there is an irreducible morphism
g:M_{n}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}M_{n-1}
in
\Gamma\hbox{-}{\rm mod}
, which must be surjective. Its kernel has length 1, so it is a simple submodule of
M_{n}
, and it coincides with
M_{1}
. It follows that
M/M_{1}\cong M_{n-1}
is indecomposable.
Then, by induction hypothesis

N/M_{1}=M_{j}/M_{1}
and, so,
M_{j}=N
, as we wanted to verify.
∎

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
Q
be a finite-dimensional hereditary algebra of infinite representation type. Then, its preprojective components, as well as its preinjective components, are
e
-discrete.

Proof.

We only consider the case of a preprojective component
{\cal P}
, the other case is tackled dually. If
M\in{\cal P}
, there is a projective module
P_{M}\in{\cal P}
and a non-negative number
\nu(M)
, such that
(DTr)^{\nu(M)}M=P_{M}
. We also know, that
{\rm End}_{Q}(M)\cong{\rm End}_{Q}(P_{M})
is a division algebra, see
[
1
, VIII.1.(1.5)]
. Therefore, from (
4.3
), we obtain
\hbox{\rm endol}\,(M)=\dim_{k}M/\dim_{k}{\rm End}_{Q}(P_{M})
. This implies that there is only a finite number of indecomposables in
{\cal P}
with the same endolength iff there is only a finite number of indecomposables in
{\cal P}
with the same dimension. That is
{\cal P}
is
e
-discrete iff it is discrete.

So, we have to show that there are only finitely many indecomposables in
{\cal P}
with the same dimension. For this, we can assume that
Q
is basic and consider a decomposition of the unit
1=\sum_{i=1}^{n}e_{i}
of
Q
, as a sum of primitive orthogonal idempotents, denote by
\underline{\dim}(M)=(\dim_{k}e_{1}M,\ldots,\dim_{k}e_{n}M)
,
the dimension vector of

M
. Recall that given
M,N\in{\cal P}
, we have that
M\cong N
iff their their dimension vectors coincide, see
[
1
, VIII.2.(2.3)]
. Then, consider the homological quadratic form
q
of the hereditary algebra
Q
, which satisfies

q(\underline{\dim}(M))=\dim_{k}{\rm End}_{Q}(M)-\dim_{k}{\rm Ext}_{Q}^{1}(M,M).

Since
M
is preprojective, we have
{\rm Ext}_{Q}^{1}(M,M)=0
, see
[
1
, VIII.1.(1.7)]
. Therefore, we get that for any
M\in{\cal P}
,
q(\underline{\dim}(M))\in\{d_{1},\ldots,d_{n}\}
, where
d_{i}:=\dim_{k}{\rm End}_{Q}(Qe_{i})
, for
i\in[1,n]
. There are only finitely many possible dimension vectors
\underline{\dim}(M)
satisfying this, so
{\cal P}
is discrete.
∎

Lemma 6.5

The morphism of algebras

Proposition 7.1

is a uniserial subcategory of

for some

\begin{matrix}\xi_{1}:\ M_{1}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}M_{2}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}M_{1},\\

&&\big\downarrow\hbox to0.0pt{$\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle f_{n-1}$}}$\hss}&&\big\downarrow\hbox to0.0pt{$\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle f$}}$\hss}&&\big\downarrow\hbox to0.0pt{$\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle f_{1}$}}$\hss}&&\\

\end{matrix}

defines a morphism of algebras
\varphi_{n}:E_{M_{n}}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}E_{M_{1}}

If
n=2
, we have an almost split sequence

\begin{matrix}0&\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}&M_{1}&\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits^{\alpha_{1}}}&M_{2}&\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits^{\beta_{1}}}&M_{1}&\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}&0.\\
\end{matrix}

Given any morphism
f_{1}:M_{1}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}M_{1}
, the composition
f_{1}\beta_{1}:M_{2}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}M_{1}
is not an isomorphism. Hence, there is a morphism
f:M_{2}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}M_{2}
such that
\beta_{1}f=f_{1}\beta_{1}
. So
\varphi_{2}
is surjective.

Assume that
n\geq 3
and that
\varphi_{n-1}
is surjective, and let us show that
\varphi_{n}
is surjective. Consider the almost split sequence

\begin{matrix}0&\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}&M_{n-1}&\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits^{\ (\alpha_{n-1},\beta_{n-2})^{t}\ }}&M_{n}\oplus M_{n-2}&\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits^{\ (\beta_{n-1},\alpha_{n-2})\ }}&M_{n-1}&\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}&0.\\
\end{matrix}

Given
f_{1}\in E_{M_{1}}
, by induction hypothesis, there is some
f_{n-1}\in E_{M_{n-1}}
with
f_{1}\hat{\beta}_{n-1}=\hat{\beta}_{n-1}f_{n-1}
. Consider the composition
f_{n-1}\beta_{n-1}:M_{n}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}M_{n-1}
, which is not an isomorphism, thus there is a morphism
h:M_{n}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}M_{n}\oplus M_{n-2}
such that
(\beta_{n-1},\alpha_{n-2})h=f_{n-1}\beta_{n-1}
. Denote the matrix components of
h
as
h=(f_{n},g)^{t}
. So, we get
\beta_{n-1}f_{n}+\alpha_{n-2}g=f_{n-1}\beta_{n-1}
. Therefore, we have

\hat{\beta}_{n-1}\beta_{n-1}f_{n}+\hat{\beta}_{n-1}\alpha_{n-2}g=\hat{\beta}_{n-1}f_{n-1}\beta_{n-1}=f_{1}\hat{\beta}_{n-1}\beta_{n-1}=f_{1}\hat{\beta}_{n}.

Moreover, we have

\hat{\beta}_{n-1}\alpha_{n-2}g=\beta_{1}\cdots\beta_{n-2}\alpha_{n-2}g=\beta_{1}\cdots\beta_{n-3}\alpha_{n-3}\beta_{n-3}g=\beta_{1}\alpha_{1}\beta_{1}\cdots\beta_{n-3}g=0.

Finally, we obtain
\hat{\beta}_{n}f_{n}=\hat{\beta}_{n-1}\beta_{n-1}f_{n}=f_{1}\hat{\beta}_{n}
, so
\varphi_{n}
is surjective.
∎

\Gamma
, we can apply this proposition to the uniserial subcategories
{\cal U}_{p}

\Gamma

Corollary 7.2
.

Let
\Gamma
be a bounded principal ideal domain. For any indecomposable
finite-dimensional
\Gamma
-module
M
, set
E_{M}={\rm End}_{\Gamma}(M)^{op}
and
K_{M}={\rm End}_{\Gamma}(M)^{op}/{\rm Rad}{\rm End}_{\Gamma}(M)^{op}
. Let
S
denote the simple socle of
M
, see (
5.2
). Then, there is an isomorphism of
k
-algebras
K_{M}\cong K_{S}=E_{S}
.

Proposition 7.3
.

Assume that
{\cal U}
is a uniserial subcategory of
\Sigma\hbox{-}{\rm mod}
satisfying the assumptions of (
7.1
). Then, if
M_{n}
is an indecomposable
\Sigma
-module in
{\cal U}
with length
n
and socle
M_{1}
, we have

\hbox{\rm endol}\,(M_{n})=n\times\hbox{\rm endol}\,(M_{1})
.

Thus, given
d\in\mathbb{N}
, the category
{\cal U}
has at most one indecomposable with endolength
d
.

Proof.

From the last proposition, we know that
\dim_{k}K_{M_{n}}=\dim_{k}K_{M_{1}}
, Then, from (
4.3
), we have

\begin{matrix}\hbox{\rm endol}\,(M_{n})&=&\dim_{k}M_{n}/\dim_{k}K_{M_{n}}\hfill\\
&=&(n\times\dim_{k}M_{1})/\dim_{k}K_{M_{1}}\hfill\\
&=&n\times\dim_{k}M_{1}/\dim_{k}K_{M_{1}}=n\times\hbox{\rm endol}\,(M_{1}).\hfill\\
\end{matrix}

∎

Corollary 7.4
.

Assume that
\widetilde{\cal U}
is a full subcategory of
\Sigma\hbox{-}{\rm mod}
, for some
k
-algebra
\Sigma
, such that
\widetilde{\cal U}=\coprod_{p\in{\cal P}}{\cal U}_{p}
, where each
{\cal U}_{p}
is a uniserial subcategory satisfying the assumptions of (
7.1
). Then, if
\widetilde{\cal U}
is not
e
-discrete, the index set
{\cal P}
is infinite and
\widetilde{\cal U}
satisfies EBTII.

Proof.

If the subcategory
\widetilde{\cal U}
is not
e
-discrete, there is some number
d
and an infinite family
\{M(i)\}_{i}
of non-isomorphic finite-dimensional indecomposable
\Sigma
-modules in
\widetilde{\cal U}
with common endolength
\hbox{\rm endol}\,(M(i))=d
. Denote by
S(i)
the simple socle of each
\Sigma
-module
M(i)
and by
n_{i}
its length. For all
i
, we have

d=\hbox{\rm endol}\,(M(i))=n_{i}\times\hbox{\rm endol}\,(S(i)),

and the endolength of all these simple modules is bounded by
d
. If
S(i)\cong S(j)
, for some
i
, we would have

n_{i}\times\hbox{\rm endol}\,(S(i))=\hbox{\rm endol}\,(M(i))=d=\hbox{\rm endol}\,(M(j))=n_{j}\times\hbox{\rm endol}\,(S(j)).

Thus,
M(i)
and
M(j)
have the same length and the same socle, which implies that they are isomorphic, thus
i=j
. So, there is some
d_{1}<d
and an infinite family of non-isomorphic simple
\Sigma
-modules
S(i)
with the same endolength
d_{1}
.

Hence, for each one of these simples
S(i)
, the indecomposable module
N_{n}(i)
with length
n
and socle
S(i)
has endolength
\hbox{\rm endol}\,(N_{n}(i))=n\times d_{1}
. The infinite family of non-isomorphic finite-dimensional indecomposable
\Sigma
-modules
\{N_{n}(i)\}_{i}
in
\widetilde{\cal U}
with endolength
n\times d_{1}
can be constructed for any
n
.
∎

Lemma 7.6
.

Assume that a functor
G:\Sigma\hbox{-}{\rm mod}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}\Lambda\hbox{-}{\rm mod}
preserves indecomposables, reflects isomorphism classes, and controls endolength of indecomposables.
Then, if EBTII holds for
\Sigma\hbox{-}{\rm mod}
, it also holds for
\Lambda\hbox{-}{\rm mod}
.

Proof.

By assumption, there are
c,c^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
such that, for any indecomposable
M\in\Sigma\hbox{-}{\rm mod}
, we have

\hbox{\rm endol}\,(M)\leq c\times\hbox{\rm endol}\,(GM)\hbox{ \ and \ }\hbox{\rm endol}\,(GM)\leq c^{\prime}\times\hbox{\rm endol}\,(M),

and there are infinitely many endolengths
d
’s such that for each one of these
d
’s there are infinitely many non-isomorphic finite-dimensional indecomposable
\Sigma
-modules with the same endolength
d
.

If we start with such an infinite family

\{M_{i}\}_{i}
in
\Sigma\hbox{-}{\rm mod}
with
\hbox{\rm endol}\,(M)=d_{1}
, we obtain an infinite subfamily
\{GM_{j}\}_{j}
of the family of images of
\{GM_{i}\}_{i}
with the same endolength
\hbox{\rm endol}\,(GM_{j})=d^{\prime}_{1}\leq c^{\prime}d_{1}
, because
\hbox{\rm endol}\,(GM_{i})\leq c^{\prime}\times\hbox{\rm endol}\,(M_{i})
.

Now, by assumption, we can choose a sufficiently large endolength
d_{2}
(say with
d_{2}>cd^{\prime}_{1}
) for which there is an infinite family of non-isomorphic finite-dimensional indecomposables
\{N_{i}\}_{i}
in
\Sigma\hbox{-}{\rm mod}
with
\hbox{\rm endol}\,(N_{i})=d_{2}
.
As before, we obtain an infinite subfamily
\{GN_{j}\}_{j}
of the family of images
\{GN_{i}\}_{i}
with common endolength
\hbox{\rm endol}\,(GN_{j})=d^{\prime}_{2}\leq c^{\prime}d_{2}
, again because
\hbox{\rm endol}\,(GN_{i})\leq c^{\prime}\times\hbox{\rm endol}\,(N_{i})
. Since
G
controls endolength of indecomposables, we obtain

cd^{\prime}_{1}<d_{2}=\hbox{\rm endol}\,(N_{i})\leq c\times\hbox{\rm endol}\,(GN_{i})=cd^{\prime}_{2}.

Thus,
d^{\prime}_{1}<d^{\prime}_{2}
. We can iterate the preceding procedure to construct inductively endolengths
d^{\prime}_{1}<d^{\prime}_{2}<\cdots
and families of non-isomorphic finite-dimensional indecomposables in
\Lambda\hbox{-}{\rm mod}
with the same endolength
d^{\prime}_{i}
, for each
d^{\prime}_{i}
.
∎

):
From (

G:\Gamma\hbox{-}{\rm mod}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}\Lambda\hbox{-}{\rm mod}
, where
\Gamma
is a bounded principal ideal domain, which is not
e
-discrete. The algebra
\Gamma
satisfies EBTII as a consequence of (
7.5
).
Our theorem follows from (
7.6
), because
G
controls endolength of indecomposables.

In the following proposition we show that the formula relating the endolength of indecomposables in
\Gamma\hbox{-}{\rm mod}
described in (
7.5
), is preserved by
the exact embedding
G:\Gamma\hbox{-}{\rm mod}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}\Lambda\hbox{-}{\rm mod}
of (
1.6
).

Proposition 7.7
.

Let
G:\Gamma\hbox{-}{\rm mod}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}\Lambda\hbox{-}{\rm mod}
be the representation embedding of (
1.6
). Consider a family of indecomposable
\Gamma
-modules
\{M_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that each
M_{n}
has socle
M_{1}
and length
n
. Then, we have

\hbox{\rm endol}\,(G(M_{n}))=n\times\hbox{\rm endol}\,(G(M_{1})).

Proof.

By construction, the functor
G
is the following composition

\Gamma\hbox{-}{\rm mod}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits^{\Psi}}{\mathtt{Q}}\hbox{-}{\rm mod}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits^{{\mathtt{L}}_{{\mathtt{Q}}}}}{\cal Q}\hbox{-}{\rm mod}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits^{F}}{\cal D}\hbox{-}{\rm mod}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits^{\Xi}}{\cal P}^{1}(\Lambda)\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits^{{\rm Cok}}}\Lambda\hbox{-}{\rm mod},

where
{\cal Q}
is a quasi-minimal ditalgebra,
{\mathtt{Q}}
is its minimal algebra,
\Psi
is a full and faithful functor,
F
is a composition of reduction functors, so it is full and faithful, and
{\rm Cok}
is a full functor.

Claim 1: There is an isomorphism of
k
-algebras
K_{G(M_{n})}\cong K_{G(M_{1})}
.

We know that
{\rm End}_{\Gamma}(M_{n})^{op}=K_{M_{n}}\oplus{\rm Rad}{\rm End}_{\Gamma}(M_{n})^{op}
.
Since
\Psi
is full and faithful, we have
{\rm End}_{\mathtt{Q}}(\Psi(M_{n}))^{op}=\Psi(K_{M_{n}})\oplus{\rm Rad}{\rm End}_{\mathtt{Q}}(\Psi(M_{n}))^{op}
, for all
n
. Moreover, from
(
4.2
), we know that
{\rm End}_{\cal Q}({\mathtt{L}}_{{\mathtt{Q}}}(\Psi(M_{n})))^{op}={\mathtt{L}}_{{\mathtt{Q}}}(K_{\Psi(M_{n})})\oplus{\rm Rad}{\rm End}_{\cal Q}({\mathtt{L}}_{{\mathtt{Q}}}(\Psi(M_{n})))^{op}
.
Since the functors
F
and
\Xi
are full and faithful, we get

{\rm End}_{{\cal P}^{1}(\Lambda)}(\Xi F{\mathtt{L}}_{{\mathtt{Q}}}(\Psi(M_{n})))^{op}=\Xi F{\mathtt{L}}_{{\mathtt{Q}}}\Psi(K_{M_{n}})\oplus{\rm Rad}{\rm End}_{{\cal P}^{1}(\Lambda)}(\Xi F{\mathtt{L}}_{{\mathtt{Q}}}\Psi(M_{n}))^{op}.

Using that the functor
{\rm Cok}
is full, we obtain

{\rm End}_{\Lambda}(G(M_{n}))^{op}={\rm Cok}\Xi F{\mathtt{L}}_{{\mathtt{Q}}}\Psi(K_{M_{n}})\oplus{\rm Rad}{\rm End}_{\Lambda}({\rm Cok}\Xi F{\mathtt{L}}_{{\mathtt{Q}}}\Psi(M_{n}))^{op}.

Hence,
{\rm Cok}\Xi F{\mathtt{L}}_{{\mathtt{Q}}}\Psi(K_{M})
is a division algebra isomorphic to
K_{G(M_{n})}
. Thus, for each
n
, we have
K_{M_{n}}\cong K_{G(M_{n})}
, for all
n
. From (
7.2
), we know that
K_{M_{n}}\cong K_{M_{1}}
. Thus the formula of our Claim 1 holds.

Claim 2: We have
\dim_{k}G(M_{n})=n\times\dim_{k}G(M_{1})
.

Indeed, for
n\geq 2
, we have exact sequences in
\Gamma\hbox{-}{\rm mod}
of the form

0\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}M_{n-1}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}M_{n}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}M_{1}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}0,

which are mapped by the exact functor
G
on the exact sequences

0\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}G(M_{n-1})\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}G(M_{n})\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}G(M_{1})\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}0

in
\Lambda\hbox{-}{\rm mod}
. The the announced formula follows by an easy induction from the additivity of dimension for exact sequences.

Finally, using the preceding Claim 1, Claim 2, and (
4.3
), we have

\begin{matrix}\hbox{\rm endol}\,(G(M_{n}))&=&\dim_{k}G(M_{n})/\dim_{k}K_{G(M_{n})}\hfill\\
&=&n\times\dim_{k}G(M_{1})/\dim_{k}K_{G(M_{1})}\hfill\\
&=&n\times\hbox{\rm endol}\,(G(M_{1})).\hfill\\
\end{matrix}

∎

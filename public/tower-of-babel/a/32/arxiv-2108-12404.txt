Residual finiteness for central extensions of lattices in PU ⁡ ( n , 1 ) \PU(n,1) and negatively curved projective varieties
Matthew Stover, Domingo Toledo

Abstract
We study residual finiteness for cyclic central extensions of cocompact arithmetic lattices Γ < PU ⁡ ( n , 1 ) \Gamma<\PU(n,1) of simple type. We prove that the preimage of Γ \Gamma in any connected cover of PU ⁡ ( n , 1 ) \PU(n,1) , in particular the universal cover, is residually finite. This follows from a more general theorem on residual finiteness of extensions whose characteristic class is contained in the span in H 2 ​ ( Γ , ℤ ) H^{2}(\Gamma,\mathbb{Z}) of the Poincaré duals to totally geodesic divisors on the ball quotient Γ \ 𝔹 n \Gamma\backslash\mathbb{B}^{n} . For n ≥ 4 n\geq 4 , if Γ \Gamma is a congruence lattice, we prove residual finiteness of the central extension associated with any element of H 2 ​ ( Γ , ℤ ) H^{2}(\Gamma,\mathbb{Z}) . Our main application is to existence of cyclic covers of ball quotients branched over totally geodesic divisors. This gives examples of smooth projective varieties admitting a metric of negative sectional curvature that are not homotopy equivalent to a locally symmetric manifold. The existence of such examples is new for all dimensions n ≥ 4 n\geq 4 .

Introduction

It is a classical result that if
\Gamma<\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{R})\cong\PU(1,1)

, even though its ambient Lie group
\widetilde{\PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
is not a linear Lie group. See
[
9

for an account of several closely-related perspectives on how one can prove these results. In the other direction, following work of Deligne
[
10
]
, one can use the congruence subgroup property to build many lattices in nonlinear Lie groups that are not linear or residually finite, e.g., the preimage of
\Sp_{2n}(\mathbb{Z})
in the universal cover of
\Sp_{2n}(\mathbb{R})
for
n\geq 2
.

These examples illustrate the interest in studying linearity of lattices in nonlinear Lie groups. Particularly interesting are the universal covering groups

of
\PU(n,1)
,
n\geq 2
. In recent work
[
24
]

, where

is a certain central simple division algebra with involution of second kind. In this paper we consider those of

, namely those constructed using hermitian forms over number fields; see §

.

Let

in

This answers

As in

Taking the special case in Theorem

Let

If

is an arithmetic lattice so that

is relevant, and remains open, for nonarithmetic lattices. We note for contrast that arithmetic ball quotients of simple type contain infinitely many distinct immersed codimension one totally geodesic subvarieties, but recent work of Bader–Fisher–Miller–Stover
[

]
and, independently, Baldi–Ullmo

proves that nonarithmetic ball quotients only contain finitely many immersed codimension one totally geodesic subvarieties (and, more generally, only finitely many of any positive dimension that are maximal with respect to inclusion).

Our final application of Theorem

Theorem 1.5
.

For all

, there are

-dimensional smooth complex projective varieties admitting a Kähler metric of strongly negative curvature, thus a Riemannian metric of strictly negative sectional curvature, that are not homotopy equivalent to a locally symmetric manifold.

Strongly negative curvature is in the sense defined by Siu
[
21
, §2]
. Examples for

[

]
, and further examples were produced by Zheng
[

,

. Deraux gave examples for

]
. We contribute a wealth of new examples for

[

[

Finally we must prove that these covers are not homotopy equivalent to a locally symmetric space.
This was shown for the previous examples by computing Chern numbers. This becomes delicate in higher dimensions, so we provide a proof of a different kind. Assuming our branched cover is homotopy equivalent to a locally symmetric manifold, hyperbolicity of the fundamental group, work of Carlson and Toledo
[

allows us to conclude that the manifold is in fact biholomorphic to a ball quotient. We then study the normal bundle to the branch locus to derive a contradiction.

Residual finiteness, central extensions, and

branched covers

2.1
Basic objects

We review the connections among the following objects. Suppose:

1.

X
is a smooth aspherical manifold with
\Gamma=\pi_{1}(X)
;

2.

p:L\to X
is a complex line bundle over
X
,
L^{\times}
is
L
with its zero section removed, and
\widetilde{\Gamma}=\pi_{1}(L^{\times})
;

3.

s:X\to L
is a section of
L
transverse to the zero section.

The homotopy sequence of the fibration
p:L^{\times}\to X
with fiber
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
gives a central extension

0\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}\longrightarrow\widetilde{\Gamma}\longrightarrow\Gamma\longrightarrow 1

connecting the above fundamental groups. The zero set

Z(s)=\{x\in X:s(x)=0\}

of
s
is a smooth real codimension two submanifold of
X
with normal bundle isomorphic to
L|_{Z(s)}
, where
L
is viewed as an
\mathbb{R}^{2}
-bundle.

2.1.1
Group of central extensions

It is well known that for a given group
\Gamma
and fixed Abelian group
A
the isomorphism classes of central extensions

E:\,0\longrightarrow A\longrightarrow\widetilde{\Gamma}\longrightarrow\Gamma\longrightarrow 1

form a group in natural bijection with
H^{2}(\Gamma,A)
, where the correspondence is given by the characteristic class
\chi(E)
of the extension
E
; e.g., see
[
7
, §VI.3]
. Briefly,
\chi(E)
is represented by the following Eilenberg–MacLane cocycle
c
whose cohomology class uniquely determines
E
: choose a set theoretic section
s:\Gamma\to\widetilde{\Gamma}
, let
c(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})=s(\gamma_{2})s(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2})^{-1}s(\gamma_{1}).
This is equivalent to writing
\widetilde{\Gamma}
as the product
\Gamma\times A
with the multiplication

(\gamma_{1},a_{1})(\gamma_{2},a_{2})=(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2},a_{1}+a_{2}+c(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})).

This correspondence is functorial in both of the variables
\Gamma
and
A
and the resulting operations on extensions are best defined in terms of
\chi(E)
. Two common examples: given an injection
{\iota:\Lambda\to\Gamma}
, then the restriction
\iota^{*}(E)
of
E
to
\Lambda
has characteristic class
\iota^{*}(\chi(E))
. Similarly, if
p:A\to B
is surjective and
p_{*}(E)
denotes the extension of
\Gamma
obtained by dividing
A
and
\widetilde{\Gamma}
by the kernel of
p
, then
\chi(p_{*}(E))=p_{*}(\chi(E))
.

Remark 2.1
.

Note that the word “extension” is used in two (closely related) meanings: the exact sequence
E
above, or just the middle group
\widetilde{\Gamma}
. We will refer to
\widetilde{\Gamma}
as
the group of the extension
E
. Given
\iota:\Lambda\to\Gamma
as above, the group
\widetilde{\Lambda}
of the extension
\iota^{*}(E)
is then the preimage of
\Lambda
in
\widetilde{\Gamma}
for the natural projection
\widetilde{\Gamma}\to\Gamma
.

2.1.2
Group of line bundles

It is also well known that given a space
X
, the isomorphism classes of complex line bundles over
X
form a group under tensor product that is isomorphic to
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
, with isomorphism given by the Chern class
c_{1}(L)
. The isomorphism is natural with respect to smooth maps
f:Y\to X
, namely
c_{1}(f^{*}L)=f^{*}(c_{1}(L))
.

2.1.3
Comparison of characteristic classes

Suppose that we are in the above situation:
X
is a smooth aspherical manifold and
\Gamma=\pi_{1}(X)
, hence
H^{*}(X)\cong H^{*}(\Gamma)
(meaning that there is an isomorphism of cohomology with any coefficients). In order to compare the two characteristic classes defined above, we need a functorial isomorphism between the two cohomologies. More precisely, we need a natural isomorphism between cohomology of the complex
C^{*}(\Gamma,\mathbb{Z})
of Eilenberg–MacLane cochains on
\Gamma
and the cohomology of a topologically defined complex
C^{*}(X,\mathbb{Z})
of cochains on
X
, where one could choose either singular or Čech cochains on
X
. There is a well known method for establishing a natural correspondence between the cohomologies of these two complexes by embedding both in the bicomplex
\bigoplus_{p,q}C^{p}(\Gamma,C^{q}(\widetilde{X},\mathbb{Z}))
of Eilenberg–MacLane cochains on
\Gamma
with coefficients in the (singular or Čech) cochains on the universal cover
\widetilde{X}
with its action of
\Gamma
. Standard arguments give natural isomorphisms from the two cohomologies in question to that of the total complex of the bicomplex. The isomorphism thus obtained will give a functorial isomorphism between
H^{2}(\Gamma,\mathbb{Z})
and
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
.

Thus, in our situation of a complex line bundle
L
over a smooth aspherical manifold
X
we have three cohomology classes in
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})\cong H^{2}(\Gamma,\mathbb{Z})
:

1.

The characteristic class
\chi(E)\in H^{2}(\Gamma,\mathbb{Z})
of the extension

E:\,0\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}\longrightarrow\widetilde{\Gamma}\longrightarrow\Gamma\longrightarrow 1,

(1)

where
\widetilde{\Gamma}=\pi_{1}(L^{\times})
.

2.

The Chern class
c_{1}(L)\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
.

3.

The Poincaré dual
[Z(s)]^{\vee}
of the homology class
[Z(s)]\in H_{n-2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
of the zero set
Z(s)
of the section
s
.

Lemma 2.2
.

The three cohomology classes defined in 1 - 3 above are equal, (except possibly up to a factor of
\pm 1
arising from sign conventions).

Proof.

The equivalence of (2) and (3) is standard; see, for example,
[
6
, Prop. 6.24]
or
[
13
, Ch. 1, Prop. 1]
. The proof of the equivalence of (1) and (2) should also be standard. Since we could not find a reference we give a quick sketch.

We start from the fact that the group-cochain analogue of the standard Čech cochain representative of
c_{1}(L)
gives its representative in group cohomology. Namely, let
p:\widetilde{X}\to X
be the universal cover, with
\Gamma
acting on
\widetilde{X}
by covering transformations. Since
p^{*}L^{\times}
is trivial, it has a nowhere vanishing section
s
. For each
\gamma\in\Gamma
define a function
a_{\gamma}:\widetilde{X}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
by
\gamma^{*}s=a_{\gamma}s
. There exist functions
\ell_{\gamma}:\widetilde{X}\to\mathbb{C}
, unique up to integral additive constant, such that
\exp(2\pi i\ell_{\gamma})=a_{\gamma}
, and these define
\ell\in C^{1}(\Gamma,C^{1}(\widetilde{X},\mathbb{Z}))
as above. Then the Eilenberg–MacLane coboundary
\delta\ell
given by

\delta\ell(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})=\ell(\gamma_{2})-\ell(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2})+\gamma_{2}^{*}\ell(\gamma_{1})

is a
\mathbb{Z}
-valued cocycle representing
c_{1}(L)\in H^{2}(\Gamma,\mathbb{Z})\cong H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
.

To show the equivalence of (1) and (2) it suffices to show that
\delta\ell
is a cocycle for the extension in Equation (
1
). To do this we represent
\widetilde{\Gamma}
as the Deck transformation group of the universal cover
\widetilde{L}^{\times}\cong\widetilde{X}\times\mathbb{C}
. Writing
\widetilde{\Gamma}
set-theoretically as
\Gamma\times\mathbb{Z}
, we see that
(\gamma,n)\in\Gamma\times\mathbb{Z}
acts on
(x,t)\in\widetilde{X}\times\mathbb{C}
by

(\gamma,n)(x,t)=(\gamma x,t+n+\ell_{\gamma}(x)).

From this it is straightforward to deduce that the cocycle giving the set
\Gamma\times\mathbb{Z}
the group structure of
\widetilde{\Gamma}
is
\delta\ell
.
∎

2.1.4
The same considerations over projective varieties

Now suppose, in addition to the above assumptions, that
X
is a smooth projective variety. Let

, where

are smooth and connected codimension one subvarieties of

. We will not distinguish the smooth codimension one subvariety

Definition 2.3
.

A pair

consisting of a smooth projective variety

.

We can assign a holomorphic line bundle

and vanishing to first order on

, meaning that, in local coordinates on

. These properties define

uniquely, and its section

uniquely up to a nonzero multiplicative constant, i.e., we can replace

is isomorphic to the normal bundle

of

Let

be an integer and suppose that

is divisible by

in the group of holomorphic line bundles on

defined by

v\longmapsto v^{d}=\overset{d~\textrm{factors}}{\overbrace{v\otimes\dots\otimes v}}.

Definition 2.4
.

Suppose
(X,D)
is a good pair and

a line bundle with

as above. Then the space

defined by

will be called a

-fold branched cover of

.

More precisely,

could be called the cyclic branched cover associated with

. Note the following properties of

:

Lemma 2.5
.

Given a good pair
(X,D)
and
L
a line bundle so
L^{\otimes^{d}}\cong\mathcal{O}(D)
, the cyclic branched cover

branched along

satisfies:

1.

As a subset of
L
,

is a smooth projective subvariety stable under the multiplication maps

where
\zeta
is a primitive

root of unity, which define an action of the cyclic group

on

.

2.

The projection

is
d
-to-

over

and

-to-

.

3.

If

denotes the zero section of

, then

By writing local equations for

is a smooth subvariety. Since

is a quasiprojective variety, so is

2.2
Residual Finiteness

Recall that a finitely generated group
\Gamma
is
residually finite
if, for every nontrivial
\gamma\in\Gamma
, there exists a homomorphism
\rho:\Gamma\to F
from
\Gamma
to a finite group

3.1
Congruence arithmetic lattices of simple type

The arithmetic lattices under consideration in this paper are those that are cocompact of

. These are constructed as follows, where we generally follow the notation established in
[
5

. Let

be a totally imaginary quadratic extension of a totally real field with

be the Galois involution of

Fix a nondegenerate hermitian vector space

be the completion of

with respect to the complex embedding

of

. We assume that

has signature
(n,1)
and that

. The assumption that

implies that there is at least one completion where

is a definite hermitian space. It follows that

is anisotropic.

will denote the restriction of scalars from

to

of the group of unitary similitudes of

Proposition 3.2
.

For

One often classifies the immersed totally geodesic complex codimension one subvarieties of arithmetic ball quotients
\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{B}^{n}
by
\Gamma

\ell\subset V
so that the restriction of the hermitian form to

is positive under
\ell\hookrightarrow V_{\tau_{1}}
or, dually, by codimension one subspaces

so that

has signature
(n-1,1)
. We instead follow the notation of Kudla–Millson
[

and Bergeron–Millson–Moeglin
[

For each

hermitian matrix
\beta\in\M_{d}(E)
that is positive definite at

, Kudla and Millson define a
special cycle

. This is a locally finite cycle consisting of totally geodesic subvarieties of (complex) codimension

[
18
, p. 132]
. Moreover, all immersed totally geodesic subvarieties are contained in the support of some

. See the above references for further technical details, since they will not concern us in this paper. As pointed out to us by Nicolas Bergeron, the following result has the exact same proof as
[

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
X=\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{B}^{n}
be a smooth compact ball quotient,
n\geq 2
, with
\Gamma
a congruence arithmetic lattice of simple type, and let
c_{1}
be the Chern form on
X
, considered as an element of
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})
. Then
c_{1}
is contained in the span of the collection of all Poincaré duals to codimension one totally geodesic subvarieties of
X
.

Proof.

Suppose not. Then there exists
\eta\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})^{*}=H^{2(n-1)}(X,\mathbb{C})
such that:

\displaystyle\int_{X}\eta\wedge c_{1}

\displaystyle\neq 0

\displaystyle\int_{C}\eta

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\textrm{for all totally geodesic}~C~\textrm{of codimension}~1

In other words, the periods of
\eta
over all codimension
1
geodesic subvarieties are zero.

Following,
[
18
, p. 126]
, let
\mathcal{L}(t)
be the space of hermitian
1\times 1
matrices of rank
t\leq 1
over the ring of integers
\mathcal{O}
of the totally imaginary field
E
from the definition of the arithmetic lattice
\Gamma
. Then
\mathcal{L}(1)
is the ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{F}
of the maximal totally real subfield
F
of
E
and
\mathcal{L}(0)=\{0\}
. For each
\beta\in\mathcal{L}(t)
, we have the totally geodesic cycle
C_{\beta}
of codimension
t
in
X
, where
C_{0}=X
and for
\beta
in the nonzero elements
\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times}
of
\mathcal{O}_{F}
we have
C_{\beta}
of codimension
1
.

For
\beta\in\mathcal{L}(1)
and
\zeta\in\mathbb{C}^{[F:\mathbb{Q}]}
, following
[
18
, p. 125]
, define

e_{*}(\beta\zeta)=\prod_{j=1}^{[F:\mathbb{Q}]}\exp(\pi i\tr_{\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R}}(\tau_{j}(\beta))\zeta_{\tau_{j}}),

where the product is over the places
\{\tau_{j}\}
of
E
and the coordinates of
\zeta
are labelled accordingly. Note that
e_{*}(0\zeta)=1
for all
\zeta
. As on
[
18
, p. 127]
we then have that

\displaystyle P(\zeta,\eta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=0}^{1}\sum_{\beta\in\mathcal{L}(t)}\left(\int_{C_{\beta}}\eta\wedge c_{1}^{1-t}\right)e_{*}(\beta\zeta)

\displaystyle=e_{*}(0\zeta)\int_{X}\eta\wedge c_{1}+\sum_{\beta\in\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times}}e_{*}(\beta\zeta)\int_{C_{\beta}}\eta

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\eta\wedge c_{1}

is a constant function of
\zeta
with value
\int_{X}\eta\wedge c_{1}\neq 0
, since
\int_{C_{\beta}}\eta=0
for all
\beta
by hypothesis.

However, by
[
18
, Thm. 3]
, the function
P(\zeta,\eta)
of
\zeta
is a holomorphic modular form of weight
n+1
for a suitable congruence subgroup of the group of
\mathcal{O}
-points of a certain form of
\U(1,1)
over
F
. Since a nonzero constant function is not a holomorphic modular form of higher weight, this contradiction proves the theorem.
∎

3.4
The cup product and special cycles

The purpose of this section is to prove the following theorem, whose proof was communicated to us by Nicolas Bergeron.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
X=\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{B}^{n}
be a compact congruence arithmetic ball quotient of simple type and
\mathrm{SC}^{1}(X)\subseteq H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})
be the span of the Poincaré duals to the totally geodesic codimension one subvarieties of
X
. Then:

1.

For any class
\sigma\in\mathrm{SC}^{1}(X)
, there is a congruence cover
p:X^{\prime}\to X
so that
p^{*}(\sigma)\in\mathrm{SC}^{1}(X^{\prime})
is in the image of the cup product map

c:\bigwedge\nolimits^{2}H^{1}(X^{\prime},\mathbb{C})\longrightarrow H^{2}(X^{\prime},\mathbb{C}).

2.

If
n\geq 3
, then for every
\phi\in H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{C})
we can find a congruence cover
p:X^{\prime}\to X
so that
p^{*}(\phi)
is contained in the image of
c
.

3.

If
n\geq 4
, then for all
\phi\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})
we can find a congruence cover
p:X^{\prime}\to X
so that
p^{*}(\phi)
is in the image of
c
.

Proof.

Let
G
be the
\mathbb{Q}
-algebraic group associated with
X
and
\Gamma
as in §
3.1
, and suppose that
X
is a connected component of the Shimura variety
S(K)
. The theorem follows fairly directly from deep structural results on cohomology arising from the theta correspondence; see
[
5
, §7]
for a precise definition.

Taking
q=1
and the appropriate values of
(a,b)
in
[
5
, Cor. 7.3]
, we see that
H^{1}(X,\mathbb{C})
is generated by the classes of theta lifts for all
n\geq 2
,
H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{C})
is generated by theta lifts when
n\geq 3
, and all of
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})
is generated by the theta correspondence for
n\geq 4
. For
n\geq 2
, Kudla and Millson proved that the Poincaré duals to codimension
1
geodesic subvarieties are contained in the subspace generated by theta lifts and Poincaré duals to special cycles span the subspace generated by theta lifts
[
16
,
17
,
18
]
(also see
[
5
, p. 9]
).

Now, suppose that
\sigma\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})
is contained in the subspace generated by theta lifts. The above discussion implies that this is the case for every class satisfying one of the three conditions from the statement of the theorem. Let

\widetilde{\sigma}\in H^{*}(\mathrm{Sh}(G),\mathbb{C})=\varinjlim_{K}H(S(K),\mathbb{C})

be the image of
\sigma
in
H^{2}(\mathrm{Sh}(G),\mathbb{C})
as in §
3.2
. Using
[
5
, Prop. 5.4 & 5.19]
, arguing almost verbatim as in the proof of
[
5
, Thm. 9.3]
(the only change being the degrees of the forms under consideration), we have that
\widetilde{\sigma}
is in the image of the cup product map

\bigwedge\nolimits^{2}H^{1}(\mathrm{Sh(G)},\mathbb{C})\longrightarrow H^{2}(\mathrm{Sh(G)},\mathbb{C}).

This implies that there is a congruence cover
p:X^{\prime}\to X
so that

p^{*}(\sigma)\in\mathrm{Im}\left(\bigwedge\nolimits^{2}H^{1}(X^{\prime},\mathbb{C})\longrightarrow H^{2}(X^{\prime},\mathbb{C})\right),

which completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

By the universal coefficients theorem, Theorem
3.4
is also true with coefficients in any subfield of
\mathbb{C}
. In particular, it holds over
\mathbb{Q}
.

Theorem
3.3
and Theorem
3.4
combine with Remark
3.5
to give the following.

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
X
be a smooth compact congruence arithmetic ball quotient of simple type with dimension

. Suppose that
\phi\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
is one of the following classes:

1.

c_{1}(X)
;

2.

a reduced effective divisor with support a union of totally geodesic subvarieties, or any linear combination of such classes;

3.

any class in

;

4.

an arbitrary element of
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})

Then there is a congruence cover
p:X^{\prime}\to X
so that the image of
p^{*}(\phi)
in
H^{2}(X^{\prime},\mathbb{Q})
is contained in the image of the cup product map

c_{\mathbb{Q}}:\bigwedge\nolimits^{2}H^{1}(X^{\prime},\mathbb{Q})\longrightarrow H^{2}(X^{\prime},\mathbb{Q}).

Remark 3.7

Passage to a finite cover in Corollary

is necessary. For example, let

be the Cartwright–Steger surface. Then

is the quotient of

by a congruence arithmetic lattice of simple type (e.g., see

5
Branched covers of ball quotients

We begin with the key application of our main results, which provides existence of the branched covers used to prove Theorem
1.5
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Fix
d\geq 2
. Let
X=\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{B}^{n}
be a smooth compact complex hyperbolic
n
-manifold,
n\geq 2
, with
\Gamma
a congruence arithmetic lattice of simple type. Then we can pass to a congruence cover
X^{\prime}=\Gamma^{\prime}\backslash\mathbb{B}^{n}
so that
X^{\prime}
contains a totally geodesic divisor
D^{\prime}
such that:

1.

(X^{\prime},D^{\prime})
is a good pair in the sense of Definition
2.3
, and

2.

there exists a smooth projective variety
Y
and a cyclic branched cover
f:Y\to X^{\prime}
with branch divisor
D^{\prime}
and branching of degree
d
along each irreducible component of
D^{\prime}
.

Proof.

As described in §
3.3
, it is well known that
X
contains an
immersed
totally geodesic codimension one ball quotient
D
. It is a standard fact that we can replace
X
with a finite congruence cover and assume that
D
is in fact embedded, hence
(X,D)
is a good pair in the sense of Definition
2.3
. Corollary
3.6
and Theorem
2.16
imply that condition
N_{2}
is satisfied by the central extension of
\Gamma
by
\mathbb{Z}
associated with
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{O}(D)^{\times})
as in §
2.1
. Therefore, by Corollary
2.15
there is a further finite cover
p:X^{\prime}\to X
so that there exists a cyclic
d
-fold branched cover
Y\to X^{\prime}
, branched over the totally geodesic divisor
D^{\prime}=p^{*}(D)
, with
Y
smooth.
∎

We will apply the following theorem of Zheng
[
27
]
, where we restate Zheng’s notion of a “good cover” with smooth totally geodesic branch divisor in our language. His condition
negative definite complex curvature operator
is the same as Siu’s very strong negative curvature, which implies strong negative curvature, which in turn implies negative sectional curvature. See
[
21
, §2]
.

Theorem 5.2

(Thm. 1
[
27
]
)
.

Let
f:Y\to X
be a
d
-fold cyclic branched cover of a smooth compact ball quotient
X
with branch divisor
D
a disjoint union of smooth totally geodesic codimension one subvarieties and branching of order
d
along each component. Then
Y
admits a Kähler metric with negative definite complex curvature operator. In fact, the metric on
Y
is strongly negative in the sense of Siu, hence
Y
is strongly rigid.

We now prove Theorem
1.5
.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Let
Y
be one of the smooth projective varieties provided by Proposition
5.1
. Then
Y
admits a metric of strongly negative curvature by Theorem
5.2
, so to complete the proof we must show that
Y
is not homotopy equivalent to a locally symmetric manifold. Since
Y
is a closed manifold admitting a complete metric of negative curvature, it is well known that
\pi_{1}(Y)
cannot contain a free abelian subgroup of rank
r\geq 2
. A cocompact lattice in a semisimple Lie group of real rank
r
contains a subgroup isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}^{r}
by a theorem of Wolf
[
26
, Thm. 4.2]
, hence we only need to rule out the possibility that
\pi_{1}(Y)
is isomorphic to a lattice in an adjoint simple Lie group of real rank one.

Let
Z
be a rank one locally symmetric space homotopy equivalent to
Y
. We can exclude
Z
being real, quaternionic, or Cayley hyperbolic by a result of Carlson and Toledo
[
8
, Cor. 3.3]
: since
Y
is a compact Kähler manifold, it cannot be homotopy equivalent to a locally symmetric space
Z
unless
Z
is locally Hermitian symmetric. This and real rank one implies that
Z
is a ball quotient. Considering cohomological dimension we see that
\dim(Y)=\dim(Z)
.

If
f:Y\to Z=\Lambda\backslash\mathbb{B}^{n}
is a homotopy equivalence, and
g:Z\to Y
a homotopy inverse to
f
, since both
Y
and
Z
have strongly negative curvature, Siu rigidity
[
21
, Thm. 1]
implies that we can assume that both
f
and
g
are holomorphic maps. Thus
f\circ g
and
g\circ f
are holomorphic self maps of
Z
and
Y
, respectively, that are homotopic to the identity, hence they are the identity. It follows that
f
is a biholomorphism and
g=f^{-1}
. See also
[
21
, Thm. 2]

By definition of a branched cover,
\mathbb{Z}/d
acts on
Y
by holomorphic automorphisms with quotient the ball quotient
X^{\prime}
from the statement of Proposition
5.1
. Let
\rho\in\Aut(Y)
be a generator for this action and
D^{\prime}
be the branch locus, which we recall is a disjoint union of smooth totally geodesic subvarieties of
X^{\prime}
. If
\mathcal{D}
denotes the preimage of
D^{\prime}
on
Y
, then
\mathcal{D}
is a divisor with support a disjoint union of smooth subvarieties of
Y
, each isomorphic to a smooth totally geodesic subvariety
D_{i}\subset X^{\prime}
. The fixed point set of
\rho
is
\mathcal{D}
, and the cyclic group generated by
\rho
acts freely on
Y\smallsetminus\mathcal{D}
.

Consider the holomorphic automorphism

\sigma=\left(f\circ\rho\circ f^{-1}\right):Z\to Z

of period
d
fixing the divisor
f(\mathcal{D})
. Since automorphisms of
Z
are isometries for its Bergman metric and fixed sets of isometries are totally geodesic, it follows that
f(\mathcal{D})
is a totally geodesic divisor on
Z
and the support of
f(\mathcal{D})
consists of a disjoint union of smooth subvarieties of
Z
isomorphic to the ball quotient
D_{i}
. In particular, each
D_{i}
is isometric to a smooth totally geodesic subvariety of both
X^{\prime}
and
Z
. Moreover, since
f
is a biholomorphism, the normal bundle to
D_{i}
in
Z
is isomorphic to the normal bundle of
D_{i}
in
Y
. We use this to derive a contradiction.

If
X
is an
n
-dimensional ball quotient and
D_{i}
a smooth totally geodesic subvariety of codimension one, then the normal bundle to
D_{i}
in
X
has first Chern class
\nu
satisfying
n\nu=c_{1}(D_{i})
in
H^{2}(D_{i},\mathbb{Q})
. One way to see this is to use the principle of proportionality in dual symmetric spaces: any relation between characteristic classes with
\mathbb{Q}
coefficients that holds in one element of a dual pair must also hold in the other. For
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
this equality is easily checked. Indeed, the normal bundle is the bundle
\nu=\mathcal{O}(1)
with
{c_{1}(\nu)=x}
, where
x
is a positive generator of
H^{2}(\mathbb{P}^{n-1},\mathbb{Z})
, while
{c_{1}(\mathbb{P}^{n-1})=nx}
, thus the equivalent equality must also hold for a geodesic divisor in a ball quotient.

In particular, the first Chern class of the normal bundle to
D_{i}
is completely independent of the ambient ball quotient. Applying this to
X^{\prime}
and considering the branched cover
Y\to X^{\prime}
, the normal bundle to
D_{i}
in
Y
has first Chern class

d\nu\in H^{2}(D_{i},\mathbb{Q}),

hence the same holds for the normal bundle to
D_{i}
in
Z
. This implies that
c_{1}(D_{i})=dc_{1}(D_{i})
, but
d\neq 1
and
c_{1}(D_{i})
is nontrivial, hence we arrive at a contradiction. This proves that
Y
cannot be homotopy equivalent to a ball quotient, which completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

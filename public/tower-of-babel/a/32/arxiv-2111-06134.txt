Abstract
This is the second part of a series devoting to the generalizations and applications of common theorems in variational bifurcation theory. Using abstract theorems in the first part we obtain many new bifurcation results for quasi-linear elliptic boundary value problems of higher order.



1
Introduction

In Part I of the series, we have generalized some famous bifurcation theorems
for potential operators by weakening standard assumptions on the differentiability of the involved functionals.
This part studies their applications to bifurcations for quasi-linear elliptic systems.
For showing our methods we only consider the Dirichlet boundary conditions in many results.

\displaystyle l+1-\frac{(k(l+1)-|\alpha|)p}{m}

\displaystyle l+1-\frac{k(l+1)p}{m}+\frac{|\alpha|p}{m}

\displaystyle 1+l-\frac{lkp}{m}

\displaystyle 1+l(1-\frac{kp}{m})<1.

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{U}}|D^{\alpha^{1}}w_{1}(x)D^{\alpha^{2}}w_{2}(x)\cdots D^{\alpha^{l}}w_{l}(x)D^{\alpha^{l+1}}w_{l+1}(x)|^{p}dx

\displaystyle\prod^{l+1}_{j=1}\left(\int_{\mathcal{U}}|D^{\alpha^{j}}w_{j}(x)|^{r_{j}p}dx\right)^{1/r_{j}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\prod^{l+1}_{j=1}(C_{\alpha^{j}})^{1/r_{j}}\prod^{l+1}_{j=1}\|w_{j}\|^{p}_{k,p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(m,k,p,V)\prod^{l+1}_{j=1}\|w_{j}\|^{p}_{k,p}.

\displaystyle(C(m,k,p,V))^{\frac{1}{p}}\max\{C_{l}(m,k,p,V),1\}

with

.

\displaystyle\hskip-22.76228pt\leq\mathfrak{g}_{1}(RC_{0})\|D^{\beta}v^{j}_{k}\|_{C^{0}}\bigg(\int_{\Omega}\bigg(1+\sum^{N}_{l=1}\sum_{m-n/2\leq|\gamma|\leq m}|D^{\gamma}w_{k}^{l}(x)|^{2_{\gamma}}\bigg)\bigg)^{1/2^{\prime}_{\alpha}}\bigg(\int_{\Omega}|D^{\alpha}v_{k}^{i}(x)|^{2_{\alpha}}\bigg)^{1/2_{\alpha}}

Appendix: A note on the Nemytski operators

When
\Lambda
consists of a single point,
the following proposition is the standard result concerning the continuity of
the Nemytski operator (cf.
[
9
, Lemma 3.2]
,

[
35
, Proposition 1.1, page 3]
and
[
12
, Corollary 1.16]
).

Proposition C.1
.

Let
G
be as above,
\Lambda
a sequential compact topology space, and let

f:G\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\times A\to{\mathbb{R}}
satisfy the following conditions:

(a)

f(x,\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{N};\lambda)
is continuous in

(\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{N};\lambda)
for almost all
x\in G
;

(b)

f(x,\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{N};\lambda)
is measurable in
x
for any fixed
(\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{N};\lambda)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\times\Lambda
;

(c)

there exist positive numbers
C
,
1<p,p_{1},\cdots,p_{N}<\infty
and a function
g\in L^{p}(G)
such that

|f(x,\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{N};\lambda)|\leq C\sum^{N}_{i=1}|\xi_{i}|^{\frac{p_{i}}{p}}+g(x),\quad\forall(x,\xi)\in\overline{\Omega}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N}.

(C.1)

Then the Nemytskii operators
F_{\lambda}:\prod^{N}_{i=1}L_{p_{i}}(G)\to L_{p}(G)
,
\lambda\in\Lambda
, defined by

F_{\lambda}(u_{1},\cdots,u_{N})(x)=f(x,u_{1}(x),\cdots,u_{N}(x);\lambda)

have an uniform bound on any bounded subset, and uniform continuity at any point with respect to
\lambda\in\Lambda
, i.e., given a point
(u_{1},\cdots,u_{N})\in\prod^{N}_{i=1}L_{p_{i}}(G)
,
\forall\epsilon>0
,
\exists\;\delta>0

such that

\|F_{\lambda}(v_{1},\cdots,v_{N})-F_{\lambda}(u_{1},\cdots,u_{N})\|_{p}<\epsilon

for all
\lambda\in\Lambda
and
(v_{1},\cdots,v_{N})\in\prod^{N}_{i=1}L_{p_{i}}(G)
with
\sum^{N}_{i=1}\|v_{i}-u_{i}\|_{p_{i}}<\delta
.

Proof.

As in the case of
\Lambda=\{\hbox{a single point}\}
, the conditions
(a) and (b) imply that

F_{\lambda}(u_{1},\cdots,u_{N})
is measurable if all
u_{1},\cdots,u_{N}
are measurable.
Thus by (
C.1
) all
F_{\lambda}
map a bounded subset of
\prod^{N}_{i=1}L_{p_{i}}(G)
into a common bounded subset of
L_{p}(G)
.

The proof for the second claim can be obtained by slightly changing the short
and elegant proof of
[
12
, Theorem 1.1.5]
.
Indeed, by contradiction there exist sequences

(\lambda_{k})\subset\Lambda
,
(u_{i,k})\subset L^{p_{i}}(G)
,
i=1,\cdots,N
, a point
(u_{1},\cdots,u_{N})\in\prod^{N}_{i=1}L_{p_{i}}(G)
and a real
\epsilon_{0}>0
such that

\displaystyle\left.\begin{array}[]{ll}&\|u_{i,k}-u_{i}\|_{p_{i}}\to 0,\;i=1,\cdots,N,\quad\hbox{and}\\
&\|F_{\lambda_{k}}(u_{1,k},\cdots,u_{N,k})-F_{\lambda_{k}}(u_{1},\cdots,u_{N})\|_{p}\geq\epsilon_{0}\quad\forall k\in{\mathbb{N}}.\end{array}\right\}

As in the proof of
[
12
, Theorem 1.15]
, passing to subsequences we may assume

u_{i,k}(x)\to u_{i}(x)\;\hbox{a.e. in $G$,\quad and\quad}|u_{i,k}(x)|\leq\Phi_{i}(x),

where
\Phi_{i}\in L_{p_{i}}(\Omega)
,
i=1,\cdots,N
. Moreover, we can also assume
\lambda_{k}\to\lambda\in\Lambda

since
\Lambda
is sequential compact. Noting that
f:G\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\times A\to{\mathbb{R}}
is continuous, we get

\displaystyle F_{\lambda_{k}}(u_{1,k},\cdots,u_{N,k})(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(x,u_{1,k}(x),\cdots,u_{N,k}(x);\lambda_{k})

\displaystyle\to F_{\lambda_{0}}(u_{1},\cdots,u_{N})(x)\;\hbox{a.e. in $G$},

\displaystyle F_{\lambda_{k}}(u_{1},\cdots,u_{N})(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(x,u_{1}(x),\cdots,u_{N}(x);\lambda_{k})

\displaystyle\to F_{\lambda_{0}}(u_{1},\cdots,u_{N})(x)\;\hbox{a.e. in $G$}.

Moreover, (
C.1
) implies that for some constant
C^{\prime}=C(N,p_{i},p,C)>0

and almost all
x\in G
,

\displaystyle|f(x,u_{1,k}(x),\cdots,u_{N,k}(x);\lambda_{k})|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(C\sum^{N}_{i=1}|u_{i,k}(x)|^{\frac{p_{i}}{p}}+g(x))^{p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C^{\prime}(\sum^{N}_{i=1}\Phi_{i}(x)^{{p_{i}}}+g(x)^{p}

Hence the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem leads to

\displaystyle\|F_{\lambda_{k}}(u_{1,k},\cdots,u_{N,k})-F_{\lambda_{0}}(u_{1},\cdots,u_{N})\|_{p}\to 0,

\displaystyle\|F_{\lambda_{k}}(u_{1},\cdots,u_{N})-F_{\lambda_{0}}(u_{1},\cdots,u_{N})\|_{p}\to 0

and therefore
\|F_{\lambda_{k}}(u_{1,k},\cdots,u_{N,k})-F_{\lambda_{k}}(u_{1},\cdots,u_{N})\|_{p}\to 0
,
which is a contradiction to (

).

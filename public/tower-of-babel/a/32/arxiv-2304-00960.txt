Two curious q q -supercongruences and their extensions Thanks: This work is supported by Natural Science Foundation of Shanghai (22ZR1424100).
Haihong He, Xiaoxia Wang ∗

Abstract
. We prove two single-parameter q q -supercongruences which were recently conjectured by Guo, and establish their further extensions with one more parameter. Crucial ingredients in the proof are the terminating form of q q -binomial theorem and a Karlsson-Minton type summation formula due to Gasper. Incidentally, an assertion of Wang, Li and Tang is also verified by establishing its q q -analogue.

Introduction

Except for investigating hypergeometric families of Calabi-Yau manifolds, Rodriguez-Villegas
[
14
]
also observed (numerically) many possible supercongruences, including the following one: for any odd prime
p
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}\frac{(\frac{1}{2})_{k}^{2}}{k!^{2}}\equiv(-1)^{(p-1)/2}\pmod{p^{2}}.

(1.1)

Here and in what follows, the the Pochhammer symbol is defined as and
(x)_{0}=1
and
(x)_{n}=x(x+1)\cdots(x+n-1)
with
n
a positive integer.
The congruence (
1.1
) was first proved by Mortenson
[
13
]
. Later, Deines et al.
[
3
]
gave the following generalization of (
1.1
): for any integer
d>1
and prime
p\equiv 1\pmod{d}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}\frac{(\frac{d-1}{d})_{k}^{d}}{k!^{d}}\equiv-\Gamma_{p}\big(\tfrac{1}{d}\big)^{d}\pmod{p^{2}},

(1.2)

where
\Gamma_{p}(x)
denotes the
p
-adic Gamma function (cf.
[
2
, §1.12]
).

For any complex variable
x
and integer
n
, define the
q
-shifted factorial
as

(x;q)_{\infty}=\prod_{k=0}^{\infty}(1-xq^{k})\quad\text{and}\quad(x;q)_{n}=\frac{(x;q)_{\infty}}{(xq^{n};q)_{\infty}}.

For simplicity, we also adopt the compact expression

(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{m};q)_{n}=(x_{1};q)_{n}(x_{2};q)_{n}\cdots(x_{m};q)_{n}.

Moreover,
[n]:=[n]_{q}=1+q+\cdots+q^{n-1}
denotes the
q
-integer
.
A
q
-analogue of (
1.1
) can be found in Guo and Zeng
[
10
, Corollary 2.4]
.
Recently, Guo
[
6
, Theorem 1.1]
established a
q
-extension of (
1.2
): for any integers
d,n>1
with
n\equiv 1\pmod{d}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{(q^{d-1};q^{d})_{k}^{d}q^{dk}}{(q^{d};q^{d})_{k}^{d}}\equiv\frac{(q^{d};q^{d})_{(d-1)(n-1)/d}q^{(d-1)(n-1)(d+n-1)/(2d)}}{(q^{d};q^{d})_{(n-1)/d}^{d-1}(-1)^{(d-1)(n-1)/d}}\pmod{\Phi_{n}(q)^{2}}.

(1.3)

Here and in what follows, the
n
-th
cyclotomic polynomial

\Phi_{n}(q)
is given by

\displaystyle\Phi_{n}(q)=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq k\leq n\\
\gcd(k,n)=1\end{subarray}}(q-\zeta^{k}),

where
\zeta
is an
n
-th primitive root of unity.
In the same paper, Guo also presented two analogous
q
-supercongruences as follows.

(i)
For any odd integer
d\geq 3
and integer
n
with
n\equiv-1\pmod{d}
and
n\geq 2d-1
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{(q^{d+1};q^{d})_{k}^{d-1}(q^{1-d};q^{d})_{k}q^{dk}}{(q^{d};q^{d})_{k}^{d}}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{(1-q)(1-q^{d-1})(q^{d};q^{d})_{n-1-(n+1)/d}}{-(q^{d};q^{d})_{(n+1)/d}^{d-1}}q^{(d(d+n)(n+1)-(n+1)^{2})/(2d)-1}\pmod{\Phi_{n}(q)^{2}}.

(1.4)

(ii)
For any even integer
d\geq 4
and positive integer
n
with
n\equiv-1\pmod{d}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{(q^{d+1};q^{d})_{k}^{d-2}(q;q^{d})_{k}^{2}q^{dk}}{(q^{d};q^{d})_{k}^{d}}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{(1-q)^{2}(q^{d};q^{d})_{n-1-(n+1)/d}}{(-1)^{(n+1)/d}(q^{d};q^{d})_{(n+1)/d}^{d-1}}q^{(d(d+n)(n+1)-(n+1)^{2})/(2d)-2}\pmod{\Phi_{n}(q)^{2}}.

(1.5)

(1.6)

(6, Conjecture 5.3)

2.
Proofs of Theorems
1.1
and
1.3

To prove Theorems
1.1
and
1.3
, we require the following key result.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
d,r
be positive integers with
d\geq 3+r
and
\gcd(d,r)=1
. Let
n
be an integer such that
n\equiv-r\pmod{d}
and
n\geq 2d-r
. Then,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{(q^{d+r};q^{d})_{k}^{d-r-1}(q^{r};q^{d})_{k}^{r}(q^{r-d};q^{d})_{k}q^{dk}}{(q^{d};q^{d})_{k}^{d}}\equiv 0\pmod{\Phi_{n}(q)^{2}}.

(2.1)

Especially, for integers
d\geq 4
and
n\geq 2d-1
with
n\equiv-1\pmod{d}
, we have,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{(q^{d+1};q^{d})_{k}^{d-2}(q,q^{1-d};q^{d})_{k}q^{dk}}{(q^{d};q^{d})_{k}^{d}}\equiv 0\pmod{\Phi_{n}(q)^{2}}.

(2.2)

Proof.

Obviously, the ratio
(q^{d+r};q^{d})_{k}^{d-r-1}/(q^{d};q^{d})_{k}^{d}
contains the factor
(1-q^{(d-1)n})^{2}
for
n-(n+r)/d\leq k\leq n-1
.
Besides, it is easy to find that for
k\geq 0
,

\displaystyle(q^{r};q^{d})_{k}^{r}(q^{r-d};q^{d})_{k}=(1-q^{r-d})(1-q^{r})^{r+1}(q^{d+r};q^{d})_{k-2}^{r+1}(1-q^{dk-d+r})^{r}.

Equivalently, we just prove the truth of the subsequent congruence,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1-(n+r)/d}\frac{(q^{d+r};q^{d})_{k}^{d-r-1}(q^{d+r};q^{d})_{k-2}^{r+1}(1-q^{dk-d+r})^{r}q^{dk}}{(q^{d};q^{d})_{k}^{d}}\equiv 0\pmod{\Phi_{n}(q)^{2}}.

(2.3)

Before that, we confirm
the following parametric generalizations of (
2.3
), which are both symmetric about
a
and
a^{-1}
:

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1-(n+r)/d}\frac{(a^{d-1}q^{d+r},a^{d-3}q^{d+r},\cdots,a^{r+2}q^{d+r},a^{-r-2}q^{d+r},\cdots,a^{3-d}q^{d+r},a^{1-d}q^{d+r};q^{d})_{k}q^{dk}}{(q^{d},a^{d-2}q^{d},a^{d-4}q^{d},\cdots,a^{r+3}q^{d},a^{-r-1}q^{d},\cdots,a^{4-d}q^{d},a^{2-d}q^{d};q^{d})_{k}}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\times\frac{(a^{r}q^{d+r},a^{r-2}q^{d+r},\cdots,a^{-r+2}q^{d+r},a^{-r}q^{d+r};q^{d})_{k-2}(1-q^{dk-d+r})^{r}}{(a^{r+1}q^{d},a^{r-1}q^{d},\cdots,a^{-r+3}q^{d},a^{-r+1}q^{d};q^{d})_{k}}

\displaystyle\qquad\equiv 0\pmod{(1-aq^{n})(a-q^{n})}

(2.4)

for
d+r\equiv 1\pmod{2}
, and

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1-(n+r)/d}\frac{(a^{d-1}q^{d+r},a^{d-3}q^{d+r},\cdots,a^{r+3}q^{d+r},q^{d+r},a^{-r-3}q^{d+r},\cdots,a^{3-d}q^{d+r},a^{1-d}q^{d+r};q^{d})_{k}}{(q^{d},a^{d-2}q^{d},a^{d-4}q^{d},\cdots,a^{r+4}q^{d},aq^{d},a^{-r-2}q^{d},\cdots,a^{4-d}q^{d},a^{2-d}q^{d};q^{d})_{k}q^{-dk}}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\times\frac{(a^{r+1}q^{d+r},a^{r-1}q^{d+r},\cdots,a^{2}q^{d+r},a^{-2}q^{d+r},\cdots,a^{-r+1}q^{d+r},a^{-r-1}q^{d+r};q^{d})_{k-2}}{(a^{r+2}q^{d},a^{r}q^{d},\cdots,a^{3}q^{d},a^{-1}q^{d},\cdots,a^{-r+2}q^{d},a^{-r}q^{d};q^{d})_{k}(1-q^{dk-d+r})^{-r}}

\displaystyle\qquad\equiv 0\pmod{(1-aq^{n})(a-q^{n})}

(2.5)

for
d\equiv r\equiv 1\pmod{2}
.

Next, we concentrate on the proof of (
2
). Since
\gcd(d,r)=\gcd(d,n)=1
,
the denominator of the left-hand side of (
2
) does not contain the factor
1-aq^{n}
or
a-q^{n}
.
Thus, for
a=q^{-n}
or
a=q^{n}
, noticing that
(q^{d+r-(d-1)n};q^{d})_{k}=0
for
k>n-1-(n+r)/d
, the left-hand side of (
2
) equals

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1-(n+r)/d}\frac{(q^{d+r-(d-1)n},q^{d+r-(d-3)n},\cdots,q^{d+r-(r+2)n},q^{d+r+(r+2)n},\cdots,q^{d+r+(d-1)n};q^{d})_{k}q^{dk}}{(q^{d},q^{d-(d-2)n},\cdots,q^{d-(r+3)n},q^{d+(r+1)n},\cdots,q^{d+(d-2)n};q^{d})_{k}}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\times\frac{(q^{d+r-rn},q^{d+r-(r-2)n},\cdots,q^{d+r+(r-2)n},q^{d+r+rn};q^{d})_{k-2}(1-q^{dk-d+r})^{r}}{(q^{d-(r+1)n},q^{d-(r-1)n},\cdots,q^{d+(r-3)n},q^{d+(r-1)n};q^{d})_{k}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1-(n+r)/d}(-1)^{k}q^{d\binom{n-1-(n+r)/d-k}{2}}{n-1-(n+r)/d\brack k}_{q^{d}}P(q^{dk}).

(2.6)

Here
P(q^{dk})
is a polynomial in
q^{dk}
of degree
n-2-(n+r)/d
and

{n\brack k}:={n\brack k}_{q}=\frac{(q;q)_{n}}{(q;q)_{k}(q;q)_{n-k}}

denotes the
q
-binomial coefficient
.
In the derivation of (
2
), we have utilized the following formulas, which can be certified readily:

\displaystyle\frac{(q^{d+r-(d-1)n};q^{d})_{k}q^{dk}}{(q^{d};q^{d})_{k}}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k}{n-1-(n+r)/d\brack k}_{q^{d}}q^{d\binom{k}{2}+(n+2d+r-dn)k},

\displaystyle d\binom{k}{2}+(n+2d+r-dn)k

\displaystyle=d\binom{n-1-(n+r)/d-k}{2}-d\binom{n-1-(n+r)/d}{2},

for
1\leq{j}\leq{d-1}
and

\displaystyle\frac{(q^{d+r-(d-2j-1)n};q^{d})_{k}}{(q^{d-(d-2j)n};q^{d})_{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(q^{d-(d-2j)n+dk};q^{d})_{(n+r)/d}}{(q^{d-(d-2j)n};q^{d})_{(n+r)/d}},

(2.7)

and for
(d-r-1)/2\leq{j}\leq(d+r-1)/2

\displaystyle\frac{(q^{d+r-(d-2j-1)n};q^{d})_{k-2}}{(q^{d-(d-2j)n};q^{d})_{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(q^{d-(d-2j)n+dk};q^{d})_{(n+r)/d-2}}{(q^{d-(d-2j)n};q^{d})_{(n+r)/d}}.

(2.8)

We see that the right-hand sides of (
2.7
) and (
2.8
) are polynomials in
q^{dk}
of degree
(n+r)/d
and
(n+r)/d-2
respectively.
Then, by the terminating form of

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}{n\brack k}q^{\binom{n-k}{2}+jk}=0\quad\text{ for }0\leq j\leq n-1,

we deduce that the right-hand side of (
2
) equals zero.
This implies that the congruence (
2
) holds modulo
(1-aq^{n})(a-q^{n})
.
Similarly, we are able to prove (
2
) and we omit the details here.

Apparently, the limit

has the factor
\Phi_{n}(q)^{2}
as

) are both coprime with

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

Proof.

We shall prove Theorem
1.2
by using Gasper’s Karlsson-Minton type summation formula (see
[
4
, (1.9.9)]
for more general form): for nonnegative integers
N,n_{1},\cdots,n_{m}
with
N=n_{1}+\cdots+n_{m}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{N}\frac{(q^{-N},b_{1}q^{n_{1}},\cdots,b_{m}q^{n_{m}};q)_{k}}{(q,b_{1},\cdots,b_{m};q)_{k}}q^{k}=(-1)^{N}\frac{(q;q)_{N}b_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots b_{m}^{n_{m}}}{(b_{1};q)_{n_{1}}\cdots(b_{m};q)_{n_{m}}}q^{\binom{n_{1}}{2}+\cdots+\binom{n_{m}}{2}}.

(3.1)

Below, the two conditions
d>3
and
d=3
of Theorem
1.2
shall be discussed separately.

(i)
For
d>3
, we consider the following generalization of (
1.7
): modulo
(1-aq^{n})(a-q^{n})
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{(a^{d-1}q^{d+1},a^{d-3}q^{d+1},\cdots,a^{4}q^{d+1},a^{2}q,q^{d+1},a^{-2}q,a^{-4}q^{d+1},\cdots,a^{3-d}q^{d+1},a^{1-d}q^{d+1};q^{d})_{k}}{(a^{d-2}q^{d},a^{d-4}q^{d},\cdots,a^{4-d}q^{d},a^{2-d}q^{d};q^{d})_{k}(q^{d};q^{d})_{k}q^{-dk}}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{(1-a^{2}q)(1-q/a^{2})(q^{d};q^{d})_{n-1-(n+1)/d}}{(a^{d-2}q^{d},a^{d-4}q^{d},\cdots,a^{4-d}q^{d},a^{2-d}q^{d};q^{d})_{(n+1)/d}}q^{(d(d+n)(n+1)-(n+1)^{2})/(2d)-2}.

(3.2)

In fact, we take the substitutions

q\rightarrow{q^{d}}
,
m=d-1
,
N=n-1-(n+1)/d
,
b_{j}=q^{d-(d-2j)n}

(1\leq j\leq d-1)
,
n_{(d-3)/2}=n_{(d+1)/2}=(n+r)/d-1

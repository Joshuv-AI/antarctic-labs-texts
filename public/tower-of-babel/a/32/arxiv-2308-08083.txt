Abstract
. We study the relation between the Hodge filtration of the de Rham cohomology of a proper smooth supervariety X X and the usual Hodge filtration of the corresponding reduced variety X 0 X_{0} .



1.
Introduction

Let
X
be a smooth proper supervariety of dimension
n|m
over
\C
.
It is known that the de Rham cohomology
H^{\bullet}_{dR}(X)
of
X
is canonically isomorphic to the de Rham cohomology
H^{\bullet}_{dR}(X_{0})
of the
underlying usual variety
X_{0}
of dimension
n
. However, the Hodge filtration
(F^{p}H^{i}_{dR}(X))

on
H^{i}_{dR}(X)
in general differs from the Hodge filtration
(F^{p}H^{i}_{dR}(X_{0}))
on
H^{i}_{dR}(X_{0})

(see
[
3
]
). It is natural to try to compare these Hodge filtrations. There is an obvious inclusion

F^{p}H^{i}_{dR}(X)\subset F^{p}H^{i}_{dR}(X_{0})

which we can think of as an upper bound for
F^{p}H^{i}_{dR}(X)
in terms of the Hodge filtration of
X_{0}
.

Our main result gives a lower bound for
F^{1}H^{i}_{dR}(X)
in terms of the Hodge filtration of
X_{0}
.
It can also be formulated in terms of the canonical map

\kappa_{n+i}:H^{i}(X,\operatorname{Ber}_{X})\to H^{n+i}_{dR}(X_{0}),

(1.1)

defined either using Serre duality or using the complex of integral forms (see Sec.
2.2
).

Theorem A
.
For each
i
, one has an inlcusion
F^{1}H^{i}_{dR}(X)\supset F^{m+1}H^{i}_{dR}(X_{0})
(recall that the dimension of
X
is
n|m
).
Equivalently, one has an inclusion

\operatorname{im}(\kappa_{n+i})\subset F^{n-m}H^{n+i}_{dR}(X_{0}).

(1.2)

The equivalence in Theorem A is a consequence of a certain duality statement: we prove that the
\kappa_{n+i}
is dual to the natural
map
H^{n-i}_{dR}(X)\to H^{n-i}(X,{\mathcal{O}})
, with respect to the Serre duality and the Poincare duality (see Proposition
2.3
).
The inclusion (
1.2
) was conjectured by Kontsevich.

It is natural to ask how to generalize Theorem A to give lower bounds for
F^{p}H^{i}_{dR}(X)
for
p>1
.
We prove a certain generalization of (
1.2
), which compares an analog of Hodge filtration for the hypercohomology
of the complex of integral forms on
X
with the classical Hodge filtration (see Theorem
2.2
).
Conjecturally, this should lead to the inclusion

F^{p+1}(X)\supset F^{2p+m+1}(X_{0}).

(see Sec.
2.2
).

The crucial role in our proof is played by a certain natural family of supervarieties
{\mathcal{X}}\to{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
, whose fiber over
t\neq 0
is isomorphic to
X
and whose
fiber over
0
is the split supervariety
X_{sp}
associated with
X
. This family is a superanalog of the standard deformation to the normal cone for the inclusion

X_{0}\subset X
.

2.
Hodge filtrations for the de Rham complex and for the complex of integral forms

2.1.
De Rham cohomology

Recall that for a smooth proper supervariety
X
we have the de Rham complex
\Omega^{\bullet}_{X}
(placed in degrees
\geq 0
).
The de Rham cohomology
H^{\bullet}_{dR}(X)
is defined as the hypercohomology of
\Omega^{\bullet}
.

Let
i:X_{0}\hookrightarrow X
be the bosonization of
X
, so
{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{0}}={\mathcal{O}}_{X}/{\mathcal{N}}
, where
{\mathcal{N}}
is the ideal generated by odd functions.
The natural map
\Omega^{\bullet}_{X}\to i_{*}\Omega^{\bullet}_{X_{0}}
is known to be a quasi-isomorphism (see e.g.,
[
3
]
), so it induces isomorphisms

H^{q}_{dR}(X)\simeq H^{q}_{dR}(X_{0})\simeq H^{q}(X_{0},\C),

(2.1)

where
H^{q}(X_{0},\C)
denotes the cohomology of the constant sheaf with respect to the analytic topology.
Equivalently, the inclusion of the constant sheaf
\C_{X}
into
\Omega^{\bullet}_{X}
induces an isomorphism on cohomology (in the analytic topology).

The Hodge filtration on de Rham cohomology is defined by

F^{p}H^{i}_{dR}(X):=\operatorname{im}(H^{i}(X,\sigma_{\geq p}\Omega^{\bullet})\to H^{i}(X,\Omega^{\bullet}),

where
\sigma_{\geq p}
denotes the stupid truncation of a complex.
Equivalently,
F^{p}H^{i}_{dR}(X)
is the kernel of the map to
H^{i}(X,\sigma_{\leq p-1}\Omega^{\bullet})
.

The commutative diagram

\begin{diagram}

(2.2)

shows that under the identification (
2.1
)
we have an inclusion
F^{p}H^{i}_{dR}(X)\subset F^{p}H^{i}_{dR}(X_{0})
.

Recall that
X
is called
split
if there is an isomorphism of algebras

{\mathcal{O}}_{X}\simeq\bigwedge_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{0}}}({\mathcal{V}})
for a vector bundle
{\mathcal{V}}
over

X_{0}
. A weaker condition is that
X
is
projected
, i.e., there exists a projection
p:X\to X_{0}
such that the composition

X_{0}\rTo{i}X\rTo{p}X_{0}
is the identity map.

Lemma 2.1
.

Assume that
X
is projected. Then under the identification (
2.1
) one has
F^{p}H^{i}_{dR}(X)=F^{p}H^{i}_{dR}(X_{0})
.

Proof.

Let
\pi:X\to X_{0}
be a projection. Then the pull-back by
\pi
gives a map

\pi^{*}:H^{i}(\Omega^{\geq p}_{X_{0}})\to H^{i}(\Omega^{\geq p}_{X})
whose composition with the pull-back by
i
is the identity
on
H^{i}(\Omega^{\geq p}_{X_{0}})
. Hence, the top horizontal map in the diagram (
2.2
)
is surjective, which implies the assertion.
∎

2.2.
Complex of integral forms

We denote by
\operatorname{Ber}_{X}
the Berezinian line bundle on
X
(obtained as the Berezinian of
\Omega^{1}_{X}
).
Recall that the complex of integral forms
\Sigma_{\bullet}=\Sigma_{X,\bullet}
is a complex of the form

\ldots\operatorname{Ber}_{X}\otimes{\bigwedge}^{2}T_{X}\rTo{\delta}\operatorname{Ber}_{X}\otimes T_{X}\rTo{\delta}\operatorname{Ber}_{X},

placed in degrees
\leq n
(so that
\Sigma_{n}=\operatorname{Ber}_{X}
). The formula for the differential
\delta
that uses the right connection on
\operatorname{Ber}_{X}
can
be found in
[
1
, ch. IV, 5.4]
,
[
2
]
.

It is known (see
[
2
]
) that there is a natural isomorphism

H^{i}(X_{0},\C)=H^{i}(X_{0},\Omega^{\bullet})\rTo{\sim}H^{i}(\Sigma_{\bullet})

(2.3)

and that the map

H^{n}(\operatorname{Ber})\to H^{2n}(\Sigma_{\bullet})\simeq H^{2n}(X_{0},\C)\simeq\C

(2.4)

is an isomorphism.

Mimicking the de Rham case, we can define the Hodge filtration on hypercohomology integral forms.
Namely, we define
F_{n-p}H^{i}(\Sigma_{\bullet})
to be the image of the map

\kappa_{i}^{n-p}:H^{i}(\sigma_{\geq n-p}\Sigma_{\bullet})\to H^{i}(\Sigma_{\bullet}).

For example, the image of the map
\kappa_{n+i}=\kappa_{n+i}^{n}
considered in (
1.1
) is
F_{n}H^{n+i}(\Sigma_{\bullet})
.

The next result is a generalization of the inclusion (
1.2
). It will be proved in Section
3
.

Theorem 2.2
.

One has an inclusion

F_{n-p}H^{i}(\Sigma_{X})\subset F_{n-2p-m}H^{i}(X_{0},\C).

It is natural to conjecture that the Hodge filtrations on the de Rham cohomology and on the hypercohomology of the complex of integral forms are related.

Conjecture B
.

F^{p+1}H^{i}(\Omega^{\bullet}_{X})
is equal to the orthogonal of
F_{n-p}H^{2n-i}(\Sigma_{X,\bullet})
with respect to the Poincare pairing on

H^{\bullet}(X_{0},\C)
.

Note that the analog of Conjecture B for usual (even) varieties is an easy corollary of the Hodge decomposition.
If Conjecture B is true then the statement of Theorem
2.2
is equivalent to the inclusion

F^{p+1}H^{i}_{dR}(X)\supset F^{2p+m+1}H^{i}_{dR}(X_{0}).

We can check Conjecture B for
p=0
.

Proposition 2.3
.

The map
\kappa_{n+i}:H^{i}(X,\operatorname{Ber}_{X})\to H^{n+i}(X_{0},\C)
is dual to the natural map
p_{n-i}:H^{n-i}(X_{0},\C)\to H^{n-i}(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{X})
,
where we use the Poincare pairing to identify
H^{n-i}(X_{0},\C)
with the dual of
H^{n+i}(X_{0},\C)
, and we use the Serre duality to
identify
H^{n-i}(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{X})
with the dual of
H^{i}(X,\operatorname{Ber}_{X})
.
Hence, Conjecture B holds for
p=0
.

Proof.

For a pair of classes
\alpha\in H^{i}(X,\operatorname{Ber}_{X})
and
\beta\in H^{n-i}(X_{0},\C)
, we have to check the equality

\langle\kappa_{i}(\alpha),\beta\rangle_{PD}=\langle\alpha,p_{n-i}(\beta)\rangle_{SD},

where on the left we use the Poincare duality and on the right we use the Serre duality.

By definition, the Serre duality is induced by the cup product and by the canonical functional on
H^{n}(X,\operatorname{Ber}_{X})
.
The latter canonical functional can be computed as the composition (
2.4
).
Hence, we need to check the following identity in
H^{2n}(X_{0},\C)
:

\kappa_{i}(\alpha)\cup\beta=\kappa_{n}(\alpha\cup p_{n-i}(\beta))=\kappa_{n}(\alpha\cup\beta),

where the cup-product
\alpha\cup\beta
is induced by the natural pairing of sheaves
\operatorname{Ber}_{X}\otimes\C_{X}\to\operatorname{Ber}_{X}
.
Now the assertion follows from the fact that the cup product on de Rham cohomology is induced by the sheaf pairing

\Sigma_{X,\bullet}\otimes\C_{X}\to\Sigma_{X,\bullet}
.
∎

Another case when Conjecture B is true is the case of a projected supervariety
X
, as follows from Lemma
2.1
and
the next result.

Assume that
X

) we have

F_{n-p}H^{i}(\Sigma_{X})=F^{n-p}H^{i}_{dR}(X_{0}).

Proof.

Let
\pi:X\to X_{0}
be a projection. It induces projections from integral forms on
X
to those on
X_{0}
, which gives a commutative

Here the bottom horizontal arrow is the inverse of the isomorphism (

On the other hand, the inlcusion

induces the map

such that
\pi_{*}\circ i_{*}=\operatorname{id}

2.3.
Gauss-Manin connection

There is a relative version
\Sigma_{{\mathcal{X}}/S}
of the complex of integral forms for a smooth proper morphism
\pi:{\mathcal{X}}\to S
.
Furthermore, for smooth
S
, there is a natural Gauss-Manin connection on
R\pi_{*}\Sigma_{{\mathcal{X}}/S}
.

The distribution
T_{{\mathcal{X}}/S}\subset T_{{\mathcal{X}}}
induces a filtration
G_{0}\Sigma_{\bullet}\subset G_{1}\Sigma_{{\mathcal{X}},\bullet}\subset\ldots\Sigma_{{\mathcal{X}},\bullet}
by subcomplexes in the complex of integral forms on
{\mathcal{X}}
, where

G_{0}\Sigma_{{\mathcal{X}},n-p}:=\operatorname{Ber}_{{\mathcal{X}}}\otimes{\bigwedge}^{p}(T_{{\mathcal{X}}/S}),\ \ G_{1}\Sigma_{{\mathcal{X}},n-p}:=\operatorname{Ber}_{{\mathcal{X}}}\otimes{\bigwedge}^{p-1}(T_{{\mathcal{X}}/S})\cdot T_{\mathcal{X}},\ldots

Using the identification
\operatorname{Ber}_{{\mathcal{X}}}\simeq\pi^{*}\operatorname{Ber}_{S}\otimes\operatorname{Ber}_{{\mathcal{X}}/S}
, we get isomorphisms of complexes

G_{0}\Sigma_{\mathcal{X}}\simeq\pi^{*}\operatorname{Ber}_{S}\otimes\Sigma_{{\mathcal{X}}/S},\ \ G_{1}\Sigma_{\mathcal{X}}/G_{0}\Sigma_{\mathcal{X}}\simeq\pi^{*}\operatorname{Ber}_{S}\otimes\Sigma_{{\mathcal{X}}/S}[1]\otimes\pi^{*}T_{S},

Thus, a short exact sequence of complexes

0\to G_{0}\to G_{1}\to G_{1}/G_{0}\to 0

(2.5)

leads to an exact triangle

\operatorname{Ber}_{S}\otimes R\pi_{*}\Sigma_{{\mathcal{X}}/S}\to R\pi_{*}G_{1}\to\operatorname{Ber}_{S}\otimes R\pi_{*}\Sigma_{{\mathcal{X}}/S}[1]\otimes\pi^{*}T_{S}\to\operatorname{Ber}_{S}\otimes R\pi_{*}\Sigma_{{\mathcal{X}}/S}[1]

We can view the corresponding morphism

\nabla_{r}:\operatorname{Ber}_{S}\otimes R\pi_{*}\Sigma_{{\mathcal{X}}/S}\otimes\pi^{*}T_{S}\to\operatorname{Ber}_{S}\otimes R\pi_{*}\Sigma_{{\mathcal{X}}/S}

as a right connection on

Using the next term of the filtration,

By the standard relation between left and right connections (see

Considering stupid truncations of the exact sequence (

3.
Deformation to normal cone

3.1.
Construction

We consider an analog of the deformation to normal cone for the embedding
X_{0}\hookrightarrow X
.
This will be a family of supervarieties
\pi:{\mathcal{X}}\to{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
, equipped with an identification

\pi^{-1}({\mathbb{A}}^{1}\setminus\{0\})=X\times({\mathbb{A}}^{1}\setminus\{0\}),

(3.1)

and an identification of
\pi^{-1}(0)
with the split supervariety
X_{sp}
associated with
X
.
In addition, we will have a natural affine morphism
f:{\mathcal{X}}\to X
, restricting to the natural projection on
\pi^{-1}({\mathbb{A}}^{1}\setminus\{0\})
.

We define
{\mathcal{X}}
using the following
\Z
-graded quasicoherent sheaf of
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-algebras:

f_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{X}}}=\ldots\oplus{\mathcal{N}}^{2}\cdot t^{-2}\oplus{\mathcal{N}}\cdot t^{-1}\oplus{\mathcal{O}}_{X}\oplus{\mathcal{O}}_{X}\cdot t\oplus{\mathcal{O}}_{X}\cdot t^{2}\oplus\ldots,

where
t
is a formal variable, with the obvious algebra structure.

The map to
X\times{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
corresponds to the natural inclusion
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}[t]\hookrightarrow f_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{X}}}
.
On the other hand, the embedding
f_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{X}}}\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{X}[t,t^{-1}]
corresponds to inverting
t
, so it gives an identification
(
3.1
).

Finally, the fiber over
t=0
corresponds to the algebra

f_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{X}}}/t\cdot f_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{X}}}\simeq\ldots\oplus{\mathcal{N}}^{2}/{\mathcal{N}}^{3}\oplus{\mathcal{N}}/{\mathcal{N}}^{2}\oplus{\mathcal{O}}_{X}/{\mathcal{N}},

which gives the split supervariety
X_{sp}
.

Note also that the bosonization of
{\mathcal{X}}
is canonically identified with
X_{0}\times{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

(i) One has an equality

t^{m}f^{*}\operatorname{Ber}_{X}=\operatorname{Ber}_{{\mathcal{X}}/{\mathbb{A}}^{1}},

where
(n|m)
is the dimension of
X
(we view both sides as subsheaves in the sheaf of rational sections of
f^{*}\operatorname{Ber}_{X}
).

(ii) For
p\geq 0
, one has an inclusion

t^{m+p}f^{*}\Sigma_{X,n-p}\subset\Sigma_{{\mathcal{X}}/{\mathbb{A}}^{1},n-p}.

Proof.

(i) The statement is clear over
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}\setminus\{0\}
, so it is enough to check it in the neighborhood of each point of
\pi^{-1}(0)
.
Let
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{m})
be local coordinates of the corresponding point of
X
. Then

(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},\theta_{1}/t,\ldots,\theta_{m}/t)
are relative local coordinates for
{\mathcal{X}}/{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
, which immediately implies the assertion.

(ii) This follows from (i) and from the inclusion
t\cdot f^{*}T_{X}\subset T_{{\mathcal{X}}/S}
which is proved using the same relative local coordinates as in (i).
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

For each
i
, the canonical identification

H^{i}R\pi_{*}\Sigma_{{\mathcal{X}}/{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}|_{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}\setminus 0}\simeq H^{i}(X,\Sigma_{X})\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}\setminus 0}\simeq H^{i}(X_{0},\C)\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}\setminus 0}

extends to an identification

H^{i}R\pi_{*}\Sigma_{{\mathcal{X}}/{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}\simeq H^{i}(X_{0},\C)\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}.

Proof.

Let
b:X_{0}\times{\mathbb{A}}^{1}\to{\mathcal{X}}
denote the embedding of the bosonization. Then
the assertion follows from the fact that the natural map

b_{*}\Omega^{\bullet}_{X_{0}\times{\mathbb{A}}^{1}/{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}\to\Sigma_{{\mathcal{X}}/{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}

induces an isomorphism on
R\pi_{*}
.
∎

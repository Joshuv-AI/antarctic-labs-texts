Convergence Analyses of Davis–Yin Splitting via Scaled Relative Graphs
Jongmin Lee †‡, Soheun Yi §‡, Ernest K. Ryu †

Abstract
Davis–Yin splitting (DYS) has found a wide range of applications in optimization, but its linear rates of convergence have not been studied extensively. The scaled relative graph (SRG) simplifies the convergence analysis of operator splitting methods by mapping the action of the operator to the complex plane, but the prior SRG theory did not fully apply to the DYS operator. In this work, we formalize an SRG theory for the DYS operator and use it to obtain tighter contraction factors.



keywords

Convex optimization, Splitting methods, Monotone operators, Euclidean geometry

Introduction

The Davis–Yin splitting (DYS) operator
[
18
]
encodes solutions of the three-operator monotone inclusion problem

\displaystyle\underset{x\in{\mathcal{H}}}{\text{find}}\quad 0\in({\mathbb{A}}+{\mathbb{B}}+{\mathbb{C}})x,

where
{\mathcal{H}}
is a real Hilbert space,
{\mathbb{A}}
and
{\mathbb{B}}
are monotone, and
{\mathbb{C}}
is cocoercive,
as fixed-points of the operator

{\mathbb{T}}={\mathbb{I}}-{\mathbb{J}}_{\alpha{\mathbb{B}}}+{\mathbb{J}}_{\alpha{\mathbb{A}}}(2{\mathbb{J}}_{\alpha{\mathbb{B}}}-{\mathbb{I}}-\alpha{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{J}}_{\alpha{\mathbb{B}}}),

where
\alpha>0
and
{\mathbb{J}}_{\alpha{\mathbb{A}}}
and
{\mathbb{J}}_{\alpha{\mathbb{B}}}
are the resolvents of
{\mathbb{A}}
and
{\mathbb{B}}
.
DYS generalizes the forward-backward splitting (FBS) and Douglas–Rachford splitting (DRS) operators, and it has found a wide range of applications
[
48
,
46
,
7
,
44
,
47
,
24
]
in optimization. However, linear convergence rates of DYS iterations have not been studied extensively.

On the other hand, the recently proposed scaled relative graph (SRG)
[
38
]
allows one to analyze the convergence of operator splitting methods by mapping the action of a multi-valued nonlinear operator to the extended complex plane, analogous to how the spectrum maps the action of a linear operator to the complex plane. Prior work used the SRG to analyze rates of convergence for FBS and DRS
[
38
]
, but the prior SRG theory did not fully apply to the DYS operator.

In this paper, we analyze convergence rates of DYS iterations using the SRG. First, we extend the SRG theory to accommodate the DYS operator. We then establish linear rates of convergence of DYS by bounding the maximum modulus of the SRG on the complex plane. We furthermore provide a new result on averagedness coefficients of DYS.

This breakthrough has found a wide range of new applications

However, the study of linear convergence rates of DYS has been limited. Bredies, Chenchene, Lorenz, and Naldi studied the convergence of DYS under a wider range of stepsizes

In general, we follow standard convex operator theoretical notations as in

Define the graph of an operator

\mathrm{graph}({\mathbb{A}})=\{(x,u)\in{\mathcal{H}}\times{\mathcal{H}}\,|\,u\in{\mathbb{A}}x\}.

For notational simplicity, we often do not distinguish an operator from its graph and, for example, write

.
Define the inverse of
{\mathbb{A}}
as

{\mathbb{A}}^{-1}=\{(u,x)\,|\,(x,u)\in{\mathbb{A}}\},

\alpha{\mathbb{A}}=\{(x,\alpha u)\,|\,(x,u)\in{\mathbb{A}}\},

Define the resolvent of

{\mathbb{J}}_{\alpha{\mathbb{A}}}=({\mathbb{I}}+\alpha{\mathbb{A}})^{-1}.

Define addition and composition of operators
{\mathbb{A}}\colon{\mathcal{H}}\rightrightarrows{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle{\mathbb{A}}+{\mathbb{B}}

\displaystyle=\left\{(x,u+v)\,|\,(x,u)\in{\mathbb{A}},(x,v)\in{\mathbb{B}}\right\},

\displaystyle{\mathbb{A}}{\mathbb{B}}

\displaystyle=\left\{(x,s)\,|\,\exists\,u\;\text{such that}\;(x,u)\in{\mathbb{B}},(u,s)\in{\mathbb{A}}\right\}.

We call

Operators in a same class need not be defined on the same Hilbert space.
For an operator

,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{A}}+{\mathcal{B}}

\displaystyle=\{{\mathbb{A}}+{\mathbb{B}}\,|\,{\mathbb{B}}\in{\mathcal{B}},{\mathbb{B}}\colon{\mathcal{H}}\rightrightarrows{\mathcal{H}}\},

\displaystyle{\mathbb{A}}{\mathcal{B}}

\displaystyle=\{{\mathbb{A}}{\mathbb{B}}\,|\,{\mathbb{B}}\in{\mathcal{B}},{\mathbb{B}}\colon{\mathcal{H}}\rightrightarrows{\mathcal{H}}\}

For an operator class
{\mathcal{A}}

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}+{\mathcal{B}}=\{{\mathbb{A}}+{\mathbb{B}}\,|\,

We now define some special operator classes.
Define the class of monotone operators as

{\mathcal{M}}=\{{\mathbb{A}}\colon{\mathcal{H}}\rightrightarrows{\mathcal{H}}\,|\,\langle x-y,u-v\rangle\geq 0,\forall\,u\in{\mathbb{A}}x,v\in{\mathbb{A}}y,\,{\mathcal{H}}\text{ is a Hilbert space}\}.

For

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}_{\mu}=\{{\mathbb{A}}\colon{\mathcal{H}}\rightrightarrows{\mathcal{H}}\,|\,

\displaystyle\langle x-y,u-v\rangle\geq\mu\lVert x-y\rVert^{2},\,\forall\,u\in{\mathbb{A}}x,v\in{\mathbb{A}}y,

For

, define the class of

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}_{L}=\{{\mathbb{A}}\colon\mathrm{dom}\,{\mathbb{A}}\to{\mathcal{H}}\,|\,

\displaystyle\lVert u-v\rVert^{2}\leq L^{2}\lVert x-y\rVert^{2},\forall\,u\in{\mathbb{A}}x,v\in{\mathbb{A}}y,

\displaystyle\mathrm{dom}\,{\mathbb{A}}\subseteq{\mathcal{H}},\,{\mathcal{H}}\text{ is a Hilbert space}\}.

For
\beta\in(0,\infty)
, define the class of
\beta
-cocoercive operators as

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}_{\beta}=\{{\mathbb{A}}\colon\mathrm{dom}\,{\mathbb{A}}\to{\mathcal{H}}\,|\,

\displaystyle\langle x-y,u-v\rangle\geq\beta\lVert u-v\rVert^{2},\forall\,u\in{\mathbb{A}}x,v\in{\mathbb{A}}y,

\displaystyle\mathrm{dom}\,{\mathbb{A}}\subseteq{\mathcal{H}},\,{\mathcal{H}}\text{ is a Hilbert space}\}.

For
\theta\in(0,1)
, define the class of
\theta

{\mathcal{N}}_{\theta}=(1-\theta){\mathbb{I}}+\theta{\mathcal{L}}_{1}.

Note that we automatically identify Lipschitz and cocoercive operators, as well as resolvents of monotone operators, as singled-value functions since they are necessarily single-valued.
For notational convenience, we write

{\mathcal{M}}_{0}={\mathcal{C}}_{0}={\mathcal{M}},\quad{\mathcal{L}}_{\infty}=\left\{{\mathbb{A}}\colon{\mathcal{H}}\rightrightarrows{\mathcal{H}}\,|\,{\mathcal{H}}\text{ is a Hilbert space}\right\}.

To clarify,

{\mathcal{M}}_{0}\neq\bigcup_{\mu>0}{\mathcal{M}}_{\mu},\quad{\mathcal{C}}_{0}\neq\bigcup_{\beta>0}{\mathcal{C}}_{\beta},\quad{\mathcal{L}}_{\infty}\neq\bigcup_{L<\infty}{\mathcal{L}}_{L}.

Arc property

Denote
\overline{z}
the conjugate of
z\in\mathbb{C}
.
Define the right-hand arc between

as

\mathrm{Arc}^{+}(z,\overline{z})=\left\{re^{i(1-2\theta)\varphi}\,\middle|\,z=re^{i\varphi},\varphi\in(-\pi,\pi],r\geq 0,\theta\in[0,1]\right\}

and the left-hand arc between

as

\mathrm{Arc}^{-}(z,\overline{z})=-\mathrm{Arc}^{+}(-z,-\overline{z}).

Note that both

If an SRG-full class

satisfies

\mathrm{Arc}^{+}(z,\overline{z})\subseteq{\mathcal{G}}\left({\mathcal{A}}\right),\qquad\forall z\in{\mathcal{G}}\left({\mathcal{A}}\right)\setminus\{\infty\},

we say
{\mathcal{A}}
satisfies the

. Similarly, if

\mathrm{Arc}^{-}(z,\overline{z})\subseteq{\mathcal{G}}\left({\mathcal{A}}\right),\qquad\forall z\in{\mathcal{G}}\left({\mathcal{A}}\right)\setminus\{\infty\},

holds, we say
{\mathcal{A}}
has the
left-arc property
.
If an SRG-full class
{\mathcal{A}}
has either the right-arc property or the left-arc property,
then it is said to have

The maximum modulus theorem

Define the boundary of
A\subseteq\mathbb{C}
as
\partial A=\mathrm{cl}{A}\setminus\mathrm{int}A
,
where
\mathrm{cl}{A}
and
\mathrm{int}A
are the closure and interior of
A
, respectively.

Fact 7
(The maximum modulus theorem
[
42
]
).

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}
be a compact set.
If
h\colon\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is holomorphic on all of
\,\mathbb{C}
, then
\max_{z\in D}|h(z)|
is attained at some point of
\partial D
.

This maximum modulus theorem simplifies our calculations of Sections
3
and
4
via the following lemma.

Lemma 1
.

Let
f\colon\mathbb{C}^{3}\to\mathbb{C}
be a polynomial of three complex variables.
Let
A
,
B
, and
C
be compact subsets of
\,\mathbb{C}
.
Then,

\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}z_{A}\in A,z_{B}\in B,\\
z_{C}\in C\end{subarray}}\lvert f(z_{A},z_{B},z_{C})\rvert=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}z_{A}\in\partial A,z_{B}\in\partial B,\\
z_{C}\in\partial C\end{subarray}}\lvert f(z_{A},z_{B},z_{C})\rvert.

Proof.

As
f
is a polynomial,
\lvert f\rvert
is continuous on its compact domain
A\times B\times C\subset\mathbb{C}^{3}
. Hence,
\lvert f\rvert
attains its maximum at some point
(z_{A}^{0},z_{B}^{0},z_{C}^{0})\in A\times B\times C
.

Furthermore,
f
is holomorphic in each argument with the other two arguments fixed as it is a polynomial.
Then, if we fix
z_{B}=z_{B}^{0}
and
z_{C}=z_{C}^{0}
,
there exists some
z_{A}^{1}\in\partial A
such that

\lvert f(z_{A}^{1},z_{B}^{0},z_{C}^{0})\rvert=\lvert f(z_{A}^{0},z_{B}^{0},z_{C}^{0})\rvert

by Fact
7
. Applying similar procedure with respect to other arguments, we obtain
z_{B}^{1}\in\partial B
and
z_{C}^{1}\in\partial C
such that

\displaystyle\lvert f(z_{A}^{1},z_{B}^{1},z_{C}^{1})\rvert

\displaystyle=\lvert f(z_{A}^{0},z_{B}^{0},z_{C}^{0})\rvert

\displaystyle=\max_{z_{A}\in A,z_{B}\in B,z_{C}\in C}\,\lvert f(z_{A},z_{B},z_{C})\rvert.

2
SRG theory

Let

\displaystyle{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathbb{A}},{\mathbb{B}},{\mathbb{C}},\alpha,\lambda}

\displaystyle=(1-\lambda){\mathbb{I}}+\lambda\left({\mathbb{I}}-{\mathbb{J}}_{\alpha{\mathbb{B}}}+{\mathbb{J}}_{\alpha{\mathbb{A}}}(2{\mathbb{J}}_{\alpha{\mathbb{B}}}-{\mathbb{I}}-\alpha{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{J}}_{\alpha{\mathbb{B}}})\right)

\displaystyle={\mathbb{I}}-\lambda{\mathbb{J}}_{\alpha{\mathbb{B}}}+\lambda{\mathbb{J}}_{\alpha{\mathbb{A}}}(2{\mathbb{J}}_{\alpha{\mathbb{B}}}-{\mathbb{I}}-\alpha{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{J}}_{\alpha{\mathbb{B}}})

be the (averaged) DYS operator for operators

, and

Analogously, define

\displaystyle=1-\lambda z_{B}+\lambda z_{A}(2z_{B}-1-\alpha z_{C}z_{B})

\displaystyle=1-\lambda z_{A}-\lambda z_{B}+\lambda(2-\alpha z_{C})z_{A}z_{B}

{\mathcal{A}}

In the Theorems

2.1
Quotient operator

For
{\mathbb{A}}\colon{\mathcal{H}}\rightrightarrows{\mathcal{H}}
and
{\mathbb{B}}\colon{\mathcal{H}}\rightrightarrows{\mathcal{H}}

\frac{{\mathbb{A}}}{{\mathbb{B}}}={\mathbb{A}}/{\mathbb{B}}={\mathbb{A}}{\mathbb{B}}^{-1}.

Correspondingly, for a class of operators

\frac{{\mathcal{A}}}{{\mathbb{B}}}={\mathcal{A}}/{\mathbb{B}}={\mathcal{A}}{\mathbb{B}}^{-1}.

For a single-valued operator
{\mathbb{A}}\colon{\mathcal{H}}\rightrightarrows{\mathcal{H}}

with respect to distinct

,

{\mathbb{A}}_{x,y}=\begin{cases}\{(x,{\mathbb{A}}x),(y,{\mathbb{A}}y)\}&\text{if }x,y\in\mathrm{dom}\,{\mathbb{A}},\\

We clarify that

.

We point out some immediate consequences.

Then, for distinct

\emptyset&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

(1)

If

is a single-valued operator, then

\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in{\mathcal{H}}\\

(2)

Lemma 5

2.2

We first establish a product theorem for SRGs, which is of independent interest. This product theorem differs from

For an operator

\displaystyle\colon{\mathcal{H}}\times{\mathcal{H}}\rightrightarrows{\mathcal{H}}\times{\mathcal{H}}

is essentially the same operator as

, with

embedded into

One can observe that

\displaystyle\imath({\mathbb{A}}+{\mathbb{B}})=\imath({\mathbb{A}})+\imath({\mathbb{B}}),\quad\imath({\mathbb{A}}{\mathbb{B}})=\imath({\mathbb{A}})\imath({\mathbb{B}}),

\displaystyle\imath(\alpha{\mathbb{A}})=\alpha\imath({\mathbb{A}}),\quad\imath({\mathbb{I}}+{\mathbb{A}})={\mathbb{I}}+\imath({\mathbb{A}}),\quad\imath({\mathbb{A}}^{-1})=\imath({\mathbb{A}})^{-1},

We omit the proof of the first five statements for conciseness, which can be done by a simple algebra.

and
{\mathcal{C}}

,
{\mathbb{B}}\in{\mathcal{B}}
, and
{\mathbb{C}}\in{\mathcal{C}}

\displaystyle{\mathcal{G}}\left({\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathbb{A}},{\mathbb{B}},{\mathbb{C}},\alpha,\lambda}\right)={\mathcal{G}}\left(\imath({\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathbb{A}},{\mathbb{B}},{\mathbb{C}},\alpha,\lambda})\right)={\mathcal{G}}\left({\mathbb{T}}_{\imath({\mathbb{A}}),\imath({\mathbb{B}}),\imath({\mathbb{C}}),\alpha,\lambda}\right)

\imath({\mathbb{A}})\in{\mathcal{A}}

\imath({\mathbb{B}})\in{\mathcal{B}}

\imath({\mathbb{C}})\in{\mathcal{C}}

\displaystyle\lvert\zeta_{\text{DYS}}(z_{A},z_{B},z_{C^{\prime}};\alpha,1)-(1-\theta)\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle=\lvert\theta^{-1}\left({z_{A}-\theta}\right)\left({z_{B}-\theta}\right)+(2-\theta^{-1}-\alpha z_{C^{\prime}})z_{A}z_{B}\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left({\theta^{-1}\lvert z_{A}-\theta\rvert\lvert z_{B}-\theta\rvert+\lvert 2-\theta^{-1}-\alpha z_{C^{\prime}}\rvert\lvert z_{A}\rvert\lvert z_{B}\rvert}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\sqrt{\theta^{-1}\lvert z_{A}-\theta\rvert^{2}\cdot\theta^{-1}\lvert z_{B}-\theta\rvert^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\sqrt{\lvert 2-\theta^{-1}-\alpha z_{C^{\prime}}\rvert^{2}\left({2-\theta^{-1}}\right)^{-1}\lvert z_{A}\rvert^{2}\cdot\left({2-\theta^{-1}}\right)\lvert z_{B}\rvert^{2}}\bigg)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left({\theta^{-1}\lvert z_{A}-\theta\rvert^{2}+\lvert 2-\theta^{-1}-\alpha z_{C^{\prime}}\rvert^{2}\left({2-\theta^{-1}}\right)^{-1}\lvert z_{A}\rvert^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\left({\theta^{-1}\lvert z_{B}-\theta\rvert^{2}+\left({2-\theta^{-1}}\right)\lvert z_{B}\rvert^{2}}\right),

where the first inequality follows from the triangle inequality and the second is from Lemma
13
.

The second factor simplifies to

\theta^{-1}\lvert z_{B}-\theta\rvert^{2}+\left({2-\theta^{-1}}\right)\lvert z_{B}\rvert^{2}=2\left|z_{B}-\frac{1}{2}\right|^{2}+\theta-\frac{1}{2}=\theta

since
z_{B}\in\partial{\mathcal{G}}\left({\mathbb{J}}_{\alpha{\mathcal{B}}}\right)={\mathrm{Circ}}\left(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right)
.

The first factor simplifies to

\displaystyle\theta^{-1}\lvert z_{A}-\theta\rvert^{2}+\lvert 2-\theta^{-1}-\alpha z_{C^{\prime}}\rvert^{2}\left({2-\theta^{-1}}\right)^{-1}\lvert z_{A}\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{(i)}}}{{=}}\theta^{-1}\lvert z_{A}-\theta\rvert^{2}+R^{2}(2-\theta^{-1})^{-1}\lvert z_{A}\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\theta^{-1}+R^{2}(2-\theta^{-1})^{-1}\right)\left|z_{A}-\frac{1}{\theta^{-1}+R^{2}(2-\theta^{-1})^{-1}}\right|^{2}+\theta-\frac{1}{\theta^{-1}+R^{2}(2-\theta^{-1})^{-1}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{(ii)}}}{{=}}2(1+\alpha\mu_{\mathcal{A}})\left|z_{A}-\frac{1}{2(1+\alpha\mu_{\mathcal{A}})}\right|^{2}+\theta-\frac{1}{2(1+\alpha\mu_{\mathcal{A}})}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{(iii)}}}{{=}}\theta,

where we used
z_{C^{\prime}}\in\partial{\mathcal{G}}\left({\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}\right)={\mathrm{Circ}}\left(\frac{2-\theta^{-1}}{\alpha},\frac{R}{\alpha}\right)
in (i),
\theta^{-1}+R^{2}(2-\theta^{-1})^{-1}=2(1+\alpha\mu_{\mathcal{A}})
in (ii), and

z_{A}\in\partial{\mathcal{G}}\left({\mathbb{J}}_{\alpha{\mathcal{A}}}\right)={\mathrm{Circ}}\left(\frac{1}{2(1+\alpha\mu_{\mathcal{A}})},\frac{1}{2(1+\alpha\mu_{\mathcal{A}})}\right)
in (iii).
Thus, we conclude

\lvert\zeta_{\text{DYS}}(z_{A},z_{B},z_{C^{\prime}};\alpha,1)-(1-\theta)\rvert^{2}\leq\theta^{2}.

Since
\zeta_{\text{DYS}}(z_{A},z_{B},z_{C^{\prime}};\alpha,\lambda)
is symmetric with respect to
z_{A}
and
z_{B}
, the second statement follows from the same argument.

The averagedness coefficient of Theorem
15
is, in fact, tight in the sense that it cannot be reduced (improved) without further assumptions. This can be proved by showing that there is a
z\in{\mathcal{Z}}^{\text{DYS}}_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}},{\mathcal{C}},\alpha,1}
such that
z\neq 1
and
z\in\partial{\mathcal{G}}({\mathcal{N}}_{\theta})
. However, we omit this proof for the sake of conciseness.

5
Conclusion

In this work, we extended the SRG theory to accommodate the DYS operator and used this theory to analyze the convergence of DYS iterations. In our view, the SRG theory of Section
2
opens the door to many avenues of future work on splitting methods, and the analyses of Section
3
serve as demonstrations of such possibilities.
One possible future direction is to further refine the analysis on the complex plane and obtain tighter contraction factors, as the contraction factors
\rho
presented in Section
3
are not tight in the sense that

\rho>\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\mathbb{T}}\in{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}},{\mathcal{C}},\alpha,\lambda}\\
x,y\in\mathrm{dom}\,{\mathbb{T}},\,x\neq y\end{subarray}}\frac{\lVert{\mathbb{T}}x-{\mathbb{T}}y\rVert}{\lVert x-y\rVert}.

Another direction is to characterize optimal or near-optimal choices of parameters
\alpha
and
\lambda
given a set of assumptions on the operators.

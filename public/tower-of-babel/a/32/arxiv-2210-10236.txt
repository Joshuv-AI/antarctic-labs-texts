Extremal tensor products of Demazure crystals Thanks: S.A. supported by Simons Award 953878.
Sami Assaf, Anne Dranowski, Nicolle González

Abstract
. Demazure crystals are subcrystals of highest weight irreducible 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} -crystals. In this article, we study tensor products of a larger class of subcrystals, called extremal, and give a local characterization for exactly when the tensor product of Demazure crystals is extremal. We then show that tensor products of Demazure crystals decompose into direct sums of Demazure crystals if and only if the tensor product is extremal, thus providing a sufficient and necessary local criterion for when the tensor product of Demazure crystals is itself Demazure. As an application, we show that the primary component in the tensor square of any Demazure crystal is always Demazure.

Primary 05E10; Secondary 05E16, 20G42

1.
Introduction

For
G
a connected, simply-connected, semi-simple Lie group, the finite dimensional simple
G
-modules
V(\lambda)
are indexed by
\lambda\in P^{+}
the set of dominant weights.
For
w
an element of the Weyl group
W
and
B\subset G
a Borel subgroup, the
Demazure module

V_{w}(\lambda)
is the
B
-submodule of
V(\lambda)
generated by the one-dimensional extremal weight space of
V_{w}(\lambda)
with weight
w\lambda

[
4
]
.

Mathieu
[
21
]
proved a conjecture of Polo
[
24
]
stating that if one twists a Demazure module by an anti-dominant character, then the resulting
B
-module can be filtered with successive quotients given by Demazure modules.
Such a filtration is called an
excellent filtration
. Excellent filtrations for
B
-modules generalize the notion of
good filtrations
for
G
-modules, where successive quotients are Weyl modules. We identify simple modules and their Weyl modules. The tensor product of two
G
-modules with good filtrations has a good filtration, but having excellent filtrations is not, in general, preserved by tensor products for
B
-modules
[
25
]
.

Kashiwara
[
12
]
introduced crystal bases as set-theoretic abstractions of Lusztig’s geometric canonical bases
[
20
]
of representations of quantized universal enveloping algebras, at
q=0
.
In particular, Weyl modules admit crystals bases and we denote the crystal of
V(\lambda)
by
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
.

Crystals carry some of the structure of their modules. For example, the character of a module can be computed from its crystal.
Moreover, there is a simple combinatorial rule for defining the tensor product crystal
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mu)
as a disjoint union of crystals which exactly matches the representations appearing in the decomposition of
V(\lambda)\otimes V(\mu)
.
Note this gives a crystal-theoretic proof that tensor products of modules with good filtrations once again have good filtrations.

The Demazure character formula
[
5
]
was proved by Joseph
[
10
]
and generalized by Ishii
[
9
]
. For
w\in W
, Littelmann
[
18
]
conjectured, and proved for classical types, the existence of a subset
\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)\subseteq\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
whose character corresponds to that of the Demazure module
V_{w}(\lambda)
. Kashiwara
[
13
]
extended the definition of Demazure crystals and gave an explicit construction for
\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)
.

For
u,v\in W
and
\mu,\nu\in P^{+}
, the subset
\mathcal{B}_{u}(\mu)\otimes\mathcal{B}_{v}(\nu)
of
\mathcal{B}(\mu)\otimes\mathcal{B}(\nu)
is not, in general, a direct sum of Demazure crystals. In part, this reflects the fact that the tensor product module
V_{u}(\mu)\otimes V_{v}(\nu)
does not, in general, admit an excellent filtration.
On the other hand, this reflects the fact that the tensor product crystal

\mathcal{B}_{u}(\mu)\otimes\mathcal{B}_{v}(\nu)
is not, in general, the crystal of
V_{u}(\mu)\otimes V_{v}(\nu)
.

Lakshmibai, Littelmann and Magyar
[
16
]
and independently Joseph
[
11
]
proved
\mathcal{B}_{e}(\mu)\otimes\mathcal{B}_{v}(\nu)
is a direct sum of Demazure crystals, giving a combinatorial version of Mathieu’s result
[
21
]
.
There are other special cases of tensors of Demazure crystals studied using Kirillov–Reshetikhin crystals
[
23
,
22
,
17
,
7
]
.
Kouno
[
15
]
recently gave a complete characterization for when
\mathcal{B}_{u}(\mu)\otimes\mathcal{B}_{v}(\nu)
is a direct sum of Demazure crystals. This characterization, however, considers certain combinatorial properties that depend on knowing exactly which Weyl group elements and dominant weights index the Demazure crystals being tensored.

In this paper, we replace Kouno’s global condition with a local criterion that does not require knowledge of the combinatorial properties above and hence is more generally applicable. More specifically, we study a broader class of subsets of crystals which we call
extremal
. These are subsets
X\subseteq\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
characterized by the so-call
string property
: for any
i
-string
S\subset\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
with highest weight element
b
, the intersection
S\cap X
is either
\varnothing
,
\{b\}
, or
S
. Kashiwara
[
13
]
notes extremality is one of the remarkable properties enjoyed by the Demazure crystals
\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)
. Thus, while all Demazure crystals are extremal, not all extremal subsets are Demazure.

As with the Demazure property, the extremal property is not preserved by tensor products. Our first main result is a local characterization for when the tensor product of extremal subsets is extremal. Here the
e_{i}
and
f_{i}
are the raising and lowering operators, respectively, on the crystals.

Theorem 1.1
.

For
X\subset\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
and
Y\subset\mathcal{B}(\mu)
extremal subsets, the tensor product
X\otimes Y
is an extremal subset of
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mu)
if and only if for every
x\otimes y\in X\otimes Y
for which
e_{i}(x\otimes y)=e_{i}(x)\otimes y\neq 0
and
f_{i}(x\otimes y)=x\otimes f_{i}(y)\neq 0
, we have
f_{i}(y)\in Y
.

Any tensor product of Demazure crystals which decomposes as a direct sum of Demazure crystals is also extremal, since Demazure crystals are extremal. Our second main result is that, remarkably, the converse is true as well.

Theorem 1.2
.

For
\lambda,\mu\in P^{+}
and
w,u\in W
, we have
\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal{B}_{u}(\mu)
is a direct sum of Demazure crystals if and only if it is extremal.

Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
give a new, local characterization to determine when a tensor product retains its Demazure structure. We apply this characterization to show the connected component of
\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)^{\otimes m}
containing
b_{\lambda}\otimes\cdots\otimes b_{\lambda}
is a direct sum of Demazure crystals, even when the full product is not.

We conclude with some evidence suggesting the existence of a modified definition of the tensor product rule for crystals which preserves the extremal property. Such a rule might lead to a characterization for which tensor products of Demazure modules admit excellent filtrations.

2.
Normal crystals

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be the complex reductive Lie algebra associated with the Lie group
G
. Let
I
be the set of vertices of the Dynkin diagram of
\mathfrak{g}
, and let
P
be the weight lattice of
\mathfrak{g}
. For each
i\in I
, we have the simple root
\alpha_{i}\in P
and its coroot
\alpha^{\vee}\in P^{\vee}=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(P,\mathbb{Z})
. We review Kashiwara’s theory of
\mathfrak{g}
-crystal, restricting to the category of highest weight crystals. For further details, see
[
14
]
.

A (normal)
\mathfrak{g}
-crystal

\mathcal{B}
consists of a (finite) set
\mathcal{B}
together with maps

\mathrm{wt}:\mathcal{B}\rightarrow P,\hskip 10.00002pt\varepsilon_{i},\varphi_{i}:\mathcal{B}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z},\hskip 10.00002pte_{i},f_{i}:\mathcal{B}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}\sqcup\{0\}

subject to the following axioms for all
i\in I
and all
b,b^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}
,

(C1)

\varphi_{i}(b)-\varepsilon_{i}(b)=\langle\alpha^{\vee}_{i},\mathrm{wt}(b)\rangle
;

(C2)

if
e_{i}(b)\in\mathcal{B}
, then
\mathrm{wt}(e_{i}(b))=\mathrm{wt}(b)+\alpha_{i}
;

if
f_{i}(b)\in\mathcal{B}
, then
\mathrm{wt}(f_{i}(b))=\mathrm{wt}(b)-\alpha_{i}
;

(C3)

b^{\prime}=e_{i}(b)
if and only if
b=f_{i}(b^{\prime})
;

(C4)

\varepsilon_{i}(b)=\max\{k\geq 0\mid e_{i}^{k}(b)\in\mathcal{B}\}
, and

\varphi_{i}(b)=\max\{k\geq 0\mid f_{i}^{k}(b)\in\mathcal{B}\}
.

The
crystal operators

\{e_{i},f_{i}\}_{i\in I}
are the
q=0
limits of the Chevalley generators
of the quantum group
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
.

Let
P^{+}=\{\lambda\in P\mid\langle\alpha^{\vee}_{i},\lambda\rangle\geq 0\ \forall i\}
be the set of
dominant weights
. This set naturally indexes the finite dimensional irreducible integrable
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
-modules. For
\lambda\in P^{+}
, let
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
be the normal crystal associated with the crystal base of the simple
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
-module with highest weight
\lambda
.

Following
[
11
]
we introduce the monoids
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{F}
generated by
\{e_{i}\}_{i\in I}
and
\{f_{i}\}_{i\in I}
, respectively.
For
b_{\lambda}
the unique element of
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
with weight
\lambda
, we have

\mathcal{E}\{b_{\lambda}\}=0,\mbox{ and }\mathcal{F}\{b_{\lambda}\}=\mathcal{B}(\lambda)\sqcup\{0\}.

In general, we say an element
b\in\mathcal{B}
is
highest weight
if
\mathcal{E}\{b\}=0
.

A connected crystal
\mathcal{B}
is a
highest weight crystal with highest weight
\lambda
if there exists a highest weight element
b_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{B}
of weight
\lambda
such that
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{F}\{b_{\lambda}\}
.

Given two
\mathfrak{g}
-crystals
\mathcal{B},\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
, a
morphism
from
\mathcal{B}
to
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
is a map which commutes with the structure maps. Note not every highest weight crystal with highest weight
\lambda
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
. Henceforth, we consider only those
\mathfrak{g}
-crystals
\mathcal{B}
for which every connected component is isomorphic to
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
for some
\lambda\in P^{+}
.

3.
Demazure crystals

Let
W
denote the Weyl group of
\mathfrak{g}
which, as a Coxeter group, is equipped with a
length function

\ell:W\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
and
Bruhat
partial order given by transitive closure of relations
u\prec ut
for
t
a reflection with
\ell(u)\leq\ell(ut)
. Equivalently,
u\prec w
if and only if a(ny) reduced expression for
w
contains as a subword a reduced expression for
u
. We refer the reader to
[
3
]
for classical results on Coxeter groups.

Littelmann
[
18
]
conjectured, and proved for classical types, the existence of a subset
\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)\subseteq\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
for any
w\in W
whose character equals that of the Demazure module
V_{w}(\lambda)
computed by Demazure’s character formula
[
4
,
10
]
. Kashiwara
[
13
]
generalized Littelmann’s construction as follows.

For
w\in W
and
s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{\ell}}
a reduced expression for
w
, define

(3.1)

\mathcal{F}_{w}=\bigcup_{m_{i}\in\mathbb{N}}f_{i_{1}}^{m_{1}}f_{i_{2}}^{m_{2}}\cdots f_{i_{\ell}}^{m_{\ell}}\subset\mathcal{F},

and define
\mathcal{E}_{w}
similarly.

Given any
X\subseteq B(\lambda)
and
w\in W
, the set
\mathcal{F}_{w}(X)
is independent of the choice of reduced expression for
w

[
11
]
. Hence, the following construction is well-defined.

Definition 3.1

(
[
13
]
)
.

For
\lambda\in P^{+}
and
w\in W
, the
Demazure crystal

\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)
is

(3.2)

\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)=\mathcal{F}_{w}\{b_{\lambda}\}.

Notice that while
B_{w}(\lambda)
is closed under
\mathcal{E}
it is not closed under
\mathcal{F}
.

The following is a restatement of the fact that
\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)
is well-defined.

Proposition 3.2

(
[
13
]
)
.

For
\lambda\in P^{+}
and
w\in W
, if
s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{\ell}}
is any reduced expression for
w
, then for any
b\in\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)
, there exist
m_{j}\geq 0
such that

b=f_{i_{1}}^{m_{1}}f_{i_{2}}^{m_{2}}\cdots f_{i_{\ell}}^{m_{\ell}}(b_{\lambda}).

The following converse will be useful in identifying Demazure crystals.

Proposition 3.3
.

For
\lambda\in P^{+}
, if
b=f_{i_{1}}^{m_{1}}f_{i_{2}}^{m_{2}}\cdots f_{i_{k}}^{m_{k}}(b_{\lambda})
with
m_{j}\geq 0
and
k
minimal, then
s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{k}}
is a reduced expression for some
w\in W
.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
k
, noting the base case
k=1
is trivial since
s_{i}
is always reduced. Now assume the result for
k
, and let
b\in\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
be written

b=f_{i}^{m}f_{i_{1}}^{m_{1}}\cdots f_{i_{k}}^{m_{k}}(b_{\lambda})

with
k+1
minimal. Set
b_{0}=f_{i_{1}}^{m_{1}}\cdots f_{i_{k}}^{m_{k}}(b_{\lambda})
. Note
k
is minimal among all such expansions, since otherwise the expansion for
b
could be shortened as well. Therefore, by induction,
s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{k}}
is a reduced expression for some
u\in W
. In particular,
b_{0}\in\mathcal{F}_{u}\{b_{\lambda}\}
and
\ell(u)=k
.

We claim
u\prec s_{i}u
. If not, then
s_{i}u\prec u
. Thus there exists a reduced expression for
u
, say
s_{i}s_{j_{2}}\cdots s_{j_{k}}
, and, by Proposition
3.2
,
b_{0}=f_{i}^{n}f_{j_{2}}^{n_{2}}\cdots f_{j_{k}}^{n_{k}}(b_{\lambda})
. However, applying
f_{i}^{m}
then gives a strictly shorter expansion

b=f_{i}^{m}(b_{0})=f_{i}^{m+n}f_{j_{2}}^{n_{2}}\cdots f_{j_{k}}^{n_{k}}(b_{\lambda}),

contradicting the minimality of
k+1
. Thus
u\prec s_{i}u
as claimed. In particular,
s_{i}s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{k}}
is a reduced expression for
s_{i}u
.
∎

For
\lambda\in P^{+}
, let
W_{\lambda}
be the stabilizer subgroup of
\lambda
in
W
, and denote by
\lfloor w\rfloor^{\lambda}
and
\lceil w\rceil^{\lambda}
the minimal and maximal length coset representatives of
wW_{\lambda}
, respectively.
Note
\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)=\mathcal{B}_{\lfloor w\rfloor^{\lambda}}(\lambda)=\mathcal{B}_{\lceil w\rceil^{\lambda}}(\lambda)
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
v,\,w\in W
and
\lambda\in P^{+}
. Then
\mathcal{B}_{v}(\lambda)\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)
if and only if
\lfloor v\rfloor^{\lambda}\preceq w
if and only if
v\preceq\lceil w\rceil^{\lambda}
. In particular,
\mathcal{B}_{v}(\lambda)=\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)
only when
v\in wW_{\lambda}
.

Proof.

If
\lfloor v\rfloor^{\lambda}\preceq w
, then by
[
13
, Prop. 3.2.4]
, we have
\mathcal{B}_{v}(\lambda)=\mathcal{B}_{\lfloor v\rfloor^{\lambda}}(\lambda)\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)
. Similarly, if
v\preceq\lceil w\rceil^{\lambda}
, the same result implies
\mathcal{B}_{v}(\lambda)\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)=\mathcal{B}_{\lceil w\rceil^{\lambda}}(\lambda)
.

Suppose
\mathcal{B}_{v}(\lambda)\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)
. Then
\lfloor v\rfloor^{\lambda}(\lambda)
is a weight that occurs in
\mathcal{B}_{\lfloor v\rfloor^{\lambda}}(\lambda)=\mathcal{B}_{v}(\lambda)\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)=\mathcal{B}_{\lceil w\rceil^{\lambda}}(\lambda)
, and so
\lfloor v\rfloor^{\lambda}\preceq\lfloor w\rfloor^{\lambda}\preceq w
and
v\preceq\lceil v\rceil^{\lambda}\preceq\lceil w\rceil^{\lambda}
.
∎

4.
Extremal crystals

Kashiwara
[
13
]
showed
\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)
satisfies the following properties.

(D1)

\mathcal{E}\left(\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)\right)\subset\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)\sqcup\{0\}
;

(D2)

if
s_{i}w\prec w
, then
\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)=\{f_{i}^{m}(b)\mid m\geq 0,b\in\mathcal{B}_{s_{i}w}(\lambda),e_{i}(b)=0\}\setminus\{0\}
;

(D3)

for any
i
-string
S
,
S\cap\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)
is either
\varnothing
or
S
or
\{b\}
, where
e_{i}(b)=0
.

Here an
i
-string
is a connected subset of a crystal closed under both
\mathcal{E}_{i}
and
\mathcal{F}_{i}
, where these denote the monoids generated by
e_{i}
and
f_{i}
, respectively.

Joseph
[
11
]
considered subsets of
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
satisfying (D1) and (D3). Following recent work of the extremal authors
[
1
]
, we refer to such subsets as extremal.

Definition 4.1
.

A subset
X\subseteq\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
is
extremal
if
X
is nonempty and for any
i
-string
S
of
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
,
S\cap X
is either
\varnothing
or
S
or
\{b\}
, where
e_{i}(b)=0
.

Notice for
X
extremal, we have
\mathcal{E}X\subset X\sqcup\{0\}
. In particular, since
X
is nonempty, it must contain a highest weight element of
\mathcal{B}
. However, not all extremal subsets are Demazure crystals. For example, take
\mathfrak{g}=A_{2}
and
\lambda=\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}
. Then
X=\{b_{\lambda},f_{1}(b_{\lambda}),f_{2}(b_{\lambda})\}
is extremal, but not Demazure.

5.
Tensor products of normal crystals

The
direct sum

\mathcal{B}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{B}_{2}
of two
\mathfrak{g}
-crystals is their disjoint union with the obvious maps. Thus every crystal decomposes as a direct sum of highest weight crystals.

The
tensor product

\mathcal{B}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{B}_{2}
is the set
\{b_{1}\otimes b_{2}\mid b_{1}\in\mathcal{B}_{1}\mbox{ and }b_{2}\in\mathcal{B}_{2}\}
with maps

\displaystyle\mathrm{wt}(b_{1}\otimes b_{2})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{wt}(b_{1})+\mathrm{wt}(b_{2}),

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{i}(b_{1}\otimes b_{2})

\displaystyle=\max(\varepsilon_{i}(b_{1}),\varepsilon_{i}(b_{2})-\mathrm{wt}_{i}(b_{1})),

\displaystyle\varphi_{i}(b_{1}\otimes b_{2})

\displaystyle=\max(\varphi_{i}(b_{2}),\varphi_{i}(b_{1})+\mathrm{wt}_{i}(b_{2})),

where
\mathrm{wt}_{i}(b)=\langle\alpha^{\vee}_{i},\mathrm{wt}(b)\rangle
. The crystal operators
e_{i},f_{i}
are defined by

\displaystyle e_{i}(b_{1}\otimes b_{2})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}e_{i}(b_{1})\otimes b_{2}&\mbox{if }\varepsilon_{i}(b_{2})\leq\varphi_{i}(b_{1}),\\
b_{1}\otimes e_{i}(b_{2})&\mbox{if }\varepsilon_{i}(b_{2})>\varphi_{i}(b_{1});\end{cases}

\displaystyle f_{i}(b_{1}\otimes b_{2})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}f_{i}(b_{1})\otimes b_{2}&\mbox{if }\varepsilon_{i}(b_{2})<\varphi_{i}(b_{1}),\\
b_{1}\otimes f_{i}(b_{2})&\mbox{if }\varepsilon_{i}(b_{2})\geq\varphi_{i}(b_{1}).\end{cases}

The tensor product is associative but not commutative, though it is functorial.

Theorem 5.1

(
[
12
]
)
.

For
\lambda,\mu\in P^{+}
,
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mu)
is a crystal for
V(\lambda)\otimes V(\mu)
.

6.
Tensor products of Demazure crystals

Given Demazure crystals
\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda),\mathcal{B}_{u}(\mu)
, it is not the case that
\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal{B}_{u}(\mu)
is always a direct sum of Demazure crystals. For example, when
\mathfrak{g}=A_{2}
, the product
\mathcal{B}_{s_{2}}(\omega_{2})\otimes\mathcal{B}_{s_{1}}(\omega_{1})
is not direct sum of Demazure crystals, which is expected since the module
V_{s_{2}}(\omega_{2})\otimes V_{s_{1}}(\omega_{1})
does not admit an excellent filtration.

The issue here is more fundamental, however, as there is no analog of Theorem
5.1
for Demazure crystals. For example, take
\mathfrak{g}=A_{2}
with
\lambda=\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}
and
w=s_{1}s_{2}
. Then
V_{w}(\lambda)\otimes V_{w}(\lambda)
admits an excellent filtration, but
\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)
is not a direct sum of Demazure crystals and so is not a crystal for
V_{w}(\lambda)\otimes V_{w}(\lambda)
.

Kouno
[
15
]
characterized when
\mathcal{B}_{u}(\mu)\otimes\mathcal{B}_{v}(\nu)
is a direct sum of Demazure crystals. For any
\sigma\in W
let
W_{\sigma}\subseteq W
denote the parabolic subgroup. Note, despite similar notation,
W_{\sigma}
is not related to the stabilizer subgroup
W_{\lambda}
for
\lambda\in P^{+}
.

W_{\sigma}=\langle\;s_{i}\in W\;|\;s_{i}\sigma\prec\sigma\;\rangle.

Theorem 6.1

(
[
15
]
)
.

Let
\lambda,\mu\in P^{+}
and
u,w\in W
. Then
B_{w}(\lambda)\otimes B_{u}(\mu)
is a direct sum of Demazure crystals if and only if
\lfloor w\rfloor^{\lambda}\in W_{\lceil u\rceil^{\mu}}
.

Kouno’s proof is quite technical, making detailed use Lakshmibai-Seshadri paths for crystals
[
16
,
19
]
. One of Kouno’s main applications is to the key positivity problem, where he notes in
[
15
, Thm 8.2]
the following special case of Theorem
6.1
for which we give a direct proof.

Proposition 6.2
.

For
\lambda,\mu\in P^{+}
and
w\in W
, we have
\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mu)
is a direct sum of Demazure crystals.

Proof.

By definition,
\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)
admits a filtration by Demazure crystals,

\{b_{\lambda}\}\subset X_{i_{1}}\subset\dots\subset X_{i_{\ell-1}}\subset X_{i_{\ell}}=\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)

where
s_{i_{\ell}}\dots s_{i_{1}}
is a reduced expression for
w
and
\mathcal{F}_{i_{r}}(X_{i_{r-1}})=X_{i_{r}}
. Consequently, we have a filtration on
X\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mu)
with
k
minimal such that

\{b_{\lambda}\}\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mu)\subset X_{i_{1}}\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mu)\subset\dots\subset X_{i_{k-1}}\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mu)\subset X_{i_{k}}\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mu)=X\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mu).

By
[
11
, Thm. 2.11]
,
\{b_{\lambda}\}\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mu)
is a direct sum of Demazure crystals. So let
X_{i_{k-1}}=\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)
and
i=i_{k}
so that
X=\mathcal{B}_{s_{i}w}(\lambda)
. For induction, assume
\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mu)
is a direct sum of Demazure crystals, and let
x\otimes b\in X\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mu)
such that
x\not\in\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)
but
e_{i}^{m}(x)\in\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)
for some
m>0
. If
e_{i}(x\otimes b)=0
then necessarily
\varepsilon_{i}(x)=0
, a contradiction. Thus we have two possibilities. If
e_{i}(x\otimes b)=e_{i}(x)\otimes b
then
\varepsilon_{i}(b)\leq\varphi_{i}(x)
. However
\varphi_{i}(x)<\varphi_{i}(e_{i}^{m}(x))
and thus
e_{i}^{m}(x\otimes b)=e_{i}^{m}(x)\otimes b\in\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mu)
. If instead
e_{i}(x\otimes b)=x\otimes e_{i}(b)
then
\varepsilon_{i}(b)>\varphi_{i}(x)
. So let
t=\varepsilon_{i}(b)-\varphi_{i}(x)
so that
\varepsilon_{i}(e_{i}^{t}(b))=\varphi_{i}(x)
and
e_{i}^{t}(x\otimes b)=x\otimes e_{i}^{t}(b)
. From this and the fact that
\varepsilon_{i}(x)>0
we see that
e_{i}(x\otimes e_{i}^{t}(b))=e_{i}(x)\otimes e_{i}^{t}(b)
. Iterating as in the previous case we obtain
e_{i}^{m+t}(x\otimes b)=e_{i}^{m}(x\otimes e_{i}^{t}(b))=e_{i}^{m}(x)\otimes e_{i}^{t}(b)
and thus
X\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mu)\subset\mathcal{F}_{i}(\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mu))
. The reverse inclusion follows from the fact that
f_{i}^{k}(x\otimes b)
will always be contained in
X\otimes\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
for any
x\otimes b\in\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mu)
and any
k\geq 0
.
∎

In order to apply Kouno’s theorem, it is necessary to have the exact values of
\lambda,\mu\in P^{+}
and
w,u\in W
that index the Demazure crystals. Sometimes, this information is not readily available. It is possible to know the crystals in question are Demazure without knowing exactly which Demazure crystals they are. In the following section we remove this constraint by using extremal crystals to give an alternative local characterization for when a product of Demazure crystals is a direct sum of Demazure crystals.

7.
Tensor products of extremal crystals

As with Demazure crystals, tensor products of extremal crystals are not always extremal. For example, take
\mathfrak{g}=A_{1}
, then
\mathcal{B}_{s_{1}}(\omega_{1})\otimes\{b_{\omega_{1}}\}
is
2
-dimensional with unique highest weight
2\omega_{1}
, and so is neither extremal nor Demazure. Below, we characterize when the tensor product of extremal crystals remains extremal.

Definition 7.1
.

An element
x\otimes y\in\mathcal{B}(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mu)
is called an
i
-hinge
if

(1)

\varepsilon_{i}(x)>0
,
\varphi_{i}(x)=0
,

(2)

\varepsilon_{i}(y)=0
,
\varphi_{i}(y)>0
.

In terms of the crystal structure, an
i
-hinge is an element where
e_{i}
acts on the left factor and
f_{i}
acts on that right factor.

Proposition 7.2
.

For
x\otimes y\in\mathcal{B}(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mu)
an
i
-hinge, we have

•

e_{i}(x\otimes y)=e_{i}(x)\otimes y\in\mathcal{B}(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mu)
, and

•

f_{i}(x\otimes y)=x\otimes f_{i}(y)\in\mathcal{B}(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mu)
.

Proof.

Since
\varphi_{i}(x)=0=\varepsilon_{i}(y)
, the tensor product rules give
e_{i}(x\otimes y)=e_{i}(x)\otimes y
and
f_{i}(x\otimes y)=x\otimes f_{i}(y)
. Since
\varepsilon_{i}(x)>0
, we have
e_{i}(x)\in\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
, and since
\varphi_{i}(y)>0
, we have
f_{i}(y)\in\mathcal{B}(\mu)
.
∎

Thus, we say an
i
-hinge
x\otimes y\in X\otimes Y
is
broken
if
f_{i}(y)\not\in Y
. Determining whether
X\otimes Y
contains a broken
i
-hinge is a local, easily checked property which precisely characterizes when tensor products of extremal crystals are extremal.

Theorem 7.3
.

Let
X\subset\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
and
Y\subset\mathcal{B}(\mu)
be extremal subsets. Then
X\otimes Y
is an extremal subset of
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mu)
if and only if
X\otimes Y
does not contain a broken
i
-hinge for any
i
.

Proof.

Suppose
x\otimes y\in X\otimes Y
is an
i
-hinge and
f_{i}(y)\not\in Y
. By Proposition
7.2
, so
e_{i}(x\otimes y)=e_{i}(x)\otimes y\in X\otimes Y
, since
X
is extremal. Thus
x\otimes y
is not the top of its
i
-string. Similarly,
f_{i}(x\otimes y)=x\otimes f_{i}(y)\in X\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mu)
. However, since
f_{i}(y)\not\in Y
, we have
x\otimes f_{i}(y)\not\in X\otimes Y
. Thus the
i
-string through
x\otimes y
violates the extremal criteria for
X\otimes Y
.

Now suppose
X\otimes Y
is not extremal. Since both
X
and
Y
are extremal, we must have
\mathcal{E}(X\otimes Y)\subset X\otimes Y\sqcup\{0\}
since
\mathcal{E}X\subset X\sqcup\{0\}
and
\mathcal{E}Y\subset Y\sqcup\{0\}
. Thus there exists
x_{0}\otimes y_{0}\in X\otimes Y
and
i\in I
such that

•

\varepsilon_{i}(x_{0}\otimes y_{0})=0
,

•

f_{i}(x_{0}\otimes y_{0})\in X\otimes Y
, and

•

for some
m>1
, we have
f_{i}^{m}(x_{0}\otimes y_{0})\not\in X\otimes Y\sqcup\{0\}
.

Taking
m>1
above to be minimal, define
x\otimes y=f_{i}^{m-1}(x_{0}\otimes y_{0})\in X\otimes Y
. We will show
x\otimes y
is an
i
-hinge with
f_{i}(y)\not\in Y
.

We claim
e_{i}(x\otimes y)=e_{i}(x)\otimes y
. Indeed, if
e_{i}(x\otimes y)=x\otimes e_{i}(y)
, then
\varepsilon_{i}(y)>\varphi_{i}(x)
, and so
f_{i}(x\otimes y)=x\otimes f_{i}(y)
as well. But since
\varepsilon_{i}(y)>\varphi_{i}(x)\geq 0
and
Y
is extremal,
f_{i}(y)\in Y\sqcup\{0\}
, contradicting that
f_{i}(x\otimes y)\not\in X\otimes Y\sqcup\{0\}
. Thus
e_{i}(x\otimes y)=e_{i}(x)\otimes y
and, in particular,
\varepsilon_{i}(y)\leq\varphi_{i}(x)
. Moreover, since
m>1
, we have
e_{i}(x)\otimes y=e_{i}(x\otimes y)\neq 0
, and so
\varepsilon_{i}(x)>0
.

By the crystal axioms, it follows as well that
e_{i}^{k}(x\otimes y)=e_{i}^{k}(x)\otimes y
for
k<m
. In particular,
y=y_{0}
, and so
\varepsilon_{i}(y)=0
.

Furthermore, since
X
is extremal and
e_{i}(x),x\in X
, we must have
f_{i}(x)\in X\sqcup\{0\}
and so
f_{i}(x)\otimes y\in X\otimes Y\sqcup\{0\}
. Thus we must have
f_{i}(x\otimes y)=x\otimes f_{i}(y)
. In particular,
\varepsilon_{i}(y)\geq\varphi_{i}(x)
. By the previous inequality, we have
\varphi_{i}(x)=\varepsilon_{i}(y)=0
.

Finally, since
x\otimes f_{i}(y)=f_{i}(x\otimes y)\not\in X\otimes Y\sqcup\{0\}
, we have
f_{i}(y)\not\in Y\sqcup\{0\}
and so
\varphi_{i}(y)>0
. Therefore
x\otimes y
is an
i
-hinge with
f_{i}(y)\not\in Y
as claimed.
∎

Thus Theorem
1.1
follows from Theorem
7.3
and Proposition
7.2
.
Notice, if
X\subset\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
has only the highest weight element or if
Y\subset\mathcal{B}(\mu)
contains all possible elements, then
X\otimes Y
contains no hinges. That is, both
\{b_{\lambda}\}\otimes\mathcal{B}_{u}(\mu)
and
\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mu)
are extremal subsets of
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mu)
. Of course, this follows by Theorem
6.1
since both are direct sums of Demazure crystals.

8.
Extremal tensor products

Our interest in extremal crystals lies not exclusively in their implicit structure, but rather in the structure imposed on the factors when a tensor product of two subsets of crystals is extremal. We begin with the following.

Proposition 8.1
.

If
X\otimes Y\subset\mathcal{B}(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mu)
is an extremal subset, then
\mathcal{E}(X)\subset X\sqcup\{0\}
. Furthermore, if
\mathcal{E}(Y)\subset Y\sqcup\{0\}
, then
X\subset\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
is an extremal subset.

Proof.

Suppose
X\otimes Y\subset\mathcal{B}(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mu)
is an extremal subset. Take
y\in Y
such that
e_{i}(y)\not\in Y
. We claim
e_{i}(x\otimes y)=e_{i}(x)\otimes y
for any
x\in X
. In particular, when
X\otimes Y
is extremal, this implies
e_{i}(x)\in X
. To see the claim, notice if
e_{i}(y)=0
, then
\varepsilon_{i}(y)=0
and so
\varphi_{i}(x)\geq\varepsilon_{i}(y)
showing
e_{i}(x\otimes y)=e_{i}(x)\otimes y
. Alternatively, if
e_{i}(y)\neq 0
and
e_{i}(x\otimes y)=x\otimes e_{i}(y)
, then since
X\otimes Y
is extremal, this implies
e_{i}(y)\in Y
contradicting the choice of
y
. Thus
\mathcal{E}(X)\subset X\sqcup\{0\}
.

Now suppose, in addition, that
\mathcal{E}(Y)\subset Y\sqcup\{0\}
. Then we may take
y\in Y
such that
e_{i}(y)=0
. Consider
x\in X
for which
e_{i}(x),f_{i}(x)\neq 0
. By the prior argument,
e_{i}(x)\in X
, and so
e_{i}(x\otimes y)=e_{i}(x)\otimes y\neq 0
since
\varphi_{i}(y)=0
. We must show
f_{i}(x)\in X
. Since
X\otimes Y
is extremal and
e_{i}(x\otimes y)\neq 0
, we have
f_{i}(x\otimes y)\in X\otimes Y\sqcup\{0\}
. Since
f_{i}(x)\neq 0
, we have
\varphi_{i}(x)>0=\varepsilon_{i}(y)
, and so
f_{i}(x\otimes y)=f_{i}(x)\otimes y
, which ensure
f_{i}(x)\in X
. Thus
X
is extremal.
∎

Notice that in Proposition
8.1
,
Y
need not be extremal, even when
\mathcal{E}Y\subset Y\sqcup\{0\}
. For example, for
\mathfrak{g}=A_{2}
with
\lambda=2\omega_{1}+2\omega_{2}
and
\mu=2\omega_{1}
, take
X=\{b_{\lambda}\}
and
Y=\{b_{\mu},f_{1}(b_{\mu})\}
. Then
X\otimes Y\cong\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{id}}(\lambda+\mu)\oplus\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{id}}(\lambda+\omega_{2})
is a direct sum of Demazure crystals, and hence extremal, but
Y
is not even extremal.

Any subset
\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal{B}_{u}(\mu)\subset\mathcal{B}(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mu)
which is a direct sum of Demazure crystals is also an extremal subset, since Demazure crystals are extremal. Amazingly, the converse is also true.

Theorem 8.2
.

For
\lambda,\mu\in P^{+}
and
w,u\in W
, we have
\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal{B}_{u}(\mu)
is an extremal subset of
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mu)
if and only if
\lfloor w\rfloor^{\lambda}\in W_{\lceil u\rceil^{\mu}}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\lfloor w\rfloor^{\lambda}\not\in W_{\lceil u\rceil^{\mu}}
. Then there exists
s_{i}\prec\lfloor w\rfloor^{\lambda}
such that
s_{i}\lceil u\rceil^{\mu}\succ\lceil u\rceil^{\mu}
. Since
\lceil u\rceil^{\mu}
is maximal length in
uW_{\mu}
then
s_{i}\lceil u\rceil^{\mu}(\mu)\neq\lceil u\rceil^{\mu}(\mu)
. Thus
B_{u}(\mu)=B_{\lceil u\rceil^{\mu}}(\mu)\subsetneqq B_{s_{i}\lceil u\rceil^{\mu}}(\mu)=B_{s_{i}u}(\mu)
and we can find
y\in B_{u}(\mu)
for which
\varphi_{i}(y)>0
but
f_{i}(y)\not\in B_{u}(\mu)
. On the other hand, since
s_{i}\prec\lfloor w\rfloor^{\lambda}
and
\lfloor w\rfloor^{\lambda}
is the shortest coset representative, by Proposition
3.2
, there exists an element
x\in B_{w}(\lambda)
given by
x=f_{i}^{m}f_{j_{k}}^{m_{k}}\dots f_{j_{1}}^{m_{1}}(b_{\lambda})
with all
m^{\prime}s
maximal for which
\varepsilon_{i}(x)>0
and
\varphi_{i}(x)=0
. So if we consider
x\otimes y\in B(\lambda)\otimes B(\mu)
we can see that
e_{i}^{m}(x\otimes y)=e_{i}^{m}(x)\otimes y\in B_{w}(\lambda)\otimes B_{u}(\mu)
but
f_{i}(x\otimes y)=x\otimes f_{i}(y)\not\in B_{w}(\lambda)\otimes B_{u}(\mu)
. Thus
B_{w}(\lambda)\otimes B_{u}(\mu)
contains a broken
i
-hinge and by Theorem
7.3
cannot be extremal.

Now suppose
B_{w}(\lambda)\otimes B_{u}(\mu)
is not an extremal subset. Then by Theorem
7.3
there exists a broken
i
-hinge
x\otimes y\in B_{w}(\lambda)\otimes B_{u}(\mu)
. In particular,
0\neq f_{i}(y)\not\in B_{u}(\mu)
so
B_{\lceil u\rceil^{\mu}}(\mu)=B_{u}(\mu)\subsetneqq B_{s_{i}u}(\mu)=B_{s_{i}\lceil u\rceil^{\mu}}(\mu)
and hence
s_{i}\lceil u\rceil^{\mu}\not\in uW_{\mu}
. Since
\lceil u\rceil^{\mu}
has maximal length then
s_{i}\lceil u\rceil^{\mu}\succ\lceil u\rceil^{\mu}
, thus
s_{i}\not\in W_{\lceil u\rceil^{\mu}}
. Moreover, since
x\in B_{w}(\lambda)
with
\varepsilon_{i}(x)>0
then
s_{i}\prec\lfloor w\rfloor^{\lambda}
which implies
\lfloor w\rfloor^{\lambda}\not\in W_{\lceil u\rceil^{\mu}}
.
∎

Combining this with Theorem
6.1
yields Theorem
1.2
, giving a local characterization for which tensor products of Demazure crystals remain Demazure.

Notice Theorem
1.2
is false for tensor products of extremal subsets. For example, take
\mathfrak{g}=A_{2}
with
\lambda=\omega_{3}
and
\mu=\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}
. Then both
X=\{b_{\lambda}\}
and
Y=\{b_{\mu},f_{1}(b_{\mu}),f_{2}(b_{\mu})\}
are extremal, but
X\otimes Y\cong Y
is extremal but not Demazure.

9.
Application to tensor squares

Even when
\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal{B}_{u}(\mu)
is not a direct sum of Demazure crystals, some connected components of it may be. For example, take
\mathfrak{g}=A_{2}
with
\lambda=\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}
and
w=s_{1}s_{2}
. Then
\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)
has four connected components, two of which are Demazure crystals and two of which are not even extremal.

Generalizing this example, we show the connected component of the tensor square
\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)
containing
b_{\lambda}\otimes b_{\lambda}
is always a Demazure crystal, even when
\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)
is not a direct sum of Demazure crystals.

Lemma 9.1
.

For
\lambda,\mu\in P^{+}
such that
\langle\alpha_{i}^{\vee},\mu\rangle=0
whenever
\langle\alpha_{i}^{\vee},\lambda\rangle=0
, if
x\otimes y\in\mathcal{F}(\{b_{\lambda}\otimes b_{\mu}\})\subseteq\mathcal{B}(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mu)
and
x\in\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)
for some
w\in W
, then
y\in\mathcal{B}_{w}(\mu)
.

Proof.

We claim if
b_{\lambda}\otimes y\in\mathcal{F}(\{b_{\lambda}\otimes b_{\mu}\})
, then
y=b_{\mu}
. Since
b_{\mu}
is highest weight,
\varepsilon_{i}(b_{\mu})=0
. Therefore, if
\varphi_{i}(b_{\lambda})>0
, then
f_{i}(b_{\lambda}\otimes b_{\mu})=f_{i}(b_{\lambda})\otimes b_{\mu}
; otherwise
\varphi_{i}(b_{\lambda})=0=\varphi_{i}(b_{\mu})
, and so
f_{i}(b_{\lambda}\otimes b_{\lambda})=b_{\lambda}\otimes f_{i}(b_{\mu})=0
. The claim follows.

Now consider
x\otimes y\in\mathcal{F}(\{b_{\lambda}\otimes b_{\mu}\})
with
x\in\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)
. By Proposition
3.2
, there exist
i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}
and
m_{1},\ldots,m_{k}\geq 0
such that
x\otimes y=f_{i_{1}}^{m_{1}}\cdots f_{i_{k}}^{m_{k}}(b_{\lambda})\otimes y
, where
s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{k}}
is a reduced expression for
w
. If
\varepsilon_{i}(b_{2})=0
, then
e_{i}(b_{1}\otimes b_{2})=e_{i}(b_{1})\otimes b_{2}
. Therefore there exist integers
n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}
with
n_{i}\geq m_{i}\geq 0
such that

e_{i_{k}}^{n_{k}}\cdots e_{i_{1}}^{n_{1}}(x\otimes y)=b_{\lambda}\otimes y_{0}

for some
y_{0}\in\mathcal{E}_{w}\{y\}
. By the opening claim, this means
y_{0}=b_{\mu}
. Reversing the relationship
y_{0}\in\mathcal{E}_{w}\{y\}
gives
y\in\mathcal{F}_{w}\{y_{0}\}=\mathcal{F}_{w}\{b_{\mu}\}=\mathcal{B}_{w}(\mu)
.
∎

Using this and Theorem
1.2
, we have the following application.

Theorem 9.2
.

For
\lambda,\mu\in P^{+}
such that
\langle\alpha_{i}^{\vee},\mu\rangle=0
whenever
\langle\alpha_{i}^{\vee},\lambda\rangle=0
, and for
u,v\in W
such that
u\preceq v
, we have

\mathcal{F}\left(\{b_{\lambda}\otimes b_{\mu}\right\})\cap\mathcal{B}_{u}(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal{B}_{v}(\mu)\cong\mathcal{B}_{u}(\lambda+\mu).

Proof.

Suppose
x\otimes y\in\mathcal{F}(\{b_{\lambda}\otimes b_{\mu}\})
is an
i
-hinge. Let
w
be minimal such that
x\in\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)
. Then
w\leq u
by Proposition
3.4
. Furthermore, since
\varepsilon_{i}(x)>0
, by Proposition
3.3
, we have
\mathcal{F}_{i}(\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda))=\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)
. By Lemma
9.1
,
y\in\mathcal{B}_{w}(\mu)
. Therefore
f_{i}(y)\in\mathcal{F}_{i}(\mathcal{B}_{w}(\mu))=\mathcal{B}_{w}(\mu)\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{v}(\mu)
by Proposition
3.4
and transitivity of
w\preceq u\preceq v
. In particular
x\otimes y
cannot be a broken
i
-hinge in
B_{u}(\lambda)\otimes B_{v}(\mu)
. Thus it is extremal, and so by Theorem
1.2
, it is Demazure. The result follows from that fact that
\mathcal{F}_{u}\left(\{b_{\lambda}\otimes b_{\mu}\}\right)
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{B}_{u}(\lambda+\mu)

[
11
]
.
∎

The associativity of the tensor product gives the following consequence. This is also studied in
[
6
]
in the special case where
w
is miniscule.

Corollary 9.3
.

For
\lambda\in P^{+}
and
w\in W
, for any
m\geq 1
we have

\mathcal{F}(\{b_{\lambda}\otimes\cdots\otimes b_{\lambda}\})\cap\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)^{\otimes m}\cong\mathcal{B}_{w}(m\lambda).

10.
Concluding remarks

Interestingly, in the example above for
\mathfrak{g}=A_{2}
with
\lambda=\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}
and
w=s_{1}s_{2}
, even though the tensor product of Demazure crystals
\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)
is not a direct sum of Demazure crystals, the corresponding tensor product of Demazure modules
V_{w}(\lambda)\otimes V_{w}(\lambda)
does admit an excellent filtration. Indeed, if one removes from
\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal{B}_{w}(\lambda)
the two edges ending in broken
i
-hinges, namely the
f_{2}
edges from
b_{\lambda}\otimes f_{1}(b_{\lambda})
and
b_{\lambda}\otimes f_{1}^{2}f_{2}(b_{\lambda})
, then the resulting structure becomes extremal and, moreover, is isomorphic to a direct sum of Demazure crystals.

As Kouno notes, Proposition
6.2
implies the positivity for type A Demazure characters proven combinatorially by Haglund, Luoto, Mason and van Willigenberg
[
8
]
. Recently, Assaf
[
2
, Thm 5.2.2]
gave a larger class of type A Demazure modules for which the product of the characters expands nonnegatively into Demazure characters. Yet this positivity does not follow from Theorem
6.1
.

In fact, in many instances, though the characters exhibit the necessary positivity and the tensor product of the modules admits an excellent filtration, the tensor product of Demazure crystals does not decompose into a direct sum of Demazure crystals. This suggests Kashiwara’s tensor product for crystals is not the correct tensor rule for Demazure crystals. This leads naturally to question the existence of a new and different tensor product for crystals that preserves the extremal property of subsets. Such a rule might lead to a characterization of when tensor products of Demazure modules admit excellent filtrations, fully generalizing the conjecture of Polo proved by Mathieu and solving the key positivity problem.

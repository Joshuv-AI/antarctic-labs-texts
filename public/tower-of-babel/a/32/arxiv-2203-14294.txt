Stability of a cascade system with two stations and its extension for multiple stations
Masakiyo Miyazawa, Evsey Morozov

Abstract
We consider a two-station cascade system in which waiting or externally arriving customers at station 1 1 move to the station 2 2 if the queue size of station 1 1 including an arriving customer itself and a customer being served is greater than a given threshold level c 1 ≥ 1 c_{1}\geq 1 and if station 2 2 is empty. Assuming that external arrivals are subject to independent renewal processes satisfying certain regularity conditions and service times are i . i . d . i.i.d. at each station, we derive necessary and sufficient conditions for a Markov process describing this system to be positive recurrent in the sense of Harris. This result is extended to the cascade system with a general number k k of stations in series. This extension requires certain traffic intensities of stations 2 , 3 , … , k − 1 2,3,\ldots,k-1 for k ≥ 3 k\geq 3 to be defined. We finally note that the modeling assumptions on the renewal arrivals and i . i . d . i.i.d. service times are not essential if the notion of the stability is replaced by a certain sample path condition. This stability notion is identical with the standard stability if the whole system is described by the Markov process which is a Harris irreducible T T -process.

1
Introduction

We are interested in the stability of a service system with two single server stations, numbered as
1,2
, where the stability means that a Markov process describing this system is positive recurrent in the sense of Harris (see Definition
2.1
). We assume the following system dynamics for this queueing model. Each station has renewal arrivals and
i.i.d.
service times. Both stations have single servers, which are independently working in parallel, but waiting or exogenously just arriving customers at station
1
moves to the station
2
if the queue size of station
1
including a customer being served is greater than a given threshold level
c_{1}\geq 1
and if station
2
is empty, where an exogenously arriving customer is counted in the queue. The customer arriving from station 1 immediately starts its service at station 2, and gets service only when no other customer is in station 2.
We refer to this queuing system as a 2-station cascade system. Since this system naturally arises in practice but its analysis is not easy, its stability is attracting researchers of queueing systems

For station
i=1,2
, let
\lambda_{i}
be the arrival rate of exogenously arriving customers, and let
\mu_{i}
is the service rate of those customers. Let
\rho_{i}=\lambda_{i}/\mu_{i}
. Since some of arriving customers at station 1 may move to station 2,
\rho_{1}
is not an actual traffic intensity (the mean amount of service processed per unit time), so it is nominal. We define
\rho^{*}_{1}(\xi)
as the time average of the probability that the station
1
is not empty for an arbitrarily given initial distribution
\xi
of the system at time
0
, which is formally defined as (
3.11
) in Section
3
. We refer to
\rho^{*}_{1}(\xi)
as an effective traffic intensity at station
1
given
\xi
.

In this setting, it is expected that the system is stable if and only if
\rho^{*}_{1}(\xi)<1
for some
\xi
and
\rho_{2}<1
. We prove this characterization of the stability in our framework (see Lemma
3.3
), but it is less tractable because
\rho^{*}_{1}(\xi)
is hard to compute. In the literasture, a computable condition has been studied (e.g., see
[
13
]
), but the stability is not fully answered. For the cascade system with more than two stations, necessary and sufficient conditions are separately considered in
[
6
]
, but there are gaps between those conditions.

In this paper, we solve this stability problem for the 2-station cascade system, assuming certain regularity conditions on the inter-arrival time distributions of exogenous customers at each station. This extends the known results. For example,
Morozov and Steyaert 2013
obtain tractable sufficient conditions for the stability under the extra assumption that the 1st station has Poisson arrivals. We show that, not assuming Poisson arrivals, those conditions are necessary and sufficient. We further extend this result for a
k
-station cascade system for a general integer
k\geq 3
using the effective traffic intensities
\rho^{*}_{1}(\xi)
for
i=2,3,\ldots,k-1
. As those traffic intensities are hard to compute, it is preferable to have tractable stability conditions. Such conditions are conjectured in
[
11
]
, but they are disproved by
[
7
]
.

Both papers of those papers
[
7
,
11
]
are based on Theorem 3.1 of the preprint
[
12
]
. This theorem is correct, but some arguments in
[
11
]
are not accurate. These motivate us to write the present paper updating
[
11
]
taking
Kim and Kim 2023
into account.

Thus, main contributions of this paper are a proof technique for the stability problem and the full characterization of the stability for
k=2
. The proof technique is a combination of sample path analysis similar to fluid approximation and the characterization of the stability based on the so-called Harris irreducibility of a Markov
T
-process with a general state space (see Proposition
2.2
). This approach is different from the method of
Morozov and Steyaert 2013
, which is based on a regenerative process, so is restrictive in applications. Another typical approach to stability problems is to use fluid approximation and Lyapunov functions (see, e.g.
[
4
]
). The fluid approximation largely depends on strong law of large numbers, strong LLN for short. However, it may have certain limitations. For example,
Tezcan 2013
shows insufficiency for studying stability through fluid approximation (see also
Chernova et al. 2013
). We encounter a similar situation because the stability condition for
k\geq 3
requires
\rho^{*}_{j}(\xi)
for downward stations, which can not be obtained from the fluid approximation. The counterexamples of
[
7
]
supports this situation.

This paper is made up by six sections. In Section
2
, we introduce the well-known framework for a Markov process to be positive Harris recurrent, which provides a base for our analysis. In Section
3
, the 2-station cascade system is detailed, and its stability conditions are presented in Theorem
3.1
, which are proved in Section
4
. The general
k
-station cascade system is considered in Section
5
. Finally, Section
6
remarks the possibility to relax the renewal assumptions on the 2-station cascade system concerning its stability. The proofs for some auxiliary results are given in the appendix.

(Irreducibility and recurrence)

For the Markov process

\displaystyle\tau_{A}=\inf\{t\geq 0;X(t)\in A\},\qquad\eta_{A}=\int_{0}^{\infty}1(X(u)\in A)du,\qquad A\in{\mathcal{B}}(S),

3
Two-station cascade system

We focus on the two-station cascade system. This is a queueing system with single-server stations 1, 2 and infinite-buffers. The servers are working in parallel, and, for

Let

Q_{i}(\cdot)
. We note the following basic facts on
\rho^{*}_{i}(\xi)
. They may look intuitively obvious, but have the important message that the stability problem on
{\boldsymbol{X}}(\cdot)
can be answered by the effective traffic intensities.

Lemma 3.3
.

Under Assumption
3.1
, (i) if
{\boldsymbol{X}}(\cdot)
is positive recurrent, then, for any distribution
\xi
on
S
,

\displaystyle\rho_{i}^{*}(\xi)=1-\mathbb{P}_{\nu}(Q_{i}(0)=0),\qquad i=1,2,

(3.12)

where
\nu
is the stationary distribution of
{\boldsymbol{X}}(\cdot)
, and
\mathbb{P}_{\nu}(A)=\int_{S}\mathbb{P}_{{\boldsymbol{x}}}(A)\nu(dx)
for
A\in{\mathcal{F}}
. Hence,
\rho^{*}_{i}(\xi)
is independent of
\xi
when
{\boldsymbol{X}}(\cdot)
is positive recurrent, (ii)
{\boldsymbol{X}}(\cdot)
is positive recurrent if and only if
\rho^{*}_{i}(\xi)<1
for
i=1,2
and some distribution
\xi
on
S
.

This lemma is proved in Appendix
A.4
, independently of Theorem
3.1
, and will be used to prove the necessity of (
3.9
) in Theorem
3.1
. Thus,
\rho^{*}_{1}(\xi)
is hard to compute, but still useful.

v=1,2,{1|2}

1|2

D_{v}(\cdot)\equiv\{D_{v}(t);t\geq 0\}

\displaystyle D_{v}(t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}1(0<t_{v}(n)\leq t).

Note that
A_{v}(0)=D_{v}(0)=0
by those definitions.
Since there is at most one class-
{1|2}
customer at station
2
, we have

\displaystyle D_{{1|2}}(t)\leq A_{{1|2}}(t)\leq D_{{1|2}}(t)+1,\qquad t\geq 0.

(4.1)

We also have the following flow balance equations.

\displaystyle Q_{1}(t)=Q_{1}(0)+A_{1}(t)-(D_{1}(t)+A_{{1|2}}(t)),

(4.2)

\displaystyle Q_{1|2}(t)=Q_{1|2}(0)+A_{{1|2}}(t)-D_{{1|2}}(t),

(4.3)

\displaystyle Q_{2}(t)=Q_{2}(0)+A_{2}(t)-D_{2}(t).

(4.4)

To describe departures by service times, we introduce the following notations.

\displaystyle B_{i}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}1(Q_{i}(u)>0)du,\qquad t\geq 0,i=1,2,

\displaystyle B_{1|2}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}1(Q_{1|2}(u)>0,Q_{2}(u)=0)du

\displaystyle J_{{1|2}}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}1(Q_{1}(u)>c_{1},Q_{2}(u)=0)du,\qquad t\geq 0,

where

\displaystyle J_{{1|2}}(t)\leq B_{{1|2}}(t)\leq I_{2}(t),\qquad t\geq 0.

(4.5)

Denote by
N_{v}(t)
the counting processes for potential numbers of class-
v

, namely,

\displaystyle N_{v}(t)=\sup\left\{n\geq 0;\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\tau_{s,v}(\ell)\leq t\right\},\qquad t\geq 0,v=1,2,{1|2},

(4.6)

where
\tau_{s,1|2}(0)=0
and
\tau_{s,1|2}(n)=t_{s,1|2}(n)-t_{s,1|2}(n-1)
for
n\geq 1
. Since
D_{v}(t)=N_{v}(B_{v}(t))
and

\displaystyle\{Q_{1}(u)>c_{1},Q_{2}(u)=0\}=\{Q_{1}(u)>c_{1},Q_{2}(u)=0,Q_{1|2}(u)>0\},\qquad u\geq 0,

it follows from (

) that

\displaystyle N_{{1|2}}(J_{{1|2}}(t))\leq D_{{1|2}}(t)\leq N_{{1|2}}(I_{2}(t)),\qquad t\geq 0.

(4.7)

4.2
Auxiliary lemmas

Recall the definition (
3.7
) of
{\boldsymbol{X}}_{2}(\cdot)
. Under the Assumption
3.1
,
{\boldsymbol{X}}_{2}(\cdot)
is a Harris irreducible Markov process with state space
\mathbb{Z}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
, and describes the
GI/G/1
queue with traffic intensity
\rho_{2}=\lambda_{2}/\mu_{2}
. As is well known,
{\boldsymbol{X}}_{2}(\cdot)
is positive recurrent if and only if
\rho_{2}<1
. Assume that
\rho_{2}<1
, then, by Little’s law for a customer in service,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\nu_{2}}(Q_{2}(0)\geq 1)=\lambda_{2}/\mu_{2}=\rho_{2},

where
\mathbb{P}_{\nu_{2}}(Q_{2}(0)\geq 1)=\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}}\mathbb{P}(Q_{2}\geq 1|{\boldsymbol{X}}_{2}={\boldsymbol{y}})\nu_{2}(d{\boldsymbol{y}})
for the stationary distribution
\nu_{2}
of
{\boldsymbol{X}}_{2}(\cdot)
, and therefore (i) of Remark
2.1
yields

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}I_{2}(t)=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}1(Q_{2}(u)=0)du=1-\rho_{2},\qquad w.p.1.

(4.8)

Lemma 4.1
.

If
\rho_{2}=\lambda_{2}/\mu_{2}<1
, then

\displaystyle\overline{D}_{{1|2}}\equiv\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}D_{{1|2}}(t)\leq\mu_{{1|2}}(1-\rho_{2}),\qquad w.p.1,

(4.9)

and therefore

\displaystyle\lambda_{1|2}\equiv\mathbb{E}\left(\overline{D}_{{1|2}}\right)\leq\mu_{{1|2}}(1-\rho_{2}).

(4.10)

Proof.

Since

\sum_{\ell=1}^{N_{{1|2}}(t)}\tau_{s,1|2}(\ell)\leq t<\sum_{\ell=1}^{N_{{1|2}}(t)+1}\tau_{s,1|2}(\ell),

strong LLN yields

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}N_{{1|2}}(t)=\mu_{{1|2}},\qquad w.p.1.

(4.11)

Combing this with (
4.7
) and (
4.8
), we have

\displaystyle\overline{D}_{{1|2}}(t)\leq\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{I_{2}(t)}{t}\frac{1}{I_{2}(t)}N_{{1|2}}(I_{2}(t))=\mu_{{1|2}}(1-\rho_{2}),\qquad w.p.1,

which proves (
4.9
).
∎

We will prove Theorem
3.1
by applying Lemmas
3.2
and
3.3
. For the sufficiency of the conditions (
3.9
) and (
3.10
), we will use the following lemma.

Lemma 4.2
.

Assume that
\rho_{2}=\lambda_{2}/\mu_{2}<1
and

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{P}_{{\boldsymbol{x}}}(Q_{1}(u)\leq\ell)du=0,\qquad\forall{\boldsymbol{x}}\in S,\forall\ell\geq 0.

(4.12)

Then, there exists a nonnegative sequence
\{s_{n};n\geq 1\}
such that
s_{n}\uparrow\infty
and, under
\mathbb{P}_{x}
,

\displaystyle\liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{s_{n}}D_{{1|2}}(s_{n})\geq\mu_{{1|2}}(1-\rho_{2}),\qquad w.p.1.

(4.13)

Proof.

In what follow, we fix
{\boldsymbol{X}}(0)={\boldsymbol{x}}
. Define
K_{1}(t)
as

\displaystyle K_{1}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}1(Q_{1}(u)\leq c_{1})du,\qquad t\geq 0.

Then, from the Markov inequality, for each
\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(K_{1}(t)>t\varepsilon)\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\mathbb{E}\Big(\frac{1}{t}K_{1}(t)\Big),

so (
4.12
) implies that

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\Big(\frac{1}{t}K_{1}(t)>\varepsilon\Big)\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\limsup_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\Big(\frac{1}{t}K_{1}(t)\Big)=\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{\varepsilon t}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{P}(Q_{1}(u)\leq c_{1})du=0.

Hence,
\frac{1}{t}K(t)
converges to
0
in probability as
t\to\infty
. Then, as is well known (e.g., see Corollary 6.1.2 of
[
2
]
), we can choose a subsequence
\{s_{n};n\geq 1\}
from
\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that
s_{n}\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
and

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{s_{n}}K_{1}(s_{n})=0,\qquad w.p.1.

(4.14)

Since

\displaystyle J_{{1|2}}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}1(Q_{2}(u)=0)du-\int_{0}^{t}1(Q_{1}(u)\leq c_{1},Q_{2}(u)=0)du

and
\int_{0}^{t}1(Q_{1}(u)\leq c_{1},Q_{2}(u)=0)du\leq K_{1}(t)
, (
4.8
) and (
4.14
) yield

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{s_{n}}J_{{1|2}}(s_{n})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{s_{n}}\int_{0}^{s_{n}}1(Q_{2}(u)=0)du=1-\rho_{2},\qquad w.p.1,

because
{\boldsymbol{X}}_{2}(\cdot)
is positive Harris recurrent by
\rho_{2}<1
. Hence, this together with (
4.7
) and (
4.11
) imply that

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{s_{n}}D_{{1|2}}(s_{n})

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{J_{{1|2}}(s_{n})}{s_{n}}\frac{1}{J_{{1|2}}(s_{n})}N_{{1|2}}(J_{{1|2}}(s_{n}))

\displaystyle=(1-\rho_{2})\liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{J_{{1|2}}(s_{n})}N_{{1|2}}(J_{{1|2}}(s_{n)})

\displaystyle=(1-\rho_{2})\mu_{{1|2}},\qquad w.p.1,

where the last equality is obtained by (
4.11
). Thus, we have proved (
4.13
).
∎

4.3
The proof of Theorem
3.1

We now start to prove Theorem
3.1
. We first prove the sufficiency of (
3.9
) and (
3.10
). We apply reduction to the absurdity. For this assume (
4.12
) for all
{\boldsymbol{x}}\in S
and
\ell\geq 0
. Since
\rho_{2}<1
, it follows from Lemmas
4.1
and
4.2
that, for the nonnegative sequence
\{s_{n};n\geq 1\}
obtained in Lemma
4.2
,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{s_{n}}A_{{1|2}}(s_{n})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{s_{n}}D_{{1|2}}(s_{n})=\mu_{{1|2}}(1-\rho_{2}),\qquad w.p.1.

(4.15)

Since
B_{1}(t)=t-\int_{0}^{t}1(Q_{1}(u)=0)du
, we have, by (
4.14
),

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{s_{n}}B_{1}(s_{n})=1-\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{s_{n}}\int_{0}^{s_{n}}1(Q_{1}(u)=0)du=1,\qquad w.p.1.

(4.16)

Furthermore, because
D_{v}(t)=N_{v}(B_{v}(t))
, strong LLN and (
4.16
) yield

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{s_{n}}A_{1}(s_{n})=\lambda_{1},\qquad w.p.1,

(4.17)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{s_{n}}D_{1}(s_{n})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{s_{n}}B_{1}(s_{n})\frac{1}{B_{1}(s_{n})}N_{1}(B_{1}(s_{n}))=\mu_{1},\qquad w.p.1.

(4.18)

Hence, (
4.2
) yields

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}-\mu_{1}-\mu_{{1|2}}(1-\rho_{2})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{s_{n}}Q_{1}(s_{n})\geq 0,\qquad w.p.1,

(4.19)

which implies that

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}\geq\mu_{1}+\mu_{{1|2}}(1-\rho_{2}).

(4.20)

Consequently, (
4.12
) for all
{\boldsymbol{x}}\in S
and
\ell>0
implies (
4.20
). Thus, taking the contraposition of this implication,

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}<\mu_{1}+\mu_{{1|2}}(1-\rho_{2})

(4.21)

implies that (
4.12
) does not hold, that is, for some
{\boldsymbol{x}}\in S
and some
\ell\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{P}_{{\boldsymbol{x}}}(Q_{1}(u)\leq\ell)du>0.

(4.22)

Hence,
{\boldsymbol{X}}(\cdot)
is positive Harris recurrent by Lemma
3.2
.

For the necessity of (
3.9
) and (
3.10
), assume that
{\boldsymbol{X}}(\cdot)
is positive Harris recurrent. As noted at the beginning of Section
4.2
, this implies that
\rho_{2}<1
. So, we only need to show the necessity of (
3.9
). From the positive recurrence of
{\boldsymbol{X}}(\cdot)
, we have
\rho^{*}_{1}(\xi)=1-\mathbb{P}_{\nu}(Q_{1}(0)=0)<1
by Lemma
3.3
. By Lemma
4.1
,
\overline{D}_{{1|2}}\leq\mu_{{1|2}}(1-\rho_{2})
. Hence, applying Little’s law to the class-1 customers who are served at station 1 and their mean number in service, which is
\rho^{*}_{1}(\xi)
, we have

\displaystyle\mu_{1}\rho^{*}_{1}(\xi)=\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{{1|2}}\geq\lambda_{1}-\mu_{{1|2}}(1-\rho_{2}).

(4.23)

On the other hand, from the definition (
3.9
) of

\widetilde{\rho}_{1}
,

\mu_{{1|2}}(1-\rho_{2})\widetilde{\rho}_{1}=\lambda_{1}-\mu_{1}\widetilde{\rho}_{1}.

Hence, it follows from (
4.23
) that

\displaystyle\mu_{1}\rho^{*}_{1}(\xi)\widetilde{\rho}_{1}\geq\lambda_{1}\widetilde{\rho}_{1}-\mu_{{1|2}}(1-\rho_{2})\widetilde{\rho}_{1}=\lambda_{1}\widetilde{\rho}_{1}-\lambda_{1}+\mu_{1}\widetilde{\rho}_{1},

which is equivalent to
\lambda_{1}(1-\widetilde{\rho}_{1})\geq\mu_{1}\widetilde{\rho}_{1}(1-\rho^{*}_{1}(\xi))
. Thus,
\rho^{*}_{1}(\xi)<1
implies
\widetilde{\rho}_{1}<1
. This proves that (
3.9
) is necessary for the stability, and the proof of Theorem
3.1
is completed.

5
General
k
-station cascade system

We next consider an extension of Theorem
3.1
to a cascade system with
k
-stations, which are numbered as
1,2,\ldots,k
from top to bottom. Each station
i
has exogenous arrivals, called class-
i
customers, and served in the manner of first come first served. If the number of class-
i
customers is greater than
c_{i}
and if station
i+1
is empty, then one class-
i
customer not in service immediately moves to station
i+1
as a class-
i|(i+1)
customer at station
i+1
for
i\leq k-1
, where
c_{i}
is a positive integer. It is assumed that a class-
i|(i+1)
customer is only served when there is no class-
(i+1)
customer, and its service is resumed when class-
(i+1)
customers vanish at station
i+1
. All customers leave the system when their service is completed. We refer to this system as a
k
-station cascade system.

Note that the system is stochastically independent of which class-
i
customer not in service moves to station
i+1
as a class-
i|(i+1)
customer. Furthermore, an exogenously arriving class-
(i+1)
customer preempts class-
i|(i+1)
customer when the latter is being served at station
(i+1)
. Hence, the stability of station
i
is independent of the dynamics of stations
1,2,\ldots,i-1
, but it is influenced from downward stations
i+1,i+2,\ldots,k
. Let
Q_{i}(t)
be the number of class-
i
customers in station
i
at time
t\geq 0
and let
Q_{i-1|i}(t)
the number of class-
(i-1)|i
customers in station
i
at time
t
. The remaining times
R_{e,i}(t)
,
R_{s,i}(t)
for
i=1,2,\ldots,k
and
R_{s,i|(i+1)}(t)
for
i=1,2,\ldots,k-1
are defined for each time
t\geq 0
similarly to the case of
k=2
.

As for the regularity condition, we need to replace Assumption
3.1
by its
k
-station version. Namely,

Assumption 5.1
.

For
i=1,2,\ldots,k
, the conditions (a) and (b) of Assumption
3.1
hold.

Similarly to the case of
k=2
, we can construct the Markov process
{\boldsymbol{X}}(\cdot)\equiv\{{\boldsymbol{X}}(t);t\geq 0\}
by using the remaining arrival and service times, where

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{X}}(t)=({\boldsymbol{Q}}_{1,k}(t),{\boldsymbol{R}}_{e,1,k}(t),{\boldsymbol{R}}_{s,1,k}(t),{\boldsymbol{R}}_{s,1,k}^{+}(t))\in S\equiv\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{2k}\times\{0,1\}^{k-1}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{3k-1},

(5.1)

where, for
i=1,2,\ldots,k
,

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{Q}}_{i,k}(t)=(Q_{i}(t),Q_{i+1}(t),\ldots,Q_{k}(t),Q_{i|(i+1)}(t),Q_{(i+1)|(i+2)}(t),\ldots,Q_{(k-1)|k}(t)),

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{R}}_{e,i.k}(t)=(R_{e,i}(t),R_{e,i+1}(t),\ldots,R_{e,k}(t)),\quad{\boldsymbol{R}}_{s,i,k}(t)=(R_{s,i}(t),R_{s,i+1}(t),\ldots,R_{s,k}(t)),

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{R}}_{s,i,k}^{+}(t)=(R_{s,i|(i+1)}(t),R_{s,(i+1)|(i+2)}(t),\ldots,R_{s,(k-1)|k}(t)).

To describe the system dynamics, we also introduce counting processes. For
v=i
and
v=(i-1)|i
, let
A_{v}(t)
be the number of the arrivals of class-
v
customers up to time
t\geq 0
, and let
N_{v}(t)
be the number of potential service completions of class-
v
customers by time
t
for
v=1,2,\ldots,k
and
v=1|2,2|3,\ldots,(k-1)|k
. It is assumed that
A_{i}(\cdot)
and
N_{i}(\cdot)
for
i=1,2,\ldots,k
and
N_{(i-1)|i}(\cdot)

i=2,3,\ldots,k
are independent renewal processes. Their rates are denoted by
\lambda_{i}
,
\mu_{i}
and
\mu_{(i-1)|i}
, respectively.

Let
D_{v}(t)
be the number of departures of class-
v
customers by time
t\geq 0
. Let

\displaystyle L_{i}(t)=Q_{i}(t)+Q_{(i-1)|i}(t)1(i\geq 2),\qquad i=1,2,\ldots,k.

Note that class-
i
and class-
(i-1)|i
customers have different service time distributions for
i\geq 2
. From the modeling assumptions, we have, for
i=1,2,\ldots,k
,

\displaystyle Q_{i}(t)=Q_{i}(0)+A_{i}(t)-(D_{i}(t)+A_{i|i+1}(t)),\quad t\geq 0,

(5.2)

\displaystyle Q_{(i-1)|i}(t)=Q_{(i-1)|i}(0)+A_{(i-1)|i}(t)-D_{(i-1)|i}(t),\quad t\geq 0,

(5.3)

where
A_{0|1}(t)=A_{k|(k+1)}(t)=0
. Note that the term
A_{(i-1)|i}(t)-D_{(i-1)|i}(t)
has no influence on the stability of
Q_{i}(t)
because the assumption on class-
(i-1)|i
customers implies

\displaystyle 0\leq A_{(i-1)|i}(t)-D_{(i-1)|i}(t)\leq 1,\qquad t\geq 0.

(5.4)

Let
\rho_{i}=\frac{\lambda_{i}}{\mu_{i}}
for
i=1,2,\ldots,k
. Similar to (

), we define
\rho^{*}_{i}(\xi)
for a distribution

\displaystyle\rho^{*}_{i}(\xi)\equiv\liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\int_{S}\mathbb{P}_{{\boldsymbol{x}}}(Q_{i}(u)\geq 1)\xi(d{\boldsymbol{x}})\Big)du,\qquad i=2,3,\ldots,k.

(5.5)

We also define a Markov process describing the last
k-i+1
stations by

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{X}}_{i,k}(\cdot)\equiv\{({\boldsymbol{Q}}_{i,k}(t),{\boldsymbol{R}}_{e,i,k}(t),{\boldsymbol{R}}_{s,i,k}(t),{\boldsymbol{R}}^{+}_{s,i,k}(t));t\geq 0\},\qquad i=1,2,\ldots,k.

Then, the following lemma is proved in the exactly same way as Lemmas
3.3
and
4.1
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Under Assumption
5.1
, for each
i=1,2,\ldots,k

{\boldsymbol{X}}_{i,k}(\cdot)
is positive Harris recurrent if and only if
\rho^{*}_{j}(\xi)<1
for
j=i,i+1,\ldots,k
for some distribution
\xi
on
S
. Furthermore, particularly when this is the case: for
i\geq 2
,

\displaystyle\overline{D}_{{(i-1)|i}}\equiv\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}D_{{(i-1)|i}}(t)\leq\mu_{{(i-1)|i}}(1-\rho^{*}_{i}(\xi)),\qquad w.p.1,

(5.6)

\displaystyle\lambda_{(i-1)|i}\equiv\mathbb{E}\left(\overline{D}_{{(i-1)|i}}\right)\leq\mu_{{(i-1)|i}}(1-\rho^{*}_{i}(\xi)).

(5.7)

Then, we have the following result similar to Theorem
3.1
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Under Assumption
5.1
, the Markov process
{\boldsymbol{X}}(\cdot)
which describes the
k
-station cascade system is positive recurrent if and only if the following conditions hold for some distribution
\xi
on
S
.

\displaystyle\widetilde{\rho}_{i}(\xi)\equiv\frac{\lambda_{i}}{\mu_{i}+\mu_{{i|(i+1)}}(1-\rho^{*}_{i+1}(\xi))}<1,\qquad i=1,2,\ldots,k-1,

(5.8)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\rho}_{k}(\xi)\equiv\rho_{k}<1.

(5.9)

The necessity of (

6
Concluding remarks

We have assumed that the exogenous arrival processes
A_{i}(\cdot)
at station
i
are independent renewal processes with finite arrival rates and the service times of each class of customers are
i.i.d.
. However, from the proof of Theorem
3.1
, those assumptions can be relaxed in proving that (
3.9
) implies (
4.22
) under the assumption
\rho_{2}<1
, which is the core part in the proof of Theorem
3.1
. Namely, this implication is valid as long as the following conditions hold.

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}A_{i}(t)=\lambda_{i},\qquad i=1,2,\;w.p.1,

(6.1)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\tau_{s,v}(\ell)=\mu_{v},\qquad v=1,2,{1|2},\;w.p.1,

(6.2)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}1(Q_{2}(s)=0)ds=\max(0,1-\rho_{2}),\qquad w.p.1,

(6.3)

where
\rho_{2}=\lambda_{2}/\mu_{2}
.

The condition (
4.22
) is much weaker than the existence of the stationary distribution of
Q_{1}(\cdot)
, but it may be interpreted as a certain stability property of the process
Q_{1}(\cdot)
. Hence, it may be interesting to investigate the stability of the 2-station cascade system in a weaker sense for more general exogenous arrival processes based on the conditions (
6.1
), (
6.2
) and (
6.3
).

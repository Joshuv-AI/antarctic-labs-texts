Vishnu Murali, Ashutosh Trivedi, Majid Zamani

Abstract
. Barrier certificates provide functional overapproximations for the reachable set of dynamical systems and provide inductive guarantees on the safe evolution of the system. Formally, a barrier certificate is a real-valued function over the state set that is required to be non-positive for the initial states, positive over the set of unsafe states, and non-increasing along the state transitions. These conditions together provide an inductive argument that the system will not reach an unsafe state “even once” as the barrier certificate remains non-positive for all reachable states. In the automata-theoretic approach to verification, a key verification query is to determine whether the system visits a given predicate over the states “finitely often”, typically resulting from the complement of the traditional Büchi acceptance condition. This paper proposes a barrier certificate approach to answer such queries by developing a notion of co-Büchi barrier certificates (CBBCs) that generalize classic barrier certificates to ensure that the traces of a system visit a given predicate a fixed number of times. Our notion of CBBC is inspired from bounded synthesis paradigm to LTL realizability, where the LTL specifications are converted to safety automata via (universal) co-Büchi automata with a bound on final state visitations provided as a hyperparameter. Our application of CBBCs in verification is analogous in nature: we fix a bound and search for a suitable barrier certificate, increasing the bound if no suitable function can be found. We then use these CBBCs to verify our system against properties specified by co-Büchi automata and demonstrate their effectiveness via some case studies. We also show that the present approach strictly generalizes performant barrier certificate based approaches that rely on “cutting” the paths of the automata that start from an initial state and reach some accepting states.



1.
Introduction

The notion of
barrier certificates

(
Prajna et al. 2002
)

combined with constraint satisfaction based search approaches have transformed automatic safety verification landscape for dynamical systems.
A barrier certificate is a function over the state set of the system, such that the value of the barrier is non-positive over the initial set of states, is non-increasing as the system evolves, and positive over the unsafe set of states.
They thus act as a functional overapproximation of the reachable set of states where any state is in the overapproximation if it has a value that is non-positive.
When combined with the positivity condition over the unsafe set, it guarantees safety.
Barrier certificates are an
abstraction
-free approach to safety that, when applicable, provide an antidote to the curse of dimensionality.

In the classic automata-theoretic approach to verification, the central query involves ascertaining whether a set of states may only be visited finitely often.
In particular, bounded synthesis based approaches
(
Schewe and Finkbeiner 2007
;
Filiot et al. 2009
;
Filiot et al. 2011
;
Bohy et al. 2012
)
are centered around the idea that the problem of determining whether a set of states is visited finitely often can be answered by demonstrating a safety condition; this condition characterizes that the number of times the system can reach the aforementioned set is bounded from above by some fixed hyperparameter.

This paper presents an abstraction-free approach to automata-theoretic verification of discrete-time dynamical systems by advocating a notion of Co-Büchi Barrier Certificates.

Contributions.

The key idea of the paper builds around the following simple observation: while the question of visiting a region finitely often is not a direct safety property for the system under consideration (and hence cannot be captured via a barrier certificate), if one considers a related system that appends the original system with a counter keeping track of the region visitation, a related bounded-visitation property can be cast as a safety property over the extended system.
Moreover, if the extended system is safe with respect to the modified safety property, it provides a certificate for bounded-visitation of the region for the original system.
In other words, while the property was not inductive on the original system, it turns inductive over the extended system.
Of course, we need not explicitly compute this modified system; instead we may simply modify the definition of barrier certificates over the original system.
We dub this modified definition
Co-Büchi barrier certificates
(CBBCs) and they act as guarantees of finite visits to a given region.
We further build on this idea of CBBC, and propose an automata-theoretic technique to verify discrete-time dynamical systems by expressing the property as universal co-Büchi automata (UCA).
We present two computational methods (SMT based and Sum-of-Squares based) to systematically search for these CBBCs and demonstrate their effectiveness on a paradigmatic case study.

Related works.

Traditional model checking approaches
(
Henzinger et al. 1997
;
Tabuada 2009
;
Baier and Katoen 2008
)
for continuous space systems against temporal properties, such as those specified in linear temporal logic
(
Pnueli 1977
)
, typically involve building and verifying their finite-state abstractions against these properties.
However, these approaches suffer from the curse of dimensionality: their complexity is exponential in the dimension of the state space.
First proposed by Prajna et al.
(
Prajna and Jadbabaie 2004
)
, the notion of barrier certificates has seen widespread use
(
Prajna and Rantzer 2007
;
Ames et al. 2016
;
Clark 2021
)
in the verification of safety for continuous-space systems as they provide an abstraction-free approach.
While these approaches are sound, techniques to find barrier certificates of a certain template (

a polynomial) that rely on semidefinite programming
(

)
such as sum-of-squares
(
Parrilo 2003
)
, have a complexity that is polynomial in the dimension of the state space (assuming their template is fixed).

Wongpiromsarn et al.
(
Wongpiromsarn et al. 2015
)
introduced the idea of using barrier certificate to verify continuous-space systems against temporal logic properties and subsequently it has inspired several extensions
(

;

While the state triplet approach is useful in verifying properties specified by automaton on finite words (especially properties that are specified by co-safe languages
(
Kupferman and Vardi 2001
)
) , it unfortunately faces limitations when dealing with general automata on infinite words.
Note that the acceptance condition of automata over infinite words ask whether an accepting state is visited infinitely often, however this state triplet approach may be used to verify only those systems whose traces reach the accepting state of the automaton at most twice (cf. Subsection
7.1
).
Secondly, note that this approach is independent of the runs in the automaton and of the initial states of the system, in the following way:
one is required to break the edge pairs of a path regardless of what states of the automaton may be encountered before or after.
We show (cf. Subsection
7.2

Our notion of CBBCs is inspired by the ideas present in bounded synthesis
(
Schewe and Finkbeiner 2007
;
Filiot et al. 2009
;
Filiot et al. 2011
;
Bohy et al. 2012
)
, where one successively increases the bound on the number of times a region is visited.
While
(
Papusha et al. 2016
;
Dimitrova and Majumdar 2014
)
rely on the product of the system with the automaton and thus might be less conservative in comparison to the state triplet approach, they do not allow the same degree of giving incremental guarantees, as one needs to unfold the automaton to increase the bound on the number of visits.

2.1.
Automata over Infinite Words

An automaton
\mathcal{A}
is a tuple
(\Sigma,Q,Q_{0},\delta,Q_{Acc})
, where
\Sigma
denotes a finite alphabet,
Q
a finite set of states,
Q_{0}\subseteq Q
an initial set of states,
\delta\subseteq Q\times\Sigma\times Q
the transition relation, and
Q_{Acc}\subseteq Q
denotes a set of accepting states.
An automaton is
deterministic
if for all
q\in Q
, and
\sigma\in\Sigma
, we have
|\delta(q,\sigma)|\leq 1
,
i.e.
,
\delta:Q\times\Sigma\to Q
is a function.
A run of the automaton
\mathcal{A}=(\Sigma,Q,q_{0},\delta,Q_{Acc})
over a word
w=\langle\sigma_{0},\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\ldots,\rangle\in\Sigma^{\omega}
, is an infinite sequence of states
\rho=\langle q_{0},q_{1},q_{2},\ldots,\rangle\in Q^{\omega}
with
q_{0}\in Q_{0}
and
q_{i+1}\in\delta(q_{i},\sigma_{i})
.
A
nondeterministic Büchi automaton
(NBA)
\mathcal{A}_{b}=(\Sigma,Q,Q_{0},\delta,Q_{Acc})
is said to accept a word
w
, if there exists a run
\rho
on
w
where
\mathsf{Inf}(\rho)\cap Q_{Acc}\neq\emptyset
.
A
Universal Co-Büchi automaton
(UCA)
\mathcal{A}_{c}=(\Sigma,Q,Q_{0},\delta,Q_{Acc})
accepts a word
w
, if for every run
\rho
on
w
we have
\mathsf{Inf}(\rho)\cap Q_{Acc}=\emptyset
.
Our definition of a UCA is based on the definition considered in
(
Filiot et al. 2011
)
, though alternative, and equivalent semantics (ensuring
\mathsf{Inf}(s)\subseteq Q_{Acc}
) exist
(
Esparza and Křetínskỳ 2014
)
.
For
k\in\mathbb{N}
, a
k
-Universal Co-Büchi automaton (
k
-UCA)
\mathcal{A}_{c,k}=(\Sigma,Q,Q_{0},\delta,Q_{Acc})
accepts a word
w
if, every run
\rho
on
w
visits some state in the set
Q_{Acc}
at most
k
times.
The language of an automaton
\mathcal{A}
denoted
L(\mathcal{A})
is defined as the set of words accepted by the automaton,
i.e.
,
L(\mathcal{A})=\{w\mid\mathcal{A}\text{ accepts word }w\}
.

It is well known that NBAs are closed under complementation
(
Safra 1988
)
: given an NBA
\mathcal{A}_{b}=(\Sigma,Q,Q_{0},\delta,Q_{Acc})
, there exists an NBA
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{b}=(\Sigma,Q^{\prime},Q^{\prime}_{0},\delta^{\prime},Q^{\prime}_{Acc})
such that
L(\mathcal{A}_{b})=\overline{L(\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{b})}
.
We also observe that the acceptance conditions of an NBA and a UCA are duals of each other,
i.e.
, given an NBA
A_{b}=(\Sigma,Q,Q_{0},\delta,Q_{Acc})
, and a UCA
\mathcal{A}_{c}=(\Sigma,Q,Q_{0},\delta,Q_{Acc})
, with the same structure (the same sets and transition relation), we have
L(\mathcal{A}_{b})=\overline{L(\mathcal{A}_{c})}
.
Observe that for natural numbers
k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
, a
k_{1}
-UCA
\mathcal{A}_{c,k_{1}}=(\Sigma,Q,Q_{0},\delta,Q_{Acc})
, and a
k_{2}
-UCA
\mathcal{A}_{c,k_{2}}=(\Sigma,Q,Q_{0},\delta,Q_{Acc})
with the same structure, we have
L(\mathcal{A}_{c,k_{1}})\subseteq L(\mathcal{A}_{c,k_{2}})
.
Finally, for a
k
-UCA
\mathcal{A}_{c,k}=(\Sigma,Q,Q_{0},\delta,Q_{Acc})
and the UCA
\mathcal{A}_{c}=(\Sigma,Q,Q_{0},\delta,Q_{Acc})
we have
L(\mathcal{A}_{c,k_{1}})\subseteq L(\mathcal{A}_{c})
.

Discrete-time Dynamical System

A discrete-time dynamical system (or simply, a system)

(1)

\mathfrak{S}:x(t+1)=f(x(t)).

A

Safety Verification and Barrier Certificates

A system

(2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(x)\leq 0

\displaystyle\text{ for all }x\in\mathcal{X}_{0}

(3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(x)>0

\displaystyle\text{ for all }x\in\mathcal{X}_{u}

(4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(f(x))-\mathcal{B}(x)\leq 0

\displaystyle\text{ for all }x\in\mathcal{X}

2.4.
Problem Definition

We focus on the problem of characterizing barrier certificates demonstrating that a set of states is visited finitely often, and use it to provide a barrier certificate based approach for automata-theoretic verification of systems.

Problem 1 (Co-Büchi Barrier Certificates (CBBC)).

What is a good (correct and effective) notion of barrier certificates to demonstrate that every state sequence of
\mathfrak{S}=(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}_{0},f)
visits a region
\mathcal{X}_{VF}\subseteq\mathcal{X}
finitely often?

We then make use of these certificates to verify systems against properties specified by automata.

Given an NBA
\mathcal{A}_{b}=(\Sigma,Q,Q_{0},\delta,Q_{Acc})
, we can construct a UCA
\mathcal{A}_{c}=(\Sigma,Q,Q_{0},\delta,Q_{Acc})
, where
L(\mathcal{A}_{c})=\overline{L(\mathcal{A}_{b})}
.
Thus showing that every trace of the system
\mathfrak{S}
visits
Q_{Acc}
finitely often is a sufficient condition to verify that the traces of the system are in
L(\mathcal{A}_{c})
and hence not in
L(\mathcal{A}_{b})
.

Problem 2 (Automata-Theoretic Verification via CBBC).

Prove that the traces
TR(\mathfrak{S},\mathcal{L})
of
\mathfrak{S}=(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}_{0},f)
under labeling
\mathcal{L}:\mathcal{X}\to\Sigma
, are in the language of a
k
-UCA
\mathcal{A}_{c,k}=(\Sigma,Q,Q_{0},\delta,Q_{Acc})
.

Corollary 3.

If the traces
TR(\mathfrak{S},\mathcal{L})
of a system
\mathfrak{S}=(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}_{0},f)
under a labeling map
\mathcal{L}:\mathcal{X}\to\Sigma
, are contained in the language of a
k
-UCA
\mathcal{A}_{c,k}=(\Sigma,Q,Q_{0},\delta,Q_{Acc})
, then they are contained in the language of the UCA
\mathcal{A}_{c}=(\Sigma,Q,Q_{0},\delta,Q_{Acc})
.

Corollary 4.

If the traces
TR(\mathfrak{S},\mathcal{L})
of a system
\mathfrak{S}=(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}_{0},f)
under a labeling map
\mathcal{L}:\mathcal{X}\to\Sigma
, are contained in the language of a UCA
\mathcal{A}_{c}=(\Sigma,Q,Q_{0},\delta,Q_{Acc})

.

3.
Co-Büchi Barrier Certificates (CBBC)

Barrier certificates act as proofs for safety, however many properties of interest require showing that a given region is visited a finite number of times rather than never visited at all.
In particular, the accepting conditions of NBAs and UCAs depend on showing that the accepting states are either visited infinitely or finitely often.

We thus consider approaches where one may use barrier certificates to show a set of states
\mathcal{X}_{VF}
is visited finitely often.
To do so, we modify the system
\mathfrak{S}=(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}_{0},f)
by appending a counter variable to the state space.
This counter keeps track of the number of times a state in
\mathcal{X}_{VF}
is seen.

Formally, we construct a system
\mathfrak{S}^{\prime}=(\mathcal{X}^{\prime},\mathcal{X}^{\prime}_{0},f^{\prime})
, where
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}=\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{N}
denotes the state set (containing an additional counter value).
The set
\mathcal{X}_{0}^{\prime}:=(\mathcal{X}_{0}\setminus\mathcal{X}_{VF})\times\{0\}\cup(\mathcal{X}_{0}\cap\mathcal{X}_{VF})\times\{1\}

consist of the states
(x_{0},0)
if the state
x_{0}
is in
\mathcal{X}_{0}
and not
\mathcal{X}_{VF}
, and
(x_{0},1)

if the state
x_{0}
is in both
\mathcal{X}_{0}
and
\mathcal{X}_{VF}
.
Lastly, the transition function
f^{\prime}:\mathcal{X}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
is defined as:

(5)

f^{\prime}((x,i))=\begin{cases}(f(x),i+1)&\text{ for all }x\in\mathcal{X},\text{ such that }\\
&f(x)\in\mathcal{X}_{VF}\text{ and }i\in\mathbb{N},\\
(f(x),i)&\text{ otherwise}\end{cases}

Observe that any state sequence
\langle x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,\rangle
of the system
\mathfrak{S}
that visits the region
\mathcal{X}_{VF}
finitely often must have some index
i\in N
, with
x_{j}\notin\mathcal{X}_{VF}
for all
j\geq i
.
This corresponds to a state sequence
\langle x^{\prime}_{0},x^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,\rangle
of the system
\mathfrak{S}^{\prime}
such that there exists some
k\in\mathbb{N}
with
x^{\prime}_{j+1}=(f(j),k)
for all
j\geq i
.
Therefore showing the system
\mathfrak{S}
visits
\mathcal{X}_{VF}
finitely often reduces to showing the system
\mathfrak{S}^{\prime}
never reaches a state
(x,k+1)
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
, and all
x\in\mathcal{X}_{VF}
.

We define CBBCs over the original system by taking this modified system’s dynamics into account.

Definition 3.0.

A function
\mathcal{B}:\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}
is a co-Büchi barrier certificate (CBBC) for a system
\mathfrak{S}
with set of states
\mathcal{X}_{VF}
that must be visited finitely often if there exists some
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that:

(6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(x,0)\leq 0

\displaystyle\text{ for all }x\in\mathcal{X}_{0}\setminus\mathcal{X}_{VF},

(7)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(x,1)\leq 0

\displaystyle\text{ for all }x\in\mathcal{X}_{0}\cap\mathcal{X}_{VF},

(8)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(x,k+1)>0

\displaystyle\text{ for all }x\in\mathcal{X}_{VF},

and for all values
0\leq i\leq k
, and states
x\in\mathcal{X}
:

(9)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(f(x),i)-\mathcal{B}(x,i)\leq 0

\displaystyle\text{if }f(x)\notin\mathcal{X}_{VF},\text{ and, }

(10)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(f(x),i+1)-\mathcal{B}(x,i)\leq 0

otherwise.

\mathfrak{S}=(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}_{0},f)

\mathcal{X}_{VF}

We now extend the results in Section

Let
\mathfrak{S}=(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}_{0},f)

\mathcal{L}:\mathcal{X}\to\Sigma

4.1.

We first take a synchronous product between the system

5.
CBBC Computation

We present two approaches to compute co-Büchi barrier certificates as in Definition
1
as well as those used to verify properties specified by an automaton as in Definition
1
.
The first relies on using Satisfiability Modulo Theory (SMT) solvers, while the second is based on a sum-of-squares (SOS) approach.

5.1.
SMT-based Approach

We make use of a Counterexample-guided Inductive Synthesis (CEGIS)
(
Solar-Lezama 2008
)
approach to find these CBBCs.
To find a CBBC as in Definition
1
, we first fix the template of the CBBC to be a polynomial of a fixed degree
d
with unknown coefficients
c_{1},\ldots,c_{z}
,
i.e.

\mathcal{B}(x,i)=\sum\limits_{m=1}^{z}c_{m}p_{m}(x,i)
where the functions
p_{m}
are monomials over the state variable and the counter value.
Observe that if the value of
x\in\mathcal{X}
and
i\in\mathbb{N}
is fixed, then the only decision variables in
\mathcal{B}(x,i)
are the coefficients
c_{m}
.
We sample
N
points from the state space of the system to create the set
S=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}\}
, and encode the constraints of the barrier certificate for each point
x_{j}\in S
and all
0\leq i\leq k
as an SMT-query over the theory of linear real arithmetic (LRA)
(
Dutertre and Moura 2006
)
using z3
(
Moura and Bjørner 2008
)
.
We encode the constraints as a conjunction of the following constraints for every state
x_{j}\in S
and every
0\leq\ell\leq k
:

(16)

\displaystyle\ \underset{x_{j}\in(\mathcal{X}_{0}\setminus\mathcal{X}_{VF})}{\bigwedge}\Big(\mathcal{B}(x_{j},0)\leq 0\Big)

(17)

\displaystyle\underset{x_{j}\in(\mathcal{X}_{0}\cap\mathcal{X}_{VF})}{\bigwedge}\Big(\mathcal{B}(x_{j},1)\leq 0\Big)

(18)

\displaystyle\;\underset{(x_{j}\in\mathcal{X}_{VF})}{\bigwedge}\Big(\mathcal{B}(x_{j},\ell+1)>0\Big)

(19)

\displaystyle\underset{(f(x_{j})\notin\mathcal{X}_{VF})}{\bigwedge}\Big(\mathcal{B}(f(x_{j}),\ell)\leq\mathcal{B}(x_{j},\ell)\Big)

(20)

\displaystyle\underset{(f(x_{j})\in\mathcal{X}_{VF})}{\bigwedge}\Big(\mathcal{B}(f(x_{j}),\ell+1)\leq\mathcal{B}(x_{j},\ell)\Big)

We then find values
c_{1},\ldots,c_{z}
for the coefficients and substitute them as a candidate CBBC
\mathcal{B}(x,i)
.
To determine if this candidate is a CBBC, we now try to find a

such that one of the conditions (
6
) to (
10
) does not hold.
We do this by encoding the negation of these conditions as an SMT query.
If such a counterexample
x_{counter}
is found, we add it to the set
S
and repeat the process.
If no such counterexample is found, then we conclude that this function is indeed a CBBC.
We adopt a similar approach to find a CBBC for the synchronized product as in Definition
1
, that acts as a proof that the traces of the system are contained in the language of a
k
-UCA
\mathcal{A}_{c,k}=(\Sigma,Q,Q_{0},\delta,Q_{Acc})
.
Here, we encode the constraints as a conjunction of the following for every state
x_{i}\in S
, and every state

(21)

\displaystyle\ \underset{x_{i}\in\mathcal{X}_{0},q_{\xi}\in(Q_{0}\setminus Q_{Acc})}{\bigwedge}\Big(\mathcal{B}(x_{i},\xi,0)\leq 0\Big),

(22)

\displaystyle\ \underset{x_{i}\in\mathcal{X}_{0},q_{\xi}\in(Q_{0}\cap Q_{Acc})}{\bigwedge}\Big(\mathcal{B}(x_{i},\xi,1)\leq 0\Big),

and

(23)

\displaystyle\quad\underset{x_{i}\in\mathcal{X},q_{\xi}\in Q_{Acc}}{\bigwedge}\Big(\mathcal{B}(x_{i},\xi,k+1)>0\Big),

and for all states

such that

, and for all counter values
0\leq\ell\leq k

(24)

\displaystyle\ \underset{x_{i}\in\mathcal{X}_{0},q_{j}\in(Q\setminus Q_{Acc})}{\bigwedge}\Big(\mathcal{B}(f(x_{i}),j,\ell)\leq\mathcal{B}(x_{i},\xi,\ell)\Big),

(25)

\displaystyle\ \underset{x_{i}\in\mathcal{X}_{0},q_{j}\in(Q_{Acc})}{\bigwedge}\Big(\mathcal{B}(f(x_{i}),j,\ell+1)\leq\mathcal{B}(x_{i},\xi,\ell)\Big).

7.
The Lifting Theorem

We show that our approach generalizes the state triplet approaches presented in
(
Wongpiromsarn et al. 2015
)
.
To do so we show that the existence of
m
-barrier certificates
\mathcal{B}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{B}_{m}
, that guarantee the system to not be in the language of an NBA
\mathcal{A}_{b}
by the state triplet approach implies the existence of a CBBC in our approach.

In particular, we prove the following theorem.

Theorem 1 (The Lifting Theorem).

Let
\mathfrak{S}=(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}_{0},f)
be a system,
\mathcal{L}:\mathcal{X}\to\Sigma
be a labeling map, and
\mathcal{A}_{b}=(\Sigma,Q,Q_{0},\delta,Q_{Acc})
be an NBA. Suppose there exist barrier certificates
\mathcal{B}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{B}_{m}
that act as a proof that no trace of the system is in
L(\mathcal{A}_{b})
by the state triplet approach, then there exists a CBBC
\mathcal{B}
that also serves as a witness that no trace is in
L(\mathcal{A}_{b})
.

The proof is broken into two parts: in Subsection
7.1
we discuss the state triplet approach and its limitations, and then we present the construction of a CBBC in Subsection
7.2
.

7.1.
The State Triplet Approach

Consider a system
\mathfrak{S}=(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}_{0},f)
, an NBA
\mathcal{A}_{b}=(\Sigma,Q,Q_{0},\delta,Q_{Acc})
, and a labeling function
\mathcal{L}:\mathcal{X}\to\Sigma
.
We first unroll the simple cycles of the automaton
\mathcal{A}_{b}
that start from an accepting state to construct an automaton
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{b}=(\Sigma,Q^{\prime},Q_{0},\delta^{\prime},Q^{\prime}_{Acc})
such that
L(\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{b})=L(\mathcal{A}_{b})
.
The states
Q^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{b}
contain all the states
Q
of
\mathcal{A}_{b}
as well as one new state
q^{\prime}_{i}
for every state
q_{i}\in Q
that can be reached from an accepting state.
Determining which states are reachable from an accepting state can be done in polynomial time
(
Kozen 2006
)
in the number of states of the automaton.

Intuitively, these new states are meant to determine the state runs of the system which have reached an accepting state and continue onward.
The set of initial states is the same as
\mathcal{A}_{b}
, while the set of accepting states consist of the states
q^{\prime}_{Acc}
,
i.e.
, those accepting states that can be reached from an accepting state.
The transitions
\delta^{\prime}
are the same as the transitions for
\mathcal{A}_{b}
, except for the transitions that leave an accepting state
q_{Acc}\in Q_{Acc}
and additional transitions added.
The transitions added are due to two reasons :

•

For every pair of states

that are reachable from an accepting state, and for any
\sigma\in\Sigma
such that
q_{j}\in\delta(q_{i},\sigma)
, we add a transition
q^{\prime}_{j}\in\delta(q^{\prime}_{i},\sigma)
.
This preserves all the transitions for the newly added states so they behave the same as before.

•

For every state
q_{i}\in Q
, and every accepting state
q_{Acc}\in Q_{Acc}
, and any letter
\sigma\in\Sigma
such that
q_{j}\in\delta(q_{Acc},\sigma)
, we add a transition
q^{\prime}_{j}\in\delta(q_{Acc},\sigma)
. For every edge that leaves the accepting state, we add an edge to move to the states of the form
q^{\prime}\in Q^{\prime}\setminus Q
. Observe that there are no transition from the states in
Q^{\prime}\setminus Q
to the ones in
Q

q^{\prime\prime}\in Q^{\prime\prime}\setminus Q^{\prime}

•

If all the states in the triplet are in

(q_{1},q^{\prime}_{1},q^{\prime\prime}_{1})

Q^{\prime}

•

The state

q
and
q^{\prime}
are in
Q^{\prime}

(q_{1},q^{\prime}_{1},q^{\prime\prime}_{1})

q_{1}

q^{\prime}_{1}

•

The states
q^{\prime}

Q^{\prime\prime}\setminus Q^{\prime}
(they are newly added states) but
q

In this case there exists an analogous state triplet
(q_{1},q^{\prime}_{1},q^{\prime\prime}_{1})

q_{1}\in Q
and the states
q^{\prime}_{1}
and
q^{\prime\prime}_{1}

Q^{\prime}
.
Hence, this again does not add a new edge pair to be considered.

Thus, one is unable to verify those traces of a system which reach and cycle on an accepting state more than twice, even if they visit an accepting state finitely often.
Note that unrolling once does have an impact since we can now consider those state triplets along the simple paths from an accepting state to an accepting state.

8.
Conclusion

This paper advocated the notion of co-Büchi barrier certificates (CBBC) that act as a proof that a region is visited finitely often.
These CBBCs have been demonstrated to be useful in the automata-theoretic verification of discrete-time dynamical systems.
This paper shows that techniques to compute standard barrier certificates can be extended to search for CBBCs.
Some promising next steps are to understand the applicability of CBBCs in verifying quantitative and time-critical properties of dynamical systems via weighted, stochastic, and timed automata based specifications.

The notion of any novel barrier certificate can be analysed from two perspectives.
The first way to look at them is as a new notion of barrier certificates capturing a property of interest.
At the same time, they can be considered as classic barrier certificates over an extended system that encodes auxiliary information about a monitoring gadget (such as
\omega
-regular automata, pushdown stack) and some timing information (such as number of steps, dwell time, clocks).
While the first viewpoint requires ingenuity on behalf of the designers, the second promises to be more amenable to mechanical exploration.
When solvers fail to find barrier certificates, one may choose to extend the state space of the system with this information, in the hope that classical barrier certificates for the extended system can be found, in a manner reminiscent of IC3
(
Bradley 2011
)
. We believe that this work will provide impetus towards further research in this promising direction.

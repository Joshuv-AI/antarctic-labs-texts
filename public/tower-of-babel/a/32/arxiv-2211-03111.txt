Introduction

A considerable effort has been devoted to exploring the problem of finite-time blow-up solutions to a numerous families of deterministic partial differential equations (PDEs). In particular, motivated by the primary and seminal papers by Kaplan
[
14
]
and Fujita
[
10
,
11
]
, new techniques were developed for studying the blow-up behavior to a more general class of nonlinear PDEs. As far as the stochastic counterpart is concerned, there are only a limited results available on the non-existence of solutions of stochastic partial differential equations (SPDEs). For SPDEs driven by Brownian motions, Mueller and Sowers
[
19
]
proved that the heat equation

u_{t}=u_{xx}+u^{\gamma}\dot{W}

with continuous and nonnegative initial data blow-up in a finite-time with positive probability when
\gamma>3/2
where
\dot{W}=\dot{W}(\cdot,\cdot)
is a two-dimensional white noise process. Chow
[
1
]
proposed the notion of blow-up solution to nonlinear stochastic wave equations whose
L^{2}
-norms explode at a finite-time in the mean-square sense under some appropriate conditions on the initial data and the noise term. The existence of global positive solutions of semilinear stochastic partial differential equations (SPDEs) has been extensively investigated in
[
2
,
3
,
9
]
, etc. Dozzi and Lòpez-Mimbelab
[
6
]
investigated the blow-up problem for the following SPDE:

\displaystyle du(t,x)=[\Delta u(t,x)+G(u(t,x))]dt+\kappa u(t,x)dW(t),

(1.1)

with the Dirichlet data, on a smooth bounded domain, perturbed by a standard one-dimensional Brownian motion
\{W(t):t\geq 0\}
defined on some probability space
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{P}}}\right)
. Here
G:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
is a locally Lipschitz function satisfying

\displaystyle G(z)\leq Cz^{1+\beta},\ \mbox{for all}\ z>0,

(1.2)

where
C\ \mbox{and}\ \beta>0
are given numbers. The bounds for the explosion time are estimated in terms of exponential functionals of
W(\cdot)
by transforming the original equation into a random PDE. For the case,
G(u)=u^{1+\beta}
with
\beta>0
, the upper and lower bounds for the explosion time are estimated in
[
16
]
by using a formula developed by Yor (cf.
[
13
,
24
,
31
]
, etc.).

The impact of Gaussian noises on the time of blow-up of solutions to the following nonlinear SPDE:

du(t,x)=[\Delta u(t,x)+G(u(t,x))]dt+k_{1}u(t,x)dW_{1}(t)+k_{2}u(t,x)dW_{2}(t),

with the Dirichlet boundary condition, where
G
satisfying (
1.2
) and
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(W_{1}(t),W_{2}(t)}}\right)_{t\geq 0}
is a two-dimensional Brownian motion was investigated by Niu and Xie
[
20
]
. Dozzi et. al.,
[
4
]
extended the results of (
1.1
) to a system of semilinear SPDEs with the Dirichlet boundary data on a bounded smooth domain defined on some probability space
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{P}}}\right)
.

Concerning semilinear SPDEs driven by fractional Brownian motions, Dozzi et. al.,
[
7
]
derived lower and upper bounds for the explosion time to a semilinear SPDE driven by a one-dimensional fractional Brownian motion
\{B^{H}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
with Hurst index
H>\frac{1}{2}
. Further, sufficient conditions for blow-up in finite-time and the existence of a global solution are deduced and the probability of finite-time blow-up are obtained. Dung
[
8
]
proposed a new approach to obtain the upper and lower bounds for the probability of the finite-time blow-up of solution in terms of the Hurst parameter
H\in(0,1)
. Note that for the case
H=\frac{1}{2}
,
B^{H}(t)=W(t)
, a standard Brownian motion. In
[
25
]
, lower and upper bounds for the explosion times of a system of semilinear SPDEs with standard Brownian motion are obtained. Sankar et. al.,
[
26
]
established the lower and upper bounds for the non-global solution for a system of semilinear SPDEs driven by two-dimensional fractional Brownian motion.

Only a few authors have addressed the explosive behavior of SPDEs with fractional Laplacian operators of the nonlocal type. Wang
[
29
]
proved the positivity of solutions to such a stochastic nonlocal heat equation under some assumptions and proved that the solution blow-up in finite-time. In a recent paper, Dozzi et. al.,
[
5
]
provided sufficient conditions for finite-time blow-up of positive weak solutions of a fractional reaction-diffusion equation perturbed by a fractional Brownian motion (fBm). Moreover, lower and upper bounds for the probability that the solution does not explode in a finite-time are also provided. However, for many classes of SPDEs, the problem of existence and non-existence of solutions is not yet completely explored.

Motivated by the above facts, in the present paper, our aim is to obtain the global existence, and lower and upper bounds for the blow-up time to the following system of semilinear SPDEs:

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\begin{aligned} du_{1}(t,x)&=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\Delta_{\alpha}u_{1}(t,x)+\gamma_{1}u_{1}(t,x)+u^{1+\beta_{1}}_{2}(t,x)}}\right]dt\\

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\begin{aligned} \frac{\partial v_{i}(t,x)}{\partial t}&=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\Delta_{\alpha}+\gamma_{i}}}\right)v_{i}(t,x)+e^{-k_{i1}B_{1}^{H}(t)-k_{i2}B_{2}^{H}(t)}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(e^{k_{j1}B_{1}^{H}(t)+k_{j2}B_{2}^{H}(t)}v_{j}(t,x)}}\right)^{1+\beta_{i}},\\

(2.2)

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
0<H<1
and let
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(B_{1}^{H}(t),B_{2}^{H}(t)}}\right)_{t\geq 0}
be a two-dimensional fractional Brownian motion with Hurst parameter
H
defined on a probability space
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{P}}}\right).
If
\nu>0,
then

\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{B^{H}_{1}(s)+B^{H}_{2}(s)-\nu s}ds<\infty}}\right)=1.

If
\nu<0,
then

\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{B^{H}_{1}(s)+B^{H}_{2}(s)-\nu s}ds=\infty}}\right)=1.

Proof.

As a consequence of the law of iterated logarithm for fractional Brownian motion
[
22
]
, we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{B_{i}^{H}(t)}{t^{H}\sqrt{2\log\log t}}=1,\ \mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.,}\

for
i=1,2.
This means that for some
t_{0}>e
and for all
t\geq t_{0},
we have

B^{H}_{i}(t)\leq 2t^{H}\sqrt{2\log\log t},\ i=1,2.

Assume that
\nu>0.
Since
H<1,
there exists
t_{1}>t_{0}
such that for each
t\geq t_{1}

2t^{H}\sqrt{2\log\log t}\leq\frac{\nu t}{4}.

Therefore, for all
t\geq t_{1},
we have

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{\infty}e^{B^{H}_{1}(s)+B^{H}_{2}(s)-\nu s}ds<\int_{t}^{\infty}e^{\frac{\nu s}{4}+\frac{\nu s}{4}-\nu s}ds=\int_{t}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{\nu s}{2}}ds\leq\frac{2}{\nu}<\infty,\ \mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

Since
B_{1}^{H}
and
B_{2}^{H}
have continuous paths, it follows that

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{B^{H}_{1}(s)+B^{H}_{2}(s)-\nu s}ds<\infty,\ \mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

If
\nu<0,
using the fact that
B_{i}^{H}(t)=-B_{i}^{H}(t)
in distribution, for all
t>t_{0},
we have

B^{H}_{i}(t)\geq-2t^{H}\sqrt{2\log\log t},\ \mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}.

Taking
t_{1}>t_{0}
such that
-\frac{\nu t}{4}>2t^{H}\sqrt{2\log\log t},
for all
t>t_{1},
we have

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{\infty}e^{B^{H}_{1}(s)+B^{H}_{2}(s)-\nu s}ds

\displaystyle>\int_{t_{1}}^{\infty}e^{B^{H}_{1}(s)+B^{H}_{2}(s)-\nu s}ds>\int_{t_{1}}^{\infty}e^{-2t^{H}\sqrt{2\log\log t}-\nu t}ds

\displaystyle>\int_{t_{1}}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{\nu s}{2}}ds=\infty,\ \mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

which completes the proof.
∎

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{\rho_{1}W_{1}(r)+\rho_{2}W_{2}(r)}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|S_{r}f_{i}}}\right\|_{\infty}^{\beta_{i}}dr

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}^{\beta_{i}}p(1,0)^{\beta_{i}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{\rho_{1}W_{1}(r)+\rho_{2}W_{2}(r)}(r+\zeta)^{-d\beta_{i}/\alpha}dr

\displaystyle=C_{2}^{\beta_{i}}p(1,0)^{\beta_{i}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{\rho_{1}W_{1}(e^{t}-1)+\rho_{2}W_{2}(e^{t}-1)+t}(e^{t}-1+\zeta)^{-d\beta_{i}/\alpha}dt,

for
i=1,2.
In view of Lemma
5.1
, we need to estimate the finiteness of the integral
\int_{\varepsilon}^{\infty}\exp\{\rho_{1}e^{t/2}X_{1}(t)+\rho_{2}e^{t/2}X_{2}(t)-b_{i}t\}dt
for any
\varepsilon>0
and
b_{i}=d\beta_{i}/\alpha-1,i=1,2.
However, it is shown in Lemma
6.1
below that this integral diverges for any
\varepsilon>0
, even if
d>\alpha/\beta_{2}.
This shows that the stochastic perturbation in the system (
1.3
) qualitatively affects the behavior of solution. The reason is that
\Delta_{\alpha}
has not enough dissipativity. Dissipativity can be increased by replacing
\Delta_{\alpha}
by a non autonomous differential operator of the type
e^{t}\Delta_{\alpha},
which transforms (
6.4
) into

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{\infty}\exp\{\rho_{1}B^{H}_{1}(r)+\rho_{2}B^{H}_{2}(r)\}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\exp\{\mathit{N}_{i}r\}\mathcal{U}(r,s)f_{i}}}\right\|_{\infty}^{\beta_{i}}dr<1,\ i=1,2,

(6.11)

for some
s\geq 0,
where
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\mathcal{U}(t,s);\ 0\leq s\leq t}}\right\}
is the two parameter semigroup associated with this operator and is given by
\mathcal{U}(t,s)=S_{e^{t}-e^{s}}.
By using (
6.1
), a simple calculation shows that (
6.11
) can be satisfied trajectory wise for
\mathit{N}_{i}=0,i=1,2
,
H=1/2
and for a suitable choice of initial data
f_{i},i=1,2
as in (
6.10
). For such a choice, the solution exist globally due to Lemma
4.2
.

Lemma 6.1
.

If
\rho_{1}>0
and
\rho_{2}>0,
then the integral
\int_{\varepsilon}^{\infty}\exp\{\rho_{1}e^{t/2}X_{1}(t)+\rho_{2}e^{t/2}X_{2}(t)-b_{i}t\}dt,
where
b_{i}=\frac{d\beta_{i}}{\alpha}-1,i=1,2
diverges for any
\varepsilon>0.

Proof.

Suppose that
\int_{\epsilon}^{\infty}\exp\{\rho_{1}e^{t/2}X_{1}(t)+\rho_{2}e^{t/2}X_{2}(t)-b_{i}t\}dt<\infty.
This implies that
\rho_{1}e^{t/2}X_{1}(t)+\rho_{2}e^{t/2}X_{2}(t)\leq td\beta_{i}/\alpha
for all
t
sufficiently large. Since
\displaystyle e^{t/2}/t\rightarrow\infty
as
t\rightarrow\infty,
this would imply that
\rho_{1}X_{1}(t)+\rho_{2}X_{2}(t)\rightarrow 0
as
t\rightarrow\infty,
which is not possible. Therefore
\rho_{1}e^{t/2}X_{1}(t)+\rho_{2}e^{t/2}X_{2}(t)>td\beta_{i}/\alpha,
and hence
\rho_{1}e^{t/2}X_{1}(t)+\rho_{2}e^{t/2}X_{2}(t)-b_{i}t>t.
Therefore, the integral diverges.
∎

Theorem 7.1
.

If

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}e^{(1+\beta_{1})k_{21}-k_{11}B^{H}_{1}(r)+(1+\beta_{1})k_{22}-k_{12}B^{H}_{2}(r)+\lambda\beta_{1}r}r^{-d\beta_{1}/\alpha}dr\geq\frac{1}{\beta_{1}p(1,0)^{\beta_{1}}C_{1}^{\beta_{1}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\psi}}\right\|_{\infty}^{\beta_{1}}},

(7.1)

Proof.

For all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\ t\geq 0,
let us define the operators
\mathcal{J}_{1},\ \mathcal{J}_{2}
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{1}v(t,x)

\displaystyle=T(t)f_{1}(x)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\exp\{(1+\beta_{1})k_{21}-k_{11}B^{H}_{1}(r)+(1+\beta_{1})k_{22}-k_{12}B^{H}_{2}(r)\}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(T(t-r)v}}\right)^{1+\beta_{1}}dr,

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{2}v(t,x)

\displaystyle=T(t)f_{2}(x)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\exp\{(1+\beta_{2})k_{11}-k_{21}B^{H}_{1}(r)+(1+\beta_{2})k_{12}-k_{22}B^{H}_{2}(r)\}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(T(t-r)v}}\right)^{1+\beta_{2}}dr,

where

is any non-negative, bounded and measurable function. Moreover, on the set

we set

\displaystyle\mathscr{G}_{1}(t)

\displaystyle=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[1-\beta_{1}\int_{0}^{t}\exp\{(1+\beta_{1})k_{21}-k_{11}B^{H}_{1}(r)+(1+\beta_{1})k_{22}-k_{12}B^{H}_{2}(r)\}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|T(r)f_{1}}}\right\|_{\infty}^{\beta_{1}}dr}}\right]^{\frac{-1}{\beta_{1}}},

\displaystyle\mathscr{G}_{2}(t)

\displaystyle=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[1-\beta_{2}\int_{0}^{t}\exp\{(1+\beta_{2})k_{11}-k_{21}B^{H}_{1}(r)+(1+\beta_{2})k_{12}-k_{22}B^{H}_{2}(r)\}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|T(r)f_{2}}}\right\|_{\infty}^{\beta_{2}}dr}}\right]^{\frac{-1}{\beta_{2}}}.

, we get

\displaystyle v_{1}(t,x)=\mathcal{J}_{1}v_{2}(t,x),\ v_{2}(t,x)=\mathcal{J}_{2}v_{1}(t,x),\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\ \ 0\leq t<\tau_{*}.

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{1}v(t,x)\leq T(t)f_{1}(x)\mathscr{G}_{1}(t)\ \text{ and }\ \mathcal{J}_{2}u(t,x)\leq T(t)f_{2}(x)\mathscr{G}_{2}(t),

\displaystyle v_{1}(t,x)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{T(t)f_{1}(x)}{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[1-\beta_{1}p(1,0)^{\beta_{1}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|f_{1}}}\right\|_{\infty}^{\beta_{1}}\int_{0}^{t}e^{(1+\beta_{1})k_{21}-k_{11}B^{H}_{1}(r)+(1+\beta_{1})k_{22}-k_{12}B^{H}_{2}(r)+\lambda\beta_{1}r}r^{-d\beta_{1}/\alpha}dr}}\right]^{\frac{1}{\beta_{1}}}},

\displaystyle w_{i}(t)

for

Let

Then
w_{i}(t)\geq x_{i}(t),\ i=1,2,

\displaystyle\dot{x}_{i}(t)

\displaystyle=g_{i,j}(t)w_{j}^{1+\beta_{i}}(t)\geq g_{i,j}(t)x_{j}^{1+\beta_{i}}(t),

\displaystyle\dot{h}_{i}(t)

\displaystyle=g_{i,j}(t)h_{j}^{1+\beta_{i}}(t).

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\begin{aligned} \dot{x}_{i}(t)-g_{i,j}(t)x_{j}^{1+\beta_{i}}(t)&\geq\dot{h}_{i}(t)-g_{i,j}(t)h_{j}^{1+\beta_{i}}(t),\\
x_{i}(r_{0})=h_{i}(r_{0})&=r_{i}e^{-\gamma_{i}r_{0}}.\end{aligned}}}\right.

h_{i}(t)\leq x_{i}(t),

for

\displaystyle=\dot{h}_{i}(t)-\dot{x}_{i}(t)\leq g_{i,j}(t)\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[h_{j}^{1+\beta_{i}}(t)-x_{j}^{1+\beta_{i}}(t)}}\right]

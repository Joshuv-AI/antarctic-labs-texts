Analyzing dynamics and average case complexity in the spherical Sherrington-Kirkpatrick model: a focus on extreme eigenvectors
Tingzhou Yu

Abstract
We explore Langevin dynamics in the spherical Sherrington-Kirkpatrick model, delving into the asymptotic energy limit. Our approach involves integro-differential equations, incorporating the Crisanti-Horner-Sommers-Cugliandolo-Kurchan equation from spin glass literature, to analyze the system’s size and its temperature-dependent phase transition. Additionally, we conduct an average case complexity analysis, establishing hitting time bounds for the bottom eigenvector of a Wigner matrix. Our investigation also includes the power iteration algorithm, examining its average case complexity in identifying the top eigenvector overlap, with comprehensive complexity bounds.

The study of aging and its effect on spin glass dynamics is an active area of physics research, with important implications for the understanding of the low-temperature behavior of these systems.

A key focus was the study of Langevin dynamics within the Sherrington-Kirkpatrick (SK) model, utilizing correlation functions. As a potential application, the insights gained from our analysis of the asymptotic limit of the energy can be utilized to develop more efficient algorithms for solving challenging linear algebra problems, such as computing the eigenvectors of Wigner random matrices. 2
Asymptotic energy dynamics in the spherical SK model

In this section, we explore the asymptotic energy dynamics of the spherical Sherrington-Kirkpatrick model. Our focus is on the Langevin dynamics, through which we analyze the system’s behavior over time. We will present our main results, including the derivation of integro-differential equations and the examination of long-term energy behavior and phase transitions in the model.

2.1
Main results

, there exists constants
C_{1},C_{2},C_{3}>0
so that

(4.4)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left({C_{1}N^{2/3}<T_{\varepsilon}<C_{2}N^{2/3}\log(C_{3}N)}\right)>1-\delta.

To prove our main theorem, we require the following lemma results.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\mathbf{J}
be the
N\times N
normalized Wigner matrix. Assume that
\mathbf{J}
obeys the same condition as in (
3.4
). Ordering its eigenvalues
|\sigma_{N}|\leq|\sigma_{N-1}|\leq\dots\leq|\sigma_{1}|
. Then we have
|\sigma_{1}|/|\sigma_{2}|=1+\mathcal{O}(N^{-2/3})

Proof.

By Theorem
3.1
, we assume that
|\sigma_{1}|=2+\gamma_{1}
and
|\sigma_{2}|=2+\gamma_{2}
with
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}=\mathcal{O}(N^{-2/3})
. Then by Taylor expansion, we get

\displaystyle\frac{|\sigma_{1}|}{|\sigma_{2}|}

\displaystyle=\frac{2+\gamma_{1}}{2}(1+\frac{\gamma_{2}}{2})^{-1}=\frac{2+\gamma_{1}}{2}\left({1-\frac{\gamma_{2}}{2}+\mathcal{O}(\gamma_{2}^{2})}\right)

\displaystyle=1+\frac{1}{2}(\gamma_{1}-\gamma_{2})+\mathcal{O}(N^{-4/3})

\displaystyle=1+\mathcal{O}(N^{-2/3}).

∎

The proof of
4.1
.

We first show that the lower bound of
T_{\varepsilon}
. Fix
\delta>0
. We will find appropriate
1\leq l<N
depending on
\varepsilon
and
\delta
(independent of
N
) to lower bound the denominator of
q_{k}\cdot u_{1}
by the first
l
terms as follows.

By (
4.1
) and (
4.3
), we have

(4.5)

\left\lvert{q_{k}\cdot u_{1}}\right\rvert=\frac{\left\lvert{\sigma_{1}^{k}\alpha_{1}}\right\rvert}{\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\alpha_{i}^{2}\sigma_{i}^{2k}}}\leq\frac{\left\lvert{\sigma_{1}^{k}\alpha_{1}}\right\rvert}{\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{l}\alpha_{i}^{2}\sigma_{i}^{2k}}}=\left({1+\sum_{i=2}^{l}\frac{\alpha_{i}^{2}}{\alpha_{1}^{2}}\frac{\sigma_{i}^{2k}}{\sigma_{1}^{2k}}}\right)^{-1/2}

By the fact that
|\sigma_{i}|\geq|\sigma_{l}|
for
i=1,\dots,l
, we obtain

(4.6)

\left\lvert{q_{k}\cdot u_{1}}\right\rvert\leq\left({1+\frac{\sigma_{l}^{2k}}{\sigma_{1}^{2k}}\sum_{i=2}^{l}\frac{\alpha_{i}^{2}}{\alpha_{1}^{2}}}\right)^{-1/2}

For
k\geq T_{\varepsilon}
, we have
\left\lvert{q_{k}\cdot u_{1}}\right\rvert\geq\varepsilon
. Thus, we get

(4.7)

T_{\varepsilon}\geq\frac{1}{2}\log^{-1}\left({\frac{|\sigma_{1}|}{|\sigma_{l}|}}\right)\log\left({\frac{\alpha_{2}^{2}+\dots+\alpha_{l}^{2}}{\alpha_{1}^{2}(\varepsilon^{-2}-1)}}\right).

Then we apply
lemma

3.6
and choose
C=2\varepsilon^{-2}-1
:

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{\alpha_{2}^{2}+\dots+\alpha_{l}^{2}}{\alpha_{1}^{2}(\epsilon^{-2}-1)}>2\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{\alpha_{1}^{2}+\alpha_{2}^{2}+\dots+\alpha_{l}^{2}}{\alpha_{1}^{2}}>\frac{2}{\epsilon^{2}}-1\right)

\displaystyle\geq 1-\delta/2,

where we take

4.2

3.1

for

(4.9)

Next, we prove the upper bound of

.

By inequality that
|\sigma_{i}|\leq|\sigma_{2}|
for

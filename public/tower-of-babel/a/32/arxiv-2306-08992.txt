On a kinematic proof of Andoyer variables canonicity
Anatoly Neishtadt

Abstract
We present a kinematic proof that the Andoyer variables in rigid body dynamics are canonical. This proof is based on the approach of “virtual rotations” by H. Andoyer. The difference from the original proof by Andoyer is that we do not assume that the fixed in body frame is the frame of principal moments of inertia, and do not use explicit formulas for the kinetic energy of the body that include moments of inertia.

3
Canonicity condition

Let
q_{1},q_{2},q_{3}
be any generalised coordinates that characterise position of the frame
Oxyz
with respect to the absolute frame
OXYZ
(e.g.,
q_{1},q_{2},q_{3}
could be the Euler angles). The kinetic energy of the body
T
is a function of these coordinates and their velocities:

T=T(q_{1},q_{2},q_{3},\dot{q}_{1},\dot{q}_{2},\dot{q}_{3})
. Denote
p_{1},p_{2},p_{3}
the momenta canonically conjugate to these coordinates,
p_{j}=\partial T/\partial\dot{q}_{j},\ j=1,2,3
. Then
q_{1},q_{2},q_{3},p_{1},p_{2},p_{3}
is a system of canonical variables for the considered problem. Express the Andoyer variables via
q_{1},q_{2},q_{3},p_{1},p_{2},p_{3}
. To prove that this transformation is canonical we should check that (e.g.,
[
3
]
, p. 241)

p_{1}dq_{1}+p_{2}dq_{2}+p_{3}dq_{3}=Ldl+Gdg+\Theta d\vartheta.

(3.1)

This would prove that the Andoyer variables are canonical.

As it is traditional in Analytical Dynamics, consider the rigid body as a system of
N
material points. Let
m_{i},\vec{r}_{i},\ i=1,2,\ldots,N
be masses and position vectors in the absolute frame of these points. Express position vectors via coordinates
q_{1},q_{2},q_{3}
:
\overline{r}_{i}=\overline{r}_{i}(q_{1},q_{2},q_{3})
. Then

\displaystyle d{\overline{r}_{i}}=\sum_{j=1}^{3}\frac{\partial{\overline{r}_{i}}}{\partial q_{j}}dq_{j},

(3.2)

\displaystyle\dot{\overline{r}_{i}}=\sum_{j=1}^{3}\frac{\partial{\overline{r}_{i}}}{\partial q_{j}}\dot{q}_{j}.

and

\frac{\partial\dot{\overline{r}_{i}}}{\partial\dot{q}_{j}}=\frac{\partial{\overline{r}_{i}}}{\partial q_{j}}

(this is a standard relation used in derivation of the Lagrange equations from the D’Alembert principle, e.g.,
[

The angular momentum
\vec{G}
and the kinetic energy of the body
T

\vec{G}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}m_{i}(\vec{r}_{i}\times\dot{\overline{r}_{i}}),\ T=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}m_{i}(\dot{\overline{r}_{i}}\cdot\dot{\overline{r}_{i}}).

Consider identities

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}m_{i}\dot{\overline{r}_{i}}\cdot d{\overline{r}_{i}}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}m_{i}\dot{\overline{r}_{i}}\cdot\sum_{j=1}^{3}\frac{\partial{\overline{r}_{i}}}{\partial q_{j}}dq_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}m_{i}\dot{\overline{r}_{i}}\cdot\sum_{j=1}^{3}\frac{\partial\dot{\overline{r}_{i}}}{\partial\dot{q}_{j}}dq_{j}

Conlusion

A kinematic proof is given that the Andoyer variables in rigid body dynamics are canonical. The proof is based on the
approach of “virtual rotations” by Andoyer. The difference from the original proof by Andoyer is that we do not assume that the fixed in body frame is the frame of principal moments of inertia, and do not use explicit formulas for the kinetic energy of the body that include moments of inertia.

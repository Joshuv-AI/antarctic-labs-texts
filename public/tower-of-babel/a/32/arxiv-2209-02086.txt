Abstract
We study lepton flavor violating (LFV) B B decays in a general two Higgs doublet model with sub-TeV exotic scalars. Two different parameter spaces are explored: one dominated by extra top Yukawa coupling ρ t ​ t \rho_{tt} , the other by LFV couplings relevant for the muon g − 2 g-2 anomaly. In the first case, flavor constraints such as ℓ → ℓ ′ ​ γ \ell\to\ell^{\prime}\gamma , h → ℓ ​ ℓ ′ h\to\ell\ell^{\prime} imply LFV B B decays are far below experimental sensitivities. The second case needs to be close to the alignment limit, but B q → τ ​ μ B_{q}\to\tau\mu and B → ( K , π ) ​ τ ​ μ B\to(K,\pi)\tau\mu rates can lie within the sensitivities of Belle II and LHCb Upgrade II. Neutral B B meson mixings and B , K , π → ℓ ​ ν B,\,K,\,\pi\to\ell\nu decays provide important flavor constraints on parameter space. B B decays involving e e – τ \tau violation are constrained by μ → e \mu\to e processes.



I
Introduction

Flavor changing neutral couplings (FCNCs) in the
Standard Model (SM) occur only beyond tree-level and the
corresponding rates are small due to suppression from GIM
mechanism
Glashow:1970gm
and vanishing neutrino masses.
But new physics (NP) beyond SM could have interactions that
allow for sizable FCNC processes. Therefore, the precise
measurements of rare FCNC decays serve as powerful probes
of physics beyond SM. In this context, rare
B
decays offer excellent opportunities as,
in addition to loop factors,
these are suppressed further by small CKM factors,
while the
b
quark mass is sufficiently large so long-
and short-distance effects can be separated and extracted
with reasonable precision.

Rare leptonic decays of
B
mesons are advantageous
for study, as all hadronic effects are contained in
the decay constant, calculable by lattice
QCD
Aoki:2021kgd
, hence the decay rates can be
predicted with great precision. On the experimental
front, there has been excellent progress with
ever increasing precision. One example is the
helicity-suppressed rare
B_{s}\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
decay, which provides
one of the most sensitive probes of scalar NP interactions.

Based on full Run 1 and Run 2 data with 9
{\rm fb}^{-1}

luminosity, LHCb
LHCb:2021vsc
;
LHCb:2021awg

reported the branching ratio,

\displaystyle{\cal B}(B_{s}\to\mu\mu)=\left(3.09^{+0.46\,+0.15}_{-0.43\,-0.11}\right)\times 10^{-9},\quad[{\rm LHCb}]

(1)

Subsequently, CMS
CMS:2022mgd
announced
their Run 2 result,

\displaystyle{\cal B}(B_{s}\to\mu\mu)=\left(3.83^{+0.38+0.19+0.14}_{-0.36-0.16-0.13}\right)\times 10^{-9},\,[{\rm CMS}]

(2)

based on 2016-2018 data with 140
{\rm fb}^{-1}

integrated luminosity.
The central values of LHCb and CMS results differ by

1.2\sigma
, and lie on opposite sides of SM expectation,

{\cal B}(B_{s}\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-})_{\rm SM}=(3.66\pm 0.14)\times 10^{-9}

Bobeth:2013uxa
;
Beneke:2019slt

both measurements agree with SM, thereby provide strong

In this article, we explore the possible size of LFV
B

decays associated with
b\to q\ell\ell^{\prime}\,(q=s,d)
in one of the simplest extensions of SM, the
general two Higgs doublet model (g2HDM)
Lee:1973iz

(for a review of 2HDMs, see
Branco:2011iw
),
sometimes denoted as 2HDM Type III
Hou:1991un
,
where the Lagrangian itself contains flavor changing neutral
couplings of exotic scalar bosons, denoted as
\rho_{ij}

(which are defined
in Eq. (
3
) later). In our
study, we investigate two very different parameter space
choices motivated by different phenomenological reasons.
In the first case, the NP Yukawa matrices are somewhat SM-like
in strength; the largest coupling, just as in SM, is the
top-related diagonal coupling
\rho_{tt}\lesssim\lambda_{t}
, where
\lambda_{t}=\sqrt{2}m_{t}/v
is the SM top Yukawa coupling. Another assumption,
supported by experiment
ParticleDataGroup:2020ssz
, is of
small but finite mixing, denoted as
c_{\gamma}\,(\equiv\cos\gamma
),
between
CP
even scalars in the model
(alignment). It was shown
Hou:2020itz
that
lepton-related NP couplings
\rho_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}
are constrained
to be small, due to bounds from
h\to\ell\ell^{\prime}

decay and
\mu\to e
,
\tau\to\mu
LFV processes. Driven
by
\rho_{tt}
(or
\rho_{tc}
), this scenario can realize
electroweak baryogenesis (EWBG) for explaining the baryon
asymmetry of the Universe(BAU)
Fuyuto:2017ewj
;
Fuyuto:2019svr
,
providing strong motivation for
experimental exploration.

In the second case, we adopt the alignment limit of
c_{\gamma}\to 0
, which then allows for sizable NP lepton Yukawa
couplings related to
\mu
–
\tau
sector. This scenario
is usually invoked for NP explanation of the anomalous
magnetic moment of muon,
(g-2)_{\mu}
, recently affirmed
by Muon g-2 collaboration
Muong-2:2021ojo
. In this
scenario, as opposed to the first case, in order to satisfy
bounds from LHC direct search for
gg\to\phi\to\tau\mu

and the flavor bound of
\tau\to\mu\gamma

Hou:2021sfl
,
the
\rho_{tt}
coupling cannot be substantial.
After evaluating constraints from neutral
B_{q}
mixing
and
H^{+}
-induced leptonic decays of
B,K,\pi
, we
identify parameter space that can lead to significantly
large rates of LFV
B
decays, which are within reach of
upcoming measurements. Though we mostly focus on
flavor violation in the
\mu
–
\tau
sector, we will
present g2HDM expectations for flavor violation
in
\mu
–
e
and
\tau
–
e
sectors as well.

II
New Yukawa interactions

Adding a second Higgs doublet to the SM gives four
new Higgs bosons, the neutral scalars
H
,
A
,
and charged Higgs boson
H^{\pm}
in mass basis. In the
limit of
CP
conserving scalar potential, the
H\,(A)

boson is
CP
-even (odd). Due to absence of discrete
Z_{2}

symmetry on Yukawa sector, it is not possible to diagonalize
simultaneously the Yukawa matrices associated with the two
Higgs doublets. As a result, the Yukawa Lagrangian of g2HDM
contains Higgs FCNCs, giving rise to flavor violation at
tree-level. Working in the so-called Higgs basis
Georgi:1978ri
;
Lavoura:1994fv
;
Botella:1994cs
,
the Yukawa Lagrangian in g2HDM is given by
Davidson:2005cw
;
Mahmoudi:2009zx
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=-

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sum_{f=u,d,\ell}\bar{f}_{i}\Big[\big(\lambda^{f}_{i}\delta_{ij}s_{\gamma}+\rho^{f}_{ij}c_{\gamma}\big)h

\displaystyle+\big(\lambda^{f}_{i}\delta_{ij}c_{\gamma}-\rho^{f}_{ij}s_{\gamma}\big)H-i\,{\rm sgn}(Q_{f})\rho^{f}_{ij}A\Big]R\,f_{j}

\displaystyle-\bar{u}_{i}\left[(V\rho^{d})_{ij}R-(\rho^{u\dagger}V)_{ij}L\right]d_{j}H^{+}

\displaystyle-\bar{\nu}_{i}\rho^{\ell}_{ij}R\,\ell_{j}H^{+}+\text{H.c.},

(3)

where indices
i,j
denote the generation of fermion
f
,

Q_{f}
the corresponding electric charge, and
R(L)=(1\pm\gamma_{5})/2
are chiral projections. Note that NP
Yukawa matrices
\rho^{f}
are in general not Hermitian, hence
elements
\rho_{ij}
can have arbitrary complex phases.

The presence of tree-level Higgs FCNCs lead to potentially
dangerous flavor violating decays of SM Higgs boson,

h\to f_{i}f_{j}\,(i\neq j)
, which are severely bound
by experiments. To evade such constraints, usually some

Z_{2}
symmetry is imposed on NP Yukawa sector
to enforce the natural flavor conservation
condition
Glashow:1976nt
. However, the vertex

hf_{i}f_{j}
in g2HDM is proportional to the mixing angle

c_{\gamma}
, therefore with suppression due to sufficiently
small
c_{\gamma}
, as hinted by current Higgs
data
ParticleDataGroup:2020ssz
, the mere existence
of Higgs FCNCs in g2HDM is not directly a cause of
concern. But, of course, the strength of Higgs
FCNC couplings will be determined by data.

The scalar potential can be found, e.g., in
Ref.
Hou:2017hiw
. For our study, besides
Eq. (
3
), we only need physical
H,A,

and
H^{+}
masses as benchmarks. We focus on
sub-TeV masses in range of
[300,\,500]
GeV and
take
m_{A}=m_{H^{+}}
, usually adopted

III
Case I: top Yukawa dominance

We assume that NP top coupling
\rho_{tt}\sim\lambda_{t}
is the
largest coupling. This assumption finds support also from
the Cheng-Sher ansatz
Cheng:1987rs
,

\rho_{ij}\propto\sqrt{m_{i}m_{j}}/v
, frequently employed
to control tree-level Higgs FCNC. But as mentioned in the
preceding section, small
c_{\gamma}
can tackle the issue
of Higgs FCNC, so we do not quite follow the ansatz.
We take
c_{\gamma}\sim 0.1
as sample value, but note that
due to
\rho_{tt}
being the dominant quark coupling, the
main g2HDM contribution to
b\to q\ell\ell^{(\prime)}

processes are induced by
H^{+}
interactions that do
not depend on
c_{\gamma}
. The
c_{\gamma}
value is relevant for
constraints from
h\to\ell\ell^{\prime}
.

The leading flavor constraints on
\rho_{tt}
are from
B physics discussed later in detail.
For LFV
B
decays, we also need to determine the strength of
lepton couplings
\rho_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}
.
We have discussed previously
Hou:2020itz

the allowed strength of
\rho^{\ell}
for large
\rho_{tt}
,
so let us give a brief summary. For finite
c_{\gamma}
,

h\to\ell\ell^{\prime}
provide important constraints
on
\rho_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}
, independent of
\rho_{tt}
. For
example, the current upper limit on
h\to\tau\mu

from CMS, based on full Run 2 data
CMS:2021rsq

\displaystyle{\cal B}(h\to\tau\mu)<0.15\%\quad{\rm(95\%\;C.L.)}

(4)

implies
|\rho_{\tau\mu}c_{\gamma}|<0.1\lambda_{\tau}
for
\rho_{\tau\mu}=\rho_{\mu\tau}
,
giving
\rho_{\tau\mu}\sim\lambda_{\tau}
for
c_{\gamma}=0.1
(see
appendix
A.2
for expressions in g2HDM).
Even if
c_{\gamma}\sim 0
so the

bound of
Eq. (
4

\mu
–
\tau

\rho_{tt}
unavoidably generate

\tau\to\mu\gamma

diagrams

\tau\to\mu\gamma<4.2\times 10^{-8}

from Belle again gives
\rho_{\tau\mu}\sim{\cal O}(\lambda_{\tau})
for

\rho_{tt}\sim\lambda_{t}
and
m_{H,A}\sim 300
GeV.

The current upper limits on the
\tau
–
e
sector, e.g.

h\to\tau e

\tau\to e\gamma

\mu\to e\gamma

, strengths of
\mu

\lambda_{e}
.
Concerning flavor conserving

V
Summary

We have explored prospects of enhanced lepton flavor
violation in
B
decays in g2HDM with sub-TeV exotic scalars.
We focus on two different cases of parameter space.
For Case I, we assume the top Yukawa coupling
\rho_{tt}
is
the dominant quark coupling and take
c_{\gamma}\sim 0.1
.
Then charged LFV processes
\tau\to\mu\gamma
and

\mu\to e\gamma
constrain
\rho_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}

to Eq. (5). Even with
{\cal O}(1)
strength of
\rho_{tt}
,
LFV
B
decay rates are highly suppressed by small

\rho_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}
, and are far from the
sensitivities of upcoming LHCb Upgrade II and Belle II.
For Case II where
\mu
–
\tau
flavor violating lepton
couplings are motivated to be about 20-30 times larger
than SM Yukawa
\lambda_{\tau}\simeq 0.01
. Contrary to Case I,
one finds that
\rho_{tt}
has to be small due to bounds from

\tau\to\mu\gamma
and
gg\to H,A\to\tau\mu
direct
search at LHC. However, together with
\rho_{bs},\,\rho_{bd}

as small as
{\cal O}(10^{-3})
, we find that Case II allows
substantial rates of
\mu
–
\tau
flavor violation in
B decays, with B mixing and
B\to\mu\nu
being the
leading, but forgiving, flavor constraints.

Concerning
\tau
–
e
flavor violation in
B
decays,
we find that
\mu\to e\gamma
would make it difficult
to have simultaneously large
\rho_{\tau e}
, making

B_{q}\to\tau e
and
B\to(K,\pi)\tau e
rates too
small to be probed at upcoming experiments. Furthermore,
we find that current constraints on
\mu
–
e
flavor
violating coupling are not that severe. The future
measurements of LFV B decays related to
b\to q\mu e

will provide crucial constraint on g2HDM.

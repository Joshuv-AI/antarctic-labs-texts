Abstract
. We study the product P m P_{m} of m m real Ginibre matrices with Gaussian elements of size N N , which has received renewed interest recently. Its eigenvalues, which are either real or come in complex conjugate pairs, become all real with probability one when m → ∞ m\to\infty at fixed N N . In this regime the statistics becomes deterministic and the Lyapunov spectrum has been derived long ago. On the other hand, when N → ∞ N\to\infty and m m is fixed, it can be expected that away from the origin the same local statistics as for a single real Ginibre ensemble at m = 1 m=1 prevails. Inspired by analogous findings for products of complex Ginibre matrices, we introduce a critical scaling regime when the two parameters are proportional, m = α ​ N m=\alpha N . We derive the expected number, variance and rescaled density of real eigenvalues in this critical regime. This allows us to interpolate between previous recent results in the above mentioned limits when α → ∞ \alpha\to\infty and α → 0 \alpha\to 0 , respectively.



1.
Introduction and discussion of main results

The study of products of random matrices has been proposed many decades ago by Bellman
[
5
]
and by Furstenberg and Kesten
[
15
]
. The motivation was to understand properties of the Lyapunov exponents
[
31
,
22
]
in this toy model for chaotic dynamical systems, where usually the matrix dimension
N
is kept fixed and the number of factors
m
tends to infinity, see
[
38
]
for a recent account. The factors are typically taken to be real non-symmetric or complex non-Hermitian matrices with Gaussian distribution of elements, from the real or complex Ginibre ensemble
[
17
]
. While Ginibre himself showed the integrability of a single complex ensemble as a determinantal point process, it has taken many joint efforts to put the real Ginibre ensemble on the same footing as a Pfaffian point process, and we refer to
[
24
]
and references therein for details.

More recently it was shown that also products of such random matrices represent determinantal respectively Pfaffian point processes at finite
N
and
m
, and we refer to
[
4
]
for a review. Naturally, multiplying real Ginibre matrices has been the most challenging in that respect, cf.
[
21
,
12
]
for results. What is special about multiplying real matrices is that its eigenvalues are either real, or come in complex conjugated pairs. It was observed numerically early on by Sommers and coworkers
[
36
]
for a single matrix
m=1
, that the fraction of real eigenvalues is
O(\sqrt{N})
. This fact was proven later by Edelman, Kostlan and Shub
[
7
]
. Much more recently Lakshminarayan
[
25
]
studied real products numerically in the context of entanglement of quantum systems and found, that for fixed
N
all eigenvalues become real when
m\to\infty
with probability one. This was proven by Forrester
[
11
]
and this
property holds beyond multiplying Gaussian Ginibre ensembles
[
19
,
34
,
33
]
.
Both the expected number of real eigenvalues and its variance were determined very recently by Simm and his coworker
[
35
,
9
]
, as we will recall below, including the opposite limit with
N\to\infty
at
m
fixed. Similar considerations have been made very recently for products of truncated orthogonal matrices
[
26
]
.

In the limit
N\to\infty
at fixed
m
, it was shown for products of complex Ginibre matrices that the singular values
[
28
]
and complex eigenvalues
[
29
]
of the product matrix have the same eigenvalue statistics as a single matrix at
m=1
, apart from the origin where the mean density diverges and new universality classes emerge, cf.
[
4
]
. It was therefore natural to ask about the existence of a critical regime, that interpolates between such a random matrix behaviour when
N\to\infty
at
m
fixed, and the deterministic Lyapunov spectrum when
m\to\infty
at
N
fixed
[
31
,
22
,
10
,
23
]
. An interpolating kernel was indeed identified
[
2
,
3
,
27
]
that describes the local statistics in a critical regime, when the two parameters are proportional,
m=\alpha N
with
\alpha>0
fixed, as conjectured in

[
1
]
.

In this article we will introduce such a critical regime for the product of real Ginibre matrices, with the goal to describe the expected number of real eigenvalues, its variance and density which are all global quantities. Here, we will rely on previous results at finite
N
and
m

[
12
]
, where the Pfaffian structure of the product of real Ginibre matrices was derived. Our results will allow us to interpolate between previous findings as a function of
\alpha

1.1.
Expected number of real eigenvalues

Let us first recall the known asymptotic behaviour of

•

Fixed

For each fixed

E_{N}(m)=N+o(1),\qquad\textup{as }m\to\infty.

Fixed

For each fixed

The special case

For the product of complex Ginibre matrices such an extension from the case of fixed

\mbox{erf}(x)=\frac{2x}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{1}\exp(-x^{2}s^{2})\,ds

Fixed

\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{V_{N}(m)}{E_{N}(m)}=0.

Fixed

1.3.
Densities of real eigenvalues

We denote by
\mathbf{R}_{N,1}^{m}
the
1
-point function of real eigenvalues
[
12
]
, see Section
2

Let us write

(1.21)

\rho_{N}^{m}(x):=\frac{1}{E_{N}(m)}\mathbf{R}_{N,1}^{m}(x)

for the normalised density of real eigenvalues of
P_{m}
in (
1.1
).

We also consider the rescaling of its argument
x

(1.22)

\lambda=\begin{cases}x^{\frac{1}{m}}&\textup{if }x>0,\\
-|x|^{\frac{1}{m}}&\textup{if }x<0.\end{cases}

Such a rescaling is well known in the study of Lyapunov
[
31
,
23
,
10
]
and stability exponents
[
33
,
1
,
20
]
.
By the change of variable (
1.22
), the associated density
\widetilde{\rho}_{N}^{m}(\lambda)
is given by

(1.23)

\widetilde{\rho}_{N}^{m}(\lambda)=m\,\lambda^{m-1}\,\rho_{N}^{m}(\lambda^{m}),

where
\rho_{N}^{m}
is given by (
1.21
).
Here and in the sequel, we shall use the tilde notation for the quantities associated with the scaled variable
\lambda
. Let us summarise again what can be said about the two densities in the limits with
N
respectively

fixed.

•

Fixed
N
and
m\to\infty
:

For each fixed
N
, one can expect that

(1.24)

\lim_{m\to\infty}\rho_{N}^{m}(x)=\delta(x)

in the sense of distribution, see the remark below. On the other hand, it follows from Newman’s celebrated work
[
31
]
, and the equivalence between Lyapunov and stability exponents (see e.g.
[
33
]
) that

(1.25)

\lim_{m\to\infty}\widetilde{\rho}_{N}^{m}(\lambda)=\mathbbm{1}_{(-1,1)}(\lambda)\cdot|\lambda|.

The density in (
1.25
) is known as Newman’s triangular law.

•

Fixed
m
and
N\to\infty
:

It was shown in
[
35
, Thm. 1.2]
that for a fixed
m
, the weak convergence

(1.26)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\rho_{N}^{m}(x)=\rho^{m}(x):=\mathbbm{1}_{(-1,1)}(x)\cdot\frac{1}{2m}|x|^{\frac{1}{m}-1}

holds, see also a recent work
[
9
]
.
The convergence (
1.26
) was previously conjectured in
[
12
]
.
Equivalently, by (
1.23
) and (
1.26
), for any fixed

,

(1.27)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\widetilde{\rho}_{N}^{m}(\lambda)=\widetilde{\rho}^{m}(\lambda):=\mathbbm{1}_{(-1,1)}(\lambda)\cdot\frac{1}{2}.

Note that the limiting density

in (

Remark
.

The degenerate limit to the Dirac delta in Theorem
1.4
(i) and in (
1.24
) can be expected from (
1.26
).
To be more precise, notice that for each

\int_{-1}^{1}x^{k}\,\rho^{m}(x)\,dx=\frac{1+(-1)^{k}}{2(1+km)}\to\begin{cases}1&k=0,\\

This gives that for a test function

(1.32)

\int_{-1}^{1}f(x)\,\rho^{m}(x)\,dx\to f(0),\qquad(m\to\infty).

Note that by (

) and (

(1.33)

\widetilde{\rho}_{\alpha}(\lambda)\sim\begin{cases}1/2&\text{as}\quad\alpha\to 0\,,\\

, respectively.

The Meijer
G

(2.1)

G^{m,n}_{p,q}\left(\begin{matrix}a_{1},\dots,a_{p}\\
b_{1},\dots,b_{q}\end{matrix}\;\middle|\;\begin{matrix}\end{matrix}\;\middle|\;z\right):=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{L}z^{s}\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{m}\Gamma(b_{j}-s)\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Gamma(1-a_{j}+s)}{\prod_{j=1+m}^{q}\Gamma(1-b_{j}+s)\prod_{j=1+n}^{p}\Gamma(a_{j}-s)}\,ds,

where the integration contour
L
in
\mathbb{C}
depends on the poles of the gamma functions. To be more precise,
L
is properly chosen so that it separates the poles of the factors
\Gamma(b_{j}-s)
from those of the factors
\Gamma(1-a_{j}+s)
, see e.g.
[
32
, Chapter 16]
.

Let us write

(2.2)

\omega(x):=G^{m,0}_{0,m}\left(\begin{matrix}-\\
0,\dots,0\end{matrix}\;\middle|\;\frac{x^{2}}{2^{m}}\right)

for the weight function of real eigenvalues.
The eigenvalues of
P_{m}
form a Pfaffian point processe with the weight

following

]

It was shown in

where

(2.4)

S_{N}(x,y)=\sum_{j=0}^{N-2}\frac{\omega(x)\,x^{j}}{(2\sqrt{2\pi}j!)^{m}}(xA_{j}(y)-A_{j+1}(y))

(2.5)

D_{N}(x,y):=-\frac{\partial}{\partial y}S_{N}(x,y),\qquad\tilde{I}_{N}(x,y):=\int_{x}^{y}S_{N}(t,y)\,dt+\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{sgn}(x-y).

Furthermore, it holds that
[

]

(2.6)

\begin{split}A_{j}(y)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\omega(v)\operatorname{sgn}(y-v)v^{j}\,dv=\begin{cases}-2^{\frac{m(j+1)}{2}}G^{m+1,0}_{1,m+1}\left(\begin{matrix}1\\
0,\frac{j+1}{2},\dots,\frac{j+1}{2}\end{matrix}\;\middle|\;\dfrac{y^{2}}{2^{m}}\right)&j\textup{ odd,}\\
y^{j+1}G^{m,1}_{1,m+1}\left(\begin{matrix}-\frac{j-1}{2}\\
0,\dots,0,-\frac{j+1}{2}\end{matrix}\;\middle|\;\dfrac{y^{2}}{2^{m}}\right)&j\textup{ even.}\end{cases}\end{split}

Following
[
11
]
, let us write

(2.7)

a_{j,k}:=G^{m+1,m}_{m+1,m+1}\left(\begin{matrix}\frac{3}{2}-j,\dots,\frac{3}{2}-j,1\\
0,k,\dots,k\end{matrix}\;\middle|\;\begin{matrix}\end{matrix}\;\middle|\;1\right).

It is also convenient to introduce

(2.8)

b_{j,k}:=\frac{a_{j,k}}{(\Gamma(j-\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(k))^{m}}.

The
k
-point correlation functions of real eigenvalues
\mathbf{R}_{N,k}^{m}
can thus be written as

(2.9)

\mathbf{R}_{N,k}^{m}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}):=\mbox{Pf}[K_{N}(x_{j},x_{l})]_{j,l=1}^{k}.

In particular we obtain for the 1- and 2-point function needed later

(2.10)

\displaystyle\mathbf{R}_{N,1}^{m}(x)

\displaystyle=S_{N}(x,x),

(2.11)

\displaystyle\mathbf{R}_{N,2}^{m}(x,y)

\displaystyle=S_{N}(x,x)S_{N}(y,y)-D_{N}(x,y)\tilde{I}_{N}(x,y)+S_{N}(x,y)S_{N}(y,x).

Notice that these
k
-point functions are not normalised, in particular because the number of real eigenvalues is a random variable itself. Furthermore, we observe that due to the symmetry of

(
2.6
) together with (

), the 1-point function is an even function in

.

Next, we collect moments of the weight function
\omega
in (

) based on the building blocks (

).
The following property was shown in

,

.

Lemma 2.1
.

The weight function
\omega
in (

) together with the functions

in (

) satisfy

(2.12)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\omega(x)\,\omega(y)\,x^{j-1}\,y^{k-1}\operatorname{sgn}(y-x)\,dx\,dy=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\omega(x)\,x^{j-1}\,A_{k-1}(x)\,dx=\alpha_{j,k}.

Due to the symmetry of the weight function the coefficients have the following properties

(2.13)

\alpha_{j,k}=-\alpha_{k,j},\quad\mbox{and}\quad\alpha_{j,k}=0\quad\mbox{if}\ \ j+k\ \ \mbox{even}.

They can be expressed as

(2.14)

\alpha_{2j-1,2k}=2^{(j+k-\frac{1}{2})m}a_{j,k},

where
a_{j,k}
is given by (

).

Let us recall the following lemma shown in
[
11
, Proposition 6]
.
By the expression (

) of
E_{N}
below, this immediately gives (

).

Lemma 2.2
.

(Cf.
[
11
, Proposition 6]
)

For each fixed
j,k
, we have

(2.15)

\lim_{m\to\infty}b_{j,k}=\begin{cases}1&j\leq k,\\
0&j>k.\end{cases}

We emphasise that Lemma

will not be used further in our proofs below.
Nevertheless, it is instructive to compare this lemma with the following proposition.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
j=tN/2
with
t\in(0,1)
and
m=\alpha N
. Let
l=k-j
be fixed.
Then as
N\to\infty
, we have

(2.16)

b_{j,k}=\frac{1}{2}\Big[1+\operatorname{erf}\Big((2l+1)\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{8t}}\Big)\Big]\cdot(1+o(1)).

Notice that for
\alpha\gg 1
, the asymptotic behaviour of (
2.16
) recovers (
2.15
).

Proof of Proposition
2.3
.

By (
2.1
) and (
2.7
), for
j\geq 1
, we have

(2.17)

a_{j,k}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{C}(\Gamma(j-\tfrac{1}{2}+s)\Gamma(k-s))^{m}\,\frac{\Gamma(-s)}{\Gamma(1-s)}\,ds=-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{C}\frac{(\Gamma(j-\frac{1}{2}+s)\Gamma(k-s))^{m}}{s}\,ds,

where
C
can be taken to be a contour starting at
-i\infty
, passing through the real axis within the interval
(\frac{1}{2}-j,0)
, and finishing at
i\infty
.
Then by (
2.8
), we obtain the contour integral representation

(2.18)

b_{j,k}=-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{C}\Big(\frac{\Gamma(j-\frac{1}{2}+s)}{\Gamma(j-\frac{1}{2})}\frac{\Gamma(k-s)}{\Gamma(k)}\Big)^{m}\,\frac{ds}{s}.

Recall here that the gamma function satisfies the asymptotic behaviour

(2.19)

\frac{\Gamma(z+a)}{\Gamma(z+b)}=z^{a-b}\Big(1+\frac{(a-b)(a+b-1)}{2z}+O(z^{-2})\Big),\qquad(z\to\infty),

see e.g.
[
32
, Eq.(5.11.13)]
.
Using this, for
j=tN/2
with
t\in(0,1)
and fixed
l=k-j
, we have

(2.20)

\frac{\Gamma(j-\frac{1}{2}+s)}{\Gamma(j-\frac{1}{2})}=j^{s}\Big(1+\frac{s^{2}-2s}{2j}+O(j^{-2})\Big)

and

(2.21)

\frac{\Gamma(k-s)}{\Gamma(k)}=\frac{\Gamma(j+l-s)}{\Gamma(j+l)}=j^{-s}\Big(1+\frac{s^{2}+(1-2l)s}{2j}+O(j^{-2})\Big).

Thus we have

(2.22)

\frac{\Gamma(j-\frac{1}{2}+s)}{\Gamma(j-\frac{1}{2})}\frac{\Gamma(k-s)}{\Gamma(k)}=1+\frac{2s^{2}-(1+2l)s}{2j}+O(j^{-2}).

Since
m=\alpha N
, this gives

(2.23)

\Big(\frac{\Gamma(j-\frac{1}{2}+s)}{\Gamma(j-\frac{1}{2})}\frac{\Gamma(k-s)}{\Gamma(k)}\Big)^{m}\sim e^{\frac{\alpha}{t}s(2s-1-2l)}.

Let us write
C_{-}
(resp.,
C_{+}
) for a contour that runs from
-i\infty
to
i\infty
and passes to the left (resp., right) of the origin.
Then by (
2.18
) and (
2.23
), we have

(2.24)

b_{j,k}=-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{C_{-}}e^{\frac{\alpha}{t}s(2s-1-2l)}\,\frac{ds}{s}\cdot(1+o(1)).

Therefore it remains to evaluate the contour integral (
2.24
).
For this purpose, we shall apply the inverse Laplace transform,

(2.25)

\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{C_{+}}e^{y\,v}\,\frac{dv}{\sqrt{v}\,(\sqrt{v}+a)}=e^{a^{2}y}\operatorname{erfc}(a\sqrt{y}),\qquad(a<0)

that can be found for instance in
[
18
, 17.13.101]
.
Notice that for
l\geq 0
,

(2.26)

\begin{split}&\quad-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{C_{-}}e^{xs(2s-1-2l)}\,\frac{ds}{s}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{C_{+}}e^{xs(2s+1+2l)}\,\frac{ds}{s}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{C_{+}}e^{x\,\frac{v-(2l+1)^{2}}{8}}\,\frac{dv}{\sqrt{v}\,(\sqrt{v}-(2l+1))},\end{split}

where we have substituted
v=8(2s^{2}+s(1+2l))+(1+2l)^{2}
.
Then by (
2.25
), we obtain

(2.27)

-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{C_{-}}e^{xs(2s-1-2l)}\,\frac{ds}{s}=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{erfc}\Big(-(2l+1)\sqrt{\frac{x}{8}}\Big)=\frac{1}{2}\Big[1+\operatorname{erf}\Big((2l+1)\sqrt{\frac{x}{8}}\Big)\Big].

This gives the asymptotic (
2.16
) for
l\geq 0
.

On the other hand, by the Cauchy’s formula, we have

(2.28)

\begin{split}&\quad-\int_{C_{-}}e^{xs(2s-1-2l)}\,\frac{ds}{s}-\int_{C_{-}}e^{xs(2s+1+2l)}\,\frac{ds}{s}\\
&=-\int_{C_{-}}e^{xs(2s-1-2l)}\,\frac{ds}{s}+\int_{C_{+}}e^{xs(2s-1-2l)}\,\frac{ds}{s}=\oint_{C_{0}}e^{xs(2s-1-2l)}\,\frac{ds}{s}=2\pi i.\end{split}

Here,
C_{0}
denotes a contour encircling the origin counterclockwise.
By (
2.27
), we have that for
l<0
,

(2.29)

\begin{split}-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{C_{-}}e^{xs(2s-1-2l)}\,\frac{ds}{s}&=1+\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{C_{-}}e^{xs(2s+1+2l)}\,\frac{ds}{s}\\
&=1-\frac{1}{2}\Big[1+\operatorname{erf}\Big((-2l-1)\sqrt{\frac{x}{8}}\Big)\Big]=\frac{1}{2}\Big[1+\operatorname{erf}\Big((2l+1)\sqrt{\frac{x}{8}}\Big)\Big],\end{split}

which gives the asymptotic (
2.16
) for
l<0
.
Now the proof is complete.
∎

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1
and asymptotic of the expected number of real eigenvalues

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.1
.
The key ingredients of the proof are the analytic expression of
E_{N}
given in Lemma
3.1
and the asymptotic behaviour of
b_{j,k}
given in Proposition
2.3
.

The following expression of
E_{N}
was obtained by Forrester and Ipsen
[
12
]
.
Nevertheless, let us recall the proof since this will be instructive to perform similar but more complicated computations in Lemma
4.3
.

Lemma 3.1
.

(Cf.
[
12
, Subsection 4.2]
)

For each
N
and
m
, we have

(3.1)

E_{N}(m)=2\sum_{j=0}^{N/2-1}b_{j+1,j+1}-2\sum_{j=0}^{N/2-2}b_{j+2,j+1}.

Proof.

It follows from the definition of the
1
-point function
\mathbf{R}_{N,1}^{m}
that

(3.2)

E_{N}(m)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbf{R}_{N,1}^{m}(x)\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}}S_{N}(x,x)\,dx.

By (
2.12
), we have

(3.3)

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\omega(x)\,x^{j+1}A_{j}(x)\,dx=-\alpha_{j+2,j+1},\qquad\int_{\mathbb{R}}\omega(x)\,x^{j}A_{j+1}(x)\,dx=-\alpha_{j+1,j+2}.

Then by (
2.4
), we have

(3.4)

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}}S_{N}(x,x)\,dx&=\sum_{j=0}^{N-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\omega(x)\,x^{j}}{(2\sqrt{2\pi}j!)^{m}}(xA_{j}(x)-A_{j+1}(x))\,dx\\
&=-\sum_{j=0}^{N-2}\frac{\alpha_{j+2,j+1}-\alpha_{j+1,j+2}}{(2\sqrt{2\pi}j!)^{m}}=-2\sum_{j=0}^{N-2}\frac{\alpha_{j+2,j+1}}{(2\sqrt{2\pi}j!)^{m}}\\
&=2\sum_{j=0}^{N/2-1}\frac{\alpha_{2j+1,2j+2}}{(2\sqrt{2\pi}(2j)!)^{m}}-2\sum_{j=0}^{N/2-2}\frac{\alpha_{2j+3,2j+2}}{(2\sqrt{2\pi}(2j+1)!)^{m}}.\end{split}

Now it follows from the duplication formula

(3.5)

\Gamma(2z+1)=\frac{2^{2z}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\Gamma(z+1)\Gamma(z+\tfrac{1}{2}),

that we have the following identities to used frequently later:

(3.6)

\frac{2^{2j}}{\sqrt{\pi}(2j)!}=\frac{1}{\Gamma(j+\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(j+1)},\qquad\frac{2^{2j+1}}{\sqrt{\pi}(2j+1)!}=\frac{1}{\Gamma(j+\frac{3}{2})\Gamma(j+1)}.

Together with (
2.13
) this implies that

(3.7)

\begin{split}E_{N}(m)&=2\sum_{j=0}^{N/2-1}\frac{a_{j+1,j+1}}{(\Gamma(j+\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(j+1))^{m}}-2\sum_{j=0}^{N/2-2}\frac{a_{j+2,j+1}}{(\Gamma(j+\frac{3}{2})\Gamma(j+1))^{m}}.\end{split}

This completes the proof.
∎

Using Proposition
2.3
and Lemma
3.1
, we prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

By (
3.1
) and Proposition
2.3
, the Riemann sum approximation gives rise to

(3.8)

\begin{split}\frac{E_{N}(\alpha N)}{N}&=\frac{2}{N}\sum_{j=0}^{N/2-1}\frac{a_{j+1,j+1}}{(\Gamma(j+\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(j+1))^{m}}-\frac{2}{N}\sum_{j=0}^{N/2-2}\frac{a_{j+2,j+1}}{(\Gamma(j+\frac{3}{2})\Gamma(j+1))^{m}}\\
&\quad\sim\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}1+\operatorname{erf}\Big(\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{8t}}\Big)\,dt-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}1-\operatorname{erf}\Big(\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{8t}}\Big)\,dt=c(\alpha),\end{split}

where
c(\alpha)
is given by (
1.6
).
This completes the proof.
∎

Let us briefly indicate how the integral in (
1.6
) can be performed in terms of elementary functions, as given in the second equation therein. Setting
y=\sqrt{\alpha/8}
and inserting the integral representation of the error function, we obtain

(3.9)

c(\alpha=8y^{2})=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{y}{\sqrt{t}}e^{-y^{2}s^{2}/t}ds\,dt.

Performing the
t
-integral first and substituting
z=1/t
we obtain

(3.10)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{y}{\sqrt{t}}e^{-y^{2}s^{2}/t}\,dt

\displaystyle=y\int_{1}^{\infty}e^{-y^{2}s^{2}z}z^{-\frac{3}{2}}\,dz=2y\,e^{-y^{2}s^{2}}-2y^{3}s^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-y^{2}s^{2}(u+1)}\frac{du}{\sqrt{u+1}}

(3.11)

\displaystyle=2y\,e^{-y^{2}s^{2}}-2s\sqrt{\pi}y^{2}(1-\operatorname{erf}(ys)),

where in the second step we have used
[
18
, 3.362.2]
. This leads to

(3.12)

c(8y^{2})=2\operatorname{erf}(y)-2y^{2}+4y^{2}\int_{0}^{1}s\operatorname{erf}(ys)ds.

The remaining integral can be determining by using an integration by parts for
\operatorname{erf}(ys)
from
[
18
, 5.41]
, leading to

(3.13)

\int_{0}^{1}s\operatorname{erf}(ys)ds=\operatorname{erf}(y)+\frac{1}{y\sqrt{\pi}}e^{-y^{2}}-\int_{0}^{1}s\operatorname{erf}(ys)ds-\frac{1}{2y^{2}}\operatorname{erf}(y).

Inserting the resulting expression for the integral into (
3.12
),

(3.14)

c(8y^{2})=2\operatorname{erf}(y)-2y^{2}+4y^{2}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{erf}(y)+\frac{1}{2y\sqrt{\pi}}e^{-y^{2}}-\frac{1}{4y^{2}}\operatorname{erf}(y)\Big)

leads to the desired result for (
1.6
).

We end this section by showing the asymptotic behaviour (
1.11
).
Recall that the error function satisfies the expansion

(3.15)

\operatorname{erf}(x)\sim 1-\frac{e^{-x^{2}}}{\sqrt{\pi}x}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{m}\frac{(2m-1)!!}{(2x^{2})^{m}},\qquad(x\to\infty),

see e.g.
[
32
, Eq.(7.12.1)]
.
Using this, we have

(3.16)

\operatorname{erf}\Big(\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{8}}\Big)\sim 1-\sqrt{\frac{8}{\pi\alpha}}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{8}}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{m}\frac{(2m-1)!!}{(\alpha/4)^{m}},\qquad(\alpha\to\infty).

This gives rise to

\displaystyle c(\alpha)

\displaystyle=\Big(1+\frac{\alpha}{4}\Big)\Big[1-e^{-\frac{\alpha}{8}}\sqrt{\frac{8}{\pi\alpha}}\Big(1-\frac{4}{\alpha}+\frac{48}{\alpha^{2}}+O\Big(\frac{1}{\alpha^{3}}\Big)\Big)\Big]-\frac{\alpha}{4}+\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{2\pi}}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{8}}

\displaystyle=1-e^{-\frac{\alpha}{8}}\sqrt{\frac{8}{\pi\alpha}}\Big(1+\frac{\alpha}{4}\Big)\Big(1-\frac{4}{\alpha}+\frac{48}{\alpha^{2}}+O\Big(\frac{1}{\alpha^{3}}\Big)\Big)+\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{2\pi}}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{8}}

\displaystyle=1-e^{-\frac{\alpha}{8}}\sqrt{\frac{8}{\pi\alpha}}\Big(\frac{\alpha}{4}+\frac{8}{\alpha}+O\Big(\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}}\Big)\Big)+\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{2\pi}}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{8}}=1-e^{-\frac{\alpha}{8}}\sqrt{\frac{8}{\pi\alpha}}\Big(\frac{8}{\alpha}+O\Big(\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}}\Big)\Big),

which leads to the desired behaviour (
1.11
).

Proof of Theorem
1.2
and Proposition
4.2

In this section, we study the variance
V_{N}
of the number of real eigenvalues.
We first show some properties of the function
s(\alpha)
in (

).
In particular, in Lemma
4.1
, we derive an alternative representation of

, which will be used in the proof of Theorem
1.2
, and in Proposition

, we derive the asymptotic behaviour of
s(\alpha)
.
The proof of Theorem

follows from the analytic expression of

given in Lemma
4.3
and the asymptotic behaviour of

given in Proposition

.

Let us begin with deriving an alternative representation of the function
s(\alpha)
.

Lemma 4.1
.

The function
s(\alpha)
in (
1.14
) has the equivalent representation

(4.1)

s(\alpha)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\Big(\operatorname{erf}\Big((2k-1)\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{8t}}\Big)\Big[\operatorname{erf}\Big((2k-1)\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{8t}}\Big)-\operatorname{erf}\Big((2k+1)\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{8t}}\Big)\Big]\Big)\,dt.

Proof.

Since

(4.2)

\int_{2k+1}^{2k-1}\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{8\pi t}}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{8t}x^{2}}\,dx=\frac{1}{2}\Big[\operatorname{erf}\Big((2k-1)\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{8t}}\Big)-\operatorname{erf}\Big((2k+1)\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{8t}}\Big)\Big],

the right-hand side of (
4.1
) can be rewritten as

(4.3)

\begin{split}s(\alpha)&=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\operatorname{erf}\Big((2k-1)\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{8t}}\Big)\int_{2k+1}^{2k-1}\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{8\pi t}}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{8t}x^{2}}\,dx\,dt\\
&=-\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\operatorname{erf}\Big((2k+1)\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{8t}}\Big)\int_{2k+1}^{2k-1}\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{8\pi t}}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{8t}x^{2}}\,dx\,dt,\end{split}

after relabelling
k\to-k
and
x\to-x
. This gives

(4.4)

\begin{split}s(\alpha)&=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\Big[\operatorname{erf}\Big((2k-1)\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{8t}}\Big)-\operatorname{erf}\Big((2k+1)\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{8t}}\Big)\Big]\int_{2k+1}^{2k-1}\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{8\pi t}}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{8t}x^{2}}\,dx\,dt.\end{split}

Using (
4.2
) once again, we obtain that the right-hand side of (
4.1
) is same as that of (
1.14
).
∎

Next, we derive the asymptotic behaviour of
s(\alpha)
.
We remark that Proposition
4.2
will not be used in the proof of Theorem
1.2
; rather as we explained in Section
1
, this provides the interpolation (
1.19
) between (
1.12
) and (

Proposition 4.2
.

The function
s(\alpha)
satisfies

(4.5)

s(\alpha)\sim\begin{cases}\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{\pi}}&\text{as}\quad\alpha\to 0\,,\\
1&\text{as}\quad\alpha\to\infty\,.\end{cases}

Proof.

We first show the asymptotic behaviour when
\alpha\to\infty
.
Indeed, this easily follows from the second expression in (
1.14
), as for
\alpha\to\infty
only the summand with
k=0
contributes,

(4.6)

s(\alpha)\sim\int_{0}^{1}\Pr(-1\leq X_{t,\alpha}\leq 1)^{2}\,dt\sim\int_{0}^{1}1\,dt=1.

Next, we show the asymptotic behaviour when
\alpha\to 0
.
By the change of variable
u=\alpha/t
in (
1.14
), we have

(4.7)

s(\alpha)=-\frac{\alpha}{8\pi}\int_{\infty}^{\alpha}\frac{1}{u}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\Big(\int_{2k-1}^{2k+1}e^{-\frac{u}{8}x^{2}}\,dx\Big)^{2}\,du.

Differentiating (
4.7
), we obtain

(4.8)

s^{\prime}(\alpha)=\frac{s(\alpha)}{\alpha}-\frac{1}{8\pi}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\Big(\int_{2k-1}^{2k+1}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{8}x^{2}}\,dx\Big)^{2}.

By (
4.8
), it suffices to show that

(4.9)

\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\Big(\int_{2k-1}^{2k+1}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{8}x^{2}}\,dx\Big)^{2}\sim\frac{4\sqrt{\pi}}{\sqrt{\alpha}},\qquad(\alpha\to 0).

Write
n=1/\sqrt{\alpha}.
Then we have

(4.10)

\begin{split}\sqrt{\alpha}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\Big(\int_{2k-1}^{2k+1}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{8}x^{2}}\,dx\Big)^{2}&=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\Big(\int_{2k-1}^{2k+1}e^{-\frac{1}{8}(\frac{x}{n})^{2}}\,dx\Big)^{2}.\end{split}

Note that for

, as

, we have

(4.11)

\Big(\int_{2k-1}^{2k+1}e^{-\frac{1}{8}(\frac{x}{n})^{2}}\,dx\Big)^{2}=2n^{2}\pi\Big[\operatorname{erf}\Big(\frac{2nt+1}{2\sqrt{2}n}\Big)-\operatorname{erf}\Big(\frac{2nt-1}{2\sqrt{2}n}\Big)\Big]^{2}\sim 4\,e^{-t^{2}}.

Here, we have used the Taylor series of the error function to first order.
Then, by the Riemann sum approximation, we obtain

(4.12)

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\Big(\int_{2k-1}^{2k+1}e^{-\frac{1}{8}(\frac{x}{n})^{2}}\,dx\Big)^{2}\sim 4\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-t^{2}}\,dt=4\sqrt{\pi}.

This gives the desired asymptotic behaviour (
4.9
), which completes the proof.
∎

Let us now begin to prove Theorem
1.2
.
It is well known that the variance

can be expressed in terms of

and

V_{N}(m)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbf{R}_{N,1}^{m}(x)\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Big(\mathbf{R}_{N,2}^{m}(x,y)-\mathbf{R}_{N,1}^{m}(x)\mathbf{R}_{N,1}^{m}(y)\Big)\,dx\,dy,

see e.g.
[
14
]
or
[
30
, Chapter 16]
.
Due to the Pfaffian structure (

(4.13)

V_{N}(m)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}S_{N}(x,x)\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Big(S_{N}(x,y)S_{N}(y,x)+[\text{sgn}(x-y)+\tilde{I}_{N}(x,y)]D_{N}(x,y)\Big)\,dx\,dy.

Furthermore, integration by parts gives rise to

(4.14)

V_{N}(m)=2E_{N}(m)-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}S_{N}(x,y)S_{N}(y,x)\,dx\,dy.

We also refer the reader to
[
6
]
for the use of such an identity in the context of the real elliptic Ginibre ensemble. Notice that this expression is very much reminiscent to the result in determinantal point processes with a single scalar kernel.

We first evaluate the double integral in (
4.14
).
Recall that
b_{j,k}
’s are given by (
2.8
).

Lemma 4.3
.

We have

(4.15)

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}S_{N}(x,y)S_{N}(y,x)\,dx\,dy&=2\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq p\leq N/2-1\\
0\leq q\leq N/2-1\end{subarray}}b_{p+1,q+1}\,b_{q+1,p+1}\\
&\quad+2\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq p\leq N/2-2\\
0\leq q\leq N/2-2\end{subarray}}b_{q+2,p+1}\,b_{p+2,q+1}\,\\
&\quad-4\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq p\leq N/2-2\\
0\leq q\leq N/2-1\end{subarray}}b_{q+1,p+1}\,b_{p+2,q+1}\,.\end{split}

Proof.

By (
2.4
), it holds that

\displaystyle S_{N}(x,y)S_{N}(y,x)=\sum_{j,k=0}^{N-2}\frac{\omega(x)\,x^{j}}{(2\sqrt{2\pi}j!)^{m}}(xA_{j}(y)-A_{j+1}(y))\frac{\omega(y)\,y^{k}}{(2\sqrt{2\pi}k!)^{m}}(yA_{k}(x)-A_{k+1}(x)).

Using (
2.12
), we have

\displaystyle\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}}\omega(x)\,x^{j}(xA_{j}(y)-A_{j+1}(y))(yA_{k}(x)-A_{k+1}(x))\,dx

\displaystyle=-y\,A_{j}(y)\alpha_{j+2,k+1}+A_{j}(y)\alpha_{j+2,k+2}+y\,A_{j+1}(y)\alpha_{j+1,k+1}-\,A_{j+1}(y)\alpha_{j+1,k+2}.

Applying (
2.12
) once again, we obtain

\displaystyle\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\omega(x)\,\omega(y)\,x^{j}\,y^{k}(xA_{j}(y)-A_{j+1}(y))(yA_{k}(x)-A_{k+1}(x))\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\omega(y)\,\,y^{k}\Big(A_{j}(y)\alpha_{j+2,k+2}-y\,A_{j}(y)\alpha_{j+2,k+1}+y\,A_{j+1}(y)\alpha_{j+1,k+1}-\,A_{j+1}(y)\alpha_{j+1,k+2}\Big)\,dy

\displaystyle=2\Big(\alpha_{j+1,k+1}\alpha_{j+2,k+2}-\alpha_{j+1,k+2}\alpha_{j+2,k+1}\Big),

upon using the antisymmetry
\alpha_{j,k}=-\alpha_{k,j}
.
Combining this identity with (
2.13
) and further properties (
2.13
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}S_{N}(x,y)S_{N}(y,x)\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle=2\sum_{j,k=0}^{N-2}\frac{\alpha_{j+1,k+1}\alpha_{j+2,k+2}-\alpha_{j+1,k+2}\alpha_{j+2,k+1}}{(2\sqrt{2\pi}j!)^{m}\,(2\sqrt{2\pi}k!)^{m}}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j+k:odd\\
0\leq j,k\leq N-2\end{subarray}}\frac{\alpha_{j+1,k+1}\alpha_{j+2,k+2}}{(2\sqrt{2\pi}j!)^{m}\,(2\sqrt{2\pi}k!)^{m}}-2\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j+k:even\\
0\leq j,k\leq N-2\end{subarray}}\frac{\alpha_{j+1,k+2}\alpha_{j+2,k+1}}{(2\sqrt{2\pi}j!)^{m}\,(2\sqrt{2\pi}k!)^{m}}.

For the first sum we obtain from the symmetry of the summand under exchanging
k
and
j

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j+k:odd\\
0\leq j,k\leq N-2\end{subarray}}\frac{\alpha_{j+1,k+1}\alpha_{j+2,k+2}}{(2\sqrt{2\pi}j!)^{m}\,(2\sqrt{2\pi}k!)^{m}}

\displaystyle=-2\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq p\leq N/2-2\\
0\leq q\leq N/2-1\end{subarray}}\frac{\alpha_{2q+1,2p+2}\,\alpha_{2p+3,2q+2}}{(2\sqrt{2\pi}(2p+1)!)^{m}\,(2\sqrt{2\pi}(2q)!)^{m}}

\displaystyle=-2\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq p\leq N/2-2\\
0\leq q\leq N/2-1\end{subarray}}\Big(\frac{2^{2p+2q+1}\,}{\pi(2p+1)!\,(2q)!}\Big)^{m}a_{q+1,p+1}\,a_{p+2,q+1}

\displaystyle=-2\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq p\leq N/2-2\\
0\leq q\leq N/2-1\end{subarray}}b_{q+1,p+1}\,b_{p+2,q+1}.

Here we have used (
2.14
) and the identities (
3.6
).
Similarly, we have for the second sum

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j+k:even\\
0\leq j,k\leq N-2\end{subarray}}\frac{\alpha_{j+1,k+2}\alpha_{j+2,k+1}}{(2\sqrt{2\pi}j!)^{m}\,(2\sqrt{2\pi}k!)^{m}}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq p\leq N/2-1\\
0\leq q\leq N/2-1\end{subarray}}\frac{\alpha_{2p+1,2q+2}\alpha_{2q+1,2p+2}}{(2\sqrt{2\pi}(2p)!)^{m}\,(2\sqrt{2\pi}(2q)!)^{m}}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq p\leq N/2-2\\
0\leq q\leq N/2-2\end{subarray}}\frac{\alpha_{2q+3,2p+2}\alpha_{2p+3,2q+2}}{(2\sqrt{2\pi}(2p+1)!)^{m}\,(2\sqrt{2\pi}(2q+1)!)^{m}}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq p\leq N/2-1\\
0\leq q\leq N/2-1\end{subarray}}\!\Big(\frac{2^{2p+2q}}{\pi(2p)!(2q)!}\Big)^{m}a_{p+1,q+1}\,a_{q+1,p+1}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq p\leq N/2-2\\
0\leq q\leq N/2-2\end{subarray}}\!\Big(\frac{2^{2p+2q+2}}{\pi(2p+1)!\,(2q+1)!}\Big)^{m}a_{q+2,p+1}\,a_{p+2,q+1}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq p\leq N/2-1\\
0\leq q\leq N/2-1\end{subarray}}b_{p+1,q+1}\,b_{q+1,p+1}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq p\leq N/2-2\\
0\leq q\leq N/2-2\end{subarray}}b_{q+2,p+1}\,b_{p+2,q+1}.

Combining all of the above, the desired equation (
4.15
) follows.
∎

We are now ready to prove Theorem

.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

By (
4.14
) and Theorem
1.1
, we have

(4.16)

\begin{split}\frac{V_{N}(\alpha N)}{E_{N}(\alpha N)}&=2-\frac{2}{E_{N}(\alpha N)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}S_{N}(x,y)S_{N}(y,x)\,dx\,dy\\
&\sim 2-\frac{2}{Nc(\alpha)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}S_{N}(x,y)S_{N}(y,x)\,dx\,dy.\end{split}

Therefore by Lemma
4.1
, it suffices to show that

(4.17)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}S_{N}(x,y)S_{N}(y,x)\,dx\,dy=s(\alpha),

where
s(\alpha)
is given by (
4.1
).

Note that by Proposition
2.3
, we have that for
l\in\mathbb{Z}
fixed,

(4.18)

b_{j,j+l}\sim\frac{1}{2}\Big[1+\operatorname{erf}\Big((2l+1)\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{8t}}\Big)\Big].

It order to evaluate the double sums in Lemma
4.3
we rearrange them in the following way:

(4.19)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq p\leq L\\
0\leq q\leq L\end{subarray}}A_{p,q}=\sum_{l=-L}^{L}\sum_{k+l=0}^{L}A_{k,k+l},\qquad\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq p\leq L-1\\
0\leq q\leq L\end{subarray}}A_{p,q}=\sum_{l=-L}^{L}\sum_{j+l=0}^{L-1}A_{j+l,j},

for the first and second, respectively third sum.
Using the asymptotic behaviour (
4.18
), we thus obtain for
the first sum in (
4.15
) that as
N\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\frac{2}{N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq p\leq N/2-1\\
0\leq q\leq N/2-1\end{subarray}}b_{q+1,p+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=-N/2+1}^{N/2-1}\,\frac{2}{N}\sum_{k+l=0}^{N/2-1}b_{k+1,j+l+1}\,b_{j+l+1,j+1}

\displaystyle\sim\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{1}\Big[1+\operatorname{erf}\Big((2l+1)\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{8t}}\Big)\Big]\cdot\Big[1-\operatorname{erf}\Big((2l-1)\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{8t}}\Big)\Big]\,dt.

Here we have used the Riemann sum approximation.
Similarly, we have for the second sum

\displaystyle\frac{2}{N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq p\leq N/2-2\\
0\leq q\leq N/2-2\end{subarray}}b_{p+2,q+1}\,b_{q+2,p+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=-N/2+2}^{N/2-2}\frac{2}{N}\sum_{j+l=0}^{N/2-2}b_{j+2,j+l+1}\,b_{j+l+2,j+1}

\displaystyle\sim\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{1}\Big[1+\operatorname{erf}\Big((2l-1)\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{8t}}\Big)\Big]\cdot\Big[1-\operatorname{erf}\Big((2l+1)\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{8t}}\Big)\Big]\,dt

and for the third

\displaystyle\frac{4}{N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq p\leq N/2-2\\
0\leq q\leq N/2-1\end{subarray}}b_{p+2,q+1}\,b_{q+1,p+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=-N/2+1}^{N/2-1}\frac{4}{N}\sum_{j+l=0}^{N/2-2}b_{j+2,j+l+1}\,b_{j+l+1,j+1}

\displaystyle\sim\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\Big[1-\operatorname{erf}\Big((2l-1)\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{8t}}\Big)^{2}\Big]\,dt.

Combining all of the above equations, we obtain (
4.17
).
This completes the proof.
∎

In this section, we study densities of real eigenvalues.
To analyse the limiting densities, we shall use the method of moments following the idea by Simm
[

]
.
Namely, we consider the moments (

) and (

To perform the asymptotic analysis, we use the analytic expressions of the moments (Lemmas

Let us first show the first part of Theorem

.
For this purpose, let

be the
k
-th the moment of the
1
-point function.
Note that
M_{0,N}(m)=E_{N}(m)
and that the 1-point function is even.

Recall the following property from
[
35

.
This can be shown using Lemma

Lemma 5.1
.

We have
M_{2k+1,N}(m)=0
and

(5.2)

M_{2k,N}(m)=M_{2k,N}^{(1)}(m)-M_{2k,N}^{(2)}(m),

(5.3)

\displaystyle M_{2k,N}^{(1)}(m)

\displaystyle:=\Big(\frac{2}{N}\Big)^{mk}\sum_{j=0}^{N/2-1}\frac{a_{j+1,j+k+1}+a_{j+k+1,j+1}}{(\Gamma(j+\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(j+1))^{m}},

(5.4)

\displaystyle M_{2k,N}^{(2)}(m)

\displaystyle:=\Big(\frac{2}{N}\Big)^{mk}\sum_{j=0}^{N/2-2}\frac{a_{j+2,j+k+1}+a_{j+k+2,j+1}}{(\Gamma(j+\frac{3}{2})\Gamma(j+1))^{m}}.

Remark
.

We remark that Lemma

was used in
[

]

To be more precise, it was shown that

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{M_{k,N}(m)}{M_{0,N}(m)}=\begin{cases}(mk+1)^{-1}&k\textup{ even},\\

Then (

To analyse the moment

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
j=tN/2
with

Then for any fixed

\Big(\frac{2}{N}\Big)^{mk}\,\frac{a_{j+1,j+k+1}+a_{j+k+1,j+1}}{(\Gamma(j+\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(j+1))^{m}}=O(t^{k\alpha N})

(5.8)

\Big(\frac{2}{N}\Big)^{mk}\,\frac{a_{j+2,j+k+1}+a_{j+k+2,j+1}}{(\Gamma(j+\frac{3}{2})\Gamma(j+1))^{m}}=O(t^{k\alpha N})

Note that by (

\frac{a_{j+1,j+k+1}}{(\Gamma(j+\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(j+1))^{m}}=\Big(\frac{\Gamma(j+k+1)}{\Gamma(j+1)}\Big)^{m}b_{j+1,k+1}.

\Big(\frac{2}{N}\Big)^{k}\frac{\Gamma(j+k+1)}{\Gamma(j+1)}\sim\Big(\frac{2}{N}j\Big)^{k}\sim t^{k},

it follows from Proposition

that

(5.11)

\begin{split}\Big(\frac{2}{N}\Big)^{mk}\frac{a_{j+1,j+k+1}}{(\Gamma(j+\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(j+1))^{m}}&=\Big(\Big(\frac{2}{N}\Big)^{k}\frac{\Gamma(j+k+1)}{\Gamma(j+1)}\Big)^{m}b_{j+1,j+k+1}=O(t^{k\alpha N}).\end{split}

Similarly, by combining

(5.12)

\Big(\frac{2}{N}\Big)^{k}\frac{\Gamma(j+k+\frac{1}{2})}{\Gamma(j+\frac{1}{2})}\sim\Big(\frac{2}{N}j\Big)^{k}\sim t^{k},\qquad\Big(\frac{2}{N}\Big)^{k}\frac{\Gamma(j+k+\frac{3}{2})}{\Gamma(j+\frac{3}{2})}\sim\Big(\frac{2}{N}j\Big)^{k}\sim t^{k}

with Proposition

, we obtain

(5.13)

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{2}{N}\Big)^{mk}\frac{a_{j+k+1,j+1}}{(\Gamma(j+\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(j+1))^{m}}

\displaystyle=O(t^{k\alpha N}),

(5.14)

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{2}{N}\Big)^{mk}\frac{a_{j+2,j+k+1}}{(\Gamma(j+\frac{3}{2})\Gamma(j+1))^{m}}

\displaystyle=O(t^{k\alpha N}),

(5.15)

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{2}{N}\Big)^{mk}\frac{a_{j+k+2,j+1}}{(\Gamma(j+\frac{3}{2})\Gamma(j+1))^{m}}

\displaystyle=O(t^{k\alpha N}).

This completes the proof.

Along the same lines of the proof of Lemma

Lemma 5.3

We have
\widetilde{M}_{2k+1,N}(m)=0

Lemma 5.4

(ii).

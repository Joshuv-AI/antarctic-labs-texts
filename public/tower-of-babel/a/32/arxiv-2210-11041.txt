The Turán Number of Surfaces
Maya Sankar

Abstract
We show that there is a constant c c such that any 3-uniform hypergraph ℋ {\mathcal{H}} with n n vertices and at least c ​ n 5 / 2 cn^{5/2} edges contains a triangulation of the real projective plane as a subgraph. This resolves a conjecture of Kupavskii, Polyanskii, Tomon, and Zakharov. Furthermore, our work, combined with prior results, asymptotically determines the Turán number of all surfaces.

Introduction

Turán-type questions are fundamental in the study of extremal combinatorics. Given a fixed
r
-uniform hypergraph
{\mathcal{F}}
, its
Turán number

\ex(n,{\mathcal{F}})
is the maximum number of edges in an
r
-uniform hypergraph
{\mathcal{H}}
on
n
vertices which does not contain
{\mathcal{F}}
as a subgraph. Estimating Turán numbers for hypergraphs remains a largely open problem; we refer the reader to the surveys
[
3
,
9
,
1
]
for a general overview.

This paper investigates a topological variant of this extremal problem. Any
r
-uniform hypergraph
{\mathcal{H}}
may be viewed as an
(r-1)
-dimensional simplicial complex whose facets are the edges of
{\mathcal{H}}
. Similarly, one may ask if any subgraph of
{\mathcal{H}}
is homeomorphic to a given
(r-1)
-dimensional simplicial complex
X
. This geometric perspective yields many natural generalizations of graph properties to higher dimensions. For example, one analogue of Hamiltonian cycles in 3-uniform hypergraphs that has received some attention (see
[
2
,
8
]
) is a spanning sub-hypergraph homeomorphic to the 2-sphere. Additionally, one is naturally interested in the following extremal quantity. Let
X
be a closed
(r-1)
-dimensional manifold. Denote by
\ex_{\hom}(n,X)
the maximum number of edges in a
r
-uniform hypergraph
{\mathcal{H}}
on
n
vertices such that no subgraph of
{\mathcal{H}}
is homeomorphic (as a simplicial complex) to
X
. This is the
Turán number
of the topological space
X
.

As part of his program in high-dimensional combinatorics, Linial
[
7
]
asked for the asymptotics of
\ex_{\hom}(n,X)
when
r\geq 3
. Linial’s question was partially motivated by the work of Sós, Erdős, and Brown
[
10
]
some decades prior, which showed that
\ex_{\hom}(n,X)=\Theta(n^{5/2})
when
X
is the 2-sphere
\mathbb{S}^{2}
. Linial
[
7
]
sketched a new proof of the lower bound
\ex_{\hom}(n,\mathbb{S}^{2})=\Omega(n^{5/2})
which generalized to all closed, connected 2-manifolds
X
; this proof is given rigorously in
[
5
, §2]
. We call such a 2-manifold a
surface
.

All surfaces fall into one of three categories: the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{2}
, the connected sum of
g\geq 1
tori, or the connected sum of
k\geq 1
real projective planes. Until recently, it was unknown if the lower bound of
n^{5/2}
was asymptotically tight for the latter two classes. Indeed, Linial
[
6
,
7
]
repeatedly conjectured a matching upper bound for the torus
\mathbb{T}^{2}
, i.e. that
\ex_{\hom}(n,\mathbb{T}^{2})=O(n^{5/2})
. Kupavskii, Polyanskii, Tomon, and Zakharov
[
5
]
proved Linial’s conjecture in 2020. Additionally, they showed that if two surfaces
X_{1},X_{2}
satisfy
\ex_{\hom}(n,X_{i})=O(n^{5/2})
, their connected sum
X_{1}\#X_{2}
also satisfies
\ex_{\hom}(n,X_{1}\#X_{2})=O(n^{5/2})
, thereby extending the upper bound
\ex_{\hom}(n,X)=O(n^{5/2})
to orientable surfaces of the form
X=\mathbb{T}^{2}\#\cdots\#\mathbb{T}^{2}
. They were unable to derive the corresponding result for any non-orientable surfaces, but conjectured that the same bound applies to all surfaces.

Our main result is the resolution of this conjecture. We show that Linial’s lower bound is asymptotically tight for the real projective plane
{\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}}^{2}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

We have
\ex_{\hom}(n,{\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}}^{2})=O(n^{5/2})
.

By Kupavskii, Polyanskii, Tomon, and Zakharov’s result on connected sums, this bound generalizes to all non-orientable surfaces
X={\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}}^{2}\#\cdots\#{\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}}^{2}
. Combining our work with the results of Sós, Erdős, and Brown
[
10
]
for the sphere and Kupavskii, Polyanskii, Tomon, and Zakharov
[
5
]
for all other orientable surfaces, we completely determine the asymptotics of
\ex_{\hom}(n,X)
for any surface
X
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
X
be any surface. Then
\ex_{\hom}(n,X)=\Theta(n^{5/2})
, where the constant coefficients may depend on the surface
X
.

Let

Definition 3.1

Suppose

Lemma 3.4

Fix

Lemma 3.5

(Lemma 3.3 in
[
5
]
)
.

Let

and let

be positive integers. Let

be a 3-uniform hypergraph with

contains a subset

of at least

is

-semi-admissible in
{\mathcal{H}}
.

Proof of
Lemma

3.4
.

Choose
c=\max\left(\frac{12r}{p}\sqrt{\frac{k}{\epsilon}},\sqrt{\frac{d}{12}}\right)
. By
Lemma

3.5
, there is a subset
F\subseteq E({\mathcal{H}})
of size at least
\frac{c}{2}n^{5/2}
with the property that any neighboring edges
xyz,x^{\prime}yz\in F
are
(p,\epsilon,k,r)
-semi-admissible in
{\mathcal{H}}
. Let
{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}\subseteq{\mathcal{H}}
be the 3-uniform hypergraph on
V({\mathcal{H}})
with edge set
F
. We use an averaging argument to show that
e({\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}_{u,u^{\prime}})\geq dn/4
for some
u,u^{\prime}\in V({\mathcal{H}})
. In this case,
G={\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}_{u,u^{\prime}}
satisfies the desired conditions.

Set
V=V({\mathcal{H}})
and randomly choose
(u,u^{\prime})\in V^{2}
uniformly among all pairs of distinct vertices. Given an unordered pair of vertices
vw\in\binom{V}{2}
, let
T_{vw}=|\{u^{\prime\prime}\in V:u^{\prime\prime}vw\in F\}|
. We have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[e({\mathcal{H}}_{u,u^{\prime}})]

\displaystyle=\sum_{vw\in\binom{V}{2}}\Pr[vw\in E({\mathcal{H}}_{u,u^{\prime}})]=\frac{1}{n(n-1)}\sum_{vw\in\binom{V}{2}}T_{vw}(T_{vw}-1).

The average of the
T_{vw}
is

T=\frac{2}{n(n-1)}\sum_{vw\in\binom{V}{2}}T_{vw}=\frac{2}{n(n-1)}\times 3|F|\geq 3c\sqrt{n}.

Then, by convexity,

{\mathbb{E}}[e({\mathcal{H}}_{u,u^{\prime}})]\geq\frac{1}{n(n-1)}\times\binom{n}{2}T(T-1)\geq\frac{1}{2}(3c\sqrt{n})(3c\sqrt{n}-1).

Because

and
n\geq 1
, we have

{\mathbb{E}}[e({\mathcal{H}}_{u,u^{\prime}})]\geq\frac{1}{2}(3c\sqrt{n})(2c\sqrt{n})=3c^{2}n\geq\frac{dn}{4}.

Thus, there are vertices
u,u^{\prime}\in V
such that

has at least
dn/4
edges. Moreover, for any incident edges
vw,vw^{\prime}\in E(G)
, the four edges

are all in

, implying that the pairs
(uvw,uvw^{\prime})
and
(u^{\prime}vw,u^{\prime}vw^{\prime})
are both
(p,\epsilon,k,r)
-semi-admissible in
{\mathcal{H}}
.

.

Fix

Suppose

Lemma 3.7

(Lemma 3.1 in
[

]
)

Fix

and integers

Proof of

.

Set

and set

.

Let us induct on

.
For the base case, suppose

, and take

. By

, the number of

-admissible edges in

-admissible edges

and

. Moreover,

.

Now, suppose that

\partial{\mathcal{D}}=v_{0}v_{1}uv_{3}v_{0}
and interior vertex set
V^{\circ}({\mathcal{D}})\subseteq U_{3}\setminus W
.

(ii)

There is
{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}\subseteq{\mathcal{H}}
homeomorphic to
\mathbb{D}^{2}
with induced boundary
\partial{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}=v_{0}v_{2}u^{\prime}v_{3}v_{0}
and interior vertex set
V^{\circ}({\mathcal{D}}^{\prime})\subseteq U_{4}\setminus W
.

Proof.

We show that (i) and (ii) each occur with probability at least
1-2r\epsilon-\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{r-5}
. Then, a union bound implies the desired result.

(i) is a direct application of
Lemma

3.3
. Recall that
U_{3}\sim_{2p}V({\mathcal{H}})
. The pair
(uv_{0}v_{1},uv_{0}v_{3})
is
(p,\epsilon,7,r)
-admissible in
{\mathcal{H}}
because
v_{0}v_{1},v_{0}v_{3}\in E(H)\subseteq E(G)
. Applying
Lemma

3.3
shows that
{\mathcal{D}}
exists with probability at least
1-2r\epsilon-\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{r-5}
.

The bound for (ii) is analogous, but requires more care because
v_{2}
is chosen randomly. Fix any
v^{*}_{2}\in N_{H}(v_{0})\setminus\{v_{1},v_{3}\}
. The pair
(u^{\prime}v_{0}v^{*}_{2},u^{\prime}v_{0}v_{3})
is
(p,\epsilon,7,r)
-admissible in
{\mathcal{H}}
because
v_{0}v^{*}_{2},v_{0}v_{3}\in E(H)\subseteq E(G)
. Moreover, because
v_{2}
and
U_{4}
are chosen independently, the distribution of
U_{4}
conditioned on the event
v_{2}=v_{2}^{*}
is still
U_{4}\sim_{2p}V({\mathcal{H}})
. Applying
Lemma

3.3
while conditioning on the event
v_{2}=v_{2}^{*}
, we have

\Pr[\exists{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}\text{ as in (ii)}\mid v_{2}=v_{2}^{*}]\geq 1-2r\epsilon-\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{r-5}.

Averaging over all possible
v_{2}^{*}\in N_{H}(v_{0})\setminus\{v_{1},v_{3}\}
yields the desired bound:

\Pr[\exists{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}\text{ as in (ii)}]=\sum_{v_{2}^{*}}\Pr[v_{2}=v_{2}^{*}]\times\Pr[\exists{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}\text{ as in (ii)}\mid v_{2}=v_{2}^{*}]\geq 1-2r\epsilon-\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{r-5}.\qed

Combining
4.2
and
4.3
via a union bound shows that the following four structures exist simultaneously with probability at least
\frac{1}{3d}-4r\epsilon-2\times\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{r-5}
.

•

A cycle
C\subseteq{\mathcal{H}}_{u,u^{\prime}}
containing the subpath
v_{1}v_{0}v_{2}
with
V(C)\setminus\{v_{0},v_{1}\}\subseteq U_{1}\setminus W
.

•

A cycle
C^{\prime}\subseteq{\mathcal{H}}_{u,u^{\prime}}
containing the subpath
v_{3}v_{0}
with
V(C)\setminus\{v_{0},v_{3}\}\subseteq U_{2}\setminus W
.

•

A subgraph
{\mathcal{D}}\subseteq{\mathcal{H}}
homeomorphic to
\mathbb{D}^{2}
with induced boundary
\partial{\mathcal{D}}=v_{0}v_{1}uv_{3}v_{0}
and interior vertex set
V^{\circ}({\mathcal{D}})\subseteq U_{3}\setminus W
.

•

A subgraph
{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}\subseteq{\mathcal{H}}
homeomorphic to
\mathbb{D}^{2}
with induced boundary
\partial{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}=v_{0}v_{2}u^{\prime}v_{3}v_{0}
and interior vertex set
V^{\circ}({\mathcal{D}}^{\prime})\subseteq U_{4}\setminus W
.

Fix
r
large enough that
2\times\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{r-5}<\frac{1}{6d}
. Choose
\epsilon
small enough (in terms of
r
) that
4r\epsilon<\frac{1}{6d}
. It follows that

\frac{1}{3d}-4r\epsilon-2\times\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{r-5}>0.

Thus, for some choice of the random partition
U_{1},\ldots,U_{4}
and random vertex
v_{2}
, there are structures
C,C^{\prime},{\mathcal{D}},{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}
satisfying the four bullet points.

We obtain a triangulation of
{\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}}^{2}
in
{\mathcal{H}}
by applying
Lemma

2.2
. By construction, the vertices
v_{0},v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}
are distinct. Moreover the five sets
V(C)\setminus\{v_{0},v_{1}\}
,
V(C^{\prime})\setminus\{v_{0},v_{3}\}
,
V^{\circ}({\mathcal{D}})
,
V^{\circ}({\mathcal{D}}^{\prime})
, and
W
are disjoint, as they are respectively contained in the five disjoint sets
U_{1}\setminus W
,
U_{2}\setminus W
,
U_{3}\setminus W
,
U_{4}\setminus W
, and
W
. Thus,
Lemma

2.2
yields that
{\mathcal{H}}
contains a triangulation of
{\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}}^{2}
.
∎

5
Concluding Remarks

Although our upper bound on
\ex_{\hom}(n,{\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}}^{2})
and the upper bound on
\ex_{\hom}(n,\mathbb{T}^{2})
given in
[
5
]
are both asymptotically
O(n^{5/2})
, we remark that neither proof naturally generalizes to all surfaces. Indeed, our work is tied to the decomposition of
{\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}}^{2}
given in
Section

2
, whereas the strategy of
[
5
]
is inherently limited to orientable surfaces. However, there are some fundamental similarities: both results follow a probabilistic approach, and the
n^{5/2}
term can be traced back to
Lemma

3.5
(which is Lemma 3.3 in
[
5
]
) in both cases. This suggests that there may be a proof of
Theorem

1.2
that unifies the two strategies.

Question 5.1
.

Can
Theorem

1.2
be proven without extensive case-specific arguments? That is, is there a single unified approach that works for all orientable and non-orientable surfaces
X
?

A positive answer to
5.1
would have further implications. Notice that the quantity
\ex_{\hom}(n,X)
can be defined for any
homogenous

(r-1)
-dimensional simplicial complex
X
, i.e. any simplicial complex
X=X({\mathcal{F}})
homeomorphic to some
r
-uniform hypergraph
{\mathcal{F}}
. When
r=3
, Keevash, Long, Narayanan, and Scott
[
4
]
recently showed that
\ex_{\hom}(n,X)=O(n^{2.8})
for any homogenous 2-dimensional simplicial complex
X
, and conjectured that the exponent could be brought down to
\frac{5}{2}
.

Conjecture 5.2
.

Let
X
be any homogenous 2-dimensional simplicial complex. Then we have
\ex_{\hom}(n,X)=O(n^{5/2})
.

This conjecture was reiterated by Kupavskii, Polyanskii, Tomon, and Zakharov
[
5
]
and previously alluded to by Linial
[
6
,
7
]
.
An affirmative answer to
5.1
would mark an important next step towards its proof.

Lastly, we ask about
\ex_{\hom}(n,X)
in higher dimensions.

Question 5.3
.

Let
r\geq 4
and let
X
be a
(r-1)
-dimensional manifold. What can we say about the asymptotics of
\ex_{\hom}(n,X)
? What if
X
is an arbitrary homogenous
(r-1)
-dimensional simplicial complex?

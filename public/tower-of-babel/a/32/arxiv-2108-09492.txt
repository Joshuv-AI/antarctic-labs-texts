Local Solubility of Hyperelliptic Curves
Omri Faraggi

Abstract
. We give a condition for a hyperelliptic curve C C over a local field K K to be locally soluble, on the condition that C C obtains semistable reduction after a tame extension of K K , and that the residue field k k is sufficiently large relative to the genus of the curve. The condition is presented in terms of the cluster picture of C C , a combinatorial object which determines much of the local arithmetic of C C .



1.
Introduction

Let
C:y^{2}=f(x)
be a hyperelliptic curve of genus
g\geq 2
over a local field
K
with residue
k
of characteristic
p>2
. In this note we give a condition for

to have a
K
-rational point in terms of the

of

C
has tame reduction — in other words, it obtains semistable reduction after a tame extension of

. The cluster picture

-adic distances between the roots of the defining polynomial

. This removes dependency on the exact nature of

, and cluster pictures have been used used to calculate the Galois representation, semistable model, conductor and minimal discriminant of
C
in
[
6
]
, the minimal snc model if
C
has tame reduction in
[
7
]
, the Tamagawa number in
[
2
]
, the root number in
[
4
]
and finally differentials in
[
8
]
and
[
10
]
. A survey article is available at
[
1
]
. Since our condition is in terms of the cluster picture of
C
, it allows us to determine whether families of hyperelliptic curves are locally soluble, rather than having to consider individual curves. With some additional work, this should allow us to determine what proportion of hyperelliptic curves of a given genus are locally soluble. This follows on from work such as
[
3
]
and
[

.

Throughout the note the hyperelliptic curve
C

The Condition

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
C:y^{2}=f(x)
be a hyperelliptic curve with tame reduction over a local field
K
and let
\mathcal{C}
be a regular snc model of
C
. Suppose that the residue field of
K
has size
q>2(g(C)^{2}-1)
. Then any smooth component
\Gamma
of the special fibre which is fixed by Frobenius has a smooth
k
-rational point.

Proof.

Assume
f
has odd degree, since the even case is dealt with similarly. Note that any smooth component fixed by Frobenius must be defined over
k
. The Hasse-Weil bound then states that
\left|\#\Gamma(k)-(q+1)\right|\leq 2g(\Gamma)\sqrt{q}
, and hence
\Gamma(k)
is non-empty if
q>2(g(\Gamma)^{2}-1)
. Since
g(\Gamma)\leq g(C)
, we are done if
\mathcal{C}
is a smooth model.

Now assume
\mathcal{C}
is a semistable model. If
\Gamma
is genus
0
then it has
k
-points, so assume it has positive genus and comes from a principal cluster
\mathfrak{s}
. Let
I
be the number of intersection points of other components. Then
\Gamma
has a smooth
k
-rational point if
q-I>2(g(\Gamma)^{2}-1)
, and hence it has a smooth
k
-point if
2g(C)^{2}-I>2g(\Gamma)^{2}
. Suppose
\mathfrak{s}=\mathcal{R}
is odd, and that
\mathfrak{s}
has
s_{s}
children of size
1
,
s_{o}
proper odd children and
s_{e}
proper even children. Then by
[
6
, Theorem 8.5]
,
I=s_{o}+2s_{e}
and
2g(\Gamma)+1=s_{o}+s_{s}
. We also know that
2g(C)+1=\deg(f)\geq s_{s}+2s_{e}+3s_{o}
. Putting these together the result follows, and similarly if
\mathfrak{s}
is even or
\mathfrak{s}\neq\mathcal{R}

Now assume
C
has tame reduction. A similar argument works using
[
7
, Theorem 7.12,7.18]
, noting that since
\Gamma
is a smooth component it has
e_{\mathfrak{s}}=1
and its only intersection points are loops or linking chains to other principal components.
∎

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
X
be a curve over a local field
K
and let
\mathscr{X}
be a regular snc model of
X
. Then
X
has a
K
-rational point if and only if
\mathscr{X}_{k}
has a component of multiplicity
1
which is fixed by Frobenius and has a smooth
k
-rational point.

Proof.

By
[
9
, Corollary 9.1.32]
there is a reduction map
\mathrm{red}:X(K)\rightarrow\mathscr{X}_{k}(k)
landing in the smooth locus of
\mathscr{X}_{k}
, which is onto since
K
is henselian. Therefore
X(K)
is empty if and only if the smooth locus of
\mathscr{X}_{k}(k)
is empty. Since the smooth locus of
\mathscr{X}_{k}(k)
consists of points lying on components of multiplicity
1
which are fixed by Frobenius, the result follows.
∎

the character

,

For brevity, call components of multiplicity

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
C
be a hyperelliptic curve with tame reduction over a local field
K
with residue field
k
of characteristic
p>2
. Suppose that
|k|=q
is such that
q>2(g(C)^{2}-1)
. Then
C
has a
K
-rational point in precisely the cases described in Theorem
2.3
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.1
, any smooth components of the special fibre fixed by Frobenius have a
k
-point. By Theorem
2.3
, a smooth component fixed by Frobenius exists and hence by Proposition
2.2

C
has a
K
-rational point.
∎

Appendix A
Cluster Pictures

We briefly collate some definitions concerning clusters for the convenience of the reader.

Definition A.1
.

Let
C:y^{2}=f(x)
be a hyperelliptic curve over
K
, with
\mathcal{R}
the set of roots of
f
. A
cluster
is a non-empty subset
\mathfrak{s}\subseteq\mathcal{R}
of the form
\mathfrak{s}=D\cap\mathcal{R}
for some disc
D=z+\pi^{n}\mathcal{O}_{K}
, where
z\in\overline{K}
and
n\in\mathbb{Q}
. If
\mathfrak{s}
is a cluster and
|\mathfrak{s}|>1
,
\mathfrak{s}
is a
proper
cluster and we define its
depth

d_{\mathfrak{s}}=\min_{r,r^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{s}}v_{K}(r-r^{\prime}).

The
cluster picture

\Sigma
is the set of all clusters of the roots of
f
.

Definition A.2
.

A cluster
\mathfrak{s}\in\Sigma_{\chi}
is
odd
(resp.
even
) if
|\mathfrak{s}|
is odd (resp. even). A
child

\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}<\mathfrak{s}
is a maximal subcluster of
\mathfrak{s}
, and its
relative depth
is
\delta_{\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}=d_{\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}-d_{\mathfrak{s}}
. The cluster
\mathfrak{s}
is
übereven
if all its children are even. It is a
twin
if
|\mathfrak{s}|=2
and
principal
if
|\mathfrak{s}|\geq 3
, except if either
\mathfrak{s}=\mathcal{R}
is even and has exactly two children, or if
\mathfrak{s}
has a child of size
2g
. It is a
cotwin
if it has a child of size
2g
whose complement is not a twin.

Definition A.3
.

Let
D
be a
p
-adic disk. Then

\nu_{D}(f)=v_{K}(c_{f})+\sum_{r\in\mathcal{R}}\min(d_{D},v_{K}(z_{D}-r)),

and for a cluster
\mathfrak{s}
,
\nu_{\mathfrak{s}}=\nu_{D(\mathfrak{s})}
.

Definition A.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{s}
be a proper cluster. Then
e_{\mathfrak{s}}\in\mathbb{Z}
is the smallest integer such that
e_{\mathfrak{s}}d_{\mathfrak{s}}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
e_{\mathfrak{s}}\nu_{\mathfrak{s}}\in 2\mathbb{Z}
.

Definition A.5
.

Let
\mathfrak{s}\in\Sigma
be a proper cluster. Then the
genus
of
\mathfrak{s}
,
g(\mathfrak{s})
, is such that
2g(\mathfrak{s})+1
or
2g(\mathfrak{s})+2
equals the number of odd children of
\mathfrak{s}
.

Definition A.6

(Invariants and Characters)
.

For
\sigma\in\mathrm{G}_{K}
let

\displaystyle\chi(\sigma)=\frac{\sigma(\pi)}{\pi}\mod\mathfrak{m}.

For a proper cluster
\mathfrak{s}\in\Sigma_{C}
define

\displaystyle\lambda_{\mathfrak{s}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\nu_{\mathfrak{s}}}{2}-d_{\mathfrak{s}}\sum_{\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}<\mathfrak{s}}\bigg\lfloor\frac{|\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}|}{2}\bigg\rfloor.

Define
\theta_{\mathfrak{s}}=\sqrt{c_{f}\prod_{r\notin\mathfrak{s}}(z_{\mathfrak{s}}-r)}.
If
\mathfrak{s}
is either even or a cotwin, we define
\epsilon_{\mathfrak{s}}:\mathrm{G}_{K}\to\{\pm 1\}
by

\epsilon_{\mathfrak{s}}(\sigma)\equiv\frac{\sigma(\theta_{\mathfrak{s}^{*}})}{\theta_{(\sigma\mathfrak{s})^{*}}}\mod\mathfrak{m}.

For all other clusters
\mathfrak{s}
set
\epsilon_{\mathfrak{s}}(\sigma)=0
.

Definition A.7
.

Let
\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}<\mathfrak{s}
be clusters in
\Sigma
. Then
\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}
is a
stable child
of
\mathfrak{s}
if the stabiliser of
\mathfrak{s}
also stabilises
\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}
.

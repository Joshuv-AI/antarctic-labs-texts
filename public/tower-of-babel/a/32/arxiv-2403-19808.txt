Twisted simplicial distributions
Cihan Okay, Walker H. Stern

Abstract
We introduce a theory of twisted simplicial distributions on simplicial principal bundles, which allow us to capture Bell’s non-locality, and the more general notion of quantum contextuality. We leverage the classical theory of simplicial principal bundles, as well as structures on categories of such bundles, to provide powerful computational tools for analyzing twisted distributions in terms of both direct constructions in simplicial sets and techniques from homological algebra. We use these techniques to analyze our key examples: quantum distributions and operator-theoretic polytopes used in the classical simulation of quantum computation.

]

In

In this work, we introduce simplicial distributions that are

The main examples of twisted distributions come from quantum distributions. There is a quantum version of the probability monad where the semiring

We introduce the notion of contextuality for twisted distributions in Section

[

]

A deterministic simplicial distribution is of the form of a delta distribution peaked at a section of the bundle. Non-contextual distributions are those that can be expressed as a probabilistic mixture of deterministic distributions. Those that cannot are called contextual.

We discuss natural examples of such morphisms.
Twisting can be used as an effective tool to simplify the measurement space for studying contextuality. More precisely, we have a “twist and collapse” result (Theorem

There is a convex isomorphism

This result has important computational applications, mainly for the computation of the vertices of the polytope of twisted distributions.
In the case of
2

[

]

2
Simplicial principal bundles

In this section, we will briefly recapitulate the theory of simplicial principal bundles. In most cases the theory is wholly analogous to that of principal bundles over a topological group, however, the details of implementation often differ. We will attempt to make clear the parallels in the general theory while presenting these details here. One important way in which the presentation here differs from the original topological setting is that we only consider
left
actions, rather than the right actions more common in the setting of topological principal bundles. In the Abelian setting where much of our work will take place, this distinction evaporates, and so the reader familiar with, e.g.
[
May67
, §18]
may safely read those later sections without reference to these.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{[X,\overline{W}K]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\eta}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{\theta^{\eta}}}

Lemma 2.15
.

The levelwise sum of two twisting functions
\eta
and
\eta^{\prime}
is a twisting function.

Proof.

Given
x\in X_{n}
, we have

\displaystyle d_{0}((\eta+\eta^{\prime})_{n}(x))

\displaystyle=d_{0}(\eta_{n}(x)+\eta^{\prime}_{n}(x))

\displaystyle=d_{0}(\eta_{n}(x))+d_{0}(\eta^{\prime}_{n}(x))

\displaystyle=\eta_{n-1}(d_{1}(x))-\eta_{n-1}(d_{0}(x))+\eta^{\prime}_{n-1}(d_{1}(x))-\eta^{\prime}_{n-1}(d_{0}(x))

\displaystyle=(\eta+\eta^{\prime})_{n-1}(d_{1}(x))-(\eta+\eta^{\prime})_{n-1}(d_{0}(x)).

For
1\leq i\leq n
, we have

\displaystyle d_{i}((\eta+\eta^{\prime})_{n}(x))

\displaystyle=d_{i}(\eta_{n}(x)+\eta^{\prime}_{n}(x))

\displaystyle=d_{i}(\eta_{n}(x))+d_{i}(\eta^{\prime}_{n}(x))

\displaystyle=\eta_{n-1}(d_{i+1}(x))+\eta^{\prime}_{n-1}(d_{i+1}(x))

\displaystyle=(\eta+\eta^{\prime})_{n-1}(d_{i+1}(x)).

For the degeneracy maps we have

(\eta+\eta^{\prime})_{n}(s_{0}(x))=\eta_{n}(s_{0}(x))+\eta^{\prime}_{n}(s_{0}(x))=0,

and for
1\leq j\leq n+1
,

\displaystyle(\eta+\eta^{\prime})_{n}(s_{j}(x))

\displaystyle=\eta_{n}(s_{j}(x))+\eta^{\prime}_{n}(s_{j}(x))

\displaystyle=s_{j-1}(\eta_{n-1}(x))+s_{j-1}(\eta^{\prime}_{n-1}(x))

\displaystyle=s_{j-1}((\eta+\eta^{\prime})_{n-1}(x)).

∎

Lemma 2.16
.

If
K
is a simplicial Abelian group, then
\operatorname{Twist}_{K}(X)
is an Abelian group.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.15

the sum is well-defined. The associativity follows from the associativity in
K_{n}
for every
n\geq 0
. The identity element is the twisting function that send every

to the identity elment of
K_{n-1}
. The inverse of
\eta
is given by
-\eta
.
∎

2.3
The theory for a simplicial Abelian group

We now specialize to the case of a simplicial Abelian group.
Let

. We denote by

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M:}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{Ab}_{\Delta}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{Ch}^{+}}

the Dold-Kan correspondence (see, e.g.,

whose group of

-simplices is

The classic theorem of Dold and Kan says that

and

define an equivalence of categories. We will leverage this to connect principal bundles to cohomology groups as follows.

Proposition 2.19
.

There is an isomorphism of chain complexes

M({\overline{W}A})\cong(MA)[-1]

natural in
A
, or, equivalently, a natural isomorphism

{\overline{W}A}\cong\Gamma(MA[-1]).

This is the content of
[

, Eq. 4.58]
.

Denote by

the chain complex of maps between the chain complexes

and
D_{\bullet}
. Denote by
\underline{\mathsf{Ab}_{\Delta}}(K,L)
the simplicial Abelian group of maps between simplicial Abelian groups

and

.

Proposition 2.20
.

Let
C_{\bullet}
and
D_{\bullet}
be chain complexes. There is a natural equivalence of simplicial Abelian groups

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Gamma\left(\underline{\mathsf{Ch}^{+}}(C_{\bullet},D_{\bullet})\right)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\underline{\mathsf{Ab}_{\Delta}}(\Gamma(C_{\bullet}),\Gamma(D_{\bullet})).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}

Proof.

This is
[
Opa21
, Thm 6.3.1]
.
∎

Corollary 2.21
.

There is a natural isomorphism of groups

\pi_{0}\left(\underline{\mathsf{Ab}_{\Delta}}(\Gamma(C_{\bullet}),\Gamma(D_{\bullet}))\right)\cong H_{0}(\underline{\mathsf{Ch}^{+}}(C_{\bullet},D_{\bullet})).

Proof.

Under the Dold-Kan correspondence, simplicial homotopy corresponds to homology. See, e.g.,
[
Wei94
, Thm 8.4.1]
for details.
∎

If we denote by

the free simplicial Abelian group functor

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{Set}_{\Delta}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{Ab}_{\Delta}}

and note that it is left adjoint to the forgetful functor

from simplicial Abelian groups to simplicial sets, we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 2.22
.

There is a natural isomorphism of groups

[X,{\overline{W}A}]\cong H_{-1}(\underline{\mathsf{Ch}^{+}}(M(\mathbb{Z}[X]),M(A))).

Proof.

This amounts to chaining together the isomorphisms of
2.21
and
2.19
, with the isomorphism

More explicitly, we have

\displaystyle=\pi_{0}(\underline{\mathsf{Set}_{\Delta}}(X,\overline{W}A))

\displaystyle\cong\pi_{0}(\underline{\mathsf{Ab}_{\Delta}}(\mathbb{Z}[X],\overline{W}A))

\displaystyle\cong\pi_{0}(\underline{\mathsf{Ab}_{\Delta}}(\mathbb{Z}[X],\Gamma(MA[-1])))

\displaystyle\cong H_{0}(\underline{\mathsf{Ch^{+}}}(M(\mathbb{Z}[X]),MA[-1]))

\displaystyle\cong H_{0}(\underline{\mathsf{Ch^{+}}}(M(\mathbb{Z}[X]),MA)[-1])

\displaystyle\cong H_{-1}(\underline{\mathsf{Ch^{+}}}(M(\mathbb{Z}[X]),MA)).

∎

Theorem 2.23
.

For a simplicial Abelian group
K
, the isomorphisms

\operatorname{Twist}_{K}(X)_{/\sim}\cong[X,\overline{W}K]\cong H_{-1}(\underline{\mathsf{Ch}^{+}}(M(\mathbb{Z}[X]),M(A)))

of Corollaries

and

define an isomorphism of groups, where
\operatorname{Twist}_{K}(X)_{/\sim}
is equipped with the levelwise sum of twisting functions.

Proof.

It is immediate from construction that the bijection

sends the levelwise sum of maps to the levelwise sum of twisting functions. Thus, the latter must define a group structure on

isomorphic to that on

. Corollary
2.22
completes the proof.
∎

2.4
The case of an Abelian group

We now specialize even further. Let

denote

Proposition 2.24
.

Proof.

This is simply an application of Corollary

In this case, we can trace through the equivalences to obtain an explicit 2-cocycle representing a twisting function. Given a twisting function

Applying the normalized chain complex functor

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces x}

To turn this into a cocycle on

, Chapter 2, Thm 2.1]

On the other hand, we have

When dealing with the nerve of an Abelian group, we can strengthen the correspondence between statements (1) and (3) in Proposition

to a statement about cochains and cocycles. To this end, let
\gamma:X_{2}\to H
be a normalized 2-cocycle, and let

be the corresponding twisting function, as described in Lemma

such that

, we construct a section of the corresponding (trivial)

-bundle

as follows.

By Lemma

in the appendix,

is relatively 2-coskeletal over

. Thus, it suffices for us to construct a map of 2-truncations

over
\operatorname{tr}_{2}(X)
by Lemma

. We define a map

for

by

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\phi_{1}(x)

\displaystyle=\alpha(x)

\displaystyle\phi_{2}(x)

\displaystyle=(\alpha(d_{2}x),\alpha(d_{1}x)-\alpha(d_{2}x)).

Denote by

the set of normalized

Proposition 2.27
.

For a twisting function
\eta
corresponding to a normalized 2-cocycle

, the map

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{No}(X,H,\gamma)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Sec}(\pi_{\eta})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi_{\alpha}}

Proof.

We first must check that
\varphi_{\alpha}
is simplicial on the 2-truncation. We first check the face maps

\displaystyle d_{i}(\phi_{2}(x),x)

\displaystyle=d_{i}((\alpha(d_{2}x),\alpha(d_{1}x)-\alpha(d_{2}x)),x)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}(\alpha(d_{1}(x))-\alpha(d_{2}(x))+\eta_{2}(x),d_{0}x)&i=0\\

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}(\alpha(d_{0}(x)),d_{0}(x))&i=0\\

\displaystyle=\alpha(d_{i}(x))

The relations with the face maps when

are immediate, since

is a singleton.

We then check the relations with the degeneracies. For

is normalized. For

\displaystyle=(\alpha(d_{2}s_{0}(x)),\alpha(d_{1}s_{0}(x))-\alpha(d_{2}s_{0}(x)))

\displaystyle=(\alpha(s_{0}d_{1}(x)),\alpha(x)-\alpha(s_{0}d_{1}(x)))

\displaystyle=(0,\alpha(x))=s_{0}(\phi_{2}(x))

by the simplicial identities and the normalization of

\displaystyle=(\alpha(d_{2}s_{1}(x)),\alpha(d_{1}s_{1}(x))-\alpha(d_{2}s_{1}(x)))

and the function

2.5
The tensor product of principal bundles

We now return to the case of a generic simplicial Abelian group. Let
K
be a simplicial Abelian group, and
X\in\mathsf{Set}_{\Delta}
a simplicial set. Throughout, we write
K
multiplicatively, to better accord with the notation for group actions. Let

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{E}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{F}}

be two principal
K
-bundles over
X
. Define an equivalence relation on the set
E\times_{X}F
by setting
(ke,f)\sim(e,kf)
for all
k\in K
,
e\in E
, and
f\in F
, and let

E\otimes_{K}F:=(E\times_{X}F)_{/\sim}.

Note that, equivalently, the equivalence relation is
(e,f)\sim(x,y)
if and only if there exists
k\in K
such that
(ke,k^{-1}f)=(x,y)
. Also note that since
E
and
F
are principal bundles, if such a
k
exists, it is unique. This construction precisely mirrors that of the tensor product of principal
A
-bundles for a topological Abelian group
A
. See, for instance
[
BSST06
, Example 2.2.4]
for details.

Proposition 2.28
.

The canonical map
\pi_{E\otimes F}:E\otimes_{K}F\to X
is a principal
K
-bundle.

Proof.

We first note that, given a map
\phi:[n]\to[m]
in the simplex category and
(k,e,f)\in K\times(E\times_{X}F)
, we have

\displaystyle\phi^{\ast}(k\cdot e,f)

\displaystyle=(\phi^{\ast}(k\cdot e),\phi^{\ast}(f))

\displaystyle=(\phi^{\ast}(k)\cdot\phi^{\ast}(e),\phi^{\ast}(f))

\displaystyle\sim(\phi^{\ast}(e),\phi^{\ast}(k)\cdot\phi^{\ast}(f))

\displaystyle=(\phi^{\ast}(e),\phi^{\ast}(k\cdot f))

\displaystyle=\phi^{\ast}(e,k\cdot f)

so that the simplicial structure on
E\times_{X}F
descends to
E\otimes_{K}F
. Moreover, since multiplication by elements of
k
occurs fiberwise, the map
\pi_{E\otimes F}
to
X
remains well defined.

We then show that
E\otimes_{K}F
carries a free
K
action with quotient
X
. The action is defined by

k\cdot[e,f]=[ke,f]

and is clearly simplicial. As above, it is immediate that the canonical map
\pi_{E\otimes F}:E\otimes_{K}F\to X
is
K
-equivariant.

To see that the action is free and transitive on fibers, suppose we are given
[e,f],[x,y]\in E\otimes_{K}F
such that
\pi_{E\otimes F}(e,f)=\pi_{E\otimes F}(x,y)
. Then choose
k\in K
and
h\in H
such that

ke=x\quad\text{and}\quad hf=y.

Then we see that

(h^{-1}ke,f)\sim(x,y)

so the action is transitive on fibers.
On the other hand, suppose that
h,k\in K
such that
(he,f)\sim(x,y)
and
(ke,f)\sim(x,y)
. Then, by the definition of the equivalence relation
\sim
, there is a unique
g\in K
such that
(gke,g^{-1}f)=(ghe,g^{-1}f)
. Since the action of
K
on
F
is free, this implies
g=1
, and so
ke=he
. Since the action of
K
on
E
is free, this implies
k=h
, and so the action of
K
on
E\otimes_{K}F
is free.
∎

Proposition 2.29
.

The cohomology class in
H_{-1}(\underline{\mathsf{C}^{+}}(M(\mathbb{Z}[X]),M(K)))
corresponding to
E\otimes_{K}F
is the sum of the cohomology classes associated to
E
and
F
, respectively.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.23
, it is sufficient to compute this on bundles of the form
K\times_{\eta}X
for a twisting function
\eta
, since every
K
-principal bundle is isomorphic to such a one. We note that when
E=K\times_{\eta}X
and
F=K\times_{\rho}X
, we can identify
E\otimes_{K}F
with the set of triples of the form
(k,1,x)
where
k\in K
and
x\in X
. We can compute the face and degeneracy maps, which are elementwise those of
K
and
X
, except for
d_{0}
. In that case, we have

d_{0}(k,1,x)=(d_{0}(k)\eta(x),1\rho(x),d_{0}(x))\sim(d_{0}(k)\eta(x)\rho(x),1,d_{0}(x)).

So that a twisting function associated to
E\otimes_{K}F
is
\eta\rho
. Since the group operation on twisting functions is identified with the group operation on cohomology, the proposition follows.
∎

In the case of a general simplicial Abelian group
K
, the same reasoning as in the proposition allows us to define a canonical isomorphism

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(K\times_{\eta}X)\otimes_{K}(K\times_{\xi}X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K\times_{\eta\xi}X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{[(k,x),(h,x)]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(kh,x)}

which will be of use when we discuss monoidal functors.

Recall that we denote by
\mathsf{Bun}_{K}(X)
the groupoid of principal
K
-bundles over
X
.

Theorem 2.30
.

The product
-\otimes_{K}-
equips
\mathsf{Bun}_{K}(X)
with the structure of a symmetric monoidal groupoid.

Proof.

This is the content of Theorem
A.3
in the appendix.
∎

As it turns out,
\mathsf{Bun}_{K}(X)
is more than simply a groupoid with a monoidal structure; it is a 2-group. See, e.g.,
[
BL04
]
for further details.

Corollary 2.31
.

The category
\mathsf{Bun}_{K}(X)
is a commutative
2
-group.

Proof.

We need only see that every object
E\to X
has an inverse up to isomorphism under the tensor product. But since the tensor product induces the same monoid structure on
\pi_{0}(\mathsf{Bun}_{K}(X))
as the bijection with
H^{2}(X,K)
, it is immediate that this property holds.
∎

Since
\mathsf{Bun}_{K}(X)
is a commutative 2-group, it can be equipped with a symmetric monoidal automorphism
\mathcal{I}:\mathsf{Bun}_{K}(X)\to\mathsf{Bun}_{K}(X)
which sends each bundle
E\to X
to a weak inverse
\overline{E}\to X
under
\otimes_{K}
. However, if we restrict our attention to bundles given by twisted products — i.e., restrict to the full monoidal subcategory
\mathsf{Bun}_{K}^{\operatorname{t}}(X)
on the twisted products
K\times_{\eta}X
— this duality may be given the computationally convenient form

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K\times_{\eta}X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K\times_{\eta^{-1}}X.}

In general, there is no
bundle
isomorphism between a bundle
E\to X
and its inverse
\overline{E}\to X
. However, as the next lemma shows, there is a
simplicial
isomorphism between
K\times_{\eta}X
and
K\times_{\eta^{-1}}X
.

Lemma 2.32
.

Let
K
be a simplicial Abelian group, and
\eta
a
K
-valued twisting function on
X
. Then the map

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f:}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K\times_{\eta}X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K\times_{\eta^{-1}}X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(k,x)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(k^{-1},x)}

is an isomorphism of simplicial sets over
X
.

Proof.

Since the structure maps
d_{i}
for
i>0
and
s_{i}
act as homomorphisms on the
K
component, it is immediate that they commute with
f
. It thus suffices for us to check that
f
commutes with
d_{0}
. We then compute

f(d_{0}^{\eta}(k,x))=f(d_{0}(k)\eta(x),d_{0}(x))=((d_{0}(k)\eta(x))^{-1},x)=d_{0}^{\eta^{-1}}(k^{-1},x)=d_{0}^{\eta^{-1}}(f(k,x)),

where we denote by

the face map in
K\times_{\eta}X
. Since the same formula provides an inverse to
f
, the lemma is proven.
∎

Note that the map
f
is not
K
-equivariant, but rather equivariant along the inverse map for
K
. Thus, while it is an isomorphism of simplicial sets over
X
, it is not an isomorphism of bundles.

, this means that there is an

applying this to the element

, we see that this implies

is specified at every

for every

for every

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces y}

p:\Delta^{n}\to D_{R}(\Delta^{n}\times_{X}E)
are in bijection with
n
-simplices of
D_{R}(\Delta^{n}\times_{X}E)
, which are in turn elements of

D_{R}((\Delta^{n}\times_{X}E)_{n}).

This bijection is given explicitly by sending
p
to
p_{u(\sigma)}
, i.e., evaluating on the unique non-degenerate
n
-simplex.

Simplicial distibutions are precisely those
p
for which the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D_{R}(E\times_{X}\Delta^{n})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta^{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D_{R}(\Delta^{n})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D_{R}(\pi)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\delta_{\Delta^{n}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}

commutes, i.e., such that the support of
p_{u(\sigma)}
lies in
\pi_{n}^{-1}(u)
, i.e.,
D_{R}(F_{\pi}(u))
. The naturality of this bijection is immediate from the definitions.
∎

From this lemma, we immediately obtain a characterization of simplicial distributions as a corollary.

Corollary 3.9
.

Let
X
be a simplicial set, and
\pi:E\to X
a principal
K
-bundle. The canonical map

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{sDist}(\pi)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lim_{(\Delta_{/X})^{\operatorname{op}}}D_{R}\circ F_{\pi}}

is a bijection. In particular, if
X
has finitely many non-degenerate simplices and
R=\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, then
\operatorname{sDist}(\pi)
is a convex polytope with finitely many vertices.

Proof.

Since every simplicial set
X
is a colimit over its category of simplices,
Proposition
3.6
shows that the canonical map

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{sDist}(\pi)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lim_{\Delta_{/X}^{\operatorname{op}}}D_{\pi}}

is an isomorphism. By Lemma
3.8
, however, we see that

\lim_{\Delta_{/X}^{\operatorname{op}}}D_{\pi}\cong\lim_{\Delta_{/X}^{\operatorname{op}}}D_{R}\circ F_{\pi}.

When
X
is a finite simplicial set and
R=\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, Lemma
B.2
in the appendix shows that we may, instead, compute the limit as the limit over
\left((\Delta_{/X})^{\leq n}\right)^{\operatorname{op}}
. Since this category is finite, we may express the limit as an equalizer of finite products:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{sDist}(\pi)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\prod\limits_{\sigma}D(F_{\pi}(\sigma))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\prod\limits_{\psi:\sigma\to\tau}D(F_{\pi}(\tau))}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces s}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces t}

where the former product is indexed over objects of
\left((\Delta_{/X})^{\leq n}\right)^{\operatorname{op}}
, and the latter is indexed over morphisms of
\left((\Delta_{/X})^{\leq n}\right)^{\operatorname{op}}
. Since each of the
D(F_{\pi}(\sigma))
are polytopes with finitely many vertices, this means that
\operatorname{sDist}(\pi)
is a subspace of a finite product of such polytopes, specified by finitely many linear equations. Thus,
\operatorname{sDist}(\pi)
is a convex polytope with finitely many vertices, as desired.
∎

We now make use of the monoidal structure on principal bundles to define a global structure on simplicial distributions on

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces m}

3.5
Equivariant distributions

We now provide a general construction of a morphism of bundle scenarios associated to a pair of bundles
\pi_{E}:E\to X
and
\pi_{F}:F\to X
.

Fix a simplicial Abelian group
K
. We will write the operation of
K
multiplicatively to better accord with the standard notation for actions of
K
on other simplicial sets.

Definition 3.21
.

Let
\pi_{E}:E\to X
be a principal
K
-bundle, and let
a,b\in\pi_{E}^{-1}(x)
be elements in the same fiber. We define
ba^{-1}\in K
to be the unique element such that

ba^{-1}\cdot a=b.

Lemma 3.22
.

Let
\pi_{E}:E\to X
be a principal
K
-bundle, and let
a,b,c\in\pi_{E}^{-1}(x)
be elements in the same fiber. Then the following computation rules hold.

1.

For
k\in K
,
(k\cdot b)a^{-1}=k(ba^{-1})
.

2.

(ba^{-1})(ac^{-1})=bc^{-1}
.

3.

(ba^{-1})^{-1}=ab^{-1}
.

4.

aa^{-1}=1
.

Proof.

If
h\cdot a=b
, then
(kh)\cdot a=kb
. By the freeness of the
K
-action, this implies
(k\cdot b)a^{-1}=k(ba^{-1})
, i.e., rule (1) holds. Similarly, if
kc=a
and
ha=b
, then
hkc=b
, so that rule (2) holds. Rule (3) is a consequence of (2) and the uniqueness of inverses. Rule (4) holds since
1\cdot a=a
for any
a\in E
.
∎

Given two principal
K
-bundles
\pi_{E}:E\to X
and
\pi_{F}:F\to X
, we can form the commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E\times_{X}F}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E\otimes_{K}F}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{FE}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{EF}}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{F}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{F}}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{E}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{FE}}

.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}

In this section, we will show that various functors and categories used in the main body are (lax) (symmetric) monoidal.

Proposition A.1
.

The functor

is lax symmetric monoidal with respect to the cartesian monoidal structure. The structure maps are

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D(X)\times D(Y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D(X\times Y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ast}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D(\ast)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces m_{X,Y}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varepsilon}

where
\ast
denotes the singleton, and
\epsilon
is the unique isomorphism between singletons.

Proof.

We first note that
m
is natural. Given maps of sets
f:X\to Z
and
g:Y\to W
and
(p,q)\in D(X)\times D(Y)
we have

\displaystyle m(D(f)\times D(g)(p,q))(z,w)

\displaystyle=D(f)(p)(z)\cdot D(g)(q)(w)

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{x\in f^{-1}(z)}p(x)\right)\cdot\left(\sum_{y\in g^{-1}(w)}q(y)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in f^{-1}(z)\\
y\in g^{-1}(w)\end{subarray}}p(x)q(y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{(x,y)\in(f\times g)^{-1}(z,w)}p(x)q(y)

\displaystyle=D(f\times g)(m(p,q))(z,w).

We then check associativity. Given
(p,q,r)\in D(X)\times D(Y)\times D(Z)
, we have

m((m\times\operatorname{id}_{D(Z)})(p,(q,r)))((x,y),z)=(p(x)q(y))r(z)

and

m((\operatorname{id}_{D(X)}\times m)((p,q),r))(x,(y,z))=p(x)(q(y)r(z))

and so the associativity of multiplication completes the argument. Unitality is immediate, and symmetry follows from the symmetry of multiplication on
[0,1]
.
∎

\sigma\in X_{k}
be a
k
-simplex of
X
. It will suffice for us to show that
(\Delta_{/X})^{\leq n}\times_{\Delta_{/X}}(\Delta_{/X})_{\sigma/}
is non-empty and connected. If
k\leq n
, then this is immediate, since
(\Delta_{/X})^{\leq n}\times_{\Delta_{/X}}(\Delta_{/X})_{\sigma/}
is then canonically isomorphic to
\left((\Delta_{/X})^{\leq n}\right)_{\sigma/}
.

In the case where
k\geq n
, let
(\phi,\tau)
be the unique decomposition of
\sigma
guaranteed by the Eilenber-Zilber Lemma
B.1
. Then
\phi:\sigma\to\tau
is an element of
(\Delta_{/X})^{\leq n}\times_{\Delta_{/X}}(\Delta_{/X})_{\sigma/}
. Now suppose there is another element
\psi:\sigma\to\rho
.

We can factor
\psi=\psi_{2}\circ\psi_{1}
, where
\psi_{2}
is injective, and
\psi_{1}
is surjective. Then
\psi_{1}^{\ast}(\psi_{2}^{\ast}(\rho))=\sigma
. However, since
\psi_{2}
is injective, this means that the dimension of
\psi_{2}^{\ast}(\rho)
is less than or equal to that of
\rho
. Thus
\psi_{2}^{\ast}(\rho)
is an object in
(\Delta_{/X})^{\leq n}
, and so
\psi_{1}:\sigma\to\psi_{2}^{\ast}(\rho)
defines an object in
(\Delta_{/X})^{\leq n}\times_{\Delta_{/X}}(\Delta_{/X})_{\sigma/}

Then we see that the commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi_{2}^{\ast}(\rho)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi_{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi}

defines a morphism in
(\Delta_{/X})^{\leq n}\times_{\Delta_{/X}}(\Delta_{/X})_{\sigma/}
. To show that
(\Delta_{/X})^{\leq n}\times_{\Delta_{/X}}(\Delta_{/X})_{\sigma/}
is connected, it thus suffices to show that there is a morphism relating
\phi:\sigma\to\tau
to
\psi_{1}:\sigma\to\psi_{2}^{\ast}(\rho)
.

If
\psi_{2}^{\ast}(\rho)
is non-degenerate, then the uniqueness of the representation
(\phi,\tau)
for
\sigma
implies that
\psi_{2}^{\ast}(\rho)=\tau
and
\psi_{1}=\phi
, so we are done. Otherwise, let
(\alpha,\gamma
be the unique representation of
\psi_{2}^{\ast}(\rho)
guaranteed by the Eilenberg-Zilber Lemma
B.1
. Then note that
\alpha\circ\psi_{1}
is surjective,
\gamma
is non-degenerate, and

(\alpha\circ\psi_{1})^{\ast}(\gamma)=\psi_{1}^{\ast}(\alpha^{\ast}(\gamma))=\psi_{1}^{\ast}(\psi_{2}^{\ast}(\rho))=\sigma.

Thus, the uniqueness guaranteed by the Eilenberg-Zilber Lemma
B.1
implies that
\alpha\circ\psi_{1}=\phi
and
\gamma=\tau
. We thus have a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi_{2}^{\ast}(\rho)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gamma}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi}

which defines a morphism in
(\Delta_{/X})^{\leq n}\times_{\Delta_{/X}}(\Delta_{/X})_{\sigma/}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Appendix C
Relative coskeletalness

We first recall classic coskeletalness.

Definition C.1
.

For
k\geq 0
, the category
\Delta_{\leq k}
is the full subcategory on the objects
[n]
with
n\leq k
. The
k
th
truncation functor
is the functor

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{tr}_{k}:}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{sSet}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Fun}(\Delta_{\leq k}^{\operatorname{op}},\mathsf{Set})}

given by restriction along the inclusion
\Delta_{\leq k}\to\Delta
. The right adjoint to
\operatorname{tr}_{k}
, given explicitly by right Kan extension, is called the
k
th
coskeleton functor
, and denoted by

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{cosk}_{k}:}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Fun}(\Delta_{\leq k}^{\operatorname{op}},\mathsf{Set})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{sSet}.}

Since
\operatorname{cosk}_{k}
is the right Kan extension along a full subcategory inclusion, the adjunction counit
\operatorname{tr}_{k}(\operatorname{cosk}_{k}(X))\to X
is an isomorphism. We will typically use the notation

\mathsf{sSet}_{\leq k}=\operatorname{Fun}(\Delta_{\leq k}^{\operatorname{op}},\mathsf{Set}).

Definition C.2
.

A simplicial set
X
is called
k
-coskeletal
if, for every
n>k
every extension problem

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\partial\Delta^{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta^{n}}

has a unique solution.

Proposition C.3
.

The adjunction unit
X\to\operatorname{cosk}_{k}(\operatorname{tr}_{k}(X))
is an isomorphism if and only if
X
is
k
-coskeletal. Thus,
\operatorname{cosk}_{k}
is fully faithful, and its essential image consists of the
k
-coskeletal simplicial sets.

Proof.

This is a classical fact. A proof may be found in, e.g.,
[
Def19
, Lemma 10.11]
.
∎

We can then turn to the relative case.

Definition C.4
.

Let
\pi:X\to Y
be a map of simplicial sets. We call
X

\pi
-relative
k
-coskeletal
(or we call
\pi

relatively
k
-coskeletal
) if, for any
n>k
, every lifting problem

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\partial\Delta^{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta^{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}

has a unique solution.

We now concentrate on principal bundles with structure group
N(H)
for
H
an Abelian group.

Lemma C.5
.

Let
X
be a simplicial set, and let
\eta
be a
N(H)
-valued twisting function on
X
. Then
\pi_{\eta}:E_{\eta}\to X
is relatively 2-coskeletal.

Proof.

Solving a lifting problem

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\partial\Delta^{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E_{\eta}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta^{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{\eta}}

is equivalent to solving the lifting problem

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\partial\Delta^{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E_{\eta}\times_{X}\Delta^{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta^{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta^{n}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces=}

Since every principal bundle over
\Delta^{n}
is trivial, this is equivalent to solving the lifting problem

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\partial\Delta^{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces N(H)\times\Delta^{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta^{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta^{n}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces=}

and thus, equivalent to showing that any diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\partial\Delta^{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces N(H)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta^{n}}

has a unique extension. However, since nerves of categories are always 2-coskeletal, this is immediate.
∎

Lemma C.6
.

Let
\pi_{E}:E\to X
and
\pi_{F}:F\to X
be maps, and suppose that
F
is
\pi_{F}
-relatively
k
-coskeletal. Suppose given a simplicial map
f:\operatorname{tr}_{k}(E)\to\operatorname{tr}_{k}(F)
over
\operatorname{tr}_{k}(X)
. Then
f
uniquely extends to a map of simplicial sets over
X
.

Proof.

It will suffice to show that
f
extends uniquely to the
(k+1)
-truncation. For each non-degenerate
(k+1)
simplex
\sigma:\Delta^{k+1}\to E
, we get an associated lifting problem

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\partial\Delta^{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta^{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f\circ\partial\sigma}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{F}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{E}\circ\sigma}

any
\widetilde{f}
extending
f
to the
(k+1)
truncation must send
\sigma
to a solution to this lifting problem. Since
F
is relatively
k
-coskeletal, such a solution is unique, and so there is at most one such
\widetilde{f}
. On the other hand, defining
\widetilde{f}(\sigma)
to be the unique solution to this lifting problem yields the desired extension.
∎

Corollary C.7
.

Let

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{E}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{F}}

be a map between relatively
k
-coskeletal simplicial sets over
X
. If
f
is an isomorphism on
k
-truncations, then
f
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

Since
f
is an isomorphism on
k
-truncations, the previous lemma implies its inverse on
k
-truncations has a unique extension to a map
g:F\to E
over
X
. The uniqueness of extensions immediately shows that
g\circ f=\operatorname{id}_{E}
and
f\circ g=\operatorname{id}_{F}
.
∎

To complete our discussion of relative coskeletalness, we can simply duplicate the characterization of coskeletal simplicial sets in terms of an adjunction. Since slices of presheaf categories are equivalent to presheaf categories on over-categories, we can identify

\mathsf{sSet}\simeq\operatorname{Fun}(\Delta_{/X}^{\operatorname{op}},\mathsf{Set})\quad\text{and}\quad\mathsf{sSet}_{\leq k}\simeq\operatorname{Fun}((\Delta_{\leq k})_{/\operatorname{tr}_{k}(X)}^{\operatorname{op}},\mathsf{Set}).

Definition C.8
.

The
k
th
relative truncation functor over
X
is the functor

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{tr}_{k}^{X}:}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{sSet}\simeq\operatorname{Fun}(\Delta_{/X}^{\operatorname{op}},\mathsf{Set})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Fun}((\Delta_{\leq k})_{/\operatorname{tr}_{k}(X)}^{\operatorname{op}},\mathsf{Set})\simeq\mathsf{sSet}_{\leq k}}

given by composing with the inclusion
(\Delta_{\leq k})_{/\operatorname{tr}_{k}(X)}\to\Delta_{/X}
. The
k
th
relative coskeleton functor over
X
is the right adjoint
\operatorname{cosk}_{k}^{X}
to
\operatorname{tr}_{k}^{X}
, given by right Kan extension. Note that the adjunction counit is an isomorphism.

Proposition C.9
.

For
X\in\mathsf{sSet}
,
\pi:E\to X
is relative
k
-coskeletal if and only if the adjunction unit

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{cosk}_{k}^{X}(\operatorname{tr}_{k}^{X}(E))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{tr}(\pi)}

is an isomorphism.

Proof.

Since the
k
th
truncation of
\partial\Delta^{n}\to\Delta^{n}
is an isomorphism whenever
n>k
, it is immediate that, for any
k
-truncated simplicial set
Y
over
\operatorname{tr}_{k}(X)
, the map
\operatorname{cosk}_{k}^{X}(Y)\to X
is relative
k
-coskeletal. Thus, if the adjunction unit is an isomorphism,
E
is relative
k
-coskeletal.

On the other hand, since the counit of the adjunction is an isomorphism, the induced map

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{tr}_{k}^{X}(E)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{tr}_{k}^{X}(\operatorname{cosk}_{k}^{X}(\operatorname{tr}_{k}^{X}(E)))}

is an isomorphism. Thus, by Cor
C.7
,
if
E
is relative
k
-coskeletal over
X
, the adjunction unit is an isomorphism.
∎

Corollary C.10
.

Let
X
be a simplicial set, and
\underline{\pi}:\underline{E}\to\operatorname{tr}_{k}(X)
be a
k
-truncated simplicial set over
E
. There is a relative
k
-coskeletal simplicial set
\pi:E\to X
over
X
whose
k
-truncation is
\underline{\pi}:\underline{E}\to\operatorname{tr}_{k}(X)
. Moreover,
E
is unique up to unique isomorphism over
X
.

Proof.

The uniqueness up to unique isomorphism is immediate from corollary
C.7
, and existence follows by applying
\operatorname{cosk}_{k}^{X}
.
∎

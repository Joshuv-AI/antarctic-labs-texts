On the time behavior of a porous thermoelastic system with only thermal dissipation given by Gurtin-Pipkin law
Afaf Ahmima, Abdelfeteh Fareh

Abstract
In the present paper we consider a porous thermoelastic system with only one dissipative mechanism generated by the heat conductivity modelled by the Gurtin-Pipkin thermal law. By the use of a semigroup approach and the Lumer-Phillips theorem we prove the existence of a unique solution. We introduce a stability number χ g \chi_{g} depends on the coefficients of the system, and establish an exponential stability result provided that χ g = 0 \chi_{g}=0 . Otherwise, if χ g ≠ 0 \chi_{g}\neq 0 , we prove the lack of exponential decay. Our result improves and generalizes the previous results in the literature obtained for Fourier’s and Cattaneo’s laws of thermal conductivity.

Theorem 2

.

Lemma 2
.

Let

be the operator defined on

by

(30)

\delta\left\|\eta\right\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}\leq-\int_{0}^{+\infty}\kappa^{\prime}(s)\left\|\eta_{x}(s)\right\|^{2}ds.

Proof.

\begin{array}[]{rl}2\langle T\eta,\eta\rangle_{\mathcal{V}}&=\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{+\infty}\kappa(s)\frac{d}{ds}\|\eta_{x}(s)\|^{2}ds\\
&=-\displaystyle\left.\kappa(s)\|\eta_{x}(s)\|^{2}\right|_{0}^{+\infty}+\int_{0}^{+\infty}\kappa^{\prime}(s)\|\eta_{x}(s)\|^{2}ds\end{array}

2\langle T\eta,\eta\rangle_{\mathcal{V}}=\displaystyle\lim_{s\rightarrow 0}\kappa(s)\|{\eta}_{x}(s)\|^{2}-\lim_{s\rightarrow\infty}\kappa(s)\|{\eta}_{x}(s)\|^{2}+\int_{0}^{+\infty}\kappa^{\prime}(s)\|\eta_{x}(s)\|^{2}ds.

(32)

\kappa\left(s\right)\|\eta_{x}\left(s\right)\|

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{s\rightarrow 0}\left(\int_{0}^{s}\kappa(s)^{1/2}\left\|{\eta}_{xs}(\tau)\right\|d\tau\right)^{2}.

The use of Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, leads to

\lim_{s\rightarrow 0}\kappa(s)\|{\eta}_{x}(s)\|^{2}\leq\limsup_{s\rightarrow 0}s\int_{0}^{s}\kappa(\tau)\left\|{\eta}_{xs}(\tau)\right\|^{2}d\tau=0.

The left-hand side of the last equality is bounded, and from (h2) both terms
of the right-hand side are non-positive, we infer that the above limit exists

\langle T\eta,\eta\rangle_{\mathcal{V}}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\kappa^{\prime}(s)\|\eta_{x}(s)\|^{2}ds,

Moreover, the use of the assumption (h4) on

, then

\|F_{n}\|_{\mathscr{H}}=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2\rho}}.

Let
U_{n}\in D(\mathscr{A})
be the solution of
(i\lambda_{n}I+\mathscr{A})U_{n}=F_{n}
, then

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}i\lambda_{n}u_{n}-v_{n}=&0\\
i\rho\lambda_{n}v_{n}-\mu u_{nxx}-b\phi_{nx}=&\sin(n\pi),\\
i\lambda_{n}\phi_{n}-\psi_{n}=&0,\\
iJ\lambda_{n}\psi_{n}-\alpha\phi_{nxx}+\xi\phi_{n}+bu_{nx}+\beta\theta_{nx}=&0,\\
ic\lambda_{n}\theta_{n}+\beta\psi_{nx}-\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}\kappa(s)\eta_{nxx}(s)ds=&0,\\
i\lambda_{n}\eta_{n}-\theta_{n}+\eta_{ns}=&0\end{array}\right.

(74)

Simplifying
v_{n},\psi_{n}
from (
74
), we obtain

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\rho\lambda_{n}^{2}u_{n}+\mu u_{nxx}+b\phi_{nx}=&-\sin(n\pi),\\
(J\lambda_{n}^{2}-\xi)\phi_{n}+\alpha\phi_{nxx}-bu_{nx}-\beta\theta_{nx}=&0,\\
ic\lambda_{n}\theta_{n}+i\lambda_{n}\beta\phi_{nx}-\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}\kappa(s)\eta_{nxx}(s)ds=&0,\\
i\lambda_{n}\eta_{n}+\theta_{n}-\eta_{ns}=&0\end{array}\right.

(75)

Taking into account the boundary conditions (
20
), we set

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}u_{n}=A_{n}\sin(nx),\\
\phi_{n}=B_{n}\cos(nx),\\
\theta_{n}=C_{n}\sin(nx),\\
\eta_{n}=D_{n}(s)\sin(nx),\end{array}\right.

(76)

where,
A_{n},B_{n},C_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
and
D_{n}:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
such that
\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\kappa(s)|D_{n}(s)|^{2}ds<0
, and
D_{n}(0)=0.
Plugging (
76
) into (
75
), we infer

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\left(\rho\lambda_{n}^{2}-\mu n^{2}\right)A_{n}-bnB_{n}=&1,\\
(J\lambda_{n}^{2}-\xi-\alpha n^{2})B_{n}-bnA_{n}-\beta nC_{n}=&0,\\
ic\lambda_{n}C_{n}-i\beta n\lambda_{n}B_{n}+n^{2}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}\kappa(s)D_{n}(s)ds=&0,\\
i\lambda_{n}D_{n}\left(s\right)+C_{n}-D_{n}^{\prime}\left(s\right)=&0.\end{array}\right.

(77)

Solving (
77
)
4
, we get

D_{n}\left(s\right)=\frac{C_{n}}{i\lambda_{n}}\left(1-e^{-i\lambda_{n}s}\right).

The substitution of
D_{n}\left(s\right)
into (
77
)
{}_{3},
entails

\begin{array}[]{rl}\left(\rho\lambda_{n}^{2}-\mu n^{2}\right)A_{n}-bnB_{n}&=-1\\
(J\lambda_{n}^{2}-\xi-\alpha n^{2})B_{n}-bnA_{n}-\beta nC_{n}&=0\\
\beta n\lambda_{n}^{2}B_{n}-\left[c\lambda_{n}^{2}-n^{2}\left(g\left(0\right)-\widehat{\kappa}\left(\lambda_{n}\right)\right)\right]C_{n}&=0\end{array}

which can be written as

\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}p_{1}\left(\lambda\right)&-bn&0\\
-bn&p_{2}\left(\lambda\right)&-\beta n\\
0&\beta n\lambda_{n}^{2}&p_{3}\left(\lambda\right)\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}A_{n}\\
B_{n}\\
C_{n}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}-1\\
0\\
0\end{array}\right)

(78)

where,

p_{1}\left(\lambda\right)=\left(\rho\lambda_{n}^{2}-\mu n^{2}\right),\text{ \ \ }p_{2}\left(\lambda\right)=(J\lambda_{n}^{2}-\xi-\alpha n^{2}),\text{ \ \ }p_{3}\left(\lambda\right)=-c\lambda_{n}^{2}+n^{2}\left(g\left(0\right)-\widehat{\kappa}\left(\lambda_{n}\right)\right).

It follows that

A_{n}=\frac{-K_{1}}{p_{1}\left(\lambda_{n}\right)K_{1}-K_{2}},

where

K_{1}=p_{2}\left(\lambda_{n}\right)p_{3}\left(\lambda_{n}\right)+\left(\beta n\lambda_{n}\right)^{2},\;K_{2}=\left(bn\right)^{2}p_{3}\left(\lambda_{n}\right).

Taking
\lambda_{n}
such that
p_{1}\left(\lambda_{n}\right)=0,
that is
n^{2}=\frac{\rho}{\mu}\lambda_{n}^{2},
then

A_{n}=\frac{K_{1}}{K_{2}}.

(79)

where,

K_{1}=\frac{\alpha}{\mu}\left(\frac{\rho}{\mu}-\frac{J}{\alpha}+\frac{\xi}{\alpha\lambda_{n}^{2}}\right)\left[c\mu-\rho g\left(0\right)+\rho\widehat{\kappa}\left(\lambda_{n}\right)\right]\lambda_{n}^{4}+\frac{\rho\beta^{2}}{\mu}\lambda_{n}^{4},

as
\frac{\xi}{\alpha\lambda_{n}^{2}}\longrightarrow 0,
then

K_{1}\simeq\frac{\alpha}{\mu}\left[\left(\frac{\rho}{\mu}-\frac{J}{\alpha}\right)\gamma_{g}+\frac{\rho\beta^{2}}{\alpha}+\left(\frac{\rho}{\mu}-\frac{J}{\alpha}\right)\rho\widehat{\kappa}\left(\lambda_{n}\right)\right]\lambda_{n}^{4}

(80)

and

\displaystyle K_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{b^{2}\rho}{\mu^{2}}\lambda_{n}^{4}\left(-c\mu+\rho g\left(0\right)-\rho\widehat{\kappa}\left(\lambda_{n}\right)\right)

(81)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{b^{2}\rho}{\mu^{2}}\left(\gamma_{g}+\rho\widehat{\kappa}\left(\lambda_{n}\right)\right)\lambda_{n}^{4}.

Substituting (
80
) and (
81
) into (
79
) we get

A_{n}\simeq-\frac{\alpha\mu\left[\chi_{g}\gamma_{g}+\left(\frac{\rho}{\mu}-\frac{J}{\alpha}\right)\rho\widehat{\kappa}\left(\lambda_{n}\right)\right]}{b^{2}\rho\left(\gamma_{g}+\rho\widehat{\kappa}\left(\lambda_{n}\right)\right)}.

•

If
\dfrac{\rho}{\mu}-\dfrac{J}{\alpha}=0
and
\gamma_{g}\neq 0,
since
\underset{n\longrightarrow\infty}{\lim}\widehat{\kappa}\left(\lambda_{n}\right)=0,
we have

A_{n}\simeq\frac{\beta^{2}\mu}{-b^{2}\left(\gamma_{g}+\rho\widehat{\kappa}\left(\lambda_{n}\right)\right)}\longrightarrow\frac{\beta^{2}\mu}{-b^{2}\gamma_{g}}=A\neq 0,

where
A
is a constant.

•

If
\dfrac{\rho}{\mu}-\dfrac{J}{\alpha}\neq 0
and
\gamma_{g}=0,
then

A_{n}\simeq-\frac{\alpha\mu\left(\frac{\rho}{\mu}-\frac{J}{\alpha}\right)}{b^{2}\rho}=A\neq 0.

•

If
\gamma_{g}=0
and
\dfrac{\rho}{\mu}-\dfrac{J}{\alpha}=0,
then

K_{1}=\frac{\rho\xi}{\mu}\widehat{\kappa}\left(\lambda_{n}\right)\lambda_{n}^{2}+\frac{\rho\beta^{2}}{\mu}\lambda_{n}^{4},\;K_{2}=-\frac{b^{2}\rho^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\widehat{\kappa}\left(\lambda_{n}\right)\lambda_{n}^{4}

then

A_{n}\simeq\frac{\mu\beta^{2}}{-b^{2}\rho\widehat{\kappa}\left(\lambda_{n}\right)}\longrightarrow\infty.

,
since
\underset{n\longrightarrow\infty}{\lim}\widehat{\kappa}\left(\lambda_{n}\right)=0.

•

If
\gamma_{g}\neq 0
, and
\chi_{g}\neq 0,
then

A_{n}\simeq-\frac{\alpha\mu\left[\chi_{g}+\left(\frac{\rho}{\mu}-\frac{J}{\alpha}\right)\rho\frac{\widehat{\kappa}\left(\lambda_{n}\right)}{\gamma_{g}}\right]}{b^{2}\rho\left(1+\rho\frac{\widehat{\kappa}\left(\lambda_{n}\right)}{\gamma_{g}}\right)},

and

A_{n}\longrightarrow-\frac{\alpha\mu\chi_{g}}{b^{2}\rho}=A\neq 0.

Therefore, if
A_{n}\longrightarrow A,
we have

\displaystyle\left\|U_{n}\right\|^{2}\geq

\displaystyle\rho\int_{0}^{\pi}\left|v_{n}\right|^{2}dx=\rho\int_{0}^{\pi}\left|\lambda_{n}u_{n}\right|^{2}dx=\rho\int_{0}^{\pi}\left|\lambda_{n}A\sin nx\right|^{2}dx,

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\rho\left|\lambda_{n}A\right|\int_{0}^{\pi}\left|\sin nx\right|^{2}dx=\frac{\rho\pi\left|\lambda_{n}A\right|}{2}\longrightarrow\infty,

and if
A_{n}\longrightarrow\infty,
we have

\left\|U_{n}\right\|^{2}\geq\mu\int_{0}^{\pi}\left|u_{n}\right|^{2}dx=\mu\int_{0}^{\pi}\left|A_{n}u_{n}\right|^{2}dx\longrightarrow\infty,

which completes the proof of Theorem
4
.

The asymptotic form of the Hagedorn temperature in planar
𝒩

Abstract
Using the supergravity dual and the plane-wave limit as a guide, we conjecture the asymptotic large coupling form of the Hagedorn temperature for planar 𝒩 = 4 \mathcal{N}=4 super Yang-Mills to order 1 / λ 1/\sqrt{\lambda} . This is two orders beyond the presently known behavior. Using the quantum spectral curve procedure of Harmark and Wilhelm, we show that our conjectured form is in excellent agreement with the numerical results.

Introduction

Confining gauge theories at low temperatures can behave like string theories. A classic sign of this is a density of states that grows as
\rho(E)\sim e^{E/T_{H}}
, where
T_{H}
is the Hagedorn temperature. At
T=T_{H}
the string theory picture should break down. This is often associated with the deconfinement of the gauge theory, but depending on the circumstances the deconfinement temperature is often below
T_{H}

[
1
,
2
]
.

By putting the gauge theory on a compact spatial manifold and taking the large-
N
limit it is possible to still have a Hagedorn temperature and a deconfinement transition, even if the gauge theory is not confining on
R^{3}

[
3
]
. In particular, for the case of planar
\mathcal{N}=4
super Yang-Mills on
S^{3}
, one finds a coupling dependent Hagedorn temperature. This was first computed at zero coupling by Sundborg
[
4
]
and independently in
[
1
]
,
where they found that
T_{H}=(2\ln(2+\sqrt{3}))^{-1}
in units where the radius of the
S^{3}
is set to
1
. Furthermore, for this zero coupling case it was shown that
T_{H}
equals the deconfinement temperature
[
4
,
1
]
. However, at large ’t Hooft coupling
\lambda
, where
\lambda=g_{YM}^{2}N
, one expects the deconfinement temperature to be the same as the Hawking-Page transition temperature
T_{HP}\sim 1
for the supergravity dual
[
5
,
3
]
, while
T_{H}\sim\lambda^{1/4}
, therefore in this regime
T_{H}
is far above the deconfinement transition. However, it still provides information about the growth of states at large energies, or equivalently, the growth of single-trace operators at large dimension.

Recently, Harmark and Wilhelm have exploited the integrability of planar
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM to make substantial progress in finding the coupling dependence of
T_{H}

[
6
,
7
,
8
]
. From the
Y
-system they computed
T_{H}
up to the two-loop level at weak coupling
[
6
]
, which previously was only known to one loop
[
9
]
. Switching over to the quantum spectral curve formalism
[
10
,
11
,
12
]
they extended their results to seven loops at weak coupling and found the asymptotic behavior numerically at strong coupling
[
7
,
8
]
.

At strong coupling Harmark and Wilhelm found that
T_{H}
has the form

\displaystyle T_{H}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}c_{n}g^{(1-n)/2}\approx 0.3989g^{1/2}+0.159-0.0087g^{-1/2}+0.037g^{-1}+\dots\,,

(1.1)

where
g\equiv\frac{\sqrt{\lambda}}{4\pi}
and the uncertainties are of order
1
in the last digit for
c_{0}
and
c_{1}
and order
5
in the last digit for
c_{2}
and
c_{3}
. The first coefficient matches within the error bars the flat-space prediction
c_{0}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\approx 0.39894
, which follows from the original computation of the superstring Hagedorn temperature
[
13
]
and the AdS/CFT dictionary (
c.f.

[
1
]
and references therein). After (
1.1
) in
[
8
]
was announced, the second term was derived analytically by Maldacena and Urbach
[
14
,
15
]
, where they found that
c_{1}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\approx 0.159155
.

We will rederive this last result and also conjecture the analytic result for the next two terms in the expansion. To do this we will combine analysis from supergravity, where we take advantage of the lightness of the tachyon mode, as well as properties of the plane-wave limit of
AdS_{5}\times S^{5}
. In fact, we can derive the Maldacena-Urbach result from the multiplicity of states in the plane-wave limit.
Our conjecture for the expansion in (
1.1
) is

\displaystyle T_{H}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}g^{1/2}+\frac{1}{2\pi}+\left(\left[\frac{5}{8\pi\sqrt{2\pi}}\right]+\left\{\frac{-4\ln(2)}{4\pi\sqrt{2\pi}}\right\}\right)g^{-1/2}+\left[\frac{45}{128\pi^{2}}\right]g^{-1}+\dots\,,

(1.2)

where the terms in the square brackets come from supergravity corrections while the term in the curly brackets comes from a shift in the string zero-point energy.

To test the conjecture we take the QSC prescription described in
[
8
]
and go to higher orders in the series expansions for the
Q
-functions to improve the numerical estimates. We find

\displaystyle T_{H}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle 0.39894g^{1/2}+0.15916-0.00865g^{-1/2}+0.0356g^{-1}

(1.3)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad-0.008196g^{-3/2}-0.00671g^{-2}+\dots\,,

where the error is
1
in the last digit for the first four coefficients and
3
for the last two. The coefficient
c_{1}
is computed assuming that
c_{0}
takes its analytic value, hence improving its error bars, while
c_{2}
and
c_{3}
are computed together assuming that
c_{1}
also takes its analytic value. The results for
c_{4}
and
c_{5}
assume that
c_{2}
and
c_{3}
take the form in (
1.2
).

The conjectured
c_{2}
and
c_{3}
are approximately
-0.0086538
and
0.03562
respectively, so we see that they are in very good agreement with the numerical approximations in (
1.3
).
It turns out that our prediction for
c_{3}
is more robust than our prediction for
c_{2}
. If we assume that
c_{3}
equals its conjectured analytic value in (
1.2
) then our numerical prediction for
c_{2}
improves to
c_{2}=-0.0086538\pm 3\times 10^{-7}
, which is right on the nose with the conjectured result. The zero-point shift that affects
c_{2}
is twice as large as the shift found in the plane-wave limit. At the moment we are unable to derive this shift from first principles.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In section 2 we rederive the Maldacena-Urbach result by equating the supergravity calculation for the Hagedorn temperature to finding the ground-state energy for a four-dimensional harmonic oscillator. We then use first and second order perturbation theory on the perturbed oscillator to find the corrections in the square brackets in (
1.2
). In section 3 we consider the plane-wave limit for
AdS_{5}\times S^{5}
and show that the growth of states leads to the analytic result for
c_{1}
. We also provide two arguments why the plane-wave captures the correct result. We further show that the shift to the zero-point energy will lead to half the result in the curly brackets in (
1.2
) while also showing that it does not contribute to
c_{3}
. In section 4 we summarize the QSC prescription and give further details about our numerical results.

2
Corrections from supergravity

The Hagedorn temperature,
T_{H}
in string theory is inversely proportional to the string length. This means that for
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM on
S^{3}
,
T_{H}\sim\lambda^{1/4}
, where we have chosen units where the radius of the
S^{3}
is unity. It is also known that
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM undergoes a deconfinement transition at the Hawking-Page temperature
[
5
,
3
]
, which at strong coupling is
T_{HP}=\frac{3}{2\pi}
. This is well below
T_{H}
, hence at
T=T_{H}
there is not actually a transition (at least at strong coupling)
[
1
]
. However,
T_{H}
still provides information about the density of single trace states at zero temperature. That is, we expect that
\rho(E)\sim e^{E/T_{H}}
.

Since we are really working at
T=0
, the relevant geometry to consider in the gravity dual is
not
the AdS black hole. Instead we should consider empty Euclidean
AdS_{5}\times S^{5}
with the Euclidean time direction
\tau
identifiied by
\tau\equiv\tau+\beta
. The metric for Euclidean
AdS_{5}
in global coordinates can be written as

\displaystyle ds^{2}=(1+R^{2})d\tau^{2}+\frac{dR^{2}}{1+R^{2}}+R^{2}d\Omega_{3}^{2}\,,

(2.1)

where
R\geq 0
and
d\Omega_{3}^{2}
is the unit metric on
S^{3}
. The string tension is given by
{2\pi}/{\alpha^{\prime}}=2\pi\sqrt{\lambda}
.

2.1
Zeroth order

To find the Hagedorn temperature we consider a winding string that wraps around the
\tau
direction
[
19
,
20
,
21
]
. At the same time the fermions with odd winding states have anti-periodic boundary conditions, hence the world-sheet ground state energy is shifted to
-\frac{2}{\alpha^{\prime}}
in the flat-space limit. Hence the corresponding mass squared for a single winding state in the flat-space limit is

\displaystyle m^{2}=\left(\frac{\beta}{2\pi\alpha^{\prime}}\right)^{2}-\frac{2}{\alpha^{\prime}}\,.

(2.2)

The Hagedorn temperature is determined by setting
m^{2}
to zero, to which we find

\displaystyle T_{H}=\frac{1}{\beta_{H}}=\frac{\lambda^{1/4}}{\sqrt{8\pi^{2}}}\,.

(2.3)

To find the next term in the expansion, we note that corrections to the world-sheet ground state energy in the single winding sector should be of order
{\mathcal{O}}(\lambda^{0})
, and hence will not affect the next term in the expansion. In fact, since we are assuming that we are tuning the mass to be very small, we can treat the problem as that for a point-particle in a supergravity background. The winding mode is a scalar field,
\chi
, hence its contribution to the action is

\displaystyle\int d^{5}X\sqrt{g}\left(\nabla^{\mu}\chi\nabla_{\mu}\chi+m^{2}(R)\chi^{2}\right)\,,

(2.4)

where
m^{2}(R)
is the radial dependent mass term

\displaystyle m^{2}(R)=(1+R^{2})\left(\frac{\beta}{2\pi\alpha^{\prime}}\right)^{2}+C\,,

(2.5)

with
C\approx-{2}/{\alpha^{\prime}}
.

Assuming that
\chi
has a nontrivial profile only in the
R
direction and minimizing the action, we find the equations of motion are
approximately

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{R^{3}}\frac{d}{dR}R^{3}\frac{d}{dR}\chi(R)+\left(\frac{\beta}{2\pi\alpha^{\prime}}\right)^{2}R^{2}\chi(R)=\left(\frac{2}{\alpha^{\prime}}-\left(\frac{\beta}{2\pi\alpha^{\prime}}\right)^{2}\right)\chi(R)\,.

(2.6)

The solution should be normalizable and hence fall off to zero as
R\to\infty
. Therefore, solving (
2.6
) is equivalent to finding the ground-state solution for a rotationally symmetric four-dimensional harmonic oscillator with
\omega=\frac{\beta}{2\pi\alpha^{\prime}}
and energy

\displaystyle E=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{2}{\alpha^{\prime}}-\left(\frac{\beta}{2\pi\alpha^{\prime}}\right)^{2}\right)=2\omega=2\frac{\beta}{2\pi\alpha^{\prime}}\,,

(2.7)

which leads to

\displaystyle\frac{\beta^{2}}{4\pi\alpha^{\prime}}=2\pi-2\beta\,.

(2.8)

To leading order, the solution of (
2.8
) gives the Hagedorn temperature

\displaystyle T_{H}=\frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{2\alpha^{\prime}}}+\frac{1}{2\pi}+\dots\,.

(2.9)

2.2
First order

At the next order we have to consider first order corrections in
\alpha^{\prime}
to the world-sheet sigma model. We write

C=-\frac{2}{\alpha^{\prime}}+\Delta C
to take this correction into account. There is also the correction to the harmonic oscillator “Hamiltonian”,

\displaystyle\Delta H=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{R^{3}}\frac{d}{dR}R^{5}\frac{d}{dR}\,,

(2.10)

which leads to the correction to the energy

\displaystyle\Delta E^{(1)}=\langle\psi_{0}|\Delta H|\psi_{0}\rangle=3\,,

(2.11)

where
the normalized
1
1

1

We set
\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}R^{3}\psi_{0}(R)\psi_{0}(R)dR=1
.
ground-state wave-function is
\psi_{0}(R)=\sqrt{2}\omega e^{-\frac{1}{2}\omega R^{2}}
, we find

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{0}|\Delta H^{(1)}|\psi_{0}\rangle=\langle\psi_{0}|\frac{1}{2}\left(2\omega R^{2}-\omega^{2}{\cal R}^{4}\right)|\psi_{0}\rangle=-1\,.

(2.12)

Hence we find,

\displaystyle 2\frac{\beta}{2\pi\alpha^{\prime}}+3=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{2}{\alpha^{\prime}}-\Delta C-\left(\frac{\beta}{2\pi\alpha^{\prime}}\right)^{2}\right)\,,

(2.13)

which we rewrite as

\displaystyle\frac{\beta^{2}}{4\pi\alpha^{\prime}}=2\pi-2\beta-{\pi\alpha^{\prime}}\Delta C-6\pi\alpha^{\prime}\,.

(2.14)

We will later conjecture that
\Delta C
has the form
\Delta C=\frac{\beta^{2}}{2\pi^{2}\alpha^{\prime}}\Delta c
, in which case solving for

T_{H}
to the next order we find

\displaystyle T_{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{2\alpha^{\prime}}}+\frac{1}{2\pi}+\frac{5+2\Delta c}{4\sqrt{2}\pi}\sqrt{\alpha^{\prime}}+\dots

(2.15)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{g}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}+\frac{1}{2\pi}+\frac{5+2\Delta c}{8\pi\sqrt{2\pi}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{g}}+\dots\,,

where
g
is defined as
g=\frac{\sqrt{\lambda}}{4\pi}=\frac{1}{4\pi\alpha^{\prime}}
.

2.3
Second order

If we assume that the corrections to
C
are in even powers of
\beta
only, then the next order correction to
\Delta C
won’t affect the second order result. The correction to the energy is computed using second order perturbation theory, where we find

\displaystyle\Delta E^{(2)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\langle\psi_{0}|\Delta H^{(1)}|\psi_{2}\rangle\langle\psi_{2}|\Delta H^{(1)}|\psi_{0}\rangle}{-2\,\omega}+\frac{\langle\psi_{0}|\Delta H^{(1)}|\psi_{4}\rangle\langle\psi_{4}|\Delta H^{(1)}|\psi_{0}\rangle}{-4\,\omega}

(2.16)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0+\frac{(-\sqrt{3})^{2}}{-4\,\omega}=-\frac{3\pi\alpha^{\prime}}{2\,\beta}\,.

In computing (
2.16
) we used that the excited normalized wave-functions are given by

\displaystyle\psi_{2}(R)=\omega(\omega R^{2}-2)e^{-\frac{1}{2}\omega R^{2}}\,,\qquad\psi_{4}(R)=\frac{\omega}{\sqrt{6}}(\omega^{2}R^{4}-6\omega R^{2}+6)e^{-\frac{1}{2}\omega R^{2}}\,.

(2.17)

Hence we reach the equation

\displaystyle 2\frac{\beta}{2\pi\alpha^{\prime}}+3-\frac{3\pi\alpha^{\prime}}{2\,\beta}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{2}{\alpha^{\prime}}-\frac{\beta^{2}\Delta c}{2\pi^{2}\alpha^{\prime}}-\left(\frac{\beta}{2\pi\alpha^{\prime}}\right)^{2}\right)\,,

(2.18)

which is equivalent to

\displaystyle\frac{\beta^{2}}{4\pi\alpha^{\prime}}=2\pi-2\beta-\frac{\beta^{2}\Delta c}{2\pi}-6\pi\alpha^{\prime}+\frac{3\pi^{2}(\alpha^{\prime})^{2}}{\beta}\,.

(2.19)

Hence the Hagedorn temperature as a function of
g
is

\displaystyle T_{H}(g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{g}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}+\frac{1}{2\pi}+\frac{5+2\Delta c}{8\pi\sqrt{2\pi}\sqrt{g}}+\frac{45}{128\pi^{2}g}+{\mathcal{O}}(g^{-3/2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.398942\sqrt{g}+0.159155+\frac{0.079367+0.0317468\,\Delta c}{\sqrt{g}}+\frac{0.0356207}{g}+{\mathcal{O}}(g^{-3/2})\,.

Notice that the last term is independent of
\Delta c
.

3
The Hagedorn temperature from the plane-wave

As we have indicated, the Hagedorn temperature is dependent on the growth of string states as the energy increases. Unfortunately, the exact spectrum is unknown for type IIB string theory on
AdS_{5}\times S_{5}
. However, it is instructive to consider the IIB theory in the plane-wave limit where the spectrum is known.

The analysis of the Hagedorn temperature in the plane-wave limit was originally carried out in

[
22
,
23
,
24
]
where an exact equation for the Hagedorn temperature in the plane wave background was derived. Here we will review this analysis, mainly following the discussion in
[
24
]
. We then show that in the plane-wave limit, one finds the same first order correction for the Hagedorn temperature found numerically in
[
8
]
and using the supergravity dual in
[
14
,
15
]
. We further show that the zero-point energy leads to a correction to the second-order term in the expansion, but not the third-order term.

Consider the full metric for the
AdS_{5}\times S^{5}
type IIB background,

\displaystyle ds^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(1+R^{2})dt^{2}+\frac{dR^{2}}{1+R^{2}}+R^{2}d\Omega_{3}^{2}+(1-Z^{2})d\psi^{2}+\frac{dZ^{2}}{1-Z^{2}}+Z^{2}d{\Omega_{3}^{\prime}}^{2}

(3.2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigg(-(1+R_{I}^{2})dt^{2}+(1-Z_{I}^{2})d\psi^{2}+dR_{I}^{2}+dZ_{I}^{2}\bigg)+\left\{-\frac{R^{2}dR^{2}}{1+R^{2}}+\frac{Z^{2}dZ^{2}}{1-Z^{2}}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigg(-2dx^{+}dx^{-}-\frac{1}{2}(R_{I}^{2}+Z_{I}^{2})dx^{+}dx^{+}+dR_{I}^{2}+dZ_{I}^{2}

\displaystyle-\left[({R}^{2}-Z^{2})dx^{+}dx^{-}+\frac{1}{2}({R}^{2}+{Z}^{2})dx^{-}dx^{-}\right]\bigg)+\left\{-\frac{R^{2}dR^{2}}{1+R^{2}}+\frac{Z^{2}dZ^{2}}{1-Z^{2}}\right\},

where
x^{\pm}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(t\pm\psi)
and
I=1,\dots 4
. If we consider geodesics where
R^{2},\ Z^{2}\ll 1
, then we can drop the term in the curly brackets. If we further focus on geodesics with large angular momentum along
\psi
, then
\dot{x}^{-}\ll\dot{x}^{+}
and we can drop the term in the square brackets, leaving the plane-wave metric

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-2dx^{+}dx^{-}-f^{2}x_{I}^{2}dx^{+}dx^{+}+dx_{I}^{2}\,,

(3.3)

where
I=1,\dots 8
and
f=1/\sqrt{2}
. There is also a background Ramond-Ramond five-form field-strength,

\displaystyle F_{+1234}=F_{+5678}=2f\,.

(3.4)

In terms of the dimension
\Delta
and
R
-charge
J
of the dual operators, we have

\displaystyle p^{-}=\frac{f}{\sqrt{2}}(\Delta-J)\,,\qquad\qquad p^{+}=\frac{\Delta+J}{\sqrt{2}f}\,.

(3.5)

Type IIB string theory on the plane-wave metric in (
3.3
) with the field-strength in (
3.4
) is exactly solvable and can be quantized in light-cone gauge
[
25
,
26
]
. In particular, one finds for the light-cone Hamiltonian

\displaystyle H=P^{-}=f(N_{0}^{B}+N_{0}^{F}+4)+\frac{1}{\alpha^{\prime}p^{+}}\sum_{{{\mathcal{I}}}=1}^{2}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sqrt{m^{2}+(\alpha^{\prime}p^{+}f)^{2}}(N_{{\mathcal{I}}m}^{B}+N_{{\mathcal{I}}m}^{F})\,,

(3.6)

where
{\mathcal{I}}
counts the fermionic world-sheet variables,
N_{m}^{B}
counts the bosonic oscillators at level
m
and
N_{m}^{F}
counts the fermionic oscillators at level
m
. More details can be found in
[
26
]
.

The free energy is the sum over the free energies for each species of the single string spectrum. The bosons contribute

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta}\textrm{Tr}\ln\left(1-e^{-\beta p^{0}}\right)=-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n\beta}\textrm{Tr}\,e^{-\frac{n\beta}{\sqrt{2}}(p^{+}+p^{-})}\,,

(3.7)

while the fermions contribute

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\beta}\textrm{Tr}\ln\left(1+e^{-\beta p^{0}}\right)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n\beta}\textrm{Tr}\,e^{-\frac{n\beta}{\sqrt{2}}(p^{+}+p^{-})}\,.

(3.8)

The trace over states includes an integration over
p^{+}
which is normalized to
[
24
]

\displaystyle\frac{L}{\pi\sqrt{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}dp^{+}\,,

(3.9)

where
L
is the length along the spatial part of the light-cone. If we change variables to
\tau_{2}
, where

\displaystyle\tau_{2}=\frac{n\beta}{2\pi\sqrt{2}\alpha^{\prime}p^{+}},

(3.10)

then averaging over the free energy from either bosons and fermions leads to

\displaystyle F=-\sum_{n=1,\tt odd}^{\infty}\frac{L}{4\pi^{2}\alpha^{\prime}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\tau_{2}}{\tau_{2}^{2}}e^{-\frac{n^{2}\beta^{2}}{4\pi\alpha^{\prime}\tau_{2}}}\textrm{tr}\,e^{-\frac{n\beta}{\sqrt{2}}p^{-}}-\frac{L\pi}{24\beta^{2}}\,,

(3.11)

where
tr
refers to the trace over all possible string oscillator modes. The last term arises because of a mismatch between the number of bosonic and fermionic states at
p^{-}=0
. Explicitly writing the oscillators and enforcing level matching by introducing an integral over
\tau_{1}
, the free energy becomes

\displaystyle F=-\sum_{n=1,\tt odd}^{\infty}\frac{L}{4\pi^{2}\alpha^{\prime}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\tau_{2}}{\tau_{2}^{2}}\int_{-\frac{1}{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}d\tau_{1}e^{-\frac{n^{2}\beta^{2}}{4\pi\alpha^{\prime}\tau_{2}}}\prod_{m=-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1+e^{-2\pi\tau_{2}\sqrt{m^{2}+\nu^{2}}+2\pi i\tau_{1}m}}{1-e^{-2\pi\tau_{2}\sqrt{m^{2}+\nu^{2}}+2\pi i\tau_{1}m}}\right)^{8}-\frac{L\pi}{24\beta^{2}}\,,

where
\nu=\frac{n\beta f}{2\pi\sqrt{2}\tau_{2}}
. We then observe that

\displaystyle\prod_{m=-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1+e^{-2\pi\tau_{2}\sqrt{m^{2}+\nu^{2}}}}{1-e^{-2\pi\tau_{2}\sqrt{m^{2}+\nu^{2}}}}\right)^{8}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\left(-8\sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty}\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}\left[\frac{(-1)^{p}}{p}-\frac{1}{p}\right]e^{-2\pi p\tau_{2}\sqrt{m^{2}+\nu^{2}}}\right)

(3.13)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\left(2\sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty}\sum_{p=1,\tt odd}^{\infty}\frac{1}{p}\frac{16}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\,e^{-t^{2}-\frac{\pi^{2}\tau_{2}^{2}p^{2}m^{2}}{t^{2}}-\frac{n^{2}\beta^{2}f^{2}p^{2}}{8t^{2}}}\right)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\exp\left(\frac{8n\beta f}{\pi\sqrt{2}\,\tau_{2}}\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}\frac{1-(-1)^{p}}{p}K_{1}\left(\frac{n\beta fp}{\sqrt{2}}\right)\right)\,,

where
K_{1}(x)
is the modified Bessel function. To get to the last step we approximated the sum over
m
as an integral, since in the following step we will assume that
\tau_{2}\to 0
.

The Hagedorn temperature is determined by taking
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\to 0
. Using the results from above, the free energy behaves as

\displaystyle F\sim-\sum_{n=1,\tt odd}^{\infty}\frac{L}{4\pi^{2}\alpha^{\prime}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\tau_{2}}{\tau_{2}^{2}}e^{-\frac{n^{2}\beta^{2}}{4\pi\alpha^{\prime}\tau_{2}}}\exp\left(\frac{8n\beta f}{\pi\sqrt{2}\,\tau_{2}}\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}\frac{1-(-1)^{p}}{p}K_{1}\left(\frac{n\beta fp}{\sqrt{2}}\right)\right)\,.

(3.14)

The dominant contribution comes from
n=1
, which corresponds to a single winding around the thermal circle. The free energy diverges as
\tau_{2}\to 0
when the relation

\displaystyle\frac{\beta^{2}}{4\pi\alpha^{\prime}}=\frac{8\beta f}{\pi\sqrt{2}}\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}\frac{1-(-1)^{p}}{p}K_{1}\left(\frac{\beta fp}{\sqrt{2}}\right)\,

(3.15)

is satisfied.
Solving this equation for
\beta
determines the Hagedorn temperature for the plane-wave. This equation can be expressed as a series in
\beta
by implementing a Mellin transform on the right hand side and then inverting the transform. After these steps one finds

\displaystyle\frac{\beta^{2}}{4\pi\alpha^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\pi-{2\beta(\sqrt{2}f)}+\frac{\beta^{2}(\sqrt{2}f)^{2}2\ln 2}{2\pi}

(3.16)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}(2^{2k}-4)4\sqrt{\pi}}{k!}\left(\frac{\beta(\sqrt{2}f)}{4\pi}\right)^{2k}\Gamma(k-\frac{1}{2})\zeta(2k-1)\,.

If we now set
f=1/\sqrt{2}
to match the plane-wave limit of
AdS_{5}\times S^{5}
and compare (
3.16
) to (
2.19
), we see that the linear term in
\beta
is the same in both equations. Hence, the first order correction to the Hagedorn temperature matches the Maldacena-Urbach result from supergravity. Given the success matching this term, it is tempting to set
\Delta c=-2\ln 2
in (
2.19
) so that the terms quadratic in
\beta
also match. The higher order terms in (
3.16
) lead to corrections of order
g^{-3/2}
and higher and are irrelevant for this study, while the last two terms in (
2.19
) are from non-quadratic corrections.

Note that the right hand side of (
3.16
) with the second term removed is an even function of
\beta
. The second term is the correction coming from the winding mode on the thermal circle with the extra quadratic piece. The even terms make up the zero-point energy on the string world-sheet. The first term is the flat-space contribution, while the third term is the leading order correction.

We now give two arguments why the plane-wave leads to the correct result for the leading order correction to the Hagedorn temperature. In this analysis the Hagedorn temperature is found by taking
\tau_{2}\to 0
in (
3.14
), such that
\tau_{2}\ll\frac{\beta^{2}}{\alpha^{\prime}}\sim 1
. Using (
3.10
) this corresponds to having
p^{+}\gg\beta^{-1}
. On the other hand, the modes that contribute to the second line of (
3.13
) cut off around
m\sim 1/\tau_{2}
, hence these have
p^{-}\sim\beta^{-1}
. Therefore, the regime that determines the Hagedorn temperature has
p^{+}\gg p^{-}
, which focuses on the plane-wave limit. This suggests that including the term inside the square brackets in (
3.2
) would not affect the Hagedorn temperature, at least to leading order, since as
\tau_{2}\to 0
the contribution to the free energy would approach that in (
3.14
). But this term plus the plane-wave metric gives the quadratic metric inside the parentheses in (
3.2
). Since it is effectively this metric that was used in
[
14
,
15
]
to compute the leading order correction to the Hagedorn temperature, this shows why the plane-wave limit can give the correct result.

A second way to see why the plane-wave limit can give the correct result is by turning on a chemical potential
\mu
for the charge
J=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(p^{+}-p^{-})
. The effect of the chemical potential is to modify the free energy to
[
23
,
24
]

\displaystyle F\sim\textrm{Tr}\ln\left(-\partial_{+}\partial_{-}+{\frac{\beta^{2}-\mu^{2}}{4\pi\alpha^{\prime}}-\left[2\pi-2(\beta-\mu)+\frac{(\beta-\mu)^{2}\ln 2}{\pi}+\dots\right]}\right)\,.

(3.17)

Hence, the expectation value of
J
is

\displaystyle\langle J\rangle=\beta\frac{\partial F}{\partial\mu}\sim\textrm{Tr}\frac{-\frac{\mu}{2\pi\alpha^{\prime}}-2+\dots}{\left(-\partial_{+}\partial_{-}+\frac{\beta^{2}-\mu^{2}}{4\pi\alpha^{\prime}}-\left[2\pi-2(\beta-\mu)+\frac{(\beta-\mu)^{2}\ln 2}{\pi}+\dots\right]\right)}\,.

(3.18)

Clearly,
\langle J\rangle<0
if
\mu=0
. This nonzero value for
\langle J\rangle
is due to the non-symmetric nature of the plane-wave metric. To compensate for this we set
\mu=-4\pi\alpha^{\prime}+\dots
. This results in the new Hagedorn equation

\displaystyle\frac{\beta^{2}}{4\pi\alpha^{\prime}}=2\pi-2\beta+\frac{\beta^{2}\ln 2}{\pi}-4\pi\alpha^{\prime}+\dots\,.

(3.19)

Since the term linear in
\beta
is unchanged, the first correction to the Hagedorn temperature also remains unchanged. However, the last term in (
3.19
) will affect the next order correction.

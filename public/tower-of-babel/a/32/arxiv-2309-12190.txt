Regret and Conservatism of Distributionally Robust Constrained Stochastic Model Predictive Control
Maik Pfefferkorn maik.pfefferkorn@iat.tu-darmstadt.de, Venkatraman Renganathan venkatraman.renganathan@control.lth.se, Rolf Findeisen rolf.findeisen@iat.tu-darmstadt.de

Abstract
We analyse the conservatism and regret of distributionally robust (DR) stochastic model predictive control (SMPC) when using moment-based ambiguity sets for modeling unknown uncertainties. To quantify the conservatism, we compare the deterministic constraint tightening while taking a DR approach against the optimal tightening when the exact distributions of the stochastic uncertainties are known. Furthermore, we quantify the sub-optimality gap and regret by comparing the performance when the distributions of the stochastic uncertainties are known and unknown. Analysing the accumulated sub-optimality of SMPC due to the lack of knowledge about the true distributions of the uncertainties marks the novel contribution of this work.



1
Introduction

Recently, there has been a surge of interest in analyzing control algorithms that operate subject to unknown
quantities of interest
(QIs) through the lens of regret analysis.
Lack of knowledge about a QI induces regret for the controller, i.e., a performance loss compared to when the QI was known.
Notably, many robust control algorithms incorporate caution mechanisms into their decision making scheme in order to account for the lack of knowledge about the QIs.
The cautiousness in the decision making can be quantified as the conservatism of the control algorithm which in turn incurs regret.
For instance, the regret of
H_{\infty}
control compared against an optimal controller that knows the disturbance sequences exactly beforehand was examined in
Karapetyan et al. 2022
.
Further works investigated regret in different variants of the finite- and infinite-horizon linear-quadratic regulator problem, see e.g.
Li et al. 2019
;
Chen et al. 2023
and references therein.

In this research, we are interested in comparing controllers that are robust with respect to a moment-based ambiguity set of distributions against a fully informed counterpart that knows the true uncertainty distribution (i.e. QI).
Specifically, we study the conservatism and regret of the DR SMPC formulation for unknown uncertainty distributions compared against its fully informed counterpart. There have been recent works on DR MPC
Fochesato & Lygeros 2022
;
McAllister & Rawlings 2023
and more specifically on investigating its regret
Yan et al. 2023
. However, they all consider Wasserstein-based formulations. On the contrary, we consider a moment-based ambiguity set formulation.

Our main contributions are: 1) We define
constraint conservatism, sub-optimality gap
and
distributional regret
of DR SMPC with uncertainties modeled using moment-based ambiguity sets, and 2) We develop a framework for quantifying conservatism and for analyzing the resulting regret behavior. We derive analytic expressions to identify and analyze the effects that lead to regret.

The paper is organised as follows: The DR SMPC problem is introduced in Section
2
.
Regret and conservatism associated with DR SMPC are presented in Section
3
, while some of its features are demonstrated using numerical simulation in Section
4
before concluding in Section
5
.

DR SMPC Problem Formulation

We consider DR SMPC problem with joint chance constraints similar to
Paulson et al. 2020
using moment-based ambiguity set modeling to describe the stochastic system uncertainties.

2.1
System Dynamics & Constraints

We consider a stochastic linear time-invariant system

x_{k+1}=Ax_{k}+Bu_{k}+w_{k},\quad\forall k\in\mathbb{N},

(1)

where
x_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
u_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
are the system state and input at time step
k
, respectively. The matrices
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
and
B\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
denote the dynamics matrix and the input matrix respectively. For the ease of exposition, we will assume that
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is known. The process noise
w_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a zero-mean random vector that is independent and identically distributed over time.
The distribution of
w_{k}
, namely
\mathbb{P}^{w}
, is unknown.
However, it is known to belong to a moment-based ambiguity set of distributions given by

\mathcal{P}^{w}=\left\{\mathbb{P}^{w}\mid\mathbb{E}[w_{k}]=0,\mathbb{E}[w_{k}w_{k}^{\top}]={\Sigma}_{w}\right\}.

(2)

We denote by
N\in\mathbb{N}
the prediction horizon of the predictive control problem to be defined shortly.
The states, inputs and the disturbances over the prediction horizon are given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}_{k}

\displaystyle:=\begin{bmatrix}x^{\top}_{0\mid k}&x^{\top}_{1\mid k}&\cdots&x^{\top}_{N\mid k}\end{bmatrix}^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{(N+1)n},

(3)

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}_{k}

\displaystyle:=\begin{bmatrix}u^{\top}_{0\mid k}&u^{\top}_{1\mid k}&\cdots&u^{\top}_{N-1\mid k}\end{bmatrix}^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{Nm},

(4)

\displaystyle\mathbf{w}_{k}

\displaystyle:=\begin{bmatrix}w^{\top}_{0\mid k}&w^{\top}_{1\mid k}&\cdots&w^{\top}_{N-1\mid k}\end{bmatrix}^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{Nn}.

(5)

Here, the subscript
i\!\mid\!k
indicates
i
time steps ahead of
k
and
x_{0\mid k}=x_{k}
.
Starting at
x_{k}
, we compactly write the evolution of (
1
) over the prediction horizon as

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}_{k}=\mathbf{A}x_{0\mid k}+\mathbf{B}\mathbf{u}_{k}+\mathbf{D}\mathbf{w}_{k},

(6)

with matrices
\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}
, and
\mathbf{D}
being of appropriate dimension.
Note that
\mathbf{x}_{k}
and
\mathbf{w}_{k}
are random vectors as the realizations of future disturbances
w_{i\mid k},i\in[0:N-1]
are a-priori unknown.
The mean and covariance of (
6
) evolve as

\displaystyle\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{k}

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{x}_{k}]=\mathbf{A}x_{0\mid k}+\mathbf{B}\mathbf{u}_{k},

(7)

\displaystyle\mathbf{\Sigma}_{\mathbf{x}}

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{E}[(\mathbf{x}_{k}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{k})(\mathbf{x}_{k}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{k})^{\top}]=\mathbf{D}\mathbf{\Sigma}_{\mathbf{w}}\mathbf{D}^{\top},

(8)

where
\mathbf{\Sigma}_{\mathbf{w}}
is a block diagonal matrix with
N
blocks and each block being equal to
\Sigma_{w}
.
Note that the state and disturbance sequences
(x_{i})_{i\in[0:k]}
and
(w_{i})_{i\in[0:k-1]}
have been realized at time step
k
and are hence deterministic.
We denote those realizations by
(\hat{x}_{i})_{i\in[0:k]}
and
(\hat{w}_{i})_{i\in[0:k-1]}
, respectively, where
\hat{x}_{0}=x_{0}
is the deterministic initial condition.
Since the support of
\mathbb{P}^{w}
can be unbounded, we cannot guarantee the satisfaction of hard state constraints for all
k
. Hence, we enforce a DR joint risk constraint on the states as

\displaystyle\sup_{\mathbb{P}_{k}^{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathcal{P}^{\mathbf{x}}_{k}}\mathbb{P}_{k}^{\mathbf{x}}\left[\mathbf{x}_{k}\notin\mathcal{X}\right]\leq\Delta,

(9)

where
\mathcal{P}^{\mathbf{x}}_{k}
denotes the moment-based ambiguity set for the compact state
\mathbf{x}_{k}
and is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{\mathbf{x}}_{k}=\left\{\mathbb{P}^{\mathbf{x}}_{k}\mid\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{x}_{k}]=\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{k},\mathbb{E}[(\mathbf{x}_{k}\!-\!\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{k})(\mathbf{x}_{k}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{k})^{\top}]=\mathbf{\Sigma}_{\mathbf{x}}\right\}.

The set
\mathcal{X}
is assumed to be a convex polytope defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}

\displaystyle:=\bigcap^{n_{x}}_{i=1}\left\{\mathbf{x}\mid f^{\top}_{i}\mathbf{x}\leq g_{i}\right\}=\left\{\mathbf{x}\mid\mathbf{F}\mathbf{x}\leq\mathbf{g}\right\},

(10)

where
f_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{(N+1)n},g_{i}\in\mathbb{R},\mathbf{F}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{x}\times(N+1)n}
and
\mathbf{g}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{x}}
.
Further,
\Delta\in(0,0.5]
represents a user-prescribed total risk budget for the worst-case probability of constraint violation over the entire prediction horizon.
A similar probabilistic treatment of control constraints can also be formulated. For the ease of exposition, we consider the hard input constraint formulation
u_{k}\in\mathcal{U},\forall k
, where
\mathcal{U}
is a convex polytope

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}

\displaystyle:=\bigcap^{n_{u}}_{j=1}\left\{\mathbf{u}\mid c^{\top}_{j}\mathbf{u}\leq d_{j}\right\}=\left\{\mathbf{u}\mid\mathbf{C}\mathbf{u}\leq\mathbf{d}\right\}

(11)

with
c_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{Nm},d_{i}\in\mathbb{R},\mathbf{C}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{u}\times Nm}
and
\mathbf{d}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{u}}
.
We employ SMPC as a solution approach to minimizing the cost

J_{\infty}(\mathbf{x}_{\infty},\mathbf{u}_{\infty})=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\lVert x_{k}\rVert_{Q}^{2}+\lVert u_{k}\rVert_{R}^{2},

(12)

where
\mathbf{x}_{\infty}=(x_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
,
\mathbf{u}_{\infty}=(u_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
,
Q\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n},Q\succeq 0
and
R\in\mathbb{N}^{m\times m},R\succ 0
and which is an intractable problem due to the infinite horizon as well as the stochastic dynamics.
The objective of the SMPC is to minimize the cost

\displaystyle J_{\mathrm{SMPC}}(\mathbf{u}_{k},x_{0\mid k}):=\mathbb{E}\left[\left\lVert{\mathbf{x}_{k}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\mathbf{Q}}+\left\lVert{\mathbf{u}_{k}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\mathbf{R}}\right]

(13)

defined for a finite horizon
N
and where the expectation
\mathbb{E}[\cdot]
accounts for the stochasticity of the states.
Note that
\mathbf{Q}
is a block-diagonal matrix with
N+1
blocks of which the first
N
are equal to
Q\succeq 0
and the last block is given by
Q_{\mathrm{f}}\succeq 0
.
For appropriately designed
Q_{\mathrm{f}}
, (
13
) approximates the expected, remaining infinite-horizon cost starting at
x_{k}
at time point
k
.
Similarly,
\mathbf{R}
is a block-diagonal matrix with
N
blocks and each block being equal to
R\succ 0
.
Then,
J_{\mathrm{SMPC}}(\mathbf{u}_{k},x_{0\mid k})
can be rewritten as

\displaystyle J_{\mathrm{SMPC}}(\mathbf{u}_{k},x_{0\mid k})=\left\lVert{\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{k}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\mathbf{Q}}+\left\lVert{\mathbf{u}_{k}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\mathbf{R}}+\mathbf{Tr}(\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{\Sigma}_{\mathbf{x}}).

(14)

We now formally state the DR SMPC optimization problem along with the state and input constraints.

are known and unknown respectively, the resulting open-loop costs (

This difference of the optimal costs is essentially referred to as regret.

Assumption 2
.

Both the systems under the DR and fully informed SMPC respectively encounter the same disturbance realization

at all time steps
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Definition 4
.

Given Assumption

, the closed-loop regret accumulated up to time point

is defined as the difference in the closed-loop costs with and without knowledge of
\mathbb{P}^{w}
and
\mathbb{P}_{k}^{\mathbf{x}}
respectively. That is,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{R}_{k}\!=\!\sum_{i=0}^{k}\left(\lVert\hat{x}_{i}^{\dagger}\rVert_{Q}^{2}\!+\!\lVert u^{\dagger}_{i}\rVert_{R}^{2}\right)\!-\!\left(\lVert\hat{x}_{i}^{\star}\rVert_{Q}^{2}\!+\!\lVert u_{i}^{\star}\rVert_{R}^{2}\right)

(33)

where
\cdot^{\star}
and
\cdot^{\dagger}
indicate the fully informed and DR quantities respectively, with
x_{0}^{\dagger}=x_{0}^{\star}=x_{0}
and
\mathfrak{R}_{0}^{\mathrm{cl}}=0
.

Besides
\mathfrak{R}_{k}
, we can also exploit the information obtained from solving the surrogate SMPC (

) to define the expected remaining infinite-horizon regret from time point
k
on in open-loop – given that (

) is designed to approximate (

Definition 5
.

The suboptimality gap, which corresponds to the expected remaining infinite-horizon cost difference in open-loop from time

on, is defined as the difference in the optimal value functions of DR SMPC (

) with and without knowledge of
\mathbb{P}^{w}
and

respectively. That is,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{G}_{k}:=J^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{SMPC}}(x_{k}^{\dagger})-J^{\star}_{\mathrm{SMPC}}(x_{k}^{\star}).

(34)

To analyze
\mathfrak{R}_{k}
and
\mathfrak{G}_{k}
, we derive a closed-form expression for

and
\mathbf{u}^{\dagger}_{k}
, whose first elements also constitute the closed-loop inputs

and

We reformulate OCP (
18
) as a quadratic program (QP) of the form

\displaystyle\!\underset{\mathbf{u_{k}}}{\mathrm{min}}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert{\mathbf{u}_{k}}\right\rVert^{2}_{H}+h_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{u}_{k}+r_{k}

(35a)

\displaystyle\mathrm{s.}\,\mathrm{t.}

\displaystyle\mathbf{M}\mathbf{u}_{k}-\mathbf{b}_{k}\leq\mathbf{0}.

(35b)

We start by reformulating the cost function (
14
) of OCP (
18
).
Substituting (
7
) and (
8
) into (

\displaystyle J_{\mathrm{SMPC}}(\mathbf{u}_{k},x_{k})=\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert{\mathbf{u}_{k}}\right\rVert^{2}_{H}+h_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{u}_{k}+r_{k},

(36)

where
H=2(\mathbf{B}^{\top}\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{B}+\mathbf{R})\succ 0,h_{k}^{\top}=2x^{\top}_{0\mid k}\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{B}
and
r_{k}=\mathbf{Tr}\left(\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{D}\mathbf{\Sigma}_{w}\mathbf{D}^{\top}\right)+\left\lVert{x_{0\mid k}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{A}}
.
Next, we substitute (
7
) and (
8
) into (

) to reformulate the tightened state constraints in terms of only the input as

\displaystyle\underbrace{f_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{B}}_{=:\bar{f}_{i}^{\top}}\mathbf{u}_{k}\leq\underbrace{g_{i}-f^{\top}_{i}\mathbf{A}x_{0\mid k}}_{=:\bar{g}_{k,i}}-\psi_{i}\underbrace{\left\lVert{(\mathbf{D}\mathbf{\Sigma}_{\mathbf{w}}\mathbf{D}^{\top})^{\frac{1}{2}}f_{i}}\right\rVert_{2}}_{=:v_{i}}.

(37)

Then,
\forall i\in\llbracket n_{x}\rrbracket
, writing (

\mathbf{\bar{F}}\mathbf{u}_{k}\leq\mathbf{\bar{g}}_{k}-\bm{\psi}^{\top}\mathbf{v}
enables us to define (

) via

\displaystyle\mathbf{M}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{C}\\
\mathbf{\bar{F}}\end{bmatrix},\quad\mathbf{b}_{k}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{d}\\
\mathbf{\bar{g}}_{k}-\mathrm{diag}({\bm{\psi}})\mathbf{v}\end{bmatrix}.

(38)

We now define when an inequality constraint is called active.

Definition 6
.

An inequality constraint is said to be active if
\mathbf{M}_{i:}\mathbf{u}_{k}-\mathbf{b}_{k,i}=0
and inactive if
\mathbf{M}_{i:}\mathbf{u}_{k}-\mathbf{b}_{k,i}<0
, where
\mathbf{M}_{i:}
denotes the i
{}^{\text{th}}
row of
\mathbf{M}
and
\mathbf{b}_{k,i}
the i
{}^{\text{th}}
entry of
\mathbf{b}_{k}
. The active set
\mathcal{A}_{k}\subseteq\llbracket n_{x}+n_{u}\rrbracket
is the index set of active inequality constraints.

Assumption 3
.

The active set
\mathcal{A}_{k}^{\diamond}
of QP (
35
) is known
\forall k
.

To solve the QP, we assume the following regularity condition on the constraints.

Assumption 4
.

QP (
35
) satisfies the linear independence constraint qualification (LICQ) criterion, i.e., the gradients of the active inequality constraints are linearly independent.

Proposition 1
.

Under Assumptions
3
and
4
, the unique and global solution to QP (
35
) at time step
k
is given by

\mathbf{u}_{k}^{\diamond}=\left(V_{k}^{\diamond}\mathbf{\tilde{M}}^{\diamond}_{k}H^{-1}-H^{-1}\right)h_{k}+V_{k}^{\diamond}\mathbf{\tilde{b}}_{k}^{\diamond},

(39)

where
V_{k}^{\diamond}=H^{-1}\mathbf{\tilde{M}}_{k}^{\diamond^{\top}}\left(\mathbf{\tilde{M}}_{k}^{\diamond}H^{-1}\mathbf{\tilde{M}}_{k}^{\diamond^{\top}}\right)^{-1}
,
\mathbf{\tilde{M}_{k}^{\diamond}}=[\mathbf{M}_{i:}]_{i\in\mathcal{A}_{k}^{\diamond}}
, and
\mathbf{\tilde{b}}_{k}^{\diamond}=[\mathbf{b}_{k,i}]_{i\in\mathcal{A}^{\diamond}_{k}}
.

Proof.

The result directly follows from applying the method of Lagrange multipliers
Ghojogh et al. 2021
;
Boyd & Vandenberghe 2004
to QP (
35
), exploiting sufficiency of the active set
Arnström 2023
.
We first formulate the Lagrangian as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{u}_{k},\mathbf{\mu}_{k})=\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{u}_{k}^{\top}H\mathbf{u}_{k}\!+\!h_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{u}_{k}\!+\!r_{k}\!+\!\bm{\mu}_{k}^{\top}(\mathbf{M}\mathbf{u}_{k}\!-\!\mathbf{b}_{k}),

(40)

where
\mathbf{\mu}_{k}
is the vector of Lagrange multipliers.
Applying the Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) conditions yields

\displaystyle H\mathbf{u}_{k}^{\diamond}+h_{k}+\mathbf{M}^{\top}\bm{\mu}^{\diamond}_{k}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{0}

(41a)

\displaystyle\mathbf{M}\mathbf{u}_{k}^{\diamond}-\mathbf{b}_{k}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{0}

(41b)

\displaystyle\bm{\mu}^{\diamond}_{k}

\displaystyle\geq\mathbf{0}

(41c)

\displaystyle\mu_{k,i}^{\diamond}(\mathbf{M}_{i:}\mathbf{u}_{k}^{\diamond}-\mathbf{b}_{k,i})

\displaystyle=0,\quad\forall i\in\llbracket n_{x}+n_{u}\rrbracket.

(41d)

According to the complementary slackness condition (
41d
), we have
\mu_{k,i}^{\diamond}=0,\forall i\in\llbracket n_{x}+n_{u}\rrbracket\setminus\mathcal{A}_{k}^{\diamond}
.
This enables to remove inactive inequality constraints from the problem formulation (c.f., sufficiency of the active set
Arnström 2023
).
Hence, the KKT system (
41
) is reduced to a linear system of equations given by

\begin{bmatrix}H&\mathbf{\tilde{M}}_{k}^{\diamond^{\top}}\\
\mathbf{\tilde{M}}_{k}^{\diamond}&\mathbf{0}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{u}_{k}^{\diamond}\\
\bm{\tilde{\mu}}_{k}^{\diamond}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}-h_{k}\\
\mathbf{\tilde{b}}_{k}^{\diamond}\end{bmatrix},

(42)

where
\bm{\tilde{\mu}}_{k}^{\diamond}=[\mathbf{\mu}_{k,i}^{\diamond}]_{i\in\mathcal{A}_{k}^{\diamond}},\mathbf{\tilde{M}_{k}^{\diamond}}=[\mathbf{M}_{i:}]_{i\in\mathcal{A}_{k}^{\diamond}}
,
\mathbf{\tilde{b}}_{k}^{\diamond}=[\mathbf{b}_{k,i}]_{i\in\mathcal{A}^{\diamond}_{k}}
and the dual feasibility condition (
41c
) is trivially fulfilled if
\mathcal{A}_{k}^{\diamond}
is the correct active set.
Exploiting invertibility of the coefficient matrix of (
42
) yields (
39
).
Expression (
39
) is guaranteed to be the optimal solution to QP (
35
) as the KKT conditions are necessary and sufficient under Assumption
4
.
The solution is unique and global as
H\succ 0
.
∎

The optimal inputs
\mathbf{u}^{\star}_{k}
and
\mathbf{u}^{\dagger}_{k}
are obtained using (
39
) when exact and DR constraint tightening are used, respectively.

Lemma 2

Under Assumptions

5

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[x^{\diamond}_{k+i}\in\Phi,\forall i\leq\bar{K}]\geq 1-\varepsilon.

Proof.

Lemma 3
.

Under Assumptions
1
-
5

\tilde{\beta}\in\mathcal{KL}

Proof.

Suppose that
x_{k}^{\star}\in\Omega^{\star}
and
x_{k}^{\dagger}\in\Omega^{\dagger}
and set
\bar{x}_{k}^{\star}=x_{k}^{\star}
and
\bar{x}_{k}^{\dagger}=x_{k}^{\dagger}

\mathbb{P}[x_{k+1}^{\star}\in\Phi,\forall i\leq\bar{K}]\geq 1-\varepsilon

\mathbb{P}[x_{k+i}^{\dagger}\in\Phi,\forall i\leq\bar{K}]\geq 1-\varepsilon

If

Hence, the error dynamics between the fully informed and the DR case is given by

As the same disturbances will act in both the fully informed and the DR case by Assumption

Exploiting stability according to Assumption
1
and Corollary

, the state trajectories of the fully informed and the DR converge to each other and we can bound the error from above by

\displaystyle\|e_{k+i}\|=\|\bar{x}_{k+i}^{\star}-\bar{x}_{k+i}^{\dagger}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|\bar{x}_{k+i}^{\star}\|+\|\bar{x}_{k+i}^{\dagger}\|

\displaystyle\leq\beta^{\star}(\|x_{k}^{\star}\|,i)+\beta^{\dagger}(\|x_{k}^{\dagger}\|,i)

\displaystyle=M^{\star}{(\nu^{\star})}^{i}\|\bar{x}_{k}^{\star}\|+M^{\dagger}{(\nu^{\dagger})}^{i}\|\bar{x}_{k}^{\dagger}\|

\displaystyle\leq\max\{M^{\star},M^{\dagger}\}(\max\{\nu^{\star},\nu^{\dagger}\})^{i}(\|\bar{x}_{k}^{\star}\|+\|\bar{x}_{k}^{\dagger}\|)

\displaystyle=:\tilde{\beta}(\|\bar{x}_{k}^{\star}\|+\|\bar{x}_{k}^{\dagger}\|,i)

5
Conclusion

A linear DR SMPC formulation exploiting moment-based ambiguity sets has been analyzed in regard of conservatism and regret.
We have analyzed the different aspects of regret, which together create the overall picture of its behavior.
Especially, regret was shown to converge to constant values over finite time periods and to increase only in between two such periods.
These findings were underlined by simulations.
Future research will be dedicated towards applying the established framework to extended SMPC formulations that may include online estimation or learning of unknown quantities.
Furthermore, the effect of non-uniform risk allocation on conservatism and regret is to be further investigated.

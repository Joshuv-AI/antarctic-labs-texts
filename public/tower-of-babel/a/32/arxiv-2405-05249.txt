Introduction

Let
\Gamma_{0}(1)=\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, and consider the hyperbolic surface
Y_{0}(1)=\Gamma_{0}(1)\backslash\mathbb{H}
. The volume form is
d\mu=y^{-2}dxdy
, and

H_{1}
and

\langle H_{1},H_{2}\rangle=\int_{Y_{0}(1)}H_{1}(z)\overline{H_{2}(z)}d\mu.

In this paper, we focus on a holomorphic variant of quantum unique ergodicity (QUE).
Let
z=x+iy
, and let

f(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{f}(n)n^{\frac{k-1}{2}}e^{2\pi inz}=e^{2\pi iz}+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\lambda_{f}(n)n^{\frac{k-1}{2}}e^{2\pi inz}

k

Consider a sequence of such

Write

\psi

(1.1)

\lim_{k\to\infty}\langle\psi,|F_{k}|^{2}\rangle=\frac{3}{\pi}\langle\psi,1\rangle.

[

,

If

For non-archimedean

(3.1)

At the archimedean place of

), we use the archimedean case of the local Langlands correspondence

]
(resp.

) and (

|\alpha_{j,j^{\prime},\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(p)|\leq p^{\theta_{m}+\theta_{m^{\prime}}},\qquad\Real(\mu_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(j,j^{\prime}))\geq-(\theta_{m}+\theta_{m^{\prime}}).

If

(3.6)

a_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(p^{k})=\begin{cases}a_{\pi}(p^{k})a_{\pi^{\prime}}(p^{k})&\text{if }p\nmid N_{\pi}N_{\pi^{\prime}},\\
\sum_{j=1}^{m}\sum_{j^{\prime}=1}^{m^{\prime}}\alpha_{j,j^{\prime},\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(p)^{k}&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

We define

by the Dirichlet series identity

(3.7)

-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})=\sum_{p}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(p^{k})\log p}{p^{ks}}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\Lambda_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(n)}{n^{s}},\quad\Real(s)>1.

As with

(3.8)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{C}(\pi\times\pi^{\prime},t)\coloneqq N_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\prod_{j^{\prime}=1}^{m^{\prime}}(3+|it+\mu_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(j,j^{\prime})|),\quad\mathfrak{C}(\pi\times\pi^{\prime})\coloneqq\mathfrak{C}(\pi\times\pi^{\prime},0).

The combined work of Bushnell and Henniart

and Brumley
[

yields

(3.9)

\mathfrak{C}(\pi\times\pi^{\prime},t)\ll\mathfrak{C}(\pi\times\pi^{\prime})(3+|t|)^{mm^{\prime}},\quad\mathfrak{C}(\pi\times\pi^{\prime})\ll\mathfrak{C}(\pi)^{m^{\prime}}\mathfrak{C}(\pi^{\prime})^{m}.

(Theorem 2 of

The second result is a Brun–Titchmarsh type upper bound with mild dependence on

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{m}
and
\pi^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{F}_{m^{\prime}}
. If
x\geq 2
and
\pi
satisfies GRC, then

\sum_{n\leq x}|\Lambda_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(n)|\ll x\sqrt{\log\mathfrak{C}(\pi^{\prime})}.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.2
and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we have that

(3.10)

\sum_{n\leq x}|\Lambda_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(n)|\leq\Big(\sum_{n\leq x}\Lambda_{\pi\times\widetilde{\pi}}(n)\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\sum_{n\leq x}\Lambda_{\pi^{\prime}\times\widetilde{\pi}^{\prime}}(n)\Big)^{1/2}.

Since
\pi
satisfies GRC, it follows from the prime number theorem that

(3.11)

\Big(\sum_{n\leq x}\Lambda_{\pi\times\widetilde{\pi}}(n)\Big)^{1/2}\leq\Big(\sum_{n\leq x}m^{2}\Lambda(n)\Big)^{1/2}\ll\sqrt{x}.

We now fix a smooth compactly supported function
\phi
with
\phi^{(j)}(y)\ll_{j}1
such that
\phi
is supported on
[1/2,5/2]
and at least
1
on
[1,2]
. Let
\widehat{\phi}
denote the Mellin transform of
\phi
, and let
\rho
range over all zeros of
L(s,\pi^{\prime}\times\widetilde{\pi}^{\prime})
. By the Mellin inversion formula and pushing the contour all the way to the left, we have that

(3.12)

\sum_{x\leq n\leq 2x}\Lambda_{\pi^{\prime}\times\widetilde{\pi}^{\prime}}(n)\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\Lambda_{\pi^{\prime}\times\widetilde{\pi}^{\prime}}(n)\phi(n/x)=x\widehat{\phi}(1)-\sum_{\rho}x^{\rho}\widehat{\phi}(\rho).

By (
3.2
) and (
3.5
), the contribution from trivial zeros is
\ll x
. For nontrivial zeros, we have that
|x^{\rho}|\leq x
, trivially. Write
\rho=\beta+i\gamma
. By integration by parts and the derivative estimates of
\phi
, it follows that
|\widehat{\phi}(\rho)|\ll_{\phi}(|\gamma|^{2}+1)^{-1}
. Since there are
O(\log\mathfrak{C}(\pi^{\prime}\times\widetilde{\pi}^{\prime},t))
zeros
\rho
in a unit strip around

(3.13)

\sum_{\rho}\widehat{\phi}(\rho)\ll\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}(\log\mathfrak{C}(\pi^{\prime}\times\widetilde{\pi}^{\prime})+\log(|t|+3))\frac{dt}{|t|^{2}+1}\ll\log\mathfrak{C}(\pi^{\prime}).

By (
3.12

\sum_{x\leq n\leq 2x}\Lambda_{\pi^{\prime}\times\widetilde{\pi}^{\prime}}(n)\ll x\log\mathfrak{C}(\pi^{\prime})

3.10
), and (
3.11
).
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Let

We first prove (

By Lemma

and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we have

(3.17)

(3.18)

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}(m)
and
\pi^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{F}(m^{\prime})
. Let
\pi
satisfy GRC. If

\sum_{n\leq x}|\lambda_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(n)|\ll x(\log x)^{\frac{m^{2}+1}{2}}\mathfrak{C}(\pi^{\prime})^{\varepsilon}.

Moreover, if

\sum_{x<n\leq x+y}|\lambda_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(n)|\ll x^{3/4}y^{1/4}(\log x)^{\frac{m^{4}+2}{4}}\mathfrak{C}(\pi^{\prime})^{\varepsilon}.

Proof.

The first assertion follows from (

3.6
and Rankin’s trick:

\sum_{n\leq x}|\lambda_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(n)|\leq x\sum_{n\leq x}\frac{|\lambda_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(n)|}{n}\ll x(\log x)^{\frac{m^{2}+1}{2}}\mathfrak{C}(\pi^{\prime})^{\varepsilon}.

For the second assertion, it follows from Lemma
3.3
and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality that

\sum_{x<n\leq x+y}|\lambda_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(n)|\leq\Big(\sum_{x<n\leq x+y}\lambda_{\pi\times\widetilde{\pi}}(n)\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\sum_{x<n\leq x+y}\lambda_{\pi^{\prime}\times\widetilde{\pi}^{\prime}}(n)\Big)^{1/2}.

\lambda_{\pi\times\widetilde{\pi}}(n)\leq\tau_{m^{2}}(n)

\Big(\sum_{x<n\leq x+y}\lambda_{\pi\times\widetilde{\pi}}(n)\Big)^{1/2}\leq y^{1/4}\Big(\sum_{x<n\leq x+y}\lambda_{\pi\times\widetilde{\pi}}(n)^{2}\Big)^{1/4}\leq(xy)^{1/4}\Big(\sum_{x<n\leq x+y}\frac{\tau_{m^{2}}(n)^{2}}{n}\Big)^{1/4}.

Since

\displaystyle(xy)^{1/4}\Big(\sum_{x<n\leq x+y}\frac{\tau_{m^{4}}(n)}{n}\Big)^{1/4}

\displaystyle\leq e(xy)^{1/4}\Big(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\tau_{m^{4}}(n)}{n^{1+1/\log(2ex)}}\Big)^{1/4}\ll(xy)^{1/4}(\log x)^{{m^{4}}/{4}}.

Since
\lambda_{\pi^{\prime}\times\widetilde{\pi}^{\prime}}(n)
is nonnegative, we apply Rankin’s trick and Theorem

\Big(\sum_{x<n\leq x+y}\lambda_{\pi^{\prime}\times\widetilde{\pi}^{\prime}}(n)\Big)^{1/2}\leq x^{1/2}\Big(e\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{\pi^{\prime}\times\widetilde{\pi}^{\prime}}(n)}{n^{1+1/\log(2ex)}}\Big)^{1/2}\ll x^{1/2}(\log x)^{1/2}\mathfrak{C}(\pi^{\prime})^{\varepsilon}.

4.
Weak subconvexity bound

We now establish Theorem
1.3
. Our proof follows the framework introduced by Soundararajan in
[
33
]
. Consequently, we will exclude specific details mirroring Soundararajan’s method and instead focus on highlighting the distinctive refinements in our approach.

4.1.
Notation

Consider
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{m}
and
\pi^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{F}_{m^{\prime}}
. We assume that
L(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})
is entire, and
\pi
satisfies GRC. Following is a list of the conventions and notation used in this section.

•

C=\mathfrak{C}(\pi\times\pi^{\prime})
.

•

x\geq 2
,
X\geq\max(x,10)
, and
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
.

•

S(x)=\sum_{n\leq x}\lambda_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(n)
and
\widetilde{S}(x)=\sum_{n\leq x}\lambda_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(n)\log n
.

•

R=\lfloor 72m^{2}/\varepsilon^{2}\rfloor+1
,
L=\lfloor 5(m^{2}+1)R\rfloor
and
\underline{L}=(L,\ldots,L)
.

•

w\in[2,\exp((\log X)^{1/(3R)})]
is an integer.

•

T=\exp((\log\log X)^{2})
and
\underline{\tau}=(\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\ldots,\tau_{R})\in\mathbb{R}^{R}
with
|\tau_{j}|\leq T
.

•

l_{1},\ldots,l_{R},j_{1},\ldots,j_{R}\in\mathbb{Z}
are nonnegative,
\underline{l}=(l_{1},\ldots,l_{R})
, and
\underline{j}=(j_{1},\ldots,j_{R})
.

•

\underline{j}\leq\underline{l}
indicates that
0\leq j_{1}\leq l_{1},\ldots,0\leq j_{R}\leq l_{R}
.

•

\binom{\underline{l}}{\underline{j}}=\binom{l_{1}}{j_{1}}\cdots\binom{l_{R}}{j_{R}}

\binom{a}{b}
are the binomial coefficients.

•

\mathcal{O}_{\underline{l}}(x,w)=\mathcal{O}_{\underline{l}}(x,w;\underline{\tau})=\sum_{\underline{j}\leq\underline{l}}(-1)^{j_{1}+\cdots+j_{R}}\binom{\underline{l}}{\underline{j}}w^{j_{1}(1+i\tau_{1})+\cdots+j_{R}(1+i\tau_{R})}S(x/w^{j_{1}+\cdots+j_{R}})
.

•

\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}_{\underline{l}}(x,w)=\mathcal{O}_{\underline{l}}(x,w;\underline{\tau})=\sum_{\underline{j}\leq\underline{l}}(-1)^{j_{1}+\cdots+j_{R}}\binom{\underline{l}}{\underline{j}}w^{j_{1}(1+i\tau_{1})+\cdots+j_{R}(1+i\tau_{R})}\widetilde{S}(x/w^{j_{1}+\cdots+j_{R}})
.

•

\mathcal{L}_{\underline{l}}(s)=L(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})\prod_{j=1}^{R}(1-w^{1+i\tau_{j}-s})^{l_{j}}
.

4.2.
Main results

As in Soundararajan’s work
[
33
]
, the essence of our weak subconvexity proof is the slow variation of the mean values of multiplicative functions. Consider the multiplicative function
\lambda_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(n)
. We will use the convexity bound for
L(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})
(see Lemma
4.4
) to prove that there exists a constant

such that

\sum_{n\leq x}\lambda_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(n)\ll\frac{x}{\log x}\mathfrak{C}(\pi^{\prime})^{\varepsilon},\qquad x\geq C^{1/2}(\log C)^{B_{0}}.

Next, we prove that for all
B>0
, similar cancellation holds even when

. In particular, we have the following theorem.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
be as in Theorem
1.3
. If
\varepsilon>0
,
B>0
, and
x\geq C^{1/2}(\log C)^{-B}

\sum_{n\leq x}\lambda_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(n)\ll_{B}\frac{x}{(\log x)^{1-\varepsilon}}\mathfrak{C}(\pi^{\prime})^{\varepsilon}.

Recall the notation in Section
4.1
. Theorem
4.1
follows from our next theorem.

Theorem 4.2
.

With the notation in Section
4.1
, there exist real numbers
\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{R}
, whose absolute values are at most
\exp((\log\log X)^{2})
, such that if
2\leq x\leq X
, then

|\mathcal{O}_{\underline{L}}(x,w;\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{R})|\ll\frac{x}{\log x}(\log X)^{\varepsilon}\mathfrak{C}(\pi^{\prime})^{\varepsilon}.

5.
Background on
\GL(2)
automorphic forms

We recall here some standard properties of
classical automorphic forms, including holomorphic newforms, Hecke–Maaß cusp forms, Eisenstein series, incomplete Eisenstein series, and incomplete Poincaré series. We will also discuss the spectral decomposition and the Poincaré decomposition. For complete definitions and proofs, see, e.g.,
[
14
]
,
[
27
]
, and
[
29
]
.

5.1.
Holomorphic newforms.

Let
k\geq 2
be an even integer and
\alpha\in\GL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
with positive determinant, where
\alpha
acts on
\mathbb{H}
by fractional linear transformations. Given a function
f:\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{C}
, we denote
j((\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}),z)=cz+d
and recall that
f|_{k}\alpha(z)=(\det\alpha)^{k/2}j(\alpha,z)^{-k}f(\alpha z)
.

A
holomorphic cusp form
on
\Gamma_{0}(q)
of weight
k
with trivial nebentypus is a holomorphic function
f:\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{C}
such that
f|_{k}\gamma=f
for all
\gamma\in\Gamma_{0}(q)
and
f
vanishes at the cusps of
\Gamma_{0}(q)
.
We denote the vector space of holomorphic cusp forms on
\Gamma_{0}(q)
of weight
k
with trivial nebentypus as
S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(q))
.

Define
S_{k}^{\new}(\Gamma_{0}(q))
to be the subspace of
S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(q))
that is orthogonal to the subspace of oldforms, denoted as
S_{k}^{\textup{old}}(\Gamma_{0}(q))
, with respect to the Petersson inner product.
A
holomorphic newform
is a cusp form in
S_{k}^{\new}(\Gamma_{0}(q))
that is also an eigenform of all of the Hecke operators and all of the Atkin–Lehner involutions. The space
S_{k}^{\new}(\Gamma_{0}(q))
has a basis of normalized newforms, which we denote as
\mathcal{B}_{k}(q)
(see more discussion in
[
29
, Chapter 2, Section 5]
).

Consider
f\in\mathcal{B}_{k}(q)
. Let
e(z)=e^{2\pi iz}
and let
\lambda_{f}(n)
be the eigenvalue of the
n
-th Hecke operator
T_{n}
. Then the Fourier expansion of
f
can be written as

(5.1)

f(z)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\lambda_{f}(n)n^{\frac{k-1}{2}}e(nz),

where
\lambda_{f}(1)=1
. If we let
\tau(n)
denote the number of positive divisors of
n
, then
\lambda_{f}(n)
is real, multiplicative and satisfies Deligne’s bound
|\lambda_{f}(n)|\leq\tau(n)
.

For any
\gamma\in\Gamma_{0}(q)
and
f,g\in\mathcal{B}_{k}(q)
, if we write
z^{\prime}=\gamma z=x^{\prime}+iy^{\prime}
, then

y^{\prime k/2}f(z^{\prime})=(j(\gamma,z)/|j(\gamma,z)|)^{k}y^{k/2}f(z).

This observation implies the
\Gamma_{0}(q)
-invariance for both
z\mapsto y^{k}|f(z)|^{2}
and
z\mapsto y^{k}f(z)\overline{g(z)}
.
Recall that we write
F_{k}(z)=\rho_{f}(1)y^{k/2}f(z)
,
where
\rho_{f}(1)
is chosen such that
\langle 1,|F_{k}(z)|^{2}\rangle_{q}=1
. By the Rankin–Selberg theory, we have

(5.2)

|\rho_{f}(1)|^{2}\asymp\frac{(4\pi)^{k}}{kq\Gamma(k-1)L(1,\ad f)},

where the implied constants are absolute.

5.2.
Cusps of
\Gamma_{0}(q)

We collect here some properties of cusps of
\Gamma_{0}(q)
(see
[
27
, Section 3.4]
for more details). Let
\tau
traverse a set of representatives for the double coset space
\Gamma_{\infty}\backslash\Gamma_{0}(1)/\Gamma_{0}(q)
. Then
\mathfrak{a}:=\tau^{-1}\infty\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q})
traverses a set of inequivalent cusps of
\Gamma_{0}(q)
. Denote the set of cusps of
\Gamma_{0}(q)
as
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}(\Gamma_{0}(q))=\{\mathfrak{a}_{j}\}_{j}
. The width of
\mathfrak{a}_{j}=\tau_{j}^{-1}\infty
is given by
w_{j}=[\Gamma_{\infty}:\Gamma_{\infty}\cap\tau_{j}\Gamma_{0}(q)\tau_{j}^{-1}]
, and the scaling matrix of
\mathfrak{a}_{j}
is

(5.3)

\sigma_{j}=\tau_{j}^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}w_{j}&\\
&1\end{pmatrix}.

If the bottom row of
\sigma_{j}
is
(c_{j},d_{j})
, then we define
c_{j}
as the denominator of the cusp
\mathfrak{a}_{j}
. Such
c_{j}
is a positive divisor of
q
, and we have that the width
w_{j}
equals
q/(c_{j}^{2},q)=[q/c_{j}^{2},1]
. For each positive divisor
c|q
, if we denote the set of cusps of denominator
c
by

(5.4)

\mathcal{C}[c]:=\{\mathfrak{a}_{j}\in\mathcal{C}:c_{j}=c\},

then
\#\mathcal{C}[c]
equals
\varphi((c,q/c))
. If
q
is squarefree, then
\#\mathcal{C}[c]=1
. In that case, the multiset of the widths of cusps of
\Gamma_{0}(q)
is
\{d\in\mathbb{Z}:d|q,d\geq 1\}
.

We now collect necessary properties of the Fourier expansion of
|f|^{2}
at the cusp
\mathfrak{a}_{j}
. For any positive divisor
c|q
and
d\in(\mathbb{Z}/(c,q/c))^{\times}
, we write
\mathfrak{a}_{d/c}\in\mathcal{C}(\Gamma_{0}(q))
for the corresponding cusp. For
\mathfrak{a}_{j}=\mathfrak{a}_{d/c}
, we may take
\tau_{j}=\begin{pmatrix}*&*\\
c&d^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}
for any integer
d^{\prime}
for which
(d^{\prime},c)=1
and
d^{\prime}\equiv d\pmod{(c,q/c)}
. Such
\tau_{j}
’s then form a set of representatives of
\Gamma_{\infty}\backslash\Gamma_{0}(1)/\Gamma_{0}(q)
, and the corresponding scaling matrix
\sigma_{j}
is defined as in (
5.3
).

Write
z_{j}=x_{j}+iy_{j}
for the change of variable
z_{j}:=\sigma_{j}^{-1}z
. We observe that
|f(z)|^{2}y^{k}=|f(\sigma_{j}z_{j})|^{2}\Imag(\sigma_{j}z_{j})^{k}=|f|_{k}\sigma_{j}(z_{j})|^{2}y_{j}^{k}
. For some coefficients
\lambda_{j}(n)\in\mathbb{C}
, we may write

(5.5)

f|_{k}\sigma_{j}(z_{j})=\sum_{n\geq 1}\lambda_{j}(n)n^{\frac{k-1}{2}}e(nz_{j}).

In the special case that
\mathfrak{a}_{j}=\infty
, we observe that
\lambda_{j}(n)=\lambda(n).

In general,
\lambda_{j}
is not multiplicative, so we instead work with the root-mean-square of
\lambda_{j}
taken over all cusps of a given denominator. For each positive divisor
c
of
q
, we define

(5.6)

\lambda_{[c]}(n):=\Big(\frac{1}{\#\mathcal{C}[c]}\sum_{\mathfrak{a}_{j}\in\mathcal{C}[c]}|\lambda_{j}(n)|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}.

With the above definition and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we observe that

(5.7)

\sum_{a_{j}\in\mathcal{C}}|\lambda_{j}(n_{1})\lambda_{j}(n_{2})|\leq\sum_{c\mid q}\#\mathcal{C}[c]\lambda_{[c]}(n_{1})\lambda_{[c]}(n_{2}).

5.3.
Hecke–Maaß cusp forms

A
Maaß cusp form

\phi
of level 1 is an
\Gamma_{0}(1)
-invariant eigenfunction of the hyperbolic Laplacian
\Delta:=-y^{2}(\partial_{x}^{2}+\partial_{y}^{2})
on
\mathbb{H}
that decays rapidly at infinity. We denote the spectral parameter of
\phi
by
t_{\phi}
, which is given by
\Delta\phi=(1/4+t_{\phi}^{2})\phi
.
A
Hecke–Maaß cusp form
is a Maaß cusp form that is also an eigenfunction of all of the Hecke operators and the involution
T_{-1}:\phi\mapsto[z\mapsto\phi(-\overline{z})],
which commute with one another as well as with
\Delta
. A Hecke–Maaß cusp form
\phi
admits a Fourier expansion

\phi(z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\neq 0}}\frac{\lambda_{\phi}(n)}{\sqrt{|n|}}\kappa_{\phi}(ny)e(nx),

where
\kappa_{\phi}(y)=2|y|^{1/2}K_{ir}(2\pi|y|)\mathrm{sgn}(y)^{\frac{1-\delta}{2}}
with
K_{ir}
the standard
K
-Bessel function,
\mathrm{sgn}(y)
the sign function, and
\delta\in\{\pm 1\}
the
T_{-1}
-eigenvalue of
\phi
.

We normalize a Hecke–Maaß cusp form
\phi
such that
\langle\phi,\phi\rangle_{1}=1
and assume that
\phi
is even; otherwise,
\langle\phi,|F_{k}|^{2}\rangle_{q}=0
. Define
N_{\Gamma_{0}(1)}(T):=|\{\phi:|t_{\phi}|\leq T\}|
, the number of Hecke–Maaß cusp forms such that
|t_{\phi}|\leq T
. By Weyl’s law (see, e.g.,
[
15
, Corollary 11.2]
), we have

(5.8)

N_{\Gamma_{0}(1)}(T)=\frac{T^{2}}{12}+O(T\log T).

5.4.
Eisenstein series

Let
s\in\mathbb{C}
,
z\in\mathbb{H}
, and
\Gamma_{\infty}=\big\{\pm\big(\begin{smallmatrix}1&n\\
&1\end{smallmatrix}\big):n\in\mathbb{Z}\big\}
. The
real-analytic Eisenstein series
of level 1 is defined as

E(s,z)=\sum_{\Gamma_{\infty}\backslash\Gamma_{0}(1)}\Imag(\gamma z)^{s}.

The series
E(s,z)
converges normally for
\Real(s)>1
and continues meromorphically to
\Real(s)\geq 1/2
, where
E(s,z)
has a unique simple pole at
s=1
of residue
\res_{s=1}E(s,z)=3/\pi
.

Let
\zeta(s)
be the Riemann zeta function,
\lambda_{s}(n)=\sum_{ab=n}(a/b)^{s}
, and
\theta(s)=\pi^{-s}\Gamma(s)\zeta(2s)
. The Eisenstein series admits the Fourier expansion

E(s,z)=y^{s}+\frac{\theta(1-s)}{\theta(s)}y^{1-s}+\frac{2\sqrt{y}}{\theta(s)}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\neq 0}}\tau_{s-1/2}(|n|)K_{s-1/2}(2\pi|n|y)e(nx).

For
\Real(s)=1/2
, we obtain
|\theta(1-s)/\theta(s)|=1
, and such
E(s,z)
is called a
unitary Eisenstein series
. Let
E_{t}
denote
E(1/2+it,\cdot)
. The work of Huang–Xu
[
9
, Theorem 1]
shows that for
y\geq 1/2
, if
z=x+iy
, then

(5.9)

|E_{t}(z)|\ll\sqrt{y}(1+|t|)^{3/8+\varepsilon}.

5.5.
Spectral decomposition

Let
Y_{0}(1)=\Gamma_{0}(1)\backslash\mathbb{H}
, and let
L^{2}(Y_{0}(1))
denote the space of square-integrable functions on
Y_{0}(1)
. One can decompose any
\psi\in L^{2}(Y_{0}(1))
into three types of spectra: a constant function, Hecke–Maaß cusp forms, and unitary Eisenstein series.

Lemma 5.1

(Theorem 15.5 of
[
14
]
)
.

Let
\{\phi_{j}\}_{j}
be an orthonormal basis of Hecke–Maaß cusp forms. For any
t\in\mathbb{R}
, let
E_{t}
be a unitary Eisenstein series. If
\psi\in L^{2}(Y_{0}(1))
, then

\psi(z)=\frac{3}{\pi}\langle\psi,1\rangle_{1}+\sum_{j\geq 1}\langle\psi,\phi_{j}\rangle_{1}\phi_{j}(z)+\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\langle\psi,E_{t}\rangle_{1}E_{t}(z)dt.

The above decomposition gives us that

(5.10)

\langle\psi,\overline{F_{k}}G_{k}\rangle_{q}=\frac{3}{\pi}\langle\psi,1\rangle_{1}\mathds{1}_{f=g}+\sum_{j\geq 1}\langle\psi,\phi_{j}\rangle_{1}\langle\phi_{j},\overline{F_{k}}G_{k}\rangle_{q}+\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\langle\psi,E_{t}\rangle_{1}\langle E_{t},\overline{F_{k}}G_{k}\rangle_{q}dt.

To obtain a rate of convergence for
\langle\psi,\overline{F_{k}}G_{k}\rangle_{q}
, we need to estimate
\langle\phi_{j},\overline{F_{k}}G_{k}\rangle_{q}
,
\langle E_{t},\overline{F_{k}}G_{k}\rangle_{q}
,
\langle\psi,\phi_{j}\rangle_{1}
, and
\langle\psi,E_{t}\rangle_{1}
. For the last two quantities, we recall the following lemma.

Lemma 5.2

(Lemma 2.2 of
[
10
]
)
.

Let
M\geq 1
,
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(Y_{0}(1),M)
, and
\phi
be a Hecke–Maaß cusp form with spectral parameter
t_{\phi}
. If
A\geq 0
, then

|\langle\psi,\phi\rangle_{1}|\ll_{A}\Big(\frac{M}{1+|t_{\phi}|}\Big)^{A}\quad\text{and}\quad|\langle\psi,E_{t}\rangle_{1}|\ll_{A}\Big(\frac{M}{1+|t|}\Big)^{A}(1+|t|)^{3/8+\varepsilon}.

5.6.
Incomplete Poincaré series and the Poincaré decomposition.

Recall the notation in Section
1
. We discuss here the Poincaré decomposition, which plays a crucial role when applying Holowinsky’s method in Section
8.2
(see more details in
[
7
, Section 4]
).

Let
M\geq 1
and
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(Y_{0}(1),M)
. Let
\check{\psi}
be the function on
\mathbb{H}
to
\mathbb{C}
such that
\check{\psi}(z)=\psi(z)
if
z\in\mathcal{F}
, and
\check{\psi}(z)=0
otherwise.
Let
\check{\Psi}(z)
be the extension of
\check{\psi}(z)
to
\mathbb{H}
by
\Gamma_{\infty}=\big\{\pm\big(\begin{smallmatrix}1&n\\
&1\end{smallmatrix}\big):n\in\mathbb{Z}\big\}
.
For any
z=x+iy\in\mathbb{H}
, we denote the
m
-th Fourier coefficients of
\check{\Psi}(z)
as

\Psi_{m}(y)=\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}\check{\Psi}(x+iy)e(-mx)\,dx.

By integration by parts, we have that

(5.11)

|\Psi_{m}(y)|\ll_{A}(M/|m|)^{A}.

Then the Fourier expansion of
\check{\Psi}(z)
can be defined as

\check{\Psi}(z)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\Psi_{m}(y)e(mx).

For any
\Psi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, a smooth function on
\mathbb{R}
, we define the
incomplete Poincaré series
as

P_{m}(z,\Psi)=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{\infty}\backslash\Gamma_{0}(1)}e(m\Real(\gamma z))\Psi(\Imag(\gamma z)).

When
m=0
,
P_{0}(z,\Psi)
is called the
incomplete Eisenstein series
, also denoted by
E(\cdot|\Psi)
. Observe that
\psi(z)=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{\infty}\backslash\Gamma_{0}(1)}\check{\Psi}(\gamma z)
. Then we have that

(5.12)

\psi(z)=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{\infty}\backslash\Gamma_{0}(1)}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\Psi_{m}(\Imag(\gamma z))e(m\Real(\gamma z))=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}P_{m}(z,\Psi_{m}).

For any
\Psi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, let
\widehat{\Psi}(s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\Psi(y)y^{s-1}\,dy

denote the Mellin transform of
\Psi(y)
.
By unfolding, we have
\langle E(\cdot|\Psi),1\rangle_{1}=\widehat{\Psi}(-1)
and
\langle P_{m}(\cdot,\Psi),1\rangle_{1}=0
. By (
5.12
), it follows that

(5.13)

\langle\psi,\overline{F_{k}}G_{k}\rangle_{q}-\frac{3}{\pi}\langle\psi,1\rangle_{1}\mathds{1}_{f=g}\\
=\langle E(\cdot|\Psi_{0}),\overline{F_{k}}G_{k}\rangle_{q}-\frac{3}{\pi}\widehat{\Psi}_{0}(-1)\mathds{1}_{f=g}+\sum_{m\neq 0}\langle P_{m}(\cdot,\Psi_{m}),\overline{F_{k}}G_{k}\rangle_{q}.

7.
Bounds for

In this section, we prove lower bounds for the adjoint lift of a holomorphic newform. We also establish bounds for

-functions that will be useful for proving Propositions

and

Let
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{2}
be non-dihedral with central character
\omega_{\pi}
. The adjoint
L
-function of
\pi
is

L(s,\ad\pi)=L(s,\pi\times\widetilde{\pi})\zeta(s)^{-1}.

Since
\pi
is non-dihedral, Gelbert and Jacquet
[
4
]
shows that
\ad\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{3}
. Thus,
L(s,\ad\pi)
admits all of the analytic properties of a standard
L
-function in Section
3.1
.

If
\pi
is a holomorphic newform of weight
k
, the gamma factor of
L(s,\ad\pi)
is defined as

L_{\infty}(s,\ad\pi)=\pi^{-\frac{3s}{2}}\Gamma\Big(\frac{s+1}{2}\Big)\Gamma\Big(\frac{s+k-1}{2}\Big)\Gamma\Big(\frac{s+k}{2}\Big),

and if
\pi
is a Hecke–Maaß form with eigenvalue
\lambda=1/4+t^{2}
, we have

L_{\infty}(s,\ad\pi)=\pi^{-\frac{3s}{2}}\Gamma\Big(\frac{s}{2}\Big)\Gamma\Big(\frac{s}{2}+it\Big)\Gamma\Big(\frac{s}{2}+it\Big).

Since
\pi
is non-dihedral,
L(s,\ad\pi)
has no pole at
s=1
, so the completed
L
-function

\Lambda(s,\ad\pi)=N_{\ad\pi}^{s/2}L(s,\ad\pi)L_{\infty}(s,\ad\pi)

is entire of order 1, and satisfies the functional equation
\Lambda(s,\ad\pi)=\Lambda(1-s,\ad\pi)
.

Write
\mathfrak{C}(\pi)=N_{\pi}K_{\pi}
and
\mathfrak{C}(\ad\pi)=N_{\ad\pi}K_{\ad\pi}
. Then
N_{\ad\pi}=N_{\pi\times\widetilde{\pi}}\mid N_{\pi}^{2}
and
K_{\ad\pi}\asymp K_{\pi}
. We now refine the work of Goldfeld, Hoffstein, and Lieman in
[
5
, Appendix]
.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{2}
be non-dihedral with central character
\omega_{\pi}
. There exists an absolute and effectively computable constant
>0
such that
L(s,\ad\pi)\neq 0
in the region

(7.1)

\mathrm{Re}(s)\geq 1-\frac{\Cr{zfr}}{\log(N_{\ad\pi}K_{\pi}(|t|+3))},

and

(7.2)

L(1,\ad\pi)\gg(\log N_{\ad\pi}K_{\pi})^{-1}.

Proof.

The zero-free region (
7.1
) holds when
t\neq 0
by Theorem A.1 in
[
11
, Appendix]
. When
t=0
, the proof is the same as in
[
5
, Appendix]
apart from the observation that

\varphi(s)=\zeta(s)L(s,\ad\pi)^{2}L(s,\ad\pi\times\ad\pi)=L(s,(\mathbbm{1}\boxplus\ad\pi)\times(\mathbbm{1}\boxplus\ad\pi)).

The analytic conductor of
\varphi(s)
is
O((N_{\ad\pi}K_{\pi})^{8})
, which permits us to express the zero-free region for
L(s,\ad\pi)
in terms of
N_{\ad\pi}
instead of
N_{\pi}
. Lastly, (
7.2
) follows from the proof of the main theorem in
[
5
, Appendix]
, but we replace their zero-free region with (

).
∎

Recall the notation in Sections
1
and
5.1
. For

>0

The
\mathrm{GL}_{2}\times\mathrm{GL}_{3}

by Jesse Thorner

Let

k\geq 2
and
\phi
be a Hecke–Maaß cusp form, both of level 1. Each corresponds with a certain unitary cuspidal automorphic representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})

and
\lambda_{\phi}(n)

and

Let

is a normalizing constant so that if
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is the Petersson inner product for
Y_{0}(1)

Iwaniec
[
16

and Lester, Matomäki, and Radziwiłł
[

describe a method for showing that

without a weak subconvexity bound for

\langle\phi,|F_{k}|^{2}\rangle

\phi
-dependence.

It is proved in
[
16
, (3.2) and (11.1)]
and
[
22
, (4.2)]
that

(A.1)

|\langle\phi,|F_{k}|^{2}\rangle|\ll_{A,\phi,\varepsilon}\frac{(\log\log k)^{3}}{\sqrt{k}}|L(\tfrac{1}{2},\mathrm{Ad}\,f\times\phi)|^{\frac{1}{2}}\prod_{p\leq k}\Big(1-\frac{\lambda_{f}(p^{2})}{p}\Big)+\frac{1}{(\log k)^{A}}.

A bound for

essentially of the form

(A.2)

|L(\tfrac{1}{2},\mathrm{Ad}\,f\times\phi)|\ll_{A,\phi,\varepsilon}\sum_{n\leq k^{2}(\log k)^{\varepsilon}}\frac{|\lambda_{f}(n^{2})\lambda_{\phi}(n)|}{\sqrt{n}}+\frac{k}{(\log k)^{A}}

(a weak form of the approximate functional equation) was asserted without proof in
[
16

and
[
22
, (4.3)]

A.1
below.

Theorem A.1
.

Let

be a holomorphic cuspidal Hecke eigenform and
\phi
be a Hecke–Maaß newform, each of level
1

such that in the region

By multiplicativity of the Hecke eigenvalues, it suffices to work locally. Let
p
be prime. For any

of conductor
1
, we have that

\displaystyle L_{p}(s,\pi)

\displaystyle=\prod_{\ell\in\{-1,1\}}\frac{1}{1-\alpha_{\pi}(p)^{\ell}p^{-s}}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{\pi}(p^{j})}{p^{js}},

\displaystyle=\prod_{\ell\in\{-1,0,1\}}\frac{1}{1-\alpha_{\pi}(p)^{2\ell}p^{-s}}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{\mathrm{Ad}\,\pi}(p^{j})}{p^{js}}.

We write

Note that

and

A.3

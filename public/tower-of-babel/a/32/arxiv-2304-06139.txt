Dynamics of packed swarms: time-displaced correlators of two dimensional incompressible flocks
Leiming Chen, Chiu Fan Lee, Ananyo Maitra, John Toner

Abstract
We analytically calculate the scaling exponents of a two-dimensional KPZ-like system: coherently moving incompressible polar active fluids. Using three different renormalization group approximation schemes, we obtain values for the “roughness” exponent χ \chi and anisotropy exponent ζ \zeta that are extremely near the known exact results. This implies our prediction for the previously completely unknown dynamic exponent z z is quantitatively accurate.



The Mermin-Wagner theorem
MW
states that in equilibrium, two-dimensional systems with finite-range interactions cannot spontaneously break a continuous symmetry at any finite temperature.
One of the earliest and most surprising results in the field of active matter was that there is no analogue of the Mermin-Wagner theorem in nonequilibrium matter. In particular, it was
explicitly
demonstrated that active polar units moving on a two-dimensional frictional substrate and with

purely
short-range interactions
can
spontaneously break continuous rotation symmetry in two dimensions and form long-range ordered flocks
Vicsek et al. 1995
;
Toner and Tu 1995
;
Toner and Tu 1998
;
toner_pre12
;
Chate_DADAM_rev
. Such flocks have a
non-zero
mean speed
\langle{\bf v}\rangle
even at finite noise strengths.

Despite the fact that
the
existence
of long-range-ordered two-dimensional flocks has been demonstrated analytically, determining their scaling behavior analytically has proved much more challenging.
One class of systems that
has
proved amenable to analytical treatment is
incompressible
flocks
inc_trans
;
Chen et al. 2016
;
Chen
;
Ano_pol
;
CLMT-PRL1
;
CLMT-PRE1
.

Incompressibility can arise in
many ways. One way is to make the density extremely high. In this limit
the effective compressibility of the flockers vanishes, with any departure from the mean density being severely penalised
Chen et al. 2016
;
Ano_pol
. More accurately, as the compressibility goes to
0
, the lengthscale up to which the system is effectively incompressible diverges. A flock formed by a suspension of active swimmers in an incompressible fluid in a narrow channel also inherits the incompressibility of the fluid and is therefore incompressible. This is technically distinct from the model we will consider here due to the presence of an extra conserved quantity – the number of swimmers. However, if the number of active units is
not
conserved, for instance, due to birth and death
Toner_Malthus
;
Chen et al. 2020a
;
Chen et al. 2020b
, the dynamics of such a system does belong to the “incompressible flock” universality class we consider here.

Our understanding of the
equal-time
behavior of incompressible two dimensional flocks is quite complete. Although they are non-equilibrium systems, their hydrodynamic equations prove to be equivalent, once terms that are “irrelevant” in the renormalization group sense are dropped, to those of an equilibrium magnetic system
Chen et al. 2016
;
Kashuba
with long-ranged interactions. Because of those long-ranged interactions, the Mermin-Wagner theorem does not apply to these magnetic systems. The partition function for this equilibrium system can then be further mapped
Chen et al. 2016
onto that for a two-dimensional smectic, whose equal-time scaling laws are known
exactly
via a further mapping
Golub
onto the
1+1
-dimensional KPZ equation
KPZ
. This analysis
Chen et al. 2016
gives the scaling law for the equal time fluctuations
\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{r},t)
of the local active fluid velocity
\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{r},t)
about its mean value
\langle\mathbf{v}\rangle\equiv v_{0}\hat{\bf x}
, where we’ve defined our coordinate system so that
{\bf\hat{x}}
is along the mean velocity spontaneously chosen by the system. Specifically

\langle\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{r},t)\cdot\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{0},t)\rangle=|x|^{2\chi}{\cal G}_{{}_{ET}}\left({|y|\over|x|^{\zeta}}\right)\,,

(1)

where “ET” stands for “equal-time”. The exponents
\chi
and
\zeta
were determined

by the aforementioned mappings
Chen et al. 2016
to be
\chi=-1/2
and
\zeta=3/2
.

Unfortunately, since this analysis was based entirely on the partition function for the equivalent equilibrium model, no information about the
dynamics
of the system can be obtained by this exact mapping. Most of this missing information can be encoded in a single additional universal exponent, namely, the “dynamical” exponent
z
. This can be defined by considering the
unequal
- time correlation function of the velocity fluctuations, which obeys the scaling law

\displaystyle\langle\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{r},t)\cdot\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{0},0)\rangle

(2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|x-v_{b}t|^{2\chi}{\cal G}_{{}_{UET}}\left({|y|\over|x-v_{b}t|^{\zeta}},{|t|\over|x-v_{b}t|^{z}}\right)\,,

and the subscript “UET” now means “unequal-time”. The ”boost velocity

is a phenomenological parameter of our model.

In this Letter, we obtain the dynamical exponent

for the polar phase of two-dimensional, incompressible flocks using three different dynamic renormalization group (DRG)
schemes: two different uncontrolled calculations exactly in two dimensions, and a
d=(d_{c}-\epsilon)
-expansion.
We get

z=1.65\pm 0.06\,,

(3)

where the error bars correspond to the averages over the three DRG schemes.
We also calculate the the roughness exponent
\chi
and the anisotropic exponent

As mentioned earlier, we already know the static exponents

and

and

We therefore believe our prediction (

Inserting (

We’ll now present the drivation of the above results. This begins with

Finally,

Expanding (

\displaystyle-\partial_{i}\Pi+\mu_{\perp}\partial_{y}^{2}u_{i}+\mu_{x}\partial_{x}^{2}u_{i}

-{\rm i}\omega u_{y}(\tilde{\mathbf{q}})=P_{yx}\left(\mathbf{q}\right)\mathcal{F}_{\tilde{\mathbf{q}}}\left[-\alpha\left(u_{x}+{u_{y}^{2}\over 2}\right)\right]+P_{yy}\left(\mathbf{q}\right)\mathcal{F}_{\tilde{\mathbf{q}}}\left[-\alpha\left(u_{x}+{u_{y}^{2}\over 2}\right)u_{y}+\mu_{x}\partial_{x}^{2}u_{y}+f_{y}\right]\,,

(1)

where

\tilde{\mathbf{q}}\equiv(\omega,\mathbf{q})\,,~~~P_{yy}(\mathbf{q})=q_{x}^{2}/q^{2}\,,~~~P_{xy}(\mathbf{q})=-q_{x}q_{y}/q^{2}\,.

(2)

We can solve (
1
) for
u_{y}(\tilde{\mathbf{q}})
to get

\displaystyle u_{y}(\tilde{\mathbf{q}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G(\tilde{\mathbf{q}})\Bigg[P_{yy}(\mathbf{q})f_{y}(\tilde{\mathbf{q}})+\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)P_{yx}(\mathbf{q})\int_{\tilde{\mathbf{k}}}u_{y}(\tilde{\mathbf{k}})u_{y}(\tilde{\mathbf{q}}-\tilde{\mathbf{k}})-\alpha P_{yy}(\mathbf{q})\int_{\tilde{\mathbf{k}}}u_{y}(\tilde{\mathbf{k}})u_{x}(\tilde{\mathbf{q}}-\tilde{\mathbf{k}})

(3)

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)P_{yy}(\mathbf{q})\int_{\tilde{\mathbf{k}},\tilde{\mathbf{k}}^{\prime}}u_{y}(\tilde{\mathbf{k}})u_{y}(\tilde{\mathbf{k}}^{\prime})u_{y}(\tilde{\mathbf{q}}-\tilde{\mathbf{k}}-\tilde{\mathbf{k}}^{\prime})\Bigg]\,,

where

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbf{k}}\equiv(\Omega,\mathbf{k})\,,~~~\tilde{\mathbf{k}}^{\prime}\equiv(\Omega^{\prime},\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\,,

(4)

\displaystyle\int_{\tilde{\mathbf{k}}}\equiv\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{{\rm d}\Omega\over\sqrt{2\pi}}\int{{\rm d}^{d}k\over(2\pi)^{d/2}}\,,~~~\int_{\tilde{\mathbf{k}}^{\prime}}\equiv\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{{\rm d}\Omega^{\prime}\over\sqrt{2\pi}}\int{{\rm d}^{d}k^{\prime}\over(2\pi)^{d/2}}\,,

(5)

where the integrals of
\mathbf{k}
and
\mathbf{k}^{\prime}
are over the entire Brillouin zone. The propagator
G(\tilde{\mathbf{q}})
is defined as:

G(\tilde{\mathbf{q}})=\frac{1}{-{\rm i}\omega+\mu_{x}q_{x}^{2}+\mu_{\perp}q_{y}^{2}+\alpha\frac{q_{y}^{2}}{q^{2}}}\approx\frac{1}{-{\rm i}\omega+\mu_{x}q_{x}^{2}+\alpha\frac{q_{y}^{2}}{q_{x}^{2}}}\ ,

(7)

where the second, approximate, equality is asymptotically exact in the long wave length limit
q\to 0
.

The statistics of the random force
f_{y}
in Fourier space are given by

\langle f_{y}(\tilde{\mathbf{q}})f_{y}(\tilde{\mathbf{q}}^{\prime})\rangle=2D\delta^{d}(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{q}^{\prime})\delta(\omega+\omega^{\prime})\ .

(8)

In the linear approximation, Eq. (
3
) reduces to

u_{y}(\tilde{\mathbf{q}})=G(\tilde{\mathbf{q}})P_{yy}(\mathbf{q})f_{y}(\tilde{\mathbf{q}})\,.

(9)

Autocorrelating both sides of (
11
) we get

\langle u_{y}(\tilde{\mathbf{q}})u_{y}(\tilde{\mathbf{q}}^{\prime})\rangle=2D\left(q_{x}^{2}\over q^{2}\right){C(\tilde{\mathbf{q}})\delta(\omega+\omega^{\prime})\delta(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{q}^{\prime})}\,,

(10)

where

C(\tilde{\mathbf{q}})=\frac{2D}{\omega^{2}+\left(\mu_{x}q_{x}^{2}+\alpha{q_{y}^{2}\over q_{x}^{2}}\right)^{2}}\ .

(11)

Combining this with the incompressibility constraint
u_{x}(\tilde{\mathbf{q}})=-q_{y}u_{y}(\tilde{\mathbf{q}})/q_{x}

leads to the correlators for other components of
\mathbf{u}(\tilde{\mathbf{q}})
:

\displaystyle\langle u_{x}(\tilde{\mathbf{q}})u_{x}(\tilde{\mathbf{q}}^{\prime})\rangle=2D\left(q_{y}^{2}\over q^{2}\right){C(\tilde{\mathbf{q}})\delta(\omega+\omega^{\prime})\delta(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{q}^{\prime})},

(12a)

\displaystyle\langle u_{x}(\tilde{\mathbf{q}})u_{y}(\tilde{\mathbf{q}}^{\prime})\rangle=-2D\left(q_{x}q_{y}\over q^{2}\right)C(\tilde{\mathbf{q}}){\delta(\omega+\omega^{\prime})\delta(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{q}^{\prime})}\,.

(12b)

Adding (
10
) and (
12a
) together, we get the full auto-correlator of
\mathbf{u}
:

\langle\mathbf{u}(\tilde{\mathbf{q}})\cdot\mathbf{u}(\tilde{\mathbf{q}}^{\prime})\rangle=2D{C(\tilde{\mathbf{q}})\delta(\omega+\omega^{\prime})\delta(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{q}^{\prime})}\,.

(13)

We see that in the low frequency and small wavenumber limit (i.e.,
\omega\to 0
,
q\to 0
)
\langle\mathbf{u}(\tilde{\mathbf{q}})\cdot\mathbf{u}(\tilde{\mathbf{q}}^{\prime})\rangle
diverges most strongly in the regime
\omega\sim q_{y}\sim q_{x}^{2}
. In this dominant regime,
P_{yy}(\mathbf{q})
can be approximated as 1. Therefore, the EOM (
3

\displaystyle u_{y}(\tilde{\mathbf{q}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G(\tilde{\mathbf{q}})\Bigg[f_{y}(\tilde{\mathbf{q}})+\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)P_{yx}(\mathbf{q})\int_{\tilde{\mathbf{k}}}u_{y}(\tilde{\mathbf{k}})u_{y}(\tilde{\mathbf{q}}-\tilde{\mathbf{k}})-\alpha\int_{\tilde{\mathbf{k}}}u_{y}(\tilde{\mathbf{k}})u_{x}(\tilde{\mathbf{q}}-\tilde{\mathbf{k}})

(14)

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)\int_{\tilde{\mathbf{k}},\tilde{\mathbf{k}}^{\prime}}u_{y}(\tilde{\mathbf{k}})u_{y}(\tilde{\mathbf{k}}^{\prime})u_{y}(\tilde{\mathbf{q}}-\tilde{\mathbf{k}}-\tilde{\mathbf{k}}^{\prime})\Bigg]\,.

\mathbf{u}(\tilde{\mathbf{q}})
into “slow” and “fast” parts
\mathbf{u}^{<}(\tilde{\mathbf{q}})
and
\mathbf{u}^{>}(\tilde{\mathbf{q}})
, which are supported on small
\mathbf{q}
’s and large
\mathbf{q}

(

after the decomposition are represented by the graphs in Figs.

and

Next we iteratively solve for

4
represents a contribution to the large wave length noise
G(\tilde{\mathbf{q}})f_{y}^{<}(\tilde{\mathbf{q}})

Eventually, eliminating

\displaystyle u^{<}_{y}(\tilde{\mathbf{q}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G(\tilde{\mathbf{q}})\Bigg[f^{<}_{y}(\tilde{\mathbf{q}})+\delta f^{<}_{y}(\tilde{\mathbf{q}})+{\rm i}\omega\left(\eta_{\omega}{\rm d}\ell\right)u^{<}_{y}(\tilde{\mathbf{q}})-\alpha\left(\eta_{\alpha}^{dir}{\rm d}\ell\right)P_{yx}(\mathbf{q})u_{x}^{<}(\tilde{\mathbf{q}})-\mu_{x}\left(\eta_{\mu}^{dir}{\rm d}\ell\right)q_{x}^{2}u^{<}_{y}(\tilde{\mathbf{q}})\Bigg]

\displaystyle+G(\tilde{\mathbf{q}})\Bigg\{\alpha\left(1+\eta_{\alpha}^{dir}{\rm d}\ell\right)\mbox{NL}\left[\mathbf{u}^{<}(\tilde{\mathbf{q}})\right]\Bigg\}\,,

where

stands for the nonlinear part:

\displaystyle\mbox{NL}\left[\mathbf{u}(\tilde{\mathbf{q}})\right]=\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)P_{yx}(\mathbf{q})\int_{\tilde{\mathbf{k}}}u_{y}(\tilde{\mathbf{k}})u_{y}(\tilde{\mathbf{q}}-\tilde{\mathbf{k}})-\int_{\tilde{\mathbf{k}}}u_{y}(\tilde{\mathbf{k}})u_{x}(\tilde{\mathbf{q}}-\tilde{\mathbf{k}})-\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)\int_{\tilde{\mathbf{k}},\tilde{\mathbf{k}}^{\prime}}u_{y}(\tilde{\mathbf{k}})u_{y}(\tilde{\mathbf{k}}^{\prime})u_{y}(\tilde{\mathbf{q}}-\tilde{\mathbf{k}}-\tilde{\mathbf{k}}^{\prime})\,.

(16)

The autocorrelation of the noise contribution

is given by

\langle 2\delta f^{<}_{y}(\tilde{\mathbf{q}})\delta f^{<}_{y}(\tilde{\mathbf{q}}^{\prime})\rangle=2\left(1+\eta_{D}^{dir}{\rm d}\ell\right)D\delta^{d}(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{q}^{\prime})\delta(\omega+\omega^{\prime})\,.

The detailed calculation of

We multiply both sides of (

\left[G(\tilde{\mathbf{q}})\right]^{-1}

and rearrange the terms to get

\displaystyle-{\rm i}\omega(1+\eta_{\omega}{\rm d}\ell)u^{<}_{y}(\tilde{\mathbf{q}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Bigg[f^{\prime}_{y}(\tilde{\mathbf{q}})-\alpha\left(1+\eta_{\alpha}^{dir}{\rm d}\ell\right)P_{yx}(\mathbf{q})u_{x}^{<}(\tilde{\mathbf{q}})-\mu_{x}\left(1+\eta_{\mu}^{dir}{\rm d}\ell\right)q_{x}^{2}u^{<}_{y}(\tilde{\mathbf{q}})\Bigg]

\displaystyle+\Bigg\{\alpha\left(1+\eta_{\alpha}^{dir}{\rm d}\ell\right)\mbox{NL}\left[\mathbf{u}^{<}(\tilde{\mathbf{q}})\right]\Bigg\}\,.

\displaystyle-{\rm i}\omega u^{<}_{y}(\tilde{\mathbf{q}})

\displaystyle D_{r}={\left(1+\eta_{D}^{dir}{\rm d}\ell\right)D\over(1+\eta_{\omega}{\rm d}\ell)^{2}}=\left[1+\left(\eta_{D}^{dir}-2\eta_{\omega}\right){\rm d}\ell\right]D\,,

Note that the graphical correction for each parameter has two parts: the
\eta
with superscript “dir” and
\eta_{\omega}
(or 2
\eta_{\omega}
in the case of

This symmetry argument implies that we do not have to explicitly calculate graphical corrections to the quadratic and cubic vertices themselves, thus simplifying our calculation considerably.

Also note that EOM (
1
) can be written as a detailed-balance-obeying dynamics of the form

\partial_{t}u_{l}=-\Gamma\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}}{\delta u_{l}}+f_{l}

(24)

where

is the Hamiltonian for a divergence-free magnet, and the friction coefficient

is taken to be unity. This dynamics obeys fluctuation-dissipation theorem which

be preserved under renormalization. This imposes a constraint: the ratio of the remormalized noise strength to the renormalized friction coefficient is fixed
The renormalized friction coefficient appears as the common factor

on the RHS in (

Inserting (

\displaystyle\eta_{D}=-\eta^{dir}_{D}\,,

(26)

\displaystyle\eta_{\alpha}=\eta^{dir}_{\alpha}-\eta_{D}^{dir}\,,

(27)

\displaystyle\eta_{\mu}=\eta^{dir}_{\mu}-\eta_{D}^{dir}\,.

(28)

That is, because of (
25
) , we do not need to explicitly calculate the graphical corrections to
-i\omega u^{<}_{y}(\tilde{\mathbf{q}})
.

Finally we rescale time, lengths, and fields as

\displaystyle t\to te^{z{\rm d}\ell},~~x\to xe^{{\rm d}\ell},~~y\to ye^{\zeta{\rm d}\ell},

(29)

\displaystyle u_{y}\to u_{y}e^{\chi{\rm d}\ell},~~u_{x}\to u_{x}e^{\left(\chi+1-\zeta\right){\rm d}\ell}\,,

(30)

to restore the Brillouin zone to its original size.

Correlation length lower bound for the random-field Potts model with the greedy lattice animal
Pete Rigas

Abstract
Motivated by recent developments over the past few years in the study of the correlation length of the random-field Ising model due to Ding and Wirth in a paper first available in 2020, we pursue one natural direction of research that the authors propose is of interest, namely in confirming that the same scaling for the correlation length for the random-field Ising model equals that of the random-field Potts model. To demonstrate that the 4 3 \frac{4}{3} emergence in the correlation length scaling for the random-field Potts model coincides with that correlation length scaling for the random-field Ising model, we refer to arguments due to Talagrand, in which an upper bound on the greedy lattice animal readily provides a corresponding lower bound on the correlation length. Contributions from the 4 3 \frac{4}{3} exponent appearing in the correlation length scaling for the random-field Ising, and random-field Potts models alike are reminiscent of 4 3 \frac{4}{3} exponents in upper bounds taken under the Liouville quantum gravity metric in high temperature. 1 1 1 Keywords : Random-field Ising model, Random-field Potts model, Imry-Ma phenomenon, spontaneous magnetization, greedy lattice animal

Introduction

1.1
Overview

The random-field Ising model, as a variant of the classic Ising model, has received attention for behavior in three-dimensions at zero temperature
[2]
, lower critical dimensions
[10,11]
, universality in three-dimension permitting for exact computation of all critical exponents
[8]
, correlation inequalities
[4]
, algorithmic complexity and connections to phase transitions
[1]
, long range order
[6]
, replica symmetry breaking
[16]
, supersymmetry
[23]
, thermal ground states
[25]
, and other related developments
[7,10,15,17,20]
. As a generalization of the Ising model, the Potts model instead has spins that take on an arbitrary, strictly positive, number of
q
values, with its randomized-field version also being subject of interest, particularly relating to characterizations of dipolar-like interactions
[3]
, a phase diagram in three dimensions
[9]
, with related characterizations of phase transitions
[24]
, critical behavior for the three-state, random-field, model
[12]
, approximation of ground states from graph cuts
[13]
, formulation of algorithms for quasi-exact approximation of ground states
[14]
, modeling of polar domain interactions of Magnesium compounds
[18]
, and simulating behaviors via Monte Carlo
[19]
. In
[5]
, the authors make use of arguments provided in
[21,22]
to obtain upper and lower bounds for the correlation length of the random-field Ising model. To extend their arguments to other models of interest in Statistical Mechanics, specifically the random-field Potts model which they remark is expected to have the same correlation length scaling, we introduce the quantities used throughout the argument for obtaining the lower bound on the correlation length.

Paper organization

We provide an overview of the random-field Potts model (RFPM), and then describe the extension of arguments presented in
[5]
for obtaining the same correlation length scaling obtained for the random-field Ising model.

3
From upper bounds on the greedy lattice animal to lower bounds on the correlation length

In the following arguments, we make use of the following procedure for constructing polygons.

Algorithm
(
constructing polygons for obtaining the
\frac{4}{3}
exponent
). Fix
N\geq\mathrm{exp}\big(C\epsilon^{-\frac{4}{3}}\big)
, and denote,

\displaystyle\underset{0\leq\alpha\leq 1-q}{\underset{i\in A}{{\sum}}}h^{\alpha}_{i}\equiv w\big(A\big)\text{ }\text{ . }

To construct a polygon
P\subset[-N,N]^{2}
,

\bullet

Step 1
(
initialization
). Set
P_{1}\equiv[-\frac{N}{2},\frac{N}{2}]^{2}
.

\bullet

Step 2
(
constructing
P_{2}
from
P_{1}
). For each side
S
of
P_{1}
, construct an isosceles triangle
T_{S}
, with length
\frac{l(S)}{2}
, and height
\frac{\epsilon^{\frac{2}{3}}l(S)}{8}
. With probability
\frac{1}{2}
, if
w\big(T_{S}\big)>0
, append
T_{s}
to
P_{1}
. Otherwise, if
w\big(T_{S}\big)<0
, partition
S
into four sides of equal length, from which
the next polygon
P_{2}
is obtained from
P_{1}
by applying the procedure described earlier in this step for obtaining triangles of prescribed lengths and heights.

\bullet

Step 3
(
iterating the procedure to obtain the polygon
P_{k+1}
from the polygon
P_{k}
). Given
P_{k}
, construct
P_{k+1}
by applying
Step 2

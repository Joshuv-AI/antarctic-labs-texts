A refinement of bounded cohomology
Światosław Gal, Jarek Kędra

Abstract
. We introduce a refinement of bounded cohomology and prove that the suitable comparison homomorphisms vanish for an amenable group. We investigate in this context Thompson’s group F F and provide further evidence towards its amenability. We show that the space of 1-bounded cocycles of degree two is essentially as big as the space of Lipschitz functions on the underlying group. We also explain that such classes define metrics on the group.



1.
Semibounded cohomology

Let
\Gamma
be a discrete group.
An
n
-cochain

\omega\colon\Gamma^{n}\to{\mathbf{R}}
is called
p
-bounded
if for fixed

g_{p+1},\dots,g_{n}\in\Gamma
the function

\Gamma^{p}\ni g_{1},\dots,g_{p}\mapsto\omega(g_{1},\dots,g_{p},g_{p+1},\dots,g_{n})

is bounded. For example, an
n
-bounded cochain is bounded in the usual sense.
It is straightforward to check that
the subcomplex
C^{*}_{(p)}(\Gamma;{\mathbf{R}})\subseteq C^{*}(\Gamma;{\mathbf{R}})

consisting of
p
-bounded cochains is preserved by the codifferential.
It’s cohomology will be denoted
H^{n}_{(p)}(\Gamma;{\mathbf{R}})
.
The inclusions
C^{*}_{(p)}(\Gamma;{\mathbf{R}})\subseteq C^{*}_{(p-1)}(\Gamma;{\mathbf{R}})

induce maps in cohomology

H^{n}_{(n)}(\Gamma;{\mathbf{R}})\to H^{n}_{(n-1)}(\Gamma;{\mathbf{R}})\to\cdots\to H^{n}_{(1)}(\Gamma;{\mathbf{R}})\to H^{n}_{(0)}(\Gamma;{\mathbf{R}})=H^{n}(\Gamma;{\mathbf{R}}).

called
comparison homomorphisms
.
In what follows, we will refer to
H^{n}(\Gamma;{\mathbf{R}})
as standard cohomology.

It is well known
[
6
, Section 3.0]
that if a group
\Gamma
is
amenable then its bounded cohomology vanishes in positive degrees. In
particular, the comparison homomorphisms between bounded and standard
cohomology are trivial. We generalise this result to the
p
-bounded
cohomology.

Theorem 1.1
.

If
\Gamma
is an amenable group then the comparison homomorphisms

H^{n}_{(p)}(\Gamma;{\mathbf{R}})\to H^{n}_{(p-1)}(\Gamma;{\mathbf{R}})

vanish for all
n>0
and
p=1,2,\dots,n
.

Proof.

The argument follows the proof of the fact that bounded cohomology
of an amenable group vanishes in positive degree.
Let
{\mathfrak{m}}
be a right-invariant mean on
\Gamma
. Define a map

M\colon C^{n+1}_{(p)}(\Gamma;{\mathbf{R}})\to C^{n}_{(p-1)}(\Gamma;{\mathbf{R}})
as

M\omega(g_{1},\dots,g_{n})=\int\omega(h,g_{1},\dots,g_{n})\,{\mathfrak{m}}(h).

Then

\displaystyle M\delta\omega(g_{1},\dots,g_{n})

\displaystyle=\int\delta\omega(h,g_{1},\dots,g_{n})\,{\mathfrak{m}}(h)

\displaystyle=\int\omega(g_{1},\dots,g_{n}){\mathfrak{m}}(h)

\displaystyle\hphantom{=}-\int\omega(hg_{1},g_{2},\dots,g_{n})\,{\mathfrak{m}}(h)+\int\omega(h,g_{1}g_{2},\dots,g_{n})\,{\mathfrak{m}}(h)\pm\dots

\displaystyle\overset{!}{=}\omega(g_{1},\dots,g_{n})

\displaystyle\hphantom{=}-\int\omega(h,g_{2},\dots,g_{n})\,{\mathfrak{m}}(h)+\int\omega(h,g_{1}g_{2},\dots,g_{n})\,{\mathfrak{m}}(h)\pm\dots

\displaystyle=\omega(g_{1},\dots,g_{n})-M\omega(g_{2},\dots,g_{n})+M\omega(g_{1}g_{2},\dots,g_{n})\mp\dots

\displaystyle=\omega(g_{1},\dots,g_{n})-\delta M\omega(g_{1},\dots,g_{n}),

where, in the marked equality, we used, for the second term, invariance of
{\mathfrak{m}}
.
We obtain that

M\delta\omega+\delta M\omega=\omega

and hence
M
is a homotopy for the comparison map.
∎

Remark 1.2
.

In this paper we are interested in cohomology with trivial real coefficients,
but parts of it could be written without a change for any coefficients with a metric (
e.g.
normed
vector space) on which the group acts in uniformly bounded way. Otherwise,
p
-bounded cocycles
may not form a chain complex.
∎

Remark 1.3
.

One could use another definition of
p
-boundedness.
Namely, an
n
-cochain

\omega\colon\Gamma^{n}\to{\mathbf{R}}
is called
left-
p
-bounded
if for fixed

g_{1},\dots,g_{n-p}\in\Gamma
the function

\Gamma^{p}\ni g_{n-p+1},\dots,g_{n}\mapsto\omega(g_{1},\dots,g_{p-n},g_{p-n+1},\dots,g_{n})

is bounded. Such definition (of 1-bounded 2-cocycle) appears in
[
4
, Section 4]

under the name of
semibounded
cocycle, where the authors use it
to prove Polterovich’s theorem stating that
on the group of symplectic diffeomorphisms of a symplectically hyperbolic
manifold all its elements are undistorted
[
4
, Theorem 5.2]
.

Nevertheless, the involution

\widehat{\omega}(g_{1},\dots,g_{n})=\omega(g_{n}^{-1},\dots,g_{1}^{-1})

is a chain map that interchanges
p
-bounded and left-
p
-bounded cocycles
(with trivial coefficients), so both notions define isomorphic objects.
∎

Remark 1.4
.

A similar concept to 1-bounded cocycles was defined by Neumann and Reeves

[
11
]
for 2-cocycles and by Frigerio and Sisto

[
3
]
for any degree under the name
weakly bounded

cocycles. Both papers consider cocycles that take finite values as first
argument varies.

Weakly bounded 2-cocycles were used to characterise central extensions of
finitely generated groups that are quasi-isometric with trivial extensions.
It was first observed by Gersten that this holds if the definig cocycle for an
extension is bounded. Necessary and sufficient condition in terms of

\ell^{\infty}
-cohomology (see
[
10
]
) was given by
Kleiner and Leeb
[
9
, Proposition 8.3]
. The characterisation in
terms of weakly bounded cocycles is due to Frigerio and Sisto
[
3
, Corollary
2.5]
.

The connection between weakly bounded cocycles and
\ell^{\infty}
-cohomology
is a direct analogy to our Theorem
2.3
.
∎

2.
The 1-bounded cohomology

Let
\ell^{\infty}\Gamma
denote the left module of bounded functions on
\Gamma

with the action defined by
(hF)(g)=F(gh)
.

Lemma 2.1
.

The map
\Lambda_{0}\colon C^{n}_{(1)}(\Gamma;{\mathbf{R}})\to C^{n-1}(\Gamma;\ell^{\infty}\Gamma)

given by the formula

\Lambda_{0}\omega(g_{1},\dots,g_{n})(h)=\omega(h,g_{1},\dots,g_{n})

is a homotopy, i.e.
\delta\Lambda_{0}\omega+\Lambda_{0}\delta\omega=\omega
.

Proof.

\displaystyle\Lambda_{0}\delta\omega(g_{0},\dots,g_{n})(h)

\displaystyle=\delta\omega(h,g_{0},\dots,g_{n})

\displaystyle=\omega(g_{0},\dots,g_{n})

\displaystyle\hphantom{=}-\omega(hg_{0},\dots,g_{n})+\omega(h,g_{0}g_{1},\dots,g_{n})\pm\dots

\displaystyle=\omega(g_{0},\dots,g_{n})

\displaystyle\hphantom{=}-\Lambda_{0}\omega(g_{1},\dots,g_{n})(hg_{0})+\Lambda_{0}\omega(g_{0}g_{1},\dots,g_{n})(h)\pm\dots

\displaystyle=\omega(g_{0},\dots,g_{n})-\delta\Lambda_{0}\omega(g_{0},\dots,g_{n})(h).

∎

The induced surjective map
\Lambda\colon C^{n}_{(1)}(\Gamma;{\mathbf{R}})\to C^{n-1}(\Gamma;\ell^{\infty}\Gamma/{\mathbf{R}})

anticommutes with the codifferential. The kernel consists of cocycles that do not depend on the first variable.
We call them
1-constant
and denote by
C^{n}_{[1]}(\Gamma,{\mathbf{R}})
.

Lemma 2.2
.

The complex of 1-constant cochains with restricted codifferential is acyclic.

Proof.

A straightforward computation shows that
\delta
preserves 1-constant cochains.
The map
H
defined by

H\omega(g_{2},\dots,g_{n})=\omega(1,1,g_{3},\dots,g_{n})

is a homotopy proving acyclicity.
∎

Consider the following short exact sequence

0\to C^{n}_{[1]}(\Gamma,{\mathbf{R}})\to C^{n}_{(1)}(\Gamma;{\mathbf{R}})\to C^{n-1}(\Gamma;\ell^{\infty}\Gamma/{\mathbf{R}})\to 0

and the induced long exact sequence in cohomology. As a corollary, we
deduce the following theorem.

Theorem 2.3
.

The map induced by
\Lambda
,

H^{n}_{(1)}(\Gamma;{\mathbf{R}})\to H^{n-1}(\Gamma;\ell^{\infty}\Gamma/{\mathbf{R}})

is an isomorphism.
Its composition with the Bockstein homomorphism

H^{n-1}(\Gamma;\ell^{\infty}\Gamma/{\mathbf{R}})\to H^{n}(\Gamma;{\mathbf{R}})

corresponding to the short exact sequence of coefficients

{\mathbf{R}}\to\ell^{\infty}\Gamma\to\ell^{\infty}\Gamma/{\mathbf{R}}

is equal to the comparison map
H^{n}_{(1)}(\Gamma;{\mathbf{R}})\to H^{n}(\Gamma;{\mathbf{R}})
.

Proof.

Let
\omega\in C^{n}_{(1)}(\Gamma;{\mathbf{R}})
be a cocycle.
Then
\Lambda_{0}(\omega)\in C^{n-1}(\Gamma;\ell^{\infty}\Gamma)
is
the lift of
\Lambda(\omega)\in C^{n-1}(\Gamma;\ell^{\infty}\Gamma/{\mathbf{R}})
.
By definition, the Bockstein homomorphism, sends
[\Lambda(\omega)]
to

[\delta\Lambda_{0}(\omega)]
. Since
\omega
is a cocycle we get that

\delta\Lambda_{0}(\omega)=\omega-\Lambda_{0}(\delta\omega)=\omega
which proves the
statement.
∎

Notice that a group
\Gamma
is amenable if and only if the short exact
sequence of coefficients
0\to{\mathbf{R}}\to\ell^{\infty}\Gamma\to\ell^{\infty}\Gamma/{\mathbf{R}}\to 0
splits.
In such a case the Bockstein map is trivial in accordance with Theorem
1.1
.

3.
1-bounded 2-cocycles

In this section we discuss two constructions of pseudometrics on a group
defined by 1-bounded 2-cocycles (in what follows, we will abuse terminology
and refer to them as metrics). The first metric is defined by cocycles that
are trivial in the standard cohomology. The second construction is defined for
arbitrary cocycles.

We start with the first construction.
Recall, that any bounded 2-cocycle in the kernel of the comparison homomorphism

H^{2}_{b}(\Gamma;{\mathbf{R}})\to H^{2}(\Gamma;{\mathbf{R}})
is a codifferential of a
quasimorphism,
i.e.
, a function
\phi\colon\Gamma\to{\mathbf{R}}
such that

\sup_{g,h\in\Gamma}|\phi(g)-\phi(gh)+\phi(h)|<\infty
. Thus the kernel of the comparison
map can be identified with the space of quasimorphisms divided by the sum of
the space of homomorphisms (annihilated by the codifferential) and the space of bounded functions
(which correspond to coboundaries).

Similarly, any 1-bounded 2-cocycle in the kernel of the comparison homomorphism

H^{2}_{(1)}(\Gamma;{\mathbf{R}})\to H^{2}(\Gamma;{\mathbf{R}})

is a codifferential of a function
\phi\colon\Gamma\to{\mathbf{R}}
such that
for each fixed
g\in\Gamma
we have,
\sup_{h\in\Gamma}|\phi(g)-\phi(gh)+\phi(h)|<\infty
. That is,

\|g\phi-\phi\|_{0}:=\sup_{h\in\Gamma}|\phi(gh)-\phi(h)|<\infty.

We call such functions
abstractly Lipshitz
.
The above argument can be then summed up as follows.

Proposition 3.1
.

The kernel of the comparison map
H^{2}_{(1)}(\Gamma;{\mathbf{R}})\to H^{2}(\Gamma;{\mathbf{R}})

is isomorphic to the quotient on abstractly Lipschitz functions divided
by the sum of the space of homomorphisms and the space of bounded functions.∎

On the other hand, given abstractly Lipshitz function
\phi
, the map

\delta\phi(h)=h\phi-\phi
is a 1-cocycle with values in
\ell^{\infty}\Gamma

and taking the class of
\delta\phi
in
H^{1}(\Gamma;\ell^{\infty}\Gamma/{\mathbf{R}})\cong H^{2}_{(1)}(\Gamma;{\mathbf{R}})
corresponds to taking class of
\phi
modulo
homomorphisms (constant cocycles) and bounded functions (coboundaries).

The name
abstractly Lipschitz
is motivated by the following observation.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\phi
be a function on a group
\Gamma
.
The following are equivalent

(1)

\phi
is abstractly Lipschitz,

(2)

there exists a length function
|\cdot|
on
\Gamma
,
such that
\phi
is Lipschitz with respect to
|\cdot|
.

Proof.

Assume (1). Then
\phi
is Lipschitz with respect to

|g|_{\phi}=\|g\phi-\phi\|_{0}.

Assume (2). Then
\|g\phi-\phi\|_{0}\leq|g|
.
∎

Notice that if
\Gamma
is finitely generated then abstractly Lipschitz
functions are Lipschitz with respect to the word norm associated
with a finite generating set.

The second construction goes as follows.
Let
\omega
be an arbitrary 1-bounded 2-cocycle. For a function
\psi\in\ell^{\infty}\Gamma

define
|\psi|_{\mathrm{osc}}=\sup_{h,h^{\prime}\in\Gamma}|\psi(h)-\psi(h^{\prime})|
and define
\|g\|_{\omega}=|\omega(g,\cdot)|_{\mathrm{osc}}
.

Proposition 3.3
.

The function
\|\cdot\|_{\omega}
is a well defined metric on
\Gamma
. Moreover, if
\eta
is a bounded 1-cochain,
then
\|\cdot\|_{\omega}
and
\|\cdot\|_{\omega+\delta\eta}
are within finite (at most
2|\eta|_{\mathrm{osc}}
) distance.

Proof.

By cocycle identity

\displaystyle\omega(g_{1}g_{2},h)-\omega(g_{1}g_{2},h^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\omega(g_{2},h)-\omega(g_{1},g_{2}h)+\omega(g_{1},g_{2})

\displaystyle\hphantom{=}-\omega(g_{2},h^{\prime})+\omega(g_{1},g_{2}h^{\prime})-\omega(g_{1},g_{2})

\displaystyle=\omega(g_{2},h)-\omega(g_{2},h^{\prime})+\omega(g_{1},g_{2}h)-\omega(g_{1},g_{2}h^{\prime}).

Taking supremum of the absolute value of the above over all
h
and
h^{\prime}
we get
the triangle identity.
The last statement is obvious.
∎

One can rephrase the above statement, that every element of
H^{2}_{(1)}(\Gamma,{\mathbf{R}})
defines
a class of metrics on
\Gamma
which are within finite distance from each other.

In
[
4
, Theorem 5.2]
we essentially prove that a certain
1-bounded 2-cocycle
{\mathfrak{G}}
defines a metric on the group of Hamiltonian
diffeomorphisms
\operatorname{Ham}(X,\sigma)
of a symplectically hyperbolic
manifold
(X,\sigma)
and prove that each element of this group is undistorted
with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{{\mathfrak{G}}}
. Notice, that in
[
4
]
the
function
\|\cdot\|_{{\mathfrak{G}}}
was defined slightly differently and did not
satisfy triangle inequality (cf.
[
4
, Lemma 4.1]
).

Let us compare the two constructions. Given a 1-bounded 2-cocycle in the
kernel of the comparison map, we defined two norms
\|\cdot\|_{\delta\phi}

and
|\cdot|_{\phi}
. Since

\displaystyle\delta\phi(g,h)-\delta\phi(g,h^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\phi(g)-\phi(gh)+\phi(h)-\phi(g)+\phi(gh^{\prime})-\phi(h^{\prime})

\displaystyle=(g\phi-\phi)(h^{\prime})-(g\phi-\phi)(h),

we see that
\|g\|_{\delta\phi}=|g\phi-\phi|_{\mathrm{osc}}\leq 2|g|_{\phi}
.

4.
Hyperbolic classes

A good source of 1-bounded classes is provided by hyperbolic cocycles which
are defined as follows.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
X
be a finite CW–complex and let

p\colon\widetilde{X}\to X
be its universal cover.
A cellular cocycle
\alpha\in C^{k}(X;{\mathbf{R}})
is called
hyperbolic
if
the cocycle
p^{*}\alpha
is a coboundary of a bounded cochain.
A cohomology class in
H^{k}(X;{\mathbf{R}})
is called hyperbolic if it is represented
by a hyperbolic cocycle.

The notion of a hyperbolic class is inspired by Gromov
[
7
]

who considered differential forms
\alpha
with the property that the
lift
p^{*}\alpha
to the universal cover has a primitive that is bounded with
respect to the Riemannian induced metric. By integrating such classes over
cells we get that they yield hyperbolic classes in our sense.
Moreover, Brunnbauer and Kotschick
[
2
]
defined and investigated
hyperbolic cohomology classes on general Riemannian simplicial complexes and
proved vanishing theorems for complexes with amenable groups (see Proposition
5.1
).

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
X
be a finite connected CW–complex with
\pi_{1}(X)=\Gamma
and let

\alpha\in C^{k}(X;{\mathbf{R}})
be a hyperbolic cocycle. If the universal cover of
X

is
(k-1)
-connected then there exists a 1-bounded cocycle

\omega\in C^{k}_{(1)}(\mathrm{B}\Gamma;{\mathbf{R}})
such that
c^{*}[\omega]=[\alpha]
, where

c\colon X\to\mathrm{B}\Gamma
is the map classifying the universal cover.

Proof.

Let
\mathrm{E}\Gamma^{\prime}
be an invariant subdivision of the
k
-skeleton of the simplicial
complex
\mathrm{E}\Gamma
corresponding to the bar resolution.
There exists an equivariant map
f\colon\mathrm{E}\Gamma^{\prime}\to\widetilde{X}
, such that the
composition
c\circ f
is homotopic to the inclusion
\mathrm{E}\Gamma^{\prime}\to\mathrm{E}\Gamma
.

Let
\alpha\in C^{k}(X;{\mathbf{R}})
be a hyperbolic cocycle and let

p^{*}(\alpha)=\delta\beta
, where
\beta
is a bounded cochain.
As
\mathrm{E}\Gamma^{\prime}
is a subdivision of the
k
-skeleton of
\mathrm{E}\Gamma
, the cochain
f^{*}\beta
can be considered as a cochain
on
\mathrm{E}\Gamma
.
Since
\beta
is not required to be
\Gamma
-equivariant, neither is
f^{*}\beta
.
Let
\overline{\beta}
be a
\Gamma
-
equivariant
cochain on

\mathrm{E}\Gamma
that agrees with
f^{*}\beta
on simplices whose first vertex is
1
.

It follows that the differentials
\delta f^{*}\beta
and
\delta\overline{\beta}

differ in only one term. That is, if
\omega=\delta(f^{*}\beta-\overline{\beta})

then

\omega(g_{1},\dots,g_{k})=\langle g_{1}^{*}\beta-\beta,f_{*}\triangle(g_{2},\dots,g_{k})\rangle,

where
\triangle(g_{2},\dots,g_{k})
is the subdivision in
\mathrm{E}\Gamma^{\prime}

of a simplex of
\mathrm{E}\Gamma
with vertices
1,g_{2},g_{2}g_{3},\dots,g_{2}\cdots g_{k}
.
Since
\beta
is bounded and the subdivision of
\triangle(g_{2},\dots,g_{k})

does not depend on
g_{1}
we see that
\omega
is 1-bounded.
It follows that
[\omega]=f^{*}[\alpha]
, and thus
c^{*}[\omega]=[\alpha]

which finishes the proof.
∎

5.
Hyperbolic classes and amenability

It immediately follows from Theorems
1.1
and
4.2
that
any hyperbolic class on a CW–complex
X
as above is trivial if
\pi_{1}(X)
is
amenable. However, a more direct proof yields the following slightly stronger
observation originally due to Brunnbauer Kotschick
[
2
, Theorem
3.2]
who used an isoperimetric characterisation of manifolds
with amenable fundamental group due to Brooks.
A different argument was used in
[
8
]
to show that the
fundamental group of a closed symplectically hyperbolic manifold cannot be
amenable.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
X
be a finite complex and let
\alpha
be a hyperbolic cocycle on
X
.
If the fundamental group of
X
is amenable, then
\alpha
is a coboundary.

Proof.

Let
p^{*}\alpha=\delta\beta
with
\beta
bounded cochain on
\widetilde{X}
.
By averaging this equation with respect to a mean on the deck transformation
group
\pi_{1}(X)
we find
\beta
which is
\pi_{1}(X)
–invariant, thus

\beta
is a lift of a cocycle
\beta^{\prime}
on
X
and
\alpha=\delta\beta^{\prime}

which proves the claim.
∎

6.
Test case: Thompson’s group
F

It is a stimulating open question to determine whether Thompson’s group
F
is
amenable. Since it is a split extension

[F,F]\to F\to{\mathbf{Z}}^{2},

its amenability is equivalent to the amenability of its commutator subgroup.
The cohomology ring
H^{*}([F,F];{\mathbf{R}})={\mathbf{R}}[u]
, where
\deg(u)=2
.
The explicit cocycle
\alpha
representing
u
has been given by Ghys and Sergiescu

[
5
, Corollaire 4.5]
. Let
{\mathbf{Z}}^{2}\to[F,F]
be an injective
homomorphism generate by two commuting nontrivial elements.
It is easy to choose such elements so that the pull-back of
u
is nontrivial
in
H^{2}({\mathbf{Z}}^{2};{\mathbf{R}})
and, since
{\mathbf{Z}}^{2}
is amenable, it can’t be represented
by a 1-bounded cocycle. More generally, we have the following observation
which provides further evidence for a possible amenability of
F
.

Proposition 6.1
.

For every positive integer
n\in{\mathbf{N}}
there exists an injective homomorphism

\psi\colon{\mathbf{Z}}^{2n}\to[F,F]
such that
\psi^{*}(u^{n})\neq 0

in
H^{2n}({\mathbf{Z}}^{2n};{\mathbf{R}})
. Consequently,
u^{n}
cannot be represented
by a 1-bounded cocycle.

Proof.

Let
F^{\prime}=[F,F]
denote the commutator subgroup of Thompson’s group
F
.
Let
\alpha\colon F^{\prime}\times F^{\prime}\to{\mathbf{R}}
be a cocycle defined by the
following formula

\alpha(f,g)=\sum_{x}\det\left(\begin{smallmatrix}log_{2}\ g^{\prime}_{L}(x)\ &\ log_{2}\ (f\circ g)^{\prime}_{L}(x)\\
log_{2}\ g^{\prime}_{R}(x)\ &\ log_{2}\ (f\circ g)^{\prime}_{R}(x)\\
\end{smallmatrix}\right)

where the summation is over the breakpoints. The subscripts
L
and
R

refer to the left and right derivatives. This cocycle represents
a generator of the cohomology ring
H^{*}(F^{\prime};{\mathbf{R}})={\mathbf{R}}[u]
.
Let
f,g\in F^{\prime}
be elements defined by the following pictures.
f
g
f\circ g=g\circ f

Let
\psi\colon{\mathbf{Z}}^{2}\to F^{\prime}
be an injective homomorphism defined by

\psi(e_{1})=f\text{ and }\psi(e_{2})=g,

where
e_{1}=(1,0)
and
e_{2}=(0,1)
. It is a direct computation that

\alpha(f,g)=1
and that
\alpha(g,f)=-1
(only the breaking point
(1/2,1/2)

contributes). Since
\zeta_{2}=(e_{1}|e_{2})-(e_{2}|e_{1})
is a cycle representing
nontrivial class in
H_{2}({\mathbf{Z}}^{2};{\mathbf{R}})
we get that

\langle\psi^{*}\alpha,\zeta_{2}\rangle=2
and hence
\psi^{*}(u)\neq 0
in
H^{2}({\mathbf{Z}}^{2};{\mathbf{R}})
.
Since
{\mathbf{Z}}^{2}
is amenable,
u
cannot be represented by a 1-bounded cocycle.

Consider
\psi\colon{\mathbf{Z}}^{4}\to F^{\prime}
generated by

f*1,\ g*1,\ 1*f,\ 1*g,

where
f,g\in F^{\prime}
are as above and the
*
-product
a*b
is a homeomorphism
given by rescaled
a
on
[0,1/2]
and rescaled
b
on
[1/2,1]
;
see
[
1
]
for more details.
Let
\zeta_{4}=\sum(-1)^{{\rm sgn}{\sigma}}(e_{\sigma(1)}|\cdots|e_{\sigma(4)})

be the cycle generating
H^{4}({\mathbf{Z}}^{4};{\mathbf{R}})
. A slightly cumbersome calculation
shows that
\langle\psi^{*}\alpha^{2},\zeta_{4}\rangle=8
which shows that

\psi^{*}[u^{2}]\neq 0
in
H^{4}({\mathbf{Z}}^{4};{\mathbf{R}})
. As above, it follows that
u^{2}
cannot
be 1-bounded.

By repeating inductively a similar trick with taking the
*
-product we get
that
\psi^{*}(u^{2^{n-1}})\neq 0
in
H^{2^{n}}({\mathbf{Z}}^{2^{n}};{\mathbf{R}})
which proves the
statement.
∎

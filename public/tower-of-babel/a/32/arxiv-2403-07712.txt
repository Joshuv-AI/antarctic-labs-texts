Abstract
In this paper, we consider a new nonlocal approximation to the linear Stokes system with periodic boundary conditions in two and three dimensional spaces . A relaxation term is added to the equation of nonlocal divergence free equation, which is reminiscent to the relaxation of local Stokes equation with small artificial compressibility. Our analysis shows that the well-posedness of the nonlocal system can be established under some mild assumptions on the kernel of nonlocal interactions. Furthermore, the new nonlocal system converges to the conventional, local Stokes system in second order as the horizon parameter of the nonlocal interaction goes to zero. The study provides more theoretical understanding to some numerical methods, such as smoothed particle hydrodynamics, for simulating incompressible viscous flows.

In many applications, nonlocal models have also been used as integral approximation to the conventional PDE models.
One can find studies on the natural link between the nonlocal integral relaxations and numerical schemes like the smoothed particle
hydrodynamics (SPH)
[

,

. Originally developed for astrophysical applications
[

, SPH has now become very popular in simulating complex flows. In this paper, we like to provide more theoretical understanding to SPH by constructing and analyzing a nonlocal approximation to the linear Stokes system.
Previously, the mathematical analysis of
nonlocal integral relaxations to
the linear steady Stokes system has been studied in

[
8
,
15
]

for incompressible viscous
flows. In
[
8
]
, the nonlocal systems employed a nonlocal Laplacian operator
together with a nonlocal gradient and a nonlocal divergence, with the latter two
sharing an anti-symmetric nonlocal interaction kernel.
The well-posedness of the nonlocal system was shown under the assumption that
the kernel functions defining the nonlocal gradient and divergence
have to satisfy some extra conditions. These conditions imply certain strengthened
nonlocal interactions for nearby points. Under such conditions, the spaces
corresponding to local and nonlocal
incompressible vector fields actually coincide with each other. A natural question is
if such strengthened interactions is truly necessary since the popular kernels
used in many practical SPH implementation do not share such properties.
In
[
15
]
, it was shown that one can get a well-posed nonlocal Stokes equation
with nonlocal gradient and nonlocal divergence defined by operators involving more
general kernels but with a non-radial symmetric interaction neighborhood.
Both models, given respectively in

[
8
]
and
[
15
]
, replaced the local divergence operator in the
continuity equation (divergence-free constraint) by different nonlocal
divergence operators, Moreover, in both cases, the nonlocal Laplacian operator can
be defined by the composition of the
same pair of nonlocal divergence and nonlocal gradient operators used for the
incompressibility and that acting on the pressure in the momentum equation.

In this paper, we consider an alternative nonlocal approximation by adding a relaxation term in the continuity equation, i.e., the divergence free or incompressibility constraint. Nonlocal gradient and divergence operators associated with
anti-symmetric kernels are retained as in
[
8
]
. However, a relaxation term is added, given
by the nonlocal Laplacian of the pressure
with a coefficient dependent on the range of nonlocal interactions.
With the new relaxation term, the assumptions on the kernel functions can also be relaxed, same as
the conclusion in
[
15
]
, but without the need of introducing non-radial symmetric interaction neighborhood.
The coefficient is properly chosen so that
in the local limit, the extra term vanishes and the local incompressibility condition is recovered. Furthermore,
the extra term does not degrade the order of the approximations to the local limit. That is,
the nonlocal model converges to the original local Stokes system in second order accuracy with respect to the nonlocal horizon parameter.
Thus, we provide justifications to the use of more general kernels for incompressible SPH implementations,
although this must be accompanied by the nonlocal relaxation to the incompressibility.
For simplicity, we follow earlier works by considering problems defined in a periodic cell. The more general
cases involving various other popular boundary conditions will be studied in upcoming works.

2
Model description

We consider the linear Stokes system with a periodic boundary condition.
Let
u
be the velocity field,
p
the pressure,
f
the body force,

\Omega=(-\pi,\pi)^{d}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
a bounded domain with a boundary
\partial\Omega
,
d=2
or
3
.
The classical, local Stokes equations of interests here refer to the system

\begin{cases}-\Delta\textbf{u}+\nabla p=\textbf{f},&\mathbf{x}\in\ \Omega,\\
\nabla\cdot\textbf{u}=0,&\mathbf{x}\in\ \Omega.\end{cases}

(1)

We also impose average zero condition the remove the ambiguity of the constant

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\textbf{u}(\mathbf{x})d\mathbf{x}=0

(2)

and

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}p(\mathbf{x})d\mathbf{x}=0.

(3)

In
[
8
]
, a nonlocal version of the above Stokes system with periodic boundary condition has been studied. The nonlocal model proposed there is

\begin{cases}-\mathcal{L}_{\delta}\textbf{u}_{\delta}(\mathbf{x})+\mathcal{G}_{\delta}p_{\delta}(\mathbf{x})=\textbf{f}(\mathbf{x}),&\mathbf{x}\in\Omega,\\
\mathcal{D}_{\delta}\textbf{u}_{\delta}(\mathbf{x})=0,&\mathbf{x}\in\Omega,\end{cases}

(4)

The associated nonlocal operators are the nonlocal diffusion (Laplacian) operator
\mathcal{L}_{\delta}
, nonlocal gradient operator
\mathcal{G}_{\delta}
, and
nonlocal divergence operator
\mathcal{D}_{\delta}
, given respectively by

\mathcal{L}_{\delta}\textbf{u}(\mathbf{x})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\omega_{\delta}(|\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{x}|)(\textbf{u}(\mathbf{y})-\textbf{u}(\mathbf{x}))d\mathbf{y},

(5)

\mathcal{G}_{\delta}p(\mathbf{x})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bm{\omega}_{\delta}(\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{x})(p(\mathbf{y})-p(\mathbf{x}))d\mathbf{y},

(6)

\mathcal{D}_{\delta}\textbf{u}(\mathbf{x})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bm{\omega}_{\delta}(\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{x})\cdot(\textbf{u}(\mathbf{y})-\textbf{u}(\mathbf{x}))d\mathbf{y},

(7)

The above operators are determined by a nonlocal scalar-valued kernels
\omega_{\delta}
and a vector-valued kernel
\bm{\omega}_{\delta}
.
The vector-valued kernel has a special form
\bm{\omega}_{\delta}(\mathbf{x})=\hat{\omega}_{\delta}(|\mathbf{x}|)\mathbf{x}/|\mathbf{x}|
.
Here
\delta>0
is a parameter that characterizes the range of nonlocal intersection.

Since the boundary condition is assumed to be periodic and nonlocal operators are all of the convolution type,
it is convenient to analyze the nonlcoal model via Fourier analysis.
In Fourier space, nonlocal model (
4
) becomes

\left(\ \begin{matrix}\displaystyle\lambda_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})I_{d}&i\textbf{b}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})\\
i(\textbf{b}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi}))^{T}&0\end{matrix}\ \right)\left(\ \begin{matrix}\hat{\textbf{u}}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})\\
\hat{p}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})\end{matrix}\ \right)=\left(\ \begin{matrix}\hat{\mathbf{f}}(\bm{\xi})\\
0\end{matrix}\ \right)

(8)

where
i=\sqrt{-1}
,
\lambda_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})
and
\textbf{b}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})
are Fourier symbols of
\mathcal{L}_{\delta}
and
\mathcal{G}_{\delta}
respectively.
It is easy to check that the matrix in (
8
) is invertible if and only if
\textbf{b}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})\neq 0
. Then we have to impose proper conditions on
\hat{\omega}

to guarantee
\textbf{b}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})\neq 0,\;\forall\bm{\xi}
. The conditions proposed in
[
8
]
is that

1.

r^{d-1}\hat{\omega}(r)
is nonincreasing for
r\in(0,1)
;

2.

\hat{\omega}(r)
is of fractional type in at least a small neighborhood of origin, namely, there exists
\epsilon>0
such that for
s\in(0,\epsilon)
,

\hat{\omega}(r)=\frac{c}{r^{d+\beta}},

for some constant
c>0
and
\beta\in(-1,1)
.

Under above assumptions,
\hat{\omega}
blows up near the origin. This singularity may introduce extra complications in the computation. To avoid such assumptions,
[
15
]
studied a similar nonlocal system (
4
), but with domains
of nonlocal interactions for the nonlocal
operators
\mathcal{G}_{\delta}
, and

\mathcal{D}_{\delta}
changed to non-radial ones (half disks for
d=2
and half spheres for
d-3
, as examples).

In this paper, we consider another nonlocal model to remove the singular assumption of the kernel function. The idea is to add an extra relaxation term in the divergence free equation. Namely,
in our new model, the nonlocal counterpart of the divergence free equation is changed to

\mathcal{D}_{\delta}\textbf{u}_{\delta}(\mathbf{x})-\delta^{2}\mathcal{R}_{\delta}p_{\delta}(\mathbf{x})=0.

(9)

\mathcal{R}_{\delta}
is another nonlocal diffusion operator

\mathcal{R}_{\delta}p(\mathbf{x})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\tilde{\omega}_{\delta}(|\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{x}|)(p(\mathbf{y})-p(\mathbf{x}))d\mathbf{y},

(10)

with a given kernel function
\tilde{\omega}_{\delta}
. The factor
\delta^{2}
is small as
\delta\to 0
, and it is
used to recover the local incompressibility condition and to preserve the second order accuracy of the nonlocal approximation.

With the relaxation term in the divergence free equation, in the Fourier space, the nonlocal model becomes

\left(\ \begin{matrix}\displaystyle\lambda_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})I_{d}&i\textbf{b}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})\\
i(\textbf{b}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi}))^{T}&\delta^{2}c_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})\end{matrix}\ \right)\left(\ \begin{matrix}\hat{\textbf{u}}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})\\
\hat{p}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})\end{matrix}\ \right)=\left(\ \begin{matrix}\hat{\mathbf{f}}(\bm{\xi})\\
0\end{matrix}\ \right)

(11)

with
c_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})
is the Fourier symbol of
\mathcal{R}_{\delta}
. This linear system is always invertible such that the assumption on the kernel function in
[
8
]
is
removed.

For the sake of generality,
in this paper, we consider a more general divergence equation instead of the divergence free condition, i.e.

\nabla\cdot\textbf{u}(\mathbf{x})=g(\mathbf{x}),\quad\mathbf{x}\in\Omega.

(12)

with
g
being a given function. Here, we require that
f
and
g
have mean zero to assure the compatibility, i.e.

\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x})d\mathbf{x}=\textbf{0},\quad\int_{\Omega}g(\mathbf{x})d\mathbf{x}=0.

(13)

We define a nonlocal model as follows:

\begin{cases}-\mathcal{L}_{\delta}\textbf{u}_{\delta}(\mathbf{x})+\mathcal{G}_{\delta}p_{\delta}(\mathbf{x})=\textbf{f}(\mathbf{x}),&\mathbf{x}\in\Omega,\\
\mathcal{D}_{\delta}\textbf{u}_{\delta}(\mathbf{x})-\delta^{2}\mathcal{R}_{\delta}p_{\delta}(\mathbf{x})=g(\mathbf{x}),&\mathbf{x}\in\Omega,\end{cases}

(14)

with normalization conditions on
\textbf{u}_{\delta}
and
p_{\delta}
to eliminate constant shifts,

\int_{\Omega}\textbf{u}_{\delta}(\mathbf{x})d\mathbf{x}=0,\qquad\int_{\Omega}p_{\delta}(\mathbf{x})d\mathbf{x}=0.

(15)

The nonlocal operators used above are those defined in (
5
), (
6
), (
7
) and (
10
).
We assume that
\omega_{\delta}
,
\hat{\omega}_{\delta}
and
\tilde{\omega}_{\delta}
are all non-negative, radial symmetric and with a compact support in the
\delta
neighborhood
B(0,\delta)
of the origin.

3
Well-posedness of the nonlocal Stokes system

In this section, we study the well-posedness of the nonlocal system (
14
).
First, we make some assumptions on the kernels
\omega_{\delta}=\omega_{\delta}(|\mathbf{x}|)
,
\tilde{\omega}_{\delta}=\tilde{\omega}_{\delta}(|\mathbf{x}|)
and

\hat{\omega}_{\delta}=\hat{\omega}_{\delta}(|\mathbf{x}|)
and introduce some notations.

3.1
Assumption and notation

Throughout this paper, the kernel functions are assumed to satisfy.

Assumption 1
.

1.

\omega_{\delta},\tilde{\omega}_{\delta},\hat{\omega}_{\delta}
have compact support in the sphere
B(0,\delta)
and satisfy the normalization conditions:

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\omega_{\delta}(|\mathbf{x}|)|\mathbf{x}|^{2}d\mathbf{x}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\hat{\omega}_{\delta}(|\mathbf{x}|)|\mathbf{x}|d\mathbf{x}=d;\\
m\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\tilde{\omega}_{\delta}(|\mathbf{x}|)|\mathbf{x}|^{2}d\mathbf{x}\leq M\end{split}

(16)

for some constant
\ 0<m<M
.

2.

\omega_{\delta},\tilde{\omega}_{\delta},\hat{\omega}_{\delta}
are rescaled from kernels
\omega
,
\tilde{\omega}
and
\hat{\omega}
that have compact support in the unit sphere

\mbox{ }\qquad\omega_{\delta}(|\mathbf{x}|)=\frac{1}{\delta^{d+1}}\omega(\frac{|\mathbf{x}|}{\delta}),\;\;\tilde{\omega}_{\delta}(|\mathbf{x}|)=\frac{1}{\delta^{d+1}}\tilde{\omega}(\frac{|\mathbf{x}|}{\delta}),\;\;\hat{\omega}_{\delta}(|\mathbf{x}|)=\frac{1}{\delta^{d+2}}\hat{\omega}(\frac{|\mathbf{x}|}{\delta}).

(17)

Remark 1
.

Actually, we can allow
\omega_{\delta}
,
\tilde{\omega}_{\delta}
and
\hat{\omega}_{\delta}
to have non-compact support. The condition of compact support can be relaxed to, for some constant
C>0
,

\int_{1/\epsilon}^{\infty}\omega(|\mathbf{x}|)|\mathbf{x}|^{2}d\mathbf{x}\leq C\epsilon^{2},\;\;\int_{1/\epsilon}^{\infty}\hat{\omega}(|\mathbf{x}|)|\mathbf{x}|d\mathbf{x}\leq C\epsilon^{2},\;\;\int_{1/\epsilon}^{\infty}\tilde{\omega}(|\mathbf{x}|)|\mathbf{x}|^{2}d\mathbf{x}\leq C\epsilon^{2},

(18)

for any
\epsilon>0
. Under such condition, the same theoretical results can be established.

Remark 2
.

The removal of the assumptions on
\hat{\omega}
in
[
8
]
widely broaden the choice of
\hat{\omega}
. For instance, we can simply choose a constant function
\hat{\omega}\equiv 3/\pi
inside the unit disk and vanishes outside, or certain smooth trigonometric functions that are commonly used in the nonlocal theory. This greatly reduces the complication of the nonlocal model since none of the kernel functions necessarily have singularity near the origin and we can still expect a uniform bound on their corresponding Fourier symbols.

Similarly as in
[
8
,
15
]
, we utilize the Fourier space representation of the periodic functions.
That is,

\textbf{u}(\mathbf{x})=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\sum_{\bm{\xi}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},\bm{\xi}\neq 0}\hat{\textbf{u}}(\bm{\xi})e^{i\bm{\xi}\cdot\mathbf{x}},\quad p(\mathbf{x})=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\sum_{\bm{\xi}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},\bm{\xi}\neq 0}\hat{p}(\bm{\xi})e^{i\bm{\xi}\cdot\mathbf{x}},

where

\hat{\textbf{u}}(\bm{\xi})=\int_{\Omega}\textbf{u}(\mathbf{x})e^{-i\bm{\xi}\cdot\mathbf{x}}d\mathbf{x},\quad\hat{p}(\bm{\xi})=\int_{\Omega}p(\mathbf{x})e^{-i\bm{\xi}\cdot\mathbf{x}}d\mathbf{x}.

It is easy to check that
the Fourier symbols of operators
\mathcal{L}_{\delta}
,
\mathcal{G}_{\delta}
,
\mathcal{D}_{\delta}
and
\mathcal{R}_{\delta}
are given by (see Lemma 2 in
[
8
]
):

\widehat{\mathcal{L}_{\delta}\textbf{u}}(\bm{\xi})=-\lambda_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})\hat{\textbf{u}}(\bm{\xi}),\quad\widehat{\mathcal{G}_{\delta}p}(\bm{\xi})=i\textbf{b}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})\hat{p}(\bm{\xi}),

\widehat{\mathcal{D}_{\delta}\textbf{u}}(\bm{\xi})=i(\textbf{b}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi}))^{T}\hat{\textbf{u}}(\bm{\xi}),\quad\widehat{\mathcal{R}_{\delta}p}(\bm{\xi})=-c_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})\hat{p}(\bm{\xi}),

where
\lambda_{\delta}
,
\textbf{b}_{\delta}
and
c_{\delta}
are given by

\lambda_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})=\int_{|\mathbf{s}|\leq\delta}\omega_{\delta}(|\mathbf{s}|)(1-\cos(\bm{\xi}\cdot\mathbf{s}))d\mathbf{s},

\textbf{b}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})=\int_{|\mathbf{s}|\leq\delta}\hat{\omega}_{\delta}(|\mathbf{s}|)\frac{\mathbf{s}}{|\mathbf{s}|}\sin(\bm{\xi}\cdot\mathbf{s})d\mathbf{s},

c_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})=\int_{|\mathbf{s}|\leq\delta}\tilde{\omega}_{\delta}(|\mathbf{s}|)(1-\cos(\bm{\xi}\cdot\mathbf{s}))d\mathbf{s}.

The key to establish the well-posedness of the nonlocal system
is to bound
\lambda_{\delta}(\bm{\xi}),\;|\textbf{b}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})|
and
c_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})
, particularly from below.

Proposition 3
.

Under the assumption
1
, we have

1.

For
\delta|\bm{\xi}|>1
,

\displaystyle|\bm{\xi}|^{2}\geq\lambda_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})\geq\frac{1}{16\ \delta^{2}},

\displaystyle M|\bm{\xi}|^{2}\geq c_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})\geq\frac{m}{16\ \delta^{2}}.

2.

For
0<\delta|\bm{\xi}|\leq 1
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}|\bm{\xi}|^{2}<|\bm{\xi}|^{2}-\frac{|\bm{\xi}|^{2}}{20}\ (\delta|\bm{\xi}|)^{2}\leq\lambda_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})\leq|\bm{\xi}|^{2},

\displaystyle\frac{m}{2}|\bm{\xi}|^{2}\leq c_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})\leq M|\bm{\xi}|^{2},

\displaystyle|\bm{\xi}|\geq|\textbf{b}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})|\geq|\bm{\xi}|-\frac{(\delta|\bm{\xi}|)^{2}}{10}|\bm{\xi}|>\frac{1}{2}|\bm{\xi}|.

The proof can be found in Appendix A.

Remark 4
.

The estimates in the above proposition imply that, when
\delta|\bm{\xi}|>1
,
\lambda_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})
has a uniform lower bound
\frac{1}{16\delta^{2}}
that is independent of its corresponding kernel
\omega(\mathbf{x})
. This bound can be very useful in
the analysis of the convergence order of our nonlocal model to its local limit, which is studies in the next section.
Similar bounds have also been derived in the literature, for example,
the same lower bound was shown in
[
9
]

for the case that

is decreasing on
r\in(0,\infty)
. For more general
\omega
, a lower bound for
\lambda_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})
that depends on the choice of
\omega
was given in the proof of Proposition
9
of
[
8
]
. Here, we get the uniform bound independent on
\omega
under the very general assumption

.

3.2
Well-posedness

First, we transform the nonlocal system to the Fourier space

A_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})\left(\ \begin{matrix}\hat{\textbf{u}}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})\\
\hat{p}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})\end{matrix}\ \right)=\left(\ \begin{matrix}\hat{\mathbf{f}}(\bm{\xi})\\
\hat{g}(\bm{\xi})\end{matrix}\ \right)

(19)

where

A_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})=\left(\ \begin{matrix}\displaystyle\lambda_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})I_{d}&i\textbf{b}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})\\
i(\textbf{b}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi}))^{T}&\delta^{2}c_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})\end{matrix}\ \right).

For notation convenience, let us define
q_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})=|\textbf{b}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})|^{2}+\delta^{2}c_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})\lambda_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})
.
With both
\lambda_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})
and
c_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})
positive for each fixed
\bm{\xi}\neq 0
, we have

q_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})
also positive. Thus,
the inverse of
A_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})
exists and has a closed form

(A_{\delta}(\bm{\xi}))^{-1}=\left(\ \begin{matrix}\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}(I_{d}-\frac{\textbf{b}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})\ \otimes\ \textbf{b}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}{q_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})})&\displaystyle\frac{-i\textbf{b}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}{q_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}\\[11.38092pt]
\displaystyle\frac{-i(\textbf{b}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi}))^{T}}{q_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}&\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}{q_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}\end{matrix}\ \right).

(20)

This implies that

\displaystyle\hat{\textbf{u}}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}(I_{d}-\frac{\textbf{b}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})\otimes\textbf{b}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}{q_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})})\hat{\mathbf{f}}(\bm{\xi})\ -\ \frac{i\ (\textbf{b}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi}))^{T}}{q_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}\hat{g}(\bm{\xi}),

(21)

\displaystyle\hat{p}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})

\displaystyle=-\frac{i(\textbf{b}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi}))^{T}}{q_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}\hat{\mathbf{f}}(\bm{\xi})+\frac{\lambda_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}{q_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}\hat{g}(\bm{\xi}).

(22)

To prove the well-posedness and to obtain the desired a priori bounds,
we need to get bound on
(A_{\delta}(\bm{\xi}))^{-1}
, which is, equivalently, to bound the coefficients in front of
\hat{\mathbf{f}}(\bm{\xi})
and
\hat{g}(\bm{\xi})
uniformly in
\bm{\xi}
for
\bm{\xi}\neq 0
.

First, from the average free condition (
13
),
\hat{\mathbf{f}}(0)=0
and
\hat{g}(0)=0
, so we only need to consider the case
|\bm{\xi}|>0
.

For the high frequency components such that
\delta|\bm{\xi}|>1
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{\lambda_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}\ \frac{\textbf{b}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})\otimes\textbf{b}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}{q_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}\right|=\frac{1}{\lambda_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}\ \frac{|\textbf{b}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})|^{2}}{q_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}\leq\frac{1}{\lambda_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}\leq 16\delta^{2},

\displaystyle\frac{|(\textbf{b}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi}))^{T}|}{q_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}\leq\frac{|(\textbf{b}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi}))^{T}|}{2\delta\sqrt{c_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})\lambda_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}\ |\textbf{b}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})|}=\frac{1}{2\delta\ \sqrt{c_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})\lambda_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}}\leq\frac{8}{\sqrt{m}}\delta,

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}{q_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}\leq\frac{\lambda_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}{\delta^{2}c_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})\lambda_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}\leq\frac{16}{m}.

Note that in the above and hereinafter, the matrix norm corresponds to that induced by the vector Euclidean norm.

Likewise, for the low frequency components,
0<\delta|\bm{\xi}|\leq 1
,

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{\lambda_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}\ \frac{\textbf{b}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})\otimes\textbf{b}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}{q_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}\right|\leq\frac{1}{\lambda_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}\leq\frac{2}{|\bm{\xi}|^{2}},

\displaystyle\frac{|(\textbf{b}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi}))^{T}|}{q_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}\leq\frac{1}{|\textbf{b}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})|}\leq\frac{2}{|\bm{\xi}|},

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}{q_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}\leq\frac{\lambda_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}{|\textbf{b}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})|^{2}}\leq\frac{|\bm{\xi}|^{2}}{(\frac{1}{2}|\bm{\xi}|)^{2}}=4.

Above estimates directly imply the following theorem on the well-posedness of the nonlocal system.

Theorem 5
.

Assume that the kernels
\omega_{\delta}
,
\tilde{\omega}_{\delta}
and
\hat{\omega}_{\delta}
satisfy the assumption
1
. Given
0<\delta<1
, there exists a unique solution
(\textbf{u}_{\delta},p_{\delta})
to the nonlocal Stokes system
(
14
)
with periodic boundary condition given in the form of their Fourier series with
(\hat{\textbf{u}}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi}),\hat{p}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi}))
through

\left(\ \begin{matrix}\hat{\textbf{u}}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})\\
\hat{p}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})\end{matrix}\ \right)=(A_{\delta}(\bm{\xi}))^{-1}\left(\ \begin{matrix}\hat{\mathbf{f}}(\bm{\xi})\\
\hat{g}(\bm{\xi})\end{matrix}\ \right)

where
(A_{\delta}(\bm{\xi}))^{-1}
is defined by
(
20
)
. In addition, with
C>0
depend only on
m
and independent of
\delta
,
\mathbf{f}
,
g
,
\omega_{\delta}
,
\tilde{\omega}
, we have

\left\lVert\textbf{u}_{\delta}\right\rVert_{[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{d}}\leq C(\left\lVert\mathbf{f}\right\rVert_{[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{d}}+\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}),

(23)

and

\left\lVert p_{\delta}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C(\left\lVert\mathbf{f}\right\rVert_{[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{d}}+\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}).

(24)

3.3
Regularity pick-up

In the original Stokes system, the solution is more regular than the right hand side
\mathbf{f}
. However, in the nonlocal system, if the kernel function

\omega(|\mathbf{x}|)
is only assumed to be integrable, we can not get the regularity lift. Because in this case, the high frequency components of
\mathcal{L}_{\delta}
is bounded above rather than
growing like
|\bm{\xi}|^{2}
. This observation is similar to the case of other nonlocal elliptic problems
[
9
,
8
]
.
On the other hand, this fact tells us that to get regularity lift, we need to use more singular kernel functions like those used in
[
8
]
. Indeed, if

\frac{C_{1}}{r^{d+2\alpha}}\leq\omega(r)\leq\frac{C_{2}}{r^{d+2\alpha}},\qquad\forall\ r\in(0,1),

(25)

for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
and constants
C_{1}
,
C_{2}>0
, then the corresponding
\lambda_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})
grows as
|\bm{\xi}|^{2\alpha}
in high frequency part. Consequently, we can get
2\alpha
regularity pick-up,

\left\lVert\textbf{u}_{\delta}\right\rVert_{[H^{\alpha}(\Omega)]^{d}}+\left\lVert p_{\delta}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C(\left\lVert\mathbf{f}\right\rVert_{[H^{-\alpha}(\Omega)]^{d}}+\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}),

(26)

where the constant
C
depends only on
C_{1}
. The proof of this result is in Appendix B.

Although the solution becomes more regular, the singular nature of the kernel function may complicate
the numerical integration in practice. To avoid such issue, a modified nonlocal system will be considered later
in section
5
.

4
Vanishing Nonlocality

Since the nonlocal model is constructed to approximate the original Stokes system.
After the well-posedness of the nonlocal model is obtained, one natural problem follows is the accuracy of the nonlocal approximation. In this section, we will show that the nonlocal solutions converge to its local counterpart as
\delta\to 0
and the convergence rate is of second order.

Theorem 6
.

Assume that the kernels
\omega_{\delta}
,

(27)

We have, for some constant

C
depend only on
m,M

\displaystyle\left\lVert\textbf{u}-\textbf{u}_{\delta}\right\rVert_{[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{d}}\leq C(\delta^{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{f}\right\rVert_{[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{d}}+\delta^{\min\{2,1+\beta\}}\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{H^{\beta}(\Omega)}),

(28)

\displaystyle\left\lVert p-p_{\delta}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C(\delta^{1+\eta}\left\lVert\mathbf{f}\right\rVert_{[H^{\eta}(\Omega)]^{d}}+\delta^{\beta}\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{H^{\beta}(\Omega)}),

for any

,

for some

and constants

,

(31)

\displaystyle\left\lVert p-p_{\delta}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C(\delta^{1-\alpha+\eta}\left\lVert\mathbf{f}\right\rVert_{[H^{\eta-\alpha}(\Omega)]^{d}}+\delta^{\beta}\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{H^{\beta}(\Omega)}),

for any

and

Proof.

Let us take the case

\displaystyle\hat{\textbf{u}}(\bm{\xi})-\hat{\textbf{u}}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})=

\displaystyle\big[(\frac{1}{\lambda_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}-\frac{1}{|\bm{\xi}|^{2}})I_{d}-(\frac{1}{\lambda_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}\frac{\textbf{b}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})\otimes\textbf{b}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}{q_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}-\frac{\bm{\xi}\otimes\bm{\xi}}{|\bm{\xi}|^{4}})\big]\ \hat{\mathbf{f}}(\bm{\xi})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-i\big[\frac{(\textbf{b}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi}))}{q_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}-\frac{\bm{\xi}}{|\bm{\xi}|^{2}}\big]\hat{g}(\bm{\xi}),

(33)

\displaystyle\hat{p}(\bm{\xi})-\hat{p}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})=

\displaystyle-i\big[\frac{(\textbf{b}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi}))}{q_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}-\frac{\bm{\xi}}{|\bm{\xi}|^{2}}\big]\hat{\mathbf{f}}(\bm{\xi})+\big[\frac{\lambda_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}{q_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}-1\big]\hat{g}(\bm{\xi}).

For the low frequency part,

(36)

and

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{\lambda_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}\frac{\textbf{b}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})\otimes\textbf{b}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}{q_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}-\frac{\bm{\xi}\otimes\bm{\xi}}{|\bm{\xi}|^{4}}\right|\leq

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\lambda_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}\ \left|\frac{\textbf{b}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})\otimes\textbf{b}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}{q_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}-\frac{\textbf{b}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})\otimes\textbf{b}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}{|\textbf{b}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})|^{2}}\right|.

\begin{split}\left|\frac{1}{\lambda_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}\frac{\textbf{b}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})\otimes\textbf{b}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}{q_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}-\frac{\bm{\xi}\otimes\bm{\xi}}{|\bm{\xi}|^{4}}\right|&\leq\left|\frac{1}{\lambda_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}-\frac{1}{|\bm{\xi}|^{2}}\right|+\frac{\delta^{2}c_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}{q_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}\\

(38)

To estimate the difference of the pressure, again from the Proposition

\begin{split}\left|\frac{\lambda_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}{q_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}-1\right|&\leq\left|\frac{\lambda_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})-|\textbf{b}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})|^{2}-\delta^{2}c_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})\lambda_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}{|\textbf{b}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})|^{2}}\right|\\

Remark 7
.

1.

The case
g\equiv 0
in the system
(
27
)
gives the standard Stokes system. Under such case, we end up with

\left\lVert\textbf{u}-\textbf{u}_{\delta}\right\rVert_{[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{d}}\leq C\delta^{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{f}\right\rVert_{[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{d}},

(47)

and

\left\lVert p-p_{\delta}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\delta^{\min\{2,1+\eta\}}\left\lVert\mathbf{f}\right\rVert_{[H^{\eta}(\Omega)]^{d}},

(48)

for any
\eta\in[0,1]
and some constant
C
independent of
\delta,\omega,\hat{\omega}
and
\tilde{\omega}
.

2.

When
g
is only an
L^{2}
function and has no higher regularity, we cannot directly obtain from
(
29
)
that
p_{\delta}
converges to
p
in
L^{2}
as
\delta\to 0
. However, such convergence still holds, but the rate depends on
g
. To see why this is the case, we first obtain from the above calculation that
\big[\frac{\lambda_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}{q_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}-1\big]
is bounded above by some constant, and it goes to
0
as
\delta|\bm{\xi}|\to 0
. Then for any
\epsilon>0
, we can find
N\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
that
\sum\limits_{\bm{\xi}=N}^{\infty}\hat{g}(\bm{\xi})<\epsilon
; also, we can find
\delta_{0}
such that
\big[\frac{\lambda_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}{q_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}-1\big]<\epsilon
for all
\delta|\bm{\xi}|<\delta_{0}\ N
. Hence for any
\delta<\delta_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\bm{\xi}=1}^{\infty}\big[\frac{\lambda_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}{q_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}-1\big]\hat{g}(\bm{\xi})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\bm{\xi}=1}^{N-1}\big[\frac{\lambda_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}{q_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}-1\big]\hat{g}(\bm{\xi})\ +\sum\limits_{\bm{\xi}=N}^{\infty}\big[\frac{\lambda_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}{q_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}-1\big]\hat{g}(\bm{\xi})

\displaystyle<\epsilon\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+C\epsilon.

since
\epsilon>0
is arbitrary, we see that

\sum\limits_{\bm{\xi}=1}^{\infty}\big[\frac{\lambda_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}{q_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})}-1\big]\hat{g}(\bm{\xi})\to 0,\;\mbox{ as }\;\delta\to 0.

Therefore, we can easily observe from
(
34
)
that
p-p_{\delta}\to 0
in
L^{2}
as
\delta\to 0
.

5
A Modified Nonlocal Model

We see from the estimates (
28
)-(
29
) in the
theorem
6
that the second order convergence to the local limit rests upon the regularity of data.
The latter can be improved by considering a modified nonlocal model with regularized right hand sides
through a convolution operation.
If the kernel function used in the convolution is in
H^{\gamma}
, then the regularity of the right hand side is
lifted by order
\gamma
. Consequently, the
solution of the nonlocal system also becomes more regular, and the convergence order gets higher as well.

Based on this observation, we modify the nonlocal model (
14
) as follows:

\begin{cases}-\mathcal{L}_{\delta}\textbf{u}^{0}_{\delta}(\mathbf{x})+\mathcal{G}_{\delta}p^{0}_{\delta}(\mathbf{x})=\textbf{f}_{\delta}(\mathbf{x}),&\mathbf{x}\in\Omega,\\
\mathcal{D}_{\delta}\textbf{u}^{0}_{\delta}(\mathbf{x})-\delta^{2}\mathcal{R}_{\delta}p^{0}_{\delta}(\mathbf{x})=g_{\delta}(\mathbf{x}),&\mathbf{x}\in\Omega,\end{cases}

(49)

where

\mathbf{f}_{\delta}(\mathbf{x})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bar{\omega}_{\delta}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})\ \mathbf{f}(\mathbf{y})d\mathbf{y},\qquad g_{\delta}(\mathbf{x})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bar{\omega}_{\delta}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})\ g(\mathbf{y})d\mathbf{y},

(50)

here the kernel
\bar{\omega}_{\delta}
is rescaled from a function
\bar{\omega}
and satisfies the following assumption.

Assumption 2
.

1.

\bar{\omega}
is non-negative, radial symmetric and is supported in the unit sphere.

2.

\bar{\omega}
is integrable in
{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bar{\omega}(|\mathbf{x}|)d\mathbf{x}=1,

(51)

3.

\bar{\omega}
lies in
H^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\;\gamma>0
.

4.

\displaystyle\bar{\omega}_{\delta}(|\mathbf{x}|)=\frac{1}{\delta^{d}}\bar{\omega}(\frac{|\mathbf{x}|}{\delta}).

Based on the assumption
2
, we have that for any
s\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\|f_{\delta}\|_{[H^{\gamma+s}(\Omega)]^{d}}\leq C_{\delta}\|f\|_{[H^{s}(\Omega)]^{d}},\quad\|g_{\delta}\|_{[H^{\gamma+s}(\Omega)]^{d}}\leq C_{\delta}\|g\|_{[H^{s}(\Omega)]^{d}},

(52)

where
C_{\delta}
is a constant depend on
\delta
and
m
. Then following the same proof as that in Theorem
5
, we have that

\left\lVert\textbf{u}^{0}_{\delta}\right\rVert_{[H^{\gamma}(\Omega)]^{d}}\leq C_{\delta}(\left\lVert\mathbf{f}\right\rVert_{[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{d}}+\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}),

(53)

and

\left\lVert p^{0}_{\delta}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C_{\delta}(\left\lVert\mathbf{f}\right\rVert_{[H^{-\gamma}(\Omega)]^{d}}+\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}),

(54)

where
C_{\delta}
depend on
m
and
\delta
. Moreover, the modified nonlocal model still has the same order of convergence as
u
and
p
in (
28
) and (
29
).

Theorem 8
.

Assume that the kernels
\omega_{\delta}
,
\tilde{\omega}_{\delta}
and
\hat{\omega}_{\delta}
satisfy Assumption
1
, and
\bar{\omega}_{\delta}
satisfy the assumption
2
. Let
(\textbf{u}^{0}_{\delta},p^{0}_{\delta})
be the solution of the modified nonlocal system
(
49
)
, and
(\textbf{u},p)
be the solution of the generalized Stokes system
(
27
)
, then

\left\lVert\textbf{u}-\textbf{u}^{0}_{\delta}\right\rVert_{[H^{\gamma}(\Omega)]^{d}}\leq C(\delta^{\min\{2,2-\gamma+\eta\}}\left\lVert\mathbf{f}\right\rVert_{[H^{\eta}(\Omega)]^{d}}+\delta^{\min\{2,1+\beta-\gamma\}}\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{H^{\beta}(\Omega)}),

(55)

and

\left\lVert p-p^{0}_{\delta}\right\rVert_{H^{\gamma}(\Omega)}\leq C(\delta^{\min\{2,1-\gamma+\eta\}}\left\lVert\mathbf{f}\right\rVert_{[H^{\eta}(\Omega)]^{d}}+\delta^{\min\{2,\beta-\gamma\}}\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{H^{\beta}(\Omega)}),

(56)

for any
\eta>0
and
\beta>\gamma
, where the constant
C
depend only on the constant
m
and
M
.

Proof.

First, we split the error to two terms:

\displaystyle\textbf{u}-\textbf{u}_{\delta}^{0}=

\displaystyle(\textbf{u}-\bar{\textbf{u}}_{\delta})+(\bar{\textbf{u}}_{\delta}-\textbf{u}_{\delta}^{0}),

\displaystyle p-p_{\delta}^{0}=

\displaystyle(p-\bar{p}_{\delta})+(\bar{p}_{\delta}-p_{\delta}^{0}),

where
\bar{\textbf{u}}_{\delta}
and
\bar{p}_{\delta}
solve a local Stokes system but with regularized data:

\displaystyle-\Delta\bar{\textbf{u}}_{\delta}+\nabla\bar{p}_{\delta}=

\displaystyle\textbf{f}_{\delta},\quad\nabla\cdot\bar{\textbf{u}}_{\delta}=g_{\delta}.

For the second term,
\bar{\textbf{u}}_{\delta}-\textbf{u}_{\delta}^{0}
and
\bar{p}_{\delta}-p_{\delta}^{0}
,
by mimicking the proof of theorem
6
and using the regularity lift result (
52
), we can conclude the convegence estimate,

\left\lVert\bar{\textbf{u}}_{\delta}-\textbf{u}^{0}_{\delta}\right\rVert_{[H^{\gamma}(\Omega)]^{d}}\leq C(\delta^{\min\{2,2-\gamma+\eta\}}\left\lVert\mathbf{f}\right\rVert_{[H^{\eta}(\Omega)]^{d}}+\delta^{\min\{2,1+\beta-\gamma\}}\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{H^{\beta}(\Omega)}),

and

\left\lVert\bar{p}_{\delta}-p^{0}_{\delta}\right\rVert_{H^{\gamma}(\Omega)}\leq C(\delta^{\min\{2,1-\gamma+\eta\}}\left\lVert\mathbf{f}\right\rVert_{[H^{\eta}(\Omega)]^{d}}+\delta^{\min\{2,\beta-\gamma\}}\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{H^{\beta}(\Omega)}).

The calculation of the first term is also straightforward. Using Fourier transform, we have for
\bm{\xi}\neq 0
,

\begin{split}\left(\ \begin{matrix}\displaystyle\hat{\textbf{u}}(\bm{\xi})-\hat{\bar{\textbf{u}}}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})\\
\displaystyle\hat{p}(\bm{\xi})-\hat{\bar{p}}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})\end{matrix}\ \right)=\left(\ \begin{matrix}\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\bm{\xi}|^{2}}(I_{d}-\frac{\bm{\xi}\ \otimes\ \bm{\xi}}{|\bm{\xi}|^{2}}\ )&\displaystyle-i\frac{\bm{\xi}}{|\bm{\xi}|^{2}}\\
\displaystyle-i\frac{\bm{\xi}^{T}}{|\bm{\xi}|^{2}}&\displaystyle 1\end{matrix}\ \right)\left(\ \begin{matrix}\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{f}}(\bm{\xi})-\hat{\mathbf{f}}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})\\
\displaystyle\hat{g}(\bm{\xi})-\hat{g}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})\end{matrix}\ \right),\end{split}

(57)

which implies that

\displaystyle|\hat{\textbf{u}}(\bm{\xi})-\hat{\bar{\textbf{u}}}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})|\leq

\displaystyle|\bm{\xi}|^{-2}|\hat{\mathbf{f}}(\bm{\xi})-\hat{\mathbf{f}}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})|+|\bm{\xi}|^{-1}|\hat{g}(\bm{\xi})-\hat{g}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})|,

(58)

\displaystyle|\hat{p}(\bm{\xi})-\hat{\bar{p}}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})|\leq

\displaystyle|\bm{\xi}|^{-1}|\hat{\mathbf{f}}(\bm{\xi})-\hat{\mathbf{f}}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})|+|\hat{g}(\bm{\xi})-\hat{g}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})|

(59)

Using the definition of
\mathbf{f}_{\delta}
and
g_{\delta}
, we have

\begin{cases}\hat{\mathbf{f}}(\bm{\xi})-\hat{\mathbf{f}}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})=d_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})\ \hat{\mathbf{f}}(\bm{\xi}),\\
\hat{g}(\bm{\xi})-\hat{g}_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})=d_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})\ \hat{g}(\bm{\xi}),\end{cases}

(60)

where

\displaystyle d_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})=

\displaystyle\int_{|\mathbf{s}|\leq\delta}\bar{\omega}_{\delta}(|\mathbf{s}|)(1-\cos(\bm{\xi}\cdot\mathbf{s}))d\mathbf{s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\pi\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\sin(\phi)\int_{0}^{1}r^{2}\bar{\omega}(r)(1-\cos(r\ \cos(\phi)\delta|\bm{\xi}|))drd\phi.

For
\delta|\bm{\xi}|<1
, we see

\displaystyle|d_{\delta}(\bm{\xi})|=

\displaystyle 4\pi\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\sin(\phi)\int_{0}^{1}r^{2}\bar{\omega}(r)(1-\cos(r\ \cos(\phi)\delta|\bm{\xi}|))drd\phi

\displaystyle\leq

7
Conclusion and discussions

In this paper, we propose a nonlocal approximation to the linear Stokes system. Under more general assumptions on the kernel functions (than those studied before, e.g.
[
8
]
), we prove that the nonlocal model is well-posed and converges to the original Stokes system as the length of the nonlocal interaction goes to zero.
A key ingredient in achieving this is to add a relaxation term to the incompressibility constraint, which is reminiscent to
commonly used techniques of using small artificial compressibility to approximate the incompressibility.
Furthermore, we can show that the rate of convergence is of second order for regular
f
and
g
. In addition, we also give more precise upper bounds of the constants in the estimate of the convergence rate and such bounds are independent on the kernel functions.
Comparing to the previous works presented in
[
8
,
15
]
, our model allows the use of radial symmetric nonlocal interactions and has more freedom in choosing the nonlocal kernel functions. This offers more
convenience in the practical implementation of numerical schemes based on the nonlocal approximations
(such as the SPH used in the fluid simulations).

Since Fourier transform is used as the main tool for the mathematical analysis, the techniques in this paper is
more directly applicable to the case of periodic boundary conditions. An interesting problem is to generalize the nonlocal model to more general geometric domains with general boundary conditions, for instance,
the nonlocal Dirichlet boundary condition. Extensions to nonlinear problems are also of much practical importance.
Although new techniques need to be developed for these further studies,
the results obtained in this paper give some valuable insights to the future development of nonlocal modeling and nonlocal
relaxations of local continuum models.

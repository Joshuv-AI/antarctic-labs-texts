Comment on “A fractal LTB model cannot explain Dark Energy”
L. Cosmai, G. Fanizza, F. Sylos Labini, L. Pietronero, L. Tedesco

Abstract
We reply to the criticisms moved in Pasten:2022ggz against our results presented in Cosmai:2018nvx . In particular, we show that our fractal model has none of the problems claimed in Pasten:2022ggz . The latters can be addressed to the overlooked nonlinear behaviour of the Einstein’s equations.



In a recent preprint, Pastén et
al.

Pasten:2022ggz
have claimed that the fractal Lemaître-Tolman-Bondi (LTB) solution proposed by Cosmai et al.
Cosmai:2018nvx
is not a viable fit for the large scale Hubble-Lemaître diagram. In fact in
Cosmai:2018nvx
we have shown that a fractal distribution of matter is able to fit the UNION 2 data
Amanullah:2010vv
and may provide then a possible explanation for the (apparent) acceleration in the late time Universe.

Pastén et
al
argue that the solution derived in
Cosmai:2018nvx
is a simple redefinition of the radial coordinate in the Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker (FLRW) metric in a Universe filled with dark matter. As that, hence, it is just the Einstein-deSitter model: this statement is incorrect, as we are going to demonstrate.

In fact, the authors of
Pasten:2022ggz
claim that, following the treatment of
Cosmai:2018nvx
, the solution for the proposed LTB model is given by their Eq. (28). This equation is stated as the correct representation of the LTB model in their paper and is given by

R(t,r)=\left(\frac{9M(r)}{2}\right)^{1/3}t^{2/3}\,,

(1)

where
M(r)
is the mass content in a sphere of radius
r
which scales as
r^{D}
for fractal distribution of matter, where
D
is the fractal dimension.
What the authors in
Pasten:2022ggz
argue is that this solution is just an overlooked gauge fixing which leads to Eq. (6) of
Cosmai:2018nvx
, which is (in
Cosmai:2018nvx

A(t,r)
is
R(t,r)
)

A(t,r)=A_{0}(r)\left[1+\frac{3}{2}\,\sqrt{\frac{2GM(r)}{A^{3}_{0}(r)}}\,t\right]^{2/3}\,.

(2)

This statement is not correct. At first glance, we can already appreciate that radial and time dependences in Eq. (
1
) factorise and this is suspect for a radially inhomogeneous model, since Einstein equations are nonlinear. In fact, this factorisation does not occur in general for the solution in Eq. (
2
) precisely as a consequence of the nonlinear dynamical evolution given by General Relativity in presence of radial inhomogeneties.

Moreover, we outline the following discrepancies between our model and the one discussed in
Pasten:2022ggz
.

•

The authors of
Pasten:2022ggz
claims that in
Cosmai:2018nvx
the bang time has been set to 0. This is not true since
in Eq. (
2
) the bang time is related to
2GM(r)
and
A_{0}(r)
. Hence it is not a further choice on top of the setting of
A_{0}(r)
and
2GM(r)
. To see that, we recall that the bang time
t_{BT}(r)
is defined by condition
A(t_{BT}(r),r)=0
, which returns, from Eq. (
2
)

t_{BT}(r)=-\frac{2}{3}\sqrt{\frac{A^{3}_{0}(r)}{2GM(r)}}\,.

(3)

This shows that the solution presented in
Cosmai:2018nvx
describes a inhomogeneous Big-Bang model rather than an Einstein-de Sitter one, and this is independent of the unique actual gauge fixing at our disposal, contrary to what the authors claim in their work
Pasten:2022ggz
(in
Cosmai:2018nvx
, it has been settled
A_{0}(r)=r
).

•

As a corollary to the previous bullet point, we also have that the Hubble-Lemaître function reported in Eq. (29) of
Pasten:2022ggz
is not the same as one gets from Eq. (
2
) in this paper. In fact, if we define
\dot{f}\equiv\partial_{t}f
and
f^{\prime}\equiv\partial_{r}f
, we have

\frac{\dot{A}}{A}(t,r)=\frac{2}{3}\left[t-t_{BT}(r)\right]^{-1}\,.

(4)

Hence, today (
t=0
) the inhomogeneous Hubble-Lemaître
H_{0}(r)
function is

H_{0}(r)\equiv\frac{\dot{A}}{A}(0,r)=-\frac{2}{3\,t_{BT}(r)}\,,

(5)

which is correctly positively defined, since
t_{BT}(r)<0
.
Moreover, for what concern the Hubble functions, it is well-known
Alnes:2005rw
that for the LTB models Eq. (
4
) describes the transverse Hubble-Lemaître flow
H_{\perp}(t,r)
whereas the longitudinal expansion rate is given by

H_{\rVert}(t,r)\equiv\frac{\dot{A}^{\prime}}{A^{\prime}}(t,r)
. These are equal only in the FLRW limit. From Eq. (
2
), we have then that

\frac{\dot{A}^{\prime}}{A^{\prime}}(t,r)=\frac{2}{3}\frac{3t_{BT}(r)A_{0}^{\prime}(r)(t-t_{BT}(r))+A_{0}(r)(3t_{BT}(r)-2t)t_{BT}^{\prime}(r)}{3t_{BT}(r)A_{0}^{\prime}(r)(t-t_{BT}(r))^{2}+2tA_{0}(r)(t_{BT}(r)-t)t_{BT}^{\prime}(r)}\,,

(6)

which is clearly different from
H_{\rVert}
regardless of the gauge fixing for
A_{0}(r)
. Instead, the solution (
1
) provides

\frac{\dot{R}}{R}=\frac{\dot{R}^{\prime}}{R^{\prime}}=\frac{2}{3t}\,,

(7)

which is precisely what happens in the Einstein-de Sitter Universe.

The physical reason behind the apparent contradictory result found by Pastén et al. is the following.

Figure 1:
Plot of
d_{L}(z)=(1+z)^{2}A(t(z),r(z))
for two different cases of the Einstein-deSitter Universe (
M(r)\sim A^{3}_{0}(r)
) and for the fractal Universe (
M(r)\sim A^{D}_{0}(r)
). We have considered three different choices for the gauge fixing of
A_{0}(r)
and integrated the
t(z)
and
r(z)
according to the Eqs. (20)-(21) of
Pasten:2022ggz
or, equivalently, Eqs. (14) of
Cosmai:2018nvx
. It is evident that the choice of
A_{0}(r)
is irrelevant for the ultimate value of
d_{L}(z)
and the only thing that matters is the scaling of
M(r)
in terms of
A_{0}(r)
.

The way to connect Eq. (
1
) with Eq. (
2
) is to require that
M(r)\sim A^{3}_{0}(r)
. In
Pasten:2022ggz
, it is claimed that this is just a gauge fixing of the free function
A_{0}(r)
. On the contrary, this is precisely the physical statement that the Universe they consider is the Einstein-deSitter one. In fact, when
A_{0}(r)
is not yet chosen, it plays the role of the radius, as one can directly check in the LTB line element.
Hence, stating that
M(r)\sim A^{3}_{0}(r)
corresponds to the
physical assumption
that the mass in a sphere of radius
A_{0}(r)
scales as
A^{3}_{0}(r)
, which is the perfectly homogeneous Universe. As a corollary, the correct way to implement a fractal distribution when
A_{0}(r)
is not yet fixed is
M(r)\sim A^{D}_{0}(r)
. In
Cosmai:2018nvx
, this choice reduced to
M(r)\sim r^{D}
since it has been settled
A_{0}(r)=r
. This statement is fully supported by Fig.
1
and has a simple explanation since
d_{L}(z)
is a relation between observables and then cannot depend on a coordinate redefinition.

Our comparison then shows that the solution provided in
Cosmai:2018nvx
is compatible with the gauge freedom allowed by the coordinate redefinition and physically different from the one presented in
Pasten:2022ggz
. The latter is just a coordinate rescaling allowed by the FLRW metric. We conclude then that our fractal model for the late time Universe has none of the problems highlighted in
Pasten:2022ggz
.

Abstract
. Given a quasi-hereditary superalgebra A A , the first author and R. Muth have defined generalized Schur bi-superalgebras T A ​ ( n ) T^{A}(n) and proved that these algebras are again quasi-hereditary. In particular, T A ​ ( n ) T^{A}(n) comes with a family of standard modules. Developing the work of Donkin and Mathieu on good filtrations, we prove that tensor product of standard modules over T A ​ ( n ) T^{A}(n) has a standard filtration.

1.
Introduction

Let
S(n,d)
be the classical Schur algebra as in
[
7
]
. A fundamental fact going back to
[
8
]
is that the algebra
S(n,d)
is (based) quasi-hereditary, and so by
[
1
]
, the module category
S(n,d)\!\operatorname{-mod}
is a highest weight category, cf.
[
15
, (2.5.3)]
, with standard modules
\{\Delta(\lambda)\mid\lambda\in\Lambda_{+}(n,d)\}
, where
\Lambda_{+}(n,d)
is the set of partitions of
d
with at most
n
parts. Moreover, there is a coproduct

{\tt\Delta}

on
S(n)=\bigoplus_{d=0}^{\infty}S(n,d)
, which makes it into a bialgebra. Upon restriction,

\reflectbox{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{${\tt\Delta}$}}:S(n,d)\to\bigoplus_{d_{1}+d_{2}=d}S(n,d_{1})\otimes S(n,d_{2}),

so for
\lambda\in\Lambda_{+}(n,d_{1})
and
\mu\in\Lambda_{+}(n,d_{2})
, the tensor product
\Delta(\lambda)\otimes\Delta(\mu)
becomes an
S(n,d_{1}+d_{2})
-module. It follows from (the easy type
A
case of) the work of Donkin
[
2
]
and Mathieu
[
14
]
that
\Delta(\lambda)\otimes\Delta(\mu)
has a standard filtration, i.e. a filtration whose subquotients are standard modules
\Delta(\nu)
with
\nu\in\Lambda_{+}(n,d_{1}+d_{2})
.

Let
R
be a characteristic
0
domain and
\mathbb{F}
be a field which is an
R
-module. For the purposes of this introduction, we can take
R=\mathbb{Z}
and
\mathbb{F}
any field.
Let
A
be a (based) quasi-hereditary superalgebra over
R
, i.e.
A
is endowed with a heredity data
I,X,Y
, see §
2.3
for details. We make a technical assumption that the data
I,X,Y
are conforming—this assumption is vacuous in the purely even situation and holds in all the important examples known to us, for example when
A
is an extended zigzag superalgebra.

Developing a construction of Turner
[
16
,
17
,
18
]
, the first author and Muth defined generalized Schur (super)algebras

T^{A}(n)=\bigoplus_{d=0}^{\infty}T^{A}(n,d),

see
[
10
]
, cf. also
[
5
]
. Importantly, we must first define
R
-forms

T^{A}(n,d)_{R}
of the algebras and then extend scalars to get
T^{A}(n,d)=\mathbb{F}\otimes_{R}T^{A}(n,d)_{R}
,
so it is crucial that
A
is defined over
R
. When
A
is the trivial algebra
R
, the generalized Schur algebra returns the classical Schur algebra
S(n,d)
.

As predicted by Turner, certain generalized Schur algebras play an important role in modular representation theory, see
[
16
,
6
]
. For example, it is conjectured in
[
11
, Conjecture 7.61]
that weight
d
RoCK blocks of the classical Schur algebras and
q
-Schur algebras are Morita equivalent to the generalized Schur algebras of the form
T^{{\mathsf{Z}}}(n,d)
, where
{\mathsf{Z}}
is an extended zigzag algebra.

It is proved in
[
11
]
that under the assumption
d\leq n
the algebras
T^{A}(n,d)
are again based quasi-hereditary, with heredity data
\Lambda^{I}_{+}(n,d),{\mathcal{X}},{\mathcal{Y}}
, where
\Lambda^{I}_{+}(n,d)
is the set of
I
-multipartitions of
d
, see §
3.4
for details. In particular, the category of finite dimensional
T^{A}(n,d)
-modules is a highest weight category with standard and costandard modules

\{\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\lambda$})\mid\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}\in\Lambda_{+}^{I}(n,d)\}\quad\text{and}\quad\{\nabla(\text{\boldmath$\lambda$})\mid\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}\in\Lambda_{+}^{I}(n,d)\},

respectively.
If
d>n
, the ‘standard’ modules
\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\lambda$})
and the ‘costandard’ modules
\nabla(\text{\boldmath$\lambda$})
are still defined and play an important role. For example, if
A
has a standard anti-involution then
T^{A}(n,d)
is cellular with the cell modules
\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\lambda$})
, see
[
11
, Lemma 6.25]
.

There is a natural coproduct

{\tt\Delta}

on
T^{A}(n)
, with

\reflectbox{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{${\tt\Delta}$}}:T^{A}(n,d)\to\bigoplus_{d_{1}+d_{2}=d}T^{A}(n,d_{1})\otimes T^{A}(n,d_{2}).

So for
\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}\in\Lambda^{I}_{+}(n,d_{1})
and
\text{\boldmath$\mu$}\in\Lambda^{I}_{+}(n,d_{2})
, the tensor product
\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\lambda$})\otimes\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\mu$})
becomes a
T^{A}(n,d_{1}+d_{2})
-module. The main goal of this note is to prove the following

Main Theorem.

The tensor product of two standard (resp. costandard) modules over
T^{A}(n)
has a standard (resp. costandard) filtration.

We hope that this theorem might be useful to study Ringel and Koszul duality for generalized Schur algebras. In particular, we are interested in generalizing the results of Donkin
[
3
,
4
]
on Ringel duality for classical (and
q
-) Schur algebras.
In the follow up paper
[
12
]
, we will use the Main Theorem to prove that the extended zigzag Schur algebra
T^{\mathsf{Z}}(n,d)
is Ringel self-dual.

The paper is organized as follows. Section
2
is preliminary. In particular, in §
2.3
, we review the theory of based quasi-hereditary algebras, and in §§
2.4
,
2.5
, we introduce the necessary combinatorial notions needed to handle the formal characters of standard modules over generalized Schur algebras. In Section
3
, we begin to work with generalized Schur algebras. After reviewing the definition and main properties, in §
3.5
we prove a character identity which is a combinatorial version of the standard filtration of the tensor product of two standard modules. In Section
4
, we prove the Main Theorem. Our argument, when specialized to the case
A=R
, yields a very easy proof for the classical Schur algebra case which seems to be new (or at least not well-known).

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
General notation

For
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
, we denote
[m,n]:=\{k\in\mathbb{Z}\mid m\leq k\leq n\}
. If

n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}

Throughout the paper,

I
with the set
\{0,1,\dots,l\}
for

, so that the standard total order on integers refines the partial order on

For a set

, we often write elements of

as words
\text{\boldmath$s$}=s_{1}\cdots s_{d}
with
s_{1},\dots,s_{d}\in S
.
The symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{d}
acts on the right on

by place permutations:

(s_{1}\cdots s_{d})\sigma=s_{\sigma 1}\cdots s_{\sigma d}.

An (arbitrary) ground field is denoted by
\mathbb{F}
.
Often we will also need to work over a characteristic
0
domain
R
such that
\mathbb{F}
is a
R
-module, so that we can change scalars from
R
to
\mathbb{F}
(in all examples of interest to us, one can use
R=\mathbb{Z}
). We use
\Bbbk
to denote
\mathbb{F}
or
R
and use it whenever the nature of the ground ring is not important. On the other hand, when it is important to emphasize whether we are working over
R
or
\mathbb{F}
, we will use lower indices; for example for an
R
-algebra
A_{R}
and an
A_{R}
-module
V_{R}
, after extending scalars we have that
V_{\mathbb{F}}:=\mathbb{F}\otimes_{R}V_{R}
is a module over

.

2.2.
Superspaces and supermodules

Let
V=\bigoplus_{{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{Z}/2}V_{\varepsilon}
be a
\Bbbk
-supermodule.
If
v\in V_{\varepsilon}\setminus\{0\}
for
{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{Z}/2
, we say
v
is
homogeneous
, write
|v|={\varepsilon}
, and refer to
{\varepsilon}
as the
parity
of
v
.
If
S\subseteq V
, we denote
S_{\bar{0}}:=S\cap V_{\bar{0}}
and
S_{\bar{1}}:=S\cap V_{\bar{1}}
. If
S
consists of homogeneous elements then
S=S_{\bar{0}}\sqcup S_{\bar{1}}
.
Let
V
and
W
be superspaces. For
\delta\in\mathbb{Z}/2
, a parity
\delta
(homogeneous) linear map
f:V\to W
is a linear map satisfying
f(V_{{\varepsilon}})\subseteq W_{{\varepsilon}+\delta}
for all
{\varepsilon}
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. The group
\mathfrak{S}_{d}
acts on
V^{\otimes d}
on the right with automorphisms, such that for all homogeneous
v_{1},\dots,v_{d}\in V
and
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{d}
, we have

(v_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes v_{d})^{\sigma}=(-1)^{\langle\sigma;\text{\boldmath$v$}\rangle}v_{\sigma 1}\otimes\dots\otimes v_{\sigma d},

(2.1)

where, setting
\text{\boldmath$v$}:=v_{1}\cdots v_{d}\in V^{d}
, we have put:

\langle\sigma;\text{\boldmath$v$}\rangle:=\sharp\{(k,l)\in[d]^{2}\mid k<l,\,\sigma^{-1}k>\sigma^{-1}l,\ \text{and}\ v_{k},v_{l}\in V_{\bar{1}}\}.

(2.2)

For
0\leq c\leq d
, denote by
{}^{(c,d-c)}{\mathscr{D}}
the set of the shortest coset representatives for
(\mathfrak{S}_{c}\times\mathfrak{S}_{d-c})\backslash\mathfrak{S}_{d}
. Given
w_{1}\in V^{\otimes c}
and
w_{2}\in V^{\otimes(d-c)}
, we define the
star product

w_{1}*w_{2}:=\sum_{\sigma\in{}^{(c,d-c)}{\mathscr{D}}}(w_{1}\otimes w_{2})^{\sigma}\in V^{\otimes d}.

(2.3)

Let
V
and
W
be superspaces,
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, and
\text{\boldmath$v$}=v_{1}\cdots v_{d}\in V^{d}
and
\text{\boldmath$w$}=w_{1}\cdots w_{d}\in W^{d}
be
d
-tuples of homogeneous elements. We denote

\langle\text{\boldmath$v$},\text{\boldmath$w$}\rangle:=\sharp\{(k,l)\in[d]^{2}\mid k>l,\ v_{k}\in V_{\bar{1}},w_{l}\in W_{\bar{1}}\}.

(2.4)

Let now
A
be a unital
\Bbbk
-superalgebra.
As usual, the tensor product
A^{\otimes d}
is a superalgebra with respect to

(a_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes a_{d})(b_{1}\otimes\dots b_{d})=(-1)^{\langle\text{\boldmath$a$},\text{\boldmath$b$}\rangle}a_{1}b_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes a_{d}b_{d},

where we have put
\text{\boldmath$a$}:=a_{1}\cdots a_{d}
,
\text{\boldmath$b$}:=b_{1}\cdots b_{d}
(here and below, in expressions like this, we assume that all elements are homogeneous).

For any superspace
V
, we consider the subspace of invariants

\Gamma^{d}V:=\big(V^{\otimes d}\big)^{\mathfrak{S}_{d}}=\{w\in V^{\otimes d}\mid w^{\sigma}=w\ \text{for all $\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{d}$}\}.

(2.5)

If
A
is a superalgebra, then
\Gamma^{d}A
is a subsuperalgebra of
A^{\otimes d}
.

Let
A
be a unital
\Bbbk
-superalgebra and
V,W
be
A
-supermodules. A homogeneous
A
-supermodule homomorphism

f:V\to W
is a homogeneous linear map
f:V\to W
satisfying
f(av)=(-1)^{|f||a|}af(v)
for all (homogeneous)
a,v
. Let

\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(V,W)=\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(V,W)_{\bar{0}}\oplus\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(V,W)_{\bar{1}}

be the superspace of all
A
-supermodule homomorphisms from
V
to
W
.
We denote by
A\!\operatorname{-mod}
the category of all finitely generated (left)
A
-supermodules and all
A
-supermodule homomorphisms. We denote by ‘
\cong
’ an isomorphism in this category and by ‘
\simeq
’ an
even isomorphism
in this category.

We have the parity change functor
\Pi
on
A\!\operatorname{-mod}
:
for
V\in A\!\operatorname{-mod}
we have
\Pi V\in A\!\operatorname{-mod}
with
(\Pi V)_{\varepsilon}=V_{{\varepsilon}+{\bar{1}}}
for all
{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{Z}/2
and the new action

a\cdot v=(-1)^{|a|}av
for
a\in A,v\in V
. We have
V\cong\Pi V
via the identity map.

All subspaces, ideals, submodules, etc. are assumed to be homogeneous.
For example, given homogeneous elements
v_{1},\dots,v_{k}
of an
A
-supermodule
V
, we have the
A
-submodule
A\langle v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\rangle\subseteq V
generated by
v_{1},\dots,v_{k}
.

2.3.
Based quasi-hereditary algebras

The main reference here is
[
9
]
.
Let
A
be a
\Bbbk
-superalgebra.

Definition 2.6
.

[
9
]
Let
I
be a finite partially ordered set and let
X=\bigsqcup_{i\in I}X(i)
and
Y=\bigsqcup_{i\in I}Y(i)
be finite sets of homogeneous elements of
A
with distinguished
initial elements

e_{i}\in X(i)\cap Y(i)
for each
i\in I
.
For each
i\in I
, we set

A^{>i}:=\operatorname{span}\{xy\mid j>i,\,x\in X(j),\,y\in Y(j)\}
.
We say that
I,X,Y
is
heredity data
if the following axioms hold:

(a)

B:=\{xy\mid i\in I,\,x\in X(i),\,y\in Y(i)\}
is a basis of
A
;

(b)

For all
i\in I
,
x\in X(i)
,
y\in Y(i)
and
a\in A
, we have

ax\equiv\sum_{x^{\prime}\in X(i)}l^{x}_{x^{\prime}}(a)x^{\prime}\text{ }(\text{\rm mod }A^{>i})\,\ \ \text{and}\ \ ya\equiv\sum_{y^{\prime}\in Y(i)}r^{y}_{y^{\prime}}(a)y^{\prime}\text{ }(\text{\rm mod }A^{>i})\,

for some
l^{x}_{x^{\prime}}(a),r^{y}_{y^{\prime}}(a)\in\Bbbk
;

(c)

For all
i,j\in I
and
x\in X(i),\ y\in Y(i)
we have

\displaystyle xe_{i}=x,\ e_{i}x=\delta_{x,e_{i}}x,\ e_{i}y=y,\ ye_{i}=\delta_{y,e_{i}}y

\displaystyle e_{j}x=x\ \text{or}\ 0,\ ye_{j}=y\ \text{or}\ 0.

If
A
is endowed with heredity data
I,X,Y
, we call
A

based quasi-hereditary
, and refer to
B
as a
heredity basis
of
A
.

From now on,
A
is a based quasi-hereditary superalgebra with heredity data
I,X,Y
. We refer to the idempotents
e_{i}
as the
standard idempotents
of the heredity data.
We have
B=B_{\bar{0}}\sqcup B_{\bar{1}}
and

B_{\bar{0}}=B_{\mathfrak{a}}\sqcup B_{\mathfrak{c}},

(2.7)

where

B_{\mathfrak{a}}:=\{xy\mid i\in I,x\in X(i)_{\bar{0}},y\in Y(i)_{\bar{0}}\},\quad B_{\mathfrak{c}}:=\{xy\mid i\in I,x\in X(i)_{\bar{1}},y\in Y(i)_{\bar{1}}\}.

The heredity data
I,X,Y
of
A
is called
conforming
if
B_{\mathfrak{a}}
spans a unital subalgebra of
A
.

Lemma 2.8
.

[
9
, Lemmas 2.7, 2.8]

Let
i,j\in I
and
x\in X(i)
,
y\in Y(i)
.

(i)

e_{i}e_{j}=\delta_{i,j}e_{i}

(ii)

If
j\not\leq i
, then
e_{j}x=ye_{j}=0
.

Corollary 2.9
.

We have
X\cap Y=\{e_{i}\mid i\in I\}
.

Proof.

Let
z\in X\cap Y
. As
z\in X
we have
z\in X(i)
so
ze_{i}=z
for some
i\in I
. As
z\in Y
, we have
z\in Y(j)
so
e_{j}z=z
for some

. By Lemma
2.8
(ii),

, and the result follows from Definition
2.6
(c).
∎

Definition 2.10
.

Let
0\neq V\in A\!\operatorname{-mod}
and
i\in I
. We call

)
if there exists a homogeneous

such that

is spanned by

, and

of

.

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
i\in I
,
0\neq V\in A\!\operatorname{-mod}
and

,

, and

Since

Fix
i\in I
. Note that

is an ideal in

\tilde{A}:=A/A^{>i}
,
\tilde{a}:=a+A^{>i}\in\tilde{A}

.
By inflation,

-(super)modules will be automatically considered as

-(super)modules. In particular, the

is considered as an

is a free

-module with basis

cf.

. Denoting

, and

is a highest weight module of weight

We also have the right standard

Let

. A

We refer to

Suppose now until the end of the subsection that
\Bbbk=\mathbb{F}

Then each

Proposition 2.12
.

(Universality of standard modules)

Let
\Bbbk=\mathbb{F}
,
i\in I
, and
V
be a highest weight module of weight
i
with highest weight vector
v
. Then there is an homogeneous surjection
\Delta(i){\twoheadrightarrow}V
of parity
|v|
; in particular
e_{j}V\neq 0
implies
j\leq i
.

Proof.

Let
e_{i}V
be spanned by
v\in V
. There is a homogeneous surjective
A
-supermodule homomorphism
{\varphi}:Ae_{i}{\twoheadrightarrow}V,\ ae_{i}\mapsto av
of parity
|{\varphi}|=|v|
.
As
e_{i}L(i)
is
1
-dimensional and
e_{i}L(j)\neq 0
implies
i\leq j
, we have that
Ae_{i}=P(i)\oplus P
, where
P
is a direct sum of supermodules isomorphic to
P(j)
with
j>i
.

Note that
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(\Delta(j),V)=0
for any
j>i
, so
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(P(j),V)=0
for all
j>i
, and we deduce
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(P,V)=0
. So the map
{\varphi}
factors to give a surjection
P(i){\twoheadrightarrow}V
. Moreover,
P(i)
has a standard filtration with top factor
\Delta(i)
and other factors isomorphic to
\Delta(j)
with
j>i
, so the map further factors through the surjection
\Delta(i){\twoheadrightarrow}V
.
∎

The following is a useful criterion for
V
to have a standard filtration.

Corollary 2.13
.

Let
\Bbbk=\mathbb{F}
,
V\in A\!\operatorname{-mod}
,
v_{1}\dots,v_{t}\in V
be homogeneous elements, and set
V_{s}:=A\langle v_{1},\dots,v_{s}\rangle
for
s=1,\dots,t
. Suppose that the following conditions hold:

(1)

V_{t}=V
;

(2)

for each
s=1,\dots,t
there exists
i_{s}\in I
such that
e_{i_{s}}v_{s}-v_{s}\in V_{s-1}
and
yv_{s}\in V_{s-1}
for all
y\in Y\setminus\{e_{i_{s}}\}
;

(3)

\dim V=\sum_{s=1}^{t}\dim\Delta(i_{s})
.

\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\lambda$})\simeq\bigotimes_{i\in I}\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\iota$}_{i}(\lambda^{(i)})).

3.5.
Character formula

Throughout the subsection we continue to assume that

{}_{j}X(i):=\{x\in X(i)\mid e_{j}x=x\}\qquad(i,j\in I).

Note that

only if

.
For

Fix

\chi:{\mathrm{Sym}}^{X(i)}\to{\mathrm{Sym}}^{I},\ \bigotimes_{x\in X(i)}f_{x}\mapsto\bigotimes_{j\in I}\prod_{x\in{}_{j}X(i)}f_{x},

\text{\boldmath$\nu$}\in\Lambda_{+}^{X(i)}

\chi\left(\text{\boldmath$s$}_{\text{\boldmath$\nu$}}\right)=\sum_{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}\in\Lambda_{+}^{I}}\prod_{j\in I}c_{{}_{j}\text{\boldmath$\nu$}}^{\gamma^{(j)}}\text{\boldmath$s$}_{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}}.

(3.29)

For a multipartition

its
superconjugate
multipartition

\tilde{\nu}^{(x)}

if

is odd.
Using
[

{\mathsf{con}}:{\mathrm{Sym}}^{X(i)}\to{\mathrm{Sym}}^{X(i)},s_{\text{\boldmath$\nu$}}\mapsto s_{\text{\boldmath$\nu$}^{\mathsf{con}}}.

Let
t:=|X(i)|
. By choosing a total order on

we will identify

\Lambda_{+}^{X(i)}
with
\Lambda_{+}^{t}
,

{\mathrm{Sym}}^{X(i)}
with
{\mathrm{Sym}}^{\otimes t}
, etc. In particular, we have a well-defined map

Recalling (
2.19
) and (

), we now have:

Theorem 3.30
.

Let

,
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, with
d\leq n
, and

\lambda\in\Lambda_{+}(n,d)\subseteq\Lambda_{+}
and

. Then

\operatorname{ch}\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\iota$}_{i}(\lambda))=\rho_{n}^{I}\circ\chi\circ{\mathsf{con}}\circ{\tt\Delta}^{t-1}(s_{\lambda}).

Proof.

By
[
11
, Proposition 7.45]
, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{ch}\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\iota$}_{i}(\lambda))

\displaystyle=\sum_{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}\in\Lambda^{I}_{+}(n)}\,\sum_{\text{\boldmath$\nu$}\in\Lambda_{+}(n)^{t}}c^{\lambda}_{\text{\boldmath$\nu$}^{{\mathsf{con}}}}\bigg(\prod_{j\in I}c^{\gamma^{(j)}}_{\,{}_{j}\text{\boldmath$\nu$}}\bigg)s_{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})

\displaystyle=\rho_{n}^{I}\Bigg(\sum_{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}\in\Lambda^{I}_{+}}\,\sum_{\text{\boldmath$\nu$}\in\Lambda_{+}^{t}}c^{\lambda}_{\text{\boldmath$\nu$}^{{\mathsf{con}}}}\bigg(\prod_{j\in I}c^{\gamma^{(j)}}_{\,{}_{j}\text{\boldmath$\nu$}}\bigg)s_{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}}\Bigg)

\displaystyle=\rho_{n}^{I}\circ\chi\Bigg(\sum_{\text{\boldmath$\nu$}\in\Lambda_{+}^{t}}c^{\lambda}_{\text{\boldmath$\nu$}^{{\mathsf{con}}}}s_{\text{\boldmath$\nu$}}\Bigg)

\displaystyle=\rho_{n}^{I}\circ\chi\circ{\mathsf{con}}\Bigg(\sum_{\text{\boldmath$\nu$}\in\Lambda_{+}^{t}}c^{\lambda}_{\text{\boldmath$\nu$}}s_{\text{\boldmath$\nu$}}\Bigg)

\displaystyle=\rho_{n}^{I}\circ\chi\circ{\mathsf{con}}\circ{\tt\Delta}^{t-1}(s_{\lambda}),

where we have used (
3.29
) for the third equality and (
2.21
) for the last equality.
∎

Theorem 3.31
.

Let
\lambda\in\Lambda_{+}(n,d)
,
\mu\in\Lambda_{+}(n,e)
and
i\in I
. If
d+e\leq n
then

\operatorname{ch}\big(\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\iota$}_{i}(\lambda))\otimes\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\iota$}_{i}(\mu))\big)=\sum_{\nu\in\Lambda_{+}(n,d+e)}c^{\,\nu}_{\lambda,\mu}\operatorname{ch}\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\iota$}_{i}(\nu)).

Proof.

By Lemma
3.13
, Theorem
3.30
and the Littlewood-Richardson rule, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{ch}\big(\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\iota$}_{i}(\lambda))\otimes\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\iota$}_{i}(\mu))\big)

\displaystyle=\big(\operatorname{ch}\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\iota$}_{i}(\lambda))\big)\big(\operatorname{ch}\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\iota$}_{i}(\mu))\big)

\displaystyle=\big(\rho_{n}^{I}\circ\chi\circ{\mathsf{con}}\circ{\tt\Delta}^{t-1}(s_{\lambda})\big)\,\big(\rho_{n}^{I}\circ\chi\circ{\mathsf{con}}\circ{\tt\Delta}^{t-1}(s_{\mu})\big)

\displaystyle=\rho_{n}^{I}\circ\chi\circ{\mathsf{con}}\circ{\tt\Delta}^{t-1}(s_{\lambda}s_{\mu})

\displaystyle=\rho_{n}^{I}\circ\chi\circ{\mathsf{con}}\circ{\tt\Delta}^{t-1}\Bigg(\sum_{\nu\in\Lambda_{+}(n,d+e)}c^{\,\nu}_{\lambda,\mu}s_{\nu}\Bigg)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\nu\in\Lambda_{+}(n,d+e)}c^{\,\nu}_{\lambda,\mu}\operatorname{ch}\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\iota$}_{i}(\nu)),

as required.
∎

For
\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}=(\lambda^{(j)})_{j\in I}\in\Lambda^{I}_{+}(n,d)
,
\text{\boldmath$\mu$}=(\mu^{(j)})_{j\in I}\in\Lambda^{I}_{+}(n,e)

Let

4.

We again fix a based quasi-hereditary superalgebra
A_{R}
over

with conforming heredity data

), we have the (
\mathbb{F}
-)superalgebra

4.1.
Reduction

We begin to prove Main Theorem by
reducing to the case of ‘one color’ and ‘fundamental dominant weights’, cf.
[
19
, (3.5)]
,
[
2
, Proposition 3.5.4(i)]
. For integer
0\leq c\leq n
, recalling (
2.16
), we have

\omega_{c}:={\varepsilon}_{1}+\dots+{\varepsilon}_{c}\in\Lambda_{+}(n,c).

Proposition 4.1
.

Suppose that for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
,
d,c\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
with
d+c\leq n
,
\lambda\in\Lambda_{+}(n,d)
, and
i\in I
, the tensor product
\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\iota$}_{i}(\lambda))\otimes\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\iota$}_{i}(\omega_{c}))
has a standard filtration. Then for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
,
d,c\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
with
d+c\leq n
,
\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}\in\Lambda^{I}_{+}(n,d)
,
\text{\boldmath$\mu$}\in\Lambda_{+}^{I}(n,c)
, the tensor product
\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\lambda$})\otimes\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\mu$})
has a standard filtration.

Proof.

We apply induction on the total degree
d+c
, the base case
d+c=0
being trivial, since
T(n,0)\cong\mathbb{F}
. Let
d+c>0
.
Take
\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}=(\lambda^{(0)},\dots,\lambda^{(l)})\in\Lambda^{I}_{+}(n,d)
and
\text{\boldmath$\mu$}=(\mu^{(0)},\dots,\mu^{(l)})\in\Lambda^{I}_{+}(n,c)
. For all
i\in I
, set
d_{i}=|\lambda^{(i)}|
and
c_{i}:=|\mu^{(i)}|
.
By Theorem
3.28
, we have

\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\lambda$})\otimes\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\mu$})\simeq\bigotimes_{i\in I}\Big(\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\iota$}_{i}(\lambda^{(i)}))\otimes\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\iota$}_{i}(\mu^{(i)}))\Big).

(4.2)

Suppose there exist distinct
j,k\in I
with
d_{j},d_{k}>0
. Then
d_{i}<d
for all
i\in I
. By the inductive assumption, for all
i\in I
, we then have that
\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\iota$}_{i}(\lambda^{(i)}))\otimes\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\iota$}_{i}(\mu^{(i)}))
has a standard filtration. It follows from Lemma
3.26
and Theorem
3.31
that in this filtration only subquotients of the form
\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\iota$}_{i}(\nu^{(i)}))
with
\nu^{(i)}\in\Lambda_{+}(n,d_{i}+c_{i})
appear. Hence by Theorem
3.28
, the right hand side of (
4.2
) has a filtration with subquotients of the form

\bigotimes_{i\in I}\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\iota$}_{i}(\nu^{(i)}))\simeq\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\nu$})
. Thus we may assume that there exists a unique
i
with
d_{i}=d
and
d_{k}=0
for all
k\neq i
, i.e.
\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}=\text{\boldmath$\iota$}_{i}(\lambda)
for some
i\in I
and
\lambda\in\Lambda_{+}(n,d)
. Similarly we may assume that
\text{\boldmath$\mu$}=\text{\boldmath$\iota$}_{j}(\mu)
for some
j\in I
and
\mu\in\Lambda_{+}(n,c)
. Moreover, we may assume that
j=i
since otherwise

\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\lambda$})\otimes\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\mu$})=\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\iota$}_{i}(\lambda))\otimes\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\iota$}_{j}(\mu))
is a standard module, thanks to Theorem
3.28
.

We now also apply induction on the dominance order on
\mu
. If
\mu
is minimal in the dominance order, then
\mu=\omega_{c}
and we are done by assumption. Otherwise, we can write
\mu=\gamma+\omega_{r}
for
\gamma\in\Lambda_{+}(n,s)
with
0<s,r<c
. By the inductive assumption on the degree, we have that
\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\iota$}_{i}(\gamma))\otimes\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\iota$}_{i}(\omega_{r}))
has a standard filtration. By Lemma
3.26
and Theorem
3.31
, in this filtration
\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\iota$}_{i}(\mu))
appears once and other standard subquotients are of the form
\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\iota$}_{i}(\nu))
with
\nu\lhd\mu
.
By
[
4
, Proposition A2.2(i)]
, there is a short exact sequence

0\to\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\iota$}_{i}(\mu))\to\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\iota$}_{i}(\gamma))\otimes\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\iota$}_{i}(\omega_{r}))\to Q\to 0,

where
Q
has a standard filtration with subquotients of the form
\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\iota$}_{i}(\nu))
with
\nu\lhd\mu
. Tensoring with
\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\iota$}_{i}(\lambda))
we get a short exact sequence

0\to\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\iota$}_{i}(\lambda))\otimes\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\iota$}_{i}(\mu))\to\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\iota$}_{i}(\lambda))\otimes\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\iota$}_{i}(\gamma))\otimes\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\iota$}_{i}(\omega_{r}))\to\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\iota$}_{i}(\lambda))\otimes Q\to 0.

By induction on the dominance order,
\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\iota$}_{i}(\lambda))\otimes Q
has a standard filtration. By induction on the degree, using Lemma
3.26
and Theorem
3.31
, we have that
\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\iota$}_{i}(\lambda))\otimes\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\iota$}_{i}(\gamma))
has a standard filtration with subquotients of the form
\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\iota$}_{i}(\kappa))
, hence by assumption, the middle term has a standard filtration. By
[
4
, Proposition A2.2(v)]
, the first term has a standard filtration.
∎

r=0,1,\dots,t
, we denote

M_{r}:=T(n,d)\langle v_{\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}}\otimes w_{P_{s}}\mid 1\leq s\leq r\rangle\subseteq\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\lambda$})\otimes\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\mu$}).

In view of Lemma
4.7
, we have a filtration

0=M_{0}\subseteq M_{1}\subseteq\dots\subseteq M_{t}=\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\lambda$})\otimes\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\mu$}).

(4.9)

Our goal is to show that

M_{r}/M_{r-1}\simeq\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\iota$}_{i}(\lambda+{\varepsilon}_{P_{r}}))
for all
r=1,\dots,t
, to get the required standard filtration of
\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\lambda$})\otimes\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\mu$})
.

Lemma 4.10
.

If
1\leq r\leq t
and
P\in\mathfrak{S}_{\lambda}\cdot P_{r}
, then
v_{\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}}\otimes w_{P}\in M_{r}
.

Proof.

Write
P=\{p_{1},\dots,p_{c}\}
with
p_{1}<\dots<p_{c}
.
Let
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{\lambda}
be such that
\sigma P_{r}=P
and
\sigma p_{1}<\dots<\sigma p_{c}
.
Note using Lemmas
3.24
,
4.5
and
3.18
that

v_{\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}}\otimes w_{P}=\pm(\xi_{d}(\text{\boldmath$\iota$}_{i}(\sigma))v_{\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}})\otimes(\xi_{c}(\text{\boldmath$\iota$}_{i}(\sigma))w_{P_{r}})=\pm\xi_{d+c}(\text{\boldmath$\iota$}_{i}(\sigma))(v_{\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}}\otimes w_{P_{r}})\in M_{r},

as required.
∎

Lemma 4.11
.

Let
1\leq r\leq t
and
E\in{\mathcal{Y}}(n,d+c)
. If
E(v_{\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}}\otimes w_{P_{r}})\not\in M_{r-1}
then
E=\eta_{\text{\boldmath$\iota$}_{i}(\lambda+{\varepsilon}_{P_{r}})}
. In particular,
M_{r}/M_{r-1}
is a highest weight module of weight
\text{\boldmath$\iota$}_{i}(\lambda+{\varepsilon}_{P_{r}})
.

Proof.

Suppose
E(v_{\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}}\otimes w_{P_{r}})\not\in M_{r-1}
. Write
E={\mathcal{Y}}_{\text{\boldmath$T$}}:=\eta^{\text{\boldmath$y$}^{\text{\boldmath$T$}}}_{\text{\boldmath$l$}^{\text{\boldmath$\nu$}},\text{\boldmath$l$}^{\text{\boldmath$T$}}}
, with
\text{\boldmath$T$}\in\operatorname{Std}^{Y}(\text{\boldmath$\nu$})
for some
\text{\boldmath$\nu$}\in\Lambda^{I}_{+}(n,d+c)
.
By Lemma
3.12
,
\reflectbox{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{${\tt\Delta}$}}(\eta^{\text{\boldmath$y$}^{\text{\boldmath$T$}}}_{\text{\boldmath$l$}^{\text{\boldmath$\nu$}},\text{\boldmath$l$}^{\text{\boldmath$T$}}})
is a linear combination of elements of the form
\eta_{\text{\boldmath$r$},\text{\boldmath$s$}}^{\text{\boldmath$y$}}\otimes\eta_{\text{\boldmath$r$}^{\prime},\text{\boldmath$s$}^{\prime}}^{\text{\boldmath$y$}^{\prime}}
such that
\text{\boldmath$y$}\text{\boldmath$y$}^{\prime}\sim\text{\boldmath$y$}^{T}
. By Lemma
3.27
,
\eta_{\text{\boldmath$r$},\text{\boldmath$s$}}^{\text{\boldmath$y$}}v_{\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}}\neq 0
only if
\text{\boldmath$y$}=e_{i}^{d}
, and

\eta_{\text{\boldmath$r$}^{\prime},\text{\boldmath$s$}^{\prime}}^{\text{\boldmath$y$}^{\prime}}w_{P_{r}}\neq 0
only if
\text{\boldmath$y$}^{\prime}=e_{i}^{c}
.
We conclude that

\text{\boldmath$y$}^{\text{\boldmath$T$}}=e_{i}^{d+c}
, and so

E
can be written in the form
E=\eta^{e_{i}^{d+c}}_{\text{\boldmath$r$},\text{\boldmath$s$}}
with
r_{k}\leq s_{k}
for all
k
.

If
r_{k}<s_{k}
for some
k
, then in view of Lemma
3.12
and Lemma
4.4
,
E(v_{\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}}\otimes w_{P_{r}})
is a multiple of
v_{\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}}\otimes w_{P}
for some
P<P_{r}
. By Lemma
4.3
,
P\in\mathfrak{S}_{\lambda}\cdot P_{s}
for some
s<r
, hence
v_{\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}}\otimes w_{P}\in M_{s}\subseteq M_{r-1}
by Lemma
4.10
, giving a contradiction.
So
r_{k}=s_{k}
for all
k
. Then
E
is of the form
\eta_{\text{\boldmath$\nu$}}
, and
E(v_{\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}}\otimes w_{P_{r}})\neq 0
implies
\text{\boldmath$\nu$}=\text{\boldmath$\iota$}_{i}(\lambda+{\varepsilon}_{P_{r}})
.

The second statement now follows from Lemma
2.11
.
∎

Theorem 4.12
.

We have a filtration of
T(n,d+c)
-modules

0=M_{0}\subseteq M_{1}\subseteq\dots\subseteq M_{t}=\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\lambda$})\otimes\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\mu$})

such that
M_{r}/M_{r-1}\simeq\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\iota$}_{i}(\lambda+{\varepsilon}_{P_{r}}))
for all
r=1,\dots,t
.

Proof.

We consider the filtration (
4.9
). By Lemma
4.11
, each
M_{r}/M_{r-1}
is a highest weight module of weight
\text{\boldmath$\iota$}_{i}(\lambda+{\varepsilon}_{P_{r}})
.
Moreover, recalling that
\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}=\text{\boldmath$\iota$}_{i}(\lambda)
and
\text{\boldmath$\mu$}=\text{\boldmath$\iota$}_{i}(\omega_{c})
, by Theorem
3.31
, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{ch}\big(\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\lambda$})\otimes\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\mu$})\big)=\sum_{\nu\in\Lambda_{+}(n,d+e)}c^{\,\nu}_{\lambda,\omega_{c}}\operatorname{ch}\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\iota$}_{i}(\nu))=\sum_{r=1}^{t}\operatorname{ch}\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\iota$}_{i}(\lambda+{\varepsilon}_{P_{r}})),

where we have used Pieri’s rule for the last equality.
Therefore, using linear independence of characters, we get

\dim\big(\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\lambda$})\otimes\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\mu$})\big)=\sum_{r=1}^{t}\dim\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\iota$}_{i}(\lambda+{\varepsilon}_{P_{r}})).

An application of Corollary
2.13
yields that each
M_{r}/M_{r-1}
must be isomorphic to
\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\iota$}_{i}(\lambda+{\varepsilon}_{P_{r}}))
.
∎

Recall (
2.15
) and (
3.32
).

Corollary 4.13
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
,
d,c\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
with
d+c\leq n
,
\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}\in\Lambda^{I}_{+}(n,d)
,
\text{\boldmath$\mu$}\in\Lambda_{+}^{I}(n,c)
and
\text{\boldmath$\nu$}\in\Lambda^{I}_{+}(n,d+c)
. Then the tensor product
\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\lambda$})\otimes\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\mu$})
has a standard filtration, and

(\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\lambda$})\otimes\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\mu$}):\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\nu$}))=c_{\text{\boldmath$\lambda$},\text{\boldmath$\mu$}}^{\text{\boldmath$\nu$}}.

Proof.

The first statement follows from Proposition
4.1
and Theorem
4.12
. The second statement now follows from Corollary
3.33
using linear independence of formal characters.
∎

By a symmetric argument (switching the roles of
{\mathcal{X}}
and
{\mathcal{Y}}
everywhere), we also have the right module version of Corollary
4.13
, which claims that the right
T(n,d+c)
-module
\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}(\text{\boldmath$\lambda$})\otimes\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}(\text{\boldmath$\mu$})
has a
\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}
-filtration.
In view of (
2.14
), by dualizing, we now get:

Corollary 4.14
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
,
d,c\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
with
d+c\leq n
,
\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}\in\Lambda^{I}_{+}(n,d)
,
\text{\boldmath$\mu$}\in\Lambda_{+}^{I}(n,c)
and
\text{\boldmath$\nu$}\in\Lambda^{I}_{+}(n,d+c)
. Then the tensor product
\nabla(\text{\boldmath$\lambda$})\otimes\nabla(\text{\boldmath$\mu$})
has a costandard filtration.

Remark 4.15
.

Note that in Theorem
4.12
, the factors of the standard filtration are isomorphic to standard modules via
even
isomorphisms. Using this fact and (an appropriate strengthening of) Proposition
4.1
, one can similarly strengthen Corollaries
4.13
and
4.14
.

4.3.
The case of small
n

Let
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. If
n<d
, the algebra
T(n,d)
does not have to be quasihereditary, but it still has a natural family of ‘standard’ and ‘costandard’ modules which play an important role. For example, if
A
has a standard anti-involution then
T(n,d)
is cellular with ‘standard’ modules being the cell modules, see
[
11
, Lemma 6.25]
. These ‘standard’ (resp. ‘costandard’) modules are obtained by an idempotent truncation from the standard modules
\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\lambda$})
(resp. costandard modules
\nabla(\text{\boldmath$\lambda$})
) over
T(N,d)
for any
N\geq d
. We now provide details on that.

Throughout the subsection we assume that
N\geq n
. In view of Lemma
3.20
, we now always identify the algebras
T(n,d)
and

\eta^{N}_{n}(d)T(N,d)\eta^{N}_{n}(d)
. For any
T(N,d)
-module
V
, we consider
\eta^{N}_{n}(d)V
as a module over
T(n,d)=\eta^{N}_{n}(d)T(N,d)\eta^{N}_{n}(d)
.

We always consider
\Lambda_{+}^{I}(n,d)
as a subset of
\Lambda_{+}^{I}(N,d)
by adding
N-n
zeroes to every component
\lambda^{(i)}
of
\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}=(\lambda^{(0)},\dots,\lambda^{(l)})\in\Lambda_{+}^{I}(n,d)
. Note that this embedding is a bijection if
n\geq d
. However, when we consider
\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}\in\Lambda_{+}^{I}(n,d)
as an element of
\Lambda_{+}^{I}(N,d)
the set
\operatorname{Std}^{X}(\text{\boldmath$\lambda$})
of standard
X
-colored
\lambda
-tableaux changes, so in this subsection we will use the more detailed notation
\operatorname{Std}^{X}_{n}(\text{\boldmath$\lambda$})
to indicate that the entries of the tableaux are of the form
r^{x}
with
r\in[n]
. We will also use the
more detailed notation
\Delta_{n}(\text{\boldmath$\lambda$})
for the standard
T(n,d)
-module
\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\lambda$})
which so far has only been defined for all
\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}\in\Lambda_{+}^{I}(n,d)
when
n\geq d
. Recall from (
3.22
) that for
n\geq d
we have that
\Delta_{n}(\text{\boldmath$\lambda$})
has basis
\{v_{\text{\boldmath$T$}}:={\mathcal{X}}_{\text{\boldmath$T$}}v_{\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}}\mid\text{\boldmath$T$}\in\operatorname{Std}^{X}_{n}(\text{\boldmath$\lambda$})\}
.

Let
N\geq d
. Fix
\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}\in\Lambda_{+}^{I}(N,d)
. Recall the idempotent
\eta^{N}_{n}(d)
of (
3.19
). It is easy to see that for
\text{\boldmath$T$}\in\operatorname{Std}_{N}^{X}(\text{\boldmath$\lambda$})
, we have

\eta^{N}_{n}(d)v_{\text{\boldmath$T$}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}v_{\text{\boldmath$T$}}&\hbox{if $\text{\boldmath$T$}\in\operatorname{Std}_{n}^{X}(\text{\boldmath$\lambda$})$},\\
0&\hbox{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

(4.16)

If
n\geq d
it follows from (
4.16
) that
\dim\Delta_{n}(\text{\boldmath$\lambda$})=\dim\eta^{N}_{n}(d)\Delta_{N}(\text{\boldmath$\lambda$})
. Since the
T(n,d)
-module
\eta^{N}_{n}(d)\Delta_{N}(\text{\boldmath$\lambda$})
is easily seen to be a highest weight module of weight
\lambda
, Proposition
2.12
now yields an isomorphism of
T(n,d)
-modules

\Delta_{n}(\text{\boldmath$\lambda$})\simeq\eta^{N}_{n}(d)\Delta_{N}(\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}).

(4.17)

Now for
n<d
and
\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}\in\Lambda_{+}^{I}(N,d)
, we define the ‘standard’ module

\Delta_{n}(\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}):=\eta^{N}_{n}(d)\Delta_{N}(\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}).

By (
4.17
), this definition does not depend on the choice of
N\geq d
. However, note that some of the
\Delta_{n}(\text{\boldmath$\lambda$})
’s might be zero. Define

{\mathscr{P}}_{+}^{X}(n,d):=\{\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}\in\Lambda_{+}^{I}(N,d)\mid\operatorname{Std}^{X}_{n}(\text{\boldmath$\lambda$})\neq\varnothing\}.

Note that
{\mathscr{P}}_{+}^{X}(n,d)
does not depend on the choice of
N\geq d
. Moreover,

\Lambda_{+}^{I}(n,d)\subseteq{\mathscr{P}}_{+}^{X}(n,d)\subseteq\Lambda_{+}^{I}(N,d),

with containments being equalities when
n\geq d
. By (
4.16
), we have:

Lemma 4.18
.

Let
N\geq d>n
and
\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}\in\Lambda_{+}^{I}(N,d)
. Then
\Delta_{n}(\text{\boldmath$\lambda$})\neq 0
if and only if
\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}\in{\mathscr{P}}_{+}^{X}(n,d)
.

The story for the costandard modules
\nabla_{n}(\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}):=\eta^{N}_{n}(d)\nabla_{N}(\text{\boldmath$\lambda$})
is entirely similar, the non-zero ones being labeled by
{\mathscr{P}}_{+}^{Y}(n,d):=\{\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}\in\Lambda_{+}^{I}(N,d)\mid\operatorname{Std}^{Y}_{n}(\text{\boldmath$\lambda$})\neq\varnothing\}.

Theorem 4.19
.

Let
\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}\in{\mathscr{P}}_{+}^{X}(n,d)
and
\text{\boldmath$\mu$}\in{\mathscr{P}}_{+}^{X}(n,c)
. Then the
T(n,d+c)
-module
\Delta_{n}(\text{\boldmath$\lambda$})\otimes\Delta_{n}(\text{\boldmath$\mu$})
has a filtration with factors of the form
\Delta_{n}(\text{\boldmath$\nu$})
with
\text{\boldmath$\nu$}\in{\mathscr{P}}_{+}^{X}(n,d+c)
. Similarly for
\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}\in{\mathscr{P}}_{+}^{Y}(n,d)
and
\text{\boldmath$\mu$}\in{\mathscr{P}}_{+}^{Y}(n,c)
, the
T(n,d+c)
-module
\nabla_{n}(\text{\boldmath$\lambda$})\otimes\nabla_{n}(\text{\boldmath$\mu$})
has a filtration with factors of the form
\nabla_{n}(\text{\boldmath$\nu$})
with
\text{\boldmath$\nu$}\in{\mathscr{P}}_{+}^{Y}(n,d+c)
.

Proof.

We prove the result for the
\Delta
’s, the proof for
\nabla
’s being similar.
Choose
N\geq d+c
. By Corollary
4.13
,
\Delta_{N}(\text{\boldmath$\lambda$})\otimes\Delta_{N}(\text{\boldmath$\mu$})
has a filtration with factors of the form
\Delta_{N}(\text{\boldmath$\nu$})
with
\text{\boldmath$\nu$}\in\Lambda_{+}^{I}(N,d+c)
. Applying the exact functor

T(N,d+c)\!\operatorname{-mod}\to T(n,d+c)\!\operatorname{-mod},\ V\mapsto\eta^{N}_{n}(d)V

to this filtration and using Lemma
3.21
, we get the required result.
∎

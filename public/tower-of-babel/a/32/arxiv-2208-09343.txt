Free field realisation of the chiral universal centraliser
Christopher Beem and Sujay Nair

Abstract
In the TQFT formalism of Moore–Tachikawa for describing Higgs branches of theories of class 𝒮 \mathcal{S} , the space associated to the unpunctured sphere in type 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} is the universal centraliser ℨ G \mathfrak{Z}_{G} , where 𝔤 = L ​ i ​ e ​ ( G ) \mathfrak{g}=Lie(G) . In more physical terms, this space arises as the Coulomb branch of pure 𝒩 = 4 \mathcal{N}=4 gauge theory in three dimensions with gauge group G ˇ \check{G} , the Langlands dual. In the analogous formalism for describing chiral algebras of class 𝒮 \mathcal{S} , the vertex algebra associated to the sphere has been dubbed the chiral universal centraliser . In this paper, we construct an open, symplectic embedding from a cover of the Kostant–Toda lattice of type 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} to the universal centraliser of G G —extending a classic result of Kostant. Using this embedding and some observations on the Poisson algebraic structure of ℨ G \mathfrak{Z}_{G} , we propose a free field realisation of the chiral universal centraliser for any simple group G G . We exploit this realisation to develop free field realisations of chiral algebras of class 𝒮 \mathcal{S} of type 𝔞 1 \mathfrak{a}_{1} for theories of genus zero with n = 1 , … , 6 n=1,\ldots,6 punctures. These realisations make generalised S S -duality completely manifest, and the generalisation to n ⩾ 7 n\geqslant 7 punctures is conceptually clear, though technically burdensome.

Introduction

The occurrence of vertex operator algebra (VOA) structures within the operator algebras of supersymmetric quantum field theories in various dimensions (see,
e.g.
,

;

;

;

Furthermore, physical intuition can potentially provide new insights into the study of VOAs arising through these correspondences at a purely formal level, and indeed developments in this area have led to a rich dialogue between physics and pure mathematics (see,
e.g.
,
Arakawa:2018egx
;
Creutzig:2017qyf
;
Creutzig:2018lbc
;
Costello:2018swh
;
Xie:2019yds
;
Xie:2019vzr
;
Creutzig:2021ext
;
Beem:2022mde
for an extremeley incomplete selection).

A surprising property of many VOAs arising in a physical context is their
geometric
nature. This is perhaps most clearly exemplified through the Higgs Branch Conjecture of
Beem:2017ooy
, which implies that the VOAs arising in connection with four-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=2
SCFTs are
chiral quantisations
of their Higgs branches (
cf.

Arakawa:2012c2
). A related, but less well formalised, idea is that the VOAs arising in this setting admit certain (generalised) free field realisations (FFRs) the structure of which is closely connected with the Poisson geometry of the Higgs branch
Beem:2019tfp
;
Beem:2019snk
;
Beem:2021jnm
. Physically, these free field realisations should be thought of as encoding the operator algebra of an SCFT in terms of degrees of freedom that survive in the effective field theory on the moduli space after (partial) Higgsing. This intuition (and the examples that bear it out) has thus far been primarily restricted to the setting of VOAs associated to four-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=2
SCFTs, but it seems likely that the principle holds somewhat more generally.

In this paper, we will be investigating the application of this free field philosophy to a family of VOAs that are exotic from a four-dimensional perspective, but potentially more conventional in the three-dimensional setting. These are the
chiral universal centralisers
defined in
Arakawa:2018egx
, which were previously identified as the VOAs associated to the
sphere
in the setting of chiral algebras of class
\mathcal{S}

Beem:2014rza
. The associated variety of this VOA is the
universal centraliser

\mathfrak{Z}_{G}
, the definition of which we recall in Section
2
—this is the holomorphic-symplectic variety associated to the sphere by the Moore–Tachikawa topological field theory for class
\mathcal{S}
Higgs branches
Moore:2011ee
. While in the context of class
\mathcal{S}
the sphere is a somewhat pathological case, the universal centraliser appears in a more physically sensible way as the Coulomb branch of pure
\mathcal{N}=4
gauge theory in three dimensions. Thus the chiral quantisation of this space might naturally be expected to appear as a boundary VOA in the sense of
Costello:2019
for pure gauge theory.

Our approach to constructing a candidate free field realisation for the chiral universal centraliser will follow the general theme of
Beem:2019tfp
. We start with a detailed understanding of the Poisson geometry of the universal centraliser, an introduction to which we provice in Section
2
. In Section
3
, we give an explicit characterisation of a map,
\varphi
, from the Kostant–Toda lattice to a Zariski-open subset of
\mathfrak{Z}_{G}
. Such a map was known to Kostant for the case when
G
is of adjoint type, and in Theorem
3.3
we construct a generalisation of
(
Kostant:1979195
, Theorem 2.6)
for any semi-simple
G
. This gives rise to Propositions
3.6
and
3.7
, in which we show that
\varphi
is an open immersion and symplectic.

Altogether, this gives a finite-dimensional analogue of a free field realisation for the Poisson algebra of functions
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{Z}_{G}]
. In Section
4
we propose a chiralisation of this construction in terms of a lattice vertex algebra that we call the chiral Kostant–Toda lattice. In particular, we define a sub-VOA of this lattice VOA (by specifying a weak generating set) that we conjecture to match the chiral universal centraliser as defined by Arakawa. In defining this sub-VOA, we encounter an (apparently novel) generalisation of the Miura transform, and in Remark
4.1
we explain how that the usual Miura transform—realising principal
{\cal W}
-algebras in products of Heisenberg vertex algebras—can be generalised to a family of such embeddings based on the lower central series of the upper nilpotent subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
.

We verify that our construction reproduces the chiral universal centraliser (defined
via
Drinfel’d–Sokolov reduction) explicity for the case
G={\rm SL}_{2}
, and we also study the
G={\rm SL}_{3}
case in detail and confirm that the VOA according to our prescription is indeed a chiral quantisation of universal centraliser. Here a direct comparison to the definition in terms of DS reduction would require a detailed analysis of said reduction, and this has not appeared in the literature to date so far as the authors are aware. Some interesting structure regarding gradings and simplicity of these VOAs become apparent in our examples, and we elaborate on this in Section
4.4
, where we define a “more universal” centraliser that returns the universal centraliser by central quotient.

An additional benefit of our free field construction is that for chiral algebras of class
\mathcal{S}
corresponding to physical theories (so with three or more punctures), the sphere free field realisation can act as the starting point for free field realisations that treat all punctures on equal footing (and so manifest generalised
S
-duality at the level of FFRs). In Section
5

\mathfrak{a}_{1}
theories with various numbers of punctures. We give free field realisations for these vertex algebras that incorporate the sphere FFR, which leads to a very uniform construction. Analogous realisations for other class
\mathcal{S}
families should arise in much the same way. We conclude in Section
6

2.2

{\rm SL}_{N}

2
The universal centraliser

The universal centraliser admits several equivalent definitions, some of which arise naturally by way of three- and four-dimensional physics. In this section we will review these definitions and some important properties of the universal centraliser, and also establish notation for what follows.

2.1
Background and definitions

Let
G
be a simple algebraic Lie group and
\mathfrak{g}=\mathrm{Lie}(G)
. Let
\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
be the linear dual of
\mathfrak{g}
, and using the Killing form
(\cdot,\cdot)
one may identify
\mathfrak{g}
with
\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
via the isomorphism
x\mapsto(x,\cdot)
for
x\in\mathfrak{g}
.

Furthermore, let
B\subset G
be a Borel subgroup with splitting
B=N\rtimes T
for a maximal torus
T
and unipotent group
N
. The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
admits a Cartan decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{n_{+}}\oplus\mathfrak{t}\oplus\mathfrak{n_{-}}
, where
\mathfrak{t}=\mathrm{Lie}~T
and
\mathfrak{n}_{+}=\mathrm{Lie}~N
. (For future reference, we also denote by
N_{-}
the lower unipotent group with
\mathfrak{n}_{-}=\mathrm{Lie}~N_{-}
.)

We denote adjoint and coadjoint actions of
G
on
\mathfrak{g}
by
\mathrm{Ad}
and
\mathrm{Ad}^{\ast}
, respectively. Similarly,
\mathrm{ad}
and
\mathrm{ad}^{\ast}
denote the respective
\mathfrak{g}
-actions. The stabiliser,
G_{x}
, of an element
x\in\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
is the subgroup of
G
that leaves
x
invariant under the coadjoint action,

G_{x}=\{g\in G|\,\mathrm{Ad}^{\ast}_{g}\,x=x\}~.

(1)

The Zariski open subset
\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}_{\rm reg}\subset\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
is defined to be the set of all elements whose centralisers have dimension equal to the rank of
\mathfrak{g}
. This is the complement of the union of non-principal nilpotent orbits in
\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
, or alternatively, the union of all non-nilpotent orbits along with the principal nilpotent orbit in
\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
.

Let
T^{\ast}G
be the total space of the cotangent bundle of
G
; one has the trivialisation
T^{\ast}G\cong\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}\times G
using the realisation of
\mathfrak{g}
by left-invariant vector fields on
G
. On the cotangent bundle
T^{\ast}G
there are two commuting, Hamiltonian
G
actions, with moment maps
\mu_{L}
and
\mu_{R}
given by

\mu_{L}(g,x)=x~,\qquad\mu_{R}(g,x)=\mathrm{Ad}_{g}^{*}\,x~,

(2)

for
(g,x)\in G\times\mathfrak{g}^{*}\cong T^{\ast}G
. Let
\mu_{\rm diag}=\mu_{L}-\mu_{R}
be the moment map for the diagonal
G
action. The universal centraliser is then defined as the following diagonal Hamiltonian reduction
Bezrukavnikov:2003
,​

\mathfrak{Z}_{G}

\mathfrak{Z}_{G}=\big(\mu_{\rm diag}^{-1}(0)\cap(\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}_{\rm reg}\times G)\big)/\!\!/G_{\rm diag}~.

(3)

This definition gives the universal centraliser the structure of a Poisson variety (and in fact a symplectic variety). Furthermore,
\mathfrak{Z}_{G}
is naturally a scheme over the orbit space
\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}_{\rm reg}/\!\!/G\cong\mathfrak{g}^{*}/\!\!/G
. We let
(P_{i})_{i=1}^{\mathrm{rk}~\mathfrak{g}}
denote the generators of the
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}]^{G}
(the

) corresponding to the fundamental invariants of
\mathfrak{g}
.

In terms of class
{\cal S}

\mathfrak{Z}_{G}
can be formally identified as the Higgs branch associated to an unpunctured sphere. While there is no physical SCFT in four-dimensions for which this is the Higgs branch, one can still interpret such an object in terms of,
e.g.
, the Moore--Tachikawa TQFT.​

Or alternatively, in terms of compactification of the
(2,0)
theory on a Riemann surface of finite size, as in
Gaiotto:2011xs

In that setting, the operation of closing full punctures on the UV curve is implemented by performing Kostant–Whittaker (KW) reduction on the associated symplectic variety with respect to the Hamiltonian
G
-actions associated to the closed punctures. The sphere can then be obtained, for instance, by closing two punctures on the cylinder, which itself is associated to
T^{\ast}G
. This leads to another definition of
\mathfrak{Z}_{G}
as the two-sided KW reduction of the cotangent bundle
T^{*}G
, as seen in
Ginzburg:2018Nil
. This perspective will also have the most natural link to the chiralisation of
\mathfrak{Z}_{G}
.

Let us make this characterisation more concrete. Let
\Delta
denote the set of (positive) simple roots of
\mathfrak{g}
, and let

f=\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta}e_{-\alpha}~,

(4)

be the sum of the negative simple roots. By the Jacobson–Morozov theorem, such an
f
can be completed to an
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple
(e,h,f)
. Let
\chi\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
be the (Killing) dual of
f
.

The moment maps on
T^{*}G
can be restricted to
\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{*}
-valued moment maps by means of the composition
\bar{\mu}_{L,R}:T^{*}G\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{*}\twoheadrightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{*}/\mathfrak{b}_{+}^{*}\cong\mathfrak{n}^{*}_{-}
, where
\mathfrak{b}_{+}=Lie(B)
is our Borel subalgebra. The maximal (upper) unipotent subgroups of the left and right
G
-actions are denoted by
N_{L}
and
N_{R}
, respectively. The KW realisation of the universal centraliser then takes the following form,

\mathfrak{Z}_{G}\cong T^{*}G\overset{~\chi}{\,/\!\!/\,}(N_{L}\times N_{R})=(\bar{\mu}^{-1}_{L}(\chi)\cap\bar{\mu}^{-1}_{R}(\chi))/\!\!/(N_{L}\times N_{R})~,

(5)

which again encodes
\mathfrak{Z}_{G}
as a symplectic variety.

Now let
\mathfrak{g}^{e}\subset\mathfrak{g}
be the
\mathrm{ad}_{e}
-invariant subspace. The
Slodowy slice

S_{f}\subset\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}_{\rm reg}\subset\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
is the image of
f+\mathfrak{g}^{e}
under the Killing isomorphism, or more concretely,

S_{f}=\chi_{f}+\{(x,\cdot)\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}|\,x\in\mathfrak{g}^{e}\}~.

(6)

All
G
-orbits in
\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}_{\rm reg}
intersect
S_{f}
transversally

\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}_{\rm reg}=G\,\cdot S_{f}~.

(7)

This structure leads to Kostant’s isomorphism
\mathfrak{g}^{*}\,/\!\!/\,G\cong S_{f}
. At the level of varieties (schemes), performing KW reduction on a Hamiltonian
G
-space
X
(with respect to the principal nilpotent conjugacy class) is equivalent to taking the fibre-product of
X
with
S_{f}
over
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, see
Ginzburg:2008HC
. We can perform two-sided KW reduction in stages, and after taking just the reduction by the left action we have

T^{*}G\overset{\chi}{\,/\!\!/\,}N_{L}\cong T^{*}G\!\!\!\underset{\mu_{L},\mathfrak{g}^{*}}{\times}\!\!\!S_{f}=G\times S_{f}~.

(8)

The variety
G\times S_{f}
(with the symplectic structure induced by KW reduction) is also known as the equivariant Slodowy slice

;
Losev2
. This inherits the right-action moment map, and performing the second reduction then gives

\begin{split}\mathfrak{Z}_{G}\cong(G\times S_{f})\overset{\chi}{\,/\!\!/\,}N_{R}&\cong(G\times S_{f})\underset{\mu_{R},\mathfrak{g}^{*}}{\times}S_{f}\\
&\cong\{(g,s)\in G\times S_{f}\,|\,\mathrm{Ad}^{*}_{g}s\in S_{f}\}~.\end{split}

(9)

This gives a definition of
\mathfrak{Z}_{G}
as the group scheme centralising points in
S_{f}
, so in particular as a group scheme over
S_{f}\cong\mathbb{C}^{\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}}
.
S_{f}
can then be thought of as the base of an integrable system on
\mathfrak{Z}_{G}
with action variables given by the (restriction to
S_{f}
of the) generators of the Harish-Chandra centre.

The universal centraliser of type

For completeness, we recall this definition here. Let
\check{G}({\cal O})={\rm Hom}(\mathbb{D},G)

\check{G}({\cal K})={\rm Hom}(\mathbb{D}^{\times},G)

\mathfrak{Z}_{G}\cong\mathrm{Spec}~H_{\bullet}^{\check{G}({\cal O})}(\mathrm{Gr}_{\check{G}})~.

2.2
Examples in rank one

To make these spaces a bit more tangible, we consider in detail the two rank-one examples:
G={\rm SL}_{2}
and
G={\rm PGL}_{2}
.

By the Killing isomorphism,
S_{f}
is identified with the elements of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
of the form

\begin{pmatrix}\,0\,&\,S\,\\
\,1\,&\,0\,\end{pmatrix}~,\quad S\in\mathbb{C}~.

(12)

The most general element of
{\rm SL}_{2}
that commutes with such a matrix is of the form

\sqrt{-1}\begin{pmatrix}Y&SX\\
X&Y\end{pmatrix}~,\quad X,Y,S\in\mathbb{C}~,

(13)

where the overall factor of
\sqrt{-1}
is purely conventional. Therefore,
\mathfrak{Z}_{{\rm SL}_{2}}\subset\mathbb{A}^{3}
can be realised as an affine variety with coordinate ring

I_{{\rm SL}_{2}}=\frac{\mathbb{C}[X,Y,S]}{\langle SX^{2}-Y^{2}-1\rangle}~,

(14)

which is the presentation appearing in
Bezrukavnikov:2003

The Poisson bracket can be computed by carrying out the two sided KW reduction of
T^{*}{{\rm SL}_{2}}
.​

\{S,X\}=Y~,\quad\{S,Y\}=SX~,\quad\{Y,X\}=\frac{1}{2}X^{2}~,

(15)

I_{{\rm SL}_{2}}
is generated by just
S
and

{\rm SL}_{N}

Now for the adjoint case

2.3
Example in rank two

It will be useful in what follows to also have an example in rank two at our disposal. Here we choose the universal centraliser for
G={\rm SL}_{3}

2.4
Poisson algebraic structure for
{\rm SL}_{N}

In this section, we will codify some patterns we have observed in the examples of
{\rm SL}_{2}
and
{\rm SL}_{3}
. We generalise these to statements about
{\rm SL}_{N}
and discuss their further generalisation to arbitrary simple algebraic Lie groups.

We realise
{\rm SL}_{N}
as the closed subgroup of
{\rm GL}_{N}
satisfying the unit determinant condition. Let
(g_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{N}
be the linear coordinates on
{\rm GL}_{N}
that are conjugate to the matrix entries. These restrict to linear coordinates functions on
{\rm SL}_{N}
.

In both of the examples
{\rm SL}_{2}
and
{\rm SL}_{3}
, the ring
I_{G}
was generated by the Slodowy slice generators and the matrix entries in the bottom row of the group element. This observation generalises as follows.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
s\in S_{f}
. An element,
g
, of
\,{\rm SL}_{N}
that stabilises
s
is completely fixed by specifying the matrix entries of the bottom row of
g
.

In other words, if
g_{ij}
are the coordinate functions conjugate to the matrix entries, then
I_{{\rm SL}_{N}}
is generated, as a ring, by the restrictions of the fundamental invariants to
S_{f}
,
(P_{i})_{i=1}^{N}
, and by
(g_{Ni})_{i=1}^{N}
.

Proof.

Let
g\in{\rm SL}_{N}
and
s\in S_{f}
, then the condition
\mathrm{Ad}^{\ast}_{g}s=s
is equivalent to requiring
[g,s]=0
, where we think of
g
as a matrix of
\mathfrak{gl}_{N}
.

The kernel of
[-,s]
in
\mathfrak{gl}_{N}
is
N
-dimensional since
S_{f}\subset\mathfrak{gl}_{N}
is regular. The proposition will follow if we can show that there is a basis,
(v_{i})_{i=1}^{N}
, of
\mathrm{ker}~[-,s]
consisting of matrices
v_{i}
such that
(v_{i})_{Nj}=\delta_{ij}
. In words,
v_{i}
is an element of
\mathrm{ker}~[-,s]
where the
i
th entry along the bottom row is equal to one and the rest of the bottom row entries vanish.

We can explicitly construct such a basis. First, let
v_{N}=\mathbbm{1}
,
i.e.
, the identity matrix. We also fix
v_{N-1}=s
. It is clear that both
v_{N-1}
and
v_{N}
commute with
s
and satisfy the required conditions on the bottom row.

Now consider the matrix product,
s^{k}
, the entries
(s^{k})_{Ni}
are zero for
i<N-k
and for
i=N-K+1
. The entry
(s^{k})_{N,N-k}=1
and the entries
s^{k}_{Ni}
for
k>N-k
are generically nonzero. By induction, we can take linear combinations,

s^{k}+\sum_{j=0}^{k-2}c_{j}s^{j}~,

(26)

for
c_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
to set all the bottom entries to zero, except the
(N,N-k)
entry. We set
v_{N-k}
equal to this linear combination.

This provides a linearly independent set
(v_{i})_{i=1}^{N}
which all lie in the kernel of
[-,s]
, since
s
commutes with all powers
s^{k}
. Therefore the
v_{i}
form a basis of
\mathrm{ker}~[-,s]
and we are done.
∎

Later on this Proposition will yield a significant simplification in the procedure for computing the ring
I_{G}
in terms of Kostant–Toda lattice generators. There is also another simplification to be had.

Theorem 2.2
.

The Poisson algebra
I_{{\rm SL}_{N}}
is Poisson generated by
(P_{i})_{i=1}^{\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}}
and the generator
X=g_{N1}
.

Proof.

The proof of this theorem relies upon machinery that will be introduced in later sections. As such we relegate it to Appendix
A
.
∎

We expect a generalisation of these results to any simple algebraic Lie group
G
to hold as well. To justify this, let us characterise this preferred element
X
in more representation theoretic terms. By the Peter–Weyl decomposition, the coordinate ring of
G
decomposes into
G-G
bi-modules according to

\mathbb{C}[G]\cong\bigoplus_{\lambda\in P^{+}}V_{\lambda}\otimes V_{\lambda}^{*}~,

(27)

where
P^{+}
is the set of integral dominant weights of
\mathfrak{g}
,
V_{\lambda}
is the finite-dimensional representation of
\mathfrak{g}
with highest weight
\lambda
and
V_{\lambda}^{*}
is the contragredient dual representation. The vector space of functions on
T^{*}G\cong\mathfrak{g}^{*}\times G
therefore takes the form

\mathbb{C}[T^{*}G]=\mathrm{Sym}~\mathfrak{g}~\otimes\bigoplus_{\lambda\in P^{+}}V_{\lambda}\otimes V_{\lambda^{*}}~.

(28)

Let
V_{F}
be the lowest dimensional faithful representation of
G
. If
G
is not of type
D_{2n}
then
V_{F}
is irreducible, so let us make this assumption for the moment. Since
V_{F}
is faithful, every irreducible representation
V_{\lambda}
will appear in the decomposition of the tensor powers of
V_{F}
. In light of the Peter-Weyl decomposition, this means that one can choose as generators of
\mathbb{C}[G]
the functions transforming in the representation
V_{F}\otimes V_{F}^{*}
in (
27
). For example, if
G={\rm SL}_{n}
then
V_{F}
is the defining representation. One usually picks the linear coordinates
\{g_{ij}\}
—conjugate to the matrix entries—to be generators of
\mathbb{C}[{\rm SL}_{n}]
, and indeed these constitute precisely the term
V_{F}\otimes V_{F}^{*}
in (
27
).

Let
v_{F}\in V_{F}
be the highest-weight vector, then
V_{F}
is generated, as a
\mathfrak{g}
-module by
v_{F}
. Now, suppose
\widetilde{X}
is the function in
\mathbb{C}[G]\subset\mathbb{C}[T^{*}G]
transforming in the highest weight state
v_{F}\otimes v_{F}^{*}
. Then
\widetilde{X}
is not in the ideal generated by
\overline{\mu}_{L}
and
\overline{\mu}_{R}
, and moreover it is
N_{L}\times N_{R}
invariant—since it is the highest-weight state. As a result,
\widetilde{X}
will descend unmodified to the two-sided KW reduction of
T^{*}G
. For
{\rm SL}_{N}
,
\widetilde{X}
is the linear coordinate function conjugate to the matrix entry
g_{N1}
, and after KW reduction it will become the function
X
introduced in Theorem
2.2
.

The bottom row
(g_{Ni})_{i=1}^{N}
that algebraically generate (along with the fundamental invariants)
I_{\rm SL_{N}}
are identified with the vector subspace
v_{F}\otimes V_{F}^{*}
of
V_{F}\otimes V_{F}^{*}
. This provides a more abstract characterisation of the algebraic generators of
I_{\rm SL_{N}}
. For general
G
these functions are
N_{L}
invariant and can be made
N_{R}
invariant by corrections that are linear in the generators of
v_{f}\otimes V_{F}^{*}
. We should therefore expect a correspondence between (a subset) of the algebraic generators of
I_{G}
and the weight vectors of
v_{F}\otimes V_{F}^{*}
.

The Poisson algebra
\mathbb{C}[T^{*}G]
has two Poisson-commuting subalgebras, each isomorphic to
\mathrm{Sym}~\mathfrak{g}
, arising from the moment maps for the left and right
G
-actions. The subspace
V_{F}\otimes V_{F}^{*}\subset\mathbb{C}[T^{*}G]
is generated by the (Poisson) action of the left and right moment maps on
\widetilde{X}
, and since
V_{F}\otimes V_{F}^{*}
generates
\mathbb{C}[G]
, we can choose
\widetilde{X}
and the generators of the left and right moment map as Poisson generators of
\mathbb{C}[T^{*}G]
. After two-sided KW reduction, what remains of the moment maps are the generators of
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}^{*}]^{G}\cong\mathbb{C}[S_{f}]
. It seems reasonable, then, to conjecture that
X
and
\mathbb{C}[S_{f}]
, which are in an appropriate sense the reductions of the Poisson generators of
\mathbb{C}[T^{*}G]
, will be Poisson generators of the reduced algebra.

Let us now return to the case where
G
is of type
D_{2N}
. If
G
is not simply connected, then
Z(G)
is cyclic and our previous description still holds and
V_{F}
is irreducible. If
G
is of type
D_{2N}
and simply connected then
V_{F}
is not irreducible—it is the Dirac spinor,
V_{\rm s}\oplus V_{\rm c}
, where
V_{\rm s}
and
V_{\rm c}
are the smallest spin representations of
\rm Spin(N)
. In this case, we must pick two such highest-weight states,
X_{s}
and
X_{c}
in representations
V_{\rm s}\otimes V_{\rm s}^{*}
and
V_{\rm c}\otimes V_{\rm c}^{*}
respectively. Similarly, the
N_{L}
invariant subspace is now
(v_{\rm s}\otimes V_{\rm s}^{*})\oplus(v_{\rm c}\otimes V_{\rm c}^{*})
, where
v_{s}
and
v_{c}
are highest weight states of
V_{\rm s}
and
V_{\rm c}
respectively.

Putting this all together, we have the following conjectures on the algebraic and Poisson algebraic structures of
I_{G}
.

Conjecture 2.3
.

If
G
is not simply connected, or if
G
is not of type
D_{2N}
, then
I_{G}
is generated, as a ring, by the fundamental invariants
(P_{i})_{i=1}^{\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}}
and generators
(g_{i})_{i=1}^{{\rm dim}\,V_{F}}
which are in one-to-one correspondence with the weight vectors of
V_{F}^{*}
.

If
G
is simply connected and of type
D_{2N}
,
I_{G}
is generated, as a ring, by the fundamental invariants
(P_{i})_{i=1}^{\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}}
and two sets of generators
(g_{{\rm s}i})_{i=1}^{{\rm dim}\,V_{\rm s}}
, and
(g_{{\rm c}i})_{i=1}^{{\rm dim}\,V_{\rm c}}
. The generators
g_{{\rm s}i}
(repectively
g_{{\rm c}i}
) are in one-to-one correspondence with the weight vectors of
V_{\rm s}^{*}
(repectively
V_{\rm c}^{*}
).

Conjecture 2.4
.

If
G
is not simply connected, or if
G
is not of type
D_{2N}
, then
I_{G}
is Poisson generated by the fundamental invariants
\big(P_{i}\big)_{i=1}^{\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}}
and
X
.

If
G
is simply connected and of type
D_{2N}
, i.e., if
G=\mathrm{Spin}(2N)
, then
I_{G}
is Poisson generated by the fundamental invariants
\big(P_{i}\big)_{i=1}^{\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}}
,
X_{s}
, and
X_{c}
.

3
The universal centraliser and the Kostant–Toda lattice

The geometric structure that apparently underlies the free field realisations in
Beem:2019tfp
is the existence of some particularly simple Zariski open subset(s)
U
of the Higgs branch
X
in terms of which the global functions on
X
can be realised via the inclusion
\mathbb{C}[X]\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}[U]
. These subsets are generally identified (including holomorphic symplectic structure) with cotangent bundles of the form
(T^{\ast}\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{n}\times(T^{\ast}\mathbb{C})^{m}
with canonical Poisson structures,​
5
5

5

As in
Beem:2019tfp
, we take our standard Poisson structure on
T^{\ast}\mathbb{C}^{\times}
to be
\{p,e\}=e
where
p
is the fibre coordinate and
e
is the base coordinate.
so these can be thought of as Darboux (Zariski) neighbourhoods in
X
. In practice, the expressions for the images of generators of
\mathbb{C}[X]
in terms of
\mathbb{C}[U]
can often be inferred using symmetry data, such as weights under the contracting
\mathbb{C}^{\ast}
action on
X
and the action of additional global symmetries. Such “finite-dimensional free field realisations” form the foundation for a chiral analogue, where the associated VOA is realised in terms of
\beta\gamma
systems and isotropic lattice bosons that naturally chiralise
\mathbb{C}[U]
. (We will say more about these isotropic lattice VOAs in Section
4
.)

In previous examples in the literature, the open set
U
has been defined with reference to a (partial) Higgsing of some global symmetry acting on
X
. However, it is not at all clear that there is anything fundamental about this particular origin for such Darboux neighborhoods, and a more general story may certainly exist. Indeed, for the case of the universal centraliser, there are no global symmetries to speak of, so a new starting point is needed.

Fortunately, it has been known since work of Kostant (see
Kostant:1979195
) that the integrable system of the Kostant–Toda lattice of type
\mathfrak{g}
and the universal centraliser
\mathfrak{Z}_{G}
(for
G
of adjoint type) are intimately connected, with the former having precisely the Darboux structure needed. In particular, the level sets of the Kostant–Toda lattice (as an integrable system) can be identified with (open) subsets of stabilisers of elements in
\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}_{\rm reg}
. Recently, Crooks has provided a complex-analytic embedding of an open dense subset of the Kostant–Toda lattice inside the universal centraliser
Crooks:2020
, still with the caveat that
G
be of adjoint type.

In this section, we will adapt this construction to the algebraic setting and extend the domain of the morphism to the full Kostant–Toda lattice. Generalising further to allow for any simple
G
(including,
e.g.
, both adjoint and simply-connected forms of the group) will require us to replace the Kostant–Toda lattice with a certain covering space that we define. Our main result is the construction of an open immersion from the (cover of the) Kostant–Toda lattice to the universal centraliser of
G
, and thus a finite-dimensional free field realisation of the universal centraliser.

3.1
The Kostant–Toda lattice

Consider a dynamical system of
N
particles on a line, with Hamiltonian

\mathcal{H}_{KT}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{p_{i}^{2}}{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{l}e^{\psi_{i}(\mathbf{q})}~,

(29)

where
\psi_{i}(\mathbf{q})
are linear functions of the canonical position coordinates
\mathbf{q}=(q_{1},q_{2},\dots,q_{N})
. There is a natural inner product on the vector space spanned by the
\psi_{i}

Kostant:1978
. When this inner product space agrees with the root system of some real Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{R}}
endowed with the Killing form, the
\psi_{i}
define a set of roots for
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{R}}
, and in this case the representation theory of
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{R}}
can be leveraged to integrate Hamilton’s equations. Indeed, the system is integrable in the classical sense.

The complexified phase space
\mathrm{KT}_{\mathfrak{g}}
of the Kostant–Toda Lattice of type
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{R}}
can be identified (adopting the notation of the previous section) with an affine subspace of
\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}^{\ast}
,

\mathrm{KT}_{\mathfrak{g}}=\chi+\mathfrak{t}^{*}+\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta}\mathfrak{g}^{*}_{\alpha}\backslash\{0\}~.

(30)

Let us denote by
(\alpha_{i})_{i=1}^{\mathrm{rk}~\mathfrak{g}}
the simple roots of
\mathfrak{g}
, by
e_{\alpha_{i}}
the corresponding simple root elements of
\mathfrak{n}_{+}
, and by
h_{i}\in\mathfrak{t}
the corresponding Cartan elements. Let
h_{i}^{*}
and
e_{\alpha_{i}}^{*}
be the Killing duals to
h_{i}
and
e_{\alpha_{i}}
, respectively. An element
y\in\mathrm{KT}_{\mathfrak{g}}
is of the form

y=\chi+\sum_{i=1}^{\mathrm{rk}~\mathfrak{g}}b_{i}(y)h_{i}^{*}+\sum_{i=1}^{\mathrm{rk}~\mathfrak{g}}\gamma_{i}(y)e_{\alpha_{i}}^{*}~,

(31)

so
b_{i}:\mathrm{KT}_{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
and
\gamma_{i}:\mathrm{KT}_{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
furnish a basis of linear coordinates. The coordinate ring is then simply

\mathbb{C}[\mathrm{KT}_{\mathfrak{g}}]\cong\mathbb{C}[b_{i},\gamma^{\pm 1}_{i}\,|\,i=1,\dots,\mathrm{rk}~\mathfrak{g}]~.

(32)

The Poisson structure descends from the Kirillov–Kostant–Souriau bracket on
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}^{*}]\cong\mathrm{Sym}\,\mathfrak{g}
(see,
e.g.
,
Kostant:1979195
), and we have

\{b_{i},\gamma_{j}^{\pm}\}=\delta_{ij}\gamma_{i}^{\pm}~,

(33)

with all other brackets vanishing.​
6
6

6

Note that the
b_{i}
,
\gamma_{i}
are
linear
duals of
h_{i}^{*}
and
e_{\alpha_{i}}^{*}
, respectively. That is
b_{i}(h^{\ast}_{j})=\delta_{ij}
and
\gamma_{i}(e^{\ast}_{\alpha_{j}})=\delta_{ij}
, so in particular
b_{i}\neq h_{i}
under the canonical identification
\mathfrak{g}=(\mathfrak{g}^{\ast})^{\ast}
.
Thus we identify
\mathrm{KT}_{\mathfrak{g}}\cong T^{*}(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{\mathrm{rk}~\mathfrak{g}}
as symplectic varieties. The structure of a complex integrable system on
\mathrm{KT}_{\mathfrak{g}}
is then manifested through the action variables
P_{i}|_{\mathrm{KT}_{\mathfrak{g}}}
for
i=1,2,\dots,\mathrm{rk}~\mathfrak{g}
, which are the restrictions of the generators of the Harish-Chandra centre,
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}^{*}]^{G}
to
\mathrm{KT}_{\mathfrak{g}}
.

In the construction of
Ginzburg:2018
—and its chiralisation in
Arakawa:2018egx
—the structure of Higgs branches of class
{\cal S}
as
S_{f}
-schemes plays an integral role. Here, the structure morphism of
\mathrm{KT}_{\mathfrak{g}}
as an
S_{f}
-scheme is the composition

\begin{split}\mathrm{KT}_{\mathfrak{g}}&\xrightarrow{P}\mathfrak{g}^{*}/\!\!/G\xrightarrow{\sim}S_{f}~,\\
y&\mapsto(P_{1}(y),P_{2}(y),\dots P_{\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}}(y))~,\end{split}

(34)

where the second morphism is Kostant’s isomorphism
\mathfrak{g}^{*}/\!\!/G\cong S_{f}
. This structure morphism is thus exactly the map to the base of the aforementioned complex integrable system, up to the identification of the base with
S_{f}
.

We have deliberately labelled the Kostant–Toda lattice with the Lie algebra and not the group, whereas the universal centraliser depends on the choice of group
G
. In
Kostant:1979195
;
Crooks:2020
, the embedding from this lattice targets the universal centraliser of the
adjoint
group. However, for the purposes of class
{\cal S}
constructions we expect to be mainly interested in the simply connected group
G
with
\mathrm{Lie}~G=\mathfrak{g}
. To generalise the embedding appropriately, we then need to replace
\mathrm{KT}_{\mathfrak{g}}
with a suitable cover.

The root lattice of the Lie
group

G
always contains the root lattice of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
as a sublattice. Let
\Delta_{G}
denote the simple roots of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
embedded into the root lattice of the Lie group
G
. Such a simple root,
\alpha\in\Delta_{G}
, is then a character
\alpha:T\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
, defined by its action on the simple root generator
e_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{g}
as

\mathrm{Ad}_{t}e_{\alpha}=\alpha(t)\,e_{\alpha}~,\qquad\forall t\in T~.

(35)

Let
Z_{G}:T\rightarrow\big(\mathbb{C}^{\times}\big)^{\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}}
be defined by

\begin{split}t\mapsto(\alpha(t))_{\alpha\in\Delta_{G}}~,\qquad\forall t\in T~,\\
\end{split}

(36)

This map is an isomorphism for adjoint
G
, but if
G
has a nontrivial centre,
Z(G)
, then it is only surjective. For any
G
,
Z_{G}
is étale and the fibre above a point has cardinality
|Z(G)|
.

Now consider the map
\mathrm{KT}_{\mathfrak{g}}\twoheadrightarrow\big(\mathbb{C}^{\times}\big)^{\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}}
that projects to the base of the cotangent bundle in
\mathrm{KT}_{\mathfrak{g}}\cong T^{*}(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{\mathrm{rk}~\mathfrak{g}}
. We define
\mathrm{KT}_{G}
to be the pullback of this projection and the map
T
given above,

(37)

The structure sheaf of
\mathrm{KT}_{G}
has the structure of an
{\cal O}_{(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}}}
-module given by

{\cal O}_{\mathrm{KT}_{G}}={\cal O}_{\mathrm{KT}_{\mathfrak{g}}}\otimes_{{\cal O}_{(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}}}}{\cal O}_{T}~,

(38)

with the
{\cal O}_{(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}}}
-module structure on
{\cal O}_{T}
coming from restriction of scalars along
Z_{G}^{\#}:{\cal O}_{(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}}}\rightarrow{\cal O}_{T}
. Notice that we have the identification

\begin{split}\mathrm{KT}_{G}&=\{(y,t)\,|\,y\in\mathrm{KT}_{\mathfrak{g}}~,~t\in T~,~(\gamma_{1}(y),\gamma_{2}(y),\dots,\gamma_{\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}}(y))=Z_{G}(t)\}~,\\
&\cong\{(b_{1}(y),b_{2}(y),\dots,b_{\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}}(y),t)\in(\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}}\,\}~,\end{split}

(39)

and so
\mathrm{KT}_{G}\cong\mathrm{KT}_{\mathfrak{g}}
as varieties. (In fact, by a linear redefinition of the coordinate functions
b
, this isomorphism can be realised as one of symplectic varieties as well.)

However, the canonical projection
\pi_{G}:\mathrm{KT}_{G}\rightarrow\mathrm{KT}_{\mathfrak{g}}
, coming from the pullback (
37
) is not an isomorphism. Indeed, by standard properties of the pullback
\pi_{G}
is étale, since
Z_{G}
is étale. We should therefore treat
\mathrm{KT}_{G}
as a covering space of
\mathrm{KT}_{\mathfrak{g}}
by way of
\pi_{G}
.

From its definition (
37
), we also have a canonical projection

\pi_{T}:\mathrm{KT}_{G}\rightarrow T~.

(40)

Since
\pi_{T}
is a base change of the surjective morphism
\mathrm{KT}_{\mathfrak{g}}\twoheadrightarrow(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}}
(and self-evidently from (
39
)), it too is surjective.

By construction, the fibre of
\pi_{G}
above a point in
\mathrm{KT}_{\mathfrak{g}}
will contain
|Z(G)|
many points. These fibres admit a
Z(G)
action which makes
\pi_{G}:\mathrm{KT}_{G}\rightarrow\mathrm{KT}_{\mathfrak{g}}
into a
Z(G)
-torsor. In particular, let
y\in\mathrm{KT}_{\mathfrak{g}}
. The points in the fibre
\pi_{G}^{-1}(y)
are mapped bijectively by
\pi_{T}
to a
Z(G)
orbit in
T
. Let
\tilde{y}\in\pi_{G}^{-1}(y)
and let
z\in Z(G)
. The
Z(G)
action is defined uniquely by specifying

z\cdot\tilde{y}\coloneqq\tilde{y}^{\prime}~,\quad\text{such that}\quad\pi_{T}(\tilde{y}^{\prime})=z\cdot\pi_{T}(\tilde{y})~,\quad\pi_{G}(\tilde{y})=\pi_{G}(\tilde{y}^{\prime})~.

(41)

By construction, this gives the following:

Lemma 3.1
.

The map
\pi_{T}:\mathrm{KT}_{G}\rightarrow T
is
Z(G)
-equivariant.

In light of this, the projection
\pi_{G}:\mathrm{KT}_{G}\rightarrow\mathrm{KT}_{\mathfrak{g}}
is just the natural projection to
Z(G)
orbits,
\mathrm{KT}_{G}\rightarrow\mathrm{KT}_{G}/\!\!/Z(G)
, where
\mathrm{KT}_{\mathfrak{g}}
is identified with the quotient space
\mathrm{KT}_{G}/\!\!/Z(G)
. Letting
(y,t)\in\mathrm{KT}_{G}
, with
y\in\mathrm{KT}_{\mathfrak{g}}
and
t\in T

\pi_{G}(y,t)=\chi+\sum^{\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}}_{i}b_{i}(y)h^{*}_{i}+\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta}\alpha(t)e_{\alpha}^{*}~.

(42)

Since the map
\pi_{G}
is étale, we can pullback the symplectic form on
\mathrm{KT}_{\mathfrak{g}}
to a symplectic form on
\mathrm{KT}_{G}
, endowing its coordinate ring in particular with a Poisson bracket. Furthermore,
\mathrm{KT}_{G}
can be made into an
S_{f}
-scheme with structure morphism

\mathrm{KT}_{G}\xrightarrow{\pi_{G}}\mathrm{KT}_{\mathfrak{g}}\xrightarrow{P}\mathfrak{g}^{*}/\!\!/G\xrightarrow{\sim}S_{f}~.

(43)

We are primarily interested in the case when
G
is simply-connected; in this case the functions on
\mathrm{KT}_{G}
have a more concrete presentation. Let
\big(\alpha_{i}\big)_{i=1}^{\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}}
be the simple roots of
G
and
a_{ij}
be the matrix elements of the Cartan matrix of
\mathfrak{g}
. Let
\widetilde{a}_{ij}
be the matrix elements of the inverse Cartan matrix. Then
{\cal O}_{\mathrm{KT}_{\mathfrak{g}}}(\mathrm{KT}_{\mathfrak{g}})\cong\mathbb{C}[b_{\alpha_{i}},\gamma_{\alpha_{i}}\,|\,i=1,\dots,\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}]
and

{\cal O}_{\mathrm{KT}_{G}}(\mathrm{KT}_{G})\cong\mathbb{C}[b_{\alpha_{i}},\widetilde{\gamma}_{\alpha_{i}}|i=1,\dots,\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}]~,

(44)

where

\widetilde{\gamma}_{\alpha_{i}}=\prod_{j=1}^{\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}}\gamma^{\widetilde{a}_{ij}}_{\alpha_{j}}~.

(45)

The map
\pi_{G}
, at the level of rings is,

b_{\alpha_{i}}\mapsto b_{\alpha_{i}}~,\quad\gamma_{\alpha_{i}}\mapsto\prod_{i=1}^{\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}}\widetilde{\gamma}_{j}^{a_{ij}}~,\qquad i=1,\dots,\mathrm{rk}~\mathfrak{g}~.

(46)

The Poisson structure inherited by
\mathrm{KT}_{G}
is just the natural extension of the Poisson bracket on
\mathrm{KT}_{\mathfrak{g}}
to the
\widetilde{\gamma}_{\alpha_{i}}
by imposing the chain rule.

When
G
is simply connected, we can also give a more concrete presentation of
\pi_{T}
. Let
(t_{i})_{i=1}^{\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}}
be the generators of the root lattice of
G
. Since the root lattice of
G
is isomorphic to the weight lattice of the Lie algebra,
\mathfrak{g}
, these generators can be identified with the fundamental weights,
(\omega_{i})^{\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}}_{i=1}
, of
\mathfrak{g}
. Let
V_{i}
be the finite dimensional representation of
\mathfrak{g}
with highest weight
\omega_{i}
, and let
v_{i}
be the highest weight vector. Then, if
h\in T

hv_{i}=t_{i}(h)v_{i}~.

(47)

The
t_{i}
are good coordinates on the torus and the map
\pi_{T}
is induced by the ring homomorphism,

\begin{split}\pi_{T}^{\#}:\mathbb{C}[t_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,t^{\pm 1}_{\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}}]&\rightarrow\mathbb{C}[b_{\alpha_{i}},\widetilde{\gamma}^{\pm 1}_{\alpha_{j}}\,|\,i,j=1,\dots\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}]\\
t_{i}&\mapsto\widetilde{\gamma}_{\alpha_{i}}=\prod_{j=1}^{\mathrm{rk}~\mathfrak{g}}\gamma_{\alpha_{j}}^{\widetilde{a}_{ij}}\end{split}

(48)

Suppose
G
is neither simply-connected nor of adjoint type. Then
G
is a quotient of the simply-connected group,
G_{sc}
, by some central subgroup
Z^{\prime}\subset Z(G_{sc})
with
Z^{\prime}\neq Z(G_{sc})
. Coordinates on
G
are then realised by passing to
Z^{\prime}
invariants of the coordinate ring of the simply-connected group.

3.2
The embedding in rank one

To make this abstract construction a bit more concrete, we consider in detail the two rank-one examples:
G={\rm SL}_{2}
and
G={\rm PGL}_{2}
. We will then see directly how these Kostant–Toda lattices embed into the corresponding universal centralisers.

The
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
Kostant–Toda lattice, thought of as a subset of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
using the Killing form, consists of all elements of the form

y=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}b(y)&0\\
0&-b(y)\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}0&\gamma(y)\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}~,

(49)

with
b(y)\in\mathbb{C}
and
\gamma(y)\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
. The coordinate ring is
\mathbb{C}[b,\gamma^{\pm}]
, with non-vanishing Poisson brackets

\{b,\gamma^{\pm}\}=\pm\gamma^{\pm}~.

(50)

For
G={\rm PGL}_{2}
the maximal torus is isomorphic to
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
and the simple root,
\alpha:\mathbb{C}^{\times}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
, is just the identity morphism. Following the construction above, this means that the structure sheaf
{\cal O}_{\mathrm{KT}_{{\rm PGL}_{2}}}
is isomorphic to
{\cal O}_{\mathrm{KT}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}}
as
{\cal O}_{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}
-modules. The global sections are just
\mathbb{C}[b,\gamma^{\pm 1}]={\cal O}_{\mathrm{KT}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}}(\mathrm{KT}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}})
and the map
\pi_{PGL_{2}}:\mathrm{KT}_{\rm PGL_{2}}\rightarrow\mathrm{KT}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
is an isomorphism. Here we see directly that with
\rm PGL_{2}
being of adjoint type, the covering space
\mathrm{KT}_{\rm PGL_{2}}
is exactly identified with
\mathrm{KT}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
. The structure morphism to
S_{f}
is given by
b^{2}+\gamma
.

Alternatively, for
G={\rm SL}_{2}
the simple root
\alpha
is given by
\alpha:\mathbb{C}^{\times}\xrightarrow{z^{2}}\mathbb{C}^{\times}
. The structure sheaf of
\mathrm{KT}_{G}
is
{\cal O}_{\mathrm{KT}_{G}}={\cal O}_{T}\otimes_{{\cal O}_{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}}{\cal O}_{\mathrm{KT}_{\mathfrak{g}}}
, with global sections

{\cal O}_{\mathrm{KT}_{G}}(\mathrm{KT}_{G})\cong\frac{\mathbb{C}[h^{\pm 1}]\otimes\mathbb{C}[b,\gamma^{\pm 1}]}{\langle h^{2}-\gamma\rangle}\cong\mathbb{C}[b,\gamma^{\pm\frac{1}{2}}].

(51)

which has the obvious module structure over
\mathbb{C}[b,\gamma^{\pm 1}]
. The map
\pi_{{\rm SL}_{2}}
on rings is just the natural inclusion
\pi_{{\rm SL}_{2}}^{\#}:\mathbb{C}[b,\gamma^{\pm 1}]\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}[b,\gamma^{\pm\frac{1}{2}}]
. The Poisson structure, inherited from
\mathrm{KT}_{\mathfrak{g}}
is

\{b,\gamma^{\pm\frac{1}{2}}\}=\pm\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{\pm\frac{1}{2}}~.

(52)

Clearly, with a rescaling of
b
we have
\mathrm{KT}_{{\rm SL}_{2}}\cong T^{*}\mathbb{C}^{\times}\cong\mathrm{KT}_{{\rm PGL}_{2}}
including Poisson structures. However, unlike the adjoint case, here our construction gives
\mathrm{KT}_{{\rm SL}_{2}}
as a
2
-cover of
\mathrm{KT}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
along
\pi_{{\rm SL}_{2}}
. The structure morphism for
\mathrm{KT}_{{\rm SL}_{2}}
is
b^{2}+\gamma^{\frac{1}{2}}\gamma^{\frac{1}{2}}
.

Now we turn to the universal centralisers for these two groups. From Section
2.2
we know that
\mathfrak{Z}_{{\rm SL}_{2}}\subset\mathbb{A}^{3}
can be realised as an affine variety with coordinate ring

I_{{\rm SL}_{2}}=\frac{\mathbb{C}[X,Y,S]}{\langle SX^{2}-Y^{2}-1\rangle}~.

(53)

Similarly, the adjoint version,
\mathfrak{Z}_{\rm PGL_{2}}
, has coordinate ring

I_{\rm PGL_{2}}\cong\frac{\mathbb{C}[S,X^{2},XY,Y^{2}]}{\langle SX^{2}-Y^{2}-1\rangle}\cong\frac{\mathbb{C}[S,A,B,C]}{\langle B^{2}-AC,SA-C-1\rangle}~.

(54)

Let
U_{X}\subset\mathfrak{Z}_{{\rm SL}_{2}}
be the elementary open set defined by localising on the ideal
\langle X\rangle
. In this open subset, the relation
SX^{2}-Y^{2}-1
can be solved for
S
according to

S=\frac{1}{X^{2}}+\frac{Y^{2}}{X^{2}}~.

(55)

So
{\cal O}_{\mathfrak{Z}_{{\rm SL}_{2}}}(U_{X})\cong\mathbb{C}[Y,X^{\pm 1}]
.

Constructing an open immersion
\mathrm{KT}_{{\rm S}_{2}}\rightarrow\mathfrak{Z}_{{\rm SL}_{2}}
is straightforward in this case. The open subset
U_{X}
can be identified with
\mathrm{KT}_{G}
as Poisson schemes over
S_{f}
via the map

X\mapsto\gamma^{-\frac{1}{2}}~,\quad Y\mapsto-b\gamma^{-\frac{1}{2}}~.

(56)

The Slodowy slice coordinate function is then realised as follows,

S\mapsto b^{2}+\gamma^{\frac{1}{2}}\gamma^{\frac{1}{2}}=b^{2}+\gamma~.

(57)

This, of course, agrees with the structure morphism of
\mathrm{KT}_{{\rm SL}_{2}}
, so this morphism is an open immersion of (Poisson)
S_{f}
-schemes.

Now let
U_{A}
be the elementary open set obtained by localising
I_{\rm PGL_{2}}
at the ideal
\langle A\rangle
. In this open set, the relations (
54
) can be solved according to

C=\frac{B^{2}}{A}~,\quad S=\frac{1}{A}+\frac{B^{2}}{A^{2}}~.

(58)

Therefore,
U_{A}\cong\mathrm{Spec}~\mathbb{C}[B,A^{\pm 1}]\cong T^{*}\mathbb{C}^{\times}
as schemes. We are free to pick generators
\frac{B}{A}
and
A^{\pm 1}
for the functions on
U_{A}
. The Poisson bracket is then determined by

\left\{\frac{B}{A},A^{\pm}\right\}=\pm A^{\pm}~,

(59)

so we can identify
U_{A}
with
T^{*}\mathbb{C}^{\times}
as Poisson schemes. Now to embed
\mathrm{KT}_{\rm PGL_{2}}
, we make the following identifications

A\mapsto\gamma^{-1}~,\quad\frac{B}{A}\mapsto b~.

(60)

This extends to

C\mapsto b^{2}\gamma^{-1}~,\quad S\mapsto b^{2}+\gamma

(61)

These identifications induce an open immersion of
S_{f}
schemes
\mathrm{KT}_{\rm PGL_{2}}\rightarrow\mathfrak{Z}_{\rm PGL_{2}}
.

Since the open subsets
U_{X}
and
U_{A}
are both isomorphic to
T^{*}\mathbb{C}^{\times}
as Poisson varieties, one might at first inquire as to whether there is any reason to introduce a covering space after all. Indeed, one may construct the open immersion
\mathrm{KT}_{\rm PGL_{2}}\rightarrow\mathfrak{Z}_{{\rm SL}_{2}}
, with image
U_{X}
, by making the identificationes

X\mapsto\gamma^{-1}~,\quad Y\mapsto-\frac{1}{2}b\gamma^{-1}~,\quad S\mapsto\frac{1}{4}b^{2}+\gamma^{2}~.

(62)

This map is an open immersion of Poisson schemes, however it fails to be a morphism of
S_{f}
-schemes.
Namely, the image of
S
is not
b^{2}+\gamma
. Insisting that the open immersion must be a morphism of
S_{f}
-schemes is natural—it preserves some Lie theoretic data, namely the
\gamma
can be thought of as linear function on
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. Furthermore, this ensures that the inclusion
I_{{\rm SL}_{2}}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}[\mathrm{KT}_{{\rm SL}_{2}}]
is a map of
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}^{*}]^{G}
-modules; this structure appears to be of general importance in the construction of Higgs branches for theories of class
{\cal S}
.

3.3
The embedding in the general case and some technicalities

For higher rank cases, the relationship between
\mathrm{KT}_{G}
and
\mathfrak{Z}_{G}
is more intricate. In this section we provide an abstract description of an open immersion
\mathrm{KT}_{G}\xrightarrow{\varphi}\mathfrak{Z}_{G}
. For
G
of adjoint type, this map was described in less concrete terms in
Kostant:1979195
, and our analysis follows and extends that of
Crooks:2020
.

We decompose
\varphi
into morphisms
\beta:\mathrm{KT}_{G}\rightarrow S_{f}
to the base and
\lambda:\mathrm{KT}_{G}\rightarrow G
to the fibre,
i.e.

\lambda(y)\in G_{\beta(y)}

\mathfrak{b}^{*}

\begin{split}\beta:\mathrm{KT}_{\mathfrak{g}}&\rightarrow S_{f}~,\\

(

will differ by an action of
Z(G)
, and so will
\overline{K}^{-1}_{p}(x)
and
\overline{K}^{-1}_{p}(y)
. Thus, we conclude that
x\overset{!}{=}y
if they map to the same point under
\overline{K}_{p}^{-1}
.
∎

To summarise, the map
\lambda:\mathrm{KT}_{G}\rightarrow G^{*}
is defined by

\lambda(y)\coloneqq\delta_{p}\overline{K}^{-1}_{p}(y)\delta^{-1}_{p}~,

(87)

for
y\in\mathrm{KT}_{G}
and
p=\overline{P}(y)
. Unravelling the definitions again gives the more compact and algebraic form,

\begin{split}\lambda:~&\mathrm{KT}_{G}\rightarrow G^{*}\\
&y\mapsto\widetilde{\rho}_{N}(y)^{-1}\widetilde{w_{0}}\big(\pi_{T}(y)\big)^{-1}\rho_{N}(y)~.\end{split}

(88)

For any
y\in\mathrm{KT}_{G}
, we have that
\lambda(y)\in G^{*}_{\beta(y)}
, and as an immediate corollary of Theorem
3.4
, we have the following:

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
p\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}/\!\!/G
and let
s_{p}\in S_{f}
be the corresponding Slodowy slice element under Kostant’s isomorphism. Then, the restriction of
\lambda
to
\overline{P}^{-1}(p)
gives an isomorphism

\lambda:\overline{P}^{-1}(p)\xrightarrow{\sim}G_{s_{p}}^{*}~.

(89)

Proof.

Follows immediately from Theorem
3.4
and the definition of
\lambda
.
∎

Thus, we have the morphism

\begin{split}\varphi:\mathrm{KT}_{G}&\rightarrow\mathfrak{Z}_{G}~,\\
x&\mapsto(\lambda(x),\beta(x))~.\end{split}

(90)

Let
\mathfrak{Z}^{*}_{G}\subset G^{*}\times S_{f}
be the closed subscheme,

\mathfrak{Z}^{*}_{G}=\{(g,s)\in G^{*}\times S_{f}|\mathrm{Ad}^{*}_{g}s=s\}~.

(91)

Note that
\mathfrak{Z}_{G}^{*}=(G^{*}\times S_{f})\cap\mathfrak{Z}_{G}
inside
G\times S_{f}
, and so is Zariski open in
\mathfrak{Z}_{G}
. The open subset
\mathfrak{Z}^{*}_{G}
can alternatively be characterised as the Hamiltonian reduction

\mathfrak{Z}^{*}_{G}\cong\big(\mu_{diag}^{-1}(0)\cap(G^{*}\times\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}_{\rm reg})\big)/\!\!/G_{diag}~.

(92)

Proposition 3.6
.

The morphism
\varphi:\mathrm{KT}_{G}\rightarrow\mathfrak{Z}_{G}
is an open immersion of
S_{f}
-schemes, with image equal to
\mathfrak{Z}^{*}_{G}

Proof.

We have a foliation of
\mathrm{KT}_{G}
,

\mathrm{KT}_{G}=\bigsqcup_{p\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}/\!\!/G}\overline{P}^{-1}(p)

(93)

From Proposition
3.2
, the morphism
\beta
can be used to rewrite the above as a foliation over
S_{f}
,

\mathrm{KT}_{G}=\bigsqcup_{s\in S_{f}}\beta^{-1}(s)~.

(94)

The leaf
\beta^{-1}(s)
can be identifed with the leaf
\overline{P}^{-1}(p)
when
s=s_{p}
. By Corollary
3.5
, the morphism
\lambda
gives an isomorphism between each leaf of this foliation and
G^{*}_{s}
. Thus,

\mathrm{KT}_{G}\cong\bigsqcup_{s\in S_{f}}G^{*}_{s}=\mathfrak{Z}_{G}^{*}~.

(95)

By construction,
\varphi
sends the leaf of
\mathrm{KT}_{G}
over
s\in S_{f}
to the leaf of
\mathfrak{Z}_{G}^{*}
over the same point. Therefore the diagram,

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{KT}_{G}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{Z}_{G}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S_{f}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\beta}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi}

(96)

commutes and so
\varphi
is a morphism of
S_{f}
-schemes.

∎

We would like
\varphi
to not just be an open immersion, but also a symplectic morphism.

Proposition 3.7
.

For any simple, algebraic Lie group
G

where
\omega_{\mathrm{KT}_{\mathfrak{g}}}
is the symplectic form on
\mathrm{KT}_{\mathfrak{g}}
. Since the symplectic form on
\mathrm{KT}_{G}
,
\omega_{\mathrm{KT}_{G}}
, is defined by pullback along

, we have that

and

\pi_{\mathfrak{Z}}^{*}(\omega_{\mathfrak{Z}_{ad}})=\omega_{\mathfrak{Z}_{G}}
, where
\omega_{\mathfrak{Z}_{G}}

\mathfrak{Z}_{G}
. Therefore,

(\varphi^{*}\circ\pi_{\mathfrak{Z}}^{*})(\omega_{\mathfrak{Z}_{ad}})=\varphi^{*}(\omega_{\mathfrak{Z}_{G}})=\omega_{\mathrm{KT}_{G}}~,

(103)

and
\varphi
is symplectic.
∎

3.4
An algorithmic construction for
\varphi

With the morphism
\varphi:\mathrm{KT}_{G}\rightarrow\mathfrak{Z}_{G}
established, we turn to the more practical problem of finding explicit expressions for the components of this map. Here we will restrict our attention to the case
G={\rm SL}_{N}
, whose structure as an algebraic group is particularly transparent. In the case of other algebraic Lie groups, this level of explicit detail would entail more work, though the story should be essentially analogous.

The embedding
\varphi
consists of two parts,
\lambda
and
\beta
. Let us first discuss
\beta
. An element
y\in\mathrm{KT}_{\mathfrak{g}}
has form

y=\begin{pmatrix}b_{1}(y)&\gamma_{1}(y)&0&\dots\\
1&b_{2}(y)-b_{1}(y)&\gamma_{2}(y)&\dots\\
0&1&b_{3}(y)-b_{2}(y)&\dots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots\end{pmatrix}~,

(104)

when considered as an element of
\mathfrak{g}
.​
8
8

8

Throughout this section we make liberal use of the Killing isomorphism to represent elements of
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
as elements in
\mathfrak{g}
without further comment.
The fibre of the map
\pi_{G}
at
y
consists of elements
\{\tilde{y}_{k},~k=0,\ldots,N-1\}
that are permuted cyclically under the action of
Z(G)
.

For
x\in\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
realised as a matrix in
\mathrm{End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
, the fundamental invariants (up to conventions regarding overall scaling) can be computed as

P_{j}(x)=\frac{1}{j+1}\mathrm{Tr}\big(x^{j+1}\big)~,\qquad\text{for }n=1,2,\dots,\mathrm{rk}~\mathfrak{g}~.

(105)

The components of
\beta(y)
are given by
P_{1}(y),P_{2}(y),\ldots P_{\mathrm{rk}_{\mathfrak{g}}}(y)
and for a given value of
N
can be easily computed by the trace formula above. To compute
\beta(\tilde{y}_{k})
we simply project to
y
by
\pi_{G}
before applying the expression above.

Next let us consider the more elaborate morphism
\lambda
. The definition (
88
) of
\lambda
applied to a point in
\mathrm{KT}_{G}
takes the form

\lambda(\tilde{y}_{k})=\widetilde{\rho}_{N}(\tilde{y}_{k})^{-1}\widetilde{w}_{0}\big(\pi_{T}(\tilde{y}_{k})\big)^{-1}\rho_{N}(\tilde{y}_{k})~.

(106)

From the expression in (
48
), the map
\pi_{T}
is given by

\pi_{T}=\begin{pmatrix}\widetilde{\gamma}_{1}&0&0&0&\dots\\
0&\frac{\widetilde{\gamma}_{2}}{\widetilde{\gamma}_{1}}&0&0&\dots\\
0&0&\ddots&0&\dots\\
\dots&0&0&\frac{\widetilde{\gamma}_{N}}{\widetilde{\gamma}_{N-1}}&0\\
\dots&0&0&0&\widetilde{\gamma}_{N}^{-1}\end{pmatrix}~.

(107)

Like
\beta
, the maps
\rho_{N}
and
\widetilde{\rho}_{N}
only depend on the image of
\tilde{y}_{k}
under
\pi_{G}
, whereas
\pi_{T}
satisfies

\pi_{T}(\tilde{y}_{k})=\xi^{k}\pi_{T}(\tilde{y}_{0})~.

(108)

A key input into the maps
\rho_{N}
and
\widetilde{\rho}_{N}
is the inverse map
\psi^{-1}:\chi+\mathfrak{b}^{*}\rightarrow N\times S_{f}
of the Kostant isomorphism. An element of
N\times S_{f}
can be written as
(n,s)
, where we have

n=\begin{pmatrix}1&n_{1,1}&n_{1,2}&n_{1,3}&\dots\\
0&1&n_{2,1}&n_{2,2}&\dots\\
0&0&1&n_{3,1}&\dots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots\end{pmatrix}~,\qquad s=\begin{pmatrix}0&p_{1}&p_{2}&p_{3}&\dots\\
1&0&p_{1}&p_{2}&\dots\\
0&1&0&p_{1}&\dots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots\end{pmatrix}~.

(109)

All entries are
\mathbb{C}
-valued. Under
\psi
the element
(n,s)
is taken to the element of
\mathrm{KT}_{\mathfrak{g}}
whose matrix entries are

\begin{split}Ad^{\ast}_{n}s=nsn^{-1}&=\begin{pmatrix}n_{1,1}&p_{1}-(n_{1,1})^{2}+n_{1,2}&\ast&\dots\\
1&n_{2,1}-n_{1,1}&p_{1}-(n_{2,1})^{2}+n_{2,1}n_{1,1}+n_{2,2}-n_{2,1}&\dots\\
0&1&n_{3,1}-n_{2,1}&\dots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots\end{pmatrix}\\
&\overset{!}{=}\begin{pmatrix}b_{1}&\gamma_{1}&0&\dots\\
1&b_{2}-b_{1}&\gamma_{2}&\dots\\
0&1&b_{3}-b_{2}&\dots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\end{pmatrix}=y~.\end{split}

(110)

The inverse morphism
\psi^{-1}
is determined by systematically solving this equation for the coordinates
n_{i,j}
and
p_{i}
on
N\times S_{f}
. In practice, equality of diagonals sets
n_{i,1}=b_{i}
, and qquating the first superdiagonals then fixes the values of
n_{i,2}
and
p_{1}
. The next superdiagonal fixes
n_{i,3}
and
p_{2}
, and so on continuing to higher superdiagonals. At each step, the new unfixed coordinates appear only linearly. The value of
\rho_{N}(\tilde{y}_{k})
is then just
n
, re-expressed in terms of the
b
and
\tilde{\gamma}
variables according to this procedure.

The map
\widetilde{\rho}_{N}
can be determined in the same manner. Note that the conjugate
\mathrm{Ad}_{\widetilde{w}_{0}(\pi_{T}(\tilde{y}_{0}))^{-1}}y
will be the reflection of
y
along the antidiagonal. To see this, observe that the element
\pi_{T}(\tilde{y}_{0})^{-1}
leaves the
b_{i}
invariant and contracts each
\gamma_{i}
entry to
1
while dilating
\chi
to
\sum_{i=1}^{\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}}\gamma_{i}e_{-\alpha_{i}}
(as the negative simple roots transform with opposite weights). In effect, this reflects
y
across the diagonal. On the other hand, the element
\widetilde{w}_{0}
, acts by sequential reflection across the diagonal and the antidiagonal, so in combination the effect is that
\mathrm{Ad}_{\widetilde{w}_{0}(\pi_{T}(\tilde{y}_{0}))^{-1}}
is the antidiagonal reflection. Consequently, in practice the expression for
\widetilde{\rho}_{N}(y)
can simply be obtained from the
n_{i,j}
above by making the replacement
(b_{i},\gamma_{i})\leftrightarrow(-b_{N-1-i},\gamma_{N-1-i})
.

For the purpose of realising the universal centraliser as a symplectic variety, this procedure can be expedited as a result of Proposition
2.1
and Theorem
2.2
. These imply that rather than constructing
\lambda
in its entirety, it is sufficient to determine just the lower left-hand corner entry.

By definition, both
\rho_{N}(\tilde{y}_{k})
and
\widetilde{\rho}_{N}(\tilde{y}_{k})
are upper unitriangular matrices, while
\widetilde{w}_{0}\big(\pi_{T}(\tilde{y})_{k}\big)^{-1}
is supported along the antidiagonal as in (
107
). The bottom row of the stabilising group element is therefore completely fixed by the first row of the map
\rho_{N}
, and what’s more, the lower-left corner entry is just equal to the reciprocal of the upper-left corner entry of
\pi_{T}
, so in particular we have

X\coloneqq\widetilde{\gamma}^{-1}_{1}=\prod_{j=1}^{\mathrm{rk}~\mathfrak{g}}\gamma_{j}^{-\widetilde{a}_{1j}}~.

(111)

From this and the expression for the
P_{j}
functions, Theorem
2.2
allows us to generate all of
I_{{\rm SL}_{N}}
.

For
G\neq\rm SL_{N}
we have a
conjectural
expression for
X
, coming from the discussion in Section
2.4
. Suppose that
G
is not
\mathrm{Spin}(4N)
, then the lowest dimensional faithful representation,
V_{F}
is irreducible. Let
(a_{1},a_{2},\dots_{,}a_{\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}})
be the components of the highest weight of
V_{F}

in the simple root basis
. We conjecture that

X=\prod_{j=1}^{\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}}\gamma_{j}^{-a_{j}}~.

(112)

For
G={\rm SL}_{N}
this specialises to
X=\prod_{j=1}^{\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}}\gamma_{j}^{-\widetilde{a}_{1j}}
, where
\widetilde{a}_{ij}
are again the entries of the inverse Cartan matrix. Similarly, if
G
is
{\rm Spin}(4N)
we define
X_{\rm s}
and
X_{\rm c}
analogously to (
112
) using the highest weights of
V_{\rm s}
and
V_{\rm c}
respectively.

3.5
The embedding for
{\rm SL}_{3}

The ring of functions on the Kostant–Toda lattice for
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
is given by

\mathbb{C}[\mathrm{KT}_{\mathfrak{g}}]=\mathbb{C}[b_{1},b_{2},\gamma_{1}^{\pm 1},\gamma_{2}^{\pm 1}]~.

(113)

Following the general prescription, for
G={\rm SL}_{3}
the covering space
\mathrm{KT}_{G}
comes with coordinate ring

\mathbb{C}[\mathrm{KT}_{G}]=\mathbb{C}[b_{1},b_{2},\widetilde{\gamma}^{\pm 1}_{1},\widetilde{\gamma}^{\pm 1}_{2}]~,

(114)

where
\widetilde{\gamma}_{1}=\gamma^{-\frac{2}{3}}_{1}\gamma_{2}^{-\frac{1}{3}}
and
\widetilde{\gamma}_{2}=\gamma^{-\frac{2}{3}}_{2}\gamma_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}
, and the Poisson bracket follows accordingly. We can make explicit the maps involved in the definition of
\varphi
. For
\pi_{T}:\mathrm{KT}_{G}\rightarrow T
we have (at the level of rings of functions)

t_{1}\mapsto\widetilde{\gamma}_{1}~,\quad t_{2}\mapsto\widetilde{\gamma}_{2}~.

(115)

The longest element of the Weyl group of

can be lifted to
N_{{\rm SL}_{3}}(T)
as

\widetilde{w}_{0}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&-1\\
0&-1&0\\
-1&0&0\end{pmatrix}~.

(116)

The coordinate ring of the Slodowy slice,
S_{f}
, is embedded (up to normalisation) as

\begin{split}S&=\frac{1}{2}\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\nolimits(y^{2})=b_{1}^{2}+b_{2}^{2}-b_{1}b_{2}+\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}~,\\
W&=\frac{1}{3}\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\nolimits(y^{3})=b_{1}^{2}b_{2}-b_{1}b_{2}^{2}+\gamma_{1}b_{2}-\gamma_{2}b_{1}~.\end{split}

(117)

The image of
\lambda

as described above, one finds

\rho_{N}(y)=\begin{pmatrix}~~1~~&~~b_{1}~~&\frac{1}{2}(b_{1}^{2}+b_{1}b_{2}-b_{2}^{2}+\gamma_{1}-\gamma_{2})\\
0&1&b_{2}\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix}~.

(118)

The group element

\tilde{w}_{0}(\pi_{T}(y))^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&-1\\
0&-1&0\\
-1&0&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{\gamma}_{1}&0&0\\

0&0&\frac{1}{\tilde{\gamma}_{2}}\end{pmatrix}^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&-\gamma_{1}^{\frac{1}{3}}\gamma_{2}^{\frac{2}{3}}\\

Solving for the matrix

then produces the result

\widetilde{\rho_{N}}(y)=\begin{pmatrix}~~1~~&~~-b_{2}~~&\frac{1}{2}(-b_{1}^{2}+b_{1}b_{2}+b_{2}^{2}-\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2})\\

Putting everything together, this gives the following parameterisation of (an open subset of) stabiliser of the point

\lambda(y)=-\frac{1}{\gamma_{1}^{\frac{2}{3}}\gamma_{2}^{\frac{1}{3}}}\begin{pmatrix}b_{1}^{2}+\gamma_{1}-\frac{S}{2}&W+\frac{b_{1}S}{2}&b_{1}W+\frac{S^{2}}{4}\\
b_{1}&b_{1}^{2}+\gamma_{1}&W+\frac{b_{1}S}{2}\\

(121)

where to simplify the expression we have used
S
and

\begin{split}X&\coloneqq-\gamma_{1}^{-\frac{2}{3}}\gamma_{2}^{-\frac{1}{3}}~,\\
Y&\coloneqq-\-\gamma_{1}^{-\frac{2}{3}}\gamma_{2}^{-\frac{1}{3}}b_{1}~,\\
Z&\coloneqq-\frac{1}{2}\gamma_{1}^{-\frac{2}{3}}\gamma_{2}^{-\frac{1}{3}}(\gamma_{1}-\gamma_{2}+b_{1}^{2}-b_{2}^{2}+b_{1}b_{2})~.\end{split}

By Proposition

\varphi(y)=\left(\begin{pmatrix}Z~&~WX+\frac{1}{2}SY~&~\frac{1}{4}S^{2}X+WY\\

0&1&0\end{pmatrix}\right)~.

Note that this agrees with the presentation of Section

\begin{split}D_{{\rm SL}_{3}}=&-\frac{1}{8}S^{3}X^{3}+W^{2}X^{3}+\frac{1}{2}SWX^{2}Y+\frac{1}{2}S^{2}XY^{2}+WY^{3}-\frac{1}{4}S^{2}X^{2}Z\\

is satisfied identically by the expressions for

4
Chiralising the universal centraliser

In
Arakawa:2018egx
, the (critical level) chiral universal centraliser (
\mathbf{I}_{G}
, denoted in
loc.

cit.
by
\mathbf{I}_{G}
), was defined to be the two-sided quantum Drinfel’d–Sokolov reduction of critical level chiral differential operators (CDOs) on
G
(
\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{G,\kappa_{c}}
).​
9
9

9

The chiral universal centraliser
\mathbf{I}_{{\rm SL}_{2}}
at
non-critical level
appeared previously in
Frenkel:200657
as the regular representation of the Virasoro algebra.
The operation of Drinfel’d–Sokolov reduction is a chiral analogue of Kostant–Whittaker reduction, and
\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{G,\kappa_{c}}
represents a
chiral quantisation
(defined in the following section) of
T^{*}G
, so
\mathbf{I}_{G}
is indeed a natural chiral analogue of
\mathfrak{Z}_{G}
. By virtue of its definition in terms of DS reduction, it should be identified with the VOA assigned to the sphere of type
G
in class
{\cal S}

Beem:2014rza
.

In the previous section, we presented a method for realising
I_{G}=\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{Z}_{G}]
by way of the Kostant–Toda lattice
\mathrm{KT}_{G}
. The open immersion
\varphi:\mathrm{KT}_{G}\rightarrow\mathfrak{Z}_{G}
gives a realisation of
I_{G}
in terms of functions on an algebraically trivial space, which we think of as a (finite-dimensional, classical) “free field realisation” of
I_{G}
. In this section we describe a chiral analogue of this construction, and propose a strategy for producing (chiral, quantum) free field realisations for
\mathbf{I}_{G}
for general
G
. We make this completely explicit for the cases
G={\rm SL}_{2}
and
G={\rm SL}_{3}
.

In this section

will be assumed to be simply connected unless otherwise stated.

4.1
Chiralising the Kostant–Toda lattice

The Kostant–Toda lattice is isomorphic as a symplectic variety to
(T^{\ast}\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}}
. The standard chiral quantisation(s) of this space are given by CDOs on
(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}}
. In
Beem:2019tfp
, these CDOs were presented in terms of a lattice boson system. We will review this construction here in connection with the Kostant–Toda lattice and its refinement.

Let chiral bosons
\phi_{i}
,
\delta_{j}
for
i,j=1,2,\dots,\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}
be normalised according to

\phi_{i}(z)\phi_{j}(w)\sim-\delta_{ij}\log(z-w)~,\quad\delta_{i}(z)\delta_{j}(w)\sim\delta_{ij}\log(z-w)~.

(125)

Let
\Pi_{\mathfrak{g}}
be the isotropic lattice vertex algebra generated by
\partial\phi_{i}
,
\partial\delta_{i}
, and vertex operators,
e^{n(\phi_{i}+\delta_{i})}
, for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Since the
\phi_{i}+\delta_{i}
operator has regular self-OPE, the exponential vertex operators form a commutative vertex algebra. This is a “half-lattice vertex algebra” in the sense of
half_lattice
.

In fact,
\Pi_{\mathfrak{g}}
is strongly generated by the operators

\gamma_{i}^{\pm 1}=e^{\pm(\phi_{i}+\delta_{i})}~,\quad b_{i}=\frac{1}{2}\partial(\delta_{i}-\phi_{i})~,

(126)

whose only nonvanishing singular OPEs are given by

b_{i}(z)\gamma_{j}^{\pm 1}(w)\sim\frac{\pm\delta_{ij}\gamma_{j}^{\pm 1}(w)}{z-w}~,

(127)

and which also satisfy the suggested relation
(\gamma_{i}^{-1}\gamma_{i}^{+1})=1
. This presentation is the one relevant to description of this vertex algebra as CDOs on the multiplicative group
(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}}
, which itself can be thought of as the localisation of
\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}
many
\beta\gamma
systems (
i.e.
, CDOs on
\mathbb{C}^{\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}}

Malikov:1999
). The lattice representation then arises via Friedan–Martinec–Shenker bosonisation of the
\beta\gamma
system.

This presentation in terms of lattice bosons makes the following generalisation natural. We pass to a refined lattice with rational spacings, specifically with denominator
|Z(G)|
for
G
simply connected, and define the vertex subalgebra
\Pi_{G}
to be strongly generated by

\widetilde{\gamma}^{\pm 1}_{i}=\prod_{i=1}^{\mathrm{rk}{\mathfrak{g}}}e^{\pm\tilde{a}_{ij}(\phi_{j}+\delta_{j})}~,\quad b_{i}=\frac{1}{2}\partial(\delta_{i}-\phi_{i})~,

(128)

where, as previously, the
\tilde{a}_{ij}
are matrix elements of the inverse Cartan matrix of
\mathfrak{g}
. Note that the
b_{i}
are unchanged. For
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
, this is precisely the lattice
\Pi_{\frac{1}{2}}
appearing in the free field constructions of
Beem:2019tfp
(and previously in
Adamovic2019
). The vertex algebra
\Pi_{G}
admits a natural action of
Z(G)
according to the prescription in (
41
), extended to be
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-linear.

Now suppose
G^{\prime}
is a non-simply connected algebraic Lie group with the same Lie algebra as
G
. Then
G^{\prime}\cong Z^{\prime}(G)\backslash G
for some central subgroup
Z^{\prime}(G)\subset Z(G)
. Let
\gamma_{i}^{\prime}
be the generators of the ring
\mathbb{C}[\widetilde{\gamma}_{i}]^{Z^{\prime}(G)}
. Then we define a lattice vertex subalgebra
\Pi_{G^{\prime}}\subset\Pi_{G}
that is strongly generated by the
b_{i}
and the
Z(G)
invariants,
(\gamma_{i}^{\prime})^{\pm 1}
.

There is an obvious inclusion of vertex algebras
\Pi_{\mathfrak{g}}\hookrightarrow\Pi_{G}
, given by

\gamma_{i}^{\pm 1}\mapsto\prod_{j=1}^{\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}}(\widetilde{\gamma}_{j}^{\pm 1})^{a_{ij}}~,\qquad b_{i}\mapsto b_{i}~.

(129)

The image of
\Pi_{\mathfrak{g}}
inside
\Pi_{G}
is precisely the
Z(G)
invariant subalgebra of
\Pi_{G}
—indeed, for the case that
G^{\prime}
is the adjoint group, we have
\Pi_{G^{\prime}}=\Pi_{\mathfrak{g}}
.

Our vertex algebras
\Pi_{G}
and the Kostant–Toda lattices are related in the sense that the former is a chiral quantisation of the latter. The relationship between vertex algebras and geometric spaces were explored by Arakawa–Moreau
Arakawa:2012c2
;
Arakawa2017introduction
;
Arakawa_Moreau:2018
;
Arakawa_Moreau:2017157
;
Arakawa_Moreau:2018542
. We shall recall some of the relevant definitions now. For a more pedagogical introduction, we recommend the monograph
Arakawa_Moreau:jet
.

For a scheme
X
over
\mathbb{C}
, we denote its arc space by
J_{\infty}X\coloneqq\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{D},X)
, where
\mathbb{D}=\mathrm{Spec}~\mathbb{C}[\![t]\!]
is the formal disc. For
X
Poisson, functions on the arc space
{\cal O}(J_{\infty}X)
has a canonical vertex Poisson algebra structure
Arakawa:2012c2
.

Every vertex algebra has a canonical decreasing filtration,
F^{\bullet}
, known as the Li filtration
Li:2005
. Specifically, for
V
a vertex algebra with vacuum
|0\rangle
, we have

F^{i}V=\bigg\{v_{1,(-n_{1}-1)}v_{2,(-n_{2}-1)}\dots v_{k,(-n_{k}-1)}|0\rangle\,\bigg|\,k\in\mathbb{N}\,,~~v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{k}\in V\,,~~\sum_{j=1}^{k}n_{j}\geqslant i\,\bigg\}~.

(130)

V
is
separated
if
\bigcap_{i=1}F^{i}V=0
and if
V
is a separated vertex algebra, then the vector space
R_{V}\coloneqq V/F^{1}(V)
is a Poisson algebra, with multiplication arising from the normally ordered product and the Poisson bracket coming from simple poles in the OPE. This Poisson algebra is called the
Zhu’s
C_{2}
algebra
of
V
, and
X_{V}\coloneqq\mathrm{Specm}\,R_{V}
is the
associated variety
of
V
.
\overline{X}_{V}\coloneqq\mathrm{Spec}\,R_{V}
is then the
associated scheme
of
V
.

Now let
X
be an affine, Poisson scheme; a vertex algebra
V
is said to be a
chiral quantisation
of
X

Arakawa2017introduction
if, as Poisson varieties,

X_{V}\cong X_{red}~.

(131)

It is a
strict chiral quantisation
of
X
if furthermore,

\overline{X}_{V}\cong X\qquad\mathrm{and}\qquad\mathrm{Spec}(\mathrm{gr}_{F}\,V)\cong J_{\infty}X~.

(132)

A vertex algebra satisfying
\mathrm{Spec}~(\mathrm{gr}_{F}V)\cong J_{\infty}\mathrm{Spec}~\overline{X}_{V}
is also known as
classically free
.

The vertex algebra
\Pi_{\mathfrak{g}}
is a strict chiral quantisation of
T^{\ast}\big(\mathbb{C}^{\times}\big)^{\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}}
, and given its definition in terms of strong generators, it is straightforward to see that
R_{\Pi_{G}}\cong\mathbb{C}[b_{i},\widetilde{\gamma}_{j}\,|\,i,j=1,\dots,\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}]
with the expected Poisson brackets. Therefore,
\overline{X}_{\Pi_{G}}
is reduced and can be identified with
\mathrm{KT}_{G}
as a symplectic variety. Moreover,
\Pi_{G}
is classically free, so
\Pi_{G}
is a strict chiral quantisation of
\mathrm{KT}_{G}
for any
G
. Moreover, the inclusion
\Pi_{\mathfrak{g}}\hookrightarrow\Pi_{G}
as the
Z(G)
invariant subalgebra descends, at the level of associated schemes, to the covering map
\pi_{G}^{\#}:{\cal O}(\mathrm{KT}_{\mathfrak{g}})\hookrightarrow{\cal O}(\mathrm{KT}_{G})
.

Finally, let us briefly remark on the conformal structure of these vertex algebras. Chiral bosons admit a well-known family of conformal vectors, which in our case amount to a
\mathbb{C}^{2\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}}
parameter space of conformal vectors of the form

T=\sum_{i}\frac{1}{2}\bigg(\partial\delta_{i}\partial\delta_{i}-\partial\phi_{i}\partial\phi_{i}+\alpha_{i}\partial^{2}\phi_{i}+\beta_{i}\partial^{2}\delta\bigg)~,

(133)

where
\alpha_{i},\beta_{i}\in\mathbb{C}
are background charges for
\phi_{i}
and
\delta_{i}
, respectively. Tuning the values of
\alpha_{i}
and
\beta_{i}
changes the conformal grading on
\Pi_{G}
, and in particular changes the weight of the
\gamma_{i}
operators. To match with the
\mathbb{C}^{\ast}
grading on
\mathrm{KT}_{G}
, we require the weights
\Delta_{\gamma_{i}}=2
and
\Delta_{b_{i}}=1
, which is accomplished by setting
\alpha_{i}+\beta_{i}=-2
. With no further input, this leaves
\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}
many unconstrained background charges, one combination of which could further be constrained by demanding that the central charge match the expected value for the chiral universal centraliser,

c_{\mathfrak{g}}=\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}+48(\rho_{\mathfrak{g}},\rho_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\vee})~,

(134)

where
\rho_{\mathfrak{g}}
is the half-sum of positive roots and
\rho_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\vee}
is the half-sum of positive coroots. For simply laced
\mathfrak{g}
, we can apply the strange formula of Freudenthal and de Vries to simplify this expression to

c_{\mathfrak{g}}=\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}+2h_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\vee}\,{\rm dim}\,\mathfrak{g}~,

(135)

where
h_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\vee}
is the dual Coxeter number.

4.2
A chiral embedding and free field realisation

The aim in this section is to identify a candidate for a free field realisation of the chiral universal centraliser by constructing a chiral version of the morphism
\varphi:\mathrm{KT}_{G}\rightarrow\mathfrak{Z}_{G}
. Recall that
\varphi
decomposes into a map
\beta
to the Slodowy slice
S_{f}
and a map
\lambda
to the centraliser in
G
of
\beta(y)
. We will treat the chiral version in a similar matter.

The chiral analogue of
\mathbb{C}[S_{f}]
is the Feigin–Frenkel centre
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})

Frenkel:2007
. Indeed, as
\mathbf{I}_{G}
is defined as the two-sided Drinfel’d—Sokolov reduction of
\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{G,\kappa_{c}}
, it has a subalgebra isomorphic to
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})
(
cf.

Arakawa:2018egx
;
Beem:2022mde
for discussion of Feigin–Frenkel centres in chiral algebras of class
{\cal S}
), so there is a map
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})\rightarrow\mathbf{I}_{G}
, the image of which should appear in our free field realisation as a Feigin–Frenkel center subalgebra in
\Pi_{G}
.

To construct this Feigin–Frenkel subalgebra, we specify an Ansatz by asserting a chiral version of the map
\beta
. This Ansatz will be a quantum corrected version of the expressions for the generators of
\mathbb{C}[S_{f}]
, which arise from the ring map
\beta^{\#}:\mathbb{C}[S_{f}]\rightarrow\mathbb{C}[\mathrm{KT}_{G}]
.

We make the following observation: the morphism
\beta
comes from the restriction of
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}^{*}]^{G}
to
\mathrm{KT}_{\mathfrak{g}}
, so in particular the functions on
\mathrm{im}\,\beta
extend smoothly to the closure
\overline{\mathrm{KT}}_{\mathfrak{g}}=\chi+\mathfrak{t}^{*}+\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Delta}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
. Therefore, the expressions for the generators will only feature positively graded generators of
\mathbb{C}[\mathrm{KT}_{\mathfrak{g}}]
. Furthermore, the classical map
\beta
does not require passage to
\mathrm{KT}_{G}
, so the expressions for
\beta^{\#}(P_{i})
only feature positive integer powers of the
\gamma_{i}
and
b_{i}
.

We can construct a chiral analogue of
\beta
in the following manner. Let
V^{0}(\mathfrak{b})
be the universal affine vertex algebra associated to the Borel subalgebra
\mathfrak{b}
at level zero. This algebra contains a descending chain of vertex ideals, which are chiral analogues of the lower central series of
\mathfrak{n}
, given by

V^{0}(\mathfrak{b})\supset V(\mathfrak{n})\supset V([\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{n}])\supset V([\mathfrak{n},[\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{n}]])\supset\dots\supset 0~,

(136)

where the chain terminates in finitely many steps since
\mathfrak{n}
is solvable. The quotient
V^{0}(\mathfrak{b})/V([\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{n}])
is a chiral quantisation of
\overline{\mathrm{KT}}_{\mathfrak{g}}\cong(\mathfrak{b}/[\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{n}])^{*}
, and there is a corresponding inclusion
V^{0}(\mathfrak{b})/V([\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{n}])\hookrightarrow\Pi_{\mathfrak{g}}
, where we can view
\Pi_{\mathfrak{g}}
as a localisation of
V^{0}(\mathfrak{b})/V([\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{n}])
.

Starting with
V^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{g})
, the universal affine vertex algebra of
\mathfrak{g}
at level
\kappa
, principal Drinfel’d–Sokolov reduction returns the (universal) principal
{\cal W}
-algebra
{\cal W}_{prin}(\mathfrak{g},\kappa)
. This
{\cal W}
-algebra can be realised as a subalgebra of the semi-infinite BRST complex that computes the reduction, with explicit expressions for the generators of this embedded subalgebra computed by the tic-tac-toe algorithm of
deBoer:1993iz
. In particular, the generators of
{\cal W}_{prin}(\mathfrak{g},\kappa)
can be expressed entirely in terms of the so-called
hatted current subalgebra
, which itself is isomorphic to
V^{\kappa-\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{b})
—note the shift by the critical level
\kappa_{c}
. It was also observed in
loc. cit
. that passing to the quotient
V^{\kappa-\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{b})/V(\mathfrak{n})\cong V^{\kappa-\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{t})
produces the usual quantum Miura transformation, expressing generators of
{\cal W}_{prin}(\mathfrak{g},\kappa)
in terms of
\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}
many Abelian currents.

At the critical level, the principal
{\cal W}
-algebra is isomorphic to the Feigin–Frenkel centre,
i.e.
,
{\cal W}_{prin}(f,\kappa_{c})\cong\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})
. One therefore has an embedding
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})\hookrightarrow V^{0}(\mathfrak{b})
. Since
V([\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{n}])
is a vertex ideal, this map can be extended to the quotient giving a map
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})\hookrightarrow V^{0}(\mathfrak{b})\twoheadrightarrow V^{0}(\mathfrak{b})/V([\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{n}])
, which gives rise to a commutative vertex subalgebra—that is isomorphic to the Feigin–Frenkel centre—contained in
V^{0}(\mathfrak{b})/V([\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{n}])
. Our candidate chiralisation of
\beta
is then given by the further composition

\beta^{ch}:\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})\hookrightarrow V^{0}(\mathfrak{b})/V([\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{n}])\hookrightarrow\Pi_{\mathfrak{g}}~.

(137)

Concretely,
\beta^{ch}(P_{i})=(P_{i}^{t})|_{\mathfrak{b}/[\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{n}]}
, where
P_{i}^{t}
are the tic-tac-toed expressions for the generators of
{\cal W}_{prin}(\mathfrak{g},\kappa_{c})
, as detailed in
deBoer:1993iz
and
|_{\mathfrak{b}/[\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{n}]}
denotes the projection
V^{0}(\mathfrak{b})\twoheadrightarrow V^{0}(\mathfrak{b})/V([\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{n}])
. In a sense,
\beta^{ch}
is a deformed version of the Miura transformation. Of course, this construction also applies away from the critical level, giving rise to an embedding
\beta^{ch}:{\cal W}_{prin}(\mathfrak{g},\kappa)\hookrightarrow V^{\kappa-\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{b})
.

Remark 4.1
.

The above construction is actually just one in a series of embedded realisations of principal
{\cal W}
-algebras. Let
\mathfrak{n}_{i}
be the lower central series of
\mathfrak{n}
, i.e.,

\mathfrak{n}_{j}=\underbrace{[\mathfrak{n},[\mathfrak{n},[\mathfrak{n},[\dots,\mathfrak{n}]]\dots]]}_{j}~,

(138)

for
j=1,\dots,d_{\mathfrak{g}}
, where
\mathfrak{n}_{d_{\mathfrak{g}}}
is the last non-zero term. There is a system of embeddings
\beta^{ch}_{j}:{\cal W}_{prin}(\mathfrak{g},\kappa)\hookrightarrow V^{\kappa-\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{b})/V(\mathfrak{n}_{k})
for
j=1,\dots,d_{\mathfrak{g}}+1
, which maps generators according to

\beta^{ch}_{j}(P_{i})=P_{i}^{t}|_{\mathfrak{b}/\mathfrak{n}_{k}}~,

(139)

where
|_{\mathfrak{b}/\mathfrak{n}_{j}}
is the projection
V^{0}(\mathfrak{b})\twoheadrightarrow V^{0}(\mathfrak{b})/V(\mathfrak{n}_{j})
. The usual Miura transform is given by
\beta^{ch}_{1}
, our embedding corresponds to
\beta^{ch}_{2}
, and
\beta^{ch}_{d_{\mathfrak{g}}+1}
is the usual tic-tac-toe embedding from
deBoer:1993iz
.

It would be interesting to know whether these generalised Miura maps have a more geometric interpretation, viz., the relation of the Miura map to opers in the critical case.

In practice, an alternative strategy for calculating these generators would be to write down the most general correction to the classical expression,
\beta^{\#}(P_{i})
, and then fix free coefficients by imposing that all OPEs amongst the generators are regular. In principle, one might worry that the “most general correction” may have an infinite number of unfixed parameters, because the vertex algebra
\mathbf{I}_{G}
is not conical. However, based on our discussion of
\beta^{ch}
we should only look for corrections which contain positive powers of the
(\gamma_{i})_{i=1}^{\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}}
—of which there are only finitely many. In the examples in the following sections, the expressions that one arrives at by imposing these regularity constraints are precisely the ones obtained by actually computing
\beta^{ch}
with the tic-tac-toe algorithm.

With a realisation of the Feigin–Frenkel centre established, we turn to the additional generators of
\mathbf{I}_{G}
. We shall proceed by defining a free-field expression for
X
that arises from the finite setting in (
112
). Suppose that
G
is not simply-connected of type
D_{2N}
, then
V_{F}
is irreducible. Let
(a_{1},a_{2},\dots_{,}a_{\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}})
be the components of the highest weight of
V_{F}
in the simple root basis. We then define

X\coloneqq\prod_{j=1}^{\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}}\gamma_{j}^{-a_{j}}~.

(140)

Similarly, if
G
is
{\rm Spin}(2N)
we define
X_{\rm s}
and
X_{\rm c}
analogously to (
140
) using the highest weights of
V_{\rm s}
and
V_{\rm c}
respectively. We now formulate the chiral version of Conjecture
2.4
:

Conjecture 4.2
.

Suppose
G
is not
\mathrm{Spin}(2N)
. Then the vertex algebra
\mathbf{I}_{G}
is isomorphic to the vertex subalgebra of
\Pi_{G}
that is weakly generated by the generators
(\beta^{ch}(P_{i}))_{i=1}^{\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}})
of the Feigin–Frenkel centre and by the generator
X
.

If
G
is
\mathrm{Spin}(2N)
, then
\mathbf{I}_{G}
is isomorphic to the vertex subalgebra of
\Pi_{G}
that is weakly generated by the generators
(\beta^{ch}(P_{i}))_{i=1}^{\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}})
of the Feigin–Frenkel centre and by the generators
X_{\rm s}
and
X_{\rm c}
.

Again inspired by the finite dimensional setting we have beliefs on the set of strong generators of
\mathbf{I}_{G}
.

Conjecture 4.3
.

Suppose
G
is not
\mathrm{Spin}(2N)
. Then
\mathbf{I}_{G}
is strongly generated by the generators
(P_{i})_{i=1}^{\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}}
of the Feigin–Frenkel centre and generators
(\hat{g}_{i})_{i=1}^{{\rm dim}V_{F}}
in one-to-one correspondence with the weight vectors of
V_{F}^{*}
.

If
G
is
\mathrm{Spin}(2N)
, then
\mathbf{I}_{G}
is strongly generated by by the generators
(P_{i})_{i=1}^{\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}}
of the Feigin–Frenkel centre and generators,
(\hat{g}_{{\rm s}i})_{i=1}^{{\rm dim}\,V_{\rm s}}
and
(\hat{g}_{{\rm c}i})_{i=1}^{{\rm dim}\,V_{\rm c}}
in one-to-one correspondence with the weight vectors of
V_{\rm s}^{*}
and
V_{\rm c}^{*}
respectively.

Unlike the finite dimensional setting, we will not attempt a proof of these conjectures. This should potentially be dealt with in terms of the (micro-)localisation of
\mathbf{I}_{G}
over its associated variety. Such a localisation theory is still in its early stages (see,
e.g.
,
Arakawa:2015loc
;
Kuwabara:2021hyp
) but we hope that a geometric strategy will prove fruitful. For our purposes here, we will be content to consider examples for the low rank cases of
{\rm SL}_{2}
and
{\rm SL}_{3}
, and leave more extensive analysis of examples to future investigation.

It is known
Arakawa:2018egx
that
\mathbf{I}_{G}
is conformal—with critical central charge given by

c_{\mathbf{I}_{G}}=2\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}+(\rho,\rho^{\vee})~,

(141)

where
\rho
is the half sum of positive roots of
\mathfrak{g}
—
i.e.
, the Weyl vector—and
\rho^{\vee}
is the half sum of positive coroots. After imposing the
\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}
many constraints on (
133
), which fixed
\Delta_{\gamma_{i}}=2
we are left with
\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}
many unfixed background charges. These are fixed by requiring that the quadratic Feigin–Frenkel generator,
P_{1}
is quasiprimary. The residue
T_{(2)}P_{1}
is linear in the background charges and setting it to zero produces
2\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}
many linear equations for the background charges. However, these equations are not linearly independent. The expression for
P_{1}
is unchanged under the symmetry
\partial\phi\mapsto-\partial\delta
and
\partial\delta\mapsto\partial\phi
. Thus, the swaps
\alpha_{i}\mapsto-\beta_{i}
and
\beta_{i}\mapsto-\alpha_{i}
leaves the system of linear equations invariant. This symmetry cuts the system down to
\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}
many linear equations for
\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}
many unknowns. In the
{\rm SL}_{N}
case it is schematically clear that these equations are independent, leading to a unique assignment of background charges. For other choices of
G
we expect the same argument to hold, but we have not attempted a careful analysis. This assignment of background charges, in the
{\rm SL}_{N}
case, fixes the central charge to agree with (
141
) without additional input, and the general
G
case should behave similarly.

The expression (
133
), with the correctly chosen values of
\alpha_{i},\beta_{i}
, gives a free field realisation of the conformal vector of
\mathbf{I}_{G}
. On general grounds, we do not expect this to be a strong generator of
\mathbf{I}_{G}
, but rather it should arise as a composite of the other strong generators. We will verify this explicitly in the case
G=SL_{2}
below.

4.3
Free field realisation for
\mathbf{I}_{{\rm SL}_{2}}

Let us work through the
{\rm SL}_{2}
case as an illustrative example. The chiralisation of
\mathrm{KT}_{G}
is
\Pi_{{\rm SL}_{2}}
, which is strongly generated by

\gamma^{\pm\frac{1}{2}}\equiv e^{\pm\frac{1}{2}(\phi+\delta)}~,\quad b\equiv\frac{1}{2}\partial(\delta-\phi)~.

(142)

We first find the expression for
S=\beta^{ch}(P_{1})
, the generator of the Feigin–Frenkel centre. Our prescription above yields (
cf.

(
deBoer:1993iz
, Eqn. (3.4))
, with some change of conventions),

S=bb+\gamma+\partial b~.

(143)

We note here that rather than using this deformed Miura map, we could adopt a more bootstrap-inspired approach. The non-chiral image
\beta(P_{1})
is generated by
S=b^{2}+\gamma
, and we should allow for quantum corrections as the self-OPE of
bb+\gamma
is not regular. The most general corrected Ansatz is

S=bb+\gamma+\alpha\partial b~,

(144)

for some
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
. The normalisation is such that the Fourier mode
S_{(1)}
acts via the generalised eigenvalue
\frac{1}{4}\lambda(2\lambda+1)
on the Weyl module
\mathbb{V}_{\lambda}
. Imposing regularity of the
S\times S
OPE fixes
\alpha=1
and so fixes the corrected
S
to be precisely of the form returned by the deformed Miura map.

We expect only one additional strong generator, corresponding to the function
Y
on
\mathfrak{Z}_{{\rm SL}_{2}}
. This operator appears in the simple pole in the
S\times X
OPE, which is the chiral version of the extraction of the non-chiral
Y
from the Poisson bracket
\{S,X\}
. Indeed, defining
Y
as such, the OPEs close and we find a strongly generated vertex algebra with the following generators,

\begin{split}S&=bb+\gamma+\partial b~,\\
X&=\gamma^{-\frac{1}{2}}~,\\
Y&=-b\gamma^{-\frac{1}{2}}~.\end{split}

(145)

Comparing with (
56
), the expression for
Y
(and
X
) in terms of
\Pi_{G}
fields matches precisely the expression in terms of
\mathrm{KT}_{G}
previously with no quantum corrections, but this is probably not particularly meaningful. Indeed,
Y
defined here is not quasiprimary, but rather
Y+\frac{3}{4}X^{\prime}
is instead, and if we worked in terms of that quasiprimary instead there would indeed be quantum corrections.

The OPEs between these strong generators are given by

\begin{split}S(z)X(w)&\sim\frac{\frac{3}{4}X(w)}{(z-w)^{2}}+\frac{Y(w)}{z-w}~,\\
S(z)Y(w)&\sim\frac{\frac{3}{4}Y(w)}{(z-w)^{2}}+\frac{(SX)(w)}{z-w}~,\\
Y(z)X(w)&\sim\frac{\frac{1}{2}(XX)(w)}{z-w}~,\\
Y(z)Y(w)&\sim\frac{-\frac{1}{4}(XX)(w)}{(z-w)^{2}}-\frac{\frac{1}{4}(X\partial X)(w)}{z-w}~.\end{split}

(146)

The determinantal relation
D_{{\rm SL}_{2}}=SX^{2}-Y^{2}\overset{!}{=}1
is chiralised here to the relation

\hat{D}_{{\rm SL}_{2}}=SXX-YY-\tfrac{3}{2}\partial YX+\tfrac{3}{2}Y\partial X-\tfrac{3}{4}\partial X\partial X-\tfrac{1}{8}X\partial^{2}X\overset{!}{=}\mathbbm{1}~.

(147)

The generators
S
,
X
, and
Y
, together with the OPEs and relation above define a genuine strongly generated vertex algebra, which is a vertex subalgebra of
\Pi_{{\rm SL}_{2}}
.

As expected, the free-field conformal vector (with background charges
\alpha=\frac{3}{2}
and
\beta=-\frac{1}{2}
) can be expressed as the following composite operator in terms of the strong generators,

\begin{split}T_{0}=2S(\partial YX-Y\partial X)+3S\partial X\partial X+\tfrac{1}{2}Y\partial^{3}X-\partial SYX+\tfrac{5}{2}\partial S\partial XX-2\partial Y\partial Y&\\
+\tfrac{3}{2}\partial Y\partial^{2}X-\tfrac{3}{4}\partial^{2}X\partial^{2}X-\tfrac{3}{2}\partial^{2}Y\partial X-\tfrac{7}{12}\partial^{3}X\partial X-\tfrac{1}{2}\partial^{3}YX-\tfrac{1}{24}\partial^{4}XX&~.\end{split}

(148)

The central charge is
c=26
, which agrees with (
141
).

The equivariant affine
{\cal W}
-algebra for
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
was presented in terms of strong generators and their OPEs in
Arakawa:2018egx
. By performing the Drinfel’d–Sokolov reduction, which is still tractable in this case, one can show that the the resulting vertex algebra is isomorphic to our realisation. We will not review this computation here.

4.4
Interlude—a more universal centraliser

It is interesting to observe that the singular OPEs in (
146
) define, abstractly, a strongly generated vertex algebra that we denote
\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}_{{\rm SL}_{2}}
in which the vertex ideal generated by
\hat{D}_{{\rm SL}_{2}}-\mathbbm{1}
is not quotiented out. That is to say, the OPEs satisfy the Jacobi identities without removing any null states. The (now nonvanishing) operator
\hat{D}_{{\rm SL}_{2}}
is not only null, but is actually central in
\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}_{{\rm SL}_{2}}
. Consequently, there is a one parameter family of analogous chiral universal centralisers given by the quotient of
\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}_{{\rm SL}_{2}}
by the ideal
\hat{D}_{{\rm SL}_{2}}-\xi\mathbbm{1}
for
\xi\in\mathbb{C}
. For
\xi\neq 0
, these quotients are all isomorphic to
\mathbf{I}_{{\rm SL}_{2}}
, with the isomorphism realised by rescaling the strong generators
X
and
Y
. However, the centreless specialisation
\hat{D}_{{\rm SL}_{2}}=0
is not isomorphic to
\mathbf{I}_{{\rm SL}_{2}}
. This centreless specialisation seems to be something of an oddity; in particular it is not a chiral quantisation of
\mathfrak{Z}_{{\rm SL}_{2}}
since the determinant is set to zero.

The conformal vector
T_{0}
does not lift to one in
\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}_{{\rm SL}_{2}}
. For example, the cubic pole in the
T_{0}\times T_{0}
OPE is proportional to
\partial\hat{D}_{{\rm SL}_{2}}
(and the other poles are also polluted by the appearence of
\partial\hat{D}_{{\rm SL}_{2}}
and its derivatives). The Virasoro OPE only holds in the quotient. Indeed,
\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}_{{\rm SL}_{2}}
does not have a conformal vector. Any candidate conformal vector must be built out of the
S,X,Y
, and the OPEs amongst these generators are homogeneous in the number of
X
and
Y
fields appearing (
i.e.
, there is an outer
U(1)
symmetry of this vertex algebra under which
X
and
Y
have charge one and
S
has charge zero). Charge conservation then prevents any candidate conformal vector from realising the Virasoro OPE.

This outer
U(1)
action leads to a family of gradings on
\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}_{{\rm SL}_{2}}
—the OPEs are compatible with any grading that assign
|S|=2
,
|X|=\Delta
and
|Y|=\Delta+1
for any
\Delta\in\mathbb{Z}
. The simple quotient
\hat{D}_{{\rm SL}_{2}}-\xi\mathbbm{1}
, for
\xi\neq 0
, violates this grading for all choices except
\Delta=-1
. The centreless specialisation,
\xi=0
, however, is compatible with this grading for any
\Delta\in\mathbb{Z}
.

From a geometric point of view, this one parameter family arises from a closed embedding of
{\rm SL}_{N}
into the ambient space of
{\rm GL}_{N}
. The cotangent bundle
T^{\ast}{\rm GL}_{N}\cong\mathfrak{gl}_{N}^{*}\times{\rm GL}_{N}
has a splitting

T^{*}{\rm GL}_{N}\cong\mathfrak{gl}_{N}^{*}\times{\rm GL}_{N}\cong(\mathfrak{sl}_{N}^{*}\times\mathfrak{gl}^{*}_{1})\times{\rm GL}_{1}\times{\rm SL}_{N}\cong(T^{*}\mathbb{C}^{\times})\times(T^{*}{\rm SL}_{N})~,

(149)

arising from the splitting
\mathfrak{gl}_{N}\cong\mathfrak{gl}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{sl}_{N}
and
{\rm GL}_{N}\cong{\rm SL}_{N}\rtimes{\rm GL}_{1}
. Consider the Poisson subalgebra of
\mathbb{C}[T^{*}{\rm GL}_{N}]
generated by the
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{sl}_{N}^{*}]\otimes\mathbb{C}[{\rm GL}_{N}]
factors in the splitting. We denote its spectrum by
\widetilde{T}^{*}{\rm SL}_{N}
. As varieties, we have that
\widetilde{T}^{*}{\rm SL}_{N}\cong{{\rm GL}_{1}}\times T^{*}{\rm SL}_{N}\cong\mathfrak{sl}_{N}^{*}\times{\rm GL}_{N}
.

This thickened space inherits Hamiltonian left and right action of
{\rm SL}_{N}
, and we can perform the corresponding two-sided Kostant–Whittaker reduction of this ambient space. The
{\rm GL}_{1}
factor of the splitting is
N_{L}\times N_{R}
invariant, and lies in the (intersection)
\overline{\mu}_{L}^{-1}(\chi)\cap\overline{\mu}_{R}^{-1}(\chi)
. Therefore, the two-sided reduction will be isomorphic to
\mathbb{C}^{\times}\times\mathfrak{Z}_{{\rm SL}_{N}}
, and we denote this space by
\widetilde{\mathfrak{Z}}_{{\rm SL}_{N}}
. It is naturally a scheme over
S_{f}
by extending the structure morphism of
\mathfrak{Z}_{{\rm SL}_{N}}
. The cotangent bundle
T^{*}{\rm SL}_{N}
is a closed, Poisson subvariety of
\widetilde{T}^{*}{\rm SL}_{N}
, so by functoriality of reduction,
\mathfrak{Z}_{{\rm SL}_{N}}
is a closed, Poisson, subvariety of
\widetilde{\mathfrak{Z}}_{{\rm SL}_{N}}
.

There exist a family of
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
actions on
T^{*}{\rm GL}_{N}
, which acts as uniform rescaling of the vector space
\mathfrak{gl}_{N}^{*}
and as left multiplication by a constant diagonal matrix on
{\rm GL}_{N}
. Under these
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
actions, the linear functions on
\mathfrak{gl}_{N}^{*}
can be assigned unit weight, in which case the Poisson bracket has degree
-1
, while the weights of the matrix entries,
g_{ij}
, which generate
\mathbb{C}[{\rm GL}_{N}]
are unfixed. Any assignment
|g_{ij}|=\Delta
for all
i,j=1,\dots,N
is compatible with the Poisson structure. The fundamental invariants of
\mathfrak{sl}_{N}
,
(P_{i})_{i=1}^{N}
will have weight
|P_{i}|=i+1
for
i=1,\dots,N
,
i.e.
, the weight is equal to their degree. The thickened space
\widetilde{T}^{*}{\rm SL}_{N}
inherits this grading.

Similarly, the fibres of
\widetilde{\mathfrak{Z}}_{{\rm SL}_{N}}
will inherit the grading on
\widetilde{T}^{*}{\rm SL}_{N}
. Under Kostant–Whittaker reduction the
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
weights are shifted by the weight associated to the principal
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-embedding. Consider, specifically, the bottow row
g_{Nj}
whose weights under the
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
action must satisfy
|g_{Nj}|=|g_{N1}|+(j-1)
. Denoting
g_{N1}
by
X
, as is our convention, and denoting its weight by
|X|=\Delta
, any grading where
|g_{Nj}|=\Delta+(j-1)
is compatible with the Poisson bracket.

Picking out a closed subvariety by imposing the determinantal relation
D_{{\rm GL}_{N}}\overset{!}{=}1
is incompatible with this grading—unless
\Delta=1-N
. Note that any such relation
D_{{\rm GL}_{N}}\overset{!}{=}\xi
, for
\xi\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
forces
\Delta=1-N
. The closed subvariety satisfying
D_{{\rm GL}_{N}}=1
gives a closed embedding
\mathfrak{Z}_{{\rm SL}_{N}}\hookrightarrow\widetilde{\mathfrak{Z}}_{{\rm SL}_{N}}
. In fact, any subvariety
D_{{\rm GL}_{N}}=\xi
gives a closed embedding of
\mathfrak{Z}_{\rm SL_{N}}
inside
\widetilde{\mathfrak{Z}}_{{\rm SL}_{N}}
as schemes over
S_{f}
.

The thickened space,
\widetilde{T}^{*}{\rm SL}_{N}
has a closure isomorphic to
\mathbb{C}\times{\rm SL}_{N}
. Performing Kostant–Whittaker reduction on this space gives a closure of
\widetilde{\mathfrak{Z}}_{{\rm SL}_{N}}
. In this closure, we have an additional subvariety with vanishing ideal
D_{{\rm GL}_{N}}
. This subvariety of determinant zero matrices is not isomorphic to
\mathfrak{Z}_{{\rm SL}_{N}}
and seems fairly exotic.

The geometric construction above reflects many of the properties we have seen in
\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}_{{\rm SL}_{2}}
, namely the auxiliary grading and one-parameter family of quotients. Let us present a schematic chiral version of this construction that should explain the existence of this more general vertex algebra.

Let
\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{{\rm GL}_{N},-N}
be CDOs on
{\rm GL}_{N}
at level
-N
; these admit a splitting
\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{{\rm GL}_{N},-N}\cong\mathcal{D}^{ch}(\mathbb{C}^{\times})\otimes\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{{\rm SL}_{N},-N}
. This is the chiral version of the splitting (
149
). Functions on
T^{*}\mathbb{C}^{\times}
are generated by
D_{{\rm GL}_{N}}^{\pm}
and the fibre coordinate
p
, with Poisson bracket
\{p,D_{{\rm GL}_{N}}^{\pm}\}=\pm D_{{\rm GL}_{N}}^{\pm}
. Abusing notation, the CDOs on
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
are strongly generated by
D_{{\rm GL}_{N}}^{\pm}
and
p
with OPE

p(z)D_{{\rm GL}_{N}}(w)^{\pm}\sim\frac{D_{{\rm GL}_{N}}^{\pm}(w)}{z-w}~.

(150)

Let
\overline{\mathcal{D}^{ch}}_{SL_{N},-N}\subset\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{{\rm GL}_{N},-N}
be the vertex subalgebra corresponding to
\mathrm{ker}~p_{(0)}
. It is strongly generated by the strong generators of
\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{{\rm SL}_{N},-N}
and by
D_{{\rm GL}_{N}}
. To recover
\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{{\rm SL}_{N},-N}
we quotient out by the vertex ideal generated by
D_{{\rm GL}_{N}}-\mathbbm{1}
. Under Drinfel’d–Sokolov reduction, the
\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{{\rm SL}_{N},-N}
subalgebra reduces to
\mathbf{I}_{{\rm SL}_{N}}
. Meanwhile, the generator
D_{{\rm GL}_{N}}
gets corrected to
\hat{D}_{{\rm GL}_{N}}
, which is the composite expression for the determinant inside
\mathbf{I}_{{\rm SL}_{N}}
.

Thus, the Drinfel’d–Sokolov reduction of
\overline{D}_{{\rm SL}_{N},-N}
yields
\mathbf{I}_{{\rm SL}_{N}}\otimes\langle\hat{D}_{{\rm GL}_{N}}\rangle
, where
\langle\hat{D}_{{\rm GL}_{N}}\rangle
is a commutative vertex algebra generated by
\hat{D}_{{\rm GL}_{N}}
and its inverse. The reduction has a one-parameter family of quotients by setting
\hat{D}_{{\rm GL}_{N}}=\xi\mathbbm{1}
for
\xi\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
. Each of these quotients is isomorphic to
\mathbf{I}_{{\rm SL}_{N}}
, and we can identify this reduction as the more universal centraliser
\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}_{{\rm SL}_{N}}
.

4.5
Free field realisation for
\mathbf{I}_{{\rm SL}_{3}}

We return to another example, this time in rakn two. Once again, our starting point is to find expressions for the Feigin–Frenkel generators
S
and
W
. Like in the
{\rm SL}_{2}
case, we can utilise our deformed Miura approach to produce the right expressions. For

\begin{split}S&=\frac{1}{2}(b_{1}^{2}+b_{2}^{2}-b_{1}b_{2}+\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2})~,\\
W&=\frac{1}{3}(b_{1}b_{2}(b_{1}-b_{2})+\gamma_{1}b_{2}-\gamma_{2}b_{1}~)~,\end{split}

(151)

and by performing the tic-tac-toe procedure as in
(
deBoer:1993iz
, Eqn. (3.10))
, we arrive at chiral versions given by

\begin{split}S&=\frac{1}{2}(b_{1}^{2}+b_{2}^{2}-b_{1}b_{2}+\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2})+\frac{1}{2}\partial(b_{1}+b_{2})~,\\

&\phantom{=}+\frac{1}{6}(b_{2}\partial b_{1}-b_{1}\partial b_{2})+\frac{1}{6}\partial^{2}(b_{1}-b_{2})~.\end{split}

(152)

Alternatively, we can start with an Ansatz based on the finite-dimensional expression and add quantum corrections while demanding regularity of the OPEs. The appropriate Ansätze are

(153)

where
\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{C}
. The corrections are constrained to satisfy the requirement that
S
be invariant under the outer-automorphism
b_{1}\mapsto b_{2}
,
e_{1}\mapsto e_{2}
while
W
is negated. Requiring regularity of the

\alpha_{1}=1/2

and

\alpha_{2}=\frac{1}{6}~,\qquad\alpha_{3}=\frac{1}{3}~,\qquad\alpha_{4}=\frac{1}{6}~,\qquad\alpha_{5}=\frac{1}{6}~,

(154)

which agrees exactly with the expressions arising from

and

\begin{split}S(z)X(w)&\sim\frac{\frac{4}{3}X}{(z-w)^{2}}+\frac{-Y}{z-w}~,\\

&+\frac{-(WY)+\frac{1}{6}(SZ)+\frac{1}{6}(\partial SY)-\frac{1}{4}(SSX)}{z-w}~,\end{split}

(156)

where we have suppressed the dependence of the residues on the coordinate
w
for clarity. Similarly, for the OPEs amongst
X
,
Y
, and

, we have

\displaystyle Y(z)X(w)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{-\frac{2}{3}(XX)}{z-w}~,

(157)

\displaystyle Y(z)Y(w)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{-\frac{4}{9}(XX)}{(z-w)^{2}}+\frac{-\frac{4}{9}(X\partial X)}{z-w}~,

(158)

\displaystyle Z(z)X(w)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{\frac{1}{9}(XX)}{(z-w)^{2}}+\frac{-\frac{5}{6}(YX)-\frac{4}{9}(X\partial X)}{z-w}~,

(159)

\displaystyle Z(z)Y(w)

fundamentals of the remaining
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
, along with a Feigin–Frenkel generator. The extra weight
+\frac{1}{2}
generators can be written as
Y^{a}=J^{ab}X_{b}
, which in terms of free fields gives

\begin{split}Y^{+}&=b\gamma^{-\frac{1}{2}}e_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}~.\\
Y^{-}&\coloneqq f_{1,(0)}Y^{+}=b(b+\partial\delta_{1})\gamma^{-\frac{1}{2}}e_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}+\gamma^{\frac{1}{2}}e_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}~.\end{split}

(179)

Meanwhile, the Feigin–Frenkel generator
S=J^{ab}J_{ab}
is, of course, given as in
\mathbf{I}_{{\rm SL}_{2}}
by

S=b^{2}+\gamma+\partial b~.

(180)

The
X^{a}
,
Y^{a}
, and
S
also strongly generate
\mathbf{W}_{{\rm SL}_{2}}
, with their singular OPEs being given by

\begin{split}S(z)X^{a}(w)&=\frac{\frac{3}{4}X^{a}}{(z-w)^{2}}+\frac{Y^{a}}{z-w}~,\\
S(z)Y^{a}(w)&=\frac{\frac{3}{4}Y^{a}}{(z-w)^{2}}+\frac{SX^{a}}{z-w}~,\\
Y^{a}(z)X^{b}(w)&=\frac{\frac{1}{2}(X^{a}X^{b})}{z-w}~,\\
Y^{a}(z)Y^{b}(w)&=-\frac{\frac{1}{4}(X^{a}X^{b})}{(z-w)^{2}}+\frac{\epsilon^{ab}\frac{1}{2}\big(Y^{c}X_{d}-\frac{1}{2}\partial X^{c}X_{d}\big)-\frac{1}{4}\partial X^{a}X^{b}}{z-w}~.\end{split}

(181)

The null relation in this case is a lift of (
147
) in
\mathbf{I}_{{\rm SL}_{2}}
, and is given by

(X^{a}Y_{a})+\frac{1}{2}\big(\partial X^{a}X_{a}\big)=\mathbbm{1}~,

(182)

which can also be thought of as the chiralised version of the determinantal relation in the equivariant Slodowy slice
{\rm SL}_{2}\times S_{f}
.

In this presentation, the affine currents
e_{1}
,
h_{1}
,
f_{1}
are realised as composites,

\begin{split}e_{1}=&SX^{+}X^{+}-Y^{+}Y^{+}-\frac{3}{2}\big(X^{+}\partial Y^{+}-\partial X^{+}Y^{+})-\frac{3}{4}\partial X^{+}\partial X^{+}-\frac{1}{8}X^{+}\partial^{2}X^{+}~,\\
h_{1}=&-2SX^{-}X^{+}+2Y^{-}Y^{+}+\frac{3}{2}(X^{-}\partial Y^{+}+X^{+}\partial Y^{-}-\partial X^{-}Y^{+}-\partial X^{+}Y^{-})\\
&+\frac{3}{2}\partial X^{+}\partial X^{-}+\frac{1}{4}X^{+}\partial^{2}X^{-}~,\\
f_{1}=&-SX^{-}X^{-}+Y^{-}Y^{-}+\frac{3}{2}\big(X^{-}\partial Y^{-}-\partial X^{-}Y^{-}\big)+\frac{3}{4}\partial X^{-}\partial X^{-}+\frac{1}{8}X^{-}\partial^{2}X^{-}~.\end{split}

(183)

Similarly, the stress tensor
T_{1}
is composite and takes the form

\begin{split}T_{1}&=-S\big(\partial X^{a}X_{a}\big)+\partial Y^{a}Y_{a}+\frac{3}{2}\big(\partial X^{a}\partial Y_{a}\big)+\frac{3}{8}\partial^{2}X^{a}\partial X_{a}+\frac{1}{6}\partial^{3}X^{a}X_{a}~.\end{split}

(184)

(The conformal vector can also be expressed in terms of just the currents and the
X^{a}
, but the form is not particularly illuminating.) This second presentation of the cap agrees precisely with the expressions appearing in the appendix of
Arakawa:2018egx
after making the identifications
S\mapsto-S
,
X^{i}\mapsto-\begin{pmatrix}c\\
a\end{pmatrix}
and
Y^{i}\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}d\\
b\end{pmatrix}
.

5.2.2
The cylinder

Given
\mathbf{W}_{{\rm SL}_{2}}
, we can construct the cylinder VOA as a subalgebra
\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{{\rm SL}_{2},-2}\subset\mathbf{W}_{{\rm SL}_{2}}\otimes\Pi_{{\rm SL}_{2}}
. This involves first defining a new
V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
subalgebra inside
\mathbf{W}_{{\rm SL}_{2}}\otimes\Pi_{{\rm SL}_{2}}
—now with generators
e_{2},h_{2},f_{2}
—which commutes with the previous affine current algebra.
We further define generators

\begin{split}X^{a+}&\coloneqq X^{a}e_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}~,\\
X^{a-}&\coloneqq f_{2,(0)}X^{a+}~.\end{split}

(185)

Replacing the
X^{a}
by their free field expressions from the previous subsection, we have

\begin{split}X^{++}&=\gamma^{-\frac{1}{2}}e_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}e_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}~,\\
X^{-+}&=-(b+\partial\delta_{1})\gamma^{-\frac{1}{2}}e_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}e_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}~,\\
X^{--}&=\big((b+\partial\delta_{1})(b+\partial\delta_{2})+\big)\gamma^{-\frac{1}{2}}e_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}e_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}~.\end{split}

(186)

The expressions for
X^{-+}
and
X^{+-}
are related by performing the permutation
1\leftrightarrow 2
on all subscripts. This arises from the manifest permutation symmetry of our construction.

The generators
X^{ab}
live in the bifundamental representation of
(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})_{1}\times(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})_{2}
, with action given by the zero modes of
e_{i}
,
h_{i}
,
f_{i}
. The OPEs between the
X^{ab}
are regular; the only non-trivial OPEs involve
e_{i}
,
h_{i}
,
f_{i}
.

The (central) null ideal in the cap is now lifted to

\begin{vmatrix}X^{++}&X^{+-}\\
X^{-+}&X^{--}\end{vmatrix}=X^{++}X^{--}-X^{+-}X^{-+}\overset{!}{=}\mathbbm{1}~,

(187)

and the commutative subalgebra generated by the
X^{ab}
is identified with the commutative vertex subalgebra
{\cal O}(J_{\infty}{\rm SL}_{2})
,
i.e.
, the chiralisation of
{\cal O}({\rm SL}_{2})
inside
\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{{\rm SL}_{2},-2}
. The
X^{ab}
along with the currents,
e_{1},h_{1},f_{1}
, are strong generators of
\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{{\rm SL}_{2},-2}
.

The conformal vector is again composite, now being given by

T_{2}=J^{ab}\partial X_{a}\,\!^{c}X_{cb}+\partial^{2}X^{ab}X_{ab}+2\partial X^{ab}\partial X_{ab}~.

(188)

Replacing all strong generators with their free field expressions, this reduces exactly to the expression for the canonical
T_{2}
in terms of lattice bosons with background charges.

5.2.3
Three punctures

Now we reach the traditional starting point of the free field realisation of
Beem:2019tfp
, the three punctured sphere. The vertex algebra
\mathbf{V}_{{\rm SL}_{2},3}
is isomorphic to the symplectic bosons associated to the
\mathbf{2}\otimes\mathbf{2}\otimes\mathbf{2}
representation of
(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})_{1}\times(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})_{2}\times(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})_{3}
. Repeating the same steps that are by now becoming familiar, we define a
V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
subalgebra inside
\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{{\rm SL}_{2},-2}\otimes\Pi_{{\rm SL}_{2}}
, generated by
e_{3}
,
h_{3}
,
f_{3}
. We further define trifundamental generators

X^{ab+}\coloneqq X^{ab}e_{3}^{\frac{1}{2}}~,\quad X^{ab-}\coloneqq f_{3,(0)}X^{ab+}~,

(189)

In terms of the full free field realisation, these generators are given by:

\begin{split}X^{+++}&=\gamma^{-\frac{1}{2}}e_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}e_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}e_{3}^{\frac{1}{2}}~,\\
X^{-++}&=-(b+\partial\delta_{1})\gamma^{-\frac{1}{2}}e_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}e_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}e_{3}^{\frac{1}{2}}~,\\
X^{--+}&=\big((b+\partial\delta_{1})(b+\partial\delta_{2})+\gamma\big)\gamma^{-\frac{1}{2}}e_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}e_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}e_{3}^{\frac{1}{2}}~,\\
X^{---}&=-\big((b+\partial\delta_{1})(b+\partial\delta_{2})(b+\partial\delta_{3})+(b+\partial\delta_{1}+\partial\delta_{2}+\partial\delta_{3})\gamma-\partial\gamma\big)\gamma^{-\frac{1}{2}}e_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}e_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}e_{3}^{-\frac{1}{2}}~,\\
\end{split}

(190)

where the other generators are related to the ones displayed by permutations of the lattice variables. The OPEs can be computed direclty, and now take the (expected) exceptionally simple form,

X^{abc}(z)X^{a^{\prime}b^{\prime}c^{\prime}}(w)\sim\frac{\epsilon^{aa^{\prime}}\epsilon^{bb^{\prime}}\epsilon^{cc^{\prime}}}{z-w}~.

(191)

There are no non-trivial null relations amongst the
X^{abc}
, but the relations in the cylinder now lift to relations identifying the various current subalgebras as composites of the
X^{abc}
. In particular, we now have

\begin{split}J^{ab}_{1}=\frac{1}{2}X^{ai_{1}j_{1}}X^{b}_{i_{1}j_{1}}~,\\
J^{ab}_{2}=\frac{1}{2}X^{i_{1}aj_{1}}X_{i_{1}\phantom{b}j_{1}}^{\phantom{i_{1}}b}~,\\
J^{ab}_{3}=\frac{1}{2}X^{i_{1}j_{1}a}X_{i_{1}j_{1}}^{\phantom{i_{1}j_{1}}b}~.\end{split}

(192)

The stress tensor
T_{3}
is just the standard symplectic boson stress tensor, which again reduces to the canonical lattice conformal vector with background charges.

5.2.4
Four Punctures

The free field realisation of
\mathbf{V}_{{\rm SL}_{2},4}
inside
\mathbf{V}_{{\rm SL}_{2},3}\otimes\Pi_{{\rm SL}_{2}}
can be found in
Beem:2019tfp
, but we will revisit the computation in the present context. We again realise a new
V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
subalgebra inside
\mathbf{V}_{{\rm SL}_{2},3}\otimes\Pi_{{\rm SL}_{2}}
, and as above, adopt a basis for the currents
J^{ab}_{4}
.

The quadrifundamental generators take the usual form,

X^{a_{1}a_{2}a_{3}+}\coloneqq X^{a_{1}a_{2}a_{3}}e_{4}^{\frac{1}{2}}~,\quad X^{a_{1}a_{2}a_{3}-}\coloneqq f_{4,(0)}X^{a_{1}a_{2}a_{3}+}~.

(193)

which can be expressed entirely in terms of free fields as follows

\begin{split}X^{++++}&=\gamma^{-\frac{1}{2}}e_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}e_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}e_{3}^{\frac{1}{2}}e_{4}^{\frac{1}{2}}~,\\
X^{-+++}&=-(b+\partial\delta_{1})\gamma^{-\frac{1}{2}}e_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}e_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}e_{3}^{\frac{1}{2}}e_{4}^{\frac{1}{2}}~,\\
X^{--++}&=\big((b+\partial\delta_{1})(b+\partial\delta_{2})+\gamma\big)\gamma^{-\frac{1}{2}}e_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}e_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}e_{3}^{\frac{1}{2}}e_{4}^{\frac{1}{2}}~,\\
X^{---+}&=-\big((b+\partial\delta_{1})(b+\partial\delta_{2})(b+\partial\delta_{3})+(b+\partial\delta_{1}+\partial\delta_{2}+\partial\delta_{3})\gamma-\partial\gamma\big)\gamma^{-\frac{1}{2}}e_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}e_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}e_{3}^{-\frac{1}{2}}e_{4}^{\frac{1}{2}}~,\\
X^{----}&=\bigg(\prod_{i=1}^{4}(b+\partial\delta_{i})+\sum_{i<j}^{4}(b+\partial\delta_{i})(b+\partial\delta_{j})\gamma-2(b+\partial\delta_{1}+\partial\delta_{2}+\partial\delta_{3}+\partial\delta_{4})(b+\partial\gamma^{\frac{1}{2}}\gamma^{-\frac{1}{2}})\gamma\\
&-2(bb+\partial b)\gamma+b\partial\gamma+\gamma^{2}+\partial^{2}\gamma\bigg)\gamma^{-\frac{1}{2}}e_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}e_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}e_{3}^{-\frac{1}{2}}e_{4}^{-\frac{1}{2}}~.\end{split}

(194)

Again one finds free-field expressions for the unlisted generators by applying the appropriate permutation on subscripts. The OPE between the generators is

X^{a_{1}a_{2}a_{3}a_{4}}(z)X^{b_{1}b_{2}b_{3}b_{4}}(w)\sim\frac{2\prod_{i=1}^{4}\epsilon^{a_{i}b_{i}}}{(z-w)^{2}}+\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{4}\prod_{j\neq i}\epsilon^{a_{j}b_{j}}J^{(a_{i}b_{i})}(w)}{z-w}~,

(195)

and taken together with the three sets of
V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
currents these are strong generators of
L_{-2}(\mathfrak{d}_{4})
, the simple quotient of
V^{-2}(\mathfrak{d}_{4})
.
The null ideals of this vertex algebra and their relation to Joseph’s minimal realisations
Joseph:1974min
were investigated in
Beem:2019tfp
.

5.2.5
Five Punctures

Continuing further, we come to a realisation of
\mathbf{V}_{{\rm SL}_{2},5}
. We define the usual
e_{5},h_{5},f_{5}
currents and the generators
X^{a_{1}a_{2}a_{3}a_{4}a_{5}}
as before. In terms of free-fields, the expressions are somewhat burdensome to write down. (Additionally, it is only the state
X^{-----}
that is novel. All other states can be constructed (by appealing to symmetry) from the free-field realisations we have already constructed.)

The nontrivial OPEs are the OPEs of the pentafundamental generators
X^{a_{1}a_{2}a_{3}a_{4}a_{5}}
amongst themselves, and at this stage the OPEs are very intricate and so to keep the expressions to a manageable length we will suppress all conformal descendants,
i.e.
we only show contributions from conformal primaries in the OPE. We then have

\begin{split}X^{a_{1}a_{2}a_{3}a_{4}a_{5}}(z)X^{b_{1}b_{2}b_{3}b_{4}b_{5}}(w)&\sim\frac{4\prod_{i=1}^{5}\epsilon^{a_{i}b_{i}}}{(z-w)^{3}}+\frac{2\sum_{i=1}^{5}\prod_{j\neq i}^{5}\epsilon^{a_{j}b_{j}}J_{i}^{(a_{i}b_{i})}(w)}{(z-w)^{2}}+\\
&\frac{1}{z-w}\bigg(\sum_{i<j}^{5}\prod_{k\neq i,j}^{5}\epsilon^{a_{k}b_{k}}(J_{i}^{(a_{i}b_{i})}J_{j}^{(a_{j}b_{j})})(w)-2\prod_{i=1}^{5}\epsilon^{a_{i}b_{i}}(S+\tfrac{1}{4}T_{5})(w)\bigg)~.\end{split}

(196)

Here, we have the appearance of a new primary
S+\frac{1}{4}T_{5}
. In general, one can show that the state
S-\frac{6}{c_{s}}
is a primary for
T_{s}
, with the appropriate value of the central charge.

Finding the exact expression for the generators of the null ideal is now somewhat involved, though largely a book-keeping exercise using the free field expressions. We will not reproduce them here or in the next example.

5.2.6
Six Punctures

We conclude with the realisation of
\mathbf{V}_{\mathfrak{a}_{1},6}
, which has strong generators
(e_{i},h_{i},f_{i})
for
i=1,2,\dots,6
, the hexafundamental
X^{a_{1}a_{2}a_{3}a_{4}a_{5}a_{6}}

T_{6}
. The nontrivial OPE is the
X\times X
self-OPE of the hexafundamental, which takes the form

\begin{split}X^{a_{1}a_{2}a_{3}a_{4}a_{5}a_{6}}(z)X^{b_{1}b_{2}b_{3}b_{4}b_{5}b_{6}}(w)&\sim\frac{8\prod_{i=1}^{6}\epsilon^{a_{i}b_{i}}}{(z-w)^{4}}+4\frac{\sum_{i}\prod_{j\neq i}\epsilon^{a_{j}b_{j}}J^{(a_{i}b_{i})(w)}}{(z-w)^{3}}+\\
&\frac{1}{(z-w)^{2}}\bigg(2\sum_{i<j}\prod_{k\neq i,j}^{6}\epsilon^{a_{k}b_{k}}(J^{(a_{i}b_{i})}J^{(a_{j}b_{j})})(w)-6\prod_{i=1}^{6}\epsilon^{a_{i}b_{i}}(S+\tfrac{3}{17}T_{6})\bigg)+\\
&\frac{1}{(z-w)}\bigg(\sum_{i<j<k}\prod_{l\neq i,j,k}\epsilon^{a_{l}b_{l}}(J^{(a_{i}b_{i})}J^{(a_{j}b_{j})}J^{(a_{k}b_{k})})\\
&-2\sum_{i}^{6}\prod_{k\neq i}\epsilon^{a_{k}b_{k}}\bigg((S+\tfrac{3}{17}T_{6})J^{(a_{i}b_{i})}+A^{a_{i}b_{i}}\bigg)\end{split}

(197)

where there appear novel primary operators,
A^{a_{i}b_{i}}
, given by

A_{i}^{a_{i}b_{i}}=\bigg((J_{i})^{a_{i}}_{c}(\partial J_{i})^{cb_{i}}-\frac{1}{8}T(J_{i})^{a_{i}b_{i}}-\frac{5}{48}(\partial^{2}J_{i})^{a_{i}b_{i}}\bigg)~.

(198)

It may be noteworthy that there seems to be some numerology involved in the residues appearing in the OPE. This is not entirely surprising, since the OPEs in the free field realisation are computed by Wick contractions—which are manifestly of a combinatorial nature. It would be interesting to look for a more elegant combinatorial description of these OPEs that makes the
n
-punctured generalisation immediate, but we will not pursue this for now.

6
Concluding remarks

6.1
Screening charges

In many instances, free-field realisations of vertex algebras are accompanied by certain screening charges whose kernel recovers the subalgebra of interest (
cf.

Adamovic2019
;
Adamovic2003115
;
deBoer:1996ti
;
Frenkel:2007
). One can then often think of a free field realisation of a vertex algebra as a resolution whose first differential is the screening charge.

In the free field realisation of Wakimoto modules, for example, such screening charges exist and admit a geometric interpretation
Frenkel:geo2008
. Namely, they arise from the Cousin–Grothendieck spectral sequence associated to the Bruhat stratification on the flag variety; the free-field realisation is a chiral analogue of the BGG resolution for
\mathfrak{g}
-modules.

A slight modification of the arguments in
loc. cit.
can be applied to our case. First, we fix some partial ordering
w_{0}\geq w_{1}\geq w_{2}\geq\dots w_{N}
on the Weyl group
W
, with
w_{0}
the longest element as before. Then
G
has a stratification

G=\underset{i}{\bigsqcup}\,G^{i}~,

(199)

where
G^{i}=Bw_{i}B
and
G^{0}\equiv G^{*}
. Let
j_{i}:G^{i}\hookrightarrow G
denote the immersions of the strata. If
M
is a
D
-module on
G
, then the Cousin–Grothendieck spectral sequence tells us that
M
has a resolution

0\rightarrow M\hookrightarrow j_{0*}j_{0}^{!}M\xrightarrow{\delta_{0}}j_{1*}j_{1}^{!}M\xrightarrow{\delta_{1}}\dots\xrightarrow{\delta_{N-1}}j_{N*}j_{N}^{!}M\rightarrow 0~.

(200)

In particular, if
M={\cal D}_{G}
is the sheaf of differential operators on
G
, then
j_{0}^{!}{\cal D}_{G}
can be identified with the sheaf of differential operators on the big cell,
{\cal D}_{G^{*}}
. From
(
Arakawa:tdo2011
, Theorem 5.2.)
, this lifts to a resolution of the sheaf of (critical level) chiral differential operators on
G
,
\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{G,\kappa_{c}}
. Taking global sections, we have a complex of vertex algebras
(C^{\bullet},\delta_{\bullet})
, with
C^{0}=\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{G^{*},\kappa_{c}}(G^{*})
.

To construct a resolution of the chiral universal centraliser, we can perform the two-sided Drinfel’d–Sokolov reduction on
\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{G}(G)
. We form the tri-complex
(C^{\bullet\bullet\bullet},\delta,d_{L},d_{R})
, where
d_{L}
and
d_{R}
are the BRST differentials computing the left and right DS reductions, respectively. Passing to
\delta
-cohomology and then
d_{L}
and
d_{R}
cohomology will, by construction, recover the chiral universal centraliser in cohomological degree zero.

Note that the DS reduction subcomplex is acyclic away from degree zero (see
Frenkel:lan2006
for more detail), so a spectral sequence argument implies that we can algernatively compute the
d_{L}
and
d_{R}
cohomology first before computing the
\delta
-cohomology and still realise the chiral universal centraliser in degree zero. Since
G^{*}\cong N\times T\times N
, the two-sided DS reduction gives
\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{T}(T)
in degree zero. The resulting complex,
(\tilde{C}^{\bullet},\delta)
has zeroth term
\tilde{C}^{0}=\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{T}(T)
, with

H^{0}(\tilde{C},\delta)\equiv\mathrm{ker}~\delta_{0}\cong\mathbf{I}^{cl}_{G}~.

(201)

Of course,
\mathcal{D}^{ch}_{T}(T)
is isomorphic to the free field vertex algebra
\Pi_{G}
, so the above presentation suggests a reinterpretation of our free-field realisation along with some kind of screening charge
\delta_{0}
,
i.e.
, the restriction of the Cousin–Grothendieck differential. However, the above analysis at this level of abstraction does not lead to an explicit expression for a screening charge in the usual sense. We leave a more detailed and concrete analysis for future work.

6.2
Monopoles and Abelianisation

Our realisation of the universal centraliser in terms of functions on the Kostant–Toda lattice is somewhat evocative of Abelianisation-type constructions, in that
\mathrm{KT}_{G}\cong T^{*}(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{\mathrm{rk}\,\mathfrak{g}}\cong T^{*}\check{T}
and
T^{\ast}\mathbb{C}^{\times}
may be identified as the Coulomb branch of pure
U(1)

\mathcal{N}=4
gauge theory in three dimensions. Indeed, the construction of Braverman–Finkelberg–Nakajima for the Coulomb branches of gauge theories comes with an Abelianisation map. Let
\check{T}
be the Langlands dual of the maximal torus
T
, and let
\mathrm{Gr}_{\check{T}}=\check{T}({\cal K})/\check{T}({\cal O})
be the affine Grassmannian. Since
\check{T}
is abelian, the affine Grassmannian is just a collection of points, parameterised by the coweights of
\check{T}
. There is a closed embedding
i:\mathrm{Gr}_{\check{T}}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Gr}_{\check{G}}
, which gives rise to a dominant morphism
Braverman:2016wma
,

\mathfrak{Z}_{G}\cong\mathrm{Spec}~H^{\check{G}({\cal O})}_{\bullet}(\mathrm{Gr}_{\check{G}})\rightarrow\mathrm{Spec}~H^{\check{T}({\cal O})}_{\bullet}(\mathrm{Gr}_{\check{T}})/\!\!/W\cong(\mathfrak{t}\times\check{T})/\!\!/W~.

(202)

This is actually an isomorphism away from the closed subspace
\mathfrak{t}^{\circ}=\bigcup_{\alpha\in\Delta}\mathfrak{t}_{\alpha}
, where
\mathfrak{t}_{\alpha}
is the root hyperplane
\alpha=0
. As a result,
\mathfrak{Z}_{G}
and
(\mathfrak{t}\times\check{T})/\!\!/W
are birational. In
Bezrukavnikov:2003
, this was used to construct the universal centraliser in terms of a blow up of
(\mathfrak{t}\times\check{T})/\!\!/W
.

In the physics literature,
(\mathfrak{t}\times\check{T})/\!\!/W
is just the classical description of the Coulomb branch, which receives quantum corrections from monopole operators
Bullimore:2015lsa
. It is tempting, then, to attempt to interpret our free field realisations (and the corresponding finite-dimensional constructions) as an instance of this Abelianisation paradigm. However, such an interpretation is not immediately forthcoming, as in particular for us the Poisson structure on the “Abelian” space (
\mathrm{KT}_{G}
) is
not corrected
, by monopole operators or otherwise.

A construction of Darboux coordinates by Kato has appeared in
Kato:2020jup
, however this is for the
K
-theoretic Coulomb branch of pure gauge theory, which is not the universal centraliser.

Nevertheless, it would be interesting to investigate the possibility of a link between the two pictures. This could help frame our construction in terms that are more in keeping with both physics and geometric representation theory, as well as offering the possibility for generalisation away from the case of pure gauge theory.

6.3
Open questions

In addition to the various conjectures outlined in the main body of the papers, there are a number of issues that remain open to which we hope someone (ourselves or otherwise) will return.

First of all, in starting our construction of chiral algebras of class
{\cal S}
with the universal centraliser (of type
{\mathrm{S}L}_{2}
), it is clear that the vertex algebras
\mathbf{V}_{{\mathrm{S}L}_{2},s}
are sensitive to the choice of the Lie
group
. Indeed, one could replicate the construction, starting with
\mathbf{I}_{{\mathrm{P}GL}_{2}}
, and recover a series of vertex algebras
\mathbf{V}_{{\mathrm{P}GL}_{2},s}
which are not isomorphic to
\mathbf{V}_{{\mathrm{S}L}_{2},s}
. It would seem natural to expect the
\mathbf{V}_{{\mathrm{P}GL}_{2},s}
to be finite quotients of the
\mathbf{V}_{{\mathrm{S}L}_{2},s}
with respect to the
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
action of the centre. However, it is unclear whether passing to the
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
invariants in
\mathbf{V}_{SL_{2},s}
commutes with passing to the Zhu’s
C_{2}
algebra.

The most natural progression is to continue the free-field program for
\mathbf{V}_{G,s}
to groups of higher rank. For
{\rm SL}_{3}
, we already have a concrete presentation for the chiral universal centraliser and one could hope in this manner to obtain a presentation of the equivariant affine
{\cal W}
-algebra as a strongly generated vertex algebra. However, the calculations for
{\rm SL}_{2}
already become unwieldy at six punctures. To establish the free field realisation on stronger footing we would need to develop a more abstract formalism—rather than the algorithmic approach we have taken so far. We hope to develop such a formalism in upcoming work
BBS:unpub
.

The free-field realisations naturally give rise to a construction of a class of vertex algebra modules. Namely, there are natural restriction functors arising from
\Pi_{{\rm SL}_{2}}^{\otimes s}
modules to modules of
\mathbf{V}_{{\rm SL}_{2},s}
. There is also, of course, a web of such restrictions,
e.g.
, from
\Pi_{{\rm SL}_{2}}\otimes\mathbf{V}_{{\rm SL}_{2},s}
to

Dissipative Quantum Feedback in Measurements Using a Parametrically Coupled Microcavity
Liu Qiu, Guanhao Huang, Itay Shomroni, Jiahe Pan, Paul Seidler, Tobias J. Kippenberg

Abstract
Micro- and nanoscale optical or microwave cavities are used in a wide range of classical applications and quantum science experiments, ranging from precision measurements, laser technologies to quantum control of mechanical motion. The dissipative photon loss via absorption, present to some extent in any optical cavity, is known to introduce thermo-optical effects and thereby impose fundamental limits on precision measurements. Here, we theoretically and experimentally reveal that such dissipative photon absorption can result in quantum feedback via in-loop field detection of the absorbed optical field, leading to the intracavity field fluctuations to be squashed or antisquashed. A closed-loop dissipative quantum feedback to the cavity field arises. Strikingly, this modifies the optical cavity susceptibility in coherent response measurements (capable of both increasing or decreasing the bare cavity linewidth) and causes excess noise and correlations in incoherent interferometric optomechanical measurements using a cavity, that is parametrically coupled to a mechanical oscillator. We experimentally observe such unanticipated dissipative dynamics in optomechanical spectroscopy of sideband-cooled optomechanical crystal cavitiess at both cryogenic temperature (approximately 8 K) and ambient conditions. The dissipative feedback introduces effective modifications to the optical cavity linewidth and the optomechanical scattering rate and gives rise to excess imprecision noise in the interferometric quantum measurement of mechanical motion. Such dissipative feedback differs fundamentally from a quantum nondemolition feedback, e.g., optical Kerr squeezing. The dissipative feedback itself always results in an antisqueezed out-of-loop optical field, while it can enhance the coexisting Kerr squeezing under certain conditions. Our result applies to cavity spectroscopy in both optical and superconducting microwave cavities, and equally applies to any dissipative feedback mechanism of different bandwidth inside the cavity. It has wide-ranging implications for future dissipation engineering, such as dissipation enhanced sideband cooling and Kerr squeezing, quantum frequency conversion, and nonreciprocity in photonic systems.

I
Theory

We first consider an optical cavity of resonant frequency
\omega_{c}
, which is coupled to an input field
\hat{a}_{\mathrm{ex,in}}=(\bar{a}_{\mathrm{in}}+\delta a_{\mathrm{in}})e^{-i\omega_{L}t}
of frequency
\omega_{L}
at a rate of
\kappa_{\mathrm{ex}}
.
In the rotating frame of the pumping frequency
\omega_{L}
,
the dynamics of the intracavity field
\hat{a}
can be obtained through the quantum Langevin equation, considering the dissipative photon absorption,

\dot{\hat{a}}=[i\left(\Delta-\Delta_{\mathrm{th}}\right)-\frac{\kappa}{2}]\hat{a}+\sqrt{\kappa{{}_{\mathrm{ex}}}}\hat{a}_{\mathrm{ex,in}}+\sqrt{\kappa{{}_{\mathrm{s}}}}\delta\hat{a}_{\mathrm{s,in}}+\sqrt{\kappa{{}_{\mathrm{a}}}}\delta\hat{a}_{\mathrm{a,in}}

(1)

with
\Delta=\omega_{L}-\omega_{c}
, and
\Delta{{}_{\mathrm{th}}}=g_{\mathrm{th}}\delta T
the cavity frequency shift via optical absorption induced temperature change
\delta T
of coefficient
g_{\mathrm{th}}
.
The intrinsic cavity losses
\kappa_{0}
in this treatment is separated into an absorption loss channel with rate
\kappa_{\mathrm{a}}
(the equivalent rate of photons absorbed which lead to local heating of the cavity material, followed by a photo-thermal induced cavity frequency shift) as well as a scattering loss channel with rate
\kappa_{s}
(which does not lead to a local heating of the cavity material).
As a result of the presence of two dissipation channels, from the input-output relations for an open quantum system
Gardiner and Collett 1985
, the optical cavity is driven by input vacuum fluctuations
\delta\hat{a}_{\mathrm{a,in}}
and
\delta\hat{a}_{\mathrm{s,in}}
from both channels (cf. Eq.(
1
) right hand side), with a total cavity loss being
\kappa=\kappa_{\mathrm{ex}}+\kappa_{0}
.
In the weak coupling regime, we can linearize the intracavity field
\hat{a}(t)=\bar{a}(t)+\delta\hat{a}(t)
, with
\bar{a}
the mean field amplitude and
\delta\hat{a}
the field fluctuation.

We adopt a quantum treatment of the dissipative photon absorption, whose field amplitude can be obtained by,

\hat{a}_{\mathrm{a,out}}=\delta\hat{a}_{\mathrm{a,in}}-\sqrt{\kappa_{\mathrm{a}}}\hat{a},

(2)

with corresponding absorbed photon flux,

\hat{I}_{\mathrm{a}}=\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{a,out}}\hat{a}_{\mathrm{a,out}}.

(3)

The absorption photon flux is composed of two terms, i.e. a DC term
\bar{I}_{\mathrm{a}}=\kappa_{\mathrm{a}}|\bar{a}|^{2}
,
and a fluctuating term,

\delta\hat{I}_{\mathrm{a}}=\kappa_{\mathrm{a}}(\bar{a}\delta\hat{a}^{\dagger}+\bar{a}^{*}\delta\hat{a})-\sqrt{\kappa_{\mathrm{a}}}(\bar{a}^{*}\delta\hat{a}_{\mathrm{a,in}}+\delta\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{a,in}}\bar{a}).

(4)

The dynamics of the temperature change due to the optical absorption is given by,

\delta\dot{T}(t)=-\gamma_{\mathrm{th}}\delta T(t)+g_{a}\hat{I}_{\mathrm{a}}
,
where
g_{a}\hat{I}_{\mathrm{a}}
is the rate of the temperature change due to the optical absorption and
\gamma_{\mathrm{th}}
is the thermal decay rate.
The macroscopic aparatus, i.e.
\delta T
, is not quantized, which is however correlated to the fluctuations of the in-loop photodetection represented with quantum operators.
For simplicity, we assume a one pole model of thermal heating response. More details considering the geometric dependence are discussed in Appendix
C
.
The quantum Langevin equations of the linearized optical field fluctuations can be obtained in the frequency domain,

\displaystyle\chi^{-1}_{\mathrm{c,0}}(\Omega)\delta{\hat{a}}=

\displaystyle\sigma_{d}(\Omega)\kappa_{a}(\delta\hat{a}+\delta\hat{a}^{\dagger})+\sqrt{\kappa_{\mathrm{a}}}\delta\hat{a}_{d}

(5)

\displaystyle+\sqrt{\kappa_{\mathrm{ex}}}\delta\hat{a}_{\mathrm{ex,in}}+\sqrt{\kappa_{s}}\delta\hat{a}_{\mathrm{s,in}},

with the
intrinsic
optical susceptibility,

\chi_{\mathrm{c,0}}(\Omega)=1/(\kappa/2-i(\Omega+\bar{\Delta})),

(6)

and
\bar{\Delta}=\Delta-g_{a}\kappa_{a}g_{\mathrm{th}}\bar{n}_{c}/\gamma_{\mathrm{th}}
,
incorporating the additional static cavity frequency shift.
The mean intracavity photon number is

\bar{n}_{c}=|\bar{a}|^{2}=\kappa_{\mathrm{ex}}|\bar{a}_{\mathrm{in}}|^{2}/(\kappa^{2}/4+\bar{\Delta}^{2})
.
We note that, the intracavity field is coupled to an effective dissipative reservoir,

\delta\hat{a}_{d}=\delta\hat{a}_{\mathrm{a,in}}-\sigma_{d}(\Omega)\left(\delta\hat{a}_{\mathrm{a,in}}+\delta\hat{a}_{\mathrm{a,in}}^{\dagger}\right),

(7)

by coupling additionally to the amplitude quadrature
\left(\delta\hat{a}_{\mathrm{a,in}}+\delta\hat{a}_{\mathrm{a,in}}^{\dagger}\right)

of the absorption field. Here we introduce a unitless photon-number-enhanced dissipation coefficient,

\sigma_{d}(\Omega)=\frac{g_{a}g_{\mathrm{th}}}{\Omega+i\gamma_{\mathrm{th}}}\bar{n}_{c},

(8)

with
\sigma^{*}_{d}(-\Omega)=-\sigma_{d}(\Omega)
.
A constant single photon dissipation coefficient is given by
\sigma_{\mathrm{0}}(\Omega)=\sigma_{d}(\Omega)/\bar{n}_{c}
.

Novel dynamics arises in the cavity field (cf. Eq.
5
),
which can be interpreted as a closed-loop dissipative feedback to optical cavity as shown in Fig.

.
The photon absorption manifests as an in-loop photodetection, which results in a feedback path to the optical cavity, i.e. by changing the cavity frequency via photothermal effects.
The noise spectral density of the in-loop photon flux fluctuation

\delta I_{\mathrm{a}}
, when normalized to shot noise, is given by

S_{\mathrm{\delta I_{a}}}(\Omega)=\left|1-\chi_{\mathrm{fb}}(\Omega)\right|^{-2},

(9)

where

\chi_{\mathrm{fb}}(\Omega)=\sigma_{d}(\Omega)\kappa_{a}\left(\chi_{\mathrm{c,0}}(\Omega)-\chi^{*}_{\mathrm{c,0}}(-\Omega)\right).

(10)

The dissipative feedback results in squashed or anti-squashed in-loop optical field.
We can obtain the
modified optical susceptibility

\chi_{\mathrm{c,eff}}(\Omega)=\chi_{\mathrm{c,0}}(\Omega)/(1-\chi_{\mathrm{fb}}(\Omega)).

(11)

In the case of
\bar{\Delta}\ll-\kappa

, the PSD of the in-loop photon flux at
\Omega\sim|\bar{\Delta}|
is,

S_{\mathrm{\delta I_{a}}}(\Omega)\simeq\left|1-\frac{2\bar{n}_{c}\kappa_{a}g_{a}g_{\mathrm{th}}}{\Omega\kappa}\right|^{-2},

(12)

and the effective optical susceptibility can be further simplified,

\chi_{\mathrm{c,eff}}(\Omega)=\frac{1}{\kappa_{\mathrm{eff}}/2-i(\bar{\Delta}_{\mathrm{eff}}+\Omega)},

(13)

with a modified effective cavity linewidth

\kappa_{\mathrm{eff}}=\kappa-2\kappa_{a}\operatorname{Re}(\sigma_{d}(\Omega)),

(14)

and a modified effective detuning

\bar{\Delta}_{\mathrm{eff}}=\bar{\Delta}+\kappa_{a}\operatorname{Im}(\sigma_{d}(\Omega)),

(15)

where

\bar{\Delta}=\Delta-\bar{n}_{c}g_{a}\kappa_{a}g_{\mathrm{th}}/\gamma_{\mathrm{th}}
.
Due to the squashed or anti-squashed absorbed photon fluctuations, the dissipative feedback leads to modified cavity susceptibility for the input probing field.
In practice, the photothermal coefficient
g_{\mathrm{th}}
can have different signs at room temperatures and cryogenic temperatures for different materials.
For
g_{\mathrm{th}}<0
, we have
\kappa_{\mathrm{eff}}>\kappa

and
\bar{\Delta}_{\mathrm{eff}}>\bar{\Delta}
; while
for
g_{\mathrm{th}}>0
, we have
\kappa_{\mathrm{eff}}<\kappa

and
\bar{\Delta}_{\mathrm{eff}}<\bar{\Delta}
.

As shown in Eq.
5
, the intracavity field is coupled to an effective dissipative reservoir
\delta\hat{a}_{d}
.
Different from the vacuum noise, the noise operator for the dissipative reservoir
\delta\hat{a}_{d}
satisfies the following correlations,

\displaystyle\left<\delta\hat{a}_{d}^{\dagger}(\Omega)\delta\hat{a}_{d}(\Omega^{\prime})\right>=

\displaystyle-\sigma_{d}(\Omega)\sigma_{d}(\Omega^{\prime})\delta(\Omega+\Omega^{\prime})2\pi

(16)

\displaystyle\left<\delta\hat{a}_{d}(\Omega)\delta\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{d}(\Omega^{\prime})\right>=

\displaystyle(1-\sigma_{d}(\Omega))(1+\sigma_{d}(\Omega^{\prime}))\delta(\Omega+\Omega^{\prime})2\pi,

which may result in incoherent excess noise and noise correlation for the intracavity field.

To give an example, we show how the dissipative feedback can result in modified cavity susceptibility and excess noise correlation in the quantum measurement of mechanical motion.
In such cavity optomechanical system, a mechanical mode of frequency
\Omega_{m}
is dispersively coupled to an optical mode of frequency
\omega_{c}
at a vacuum optomechanical coupling rate of
g_{0}
via radiation pressure.
A cooling tone is applied close to the cavity red sideband, i.e.
\bar{\Delta}\simeq-\Omega_{m}
.
The cooling tone gives rises to cavity enhanced anti-Stokes scattering of thermomechanical sideband around the cavity resonance.
In the weak coupling regime, we can linearize the mechanical displacement
\hat{b}\rightarrow\bar{b}+\delta\hat{b}
.
In a resolved-sideband system, i.e.
\kappa\ll\Omega_{m}
, we can obtain the quantum Langevin equations for the field fluctuations in the rotating frame of the cooling tone.
Within the rotating-wave approximation, we obtain,

\displaystyle\chi^{-1}_{\mathrm{c,eff}}(\Omega)\delta{\hat{a}}=

\displaystyle ig_{0}\bar{a}\delta\hat{b}+\sqrt{\kappa_{a}}\delta\hat{a}_{d}+\sqrt{\kappa_{{}_{\mathrm{ex}}}}\delta\hat{a}_{\mathrm{ex,in}}+\sqrt{\kappa_{s}}\delta\hat{a}_{\mathrm{s,in}}

(17)

\displaystyle\chi_{m}^{-1}(\Omega)\delta\hat{b}=

\displaystyle ig_{0}\bar{a}\delta\hat{a}+\sqrt{\Gamma_{m}}\delta\hat{b}_{\mathrm{in}}

with mechanical susceptibility
\chi_{m}(\Omega)=1/(\Gamma_{m}-i(\Omega-\Omega_{m}))
and
\chi_{\mathrm{c,eff}}
as defined in Eq.
13
.

Both coherent and incoherent optomechanical spectroscopy of the out-of-loop optical field can be adopted for the measurement of the mechanical motion
Qiu et al. 2019
;
Qiu et al. 2020
.
In a coherent spectroscopy, a weak probing tone is generated from the strong pumping tone, e.g. via an electro-optical-modulator, with frequency separation set by the microwave tone.
The reflected light from the cavity is sent to the photodetector directly.
The photocurrent is demodulated at the microwave frequency, from which we can obtain a coherent cavity response with the probing tone sweeping across the optical micro-cavity resonance.
The cavity susceptibility is modified by the mechanical motion,

\chi_{\mathrm{c,om}}(\Omega)=\frac{1}{\kappa_{\mathrm{eff}}/2-i(\bar{\Delta}_{\mathrm{eff}}+\Omega)+\bar{n}_{c}g_{0}^{2}\chi_{m}(\Omega)}.

(18)

The mechanical motion leads to a destructive interference in the out-of-loop optical field.
The coherent optomechanical spectroscopy of the cavity field results in the optomechanical induced transparency
Safavi-Naeini et al. 2011
;
Weis et al. 2010
.
The dynamical back-action results in a modified mechanical susceptibility,

\chi_{\mathrm{m,eff}}(\Omega)=1/(\Gamma_{\mathrm{eff}}/2-i(\Omega-\Omega_{m}))
, with effective mechanical linewidth
\Gamma_{\mathrm{eff}}(\Omega)=\Gamma_{m}+\Gamma_{\mathrm{opt}}(\Omega)
and optomechanical damping rate
\Gamma_{\mathrm{opt}}(\Omega)=\kappa_{\mathrm{eff}}\bar{n}_{c}g^{2}_{0}|\chi_{\mathrm{c,eff}}(\Omega)|^{2}
.

For
g_{\mathrm{th}}>0
, i.e. temperature increase results in blue-shift of optical cavity frequency, the in-loop photon fluctuation is enhanced, i.e.
S_{\mathrm{\delta I_{a}}}(\Omega_{m})>1
. This accordingly results in a decreased
\kappa_{\mathrm{eff}}
and an increased optomechanical damping rate.
For
\Omega_{\mathrm{m}}\gg\gamma_{\mathrm{th}}
, such as in an optomechanical crystal cavity (OMC), the effective detuning change due to the dissipative feedback is negligible, i.e.
\bar{\Delta}_{\mathrm{eff}}\simeq\bar{\Delta}
.
We note that, the photothermal effects can also change the mechanical susceptibility by heating the mechanical oscillators

;
Metzger and Karrai 2004
;
Restrepo et al. 2011
,
which is not considered here in our analysis.
The coherent dissipative dynamics has been shown to introduce Floquet dynamics in quantum measurement of mechanical motion

and photothermal induced transparency

Similar mechanism has been reported in a membrane in the middle system with active feedback

This can give rises to enhanced sideband cooling and even normal-mode splitting in a weakly coupled optomechanical system.

The coupling to the dissipative reservoir can introduce excess perturbation to the mechanical motion.
In the resolved-sideband limit, the minimun final occupancy is obtained with

In the high cooperativity limit, i.e.

In the balanced heterodyne detection (BHD), the local oscillator of frequency

is placed on the blue side of the pump with a frequency separation close to the mechanical frequency.
In an ideal detection, the symmetrized power spectral density (PSD) of the output photocurrent from BHD

takes the form,

\displaystyle S_{I}(\Omega+\Delta_{\mathrm{LO}})=

\displaystyle\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm{ex}}}{\kappa_{\mathrm{eff}}}\Gamma_{\mathrm{opt}}(\Omega+\Omega_{m})\Gamma_{\mathrm{eff}}|\chi_{\mathrm{m,eff}}(\Omega+\Omega_{m})|^{2}

(21)

\displaystyle\times(\bar{n}_{f}-2\bar{n}_{l})+1+4\bar{n}_{l}\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm{ex}}}{\kappa_{\mathrm{eff}}},

assuming

.

between measurement imprecision and backaction arises due to the dissipative dynamics, which can even lead to

, i.e. when

III
Optical Kerr Squeezing

The dissipative feedback arises in optomechanical spectroscopy due to photon absorption.
Such photothermal effects manifest as a Kerr-type nonlinearity
Qiu et al. 2019
, where the cavity frequency shift is proportional to the intracavity photon number.
Kerr medium has been suggested as a quantum non-demolition (QND) device in a feedback loop to generate optical squeezing
Shapiro et al. 1987
;
Haus and Yamamoto 1986
.
In such QND feedback, the intensity of in-loop optical field is measurement without quantum backaction, although phase fluctuations are added.
The dissipative feedback is fundementally different from a QND feedback, e.g. Kerr squeezing
Shapiro et al. 1987
;
Haus and Yamamoto 1986
, as excess noise always arises in the out-of-loop field due to uncorrelated noise from the in-loop field detection, i.e. photon absorption.

Here we consider a micro-cavity with coexisting dissipative feedback via photothermal effects and Kerr nonlinearity. We can obtain the quantum Langevin equations of the field fluctuation in the frequency domain,

\displaystyle\chi^{-1}_{\mathrm{c,0}}(\Omega)\delta{\hat{a}}=

\displaystyle\left(\bar{n}_{c}\sigma_{0}(\Omega)\kappa_{a}-i\bar{n}_{c}g_{\mathrm{Kerr}}\right)(\delta\hat{a}+\delta\hat{a}^{\dagger})

(22)

\displaystyle+\sqrt{\kappa_{a}}\delta\hat{a}_{d}+\sqrt{\kappa_{{}_{\mathrm{ex}}}}\delta\hat{a}_{\mathrm{ex,in}}+\sqrt{\kappa_{s}}\delta\hat{a}_{\mathrm{s,in}}

where
g_{\mathrm{Kerr}}
is the single photon nonlinear Kerr coupling rate
Matsko et al. 2005
and
\chi_{\mathrm{c,0}}(\Omega)
is given by Eq.
6
with
\bar{\Delta}=\Delta-\left(g_{a}\kappa_{a}g_{\mathrm{th}}/\gamma_{\mathrm{th}}+g_{\mathrm{Kerr}}\right)\bar{n}_{c}
.
We can obtain the symmetrized PSD of the out-of-loop field quadrature,
\hat{X}_{\theta}=\delta\hat{a}_{\mathrm{ex,out}}e^{-i\theta}+\delta\hat{a}_{\mathrm{ex,out}}^{\dagger}e^{i\theta}
,
in a balanced homodyne detection, with LO of the pumping tone frequency and
\theta
as the phase difference between the LO and the pump.
We focus on the simple case where the laser detuning
\bar{\Delta}=0
.
For Kerr squeezing, i.e.
g_{\mathrm{th}}=0
, the PSD takes the form,

S_{\mathrm{I,Kerr}}(\Omega)=1-\frac{16\bar{n}_{c}\eta_{c}\kappa\sin(\theta)g_{\mathrm{Kerr}}\left((\kappa^{2}+4\Omega^{2})\cos(\theta)-4\bar{n}_{c}\kappa\sin(\theta)g_{\mathrm{Kerr}}\right)}{(\kappa^{2}+4\Omega^{2})^{2}}.

(23)

where we assume an ideal detection and
\eta_{c}=\kappa_{\mathrm{ex}}/\kappa
.
Such QND feedback can result in Kerr squeezing in the output field for an optical quadrature with minimum variance below the vacuum noise at an optimal angle, as detailed in Appendix
A
.
In contrast, for dissipative dynamics only, i.e.
g_{\mathrm{Kerr}}=0
, the PSD takes the form,

S_{\mathrm{I,a}}(\Omega)=1+S^{\mathrm{ex}}_{\mathrm{I,a}}(\Omega)
,
where the incoherent optical absorption always results in an excess noise, i.e. the photothermal noise,

S^{\mathrm{ex}}_{\mathrm{I,a}}(\Omega)=\frac{16\bar{n}_{c}\kappa_{a}\kappa_{\mathrm{ex}}\sin(\theta)^{2}g_{a}^{2}g_{\mathrm{th}}^{2}}{(\kappa^{2}+4\Omega^{2})(\Omega^{2}+\gamma_{\mathrm{th}}^{2})}\geq 0.

(24)

We note that, this coincides with earlier experimental and theoretical works in optical systems with intensity feedback,
where in-loop photocurrent can be squashed while the out-of-loop photocurrent always becomes anti-squashed
Taubman et al. 1995
;
Wiseman 1998
;
Wiseman 1999
.
Kerr nonlinearity on the other hand, demonstrates a QND detection of the in-loop optical field, which enables the Kerr squeezing in the out-of-loop field, as expected
Shapiro et al. 1987
;
Haus and Yamamoto 1986
.

When Kerr nonlinearity and dissipative dynamics coexist, the total PSD is given by
S_{I}(\Omega)=S_{\mathrm{I,Kerr}}(\Omega)+S^{\mathrm{ex}}_{\mathrm{I,tot}}(\Omega)
, with total excess noise
S^{\mathrm{ex}}_{\mathrm{I,tot}}(\Omega)=S^{\mathrm{ex}}_{\mathrm{I,a}}(\Omega)+S^{\mathrm{ex}}_{\mathrm{I,c}}(\Omega)
due to the dissipative dynamics.
The coherent dissipative dynamics gives arises to,

S^{\mathrm{ex}}_{\mathrm{I,c}}(\Omega)=\frac{64\bar{n}^{2}_{c}\kappa_{a}\kappa_{\mathrm{ex}}g_{a}g_{\mathrm{Kerr}}g_{\text{th}}\sin^{2}(\theta)\left(\kappa\gamma_{\text{th}}-2\Omega^{2}\right)}{\left(\kappa^{2}+4\Omega^{2}\right)^{2}\left(\gamma_{\text{th}}^{2}+\Omega^{2}\right)},

(25)

We note that, when

g_{a}g_{\text{th}}\left(\kappa^{2}+4\Omega^{2}\right)<g_{\mathrm{Kerr}}\left(8\Omega^{2}-4\kappa\gamma_{\text{th}}\right)
,
the total excess noise
S^{\mathrm{ex}}_{\mathrm{I,tot}}<0
, which
opens an interesting regime for dissipation improved Kerr squeezing
.
Such regime is independent of the intracavity photon number and squeezing angle.

In Fig.
4
, we show the theoretical curves of estimated Kerr squeezing in a state-of-art
Si
3
N
4
micro-ring resonators at optimal squeezing angles with realistic parameters
Liu et al. 2021
.
Kerr nonlinearity in such micro-ring resonator can lead to frequency comb and dissipative soliton generation at low pumping powers (
\sim
mW)
Gaeta et al. 2019
, due to the extremely low optical loss (
\kappa/2\pi<20\mathrm{MHz}
). The red curve includes only Kerr nonlinearity while the green curve considers both the Kerr nonlinearity and the dissipative dynamics. As shown in Fig.
4
, the green curve exhibits excess noise at low frequencies due to the incoherent dissipative dynamics, while shows slight improvement of Kerr squeezing at frequencies between 2MHz
\sim
10MHz due to the coherent dissipative dynamics.
In practice, the Kerr squeezing is limited by the detection efficiency and the thermorefractive noise
Braginsky et al. 2000
;
Huang et al. 2019
;
Panuski et al. 2020
.

IV
Conclusion

We observe the dissipative dynamics resulting from photon absorption in quantum optomechanical spectroscopy of sideband-cooled optomechanical crystal cavities under different conditions and develop a theoretical model for our experimental observations.
Such dissipative dynamics comes from a dissipative quantum feedback to the optical cavity.
The in-loop detection of the optical absorption field results in a modified optical susceptibility, and results in excess noise correlations in the quantum measurement of mechanical motion.
Such dissipative feedback differs from the quantum non-demolition feedback, e.g. Kerr squeezing.
The observed dynamics is applicable to any dissipative feedback in the cavities of different bandwidths, such as TPA, FCA, and photorefractive effects
Li et al. 2019
.
It offers crucial understanding to cavity spectroscopy in both optical and superconducting microwave micro-cavities, such as noise thermometry, quantum enhanced sensing, and microwave-optical frequency conversions
Sahu et al. 2021
;
Holzgrafe et al. 2020
.
While our study has experimentally considered the optical domain, it is worth pointing out, that our results also apply to the microwave domain, as studied in circuit optomechanics or used in superconducting circuits based research
Aspelmeyer et al. 2014
;
Blais et al. 2021
. While it is well known that TLS can lead to a drive power dependent quality factor of superconducting microwave cavities, the described phenomena may be applicable to mechanisms which result in power or temperature dependent cavity frequencies, e.g. due to quasiparticles
Pop et al. 2014
;
Bespalov et al. 2016
;
Gustavsson et al. 2016
;
Tai et al. 2021
.
The dissipative feedback can also be exploited for future dissipation engineering, such as enhanced sideband cooling and Kerr squeezing, and nonreciprocal photonic devices.

Appendix A
Theory

Optomechanical Spectroscopy

For laser sideband cooling in a resolved-sideband optomechanical system coupled to a dissipative reservoir,
the quantum Langevin equation takes the form,

\displaystyle\chi^{-1}_{\mathrm{c,eff}}(\Omega)\delta{\hat{a}}=

\displaystyle ig_{0}\bar{a}\delta\hat{b}+\sqrt{\kappa_{a}}\delta\hat{a}_{d}+\sqrt{\kappa_{{}_{\mathrm{ex}}}}\delta\hat{a}_{\mathrm{ex,in}}+\sqrt{\kappa_{s}}\delta\hat{a}_{\mathrm{s,in}}

(26)

\displaystyle\chi_{m}^{-1}(\Omega)\delta\hat{b}=

\displaystyle ig_{0}\bar{a}\delta\hat{a}+\sqrt{\Gamma_{m}}\delta\hat{b}_{\mathrm{in}}

and the noise operators satisfy the following correlations,

\displaystyle\left<\delta\hat{a}_{\mathrm{ex,in}}^{\dagger}(\Omega)\delta\hat{a}_{\mathrm{ex,in}}(\Omega^{\prime})\right>=

\displaystyle 0

(27)

\displaystyle\left<\delta\hat{a}_{\mathrm{ex,in}}(\Omega)\delta\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{ex,in}}(\Omega^{\prime})\right>=

\displaystyle\delta(\Omega+\Omega^{\prime})2\pi

\displaystyle\left<\delta\hat{a}_{\mathrm{0,in}}^{\dagger}(\Omega)\delta\hat{a}_{\mathrm{0,in}}(\Omega^{\prime})\right>=

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\left<\delta\hat{a}_{\mathrm{0,in}}(\Omega)\delta\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{0,in}}(\Omega^{\prime})\right>=

\displaystyle\delta(\Omega+\Omega^{\prime})2\pi

\displaystyle\left<\delta\hat{b}_{\mathrm{in}}^{\dagger}(\Omega)\delta\hat{b}_{\mathrm{in}}(\Omega^{\prime})\right>=

\displaystyle\bar{n}_{\mathrm{th}}\delta(\Omega+\Omega^{\prime})2\pi

\displaystyle\left<\delta\hat{b}_{\mathrm{in}}(\Omega)\delta\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{in}}(\Omega^{\prime})\right>=

\displaystyle(\bar{n}_{\mathrm{th}}+1)\delta(\Omega+\Omega^{\prime})2\pi

In addition, the noise operator for the dissipative reservoir satisfies the following correlations,

\displaystyle\left<\delta\hat{a}_{d}^{\dagger}(\Omega)\delta\hat{a}_{d}(\Omega^{\prime})\right>=

\displaystyle-\sigma_{d}(\Omega)\sigma_{d}(\Omega^{\prime})\delta(\Omega+\Omega^{\prime})2\pi

(28)

\displaystyle\left<\delta\hat{a}_{d}(\Omega)\delta\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{d}(\Omega^{\prime})\right>=

\displaystyle(1-\sigma_{d}(\Omega))(1+\sigma_{d}(\Omega^{\prime}))\delta(\Omega+\Omega^{\prime})2\pi

\displaystyle\left<\delta\hat{a}_{d}(\Omega)\delta\hat{a}_{d}(\Omega^{\prime})\right>=

\displaystyle-(1-\sigma_{d}(\Omega))\sigma_{d}(\Omega^{\prime})\delta(\Omega+\Omega^{\prime})2\pi

\displaystyle\left<\delta\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{d}(\Omega)\delta\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{d}(\Omega^{\prime})\right>=

\displaystyle\sigma_{d}(\Omega)(1+\sigma_{d}(\Omega^{\prime}))\delta(\Omega+\Omega^{\prime})2\pi.

We can obtain the noise spectrum of the mechanical oscillator,

\displaystyle S_{\mathrm{\hat{b}\hat{b}}}(\Omega)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left<\delta\hat{b}^{\dagger}(t)\delta\hat{b}(t^{\prime})\right>e^{i\Omega t}dt

(29)

\displaystyle=\left(\bar{n}_{\mathrm{th}}\Gamma_{m}+\Gamma_{\mathrm{+}}\right)|\chi_{\mathrm{eff}}(-\Omega)|^{2}

where
\Gamma_{\mathrm{+}}=\bar{n}_{c}g^{2}_{0}|\chi_{c}(\Omega_{m})|^{2}\kappa_{a}\sigma^{2}_{d}
, and

\displaystyle S_{\mathrm{\hat{b}^{\dagger}\hat{b}^{\dagger}}}(\Omega)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left<\delta\hat{b}(t)\delta\hat{b}^{\dagger}(t^{\prime})\right>e^{i\Omega t}dt

(30)

\displaystyle=\left((\bar{n}_{\mathrm{th}}+1)\Gamma_{m}+\Gamma_{\mathrm{-}}\right)|\chi_{\mathrm{eff}}(\Omega)|^{2}

where

\Gamma_{\mathrm{-}}=\bar{n}_{c}g^{2}_{0}|\chi_{c}(\Omega_{m})|^{2}(\kappa_{\mathrm{eff}}+\kappa_{a}\sigma_{d}^{2})
.
The two-sided mechanical noise spectrum can be obtained,

\displaystyle S_{\mathrm{\hat{x}\hat{x}}}(\Omega)/x^{2}_{\mathrm{zpf}}

\displaystyle=S_{\mathrm{\hat{b}^{\dagger}\hat{b}^{\dagger}}}(\Omega)+S_{\mathrm{\hat{b}\hat{b}}}(\Omega)

(31)

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{\mathrm{eff}}\left[|\chi_{\mathrm{eff}}(\Omega)|^{2}(\bar{n}_{f}+1)+|\chi_{\mathrm{eff}}(-\Omega)|^{2}\bar{n}_{f}\right],

where the final occupancy takes the form,

\bar{n}_{f}=\frac{\bar{n}_{\mathrm{th}}\Gamma_{m}+\Gamma_{\mathrm{opt}}\bar{n}_{l}}{\Gamma_{\mathrm{eff}}},

(32)

with
\Gamma_{\mathrm{opt}}=\kappa_{\mathrm{eff}}\bar{n}_{c}g^{2}_{0}|\chi_{\mathrm{c,eff}}(\Omega)|^{2}
and
\bar{n}_{l}=\kappa_{a}\bar{n}^{2}_{c}\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{0}}/\kappa_{\mathrm{eff}}
.
The coupling to the dissipative reservoir results in excess backaction heating of the mechanical oscillator.
In a balanced heterodyne measurement, the output field is beating with a strong LO of frequency
\omega_{\mathrm{LO}}
,
which is placed on the blue side of the pumping tone with a frequency separation close to the mechanical frequency.
The symmetrized power spectral density (PSD) of the photocurrent from the BHD is given by,

S_{I}(\Omega)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\langle\overline{\left\{\delta I\left(t+t^{\prime}\right),\delta I\left(t^{\prime}\right)\right\}}\rangle e^{i\Omega t}dt
.
In practice, there are other losses in the photodetection, such as in the fiber or the photodetection,
resulting in a limited external detection efficiency
\eta_{\mathrm{ex}}
.
Under optimal sideband cooling condition, i.e.
\bar{\Delta}_{\mathrm{eff}}=-\Omega_{m}
,
the photocurrent PSD, when normalized to the shot noise floor, is given by,

\begin{aligned} S_{I}(\Omega+\Delta_{\mathrm{LO}})=&\eta_{\mathrm{ex}}\frac{4\kappa_{\mathrm{ex}}\bar{n}_{c}g^{2}_{0}}{\kappa^{2}_{\mathrm{eff}}}\Gamma_{\mathrm{eff}}|\chi_{\mathrm{m,eff}}(\Omega+\Omega_{m})|^{2}\\
&\times(\bar{n}_{f}-2\bar{n}_{l})+1+4\eta_{\mathrm{ex}}\bar{n}_{l}\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm{ex}}}{\kappa_{\mathrm{eff}}}\end{aligned},

(33)

with
\Delta_{\mathrm{LO}}=\Omega_{m}+\omega_{L}-\omega_{\mathrm{LO}}>0
and
\Gamma_{\mathrm{eff}}=\Gamma_{m}+4\bar{n}_{c}g^{2}_{0}/\kappa_{\mathrm{eff}}
.
At low cooling power, the signal-to-noise ratio of the thermomechanical sideband is given by,

\mathrm{SNR}=\frac{16\eta_{\mathrm{ex}}\bar{n}_{c}g^{2}_{0}\kappa_{\mathrm{ex}}k_{B}T}{\kappa^{2}\Gamma_{m}\hbar\Omega_{m}},

(34)

with bath temperature T and negligible dynamical backaction.
In the fitting of
\bar{n}_{c}
dependent
\Gamma_{\mathrm{eff}}
in the main text, the inferred
\kappa_{\mathrm{eff}}
is incorporated, with
g_{0}
and
\Gamma_{m}
as free fitting parameters.
The mechanical noise thermometry can be performed by anchoring the phonon occupancy to the lowest power via Eq.
33
, incorporating the inferred
\kappa_{\mathrm{eff}}
in the optomechanical damping rate.
The external detection efficiency
\eta_{\mathrm{ex}}
can be obtained accordingly, and is estimated to be
\sim 0.15
in the room temperature experiments.
The dissipative dynamics results in an increased noise floor of

BG_{\mathrm{ex}}=4\eta_{\mathrm{ex}}\bar{n}_{\mathrm{l}}\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm{ex}}}{\kappa_{\mathrm{eff}}}=\frac{4\eta_{\mathrm{ex}}\kappa_{a}\kappa_{\mathrm{ex}}\bar{n}^{2}_{c}\sigma^{2}_{0}}{\kappa_{\mathrm{eff}}^{2}}.

(35)

However, Eq.
35
is not sufficient to fully capture the observed noise increase, which may be partially from the cavity frequency noise due to free-carrier absorption
Barclay et al. 2005
;
Hamerly and Mabuchi 2015
.
For the fitting of
\bar{n}_{c}
dependent
\bar{n}_{f}
in the main text, we incorporate a linear optical absorption for the mechanical motion, as in Ref.
Qiu et al. 2019
.

Optical Kerr Squeezing

The Kerr nonlinearity coupling rate in the main text can be estimated through
Matsko et al. 2005

g_{\mathrm{Kerr}}=-\omega_{c}\frac{n_{2}}{n_{0}}\frac{\hbar\omega_{c}c}{V_{\mathrm{mode}}n_{0}}<0,

(36)

where
n_{0}
is the linear refractive index,
n_{2}
the Kerr coefficient,
V_{\mathrm{mode}}
the mode volume of the cavity, and
c
the speed of light.
The output field quadrature can be linearly measured in a balanced homodyne detection, by beating with a strong LO of the pumping tone frequency.
This results in the photocurrent fluctuation,

\delta\hat{I}(t)=|\bar{a}_{\mathrm{LO}}|\hat{X}_{\theta}
,
where
\theta
is the phase difference between the LO and pumping tone.
The symmetrized noise power spectral density (PSD) of the output photocurrent, when normalized to the shot-noise floor, is

\displaystyle S_{I}(\Omega)

\displaystyle=\left\langle\hat{X}_{\theta}(-\Omega)\hat{X}_{\theta}(\Omega)\right\rangle_{s}

(37)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\left\langle\hat{X}_{\theta}(-\Omega)\hat{X}_{\theta}(\Omega)\right\rangle+\left\langle\hat{X}_{\theta}(\Omega)\hat{X}_{\theta}(-\Omega)\right\rangle\right)

The full expression of the PSD can be obtained analytically, which is too complicated to be presented here.
We focus on the simple case where the laser detuning
\bar{\Delta}=0
.
In absence of the dissipative dynamics, i.e.
g_{\mathrm{th}}=0
, the PSD takes the form,

S_{\mathrm{I,Kerr}}(\Omega)=1-\frac{16\bar{n}_{c}\eta_{\mathrm{ex}}\eta_{c}\kappa\sin(\theta)g_{\mathrm{Kerr}}\left((\kappa^{2}+4\Omega^{2})\cos(\theta)-4\bar{n}_{c}\kappa\sin(\theta)g_{\mathrm{Kerr}}\right)}{(\kappa^{2}+4\Omega^{2})^{2}}.

(38)

with cavity coupling efficiency
\eta_{c}=\kappa_{\mathrm{ex}}/\kappa
and external detection efficiency
\eta_{\mathrm{ex}}
.
The minimum PSD,

S_{\mathrm{I,Kerr}}^{\mathrm{min}}(\Omega)=1-\frac{2\eta_{\mathrm{ex}}\eta_{c}}{\sqrt{\left((\kappa^{2}+4\Omega^{2})/4\bar{n}_{c}\kappa g_{\mathrm{Kerr}}\right)^{2}+1}+1},

(39)

which is limited by the cavity linewidth and total detection efficiency
\eta_{\mathrm{ex}}\eta_{c}
,
can be obtained at

\theta_{\mathrm{opt}}(\Omega)=\tan^{-1}\left((\kappa^{2}+4\Omega^{2})/4\bar{n}_{c}\kappa g_{\mathrm{Kerr}}\right)/2
.
In absence of the Kerr nonlinearity, i.e.
g_{\mathrm{Kerr}}=0
, the PSD takes the form

S_{\mathrm{I,a}}(\Omega)=1+S^{\mathrm{ex}}_{\mathrm{I,a}}(\Omega)
,
where the incoherent dissipative dynamics always results in an excess noise,

S^{\mathrm{ex}}_{\mathrm{I,a}}(\Omega)=\frac{16\bar{n}_{c}\eta_{\mathrm{ex}}\eta_{a}\eta_{c}\kappa^{2}\sin(\theta)^{2}g_{a}^{2}g_{\mathrm{th}}^{2}}{(\kappa^{2}+4\Omega^{2})(\Omega^{2}+\gamma_{\mathrm{th}}^{2})}\geq 0,

(40)

with
\eta_{\mathrm{a}}=\kappa_{a}/\kappa
.

In most optical systems, the Kerr nonlinearity and dissipative dynamics coexist, the total PSD is given by

S_{I}(\Omega)=S_{\mathrm{I,Kerr}}(\Omega)+S^{\mathrm{ex}}_{\mathrm{I,tot}}(\Omega),

(41)

with total excess noise
S^{\mathrm{ex}}_{\mathrm{I,tot}}(\Omega)=S^{\mathrm{ex}}_{\mathrm{I,a}}(\Omega)+S^{\mathrm{ex}}_{\mathrm{I,c}}(\Omega)
due to the absorption.
The
coherent dissipative dynamics
gives rise to,

S^{\mathrm{ex}}_{\mathrm{I,c}}(\Omega)=\frac{64\bar{n}^{2}_{c}\eta_{\mathrm{ex}}\eta_{a}\eta_{c}\kappa^{2}g_{a}g_{\mathrm{Kerr}}g_{\text{th}}\sin^{2}(\theta)\left(\kappa\gamma_{\text{th}}-2\Omega^{2}\right)}{\left(\kappa^{2}+4\Omega^{2}\right)^{2}\left(\gamma_{\text{th}}^{2}+\Omega^{2}\right)}.

(42)

The minimum quadrature can be obtained accordingly at the frequency dependent optimal squeezing angle,

\theta_{\mathrm{opt}}(\Omega)=\begin{cases}\frac{\arctan(-B/A)}{2}&A<0\\
\frac{\arctan(-B/A)+\pi}{2}&A>0\end{cases},

(43)

where

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=\bar{n}_{c}\kappa\left(4\eta_{a}g_{a}g_{\mathrm{Kerr}}g_{\text{th}}\left(2\Omega^{2}-\kappa\gamma_{\text{th}}\right)-\eta_{a}g_{a}^{2}g_{\text{th}}^{2}\left(\kappa^{2}+4\Omega^{2}\right)-4g_{\mathrm{Kerr}}^{2}\left(\gamma_{\text{th}}^{2}+\Omega^{2}\right)\right)/g_{\mathrm{Kerr}}

(44)

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=\left(\kappa^{2}+4\Omega^{2}\right)\left(\gamma_{\text{th}}^{2}+\Omega^{2}\right)>0.

We note that, when

g_{a}g_{\text{th}}\left(\kappa^{2}+4\Omega^{2}\right)<g_{\mathrm{Kerr}}\left(8\Omega^{2}-4\kappa\gamma_{\text{th}}\right)
,
the total excess noise
S^{\mathrm{ex}}_{\mathrm{I,tot}}<0
.
This brings about an interesting regime for dissipation assisted Kerr squeezing, which is independent from the intracavity photon number and squeezing angle.

Thermal Response Simulations

In the theoretical treatment of the temperature dynamics in the main text, we neglect the geometric dependence.
In practice, the thermal response can be rather complex as dissipative absorption in the microcavities can dissipate via different mechanisms, such as radiation, conduction, and convection.

The thermal response can be obtained with finite element methods (FEM) simulations.
We first simulate the electric field distribution of the optical mode
\vec{E}(\vec{r})
.
The bulk absorption heating

P(\vec{r})=P_{\mathrm{abs}}\epsilon_{0}\vec{E}(\vec{r})\times\hat{\epsilon}\vec{E}(\vec{r})/W_{E},

(45)

can be added as a heat source in the heat transfer model,
with
\hat{\epsilon}=\hat{\epsilon}_{0}+\hat{\epsilon}_{1}
the permittivity,
and
W_{E}=\int\epsilon_{0}\vec{E}(\vec{r})\times\hat{\epsilon}\vec{E}(\vec{r})dV
the mode full energy. The frequency component of the temperature distribution
\widetilde{T}(\omega,\vec{r})
can be solved in the Fourier-domain heat equation,

i\Omega\rho C\widetilde{T}+k\Delta\widetilde{T}=\widetilde{P},

(46)

where
\rho
is the density,
C
the heat capacity, and
k
the thermal conductivity.
The cavity shift frequency takes the form,

\frac{\delta\omega_{c}(\Omega)}{\omega_{c}}=-\frac{1}{W_{E}}\int\widetilde{T}(\Omega,\vec{r})\epsilon_{0}\sqrt{\epsilon(\vec{r})}\frac{dn}{dT}(\vec{r})|\vec{E}(\vec{r})|^{2}dV.

(47)

.

Qiu et al. 2019
.

Appendix D
Excess Noise

Excess laser noise is known to limit sideband cooling and to introduce classical correlations in linear measurements of mechanical motion, which becomes extremely critical to deep sideband cooling,
where optical filters are typically required to reject the excess laser noise around the mechanical frequency.
External cavity diode lasers are well-known to have excess noise in the GHz range, due to the damped relaxation oscillation caused by carrier population dynamics.
In our experiments, the excess laser frequency noise spectrum density
S_{\omega\omega}(\Omega)
at frequency of
5.2\mathrm{GHz}
is measured below
10^{5}\mathrm{rad^{2}}\mathrm{Hz}
.
In BHD, we choose a LO power of around
6\mathrm{mW}
.
The beating between the high reflected power (
>100\mathrm{\mu W}
) and the noise from the LO can lead to an increased noise floor by
\sim 1\%
, due to the excess noise in the LO
Qiu et al. 2020
;
Qiu 2020
.

Brillouin scattering in optical fibers is known to generate excess noises, from bulk elastic waves or surface acoustic waves
Beugnot et al. 2014
.
In our experiments, we don’t observe evident excess noise in all the optical equipments, such as optical switches, mirrors and fibers, with a quantum limited detection balanced heterodyne detection.

Fundamental temperature fluctuation can in principle result in excess thermo-refractive noise (TRN) in the cavity.
TRN has received significant attentions in the gravitational wave detection community due to the high powers and low detection bandwidth and limits the ultimate sensitivity.
Over the last years, there have been continuous efforts in the characterization of TNR in integrated devices.
The TRN is estimated to have a large impact on the on-chip Kerr squeezing due to the low detection bandwidth.
In our measurements with optomechanical spectroscopy, such effects from TRN might be negligible due to the large mechanical frequency.

The optical resonant wavelength of the silicon OMC is in the bandgap of silicon, which should in principle introduce negligible optical absorption.
Due to the ultra-small mode volume and the strong cavity field enhancement, nonlinear optical dynamics can arise in the cavity at high pumping powers, such as two-photon absorption (TPA)
Barclay et al. 2005
.
The TPA in the silicon cavity can introduce excess nonlinear optical loss
\kappa_{\mathrm{tpa}}
and generate free carriers.
The generated free carriers in turn lead to excess optical loss
\kappa_{\mathrm{fc}}
and cavity dispersion
\Delta_{\mathrm{fc}}
.
The total optical cavity loss is thus given by
\kappa=\kappa_{\mathrm{ex}}+\kappa_{s}+\kappa_{a}+\kappa_{\mathrm{tpa}}+\kappa_{\mathrm{fc}}
,
where
\kappa_{\mathrm{tpa}}=\kappa^{0}_{\mathrm{tpa}}\bar{n}_{c}
and
\kappa_{\mathrm{fc}}\simeq\kappa^{0}_{\mathrm{fc}}\bar{n}^{2}_{c}
.
At room temperature, the estimated
\kappa^{0}_{\mathrm{tpa}}/2\pi\sim 3\mathrm{kHz}
and
\kappa^{0}_{\mathrm{fc}}/2\pi<10\mathrm{Hz}
, taking the parameters in Ref.
Sun et al. 2013
;
Barclay et al. 2005
;
Wu et al. 2017
and estimated mode volume from finite element simulation. We note that,
\kappa^{0}_{\mathrm{fc}}
also depends on the carrier lifetime. In our estimation, we assume a carrier lifetime of
\sim 10\mathrm{ns}
, which in practice depends on the density of the carrier in the optical cavity
Barclay et al. 2005
. At cryogenic temperatures, the carrier lifetime can decreases by few orders of magnitude compared to room temperature
Sun et al. 2013
, which results in lower
\kappa^{0}_{\mathrm{fc}}
.
The optical cavity dispersion due to the free carrier is estimated
\Delta^{0}_{\mathrm{fc}}<10\mathrm{Hz}

Sun et al. 2013
;
Barclay et al. 2005
;
Wu et al. 2017
.
The nonlinear dynamics due to TPA and FC can in principle also result in complex dissipative dynamics in the optical cavity, which is not considered in the main text.

We note that, theoretical predictions show that the free-carriers dispersion in the optical cavity can introduce excess noise due to the incoherent carrier excitation and decay processes
Hamerly and Mabuchi 2015
, which may partially explain the noise floor we observe in the experiments and requires further investigation.

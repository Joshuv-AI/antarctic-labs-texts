An Inverse Problem with Partial Neumann Data and L n / 2 L^{n/2} Potentials
Leonard Busch, Leo Tzou

Abstract
We consider a partial data inverse problem with unbounded potentials. Rather than rely on functional analytic arguments or Carleman estimates, we construct an explicit Green’s function with which we construct complex geometric optics (CGO) solutions and show unique determinability of potentials in L n / 2 L^{n/2} for the Schrödinger equation with partial Neumann data. Keywords: Schrödinger equation, Calderón problem, inverse problems, pseudo-differential operators, partial data, Green’s function

Introduction

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a fixed, bounded, domain with smooth boundary in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
n\geq 3
, let
q\in L^{n/2}(\Omega)
be a potential.

For
\Gamma\subset\partial\Omega
and
f\in H^{-1/2}(\Gamma)
, consider the forward problem concerned with finding a solution
u\in H^{1}(\Omega)
to

(\Delta+q)u=0\text{ in }\Omega\,,\quad\text{and}\quad\partial_{\nu}u|_{\Gamma}=f\,,\quad\partial_{\nu}u|_{\partial\Omega\setminus\Gamma}=0\,.

(1.1)

In the inverse problem associated to
Eq.

1.1
, we seek to address whether the potential
q
is uniquely determined by the set of boundary values
u|_{\Gamma}
for solutions
u
of
Eq.

1.1
with

f
ranging in
H^{-1/2}(\Gamma)
.

When the Neumann-to-Dirichlet map
N_{q}\colon H^{-1/2}(\partial\Omega)\to H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega),f\mapsto u|_{\partial\Omega}
is well-defined for
Eq.

1.1
,
[
Chu15
]
showed unique determination of potentials
q\in L^{\infty}
in dimensions
n\geq 3
under some restrictions on
\Gamma
. In dimension
2
,
[
IUY12
]
establish the same uniqueness for potentials
q\in W^{1,p},p>2
.

The problem is motivated by what is now called electrical impedance tomography or the Calderón problem from the eponymous
[
Cal80
]
. Here, one attempts to recover the electrical conductivity of a medium by taking current and voltage measurements only at the medium’s boundary. After a change of variables, the associated conductivity equation becomes the Schrödinger equation as it is in
Eq.

1.1
. In this setting, the boundary data
u|_{\partial\Gamma}
and
\partial_{\nu}u|_{\partial\Gamma}
are interpreted as voltage and current flux multiplied by surface conductivity, respectively. See also a survey of recent developments regarding electrical impedance tomography
[
Uhl09
]
.

Also motivated by electrical impedance tomography is the closely related Dirichlet boundary formulation of the inverse problem arising from
Eq.

1.1
, which has been studied much more extensively. In this formulation, relying on work from
[
KV84
]
,
[
SU87
]
were the first to show uniqueness of potentials in dimensions
\geq 3
given full boundary data, and
[
Nac88
]
gave a reconstruction algorithm. The first such results in dimension
2
for the Schrödinger equation came in
[
Buk08
]
.

In practical settings, measurements of the full data cannot be guaranteed, and one may ask whether the potential is uniquely determined when
\Gamma\neq\partial\Omega
, possibly with some restrictions upon
\Gamma
. For the Dirichlet formulation in dimension
2
in the case of partial data, uniqueness was shown in
[
IUY10
]
and was generalized to Riemann surfaces in
[
GT11
]
. In dimensions
\geq 3
,
[
BU02
]
proved uniqueness of potentials in
L^{\infty}
. In partial data results, the restrictions upon the partial boundary are generally non-mutually inclusive, for a survey regarding other partial data results with the Dirichlet boundary formulation see
[
KS14
]
and the references therein.

Of particular interest to us is
[
CT20
]
, which proved the uniqueness of potentials
q\in L^{n/2}(\Omega)
in the partial data case where
n\geq 3
. The reason the exponent
n/2
may not come entirely out of nowhere is the following. The inverse problem for the Schrödinger equation may be considered related to the unique continuation property with Cauchy data, and according to
[
JK85
]
, the value
n/2
is the optimal exponent of Lebesgue spaces for the strong unique continuation property to hold.

The goal of this paper is to reproduce
[
CT20
]
with partial Neumann data, that is, show uniqueness of potentials in
L^{n/2}(\Omega)
in the formulation from
Eq.

1.1
.

The motivation for studying the Neumann data case instead of the Dirichlet case is the following: in physical implementations of electrical impedance tomography, a set of electrodes is attached to a medium. A subset of these electrodes apply currents and the remaining ones measure voltages, see
[
AH21
]
for an overview. This corresponds to prescribed data
\partial_{\nu}u|_{\partial\Omega}
and measured data
u|_{\partial\Omega}
, which is the Neumann formulation of the inverse problem.

Setup

The strategy in our proofs will be to flatten out the boundary of
\Omega
along each of its boundary defining functions, proving results there and transforming back to the original coordinates of
\Omega
.

2.1
Change of Coordinates

Fix some open
\Gamma\subset\partial\Omega
and
g\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})
so that
g
defines the boundary of
\Omega
in a neighborhood of
\Gamma
, by which we mean that

\Omega\cap U=\{y\in U\colon y_{n}-g(y^{\prime})>0\}\,,\quad\text{and}\quad\partial\Omega\cap U=\{y\in U\colon y_{n}-g(y^{\prime})=0\}\,,

(2.1)

for some open, bounded neighborhood

of
\Gamma
with
\Gamma\Subset\partial\Omega\cap U
.

Note that, given the boundary defining function
y_{n}-g
, the outward unit normal vector
\nu
is defined as

\nu(y)=-\frac{\nabla(y_{n}-g(y^{\prime}))}{\left\lvert\nabla(y_{n}-g(y^{\prime}))\right\rvert}=(1+\left\lvert K(y^{\prime})\right\rvert^{2})^{-1/2}\begin{pmatrix}\nabla^{\prime}g(y^{\prime})\\
-1\end{pmatrix}\,,

(2.2)

where we put

(2.3)

In order to flatten out the boundary we define

\gamma\colon(y^{\prime},y_{n})\mapsto(x^{\prime},x_{n})=(y^{\prime},y_{n}-g(y^{\prime}))\,,

(2.4)

so that
\tilde{\Omega}\coloneqq\gamma(\Omega)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
, and
\tilde{\Gamma}\coloneqq\gamma(\Gamma)\subset\{x_{n}=0\}
, where
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}=\{(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\colon x_{n}>0\}
.

From here on out, expressions marked with a tilde generally denote objects in these transformed variables, and as a convention we will use
x
as a variable when working on objects defined in the upper half space
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
.

In particular, we will now calculate the normal derivative in transformed coordinates. Let
\gamma^{\ast},(\gamma^{-1})^{\ast}
be pullbacks by
\gamma,\gamma^{-1}
respectively. If

, where

, so that
u(y)=\tilde{u}(y^{\prime},y_{n}-g(y^{\prime}))
.

Using
Eq.

2.2
, we see that

\partial_{\nu}u(y)=(1+\left\lvert K\right\rvert^{2})^{-1/2}(K\cdot(\nabla_{x^{\prime}}\tilde{u})(\gamma(y))-(1+\left\lvert K\right\rvert^{2})(\partial_{x_{n}}\tilde{u})(\gamma(y)))=(\gamma^{\ast}\circ\tilde{\partial}_{\nu}\tilde{u})(y)\,,

where

\tilde{\partial}_{\nu}\coloneqq(1+\left\lvert K(x^{\prime})\right\rvert^{2})^{-1/2}(K(x^{\prime})\cdot\nabla_{x^{\prime}}-(1+\left\lvert K(x^{\prime})\right\rvert^{2})\partial_{x_{n}})\,,

(2.5)

so that if

, then

, and

(2.6)

Now if

, and
\gamma^{\ast}\circ\tilde{A}\tilde{u}(y)=\tilde{A}\tilde{u}(x)|_{x=\gamma(y)}

Thus our strategy will be to construct an operator

that has the properties we want on functions defined on
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
and transform this operator back to the original coordinates.

\lim_{h\to 0}\left\lvert\int_{\Omega}e^{2i\xi\cdot y}qr_{+}r_{-}\mathrm{d}y\right\rvert\leq\lim_{h\to 0}\left(\left\lVert q^{\flat}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert r_{+}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert r_{-}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert q^{\sharp}\right\rVert_{L^{n/2}}\left\lVert r_{+}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\left\lVert r_{-}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\right)\leq C\varepsilon\,,

which then must also be zero, taking care of the final term in
Eq.

6.5
.

Since the only term surviving in
Eq.

6.5
is the first, we may conclude that

\mathcal{F}(q)(-2\xi)=\int_{\Omega}e^{2i\xi\cdot y}q\mathrm{d}y=0\,.

This calculation is true for all
\xi
with
\xi\perp\omega
. If we perturb
\omega
by small amounts,
\Xi_{+}
will still be a subset of
\{y\in\partial\Omega\colon\omega\cdot\nu(y)<0\}
. This means perturbing
\omega
slightly, we get an open set of
\xi
in which
\mathcal{F}(q)(-2\xi)=0
, which by the analyticity of
\mathcal{F}
means that
q=q_{+}-q_{-}=0
.
∎

As a direct consequence we are able to prove the uniqueness of conductivies as well.

Proof of
Corollary

1.2
.

This proof follows an argument in
[

]
. For
q_{\pm}\coloneqq-\gamma_{\pm}^{-1/2}\Delta(\gamma^{1/2}_{\pm})\in L^{n/2}(\Omega)
let

satisfy

(\Delta+q_{\pm})u_{\pm}=0\text{ in }\Omega\,,\,u_{\pm}\in H_{\Delta}^{\sharp}(\Omega)\,,\,\mathrm{supp}(\partial_{\nu}u_{\pm}|_{\partial\Omega})\subset F\,.

Defining
w_{\pm}\coloneqq\gamma^{-1/2}_{\pm}u_{\pm}
, a calculation shows

\mathrm{div}(\gamma_{\pm}\nabla w_{\pm})=0\text{ in }\Omega\,,\,w_{\pm}\in H^{1}(\Omega)\,,\,\mathrm{supp}(\partial_{\nu}w_{\pm}|_{\partial\Omega})\subset F\,.

Due to
\partial_{\nu}\gamma_{+}|_{\partial\Omega}=\partial_{\nu}\gamma_{-}|_{\partial\Omega}=0
, we have

(u_{\pm}|_{B},\partial_{\nu}u_{\pm}|_{\partial\Omega})=(\gamma_{\pm}^{1/2}w_{\pm}|_{B},\gamma^{1/2}_{\pm}\partial_{\nu}w_{\pm}|_{\partial\Omega})\,,

and defining the map
M_{\gamma_{\pm}}\colon(v_{1},v_{2})\mapsto(\gamma_{\pm}^{1/2}v_{1},\gamma_{\pm}^{-1/2}v_{2})
, we see

C_{q_{\pm}}^{B,F}\subset M_{\gamma_{\pm}}C_{\gamma_{\pm}}^{B,F}\,.

Together with a Sobolev embedding, a similar argument will lead to the reverse inclusion, so that given the assumptions
C_{\gamma_{+}}^{B,F}=C_{\gamma_{-}}^{B,F}
and
\gamma_{+}|_{\partial\Omega}=\gamma_{-}|_{\partial\Omega}

and an application of
Theorem

1.1
gives
q_{+}=q_{-}
.

Writing out the definitions of
q_{+},q_{-}
, we thus have

0=2\gamma_{+}^{-1/2}\Delta(\gamma_{+}^{1/2})-2\gamma_{-}^{-1/2}\Delta(\gamma_{-}^{1/2})=\Delta(\log\gamma_{+}-\log\gamma_{-})+\frac{1}{2}\nabla(\log\gamma_{+}+\log\gamma_{-})\cdot\nabla(\log\gamma_{+}-\log\gamma_{-})\,.

Putting
\gamma\coloneqq\log\gamma_{+}-\log\gamma_{-}\in W^{2,n/2}(\Omega)\subset W^{1,2}(\Omega)
, we have
\gamma|_{\partial\Omega}=0
and we may apply the weak maximum principle (see
[

, Cor. 8.2]
) to conclude that
\gamma_{+}=\gamma_{-}
.
∎

Lemma .1
.

Let

Eq.

C.6
.
We have

are the seminorm bounds for the symbol
\langle\xi^{\prime}\rangle^{k}a(x^{\prime},\xi^{\prime})
,
C_{n}
depends only on the dimension
n
and
C_{K}

K
.

Assume that
u\in{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})

Remark that by partial integration

K
, and
C_{n}=(2\pi)^{-n+1}
.

Using

C.8

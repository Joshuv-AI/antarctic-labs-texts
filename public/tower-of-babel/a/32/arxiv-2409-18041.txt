We study the existence of ground state solutions and least energy sign-changing solutions for the following problem

\Delta_{p}^{2}u-\lambda_{1}\frac{|u|^{p-2}}{|x|^{2p}}=\frac{|u|^{p_{*}(\alpha)-2}}{|x|^{\alpha}}u+\lambda_{2}\Big(|x|^{-\beta}*|u|^{q}\Big)|u|^{q-2}u\quad\mbox{ in }{\mathbb{R}}^{N},

1.1
Notations and Variational Framework

•

H_{0}^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})=W_{0}^{2,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
is the Hilbert-Sobolev space and we will be denoting it by
E
throughout this article.

•

The Hardy-Sobolev exponent is related to the following Rellich inequality

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\frac{|u|^{p}}{|x|^{2p}}\;\;dx\leq\Big(\frac{p^{2}}{N(p-1)(N-2p)}\Big)^{p}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|\Delta u|^{p}\;\;dx,\quad\mbox{ for all }u\in E.

As per Rellich inequality,
E
can be endowed with the norm

||u||_{E}=\Big(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|\Delta u|^{p}-\lambda_{1}\frac{|u|^{p}}{|x|^{2p}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}},

provided

0<\lambda_{1}<\Big(\frac{N(p-1)(N-2p)}{p^{2}}\Big)^{p}.

•

L^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
denotes the Lebesgue space in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
of order
s\in[1,\infty]
with norm
||.||_{s}
.

•

||u||_{r}\leq S_{r}^{\frac{-1}{p}}||u||_{E},

for all
u\in E
and
p\leq r\leq p_{*}
. Here,

S_{r}=\inf_{u\in E}\frac{\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\Big(|\Delta u|^{p}-\lambda_{1}\frac{|u|^{p}}{|x|^{2p}}\Big)}{\Big(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|x|^{-\alpha}|u|^{r}\Big)^{\frac{p}{r}}}.

•

\hookrightarrow
denotes the continuous embeddings.

Note:
The embedding

E=H_{0}^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})\hookrightarrow L^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}),

is continuous for all
p\leq s\leq p_{*}
and is compact for all
p\leq s<p_{*}
.

•

We will be using the following Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality

\Big|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\Big(|x|^{-\gamma}*u\Big)v\Big|\leq C\|u\|_{r}\|v\|_{t},

(1.6)

for
\gamma\in(0,N)
,
u\in L^{r}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
and
v\in L^{t}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
such that

\frac{1}{r}+\frac{1}{t}+\frac{\gamma}{N}=2.

•

Assume that
q

\frac{p(2N-\beta)}{2N}<q<\frac{p(2N-\beta)}{2(N-2p)}.

(1.7)

•

The equation (
1.1

{\mathcal{J}}:E\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
by

\displaystyle{\mathcal{J}}(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p}\|u\|_{E}^{p}-\frac{1}{p_{*}(\alpha)}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|x|^{-\alpha}|u|^{p_{*}(\alpha)}-\frac{\lambda_{2}}{2q}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\Big(|x|^{-\beta}*|u|^{q}\Big)|u|^{q}.

Using Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality (

) with (

is a solution of (

) .

1.2
Main Results.

Now, we present our main result on existence of ground state solution. We define the Nehari manifold associated with the energy functional
J
by

{\mathcal{N}}=\{u\in E\setminus\{0\}:\langle{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}(u),u\rangle=0\},

(1.9)

and the ground state solutions will be obtained as minimizers of

m=\inf_{u\in{\mathcal{N}}}{\mathcal{J}}(u).

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
N\geq 5
,
\lambda_{2}>0
,
p_{*}(\alpha)>2q>p
and
q
satisfies (
1.7
). Then the equation (
1.1
) has a ground state solution
u\in E
.

Next, we investigate the existence of least energy sign changing solutions for the equation (
1.1
). Now, we use the minimization method on the Nehari nodal set defined as

{\cal\overline{N}}=\Big\{u\in E:u^{\pm}\neq 0\mbox{ and }\langle{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}(u),u^{\pm}\rangle\mbox{ = }0\Big\},

and solutions will be obtained as minimizers for

\overline{m}=\inf_{u\in{\cal\overline{N}}}{\mathcal{J}}(u).

Here,

\displaystyle\langle{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}(u),u^{\pm}\rangle

\displaystyle=\|u^{\pm}\|_{E}^{p}-\frac{1}{p_{*}(\alpha)}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|x|^{-\alpha}(u^{\pm})^{p_{*}(\alpha)}-\frac{\lambda_{2}}{2q}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\Big(|x|^{-\beta}*(u^{\pm})^{q}\Big)(u^{\pm})^{q}-\frac{\lambda_{2}}{2q}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\Big(|x|^{-\beta}*(u^{\pm})^{q}\Big)(u^{\mp})^{q}.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
N\geq 5
,
\lambda_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
p_{*}(\alpha)>2q>p
and
q
satisfies (
1.7
). Then the equation (
1.1
) has a least energy sign changing solution
u\in E
.

\varPhi:D\rightarrow[0,1]
by

\varPhi(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}1\quad\mbox{ if }(\theta_{1}-\bar{\theta_{1}})^{2}+(\theta_{2}-\bar{\theta_{2}})^{2}\leq\frac{\Xi^{2}}{16},\\
0\quad\mbox{ if }(\theta_{1}-\bar{\theta_{1}})^{2}+(\theta_{2}-\bar{\theta_{2}})^{2}\geq\frac{\Xi^{2}}{4}.\end{array}\right.

Next, we define a continuous map
T:D\rightarrow E
by

T(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})=\theta_{1}u^{+}+\theta_{2}u^{-}+\Xi\varPhi(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})\bar{\sigma}\quad\mbox{ for all }(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})\in D

and
Q:D\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
by

Q(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})=(\langle{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}(T(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})),T(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})^{+}\rangle,\langle{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}(T(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})),T(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})^{-}\rangle)\quad\mbox{ for all }(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})\in D.

One could deduce that
Q
is continuous as the mapping
u\mapsto u^{+}
is continuous in
E
. In case, we are on the boundary of
D
, that is,
(\theta_{1}-\bar{\theta_{1}})^{2}+(\theta_{2}-\bar{\theta_{2}})^{2}=\Xi^{2}
, then
\varPhi=0
. Hence, one could get
T(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})=\theta_{1}u^{+}+\theta_{2}u^{-}
and by Lemma
4.1
, we have

Q(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})\neq 0\quad\mbox{ on }\partial{D}.

Therefore, the Brouwer degree is well defined and
\deg(Q,{\rm int}(D),(0,0))=1
and there exists
(\theta_{11},\theta_{21})\in{\rm int}(D)
such that
Q(\theta_{11},\theta_{21})=(0,0)
. This further implies that
T(\theta_{11},\theta_{21})\in{\cal\overline{N}}
and by the definition of
\overline{m}
one could deduce

{\mathcal{J}}(T(\theta_{11},\theta_{21}))\geq\overline{m}.

(4.6)

Using the equation (
4.5
), we get

\displaystyle{\mathcal{J}}(T(\theta_{11},\theta_{21}))

\displaystyle={\mathcal{J}}(\theta_{11}u^{+}+\theta_{21}u^{-})+\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d}{dt}{\mathcal{J}}(\theta_{11}u^{+}+\theta_{21}u^{-}+\Xi t\varPhi(\theta_{11},\theta_{21})\bar{\sigma})dt

(4.7)

\displaystyle={\mathcal{J}}(\theta_{11}u^{+}+\theta_{21}u^{-})-\Xi\varPhi(\theta_{11},\theta_{21}).

Next, by definition of
\varPhi
we get
\varPhi(\theta_{11},\theta_{1})=1
when
(\theta_{11},\theta_{21})=(\bar{\theta_{1}},\bar{\theta_{2}})
. Therefore, we have

{\mathcal{J}}(T(\theta_{11},\theta_{21}))\leq{\mathcal{J}}(\theta_{11}u^{+}+\theta_{21}u^{-})-\Xi\leq\overline{m}-\Xi<\overline{m}.

When
(\theta_{11},\theta_{21})\neq(\bar{\theta_{1}},\bar{\theta_{2}})
, then by Lemma
4.1
we have

{\mathcal{J}}(\theta_{11}u^{+}+\theta_{21}u^{-})<{\mathcal{J}}(\bar{\theta_{1}}u^{+}+\bar{\theta_{2}}u^{-})=\overline{m},

which yields

{\mathcal{J}}(T(\theta_{11},\theta_{21}))\leq{\mathcal{J}}(\theta_{11}u^{+}+\theta_{21}u^{-})<\overline{m},

a contradiction to the equation (
4.6
). Hence, we conclude our proof.

Multidimensional Bohr radii for holomorphic functions with values in complex Banach spaces
Vasudevarao Allu, Himadri Halder, Subhadip Pal

Abstract
. The main aim of this paper is to answer certain open questions related to the exact values of multidimensional Bohr radii by using the concept of arithmetic Bohr radius for holomorphic functions defined in complete Reinhardt domains in ℂ n \mathbb{C}^{n} with values in complex Banach spaces. More specifically, for holomorphic functions with values in arbitrary complex Banach spaces, we explore the asymptotic estimates of the arithmetic Bohr radius in the unit ball of ℓ q n \ell^{n}_{q} ( 1 ≤ q ≤ ∞ ) (1\leq q\leq\infty) spaces. As an application, we obtain the exact value of multidimensional Bohr radius for the unit ball in ℓ 1 n \ell^{n}_{1} spaces which improves the previously known result of Aizenberg.

1.
Introduction

The Bohr radius problem plays a crucial role in connecting two major areas of research in pure mathematics, namely complex analysis and functional analysis. The Bohr radius problem’s situation in multidimensional settings is much more complicated. Several research problems regarding the behavior of the multidimensional Bohr radius remain unresolved due to the lack of certain essential tools. The more interesting aspect of the Bohr radius problem is when we consider it in multidimensional settings for holomorphic functions with values in complex Banach spaces.

For the last two decades, a lot of attention has been paid to the generalizations of the multidimensional Bohr radius problem in various settings (see
[
1
,
3
,
6
,
10
,
12
,
15
,
20
]
). One such generalization is studying Bohr’s theorem for the power series of holomorphic functions defined in complete Reinhardt domains in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. A complete Reinhardt domain
\Omega
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
is a domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that if
z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\in\Omega
, then
(\eta e^{i\theta_{1}}z_{1},\ldots,\eta e^{i\theta_{n}}z_{n})\in\Omega
for all
\eta\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}
and all
\theta_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
,
i=1,\ldots,n
. For a complex Banach space
X
, let
\mathcal{F}(\Omega,X)
be the space of all holomorphic mappings
f
in
\Omega
into
X
and
\mathcal{P}^{k}(\Omega,X)
be the space of all
k
-homogeneous vector-valued polynomials, where
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
H_{\infty}(\Omega,X)
be the set of all bounded holomorphic mappings on
\Omega
into a complex Banach space
X
. In this article, we mostly focus on the aforesaid spaces with
\Omega=B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}
, where
q\in[1,\infty]
. For
q\in[1,\infty)
,

B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:\left\lVert z\right\rVert_{q}=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|z_{i}|^{q}\right)^{1/q}<1\}

and
B_{\ell^{n}_{\infty}}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:\left\lVert z\right\rVert_{\infty}=\sup_{1\leq i\leq n}|z_{i}|<1\}
. For any given
p\in[1,\infty)
,
\lambda\geq 1
, and Reinhardt domain
\Omega
, the
\lambda_{p}
-Bohr radius
r^{n}_{p}(\Omega,X,\lambda)
of
\Omega
with respect to
\mathcal{F}(\Omega,X)
is defined as the supremum of all
r\in[0,1]
such that

\sup_{z\in r\Omega}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}\left\lVert x_{\alpha}(f)z^{\alpha}\right\rVert^{p}\leq\lambda\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{p}_{\Omega}

for all
f\in\mathcal{F}(\Omega,X)
with
f(z)=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}x_{\alpha}(f)z^{\alpha}
and
\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\Omega}=\sup\{\|f(z)\|:z\in\Omega\}
and
\mathbb{N}_{0}=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. We write
r^{n}_{p}(\Omega,X,\lambda)=r_{p}(\Omega,X,\lambda)
for
n=1
.

The remarkable result of Bohr
[
8
]
states that
r_{1}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C},1)=1/3
. Much of the recent interest in Bohr’s theorem stems from Dixon’s work
[
16
]
, when the author used it to disprove a long-standing open problem on the characterization of the Banach algebra satisfying the von Neumann inequality. Since then, a lot of research has been done to generalize Bohr’s theorem in multidimensional and more abstract settings (see
[
2
]
). A significant portion of this research focuses on studying Bohr’s theorem in several variables for vector-valued functions, which led to the introduction of the quantity
r^{n}_{p}(\Omega,X,\lambda)
. However, determining the exact value of
r^{n}_{p}(\Omega,X,\lambda)
is very challenging, and it seems there has not been much progress on this problem even for
X=\mathbb{C}
. For
\lambda\in[1,\sqrt{2}]
, Bombieri
[
9
]
has shown that

r_{1}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C},\lambda)=\frac{1}{3\lambda-2\sqrt{2(\lambda^{2}-1)}}

and for
\lambda
very close to
\infty
, Bombieri and Bourgain
[
10
]
have obtained the following estimate:

r_{1}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C},\lambda)\sim\frac{\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-1}}{\lambda}.

In
1989
, Dineen and Timoney
[
17
]
extensively studied the constant
r^{n}_{1}(B_{\ell^{n}_{\infty}},\mathbb{C},1)
and their result has been clarified in
[
6
]
. In
1997
, Boas and Khavinson
[
6
]
obtained the following estimate for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
n\geq 2
,

(1.1)

\frac{1}{3\sqrt{n}}<r^{n}_{1}(B_{\ell^{n}_{\infty}},\mathbb{C},1)<2\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n}}.

The exact value of
r^{n}_{1}(B_{\ell^{n}_{\infty}},\mathbb{C},1)
is still an open problem, and it has drawn the attention of many mathematicians to work further on the Bohr radius problem. Motivated by the above estimate of Boas and Khavinson, in
2000
, Aizenberg
[
1
]
studied the constant
r^{n}_{1}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},\mathbb{C},1)
with
q=1
and proved that

(1.2)

\lx@nobreakspace\lx@nobreakspace\frac{1}{3e^{1/3}}<r^{n}_{1}(B_{\ell^{n}_{1}},\mathbb{C},1)\leq\frac{1}{3}.

In the same year, Boas
[
7
]
generalized (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) to
r^{n}_{1}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},\mathbb{C},1)
for
1<q<\infty
. For
n>1
, Boas
[
7
]
has proved that, if
1\leq q<2
, then

(1.3)

\frac{1}{3\sqrt[3]{e}}\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)^{1-\frac{1}{q}}\leq r^{n}_{1}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},\mathbb{C},1)<3\left(\frac{\log\,n}{n}\right)^{1-\frac{1}{q}}

and if
2\leq q\leq\infty
, then

(1.4)

\frac{1}{3}\sqrt{\frac{1}{n}}\leq r^{n}_{1}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},\mathbb{C},1)<2\sqrt{\frac{\log\,n}{n}}.

It is easy to see in (
1.3
) and (
1.4
) that the upper bounds contain a logarithmic factor that is not present in the lower bounds. Therefore, Boas observed that (
1.3
) and (
1.4
) do not give a sharp decay rate of Bohr radius with the dimension, and hence he conjectured that this logarithmic factor should not be really present. In
2006
, Defant
et al.

[
14
]
disproved this conjecture by obtaining a logarithmic lower bound that is almost correct asymptotic estimates for the Bohr radius
r^{n}_{1}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},\mathbb{C},1)
with
1\leq q\leq\infty
. In particular, Defant
et al.
have proved that if
1\leq q\leq\infty
then there is a constant
c>0
such that

(1.5)

\lx@nobreakspace\lx@nobreakspace\lx@nobreakspace\frac{1}{c}\left(\frac{\log\,n/\log\,\log\,n}{n}\right)^{1-\frac{1}{\min(q,2)}}\leq r^{n}_{1}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},\mathbb{C},1)\,\,\,\,\,\mbox{for all}\,\,\,n>1.

In
2017
, Blasco
[
5
]
extensively studied the constant
r^{n}_{p}(\Omega,X,\lambda)
for
\Omega=\mathbb{D},n=1,\lambda=1
which is usually known as
p
-Bohr radius
r_{p}(\mathbb{D},X,1)
for arbitrary Banach space
X
. It is worth noting that the precise value of
r_{p}(\mathbb{D},X,1)
is very difficult to compute even for
X=\mathbb{C}
. Blasco
[
5
]
has obtained a lower estimate of
r_{p}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C},1)
and showed that
r_{p}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C},1)\geq p/(p+2)
which is sharp only for
p=1
.

In view of the above discussions together with the estimates (
1.3
), (
1.4
), and (
1.5
), we observe that it is very challenging to determine the exact value of
r^{n}_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,1)
even for
X=\mathbb{C}
. This led us to propose the following challenging problem in more general settings.

Problem 1.6
.

Let
X
be an arbitrary complex Banach space. For every
p\in[1,\infty)
,
q\in[1,\infty]
,
\lambda\geq 1
, and
n>1
, what is the exact value of
r^{n}_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)?

One of our main contributions to this paper is to affirmatively answer the aforesaid problem for arbitrary complex Banach spaces in terms of arithmetic Bohr radius. Another aspect of the multidimensional Bohr radius is the arithmetic Bohr radius, which is more than a curious variant of the classical Bohr radius. In fact, we shall introduce new variants of the arithmetic Bohr radius, which we are going to define in the next two paragraphs.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a complete Reinhardt domain. For each
1\leq p<\infty
and
\lambda\geq 1
, the
\lambda_{p}
-
arithmetic Bohr radius
of
\Omega
with respect to
\mathcal{F}(\Omega,X)
is defined as

A_{p}(\mathcal{F}(\Omega,X),\lambda):=\sup\left\{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}\,|\,r\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0},\,\mbox{for all}\,f\in\mathcal{F}(\Omega,X):\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}\left\lVert x_{\alpha}(f)\right\rVert^{p}r^{p\alpha}\leq\lambda\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{p}_{\Omega}\right\},

where
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}=\{r=(r_{1},.\,.\,.\,,r_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:r_{i}\geq 0,1\leq i\leq n\}.
We write
A_{p}(\Omega,X,\lambda)
for
A_{p}(H_{\infty}(\Omega,X),\lambda)
,
A_{p}(\Omega,X)
for
A_{p}(\Omega,X,1)
and
A^{k}_{p}(\Omega,X,\lambda)
for
A_{p}(\mathcal{P}^{k}(\Omega,X),\lambda)
. For
X=\mathbb{C}
and
p=1
, Defant
et al.

[
15
]
have first considered the constant
A_{1}(\mathcal{F}(\Omega,\mathbb{C}),\lambda)
which is known as usual arithmetic Bohr radius. Recently, Kumar
[
18
]
has also studied the constant
A_{1}(\mathcal{F}(\Omega,\mathbb{C}),\lambda)
. The arithmetic Bohr radius
A_{1}(\mathcal{F}(\Omega,\mathbb{C}),\lambda)
forms a useful tool to describe the domain of existence of the monomial expansion of complex-valued holomorphic functions in a complete Reinhardt domain (cf.
[
19
]
). This led us to introduce the notion of
\lambda_{p}
-arithmetic Bohr radius
A_{p}(\mathcal{F}(\Omega,X),\lambda)
for arbitrary complex Banach spaces, which plays a crucial role in estimating sequences in the set of monomial convergence of vector-valued holomorphic functions in a Reinhardt domain
\Omega
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. One of the main purposes of this paper is to investigate the constant
A_{p}(\mathcal{F}(\Omega,X),\lambda)
by establishing a relation between
\lambda_{p}
- Bohr radius and
\lambda_{p}
-arithmetic Bohr radius. Our results mainly generalize the results obtained in
[
15
]
.

We now concentrate on another multidimensional generalization of the Bohr radius
R_{p,q}(X)
which has been introduced for the first time by Blasco
[
4
]
in order to study the Bohr inequality for Banach space valued holomorphic functions in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
. In this paper, we introduce a new notion of
R_{p,q}(X)
in more general multidimensional settings. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a complete Reinhardt domain. For each
1\leq p,q<\infty
and
\lambda\geq 1
, we define
\lambda_{p,q}
-
Bohr radius
, denoted by
R^{n}_{p,q}(\mathcal{F}(\Omega,X),\lambda)
, of
\Omega
with respect to
\mathcal{F}(\Omega,X)
to be the supremum of all
r\in[0,1]
such that

(1.7)

\sup_{z\in r\Omega}\left(\left\lVert x_{0}\right\rVert^{p}+\left(\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left\lVert x_{\alpha}(f)z^{\alpha}\right\rVert\right)^{q}\right)\leq\lambda\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\Omega}

for all
f\in\mathcal{F}(\Omega,X)
with
f(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=k}x_{\alpha}(f)z^{\alpha}
. We use the convention
R^{n}_{p,q}(\Omega,X,\lambda)
instead of
R^{n}_{p,q}(\mathcal{F}(\Omega,X),\lambda)
and
R^{n}_{p,q}(\Omega,X,\lambda)=R_{p,q}(\Omega,X,\lambda)
for
n=1
.
Blasco
[
4
]
has extensively studied the Bohr radius
R_{p,q}(\mathbb{D},X,1)
for complex Banach spaces
X
. In the same paper, it has been established
[
4
, Theorem 1.2]
that the classical Bohr radius
R(\mathbb{D},\ell^{n}_{p},1)=0
for
1\leq p<\infty
. To make this modified Bohr radius more significant, we introduce the constant
\lambda
in (
1.7
). Determining the exact value of
\lambda_{p,q}
-Bohr radius is difficult in-general for different complex Banach spaces. Recently, Das
[
11
]
has obtained the exact value of
R_{p,q}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C},1)
for
p,q\in[1,\infty)
and the asymptotic behavior of
R^{n}_{p,q}(B_{\ell^{n}_{\infty}},X,1)
for infinite dimensional Banach spaces. In this situation, our aim is to study
\lambda_{p,q}
-Bohr radius
R_{p,q}(\mathbb{D},X,\lambda)
in terms of another notion of arithmetic Bohr radius, namely,
\lambda_{p,q}
-
arithmetic Bohr radius

A_{p,q}(\mathcal{F}(\Omega,X),\lambda)
of
\Omega
with respect to
\mathcal{F}(\Omega,X)
, which is defined by

\displaystyle A_{p,q}(\mathcal{F}(\Omega,X),\lambda):=

\displaystyle\sup\left\{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}\,|\,r\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0},\,\mbox{for all}\,f\in\mathcal{F}(\Omega,X):\left\lVert x_{0}\right\rVert^{p}+\left(\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left\lVert x_{\alpha}(f)\right\rVert r^{\alpha}\right)^{q}\leq\lambda\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\Omega}\right\},

where
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}=\{r=(r_{1},.\,.\,.,r_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:r_{i}\geq 0,1\leq i\leq n\}
and
\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\Omega}=\sup\{\|f(z)\|:z\in\Omega\}
.

The organization of this paper is as follows: In Theorem
2.1
, we provide the estimate of
\lambda_{p}
-arithmetic Bohr radius of bounded holomorphic functions in terms of
\lambda_{p}
-arithmetic Bohr radius of
k
-homogeneous polynomials in complete Reinhardt domains. Theorem
2.2
gives the asymptotic estimates of the
\lambda_{p}
-arithmetic Bohr radius for the
n
-dimensional
\ell^{n}_{q}
-balls. Next, Theorem
2.5
relates the classical
\lambda_{p}
-Bohr radius and the
\lambda_{p}
-arithmetic Bohr radius in the unit ball of
\ell^{n}_{q}
spaces. After giving an important estimate of the arithmetic Bohr radius of homogeneous polynomials in Proposition
2.5
, we give a positive answer to Problem
1.6
in Theorem
2.4
with regard to the exact value of
\lambda_{p}
-Bohr radii in unit ball of
\ell^{n}_{q}
spaces. We establish the connection between
\lambda_{p,q}
-arithmetic Bohr radii of different vector-valued holomorphic function spaces and the bounded holomorphic function spaces in Theorem
2.5
. Finally, we investigate the exact value of
\lambda_{p,q}
-arithmetic Bohr radius of unit ball in
\ell^{n}_{1}
space through the proof of Theorem
2.6
.

2.
Main Results

Our first result provides an estimate of
\lambda_{p}
-arithmetic Bohr radius of bounded holomorphic functions in terms of
\lambda_{p}
-arithmetic Bohr radius of
k
-homogeneous polynomials in complete Reinhardt domains.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a complete Reinhardt domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
\lambda\geq 1
,
1\leq p<\infty
, and
C=\max\{1/3,((\lambda-1)/(2\lambda-1))^{1/p}\}
. Then we have

C\,A_{p}\left(\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{P}^{k}(\Omega),\mathbb{C},\lambda\right)\leq A_{p}\left(H_{\infty}(\Omega),\lambda\right)\leq A_{p}\left(\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{P}^{k}(\Omega),\mathbb{C},\lambda\right).

It is worth mentioning that the result of Defant
et. al.

[
15
, Proposition 2.2]
follows from Theorem
2.1
for
p=1
. In the following result, Theorem
2.2
, we are going to give the asymptotic estimates of the
\lambda_{p}
-arithmetic Bohr radius for the
n
-dimensional
\ell^{n}_{p}
-balls. The main ingredients of our proof of Theorem
2.2
are the following facts, which we record as Lemma
2.3
, Lemma
2.4
, and Proposition
2.5
. To make the statement of these results brief, let us recall the following convention from
[
15
]
.
For bounded Reinhardt domains
\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
, let

S(\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2}):=\inf\left\{s>0:\Omega_{1}\subset s\Omega_{2}\right\}.

By a Banach sequence space
Y
, we mean a complex Banach space
Y\subset\mathbb{C}^{\mathbb{N}}
such that
\ell_{1}\subset Y\subset\ell_{\infty}
. If
\Omega
is a bounded Reinhardt domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
Y
and
Z
are Banach sequence spaces, we write

(2.1)

S(\Omega,B_{Y_{n}})=\sup_{z\in\Omega}\left\lVert z\right\rVert_{Y}\quad\mbox{and }\quad S(B_{Y_{n}},B_{Z_{n}})=\left\lVert\mbox{id}:Y_{n}\rightarrow Z_{n}\right\rVert,

where
Y_{n}
(resp.
Z_{n}
) is the space spanned by first
n
canonical basis vectors
e_{n}
in
Y
(resp.
Z
). Throughout the paper,
X
will stand for a complex Banach space. The next result compares the
\lambda_{p}
-arithmetic Bohr radii for two bounded Reinhardt domains in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2}
be bounded Reinhardt domains in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
1\leq p<\infty
. Then for
\lambda\geq 1
, we have

A_{p}(\Omega_{1},X,\lambda)\leq S(\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2})\,A_{p}(\Omega_{2},X,\lambda).

Remark 2.1
.

(a)

For a bounded Reinhardt domain
\Omega
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, it is easy to observe that
S(\Omega,t\Omega)=1/t
and
S(t\Omega,\Omega)=t
for all
t>0.
Therefore, an immediate consequence from Lemma
2.2
we have that for any
1\leq p<\infty
,

A_{p}(t\Omega_{1},X,\lambda)=tA_{p}(\Omega_{1},X,\lambda),\quad\mbox{for all}\quad t>0.

(b)

A_{p}(\cdot,X,\lambda)
is increasing, that is,
A_{p}(\Omega_{1},X,\lambda)\leq A_{p}(\Omega_{2},X,\lambda)
whenever
\Omega_{1}\subset\Omega_{2}.

(c)

It is obvious that
A_{p}(\Omega,X,\lambda)\leq A^{k}_{p}(\Omega,X,\lambda)
.

(d)

We note that Lemma
2.2
generalizes the result of Defant
et. al.

[
15
, Lemma 4.2]
.

Using the above notation, the following lemma gives a connection between the
\lambda_{p}
-arithmetic Bohr radius
A_{p}(\Omega,X,\lambda)
for bounded Reinhardt domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
and the
p
-Bohr radius
r^{n}_{p}(\Omega,X,1)
of a complex Banach space
X
, which helps us to obtain the lower bound in Theorem
2.2
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a bounded Reinhardt domain and
1\leq p<\infty
. Then for
\lambda\geq 1
we have,

A_{p}(\Omega,X,\lambda)\geq\frac{S(\Omega,B_{\ell^{n}_{1}})}{n}r^{n}_{p}(\Omega,X,1).

We need the following estimate for
k
-homogeneous polynomials
(k\geq 2)
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
which has been proved by Boas
[
7
]
.

Lemma 2.4
.

[
7
]

Let
1\leq q\leq\infty
and
n
and
k
are positive integers greater than
1
. Then there exists a homogeneous polynomial of degree
k
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and signs
\epsilon_{\alpha}\in\{-1,1\}
such that

\bigg\|\sum_{|\alpha|=k}\epsilon_{\alpha}\frac{k!}{\alpha!}z^{\alpha}\bigg\|_{B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}}\leq\sqrt{32k\log(6k)}\,\times\,\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}n^{\frac{1}{2}}(k!)^{1-\frac{1}{q}}&\mbox{ if \,$q\leq 2$},\\[2.84526pt]
n^{\frac{1}{2}+\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}\right)k}(k!)^{\frac{1}{2}}&\mbox{ if \,$q\geq 2$}.\end{array}\right.

Now we obtain an upper bound for
\lambda_{p}
-arithmetic Bohr radius of
k
-homogeneous polynomials in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
which later gives an upper bound for
\lambda_{p}
-arithmetic Bohr radius of
B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}\,\,(1\leq q\leq\infty)
with respect to
H_{\infty}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X).

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
\lambda\geq 1
. Then for all
1\leq q\leq\infty
and
k,n\geq 2
we have

A^{k}_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)\leq d\sqrt[pk]{\lambda}\sqrt[2k]{k\log k(k!)^{2(1-(1/\min\{2,q\}))}n}\,\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{\max\{2,q\}}-\frac{1}{2}},

where
d
is a uniform constant.

Following the techniques from
[
15
,
13
]
and all above preparation, we now wish to state one of our main results with regard to the asymptotic estimates of the
\lambda_{p}
-arithmetic Bohr radius for the
n
-dimensional
\ell^{n}_{q}
-balls.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
\lambda\geq 1
. Then there exists a constant
d>0
such that for all
1\leq q\leq\infty
and
n>1
, we have

(2.6)

\frac{r^{n}_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,1)}{n^{1/q}}\leq A_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)\leq d\,\frac{\left(\log n\right)^{1-\left(1/\min\{q,2\}\right)}}{n^{\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{\max\{2,q\}}-\frac{1}{2}}}.

Remark 2.2
.

In Theorem
2.2
, for
p=1
we obtain the upper estimate of
[
15
, Theorem 4.1]
, but we feel that the lower estimate of (
2.6
) can be improved for arbitrary complex Banach spaces.

Next, we present a relationship between arithmetic Bohr radius and geometric property of complex Banach spaces. Recently, Das
[
11
]
has studied the
p
-Bohr radius
r_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{\infty}},X,1)
of a complex Banach space
X
. In the same paper
[
11
, Theorem 1.5]
, the author has established the asymptotic estimates of
r_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{\infty}},X,1)
for any
p
-uniformly
PL
-convex
(p\geq 2)
complex Banach spaces
X
. A complex Banach space
X
is said to be
p
-uniformly
PL
-convex
(2\leq p<\infty)
if there exists a constant
\delta>0
such that

(2.7)

\left\lVert x\right\rVert^{p}+\delta\left\lVert y\right\rVert^{p}\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left\lVert x+e^{i\theta}y\right\rVert^{p}\,d\theta

for all
x,y\in X
. We denote
I_{p}(X)
be the supremum of all such
\delta>0
satisfying (
2.7
).

In view of
[
11
, Theorem 1.5]
, Lemma
2.3
and the fact that
S(B_{\ell^{n}_{\infty}},B_{\ell^{n}_{1}})=1/n
, we have the following consequence.

Corollary 2.8
.

Let
B_{\ell^{n}_{\infty}}
be the unit polydisk in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
2\leq p<\infty
. Then for all
\lambda\geq 1
and
n>1
, we have

A_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{\infty}},X,\lambda)\geq\frac{1}{n^{2}}\left(\frac{I_{p}(X)}{2^{p}+I_{p}(X)}\right)^{2/p},

where
X
is any
p
-uniformly PL-convexity complex Banach space
X
with dim
(X)\geq 2
.

Now we are in position to give an interesting connection between the classical
\lambda_{p}
-Bohr radius and
\lambda_{p}
-arithmetic Bohr radius in unit ball of
\ell^{n}_{q}
spaces.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
1\leq\lambda<\infty
and
1\leq p<\infty
. Then for every
n
and
1\leq q<\infty
we have

\frac{r_{p}(\mathbb{D},X,\lambda)}{n}\leq A_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)\leq\left(\frac{r_{p}(\mathbb{D},X,\lambda)}{n^{1/p}}\right)^{1/q}.

As we promised before, we obtain an affirmative answer of our proposed Problem
1.6
in the following result.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
X
be a complex Banach space and
1\leq p<\infty
and
1\leq q\leq\infty
. Then for
\lambda\geq 1
, and for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

A_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)=\frac{r^{n}_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)}{n^{1/q}}.

From Theorem
2.3
and Theorem
2.4
, we obtain the following important result.

Corollary 2.9
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
\lambda\geq 1
. Then for every
1\leq q<\infty
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\frac{r_{p}(\mathbb{D},X,\lambda)}{n^{1-(1/q)}}\leq r^{n}_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)\leq\left(\frac{r_{p}(\mathbb{D},X,\lambda)}{n^{(1/p)-1}}\right)^{1/q}.

In view of Corollary
2.9
, we obtain a surprising consequence that the vector-valued Bohr radius of unit ball in
\ell^{n}_{1}
space coincides with the classical vector-valued Bohr radius in unit disk in complex plane. We state the consequence as our next corollary which reduces the result of Kumar
[
18
, Corollary 2.6]
for
X=\mathbb{C}
.

Corollary 2.10
.

For every
\lambda\in[1,\infty)
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have
r^{n}_{1}(B_{\ell^{n}_{1}},X,\lambda)=r_{1}(\mathbb{D},X,\lambda).

For
p=1
and
\lambda=1
, the exact value of arithmetic Bohr radius
A_{1}(B_{\ell^{n}_{1}},\mathbb{C},1)
was settled by Defant and his coauthors
[
15
, Theorem 3.1]
: For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

(2.11)

A_{1}(B_{\ell^{n}_{1}},\mathbb{C},1)=\frac{1}{3n}.

By virtue of Theorem
2.4
and (
2.11
) we obtain the following important observation.

Corollary 2.12
.

For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

(2.13)

r^{n}_{1}(B_{\ell^{n}_{1}},\mathbb{C},1)=\frac{1}{3}.

In view of the above Corollary
2.12
, it is very interesting to note that we obtain the exact value of multidimensional Bohr radius
r^{n}_{1}(B_{\ell^{n}_{1}},\mathbb{C},1)
for the unit ball in
\ell^{n}_{1}
spaces as
1/3
, which is the classical Bohr radius. It is also worth noting that the result (
2.13
) significantly improves the estimate (
1.2
) of Aizenberg
[
1
]
.

We want to recall that we have provided the notion of
\lambda_{p,q}
-arithmetic Bohr radius in Section
1
. In the following main result, we establish that the
\lambda_{p,q}
-arithmetic Bohr radii of different vector-valued holomorphic function spaces
\mathcal{O}
corresponds to
\lambda_{p,q}
-arithmetic Bohr radius of bounded holomorphic function space if
\mathcal{O}
contains all vector-valued polynomials.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
\Omega
be a complete Reinhardt domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
\lambda\geq 1
. If
\mathcal{O}(\Omega,X)
is a space of all holomorphic mappings on
\Omega
into a complex Banach space
X
containing all vector-valued polynomials then for every
1\leq p,q,<\infty
, we have

A_{p,q}(\mathcal{O}(\Omega,X),\lambda)=A_{p,q}(H_{\infty}(\Omega,X),\lambda).

An analogous version of Theorem
2.3
can also be shown in the setting of
\lambda_{p,q}
-arithmetic Bohr radius which later provides us an exact value of arithmetic Bohr radius.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
1\leq q<\infty
and
\lambda\geq 1
. Then for every
1\leq p_{1},q_{1}<\infty
and
n
we have

\frac{R_{p_{1},q_{1}}(\mathbb{D},X,\lambda)}{n}\leq A_{p_{1},q_{1}}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)\leq\left(\frac{R_{p_{1},q_{1}}(\mathbb{D},X,\lambda)}{n}\right)^{1/q}.

In 2010, O. Blasco
[
4
, Proposition 1.4]
, established the exact value of Bohr radius
R_{p,1}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C})
as
p/(p+2)
for
1\leq p\leq 2
and
\lambda=1
. In view of of Blasco’s result, we record the following easy consequence of Theorem
2.6
as the exact value of arithmetic Bohr radius of the unit ball in
\ell^{n}_{1}
.

Corollary 2.14
.

For every
1\leq p\leq 2
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have
A_{p,1}(B_{\ell^{n}_{1}},\mathbb{C},1)=p/(n(p+2)).

3.
Proof of Main Results

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

The right-hand inequality is clear from the fact that

\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{P}^{k}(\Omega)\subset H_{\infty}(\Omega).

Choose
r\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}
such that for all
k\geq 1
and for all
k
-homogeneous polynomial
h\in\mathcal{P}^{k}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
, we have

(3.1)

\sum_{|\alpha|=k}|c_{\alpha}(h)r^{\alpha}|^{p}\leq\lambda\left\lVert h\right\rVert^{p}_{\Omega}.

We wish to show that

(3.2)

\max\left\{\frac{1}{3},\left(\frac{\lambda-1}{2\lambda-1}\right)^{1/p}\right\}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}\leq A_{p}(H_{\infty}(\Omega)_{,}\,\lambda).

Fix
f\in H_{\infty}(\Omega)
. Without loss of generality, we assume that
\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\infty}=1
and consider its monomial series expansion as
f(z)=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}c_{\alpha}(f)z^{\alpha}
.

Given
z\in\Omega
, we define the function
h:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
by

h(\zeta)=f(\zeta z)=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}c_{\alpha}(f)\zeta^{|\alpha|}z^{\alpha}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{|\alpha|=k}c_{\alpha}(f)z^{\alpha}\right)\zeta^{k}.

Let
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
be such that
e^{i\theta}c_{0}(f)=|c_{0}(f)|
and consider the function
F(\zeta)=1-e^{i\theta}h(\zeta)
,
\eta\in\mathbb{D}
. It is worth to notice that
\zeta z\in\Omega
for every
\zeta\in\mathbb{D}
, thus we have
\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq 1
on
\mathbb{D}
and

\mbox{Re}\,F(\zeta)=\mbox{Re}\left(1-e^{i\theta}h(\zeta)\right)\geq 0.

Suppose
F(\zeta)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}\zeta^{k}
, then

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}\zeta^{k}=1-\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(e^{i\theta}\sum_{|\alpha|=k}c_{\alpha}(f)z^{\alpha}\right)\zeta^{k}=1-e^{i\theta}c_{0}(f)+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(e^{i\theta}\sum_{|\alpha|=k}c_{\alpha}(f)z^{\alpha}\right)\zeta^{k}.

It follows that
a_{0}=1-|c_{0}(f)|
and
a_{k}=e^{i\theta}\sum_{|\alpha|=k}c_{\alpha}(f)z^{\alpha}
, for
k\in\mathbb{N}.

In view of Carathéodory’s theorem
[
3
]
, we obtain that

(3.3)

\left|\sum_{|\alpha|=k}c_{\alpha}(f)z^{\alpha}\right|\leq 2(1-|c_{0}(f)|).

From (
3.1
) and (
3.3
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}\left|c_{\alpha}(f)\left(\frac{r}{3}\right)^{\alpha}\right|^{p}

\displaystyle=|c_{0}(f)|^{p}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=k}\left|c_{\alpha}(f)\left(\frac{r}{3}\right)^{\alpha}\right|^{p}

\displaystyle=|c_{0}(f)|^{p}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{3^{pk}}\sum_{|\alpha|=k}|c_{\alpha}(f)|^{p}r^{p\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq|c_{0}(f)|^{p}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{3^{pk}}\,\lambda\sup_{z\in\Omega}\left|\sum_{|\alpha|=k}c_{\alpha}(f)z^{\alpha}\right|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq|c_{0}(f)|^{p}+\lambda\,\frac{2^{p}}{3^{p}-1}\left(1-|c_{0}(f)|\right)^{p}\leq\lambda=\lambda\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\Omega}.

This gives one of the estimates in (
3.2
). To obtain the other estimate, we set
s=({(2\lambda-1)}/{(\lambda-1)})^{1/p}.
By going similar lines of argument as above and using the well-known Cauchy’s inequalities, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}\left|c_{\alpha}(f)\left(\frac{r}{s}\right)^{\alpha}\right|^{p}

\displaystyle=|c_{0}(f)|^{p}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{s^{pk}}\sum_{|\alpha|=k}|c_{\alpha}(f)|^{p}r^{p\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq|c_{0}(f)|^{p}+\lambda\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{s^{pk}}\left\lVert P_{k}(f)\right\rVert^{p}_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq|c_{0}(f)|^{p}+\lambda\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{p}_{\Omega}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{s^{pk}}\leq\left(1+\frac{\lambda}{s^{p}-1}\right)\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{p}_{\Omega}=\lambda\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{p}_{\Omega}.

Therefore,
(1/sn)\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}\leq A_{p}(H_{\infty}(\Omega),\mathbb{C},\lambda).
This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Lemma
2.2
.

Let
r=(r_{1},\cdots,r_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}
be such that

\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}\left\lVert x_{\alpha}(f)r^{\alpha}\right\rVert^{p}\leq\lambda\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{p}_{\Omega_{1}}

for all
f(z)=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}x_{\alpha}(f)z^{\alpha}\in H_{\infty}(\Omega_{1},X)
. Set
t:=S(\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2})
. Then for all
\epsilon>0
, we have

\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}\left\lVert x_{\alpha}(f)\right\rVert^{p}\left(\frac{r}{t+\epsilon}\right)^{p\alpha}\leq\lambda\bigg\|\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}x_{\alpha}(f)\left(\frac{z}{t+\epsilon}\right)^{\alpha}\bigg\|^{p}_{\Omega_{1}}\leq\lambda\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{p}_{\Omega_{2}}

for all
f\in H_{\infty}(\Omega_{2},X).
By letting
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
, we obtain

\frac{1}{nt}\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}\leq A_{p}(\Omega_{2},X,\lambda).

Therefore, it follows that
A_{p}(\Omega_{1},X,\lambda)\leq S(\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2})\,A_{p}(\Omega_{2},X,\lambda).

∎

Proof of Lemma
2.3
.

It is sufficient to give a proof for the case
\lambda=1
. That is, for arithmetic Bohr radius
A_{p}(\Omega,X)
, as
A_{p}(\Omega,X,\lambda)
is increasing in
\lambda
. In view of (
2.1
), we have

S(\Omega,B_{\ell^{n}_{1}})=\sup_{z\in\Omega}\left\lVert z\right\rVert_{\ell^{n}_{1}}.

Therefore, for given
0<\epsilon<r^{n}_{p}(\Omega,X,1)
, we can find an element
\tilde{z}\in\Omega
such that

\left\lVert\tilde{z}\right\rVert_{\ell^{n}_{1}}\geq S(\Omega,B_{\ell^{n}_{1}})-\epsilon.

Let
s:=r^{n}_{p}(\Omega,X,1)-\epsilon
,
w:=s\tilde{z}
, and
r:=s|\tilde{z}|=|w|.
Since
w\in s\Omega
and
s<r^{n}_{p}(\Omega,X,1)
, for
f=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}x_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}\in H_{\infty}(\Omega,X)
, we have

\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}\left\lVert x_{\alpha}\right\rVert^{p}r^{p\alpha}=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}\left\lVert x_{\alpha}w^{\alpha}\right\rVert^{p}\leq\bigg\|\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}x_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}\bigg\|^{p}_{s\Omega}\leq\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{p}_{\Omega}.

Therefore, we obtain

A_{p}(\Omega,X)\geq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}=\frac{r^{n}_{p}(\Omega,X,1)-\epsilon}{n}\left\lVert\tilde{z}\right\rVert_{\ell^{n}_{1}}\geq\frac{r^{n}_{p}(\Omega,X,1)-\epsilon}{n}\left(S(\Omega,B_{\ell^{n}_{1}})-\epsilon\right)

holds for all
\epsilon>0.
This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Proposition
2.5
.

Let
r=(r_{1},.\,.\,.,r_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}
be such that

\sum_{|\alpha|=k}\left\lVert x_{\alpha}(f)\right\rVert^{p}r^{p\alpha}\leq\lambda\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{p}_{B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}}

for all
f\in\mathcal{P}^{k}(\mathbb{C}^{n},X)
. From Hölder’s inequality, we have
\left\lVert r\right\rVert^{p}_{1}\leq n^{p-1}\left\lVert r\right\rVert^{p}_{p}
. In view of Lemma
2.4
, there exist signs
\epsilon_{\alpha}\in\{-1,1\}
such that

\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}\right)^{pk}\leq\frac{1}{n^{k}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}r^{p}_{i}\right)^{k}=\frac{1}{n^{k}}\sum_{|\alpha|=k}\frac{k!}{\alpha!}r^{p\alpha}\leq\frac{\lambda}{n^{k}}\bigg\|\sum_{|\alpha|=k}\epsilon_{\alpha}\frac{k!}{\alpha!}z^{\alpha}\bigg\|^{p}_{B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}}\leq\frac{\lambda}{n^{k}}R^{p}_{k},

where
R_{k}
is the expression in the right-hand side of Lemma
2.4
. Taking the
pk
-th root of both sides, we obtain

A^{k}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)\leq\frac{\sqrt[pk]{\lambda}\sqrt[k]{R_{k}}}{n^{1/p}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.

In view of Lemma
2.3
and using the fact that

S(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},B_{\ell^{n}_{1}})=n^{1-(1/q)}\quad\,\mbox{for all}\,\,\,1\leq q\leq\infty,

we obtain the lower bound of (
2.6
). For the upper bound of
A_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)
, by Proposition
2.5
, it is enough to show that there exists a constant
d>0
such that for all
n>1
we are able to find a
k\,(\geq 2)
with

(3.4)

\sqrt[pk]{\lambda}\sqrt[2k]{k\log k(k!)^{2\left(1-\left(1/\min\{2,q\}\right)\right)}n}\leq d\,\left(\log n\right)^{1-\left(1/\min\{2,q\}\right)}.

It is worth to note that the quantity
\sqrt[2k]{k\log k}
is bounded in
k
and also
\sqrt[k]{k!}\leq k/\sqrt{2}.
So we can estimate the left-hand side of (
3.4
) upto a constant independent of
k
and
n
by

(3.5)

\sqrt[pk]{\lambda}\,k^{1-\left(1/\min\{2,q\}\right)}\sqrt[2k]{n}.

Let us choose
k=2
for
n=2,.\,.\,.,8
and
k=[\log n]
(the greatest integer less than or equal to
\log n
) for
n\geq 9
. Then, the above expression (
3.5
) is bounded above by

\lambda^{\frac{2}{p\log n}}(\log n)^{1-\frac{1}{\min\{2,q\}}}e,

which gives the desired bound. This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.3
.

Let
r=r_{p}(\mathbb{D},X,\lambda)
and
f(z)=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}x_{\alpha}(f)z^{\alpha}\in H_{\infty}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X)
. Consider the function
g(z)=f(\xi z)
, where
\xi=(1,0,.\,.\,.,0)\in B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}
and
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Clearly,
g\in H_{\infty}(\mathbb{D},X)
with
\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{\mathbb{D}}=\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}}
.
Therefore, we have

\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}\left\lVert x_{\alpha}(f)\right\rVert^{p}(r,0,\,.\,.\,.,0)^{p\alpha}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left\lVert x_{k\xi}(f)\right\rVert^{p}r^{pk}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left\lVert x_{k}(g)\right\rVert^{p}r^{pk}\leq\lambda\left\lVert g\right\rVert^{p}_{\mathbb{D}}=\lambda\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{p}_{B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}}.

This shows that
(r/n)\leq A_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)
, and hence we obtain
(r_{p}(\mathbb{D},X,\lambda)/n)\leq A_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda).
Conversely, we prove that

A_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)\leq\left(\frac{r_{p}(\mathbb{D},X,\lambda)}{n^{1/p}}\right)^{1/q}.

Suppose
r\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}
such that for all
h\in H_{\infty}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X)
,

\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}\left\lVert x_{\alpha}(h)\right\rVert^{p}r^{p\alpha}\leq\lambda\left\lVert h\right\rVert^{p}_{B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}}.

We want to prove that

\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{n}r_{j}}{n}\leq\left(\frac{r_{p}(\mathbb{D},X,\lambda)}{n^{1/p}}\right)^{1/q}.

Let
f:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow X
be a bounded holomorphic map and we consider the function
s:B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
defined by

s(z)=z^{q}_{1}+\cdots+z^{q}_{n},\quad z\in B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}.

Now if we set
h=f\circ s
, then we have
\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}}=\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\mathbb{D}}.

Moreover, for each
z\in B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}
,

h(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}x_{k}(f)s(z)^{k}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}x_{k}(f)\sum_{|\alpha|=k}\frac{k!}{\alpha!}z^{q\alpha}=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}x_{\alpha}(h)z^{q\alpha},

where

x_{\alpha}(h)=\frac{k!}{\alpha!}x_{k}(f)

whenever
|\alpha|=k
. Then for all
z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}\left\lVert x_{\alpha}(h)\right\rVert^{p}|z|^{pq\alpha}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left\lVert x_{k}(f)\right\rVert^{p}\sum_{|\alpha|=k}\left(\frac{k!}{\alpha!}\right)^{p}|z|^{pq\alpha}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left\lVert x_{k}(f)\right\rVert^{p}\sum_{|\alpha|=k}\frac{k!}{\alpha!}|z|^{pq\alpha}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left\lVert x_{k}(f)\right\rVert^{p}\left\lVert z\right\rVert^{pqk}_{pq}.

An observation shows that

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left\lVert x_{k}(f)\right\rVert^{p}\left\lVert r\right\rVert^{pqk}_{pq}\leq\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}\left\lVert x_{\alpha}(h)r^{q\alpha}\right\rVert^{p}\leq\lambda\left\lVert h\right\rVert^{p}_{B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}}=\lambda\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{p}_{\mathbb{D}},

which implies that
\left\lVert r\right\rVert^{q}_{pq}\leq r_{p}(\mathbb{D},X,\lambda).
Again we have
\left\lVert r\right\rVert^{pq}_{1}\leq n^{(pq-1)}\left\lVert r\right\rVert^{pq}_{pq}.
Hence we obtain
n^{(1-pq)}\left\lVert r\right\rVert^{pq}_{1}\leq r^{p}_{p}(\mathbb{D},X,\lambda)
. This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.4
.

The inequality
A_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)\geq{r^{n}_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)}/{n^{1/q}}
follows from Lemma
2.3
. Therefore, we only need to show that

(3.6)

A_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)\leq\frac{r^{n}_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)}{n^{1/q}}.

Let
r=(r_{1},.\,.\,.,r_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}
be such that

\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}\left\lVert x_{\alpha}(g)\right\rVert^{p}r^{p\alpha}\leq\lambda\left\lVert g\right\rVert^{p}_{B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}}

for all
g(z)=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}x_{\alpha}(g)z^{\alpha}\in H_{\infty}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X)
. Then, there exists
\tilde{z}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\in r^{n}_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)\overline{B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}}
with
|z_{j}|=r_{j}
for
1\leq j\leq n
satisfying
\left\lVert\tilde{z}\right\rVert_{q}=\left\lVert r\right\rVert_{q}\leq r^{n}_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)
and

\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}\left\lVert x_{\alpha}(g)\tilde{z}\right\rVert^{p}\leq\lambda\left\lVert g\right\rVert^{p}_{B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}}.

To show (
3.6
), it suffices to prove that
n^{(1/q)-1}\left\lVert r\right\rVert_{1}\leq r^{n}_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda).

Fix
f\in H_{\infty}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X)
, then for
v\in n^{(1/q)-1}\left\lVert r\right\rVert_{1}\overline{B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}}
, we have
\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{q}\leq\left\lVert r\right\rVert_{q}\leq r^{n}_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)
.
Hence, by virtue of the definition of Bohr radius we obtain

\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}\left\lVert x_{\alpha}(f)v^{\alpha}\right\rVert^{p}\leq\lambda\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{p}_{B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}}

holds for every
v\in n^{(1/q)-1}\left\lVert r\right\rVert_{1}\overline{B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}}.
Therefore, it follows that

n^{1/q}A_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)\leq r^{n}_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda),

which gives our conclusion. This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.5
.

We start with an observation that

\mathcal{P}(\Omega,X)\subseteq\mathcal{O}(\Omega,X)\subseteq H_{\infty}(\Omega,X).

Then, we have the following inclusion

A_{p,q}(H_{\infty}(\Omega,X),\lambda)\leq A_{p,q}(\mathcal{O}(\Omega,X),\lambda)\leq A_{p,q}(\mathcal{P}(\Omega,X),\lambda).

It suffices to show that for any
\lambda\geq 1
,
A_{p,q}(\mathcal{P}(\Omega,X),\lambda)\leq A_{p,q}(H_{\infty}(\Omega,X),\lambda)
.

Assume
r\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}
such that for all
P\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{C}^{n},X)
,

(3.7)

\left\lVert x_{0}(P)\right\rVert^{p}+\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left\lVert x_{\alpha}(P)\right\rVert r^{\alpha}\right)^{q}\leq\lambda\left\lVert P\right\rVert_{\Omega}.

Fix
f\in H_{\infty}(\Omega,X)
and
0<t<1
. Our aim is to show that

(3.8)

\left\lVert x_{0}(f)\right\rVert^{p}+\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left\lVert x_{\alpha}(f)\right\rVert(tr)^{\alpha}\right)^{q}\leq\lambda\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\Omega}.

For
k\geq 0
, consider the
k
-homogeneous polynomials
\chi_{k}=\sum_{|\alpha|=k}x_{\alpha}(f)z^{\alpha}
, where
x_{\alpha}(f)
is the
\alpha
-th coefficient in the monomial series expansion
\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}x_{\alpha}(f)z^{\alpha}
of
f
. Note that
\Omega
is balanced, so by Cauchy inequalities we have

(3.9)

\left\lVert\chi_{k}\right\rVert_{t\Omega}=t^{k}\left\lVert\chi_{k}\right\rVert_{\Omega}\leq t^{k}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\Omega}.

Therefore, the series
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\chi_{k}
converges absolutely and uniformly to
f
on
t\Omega
. Thus, given
\epsilon>0
, there exits
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
n\geq n_{0}
,

\bigg\|\sum_{k=0}^{n}\chi_{k}\bigg\|_{t\Omega}\leq\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{t\Omega}+\epsilon\leq\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\Omega}+\epsilon.

We now consider the polynomials
\Gamma_{k}(z)=\chi_{k}(tz)
for
z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Then,

x_{\alpha}(\Gamma_{k})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}t^{k}x_{\alpha}(f)&\mbox{ if \,$|\alpha|=k$},\\[2.84526pt]
0&\mbox{ if \,$|\alpha|\neq k$}.\end{array}\right.

From (
3.7
) and (
3.9
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\left\lVert x_{0}(f)\right\rVert^{p}+\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left\lVert x_{\alpha}(f)\right\rVert(tr)^{\alpha}\right)^{q}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert x_{0}(\Gamma_{0})\right\rVert^{p}+\left(\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}\bigg\|x_{\alpha}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\Gamma_{k}\right)r^{\alpha}\bigg\|\right)^{q}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\bigg\|\sum_{k=0}^{n}\Gamma_{k}\bigg\|_{\Omega}=\lambda\bigg\|\sum_{k=0}^{n}\chi_{k}\bigg\|_{t\Omega}\leq\lambda\left(\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\Omega}+\epsilon\right),

which holds for all
\epsilon>0
. Consequently, this proves (
3.8
).
Hence for every
r\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}
satisfying (
3.7
) and for all
f\in H_{\infty}(\Omega,X)
and
0<t<1
, we have shown that

t\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}\leq A_{p,q}(H_{\infty}(\Omega,X),\lambda),

which proves our claim. This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.6
.

First we show the left-hand inequality
(R_{p_{1},q_{1}}(\mathbb{D},X,\lambda))/n\leq A_{p_{1},q_{1}}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda).
Assume
r=R_{p_{1},q_{1}}(\mathbb{D},X,\lambda)
and
f\in H_{\infty}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X)
. We define
g(z)=f(ze_{1})=f(z,0,.\,.\,.,0)
for
z\in\mathbb{D}.
Then
g\in H_{\infty}(\mathbb{D},X)
and
\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{\mathbb{D}}=\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}}
. Further,

\displaystyle\left\lVert x_{0}(f)\right\rVert^{p_{1}}+\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left\lVert x_{\alpha}(f)\right\rVert(r,0,.\,.\,.,0)^{\alpha}\right)^{q_{1}}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert x_{0}(f)\right\rVert^{p_{1}}+\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left\lVert x_{ke_{1}}(f)\right\rVert r^{k}\right)^{q_{1}}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert x_{0}(g)\right\rVert^{p_{1}}+\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left\lVert x_{k}(g)\right\rVert r^{k}\right)^{q_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{\mathbb{D}}=\lambda\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}}.

Therefore, we have
r/n=A_{p_{1},q_{1}}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)
, which gives the desired inequality.

On the other hand, we want to prove that
A_{p_{1},q_{1}}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)\leq\left({R_{p_{1},q_{1}}(\mathbb{D},X,\lambda)}/{n}\right)^{1/q}.

Let
r\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}
be such that

\left\lVert x_{0}(u)\right\rVert^{p_{1}}+\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=k}\left\lVert x_{\alpha}(u)\right\rVert r^{\alpha}\right)^{q_{1}}\leq\lambda\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}}

for all
u\in H_{\infty}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X)
. Now our aim is to show that

\frac{1}{n}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}r_{j}\right)\leq\left(\frac{R_{p_{1},q_{1}}(\mathbb{D},X,\lambda)}{n}\right)^{1/q}.

Fix
f\in H_{\infty}(\mathbb{D},X)
, and define the function

v(z)=z^{q}_{1}+\cdots+z^{q}_{n},\quad z=(z_{1},.\,.\,.,z_{n})\in B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}.

Then for
u=f\circ v
, we have
\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}}=\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\mathbb{D}}
. Also for each
z\in B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}
, we obtain

u(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}x_{k}(f)v(z)^{k}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}x_{k}(f)\sum_{|\alpha|=k}\frac{k!}{\alpha!}z^{q\alpha}=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}x_{\alpha}(u)z^{q\alpha},

where
x_{\alpha}(u)=x_{k}(f)(k!/\alpha!)
whenever
|\alpha|=k
. Then for all
z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert x_{0}(u)\right\rVert^{p_{1}}+\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left\lVert x_{\alpha}(u)\right\rVert|z|^{q\alpha}\right)^{q_{1}}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert x_{0}(f)\right\rVert^{p_{1}}+\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left\lVert x_{k}(f)\right\rVert\sum_{|\alpha|=k}\frac{k!}{\alpha!}|z|^{q\alpha}\right)^{q_{1}}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert x_{0}(f)\right\rVert^{p_{1}}+\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left\lVert x_{k}(f)\right\rVert\left\lVert z\right\rVert^{qk}_{q}\right)^{q_{1}},

and hence

\displaystyle\left\lVert x_{0}(f)\right\rVert^{p_{1}}+\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left\lVert x_{k}(f)\right\rVert\left\lVert r\right\rVert^{qk}_{q}\right)^{q_{1}}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert x_{0}(u)\right\rVert^{p_{1}}+\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left\lVert x_{\alpha}(u)\right\rVert r^{q\alpha}\right)^{q_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}}=\lambda\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\mathbb{D}}.

Therefore, it follows that
\left\lVert r\right\rVert^{q}_{q}\leq R_{p_{1},q_{1}}(\mathbb{D},X,\lambda)
. Since we have
\left\lVert r\right\rVert^{q}_{1}\leq n^{q-1}\left\lVert r\right\rVert^{q}_{q}
, finally we obtain
n^{1-q}\left\lVert r\right\rVert^{q}_{1}\leq R_{p_{1},q_{1}}(\mathbb{D},X,\lambda)
. This completes the proof.
∎

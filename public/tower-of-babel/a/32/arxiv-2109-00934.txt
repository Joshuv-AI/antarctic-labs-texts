Mean value formulas for classical solutions to uniformly parabolic equations in divergence form
Emanuele Malagoli , Diego Pallara Sergio Polidoro

Abstract
We prove surface and volume mean value formulas for classical solutions to uniformly parabolic equations in divergence form. We then use them to prove the parabolic strong maximum principle and the parabolic Harnack inequality. We emphasize that our results only rely on the classical theory, and our arguments follow the lines used in the original theory of harmonic functions. We provide two proofs relying on two different formulations of the divergence theorem, one stated for sets with almost C 1 C^{1} -boundary , the other stated for sets with finite perimeter.

Let

we have

We outline here the parametrix method for the construction of a fundamental solution
{\Gamma}
of
\mathscr{L}
.
We first note that, if the matrix
A
in the operator
\mathscr{L}
is constant, then the fundamental solution of

is explicitly known

{\Gamma}_{A}(z;{\zeta})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{(4\pi(t-{\tau}))^{N}\det A}}\exp\left(-\frac{\langle A^{-1}(x-{\xi}),x-{\xi}\rangle}{4(t-{\tau})}\right),

(2.1)

and moreover the

{\Gamma}_{A}(z;{\zeta})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}{\Gamma}_{A}(x,t;y,s){\Gamma}_{A}(y,s;{\xi},{\tau})dy,

(2.2)

for every

and
s\in\mathbb{R}
with
{\tau}<s<t
.
A direct computation shows that, for every
T>0
, and
\Lambda^{+}>\Lambda
as in (

),
there exists a positive constant

such that

\begin{split}\left|\frac{\partial{\Gamma}_{A}}{\partial{x_{j}}}(z;{\zeta})\right|\leq\frac{C^{+}}{\sqrt{t-{\tau}}}{\Gamma}^{+}(z;{\zeta}),\qquad\left|\frac{\partial^{2}{\Gamma}_{A}}{\partial{x_{i}x_{j}}}(z;{\zeta})\right|\leq\frac{C^{+}}{t-{\tau}}{\Gamma}^{+}(z;{\zeta})\end{split}

Here the function

is the fundamental solution of
\Lambda^{+}\Delta-\frac{\partial}{\partial t}

The

More specifically, for every fixed

3
A generalized divergence theorem

Let
\Omega
be an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and let
\Phi\in C^{1}\left(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
. The classical divergence formula reads

\int_{E}\mathrm{div}\,\Phi\ dz=-\int_{\partial E}\langle\nu,\Phi\rangle\ d\mathscr{H}^{n-1},

(3.1)

where
E
is a bounded set such that
\overline{E}\subset\Omega
and its boundary is
C^{1}
.

We are interested in the situation in which
E
is the
super-level
set of a real valued
function
F\in C^{1}\left({\Omega}\right)
, that is
E=\left\{F>y\right\}
for some
y\in\mathbb{R}
. At every point

z\in\partial E
such that
\nabla F(z)\neq 0
the
inner
unit normal vector
\nu=\nu(z)

appearing in (
3.1
) is defined as
\nu(z)=\frac{1}{|\nabla F(z)|}\nabla F(z)
and
\partial E

is a
C^{1}
manifold in a neighborhood of
z
. But, if we denote

\mathrm{Crit}\left(F\right):=\left\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\nabla F=0\right\},

the set of
critical points
and
F\left(\mathrm{Crit}\left(F\right)\right)
the set of
critical values
of

F
, under our hypotheses we cannot apply the classical Sard theorem to state that
“for almost every
y\in\mathbb{R}
the level set
\{F=y\}
is globally a
C^{1}
manifold”. Indeed, Whitney proves in
[
22
]
that there exist functions
F\in C^{1}\left({\Omega}\right)
having the property that
\{F=y\}\cap\mathrm{Crit}\left(F\right)
is not empty for every
y
.
Therefore, the purpose of this section is to discuss a version of (
3.1
)
when the boundary of
E
is
C^{1}
up to a closed set of null Hausdorff measure and to see
how it can be applied in our framework. We first introduce the class of sets with the relevant
regularity and state the corresponding divergence formula.
We draw this definition and the following theorem from
[
15
, Section 9.3]
.

Definition 3.1

An open set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
has almost
C^{1}
-boundary if there is a closed set
M_{0}\subset\partial E

with
\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(M_{0})=0
such that, for every
z_{0}\in M=\partial E\setminus M_{0}
there exist
s>0
and

F\in C^{1}(B(z_{0},s))
with the property that

\displaystyle B(z_{0},s)\cap E

\displaystyle=\{z\in B(z_{0},s):\ F(z)>0\},

\displaystyle B(z_{0},s)\cap\partial E

\displaystyle=\{z\in B(z_{0},s):\ F(z)=0\}

and
\nabla F(z)\neq 0
for every
z\in B(z_{0},s)
.
We call
M
the regular part of
\partial E
(note that
M
is a
C^{1}
-hypersurface).
The inner unit normal to
E
is the continuous vector field

\nu\in C^{0}(M;{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1})
given by

\nu(z)=\frac{\nabla F(z)}{|\nabla F(z)|},\quad z\in B(z_{0},s)\cap M.

Let us state the divergence theorem for sets with almost
C^{1}
-boundary.

Theorem 3.2

If
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an open set with almost
C^{1}
-boundary and
M
is the regular part of its boundary,
then for every
\Phi\in C^{1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{n})

\int_{E}\mathrm{div}\,\Phi\ dz=-\int_{M}\langle\nu,\Phi\rangle\ d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}.

(3.2)

If
F\in C^{1}\left({\Omega}\right)
and
E=\left\{F>y\right\}
for some

BV
Functions)

Let
u\in L^{1}\left(\Omega\right)
; we say that
u
is a function of bounded variation in
\Omega
if its
distributional derivative
Du=\left(D_{1}u,\ldots,D_{n}u\right)
is an
\mathbb{R}^{n}
-valued Radon measure in
\Omega
,
i.e., if

\int_{\Omega}u\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial z_{i}}\ dz=-\int_{\Omega}\varphi\,dD_{i}u,\quad\forall\>\varphi\in C_{c}^{1}\left(\Omega\right),\;i=1,\ldots,n

or, in vectorial form,

\int_{\Omega}u\,\mathrm{div}\,\Phi\ dz=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{\Omega}\Phi_{i}\ dD_{i}u=-\int_{\Omega}\langle\Phi,Du\rangle,\quad\forall\>\Phi\in C_{c}^{1}\left(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n}\right).

(6.1)

The vector space of all functions of bounded variation in
\Omega
is denoted by
BV\left(\Omega\right)
.
The variation
V\left(u,\Omega\right)
of
u
in
\Omega
is defined by:

V\left(u,\Omega\right):=\sup\left\{\int_{\Omega}u\,\mathrm{div}\,\Phi\ dz:\Phi\in C^{1}_{c}\left(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n}\right),\,\left\lVert\Phi\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq 1\right\}.

We recall that
{V\left(u,\Omega\right)=\lvert Du\rvert\left(\Omega\right)}<\infty
for any
u\in BV\left(\Omega\right)
, where

\lvert Du\rvert
denotes the total variation of the measure
Du
. We also recall that if

u\in C^{1}\left(\Omega\right)
then

V\left(u,\Omega\right)=\int_{\Omega}\lvert\nabla u\rvert dz.

When the function
u
is the characteristic functions
\chi_{E}
of some measurable set, its variation is said
perimeter of
E
.

Definition 6.2
(
Sets of finite perimeter)

Let
E
be an
\mathcal{L}^{n}-
measurable subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For any open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
the perimeter of
E
in
\Omega
is denoted by
P\left(E,\Omega\right)
and it is the variation of
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}_{E}
in

\Omega
, i.e.,

P\left(E,\Omega\right):=\sup\left\{\int_{E}\mathrm{div}\,\Phi\ dz:\Phi\in C^{1}_{c}\left(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n}\right),\,\left\lVert\Phi\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq 1\right\}.

We say that
E
is a set of finite perimeter in
\Omega
if
P\left(E,\Omega\right)<\infty
.

Obviously, several properties of the perimeter of
E
can be stated in terms of the variation of
\chi_{E}
. In particular, if
\mathcal{L}^{n}(E\cap\Omega)
is finite, then
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}_{E}\in L^{1}\left(\Omega\right)
and
E
has finite perimeter in
\Omega
if and only if
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}_{E}\in BV\left(\Omega\right)
and
P\left(E,\Omega\right)=\lvert D{{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}_{E}}\rvert\left(\Omega\right)
. Both the notations
|D\chi_{E}|(B)
and
P(E,B)
,
B
Borel, are used
to denote the total variation measure of
\chi_{E}
on a Borel set
B
and we say that
E
is a set of locally
finite perimeter in
\Omega
if
P(E,K)<\infty
for every compact set
K\subset\Omega
. Finally, formula (
6.1
) looks like a divergence theorem:

\int_{E}\,\mathrm{div}\,\Phi\ dz=-\int_{\Omega}\langle\Phi,D\chi_{E}\rangle,\quad\forall\>\Phi\in C_{c}^{1}\left(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n}\right),

(6.2)

but it becomes more readable if some precise information is given on the set where the measure
D\chi_{E}

is concentrated. Therefore, we introduce the notions of
reduced boundary
and of
density
and
recall the structure theorem for sets with finite perimeter due to E. De Giorgi, see
[
7
]

and
[
1
, Theorem 3.59]
, and the characterization due to H. Federer.

Definition 6.3
(
Reduced boundary)

Let
\Omega
be an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and let
E
be a set of locally finite perimeter in
\Omega
.
We say that
z\in\Omega
belongs to the reduced boundary
{\mathcal{F}}E
of
E
if
|D\chi_{E}|(B_{\varrho}(z))>0

for every
{\varrho}>0
and the limit

\nu_{E}\left(z\right):=\lim_{{\varrho}\to 0^{+}}\frac{D{{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}_{E}}\left(B_{{\varrho}}\left(z\right)\right)}{\lvert D{{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}_{E}}\rvert\left(B_{{\varrho}}\left(z\right)\right)}

exists in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and satisfies
\lvert\nu_{E}\left(z\right)\rvert=1
. The function

\nu_{E}:{\mathcal{F}}E\rightarrow{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}

is Borel continuous and it is called the generalized (or measure-theoretic) inner normal to
E
.

Notice that the reduced boundary is a subset of the topological boundary. The Besicovitch differentiation
theorem, see e.g.
[
1
, Theorem 2.22]
, yields
D{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}_{E}=\nu_{E}\lvert D{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}_{E}\rvert
,
and
\lvert D{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}_{E}\rvert(\Omega\setminus{\mathcal{F}}E)=0
, hence (
6.2
) becomes

\int_{E}\mathrm{div}\,\Phi\ dz=-\int_{{\mathcal{F}}E}\langle\nu_{E},\Phi\rangle\ d\lvert D{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}_{E}\rvert,\quad\forall\>\Phi\in C^{1}_{c}\left(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N}\right).

(6.3)

The relation between the topological boundary and the reduced boundary can be further analyzed by
introducing the notion of
density
of a set at a given point.

Definition 6.4
(
Points of density
\alpha
)

For every
\alpha\in\left[0,1\right]
and every
\mathcal{L}^{n}-
measurable set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
we denote by
E^{\left(\alpha\right)}
the set

E^{\left(\alpha\right)}=\left\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\lim_{{\varrho}\to 0^{+}}\frac{\mathcal{L}^{n}(E\cap B_{\varrho}\left(z\right))}{\mathcal{L}^{n}(B_{\varrho}\left(z\right))}=\alpha\right\}.

Thus
E^{\left(\alpha\right)}
, which turns out to be a Borel set, is the set of all points where
E
has density

\alpha
. The sets
E^{\left(0\right)}
and
E^{\left(1\right)}
are called
the measure-theoretic exterior
and
interior
of
E
and, in general, strictly contain the topological exterior and interior of the set
E
, respectively.
We recall the well known
Lebesgue’s density theorem
, that asserts that for every

\mathcal{L}^{n}-
measurable set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

\mathcal{L}^{n}(E\triangle E^{\left(1\right)})=0,\quad\mathcal{L}^{n}(\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus E\right)\triangle E^{\left(0\right)})=0,

i.e.
, the density of
E
is
0
or
1
at
\mathcal{L}^{n}-
almost every point in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
This notion allows to introduce the

essential
or
measure-theoretic
boundary of
E
as

\partial^{*}E=\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus(E^{\left(0\right)}\cup E^{\left(1\right)})
, which is contained in the topological boundary
and contains the reduced boundary. Finally, the De Giorgi structure theorem says that

|D{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}_{E}|=\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}{\mathcal{F}}E
and a deep result due to Federer
(see
[
10
, 4.5.6]
or

[
1
, Theorem 3.61]
) states that if
E
has finite perimeter in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
then

\mathcal{F}E\subset E^{\left(1/2\right)}\subset\partial^{*}E\quad\text{and}\quad\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus(E^{\left(0\right)}\cup\mathcal{F}E\cup E^{\left(1\right)}))=0

hence, in particular,
\nu_{E}
is defined
\mathscr{H}^{n-1}-
a.e. in
\partial^{*}E
. Notice also (see

[
1
, Theorem 3.62]
) that if
\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(\partial E)<\infty
then
E
has finite
perimeter. The results of De Giorgi and Federer imply that if
E
is a set of finite perimeter in

\Omega
then
D{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}_{E}=\nu_{E}\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\mathcal{F}E
and the divergence
theorem (
6.3
) can be rewritten in the form:

\int_{E}\mathrm{div}\,\Phi\ dz=-\int_{{\mathcal{F}}E}\langle\nu_{E},\Phi\rangle\ d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}=-\int_{\partial^{*}E}\langle\nu_{E},\Phi\rangle\ d\mathscr{H}^{n-1},\quad\forall\>\Phi\in C^{1}_{c}\left(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n}\right),

(6.4)

much closer to the classical formula (
3.1
). Indeed, the only difference is that the inner normal and the boundary are understood in a measure-theoretic sense and not in the topological one; in particular, for a generic set of finite perimeter,
{\mathcal{F}}E
needs not to be closed and
\nu_{E}
needs not to be continuous.
Moreover,
\nu_{E}
is defined
\mathscr{H}^{n-1}-
a.e. in
\partial^{*}E
.

Let us see now how we can rephrase the results of Section
3
in terms of
perimeters and how we can modify the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
We first recall the Fleming–Rischel formula
(see
[
11
]
or
[
1
, Theorem 3.40]
),
i.e.
, the coarea
formula for
BV
functions.

Theorem 6.5
(
Coarea formula in
BV
)

For any open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
G\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}\left(\Omega\right)
one has

V\left(G,\Omega\right)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}P\left(\{z\in\Omega:G\left(z\right)>y\},\Omega\right)\ dy.

In particular, if
G\in BV\left(\Omega\right)
the set
\left\{G>y\right\}
has finite perimeter in
\Omega
for

\mathscr{H}^{1}-
a.e.
y\in\mathbb{R}
and

\lvert DG\rvert\left(B\right)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\lvert D{{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}_{\left\{G>y\right\}}}\rvert\left(B\right)\ dy,\quad DG\left(B\right)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}D{{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}_{\left\{G>y\right\}}}\left(B\right)\ dy,\quad\forall\>B\in\mathcal{B}\left(\Omega\right).

Now we are ready to state the analogue of Proposition
3.4
and to prove Theorem
1.1

again.

Proposition 6.6

Let
\Omega
be an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and let
F\in BV\left({\Omega};\mathbb{R}\right)\cap C\left({\Omega};\mathbb{R}\right)
.
Then, for
\mathscr{H}^{1}-
almost every
y\in\mathbb{R}
, we have:

\int_{\left\{F>y\right\}}\mathrm{div}\,\Phi\ dz=-\int_{\partial^{*}\{F>y\}}\langle\nu,\Phi\rangle\ d\mathscr{H}^{n-1},\quad\forall\>\Phi\in C_{c}^{1}\left(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n}\right),

(6.5)

were
\nu
is the generalized inner normal to
\{F>y\}
.

Proof.
By Theorem
6.5
,
the set
\left\{F>y\right\}
has finite perimeter in
\Omega
for
\mathscr{H}^{1}-
a.e.
y\in\mathbb{R}
,
hence we may apply (
6.4
) with
E=\{F>y\}
and conclude.

\square

As in Section
3
, we have to cut the integration
domain: therefore, we study the intersection between the super-level set of a
generic function
G\in BV\left(\Omega;\mathbb{R}\right)\cap C\left(\Omega;\mathbb{R}\right)
and a half-space

H_{t}=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\langle x,e\rangle<t\right\}
,
for some
e\in{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
,
t\in\mathbb{R}

, Theorem 16.3]
.

Theorem 6.7
(
Intersection of sets of finite perimeter)

If
A
and
B
are sets of locally finite perimeter in
\Omega
, and we let

\left\{\nu_{A}=\nu_{B}\right\}=\left\{x\in{\mathcal{F}}A\cap{\mathcal{F}}B:\nu_{A}\left(x\right)=\nu_{B}\left(x\right)\right\},

then
A\cap B
is a set of locally finite perimeter in
\Omega
, with

D{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}_{A\cap B}=D{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}_{A}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}B^{\left(1\right)}+D{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}_{B}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}A^{\left(1\right)}+\nu_{A}\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\left\{\nu_{A}=\nu_{B}\right\}.

(6.6)

In the case in which
B
is a half-space, formula (
6.6
) can be greatly simplified;
indeed we can prove the following corollary.

Corollary 6.8
(
Intersections with a half-space)

Let
E
be a set of locally finite perimeter in
\Omega
and let

H_{t}=\left\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\langle z,e\rangle<t\right\}
for some
e\in{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Then, for every

t\in\mathbb{R}
,
E\cap H_{t}
is a set of locally finite perimeter in
\Omega
and moreover,
for
\mathscr{H}^{1}-
almost every
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

D{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}_{E\cap H_{t}}=D{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}_{E}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}H_{t}-e\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\left(E\cap\left\{\langle x,e\rangle=t\right\}\right).

Proof.

The half-space
H_{t}
is clearly a set of locally finite perimeter in
\Omega
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
and for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
we have,
H_{t}^{\left(1\right)}=H_{t}
,

\mathcal{F}H_{t}=\partial H_{t}=\left\{\langle x,e\rangle=t\right\}
and
\nu_{H_{t}}\equiv-e
.
Then, applying Theorem
6.7
we see that
E\cap H_{t}
is a set of locally finite perimeter
in
\Omega
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
and (
6.6
) reads

D{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}_{E\cap H_{t}}=D{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}_{E}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}H_{t}-e\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}(E^{\left(1\right)}\cup\left\{\nu_{E}=\nu_{H_{t}}\right\}).

Since by Fubini theorem

0=\mathcal{L}^{n}(E\triangle E^{(1)})=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\left((E\triangle E^{(1)})\cap\left\{\langle x,e\rangle=t\right\}\right)\ dt,

for
\mathscr{H}^{1}-

\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\left(E\triangle E^{\left(1\right)}\cap\left\{\langle x,e\rangle=t\right\}\right)=0.

Therefore,

D{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}_{H_{t}}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}E=D{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}_{H_{t}}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}E^{\left(1\right)}=-e\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}(E\cap\{\langle x,e\rangle=t\})=-e\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}(E\cap\{\nu_{E}=\nu_{H_{t}}\})

for
\mathscr{H}^{1}-
a.e.

and the thesis follows.

\square

Corollary 6.9

Let
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{N+1}\setminus\left\{\left(z_{0}\right)\right\}
,
G\in BV\left(\Omega;\mathbb{R}\right)\cap C\left(\Omega;\mathbb{R}\right)
,

H_{t}=\left\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1}:\langle z,e\rangle<t\right\}
for some

,

. Then,
for
\mathscr{H}^{1}-
almost every

and for every

the set

E\cap H_{t}
has locally finite perimeter in
\Omega
and

\int_{\left\{G>w\right\}\cap H_{t}}\mathrm{div}\,\Phi\ dz=-\int_{\partial^{*}\{G>w\}\cap H_{t}}\langle\nu,\Phi\rangle\ d\mathscr{H}^{N}+\int_{\left\{G>w\right\}\cap\left\{\langle x,e\rangle=t\right\}}\langle e,\Phi\rangle\ d\mathscr{H}^{N},

for every
\Phi\in C^{1}_{c}\left(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
, where
\nu
is the generalized inner normal to

\partial^{*}(\{G>w\})
.
In particular, if
e=\left(0,\ldots,0,1\right)
, for every

\int_{\left\{G>w\right\}\cap\left\{t<t_{0}-\varepsilon\right\}}\!\!\!\mathrm{div}\,\Phi\ dz=-\int_{\partial^{*}\{G>w\}\cap\left\{t<t_{0}-\varepsilon\right\}}\!\!\langle\nu,\Phi\rangle d\mathscr{H}^{N}+\int_{\left\{G>w\right\}\cap\left\{t=t_{0}-\varepsilon\right\}}\!\!\langle e,\Phi\rangle d\mathscr{H}^{N}.

Notice that the difference between Proposition

and

is that in the former we can exclude the set of critical points of

from

{\rm Crit}(G)
and
\nu
is defined
\mathscr{H}^{N}-
a.e.
on

out of

,

6.9

) and (

\mathscr{H}^{N}(\{G=w\}\setminus\partial^{*}\{G>w\})=0

\mathscr{H}^{1}

.
Let us see how this entails modifications of the

: the proof goes in the same vein until (

),

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to+\infty}\int_{\psi_{r}(z_{0})\cap\left\{t<t_{0}-\varepsilon_{k}\right\}}\langle\nu,\Phi\rangle d\mathscr{H}^{N}

\displaystyle=\int_{\psi_{r}(z_{0})}K(z_{0};z)u(z)d\mathscr{H}^{N}

. Let ω > 0 \omega>0 . Given a strongly continuous semigroup { e t ​ A } \{e^{tA}\} on a Banach space and an element f ∈ 𝐃 ⁡ ( A 2 ) f\in{\mathbf{D}}(A^{2}) satisfying the exponential orbital estimates ‖ e t ​ A ​ f ‖ ≤ e − ω ​ t ​ ‖ f ‖ and ‖ e t ​ A ​ A 2 ​ f ‖ ≤ e − ω ​ t ​ ‖ A 2 ​ f ‖ , t ≥ 0 , \|e^{tA}f\|\leq e^{-\omega t}\|f\|\quad\text{and}\quad\|e^{tA}A^{2}f\|\leq e^{-\omega t}\|A^{2}f\|,\quad t\geq 0, a dynamical inequality for ‖ A ​ f ‖ \|Af\| in terms of ‖ f ‖ \|f\| and ‖ A 2 ​ f ‖ \|A^{2}f\| was derived by Herzog and Kunstmann (Studia Math., 2014). 1.
Introduction

The classical Landau inequality for functions defined on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}:=(0,\infty)
states

\|f^{\prime}\|_{\infty}^{2}\leq 4\|f\|_{\infty}\|f^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty},

where
f^{\prime}=\frac{df}{dx}
and
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}=\|\cdot\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})}
.
The constant 4 can be replaced by 2 if one considers the Hilbert space
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
instead of
L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
;
this is a classical result of Hardy, Littlewood, and Pólya.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

Suppose that for some

a=\|f\|,\quad b=\frac{\|Af\|}{\omega}\quad\text{and}\quad c=\frac{\|A^{2}f\|}{\omega^{2}},

\phi(s)(a+c)+s(c-b)+(a-c)\geq 0,\quad\forall\,s\geq 0.

(1.5)

In particular,

b^{2}\leq 4ac.

(1.6)

Proof of (

We follow the beautiful proof of (

For

we have the following integral representation

\displaystyle e^{tA}f

\displaystyle=f+\int_{0}^{t}e^{\tau A}Afd\tau

\displaystyle=f+\int_{0}^{t}\left(Af+\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{\tau^{\prime}A}A^{2}fd\tau^{\prime}\right)d\tau

\displaystyle=f+tAf+\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)e^{\tau A}A^{2}fd\tau,\quad t\geq 0.

In the last step we used the integration by parts.
Then by the triangle inequality and the orbital decay assumptions for
e^{tA}
on

and
A^{2}f
, we have

\displaystyle\phi(\omega t)\|f\|

\displaystyle\geq\|e^{tA}f\|

\displaystyle\geq\|f+tAf\|-\left(\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)\phi^{\prime\prime}(\omega\tau)d\tau\right)\|A^{2}f\|

\displaystyle\geq t\|Af\|-\|f\|-\|A^{2}f\|\left(\frac{\phi(\omega t)-1}{\omega^{2}}+\frac{t}{\omega}\right).

In the last step we used again the integration by parts, together with
\phi(0)=-\phi^{\prime}(0)=1

Rewriting (

4.
Kato inequality via semigroups orbits

As Open Problem, Herzog and Kunstmann demanded at the end of
[
HK14
]
an analogue of Theorem
1.1
for (
1.2
).
The aim of this section is to provide such a result by using the Hilbertian machinery of Hayashi and Ozawa in

HO17
]

Thus we are working in a Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
with norm
\|\cdot\|
and inner product
(\cdot\mid\cdot)
.
The following lemma based on Hilbertian geometry is elementary but very useful.

Lemma 4.1
.

For all
f_{1}
,
f_{2}
and
f_{3}
that are elements in
{\mathcal{H}}
, we have

\|f_{1}+f_{2}+f_{3}\|^{2}+\|f_{2}\|^{2}=\|f_{1}\|^{2}+\|f_{3}\|^{2}+2\text{{\rm Re}}\,(f_{1}+f_{2}\mid f_{2}+f_{3}).

Proof.

By straightforward expansion of the involved norms and inner products.
∎

For any
\lambda\geq 0
and
f\in{\mathbf{D}}(A^{2})\backslash\{0\}
, taking in Lemma
4.1

f_{1}=A^{2}f,\quad f_{2}=\lambda Af\quad\text{and}\quad f_{3}=\lambda^{2}f

and dropping out the nonnegative term
\|f_{1}+f_{2}+f_{3}\|^{2}
, we have

2

2

Note that the case
\lambda=0
for (
4.1
) is trivial.

\lambda^{2}\|Af\|^{2}\leq\|A^{2}f\|^{2}+\lambda^{4}\|f\|^{2}

(4.1)

once we assume the following “restricted dissipativity”

\text{{\rm Re}}\,(A(A+\lambda)f\mid(A+\lambda)f)\leq 0,\quad\lambda>0.

(4.2)

Note that the inequality (
4.1
) is equivalent to the Kato inequality (
1.2
).

Now, suppose for some
f\in{\mathbf{D}}(A^{2})\backslash\{0\}
one has a family of orbital estimates

\|e^{tA}(A+\lambda)f\|\leq\|(A+\lambda)f\|,\quad t\geq 0,\quad\lambda>0.

From (
4.3

\text{{\rm Re}}\,(e^{tA}(A+\lambda)f\mid(A+\lambda)f)\leq((A+\lambda)f\mid(A+\lambda)f)

and its infinitesimal version (

Hence we proved the following “semigroup orbits” result in the spirit of

]

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\{e^{tA}\}_{t\geq 0}
be a strongly continuous semigroup on a Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
with norm
\|\cdot\|
.
Then, the family of semigroup orbital estimates (
4.3
) for some element
f\in{\mathbf{D}}(A^{2})\backslash\{0\}

implies the Kato inequality (
1.2
) for the same
f
.

Remark 4.3
.

The orbital estimates (
1.3

and
f

\lambda f

\lambda>0

whereas (
4.3

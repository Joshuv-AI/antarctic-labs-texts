On the Dirichlet problem for the one-dimensional ROF functional
Piotr Rybka

Abstract
We provide a number of sufficient conditions for that minimizers of the one-dimensional Rudin-Osher-Fatemi functional satisfy the Dirichlet data in the trace sense. For this purpose we use results specific for the total variation flow. We also show a number of counterexamples.



in memory of Haïm Brezis

Key words:

BV
functions, Rudin-Osher-Fatemi functional, total variation flow, Dirichlet boundary data

1
Introduction

We study the solvability of the minimization problem for
the Rudin-Osher-Fatemi functional (ROF functional for short)
\Phi_{f,\lambda,\varphi}
in one dimension over functions satisfying the Dirichlet boundary conditions, i.e.,

\gamma u=\varphi,

(1.1)

where
\gamma:BV(\Omega)\to L^{1}(\partial\Omega)
is the trace operator.

More precisely, we define the ROF functional over
L^{2}(\Omega)

as follows

\Phi_{f,\lambda,\varphi}(u)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\int_{0}^{L}|Du|+\frac{\lambda}{2}\int_{0}^{L}(u-f)^{2}\,dx,\quad\gamma u=\varphi&u\in BV(\Omega),\\
+\infty&u\in L^{2}(\Omega)\setminus BV(\Omega).\end{array}\right.

(1.2)

where
\Omega=(0,L)
.
Here,
|Du|
is the variation of the measure
Du
,
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and
\varphi:\partial\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
, i.e.,
\varphi(0)=a,

\varphi(L)=b
.

The first issue we face is the lack of the lower semicontinuity of
\Phi_{f,\lambda,\varphi}
. If we want to study minimizers of
\Phi_{f,\lambda,\varphi}
, then we
have to consider the lower semicontinuous envelope,
\bar{\Phi}_{f,\lambda,\varphi}
instead of
\Phi_{f,\lambda,\varphi}
. It is well-known, see
[
5
]
, that we have

\displaystyle\bar{\Phi}_{f,\lambda,\varphi}(u)

\displaystyle=\Phi_{f,\lambda,\varphi}(u)+\int_{\partial\Omega}|\gamma u-\varphi|\,d\mathcal{H}^{0}

(1.3)

\displaystyle\equiv\Phi_{f,\lambda,\varphi}(u)+|\gamma u-\varphi|(0)+|\gamma u-\varphi|(L).

Now, by the direct method of the calculus of variation we can guarantee the existence of solutions to the following minimization problem,

\bar{\Phi}_{f,\lambda,\varphi}(u_{f,\lambda,\varphi})=\min\{\bar{\Phi}_{f,\lambda,\varphi}(v):v\in BV(\Omega)\}.

(1.4)

Moreover, the strict convexity of
\bar{\Phi}_{f,\lambda,\varphi}
implies uniqueness of
u_{f,\lambda,\varphi}
. However, in general
\bar{\Phi}_{f,\lambda,\varphi}(u_{f,\lambda,\varphi})\leq\Phi_{f,\lambda,\varphi}(u_{f,\lambda,\varphi})
, because
u_{f,\lambda,\varphi}
need not satisfy (
1.1
)
in the trace sense.

Inspired by Haïm Brezis Open Problem 3 in
[
4
]
,
we would like to present quite general sufficient conditions for

\bar{\Phi}_{f,\lambda,\varphi}(u_{f,\lambda,\varphi})=\Phi_{f,\lambda,\varphi}(u_{f,\lambda,\varphi}),

where
u_{f,\lambda,\varphi}
is a solution of (
1.4
).
Brezis stated his question for
\lambda=1
. We find it advantageous to immerse it into a broader family of problems depending on the triple
D=(f,\lambda,\varphi)
.
Our analysis depends on all pieces of the data so that we make this dependence explicit in the definition of the ROF functional. At the same time for the sake of simplicity of notation we will write
\bar{\Phi}_{D}
for
\bar{\Phi}_{f,\lambda,\varphi}
and
u_{D}
for
u_{f,\lambda,\varphi}
.

We will be able to answer the question of attainment of the boundary data in the trace sense in the following cases:

(1) The forcing term
f\in BV(\Omega)
satisfies the boundary conditions,
\gamma f=\varphi
. Then we can show that the minimizer
u_{D}
inherits this property, see Theorem
4.1
. The converse does not hold.

(2) When the data
f,\lambda
are small, i.e.
\lambda\|f\|_{L^{1}}\leq 1
, then
u_{D}
is constant, see Theorem
4.2
. Hence, the answer to our question is affirmative when
u_{D}=\varphi=const
. The converse does not hold either. It is worth mentioning that a similar phenomenon, i.e. zero solution for non-zero data, was noticed for equation

\frac{d}{dx}(\mathcal{L}(u_{x}))=f\quad\hbox{in }(0,1),\qquad u(0)=A,\ u(1)=B,

where
\mathcal{L}
is a maximal monotone graph and
f\in L^{\infty}(0,1)
is small, see
[
10
]
. Results of this sort were known earlier, see
[
9
, Lemma 4]
, for the ROF functional considered with the natural boundary conditions.

(3) The data
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
is arbitrary, but
\lambda
is sufficiently small. We know that there is a terminal state
u_{T}
of the dynamical problem

u_{t}\in-\partial\Psi,\qquad u(0)=f\in L^{2}(\Omega),

(1.5)

where
\Psi=\bar{\Phi}_{0,0,\varphi}
, i.e. the extinction time is finite. To some extend we will treat
u_{T}
as easily computable. We show that
u_{T}
is the minimizer of
\bar{\Phi}_{D}
, when
\lambda
is small. For enough regular
f
we are able to provide sufficient conditions for that
u_{T}
attains the boundary conditions in the trace sense.

Our main contribution is related to case (3). Our starting point is the observation that the minimization problem (
1.4
) is in fact the time implicit semidiscretization of (
1.5
), where
f
is the initial state and
1/\lambda
is the time step. Our idea is that if
\lambda
is small i.e. the time step
1/\lambda
is large, then the time discretization reaches the terminal state
u_{T}
of the evolution (
1.5
). Since the terminal states satisfy
0\in\Psi(u_{T})
we deduce that
u_{T}
is a minimizer of
\Psi
. In principle, we may check if
u_{T}
satisfies (
1.1
) in the trace sense. We also provide a sufficient condition for that, see Corollary
4.6
.

We expect that there is
0<\lambda_{0}
such that
u_{T}
is a miminizer of (
1.4
)
for all
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{0})
.
Our lower bound on
\lambda_{0}
is explicit, namely
\lambda_{0}>\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{\|f-u_{T}\|_{L^{1}}}
.
We carry out this program in two stages. Initially, we assume that
f
is nice, meaning
f\in BV(\Omega)
it has a finite number of facets and a finite number of monotonicity changes, see Theorem
4.5
. Our proof that
u_{T}
is a minimizer of
\bar{\Phi}_{f,\lambda,\varphi}
depends on analysis of behavior of solutions to (
1.5
) in a boundary layer. We prove a sort of a precise maximum principle. For this purpose we devote our attention to the evolution problem (
1.5
) in Section
3
.

Once we complete the program for nice data
f
we return to the general data, i.e.
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, this is done in Theorem
4.7
. As expected we will approximate
f
by functions for which we can answer the original question. The desired approximation is provided by the solution to (
1.5
) with initial condition
f
. Here, we need to recall the appropriate result for the solutions of (
1.5
).

Let us stress that our ability to answer the original question does not mean that the answer is positive. In addition, our approach does not give necessary conditions.

We close the paper by showing examples of data for which minimizers of (
1.4
) certainly do not satisfy (
1.1
) in the trace sense.
We conclude by saying that we have a range of conditions leading to a positive answer to the original question, but none of them seems also necessary.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we describe the ROF functional and its basic properties. In Section
3
we discuss the total variation flow and the Comparison Principle. The main affirmative results are shown in Section
4
, while the negative ones are established in Section
5
.

2
The ROF functional over
BV(0,L)
with Dirichlet boundary condition

In this section we gather the preliminary material regarding convex analysis.

2.1
The subdifferential of
\bar{\Phi}_{f,\lambda,\varphi}

For the sake of simplicity of notation we shall frequently write
\bar{\Phi}_{D}
, instead of
\bar{\Phi}_{f,\lambda,\varphi}
, when the actual data are less important. Obviously, functional
\bar{\Phi}_{D}
defined in (
1.3
) is convex.
We recall that due to
[
5
]
functional
\bar{\Phi}_{D}
is also lower semicontinuous in the
L^{1}
topology. Hence, the subdifferential of
\bar{\Phi}_{D}
,

\partial\bar{\Phi}_{D}(u)=\{z\in L^{2}(\Omega):\forall h\in L^{2}(\Omega)\quad\bar{\Phi}_{D}(u+h)-\bar{\Phi}_{D}(u)\geq\langle z,h\rangle\}

is well defined.

It is also well-known that
u_{D}
is a solution to the minimization problem (
1.4
)
if and only if

0\in\partial\bar{\Phi}_{D}.

(2.1)

In order to extract properties of minimizers of
\bar{\Phi}_{D}
we have to analyse inclusion (
2.1
). In fact, the characterization of the subdifferential
\partial\bar{\Phi}_{0,0,\varphi}
is known for
D=(0,0,\varphi)
, see
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
and
[
7
]
. This leads to the following statement, the details are left to the interested reader:

Proposition 2.1
.

If
\bar{\Phi}_{f,\lambda,\varphi}
is defined by (
1.3
),
\lambda\geq 0
, then

z\in\partial\bar{\Phi}_{f,\lambda,\varphi}(u)
if and only if
z\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
and

\displaystyle\|z\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 1;

\displaystyle(z,Du)=|Du|\quad\hbox{as measures};

\displaystyle 0=-z_{x}+\lambda(u-f)\equiv-\hbox{div}\,z+\lambda(u-f);

(2.2)

\displaystyle[z,\nu^{\Omega}]\in\hbox{sgn}\,(\varphi-u)\quad\hbox{on }\partial\Omega,

where
\nu^{\Omega}
is the outer normal to the boundary of
\Omega
and
sgn
is multivalued sign function, i.e.
\hbox{sgn}\,0=[-1,1]
. Since our domain is one-dimensional, then the last condition above reads as

-z(0)\in\hbox{sgn}\,(\varphi(0)-\gamma u(0)),\qquad z(L)\in\hbox{sgn}\,(\varphi(L)-\gamma u(L)).

(2.3)

\Box

The difficulty related with this proposition is that the absolute value appearing in the definition of the total variation
\int_{\Omega}|Du|
is not differentiable. Its linear growth is another issue.

We may approach the question of the Euler-Lagrange equation from a different direction. Namely, we may approximate
|\cdot|
by a smooth function of the quadratic growth, e.g.
W_{\varepsilon}(p)+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}p^{2}
, where
W_{\varepsilon}
is a mollification of the absolute value. Then, we show that the Euler-Lagrange for the regularization of
\Phi_{D}
, i.e.

\Phi^{\varepsilon}_{D}(u)=\int_{\Omega}(W_{\varepsilon}(u_{x})+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}|u_{x}|^{2}+\frac{\lambda}{2}(u-f)^{2})\,dx

converges to
z
. In this way we may recover (
2.1
) except for (
2.1
2
). This program was carried out in
[
12
]
.

2.2
The continuous dependence of minimizers upon data

After gathering the preliminary material we present our first observation on the stability of minimizers with respect to data. On the one hand they looks promising, on the other hand it turns out to be our tool in our construction of counterexamples.

Proposition 2.2
.

(a) Let us suppose that
f_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, belong to
L^{2}(\Omega)
,
\lambda>0
and
u_{n}
are the corresponding minimizers of
\bar{\Phi}_{f_{n},\lambda,\varphi}
. If
f_{n}
converge to
f_{0}
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, then
u_{n}
go to
u_{0}
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. Moreover,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\bar{\Phi}_{f_{n},\lambda,\varphi}(u_{n})=\bar{\Phi}_{f_{0},\lambda,\varphi}(u_{0}).

(b) Let us suppose that
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
,
\lambda>0
,
\{\varphi_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
u_{n}
are the corresponding minimizers of
\bar{\Phi}_{f,\lambda,\varphi_{n}}
. If
\varphi_{n}
converge to
\varphi_{0}
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, then
\lim_{n\to\infty}u_{n}=u_{0}
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. Moreover,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\bar{\Phi}_{f,\lambda,\varphi_{n}}(u_{n})=\bar{\Phi}_{f_{0},\lambda,\varphi}(u_{0}).

(2.4)

(c) Let us suppose that
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
,
\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
u_{n}
are the corresponding minimizers of
\bar{\Phi}_{f,\lambda_{n},\varphi}
. If
\lambda_{n}
converge to
\lambda_{0}>0
, then
u_{n}\to u_{0}
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. Moreover,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\bar{\Phi}_{f,\lambda,\varphi_{n}}(u_{n})=\bar{\Phi}_{f_{0},\lambda,\varphi}(u_{0}).

(2.5)

Proof.

Part (a), we will first show convergence of minimizers.
Since
u_{n}
is a minimizer, then
0\in\partial\Phi_{f_{n},\lambda,\varphi}(u_{n})
. Now, we are using the characterization of the elements of the subdifferential provided by Proposition
2.1
. Since
0\in\partial\Phi_{f_{n},\lambda,\varphi}(u_{n})
, then there exist
z_{n}\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
such that

0=-\hbox{div}\,z_{n}+\lambda(u_{n}-f_{n}).

We take the difference of the right-hand-side (RHS for short) above for
n
and
k
. Subsequently, we compute the inner product of the result with
u_{n}-u_{k}
. Hence we reach,

0=(\hbox{div}\,z_{n}-\hbox{div}\,z_{k},u_{n}-u_{k})+\lambda((u_{n}-f_{n})-(u_{k}-f_{k}),u_{n}-u_{k}).

Now, we use the fact that the subdifferential of the total variation is a maximal monotone operator. Thus, we reach

(\hbox{div}\,z_{n}-\hbox{div}\,z_{k},u_{n}-u_{k})\geq 0
. Hence, we deduce,

0\geq\|u_{n}-u_{k}\|^{2}-(f_{n}-f_{k},u_{n}-u_{k}).

At this point the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality yields

\|f_{n}-f_{k}\|\geq\|u_{n}-u_{k}\|,

i.e.
u_{n}
is a Cauchy sequence in
L^{2}
converging to
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
.

Now, we are going to show that
u_{0}
is a minimizer. Let us take any
\bar{u}\in BV(\Omega)
. We have to show that
\Phi_{f_{0},\lambda,\varphi}(\bar{u})\geq\Phi_{f_{0},\lambda,\varphi}(u_{0})
. Of course, we have

\bar{\Phi}_{f_{n},\lambda,\varphi}(\bar{u})\geq\bar{\Phi}_{f_{n}\lambda,\varphi}(u_{n}).

We take the limit on both sides. Then, we have

\bar{\Phi}_{f_{0},\lambda,\varphi}(\bar{u})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\bar{\Phi}_{f_{n}\lambda,\varphi}(\bar{u})\geq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\bar{\Phi}_{f_{n},\lambda,\varphi}(u_{n})\geq\bar{\Phi}_{f_{0},\lambda,\varphi}(u_{0}),

where we used the lower semicontinuity of
\bar{\Phi}_{0,0,\varphi}(u)\equiv\int_{\Omega}|Du|+\int_{\partial\Omega}|\gamma u-\varphi|
.

Now, we can show that the convergence
u_{n}\to u_{0}
is better than just in
L^{2}
.
The lower semicontinuity of
\bar{\Phi}_{f,\lambda,\varphi}
and the convergence
f_{n}\to f
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
yield

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\bar{\Phi}_{f_{n},\lambda,\varphi}(u_{n})\geq\bar{\Phi}_{f_{0},\lambda,\varphi}(u_{0}).

At the same time we have

\bar{\Phi}_{f_{0},\lambda,\varphi}(u_{0})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\bar{\Phi}_{f_{n},\lambda,\varphi}(u_{0})\geq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\bar{\Phi}_{f_{n},\lambda,\varphi}(u_{n})\geq\bar{\Phi}_{f_{0},\lambda,\varphi}(u_{0}).

Hence, all inequalities become equalities and our claim follows.

Part (b). We are going to show the continuous dependence of solution on the boundary data.
First of all we notice that for all
v\in BV(\Omega)
, we have

\bar{\Phi}_{f_{n},\lambda,\varphi}(v)=\bar{\Phi}_{f_{n},\lambda,\varphi_{k}}(v)+|\gamma v-\varphi_{n}|-|\gamma v-\varphi_{k}|.

Now, the triangle inequality implies that
||\gamma v-\varphi_{n}|-|\gamma v-\varphi_{k}||\leq|\varphi_{n}-\varphi_{k}|
. Hence,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\bar{\Phi}_{f_{n},\lambda,\varphi_{n}}(v)=\bar{\Phi}_{f_{n},\lambda,\varphi_{0}}(v).

(2.6)

Let us now suppose that
u_{n}
is a minimizer of
\bar{\Phi}_{f_{n},\lambda,\varphi_{n}}
. Since

|Du_{n}|(\Omega)\leq\bar{\Phi}_{f_{n},\lambda,\varphi_{n}}(0)
we deduce that there is
M>0
such that
\|u_{n}\|_{BV}\leq M
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Thus, we may extract a subsequence (not relabeled) converging in
L^{2}
to
u_{0}
.
By the lower semicontinuity of
\bar{\Phi}_{f_{n},\lambda,\varphi_{0}}
, we have

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\bar{\Phi}_{f_{n},\lambda,\varphi_{0}}(u_{n})\geq\bar{\Phi}_{f_{n},\lambda,\varphi_{0}}(u_{0}).

(2.7)

As a result
u_{0}\in BV(\Omega).

We wish to show that
u_{0}
is a minimizer of
\bar{\Phi}_{f_{n},\lambda,\varphi_{0}}
. We take
v\in BV(\Omega)
. Then we have

\bar{\Phi}_{f_{n},\lambda,\varphi_{n}}(v)\geq\bar{\Phi}_{f_{n},\lambda,\varphi_{n}}(u_{n})=\bar{\Phi}_{f_{n},\lambda,\varphi_{0}}(u_{n})+|\gamma u_{n}-\varphi_{0}|-|\gamma u_{n}-\varphi_{n}|.

Since the difference of boundary terms goes to zero, we see that (
2.6
) and (
2.7
) imply the following inequalities

\bar{\Phi}_{f_{n},\lambda,\varphi_{0}}(u_{0})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\bar{\Phi}_{f_{n},\lambda,\varphi_{n}}(v)\geq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\bar{\Phi}_{f_{n},\lambda,\\
vfi_{n}}(u_{n})=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\bar{\Phi}_{f_{n},\lambda,\varphi_{0}}(u_{n})\geq\bar{\Phi}_{f_{n},\lambda,\varphi_{0}}(u_{0}).

(2.8)

Since
u_{0}
is a unique minimizer of
\bar{\Phi}_{f,\lambda,\varphi_{0}}
, we deduce that not only a subsequence of
u_{n}
converges to
u_{0}
but the whole sequence does. The first part of (b) follows.

In order to show (
2.4
) we notice that (
2.6
) and (
2.8
) imply

\bar{\Phi}_{f_{n},\lambda,\varphi_{0}}(u_{0})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\bar{\Phi}_{f_{n},\lambda,\varphi_{n}}(u_{0})\geq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\bar{\Phi}_{f_{n},\lambda,\varphi_{n}}(u_{n})\geq\bar{\Phi}_{f_{n},\lambda,\varphi_{0}}(u_{0}).

As as a result all inequalities are equalities. Formula (
2.4
) holds.

(c) If
\lambda_{n}\to\lambda_{0}
, then the corresponding minimizers form a bounded sequence in
BV
. We may select a subsequence convergent to
u_{0}
in
L^{2}
. From this point the argument goes like that in part (b). We deduce that
u_{0}
is the unique minimizer and (
2.5
) follows.

\Box

This proposition is not sufficient to claim that the limit minimizer,
u_{0}
, has the right trace.

3
The gradient flow of the total variation with the Dirichlet boundary condition

We present here the information necessary for the development of tools related to the
gradient flow generated by
a lower semicontinuous convex functional
\bar{\Phi}_{0,0,\varphi}
. We offer two viewpoints on this question. The first one is based on the convex analysis tools. In this approach the gradient flow
is understood as the differential inclusion (
1.5
).

The second viewpoint is based on the approximation the differential inclusion (
1.5
) by strictly parabolic problems, see
[
11
]
and
[
8
]
.

We first notice that the existence of the dynamical problem (
1.5
) follows from the well-established theory of monotone operators, see
[
3
]
. We can show:

Proposition 3.1
.

(a) Let us suppose that
u_{0}\in D(\partial\bar{\Phi}_{0,0,\varphi})
. Then, there is a unique solution to (
1.5
), i.e.

u_{t}\in-\partial\bar{\Phi}_{0,0,\varphi}(u),\qquad u(0)=u_{0}

that

u\in C([0,\infty),L^{2}(\Omega)),\quad\frac{du}{dt}\in L^{\infty}(0,\infty),\ u(0)=u_{0}.

(b) Let us suppose that
u_{0}\in L^{2}
. Then, there is a unique solution to (
1.5
). Moreover,

u\in C([0,\infty),L^{2}(\Omega)),\quad t\frac{du}{dt}\in L^{\infty}(0,\infty,L^{2}(\Omega)),\quad u(0)=u_{0}

and for all
t>0
, we have
u(t)\in D(\partial\bar{\Phi}_{0,0,\varphi})
.
In addition for all
t>0
, we have

\frac{d^{+}}{dt}u=\partial\bar{\Phi}_{0,0,\varphi}^{o}(u),

where
\frac{d^{+}}{dt}u
denotes the right derivative and
\partial\bar{\Phi}_{0,0,\varphi}^{o}(u)
is the minimal section of the subdifferential. We recall that
v=\partial\bar{\Phi}_{0,0,\varphi}^{o}(u)
if it has the smallest
L^{2}
norm of all elements of
\partial\bar{\Phi}_{0,0,\varphi}(u)
.

Proof.

Since
\bar{\Phi}_{0,0,\varphi}
is convex and lower semicontinuous on
L^{2}(\Omega)
, then we may apply
[
3
, Théorème 3.1]
to deduce part (a). Part (b) follows from
[
3
, Théorème 3.2]
.

\Box

We remark that the minimality of the selection of
\partial\bar{\Phi}_{0,0,\varphi}
means that (
1.5
) takes the following form,

u_{t}=z_{x},

where
z(\cdot,t)\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
,
|z(x,t)|\leq 1
for a.e.
(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T)
and
z(\cdot,t)
is a minimizer of the following functional

\int_{\Omega}\zeta_{x}^{2}\,dx,\quad\zeta\in\partial\bar{\Phi}_{0,0,\varphi}(u).

(3.1)

This Proposition does not say anything about attainment of the boundary conditions. For this purpose we restate the condition (
2.3
). Below we recall the notion introduced in
[
7
]
and later for the one dimensional problems in
[
8
]
:

Definition 3.2
.

We shall say that
u
a minimizer of (
1.4
) (resp. a solution to (
1.5
)) satisfies the Dirichlet boundary condition

u=\varphi\qquad\hbox{on }\partial\Omega

(resp. satisfies it at time
t
)
in the
viscosity sense
provided that

\gamma u(0)=\varphi(0)\quad\hbox{or}\quad\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\hbox{if }\gamma u(0)>\varphi(0),&\hbox{then, }z(0)=1,\\
\hbox{if }\gamma u(0)<\varphi(0),&\hbox{then, }z(0)=-1\end{array}\right.

and

\gamma u(L)=\varphi(L)\quad\hbox{or}\quad\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\hbox{if }\gamma u(L)>\varphi(L),&\hbox{then }z(0)=-1,\\
\hbox{if }\gamma u(L)<\varphi(L),&\hbox{then }z(0)=1.\end{array}\right.

In the case
u
is a solutions to (
1.5
), we replace
\gamma u(0)
or
\gamma u(L)
by
\gamma u(0,t)
or
\gamma u(L,t)
, respectively for a.e.
t>0
.

It is natural to ask if solutions to (
1.5
) satisfy the Dirichlet boundary conditions in this weak sense. In order to address this question we prefer to present a different approach to (
1.5
) based on approximation of this eq. by strictly parabolic problems with smooth coefficients developed in
[
11
]
and
[
8
]
, because we want to use methods established there.

Since we want to use results of
[
8
]
to analyse properties of solutions to (
1.5
), then we recall the language of that paper, which was devoted to

\displaystyle u_{t}=\left(\frac{d}{dp}W(p)\right)_{x},

\displaystyle(x,t)\in(0,L)\times(0,\infty),

\displaystyle u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),

\displaystyle x\in(0,L),

(3.2)

\displaystyle u(0,t)=A,

\displaystyle u(L,t)=B,

where the boundary conditions were understood in the sense of Definition
3.2
and the emphasis was put on

W(p)=|p-1|+|p+1|
. However, the results of
[
8
]
are extendable to more general piecewise linear
W
. We write
\mathcal{L}(p):=\frac{d}{dp}W(p)
. Since
W
is convex, then
\mathcal{L}
is increasing with possible discontinuities, their number is finite, when
W
is piecewise linear. Here we are mainly interested in
W(p)=|p|
.

Existence of solutions to (
3
) was established by approximating this equation by strictly parabolic problems with smooth coefficients, see
[
8
]
and
[
10
]
. We also showed in
[

, Theorem 2.3, part (1)]
that the solution we constructed is the solution provided by
[
3
]
. Hence, by uniqueness provided by

w(t)=w^{*}(x,t)\leq u_{*}(x,t)\quad\hbox{and}\quad u^{*}(x,t)\leq v_{*}(x,t)=v(t)

\gamma u(0,t)=u^{*}(0,t)\leq v(t)

It suffices only to show (i). Due to our assumption there is a finite number of open intervals

such that

is strictly monotone and facets separating these intervals. We consider different types of behavior of the solution

4
Conditions for solvability of the Dirichlet problem in the trace sense

Here is the first of our main observations.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let us suppose that
f\in BV(\Omega)
,
\gamma f=\varphi
,
\lambda
is any positive number and
u
is a minimizer of
\bar{\Phi}_{f,\lambda,\varphi}
. Then
\gamma u=\varphi
.

Proof.

Step 1.

Since
u
is a minimizer of
\Phi_{f,\lambda,\varphi}
, then
0\in\partial\Phi_{f,\lambda,\varphi}(u)
. We know the characterization of the subdifferential of
\Phi_{f,\lambda,\varphi}(u)
, i.e, then there exists
z\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
satisfying

0=-z_{x}+\lambda(u-f),\qquad\hbox{in }\Omega,

(4.1)

where for
x\in(0,L)
we have

z(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\in[-1,1]&\hbox{if }Du=0,\\
\hbox{sgn}\,(Du)&\hbox{if }Du\neq 0.\\
\end{array}\right.

In particular, if
x\in\partial\Omega
, then we recall (
2.3
),

-z(0)\in\hbox{sgn}\,(\varphi(0)-\gamma u(0)),\qquad z(L)\in\hbox{sgn}\,(\varphi(L)-\gamma u(L)).

Let us integrate (
4.1
) over
(0,x_{n})
where the sequence
x_{n}
converges to
0
.

z(x_{n})=z(0)+\lambda\int_{0}^{x_{n}}(u-f)\,ds

Step 2.

We have to investigate the value of
z
at
x=0
. the boundary of
\Omega
. We claim that if
\gamma u(0)>\gamma f(0)
, then there is
x_{0}\in(0,L)
such that

\int_{0}^{x_{0}}(u(s)-f(s))\,ds>0.

Let us suppose that the claim is false and for all
x\in(0,L)
we have

\int_{0}^{x_{0}}(u(s)-f(s))\,ds\leq 0.

(4.2)

We divide both sides of this inequality by
x_{n}
and we want to pass to the limit
x\to 0
. We first notice that by the definition of the trace we have

\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{1}{x}\int_{0}^{x}v(s)\,ds=\gamma v(0).

If we apply this observation to
v=u-f
and take into account (
4.2
), then we see,

0\geq\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{1}{x}\int_{0}^{x}(u(s)-f(s))\,ds=\gamma(u-f)(0)>0.

Thus, our claim follows.

Step 3.
Let us suppose that the theorem is false and
\gamma u(0)>\gamma f(0)
. Since
z(0)\in\partial|\gamma(u-f)|
, then we infer

z(0)=1.

(4.3)

Since
\gamma u(0)>\gamma f(0)
, then Step 2 yields existence of
x_{0}\in(0,L)
such that

\int_{0}^{x_{0}}(u(s)-f(s))\,ds>0.

This observation combined with (
4.3
) leads to
z(x_{0})>1
, but this is not possible. In the same manner we show that
\gamma u(0)<\gamma f(0)
is impossible.

\Box

We will state another attainment result. This time we assume that the data are small.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let us suppose that
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
,
\lambda>0
are such that
\lambda\|f\|_{L^{1}}\leq 1
and
\varphi=\bar{f}=\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}f(x)\,dx
, then
u=\bar{f}
is a constant minimizer of
\bar{\Phi}_{f,\lambda,\varphi}
. Moreover, the boundary data are attained in the trace sense.

Proof.

It suffices to show that if we set
u=\bar{f}
, then formula (
4.1
) leads to a right selection of
\partial\bar{\Phi}_{f,\lambda,\varphi}
. Integrating (
4.1
) over
(0,x)
yields

z(x)=z(0)+\lambda\int_{0}^{x}(\bar{f}-f(y))\,dy.

We have to show that there is a choice of
z(0)\in[-1,1]
such that
|z(x)|\leq 1
. Let us define
g(x)=\int_{0}^{x}(\bar{f}-f(y))\,dy
for
x\in[0,L]
. We will estimate the length of the interval

[\min\{g(x):x\in[0,L]\},\ \max\{g(x):x\in[0,L]\}].

If the minimum of
g
is attained at
x_{m}
and its maximum is at
x_{M}
, then we have

g(x_{M})-g(x_{m})=\int_{x_{m}}^{x_{M}}(\bar{f}-f(y))\,dy\leq|\int_{0}^{b}f(y)\,dy|+\|f\|_{L^{1}}\leq\frac{2}{\lambda}.

We define
z(0)
as follows. If
\lambda g(x_{m}),\lambda g(x_{M})\in[-1,1]
, then we set
z(0)=0
. If
\lambda g(x_{m})<-1
, then we set

z(0)=-1-\lambda g(x_{m})
. Similarly, if
\lambda g(x_{M})>1
, then

z(0)=1-\lambda g(x_{M})
. Obviously,
z(x)\in[-1,1]
for all
x\in[0,L]
. Hence
u=\bar{f}
is a minimizer provided that we have no constraints on
z(0)
and
z(L)
. This happens only when
\gamma u=\bar{f}=\varphi
.

\Box

Remark 4.3
.

Let us notice that the construction of
z(0)
presented in the proof above does not work when
\varphi\neq\bar{f}
, because
z(0)
must be equal
\pm 1
.

Now, we would like to put the attainment problem in the context of the dynamical problem, see (
1.5
). The parameter
\lambda
is the inverse of the time step. The variational problem is the time semidiscretization of the dynamics. The solution to the minimization problem for a given
\lambda
is different from the solution of the dynamics at
1/\lambda
with initial condition
f
. However, it is relatively easy to determine the final state of evolution, but with only an estimate on the extinction time
T_{ext}
.

Our main observation is the following: for a range of
\lambda
the terminal state is also a minimizer of (
1.4
). In this way we become interested in a slightly modified attainment problem, where we vary the parameter
\lambda
.
Moreover,
once we have the final state, we can check if it satisfies the boundary condition in the trace sense. If it does, then we can give an affirmative answer to our problem by saying: for a range of parameter
\lambda
the minimizer of (
1.4
) attains the boundary data in the trace sense.

If it happens that the terminal state does not satisfy the boundary data in the trace sense, then the answer to our problem is: for a large set of
\lambda
the minimizer of (
1.4
) does not satisfy the boundary data in the trace sense.

The disadvantage of our method is that we give only an estimate on the range of
\lambda
’s.

Let us notice that always the terminal state satisfies the boundary condition in the viscosity sense, otherwise
\hbox{div}\,z\neq 0
and the system evolves.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let us suppose that
u_{T}
is the terminal state of the evolution, i.e.
u_{T}=u(T_{ext})
for eq. (
1.5
) with initial condition
f\in BV(\Omega)
and
f
satisfies (
3.4
). Then,

(a)
0\in\partial\bar{\Phi}_{0,0,\varphi}(u_{T})
, i.e.
u_{T}
is a minimizer of the total variation,

\int_{\Omega}|Du_{T}|+\int_{\partial\Omega}|\gamma u_{T}-\varphi|\,d\mathcal{H}^{0}\leq\int_{\Omega}|Dv|+\int_{\partial\Omega}|\gamma v-\varphi|\,d\mathcal{H}^{0},\qquad\hbox{for all }v\in BV(\Omega).

(b) There is
\lambda_{0}>0
such that for all
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{0})

u_{T}
is a minimizer of
\bar{\Phi}_{\lambda,f,\varphi}
.

Proof.

Part (a). Since
u_{T}
is the terminal state of evolution, this means that it satisfies

0\in\partial\Phi_{0,0,\varphi}(u_{T}),

because
\Phi_{0,0,\varphi}(u)=\int_{\Omega}|Du_{T}|+\int_{\partial\Omega}|\gamma u_{T}-\varphi|\,d\mathcal{H}^{0}
. However, the above inclusion means that
u_{T}
is a minimizer of
\Phi_{f,0,\varphi}
,

Part (b). Let us set
\lambda_{1}^{-1}=\frac{1}{2}\|f-u_{T}\|_{L^{1}}
. We claim that element
u_{T}
is a minimizer of
\bar{\Phi}_{f,\varepsilon,\varphi}
for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\lambda_{1})
. Hence,
\lambda_{0}\geq\lambda_{1}
.

We will show directly that for all
v\in BV(\Omega)
we have

\bar{\Phi}_{f,\varepsilon,\varphi}(v)\geq\bar{\Phi}_{f,\varepsilon,\varphi}(u_{T}).

(4.4)

Step 1.
We first consider
v=u_{T}+\Delta u
such that

\Delta u=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}h_{i}\chi_{I_{i}},

where intervals
I_{i}
are pairwise disjoint.

Our computations distinguish two cases:

(1) no interval
\bar{I}_{i}
intersects
\partial\Omega
;

(2) there is an interval
I_{i_{0}}
such that
\bar{I}_{i_{0}}\cap\partial\Omega\neq\emptyset.

We will consider them one by one. If intervals
I_{i}
defining
\Delta u
are pairwise disjoint, then case (1) yields

\int_{\Omega}|Dv|+\int_{\partial\Omega}|v-\varphi|\,d\mathcal{H}^{0}=\int_{\Omega}|Du_{T}|+2\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}|h_{i}|+\int_{\partial\Omega}|u_{T}-\varphi|\,d\mathcal{H}^{0},

because the perturbation introduces jumps at the endpoints of all
I_{i}
. Here, we use the assumption (1).
Now, we look at the difference
\bar{\Phi}_{f,\varepsilon,\varphi}(u_{T}+\Delta u)-\bar{\Phi}_{f,\varepsilon,\varphi}(u_{T})=:\Delta\bar{\Phi}
, we see

\Delta\bar{\Phi}=2\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}|h_{i}|+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\int_{I_{i}}[(u_{T}+h_{i}-f)^{2}-(u_{T}-f)^{2}]\,dx.

We notice that

\int_{I_{i}}[(u_{T}+h_{i}-f)^{2}-(u_{T}-f)^{2}]\,dx=2\int_{I_{i}}h_{i}(u_{T}-f)\,dx+\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}(I_{i})}^{2}.

Hence, by definition of
\lambda_{1}
we obtain

\displaystyle\Delta\bar{\Phi}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}|h_{i}|(2+\varepsilon\hbox{sgn}\,(h_{i})\int_{I_{i}}(u_{T}-f)\,dx+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}|h_{i}||I_{i}|)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}|h_{i}|(2-2\varepsilon\lambda_{1}^{-1}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}|h_{i}||I_{i}|)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\|\Delta u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Our claim follows in case (1). Now, we turn our attention to case (2). The argument presented above shows that is suffices to check that
\Delta\bar{\Phi}:=\bar{\Phi}_{f,\varepsilon,\varphi}(u_{T}+h\chi_{I})-\bar{\Phi}_{f,\varepsilon,\varphi}(u_{T})\geq 0
, where
0\in\bar{I}
. The argument when
L\in\bar{I}
is the same. We have

\Delta\bar{\Phi}=|h|+|\gamma u_{T}+h-\varphi|-|\gamma u_{T}-\varphi|+\varepsilon h\int_{I}(u_{T}-f)\,dx+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}h^{2}|I|.

If
u_{T}
satisfies the boundary condition, i.e.
\gamma u_{T}=\varphi
, then the argument used to establish (1) applies again and
\Delta\bar{\Phi}\geq 0
. If
\gamma u_{T}\neq\varphi
, then we have to argue in a different way.

We recall that
since
u_{T}
is a terminal state it means that function
\tilde{u}_{T}

is monotone, this follows from Proposition
2.1
. Otherwise
u_{T}
is not a terminal state. It suffices to consider the case when
\tilde{u}_{T}
is increasing. This means that
\gamma u_{T}>\varphi
at
x=0
is the only possibility when
\gamma u_{T}(0)\neq\varphi(0)
and
\varphi>\gamma u_{T}
at
x=L
when
\gamma u_{T}(L)\neq\varphi(L)
.

Let us now suppose that
h>0
. Then,
\Delta\bar{\Phi}
takes on the following form,

\Delta\bar{\Phi}=2h+\varepsilon h\int_{I}(u_{T}-f)\,dx+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}h^{2}|I|

and by argument used in case (1), we see that
\Delta\bar{\Phi}\geq 0
.

Let us consider
h<0
, then

\Delta\bar{\Phi}=|h|+h+\varepsilon h\int_{I}(u_{T}-f)\,dx+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}h^{2}|I|=\varepsilon h\int_{I}(u_{T}-f)\,dx+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}h^{2}|I|.

Again we consider two cases: (a)
|I|\leq\delta
, (b)
|I|>\delta
, where
\delta
is provided by Lemma
3.12
.

In case (a) since
u_{T}
is the terminal state, we invoke Theorem
3.13
to conclude
that
u_{T}\leq f
on
(0,\delta)
. Then, the integral on the RHS above is non-positive. Altogether this yields
h\int_{I}(u_{T}-f)\,dx\geq 0
. As a result,

\Delta\bar{\Phi}\geq 0

and our claim follows.

Suppose now (b) holds. Then, we replace the perturbation
h\chi_{I}
by
(h+\eta)\chi_{(0,\delta)}+h\chi_{I^{\prime}}
, where
\eta\in(0,-h_{i})
and
I^{\prime}=I\setminus(0,\delta)
. This perturbation falls in the categories we considered, hence

0\leq\Delta_{\eta}\bar{\Phi}:=\varepsilon(h+\eta)\int_{I}(u_{T}-f)\,dx+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}(h+\eta)^{2}|I|.

Of course

0\leq\lim_{\eta\to 0}\Delta_{\eta}\bar{\Phi}=\Delta\bar{\Phi}.

The essential observation is that the limit
\bar{\Phi}(u_{T}+\Delta_{\eta}u)
as
\eta
goes to zero equals
\bar{\Phi}(u_{T}+\Delta_{0}u)
, hence the claim follows.

Step 2.
Let us suppose that
\Delta u\in BV(\Omega)
is arbitrary. We can construct a sequence
g_{n}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
converging to
\Delta u
in
L^{\infty}
and
|Dg_{n}|(\Omega)\to|D\Delta u|
, i.e. the convergence is in the intermediate sense implying convergence of trace of
g_{n}
to the trace of
\Delta
. Thus, we have

\Delta\bar{\Phi}\geq\bar{\Phi}(u_{T}+g_{n})-\bar{\Phi}(u_{T})-\eta_{n}\equiv\Delta_{n}\bar{\Phi}-\eta_{n},

where
\eta_{n}\to 0
. Due to Step 1 we conclude that
\Delta_{n}\bar{\Phi}\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\|g_{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
. If we pass to the limit,
n\to\infty
, then we see

\Delta\bar{\Phi}\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\|\Delta u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Thus, for any
v\in BV(\Omega)
, we have (
4.4
).

By definition

\lambda_{0}=\sup\{\lambda>0:u_{T}\hbox{ is a minimizer of }\bar{\Phi}_{f,\mu,\varphi}\hbox{ for all }\mu\in(0,\lambda)\}.

Since
\lambda_{1}
satisfies the desired property, we deduce that
\lambda_{0}\geq\lambda_{1}>0
.

\Box

With this lemma at hand we can state the last of our main results. For the sake of clarity we deal separately with
f
satisfying (
3.4
) and general data
\in L^{2}(\Omega)
.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let us suppose that
f\in BV(\Omega)
satisfies (
3.4
) and
\varphi:\partial\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
are given. Then, there is
\lambda_{0}
such that for all
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{0})
the terminal state of the evolution problem (
1.5
),
u_{T}
, is a solution to the variational problem (
1.4
).

Proof.

Existence of the terminal state is the result of the final extinction time, see Proposition
3.8
. The last claim follows from Lemma
4.4
.

\Box

We could treat
u_{T}
as easily computable, whose attainment of boundary conditions is no longer any issue. However, we may combine this theorem with Corollary
3.14
. Then we reach an clean partial answer to the question posed by Brezis in
[
4
, Open Problem 3]
.

Corollary 4.6
.

Let us suppose that
f
satisfies assumptions of the previous theorem. If in addition

\gamma f(0)\not\in(\min\varphi,\max\varphi),\quad\hbox{and}\quad\gamma f(L)\not\in(\min\varphi,\max\varphi),

then
\gamma u_{T}=\varphi
.
\Box

Unfortunately, the condition stated in the corollary above is not necessary as the following example shows.

Example 1
.

Let us take
L=2
,
\varphi(0)=-1
,
\varphi(2)=1
and

f(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&x\in(0,\frac{1}{/}2)\cup[\frac{3}{2},2),\\
-k,&x\in[\frac{1}{2},1),\\
k,&x\in[1,\frac{3}{2}),\end{array}\right.

where
k\geq 1
. It is easy to check that in this case

u_{T}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-1,&x\in(0,1),\\
1,&x\in(1,2).\end{array}\right.

We are now turning our attention to the case of general
f\in L^{2}
. We offer only a result similar to that in Theorem
4.5
.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let us suppose that
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and
\varphi:\partial\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
are given. Then, there is
\lambda_{0}
such that for all
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{0})
the terminal state of the evolution problem (
1.5
),
u_{T}
, is a solution to the variational problem (
1.4
).

Proof.

Existence of the terminal state
u_{T}
is a consequence of Proposition
3.8
.

We know that a.e.
\tau>0
is a typical time, as a result we may pick a sequence
\tau_{n}>0
converging to zero such that each
u(\tau_{n})
satisfies the assumptions of the previous theorem. We conclude that the terminal state
u_{T}
for the dynamics is the minimizer of
\bar{\Phi}_{u(\tau_{n}),\lambda,\varphi}
for
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{0})
, where
\lambda_{0}\geq 2/\|u_{T}-u(\tau_{n})\|_{L^{2}}
.

Now, let us take any
\lambda<2/\|u_{T}-f\|_{L^{2}}
. Since (
1.5
) is a semigroup of contractions, we see that

\|u(\tau_{n})-u_{T}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|f-u_{T}\|_{L^{2}}.

i.e.
\lambda<2/\|u_{T}-u(\tau_{n})\|_{L^{2}}
. We may
apply Proposition
2.2
to pass to the limit with
n\to\infty
to deduce that
u_{T}
is the minimizer of
\bar{\Phi}_{f,\lambda,\varphi}
.

\Box

Unfortunately, when we only know that
f\in L^{2}
we cannot offer a clean condition for the attainment of the boundary conditions by
u_{T}
.

Let us stress that we showed a range of parameters
\lambda
for which the terminal state of evolution of (
1.5
) is a minimizer of (
1.4
). The terminal states are minimizers of the relaxed total variation, hence they are monotone functions as observed in
[
8
]
. Hence, we found a partial solution to
[
4
, Open Problem 3]
.

5
Negative results

We are going to present two types of results. The first one says that if
\lambda>0
and
f\in L^{2}
are fixed, then we can find boundary conditions which are too far apart for existence of minimizers attaining the data in the trace sense. Another result says that despite the continuous dependence of
u_{D}
on
D
, the following implication is false:

if for a data
D
the minimizer
u_{D}
attains the boundary conditions in the trace sense and the distance between
D
and
D^{\prime}
is small, then
the minimizer
u_{D^{\prime}}
attains the boundary conditions in the trace sense.

5.1
Negative results large data

Essentially, the results established in the previous section, i.e. Theorem
4.5
and Theorem
4.7
were valid for small
\lambda
. Here, we shall show that if
\lambda>0
is arbitrary and the boundary data
\varphi
are large in a sense explained below, then the minimizer of
\bar{\Phi}_{f,\lambda,\varphi}
does not satisfy the boundary conditions in the trace sense.

We have already noticed in Proposition
2.1
that the minimizer of
\bar{\Phi}_{f,\lambda,\varphi}
satisfies the boundary condition in the
viscosity sense
, see Definition
3.2
. This means that if for
u\in BV(\Omega)
and
\varphi:\partial\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
we define function
\tilde{u}
by formula (
3.6
),
then for each
a\in\partial\Omega
there exists
U_{a}
, a neighborhood of
a
, such that
\tilde{u}|_{U_{a}}
is a monotone function.

Here is our observation.

Theorem 5.1

(non-existence for large boundary data)
.

Let us suppose that
f\in BV(\Omega)
and

u\in BV(\Omega)
is a minimizer of
\bar{\Phi}_{f,\lambda,0}
. There exists
\varphi:\partial\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
such that

|\varphi(L)-\varphi(0)|>|Df|(\Omega)+\int_{\partial\Omega}|\gamma f|\,d\mathcal{H}^{0}

and
\tilde{u}
defined in (
3.6
) satisfies the boundary conditions
\varphi
in the viscosity sense. Then,

(1)
u
is a minimizer of
\bar{\Phi}_{f,\lambda,\varphi}
;

(2)
\gamma u\neq\varphi
;

Proof.

Part (1). There exists a minimizer of
\bar{\Phi}_{f,\lambda,0}
. We can take any
\varphi(0)
(resp.
\varphi(L)
) such that
z
for
u
and
\tilde{u}
are the same. Hence
u
is a minimizer of
\bar{\Phi}_{f,\lambda,\varphi}
. We notice that the set of
\varphi
satisfying imposed conditions is open.

Part (2) is easy. Of course

|\varphi(L)-\varphi(0)|>|Df|(\Omega)+\int_{\partial\Omega}|\gamma f|\geq|Du|(\Omega)+\int_{\partial\Omega}|\gamma u|+\frac{\lambda}{2}\int_{\Omega}(u-f)^{2}\,dx.

Thus,

|\varphi(L)-\varphi(0)|>|Du|(\Omega)\geq|(\gamma u)(L)-(\gamma u)(0)|.

As a result the boundary conditions cannot be attained in the trace sense.

\Box

5.2
The lack of continuous dependence

We explain that it is unreasonable to expect that if
(f_{1},\varphi_{1})
and
(f_{2},\varphi_{2})
are close and minimizer
u_{f_{1},\lambda,\varphi_{1}}
satisfies the boundary condition in the trace sense so does
u_{f_{2},\lambda,\varphi_{2}}
, which is close to
u_{f_{1},\lambda,\varphi_{1}}
by Proposition
2.2
.

Proposition 5.2
.

(a) There exist
f_{\varepsilon},f_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
,
\varphi\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
\lambda>0
such that
f_{\varepsilon}\to f_{0}
in
L^{2}
and
\gamma u_{f_{\varepsilon},\lambda,\varphi}=\varphi

for all
\varepsilon>0
, but
\gamma u_{f_{0},\lambda,\varphi}\neq\varphi
.

(b) There exist
\varphi_{\varepsilon},\varphi_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
\varphi\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
\lambda>0
such that
\varphi_{\varepsilon}\to\varphi_{0}
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
\gamma u_{f,\lambda,\varphi_{0}}=\varphi
, but
\gamma u_{f,\lambda,\varphi_{\varepsilon}}\neq\varphi_{\varepsilon}

for all
\varepsilon>0
.

Proof.

(a) We take any
\lambda>0
any monotone decreasing function
f_{0}
. We choose
\varphi
so that
\varphi(0)>\gamma f_{0}(0)
and
\varphi(L)=\gamma f_{0}(L)
. Moreover, for any
\varepsilon>0
we define

f_{\varepsilon}=\varphi(0)\chi_{(0,\varepsilon)}+f_{0}\chi_{[\varepsilon,L)}.

Obviously
\gamma f_{\varepsilon}=\varphi
for
\varepsilon>0
and
\gamma f_{0}\neq\varphi
. We can also easily check that

0\in\partial\bar{\Phi}_{f_{\varepsilon},\lambda,\varphi}(f_{\varepsilon}),\qquad\varepsilon\geq 0.

Hence,
f_{\varepsilon}
is a minimizer of
\bar{\Phi}_{f_{\varepsilon},\lambda,\varphi}
. By Proposition
2.2
the limit of minimizers is the minimizer of (
1.4
) for
\bar{\Phi}_{f_{0},\lambda,\varphi}
. Hence,
f_{0}
is a minimizer, but it does not satisfy the boundary conditions. Our claim follows.

(b) We proceed in a similar manner. Let us take any
\lambda
and any decreasing function
f
. We set
\varphi_{0}:=\gamma f
and

\varphi_{\varepsilon}(0)=\varphi_{0}(0)+\varepsilon,\qquad\varphi_{\varepsilon}(L)=\varphi_{0}(L).

We notice that
\tilde{f}_{\varepsilon}
defined as in (
3.6
)
is a decreasing function and

\gamma\tilde{f}_{\varepsilon}\neq\varphi_{\varepsilon}
. However,

f_{\varepsilon}
is a minimizer of
\bar{\Phi}_{f_{\varepsilon},\lambda,\varphi_{\varepsilon}}
. Our claim follows again.

\Box

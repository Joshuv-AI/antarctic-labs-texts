Variants of the A-HPE and large-step A-HPE algorithms for strongly convex problems with applications to accelerated high-order tensor methods
M. Marques Alves

Abstract
For solving strongly convex optimization problems, we propose and study the global convergence of variants of the accelerated hybrid proximal extragradient (A-HPE) and large-step A-HPE algorithms of Monteiro and Svaiter [ 21 ] . We prove linear and the superlinear 𝒪 ⁡ ( k − k ⁡ ( p − 1 p + 1 ) ) \mathcal{O}\left(k^{\,-k\left(\frac{p-1}{p+1}\right)}\right) global rates for the proposed variants of the A-HPE and large-step A-HPE methods, respectively. The parameter p ≥ 2 p\geq 2 appears in the (high-order) large-step condition of the new large-step A-HPE algorithm. We apply our results to high-order tensor methods, obtaining a new inexact (relative-error) tensor method for (smooth) strongly convex optimization with iteration-complexity 𝒪 ⁡ ( k − k ⁡ ( p − 1 p + 1 ) ) \mathcal{O}\left(k^{\,-k\left(\frac{p-1}{p+1}\right)}\right) . In particular, for p = 2 p=2 , we obtain an inexact proximal-Newton algorithm with fast global 𝒪 ( k − k / 3 ) \mathcal{O}\left(k^{\,-k/3}\right) convergence rate. 2000 Mathematics Subject Classification: 90C60, 90C25, 47H05, 65K10. Key words: Convex optimization, strongly convex, accelerated methods, proximal-point algorithm, large-step, high-order tensor methods, superlinear convergence, proximal-Newton method.

Introduction

The
proximal-point method

[
17
,
28
]
is one of the most popular algorithms for solving nonsmooth convex optimization problems.
For the general problem of minimizing a convex function
h(\cdot)
, its
exact
version can be described by the iteration

\displaystyle x^{k+1}=\mbox{arg}\min_{x}\,\left\{h(x)+\dfrac{1}{2\lambda}\|{x-x^{k}}\|^{2}\right\},\qquad k\geq 0,

(1)

where
\lambda=\lambda_{k+1}>0
and
x^{k}
is the current iterate.
Motivated by the fact that in many cases the computation of
x^{k+1}
is numerically expensive, several authors have proposed
inexact
versions of (
1
).
Among them, inexact proximal-point methods based on
relative-error
criterion for the subproblems are currently quite popular.
For the more abstract setting of solving inclusions for maximal monotone
operators, this approach was initially developed by Solodov and Svaiter (see, e.g.,
[
29
,
30
,
31
,
32
]
), subsequently studied, from the viewpoint of computational complexity, by
Monteiro and Svaiter
(see, e.g.,
[
19
,
18
,
20
,
21
]
) and has gained a lot of attention by different authors and research groups
(see, e.g.,
[
4
,
5
,
10
,
14
,
16
]
) with many applications
in optimization algorithms and related topics such as variational inequalities, saddle-point problems, etc.

The starting point of this contribution is
[
21
]
, where the
relative-error inexact hybrid proximal extragradient (HPE) method
[
18
,
29
]

was accelerated for convex optimization, by using Nesterov’s acceleration
[
22
]
.
The resulting accelerated HPE-type algorithms, called A-HPE and large-step A-HPE, were applied to first- and second-order optimization, with iteration-complexities
\mathcal{O}\left(1/k^{2}\right)
and

\mathcal{O}\left(1/k^{7/2}\right)
, respectively. The A-HPE and/or the large-step A-HPE algorithms were recently studied also in

[
3
,
5
,
6
,
11
,
14
,
16
]
,
with applications in high-order optimization, machine learning and tensor methods.

In this paper, we consider the (unconstrained) convex optimization problem

\displaystyle\min_{x}\,\left\{h(x):=f(x)+g(x)\right\},

(2)

where
f
is convex and
g
is
strongly convex
.
For solving (
2
), we propose and study the convergence rates of variants of the A-HPE and large-step A-HPE algorithms. The new algorithms are designed especially for strongly convex problems, and the resulting global convergence rates are
linear
and

\mathcal{O}\left(k^{\,-k\left(\frac{p-1}{p+1}\right)}\right)
for the variants of the A-HPE and large-step A-HPE, respectively.
(the parameter
p\geq 2
appears in the high-order large-step condition (see
[
14
,
16
]
.)
We also apply our study to tensor algorithms for high-order convex optimization, a topic which has been the object of investigation of several authors
(see, e.g.,
[
6
,
7
,
13
,
14
,
16

24
,
25

and references therein).
The proposed inexact (relative-error)
p

superlinear

\mathcal{O}\left(k^{\,-k\left(\frac{p-1}{p+1}\right)}\right)
convergence rate. We also mention that, for
p=2
we obtain, as a by-product of our approach to high-order optimization, a fast
\mathcal{O}\left(k^{-k/3}\right)
proximal-Newton method for strongly convex optimization.

General notation.

We denote by
\mathcal{H}
a finite-dimensional real vector space with inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and induced norm
\|{\cdot}\|=\sqrt{\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle}
.
The
\varepsilon
-subdifferential
and the
subdifferential
of a convex function

g:\mathcal{H}\to(-\infty,\infty]
at
x\in\mathcal{H}
are defined as

\partial_{\varepsilon}g(x):=\{u\in\mathcal{H}\;|\;g(y)\geq g(x)+\langle u,y-x\rangle-\varepsilon\quad\forall y\in\mathcal{H}\}
and

\partial g(x):=\partial_{0}g(x)
, respectively. For additional details on standard notations and definitions of convex analysis we refer the reader to the reference
[
27
]
.
Recall that
g:\mathcal{H}\to(-\infty,\infty]
is
\mu
-strongly convex if

\mu>0
and, for all
x,y\in\mathcal{H}
,

\displaystyle g(\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y)\leq\lambda g(x)+(1-\lambda)g(y)-\dfrac{1}{2}\mu\lambda(1-\lambda)\|{x-y}\|^{2},\qquad\forall\lambda\in[0,1].

A variant of the A-HPE algorithm for strongly convex problems

In this section, we consider the convex optimization problem (
2
), i.e.,

\displaystyle\min_{x\in\mathcal{H}}\,\{h(x):=f(x)+g(x)\},

where
f,g:\mathcal{H}\to(-\infty,\infty]
are proper, closed and convex functions,
\mbox{dom}\,h\neq\emptyset
, and

is
\mu
-strongly convex
, for some
\mu>0
. We will denote by
x^{*}
the unique solution of (
2
).

Next we present the main algorithm of this section for solving (
2
), whose the complexity analysis will be presented in Theorems
2.6
and

Algorithm 1
.

A variant of the A-HPE algorithm for solving the (strongly convex) problem (
2
)

0)

Choose
x^{0},y^{0}\in\mathcal{H}
,
\sigma\in[0,1]
, let
A_{0}=0
and set
k=0
.

1)

Compute
\lambda_{k+1}>0
and
(y^{k+1},v^{k+1},\varepsilon_{k+1})\in\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{H}\times\mathbb{R}_{++}
such that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &v^{k+1}\in\partial_{\varepsilon_{k+1}}f(y^{k+1})+\partial g(y^{k+1}),\\[8.53581pt]
&\dfrac{\|{\lambda_{k+1}v^{k+1}+y^{k+1}-\widetilde{x}^{k}}\|^{2}}{1+\lambda_{k+1}\,\mu}+2\lambda_{k+1}\varepsilon_{k+1}\leq\sigma^{2}\|{y^{k+1}-\widetilde{x}^{k}}\|^{2},\end{aligned}

(4)

where

\displaystyle\widetilde{x}^{k}=\left(\dfrac{a_{k+1}-\mu A_{k}\lambda_{k+1}}{A_{k}+a_{k+1}}\right)x^{k}+\left(\dfrac{A_{k}+\mu A_{k}\lambda_{k+1}}{A_{k}+a_{k+1}}\right)y^{k},

(5)

\displaystyle a_{k+1}=\dfrac{(1+2\mu A_{k})\lambda_{k+1}+\sqrt{(1+2\mu A_{k})^{2}\lambda_{k+1}^{2}+4(1+\mu A_{k})A_{k}\lambda_{k+1}}}{2}.

(6)

2)

Let

\displaystyle A_{k+1}=A_{k}+a_{k+1},

(7)

\displaystyle x^{k+1}=\left(\dfrac{1+\mu A_{k}}{1+\mu A_{k+1}}\right)x^{k}+\left(\dfrac{\mu a_{k+1}}{1+\mu A_{k+1}}\right)y^{k+1}-\left(\dfrac{a_{k+1}}{1+\mu A_{k+1}}\right)v^{k+1}.

(8)

3)

Set
k=k+1
and go to step 1.

Next we make some remarks about Algorithm
1
:

(i)

By letting

[

\mathcal{O}\left(1/k^{2}\right)

[

On the other hand, thanks to the strong-convexity assumption on

We will also study a

Applications of the latter result to high-order tensor methods for convex optimization will also be discussed in Section

(ii)

Since the cost of computing

and

(

)–(

In this regard, note that if

On the other hand, in the more general setting of

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\gamma_{k}(\cdot)

\Gamma_{k}(\cdot)

The following holds:

(a)

For all

\gamma_{k}(x)\leq h(x),\quad\forall x\in\mathcal{H}

(b)

For all
k\geq 0
, we have
x^{k}=\arg\min_{x\in\mathcal{H}}\{A_{k}\Gamma_{k}(x)+\frac{1}{2}\|{x-x^{0}}\|^{2}\}

Proof.

(a) In view of the inclusion in (

k\geq 1
,

v^{k}=r^{k}+s^{k}
, where
r^{k}\in\partial_{\varepsilon_{k}}f(y^{k})

Using the assumption that

\mu
-strongly convex and the definition of the

-subdifferential of

\displaystyle f(x)\geq f(y^{k})+\langle r^{k},x-y^{k}\rangle-\varepsilon_{k},

\displaystyle g(x)\geq g(y^{k})+\langle s^{k},x-y^{k}\rangle+\dfrac{\mu}{2}\|{x-y^{k}}\|^{2},

which in turn combined with the definition of
h(\cdot)
in (
2
),
the fact that

(b) Let us proceed by induction on

. The result is trivially true for

A_{0}\Gamma_{0}=0
).
Assume now that it is true for some

, i.e., assume that

x^{k}=\arg\min_{x}\{A_{k}\Gamma_{k}(x)+\frac{1}{2}\|{x-x^{0}}\|^{2}\}

Using the latter identity, (
16
)–(
18
) and Taylor’s theorem we find

\displaystyle A_{k+1}\Gamma_{k+1}(x)+\dfrac{1}{2}\|{x-x^{0}}\|^{2}

4
Applications to accelerated high-order tensor methods for strongly convex objectives

In this section, we consider the problem

\displaystyle\min_{x\in\mathcal{H}}\,\left\{h(x):=f(x)+g(x)\right\},

(56)

where
f,g:\mathcal{H}\to(-\infty,\infty]
are proper, closed and convex functions,
\mbox{dom}\,h\neq\emptyset
, and

g
is
\mu
-strongly convex
on
\mathcal{H}
and
p\geq 2

times continuously differentiable

on
\Omega\supseteq\mbox{Dom}\,(\partial f)

with
D^{p}g(\cdot)
being
L_{p}
-Lipschitz continuous on
\Omega
:
0<L_{p}<+\infty
and

\displaystyle\|{D^{p}g(x)-D^{p}g(y)}\|\leq L_{p}\|{x-y}\|,\qquad\forall x,y\in\Omega.

(57)

Define

\displaystyle g_{x,p}(y):=g(x)+\sum_{k=1}^{p}\,\dfrac{1}{k!}D^{k}g(x)[y-x]^{k}+\dfrac{M}{(p+1)!}\|{y-x}\|^{p+1},\qquad(x,y)\in\Omega\times\mathcal{H},

(58)

where
M>0
is such that
M\geq pL_{p}
.

As observed by Nesterov in
[
26
]
, the function
g_{x,p}(\cdot)
is convex whenever
M\geq pL_{p}
and, moreover,

\displaystyle\|{\nabla g(y)-\nabla g_{x,p}(y)}\|\leq\dfrac{L_{p}+M}{p!}\|{y-x}\|^{p},\qquad\forall(x,y)\in\Omega\times\mathcal{H}.

(59)

At each iteration of the (exact) Proximal-Tensor method for solving (
56
) one has to find

y\in\mathcal{H}
solving an inclusion of the form

\displaystyle 0\in\lambda\Big(\partial f(y)+\nabla g_{z,p}(y)\Big)+y-x,

(60)

where
z=P_{\Omega}(x)
and
\lambda>0
. Note also that (
60
) is equivalent to solving the convex problem

\displaystyle\min_{y\in\mathcal{H}}\,\left\{f(y)+g_{z,p}(y)+\dfrac{1}{2\lambda}\|{y-x}\|^{2}\right\}.

(61)

Next definition introduces a notion of
relative-error
inexact solution for (
60
) (or, equivalently, (
61
)). It will be used in step 2 (see (
66
)) of Algorithm
3
, and can be motivated as follows.
Note that the inclusion (
60
) can be splitted as an inclusion-equation system:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}u\in\partial f(y),&\\[5.69054pt]
\lambda\Big(u+\nabla g_{z,p}(y)\Big)+y-x=0.\end{cases}

Definition
4.1
below relaxes the latter conditions by allowing errors in both the inclusion
(
u
will belong to
\partial_{\varepsilon}f(y)
instead of belonging to
\partial f(y)
) and the equation.

Definition 4.1
.

The triple
(y,u,\varepsilon)\in\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{H}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
is a
\hat{\sigma}
-approximate
Tensor solution
of (
60
) at
(x,\lambda)\in\mathcal{H}\times\mathbb{R}_{++}
if

\hat{\sigma}\geq 0
and

\displaystyle u\in\partial_{\varepsilon}f(y),\qquad\dfrac{\left\|\lambda\Big(u+\nabla g_{z,p}(y)\Big)+y-x\right\|^{2}}{1+\lambda\mu}+2\lambda\varepsilon\leq\hat{\sigma}^{2}\|{y-x}\|^{2},

(62)

where
z=P_{\Omega}(x)
.

Note that if
\hat{\sigma}=0
in (
62
), then it follows that

\varepsilon=0
,
u\in\partial f(y)
and
\lambda\Big(u+\nabla g_{z,p}(y)\Big)+y-x=0
, which implies that

y
is the solution of (
60
).
We also mention that if we set
\mu=0
in Definition
4.1
then we recover

[
14
, Definition 2.1]
(see also
[
16
, Definition 1]
).

Next proposition shows that
\hat{\sigma}
-approximate solutions of (
60
) provide relative-error approximate solutions in the sense of (
48
).

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
(u,y,\varepsilon)
be a
\hat{\sigma}
-approximate Tensor solution of (
60
) at

(x,\lambda)\in\mathcal{H}\times\mathbb{R}_{++}

(
in the sense of
Definition
4.1
)
and define

\displaystyle v=u+\nabla g(y),\qquad\sigma=\dfrac{\lambda(L_{p}+M)\|{y-x}\|^{p-1}}{p!\sqrt{1+\lambda\mu}}+\hat{\sigma}.

(63)

Then,

\displaystyle v\in\partial_{\varepsilon}f(y)+\nabla g(y),\qquad\dfrac{\|{\lambda v+y-x}\|^{2}}{1+\lambda\mu}+2\lambda\varepsilon\leq\sigma^{2}\|{y-x}\|^{2}.

(64)

Proof.

Note that the inclusion in (
64
) follows from the definition of
v
in (
63
) and the inclusion in (
62
).
To prove the inequality in (

in (

\displaystyle\|{\lambda v+y-x}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|{\lambda\left(u+\nabla g_{z,p}(y)\right)+y-x+\lambda\big(\nabla g(y)-\nabla g_{z,p}(y)\big)}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\|{\lambda\left(u+\nabla g_{z,p}(y)\right)+y-x}\|+\lambda\|{\nabla g(y)-\nabla g_{z,p}(y)}\|\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\|{\lambda\left(u+\nabla g_{z,p}(y)\right)+y-x}\|+\frac{\lambda(L_{p}+M)}{p!}\|{y-z}\|^{p}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\|{\lambda\left(u+\nabla g_{z,p}(y)\right)+y-x}\|+\frac{\lambda(L_{p}+M)}{p!}\|{y-x}\|^{p}\right)^{2},

where in the last inequality we also used the fact that
\|{y-z}\|\leq\|{y-x}\|
.
(because

and

Hence,

\displaystyle\dfrac{\|{\lambda v+y-x}\|^{2}}{1+\lambda\mu}+2\lambda\varepsilon

\displaystyle\leq\left(\dfrac{\|{\lambda\left(u+\nabla g_{z,p}(y)\right)+y-x}\|}{\sqrt{1+\lambda\mu}}+\frac{\lambda(L_{p}+M)}{p!\sqrt{1+\lambda\mu}}\|{y-x}\|^{p}\right)^{2}+2\lambda\varepsilon.

Using now the elementary inequality
(a+b)^{2}+c\leq\left(b+\sqrt{a^{2}+c}\right)^{2}

with

,

b=\lambda(L_{p}+M)\|{y-x}\|^{p}/(p!\sqrt{1+\lambda\mu})

and

, we find

\displaystyle\dfrac{\|{\lambda v+y-x}\|^{2}}{1+\lambda\mu}+2\lambda\varepsilon

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{\lambda(L_{p}+M)}{p!\sqrt{1+\lambda\mu}}\|{y-x}\|^{p}+\sqrt{\dfrac{\|{\lambda\left(u+\nabla g_{z,p}(y)\right)+y-x}\|^{2}}{1+\lambda\mu}+2\lambda\varepsilon}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{\lambda(L_{p}+M)}{p!\sqrt{1+\lambda\mu}}\|{y-x}\|^{p}+\hat{\sigma}\|{y-x}\|\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\lambda(L_{p}+M)}{p!\sqrt{1+\lambda\mu}}\|{y-x}\|^{p-1}+\hat{\sigma}\right)^{2}\|{y-x}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sigma^{2}\|{y-x}\|^{2},

where in the second inequality we used the inequality in (
62
) and in the second identity we used the second
equality (
63
).
∎

Appendix A
Some auxiliary results

Lemma A.1
.

For all
k\geq 1
, the optimal value of the minimization problem, over
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k}>0
,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\min\,\prod_{j=1}^{k}\left(1+\lambda_{j}\right)\\[5.69054pt]
&\emph{s.t.}\;\;\sum_{j=1}^{k}\dfrac{1}{\lambda_{j}^{q}}\leq c,\end{aligned}

(89)

where
c>0
and
q\geq 1
, is given by

\left(1+\left(\dfrac{k}{c}\right)^{1/q}\right)^{k}.

Proof.

First consider the convex problem

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\min\,\sum_{j=1}^{k}\,\log(1+e^{t_{j}})\\[5.69054pt]
&\mbox{s.t.}\;\;\sum_{j=1}^{k}\dfrac{1}{e^{qt_{j}}}\leq c.\end{aligned}

(90)

Since the objective and constraint functions in (
90
) are convex and invariant under permutations on

(t_{1},\dots,t_{k})
, it follows that one of its solutions takes the form
(t,\dots,t)
. It is also clear that at any solution
the inequality in (
90
) must hold as an equality. Hence,
k\frac{1}{e^{qt}}=c
, i.e.,

e^{t}=\left(\frac{k}{c}\right)^{1/q}
. As a consequence, for all
(t_{1},\dots,t_{k})
such that

\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{1}{e^{qt_{j}}}\leq c
,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k}\,\log(1+e^{t_{j}})\geq k\log(1+e^{t})=k\log\left(1+\left(\dfrac{k}{c}\right)^{1/q}\right)=\log\left(\left(1+\left(\dfrac{k}{c}\right)^{1/q}\right)^{k}\right).

(91)

Now let
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k}>0
be such that
\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{1}{\lambda_{j}^{q}}\leq c
and define

t_{j}:=\log(\lambda_{j})
, for
j\in\{1,\dots,k\}
. Then, since in this case
\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{1}{e^{qt_{j}}}\leq c
, using
(
91
) and some basic properties of logarithms we find

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{k}\,(1+\lambda_{j})=\prod_{j=1}^{k}\,(1+e^{t_{j}})

\displaystyle=e^{\log\left(\prod_{j=1}^{k}\,(1+e^{t_{j}})\right)}

\displaystyle=e^{\sum_{j=1}^{k}\,\log(1+e^{t_{j}})}

\displaystyle\geq e^{\log\left(\left(1+\left(\dfrac{k}{c}\right)^{1/q}\right)^{k}\right)}

\displaystyle=\left(1+\left(\dfrac{k}{c}\right)^{1/q}\right)^{k},

which concludes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Lemma A.2
.

The following holds for

defined by

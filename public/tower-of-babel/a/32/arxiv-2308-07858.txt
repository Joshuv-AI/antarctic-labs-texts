Tilt-induced charge localisation in phosphide antiperovskite photovoltaics
Ruiqi Wu, Alex M. Ganose

Abstract
Antiperovskites are a rich family of compounds with applications in battery cathodes, superconductors, solid-state lighting, and catalysis. Recently, a novel series of antimonide phosphide antiperovskites ( A 3 ​ SbP \text{\text{$A$}}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}\text{SbP} , where A A = Ca, Sr, Ba) were proposed as candidate photovoltaic absorbers due to their ideal band gaps, small effective masses and strong optical absorption. In this work, we explore this series of compounds in more detail using relativistic hybrid density functional theory. We reveal that the proposed cubic structures are dynamically unstable and instead identify a tilted orthorhombic P ​ n ​ m ​ a Pnma phase as the ground state. Tilting is shown to induce charge localisation that widens the band gap and increases the effective masses. Despite this, we demonstrate that the predicted maximum photovoltaic efficiencies remain high ( 24 – 31 % 2431\text{\,}\mathrm{\char 37\relax} for 200 nm 200\text{\,}\mathrm{nm} thin films) by bringing the band gaps into the ideal range for a solar absorber. Finally, we assess the band alignment of the series and suggest hole and electron contact materials for efficient photovoltaic devices.

One route to resolving the ground state structure of dynamically unstable phases is through phonon mode-mapping.
In this approach, the structure is displaced along the eigenvectors of an imaginary mode and allowed to relaxed.
The phonons are recalculated and the process repeated until no imaginary modes remain.
This approach has been successfully applied to discover the complex ground state of bismuth stannate containing 176 atoms.
Rahim et al.

To understand the trend in the band gaps of the series, we examine the orbital contributions at the band edges.
The orbital-projected density of states of

2.8\text{\,}\mathrm{eV}

-0.11\text{\,}\mathrm{eV}

-0.06\text{\,}\mathrm{eV}

-0.02\text{\,}\mathrm{eV}

3.4
Band alignment

Photovoltaic devices with high performance require electron and hole contact materials with suitable alignments to the valence and conduction bands of the absorber.
An efficient alignment prevents open-circuit voltage losses and minimises barriers for carrier diffusion at the interfaces.
To provide insight into ideal device architectures, we perform band alignment calculations using a slab–vacuum model.
Our calculations aim to understand the “bulk” band alignment — namely, they do not take into account relaxations and band bending at the surface.
We calculate ultra-low ionisation potentials and electron affinities of
2.62.9\text{\,}\mathrm{eV}
and
1.11.2\text{\,}\mathrm{eV}
, respectively.
We find the depth of the conduction band remains roughly fixed down the series, whereas the valence band edge is raised monotonically from Ca to Ba.
Our calculated band offsets are roughly
1\text{\,}\mathrm{eV}
smaller than those found in the nitride antiperovskites.
Kang 2022

For example,
Kang 2022
calculated an ionisation potential and electron affinity of
3.3\text{\,}\mathrm{eV}
and
2.4\text{\,}\mathrm{eV}
for
\text{Sr}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}\text{SbN}
, using a slab–vaccuum approach with PBE+SOC.
The origin of this shift can be attributed to the greater electronegativity of nitrogen, which acts to push the valence band further from the vacuum level.

5

to be considerably smaller than any other commonly used absorber, for example Si and AgCuS which are closest in magnitude at

Conclusion

We evaluated the
\text{\text{$A$}}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}\text{SbP}
antiperovskites (where A = Ca, Sr, Ba) as potential photovoltaics using relativistic hybrid density functional theory calculations.
In contrast to previous reports,
Liang et al.
we demonstrate the cubic
Pm\mkern 1.0mu\overline{\mkern-1.0mu3\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.0mum
phase is likely unstable and will spontaneously distort to an orthorhombic
Pnma
structure.
We evaluate the thermodynamic stability of this phase against other antiperovskite-like compositions and reveal that it is dynamically stable, with no imaginary modes found in the phonon band structure.
The series possesses ideal band gaps between
1.41.6\text{\,}\mathrm{eV}
, with small electron effective masses, and strong visible light absorption.
The predicted maximum theoretical efficiencies of
2431\text{\,}\mathrm{\char 37\relax}
for
200\text{\,}\mathrm{nm}
thin films are competitive with other state-of-the-art and emerging absorbers.
Furthermore, we performed band alignment calculations to recommend hole and electron contact materials that will provide efficient charge extraction in optimised devices.
We believe that the
\text{\text{$A$}}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}\text{SbP}
series show potential as photovoltaic absorbers and should be targeted for experimental verification.

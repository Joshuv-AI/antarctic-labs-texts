Scaling Asymptotics of Wigner Distributions of Harmonic Oscillator Orbital Coherent States
Nicholas Lohr

Abstract
. The main result of this article gives scaling asymptotics of the Wigner distributions W φ N γ , φ N γ W_{\varphi_{N}^{\gamma},\varphi_{N}^{\gamma}} of isotropic harmonic oscillator orbital coherent states φ N γ \varphi_{N}^{\gamma} concentrating along Hamiltonian orbits γ \gamma in shrinking tubes around γ \gamma in phase space. In particular, these Wigner distributions exhibit a hybrid semi-classical scaling. That is, simultaneously, we have an Airy scaling when the tube has radius N − 2 / 3 N^{-2/3} normal to the energy surface Σ E \Sigma_{E} , and a Gaussian scaling when the tube has radius N − 1 / 2 N^{-1/2} tangent to Σ E \Sigma_{E} .

1.
Introduction

Coherent states centered at points in phase space were introduced by Schrödinger
[
S26
]
in 1926 as minimal uncertainty states. In 1963, Glauber
[
G63
]

extended coherent states to quantum electrodynamics. This paper studies
orbital coherent states
centered on periodic classical Hamiltonian orbits. Orbital coherent states were first studied in 1989 for the Hydrogen atom in the papers of Gay et al.
[
GDB89
]
and Nauenberg
[
N89
]
, and more recently in papers by Klauder
[
K96
]
and Villegas-Blas et al.
[
VB96
,
TVB97
]
. In 1992, De Bièvre
[
De92
]
studied orbital coherent states (
4
) for the
two-dimensional isotropic harmonic oscillator:

\widehat{H}_{\hbar}\coloneqq-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2}\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}+\frac{|x|^{2}}{2}.

(1)

For
\gamma_{0}(t)\coloneqq\sqrt{E}(\cos t,\sin t,-\sin t,\cos t)
in
T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{2}
with fixed energy
E=\hbar(N+1)
, the
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
-normalized orbital coherent state associated to
\gamma_{0}
is

\varphi_{N}^{\gamma_{0}}(x_{1},x_{2})=\frac{(N+1)^{\frac{N+1}{2}}}{E^{\frac{N+1}{2}}\sqrt{\pi N!}}(x_{1}+ix_{2})^{N}e^{-\tfrac{(N+1)}{2E}|x|^{2}}.

(2)

From this initial coherent state
\varphi_{N}^{\gamma_{0}}
, we define
\varphi_{N}^{\gamma}
for a general Hamiltonian orbit
\gamma
with the metaplectic representation (see (
4
) of Section
1.1
).
This article concerns the Wigner distributions of these orbital coherent states. We recall that in dimension 2, the Wigner distribution
W_{\varphi_{N}^{\gamma},\varphi_{N}^{\gamma}}:T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
of
\varphi_{N}^{\gamma}
is
defined by

\displaystyle W_{\varphi_{N}^{\gamma},\varphi_{N}^{\gamma}}(x,\xi)\coloneqq

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{4\pi^{2}\hbar^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\varphi_{N}^{\gamma}(x+\tfrac{v}{2})\overline{\varphi_{N}^{\gamma}(x-\tfrac{v}{2})}e^{-\frac{i}{\hbar}\langle v,\xi\rangle}\ dv

(see
[
W32
]
).
It is well-known that for fixed energy, the Wigner distributions tend in the weak
sense to a delta function along the orbit
\gamma
as
N\to\infty
, i.e.

W_{\varphi_{N}^{\gamma},\varphi_{N}^{\gamma}}\rightharpoonup\delta_{\gamma}

(3)

(see
[
De92
,
De93
]
). For completeness, we prove (
3
) in Proposition
1.1
. Our purpose is to study the
fine structure of this limit in small tubes around
\gamma
. The main result of this article gives Airy and Gaussian scaling asymptotics
of the Wigner distribution

W_{\varphi_{N}^{\gamma},\varphi_{N}^{\gamma}}
of

\varphi_{N}^{\gamma}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
(see Theorem

1.3
). In this article, we prove all of the results in dimension 2 for notational convenience. These results generalize to dimension
d
and the same arguments hold, as explained in Section
4
.

1.1.
Statements of Results

The orbital coherent states (
4
) are eigenstates of (
1
),

\widehat{H}_{\hbar}\varphi_{N}^{\gamma_{0}}=E\varphi_{N}^{\gamma_{0}}

where
E=\hbar(N+1)
. The non-constant Hamiltonian orbits of the classical Hamiltonian
H(x,\xi)=\frac{1}{2}|\xi|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|x|^{2}
are great circles on the energy surface
\Sigma_{E}:=\{(x,\xi):H(x,\xi)=E\}
. Modding out by the points on the same orbit, we see that the space of non-constant orbits is isomorphic to
S^{3}/S^{1}=\mathbb{C}\text{P}^{1}
. Since
\operatorname{SU}(2)
acts transitively on
\mathbb{C}\text{P}^{1}
by rotation, for any non-constant Hamiltonian orbit
\gamma
there exists a
U\in\operatorname{SU}(2)
such that
U\cdot\gamma_{0}=\gamma
. We define (up to a phase factor) the coherent state centered at the Hamiltonian orbit
\gamma
by

\varphi_{N}^{\gamma}\coloneqq\mu(U)\varphi_{N}^{\gamma_{0}}

(4)

where
\mu
is the metaplectic representation on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})

(see Chapter 4 of
[
F89
]
for details on the metaplectic representation).

The first result of this article is a proof of (
3
).

Proposition
1.1
.

Let
a:T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
be a smooth function with exponential decay. Then in the limit
N\to\infty
where
E=\hbar(N+1)
, we have

\int_{T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{2}}a(x,\xi)W_{\varphi_{N}^{\gamma},\varphi_{N}^{\gamma}}(x,\xi)\ dxd\xi\xrightarrow{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}a\big(\gamma(t)\big)dt.

(5)

It was proved in
[
De92
]
that given a smooth function
a:T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
with exponential decay, in the limit
N\to\infty
where
E=\hbar(N+1)
, we have

\langle\varphi_{N}^{\gamma},\operatorname{Op}_{N}^{aw}(a)[\varphi_{N}^{\gamma}]\rangle\xrightarrow{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}a\big(\gamma(t)\big)dt,

(6)

where
\operatorname{Op}_{N}^{aw}(a)
is the anti-Wick quantization of

. Proposition

(7)

The next result gives the pointwise asymptotics of

W_{\varphi_{N}^{\gamma},\varphi_{N}^{\gamma}}(x,\xi)
for
(x,\xi)
on and off the orbit
\gamma
(see Figure

1.2.
Future Work

The asymptotics of the Wigner distributions in this paper are some of the few explicit results on Wigner distributions of eigenfunctions. In future work, we plan to study the asymptotics of Wigner distributions of orbital coherent states of the hydrogen atom. They are much more difficult to compute, but the isotropic harmonic oscillator of this paper provides a more readily computable model which guides future work.

Preliminaries

2.1.1.
Formula

Before we get into the proofs of the theorems, we first write
down a formula for the Wigner distribution. Recall from (
4
) that the
coherent state
\varphi_{N}^{\gamma_{0}}
is

\varphi_{N}^{\gamma_{0}}(x)=\frac{(N+1)^{\frac{N+1}{2}}}{E^{\frac{N+1}{2}}\sqrt{\pi N!}}(x_{1}+ix_{2})^{N}e^{-\tfrac{(N+1)}{2E}|x|^{2}}.

The Wigner distribution for
E=(N+1)\hbar
is then

\displaystyle W_{\varphi_{N}^{\gamma_{0}},\varphi_{N}^{\gamma_{0}}}(x,\xi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\varphi_{N}^{\gamma_{0}}(x+\tfrac{v}{2})\overline{\varphi_{N}^{\gamma_{0}}(x-\tfrac{v}{2})}e^{-\frac{i}{\hbar}\langle v,\xi\rangle}\frac{dv}{4\pi^{2}\hbar^{2}}

(8)

\displaystyle=\frac{(N+1)^{N+3}}{4\pi^{3}E^{N+3}N!}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(x+\tfrac{v}{2})^{N}\overline{(x-\tfrac{v}{2})}^{N}e^{-\frac{N+1}{2E}|x-\frac{v}{2}|^{2}}e^{-\frac{N+1}{2E}|x+\frac{v}{2}|^{2}}e^{-i\frac{(N+1)}{E}\langle v,\xi\rangle}dv

where the multiplication within the integral is complex multiplication. Since

|x-\tfrac{v}{2}|^{2}+|x+\tfrac{v}{2}|^{2}=2|x|^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}|v|^{2},

we have

\displaystyle W_{\varphi_{N}^{\gamma_{0}},\varphi_{N}^{\gamma_{0}}}(x,\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{(N+1)^{N+3}}{4\pi^{3}E^{N+3}N!}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(x+\tfrac{v}{2})^{N}\overline{(x-\tfrac{v}{2})}^{N}e^{-\frac{N+1}{2E}|x-\frac{v}{2}|^{2}}e^{-\frac{N+1}{2E}|x+\frac{v}{2}|^{2}}e^{-i\frac{(N+1)}{E}\langle v,\xi\rangle}dv

\displaystyle=\frac{(N+1)^{N+3}}{4\pi^{3}E^{N+3}N!}e^{-\frac{(N+1)}{E}|x|^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(x+\tfrac{v}{2})^{N}\overline{(x-\tfrac{v}{2})}^{N}e^{-\frac{N+1}{4E}|v|^{2}}e^{-i\frac{(N+1)}{E}\langle v,\xi\rangle}dv

(9)

\displaystyle=\frac{(N+1)^{N+3}}{4\pi^{3}E^{N+2}N!}e^{-\frac{(N+1)}{E}|x|^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(x+\tfrac{v}{2}\sqrt{E})^{N}\overline{(x-\tfrac{v}{2}\sqrt{E})}^{N}e^{-\frac{N+1}{4}|v|^{2}}e^{-i\frac{(N+1)}{\sqrt{E}}\langle v,\xi\rangle}dv,

where the last line we applied the substitution
v\to\sqrt{E}v
.
To apply the stationary phase method on this formula later on, we write

e^{N\log(x+\frac{v}{2}\sqrt{E})\overline{(x-\frac{v}{2}\sqrt{E})}}=(x+\tfrac{v}{2}\sqrt{E})^{N}\overline{(x-\tfrac{v}{2}\sqrt{E})}^{N}

where we use the principal branch of
\log
and define the complex phase

\displaystyle\Psi(x,\xi,v)\coloneqq

\displaystyle-i\log(x+\tfrac{v}{2}\sqrt{E})\overline{(x-\tfrac{v}{2}\sqrt{E})}+i\log(E)+i(\tfrac{|x|^{2}}{E}-1)+i\tfrac{|v|^{2}}{4}-\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{E}}\langle v,\xi\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-i\log(\tfrac{x}{\sqrt{E}}+\tfrac{v}{2})\overline{(\tfrac{x}{\sqrt{E}}-\tfrac{v}{2})}+i(\tfrac{|x|^{2}}{E}-1)+i\tfrac{|v|^{2}}{4}-\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{E}}\langle v,\xi\rangle

(10)

The Wigner distribution is then

W_{\varphi_{N}^{\gamma_{0}},\varphi_{N}^{\gamma_{0}}}(x,\xi)=\frac{(N+1)^{N+3}}{4\pi^{3}E^{2}N!}e^{-N-\frac{1}{E}|x|^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{-\frac{1}{4E}|v|^{2}}e^{-\frac{i}{E}\langle v,\xi\rangle}e^{iN\Psi(x,\xi,v)}dv.

(11)

We can use Stirling’s approximation to write

W_{\varphi_{N}^{\gamma_{0}},\varphi_{N}^{\gamma_{0}}}(x,\xi)=\frac{(N+1)^{5/2}}{4\pi^{3}\sqrt{2\pi}E^{2}}e^{1-\frac{1}{E}|x|^{2}}\big(1+O(\tfrac{1}{N})\big)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{-\frac{1}{4}|v|^{2}}e^{-\frac{i}{\sqrt{E}}\langle v,\xi\rangle}e^{iN\Psi(x,\xi,v)}dv,

which implies

\tfrac{1}{N^{5/3}}W_{\varphi_{N}^{\gamma_{0}},\varphi_{N}^{\gamma_{0}}}(x,\xi)=\frac{N^{5/6}}{4\pi^{3}\sqrt{2\pi}E^{2}}e^{1-\frac{1}{E}|x|^{2}}\big(1+O(\tfrac{1}{N})\big)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{-\frac{1}{4}|v|^{2}}e^{-\frac{i}{\sqrt{E}}\langle v,\xi\rangle}e^{iN\Psi(x,\xi,v)}dv.

(12)

The next lemma will be very useful for our stationary phase
approximations.

Lemma
2.1
.

For
\Psi
defined in (
10
), we have

\operatorname{Im}\Psi(x,\xi,v)\geq 0

with equality if and only if
|v|^{2}=4(1-\tfrac{1}{E}|x|^{2})
and
\langle x,v\rangle=0
.

Proof.

Indeed,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Im}\Psi(x,\xi,v)

\displaystyle\overset{\mathclap{\eqref{eq:phase}}}{=}-\log\Big|(\tfrac{x}{\sqrt{E}}+\tfrac{v}{2})\overline{(\tfrac{x}{\sqrt{E}}-\tfrac{v}{2})}\Big|+\tfrac{|x|^{2}}{E}-1+\tfrac{|v|^{2}}{4}

\displaystyle=-\log\big|\tfrac{|x|^{2}}{E}-\tfrac{|v|^{2}}{4}+i\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{E}}\operatorname{Im}(v\overline{x})\big|+\tfrac{|x|^{2}}{E}-1+\tfrac{|v|^{2}}{4}

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{1}{2}\log\Big(\big(\tfrac{|x|^{2}}{E}-\tfrac{|v|^{2}}{4}\big)^{2}+\tfrac{1}{E}\big(\operatorname{Im}(v\overline{x})\big)^{2}\Big)+\tfrac{|x|^{2}}{E}-1+\tfrac{|v|^{2}}{4}

\displaystyle\geq-\tfrac{1}{2}\log\Big(\big(\tfrac{|x|^{2}}{E}-\tfrac{|v|^{2}}{4}\big)^{2}+\tfrac{1}{E}|xv|^{2}\Big)+\tfrac{|x|^{2}}{E}-1+\tfrac{|v|^{2}}{4}

\displaystyle=-\log\big(\tfrac{|x|^{2}}{E}+\tfrac{|v|^{2}}{4}\big)+\tfrac{|x|^{2}}{E}-1+\tfrac{|v|^{2}}{4}

\displaystyle=-\log\big(1+\tfrac{|x|^{2}}{E}-1+\tfrac{|v|^{2}}{4}\big)+\tfrac{|x|^{2}}{E}-1+\tfrac{|v|^{2}}{4}

\displaystyle\geq 0,

where the first inequality we use the fact that
|\operatorname{Im}z|\leq|z|
(with equality if and only if
\operatorname{Re}z=0
), and the last inequality we use the fact that
-\log(1+x)+x\geq 0
for all
x\geq-1
(with equality if and only if
x=0
), which completes the proof.
∎

2.1.2.
Symmetries

One important property of Wigner distributions is the so-called
metaplectic covariance. In dimension 2, it states that given
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and
\mu
the metaplectic representation
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, we have

W_{\mu(A)f,\mu(A)f}(x,\xi)=W_{f,f}\big(A^{*}(x,\xi)\big)

(13)

for all
A\in\operatorname{Sp}(4,\mathbb{R})
(see page 180 of
[
F89
]
). This is useful
in proving results for the Wigner distributions of general
coherent states
\varphi_{N}^{\gamma}
from that of Wigner distributions of

\varphi_{N}^{\gamma_{0}}
. In particular, if
U\in\operatorname{SU}(2)\subset\operatorname{Sp}(4,\mathbb{R})
is
such that
U\cdot\gamma_{0}=\gamma
, then we have

W_{\varphi_{N}^{\gamma},\varphi_{N}^{\gamma}}(x,\xi)\overset{\mathclap{\eqref{eq:meta}}}{=}W_{\mu(U)\varphi_{N}^{\gamma_{0}},\mu(U)\varphi_{N}^{\gamma_{0}}}(x,\xi)\overset{\mathclap{\eqref{eq:genphi}}}{=}W_{\varphi_{N}^{\gamma_{0}},\varphi_{N}^{\gamma_{0}}}\big(U^{*}(x,\xi)\big).

(14)

The next lemma will be useful in proving our results for general points in phase space from an initial point.

Lemma
2.2
.

For
g_{\theta}=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta&-\sin\theta\\
\sin\theta&\cos\theta\end{pmatrix}\in\operatorname{SO}(2)
, we have

W_{\varphi_{N}^{\gamma_{0}},\varphi_{N}^{\gamma_{0}}}(g_{\theta}\cdot x,g_{\theta}\cdot\xi)=W_{\varphi_{N}^{\gamma_{0}},\varphi_{N}^{\gamma_{0}}}(x,\xi).

In particular,
W_{\varphi_{N}^{\gamma},\varphi_{N}^{\gamma}}
is radial in the plane containing
\gamma
.

Proof.

One proof is to use the fact that the restricted representation of the metaplectic representation to the subgroup
\operatorname{SO}(2)
is rotation of functions. The coherent state
\varphi_{N}^{\gamma_{0}}
is invariant under rotation up to a phase factor, which results in equal Wigner distributions. Alternatively,
we can use formula (
9
). We have

\displaystyle W_{\varphi_{N}^{\gamma_{0}},\varphi_{N}^{\gamma_{0}}}

\displaystyle(g_{\theta}\cdot x,g_{\theta}\cdot\xi)=\frac{(N+1)^{N+3}}{4\pi^{3}E^{N+3}N!}e^{-\frac{(N+1)}{E}|g_{\theta}\cdot x|^{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(g_{\theta}\cdot x+\tfrac{v}{2})^{N}\overline{(g_{\theta}\cdot x-\tfrac{v}{2})}^{N}e^{-\frac{N+1}{4E}|v|^{2}}e^{-i\frac{(N+1)}{E}\langle v,g_{\theta}\cdot\xi\rangle}dv

\displaystyle=\frac{(N+1)^{N+3}}{4\pi^{3}E^{N+3}N!}e^{-\frac{(N+1)}{E}|x|^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(g_{\theta}\cdot x+\tfrac{v}{2})^{N}\overline{(g_{\theta}\cdot x-\tfrac{v}{2})}^{N}e^{-\frac{N+1}{4E}|v|^{2}}e^{-i\frac{(N+1)}{E}\langle g_{\theta}^{*}\cdot v,\xi\rangle}dv

\displaystyle=\frac{(N+1)^{N+3}}{4\pi^{3}E^{N+3}N!}e^{-\frac{(N+1)}{E}|x|^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(g_{\theta}\cdot x+\tfrac{g_{\theta}\cdot v}{2})^{N}\overline{(g_{\theta}\cdot x-\tfrac{g_{\theta}\cdot v}{2})}^{N}e^{-\frac{N+1}{4E}|g_{\theta}\cdot v|^{2}}e^{-i\frac{(N+1)}{E}\langle v,\xi\rangle}dv

\displaystyle=\frac{(N+1)^{N+3}}{4\pi^{3}E^{N+3}N!}e^{-\frac{(N+1)}{E}|x|^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\big(g_{\theta}\cdot(x+\tfrac{v}{2})\big)^{N}\overline{\big(g_{\theta}\cdot(x-\tfrac{v}{2})\big)}^{N}e^{-\frac{N+1}{4E}|v|^{2}}e^{-i\frac{(N+1)}{E}\langle v,\xi\rangle}dv

\displaystyle=\frac{(N+1)^{N+3}}{4\pi^{3}E^{N+3}N!}e^{-\frac{(N+1)}{E}|x|^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{iN\theta}(x+\tfrac{v}{2})^{N}e^{-iN\theta}\overline{(x-\tfrac{v}{2})}^{N}e^{-\frac{N+1}{4E}|v|^{2}}e^{-i\frac{(N+1)}{E}\langle v,\xi\rangle}dv

\displaystyle=W_{\varphi_{N}^{\gamma_{0}},\varphi_{N}^{\gamma_{0}}}(x,\xi),

where the third line we switch variables
v\to g_{\theta}\cdot v
. The last statement of the lemma follows from an application of (
14
).
∎

We now apply the method of stationary phase for Bott-Morse

in Section

(19)

Proof.

We show (
42

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{C}\text{P}^{1}}\langle\mu(g^{-1})[\varphi_{N}^{\gamma}],f\rangle\varphi_{N}^{\gamma}d\nu(\gamma)

5.1.
Airy Function

The Airy function
\operatorname{Ai}(x)
on
\mathbb{R}
is
defined as the inverse Fourier transform of
\xi\mapsto e^{i\xi^{3}/3}
. This implies
\operatorname{Ai}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
, but if we view the integral as an
improper Riemann integral on
[-L,L]
with
L\to\infty
, we
see that
\operatorname{Ai}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
by an
integration by parts argument. So we define

\operatorname{Ai}(x)\coloneqq\lim_{L\to\infty}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-L}^{L}\cos(\xi^{3}/3+\xi x)d\xi.

This function satisfies the differential equation

\operatorname{Ai}^{\prime\prime}(x)-x\operatorname{Ai}(x)=0,

and it can be extended to an entire function on
\mathbb{C}
using this differential equation.

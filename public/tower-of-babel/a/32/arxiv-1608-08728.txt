1.
introduction

Since Calderón and Zygmund’s work, the singular integral theory has
been one of most important fields in Mathematics and it
has been developed considerably in various directions
(see e.g.
[
2
,
11
]
). In
particular, due to Hörmander the singular integral

\displaystyle Tf(x):=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}K(x,y)f(y)dy

(1.1)

becomes a bounded operator on
L_{p}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
if the kernel
K
satisfies the Hörmander condition
(see
[
11
, Theorem I.5.3]
)

\displaystyle\sup_{x,y\in\mathbf{R}^{d}}\int_{|x-z|>2|x-y|}|K(x,z)-K(y,z)|dz<\infty.

(1.2)

Hörmander’s condition is considered as one of most general conditions in the theory of the singular integral, and there is a huge number of applications to partial differential equations. For instance, consider the heat equation

\displaystyle u_{t}=\Delta u+f,\quad(t,x)\in(0,T]\times\mathbf{R}^{d},\quad u(0)=0.

(1.3)

As is well known, for the solution
u
we have

u_{x^{i}x^{j}}(t,x)=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}p_{x^{i}x^{j}}(s,t,x-y)f(s,y)dyds,

where
p(t,x)
is the heat kernel. One can prove that the kernel

K(s,t,x,y)=1_{s<t}p_{x^{i}x^{j}}(t-s,x-y)
is singular but
satisfies (
1.2
) on
\mathbf{R}^{d+1}
. Consequently this leads to

\displaystyle\|u_{xx}\|_{L_{p}((0,T)\times\mathbf{R}^{d})}\leq C\|f\|_{L_{p}((0,T)\times\mathbf{R}^{d})}.

Regarding the
L_{p}
-theory for stochastic PDEs, Krylov
[
6
,
7
]
firstly
introduced the maximal
L_{p}
-regularity of the stochastic heat equation

\displaystyle du=\Delta udt+gdW_{t},\,\,t>0,\quad u(0,\cdot)=0.

(1.4)

In particular, he proved the
L_{p}
-boundedness of

\displaystyle\nabla u(t,x)=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\nabla p(t-s,x-y)g(s,y)dydW_{s}.

(1.5)

The right hand side of (
1.5
) becomes a stochastic
singular integral in the sense that

\mathbb{E}\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}|\nabla p(s,t,x-y)|dydW_{s}\right|^{p}=\infty,\quad\forall\,t,p>0.

Lately,
L_{p}
-theory has been further developed for
high-order stochastic PDEs,
stochastic integro-differential equations and certain stochastic
pseudo-differential equations. For related works, we refer to

[
4
,
6
,
7
,
9
]
(Krylov’s analytic approach) and
[
12
,
13
]
(
H^{\infty}
-calculus).
Krylov’s approach requires differentiability of the kernel, and
H^{\infty}
-calculus approach works only if the corresponding operator is a generator of bounded analytic semigroup and does not depend on the time variable.

Our primary goal is to introduce a theory with which one can investigate the maximal regularity for very large classes of stochastic partial differential equations.
The stochastic singular integral of type (
0.1
) naturally appears if one tries to obtain the maximal
L_{p}
-regularity of solutions to stochastic partial differential equations. We prove that the stochastic Hörmander condition is sufficient for the
L_{p}
-boundedness of the stochastic integral and demonstrate that our result on stochastic singular integral (
0.1
) leads to the maximal
L_{p}
-regularity of large classes of stochastic partial differential equations.

Here is a brief comment on our approach.
We noticed that some key techniques in Krylov’s approach, e.g. integration by parts, are not applicable for general
kernels. Hence we combined Krylov’s idea with some tools used for the deterministic singular integral theory and Calderón-Zygmund theorem.

The article is organized as follows. The main theorem is given Section 2 and the related parabolic
Littlewood-Paley inequality is introduced and proved in Section 3.
In section 4, the main theorem is proved on the basis of the
parabolic Littlewood-Paley inequality. Finally, the maximal
L_{p}
-regularity result for
SPDEs is given in Section 5.

2.
main result

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)
be a probability space and

\{\mathcal{F}_{t},t\geq 0\}
be an increasing filtration of

\sigma
-fields on
\Omega
satisfying the usual condition, i.e.

\mathcal{F}_{t}\subset\mathcal{F}
contains all
(\mathcal{F},P)
-null sets and
\mathcal{F}_{t}=\bigcap_{s>t}\mathcal{F}_{s}
.
By
\mathcal{P}
we denote the predictable
\sigma
-algebra, that is,
\mathcal{P}
is the smallest
\sigma
-algebra containing
the collection of all sets
A\times(s,t]
, where
0\leq s\leq t<\infty
and
A\in\mathcal{F}_{s}
.
Let
W^{1}_{t},W^{2}_{t},\cdots
be an infinite sequence of independent one-dimensional Wiener
processes defined on
\Omega
, each of which is a Wiener
process relative to
\{\mathcal{F}_{t},t\geq 0\}
.
For
T\in(0,\infty]
and a domain
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathbf{R}^{d}
, we denote

\mathcal{O}_{T}:=(0,T)\times\mathcal{O}.

Define

\mathbb{L}_{p}(\mathcal{O}_{T})=L_{p}(\Omega\times(0,T),\mathcal{P};L_{p}(\mathcal{O})),\quad\mathbb{L}_{p}(\mathcal{O}_{T},l_{2})=L_{p}(\Omega\times(0,T),\mathcal{P};L_{p}(\mathcal{O};l_{2})),

and

\|g\|_{\mathbb{L}_{p}(\mathcal{O}_{T},l_{2})}=\left(\mathbb{E}\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\mathcal{O}}|g|^{p}_{l_{2}}dxdt\right)^{1/p}.

If
\mathcal{O}_{T}=(0,\infty)\times\mathbf{R}^{d}
, we simply put
\mathbb{L}_{p}(\mathcal{O}_{T})=\mathbb{L}_{p}
and
\mathbb{L}_{p}(\mathcal{O}_{T},l_{2})=\mathbb{L}_{p}(l_{2})
.

Denote
\mathbf{R}_{+}=(0,\infty)
and let
K(r,t,z,x)=K(\omega,r,t,z,x)
be a
\mathcal{P}\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mathbf{R}_{+})\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{O})\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{O})
-measurable function such that

K(r,t,z,x)=0
if
r\geq t
. For
g=(g^{1},g^{2},\ldots)\in\mathbb{L}_{p}(\mathcal{O},l_{2})
and
(t,x)\in\mathcal{O}_{T}
,
define

\displaystyle\mathbb{T}_{\varepsilon}g(t,x):=\int_{0}^{t-\varepsilon}\int_{\mathcal{O}}K(r,t,z,x)g^{k}(r,z)dzdW_{r}^{k}

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{T}g(t,x)

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{O}}K(r,t,z,x)g^{k}(r,z)dzdW_{r}^{k}

\displaystyle:=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\mathbb{T}_{\varepsilon}g(t,x),

where the sense of convergence will be specified in Assumption
2.3
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
D
be a subset of
\mathbf{R}^{d+1}
.
A function
\rho(X,Y)
defined on
D\times D

is called a quasi-metric iff the following four properties hold:

(i)
\rho(X,Y)\geq 0
for all
X,Y\in D

(ii)
\rho(X,Y)=0
iff
X=Y

(iii)
\rho(X,Y)=\rho(Y,X)
for all
X,Y\in D

(iv) There exists a constant
N_{\rho}\geq 1
such that
\rho(X,Y)\leq N_{\rho}\left(\rho(X,Z)+\rho(Z,Y)\right)

for all
X,Y,Z\in D
.

Define balls related to the quasi-metric
\rho
as

\displaystyle B_{c}(X):=\{Z\in D:\rho(X,Z)<c\},\quad X\in D,~c>0.

Note that the center
X
of the ball
B_{c}(X)
is always in
D
.

Throughout the article we assume that the quasi-metric
\rho
satisfies the doubling ball condition on
D
, that is,
for any
\gamma>0
there exists a constant
N_{\gamma}
so that

\displaystyle|B_{\gamma c}(X)|\leq N_{\gamma}|B_{c}(X)|\quad\forall c>0,~X\in D.

(2.1)

For a locally integrable function
f
on
D
, define its sharp function as

\displaystyle f^{\sharp}(t,x)

\displaystyle:=\sup-\hskip-10.81218pt\int_{B_{c}(Y)}|f(r,z)-f_{B_{c}(Y)}|~drdz

\displaystyle:=\sup\frac{1}{|B_{c}(Y)|}\int_{B_{c}(Y)}|f(r,z)-f_{B_{c}(Y)}|~drdz

\displaystyle\approx\sup\frac{1}{|B_{c}(Y)|^{2}}\int_{B_{c}(Y)}\int_{B_{c}(Y)}|f(Z)-f(Z^{\prime})|~dZdZ^{\prime}

where the sup is taken over all
B_{c}(Y)
containing
X=(t,x)
and

f_{B_{c}(Y)}=-\hskip-10.81218pt\int_{B_{c}(Y)}f(r,z)~drdz.

Similarly, the maximal function
\mathcal{M}f(t,x)
is defined as

\mathcal{M}f(t,x):=\sup-\hskip-10.81218pt\int_{B_{c}(Y)}|f(r,z)|~drdz,

where the sup is taken over all
B_{c}(Y)
containing
X=(t,x)
.

Below is a version of Hardy-Littlewood and Fefferman-Stein theorems.

Theorem 2.2
.

For any
p>1
,

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{M}f\|_{L_{p}(D)}\leq N(p)\|f\|_{L_{p}(D)},\quad\forall f\in L_{p}(D).

(2.2)

Furthermore, if
|D|=\infty
, then

\|f\|_{L_{p}(D)}\leq N_{p}\|f^{\sharp}\|_{L_{p}(D)}

(2.3)

For the proof of this theorem, see e.g.
[
1
, Theorem 2.2 and Theorem 2.4]
.
If (
2.3
) holds, we say
\rho
admits the Feffreman-Stein theorem (
FS
), which obviously holds if
|D|=\infty
.

Assumption 2.3

(
L_{2}
-boundedness)
.

For each

,

\mathbb{T}_{\varepsilon}g(t,x)
converges in

as

Moreover the operator

is bounded from

to

, i.e.,

such that for all

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{T}g\|_{\mathbb{L}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{T})}\leq N_{0}\|g\|_{\mathbb{L}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{T},l_{2})}.

(2.4)

(A stochastic Hörmander condition)

There exist positive constants

\displaystyle\sup_{\omega\in\Omega}\sup_{X,Y}\int_{0}^{T}\left[\int_{\rho(X,Z)\geq C_{0}\rho(X,Y)}|K(r,t,z,x)-K(r,s,z,y)|~dz\right]^{2}dr\leq N_{1},

By

3.
Parabolic Littlewood-Paley inequality

For
l_{2}
-valued measurable functions
f=(f^{1},f^{2},\cdots)
on
\mathcal{O}_{T}
, denote

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}f(t,x)

\displaystyle:=\left[\int_{0}^{t}\left|\int_{\mathcal{O}}K(r,t,z,x)f(r,z)dz\right|^{2}_{l_{2}}~dr\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle:=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\left[\int_{0}^{t-\varepsilon}\left|\int_{\mathcal{O}}K(r,t,z,x)f(r,z)dz\right|^{2}_{l_{2}}~dr\right]^{1/2}.

(3.1)

In this section we study the boundedness of operator
\mathcal{G}
in
L_{p}(\mathcal{O}_{T};l_{2})
. Since the integral above is deterministic one may assume that the kernel
K
is nonrandom throughout this section.

Theorem
3.1
below is the main result of this section which we call “Parabolic Littlewood-Paley inequality”. This inequality was first proved by Krylov for
K(r,t,x,y)=\nabla_{x}p(t-r,x-y)
, where
p(t,x)=\frac{1}{(4\pi t)^{d/2}}e^{-|x|^{2}/(4t)}
is the heat kernel. If
f
is independent of
t
then parabolic Littlewood-Paley inequality with
K=\nabla_{x}p(t-r,x-y)
leads to the classical (elliptic) Littlewood-Paley inequality (for instance, see
[
4
, Section 1]
).

Theorem 3.1

(Parabolic Littlewood-Paley inequality)
.

Let
p\geq 2
.
Suppose Assumptions
2.3
and
2.4
hold. Then for any
f\in L_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{T};l_{2})\cap L_{\infty}(\mathcal{O}_{T};l_{2})
,

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{G}f\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{O}_{T})}\leq N\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{O}_{T};l_{2})},

where
N
depends only on
d
,
p
,
C_{0}
,
N_{1}
, and
N_{2}
.

The proof of this theorem will be given at the end of this section.

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose Assumption
2.3
holds.
Then for each
f\in L_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{T};l_{2})
,
\mathcal{G}f(t,x)
is finite almost everywhere, and moreover
the operator
f\rightarrow\mathcal{G}f
is a bounded operator from
L_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{T};l_{2})
to
L_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{T})
.

Proof.

Obviously, since
f
is nonrandom,
f\in\mathbb{L}_{2}(l_{2})
. By Itô’s isometry and (
2.4
),

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{O}}|\mathcal{G}f(t,x)|^{2}dxdt

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{O}}|\mathbb{T}f(t,x)|^{2}dxdt

\displaystyle\leq N_{0}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{O}}|f(t,x)|_{l_{2}}^{2}dxdt

\displaystyle=N_{0}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{O}}|f(t,x)|_{l_{2}}^{2}dxdt.

Thus the lemma is proved.

\Box

Denote

\mathcal{K}f(r,t,x)=\int_{\mathcal{O}}K(r,t,z,x)f(r,z)dz

and

\displaystyle Gf(t,s,x,y)=\left[\int_{0}^{T}|\mathcal{K}f(r,t,x)-\mathcal{K}f(r,s,y)|_{l_{2}}^{2}~dr\right]^{1/2}.

Observe

\mathcal{G}f(t,x)=\left[\int_{0}^{t}\left|\mathcal{K}f(r,t,x)\right|^{2}_{l_{2}}~dr\right]^{1/2}

and

Gf(t,s,x,y)=\left[\int_{0}^{T}|1_{r<t}\mathcal{K}f(r,t,x)-1_{r<s}\mathcal{K}f(r,s,y)|_{l_{2}}^{2}~dr\right]^{1/2},

where the last equality is due to the assumption that
K(r,t,z,x)=0
if
t\leq r
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
(t_{1},x_{1})\in B_{c}(X_{0})

Then for any

\displaystyle-\hskip-10.81218pt\int_{B_{c}(X_{0})}-\hskip-10.81218pt\int_{B_{c}(X_{0})}|\mathcal{G}(f_{1}+f_{2})(t,x)-\mathcal{G}(f_{1}+f_{2})(s,y)|~dtdxdsdy

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{G}f_{1})(t_{1},x_{1})+-\hskip-10.81218pt\int_{B_{c}(X_{0})}-\hskip-10.81218pt\int_{B_{c}(X_{0})}Gf_{2}(t,s,x,y)~dtdxdsdy.

(3.2)

Proof.

Set

and let

.
By Lemma

Proof of Theorem
3.1

Since the case
p=2
is already proved in Lemma
3.2
, we
assume
p>2
. Let
f\in L_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{T};l_{2})\cap L_{\infty}(\mathcal{O}_{T};l_{2})
.
For
\lambda>0
, we put

f_{1,\lambda}(t,x)=f(t,x)1_{|f|>\delta\lambda}(t,x)\quad\text{and}\quad f_{2,\lambda}(t,x)=f(t,x)1_{|f|\leq\delta\lambda}(t,x),

where
\delta
is a positive constant which will be specified
later. Obviously,

f=f_{1,\lambda}+f_{2,\lambda}.

Assume

\lambda\leq[\mathcal{G}(f)]^{\sharp}(t,x).

Then by Lemma
3.6
,

\displaystyle\lambda\leq[\mathcal{G}(f)]^{\sharp}(t,x)

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{G}f_{1,\lambda})(t,x)+N\|f_{2,\lambda}\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathcal{O}_{T};l_{2})}

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{G}f_{1,\lambda})(t,x)+N\delta\lambda,

where
N
is independent of
\lambda
and
\delta
. Take
\delta>0
so that
N\delta<1/2
. Then the above inequality implies that

\lambda\leq 4\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{G}f_{1,\lambda})(t,x).

Thus

\displaystyle|\{(t,x)\in\mathcal{O}_{T}:\lambda\leq[\mathcal{G}(f)]^{\sharp}(t,x)\}|\leq|\{(t,x)\in\mathcal{O}_{T}:\lambda\leq 4\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{G}f_{1,\lambda})(t,x)\}|.

(3.6)

By (
2.2
),

\|\mathcal{G}f\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{O}_{T})}\leq N_{p}\|[\mathcal{G}f]^{\sharp}\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{O}_{T})}.

Observe that

\displaystyle\|[\mathcal{G}f]^{\sharp}\|^{p}_{L_{p}(\mathcal{O}_{T})}=p\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{p-1}|\{(t,x)\in\mathcal{O}_{T}:\lambda\leq[\mathcal{G}(f)]^{\sharp}(t,x)\}|~d\lambda.

Therefore by (
3.6
), Chebyshev’s inequality, (
2.2
), and Lemma
3.2
,

\displaystyle\|[\mathcal{G}f]^{\sharp}\|^{p}_{L_{p}(\mathcal{O}_{T})}

\displaystyle\leq p\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{p-1}|\{(t,x)\in\mathcal{O}_{T}:\lambda\leq 4\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{G}f_{1,\lambda})(t,x)\}|d\lambda

\displaystyle\leq N\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{p-3}\int_{\mathcal{O}_{T}}|\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{G}f_{1,\lambda})(t,x)|^{2}dtdxd\lambda

\displaystyle\leq N\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{p-3}\int_{\mathcal{O}_{T}}|\mathcal{G}f_{1,\lambda}(t,x)|^{2}dtdxd\lambda

\displaystyle\leq N\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{p-3}\int_{\mathcal{O}_{T}}|f_{1,\lambda}(t,x)|^{2}dtdxd\lambda

\displaystyle\leq N\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{p-3}\int_{\mathcal{O}_{T}\cap\{|f|>\delta\lambda\}}|f(t,x)|^{2}dtdxd\lambda

\displaystyle=N\int_{\mathcal{O}_{T}}\left(\int^{|f|/\delta}_{0}\lambda^{p-3}d\lambda\right)\,|f(t,x)|^{2}dtdx

\displaystyle\leq N\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{O}_{T};l_{2})}.

The last inequality is due to
p>2
. The theorem is proved.

5.
Application to SPDE: Maximal
L_{p}
-regularity

We study the maximal
L_{p}
-regularity of SPDEs of the type

\displaystyle du(t,x)=A(t)u(t,x)dt+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}g^{k}(t,x)dW^{k}_{t},\quad(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbf{R}^{d};\quad u(0)=0,

(5.1)

where
W^{k}_{t}
are independent one-dimensional Wiener process defined on
\Omega
.

5.1.
Time measureable pseudo-differential operator

Assume that
A(t)
is a pseudo differential operator with the symbol
\psi(t,\xi)
, that is,

A(t)f(x):=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left[\psi(t,\xi)\mathcal{F}(f)(\xi)\right](x).

We set

d_{0}:=\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor+1,

and assume there exists a constant
\nu>0
such that

\displaystyle\Re[-\psi(t,\xi)]\geq\nu|\xi|^{\gamma},

(5.2)

and

\displaystyle\int_{R\leq|\xi|<2R}\prod_{i=1}^{d_{0}}\left|D^{\alpha_{i}}_{\xi}\psi(t,\xi)\right|^{k_{i}}d\xi

\displaystyle\quad\leq\nu^{-1}R^{\left(d+k_{1}(\gamma-|\alpha_{1}|)+k_{2}(\gamma-|\alpha_{2}|)+\cdots+k_{d_{0}}(\gamma-|\alpha_{d_{0}}|)\right)}

(5.3)

for any
R>0
, multi-indexes
\alpha_{i}\in(\mathbb{Z}_{+})^{d}
and
k_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
(
i=1,2,\cdots,d_{0}
) such that

\sum_{i=1}^{d_{0}}|\alpha_{i}|+\sum_{i=1}^{d_{0}}k_{i}\leq d_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{d_{0}}1_{k_{i}>0}.

Remark 5.1
.

Here is a sufficient condition for (
5.3
):
\exists\,c>0
such that

\displaystyle|D^{\alpha}_{\xi}\psi(t,\xi)|\leq c|\xi|^{\gamma-|\alpha|},\quad\quad\forall\xi\in\mathbf{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\},\quad\forall\,|\alpha|\leq d_{0}.

(5.4)

Indeed, if this holds then, for
R\leq|\xi|<2R
,

\prod_{i=1}^{d_{0}}|D^{\alpha_{i}}_{\xi}\psi(t,\xi)|^{k_{i}}\leq N(c,d)R^{k_{1}(\gamma-|\alpha_{1}|)+k_{2}(\gamma-|\alpha_{2}|)+\cdots+k_{d_{0}}(\gamma-|\alpha_{d_{0}})|}

Thus by integrating on
\{\xi\in\mathbf{R}^{d}:R\leq|\xi|<2R\}
we certainly get (
5.3
).

Define

p(s,t,x):=1_{0<s<t}\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left[\exp\left(\int_{s}^{t}\psi(r,\xi)dr\right)\right](x).

and

(-\Delta)^{\gamma/4}p(s,t,x):=1_{0<s<t}\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left[|\xi|^{\gamma/2}\exp\left(\int_{s}^{t}\psi(r,\xi)dr\right)\right](x).

Then
for any
g\in\mathbb{L}_{2}(l_{2})
, the (weak) solution to (
5.1
) is given by

u(t,x)=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}p(s,t,x-y)g^{k}(s,y)dydW_{s}^{k}.

(5.5)

See e.g.
[
7
, Theorem 4.2]
for details. Actually in
[
7
]
the representation formula of the weak solution is derived only for
\psi(t,\xi)=-|\xi|^{2}
, but one can easily check the argument there works for the general case.

Due to (
5.2
) we may say
A(t)
is a linear operator of order
\gamma
. Applying the Ito’s formula to
|u(t,x)|^{2}
, taking the expectation, and then integrating over
\mathbf{R}^{d}
, we get for any
t>0
,

\mathbb{E}\|u(t)\|^{2}_{L_{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})}-2\Re\left[\mathbb{E}\int^{t}_{0}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}u\overline{Au}dxds\right]=\mathbb{E}\int^{t}_{0}\|g\|^{2}_{L_{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d};l_{2})}ds.

By Plancherel’s theorem and (
5.2
),

\displaystyle-\Re\left[\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}u\overline{Au}dxds\right]=-\Re\left[\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\overline{\psi(s,\xi)}|\mathcal{F}(u)|^{2}d\xi\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\Re[-\psi(s,\xi)]|\mathcal{F}(u)|^{2}d\xi\geq\nu\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}|\xi|^{\gamma}|\mathcal{F}(u)|^{2}d\xi=\nu\|(-\Delta)^{\gamma/4}u\|^{2}_{L_{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})}.

It follows that

\mathbb{E}\|u(t)\|^{2}_{L_{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})}+2\nu\mathbb{E}\int^{t}_{0}\|(-\Delta)^{\gamma/4}u\|^{2}_{L_{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})}ds\leq\mathbb{E}\int^{t}_{0}\|g\|^{2}_{L_{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d};l_{2})}ds,

and above calculations suggest that
(-\Delta)^{\gamma/4}u
is the maximal regularity of solutions if there is no smoothness condition on
g
.

The following theorem extends the above
L_{2}
-estimate to
L_{p}
-estimate.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
p\geq 2
and assume (
5.2
) and (
5.3
) hold. Then for any
g\in\mathbb{L}(l_{2})
and
u
defined as in (
5.5
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{\infty}\|(-\Delta)^{\gamma/4}u(t,\cdot)\|^{p}_{\mathbb{L}_{p}}dt\leq N(d,p,\gamma,\nu)\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{\infty}\|g(t,\cdot)\|^{p}_{L_{p}(l_{2})}dt.

(5.6)

Remark 5.3
.

A proof of (
5.6
) is given in
[
4
]
with a stronger condition than (

\displaystyle|D^{\alpha}_{\xi}\psi(t,\xi)|\leq\nu^{-1}|\xi|^{\gamma-|\alpha|},\quad\forall\,|\alpha|\leq d_{0}+1.

The proof of

[

highly depends on the integration by parts,

Lemma 5.6

For each

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{\infty}\|\mathbb{T}_{\psi}g(t,\cdot)\|_{L_{2}}^{2}dt\leq N_{0}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{\infty}\|g(t,\cdot)\|_{L_{2}(l_{2})}^{2}dt.

(5.8)

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon_{1}>\varepsilon_{2}>0
. Then by Fubini’s theorem, Itô’s isometry, and Plancherel’s theorem,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{\infty}\|\mathbb{T}_{\psi,\varepsilon_{1}}g(t,\cdot)-\mathbb{T}_{\psi,\varepsilon_{1}}g(t,\cdot)\|_{L_{2}}^{2}dt

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\left|\int_{t-\varepsilon_{1}}^{t-\varepsilon_{2}}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}(-\Delta)^{\gamma/4}p(r,t,x-y)g^{k}(r,y)dydW_{r}^{k}\right|^{2}dtdx

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\int_{t-\varepsilon_{1}}^{t-\varepsilon_{2}}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\left|\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}(-\Delta)^{\gamma/4}p(r,t,x-y)g^{k}(r,y)dy\right|_{l_{2}}^{2}dxdrdt

\displaystyle=N(d)\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\int_{t-\varepsilon_{1}}^{t-\varepsilon_{2}}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}|\xi|^{\gamma}\exp\left(2\int_{r}^{t}\Re[\psi(\rho,\xi)]d\rho\right)|\mathcal{F}(g)(r,\cdot)|^{2}_{l_{2}}d\xi drdt

is well-defined and using Fubini’s theorem, Itô’s isometry, and Plancherel’s theorem again, we get

.
In other words, we assume that
(-\Delta)^{\gamma/4}p(r,t,x)=0
unless
0<r<t
.
Moreover we may assume
t\geq s
without loss of generality.
Since the proof of the case
t=s
is simpler, we only prove the case
t>s
.

Fix a constant
\delta\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\wedge\frac{\gamma}{2}\right)
.
Denote

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(r,X,Y)

\displaystyle=\left[\int_{A(r,X,Y)}\left|(-\Delta)^{\gamma/4}p(r,t,x-z)-(-\Delta)^{\gamma/4}p(r,s,y-z)\right|~dz\right]^{2}.

Obviously
\mathcal{I}(r,X,Y)=0
if
r\geq t
. Thus

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{I}(r,X,Y)dr

\displaystyle=\int_{2s-t}^{t}\mathcal{I}(r,X,Y)dr+\int_{0}^{2s-t}\mathcal{I}(r,X,Y)dr

\displaystyle=:\mathcal{I}_{1}(X,Y)+\mathcal{I}_{2}(X,Y).

First we estimate
\mathcal{I}_{1}(X,Y)
.
By (
5.18
),

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{1}(X,Y)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{2s-t}^{t}\left[\int_{|z|\geq|t-s|^{1/\gamma}}\left|(-\Delta)^{\gamma/4}p(r,t,z)\right|~dz\right]^{2}dr

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{2s-t}^{s}\left[\int_{|z|\geq|t-s|^{1/\gamma}}\left|(-\Delta)^{\gamma/4}p(r,s,z)\right|~dz\right]^{2}dr\leq N.

We split
\mathcal{I}_{2}
.
Observe

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{2}\leq\mathcal{I}_{2,1}+\mathcal{I}_{2,2}

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{2s-t}\left[\int_{A(r,X,Y)}\left|(-\Delta)^{\gamma/4}p(r,t,x-z)-(-\Delta)^{\gamma/4}p(r,t,y-z)\right|~dz\right]^{2}dr

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{2s-t}\left[\int_{A(r,X,Y)}\left|(-\Delta)^{\gamma/4}p(r,t,y-z)-(-\Delta)^{\gamma/4}p(r,s,y-z)\right|~dz\right]^{2}dr.

If
|x-y|\leq(t-s)^{1/\gamma}
then by (
5.19
),

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{2,1}\leq N\left(|x-y|(t-s)^{-1/\gamma}\right)^{2}\leq N.

On the other hand, if
|x-y|>(t-s)^{1/\gamma}
, then

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{2,1}\leq 2\mathcal{I}_{2,1,1}+\mathcal{I}_{2,1,2},

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{2,1,1}:=\int_{s-|x-y|^{\gamma}}^{t}\left[\int_{|z|\geq|t-s|^{1/\gamma}+|x-y|}\left|(-\Delta)^{\gamma/4}p(r,t,z)\right|~dz\right]^{2}dr,

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{2,1,2}

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{s-|x-y|^{\gamma}}\left[\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\left|(-\Delta)^{\gamma/4}p(r,t,x-z)-(-\Delta)^{\gamma/4}p(r,t,y-z)\right|~dz\right]^{2}dr.

By (
5.18
) again,

\mathcal{I}_{2,1,1}\leq N\left(\left(t-s+|x-y|^{\gamma}\right)^{-1/\gamma}\left((t-s)^{1/\gamma}+|x-y|\right)\right)^{2\delta}\leq N

and by (
5.19
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{2,1,2}\left(|x-y|\left(t-s+|x-y|^{\gamma}\right)^{-1/\gamma}\right)^{2}\leq N.

It only remains to estimate
\mathcal{I}_{2,2}
. However, this is an easy consequence of (
5.20
)
since
2s-t<t
.
Indeed,

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{2,2}\leq N\left((t-s)|t-s|^{-1}\right)^{2}\leq N.

The corollary is proved.

\Box

Finally, applying Theorem
2.5
with

\mathbb{T}_{\varepsilon}=\mathbb{T}_{\psi,\varepsilon}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{T}_{\psi},

we obtain (
5.6
).

5.2.
Infinitesimal generators of subordinate Brownian motions

In this subsection we consider the infinitesimal generators of subordinate Brownian motions. In general the symbols of such operators do not satisfy (
5.2
) which is assumed in the previous subsection.

Let
S_{t}
be a subordinator, that is, an increasing Lévy process taking values in
[0,\infty)
with
S_{0}=0
. A subordinator
S
is completely characterized by its Laplace exponent
\phi
, i.e.

\mathbb{E}e^{-\lambda S_{t}}=e^{-t\phi(\lambda)}
for
\lambda>0
. Actually a function function
\phi:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)

with
\phi(0+)=0
is a Laplace exponent of a subordinator if and only if it is a Bernstein function (i.e.
(-1)^{n}D^{n}\phi\leq 0,\forall n
). Also it is of
the form

\phi(\lambda)=b\lambda+\int_{(0,\infty)}(1-e^{-\lambda t})\,\mu(dt)\,,\quad\lambda>0\,,

where
b\geq 0
and
\mu
is a measure on
(0,\infty)
satisfying
\int_{(0,\infty)}(1\wedge t)\,\mu(dt)<\infty
, called the Lévy measure. Let
B_{t}
be a
d
-dimensional Brownian motion independent of
S_{t}
. Then
\phi(\Delta)
can be defined as the infinitesimal generator of the subordinate Brownian motion
B_{S_{t}}
:

\phi(\Delta)f(x)=\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{\mathbb{E}f(x+B_{S_{t}})-f(x)}{t},\quad f\in C^{2}_{b}(\mathbf{R}^{d}),

and its integral version is

b\Delta f(x)+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\left(f(x+y)-f(x)-\nabla f(x)\cdot y{\mathbf{1}}_{\{|y|\leq 1\}}\right)\,J(y)\,dy\,,

(5.21)

where
J(x)=j(|x|)
with
j:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
given by

j(r)=\int_{0}^{\infty}(4\pi t)^{-d/2}e^{-r^{2}/(4t)}\,\mu(dt)\,.

See e.g.
[
10
]
for more details. In general for
n=0,1,2,\cdots
, we define

\phi(\Delta)^{n/2}
on the Schwartz space
\mathcal{S}
as the operator with symbol
-\phi(|\xi|^{2})
, i.e.

\phi(\Delta)^{n/2}f(x):=-\phi(-\Delta)^{n/2}f(x):=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left[-\phi(|\xi|^{2})^{n/2}\mathcal{F}f(\xi)\right](x).

Consider the operator
A(t)=\phi(\Delta)
. Then (
5.1
) has a solution
u
given by

u(t,x)=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}p(t-s,x-y)g^{k}(s,y)dydW_{s}^{k},

where

p(t,x)=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left[\exp\left(-t\phi(|\xi|^{2})\right)\right](x).

Let
\phi^{-1}
denote the generalized inverse of
\phi
, i.e.

\phi^{-1}(t):=\inf\{s>0:\phi(s)\geq t\}.

Assumption 5.14
.

(i) There exists a constant
N
such that for all
t\leq T\in(0,\infty]
and

x\in\mathbf{R}^{d}

\displaystyle\left|\phi(\Delta)^{1/2}p(t,\cdot)(x)\right|\leq N\left(t^{-1/2}(\phi^{-1}(t^{-1}))^{d/2}\wedge\frac{\phi(|x|^{-2})^{1/2}}{|x|^{d}}\right),

(5.22)

\displaystyle\left|\phi(\Delta)^{1/2}\nabla p(t,\cdot)(x)\right|\leq N\left(t^{-1/2}(\phi^{-1}(t^{-1}))^{(d+1)/2}\wedge\frac{\phi(|x|^{-2})^{1/2}}{|x|^{d+1}}\right),

(5.23)

and

\displaystyle\left|\phi(\Delta)^{3/2}p(t,\cdot)(x)\right|\leq N\left(t^{-3/2}(\phi^{-1}(t^{-1}))^{d/2}\wedge t^{-1}\frac{\phi(|x|^{-2})^{1/2}}{|x|^{d}}\right).

(5.24)

(ii)
\phi
satisfies the following scaling property: there exist
positive constants
N_{1}
,
N_{2}
,
\delta_{1}
, and
\delta_{2}
so that

\displaystyle N_{1}\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^{\delta_{1}}\leq\frac{\phi(b)}{\phi(a)}\leq N_{2}\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^{\delta_{2}},\quad\quad\forall\,\,0<a\leq b.

(5.25)

Using (
5.25
) one can find constants
\bar{\delta}_{1}
,
\bar{\delta}_{2}
,
\bar{N}_{1}
, and
\bar{N}_{2}

depending only on
\delta_{1}
,

\delta_{2}
,
N_{1}
, and
N_{2}

so that

\displaystyle\bar{N}_{1}\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^{\bar{\delta}_{1}}\leq\frac{\phi^{-1}(b)}{\phi^{-1}(a)}\leq\bar{N}_{2}\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^{\bar{\delta}_{2}},\quad\quad 0<a\leq b.

(5.26)

Furthermore,

\lim_{t\uparrow\infty}\phi(t)=\lim_{t\uparrow\infty}\phi^{-1}(t)=\infty,\quad\quad\lim_{t\downarrow 0}\phi(t)=\lim_{t\downarrow 0}\phi^{-1}(t)=0,

and

\displaystyle\phi(\phi^{-1}(t))=t.

(5.27)

Example 5.15
.

A sufficient condition to (
5.22
)-(
5.25
) can be founded e.g. in

[
3
]
:

(H1):
\exists
constants
0<\delta_{1}\leq\delta_{2}<1
and
c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that

c_{1}\lambda^{\delta_{1}}\phi(t)\leq\phi(\lambda t)\leq c_{2}\lambda^{\delta_{2}}\phi(t),\quad\lambda\geq 1,t\geq 1\,;

(H2):
\exists
constants
0<\delta_{3}\leq 1
and

c_{3}>0
such that

\phi(\lambda t)\leq c_{3}\lambda^{\delta_{3}}\phi(t),\quad\lambda\leq 1,t\leq 1\,.

Actually using (H1) and (H2) one can prove (see
[
3
]
)

\displaystyle\left|\phi(\Delta)^{n/2}D^{\beta}p(t,\cdot)(x)\right|\leq N\left(t^{-n/2}(\phi^{-1}(t^{-1}))^{(d+|\beta|)/2}\wedge t^{-(n-1)/2}\frac{\phi(|x|^{-2})^{1/2}}{|x|^{d+|\beta|}}\right)

for any
n\leq 3
and multi-index
\beta
with
|\beta|\leq 2
.

Here are some examples of Bernstein functions
satisfying (H1) and (H2):

(1)

\phi(\lambda)=\lambda^{\alpha}+\lambda^{\beta}
,
0<\alpha<\beta<1
;

(2)

\phi(\lambda)=(\lambda+\lambda^{\alpha})^{\beta}
,
\alpha,\beta\in(0,1)
;

(3)

\phi(\lambda)=\lambda^{\alpha}(\log(1+\lambda))^{\beta}
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
,

\beta\in(0,1-\alpha)
;

(4)

\phi(\lambda)=\lambda^{\alpha}(\log(1+\lambda))^{-\beta}
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
,

\beta\in(0,\alpha)
;

(5)

\phi(\lambda)=(\log(\cosh(\sqrt{\lambda})))^{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
;

(6)

\phi(\lambda)=(\log(\sinh(\sqrt{\lambda}))-\log\sqrt{\lambda})^{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
.

For example, the subordinate Brownian motion corresponding to the
example (1)
\phi(\lambda)=\lambda^{\alpha}+\lambda^{\beta}
is the
sum of two independent symmetric
\alpha
and
\beta
stable
processes, and its infinitesimal generator is

-(-\Delta)^{\beta/2}-(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}
.

Define

\displaystyle\mathbb{T}_{\phi,\varepsilon}g:=\int_{0}^{t-\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\phi(\Delta)^{1/2}p(t-r,x-y)g^{k}(r,y)dydW_{r}^{k}

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{T}_{\phi}g:=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\int_{0}^{t-\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\phi(\Delta)^{1/2}p(t-r,x-y)g^{k}(r,y)dydW_{r}^{k},

where the limit is in the sense of
\mathbb{L}_{2}
-norm.

Here is the main result of this subsection.

Theorem 5.16
.

Let
p\in[2,\infty)
and Assumption
5.14
hold. Then

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{T}_{\phi}g\|_{\mathbb{L}_{p}(\mathcal{O}_{T})}\leq N\|g\|_{\mathbb{L}_{p}(\mathcal{O}_{T},l_{2})}\quad\forall g\in\mathbb{L}_{p}(\mathcal{O}_{T},l_{2}),

where
N
depends only on
d
and the constants appearing in Assumption
5.14
.

To apply Theorem
2.5
, we set
\mathcal{O}=\mathbf{R}^{d}
and

\rho(X,Y)=\left(\phi^{-1}\left(|t-s|^{-1}\right)\right)^{-1/2}+|x-y|,

where
X=(t,x)
,
Y=(s,y)
, and
\left(\phi^{-1}\left(0^{-1}\right)\right)^{-1/2}:=0
.
Due to (
5.26
), one can easily check that
\rho
is a quasi-metric and satisfies the doubling ball condition.
Thus, we only need to check that

K(t-r,z,x):=1_{0<r<t<T}\phi(\Delta)^{1/2}p(t-r,x-z)

satisfies Assumption

2.4

since the proof of
L_{2}
-boundedness of
\mathcal{T}_{\phi}g
can be easily proved as the proof of Lemma
5.6
.

Lemma 5.17
.

There exists a constant
N
such that for all
0<a<s<t<T
,
c>0
,

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}\left[\int_{|z|\geq c}\left|\phi(\Delta)^{1/2}p(t-r,z)\right|~dz\right]^{2}dr\leq N(t-s)\phi(c^{-2}),

(5.28)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{a}\left[\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\left|\phi(\Delta)^{1/2}p(t-r,z+h)-\phi(\Delta)^{1/2}p(t-r,z)\right|~dz\right]^{2}dr

\displaystyle\leq N|h|^{2}\phi^{-1}\left((t-a)^{-1}\right),

(5.29)

and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{a}\left[\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\left|\phi(\Delta)^{1/2}p(t-r,z)-\phi(\Delta)^{1/2}p(s-r,z)\right|~dz\right]^{2}dr\leq N(t-s)^{2}(s-a)^{-2},

(5.30)

where
N
depends only on
d
and the constants appearing in Assumption
5.14
.

Proof.

First we prove (
5.28
).
This is an easy consequence of assumptions on
\phi
.
Indeed, by (
5.22
) and (
5.25
),

\displaystyle\int_{|z|\geq c}|\phi(\Delta)^{1/2}p(t-r,z)|~dz

\displaystyle\leq\int_{|z|\geq c}\frac{\phi(|z|^{-2})^{1/2}}{|z|^{d}}~dz

\displaystyle\leq\int_{|z|\geq 1}\frac{\phi(|cz|^{-2})^{1/2}}{|z|^{d}}~dz

\displaystyle\leq N\phi(c^{-2})^{1/2}.

Next we prove (
5.29
).
By the fundamental theorem of calculus,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\left|\phi(\Delta)^{1/2}p(t-r,z+h)-\phi(\Delta)^{1/2}p(t-r,z)\right|~dz

\displaystyle\leq|h|\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\big|\phi(\Delta)^{1/2}\nabla p(t-r,z)dz.

Denote

a_{t}:=\left(\phi^{-1}(t^{-1})\right)^{1/2}.

Then by (
5.23
), (
5.25
), and (
5.27
),

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\left|\phi(\Delta)^{1/2}\nabla p(t-r,z)\right|dz

\displaystyle=\int_{|z|<1/a_{t-r}}\left|\phi(\Delta)^{1/2}\nabla p(t-r,z)\right|dz+\int_{|z|\geq 1/a_{t-r}}\left|\phi(\Delta)^{1/2}\nabla p(t-r,z)\right|dz

\displaystyle\leq N\left((1/a_{t-r})^{d}(t-r)^{-1/2}(\phi^{-1}((t-r)^{-1}))^{(d+1)/2}+\int_{|z|\geq 1/a_{t-r}}\frac{\phi(|z|^{-2})^{1/2}}{|z|^{d+1}}dx\right)

\displaystyle\leq N\left((t-r)^{-1/2}(\phi^{-1}((t-r)^{-1}))^{1/2}\right).

Hence by (
5.26
),

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{a}\left[\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\big|\phi(\Delta)^{1/2}p(t-r,z+h)-\phi(\Delta)^{1/2}p(t-r,z)\big|~dz\right]^{2}dr

\displaystyle\leq N|h|^{2}\left(\int_{0}^{a}(t-r)^{-1}\phi^{-1}\left((t-r)^{-1}\right)dr\right)

\displaystyle\leq N|h|^{2}\left(\phi^{-1}\left((t-a)^{-1}\right)\right).

Finally we prove (
5.30
).
By the mean-value theorem, (
5.24
), and (
5.25
),

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\left|\phi(\Delta)^{1/2}p(t-r,z)-\phi(\Delta)^{1/2}p(s-r,z)\right|~dz

\displaystyle=(t-s)\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\left|\frac{d}{dr}\phi(\Delta)^{1/2}p(\theta t+(1-\theta)s-r,z)\right|~dz

\displaystyle=(t-s)\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\left|\phi(\Delta)^{3/2}p(\theta t+(1-\theta)s-r,z)\right|~dz

\displaystyle\leq N(t-s)(\theta t+(1-\theta)s-r)^{-3/2}

\displaystyle\quad+N(t-s)\int_{|z|\geq 1/a_{\theta t+(1-\theta)s-r}}(\theta t+(1-\theta)s-r)^{-1}\frac{\phi(|z|^{-2})^{1/2}}{|z|^{d}}dz

\displaystyle\leq N(t-s)(\theta t+(1-\theta)s-r)^{-3/2},

where
\theta\in[0,1]
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{a}\left[\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}|\phi(\Delta)^{1/2}p(t-r,z)-\phi(\Delta)^{1/2}p(s-r,z)|~dz\right]^{2}dr

\displaystyle\leq N(t-s)^{2}\int_{0}^{a}(\theta t+(1-\theta)s-r)^{-3}dr\leq(t-s)^{2}(s-a)^{-2}.

The lemma is proved.

\Box

In the following corollary, we finally prove that the kerenel

K(t-r,z,x):=1_{0<r<t<T}\phi(\Delta)^{1/2}p(t-r,x-z)

satisfies Assumption
2.4
.
Recall

\rho(X,Y)=\left(\phi^{-1}\left(|t-s|^{-1}\right)\right)^{-1/2}+|x-y|.

Due to (
5.26
), there exists a constant
N_{\phi}\geq 1
so that

\phi^{-1}\left(a\right)\leq N_{\phi}\phi^{-1}\left(2^{-1}a\right)\qquad\forall a>0.

For
r>0
,
X=(t,x),Y=(s,y)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbf{R}^{d}
set

\displaystyle A(r,X,Y):=\left\{z\in\mathbf{R}^{d}:\rho(X,Z)\geq 4N_{\phi}\rho(X,Y)\right\},

where
Z=(r,z)
.
For the notational convenience, we use

\phi(\Delta)^{1/2}p(t-r,x-z)

to denote

1_{0<r<t<T}\phi(\Delta)^{1/2}p(t-r,x-z),

that is, we assume

\phi(\Delta)^{1/2}p(t-r,x-z)=0

unless
0<r<t<T
.

Corollary 5.18
.

There exists a constant
N
so that for all
X=(t,x),Y=(s,y)\in(0,T)\times\mathbf{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\left[\int_{A(r,X,Y)}\left|\phi(\Delta)^{1/2}p(t-r,x-z)-\phi(\Delta)^{1/2}p(s-r,y-z)\right|~dz\right]^{2}dr\leq N,

where
N
depends only on
d
and the constants appearing in Assumption
5.14
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume
t\geq s
.
We only focus on proving the case
t>s
since the proof of the case
t=s
is simpler.
Denote

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(r,X,Y)=\left[\int_{A(r,X,Y)}\left|\phi(\Delta)^{1/2}p(t-r,x-z)-\phi(\Delta)^{1/2}p(s-r,y-z)\right|~dz\right]^{2}.

If
r\geq t
, then
\mathcal{I}(r,X,Y)=0
. Thus

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{I}(r,X,Y)dr

\displaystyle=\int_{2s-t}^{t}\mathcal{I}(r,X,Y)dr+\int_{0}^{2s-t}\mathcal{I}(r,X,Y)dr

\displaystyle=:\mathcal{I}_{1}(X,Y)+\mathcal{I}_{2}(X,Y).

First we estimate
\mathcal{I}_{1}(X,Y)
.
By (
5.28
),

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{1}(X,Y)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{2s-t}^{t}\left[\int_{|z|\geq\left(\phi^{-1}(|t-s|^{-1})\right)^{-1/2}}\left|\phi(\Delta)^{1/2}p(t-r,z)\right|~dz\right]^{2}dr

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{2s-t}^{s}\left[\int_{|z|\geq\left(\phi^{-1}(|t-s|^{-1})\right)^{-1/2}}\left|\phi(\Delta)^{1/2}p(s-r,z)\right|~dz\right]^{2}dr\leq N.

We split
\mathcal{I}_{2}
.
Observe

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{I}_{2,1}+\mathcal{I}_{2,2}

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{2s-t}\left[\int_{A(r,X,Y)}\left|\phi(\Delta)^{1/2}p(t-r,x-z)-\phi(\Delta)^{1/2}p(t-r,y-z)\right|~dz\right]^{2}dr

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{2s-t}\left[\int_{A(r,X,Y)}\left|\phi(\Delta)^{1/2}p(t-r,y-z)-\phi(\Delta)^{1/2}p(s-r,y-z)\right|~dz\right]^{2}dr.

If
|x-y|\leq\left(\phi^{-1}(|t-s|^{-1})\right)^{-1/2}
then by (
5.29
),

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{2,1}\leq N|x-y|^{2}\left(\phi^{-1}\left(|t-s|^{-1}\right)\right)\leq N.

On the other hand, if

\displaystyle|x-y|>\left(\phi^{-1}(|t-s|^{-1})\right)^{-1/2},

then

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{2,1}\leq 2\mathcal{I}_{2,1,1}+\mathcal{I}_{2,1,2},

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{2,1,1}:=\int_{s-\left(\phi\left(|x-y|^{-2}\right)\right)^{-1}}^{t}\left[\int_{|z|\geq\left(\phi^{-1}(|t-s|^{-1})\right)^{-1/2}+|x-y|}\left|\phi(\Delta)^{1/2}p(t-r,z)\right|~dz\right]^{2}dr

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{2,1,2}

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{s-\left(\phi\left(|x-y|^{-2}\right)\right)^{-1}}\left[\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\left|\phi(\Delta)^{1/2}p(t-r,x-z)-\phi(\Delta)^{1/2}p(t-r,y-z)\right|~dz\right]^{2}dr.

By (
5.28
),

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{2,1,1}

\displaystyle\leq N\left[\left(t-s+\left(\phi\left(|x-y|^{-2}\right)\right)^{-1}\right)\phi\left(\left(\left(\phi^{-1}(|t-s|^{-1})\right)^{-1/2}+|x-y|\right)^{-2}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq N

and by (
5.29
),

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{2,1,2}

\displaystyle\leq N|x-y|^{2}\phi^{-1}\left(\left(t-s+\left(\phi\left(|x-y|^{-2}\right)\right)^{-1}\right)^{-1}\right)\leq N.

It only remains to estimate
\mathcal{I}_{2,2}
. However, this is an easy consequence of (
5.30
).
Indeed,

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{2,2}\leq N\left((t-s)|t-s|^{-1}\right)^{2}\leq N.

The corollary is proved.

\Box

Consequently, to prove Theorem
5.16
it is enough to apply Theorem
2.5
with

\mathbb{T}_{\varepsilon}=\mathbb{T}_{\phi,\varepsilon}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{T}_{\phi}.

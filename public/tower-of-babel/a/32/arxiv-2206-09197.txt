C*-ALGEBRAIC CASAS-ALVERO CONJECTURE
K. MAHESH KRISHNA

Abstract




Abstract
: Based on Casas-Alvero conjecture
[J. Algebra, 2001]
we formulate the following conjecture.

C*-algebraic Casas-Alvero Conjecture : Let
\mathcal{A}
be a commutative C*-algebra,
n\in\mathbb{N}
and let
P(z)\coloneqq(z-a_{1})(z-a_{2})\cdots(z-a_{n})
be a polynomial over
\mathcal{A}
with
a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{n}\in\mathcal{A}
. If
P
shares a common zero with each of its (first)
n-1
derivatives, then it is
n^{\text{th}}
power of a linear monic C*-algebraic polynomial.

We show that C*-algebraic Casas-Alvero Conjecture holds for C*-algebraic polynomials of degree 2.

Keywords
: C*-algebra, Casas-Alvero Conjecture.

Mathematics Subject Classification (2020)
: 46L05, 30C10.

2.
C*-algebraic Casas-Alvero conjecture

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a C*-algebra. For
P(z)\coloneqq(z-a_{1})(z-a_{2})\cdots(z-a_{n})
for all
z\in\mathcal{A}
with
a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{n}\in\mathcal{A}
, we define

\displaystyle P^{\prime}(z)\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{n}(z-a_{1})\cdots\widehat{(z-a_{j})}\cdots(z-a_{n}),\quad\forall z\in\mathcal{A}

where the term with cap is missing.
We can merely formulate the C*-algebraic version of Casas-Alvero conjecture as follows.

Conjecture 2.1
.

(C*-algebraic Casas-Alvero Conjecture)
Let
\mathcal{A}
be a commutative C*-algebra,
n\in\mathbb{N}
and let
P(z)\coloneqq(z-a_{1})(z-a_{2})\cdots(z-a_{n})
be a polynomial over
\mathcal{A}
with
a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{n}\in\mathcal{A}
. If
P
shares a common zero with each of its (first)
n-1
derivatives, then it is
n^{\text{th}}
power of a linear monic C*-algebraic polynomial.

Theorem 2.2
.

Conjecture
2.1
holds for C*-algebraic polynomials of degree 2.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a commutative C*-algebra and let
P(z)\coloneqq(z-a)(z-b)
,
a,b\in\mathcal{A}
. Assume that there is an element
c\in\mathcal{A}
such that
P(c)=P^{\prime}(c)=0
. Then

\displaystyle(c-a)(c-b)=0=2c-a-b\implies c=\frac{a+b}{2}

\displaystyle\implies\left(\frac{a+b}{2}-a\right)\left(\frac{a+b}{2}-b\right)=0\implies(b-a)^{2}=0.

By Gelfand-Naimark theorem
a
and
b
can be regarded as a complex valued functions. Therefore
a-b=0
which gives
a=b
.
∎

Abstract




Abstract
Let
\Omega\in L^{1}{({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}
, be a function of homogeneous of degree zero, and
M_{\Omega}
be the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator associated with
\Omega
defined by
M_{\Omega}(f)(x)=\sup_{r>0}\frac{1}{r^{n}}\int_{|x-y|<r}|\Omega(x-y)f(y)|dy.
It was shown by Christ and Rubio de Francia that
\|M_{\Omega}(f)\|_{L^{1,\infty}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}\leq C(\|\Omega\|_{L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}+1)\|f\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}
provided
\Omega\in L\log L{({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}
.
In this paper, we show that, if
\Omega\in L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})
, then for all
f\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
,
M_{\Omega}
enjoys the limiting weak-type behaviors that

\lim_{\lambda\to 0^{+}}\lambda|\{x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}:M_{\Omega}(f)(x)>\lambda\}|=n^{-1}\|\Omega\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}\|f\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}.

This removes the smoothness restrictions on the kernel
\Omega
, such as Dini-type conditions, in previous results. To prove our result, we present a new upper bound of
\|M_{\Omega}\|_{L^{1}\to L^{1,\infty}}
, which essentially improves the upper bound
C(\|\Omega\|_{L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}+1)
given by Christ and Rubio de Francia. As a consequence, the upper and lower bounds of
\|M_{\Omega}\|_{L^{1}\to L^{1,\infty}}
are obtained for
\Omega\in L\log L{({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}
.

1
Introduction

As one of the fundamental operators in modern analysis, the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator

M(f)(x)=\sup_{r>0}\frac{1}{r^{n}}\int_{|x-y|<r}|f(y)|dy,

has played very important roles in several fields, such as Harmonic analysis, ergodic theory and index theory.
It was well known that the almost everywhere convergence of some important operators is usually closely related to whether the associated maximal operators satisfy weak type inequalities or not. The Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator and its purpose in differentiation were first introduced by Hardy and Littlewood
[
11
]
on
\mathbb{R}
, and later extended and developed by Wiener
[
15
]
on
{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
. It was shown that
M
is of weak
(1,1)
type and
L^{p}
bounded for
p>1
.

In 1956, Calderón and Zygmund
[
1
]
considered the following rough Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator

M_{\Omega}(f)(x)=\sup_{r>0}\frac{1}{r^{n}}\int_{|x-y|<r}|\Omega(x-y)f(y)|dy,

where
\Omega
is a function of homogeneous of degree zero and
\Omega\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
. It was shown in
[
1
]
that
M_{\Omega}
is bounded on
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
for all
p>1
by using the method of rotation. The weak
(1,1)
boundedness of
M_{\Omega}
was given by Christ
[
2
]
when
\Omega\in L^{q}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})
for
q>1
. Later on, Christ and Rubio de Francia
[
3
]
extended the kernel condition
\Omega\in L^{q}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})
to a more larger space
L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})
(since
L^{q}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})\subsetneq L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})\subsetneq L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})
).
Their result can be stated as follows:

Theorem A

(
[
3
]
)
.

For all
n\geq 2
and
\Omega\in L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})
,
M_{\Omega}
is of weak type
(1,1)
, and enjoys

\|M_{\Omega}(f)\|_{L^{1,\infty}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}\leq C(\|\Omega\|_{L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}+1)\|f\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}.

(1.1)

Remark 1.1
.

It is worthy pointing that, similar result like (
1.1
) also holds for the singular integral
T_{\Omega}
with rough homogeneous kernels
[
1
]
, and the constant on the right side is also
C(\|\Omega\|_{L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}+1)
.

Now let’s turn to the best constants problem for the weak norm inequalities of some important operators in Harmonic analysis. This belongs to less fine problems and has attracted lots of attentions. For example, in one deminsion
\mathbb{R}
, Davis
[
4
]
obtained the best constant of weak-type
(1,1)
for Hilbert transform; Melas
[
13
]
proved that
\|M\|_{L^{1}\to L^{1,\infty}}=\frac{11+\sqrt{61}}{11}
for the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator
M
; Grafakos and Montgomery-Smith
[
9
]
showed that the
(p,p)
norm of the uncentered Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator is the unique positive solution of the equation
(p-1)x^{p}-px^{p-1}=1
; Grafakos and Kinnunen
[
8
]
pointed out that the upper bound of the weak
(1,1)
norm is
2
for uncentered Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator, and the results in
[
8
]
can be extended to more general measure space. However, when the dimension
n
is bigger than
1
, things become more subtle. Even for the well-known Riesz transform, there is no such information.

To explore the lower bounds of
\|M\|_{L^{1}\to L^{1,\infty}}
, in 2006, Janakiraman
[
12
]
considered the following limiting weak-type behavior for the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator
M
:

\lim_{\lambda\to 0^{+}}\lambda|\{x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}:M(f)(x)>\lambda\}|=\|f\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})},

(1.2)

which yields the lower bound in the way that

\|M\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})\to L^{1,\infty}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}\geq 1.

(1.3)

Although (
1.3
) can be concluded from a geometric point of view by simply taking
f=\chi_{B(0,1)}
, the limiting weak-type behavior still offers us a new viewpoint in obtaining the lower bounds of the best constants.

In 2017, Ding and Lai
[
6
]
extended Janakiraman’s results to
M_{\Omega}
with the kernel
\Omega
satisfies the
L^{1}
-Dini condition, see also
[
10
]
for more general limiting weak-type behaviors. Recall that,
\Omega\in L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})
is said to satisfy the the
L^{1}
-Dini condition if

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\omega(\delta)}{\delta}d\delta<\infty,

where
\omega(\delta):=\sup_{\|\rho\|\leq\delta}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\Omega(\rho\theta)-\Omega(\theta)|d\sigma(\theta)
for
\delta>0
, here
\rho
denotes a rotation on
{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
and
\|\rho\|:=\sup\{|\rho\theta-\theta|:\,\theta\in{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\}
.

Theorem B

(
[
6
]
)
.

Let
\Omega
satisfy the
L^{1}
-Dini condition and
f\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
. Then

\lim_{\lambda\to 0^{+}}\lambda|\{x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}:M_{\Omega}(f)(x)>\lambda\}|=n^{-1}\|\Omega\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}\|f\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}.

As we mentioned before, in Theorem A,
\Omega\in L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})
is a sufficient condition to guarantee the weak-type
(1,1)
boundedness of
M_{\Omega}
. Note that

L^{1}-\text{Dini}\subsetneq L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})\subsetneq L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}),

and the result of Ding and Lai
[
6
]
was obtained under
L^{1}
-Dini condition.
It is natural to ask the following question:

Question 1
.

Does
M_{\Omega}
still enjoy the limiting weak-type behavior when
\Omega\in L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})
?

One of the main purpose in this paper is to address the question above. Our first result can be formulated as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega\in L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})
. Then for all
f\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
, it holds that

(i)

\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\to 0^{+}}\lambda|\{x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}:M_{\Omega}(f)(x)>\lambda\}|=n^{-1}\|\Omega\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}\|f\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})};

(ii)

\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\to 0^{+}}\lambda\big|\big\{x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}:\big|M_{\Omega}(f)(x)-\|f\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}|\Omega(x)||x|^{-n}\big|>\lambda\big\}\big|=0.

Remark 1.2
.

Note that, in Theorem
1.1
, we removed the smoothness condition and only assumed the kernel
\Omega\in L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})
. Therefore, Theorem
1.1
essentially improved the result in Theorem B.

Furthermore, in order to show Theorem
1.1
, it requires that the upper bound of
\|M_{\Omega}\|_{L^{1}\to L^{1,\infty}}
tends to
0
as
\|\Omega\|_{L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}\to 0
. However, it is clear that the upper bound in (
1.1
) of Theorem A does not satisfies this requirement. This naturally leads to the following question.

Question 2
.

Is it possible to give an improvement of the upper bound
\|\Omega\|_{L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}+1
in the inequality (
1.1
) ?

This question will be affirmed by our next theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose
\Omega\in L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})
. Then
M_{\Omega}
is of weak type
(1,1)
, and enjoys

\|M_{\Omega}\|_{L^{1,\infty}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}\leq C{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}}\|f\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})},

where

{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}}=\|\Omega\|_{L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}+\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\Omega(\theta)|\Big(1+\log^{+}\frac{|\Omega(\theta)|}{\|\Omega\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}}\Big)d\sigma(\theta).

Remark 1.3
.

Note that
{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}}\leq 3(\|\Omega\|_{L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}+1)
. Hence, Theorem
1.2
is an essential improvement of Theorem A. Moreover,
{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}}
has a nice property that it tends to
0
as
\|\Omega\|_{L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}
tends to
0
. This improvement plays an important role in proving the limiting weak-type behaviors of
M_{\Omega}
in Theorem
1.1
, and it has its own interest in bounding the range of
\|M_{\Omega}\|_{L^{1}\to L^{1,\infty}}
.

As a consequence of Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
, we obtain the upper and lower bound of the weak norm of
M_{\Omega}
.

Corollary 1.1
.

Let
\Omega\in L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})
. Then it holds that

n^{-1}\|\Omega\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}\leq\|M_{\Omega}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})\to L^{1,\infty}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}\leq C{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}}.

Remark 1.4
.

Very recently, the corresponding results for singular integrals with rough kernels
\Omega\in L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})
were established in
[
14
]
.

The organization of this paper is as follows. Section
2
will be devoted to proving Theorem
1.2
. In Section
3
, the proof of Theorem
1.1
will be demonstrated.

Throughout this paper, the letter C will stand for positive constants not necessarily the same one
at each occurrence but independent of the essential variables

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

We need to recall some notations first. Let
{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
be the unit sphere on the Euclidean space
{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
,
d\sigma(\cdot)
be the induced Lebesgue measure on
{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
, and
L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})
be the function space on
{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
contains all
\Omega
satisfying

\|\Omega\|_{L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\Omega(\theta)|\log(\text{e}+|\Omega(\theta)|)d\sigma(\theta)<\infty.

Set
\phi
be a smooth, radial, nonnegative function in
{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
which supported in
\{x:1/2\leq|x|\leq 4\}
, satisfies
\phi\leq 1
and

\phi(x)\equiv 1\quad\text{for }1\leq|x|\leq 2.

In order to prove Theorem

, we need the following lemma which is attributed to Christ and Rubio de Francia
[

Lemma 2.1

(
[

]
)
.

Define

L^{x}(\Omega)(\theta)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(x-t\theta)\phi(t\theta)\Omega(x-t\theta)dt,

and

T(\Omega)(\theta)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}L^{x}(\Omega)(\theta)\widetilde{B}_{-s}(x)dx,

where
\widetilde{B}_{-s}=\sum\limits_{\widetilde{Q}}b_{\widetilde{Q}}
satisfies

(i)

There exists
x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
, for all
\widetilde{Q}
,
x_{0}+\widetilde{Q}
is a dyadic cube with length
2^{-s}
;

(ii)

\text{supp }b_{\widetilde{Q}}\subset\widetilde{Q}
,
\int b_{\widetilde{Q}}=0
and
\|b_{\widetilde{Q}}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}\leq 2^{n+1}\lambda|\widetilde{Q}|

Then there exists
\gamma>0
, for all
\Omega\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})
, all
s>-3
and all
\widetilde{B}_{-s}
,

\|T(\Omega)\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}\leq C2^{-\gamma s}\lambda\|\Omega\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

The main idea of this proof is from Christ and Rubio de Francia
[
3
]
, Ding and Lai
[
7
]
. Without losing generality, we assume
\Omega
and

are both nonnegative and
{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}}>0
. For readability, we split the proof into four steps.

Step 1: Control of
M_{\Omega}(f)
.

Since
\phi
is smooth, then there exists
\alpha\in(0,1)
such that

\phi(x)>\frac{1}{2}\quad\text{for }2^{-\alpha}\leq|x|\leq 2.

Define

\phi_{j}(x)=\frac{1}{2^{jn}}\phi(2^{-j}x).

thus

\phi_{j}(x)>\frac{1}{2^{jn+1}}\quad\text{for }2^{j-\alpha}\leq|x|\leq 2^{j+1}.

Let
\Omega_{j}=\phi_{j}\Omega
. For any fixed
x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
, by the definition of
M_{\Omega}(f)(x)
, there exists
r_{x}\in(0,\infty)
such that

M_{\Omega}(f)(x)\leq\frac{2^{\alpha n/2}}{r_{x}^{n}}\int_{B(x,r_{x})}\Omega(x-y)f(y)dy.

Let
j_{x}\in\mathbb{Z}
, satisfy
2^{j_{x}}\leq r_{x}<2^{j_{x}+1}
, then

\displaystyle M_{\Omega}(f)(x)\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2^{\alpha n/2}}{2^{j_{x}n}}\int_{B(x,2^{j_{x}+1})}\Omega(x-y)f(y)dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2^{1+\alpha n/2}}{2^{j_{x}n+1}}\int_{B(x,2^{j_{x}+1})\backslash B(x,2^{j_{x}-\alpha})}\Omega(x-y)f(y)dy

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2^{\alpha n/2}2^{(j_{x}-\alpha)n}}\int_{B(x,2^{j_{x}-\alpha})}\Omega(x-y)f(y)dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{1+\alpha n/2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\phi_{j_{x}}(x-y)\Omega(x-y)f(y)dy+\frac{1}{2^{\alpha n/2}}M_{\Omega}(f)(x).

Therefore, for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
,

M_{\Omega}(f)(x)\leq C\Omega_{j_{x}}\ast f(x)\leq C\sup_{j}\Omega_{j}\ast f(x).

(2.1)

Step 2: C-Z decomposition.

For
\lambda>0
, applying C-Z decomposition at level
\frac{n\lambda}{2^{3n+1}{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}}}
, we have the following conclusions:

(cz-f)

f=g+b=g+\sum\limits_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}b_{Q}
;

(cz-g)

\|g\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}\leq\frac{n\lambda}{2^{2n+1}{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}}}
;

(cz-Q)

\mathcal{Q}
is a countable set of disjoint dyadic cubes. Let
E=\bigcup_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}Q
, then
|E|\leq\frac{2^{3n+1}{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}}}{n\lambda}\|f\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}
;

(cz-b)

For each

, we have
\text{supp }b_{Q}\subset Q
,
\int b_{Q}=0
and
\|b_{Q}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}\leq\frac{n\lambda}{2^{2n}{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}}}|Q|
. Thus by (cz-Q), we have
\|b\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}\leq 2^{2n+1}\|f\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}
.

Therefore it follows from (

) that

\displaystyle|\{x:M_{\Omega}(f)(x)>\lambda\}|\leq C|\{x:\sup_{j}\Omega_{j}\ast f(x)>\lambda\}|

(2.2)

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\big|\big\{x:\sup_{j}|\Omega_{j}\ast g(x)|>\frac{\lambda}{2}\big\}\big|+\big|\big\{x:\sup_{j}|\Omega_{j}\ast b(x)|>\frac{\lambda}{2}\big\}\big|\Big).

As a matter of fact, for all

\displaystyle|\Omega_{j}\ast g(x)|\leq

Let
B_{s}=\sum\limits_{Q\in\mathcal{Q},|Q|=2^{sn}}b_{Q}

\sup_{j}|\Omega_{j}\ast b(x)|=\sup_{j}|\sum_{s}\Omega_{j}\ast B_{j-s}(x)|\leq\sup_{j}\sum_{s}|\Omega_{j}\ast B_{j-s}(x)|\leq\sum_{s}\sup_{j}|\Omega_{j}\ast B_{j-s}(x)|.

and

\Omega_{j}\ast B_{j-s}=0
. Therefore

\displaystyle|\{x:M_{\Omega}(f)(x)>\lambda\}|\leq

\displaystyle C\frac{{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}}}{\lambda}\|f\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}

\displaystyle+C\big|\big\{x\in(E^{\ast})^{c}:\sum_{s>-3}\sup_{j}|\Omega_{j}\ast B_{j-s}|(x)>\frac{\lambda}{2}\big\}\big|.

Step 3: Split of
\Omega_{j}

Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{s}

\displaystyle=\{\theta\in{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}:\Omega(\theta)\geq 2^{\gamma s/3}\|\Omega\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}\};

\displaystyle E_{s}

Combining (

2.7
) with (

|\{x:M_{\Omega}(f)(x)>\lambda\}|\leq C\frac{{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}}}{\lambda}\|f\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}.

This is the desired conclusion. It remains to prove (

Step 4: Proof of (

Once we show that

\|K_{j}^{s}\ast B_{j-s}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}^{2}\leq C2^{-\gamma s/3}\lambda\|\Omega\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}\|B_{j-s}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})},

(2.8)

it follows immediately that

\displaystyle\big\|\sup_{j}|K_{j}^{s}\ast B_{j-s}|\big\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\Big\|\Big(\sum_{j}|K_{j}^{s}\ast B_{j-s}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}\|K_{j}^{s}\ast B_{j-s}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C2^{-\gamma s/3}\lambda\|\Omega\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}\sum_{j}\|B_{j-s}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}

\displaystyle=C2^{-\gamma s/3}\lambda\|\Omega\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}\|b\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})},

where the last equality is from (cz-b).

To see (
2.8
), we rewrite
\|K_{j}^{s}\ast B_{j-s}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}^{2}

\displaystyle\|K_{j}^{s}\ast B_{j-s}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}K_{j}^{s}\ast B_{j-s}(x)\cdot K_{j}^{s}\ast B_{j-s}(x)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{F}^{-1}(K_{j}^{s})(x)\cdot\mathcal{F}^{-1}(B_{j-s})(x)\cdot\mathcal{F}\big(K_{j}^{s}\ast B_{j-s}\big)(x)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{F}(\widetilde{K}_{j}^{s})(x)\cdot\mathcal{F}^{-1}(B_{j-s})(x)\cdot\mathcal{F}\big(K_{j}^{s}\ast B_{j-s}\big)(x)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{F}\big(\widetilde{K}_{j}^{s}\ast K_{j}^{s}\ast B_{j-s}\big)(x)\cdot\mathcal{F}^{-1}(B_{j-s})(x)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\widetilde{K}_{j}^{s}\ast K_{j}^{s}\ast B_{j-s}(x)\cdot B_{j-s}(x)dx,

where
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{F}^{-1}
are the Fourier transform and the inverse Fourier transform, and
\widetilde{K}_{j}^{s}(x)=K_{j}^{s}(-x)
. Then we only need to show that

\|\widetilde{K}_{j}^{s}\ast K_{j}^{s}\ast B_{j-s}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}\leq C2^{-\gamma s/3}\lambda\|\Omega\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}.

Furthermore, the problem can be reduced to prove that

|\widetilde{K}_{0}^{s}\ast K_{0}^{s}\ast\widetilde{B}_{-s}(0)|\leq C2^{-\gamma s/3}\lambda{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}}\|\Omega\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})},

(2.9)

\widetilde{B}_{-s}

2.1
. Suppose (
2.9

B_{j-s}^{j}(z)=B_{j-s}(2^{j}z),\qquad B_{j-s}^{j,x}(z)=B_{j-s}^{j}(z+2^{-j}x).

It is not hard to verify that
\frac{2^{2n}{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}}}{n}B_{j-s}^{j,x}
is one of
\widetilde{B}_{-s}
. Therefore

\displaystyle|\widetilde{K}_{j}^{s}\ast K_{j}^{s}\ast B_{j-s}(x)|

\displaystyle=\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{2^{2jn}}\widetilde{K}_{0}^{s}(2^{-j}(x-y-z))K_{0}^{s}(2^{-j}y)B_{j-s}(z)dydz\Big|

\displaystyle=\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\widetilde{K}_{0}^{s}(2^{-j}x-y-z)K_{0}^{s}(y)B_{j-s}^{j}(z)dydz\Big|

\displaystyle=\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\widetilde{K}_{0}^{s}(0-y-z)K_{0}^{s}(y)B_{j-s}^{j,x}(z)dydz\Big|

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{2^{2n}{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}}}\Big|\widetilde{K}_{0}^{s}\ast K_{0}^{s}\ast\frac{2^{2n}{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}}}{n}B_{j-s}^{j,x}(0)\Big|\leq C2^{-\varepsilon s/3}\lambda\|\Omega\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}.

Back to (
2.9

\Omega\chi_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\backslash E_{s}}
by an even function
\Omega_{s}^{\prime}
and an odd function
\Omega_{s}^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle\Omega(x)\chi_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\backslash E_{s}}(x)=

\displaystyle\frac{\Omega(x)\chi_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\backslash E_{s}}(x)+\Omega(-x)\chi_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\backslash E_{s}}(-x)}{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{\Omega(x)\chi_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\backslash E_{s}}(x)-\Omega(-x)\chi_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\backslash E_{s}}(-x)}{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle:\Omega_{s}^{\prime}(x)+\Omega_{s}^{\prime\prime}(x).

Obviously
\Omega_{s}^{\prime}

\Omega_{s}^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle\|\Omega_{s}^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\Omega\chi_{{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\backslash\mathcal{D}_{s}}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}\leq 2^{\gamma s/3}\|\Omega\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})};

(2.10)

\displaystyle\|\Omega_{s}^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\Omega\chi_{{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\backslash\mathcal{D}_{s}}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}\leq 2^{\gamma s/3}\|\Omega\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}.

Thus

\displaystyle\widetilde{K}_{0}^{s}\ast K_{0}^{s}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\phi(y-x)\Omega(y-x)\chi_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\backslash E_{s}}(y-x)\phi(y)\Omega(y)\chi_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\backslash E_{s}}(y)dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\phi(x-y)\phi(y)\big(\Omega_{s}^{\prime}(x-y)-\Omega_{s}^{\prime\prime}(x-y)\big)\big(\Omega_{s}^{\prime}(y)+\Omega_{s}^{\prime\prime}(y)\big)dy

\displaystyle=(\phi\Omega_{s}^{\prime})\ast(\phi\Omega_{s}^{\prime})(x)-(\phi\Omega_{s}^{\prime\prime})\ast(\phi\Omega_{s}^{\prime\prime})(x).

Reform

\displaystyle(\phi\Omega_{s}^{\prime})\ast(\phi\Omega_{s}^{\prime})(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\phi(x-y)\phi(y)\Omega_{s}^{\prime}(x-y)\Omega_{s}^{\prime}(y)dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(x-t\theta)\phi(t\theta)\Omega_{s}^{\prime}(x-t\theta)t^{n-1}dt\Omega_{s}^{\prime}(\theta)d\sigma(\theta)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}L^{x}(\Omega_{s}^{\prime})(\theta)\Omega_{s}^{\prime}(\theta)d\sigma(\theta),

where the second equality follows from

is homogeneous of degree
0
, and

\displaystyle(\phi\Omega_{s}^{\prime})\ast(\phi\Omega_{s}^{\prime})\ast\widetilde{B}_{-s}(0)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}L^{x}(\Omega_{s}^{\prime})(\theta)\widetilde{B}_{-s}(-x)dx\Omega_{s}^{\prime}(\theta)d\sigma(\theta)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}T(\Omega_{s}^{\prime})(\theta)\Omega_{s}^{\prime}(\theta)d\sigma(\theta)

and

(\phi\Omega_{s}^{\prime\prime})\ast(\phi\Omega_{s}^{\prime\prime})\ast\widetilde{B}_{-s}(0)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}T(\Omega_{s}^{\prime\prime})(\theta)\Omega_{s}^{\prime\prime}(\theta)d\sigma(\theta),

here

2.1
. It follows from (

\displaystyle|\widetilde{K}_{0}^{s}\ast K_{0}^{s}\ast\widetilde{B}_{-s}(0)|

\displaystyle\leq\Big|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}T(\Omega_{s}^{\prime})(\theta)\Omega_{s}^{\prime}(\theta)d\sigma(\theta)\Big|+\Big|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}T(\Omega_{s}^{\prime\prime})(\theta)\Omega_{s}^{\prime\prime}(\theta)d\sigma(\theta)\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\|\Omega_{s}^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}\|T(\Omega_{s}^{\prime})\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}+\|\Omega_{s}^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}\|T(\Omega_{s}^{\prime\prime})\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}

So we only need to show that

\|T(\Omega^{\prime})\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}\leq C2^{-2\gamma s/3}\lambda{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}}\quad\text{and}\quad\|T(\Omega^{\prime\prime})\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}\leq C2^{-2\gamma s/3}\lambda{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}},

which is an immediate consequence of Lemma

\|T(\Omega^{\prime})\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}\leq C2^{-\gamma s}\lambda\|\Omega^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}\leq C2^{-2\gamma s/3}\lambda\|\Omega\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}\leq C2^{-2\gamma s/3}\lambda{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}};

\|T(\Omega^{\prime\prime})\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}\leq C2^{-\gamma s}\lambda\|\Omega^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}\leq C2^{-2\gamma s/3}\lambda\|\Omega\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}\leq C2^{-2\gamma s/3}\lambda{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}}.

The proof of Theorem
1.2
is finished.

Lemma 3.1

(
[

]
)
.

Let
\lambda>0
,
\Phi\in L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})
,

be a measurable set. Then we have

\bigg|\bigg\{x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}:\frac{|\Phi(x\big/|x|)|}{|x|^{n}}>\lambda,\frac{x}{|x|}\in S\bigg\}\bigg|=\frac{\|\Phi\|_{L^{1}(S)}}{n\lambda}.

Now we state some basic properties of

Lemma 3.2

(
[
14
]
)
.

If
\Phi_{1}(\theta),\Phi_{2}(\theta)\in L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})
, then the following properties hold:

(i)

\Phi_{1}(\theta),\Phi_{2}(\theta)\in L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})
, and for

\|\Phi_{i}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}\leq\|\Phi_{i}\|_{L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})};

\|\Phi_{1}+\Phi_{2}\|_{L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}\leq 4(\|\Phi_{1}\|_{L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}+\|\Phi_{2}\|_{L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}).

Proof of Theorem
1.1
(i).

Without loss of generality, we may still assume
\Omega
and
f
are nonnegative, and
\|f\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}=1
.

For any
0<\varepsilon\ll\min\{1,\|\Omega\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}\}
, it is easy to see that there exists
r_{\varepsilon}>1
, such that

\int_{B(0,r_{\varepsilon})}f(x)dx>1-\varepsilon.

Now for
\lambda,\beta>0
, we denote

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}:M_{\Omega}(f)(x)>\lambda\};

\displaystyle F_{1}(\beta)

\displaystyle=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}:M_{\Omega}(f\chi_{B(0,r_{\varepsilon})^{c}})(x)>\beta\lambda\};

\displaystyle F_{2}(\beta)

\displaystyle=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}:M_{\Omega}(f\chi_{B(0,r_{\varepsilon})})(x)>\beta\lambda\}.

By the sublinearity of
M_{\Omega}
and the fact
M_{\Omega}(f\chi_{B(0,r_{\varepsilon})})\leq M_{\Omega}(f)
, it follows that

F_{2}(1)\subset F\subset F_{1}(\sqrt{\varepsilon})\cup F_{2}(1-\sqrt{\varepsilon}),

which indicates that

|F_{2}(1)|\leq|F|\leq|F_{1}(\sqrt{\varepsilon})|+|F_{2}(1-\sqrt{\varepsilon})|.

(3.1)

For
|F_{1}(\sqrt{\varepsilon})|
, Theorem
1.2
yields that

\sqrt{\varepsilon}\lambda|F_{1}(\sqrt{\varepsilon})|\leq C{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}}\|\chi_{B(0,r_{\varepsilon})^{c}}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}\leq C{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}}\varepsilon\leq C{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}}\varepsilon^{3/4},

which gives that

|F_{1}(\sqrt{\varepsilon})|\leq C{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}}\frac{\varepsilon^{1/4}}{\lambda}.

(3.2)

Thus by (
3.1
) and (
3.2
), we have

|F_{2}(1)|\leq|F|\leq|F_{2}(1-\sqrt{\varepsilon})|+C{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}}\frac{\varepsilon^{1/4}}{\lambda}.

(3.3)

To estimate
|F_{2}(1)|
and
|F_{2}(1-\sqrt{\varepsilon})|
, we need to decompose the rough kernel
\Omega
first. Since
C({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})
is dense in

, then there exists a nonnegative, continuous function
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
on
{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
such that

\|\Omega-\Omega_{\varepsilon}\|_{L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}<\varepsilon.

We extend
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
from
{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
to
{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
such that it is also homogeneous of degree
0
. Furthermore, since for all
0<t<1
,
t|\log t|\leq 4t^{3/4}/\text{e}
holds, therefore

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\Omega-\Omega_{\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle\leq 3\|\Omega-\Omega_{\varepsilon}\|_{L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}+\|\Omega-\Omega_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}\log^{+}\frac{1}{\|\Omega-\Omega_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}}

\displaystyle\leq 3\varepsilon+\frac{4}{\text{e}}\|\Omega-\Omega_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}^{3/4}\leq\left(3+\frac{4}{\text{e}}\right)\varepsilon^{3/4}.

Now let

\displaystyle F_{2}^{1}(\beta)

\displaystyle=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}:M_{\Omega-\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(f\chi_{B(0,r_{\varepsilon})})(x)|>\beta\lambda\};

\displaystyle F_{2}^{2}(\beta)

\displaystyle=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}:M_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(f\chi_{B(0,r_{\varepsilon})})(x)|>\beta\lambda\}.

Then one can easily deduce that

F_{2}^{2}(1+\sqrt{\varepsilon})\backslash F_{2}^{1}(\sqrt{\varepsilon})\subset F_{2}\qquad\text{and}\qquad F_{2}(1-\sqrt{\varepsilon})\subset F_{2}^{1}(\sqrt{\varepsilon})\cup F_{2}^{2}(1-2\sqrt{\varepsilon}),

which, with (

|F_{2}^{2}(1+\sqrt{\varepsilon})|-|F_{2}^{1}(\sqrt{\varepsilon})|\leq F\leq|F_{2}^{1}(\sqrt{\varepsilon})|+|F_{2}^{2}(1-2\sqrt{\varepsilon})|+C{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}}\frac{\varepsilon^{1/4}}{\lambda}.

(3.4)

As for

, it follows from Theorem

that

\sqrt{\varepsilon}\lambda|F_{2}^{1}(\sqrt{\varepsilon})|\leq C\mathcal{C}_{\Omega-\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\|f\chi_{B(0,r_{\varepsilon})}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}\leq C\varepsilon^{3/4},

which indicates that

|F_{2}^{1}(\sqrt{\varepsilon})|\leq C\frac{\varepsilon^{1/4}}{\lambda}.

(3.5)

Therefore, by (
3.4
) and (
3.5
) it holds that

|F_{2}^{2}(1+\sqrt{\varepsilon})|-C\frac{\varepsilon^{1/4}}{\lambda}\leq F\leq|F_{2}^{2}(1-2\sqrt{\varepsilon})|+C({\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}}+1)\frac{\varepsilon^{1/4}}{\lambda}.

(3.6)

Here we state the main idea of the proof of Theorem
1.1
(i). To estimate
|F|
, we need to give an upper estimate of
|F_{2}^{2}(1-2\sqrt{\varepsilon})|
and a lower estimate of
|F_{2}^{2}(1+\sqrt{\varepsilon})|

has compact support and
\Omega_{\varepsilon}

Part 1: Upper estimate of

Since

{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}

Now let

Therefore we can get

|\Omega_{\varepsilon}(x-y)-\Omega_{\varepsilon}(x)|=\Big|\Omega_{\varepsilon}\Big(\frac{x-y}{|x-y|}\Big)-\Omega_{\varepsilon}\Big(\frac{x}{|x|}\Big)\Big|<\varepsilon.

(3.7)

Notice that when

and

which means that the radius in the supremum of

\displaystyle M_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(f\chi_{B(0,r_{\varepsilon})})(x)\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1-\varepsilon)^{n}|x|^{n}}\int_{B(x,(1+\varepsilon)|x|)}\Omega_{\varepsilon}(x-y)f(y)\chi_{B(0,r_{\varepsilon})}(y)dy

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1-\varepsilon)^{n}|x|^{n}}\int_{B(0,r_{\varepsilon})}(\Omega_{\varepsilon}(x)+\varepsilon)f(y)dy

\displaystyle S_{\varepsilon}:=

\displaystyle\{\theta\in{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}:\Omega_{\varepsilon}(\theta)>\varepsilon\},

\displaystyle\big\{x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}:\frac{x}{|x|}\in S_{\varepsilon}\big\}.

\displaystyle M_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(f\chi_{B(0,r_{\varepsilon})})(x)\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1+\varepsilon)^{n}|x|^{n}}\int_{B(x,(1+\varepsilon)|x|)}\Omega_{\varepsilon}(x-y)f(y)\chi_{B(0,r_{\varepsilon})}(y)dy

\displaystyle\frac{(1-\varepsilon)\|\Omega_{\varepsilon}-\varepsilon\|_{L^{1}(S_{\varepsilon})}}{n(1+\sqrt{\varepsilon})^{n+1}\lambda}-|\overline{B(0,R_{\varepsilon})}|,

. Multiplying

\displaystyle\varliminf_{\lambda\to 0^{+}}\lambda|F|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(1-\varepsilon)(\|\Omega_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}(S_{\varepsilon})}-\sigma(S_{\varepsilon})\varepsilon)}{n(1+\sqrt{\varepsilon})^{n+1}}-C{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}}\varepsilon^{1/4}

\displaystyle=\frac{(1-\varepsilon)(\|\Omega_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}-\|\Omega_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\backslash S_{\varepsilon})}-\sigma(S_{\varepsilon})\varepsilon)}{n(1+\sqrt{\varepsilon})^{n+1}}-C{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}}\varepsilon^{1/4}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(1-\varepsilon)(\|\Omega\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}-(1+\sigma({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}))\varepsilon)}{n(1+\sqrt{\varepsilon})^{n+1}}-C{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}}\varepsilon^{1/4}.

It follows from the arbitrariness of
\varepsilon
that

\varliminf_{\lambda\to 0^{+}}\lambda|F|\geq\frac{\|\Omega\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}}{n}.

(3.12)

Finally, from (
3.10
) and (
3.12
), it follows that

\lim_{\lambda\to 0^{+}}\lambda|F|=\frac{\|\Omega\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}}{n}.

This completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
(i).
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
(ii).

For
\lambda,\beta>0
, we set

\displaystyle G=

\displaystyle\Big\{x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}:\Big|M_{\Omega}(f)(x)-\frac{\Omega(x)}{|x|^{n}}\Big|>\lambda\Big\};

\displaystyle G_{1}(\beta)=

\displaystyle\Big\{x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}:\Big|\frac{\Omega(x)-\Omega_{\varepsilon}(x)}{|x|^{n}}\Big|>\beta\lambda\Big\};

\displaystyle G_{2}(\beta)=

\displaystyle\Big\{|x|>R_{\varepsilon}:\Big|M_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(f\chi_{B(0,r_{\varepsilon})})(x)-\frac{\Omega_{\varepsilon}(x)}{|x|^{n}}\Big|>\beta\lambda\Big\}.

Now we claim that

G\subset F_{1}(\sqrt{\varepsilon})\cup F_{2}^{1}(\sqrt{\varepsilon})\cup G_{1}(\sqrt{\varepsilon})\cup G_{2}(1-3\sqrt{\varepsilon})\cup\overline{B(0,R_{\varepsilon})}.

(3.13)

Indeed, to show this claim is true, it suffices to show the complementary set of right side in (
3.13
) is contained in
G^{c}
. For any
x
in the complementary set of right side, we have
|x|>R_{\varepsilon}
and the following facts:

\Big|\frac{\Omega(x)-\Omega_{\varepsilon}(x)}{|x|^{n}}\Big|\leq\sqrt{\varepsilon}\lambda;

\Big|M_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(f\chi_{B(0,r_{\varepsilon})})(x)-\frac{\Omega_{\varepsilon}(x)}{|x|^{n}}\Big|\leq(1-3\sqrt{\varepsilon})\lambda;

M_{\Omega}(f\chi_{B(0,r_{\varepsilon})^{c}})(x),M_{\Omega-\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(f\chi_{B(0,r_{\varepsilon})})(x)|\leq\sqrt{\varepsilon}\lambda.

From these inequalities, it’s easy to deduce that

\displaystyle M_{\Omega}(f)(x)

\displaystyle\leq M_{\Omega}(f\chi_{B(0,r_{\varepsilon})})(x)+\sqrt{\varepsilon}\lambda\leq M_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(f\chi_{B(0,r_{\varepsilon})})(x)+2\sqrt{\varepsilon}\lambda

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\Omega_{\varepsilon}(x)}{|x|^{n}}+(1-\sqrt{\varepsilon})\lambda\leq\frac{\Omega(x)}{|x|^{n}}+\lambda

and

\displaystyle M_{\Omega}(f)(x)

\displaystyle\geq M_{\Omega}(f\chi_{B(0,r_{\varepsilon})})(x)\geq M_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(f\chi_{B(0,r_{\varepsilon})})(x)-\sqrt{\varepsilon}\lambda

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\Omega_{\varepsilon}(x)}{|x|^{n}}-(1-2\sqrt{\varepsilon})\lambda\geq\frac{\Omega(x)}{|x|^{n}}-(1-\sqrt{\varepsilon})\lambda\geq\frac{\Omega(x)}{\nu_{n}|x|^{n}}-\lambda,

which implies that
x\in G^{c}
. Therefore claim (
3.13
) is true.

By (
3.2
) and (
3.5
), one immediately gets that

|G|\leq|G_{1}(\sqrt{\varepsilon})|+|G_{2}(1-3\sqrt{\varepsilon})|+C({\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}}+1)\frac{\varepsilon^{1/4}}{\lambda}+|\overline{B(0,R_{\varepsilon})}|.

(3.14)

In regard to
|G_{1}(\sqrt{\varepsilon})|
, applying Lemma
3.1
, we obtain

|G_{1}(\sqrt{\varepsilon})|=\frac{\|\Omega-\Omega_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}}{n\sqrt{\varepsilon}\lambda}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{n\sqrt{\varepsilon}\lambda}\leq C\frac{\varepsilon^{1/4}}{\lambda}.

With (
3.14
), we have

|G|\leq|G_{2}(1-3\sqrt{\varepsilon})\cap V_{\varepsilon}|+|G_{2}(1-3\sqrt{\varepsilon})\cap V_{\varepsilon}^{c}|+C({\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}}+1)\frac{\varepsilon^{1/4}}{\lambda}+|\overline{B(0,R_{\varepsilon})}|.

(3.15)

Now for any
x\in G_{2}(1-3\sqrt{\varepsilon})\cap V_{\varepsilon}
, from (
3.9
) and (
3.11
), we know that
M_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(f\chi_{B(0,r_{\varepsilon})})(x)
and
\Omega_{\varepsilon}(x)\big/|x|^{n}
are between
(1-\varepsilon)(\Omega_{\varepsilon}(x)-\varepsilon)\big/((1+\varepsilon)^{n}|x|^{n})
and
(\Omega_{\varepsilon}(x)+\varepsilon)\big/((1-\varepsilon)^{n}|x|^{n})
, which implies that

\Big|M_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(f\chi_{B(0,r_{\varepsilon})})(x)-\frac{\Omega_{\varepsilon}(x)|}{|x|^{n}}\Big|\leq\frac{\Omega_{\varepsilon}(x)+\varepsilon}{(1-\varepsilon)^{n}|x|^{n}}-\frac{(1-\varepsilon)(\Omega_{\varepsilon}(x)-\varepsilon)}{(1+\varepsilon)^{n}|x|^{n}}=:\frac{I_{\Omega,\varepsilon}(x)}{|x|^{n}}.

Thus by Lemma
3.1
, one may obtain

|G_{2}(1-3\sqrt{\varepsilon})\cap V_{\varepsilon}|\leq\Big|\Big\{x\in V_{\varepsilon}:\frac{I_{\Omega,\varepsilon}(x)}{|x|^{n}}>(1-3\sqrt{\varepsilon})\lambda\Big\}\Big|=\frac{\|I_{\Omega,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}(S_{\varepsilon})}}{n(1-3\sqrt{\varepsilon})\lambda}.

It is easy to see that

\displaystyle\|I_{\Omega,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}(S_{\varepsilon})}

\displaystyle=\|\Omega_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}(S_{\varepsilon})}\left(\frac{1}{(1-\varepsilon)^{n}}-\frac{1-\varepsilon}{(1+\varepsilon)^{n}}\right)+\left(\frac{1}{(1-\varepsilon)^{n}}+\frac{1-\varepsilon}{(1+\varepsilon)^{n}}\right)\sigma(S_{\varepsilon})\varepsilon

\displaystyle\leq C(\|\Omega\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}+1)\varepsilon\leq C({\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}}+1)\varepsilon^{1/4}.

Therefore

|G_{2}(1-3\sqrt{\varepsilon})\cap V_{\varepsilon}|\leq C({\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}}+1)\frac{\varepsilon^{1/4}}{\lambda}.

(3.16)

On the other hand, if
x\in G_{2}(1-3\sqrt{\varepsilon})\cap V_{\varepsilon}^{c}
, it follows from (
3.9
) that

\Big|M_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(f\chi_{B(0,r_{\varepsilon})})(x)-\frac{\Omega_{\varepsilon}(x)|}{|x|^{n}}\Big|\leq 2\frac{\Omega_{\varepsilon}(x)+\varepsilon}{(1-\varepsilon)^{n}|x|^{n}}\leq 2\frac{\Omega_{\varepsilon}(x)+\varepsilon}{(1-3\sqrt{\varepsilon})^{n}|x|^{n}}.

Then by Lemma
3.1
,
|G_{2}(1-3\sqrt{\varepsilon})\cap V_{\varepsilon}^{c}|
is dominated by

|G_{2}(1-3\sqrt{\varepsilon})\cap V_{\varepsilon}^{c}|\leq\frac{2\|\Omega_{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\backslash S_{\varepsilon})}}{n(1-3\sqrt{\varepsilon})^{n+1}\lambda}\leq\frac{4\sigma({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\backslash S_{\varepsilon})\varepsilon}{n(1-2\sqrt{\varepsilon})^{n+1}\lambda}\leq C\frac{\varepsilon^{1/4}}{\lambda}.

(3.17)

Finally, by (
3.15
)-(
3.17
), it holds that

\displaystyle|G|\leq C({\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}}+1)\frac{\varepsilon^{1/4}}{\lambda}.

Multiplying
\lambda
on both sides, and by the arbitrariness of
\varepsilon
, we finally get

\lim_{\lambda\to 0^{+}}\lambda|G|=0,

which finishes the proof of Theorem
1.1
(ii).
∎

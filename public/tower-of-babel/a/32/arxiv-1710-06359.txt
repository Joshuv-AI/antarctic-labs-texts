Wigner functions for the pair angle and orbital angular momentum: Possible applications in quantum information
H. A. Kastrup

Abstract
The framework of Wigner functions for the canonical pair angle and orbital angular momentum, derived and analyzed in 2 recent papers [H. A. Kastrup, Phys. Rev. A 94 , 062113(2016) and Phys. Rev. A 95 , 052111(2017)], is applied to elementary concepts of quantum information like qubits and 2-qubits, e.g., entangled EPR/Bell states etc.. Properties of the associated Wigner functions of such superposed states (pure and mixed) are discussed and illustrated. The Wigner functions of EPR/Bell states are distinguished by their topologically “twisted” domain on the configuration subspace 𝕊 1 × 𝕊 1 \mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{S}^{1} , a torus, of the total phase space. Like the applications of Wigner functions in quantum optics the results presented in the present paper may be useful for the description and analysis of quantum information experiments with orbital angular momenta of light beams or electron beams, respectively.



I
Introduction

In two recent papers
Kastrup 2016
;
Kastrup 2017
basic properties of Wigner functions on
cylindrical phase spaces
\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{R}
(angle and orbital angular momentum, denoted by “A-OAM” in the following)
were derived and discussed. The possible usefulness of that concept has, of course, to be demonstrated
by its applications to special systems and associated experiments.

A few simple typical example were discussed in
Ch. IV C of Ref.
Kastrup 2016
. The present paper suggests possible applications to such elementary
concepts as “qubits” and “2-qubits” of quantum information, see, e.g., Refs.
Nielsen and Chuang 2010
;
Barnett 2009
.

The quantized canonical system of the pair
angle and orbital angular momentum
is of special
theoretical interest for
quantum information
because it provides as a basic framework an infinite dimensional Hilbert space

L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1},d\varphi/2\pi;\delta)
, with orthonormal
basis

e_{m,\delta}(\varphi)=e^{i(m+\delta)\varphi},\;\;m\in\mathbb{Z},\;\delta\in[0,1),

(1)

with scalar product

(\psi^{[\delta]}_{2},\psi^{[\delta]}_{1})=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{d\varphi}{2\pi}\psi^{[\delta]\ast}_{2}(\varphi)\psi^{[\delta]}_{1}(\varphi),\;\;(e_{m,\delta},e_{n,\delta})=\delta_{mn}

(2)

(
\delta_{mn}
: Kronecker symbol) and the expansions

\displaystyle\psi^{[\delta]}(\varphi)=

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{m}\,e_{m,\delta]}(\varphi),\;\;c_{m}=(e_{m,\delta}\psi),

(3)

\displaystyle\psi^{[\delta]}(\varphi+2\pi)=e^{i2\pi\delta}\psi^{[\delta]}(\varphi).

The number
\delta\in[0,1)
mathematically characterizes a covering of the group
U(1)
and physically a fractional OAM
Kastrup 2006
. The following discussions
assume
\delta=0
and denote
L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1},d\varphi/2\pi)\equiv L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1},d\varphi/2\pi;\delta=0),\,e_{m}\equiv e_{m,\delta=0}
etc..
The essential generalizations of the main results obtained for
\delta=0
for
the case
\delta\neq 0
are discussed in Appendix A.
Note that the coefficients
c_{m}
in Eq. (
3
)
are independent of
\delta
.

One of the advantages of A-OAM systems for quantum information theories is that one can select finite dimensional subspaces of any dimension
d
:

d=2
: qubits,
d=3
: qutrits,
\ldots,~d
: “qudits”, like, e.g.,

(e_{m_{0}}+e_{m_{1}}+\ldots+e_{m_{d-1}})/\sqrt{d}

(4)

and associated tensor product spaces which then contain entangled states. (For a recent general discussion of entanglement in quantum theory
see Ref.
Witten 2018
.)

In those
d
-dimensional subspaces
\varphi
-independent (“global”) unitary transformations
U(d)
and other linear mappings (called “gates” in quantum information) may act.

Especially one can incorporate the usual elementary qubits from 2-dimensional spaces
bar a
, e.g.,

(|0\rangle\pm|1\rangle)/\sqrt{2},

(5)

and associated entangled EPR/Bell product states
bar a

\displaystyle(|00\rangle\pm|11\rangle)/\sqrt{2}\equiv

\displaystyle(|0\rangle\otimes|0\rangle\pm|1\rangle\otimes|1\rangle)/\sqrt{2},

(6)

\displaystyle(|01\rangle\pm|10\rangle)/\sqrt{2}\equiv

\displaystyle(|0\rangle\otimes|1\rangle\pm|1\rangle\otimes|0\rangle)/\sqrt{2}

(7)

etc.

In the following the A-OAM Wigner functions of the most general qubits and 2-qubits will be derived and

II
Wigner functions for qubits

II.1
Pure states

The most general qubit of a A-OAM system is given by

\displaystyle\chi_{m_{0},m_{1}}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(\varphi)=\cos\beta\,e_{m_{0}}(\varphi)+\sin\beta\,e^{i\alpha}\,e_{m_{1}}(\varphi),

(8)

\displaystyle m_{0},m_{1}\in\mathbb{Z},\;m_{1}\neq m_{0};\,\beta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2});\,\alpha\in[0,2\pi),

\displaystyle|\chi_{m_{0},m_{1}}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(\varphi)|^{2}=1+\sin 2\beta\cos[(m_{0}-m_{1})\varphi-\alpha].

(9)

The states (
8
) are elements of the 2-dimensional subspace

Q^{2}_{m_{0},m_{1}}=\{\chi_{m_{0},m_{1}}=c_{0}\,e_{m_{0}}+c_{1}\,e_{m_{1}},\;|c_{0}|^{2}+|c_{1}|^{2}=1\}

(10)

of the overall Hilbert space
L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1},d\varphi/2\pi)
.
Here we assume the correspondences

|0\rangle\leftrightarrow e_{m_{0}},\,\,|1\rangle\leftrightarrow e_{m_{1}}

(11)

for the bases of the 2-dimensional space of qubits and the space
Q^{2}_{m_{0},m_{1}}
.

The angular momentum operator (
\hbar=1
in the following)

L=(1/i)\partial_{\varphi}

(12)

has the - obvious - expectation value

(\chi_{m_{0},m_{1}}^{(\alpha,\beta)},L\chi_{m_{0},m_{1}}^{(\alpha,\beta)})=m_{0}\cos^{2}\beta+m_{1}\sin^{2}\beta,

(13)

which for
m_{1}=-m_{0}
becomes

(\chi_{m_{0},-m_{0}}^{(\alpha,\beta)},L\chi_{m_{0},-m_{0}}^{(\alpha,\beta)})=m_{0}\,\cos 2\beta\,.

(14)

This vanishes for
\beta=\pi/4
.

If

\chi_{m_{0},m_{1}}^{(\hat{\alpha},\hat{\beta})}(\varphi)=\cos\hat{\beta}\,e_{m_{0}}(\varphi)+\sin\hat{\beta}\,e^{i\hat{\alpha}}\,e_{m_{1}}(\varphi)

(15)

is another qubit of the type (
8
) in the same 2-dimensional space, then the scalar product of both is given by

\displaystyle(\chi_{m_{0},m_{1}}^{(\alpha,\beta)},\chi_{m_{0},m_{1}}^{(\hat{\alpha},\hat{\beta})})=

(16)

\displaystyle\cos\beta\,\cos\hat{\beta}+e^{-i(\alpha-\hat{\alpha})}\sin\beta\sin\hat{\beta},

with the associated transition probability

\displaystyle|(\chi_{m_{0},m_{1}}^{(\alpha,\beta)},\chi_{m_{0},m_{1}}^{(\hat{\alpha},\hat{\beta})})|^{2}=

(17)

\displaystyle\cos^{2}\beta\cos^{2}\hat{\beta}+\sin^{2}\beta\sin^{2}\hat{\beta}+\frac{1}{2}\sin 2\beta\sin 2\hat{\beta}\cos(\alpha-\hat{\alpha}),

which equals
\cos^{2}(\beta-\hat{\beta)}
for
\hat{\alpha}=\alpha
. Eq. (
17
) is of interest in a discussion below (see Eq. (
28
)).

According to Ch. IV of Ref.
Kastrup 2016
the A-OAM Wigner function
V_{\psi}(\theta,p)
for a wave
function
\psi(\varphi)
from Eq. (
3
) is given by (with
\hbar
explicit in the following two Eqs.)

\displaystyle V_{\psi}(\theta,p)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{d\vartheta}{2\pi}e^{-i(p/\hbar)\vartheta}\psi^{\ast}(\theta-\vartheta/2)\,\psi(\theta+\vartheta/2)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{m}^{\ast}V_{mn}(\theta,p)c_{n}\,,

(18)

\displaystyle V_{mn}(\theta,p)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}e^{i(n-m)\theta}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{d\vartheta}{2\pi}e^{i[(m+n)/2-p/\hbar]\vartheta}

(19)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}e^{i(n-m)\theta}\sinc\pi[p/\hbar-(m+n)/2],

\displaystyle\sinc\pi x=\frac{\sin\pi x}{\pi x}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}d\vartheta\,e^{ix\vartheta}.

(20)

Here
\theta
describes (by means of the pair
(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)
)
the points
on the (configuration) circle
\mathbb{S}^{1}
of the classical phase space
\mathcal{P}^{2}(\theta,p)=\{(\theta,p)\in\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{R}\}
, with

p\in\mathbb{R}
the classical canonically conjugate OAM. Integration measure on
\mathcal{P}^{2}
is
d\theta dp\;(\hbar=1)
. The letter
"V"
, denoting the Wigner
function (
II.1
), stands for “Vortex”.

The remarkable significance of the sinc-function in the context of the A-OAM Wigner function is elaborately discussed in Ref.
Kastrup 2016
.

Taking for
\psi(\varphi)
the wave function (
8
) gives

\displaystyle 2\pi\,V_{m_{0},m_{1}}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(\theta,p)

(21)

\displaystyle=\cos^{2}\beta\sinc\pi(p-m_{0})+\sin^{2}\beta\sinc\pi(p-m_{1})

\displaystyle+\sin 2\beta\cos[(m_{0}-m_{1})\theta-\alpha]\sinc\pi[p-(m_{0}+m_{1})/2].

The last line in Eq. (
21
) represents the probability interference term in
(\chi_{m_{0},m_{1}}^{(\alpha,\beta)},\chi_{m_{0},m_{1}}^{(\alpha,\beta)})

– see Eq. (
9
) – and describes, therefore, essential
quantum mechanical
properties of the state!

That means the
\theta
-dependent part of the A-OAM Wigner function

V_{m_{0},m_{1}}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(\theta,p)
of the qubit (
8

That part vanishes
for

and/or
if
\alpha
and
\theta

\cos[(m_{0}-m_{1})\theta-\alpha]=0

On the other hand, the factors of
\cos[(m_{0}-m_{1})\theta-\alpha]
in Eq. (
21

(=1)
for
\beta=\pi/4
and
p=(m_{0}+m_{1})/2

The angles
\alpha
and
\beta
may depend on other parameters, e.g., time
t

be manipulated from outside. Their values can be represented
by points on the 2-dimensional surface of a “Bloch” sphere
bar b

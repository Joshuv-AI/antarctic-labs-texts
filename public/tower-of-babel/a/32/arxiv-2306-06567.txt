Abstract
. We prove that certain subcritical Paneitz-Branson type equations on a closed Riemannian manifold ( M , g ) (M,g) have at least Cat ⁡ ( M ) \mathrm{Cat}(M) positve solutions, where Cat ⁡ ( M ) \mathrm{Cat}(M) is the Lusternik-Schnirelmann category of M M . This implies that if ( X , h ) (X,h) is a closed positive Einstein manifold then for ε > 0 \varepsilon>0 small enough there are at least Cat ⁡ ( M ) \mathrm{Cat}(M) metrics of constant Q Q -curvature in the conformal class of the Riemannian product g + ε ​ h g+\varepsilon h .



1.
Introduction

Consider a closed Riemannian manifold
(\mathbf{M},\mathfrak{g})
of dimension
N\geq 5
.
The Paneitz operator
P_{\mathfrak{g}}
is defined using a local
\mathfrak{g}
-orthonormal frame
\left(e_{i}\right)_{i=1}^{n}
, as

(1.1)

P_{\mathfrak{g}}\psi:=\Delta_{\mathfrak{g}}^{2}\psi+\frac{4}{N-2}\operatorname{div}_{\mathfrak{g}}\left(\operatorname{Ric}_{\mathfrak{g}}\left(\nabla\psi,e_{i}\right)e_{i}\right)-\frac{N^{2}-4N+8}{2(N-1)(N-2)}\operatorname{div}_{\mathfrak{g}}\left(\operatorname{scal}_{\mathfrak{g}}\nabla\psi\right)+\frac{N-4}{2}Q_{\mathfrak{g}}\psi,

where
\Delta_{\mathfrak{g}}u=\operatorname{div}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\nabla_{\mathfrak{g}}u)
is the (negative) Laplace operator on
(\mathbf{M},\mathfrak{g})
,
\operatorname{div}_{\mathfrak{g}}
is the divergence,
\operatorname{scal}_{\mathfrak{g}}
is
the scalar curvature and
\mathrm{Ric}_{\mathfrak{g}}
is the Ricci tensor.
Q_{\mathfrak{g}}
is the
Q
-curvature of
\mathfrak{g}
, which is given by:

(1.2)

Q_{\mathfrak{g}}:=-\frac{1}{2(N-1)}\Delta_{\mathfrak{g}}\operatorname{scal}_{\mathfrak{g}}-\frac{2}{(N-2)^{2}}\left|\operatorname{Ric}_{\mathfrak{g}}\right|^{2}+\frac{N^{3}-4N^{2}+16N-16}{8(N-1)^{2}(N-2)^{2}}\operatorname{scal}_{\mathfrak{g}}^{2}.

The Paneitz operator and the related
Q
-curvature were introduced by Stephen Paneitz
[
24
]
and by Thomas Branson
[
8
]
.
We denote by
\left[\mathfrak{g}_{0}\right]
the conformal class of a given metric
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
in
\mathbf{M}
.
For practical reasons, we usually write a conformal metric
\mathfrak{g}\in\left[\mathfrak{g}_{0}\right]
as
\mathfrak{g}=u^{\frac{4}{N-4}}\mathfrak{g}_{0}
, for a positive function
u:\mathbf{M}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
.
The Paneitz operator is conformally invariant in the sense that for any
\psi:\mathbf{M}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,

P_{\mathfrak{g}}\psi=u^{-\frac{N+4}{N-4}}P_{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}(u\psi).

It follows that we can express the
Q
-curvature of
\mathfrak{g}
in terms of
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
and
u
by:

Q_{\mathfrak{g}}=\frac{2}{N-4}P_{\mathfrak{g}}(1)=\frac{2}{N-4}u^{-\frac{N+4}{N-4}}P_{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}(u).

Similarly to the Yamabe problem of finding metrics of constant scalar curvature in a conformal class, there has been great interest in studying the problem of finding metrics of constant
Q
-curvature in a conformal class.

It follows from the previous comments that finding a conformal metric
\mathfrak{g}=u^{\frac{4}{n-4}}\mathfrak{g}_{0}
with constant
Q
-curvature amounts to finding a positive function
u
which solves the fourth order equation

(1.3)

P_{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}u=\lambda u^{\frac{N+4}{N-4}},\quad\lambda\in\mathbb{R}.

We call this equation the Paneitz-Branson Equation.

Note that the
Q
-curvature is defined if
\mathbf{M}
has dimension greater than 2.
However, when the dimension is 3 or 4, the constant curvature equation has a different form.
Some significant results obtained in the
4
-dimensional case are given, for instance, by Sun-Yung A. Chang and Paul C. Yang
[
10
]
, by Simon Brendle
[
9
]
and by Zindine Djadli and Andrea Malchiodi
[
12
]
.
In this work, we restrict our discussion to the case when the dimension is at least 5.

Previous research has considered the problem of the existence of metrics of constant
Q
-curvature in dimension
N\geq 5
.
For example, Jie Qing and David Raske in
[
26
]
proved the existence of a positive solution of the Paneitz-Branson equation on a locally conformally flat manifold of positive scalar curvature and Poincaré exponent less than
\frac{N-4}{2}
.
Meanwhile, Emmanuel Hebey and Frédéric Robert in
[
11
]
also proved the existence of a positive solution for locally conformally flat manifolds of positive scalar curvature, assuming that the Paneitz operator and the corresponding Green function were positive.
Matthew J. Gursky and Andrea Malchiodi in
[
15
]
proved the existence of a positive solution under the conditions that
Q_{\mathfrak{g}}
is non-negative and positive somewhere in
M
and
\operatorname{scal}_{\mathfrak{g}}\geq 0
.

Solutions of the Paneitz-Branson equation are the critical points of the functional
\mathcal{P}_{\mathfrak{g}}:H^{2}(\mathbf{M})\setminus\{0\}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, given by

\mathcal{P}_{\mathfrak{g}}(u):=\frac{2}{N-4}\frac{\int_{\mathbf{M}}u\,P_{\mathfrak{g}}(u)\ dv_{g}}{\left(\int_{\mathbf{M}}|u|^{\frac{2N}{N-4}}dv_{g}\right)^{\frac{N-4}{N}}}.

Now consider the constant

Y_{4}(\mathfrak{g})=\inf_{u\in C^{\infty}(M),u>0}\mathcal{P}_{\mathfrak{g}}(u).

Fengbo Hang and Paul C. Yang proved in
[
17
]
that if
Y_{4}(\mathfrak{g})
is positive, and also
Q_{\mathfrak{g}}>0
and
\operatorname{scal}_{\mathfrak{g}}>0
(under some weaker hypotheses, it is only needed that the Yamabe invariant of the conformal class of
\mathfrak{g}
is positive and that
Q_{\mathfrak{g}}\geq 0
and positive somewhere) then

Y_{4}(\mathfrak{g})\leq Y_{4}(\mathfrak{g}^{N}_{0})
, where
\mathfrak{g}_{0}^{N}
is the round metric on the
N
-sphere, and the infimum is always realized by a positive function, which is a solution of the Paneitz-Branson equation.
Other related existence results can be found in
[
11
,
12
,
13
,
14
,
16
,
27
]
.

In the case of the round sphere
(\mathbb{S}^{N},\mathfrak{g}_{0}
), there is a non-compact family of solutions to the Paneitz-Branson equation that comes from the family of conformal diffeomorphisms of the sphere.
These are all the solutions of the Paneitz-Branson equation on the sphere by a theorem of Chang-Shou Lin
[
21
]
.

There are also important results about the compactness of the space of solutions, for instance see
[
18
,
19
,
20
,
22
]
.
Examples of non-compactness of the space of positive solutions, different from the constant curvature metric on the sphere, were constructed in high dimensions by Juncheng Wei and Chunyi Zhao
[
30
]
.
Naturally, if
\mathfrak{g}
is Einstein it has constant
Q
-curvature.
Moreover, Jérôme Vétois
[
29
]
proved that if
\mathfrak{g}
is Einstein and it is not isometric to the constant curvature metric on the sphere, then it is, up to multiplication by a constant, the only metric of constant
Q
-curvature in its conformal class.
This result is an analogue of the classical due to Morio Obata
[
23
]
about constant scalar curvature.

There are a few other cases where one can show the non-uniqueness of conformal metrics of constant
Q
-curvature.
One case, which also appears for the Yamabe problem, is that of Riemannian products.
If
(M,g)
and
(X,h)
are closed Einstein manifolds with positive Einstein constant, of dimension at least
3
then
for any
\delta>0
, the Riemannian product
(M\times X,g+\delta h)
has constant positive
Q
-curvature and constant positive scalar curvature.
It is easy to compute that as
\delta\rightarrow 0
or
\delta\rightarrow\infty
we have that
\mathcal{P}_{g+\delta h}(1)\rightarrow\infty
.
We are in the conditions of the result mentioned above
[
17
]
, and therefore in the conformal class of
g+\delta h
, there must be at least one other metric of constant
Q
-curvature, which minimizes
\mathcal{P}_{g+\delta h}
.
Note that a similar argument works for the Yamabe problem, but in that case, one only needs that the scalar curvatures are constant and positive.

Renato Bettiol, Paolo Piccione and Yannick Sire
[
6
]
considered appropriate Riemannian submersions and reduce the equation to basic functions (functions which are constant along the fibers of the submersion).
Using bifurcation theory for this reduced equation on the canonical variation obtained by varying the size of the fibers (under certain conditions the total spaces of these variations have constant
Q
-curvature), they prove the existence of bifurcation instants, so proving the existence of metrics in the family for which there are other conformal metrics of constant
Q
-curvature.

This paper aims to provide a lower bound for the number of metrics of constant
Q
-curvature for a Riemannian product
(M\times X,g+\delta h)
within the family of conformal metrics to
g+\delta h
.
Using the Lusternik-Schnirelmann category, we prove the following multiplicity result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a closed
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold and
(X,h)
a closed
m
-dimensional Einstein manifold with Einstein constant
\Lambda_{0}>0
.
Consider the Riemannian product
(M\times X,\mathfrak{g}_{\varepsilon}:=g+\varepsilon^{2}h)
.
Assume that
m\geq 3
or, if
m=2
, that
n\geq 7
.
Then there exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that for any
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
the Paneitz-Branson equation on
(M\times X,\mathfrak{g}_{\varepsilon})
has at least
\mathrm{Cat}(M)
positive solutions.

Here
\mathrm{Cat}(M)
denotes the Lusternick-Schnirelmann category of
M
, a topological invariant defined as the minimum number of contractible open sets needed to cover it.
If
M
is a manifold,
\mathrm{Cat}(M)
coincides with the minimal number of critical points among all smooth scalar maps
M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
(see
[
28
]
).
The solutions given in the theorem are functions on
M
.
We will see in Section
2
that a function
u:M\rightarrow(0,\infty)
solves the
Paneitz-Branson equation on
(M\times X,\mathfrak{g}_{\varepsilon})
if and only if

(1.4)

\Delta^{2}_{g}u-\frac{N^{2}-4N+8}{2(N-1)(N-2)}(m\Lambda_{0}\varepsilon^{-2})\Delta_{g}u+\frac{N-4}{2}\varepsilon^{-4}A_{N,m}u+\varphi(u)+\frac{N-4}{2}(f_{0}+\varepsilon^{-2}f_{2})u=u^{\frac{N+4}{N-4}}

where
N=n+m
is the dimension of the product manifold
M\times X
,
\varphi(u)
is a second order operator on
u
which verifies

\int_{M}\varphi(u)\,u\,dv_{g}\leq C\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}dv_{g},

for some constant
C
,
A_{N,m}
is a positive constant that depends on
N,m
, and
f_{0}
and
f_{2}
are
certain functions on
M
.

In general, for a closed
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold
({M},g)
we can consider an equation of the form

\Delta_{g}^{2}u+f(x)\Delta_{g}u+\phi(u)+g(x)u=h(x)u^{q},

for functions
f,g,h
on M,
q\in(1,\infty)
, and a second order operator
\phi
.
This equation is called critical if
q=\frac{n+4}{n-4}
, subcritical if
q<\frac{n+4}{n-4}
and supercritical if
q>\frac{n+4}{n-4}
(if
n\leq 4
it is considered subcritical for any
q>1
).
For instance, in
[
11
, Section 2]
the authors study the critical case
when
f,g
are constant,
\phi=0
.

Consider the equation

(1.5)

\Delta_{g}^{2}u-b\varepsilon^{-2}\Delta_{g}u+a\varepsilon^{-4}u=u^{q}\quad\text{on }M

Assume that
a,b>0
.
For
\varepsilon
small Equation (
1.4
) is, in certain sense, close to Equation
1.5
.
It will be easy to check along the proof of Theorem
1.1
that it also gives a proof of

Theorem 1.2
.

If
b^{2}\geq 4a
and
q<\frac{n+4}{n-4}
(if
n>4
), then there exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that for any

\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
, Equation (
1.5
) has at least
\mathrm{Cat}(M)
positive solutions.

In Section
2
we will give the explicit formula for the Paneitz operator
P_{\mathfrak{g}_{\varepsilon}}
. In
Section
3
we will see that the positive solutions of the Paneitz-Branson equation for

\mathfrak{g}_{\varepsilon}
are precisely the critical points of the functional

J_{\varepsilon,q}^{g,+}(u)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{n}}\int_{M}\!\Big(\frac{\varepsilon^{4}}{2}(\Delta_{g}u)^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\frac{b_{N,m}}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+\frac{a_{N,m}}{2}u^{2}+\varepsilon^{4}\varphi(u)u+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}(\varepsilon^{2}f_{0}(x)+f_{2}(x))u^{2}-\frac{(u^{+})^{q+1}}{q+1}\Big)dv_{g}\!

restricted to the associated Nehari manifold
\mathcal{N}^{g}_{\varepsilon}
. In the functional

u^{+}=\max\{u,0\}
.
We will also show that
J_{\varepsilon,q}^{g,+}
restricted to
\mathcal{N}^{g}_{\varepsilon}
is bounded from below and satisfies the Palais-Smale
condition. By Lusternik-Schnirelmann theory it follows that for any
d
the number of positive solutions of the Paneitz-Branson
equation is at least
\mathrm{Cat}(\Sigma_{\varepsilon,d})
,
where
\Sigma_{\varepsilon,d}=\{u\in\mathcal{N}^{g}_{\varepsilon}:J^{g,+}_{\varepsilon,q}(u)\leq d\}
. Let

{\bf m}_{\varepsilon}:=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{N}^{g}_{\varepsilon}}J^{g,+}_{\varepsilon,q}(u).

The rest of the article is devoted to prove that for
\varepsilon,\delta
small enough we have that

\mathrm{Cat}(\Sigma_{\varepsilon,{\bf m}_{\varepsilon}+\delta})\geq\mathrm{Cat}(M)
.
To prove this inequality we will use a method introduced by Vieri Benci and Giovanna Cerami in
[
3
]
to study multiplicity results for solutions of nonlinear elliptic problems in Euclidean domains.
The method was later used also to study similar problems in Riemannian manifolds, for instance in
[
2
]
.
It is sometimes called the photography method, see for instance
[
5
]
where there is a very clear and general description of the method.

In Section
4
we study the limit equation and functionals on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
A positive, minimizing, solution of this limit equation is used later to construct, for any
x\in M
a function in

{\bf p}(x)\in\Sigma_{\varepsilon,{\bf m}_{\varepsilon}+\delta}
, which is concentrated around
x
.

In Section
6
we show that for
\varepsilon,\delta
small enough, functions
u\in\Sigma_{\varepsilon,{\bf m}_{\varepsilon}+\delta}
are concentrated around some point.
This is used in Section
7
to show that there is a well defined center of mass function
{\bf b}:\Sigma_{\varepsilon,{\bf m}_{\varepsilon}+\delta}\rightarrow M
.
It is easily checked that
{\bf b}\circ{\bf p}:M\rightarrow M
is close, and therefore homotopic to the identity. This implies the inequality
\mathrm{Cat}(\Sigma_{\varepsilon,{\bf m}_{\varepsilon}+\delta})\geq\mathrm{Cat}(M)
.

2.
Constant
Q
-curvature on a product manifold

In this section, we study the Paneitz operator and the
Q
-curvature in the case of a product manifold.
Here we establish the necessary hypotheses to apply the techniques mentioned above.

Let
(M,g)
be a closed
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold and
(X,h)
a closed
m
-dimensional Einstein manifold with positive Einstein constant
\Lambda_{0}
,
i.e.
, its scalar curvature is
\operatorname{scal}_{h}=m\Lambda_{0}
.
We are interested in positive solutions of the constant
Q
-curvature equation (
1.3
) for the product manifold
(M\times X,\mathfrak{g}_{\varepsilon}:=g+\varepsilon^{2}h)
, which is given by

P_{\mathfrak{g}_{\varepsilon}}u=\lambda_{\varepsilon}u^{\frac{n+m+4}{n+m-4}},\quad\lambda_{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{R},

where
P_{\mathfrak{g}_{\varepsilon}}
is the Paneitz operator defined in (
1.1
).
We consider the case
\lambda_{\varepsilon}>0
and so we can renormalize the previous equation as

P_{\mathfrak{g}_{\varepsilon}}u=u^{\frac{n+m+4}{n+m-4}}.

We put
N:=n+m
and compute the
Q
-curvature for the product metric.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a closed
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold and let
(X,h)
be a closed
m
-dimensional Einstein manifold with positive Einstein constant
\Lambda_{0}
.
If
N=n+m
, then the
Q
-curvature for the product manifold
(M\times X,\mathfrak{g}_{\varepsilon})
is

\displaystyle Q_{\mathfrak{g}_{\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2(N-1)}\Delta_{g}\operatorname{scal}_{g}-\frac{2}{(N-2)^{2}}\left|\mathrm{Ric}_{g}\right|^{2}+\frac{N^{3}-4N^{2}+16N-16}{8(N-1)^{2}(N-2)^{2}}\operatorname{scal}_{g}^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\ \frac{N^{3}-4N^{2}+16N-16}{8(N-1)^{2}(N-2)^{2}}2m\operatorname{scal}_{g}\Lambda_{0}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{4}}\left(-\frac{2m\Lambda_{0}^{2}}{(N-2)^{2}}+\frac{N^{3}-4N^{2}+16N-16}{8(N-1)^{2}(N-2)^{2}}m^{2}\Lambda_{0}^{2}\right).

Proof.

By using (
1.2
) and the fact that

\left|\mathrm{Ric}_{\mathfrak{g}_{\varepsilon}}\right|^{2}=\left|\mathrm{Ric}_{g}\right|^{2}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{4}}\left|\mathrm{Ric}_{h}\right|^{2}=\left|\mathrm{Ric}_{g}\right|^{2}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{4}}m\Lambda_{0}^{2},

straightforward calculations leads to the claim.
∎

Now, calling

(2.1)

\displaystyle f_{0}

\displaystyle:=-\frac{1}{2(N-1)}\Delta_{g}\operatorname{scal}_{g}-\frac{2}{(N-2)^{2}}\left|\mathrm{Ric}_{g}\right|^{2}+\frac{N^{3}-4N^{2}+16N-16}{8(N-1)^{2}(N-2)^{2}}\left(\operatorname{scal}_{g}^{2}\right),

\displaystyle f_{2}

\displaystyle:=\frac{N^{3}-4N^{2}+16N-16}{4(N-1)^{2}(N-2)^{2}}m\operatorname{scal}_{g}\Lambda_{0},

and

(2.2)

A_{N,m}:=\frac{m\Lambda_{0}^{2}}{(N-2)^{2}}\left(\frac{N^{3}-4N^{2}+16N-16}{8(N-1)^{2}}m-2\right),

we get

Q_{\mathfrak{g}_{\varepsilon}}=f_{0}+\varepsilon^{-2}f_{2}+\varepsilon^{-4}A_{N,m}.

We get the following simple computation:

Lemma 2.2
.

If
m=2
then
A_{N,2}<0
for
N=5,6,7,8
and
A_{N,2}>0
for
N\geq 9
.
If
m\geq 3
, then
A_{N,m}>0
.

Proof.

Since we have a closed
m
-dimensional Einstein manifold with positive Einstein constant
\Lambda_{0}
, we must have
m\geq 2
.

Assume first that
m=2
. From (
2.2
), we have that
A_{N,2}>0
if and only if

N^{3}-4N^{2}+16N-16>8(N-1)^{2}.

This inequality clearly holds if
N\geq 9
, whereas if
N=5,6,7,8
, we get

A_{N,2}<0
.

Now, let
m\geq 3
. Then,
A_{N,m}>0
if and only if

m(N^{3}-4N^{2}+16N-16)>16(N-1)^{2}.

The expression on the left is clearly increasing with
m
and the lemma follows from straightforward calculations.
∎

By restricting our study to functions that depend only on the first factor,
u:M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, we obtain the following expression for the restricted Paneitz operator:

P_{\mathfrak{g}_{\varepsilon}}u=\Delta_{g}^{2}u+\frac{4}{N-2}\operatorname{div}_{g}\left(\operatorname{Ric}_{g}\left(\nabla u,e_{i}\right)e_{i}\right)-\frac{N^{2}-4N+8}{2(N-1)(N-2)}\operatorname{div}_{\mathfrak{g}_{\varepsilon}}\left(\operatorname{scal}_{\mathfrak{g}_{\varepsilon}}\nabla u\right)+\frac{N-4}{2}Q_{\mathfrak{g}_{\varepsilon}}u

for a
g
-orthonormal frame
\left\{e_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{n}
. Then, since
\mathfrak{g}_{\varepsilon}=g+\varepsilon^{2}h
, we get

(2.3)

\begin{array}[c]{ll}P_{g+\varepsilon^{2}h}u\displaystyle=\Delta_{g}^{2}u+\dfrac{4}{N-2}\operatorname{div}_{g}\left(\operatorname{Ric}_{g}\left(\nabla u,e_{i}\right)e_{i}\right)-\dfrac{N^{2}-4N+8}{2(N-1)(N-2)}\Big(\operatorname{div}_{g}\left(\operatorname{scal}_{g}\nabla u\right)\\
\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad+\varepsilon^{-2}m\Lambda_{0}\Delta_{g}u\Big)+\dfrac{N-4}{2}(f_{0}+\varepsilon^{-2}f_{2}+\varepsilon^{-4}A_{N,m})u.\end{array}

Finally, the constant
Q
-curvature equation (
1.3
) for the
product manifold
(M\times X,g+\varepsilon^{2}h)
for a function
u:M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is

(2.4)

\Delta^{2}_{g}u-\frac{N^{2}-4N+8}{2(N-1)(N-2)}\frac{m\Lambda_{0}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\Delta_{g}u+\frac{N-4}{2}\frac{A_{N,m}}{\varepsilon^{4}}u+\varphi(u)+\frac{N-4}{2}(f_{0}+\varepsilon^{-2}f_{2})u=u^{\frac{N+4}{N-4}},

where
\varphi
is a linear second order operator given by

(2.5)

\varphi(u):=\frac{4}{N-2}\operatorname{div}_{g}\left(\operatorname{Ric}_{g}\left(\nabla u,e_{i}\right)e_{i}\right)-\frac{N^{2}-4N+8}{2(N-1)(N-2)}\operatorname{div}_{g}\left(\operatorname{scal}_{g}\nabla u\right).

Next we prove an elementary property of this operator.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\varphi
be the operator defined in (
2.5
). There exists a positive constant
C

such that

\int_{M}\varphi(u)\,u\,dv_{g}\leq C\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}dv_{g}.

Proof.

Using the divergence theorem, we have

\int_{M}\varphi(u)\,u\,dv_{g}\begin{array}[t]{l}\displaystyle=\int_{M}\operatorname{div}_{g}\left(\frac{4}{N-2}\left(\operatorname{Ric}_{g}\left(\nabla u,e_{i}\right)e_{i}\right)-\frac{N^{2}-4N+8}{2(N-1)(N-2)}\operatorname{scal}_{g}\nabla u\right)u\ dv_{g}\\
\displaystyle=\int_{M}\left(\frac{4}{N-2}g_{ij}\operatorname{Ric}_{g}\left(\nabla u,e_{i}\right)(\nabla u)^{i}-\frac{N^{2}-4N+8}{2(N-1)(N-2)}\operatorname{scal}_{g}|\nabla u|^{2}\right)dv_{g}\\
\displaystyle\leq C\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}dv_{g},\end{array}

where
C=C(m,n,g)
.
∎

Let

(2.6)

b_{N,m}:=\frac{N^{2}-4N+8}{2(N-1)(N-2)}m\Lambda_{0},\quad\text{and }\quad a_{N,m}:=\frac{N-4}{2}A_{N,m},

with
A_{N,m}
as in (
2.2
).
Then, from (
2.3
), we get

P_{g+\varepsilon^{2}h}u=\Delta_{g}^{2}u-b_{N,m}\varepsilon^{-2}\Delta_{g}u+a_{N,m}\varepsilon^{-4}u+\varphi(u)+\frac{N-4}{2}(f_{0}+\varepsilon^{-2}f_{2})u,

where
f_{0}
and
f_{2}
are given by (
2.1
).
Consequently, after renormalization, we can rewrite the equation (
2.4
) as

(2.7)

\varepsilon^{4}\Delta^{2}_{g}u-\varepsilon^{2}b_{N,m}\Delta_{g}u+a_{N,m}u+\varepsilon^{4}\varphi(u)+\frac{N-4}{2}(\varepsilon^{4}f_{0}+\varepsilon^{2}f_{2})u=u^{\frac{N+4}{N-4}}.

Observe that if
\Lambda_{0}>0
then
b_{N,m}>0
, and by Lemma
2.2
,
a_{N,m}>0
if
m\geq 3
or
m=2
and
N\geq 9
.
We also get the following result.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
b_{N,m}
and
a_{N,m}
be the constants defined in (
2.6
). Then
m\geq 3
, or
m=2
and
N\geq 9
, imply that

b_{N,m}>2\sqrt{a_{N,m}}
.

Proof.

We have

\begin{array}[t]{ll}b_{N,m}^{2}-4{a_{N,m}}\displaystyle\frac{16N\Lambda_{0}^{2}m^{2}}{4(N-1)^{2}(N-2)^{2}}>0.\end{array}

The proof finishes from the previous calculation and bearing in mind that
b_{N,m}>0
and
a_{N,m}>0
.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

It is crucial for the computations throughout the rest of this paper to highlight that

\begin{array}[t]{ll}\displaystyle\varepsilon^{4}\int_{M}u\,P_{g+\varepsilon^{2}h}u\ dv_{g}&\!\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{4}\int_{M}(\Delta_{g}u)^{2}dv_{g}+\varepsilon^{2}\int_{M}b_{N,m}|\nabla u|^{2}dv_{g}+\int_{M}a_{N,m}u^{2}\ dv_{g}\\
&\qquad\qquad\displaystyle+\varepsilon^{4}\int_{M}u\,\varphi(u)\ dv_{g}+\frac{N-4}{2}\int_{M}(\varepsilon^{4}f_{0}+\varepsilon^{2}f_{2})u^{2}dv_{g}.\end{array}

It is easy to see then that for any
\delta>0
, we can pick
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that for any
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
and for any
u\in H^{2}(M)
we have that

\begin{array}[t]{ll}\displaystyle(1+\delta)^{-1}\left(\varepsilon^{4}\int_{M}(\Delta_{g}u)^{2}dv_{g}+\varepsilon^{2}\int_{M}b_{N,m}|\nabla u|^{2}dv_{g}+\int_{M}a_{N,m}u^{2}\ dv_{g}\right)\\
\displaystyle\qquad\leq\varepsilon^{4}\int_{M}u\,P_{g+\varepsilon^{2}h}u\,dv_{g}\\
\displaystyle\qquad\leq(1+\delta)\left(\varepsilon^{4}\int_{M}(\Delta_{g}u)^{2}dv_{g}+\varepsilon^{2}\int_{M}b_{N,m}|\nabla u|^{2}dv_{g}+\int_{M}a_{N,m}u^{2}\ dv_{g}\right).\end{array}

We will use the previous remark in next sections to obtain estimates for
\varepsilon^{4}\int_{M}u\,P_{g+\varepsilon^{2}h}u\ dv_{g}
by using estimates for the simpler expression
\varepsilon^{4}\int_{M}(\Delta_{g}u)^{2}dv_{g}+\varepsilon^{2}\int_{M}b_{N,m}|\nabla u|^{2}dv_{g}+\int_{M}a_{N,m}u^{2}\ dv_{g}
.

In this section we establish some assumptions in order to study the problem of constant

-curvature on a product manifold.
Again, we consider a closed

(M,g)

We consider

For

\displaystyle\quad\quad\,\,\int_{M}\!\!\big(\varepsilon^{4}(\Delta u)^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}{b_{N,m}}|\nabla u|^{2}+a_{N,m}u^{2}+\varepsilon^{4}\Phi(u)+\varepsilon^{2}F(x,\varepsilon)u^{2}\big)\,dv_{g}=\int_{M}(u^{+})^{q+1}dv_{g}\bigg\}\end{array}

Lemma 3.2

Assume that

and if

The functional

\displaystyle\left\langle(J_{\varepsilon,q}^{g,+})^{\prime}(u),v\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\!\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{n}}\left(\!\varepsilon^{4}\!\int_{M}\Delta_{g}u\Delta_{g}v\,dv_{g}+b_{N,m}\varepsilon^{2}\!\int_{M}\nabla u\nabla v\,dv_{g}+a_{N,m}\!\int_{M}\!u\,v\,dv_{g}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{n}}\!\left(\!\varepsilon^{4}\int_{M}\varphi(u)v\,dv_{g}+\varepsilon^{2}\!\int_{M}\left(\varepsilon^{2}f_{0}+f_{2}\right)u\,v\,dv_{g}-\int_{M}(u^{+})^{q-1}u\,v\,dv_{g}\!\right),

for all

Moreover, if

, then

Proof.

First, we write
J^{g,+}_{\varepsilon,q}=\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon^{n}}\left(I^{1}_{\varepsilon}-I^{2}_{\varepsilon}\right)

It is easy to see that for all

Next, we show

Let
\left\{u_{j}\right\}\subset H^{2}(M)

, strongly in

as

Since

, there exists a subsequence of

By Hölder’s inequality, we get

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle{I^{2}_{\varepsilon}}^{\prime}(u_{j}),v\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{M}\left|(u_{j}^{+})^{q-1}u_{j}v\right|\ dv_{g}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{M}|u_{j}|^{q}|v|\ dv_{g}

∎

From the previous lemma, it follows that positive solutions of (

\mathcal{N}^{g}_{\varepsilon}
and are critical points for

.

Lemma 3.3

\varepsilon
small enough, the critical points of

Proof.

Let

be a critical point of

.
Then

Since the embedding of

By regularity theory (see

Finally, by the theorem of Gursky and Malchiodi

∎

By definition, a sequence

of functions in

.

, if for any Palais-Smale sequence

Theorem 3.4

Assume that
q>1
and if

. Then,

restricted to
\mathcal{N}^{g}_{\varepsilon}

Proof.

Note that if

then
J^{g,+}_{\varepsilon,q}(u)\geq 0
.
Let
\left\{u_{i}\right\}
be a Palais-Smale sequence in
\mathcal{N}^{g}_{\varepsilon}

Then, for

O(1)+o(1)\|u_{i}\|_{H^{2}(M)}\begin{array}[t]{ll}=(q+1)J^{g,+}_{\varepsilon,q}(u_{i})-\langle(J^{g,+}_{\varepsilon,q})^{\prime}(u_{i}),u_{i}\rangle\\
\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{n}}\bigg(\frac{q-1}{2}\int_{M}\big(\varepsilon^{4}(\Delta u)^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}b_{N,m}|\nabla u|^{2}+a_{N,m}u^{2}\big)\,dv_{g}\\
\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\varepsilon^{4}q\int_{M}\varphi(u)\,u\,dv_{g}+\frac{q-1}{2}\int_{M}\varepsilon^{2}F(\varepsilon,x)u^{2}dv_{g}\bigg)\\
\displaystyle\geq C\|u_{i}\|^{2}_{H^{2}(M)},\end{array}

where

.
It follows that

is bounded in
H^{2}(M)
and in consequence, there is a subsequence, still denoted

which converges weakly to a function

We have

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{4}\int_{M}|\Delta_{g}u_{i}|^{2}\,dv_{g}+b_{N,m}\ \varepsilon^{2}\int_{M}|\nabla u_{i}|^{2}\,dv_{g}+a_{N,m}\int_{M}u_{i}^{2}\,dv_{g}\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt

for all

4.
The limit equation and functionals on
\mathbb{R}^{n}

In this section we introduce the limit problem.
Recall that in the equation
2<q+1<2_{n}^{\sharp}

if

For

Let

\mathcal{N}(\alpha,\beta,q):=\bigg\{u\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\setminus\{0\}:2J_{\alpha,\beta}(u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u|^{q+1}dx\bigg\}.

(4.1)

\displaystyle m_{\alpha,\beta}

\displaystyle=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{N}(\alpha,\beta,q)}\frac{q-1}{2(q+1)}\|u\|_{q+1}^{q+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{q-1}{2(q+1)}\left(\inf_{u\in\mathcal{N}(\alpha,\beta,q)}\|u\|_{q+1}^{q-1}\right)^{\frac{q+1}{q-1}}

\displaystyle m^{+}_{\alpha,\beta}

\displaystyle=\frac{q-1}{2(q+1)}\left(\inf_{u\in\mathcal{N}^{+}(\alpha,\beta,q)}\|u^{+}\|_{q+1}^{q-1}\right)^{\frac{q+1}{q-1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{q-1}{q+1}\ 2^{\frac{2}{q-1}}\left(\inf_{u\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\ u^{+}\neq 0}Y^{+}_{\alpha,\beta,q}(u)\right)^{\frac{q+1}{q-1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{q-1}{q+1}\ 2^{\frac{2}{q-1}}\left(\inf_{u\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\|u^{+}\|_{q+1}=1}J_{\alpha,\beta}(u)\right)^{\frac{q+1}{q-1}}.

Remark 4.1
.

It follows easily from the previous observations that
m_{\alpha,\beta}\leq m^{+}_{\alpha,\beta}
.
But since we have a minimizer for

which is a positive function it follows that actually

Throughout the paper, we will use the following notation:
B(0,r)
is the ball in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
centered at
0

r
.

Given
\varepsilon>0
and

u_{\varepsilon}(x)=u(\varepsilon^{-1}x)

satisfies the following properties:

\Delta u_{\varepsilon}(x)=\varepsilon^{-2}\Delta u(\varepsilon^{-1}x),\quad\text{and}\quad|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(x)|^{2}=\varepsilon^{-2}|\nabla u(\varepsilon^{-1}x)|^{2}.

Note also that for any function
f
we have for any

,

\int_{B(0,\varepsilon r)}f_{\varepsilon}(x)\,dx=\varepsilon^{n}\int_{B(0.r)}f(x)\,dx,

and, in consequence,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f_{\varepsilon}(x)\,dx=\varepsilon^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\,dx.

Therefore

Volterrra-type Operators from analytic Morrey spaces to Bloch space The article was partially supported by Special Fund of Colleges and Universities in Fujian Province (No: JK2012010) and Natural Science Foundation of Fujian Province (No:2009J01004), China.

Abstract
In this note, we study the boundedness and compactness of integral operators I g I_{g} and T g T_{g} from analytic Morrey spaces to Bloch space. Furthermore, the norm and essential norm of those operators are given. Keywords Analytic Morrey space; Bloch space; Volterra type operator; essential norm 2010 MR Subject Classification 47B38, 30H30, 30H99

Introduction

Let
\mathbbm{D}=\{z:|z|<1\}
and
\partial\mathbbm{D}=\{z:|z|=1\}
denote respectively the open unit disc and the unit
circle in the complex plane
\mathbbm{C}
. Let
H(\mathbbm{D})
be the space of all analytic functions on

\mathbbm{D}
and
dm(z)=\frac{1}{\pi}dxdy
the normalized area Lebesgue measure.

The aim of this paper is to characterize the boundedness and compactness of two Volterra type operators
I_{g}
and
T_{g}
from the analytic Morrey spaces
\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}
to the classical Bloch space
B
, and from the little analytic Morrey spaces
\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}_{0}
to the little Bloch space
B_{0}
. Also, wee estimate the essential norm of
I_{g}
and
T_{g}
.

Morrey
space was initially introduced in 1938 by Morrey
[
20
]
to show that certain systems of partial differential equations (PDEs) had Hölder continuous solutions. In the past,
Morrey
space has been studied heavily in different areas. For example, Adams and Xiao studied
Morrey
spaces which is defined on Euclidean spaces
\mathbb{R}^{n}
by potential theory and Hausdorff capacity in
[
3
,
4
]
. But here we will be mostly interested in the analytic
Morrey
spaces
\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}
in the unit disk. It was introduced and studied by Wu and Xie in
[
29
]
.

For an arc
I\subset\partial\mathbb{D}
, let
|I|=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{I}|d\zeta|
be the normalized arc length of
I
,

f_{I}=\frac{1}{|I|}\int_{I}f(\zeta)\frac{|d\zeta|}{2\pi},f\in H(\mathbbm{D}),

and
S(I)
be the Carleson box based on
I
with

S(I)=\{z\in\mathbbm{D}:1-|I|\leq|z|<1,\frac{z}{|z|}\in I\}.

Cleatly, if
I=\partial\mathbb{D}
, then
S(I)=\mathbb{D}
.

Denote
\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}(\mathbbm{D})
the analytic
Morrey
spaces of all analytic functions
f\in H^{2}
on
\mathbb{D}
such that

\sup_{I\subset\partial\mathbbm{D}}\big(\frac{1}{|I|^{\lambda}}\int_{I}|f(\zeta)-f_{I}|^{2}\frac{|d\zeta|}{2\pi}\big)^{1/2}<\infty,

where
0<\lambda\leq 1
and the Hardy space
H^{2}
consists of analytic functions f in
\mathbb{D}
satisfying

\sup_{0<r<1}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f(re^{i\theta})|^{2}\,d\theta<\infty.

Similarly to the relation between
BMOA
space and
VMOA
space, we have that
f\in\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}_{0}(\mathbbm{D})
, the little
Morrey
spaces, if
f\in\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}(\mathbbm{D})
and

\lim_{|I|\rightarrow 0}\big(\frac{1}{|I|^{\lambda}}\int_{I}|f(\zeta)-f_{I}|^{2}\frac{|d\zeta|}{2\pi}\big)^{1/2}=0.

Xiao and Xu
[
33
]
studied the composition operators of
\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}
spaces. Cascante, Fàbrega and Ortega
[
12
]
studied the Corona theorem of
\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}
. It is a useful tools for the study of harmonic analysis and partial differential equations, We refer the readers to
[
20
,
23
,
37
]
.

The following lemma gives some equivalent conditions of
\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}(\mathbbm{D})
(see Theorem 3.21 of
[
32
]
or Theorem 3.1 of
[
34
]
).

Lemma 1.1

Suppose that
0<\lambda<1
and
f\in H(\mathbbm{D})
. Let
a\in\mathbbm{D}
,
\varphi_{a}(z)=\frac{a-z}{1-\overline{a}z}
. Then the
following statements are equivalent.

(i)~f\in\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}(\mathbbm{D})
;

(ii)~\displaystyle\sup_{I\subset\partial\mathbbm{D}}\frac{1}{|I|^{\lambda}}\int_{S(I)}|f^{\prime}(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})dm(z)<\infty
;

(iii)~\displaystyle\sup_{a\in\mathbbm{D}}(1-|a|^{2})^{1-\lambda}\int_{\mathbbm{D}}|f^{\prime}(z)|^{2}(1-|\varphi_{a}(z)|^{2})dm(z)<\infty
.

From the lemma above, we can define the norm of function

f\in\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}(\mathbbm{D})
and equivalent formula as follows

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|f(0)|+\sup_{I\subset\partial\mathbbm{D}}\big(\frac{1}{|I|^{\lambda}}\int_{S(I)}|f^{\prime}(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})dm(z)\big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle|f(0)|+\sup_{a\in\mathbbm{D}}\big((1-|a|^{2})^{1-\lambda}\int_{\mathbbm{D}}|f^{\prime}(z)|^{2}(1-|\varphi_{a}(z)|^{2})dm(z)\big)^{1/2}.

It is known that
\mathcal{L}^{2,1}(\mathbbm{D})=BMOA
and if
0<\lambda<1
,
BMOA\subsetneq\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}(\mathbbm{D})
. For more information on
BMOA
and
VMOA
, see
[
15
]
.

A function
f
analytic on the unit disk is said to belong to the
Bloch
space
B
if

\|f\|_{B}=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\{(1-|z|^{2})|f^{\prime}(z)|\}<\infty,

and to the little
Bloch
space
B_{0}
if
f\in B
and

\lim_{|z|\to 1^{-}}(1-|z|^{2})|f^{\prime}(z)|=0.

It is well known that
B
is a Banach space under the norm

\||f\||_{B}=|f(0)|+\|f\|_{B}
and
B_{0}
is a closed subspace of
B
. See
[
5
]
. By
[
8
,
31
]
, together with Lemma 2.1 in
[
7
]
, we have the following equivalent statements about the norm of
f\in B
.

Proposition 1.1

For all
p\in(1,\infty)
,

\displaystyle\||f\||_{B}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle|f(0)|+\sup_{a\in\mathbbm{D}}\big(\int_{\mathbbm{D}}|f^{\prime}(z)|^{2}(1-|\varphi_{a}(z)|^{2})^{p}dm(z)\big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle|f(0)|+\sup_{I\subset\partial\mathbbm{D}}\big(\frac{1}{|I|^{p}}\int_{S(I)}|f^{\prime}(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{p}dm(z)\big)^{1/2}.

Suppose that
g:\mathbbm{D}\to\mathbb{C}
is a holomorphic map. The integral operator
T_{g}
, called Volterra-type operator, is defined as

T_{g}f(z)=\int_{0}^{z}f(w)g^{\prime}(w)dw,~~~z\in\mathbbm{D},~~f\in H(\mathbbm{D}).

In
[
22
]
Pommerenke introduced the operator
T_{g}
and showed that
T_{g}
is a bounded operator on the Hardy space
H^{2}
if and only if
g\in BMOA
.

The companion integral operator
I_{g}
is spontaneously defined as

I_{g}f(z)=\int_{0}^{z}f^{\prime}(w)g(w)dw,~~~z\in\mathbbm{D},~~f\in H(\mathbbm{D}).

The boundedness, compactness or essential norm of
T_{g}
and
I_{g}
between spaces of analytic functions were investigated by many authors.
Aleman and Siskakis in
[
1
]
studied the integral operator
T_{g}
on the Bergman space, and then Aleman considered with Cima
T_{g}
acting on the Hardy space in
[
2
]
. Siskakis and Zhao
[
24
]
also investigated
T_{g}
on the space
BMOA
.
T_{g}
on the
Q_{p}
space was studied by Xiao in
[
30
]
. Li and Stević in
[
19
]
studied the boundedness and compactness of
T_{g}
and
I_{g}
on the
Zygmund
Spaces and the little
Zygmund
spaces. Cinstantin in
[
13
]
considered the boundedness and compactness of
T_{g}
on
Fock
spaces. Ye in
[
35
]
studied products of Volterra-type operators and composition operators on logarithmic
Bloch
space. Ye and Gao in
[
36
]
gave the boundedness and compactness of
T_{g}
between different weighted
Bloch
spaces.

There are some articles about the integral operator acting on
Morrey
space. For example, Wu in
[
28
]
considered
T_{g}
from Hardy to analytic
Morrey
spaces, Li, Liu and Lou
[
18

characterized the boundedness and essential norm of
T_{g}
and
I_{g}
on analytic
Morrey

Now, we need two spaces. Let
\alpha>-1
. Recall that
f\in H(\mathbbm{D})
belongs to the weighted space
H_{\alpha}^{\infty}
if it satisfies with

\sup_{z\in\mathbbm{D}}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}|f(z)|<\infty.

When
\alpha>-1
,
H_{\alpha}^{\infty}
endowed with the norm
\|f\|_{\alpha}=\sup_{z\in\mathbbm{D}}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}|f(z)|
is a Banach space. This space is connected with the study of growth conditions of analytic functions and was also studied in detail , see
[
9
,
10
,
25
,
26
]
.
The space
H_{\alpha}^{\infty}
is used in the characterizations of the boundedness and essential norm of
I_{g}
. Then we conclude the boundedness and essential norm of
T_{g}
by introducing the following
Bloch-Morrey
type space.

Definition 1.1

Let
0<\lambda\leq 1
and
p>1
. The
Bloch-Morrey
type space
B\mathcal{L}^{p,\lambda}
is the set of all
g\in H(\mathbbm{D})
such that

M(g)=\sup_{I\subset\partial\mathbbm{D}}\big(\frac{1}{|I|^{p-\lambda+1}}\int_{S(I)}|g^{\prime}(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{p}dm(z)\big)^{1/2}<\infty.

The corresponding subspace
B\mathcal{L}^{p,\lambda}_{0}
, the little
Bloch-Morrey
type space, can be defined as

B\mathcal{L}^{p,\lambda}_{0}=\{g\in B\mathcal{L}^{p,\lambda},~~\lim_{|I|\rightarrow 0}\big(\frac{1}{|I|^{p-\lambda+1}}\int_{S(I)}|g^{\prime}(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{p}dm(z)\big)^{1/2}=0\}.

It is easy to prove that
B\mathcal{L}^{p,\lambda}
is a Banach space under the norm

\|g\|_{B\mathcal{L}^{p,\lambda}}=|g(0)|+M(g).

Clearly,
B\mathcal{L}^{p,1}=Bloch
. From
[
7
]
, we know that
\|g\|_{B\mathcal{L}^{p,\lambda}}
is comparable with the norm

|g(0)|+\sup_{a\in\mathbbm{D}}\big(\int_{\mathbbm{D}}(\frac{1-|a|^{2}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{2}})^{p+1-\lambda}|g^{\prime}(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{p}dm(z)\big)^{1/2}.

Notations
: For two functions
F
and
G
, if there is a constant
C>0
dependent only on indexes
p,\lambda...
such that
F\leq CG
, then we say that
F\lesssim G
. Furthermore, denote that
F\thickapprox G
(
F
is comparable with
G
) whenever
F\lesssim G\lesssim F
.

2
\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}
vs
B

Evidently, when
0<\lambda<1
,
BMOA\subsetneq\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}(\mathbbm{D})
. On the other hand
BMOA\subsetneq B
. Does
\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}
and
B
have the inclusion relation ? We claim the answer is negative by the following two proposition. This makes our job more signality.

Proposition 2.1

\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}\nsubseteq B.

Considering
g(z)=(\log\frac{1}{1-z})^{2}
, which is obviously not a
Bloch
function. We claim that
g(z)\in\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}
. Indeed,

\displaystyle\sup_{a\in\mathbbm{D}}(1-|a|^{2})^{1-\lambda}\int_{\mathbbm{D}}|g^{\prime}(z)|^{2}(1-|\rho_{a}(z)|^{2})dm(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sup_{a\in\mathbbm{D}}(1-|a|^{2})^{1-\lambda}\int_{\mathbbm{D}}|\frac{1}{1-z}|^{2}\log^{2}\frac{1}{1-|z|}(1-|\rho_{a}(z)|^{2})dm(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbbm{D}}|\frac{1}{1-z}|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{1-\lambda}\log^{2}\frac{1}{1-|z|}dm(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|\frac{1}{1-re^{i\theta}}|^{2}d\theta(1-r^{2})^{1-\lambda}\log^{2}\frac{1}{1-r}dr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}(1-r^{2})^{-\lambda}\log^{2}\frac{1}{1-r}dr<\infty.

This finish the proof.

Conversely, the function
\displaystyle f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}z^{2^{n}}
is a
Bloch
function (see
[
6
]
) and it is well known that it has a radial limit almost nowhere. Consequently,
f
does not belong to any of the
Hardy
spaces and so
f\notin\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}
. So we have the following proposition.

Proposition 2.2

B\nsubseteq\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}.

In this section, we prove the boundedness and estimate the norms of

and

Lemma 3.1

Let
0<\lambda<1
and
b\in\mathbbm{D}
. We set functions
f_{b}(z)
and
F_{b}(z)
as

f_{b}(z)=(1-|b|^{2})^{\frac{1-\lambda}{2}}(\rho_{b}(z)-b),~~~F_{b}(z)=(1-|b|^{2})(1-\overline{b}z)^{\frac{\lambda-3}{2}}.

then
f_{b}(z)\in\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}(\mathbbm{D})
and
F_{b}(z)\in\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}(\mathbbm{D})
. Particularly, we have
f_{b}(z)\in\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}_{0}(\mathbbm{D})
and
F_{b}(z)\in\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}_{0}(\mathbbm{D})
. Moreover,
\|f_{b}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}}\lesssim 1
,

\|F_{b}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}}\lesssim 1
.

See Lemma 4 in
[
18
]
. From its proof, we futher deduce that

f_{b}(z)\in\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}_{0}(\mathbbm{D})
and
F_{b}(z)\in\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}_{0}(\mathbbm{D})
.

we get a result about the growth rate of functions in

\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}(\mathbbm{D})
from
[
18
]
.

Lemma 3.2

Let
0<\lambda<1
. If
f\in\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}(\mathbbm{D})
. then

|f(z)|\lesssim\frac{\|f\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}}}{(1-|z|^{2})^{\frac{1-\lambda}{2}}},~~~~~z\in\mathbbm{D}.

We first consider the boundedness of
I_{g}:\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}\rightarrow B
.

Theorem 3.1

Let
0<\lambda<1
and
g\in H(\mathbbm{D})
. Then
I_{g}:\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}\rightarrow B
is bounded if and only if

g\in H_{\frac{\lambda-1}{2}}^{\infty}.
Moreover the operator norm satisfies

\|I_{g}\|\thickapprox\|g\|_{\frac{\lambda-1}{2}}.

For
0<\lambda<1
,
1<2-\lambda
, we set
B=Q_{2-\lambda}.

Sufficiency: let
g\in H_{\frac{\lambda-1}{2}}^{\infty}
. For any
f\in\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}(\mathbbm{D})
, we have

\displaystyle\|I_{g}f\|_{B}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\sup_{a\in\mathbbm{D}}\big(\int_{\mathbbm{D}}|f^{\prime}(z)|^{2}|g(z)|^{2}(1-|\varphi_{a}(z)|^{2})^{2-\lambda}dm(z)\big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{a\in\mathbbm{D}}\big((1-|a|^{2})^{1-\lambda}\int_{\mathbbm{D}}|f^{\prime}(z)|^{2}(1-|\varphi_{a}(z)|^{2})|g(z)|^{2}(\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{2}})^{1-\lambda}dm(z)\big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|g\|_{\frac{\lambda-1}{2}}\cdot\|f\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}}.

These inequalities imply
I_{g}
is bounded and
\|I_{g}\|\lesssim\|g\|_{\frac{\lambda-1}{2}}.

Necessity: let
I_{g}
is bounded. For any
b\in\mathbbm{D}
, considering functions
f_{b}(z)
in Lemma
3.1
, we have
\|f_{b}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}}\lesssim 1
. Thus

\displaystyle\|I_{g}\|

\displaystyle\gtrsim

\displaystyle\|I_{g}f_{b}\|_{B}

(3.1)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\sup_{a\in\mathbbm{D}}\big(\int_{\mathbbm{D}}|f_{b}^{\prime}(z)|^{2}|g(z)|^{2}(1-|\varphi_{a}(z)|^{2})^{2-\lambda}dm(z)\big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{a\in\mathbbm{D}}\big(\int_{\mathbbm{D}}\frac{(1-|b|^{2})^{1+\lambda}}{|1-\overline{b}z|^{4}}|g(z)|^{2}(1-|\varphi_{a}(z)|^{2})^{1+1-\lambda}dm(z)\big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\big(\int_{\mathbbm{D}}\frac{(1-|b|^{2})^{2}}{|1-\overline{b}z|^{4}}|g(z)|^{2}(\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{|1-\overline{b}z|^{2}})^{1-\lambda}(1-|\rho_{b}(z)|^{2})dm(z)\big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\int_{\mathbbm{D}}|\rho_{b}^{\prime}(z)|^{2}|g(z)|^{2}(\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{|1-\overline{b}z|^{2}})^{1-\lambda}(1-|\rho_{b}(z)|^{2})dm(z)\big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\int_{\mathbbm{D}}|g(\rho_{b}(w))|^{2}(\frac{1-|\rho_{b}(w)|^{2}}{|1-\overline{b}\rho_{b}(w)|^{2}})^{1-\lambda}(1-|w|^{2})dm(w)\big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\gtrsim

\displaystyle|\frac{g(b)}{(1-|b|^{2})^{\frac{1-\lambda}{2}}}|.

where we used Lemma 4.12 of
[
38
]
in the last inequality. Since
b
is arbitrary, we have
\|I_{g}\|\gtrsim\|g\|_{\frac{\lambda-1}{2}}.
The proof is finished.

With the space
B\mathcal{L}^{p,\lambda}
, we can establish the boundedness of
T_{g}:\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}\rightarrow B
as the following theorem.

Theorem 3.2

Suppose that
0<\lambda<1
and
g\in H(\mathbbm{D})
. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

T_{g}:\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}\rightarrow B
is bounded;

(ii)

g\in B\mathcal{L}^{p,\lambda}
for all
p\in(1,\infty)
;

(iii)

g\in B\mathcal{L}^{p,\lambda}
for some
p\in(1,\infty)
.

Moreover,

\|T_{g}\|\thickapprox M(g).

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
. Suppose that
T_{g}:\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}\rightarrow B
is bounded. For any
I\subset\partial\mathbbm{D}
, let
b=(1-|I|)\zeta\in\mathbbm{D}
, where
\zeta
is the centre of
I
. Then

(1-|b|^{2})\approx|1-\overline{b}z|\approx|I|,~~z\in S(I).

Considering the functions
F_{b}(z)
in Lemma
3.1
,
\|F_{b}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}}\lesssim 1
. This together with Proposition
1.1
, we obtain that for any
p\in(1,\infty)
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|I|^{p-\lambda+1}}\int_{S(I)}|g^{\prime}(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{p}dm(z)

\displaystyle\thickapprox

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|I|^{p}}\int_{S(I)}|F_{b}(z)|^{2}|g^{\prime}(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{p}dm(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|T_{g}F_{b}\|_{B}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|T_{g}\|^{2}\|F_{b}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|T_{g}\|^{2}.

Since
I
is arbitrary, we have
M(g)\lesssim\|T_{g}\|
.

(ii)\Rightarrow(iii)
. It is obvious.

(iii)\Rightarrow(i)
. Suppose that fixed
p\in(1,\infty)
and
M(g)<\infty
. For
f\in\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}(\mathbbm{D})
and any
I\subset\partial\mathbbm{D}
, from Lemma
3.2
, it follows that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|T_{g}f\|_{B}&\approx&\sup_{I\subset\partial\mathbbm{D}}\big(\frac{1}{|I|^{p}}\int_{S(I)}|f(z)|^{2}|g^{\prime}(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{p}dm(z)\big)^{1/2}~~~~~~~~~~\\
&\lesssim&\|f\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}}\cdot\sup_{I\subset\partial\mathbbm{D}}\big(\frac{1}{|I|^{p}}\int_{S(I)}|g^{\prime}(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{p+\lambda-1}dm(z)\big)^{1/2}.\end{split}

(3.2)

To the end, for a given subarc
I
of
\partial\mathbbm{D}
, let
\mathcal{D}_{n}(I)
represent the set of
2^{n}
subarcs of length

2^{-n}|I|
obtained by
n
successive bipartition of
I
. For each
J\in\mathcal{D}_{n}(I)
write
T(J)
for the top half Carleson box of
S(J)
, i.e.,

T(J)=\{z\in S(J):\frac{z}{|z|}\in J,1-|J|<|z|<1-\frac{|J|}{2}\}.

Then

S(I)=\mathop{\bigcup}\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathop{\bigcup}\limits_{J\in\mathcal{D}_{n}(I)}T(J).

Noting that
z\in T(J)
,
1-|z|\approx|J|
,
one has

\displaystyle\int_{S(I)}|g^{\prime}(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{p+\lambda-1}dm(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{J\in\mathcal{D}_{n}(I)}\int_{T(J)}|g^{\prime}(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{p+\lambda-1}dm(z)

(3.3)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{J\in\mathcal{D}_{n}(I)}\int_{T(J)}|J|^{\lambda-1}|g^{\prime}(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{p}dm(z)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{J\in\mathcal{D}_{n}(I)}\int_{S(J)}|J|^{\lambda-1}|g^{\prime}(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{p}dm(z)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{J\in\mathcal{D}_{n}(I)}M(g)^{2}|J|^{\lambda-1}|J|^{p-\lambda+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}2^{n}M(g)^{2}|J|^{p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(2^{n})^{1-p}M(g)^{2}|I|^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle M(g)^{2}|I|^{p}.

Now invoking
(\ref{32})
,

\displaystyle\|T_{g}f\|_{B}\lesssim M(g)\cdot\|f\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}}

As a result,
\|T_{g}\|\lesssim M(g)
.

. Then

4
Essential norm of
I_{g}
and
T_{g}
from
\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}
to
B

Let
X
and
Y
be Banach spaces. The essential norm of a bounded operator
T:X\rightarrow Y
,
\|T\|_{e,X\rightarrow Y}
, is
defined as the distance from
T
to the space of compact operators,

\|T\|_{e,X\rightarrow Y}=inf\{\|T-K\|_{X\rightarrow Y}:~~~K~is~any~compact~operator\},

where the norm of
T
is denoted by
\|\cdot\|_{X\rightarrow Y}
.

Since that
T
is compact if and only if
\|T\|_{e,X\rightarrow Y}=0
, then the estimation of
\|T\|_{e,X\rightarrow Y}

indicates the condition for
T
to be compact. For some recent results related to the essential norm , see

[
16
,
17
,
27
,
18
]
. and the references therein.

In this section, we estimate the essential norm of
I_{g}
and
T_{g}
from
\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}
to
B
. We need some auxiliary results.

Lemma 4.1

Let
0<\lambda<1
. For
0<t<1
,
z\in\mathbbm{D}
,
f_{t}(z)=f(tz)
. If
f\in\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}(\mathbbm{D})
, then

f_{t}\in\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}_{0}(\mathbbm{D})
and
\|f_{t}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}}\leq\|f\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}}
.

If
f\in\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}(\mathbbm{D})
and
0<t<1
, then,
f_{t}
is analytic on the closed unit disk
\bar{\mathbbm{D}}
. A simple computation shows that
f_{t}\in\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}_{0}(\mathbbm{D})
.
In addition, by Poisson formula, we have

f_{t}(z)=\int_{0}^{2\pi}f(ze^{i\theta})\frac{1-t^{2}}{|e^{i\theta}-t|^{2}}\frac{d\theta}{2\pi},~~~z\in\mathbbm{D}.

So,

\displaystyle\sup_{a\in\mathbbm{D}}(1-|a|^{2})^{1-\lambda}\int_{\mathbbm{D}}|f_{t}^{\prime}(z)|^{2}(1-|\varphi_{a}(z)|^{2})dm(z)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{a\in\mathbbm{D}}(1-|a|^{2})^{1-\lambda}\int_{\mathbbm{D}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f^{\prime}(ze^{i\theta})|^{2}\frac{1-t^{2}}{|e^{i\theta}-t|^{2}}\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}(1-|\varphi_{a}(z)|^{2})dm(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\sup_{a\in\mathbbm{D}}(1-|a|^{2})^{1-\lambda}\int_{\mathbbm{D}}|f^{\prime}(ze^{i\theta})|^{2}(1-|\varphi_{a}(z)|^{2})dm(z)\frac{1-t^{2}}{|e^{i\theta}-t|^{2}}\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{a\in\mathbbm{D}}(1-|a|^{2})^{1-\lambda}\int_{\mathbbm{D}}|f^{\prime}(z)|^{2}(1-|\varphi_{a}(z)|^{2})dm(z)\cdot\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{1-t^{2}}{|e^{i\theta}-t|^{2}}\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{a\in\mathbbm{D}}(1-|a|^{2})^{1-\lambda}\int_{\mathbbm{D}}|f^{\prime}(z)|^{2}(1-|\varphi_{a}(z)|^{2})dm(z).

Thus,
\|f_{t}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}}\leq\|f\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}}
.

By Lemma
3.2
and standard arguments(see, e.g.,
[
11
]
,Proposition 3.11), the following lemma follows.

Lemma 4.2

Assume that g is an analytic function on
\mathbbm{D}
. Then
T_{g}(orI_{g}):\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}\rightarrow B
is compact if and only if
T_{g}(orI_{g}):\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}\rightarrow B
is bounded, and for any bounded sequence
(f_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}
which converges to zero uniformly on
\mathbbm{D}
as
k\to\infty
,
\|T_{g}f_{k}\|_{B}\to 0(or~\|I_{g}f_{k}\|_{B}\to 0)
as
k\to\infty
.

Lemma 4.3

Suppose that
0<\lambda<1
and
p>1
. For
g\in B\mathcal{L}^{p,\lambda}
, define the following operators
T_{g,r}:\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}\rightarrow B
:

T_{g,r}f(z)=\int_{0}^{z}f(rw)g^{\prime}(w)dw.

where
r\in(0,1)
. Then
T_{g,r}
is compact.

Let
\{f_{n}\}
be such that
\|f_{n}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}}\leq 1
and
f_{n}\to 0
uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbbm{D}
as
n\to\infty
. We are required to show that
\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|T_{g,r}f_{n}\|_{B}=0.

In fact, Since
\|g\|_{B}\lesssim M(g)
, we have
|g^{\prime}(z)|\lesssim\frac{M(g)}{1-|z|^{2}}
. From
\|f_{n}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}}\leq 1
and Lemma
3.2
, it yields that
(1-|r|^{2})^{(1-\lambda)/2}|f_{n}(rz)|\lesssim 1
.
Thus

\displaystyle\|T_{g,r}f_{n}\|_{B}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in\mathbbm{D}}(1-|z|^{2})|f_{n}(rz)||g^{\prime}(z)|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle M(g)\sup_{z\in\mathbbm{D}}\frac{1}{(1-r^{2})^{(1-\lambda)/2}}.

Accordingly, by Dominated Convergence Theorem one reaches
\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|T_{g_{r}}f_{n}\|_{B}=0.

Now, we present the main result of this section.

Theorem 4.1

Suppose
0<\lambda<1
and
g\in H(\mathbbm{D})
. If
I_{g}:\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}\rightarrow B
is bounded, then

\|I_{g}\|_{e,\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}\to B}\thickapprox\|g\|_{\frac{\lambda-1}{2}}.

Choose the zero operator
O:\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}\rightarrow B:f\mapsto 0
. Since
O
is compact and
\|O\|=0
, we get

\|I_{g}\|_{e,\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}\to B}=\inf_{K}\|I_{g}-K\|\leq\|I_{g}\|\lesssim\|g\|_{\frac{\lambda-1}{2}}.

Conversely, choose the sequence
\{b_{n}\}\subset\mathbbm{D}
such that
|b_{n}|\rightarrow 1
as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Considering the sequence of functions
f_{n}(z)=(1-|b_{n}|^{2})^{\frac{1-\lambda}{2}}(\rho_{b_{n}}(z)-b_{n})
, we obtain
\|f_{n}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}}\lesssim 1
by Lemma
3.1
.
By easy calculation,
f_{n}(z)=-(1-|b_{n}|^{2})^{\frac{1+\lambda}{2}}\int_{0}^{z}\frac{dw}{(1-\overline{b_{n}}z)^{2}}
, and thus
f_{n}
converges to zero uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbbm{D}
. Then
\|Kf_{n}\|_{B}\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
for any compact operator
K
. So

\|I_{g}-K\|\gtrsim\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|(I_{g}-K)f_{n}\|_{B}\geq\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}(\|I_{g}f_{n}\|_{B}-\|Kf_{n}\|_{B})\geq\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|I_{g}f_{n}\|_{B}.

By
(\ref{31})
, we have

\|I_{g}-K\|\gtrsim\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}|\frac{g(b_{n})}{(1-|b_{n}|^{2})^{\frac{1-\lambda}{2}}}|.

The arbitrary choice of the sequence
\{b_{n}\}
implies

\|I_{g}\|_{e,\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}\to B}\gtrsim\|g\|_{\frac{\lambda-1}{2}}.

Theorem 4.2

Suppose
0<\lambda<1
and
g\in H(\mathbbm{D})
. If
T_{g}:\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}\rightarrow B
is bounded, then

\|T_{g}\|_{e,\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}\to B}\thickapprox\limsup_{|a|\rightarrow 1}\big(\int_{\mathbbm{D}}(\frac{1-|a|^{2}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{2}})^{p+1-\lambda}|g^{\prime}(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{p}dm(z)\big)^{1/2}.

For any
r_{n}\in(0,1)
such that
r_{n}\rightarrow 1
as
n\rightarrow\infty
, we introduce
T_{g,r_{n}}:\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}\rightarrow B
which is compact. Let
s\in(0,1)
, we have

\displaystyle\|T_{g}\|_{e,\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}\to B}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|T_{g}-T_{g,r_{n}}\|

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\sup_{\|f\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}}=1}\|T_{g}-T_{g,r_{n}}\|_{B}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{\|f\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}}=1}\sup_{a\in\mathbbm{D}}\big(\int_{\mathbbm{D}}|f(z)-f(r_{n}z)|^{2}|g^{\prime}(z)|^{2}(1-|\varphi_{a}(z)|^{2})^{p+1-\lambda}dm(z)\big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{\|f\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}}=1}\sup_{|a|\leq s}\big(\int_{\mathbbm{D}}|f(z)-f(r_{n}z)|^{2}|g^{\prime}(z)|^{2}(1-|\varphi_{a}(z)|^{2})^{p+1-\lambda}dm(z)\big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle~~+

\displaystyle\sup_{\|f\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}}=1}\sup_{|a|>s}\big(\int_{\mathbbm{D}}|f(z)-f(r_{n}z)|^{2}|g^{\prime}(z)|^{2}(1-|\varphi_{a}(z)|^{2})^{p+1-\lambda}dm(z)\big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{\|f\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}}=1}\sup_{|a|\leq s}\big(\int_{\mathbbm{D}}|f(z)-f(r_{n}z)|^{2}|g^{\prime}(z)|^{2}(1-|\varphi_{a}(z)|^{2})^{p+1-\lambda}dm(z)\big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle~~+

\displaystyle 2\sup_{|a|>s}\int_{\mathbbm{D}}(\frac{1-|a|^{2}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{2}})^{p+1-\lambda}|g^{\prime}(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{p}dm(z)\big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\triangleq

\displaystyle K_{1}+K_{2}.

Since
|a|\leq s
is a closed set of
\mathbbm{D}
and
g\in B\mathcal{L}^{p,\lambda}
, the Dominated Convergence Therorem yields
K_{1}\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

Now, letting
n\rightarrow\infty
and then letting
s\rightarrow 1
, we get

\|T_{g}\|_{e,\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}\to B}\lesssim\limsup_{|a|\rightarrow 1}\big(\int_{\mathbbm{D}}(\frac{1-|a|^{2}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{2}})^{p+1-\lambda}|g^{\prime}(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{p}dm(z)\big)^{1/2}.

Conversely, Let
I_{n}\subset\partial\mathbbm{D}
such that
|I_{n}|\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.
\zeta_{n}
is the center of arc
I
and
b_{n}=(1-|I_{n}|)\zeta
, so

(1-|b_{n}|^{2})\approx|1-\overline{b_{n}}z|\approx|I_{n}|,~~z\in S(I_{n}).

Consider the function
F_{n}(z)=(1-|b_{n}|^{2})(1-\overline{b_{n}}z)^{\frac{\lambda-3}{2}}
. Then
\|F_{n}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}}\lesssim 1
and
F_{n}\to 0
uniformly on the compact subsets of
\mathbbm{D}
as
n\rightarrow\infty
by Lemma
3.1
. Thus
\|KF_{n}\|_{B}\rightarrow 0
for any compact operator
K
.
Therefore

\displaystyle\|T_{g}-K\|\gtrsim\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|(T_{g}-K)F_{n}\|_{B}\geq\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}(\|T_{g}F_{n}\|_{B}-\|KF_{n}\|_{B})\geq\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|T_{g}f_{n}\|_{B}

\displaystyle\gtrsim\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\big(\frac{1}{|I_{n}|^{p}}\int_{S(I_{n})}|F_{n}(z)|^{2}|g^{\prime}(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{p}dm(z)\big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\thickapprox\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\big(\frac{1}{|I_{n}|^{p+1-\lambda}}\int_{S(I_{n})}|g^{\prime}(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{p}dm(z)\big)^{1/2}.~~~

Since the sequence
\{I_{n}\}
is arbitrary, we conclude

\displaystyle\|T_{g}\|_{e,\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}\to B}

\displaystyle\gtrsim

\displaystyle\limsup_{|I|\rightarrow 0}\big(\frac{1}{|I|^{p+1-\lambda}}\int_{S(I)}|g^{\prime}(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{p}dm(z)\big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\thickapprox

\displaystyle\limsup_{|a|\rightarrow 1}\big(\int_{\mathbbm{D}}(\frac{1-|a|^{2}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{2}})^{p+1-\lambda}|g^{\prime}(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{p}dm(z)\big)^{1/2}.

This completes the proof.

We have the following corollary about their compactness.

Corollary 4.1

Suppose that
0<\lambda<1
and
p>1
. Then

(i)

I_{g}:\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}\rightarrow B
is compact if and only if
g=0
.

(ii)

T_{g}:\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}\rightarrow B
is compact if and only if
g\in B\mathcal{L}^{p,\lambda}_{0}
.

Theorem 5.1

Let
0<\lambda<1
and
g\in H(\mathbbm{D})
. Then
I_{g}:\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}_{0}\rightarrow B_{0}
is bounded if and only if

g\in H_{\frac{\lambda-1}{2}}^{\infty}.
Moreover,

\|I_{g}\|\thickapprox\|g\|_{\frac{\lambda-1}{2}}.

Necessity: assume that
I_{g}:\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}_{0}\rightarrow B_{0}
is bounded. Then it is clear that
I_{g}:\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}_{0}\rightarrow B
is bounded. The necessity of Theorem

3.1
, together with
f_{b}(z)\in\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}_{0}(\mathbbm{D})
, proves
g\in H_{\frac{\lambda-1}{2}}^{\infty}.

Sufficiency: let
g\in H_{\frac{\lambda-1}{2}}^{\infty}
. Then from Theorem
3.1
,
I_{g}:\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}\rightarrow B
is bounded and hence
I_{g}:\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}_{0}\rightarrow B
is bounded. It suffices to prove that for any
f\in\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}_{0}
,
I_{g}f\in B_{0}
. In fact, for any
f\in\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{|a|\to 1}\int_{\mathbbm{D}}|f^{\prime}(z)|^{2}|g(z)|^{2}(1-|\varphi_{a}(z)|^{2})^{2-\lambda}dm(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{|a|\to 1}(1-|a|^{2})^{1-\lambda}\int_{\mathbbm{D}}|f^{\prime}(z)|^{2}(1-|\varphi_{a}(z)|^{2})|g(z)|^{2}(\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{2}})^{1-\lambda}dm(z)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|g\|_{\frac{\lambda-1}{2}}\cdot\lim_{|a|\to 1}(1-|a|^{2})^{1-\lambda}\int_{\mathbbm{D}}|f^{\prime}(z)|^{2}(1-|\varphi_{a}(z)|^{2})dm(z)=0.

Consequently,
I_{g}:\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}_{0}\rightarrow B_{0}
is bounded.

Theorem 5.2

Suppose that
0<\lambda<1
and
g\in H(\mathbbm{D})
. If
I_{g}:\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}_{0}\rightarrow B_{0}
is bounded, then

\|I_{g}\|_{e,\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}_{0}\to B_{0}}\thickapprox\|g\|_{\frac{\lambda-1}{2}}.

As a matter of fact, if
g\in H_{\frac{\lambda-1}{2}}^{\infty}
, then for any
f\in\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}_{0}
,
I_{g}f\in B_{0}
, Since
f_{n}(z)\in\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}_{0}
(see Theorem
4.1
), we complete the proof as the same as in the proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Theorem 5.3

Suppose that
0<\lambda<1
and
g\in H(\mathbbm{D})
. Then the following conditions are
equivalent.

(i)

T_{g}:\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}_{0}\rightarrow B_{0}
is bounded;

(ii)

g\in B\mathcal{L}^{p,\lambda}_{0}
for all
p\in(1,\infty)
;

(iii)

g\in B\mathcal{L}^{p,\lambda}_{0}
for some
p\in(1,\infty)
.

Moreover,

\|T_{g}\|\thickapprox M(g).

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
. Suppose that
T_{g}
is bounded. For any
I\subset\partial\mathbbm{D}
, let
b=(1-|I|)\zeta
, where
\zeta
is the centre of
I
. Then

(1-|b|^{2})\approx|1-\overline{b}z|\approx|I|,~~z\in S(I).

Concerning the functions
F_{b}(z)
in Lemma
3.1
, we have
F_{b}(z)\in\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}_{0}(\mathbbm{D})
, thus
T_{g}(F_{b}(z))\in B_{0}
, Furthermore, from Proposition
1.1
it yields
g\in B\mathcal{L}^{p,\lambda}_{0}
.

(ii)\Rightarrow(iii)
. It is obvious.

(iii)\Rightarrow(i)
. Let fix
p\in(1,\infty)
and
g\in B\mathcal{L}^{p,\lambda}_{0}
. Then from Theorem
3.2
,
T_{g}:\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}\rightarrow B
is bounded and hence
T_{g}:\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}_{0}\rightarrow B
is bounded. It suffices to prove that for any
f\in\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}_{0}
,
T_{g}f\in B_{0}
. Indeed,
g\in B\mathcal{L}^{p,\lambda}_{0}
, for every
\varepsilon>0
there is an integer
\delta>0
such that as
|J|<\delta
,

\frac{1}{|J|^{p-\lambda+1}}\int_{S(J)}|g^{\prime}(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{p}dm(z)<\varepsilon.

With the above
\delta
, for any
|I|
satisfying
|I|<\delta
, we break up
S(I)
in the same way in Theorem
3.2
, then by
(\ref{32})

\displaystyle\int_{S(I)}|g^{\prime}(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{p+\lambda-1}dm(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{J\in\mathcal{D}_{n}(I)}\int_{S(J)}|J|^{\lambda-1}|g^{\prime}(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{p}dm(z)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{J\in\mathcal{D}_{n}(I)}\varepsilon|J|^{\lambda-1}|J|^{p-\lambda+1}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\varepsilon|I|^{p}.

namely,

\lim_{|I|\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{|I|^{p}}\int_{S(I)}|g^{\prime}(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{p+\lambda-1}dm(z)=0.

Now, it is easy to see that

\displaystyle\lim_{|I|\rightarrow 0}\big(\frac{1}{|I|^{p}}\int_{S(I)}|f(z)|^{2}|g^{\prime}(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{p}dm(z)\big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}}\cdot\lim_{|I|\rightarrow 0}\big(\frac{1}{|I|^{p}}\int_{S(I)}|g^{\prime}(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{p+\lambda-1}dm(z)\big)^{1/2}=0.

In conclusion,
T_{g}:\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}_{0}\rightarrow B_{0}
is bounded.

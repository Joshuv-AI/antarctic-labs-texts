Abstract
. Impressed by the remarkable results about the characterization of (global) Sobolev-Poincaré imbedding domains in [ 6 ] by Buckley and Koskela. In this article, we give characterizations to local Sobolev-Poincaré imbedding domains. The main result reads as below. (1) For 1 ≤ p ≤ n 1\leq p\leq n , a bounded domain with slice condition is a local Sobolev-Poincaré domain of order p p if and only if it is a uniform domain. Combine it with results in [ 6 ] , there exists a global Sobolev-Poincaré imbedding domain of order p p which is not a local Sobolev-Poincaré imbedding domain of order p p . (2) For n < p < ∞ n<p<\infty , a bounded domain with slice condition is a local Sobolev-Poincaré imbedding domain of order p p if and only if it is an α \alpha -cigar domain for α = ( p − n ) / ( p − 1 ) \alpha=(p-n)/(p-1) . Combine it with results in [ 6 ] , a domain is a local Sobolev-Poincaré imbedding domain of order p p if and only if it is a (global) Sobolev-Poincaré imbedding domain of order p p .



1.
Introduction

In this paper, we give geometrical characterizations to bounded domains which support local Sobolev-Poincaré imbeddings. In this paper, if we do not explain deliberately, we always assume domains are bounded. First, we review some existing results about domains that support (global) Sobolev-Poincaré imbeddings. If a bounded domain
{\Omega}\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
has some sufficiently nice geometrical property, for example the boundary of
{\Omega}
is smooth, then this domain supports (global) Sobolev-Ppoincaré imbeddings. The classical Sobolev-Poincaré imbeddings for sufficiently nice bounded domain depend on weather the exponent
p
is less than or equaivalent to or larger than the dimension
n
. For
1\leq p<n
, for every function
u
in the Sobolev class
W^{1,p}({\Omega})
, we have the following Sobolev-Poincaré inequality

(1.1)

{}\inf\limits_{c\in{\mathbb{R}}}\left(\int_{\Omega}\left|u(x)-c\right|^{\frac{np}{n-p}}dx\right)^{\frac{n-p}{np}}\leq C\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

with a constant
C
independent of
u
. By making use of the triangle inequality, the inequality is equivalent to the following inequality

(1.2)

\left(\int_{\Omega}|u(x)-u_{\Omega}|^{\frac{np}{n-p}}dx\right)^{\frac{n-p}{np}}\leq C\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

with maybe a different constant
C
which is also independent of
u
, where
u_{\Omega}
means the integral average of
u
over the domain
{\Omega}
. This inequality was first proved by Sobolev in
[
54
,
55
]
for
1<p<n
. For
p=1
, it was proved by Gagliardo
[
15
]
and Nirenberg
[
49
]
(also see
[
46
]
by Maz’ya).

For
p=n
, for a sufficiently nice domain
{\Omega}\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
and every
u\in W^{1,n}({\Omega})
, one gets the following Trudinger inequality

(1.3)

\|u-u_{\Omega}\|_{\phi(L)({\Omega})}\leq C\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{n}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}

with a constant
C
independent of
u
. Please refer to
[
57
]
for a proof. Here

\phi(t)=\exp\left(t^{\frac{n}{n-1}}\right)-1

and
\|\cdot\|_{\phi(L)({\Omega})}
is the corresponding Orlicz norm on
{\Omega}
defined by

\|f\|_{\phi(L)({\Omega})}:=\inf\left\{s>0:\int_{\Omega}\phi\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{s}\right)dx\leq 1\right\}.

It is well-known that the inequality (
1.3
) is equivalent to the following inequality

(1.4)

\inf\limits_{c\in{\mathbb{R}}}\|u-c\|_{\phi(L)({\Omega})}\leq C\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{n}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}

with maybe a different constant
C
independent of
u
, see
[
47
]
.

For
n<p<\infty
, for certain domains
{\Omega}
(including all balls) and
u\in W^{1,p}({\Omega})
, one gets the following Hölder imbedding inequality (the so-called Morry’s inequality)

(1.5)

|u(x_{1})-u(x_{2})|\leq C|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{1-\frac{n}{p}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla(x)|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

for almost every
x_{1},x_{2}\in{\Omega}
and a constant
C
independent of
u
.

As we know, Sobolev-Poincaré imbedding inequalities (
1.2
), (
1.3
) and (
1.5
) hold on sufficiently nice domains (like balls). We say a domain is a Sobolev-Poincaré imbedding domain of order
p
, if the corresponding Sobolev-Poincaré inequality holds on the domain. By making use of the result from
[
31
]
, it is not difficult to show bounded
W^{1,p}
-extension domains for
1\leq p<\infty
are Sobolev-Poincaré imbedding domains of order
p
. Roughly, a domain
{\Omega}\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
is called a
W^{1,p}
-extension domain, if every function in
W^{1,p}({\Omega})
can be extended to be a function in
W^{1,p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
whose norm is controlled from above uniformly by the norm of the original function. Please see the precise definition in next section. Then the following natural question arises.

What is the geometric characterizations of Sobolev-Poincaré imbedding domains?

For
1\leq p<n
, Bojarski proved that John domains are Sobolev-Poincaré imbedding domains of order
p
, see
[
2
]
. Also see
[
29
]
for this result. Roughly, a domain is called a John domain, if every point in the domain can be connected to a center point by a twisted cone whose size is comparable to the Euclidean distance from the point to the center point. Please see the precise definition below. Conversely, under some very weak connecting assumption that is the slice condition, Buckley and Koskela
[
5
]
proved that a Sobolev-Poincaré imbedding domain of order
p
for

must be a John domain. Please see the prercise definitions of the slice property with respect to a point and the slice condition for a domain in next section. By the result in
[
19
]
from Gehring and Martio, if a domain is quasiconformally equivalent to the unit ball, it satisfies the slice condition. The Riemann Mapping Theorem implies that every proper simply connected domain in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
satisfies the slice condition. For
p=n
, Smith and Stegenga proved that
0
-carrot domains are Sobolev-Poincaré imbedding domains of order
n
. Conversely, Buckley and Koskela proved that if a domain satisfying the slice condition is a Sobolev-Poincaré imbedding domain of order

, then it must be a
0
-carrot domain. See the precise definition of

-carrot domain for

below. By the definition, it is easy to verify that John domains are
0
-carrot domains. For
n<p<\infty

[
5

proved that a domain is a Sobolev-Poincaré imbedding domain of order

if and only if it is an
\alpha
-cigar domain for
\alpha=(p-n)/(p-1)
. See the precise definition of cigar domains below. In the paper
[

, Zhou generalized these results to the fractional Hajłasz-Sobolev spaces.

A domain
{\Omega}\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
is called a local Sobolev-Poincaré imbedding domain of order

, if it satisfies a local version of one of the corresponding inequalities (

), (
1.4
) and (

and every

, the inequality

(1.6)

\displaystyle\inf\limits_{c\in\mathbb{R}}\left(\int_{B(x,r)\cap{\Omega}}|u(y)-c|^{\frac{np}{n-p}}dy\right)^{\frac{n-p}{np}}\leq C\left(\int_{B(x,\lambda r)\cap{\Omega}}|\nabla u(y)|^{p}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

holds with constants
C,\lambda\geq 1
independent of
u
and
B(x,r)
. For
p=n
, if for every
u\in{W}^{1,n}(\Omega)
and every
x\in\Omega
and
0<r<{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,diam\,}}({\Omega})
, the inequality

(1.7)

\displaystyle\inf\limits_{c\in\mathbb{R}}\int_{B(x,r)\cap\Omega}\exp\left(\frac{A|u(y)-c|}{\|\nabla u\|_{L^{n}(B(x,\lambda r)\cap\Omega)}}\right)^{\frac{n}{n-1}}\,dy\leq C|B(x,r)|

holds with constants
C,\lambda\geq 1
and
A>0
independent of
u
and
B(x,r)
. For
n<p<\infty
, if for every
u\in{W}^{1,p}(\Omega)
and every
x\in\Omega
and
0<r<{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,diam\,}}({\Omega})
, the inequality

(1.8)

\displaystyle|u(x_{1})-u(x_{2})|\leq C|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{1-\frac{n}{p}}\left(\int_{B(x,\lambda r)\cap{\Omega}}|\nabla u(y)|^{p}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

holds for almost every
x_{1},x_{2}\in B(x,r)\cap{\Omega}
and constants
C,\lambda\geq 1
independent of
u
and
B(x,r)
. As we explained before, the geometric characterization of Sobolev-Poincaré imbedding domains is already clear. For local Sobolev-Poincaré imbedding domains, a similar natural question arises.

What is the geometric characterization of local Sobolev-Poincaré imbedding domains?

For bounded domains, by taking the radius
r
to be large enough, one can see that a local Sobolev-Poincaré imbedding domain of order
p
is also a (global) Sobolev-Poincaré imbedding domain of order
p
, for every
1\leq p<\infty
. But the converse is not correct for
1\leq p\leq n
. The planar slit disk is a typical countexample. It is the unit disk with one radial segment removed. The slit disk is a typical John domain with slice condition, so it is a Sobolev-Poincaré imbedding domain of order
p
for every
1\leq p\leq n
. See the results in
[
6
]
. However, it is not difficult to check that the slit disk is not a local Sobolev-Poincaré imbedding domain of order
p
for any
1\leq p<\infty
. Hence, for every
1\leq p\leq n
, the class of local Sobolev-Poincaré imbedding domains of order
p
must be a proper subclass of (global) Sobolev-Poincaré imbedding domains of order
p
. The class of uniform domains is a subclass of John domains. Uniform domains were first introduced by Martio and Sarvas
[
45
]
. Roughly, a domain is called a uniform domain, if arbitrary two points inside the domain can be connected by a twisted double cone whose size is comparable to the distance between these two points. Please see the precise definition below. In
[
33
]
, Jones proved that uniform domains are
W^{k,p}
-extension domains for arbitrary
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
1\leq p\leq\infty
. In the main result of the paper, we will show a bounded domain with slice condition is a local Sobolev-Poincaré imbedding domain of order
p
for
1\leq p\leq n
if and only if it is a uniform domain.

Theorem 1.1
.

If
{\Omega}\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
is a bounded domain with the slice condition, then it is a local Sobolev-Poincaré imbedding domain of order
p
with
1\leq p\leq n
if and only if it is a uniform domain.

A result in
[
4
]
by Buckley and Herron tells us that uniform domains are
(LLC)
and if a
(LLC)
domain is also quasiconvex and satisfies the slice condition, then it is also uniform. Here
LLC
is the abbreviation of “locally linearly connection”. Please see the precise definition in next section.
As we discussed before, for
1\leq p\leq n
, the class of local Sobolev-Poincaré imbedding domains of order
p
is a proper subclass of (global) Sobolev-Poincaré imbedding domains of order
p
. However, the next theorem says they are the same for
n<p<\infty
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
{\Omega}\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
be a bounded domain with the slice condition. Then
{\Omega}
is a local Sobolev-Poincaré imbedding domain of order
p
for
n<p<\infty
if and only if it is an
\alpha
-cigar domain with
\alpha=(p-n)/(p-1)
.

As we discussed before, bounded planar simply connected domains satisfy the slice condition. Hence, if we only consider planar bounded simply connected domains, we can obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 1.1
.

If
{\Omega}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
is a bounded simply connected domain, then

•

for
1\leq p\leq n
, it is a local Sobolev-Poincaré imbedding domain of order
p
if and only if it is a uniform domain;

•

for
n<p<\infty
, it is a local Sobolev-Poincaré imbedding domain of order
p
if and only if it is an
\alpha
-cigar domain with
\alpha=(p-n)/(p-1)
.

2.
Preliminary

We write
C
as generic positive constants. These constants may change even in a single string of estimates. The dependence of the constant on a parameters
\alpha,\beta,\cdots
is expressed by the notation
C=C(\alpha,\beta,\cdots)
if needed. For two points
x,y\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
,
|x-y|
means the Euclidean distance between them. For a Lebesgue measurable set
E\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
,
|E|
means the Lebesgue measure of it. For a measurable function
u
defined on
E
with
0<|E|<\infty
, we define

u_{E}:=\fint_{E}u(x)dx:=\frac{1}{|E|}\int_{E}u(x)dx

to be the integral average of
u
over the set
E
. A continuous mapping
\gamma:[a,b]\to{\Omega}\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
is called a curve. Recall that the length of a curve
\gamma:[a,b]\to{\Omega}
is the total variation over
[a,b]

l(\gamma):=\sup\left\{\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\left|\gamma(t_{i})-\gamma(t_{i+1})\right|\right\}

where the supremum is taken over all finite partitions

a=t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{n}=b

of the interval
[a,b]
. If
l(\gamma)<\infty
, the curve
\gamma
is called rectifiable. Recall that every rectifiable curve
\gamma
admits a parametrization by the arc-length that
\gamma_{0}:[0,l(\gamma)]\to{\Omega}
. For the arc-length parametrization
\gamma_{0}
of
\gamma
, we have

l\left(\gamma_{o}\big|_{[t_{1},t_{2}]}\right)=t_{2}-t_{1}

for all
0\leq t_{1}\leq t_{2}\leq l(\gamma)
. The integral of a Borel function
\rho
over a rectifiable curve
\gamma
is usually defined via the arc-length parametrization
\gamma_{0}
of
\gamma
by setting

\int_{\gamma}\rho(z)|d(z)|:=\int_{[0,l(\gamma)]}\rho\circ\gamma_{0}(t)dt.

From now on, we only consider curves that are arc-length parametrized. For a curve
\gamma\subset{\Omega}
and
x,y\in{\Omega}
, we use the notation
\gamma^{[x,y]}
to denote the subcurve of
\gamma
between
x
and
y
. A domain
{\Omega}\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
is called quasiconvex, if there exists a large enough constant
C>1
such that for every
x,y\in{\Omega}
, there exists a rectifiable curve
\gamma\subset{\Omega}
joining
x
and
y
such that

l(\gamma)\leq C|x-y|.

Let us give some definitions next. First, let us recall the definition of John domains.

Definition 2.1
.

A domain
{\Omega}\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
is called a
c_{0}
-John domain for some constant
0<c_{0}\leq 1
, if it is bounded and there exists a distinguished center point
x_{0}\in{\Omega}
such that for every
x\in{\Omega}
, there exists a curve
\gamma:[0,l]\to{\Omega}
parametrized by arclength such that
\gamma(0)=x
,
\gamma(l)=x_{0}
such that for every
t\in[0,l]
, we have

{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,dist\,}}(\gamma(t),\partial{\Omega})\geq c_{0}t.

Roughly, every point inside a John domain can be connected to a center by a twisted cone whose size is comparable to the Euclidean distance between
x
and
x_{0}
. Next we give the definition of uniform domains.

Definition 2.2
.

A domain
\Omega\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
is called
\epsilon_{0}
-uniform for
0<\epsilon_{0}\leq 1
, if for all
x,y\in\Omega
,
there exists a rectifiable curve
\gamma\subset\Omega
joining
x,y
with

l(\gamma)\leq\frac{1}{\epsilon_{0}}|x-y|,

and for every
z\in\gamma
, we have

{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,dist\,}}(z,\partial\Omega)\geq\epsilon_{0}\frac{|x-z||y-z|}{|x-y|}.

Roughly, every pair of points inside the uniform domain can be connected by a twisted double cone whose size is comparable to the Euclidean distance between
x
and
y
. By the definition, it is easy to see that every bounded uniform domain is also a John domain. The planar slit disk is a typical John domain which is not a uniform domain. Indeed, under the assumption with slice condition, to prove a local Sobolev-Poincaré imbedding domain of order
p
for
1\leq p\leq n
is also a uniform domain, we will prove that it is quasiconvex and satisfies the following locally linear connection (for short
LLC
) condition.

Definition 2.3
.

We say a domain
\Omega\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
satisfies locally linear connection (for short
LLC
) condition if there exists a constant
b\in(0,1]
such that for every
z\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
and
r>0
, we have

•

LLC(1)
: Points in
\Omega\cap B(z,r)
can be joined by a locally rectifiable curve in
\Omega\cap B(z,\frac{r}{b})
;

•

LLC(2)
: Points in
\Omega\setminus B(z,r)
can be joined by a locally rectifiable curve in
\Omega\setminus B(z,br)
.

A homeomorphism
h:{\Omega}\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}\to{\Omega}^{\prime}\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
in the class
W^{1,n}_{\rm loc}({\Omega},{\Omega}^{\prime})
is called a quasiconformal mapping, if there exists a constant
K>1
such that for almost every
z\in{\Omega}
, we have

|Dh(z)|^{n}\leq K|J_{h}(z)|,

where
|Dh(z)|
means the operator norm of the differential matrix
Dh(z)
and
|J_{h}(z)|
is the absolutely value of the Jacobian determinate. A domain
{\Omega}
is called quasiconformally equivalent to another domain
{\Omega}^{\prime}
, if there exists a quasiconformal mapping from
{\Omega}
onto
{\Omega}^{\prime}
. According to the results in
[
19
]
by Gehring and Martio, a domain quasiconformally equivalent to a uniform domain is
LLC
; conversely, if a
LLC
domain is quasiconvex and satisfies the following slice condition, it is also a uniform domain. Now, let us give the definition of the slice condition.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
{\Omega}\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
be a domain with a distinguished point
x_{0}
and
C>1
be a constant. We say that
{\Omega}
has the
C
-slice property with respect to
x_{0}
, if for every
x\in{\Omega}
, there is a curve
\gamma:[0,1]\to{\Omega}
with
\gamma(0)=x_{0}
and
\gamma(1)=x
, and there is a pairwise disjoint collection of open subsets
\{U_{i}\subset{\Omega}:i=0,\cdots,j\}
such that:

(1)

x_{0}\in U_{0}
,
x\in U_{j}
, and
x_{0}
and
x
are in different components of
{\Omega}\setminus\overline{U_{i}}
for all
0<i<j
;

(2)

If
\tilde{\gamma}\subset\subset{\Omega}
is a curve containing both
x
and
x_{0}
, then for all
0<i<j
, we have

{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,diam\,}}(U_{i})\leq Cl(\tilde{\gamma}\cap U_{i});

(3)

For every
t\in[0,1]
, we have

B\left(\gamma(t),\frac{{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,dist\,}}(\gamma(t),\partial{\Omega})}{C}\right)\subset\bigcup_{i=0}^{j}\overline{U_{i}};

(4)

For every
0\leq i\leq j
, we have

{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,diam\,}}(U_{i})\leq C{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,dist\,}}(\gamma(t),\partial{\Omega})

for all
\gamma(t)\in\gamma\cap U_{i}.
Also there exists
x_{i}\in\gamma\cap U_{i}
as
x_{0}
is previously defined and
x_{j}=x
such that

B\left(x_{i},\frac{{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,dist\,}}(x_{i},\partial{\Omega})}{C}\right)\subset U_{i}.

We say that
{\Omega}
satisfies the slice condition if it has the
C
-slice property with respect to every point
x\in{\Omega}
for some
C>1
.

There are also other kinds of slice conditions, please see the article
[
3
]
for an introduction. The following lemma was proved by Buckley and Herron in
[
4
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

A domain
{\Omega}\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
is a uniform domain if and only if it is quasiconvex,
LLC
and satisfies the slice condition.

Let us give the definition of cigar domains and carrot domains. We follow the definition given by Buckley and Koskela in
[
6
]
where they called them weak cigar domains and weak carrot domains. To simplify the notation, we just say cigar domains and carrot domains. First, let us give the definition of cigar domains.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
0<\alpha<1
and
0<\beta\leq\alpha
. A domain
{\Omega}\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
is called an
(\alpha,\beta)
-cigar domain, if for every pair of points
x,y\in{\Omega}
, there exists a curve
\gamma:[0,1]\to{\Omega}
such that
\gamma(0)=x
,
\gamma(1)=y
and the inequality

\int_{\gamma}{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,dist\,}}(z,\partial{\Omega})^{\alpha-1}|dz|\leq C|x-y|^{\beta}

holds with a constant
C
independent of
x,y
. A domain
{\Omega}\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
is called a
(0,0)
-cigar domain, if for every pair of points
x,y\in{\Omega}
, there exists a curve
\gamma:[0,1]\to{\Omega}
such that
\gamma(0)=x
,
\gamma(1)=y
and the inequality

\int_{\gamma}\frac{1}{{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,dist\,}}(z,\partial{\Omega})}|dz|\leq C\log\left(1+\frac{|x-y|}{\min\{{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,dist\,}}(x,\partial{\Omega}),{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,dist\,}}(y,\partial{\Omega})\}}\right)

holds with a constant
C
independent of
x,y
.

To simplify the notation, we use
\alpha
-cigar domain for
(\alpha,\alpha)
-cigar domain. The class of
(0,0)
-cigar domains coincides with the class of uniform domains, see
[
17
,
32
]
. It was shown in
[
18
]
that when
\alpha>0
, the class of
(\alpha,\beta)
-cigar domains is strictly larger than the class of uniform domains. In
[
44
]
, Lappalainen proved that every
\alpha
-Hölder continuous function defined on an
(\alpha,\beta)
-cigar domain can be extended to be a global
\beta
-Hölder continuous function. Now, let us give the definition of carrot domains.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
0\leq\alpha<1
. A domain
{\Omega}\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
is called an
\alpha
-carrot domain, if there exists a distinguished center
x_{0}\in{\Omega}
such that for every
x\in{\Omega}
, there exists a curve
\gamma:[0,1]\to{\Omega}
such that
\gamma(0)=x
,
\gamma(1)=1
and the inequality

(2.1)

\int_{\gamma}{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,dist\,}}(z,\partial{\Omega})^{\alpha-1}|dz|\leq\begin{cases}C,&\ \ {\rm if}\ 0<\alpha<1,\\
C\left(1+\log\left(\frac{C_{1}}{{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,dist\,}}(x,\partial{\Omega})}\right)\right),&\ \ {\rm if}\ \alpha=0,\end{cases}

holds for constants
C,C_{1}
independent of
x
.

By the definition, for arbitrary
0\leq\alpha<1
, an
\alpha
-carrot domain must be bounded. It is easy to verify that John domains are
\alpha
-carrot domains for all
0\leq\alpha<1
. The converse is not correct. It is not hard to construct an
\alpha
-carrot domain which is not a John domain, see
[
18
,
6
]
. The condition in the definition of carrot domains is widely known as quasihyperbolic boundary condition, see
[
38

and references therein.

The definitions of uniform domains and John domains show these two classes of domains are closely relative. The next lemma shows that a uniform domain is not far from John domain locally. The case
{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,diam\,}}({\Omega})=\infty
was already considered in
[
58
, Lemma 1.9]
. It seems their argument is not easy to be extended to the bounded case. So we prove the result for the case
{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,diam\,}}({\Omega})<\infty
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose that
\Omega
is an
\epsilon_{0}
-uniform domain with
{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,diam\,}}\Omega<\infty
.
Given any
x_{0}\in\Omega
and
r>0
,
there is a
c_{0}
-John domain
\Omega_{r}\subset\Omega
and a constant
\lambda>1
such that

B(x_{0},r)\cap\Omega\subset\Omega_{r}\subset B(x_{0},\lambda r)\cap\Omega.

Moreover, the constant
c_{0}
only depends on the constant
\epsilon_{0}
.

Proof.

Assume there exists a point
y_{0}\in\Omega
such that
|x_{0}-y_{0}|\geq\frac{4r}{\epsilon_{0}}
. Otherwise, let
\lambda=\frac{4}{\epsilon_{0}}
, we have

B(x_{0},\lambda r)\cap\Omega=\Omega.

The result in
[
18
, Lemma 2.18]
says that a bounded uniform domain is always a John domain. Hence, we can just take
\Omega_{r}=\Omega
to obtain

B(x_{0},r)\cap\Omega\subset\Omega_{r}\subset B(x_{0},\lambda r)\cap\Omega.

In the other case, fix
y_{0}\in\Omega
with
|x_{0}-y_{0}|=\frac{4r}{\epsilon_{0}}
. There exists a curve
\gamma_{0}:[0,1]\to{\Omega}
with
\gamma_{0}(0)=x_{0}
,
\gamma_{0}(1)=y_{0}
and
\gamma_{0}
satisfies the conditions in Definition
2.2
.
Set

\displaystyle G_{0}:=\left\{z\in\Omega\,:\exists\,v\in\gamma_{0}\,such\,\,that\,|z-v|<\frac{\epsilon_{0}|x_{0}-v||y_{0}-v|}{|x_{0}-y_{0}|}\right\}.

Then
G_{0}\subset\Omega
contains a point
z_{0}\in\gamma_{0}
with
|x_{0}-z_{0}|=\frac{2r}{\epsilon_{0}}
and
B(z_{0},r)\subset G_{0}
.
Indeed, the triangle inequality gives

\displaystyle\epsilon_{0}\frac{|x_{0}-z_{0}||y_{0}-z_{0}|}{|x_{0}-y_{0}|}\geq\epsilon_{0}\frac{|x_{0}-z_{0}|(|x_{0}-y_{0}|-|x_{0}-z_{0}|)}{|x_{0}-y_{0}|}\geq r.

Fix
x\in(B(x_{0},r)\cap\Omega)\backslash B(z_{0},r)
. There exists a curve
\gamma_{x}:[0,1]\to{\Omega}
with
\gamma_{x}(0)=z_{0}
and
\gamma_{x}(1)=x
and the curve
\gamma_{x}
satisfies the conditions in Definition
2.2
. Similar to
G_{0}
, define a domain
G_{x}\subset\Omega
by setting

\displaystyle G_{x}:=\left\{z\in\Omega\,:\,\exists\,v\in\gamma_{x}\,such\,that\,|v-z|<\frac{\epsilon_{0}|x-v||z_{0}-v|}{|x-z_{0}|}\right\}.

Next we prove that

\displaystyle\Omega_{r}:=\bigcup_{x\in(B(x_{0},r)\cap\Omega)\backslash B(z_{0},r)}G_{x}\cup B(z_{0},r)

is a John domain. For every
x\in(B(x_{0},r)\cap{\Omega})\setminus B(z_{0},r)
, we have

r\leq|x-z_{0}|\leq|x-x_{0}|+|x_{0}-z_{0}|\leq r\left(1+\frac{2}{\epsilon_{0}}\right).

Hence, for every
z\in G_{x}
, there exists
v\in\gamma_{x}
such that

\displaystyle|x_{0}-z|\leq|x_{0}-x|+|x-v|+|v-z|.

Obviously,
|x_{0}-x|<r
. Denote
\gamma_{x}^{[x,v]}
to be the subcurve of
\gamma_{x}
from
x
to
v
and
\gamma_{x}^{[v,z_{0}]}
to be the subcurve of
\gamma_{x}
from
v
to
z_{0}
. Since
\gamma_{x}
satisfies the conditions in Definition
2.2
, we have

|x-v|\leq l\left(\gamma_{x}^{[x,v]}\right)\leq l(\gamma_{x})\leq\frac{1}{\epsilon_{0}}|x-z_{0}|.

Since

l\left(\gamma_{x}^{[x,v]}\right)+l\left(\gamma_{x}^{[v,z_{0}]}\right)=l(\gamma_{x}),

by the definition of the set
G_{x}
, we have

|v-z|<\frac{\epsilon_{0}|x-v||z_{0}-v|}{|x-z_{0}|}\leq\frac{\epsilon_{0}l\left(\gamma_{x}^{[x,v]}\right)l\left(\gamma_{x}^{z_{0},v}\right)}{|x-z_{0}|}\leq\frac{\epsilon_{0}\left(l(\gamma_{x})\right)^{2}}{4|x-z_{0}|}\leq\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{4}|x-z_{0}|.

Combine these inequalities together, we obtain

|x_{0}-z|\leq r+\frac{1}{\epsilon_{0}}|x-z_{0}|+\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{4}|x-z_{0}|\leq r\left(1+\frac{(\epsilon_{0}+4)^{2}}{4\epsilon_{0}^{2}}\right).

Hence, take

\lambda=1+\frac{(\epsilon_{0}+4)^{2}}{4\epsilon_{0}^{2}},

we have

\Omega_{r}\subset B\left(x_{0},r\left(1+\frac{(\epsilon_{0}+4)^{2}}{4\epsilon_{0}^{2}}\right)\right)\cap\Omega.

Now, let us prove that
{\Omega}_{r}
is a John domain with the distinguished center
z_{0}
. If
z\in B(z_{0},r)
, it is not difficult to check that the segment
[z,z_{0}]
between
z
and
z_{0}
satisfies the condition in Definition
2.1
with the constant
c=1
. Let us consider the case that
z\in G_{x}\backslash B(z_{0},r)
for some
x\in(B(x_{0},r)\cap{\Omega})\setminus B(z_{0},r)
. By the definition of
G_{x}
, there exists a point
v\in\gamma_{x}
such that

z\in B\left(v,\frac{\epsilon_{0}|x-v||z_{0}-v|}{|x-z_{0}|}\right)\subset G_{x}.

So
[z,v]\subset G_{x}
and

[z,v]\cup\gamma^{[v,z_{0}]}_{x}\subset{\Omega}_{r}

is a rectifiable curve from
z
to
z_{0}
. There exists a unique
t\in(0,1)
such that

|\gamma_{x}(t)-v|=\frac{\epsilon_{0}|x-v||z_{0}-v|}{2|x-z_{0}|}

and for every
\tilde{t}\in(0,t)
, we have

|\gamma_{x}(\tilde{t})-v|>\frac{\epsilon_{0}|x-v||z_{0}-v|}{2|x-z_{0}|}.

Denote
w:=\gamma_{x}(t)
. Then

\tilde{\gamma}:=[z,v]\cup[v,w]\cup\gamma_{x}^{[w,z_{0}]}\subset{\Omega}_{r}

is also a rectifiable curve from
z
to
z_{0}
.

Now, let us check the curve
\tilde{\gamma}
satisfies the conditions in Definition
2.1
with a constant
c=\frac{6(\epsilon_{0}+2)}{\epsilon_{0}^{3}}
depending only on
\epsilon_{0}
. For every
s\in[z,v]\subset\tilde{\gamma}
, since
G_{x}\cup B(z_{0},r)\subset{\Omega}_{r}
and

z\in B\left(v,\frac{\epsilon_{0}|x-v||z_{0}-v|}{|x-z_{0}|}\right)\subset G_{x},

we have

\displaystyle d(s,\partial{\Omega}_{r})

\displaystyle\geq d(s,\partial(G_{x}\cup B(z_{0},r)))\geq d(s,\partial G_{x})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\epsilon_{0}|x-v||z_{0}-v|}{|x-z_{0}|}-|s-v|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\epsilon_{0}|x-v||z_{0}-v|}{|x-z_{0}|}-\left(|z-s|-|v-z|\right)

\displaystyle\geq|z-s|=l(\tilde{\gamma}^{[z,s]})\geq\frac{\epsilon_{0}^{3}l(\tilde{\gamma}^{[z,s]})}{6(\epsilon_{0}+2)}.

For every
s\in[v,w]

we know

\displaystyle d(s,\partial{\Omega}_{r})

\displaystyle\geq d(s,\partial(G_{x}\cup B(z_{0},r)))\geq d\left(s,\partial B\left(v,\frac{\epsilon_{0}|x-v||z_{0}-v|}{|x-z_{0}|}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\epsilon_{0}|x-v||z_{0}-v|}{|x-z_{0}|}-|v-s|\geq\frac{\epsilon_{0}|x-v||z_{0}-v|}{2|x-z_{0}|}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{3}\left(|z-v|+|v-s|\right)>\frac{\epsilon_{0}^{3}l(\tilde{\gamma}^{[z,s]})}{6(\epsilon_{0}+2)}.

For every
s\in\tilde{\gamma}^{[w,z_{0}]}=\gamma^{[w,z_{0}]}_{x}
, we have

|s-v|\geq\frac{\epsilon_{0}|x-v||z_{0}-v|}{2|x-z_{0}|},

we have

|v-z|\leq 2|s-v|\leq 2l(\tilde{\gamma}^{[v,s]}).

We have

l(\tilde{\gamma}^{[z,s]})\leq l([z,v])+l(\gamma_{x}^{[x,s]})-l\gamma_{x}^{[x,v]}\leq 3l(\gamma_{x}^{[x,s]}).

If
|s-z_{0}|\geq\frac{r}{2}
, then we have

\displaystyle d(s,\partial{\Omega}_{r})

\displaystyle\geq d(s,\partial(G_{x}\cup B(z_{0},r)))\geq d(s,\partial G_{x})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\epsilon_{0}|x-s||z_{0}-s|}{|x-z_{0}|}\geq\frac{\epsilon_{0}^{2}|x-s|}{2(\epsilon_{0}+2)}\geq\frac{\epsilon_{0}^{3}l(\gamma_{x}^{[x,s]})}{6(\epsilon_{0}+2)}\geq\frac{\epsilon_{0}^{3}l(\tilde{\gamma}^{[z,s]})}{6(\epsilon_{0}+2)}.

If
|s-z_{0}|\leq\frac{r}{2}
, since
B(z_{0},r)\subset\Omega
, we have

\displaystyle d(s,\partial(G_{x}\cup B(z_{0},r)))\geq d(s,\partial B(z_{0},r))\geq r-|s-z_{0}|\geq\frac{r}{2}.

Combine it with

|x-z_{0}|\leq r\left(1+\frac{2}{\epsilon_{0}}\right),

we have

\displaystyle d(s,\partial{\Omega}_{r})

\displaystyle\geq d(s,\partial(G_{x}\cup B(z_{0},r)))\geq\frac{r}{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\epsilon_{0}|x-z_{0}|}{2(\epsilon_{0}+2)}\geq\frac{\epsilon_{0}^{2}l(\gamma_{x})}{2(\epsilon_{0}+2)}\geq\frac{\epsilon_{0}^{3}l(\tilde{\gamma}^{[z,s]})}{6(\epsilon_{0}+2)}.

Combine all the estimates above, we can take
c_{0}={\epsilon_{0}^{3}}/{6(\epsilon_{0}+2)}
and obtain the desired result.

Let
{\Omega}\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
be a domain. We define the intrinsic metric
d_{\Omega}
on
{\Omega}
by setting

d_{\Omega}(x_{1},x_{2}):=\inf\left\{l(\gamma):\gamma\subset{\Omega}\ {\rm is\ a\ locally\ rectifiable\ curve\ joining}\ x_{1}\ {\rm and}\ x_{2}\right\}.

If there is no such curve, we simply set

d_{\Omega}(x_{1},x_{2})=\infty.

For arbitrary
x\in{\Omega}
and
r>0
, we define the intrinsic ball
B_{\Omega}(x,r)
by setting

B_{\Omega}(x,r):=\left\{y\in{\Omega}:d_{\Omega}(x,y)<r\right\}

and define the intrinsic boundary
\partial_{\Omega}B_{\Omega}(x,r)
of the intrinsic ball
B_{\Omega}(x,r)
by setting

\partial_{\Omega}B_{\Omega}(x,r):=\left\{y\in{\Omega}:d_{\Omega}(x,y)=r\right\}.

Obviously,
B_{\Omega}(x,r)
is a subset of
B(x,r)\cap{\Omega}
. A domain
{\Omega}\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
is called Ahlfors
n
-regular, if there exists a constant
0<c<1
and some
r_{o}>0
such that for every
x\in\overline{{\Omega}}
and every
0<r<r_{o}
, we have

|B(x,r)\cap{\Omega}|\geq c|B(x,r)|.

Similarly, we say that
{\Omega}
is intrinsic Ahlfors
n
-regular, if there exists a constant
0<c<1
and some
r_{o}>0
such that for every
x\in\overline{{\Omega}}
and
0<r<r_{o}
, we have

|B_{\Omega}(x,r)|\geq c|B(x,r)|.

It is easy to see that intrinsic Ahlfors
n
-regularity can imply Ahlfors
n
-regularity, since
B_{\Omega}(x,r)
is a subset of
B(x,r)\cap{\Omega}
.

By the result in
[
25
]
due to Hajłasz, Koskela and Tuominen, we can conclude that a local Sobolev-Poincaré imbedding domain of order
p
for
1\leq p<\infty
is Ahlfors
n
-regular. This observation may not come from their result in the paper but comes from the proof in the paper directly. Nevertheless, we will prove a stronger result below. Of course, the idea behind the argument comes from the paper
[
25
]
by Hajłasz, Koskela and Tuominen.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
{\Omega}\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
be a local Sobolev-Poincaré imbedding domain of order
p
for
1\leq p<\infty
. Then
{\Omega}
is intrinsic Ahlfors
n
-regular.

Proof.

Fix
x\in{\Omega}
and
0<r<r_{o}
with

r_{o}=\min\left\{1,\left(\frac{|{\Omega}|}{2\omega_{n}}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}\right\},

where
\omega_{n}
means the volume of
n
-dimensional unit ball. Let
b_{0}=1
and
b_{j}\in(0,1)
for
j\in N
such that

(2.2)

|B_{\Omega}(x,b_{j}r)|=2^{-1}|B_{\Omega}(x,b_{j-1}r)|=2^{-j}|B_{\Omega}(x,r)|.

For each
j\geq 0
, define a function
u_{x,b_{j+1},b_{j}}
on
\Omega
by setting

\displaystyle u_{x,b_{j+1},b_{j}}(y):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1,&y\in B_{\Omega}(x,b_{j+1}r),\\
\frac{b_{j}r-d_{\Omega}(y,x)}{b_{j}r-b_{j+1}r},&y\in B_{\Omega}(x,b_{j}r)\setminus B_{\Omega}(x,b_{j+1}r),\\
0,&elsewhere.\end{array}\right.

Some simple computation gives

(2.3)

|\nabla u_{x,b_{j+1},b_{j}}(y)|\leq\begin{cases}\frac{1}{b_{j}r-b_{j+1}r},\ &\ y\in B_{\Omega}(x,b_{j}r)\setminus B_{\Omega}(x,b_{j+1}r),\\
0,\ &\ {\rm elsewhere}.\end{cases}

Some simple computation gives

\int_{\Omega}|u_{x,b_{j+1},b_{j}}(y)|^{p}dy\leq|B_{{\Omega}}(x,b_{j}r)|.

For every
\lambda\geq 1
, we have

(2.4)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{x,b_{j+1},b_{j}}(y)|^{p}\,dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}=\left(\int_{B(x,\lambda r)\cap\Omega}|\nabla u_{x,b_{j+1},b_{j}}(y)|^{p}\,dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\frac{|B_{\Omega}(x,b_{j}r)|^{\frac{1}{p}}}{b_{j}r-b_{j+1}r}.

Hence,
u_{x,b_{j+1},b_{j}}\in W^{1,p}({\Omega})
. According to the relationship between
p
and
n
, we divide the argument below into three cases.

Case (
1\leq p<n
):

Let
j\geq 0
. For each
c\in\mathbb{R}
, by the definition of
u_{x,b_{j+1},b_{j}}
, we have

u_{x,b_{j+1},b_{j}}-c\geq\frac{1}{2}

either on
B_{\Omega}(x,b_{j+1}r)
or on
\Omega\setminus B_{\Omega}(x,b_{j}r)
. Since
0<r<r_{o}
, we have

|{\Omega}\setminus B_{\Omega}(x,b_{j}r)|\geq|B_{\Omega}(x,b_{j+1}r)|.

Hence, we have

(2.5)

\inf\limits_{c\in\mathbb{R}}\left(\int_{B(x,r)\cap\Omega_{0}}|u_{x,b_{j+1},b_{j}}(y)-c|^{\frac{np}{n-p}}\,dy\right)^{\frac{n-p}{np}}\geq\frac{1}{2}|B_{\Omega}(x,b_{j+1}r)|^{\frac{n-p}{np}}.

By combining (
1.6
), (
2.2
), (
2.4
) and (
2.5
), we obtain

(2.6)

\displaystyle\left|B_{\Omega}(x,b_{j}r)\right|^{\frac{n-p}{np}}\leq\frac{C|B_{\Omega}(x,b_{j}r)|^{\frac{1}{p}}}{b_{j}r-b_{j+1}r},

that is,

\displaystyle b_{j}r-b_{j+1}r\leq C|B_{\Omega}(x,b_{j}r)|^{\frac{1}{n}}\leq C2^{-j/n}|B_{\Omega}(x,r)|^{\frac{1}{n}}.

Since
b_{j}\rightarrow 0
as
j\rightarrow\infty
, we have

\displaystyle r=\sum\limits_{j\geq 0}(b_{j}r-b_{j+1}r)\leq C\sum\limits_{j\geq 0}2^{-j/n}|B_{\Omega}(x,r)|^{\frac{1}{n}}\leq C|B_{\Omega}(x,r)|^{\frac{1}{n}}.

It implies
{\Omega}
is intrinsic Ahlfors
n
-regular.

Case (
p=n
):

As same as in the last case, for each
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
, we have

|u_{x,b_{j+1},b_{j}}-c|\geq\frac{1}{2}

either on
B_{\Omega}(x,b_{j+1}r)
or on
{\Omega}\setminus B_{\Omega}(x,b_{j}r)
.
Since

|{\Omega}\setminus B_{\Omega}(x,b_{j}r)|\geq|B_{\Omega}(x,b_{j+1}r)|,

combine (
2.4
) and (
1.7
), we obtain

|B_{\Omega}(x,b_{j}r)|\exp\left(\frac{A(b_{j}r-b_{j+1}r)}{2|B_{\Omega}(x,b_{j}r)|^{\frac{1}{n}}}\right)^{\frac{n}{n-1}}\leq C|B(x,r)|.

It implies that

b_{j}-b_{j+1}\leqslant\frac{2|B_{\Omega}(x,b_{j}r)|^{\frac{1}{n}}}{Ar}\log^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\left(\frac{C|B(x,r)|}{|B_{\Omega}(x,b_{j}r)|}\right).

This leads to

\displaystyle b_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j\geqslant 0}(b_{j}-b_{j+1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sum\limits_{j\geqslant 0}\frac{|B_{\Omega}(x,b_{j}r)|^{\frac{1}{n}}}{|B(x,r)|^{\frac{1}{n}}}\log^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\left(\frac{|B(x,r)|}{|B_{\Omega}(x,b_{j}r)|}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sum\limits_{j\geqslant 0}2^{-j/n}\frac{|B_{\Omega}(x,r)|^{\frac{1}{n}}}{|B(x,r)|^{\frac{1}{n}}}\log^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\left(\frac{2^{nj}|B(x,r)|}{|B_{\Omega}(x,r)|}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sum\limits_{j\geq 0}2^{-j/n}\frac{|B_{\Omega}(x,r)|^{\frac{1}{n}}}{|B(x,r)|^{\frac{1}{n}}}\left(\log(2^{jn})+\log\left(\frac{|B(x,r)|}{|B_{\Omega}(x,r)|}\right)\right)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sum\limits_{j\geq 0}2^{-j/n}\log^{\frac{n-1}{n}}(2^{jn})\frac{|B_{\Omega}(x,r)|^{\frac{1}{n}}}{|B(x,r)|^{\frac{1}{n}}}

\displaystyle+C\sum\limits_{j\geq 0}2^{-j/n}\frac{|B_{\Omega}(x,r)|^{\frac{1}{n}}}{|B(x,r)|^{\frac{1}{n}}}\log^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\left(\frac{|B(x,r)|^{\frac{1}{n}}}{|B_{{\Omega}}(x,r)|^{\frac{1}{n}}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\frac{|B_{\Omega}(x,r)|^{\frac{1}{n}}}{|B(x,r)|^{\frac{1}{n}}}+C\frac{|B_{\Omega}(x,r)|^{\frac{1}{n}}}{|B(x,r)|^{\frac{1}{n}}}\log^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\left(\frac{|B(x,r)|^{\frac{1}{n}}}{|B_{\Omega}(x,r)|^{\frac{1}{n}}}\right)=:A_{1}+A_{2}.

Hence, at least one of
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
must be not less than
\frac{1}{2}
. If
A_{1}\geq\frac{1}{2}
, then we have

|B_{\Omega}(x,r)|\geq cr^{n}

for some small constant
0<c<1
. It implies that
{\Omega}
is intrinsic Ahlfros
n
-regular. If
A_{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}
, then we have

\frac{|B_{\Omega}(x,r)|^{\frac{1}{n}}}{|B(x,r)|^{\frac{1}{n}}}\log^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\left(\frac{|B(x,r)|^{\frac{1}{n}}}{|B_{\Omega}(x,r)|^{\frac{1}{n}}}\right)\geq\tilde{c}

for a sufficiently small
0<\tilde{c}<1
. Since

|B_{\Omega}(x,r)|\leq|B(x,r)|,

it implies that there exists a small enough constant
c>0
such that

|B_{\Omega}(x,r)|\geq cr^{n}.

Hence,
{\Omega}
is intrinsic Ahlfors
n
-regular.

Case (
n<p<\infty
):

For every
j\geq 0
, fix a point
x_{j}\in\partial_{\Omega}B_{\Omega}(x,b_{j}r)
. By the definition of intrinsic metric, for arbitrary
\epsilon>0
, there exists a curve
\gamma_{\epsilon}\subset{\Omega}
joining
x
and
x_{j}
such that

l(\gamma_{\epsilon})<b_{j}r+\epsilon.

Choose one point
\tilde{x}_{j}\in\gamma_{\epsilon}

l\left(\gamma_{\epsilon}^{[\tilde{x}_{j},x_{j}]}\right)=b_{j}r-b_{j+1}r+\epsilon.

Then the triangle inequality gives

\tilde{x}_{j}\in B_{\Omega}(x,b_{j+1}r)\ {\rm and}\ |x_{j}-\tilde{x}_{j}|\leq b_{j}r-b_{j+1}r+\epsilon.

Apply inequalities (
1.8
) and (
2.4
) to the function

1\leq C\left(b_{j}r-b_{j+1}r+\epsilon\right)^{1-\frac{n}{p}}\frac{|B_{\Omega}(x,b_{j}r)|^{\frac{1}{p}}}{b_{j}r-b_{j+1}r}.

Since

b_{j}r-b_{j+1}r\leq C|B_{\Omega}(x,b_{j}r)|^{\frac{1}{n}}.

\displaystyle b_{0}r=\sum\limits_{j\geq 0}(b_{j}r-b_{j+1}r)\leq C\sum\limits_{j\geq 0}|B_{\Omega}(x,b_{j}r)|^{\frac{1}{n}}\leq C\sum\limits_{j\geq 0}2^{-j/n}|B_{\Omega}(x,r)|^{\frac{1}{n}}.

It implies that there exists a small enough constant
0<c<1
such that

|B_{\Omega}(x,r)|\geq cr^{n}.

So
{\Omega}
is intrinsic Ahlfors
n
-regular.

0<r<{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,diam\,}}({\Omega})
, we have

(2.7)

\fint_{B(x,r)\cap{\Omega}}\left|u(y)-u_{B(x,r)\cap{\Omega}}\right|dy\leq Cr\left(\fint_{B(x,\lambda r)\cap{\Omega}}\left|\nabla u(y)\right|^{p}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

with constants
C,\lambda
independent of
u
,
x
and
r
.

In next lemma, we will see that a local Sobolev-Poincaré imbedding domain of order
p
is also a
p
-Poincaré domain.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
{\Omega}\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
be a local Sobolev-Poincaré imbedding domain of order
p
for
1\leq p<\infty
. Then it is also a
p
-Poincaré domain.

Proof.

According to
p
is less than or equivalent to or larger than
n
, we divide the following argument into three cases.

Case (
1\leq p<n
):
For every
u\in W^{1,p}({\Omega})
,
x\in{\Omega}
and
0<r<{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,diam\,}}({\Omega})
, we have

\inf\limits_{c\in\mathbb{R}}\left(\int_{B(x,r)\cap{\Omega}}\left|u(y)-c\right|^{\frac{np}{n-p}}dy\right)^{\frac{n-p}{np}}\leq C\left(\int_{B(x,\lambda r)\cap{\Omega}}|\nabla u(y)|^{p}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

with constants
C,\lambda>1
independent of
u,x
and
r
. This inequality is equivalent to

\left(\int_{B(x,r)\cap{\Omega}}\left|u(y)-u_{B(x,r)\cap{\Omega}}\right|^{\frac{np}{n-p}}dy\right)^{\frac{n-p}{np}}\leq C\left(\int_{B(x,\lambda r)\cap{\Omega}}|\nabla u(y)|^{p}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

with a constant
C
which may be different to the one above. By Lemma
2.3
,
{\Omega}
is intrinsic Ahlfors
n
-regular, so the inequality above gives

\left(\fint_{B(x,r)\cap{\Omega}}\left|u(y)-u_{B(x,r)\cap{\Omega}}\right|^{\frac{np}{n-p}}dy\right)^{\frac{n-p}{np}}\leq Cr\left(\fint_{B(x,\lambda r)\cap{\Omega}}|\nabla u(y)|^{p}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Then the Hölder inequality implies

\fint_{B(x,r)\cap{\Omega}}\left|u(y)-u_{B(x,r)\cap{\Omega}}\right|dy\leq Cr\left(\fint_{B(x,\lambda r)\cap{\Omega}}|\nabla u(y)|^{p}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

It shows
{\Omega}\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
is a
p
-Poincaré domain.

Case (
p=n
):
For every
u\in W^{1,n}({\Omega})
,
x\in{\Omega}
and
0<r<{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,diam\,}}({\Omega})
, we have

\inf\limits_{c\in\mathbb{R}}\int_{B(x,r)\cap\Omega}\exp\left(\frac{A|u(y)-c|}{\|\nabla u\|_{L^{n}(B(x,\lambda r)\cap\Omega)}}\right)^{\frac{n}{n-1}}\,dy\leq C|B(x,r)|.

Since
{\Omega}
is intrinsic Ahlfors
n
-regular, the inequality above is equivalent to

\fint_{B(x,r)\cap{\Omega}}\exp\left(\frac{A|u(y)-u_{B(x,r)\cap{\Omega}}|}{\|\nabla u\|_{L^{n}\left(B(x,\lambda r)\cap{\Omega}\right)}}\right)^{\frac{n}{n-1}}dy\leq C.

It is easy to check the function

f(t)=\exp(t^{\frac{n}{n-1}})

is convex on
(0,\infty)
. Then the Jensen inequality and the fact that
{\Omega}
is intrinsic Ahlfors
n
-regular imply the following inequality that

\fint_{B(x,r)\cap{\Omega}}\left|u(y)-u_{B(x,r)\cap{\Omega}}\right|dy\leq Cr\left(\fint_{B(x,\lambda r)\cap{\Omega}}|\nabla u(y)|^{n}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}.

It shows
{\Omega}
is a
n
-Poincaré domain.

Case (
n<p<\infty
):
For every
u\in W^{1,p}({\Omega})
,
x\in{\Omega}
and
0<r<{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,diam\,}}({\Omega})
, we have

\displaystyle\left|u(x_{1})-u(x_{2})\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{1-\frac{n}{p}}\left(\int_{B(x,\lambda r)\cap{\Omega}}|\nabla u(y)^{p}dy|\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Cr^{1-\frac{n}{p}}\left(\int_{B(x,\lambda r)\cap{\Omega}}|\nabla u(y)|^{p}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

for almost every
x_{1},x_{2}\in B(x,r)\cap{\Omega}
. Do the integration twice over the set
B(x,r)\cap{\Omega}
. Then the triangle inequality and the fact
{\Omega}
is intrinsic Ahlfors
n
-regular give the following inequality that

\displaystyle\fint_{B(x,r)\cap{\Omega}}\left|u(y)-u_{B(x,r)\cap{\Omega}}\right|dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\fint_{B(x,r)\cap{\Omega}}\fint_{B(x,r)\cap{\Omega}}|u(x_{1})-u(x_{2})|dx_{1}dx_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Cr\left(\fint_{B(x,\lambda r)\cap{\Omega}}|\nabla u(y)|^{p}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

It shows
{\Omega}
is a
p
-Poincaré domain.
∎

Implied by Lemma
2.1
, to prove the sufficient part of Theorem
1.1
, we should show the domain
{\Omega}
is quasiconvex and
LLC
. For a metric measure space
(X,d,\mu)
, we say
\mu
is doubling, if there exists a large enough constant
C>1
such that for every
x\in X
and
0<r<{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,diam\,}}(X)
, we have

\mu(B(x,2r))\leq C\mu(B(x,r)).

It was proved by David and Semmes in
[
9
]
that a complete
p
-Poincaré space
(X,d,\mu)
with
1\leq p<\infty
and a doubling
\mu
is quasiconvex. However, a domain
{\Omega}\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
obviously is not complete. However, the Sobolev extension property of relative domains can help us to overcome this gap.

Definition 2.8
.

A domain
{\Omega}\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
is called a
W^{1,p}
-extension domain for
1\leq p\leq\infty
, if for every
u\in W^{1,p}({\Omega})
, there exists a function
E(u)\in W^{1,p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
such that the restriction of
E(u)
onto the domain
{\Omega}
always equals to
u
and the inequality

\|E(u)\|_{W^{1,p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}\leq C\|u\|_{W^{1,p}({\Omega})}

holds with a constant
C
independent of
u
.

The study of geometrical and analytical properties of Sobolev extension domains has been one of the central topics in analysis for long time. In
[
7
,
56
]
, Calderón and Stein proved every Lipschitz domain is a
W^{1,p}
-extension domain for every
1\leq p\leq\infty
. In few years, Jones generalized this result to the class of the so-called
(\epsilon,\delta)
-domains. It is a much larger class than the class of Lipschitz domains. By the result in
[
25
,
26
]
from Hajłasz, Koskela and Tuominen, for every
W^{1,p}
-extension domain with
1<p<\infty
, there always exists a bounded linear extension operator. Also refer to
[
51
,
52
]
for this result. There are also so many other interesting articles about Sobolev extension theory that we cannot list all of them in references. For those who are interested in it, please search them online. Since the Euclidean space
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
is obviously a
p
-PI space for every
1<p<\infty
, the following lemma is a special case of
[
16
, Proposition 1.9]
. Please refer to
[
16
]
for the definition of
p
-PI spaces.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
{\Omega}\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
be an Ahlfors
n
-regular
p
-Poincaré domain for
1<p<\infty
. Then it is also a
W^{1,p}
-extension domain. Especially, a local Sobolev-Poincaré embedding domain of order
p
for
1<p<\infty
is a
W^{1,p}
-extension domain.

The Hölder inequality directly implies that a
p
-Poincaré domain is also a
\tilde{p}
-Poincaré domain for every
1\leq p\leq\tilde{p}<\infty
. Hence, an Ahlfors
n
-regular
p
-Poincaré domain is always a
W^{1,\tilde{p}}
-extension domain for arbitrary
1\leq p<\tilde{p}<\infty
.
Let us recall the definition of
p
-capacity which can be found in
[
21
,
28
,
43
,
48
]
.

Definition 2.9
.

A condenser in a domain
{\Omega}\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
is a pair
(E,F)
of bounded compact connected subsets of
\overline{{\Omega}}
with

{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,dist\,}}(E,F)>0.

Fix
1\leq p<\infty
.The set of
p
-admissible functions for the triple
(E,F;{\Omega})
is

\mathcal{W}_{p}(E,F;{\Omega}):=\left\{u\in W^{1,p}({\Omega})\cap C({\Omega}\cup E\cup F):u\big|_{E}\geq 1,u\big|_{F}\leq 0\right\}.

We define the
p
-capacity of the pair
(E,F)
with respect to
{\Omega}
by setting

Cap_{p}(E,F;{\Omega}):=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{W}_{p}(E,F;{\Omega})}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{p}dx.

For a pair
(U,V)
of arbitrary bounded connected open subsets of
{\Omega}
with
{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,dist\,}}(U,V)>0
, we define the
p
-capacity by setting

Cap_{p}(U,V;{\Omega}):=\sup_{E\subset U,F\subset F}Cap_{p}(E,F;{\Omega}),

where the supremum is taken over all compact connected subsets of
{\Omega}
with
E\subset U
and
F\subset V
.

For a
W^{1,p}
-extension domain, the following lemma tells us the
p
-capacity with respect to the full space
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
can be controlled from above by the
p
-capacity with respect to the domain. The readers can find a proof in
[
36
]

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
{\Omega}\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
be a bounded
W^{1,p}
-extension domain for
1<p<\infty
. Then for every bounded connected open subsets
U,V\subset{\Omega}
with

{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,dist\,}}(U,V)>0,

the inequality

(2.8)

Cap_{p}\left(U,V;{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}\right)\leq CCap_{p}\left(U,V;{\Omega}\right)

holds with a constant
C
independent of
U
and
V
.

In the existing literature, the domain supporting (
2.8
) is called a
p
-quasiextremal distance domain. See
[
19
]
for
p=n
and
[
36
]
for arbitrary
1<p<\infty
. It was also proved in
[
36
]
that a
p
-quasiextremal distance domain for
n\leq p<\infty
must be quasiconvex. Now, we are ready to prove that a local Sobolev-Poincaré imbedding domain of order
p
for
1\leq p<\infty
must be quasiconvex.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
{\Omega}\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
be a bounded local Sobolev-Poincaré imbedding domain of order
p
for
1\leq p<\infty
. Then it must be quasiconvex.

Proof.

Let
{\Omega}\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
be a local Sobolev-Poincaré imbedding domain of order
p
for
1\leq p<\infty
. The Lemma
2.3
tells us that it is Ahlfors
n
-regular. By Lemma
2.4
and the Hölder inequality,
{\Omega}
is a
\tilde{p}
-Poincaré domain for some
n\leq\tilde{p}<\infty
. Then, by Lemma
2.5
, it is a
W^{1,\tilde{p}}
-extension domain. Finally, by Lemma
2.6
, it satisfies the inequality (
2.8
) for
\tilde{p}
-capacity and is a
\tilde{p}
-quasiextremal distance domain. By the result in
[
36
]
from Koskela,
{\Omega}
is quasiconvex.
∎

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

3.1.
From uniform condition to local Sobolev-Poincaré imbedding

In this section, we will prove the sufficient part of Theorem
1.1
. We will show that a uniform domain is also a local Sobolev-Poincaré imbedding domain of order
p
for all
1\leq p\leq n
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
{\Omega}\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
be a uniform domain. Then it is also a local Sobolev-Poincaré imbedding domain of order
p
for every
1\leq p\leq n
.

Proof.

Let
\Omega
be a
\epsilon_{0}
-uniform domain for
0<\epsilon_{0}\leq 1
. Fix
x_{0}\in{\Omega}
and
0<r<{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,diam\,}}({\Omega})
. By Lemma
2.2
, there always exists a
c_{0}
-John domain
\Omega_{r}\subset\Omega
with
c_{0}
only depends on
\epsilon_{0}
and a constant
\lambda>1
such that

B(x_{0},r)\cap\Omega\subset\Omega_{r}\subset B(x_{0},\lambda r)\cap\Omega.

Depending on if
p
equals to
n
or not, we divide the argument below into two cases.

Case (
1\leq p<n
):

Let
u\in{W}^{1,p}(\Omega)
be arbitrary. Since
B(x_{0},r)\cap\Omega\subset\Omega_{r}
, we have

\displaystyle\inf\limits_{c\in\mathbb{R}}\left(\int_{B(x_{0},r)\cap\Omega}|u(x)-c|^{\frac{np}{n-p}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{n-p}{np}}

\displaystyle\leq\inf\limits_{c\in\mathbb{R}}\left(\int_{\Omega_{r}}|u(x)-c|^{\frac{np}{n-p}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{n-p}{np}}.

Since
{\Omega}_{r}
is a
c_{0}
-John domain with some constant
c_{0}
only depends on
\epsilon_{0}
, by the result in
[
2
]
by Bojarski, there exists
C\geq 1
independent of
u,x_{0}
and
r
such that

\displaystyle\inf\limits_{c\in\mathbb{R}}\left(\int_{\Omega_{r}}|u(x)-c|^{\frac{np}{n-p}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{n-p}{np}}\leq C\left(\int_{\Omega_{r}}|\nabla u(x)|^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq C\left(\int_{B(x_{0},\lambda r)\cap\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Hence we have

\displaystyle\inf\limits_{c\in\mathbb{R}}\left(\int_{B(x_{0},r)\cap\Omega}|u(x)-c|^{\frac{np}{n-p}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{n-p}{np}}\leq C\left(\int_{B(x_{0},\lambda r)\cap\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

It implies
{\Omega}
is a local Sobolev-Poincaré imbedding domain of order
p
for every
1\leq p<n
.

Case (
p=n
):
Let
u\in{W}^{1,n}(\Omega)
be arbitrary. Since

B(x_{0},r)\cap\Omega\subset\Omega_{r}\subset B(x_{0},\lambda r)\cap{\Omega},

we have

\displaystyle\inf\limits_{c\in\mathbb{R}}\int_{B(x_{0},r)\cap\Omega}\exp\left(\frac{A|u(x)-c|}{\|\nabla u\|_{L^{n}(B(x,\lambda r)\cap\Omega)}}\right)^{\frac{n}{n-1}}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\inf\limits_{c\in\mathbb{R}}\int_{\Omega_{r}}\exp\left(\frac{A|u(x)-c|}{\|\nabla u\|_{L^{n}(\Omega_{r})}}\right)^{\frac{n}{n-1}}\,dx.

By
[
18
, Lemma 3.11]
, we know
\Omega_{r}
is also a
0
-carrot domain. By the result dues to Smith and Stegenga
[
53
]
, we have

\displaystyle\inf\limits_{c\in\mathbb{R}}\int_{\Omega_{r}}\exp\left(\frac{A|u(x)-c|}{\|\nabla u\|_{L^{n}(\Omega_{r})}}\right)^{\frac{n}{n-1}}\,dx\leq C|\Omega_{r}|

for a constant
C
independent of
x_{0},r,u
. Hence, we have

\displaystyle\inf\limits_{c\in\mathbb{R}}\int_{B(x_{0},r)\cap\Omega}\exp\left(\frac{A|u(x)-c|}{\|\nabla u\|_{L^{n}(B(x_{0},\lambda r)\cap\Omega)}}\right)^{\frac{n}{n-1}}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C|\Omega_{r}|\leq C|B(x_{0},\lambda r)\cap\Omega|

\displaystyle\leq C|B(x_{0},\lambda r)|\leq C|B(x_{0},r)|.

It implies the uniform domain
{\Omega}
is also a local Sobolev-Poincaré imbedding domain of order
n
.
∎

3.2.
From local Sobolev-Poincaré imbedding to
(LLC)

In this section, we prove the necessary part of Theorem
1.1
. We will show that under the assumption that the domain satisfies the slice condition, a local Sobolev-Poincaré imbedding domain of order
p
for
1\leq p\leq n
is also a uniform domain. By Lemma
2.1
, it suffices to prove that it is quasiconvex and
LLC
. The Lemma
2.7
already shows it is quasiconvex. By the definition, the fact that a domain is quasiconvex implies that this domain is also
LLC(1)
. Hence, we only need to show it is
LLC(2)
. According to
p
equals to
n
or not, we divide the argument into two separated cases.

First, let us consider the case that
1\leq p<n
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
be arbitrary for
1\leq p<n
. If there exist constants
C,\lambda>1
such that the inequality

(3.1)

\displaystyle\inf\limits_{c\in\mathbb{R}}\left(\int_{B(x_{0},r)\cap\Omega}|u(x)-c|^{\frac{np}{n-p}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{n-p}{np}}\leq C\left(\int_{B(x_{0},\lambda r)\cap\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

holds for every
x_{0}\in\Omega
and
0<r<\infty
, then
\Omega
is
LLC
.

Proof.

As we know, the Lemma
2.7
tells us that
{\Omega}
is quasiconvex. By the definitions, a quasiconvex domain must be
LLC(1)
. So we only need to show that
{\Omega}
is
LLC(2)
. Without loss of generality, we can only consider the case that
x_{1},x_{2}\in\Omega\backslash B(x_{0},r)
for some
x_{0}\in\Omega
and
0<r<{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,diam\,}}({\Omega})
. Furthermore, we can assume that
x_{1},x_{2}\in\partial B(x_{0},r)\cap\Omega
. Assume that
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
are not in the same component of
\Omega\backslash{B(x_{0},br)}
for
b\in(0,1)
, it suffices to find a uniform lower bound for
b
. We may assume
b<\frac{1}{16}
. Denote by
\Omega_{i}
the connected component of
\Omega\backslash{B(x_{0},br)}
which contains
x_{i}
for
i=1,2
, then
\Omega_{1}\cap\Omega_{2}=\emptyset
. Without loss of generality, we can only consider the case that
0<r\leq r_{o}
where
r_{o}
comes from the fact that
{\Omega}
is intrinsic Ahlfors
n
-regular. If we proved that for every
0<r\leq r_{o}
, there exists
0<a<1
such that
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
can be connected by a locally rectifiable curve
\gamma\subset{\Omega}\setminus B(x_{0},ar)
. Then for every
r_{o}<r<{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,diam\,}}({\Omega})
,
x_{1},x_{2}
can be connected by a locally rectifiable curve

\gamma\subset{\Omega}\setminus B\left(x_{0},\frac{ar_{o}}{{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,diam\,}}({\Omega})}r\right).

Fix
0<r\leq r_{o}
. Define
F_{i}
to be the connected component of
\Omega\cap B(x_{i},\frac{r}{2})
which contains
x_{i}
for
i=1,2
.
Obviously, we have
F_{1},F_{2}\subset B(x_{0},2r)
,
F_{1}\cap F_{2}=\emptyset
and
F_{i}\cap B(x_{0},\frac{r}{2})=\emptyset
for
i=1,2
. Since
{\Omega}
is intrinsic Ahlfors
n
-regular, there exists a sufficient small constant
0<c<1
such that we have

|F_{1}|\geq cr^{n}\ {\rm and}\ |F_{2}|\geq cr^{n}.

Define a function
u
on
{\Omega}
by setting

\displaystyle u(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1,&x\in\Omega_{2}\backslash B(x_{0},\frac{r}{2})\\
\frac{1}{\log\frac{1}{2b}}log\frac{|x-x_{0}|}{br},&x\in\Omega_{2}\cap\left(B(x_{0},\frac{r}{2})\backslash B(x_{0},br)\right)\\
0,&elsewhere.\end{array}\right.

Obviously, this function is well defined. Noting that

\displaystyle|\nabla u(x)|=\frac{1}{\log\frac{1}{2b}}\frac{\chi_{\Omega_{2}\cap\left(B(x_{0},\frac{r}{2})\backslash B(x_{0},br)\right)}(x)}{|x-x_{0}|},

we have

(3.2)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{B(x_{0},\lambda r)\cap\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\log\frac{1}{2b}}\left(\int_{\Omega_{2}\cap\left(B(x_{0},\frac{r}{2})\backslash B(x_{0},br)\right)}|x-x_{0}|^{-p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\log\frac{1}{2b}}r^{\frac{n-p}{p}}\left[\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{n-p}-b^{n-p}\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{Cr^{\frac{n-p}{p}}}{\log\frac{1}{2b}}.

For each
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
|u-c|\geq\frac{1}{2}
holds either on
\Omega_{2}\cap\left(B(x_{0},r)\setminus B\left(x_{0},\frac{r}{2}\right)\right)
or on
\Omega_{1}\cap\left(B(x_{0},r)\setminus B\left(x_{0},\frac{r}{2}\right)\right)
. Since
{\Omega}
is intrinsic Ahlfors
n
-regular, we have both

\left|{\Omega}_{i}\cap\left(B(x_{0},r)\setminus B\left(x_{0},\frac{r}{2}\right)\right)\right|\geq cr^{n}

for
i=1,2
and a sufficient small constant
0<c<1
. Hence, we have

(3.3)

\displaystyle\inf\limits_{c\in\mathbb{R}}\left(\int_{B(x_{0},r)\cap\Omega}|u(x)-c|^{\frac{np}{n-p}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{n-p}{np}}\geq cr^{\frac{n-p}{n}},

for a sufficient small
0<c<1
. By combining inequalities (
1.6
), (
3.2
) and (
3.3
), we obtain

\frac{1}{\log\frac{1}{2b}}>c

for some sufficient small
0<c<1
. It gives a lower bound to
b
and proves
{\Omega}
is
LLC(2)
.
∎

Next, we consider the case that
p=n
.

Theorem 3.3
.

If for every
u\in W^{1,n}(\Omega)
, there exist constants
C,\lambda>1
the following inequality

(3.4)

\displaystyle\inf\limits_{c\in\mathbb{R}}\int_{B(x_{0},r)\cap\Omega}\exp\left(\frac{A|u(x)-c|}{\|\nabla u\|_{L^{n}(B(x_{0},\lambda r)\cap\Omega)}}\right)^{\frac{n}{n-1}}\,dx\leq C|B(x_{0},r)|

holds with every
x_{0}\in\Omega
and
0<r<{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,diam\,}}\Omega
, then
\Omega
is
LLC
.

Proof.

As we know, the Lemma
2.7
implies that
{\Omega}
is quasiconvex. By the definitions, a quasiconvex domain must be
LLC(1)
. So we only need to show that
{\Omega}
is
LLC(2)
below. All argument is almost as same as the argument to show that
{\Omega}
is
LLC(2)
in the proof of Theorem
3.2
. The difference is that we need to replace the inequality (
1.6
) by the inequality (
1.7
) and to estimate the
n
-Dirichlet energy of the test function
u
. Some simple computation gives

\displaystyle\left(\int_{B(x_{0},\lambda r)\cap\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{n}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\log\frac{1}{2b}}\left(\int_{\Omega_{2}\cap\left(B(x_{0},\frac{r}{2})\backslash B(x_{0},br)\right)}|x-x_{0}|^{n}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}

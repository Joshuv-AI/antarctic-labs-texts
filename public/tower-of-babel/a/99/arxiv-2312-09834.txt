Anisotropic Proximal Point Algorithm
Emanuel Laude, Panagiotis Patrinos

Abstract
In this paper we study a nonlinear dual space preconditioning approach for the relaxed Proximal Point Algorithm (PPA) with application to monotone inclusions, called anisotropic PPA. The algorithm is an instance of Luque’s nonlinear PPA wherein the nonlinear preconditioner is chosen as the gradient of a Legendre convex function. Since the preconditioned operator is nonmonotone in general, convergence cannot be shown using standard arguments, unless the preconditioner exhibits isotropy (preserves directions) as in existing literature. To address the broader applicability we show convergence along subsequences invoking a Bregman version of Fejèr-monotonicity in the dual space. Via a nonlinear generalization of Moreau’s decomposition for operators, we provide a dual interpretation of the algorithm in terms of a forward iteration applied to a D D -firmly nonexpansive mapping which involves the Bregman resolvent. For a suitable preconditioner, convergence rates of arbitrary order are derived under a mild Hölder growth condition. Finally, we discuss an anisotropic generalization of the proximal augmented Lagrangian method obtained via the propsed scheme. This has strong connections to Rockafellar’s generalized and sharp Lagrangian functions.

Introduction

1.1
Motivation and prior work

This paper introduces a nonlinear dual space preconditioning scheme for the relaxed
Proximal Point Algorithm
(PPA) aimed at solving monotone and relatively cohypomonotone
[
27
]
inclusion problems. Our proposed algorithm left-preconditions the operator with the gradient of a Legendre convex function which renders our method akin to an adapted version of the classical PPA. Specifically, our proposed scheme represents an instance of Luque’s nonlinear PPA
[
18
,
21
]
, wherein the preconditioner itself is another maximal monotone mapping.

However, to establish convergence using standard arguments, Luque’s framework assumes isotropy in the preconditioner, which implies the preservation of the update direction in relation to the classical PPA update. This assumption can restrict the general applicability of the method. To address this limitation, we show convergence in the dual space by harnessing the gradient structure intrinsic to the preconditioner.

A widely adopted approach for nonlinear extensions of the PPA is the Bregman PPA
[
36
,
11
]
. Similar to the Bregman PPA formulated using the well-established Bregman or
D
-resolvent
[
36
,
2
,
4
,
34
,
11
]
, the anisotropic PPA can be expressed in terms of the anisotropic resolvent. In this work we reveal a relationship between the anisotropic resolvent and the Bregman resolvent, establishing a duality between them which can be be expressed within the more general framework of convolutions of maximal monotone operators
[
19
,
18
]
more commonly referred to as the parallel sum of two operators
[
28
]
. Specifically, when the operator represents a subdifferential, this relationship manifests as a Bregman variant of Moreau’s decomposition
[
9
]
. This allows one to interpret anisotropic PPA as a nonlinearly preconditioned forward-iteration applied to a
D
-firmly nonexpansive mapping
[
2
]
. Moreover, while the anisotropic resolvent aligns with dual-space preconditioning, the
D
-resolvent finds its interpretation in primal-space preconditioning. This is achieved by applying the gradient mapping of a Legendre convex function to the primal variables within the classical resolvent. The aforementioned nonlinearly preconditioned forward iteration applied to a
D
-firmly nonexpansive gradient map was also studied by
[
22
]
who revealed another connection between dual and primal space preconditioning achieved by “flipping” the roles of preconditioner
\nabla\phi^{*}
and operator
\partial f
.
In light of
[
37
,
15
,
14
]
, the notion of
D
-firm nonexpansiveness of a subdifferential operator is also connected to anisotropic smoothness
[
13
,
15
,
14
]
and the algorithm in
[
22
]
can be interpreted in terms of a majorize-minimize scheme called anisotropic proximal gradient
[
14
]
.

The step-size in the forward iteration interpretation of anisotropic PPA is interconnected to a relaxation step in the PPA. Specifically, a non-unit relaxation parameter allows one to derive a convergent scheme beyond monotonicity to handle relatively cohypomonotone mappings
[
27
]
, a generalization of cohypomonotoncity
[
8
,
12
]
which is itself a generalization of classical monotonicity.

1.2
Contributions

The main objective of this manuscript is to study the convergence of the anisotropic proximal point algorithm with under-relaxation for monotone and cohypomonotone inclusions beyond the isotropic case and unit relaxation parameter as in
[
18
]
. Since existing techniques do not apply in the anisotropic case, we prove global convergence along subsequences using an unusual technique that is based on a Bregman version of Fejèr-monotonicity in the dual space.

We analyze the local convergence rate of our method under a mild Hölder growth condition restricting the preconditioner to the inverse gradient of a higher-power of the
\ell^{p}
-norm. If the growth of the preconditioner dominates the inverse growth of the operator we show that the algorithm converges locally with arbitrary order.

Error tolerances in the update are incorporated using
\varepsilon
-enlargements of monotone operators
[
5
]
.

Finally, we discuss an anisotropic generalization of the proximal augmented Lagrangian method obtained via the proposed scheme. This has strong connections to Rockafellar’s generalized and sharp Lagrangians
[
32
]
.

1.3
Standing assumptions and notation

Let
X
be a Euclidean space. Although primal and dual space in this case are identical, to improve clarity we will stick to the convention that dual objects are element of
X^{*}=X
. Hence, we interpret the Euclidean inner product in terms of the dual pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:X\times X^{*}\to\mathbb{R}
and denote by
\|x\|_{2}=\sqrt{\langle x,x\rangle}
the Euclidean norm.
If not stated otherwise
T:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
is a set-valued, maximal monotone mapping. Let
\phi:X\to\mathbb{R}
be a strictly convex, smooth and super-coercive function
\phi:X\to\mathbb{R}
, referred to as the prox-function in the remainder of this manuscript. Then the same is true for the convex conjugate prox-function
\phi^{*}:X^{*}\to\mathbb{R}
, and its gradient is the bijection
\nabla\phi:X\to X^{*}
with inverse
\nabla\phi^{*}
. Throughout we assume that
\nabla\phi(0)=0
,
and hence
\nabla\phi^{*}(0)=0
. Note that this is not a big restriction since we can always redefine
\phi
as
\phi(x)-\langle x,\nabla\phi(0)\rangle
.
An important example is the separable function
\phi(x)=\frac{1}{p}\|x\|_{p}^{p}
with
p>2
. Then
\phi^{*}(v)=\frac{1}{q}\|v\|_{q}^{q}
with
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
. Another interesting example is
\phi(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\cosh(x_{i})
. In addition we introduce the epi-scaling
\lambda\star\phi
of
\phi

(\lambda\star\phi)(x)=\begin{cases}\lambda\phi(\lambda^{-1}x),\quad&\lambda>0\\
\delta_{\{0\}}(x),\quad&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

(1.1)

whose convex conjugate amounts to
(\lambda\star\phi)^{*}=\lambda\phi^{*}.
Let
C\subset X
be a closed convex nonemtpy set. Then we denote by
\Pi_{C}(y):=\argmin_{x\in C}\|x-y\|_{2}
the Euclidean projection of
x
onto
C
and by
N_{C}(y):=\{v\in X^{*}:\langle x-y,v\rangle\leq 0,\;\forall x\in C\}
the normal cone of
C
at
y\in C
with the convention
N_{C}(y)=\emptyset
if
y\notin C
.

2
Anisotropic Proximal Point Algorithm with relaxation

In this paper we investigate a nonlinear proximal point algorithm for solving monotone inclusion problems that are defined as follows: Given a (maximally) monotone mapping
T:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
we seek to compute a point
x^{\star}\in X
, such that
0\in T(x^{\star})
. This formulation subsumes a multitude of problems such as convex nonsmooth optimization, variational inequalities and convex-concave saddle-point problems.
We consider the following proximal point-type algorithm with under-relaxation parameter
\lambda\in(0,1]
which, at each iteration
k
, updates the variable
x^{k}
by computing a triplet
(x^{k+1},z^{k},v^{k})\in X\times X\times X^{*}
that satisfies

\displaystyle v^{k}

\displaystyle\in T(z^{k})

(2.1a)

\displaystyle z^{k}

\displaystyle=x^{k}-\nabla\phi^{*}(v^{k})

(2.1b)

\displaystyle x^{k+1}

\displaystyle=x^{k}+\lambda(z^{k}-x^{k}).

(2.1c)

This shows that fixed-points
x^{\star}
of the algorithm
Eq.

2.1
correspond to points
x^{\star}\in(\nabla\phi^{*}\circ T)^{-1}(0)
.
Since
\nabla\phi^{*}(0)=0
,

x^{\star}\in(\nabla\phi^{*}\circ T)^{-1}(0)\Leftrightarrow x^{\star}\in T^{-1}(0)=:\zer T,

and hence fixed-points of the algorithm are zeros of
T
.

Next, we define the
anisotropic
resolvent of
T
:

Definition 2.1
.

Let
T:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
be a set-valued mapping. Then the mapping
\operatorname{ares}_{T}^{\phi}:X\rightrightarrows X
defined by

\operatorname{ares}_{T}^{\phi}:=(\id+\nabla\phi^{*}\circ T)^{-1},

is called the anisotropic resolvent of
T
.

Using the above definition the algorithm can be rewritten more compactly as

\displaystyle x^{k+1}=(1-\lambda)x^{k}+\lambda\operatorname{ares}_{T}^{\phi}(x^{k}).

(2.2)

Furthermore, this shows that the proposed algorithm is identical to the classical relaxed
Proximal Point Algorithm
(PPA) applied to the mapping
\nabla\phi^{*}\circ T
. In spite of
\nabla\phi^{*}
and
T
being monotone, the composition
\nabla\phi^{*}\circ T
is potentially nonmonotone as showcased by the following counter-example:

Example 2.2
.

Choose the monotone linear operator
T:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
defined by the matrix

\displaystyle T=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{bmatrix},

(2.3)

and the convex and smooth prox-function
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
with
\phi=\frac{1}{p}\|\cdot\|_{p}^{p}
with
p=4
. Hence,
\phi^{*}(v_{1},v_{2})=\frac{1}{q}|v_{1}|^{q}+\frac{1}{q}|v_{2}|^{q}
with
q=\frac{4}{3}
and
\nabla\phi^{*}(v_{1},v_{2})=(\sign(v_{1})|v_{1}|^{\frac{1}{3}},\sign(v_{2})|v_{2}|^{\frac{1}{3}})
. For any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
we have

\nabla\phi^{*}(T(x))=(\sign(x_{2})|x_{2}|^{\frac{1}{3}},-\sign(x_{1})|x_{1}|^{\frac{1}{3}})

and thus for
y=0
:

\displaystyle\langle\nabla\phi^{*}(T(x))-\nabla\phi^{*}(T(y)),x-y\rangle=\langle\nabla\phi^{*}(T(x)),x\rangle

\displaystyle=\sign(x_{2})|x_{2}|^{\frac{1}{3}}x_{1}-\sign(x_{1})|x_{1}|^{\frac{1}{3}}x_{2}.

Choose
x=(1,2)
. Then we obtain

\displaystyle\langle\nabla\phi^{*}(T(x))-\nabla\phi^{*}(T(y)),x-y\rangle

\displaystyle=\sqrt[3]{2}-2<0.

In light of the above example, existing convergence results for classical PPA do not apply in our case.

For
T=\partial f
being the subdifferential of a convex, proper lsc function
f:X\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
anisotropic PPA with
\lambda=1
corresponds to the update

\displaystyle x^{k+1}=\argmin_{x\in X}\big\{f(x)+\phi(x^{k}-x)\big\},

(2.4)

which can be seen as an instance of the recent anisotropic
Proximal Gradient Method

[
14
]
by taking the smooth part to be
0
.
For
\phi=\frac{1}{p}\|\cdot\|_{2}^{p}
,
p>2
, this is identical to the higher-order proximal point algorithm studied by
[
24
]
. More generally, if
\phi=\frac{1}{p}\|\cdot\|_{p}^{p}
,
p>2
the function minimization case is recently explored in
[
25
]
.

It is instructive to observe that the anisotropic proximal point algorithm (with
\lambda=1
) differs from the well-known Bregman proximal point algorithm
[
11
,
34
]
which, at each iteration
k
updates the variable
x^{k}
by computing a pair
(x^{k+1},v^{k})\in X\times X^{*}
that satisfies:

\displaystyle v^{k}

\displaystyle\in T(x^{k+1})

(2.5a)

\displaystyle x^{k+1}

\displaystyle=\nabla\phi^{*}(\nabla\phi(x^{k})-v^{k}).

(2.5b)

The anisotropic PPA (for
\lambda=1
) resp. the Bregman PPA can be interpreted in terms of dual space resp. primal space nonlinear preconditioning for the classical PPA: While the anisotropic PPA applies the inverse gradient
\nabla\phi^{*}
to the dual iterates, resulting in update directions
\nabla\phi^{*}(v^{k})
, the Bregman PPA preconditions the primal iterates with the gradient of
\phi
which results in the update
x^{k+1}=\nabla\phi^{*}(\nabla\phi(x^{k})-v^{k})
. In fact, the difference between Bregman PPA and anisotropic PPA boils down to an interchange of the roles of
T
and
\nabla\phi^{*}
.
In case
\phi(x)=\frac{1}{2}\langle x,Ax\rangle
, for
A
being symmetric and positive definite,
\nabla\phi(x)=Ax
and
\nabla\phi^{*}(v)=A^{-1}v
are linear and hence the two algorithms are identical since
\nabla\phi^{*}(\nabla\phi(x^{k})-v^{k})=A^{-1}Ax^{k}-A^{-1}v^{k}=x^{k}-\nabla\phi^{*}(v^{k})
.

In spite of the differences for nonquadratic
\phi
, there holds an important duality relation between the Bregman resolvent and the anisotropic resolvent as examined in the next section.

Definition 3.1

Let

\operatorname{bres}_{S}^{\phi^{*}}:X^{*}\rightrightarrows X^{*}

In light of

We recall the definition of

Definition 3.2

Let
C\subseteq X^{*}

A:C\to X^{*}

\langle\nabla\phi^{*}(A(x))-\nabla\phi^{*}(A(y)),A(x)-A(y)\rangle\leq\langle\nabla\phi^{*}(x)-\nabla\phi^{*}(y),A(x)-A(y)\rangle.

In the Euclidean case, i.e.,
\nabla\phi^{*}=\id

\displaystyle\langle A(x)-A(y),A(x)-A(y)\rangle\leq\langle x-y,A(x)-A(y)\rangle,

(3.1)

which can be rewritten by completing the square

\displaystyle\|A(x)-A(y)\|^{2}+\|(\id-A)(x)-(\id-A)(y)\|^{2}\leq\|x-y\|^{2}.

(3.2)

In light of
[
4
, Remark 3.3]

\nabla\phi^{*}
-firmly nonexansive mappings are at most single-valued.
We have the following specialization of
[
4

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
S:X^{*}\rightrightarrows X
be a set-valued monotone mapping. Then
\operatorname{bres}_{S}^{\phi^{*}}:=(\nabla\phi^{*}+S)^{-1}\circ\nabla\phi^{*}

\nabla\phi^{*}

Next we state a specialization of

showing that any

-firmly nonexpansive mapping

can be written in terms of the Bregman resolvent of a monotone mapping:

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
C\subseteq X^{*}
and
A:C\to X^{*}
, and set
F_{A}=\nabla\phi^{*}\circ A^{-1}-\nabla\phi^{*}
. Then

(i)

The Bregman resolvent of
F_{A}
is A:
\operatorname{bres}_{F_{A}}^{\phi^{*}}=A
.

(ii)

If
A
is
\nabla\phi^{*}
-firmly nonexpansive, then
F_{A}
is monotone.

(iii)

If
A
is
\nabla\phi^{*}
-firmly nonexpansive, then
C=X^{*}\Leftrightarrow F_{A}
is maximal monotone.

An important instance for
\nabla\phi^{*}
-firm nonexpansiveness is the class of mappings
A=\partial f\circ\nabla\phi^{*}
for an infimal convolution
f=\xi\mathbin{\square}\phi
,
[
37
]
which is equivalent to the notion of anisotropic smoothness
[
15
,
14
]
of
f
.
Next we prove the following relation between the anisotropic resolvent of
T
and the Bregman resolvent of
T^{-1}
, which generalizes Moreau’s decomposition for the Bregman proximal mapping
[
9
]
to operators:

Proposition 3.5

(Moreau’s decomposition)
.

Let
T:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
be a set-valued mapping. Then the following identity holds:

\displaystyle\operatorname{ares}_{T}^{\phi}=\id-\nabla\phi^{*}\circ\operatorname{bres}_{T^{-1}}^{\phi^{*}}\circ\nabla\phi.

(3.3)

Proof.

We have that:

\displaystyle x\in\operatorname{ares}_{T}^{\phi}(y)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow x\in(\id+\nabla\phi^{*}\circ T)^{-1}(y)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow y\in(\id+\nabla\phi^{*}\circ T)(x)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\nabla\phi(y-x)\in T(x)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow x\in T^{-1}(\nabla\phi(y-x))

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\nabla\phi^{*}(\nabla\phi(y))-\nabla\phi^{*}(\nabla\phi(y-x))\in T^{-1}(\nabla\phi(y-x))

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\nabla\phi^{*}(\nabla\phi(y))\in(\nabla\phi^{*}+T^{-1})(\nabla\phi(y-x))

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\nabla\phi(y-x)\in((\nabla\phi^{*}+T^{-1})^{-1}\circ\nabla\phi^{*})(\nabla\phi(y))

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow x\in y-\nabla\phi^{*}(((\nabla\phi^{*}+T^{-1})^{-1}\circ\nabla\phi^{*})(\nabla\phi(y))

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow x\in y-\nabla\phi^{*}(\operatorname{bres}_{T^{-1}}^{\phi^{*}}(\nabla\phi(y)).\qed

Alternatively the result follows from
[
19
, Proposition 2.8]
with
S=\nabla\phi
being single-valued.
In light of
Proposition

3.5
anisotropic proximal point
Eq.

2.2
corresponds to the forward-iteration

\displaystyle x^{k+1}=(1-\lambda)x^{k}+\lambda\operatorname{ares}_{T}^{\phi}(x^{k})=x^{k}-\lambda(\nabla\phi^{*}\circ\operatorname{bres}_{T^{-1}}^{\phi^{*}}\circ\nabla\phi)(x^{k}).

(3.4)

Conversely, by the above results, if

\displaystyle x^{k+1}

\displaystyle=x^{k}-\lambda(\nabla\phi^{*}\circ A\circ\nabla\phi)(x^{k})

\displaystyle=x^{k}-\lambda(\nabla\phi^{*}\circ\operatorname{bres}_{F_{A}}^{\phi^{*}}\circ\nabla\phi)(x^{k})

where the second equality follows from

Proposition 3.8

Let

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
T:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
be a set-valued mapping and let
\rho\geq 0

\tau>0
. Then the following identity holds:

(\id+\tau\nabla\phi^{*}\circ T_{\rho})^{-1}=(1-\lambda)\id+\lambda(\id+\gamma\nabla\phi^{*}\circ T)^{-1},

for
\gamma=\tau+\rho
and
\lambda=\tfrac{\tau}{\gamma}\in(0,1]

Proof.

Note that
T_{\gamma}=\operatorname{bres}_{T^{-1}}^{\gamma\phi^{*}}\circ\nabla(\gamma\star\phi)

Invoking

3.5

(\gamma\star\phi)^{*}=\gamma\phi^{*}

\displaystyle\gamma\nabla\phi^{*}(T_{\gamma}(x))

\displaystyle=(x-(\id+\gamma\nabla\phi^{*}\circ T)^{-1}(x)).

Define
T_{\rho}^{-1}:=(T_{\rho})^{-1}=\rho\nabla\phi^{*}+T^{-1}
.
Then we have for any

\displaystyle(1-\lambda)x+\lambda(\id+\gamma\nabla\phi^{*}\circ T)^{-1}(x)

\displaystyle=x-\lambda(x-(\id+\gamma\nabla\phi^{*}\circ T)^{-1}(x))

\displaystyle=x-\tau\nabla\phi^{*}(T_{\gamma}(x))

\displaystyle=x-\tau\nabla\phi^{*}\big((\tau\nabla\phi^{*}+(\rho\nabla\phi^{*}+T^{-1}))^{-1}(x)\big)

\displaystyle=x-\tau\nabla\phi^{*}\big((\tau\nabla\phi^{*}+T_{\rho}^{-1})^{-1}(x)\big)

\displaystyle=x-\tau\nabla\phi^{*}\big(\operatorname{bres}_{T_{\rho}^{-1}}^{\tau\phi^{*}}(\nabla(\tau\star\phi)(x))\big)

\displaystyle=(\id+\tau\nabla\phi^{*}\circ T_{\rho})^{-1}(x),

where the second identity follows from
Eq.

3.8
since
\lambda\gamma=\tau
, the third by
\gamma=\tau+\rho
and the definition of
T_{\gamma}
the fourth by definition of
T_{\rho}^{-1}
and the last by
Proposition

3.5
.
∎

Thanks to the above result we can rewrite the relaxed PPA iteration in terms of the PPA iteration with unit relaxation parameter by replacing
T
with its Bregman–Yosida regularization.

Corollary 3.10
.

Let
T:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
be a set-valued mapping. Then the following identity holds:

(1-\lambda)\id+\lambda\operatorname{ares}_{T}^{\phi}=\operatorname{ares}_{T_{1-\lambda}}^{\lambda\star\phi}.

Proof.

Choose
\rho=1-\lambda
and
\tau=\lambda
. Hence,
\gamma=\tau+\rho=1
and
\tau/\gamma=\lambda
and we obtain the desired result from
Proposition

3.9
.
∎

Remark 3.11
.

In light of the above corollary we can rewrite the algorithm
Eq.

2.2
with under-relaxation parameter
\lambda\in(0,1]
as:

\displaystyle x^{k+1}=(1-\lambda)x^{k}+\lambda\operatorname{ares}_{T}^{\phi}(x^{k})=\operatorname{ares}_{T_{1-\lambda}}^{\lambda\star\phi}(x^{k}),

(3.9)

where the right hand side is anisotropic PPA with unit relaxation parameter and preconditioner
\lambda\nabla\phi^{*}
applied to the Bregman–Yosida regularization
T_{\rho}
of
T
with parameter
\rho=1-\lambda
.
Since the Bregman–Yosida regularization
T_{1-\lambda}
inherits all the necessary properties from
T
, we can harmlessly restrict the under-relaxation parameter
\lambda=1
in the analysis of the anisotropic proximal point algorithm
Eq.

2.2
. In particular
Eq.

2.1
applied to
T
with
\lambda<1
produces the same dual iterates

as the version without relaxation applied to
T_{1-\lambda}
:
We have

\displaystyle v^{k}\in T_{1-\lambda}(x^{k+1})\Leftrightarrow x^{k+1}\in(1-\lambda)\nabla\phi^{*}(v^{k})+T^{-1}(v^{k}).

(3.10)

This means equivalently that there is
z^{k}\in T^{-1}(v^{k})
such that
x^{k+1}=(1-\lambda)\nabla\phi^{*}(v^{k})+z^{k}
. Solving the identity for
z^{k}
since
x^{k+1}=x^{k}-\lambda\nabla\phi^{*}(v^{k})
we obtain
z^{k}=x^{k}-\nabla\phi^{*}(v^{k})
and hence
x^{k+1}=(1-\lambda)x^{k}+\lambda z^{k}
.

The following coercivity property is useful in the analysis of the proposed method:

Definition 3.12
.

Let
T:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
be a set-valued mapping. We say that
T
is coercive if

\lim_{\|x\|\to\infty}\inf\|Tx\|_{*}=\infty,

where
\inf\emptyset:=\infty
.

Clearly,
T
is coercive if
\dom T
is bounded.

As we show next
T_{\rho}
inherits the coercivity from
T
:

Lemma 3.13
.

Let
T:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
be a set-valued mapping. Then
T
is coercive if and only if
T_{\rho}
is coercive.

Proof.

Let
\|z^{\nu}\|\to\infty
and
v^{\nu}\in T_{\rho}(z^{\nu})=(\rho\nabla\phi^{*}+T^{-1})^{-1}(z^{\nu})
. This means that
z^{\nu}=\rho\nabla\phi^{*}(v^{\nu})+x^{\nu}
for some
x^{\nu}\in T^{-1}(v^{\nu})
.
Suppose that
\|v^{\nu}\|_{*}\not\to\infty
. This means we can find a subsequence
v^{\nu_{j}}\to v^{\infty}
. Since
\|z^{\nu_{j}}\|=\|\rho\nabla\phi^{*}(v^{\nu_{j}})+x^{\nu_{j}}\|\to\infty
and
\|z^{\nu_{j}}\|-\rho\|\nabla\phi(v^{\nu_{j}})\|\leq\|x^{\nu_{j}}\|
we have that
\|x^{\nu_{j}}\|\to\infty
. Since
T
is coercive we have that
\|v^{\nu_{j}}\|\to\infty
, a contradiction.

To prove the opposite direction assume that
T_{\rho}
is coercive and consider
\|x^{\nu}\|\to\infty
and
v^{\nu}\in T(x^{\nu})
. Suppose that
\|v^{\nu}\|_{*}\not\to\infty
. This means we can find a subsequence
v^{\nu_{j}}\to v^{\infty}
. We have that
z^{\nu}:=\rho\nabla\phi^{*}(v^{\nu})+x^{\nu}\in(T_{\rho})^{-1}(v^{\nu})
, and hence
\|z^{\nu_{j}}\|\to\infty
. By coercivity of
T_{\rho}
this means that
\|v^{\nu_{j}}\|\to\infty
, a contradiction.
∎

In this section we analyze the convergence of the algorithm. In light of
Corollaries

3.10
and
3.11
, we will harmlessly restrict
\lambda=1
. Then the variable

can be eliminated and the algorithm in

simplifies to:

\displaystyle v^{k}

\displaystyle\in T(x^{k+1})

(4.1a)

\displaystyle x^{k+1}

\displaystyle=x^{k}-\nabla\phi^{*}(v^{k}),

(4.1b)

or written in terms of the resolvent:

\displaystyle x^{k+1}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{ares}_{T}^{\phi}(x^{k}).

(4.2)

4.1
Isotropic case

4.1.1
Global convergence

In this subsection we recapitulate the global convergence of anisotropic PPA under isotropy providing a specialization of the main results of Luque
[
20
,
21
]
and refine the local convergence analysis invoking a recent result by Rockafellar
[
31
]
. Luque’s PPA takes the form
x^{k+1}=(I+S^{-1}\circ T)^{-1}(x^{k})
for a maximal monotone map
S
that preserves directions in the sense that
v\in S(y)
implies that
v=\lambda y
, for some
\lambda>0
. In fact, this assumption seems crucial as there exist choices for
T
and
S
in terms of rotators such that the algorithm is nonconvergent
[
20
]
.
For
S=\nabla\phi^{*}
being the gradient of a Legendre convex function directions are preserved if
\phi
exhibits isotropy: We say that
\phi
is
isotropic
if it can be written as

\displaystyle\phi(x)=\varphi(\|x\|_{2}),

(4.3)

for an increasing function
\varphi:[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
that vanishes only at zero. We refer to this algorithm as
isotropic
PPA. The more general anisotropic case is the focus of the next subsection.
Before we restrict ourselves to isotropic
\phi
, it is instructive to discuss a projective interpretation of the algorithm, which is classically used to incorporate error tolerances; see, e.g.,
[
33
]
. Besides its elegance the projection viewpoint improves our geometric intuition of the progress of the algorithm in both, the isotropic and the anisotropic case.
Recall that
\operatorname{ares}_{T}^{\phi}(x^{k})=(I+\nabla\phi^{*}\circ T)^{-1}(x^{k})
and hence,

\displaystyle\operatorname{ares}_{T}^{\phi}(x^{k})=x^{k}-\nabla\phi^{*}(v^{k})\quad\text{for a unique}\quad v^{k}\in T(\operatorname{ares}_{T}^{\phi}(x^{k})).

(4.4)

We define the set of solutions

\displaystyle\zer T:=\{x\in X:0\in T(x)\},

(4.5)

and at each iteration
k
we construct the half-space

\displaystyle H_{k}:=\{w\in X:\langle w-\operatorname{ares}_{T}^{\phi}(x^{k}),v^{k}\rangle\leq 0\},

(4.6)

whose normal and support vectors,
v^{k}=\nabla\phi(x^{k}-\operatorname{ares}_{T}^{\phi}(x^{k}))
and
\operatorname{ares}_{T}^{\phi}(x^{k})
, are readily available upon evaluation of the resolvent operator.

Then
H_{k}
strictly separates
x^{k}
from the solutions in
\zer T
, unless
x^{k}\in\zer T
. Furthermore, the proximal point
\operatorname{ares}_{T}^{\phi}(x^{k})
can be interpreted in terms of the
anisotropic
projection of the old iterate onto the half-space
H_{k}
, i.e., the following identity holds true:

\displaystyle\operatorname{ares}_{T}^{\phi}(x^{k})=\Pi_{H_{k}}^{\phi}(x^{k}):=\argmin_{x\in H_{k}}\,\phi(x^{k}-x).

(4.7)

Unfortunately, the anisotropic projection does not enjoy nonexpansiveness in the Euclidean sense in general as evidenced in
Fig.

1
.
An important exception is the class of isotropic
\phi
as defined above in
Eq.

4.3
. Then the anisotropic projection is identical to the Euclidean one. All of these properties are established in the next proposition:

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\phi\in\Gamma_{0}(X)
be Legendre and super-coercive with full domain and let
\lambda=1
. Then the following properties hold:

(i)

x^{\star}\in H_{k}

(ii)

x^{k}\not\in H_{k}
unless
x^{k}\in\zer T

(iii)

\operatorname{ares}_{T}^{\phi}(x^{k})
is the anisotropic projection of
x^{k}
onto
H_{k}
, i.e.,
Eq.

4.7

(iv)

If, in addition,
\phi=\varphi(\|\cdot\|_{2})
for some
\varphi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
increasing we have that:

\displaystyle\argmin_{x\in H_{k}}\,\phi(x^{k}-x)=\Pi_{H_{k}}(x^{k})=\argmin_{x\in H_{k}}\,\|x^{k}-x\|_{2}=\Pi_{H_{k}}(x^{k}).

(4.8)

Proof.

“
4.1(i)
”: Since
T
is monotone
we have that

\displaystyle\langle x^{\star}-\operatorname{ares}_{T}^{\phi}(x^{k}),v^{k}-0\rangle\leq 0,

(4.9)

and hence
x^{\star}\in H_{k}
.

“
4.1(ii)
”: Since
x^{k}-\operatorname{ares}_{T}^{\phi}(x^{k})=\nabla\phi^{*}(v^{k})
we have by strict monotonicity of
\nabla\phi^{*}
and the relation
\nabla\phi^{*}(0)=0
that

\displaystyle\langle x^{k}-\operatorname{ares}_{T}^{\phi}(x^{k}),v^{k}\rangle=\langle\nabla\phi^{*}(v^{k})-\nabla\phi^{*}(0),v^{k}-0\rangle>0,

(4.10)

i.e.,
x^{k}\not\in H_{k}
, unless
v^{k}=0
or equivalently,
\operatorname{ares}_{T}^{\phi}(x^{k})=x^{k}
and hence
0\in T(\operatorname{ares}_{T}^{\phi}(x^{k}))
and
x^{k}\in\zer T
.

“
4.1(iii)
”: Firstly, note that
\operatorname{ares}_{T}^{\phi}(x^{k})\in H_{k}
since
\langle x^{k+1}-\operatorname{ares}_{T}^{\phi}(x^{k}),v^{k}\rangle=0
.
Secondly, by construction of
H_{k}
, since
v^{k}=\nabla\phi(x^{k}-\operatorname{ares}_{T}^{\phi}(x^{k}))
,
\operatorname{ares}_{T}^{\phi}(x^{k})
satisfies the first-order optimality condition of the projection which amounts to

\displaystyle\nabla\phi(x^{k}-\operatorname{ares}_{T}^{\phi}(x^{k}))\in N_{H_{k}}(\operatorname{ares}_{T}^{\phi}(x^{k}))=\{\lambda v^{k}:\lambda\geq 0\},

(4.11)

and hence the third item is proved.

“
4.1(iv)
”: This readily follows from the fact that
\varphi
is monotonically increasing.
∎

In the isotropic case, a prominent choice is
\varphi(t)=\frac{1}{p}t^{p}
for some
p>1
.
As the following example reveals not even in this case

is monotone.

Example 4.2
.

Choose the monotone linear operator

defined by the matrix

\displaystyle T=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{bmatrix},

(4.12)

and the convex and smooth function

with

with

. Then
\nabla\phi^{*}(v)=\|v\|_{2}^{2}v

\nabla\phi^{*}(T(x_{1},x_{2}))=\|(x_{1},x_{2})\|_{2}^{2}(x_{2},-x_{1})

and thus we have for any

\displaystyle\langle\nabla\phi^{*}(T(x_{1},x_{2}))-\nabla\phi^{*}(T(y_{1},y_{2})),(x_{1},x_{2})-(y_{1},y_{2})\rangle

\displaystyle=(\|(x_{1},x_{2})\|_{2}^{2}-\|(y_{1},y_{2})\|_{2}^{2})(x_{1}y_{2}-x_{2}y_{1})

\displaystyle\langle\nabla\phi^{*}(T(x_{1},x_{2}))-\nabla\phi^{*}(T(y_{1},y_{2})),(x_{1},x_{2})-(y_{1},y_{2})\rangle

Luque analyzed the isotropic PPA under the following error tolerance, which allows the next iterate

to deviate slightly from the exact proximal point

\displaystyle\|\operatorname{ares}_{T}^{\phi}(x^{k})-x^{k+1}\|_{2}\leq\varepsilon_{k},

Henceforth, we call

\varepsilon
-subgradients.
Next we prove the global convergence of isotropic PPA.
The algorithm is essentially an instance of classical PPA with implicitly defineded step-sizes. Hence the following result is an implication of
[
30

. However, to emphasize the connections to the anisotropic projection we provide an alternative proof using the algorithm’s projective interpretation.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\phi\in\Gamma_{0}(X)
be Legendre super-coercive with full domain such that
\phi=\varphi(\|\cdot\|_{2})
for some increasing
\varphi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
. Let

be maximal monotone. Let

such that

4.13
holds true and let

be defined according to

4.4
.
Then the following hold:

(i)

\{x^{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
is quasi-Fejèr-monotone relative to the solutions in
\zer T
, i.e., for any

\|x^{k+1}-x^{\star}\|_{2}\leq\|x^{k}-x^{\star}\|_{2}+\varepsilon_{k}.

(4.14)

(ii)

\lim_{k\to\infty}x^{k}=x^{\infty}\in\zer T
.

(iii)

\lim_{k\to\infty}v^{k}=0
and
\lim_{k\to\infty}\|x^{k}-x^{k+1}\|_{2}=0

Proof.

Let
x^{\star}\in\zer T
.
Invoking
4.1(iii)
and
4.1(iv)
we have that
\operatorname{ares}_{T}^{\phi}(x^{k})=\Pi_{H_{k}}(x^{k})
and

Thanks to the nonexpansiveness of

we have that

\displaystyle\|x^{k+1}-x^{\star}\|_{2}

\displaystyle=\|x^{k+1}-\operatorname{ares}_{T}^{\phi}(x^{k})+\operatorname{ares}_{T}^{\phi}(x^{k})-x^{\star}\|_{2}

The remainder of the proof follows by invoking the arguments in the proof of
[
30

∎

4.2
Anisotropic case

4.2.1
Global convergence

The class of isotropic prox-functions
\phi
, discussed in the previous subsection are nonseparable in general unless
\varphi(t)=t^{2}
. However, in the context of primal-dual minimization, separability, at least between optimization and multiplier variables, is desirable in practice; see
Section

5
.
Hence, in this subsection we study the convergence of the anisotropic PPA for general
\phi
harnessing the gradient structure of the preconditioner
S=\nabla\phi
to bypass the isotropy assumption in Luque’s PPA.

Unfortunately, in contrast to the Euclidean projection the anisotropic projection does not enjoy firm nonexpansiveness. As we shall demonstrate in
Remark

4.11
, standard techniques for the classical or the Bregman PPA for monotone operators, in our case, lead to an inequality that cannot be telescoped.
In the same way that isotropic PPA can be seen as classical PPA with implicitly defined step-sizes, anisotropic PPA appears similar in form to a variable metric PPA with implicitly defined metric
B_{k}
. Therefore it is tempting to deduce convergence via a variable metric PPA interpretation. However, the assumptions on the metric
B_{k}
imposed in
[
31
]
are too strong to cover this case:

Remark 4.8

(variable metric interpretation)
.

Let
X=\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Choose
\phi(x)=\frac{1}{3}\|x\|_{3}^{3}
. Then
\phi^{*}(v)=\frac{2}{3}\|v\|^{\frac{3}{2}}
. As long as
(v^{k})_{i}\neq 0
for all
i
,
\nabla\phi^{*}(v)=\big(|(v^{k})_{i}|^{-\frac{1}{2}}(v^{k})_{i}\big)_{i=1}^{n}
and the algorithm can be written as

\displaystyle v^{k}

\displaystyle\in T(x^{k+1})

(4.20)

\displaystyle x^{k+1}

\displaystyle=x^{k}-B_{k}^{-1}\cdot v^{k},

(4.21)

for diagonal matrix
(B_{k})_{ii}=\sqrt{|(v^{k})_{i}|}
.
Note that unfortunately,
(v^{k})_{i}=0
for some
i
despite
v^{k}\neq 0
and the algorithm is far from termination.

Instead, if
T=\partial f
is the subdifferential of a convex function, one can utilize
f
or the value-function of the anisotropic PPA update, i.e., the infimal convolution of
f
and
\phi
, as Lyapunov functions which is explored in
[
25
]
for the special case
\phi=\frac{1}{p}\|\cdot\|_{p}^{p}
,

.
For general monotone operators

we will track the algorithm’s progress in the dual space using Bregman distances. This enables us to deduce its primal subsequential convergence indirectly; see
Theorem

4.18
. For that purpose we define the Bregman distance
D_{\phi^{*}}:X^{*}\times X^{*}\to\mathbb{R}
generated by the conjugate prox-function
\phi^{*}:X^{*}\to\mathbb{R}
as

\displaystyle D_{\phi^{*}}(u,v)=\phi^{*}(u)-\phi^{*}(v)-\langle\nabla\phi^{*}(v),u-v\rangle.

(4.22)

Thanks to our standing assumptions for
\phi

enjoys the following favorable properties:

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
\phi\in\Gamma_{0}(X)
be Legendre, super-coercive with full domain. Then the same is true for the convex conjugate prox-function
\phi^{*}\in\Gamma_{0}(X^{*})
and the following properties hold:

(i)

D_{\phi^{*}}(u,\cdot)
is coercive for every
u\in X^{*}
.

(ii)

If
\lim_{k\to\infty}v^{k}=v
we have
\lim_{k\to\infty}D_{\phi^{*}}(v^{k},v)=0
.

(iii)

Let
\{v^{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
and
\{u^{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
be sequences in

and assume that one of the two sequences converges to some limit. Then
\lim_{k\to\infty}D_{\phi^{*}}(u^{k},v^{k})=0
implies that the other sequence converges to the same limit.

(iv)

For any
x,y\in X
we have
D_{\phi^{*}}(\nabla\phi(x),\nabla\phi(y))=D_{\phi}(y,x)
.

Proof.

“

and
4.9(ii)

“

We state the celebrated three-point identity
[
7
]
which is key to establish the convergence of the anisotropic PPA in the dual space. Classically, it is used to establish convergence of the Bregman PPA in the primal space

Lemma 4.10

(Three-point identity)
.

For any

the following identity holds true:

\displaystyle D_{\phi^{*}}(w,v)=D_{\phi^{*}}(u,v)+D_{\phi^{*}}(w,u)+\langle\nabla\phi^{*}(u)-\nabla\phi^{*}(v),w-u\rangle.

(4.23)

Remark 4.11
.

In the classical and the Bregman PPA the monotonicity of
T
between the pairs
(x^{k+1},v^{k})
and
(x^{\star},0)
is utilized to establish Fejèr monotonicity of the primal iterates
\{x^{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
wrt. to the solution set
\zer T
which ultimately ensures convergence. It is tempting to apply this pattern in the anisotropic case as well. However, this leads to an inequality that cannot be telescoped:
By
Eq.

4.1b
one has
v^{k}=\nabla\phi(x^{k}-x^{k+1})
and since by assumption,
\nabla\phi(0)=0
, monotonicity of
T
yields:

\displaystyle\langle x^{k}-x^{\star}-(x^{k}-x^{k+1}),\nabla\phi(x^{k}-x^{k+1})-\nabla\phi(0)\rangle\geq 0.

(4.24)

Using the identity transformation in
Lemma

4.10
with
D_{\phi}
and
u=x^{k}-x^{k+1}
,
v=0
and
w=x^{k}-x^{\star}
we arrive to:

\displaystyle D_{\phi}(x^{k}-x^{\star},0)\geq D_{\phi}(x^{k}-x^{k+1},0)+D_{\phi}(x^{k}-x^{\star},x^{k}-x^{k+1}),

(4.25)

which can be rewritten using
\nabla\phi(0)=0
:

\displaystyle D_{\phi}(x^{k}-x^{\star},x^{k}-x^{k+1})+\phi(x^{k}-x^{k+1})\leq\phi(x^{k}-x^{\star}).

(4.26)

Unfortunately, this inequality cannot be telescoped since
D_{\phi}
is not translation invariant wrt.
x^{k}
.
If, furthermore,
\phi(x)=\frac{1}{p}\|x\|_{p}^{p}
for
p\geq 2
,
\phi
is uniformly convex relative to itself and hence, in light of
[
23
, Lemma 4.2.3]
, we have
D_{\phi}(x,y)\geq\tfrac{1}{2^{p-2}}\phi(x-y)
. In this case we can further bound

:

\phi(x^{k+1}-x^{\star})+2^{p-2}\phi(x^{k}-x^{k+1})\leq 2^{p-2}\phi(x^{k}-x^{\star}).

This inequality can be telescoped only if
p=2
which recovers the known Euclidean case.
As standard techniques fail to derive convergence we propose to establish Fejèr monotonicity in the dual space.

Lemma 4.12
.

Let

and

The next result shows that for
\varepsilon_{k}=0
, dual space Fejèr montonicity readily implies convergence of the full sequence if

Definition 4.15
.

Let
T:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
be a set-valued mapping. We say that
T
is uniformly monotone with modulus
\omega:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
if
\omega
is increasing and vanishes only at 0 such that for all
(x,u),(y,v)\in\gph T
:

\displaystyle\langle x-y,u-v\rangle\geq\omega(\|x-y\|_{2}).

(4.43)

Proposition 4.16
.

Assume that
T
is uniformly monotone with modulus
\omega
and assume that
\varepsilon_{k}=0
. Then the following hold:

(i)

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\omega(\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|_{2})<\infty

(ii)

\lim_{k\to\infty}x^{k}=x^{\star}=T^{-1}(0)
.

Proof.

In light of
4.13(v)
, using the uniform monotonicity of
T
, since
v^{k+1}\in T(x^{k+2})
and
v^{k}\in T(x^{k+1})
we have that

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\omega(\|x^{k+2}-x^{k+1}\|_{2})\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\langle x^{k+2}-x^{k+1},v^{k+1}-v^{k}\rangle<\infty.

\{\omega(\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|_{2})\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
is summable we have that
\omega(\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|_{2})\to 0
. Suppose that
\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|_{2}\not\to 0
. This means that there is a subsequence indexed by
k_{j}
along which

\omega

, and hence
x^{k}=T^{-1}(v^{k})\to T^{-1}(0)=x^{\star}
.

In general, Fejèr monotonicity is not sufficient for convergence to a solution: For
v^{k}=v\neq 0
the inequality

D_{\phi^{*}}(v,0)\leq D_{\phi^{*}}(v,0)-D_{\phi^{*}}(v,v)

reduces to
0\leq 0
.
However, as we shall prove next, this scenario can be ruled out: As shown in
Proposition

4.1
, at each iteration
k
, the algorithm generates a separating hyperplane, supported by
x^{k+1}
and spanned by normal vector
v^{k}
, that separates the current iterate
x^{k}
from any solution. Consequently, if
v^{k}\to v^{\star}
converges, the separating hyperplanes are parallel and equidistant in the limit contradicting the separation of the current iterate
x^{k}
from a solution. This is illustrated in

and proved in the following standalone result:

Proposition 4.17
.

Let
T:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
be monotone and assume that
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\varepsilon_{k}<\infty
.
Suppose that
\lim_{k\to\infty}v^{k}=v^{\star}
. Then necessarily
v^{\star}=0
.

Proof.

Since
x^{k+1}=x^{k}-\nabla\phi^{*}(v^{k})
we have for any
N\in\mathbb{N}
that
x^{k+1+N}=x^{k}-\sum_{i=0}^{N}\nabla\phi^{*}(v^{k+i})
. Thus we obtain using the relation
\nabla\phi^{*}(0)=0

\displaystyle\langle x^{\star}-x^{k+1+N},v^{k+N}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle x^{\star}-x^{k},v^{k+N}\rangle+\sum_{i=0}^{N}\langle\nabla\phi^{*}(v^{k+i}),v^{k+N}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle x^{\star}-x^{k},v^{k+N}\rangle+\sum_{i=0}^{N}\langle\nabla\phi^{*}(v^{k+i})-\nabla\phi^{*}(0),v^{k+N}-0\rangle.

(4.44)

Assume that the contrary holds, i.e.,
v^{\star}\neq 0
. Then by strict monotonicity of
\nabla\phi^{*}
we have that

\langle\nabla\phi^{*}(v^{\star})-\nabla\phi^{*}(0),v^{\star}-0\rangle=2\varepsilon>0,

for some
\varepsilon>0
.
By continuity of the mapping
(\xi,\eta)\mapsto\langle\nabla\phi^{*}(\xi)-\nabla\phi^{*}(0),\eta-0\rangle
we know there exists
\delta>0
such that for all
\xi,\eta\in B_{\|\cdot\|_{*}}(v^{\star},\delta)
one has,

\langle\nabla\phi^{*}(\xi)-\nabla\phi^{*}(0),\eta-0\rangle>\varepsilon>0.

Since
\lim_{k\to\infty}v^{k}=v^{\star}
, for
k
sufficiently large we have that for any
i,N\in\mathbb{N}
,
v^{k+i},v^{k+N}\in B_{\|\cdot\|_{*}}(v^{\star},\delta)
, where
B_{\|\cdot\|_{*}}(v^{\star},\delta)
denotes the open
\|\cdot\|_{*}
-ball with radius
\delta
around
v^{\star}
, and hence

\displaystyle\langle\nabla\phi^{*}(v^{k+i})-\nabla\phi^{*}(0),v^{k+N}-0\rangle>\varepsilon.

(4.45)

Combining
Eqs.

4.45
and
4.44
yields that

\displaystyle\langle x^{\star}-x^{k+1+N},v^{k+N}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle x^{\star}-x^{k},v^{k+N}\rangle+\sum_{i=0}^{N}\langle\nabla\phi^{*}(v^{k+i})-\nabla\phi^{*}(0),v^{k+N}-0\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\langle x^{\star}-x^{k},v^{k+N}\rangle+(N+1)\varepsilon,

for any
k>K
,
K
sufficiently large. Let now
k=K+1
fixed and pass to the limit
\lim_{N\to\infty}v^{k+N}=v^{\star}
. Then we have that

\lim_{N\to\infty}\langle x^{\star}-x^{k},v^{k+N}\rangle=\langle x^{\star}-x^{k},v^{\star}\rangle

and
\lim_{N\to\infty}(N+1)\varepsilon=\infty
and hence

\lim_{N\to\infty}\langle x^{\star}-x^{k+1+N},v^{k+N}\rangle=+\infty.

Since
v^{k+N}\in T^{e}(\varepsilon_{k+N},x^{k+1+N})
and
x^{\star}\in T(0)
we know that

\langle x^{\star}-x^{k+1+N},v^{k+N}\rangle\leq\varepsilon_{k+N},

with
\lim_{N\to\infty}\varepsilon_{k+N}\to 0
,
a contradiction. Hence,
\lim_{k\to\infty}v^{k}=0
.

H_{k}
does not separate the old iterate
x^{k}
from the solution
x^{\star}
and hence the algorithm “overshoots” the point
x^{\star}
. This contradicts the monotonicity of
T
and hence the assumption that
v^{k}\to v\neq 0
was wrong.

In the previous standalone result we have shown that convergence of the dual sequence
\{v^{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
implies that its limit is
0
. However, this does not exclude the possibility of the sequences being caught in limit cycles.
Moreover, the above result implicitly relies on the property that
\{v^{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
is a Cauchy sequence, which cannot be guaranteed a priori. Building upon the proof of the previous result we show in the next theorem that every limit point is a solution excluding limit cycles. For that purpose we exploit the fact that the Bregman distance
D_{\phi^{*}}(v^{k},v^{k+1})
between consecutive dual iterates
v^{k+1},v^{k}
vanishes and assume that
\{x^{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
has a limit point.
The first part of the proof of the next result follows along the lines of
Proposition

4.17
:

Theorem 4.18
.

Let
T:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
be maximal monotone and assume that
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\varepsilon_{k}<\infty
. Then the following hold

(i)

Suppose that
\{x^{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
has a limit point, then
\lim_{k\to\infty}v^{k}=0

(ii)

every limit point of the sequence
\{x^{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
is a zero of
T
.

Proof.

“
4.18(i)
”: Following the first step of the proof of
Proposition

4.17
we obtain the identity

\displaystyle\langle x^{\star}-x^{k+1+N},v^{k+N}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle x^{\star}-x^{k},v^{k+N}\rangle+\sum_{i=0}^{N}\langle\nabla\phi^{*}(v^{k+i})-\nabla\phi^{*}(0),v^{k+N}-0\rangle.

(4.46)

Let
x^{\infty}
be a limit point of
\{x^{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
. Hence, there is a subsequence
x^{k_{j}}\to x^{\infty}
. In light of
4.13(vi)
the dual sequence
\{v^{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
is bounded. Consequently, there exists a constant
\zeta>0
such that
\langle x^{\star}-x^{k_{j}},v^{k_{j}+N}\rangle\geq-\zeta
for all
j,N\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then the identity in
Eq.

4.46
yields

\displaystyle\langle x^{\star}-x^{k_{j}+1+N},v^{k_{j}+N}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq-\zeta+\sum_{i=0}^{N}\langle\nabla\phi^{*}(v^{k_{j}+i})-\nabla\phi^{*}(0),v^{k_{j}+N}-0\rangle,

(4.47)

for all
j,N\in\mathbb{N}
.
Suppose that
v^{k_{j}}\not\to 0
.
Since
\{v^{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
is bounded by going to another subsequence if necessary there is some
v^{\star}\in X^{*}
,
v^{\star}\neq 0
such that
\lim_{j\to\infty}v^{k_{j}}=v^{\star}
.
By strict monotonicity of
\nabla\phi^{*}
it holds that

\langle\nabla\phi^{*}(v^{\star})-\nabla\phi^{*}(0),v^{\star}-0\rangle=2\varepsilon>0,

for some
\varepsilon>0
. Choose
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
N>\zeta/\varepsilon
and keep it fixed.

By continuity of the mapping
(\xi,\eta)\mapsto\langle\nabla\phi^{*}(\xi)-\nabla\phi^{*}(0),\eta-0\rangle
we know there exists
\delta>0
such that for all
\xi,\eta\in B_{\|\cdot\|_{*}}(v^{\star},\delta)
we have that

\displaystyle\langle\nabla\phi^{*}(\xi)-\nabla\phi^{*}(0),\eta-0\rangle>\varepsilon>0.

(4.48)

Thanks to
4.13(iii)
,
D_{\phi^{*}}(v^{k_{j}},v^{k_{j}+1})\to 0
. Since
\lim_{j\to\infty}v^{k_{j}}=v^{\star}
in light of
4.9(iii)
it holds that
\lim_{j\to\infty}v^{k_{j}+1}=v^{\star}
. Since
N>\zeta/\varepsilon
is a fixed finite integer that does not depend on
k_{j}
, repeating the same argument
N
times, we obtain that for any
0\leq i\leq N
,
\lim_{j\to\infty}v^{k_{j}+i}=v^{\star}
. Consequently, for every
0\leq i\leq N
there exists
M_{i}>0
such that for all
j\geq M_{i}
,
v^{k_{j}+i}\in B_{\|\cdot\|_{*}}(v^{\star},\delta)
. Now choose
M(N)=\max_{1\leq i\leq N}M_{i}
. Then, for any
j\geq M(N)
and every
1\leq i\leq N
,
v^{k_{j}+i}\in B_{\|\cdot\|_{*}}(v^{\star},\delta)
and hence we obtain using
Eq.

4.48

\langle\nabla\phi^{*}(v^{{k_{j}}+i})-\nabla\phi^{*}(0),v^{{k_{j}}+N}-0\rangle>\varepsilon.

This means that for
j\geq M(N)
, by the choice of
N>\zeta/\varepsilon
, one obtains

\displaystyle\langle x^{\star}-x^{k_{j}+1+N},v^{k_{j}+N}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq-\zeta+\sum_{i=0}^{N}\langle\nabla\phi^{*}(v^{k_{j}+i})-\nabla\phi^{*}(0),v^{k_{j}+N}-0\rangle

(4.49)

\displaystyle\geq-\zeta+(N+1)\varepsilon>-\zeta+\tfrac{\zeta}{\varepsilon}\varepsilon+\varepsilon=\varepsilon.

(4.50)

Since
v^{k_{j}+N}\in T^{e}(\varepsilon_{k_{j}+N},x^{k_{j}+1+N})
and
\lim_{j\to\infty}\varepsilon_{k_{j}+N}=0
we have that for
j
sufficiently large

\langle x^{\star}-x^{k_{j}+1+N},v^{k_{j}+N}\rangle\leq\varepsilon_{k_{j}+N}<\varepsilon,

a contradiction. Thus
\lim_{j\to\infty}v^{k_{j}}=0
and by
4.9(ii)
,
\lim_{j\to\infty}D_{\phi^{*}}(v^{k_{j}},0)=0
. In light of
4.13(ii)
,
\{D_{\phi^{*}}(v^{k},0)\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
converges. Thus,

\lim_{k\to\infty}D_{\phi^{*}}(v^{k},0)=\lim_{j\to\infty}D_{\phi^{*}}(v^{k_{j}},0)=0.

Invoking
4.9(iii)
we obtain
\lim_{k\to\infty}v^{k}=0
.

“
4.18(ii)
”: Let
x^{\infty}
be a limit point of the sequence
\{x^{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
. Hence there exists a subsequence indexed by
k_{j}
along which
\lim_{j\to\infty}x^{k_{j}}=x^{\infty}
. In light of
4.18(i)
,
\lim_{k\to\infty}v^{k}\to 0
. Since
v^{k_{j}-1}\in T^{e}(\varepsilon_{k_{j}-1},x^{k_{j}})
,
\lim_{j\to\infty}v^{k_{j}-1}=0
and
\lim_{j\to\infty}\varepsilon_{k_{j}-1}=0
, in light of
[
5
, Proposition 1(iv)]
, we have that
0\in T(x^{\infty})
.
∎

Boundedness of the primal iterates
x^{k}
, and hence existence of a limit point, can be guaranteed if
T
is coercive as shown in the following corollary:

Corollary 4.19
.

Assume that
T:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
is monotone and coercive. Then the sequence
\{x^{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
is bounded. Therefore
\lim_{k\to\infty}v^{k}=0
and
\{x^{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
has all its limit points in
\zer T
.

Proof.

In light of
4.13(vi)
the dual sequence
\{v^{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
is bounded. Suppose that the primal sequence
\{x^{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
is unbounded. This implies the existence of a subsequence indexed by
k_{j}
along which
\lim_{j\to\infty}\|x^{k_{j}}\|=\infty
. Then, by coercivity of
T
,
\infty=\lim_{j\to\infty}\inf\{\|v\|_{*}:v\in T(x^{k_{j}})\}\leq\lim_{j\to\infty}\|v^{k_{j}}\|_{*}
, a contradiction. Hence, the primal sequence
\{x^{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
is bounded as well and therefore it has a limit point. Invoking
Theorem

4.18
we obtain the desired result.
∎

Remark 4.20

(coercivity and uniform monotonicity)
.

Let
T
be uniformly monotone with a super-coercive modulus
\omega
. Then
T
is coercive by
[
3
, Proposition 22.11]
. In this case the above theorem implies the conclusion of

for
\varepsilon_{k}=0
.
In light of
[

the modulus of

To conclude, we provide an example for a coercive operator in the context of minimax problems:

Example 4.21
.

Consider the optimization problem:

\displaystyle\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)+g(Ax),

(4.51)

for some
A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
,
f\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\dom f
bounded and
g\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
with
\dom g=\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
This is equivalent to the following saddle-point problem

\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\max_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\big\{L(x,y):=f(x)+\langle Ax,y\rangle-g^{*}(y)\big\}.

Since
g
has full domain by
[

, Theorem 11.8(d)]

g^{*}
is super-coercive. Hence, the saddle-point operator

and

are considered the primal variables.

4.2.2
Local convergence under Hölder growth

Finally, we analyze the local convergence rate of the method specializing
\phi=\frac{1}{p}\|\cdot\|_{p}^{p}
for
p>1
. Denote
by
d_{p}(x,\zer T)=\inf_{y\in\zer T}\|x-y\|_{p}
and assume that the following growth property holds true:

\displaystyle\exists\delta,\nu,\rho>0:x\in T^{-1}(v),\|v\|_{q}<\delta\Rightarrow d_{p}(x,\zer T)\leq\rho\|v\|_{q}^{\nu}.

(4.52)

Note that the above property is more restrictive than
Eq.

4.15
as we require the bound to hold for any
x
and not only for points that are near a solution
x^{\star}\in\zer T
.

Example 4.22
.

Let
p\geq 2
and
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
.
Choose

\displaystyle T(x)=Ax-b\quad\text{for}\quad A=\begin{bmatrix}0&-\tfrac{1}{2}\\
\tfrac{1}{2}&0\end{bmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad b=\begin{bmatrix}1\\
1\end{bmatrix}.

(4.53)

Then

T^{-1}(0)=\begin{bmatrix}2\\
-2\end{bmatrix}=:x^{\star}

is single-valued and hence
d_{p}(x,\zer T)=\|x-x^{\star}\|_{p}
. We have that
\|Ax\|_{q}=\tfrac{1}{2}\|x\|_{q}
and
Ax^{\star}=b
.
By equivalence of the
\ell^{p}
-norm and the
\ell^{q}
-norm, since

, we have that

\|x-x^{\star}\|_{p}\leq\|x-x^{\star}\|_{q}=2\|Ax-b\|_{q}=2\|T(x)\|_{q},

and hence
Eq.

4.52
holds for
\delta=\infty
,
\rho=2
and
\nu=1
.

Proposition 4.23
.

Let
\phi(x)=\frac{1}{p}\|x\|_{p}^{p}
for
p>1
and assume that
\varepsilon_{k}=0
. Assume that
Eq.

4.52
holds and that
\{x^{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
has a limit point. Choose
p>\frac{1}{\nu}+1
. Then the algorithm converges locally with order
\nu(p-1)
and rate
\rho
, i.e., for
k\geq K
,
K
sufficiently large we have
d_{p}(x^{k+1},\zer T)\leq\rho d_{p}^{\nu(p-1)}(x^{k},\zer T)
.

Proof.

Let
x\in X
. By convex duality we have that
\phi^{*}(\nabla\phi(x))=\langle x,\nabla\phi(x)\rangle-\phi(x)
.
Since
\phi(x)=\tfrac{1}{p}\|x\|_{p}^{p}

\phi^{*}(v)=\frac{1}{q}\|v\|_{q}^{q}

\phi^{*}(\nabla\phi(x))=\frac{1}{q}\|\nabla\phi(x)\|_{q}^{q}=(1-\tfrac{1}{p})\|x\|_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle\|\nabla\phi(x)\|_{q}=\|x\|_{p}^{p-1}.

(4.54)

In light of
4.18(i)

T(x^{k+1})\ni v^{k}\to 0
. Hence for

,
K

and thus using

and

\displaystyle d_{p}(x^{k+1},\zer T)\leq\rho\|v^{k}\|_{q}^{\nu}=\rho\|x^{k}-x^{k+1}\|_{p}^{\nu(p-1)}.

(4.55)

Since

\displaystyle\|x^{k}-x^{k+1}\|_{p}=d_{p}(x^{k},H_{k})=\inf_{x\in H_{k}}\|x^{k}-x\|_{p}\leq\inf_{x\in\zer T}\|x^{k}-x\|_{p}=d_{p}(x^{k},\zer T).

(4.56)

Combining

4.56
and
4.55
we obtain:

d_{p}(x^{k+1},\zer T)\leq\rho\|x^{k}-x^{k+1}\|_{p}^{\nu(p-1)}\leq\rho d_{p}^{\nu(p-1)}(x^{k},\zer T).\qed

6
Conclusion

In this paper we have considered dual space nonlinear preconditioning for proximal point beyond the isotropic case studied by Luque. This is based on an anisotropic generalization of the resolvent of a monotone mapping which enjoys a duality relation with the well-studied Bregman resolvent. Connections between Bregman resolvents, the Bregman–Yosida regularization and convolutions of monotone operators aka parallel sums were established. Using a Fejèr monotonicity property in the dual space subsequential convergence is established. Local convergence rates are investigated showing that under a local Hölder growth and a suitable preconditioner convergence rates of arbitrary order are attained.
An anisotropic generalization of the proximal augmented Lagrangian method is described and applied to a two-player zero-sum game and a regularized regression problem.
As for future work we investigate a generalization to Banach spaces.

Abstract




Abstract.

In this paper, we propose a novel high order unfitted finite element method on Cartesian meshes for solving the acoustic wave equation with discontinuous coefficients having complex interface geometry. The unfitted finite element method does not require any penalty to achieve optimal convergence. We also introduce a new explicit time discretization method for the ODE system resulting from the spatial discretization of the wave equation. The strong stability and optimal
hp
-version error estimates both in time and space are established. Numerical examples confirm our theoretical results.

Key words.

Explicit time discretization; strong stability; unfitted finite element;
hp
error estimates.

Introduction

The wave equation is a fundamental equation in mathematical physics describing the phenomena of wave propagation. It finds diverse applications in science and engineering, including geoscience, petroleum engineering, and telecommunication (see
[
30
,
31
]
and the references therein). Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a bounded Lipschitz domain and
T>0
be the length of the time interval.
We consider in this paper the acoustic wave equation

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{1}{\rho c^{2}}\partial_{t}u=\mbox{div}\,\mathbf{q}+f,\ \ \rho\partial_{t}\mathbf{q}=\nabla u\ \ \ \ \mbox{in }\Omega\times(0,T),\\
&[\![u]\!]=0,\quad[\![\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{n}]\!]=0\ \ \ \ \text{ on }\Gamma\times(0,T),\\
&u=0\quad\text{ on }\partial\Omega\times(0,T),\\
&u(\mathbf{x},0)=u_{0}(\mathbf{x}),\ \ \mathbf{q}(\mathbf{x},0)=\mathbf{q}_{0}(\mathbf{x})\ \ \ \ \mbox{in }\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

where
u
is the pressure,
\mathbf{q}
is the speed of the displacement in the medium, and
f
is the source. The domain
\Omega
is assumed to be divided by a
C^{2}
-smooth interface
\Gamma
into two nonintersecting subdomains such that

\Omega=\Omega_{1}\cup\Gamma\cup\Omega_{2}
and
\Omega_{1}\subset\bar{\Omega}_{1}\subset\Omega
.
For simplicity, we assume that the density of the medium
\rho
and the speed of the propagation of the wave
c
are piecewise constants, namely,

\displaystyle\rho=\rho_{1}\chi_{\Omega_{1}}+\rho_{2}\chi_{\Omega_{2}},\ \ c=c_{1}\chi_{\Omega_{1}}+c_{2}\chi_{\Omega_{2}},

where for
i=1,2
,
\rho_{i},c_{i}
are positive constants and
\chi_{\Omega_{i}}
denotes the characteristic function of
\Omega_{i}
. Here
\mathbf{n}
is the unit outer normal to
\Omega_{1}
, and
[\![v]\!]|_{\Gamma}:=v|_{\Omega_{1}}-v|_{\Omega_{2}}
denotes the jump of a function
v
across the interface
\Gamma
.

There exists a large literature on numerical methods for solving the wave equation on conforming quadrilateral/hexahedral or triangular/tetrahedral meshes for which we refer to the monograph
[
20
]
and
[
22
,
40
]
for the construction of the algorithms and the finite element error analysis. Local discontinuous Galerkin (DG) methods for the wave equation are studied in
[
17
,
39
]
. Optimal error estimates for sufficiently smooth solutions are proved in
[
17
]
on Cartesian meshes without using the penalty and in
[
39
]
on unstructured meshes by adding appropriate penalty terms, which leads to however a dissipative method. In
[
23
]
, both dissipative and non-dissipative variants of the hybridizable DG methods are proposed, where it is shown that the dissipative method has the optimal error estimate and the non-dissipative method whose numerical flux includes the time derivative of the pressure is sub-optimal.

In order to deal with an arbitrarily shaped interface where the coefficients of the partial differential equations are discontinuous, immersed or unfitted mesh methods are developed to avoid expensive work of mesh generation using body-fitted methods in e.g.,
[
3
,
16
]
. For acoustic wave equations with discontinuous coefficients, a second order immersed interface method on Cartesian meshes with suitable modification of the finite difference stencil near the interface is developed in
[
28
]
. In
[
2
]
, second and third order immersed DG methods are proposed which design polynomial shape functions to approximately satisfy the interface conditions. In
[
36
,
34
]
high order cut finite elements for solving the wave equation are studied. The small cut cell problem, that is, the small intersection of the interface and the elements of the mesh can always occur, is treated by adding penalty terms of jumps of high order derivatives over interior sides of cut elements in
[
36
]
and by the approach of cell merging in
[
34
]
following an idea in
[
29
]
for elliptic equations. We remark that appropriate penalties are crucial in
[
2
,
36
,
34
]
in designing DG methods for solving the wave equation.

The first objective of this paper is to propose an arbitrarily high order unfitted finite element method for solving (
1.1
) without adding any penalty terms. Our method is defined on an induced mesh from a Cartesian mesh with hanging nodes by merging small interface elements with their neighboring elements so that the elements in the induced mesh are large with respect to both subdomains
\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2}
. A reliable algorithm to generate the induced mesh from the Cartesian mesh with hanging nodes is constructed in
[
14
]
for any
C^{2}
-smooth interface. We will show in this paper that the same induced mesh also allows us to define a new unfitted finite element space which is conforming in each subdomain
\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2}
. This new finite element space, together with a new observation of DG methods (see (
2.30
) below) and the lifted regular decomposition theorem of vector fields, leads to optimal energy error estimates of our semi-discrete unfitted finite element method without resorting to the penalties. This new piecewise conforming unfitted finite element space, which is less expensive than the standard unfitted finite element space first introduced in the seminal work
[
25
]
, is of independent interest. We refer to
[
13
,
14
]
for more references on the development of unfitted finite element methods in the setting of elliptic equations. We remark that the theoretical results in this paper can be extended to the three-dimensional case, but the reliable algorithm for constructing cubic macro-elements is more challenging. We leave the extension to solve the three-dimensional wave problems in a future work.

After spatial discretization, we obtain a linear ODE system of the form

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathbf{Y}=\mathbb{D}\mathbf{Y}+\mathbf{R},

(1.2)

where
\mathbf{Y},\mathbf{R}\in\mathbb{R}^{M}
, and
\mathbb{D}\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times M}
is a constant matrix. Here
M
is the number of degrees of freedom for the spatial discretization.
Explicit Runge-Kutta (RK) methods have been successfully used for time integration for hyperbolic conservation laws when coupled with the DG scheme in space
[
19
]
.
In
[
38
]
, the strong stability of explicit RK methods is studied for semi-negative autonomous linear systems, that is,
\mathbb{D}^{T}\mathbb{H}+\mathbb{H}\mathbb{D}
is semi-negative definite for some symmetric positive definite matrix
\mathbb{H}\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times M}
. It is proved in
[
38
]
that for
r\geq 1
, the standard
r
stage
r
order RK methods are strongly stable when
r=3\,({\rm mod}\,4)
, not strongly stable when
r=1,2\,({\rm mod}\,4)
. When
r=0\,({\rm mod}\,4)
, the
r
stage
r
order RK method is strongly stable under the condition
\mathbb{D}^{T}\mathbb{H}+\mathbb{H}\mathbb{D}=\mathbb{O}
, where
\mathbb{O}\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times M}
is the zero matrix.

The second objective of this paper is to propose a strongly stable and arbitrarily high order explicit time discretization for (
1.2
) by using the property
\mathbb{D}+\mathbb{D}^{T}=\mathbb{O}
which results from the duality of the DG spatial discretization of gradient and divergence operators. The scheme is formulated in the finite element framework, that is, we find a continuous piecewise polynomial function of time to discretize (
1.2
). This allows us to prove the stability and
hp
-version error estimates under explicit CFL bounds in the whole time interval instead of only at time discretization nodes. We also introduce an efficient finite difference implementation of our finite element time scheme based on Legendre polynomial basis functions.

The layout of this paper is as follows. In section
2
we introduce the semi-discrete unfitted finite element method and prove the energy conservation property and
hp
optimal error estimates. In section
3
we introduce the explicit time discretization for (
1.2
) and prove the strong stability property and the error estimates under suitable CFL conditions. In section
4
we consider the full discretization scheme for (
1.1
) and prove
hp
-version error estimates. In section
5
we provide some numerical examples to verify our theoretical results. In section 6 we show the compatibility property of the induced mesh by the merging algorithm in
[
14
]
.

2
The semi-discrete unfitted finite element method

In this section we first recall some elements of the unfitted finite element method in the framework of Chen et al
[
13
]
in subsection 2.1. In subsection 2.2, we introduce a new unfitted finite element space which is conforming in each subdomain
\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2}
. We propose the semi-discrete unfitted finite element method for solving (
1.1
) and prove the optimal energy error estimates in subsection 2.3.

2.1
The induced mesh

Let
\Omega
be the union of rectangles so that it can be covered by a Cartesian mesh
\mathcal{T}_{0}
. The extension to the case when
\Omega
is a smooth domain can be done in a straightforward way by using the ideas in this paper. The general case when
\Omega
is a Lipschitz domain with piecewise smooth boundary can be studied by combining the ideas in
[
13
]
on the large element and interface deviation for piecewise smooth interfaces with the extension of the merging algorithm in Chen and Liu
[
14
]
for
C^{2}
smooth interfaces to deal with the piecewise smooth boundaries, for which we will pursue in a future work.
We remark that the special case when
\Omega
is a rectangle is of particular interests when solving the acoustic scattering problems by the method of perfectly matched layer (PML) to truncate the unbounded domains
[
15
]
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a Cartesian partition of the domain
\Omega
obtained by quad refinements of
\mathcal{T}_{0}
with possible local refinements and hanging nodes. We assume each element
K\in\mathcal{T}
is intersected by the interface
\Gamma
at most twice at different (open) sides. From
\mathcal{T}
we want to construct an induced mesh
\mathcal{M}
which avoids possible small intersections of the interface and the elements of the mesh. We start by defining the concept of large element.

Definition 2.1
.

For
i=1,2
, an element
K\in\mathcal{T}
, is called a large element with respect to
\Omega_{i}
if
K\subset\Omega_{i}
; or
K\in\mathcal{T}^{\Gamma}:=\{K\in\mathcal{T}:K\cap\Gamma\not=\emptyset\}
for which there exists a
\delta_{0}\in(0,1/2)
such
|e\cap\Omega_{i}|\geq\delta_{0}|e|
for each side
e
of
K
having nonempty intersection with
\Omega_{i}
.

When the element
K\in\mathcal{T}^{\Gamma}
is not large with respect to both
\Omega_{i}
,
i=1,2
, we make the following assumption as in
[
13
]
.

Assumption (H1)
: For each
K\in\mathcal{T}^{\Gamma}
, there exists a rectangular macro-element
M(K)
which is a union of
K
and its surrounding element (or elements) such that
M(K)
is large with respect to both
\Omega_{1}
,
\Omega_{2}
. We assume
h_{M(K)}\leq C_{0}h_{K}
for some fixed constant
C_{0}
.

This assumption can always be satisfied by using the idea of cell merging originated in Johansson and Larson
[
29
]
. We refer to
[
14
]
for a reliable algorithm to satisfy this assumption when the interface is
C^{2}
smooth. Set
M(K)=K
if
K\in\mathcal{T}^{\Gamma}
and
K
is large with respect to both
\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2}
. Then the induced mesh

\mathcal{M}=\{M(K):K\in\mathcal{T}^{\Gamma}\}\cup\{K\in\mathcal{T}:K\subset\Omega_{i},i=1,2,K\not\subset M(K^{\prime})\mbox{ for some }K^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}^{\Gamma}\}

satisfies the desired property that the elements in
\mathcal{M}
are large with respect to both domains
\Omega_{1}
,
\Omega_{2}
and the interface
\Gamma
intersects the boundary of element
K\in\mathcal{M}
also twice at different sides. We denote
\mathcal{M}={\rm Induced}(\mathcal{T})
. We require the following compatibility assumption on the induced mesh.

Assumption (H2)
: For any
e=\partial K\cap\partial K^{\prime}
,
K,K^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}
, let
f,f^{\prime}
be respectively the sides of
K,K^{\prime}
including
e
, then either (1)
f\subset f^{\prime}
or
f^{\prime}\subset f
; or (2)
e\cap\Gamma\not=\emptyset
.

The first condition in the assumption (H2) is standard in the literature in order to define conforming finite element methods on meshes with possible hanging nodes (see, e.g., Bonito et al
[
9
, §4.1]
). The second condition when the interface is present is new, which is important for us to define a new unfitted finite element space which is conforming in each domain
\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2}
. In the appendix of this paper we will show that the induced mesh obtained by the merging algorithm in
[
13
, Algorithm 6]
will satisfy the assumption (H2).

For any
K\in\mathcal{M}
, we denote
h_{K}
the diameter of
K
. For
K\in\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}:=\{K\in\mathcal{M}:K\cap\Gamma\not=\emptyset\}
, denote
\Gamma_{K}=K\cap\Gamma
and
\Gamma_{K}^{h}
the (open) straight segment connecting the two intersection points of
\Gamma
and
\partial K
. For
i=1,2
, let
K_{i}=K\cap\Omega_{i}
,
K_{i}^{h}
the polygon whose vertices are the vertex (vertices) of
K
inside
\Omega_{i}
and the endpoints of
\Gamma_{K}^{h}
, and
A_{K}^{i}
the vertex of
K
within
\Omega_{i}
which has the maximum distance to
\Gamma_{K}^{h}
.
The following lemma shows that
K_{i}^{h}
is the union of shape regular triangles as the consequence of
K
being a large element. The lemma can be easily proved and we omit the details.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
K\in\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}
. Then for
i=1,2
,
K^{h}_{i}
is the union of triangles
K^{h}_{ij}
,
j=1,\cdots,J_{i}^{K}
,
1\leq J_{i}^{K}\leq 3
, such that
K_{ij}^{h}
has one vertex at
A_{K}^{i}
and the other two vertices being the endpoints of
\Gamma_{K}^{h}
or the vertex of
K
in
\Omega_{i}
. Moreover,
K_{ij}^{h}
,
j=1,\cdots,J_{i}^{K}
,
i=1,2
, is shape regular in the sense that the radius of the inscribed circle of
K_{ij}^{h}
is bounded below by
c_{0}h_{K}
for some constant
c_{0}>0
depending only on
\delta_{0}
in Definition
2.1
. We always set
K_{i1}^{h}
the triangle with
\Gamma_{K}^{h}
as one of its sides, see Fig.
2.1
.

Illustration of
K_{ij}^{h}
,

We now recall an important concept of
K
-mesh in Babuška and Miller
[
4
]
for the Cartesian mesh
\mathcal{T}
having hanging nodes. Let
\mathcal{N}^{0}
be the set of conforming nodes of
\mathcal{T}
which are the vertices of the elements either locating on the boundary

\psi_{P}\in H^{1}(\Omega)

\psi_{P}(Q)=\delta_{PQ}

.

Assumption (H3)
: There exists a constant

\mathcal{T}
such that for any conforming node

{\rm diam}({\rm supp}(\psi_{P}))\leq C\min_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{P}}h_{K}
, where
\mathcal{T}_{P}=\{K\in\mathcal{T}:K\subset{\rm supp}(\psi_{P})\}

Further properties of
K
-meshes can be found in
[
4
]
. A refinement algorithm to enforce the assumption (H3) can be found in Bonito and Nochetto
[

Let
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{E}^{\rm side}\cup\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}\cup\mathcal{E}^{\rm bdy}
, where
\mathcal{E}^{\rm side}:=\left\{e=\partial K\cap\partial K^{\prime}:K,K^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}\right\}
,
\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}:=\{\Gamma_{\!\!K}=\Gamma\cap K:K\in\mathcal{M}\}
, and

can be part of a side of an adjacent element. For any subset
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}\subset\mathcal{M}
and
\widehat{\mathcal{E}}\subset\mathcal{E}
, we use the notation

\displaystyle(u,v)_{\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}=\sum_{K\in\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}(u,v)_{K},\ \ \langle u,v\rangle_{\widehat{\mathcal{E}}}=\sum_{e\in\widehat{\mathcal{E}}}\langle u,v\rangle_{e},

where
(\cdot,\cdot)_{K}
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{e}
denote the inner product of
L^{2}(K)
and
L^{2}(e)
, respectively.

For any
e\in\mathcal{E}
, we fix a unit normal vector
\mathbf{n}_{e}
of
e
with the convention that
\mathbf{n}_{e}
is the unit outer normal to
\partial\Omega_{1}
if
e\in\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}
and to
\partial\Omega
if
e\in\mathcal{E}^{\rm bdy}
. Define the normal function
\mathbf{n}|_{e}=\mathbf{n}_{e}\ \forall e\in\mathcal{E}
. For any
v\in H^{1}(\mathcal{M}):=\{v_{1}\chi_{K_{1}}+v_{2}\chi_{K_{2}}:v_{1},v_{2}\in H^{1}(K),K\in\mathcal{M}\}
, we define the jump operator of

e
:

\displaystyle[\![v]\!]|_{e}:=v^{-}-v^{+}\ \ \forall e\in\mathcal{E}^{\rm side}\cup\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma},\ \ \ \ [\![v]\!]|_{e}:=v^{-}\ \ \forall e\in\mathcal{E}^{\rm bdy},

where
v^{\pm}(\mathbf{x}):=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}v(\mathbf{x}\pm\varepsilon\mathbf{n}_{e})
for any
\mathbf{x}\in e
. The mesh function
h|_{e}=(h_{K}+h_{K^{\prime}})/2
if

and
h|_{e}=h_{K}
if
e=K\cap\Gamma\in\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}
or
e=\partial K\cap\partial\Omega\in\mathcal{E}^{\rm bdy}
for some
K\in\mathcal{M}
.

For any
p\geq 1
and any Lipschitz domain
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 1
, we denote
Q_{p}(D)
the set of polynomials of degree at most
p
in each variable. The following lemma on the local smoothing operator on

-meshes is proved in

Lemma 2.2
.

There exists an interpolation operator
\pi_{h}
:
\mathbb{V}_{p}(\mathcal{T})\rightarrow\mathbb{V}_{p}(\mathcal{T})\cap H^{1}(\Omega)
such that for any

,

\displaystyle\|v-\pi_{h}v\|_{L^{2}(K)}\leq C\|p^{-1}h^{1/2}[{\hskip-1.5pt}[v]{\hskip-1.5pt}]\|_{L^{2}(\sigma(K))},

\displaystyle\|\nabla(v-\pi_{h}v)\|_{L^{2}(K)}\leq C\|ph^{-1/2}[{\hskip-1.5pt}[v]{\hskip-1.5pt}]\|_{L^{2}(\sigma(K))},

where
\mathbb{V}_{p}(\mathcal{T})=\prod_{K\in\mathcal{T}}Q_{p}(K)
and
\sigma(K)=\{e\in\mathcal{E}^{side}:e\subset\tilde{\omega}(K)\}
,
\tilde{\omega}(K)
is a set of elements including

\text{diam}(\tilde{\omega}(K))\leq Ch_{K}
. The constant

is independent of
h_{K}
,

. Moreover,
\pi_{h}v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)

Since the induced mesh

2.2
Unfitted finite element spaces

In this subsection we introduce the scalar and vector unfitted finite element spaces on the induced mesh
\mathcal{M}
which are motivated by the idea of “doubling of unknowns” in Hansbo and Hansbo
[
25
]
. For any
p,q\geq 1
and any Lipschitz domain
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

, the space
P_{p}(D)
denotes the space of polynomials of degree at most

in

, and

p
for the first variable and
q
for the second variable in

. For any
K\in\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}
, in the notation in Lemma

, we know that

\displaystyle K=K_{1}^{h}\cup\Gamma_{K}^{h}\cup K_{2}^{h},\ \ \bar{K}_{i}^{h}=\cup^{J_{i}^{K}}_{j=1}\bar{K}_{ij}^{h},\ \ i=1,2.

From

\widetilde{K}_{ij}^{h}

\displaystyle\widetilde{K}_{i1}^{h}=(K_{i}\cap K_{i1}^{h})\cup(K_{i}\backslash\bar{K}_{i}^{h}),\ \ \widetilde{K}_{ij}^{h}=K_{i}\cap K_{ij}^{h},\ \ j=2,\cdots,J_{i}^{K}.

Then we have

\displaystyle K=K_{1}\cup\Gamma_{K}\cup K_{2},\ \ K_{i}=\mbox{\rm the interior of }\overline{\cup^{J_{i}^{K}}_{j=1}{\widetilde{K}}_{ij}^{h}},\ \ i=1,2.

For
i=1,2
, let
\mathcal{M}_{i}
be the union of elements of
\mathcal{M}
which is inside
\Omega_{i}

Lemma 2.3

For
i=1,2

are one of the elements
\{\widetilde{M}_{ij}^{h}\}^{J_{i}^{M}}_{j=1}

\{\widetilde{M}^{{}^{\prime}h}_{ij}\}^{J_{i}^{M^{\prime}}}_{j=1}

For any

\displaystyle W_{p}(K)=\{\varphi:\varphi|_{\widetilde{K}_{ij}^{h}}\in P_{p}(\widetilde{K}_{ij}^{h}),\ i=1,2,\ j=1,\cdots,J_{i}^{K}\},

Now we define the following unfitted finite element spaces

\displaystyle X_{p}(\mathcal{M}):=\{v\in H^{1}(\Omega_{1}\cup\Omega_{2}):v|_{K}\in X_{p}(K)\ \ \forall K\in\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma},

\displaystyle\hskip 136.5733ptv|_{K}\in Q_{p}(K)\ \ \forall K\in\mathcal{M}\backslash\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}\},

\displaystyle\mathbf{W}_{p}(\mathcal{M}):=\{\bm{\psi}\in[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{2}:\bm{\psi}|_{K}\in[W_{p}(K)]^{2}\ \ \forall K\in\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma},

Let

Our finite element space

for the interface elements is different from the one in

is chosen such that

for any

To proceed, we recall the concept of interface deviation introduced in
[
13
]
in order to quantify how the mesh resolves the geometry of the interface.

Definition 2.2
.

For any
K\in\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}
, the interface deviation
\eta_{K}
is defined as

\displaystyle\eta_{K}=\max_{i=1,2}\frac{\mbox{\rm dist}_{\rm H}(\Gamma_{\!\!K},\Gamma_{\!\!K}^{h})}{\mbox{\rm dist}(A_{K}^{i},\Gamma_{\!\!K}^{h})},

where
\displaystyle\mbox{\rm dist}_{\rm H}(\Gamma_{\!\!K},\Gamma_{\!\!K}^{h})=\max_{x\in\Gamma_{\!\!K}}(\min_{y\in\Gamma_{\!\!K}^{h}}|x-y|)
and
\displaystyle\mbox{\rm dist}(A_{K}^{i},\Gamma_{\!\!K}^{h})={{\min_{y\in\Gamma_{\!\!K}^{h}}|A_{K}^{i}-y|}}
.

It is easy to show that if the interface
\Gamma
is
C^{2}
-smooth, there exists a constant
C
which is independent of
h_{K}
such that
\eta_{K}\leq Ch_{K}
. Thus the following assumption is not very restrictive in practical applications.

Assumption (H4)
: For any
K\in\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}
,
\eta_{K}\leq 1/2
.

The interface deviation is crucial for us to show the inverse estimates on curved domains which play an important role in our study of unfitted finite element methods. We start
from the following one dimensional domain inverse estimate proved in
[
13
, Lemma 2.3]

\displaystyle\|g\|_{L^{2}(I_{\lambda}\backslash\bar{I})}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\left[(\lambda+\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-1})^{2p+1}-1\right]\|g\|_{L^{2}(I)}^{2}\ \ \forall g\in Q_{p}(I).

(2.1)

where
I=(-1,1)
,
I_{\lambda}=(-\lambda,\lambda)
,
\lambda>1
. We remark that the growing factor
(\lambda+\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-1})^{2p+1}
in above bound is sharp which is attained by the Chebyshev polynomials
C_{n}(t)
,
n\geq 0
. It is well-known (e.g., DeVore and Lorentz
[
21
, P.76]
) that
C_{n}(t)=\frac{1}{2}[(t+\sqrt{t^{2}-1})^{n}+(t-\sqrt{t^{2}-1})^{n}]
,
n\geq 0
.

By (
2.1
), one can prove the following two dimensional domain inverse estimate by the same argument as that in
[

, Lemma 2.4]
, where domain inverse estimates for

Lemma 2.4
.

Let

,

\displaystyle\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Delta)}\leq\mathsf{T}\left(\frac{1+\delta a_{2}^{-1}}{1-\delta a_{2}^{-1}}\right)^{p+3/2}\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Delta_{\delta})}\ \ \forall v\in P_{p}(\Delta),

Set

\displaystyle\Theta_{K}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathsf{T}(\frac{1+3\eta_{K}}{1-\eta_{K}})^{2p+3}&\text{ if }K\in\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma},\\
\\
1&\text{ otherwise },\end{array}\right.

Lemma 2.7

For any

,

where we have used
\|\varphi\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial T)}\leq C\|\varphi\|_{H^{1}(T)}\ \forall\varphi\in H^{1}(T)
and the multiplicative trace inequality (
2.9
). Now by the scaling argument we obtain

\displaystyle\|v^{2}\|_{L^{2}(K_{ij}^{h})}+\Big(\frac{h_{K}}{p}\Big)\|\nabla v^{2}\|_{L^{2}(K_{ij}^{h})}

(2.14)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(1+\log p)\Big(\|v-v^{1}\|_{L^{2}(K_{ij}^{h})}+\frac{h_{K}}{p}\|\nabla(v-v^{1})\|_{L^{2}(K_{ij}^{h})}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(1+\log p)\frac{h_{K}^{\nu}}{p^{s-1/2}}\|u\|_{H^{s}(\tilde{\omega}(K))},

where we have used (
2.12
)-(
2.13
). In a similar way,
for
i=2,j=1
, we let
v^{2}|_{K_{21}^{h}}\in P_{p}(K_{21}^{h})
be the lifting of the edge values of
(v^{1}+v^{2})|_{K_{2j}^{h}}-v^{1}|_{K_{21}^{h}}
on
\partial K_{21}^{h}\cap\partial K_{2j}^{h}
,
j=2,3
, which satisfies

\displaystyle\|v^{2}\|_{L^{2}(K_{21}^{h})}+\Big(\frac{h_{K}}{p}\Big)\|\nabla v^{2}\|_{L^{2}(K_{21}^{h})}

(2.15)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(1+\log p)\sum^{3}_{j=1}\Big(\|v^{1}\|_{L^{2}(K_{2j}^{h})}+\frac{h_{K}}{p}\|\nabla v^{1}\|_{L^{2}(K_{2j}^{h})}\Big)

\displaystyle\,+\,C(1+\log p)\sum^{3}_{j=2}\Big(\|v^{2}\|_{L^{2}(K_{2j}^{h})}+\frac{h_{K}}{p}\|\nabla v^{2}\|_{L^{2}(K_{2j}^{h})}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(1+\log p)^{2}\frac{h_{K}^{\nu}}{p^{s-1/2}}\|u\|_{H^{s}(\tilde{\omega}(K))}.

Let
w=\gamma_{K}^{hp}(u)+v^{1}+v^{2}\in U_{p}(K)
. Since
v^{2}=v-v^{1}
on
\partial K_{ij}^{h}\cap\partial K
, we know that
v^{1}+v^{2}+\gamma_{K}^{hp}(u)=\Pi_{hp}(u)
on
\partial K
. Moreover, since
v^{2}|_{K_{21}^{h}}=(v^{1}+v^{2})|_{K_{2j}^{h}}-v^{1}|_{K_{21}^{h}}
on
\partial K_{21}^{h}\cap\partial K_{2j}^{h}
,
j=2,3
, we know that
v^{1}+v^{2}
is continuous across
\partial K_{21}^{h}\cap\partial K_{2j}^{h}
,
j=2,3
. Thus
w\in H^{1}(K_{1}^{h}\cup K_{2}^{h})
. Now we define
\tilde{\Pi}_{hp}(u)=\Lambda_{K}(w)
. Then

\tilde{\Pi}_{hp}(u)\in X_{p}(K)
and
\tilde{\Pi}_{hp}(u)-\gamma_{K}^{hp}(u)=\Lambda_{K}(v^{1}+v^{2})
. Now by (
2.5
), Lemma
2.6
, and above estimates,

\displaystyle\|u-\tilde{\Pi}_{hp}(u)\|_{L^{2}(K)}+\Big(\frac{h_{K}}{p}\Big)\|\nabla(u-\tilde{\Pi}_{hp}(u))\|_{L^{2}(K)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|u-\gamma_{K}^{hp}(u)\|_{L^{2}(K)}+\Big(\frac{h_{K}}{p}\Big)\|\nabla(u-\gamma_{K}^{hp}(u))\|_{L^{2}(K)}

\displaystyle+C\Theta_{K}^{1/2}\Big(\|v^{1}+v^{2}\|_{L^{2}(K)}+\frac{h_{K}}{p}\|\nabla_{h}(v^{1}+v^{2})\|_{L^{2}(K)}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\Theta_{K}^{1/2}(1+\log p)^{2}\frac{h_{K}^{\nu}}{p^{s-1/2}}\|u\|_{H^{s}(\tilde{\omega}(K))}.

This completes the proof.
∎

The following theorem is the main result of this subsection.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
s>3/2
. There exists an interpolation operator

such that for any

,

\displaystyle\|u-I_{hp}(u)\|_{\mathcal{M}}+\Big(\frac{h}{p}\Big)\|\nabla_{h}(u-I_{hp}(u))\|_{\mathcal{M}}

(2.16)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\Theta^{1/2}(1+\log p)^{2}\frac{h^{\nu}}{p^{s-1/2}}\|u\|_{H^{s}(\Omega_{1}\cup\Omega_{2})}.

Moreover, for
u=u_{1}\chi_{\Omega_{1}}+u_{2}\chi_{\Omega_{2}}
,

\displaystyle\|u_{i}-[I_{hp}(u)]_{i}\|_{\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}}+\Big(\frac{h}{p^{3/2}}\Big)\|\nabla(u_{i}-[I_{hp}(u)]_{i})\|_{\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}}

(2.17)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\Theta^{1/2}(1+\log p)^{2}\frac{h^{\nu-1/2}}{p^{s-1}}\|u\|_{H^{s}(\Omega_{i})},\ \ i=1,2.

Here
h=\max_{K\in\mathcal{M}}h_{K}
,
\Theta=\max_{K\in\mathcal{M}}\Theta_{K}
and the constant
C
is independent of
p
,
h_{K}
for all
K\in\mathcal{M}
, and
\eta_{K}
for all

. Moreover,

if in addition,

.

Proof.

For
i=1,2
, let
\tilde{u}_{i}\in H^{s}(\Omega)
be the Stein extension of
u_{i}:=u|_{\Omega_{i}}\in H^{s}(\Omega_{i})
such that
\|\tilde{u}_{i}\|_{H^{s}(\Omega)}\leq C\|u_{i}\|_{H^{s}(\Omega_{i})}
. We define

\displaystyle I_{hp}(u)|_{K}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Pi_{hp}(\tilde{u}_{i})&\mbox{if }K\in\mathcal{M},K\subset\Omega_{i},i=1,2,\\
\tilde{\Pi}_{hp}(\tilde{u}_{1})\chi_{K_{1}}+\tilde{\Pi}_{hp}(\tilde{u}_{2})\chi_{K_{2}}&\mbox{if }K\in\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}.\end{array}\right.

The estimate (
2.16
) follows from Lemma
2.7
and Lemma
2.9
.

Now we prove the estimate for
\|\nabla(u_{i}-[I_{hp}(u)]_{i})\|_{\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}}
. The estimate for
\|u_{i}-[I_{hp}(u)]_{i}\|_{\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}}
can be proved similarly. Notice that by definition, for any

,

on
\Gamma_{K}
. By the trace inequality on curved domains in Xiao et al
[
42
, Lemma 3.1]
,

\displaystyle\|\nabla(\tilde{u}_{i}-\gamma_{K}^{hp}(\tilde{u}_{i}))\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{K})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\nabla(\tilde{u}_{i}-\gamma_{K}^{hp}(\tilde{u}_{i}))\|_{L^{2}(K_{i})}^{1/2}\|\nabla(\tilde{u}_{i}-\gamma_{K}^{hp}(\tilde{u}_{i}))\|_{H^{1}(K_{i})}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\,+\,C\|\nabla(\tilde{u}_{i}-\gamma_{K}^{hp}(\tilde{u}_{i}))\|_{L^{2}(\partial K_{i}\backslash\bar{\Gamma}_{K})}.

Since

2.6
we obtain

\displaystyle\|\nabla(\tilde{u}_{i}-\gamma_{K}^{hp}(\tilde{u}_{i}))\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{K})}\leq C\frac{h_{K}^{\nu-3/2}}{p^{s-3/2}}\|\tilde{u}_{i}\|_{H^{s}(\tilde{\omega}(K))}.

(2.19)

Notice that by the construction of
\tilde{\Pi}_{hp}
in Lemma
2.9
, we have
\gamma^{hp}_{K}(\tilde{u}_{i})-\tilde{\Pi}_{hp}(\tilde{u}_{i})=\Lambda_{K}(v_{i}^{1}+v_{i}^{2})

, where
v_{i}^{1}
,

with respect to
\tilde{u}_{i}

\displaystyle\|\nabla(\gamma_{K}^{hp}(\tilde{u}_{i})-\tilde{\Pi}_{hp}(\tilde{u}_{i}))\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{K})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\Lambda_{K}(\nabla(v_{i}^{1}+v_{i}^{2}))\|_{L^{2}(K_{i})}^{1/2}\|\Lambda_{K}(\nabla(v_{i}^{1}+v_{i}^{2}))\|_{H^{1}(K_{i})}^{1/2}

Since for any

\displaystyle\|\nabla(\gamma_{K}^{hp}(\tilde{u}_{i})-\tilde{\Pi}_{hp}(\tilde{u}_{i}))\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{K})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\Theta_{K}^{1/2}\|\nabla(v^{1}_{i}+v_{i}^{2})\|_{L^{2}(K_{i}^{h})}^{1/2}\|\nabla(v_{i}^{1}+v_{i}^{2})\|_{H^{1}(K_{i}^{h})}^{1/2}+C\|\nabla(v_{i}^{1}+v_{i}^{2})\|_{L^{2}(\partial K_{i}^{h}\cap\partial K)}

\displaystyle C\Theta_{K}^{1/2}(1+\log p)^{2}\frac{h_{K}^{\nu-3/2}}{p^{s-5/2}}\|u\|_{H^{s}(\tilde{\omega}(K))},

where we have used (

2.3
The unfitted finite element method

In this subsection we introduce our semi-discrete unfitted finite element method for the wave equation (
1.1
). Let
P_{h}:L^{2}(\Omega)\to X_{p}^{0}(\mathcal{M})
and
\bm{P}_{h}:[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{2}\to\mathbf{W}_{p}(\mathcal{M})
be the standard
L^{2}
projection operators

\displaystyle(P_{h}v,\varphi_{h})_{\mathcal{M}}=(v,\varphi_{h})_{\mathcal{M}}\quad\forall v\in L^{2}(\Omega),\ \forall\varphi_{h}\in X_{p}^{0}(\mathcal{M}),

(2.20)

\displaystyle(\bm{P}_{h}\bm{\sigma},\bm{\psi}_{h})_{\mathcal{M}}=(\bm{\sigma},\bm{\psi}_{h})_{\mathcal{M}}\quad\forall\bm{\sigma}\in[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{2},\ \forall\bm{\psi}_{h}\in\mathbf{W}_{p}(\mathcal{M}).

(2.21)

The semi-discrete unfitted finite element method for solving (
1.1
) is then to find
(u_{h},\mathbf{q}_{h})\in X_{p}^{0}(\mathcal{M})\times\mathbf{W}_{p}(\mathcal{M})
such that
for any
(\varphi_{h},\bm{\psi}_{h})\in X_{p}^{0}(\mathcal{M})\times\mathbf{W}_{p}(\mathcal{M})
,

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{1}{\rho c^{2}}\partial_{t}u_{h},\varphi_{h}\Big)_{\mathcal{M}}=\mathcal{H}^{-}(\mathbf{q}_{h},\varphi_{h})+(f,\varphi_{h})_{\mathcal{M}},

(2.22)

\displaystyle(\rho\partial_{t}\mathbf{q}_{h},\bm{\psi}_{h})_{\mathcal{M}}=\mathcal{H}^{+}(u_{h},\bm{\psi}_{h}),

(2.23)

\displaystyle u_{h}(\mathbf{x},0)=(P_{h}u_{0})(\mathbf{x}),\ \ \mathbf{q}_{h}(\mathbf{x},0)=(\bm{P}_{h}\mathbf{q}_{0})(\mathbf{x})\ \ \mbox{in }\Omega,

(2.24)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{-}(\mathbf{q}_{h},\varphi_{h})=-(\mathbf{q}_{h},\nabla_{h}\varphi_{h})_{\mathcal{M}}+\langle\mathbf{q}^{-}_{h}\cdot\mathbf{n},[\![\varphi_{h}]\!]\rangle_{\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}},

(2.25)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{+}(u_{h},\bm{\psi}_{h})=-(u_{h},\text{div}_{h}\bm{\psi}_{h})_{\mathcal{M}}+\langle u^{+}_{h},[{\hskip-1.5pt}[\bm{\psi}_{h}]{\hskip-1.5pt}]\cdot\mathbf{n}\rangle_{\mathcal{E}}.

(2.26)

Here for any
\bm{\psi}_{h}\in\mathbf{W}_{p}(\mathcal{M})
,

\displaystyle\text{div}_{h}\bm{\psi}_{h}|_{K}=({\rm div}\bm{\psi}_{h})|_{K},\ \ \mbox{if }K\in\mathcal{M}\backslash\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma},

\displaystyle\text{div}_{h}\bm{\psi}_{h}|_{\widetilde{K}_{ij}^{h}}=({\rm div}\bm{\psi}_{h})|_{\widetilde{K}_{ij}^{h}},i=1,2,j=1,\cdots,J_{i}^{K},\ \ \mbox{if }K\in\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}.

Since
[{\hskip-1.5pt}[\varphi_{h}]{\hskip-1.5pt}]=0
on
\mathcal{E}^{\rm side}
for any
\varphi_{h}\in X_{p}^{0}(\mathcal{M})\subset H^{1}(\Omega_{1}\cup\Omega_{2})
, it is easy to see by integration by parts that for
(\varphi_{h},\bm{\psi}_{h})\in X_{p}^{0}(\mathcal{M})\times\mathbf{W}_{p}(\mathcal{M})
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{+}(\varphi_{h},\bm{\psi}_{h})=(\nabla_{h}\varphi_{h},\bm{\psi}_{h})_{\mathcal{M}}-\langle[\![{\varphi_{h}}]\!],\bm{\psi}^{-}_{h}\cdot\mathbf{n}\rangle_{\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}}.

Thus

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{-}(\bm{\psi}_{h},\varphi_{h})+\mathcal{H}^{+}(\varphi_{h},\bm{\psi}_{h})=0\ \ \ \forall\varphi_{h}\in X_{p}^{0}(\mathcal{M}),\ \bm{\psi}_{h}\in\mathbf{W}_{p}(\mathcal{M}).

(2.27)

This identity reflects the duality of the gradient and divergence operators in the discrete setting.

We remark that our method (
2.22
)-(
2.23
) reduces to the mixed formulation of the spectral element method in
[
20
, §13.4]
when the interface is absent and the mesh are conforming. It is shown in
[
20
]
that the mixed formulation of the spectral element method is equivalent to the standard spectral element method for solving the wave equation in the second order form but has favorable properties in terms of the storage and the CPU time.

The following proposition shows that the proposed semi-discrete method conserves energy when the source is absent.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
f=0
in
\Omega\times(0,T)
. Then the (continuous) energy

\displaystyle E_{h}(t)=\int_{\Omega}\Big(\frac{1}{\rho c^{2}}|u_{h}|^{2}+\rho|\mathbf{q}_{h}|^{2}\Big)\,d\mathbf{x}

is conserved by the semi-discrete method (
2.22
)-(
2.23
) for all time.

Proof.

By taking the test functions
\varphi_{h}=u_{h}
and
\bm{\psi}_{h}=\mathbf{q}_{h}
in (
2.22
)-(
2.23
), adding the equations, and using (
2.27
), we obtain

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{1}{\rho c^{2}}\partial_{t}u_{h},u_{h}\Big)_{\mathcal{M}}+(\rho\partial_{t}\mathbf{q}_{h},\mathbf{q}_{h})_{\mathcal{M}}=0.

Therefore, the quantity
E_{h}(t)
is invariant in time.
∎

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
s>3/2
. The
L^{2}
projection operators in (
2.20
)-(
2.21
) satisfy the following error estimates

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pt\|v-P_{h}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\Theta^{1/2}(1+\log p)^{2}\frac{h^{\min(p+1,s)}}{p^{s-1/2}}\|v\|_{H^{s}(\Omega_{1}\cup\Omega_{2})}\ \ \forall v\in H^{s}(\Omega_{1}\cup\Omega_{2}),

Moreover, for
\bm{\sigma}=\bm{\sigma}_{1}\chi_{\Omega_{1}}+\bm{\sigma}_{2}\chi_{\Omega_{2}}

\displaystyle\|\bm{\sigma}_{i}-(\bm{P}_{h}\bm{\sigma})_{i}\|_{\mathcal{E}^{\Gamma}}\leq C\Theta^{1/2}\frac{h^{\min(p+1,s)-1/2}}{p^{s-1}}\|\bm{\sigma}\|_{H^{s}(\Omega_{i})}\quad\forall\bm{\sigma}\in[H^{s}(\Omega_{i})]^{2}.

Here the constant
C
is independent of

,
h_{K}
for all
K\in\mathcal{M}
, and
\eta_{K}
for all
K\in\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}
.

We remark that the first estimate is slightly sub-optimal in the power of

compared with (
2.8
). This is due to the presence of the curved interface. Optimal
hp
error estimates for the

projection operator on Cartesian meshes are known, see Houston et al
[
27
, Lemma 3.6]
.

Proof.

The first estimate follows directly from Theorem
2.1
since
\|v-P_{h}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq||v-I_{hp}(v)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
. Now we show the the second and the third estimates.
For any
\bm{\sigma}\in[H^{s}(\Omega_{1}\cup\Omega_{2})]^{2}

\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}\in[H^{s}(\Omega)]^{2}
the Stein extension of
\bm{\sigma}_{i}:=\bm{\sigma}|_{\Omega_{i}}\in[H^{s}(\Omega_{i})]^{2}
. Since
[Q_{p-1}(\mathcal{M})]^{2}\subset\mathbf{W}_{p}(\mathcal{M})
, we define the interpolation operator
\hat{\Pi}_{hp}:[H^{s}(\Omega_{1}\cup\Omega_{2})]^{2}\to\mathbf{W}_{p}(\mathcal{M})

\displaystyle\hat{\Pi}_{hp}(\bm{\sigma})|_{K}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\pi_{K}^{h,p-1}(\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}),&\text{if }K\in\mathcal{M},K\subset\Omega_{i},i=1,2,\\

∎

For any Lipschitz domain

, we use the standard notation

\displaystyle Z_{k}:=H^{k+1}(\Omega_{1}\cup\Omega_{2})\times H^{k}({\rm div\,};\Omega_{1}\cup\Omega_{2})

which is a Hilbert space under the graph norm.

Now we define an elliptic projection operator
\Upsilon_{h}:Z_{0}\to X_{p}^{0}(\mathcal{M})\times\mathbf{W}_{p}(\mathcal{M})
which plays an important role in studying the convergence of our unfitted finite element method in this paper. For any
(v,\bm{\sigma})\in Z_{0}
, let
(v_{I},\bm{\sigma}_{I})=\Upsilon_{h}(v,\bm{\sigma})\in X_{p}^{0}(\mathcal{M})\times\mathbf{W}_{p}(\mathcal{M})

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{1}{\rho c^{2}}(v_{I}-v),\varphi_{h}\Big)_{\mathcal{M}}=\mathcal{H}^{-}(\bm{\sigma}_{I}-\bm{\sigma},\varphi_{h})\ \ \forall\varphi_{h}\in X_{p}^{0}(\mathcal{M}),

(2.28)

\displaystyle(\rho(\bm{\sigma}_{I}-\bm{\sigma}),\bm{\psi}_{h})_{\mathcal{M}}=\mathcal{H}^{+}(v_{I}-v,\bm{\psi}_{h})\ \ \forall\bm{\psi}_{h}\in\mathbf{W}_{p}(\mathcal{M}).

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{-}(\bm{\psi},\varphi)=-(\bm{\psi},\nabla_{h}\varphi)_{\mathcal{M}}+\langle\bm{\psi}^{-}\cdot{\bf n},[{\hskip-1.5pt}[\varphi]{\hskip-1.5pt}]\rangle_{\Gamma},

where

is the duality pairing between

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{+}(\varphi,\bm{\psi})=(\nabla_{h}\varphi,\bm{\psi})_{\mathcal{M}}-\langle[{\hskip-1.5pt}[\varphi]{\hskip-1.5pt}],\bm{\psi}^{-}\cdot{\bf n}\rangle_{\Gamma}.

\bm{\psi}_{2}\in[W_{p}(K)]^{2}

in (
2.29

\displaystyle(\rho(\bm{\sigma}_{I}-\bm{\sigma}),\bm{\psi}_{2})_{K_{2}}=(\nabla(v_{I}-v),\bm{\psi}_{2})_{K_{2}}\ \ \forall\bm{\psi}_{2}\in[W_{p}(K)]^{2},

where

L^{2}(K_{2})
. This implies
\rho(\bm{\sigma}_{I}-\bm{P}_{h}\bm{\sigma})=\nabla v_{I}-\bm{P}_{h}(\nabla v)
in
K_{2}
. Thus we have

\displaystyle\rho^{+}(\bm{\sigma}_{I}-\bm{P}_{h}\bm{\sigma})^{+}=(\nabla v_{I}-\bm{P}_{h}(\nabla v))^{+}\ \ \mbox{on }\Gamma.

(2.30)

We remark that the same argument applying to (
2.23

\rho^{+}\partial_{t}\mathbf{q}_{h}^{+}=\nabla_{h}u_{h}^{+}
on
\Gamma
. This property seems to be new in the literature, which is crucial for us to derive optimal energy error estimates without using the penalty terms.

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
(v,\bm{\sigma})\in Z_{k}
,
k\geq 1
, and
(v_{I},\bm{\sigma}_{I})=\Upsilon_{h}(v,\bm{\sigma})
be the solution of (
2.28
)-(

). Then we have

\displaystyle\|v-v_{I}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\bm{\sigma}-\bm{\sigma}_{I}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\Theta(1+\log p)^{2}\frac{h^{\min(p,k)}}{p^{k-3/2}}\|(v,\bm{\sigma})\|_{Z_{k}},

where the constant
C
is independent of
p
,
h_{K}
for all
K\in\mathcal{M}
, and
\eta_{K}
for all
K\in\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}
.

Proof.

We first introduce an interpolation function for
\bm{\sigma}\in H^{k}({\rm div\,};\Omega_{1}\cup\Omega_{2})
. By the lifted regular decomposition theorem for Lipschitz domains in Hiptmair et al
[
26
, Theorem 5.2]
, there exist
\bm{u}\in[H^{k+1}(\Omega_{1}\cup\Omega_{2})]^{2},w\in H^{k+1}(\Omega_{1}\cup\Omega_{2})
such that
\bm{\sigma}=\bm{u}+{\bf curl\,}w
, and

\displaystyle\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{k+1}(\Omega_{1}\cup\Omega_{2})}+\|w\|_{H^{k+1}(\Omega_{1}\cup\Omega_{2})}\leq C\|\bm{\sigma}\|_{H^{k}({\rm div\,};\Omega_{1}\cup\Omega_{2})}.

(2.31)

Define

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{P}}_{h}\bm{\sigma}=\bm{P}_{h}(\bm{u}+{\bf curl\,}I_{hp}(w))\in\mathbf{W}_{p}(\mathcal{M}).

By the triangle inequality, Lemma
2.10
, and Theorem
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle\|\bm{\sigma}-\hat{\bm{P}}_{h}\bm{\sigma}\|_{\mathcal{M}}

(2.32)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\bm{u}-\bm{P}_{h}\bm{u}\|_{\mathcal{M}}+\|{\bf curl\,}w-\bm{P}_{h}{\bf curl\,}w\|_{\mathcal{M}}+\|{\bf curl\,}(w-I_{hp}(w))\|_{\mathcal{M}}

\displaystyle\leq

Theorem 2.2
.

Assume that
(u_{0},\mathbf{q}_{0})\in Z_{k}
for some integer
k\geq 1
. Let
(u,\mathbf{q})\in H^{1}(0,T;Z_{k})
be the solution of the wave equations (
1.1
), and
(u_{h},\mathbf{q}_{h})\in X_{p}^{0}(\mathcal{M})\times\mathbf{W}_{p}(\mathcal{M})
be the solution of (
2.22
)-(
2.24

C
independent of

,
h_{K}
for all
K\in\mathcal{M}
, and
\eta_{K}
for all

such that

\displaystyle\max_{0\leq t\leq T}(\|u-u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{q}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\Theta(1+\log p)^{2}\frac{h^{\min(p,k)}}{p^{k-1}}\|(u_{0},\mathbf{q}_{0})\|_{Z_{k}}

\displaystyle+\,C\Theta(1+\log p)^{2}(1+T^{1/2})\frac{h^{\min(p,k)}}{p^{k-3/2}}\|(u,\mathbf{q})\|_{H^{1}(0,T;Z_{k})}.

We remark that the regularity assumption

1.1

1.1
) if

Proof.

It follows from the equations in (
1.1
) that

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{1}{\rho c^{2}}\partial_{t}u,\varphi_{h}\Big)_{\mathcal{M}}=\mathcal{H}^{-}(\mathbf{q},\varphi_{h})+(f,\varphi_{h})_{\mathcal{M}}\quad\forall\varphi_{h}\in X_{p}^{0}(\mathcal{M}),

\displaystyle(\rho\partial_{t}\mathbf{q},\bm{\psi}_{h})_{\mathcal{M}}=\mathcal{H}^{+}(u,\bm{\psi}_{h})\quad\forall\bm{\psi}_{h}\in\mathbf{W}_{p}(\mathcal{M}).

Denote
(u_{I},\mathbf{q}_{I})=\Upsilon_{h}(u,\mathbf{q})\in X_{p}^{0}(\mathcal{M})\times\mathbf{W}_{p}(\mathcal{M})
defined in (
2.28
)-(
2.29
). Then for
(\varphi_{h},\bm{\psi}_{h})\in X_{p}^{0}(\mathcal{M})\times\mathbf{W}_{p}(\mathcal{M})
,

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{1}{\rho c^{2}}\partial_{t}u_{I},\varphi_{h}\Big)_{\mathcal{M}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{-}(\mathbf{q}_{I},\varphi_{h})+(f,\varphi_{h})_{\mathcal{M}}+(R_{u},\varphi_{h})_{\mathcal{M}},

(2.42)

\displaystyle(\rho\partial_{t}\mathbf{q}_{I},\bm{\psi}_{h})_{\mathcal{M}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{+}(u_{I},\bm{\psi}_{h})+(\mathbf{R}_{\mathbf{q}},\bm{\psi}_{h})_{\mathcal{M}},

(2.43)

where
(R_{u},\mathbf{R}_{\bm{q}})\in X_{p}^{0}(\mathcal{M})\times\mathbf{W}_{p}(\mathcal{M})
are defined by

\displaystyle(R_{u},\varphi_{h})_{\mathcal{M}}

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{1}{\rho c^{2}}[\partial_{t}(u_{I}-u)+(u-u_{I})],\varphi_{h}\Big)_{\mathcal{M}}\ \ \forall\varphi_{h}\in X_{p}^{0}(\mathcal{M}),

(2.44)

\displaystyle=(\rho[\partial_{t}(\mathbf{q}_{I}-\mathbf{q})+(\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{q}_{I})],\bm{\psi}_{h})_{\mathcal{M}}\ \ \forall\bm{\psi}_{h}\in\mathbf{W}_{p}(\mathcal{M}).

Thus subtract (
2.42

2.43

2.22
)-(
2.23
), take

,

2.27

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\Big(\|(\rho c^{2})^{-1/2}(u_{I}-u_{h})\|_{\mathcal{M}}^{2}+\|\rho^{1/2}(\mathbf{q}_{I}-\mathbf{q}_{h})\|_{\mathcal{M}}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\Theta(1+\log p)^{2}\frac{h^{\min(p,k)}}{p^{k-3/2}}(\|(u,\mathbf{q})\|_{Z_{k}}+\|\partial_{t}(u,\mathbf{q})\|_{Z_{k}})(\|\varphi_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\bm{\psi}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}).

3
The explicit time discretization for the ODE system

In this section, we propose a strongly stable high order explicit time discretization method for the ODE system

\displaystyle\mathbf{Y}^{\prime}(t)=\mathbb{D}\mathbf{Y}(t)+\mathbf{R}(t)\ \ \mbox{in }(0,T),\ \ \ \ \mathbf{Y}(0)=\mathbf{Y}^{0},

(3.1)

where for
M\geq 1
,
\mathbf{Y}\in\mathbb{R}^{M},\mathbb{D}\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times M}
satisfies (
2.53
), and
\mathbf{R},\mathbf{Y}^{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{M}
.

Let
0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{N}=T
be a partition of the interval
(0,T)
with the time step sizes
\tau_{n}=t_{n+1}-t_{n}
. We set
\displaystyle\tau=\max_{0\leq n\leq N-1}\tau_{n}
. For any integer
m\geq 1
, we define the finite element space

\displaystyle\qquad\mathbf{V}_{m}(0,T)=\{\mathbf{v}\in(C[0,T])^{M}:\mathbf{v}|_{(t_{n},t_{n+1})}\in[P^{m}(t_{n},t_{n+1})]^{M},0\leq n\leq N-1\}.

From (
3.1
) we know that for
j\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{Y}^{(j)}(t)=\mathbb{D}^{j}\mathbf{Y}(t)+\sum^{j-1}_{\ell=0}\mathbb{D}^{j-1-\ell}\mathbf{R}^{(\ell)}(t).

Thus by Taylor expansion, we know that for
t\in(t_{n},t_{n+1})
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{Y}(t)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\sum^{r}_{j=0}\frac{1}{j!}\mathbf{Y}^{(j)}(t_{n})(t-t_{n})^{j}

(3.2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum^{r}_{j=0}\frac{1}{j!}\left[\mathbb{D}^{j}\mathbf{Y}(t_{n})+\sum^{j-1}_{\ell=0}\mathbb{D}^{j-1-\ell}\mathbf{R}^{(\ell)}(t_{n})\right](t-t_{n})^{j}.

For
r\geq 1
, the
r
stage
r
order explicit Runge-Kutta scheme is equivalent to compute
\mathbf{Y}_{r}(t_{n+1})
, which is the approximate value of
\mathbf{Y}(t_{n+1})
, from the approximate value
\mathbf{Y}^{n}
of
\mathbf{Y}_{r}(t_{n})
, where

\displaystyle\mathbf{Y}_{r}(t)=\sum^{r}_{j=0}\frac{1}{j!}\left[\mathbb{D}^{j}\mathbf{Y}^{n}+\sum^{j-1}_{\ell=0}\mathbb{D}^{j-1-\ell}\mathbf{R}^{(\ell)}(t_{n})\right](t-t_{n})^{j}.

(3.3)

This scheme is, unfortunately, not strongly stable when
r=1,2\,(\mbox{mod }4)
, see Sun and Shu
[
38
, Theorem 4.2]
. To overcome the difficulty, we propose to add a stabilization term in (
3.3
) and introduce the following scheme when
r=1,2\,(\mbox{mod }4)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{r}(t)=\mathbf{Y}_{r}(t)+\frac{1}{r!(r+\gamma)}\left[\mathbb{D}^{r+1}\mathbf{Y}^{n}+\sum^{r}_{\ell=0}\mathbb{D}^{r-\ell}\mathbf{R}^{(\ell)}(t_{n})\right](t-t_{n})^{r+1}.

(3.4)

Notice that
\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{r}(t)=\mathbf{Y}_{r+1}(t)
if
\gamma=1
. We will show that (
3.4
) leads to a strongly stable explicit scheme when chosing
\gamma\in(0,1)
for
r=1,2\,(\mbox{mod }4)
.

Our explicit time discretization method for solving (
3.1
) is the following algorithm which iteratively computes
\mathbf{Y}_{r},\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{r}
in (
3.3
), (
3.4
). Given
\mathbf{R}(t)\in(C^{r}[0,T])^{M}
, we find
\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{r}\in\mathbf{V}_{r+1}(0,T)
such that
\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{r}(0)=\mathbf{Y}^{0}
, and in each time interval
(t_{n},t_{n+1})
,
0\leq n\leq N-1
,
\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{r}
is computed by the following algorithm with
\mathbf{Y}^{n}=\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{r}(t_{n})
.

Algorithm 3.1
.

Given
\gamma\in(0,1)
,
\mathbf{Y}^{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{M}
, and
\mathbf{R}\in\mathbb{R}^{M}
.

1^{\circ}
Set
\mathbf{Y}_{0}=\mathbf{Y}^{n}
and find
\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{0}\in[P^{1}(t_{n},t_{n+1})]^{M}
such that

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{0}^{\prime}=\frac{1}{\gamma}\,\mathbb{D}\mathbf{Y}^{n},\ \ \widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{0}(t_{n})=\mathbf{Y}^{n}.

2^{\circ}
For
1\leq m\leq r
, compute
\mathbf{Y}_{m}\in[P^{m}(t_{n},t_{n+1})]^{M}
such that

\displaystyle\mathbf{Y}_{m}^{\prime}=\mathbb{D}\mathbf{Y}_{m-1}+I_{m-1}\mathbf{R},\ \ \mathbf{Y}_{m}(t_{n})=\mathbf{Y}^{n}.

3^{\circ}
For
1\leq m\leq r
, compute
\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{m}\in[P^{m+1}(t_{n},t_{n+1})]^{M}
such that

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{m}^{\prime}=\mathbb{D}[\gamma_{m}^{r}\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{m-1}+(1-\gamma_{m}^{r})\mathbf{Y}_{m-1}]+\gamma_{m}^{r}\tilde{I}_{m-1}\mathbf{R}+(1-\gamma_{m}^{r})I_{m-1}\mathbf{R},

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{m}(t_{n})=\mathbf{Y}^{n},

where for
1\leq m\leq r
, the parameter

\displaystyle\gamma_{m}^{r}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}0,&\text{ if }r=0,3\,(\mbox{\rm mod}\,4),\\
\frac{(m+1)(m-1+\gamma)}{m(m+\gamma)},&\text{ if }r=1,2\,(\mbox{\rm mod}\,4),\end{array}\right.

and

\displaystyle I_{m-1}\mathbf{R}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\frac{1}{j!}\mathbf{R}^{(j)}(t_{n})(t-t_{n})^{j},

\displaystyle\tilde{I}_{m-1}\mathbf{R}

\displaystyle=I_{m-1}\mathbf{R}+\frac{1}{(m-1)!(m-1+\gamma)}\mathbf{R}^{(m)}(t_{n})(t-t_{n})^{m}.

The parameter
\gamma^{r}_{m}
is so chosen that one can easily show by mathematical induction that the outcome of Algorithm
3.1
is
\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{r}=\mathbf{Y}_{r}
in (
3.3
) if
r=0,3\,(\mbox{\rm mod}\,4)
and
\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{r}
in (
3.4
) if
r=1,2\,(\mbox{\rm mod}\,4)
. We also remark that Algorithm
3.1
is fully explicit and we will construct an efficient implementation of Algorithm
3.1
in subsection 3.3 based on recursion formulas.

3.1
Strong stability

In this subsection, we show the strong stability of the proposed time discretizaiton method. For simplicity, we first consider the strong stability without source terms. We denote
(\cdot,\cdot)
the usual
\ell_{2}
inner product and
\|\cdot\|_{\ell_{2}}
the
\ell_{2}
norm of
\mathbb{R}^{M}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\mathbf{R}=0
in Algorithm
3.1
. Then we have

\displaystyle\max_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{r}\|_{\ell_{2}}\leq\|\mathbf{Y}^{0}\|_{\ell_{2}}

under the CFL condition
\lambda=\tau\|\mathbb{D}\|_{\ell_{2}}\leq\lambda(r,\gamma)
, where

\displaystyle\lambda(r,\gamma)=\sqrt{6}\ \ \mbox{if }r=0\,(\mbox{\rm mod}\,4),

(3.6)

\displaystyle\lambda(1,\gamma)=\sqrt{\frac{1-\gamma^{2}}{2}},\ \ \lambda(r,\gamma)=\sqrt{\frac{2(1-\gamma)}{3-\gamma}}\ \ \mbox{if }r=1\,(\mbox{\rm mod}\,4),r\geq 5,

(3.7)

\displaystyle\lambda(2,\gamma)=\sqrt{\frac{2(4-\gamma^{2})}{3}},\ \ \lambda(r,\gamma)=\sqrt{\frac{6(2-\gamma)}{4-\gamma}}\ \ \mbox{if }r=2\,(\mbox{\rm mod}\,4),r\geq 6,

(3.8)

\displaystyle\lambda(r,\gamma)=\sqrt{2}\ \ \mbox{if }r=3\,(\mbox{\rm mod}\,4).

(3.9)

Proof.

If
r\equiv 0,3\,(\mbox{\rm mod}\,4)
, by using
\mathbb{D}+\mathbb{D}^{T}=\mathbb{O}
in (
2.53
) we have
(\mathbb{D}\mathbf{Y}_{r},\mathbf{Y}_{r})=0
. By the second step of Algorithm
3.1
and (
3.3
)

\displaystyle(\mathbf{Y}_{r}^{\prime}(t),\mathbf{Y}_{r}(t))

\displaystyle=(\mathbb{D}\mathbf{Y}_{r-1}(t),\mathbf{Y}_{r}(t))

\displaystyle=(\mathbb{D}(\mathbf{Y}_{r-1}(t)-\mathbf{Y}_{r}(t)),\mathbf{Y}_{r}(t))

\displaystyle=\Big(-\frac{1}{r!}\mathbb{D}^{r+1}\mathbf{Y}^{n}(t-t_{n})^{r},\mathbf{Y}_{r}(t)\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{r}\beta_{r+k}(t-t_{n})^{r+k},

where

\displaystyle\beta_{r+k}=(-1)^{r}\frac{1}{r!k!}(\mathbf{Y}^{n},\mathbb{D}^{r+k+1}\mathbf{Y}^{n}).

Thus
\beta_{r+k}=0
if
r+k+1
is odd.

If
r=0\,(\mbox{\rm mod}\,4)
, we assume
r=4s
,
s\geq 0
. Then
\beta_{r+k}\not=0
only when
k
is odd, that is,
k=2q+1
for some
q\geq 0
. Hence

\displaystyle(\mathbf{Y}_{r}^{\prime}(t),\mathbf{Y}_{r}(t))

\displaystyle=\sum_{q=0}^{2s-1}(-1)^{q+1}\frac{1}{r!(2q+1)!}\|\mathbb{D}^{2s+q+1}\mathbf{Y}^{n}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}(t-t_{n})^{r+2q+1}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}\Big(-\frac{1}{r!(4j+1)!}+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{r!(4j+3)!}\Big)\|\mathbb{D}^{2s+4j+1}\mathbf{Y}^{n}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}(t-t_{n})^{r+4j+1}.

This implies
(\mathbf{Y}_{r}^{\prime}(t),\mathbf{Y}_{r}(t))\leq 0
if
\lambda^{2}\leq(4j+3)(4j+2)\ \ \forall j=0,\ldots s-1
. Hence
\displaystyle\max_{t_{n}\leq t\leq t_{n+1}}\|\mathbf{Y}_{r}\|_{\ell_{2}}\leq\|\mathbf{Y}^{n}\|_{\ell_{2}}
when
\lambda\leq\sqrt{6}
. This proves the theorem when (
3.6
) holds.

If
r=3\ (\mbox{\rm mod}\,4)
, we assume
r=4s+3
,
s\geq 0
. Then
\beta_{r+k}\not=0
only when
k
is even, that is,
k=2q
for some
q\geq 0
. This yields

\displaystyle(\mathbf{Y}_{r}^{\prime}(t),\mathbf{Y}_{r}(t))

\displaystyle=\sum_{q=0}^{2s+1}(-1)^{q+1}\frac{1}{r!(2q)!}\|\mathbb{D}^{2s+q+2}\mathbf{Y}^{n}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}(t-t_{n})^{r+2q}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=0}^{s}\Big(-\frac{1}{r!(4j)!}+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{r!(4j+2)!}\Big)\|\mathbb{D}^{2s+4j+2}\mathbf{Y}^{n}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}(t-t_{n})^{r+4j}.

This implies
(\mathbf{Y}_{r}^{\prime}(t),\mathbf{Y}_{r}(t))\leq 0
if
\lambda^{2}\leq(4j+2)(4j+1)\ \ \forall j=0,\ldots s
. This proves the theorem when (
3.9
) holds.

If
r=1,2\ (\mbox{\rm mod}\,4)
, by using (
2.53
), we have

\displaystyle(\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{r}^{\prime}(t),\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{r}(t))=

\displaystyle(\mathbb{D}(\gamma_{r}^{r}\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{r-1}(t)+(1-\gamma_{r}^{r})\mathbf{Y}_{r-1}(t)),\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{r}(t))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathbb{D}(\gamma_{r}^{r}\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{r-1}(t)+(1-\gamma_{r}^{r})\mathbf{Y}_{r-1}(t)-\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{r}(t)),\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{r}(t))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1-\gamma}{r!(r+\gamma)}\mathbb{D}^{r+1}\mathbf{Y}^{n}(t-t_{n})^{r}-\frac{1}{r!(r+\gamma)}\mathbb{D}^{r+2}\mathbf{Y}^{n}(t-t_{n})^{r+1},\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{r}(t)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{r+2}\delta_{r+k}(t-t_{n})^{r+k},

where

\displaystyle\delta_{r+k}=(-1)^{r+1}\Big(\frac{(k+1-\gamma)}{r!k!(r+\gamma)}\mathbf{Y}^{n},\mathbb{D}^{r+k+1}\mathbf{Y}^{n}\Big),\quad k=0,\ldots,r,

\displaystyle\delta_{2r+1}=\frac{r+1}{(r+\gamma)^{2}(r!)^{2}}\|\mathbb{D}^{r+1}\mathbf{Y}^{n}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2},\quad\delta_{2r+2}=0.

It is clear that for
k=1,\cdots,r
,
\delta_{r+k}=0
if
r+k+1
is odd since
\mathbb{D}+\mathbb{D}^{T}=\mathbb{O}
.

Hence, if
r=1\ (\mbox{\rm mod}\,4)
, we assume
r=4s+1
,
s\geq 0
, then
\delta_{r+k}\not=0
when
k=2q
,
q\geq 0
. We have

\displaystyle(\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{r}^{\prime}(t),\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{r}(t))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{q=0}^{2s}(-1)^{q+1}\frac{2q+1-\gamma}{(r+\gamma)r!(2q)!}\|D^{2s+q+1}\mathbf{Y}^{n}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}(t-t_{n})^{r+2q}

\displaystyle+\frac{r+1}{(r+\gamma)^{2}(r!)^{2}}\|\mathbb{D}^{r+1}\mathbf{Y}^{n}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}(t-t_{n})^{2r+1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}\left[-\frac{4j+1-\gamma}{(r+\gamma)r!(4j)!}+\frac{4j+3-\gamma}{(r+\gamma)r!(4j+2)!}\lambda^{2}\right]\|\mathbb{D}^{2s+2j+1}\mathbf{Y}^{n}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}(t-t_{n})^{r+4j}

\displaystyle+\left[-\frac{r(r-\gamma)}{(r+\gamma)(r!)^{2}}+\frac{r+1}{(r+\gamma)^{2}(r!)^{2}}\lambda^{2}\right]\|\mathbb{D}^{r}\mathbf{Y}^{n}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}(t-t_{n})^{2r-1}.

This implies
(\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{r}^{\prime}(t),\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{r}(t))\leq 0
if

\displaystyle\lambda^{2}\leq\frac{(4j+1-\gamma)(4j+1)(4j+2)}{4j+3-\gamma},\ \ j=0,\ldots,s-1,\text{ and }\lambda^{2}\leq\frac{r(r^{2}-\gamma^{2})}{r+1}.

This proves the theorem when (
3.7
) holds.

If
r=2\ (\mbox{\rm mod}\,4)
, we assume
r=4s+2
,
s\geq 0
, then
\delta_{r+k}\not=0
when
k=2q+1
for some
q\geq 0
. We have

\displaystyle(\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{r}^{\prime}(t),\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{r}(t))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{q=0}^{2s}(-1)^{q+1}\frac{(2q+2-\gamma)}{(r+\gamma)r!(2q+1)!}\|\mathbb{D}^{2s+q+2}\mathbf{Y}^{n}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}(t-t_{n})^{r+2q+1}

\displaystyle+\frac{(r+1)}{(r+\gamma)^{2}(r!)^{2}}\|\mathbb{D}^{r+1}\mathbf{Y}^{n}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}(t-t_{n})^{2r+1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}\left[-\frac{4j+2-\gamma}{(r+\gamma)r!(4j+1)!}+\frac{4j+4-\gamma}{(r+\gamma)r!(4j+3)!}\lambda^{2}\right]\|\mathbb{D}^{2s+2j+2}\mathbf{Y}^{n}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}(t-t_{n})^{r+4j+1}

\displaystyle+\left[-\frac{r(r-\gamma)}{(r+\gamma)(r!)^{2}}+\frac{r+1}{(r+\gamma)^{2}(r!)^{2}}\lambda^{2}\right]\|\mathbb{D}^{r}\mathbf{Y}^{n}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}(t-t_{n})^{2r-1}.

This implies
(\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{r}^{\prime}(t),\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{r}(t))\leq 0
if

\displaystyle\lambda^{2}\leq\frac{(4j+2-\gamma)(4j+2)(4j+3)}{4j+4-\gamma},\ \ j=0,\ldots,s-1,\text{ and }\lambda^{2}\leq\frac{r(r^{2}-\gamma^{2})}{r+1}.

This proves the theorem when (
3.8
) holds.
∎

Remark 3.1
.

The strong stability in Theorem
3.1
is obtained thanks to the important relationship of the spatial discretization operators
D^{-}
and
D^{+}
in (
2.48
). The energy conserving mixed finite element methods for solving the Hodge wave equation in Wu and Bai
[
40
]
all satisfy this relation and thus the explicit time stepping method proposed in this section can be used to solve the ODE systems resulting from their mixed finite element methods.

Remark 3.2
.

A careful check of the proof of Theorem
3.1
shows that when
r=2\,({\rm mod}\,4)
, the strong stability holds for any
\gamma\in(0,2)
. In particular, if
\gamma=1
, then
\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{r}=\mathbf{Y}_{r+1}
by (
3.4
). However, since
\lambda(r,\gamma)
increases as
\gamma
decreases, the choice
\gamma\in(0,1)
is more favorable than the choice
\gamma\in[1,2)
. This is the reason that we also impose
\gamma\in(0,1)
in Algorithm
3.1
when
r=2\,({\rm mod}\,4)
, which is the same as the case when
r=1\,({\rm mod}\,4)
. In practical computations, one can choose
\gamma=0.1
.

Remark 3.3
.

For the cases of
r=0,3\,({\rm mod}\,4)
, our time discretization without source terms at nodes is equivalent to the standard
r
stage
r
order explicit RK method whose strong stability is proved in
[
38
, Theorems 4.2 and 4.5]
. Our Theorem
3.1
improves the results in
[
38
]
in that we prove the strong stability in the whole time interval
(0,T)
instead of only at the times
t=t_{n},1\leq n\leq N
. Moreover, we also provide explicit upper bounds for the CFL conditions.

As a direct corollary, we obtain the following strong stability results with the source term.

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
\mathbf{R}\in C^{r}([0,T];\ell_{2})
. Under the CFL condition
\lambda=\tau\|\mathbb{D}\|_{\ell_{2}}\leq\lambda(r,\gamma)
, the solution
\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{r}
of Algorithm
3.1
satisfies

\displaystyle\max_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{r}\|_{\ell_{2}}\leq\|\mathbf{Y}^{0}\|_{\ell_{2}}+CT\|\mathbf{R}\|_{C^{r}([0,T];\ell_{2})},

where the constant
C
is independent of
\tau
and
r
.

Proof.

We only prove the case when
r=1,2\,(\mbox{\rm mod}\,4)
. By (
3.4
) and Theorem
3.1
, we have
for
t\in[t_{n},t_{n+1}]
,
0\leq n\leq N-1
,

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{r}\|_{\ell_{2}}\leq

\displaystyle\,\left\|\sum_{j=0}^{r}\frac{1}{j!}\,\mathbb{D}^{j}\mathbf{Y}^{n}(t-t_{n})^{j}+\frac{1}{r!(r+\gamma)}\mathbb{D}^{r+1}\mathbf{Y}^{n}(t-t_{n})^{r+1}\right\|_{\ell_{2}}+{\rm VI}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{Y}^{n}\|_{\ell_{2}}+{\rm VI},

where

\displaystyle{\rm VI}=

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{j=0}^{r}\sum_{\ell=0}^{j-1}\frac{1}{j!}\mathbb{D}^{j-\ell-1}\mathbf{R}^{(\ell)}(t_{n})(t-t_{n})^{j}+\sum_{\ell=0}^{r}\frac{1}{r!(r+\gamma)}\mathbb{D}^{r-\ell}\mathbf{R}^{(\ell)}(t_{n})(t-t_{n})^{r+1}\right\|_{\ell_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{R}\|_{C^{r}([t_{n},t_{n+1}];\ell_{2})}\left(\sum^{r}_{j=0}\frac{\tau^{j}}{j!}\sum^{j-1}_{\ell=0}\|\mathbb{D}\|^{j-\ell-1}_{\ell_{2}}+\sum^{r}_{j=0}\frac{\tau^{r+1}}{r!(r+\gamma)}\|\mathbb{D}\|_{\ell_{2}}^{r-\ell}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{r+1}{r+\gamma}\tau\|\mathbf{R}\|_{C^{r}([t_{n},t_{n+1}];\ell_{2})}\sum^{r+1}_{j=0}\frac{1}{j!}\sum^{j-1}_{\ell=0}\lambda(r,\gamma)^{j-\ell-1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\tau\|\mathbf{R}\|_{C^{r}([t_{n},t_{n+1}];\ell_{2})},

where the constant
C
depends only on
\lambda(r,\gamma)
whose upper bound is independent of
r
by Theorem
3.1
. This completes the proof.
∎

3.2
Error estimates

In this subsection, we give the error estimates of our time discretization method.

Theorem 3.2
.

Assume that the CFL condition
\tau\|\mathbb{D}\|_{\ell_{2}}\leq\lambda(r,\gamma)
is satisfied. Let
\mathbf{Y}(t)\in C^{r+1}([0,T];\ell_{2})
be the exact solution of the ODE systems (
3.1
), then we have

\displaystyle\max_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\mathbf{Y}-\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{r}\|_{\ell_{2}}\leq CT\frac{\tau^{r}}{(r+1)!}\|\mathbf{Y}^{(r+1)}\|_{C([0,T];\ell_{2})},

where the constant
C
is independent of
\tau
and
r
.

Proof.

We only prove the case when
r=1,2\,(\rm{mod}\,4)
. We first recall the following well-known formula for the Taylor expansion which can be easily proved by integration by parts

\displaystyle\mathbf{Y}(t)=\sum^{r}_{j=0}\frac{1}{j!}\mathbf{Y}^{(j)}(t_{n})(t-t_{n})^{j}+\frac{1}{r!}\int^{t}_{t_{n}}(t-s)^{r}\mathbf{Y}^{(r+1)}(s)ds\ \ \ \ \forall t\in(0,T).

(3.10)

We define

\displaystyle\Pi_{r}\mathbf{Y}(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{r}\frac{1}{j!}\mathbf{Y}^{(j)}(t_{n})(t-t_{n})^{j}+\frac{1}{r!(r+\gamma)}\mathbf{Y}^{(r+1)}(t_{n})(t-t_{n})^{r+1},

then by (
3.10
)

\displaystyle\max_{t_{n}\leq t\leq t_{n+1}}\|\Pi_{r}\mathbf{Y}-\mathbf{Y}\|_{\ell_{2}}\leq(2+r^{-1})\frac{\tau^{r+1}}{(r+1)!}\|\mathbf{Y}^{(r+1)}\|_{C([t_{n},t_{n+1}];\ell_{2})}.

(3.11)

By (
3.4
) and (
3.2
), we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{r}-\Pi_{r}\mathbf{Y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{r}\frac{1}{j!}\,\mathbb{D}^{j}(\mathbf{Y}^{n}-\mathbf{Y}(t_{n}))(t-t_{n})^{j}

(3.12)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{r!(r+\gamma)}\mathbb{D}^{r+1}(\mathbf{Y}^{n}-\mathbf{Y}(t_{n}))(t-t_{n})^{r+1}.

From the proof of Theorem
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle\max_{t_{n}\leq t\leq t_{n+1}}\|\Pi_{r}\mathbf{Y}-\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{r}\|_{\ell_{2}}\leq\|\mathbf{Y}^{n}-\mathbf{Y}(t_{n})\|_{\ell_{2}}=\|\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{r}(t_{n})-\mathbf{Y}(t_{n})\|_{\ell_{2}}.

(3.13)

By the triangle inequality and (
3.11
)

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{r}(t_{n+1})-\mathbf{Y}(t_{n+1})\|_{\ell_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{r}(t_{n+1})-\Pi_{r}\mathbf{Y}(t_{n+1})\|_{\ell_{2}}+\|\mathbf{Y}(t_{n+1})-\Pi_{r}\mathbf{Y}(t_{n+1})\|_{\ell_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{r}(t_{n})-\mathbf{Y}(t_{n})\|_{\ell_{2}}+\frac{3\tau^{r+1}}{(r+1)!}\|\mathbf{Y}^{(r+1)}\|_{C([0,T];\ell_{2})}.

This yields, since
\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{r}(t_{0})=\mathbf{Y}(t_{0})
,

\displaystyle\max_{1\leq n\leq N}\|\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{r}(t_{n})-\mathbf{Y}(t_{n})\|_{\ell_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 3n\frac{\tau^{r+1}}{(r+1)!}\|\mathbf{Y}^{(r+1)}\|_{C([0,T];\ell_{2})}.

This togethers with (
3.11
) and (
3.13
) completes the proof.
∎

To conclude this section, we remark that if
\mathbf{Y}_{h}\in C^{r+1}([0,T];\ell_{2})
satisfies

\displaystyle\mathbf{Y}_{h}^{\prime}=\mathbb{D}\mathbf{Y}_{h}+\mathbf{R}+\mathbf{R}_{h}\ \ \mbox{in }(0,T),\ \ \ \ \mathbf{Y}_{h}(0)=\mathbf{Y}^{0},

(3.14)

where
\mathbf{R}_{h}
is the error due to some spatial discretization. Let
\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}^{h}_{r}\in{\bf V}_{r+1}(0,T)
such that
\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}^{h}_{r}(0)=\mathbf{Y}^{0}
, and in each time interval
(t_{n},t_{n+1})
,
0\leq n\leq N-1
,
\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}^{h}_{r}
is computed by Algorithm
3.1
with
\mathbf{Y}^{n}=\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}^{h}_{r}(t_{n})
and the source
\mathbf{R}+\mathbf{R}_{h}\in\mathbb{R}^{M}
. Then by Theorem
3.2
and Corollary
3.1
we have

\displaystyle\max_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\mathbf{Y}_{h}-\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{r}\|_{\ell_{2}}

(3.15)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max_{0\leq t\leq T}(\|\mathbf{Y}_{h}-\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}^{h}_{r}\|_{\ell_{2}}+\|\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}^{h}_{r}-\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{r}\|_{\ell_{2}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle CT\frac{\tau^{r}}{(r+1)!}\|\mathbf{Y}^{(r+1)}_{h}\|_{C([0,T];\ell_{2})}+CT\|\mathbf{R}_{h}\|_{C^{r}([0,T];\ell_{2})}.

This estimate will be used in section
4
.

3.3
Implementation of the time discretization method

In this subsection, we propose a recursive implementation of Algorithm
3.1
in each time interval
(t_{n},t_{n+1})
,

0\leq n\leq N-1
. Let
\{L_{j}\}^{\infty}_{j=0}
be the standard Legendre polynomials
on the interval
(-1,1)
. Denote
\psi:(-1,1)\to(t_{n},t_{n+1})
the affine transform
\psi(\xi)=\frac{t_{n}+t_{n+1}}{2}+\frac{t_{n+1}-t_{n}}{2}\xi\ \ \forall\xi\in(-1,1)
,
then
\{\widetilde{L}_{j}\}^{\infty}_{j=0}
,
\widetilde{L}_{j}=L_{j}\circ\psi^{-1}
, defines a complete orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(t_{n},t_{n+1})
. It follows from the standard identity for Legendre polynomials, cf., e.g.,
[
7
]
,

\displaystyle\int^{1}_{-1}L_{k}^{\prime}(\xi)L^{\prime}_{j}(\xi)(1-\xi^{2})d\xi=\frac{k(k+1)}{k+\frac{1}{2}}\delta_{k,j}

that

\displaystyle\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\widetilde{L}^{\prime}_{k}(t)\widetilde{L}^{\prime}_{j}(t)(t-t_{n})(t_{n+1}-t)\,dt=\frac{\tau_{n}}{2}\frac{k(k+1)}{k+\frac{1}{2}}\delta_{k,j},

(3.16)

where
\delta_{k,j}
is the Kronecker delta function. We will also use the recursion relation
(2k+1)L_{k}=L_{k+1}^{\prime}-L_{k-1}^{\prime}
, which implies

\displaystyle(2k+1)\widetilde{L}_{k}=\frac{\tau_{n}}{2}(\widetilde{L}_{k+1}^{\prime}-\widetilde{L}_{k-1}^{\prime}).

(3.17)

We assume

\displaystyle\mathbf{Y}_{m}(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{r_{m}}\mathbf{a}_{j}^{m}\widetilde{L}_{j}(t),\quad\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{m}(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{\tilde{r}_{m}}\tilde{\mathbf{a}}_{j}^{m}\widetilde{L}_{j}(t),

(3.18)

\displaystyle I_{m-1}\mathbf{R}(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\mathbf{R}_{j}^{m}\widetilde{L}_{j}(t),\quad\tilde{I}_{m-1}\mathbf{R}(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\tilde{\mathbf{R}}_{j}^{m}\widetilde{L}_{j}(t),

(3.19)

where
0\leq m\leq r
,

, and

Appendix: The Assumption (H2)

In this section we show that the induced mesh obtained by the merging algorithm developed in
[
14
, Algorithm 6]
satisfies the assumption (H2). The merging algorithm is based on the concept of the admissible chain of interface elements, the classification of patterns for merging elements, and appropriate ordering in generating macro-elements from the patterns so that the reliability of the algorithm can be proved. In the following, we first recall the concept of the admissible chain and five types of patterns of merging elements in
[
14
]
and then show that any algorithm generating macro-elements from the admissible chain of interface elements by using the patterns will output an induced mesh which satisfies the Assumption (H2).

Since the interface intersects the boundary of
K
twice at different sides (including the end points), there are only four possible ways for the interface to intersects the element as shown in Fig.

\mathcal{T}_{1}
the set of interface elements shown in Fig.

(a),
\mathcal{T}_{2}
the set of interface elements shown in Fig.

\mathcal{T}_{3}
the set of interface elements shown in Fig.
6.1
(d). By Definition
2.1
, each element in
\mathcal{T}_{3}
is a large element. Thus we only need to consider the merging of type
\mathcal{T}_{1}
and

A chain of interface elements
\mathfrak{C}=\{G_{1}\rightarrow G_{2}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow G_{n}\}
orderly consists of
n

,

and denote
\mathfrak{C}\{i\}=G_{i}
,

For any element

, that is,
\mathcal{S}(K)_{j}
is the set of all
k

elements surrounding
K
,

. Obviously,
\mathcal{S}(K)_{0}\subset\mathcal{S}(K)_{1}\subset\cdots\subset\mathcal{S}(K)_{j}
for any

. The following concept is introduced in
[
14

Definition 6.1
.

A chain of interface elements
\mathfrak{C}
is called admissible if the following rules are satisfied.

1.

For any
K\in\mathfrak{C}
, all elements in
\mathcal{S}(K)_{2}
have the same size as
that of
K
.

2.

If

has a side

must be a side of some neighboring element

,

.

Any elements

\mathfrak{C}
.

4.

For any
K\subset\Omega_{i}
, the interface elements in
\mathcal{S}(K)_{j}
,

set
\cup\{\bar{G}:G\in\mathcal{S}(K)_{j}\cap\mathcal{T}^{\Gamma}\}

The four rules in the definition can be easily satisfied if the mesh is well refined near the interface. The guideline for introducing the rules is to have enough non-interface elements in the vicinity of each interface element so that the merging algorithm is successful. We refer to
[
14
]
for further details on the properties of the rules.

A pattern is a set of interface elements and their neighboring and diagonal elements whose union consists of a macro-element. We introduce five types of patterns according to the combination of different types of interface elements, see Fig.

\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2}
.

are large. It can consist of

if

is small but
|e_{2}|
is large. It can consist of all
9

are small. One can find the precise definition in
[
14

Theorem 6.1

or

; or (2)

Let
\mathcal{S}(\Gamma)_{0}=\mathcal{T}^{\Gamma}

j

\Gamma

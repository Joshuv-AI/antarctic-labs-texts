Ladyzhenskaya-Prodi-Serrin condition for fluid-structure interaction systems
Dominic Breit, Prince Romeo Mensah, Sebastian Schwarzacher, Pei Su

Abstract
. We consider the interaction of a viscous incompressible fluid with a flexible shell in three space dimensions. The fluid is described by the three-dimensional incompressible Navier–Stokes equations in a domain that is changing in accordance with the motion of the structure. The displacement of the latter evolves along a visco-elastic shell equation. Both are coupled through kinematic boundary conditions and the balance of forces. We prove a counterpart of the classical Ladyzhenskaya-Prodi-Serrin condition yielding conditional regularity and uniqueness of a solution. Our result is a consequence of the following three ingredients which might be of independent interest: (i) the existence of local strong solutions, (ii) an acceleration estimate (under the Serrin assumption) ultimately controlling the second-order energy norm, and (iii) a weak-strong uniqueness theorem. The first point, and to some extent, the last point were previously known for the case of elastic plates, which means that the relaxed state is flat. We extend these results to the case of visco-elastic shells, which means that more general reference geometries are considered such as cylinders or spheres. The second point, i.e. the acceleration estimate for three-dimensional fluids is new even in the case of plates.

Analysis of fluid-structure interactions

For weak solutions, a semi-group approach is used in
[

The existence of a weak solution is also shown in

The seminal work

The authors in

study the interaction of an incompressible Newtonian fluid with a linearly elastic Koiter shell. Here, the fluid’s boundary is described by the mid-section of the shell and the authors show the existence of weak solutions, without self-intersections of the shell, using an Aubin–Lions type argument. Eventually, an existence result for the fully nonlinear Koiter shell model has been proved in

When it comes to strong solutions, short time existence and uniqueness of solutions in Sobolev spaces are studied in

Another local-in-time strong existence result is

1.2.
The fluid-structure interaction problem

We are interested in the interaction of an incompressible fluid with a flexible shell where the shell reacts to the surface forces induced by the fluid and deforms the spatial reference domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}

\Omega_{\eta(t)}

[

40

\gamma\Delta_{\mathbf{y}}^{2}\partial_{t}\eta+B^{\prime}\partial_{t}\eta

\alpha\Delta_{\mathbf{y}}^{2}\eta

Remark 1.3

(Stability and convergence of numerical schemes)
.

Related to the weak-strong uniqueness results is a stability estimate (see Section
5
). It comes naturally, as the difference of two solutions is estimated. Hence a further result of this work is that any solution satisfying (
1.6
) and (
1.7
) are actually attractors in the respective uniqueness class.

Stability are of particular importance for numeric applications. Indeed they form the first step in order to show that the difference between a discrete solution and the continuous solution decreases (with a rate), provided that the continuous solution is unique, as was shown for plates in 2D
[
47
,
48
]
.

The formal proof for the regularity of Theorem
1.1
consists in proving an acceleration estimate which combines and extends the results in
[
6
,
22
]
. In
order to appreciate the moving boundary, the correct test function for the momentum equation is – roughly speaking – the material derivative
\partial_{t}\mathbf{v}+{\bf v}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{v}
combined with the test-function
\partial_{t}^{2}\eta
for the structure equation. A key tool is eventually to estimate
\nabla^{2}\mathbf{v}
(as well as
\nabla\pi
) by means of
\partial_{t}\mathbf{v}+{\bf v}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{v}
. This can be done by means of a steady Stokes theory for irregular domains proved in
[
6
]
(applied to the domain
\Omega_{\eta(t)}
for fixed
t
). It strongly requires a boundary with a small local Lipschitz constant and thus (
1.7
) is essentially needed here. Otherwise, such a regularity estimate is not known and probably not even expected.
Furthermore, in order to avoid the appearance of the pressure function, an extension operator from
\omega
to
\Omega_{\eta}
has been used in
[
22
]
which is at the same time solenoidal and satisfies a homogeneous Neumann-type boundary condition. The construction of the latter is only possible for a flat reference domain. Hence, we introduce the pressure function and work with a more common extension operator which does not preserve solenoidability.
The advantage of the latter is that it has the usual regularization property (it “gains” the differentiability which is lost by the trace theorem, see Section
2.5
) different from the solenoidal extensions used in
[
22
]
,
[
35
]
and
[
42
]
.
A major difference between the 2D and 3D cases is that one has to use the full strength of this operator to compensate for the worsening embeddings.

In order to make this argument rigorous, we work with a strong solution to (
1.2
)–(
1.4
).
Thus, we prove the existence of a local strong solution in Theorem

3.1
.
With a strong solution at hand, we can justify the estimates mentioned above. To close the argument (see the proof of Theorem
6.1
), we have to compare weak and strong solutions by means of a weak-strong uniqueness result.
The difficulty of the latter is that one needs to compare two velocity fields which are a priori defined on different (time-changing) domains. Nevertheless,
such a result
has been established very recently in
[
48
]
for linear elastic plates. The key idea is to transform the strong solution into the domain of the weak solution and then estimate their difference. When doing so, the strong solution loses its solenoidal character which must be corrected to avoid the appearance of the pressure function in the weak formulation. In the case of a flat geometry as in
[
48
]
this can be done by an explicit construction, but our situation is more complicated. We thus work with a Bogovkij-operator for moving domains
[
29
]
.
Its properties crucially hinge on the spatial Lipschitz continuity of the moving boundary and thus require
that the weak solution is
L^{\infty}(C^{0,1})
. For details on the criticality of Lipschitz regularity see
[
46
]
, where estimates for the Bogovkij-operator in rough time-dependent domains are studied.

as well. The space
W^{k,p}(I;X)
is defined accordingly.
We use the shorthand
L^{p}_{t}X
for
L^{p}(I;X)
. For instance, we write
L^{p}_{t}W^{1,p}_{\mathbf{x}}
for
L^{p}(I;W^{1,p}(\mathcal{O}))
. Similarly,
W^{k,p}_{t}X
stands for
W^{k,p}(I;X)
.

2.3.
Fractional differentiability and Sobolev mulitpliers

For
p\in[1,\infty)
, the fractional Sobolev space (Sobolev-Slobodeckij space) with differentiability
s>0
with
s\notin\mathbb{N}
will be denoted by
W^{s,p}(\mathcal{O})
. For
s>0
, we write
s=\lfloor s\rfloor+\{s\}
with
\lfloor s\rfloor\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
\{s\}\in(0,1)
.
We denote by
W^{s,p}_{0}(\mathcal{O})
, the closure of the smooth and compactly supported functions in
W^{s,p}(\mathcal{O})
. For
s>\frac{1}{p}
this coincides with the functions vanishing
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
-a.e. on
\partial\mathcal{O}
provided that
\partial\mathcal{O}
is regular enough. We also denote by
W^{-s,p^{\prime}}(\mathcal{O})
, for
s>0
and
p,p^{\prime}\in[1,\infty)
, with
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1
, the dual of
W^{s,p}_{0}(\mathcal{O})
. Similar to the case of unbroken differentiabilities above, we use the shorthand notations
W^{s,p}_{\mathbf{x}}
and
W^{s,p}_{\mathbf{y}}
.
We will denote by
B^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the standard Besov spaces on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with differentiability
s>0
, integrability
p\in[1,\infty]
and fine index
q\in[1,\infty]
. They can be defined (for instance) via Littlewood-Paley decomposition leading to the norm
\|\cdot\|_{B^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
. We refer to
[
45
]
and
[
52
,
53
]
for an extensive description.
For a bounded domain
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the Besov spaces
B^{s}_{p,q}(\mathcal{O})
are defined as the restriction of functions from
B^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, that is

\displaystyle B^{s}_{p,q}(\mathcal{O})

\displaystyle:=\{f|_{\mathcal{O}}:\,f\in B^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\},

\displaystyle\|g\|_{B^{s}_{p,q}(\mathcal{O})}

\displaystyle:=\inf\{\|f\|_{B^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:\,f|_{\mathcal{O}}=g\}.

If
s\notin\mathbb{N}
and
p\in(1,\infty)
we have
B^{s}_{p,p}(\mathcal{O})=W^{s,p}(\mathcal{O})
.

In accordance with
[
39
, Chapter 14]
, the Sobolev multiplier norm is given by

(2.1)

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|_{\mathcal{M}(W^{s,p}(\mathcal{O}))}:=\sup_{\mathbf{u}:\,\|\mathbf{u}\|_{W^{s-1,p}(\mathcal{O})}=1}\|\nabla\varphi\cdot\mathbf{u}\|_{W^{s-1,p}(\mathcal{O})},

where
p\in[1,\infty]
and
s\geq 1
.
The space
\mathcal{M}(W^{s,p}(\mathcal{O}))
of Sobolev multipliers is defined as those objects for which the
\mathcal{M}(W^{s,p}(\mathcal{O}))
-norm is finite. By mathematical induction with respect to
s
, one can prove for Lipschitz-continuous functions
\varphi
that membership to
\mathcal{M}(W^{s,p}(\mathcal{O}))
, in the sense of (
2.1
), implies that

(2.2)

\displaystyle\sup_{w:\,\|w\|_{W^{s,p}(\mathcal{O})}=1}\|\varphi\,w\|_{W^{s,p}(\mathcal{O})}<\infty.

The quantity (
2.2
) also serves as customary definition of the Sobolev multiplier norm in the literature but (
2.1
) is more suitable for our purposes.
Note that in our applications, we always assume that the functions in question are Lipschitz continuous so that the implication above holds true.

Let us finally collect some useful properties of Sobolev multipliers.
By
[
39
, Corollary 14.6.2]
we have

(2.3)

\displaystyle\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{M}(W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\lesssim\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

provided that one of the following conditions holds:

•

p(s-1)<n
and
\phi\in B^{s}_{\varrho,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\varrho\in\big[\frac{n}{s-1},\infty\big]
;

•

p(s-1)=n
and
\phi\in B^{s}_{\varrho,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\varrho\in(p,\infty]
.

Note that the hidden constant in (
2.3
) depends on the
B^{s}_{\varrho,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
-norm of
\phi
.
By
[
39
, Corollary 4.3.8]
, it holds

(2.4)

\displaystyle\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{M}(W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\approx\|\nabla\phi\|_{W^{s-1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

for
p(s-1)>n
.
Finally, we note the following rule about the composition with Sobolev multipliers which is a consequence of
[
39
, Lemma 9.4.1]
. For open sets
\mathcal{O}_{1},\mathcal{O}_{2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
u\in W^{s,p}(\mathcal{O}_{2})
and a Lipschitz continuous function
{\bm{\phi}}:\mathcal{O}_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{2}
with Lipschitz continuous inverse and
{\bm{\phi}}\in\mathcal{M}(W^{s,p}(\mathcal{O}_{1}))
we have

(2.5)

\displaystyle\|u\circ{\bm{\phi}}\|_{W^{s,p}(\mathcal{O}_{1})}\lesssim\|u\|_{W^{s,p}(\mathcal{O}_{2})}

with constant depending on
{\bm{\phi}}
. Using Lipschitz continuity
of
{\bm{\phi}}
and
{\bm{\phi}}^{-1}
, estimate (
2.5
) is obvious for
s\in(0,1]
. The general case can be proved by mathematical induction with respect to
s
.

2.4.
Function spaces on variable domains

The spatial domain
\Omega
is assumed to be an open bounded subset of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
and an outer unit normal
{{\bf n}}
. We assume that

\partial\Omega
can be parametrised by an injective mapping
{{\bm{\varphi}}}\in C^{k}(\omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
for some sufficiently large
k\in\mathbb{N}
. We suppose for all points
\mathbf{y}=(y_{1},y_{2})\in\omega
that the pair of vectors

\partial_{i}{{\bm{\varphi}}}(\mathbf{y})
,
i=1,2,
are linearly independent.
For a point
\mathbf{x}
in the neighborhood
of
\partial\Omega
, we define the functions
\mathbf{y}
and
s
by

\displaystyle\mathbf{y}(\mathbf{x})=\arg\min_{\mathbf{y}\in\omega}|\mathbf{x}-{\bm{\varphi}}(\mathbf{y})|,\quad s(\mathbf{x})=(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}(\mathbf{x}))\cdot{\bf n}(\mathbf{y}(\mathbf{x})).

Moreover, we define the projection
{\bf p}(\mathbf{x})={\bm{\varphi}}(\mathbf{y}(\mathbf{x}))
. We define
L>0
to be the largest number such that
s,\mathbf{y}
and
{\bf p}
are well-defined on
S_{L}
, where

(2.6)

\displaystyle S_{L}=\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\,\mathrm{dist}(\mathbf{x},\partial\Omega)<L\}.

Due to the smoothness of
\partial\Omega
for
L
small enough we have
{\lvert{s(\mathbf{x})}\rvert}=\min_{\mathbf{y}\in\omega}|\mathbf{x}-{\bm{\varphi}}(\mathbf{y})|
for all
\mathbf{x}\in S_{L}
. This implies that
S_{L}=\{s{\bf n}(\mathbf{y})+\mathbf{y}:(s,\mathbf{y})\in(-L,L)\times\omega\}
.
For a given function
\eta:I\times\omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
we parametrise the deformed boundary by

\displaystyle{{\bm{\varphi}}}_{\eta}(t,\mathbf{y})={{\bm{\varphi}}}(\mathbf{y})+\eta(t,\mathbf{y}){{\bf n}}(\mathbf{y}),\quad\,\mathbf{y}\in\omega,\,t\in I.

By possibly decreasing
L
, one easily deduces from this formula that
\Omega_{\eta}
does not degenerate, that is

(2.7)

\displaystyle\partial_{1}{\bm{\varphi}}_{\eta}\times\partial_{2}{\bm{\varphi}}_{\eta}(t,\mathbf{y})\neq 0,\quad{\bf n}(\mathbf{y})\cdot{\bf n}_{\eta(t)}(\mathbf{y})

\displaystyle>0,\quad\,\mathbf{y}\in\omega,\,t\in I,

provided that
\sup_{t}\|\eta\|_{W^{1,\infty}_{\mathbf{y}}}<L
. Here
{\bf n}_{\eta(t)}

is the normal of the domain
\Omega_{\eta(t)}

defined through

(2.8)

\partial\Omega_{\eta(t)}={\{{{{\bm{\varphi}}}(\mathbf{y})+\eta(t,\mathbf{y}){{\bf n}}(\mathbf{y}):\mathbf{y}\in\omega}\}}.

With the abuse of notation we define deformed space-time cylinder
I\times\Omega_{\eta}=\bigcup_{t\in I}{\{{t}\}}\times\Omega_{\eta(t)}\subset\mathbb{R}^{4}
.
The corresponding function spaces for variable domains are defined as follows.

Definition 2.1
.

(Function spaces)
For
I=(0,T)
,
T>0
, and
\eta\in C(\overline{I}\times\omega)
with
\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}(I\times\omega)}<L
we define for
1\leq p,r\leq\infty

\displaystyle L^{p}(I;L^{r}(\Omega_{\eta}))

\displaystyle:=\big\{v\in L^{1}(I\times\Omega_{\eta}):\,\,v(t,\cdot)\in L^{r}(\Omega_{\eta(t)})\,\,\text{for a.e. }t,\,\,\|v(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{r}(\Omega_{\eta(t)})}\in L^{p}(I)\big\},

\displaystyle L^{p}(I;W^{1,r}(\Omega_{\eta}))

\displaystyle:=\big\{v\in L^{p}(I;L^{r}(\Omega_{\eta})):\,\,\nabla v\in L^{p}(I;L^{r}(\Omega_{\eta}))\big\}.

In order to establish a relationship between the
fixed domain and the time-dependent domain, we introduce the Hanzawa transform
\bm{\Psi}_{\eta}:\Omega\rightarrow\Omega_{\eta}
defined by

(2.9)

{\bm{\Psi}}_{\eta}(\mathbf{x})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{x})+\big(s(\mathbf{x})+\eta(\mathbf{y}(\mathbf{x}))\phi(s(\mathbf{x}))\big)\mathbf{n}(\mathbf{y}(\mathbf{x}))&\text{if dist}(\mathbf{x},\partial\Omega)<L,\\
\mathbf{x}&\text{elsewhere}.\end{array}\right.

for any
\eta:\omega\rightarrow(-L,L)
. Here
\phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
is such that

\phi\equiv 0
in a neighborhood of
-L
and
\phi\equiv 1
in a neighborhood of
0
. The other variables
\mathbf{p}
,
s
and
\mathbf{n}
are as defined earlier in this Section
2.4
.
Due to the size of
L
, we find that
{\bm{\Psi}}_{\eta}
is a homomorphism such that
{\bm{\Psi}}_{\eta}|_{\Omega\setminus S_{L}}
is the identity. Furthermore,
\bm{\Psi}_{\eta}
together with its inverse
3
3

3

It exists provided that we choose
\phi
such that
|\phi^{\prime}|<L/\alpha
.

\bm{\Psi}_{\eta}^{-1}:\Omega_{\eta}\rightarrow\Omega
possesses the following properties, see
[
6
]
for details. If for some
\alpha,R>0
, we assume that

\displaystyle\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}_{\mathbf{y}}}+\|\zeta\|_{L^{\infty}_{\mathbf{y}}}<\alpha<L\qquad\text{and}\qquad\|\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}\eta\|_{L^{\infty}_{\mathbf{y}}}+\|\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}\zeta\|_{L^{\infty}_{\mathbf{y}}}<R

holds, then for any
s>0
,
\varrho,p\in[1,\infty]
and for any

, we have that

(2.10)

\displaystyle\|\bm{\Psi}_{\eta}\|_{B^{s}_{\varrho,p}(\Omega\cup S_{\alpha})}+\|\bm{\Psi}_{\eta}^{-1}\|_{B^{s}_{\varrho,p}(\Omega\cup S_{\alpha})}\lesssim 1+\|\eta\|_{B^{s}_{\varrho,p}(\omega)},

(2.11)

\displaystyle\|\bm{\Psi}_{\eta}-\bm{\Psi}_{\zeta}\|_{B^{s}_{\varrho,p}(\Omega\cup S_{\alpha})}+\|\bm{\Psi}_{\eta}^{-1}-\bm{\Psi}_{\zeta}^{-1}\|_{B^{s}_{\varrho,p}(\Omega\cup S_{\alpha})}\lesssim\|\eta-\zeta\|_{B^{s}_{\varrho,p}(\omega)}

and

(2.12)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}\bm{\Psi}_{\eta}\|_{B^{s}_{\varrho,p}(\Omega\cup S_{\alpha})}\lesssim\|\partial_{t}\eta\|_{B^{s}_{\varrho,p}(\omega)},\qquad\eta\in W^{1,1}(I;B^{s}_{\varrho,p}(\omega))

holds uniformly in time with the hidden constants depending only on the reference geometry, on

and

.

2.5.
Extension and smooth approximation on variable domains

In this subsection, we construct an extension operator
which extends functions from
\omega
to the moving domain
\Omega_{\eta}
for a given function
\eta
defined on
\omega
. We follow
[
10
, Section 2.3]
.
Since
\Omega
is assumed to be sufficiently smooth, it is well-known that there is an extension operator
\mathscr{F}_{\Omega}
which extends functions from
\partial\Omega
to
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and satisfies

\mathscr{F}_{\Omega}:W^{\sigma,p}(\partial\Omega)\rightarrow W^{\sigma+1/p,p}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),

for all
p\in[1,\infty]
and all
\sigma>0
, as well as
\mathscr{F}_{\Omega}v|_{\partial\Omega}=v
. Now we define
\mathscr{F}_{\eta}
by

(2.13)

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}_{\eta}b=\mathscr{F}_{\Omega}((b{\bf n})\circ{\bm{\varphi}}^{-1})\circ{{\bm{\Psi}}}_{\eta}^{-1},\quad b\in W^{\sigma,p}(\omega),

where
{\bm{\varphi}}
is the function in the parametrization of
\partial\Omega
.
If
\eta
is regular enough,
\mathscr{F}_{\eta}
behaves as a classical extension.
We obtain the following Lemma which is a version of
[
6
, Lemma 2.2]
, but also includes differentiabilities larger than 1.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\sigma>0
and
p\in[1,\infty]
.
Let
\eta\in C^{0,1}(\omega)
with
\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}_{\mathbf{y}}}<\alpha<L
. Suppose further that
\eta\in B^{\sigma+1/p}_{\varrho,p}(\omega)
, where
p
and
\varrho
are related as in (
2.3
) and (
2.4
).
The operator

\mathscr{F}_{\eta}
defined in (
2.13
) satisfies

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}_{\eta}:W^{\sigma,p}(\omega)\rightarrow W^{\sigma+1/p,p}(\Omega\cup S_{\alpha})

and
(\mathscr{F}_{\eta}b)\circ{\bm{\varphi}}_{\eta}=b{\bf n}
on
\omega
for all
b\in W^{\sigma,p}(\omega)
. In particular, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathscr{F}_{\eta}b\|_{W^{\sigma+1/p,p}(\Omega\cup S_{\alpha})}\lesssim\|b\|_{W^{\sigma,p}(\omega)},

where the hidden constant depends only on
\Omega,p,\sigma
,
\|\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}\eta\|_{L^{\infty}_{\mathbf{y}}}
,
\|\eta\|_{B^{\sigma}_{\varrho,p}}
and
L-\alpha
.

Proof.

On account of (
2.10
)
we have
{\bm{\Psi}}_{\eta}^{-1}\in B^{\sigma+1/p}_{\varrho,p}(\Omega\cup S_{\alpha})
as well.
By (
2.3
) and (
2.4
) this implies that
{\bm{\Psi}}_{\eta}^{-1}\in\mathcal{M}(W^{\sigma+1/p,p}(\Omega\cup S_{\alpha}))
. Now
(
2.5
) becomes applicable and we obtain

\displaystyle\|\mathscr{F}_{\eta}b\|_{W^{\sigma+1/p,p}(\Omega\cup S_{\alpha})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\mathscr{F}_{\Omega}((b{\bf n})\circ{\bm{\varphi}}^{-1})\|_{W^{\sigma+1/p,p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|(b{\bf n})\circ{\bm{\varphi}}^{-1})\|_{W^{\sigma,p}(\partial\Omega)}\lesssim\|b\|_{W^{\sigma,p}(\omega)},

which yields the claim.
∎

Finally, we prove the following smooth approximation result. For that we also require a solenoidal extension. The proof has been provided in
[
42
, Proposition 3.3]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

For a given
\eta\in L^{\infty}(I;W^{1,2}(\omega))
with
\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,y}}<\alpha<L
, there are linear operators

\displaystyle\mathscr{K}_{\eta}:L^{1}(\omega)\rightarrow\mathbb{R},\quad{\mathscr{F}}^{\Div}_{\eta}:\{\xi\in L^{1}(I;W^{1,1}(\omega)):\,\mathscr{K}_{\eta}(\xi)=0\}\rightarrow L^{1}(I;W^{1,1}_{\Div}(\Omega\cup S_{\alpha})),

such that the tuple
({\mathscr{F}}^{\Div}_{\eta}(\xi-\mathscr{K}_{\eta}(\xi)),\xi-\mathscr{K}_{\eta}(\xi))
satisfies

\displaystyle{\mathscr{F}}^{\Div}_{\eta}(\xi-\mathscr{K}_{\eta}(\xi))

\displaystyle\in L^{\infty}(I;L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta}))\cap L^{2}(I;W^{1,2}_{\Div}(\Omega_{\eta})),

\displaystyle\xi-\mathscr{K}_{\eta}(\xi)

\displaystyle\in L^{\infty}(I;W^{2,2}(\omega))\cap W^{1,\infty}(I;L^{2}(\omega)),

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}_{\eta}({\mathscr{F}}^{\Div}_{\eta}

\displaystyle(\xi-\mathscr{K}_{\eta}(\xi))=\xi-\mathscr{K}_{\eta}(\xi),

\displaystyle{\mathscr{F}}^{\Div}_{\eta}(\xi-\mathscr{K}_{\eta}

\displaystyle(\xi))(t,x)=0\text{ for }(t,x)\in I\times(\Omega\setminus S_{\alpha})

provided that we have
\xi\in L^{\infty}(I;W^{2,2}(\omega))\cap W^{1,\infty}(I;L^{2}(\omega))
.
In particular, we have the estimates

\displaystyle\|{\mathscr{F}}^{\Div}_{\eta}(\xi-\mathscr{K}_{\eta}(\xi))\|_{L^{q}(I;W^{1,p}(\Omega\cup S_{\alpha}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\xi\|_{L^{q}(I;W^{1,p}(\omega))}+\|\xi\nabla\eta\|_{L^{q}(I;L^{p}(\omega))},

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}{\mathscr{F}}^{\Div}_{\eta}(\xi-\mathscr{K}_{\eta}(\xi))\|_{L^{q}(I;L^{p}(\Omega\cup S_{\alpha}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\partial_{t}\xi\|_{L^{q}(I;L^{p}(\omega))}+\|\xi\partial_{t}\eta\|_{L^{q}(I;L^{p}(\omega))},

\displaystyle\|{\mathscr{F}}^{\Div}_{\eta}(\xi-\mathscr{K}_{\eta}(\xi))\|_{L^{q}(I;W^{2,p}(\Omega\cup S_{\alpha}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\xi\|_{L^{q}(I;W^{2,p}(\omega))}+\|\xi\nabla^{2}\eta\|_{L^{q}(I;L^{p}(\omega))}

\displaystyle\quad+\|{\lvert{\nabla\xi}\rvert}{\lvert{\nabla\eta}\rvert}\|_{L^{q}(I;L^{p}(\omega))}+\|{\lvert{\xi}\rvert}{\lvert{\nabla\eta}\rvert}^{2}\|_{L^{q}(I;L^{p}(\omega))}

\displaystyle\quad+\|\xi\nabla\eta\|_{L^{q}(I;L^{p}(\omega))},

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}{\mathscr{F}}^{\Div}_{\eta}(\xi-\mathscr{K}_{\eta}(\xi))\|_{L^{q}(I;W^{1,p}(\Omega\cup S_{\alpha}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\partial_{t}\xi\|_{L^{q}(I;W^{1,p}(\omega))}+\|\xi\partial_{t}\nabla\eta\|_{L^{q}(I;L^{p}(\omega))}

\displaystyle\quad+\|{\lvert{\partial_{t}\xi}\rvert}{\lvert{\nabla\eta}\rvert}\|_{L^{q}(I;L^{p}(\omega))}+\|{\lvert{\nabla\xi}\rvert}{\lvert{\partial_{t}\eta}\rvert}\|_{L^{q}(I;L^{p}(\omega))}

\displaystyle\quad+\|\xi{\lvert{\partial_{t}\eta}\rvert}{\lvert{\nabla\eta}\rvert}\|_{L^{q}(I;L^{p}(\omega))},

for any
p\in(1,\infty),q\in(1,\infty]
.

With the help of Lemma
2.3
we obtain the following.

Lemma 2.4
.

For any tuple
(\eta,{\bf v})
belonging to the class

(2.14)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &L^{2}\big(I;W^{2,\infty}(\omega)\big)\cap W^{1,2}(I;W^{2,2}(\omega))\times L^{2}\big(I;W^{1,2}_{\Div}(\Omega_{\eta})\big)\cap C^{0}(\overline{I};L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta}))\end{aligned}

and satisfying
{\bf v}\circ{\bm{\varphi}}_{\eta}=\partial_{t}\eta\mathbf{n}
on
\omega
, where
\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,y}}<\alpha<L
there is a sequence
(\eta_{n},{\bf v}_{n})

which belongs to the class (
2.14
) and satisfies additionally

\displaystyle\eta_{n}\in C^{\infty}(I\times\omega),\quad{\bf v}_{n}\in W^{1,2}\big(I;W^{1,2}_{\Div}(\Omega_{\eta})\big),

and
{\bf v}_{n}\circ{\bm{\varphi}}_{\eta}=(\partial_{t}\eta_{n}-\mathscr{K}_{\eta}(\partial_{t}\eta_{n})){\bf n}
on
\omega
, which converges to
(\eta,{\bf v})
strongly and has uniform bounds in the spaces given in (
2.14
).

Proof.

The proof is strongly related to
[
42
, Section 6]
. We define

\displaystyle\tilde{\bf v}_{0}={\bf v}-{\mathscr{F}}^{\Div}_{\eta}

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}\eta{\bf n}),

which we use as decomposition.
Note that
\partial_{t}\eta{\bf n}
is the trace of the divergence free function
{\bf v}
, then it is not difficult to derive that (please refer to
[
42
, Lemma 6.3]
for more details)

\displaystyle\mathscr{K}_{\eta}(\partial_{t}\eta{\bf n})=0.

This implies that
{\mathscr{F}}^{\Div}_{\eta}(\partial_{t}\eta{\bf n})
is well defined. Further
\tilde{\bf v}_{0}
has zero trace on
\partial\Omega_{\eta}
.

Each part can be smoothly approximated. The first part uses
\eta_{n}
as a smooth approximation of
\eta
for instance by convolution in space and time. This also makes
\partial_{t}\eta_{n}
and
\partial_{t}^{2}\eta_{n}
smooth. Thus by Lemma
2.3
we obtain

\displaystyle{\mathscr{F}}^{\Div}_{\eta}

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}\eta_{n}{\bf n})\in W^{1,2}\big(I;W^{1,2}(\Omega_{\eta})\big)\cap L^{2}\big(I;W^{2,2}(\Omega_{\eta})\big)\cap C^{0}(\overline{I};W^{1,2}(\Omega_{\eta})).

Moreover, the tuple
(\eta_{n},{\mathscr{F}}^{\Div}_{\eta}(\partial_{t}\eta_{n}-\mathscr{K}_{\eta}(\partial_{t}\eta_{n})){\bf n}))
converges to the expected limit with respect to the topology from (
2.14
). As was already realized in
[
42
,
48
]
this part of the approximation only uses the regularity of
\eta
related to the energy estimate.

It is for the approximation of
\tilde{\bf v}_{0}
that more regularity has to be assumed for
\eta
. This is where we use the Piola transformation
\mathscr{T}_{\eta}
that changes the support of a function from
\Omega
to
\Omega_{\eta}
without changing the divergence.
It is defined as

\displaystyle\mathscr{T}_{\eta}{\bf w}=\big(\nabla\bm{\Psi}_{\eta}(\mathrm{det}\nabla\bm{\Psi}_{\eta})^{-1}{\bf w}\big)\circ\bm{\Psi}_{\eta}^{-1}\text{ with inverse }\mathscr{T}_{\eta}^{-1}{\bf w}=\big((\nabla\bm{\Psi}_{\eta})^{-1}(\mathrm{det}\nabla\bm{\Psi}_{\eta}){\bf w}\big)\circ\bm{\Psi}_{\eta}.

Please note that the derivatives of
\mathscr{T}_{\eta}{\bf w}
and
\mathscr{T}_{\eta}^{-1}{\bf w}
can naturally be bounded by the respective derivatives of
\eta
. Hence as
\eta\in L^{2}(I,W^{2,\infty}(\omega))\cap W^{1,2}(I;W^{2,2}(\omega))
we find for any
p,q\in[1,\infty]
that

\displaystyle\mathscr{T}_{\eta}:W^{1,2}(I;W^{1,2}_{0,\Div}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\rightarrow W^{1,2}\big(I;W^{1,2}_{0,\Div}(\Omega_{\eta})\big),

\displaystyle\mathscr{T}_{\eta}:L^{p}\big(I;L^{q}(\Omega)\big)

\displaystyle\to L^{p}\big(I;L^{q}(\Omega_{\eta})\big),

\displaystyle\mathscr{T}_{\eta}:L^{2}\big(I;W^{1,2}_{0,\Div}(\Omega)\big)\cap L^{\infty}\big(I;L^{2}(\Omega)\big)

\displaystyle\rightarrow L^{2}\big(I;W^{1,2}_{0,\Div}(\Omega_{\eta}))\cap L^{\infty}\big(I;L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta})\big),

with uniform bounds. The same bounds hold for
\mathscr{T}_{\eta}^{-1}
. Estimates yielding these continuities can be shown by direct computations. Note, for instance, that the first estimate requires that
\nabla\eta\in L^{\infty}(I\times\omega)
.

The approximation is then defined by first considering

\mathscr{T}_{\eta}^{-1}\tilde{\bf v}_{0}\in L^{2}\big(I;W^{1,2}_{0,\Div}(\Omega))\cap W^{1,2}\big(I;L^{2}(\Omega)\big).

This function can now be smoothly approximated by a sequence
(\mathscr{T}_{\eta}^{-1}\tilde{\bf v}_{0})_{n}\subset W^{1,2}(I;C^{\infty}_{c,\Div}(\Omega))
, where
(\mathscr{T}_{\eta}^{-1}\tilde{\bf v}_{0})_{n}\to\mathscr{T}_{\eta}^{-1}\tilde{\bf v}_{0}
almost everywhere as
n\rightarrow\infty
. This can be achieved by cutting-off the boundary, then applying a Bogovskij operator to make the function solenoidal and convoluting in time-space. We remark that all these operations are linear and continuous in the spaces regarded here. Now we fix

\tilde{\bf v}_{0,n}:=\mathscr{T}_{\eta}(\mathscr{T}_{\eta}^{-1}\tilde{\bf v}_{0})_{n}.

Now, by construction we have
\tilde{\bf v}_{0,n}\in W^{1,2}\big(I;W^{1,2}_{\Div}(\Omega_{\eta})\big)
, as required. Furthermore, we find that a.e.

\displaystyle{\lvert{\nabla\tilde{\bf v}_{0,n}}\rvert}\lesssim{\lvert{(\mathscr{T}_{\eta}^{-1}\tilde{\bf v}_{0})_{n}}\rvert}+{\lvert{\nabla(\mathscr{T}_{\eta}^{-1}\tilde{\bf v}_{0})_{n}}\rvert}

with a hidden constant depending on
{\lvert{\nabla^{2}\eta}\rvert}
.
Hence we obtain that

\displaystyle{\lVert{\nabla\tilde{\bf v}_{0,n}}\rVert}_{L^{2}(I,L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim{\lVert{\nabla(\mathscr{T}_{\eta}^{-1}\tilde{\bf v}_{0})_{n}}\rVert}_{L^{2}(I,L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta}))}+{\lVert{(\mathscr{T}_{\eta}^{-1}\tilde{\bf v}_{0})_{n}}\rVert}_{L^{\infty}(I;L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim{\lVert{\tilde{\bf v}_{0}}\rVert}_{L^{2}(I,W^{1,2}(\Omega_{\eta}))}+{\lVert{\tilde{\bf v}_{0}}\rVert}_{L^{\infty}(I;L^{2}(\Omega_{\eta}))},

with a hidden constant depending on
{\lVert{\nabla\eta}\rVert}_{L^{\infty}(I\times\omega)}
and
{\lVert{\nabla^{2}\eta}\rVert}_{L^{2}(I;L^{\infty}(\omega))}
.
Combining the above, the sequence

(\eta_{n},\tilde{\bf v}_{0,n}+{\mathscr{F}}^{\Div}_{\eta}((\partial_{t}\eta_{n}-\mathscr{K}_{\eta}(\partial_{t}\eta_{n})){\bf n}))
has the desired properties.
∎

In this section, we present the necessary framework to parametrise

We assume that

, such that
the following holds. For each

and a local coordinate system

), a function

\varphi_{j}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

and
r_{j}>0

with the following properties:

(A1)

There is
h_{j}>0
such that

\mathcal{U}^{j}=\{\mathbf{x}=\mathcal{Q}_{j}\mathbf{z}+\mathbf{y}^{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:\,\mathbf{z}=(\mathbf{z}^{\prime},z_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\,|\mathbf{z}^{\prime}|<r_{j},\,|z_{3}-\varphi_{j}(\mathbf{z}^{\prime})|<h_{j}\}.

(A2)

For
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{U}^{j}
we have with
\mathbf{z}=\mathcal{Q}_{j}^{\intercal}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}^{j})

•

if and only if

•

\mathbf{x}\in\Omega

•

\mathbf{x}\notin\Omega

(A3)

We have that

\partial\Omega\subset\bigcup_{j=1}^{\ell}\mathcal{U}^{j}.

In other words, for any
\mathbf{x}_{0}\in\partial\Omega
there is a neighborhood
U
of
\mathbf{x}_{0}
and a function
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

4

4

By translation via

is pointing in the negative

U\cap\Omega=U\cap G,\quad G={\{{(\mathbf{x}^{\prime},x_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\,:\,\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{2},x_{3}>\phi(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})}\}}.

The regularity of
\partial\Omega
will be described by means of local coordinates as just described.

Definition 2.7
.

Let

2.8.
Universal Bogovskij

Bogovskij operators are natural to be considered in star-shaped domains. As Lipschitz domains are unions of star-shaped domains, for some time Bogovskij operators are available on Lipschitz domains. Recently the concept of universal Bogovskij operators was introduced in
[
29
]
. Observe that the same Bogovskij operator actually can be used for a family of domains, as long as the Lipschitz constant is controlled. This allows to use a (locally) steady operator to correct the divergence in the time-changing domains.

More precisely in
[
29
, Corollary 3.4]
the following statement was shown:

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
be a bounded Lipschitz-domain,
M>\gamma>0
,
C_{L}>0
,
b\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Sigma\times[0,\gamma])
with unit integral. Then there exists a linear, universal Bogovskij operator
\Bog:C_{0}^{\infty}(\Sigma\times[0,M])\to C_{0}^{\infty}(\Sigma\times[0,M];\mathbb{R}^{n-1})
such that for any
C_{L}
-Lipschitz function (i.e. with Lipschitz constant
C_{L}
)
\eta:\Sigma\to[\gamma,M]
and
\Omega_{\eta}:=\{(\mathbf{x}^{\prime},x_{n})\in\Sigma\times[0,M]:0<x_{n}<\eta(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})\}
the operator
\Bog
maps
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega_{\eta})
to
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega_{\eta};\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\Div\Bog f=f-b\int f\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}
.
In addition,

{\lVert{\Bog(f)}\rVert}_{W^{s+1,p}(\Omega_{\eta};\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C_{B}^{s,p}{\lVert{f}\rVert}_{W^{s,p}(\Omega_{\eta})},

for all
1<p<\infty
and
s\geq 0

with
C_{B}^{s,p}
only depending on
s
,
p
,
\operatorname{diam}(\Sigma),C_{L},\gamma
and the Lipschitz properties of
\Sigma
.

In order to make this operator admissible for our needs we introduce the following version.

Theorem 2.11
.

There is a universal Bogovskij operator, such that for all
\Omega_{\eta}
defined through (
2.8
) with
{\lVert{\nabla\eta}\rVert}_{\infty}\leq C_{L}
,
{\lVert{\eta}\rVert}_{\infty}\leq L
and
b\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega\setminus S_{L})
with unit integral

\Bog:C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega_{\eta})\to C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega_{\eta};\mathbb{R}^{n})\text{ with }\Div\Bog f=f-b\int f\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}.

In addition,

{\lVert{\Bog(f)}\rVert}_{W^{s+1,p}(\Omega_{\eta};\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C_{B}^{s,p}{\lVert{f}\rVert}_{W^{s,p}(\Omega_{\eta})},

for all
1<p<\infty
and
s\geq 0

with
C_{B}^{s,p}
only depending on
L,\varphi,C_{L}
.

Proof.

The proof is by now standard. One covers the domain
S_{L}
with balls of finite overlap, such that on each ball all possible functions
\eta
can be written as a graph. On these sets one may apply Theorem
2.10
. Hence the partition of unity argument introduced in
[
46
, Section 3.1]
allows to construct the desired operator.
∎

Remark 2.12

(Time-derivative of Bogovskij operator)
.

Please observe that the same operator can be applied to a time-changing function with time-changing support. The operator then automatically has zero trace on the variable support of the function. In particular for
\sup_{t}{\lVert{\nabla\eta(t)}\rVert}_{\infty}\leq C_{L}
,
\sup_{t}{\lVert{\eta(t)}\rVert}_{\infty}\leq L
, we find that

\partial_{t}\mathcal{B}(f\chi_{\Omega_{\eta}})=\mathcal{B}(\partial_{t}f\chi_{\Omega_{\eta}})
and
\mathcal{B}(\partial_{t}f\chi_{\Omega_{\eta}})=0
on
\partial\Omega_{\eta}
.

Proposition 3.3

Suppose that the dataset

\displaystyle

The solution can be constructed by a finite-dimensional Galerkin approximation.
Let us explain how to construct the Galerkin basis. By solving eigenvalue problems of the Stokes operator we construct a smooth orthogonal basis
(\tilde{\bf X}_{\ell})_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}

of

. Further by solving eigenvalue problems for the Laplace operator we construct a smooth orthogonal basis

Then we set

\displaystyle\{{\bf w}\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega):\,\,\mathbf{B}_{\eta_{0}}:\nabla{\bf w}=0\}.

is a solenoidal extension operator. It can be constructed by means of a standard extension and a Bogosvkii correction and thus maps

for

such that the
\tilde{\bf Y}_{\ell}
’s are smooth. The functions

{\bf Y}_{\ell}:=\tilde{\bf Y}_{\ell}\circ{\bm{\Psi}}_{\eta_{0}}

3.10
). Now we choose an enumeration

of

. Note that we use
(Y_{\ell})_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}
as the Ansatz space for
\partial_{t}\eta
and not
\eta
, as this is what is related to the fluid-velocity. The terms depending on

are hence taken as primitive of the discretization of
\partial_{t}\eta
.
We now give a formal proof of estimate (

Consider the pair of test functions
(\partial_{t}\eta,\overline{\mathbf{v}})
(which can be used as a test-function on the Galerkin level by construction) for

If we now consider

Based on Proposition

We assume that the triplet

\displaystyle\|(\zeta,\overline{\mathbf{w}},\overline{q})\|_{X_{I_{*}}}^{2}

\displaystyle:=\sup_{I_{*}}\int_{\omega}\big(|\partial_{t}\zeta|^{2}+|\partial_{t}\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}\zeta|^{2}+|\Delta_{\mathbf{y}}\zeta|^{2}+|\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}\Delta_{\mathbf{y}}\zeta|^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{I_{*}}\int_{\omega}\big(|\partial_{t}\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}\zeta|^{2}+|\partial_{t}\Delta_{\mathbf{y}}\zeta|^{2}+|\partial_{t}^{2}\zeta|^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad+\sup_{I_{*}}\int_{\Omega}\big(|\overline{\mathbf{w}}|^{2}+|\nabla\overline{\mathbf{w}}|^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{I_{*}}\int_{\Omega}\big(|\nabla\overline{\mathbf{w}}|^{2}+|\nabla^{2}\overline{\mathbf{w}}|^{2}+|\partial_{t}\overline{\mathbf{w}}|^{2}+|\overline{q}|^{2}+|\nabla\overline{q}|^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\,\mathrm{d}t.

Let
B_{R}^{X_{I_{*}}}
be a ball defined as

\displaystyle B_{R}^{X_{I_{*}}}:=\big\{(\zeta,\overline{\mathbf{w}},\overline{q})\in X_{I_{*}}\text{ with }\quad\zeta(0)=\eta_{0},\quad\partial_{t}\zeta(0)=\eta_{*},\quad\overline{\mathbf{w}}(0)=\overline{\mathbf{v}}_{0}\,:\,\|(\zeta,\overline{\mathbf{w}},\overline{q})\|_{X_{I_{*}}}^{2}\leq R\big\},

for some

large enough.

Theorem 3.4
.

There exists a time
T>0
and
R>0
, such that the map
\mathcal{T}
, defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}:B_{R}^{X_{I_{*}}}

\displaystyle\rightarrow B_{R}^{X_{I_{*}}}

\displaystyle(\zeta,\overline{\mathbf{w}},\overline{q})

\displaystyle\mapsto(\eta,\overline{\mathbf{v}},\overline{\pi}),

is a contraction map, which thereby possesses a fixed point in
X_{I_{*}}
.

Proof.

We would like to show that the map
\mathcal{T}
defined above maps the ball

into itself and that for any
(\zeta_{i},\overline{\mathbf{w}}_{i},\overline{q}_{i})\in B_{R}^{X_{I_{*}}}
, for

such that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{T}(\zeta_{1},\overline{\mathbf{w}}_{1},\overline{q}_{1})-\mathcal{T}(\zeta_{2},\overline{\mathbf{w}}_{2},\overline{q}_{2})\|_{X_{I_{*}}}\leq\rho\|(\zeta_{1},\overline{\mathbf{w}}_{1},\overline{q}_{1})-(\zeta_{2},\overline{\mathbf{w}}_{2},\overline{q}_{2})\|_{X_{I_{*}}}.

To present the proof clearly, we divide it in the following two steps.

Step 1:

We show that
\mathcal{T}:B_{R}^{X_{I_{*}}}\rightarrow B_{R}^{X_{I_{*}}}
, i.e. the ball

is
\mathcal{T}
-invariant.

To do this, we need to show that for any

, we have

(3.23)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{T}(\zeta,\overline{\mathbf{w}},\overline{q})\|_{X_{I_{*}}}^{2}=\|(\eta,\overline{\mathbf{v}},\overline{\pi})\|_{X_{I_{*}}}^{2}\leq R.

Indeed, according to (
3.9
), we deduce the following estimate from (
3.20
)–(
3.22
)

(3.24)

\displaystyle\|(\eta,\overline{\mathbf{v}},\overline{\pi})\|_{X_{I_{*}}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathcal{D}(g,\eta_{0},\eta_{*},\overline{\mathbf{v}}_{0})+\int_{I_{*}}\big(\|\partial_{t}h_{\zeta}(\overline{\mathbf{w}})\|_{W^{-1,2}(\Omega)}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t+\|h_{\zeta}(\overline{\mathbf{w}})\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega)}^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{I_{*}}\big(\|\mathbf{h}_{\zeta}(\overline{\mathbf{w}})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\mathbf{H}_{\zeta}(\overline{\mathbf{w}},\overline{q})\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega)}^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=:\mathcal{D}(g,\eta_{0},\eta_{*},\overline{\mathbf{v}}_{0})+\overline{K}_{1}+\overline{K}_{2}+\overline{K}_{3}+\overline{K}_{4},

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(g,\eta_{0},\eta_{*},\overline{\mathbf{v}}_{0})

\displaystyle=\|\eta_{*}\|_{W^{1,2}(\omega)}^{2}+\|\eta_{0}\|_{W^{3,2}(\omega)}^{2}+\|\overline{\mathbf{v}}_{0}\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\int_{I_{*}}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t.

Recalling the regularity assumption in (

), we choose

). We show in what follows that the sum of the

).

To estimate

, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}h_{\zeta}(\overline{\mathbf{w}})

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{B}_{\eta_{0}}-\mathbf{B}_{\zeta}):\partial_{t}\nabla\overline{\mathbf{w}}-(\partial_{t}\mathbf{B}_{\zeta}):\nabla\overline{\mathbf{w}}.

(3.26)

\displaystyle L^{\infty}(I_{*};W^{3,2}(\omega))\cap W^{1,2}(I_{*};W^{2,2}(\omega))\hookrightarrow C^{0,1/8}(I_{*};W^{11/4,2}(\omega))\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(I_{*};W^{1,\infty}(\omega)),

.

)–(

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{I_{*}}\|\mathbf{B}_{\eta_{0}}-\mathbf{B}_{\zeta}\|_{L^{\infty}_{\mathbf{x}}}^{2}\|\partial_{t}\overline{\mathbf{w}}\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbf{x}}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{I_{*}}\|\eta_{0}-\zeta\|_{W^{1,\infty}_{\mathbf{y}}}^{2}\int_{I_{*}}\|\partial_{t}\overline{\mathbf{w}}\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbf{x}}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t

On the other hand, due to the continuous embedding

Combining (

) with (

), we have

\displaystyle\overline{K}_{1}\lesssim T^{1/2}_{*}\|(\zeta,\overline{\mathbf{w}},\overline{q})\|_{X_{I_{*}}}^{2}.

To estimate

) again,

)–(

\displaystyle

4.
The acceleration estimate

In this section, we prove the acceleration estimate for a solution satisfying the Serrin condition. In order to make the proof rigorous, we work with a strong solution, the existence of which is guaranteed locally in time by Theorem
3.1
. Eventually, we compare weak and strong solution by means of Theorem
5.1
and compare the result for a weak solution in Theorem
6.1
.
Let
(\mathbf{v},\eta)
be a strong solution (
1.2
)–(
1.4
) in the sense of Definition
2.6
with data
({\bf f},g,\eta_{0},\eta_{*},\mathbf{v}_{0})
, which is in particular a weak solution (see Definition

) and thus satisfies the standard energy estimates

(4.1)

\displaystyle\sup_{I^{\ast}}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbf{x}}}^{2}+\int_{I^{\ast}}\|\nabla\mathbf{v}\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbf{x}}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\lesssim\,C_{0},

(4.2)

\displaystyle\sup_{I^{\ast}}\|\partial_{t}\eta\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbf{y}}}^{2}+\sup_{I^{\ast}}\|\Delta_{\mathbf{y}}\eta\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbf{y}}}^{2}+\int_{I^{\ast}}\|\partial_{t}\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}\eta\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbf{y}}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\lesssim\,C_{0},

where

\displaystyle C_{0}

\displaystyle:=\|\mathbf{v}_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbf{x}}}^{2}+\|\eta_{\ast}\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbf{y}}}^{2}+\|\Delta_{\mathbf{y}}\eta_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbf{y}}}^{2}+\int_{I^{\ast}}\|{\bf f}\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbf{x}}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{I^{\ast}}\|g\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbf{y}}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t.

The following acceleration estimate (or second order energy estimate) is one of the core results of the paper and directly leads to the main result in Theorem

. Under the Serrin condition, it holds uniformly in time allowing us to extend the local solution globally in time.

Theorem 4.1
.

Suppose that the dataset

({\bf f},g,\eta_{0},\eta_{*},\mathbf{v}_{0})

satisfies (
2.15
) and
(
3.1
).
Suppose that
(\eta,\mathbf{v})
is a strong solution to (

)–(
1.4
) in the sense of Definition
2.6
.
Furthermore, for some

and

we set

\displaystyle C_{1}

\displaystyle:=\|\mathbf{v}\|_{L^{r}(I;L^{s}(\Omega_{\eta}))},\quad\tfrac{2}{r}+\tfrac{3}{s}\leq 1,

(4.4)

\displaystyle C_{2}

\displaystyle:=\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}(I;C^{1}(\omega))}.

Finally, suppose that there is no degeneracy in the sense of (

).
Then we have the estimate

(4.5)

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\sup_{I_{\ast}}\int_{\omega}\big(|\partial_{t}\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}\eta|^{2}+|\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}\Delta_{\mathbf{y}}\eta|^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y}+\sup_{I_{\ast}}\int_{\Omega_{\eta}}|\nabla\mathbf{v}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle+\int_{I_{\ast}}\int_{\omega}\big(|\partial_{t}\Delta_{\mathbf{y}}\eta|^{2}+|\partial_{t}^{2}\eta|^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y}\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{I_{\ast}}\int_{\Omega_{\eta}}\big(|\nabla^{2}\mathbf{v}|^{2}+|\partial_{t}\mathbf{v}|^{2}+|\nabla\pi|^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{I_{\ast}}\int_{\Omega_{\eta}}|{\bf f}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{I_{\ast}}\int_{\omega}|g|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y}\,\mathrm{d}t,

where the hidden constant depends only on

and

Proof.

We use

{\bm{\phi}}=\partial_{t}\mathbf{v}+\mathscr{F}_{\eta}(\partial_{t}\eta{\bf n})\cdot\nabla\mathbf{v}

and

as test functions for the fluid and shell equations respectively. Here
\mathscr{F}_{\eta}
is the extension operator introduced in Section

. From the momentum equation in the strong form (

), we obtain for

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega_{\eta}}\big(\partial_{t}\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{v}\big)\cdot\big(\partial_{t}\mathbf{v}+\mathscr{F}_{\eta}(\partial_{t}\eta{\bf n})\cdot\nabla\mathbf{v}\big)\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega_{\eta}}\Div{\bm{\tau}}\cdot\big(\partial_{t}\mathbf{v}+\mathscr{F}_{\eta}(\partial_{t}\eta{\bf n})\cdot\nabla\mathbf{v}\big)\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega_{\eta}}{\bf f}\cdot\big(\partial_{t}\mathbf{v}+\mathscr{F}_{\eta}(\partial_{t}\eta{\bf n})\cdot\nabla\mathbf{v})\big)\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma,

where
{\bm{\tau}}=\nabla\mathbf{v}+\nabla\mathbf{v}^{\intercal}-\pi\mathbb{I}_{3\times 3}
is the Cauchy stress.
We now aim at integrating by parts in the first term on the right-hand side
obtaining

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega_{\eta}}\Div{\bm{\tau}}\cdot\big(\partial_{t}\mathbf{v}+\mathscr{F}_{\eta}(\partial_{t}\eta{\bf n})\cdot\nabla\mathbf{v}\big)\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{\eta}}|\nabla\mathbf{v}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}+\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{\eta}}|\nabla\mathbf{v}_{0}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}+\int_{I_{\ast}}\int_{\partial\Omega_{\eta}}(\partial_{t}\eta{\bf n})\circ{\bm{\varphi}}_{\eta}^{-1}\cdot{\bf n}_{\eta}\circ{\bm{\varphi}}_{\eta}^{-1}|\nabla\mathbf{v}|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega_{\eta}}\nabla\mathbf{v}:\nabla\big(\mathscr{F}_{\eta}(\partial_{t}\eta{\bf n})\cdot\nabla\mathbf{v}\big)\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega_{\eta}}\pi\,\Div\big(\mathscr{F}_{\eta}(\partial_{t}\eta{\bf n})\nabla\mathbf{v}\big)\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma

(4.6)

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\omega}{\bf F}\,\partial_{t}^{2}\eta\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma

with

(4.7)

Note that we also used Reynold’s transport theorem (applied to

Although all the terms in equation (

Multiply the structure equation (

It can be made rigorous by means of a spatial regularization argument. Since we consider periodic boundary conditions a spatial convolution can be applied without further difficulty.
Combining both, using Young’s inequality and writing

\displaystyle\int_{I_{\ast}}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\eta}}|\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{v}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\,\mathrm{d}t\lesssim\int_{I_{\ast}}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{L^{4}_{\mathbf{x}}}^{2}\|\nabla\mathbf{v}\|_{L^{4}_{\mathbf{x}}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\lesssim\sup_{I_{\ast}}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{W^{1,2}_{\mathbf{x}}}^{2}\int_{I_{\ast}}\|\nabla\mathbf{v}\|_{W^{1,2}_{\mathbf{x}}}^{2}\leq c,

which completes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

We remark here that the conditions we proposed in Theorem

is the minimal assumption for the conditional strong solution for the fluid-structure system (

)–(
1.4

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\mathbf{v}_{1}-\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{2}\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega_{\eta_{1}})}\lVert\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{2}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega_{\eta_{1}})}\lVert(\partial_{t}\eta_{1}-\partial_{t}\eta_{2}){\bf n}_{\eta_{1}}\circ{\bm{\varphi}}_{\eta_{1}}^{-1}\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega_{\eta_{1}})}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma

Putting all the estimates together and taking the supremum with respect to time on both sides of (

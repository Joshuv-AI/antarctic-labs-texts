Local well-posedness of Dirac equations with nonlinearity derived from honeycomb structure in 2 dimensions Thanks: 2010 Mathematics Subject Classification. 35Q55, 35Q40. Thanks: Key words and phrases. Dirac equations, Honeycomb lattice, local well-posedness, non-smoothness, Bourgain’s space.
Kiyeon Lee

Abstract
. The aim of this paper is to show local well-posedness of 2 dimensional Dirac equations with power type and Hartree type nonlinearity derived from honeycomb structure in H s H^{s} for s > 7 8 s>\frac{7}{8} and s > 3 8 s>\frac{3}{8} , respectively. We also provide the smoothness failure of flows of Dirac equations.



1.
Introduction

In this paper we consider following two Cauchy problems for massless honeycomb lattice power type Dirac equations(
\ell=1
) and Hartree type Dirac equations
(\ell=2)
:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}(\partial_{t}+{\alpha}\cdot D)\psi=-i\kappa\mathcal{N}_{\ell}(\psi,\psi)\psi\\
\psi(0)=\psi_{0}\end{array}\right.

where
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{1+2}\to\mathbb{C}^{2}
is the spinor field represented by a column vector,
{\kappa}
is constant,
D=-i\nabla
, and
{\alpha}=(P_{\#}{\alpha}^{1},P_{\#}{\alpha}^{2})
are the Dirac matrices defined by

\displaystyle\alpha^{1}

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}0&\;\;1\\
1&\;\;0\end{array}\right),\qquad\alpha^{2}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}0&-i\\
i&\;\;0\end{array}\right),

with
P_{\#}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\overline{\eta_{\#}}&0\\
0&\eta_{\#}\end{array}\right)
for honeycomb lattice constant
\eta_{\#}\neq 0
arising from nonlinear Schrödinger equations(NLS) with honeycomb lattice potentials(see the Section II in
[
1
]
). The nonlinearities
N_{\ell}
are defined by

\mathcal{N}_{1}(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}b_{1}\psi_{11}\overline{\psi_{21}}+2b_{2}\psi_{12}\overline{\psi_{22}}&0\\
0&b_{1}\psi_{12}\overline{\psi_{22}}+2b_{2}\psi_{11}\overline{\psi_{21}}\end{array}\right),\quad\mathcal{N}_{2}(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})=\left(|x|^{-1}*(\psi_{1}^{\dagger}\psi_{2})\right)

where
\psi_{j1},\psi_{j2}
are components of
\psi_{j}
and the coefficients
b_{1},b_{2}>0
which are an amplitude of Bloch waves. The symbol
*
denotes convolution operator in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and the
\psi^{\dagger}
is the complex conjugate transpose of
\psi
.

Our main equations with the nonlinearity
\mathcal{N}_{\ell}
are derived from two dimensional Schrödinger equations with honeycomb lattice potential. Its rigorous derivation appears in
[
1
]
. The honeycomb lattice structure has appeared in the fabrication of graphene, a mono-crystalline graphitic film in which electrons behave like massless Dirac fermions(see
[
6
]
). Also, the nonlinear optics which model laser beam propagator in particular types of photonic crystals, have the honeycomb structure(see
[
2
,
14
]
).

The equation (
1
) for
\ell=1
has the scaling invariance structure in
\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}
. That is, for
\psi_{1}
the solution to (
1
) with
\ell=1
, the function
\psi_{1,{\lambda}}
defined by
\psi_{1,{\lambda}}(t,x)={\lambda}^{\frac{1}{2}}\psi_{1}({\lambda}t,{\lambda}x)
is also the solution to the equation (
1
) with
\ell=1
and satisfies that
\|\psi_{1,{\lambda}}(0,\cdot)\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}=\|\psi_{1}(0,\cdot)\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}
. By this reason, the (
1
) for
\ell=1
is said to be mass-supercritical case. Also, since
\|\psi_{2,{\lambda}}(0,\cdot)\|_{L_{x}^{2}}=\|\psi_{2}(0,\cdot)\|_{L_{x}^{2}}
for
\psi_{2,{\lambda}}(t,x)={\lambda}\psi_{2}({\lambda}t,{\lambda}x)
where
\psi_{2}
is solution to (
1
) with
\ell=2
, the equation (
1
) with
\ell=2
has the scaling invariance structure in
L_{x}^{2}
. The (
1
) for
\ell=2
is called to be mass-critical case.

Now we state the main theorem of this paper. For simplicity of representation, we set an index
s(\ell)
by

\displaystyle s(\ell)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{1}{2}&\;\mbox{ if }\ell=1,\\
0&\;\,\mbox{ if }\ell=2.\end{array}\right.

Theorem 1.1

(Local well-posedness for
H^{s}
data)
.

Let
s>s(\ell)+\frac{3}{8}
for
\ell=1,2
. Then (
1
) is locally well-posed for initial data in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
.

Here a definition of the fractional Sobolev space
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
is placed in
Notations
below. In particular, LWP result of Dirac equations which have same nonlinearity
\mathcal{N}_{\ell}
has been studied in
[
1
]
for
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
with
s>1
.

We can prove Theorem 1 for massive cases (
m>0
) in the same way as proof of Theorem 1. Since the Physical model comes from massless Dirac Fermions, we only consider the massless case(
m=0
) in this paper.

Lemma
3.2
is deduced from Selberg’s estimates and we get a coefficient
\mu^{\frac{3}{8}-s}
in the result (3) of Lemma
3.2
. Then the condition
s>s(\ell)+\frac{3}{8}
is necessary in process of proof of Theorem
1.1
and the coefficient
\mu^{\frac{3}{8}-s}
makes the gap between scaling critical index
s(\ell)
and our well-posedness index
s(\ell)+\frac{3}{8}
.

1
) are well known as semi-relativistic equations as follows:

(1.4)

\displaystyle iu_{t}+\sqrt{m^{2}-\Delta}u

\displaystyle={\lambda}|u|^{2}u,

(1.5)

\displaystyle iu_{t}+\sqrt{m^{2}-\Delta}u

\displaystyle={\lambda}(|x|^{-1}*|u|^{2})u.

. The Cauchy problem for 3 dimensional Hartree type semi-relativistic equations (

The difficulty stems from the absence of null-structure of
\mathcal{N}_{\ell}
. We describe the difference between
\psi^{\dagger}\beta\psi
and
|\psi|^{2}
where
\beta=\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\;\,\,\,0\\
0&-1\end{array}\right)
. The quadratic term
\psi^{\dagger}\beta\psi
has a null-structure which represents like
|\psi_{1}|^{2}-|\psi_{2}|^{2}
. On the other hand, another term
|\psi|^{2}=|\psi_{1}|^{2}+|\psi_{2}|^{2}
does not have the null-structure. Since this structure induces delicate bilinear estimates, Dirac equations with null-structure lead to better results than the case without null-structure. However, we do not use this structure, because our nonlinearities
\mathcal{N}_{1}
are essentially the same as
|\psi|^{2}
. For this reason, it is picky to control the nonlinear term
\mathcal{N}_{\ell}
. Hence we describe the Lemma
3.2
which used crucially in the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Also we consider the Dirac equation with Coulomb type nonlinearity which has null-structure:

(1.6)

\displaystyle(i\partial_{t}+{\alpha}\cdot D)\psi

\displaystyle={\lambda}\left(\psi^{\dagger}\beta\psi\right)\psi,

(1.7)

\displaystyle(i\partial_{t}+{\alpha}\cdot D)\psi

\displaystyle={\lambda}\Big(|x|^{-{\gamma}}*\left(\psi^{\dagger}\beta\psi\right)\Big)\psi.

As known result for the equation (
1.6
), Bejenaru and Herr(
[
3
]
) showed the GWP in
H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. And the known results for the equation (
1.7
) are in
[
8
,
20
]
. In
[
20
]
, An author of this paper revealed the LWP in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
with
s>{\frac{{\gamma}-1}{2}}
and
1\leq{\gamma}<2
. It was studied in
[
8
]
that global well-posedness and small data scattering holds in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for
s>{{{\gamma}-1}}
and
1<{\gamma}<2
. As related to (
1.7
), there is a Dirac equation with Yukawa potential. One may find many results of the Dirac equation which has Yukawa potential nonlinearity in
[
7
,
25
,
26
,

]
.

In view of scaling we expect that LWP results for (
1
) is optimal in
H^{s(\ell)}
. For this expectation we introduce the following theorem which denotes the smooth failure of our main equation (
1
) for
s<s(\ell)
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
s<s(\ell)
and
T>0
. If the flow map
\phi\mapsto u
in (
1
) exists as a map from
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
to
C([-T,T];H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})),
it fails to be
C^{3}
at the origin.

If the equation (
1
) has well-posedness in
[-T,T]
for some
T>0
, the flows of (
1
) have the smoothness in
[-T,T]
. Since Theorem
1.2
implies that the smoothness of flows of (
1
) fails, This yields the ill-posedness of (
1
) for
H^{s}
with
s<s(\ell)
.

The smoothness failure of some equations was studied for many authors in
[
22
,
4
,
15
,
20
]
. Molinet, Saut, and Tzvetkov(
[
22
]
), Bejenaru and Tao(
[
4
]
), and Herr and Lenzmann(
[
15
]
) have proved the ill-posedness results similar to Theorem (
1.2
) for Benjamin-Ono equations, 1-d Schrödinger equations and semi-relativistic equations, respectively. For Dirac equation, ill-posedness results have been shown in
[
20
]
.

It is still opened the well-posedness of (
1
) in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for
s(\ell)\leq s\leq s(\ell)+\frac{3}{8}
. For filling up this gap, we have to obtain better bilinear estimates than Lemma
3.2
. For this purpose we should find some structure of nonlinearity of (
1
) like null-structure. Then we may improve LWP in
H^{s}
with sobolev index
s
below
s(\ell)+\frac{3}{8}
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, for simplicity of the Cauchy problem, we define the projection operators and rewrite the equations (
1
) to integral equations.

2.1.
Projection operator

We first define the projections about (
1
) as following:

\displaystyle\Pi^{\pm}(D):=\frac{1}{2}\left(I\pm\frac{{\alpha}\cdot D}{|\eta_{\#}||\nabla|}\right).

Then we get

{\alpha}\cdot D=|\eta_{\#}||\nabla|\left(\Pi^{+}(D)-\Pi^{-}(D)\right).

Using these projection operators, we decompose

\psi=\psi_{+}+\psi_{-}

where
\psi_{\pm}:=\Pi^{\pm}(D)\psi
. Also, these projection operators satisfy that

\Pi^{\pm}(D)\Pi^{\pm}(D)=\Pi^{\pm}(D),\qquad\Pi^{\pm}(D)\Pi^{\mp}(D)=0.

Applying these operator to (
1
) we see that

(2.1)

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}\pm|\eta_{\#}||\nabla|)\psi_{\pm}=-i\kappa\Pi^{\pm}(D)\mathcal{N}_{\ell}(\psi,\psi)\psi

for
\ell=1,2
with initial data

\psi_{\pm}(0)=:\psi_{0,\pm}\in H^{s}.

To simplify the representation of (
2.1
), we set the spinner
\phi_{\pm}(t,x)=\psi_{\pm}\left(\frac{t}{|\eta_{\#}|},x\right)
. Hence
\phi
satisfies that

(i\partial_{t}\pm|\nabla|)\phi_{\pm}=\frac{1}{|\eta_{\#}|}(i\partial_{t}\pm|\eta_{\#}||\nabla|)\psi_{\pm}=-\frac{i\kappa}{|\eta_{\#}|}\Pi^{\pm}(D)\mathcal{N}_{\ell}(\phi,\phi)\phi

for
\ell=1,2
. We still call the spinner to
\psi
. Then we finally get the second main equation

(2.2)

\displaystyle(i\partial_{t}\pm|\nabla|)\psi_{\pm}=-i\kappa_{\#}\Pi^{\pm}(D)\mathcal{N}_{\ell}(\psi,\psi)\psi.

where
\kappa_{\#}=\frac{\kappa}{|\eta_{\#}|}
.

By Duhamel’s formula, we can represent the equations (
2.2
) written as an integral equation

(2.3)

\displaystyle\psi_{\pm}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S_{\pm}(t)\psi_{0,\pm}+\kappa_{\#}\int_{0}^{t}S_{\pm}(t-t^{\prime})\Pi^{\pm}(D)\left[\mathcal{N}_{\ell}\Big(\psi(t^{\prime}),\psi(t^{\prime})\Big)\psi(t^{\prime})\right]dt^{\prime}

for
\ell=1,2
. Here we define the linear propagator
S_{\pm}(t)
as following:

(2.4)

\displaystyle S_{\pm}(t)f=e^{\mp it|\nabla|}f.

2.2.
Fractional Leibniz rule

Lemma 2.1

(

,

,

Let

provided
s=s_{1}+s_{2}
with
0\leq s_{1},s_{2}\leq 1
and
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}
.

The proof of Lemma
2.1
is in
[
16
,
18
,

]
.

Function spaces and Bilinear estimates

3.1.
Functions spaces:
X^{s,b}
-space

We first introduce

space which will be useful in local theories. (See e.g.
[

,
19
,

Let us define the norm for

as follows:

X_{\pm}^{s,b}(T):=\Big\{\psi:\Big\|\chi_{[-T,T]}\psi\Big\|_{X_{\pm}^{s,b}}<\infty\Big\}

with a norm

\|\psi\|_{X_{\pm}^{s,b}}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{1+2}}\left|\left<\xi\right>^{s}\left<\tau\mp|\xi|\right>^{b}\widetilde{\psi}(\tau,\xi)\right|^{2}d\tau d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

In particular, we denote that
X_{\pm_{j}}^{s,b}
is
X_{+}^{s,b}
for
\pm_{j}=+
and
X_{\pm_{j}}^{s,b}
is
X_{-}^{s,b}
for
\pm_{j}=-
.
These function spaces satisfy the embedding for

X_{\pm}^{s,b}(T)\hookrightarrow C([-T,T];H^{s}).

3.2.
Bilinear estimates

Lemma 3.1

(Theorem 2.1 of
[
23
]
)
.

Let
{\lambda}>0
and
L\geq 1
. Let us define the thickened cones

\displaystyle K_{{\lambda},L}^{\pm}=\Big\{(\tau,\xi):|\xi|\lesssim{\lambda},\;\tau\mp|\xi|=O(L)\Big\}

Then

\|P_{K_{{\lambda},L}^{\pm}\cap(\mathbb{R}\times B_{\mu})}u\|_{L_{t,x}^{4}}\lesssim\mu^{\frac{1}{4}}{\lambda}^{\frac{1}{8}}L^{\frac{3}{8}}\|P_{K_{{\lambda},L}^{\pm}\cap(\mathbb{R}\times B_{\mu})}u\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}

for
u:\mathbb{R}^{1+2}\to\mathbb{C}
and any ball
B_{\mu}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
with radius
\mu>0
.

The following lemma is readily obtained by Lemma
3.1
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
s>\frac{3}{8}
,
b>\frac{1}{2}
, and
u:\mathbb{R}^{1+2}\to\mathbb{C}
. Then the following holds:

(1)

\|P_{B_{\mu}}u\|_{L_{t,x}^{4}}\lesssim\mu^{\frac{1}{4}}\|P_{B_{\mu}}u\|_{X_{\pm}^{\frac{1}{8},b}}
for
u\in X_{\pm}^{\frac{1}{8},b}
and any ball
B_{\mu}
with radius
\mu>0
,

(2)

\|u\|_{L_{t,x}^{4}}\lesssim\|u\|_{X_{\pm}^{\frac{3}{8},b}}
for
u\in X_{\pm}^{\frac{3}{8},b}
,

(3)

\|P_{\mu}(u_{1}\overline{u_{2}})\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}\lesssim\mu^{\frac{3}{8}-s}\|u_{1}\|_{X_{\pm_{1}}^{s,b}}\|u_{2}\|_{X_{\pm_{2}}^{s,b}}
for
\mu>0
,
u_{j}\in X_{\pm_{j}}^{s,b}
.

Proof.

We first prove (1). Lemma
3.1
yields that, for
{\lambda}\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\|P_{B_{\mu}}P_{{\lambda}}u\|_{L_{t,x}^{4}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{L\geq 1}\mu^{\frac{1}{4}}{\lambda}^{\frac{1}{8}}L^{\frac{3}{8}}\|P_{K_{{\lambda},L}^{\pm}\cap(\mathbb{R}\times B_{\mu})}u\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{L\geq 1}\mu^{\frac{1}{4}}{\lambda}^{\frac{1}{8}}L^{\frac{3}{8}}\|P_{K_{{\lambda},L}^{\pm}\cap(\mathbb{R}\times B_{\mu})}u\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}\lesssim\sum_{L\geq 1}\mu^{\frac{1}{4}}{\lambda}^{\frac{1}{8}}L^{\frac{3}{8}-b}\|L^{b}\chi_{K_{{\lambda},L}^{\pm}\cap(\mathbb{R}\times B_{\mu})}\widetilde{u}\|_{L_{\tau,\xi}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{L\geq 1}\mu^{\frac{1}{4}}{\lambda}^{\frac{1}{8}}L^{\frac{3}{8}-b}\|\left<\tau\mp|\xi|\right>^{b}P_{B_{\mu}}\widetilde{u}\|_{L_{\tau,\xi}^{2}}\lesssim\mu^{\frac{1}{4}}{\lambda}^{\frac{1}{8}}\|P_{B_{\mu}}u\|_{X_{\pm}^{0,b}}.

Then we have

\|P_{B_{\mu}}u\|_{L_{t,x}^{4}}\lesssim\sum_{{\lambda}\geq 1}\|P_{B_{\mu}}P_{{\lambda}}u\|_{L_{t,x}^{4}}\lesssim\sum_{{\lambda}\geq 1}\mu^{\frac{1}{4}}{\lambda}^{\frac{1}{8}}\|P_{B_{\mu}}P_{{\lambda}}u\|_{X_{\pm}^{0,b}}\lesssim\mu^{\frac{1}{4}}\|P_{B_{\mu}}u\|_{X^{\frac{1}{8},b}_{\pm}}.

For (2), by (1) we obtain

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L_{t,x}^{4}}\lesssim\left(\sum_{\mu\geq 1}\|P_{\mu}u\|_{L_{t,x}^{4}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\left(\sum_{\mu\geq 1}\mu^{\frac{1}{4}}\|P_{\mu}u\|_{X_{\pm}^{\frac{1}{8},b}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\|u\|_{X_{\pm}^{\frac{3}{8},b}}.

Let us now prove (3). Using frequency localization and (2), we see that

\displaystyle\left\|P_{\mu}(u_{1}\overline{u_{2}})\right\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{l}{\lambda}_{1},{\lambda}_{2}\geq 1\\
\mu\lesssim{\lambda}_{1}\sim{\lambda}_{2}\end{subarray}}\left\|P_{\mu}(u_{1,{\lambda}_{1}}\overline{u_{2,{\lambda}_{2}}})\right\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{l}\;\;\;{\lambda}_{1},{\lambda}_{2}\geq 1\\
{\lambda}_{\min}\lesssim{\lambda}_{\max}\sim\mu\end{subarray}}\left\|P_{\mu}(u_{1,{\lambda}_{1}}\overline{u_{2,{\lambda}_{2}}})\right\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{l}{\lambda}_{1},{\lambda}_{2}\geq 1\\
\mu\lesssim{\lambda}_{1}\sim{\lambda}_{2}\end{subarray}}\|u_{1,{\lambda}_{1}}\|_{L_{t,x}^{4}}\|u_{2,{\lambda}_{2}}\|_{L_{t,x}^{4}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{l}\;\;\;{\lambda}_{1},{\lambda}_{2}\geq 1\\
{\lambda}_{\min}\lesssim{\lambda}_{\max}\sim\mu\end{subarray}}\|u_{1,{\lambda}_{1}}\|_{L_{t,x}^{4}}\|u_{2,{\lambda}_{2}}\|_{L_{t,x}^{4}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{l}{\lambda}_{1},{\lambda}_{2}\geq 1\\
\mu\lesssim{\lambda}_{1}\sim{\lambda}_{2}\end{subarray}}{\lambda}_{1}^{\frac{3}{8}-s}{\lambda}_{2}^{\frac{3}{8}-s}\|u_{1,{\lambda}_{1}}\|_{X_{\pm_{1}}^{s,b}}\|u_{2,{\lambda}_{2}}\|_{X_{\pm_{2}}^{s,b}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{l}\;\;\;{\lambda}_{1},{\lambda}_{2}\geq 1\\

0\end{array}\right)
. Then we have
\|\varphi\|_{H^{s}}\sim\mu{\lambda}^{s}
.

To lead a contradiction we adopt a following estimate:

(6.6)

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{\pm_{j},j=1,\cdots,4}\mathcal{F}_{x}\Big[\mathcal{L}_{\ell}^{1\cdots 4}({\varphi})(t)\Big](\xi)\right|\gtrsim t\mu^{3+2s(j)}.

We now prove the (
6.6
). By taking Fourier transform we see that

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{x}\Big[\mathcal{L}_{\ell}^{1\cdots 4}({\varphi})(t)\Big](\xi)

\displaystyle=\Pi^{\pm}(\xi)\int_{0}^{t}\int e^{-\pm_{1}i(t-t^{\prime})|\xi|}\mathcal{F}_{x}\left[\mathcal{N}_{\ell}\Big(S_{\pm_{2}}(t^{\prime}){\varphi},S_{\pm_{3}}(t^{\prime}){\varphi}\Big)\right](\sigma)\mathcal{F}_{x}\left[S_{\pm_{4}}(t^{\prime}){\varphi}\right](\xi-\sigma)d\sigma dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle=-\Pi^{\pm}(\xi)\int_{|\sigma|\lesssim\mu}\int_{-B_{\mu}}\mathbf{p}_{1\cdots 4}(t,\xi,\sigma,\zeta)|\sigma|^{-1+2s(j)}\chi_{B_{\mu}}(-\zeta)\chi_{B_{\mu}}(\sigma-\zeta)\chi_{B_{\mu}}(\xi-\sigma)d\zeta d\sigma

where
-B_{\mu}:=\{\xi=(\xi_{1},\xi_{2}):(-\xi_{1},-\xi_{2})\in B_{\mu}\}
and

\displaystyle\mathbf{p}_{1\cdots 4}(t,\xi,\sigma,\zeta)

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{t}e^{-i(\pm_{1}(t-t^{\prime})|\xi|\pm_{2}t^{\prime}|\zeta|\pm_{3}t^{\prime}|\sigma-\zeta|\pm_{4}t^{\prime}|\sigma|)}dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{-\pm_{1}it|\xi|}(e^{it\omega}-1)}{i\omega}

with

\displaystyle\omega=\pm_{1}|\xi|\pm_{2}|\zeta|\pm_{3}|\sigma-\zeta|\pm_{4}|\sigma|.

From the support condition it follows that
|\sigma|\lesssim 2\mu
, provided
\xi\in B_{3\mu}
. Then
|\omega|\lesssim{\lambda}
.

We set
t=\delta{\lambda}^{-1-\varepsilon}
for fixed
0<\delta\ll 1
. Since
|t\omega|\ll 1
for
{\lambda}
large enough, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{\pm_{j},j=1,2,3,4}\mathbf{p}_{1\cdots 4}(t,\xi,\sigma,\zeta)

\displaystyle\qquad=\sum_{\pm_{j},j=1,2,3,4}te^{-\pm_{1}it|\xi|}\left(\frac{\cos(t\omega)-1}{it\omega}+i\frac{\sin(t\omega)}{it\omega}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad=\sum_{\pm_{j},j=1,2,3,4}te^{-\pm_{1}it|\xi|}(O_{\pm}(\delta)+i)

\displaystyle\qquad=\sum_{\pm_{j},j=1,2,3,4}te^{-\pm_{1}it|\xi|}O_{\pm}(\delta)+i\sum_{\pm_{j},j=1,2,3,4}te^{-\pm_{1}it|\xi|}

\displaystyle\qquad=\sum_{\pm_{j},j=1,2,3,4}te^{-\pm_{1}it|\xi|}O_{\pm}(\delta)+8it\cos(t|\xi|)

\displaystyle\qquad=\sum_{\pm_{j},j=1,2,3,4}te^{-\pm_{1}it|\xi|}O_{\pm}(\delta)+8it(1+O(\delta)).

Hence we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{\pm_{j},j=1,2,3,4}\mathcal{F}_{x}\Big[\mathcal{L}_{\ell}^{1\cdots 4}({\varphi})(t)\Big](\xi)\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\gtrsim\left|\sum_{\pm_{j},j=1,2,3,4}\int_{|\sigma|\lesssim\mu}\int_{-B_{\mu}}\mathbf{p}_{1\cdots 4}(t,\xi,\sigma,\zeta)|\sigma|^{-1+2s(\ell)}\chi_{B_{\mu}}(-\zeta)\chi_{B_{\mu}}(\sigma-\zeta)\chi_{B_{\mu}}(\xi-\sigma)d\zeta d\sigma\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\gtrsim t\left|\int_{|\sigma|\lesssim\mu}\int_{-B_{\mu}}|\sigma|^{-1+2s(\ell)}\chi_{B_{\mu}}(-\zeta)\chi_{B_{\mu}}(\sigma-\zeta)\chi_{B_{\mu}}(\xi-\sigma)d\zeta d\sigma\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\gtrsim t\mu^{3+2s(\ell)}.

Therefore we get (
6.6
).

We return to the main proof. Since
\mathcal{F}_{x}\Big[\mathcal{L}_{\ell}^{1\cdots 4}({\varphi})(t)\Big](\xi)=0
for
\xi\notin B_{3\mu}
, the (
6.6
) yields that

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{L}_{\ell}({\varphi})(t)\right\|_{H^{s}}=\left\|\langle\xi\rangle^{s}\sum_{\pm_{j},j=1,2,3,4}\mathcal{F}_{x}\Big[\mathcal{L}_{\ell}^{1\cdots 4}({\varphi})(t)\Big](\xi)\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{2}}

\displaystyle\gtrsim t\mu^{3+2s(\ell)}\left\|\langle\xi\rangle^{s}\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{2}(B_{3\mu})}\gtrsim t\mu^{4+2s(\ell)}{\lambda}^{s}.

This gives us that

(6.7)

\displaystyle t\mu^{4+2s(\ell)}{\lambda}^{s}\lesssim\left\|\langle\xi\rangle^{s}\sum_{\pm_{j},j=1,\cdots,4}\mathcal{F}_{x}\Big[\mathcal{L}_{j}^{1\cdots 4}({\varphi})(t)\Big](\xi)\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{2}}\lesssim\mu^{3}{\lambda}^{3s}.

Therefore, by (
6.7
) and
t=\delta{\lambda}^{-1-\varepsilon}
, we have

(6.8)

\displaystyle\delta\lesssim\mu^{-1-2s(\ell)}{\lambda}^{2s+1+{\varepsilon}}={\lambda}^{2s+2s(\ell)+2\varepsilon\big(1+s(\ell)\big)}.

Then since the (
6.8
) does not hold for
s<s(\ell)
and
{\lambda}\gg 1
, we reach a contradiction. This completes the proof of Proposition
6.1
.
∎

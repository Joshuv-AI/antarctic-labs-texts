Approximation of polynomials from Walsh tail spaces Thanks: This material is based upon work supported by the NSF grant DMS-1929284 while the authors were in residence at ICERM for the Harmonic Analysis and Convexity program.
Alexandros Eskenazis, Haonan Zhang

Abstract
. We derive various bounds for the L p L_{p} distance of polynomials on the hypercube from Walsh tail spaces, extending some of Oleszkiewicz’s results (2017) for Rademacher sums.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification.
Primary: 42C10; Secondary: 41A17, 60E15.

Key words.
Discrete hypercube, polynomial inequalities, Rademacher sum, tail space.

1.
Introduction

Given
n\in\mathbb{N}=\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, every function
f:\{-1,1\}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
admits a unique Fourier–Walsh expansion

\forall\ x\in\{-1,1\}^{n},\qquad f(x)=\sumop\displaylimits_{S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}}\widehat{f}(S)w_{S}(x),

(1)

where the Walsh function
w_{S}
is given by
w_{S}(x)=\prodop\displaylimits_{i\in S}x_{i}
for
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\{-1,1\}^{n}
. We shall say that
f
is of degree at most
k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
if
\widehat{f}(S)=0
for every subset
S
of
\{1,\ldots,n\}
with
|S|>k
. Similarly, we say that
f
belongs on the
k
-th tail space, where
k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, if
\widehat{f}(S)=0
for every subset
S
with
|S|\leq k
. More generally, given a nonempty subset
I\subseteq\{0,1,\ldots,n\}
, we denote by

\mathscr{P}_{I}^{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\big\{f:\{-1,1\}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}:\ \widehat{f}(S)=0\mbox{ for every }S\mbox{ with }|S|\notin I\big\}.

(2)

We shall also adopt the natural notations
\mathscr{P}_{>k}^{n}=\mathscr{P}_{\{k+1,\ldots,n\}}^{n}
,
\mathscr{P}_{\leq k}^{n}=\mathscr{P}_{\{0,1,\ldots,k\}}^{n}
,
\mathscr{P}_{=k}^{n}=\mathscr{P}_{\{k\}}^{n}
and so on.

Many modern developments in discrete analysis (see
[
18
]
) are centered around quantitative properties of functions whose spectrum is bounded above or below, in analogy with estimates established for polynomials in classical approximation theory on the torus
\mathbb{T}^{n}
or on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. One of the first results of this nature, going back at least to
[
3
,
4
]
, is the important fact that all finite moments of low-degree Walsh polynomials are equivalent to each other up to dimension-free factors. Namely, given any
1\leq p\leq q<\infty
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a (sharp) constant
\mathsf{M}_{p,q}(k)
such that for any
n\geq k
, every polynomial
f:\{-1,1\}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
of degree at most
k
satisfies

\|f\|_{q}\leq\mathsf{M}_{p,q}(k)\|f\|_{p},

(3)

where
\|\cdot\|_{r}
always denotes the
L_{r}
norm on
\{-1,1\}^{n}
with respect to the uniform probability measure. Note that the reverse of (
3
) holds trivially with constant
1
by Hölder’s inequality. We refer to
[
8
,
13
,
16
]
for the best known bounds on the implicit constant
\mathsf{M}_{p,q}(k)
. In the special case
k=1
, (
3
) is the celebrated Khintchine inequality
[
15
]
for Rademacher sums.

Our starting point is the simple observation that the moment comparison estimates (
3
) have the following (equivalent) dual formulation in terms of distances from tail spaces.

Proposition 1
.

For every
1\leq p\leq q<\infty
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the constant
\mathsf{M}_{p,q}(k)
in inequality (
3
) is also the least constant for which every function
f:\{-1,1\}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
, where
n\geq k
, satisfies

\inf_{g\in\mathscr{P}^{n}_{>k}}\|f-g\|_{p^{\ast}}\leq\mathsf{M}_{p,q}(k)\inf_{g\in\mathscr{P}^{n}_{>k}}\|f-g\|_{q^{\ast}},

(4)

where the conjugate exponent
r^{\ast}
of
r\in[1,\infty]
satisfies
\frac{1}{r^{\ast}}+\frac{1}{r}=1
.

Again, the reverse of (
4
) holds with constant
1
. In the special case
k=0
, inequality (
4
) becomes trivial with
\mathsf{M}_{p,q}(0)=1
as both sides are equal to
|\mathbb{E}f|
. When
k=1
, which corresponds to the dual of the classical Khintchine inequality, we derive the following more precise formula for the distance from the tail space
\mathscr{P}_{>1}^{n}
.

Theorem 2
.

For every
1<r\leq\infty
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, every
f:\{-1,1\}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies
1
1

1

Throughout the paper we shall use standard asymptotic notation. For instance,
\xi\lesssim\eta
(or
\eta\gtrsim\xi)
means that there exists a universal constant
c>0
such that
\xi\leq c\eta
and
\xi\asymp\eta
stands for
(\xi\lesssim\eta)\wedge(\eta\lesssim\xi)
. We shall use subscripts of the form
\lesssim_{t},\gtrsim_{t},\asymp_{t}
when the implicit constant
c
depends on some prespecified parameter
t
.

\inf_{g\in\mathscr{P}^{n}_{>1}}\|f-g\|_{r}\asymp|\mathbb{E}f|+\max_{i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}}\big|\widehat{f}(\{i\})\big|+\sqrt{\frac{r-1}{r}}\Big(\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}\widehat{f}(\{i\})^{2}\Big)^{1/2}.

(5)

This is the dual to a well-known result of Hitczenko
[
10
]
(see also
[
17
,
11
]
), obtaining
p
-independent upper and lower bounds for the
L_{p}
-norms of Rademacher sums, where
p\in[1,\infty)
.

At this point, we should point out that in both Proposition
1
and Theorem
2
, the exponents of the norms are always strictly greater than 1. For instance, choosing
f_{1}(x)=\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}
, (
4
) gives

\forall\ r\in(1,\infty],\qquad\inf_{g\in\mathscr{P}^{n}_{>k}}\|f_{1}-g\|_{r}\asymp_{r,k}\inf_{g\in\mathscr{P}^{n}_{>k}}\|f_{1}-g\|_{2}=\sqrt{n}.

(6)

On the other hand, it follows from a result of Oleszkiewicz
[
19
]
, which is the main precursor to this work, that the
L_{1}
-distance of
f_{1}
from the
k
-th tail space satisfies

\inf_{g\in\mathscr{P}^{n}_{>k}}\|f_{1}-g\|_{1}\asymp\min\{k,\sqrt{n}\},

(7)

and thus exhibits a starkly different behavior as
n\to\infty
from the
L_{r}
norms with
r>1
.

More generally, it is shown in
[
19
]
that for every
a_{1}\geq\cdots\geq a_{n}\geq 0
, we have

\inf_{g\in\mathscr{P}^{n}_{>k}}\|f_{\boldsymbol{a}}-g\|_{1}\asymp\min_{r\in\{0,1,\ldots,n\}}\bigg\{\Big(\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}+ka_{r+1}\bigg\},

(8)

where for
\boldsymbol{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
we denote
f_{\boldsymbol{a}}(x)=\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}x_{i}
and we make the convention that
a_{n+1}=0
. The quantity appearing on the right hand side of (
8
) can be rephrased in terms of the
\mathsf{K}
-functional of real interpolation (see
[
1
, Chapter 3]
). Recall that if
(A_{0},A_{1})
is an interpolation pair, then the Lions–Peetre
\mathsf{K}
-functional is defined for every
t\geq 0
and
a\in A_{0}+A_{1}
as

\mathsf{K}(a,t;A_{0},A_{1})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\inf\big\{\|a_{0}\|_{A_{0}}+t\|a_{1}\|_{A_{1}}:\ a=a_{0}+a_{1}\big\}.

(9)

It is elementary to check (see
[
12
]
), that if
a_{1}\geq\cdots\geq a_{n}\geq 0
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
, then

\min_{r\in\{0,1,\ldots,n\}}\bigg\{\Big(\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}+ka_{r+1}\bigg\}\asymp\mathsf{K}(\boldsymbol{a},k;\ell_{2}^{n},\ell_{\infty}^{n}).

(10)

Note that the right-hand side is invariant under permutations of the entries of
\boldsymbol{a}
.
The main result of this work is an appropriate extension of the upper bound in Oleszkiewicz’s result (
8
) to polynomials of arbitrary degree on the discrete hypercube.

Theorem 3
.

For every
d\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
\mathsf{C}_{d}\in(0,\infty)
such that for any
n\geq k\geq d
, every polynomial
f:\{-1,1\}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
of degree at most
d
satisfies

\inf_{g\in\mathscr{P}_{>k}^{n}}\|f-g\|_{1}\leq\mathsf{K}\big(\widehat{f},\mathsf{C}_{d}k^{d};\ell_{2}^{m},\ell_{\frac{2d}{d-1}}^{m}\big),

(11)

where
\widehat{f}
is the vector of Fourier coefficients of
f
, viewed as an element of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
with
m=\binom{n}{0}+\cdots+\binom{n}{d}
.

As was already pointed out by Oleszkiewicz, the method of
[
19
]
does not appear to extend beyond Rademacher sums. Instead, in our proof we shall employ the discrete
Bohnenblust–Hille
inequality from approximation theory (see
[
2
,
7
,
6
,
5
]
) along with a classical bound of Figiel on the Rademacher projection of polynomials. A discussion concerning the size of the implicit constant
\mathsf{C}_{d}
appearing in (
11
) is postponed to Section
2
(see Remark
8
there).

Unlike the two-sided inequality (
8
), our bound (
11
) is only one-sided and as a matter of fact there are examples in which it is far from optimal. In particular, for functions which are permutationally symmetric, we obtain a more accurate estimate. In what follows, we shall denote by
T_{k}(x)=\sumop\displaylimits_{\ell=0}^{k}c(k,\ell)x^{\ell}
the
k
-th Chebyshev polynomial of the first kind characterized by the property
T_{k}(\cos\theta)=\cos(k\theta)
, where
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
. Moreover, we shall use the ad hoc notation

\tilde{c}(k,\ell)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\begin{cases}c(k,\ell),&\mbox{ if }k-\ell\mbox{ is even}\\
c(k-1,\ell),&\mbox{ if }k-\ell\mbox{ is odd}\end{cases}.

(12)

For
\ell\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, let
f_{\ell}
be the
\ell
-th elementary symmetric multilinear polynomial

\forall\ x\in\{-1,1\}^{n},\qquad f_{\ell}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\sumop\displaylimits_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}:\\
|S|=\ell\end{subarray}}w_{S}(x).

(13)

We have the following bound on the distance of symmetric polynomials from tail spaces.

Theorem 4
.

Let
n,k,d\in\mathbb{N}
with
n\geq k\geq d
. Then, every symmetric polynomial

f=\sumop\displaylimits_{\ell=0}^{d}\alpha_{\ell}f_{\ell}

(14)

of degree at most
d
on
\{-1,1\}^{n}
satisfies

\inf_{g\in\mathscr{P}_{>k}^{n}}\|f-g\|_{1}\leq\sumop\displaylimits_{\ell=0}^{d}|\alpha_{\ell}||\tilde{c}(k,\ell)|.

(15)

This bound can sometimes be reversed and, in particular, it gives a sharp estimate as
n\to\infty
for the
L_{1}
-distance of the elementary symmetric polynomial
f_{d}
from the
k
-th tail space.

Corollary 5
.

For every
n,k,d\in\mathbb{N}
with
n\geq k\geq d
, there exists
\varepsilon_{n}(k,d)>0
such that

|\tilde{c}(k,d)|-\varepsilon_{n}(k,d)\leq\inf_{g\in\mathscr{P}_{>k}^{n}}\|f_{d}-g\|_{1}\leq|\tilde{c}(k,d)|

(16)

and
\lim_{n\to\infty}\varepsilon_{n}(k,d)=0
.

The main motivation behind the work
[
19
]
was a question of Bogucki, Nayar and Wojciechowski, asking to estimate the
L_{1}
-distance of the Rademacher sum
f_{1}
from the
k
-th tail space. Corollary
5
extends (at least asymptotically in
n
) the answer given by Oleszkiewicz to all symmetric homogeneous polynomials. We point out though that for
k=1
, (
16
) is sharper than Oleszkiewicz’s bound (
7
) as
n\to\infty
, as (
7
) is tight only up to a multiplicative constant.

2.
Proofs

We proceed to the proofs of our results. We start with the simple duality argument leading to Proposition
1
, variants of which will be used throughout the paper.

Proof of Proposition
1
.

Consider the identity operator acting as
\mathsf{id}(h)=h
on a function of the form
h:\{-1,1\}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
. Then, the optimal constant
\mathsf{M}_{p,q}(k)
can be expressed as

\mathsf{M}_{p,q}(k)=\big\|\mathsf{id}:(\mathscr{P}_{\leq k}^{n},\|\cdot\|_{p})\to(\mathscr{P}_{\leq k}^{n},\|\cdot\|_{q})\big\|=\big\|\mathsf{id}^{*}:(\mathscr{P}_{\leq k}^{n},\|\cdot\|_{q})^{\ast}\to(\mathscr{P}_{\leq k}^{n},\|\cdot\|_{p})^{\ast}\big\|

(17)

by duality. Moreover, observe that since
(\mathscr{P}_{\leq k}^{n},\|\cdot\|_{r})
is a subspace of
L_{r}
, its dual is isometric to

(\mathscr{P}_{\leq k}^{n},\|\cdot\|_{r})^{\ast}=L_{r^{\ast}}\big/(\mathscr{P}_{\leq k}^{n})^{\perp}=L_{r^{\ast}}\big/\mathscr{P}_{>k}^{n},

(18)

where
A^{\perp}
is the annihilator of
A
. Since it is also clear that
\mathsf{id}^{*}=\mathsf{id}
, (
17
) concludes the proof.
∎

Using a theorem of Hitczenko
[
10
]
as input and the same duality, we deduce Theorem
2
.

Proof of Theorem
2
.

The result of
[
10
]
asserts that if
\boldsymbol{a}=(a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
and
f_{\boldsymbol{a}}(x)=a_{0}+\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}x_{i}
,

\|f_{\boldsymbol{a}}\|_{r^{\ast}}=\bigg(\mathbb{E}\Big|\sumop\displaylimits_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}x_{i}\Big|^{r^{*}}\bigg)^{1/r^{*}}\asymp\mathsf{K}(\boldsymbol{a},\sqrt{r^{\ast}};\ell_{1}^{n+1},\ell_{2}^{n+1}),

(19)

where
x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
are independent Bernoulli random variables, and the first equality holds due to symmetry. In other words, the linear operator

T:\big(\mathbb{R}^{n+1},\mathsf{K}(\cdot,\sqrt{r^{\ast}};\ell_{1}^{n+1},\ell_{2}^{n+1})\big)\to(\mathscr{P}_{\leq 1}^{n},\|\cdot\|_{r^{\ast}})

(20)

given by
T{\boldsymbol{a}}=f_{\boldsymbol{a}}
is an isomorphism, and thus the same holds for its adjoint. Recalling that

\mathsf{K}(\boldsymbol{a},\sqrt{r^{\ast}};\ell_{1}^{n+1},\ell_{2}^{n+1})=\inf\big\{\|\boldsymbol{b}\|_{\ell_{1}^{n+1}}+\sqrt{r^{\ast}}\|\boldsymbol{c}\|_{\ell_{2}^{n+1}}:\ \boldsymbol{a}=\boldsymbol{b}+\boldsymbol{c}\big\}

(21)

and the duality between sums and intersections of normed spaces
[
1
, Theorem 2.7.1]
, we see that the dual space of
(\mathbb{R}^{n+1},\mathsf{K}(\cdot,\sqrt{r^{\ast}};\ell_{1}^{n+1},\ell_{2}^{n+1}))
can be identified with

\forall\ y\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1},\qquad\|y\|_{(\mathbb{R}^{n+1},\mathsf{K}(\cdot,\sqrt{r^{\ast}};\ell_{1}^{n+1},\ell_{2}^{n+1}))^{\ast}}=\max\Big\{\|y\|_{\ell_{\infty}^{n+1}},\frac{\|y\|_{\ell_{2}^{n+1}}}{\sqrt{r^{\ast}}}\Big\}.

(22)

By Parseval’s identity, the action of the adjoint

T^{\ast}:L_{r}\big/\mathscr{P}_{>1}^{n}\to(\mathbb{R}^{n+1},\mathsf{K}(\cdot,\sqrt{r^{\ast}};\ell_{1}^{n+1},\ell_{2}^{n+1}))^{\ast}

(23)

is given by

T^{\ast}(f+\mathscr{P}_{>1}^{n})=\big(\mathbb{E}f,\widehat{f}(\{1\}),\ldots,\widehat{f}(\{n\})\big)

(24)

and thus the conclusion is equivalent to fact that
T^{\ast}
is an isomorphism.
∎

We now proceed to the proof of the general upper bound for polynomials given in Theorem
3
. The first ingredient for the proof is a discrete version of the classical Bohnenblust–Hille inequality from approximation theory (see the survey
[
7
]
) proven in
[
2
,
6
]
. This asserts that for every
d\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a (sharp) constant
\mathsf{B}_{d}\in(0,\infty)
such that for any
n\geq d
, every polynomial
f:\{-1,1\}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
of degree at most
d
satisfies

\Big(\sumop\displaylimits_{S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}}|\widehat{f}(S)|^{\frac{2d}{d+1}}\Big)^{\frac{d+1}{2d}}\leq\mathsf{B}_{d}\|f\|_{\infty}.

(25)

Moreover,
\frac{2d}{d+1}
is the least exponent for which the implicit constant becomes independent of the ambient dimension
n
. The best known upper bound
B_{d}\leq\exp(C\sqrt{d\log d})
for the constant
\mathsf{B}_{d}
is due to the work of Defant, Mastyło and Pérez
[
6
]
.

The level
\ell
-Rademacher projection of a function
f:\{-1,1\}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is defined as

\forall\ x\in\{-1,1\}^{n},\qquad\mathrm{Rad}_{\ell}f(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\sumop\displaylimits_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}:\\
|S|=\ell\end{subarray}}\widehat{f}(S)w_{S}(x).

(26)

Moreover, we write
\mathrm{Rad}_{\leq d}=\sumop\displaylimits_{\ell\leq d}\mathrm{Rad}_{\ell}
. Apart from the discrete Bohnenblust–Hille inequality (
25
), we will also use a standard bound on the norm of the
\ell
-Rademacher projections which is usually attributed to Figiel (see also
[
9
, Section 3]
for a short proof).

Proposition 6
.

Let
n\geq k\geq d
. Then, every function
f:\{-1,1\}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
of degree at most
k
satisfies

\forall\ 0\leq\ell\leq d,\qquad\big\|\mathrm{Rad}_{\ell}f\big\|_{\infty}\leq|\tilde{c}(k,\ell)|\ \|f\|_{\infty},

(27)

and thus,

\big\|\mathrm{Rad}_{\leq d}f\big\|_{\infty}\leq\sumop\displaylimits_{\ell=0}^{d}\big\|\mathrm{Rad}_{\ell}f\big\|_{\infty}\leq\sumop\displaylimits_{\ell=0}^{d}|\tilde{c}(k,\ell)|\ \|f\|_{\infty},

(28)

where
\tilde{c}(k,\ell)
is given by (
12
). It is moreover known that
|\tilde{c}(k,\ell)|\leq\frac{k^{\ell}}{\ell!}
.

Combining the above with Parseval’s identity, we deduce the following bound.

Lemma 7
.

Let
n\geq k\geq d
. Then, every function
f:\{-1,1\}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
of degree at most
k
satisfies

\max\big\{\big\|\widehat{\mathrm{Rad}_{\leq d}(f)}\big\|_{\ell_{2}^{m}},\sigma(k,d)^{-1}\big\|\widehat{\mathrm{Rad}_{\leq d}(f)}\big\|_{\ell_{\frac{2d}{d+1}}^{m}}\big\}\leq\inf_{g\in\mathscr{P}_{>d}^{n}\cap\mathscr{P}_{\leq k}^{n}}\|f-g\|_{\infty},

(29)

where
m=\binom{n}{0}+\cdots+\binom{n}{d}
and
\sigma(k,d)=\mathsf{B}_{d}\sumop\displaylimits_{\ell=0}^{d}|\tilde{c}(k,\ell)|
.

Proof.

Fix a function
g\in\mathscr{P}_{>d}^{n}\cap\mathscr{P}_{\leq k}^{n}
. Then,

\big\|\widehat{\mathrm{Rad}_{\leq d}(f)}\big\|_{\ell_{2}^{m}}\leq\big\|\widehat{f}-\widehat{g}\big\|_{\ell_{2}^{M}}=\|f-g\|_{2}\leq\|f-g\|_{\infty},

(30)

where
M=\binom{n}{0}+\cdots+\binom{n}{k}
. Moreover, we have

\big\|\widehat{\mathrm{Rad}_{\leq d}(f)}\big\|_{\ell_{\frac{2d}{d+1}}^{m}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:bh}}}{{\leq}}\mathsf{B}_{d}\big\|\mathrm{Rad}_{\leq d}(f)\big\|_{\infty}=\mathsf{B}_{d}\big\|\mathrm{Rad}_{\leq d}(f-g)\big\|_{\infty}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:figiel}}}{{\leq}}\mathsf{B}_{d}\sumop\displaylimits_{\ell=0}^{d}|\tilde{c}(k,\ell)|\ \|f-g\|_{\infty}.\hfill\qed

Equipped with Lemma
7
, we can complete the proof of Theorem
3
.

Proof of Theorem
3
.

Consider the normed spaces
X=(\mathscr{P}_{\leq k}^{n},\|\cdot\|_{\infty})
and
Y=(\mathbb{R}^{m},\|\cdot\|_{Y})
with

\forall\ y\in\mathbb{R}^{m},\qquad\|y\|_{Y}=\max\big\{\|y\|_{\ell_{2}^{m}},\sigma(k,d)^{-1}\|y\|_{\ell_{\frac{2d}{d+1}}^{m}}\big\}

(31)

and
m=\binom{n}{0}+\cdots+\binom{n}{d}
. Moreover, let
Z=\mathscr{P}^{n}_{>d}\cap\mathscr{P}_{\leq k}^{n}\subset X
, viewed as a normed subspace of
X
. Lemma
7
asserts that the linear operator
A:X/Z\to Y
given by

\forall\ f\in X,\qquad A(f+Z)=\big(\widehat{f}(S)\big)_{\begin{subarray}{c}|S|\leq d\end{subarray}}

(32)

has norm
\|A\|\leq 1
. Therefore, the same holds for its adjoint
A^{*}:Y^{*}\to(X/Z)^{*}
.

By the usual duality between sums and intersections of normed spaces
[
1
, Theorem 2.7.1]
, we see that the space
Y^{*}
is isometric to

\forall\ w\in\mathbb{R}^{m},\qquad\|w\|_{Y^{\ast}}=\mathsf{K}\big(w,\sigma(k,d);\ell_{2}^{m},\ell_{\frac{2d}{d-1}}^{m}\big).

(33)

Moreover, as
X/Z
is a quotient of
X
, its dual is the subspace of
X^{*}=L_{1}/\mathscr{P}^{n}_{>k}
which is identified with the annihilator of
Z
inside
X^{\ast}
. In other words, it is the set

(X/Z)^{\ast}=\big\{f+\mathscr{P}_{>k}^{n}:\ \mathbb{E}[fg]=0\ \mbox{for every }g\in Z\big\}=\big\{f+\mathscr{P}_{>k}^{n}:\ f\in\mathscr{P}_{\leq d}^{n}\big\}=\mathrm{span}(\mathscr{P}_{\leq d}^{n}\cup\mathscr{P}_{>k}^{n})\big/\mathscr{P}_{>k}^{n}

(34)

equipped with the
L_{1}
quotient norm. Finally, for a sequence
\boldsymbol{a}=(a_{S})_{|S|\leq d}\in Y^{\ast}
and an equivalence class
f+Z\in X/Z
, we have

\langle\boldsymbol{a},A(f+Z)\rangle=\sumop\displaylimits_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}:\\
|S|\leq d\end{subarray}}a_{S}\widehat{f}(S)=\Big\langle\sumop\displaylimits_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}:\\
|S|\leq d\end{subarray}}a_{S}w_{S}+\mathscr{P}_{>k}^{n},f+Z\Big\rangle=\langle A^{*}(\boldsymbol{a}),f+Z\rangle,

(35)

where the first brackets
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denote the duality in
Y
and the following brackets denote the duality in
X/Z
. Therefore, we conclude that

\forall\ \boldsymbol{a}\in Y^{*},\qquad A^{*}(\boldsymbol{a})=\sumop\displaylimits_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}:\\
|S|\leq d\end{subarray}}a_{S}w_{S}+\mathscr{P}_{>k}^{n}

(36)

and thus, the condition
\|A^{*}\|\leq 1
means that for any
f:\{-1,1\}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
of degree at most
d
,

\inf_{g\in\mathscr{P}_{>k}^{n}}\|f-g\|_{1}=\big\|A^{*}\big(\widehat{f}\big)\big\|_{(X/Z)^{*}}\leq\big\|\widehat{f}\big\|_{Y^{*}}=\mathsf{K}\big(\widehat{f},\sigma(k,d);\ell_{2}^{m},\ell_{\frac{2d}{d-1}}^{m}\big).

(37)

Finally, since

\sigma(k,d)\leq\mathsf{B}_{d}\sumop\displaylimits_{\ell=0}^{d}|\tilde{c}(k,\ell)|\leq\mathsf{B}_{d}\sumop\displaylimits_{\ell=0}^{d}\frac{k^{\ell}}{\ell!}\leq e\mathsf{B}_{d}k^{d},

(38)

we deduce the conclusion of the theorem with
\mathsf{C}_{d}=e\mathsf{B}_{d}
.
∎

Remark 8
.

To the best of our knowledge, there are no nonconstant lower bounds on the size of the discrete Bohnenblust–Hille constant
\mathsf{B}_{d}
, so it is even conceivable that the constant
\mathsf{C}_{d}
in (
11
) can be chosen to be independent of
d
.

Remark 9
.

A duality argument similar to that employed for Theorem
3
shows that for every
d\in\mathbb{N}
, the constant
\mathsf{B}_{d}
in inequality (
25
) is also the least constant for which every function
f:\{-1,1\}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
, where
n\geq d
, satisfies

\inf_{g\in\mathscr{P}_{>d}^{n}}\|f-g\|_{1}\leq\mathsf{B}_{d}\bigg(\sumop\displaylimits_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}:\\
|S|\leq d\end{subarray}}|\widehat{f}(S)|^{\frac{2d}{d-1}}\bigg)^{\frac{d-1}{2d}}.

(39)

Remark 10
.

It was pointed out to us by Oleszkiewicz that the main result (
8
) of
[
19
]
also admits a dual formulation. Namely, for every
\boldsymbol{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

\inf\big\{\|f_{\boldsymbol{a}}-g\|_{\infty}:\ g\in\mathscr{P}_{\{0\}\cup\{2,\ldots,k\}}^{n}\big\}\asymp\max\Big\{\|\boldsymbol{a}\|_{\ell_{2}^{n}},\frac{\|\boldsymbol{a}\|_{\ell_{1}^{n}}}{k}\Big\}.

(40)

This can be proven using similar ideas as in the proof of Theorem
3
.

A slight variant of the arguments above also yields Theorem
4
for symmetric functions.

Proof of Theorem
4
.

Let
f
be a symmetric function of the form
f=\sumop\displaylimits_{\ell=0}^{d}\alpha_{\ell}f_{\ell}
where
f_{\ell}
is the
\ell
-th elementary symmetric polynomial. Then, the Hahn–Banach theorem gives

\inf_{g\in\mathscr{P}_{>k}^{n}}\|f-g\|_{1}=\sup_{0\neq h\in\mathscr{P}_{\leq k}^{n}}\frac{\mathbb{E}[fh]}{\|h\|_{\infty}}.

(41)

Observe now that we can write

\mathbb{E}[fh]=\sumop\displaylimits_{\ell=0}^{d}\alpha_{\ell}\mathbb{E}[f_{\ell}h]=\sumop\displaylimits_{\ell=0}^{d}\alpha_{\ell}\sumop\displaylimits_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}:\\
|S|=\ell\end{subarray}}\widehat{h}(S)=\sumop\displaylimits_{\ell=0}^{d}\alpha_{\ell}\mathrm{Rad}_{\ell}h(1,\ldots,1).

(42)

Thus, by Figiel’s bound (
27
),

\mathbb{E}[fh]\leq\sumop\displaylimits_{\ell=0}^{d}|\alpha_{\ell}|\ \|\mathrm{Rad}_{\ell}h\|_{\infty}\leq\sumop\displaylimits_{\ell=0}^{d}|\alpha_{\ell}||\tilde{c}(k,\ell)|\ \|h\|_{\infty}

(43)

and the desired inequality follows from (
41
).
∎

Equipped with Theorem
4
, we present the proof of Corollary
5
.

Proof of Corollary
5
.

The upper bound in (
16
) follows immediately from Theorem
4
. For the lower bound, consider the auxiliary symmetric function
H_{k,n}:\{-1,1\}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
given by

\forall\ x\in\{-1,1\}^{n},\qquad H_{k,n}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}T_{k}\left(\frac{x_{1}+\cdots+x_{n}}{n}\right)=\sumop\displaylimits_{\ell=0}^{k}\beta_{\ell,k,n}\ f_{\ell}(x),

(44)

where
f_{\ell}
is the
\ell
-th elementary symmetric polynomial, and notice that
H_{k,n}
has degree at most
k
. As
T_{k}(x)
is odd or even when
k
is odd or even respectively, it follows that
\beta_{\ell,k,n}=0
if
k-\ell
is odd. We distinguish two cases depending on the parity of
k-d
.

\bullet
Suppose that
k-d
is even and consider the function
\varphi_{d,k,n}:\{-1,1\}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
given by

\forall\ x\in\{-1,1\}^{n},\qquad\varphi_{d,k,n}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\sumop\displaylimits_{0\leq\ell\leq d:\ 2|d-\ell}\mathrm{sign}(\beta_{\ell,k,n})f_{\ell}(x)

(45)

that is also symmetric and of degree at most
d
. Then, on one hand we know that

\inf_{g\in\mathscr{P}_{>k}^{n}}\|\varphi_{d,k,n}-g\|_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:sym}}}{{\leq}}\sumop\displaylimits_{0\leq\ell\leq d:\ 2|d-\ell}|\tilde{c}(k,\ell)|=\sumop\displaylimits_{0\leq\ell\leq d:\ 2|d-\ell}|c(k,\ell)|.

(46)

On the other hand, we have the following lower estimate,

\inf_{g\in\mathscr{P}_{>k}^{n}}\|\varphi_{d,k,n}-g\|_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:hb}}}{{=}}\sup_{0\neq h\in\mathscr{P}_{\leq k}^{n}}\frac{\mathbb{E}[\varphi_{d,k,n}h]}{\|h\|_{\infty}}\geq\frac{|\mathbb{E}[\varphi_{d,k,n}H_{k,n}]|}{\|H_{k,n}\|_{\infty}}.

(47)

By definition,
\|H_{k,n}\|_{\infty}\leq\sup_{x\in[-1,1]}|T_{k}(x)|\leq 1
and
H_{k,n}(1,\ldots,1)=T_{k}(1)=1
. Therefore,

\begin{split}\inf_{g\in\mathscr{P}_{>k}^{n}}\|\varphi_{d,k,n}-g\|_{1}\geq|\mathbb{E}[\varphi_{d,k,n}H_{k,n}]|&=\bigg|\sumop\displaylimits_{0\leq\ell\leq d:\ 2|d-\ell}\mathrm{sign}(\beta_{\ell,k,n})\mathrm{Rad}_{\ell}H_{k,n}(1,\dots,1)\bigg|\\
&=\sumop\displaylimits_{0\leq\ell\leq d:\ 2|d-\ell}\left|\mathrm{Rad}_{\ell}H_{k,n}(1,\dots,1)\right|.\end{split}

(48)

To further estimate this sum, we use
[
14
, Lemma 27]
which implies that there exists a positive constant
\varepsilon_{n}(k,d)>0
with
\varepsilon_{n}(k,d)=O_{k,d}(1/n)
as
n\to\infty
, such that

\sumop\displaylimits_{0\leq\ell\leq d:\ 2|d-\ell}\left|\mathrm{Rad}_{\ell}H_{k,n}(1,\dots,1)\right|\geq\sumop\displaylimits_{0\leq\ell\leq d:\ 2|d-\ell}|c(k,\ell)|-\varepsilon_{n}(k,d).

(49)

Hence, combining the above we conclude that

\inf_{g\in\mathscr{P}_{>k}^{n}}\|\varphi_{d,k,n}-g\|_{1}\geq\sumop\displaylimits_{0\leq\ell\leq d:\ 2|d-\ell}|c(k,\ell)|-\varepsilon_{n}(k,d).

(50)

Finally, to bound from below the
L_{1}
-distance of
f_{d}
from the tail space, we write (putting
\varphi_{0,k,n}=\varphi_{-1,k,n}\equiv 0
)

f_{d}=\mathrm{sign}(\beta_{d,k,n})(\varphi_{d,k,n}-\varphi_{d-2,k,n})

(51)

and using the triangle inequality, we get

\begin{split}\inf_{g\in\mathscr{P}_{>k}^{n}}&\left\|f_{d}-g\right\|_{1}\geq\inf_{g\in\mathscr{P}_{>k}^{n}}\left\|\varphi_{d,k,n}-g\right\|_{1}-\inf_{g\in\mathscr{P}_{>k}^{n}}\left\|\varphi_{d-2,k,n}-g\right\|_{1}\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{ubb}\wedge\eqref{ineq: sharp2}}}{{\geq}}\sumop\displaylimits_{0\leq\ell\leq d:\ 2|d-\ell}|c(k,\ell)|-\varepsilon_{n}(k,d)-\sumop\displaylimits_{0\leq\ell\leq d-2:\ 2|d-2-\ell}|c(k,\ell)|=|c(k,d)|-\varepsilon_{n}(k,d),\end{split}

(52)

thus concluding the proof of the lower bound in (
16
).

\bullet
If
k-d
is odd, we use the identity (putting
\varphi_{0,k,n}=\varphi_{-1,k,n}=\varphi_{d,-1,n}\equiv 0
)

f_{d}=\mathrm{sign}(\beta_{d,k-1,n})(\varphi_{d,k-1,n}-\varphi_{d-2,k-1,n}).

(53)

The rest of the argument is identical.
∎

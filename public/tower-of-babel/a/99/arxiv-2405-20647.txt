2.1

Let

An element

(I^{n+1}:x)\cap I^{c}=I^{n}.

If depth

Superficial elements exist if the residue field is infinite, see

2.2
.

A filtration of ideals

is said to be
I
-filtration if it satisfies the following conditions:

(1)

I_{0}=A
and
I_{n+1}\subseteq I_{n}
for all

(2)

I\subseteq I_{1}
and

(3)

.

In addition, if

then the

-filtration

is called as an

-stable filtration.

It is well known that if

-stable filtration, then the Rees algebra

is a finite module over

.

A filtration of submodules

-filtration if it satisfies the following conditions:

(1)

for all

.

(2)

IM_{n}\subseteq M_{n+1}
for all

In addition, if

-filtration

-stable filtration.

2.4
.

3.
Polynomial growth of finitely generated Rees modules

This section assumes that
M_{\mathfrak{p}}
is free for all
\mathfrak{p}\neq\mathfrak{m}
and the ring
(A,\mathfrak{m})
has positive depth
. See
[
16
, section 6]
for an example of such modules. We prove that if the associated graded ring of
A
with respect to
I
is unmixed and equidimensional, then the functions

n\mapsto\lambda(\overline{I^{n}}/I^{n})\quad\text{and}\quad n\mapsto\lambda((I^{n})^{*}/I^{n})

are of polynomial type, and they attain their maximal degree whenever these functions are nonzero, where
\overline{I^{n}}
and
(I^{n})^{*}
are integral closure and tight closure, respectively, of
I^{n}
for all
n\geq 1
.

The authors proved the following theorem in
[
16
, Theorem 6.7]
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
I
be a regular ideal of
A
. Then the function
n\to\lambda(r(I^{n},M)/\widetilde{I^{n}})
is of polynomial type. Furthermore, if
A^{*}(I)\cap\mathfrak{m}{\operatorname{Spec}}(A)=\phi
then
r(I^{n},M)=\widetilde{I^{n}}
for all
n\geq 1
.

The following fact is well known. We give proof for the convenience of the reader.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a local ring. Let
\mathcal{F}=\{I_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
and
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}=\{J_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
be two
I
-stable filtration of ideals of
A
such that
J_{n}\subseteq I_{n}
for all
n\geq 1
. Set
E=\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{F})/\mathcal{R}{(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})}=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}I_{n}/J_{n}
. If
\lambda(E_{n})<\infty
for all
n\geq 1
then the function
n\mapsto\lambda(E_{n})
is coincides with a polynomial of degree at most
\ell(I)-1
for all
n\gg 0.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
are
I
-stable filtrations, the Rees algebras
\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{F})
and
\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
are finitely generated
\mathcal{R}
-modules. It follows that
E
is a finitely generated
\mathcal{R}
-module. Since
\lambda(E_{n})<\infty
for all
n\geq 1
, there exists
s\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathfrak{m}^{s}E=0.
Set
B_{i}=\mathfrak{m}^{i}E/\mathfrak{m}^{i+1}E
. It is clear that
B_{i}
is a finitely generated graded
\mathcal{R}/\mathfrak{m}\mathcal{R}
-module. Therefore,
\lambda((B_{i})_{n})
coincides with the polynomial of degree at most
\ell(I)-1
for
n\gg 0.
Now, we have the following filtration of submodules of
E

E\supseteq\mathfrak{m}E\supseteq\mathfrak{m}^{2}E\supseteq\ldots\supseteq\mathfrak{m}^{s-1}E\supseteq 0\ldots.

It follows that
\lambda(E_{n})=\sum_{i=0}^{s-1}\lambda(\mathfrak{m}^{i}E_{n}/\mathfrak{m}^{i+1}E_{n})
. Therefore, the function
n\mapsto\lambda(E_{n})
coincides with a polynomial of degree at most
\ell(I)-1
for all
n\gg 0.

∎

To establish the main theorem, we require the following result, whose proof is similar to that of
[
15
, Proposition 5.6]
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
I
be an ideal of
A
,
\mathcal{F}=\{I_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
be an
I
-stable filtration of
A
. Set
\mathcal{R}=A[It]
. Then

\operatorname{Ass}_{\mathcal{R}}({G}_{\mathcal{F}}(A))=\operatorname{Ass}_{\mathcal{R}}({L}^{\mathcal{F}}(A)).

Proof.

Set
G={G}_{\mathcal{F}}(A)
and
L={L}^{\mathcal{F}}(A)
.
Since
G\subseteq L
, we can conclude that
\operatorname{Ass}_{\mathcal{R}}(G)\subseteq\operatorname{Ass}_{\mathcal{R}}(L)
.
Let
L_{s}
be an
{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{F})
-submodule of
L
defined as follows:

L_{s}=\left\langle\bigoplus_{n=0}^{s}\dfrac{A}{{I_{n+1}}}\right\rangle.

It can be easily checked that the
n
-th graded component of
L_{s}
is

(L_{s})_{n}=\begin{cases}\dfrac{A}{I_{n+1}},&\text{if }n\leq s,\\[8.0pt]
\dfrac{I_{\,n-s}}{I_{\,n+1}},&\text{if }n>s.\end{cases}

Note that
L_{s}
is a finitely generated
{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{F})
-module and so of
\mathcal{R}
-module. It is clear that
L_{s}\subseteq L_{s+1}
for all
s\geq 0
, and
L=\bigcup_{s\geq 0}L_{s}
. Thus,

\operatorname{Ass}_{\mathcal{R}}(L)=\bigcup_{s\geq 0}\operatorname{Ass}_{\mathcal{R}}(L_{s}).

Also note that
L_{0}=G
. For all
s\geq 0
, we have an exact sequence

0\to L_{s-1}\to L_{s}\to G(-s)\to 0.

For the case where
s=1
, we obtain the exact sequence

0\to G\to L_{1}\to G(-1)\to 0.

This implies that
\operatorname{Ass}_{\mathcal{R}}(L_{1})\subseteq\operatorname{Ass}_{\mathcal{R}}(G)
. Continuing this process, we obtain
\operatorname{Ass}_{\mathcal{R}}(L_{s})\subseteq\operatorname{Ass}_{\mathcal{R}}(G)
for all
s\geq 1.
Therefore,
\operatorname{Ass}_{\mathcal{R}}(L)\subseteq\operatorname{Ass}_{\mathcal{R}}(G)
.
Hence
\operatorname{Ass}_{\mathcal{R}}(G)=\operatorname{Ass}_{\mathcal{R}}(L)
.
∎

The next result is a key theorem of this paper.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a Noetherian local ring of dimension

and
I
be an ideal of
A
. Let
\mathcal{F}=\{I_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}

\mathcal{F}^{\prime}=\{J_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
be two
I
-stable filtrations of
A
such that
J_{n}\subseteq I_{n}
for all
n\geq 1
. Set
E=\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{F})/\mathcal{R}{(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})}
. Assume that
\lambda(E_{n})<\infty
for all
n\geq 1
. If

is unmixed and equidimensional, then either

or the function

coincides with a polynomial of degree

for all

Moreover, if

then

Proof.

Note that
E
is a finitely generated
\mathcal{R}
-module. If
E=0
, then we have nothing to show. Suppose

The short exact sequence

yields

\operatorname{Ass}_{\mathcal{R}}(E)\subseteq\operatorname{Ass}_{\mathcal{R}}({L}^{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}(A))=\operatorname{Ass}_{\mathcal{R}}({G}_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}(A))

and
\operatorname{Min}_{\mathcal{R}}(E)\subseteq\operatorname{Ass}_{\mathcal{R}}(E)\subseteq\operatorname{Ass}_{\mathcal{R}}({G}_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}(A)).

Since

Example 3.13

Let

Lemma 4.1

Since localization commutes with taking radicals. Thus,

5.
Lower bounds to
e^{*}_{1}(I)
and
\overline{e}_{1}({I})

In this section, we provide lower bounds for
e^{*}_{1}(I)
and
\overline{e}_{1}({I})
in terms of the local cohomology of
A
. We also discuss the consequences when it attains its lower bound. To do this, we need to recall the following:

5.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}=\{I_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
be an
I
-stable filtration, where
I
is a
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal of a Noetherian local ring
A
. Then the Rees algebra
\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{F})=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}I_{n}t^{n}
is a finitely generated
\mathcal{R}
-module. Consequently, the associated graded ring
G_{\mathcal{F}}(A)=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}I_{n}/I_{n+1}
is a finitely generated graded module over the associated graded ring
G_{I}(A)
. Therefore, the Hilbert-Samuel function defined by
H_{\mathcal{F}}(n)=\lambda(A/(I_{n+1})
coincides with a polynomial,
P_{\mathcal{F}}(n)
, of degree
d=\text{dim }A
for all
n\gg 0.
The polynomial can be expressed as:

P_{\mathcal{F}}(n)=e_{0}({\mathcal{F}})\binom{n+d}{d}-e_{1}({\mathcal{F}})\binom{n+d-1}{d-1}+\ldots+(-1)^{d}e_{d}({\mathcal{F}}),

where
e_{i}({\mathcal{F}})\in\mathbb{Z}
and it is called as the
i
-th Hilbert coefficient of
G_{\mathcal{F}}(A)
.

In particular:

(1)

For the filtration
\{\overline{I^{n}}\}_{n\geq 0}
, the coefficients
\overline{e}_{i}(I)
are called the
i
-th normal Hilbert coefficients (when
A
is analytically unramified).

(2)

For the filtration
\{(I^{n})^{*}\}_{n\geq 0}
, the coefficients
e_{i}^{*}(I)
are called the tight Hilbert coefficients (when
A
is analytically unramified of characteristic
p>0
).

(3)

For the
I
-adic filtration
\mathcal{F}=\{I^{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
, we denote the coefficients by
e_{i}(I)
, the
i
-th Hilbert coefficients of
G_{I}(A)
.

It is well known that
e_{0}(I)=e^{*}_{0}(I)=\overline{e}_{0}({I}).

5.2
.

The ring
(A,\mathfrak{m})
of dimension
d
is said to be generalized Cohen-Macaulay if

\lambda(H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(A))<\infty
for all
i=0,\ldots,d-1
, where
H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(A)
is
i

The following result examines the implications of the equality
\overline{e}_{1}(I)=e_{1}(I).
A similar result was established in
[
11
, Theorem 1]
.

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay and analytically unramified local ring of dimension
d\geq 1
. Let
I
be a system of parameters. If
\overline{e}_{1}(I)=e_{1}(I)
, then
A
is a regular local ring.

Proof.

For each
n\geq 0
, we have the following short exact sequence

0\to\overline{I^{n+1}}/I^{n+1}\to A/I^{n+1}\to A/\overline{I^{n+1}}\to 0.

This induces

0\to E\to L^{I}(A)\to L^{\overline{I}}(A)\to 0,

where
E=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\overline{I^{n+1}}/I^{n+1}
,
L^{I}=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}A/I^{n+1}
and
L^{\overline{I}}=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}A/\overline{I^{n+1}}.
This implies for sufficiently large
n
,

\lambda(\overline{I^{n+1}}/I^{n+1})=(\overline{e}_{1}({I})-e_{1}(I))\binom{n+d-1}{d-1}+\text{ lower degree terms}.

Since
A
is Cohen-Macaulay and
I
is a system of parameters,
I
is generated by a regular sequence. Therefore,
G_{I}(A)
is Cohen-Macaulay. By Theorem
3.9
, either
\overline{I^{n}}=I^{n}
for all
n\geq 1
, or the function
n\mapsto\lambda(\overline{I^{n}}/I^{n})
coincides with a polynomial of degree
d-1
for all
n\gg 0
. Since
\overline{e}_{1}(I)=e_{1}(I)
, it follows that
\overline{I^{n}}=I^{n}
for all
n\geq 1
. The result then follows from Theorem 1.1 of
[
7
]
.
∎

The following result explores the implications of the equality
{e}^{*}_{1}(I)=e_{1}(I).
A related result was established in
[
5
, Corollary 4.6]
, where the authors first reduced the problem to the Cohen–Macaulay case and then proved it.

Theorem 5.8
.

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay and analytically unramified local ring of dimension
d\geq 1
with characteristics
p>0
. Let
I
be a system of parameters. If
{e}^{*}_{1}(I)=e_{1}(I)
, then
A
is an
F
-rational ring.

Proof.

By the same arguments in Theorem
5.7
, we obtain that
G_{I}(A)
is Cohen-Macaulay and for sufficiently large
n
,

\lambda(({I^{n+1})^{*}}/I^{n+1})=({e}^{*}_{1}({I})-e_{1}(I))\binom{n+d-1}{d-1}+\text{ lower degree terms}.

Since
e^{*}_{1}({I})=e_{1}(I)
, it follows that from Corollary
3.12
that
(I^{n})^{*}=I^{n}
for
n\geq 1
. The result then follows from
[
2
, Proposition 10.3.5]
.
∎

Remark 5.9
.

[
19
, Lemma 1.2(iv)]

If
(A,\mathfrak{m})
is a generalized Cohen-Macaulay local ring having positive depth, then
A
is unmixed and equidimensional.

Theorem 5.10
.

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a generalized Cohen-Macaulay and analytically unramified local ring of dimension
d\geq 1
. Let
I
be a standard system of parameters. Then,

(1)

\overline{e}_{1}({I})\geq-\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}\binom{d-2}{j-1}\lambda(H^{j}_{\mathfrak{m}}(A))\right).

(2)

If the characteristic of
A
is
p>0
,

e^{*}_{1}(I)\geq-\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}\binom{d-2}{j-1}\lambda(H^{j}_{\mathfrak{m}}(A))\right).

If equality holds, then in the first case,
A
is a regular local ring and in the second case,
A
is an
F
-rational provided
A
is a homomorphic image of a Cohen-Macaulay local ring.

Proof.

By Theorem
4.1
in
[
19
]
, we have

\begin{split}\lambda(A/I^{n+1})=&\binom{n+d}{d}e_{0}(I)+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{j=0}^{d-i}\binom{n+d-i}{d-i}\binom{d-i-1}{j-1}\lambda(H^{j}_{\mathfrak{m}}(A))\\
=&\binom{n+d}{d}e_{0}(I)+\binom{n+d-1}{d-1}\left[\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\binom{d-2}{j-1}\lambda(H^{j}_{\mathfrak{m}}(A))\right]+\text{lower degree term.}\end{split}

for all
n\geq 0,
where
\binom{d-i-1}{-1}=0
if
i\neq d
and
\binom{-1}{-1}=1.
Since
A
is analytically unramified, it has positive depth and by comparing the Hilbert coefficients, we obtain

-e_{1}(I)=\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}\binom{d-2}{j-1}\lambda(H^{j}_{\mathfrak{m}}(A)).

On the other hand, since
A
is a generalized Cohen-Macaulay local ring with positive depth, and by
[
19
, Theorem 5.4]
,
G_{I}(A)
is a generalized Cohen-Macaulay ring with positive depth. Therefore, by Remark
5.9
,
G_{I}(A)
is unmixed and equidimensional. From the proof of Theorem
5.7
and Theorem
5.8
, we obtain

\overline{e}_{1}({I})\geq\,e_{1}(I)=-\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}\binom{d-2}{j-1}\lambda(H^{j}_{\mathfrak{m}}(A))\right)

and if the characteristic of
A
is
p>0
,

e^{*}_{1}(I)\geq\,e_{1}(I)=-\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}\binom{d-2}{j-1}\lambda(H^{j}_{\mathfrak{m}}(A))\right).

Hence, by Theorem
5.7
and Theorem
5.8
, if equality holds, then in the first case
A
is a regular local ring, and in the second case
A
is an
F
-rational ring.
∎

Introduction to the problem

In this paper we consider the equation

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{\frac{n}{2}}u=(n-1)!e^{nu}\quad\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{n}$},

(1)

where
n\geq 1
and

\displaystyle V:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{nu}dx<\infty.

(2)

The operator
(-\Delta)^{\frac{n}{2}}
can be defined as follows. For
s>0
we set

\widehat{(-\Delta)^{s}\varphi}(\xi):=|\xi|^{2s}\hat{\varphi}(\xi),\quad\text{for }\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

where

\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):=\left\{u\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x|^{N}|D^{\alpha}u(x)|<\infty\text{ for all }N\in\mathbb{N}\text{ and }\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}\right\}

is the Schwartz space.
Define the space

L_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):=\left\{v\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|v(x)|}{1+|x|^{n+2s}}dx<\infty\right\}.

Then we have the following definition:

Definition 1.1

Let
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
A function
u\in L_{\frac{n}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is said to be a solution of

(-\Delta)^{\frac{n}{2}}u=f\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{n},

if

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u(-\Delta)^{\frac{n}{2}}\varphi dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f\varphi dx,\text{ for every }\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

(3)

where the integral on the left-hand side of (
3
) is well-defined thanks to Proposition
A.2
in the appendix.

Geometrically if
u
is a smooth solution of (
1
)-(
2
) then the conformal metric

on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(

) has constant

-curvature equal to
(n-1)!
. Moreover the volume and the total
Q
-curvature of the metric

are

respectively.

It is well-known that

is a solution of (

)-(

) with

W. Chen-C. Li
[

]
showed that these are the only solutions to (

)-(
2

:

[

]

Let

Moreover J. Wei and D. Ye

]
,

Let

When

Extending the results of

Lemma 2.2

For every positive integer

\displaystyle u_{0}(x)=\log\frac{1}{|x|}\quad\text{for }\,|x|\geq 1\text{ and }\quad|D^{\alpha}(-\Delta)^{\frac{n}{2}}u_{0}(x)|\leq\frac{C}{|x|^{2n+k+|\alpha|}}\quad\text{for }x\neq 0,

Lemma 2.3

Let

Thus a minimizing sequence

of

is bounded in
{H^{\frac{n}{2}}({S^{n}})}

converges weakly to

.
From the compactness of the map

from
{H^{\frac{n}{2}}({S^{n}})}

for any

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{\frac{n}{2}}w=(-\Delta)^{\frac{n}{2}}(u\circ\pi^{-1})

\displaystyle=e^{nw_{0}}\left(C_{0}\tilde{K}e^{-nw_{0}\circ\pi}e^{nu}-\alpha\tilde{\varphi_{1}}\right)\circ\pi^{-1}

Lemma A.1

(Fundamental solution)

For
n\geq 3
odd integer the function

\Phi(x):=\frac{(\frac{n-3}{2})!}{2\pi^{\frac{n+1}{2}}}\frac{1}{|x|^{n-1}}=\frac{1}{\gamma_{n}}(-\Delta)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\log\frac{1}{|x|}

is a fundamental solution of
(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
in
the sense that for all

f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
we have
\Phi\ast f\in L_{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Phi\ast f)\varphi dx:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\Phi\ast f)(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}\varphi dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f\varphi dx.

(16)

Proof.

To show
\Phi*f\in L_{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
we bound

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\Phi*f(x)|}{1+|x|^{n+1}}dx

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{1+|x|^{n+1}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n-1}}|f(y)|dy\right)dx

\displaystyle=C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(y)|\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{1+|x|^{n+1}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n-1}}dx\right)dy

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(y)|\left(\int_{B_{1}}\frac{dx}{|x|^{n-1}}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{dx}{1+|x|^{n+1}}\right)dy

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

(17)

If
f\in\ C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
then (
16
) is true by Theorem 5.9 in
[
9
]
. For the general case
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
choose
f_{k}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that

f_{k}\to f
in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then using (
17
) with
f\equiv f_{k}-f
one has

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\Phi\ast(f_{k}-f)||(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}\varphi|dx\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\Phi\ast(f_{k}-f)(x)|}{1+|x|^{n+1}}dx\leq C\|f_{k}-f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\to 0,

that is

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\Phi\ast f_{k})(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}\varphi dx\to\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\Phi\ast f)(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}\varphi dx.

Now the proof follows from

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\Phi\ast f_{k})(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}\varphi dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f_{k}\varphi dx\to\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f\varphi dx.

∎

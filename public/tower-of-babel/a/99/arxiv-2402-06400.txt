Abstract
. We consider the cubic Schrödinger equation on the line, for which the scattering theory requires modifications due to long range effects. We revisit the construction of the modified wave operator, and recall the construction of its inverse, in order to describe the asymptotic behavior of these operators near the origin. At leading order, these operators, whose definition includes a nonlinear modification in the phase compared to the linear dynamics, correspond to the identity. We compute explicitly the first corrector in the asymptotic expansion, and justify this expansion by error estimates.



1.
Introduction

We consider the cubic Schrödinger equation on the line,

(1.1)

i{\partial}_{t}u+\frac{1}{2}{\partial}_{x}^{2}u=\lambda|u|^{2}u,\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}},

with
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
. It is well known that in terms of scattering,
this equation corresponds to a borderline case, where long range
effects appear (see
[
1
]
). The goal of this paper is to
analyze the modified scattering map near the origin, as well as the
modified wave operator and its inverse. We first recall a few aspects
of scattering theory for the nonlinear Schrödinger equation in the short
range case, then turn to the specificities of this long range setting.

1.1.
Short range scattering

Denote by
U(t)
the Schrödinger group,

U(t):=e^{i\frac{t}{2}{\partial}_{x}^{2}}.

In view of the explicit formula for
U(t)
as a convolution operator,
we have

(1.2)

U(t)f(x)\mathop{\sim}\limits_{t\rightarrow\pm\infty}e^{i\frac{x^{2}}{2t}}\frac{1}{(it)^{1/2}}\widehat{f}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right),

where we normalize the Fourier transform as follows,

{\mathcal{F}}f(\xi)=\widehat{f}(\xi)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}f(x)e^{-ix\xi}dx.

See Lemma
2.2
for a more precise statement regarding
(
1.2
).
Consider, for the
sake of comparison with (
1.1
), the case of a quintic,
defocusing nonlinearity

(1.3)

i{\partial}_{t}u+\frac{1}{2}{\partial}_{x}^{2}u=|u|^{4}u,\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}.

The discussion for, e.g., the two-dimensional or the three-dimensional
cubic Schrödinger equation would be similar. Given

(1.4)

u_{-}\in\Sigma:=\left\{f\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}});\ \|f\|_{\Sigma}:=\|f\|_{L^{2}}+\|{\partial}_{x}f\|_{L^{2}}+\|xf\|_{L^{2}}<\infty\right\},

there exists a unique
u_{0}\in\Sigma=H^{1}\cap\mathcal{F}(H^{1})
such that the
(unique, global)
solution
u
to (
1.3
) with
u_{\mid t=0}=u_{0}

satisfies

\|U(-t)u(t)-u_{-}\|_{\Sigma}\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{t\rightarrow-\infty}0.

We recall that
U(t)
is unitary on
H^{1}
, but not on
\mathcal{F}(H^{1})
, this
is why the quantity measured above is not
u(t)-U(t)u_{-}
.
The map
u_{-}\mapsto u_{0}
is classically referred to as
wave
operator
(see e.g.
[
7
]
).

Conversely, given
u_{0}\in\Sigma
, there exists
u_{+}\in\Sigma
such
that

\|U(-t)u(t)-u_{+}\|_{\Sigma}\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{t\rightarrow+\infty}0.

The map
u_{-}\mapsto u_{+}
is called the
scattering operator
. It
can be defined for other (defocusing) nonlinearities, but strictly
supercubic, in view of
[
1
]
, where it is proved that,
typically for (
1.1
), it is not possible to compare the
nonlinear dynamics with the linear one for large time (see also
[
7
]
). We will see in the next
subsection that in the cubic case (
1.1
), nontrivial long range
effects must be taken into account, and that these effects are explicit.

In general, rather little is known regarding properties of the
scattering map
S
. For instance, it is proven in
[
3
]
that
for smooth, power-like nonlinearities such that short range scattering
is known, the wave and scattering operators are analytic. The formula
for the associated Taylor series is given, and the formula differs
whether the expansion is considered at the origin or at a nontrivial
state. Such asymptotic expansions (not necessarily in the analytic
case) have proven useful in the context of inverse problems, see
e.g.
[
21
,
22
]
and references therein.

1.2.
Long range case: modified scattering

The picture is different in the case of (
1.1
): the nonlinear
dynamics cannot be compared to the linear one, unless one considers
the trivial case
u\equiv 0
(
[
1
]
), so a modified scattering
theory has been developed in order to describe the large time behavior
of
u
, sharing some similarities with the linear case, presented in
e.g.
[
6
]
.

Given asymptotic states
u_{-}
and
u_{+}
, we introduce the long range phase
corrections (different whether
t\to-\infty
of
t\to+\infty
),

(1.5)

S_{\pm}(t,x):=\mp\lambda\left|\widehat{u}_{\pm}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)\right|^{2}\log|t|.

Loosely speaking, the existence of modified wave operator reads as
follows: given
u_{-}
(sufficiently small), there exists a
solution
u
to (
1.1
) such that

u(t,x)\mathop{\sim}\limits_{t\rightarrow-\infty}e^{iS_{-}(t,x)}U(t)u_{-}(x)\mathop{\sim}\limits_{t\rightarrow-\infty}e^{iS_{-}(t,x)+i\frac{x^{2}}{2t}}\frac{1}{(it)^{1/2}}\widehat{u}_{-}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right),

where the second approximation stems from (
1.2
). Note
that the phase modification
S_{-}
is by no means negligible
as
t\to-\infty
: it accounts for long range effects, as established
initially in
[
28
]
. We denote by
u_{\mid t=0}={W_{-}^{\rm mod}}(u_{-})
the
modified wave operator.

The modified asymptotic completeness is similar: given
u_{0}

(sufficiently small), there exists
u_{+}
such that the solution
u
to
(
1.1
) with
u_{\mid t=0}=u_{0}
satisfies

u(t)\mathop{\sim}\limits_{t\rightarrow+\infty}e^{iS_{+}(t)}U(t)u_{+}.

Such a result was proven initially in
[
15
]
.
The modified scattering map is given by
{S^{\rm mod}}(u_{-})=u_{+}
. At this stage,
we have not addressed the function spaces in which the above
asymptotics have been proven.

In
[
28
]
, the existence of modified wave operators was
established for
u_{-}\in\mathcal{F}(H^{2})
, with
\|\widehat{u}_{-}\|_{L^{\infty}}

sufficiently small, and the solution
u
to (
1.1
) has an

L^{2}
regularity,
u_{0}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
. We emphasize, as the notation will
be used many times, that the space
\mathcal{F}(H^{s})
is characterized by

\mathcal{F}(H^{s})=\{f\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}(R),\ \|f\|_{\mathcal{F}(H^{s})}^{2}:=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\left\langle x\right\rangle^{2s}|f(x)|^{2}dx<\infty\},\quad\left\langle x\right\rangle=\sqrt{1+x^{2}}.

The result of
[
28
]
also addresses the case where
u_{-}\in{\mathcal{H}}

(defined below, see (
1.6
)) where, provided again that

\|\widehat{u}_{-}\|_{L^{\infty}}

sufficiently small, the solution
u
to (
1.1
) has an

H^{1}
regularity, and convergence holds in this space.

In
[
15
]
, the asymptotic completeness was proven for
u_{0}\in H^{\gamma}\cap\mathcal{F}(H^{\gamma})
with
\gamma>1/2
and

\|u_{0}\|_{H^{\gamma}\cap\mathcal{F}(H^{\gamma})}
sufficiently small. The obtained
asymptotic state
u_{+}
is such that
\widehat{u}_{+}\in L^{2}\cap L^{\infty}
.

Denote

(1.6)

\displaystyle{{\mathcal{H}}}

\displaystyle:=\{f\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}});\ \left\langle x\right\rangle{\partial}_{x}f,\left\langle x\right\rangle^{3}f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})\}

\displaystyle=\{f\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}});\|f\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}:=\|\left\langle x\right\rangle{\partial}_{x}f\|_{L^{2}}+\|\left\langle x\right\rangle^{3}f\|_{L^{2}}<\infty\}.

In
[
2
]
, the main result of
[
28
]
was adapted for

u_{-}\in{\mathcal{H}}
with
\|\widehat{u}_{-}\|_{L^{\infty}}

sufficiently small, and the regularity of the solution
u
to
(
1.1
) was proven to be at least
\Sigma
,
hence
u_{0}\in\Sigma
, making it possible to connect this
result with the asymptotic completeness from
[
15
]
, thus
defining a map
u_{-}\mapsto u_{+}
from (a subset of)
{\mathcal{H}}
to

L^{2}\cap L^{\infty}
. When invoking the modified scattering operator, we
refer to this notion.

This gap in regularity between
u_{-}
and
u_{+}
was considerably
diminished in
[
16
]
, where, with the same notations as above,
the authors consider the setting (along with smallness
conditions)

u_{-}\in\mathcal{F}(H^{\alpha}),\quad u_{0}\in\mathcal{F}(H^{\beta}),\quad u_{+}\in\mathcal{F}(H^{\delta}),

with the constraints
1/2<\delta<\beta<\alpha<1
, allowing these three
indices to be arbitrarily close one from another. This is
achieved by adapting the notion of (modified) asymptotic completeness
in order to avoid loss of differentiability issues, in space
dimensions two and three, where the borderline nonlinearity in terms
of scattering is
|u|^{2/d}u
.

We also emphasize that there are many references addressing the theory
of long range scattering for nonlinear Schrödinger equations
(e.g.
[
4
,
18
,
19
,
24
,
25
,
26
,
27
]
), as
well as for
Schrödinger-like equations, like Hartree
equation (e.g.
[
8
,
12
,
13
,
29
]
), derivative nonlinear Schrödinger equation
(e.g.
[
17
,
14
]
), Maxwell-Schrödinger system (e.g.
[
10
,
9
]
),
wave-Schrödinger system (e.g.
[
11
]
), not to mention other
dispersive equations.

We note that the very definition of the modified wave and scattering
operators encodes the fact that the nonlinearity is cubic, and
recovering the nonlinearity from the scattering map does not make
sense, contrary to the case of
[
21
,
22
]
. In the case
where
\lambda
is allowed to depend on
x
in a somehow perturbative
way, Chen and Murphy
[
5
]
showed that the inverse of the
modified wave operator uniquely determines
\lambda
. One of the tools
of the proof there is to study the behavior of this operator near the
origin, thanks to a rather implicit expansion
(
[
5
, Proposition 4.1]
). We present a
more explicit formula in the next subsection, when

\lambda
is constant.

To fix the ideas, we summarize the above discussion by introducing the
following definition, where regularity aspects are left out for
simplicity.

Definition 1.1
.

Given
u_{-}
, the modified wave operator acting on
u_{-}
is given by

u_{\mid t=0}={W_{-}^{\rm mod}}(u_{-})
, where
u
solves (
1.1
), and
satisfies

u(t,x)=e^{iS_{-}(t,x)}U(t)u_{-}(x)+o(1)\text{ in }L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})\text{ as }t\to-\infty,

where
S_{-}
is defined in (
1.5
).

The modified scattering
operator is defined by
{S^{\rm mod}}(u_{-})=u_{+}
if the above solution
u

satisfies in addition

u(t,x)=e^{iS_{+}(t,x)}U(t)u_{+}(x)+o(1)\text{ in }L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})\text{ as }t\to+\infty,

where
S_{+}
is defined in (
1.5
).

1.3.
Main result

In the same spirit as what has been achieved for the wave and
scattering operators in the short range case, we consider the
asymptotic behavior of the modified wave and scattering operators,
with two restrictions compared to
[
3
]
. First, we shall
confine ourselves to the asymptotic expansion near the origin. Second, we
compute only the first two terms of this asymptotic expansion. We will
see that this already requires some amount of work, but that the
same method should provide some, if not all,
higher order terms in the expansion at the origin. On the other hand,
the description of these operators near a nontrivial state certainly
requires a different approach.

We emphasize that the question addressed here is different from the
(higher order) asymptotic expansion of the large time
behavior of
u(t)
, as studied in
[
23
]
.
Our main results are gathered in the
following statement:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
v_{-}\in{\mathcal{H}}
and
v_{0}\in\Sigma
.

•

For any
0<\eta<2
, we have, in
\Sigma
and as
\varepsilon\to 0
,

{W_{-}^{\rm mod}}(\varepsilon v_{-})=\varepsilon v_{-}+\varepsilon^{3}w_{2}+\mathcal{O}\left(\varepsilon^{5-\eta}\right),

where
w_{2}\in\Sigma
is defined by

\displaystyle w_{2}

\displaystyle=-i\lambda\int_{-\infty}^{-1}\left(U(-\tau)\left(|U(\tau)v_{-}|^{2}U(\tau)v_{-}\right)+\frac{1}{|\tau|}\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(|\widehat{v}_{-}|^{2}\widehat{v}_{-}\right)\right)d\tau

\displaystyle\quad-i\lambda\int_{-1}^{0}U(-\tau)\left(|U(\tau)v_{-}|^{2}U(\tau)v_{-}\right)d\tau.

•

For any
0<\eta<2
, we have, in
L^{2}
and as

\varepsilon\to 0
,

\left(W_{+}^{\rm mod}\right)^{-1}(\varepsilon v_{0})=\varepsilon v_{0}+\varepsilon^{3}\mu_{2}+\mathcal{O}\left(\varepsilon^{5-\eta}\right),

where
\mu_{2}=\mu_{2}(v_{0})\in L^{2}
is defined by

\displaystyle\mu_{2}

\displaystyle=-i\lambda\int_{0}^{1}\left(U(-s)\left(|U(s)v_{0}|^{2}U(s)v_{0}\right)\right)ds

\displaystyle\quad+\lambda\int_{1}^{\infty}\left(M(-\tau)\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(\left|\widehat{M(\tau)v_{0}}\right|^{2}\widehat{M(\tau)v_{0}}\right)-\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(|\widehat{v}_{0}|^{2}\widehat{v}_{0}\right)\right)\frac{d\tau}{\tau},

and
M(t)
stands for the multiplication by

e^{i\frac{x^{2}}{2t}}
.

•

For any
0<\eta<2
, we have, in
L^{2}
and as
\varepsilon\to 0
,

{S^{\rm mod}}(\varepsilon v_{-})=\varepsilon v_{-}+\varepsilon^{3}\nu_{2}+\mathcal{O}\left(\varepsilon^{5-\eta}\right),

where
\nu_{2}\in L^{2}
is defined by

\nu_{2}=w_{2}+\mu_{2}(v_{-}),

where
w_{2}
is given by the first point, and
\mu_{2}
by the
second.

Remark 1.3
.

The functions
\mu_{2}
and
\nu_{2}
are more regular than merely

L^{2}
, as we will see in Section
6
that they
belong to
H^{\gamma}\cap\mathcal{F}(H^{\gamma})
for any
0<\gamma<1
.

It may be surprising that the leading order behavior of the modified
wave and scattering operators at the origin is the identity: we recall
that from their definition, these operators are already nontrivial,
and account for long range effects. We also emphasize that the first
corrector has a more involved expression than in the short range case,
a case where we would simply have (typically for the two-dimensional
and three-dimensional
cubic Schrödinger equations, see e.g.
[
3
]
)

\displaystyle w_{2}

\displaystyle=-i\lambda\int_{-\infty}^{0}\left(U(-s)\left(|U(s)v_{0}|^{2}U(s)v_{0}\right)\right)ds,

\displaystyle\mu_{2}

\displaystyle=-i\lambda\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(U(-s)\left(|U(s)v_{0}|^{2}U(s)v_{0}\right)\right)ds.

Note that in the (one-dimensional) long range case, these integrals diverge.

As evoked above, in principle, the method of proof that we present
allows to compute (some) higher order terms in the asymptotic
expansions near the origin.

1.5.
Notations

We recall the classical factorization of the Schrödinger group,

U(t)=e^{i\frac{t}{2}{\partial}_{x}^{2}}=M(t)D(t)\mathcal{F}M(t),

where the multiplication
M(t)
, the dilation
D(t)
and
the Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}
are defined by

(1.7)

\displaystyle M(t)=e^{i\frac{x^{2}}{2t}},\quad D(t)f(x)=\frac{1}{(it)^{1/2}}f\left(\frac{x}{t}\right),

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\mathcal{F}}f(\xi)=\widehat{f}(\xi)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}f(x)e^{-ix\xi}dx.

Note that each of these three operators is unitary on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
, and
that (
1.2
) reads
U(t)\approx M(t)D(t)\mathcal{F}
, see
also Lemma
2.2
.
We recall that the space
\Sigma
is defined by

\Sigma=H^{1}\cap\mathcal{F}(H^{1})=\left\{f\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}});\ \|f\|_{\Sigma}:=\|f\|_{L^{2}}+\|{\partial}_{x}f\|_{L^{2}}+\|xf\|_{L^{2}}<\infty\right\}.

We note that
\Sigma
is a Banach algebra, invariant under the Fourier
transform, and such that
\Sigma\hookrightarrow L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}
. In
addition,
\Sigma
is invariant under the action of
U(t)
, for any

t\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

For functions
f^{\varepsilon},g^{\varepsilon}\geqslant 0
depending on time
t
and
\varepsilon
, the
notation

f^{\varepsilon}\lesssim g^{\varepsilon}

means that there exists
C
independent of
t
and
\varepsilon
such that

f^{\varepsilon}\leqslant Cg^{\varepsilon}.

2.
Technical preliminaries

In this section, we gather classical estimates which can be found in
several references cited in the introduction.

Lemma 2.1
.

The operator

J(t)=x+it{\partial}_{x}

satisfies the following properties:

•

J(t)=U(t)xU(-t)
, and therefore
J
commutes with the
linear part of (
1.1
),

(2.1)

\left[J(t),i{\partial}_{t}+\frac{1}{2}{\partial}_{x}^{2}\right]=0\,.

•

It can be factorized as

J(t)=it\,e^{i\frac{x^{2}}{2t}}{\partial}_{x}\Big(e^{-i\frac{x^{2}}{2t}}\,\cdot\Big)\,.

As a consequence,
J
yields weighted Gagliardo-Nirenberg
inequalities. For

2\leqslant r\leqslant\infty
, there exists

C(r)
depending only on
r
such that

(2.2)

\left\|f\right\|_{L^{r}}\leqslant\frac{C(r)}{|t|^{\delta(r)}}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}}^{1-\delta(r)}\left\|J(t)f\right\|_{L^{2}}^{\delta(r)},\quad\delta(r):=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{r}\,.

Also, if
F(z)=G(|z|^{2})z
is
C^{1}
, then
J(t)

acts like a derivative on
F(w)
:

(2.3)

J(t)\left(F(w)\right)={\partial}_{z}F(w)J(t)w-{\partial}_{\overline{z}}F(w)\overline{J(t)w}\,.

Lemma 2.2
.

Denote by

{\mathcal{R}}(t)=M(t)D(t)\mathcal{F}\left(M(t)-1\right)\mathcal{F}^{-1},

where the above terms are defined in (
1.7
). The following
estimates hold for
|t|\geqslant 1
:

(1)

For all
s>1/2
and
0\leqslant\theta\leqslant 1
,

\|{\mathcal{R}}(t)f\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\lesssim\frac{1}{|t|^{1/2+\theta}}\|f\|_{H^{s+2\theta}},\quad\forall f\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{R}}).

(2)

For all
0\leqslant\theta\leqslant 1
,

\|{\mathcal{R}}(t)f\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\lesssim\frac{1}{|t|^{\theta}}\|f\|_{H^{2\theta}},\quad\forall f\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{R}}).

(3)

For all
0\leqslant\theta\leqslant 1
,

\|J(t){\mathcal{R}}(t)f\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\lesssim\frac{1}{|t|^{\theta}}\|f\|_{H^{1+2\theta}},\quad\forall f\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{R}}).

(4)

We have

\|{\partial}_{x}{\mathcal{R}}(t)f\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\lesssim\frac{1}{|t|}\|f\|_{H^{1}}+\frac{1}{|t|}\|xf\|_{H^{2}},\quad\forall f\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{R}}).

Remark 2.3
.

We emphasize that
{\mathcal{R}}(t)
does not map
L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
into itself,
since
\mathcal{F}\left(M(t)-1\right)\mathcal{F}^{-1}=U(\frac{1}{t})-1
and

U(\frac{1}{t})(e^{-it\frac{x^{2}}{2}})=\delta_{0}
.

Even though such estimates can be found in the existing literature,
we give a proof, as the ideas will be resumed when
M(t)-1
is present.

Proof.

In view of the definition of
{\mathcal{R}}
, we readily have

\|{\mathcal{R}}(t)f\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}=\frac{1}{|t|^{1/2}}\|\mathcal{F}(M(t)-1)\mathcal{F}^{-1}f\|_{L^{\infty}}

Using Hausdorff-Young inequality and the general estimate

(2.4)

|M(t)-1|=2\left|\sin\left(\frac{x^{2}}{4t}\right)\right|\lesssim\left|\frac{x^{2}}{t}\right|^{\theta},\quad\forall\,0\leqslant\theta\leqslant 1,

we find

\|{\mathcal{R}}(t)f\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\lesssim\frac{1}{|t|^{1/2}}\left\|\left|\frac{x^{2}}{t}\right|^{\theta}\mathcal{F}^{-1}f\right\|_{L^{1}}.

Using the easy property
\mathcal{F}(H^{s})\hookrightarrow L^{1}
for

s>1/2
(the same is true on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
provided that
s>d/2
,
from Cauchy-Schwarz inequality),

\|{\mathcal{R}}(t)f\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\lesssim\frac{1}{|t|^{1/2+\theta}}\left\|\left\langle x\right\rangle^{s}|x|^{2\theta}\mathcal{F}^{-1}f\right\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\frac{1}{|t|^{1/2+\theta}}\|f\|_{H^{s+2\theta}},

hence the first inequality. For the second one, since
D(t)
is
unitary on
L^{2}
, Plancherel formula
and (
2.4
) yield

\|{\mathcal{R}}(t)f\|_{L^{2}_{x}}=\|(M(t)-1)\mathcal{F}^{-1}f\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\frac{1}{|t|^{\theta}}\left\||x|^{2\theta}\mathcal{F}^{-1}f\right\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\frac{1}{|t|^{\theta}}\|f\|_{H^{2\theta}}.

For the third inequality, we use the formula
J(t)=U(t)xU(-t)
, along
with the factorization

(2.5)

U(-t)=iM(-t)\mathcal{F}^{-1}D\left(\frac{1}{t}\right)M(-t),

to obtain

\|J(t){\mathcal{R}}(t)f\|_{L^{2}_{x}}=\|x(M(t)-1)\mathcal{F}^{-1}f\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\frac{1}{|t|^{\theta}}\left\||x|^{1+2\theta}\mathcal{F}^{-1}f\right\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\frac{1}{|t|^{\theta}}\|f\|_{H^{1+2\theta}}.

To estimate
{\partial}_{x}{\mathcal{R}}(t)f
, two terms appear, whether the derivative
hits the first factor
M(t)
or not, and we have

\|{\partial}_{x}{\mathcal{R}}(t)f\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\leqslant\left\|\frac{x}{t}D(t)\mathcal{F}(M(t)-1)\mathcal{F}^{-1}f\right\|_{L^{2}}+\frac{1}{|t|}\left\|{\partial}_{x}\mathcal{F}(M(t)-1)\mathcal{F}^{-1}f\right\|_{L^{2}}.

The first term on the right hand side is equal to

\displaystyle\left\|x\mathcal{F}(M(t)-1)\mathcal{F}^{-1}f\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left\|{\partial}_{x}(M(t)-1)\mathcal{F}^{-1}f\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left\|\frac{x}{t}\mathcal{F}^{-1}f\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|(M(t)-1){\partial}_{x}\mathcal{F}^{-1}f\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|t|}\|f\|_{H^{1}}+\frac{1}{|t|^{\theta}}\|xf\|_{H^{2\theta}},

for any
\theta\in[0,1]
. Note that

\frac{1}{|t|}\left\|{\partial}_{x}\mathcal{F}(M(t)-1)\mathcal{F}^{-1}f\right\|_{L^{2}}=\frac{1}{|t|}\left\|x(M(t)-1)\mathcal{F}^{-1}f\right\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\frac{1}{|t|}\left\|x\mathcal{F}^{-1}f\right\|_{L^{2}},

hence the result, by choosing
\theta=1
.
∎

3.
Modified wave operator

A key aspect in the proofs of the existence of modified wave
operators for (
1.1
), in particular to get minimal regularity
assumptions on the asymptotic state
u_{-}
, is to consider a suitable
approximate solution near
t=-\infty
. Introduce two such approximate
solutions,
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
, defined for
t\leqslant-10
, given by

u_{1}(t,x):=\frac{1}{(it)^{1/2}}e^{i\frac{x^{2}}{2t}}{\widehat{u}_{-}}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)e^{iS_{-}(t,x)}=M(t)D(t)\widehat{w}(t,x),

where

\widehat{w}(t):={\widehat{u}_{-}}e^{i\lambda|{\widehat{u}_{-}}|^{2}\log|t|},

where we resume the same notations as in
[
16
]
,
and

u_{2}(t):=U(t)\mathcal{F}^{-1}\widehat{w}.

As
t\to-\infty
,
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
are close, in view of
Lemma
2.2
, since
u_{2}(t)-u_{1}(t)={\mathcal{R}}(t)\widehat{w}
.
Our goal is to keep track of the smallness of the asymptotic state as precisely as
possible, in the sense that we consider
u_{-}^{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon v_{-}
for some
fixed
v_{-}
, assume that
\varepsilon>0
is small, and we want to get larger
powers of
\varepsilon
in the error terms whenever we can. It turns out that
apparently,
u_{2}
is a better candidate than
u_{1}
in this
direction. More explicitly, the Duhamel formula for
u-u_{1}
(see

[
16
]
) contains a linear term (
R\widehat{w}
), which we want to
remove. We first gather some estimates on
u_{1}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
{u_{-}}\in{\mathcal{H}}
:
\|u_{1}(t)\|_{L^{2}}=\|u_{2}(t)\|_{L^{2}}=\|{u_{-}}\|_{L^{2}}
for all
t\leqslant-10
.
There exists
C>0
independent of
{u_{-}}
such that for all
t\leqslant-10
,

\displaystyle\|u_{1}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leqslant\frac{\|{\widehat{u}_{-}}\|_{L^{\infty}}}{\sqrt{|t|}},

\displaystyle\|{\partial}_{x}u_{1}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leqslant C\left(\|{u_{-}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}+\|{u_{-}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{3}\right),

\displaystyle\|J(t)u_{1}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leqslant C\|{u_{-}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}\left(1+\|{u_{-}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{2}\log|t|\right).

Proof.

The conservation of the
L^{2}
-norm is obvious, as well as
the estimate

\|u_{1}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leqslant\frac{\|{\widehat{u}_{-}}\|_{L^{\infty}}}{\sqrt{|t|}}.

The expression of
u_{1}
yields directly

\|{\partial}_{x}u_{1}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leqslant\|{\partial}_{x}{\widehat{u}_{-}}\|_{L^{2}}+|\lambda|\frac{\log|t|}{|t|}\|{\widehat{u}_{-}}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\|{\partial}_{x}{\widehat{u}_{-}}\|_{L^{2}}.

In view of the factorization for
J
stated in Lemma
2.1
,

\displaystyle J(t)u_{1}(t)

\displaystyle=itM(t){\partial}_{x}\left(D(t){\widehat{u}_{-}}e^{iS_{-}(t)}\right)

\displaystyle=e^{i\frac{x^{2}}{2t}+iS_{-}(t)}\left(iD(t)({\partial}_{x}{\widehat{u}_{-}})-\lambda D(t){\widehat{u}_{-}}\times\log|t|\times{\partial}_{x}\left|{\widehat{u}_{-}}\right|^{2}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)\right),

thus

\|J(t)u_{1}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|{\widehat{u}_{-}}\|_{H^{1}}+\log|t|\|{\widehat{u}_{-}}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\|{\widehat{u}_{-}}\|_{H^{1}}.

The lemma follows.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
{u_{-}}\in{\mathcal{H}}
.
There exists
C>0
independent of
{u_{-}}
such that for all
t\leqslant-10
,

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{R}}(t)\widehat{w}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leqslant\frac{C}{\sqrt{|t|}}\left(\|{\widehat{u}_{-}}\|_{H^{1}}+\|{\widehat{u}_{-}}\|_{H^{1}}^{3}\right),

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{R}}(t)\widehat{w}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leqslant\frac{C}{|t|^{3/2}}\left(\|{u_{-}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}+(\log|t|)^{3}\|{u_{-}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{7}\right).

\displaystyle\|U(-t){\mathcal{R}}(t)\widehat{w}\|_{\Sigma}\leqslant\frac{C}{|t|}\left(\|{u_{-}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}+(\log|t|)^{3}\|{u_{-}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{7}\right).

Proof.

If we use
the first point of Lemma
2.2
with
\theta=0
and

s>1/2
, the phase factor causes the appearance of a
\log|t|
term,
since the
H^{1}
-norm of
\widehat{w}
is involved. So we rather take

\theta>0
and
s>1/2
such that
s+2\theta=1
, and so,
for
t\leqslant-10
,

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{R}}(t)\widehat{w}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\frac{\|\widehat{w}\|_{H^{1}}}{|t|^{1/2+\theta}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|t|^{1/2+\theta}}\left(\|{\widehat{u}_{-}}\|_{H^{1}}+(\log|t|)\|{\widehat{u}_{-}}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\|{\widehat{u}_{-}}\|_{H^{1}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{|t|}}\left(\|{\widehat{u}_{-}}\|_{H^{1}}+\|{\widehat{u}_{-}}\|_{H^{1}}^{3}\right),

where the logarithmic factor was left out since
\theta>0
.
It is actually at the level of this estimate that
the
H^{1}
-norm of
{\widehat{u}_{-}}
appears, instead of merely its

L^{\infty}
-norm.

To obtain the stronger time decay, we choose
s=\theta=1
in the first
point of Lemma
2.2
, hence, since
H^{3}({\mathbb{R}})
is
an algebra,

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{R}}(t)\widehat{w}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\frac{\|\widehat{w}\|_{H^{3}}}{|t|^{3/2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|t|^{3/2}}\left(\|{\widehat{u}_{-}}\|_{H^{3}}+(\log|t|)^{3}\|{\widehat{u}_{-}}\|_{H^{3}}^{7}\right).

In view of Lemma
2.1
,

\|U(-t){\mathcal{R}}\widehat{w}\|_{\Sigma}=\|{\mathcal{R}}\widehat{w}\|_{L^{2}}+\|{\partial}_{x}{\mathcal{R}}\widehat{w}\|_{L^{2}}+\|J(t){\mathcal{R}}\widehat{w}\|_{L^{2}},

and Lemma
2.2
yields

\|U(-t){\mathcal{R}}(t)\widehat{w}\|_{\Sigma}\lesssim\frac{1}{|t|}\|\widehat{w}\|_{H^{3}}+\frac{1}{|t|}\|x\widehat{w}\|_{H^{2}}.

Using the fact that
H^{s}({\mathbb{R}})
is an algebra for
s>1/2
, we infer

\displaystyle\|U(-t){\mathcal{R}}(t)\widehat{w}\|_{\Sigma}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|t|}\left(\|{\widehat{u}_{-}}\|_{H^{3}}+(\log|t|)^{3}\|{\widehat{u}_{-}}\|_{H^{3}}^{7}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{|t|}\left(\|x{\widehat{u}_{-}}\|_{H^{2}}+(\log|t|)^{2}\|x{\widehat{u}_{-}}\|_{H^{2}}^{5}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|t|}\left(\|{u_{-}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}+\sum_{j=0,1}(\log|t|)^{2+j}\|{u_{-}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{5+2j}\right),

hence the lemma.
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

There exist
\delta_{0}>0
,
C
, and a polynomial
P
, such that the following holds.
For any
u_{-}\in L^{2}
with
{u_{-}}\in{\mathcal{H}}
, where

is
defined in (

), and such that

to (

) with

satisfies, for

,

\left\|U(-t)\left(u(t)-u_{2}(t)\right)\right\|_{\Sigma}\leqslant C\|{u_{-}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{3}P\left(\|{u_{-}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}\right)\frac{(\log|t|)^{4}}{|t|}.

Remark 3.4
.

The choice of the approximate solution
u_{2}
is tailored to ensure
that, with the proof presented below, the error term is superlinear in
some norm of
u_{-}
, uniformly in the limit
t\to-\infty
.
On the other hand, the smallness assumption, which is a consequence of
the first estimate in
Lemma
3.2
, is stronger than in the
cited references, since Sobolev embedding yields
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})\subset L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
.

Proof.

Like in
[
28
]
or
[
16
]
, the proof relies on a fixed
point argument near
t=-\infty
. Resuming the computations from

[
16
]
(and keeping the same notations),

i{\partial}_{t}\widehat{w}=\frac{\lambda}{t}|\widehat{w}|^{2}\widehat{w},

and so
we check that
u_{2}
solves the equation

i{\partial}_{t}\mathcal{F}U(-t)u_{2}=\lambda\mathcal{F}U(-t)\left(\frac{1}{t}U(t)\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(|\widehat{w}|^{2}\widehat{w}\right)\right).

Therefore, we want to solve

i{\partial}_{t}\mathcal{F}U(-t)\left(u-u_{2}\right)=\lambda\mathcal{F}U(-t)\left(|u|^{2}u-\frac{1}{t}U(t)\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(|\widehat{w}|^{2}\widehat{w}\right)\right).

We insert the term
|u_{2}|^{2}u_{2}
into the above equation,
and use the identity

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t}U(t)\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(|\widehat{w}|^{2}\widehat{w}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{t}MD\mathcal{F}M\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(|\widehat{w}|^{2}\widehat{w}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{t}{\mathcal{R}}(t)\left(|\widehat{w}|^{2}\widehat{w}\right)+\frac{1}{t}M(t)D(t)\left(|\widehat{w}|^{2}\widehat{w}\right),

with

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t}M(t)D(t)\left(|\widehat{w}|^{2}\widehat{w}\right)=M(t)|M(t)D(t)\widehat{w}|^{2}D(t)\widehat{w}=|u_{1}|^{2}u_{1},

so Duhamel’s formula reads, along with the requirement

U(-t)\left(u(t)-u_{2}(t)\right)\to 0
as
t\to-\infty
,

(3.1)

\displaystyle u(t)=u_{2}(t)

\displaystyle-i\lambda\int_{-\infty}^{t}U(t-\tau)\left(|u|^{2}u-|u_{2}|^{2}u_{2}\right)(\tau)d\tau

\displaystyle+i\lambda\int_{-\infty}^{t}U(t-\tau)\left(|u_{2}|^{2}u_{2}-|u_{1}|^{2}u_{1}\right)(\tau)d\tau

\displaystyle-i\lambda\int_{-\infty}^{t}U(t-\tau)\left(\frac{1}{\tau}{\mathcal{R}}(\tau)\left(|\widehat{w}|^{2}\widehat{w}\right)\right)(\tau)d\tau.

The last two lines correspond to source terms, involving only the
various approximate solutions, and can be estimated thanks to
Lemma
2.2
, as
u_{2}=u_{1}+{\mathcal{R}}\widehat{w}
.

Denote by
\Phi(u)=u_{2}+\Phi_{1}(u)+\Phi_{2}+\Phi_{3}
the right hand side of
(
3.1
), where
\Phi_{j}
corresponds to the
j
-th

\Phi_{2}
and
\Phi_{3}
do not depend on

\displaystyle\left|{\partial}_{x}\left(|u_{2}|^{2}u_{2}-|u_{1}|^{2}u_{1}\right)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|{\partial}_{x}\left(|{\mathcal{R}}\widehat{w}+u_{1}|^{2}({\mathcal{R}}\widehat{w}+u_{1})-|u_{1}|^{2}u_{1}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(|{\mathcal{R}}\widehat{w}|^{2}+|u_{1}|^{2}\right)|{\partial}_{x}{\mathcal{R}}\widehat{w}|+\left(|{\mathcal{R}}\widehat{w}|+|u_{1}|\right)|{\mathcal{R}}\widehat{w}||{\partial}_{x}u_{1}|.

Using the formula
J(t)=U(t)xU(-t)
from Lemma
2.1
, as well as
(
2.3
) that state that
J(t)
can be thought of as a
derivative here, we find

\displaystyle\left\|U(-t)\Phi_{2}(t)\right\|_{\Sigma}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{-\infty}^{t}\left(\|{\mathcal{R}}(\tau)\widehat{w}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\|u_{1}(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\right)\|U(-\tau){\mathcal{R}}\widehat{w}\|_{\Sigma}d\tau

\displaystyle+\int_{-\infty}^{t}\left(\|{\mathcal{R}}(\tau)\widehat{w}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|u_{1}(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)\|{\mathcal{R}}(\tau)\widehat{w}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|U(-\tau)u_{1}(\tau)\|_{\Sigma}d\tau.

We invoke Lemmas
3.1
and
3.2
to estimate the above
terms. More precisely, then factor
\|{\mathcal{R}}(\tau)\widehat{w}\|_{L^{\infty}}
is controlled by the second estimate of Lemma
3.2
, and we
get, for
\|{\widehat{u}_{-}}\|_{H^{1}}\leqslant 1
and

:

\|U(-t)\Phi_{2}(t)\|_{\Sigma}\lesssim\|{\widehat{u}_{-}}\|_{H^{1}}\|{u_{-}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{2}P_{2}\left(\|{u_{-}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}\right)\int_{-\infty}^{t}\frac{(\log|\tau|)^{4}}{\tau^{2}}d\tau,

for some polynomial
P_{2}
.
The term
\Phi_{3}
is estimated thanks to Lemma
2.2
, and the
choice of parameter is motivated by the previous estimate, keeping in
mind that a factor
\frac{1}{\tau}
is already present in the
definition of
\Phi_{3}
. For the
L^{2}
-estimate, we thus choose

\theta=1
in Lemma
2.2
, so that

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{3}(t)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{-\infty}^{t}\left\||\widehat{w}|^{2}\widehat{w}\right\|_{H^{2}}\frac{d\tau}{\tau^{2}}\lesssim\int_{-\infty}^{t}\|\widehat{w}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\|\widehat{w}\|_{H^{2}}\frac{d\tau}{\tau^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|{\widehat{u}_{-}}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\|{\widehat{u}_{-}}\|_{H^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{t}\left(1+(\log|\tau|)^{2}\|{\widehat{u}_{-}}\|_{H^{2}}^{4}\right)\frac{d\tau}{\tau^{2}}.

The norm
\|xU(-t)\Phi_{3}(t)\|_{L^{2}}=\|J(t)\Phi_{3}(t)\|_{L^{2}}
is
estimate similarly, by setting again
\theta=1
(in the third point of
Lemma
2.2
, and we get, since the
H^{2}
-norm is replaced by an

\|J(t)\Phi_{3}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|{\widehat{u}_{-}}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\|{\widehat{u}_{-}}\|_{H^{3}}\int_{-\infty}^{t}\left(1+(\log|\tau|)^{3}\|{\widehat{u}_{-}}\|_{H^{3}}^{6}\right)\frac{d\tau}{\tau^{2}}.

The choice of suitable parameters for the

\|{\partial}_{x}\Phi_{3}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\int_{-\infty}^{t}\left(\||\widehat{w}|^{2}\widehat{w}\|_{H^{1}}+\|x|\widehat{w}|^{2}\widehat{w}\|_{H^{2}}\right)\frac{d\tau}{\tau^{2}}.

The term involving the

focus on the other norm. Direct computations yield

\left|{\partial}_{x}^{2}\left(x|\widehat{w}|^{2}\widehat{w}\right)\right|\lesssim|x||\widehat{w}|^{2}|{\partial}_{x}^{2}\widehat{w}|+|\widehat{w}|^{2}|{\partial}_{x}\widehat{w}|+|x||\widehat{w}||{\partial}_{x}\widehat{w}|^{2},

and we get

\displaystyle\|{\partial}_{x}\Phi_{3}(t)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|{\widehat{u}_{-}}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\|{\widehat{u}_{-}}\|_{H^{1}}\int_{-\infty}^{t}\left(1+(\log|\tau|)\|{\widehat{u}_{-}}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\right)\frac{d\tau}{\tau^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\|{\widehat{u}_{-}}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|{u_{-}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{2}\int_{-\infty}^{t}\left(1+(\log|\tau|)^{2}\|{u_{-}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{4}\right)\frac{d\tau}{\tau^{2}}.

The source term is therefore controlled, for
t\leqslant-10
, by:

(3.2)

\|U(-t)(\Phi_{2}(t)+\Phi_{3}(t))\|_{\Sigma}\lesssim\|{\widehat{u}_{-}}\|_{H^{1}}\|{u_{-}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{2}P\left(\|{u_{-}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}\right)\int_{-\infty}^{t}\frac{\left(\log|\tau|\right)^{4}}{\tau^{2}}d\tau,

for some polynomial
P
whose precise expression is irrelevant.

The end of the proof consists of a fixed point argument. Mimicking

[
28
]
, for
\alpha\in]1/2,1[
arbitrary (but fixed),
and
T\gg 1
, introduce the
space

\displaystyle X^{\alpha}(T)

\displaystyle=\Bigl\{u\in L^{\infty}(]-\infty,-T],L^{2})\text{ such that }t\mapsto U(-t)u(t)\in L^{\infty}(]-\infty,-T],\Sigma),

\displaystyle\qquad\sup_{t\leqslant-T}|t|^{\alpha}\|U(-t)(u(t)-u_{2}(t))\|_{\Sigma}\leqslant\|{\widehat{u}_{-}}\|_{H^{1}}\Bigr\}.

and define on
X^{\alpha}(T)
the metric

d(u,v)=\sup_{t\leqslant-T}|t|^{\alpha}\|u(t)-v(t)\|_{L^{2}}.

Note that here, we choose to measure distance by considering the

L^{2}
norm only:
X^{\alpha}(T)
, equipped with this distance, is
a complete metric space.

For
u\in X^{\alpha}(T)
,
\Phi_{1}(u)
is estimated by

\displaystyle\|U(-t)\Phi_{1}(u(t))\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{-\infty}^{t}\left(\|u(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\|u_{2}(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\right)\|u(\tau)-u_{2}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\|{\widehat{u}_{-}}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\int_{-\infty}^{t}\frac{d\tau}{\tau^{1+\alpha}}\lesssim\frac{\|{\widehat{u}_{-}}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}}{|t|^{\alpha}}.

For
A\in\{{\partial}_{x},J(t)\}
, we have

(3.3)

\left|A\left(|u|^{2}u-|u_{2}|^{2}u_{2}\right)\right|\lesssim|u|^{2}|A(u-u_{2})|+\left(|u|+|u_{2}|\right)|Au_{2}||u-u_{2}|.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\|U(-t)\Phi_{1}(u(t))\|_{\Sigma}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{-\infty}^{t}\|u(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\|U(-\tau)(u(\tau)-u_{2}(\tau))\|_{\Sigma}d\tau

\displaystyle+\int_{-\infty}^{t}(\|u(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle+\|u_{2}(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}})\|U(-\tau)u_{2}(\tau)\|_{\Sigma}\|u(\tau)-u_{2}(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\|{\widehat{u}_{-}}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}P_{3}\left(\|{u_{-}}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}\right)\int_{-\infty}^{t}\frac{\log|\tau|}{\tau^{1+\alpha}}d\tau,

for some polynomial
P_{3}
whose precise expression is irrelevant,
where we have used Lemmas
3.1
and
3.2
to estimate

\|U(-\tau)u_{2}(\tau)\|_{\Sigma}
. The

L^{\infty}
-norm of
u(\tau)-u_{2}(\tau)
is controlled by

|\tau|^{-1/2-\alpha}
in view of Lemma
2.1
and the
definition of
X^{\alpha}(T)
. Therefore, by choosing
T

sufficiently large, we check that
\Phi
maps
X^{\alpha}(T)
to
itself.

To conclude by a fixed point argument, we show that

\Phi
is a contraction provided
that
\|{\widehat{u}_{-}}\|_{H^{1}}
is sufficiently small. Indeed, for
u,v\in X^{\alpha}(T)
,

\Phi(u)(t)-\Phi(v)(t)=-i\lambda\int_{-\infty}^{t}U(t-\tau)\left(|u|^{2}u-|v|^{2}v\right)(\tau)d\tau,

and so, for
t\leqslant-10
,

\displaystyle\left\|\Phi(u)(t)-\Phi(v)(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{-\infty}^{t}\left(\|u(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\|v(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\right)\|u(\tau)-v(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\|{\widehat{u}_{-}}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}d(u,v)\int_{-\infty}^{t}\frac{d\tau}{\tau^{1+\alpha}},

where we have used

\displaystyle\|u(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|v(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\|u_{2}(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}+C\|u(\tau)-u_{2}(\tau)\|_{H^{1}}+C\|v(\tau)-u_{2}(\tau)\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\|{\widehat{u}_{-}}\|_{H^{1}}}{\sqrt{|\tau|}},

since
\alpha>1/2
. Therefore,

d\left(\Phi(u),\Phi(v)\right)\lesssim\|{\widehat{u}_{-}}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}d(u,v),

hence the result provided that
\|{\widehat{u}_{-}}\|_{H^{1}}
is
sufficiently small.
∎

4.
Behavior of the modified wave operator near the origin

4.1.
Leading order asymptotic behavior

Let
u_{-}^{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon v_{-}
with
v_{-}\in{\mathcal{H}}
independent of

\varepsilon
. Denote by
u^{\varepsilon}
the solution provided by
Proposition
3.3
. Then like
u_{-}^{\varepsilon}
,
u^{\varepsilon}
is of order

\varepsilon
, and the remainder in Proposition
3.3
is

\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{3})
. The long range phase correction
S_{-}^{\varepsilon}
is

\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{2}\log|t|)
, for
t\leqslant-10
: its contribution is negligible
for times
t
such that
\varepsilon^{2}|\log|t||\ll 1
, so in particular we can match with a linear
solution at
t^{\varepsilon}_{\gamma}=-1/\varepsilon^{\gamma}
,

i{\partial}_{t}{\mathbf{u}}^{\varepsilon}_{1}+\frac{1}{2}{\partial}_{x}^{2}{\mathbf{u}}^{\varepsilon}_{1}=0,

with

{\mathbf{u}}^{\varepsilon}_{1\mid t=-1/\varepsilon^{\gamma}}=\frac{1}{(it)^{1/2}}e^{i\frac{x^{2}}{2t}}\mathcal{F}(u_{-}^{\varepsilon})\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)\big|_{t=-1/\varepsilon^{\gamma}}.

The right hand side is
M(t)D(t)\mathcal{F}u_{-}^{\varepsilon}
evaluated at
t=-1/\varepsilon^{\gamma}
.
In view of Lemma
2.2
, up to a small error term,

{\mathbf{u}}_{1}^{\varepsilon}(t)=U(t)u_{-}^{\varepsilon},\text{ or, equivalently, }{\mathbf{u}}_{1}^{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon v_{1},\text{ with }v_{1}(t)=U(t)v_{-}.

We choose this definition for the first order approximation

{\mathbf{u}}_{1}^{\varepsilon}
.
Let
t\geqslant-1/\varepsilon^{\gamma}
. Duhamel’s formula implies

\displaystyle U(-t)\left(u^{\varepsilon}(t)-v^{\varepsilon}_{1}(t)\right)

\displaystyle=U\left(-s\right)\left(u^{\varepsilon}(s)-u^{\varepsilon}_{2}(s)+u_{2}^{\varepsilon}(s)-{\mathbf{u}}^{\varepsilon}_{1}(s)\right)\Big|_{s=-1/\varepsilon^{\gamma}}

\displaystyle\quad-i\lambda\int_{-1/\varepsilon^{\gamma}}^{t}U(-\tau)\left(|u^{\varepsilon}|^{2}u^{\varepsilon}\right)(\tau)d\tau,

that is

\displaystyle U(-t)u^{\varepsilon}(t)-u_{-}^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=U\left(-s\right)\left(u^{\varepsilon}(s)-u^{\varepsilon}_{2}(s)\right)\Big|_{s=-1/\varepsilon^{\gamma}}+U(-s)u_{2}^{\varepsilon}(s)\Big|_{s=-1/\varepsilon^{\gamma}}-u_{-}^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\quad-i\lambda\int_{-1/\varepsilon^{\gamma}}^{t}U(-\tau)\left(|u^{\varepsilon}|^{2}u^{\varepsilon}\right)(\tau)d\tau,

The first term on the right hand side is estimated thanks to
Proposition
3.3
:

\left\|U\left(-s\right)\left(u^{\varepsilon}(s)-u^{\varepsilon}_{2}(s)\right)\Big|_{s=-1/\varepsilon^{\gamma}}\right\|_{\Sigma}\lesssim\varepsilon^{3+\gamma}|\log\varepsilon|.

On the other hand,

\left\|U(-s)u_{2}^{\varepsilon}(s)\Big|_{s=-1/\varepsilon^{\gamma}}-u_{-}^{\varepsilon}\right\|_{\Sigma}=\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\widehat{w}_{\mid s=-1/\varepsilon^{\gamma}}-u_{-}^{\varepsilon}\|_{\Sigma}=\|\widehat{w}_{\mid s=-1/\varepsilon^{\gamma}}-\mathcal{F}(u_{-}^{\varepsilon})\|_{\Sigma}.

Recalling that

\widehat{w}=\mathcal{F}(u_{-}^{\varepsilon})\exp\left(i|\mathcal{F}(u_{-}^{\varepsilon})|^{2}\log|t|\right),

we have directly

\displaystyle\|\widehat{w}-\mathcal{F}(u_{-}^{\varepsilon})\|_{\Sigma}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\left\langle x\right\rangle\left(\widehat{w}-\mathcal{F}(u_{-}^{\varepsilon})\right)\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|{\partial}_{x}\widehat{u_{-}^{\varepsilon}}\left(\exp\left(i|\mathcal{F}(u_{-}^{\varepsilon})|^{2}\log|t|\right)-1\right)\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+|\log\varepsilon|\left\|\mathcal{F}(u_{-}^{\varepsilon}){\partial}_{x}|\mathcal{F}(u_{-}^{\varepsilon})|^{2}\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{3}|\log\varepsilon|.

The integral term can be estimated in
\Sigma
by

\int_{-1/\varepsilon^{\gamma}}^{t}\|u^{\varepsilon}(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\|U(-\tau)u^{\varepsilon}(\tau)\|_{\Sigma}d\tau.

Since
\|U(T)u^{\varepsilon}(-T)\|_{\Sigma}\lesssim\varepsilon

from Lemmas
3.1
and
3.2
, and
Proposition
3.3
(where
T

is independent of
\varepsilon

\|u^{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon}{\left\langle t\right\rangle^{1/2}},\quad\|U(-t)u^{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{\Sigma}\lesssim\varepsilon\left\langle t\right\rangle^{C\varepsilon^{2}}.

In view of the proof of (
5.1
) and (
5.2
), we
directly know that

\|{\partial}_{t}\widehat{w}\|_{L^{1}([1,\infty[,L^{2}\cap L^{\infty})}\lesssim\varepsilon^{3},

and so

\widehat{w}(t)=\widehat{w}(1)+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{3})\text{ in }L^{\infty}([1,\infty[,L^{2}\cap L^{\infty}).

By definition,
\widehat{w}(1)=\widehat{v}(1)=\mathcal{F}\left(U(-1)u^{\varepsilon}(1)\right)=\varepsilon\widehat{v}_{0}+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{3})
in
\Sigma
.

Regarding
\Phi
, the definition (
5.6
), and (
5.7
),
yield

\|\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}}\leqslant\|\Phi-\Psi(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\Psi(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\varepsilon^{4},

where the right hand side is estimated uniformly in
t\geqslant 1
.
Therefore,

u_{+}^{\varepsilon}=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(We^{-i\Phi}\right)=\varepsilon v_{0}+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{3})\text{ in
}L^{2}.

Therefore, at leading order, we have

{S^{\rm mod}}(\varepsilon v_{-})=\varepsilon v_{-}+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{3}),\quad\left(W_{+}^{\rm mod}\right)^{-1}(\varepsilon v_{0})=\varepsilon v_{0}+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{3})\quad\text{in }L^{2}.

6.
Higher order asymptotic expansion of the final state

The higher order asymptotic expansion for
u_{+}^{\varepsilon}
, involving an

\varepsilon^{3}
term, requires more work, even at the formal level. We first
show how to derive this term, then, in a final subsection, prove the
error estimates announced in Theorem
1.2
.

6.1.
Derivation of the first corrector

We first examine the value of
u^{\varepsilon}
at time
t=1
, in view of
future connections with
Theorem
5.1
.
The idea is the same as in Section
4.2
: for finite time,
since we consider small data, the asymptotic expansion is given by
Picard’s scheme, and we have directly from Section
4
,

u^{\varepsilon}_{\mid t=1}=\varepsilon U(1)v_{0}+\varepsilon^{3}\widetilde{w}_{2}+\varepsilon^{5-\eta}\widehat{\rho}^{\varepsilon},

with

\widetilde{w}_{2}=U(1)w_{2}-i\lambda\int_{0}^{1}U(1-s)\left(|U(s)v_{0}|^{2}U(s)v_{0}\right)ds,

and
\|\rho^{\varepsilon}\|_{\Sigma}\lesssim 1
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

To derive the first corrector, we plug the asymptotic expansion

\widehat{w}(t)=\varepsilon\widehat{v}_{0}+\varepsilon^{3}\widehat{\nu}_{2}(t)+\varepsilon^{5-\eta}\widehat{r^{\varepsilon}}(t)

into (
5.4
), with
r^{\varepsilon}=\mathcal{O}(1)
in some topology we
shall precise. We first proceed formally, and then check
that the above ansatz indeed provides a corrector of
u^{\varepsilon}_{+}
in

L^{2}
. We compute successively

|\widehat{v}|^{2}=|\widehat{w}|^{2}=\varepsilon^{2}|\widehat{v}_{0}|^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{4}),

hence, for bounded
t
,

B(t)=e^{i\lambda\int_{1}^{t}|\widehat{v}(\tau)|^{2}\frac{d\tau}{\tau}}=1+i\lambda\varepsilon^{2}|\widehat{v}_{0}|^{2}\log t+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{4}),

and

\widehat{v}=\widehat{w}\overline{B}=\varepsilon\widehat{v}_{0}+\varepsilon^{3}\left(\widehat{\nu}_{2}-i\lambda|\widehat{v}_{0}|^{2}\widehat{v}_{0}\log t\right)+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{5-\eta}).

We then expand the factor
I_{1}^{\varepsilon}
and
I_{2}^{\varepsilon}
from (
5.4
):

\displaystyle I_{1}^{\varepsilon}(t)

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{3}\mathcal{F}\left(M(-t)-1\right)\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(\left|\widehat{M(t)v_{0}}\right|^{2}\widehat{M(t)v_{0}}\right)+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{5}),

\displaystyle I_{2}^{\varepsilon}(t)

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{3}\left(\left|\widehat{M(t)v_{0}}\right|^{2}\widehat{M(t)v_{0}}-|\widehat{v}_{0}|^{2}\widehat{v}_{0}\right)+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{5}).

Therefore, the natural candidate for
\nu_{2}
satisfies:

{\partial}_{t}\widehat{\nu}_{2}=\frac{\lambda}{t}(J_{1}+J_{2}),

where

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}\left(M(-t)-1\right)\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(\left|\widehat{M(t)v_{0}}\right|^{2}\widehat{M(t)v_{0}}\right),

\displaystyle J_{2}

\displaystyle=\left|\widehat{M(t)v_{0}}\right|^{2}\widehat{M(t)v_{0}}-|\widehat{v}_{0}|^{2}\widehat{v}_{0}.

The Cauchy datum for
\nu_{2}
is given at
t=1
: on the one hand, the
above asymptotic expansion implies

\widehat{v}_{\mid t=1}=\varepsilon\widehat{v}_{0}+\varepsilon^{3}\widehat{\nu}_{2\mid t=1}+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{5-\eta}),

while on the other hand, the analysis of Section
4
yields

U(-t)u^{\varepsilon}(t)_{\mid t=1}=\varepsilon v_{0}+\varepsilon^{3}U(-1)\widetilde{w}_{2}+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{5-\eta}),

so we infer

\nu_{2\mid t=1}=U(-1)\widetilde{w}_{2}=w_{2}-i\lambda\int_{0}^{1}U(-s)\left(|U(s)v_{0}|^{2}U(s)v_{0}\right)ds.

As
v_{0}\in\Sigma
, the proof of Theorem
5.1
readily
yields
\frac{1}{t}(J_{1}+J_{2})\in L^{1}([1,\infty[;L^{2}\cap L^{\infty})
.
Since
\Sigma
is a Banach algebra, invariant under the Fourier
transform, and such that
\Sigma\hookrightarrow L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}
, we
have
\nu_{2\mid t=1}\in\Sigma
, and so
\nu_{2}\in L^{\infty}([1,\infty[;L^{2}\cap L^{\infty})
and

\widehat{\nu}_{2}(t)\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}\widehat{\nu}_{2\mid t=1}+\lambda\int_{1}^{\infty}(J_{1}(\tau)+J_{2}(\tau))\frac{d\tau}{\tau}\quad\text{in }L^{2}\cap L^{\infty}.

However, in the asymptotic expansion of
B
,
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
,
the more precise information that we need is that the remainder term
is
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{5})
together with some algebraic decay in time, so we
obtain an asymptotic expansion for
\widehat{w}
in
L^{2}
. This requires
more effort, and the proof is presented in the next subsection.

6.2.
Justification of the second order asymptotic expansion

As a first step, we analyze the regularity of the corrector

\nu_{2}
. To do so, we introduce an extra function space: for

0<\gamma<1
, set

\Sigma^{\gamma}=H^{\gamma}\cap\mathcal{F}(H^{\gamma})=\{f\in H^{\gamma}({\mathbb{R}}),\ \|f\|_{\Sigma^{\gamma}}:=\|f\|_{H^{\gamma}}+\|\left\langle x\right\rangle^{\gamma}f\|_{L^{2}}<\infty\}.

We note that like
\Sigma
,
\Sigma^{\gamma}
is invariant under the
Fourier transform, as well as the action of
U(t)
, and, when

\gamma>1/2
, it is an
algebra embedded into
L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
v_{0},w_{2}\in\Sigma
. Recall that the first corrector is defined by

\widehat{\nu}_{2}(t)=\widehat{w}_{2}-i\lambda\int_{0}^{1}\mathcal{F}U(-s)\left(|U(s)v_{0}|^{2}U(s)v_{0}\right)ds+\lambda\int_{1}^{t}(J_{1}(\tau)+J_{2}(\tau))\frac{d\tau}{\tau},

where
J_{1}
and
J_{2}
are defined by

\displaystyle J_{1}(t)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}\left(M(-t)-1\right)\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(\left|\widehat{M(t)v_{0}}\right|^{2}\widehat{M(t)v_{0}}\right),

\displaystyle J_{2}(t)

\displaystyle=\left|\widehat{M(t)v_{0}}\right|^{2}\widehat{M(t)v_{0}}-|\widehat{v}_{0}|^{2}\widehat{v}_{0}.

Then
J_{1},J_{2}\in L^{\infty}([1,\infty[,\Sigma)
, with

\sup_{t\geqslant 1}\|J_{1}(t)\|_{\Sigma}+\sup_{t\geqslant 1}\|J_{2}(t)\|_{\Sigma}\lesssim\|v_{0}\|_{\Sigma}^{3},

and, for any
1/2<\gamma<1
,

\|J_{1}(t)\|_{\Sigma^{\gamma}}+\|J_{2}(t)\|_{\Sigma^{\gamma}}\lesssim\frac{\|v_{0}\|_{\Sigma}^{3}}{t^{(1-\gamma)/2}},

and therefore
\nu_{2}\in L^{\infty}_{\rm loc}([1,\infty[,\Sigma)\cap L^{\infty}([1,\infty[,\Sigma^{\gamma})
. Finally,
\widehat{\nu}_{2}(t)\to\widehat{\nu}_{2}^{\infty}
in
\Sigma^{\gamma}
as
t\to\infty
, where

\widehat{\nu}_{2}^{\infty}=\widehat{w}_{2}-i\lambda\int_{0}^{1}\mathcal{F}U(-s)\left(|U(s)v_{0}|^{2}U(s)v_{0}\right)ds+\lambda\int_{1}^{\infty}(J_{1}(\tau)+J_{2}(\tau))\frac{d\tau}{\tau}.

Proof of Lemma
6.1
.

Since
w_{2},v_{0}\in\Sigma
, the first two terms defining
\widehat{\nu}_{2}
belong to
\Sigma
(as it is an algebra). For any (fixed)

t
,
J_{1},J_{2}\in\Sigma
, and
\nu_{2}(t)\in\Sigma
: the uniform
bound in time is straightforward, but to get some time decay, we pay
a little regularity. Let
0<\gamma<1
:
like in the proof of Lemma
2.2
, write, for
t\geqslant 1
,

\displaystyle\|J_{1}(t)\|_{\Sigma^{\gamma}}

\displaystyle=\left\|\left(M(-t)-1\right)\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(\left|\widehat{M(t)v_{0}}\right|^{2}\widehat{M(t)v_{0}}\right)\right\|_{\Sigma^{\gamma}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{(1-\gamma)/2}}\left\||x|^{1-\gamma}\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(\left|\widehat{M(t)v_{0}}\right|^{2}\widehat{M(t)v_{0}}\right)\right\|_{\Sigma^{\gamma}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{(1-\gamma)/2}}\left\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(\left|\widehat{M(t)v_{0}}\right|^{2}\widehat{M(t)v_{0}}\right)\right\|_{\Sigma}\lesssim\frac{\|v_{0}\|_{\Sigma}^{3}}{t^{(1-\gamma)/2}},

since
\Sigma
is an algebra. The assumption

\gamma>1/2
simplifies the computations in the case of
J_{2}
, as

\Sigma^{\gamma}
is an algebra, and the first
inequality below is straightforward:

\displaystyle\|J_{2}(t)\|_{\Sigma^{\gamma}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\|M(t)v_{0}\|_{\Sigma^{\gamma}}^{2}+\|v_{0}\|_{\Sigma^{\gamma}}^{2}\right)\|(M(t)-1)v_{0}\|_{\Sigma^{\gamma}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|v_{0}\|_{\Sigma}^{2}\frac{1}{t^{(1-\gamma)/2}}\||x|^{1-\gamma}v_{0}\|_{\Sigma^{\gamma}}\lesssim\frac{\|v_{0}\|_{\Sigma}^{3}}{t^{(1-\gamma)/2}}.

The lemma follows, since the extra decay in time,

t^{(\gamma-1)/2}
, ensures the convergence of the integral in the
last term defining
\widehat{\nu}_{2}
.
∎

We recall two uniform estimates which will be of constant use in the
course of the proof:

(6.1)

\|\widehat{v}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\left\|\widehat{M(t)v}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\varepsilon.

The first quantity is estimated in the proof of
Theorem
5.1
, thanks to (
5.5
). The second one
is controlled thanks to (
5.3
), since, using
(
2.5
),

\left\|\widehat{M(t)v}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}=\left\|\mathcal{F}M(t)U(-t)u(t)\right\|_{L^{\infty}}=\left\|D\left(\frac{1}{t}\right)M(-t)u(t)\right\|_{L^{\infty}}=\sqrt{t}\left\|u(t)\right\|_{L^{\infty}}.

The justification of the formal asymptotic expansion derived in the
previous subsection relies on a Gronwall type argument. The error that
we want to control is
\widehat{w}-\varepsilon\widehat{v}_{0}-\varepsilon^{3}\widehat{\nu}_{2}
, which solves, by construction (keeping in mind that
v_{0}
does
not depend on time),

{\partial}_{t}\left(\widehat{w}-\varepsilon\widehat{v}_{0}-\varepsilon^{3}\widehat{\nu}_{2}\right)=\frac{\lambda}{t}B(I_{1}^{\varepsilon}+I_{2}^{\varepsilon})-\frac{\lambda}{t}\varepsilon^{3}(J_{1}+J_{2}).

We rewrite the right hand side as

\frac{\lambda}{t}B\left(I_{1}^{\varepsilon}+I_{2}^{\varepsilon}-\varepsilon^{3}J_{1}-\varepsilon^{3}J_{2}\right)+\frac{\lambda}{t}(B-1)\varepsilon^{3}(J_{1}+J_{2}),

and the last term will be considered as a source term. Indeed, from
the definition

B(t)=e^{i\lambda\int_{1}^{t}|\widehat{v}(\tau)|^{2}\frac{d\tau}{\tau}}=e^{i\lambda\int_{1}^{t}|\widehat{w}(\tau)|^{2}\frac{d\tau}{\tau}},

so from (
6.1
),

(6.2)

\|B(t)-1\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\varepsilon^{2}\log t,

and Lemma
6.1
yields

(6.3)

\left\|\varepsilon^{3}(B-1)(J_{1}+J_{2})\right\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\varepsilon^{5}\frac{\log t}{t^{(1-\gamma)/2}},

for any
1/2<\gamma<1
.

We now focus on the term
B(I_{1}^{\varepsilon}+I_{2}^{\varepsilon}-\varepsilon^{3}J_{1}-\varepsilon^{3}J_{2})
,
to estimate it in
L^{2}
,
and examine successively
B(I_{1}^{\varepsilon}-\varepsilon^{3}J_{1})

and
B(I_{2}^{\varepsilon}-\varepsilon^{3}J_{2})
. First, by definition,

I_{1}^{\varepsilon}-\varepsilon^{3}J_{1}=\mathcal{F}\left(M(-t)-1\right)\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(\left|\widehat{M(t)v}\right|^{2}\widehat{M(t)v}-\varepsilon^{3}\left|\widehat{M(t)v_{0}}\right|^{2}\widehat{M(t)v_{0}}\right),

so the term
v-\varepsilon v_{0}
is naturally factored out, and we must relate
it to the left hand side, involving
w-\varepsilon v_{0}-\varepsilon^{3}\nu_{2}
. Since

B\overline{B}=1
, we can write

\widehat{\rho}:=\widehat{w}-\varepsilon\widehat{v}_{0}-\varepsilon^{3}\widehat{\nu}_{2}=B\widehat{v}-\varepsilon\widehat{v}_{0}-\varepsilon^{3}\widehat{\nu}_{2}=B\left(\widehat{v}-\varepsilon\overline{B}\widehat{v}_{0}-\varepsilon^{3}\overline{B}\widehat{\nu}_{2}\right),

so we have

(6.4)

\widehat{v}-\varepsilon\widehat{v}_{0}=\overline{B}\left(\widehat{w}-\varepsilon\widehat{v}_{0}-\varepsilon^{3}\widehat{\nu}_{2}\right)+\varepsilon(\overline{B}-1)\widehat{v}_{0}+\varepsilon^{3}\overline{B}\widehat{\nu}_{2}.

The first term on the right hand side will be treated differently from
the last two terms, which will be considered as source terms. Using
the formula

|z_{2}|^{2}z_{2}-|z_{1}|^{2}z_{1}=|z_{2}|^{2}(z_{2}-z_{1})+z_{1}\RE(z_{2}-z_{1})(\overline{z}_{2}+\overline{z}_{1}),

with
z_{2}=\mathcal{F}(Mv)
and
z_{1}=\varepsilon\mathcal{F}(Mv_{0})
, as well as
(
6.4
), yielding

(6.5)

\mathcal{F}\left(M(v-\varepsilon v_{0})\right)=\mathcal{F}M\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(\overline{B}\left(\widehat{w}-\varepsilon\widehat{v}_{0}-\varepsilon^{3}\widehat{\nu}_{2}\right)+\varepsilon(\overline{B}-1)\widehat{v}_{0}+\varepsilon^{3}\overline{B}\widehat{\nu}_{2}\right),

we write

\|I_{1}^{\varepsilon}-\varepsilon^{3}J_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\leqslant\|G_{1}\|_{L^{2}}+\|S_{1}\|_{L^{2}}+\|S_{2}\|_{L^{2}},

where, simplifying the algebraic structure for the sake of presentation,

\displaystyle G_{1}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}\left(M^{-1}-1\right)\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(\left(|\mathcal{F}(Mv)|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}|\mathcal{F}(Mv_{0})|^{2}\right)\mathcal{F}M\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(\overline{B}\widehat{\rho}\right)\right),

\displaystyle S_{1}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\mathcal{F}\left(M^{-1}-1\right)\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(\left(|\mathcal{F}(Mv)|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}|\mathcal{F}(Mv_{0})|^{2}\right)\mathcal{F}M\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left((\overline{B}-1)\widehat{v}_{0}\right)\right),

\displaystyle S_{2}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{3}\mathcal{F}\left(M^{-1}-1\right)\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(\left(|\mathcal{F}(Mv)|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}|\mathcal{F}(Mv_{0})|^{2}\right)\mathcal{F}M\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(\overline{B}\widehat{\nu}_{2}\right)\right).

We have left out the time variable in the expression
of
M
in order to lighten the notation, and do so below for the
same reason.
The Gronwall term
G_{1}
is estimated by merely using the boundedness
on
L^{2}
of
\mathcal{F}\left(M^{-1}-1\right)\mathcal{F}^{-1}
, and the unitarity of
\mathcal{F}M\mathcal{F}^{-1}
, and we write

\|G_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\left(\|\mathcal{F}(Mv)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\|\mathcal{F}(Mv_{0})\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\right)\|\widehat{\rho}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\varepsilon^{2}\|\widehat{\rho}\|_{L^{2}},

by using (
6.1
). For the source term
S_{1}
and

S_{2}
, we invoke the same arguments as in the proof of
Lemma
2.2
, and the fact that
\mathcal{F}M\mathcal{F}^{-1}
is unitary on
H^{s}
for any
s\geqslant 0
:

\displaystyle\|S_{1}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{t}}\left\|\left(|\mathcal{F}(Mv)|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}|\mathcal{F}(Mv_{0})|^{2}\right)\mathcal{F}M\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left((\overline{B}-1)\widehat{v}_{0}\right)\right\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{t}}\left(\|v\|_{\mathcal{F}(H^{1})}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\|v_{0}\|_{\mathcal{F}(H^{1})}^{2}\right)\|(\overline{B}-1)\widehat{v}_{0}\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon^{3}}{\sqrt{t}}t^{2C\varepsilon^{2}}\|(\overline{B}-1)\widehat{v}_{0}\|_{H^{1}},

where we have used (
5.3
) for the last inequality. The last
term is controlled by

\|(\overline{B}-1)\widehat{v}_{0}\|_{H^{1}}\leqslant\|\overline{B}-1\|_{L^{\infty}}\|v_{0}\|_{\Sigma}+\|\widehat{v}_{0}{\partial}_{x}\overline{B}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\varepsilon^{2}\log t+\|\widehat{v}_{0}{\partial}_{x}\overline{B}\|_{L^{2}},

in view of (
6.2
). Writing

\widehat{v}_{0}{\partial}_{x}\overline{B}=-2i\lambda\widehat{v}_{0}\int_{1}^{t}\RE\left(\overline{\widehat{v}}{\partial}_{x}\widehat{v}\right)\frac{d\tau}{\tau},

(
5.3
) now yields

\|\widehat{v}_{0}{\partial}_{x}\overline{B}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|v_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\int_{1}^{t}\|\widehat{v}(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|{\partial}_{x}\widehat{v}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}\frac{d\tau}{\tau}\lesssim\varepsilon^{2}\int_{1}^{t}\frac{d\tau}{\tau^{1-C\varepsilon^{2}}}\lesssim\varepsilon^{2}t^{C\varepsilon^{2}}.

The estimate for
S_{2}
is rather similar:

\displaystyle\|S_{2}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon^{3}}{t^{1/2}}\left\|\left(|\mathcal{F}(Mv)|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}|\mathcal{F}(Mv_{0})|^{2}\right)\mathcal{F}M\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(\overline{B}\widehat{\nu}_{2}\right)\right\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon^{3}}{t^{1/2}}\left(\|\mathcal{F}(Mv)\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\|\mathcal{F}(Mv_{0})\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\right)\|\overline{B}\widehat{\nu}_{2}\|_{H^{1}}\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon^{5}}{t^{1/2-2C\varepsilon^{2}}}\|\overline{B}\widehat{\nu}_{2}\|_{H^{1}}.

The last term is controlled via Leibniz formula, invoking
Lemma
6.1
,

\|\overline{B}\widehat{\nu}_{2}\|_{H^{1}}\leqslant\|\widehat{\nu}_{2}\|_{H^{1}}+\|\widehat{\nu}_{2}{\partial}_{x}\overline{B}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\log t+\varepsilon^{2}t^{C\varepsilon^{2}},

and the source terms are estimated by

\|S_{1}\|_{L^{2}}+\|S_{2}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\varepsilon^{5}\frac{\log t}{t^{1/2-3C\varepsilon^{2}}}.

We now turn to the
L^{2}
estimate of
I_{2}^{\varepsilon}-\varepsilon^{3}J_{2}
, and proceed
along the same spirit.

I_{2}^{\varepsilon}-\varepsilon^{3}J_{2}=\left|\widehat{M(t)v}\right|^{2}\widehat{M(t)v}-|\widehat{v}|^{2}\widehat{v}-\varepsilon^{3}\left|\widehat{M(t)v_{0}}\right|^{2}\widehat{M(t)v_{0}}+\varepsilon^{3}|\widehat{v}_{0}|^{2}\widehat{v}_{0}.

We distinguish
\left|\widehat{M(t)v}\right|^{2}\widehat{M(t)v}-\varepsilon^{3}\left|\widehat{M(t)v_{0}}\right|^{2}\widehat{M(t)v_{0}}
and
|\widehat{v}|^{2}\widehat{v}-\varepsilon^{3}|\widehat{v}_{0}|^{2}\widehat{v}_{0}
.
Discarding the precise algebraic structure like before,

\left|\widehat{M(t)v}\right|^{2}\widehat{M(t)v}-\varepsilon^{3}\left|\widehat{M(t)v_{0}}\right|^{2}\widehat{M(t)v_{0}}\approx\left(\left|\widehat{M(t)v}\right|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\left|\widehat{M(t)v_{0}}\right|^{2}\right)\mathcal{F}M(v-\varepsilon v_{0}).

The last factor is again rewritten thanks to (
6.5
),
and we estimate
I_{2}^{\varepsilon}-\varepsilon^{3}J_{2}
as

\|I_{2}^{\varepsilon}-\varepsilon^{3}J_{2}\|_{L^{2}}\leqslant\|G_{2}\|_{L^{2}}+\|S_{3}\|_{L^{2}}+\|S_{4}\|_{L^{2}},

where

\displaystyle G_{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\left|\widehat{M(t)v}\right|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\left|\widehat{M(t)v_{0}}\right|^{2}\right)\mathcal{F}M\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(\overline{B}\left(\widehat{w}-\varepsilon\widehat{v}_{0}-\varepsilon^{3}\widehat{\nu}_{2}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\quad-\left(\left|\widehat{v}\right|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\left|\widehat{v_{0}}\right|^{2}\right)\overline{B}\left(\widehat{w}-\varepsilon\widehat{v}_{0}-\varepsilon^{3}\widehat{\nu}_{2}\right),

\displaystyle S_{3}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\left(\left|\widehat{M(t)v}\right|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\left|\widehat{M(t)v_{0}}\right|^{2}\right)\mathcal{F}M\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(\left(\overline{B}-1\right)\widehat{v}_{0}\right)

\displaystyle\quad-\varepsilon\left(\left|\widehat{v}\right|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\left|\widehat{v_{0}}\right|^{2}\right)\left(\overline{B}-1\right)\widehat{v}_{0},

\displaystyle S_{4}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{3}\left(\left|\widehat{M(t)v}\right|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\left|\widehat{M(t)v_{0}}\right|^{2}\right)\mathcal{F}M\mathcal{F}^{-1}\widehat{\nu}_{2}-\varepsilon^{3}\left(\left|\widehat{v}\right|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\left|\widehat{v_{0}}\right|^{2}\right)\widehat{\nu}_{2}.

For the Gronwall term
G_{2}
, we proceed like for
G_{1}
, and write

\|G_{2}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\varepsilon^{2}\|\widehat{w}-\varepsilon\widehat{v}_{0}-\varepsilon^{3}\widehat{\nu}_{2}\|_{L^{2}}=\varepsilon^{2}\|\widehat{\rho}\|_{L^{2}}.

The source terms
S_{3}
and
S_{4}
are readily of size
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{5})
; we
recover some decay in time by making the quantity
M-1
appear
systematically. In the case of
S_{3}
, we write

\displaystyle S_{3}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\left(\left|\widehat{M(t)v}\right|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\left|\widehat{M(t)v_{0}}\right|^{2}\right)\mathcal{F}M\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(\left(\overline{B}-1\right)\widehat{v}_{0}\right)

\displaystyle\quad-\varepsilon\left(\left|\widehat{v}\right|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\left|\widehat{v_{0}}\right|^{2}\right)\left(\overline{B}-1\right)\widehat{v}_{0}\pm\varepsilon\left(\left|\widehat{M(t)v}\right|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\left|\widehat{M(t)v_{0}}\right|^{2}\right)\left(\overline{B}-1\right)\widehat{v}_{0},

and estimate as follows:

\displaystyle\|S_{3}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{3}\|(M-1)\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(\left(\overline{B}-1\right)\widehat{v}_{0}\right)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\|\left(\overline{B}-1\right)\widehat{v}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|(M-1)\widehat{v}\|_{L^{2}}\left(\|\widehat{Mv}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\widehat{v}\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon^{3}}{\sqrt{t}}\|x\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(\left(\overline{B}-1\right)\widehat{v}_{0}\right)\|_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon^{5}\frac{\log t}{\sqrt{t}}\times\|x\widehat{v}\|_{L^{2}},

where we have used (
6.1
) and (
6.2
). We
have already estimated the
H^{1}
-norm of
\left(\overline{B}-1\right)\widehat{v}_{0}
,

\|\left(\overline{B}-1\right)\widehat{v}_{0}\|_{H^{1}}\lesssim\varepsilon^{2}\left(\log t+t^{C\varepsilon^{2}}\right),

and therefore

\|S_{3}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\varepsilon^{5}\frac{\log t}{t^{1/2-C\varepsilon^{2}}}.

The term
S_{4}
is controlled similarly, by using the same ideas as
above, and we come up with:

\frac{d}{dt}\|\widehat{\rho}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{t}\|\widehat{\rho}\|_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon^{5}\frac{\log t}{t^{3/2-3C\varepsilon^{2}}}.

Gronwall lemma then implies, provided that
\varepsilon>0
is
sufficiently small:

Proposition 6.2
.

Suppose that
u^{\varepsilon}
solves (
1.1
), with
u^{\varepsilon}_{\mid t=0}=u_{0}^{\varepsilon}\in\Sigma
such that

u_{0}^{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon v_{0}+\varepsilon^{3}w_{2}+\mathcal{O}\left(\varepsilon^{5-\eta}\right),

for
v_{0},w_{2}\in\Sigma
, and some
0<\eta<2
. Then we have

\sup_{t\geqslant 1}\|\widehat{w}(t)-\varepsilon\widehat{v}_{0}-\varepsilon^{3}\widehat{\nu}_{2}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\varepsilon^{5-\eta},

where
\nu_{2}
is defined in Lemma
6.1
.

In particular, letting
t
go to infinity, we infer

W=\varepsilon\widehat{v}_{0}+\varepsilon^{3}\widehat{\nu}_{2}^{\infty}+\mathcal{O}\left(\varepsilon^{5-\eta}\right)\quad\text{in }L^{2}.

As
u_{+}^{\varepsilon}=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(We^{-i\Phi}\right)
, and we have seen at the end
of Section
5
that
\|\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}}=\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{4})
,
we infer

u_{+}^{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon v_{0}+\varepsilon^{3}\nu_{2}+\mathcal{O}\left(\varepsilon^{5-\eta}\right)\quad\text{in }L^{2},

thus completing the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

A parafermionic hypergeometric function and supersymmetric 6 ​ j 6j -symbols


Abstract
We study properties of a parafermionic generalization of the hyperbolic hypergeometric function appearing as the most important part in the fusion matrix for Liouville field theory and the Racah-Wigner symbols for the Faddeev modular double. We show that this generalized hypergeometric function is a limiting form of the rarefied elliptic hypergeometric function V ( r ) V^{(r)} and derive its transformation properties and a mixed difference-recurrence equation satisfied by it. At the intermediate level we describe symmetries of a more general rarefied hyperbolic hypergeometric function. An important r = 2 r=2 case corresponds to the supersymmetric hypergeometric function given by the integral appearing in the fusion matrix of N = 1 N=1 super Liouville field theory and the Racah-Wigner symbols of the quantum algebra U q ​ ( osp ​ ( 1 | 2 ) ) {\rm U}_{q}({\rm osp}(1|2)) . We indicate relations to the standard Regge symmetry and prove some previous conjectures for the supersymmetric Racah-Wigner symbols by establishing their different parametrizations.

In the case of

For the Liouville field theory and Ruijsenaars-Schneider model a special integral of

It enters as the most essential part in the fusion matrix of the former model and gives eigenfunctions

-symbols for the Faddeev modular double derived by Ponsot and Teschner

Various generalizations of the mentioned problems, such as studies of

In this paper we study a parafermionic generalization of the Ruijsenaars hyperbolic hypergeometric function

We show that this function can be derived as a degeneration of the rarefied elliptic hypergeometric

[

Applying a specific limiting procedure to the transformations of the

-function found in

We consider in detail the supersymmetric case corresponding to

Specializing the so-called second symmetry transformation to

Then we check that the restriction to the Neveu-Schwarz sector of

Properties of the rarefied elliptic and hyperbolic gamma functions

The standard elliptic gamma function
\Gamma(z;p,q)
can be defined as a double infinite product:

\Gamma(z;p,q)=\prod_{j,k=0}^{\infty}{1-z^{-1}p^{j+1}q^{k+1}\over 1-zp^{j}q^{k}}\,,\quad|p|,|q|<1\,,\quad z\in\mathbb{C}^{*}.

(1)

It is symmetric in bases
p
and
q
,
\Gamma(z;p,q)=\Gamma(z;q,p)
, and satisfies the equations

\Gamma(qz;p,q)=\theta(z;p)\Gamma(z;q,p),\qquad\Gamma(pz;p,q)=\theta(z;q)\Gamma(z;q,p),

where
\theta(z;p)
is a short Jacobi theta-function

\theta(z;q)=(z;q)_{\infty}(qz^{-1};q)_{\infty},\quad(z;q)_{\infty}:=\prod_{j=0}^{\infty}(1-zq^{j}),

which is related to the standard Jacobi
\theta_{1}
-function as follows

\displaystyle\theta_{1}(u|\tau)=-\theta_{11}(u)=-\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}+1/2}e^{\pi\textup{i}\tau\ell^{2}}e^{2\pi\textup{i}\ell(u+1/2)}

\displaystyle\makebox[40.00006pt]{}=\textup{i}q^{1/8}e^{-\pi\textup{i}u}(q;q)_{\infty}\theta(e^{2\pi\textup{i}u};q),\qquad q=e^{2\pi\textup{i}\tau}\,.

The elliptic gamma function associated with the simplest lens space is defined as a product
of two standard elliptic gamma functions with different bases
[
19
]
,

\displaystyle\gamma_{e}(z,m;p,q)=\Gamma(zp^{m};p^{r},pq)\Gamma(zq^{r-m};q^{r},pq)

(2)

\displaystyle\makebox[20.00003pt]{}=\prod^{\infty}_{j,k=0}{1-z^{-1}p^{-m}(pq)^{j+1}p^{r(k+1)}\over 1-zp^{m}(pq)^{j}p^{rk}}{1-z^{-1}q^{m}(pq)^{j+1}q^{rk}\over 1-zq^{r-m}(pq)^{j}q^{rk}}\,,\quad m\in\mathbb{Z}.

As shown in
[
31
]
, function (
2
) can be written as a special
product of the standard elliptic gamma functions
with bases
p^{r}
and
q^{r}
. For the fundamental region
0\leq m\leq r
it has the form:

\gamma_{e}(z,m;p,q)=\prod_{k=0}^{m-1}\Gamma(q^{r-m}z(pq)^{k};p^{r},q^{r})\prod_{k=0}^{r-m-1}\Gamma(p^{m}z(pq)^{k};p^{r},q^{r})\,.

The quasiperiodicity property:

{\gamma_{e}(z,m+kr;p,q)\over\gamma_{e}(z,m;p,q)}=\left(-{\sqrt{pq}\over z}\right)^{mk+r{k(k-1)\over 2}}\left({q\over p}\right)^{k\left({m^{2}\over 2}+mr{k-1\over 2}+r^{2}{(k-1)(2k-1)\over 12}\right)},\quad k\in\mathbb{Z},

(3)

established in
[
31
]
, implies, that for all other values of
m
, the function
\gamma_{e}(z,m;p,q)
is determined by its form in the fundamental interval of
m
.
For further use, it is also convenient to introduce the function:

\displaystyle\Gamma^{(r)}(z,m;p,q)=(-z)^{m(m-1)\over 2}p^{m(m-1)(m-2)\over 6}q^{-{m(m-1)(m+1)\over 6}}\gamma_{e}(z,m;p,q)\,.

(4)

The elliptic gamma function has the following asymptotic behaviour
[
21

18
]
:

\Gamma(e^{-2\pi vy};e^{-2\pi v\omega_{1}},e^{-2\pi v\omega_{2}})\ \mathrel{\mathop{=}\limits_{v\to 0}}e^{-\pi(2y-\omega_{1}-\omega_{2})/12v\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}\gamma^{(2)}(y;\omega_{1},\omega_{2}),

(5)

where

is the Faddeev modular quantum dilogarithm

[
8

]
or the hyperbolic gamma function

The parameter

We use the shorthand notation

when the quasiperiods coincide with

and
\omega_{2}
. Otherwise the quasiperiods will be written
explicitly. The function

\gamma^{(2)}(y;\mathbf{\omega})=\exp\left(-\int_{0}^{\infty}\left({\sinh(2y-\omega_{1}-\omega_{2})x\over 2\sinh(\omega_{1}x)\sinh(\omega_{2}x)}-{2y-\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}\over 2\omega_{1}\omega_{2}x}\right)\right){dx\over x}

\displaystyle\ \mathrel{\mathop{\lim}\limits_{y\to\infty}}\Lambda(y;m;\mathbf{\omega})=e^{-{\textup{i}\pi\over 2}\left({1\over r}B_{2,2}(y;\mathbf{\omega})+{m^{2}\over r}-m+{r\over 6}-{1\over 6r}\right)},\quad{\rm for}\;{\rm arg}\;\omega_{1}<{\rm arg}\;y<{\rm arg}\;\omega_{2}+\pi,

Symmetries of

-symbols for the Faddeev modular double

In this section we review known symmetry properties of the following hyperbolic hypergeometric function
introduced by Ruijsenaars in 1994

J_{h}(\underline{\beta},\underline{\gamma})=\int_{-\textup{i}\infty}^{\textup{i}\infty}\prod_{a=1}^{4}\gamma^{(2)}(\beta_{a}-z;\mathbf{\omega})\gamma^{(2)}(\gamma_{a}+z;\mathbf{\omega})\frac{dz}{\textup{i}\sqrt{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}}

(16)

\beta_{a}

\gamma_{a}
satisfying the balancing condition

\sum_{a=1}^{4}(\gamma_{a}+\beta_{a})=2(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}),

The contour of integration in (

going to infinity in the right half-plane from those lying in the left half-plane.
For generic values of
\beta_{a}
and
\gamma_{a}
such a contour always exists. With the conventional choice

\omega_{1}=b
and
\omega_{2}=b^{-1}

of
6j
-symbols of the Faddeev modular quantum double and fusion matrix of the Liouville field theory
with the central charge

[
17
,
32
]

7.1
Some basics on

N=1
super Liouville field theory is defined on a two-dimensional surface with
the metric

by the local Lagrangian density

{\cal L}={1\over 2\pi}g_{ab}\partial_{a}\varphi\partial_{b}\varphi+{1\over 2\pi}(\psi\bar{\partial}\psi+\bar{\psi}\partial\bar{\psi})+2\textup{i}\mu b^{2}\bar{\psi}\psi e^{b\varphi}+2\pi\mu^{2}b^{2}e^{2b\varphi}.

(75)

The energy-momentum tensor and the superconformal current are

\displaystyle T=-{1\over 2}(\partial\varphi\partial\varphi-Q\partial^{2}\varphi+\psi\partial\psi),\quad G=\textup{i}(\psi\partial\varphi-Q\partial\psi),

(76)

\displaystyle[L_{m},L_{n}]=(m-n)L_{m+n}+{c\over 12}m(m^{2}-1)\delta_{m+n},

\displaystyle[L_{m},G_{k}]={m-2k\over 2}G_{m+k},

(77)

\displaystyle\{G_{k},G_{l}\}=2L_{l+k}+{c\over 3}\left(k^{2}-{1\over 4}\right)\delta_{k+l},

with the central charge

Here

and

Since in the Neveu-Schwarz sector we have supercurrent generators

with half-integer

descendant fields are broken into two representations of the plain Virasoro algebra
of integer and half-integer level. Thus in the Neveu-Schwarz sector there are two types of Virasoro

N_{\alpha}=e^{\alpha\varphi}
and has the conformal dimension

\Delta_{\alpha}={1\over 2}\alpha(Q-\alpha).

The physical states have
\alpha={Q\over 2}+\textup{i}P

The Ramond vertex operators and their dimensions are

where

\psi(z)\sigma^{\pm}(0)\sim{\sigma^{\mp}(0)\over\sqrt{z}}

The Neveu-Schwarz and Ramond operators with the same conformal dimensions are proportional to each other,

N_{\alpha}\propto N_{Q-\alpha},\,R_{\alpha}\propto R_{Q-\alpha}.

Invariance and near invariance for non-cyclic shift semigroups
Yuxia Liang, Jonathan R. Partington

Abstract
. We characterize the subspaces of H 2 ​ ( 𝔻 ) H^{2}(\mathbb{D}) that are invariant under the semigroups generated by two higher-order shifts S m S^{m} and S n S^{n} . Complete descriptions are obtained for both invariant and nearly invariant subspaces associated with these operators and their adjoints. The approach is based on vector-valued Hardy spaces, the Beurling–Lax theorem, and matrix-valued inner functions. Finally, we apply these results to non-cyclic shift semigroups and to Toeplitz operators induced by finite Blaschke products.

1.
Introduction

Invariant subspaces of operators on Hilbert spaces have been a central
topic connecting operator theory, complex analysis, and functional
analysis. A fundamental result is Beurling’s theorem, which characterizes
the invariant subspaces of the unilateral shift
S
on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})

as
\theta H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
, where
\theta
is an inner function
(see, e.g.,
[
20
, A. Cor. 1.4.1]
).

For an isometry
T
on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
, a closed subspace
\mathcal{M}
is called nearly

T^{*}
-invariant if

f\in T(H^{2})\cap\mathcal{M}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad T^{*}f\in\mathcal{M}.

In particular, nearly
S^{*}
-invariant subspaces have been extensively
studied by Hayashi, Hitt and Sarason in connection with Toeplitz kernels and model
spaces; see
[
12
,
13
,
23
,
24
]
and related developments

[
2
,
3
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
10
,
11
,
19
,
21
]
. More recently, the notion of nearly invariant subspaces has been extended
to left invertible operators and
C_{0}
semigroups, leading to a broader
framework for studying invariant structures of operator semigroups

[
16
,
17
,
18
]
.

Motivated by these developments, we study a different type of invariance:
simultaneous invariance under distinct powers of the unilateral shift.
Although Beurling’s theorem gives a complete description for
S
,
the structure of subspaces invariant under two higher powers

S^{m}
and
S^{n}
with
n>m
remains largely unexplored. In particular,
we ask whether there exist nontrivial subspaces invariant under both
operators but not invariant under
S
.

Simple examples show that such phenomena do indeed occur. For instance,
the closed span generated by the set

\{z^{2n+3m}:m,n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}
is invariant under both
S^{2}
and

S^{3}
, but is neither
S
-invariant nor nearly
S^{*}
-invariant.
Similar examples arise for other pairs of powers. These observations
indicate that simultaneous higher-order invariance possesses a richer
structure than the classical shift-invariant theory.

The main purpose of this paper is to provide a complete characterization
of these subspaces. Our approach is based on the identification of

H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
with vector-valued Hardy spaces through the isometric
isomorphism
T_{m}
in (
4.1
), together with the Beurling–Lax theorem and
matrix-valued inner functions.

A key operator in this analysis is the matrix-valued isometry

\displaystyle\Sigma_{m,\gamma}^{k}:=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}O_{\gamma\times(m-\gamma)}&S^{k+1}I_{\gamma\times\gamma}\\
S^{k}I_{(m-\gamma)\times(m-\gamma)}&O_{(m-\gamma)\times\gamma}\end{array}\right)_{m\times m},

where
m\geq 2,\ k\geq 1\ \text{and}\ \gamma\in\{1,2,\cdots,m-1\}.

Here
O_{m\times n}
and
I_{m\times n}
denote the
zero and identity matrices, respectively. Besides, let
H^{\infty}_{r\times r}
be the collection of all bounded analytic
r\times r
matrix-valued functions for
r\geq 1
.

The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 characterizes the subspaces invariant under
S^{2}
and
S^{2k+1}
and provides examples illustrating the difference from classical
S
-invariance. Section 3 develops the
corresponding theory of nearly invariant subspaces for the adjoint
operators. Section 4 extends the results to general pairs

S^{m}
and
S^{km+\gamma}
and applies them to non-cyclic shift
semigroups. Finally, we transfer these characterizations to Toeplitz
operators induced by finite Blaschke products.

2.
Invariance under both
S^{2}
and
S^{2k+1}

In this section, we characterize the subspaces invariant under both

S^{2}
and
S^{2k+1}
for
k\geq 1
by using the matrix

\Sigma_{2,1}^{k}
(corresponding to
m=2
and
\gamma=1
in
(
1
)) and the Beurling–Lax theorem on vector-valued Hardy
spaces.

We briefly recall that the vector-valued Hardy space

H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{n})
consists of analytic functions

F=(f_{1},\cdots,f_{n})
with
f_{j}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
.
An analytic function

\Phi:\mathbb{D}\to\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{C}^{r},\mathbb{C}^{n})
is called inner
if
\Phi\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D},\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{C}^{r},\mathbb{C}^{n}))

and
\Phi(e^{it})
is an isometry almost everywhere on
\mathbb{T}
.
We shall use the following form of the Beurling–Lax theorem
(see, e.g.,
[
15
,
22
]
).

Theorem 2.1
.

A subspace

\mathcal{K}
of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{n})
is
S
-invariant if and only if
\mathcal{K}=\Theta_{n\times r}H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{r})
, where
0\leq r\leq n
and
\Theta_{n\times r}
is an operator-valued inner function with values in
\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{C}^{r},\mathbb{C}^{n})
.

Meanwhile, the corresponding model space
K_{\Theta_{n\times r}}:=H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{n})\ominus\Theta_{n\times r}H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{r})
is
S^{*}
-invariant for
0\leq r\leq n
.

Although the
S^{2}
-invariant subspaces have been studied by several
approaches (see, e.g.,
[
9
,
14
]
), we adopt a vector-valued
Hardy space framework. Indeed, every
f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
admits the
decomposition
f(z)=f_{0}(z^{2})+zf_{1}(z^{2})
with
f_{0},f_{1}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
. Thus the mapping

\displaystyle T_{2}(f_{0}(z),f_{1}(z))=f_{0}(z^{2})+zf_{1}(z^{2})

(2.1)

defines an isometric isomorphism from

H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{2})
onto
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
. Since

S^{2}(f_{0}(z^{2})+zf_{1}(z^{2}))=(Sf_{0})(z^{2})+z(Sf_{1})(z^{2}),

the following commutative diagram holds:

\displaystyle\begin{CD}H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{2})@>{S}>{}>H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{2})\\
@V{}V{T_{2}}V@V{}V{T_{2}}V\\
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})@>{S^{2}}>{}>H^{2}(\mathbb{D}).\end{CD}

(2.2)

Hence,
S^{2}
on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
is equivalent, via
T_{2}
, to the vector-valued shift

S
on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{2})
. Theorem
2.1
then yields
the following description of
S
-invariant subspaces.

Proposition 2.2
.

A subspace
\mathcal{N}
of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{2})
is
S
-invariant if and only if
\mathcal{N}=\Theta_{2\times r}H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{r})
, where
0\leq r\leq 2
and
\Theta_{2\times r}
is an operator-valued inner function with values in
\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{C}^{r},\mathbb{C}^{2})
.

By Proposition
2.2
, we can proceed to the
S^{2}
and
S^{2k+1}
-invariant subspace in
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
.

Theorem 2.3
.

For
k\geq 1,
a subspace
\mathcal{M}
of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
is invariant under both
S^{2}
and
S^{2k+1}
if and only if
\mathcal{M}=T_{2}(\Theta_{2\times r}H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{r}))
, where
0\leq r\leq 2
, the inner function
\Theta_{2\times r}
takes values in
\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{C}^{r},\mathbb{C}^{2})
and satisfies
\Theta_{2\times r}^{*}\Sigma_{2,1}^{k}\Theta_{2\times r}\in H^{\infty}_{r\times r}
, and
T_{2}
is an isometric isomorphism as in (
2.1
).

Proof.

We first prove the sufficiency. By Proposition
2.2
,

\Theta_{2\times r}H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{r})
is invariant under the vector-valued shift. Hence, by (
2.2
),

\mathcal{M}=T_{2}(\Theta_{2\times r}H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{r}))

is
S^{2}
-invariant.

For
F\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{r})
, we have

T_{2}(\Theta_{2\times r}F)=(1,z)(\Theta_{2\times r}F)(z^{2}).

Therefore,

\displaystyle S^{2k+1}T_{2}(\Theta_{2\times r}F)

\displaystyle=(z^{2k+1},z^{2k+2})(\Theta_{2\times r}F)(z^{2})

\displaystyle=(1,z)\left(\Sigma_{2,1}^{k}\Theta_{2\times r}F\right)(z^{2}).

Since

\Theta_{2\times r}^{*}\Sigma_{2,1}^{k}\Theta_{2\times r}\in H^{\infty}_{r\times r}
,
the multiplier theorem for vector-valued Hardy spaces
(
[
1
, Theorem 4.14]
) gives

\Sigma_{2,1}^{k}\Theta_{2\times r}H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{r})\subset\Theta_{2\times r}H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{r}).

Therefore

S^{2k+1}\mathcal{M}\subset\mathcal{M}
, proving the sufficiency.

Conversely, let
\mathcal{M}
be invariant under both
S^{2}
and
S^{2k+1}
.
By Proposition
2.2
,

\mathcal{M}=T_{2}(\Theta_{2\times r}H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{r}))

for some inner matrix function
\Theta_{2\times r}
.

The
S^{2k+1}
-invariance is equivalent to

\Sigma_{2,1}^{k}\Theta_{2\times r}H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{r})\subset\Theta_{2\times r}H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{r}).

Hence

\Theta_{2\times r}^{*}\Sigma_{2,1}^{k}\Theta_{2\times r}

acts boundedly on

H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{r})
.
Applying again

[
1
, Theorem 4.14]
,
we conclude that

\Theta_{2\times r}^{*}\Sigma_{2,1}^{k}\Theta_{2\times r}\in H^{\infty}_{r\times r},

which completes the proof.
∎

By Theorem
2.3
, a function
f_{0}(z^{2})+zf_{1}(z^{2})\in\mathcal{M}^{\perp}
if and only if

\displaystyle\left\langle(f_{0},f_{1})^{\mathsf{t}},\Theta_{2\times r}F\right\rangle=0\;\mbox{for all}\;F\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{r}).

Now we conclude the result on
(S^{2})^{*}
and
(S^{2k+1})^{*}
-invariant subspaces.

For
k\geq 1,
a subspace
\mathcal{N}
of

and

\Theta_{2\times r}

and satisfies
\Theta_{2\times r}^{*}\Sigma_{2,1}^{k}\Theta_{2\times r}\in H^{\infty}_{r\times r}
, and

is an isometric isomorphism as in (

Letting

3.
near invariance under both
(S^{2})^{*}
and
(S^{2k+1})^{*}

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a nearly
(S^{2})^{*}
and
(S^{2k+1})^{*}
-invariant subspace for
k\geq 1
. Set

X:=\mathcal{M}\ominus(\mathcal{M}\cap S^{2}H^{2}(\mathbb{D})).

Let
\hat{k}_{0}(z)
and
\hat{k}_{1}(z)
be the orthogonal projections of
1
and
z
on
X
, respectively. We apply the Gram–Schmidt orthogonalization to the set
\{\hat{k}_{0}(z),\hat{k}_{1}(z)\}
and then normalize them to obtain an orthonormal set denoted by
\{e_{0}(z),e_{1}(z)\}
, i.e.,
E_{2}(z)
is the
2\times 1
matrix whose columns are the orthonormalization of the reproducing kernel and the first-derivative kernel for
\mathcal{M}
at
0.
Denote by
P_{2}
the orthogonal projection onto
X
and

\displaystyle E_{2}(z)=\left(e_{0},\;e_{1}\right)^{\mathsf{t}}(z).

(3.1)

Since
\mathcal{M}
is nearly
(S^{2})^{*}
-invariant, every
f\in\mathcal{M}
admits the decomposition

f(z)=(a_{0},\;b_{0})E_{2}(z)+S^{2}f_{1}(z).

Considering
f_{1}\perp e_{0}
and
f_{1}\perp e_{1}
, the near
(S^{2})^{*}
-invariance of
\mathcal{M}
implies
f_{1}\in\mathcal{M}
and
f_{1}(z)=(S^{2})^{*}(I-P_{2})f(z)
. Meanwhile,

\|f\|^{2}=|a_{0}|^{2}+|b_{0}|^{2}+\|f_{1}\|^{2}.
Then we repeat this process for
f_{1}
, getting that

\displaystyle f_{1}(z)=(a_{1},\;b_{1})E_{2}(z)+S^{2}f_{2}(z)

(3.2)

with
f_{2}\in\mathcal{M}
,
f_{2}(z)=(S^{2})^{*}(I-P_{2})f_{1}(z)=[(S^{2})^{*}(I-P_{2})]^{2}f(z)
, and

\|f_{1}\|^{2}=|a_{1}|^{2}+|b_{1}|^{2}+\|f_{2}\|^{2}.

Continuing recursively, we obtain

f_{j}(z)=(a_{j},\;b_{j})E_{2}(z)+S^{2}f_{j+1}(z)

with
f_{j+1}\in\mathcal{M}
,
f_{j+1}(z)=[(S^{2})^{*}(I-P_{2})]^{j+1}f(z)
, and
\|f_{j}\|^{2}=|a_{j}|^{2}+|b_{j}|^{2}+\|f_{j+1}\|^{2}.

Iterating these decompositions gives

f(z)=\left(\sum_{l=0}^{j}a_{l}z^{2l},\;\sum_{l=0}^{j}b_{l}z^{2l}\right)E_{2}(z)+z^{2(j+1)}f_{j+1}(z)

and

\|f\|^{2}=\sum_{l=0}^{j}|a_{l}|^{2}+\sum_{l=0}^{j}|b_{l}|^{2}+\|f_{j+1}\|^{2}.

We claim that
\|f_{j+1}\|\rightarrow 0
as
j\rightarrow\infty
. Since
(S^{2})^{*}
is
C_{0.}
and the range of the projection
P_{2}
is two-dimensional,

[
4

implies that
(I-P_{2})(S_{2})^{*}
is
C_{0.}.
This together with
[(S^{2})^{*}(I-P_{2})]^{j+1}=(S^{2})^{*}[(I-P_{2})(S_{2})^{*}]^{j}(I-P_{2})
imply that
(S^{2})^{*}(I-P_{2})
is
C_{0.}
, i.e.,
\|[(S^{2})^{*}(I-P_{2})]^{j+1}f\|\rightarrow 0
as
j\rightarrow\infty,
and hence

\displaystyle f(z)=\left(\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}a_{l}(z^{2})^{l},\;\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}b_{l}(z^{2})^{l}\right)E_{2}(z),\;z\in\mathbb{D},

(3.3)

where the sums converge in
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
norm and

\displaystyle\|f\|^{2}=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}|a_{l}|^{2}+\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}|b_{l}|^{2}.

Meanwhile, observing from (
3.2
) and (

\displaystyle f_{1}(z)=\left(\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}a_{l+1}(z^{2})^{l},\;\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}b_{l+1}(z^{2})^{l}\right)E_{2}(z)\in\mathcal{M}.

Then

if and only if

Conversely, assume that

(S^{2k+1})^{*}
-invariant if and only if there exists a unitary map

\displaystyle J_{2}:\;\mathcal{M}\rightarrow K_{\Theta_{2\times r}}\;\;\mbox{defined by}\;G(z^{2})E_{2}(z)\mapsto G(z),

where the inner function
\Theta_{2\times r}
takes values in
\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{C}^{r},\mathbb{C}^{2})
and satisfies
\Theta_{2\times r}^{*}\Sigma_{2,1}^{k}\Theta_{2\times r}\in H^{\infty}_{r\times r}
for
0\leq r\leq 2
.

Proof.

We first prove the sufficiency. By Proposition
3.1
, there exists a unitary map

J_{2}:\mathcal{M}\to K_{\Theta_{2\times r}}
such that every

f\in\mathcal{M}
can be written as

f(z)=(k_{0},k_{1})(z^{2})E_{2}(z),

where
(k_{0},k_{1})\in K_{\Theta_{2\times r}}
. And
\mathcal{M}
is nearly
(S^{2})^{*}
-invariant.

Assume that
f^{(n)}(0)=0
for
0\leq n\leq 2k
.
Then the definition of
E_{2}
in (
3.1
) yields

(S^{2k+1})^{*}f(z)=\big((S^{k+1})^{*}k_{0},(S^{k})^{*}k_{1}\big)(z^{2})\begin{pmatrix}S&0\\
0&S^{*}\end{pmatrix}E_{2}(z).

Thus, under the unitary map
J_{2}
, it remains to verify that

\big((S^{k+1})^{*}k_{0},(S^{k})^{*}k_{1}\big)(z)\begin{pmatrix}S&0\\
0&S^{*}\end{pmatrix}\in K_{\Theta_{2\times r}}.

By the definitions of
E_{2}
in (
3.1
) and
T_{2}
in (
2.1
), the above condition is equivalent to

\displaystyle(\Sigma_{2,1}^{k})^{*}(k_{0},k_{1})^{\mathsf{t}}\in K_{\Theta_{2\times r}}.

(3.7)

For any
F\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{r})
, it follows that

\displaystyle\left\langle(\Sigma_{2,1}^{k})^{*}(k_{0},k_{1})^{\mathsf{t}},\Theta_{2\times r}F\right\rangle=\left\langle(k_{0},k_{1})^{\mathsf{t}},\Sigma_{2,1}^{k}\Theta_{2\times r}F\right\rangle.

Since

\Theta_{2\times r}^{*}\Sigma_{2,1}^{k}\Theta_{2\times r}\in H^{\infty}_{r\times r}
, we obtain

\Sigma_{2,1}^{k}\Theta_{2\times r}F=\Theta_{2\times r}(\Theta_{2\times r}^{*}\Sigma_{2,1}^{k}\Theta_{2\times r})F\in\Theta_{2\times r}H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{r}).

Therefore the above inner product is zero, because

(k_{0},k_{1})\in K_{\Theta_{2\times r}}
. Hence (
3.7
) holds
and consequently
\mathcal{M}
is nearly

(S^{2k+1})^{*}
-invariant.

Conversely, assume that
\mathcal{M}
is nearly invariant with respect
to both
(S^{2})^{*}
and
(S^{2k+1})^{*}
.
By Proposition
3.1
, there exists a unitary map

J_{2}:\mathcal{M}\to K_{\Theta_{2\times r}}
, where

\Theta_{2\times r}
is inner and
0\leq r\leq 2
. The near invariance under
(S^{2k+1})^{*}
implies that

(\Sigma_{2,1}^{k})^{*}(k_{0},k_{1})^{\mathsf{t}}\in K_{\Theta_{2\times r}}

for every
(k_{0},k_{1})\in K_{\Theta_{2\times r}}
.
Hence, for every
F\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{r})
,

\left\langle(k_{0},k_{1})^{\mathsf{t}},\Sigma_{2,1}^{k}\Theta_{2\times r}F\right\rangle=0.

Since
K_{\Theta_{2\times r}}^{\perp}=\Theta_{2\times r}H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{r})
, it follows that

\Sigma_{2,1}^{k}\Theta_{2\times r}F\in\Theta_{2\times r}H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{r}).

Therefore

\Theta_{2\times r}^{*}\Sigma_{2,1}^{k}\Theta_{2\times r}

is a bounded multiplier on

H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{r})
, which gives

\Theta_{2\times r}^{*}\Sigma_{2,1}^{k}\Theta_{2\times r}\in H^{\infty}_{r\times r}.

The proof is complete.
∎

.

.

4.
invariance and near invariance under high order shifts

In this section, we study the (nearly) invariant subspaces associated with
two higher-order shifts
S^{m}
and
S^{n}
, where
n>m
. We first
characterize the subspaces invariant under both
S^{m},S^{n}
and their
adjoints. The essential cases are
n=mk+\gamma
, where

k\geq 1
and
\gamma\in\{1,\cdots,m-1\}
. For

m\geq 3
, every
f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
has the decomposition

f(z)=\sum_{l=0}^{m-1}z^{l}f_{l}(z^{m}),

where
f_{l}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
. This induces the isometric isomorphism

T_{m}:H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{m})\rightarrow H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
by

\displaystyle T_{m}(f_{0}(z),f_{1}(z),\cdots,f_{m-1}(z))=\sum_{l=0}^{m-1}z^{l}f_{l}(z^{m})

(4.1)

with
(f_{0},\;\cdots,\;f_{m-1})(z)\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{m}).
Since

S^{m}\left(\sum_{l=0}^{m-1}z^{l}f_{l}(z^{m})\right)=\sum_{l=0}^{m-1}z^{l}(Sf_{l})(z^{m}).

Then the commutative diagram below holds:

\displaystyle\begin{CD}H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{m})@>{S}>{}>H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{m})\\
@V{}V{T_{m}}V@V{}V{T_{m}}V\\
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})@>{S^{m}}>{}>H^{2}(\mathbb{D}).\end{CD}

(4.2)

Using Theorem
2.1
, the
S
-invariant subspace in
H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{m})

is expressed as
\Theta_{m\times r}H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{r})
with
\Theta_{m\times r}
is an operator-valued inner function with values in
\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{C}^{r},\mathbb{C}^{m})
and
0\leq r\leq m
.

4.1.
S^{m}
and
S^{km+\gamma}
-invariant subspaces

In this subsection, we continue illustrating the
S^{m}
and
S^{km+\gamma}
-invariant subspaces in
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
.

Theorem 4.1
.

For
m\geq 3
,
k\geq 1
and
\gamma\in\{1,\cdots,m-1\}
, a subspace
\mathcal{M}
of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
is invariant under both
S^{m}
and
S^{km+\gamma}
if and only if
\mathcal{M}=T_{m}(\Theta_{m\times r}H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{r}))
, where
0\leq r\leq m
, the inner function
\Theta_{m\times r}
takes values in
\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{C}^{r},\mathbb{C}^{m})
and satisfies
\Theta_{m\times r}^{*}\Sigma_{m,\gamma}^{k}\Theta_{m\times r}\in H^{\infty}_{r\times r}
, and
T_{m}
is an isometric isomorphism as in (
4.1
).

Proof.

Sufficiency.
By Proposition
2.2
,

\Theta_{m\times r}H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{r})
is invariant under the
vector-valued shift. Hence, using (
4.2
), the space

\mathcal{M}=T_{m}(\Theta_{m\times r}H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{r}))

is invariant under
S^{m}
. It behaves as

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}=\left\{(1,\;z,\;\cdots,\;z^{m-1})\left(\Theta_{m\times r}F\right)(z^{m}):F\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{r})\right\}.

Meanwhile, we have that

\displaystyle S^{km+\gamma}\mathcal{M}

(4.3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{(z^{km+\gamma},\;z^{km+\gamma+1},\;\cdots,\;z^{(k+1)m+\gamma-1})\left(\Theta_{m\times r}F\right)(z^{m}):\;F\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{r})\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{(1,\;z,\;\cdots,\;z^{m-1})\left(\Sigma_{m,\gamma}^{k}\Theta_{m\times r}F\right)(z^{m}):F\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{r})\right\}\quad\quad

with the inner
m\times m
matrix
\Sigma_{m,\gamma}^{k}
in (
1
).
Since

\Theta_{m\times r}^{*}\Sigma_{m,\gamma}^{k}\Theta_{m\times r}\in H^{\infty}_{r\times r}
, the multiplier characterization of
vector-valued Hardy spaces (
[
1
, Theorem 4.14]
) implies that

\Sigma_{m,\gamma}^{k}\Theta_{m\times r}H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{r})\subset\Theta_{m\times r}H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{r}).

Consequently,

S^{km+\gamma}\mathcal{M}\subset\mathcal{M}
, and hence
\mathcal{M}

is invariant under both
S^{m}
and
S^{km+\gamma}
.

Conversely, suppose that
\mathcal{M}
is both

S^{m}
and
S^{km+\gamma}
-invariant. By Proposition
2.2
, there
exists an inner matrix function
\Theta_{m\times r}
such that

\mathcal{M}=T_{m}(\Theta_{m\times r}H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{r})).

Using the calculations in (
4.3
), the

S^{km+\gamma}
-invariance of
\mathcal{M}
is equivalent to

\Sigma_{m,\gamma}^{k}\Theta_{m\times r}H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{r})\subset\Theta_{m\times r}H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{r}).

Therefore,

\Theta_{m\times r}^{*}\Sigma_{m,\gamma}^{k}\Theta_{m\times r}

defines a multiplier on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{r})
.
Applying again the multiplier characterization
(
[
1
, Theorem 4.14]
), we conclude that

\Theta_{m\times r}^{*}\Sigma_{m,\gamma}^{k}\Theta_{m\times r}\in H^{\infty}_{r\times r}
.
This completes the proof.
∎

Based on Theorem
4.1
,
\mathcal{M}^{\perp}
is invariant under
(S^{m})^{*}
and
(S^{km+\gamma})^{*}
, i.e.,

\mathcal{M}^{\perp}=T_{m}(K_{\Theta_{m\times r}})
under the conditions in Theorem
4.1
.

4.2.
Near invariance under both
(S^{m})^{*}
and
(S^{km+\gamma})^{*}

Let
\mathcal{M}
be nearly invariant under both
(S^{m})^{*}
and
(S^{km+\gamma})^{*}
, with
m\geq 3
,
k\geq 1
, and
\gamma\in\{1,\cdots,m-1\}
. Define

X_{m}=\mathcal{M}\ominus(\mathcal{M}\cap S^{m}H^{2}(\mathbb{D})),\qquad E_{m}(z)=(e_{0},\cdots,e_{m-1})^{\mathsf{t}}(z),

where
\{e_{j}\}_{j=0}^{m-1}
is obtained by orthonormalizing the reproducing kernel and its first
m-1
derivative kernels of
\mathcal{M}
at
0
. We also set

A(l)=(a_{0}^{l},\cdots,a_{m-1}^{l}),\qquad l\geq 0.

Firstly, considering the near
(S^{m})^{*}
-invariance, an arbitrary
f\in\mathcal{M}
has the form

f(z)=A(0)E_{m}(z)+S^{m}f_{1}(z).

Since
f_{1}\perp e_{i}
for
i=0,1,\cdots,m-1
, the near
(S^{m})^{*}
-invariance implies
f_{1}\in\mathcal{M}
. Meanwhile, it holds that

\|f\|^{2}=|A(0)|^{2}+\|f_{1}\|^{2}.
So we repeat this process for
f_{1}
, getting that

\displaystyle f_{1}(z)=A(1)E_{m}(z)+S^{m}f_{2}(z)

(4.4)

with
f_{2}\in\mathcal{M}
and

\|f_{1}\|^{2}=|A(1)|^{2}+\|f_{2}\|^{2}.
Continuing recursively, we get

f_{j}(z)=A(j)E_{m}(z)+S^{m}f_{j+1}(z)

with
f_{j+1}\in\mathcal{M}
and

\|f_{j}\|^{2}=|A(j)|^{2}+\|f_{j+1}\|^{2}.

Combining these equations together, it yields that

f(z)=\left(\sum_{l=0}^{j}(z^{m})^{l}A(l)\right)E_{m}(z)+(z^{m})^{(j+1)}f_{j+1}(z)

and

\|f\|^{2}=\sum_{l=0}^{j}|A(l)|^{2}+\|f_{j+1}\|^{2}.

Utilizing
[
4
, Lemma 3.3]
and following the similar proof process of (
3.3
), we deduce that

\displaystyle f(z)

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}(z^{m})^{l}A(l)\right)E_{m}(z)

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}a_{0}^{l}(z^{m})^{l},\;\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}a_{1}^{l}(z^{m})^{l},\;\cdots,\;\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}a_{m-1}^{l}(z^{m})^{l}\right)E_{m}(z),\;z\in\mathbb{D},

(4.5)

where the sums converge in
H^{2}
norm and
\|f\|^{2}=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}|A(l)|^{2}.

Observing from (
4.4
) and (
4.5
), it holds that

\displaystyle f_{1}(z)=\left(\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}(z^{m})^{l}A(l+1)\right)E_{m}(z)\in\mathcal{M}.

(4.6)

Hereafter, we denote

\displaystyle\Phi(z):=(\phi_{0},\;\phi_{1},\;\cdots,\;\phi_{m-1})(z)

(4.7)

with

\phi_{i}(z)=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}a_{i}^{l}z^{l}
for
0\leq i\leq m-1.
By (
4.5
), we alternatively express that
f\in\mathcal{M}
if and only if
f(z)=\Phi(z^{m})E_{m}(z)
with

\displaystyle\|f\|^{2}=\|\Phi\|^{2},

(4.8)

and
\Phi(z)
lies in an
S^{*}
-invariant subspace
K\subset H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{m})
. The closedness of
K
can be deduced from (
4.8
), and (
4.6
) entails
K
is
S^{*}
-invariant.

Conversely, if

\mathcal{M}=\left\{\Phi(z^{m})E_{m}(z):\;\Phi(z)\in K\right\}

is a closed subspace of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}),
where
K
is
S^{*}
-invariant in
H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{m})
, then
\mathcal{M}
is nearly
(S^{m})^{*}
-invariant in
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
.

Since
K
is
S^{*}
-invariant in
H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{m})
, Theorem
2.1
implies

K=K_{\Theta_{m\times r}}:=H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{m})\ominus\Theta_{m\times r}H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{r})

with
0\leq r\leq m
and the inner function
\Theta_{m\times r}
takes values in
\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{C}^{r},\mathbb{C}^{m})
.
So there exists a unitary map
J_{m}:\;\mathcal{M}\rightarrow K_{\Theta_{m\times r}}
defined by

J_{m}\left(G(z^{m})E_{m}(z)\right)=G(z),\;\mbox{for}\;\;G\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{m}).

In sum, we characterize the nearly
(S^{m})^{*}
-invariant subspaces in
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
as below.

Proposition 4.2
.

For
m\geq 3
, a subspace
\mathcal{M}
of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
is nearly
(S^{m})^{*}
-invariant if and only if there exists a uitary map

J_{m}:\;\mathcal{M}\rightarrow K_{\Theta_{m\times r}}\;\;\mbox{defined by}\;G(z^{m})E_{m}(z)\mapsto G(z),

where the inner function
\Theta_{m\times r}
takes values in
\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{C}^{r},\mathbb{C}^{m})
for
0\leq r\leq m
.

Next we present the nearly
(S^{m})^{*}
and
(S^{km+\gamma})^{*}
-invariant subspaces, which also include all nearly invariant subspaces for the non-cyclic shift semigroup
\{((S^{m})^{*})^{n}((S^{km+\gamma})^{*})^{l}:\;n,\;l\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\;k\geq 1\}
.

Theorem 4.3
.

For
m\geq 3
,
k\geq 1
and
\gamma\in\{1,\cdots,m-1\}
, a subspace
\mathcal{M}
of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
is nearly
(S^{m})^{*}
and
(S^{km+\gamma})^{*}
-invariant if and only if there exists a unitary map

\displaystyle J_{m}:\;\mathcal{M}\rightarrow K_{\Theta_{m\times r}}\;\;\mbox{defined by}\;G(z^{m})E_{m}(z)\mapsto G(z),

where the inner function
\Theta_{m\times r}
takes values in
\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{C}^{r},\mathbb{C}^{m})
and satisfies
\Theta_{m\times r}^{*}\Sigma_{m,\gamma}^{k}\Theta_{m\times r}\in H^{\infty}_{r\times r}
for
0\leq r\leq m
.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.2
, the unitary map

J_{m}:\mathcal{M}\to K_{\Theta_{m\times r}}
gives

f(z)=\Phi(z^{m})E_{m}(z)\in\mathcal{M}
with

\Phi=(\phi_{0},\cdots,\phi_{m-1})\in K_{\Theta_{m\times r}}
as in (
4.7
).

Assume that

\Theta_{m\times r}^{*}\Sigma_{m,\gamma}^{k}\Theta_{m\times r}\in H^{\infty}_{r\times r}
and that

f\in\mathcal{M}
satisfies

f^{(n)}(0)=0
for
0\leq n\leq km+\gamma-1
.
By the definition of
\Sigma_{m,\gamma}^{k}
and the relation
(
4.2
), the condition

(S^{km+\gamma})^{*}f\in\mathcal{M}
is equivalent, under
J_{m}
, to

\displaystyle(\Sigma_{m,\gamma}^{k})^{*}\Phi^{\mathsf{t}}\in K_{\Theta_{m\times r}}.

(4.9)

We verify this inclusion. For any

F\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{r})
, the assumption on

\Theta_{m\times r}
implies that

\Sigma_{m,\gamma}^{k}\Theta_{m\times r}F=\Theta_{m\times r}G
for some

G\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{r})
. Hence,

\displaystyle\left\langle(\Sigma_{m,\gamma}^{k})^{*}\Phi^{\mathsf{t}},\Theta_{m\times r}F\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\Phi^{\mathsf{t}},\Sigma_{m,\gamma}^{k}\Theta_{m\times r}F\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\Phi^{\mathsf{t}},\Theta_{m\times r}G\right\rangle=0,

because
\Phi\in K_{\Theta_{m\times r}}
. Therefore, (
4.9
) holds
and hence
\mathcal{M}
is nearly

(S^{km+\gamma})^{*}
-invariant.

Conversely, if
\mathcal{M}
is nearly

(S^{km+\gamma})^{*}
-invariant, then

K_{\Theta_{m\times r}}
is invariant under

(\Sigma_{m,\gamma}^{k})^{*}
. Equivalently,

\Sigma_{m,\gamma}^{k}\Theta_{m\times r}H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{r})\subset\Theta_{m\times r}H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{r})
.
By the multiplier characterization of vector-valued Hardy spaces
(
[
1
, Theorem 4.14]
), this is equivalent to

\Theta_{m\times r}^{*}\Sigma_{m,\gamma}^{k}\Theta_{m\times r}\in H^{\infty}_{r\times r}
.
This completes the proof.
∎

Let

Example 4.5
.

Let
\mathcal{M}=\operatorname{span}\{1,z^{3},z^{4},z^{5},\cdots\}=\mathbb{C}\oplus z^{3}H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
. Clearly,
\mathcal{M}
is neither

S
nor
S^{2}
-invariant. Using the decomposition

f(z)=f_{0}(z^{3})+zf_{1}(z^{3})+z^{2}f_{2}(z^{3})
, every
g\in\mathcal{M}
admits a
representation

\displaystyle g(z)

\displaystyle=g(0)+z^{3}(f_{0}(z^{3})+zf_{1}(z^{3})+z^{2}f_{2}(z^{3}))

\displaystyle=g(0)+(Sf_{0})(z^{3})+z(Sf_{1})(z^{3})+z^{2}(Sf_{2})(z^{3})

\displaystyle:=g_{0}(z^{3})+zg_{1}(z^{3})+z^{2}g_{2}(z^{3}),

Therefore,

\mathcal{M}=T_{3}(\Theta_{3\times 3}H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{3}))
, where

\Theta_{3\times 3}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\
0&z&0\\
0&0&z\end{pmatrix}.

\Theta_{3\times 3}^{*}\Sigma_{3,1}^{1}\Theta_{3\times 3}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&0&z^{3}\\
1&0&0\\
0&z^{2}&0\end{array}\right),\;\Theta_{3\times 3}^{*}\Sigma_{3,2}^{1}\Theta_{3\times 3}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&z^{3}&0\\
0&0&z^{2}\\
1&0&0\end{array}\right)

and both matrices belong to
H^{\infty}_{3\times 3}
. Hence, by
Theorem
4.1
,
\mathcal{M}
is invariant under

S^{3},S^{4}
and
S^{5}
. Then
\mathcal{M}^{\perp}=\operatorname{span}\{z,z^{2}\}=T_{3}(K_{\Theta_{3\times 3}})
is invariant under

(S^{3})^{*},(S^{4})^{*}
and
(S^{5})^{*}
.

5.
(Near) invariance for Toeplitz operators induced by Blaschke products

Let
B_{m}
be an
m
-degree Blaschke product,

B_{m}(z)=\lambda\prod_{j=1}^{m}\frac{z-z_{j}}{1-\overline{z_{j}}z},\qquad|\lambda|=1,\quad|z_{j}|<1.

The model space
K_{B_{m}}=H^{2}(\mathbb{D})\ominus B_{m}H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
has
dimension
m
. By the Wold decomposition,

H^{2}(\mathbb{D})=\bigoplus_{j\geq 0}B_{m}^{j}K_{B_{m}},\qquad H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{m})=\bigoplus_{j\geq 0}z^{j}K,

where
K=H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{m})\ominus zH^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{m})
.

Let
\{e_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}
and
\{\delta_{j}^{m}\}_{j=1}^{m}
be orthonormal
bases of
K_{B_{m}}
and
K
, respectively. The corresponding unitary
operator

U:H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{m})\rightarrow H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
is determined by

U(B_{m}^{j}e_{l})=z^{j}\delta_{l}^{m},\qquad j\geq 0,\ 1\leq l\leq m.

Together with the isometric isomorphism
T_{m}
in (
4.1
), we have
the intertwining relation

S^{m}(T_{m}U)=(T_{m}U)T_{B_{m}}.

Hence, for every
n\geq 1
,

(S^{m})^{n}=(T_{m}U)(T_{B_{m}})^{n}(U^{*}T_{m}^{-1}).

This unitary equivalence immediately gives the following reduction.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
m\geq 2
and
n\geq 1
, a subspace
\mathcal{M}
of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})

is
(S^{m})^{n}
-invariant (resp. nearly
(S^{m})^{*n}
-invariant)
if and only if
(U^{*}T_{m}^{-1})\mathcal{M}
is

(T_{B_{m}})^{n}
-invariant (resp. nearly

(T_{B_{m}})^{*n}
-invariant).

We next consider the simultaneous invariance under two powers of

T_{B_{m}}
. Let
n<l
and write

l=nk+\gamma
, where
k\geq 1
and

\gamma\in\{1,\cdots,n-1\}
. By Proposition
5.1
, this problem is
equivalent to the simultaneous invariance under

S^{mn}
and
S^{mnk+m\gamma}
. Therefore Theorem
4.1
with
m
replaced by
mn
and
\gamma
replaced by

m\gamma
yield the following result.

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
m\geq 1
,
n\geq 2
,
k\geq 1
, and

\gamma\in\{1,\cdots,n-1\}
, a subspace
\mathcal{M}
of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
is invariant under
both
(T_{B_{m}})^{n}
and
(T_{B_{m}})^{kn+\gamma}
if and only if

\mathcal{M}=(U^{*}T_{m}^{-1})\big[T_{mn}(\Theta_{mn\times r}H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{r}))\big],

where
0\leq r\leq mn
, the inner function
\Theta_{mn\times r}
takes values in
\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{C}^{r},\mathbb{C}^{mn})
and satisfies

\Theta_{mn\times r}^{*}\Sigma_{mn,m\gamma}^{k}\Theta_{mn\times r}\in H^{\infty}_{r\times r}.

Moreover,

\mathcal{M}^{\perp}=(U^{*}T_{m}^{-1})[T_{mn}(K_{\Theta_{mn\times r}})]
is invariant under both
(T_{B_{m}}^{*})^{n}
and

(T_{B_{m}}^{*})^{kn+\gamma}
.

Similarly, Proposition
5.1
together with Theorem
4.3

give the corresponding characterization of nearly invariant subspaces.

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
m\geq 1
,
n\geq 2
,
k\geq 1
, and

\gamma\in\{1,\cdots,n-1\}
, a subspace
\mathcal{M}
of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
is nearly invariant under
both
(T_{B_{m}}^{*})^{n}
and
(T_{B_{m}}^{*})^{kn+\gamma}
if and only if there exists a unitary map

J_{mn}:\;(T_{m}U)\mathcal{M}\longrightarrow K_{\Theta_{mn\times r}},\qquad G(z^{mn})E_{mn}(z)\mapsto G(z),

where
0\leq r\leq mn
,
\Theta_{mn\times r}
is inner and satisfies
\Theta_{mn\times r}^{*}\Sigma_{mn,m\gamma}^{k}\Theta_{mn\times r}\in H^{\infty}_{r\times r}.
Here
E_{mn}
is obtained by orthonormalizing the reproducing kernel and
the first
mn-1
derivative kernels at the origin.

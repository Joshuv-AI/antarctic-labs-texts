Excess of Fusion Frames: A Comprehensive Approach
Ehsan Ameli, Ali Akbar Arefijamaal, Fahimeh Arabyani Neyshaburi

Abstract
Computing the excess as a method of measuring the redundancy of frames was recently introduced to address certain issues in frame theory. In this paper, the concept of excess for fusion frames is studied. Then, several explicit methods are provided to compute the excess of fusion frames and their Q Q -duals. In particular, some upper bounds for the excess of Q Q -dual fusion frames are established. It turns out that, unlike ordinary frames, for every n ∈ ℕ n\in\mathbb{N} we can provide a fusion frame with its Q Q -dual whose the difference of their excess is n n . Furthermore, the connection between the excess of fusion frames and their orthogonal complement is completely characterized. Finally, several examples are exhibited to confirm the obtained results.

Introduction

The excess carries out a crucial role in frame theory and signal processing, as it allows for a more robust representation of signals in the presence of noise and common types of interference. The excess is a rather crude way of measuring the redundancy of frames. In fact, it is defined as the greatest number of elements that can be removed from a frame in a Hilbert space and still leave a set with the same closed linear span. Recently, accurate methods have been provided for computing the excess of frames and g-frames
[
5
,
14
,
16
]
.

Fusion frame theory is a generalization of ordinary frames in separable Hilbert spaces, introduced by Casazza and Kutyniok in
[
9
]
. Fusion frames play an important role in many applications in mathematical analysis and engineering, including coding theory, filter bank theory, signal and image processing and wireless communications and many other fields
[
7
,
9
,
15
,
16
,
18
]
. In this regard, understanding the redundancy of fusion frames is a fundamental issue that has an impact on many applications. Many concepts of frame theory have been generalized to the fusion frame setting
[
1
,
3
,
16
,
18
,
19
]
. In most of those results, the calculation of the excess and the construction of Riesz bases are of key importance. In this respect, having a notion of the excess of fusion frames allows us to generate fusion Riesz bases. Motivated by this point of view and the recent work of Balan et al.
[
5
]
in the direction of excess of frames in Hilbert spaces, we propose an approach to determine the excess of fusion frames. We build a connection between the excess of fusion frames and their local frames, and subsequently investigate the differences between these two. This approach would be interesting to find out whether the excess of fusion frames can be defined as the removal of redundant subspaces. In
[
18
]
, some approaches are presented to determine the redundancy of fusion frames. Moreover, the redundancy function and the concept of upper and lower redundancies are introduced in
[
6
]
as a quantitative measure for computing redundancy in ordinary frames, which is not aligned with our intuitive understanding of the excess in Hilbert spaces, as we will discuss in this paper. Hence, this survey focuses exclusively on the study of the excess of fusion frames.

Our next objective is to provide an explicit method for computing the excess of fusion frames. Furthermore, we try to obtain the excess of
Q
-dual fusion frames which are introduced in
[
13
]
. One advantage of these dual fusion frames with respect to alternate (Gãvruţa) dual fusion frames
[
12
]
is that they can be readily obtained from the left inverses of the analysis operator of fusion frames. So, the following questions naturally arise: What is the relationship between the excess of a fusion frame and its
Q
-dual fusion frame? For a given fusion frame, is there a
Q
-dual fusion frame with the same excess? How can we establish the connection between the excess of a fusion frame and its orthogonal complement fusion frame? In this study, we provide several responses to all of these questions.

The remainder of the paper is structured as follows. In Section 2, we briefly review frames and fusion frames in Hilbert spaces. In Section 3, we take an approach to introduce the excess of fusion frames and thereby we make a connection with the excess of local frames. We then present an explicit method to compute the excess of fusion frames. Furthermore, we study the relationship between the excess of fusion frames and their orthogonal complement fusion frames and we compute the excess of a class of fusion frames. Finally, Section 4 is devoted to study the excess of
Q
-dual fusion frames and to present some methods for computing their excess. Moreover, we give several examples of
Q
-dual fusion frames and we compute their excess by applying the obtained results. Specifically, we show that, unlike ordinary frames, there are
Q
-dual fusion frames in which their excess are much more than the excess of own fusion frame.

We review the basic definitions and primary results of frames and fusion frames. Throughout this paper, we suppose that
\mathcal{H}

Recall that a

Those sequences which satisfy only the upper inequality are called

T(\{c_{i}\}_{i\in I})=\sum_{i\in I}c_{i}f_{i}
.
The adjoint operator
T^{*}:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\ell^{2}(I)

is given by
T^{*}f=\{\langle f,f_{i}\rangle\}_{i\in I}
. Moreover, the

of

Sf=TT^{*}f=\sum_{i\in I}\langle f,f_{i}\rangle f_{i}

[
10

f=S^{-1}Sf=\sum_{i\in I}\left\langle f,S^{-1}f_{i}\right\rangle f_{i},\quad(f\in\mathcal{H}).

A Bessel sequence

[
2

where
\sum_{i\in I}\langle f,f_{i}\rangle u_{i}=0

.

Frames which are not bases are overcomplete, i.e. there exist proper subsets of the frame which are frame as well. The

of the frame is the greatest integer n such that n elements can be removed from the frame and still have a complete set, or

[

Proposition 1
.

[

Let

\mathcal{\widetilde{F}}=\{\tilde{f_{i}}\}_{i\in I}

3
Excess of Fusion Frames

The excess is a way of measuring the redundancy of fusion frames, see also
[
15
]
. First, we recall the concept of excess for fusion frames.

Definition 1
.

[
15
]

Let
W=\{(W_{i},\omega_{i})\}_{i\in I}
be a fusion frame for
\mathcal{H}
with the synthesis operator
T_{W}
. The excess of
W
is defined as

e(W)=\textnormal{dim}N(T_{W}).

Suppose that
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
is an orthonormal basis for
\mathcal{H}_{n}
,
W_{i}=\textnormal{span}\{e_{i}\}
and
\omega_{i}=1
,
i=1,\ldots,n
. Take

W=\left\{W_{1},W_{1},W_{2},W_{2},\ldots,W_{n},W_{n}\right\}.

Obviously
\textnormal{dim}N(T_{W})=n
, while
W
is a 2-tight fusion frame with the uniform redundancy 2, see
[
18
]
for more details. However, our approach provides a precise description of which part of each subspace can be considered as redundancy. The present paper, is concerned exclusively with the excess of fusion frames.

3.1
Computational view of point

As we observed, in ordinary frames the excess of a frame is defined as the greatest number of elements that can be removed and yet leave a set with the same closed linear span. Since the subspaces of a fusion frame are not disjoint, in general, we cannot provide an analogous approach to the excess of fusion frames. However, we can connect it to the excess of local frames. In the sequel, we compute the excess of fusion frames using their local frames. We do this first through local frames obtained from Riesz bases.

Proposition 4
.

Let
\mathcal{W}=\{(W_{i},\omega_{i})\}_{i\in I}
be a fusion frame for
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{F}=\{\omega_{i}f_{i,j}\}_{i\in I,j\in J_{i}}
its local, where
\{f_{i,j}\}_{j\in J_{i}}
is a Riesz basis for
W_{i},
for all
i\in I
. Then

e(\mathcal{W})=e(\mathcal{F}).

Proof.

Suppose that
\{g_{i}\}_{i\in I}\in\bigoplus_{i\in I}W_{i}
, then
g_{i}=\sum_{j\in J_{i}}c_{i,j}f_{i,j}
(
i\in I
), where
\{c_{i,j}\}_{j\in J_{i}}\in l^{2}(J_{i})
. If
T_{W}\big(\{g_{i}\}_{i\in I}\big)=0
, then
\sum_{i\in I,j\in J_{i}}c_{i,j}\omega_{i}f_{i,j}=\sum_{i\in I}\omega_{i}g_{i}=0
, which implies that
T_{\mathcal{F}}\big(\{c_{i,j}\}_{i\in I,j\in J_{i}}\big)=0
. Hence, there exists a bijective correspondence between
N(T_{W})
and
N(T_{\mathcal{F}})
. Therefore,
e(W)=e(\mathcal{F})
.
∎

This statement leads directly to the conclusion that the excess of
W
is not affected by the weights. Indeed, if
\mathcal{F}
is the local frame introduced in the above proposition, then it is sufficient to show that
e(\mathcal{F})=e(\mathcal{F}^{1}),
where
\mathcal{F}^{1}=\{f_{i,j}\}_{i\in I,j\in J_{i}}
is a local frame of
W^{1}=\{(W_{i},1)\}_{i\in I}.
To this end, suppose that
e(\mathcal{F}^{1})<\infty
. Then there exist
L\subseteq I
and
K_{i}\subseteq J_{i},~(i\in L)
such that
\{f_{i,j}\}_{i\in L,j\in K_{i}}
is a Riesz basis and

\sum_{i\in L}|J_{i}\smallsetminus K_{i}|+\sum_{i\in I\smallsetminus L}|J_{i}|=e(\mathcal{F}^{1})<\infty.

Obviously,
\{\omega_{i}f_{i,j}\}_{i\in L,j\in K_{i}}
is also a Riesz basis. In particular,
e(\mathcal{F})=e(\mathcal{F}^{1}).
Now, if
e(\mathcal{F}^{1})=\infty,
we claim that
e(\mathcal{F})=\infty.
Otherwise, assume that
e(\mathcal{F})<\infty.
Repeating the above argument, it follows that
e(\mathcal{F}^{1})<\infty,
which is a contradiction. Hence,
W
and
W^{1}
have the same excess, see also
(
15
, Theorem 6.8)
for another proof. In spite of this fact, we take weights into account in our examples. The following example confirms Proposition
4
.

Example 1
.

Consider

W_{2}=\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}

Theorem 5
.

Let
W=\{(W_{i},\omega_{i})\}_{i\in I}
be a fusion frame for
\mathcal{H}

e(W)=\sum_{i\in I,j\in J_{i}}\left(1-\omega_{i}^{2}\left\langle e_{i,j},S_{W}^{-1}e_{i,j}\right\rangle\right).

Specially, if

Let
\{e_{i,j}\}_{j\in J_{i}}
be an orthonormal basis for
W_{i}~(i\in I)
and denote elements
E_{i,j}
of
\bigoplus_{i\in I}W_{i}

(E_{i,j})_{k}=\begin{cases}e_{i,j},~&i=k\\
0,~&i\neq k.\end{cases}

(3)

It is known that

is an orthonormal basis for
\bigoplus_{i\in I}W_{i}

P

So, we obtain

\displaystyle e(W)=\textnormal{dim}N(T_{W})

\displaystyle=\textnormal{trace}(P)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I,j\in J_{i}}\big\langle E_{i,j},PE_{i,j}\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I,j\in J_{i}}\left(1-\left\langle T_{W}E_{i,j},S_{W}^{-1}T_{W}E_{i,j}\right\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I,j\in J_{i}}\left(1-\omega_{i}^{2}\left\langle e_{i,j},S_{W}^{-1}e_{i,j}\right\rangle\right).

In particular, if

\displaystyle e(W)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I,j\in J_{i}}\left(1-\omega_{i}^{2}\left\langle e_{i,j},S_{W}^{-1}e_{i,j}\right\rangle\right)

\textnormal{span}\{e_{i+1}\},~&i>n.\end{cases}

Then
W=\{(W_{i},\omega)\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
is a fusion frame for
\mathcal{H}
and

S_{W}^{-1}f=\omega^{-2}\left(f-\sum_{i=2}^{n}\langle f,e_{i}\rangle\frac{e_{i}}{2}\right),\quad(f\in\mathcal{H}).

Moreover,

\sum_{i=n+1}^{\infty}\left(\textnormal{dim}W_{i}-\omega^{2}\textnormal{trace}\left(\pi_{W_{i}}S_{W}^{-1}\pi_{W_{i}}\right)\right)=\sum_{i=n+1}^{\infty}\left(1-\omega^{2}\omega^{-2}\right)=0.

Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle e(W)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(\textnormal{dim}W_{i}-\omega_{i}^{2}\textnormal{trace}\left(\pi_{W_{i}}S_{W}^{-1}\pi_{W_{i}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\textnormal{dim}W_{i}-\omega_{i}^{2}\textnormal{trace}\left(\pi_{W_{i}}S_{W}^{-1}\pi_{W_{i}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=2\left[2-\omega^{2}\left(\omega^{-2}+\frac{\omega^{-2}}{2}\right)\right]+\sum_{i=2}^{n-1}\left(2-\omega^{2}\omega^{-2}\right)=n-1.

(4)

Let
\{e_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
be an orthonormal basis for
\mathcal{H}
,
W_{1}=\overline{\textnormal{span}}_{i\geq 0}\{e_{i}\}
and
W_{2}=\overline{\textnormal{span}}_{i\leq 0}\{e_{i}\}
. In
[
9
]
, it has been shown that
W=\{(W_{i},\omega)\}_{i=1}^{2}
is an exact fusion frame which is not Riesz basis. A straightforward calculation shows that
S_{W}^{-1}f=\omega^{-2}\left(f-\langle f,e_{0}\rangle\frac{e_{0}}{2}\right)
, for all
f\in\mathcal{H}
. Therefore, it follows from Theorem
5
that

\displaystyle e(W)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(1-\omega^{2}\left\langle e_{i},S_{W}^{-1}e_{i}\right\rangle\right)+\left(1-\omega^{2}\left\langle e_{0},S_{W}^{-1}e_{0}\right\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(1-\left\langle e_{i},\left(e_{i}-\langle e_{i},e_{0}\rangle\frac{e_{0}}{2}\right)\right\rangle\right)+\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{|\langle e_{i},e_{0}\rangle|^{2}}{2}+\frac{1}{2}=1.

Suppose that
W=\{(W_{i},\omega_{i})\}_{i\in I}
is a fusion frame for
\mathcal{H}
and
\{e_{j}\}_{j\in J}
is an orthonormal basis for
\mathcal{H}
. Then
\mathcal{F}_{W}=\{\omega_{i}\pi_{W_{i}}e_{j}\}_{i\in I,j\in J}
is a local frame of
W

[
3
]
. In the next theorem, we investigate the relationship between the excess of
W
and
\mathcal{F}_{W}
.

Theorem 6
.

Let
W=\{(W_{i},\omega_{i})\}_{i\in I}
be a fusion frame for
\mathcal{H}
such that
\textnormal{dim}W_{i}<\infty
for all
i\in I
. Let
\mathcal{F}_{W}=\{\omega_{i}\pi_{W_{i}}e_{j}\}_{i\in I,j\in J}
. Then

e(\mathcal{F}_{W})+\sum_{i\in I}\textnormal{dim}W_{i}=|I|\textnormal{dim}\mathcal{H}+e(W).

In particular, if
\textnormal{dim}\mathcal{H}<\infty,
then
e(\mathcal{F}_{W})=(|I|-1)\textnormal{dim}\mathcal{H}
.

Proof.

It is easily observed that the frame operators
S_{W}
and
S_{\mathcal{F}_{W}}
are the same. Indeed, for each
f\in\mathcal{H}
we have

\displaystyle S_{\mathcal{F}_{W}}f

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I}\sum_{j\in I}\langle f,\omega_{i}\pi_{W_{i}}e_{j}\rangle\omega_{i}\pi_{W_{i}}e_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I}\sum_{j\in I}\omega_{i}^{2}\pi_{W_{i}}\langle\pi_{W_{i}}f,e_{j}\rangle e_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I}\omega_{i}^{2}\pi_{W_{i}}f=S_{W}f.

Applying Proposition
1
yields

\displaystyle e(\mathcal{F}_{W})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I}\sum_{j\in I}\left(1-\left\langle\omega_{i}\pi_{W_{i}}e_{j},\omega_{i}S_{W}^{-1}\pi_{W_{i}}e_{j}\right\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I}\bigg(\textnormal{dim}\mathcal{H}-\omega_{i}^{2}\sum_{j\in I}\left\langle e_{j},\pi_{W_{i}}S_{W}^{-1}\pi_{W_{i}}e_{j}\right\rangle\bigg)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I}\left(\textnormal{dim}\mathcal{H}-\omega_{i}^{2}\textnormal{trace}\left(\pi_{W_{i}}S_{W}^{-1}\pi_{W_{i}}\right)\right).

Therefore, by using Theorem
5
we obtain

\displaystyle e(\mathcal{F}_{W})+\sum_{i\in I}\textnormal{dim}W_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I}\left(\textnormal{dim}\mathcal{H}-\omega_{i}^{2}\textnormal{trace}\left(\pi_{W_{i}}S_{W}^{-1}\pi_{W_{i}}\right)+\textnormal{dim}W_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=|I|\textnormal{dim}\mathcal{H}+\sum_{i\in I}\left(\textnormal{dim}W_{i}-\omega_{i}^{2}\textnormal{trace}\left(\pi_{W_{i}}S_{W}^{-1}\pi_{W_{i}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=|I|\textnormal{dim}\mathcal{H}+e(W).

Moreover, if
\textnormal{dim}\mathcal{H}<\infty,
then it gives

e(\mathcal{F}_{W})=|I|\textnormal{dim}\mathcal{H}-\sum_{i\in I}\textnormal{dim}W_{i}+e(W)=(|I|-1)\textnormal{dim}\mathcal{H}.

∎

Consider the fusion frame
W=\{(W_{i},\omega_{i})\}_{i=1}^{2}
introduced in Example
2
(1) with the local frame
\mathcal{F}_{W}=\{\omega_{i}\pi_{W_{i}}e_{j}\}_{i=1~j=1}^{2\hskip 8.19447ptn+1}
, where
\{e_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n+1}
is an orthonormal basis for
\mathcal{H}=\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. An easy computation shows that

S_{W}=\textnormal{diag}\bigg(\omega_{1}^{2},\underbrace{\left(\omega_{1}^{2}+\omega_{2}^{2}\right),\ldots,\left(\omega_{1}^{2}+\omega_{2}^{2}\right)}_{n-1},\omega_{2}^{2}\bigg).

Although Theorem
6
assures that
e(\mathcal{F}_{W})=n+1,
we compute the excess of
\mathcal{F}_{W}
directly by means of Proposition
1
.

\displaystyle e(\mathcal{F}_{W})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}\left(1-\omega_{i}^{2}\left\langle\pi_{W_{i}}e_{j},S_{W}^{-1}\pi_{W_{i}}e_{j}\right\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=2(n+1)-\bigg[\omega_{1}^{2}\left\langle e_{1},S_{W}^{-1}e_{1}\right\rangle+\left(\omega_{1}^{2}+\omega_{2}^{2}\right)\left\langle e_{2},S_{W}^{-1}e_{2}\right\rangle+\cdots

\displaystyle+\left(\omega_{1}^{2}+\omega_{2}^{2}\right)\left\langle e_{n},S_{W}^{-1}e_{n}\right\rangle+\omega_{2}^{2}\left\langle e_{n+1},S_{W}^{-1}e_{n+1}\right\rangle\bigg]

\displaystyle=2(n+1)-\bigg[\omega_{1}^{2}\omega_{1}^{-2}+\left(\omega_{1}^{2}+\omega_{2}^{2}\right)\left(\omega_{1}^{2}+\omega_{2}^{2}\right)^{-1}+\cdots

\displaystyle+\left(\omega_{1}^{2}+\omega_{2}^{2}\right)\left(\omega_{1}^{2}+\omega_{2}^{2}\right)^{-1}+\omega_{2}^{2}\omega_{2}^{-2}\bigg]=2(n+1)-(n+1)=n+1.

Hence, applying Theorem
6
we get

\displaystyle e(W)

\displaystyle=e(\mathcal{F}_{W})+\sum_{i=1}^{2}\textnormal{dim}W_{i}-2\textnormal{dim}\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle=3n+1-2(n+1)=n-1.

3.2
Orthogonal complement fusion frames

Through the orthogonal complement, under certain conditions, a new fusion frame is obtained from a given fusion frame; so called the orthogonal complement fusion frame
[
7
]
. Here, we discuss the excess of these fusion frames and survey the relationship between the excess of a fusion frame and its orthogonal complement fusion frame.

Definition 2
.

[
7
]

Let
W=\{(W_{i},\omega_{i})\}_{i\in I}
be a fusion frame for
\mathcal{H}
. If the family
W^{\perp}:=\{(W_{i}^{\perp},\omega_{i})\}_{i\in I}
, where
W_{i}^{\perp}
is the orthogonal complement of
W_{i}
is also a fusion frame, then we call
W^{\perp}
the orthogonal complement fusion frame obtained from
W
.

In
[
7
]
, it has been shown that if
W=\{(W_{i},\omega_{i})\}_{i\in I}
is a fusion frame for
\mathcal{H}
such that
\sum_{i\in I}\omega_{i}^{2}<\infty,
then
W^{\perp}
is a fusion frame for
\mathcal{H}
if and only if
\bigcap_{i\in I}W_{i}=\{0\}
. In what follows, without losing the generality, we assume that
\sum_{i\in I}\omega_{i}^{2}<\infty.
Note that in the case
|I|=1
,
W^{\perp}=\{0\}
is not a fusion frame. Furthermore, provided that
W
is a fusion Riesz basis, it is easily seen that
W^{\perp}
is a fusion frame. The following lemma describes the excess of the orthogonal complement fusion frame associated with a fusion Riesz basis.

Lemma 7
.

Let
W=\{(W_{i},\omega_{i})\}_{i\in I}
be a fusion Riesz basis for an infinite dimensional Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. Then the following conditions are hold.

(1)

If
|I|=2
, then
e(W^{\perp})=0
.

(2)

If
|I|>2
, then
e(W^{\perp})=\infty
.

Proof.

Assume that
|I|=2
. A simple computation confirms that
\bigcap_{i\in I}W_{i}^{\perp}=\{0\}
. Hence,
W
and
W^{\perp}
are fusion Riesz bases which assures that
e(W^{\perp})=0
. Now,
suppose that
|I|>2
and
\{e_{i,j}\}_{j\in J_{i}}
is an orthonormal basis of
W_{i}
for all
i\in I
. According to Proposition
3
, for all
k\in I
, we have

\displaystyle W_{k}^{\perp}

\displaystyle=\overline{\textnormal{span}}_{i\neq k}\left\{S_{W}^{-1}W_{i}\right\}

(4)

\displaystyle=\overline{\textnormal{span}}_{i\neq k,j\in J_{i}}\left\{S_{W}^{-1}e_{i,j}\right\}.

Note that each subspace
S_{W}^{-1}W_{i}
occurs
|I|-1
times in
W^{\perp}.
Since
\left\{S_{W}^{-1}e_{i,j}\right\}_{j\in J_{i}}
is a local Riesz basis for
S_{W}^{-1}W_{i}
, thus
e(W^{\perp})=\sum_{i\in I}(|I|-2)|J_{i}|
by Proposition
4
. The assumption
\textnormal{dim}\mathcal{H}=\infty
ensures that
|I|=\infty
or
|J_{i}|=\infty
, for some
i\in I
. In particular,
e(W^{\perp})=\infty
.
∎

The following lemma indicates that if
W=\{(W_{i},\omega_{i})\}_{i\in I}
is a fusion Riesz basis with
|I|\geq 2,
then having at least two subspaces of
W^{\perp}
is sufficient to constitute a fusion frame.

Lemma 8
.

Let
W=\{(W_{i},\omega_{i})\}_{i\in I}
be a fusion Riesz basis for
\mathcal{H}
with
|I|\geq 2.
Then
\mathcal{W}^{\perp}:=\{(W_{i}^{\perp},\omega_{i})\}_{i=\ell,k}
is a fusion frame for every distinct index
\ell,k\in I.

Proof.

Assume that
W
is a fusion Riesz basis, then
W^{\perp}
is a fusion frame for
\mathcal{H}
. In order to prove that
\mathcal{W}^{\perp}
is a fusion frame, it is sufficient to prove that
S_{W}^{1/2}\mathcal{W}^{\perp}
is so, by
(
12
, Theorem 2.4)
. Due to the fact that
S_{W}^{1/2}\mathcal{W}^{\perp}
is a fusion Bessel sequence, we just need to investigate the lower bound. To this end, we first observe that

S_{W}^{1/2}W_{i}^{\perp}=\overline{\textnormal{span}}_{j\in I,j\neq i}\left\{S_{W}^{-1/2}W_{j}\right\},\quad(i=\ell,k)

(5)

by applying (
4
). Since
\left\{\big(S_{W}^{-1/2}W_{j},1\big)\right\}_{j\in I}
is a fusion orthonormal basis
[
9
]
for
\mathcal{H}
, by employing (
5
) we get
\pi_{S_{W}^{1/2}W_{i}^{\perp}}=\sum_{j\in I,j\neq i}\pi_{S_{W}^{-1/2}W_{j}},
for
i=\ell,k
. Thus, by taking
\gamma:=\textnormal{min}\left\{\omega_{\ell}^{2},\omega_{k}^{2}\right\},
we induce

\displaystyle\gamma\|f\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\gamma\sum_{j\in I}\|\pi_{S_{W}^{-1/2}W_{j}}f\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\gamma\left(\|\pi_{S_{W}^{-1/2}W_{\ell}}f\|^{2}+\|\pi_{S_{W}^{-1/2}W_{k}}f\|^{2}+\sum_{j\in I,j\neq\ell,k}\|\pi_{S_{W}^{-1/2}W_{j}}f\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\omega_{k}^{2}\|\pi_{S_{W}^{-1/2}W_{\ell}}f\|^{2}+\omega_{\ell}^{2}\|\pi_{S_{W}^{-1/2}W_{k}}f\|^{2}+(\omega_{k}^{2}+\omega_{\ell}^{2})\sum_{j\in I,j\neq\ell,k}\|\pi_{S_{W}^{-1/2}W_{j}}f\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in I,j\neq k}\omega_{k}^{2}\|\pi_{S_{W}^{-1/2}W_{j}}f\|^{2}+\sum_{j\in I,j\neq\ell}\omega_{\ell}^{2}\|\pi_{S_{W}^{-1/2}W_{j}}f\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=\ell,k}\omega_{i}^{2}\|\pi_{S_{W}^{1/2}W_{i}^{\perp}}f\|^{2}.

Therefore,
S_{W}^{1/2}\mathcal{W}^{\perp}
is a fusion frame, as required.
∎

It should be mentioned that the converse of Lemma
8
is not generally true. For instance, one may consider the fusion frame
W=\{(W_{i},\omega_{i})\}_{i=1}^{3}
of
\mathcal{H}_{3}
where
W_{1}=\textnormal{span}\{e_{1}\},~W_{2}=\textnormal{span}\{e_{1}+e_{2}\}
and
W_{3}=\textnormal{span}\{e_{2},e_{3}\}.

The general connection between the excess of fusion frames
W
and
W^{\perp}
is given in the next theorem.

Theorem 9
.

Let
W=\{(W_{i},\omega_{i})\}_{i\in I}
be a fusion frame for
\mathcal{H}
such that
\bigcap_{i\in I}W_{i}=\{0\}
. Then

\displaystyle e(W^{\perp})+e(W)=(|I|-2)\textnormal{dim}\mathcal{H}.

(6)

Proof.

We split the proof into the following two cases.

\mathbf{Case~1.}
Assume
\textnormal{dim}\mathcal{H}=n<\infty
. Applying (
2
) yields

\displaystyle e(W^{\perp})+e(W)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I}\textnormal{dim}W^{\perp}_{i}+\sum_{i\in I}\textnormal{dim}W_{i}-2n

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I}\big(n-\textnormal{dim}W_{i}\big)+\sum_{i\in I}\textnormal{dim}W_{i}-2n

\displaystyle=n(|I|-2),

which is the desired result.

\mathbf{Case~2.}
Suppose
\textnormal{dim}\mathcal{H}=\infty
. If
W
is a fusion Riesz basis, then (
6
) obviously holds by Lemma
7
. Let
W
be a fusion frame and
\{e_{i,j}\}_{j\in J_{i}}
be an orthonormal basis of
W_{i}
for each
i\in I
. In the case
e(W)=\infty
, the equation (
6
) obviously holds. So, let
e(W)=m<\infty
and
|I|>2
. Then, without losing the generality, we suppose that
\{e_{i_{0},j}\}_{j\in L}
is a family of redundant vectors in
W_{i_{0}}
, where
L\subset J_{i_{0}}
and
|L|=m
. Take

Z_{i}:=\begin{cases}W_{i},~&i\neq i_{0}\\
\overline{\textnormal{span}}\{e_{i_{0},j}\}_{j\in J_{i_{0}}\smallsetminus L},~&i=i_{0}.\end{cases}

It is obviously seen that
Z=\{(Z_{i},\omega_{i})\}_{i\in I}
is a fusion Riesz basis for
\mathcal{H}
and
Z^{\perp}=\big\{(Z_{i}^{\perp},\omega_{i})\big\}_{i\in I}
is a fusion frame with
e(Z^{\perp})=\infty,
by Lemma
7
. Moreover, it follows from Lemma
8
that
Z_{0}^{\perp}:=\big\{(Z_{i}^{\perp},\omega_{i})\big\}_{i\in I,i\neq i_{0}}
is also a fusion frame for
\mathcal{H}
. According to Proposition
3
,
Z_{k}^{\perp}
may be written as
\overline{\textnormal{span}}_{i\neq k}\left\{S_{Z}^{-1}Z_{i}\right\},~(k\in I)
. In addition,

\displaystyle W_{i_{0}}^{\perp}

\displaystyle=\left(Z_{i_{0}}+\textnormal{span}\{e_{i_{0},j}\}_{j\in L}\right)^{\perp}

\displaystyle=Z_{i_{0}}^{\perp}\cap\left(\textnormal{span}\{e_{i_{0},j}\}_{j\in L}\right)^{\perp}.

Hence, we get

W_{i}^{\perp}=\begin{cases}\overline{\textnormal{span}}_{j\neq i}\left\{S_{Z}^{-1}Z_{j}\right\},~&i\neq i_{0}\\
\overline{\textnormal{span}}_{j\neq i}\left\{S_{Z}^{-1}Z_{j}\right\}\cap\left(\textnormal{span}\{e_{i_{0},j}\}_{j\in L}\right)^{\perp},~&i=i_{0}.\end{cases}

Furthermore, it is not difficult to see that

e(Z_{0}^{\perp})\leq e(W^{\perp})\leq e(Z^{\perp}).

(7)

As such, it suffices to prove that
e(Z_{0}^{\perp})=\infty
. An easy computation shows that every subspace
S_{Z}^{-1}Z_{i}~(i\neq i_{0})
occurs
|I|-2
times and
S_{Z}^{-1}Z_{i_{0}}
occurs
|I|-1
times in
Z_{0}^{\perp}
. Applying the same argument as in Lemma
7
, we have

e(Z_{0}^{\perp})=(|I|-2)|J_{i_{0}}|+\sum_{i\in I,i\neq i_{0}}(|I|-3)|J_{i}|.

Due to
e(Z^{\perp})=\sum_{i\in I}(|I|-2)|J_{i}|=\infty
, we infer that
|I|=\infty
or
|J_{i}|=\infty
, for some
i\in I
. Especially,
e(Z_{0}^{\perp})=\infty
. Therefore, it follows from (
7
) that
e(W^{\perp})=\infty
.
This completes the proof.
∎

The importance of Theorem
9
appears between the excess of
W
and
W^{\perp},
one of which is easily computed. We now examine the validity of the above statement through two examples.

Example 3
.

Let
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
be an orthonormal basis for
\mathcal{H}_{n}
and
\{\omega_{i}\}_{i\in I}
a family of weights.

(1)

Take
W_{2i-1}=W_{2i}=\textnormal{span}\{e_{i}\},
for
i=1,\ldots,n
. Then
W=\left\{W_{2i-1},W_{2i}\right\}_{i=1}^{n}
is a fusion frame for
\mathcal{H}_{n}
via
2n
subspaces with respect to
\{\omega_{i}\}_{i\in I}
. Moreover,
W^{\perp}=\left\{W_{2i-1}^{\perp},W_{2i}^{\perp}\right\}
is also a fusion frame for
\mathcal{H}_{n}
with respect to
\{\omega_{i}\}_{i\in I}
. In view of (
2
), we get
e(W)=n
and
e(W^{\perp})=n(2n-3)
. Hence,

\displaystyle e(W^{\perp})+e(W)

\displaystyle=n(2n-3)+n

\displaystyle=n(2n-2).

(2)

Consider
W_{i}=\textnormal{span}\left\{e_{i},e_{i+1}\right\}
for each
i\in I
, where
|I|=n-1
. Then
W=\{(W_{i},\omega_{i})\}_{i\in I}
is a fusion frame for
\mathcal{H}_{n}
and
e(W)=n-2,
by (
2
). In addition,
W^{\perp}=\left\{(W_{i}^{\perp},\omega_{i})\right\}_{i\in I}
is a fusion frame for
\mathcal{H}_{n}
, where
W_{i}^{\perp}=\textnormal{span}\{e_{j}\}_{j\neq i,i+1}
for each

. So, it gives

\displaystyle e(W^{\perp})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I}\textnormal{dim}W^{\perp}_{i}-\textnormal{dim}\mathcal{H}_{n}

\displaystyle=(n-1)(n-2)-n=n^{2}-4n+2.

Therefore,

\displaystyle e(W^{\perp})+e(W)

\displaystyle=n^{2}-4n+2+n-2

\displaystyle=n\big((n-1)-2\big)=n(|I|-2).

4
Excess of Dual Fusion Frames

One of the most important properties of computing the excess in the fusion frame setting is to facilitate the construction of fusion Riesz bases, which is
provided
by the excess of their local frames. Taking this into account, our purpose is to obtain the excess of dual fusion frames. First, we recall that two fusion frames
W=\{(W_{i},\omega_{i})\}_{i\in I}
and
Z=\{(Z_{i},\omega_{i})\}_{i\in I}
are called
equivalent

[
9
]
if there exists an invertible operator
U\in B(\mathcal{H})
such that
Z_{i}=U(W_{i}),
for all
i\in I
. It is shown that equivalent fusion frames have the same excess. Indeed, if
Z_{i}=U(W_{i})~(i\in I)
for some invertible operator
U\in B(\mathcal{H})
, then
\{f_{i}\}_{i\in I}\in N(T_{Z})\Leftrightarrow\{U^{-1}f_{i}\}_{i\in I}\in N(T_{W})
. Hence, there exists a bijective correspondence between
N(T_{Z})
and
N(T_{W})
, which implies that
e(Z)=e(W)
. In particular, every fusion frame with its canonical dual have the same excess.

We now review the concept of
Q
-dual fusion frames. We denote the set of bounded left inverses of
T_{W}^{*}
by
\mathcal{L}_{T_{W}^{*}}
.

Definition 3
.

[
13
]

A fusion frame
V=\{(V_{i},\upsilon_{i})\}_{i\in I}
of
\mathcal{H}
is called a
Q
-dual fusion frame of
W=\{(W_{i},\omega_{i})\}_{i\in I}
if there exists a
Q\in B\left(\bigoplus_{i\in I}W_{i},\bigoplus_{i\in I}V_{i}\right)
such that

T_{V}QT_{W}^{*}=I_{\mathcal{H}}.

Moreover, if the operator
M_{i}:\bigoplus_{i\in I}W_{i}\rightarrow\bigoplus_{i\in I}W_{i}
is given by
M_{i}\{f_{j}\}_{j\in I}=\{\delta_{i,j}f_{j}\}_{j\in I}
, then

(i)

Q
is called block-diagonal, if
QM_{i}\left(\bigoplus_{i\in I}W_{i}\right)\subseteq M_{i}\left(\bigoplus_{i\in I}V_{i}\right)
for all
i\in I
,

(ii)

Q
is called component preserving, if
QM_{i}\left(\bigoplus_{i\in I}W_{i}\right)=M_{i}\left(\bigoplus_{i\in I}V_{i}\right)
for all
i\in I
.

In Definition
3
, if
Q
is block-diagonal (component preserving) we say that
V
is
block-diagonal (component preserving) dual fusion frame
of
W.
It is easily seen that every alternate (Gãvruţa) dual fusion frame
V
of
W
is a block-diagonal dual fusion frame with
Q=\varphi_{VW}
, which is introduced in
[
17
]
. It is worthwhile to mention that, unlike ordinary frames, every fusion frame and its
Q
-dual may not necessarily have the same excess, see Examples
6
and
7
. However, in the following proposition, we show that every fusion frame has a non canonical
Q
-dual fusion frame with the same excess.

Proposition 10
.

Let
W=\{(W_{i},\omega_{i})\}_{i\in I}
be a fusion frame of
\mathcal{H}
. Then there exists a non canonical
Q
-dual fusion frame
V=\{(V_{i},\omega_{i})\}_{i\in I}
such that
e(V)=e(W)
.

Proof.

Assume that
\mathcal{F}=\{\omega_{i}f_{i,j}\}_{i\in I,j\in J_{i}}
is a local frame of
W,
where
\{f_{i,j}\}_{j\in J_{i}}
is a Riesz basis of
W_{i}
for all
i\in I
. Consider
V_{i}=\overline{\textnormal{span}}\left\{\omega_{i}S_{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}f_{i,j}\right\}_{j\in J_{i}},
for all
i\in I
and take

Q:\bigoplus_{i\in I}W_{i}\rightarrow\bigoplus_{i\in I}V_{i},~~Q\{h_{i}\}_{i\in I}=\left\{\sum_{j\in J_{i}}\langle h_{i},f_{i,j}\rangle S_{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}f_{i,j}\right\}_{i\in I}.

Since
\left\{\omega_{i}S_{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}f_{i,j}\right\}_{i\in I,j\in J_{i}}
is the canonical dual of
\mathcal{F},
we conclude that
V=\{(V_{i},\omega_{i})\}_{i\in I}
is a
Q
-dual fusion frame of
W,
by
(
13
, Theorem 3.12)
. Therefore, the result is obtained from Proposition

Theorem 12
.

Let

be a fusion frame for
\mathcal{H}_{n}
with a

-dual fusion frame

Moreover, if
V
is a component preserving dual fusion frame, then the right-hand side of the above identity can be summarized to

\big|e(V)-e(W)\big|=\big|\textnormal{dim}N(Q_{\mathcal{A}}^{*})-\textnormal{dim}N(Q_{\mathcal{A}})\big|.

Proof.

Since
V
is a
Q_{\mathcal{A}}
-dual fusion frame of
W,
we observe that

V_{i}^{\perp}\cap R(\mathcal{A}p_{i}^{*})\subseteq V_{i}^{\perp}\cap V_{i}=\{0\},~(i\in I).

Therefore, we get

\displaystyle\bigg|\sum_{i\in I}\textnormal{dim}\big(V_{i}

\displaystyle\cap N(p_{i}\mathcal{A}^{*})\big)-\sum_{i\in I}\textnormal{dim}N(\mathcal{A}p_{i}^{*})\bigg|

\displaystyle=\bigg|\sum_{i\in I}\textnormal{dim}\left(V_{i}\cap R(\mathcal{A}p_{i}^{*})^{\perp}\right)-\sum_{i\in I}\textnormal{dim}N(\mathcal{A}p_{i}^{*})\bigg|

\displaystyle=\bigg|\sum_{i\in I}\textnormal{dim}\left(V_{i}^{\perp}+R(\mathcal{A}p_{i}^{*})\right)^{\perp}-\sum_{i\in I}\textnormal{dim}N(\mathcal{A}p_{i}^{*})\bigg|

\displaystyle=\bigg|\sum_{i\in I}\textnormal{dim}\mathcal{H}_{n}-\sum_{i\in I}\textnormal{dim}\left(V_{i}^{\perp}+R(\mathcal{A}p_{i}^{*})\right)-\sum_{i\in I}\textnormal{dim}N(\mathcal{A}p_{i}^{*})\bigg|

\displaystyle=\bigg|\sum_{i\in I}\textnormal{dim}V_{i}-\sum_{i\in I}\textnormal{dim}R(\mathcal{A}p_{i}^{*})-\sum_{i\in I}\textnormal{dim}N(\mathcal{A}p_{i}^{*})\bigg|

\displaystyle=\bigg|\sum_{i\in I}\textnormal{dim}V_{i}-\sum_{i\in I}\textnormal{dim}W_{i}\bigg|=\big|e(V)-e(W)\big|.

For the moreover part, if
V
is a component preserving dual fusion frame, then the desired result is obtained from the fact that

\displaystyle N(Q_{\mathcal{A}}^{*})

\displaystyle=\left\{\{g_{i}\}_{i\in I}\in\bigoplus_{i\in I}V_{i}:~\sum_{i\in I}\upsilon_{i}^{-1}M_{i}\mathcal{A}^{*}g_{i}=0\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{\{g_{i}\}_{i\in I}\in\bigoplus_{i\in I}V_{i}:~p_{i}\mathcal{A}^{*}g_{i}=0,~\forall~i\in I\right\}

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{i\in I}\big(V_{i}\cap N(p_{i}\mathcal{A}^{*})\big),

and similarly
N(Q_{\mathcal{A}})=\bigoplus_{i\in I}N(\mathcal{A}p_{i}^{*})
.
∎

Theorem 15
.

\mathcal{H}_{n}

-dual fusion frame

\displaystyle e(V)+\textnormal{dim}R(Q_{\mathcal{A}}T_{W}^{*}T_{V})

\displaystyle=\textnormal{dim}\big(N(Q_{\mathcal{A}}^{*})\cap N(T_{W}Q_{\mathcal{A}}^{*})\big)

\displaystyle+\textnormal{dim}\big(R(Q_{\mathcal{A}}^{*})\cap N(T_{W})\big).

Motivated by Theorem

Theorem 17
.

Let
W=\{(W_{i},\omega_{i})\}_{i\in I}
be a fusion frame for
\mathcal{H}
with a
Q_{\mathcal{A}}
-component preserving dual fusion frame
V=\{(V_{i},\upsilon_{i})\}_{i\in I},
where
\mathcal{A}\in\mathcal{L}_{T_{W}^{*}}
and
\textnormal{dim}V_{i}<\infty
for all
i\in I
. Then

e(V)=\sum_{i\in I}\big(\textnormal{dim}V_{i}-\omega_{i}\textnormal{trace}\left(\mathcal{A}p_{i}^{*}\pi_{W_{i}}\right)\big).

Proof.

Let
\{u_{i,j}\}_{j\in J_{i}}
be an orthonormal basis for
V_{i}~(i\in I)
and
\{U_{i,j}\}_{i\in I,j\in J_{i}}
be the orthonormal basis defined as in (
3
) for
\bigoplus_{i\in I}V_{i}
. The operator
P
of
\bigoplus_{i\in I}V_{i}
onto
N(T_{V})
defined by
P=I_{\bigoplus V_{i}}-Q_{\mathcal{A}}T_{W}^{*}T_{V}
is idempotent. Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle e(V)=\textnormal{dim}N(T_{V})

\displaystyle=\textnormal{trace}(P)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I,j\in J_{i}}\big\langle U_{i,j},PU_{i,j}\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I,j\in J_{i}}\left(1-\big\langle U_{i,j},Q_{\mathcal{A}}T_{W}^{*}T_{V}U_{i,j}\big\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I,j\in J_{i}}\left(1-\big\langle U_{i,j},Q_{\mathcal{A}}\{\omega_{k}\pi_{W_{k}}\upsilon_{i}u_{i,j}\}_{k\in I}\big\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I,j\in J_{i}}\left(1-\left\langle U_{i,j},\big\{\upsilon_{i}^{-1}\mathcal{A}M_{i}\{\omega_{k}\pi_{W_{k}}\upsilon_{i}u_{i,j}\}_{k\in I}\big\}_{i\in I}\right\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I,j\in J_{i}}\left(1-\big\langle u_{i,j},\mathcal{A}\{\delta_{k,i}\omega_{k}\pi_{W_{k}}u_{i,j}\}_{k\in I}\big\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I,j\in J_{i}}\left(1-\big\langle u_{i,j},\mathcal{A}p_{i}^{*}\omega_{i}\pi_{W_{i}}u_{i,j}\big\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I}\bigg(\textnormal{dim}V_{i}-\omega_{i}\sum_{j\in J_{i}}\big\langle u_{i,j},\mathcal{A}p_{i}^{*}\pi_{W_{i}}u_{i,j}\big\rangle\bigg)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I}\big(\textnormal{dim}V_{i}-\omega_{i}\textnormal{trace}(\mathcal{A}p_{i}^{*}\pi_{W_{i}})\big).

∎

i\in I
. Then

e(V)=\sum_{i\in I}\left(\textnormal{dim}V_{i}-\omega_{i}\upsilon_{i}\textnormal{trace}\left(\pi_{V_{i}}S_{W}^{-1}\pi_{W_{i}}\right)\right).

Suppose that
W=\{(W_{i},\omega_{i})\}_{i\in I}
is a fusion frame for
\mathcal{H}
. As already mentioned, every bounded left inverse of
T_{W}^{*}
is the operator
\mathcal{A}
of the form
\mathcal{A}=S_{W}^{-1}T_{W}+RP,
where
R\in B\left(\bigoplus_{i\in I}W_{i},\mathcal{H}\right)
and
P=I_{\bigoplus W_{i}}-T_{W}^{*}S_{W}^{-1}T_{W}
is an orthogonal projection from
\bigoplus_{i\in I}W_{i}
onto
N(T_{W})
. Hence, for every
Q_{\mathcal{A}}
-dual fusion frame
V=\{(V_{i},\omega_{i})\}_{i\in I}
of
W
such that
\textnormal{dim}V_{i}<\infty
for all
i\in I
, it follows

\displaystyle e(V)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I}\big(\textnormal{dim}V_{i}-\omega_{i}\textnormal{trace}(\mathcal{A}p_{i}^{*}\pi_{W_{i}})\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I}\left(\textnormal{dim}V_{i}-\omega_{i}\textnormal{trace}\left(S_{W}^{-1}T_{W}p_{i}^{*}\pi_{W_{i}}+RPp_{i}^{*}\pi_{W_{i}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I}\left(\textnormal{dim}V_{i}-\omega_{i}^{2}\textnormal{trace}\left(\pi_{W_{i}}S_{W}^{-1}\pi_{W_{i}}\right)-\omega_{i}\textnormal{trace}\left(RPp_{i}^{*}\pi_{W_{i}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I}\big(\textnormal{dim}V_{i}-\textnormal{dim}W_{i}\big)+e(W)-\sum_{i\in I}\omega_{i}\textnormal{trace}\left(RPp_{i}^{*}\pi_{W_{i}}\right).

Here, we compute the excess of a Gãvruţa dual fusion frame of a given fusion frame.

Example 7
.

Consider the fusion frame
W=\{(W_{i},\omega)\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
and its Gãvruţa dual
V=\{(V_{i},\omega)\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
introduced in Example
4
(3). As we have already seen
e(V)=nm-1.
Now, we are going to calculate the excess of
V
by Corollary
18
. To this end, note that

\sum_{i=n+1}^{\infty}\left(\textnormal{dim}V_{i}-\omega^{2}\textnormal{trace}\left(\pi_{V_{i}}S_{W}^{-1}\pi_{W_{i}}\right)\right)=\sum_{i=n+1}^{\infty}\big(1-\omega^{2}\omega^{-2}\big)=0.

Hence, we get

\displaystyle e(V)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(\textnormal{dim}V_{i}-\omega^{2}\textnormal{trace}\left(\pi_{V_{i}}S_{W}^{-1}\pi_{W_{i}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\textnormal{dim}V_{i}-\omega^{2}\textnormal{trace}\left(\pi_{V_{i}}S_{W}^{-1}\pi_{W_{i}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=2\left[(m+1)-\omega^{2}\left(\omega^{-2}+\frac{\omega^{-2}}{2}\right)\right]+\sum_{i=2}^{n-1}\left[(m+1)-\omega^{2}\omega^{-2}\right]=nm-1.

The significance of the above example lies in the fact that if
m=2
, then for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we can provide a fusion frame
W
and a Gãvruţa dual
V
of
W
such that
e(V)-e(W)=n
. However, this statement is not possible in the context of ordinary frames.

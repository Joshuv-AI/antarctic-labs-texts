On the effective reduction of an ideal
Tomasz Rodak, Adam Różycki, Stanisław Spodzieja

Abstract
It is well known that in the Noetherian local ring with infinite residue field the reduction of 𝔪 \mathfrak{m} -primary ideal may be given in the form of a sufficiently general linear combination of its generators. In the paper we give a condition for the existence of such reduction in terms of the sum of degrees of the ideal fiber cone prime divisors in the case of any Noetherian local ring.



1
Introduction

Let
R
be a commutative ring with unity and let
\mathfrak{a}
be its ideal. We
say that ideal
\mathfrak{b}\subset\mathfrak{a}
is a
reduction
of
\mathfrak{a}

if there exists positive integer
k
such that

\mathfrak{a}^{k+1}=\mathfrak{b}\mathfrak{a}^{k}.

Obviously, ideal
\mathfrak{a}
is always a reduction of
\mathfrak{a}
. In
practice it is often important to know if an ideal has a reduction which is in
some sens smaller then the ideal itself.

Assume now that
(R,\mathfrak{m})
is a Noetherian local ring. The
fiber
cone
of ideal
\mathfrak{a}\subset R
is the ring

\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{a}}(R)=\frac{R}{\mathfrak{m}}\oplus\frac{\mathfrak{a}}{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{a}}\oplus\frac{\mathfrak{a}^{2}}{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{a}^{2}}\oplus\cdots

The ring
\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{a}}(R)
is a finitely generated graded ring
over the residue field
R/\mathfrak{m}
by elements of degree one.
The Krull dimension of
\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{a}}(R)
is called
analytic
spread
of
\mathfrak{a}
and is denoted
\ell(\mathfrak{a})
.
It is well known that the minimal number of generators in a reduction
of
\mathfrak{a}
is bounded below by
\ell(\mathfrak{a})
(see
[
3
, Corollary
8.2.5]
). Moreover, if the residue field
R/\mathfrak{m}
is infinite,
then
\mathfrak{a}
has a reduction generated by
\ell(\mathfrak{a})
elements and
every such reduction is minimal with respect to inclusion (see

[
3
, Proposition 8.3.7]
). From
[
3
, Corollary 8.3.9]

we have
0pt\mathfrak{a}\leqslant\ell(\mathfrak{a})\leqslant\dim R
. Hence, if
\mathfrak{a}

is a primary ideal belonging to
\mathfrak{m}
, then
\ell(\mathfrak{a})
is equal to
the Krull dimension
\dim R
of
R
. The following theorem is well
known (see
[
5
, Theorem 14.14]
).

Theorem 1
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be an
n
-dimensional Noetherian local ring,
and suppose that
R/\mathfrak{m}
is an infinite field; let

\mathfrak{q}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{m})R
be an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal. Then if

y_{i}=\sum a_{ij}u_{j}
for
1\leqslant i\leqslant n
are
n

’sufficiently general’ linear combinations of
u_{1},\ldots,u_{m}
, the
ideal
\mathfrak{b}=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})R
is a reduction of
\mathfrak{q}
and

y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}
is a system of parameters of
R
.

In this paper
we give an estimation for the number of linearly independent linear
forms which provide a reduction in the spirit of the above theorem for
any
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal with sufficiently small sum of the degrees of
prime divisors.

Before we state our main result, we give a definition.

Let
k
be a field and let
R
be a Noetherian graded
k
-algebra
finitely generated over
k
by elements of degree one. Then we set

\operatorname{deg.rad}R:=\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{AssMin}R}\deg R/\mathfrak{p},

where
\operatorname{AssMin}R
denotes the set of minimal prime ideals in
R
.

The number
\operatorname{deg.rad}R
is an algebraic counterpart of
the following notion introduced in the setting of complex algebraic
geometry (see
[
4
, VII.11.8]
). Let

V\subset\mathbb{P}^{m}
be an algebraic set
in a complex projective space
\mathbb{P}^{m}
. If we set

\delta(V)=\sum\deg V_{i}
, where
V=\bigcup V_{i}
is a decomposition into
irreducible components, then
\delta(V)=\operatorname{deg.rad}R
,
where
R=k[V]
.

Let
\mathfrak{q}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{m})R
, where
(R,\mathfrak{m})
is a Noetherian local
ring. Then

\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{q}}(R)\simeq\frac{\frac{R}{\mathfrak{m}}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}]}{Q},

(1)

where
Q
is the kernel of the
homomorphism
\frac{R}{\mathfrak{m}}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}]\to\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{q}}(R)

given by
X_{i}\mapsto u_{i}+\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{q}
.
We will write
\bar{P}(X)
for
the image in
\frac{R}{\mathfrak{m}}[X]
of a polynomial
P(X)\in R[X]
. Observe, that
for a homogeneous polynomial
P(X)\in R[X]
of degree
k
we have

\bar{P}(X)\in Q
if and only if
\bar{P}(X)
is a null-form of
\mathfrak{q}
,
i.e.
P(u_{1},\ldots,u_{m})\in\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{q}^{k}
.

Let
k
be a field. We will say that a sequence of linear forms

N_{1},\ldots,N_{s}\in k[X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}]
,
s\geqslant m
is independent if
for any
1\leqslant i_{1}<\cdots<i_{m}\leqslant s
the system

N_{i_{1}},\ldots,N_{i_{m}}
is linearly independent.

Our main result is

Main Theorem
.

Let
m
,
n
,
d
be some positive integers, where
m\geqslant n
.
Set
l=d(m-n)+n
. Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a local Noetherian
n
-dimensional ring and let

\bar{N}_{j}\in\frac{R}{\mathfrak{m}}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}]
,
j=1,\ldots,l
be a sequence of independent linear forms.
For any
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal
\mathfrak{q}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{m})R=(u)R
, such that

\operatorname{deg.rad}\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{q}}(R)\leqslant d.

there exist
1\leqslant i_{1}<\cdots<i_{n}\leqslant l
, such that

\mathfrak{b}=(N_{i_{1}}(u),\ldots,N_{i_{n}}(u))R
is a reduction of

\mathfrak{q}
and
N_{i_{1}}(u),\ldots,N_{i_{n}}(u)
is a system of parameters
of
R
.

Proof of the main result

Lemma 1
.

Let
I
be an ideal in a Noetherian ring
R
and let
\operatorname{AssMin}I=\{P_{1},\ldots,P_{s}\}
.
If for
a\in R
we set

\operatorname{AssMin}(P_{i}+Ra)=\{P_{i1},\ldots,P_{is_{i}}\}
, then

\operatorname{rad}(I+Ra)=\bigcap_{i=1}^{s}\bigcap_{j=1}^{s_{i}}P_{ij}.

Proof.

Let
P
be a minimal prime ideal over
I+Ra
. There exists
P_{i}
such that
P_{i}\subset P
. Since
I+Ra\subset P_{i}+Ra\subset P
, then
P
is minimal over
P_{i}+Ra
. This gives
\supset
.

Now, take
P_{ij}
which is minimal with respect to inclusion in the set
\{P_{ij}:i=1,\ldots,s,j=1,\ldots,s_{i}\}
.
It is enough to prove that
P_{ij}
is minimal over
I+Ra
. Suppose
to the contrary, that there exists a prime ideal
P^{\prime}\subset R
such
that
I+Ra\subset P^{\prime}\subsetneq P_{ij}
. Then, there exists

P_{i^{\prime}}\subset P^{\prime}
and since
a\in P^{\prime}
then as a result there exists

P_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\subset P^{\prime}
. Hence
P_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\subsetneq P_{ij}
which is
impossible by the minimality of
P_{ij}
.
∎

In what follows we will assume that
X=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{m})
is a system
of variables.

Lemma 2
.

Let
Q
be a homogeneous
ideal in a polynomial ring
k[X]
over a field
k
. If
N\in k[X]

is a linear form, then

\operatorname{deg.rad}(k[X]/(Q+k[X]N)\leqslant\operatorname{deg.rad}(k[X]/Q).

Proof.

Let
\operatorname{AssMin}Q=\{P_{1},\ldots,P_{s}\}
and let

\operatorname{AssMin}(P_{i}+k[X]N)=\{P_{i1},\ldots,P_{is_{i}}\}
. Observe that if

N\notin P_{i}
then for any
j
,
0ptP_{ij}=0ptP_{i}+1
. Indeed, let

S=k[X]_{P_{ij}}/P_{i}k[X]_{P_{ij}}
. Then

0ptP_{ij}=\dim k[X]_{P_{ij}}=0ptP_{i}k[X]_{P_{ij}}+\dim S=0ptP_{i}+\dim S.

By Krull’s
principal ideal theorem
0ptP_{ij}S\leqslant 1
, since
P_{ij}S
is minimal over
(P_{i}+k[X]N)S
,
which in turn is generated in
S
by the image of
N
. The ring
S
is
an integral domain and the image of
N
in
S
is non-zero, hence

\dim S=0ptP_{ij}S=1
, which proves the claim.

From the above we have

\deg(k[X]/(P_{i}+k[X]N))=\sum_{j=1}^{s_{i}}\lambda_{j}\deg(k[X]/P_{ij}),

where
\lambda_{j}
stands for the length of the ring

k[X]_{P_{ij}}/(P_{i}+k[X]N)k[X]_{P_{ij}}
as a module over itself.
On the other hand, since
\deg N=1
, then by

[
1
, Proposition 5.3.6]
we have

\deg(k[X]/P_{i})=\deg(k[X]/(P_{i}+k[X]N)).

This and Lemma
1
gives

\displaystyle\operatorname{deg.rad}(k[X]/Q)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{s}\deg(k[X]/P_{i})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{s}\deg(k[X]/(P_{i}+k[X]N))

\displaystyle\geqslant\sum_{i=1}^{s}\sum_{j=1}^{s_{i}}\deg(k[X]/P_{ij})

\displaystyle\geqslant\operatorname{deg.rad}(k[X]/(Q+k[X]N)).

∎

Lemma 3
.

Let
m
,
n
,
d
be positive integers with

m\geqslant n
, let
l=d(m-n)+1
, and let
N_{j}
,
j=1,\ldots,l

be a system of independent linear functions. Then for any homogeneous
ideal
Q
in
k[X]
, with
\dim(k[X]/Q)\leqslant n
and

\operatorname{deg.rad}(k[X]/Q)\leqslant d
, there exists
j
such that

\dim(k[X]/(Q+N_{j}k[X]))<n
.

Proof.

Let
\{P_{1},\ldots,P_{s}\}
be the set of minimal prime ideals of
Q
. We
have
d\geqslant\sum_{i}\deg(k[X]/P_{i})\geqslant s
. It is enough to proof that
there exists
j\in\{1,\ldots,l\}
such that
N_{j}\notin P_{i}
for any
P_{i}
with
0ptP_{i}=m-n
. Indeed, in this case for any prime ideal
P
containing

Q+N_{j}k[X]
we have
0ptP>0ptP_{i}=m-n
, and thus
\dim(k[X]/(Q+N_{j}k[X]))<n
.
Now, suppose to the contrary, that for any

j\in\{1,\ldots,l\}
there exists
\psi(j)\in\{1,\ldots,s\}
such
that
N_{j}\in P_{\psi(j)}
and

0ptP_{\psi(j)}=m-n
. Since
N_{j}
are independent then ideal
generated by any
r\leqslant m
of them is of height
r
, so we
must have
\#\psi^{-1}(i)\leqslant m-n
for any
i
. Hence

d(m-n)+1=l=\#\psi^{-1}(\{1,\ldots,s\})\leqslant s(m-n)\leqslant d(m-n),

which gives a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 4
.

Let
m
,
n
,
d
be positive integers with
m\geqslant n
, let
l=d(m-n)+n
, and let
N_{j}
,
j=1,\ldots,l
be a system of independent linear functions. Then for any homogeneous ideal

in
k[X]
, with
\dim(k[X]/Q)\leqslant n

\operatorname{deg.rad}(k[X]/Q)\leqslant d

is primary ideal belonging to
(X_{1},\ldots,X_{m})k[X]

Proof.

The proof will be carried out by induction with respect to
n\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
.
For
n=1
the assertion
follows from Lemma
3
. Indeed, there exists
N_{i_{1}}
such that

\dim(k[X]/(Q+N_{i_{1}}k[X]))=0
, hence

is
(X_{1},\ldots,X_{m})k[X]
-primary.

Assume that the assertion holds

. Take any homogeneous ideal
Q\subset k[X]
with
\dim(k[X]/Q)\leqslant n
and
\operatorname{deg.rad}(k[X]/Q)\leqslant d
. By Lemma
3
there exists, say
N_{1}
, such that
\dim(k[X]/(Q+N_{1}k[X]))<n
. By Lemma
2
,
\operatorname{deg.rad}(k[X]/(Q+N_{1}k[X])\leqslant d
.
So the induction hypothesis is fulfilled for the image of the ideal
Q
in
k[X]/k[X]N_{1}
. Hence there exists
N_{i_{2}},\ldots,N_{i_{n}}
in the family

N_{2},\ldots,N_{l}
of
l-1=d(m-n)+n-1=d((m-1)-(n-1))+n-1

is a primary ideal belonging to
(X_{1},\ldots,X_{m})k[X]
.

Remark 1
.

If
\frac{R}{\mathfrak{m}}
is infinite then by the above theorem any

\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal has a reduction generated by
n
linear
combinations of its generators with the coefficients in
R

fact, from
[

it follows that this

On the other hand, if

is a finite field, then there is only
finitely many independent linear forms in

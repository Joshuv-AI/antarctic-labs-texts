Gravitational-wave tails of memory at 4PN order
David Trestini a,b,∗ and Luc Blanchet a

Abstract
We study a novel cubic nonlinear effect, the tails-of-memory, which consist of a combination of the tail effect (backscattering of linear gravitational waves against the curvature of spacetime generated by the source) and the memory effect (due to reradiation of gravitational waves by linear gravitational waves themselves). Our final result is consistent with a straightforward direct computation of the memory effect, but also involves many non-trivial tail-like terms.

Introduction

The recent completion of the flux and phase evolution of gravitational waves generated by compact binary systems at fourth and a half post-Newtonian order (4.5PN),
[

These effects are encoded in the relation between the radiative moments, which parametrize the multipolar structure of the waveform as measured by an asymptotic far-away observer, and the so-called canonical moments, which parametrize the
linearized
vacuum metric outside the source. The canonical moments are in turn connected
via

u=t-r/c
, but also
hereditary
terms, which entail a dependence of the radiative moments on the whole past history of the source.

Some well-known hereditary effects are: the tail terms, which arise at 1.5PN order beyond quadrupole radiation, due to an interaction between the ADM mass of the system
\mathrm{M}
and the canonical quadrupole
\mathrm{M}_{ij}
; the memory effect at 2.5PN, due to the
\mathrm{M}_{ij}\times\mathrm{M}_{ij}
interaction; and the tail-of-tail
\mathrm{M}\times\mathrm{M}\times\mathrm{M}_{ij}
at 3PN. In this contribution, we are interested in the
\mathrm{M}\times\mathrm{M}_{ij}\times\mathrm{M}_{ij}
interaction which enters at 4PN order, dubbed
tails-of-memory

[

\mathrm{M}_{L}

\mathrm{S}_{L}

[

[
5

h^{\mu\nu}\equiv\sqrt{-g}g^{\mu\nu}-\eta^{\mu\nu}

with standard notation. In a general coordinate system, the equations read

\Box h^{\mu\nu}-\partial H^{\mu\nu}=\Lambda^{\mu\nu}\bigl[h,\partial h,\partial^{2}h\bigr]\,,

(1)

\Box

H^{\mu}\equiv\partial_{\nu}h^{\mu\nu}
and we introduce the useful shorthand notation

\partial\theta^{\mu\nu}\equiv 2\partial^{(\mu}\theta^{\nu)}-\eta^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\rho}\theta^{\rho}
for any
\theta^{\mu}
. With the MPM construction the radiative metric is formally decomposed to any PM order as
h^{\mu\nu}=\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}G^{n}h_{n}^{\mu\nu}

and
\overline{\mathrm{S}}_{L}
(different from
\mathrm{M}_{L}
and
\mathrm{S}_{L}
in harmonic coordinates) which parametrize the linear approximation
h_{1}^{\mu\nu}
defined as

h^{\mu\nu}_{1}\equiv h_{\text{can}\,1}^{\mu\nu}[\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{L},\overline{\mathrm{S}}_{L}]+\partial\xi_{1}^{\mu\nu}\,,

(2)

\Box h_{n}^{\mu\nu}-\partial H^{\mu\nu}_{n}=\Lambda_{n}^{\mu\nu}\,,

\mathop{\mathrm{FP}}_{B=0}

B\to 0

r\to 0

\Box v_{n}^{\mu\nu}=0

\partial_{\nu}v_{n}^{\mu\nu}=-\partial_{\nu}u_{n}^{\mu\nu}

The memory effect

The “memory” in the waveform of any radiative source can be expressed as the following contributions to the
mass-type
radiative moments
\mathcal{U}_{L}^{\mathrm{mem}}
(but
\mathcal{V}_{L}^{\mathrm{mem}}=0

\mathcal{U}_{L}^{\mathrm{mem}}=\frac{2c^{\ell-2}(2\ell+1)!!}{(\ell+1)(\ell+2)}\int\mathrm{d}\Omega\,\hat{n}_{L}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\,\frac{\mathrm{d}E_{\mathrm{gw}}}{\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}\Omega}(u-\tau)\,,

(11)

where the effective power density of gravitational waves is given by

\frac{\mathrm{d}E_{\mathrm{gw}}}{\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}\Omega}=\lim_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{r^{2}c^{3}}{32\pi G}\,\dot{h}_{ij}^{\text{TT}}\,\dot{h}_{ij}^{\text{TT}}\,.

(12)

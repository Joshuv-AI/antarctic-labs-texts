Newton Polygons of Sums on Curves II: Variation in p p -adic Families
Joe Kramer-Miller and James Upton

Abstract
In this article we study the behavior of Newton polygons along ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} -towers of curves. Fix an ordinary curve X X over a finite field 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} of characteristic p p . By a ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} -tower X ∞ / X X_{\infty}/X we mean a tower of covers … → X 2 → X 1 → X \dots\to X_{2}\to X_{1}\to X with Gal ⁡ ( X n / X ) ≅ ℤ / p n ​ ℤ \mathrm{Gal}(X_{n}/X)\cong\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z} . We show that if the ramification along the tower is sufficiently moderate, then the slopes of the Newton polygon of X n X_{n} are equidistributed in the interval [ 0 , 1 ] [0,1] as n n tends to ∞ \infty . Under a stronger congruence assumption on the ramification invariants, we completely determine the slopes of the Newton polygon of each curve. This is the first result towards ‘regularity’ in Newton polygon behavior for ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} -towers over higher genus curves. We also obtain similar results for ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} -towers twisted by a generic tame character.



1
Introduction

1.1
Motivation

An important theme in modern number theory is that of
p
-variation—the idea
of interpolating a family of mathematical objects or invariants by a
p
-adic parameter.
For instance, Iwasawa constructed an “arithmetic”
p
-adic
L
-function, which interpolates
the
p
-part of class groups of a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-tower over a number field.
The existence (and finiteness) of this object allows one to deduce the remarkable
Iwasawa class number formula (see e.g.
[
20
]
).
A general principle is as follows: if
M
is a motive over a number field
K

and
\rho:G_{K}\to\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is a additive character, we may consider a family
of motives
M\otimes\rho_{\chi}
. Here
\rho_{\chi}
denotes the composition
\chi\circ\rho
and
\chi
varies over the space
\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_{p},\mathbb{C}_{p}^{\times})
of continuous characters.
One expects that
p
-adic families of invariants can be constructed that interpolate
classical invariants.
For example, we may consider a motive
M_{f}
corresponding to an eigenform
f
.
The algebraic special values of
L(M_{f}\otimes\chi,s)^{\mathrm{alg}}
, where
\chi
is a Dirichlet character of
p
-power degree,
can be parameterized by a
p
-adic
L
-function (see e.g.
[
16
]
). This is somewhat miraculous, as it tells us that
L(M_{f}\otimes\chi,1)^{\mathrm{alg}}

is close to
L(M_{f}\otimes\chi^{\prime},1)^{\mathrm{alg}}

p
-adically if
\chi
and
\chi^{\prime}
are sufficiently close.

It is natural to ask for similar
p
-adic variational phenomena to occur over function fields.
Let
X
be a smooth affine curve over a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}

with smooth compactification
\overline{X}
. Let
M
be a motive (we are working
p
-adically so it
makes sense to realize
M
as an overconvergent
F
-isocrystal on
X
) and let
\rho:\pi_{1}(X)\to\mathbb{Z}_{p}
be a continuous character. Unlike the number field case,
it is relatively easy to construct a
p
-adic object that interpolates the
L
-functions
L(M\otimes\rho_{\chi},s)
.
This object will not be well behaved in general—for instance, the underlying Iwasawa module will no longer be finitely
generated. However, we do expect there to be well-behaved variational properties
when
\rho
is sufficiently “nice”. When
M
is trivial this expectation manifests in a program devised by Daqing Wan in
[
19
]
.
Wan identifies three properties that one may expect for nice
\rho
:

1.

(monodromy stability) We say that
\rho
is
monodromy stable
if there exist
a,b\in\mathbb{Q}
such that: for
n\gg 0
and
\chi
of finite order
p^{n}
we have
\deg(L(\rho_{\chi},s))=a+bp^{n}
.

2.

(slope uniformity) We say that
\chi
is
slope uniform
if for
\chi
of finite order
p^{n}
, the slopes of
\NP_{q}L(\rho_{\chi},s)
are equidistributed in
[0,1]
as
n
tends to infinity.

3.

(slope stability) We say that
\chi
is
slope stable
if
there exist
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{d}\in(0,1)\cap\mathbb{Q}
and
k>0
such that
for all
n>k
and all
\chi
of finite order
p^{n}
, the slopes of
\NP_{q}L(\rho_{\chi},s)

are

K\sqcup\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{d}\Bigg\{\frac{\alpha_{i}}{p^{n-k}},\frac{\alpha_{i}+1}{p^{n-k}},\dots,\frac{\alpha_{i}+p^{n-k}-1}{p^{n-k}}\Bigg\},

where
K
is a set of
2g
elements in
[0,1]
.

These properties first appear in work of Davis-Wan-Xiao
[
4
]
, who prove that all three are satisfied when

X=\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{1}
and
\rho
is a particularly simple representation.
The representations where we expect (at least some of) these properties to hold can be broken into two classes. The first
class are
\rho
that
come from geometry
(e.g.
\rho
arises from the relative
p
-adic étale cohomology
of a fiber-wise ordinary smooth proper fibration
Y\to X
). In
[
19
]
, Wan conjectures that
all three properties hold for
\rho
coming from geometry. The only progress is due to the first author
in
[
10
]
, where geometric representations are proven to satisfy a slightly weaker version
of monodromy stability.
The second class of representations are those with
strictly stable monodromy
(see §
1.2
-
1.4
below). By work of Kosters and Zhu (see
[
9
]
), we know that strictly stable towers over
\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{1}
are slope uniform.

In this article we prove slope uniformity for strictly stable representations over any
ordinary curve. We also prove slope stability for strictly stable representations that
satisfy a stronger monodromy condition. To the best of our knowledge, these are the first
examples of these phenomena for curves other than
\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{1}
and
\mathbb{G}_{m}
.
Our proof uses the local-to-global methods developed in
[
12
]
,
[
11
]
, and the previous article
[
13
]
.

1.2
Main Results

Definition 1.1
.

Let
F
be a field. A
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-tower
of fields
F_{\infty}/F
is a sequence of finite Galois extensions

\cdots\supseteq F_{2}\supseteq F_{1}\supseteq F_{0}=F,

(1)

together with an identification
\mathrm{Gal}(F_{n}/F)\cong\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z}
for all
n\geq 0
.

Suppose that
F
is a local field of characteristic
p
. Let
F_{\infty}/F
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-tower of local fields. For each
n\geq 0
, let
v_{n}
denote the highest ramification break (in upper numbering) of the finite extension
F_{n}/F
. By local class field theory, we have a lower bound on the growth of this sequence:

v_{n+1}\geq pv_{n}.

(2)

Definition 1.2
.

We say that
F_{\infty}/F
has
strictly stable monodromy
if (
2
) is an equality for all
n\gg 0
. Equivalently,
F_{\infty}/F
has strictly stable monodromy if and only if there exists
\delta\in\mathbb{Z}[\tfrac{1}{p}]
such that

v_{n}=\delta p^{n-1}

for all
n\gg 0
. In this case, we also say that
F_{\infty}/F
has
\delta
-stable
monodromy.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
X
be a curve. A
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-tower
of curves
X_{\infty}/X
is a sequence of finite étale covers

\cdots\to X_{2}\to X_{1}\to X_{0}=X.

together with an identification
\mathrm{Gal}(X_{n}/X)=\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z}
for all
n\geq 0
.

Let
X/\mathbb{F}_{q}
be a smooth affine curve with smooth compactification
\overline{X}
. Let
S
denote the complement of
X
in
\overline{X}
. For each
P\in S
, let
F_{P}
denote the local field of
X
at
P
. If
X_{\infty}/X
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-tower of curves, then by restriction we obtain a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-tower of local fields
F_{P,\infty}/F_{P}
.

Definition 1.4
.

We say that
X_{\infty}/X
has
strictly stable monodromy
if
F_{P,\infty}/F_{P}
has strictly stable monodromy for all
P\in S
. Given a tuple
\bm{\delta}=(\delta_{P})_{P\in S}
of positive numbers in
\mathbb{Z}[\tfrac{1}{p}]
, we say that
F_{\infty}/F
has
\bm{\delta}
-stable
monodromy if
F_{P,\infty}/F_{P}
has
\delta_{P}
-stable monodromy for each
P\in S
.

Let
X_{\infty}/X
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-tower of curves, corresponding to a continuous and surjective map
\rho:\pi_{1}(X)\to\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. If
\chi\in\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_{p},\mathbb{C}_{p}^{\times})
is a continuous character, then we may form the composite

\rho_{\chi}:\pi_{1}(X)\to\mathbb{Z}_{p}\xrightarrow{\chi}\mathbb{C}_{p}^{\times}.

Thus we obtain a family of
p
-adic characters of
\pi_{1}(X)
parameterized by the
p
-adic space
\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_{p},\mathbb{C}_{p}^{\times})
. In this light, it is convenient to view
X_{\infty}/X
as a family of characters of
\pi_{1}(X)
.
We can now state our first theorem:

Theorem 1.5
.

Assume that
\overline{X}
is ordinary. If
X_{\infty}/X
has strictly stable monodromy, then
X_{\infty}/X
is slope uniform.

1.2.1
Results on Newton Polygons

We now state our results regarding the interaction of Newton and Hodge polygons of characters. Let
X_{\infty}/X
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-tower of curves with
\bm{\delta}
-stable monodromy.

Definition 1.6
.

The
Hodge polygon

\HP(X_{\infty}/X)
is the convex polygon with slope set

\{\underbrace{0,...,0}_{g-1+|S|}\}\sqcup\bigsqcup_{P\in S}\left\{\frac{p-1}{\delta_{P}},\frac{2(p-1)}{\delta_{P}},...\right\}.

Let
\chi:\mathbb{Z}_{p}\to\mathbb{C}_{p}^{\times}
be a finite non-trivial character of order
p^{m_{\chi}}
. We attach to
\chi
the parameter

\pi_{\chi}=\chi(1)-1,

and regard
\chi
as taking values in the ring
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\pi_{\chi}]
. Thus, the Artin
L
-function
L(\rho_{\chi},s)
is a polynomial with coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}_{q}[\pi_{\chi}]
. In this article, it will be convenient for us to adopt the following normalization convention for Newton polgyons:

Definition 1.7
.

Let
\pi_{q,\chi}=\pi_{\chi}^{v_{p}(q)}
. The
normalized Newton polygon
of
\rho_{\chi}
is

\NP(\rho_{\chi})=\NP_{\pi_{q,\chi}}L(\rho_{\chi},s).

Let
e_{\chi}=v_{\pi_{\chi}}(p)=p^{m_{\chi}-1}(p-1)
denote the absolute ramification index of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\pi_{\chi}]
. Let
m_{0}
be the smallest non-negative integer such that for each
P\in S
the highest ramification break (in upper numbering) of
F_{P,m_{0}+1}/F_{P}
is
p^{m_{0}}\delta_{P}
.
From (
2
) we see that the highest ramification break of
F_{P,m}/F_{P}
is
p^{m-1}\delta_{P}
for all
m>m_{0}
. By work of the first author in
[
11
]
, the Hodge polygon
\HP(X_{\infty}/X)
provides a common lower bound for
\NP(\rho_{\chi})
whenever
m_{\chi}>m_{0}
:

\NP^{<e_{\chi}}(\rho_{\chi})\succeq\HP^{<e_{\chi}}(X_{\infty}/X).

(3)

If
\overline{X}
is ordinary, then these polygons have the same terminal point.
The next theorem greatly improves the estimate (
3
) when
\overline{X}
is ordinary. It shows that the Newton and Hodge polygons touch periodically:

Theorem 1.8
.

Assume that
\overline{X}
is ordinary. Let
d=p^{m_{0}}\sum_{P\in S}\delta_{P}
. For all
m_{\chi}>m_{0}
and all
1\leq n<p^{m_{\chi}-m_{0}-1}
, the polygons
\NP^{<e_{\chi}}(\rho_{\chi})
and
\HP(X_{\infty}/X)
agree on the interval

[g-1+nd,g-1+|S|+nd].

(4)

Theorem
1.5
is then an easy consequence of Theorem
1.8
. If we impose additional congruence conditions on the
\delta_{P}
, we can completely determine the Newton polygon of each finite character:

Theorem 1.9
.

Let
X_{\infty}/X
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-tower of curves with
\bm{\delta}
-stable monodromy. Then
\NP^{<e_{\chi}}(\rho_{\chi})=\HP^{<e_{\chi}}(X_{\infty}/X)
for every finite
\chi
if and only if:

1.

\overline{X}
is ordinary.

2.

m_{0}=0
, so that
\delta_{P}\in\mathbb{Z}
for all
P\in S
.

3.

p\equiv 1\pmod{\delta_{P}}
for all
P\in S
.

As a consequence of Theorem
1.9
, we deduce the full form of
[
13
, Theorem 1.3]
.
This gives a necessary and sufficient condition for the Newton polygon of a single character to coincide with its Hodge bound. Theorem
1.9
also provides our first examples of slope stability over curves of higher genus:

Corollary 1.10
.

Every
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-tower of curves
X_{\infty}/X
satisfying the conditions of Theorem
1.9
is slope stable.

1.2.2
Towers Twisted by Tame Characters

Let us continue to assume that
X_{\infty}/X
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-tower with
\bm{\delta}
-stable monodromy, corresponding to a continuous and surjective map
\rho:\pi_{1}(X)\to\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. Let
\psi:\pi_{1}(X)\to\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
be a tame character. We will write
\psi\otimes\rho
or

\psi\otimes X_{\infty}/X
for the “family” of characters
\psi\otimes\rho_{\chi}

parameterized by the space
\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_{p},\mathbb{C}_{p}^{\times})
.
By combining Theorem
1.8
and Theorem
1.9
with the results of
[
11
]
, we are able to prove
stability and uniformity results for this twisted family.

Let
f:X^{\mathrm{tame}}\to X
be the finite cyclic covering corresponding to
\ker(\psi)
. For each
P\in S
, let
s_{P}
denote the ramification index of
f
at
P
. If
Q
is a point of
\overline{X}^{\mathrm{tame}}
lying above
P
, then we define
\delta_{Q}^{\mathrm{tame}}=s_{P}\delta_{P}
. Our results are as follows:

Theorem 1.11
.

Assume
\overline{X}^{\mathrm{tame}}
is ordinary. Then
\psi\otimes X_{\infty}/X
is slope uniform.

Theorem 1.12
.

Assume that
\overline{X}^{\mathrm{tame}}
is ordinary and that
p\equiv 1\mod\delta^{\mathrm{tame}}_{Q}
for
Q
lying above
P\in S
.
Then
\psi\otimes X_{\infty}/X
is slope stable.

Remark 1.13
.

Our assumption that
\overline{X}^{\mathrm{tame}}
is ordinary is mild: A Theorem of Bouw
[
3
]

guarnatees that a generic cover will be ordinary. This uses the fact that
\deg(f)|p-1
in an essential way.
If
\deg(f)
does not divide
p-1
the lower Hodge bound is obtained by ‘averaging’ the Hodge decomposition
of each isotypical component of
H^{1}(\overline{X}^{\mathrm{tame}})
over the entire Frobenius orbit.

Remark 1.14
.

Our proof of Theorem
1.12
gives a much stronger result. We completely determine
the slopes, which depend on certain monodromy invariants. See
4.2
for more details.

Example 1.15
.

Let
\overline{X}=\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{1}
and let
S=P_{1},\dots,P_{2n}
. Let
\psi
be a quadratic character
ramified at each
P_{i}
. Let
X_{\infty}/X
be a
(1)_{P\in S}
-stable tower. Such a tower may be
obtained using Artin-Schreier-Witt theory (§
2
) from a rational function with simple poles at
P_{1},\dots,P_{2n}
. Then
X^{\mathrm{tame}}
is a hyperelliptic curve
of genus
n-1
, which we assume is ordinary. Without being too precise, we obtain the decomposition

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ind}^{X^{\mathrm{tame}}}_{X}(X_{\infty}/X)

\displaystyle=X_{\infty}/X\oplus(\psi\otimes X_{\infty}/X).

Let
\chi:\mathbb{Z}_{p}\to\mathbb{C}_{p}^{\times}
be a finite character and let
\rho_{\chi}^{\mathrm{tame}}
denote the pullback of
\rho_{\chi}
along
f
. There is a factorization

L(\rho_{\chi}^{\mathrm{tame}},s)=L(\rho_{\chi},s)L(\psi\otimes\rho_{\chi},s)
. The slopes of the
L
-functions break up as follows:

\displaystyle\NP(\rho_{\chi}^{\mathrm{tame}})

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\{0,\dots,0\}}_{3n-2}\sqcup\underbrace{\{e_{\chi},\dots,e_{\chi}\}}_{3n-2}\sqcup\bigsqcup_{i=0}^{2n}\Bigg\{\frac{p-1}{2},2\frac{(p-1)}{2},\dots,(2p^{m_{\chi}-1}-1)\frac{(p-1)}{2}\Bigg\},

\displaystyle\ \NP(\rho_{\chi})

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\{0,\dots,0\}}_{2n-1}\sqcup\underbrace{\{e_{\chi},\dots,e_{\chi}\}}_{2n-1}\sqcup\bigsqcup_{i=0}^{2n}\Bigg\{2\frac{(p-1)}{2},4\frac{(p-1)}{2},\dots,(2p^{m_{\chi}-1}-2)\frac{(p-1)}{2}\Bigg\},

\displaystyle\NP(\psi\otimes\rho_{\chi})

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\{0,\dots,0\}}_{n-1}\sqcup\underbrace{\{e_{\chi},\dots,e_{\chi}\}}_{n-1}\sqcup\bigsqcup_{i=0}^{2n}\Bigg\{\frac{p-1}{2},3\frac{(p-1)}{2},\dots,(2p^{m_{\chi}-1}-1)\frac{(p-1)}{2}\Bigg\}.

Let us point out that this decomposition can be deduced from Theorem
1.9
, without any reference to
[
11
]
. This is because Theorem
1.9
allows us to determine
\NP_{q}(\rho_{\chi}^{\mathrm{tame}})
and
\NP_{q}(\rho_{\chi})
. The “leftover” slopes
must be the slopes of the quadratic twist
\NP_{q}(\psi\otimes\chi)
.

1.2.3
Equicharacteristic Results

It is well known that the space
\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_{p},\mathbb{C}_{p})
may be identified with the open unit disk in
\mathbb{C}_{p}
(via the map
\chi\mapsto\pi_{\chi}
), or equivalently with the
\mathbb{C}_{p}
-valued points of
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket T\rrbracket)
. From this vantage point, it is natural to consider the “special” point, which can be thought of as compatifying the open disc.
The special point corresponds to an equicharacteristic character
\chi_{0}:\mathbb{Z}_{p}\to\mathbb{F}_{p}\llbracket T\rrbracket^{\times}
defined by

\chi_{0}(a)=(1+T)^{a}\in 1+T\mathbb{F}_{p}\llbracket T\rrbracket.

As before, we define the composite character.

\rho_{0}:\pi_{1}(X)\to\mathbb{Z}_{p}\xrightarrow{\chi_{0}}\mathbb{F}_{p}\llbracket T\rrbracket^{\times}.

We may think of
\rho_{0}
as the ‘limit’ of characters tending towards the boundary of the open unit disc.

Definition 1.16
.

The
equicharacterstic
L
-function
of
X_{\infty}/X
is the Artin
L
-function
L(\rho_{0},s)\in 1+s\mathbb{F}_{q}\llbracket T\rrbracket\llbracket s\rrbracket

associated to
\rho_{0}
.

The equicharacteristic
L
-function describes the limiting behavior of the
L(\rho_{\chi},s)
for finite
\chi
as
m_{\chi}\to\infty
. As an example of the well behaved nature of this
L
-function, we have:

Theorem 1.17
.

Suppose that
X_{\infty}/X
has strictly stable monodromy. Then
L(\rho_{0},s)
is a
T
-adic entire function on
\mathbb{F}_{q}(\!(T)\!)
.

In light of Theorem
1.17
, it is convenient for us to put the
L
-functions
L(\rho_{0},s)
and
L(\rho_{\chi},s)
for finite
\chi
on equal footing. Let
T_{q}=T^{v_{p}(q)}
and define the
normalized Newton polygon
of
\rho_{0}
to be the infinite convex polygon

\NP(\rho_{0})=\NP_{T_{q}}L(\rho_{0},s).

We can now state the equicharacteristic analogues of our main theorems:

Theorem 1.18
.

Assume that
\overline{X}
is ordinary. Let
X_{\infty}/X
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-tower with
\bm{\delta}
-stable monodromy. Then:

1.

The Newton polygon
\NP(\rho_{0})
lies above
\HP(X_{\infty}/X)
.

2.

Let
d
be as in Theorem
1.8
. For all
n>0
,
\NP(\rho_{0})
and
\HP(X_{\infty}/X)
coincide on the interval (
4
).

3.

Suppose that
m_{0}=0
and that
p\equiv 1\pmod{\delta_{P}}
for all
P\in S
. Then
\NP(\rho_{0})=\HP(X_{\infty}/X)
.

Corollary 1.19
.

In the setting of Theorem
1.17
, the
L
-function
L(\rho_{0},s)
factors over
\mathbb{F}_{p}(\!(T)\!)
as a product of polynomials of degree
\leq d
.

The Equicharacteristic Riemann hypothesis

In light of Corollary
1.19
, it seems natural to ask if there is a bound on the denominators of the Newton slopes of
L(\rho_{0},s)
. This question is in the spirit of Goss’s (wide open) Riemann hypothesis
for Drinfeld modules. Thus we are led to make the following definition:

Definition 1.20
.

We say that a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-tower
X_{\infty}/X
satisfies the
equicharacteristic Riemann hypothesis
if

L(\rho_{0},s)
is a
T
-adic entire function whose zeros are all contained in a finite extension of
\mathbb{F}_{p}(\!(T)\!)
.

It is not clear to us which towers should satisfy the equicharacteristic Riemann hypothesis. A hopeful guess is that
it holds for all towers with strictly stable monodromy. From
Theorem
1.18
we can establish the Riemann hypothesis for
certain towers.

Theorem 1.21
.

Assume that
\overline{X}
is ordinary. Let
X_{\infty}/X
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-tower with
\bm{\delta}
-stable monodromy. Assume one of the two conditions hold:

1.

\delta_{P}\in\mathbb{Z}
for all
P\in S
and
\sum\delta_{P}<p
.

2.

\delta_{P}\in\mathbb{Z}
for all
P\in S
and
p\equiv 1\pmod{\delta_{P}}
for all
P\in S
.

Then the equicharacteristic Riemann hypothesis holds for
X_{\infty}/X
.

To deduce the Riemann hypothesis from the first condition, use Corollary
1.19
in conjunction with the observation that there are finitely many extensions of
\mathbb{F}_{q}(\!(T)\!)
with degree less than
p
. To deduce the Riemann hypothesis from the second condition, use the third part of Theorem
1.18
.

1.3
Outline of Proof

Artin-Schreier-Witt Theory and Splitting Functions

The purpose of §
2
is to upgrade some classical constructions from the theory of exponential sums on tori to more general varieties.
In §
2.1
-
2.2
we recall the basic notions of Artin-Schreier-Witt theory, and give an alternative formulation in terms of the flat liftings used in
[
13
]
. Our reason for working with flat liftings is that they lead to a very general theory of Dwork splitting functions. Such splitting functions are an integral part of the classical theory of exponential sums (see
[
1
]
and
[
15
]
), and can be constructed quite explicitly from the Artin-Hasse exponential series (as in
[
6
]
). However,
these classical splitting functions are inapplicable to the study of exponential sums on more general varieties. In §
2.3
we construct
splitting functions using a generalized Artin-Hasse exponential. This generalized
Artin-Hasse exponential is “compatible” with a given lifting of Frobenius (as opposed to
the classical Artin-Hasse exponential, which is compatible with the Frobenius
t\to t^{p}
). In particular, it allows us to explicitly define splitting functions over any
variety.
This approach to splitting functions appears to be genuinely new, and allows us to deduce, for example, that the
F
-crystal associated to
any
character of
\pi_{1}(X)
(not necessarily finite) factoring through a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-tower is free. This result is essential in order to apply the main theorems of
[
13
]
.

Local Towers and Global Extensions

In §
3.1
we study the growth conditions satisfied by splitting functions
associated to a
\delta
-stable tower
F_{\infty}/F
of local fields. Every such tower extends in a natural way to a
\delta
-stable tower
X_{\infty}^{\mathrm{ext}}/\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{1}
over the line, whose localization at
\infty
is
F_{\infty}/F
. Let
\rho^{\mathrm{ext}}:\pi_{q}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{1})\to\mathbb{Z}_{p}
be the corresponding map.
The purpose of §
3.2
is to study the family of characters
\rho_{\chi}^{\mathrm{ext}}
.
Using work of Kosters and Zhu in
[
9
]
, we know that
\HP(X_{\infty}^{\mathrm{ext}}/\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{1})
and
\NP(\rho_{\chi}^{\mathrm{ext}})

touch periodically when
\chi
is a finite character of sufficiently large order.
With a minimal amount of extra work, the theory of Kosters-Zhu may be extended to include the equicharacteristic character
\chi_{0}
(see Theorem
3.10
).
This periodic touching between Newton and Hodge polygons is necessary to verify a technical hypotheses needed to apply results from
[
13
]
(see Corollary
3.12
).

Applying Local-to-Global results

We are now in a position to apply the main results from
[
13
]
.
Assume that
\overline{X}
is ordinary and let
X_{\infty}/X
be
a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-tower with
\bm{\delta}
-stable monodromy, corresponding to
\rho:\pi_{1}(X)\to\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. Let
\chi
be either
a finite character or the equicharacteristic character.
For each
P\in S
we may localize to obtain a tower
F_{P,\infty}/F_{P}
, which extends as above to a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-tower
tower
X_{P,\infty}^{\mathrm{ext}}/\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{1}
over the line.

The main result of
[
13
]
roughly states that

\NP^{<e_{\chi}}(\rho_{\chi})
and
\HP^{<e_{\chi}}(X_{\infty}/X)
share a vertex
if and only if
\NP^{<e_{\chi}}(\rho^{\mathrm{ext}}_{P,\chi})
and
\HP^{<e_{\chi}}(X^{\mathrm{ext}}_{P,\infty}/\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{1})

share a corresponding vertex for each
P\in S
.
Using the
periodic touching between Newton and Hodge polygons from the previous paragraph,
we obtain periodic touching between
\NP^{<e_{\chi}}(\rho_{\chi})
and
\HP^{<e_{\chi}}(X_{\infty}/X)
.
This immediately allows us to deduce Theorem
1.8
. To establish Theorem
1.9
, we use a classical result on exponential sums (see e.g.
[
1
]
). This result says that
\NP^{<e_{\chi}}(\rho^{\mathrm{ext}}_{P,\chi})
and
\HP^{<e_{\chi}}(X^{\mathrm{ext}}_{P,\infty}/\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{1})

are equal when
\chi
has order
p
and the congruence conditions of Theorem
1.9
is satisfied. We then use the work of Kosters-Zhu (and our
equicharacteristic modification) to see that
\NP^{<e_{\chi}}(\rho^{\mathrm{ext}}_{P,\chi})
and
\HP^{<e_{\chi}}(X^{\mathrm{ext}}_{P,\infty}/\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{1})
agree for any finite
\chi
or for
\chi_{0}
.
Theorem
1.9

then follows from the main result of
[
13
]
.

1.4
Future Work

It would be interesting to understand what happens when one twists a
\bm{\delta}
-stable tower
X_{\infty}/X
by a motive
M
pure of weight
k
.
Our expectation is that
M\otimes X_{\infty}/X
exhibits slope uniformity in the interval
[0,k+1]
.
Perhaps a natural first step would be to consider
M
coming from an Artin representation. Indeed, let
Y\to X
be a Galois cover
and consider the pullback tower
Y_{\infty}/Y
. Then
Y_{\infty}/Y
will be
\bm{\delta}^{\prime}
-stable for some
\bm{\delta}^{\prime}=(\delta^{\prime}_{P})
that depends
on
\bm{\delta}
and the ramification of
Y\to X
. In particular,
Y_{\infty}/Y
is slope uniform by Theorem
1.5
.
We have a decomposition
\mathrm{Ind}^{Y}_{X}(Y_{\infty}/Y)=\bigoplus\psi\otimes X_{\infty}/X
, where the sum is taken over the irreducible representations of
\mathrm{Gal}(Y/X)
.
We expect that each
\psi\otimes X_{\infty}/X
is slope uniform as well.

Another interesting direction would be to explore other classes of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-towers. Our notion of
\bm{\delta}
-stable monodromy is natural condition from an analytic standpoint, but is
somewhat ad-hoc from a geometrical point of view.
Instead, one may consider geometric towers, i.e., towers coming from the relative
p
-adic étale cohomology of a smooth fibration
Z\to X
.
This includes the Igusa tower, where the equicharacteristic
L
-function is closely related to the space of
\mathbb{F}_{q}\llbracket T\rrbracket
-valued modular forms.
In general, geometric towers are not
\bm{\delta}
-stable. However, as demonstrated by the first author in
[
10
]
, the
monodromy of a geometric towers exhibits a similar type of stability. Daqing Wan has conjectured that geometric towers should be slope stable
in the appropriate sense, but essentially nothing is known in this direction.

Finally, we believe that for a sufficiently well-behaved tower
X_{\infty}/X
, geometric data should be
encoded in the various
L
-functions under consideration. For example, we hope to establish a combinatorial formula for the
a
-numbers of each
\overline{X}_{n}
, depending on the Newton polygon of the equicharacteristic
L
-function and
the ramification breaks of the tower. This is an ongoing project with Jeremy Booher and Bryden Cais, motivated by computations and conjectures formulated by Booher and Cais in
[
2
]
.
Our hope is that this formula holds for all
\bm{\delta}
-stable towers over any ordinary curve. It seems likely that the gluing methods
developed in the prequel article
[
13
]
will allow one to bootstrap results of this form from the special case
X=\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{1}
.
More generally, we hope that for well behaved towers the
T
-adic
L
-function defined Liu-Wan
[
14
]
should contain information
on invariants of the Dieudonné module associated to
\mathrm{Jac}(X_{n})
.

2.1
Artin-Schreier-Witt Theory via Witt Vectors

Let
A
be a ring and let
W(A)
denote the ring of
p
-typical Witt vectors of
A
. Recall that as sets we have
W(A)=A^{\mathbb{N}}
. The ring structure on
W(A)
is characterized uniquely by the requirement that, for each
i\geq 0
, the
ghost map

w_{i}:W(A)\to A
defined by

w_{i}(a_{0},a_{1},...)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}p^{j}a_{j}^{p^{i-j}}

is a functorial homomorphism of rings (
[
7
]
, 15.3.10).
Let
X
be an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-scheme. Let
X_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}
denote the small étale site of
X
. For each
n\geq 0
, the assignment
U\mapsto W_{n}(\mathcal{O}_{U})
defines a sheaf of rings on
X_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}
, which we denote by
\tilde{W}_{n}
. We define
\tilde{W}=\varprojlim_{n}\tilde{W}_{n}
. We equip
\tilde{W}
with the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module endomorphism

\wp=F-\mathrm{id},

where
F
denotes the Frobenius endomorphism on
\tilde{W}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
X
be an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-scheme. For each
n\geq 0
, there is an exact sequence of étale sheaves of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-modules

0\to W_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{p})\to\tilde{W}_{n}\xrightarrow{\wp}\tilde{W}_{n}\to 0.

(5)

Proof.

This is well known for the case
n=0
(see e.g.
[
5
]
). The general case follows from an inductive lifting argument.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
X
be an affine scheme over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Then for all
n\geq 0
,
H^{1}(X_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}},\tilde{W}_{n})=0
.

Proof.

Noting that
\tilde{W}_{0}=\mathbb{G}_{a}
, by Hilbert’s theorem 90
[
17
, Remark 9.8]
we have

H^{1}(X_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}},\tilde{W}_{0})=H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})=0,

since
X
is affine. The general case follows from an inductive lifting argument as above.
∎

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
X=\mathrm{Spec}(\overline{A})
be an affine scheme over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. There is a natural isomorphism of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-modules

W(\overline{A})/\wp W(\overline{A})\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{Hom}(\pi_{1}(X),\mathbb{Z}_{p}).

Proof.

By Lemma
2.1
, for each
n\geq 0
we have a long exact sequence

0\to W_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{p})\to W_{n}(\overline{A})\xrightarrow{\wp}W_{n}(\overline{A})\to H^{1}(X_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}},W_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{p}))\to H^{1}(X_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}},\tilde{W}_{n})\to\cdots.

Note that
H^{1}(X_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}},W_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{p}))=\mathrm{Hom}(\pi_{1}(X),W_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{p}))
. By Lemma
2.2
, the map

W_{n}(\overline{A})/\wp W_{n}(\overline{A})\to\mathrm{Hom}(\pi_{1}(X),W_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{p}))

is an isomorphism. The theorem follows by passing to the inverse limit.
∎

.

2.3
Characters and Splitting Functions

In his proof of the rationality of the zeta function, Dwork uses the classical Artin-Hasse exponential to construct a “splitting function” for the
L
-functions of additive character sums over the torus
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{d}
. We will now use our notion of a flat lifting to construct general splitting functions over a smooth affine
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-scheme
X
.

Let
R
be a complete Noetherian local ring with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
and residue field
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Let
\chi:\mathbb{Z}_{p}\to R^{\times}
be a continuous character. We associate to
\chi
the parameter

\pi_{\chi}=\chi(1)-1\in\mathfrak{m}.

Note that for any
a\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, we have
\chi(a)=(1+\pi_{\chi})^{a}
and thus
\chi\mapsto\pi_{\chi}
gives a one-to-one correspondence between the set of
R
-valued characters of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and
\mathfrak{m}
. The functor sending
R\mapsto\mathfrak{m}
is representable, namely by the Iwasawa algebra
\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket T\rrbracket
. It follows that there is a universal
T
-adic character

\chi_{T}:\mathbb{Z}_{p}\to\Lambda^{\times}
, which corresponds to the topologically nilpotent element
T\in\Lambda
.

Let
X=\mathrm{Spec}(\overline{A})
be an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-scheme, and let
(A,\sigma)
be a flat lifting of
X
over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. For every character
\chi:\mathbb{Z}_{p}\to R^{\times}
as above, we define a flat lifting
(A_{\pi_{\chi}},\sigma)
over
R
, where

A_{\pi_{\chi}}=R\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}A,

and
\sigma:A_{\pi_{\chi}}\to A_{\pi_{\chi}}
is the
R
-linear lifting of Frobenius obtained by base change. For every closed point
x\in|X|
, let us write
R(x)=R\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}W(k(x))
. The Teichmüller map
\hat{x}
induces a Frobenius-compatible map

\hat{x}:A_{\pi_{\chi}}\to R(x).

Given
E\in A_{\pi_{\chi}}^{\infty}
, we will write
E(\hat{x})\in R(x)
for the “value” of
E
at
\hat{x}
.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
f\in A^{\infty}
. A
splitting function
for
f
at
\chi
is an element
E\in A_{\pi_{\chi}}^{\infty}

satisfying

(1+\pi_{\chi})^{\mathrm{Tr}_{k(x)/\mathbb{F}_{p}}f(\hat{x})}=\mathrm{N}_{k(x)/\mathbb{F}_{p}}E(\hat{x})

for all
x\in|X|
. Here, we identify the Galois group of
R(x)/R
with that of
k(x)/\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

Remark 2.10
.

Let
\rho:\pi_{1}(X)\to\mathbb{Z}_{p}
be the map corresponding to
f
by Artin-Schreier-Witt theory. Suppose that
R
is a discrete valuation ring. In the terminology of
[
13
, §5.1]
, a splitting function for
f
at
\chi
is a Frobenius structure for the unit-root
\sigma
-module over
A_{\pi_{\chi}}^{\infty}
corresponding to
\rho_{\chi}=\chi\circ\rho
. If
f
admits a splitting function at
\chi
, then this

-module is free of rank

.

Let us write
(A_{T},\sigma)
for the flat lifting corresponding to the

-adic character
\chi_{T}
. We claim that every
f\in A^{\infty}
admits a splitting function
E_{f}(T)\in A_{T}^{\infty}
at
\chi_{T}
. By specialization, we obtain a splitting function at every character
\chi:\mathbb{Z}_{p}\to R^{\times}
as above. Recall that the

is the power series

E(t)=\exp\left(\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{p^{i}}}{p^{i}}\right)=\prod_{p\nmid n}(1-t^{n})^{-\mu(n)/n}\in 1+t+t^{2}\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket t\rrbracket.

Here,
\mu
denotes the Möbius function.

Lemma 2.11

(
[
9
, Lemma 2.1]
)
.

For each
j\geq 0
,
E(t)
induces a bijection

. Moreover, for all
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and all
j\geq 0
we have

E(rT^{j}+T^{j+1}\Lambda)=1+rT^{j}+T^{j+1}\Lambda.

Definition 2.12
.

For each
j\geq 0
, we let
\tau_{j}=\tau_{j}(T)\in T\Lambda
be the unique series satisfying
E(\tau_{j}(T))=(1+T)^{p^{j}}
.

We are now ready to define our

-adic splitting functions. Recall from

, 17.2.7]
that there is a functorial embedding of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-modules

\displaystyle W(A)

\displaystyle\to 1+tA\llbracket t\rrbracket

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle\mapsto\prod_{j=0}^{\infty}E(a_{i}t^{p^{i}}).

Definition 2.13
.

For each
j\geq 0
, we define the

to be the composite

p^{j}A\xrightarrow{1/p^{j}}A\xrightarrow{D_{\sigma}}W(A)\to 1+tA\llbracket t\rrbracket\xrightarrow{t\mapsto\tau_{j}}1+\tau_{j}A\llbracket\tau_{j}\rrbracket\subseteq A_{\Lambda}^{\infty}.

Let us write
E_{f}^{j}=E_{f}^{j}(T)
for the

. By construction, the assignment

.

Lemma 2.14

(Dwork’s Splitting Lemma)
.

Let
f\in p^{j}A^{\infty}
. Then

is a splitting function for

at
\chi_{T}
.

Proof.

By functoriality, we have that

. Since

is perfect, we have a Teichmüller expansion

By

. Note that since

, we have

. But then

∎

, and let

\rho:G\to\mathbb{Z}_{p}.

(6)

For each

3.1

Our first goal is to characterize the stable monodromy condition (

) in terms of a suitable representative
\tilde{f}\in\mathcal{A}^{\infty}
of the tower

.

.

such that

. There is a unique

,

with

whenever

, and

as

.

be any basis for
\mathbb{Z}_{q}
as a

-module, and let

(see e.g.

, Example 2.4]

is

-torsion free, it follows from

, Lemma 2.3]
that the

as in Proposition

. Note that the

-adic valuation of

is independent of the choice of

. The

-adic growth of the coefficients

is closely connected to the monodromy of

:

, Proposition 3.3]
)
.

For all
j\geq 0
, let
v_{j}
denote the highest ramification break (in upper numbering) of the finite extension
F_{j}/F
. Then

v_{j}=\begin{cases}p^{j-1}\max\{kp^{-v_{p}(c_{k})}:v_{p}(c_{k})<j\}&\text{if }v_{p}(c_{k})<j\text{ for some }k\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}.

It follows from Proposition
3.2
that
F_{\infty}/F
has
\delta
-stable monodromy for
\delta\in\mathbb{Z}[\tfrac{1}{p}]
if and only if the sequence
kp^{-v_{p}(c_{k})}
attains its maximum at some
k_{0}
, and the maximal value is equal to
\delta
. We will now give another characterization which is more useful for making explicit estimates: For each
k\geq 0
, consider the Teichmüller expansion

c_{k}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}[c_{j,k}]p^{j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}.

(7)

The convergence condition on the
c_{k}
guarantees that we may write

\tilde{f}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}p^{j}F_{j}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}p^{j}\sum_{k=0}^{d_{j}}[c_{j,k}]t^{-k},

(8)

where
F_{j}
is a polynomial of degree
d_{j}
in
t^{-1}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

The following are equivalent:

1.

F_{\infty}/F
has
\delta
-stable monodromy.

2.

The sequence
(d_{j}/p^{j})
has a maximum, with the maximal value equal to
\delta
.

Proof.

Observe that if
v_{p}(c_{k})>j
then we must have
d_{j}<k
. In other words, the sequence
(d_{j})
is bounded above by the increasing sequence

q_{j}=\max\{k:v_{p}(c_{k})\leq j\}.

Moreover, we have
d_{j}=q_{j}
whenever
j
is the minimal index at which
d_{j}
attains this value. It follows that
(d_{j}/p^{j})
has a maximum if and only if
(q_{j}/p^{j})
has a maximum, and that the two maxima agree. On the other hand, we have an upper bound

\displaystyle q_{j}

\displaystyle=p^{j}\max\{kp^{-j}:v_{p}(c_{k})<j+1\}

\displaystyle\leq p^{j}\max\{kp^{-v_{p}(c_{k})}:v_{p}(c_{k})<j+1\}

\displaystyle=v_{j+1},

with equality if and only if there exists some
k
such that
v_{p}(c_{k})=j
.

First assume that
F_{\infty}/F
has
\delta
-stable monodromy. Then the sequence
kp^{-v_{p}(c_{k})}
has maximal value
\delta
, so that
q_{j}\leq p^{j}\delta
for all
j
. Let
k_{0}
be the minimal value of
k
such that
kp^{-v_{p}(c_{k})}=\delta
, and let
j_{0}=v_{p}(c_{k_{0}})
. Then
q_{j_{0}}=\delta p^{j_{0}}
so that
q_{j}/p^{j}
attains its maximal value
\delta
when
j=j_{0}
.

Conversely, suppose that
(d_{j}/p^{j})
has a maximum with maximal value
\delta
. Let
j_{0}
be the smallest value of
j
such that
q^{j}/p^{j}=\delta
, and let
k_{0}=q_{j_{0}}=p^{j_{0}}\delta
. Then by the definition of
(q_{j})
we have
j_{0}=v_{p}(c_{k_{0}})
, so that
q_{j_{0}}=v_{j_{0}+1}
. To complete the proof, it suffices to show that for all
k>k_{0}
the value
kp^{-v_{p}(c_{k})}
is bounded by
\delta
. To see this, let
j=v_{p}(c_{k})
. Since
k>k_{0}
, we have
j\geq j_{0}
and since
v_{p}(c_{k})<j+1
we have

k\leq q_{j}\leq p^{j}\delta.

Finally, we see that

kp^{-v_{p}(c_{k})}\leq p^{j}\delta p^{-v_{p}(c_{k})}=\delta.

∎

Theorem 3.10
.

Suppose that
F_{\infty}/F
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-tower of local fields with
\delta
-stable monodromy. Let
m_{0}
be the smallest non-negative integer such that

. Let

1.

For some

For every

\NP^{<r}(\rho_{\chi})
and
\HP^{<r}(\delta)

Proof.

This theorem more or less follows from the theory developed in
[
9
]
. However,
[
9
]
ignores equicharacteristic characters. Thus, we believe it
is beneficial to outline their work and explain the extra observations needed to handle this case. In

, Kosters and Zhu construct a “very generic” discrete valuation ring

with valuation

(note that they denote this ring by

For each

\mathrm{ev}_{\chi}:R_{\delta}\to R_{\chi}

\displaystyle v_{\pi_{\chi}}(\mathrm{ev}_{\chi}(x))

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{l}\text{For some finite character }\\
\text{$\chi$ with $m_{\chi}>m_{0}$ such that}\\

\displaystyle\iff\begin{array}[]{l}\text{For every finite character }\\

\text{$v_{\pi_{\chi}}(\mathrm{ev}_{\chi}(x))=v_{\delta}(x)$ }\end{array}

This is Lemma 6.1 in
[

Kosters and Zhu then construct a generic “characteristic series”

3.2
).

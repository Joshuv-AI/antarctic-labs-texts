Coupling nonconforming and enriched Galerkin methods for robust discretization and fast solvers of poroelasticity problems
Jeonghun J. Lee 1 , Jacob Moore 2

Abstract
. In this paper we propose a new finite element discretization for the two-field formulation of poroelasticity which uses the elastic displacement and the pore pressure as primary variables. The main goal is to develop a numerical method with small problem sizes which still achieve key features such as parameter-robustness, local mass conservation, and robust preconditionor construction. For this we combine a nonconforming finite element and the interior over-stabilized enriched Galerkin methods with a suitable stabilization term. Robust a priori error estimates and parameter-robust preconditioner construction are proved, and numerical results illustrate our theoretical findings.

1.
Introduction

The poroelasticity theory is a theory on fluid flows in deformable porous media. The interactive behavior of fluid flows and elastic deformation results in a system of partial differential equations coupling elasticity and porous media flow equations.
Since the first poroelasticity models were derived by Biot
[
3

4

.
The main focus of more recent studies is developing
numerical methods which provide good features of numerical solutions. The features of interest are robustness of numerical solutions for nearly incompressible solid matrix and saturating fluids, local mass conservation, and fast solvers which show robust performance for realistic physical parameter ranges. Here we list some recent results

but we admit that this list of references is by no means complete.

The goal of this paper is to develop a numerical method for poroelasticity with small problem sizes such that numerical solutions satisfy the three features: 1) the error bounds are robust for a nearly incompressible elastic matrix, for arbitrarily small constrained specific storage coefficients, for time interval, 2) local mass conservation of numerical solutions holds, 3) preconditioners robust for mesh refinements as well as the physical parameter values are available.
For this goal we use a formulation which has the displacement of poroelastic matrix
\boldsymbol{u}
and the fluid pressure
p
as unknowns.

We remark that there are a couple of previous works on robust numerical methods using the two-field formulation. In
[
10
]
, primal hybridized discontinuous Galerkin methods are used for the displacement and pressure, and it is proved that the methods give robust numerical solutions for nearly incompressible materials. In
[

[

and the enriched Galerkin method for the Poisson equations in
[

are coupled. To the best of our knowledge, construction of parameter-robust preconditioning for these methods are not available. In contrast, we show that the numerical method in this paper provides parameter-robust preconditioner construction in addition to robust error estimates and local mass conservation.

For finite element discretization of the two variables we use the Mardal–Tai–Winther (MTW) element, a nonconforming

2.2.
The Biot’s consolidation model

Throughout this paper we limit our interest on quasistatic consolidation problems.
In our formulation of the model,
\boldsymbol{u}
is the displacement of porous media,
p
is the pore pressure,
\boldsymbol{f}
is the body force,
g
is the mass change rate of fluid. The governing equations of the quasistatic consolidation model with an isotropic elastic porous medium are

(1a)

\displaystyle-\operatorname{div}\left(2\mu\epsilon(\boldsymbol{u})+(\lambda\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{u}-\alpha p)\mathbb{I}\right)

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{f},

(1b)

\displaystyle s_{0}\dot{p}+\alpha\operatorname{div}\dot{\boldsymbol{u}}-\operatorname{div}(\underline{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}\nabla p)

\displaystyle=g,

where
\mu
and
\lambda
are the Lamé coefficients,
s_{0}\geq 0
is the constrained specific storage coefficient,
\underline{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}
is the hydraulic conductivity tensor,
\alpha>0
is the Biot–Willis coefficient which is close to 1, and
\mathbb{I}
is the identity matrix.

We assume that
\mu
is uniformly bounded above and below with positive constants. We assume
\lambda
has a uniformly positive lower bound but
\lambda
may not have a uniform upper bound and
\lambda=+\infty
corresponds to the incompressibility of the solid matrix.
We assume that there are constants
c_{0},c_{1}
such that

\displaystyle 0\leq c_{0}\leq s_{0}(x)\leq c_{1},\qquad x\in\Omega.

We remark that
s_{0}
is related to
\alpha
, the porosity
\phi
, and the bulk moduli of the solid and fluid.
Under the assumption that
\phi
is uniform with
0<\phi<\alpha
, if the solid is not incompressible, then
s_{0}\geq C/\lambda
holds with a constant
C
of scale 1. However,
s_{0}
may vanish on a subdomain if
\lambda=+\infty
on the subdomain and the fluid is incompressible. The hydraulic conductivity tensor
\underline{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}=\underline{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(x)
is positive definite with uniform lower and upper bounds
\kappa_{0},\kappa_{1}>0
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\kappa_{0}|\xi|^{2}\leq\xi^{T}\underline{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(x)\xi\leq\kappa_{1}|\xi|^{2},\qquad\forall\;0\not=\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},\quad\text{a.e.}\;x\in\Omega.

On details of deriving these equations from physical modelling, we refer to standard references on poroelasticity theory, e.g.,
[
39
]
.

For well-posedness of the problem, the equations (
1
) need appropriate boundary and initial conditions. We assume that there are partitions of
\partial\Omega
which are

\displaystyle\partial\Omega=\Gamma_{p}\cup\Gamma_{f},\qquad\partial\Omega=\Gamma_{d}\cup\Gamma_{t},\qquad|\Gamma_{d}|,|\Gamma_{p}|>0

where
|\Gamma|
is the
(n-1)
-dimensional Lebesgue measure of
\Gamma
. We also assume that boundary conditions are given as

(2)

\displaystyle p(t)=0\text{ on }\Gamma_{p},\quad-\underline{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}\nabla p(t)\cdot\boldsymbol{n}=0\text{ on }\Gamma_{f},\quad\boldsymbol{u}(t)=0\text{ on }\Gamma_{d},\quad\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(t)\boldsymbol{n}=0\text{ on }\Gamma_{t},

for all
t\in(0,T]
where
\boldsymbol{n}
is the outward unit normal vector field on
\partial\Omega
and
\underline{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}:=2\mu\epsilon(\boldsymbol{u})+(\lambda\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{u}-\alpha p)\mathbb{I}
, the Cauchy stress tensor.
Here we only consider the homogeneous boundary condition for simplicity but our method can be easily extended to problems with nonhomogeneous boundary conditions. We also assume that given initial data
p(0),\boldsymbol{u}(0)
and
\boldsymbol{f}(0)
satisfy the compatibility condition (
1a
).
Well-posedness of this system under these assumptions can be found in
[
36
]
.

3.
Finite element discretization

In this section we present a variational formulation and finite element discretization of (
1
), and prove auxiliary results for the stability and error analyses.

3.1.
Variational formulation of continuous problem

Let us define function spaces

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{V}=\left\{\boldsymbol{v}\in{H}^{1}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{n})\;:\;\boldsymbol{v}|_{\Gamma_{d}}=0\right\},\quad Q=\{q\in H^{1}(\Omega)\;:\;q|_{\Gamma_{p}}=0\}.

Then, by the integration by parts, we can show that a solution of (
1
),
(\boldsymbol{u}(t),p(t))
, satisfies the following variational formulation:

(3a)

\displaystyle\left(2\mu\epsilon(\boldsymbol{u}(t)),\epsilon(\boldsymbol{v})\right)_{\Omega}+\left(\lambda\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{u}(t),\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{v}\right)_{\Omega}-\left(\alpha p(t),\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{v}\right)_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=\left(\boldsymbol{f}(t),\boldsymbol{v}\right)_{\Omega},

(3b)

\displaystyle-\left(\alpha\operatorname{div}\dot{\boldsymbol{u}}(t),q\right)_{\Omega}-\left(s_{0}\dot{p}(t),q\right)_{\Omega}-\left(\underline{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}\nabla p(t),\nabla q\right)_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=-\left(g(t),q\right)_{\Omega}

for all
(\boldsymbol{v},q)\in\boldsymbol{V}\times Q
and for all
t\geq 0
.

For well-posedness we need initial conditions
(\boldsymbol{u}(0),p(0))\in\boldsymbol{V}\times Q
. By the compatibility assumption,

(4)

\displaystyle\left(2\mu\epsilon(\boldsymbol{u}(0)),\epsilon(\boldsymbol{v})\right)_{\Omega}+\left(\lambda\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{u}(0),\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{v}\right)_{\Omega}-\left(\alpha p(0),\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{v}\right)_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=\left(\boldsymbol{f}(0),\boldsymbol{v}\right)_{\Omega}

for all
\boldsymbol{v}\in\boldsymbol{V}
.

In summary the variational problem of (
1
) is the following: For given initial condition
(\boldsymbol{u}(0),p(0))\in\boldsymbol{V}\times Q
satisfying (
4
) find
(\boldsymbol{u},p)\in C^{1}(0,T;\boldsymbol{V})\times C^{1}(0,T;Q)
such that (
3
) holds for all
t\geq 0
.

3.2.
Finite elements for spatial discretization

In this subsection we introduce finite element methods for spatial discretizations of
\boldsymbol{u}

For discretization of

\displaystyle\text{all DOFs associated to the edges/faces in }\Gamma_{d}\text{ vanish}.

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{v}\|_{1,h}\geq C\|\epsilon(\boldsymbol{v})\|_{0}

(6)

\displaystyle\inf_{q\in\mathcal{P}_{0}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}\sup_{\boldsymbol{v}\in\boldsymbol{V}_{h}}\frac{\left(\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{v},q\right)_{\Omega}}{\|\boldsymbol{v}\|_{1,h}\|q\|_{0}}\geq C>0

holds with

independent of

.

For discretization of

[
18

,

\displaystyle Q_{h}^{c}:=\mathcal{P}_{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})\cap H^{1}(\Omega),\quad Q_{h}^{0}:=\mathcal{P}_{0}(\mathcal{T}_{h}),\quad Q_{h}:=Q_{h}^{c}+Q_{h}^{0}.

Let use define

and

by

(7)

\displaystyle a_{\boldsymbol{u}}\left(\boldsymbol{v},\tilde{\boldsymbol{v}}\right)

\displaystyle:=\left(2\mu\epsilon(\boldsymbol{v}),\epsilon(\tilde{\boldsymbol{v}})\right)+\left(\lambda\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{v},\operatorname{div}\tilde{\boldsymbol{v}}\right),\qquad\|\boldsymbol{v}\|_{\boldsymbol{V}_{h}}=\left(a_{\boldsymbol{u}}\left(\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{v}\right)\right)^{1/2}.

To define the bilinear form for

with the interior over-stabilized enriched Galerkin method, we define the jump and average operators for functions which have well-defined traces on edges/faces. More specifically, for
e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{\partial}

\displaystyle\left[\!\left[{q}\right]\!\right]|_{e}=q|_{e}\boldsymbol{n},\quad\left\{\!\!\left\{{q}\right\}\!\!\right\}|_{e}=q|_{e},\qquad\left[\!\left[{\boldsymbol{q}}\right]\!\right]|_{e}=\boldsymbol{q}|_{e}\cdot\boldsymbol{n},\quad\left\{\!\!\left\{{\boldsymbol{q}}\right\}\!\!\right\}|_{e}=\boldsymbol{q}|_{e}

where

and
\boldsymbol{q}
are scalar and
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
-vector-valued functions such that their traces on edge/face

are well-defined as integrable functions, and
\boldsymbol{n}
is the outward unit normal vector field on

. For
e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}
,
T_{+}
and

are the two elements such that

. If
q
is a scalar function on
T_{+}\cup T_{-}
, then we use
q^{\pm}
and
\nabla q^{\pm}
to denote the restrictions of
q
and
\nabla q
on
T_{+}
and
T_{-}
. We use
\boldsymbol{n}^{\pm}
to denote the unit outward normal vector fields of

and we again omit the restriction

if it is clear in context. Then the jumps and the averages of

and
\boldsymbol{q}
on

are defined by

\displaystyle\left[\!\left[{q}\right]\!\right]|_{e}

\displaystyle=q^{+}\boldsymbol{n}^{+}+q^{-}\boldsymbol{n}^{-},

\displaystyle\left\{\!\!\left\{{q}\right\}\!\!\right\}|_{e}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(q^{+}+q^{-}),

\displaystyle\left[\!\left[{\boldsymbol{q}}\right]\!\right]|_{e}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{q}^{+}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}^{+}+\boldsymbol{q}^{-}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}^{-},

\displaystyle\left\{\!\!\left\{{\boldsymbol{q}}\right\}\!\!\right\}|_{e}

If

is clear in context, we will use
\left[\!\left[{q}\right]\!\right]
instead of
\left[\!\left[{q}\right]\!\right]|_{e}
for the jump of

on

. The same simplification will apply to other quantities.

For

we use

and

to denote the components in

and
Q_{h}^{0}
which are unique up to a constant addition. For

(8)

+\gamma\underline{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}_{avg}\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}}\langle h_{e}^{-1-\beta}\left[\!\left[{q^{0}}\right]\!\right],\left[\!\left[{\tilde{q}^{0}}\right]\!\right]\rangle_{e}+\gamma\underline{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}_{avg}\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{\partial}}\langle h_{e}^{-1}(q^{c}+q^{0}),(\tilde{q}^{c}+\tilde{q}^{0})\rangle_{e}

with

and a sufficiently large

. This bilinear form is well-defined independent of the choices of

We also define a stabilization bilinear form

by

(9)

\displaystyle S(q,\tilde{q})=\gamma\left(\lambda^{-1}\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}}\langle h_{e}^{-1}\left[\!\left[{q}\right]\!\right],\left[\!\left[{\tilde{q}}\right]\!\right]\rangle_{e}+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}}\langle C_{1}h_{e}^{-1}\left[\!\left[{q}\right]\!\right],\left[\!\left[{\tilde{q}}\right]\!\right]\rangle_{e}\right)

\|\boldsymbol{v}\|_{\boldsymbol{V}_{h}}

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{v}\|_{\boldsymbol{V}_{h}}^{2}:=\int_{\Omega}\left(2\mu\epsilon(\boldsymbol{v}):\epsilon(\boldsymbol{v})+\lambda(\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{v})^{2}\right)\,dx

\displaystyle\|q\|_{0,\lambda^{-1}}^{2}

\displaystyle:=\int_{\Omega}\lambda^{-1}q^{2}\,dx,

\displaystyle:=\int_{\Omega}\underline{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}\nabla q^{c}\cdot\nabla q^{c}\,dx+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}}\gamma\underline{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}_{avg}h_{e}^{-1-\beta}\int_{e}\left[\!\left[{q}\right]\!\right]^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{\partial}}\gamma\underline{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}_{avg}h_{e}^{-1}\int_{e}\left[\!\left[{q^{c}}\right]\!\right]^{2}\,ds+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{\partial}}\gamma\underline{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}_{avg}h_{e}^{-1}\int_{e}\left[\!\left[{q^{0}}\right]\!\right]^{2}\,ds.

In the rest of this subsection we introduce a few results for stability of the discretization. These results will be used to prove well-posedness as well as the a priori error analysis of the discretized system.

We begin with defining an auxiliary finite element space
R_{h}
and a linear operator

. For a vertex

of
\mathcal{T}_{h}
let

be the characteristic function on the support of the shape function of an element in

associated to the vertex degree of freedom at

. Let

be the subspace of

defined by

\displaystyle R_{h}=\{q^{0}\in Q_{h}^{0}\,:\,q^{0}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}c_{i}\chi_{i},c_{i}\in{\mathbb{R}}\}

where

is the number of vertices of
\mathcal{T}_{h}
. We define
I_{h}:Q_{h}^{c}\rightarrow R_{h}
by

\displaystyle I_{h}q^{c}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}q^{c}(v_{i})\chi_{i}.

The following result can be obtained with the same argument in
[

, Lemma 1]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Given
\boldsymbol{v}\in\boldsymbol{V}_{h}
and

(12)

\displaystyle(n+1)\left(\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{v},q^{c}\right)_{\Omega}=\left(\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{v},I_{h}q^{c}\right)_{\Omega}.

Proof.

Since

we can see that (

, so the conclusion follows.
∎

The following norm equivalence result is also proved in

The inf-sup condition

Lemma 3.6

Let

Lemma 3.7

There exists

\displaystyle\inf_{(\tilde{\boldsymbol{v}},\tilde{q})\in\boldsymbol{V}_{h}\times Q_{h}}\sup_{(\boldsymbol{v},q)\in\boldsymbol{V}_{h}\times Q_{h}}\frac{B((\boldsymbol{v},q),(\tilde{\boldsymbol{v}},\tilde{q}))}{\|(\boldsymbol{v},q)\|_{\boldsymbol{V}_{h}\times Q_{h}}\|(\tilde{\boldsymbol{v}},\tilde{q})\|_{\boldsymbol{V}_{h}\times Q_{h}}}\geq C>0.

\displaystyle\frac{\left(\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{w},\tilde{q}\right)_{\Omega}}{\|\boldsymbol{w}\|_{\boldsymbol{V}_{h}}}\geq C_{1}\|\tilde{q}\|_{0,\lambda^{-1}}-C_{2}S(\tilde{q},\tilde{q})^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle{\left(\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{w},\tilde{q}\right)_{\Omega}}\geq C_{1}^{\prime}\|\tilde{q}\|_{0,\lambda^{-1}}^{2}-C_{2}^{\prime}S(\tilde{q},\tilde{q})

from (

Before we prove the a priori error analysis, we show that the proposed method needs considerably smaller number of degrees of freedom (DOFs).
For the discretization of
\boldsymbol{u}
the MTW element has minimal inter-element continuity for discrete Korn inequality (

24
]
), so we only need to compare the degrees of freedom for

when

is discretized by the dual form of mixed method with the lowest order Raviart-Thomas element.

Remark 3.8
.

For easy computation of DOFs we consider a triangulation of the unit cube domain
\Omega
such that
\Omega
is subdivided into
N\times N\times N
subcubes and each subcube is decomposed to 6 tetrahedra. Since each subcube has 6 internal faces, the numbers of vertices, faces, and tetrahedra are
(N+1)^{3}
,
6N^{2}(N+1)+6N^{3}
,
6N^{3}
. The total number of DOFs of mixed method discretization of
p
with the lowest order Raviart-Thomas element, is
6N^{2}(N+1)+6N^{3}+6N^{3}\sim 18N^{3}
. After hybridization (see, e.g.,
[
1
]
), only one face DOF is needed, so this can be reduced to
\sim 12N^{3}
. The lowest order enriched Galerkin method needs only one DOF for vertices and one DOF for tetrahedra, so the total number of DOFs is
(N+1)^{3}+6N^{3}\sim 7N^{3}
which still is considerably smaller than the one of hybridized mixed method. For hexahedral finite elements the numbers of vertices, faces, cubes are
(N+1)^{3}
,
3N^{2}(N+1)
,

. Therefore, the hybridized method with the lowest order Raviart-Thomas element needs approximately

DOFs whereas the IOS-EG method needs approximately

DOFs.

3.3.
Semidiscrete solutions and well-posedness

According to the variational formulation (
3
) we define the semidiscrete problem as follows: For compatible numerical initial data
(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}(0),p_{h}(0))\in\boldsymbol{V}_{h}\times Q_{h}
satisfying

(24)

\displaystyle a_{\boldsymbol{u}}\left(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}(0),\boldsymbol{v}\right)-\left(\alpha p_{h}(0),\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{v}\right)_{\Omega}=\left(\boldsymbol{f}(0),\boldsymbol{v}\right)_{\Omega}\qquad\forall\boldsymbol{v}\in\boldsymbol{V}_{h}

we find a semidiscrete solution
(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},p_{h}):C^{1}(0,\infty;\boldsymbol{V}_{h}\times Q_{h})
such that

(25a)

\displaystyle a_{\boldsymbol{u}}\left(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}(t),\boldsymbol{v}\right)-\left(\alpha p_{h}(t),\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{v}\right)_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=\left(\boldsymbol{f}(t),\boldsymbol{v}\right)_{\Omega},

(25b)

\displaystyle-\left(\alpha\operatorname{div}\dot{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}(t),q\right)_{\Omega}-S\left(\dot{p}_{h}(t),q\right)-\left(s_{0}\dot{p}_{h}(t),q\right)_{\Omega}-a_{p}\left(p_{h}(t),q\right)

\displaystyle=-\left(g(t),q\right)_{\Omega}

for all
(\boldsymbol{v},q)\in\boldsymbol{V}_{h}\times Q_{h}
and for all
t\geq 0
.

Theorem 3.9
.

Suppose that
(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}(0),p_{h}(0))\in\boldsymbol{V}_{h}\times Q_{h}
satisfies (
24
). Then, (
25
) has a unique solution.

Proof.

For well-posedness of (
25
) we consider a matrix realization of (
25
). We identify
\boldsymbol{V}_{h}
and
Q_{h}
to
{\mathbb{R}}^{N_{\boldsymbol{u}}}
and
{\mathbb{R}}^{N_{p}}
with certain global bases of
\boldsymbol{V}_{h}
and
Q_{h}
, and denote the vector realizations of
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}
,
p_{h}
and the
L^{2}
projections of
\boldsymbol{f},g
to
\boldsymbol{V}_{h},Q_{h}
by
\vec{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}
,
\vec{p}_{h}
,
\vec{\boldsymbol{f}}
,
\vec{g}
. Let

\displaystyle\mathbb{A}_{\boldsymbol{u}},\quad\mathbb{B},\quad\mathbb{S},\quad\mathbb{A}_{p}

be the matrices generated by the bilinear forms

\displaystyle a_{\boldsymbol{u}}(\boldsymbol{v},\tilde{\boldsymbol{v}}),\quad-\left(\alpha\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{v},q\right)_{\Omega},\quad S(q,\tilde{q})+\left(s_{0}q,\tilde{q}\right)_{\Omega},\quad a_{p}(q,\tilde{q})

for
\boldsymbol{v},\tilde{\boldsymbol{v}}\in\boldsymbol{V}_{h}
,
q,\tilde{q}\in Q_{h}
with the identifications
\boldsymbol{V}_{h}\leftrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{N_{\boldsymbol{u}}}
and
Q_{h}\leftrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{N_{p}}
. Then, (
25
) in matrix form is

\displaystyle\mathbb{A}_{\boldsymbol{u}}{\vec{\boldsymbol{u}}}_{h}(t)+\mathbb{B}^{T}\vec{p}_{h}(t)

\displaystyle=\vec{\boldsymbol{f}(t)},

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}\dot{\vec{\boldsymbol{u}}}_{h}(t)-\mathbb{S}\dot{\vec{p}}_{h}(t)-\mathbb{A}_{p}\vec{p}_{h}(t)

\displaystyle=-\vec{g}(t).

Since
\mathbb{A}_{\boldsymbol{u}}
is invertible
\vec{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}=-\mathbb{A}_{\boldsymbol{u}}^{-1}\mathbb{B}^{T}\vec{p}_{h}+\mathbb{A}_{\boldsymbol{u}}^{-1}\vec{\boldsymbol{f}}
from the the first equation. If we use this to the second equation, then we get

\displaystyle-\left(\mathbb{B}\mathbb{A}_{\boldsymbol{u}}^{-1}\mathbb{B}+\mathbb{S}\right)\dot{\vec{p}}_{h}(t)-\mathbb{A}_{p}\vec{p}_{h}(t)=-\mathbb{B}\mathbb{A}_{\boldsymbol{u}}^{-1}\dot{\vec{\boldsymbol{f}}}(t)-\vec{g}(t).

This is a standard form of system of linear ordinary differential equations, and this equation has a unique solution if
\mathbb{B}\mathbb{A}_{\boldsymbol{u}}^{-1}\mathbb{B}+\mathbb{S}
is invertible. By Lemma
3.7
the matrix

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{A}_{\boldsymbol{u}}&\mathbb{B}^{T}\\
\mathbb{B}&-\mathbb{S}\end{pmatrix}

is invertible, so its Schur complement
-(\mathbb{B}\mathbb{A}_{\boldsymbol{u}}^{-1}\mathbb{B}+\mathbb{S})
is invertible as well. Therefore, (
25
) has a unique solution.
∎

\displaystyle-\left(\alpha\operatorname{div}(\dot{e}_{\boldsymbol{u}}^{h}(t)+\dot{e}_{\boldsymbol{u}}^{I}(t)),q\right)_{\Omega}-S(\dot{e}_{p}^{h}(t)+\dot{e}_{p}^{I}(t),q)

(31b)

\displaystyle-\left(s_{0}(\dot{e}_{p}^{h}(t)+\dot{e}_{p}^{I}(t)),q\right)_{\Omega}-a_{p}\left(e_{p}^{h}(t),q\right)

\displaystyle=0

for
(\boldsymbol{v},q)\in\boldsymbol{V}_{h}\times Q_{h}
.
For our error analysis take
\boldsymbol{v}=\dot{e}_{\boldsymbol{u}}^{h}(t)
and
q=-e_{p}^{h}(t)
and add (
31a
) and (
31b
) to obtain the following:

(32)

a_{\boldsymbol{u}}\left(e_{\boldsymbol{u}}^{h}(t),\dot{e}_{\boldsymbol{u}}^{h}(t)\right)-\left(\alpha e_{p}^{I}(t),\operatorname{div}\dot{e}_{\boldsymbol{u}}^{h}(t)\right)_{\Omega}\\
+\left(\alpha\operatorname{div}\dot{e}_{\boldsymbol{u}}^{I}(t),e_{p}^{h}(t)\right)_{\Omega}+S\left(\dot{e}_{p}^{h}(t)+\dot{e}_{p}^{I}(t),e_{p}^{h}(t)\right)\\
+\left(s_{0}(\dot{e}_{p}^{h}(t)+\dot{e}_{p}^{I}(t)),e_{p}^{h}(t)\right)_{\Omega}+a_{p}\left(e_{p}^{h}(t),e_{p}^{h}(t)\right)=0.

We can rewrite this as

(33)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}a_{\boldsymbol{u}}\left(e_{\boldsymbol{u}}^{h}(t),e_{\boldsymbol{u}}^{h}(t)\right)+\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left(s_{0}e_{p}^{h}(t),e_{p}^{h}(t)\right)_{\Omega}\\
+a_{p}\left(e_{p}^{h}(t),e_{p}^{h}(t)\right)+\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}S\left(e_{p}^{h}(t),e_{p}^{h}(t)\right)\\
=\left(\alpha e_{p}^{I}(t),\operatorname{div}\dot{e}_{\boldsymbol{u}}^{h}(t)\right)_{\Omega}-\left(\alpha\operatorname{div}\dot{e}_{\boldsymbol{u}}^{I}(t),e_{p}^{h}(t)\right)_{\Omega}-S\left(\dot{e}_{p}^{I}(t),e_{p}^{h}(t)\right).

Let us define
X(t)\geq 0
by

\displaystyle X(t)^{2}:=a_{\boldsymbol{u}}\left(e_{\boldsymbol{u}}^{h}(t),e_{\boldsymbol{u}}^{h}(t)\right)+(s_{0}e_{p}^{h}(t),e_{p}^{h}(t))_{\Omega}+S\left(e_{p}^{h}(t),e_{p}^{h}(t)\right).

For the proof of (
28
) we first prove

(34)

X(t)\leq X(0)+C\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\max_{0\leq s\leq t}\|e_{p}^{I}(s)\|_{0}\\
+C\left(\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\|\dot{e}_{p}^{I}(s)\|_{0}\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}\|\operatorname{div}\dot{e}_{\boldsymbol{u}}^{I}(s)\|_{0}\,ds\right)

for any
t>0
.

To prove it, we integrate (
33
) from 0 to
t
and get

(35)

\frac{1}{2}X(t)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}X(0)^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}a_{p}\left(e_{p}^{h}(s),e_{p}^{h}(s)\right)\,ds\\
=\int_{0}^{t}\left(\alpha e_{p}^{I}(s),\operatorname{div}\dot{e}_{\boldsymbol{u}}^{h}(s)\right)_{\Omega}\,ds-\int_{0}^{t}\left(\alpha\operatorname{div}\dot{e}_{p}^{I}(s),e_{p}^{h}(s)\right)_{\Omega}\,ds-S\left(\dot{e}_{p}^{I}(t),e_{p}^{h}(t)\right).

By the integration by parts in
t

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\left(\alpha e_{p}^{I}(s),\operatorname{div}\dot{e}_{\boldsymbol{u}}^{h}(s)\right)_{\Omega}\,ds

\displaystyle=\left(\alpha e_{p}^{I}(t),\operatorname{div}e_{\boldsymbol{u}}^{h}(t)\right)_{\Omega}-\left(\alpha e_{p}^{I}(0),\operatorname{div}e_{\boldsymbol{u}}^{h}(0)\right)_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{0}^{t}\left(\alpha\dot{e}_{p}^{I}(s),\operatorname{div}e_{\boldsymbol{u}}^{h}(s)\right)_{\Omega}\,ds.

Plugging this into (

) yields

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}X(t)^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}a_{p}\left(e_{p}^{h}(s),e_{p}^{h}(s)\right)\,ds

(36)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}X(0)^{2}+\left(\alpha e_{p}^{I}(t),\operatorname{div}e_{\boldsymbol{u}}^{h}(t)\right)_{\Omega}-\left(\alpha e_{p}^{I}(0),\operatorname{div}e_{\boldsymbol{u}}^{h}(0)\right)_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{0}^{T}\left(\alpha\dot{e}_{p}^{I}(s),\operatorname{div}e_{\boldsymbol{u}}^{h}(s)\right)_{\Omega}\,ds-\int_{0}^{T}\left(\alpha\operatorname{div}\dot{e}_{p}^{I}(s),e_{p}^{h}(s)\right)_{\Omega}\,ds

\displaystyle\quad-S\left(\dot{e}_{p}^{I}(t),e_{p}^{h}(t)\right).

We now prove (
34
) assuming that

(37)

\displaystyle X(t)=\max_{0\leq s\leq t}X(s).

With this assumption we can bound some terms in (
36
) as follows:

(38)

\displaystyle\left|\left(\alpha e_{p}^{I}(t),\operatorname{div}e_{\boldsymbol{u}}^{h}(t)\right)_{\Omega}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|e_{p}^{I}(t)\|_{0}X(t),

(39)

\displaystyle\left|\left(\alpha e_{p}^{I}(0),\operatorname{div}e_{\boldsymbol{u}}^{h}(0)\right)_{\Omega}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|e_{p}^{I}(0)\|_{0}X(0),

(40)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{t}\left(\alpha\dot{e}_{p}^{I}(s),\operatorname{div}e_{\boldsymbol{u}}^{h}(s)\right)_{\Omega}\,ds\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\|\dot{e}_{p}^{I}(s)\|_{0}\,dsX(t),

(41)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{t}\left(\alpha\operatorname{div}\dot{e}_{p}^{I}(s),e_{p}^{h}(s)\right)_{\Omega}\,ds\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{T}\|\operatorname{div}\dot{e}_{\boldsymbol{u}}^{I}(s)\|_{0}\,dsX(t).

Plugging these into (
36
), we derive

X(t)^{2}+2\int_{0}^{t}a_{p}\left(e_{p}^{h}(s),e_{p}^{h}(s)\right)\,ds\leq X(0)^{2}+C\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|e_{p}^{I}(0)\|_{0}X(0)\\
+C\left(\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|e_{p}^{I}(t)\|_{0}+\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\|\dot{e}_{p}^{I}(s)\|_{0}\,ds+\int_{0}^{T}\|\operatorname{div}\dot{e}_{\boldsymbol{u}}^{I}(s)\|_{0}\,ds\right)X(t).

If we divide by
X(t)
, then we can obtain (
34
). For general
t
such that (
37
) is not true, note that there exists
0\leq t_{M}<t
such that

\displaystyle X(t_{M})=\max_{0\leq s\leq t_{M}}X(s),\qquad X(t)<X(t_{M}).

By the same argument as before for

we can obtain

(42)

X(t_{M})\leq X(0)+C\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|e_{p}^{I}(0)\|_{0}\\
+C\left(\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|e_{p}^{I}(t_{M})\|_{0}+\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{t_{M}}\|\dot{e}_{p}^{I}(s)\|_{0}\,ds+\int_{0}^{t_{M}}\|\operatorname{div}\dot{e}_{\boldsymbol{u}}^{I}(s)\|_{0}\,ds\right).

We can now derive (
34
) by

\displaystyle X(t)<X(t_{M}),\qquad\max_{0\leq s\leq t_{M}}\|e_{p}^{I}(s)\|_{0}\leq\max_{0\leq s\leq t}\|e_{p}^{I}(t)\|_{0},

and

\lambda^{-1}\int_{0}^{t_{M}}\|\dot{e}_{p}^{I}(s)\|_{0}+\int_{0}^{t_{M}}\|\operatorname{div}\dot{e}_{\boldsymbol{u}}^{I}(s)\|_{0}\,ds\\
\leq\lambda^{-1}\int_{0}^{t}\|\dot{e}_{p}^{I}(s)\|_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\|\operatorname{div}\dot{e}_{\boldsymbol{u}}^{I}(s)\|_{0}\,ds.

Then, (
28
) follows by the triangle inequality, (
34
), (
27
), and the interpolation error bounds (
26
).
∎

5.
Preconditioners for fast solver algorithms

In this section we present an abstract form of preconditioners for our discretization of poroelasticity equations by adopting the operator preconditioning framework in
[
25
]
.

After the Crank-Nicolson time discretization we need to solve the static equation

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}((\boldsymbol{u}^{n+1},p^{n+1}),({\boldsymbol{v}},{q}))=\mathcal{F}^{n+1}({\boldsymbol{v}},{q}),\qquad({\boldsymbol{v}},{q})\in\boldsymbol{V}_{h}\times Q_{h}

where
\mathcal{F}^{n+1}
is determined by the previous time step solution
(\boldsymbol{u}^{n},p^{n})
and the right-hand side terms, and
\mathcal{B}:(\boldsymbol{V}_{h}\times Q_{h})\times(\boldsymbol{V}_{h}\times Q_{h})\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}((\boldsymbol{v},q),(\tilde{\boldsymbol{v}},\tilde{q}))

\displaystyle=a_{\boldsymbol{u}}(\boldsymbol{v},\tilde{\boldsymbol{v}})-\left(\alpha q,\operatorname{div}\tilde{\boldsymbol{v}}\right)_{\Omega}-\left(\alpha\tilde{q},\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{v}\right)_{\Omega}-\left(s_{0}q,\tilde{q}\right)_{\Omega}-S\left(q,\tilde{q}\right)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\Delta t}{2}a_{p}(q,\tilde{q}).

Let
\tilde{Q}_{h}
be the space
Q_{h}
with the norm

\displaystyle\|q\|_{\tilde{Q}_{h}}^{2}=\|q^{c}\|_{0,\lambda^{-1}}^{2}+\|q^{c}\|_{0,s_{0}}^{2}+\|q^{0}\|_{0,\lambda^{-1}}^{2}+\|q^{0}\|_{0,s_{0}}^{2}+S(q^{0},\tilde{q}^{0})+\frac{\Delta t}{2}\|q\|_{h}^{2}.

Where
q^{0},\tilde{q}^{0}
are mean-value zero on
\Omega
. For simplicity let us define
\|(\boldsymbol{v},q)\|_{\mathcal{X}}
by

\displaystyle\|(\boldsymbol{v},q)\|_{\mathcal{X}}^{2}=\|\boldsymbol{v}\|_{\boldsymbol{V}_{h}}^{2}+\|q\|_{\tilde{Q}_{h}}^{2}.

We will prove that the linear operator
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{B}}:\boldsymbol{V}_{h}\times\tilde{Q}_{h}\rightarrow(\boldsymbol{V}_{h}\times\tilde{Q}_{h})^{*}
defined by

(43)

\displaystyle\left<\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{B}}(\boldsymbol{v},q),(\tilde{\boldsymbol{v}},\tilde{q})\right>_{\left((\boldsymbol{V}_{h}\times\tilde{Q}_{h})^{*},\boldsymbol{V}_{h}\times\tilde{Q}_{h}\right)}:=\mathcal{B}((\boldsymbol{v},q),(\tilde{\boldsymbol{v}},\tilde{q})),

is a bounded isomorphism from
\boldsymbol{V}_{h}\times\tilde{Q}_{h}
to the dual space
(\boldsymbol{V}_{h}\times\tilde{Q}_{h})^{*}
. Then, a parameter-robust preconditioner can be constructed as the inverse of the block diagonal operator

(44)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{P}_{\boldsymbol{V}_{h}}&0\\
0&\mathcal{P}_{\tilde{Q}_{h}}\end{pmatrix}

such that
\mathcal{P}_{\boldsymbol{V}_{h}}:\boldsymbol{V}_{h}\rightarrow\boldsymbol{V}_{h}^{*}
and
\mathcal{P}_{\tilde{Q}_{h}}:\tilde{Q}_{h}\rightarrow\tilde{Q}_{h}^{*}
are the bounded linear operators defined by

\displaystyle\left<\mathcal{P}_{\boldsymbol{V}_{h}}\boldsymbol{v},\tilde{\boldsymbol{v}}\right>_{(\boldsymbol{V}_{h}^{*},\boldsymbol{V}_{h})}

\displaystyle:=a_{\boldsymbol{u}}(\boldsymbol{v},\tilde{\boldsymbol{v}}),

\displaystyle\left<\mathcal{P}_{\tilde{Q}_{h}}q,\tilde{q}\right>_{(\tilde{Q}_{h}^{*},\tilde{Q}_{h})}

\displaystyle:=\left(s_{0}q^{c},\tilde{q}^{c}\right)_{\Omega}+\left(\lambda^{-1}q^{c},\tilde{q}^{c}\right)_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\Delta t}{2}\left(\int_{\Omega}\underline{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}\nabla q^{c}\cdot\nabla q^{c}\,dx+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{\partial}}\gamma h_{e}^{-1}\left<\left[\!\left[{q^{c}}\right]\!\right],\left[\!\left[{\tilde{q}^{c}}\right]\!\right]\right>_{e}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\left(s_{0}q^{0},\tilde{q}^{0}\right)_{\Omega}+\left(\lambda^{-1}q^{0},\tilde{q}^{0}\right)_{\Omega}+S(q^{0},\tilde{q}^{0})

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\Delta t}{2}\left(\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}}\gamma h_{e}^{-1-\beta}\left<\left[\!\left[{q^{0}}\right]\!\right],\left[\!\left[{\tilde{q}^{0}}\right]\!\right]\right>_{e}+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{\partial}}\gamma h_{e}^{-1}\left<\left[\!\left[{q^{0}}\right]\!\right],\left[\!\left[{\tilde{q}^{0}}\right]\!\right]\right>_{e}\right).

If
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{B}}:\boldsymbol{V}_{h}\times\tilde{Q}_{h}\rightarrow(\boldsymbol{V}_{h}\times\tilde{Q}_{h})^{*}
is an isomorphism, then a preconditioner of the form (
44
) is expected to give parameter-robust preconditioners based on norm equivalence (cf.
[
25
]
).

By the definition (
43
),
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{B}}:\boldsymbol{V}_{h}\times\tilde{Q}_{h}\rightarrow(\boldsymbol{V}_{h}\times\tilde{Q}_{h})^{*}
is a bounded isomorphism independent of
h,\Delta t,\lambda
if
\mathcal{B}
is a bounded bilinear form on
\boldsymbol{V}_{h}\times\tilde{Q}_{h}
, and the following inf-sup condition holds: There exists
C>0
independent of
h
,
\lambda
,
\Delta t
such that

(45)

\displaystyle\inf_{(\tilde{\boldsymbol{v}},\tilde{q})\in\boldsymbol{V}_{h}\times Q_{h}}\sup_{(\boldsymbol{v},q)\in\boldsymbol{V}_{h}\times Q_{h}}\frac{\mathcal{B}((\boldsymbol{v},q),(\tilde{\boldsymbol{v}},\tilde{q}))}{\|(\boldsymbol{v},q)\|_{\mathcal{X}}\|(\tilde{\boldsymbol{v}},\tilde{q})\|_{\mathcal{X}}}\geq C>0.

The main result in this section is proving the inf-sup condition (
45
). As a consequence,
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{B}}
is an isomorphism, so preconditioners of the form (
44
) give robust preconditioners.

Before we prove this we state a necessary result which is proved in
[
18
]
.

Lemma 5.1
.

For given
\tilde{q}=\tilde{q}^{c}+\tilde{q}^{0}
, one can find
r^{0}\in Q_{h}^{0}
satisfying

(46)

\displaystyle\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}}\left<h_{e}^{-1}\left[\!\left[{r^{0}}\right]\!\right],\left[\!\left[{r^{0}}\right]\!\right]\right>_{e}\leq C_{r}\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}}\left<h_{e}^{-1}\left[\!\left[{\tilde{q}^{0}}\right]\!\right],\left[\!\left[{\tilde{q}^{0}}\right]\!\right]\right>_{e},

(47)

\displaystyle\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{\partial}}\left<h_{e}^{-1}\left[\!\left[{r^{0}}\right]\!\right],\left[\!\left[{r^{0}}\right]\!\right]\right>_{e}+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}}\left<h_{e}^{-1-\beta}\left[\!\left[{r^{0}}\right]\!\right],\left[\!\left[{r^{0}}\right]\!\right]\right>_{e}

\displaystyle\quad\leq C_{r}\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}}\left<h_{e}^{-1-\beta}\left[\!\left[{\tilde{q}^{0}}\right]\!\right],\left[\!\left[{\tilde{q}^{0}}\right]\!\right]\right>_{e},

\displaystyle\|r^{0}\|_{0}\leq C_{r}\|\tilde{q}^{0}\|_{0},

for some

with

C_{EG}^{\prime}

.

The inf-sup condition (

\displaystyle\geq C_{6}\|(\tilde{\boldsymbol{v}},\tilde{q})\|_{\mathcal{X}}^{2}

for some constants
C_{5},C_{6}>0
independent of
h
,
\lambda
,
\Delta t
.

Suppose that
(0,0)\not=(\tilde{\boldsymbol{v}},\tilde{q})\in\boldsymbol{V}_{h}\times Q_{h}

By Lemma
5.1

r^{0}\in Q_{h}^{0}

\Delta t

we obtain

\mathcal{B}((\boldsymbol{v},q),(\tilde{\boldsymbol{v}},\tilde{q}))\\
\geq\frac{1}{2}\|\tilde{\boldsymbol{v}}\|_{\boldsymbol{V}_{h}}^{2}+\frac{\delta_{1}C_{1}^{\prime\prime}}{2}\|\tilde{q}^{c}\|_{0,\lambda^{-1}}^{2}+\frac{\delta_{0}}{2}\|\tilde{q}^{c}\|_{0,s_{0}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}S(\tilde{q},\tilde{q})+\frac{C_{3}\Delta t}{2}\|\tilde{q}\|_{h}^{2}.

Since
\|\tilde{q}^{0}\|_{0,\lambda^{-1}}+\|\tilde{q}^{0}\|_{0,s_{0}}\leq C_{P}S(\tilde{q}^{0},\tilde{q}^{0})^{1/2}
, the above inequality gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}((\boldsymbol{v},q),(\tilde{\boldsymbol{v}},\tilde{q}))\geq C\|(\tilde{\boldsymbol{v}},\tilde{q})\|_{\mathcal{X}}^{2}

with
C>0
depending on
C_{1}^{\prime\prime}
,
C_{2}
,
C_{3}
,
\delta_{0}
,
\delta_{1}
.

On the other hand, by the definition of
(\boldsymbol{v},q)
, it is easy to check that
\|(\boldsymbol{v},q)\|_{\mathcal{X}}\leq C\|(\tilde{\boldsymbol{v}},\tilde{q})\|_{\mathcal{X}}
with
C>0
depending on
\delta_{0}
,
\delta_{1}
. Therefore, the conclusion follows.

6.
Numerical results

In this section we present numerical results which support our theoretical findings on error analysis and preconditioning. All numerical experiments are done with the Firedrake package
[
30
]
.

In our numerical experiments we set
\Omega=[0,1]\times[0,1]
with
\Gamma_{d}=\{0\}\times[0,1]
and
\Gamma_{p}=\partial\Omega
. We use the structured meshes obtained by dividing
\Omega
into
N\times N
subsquares and the dividing each subsquare into two triangles. For convergence rate tests we refine the meshes with
N=8,16,32,64
.

To check convergence rates of the errors of numerical and exact solutions, we consider a manufactured solution with

(60a)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{u}

\displaystyle=\left(-\pi x^{2}\left(1-x\right)^{2}\sin^{2}\left(\pi y\right)\cos\left(2t\right),-\pi y^{2}\left(1-y\right)^{2}\sin^{2}\left(\pi x\right)\cos\left(2t\right)\right),

(60b)

\displaystyle p

\displaystyle=x\left(1-x\right)y\left(1-y\right)\cos\left(t\right).

Note that
\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{u}\not=0
, so the
L^{2}
norm of
\boldsymbol{f}
may grow unboundedly as
\lambda\rightarrow+\infty
. In order to illustrate convergence rates of the errors independent of
\lambda
, we compute the relative errors measured by

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{a_{\boldsymbol{u}}}/\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{a_{\boldsymbol{u}}},\quad\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{1}/\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{1},\quad\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{0}/\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{0},

\displaystyle\|p-p_{h}\|_{1}/\|p\|_{1},\quad\|p-p_{h}\|_{0}/\|p\|_{0}.

In Table
1
we present the results for
\beta=1,2
cases with the Crank–Nicolson time discretization with time step size
\Delta t=1/N
. For all relative error quantities and for
\nu=0.3,0.499
we observe that optimal convergence rates are obtained.

For preconditioning we also present preconditioning test results. We compare the results for all combinations of
\beta=0,1,2
,
\nu=0.3,0.499
,
\Delta t=10^{-1},10^{-2},10^{-3}
. We use preconditioners which have the abstract form (
44
). For implementation of
\mathcal{P}_{\boldsymbol{V}_{h}}
we use the geometric multigrid preconditioner developed in
[
2
]
. For implementation of
\mathcal{P}_{\tilde{Q}_{h}}
we use the preconditioners proposed in
[
21
,
18
]
combining algebraic multigrid preconditioners for
Q_{h}^{c}
and the block Jacobi preconditioner for
Q_{h}^{0}
. Numbers of iterations are given in Table
2
. One can observe that
\beta=0
case, the original enriched Galerkin method for
p
, shows strong dependence on mesh refinement and
\nu\approx 0.5
. For
\beta=1

is not valid for GmRes, so we do not include GmRes in this paper.

7.
Conclusion

In this paper we developed a new discretization for poroelasticity. We use the primal formulations for the elastic displacement and for the pore pressure minimizing the number of variables.
In our discretization with nonconforming and the interior over-stabilized enriched Galerkin methods, numerical solutions are robust for nearly incompressible elasticity parameters, are locally mass conservative. Furthermore, we showed a theoretical discussion how to construct robust preconditioinors and the robust preconditioner construction method was verified by numerical experiments.

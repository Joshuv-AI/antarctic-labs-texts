A generalized Turán extension of the Deza–Erdős–Frankl Theorem
Charlotte Helliar, Xizhi Liu

Abstract
For an integer r ≥ 3 r\geq 3 and a subset L ⊂ [ 0 , r − 1 ] L\subset[0,r-1] , a graph G G is ( K r , L ) (K_{r},L) -intersecting if the number of vertices in the intersection of every pair of K r K_{r} in G G belongs to L L . We study the maximum number of K r K_{r} in an n n -vertex ( K r , L ) (K_{r},L) -intersecting graphs. The celebrated Ruzsa–Szemerédi Theorem corresponds to the case r = 3 r=3 and L = { 0 , 1 } L=\{0,1\} . For general L L with 2 ≤ | L | ≤ r − 1 2\leq|L|\leq r-1 , we establish the upper bound ( 1 − 1 3 ​ r ) ​ ∏ ℓ ∈ L n − ℓ r − ℓ \left(1-\frac{1}{3r}\right)\prod_{\ell\in L}\frac{n-\ell}{r-\ell} for large n n , which improves the bound provided by the celebrated Deza–Erdős–Frankl Theorem by a factor of 1 − 1 3 ​ r 1-\frac{1}{3r} . In the special case where L = { t , t + 1 , … , r − 1 } L=\{t,t+1,\ldots,r-1\} , we derive the tight upper bound for large n n and establish a corresponding stability result. This is an extension of the seminal Erdős–Ko–Rado Theorem on t t -intersecting systems to the generalized Turán setting. Our proof for the Deza–Erdős–Frankl part involves an interesting combination of the Δ \Delta -system method and Turán’s theorem. Meanwhile, for the Erdős–Ko–Rado part, we employ the stability method, which relies on a theorem of Frankl regarding t t -intersecting systems.

Introduction

The seminal Turán Theorem
[
Tur41
]
stands as a cornerstone in Extremal Graph Theory. It asserts that for
n\geq\ell\geq 2
, the maximum number of edges in an
n
-vertex
K_{\ell+1}
-free graph is achieved by the Turán graph
T(n,\ell)
. Here,
T(n,\ell)
represents the balanced complete
\ell
-partite graph on
n
vertices.
Erdős
[
Erd62
]
considered an extension of the Turán Theorem and proved that for
r\leq\ell
, the maximum number of
K_{r}
subgraphs in an
n
-vertex
K_{\ell+1}
-free graph is also attained by
T(n,\ell)
.
In general, given a graph
Q
and a family
\mathcal{F}
of graphs, let
\mathrm{ex}(n,Q,\mathcal{F})
denote the maximum number of copies of
Q
in an
n
-vertex
\mathcal{F}
-free graph.
Alon–Shikhelman
[
AS16
]
initiated a systematic study on
\mathrm{ex}(n,Q,\mathcal{F})
and obtained numerous results for various combinations of
(Q,\mathcal{F})
. Since then, this topic has been extensively studied in the recent decade.

We consider an interesting case of the generalized Turán problem motivated by theorems from Extremal Set Theory.
Recall that an
r
-graph is a collection of
r
-subsets of some finite set
V
.
We identify an
r
-graph with its edge set.
Given a subset
L\subset[0,r-1]
, an
r
-graph
\mathcal{H}
is
L
-intersecting
if
|e\cap e^{\prime}|\in L
for every distinct pair of edges
e,e^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}
.
If
L=\{t,t+1,\ldots,r-1\}
for some
t\in[r]
, then we simply say
\mathcal{H}
is
t
-intersecting
.
Determining the maximum size of an
L
-intersecting
r
-graph on
n
vertices is a central topic in Extremal Set Theory and is connected to many classical results. Below, we highlight two related theorems and refer interested readers to the survey
[
FT16
]
for more insights into this area.

The seminal Erdős–Ko–Rado Theorem established a tight upper bound for the size of
t
-intersecting
r
-graphs with a large number of vertices.

Theorem 1.1

(Erdős–Ko–Rado
[
EKR
]
)
.

Let
r>t\geq 1
be integers.
There exists
n_{0}(r,t)
such that every
t
-intersecting
r
-graph on
n\geq n_{0}(r,t)
vertices has size at most
\binom{n-t}{r-t}
.

The celebrated Deza–Erdős–Frankl Theorem established a general upper bound for the size of
L
-intersecting
r
-graphs, though finding matching lower bound constructions in general is an extremely challenging problem.

Theorem 1.2

(Deza–Erdős–Frankl
[
DEF78
]
)
.

Suppose that
r\geq 3
and
n\geq 2^{r}r^{3}
are integers and
L\subset[0,r-1]
is a subset.
Then every
n
-vertex
L
-intersecting
r
-graph has size at most
\prod_{\ell\in L}\frac{n-\ell}{r-\ell}
.

Given a graph
G
and an integer
r\geq 3
, we define the associated
r
-graph
\mathcal{K}_{G}^{r}
as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{G}^{r}\coloneqq\left\{S\in\binom{V(G)}{r}\colon G[S]\cong K_{r}\right\}.

For a subset
L\subset[0,r-1]
, we say a graph
G
is
(K_{r},L)
-intersecting
if the
r
-graph
\mathcal{K}_{G}^{r}
is
L
-intersecting.
In other words, the number of vertices in the intersection of every pair of
K_{r}
in
G
belongs to
L
.
In the special case where
L=\{t,t+1,\ldots,r-1\}
, we say
G
is
(K_{r},t)
-intersecting
.
For convenience, we use
N(K_{r},G)
to denote the number of copies of
K_{r}
in
G
, and define

\displaystyle\Psi_{r}(n,L)\coloneqq\max\left\{N(K_{r},G)\colon\text{$G$ is an $n$-vertex $(K_{r},L)$-intersecting graph}\right\}.

There are several sporadic results on
\Psi_{r}(n,L)
that are known.
The celebrated Ruzsa–Szemerédi Theorem
[
RS78
]
is equivalent to the statement that
\Psi_{3}\left(n,\{0,1\}\right)=n^{2-o(1)}
, and this was extended in a recent work by Gowers–Janzer
[
GJ20
]
, who proved that
\Psi_{r}\left(n,\{0,1,\ldots,s-1\}\right)=n^{s-o(1)}
for all
r>s\geq 1
.
An old result of Erdős–Sós
[
Sós76
]
determines the exact value of
\Psi_{3}\left(n,\{0,2\}\right)
.
Liu–Wang
[
LW21
]
extended their result and determined the exact value of
\Psi_{r}\left(n,L\right)
for large

in cases where
L=[0,r-1]\setminus\{1\}
and
L=[r-1]
.
They also obtained some bounds for the case where
L=[0,r-1]\setminus\{s\}
when
2\leq s\leq r-1
.

In this note, we aim to establish a general upper bound for
\Psi_{r}(n,L)
and determine its exact value for the case
L=\{t,t+1,\ldots,r-1\}
, extending the Deza–Erdős–Frankl Theorem and the Erdős–Ko–Rado Theorem, respectively. We hope our results could serve as a starting point for a more systematic extension of theorems from Extremal Set Theory, an extremally rich theory that has been studied for decades, to generalized Turán problems. A more detailed discussion is included in the last section.

For integers
n\geq r>t\geq 0
, let
S(n,r,t)
denote the graph obtained from the vertex-disjoint union of
K_{t}
and
T(n-t,r-t)
by adding all pairs that have a nonempty intersection with both graphs.
It is easy to see that
S(n,r,t)
is
(K_{r},t)
-intersecting and

\displaystyle N\left(K_{r},S(n,r,t)\right)=N\left(K_{r-t},T(n-t,r-t)\right)=(1+o(1))\left(\frac{n-t}{r-t}\right)^{r-t}.

In the following theorem, we determine the value of
\Psi(n,\{t,t+1,\ldots,r-1\})
for large
n
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
r>t\geq 1
be integers.
There exists
n_{0}(r,t)
such that every
(K_{r},t)
-intersecting graph on
n\geq n_{0}(r,t)
vertices satisfies

\displaystyle N(K_{r},G)\leq N\left(K_{r},S(n,r,t)\right),

and equality holds iff
G\cong S(n,r,t)
.

The corresponding stability version is as follows.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
r>t\geq 1
be integers.
There exist
\varepsilon_{0}>0
and
n_{0}(r,t)
such that the following holds for all
\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
and
n\geq n_{0}(r,t)
.
If
G
is an
n
-vertex
(K_{r},t)
-intersecting graph with
N(K_{r},G)\geq(1-\varepsilon)\left(\frac{n-t}{r-t}\right)^{r-t}
, then there exists a
t
-set
T\subset V(G)
such that
G-T
is
\ell
-partite after removing at most
2\varepsilon(n-t)^{2}
edges.

An extension of the Deza–Erdős–Frankl Theorem in the generalized Turán setting is as follows.

Theorem 1.5
.

Suppose that
r\geq 3
and
n\geq(2r)^{r+1}
are integers and
L=\{\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{s}\}\subset[0,r-1]
is a subset of size

Then every

-vertex
(K_{r},t)
-intersecting graph

\displaystyle N(K_{r},G)\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{3r}\right)\prod_{\ell\in L}\frac{n-\ell}{r-\ell}.

Remarks.

•

It is clear that Theorem
1.5
does not hold for
L=[0,r-1]
, as demonstrated by the complete graph
K_{n}
.
Similarly, it does not hold when
L=\{\ell\}
for any
\ell\in[0,r-1]
, as demonstrated by the graph obtained from the vertex-disjoint union of
K_{\ell}
and
\lfloor\frac{n-\ell}{r-\ell}\rfloor K_{r-\ell}
by adding all pairs that have nonempty intersection with both graphs.
Here,
\lfloor\frac{n-\ell}{r-\ell}\rfloor K_{r-\ell}
represents the graph consisting of
\lfloor\frac{n-\ell}{r-\ell}\rfloor
vertex-disjoint copies of

•

Assuming that

and

[

proved that for large

\displaystyle(\ell_{2}-\ell_{1})\mid(\ell_{3}-\ell_{2})\mid\cdots\mid(\ell_{s}-\ell_{s-1})\mid(r-\ell_{s}).

For
(K_{r},\{\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{s}\})
-intersecting graphs, it is possible that the bound

could be improved by a factor
1-\varepsilon
for some constant

using ideas from their proof and the proof of Theorem
5.1
.
However, this seems less interesting given that they did not attempt to optimize the coefficient

Proof of Theorem

We establish Theorem

Fact 3.1
.

Suppose that
r\geq 2
and
-1\leq x<\frac{1}{r-1}

\displaystyle(1+x)^{r}\leq 1+\frac{rx}{1-(r-1)x}.

(Fisher–Ryan
[

]
)
.

Let
\ell\geq r\geq 2
be integers.
Suppose that
G
is an
n
-vertex

-free graph.
Then

\displaystyle N(K_{r},G)\leq\binom{\ell}{r}\left({|G|}/{\binom{\ell}{2}}\right)^{\frac{r}{2}}.

Theorem 3.3

(Füredi
[
Fü15
]
)
.

Suppose that
\ell\geq 2
and
G
is an
n
-vertex
K_{\ell+1}
-free graph with

.
Then
G
can be made

-partite by removing at most

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.

Let
x\coloneqq\frac{2m}{r\binom{\ell}{r}\left(\frac{n}{\ell}\right)^{r}+(r-2)m}
.
Observe that
\binom{\ell}{r}\left(\frac{n}{\ell}\right)^{r}\frac{xr/2}{1-(r/2-1)x}=m
and

First, we prove that
|G|\geq(1-x)\binom{\ell}{2}\left(\frac{n}{\ell}\right)^{2}

Indeed, suppose to the contrary that
|G|<(1-x)\binom{\ell}{2}\left(\frac{n}{\ell}\right)^{2}

Then it follows from Theorem

\displaystyle N(K_{r},G)\leq\binom{\ell}{r}\left({|G|}/{\binom{\ell}{2}}\right)^{\frac{r}{2}}

\displaystyle<\binom{\ell}{r}\left(\frac{n}{\ell}\right)^{r}\left(1-x\right)^{\frac{r}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\binom{\ell}{r}\left(\frac{n}{\ell}\right)^{r}\left(1-\frac{xr/2}{1-(r/2-1)x}\right)

\displaystyle=\binom{\ell}{r}\left(\frac{n}{\ell}\right)^{r}-m,

a contradiction.
Therefore, we have
|G|\geq(1-x)\binom{\ell}{2}\left(\frac{n}{\ell}\right)^{2}\geq|T(n,\ell)|-x\binom{\ell}{2}\left(\frac{n}{\ell}\right)^{2}

Hence, it follows from Theorem

C\subset V
of size
\ell
, fix a maximum sunflower
\mathcal{S}_{C}\subset\mathcal{H}
with
C
as its core.
Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}\coloneqq\left\{C\in\binom{V}{\ell}\colon|\mathcal{S}_{C}|\geq r^{2}\right\}.

Claim 5.2
.

We have
U=\bigcup_{C\in\mathcal{C}}C
.
In particular,
|U|=\ell|\mathcal{C}|
.

Proof.

The inclusion
\bigcup_{C\in\mathcal{C}}C\subset U
follows easily from the definitions, so it suffices to show the other direction.
Fix
u\in U
.
Observe that the link
L_{\mathcal{H}}(u)
is an
\{\ell-1\}
-intersecting
(r-1)
-graph.
So it follows from the definition of
U
and Theorem
2.4
that
L_{\mathcal{H}}(u)
is an
(\ell-1)
-sunflower of size at least
r^{2}
. Therefore,
u
is contained in the core of an
\ell
-sunflower of size at least
r^{2}
. This implies that
U\subset\bigcup_{C\in\mathcal{C}}C
.

Claim 5.3
.

We have
C\cap C^{\prime}=\emptyset
for all distinct
C,C^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}
.

Proof of Claim
5.3
.

Suppose to the contrary that there exist distinct
C,C^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}
with
C\cap C^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
(which can only occur if
\ell>1
).
Since
\mathcal{S}_{C}
is a sunflower of size at least
r^{2}
, there exists
E\in\mathcal{S}_{C}
such that
E\setminus C
is disjoint from
C^{\prime}
, that is,
E\cap C^{\prime}=C\cap C^{\prime}
.
Similarly, since
\mathcal{S}_{C^{\prime}}
is a sunflower of size at least
r^{2}
, there exists
E^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{C^{\prime}}
such that
E^{\prime}\cap E=C\cap C^{\prime}
.
However, this implies that
1\leq|E\cap E^{\prime}|\leq\ell-1
, a contradiction.

Claim 5.4
.

For every
E\in\mathcal{K}_{G}^{r}
and every
C\in\mathcal{C}
, we have either
E\in\mathcal{S}_{C}
or
E\cap C=\emptyset
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that either
E\cap C=\emptyset
or
C\subset E
, as the latter case implies that
E\in\mathcal{S}_{C}
(since
\mathcal{K}_{G}^{r}
is
\{0,\ell\}
-intersecting).
Suppose to the contrary that there exist
E\in\mathcal{K}_{G}^{r}
and
C\in\mathcal{C}
such that
1\leq|C\cap E|\leq\ell-1
(which can only occur if
\ell>1
).
Similar to the proof of Claim
5.3
, since
\mathcal{S}_{C}
is a sunflower of size at least
r^{2}
, there exists
E^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{C}
such that
E^{\prime}\cap E=C^{\prime}\cap E
.
This implies that
1\leq|E\cap E^{\prime}|\leq\ell-1
, a contradiction.

Define an auxiliary graph
H
with vertex set
\mathcal{C}
. Two distinct members
C,C^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}
are adjacent in
H
if there exists
E\in\mathcal{S}_{C}
such that
C^{\prime}\subset E
, or there exists
E^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{C^{\prime}}
such that
C\subset E^{\prime}
.

Claim 5.5
.

The graph
H
is
K_{\lceil 2r/\ell\rceil}
-free.
In particular,

\displaystyle|H|\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{\lceil 2r/\ell\rceil-1}\right)\frac{|\mathcal{C}|^{2}}{2}\leq\left(1-\frac{\ell}{2r}\right)\frac{|\mathcal{C}|^{2}}{2}.

Proof.

Suppose to the contrary that
\{C_{1},\ldots,C_{\lceil 2r/\ell\rceil}\}\subset\mathcal{C}
induces a copy of
K_{\lceil 2r/\ell\rceil}
in
H
.
Let
S\coloneqq C_{1}\cup\cdots\cup C_{\lceil 2r/\ell\rceil}
.
It follows from the definitions of
\mathcal{K}_{G}^{r}
and
H
that the induced subgraph
G[S]
is complete and contains
\ell\cdot\lceil 2r/\ell\rceil\geq 2r
vertices.
It is easy to see that
G[S]
contains two copies of
K_{r}
whose intersection is not
0
or
\ell
(here, we used the assumption that
r\geq 3
), a contradiction.

Claim 5.6
.

For every
C\in\mathcal{C}
and
u\in C
we have
d_{\mathcal{K}_{G}^{r}}(u)\leq\frac{\ell\cdot d_{H}(C)+|\overline{U}|}{r-\ell}
.

Proof.

Fix
C\in\mathcal{C}
and
u\in C
.
It follows from Claim
5.4
that
d_{\mathcal{K}_{G}^{r}}(u)=|\mathcal{S}_{C}|
.
The definition of
H
and Claim
5.4
imply that
E\setminus C\subset\bigcup_{C^{\prime}\in N_{H}(C)}C^{\prime}\cup\overline{U}
for every
E\in\mathcal{S}_{C}
.
Since
\mathcal{S}_{C}
is a sunflower, we have
|\mathcal{S}_{C}|\leq\frac{|\bigcup_{C^{\prime}\in N_{H}(C)}C^{\prime}\cup\overline{U}|}{r-\ell}=\frac{\ell\cdot d_{H}(C)+|\overline{U}|}{r-\ell}
.

By Claims
5.2
,
5.5
, and
5.6
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{u\in U}d_{\mathcal{K}_{G}^{r}}(u)=\sum_{C\in\mathcal{C}}\sum_{u\in C}d_{\mathcal{K}_{G}^{r}}(u)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{C\in\mathcal{C}}\ell\cdot\frac{\ell\cdot d_{H}(C)+|\overline{U}|}{r-\ell}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r-\ell}\left(2|H|\cdot\ell^{2}+\ell|\mathcal{C}||\overline{U}|\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{r-\ell}\left(2\left(1-\frac{\ell}{2r}\right)\frac{|\mathcal{C}|^{2}}{2}\cdot\ell^{2}+\ell|\mathcal{C}||\overline{U}|\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r-\ell}\left(\left(1-\frac{\ell}{2r}\right)|U|^{2}+|U||\overline{U}|\right).

Therefore,

\displaystyle r\cdot|\mathcal{K}_{G}^{r}|=\sum_{u\in V}d_{\mathcal{K}_{G}^{r}}(u)

\displaystyle=\sum_{u\in U}d_{\mathcal{K}_{G}^{r}}(u)+\sum_{u\in\overline{U}}d_{\mathcal{K}_{G}^{r}}(u)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{r-\ell}\left(\left(1-\frac{\ell}{2r}\right)|U|^{2}+|U||\overline{U}|\right)+r^{2}|\overline{U}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{r-\ell}\left(\left(1-\frac{\ell}{2r}\right)|U|^{2}+|U||\overline{U}|+r^{3}|\overline{U}|\right)\leq\left(1-\frac{\ell}{2r}\right)\frac{n^{2}}{r-\ell},

where the last inequality follows from a simple analysis of the quadratic form
\left(1-\frac{\ell}{2r}\right)|U|^{2}+|U||\overline{U}|+r^{3}|\overline{U}|
(by considering
|U|
as the variable) and the assumption that
n\geq 5r^{4}
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle N(K_{r},G)=|\mathcal{K}_{G}^{r}|\leq\left(1-\frac{\ell}{2r}\right)\frac{n^{2}}{r(r-\ell)}\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{3r}\right)\frac{n(n-\ell)}{r(r-\ell)}

(3)

where the last inequality follows from the assumption that
n\geq 5r^{4}
.

Case 2:

\ell_{1}\geq 1
.

Let
\ell\coloneqq\ell_{2}-\ell_{1}\geq 1
.
Since

\displaystyle|\mathcal{K}_{G}^{r}|\geq\left(1-\frac{\ell_{2}-\ell_{1}}{2(r-\ell_{1})}\right)\frac{(n-\ell_{1})^{2}}{(r-\ell_{1})(r-\ell_{2})}\geq\frac{1}{2}\frac{(n-r)^{2}}{r^{2}}>2r^{2}n,

it follows from Theorem
2.3
that there exists an
\ell_{1}
-set
T\subset V
such that
T
is contained in every edge in
\mathcal{K}_{G}^{r}
. In other words, every copy of
K_{r}
in
G
contains
T
.
Let
N\coloneqq\bigcap_{v\in T}N_{G}(v)\subset V\setminus T
.
It is easy to see that
G[N]
is
(K_{r-\ell_{1}},\{0,\ell\})
-intersecting.
Therefore, by (
3
), we have

\displaystyle N(K_{r},G)=N(K_{r-t},G[N])

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-\frac{\ell}{2(r-\ell_{1})}\right)\frac{(n-\ell_{1})^{2}}{(r-\ell_{1})(r-\ell_{1}-\ell)}

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{\ell_{2}-\ell_{1}}{2(r-\ell_{1})}\right)\frac{(n-\ell_{1})^{2}}{(r-\ell_{1})(r-\ell_{2})}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{3r}\right)\frac{(n-\ell_{1})(n-\ell_{2})}{(r-\ell_{1})(r-\ell_{2})},

completing the proof of Theorem
5.1
.

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
1.5
.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

We prove by induction on
r
and
|L|
.
The base case
|L|=2
and
r\geq 3
was proved in Theorem
5.1
, so we may assume that
L=\{\ell_{1},\cdots,\ell_{s}\}\subset[0,r-1]
satisfies
s\geq 3
and
\ell_{1}<\cdots<\ell_{s}
.
Let
G
be a
(K_{r},L)
-intersecting graph on
n\geq(2r)^{r+1}
vertices.
Let
V\coloneqq V(G)
.

Case 1:

0\in L
.

Let
L^{\prime}\coloneqq\{\ell_{2}-1,\ldots,\ell_{s}-1\}
.
Fix
u\in V
and let
N_{u}\coloneqq N_{G}(u)
.
Notice that the induced subgraph
G[N_{u}]
is
(K_{r-1},L^{\prime})
-intersecting.
It follows from the inductive hypothesis that

\displaystyle d_{\mathcal{K}_{G}^{r}}(u)=N(K_{r-1},G[N_{u}])

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{3(r-1)}\right)\prod_{i=2}^{s}\frac{(n-1)-(\ell_{i}-1)}{(r-1)-(\ell_{i}-1)}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{3r}\right)\prod_{i=2}^{s}\frac{n-\ell_{i}}{r-\ell_{i}}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle N(K_{r},G)=|\mathcal{K}_{G}^{r}|=\frac{1}{r}\sum_{u\in V}d_{\mathcal{K}_{G}^{r}}(u)\leq\frac{n}{r}\cdot\left(1-\frac{1}{3r}\right)\prod_{i=2}^{s}\frac{n-\ell_{i}}{r-\ell_{i}}=\left(1-\frac{1}{3r}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{s}\frac{n-\ell_{i}}{r-\ell_{i}}.

Case 2:

0\not\in L
.

We may assume that
|\mathcal{K}_{G}^{r}|\geq 2^{s-1}r^{2}n^{s-1}
since otherwise it follows from
n\geq(2r)^{r+1}
that

\displaystyle N(K_{r},G)=|\mathcal{K}_{G}^{r}|<2^{s-1}r^{2}n^{s-1}\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{3r}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{s}\frac{n-\ell_{i}}{r-\ell_{i}}.

By Theorem
2.3
, there exists an
\ell_{1}
-set
T\subset V
such that
T\subset E
for all
E\in\mathcal{K}_{G}^{r}
.
Let
N\coloneqq\bigcap_{v\in T}N_{G}(v)\subset V\setminus T
.
Let
L^{\prime}\coloneqq\{0,\ell_{2}-\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{s}-\ell_{1}\}
.
It is easy to see that the induced subgraph
G[N]
is
(K_{r-\ell_{1}},L^{\prime})
-intersecting.
It follows from the inductive hypothesis that

\displaystyle N(K_{r},G)=N(K_{r-\ell_{1}},G[N])

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{3(r-\ell_{1})}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{s}\frac{(n-\ell_{1})-(\ell_{i}-\ell_{1})}{(r-\ell_{1})-(\ell_{i}-\ell_{1})}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{3r}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{s}\frac{n-\ell_{i}}{r-\ell_{i}},

completing the proof of Theorem
1.5
.

6
Concluding remarks

There are several natural questions left open regarding Theorems
1.3
and
1.5
.
For example, determining the optimal value of
n_{0}(r,t)
in Theorem
1.3
and improving the factor
1-\frac{1}{3r}
in Theorem
1.5
are interesting directions for further investigation.
It seems that our proof for Theorem
1.3
yields an exponential bound (in terms of
r
) for
n_{0}(r,t)
.

Recall that
\Psi_{r}(n,L)
is the maximum number of
K_{r}
in an
n
-vertex
(K_{r},L)
-intersecting graph.
Let
\Phi_{r}(n,L)
denote the maximum size of an
n
-vertex
L
-intersecting
r
-graph.
It is clear that
\Psi_{r}(n,L)\leq\Phi_{r}(n,L)
for all
n\geq r\geq 2
and
L\subset[0,r-1]
.
The following general question seems interesting.

Problem 6.1
.

Characterize the family of sets
L\subset[0,r-1]
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\Psi_{r}(n,L)}{\Phi_{r}(n,L)}=0.

Remark.

When
L=[0,s-1]
for some
s\in[2,r-1]
,
the celebrated work of Rödl
[
Röd85
]
show that
\Phi(n,r,L)=(1+o(1))\binom{n}{r}/\binom{r}{s}=\Theta(n^{s})
, while a simple aurgument using the Graph Removal Lemma (see
[
GJ20
]
) shows that
\Psi(n,r,L)=o(n^{s})
.

Another intriguing question, which seeks an analogue of the celebrated Frankl–Wilson Theorem
[
FW81
]
in the generalized Turán setting, is as follows.

Problem 6.2
.

Let
n>r\geq s\geq 1
be integers and
p
be a prime number.
Let
L\subset[0,p-1]
be a set of
s
integers.
What is the maximum value of
N(K_{r},G)
if
G
is an
n
-vertex graph satisfying

(i)

k\not\in L\ (\mathrm{mod}\ p)
, and

(ii)

|e\cap e^{\prime}|\in L\ (\mathrm{mod}\ p)
for all distinct edges
e,e^{\prime}\in\mathcal{K}_{G}^{r}
.

There are numerous classical theorems from Extremal Set Theory that one could consider extending to the generalized Turán setting.
Here we list a few instances and refer the reader to surveys
[
FT16
,
MV16
]
for more potential extensions.

Problem 6.3
.

Given an
n
-vertex graph
G
, what is the maximum value of
N(K_{r},G)
if

(i)

\mathcal{K}_{G}^{r}
contains at most
t
pairwise vertex-disjoint edges for
2\leq t\leq n/r
?

(ii)

\mathcal{K}_{G}^{r}
does not contain a certain
r
-uniform tree
T
?

(iii)

\mathcal{K}_{G}^{r}
does not contain the cycle
C_{\ell}^{t}
? Here
C_{\ell}^{t}
denote the
r
-uniform length-
\ell
cycle on
[(r-t)\ell]
with edge set

\displaystyle\left\{\{j(r-t)+1,\cdots,(j+1)r-jt\}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ (r-t)\ell)\colon 0\leq j\leq\ell-1\right\}.

Problem
6.3

(i)
corresponds to the famous Erdős Matching Problem
[
Erd65
]
and was addressed in
[
ZCGZ24
]
for fixed
t
and large
n
.
Problem
6.3

(ii)
corresponds to the Tree problem in
r
-graphs (see e.g.
[
BK14
,
Für14
,
FJS14
,
FJ15
,
KMV17
,
FJK+19
]
) and was studied in
[
LGH+24
,
LS23
,
LS
]
for some special cases.
Problem
6.3

(iii)
corresponds to Cycle problems in
r
-graphs (see e.g.
[
KMV15
,
FJ14
]
) and was studied in
[
LGH+24
,
LS23
,
LS
]
for the case
t=1
.

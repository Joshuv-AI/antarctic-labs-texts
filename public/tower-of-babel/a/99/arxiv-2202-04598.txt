Reproducibility in Optimization: Theoretical Framework and Limits
Kwangjun Ahn, Prateek Jain, Ziwei Ji, Satyen Kale, Praneeth Netrapalli, Gil I. Shamir

Abstract
We initiate a formal study of reproducibility in optimization. We define a quantitative measure of reproducibility of optimization procedures in the face of noisy or error-prone operations such as inexact or stochastic gradient computations or inexact initialization. We then analyze several convex optimization settings of interest such as smooth, non-smooth, and strongly-convex objective functions and establish tight bounds on the limits of reproducibility in each setting. Our analysis reveals a fundamental trade-off between computation and reproducibility: more computation is necessary (and sufficient) for better reproducibility.

Introduction

Machine learned models are increasingly entering wider ranges of domains in our lives, driving a constantly increasing number of important systems.
Large scale systems can be trained in highly parallel and distributed training environments, with a large amount of randomness in training the models. While some systems may tolerate such randomness leading to models that differ from one another every time a model retrains, for many applications,
reproducible
models are required, where slight
changes in training do not lead to drastic differences in the model learned.

Beyond practical deployments of machine learned models, the reproducibility crisis in the machine learning academic world has also been well-documented: see
(
Pineau et al. 2021
)
and the references therein for an excellent discussion of the reasons for irreproducibility (insufficient exploration of hyperparameters and experimental setups, lack of sufficient documentation, inaccessible code, and different computational hardware) and for mitigation recommendations. Recent papers
(
Chen et al. 2020
;
D’Amour et al. 2020
;
Dusenberry et al. 2020
;
Snapp and Shamir 2021
;
Summers and Dinneen 2021
;
Yu et al. 2021
)
have also demonstrated that even when models are trained on identical datasets with identical optimization algorithms, architectures, and hyperparameters, they can produce significantly different predictions on the same example.
This type of irreproducibility may be caused by multiple factors
(
D’Amour et al. 2020
;
Fort et al. 2020
;
Frankle et al. 2020
;
Shallue et al. 2018
;
Snapp and Shamir 2021
;
Summers and Dinneen 2021
)
, such as non-convexity of the objective, random initialization, nondeterminism in training such as data shuffling, parallelism, random schedules, hardware used, and round off quantization errors. Perhaps surprisingly, even if we control for the randomness by using the same “seed" for model initialization, other factors such as numerical errors introduced due to nondeterminism of modern GPUs (see, e.g.,
(
Zhuang et al. 2021
)
) may still lead to significant differences. It was empirically shown (see, e.g.,
Achille et al. 2017
) that slight deviations early in training can lead to different optima, with substantial differences in resulting models.
Thus we are forced to accept some
fundamental
level of irreproducibility that persists even after fixing all aspects of the training process under our control.

The goal of this paper is to initiate a formal study of the fundamental limits of irreproducibility.
In particular, we focus on the most basic training process:
convex optimization
. At first glance, it might seem surprising that convex optimization procedures can exhibit irreproducibility since they’re guaranteed to converge to an optimal solution.
However, in practice, the default convex optimization algorithms are iterative first-order methods; methods that only use a first-order oracle to provide an approximate gradient of the function at a given point, and converge to an approximately optimal solution.
The first-order oracle is a source of irreproducibility. In stochastic gradient descent, it returns a random vector whose expectation is the true gradient. The randomness in the stochastic gradients can lead to different outcomes of the optimization process. Similarly, there are numerical errors that can arise in the computation of the gradients due to inherent nondetermism in modern GPUs. Beyond the first-order oracle, irreproducibility may also arise in convex optimization procedures because the initial point is chosen randomly.
Thus, we attempt to answer the following questions for convex optimization procedures operating with the above sources of
irreproducibility:

\blacksquare

What are the fundamental limits of reproducibility for any convex optimization procedure?

\blacksquare

Can we design practical and efficient first-order methods
that achieve these
limits?

We study these questions in a variety of settings; including general non-smooth convex functions, smooth convex functions, strongly-convex functions, finite-sum functions, and stochastic convex optimization, under the different sources of irreproducibility mentioned above.
To the best of our knowledge, no prior theoretical work considered such questions.

The primary contribution of this paper is conceptual: the development of a rigorous theoretical framework to study the fundamental limits of reproducibility in convex optimization. The concepts developed in this framework can be extended easily to other settings of interest such as non-convex optimization. The technical contribution of this paper is the development of lower bounds on the amount of reproducibility, and matching upper bounds via analysis of specific first-order algorithms in all the different settings of convex optimization described above. Detailed technical descriptions of the results appear below in
Subsection 1.1
.

At a high level, our study provides the same message for all the different optimization settings we consider. On the lower bound side, we find that
any
first-order method would need to trade-off convergence rate (computational complexity) for more reproduciblity. On the upper bound side, we find that various forms of gradient descent, when run with
lower
step-size (and correspondingly,
more
iterations) already achieve the fundamental limits of reproducibility. One (somewhat surprising) consequence of our results is that advanced techniques like regularization, variance reduction, and acceleration do not improve reproducibility over standard gradient descent methods.

We show, for example, that when optimizing a Lipschitz non-smooth convex function
f
on a bounded domain using a first-order oracle that computes gradients with even
vanishingly small
error, two runs of
any
first-order method that obtains an
\varepsilon
suboptimal solution of
f
after
T
iterations can generate solutions that are
\Omega(\nicefrac{{1}}{{(\sqrt{T}\varepsilon)}})
apart in
\ell_{2}
distance.
Thus, if we run the method for the standard
T=O(\nicefrac{{1}}{{\varepsilon^{2}}})
iterations required to obtain
\varepsilon
-approximate solution for a non-smooth function, then obtained solutions can deviate by
\Omega(1)
distance; i.e., the method is
maximally
irreproducible. To ensure that irreproducibility is small, i.e. that the solutions are within a small distance
\gamma
of each other, we will need to run at least
\Omega(\nicefrac{{1}}{{(\varepsilon^{2}\gamma^{2})}})
iterations. Interestingly, standard gradient descent with appropriately chosen learning rate and number of iterations already achieves this trade-off.

Our results demonstrate the challenge of reproducibility even for standard convex optimization. While we provide matching lower and upper bounds in certain general settings, in
Section 7
, we outline several important open directions.
Solutions to these problems should enable better understanding of reproducibility even for deep learning.

1.2
Related work

Related notions.

In the scientific world, the terms
reproducibility
and
replicability
are often used interchangeably, but here we distinguish the two, following
Pineau et al. 2021
. Reproducibility refers to the requirement that results obtained by a computational procedure (e.g. an experiment or a statistical analysis of a data set) should be the same (or largely similar) when the procedure is repeated using the same code on the same data, whereas replicability is a different notion that requires that results be reliably the same or similar when the data are changed. The field of statistical hypothesis testing
(
Lehmann and Romano 2005
)
provides rigorous and principled techniques to minimize false discoveries and thereby promote replicability. The notion of
algorithmic stability
can also be seen as quantifying the amount of change in the output when a single data sample is changed. This notion has been extensively studied in the context of providing algorithm-dependent generalization bounds
(
Bousquet and Elisseeff 2001
;
Kutin and Niyogi 2002
)
and in developing differentially private algorithms
(
Dwork et al. 2006
;
McSherry and Talwar 2007
)
. In very recent concurrent work,
Impagliazzo et al. 2022
define a notion of
replicability
in learning that is quite different from ours: they aim to develop algorithms that generate the
exact same output
with reasonable probability given a fresh sample. Note that despite the title of their paper, technically the notion studied is replicability, not reproducibility, since they study the output of algorithms when the input data are changed.

In this paper, we specifically focus on reproducibility: how much can the results of a computation differ when it is re-run on the same data with the same code? Hence, both hypothesis testing and algorithmic stability
are orthogonal to the study in this paper, although some of our upper bounds use similar analysis techniques as algorithmic stability.
On a different note, similar to replicability, the boundary between the notions of irreproducibility and
uncertainty
in deep models is rather blurred. Several papers considered different aspects of uncertainty (see, e.g.,
(
Lakshminarayanan et al. 2017
)
and references therein), but this line of work has been empirical in nature.

Inexact oracles in optimization.

The optimization community has studied the consequences of using inexact or error-prone gradient oracles in optimization. Several papers (e.g.
(
Aybat et al. 2020
;
Devolder et al. 2014
;
d’Aspremont 2008
;
Cohen et al. 2018
)
have developed bounds on the optimization error incurred due to the use of inexact oracles. While the sources of errors are similar to the ones studied in this paper, the quantities of interest in these papers are convergence rate and optimization error rather than reproducibility. Interestingly, despite the different objective, some of the high-level conclusions are similar to our paper: for example, accelerated gradient methods do not outperform standard classical methods when used with inexact gradient oracles.

Techniques to improve reproducibility in practice.

Several recent empirical papers considered methods that can reduce levels of irreproducibility in deep models despite nondeterminism in training. Smooth activations
(
Du 2019
;
Mhaskar 1997
)
have been shown
(
Shamir et al. 2020
)
to improve reproducibilty over popular activations, as the Rectified Linear Unit
(
Nair and Hinton 2010
)
.
Ensembles
(
Dietterich 2000
)
leverage diversity of multiple different solutions to produce an average more reproducible one
(
Allen-Zhu and Li 2020
)
. Co-distillation
(
Anil et al. 2018
)
and Anti-distillation
(
Shamir and Coviello 2020
)
leverage ensembles to further push deployed models to be more reproducible. Imposing constraints
(
Bhojanapalli et al. 2021
;
Shamir 2018
)
forces models to prefer some solutions over others, but may come at the cost of reducing model accuracy performance.

Robustness of dynamical systems.

The upper bound results in our paper can be interpreted as robustness results of the (sub)gradient descent dynamics against disturbances.
In particular, our upper bounds can be viewed as some variants of the input-to-state stability
(
Sontag and Wang 1995
)
results for the dynamics (see, e.g.,
(
Tu et al. 2022
, Definition 3.2)
).

2
Problem Formulation

In this section, we define a quantitative measure of
irreproducibility
amenable to a theoretical analysis. Intuitively, a computation is
reproducible
if it generates the exact same output given the same inputs on two different runs. Irreproducibility arises because low-level operations of a computation produce different answers on two runs due to either
randomness
or
non-determinism
.

Our computation of interest is convex optimization via first-order methods, where
initialization
and
gradient computations
are the primary operations that constitute the computation and are subject to errors leading to inexact outputs. A natural measure of irreproducibility is the amount of change in the computed solution to the convex optimization problem under inexact gradient computations or inexact initialization. However, there are two nuances that must be carefully handled here. First, a trivial procedure which ignores its input and outputs a constant solution is perfectly reproducible! Unfortunately, it is perfectly useless as a convex optimization procedure as well. Thus, in order to compare different procedures by their reproducibility metrics, we must assume that the procedures are guaranteed to converge to an optimal solution. The second nuance is that we need to assume that the errors in the gradient or initialization computations are
bounded
in some manner. Evidently, without such an assumption, any non-trivial convex optimization procedure will be extremely irreproducible. We now use the above considerations to develop a precise definition of a measure of irreproducibility.

Convex function classes.

We assume that the function to be optimized is chosen from a certain class,
\mathcal{F}
, of convex functions, along with their domains, satisfying suitable
regularity
conditions (e.g. Lipschitzness, smoothness, strong-convexity, etc.) to develop convergence rates.
For clarity, we will suppress exact dependence on smoothness, Lipschitzness, and strong-convexity parameters. In particular, “smooth” will denote a convex function whose gradients are
O(1)
-Lipschitz continuous, “non-smooth” a convex function that is
O(1)
-Lipschitz continuous, and “strongly-convex” an
\Omega(1)
-strongly-convex function.
Here,
O(1)
and
\Omega(1)
denote universal constants independent of the dimension or other problem dependent quantities, which we leave unspecified to ease the exposition.

Convex optimization procedures.

A
first-order
convex optimization procedure for
\mathcal{F}
is an algorithm that, given any function
f\in\mathcal{F}
, and access to two (potentially noisy) oracles – an initialization oracle, which generates the initial point, and a gradient oracle, which computes gradients for
f
at any given query point – generates a candidate solution
{\bm{x}}_{\mathrm{out}}
for the problem of minimizing
f
over its domain. Note that the algorithm can only access
f
via the oracles provided. We call such an algorithm
\varepsilon
-accurate
if it guarantees that
\E f({\bm{x}}_{\mathrm{out}})-\inf_{{\bm{x}}\in\text{dom}f}f({\bm{x}})\leq\varepsilon
, where the expectation is over any randomness in the computation of
{\bm{x}}_{\mathrm{out}}
. Several of our lower bounds require more structure for the algorithm: specifically, a
first-order iterative
(FOI) algorithm (
à la

Nesterov 2018
) is one that starting from the point
{\bm{x}}_{0}
generated by the initialization oracle, constructs successive iterates

\displaystyle{\bm{x}}_{t}={\bm{x}}_{0}-\textstyle\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}\lambda^{(t)}_{i}g({\bm{x}}_{i})\quad\text{for some }\lambda_{i}^{(t)},~i=0,\dots,t-1,

(
FOI)

where
g({\bm{x}}_{i})
is the output of the gradient oracle query at
{\bm{x}}_{i}
, and outputs
{\bm{x}}_{T}
for some integer
T>0
.
We emphasize that for all
t
, the coefficients
\lambda^{(t)}_{i}
can be chosen adaptively based on all the previous computations.
The above class of algorithms is a canonical one to consider when proving lower bounds against gradient oracle based optimization algorithms. We refer readers to
(
Nesterov 2018
, §2.1.2)
for more background.
For the case of nonsmooth costs, we additionally assume that the coefficient of the latest gradient is nonzero, i.e.,
\lambda^{(t)}_{t-1}\neq 0
for all
t
.
We also note that one of our lower bound results (
Theorem B.1
) is
information-theoretic
(in the sense of
Nemirovski and Yudin 1983
).

Sources of errors in computation.

Errors arise due to inexactness in the outputs of the initialization or gradient oracles. Queries to these oracles on two different runs of the same algorithm might yield different outputs, but we will control the errors by assuming that the outputs are close to some reference point (that remains fixed over different runs) in a suitable metric.

Definition 2.1

(
\delta
-bounded inexact initialization oracle)
.

Given a function
f\in\mathcal{F}
and a reference initialization point
{\bm{x}}_{0}^{\text{ref}}\in\text{dom}f
, a
\delta
-bounded inexact initialization oracle for
f
is one that generates an initial point
{\bm{x}}_{0}\in\text{dom}f
such that
\|{\bm{x}}_{0}-{\bm{x}}_{0}^{\text{ref}}\|\leq\delta
.

The gradient computation oracle is said to be
\delta
-bounded
if for any
f\in\mathcal{F}
and any point
{\bm{x}}\in\text{dom}f
, it outputs a vector
g({\bm{x}})
such that
\E\left\lVert g({\bm{x}})-\nabla f({\bm{x}})\right\rVert^{2}\leq\delta^{2}
for some
\nabla f({\bm{x}})\in\partial f({\bm{x}})
, where the expectation is over any randomness in the computation of
g({\bm{x}})
. We consider both
stochastic
and
non-stochastic
inexact
\delta
-bounded gradient oracles. A stochastic gradient oracle has the additional property that its output
g({\bm{x}})
is a random vector such that
\E g({\bm{x}})=\nabla f({\bm{x}})
, with different queries being independent of each other. Stochastic inexact gradient oracles arise naturally in machine learning applications due to randomness in minibatching.
Non-stochastic inexact gradient oracles model non-deterministic numerical errors due to the accumulation of floating point errors; for giant machine learning models with billions of parameters, individual floating point errors could add up to a noticeable large error.
We formally define the two types of inexact oracles below.

Definition 2.2

(
\delta
-bounded inexact gradient oracle)
.

Given a function
f\in\mathcal{F}
, and
{\bm{x}}\in\text{dom}f
, let
\partial f({\bm{x}})
denote the sub-differential of
f
at
{\bm{x}}
.

(a)

A
stochastic inexact

\delta
-bounded gradient oracle outputs a random vector
g({\bm{x}})
such that
\E{g({\bm{x}})}=\nabla f({\bm{x}})
and
\E\left\lVert g({\bm{x}})-\nabla f({\bm{x}})\right\rVert^{2}\leq\delta^{2}
for some
\nabla f({\bm{x}})\in\partial f({\bm{x}})
. The expectation is over the randomness in
g({\bm{x}})
which is also assumed to be independent for each oracle call.

(b)

A
non-stochastic inexact

\delta
-bounded gradient oracle outputs a non-deterministic vector
g({\bm{x}})
such that

\left\lVert g({\bm{x}})-\nabla f({\bm{x}})\right\rVert^{2}\leq\delta^{2}
for some
\nabla f({\bm{x}})\in\partial f({\bm{x}})
.

Measure of irreproducibility.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a first-order,
\varepsilon
-accurate convex optimization procedure for
\mathcal{F}
with access to either a
\delta
-bounded initialization oracle (and an exact gradient oracle), or a
\delta
-bounded gradient oracle (and an exact initialization oracle).
The
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-deviation
is

Definition 2.3

(
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-deviation)
.

Given a function
f\in\mathcal{F}
, let
{\bm{x}}_{f}
and
{\bm{x}}^{\prime}_{f}
denote the outputs of
\mathcal{A}
on two independent runs of
\mathcal{A}
that result in
\varepsilon
-accurate solutions.

(a)

If a stochastic inexact
\delta
-bounded gradient oracle is used, the
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-deviation of
\mathcal{A}
is defined as
\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\E\|{\bm{x}}_{f}-{\bm{x}}^{\prime}_{f}\|^{2}
where the randomness is over the stochastic oracle in the two runs.

(b)

If either an inexact
\delta
-bounded initialization oracle or a
\delta
-bounded non-stochastic inexact gradient oracle is used, the
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-deviation of
\mathcal{A}
is defined as
\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\sup\|{\bm{x}}_{f}-{\bm{x}}^{\prime}_{f}\|^{2}
,
where the inner supremum is over the two runs.

Here we note that
{\bm{x}}_{f}
is not necessarily the last iterate of a first-order algorithm. Depending on cases, sometimes we choose
{\bm{x}}_{f}
to be the (weighted) average iterate.
We consider
\|x_{f}-x^{\prime}_{f}\|^{2}
for the notion of deviation instead of
\|x_{f}-x^{*}\|^{2}
; note that
x^{*}
may not even be unique without strong convexity.

Other notions?

Alternate definitions are certainly plausible. For example, in machine learning applications, the computed solution
{\bm{x}}
may be used as a parameter vector for a predictor function
g_{{\bm{x}}}
which maps inputs
z
to real-valued predictions
\hat{y}
whose quality is measured by a loss function
\ell(\hat{y},y)
where
y
is the true label of
z
.
Let
{\bm{x}}
and
{\bm{x}}^{\prime}
be outputs of two different runs of the optimization algorithm. Then one can also define
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-deviation based on
prediction reproducibility
: e.g.
\sup_{z}|g_{{\bm{x}}}(z)-g_{{\bm{x}}^{\prime}}(z)|
or
\E_{z}|g_{{\bm{x}}}(z)-g_{{\bm{x}}^{\prime}}(z)|
, or
loss reproducibility
: e.g.
\sup_{(z,y)}|\ell(g_{{\bm{x}}}(z),y)-\ell(g_{{\bm{x}}^{\prime}}(z),y)|
or
\E_{(z,y)}|\ell(g_{{\bm{x}}}(z),y)-\ell(g_{{\bm{x}}^{\prime}}(z),y)|
, where the expectation is over the distribution of the examples. Nevertheless, we adopt
2.3
, which is based on
parameter reproducibility
, in this paper for the following reasons:

•

The convex optimization problems studied in this paper are
more basic/fundamental
than more structured ML optimization. To the best of our knowledge, there is no pre-existing theory even for this basic setting. Parameter reproducibility is a more natural definition here since there is no notion of prediction or loss.

•

In many ML applications, the predictor function
g_{{\bm{x}}}
is Lipschitz in
{\bm{x}}
for any input
z
. In such cases,
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-deviation bounds for parameter reproducibility immediately transform into
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-deviation bounds for prediction reproducibility. Similar transformations are also generally possible
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-deviation bounds for loss reproducibility.

•

Without knowledge of how a learned parametric function is deployed for making predictions, it is difficult to analyze prediction reproducibility even in ML settings. Hence, parameter deviations provide a reasonable first approximation. Furthermore, in real systems, we often optimize multiple metrics (e.g. performance on sub-segments of populations, for fairness). Parameter reproducibility gives greater assurance on all metrics. A similar argument applies when the test distribution is different from the training distribution.

•

Finally, several recent works
(
Shamir et al. 2020
;
D’Amour et al. 2020
)
have empirically observed that even if two different runs of the algorithm resulted in parameters that have nearly the same loss, the predictions on test examples could be very different. One reason this happens is because
surrogate
losses are used in place of the true metric for optimization. So simply achieving loss reproducibility may not be sufficient for practical applications.

3
Reproducibility with Stochastic Inexact Gradient Oracles

In this section we consider reproducibility of optimizing a convex function
f\in\mathcal{F}
where we can access
f
only via a stochastic gradient oracle (see
2.2
). This setting covers several important ML optimization scenarios, e.g., when the training data is randomly sampled from a population or when the selection of mini-batches is randomized. Our main result is the following theorem.

Theorem 3.1
.

For any
\varepsilon,\delta>0
, and number of iterations
T
, the
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-deviation for optimizing convex functions with a stochastic inexact gradient oracle is as follows. Unless indicated otherwise, the lower bounds hold for any FOI algorithm, and the upper bound is achieved by stochastic gradient descent for
T=\Omega(\nicefrac{{1}}{{\varepsilon^{2}}})
in the non-strongly-convex settings and
T=\Omega(\nicefrac{{1}}{{\varepsilon}})
in the strongly-convex settings.

\blacksquare

Smooth functions
:
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-deviation is
\Theta(\nicefrac{{\delta^{2}}}{{(T\varepsilon^{2})}})
.
Furthermore, for the case
\varepsilon\lesssim\delta^{2}
there is a matching
information theoretic lower bound
.

\blacksquare

Smooth and strongly convex functions
:
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-deviation is
\Theta(\nicefrac{{\delta^{2}}}{{T}}\wedge\varepsilon)
.

\blacksquare

Lipschitz (non-smooth) functions
:
(\varepsilon,\delta)

\Theta(\nicefrac{{1}}{{(T\varepsilon^{2})}})
.

\blacksquare

.

This theorem is proved in several pieces, one corresponding to each setting (i.e., smooth, smooth & strongly convex etc.) and type of bound (i.e., upper or lower). The precise details are given in
Appendix A
.
A few remarks are in order.

Smoothness
: Intuitively, smoothness would ensure that a slight error in gradient computation need not imply catastrophic deviation in the iterates. Our matching upper and lower bounds confirm this intuition.

Non-smoothness
: Our results show that even a slight amount of noise in the gradients can lead to drastic irreproducibility in the non-smooth case. Intuitively, the reason for this phenomenon is that non-smooth functions have non-differentiable points where the slightest amount of noise in the gradients can lead to drastically different behavior. This is in line with the empirical observations of
Shamir et al. 2020
.

Strong convexity
: Note that the deviation is smaller than other cases due to the existence of a unique minimizer; the
\varepsilon
-accuracy in the cost already implies an
O(\varepsilon)
upper bound on the deviation.

Furthermore, in all the settings, our results show that using an algorithm with a larger number of iterations is more helpful; intuitively that is because more gradient samples from the oracle can help reduce the sample noise as an averaging. For example, for smooth functions, if a gradient descent type method is run for the standard
T=O(\nicefrac{{1}}{{\varepsilon^{2}}})
iterations, then the solution obtained might still suffer deviation of
\delta^{2}
, which is independent of
\varepsilon
. Thus, in order to obtain low deviation, we are forced to run the method for
\omega(\nicefrac{{1}}{{\varepsilon^{2}}})
iterations.
We also note that by relying on the standard convergence rate lower bounds
(
Nemirovski and Yudin 1983
)
, the requirement on
T
e.g.,
T=\Omega(1/\varepsilon^{2})
for the non-strongly convex setting, is without loss of generality.
Finally, we remark that the lower bound for smooth functions is information theoretic (i.e., holds against
any
algorithm) for
\varepsilon\lesssim\delta^{2}
.

Acceleration v.s. reproducibility?

6
Reproducibility in Optimization for Machine Learning

So far, we have studied reproducibility in general convex optimization with different sources of perturbation. In machine learning, however, optimization problems come with more structure, and hence a more nuanced analysis of reproducibility is called for. In this section, we study reproducibility in two specific optimization settings of interest in machine learning: finite sum minimization which corresponds to minimizing training loss, and stochastic convex optimization (SCO), which corresponds to minimizing population (or test) loss. Instead of a detailed study of different types of convex functions as done in the previous sections, here we focus on a few specific cases that yield particularly interesting insights.

6.1
Optimizing Training Loss (Finite Sum Minimization)

Optimizing the training loss in ML can be cast as minimizing a function that can be written as a finite sum of component functions:
f({\bm{x}}):=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}f_{i}({\bm{x}})\,.

Typical optimization methods such as stochastic gradient descent can be implemented to solve such problems by iteratively sampling one of the component functions and taking its gradient. So randomness in sampling as well as non-deterministic numerical errors in computing of gradient can lead to irreproducibility. Randomness in sampling is a property of the algorithm and hence under the control of the algorithm designer; however non-deterministic numerical errors in gradient computations are beyond the control of the designer, and hence we aim to quantify irreproducibility caused by this specific source (numerical errors). To capture this intuition, we consider the following inexact gradient oracle.

Definition 6.1

(
\delta
-bounded inexact component gradient oracle)
.

A
\delta
-bounded inexact
component
gradient oracle takes as input
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,m\}
and a point
x
, and outputs a vector
g_{i}({\bm{x}})
such that

\displaystyle\left\lVert g_{i}({\bm{x}})-\nabla f_{i}({\bm{x}})\right\rVert^{2}\leq\delta^{2}\text{ for some }\nabla f_{i}({\bm{x}})\in\partial f_{i}({\bm{x}}).

The number of calls to the component gradient oracle is now our primary measure of complexity. Then, a natural question that arises is whether we can we tightly characterize reproduciblity of typical first-order methods when given access to an inexact component gradient oracle. We first observe that in the special case that all
f_{i}
are identical, the inexact component gradient oracle reduces to the non-stochastic inexact oracle (
2.2
). Hence, for the smooth and non-smooth function settings, we immediately obtain lower bounds on the
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-deviation via our previous analysis; see
Theorem C.2
and
Theorem D.4
, respectively.

The non-smooth case in particular is interesting, as the deviation lower bound is
\Omega(\nicefrac{{1}}{{(T\varepsilon^{2})}}+\nicefrac{{\delta^{2}}}{{\varepsilon^{2}}})
, where
T
is the number of oracle calls. Now, if we use projected (full-batch) gradient descent (as used for the matching upper bound in
Theorem F.3
), then each iteration would require
m
oracle calls, hence the
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-deviation for such a method is
O(\nicefrac{{m}}{{(T\varepsilon^{2})}}+\nicefrac{{\delta^{2}}}{{\varepsilon^{2}}})
, which is worse than the lower bound mentioned above. Now, the key question is whether we can reduce this deviation by other means. It turns out that
stochastic
gradient descent (SGD), where in each step we sample a component function to query randomly, when run with an appropriate learning rate and using averaging,
matches the optimal deviation up to constant factors. We provide a detailed proof of the result in
Appendix G
. This provides another justification for using SGD instead of gradient descent in practice.

Theorem 6.2
.

For
G=O(1)
and
D=O(1)
, let
f_{i}
be an
G
-Lipschitz convex cost function for each
i\in[m]
, and assume that the optimum of
f
lies in a ball of radius
D
.
Let
\varepsilon,\delta>0
be given parameters such that
\delta\leq\nicefrac{{\varepsilon}}{{(2D)}}
, and
T=\Omega(1/\varepsilon^{2})
be a given number of iterations. Define the SGD updates as follows: initialize
{\bm{x}}_{0}=0
, and for
t=0,1,\ldots,T-1
, set
{\bm{x}}_{t+1}={\bm{x}}_{t}-\eta_{t}g_{i_{t}}({\bm{x}}_{t})
where
i_{t}\sim[n]
uniformly at random. Under the inexact component gradient oracle (
6.1
), the average iterate
\bar{{\bm{x}}}_{T}
of SGD with stepsize
\eta=\Theta(\nicefrac{{1}}{{(\varepsilon T)}})
satisfies

\E f(\bar{{\bm{x}}}_{T})-\inf_{{\bm{x}}\in\text{dom}f}f({\bm{x}})\leq\varepsilon
and
\E\left\lVert\bar{{\bm{x}}}_{T}-\bar{{\bm{x}}}^{\prime}_{T}\right\rVert^{2}=O(\nicefrac{{1}}{{(T\varepsilon^{2})}}+\nicefrac{{\delta^{2}}}{{\varepsilon^{2}}})
, where
\bar{{\bm{x}}}^{\prime}_{T}
is the output of an independent run of SGD.

6.2
Optimizing Population Loss (Stochastic Convex Optimization)

The fundamental problem of machine learning is to find the solution to the following
population
(or test) loss minimization problem: minimize
F({\bm{x}})=\E_{\xi\sim\Xi}f({\bm{x}},\xi)
, where
\Xi
is an unknown distribution on the examples
\xi
, given access to an oracle that sample from
\Xi
. When the function
F
is convex, this is also known as Stochastic Convex Optimization (SCO).

In this setting, the sampling oracle is a natural source of irreproducibility of optimization methods. To model this, we consider the
stochastic global oracle
, inspired by
Foster et al. 2019
.

Definition 6.3

(
\delta
-bounded stochastic global oracle)
.

Given a function
F
, a
\delta
-bounded stochastic global oracle for
F
is an algorithm that, on each query, draws an independent sample
\xi\sim\Xi
from some distribution
\Xi
, and outputs a function
f(\cdot,\xi):\text{dom}F\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that for all
x\in\text{dom}F
, we have
F({\bm{x}})=\E_{\xi\sim\Xi}f({\bm{x}},\xi)
, and,
\E_{\xi\sim\Xi}\left\lVert\nabla f({\bm{x}},\xi)-\nabla F({\bm{x}})\right\rVert^{2}\leq\delta^{2}\,.

Since in each query, the stochastic global oracle returns the complete specification of
f(\cdot,\xi)
, an algorithm using such an oracle has access to
({\bm{x}},f({\bm{x}},\xi),\nabla f({\bm{x}},\xi),\nabla^{2}f({\bm{x}},\xi),\cdots)
for all
{\bm{x}}
.

Note that the inexact stochastic gradient oracle of
2.2
is a weaker form of the stochastic global oracle. Thus, the key question is:
under the powerful stochastic global oracle, can we provide better reproducibility than the lower bounds in
Theorem 3.1
?

Surprisingly, the answer to the above question is negative. That is, despite the stronger oracle setting, there is a function class for which the lower bound on
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-deviation matches that of
Theorem 3.1
.

Theorem 6.4
.

Assume that
\varepsilon<1/200
and
\varepsilon\lesssim\delta^{2}
.
Then there exists a family of population costs
{F}_{\theta}({\bm{x}})=\E_{\xi\sim P_{\theta}}f_{\theta}({\bm{x}},\xi)
parametrized by
\theta\in[1,2]
, and a
\delta
-bounded stochastic global oracle for
{F}_{\theta}
, satisfying the following property. Suppose that
A
is any algorithm that for each
\theta\in[1,2]
uses at most
T
queries to the stochastic global oracle and outputs
{\bm{x}}_{T}^{\theta}
that is
\varepsilon
-accurate, i.e.,
\E{F}_{\theta}({\bm{x}}_{T}^{\theta})-\inf_{{\bm{x}}}{F}_{\theta}({\bm{x}})\leq\varepsilon
.
Then, there exists
\theta_{\sf bad}\in[1,2]
such that
\var({\bm{x}}_{T}^{\theta_{\sf bad}})\geq\Omega(\nicefrac{{\delta^{2}}}{{(T\varepsilon^{2})}})
.

See
Subsection B.2
for a detailed proof. On the other hand,
Theorem 3.1
also shows that standard SGD with the weaker inexact stochastic gradient oracle achieves this lower bound.

7
Conclusions

We presented a framework for studying reproducibility in convex optimization, and explored limits of reproducibility for optimizing various function classes under different sources of errors.
For each of these settings, we provide tight lower and upper bounds on reproducibility of first order iterative algorithms.
Overall, our results provide the following insights: a) Non-smooth functions can be highly susceptible to even tiny errors which can creep in due to say numerical errors in GPU. Thus introducing smoothness in deep learning models might help with reproducibility. b) Generally, gradient descent type methods with small learning rate are more reproducible. c) In finite-sum settings, despite more randomness, SGD is more reproducible than full-batch gradient descent.

The study in this paper is a first step towards addressing the challenging problem of reproducibility in a rigorous framework. So, many important directions remain unexplored. For example, study of reproducibility of adaptive methods like AdaGrad that are not captured by (
FOI
) is interesting. We study reproducibility in a strict form where we measure deviation in terms of the learned parameters of the model. However, in practice, one might care for reproducibility in model predictions only.
So, extending our results to such ML-driven scenarios should be relevant in practice. Finally, while our lower bounds on reproducibility obviously also hold for non-convex optimization, extension of the upper bounds to non-convex settings and potentially, designing novel rigorous methods in this setting is a fascinating direction for future work.

B.1
Information-theoretic lower bound for stochastic inexact gradient model

We state and prove the information-theoretic lower bound for smooth costs.

Theorem B.1
.

(Information-theoretic Lower Bound)
Assume that
\varepsilon<1/200
and
\varepsilon\lesssim\delta^{2}
. Then there exists a family of smooth cost functions
\{{F}_{\theta}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}\}
parameterized by
\theta\in[-1,1]
with the following property. Suppose
A
is any algorithm that for each
\theta\in[-1,1]
uses at most
T
queries to stochastic inexact gradient oracle and outputs
{\bm{x}}_{T}^{\theta}
that is
\varepsilon
-accurate, i.e.,
\E{F}_{\theta}({\bm{x}}_{T}^{\theta})-\inf_{\bm{x}}{F}_{\theta}({\bm{x}})\leq\varepsilon
.
Then, there exists
\theta_{\sf bad}\in[-1,1]
such that
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-deviation (
2.3
(a)) is lower bounded by
\Omega(\frac{\delta^{2}}{T\varepsilon^{2}})
.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon>0
be a fixed small constant.
We consider the following family of cost functions
\{{F}_{\theta}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}\}
parametrized by
\theta\in[-1,1]
:

\displaystyle{F}_{\theta}(x)=\begin{cases}100\varepsilon\cdot(x-\theta)^{2},&\text{for}~x\in[-1,1],\\
100\varepsilon\cdot(1-\theta)^{2}+200\varepsilon(1-\theta)(x-1)&\text{for}~x>1\\
100\varepsilon\cdot(-1-\theta)^{2}+200\varepsilon(-1-\theta)(x+1)&\text{for}~x<-1.\end{cases}

(B.1)

Note that
{F}_{\theta}
is
{200\epsilon}
-smooth for each
\theta\in[-1,1]
.

We consider the following stochastic first order oracle for each
{F}_{\theta}
.
For a queried point
x
, the oracle outputs
g_{\theta}(x)
defined as

\displaystyle g_{\theta}(x)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{200\varepsilon}\nabla{F}_{\theta}(x)+z\quad\text{for }z\sim N(0,\frac{\delta^{2}}{2\cdot{200\epsilon}}),&\text{w.p.}~{200\epsilon}\,,\\
0,&\text{w.p.}~1-{200\epsilon}\,.\end{cases}

(B.2)

Since we define
{F}_{\theta}
as a linear extension outside
[-1,1]
, below we may assume that all gradient queries are made within
[-1,1]
.

Assuming that all queries are made in
[-1,1]
, we can rewrite (
B.2
) simply as

\displaystyle g_{\theta}(x)=(x-\theta+z)\cdot{\sf Sample}\,,

(B.3)

where
{\sf Sample}\sim\bern({200\epsilon})
.
Let us verify that this is a valid stochastic first order oracle. First, it is clear that

\displaystyle\E[g_{\theta}(x)]={200\epsilon}\cdot(x-\theta+0)+(1-{200\epsilon})\cdot 0={200\epsilon}\cdot(x-\theta)=\nabla{F}_{\theta}(x).

Next, for the variance, note that

\displaystyle\E\left[g_{\theta}(x)-\nabla{F}_{\theta}(x)\right]^{2}

\displaystyle=\E\left[(x-\theta+z)\cdot{\sf Sample}-\nabla{F}_{\theta}(x)\right]^{2}

\displaystyle={200\epsilon}\cdot\E\left[x-\theta+z-{200\epsilon}\cdot(x-\theta)\right]^{2}+(1-{200\epsilon})\cdot\left[-{200\epsilon}\cdot(x-\theta)\right]^{2}

\displaystyle={200\epsilon}(1-{200\epsilon})^{2}\cdot(x-\theta)^{2}+({200\epsilon})^{2}(1-{200\epsilon})\cdot(x-\theta)^{2}+{200\epsilon}\cdot\E[z^{2}]

\displaystyle={200\epsilon}(1-{200\epsilon})\cdot(x-\theta)^{2}+\delta^{2}/2.

Hence, the variance is always upper bounded by
\delta^{2}/2+{200\epsilon}(1-{200\epsilon})2^{2}\leq\delta^{2}
, as long as
\varepsilon\lesssim\delta^{2}
.

We now prove the theorem.
From the fact that the output
x^{\theta}_{T}
is
\varepsilon
-accurate, we have

\displaystyle\forall\theta\in[-1,1],\quad 100\varepsilon\cdot\E\left[x^{\theta}_{T}-\theta\right]^{2}\leq\varepsilon.

Using Jensen’s inequality, we know
\left|\E[x^{\theta}_{T}]-\theta\right|^{2}=\left|\E[x^{\theta}_{T}-\theta]\right|^{2}\leq\E[x^{\theta}_{T}-\theta]^{2}
which implies the following condition:

\displaystyle\forall\theta\in[-1,1],\quad\left|\E[x^{\theta}_{T}]-\theta\right|\leq 0.1.

(B.4)

This condition says if we regard
A
as an estimator of
\theta
for each
\theta
, the bias is less than equal to
0.1
.
For the following argument, we hence change our perspective and regard
A
as an estimator of
\theta
based on
T
inexact gradient queries rather than an optimization algorithm.

Let us fix
\theta\in[-1,1]
.
We first that we may assume that all gradient queries are made at the point
0
.
Indeed, from the expression for the stochastic oracle (
B.3
), we know

\displaystyle g_{\theta}(x)\overset{d}{=}g_{\theta}(0)+x\cdot{\sf Sample}.

In particular, this implies that one can reconstruct a gradient query at any point
x
based on a gradient query at the point
0
.
Hence, without loss of generality, we may assume that all gradient queries are made at
x=0
.

Hence, we can regard
A
as an estimator of
\theta
based on
T
independent measurements
y_{1},\dots,y_{T}
of form

\displaystyle y_{i}=(-\theta+z_{i})\cdot{\sf Sample}_{i},\quad i=1,\dots,T\,,

(B.5)

where
z_{i}\sim N(0,\frac{\delta^{2}}{{200\epsilon}})
and
{\sf Sample}_{i}\sim\bern({200\epsilon})
.
Now with this new perspective in mind, we can lower bound the variance of the estimator
\var({\bm{x}}_{T}^{\theta})
using the Cramer-Rao lower bound.
To that end, we first calculate the fisher information of the measurement distribution.

Recall that each measurement is of form

\displaystyle y=(-\theta+z)\cdot{\sf Sample}\,,

where
z\sim N(0,\frac{\delta^{2}}{2\cdot{200\epsilon}})
and
{\sf Sample}\sim\bern({200\epsilon})
. Then, the log likelihood is

\displaystyle\ell(\theta;y)

\displaystyle=\ln\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\cdot\frac{\delta^{2}}{2\cdot{200\epsilon}}}}\exp\left(-\frac{2\cdot{200\epsilon}}{\delta^{2}}\cdot\frac{(y+\theta)^{2}}{2}\right)\right]\mathbbm{1}\left\{{\sf Sample}=1\right\}+\delta_{[y=0]}\cdot\mathbbm{1}\left\{{\sf Sample}=0\right\}\,.

Taking derivatives of the log likelihood, we get

\displaystyle\nabla_{\theta}\ell(\theta;y)

\displaystyle=-\frac{2\cdot{200\epsilon}}{\delta^{2}}\cdot(\theta+y)\cdot\mathbbm{1}\left\{{\sf Sample}=1\right\}\,,

C.1
Stochastic inexact gradient model

The proofs of FOI lower bounds are quite technical and rely on delicate constructions, and to illustrate our general proof strategy, we first present a proof that is relatively simpler, yet captures the essence of the later complicated constructions.
More specifically, in this section, we will prove the following FOI lower bounds for smooth costs against stochastic inexact oracle.

Theorem C.1
.

(Lower Bound)

Let
\varepsilon>0
, and
T
be a given number of iterations. There exists an
O(1)
-smooth convex function
f:\mathbb{R}^{O(T)}\to\mathbb{R}
and a stochastic inexact gradient oracle such that any FOI algorithm
\mathcal{A}
that starts at
{\bm{x}}_{0}={\bm{0}}
has its
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-deviation lower bounded by
\Omega(\frac{\delta^{2}}{T\varepsilon^{2}})
.

Proof.

Now consider the cost
f:{\bm{x}}=({\bm{x}}^{\sf dum},y)\in\mathbb{R}^{T}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
defined as

\displaystyle f({\bm{x}})=4\varepsilon\cdot\mathcal{F}(y+1)\,,

(C.2)

where
\mathcal{F}
is defined in (
C.1
).
Here note that
{\bm{x}}^{\sf dum}\in\mathbb{R}^{T}
is dummy coordinates which do not appear in the cost
f
.
Next, we define the stochastic inexact oracle for
t=0,1,\dots,T-1
as

\displaystyle g({\bm{x}}_{t})=\nabla f({\bm{x}}_{t})+\delta r_{t}{\bm{e}}_{1+t}\quad\text{where}~r_{t}\sim\unif\{\pm 1\}\,,

(C.3)

where
{\bm{e}}_{j}
is the
j
-th coordinate vector.
Then, clearly this stochastic gradient fulfills the definition of stochastic inexact gradient oracle.
Here the stochastic gradient noises are designed such that during the
t
-th iteration, the noise is added to the coordinate
{\bm{x}}^{\sf dum}[1+t]
.
In other words, the noises will be added to each coordinate of
u
incrementally.

From here one, let us write iterates
{\bm{x}}_{t}=({\bm{x}}^{\sf dum}_{t},y_{t})
.
Note first that for
\varepsilon
-accuracy, it must be that
|y_{T}-y_{0}|\geq 1/2
; otherwise
f({\bm{x}}_{T})>4\varepsilon\cdot\mathcal{F}(0.5)=\varepsilon
; see
Figure 1
.
Based on the definition of FOI (see (
FOI
)), let us write

\displaystyle{\bm{x}}_{T}={\bm{x}}_{0}-\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\lambda_{t}^{(T)}g({\bm{x}}_{t})\,.

Then from the construction (
C.2
), we know that for any
{\bm{x}}
, we know
\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}({\bm{x}})\in[0,8\varepsilon]
. On the other hand, as we discussed, we need
|y_{T}-y_{0}|\geq 1/2
.
Hence, in order for iterates to move far enough from the starting point, the coefficients have to add up to a sufficiently large number:

\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}|\lambda_{t}^{(T)}|\geq\frac{1}{16\varepsilon}\,,

(C.4)

since otherwise,
|y_{T}-y_{0}|<8\varepsilon\cdot\frac{1}{16\varepsilon}=1/2
.

Now we will make use of the condition (
C.4
) to show that there is a large deviation in the coordinates
{\bm{x}}^{\sf dum}
.
More specifically, let us lower bound
\E\left\lVert{\bm{x}}^{\sf dum}_{T}-\E[{\bm{x}}^{\sf dum}_{T}]\right\rVert^{2}
. From the construction of inexact oracle (
C.3
), it follows that

\displaystyle\E\left\lVert{\bm{x}}^{\sf dum}_{T}-\E[{\bm{x}}^{\sf dum}_{T}]\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\E\left\lVert\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\lambda_{t}^{(T)}\delta r_{t}\cdot{\bm{e}}_{1+t}\right\rVert^{2}=\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}(\lambda_{t}^{(T)})^{2}\delta^{2}\E[r_{t}^{2}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}(\lambda_{t}^{(T)})^{2}\delta^{2}\overset{(a)}{\geq}\delta^{2}\cdot\frac{1}{T}\cdot\left(\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}|\lambda_{t}^{(T)}|\right)^{2}\gtrsim\frac{\delta^{2}}{T\varepsilon^{2}},

where (
a
) is due to Cauchy-Schwarz inequality.
This concludes the proof. ∎

Non-stochastic inexact gradient model

Theorem C.2
.

(Lower Bound)

Let
\varepsilon>0
be a small constant, and
T
be a given number of iterations. There exists a
O(1)
-smooth convex function

Proof.

With
\mathcal{F}
defined as (

\displaystyle f(x,y)=4\varepsilon\cdot\mathcal{F}(y+1).

(C.5)

Let us write the iterate as
{\bm{x}}_{t}=(x_{t},y_{t})

\varepsilon
-accuracy, it must be that

This means that in order to achieve

Now consider the following non-stochastic inexact oracle

C.4

Theorem C.4
.

(Lower Bound)

Let
\varepsilon>0
and
T

Proof.

For
{\bm{x}}=({\bm{x}}^{\sf dum},y)
where
{\bm{x}}^{\sf dum}\in\mathbb{R}^{T}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle f(x,y):=y+\frac{\mu}{2}y^{2}+\frac{\mu}{2}\left\lVert{\bm{x}}\right\rVert^{2}\,.

{\bm{x}}_{0}=(0,0,0,\dots,0)

\displaystyle\bm{g}({\bm{x}}_{t})=\nabla f({\bm{x}}_{t})+\delta r_{t}\cdot{\bm{e}}_{1+t}\quad\text{for}~r_{t}\sim\unif\{\pm 1\}\,,

(C.8)

where
{\bm{e}}_{j}
is the
j
-th coordinate vector.
Also, throughout the proof we use the notation:

\displaystyle{\bm{g}}^{\sf dum}_{t}:=\bm{g}({\bm{x}}^{\sf dum}_{t},y_{t})[1,2,\dots,T]\quad\text{and}\quad g^{y}_{t}:=\bm{g}({\bm{x}}^{\sf dum}_{t},y_{t})[T+1]\,.

Warm-up: the case of simplified gradient noises.

For a moment, we assume that the inexact gradient oracle is non-stochastic with

\displaystyle\bm{g}({\bm{x}}_{t})=\nabla f({\bm{x}}_{t})+\delta\cdot{\bm{e}}_{1+t}\,.

We consider this case first to build the key intuition of the proof.
The first prove the following result that is crucial for the proof.

Lemma C.5
.

For each
t=0,1,\dots,T-1
, the output of a FOI algorithm satisfies

\displaystyle\delta\cdot y_{t}=\sum_{i=1}^{T}x_{t}[i]\quad\text{and}\quad\delta\cdot g^{y}_{t}=\sum_{i=1}^{T}{\bm{g}}^{\sf dum}_{t}[i]\,,\quad\text{for each }t=0,1,2,\dots,T.

(C.9)

Proof.

We prove by induction on
t
.
The statement trivially holds for
t=0
.
Assume that the conclusion holds for some
t
.
We will first show that

\displaystyle\delta\cdot y_{t+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{T}x_{t+1}[i]\,.

(C.10)

By the definition of FOI (see (
FOI
)), we have

\displaystyle\delta\cdot y_{t+1}

\displaystyle=-\delta\cdot\sum_{j=0}^{t}\lambda^{(t+1)}_{j}g^{y}_{j}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{j=0}^{t}\lambda^{(t+1)}_{j}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{T}{\bm{g}}^{\sf dum}_{j}[i]\right)=-\sum_{i=1}^{T}\sum_{j=0}^{t}\lambda^{(t+1)}_{j}{\bm{g}}^{\sf dum}_{j}[i]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{T}x_{t+1}[i].

This completes the proof of (
C.10
). Next, we will show that

\displaystyle\delta\cdot g^{y}_{t+1}=\sum_{i=1}^{T}{\bm{g}}^{\sf dum}_{t+1}[i].

(C.11)

This follows because

\displaystyle\delta\cdot g^{y}_{t+1}

\displaystyle=\delta(1+\mu y_{t+1})=\delta+\mu\left(\sum_{i=1}^{T}{\bm{x}}^{\sf dum}_{t+1}[i]\right)

\displaystyle\overset{(a)}{=}{\bm{g}}^{\sf dum}_{t+1}[t+2]+\sum_{i=1}^{t+1}{\bm{g}}^{\sf dum}_{t+1}[i]=\sum_{i=1}^{T}{\bm{g}}^{\sf dum}_{t+1}[i],

where (
a

{\bm{x}}^{\sf dum}_{t+1}[i]=0
for all
i>t+1
.
∎

By Lemma
C.5
, it holds that

\displaystyle\delta\cdot y_{T}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{T}{\bm{x}}^{\sf dum}_{T}[i]

(C.12)

On the other hand, in order to achieve
\varepsilon
-suboptimality, we need
y_{T}^{2}\gtrsim\frac{1}{\mu^{2}}
.
Moreover, for
\varepsilon
-suboptimality, we also need
\sum_{i=1}^{T}{\bm{x}}^{\sf dum}_{T}[i]^{2}\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon}{\mu}
.
Therefore, letting
{\bm{x}}_{T}^{\sf exact}=(({\bm{x}}^{\sf dum}_{T})^{\sf exact},y_{T}^{\sf exact})
be the iterate with exact gradients, since

\displaystyle\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{T}-{\bm{x}}_{T}^{\sf exact}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\left\lVert{\bm{x}}^{\sf dum}_{T}-({\bm{x}}^{\sf dum}_{T})^{\sf exact}\right\rVert^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{T}{\bm{x}}^{\sf dum}_{T}[i]^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{T}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{T}{\bm{x}}^{\sf dum}_{T}[i]\right)^{2}=\frac{1}{T}\cdot\delta^{2}\cdot\left(y_{T}\right)^{2}\gtrsim\frac{\delta^{2}}{T\mu^{2}}.

This is precisely equal to the desired lower bound.

Actual proof for the stochastic noise case.

Now coming back to the stochastic inexact gradient (

\displaystyle\begin{split}\delta\cdot|y_{t}|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{T}|{\bm{x}}^{\sf dum}_{t}[i]|\quad\text{and}\quad\delta\cdot|g^{y}_{t}|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{T}|{\bm{g}}^{\sf dum}_{t}[i]|\,,\end{split}\quad\text{for each }t=0,1,2,\dots,T.

C.6
Inexact initialization model (strongly convex costs)

Theorem C.7
.

(Lower Bound)

Let
\varepsilon>0
be a small constant, and
T
be a given number of iterations. There exists a

-smooth
\mu
-strongly convex function
f:\mathbb{R}^{\Omega(T)}\to\mathbb{R}
such that for any FOI algorithm
\mathcal{A}
the
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-deviation lower bounded by
\Omega(\exp(-\Omega(T))\delta^{2}\wedge\frac{\varepsilon}{\mu})
w.r.t. the reference point

Proof.

We use the construction in
[
Nesterov 2018
, Theorem 2.1.13]
.
In particular, for simplicity, we consider the construction for the infinite dimensional Hilbert space
\ell_{2}
as it simplifies the proof; in fact, a similar argument works for
\mathbb{R}^{\Omega(T)}

Let us recall the construction (we follow the presentation in
[
Bubeck 2014

).
Let
A:\ell_{2}\rightarrow\ell_{2}
be the linear operator that corresponds to the infinite tri-diagonal matrix with
2
on the diagonal and
-1
on the upper and lower diagonals.
For some constant
\kappa\geq 1

\mu
-strongly convex cost:

\displaystyle f^{\sf Nes}(x)=\frac{\mu(\kappa-1)}{8}\left(\langle Ax,x\rangle-2\langle{\bm{e}}_{1},x\rangle\right)+\frac{\mu}{2}\left\lVert x\right\rVert^{2}\quad\text{and}\quad q:=\frac{\sqrt{\kappa}-1}{\sqrt{\kappa}+1}.

For the zero initialization
x_{0}=(0,0,\dots)\in\ell_{2}
, the cost satisfies the following properties (see the proof of
[
Nesterov 2018
, Theorem 2.1.13]
):

•

Output of any FOI satisfies
x_{t}[i]=0,\forall i\geq t
.

•

x^{*}[i]=q^{i}
.

•

\left\lVert x_{0}-x^{*}\right\rVert^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(x^{*}[i])^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}q^{2i}=\frac{q^{2}}{1-q^{2}}
.

•

\left\lVert x_{t}-x^{*}\right\rVert^{2}\geq\sum_{i=k+1}^{\infty}q^{2i}=\frac{q^{2(t+1)}}{1-q^{2}}=q^{2t}\left\lVert x_{0}-x^{*}\right\rVert^{2}
.

Now we consider the following cost function: for
{\bm{x}}=(x,y)\in\ell_{2}\times\ell_{2}

\displaystyle f({\bm{x}})=f^{\sf Nes}(x)+f^{\sf Nes}(y)\,,

and we consider the two initializations:

\displaystyle{\bm{x}}_{0}^{\sf ref}

\displaystyle=\Big((0,0,0,\dots),(q,q^{2},q^{3},\dots)\Big)

\displaystyle{\bm{x}}_{0}

\displaystyle=\Big((0,0,0,\dots),(q,q^{2},q^{3},\dots,q^{L},0,0,\dots)\Big)

for
L=\Omega(\log(1/\delta))
is chosen such that

.
Then, it follow that
\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{0}-{\bm{x}}_{0}^{\sf ref}\right\rVert=\frac{q^{L+1}}{1-q}\leq\delta
.
On the other hand, it follows from the above property that

\displaystyle\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}_{t}^{\sf ref}\right\rVert^{2}\geq q^{t}\frac{q^{2L+1}}{1-q^{2}}\approx q^{t}\cdot\delta^{2}.

Hence, as long as
\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}^{*}\right\rVert^{2},\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t}^{\sf ref}-{\bm{x}}^{*}\right\rVert^{2}\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon}{\mu}
, the deviation lower bound follows.
∎

Appendix D
Proof of lower bounds (nonsmooth costs)

In this section, we present the proofs of lower bounds for nonsmooth costs.
The proof will be based on more complicated constructions than those for the case of smooth costs, so before we dive into the proofs, we first present some intuition behind the constructions.

D.1
Warm-up: lower bound against GD

In this section, as a warm-up, we will prove a (weaker) lower bound for a simplified setting.
In particular, we prove the lower bound against gradient descent (GD).
Formally, in the definition of
FOI
, we restrict that
\lambda^{(t)}_{i}\equiv\lambda_{i}
(i.e., the coefficient is a positive number does not depend on the iterations
t
). In other words, for
\lambda_{i}
,
i=0,1,\dots,T-1
,

\displaystyle{\bm{x}}_{t}={\bm{x}}_{0}-\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}\lambda_{i}g({\bm{x}}_{i})\quad\text{for each $t=1,2,\dots,T$}.

(D.1)

Note that this is precisely GD with step sizes
\lambda_{t}
’s.
For the lower bound construction, let
{\bm{x}}=({\bm{x}}^{\sf err},w)\in\mathbb{R}^{T}\times\mathbb{R}
and consider the cost

\displaystyle f({\bm{x}}^{\sf err},w)=\underbrace{\max\left\{0,~\max_{i=1,\dots,T}\left\{{\bm{x}}^{\sf err}[i]\right\}\right\}}_{=:G({\bm{x}}^{\sf err})}+\underbrace{2\varepsilon\cdot\max\{w+1,0\}}_{=:\ell(w)}\,.

Since the above cost function is nonsmooth, we specify the subgradient oracle as follows: for both max terms above, we consider the subgradient oracle that outputs the subgradient corresponding to the first argument that achieves the maximum.
Note that
\inf_{{\bm{x}}}f({\bm{x}})=0
.
Consider the zero initialization, i.e.,
({\bm{x}}^{\sf err}_{0},w_{0})=({\bm{0}},0)
, and we write the iterates as
{\bm{x}}_{t}:=({\bm{x}}^{\sf err}_{t},w_{t})\in\mathbb{R}^{T}\times\mathbb{R}
.

For intuition, we describe the role of each coordinate:

\blacksquare

The first
T
coordinates,
{\bm{x}}^{\sf err}\in\mathbb{R}^{T}
, correspond to the part where the errors due to inexact oracle are added.

\blacksquare

The last coordinate,
w\in\mathbb{R}
, governs the overall cost; in order to achieve
\varepsilon
-accuracy, the optimization algorithm has to decrease coordinate
w
by at least
1/2
.

The proof proceeds by considering two different scenarios:

Scenario 1 (exact gradients).

Consider the case where there is no noise in the gradients, i.e.,
g({\bm{x}}_{t})=\nabla f({\bm{x}}_{t})
for all
t
.
Then, since
{\bm{x}}^{\sf err}_{0}=\bm{0}
, it follows that
\nabla G({\bm{x}}^{\sf err}_{t})=\bm{0}
for all
t
.
Hence the algorithm will only update coordinate
w_{t}
.
Note that
\ell(w_{0})=2\varepsilon
, and hence in order to achieve
\ell(w_{T})\leq\varepsilon
, it must be that
w_{T}\leq-1/2
.
On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial w}\ell(w)=0~~\text{or}~~2\varepsilon\quad\text{for any}~~w\in\mathbb{R}.

Hence, in order to achieve
w_{T}\leq-1/2
, it must be that

\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\lambda_{t}\geq\Omega(1/\varepsilon).

(D.2)

This condition is analogous to (
C.4
) from the lower bound proof for smooth costs (
Subsection C.1
).

Scenario 2 (inexact gradients).

Now let us consider the case where the gradient error during the
t
-th iteration is non-stochastic and equal to
-\delta{\bm{e}}_{t}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle g({\bm{x}}_{t})=\nabla f({\bm{x}}_{t})-\delta{\bm{e}}_{t+1}\quad\text{for all $t=0,1,\dots,T-1$}.

Here
{\bm{e}}_{t}
denotes the
t
-th coordinate vector.
Let us assume that
\delta
is much smaller than all the step sizes
\lambda_{t}
, in particular, such that
\lambda_{i}\delta\ll\lambda_{i+1}
for all
i=0,\dots,T-2
.
Then from GD iterations defined as (
D.1
), one can deduce that

\displaystyle{\bm{x}}^{\sf err}_{t}=(-\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{0}\delta,\ -\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{1}\delta,\ \cdots,\ -\lambda_{t-1}+\lambda_{t-2}\delta,\ +\lambda_{t}\delta,0,\dots,0)\,.

Thus, the following estimate on the deviation holds:

\displaystyle\left\lVert{\bm{x}}^{\sf err}_{T}\right\rVert^{2}=\left\lVert\sum_{t=1}^{T-1}(\lambda_{t}-\lambda_{t-1}\delta){\bm{e}}_{t}+\lambda_{T-1}\delta{\bm{e}}_{T}\right\rVert^{2}=\sum_{t=1}^{T-1}(\lambda_{t}-\lambda_{t-1}\delta)^{2}\approx\sum_{t=1}^{T-1}\lambda_{t}^{2}\,.

(D.3)

Combining the two scenarios.

Thus far, we have obtained (
D.2
) and (
D.3
)
from the two different scenarios.
The condition (
D.2
) shows that in order to achieve
\varepsilon
-suboptimality, stepsizes have to add up to a large number, more precisely,
\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\lambda_{t}=\Omega(1/\varepsilon)
.
On the other hand, (
D.3
) characterizes that the deviation is on the order of the quantity
\sum_{t=1}^{T-1}\lambda_{t}^{2}
.
In order to formally connect these two conditions, we make the following assumption:

\displaystyle\lambda_{0}\leq O\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T-1}\lambda_{t}\right)\,.

(D.4)

Then with this assumption, one obtain the following deviation bound:

\displaystyle\left\lVert{\bm{x}}^{\sf err}_{T}\right\rVert^{2}\approx\sum_{t=1}^{T-1}(\lambda^{(T)}_{t})^{2}\overset{(a)}{\geq}\frac{1}{T-1}\cdot\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T-1}\lambda^{(T)}_{t}\right)^{2}\gtrsim\frac{1}{T-1}\cdot\left(\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\lambda^{(T)}_{t}\right)^{2}\gtrsim\frac{1}{T\varepsilon^{2}}\,,

where
(a)
is due to the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality.
This is precisely the desired lower bound.

For the lower bound against the entire class of FOI, there are some other technical challenges arising from the fact that the coefficients
\lambda^{(t)}_{i}
’s not only depend on
t
, but also could take negative values.
We need a more elaborate lower bound construction, as we explain in the subsequent subsections.

D.2
Helper function

Before actual proofs, we introduce a helper function that we will use throughout the proofs of FOI lower bounds.
Let

\chi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a non-smooth convex function defined as
\chi(x):=\max\{x,0\}
and the subgradients are defined as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\nabla_{x}\chi(x)&:=\begin{cases}+1,&\text{if }x\geq 0,\\
0,&\text{if }x<y.\end{cases}\end{split}

(D.5)

The choice of subgradient
+1
at the origin will play a crucial role in the later proofs.
For
{\bm{x}},{\bm{y}},{\bm{z}}\in\mathbb{R}^{T}
and
v\in\mathbb{R}
, let
\mathcal{G}:({\bm{x}},{\bm{y}},{\bm{z}})\in\mathbb{R}^{3T}\to\mathbb{R}
be defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}({\bm{x}},{\bm{y}},{\bm{z}})

\displaystyle:=\max\{0,~\mathcal{K}({\bm{x}},{\bm{y}},{\bm{z}})\}\quad\text{and}

(D.6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}({\bm{x}},{\bm{y}},{\bm{z}})

\displaystyle:=\max_{i=1,\dots,T}\Big\{\chi({\bm{y}}[i])+\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\frac{\left|{\bm{x}}[j]\right|}{2^{j-1}}+\frac{{\bm{x}}[i]}{2^{i-1}},~~\chi({\bm{z}}[i])+\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\frac{\left|{\bm{x}}[j]\right|}{2^{j-1}}-\frac{{\bm{x}}[i]}{2^{i-1}}\Big\}\,.

(D.7)

Then
\mathcal{G}
is clearly convex, as it is the maximum of convex functions.

We specify the subgradients of
\mathcal{G}
as follows: for all max terms in (
D.8
), we get the subgradient of the first argument that achieves the maximum.
Then
\mathcal{G}
is
O(1)
-Lipschitz: for any
{\bm{x}},{\bm{y}},{\bm{z}}\in\mathbb{R}^{T}
,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\nabla\mathcal{G}({\bm{x}},{\bm{y}},{\bm{z}})\right\rVert^{2}\leq 1+\sum_{j=1}^{T}(\frac{1}{2^{j-1}})^{2}\leq 1+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{4^{j-1}}\leq 1+4/3\,.

D.3
Stochastic inexact gradient model

Theorem D.1
.

(Lower Bound)

Let

There exists a

-deviation lower bounded by

Proof.

For

\displaystyle f({\bm{x}}^{\sf err},{\bm{y}},{\bm{z}},w)=\mathcal{G}({\bm{x}}^{\sf err},{\bm{y}},{\bm{z}})+\underbrace{2\varepsilon\cdot\max\{w+1,0\}}_{=:\ell(w)}\,,

(D.8)

where

is convex since both

For intuition, we describe the role of each coordinate as we did in the warm-up section (

\blacksquare

The first
T
coordinates,
{\bm{x}}^{\sf err}\in\mathbb{R}^{T}

\blacksquare

The next
2T

{\bm{y}},{\bm{z}}\in\mathbb{R}^{T}

\displaystyle g({\bm{x}}_{t})=\nabla f({\bm{x}}_{t})+\delta r_{t}\cdot{\bm{e}}_{1+t}\quad\text{for $r_{t}\overset{iid}{\sim}\unif\{\pm 1\}$},

(D.9)

Remark D.2

Note that

Lemma D.3
.

Under the inexact gradient (

Hence, we have

\displaystyle\E\left\lVert{\bm{y}}_{T}-\E[{\bm{y}}_{T}]\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{t=1}^{T-1}\E\left({\bm{y}}_{T}[t]-\E{\bm{y}}_{T}[t]\right)^{2}=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t=1,2,\dots,T-1\\
~\text{s.t.}~i_{t}=i\end{subarray}}\lambda^{(T)}_{t}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\overset{(a)}{\geq}\frac{1}{4}\cdot\frac{1}{|\mathcal{I}|}\cdot\left(\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t=1,2,\dots,T-1\\
~\text{s.t.}~i_{t}=i\end{subarray}}\lambda^{(T)}_{t}\right)^{2}=\frac{1}{4}\cdot\frac{1}{|\mathcal{I}|}\cdot\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T-1}\lambda^{(T)}_{t}\right)^{2}\overset{\eqref{large_coeff_nonsmooth}}{\gtrsim}\frac{1}{T\varepsilon^{2}}.

Here
(a)
follows from Cauchy-Schwarz inequality.
Therefore, we get the desired deviation bound as follows:

\displaystyle\E\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{T}-\E[{\bm{x}}_{T}]\right\rVert^{2}\geq\E\left\lVert{\bm{y}}_{T}-\E[{\bm{y}}_{T}]\right\rVert^{2}\gtrsim\frac{1}{T\varepsilon^{2}}.

∎

D.4
Non-stochastic inexact gradient model

Theorem D.4
.

(Lower Bound)

Let
\varepsilon>0
, and
T
be a given number of iterations. There exists a
O(1)
-Lipschitz and nonsmooth convex function
f:\mathbb{R}^{O(T)}\to\mathbb{R}
with a non-stochastic inexact gradient oracle such that for any FOI algorithm
\mathcal{A}
that satisfies
|\lambda_{0}^{(T)}|\leq O\left(\left|\sum_{t=1}^{T-1}\lambda^{(T)}_{t}\right|\right)
and starts at
{\bm{x}}_{0}={\bm{0}}
has a minimum of
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-deviation of
\Omega(\frac{1}{T\varepsilon^{2}}+\frac{\delta^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}})
.

Proof.

We consider an almost identical construction to the one considered in the proof of
Theorem D.1
, namely (
D.8
).
The only difference is that now we add an extra dummy coordinate, namely the
(3T+2)
-th coordinate, which does not appear in the cost. Let us denote this dummy coordinate by
u
.
Concretely, we consider the following cost: For
{\bm{x}}=({\bm{x}}^{\sf err},{\bm{y}},{\bm{z}},w,u)
where
{\bm{x}}^{\sf err},{\bm{y}},{\bm{z}}\in\mathbb{R}^{T}
and
w,u\in\mathbb{R}
, consider the cost

\displaystyle f({\bm{x}}^{\sf err},{\bm{y}},{\bm{z}},w,u)=\mathcal{G}({\bm{x}}^{\sf err},{\bm{y}},{\bm{z}})+\underbrace{2\varepsilon\cdot\max\{w+1,0\}}_{=:\ell(w)}\,.

(D.15)

We denote the iterates of FOI due to
exact
gradients
by

{\bm{x}}_{t}^{\sf exact}=(({\bm{x}}^{\sf err}_{t})^{\sf exact},{\bm{y}}_{t}^{\sf exact},{\bm{z}}_{t}^{\sf exact},w_{t}^{\sf exact},u_{t}^{\sf exact})\,.

Now we define the inexact gradient oracle. The noise in the inexact gradient oracle consists of two parts. For
t=0,1,2,\dots,T-1
,

\displaystyle g({\bm{x}}_{t})=\nabla f({\bm{x}}_{t})+\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{2}}{\bm{e}}_{1+t}+\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{2}}{\bm{e}}_{3T+2}\,,

(D.16)

where
{\bm{e}}_{j}
is the
j
-th coordinate vector.
Note that the first part of the error is similar to the error for the stochastic error case, and the second part of the error is added to the dummy coordinate
u
.

Then analogous to
D.3
, one can establish the following result.
We skip the proof since it is very analogous to that of
D.3
.

Corollary D.5
.

Under the inexact gradient (
D.9
), the subgradient
\nabla\mathcal{G}
has the following properties:

\blacksquare

For each
t=1,2,\dots,T-1
, there exists
i_{t}\in\{1,\dots,t\}
such that either one of the following holds:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\frac{\partial}{\partial{\bm{y}}[i_{t}]}\mathcal{G}({\bm{x}}^{\sf err}_{t},{\bm{y}}_{t},{\bm{z}}_{t})=1,~~\frac{\partial}{\partial{\bm{z}}[i_{t}]}\mathcal{G}({\bm{x}}^{\sf err}_{t},{\bm{y}}_{t},{\bm{z}}_{t})=0,\quad\text{or}\\
\frac{\partial}{\partial{\bm{y}}[i_{t}]}\mathcal{G}({\bm{x}}^{\sf err}_{t},{\bm{y}}_{t},{\bm{z}}_{t})=0,~~\frac{\partial}{\partial{\bm{z}}[i_{t}]}\mathcal{G}({\bm{x}}^{\sf err}_{t},{\bm{y}}_{t},{\bm{z}}_{t})=1.\end{cases}

Moreover, for
i\neq i_{t}
,
\frac{\partial}{\partial{\bm{y}}[i]}\mathcal{G}({\bm{x}}^{\sf err}_{t},{\bm{y}}_{t},{\bm{z}}_{t})=\frac{\partial}{\partial{\bm{z}}[i]}\mathcal{G}({\bm{x}}^{\sf err}_{t},{\bm{y}}_{t},{\bm{z}}_{t})=0
.

\blacksquare

If
i_{t}\neq t
(i.e.,
i_{t}<t
), then
i_{t}=i_{t^{\prime}}
for some
t^{\prime}<t
, and it holds that
\frac{\partial}{\partial{\bm{y}}[i_{t}]}\mathcal{G}({\bm{x}}^{\sf err}_{t},{\bm{y}}_{t},{\bm{z}}_{t})=\frac{\partial}{\partial{\bm{y}}[i_{t^{\prime}}]}\mathcal{G}({\bm{x}}^{\sf err}_{t^{\prime}},{\bm{y}}_{t^{\prime}},{\bm{z}}_{t^{\prime}})
and
\frac{\partial}{\partial{\bm{z}}[i_{t}]}\mathcal{G}({\bm{x}}^{\sf err}_{t},{\bm{y}}_{t},{\bm{z}}_{t})=\frac{\partial}{\partial{\bm{z}}[i_{t^{\prime}}]}\mathcal{G}({\bm{x}}^{\sf err}_{t^{\prime}},{\bm{y}}_{t^{\prime}},{\bm{z}}_{t^{\prime}})
.

The rest of the proof is similar to that of
Theorem D.1
.
From the construction (
D.15
), we know that
f({\bm{x}}_{0})=2\varepsilon
.
In order to achieve
\varepsilon
-accuracy, one can similarly deduce that (
D.12
) holds.

With
i_{t}
defined in the statement of
D.5
,
let

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}:=\left\{i_{t}\in\{1,2,\dots,T\}~:~t\in\mathcal{T}\right\}\,.

Then one can similarly argue using
D.5
that for each
i\in\mathcal{I}
, either

\displaystyle{\bm{y}}_{T}[i]=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t=1,2,\dots,T-1\\
~\text{s.t.}~i_{t}=i\end{subarray}}\lambda^{(T)}_{t}\quad\text{or}\quad{\bm{z}}_{T}[i]=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t=1,2,\dots,T-1\\
~\text{s.t.}~i_{t}=i\end{subarray}}\lambda^{(T)}_{t}\,.

This, together with the fact that
{\bm{y}}_{T}^{\sf exact}={\bm{0}}
and
{\bm{z}}_{T}^{\sf exact}={\bm{0}}
, implies that

\displaystyle\left\lVert{\bm{y}}_{T}-{\bm{y}}_{T}^{\sf exact}\right\rVert^{2}+\left\lVert{\bm{z}}_{T}-{\bm{z}}_{T}^{\sf exact}\right\rVert^{2}\geq\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t=1,2,\dots,T-1\\
~\text{s.t.}~i_{t}=i\end{subarray}}\lambda^{(T)}_{t}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\overset{(a)}{\geq}\frac{1}{|\mathcal{I}|}\cdot\left(\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t=1,2,\dots,T-1\\
~\text{s.t.}~i_{t}=i\end{subarray}}\lambda^{(T)}_{t}\right)^{2}=\frac{1}{4}\cdot\frac{1}{|\mathcal{I}|}\cdot\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T-1}\lambda^{(T)}_{t}\right)^{2}\overset{\eqref{large_coeff_nonsmooth}}{\gtrsim}\frac{1}{T\varepsilon^{2}}.

On the other hand, since

\displaystyle|u_{T}-u_{T}^{\sf exact}|^{2}=\left(\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{2}}\cdot\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\lambda^{(T)}_{t}\right)^{2}\gtrsim\frac{\delta^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\,.

\displaystyle\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{T}-{\bm{x}}_{T}^{\sf exact}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\left\lVert{\bm{y}}_{T}-{\bm{y}}_{T}^{\sf exact}\right\rVert^{2}+\left\lVert{\bm{z}}_{T}-{\bm{z}}_{T}^{\sf exact}\right\rVert^{2}+\left\lVert u_{T}-u_{T}^{\sf exact}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\gtrsim\frac{1}{T\varepsilon^{2}}+\frac{\delta^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\,,

as desired.
∎

D.5
Inexact initialization model

Theorem D.6
.

(Lower Bound)

Let
\varepsilon>0
be a small constant, and
T
be a given number of iterations. There exists a
O(1)
-Lipschitz (nonsmooth) convex function
f:\mathbb{R}^{O(T)}\to\mathbb{R}
such that for any FOI algorithm
\mathcal{A}
the
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-deviation lower bounded by
\Omega(\frac{1}{T\varepsilon^{2}}+\delta^{2})
w.r.t. the reference point
{\bm{x}}_{0}^{\sf ref}={\bm{0}}
.

Proof.

For the initialization error model, we use a simpler construction.
For
{\bm{x}}=({\bm{x}}^{\sf err},{\bm{y}},w,u)\in(\mathbb{R}^{T})^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
, consider the cost defined as

\displaystyle f({\bm{x}}^{\sf err},{\bm{y}},w)=\max\{0,~{\bm{x}}^{\sf err}[1]+{\bm{y}}[1],~\dots,~{\bm{x}}^{\sf err}[T]+{\bm{y}}[T]\}+2\varepsilon\cdot\max\{w+1,0\}\,.

Here
u
is a dummy coordinate that does not appear in the cost.
For both max terms above, we consider the subgradient that outputs the gradient of the first argument that achieves the maximum. Then,
f
is clearly
O(1)
-Lipschitz.

We set the reference and inexact intializations as follows:

\displaystyle{\bm{x}}_{0}^{\sf ref}=(0,0,\dots,0)\quad\text{and}\quad{\bm{x}}_{0}=(\underbrace{\delta/\sqrt{2T},\cdots,\delta/\sqrt{2T}}_{\text{first $T$}},\underbrace{0,\cdots,0}_{\text{second $T$}},0,\delta/\sqrt{2})\,.

(D.17)

Following the previous notations, we write the iterates as
{\bm{x}}_{t}=({\bm{x}}^{\sf err}_{t},{\bm{y}}_{t},w_{t})\in(\mathbb{R}^{T})^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
.
Moreover, we will write the iterates corresponding to the reference initialization as

{\bm{x}}_{t}^{\sf ref}=(({\bm{x}}^{\sf err}_{t})^{\sf ref},{\bm{y}}_{t}^{\sf ref},w_{t}^{\sf ref})
.

From the fact that the algorithm has to achieve
\varepsilon
-suboptimality, it follows that
w_{T}\leq-1/2
.
On the other hand, from the inexact initialization
(
D.17
), it holds that for all
t=0,\dots,T-1
,

\displaystyle\exists i_{t}\in\{1,\dots,T\}~s.t.~\begin{cases}\frac{\partial}{\partial{\bm{y}}[i_{t}]}f({\bm{x}}^{\sf err}_{t},{\bm{y}}_{t},w_{t})=1&\text{and}\\
\frac{\partial}{\partial{\bm{y}}[i]}f({\bm{x}}^{\sf err}_{t},{\bm{y}}_{t},w_{t})=0&\text{for }i\neq i_{t}.\end{cases}

Hence, it holds that
\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}w_{T}=\sum_{i=1}^{T}{\bm{y}}_{T}[i]
.
Since we know
{\bm{y}}_{T}^{\sf ref}=(0,0,\dots,0)
and
u^{\sf ref}=0
, the following deviation lower bound holds:

\displaystyle\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{T}-{\bm{x}}_{T}^{\sf ref}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\left\lVert{\bm{y}}_{T}-{\bm{y}}_{T}^{\sf ref}\right\rVert^{2}+\left\lVert u_{T}-u_{T}^{\sf ref}\right\rVert^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{T}({\bm{y}}_{T}[i])^{2}+u_{T}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{T}\cdot\left(\sum_{i=1}^{T}{\bm{y}}_{T}[i]\right)^{2}+\frac{\delta^{2}}{2}=\frac{1}{T}\cdot\frac{1}{4\varepsilon^{2}}\cdot\left(w_{T}\right)^{2}+\frac{\delta^{2}}{2}\gtrsim\frac{1}{T\varepsilon^{2}}+\delta^{2}

This completes the proof.
∎

D.6
Stochastic inexact gradient model (strongly-convex costs)

Theorem D.7
.

(Lower Bound)

Let
\varepsilon>0
and
T
be a given number of iterations. There exists a
O(1)
-Lipschitz (nonsmooth) and
\mu
-strongly convex function
f:\mathbb{R}^{O(T)}\to\mathbb{R}
with a stochastic inexact gradient model such that any FOI algorithm
\mathcal{A}
that starts at
{\bm{x}}_{0}={\bm{0}}
has its
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-deviation lower bounded by
\Omega(\frac{1}{T\mu^{2}}\wedge\frac{\varepsilon}{\mu})
.

Proof.

For
{\bm{x}}=({\bm{x}}^{\sf err},{\bm{y}},{\bm{z}},w)\in(\mathbb{R}^{T})^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
, consider the cost defined as

\displaystyle f({\bm{x}}^{\sf err},{\bm{y}},{\bm{z}},w)=\underbrace{\mathcal{G}({\bm{x}}^{\sf err}+\delta{\bm{e}}_{1},{\bm{y}},{\bm{z}})+\frac{\mu}{2}\left\lVert({\bm{x}}^{\sf err},{\bm{y}},{\bm{z}})\right\rVert^{2}}_{=:\mathcal{G}^{\mu}({\bm{x}}^{\sf err},{\bm{y}},{\bm{z}})}+\underbrace{w+\frac{\mu}{2}w^{2}}_{=:\ell^{\mu}(w)}\,.

(D.18)

We consider the same inexact gradient oracle defined in (
D.9
).
Then similarly to

D.3
, it holds that for each
t=1,2,\dots,T-1
, there exists
i_{t}\in\{1,\dots,t\}
such that the following holds:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\frac{\partial}{\partial{\bm{y}}[i_{t}]}\mathcal{G}({\bm{x}}^{\sf err}_{t},{\bm{y}}_{t},{\bm{z}}_{t},v_{t})=1,~~\frac{\partial}{\partial{\bm{z}}[i_{t}]}\mathcal{G}({\bm{x}}^{\sf err}_{t},{\bm{y}}_{t},{\bm{z}}_{t},v_{t})=0&\text{with probability }$1/2$,\\
\frac{\partial}{\partial{\bm{y}}[i_{t}]}\mathcal{G}({\bm{x}}^{\sf err}_{t},{\bm{y}}_{t},{\bm{z}}_{t},v_{t})=0,~~\frac{\partial}{\partial{\bm{z}}[i_{t}]}\mathcal{G}({\bm{x}}^{\sf err}_{t},{\bm{y}}_{t},{\bm{z}}_{t},v_{t})=1&\text{with probability }$1/2$.\end{cases}

(D.19)

Moreover, for
i\neq i_{t}
,
\frac{\partial}{\partial{\bm{y}}[i]}\mathcal{G}({\bm{x}}^{\sf err}_{t},{\bm{y}}_{t},{\bm{z}}_{t},v_{t})=\frac{\partial}{\partial{\bm{z}}[i]}\mathcal{G}({\bm{x}}^{\sf err}_{t},{\bm{y}}_{t},{\bm{z}}_{t},v_{t})=0
.
Throughout the rest of the proof, we use the following notations:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\bm{g}^{\bm{y}}_{t}=(\bm{g}^{\bm{y}}_{t}[1],\bm{g}^{\bm{y}}_{t}[2],\dots,\bm{g}^{\bm{y}}_{t}[T]):=\nabla_{\bm{y}}\mathcal{G}^{\mu}({\bm{x}}^{\sf err}_{t},{\bm{y}}_{t},{\bm{z}}_{t},v_{t}),\\
\bm{g}^{\bm{z}}_{t}=(\bm{g}^{\bm{z}}_{t}[1],\bm{g}^{\bm{z}}_{t}[2],\dots,\bm{g}^{\bm{z}}_{t}[T]):=\nabla_{\bm{z}}\mathcal{G}^{\mu}({\bm{x}}^{\sf err}_{t},{\bm{y}}_{t},{\bm{z}}_{t},v_{t})\\
g^{w}_{t}:=\frac{\partial\ell^{\mu}}{\partial w}(w_{t})\end{cases}

We prove the following crucial result for the proof.

Lemma D.8
.

For each
t=0,1,2,\dots,T
, the output of a FOI algorithm satisfies the following:

\displaystyle\begin{split}w_{t}=\sum_{i=1}^{T}{\bm{y}}_{t}[i]+{\bm{z}}_{t}[i]\quad\text{and}\quad g^{w}_{t}=g^{v}_{t}+\sum_{i=1}^{T}\bm{g}^{\bm{y}}_{t}[i]+\bm{g}^{\bm{z}}_{t}[i]\,.\end{split}

(D.20)

Proof.

We prove by induction on
t
.
We first prove the statement for
t=0
.
Recall the definition
\mathcal{G}^{\mu}({\bm{x}}^{\sf err},{\bm{y}},{\bm{z}}):=\mathcal{G}({\bm{x}}^{\sf err}+\delta{\bm{e}}_{1},{\bm{y}},{\bm{z}})+\frac{\mu}{2}\left\lVert({\bm{x}}^{\sf err},{\bm{y}},{\bm{z}})\right\rVert^{2}
. Since the first coordinate of
{\bm{x}}^{\sf err}
is
\delta
, it follows that
g^{w}_{0}=1
,
\bm{g}^{\bm{y}}_{0}={\bm{e}}_{1}
and
\bm{g}^{\bm{z}}_{0}=\bm{0}
. Hence, the statement holds for
t=0
.

Assume that the conclusion holds for some
t
.
We will first show that
w_{t+1}=\sum_{i=1}^{T}{\bm{y}}_{t+1}[i]+{\bm{z}}_{t+1}[i]
.
Using the definition of
FOI
together with the inductive hypothesis, we have

\displaystyle w_{t+1}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{j=0}^{t}\lambda^{(t+1)}_{j}g^{w}_{j}=-\sum_{j=0}^{t}\lambda^{(t+1)}_{j}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{T}\bm{g}^{\bm{y}}_{j}[i]+\bm{g}^{\bm{z}}_{j}[i]\right)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i=1}^{T}\sum_{j=0}^{t}\lambda^{(t+1)}_{j}\left(\bm{g}^{\bm{y}}_{j}[i]+\bm{g}^{\bm{z}}_{j}[i]\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{T}{\bm{y}}_{t+1}[i]+{\bm{z}}_{t+1}[i].

Next, we show that
g^{w}_{t+1}=\sum_{i=1}^{T}\bm{g}^{\bm{y}}_{t+1}[i]+\bm{g}^{\bm{z}}_{t+1}[i]
. Using the conclusion we just proved, we obtain

\displaystyle g^{w}_{t+1}

\displaystyle=1+\mu w_{t+1}=1+\mu\left(\sum_{i=1}^{T}{\bm{y}}_{t+1}[i]+{\bm{z}}_{t+1}[i]\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{T}\left(\bm{g}^{\bm{y}}_{t+1}[i]+\bm{g}^{\bm{z}}_{t+1}[i]\right)\,,

where in the last equality, we used the fact that
\nabla\mathcal{G}^{\mu}({\bm{x}}^{\sf err}_{t},{\bm{y}}_{t},{\bm{z}}_{t})
is zero except for a single coordinate that is equal to
1
.
∎

Note that in order for
f({\bm{x}}_{T})
to achieve
\varepsilon
-accuracy, it must be that

\displaystyle w_{T}\in\left[-\frac{1}{2\mu}-O(\sqrt{\varepsilon}),-\frac{1}{2\mu}+O(\sqrt{\varepsilon})\right]\,.

Note that by symmetry,
{\bm{y}}_{T}[i]+{\bm{z}}_{T}[i]
is a deterministic quantity.

D.3
ensures that

\displaystyle{\bm{y}}_{T}[i]=\begin{cases}{\bm{y}}_{T}[i]+{\bm{z}}_{T}[i]&\text{with probability}~$1/2$,\\
0&\text{with probability}~$1/2$,\end{cases}\quad\text{for all}~i=2,\dots,T.

Hence, the deviation
\E\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{T}-\E[{\bm{x}}_{T}]\right\rVert^{2}
is again lower bounded by
\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i=2}^{T}({\bm{y}}_{T}[i]+{\bm{z}}_{T}[i])^{2}
.
Since either
{\bm{y}}_{T}[i]
or
{\bm{z}}_{T}[i]
has to be zero, it follows that
({\bm{y}}_{T}[i]+{\bm{z}}_{T}[i])^{2}=({\bm{y}}_{T}[i])^{2}+({\bm{z}}_{T}[i])^{2}
.
From the
\varepsilon
-suboptimality of
{\bm{x}}_{T}
, it also holds that
\sum_{i=2}^{T}({\bm{y}}_{T}[i])^{2}+({\bm{z}}_{T}[i])^{2}\leq\frac{2}{\mu}\varepsilon
.
Hence either
\sum_{i=2}^{T}({\bm{y}}_{T}[i])^{2}+({\bm{z}}_{T}[i])^{2}=\Omega(\frac{\varepsilon}{\mu})
(in which case the deviation is lower bounded by
\Omega(\frac{\varepsilon}{\mu})
), or we use
D.8
to conclude that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=2}^{T}({\bm{y}}_{T}[i]+{\bm{z}}_{T}[i])^{2}

\displaystyle\overset{(a)}{\geq}\frac{1}{T-1}\cdot\left(\sum_{i=2}^{T}{\bm{y}}_{T}[i]+{\bm{z}}_{T}[i]\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{T-1}\cdot\left(w_{T}-({\bm{y}}_{T}[1]+{\bm{z}}_{T}[1])\right)^{2}\gtrsim\frac{1}{T\mu^{2}}\,,

where
(a)
follows form the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality, and the last inequality is due to the fact that
({\bm{y}}_{T}[1]+{\bm{z}}_{T}[1])^{2}\lesssim\varepsilon
. This completes the proof.
∎

D.7
Non-stochastic inexact gradient model (strongly-convex costs)

Theorem D.9
.

(Lower Bound)

Let
\varepsilon>0
and
T
be a given number of iterations. There exists a
O(1)
-Lipschitz (nonsmooth) and
\mu
-strongly convex function
f:\mathbb{R}^{O(T)}\to\mathbb{R}
with a non-stochastic inexact gradient model such that any FOI algorithm
\mathcal{A}
that starts at
{\bm{x}}_{0}={\bm{0}}
has its
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-deviation lower bounded by

.

Proof.

We consider the same construction as the one considered in the proof of
Theorem D.7
.
The only difference is that now we add an extra dummy coordinate
u
(the
(3T+2)
-th coordinate), and add
\frac{\mu}{2}u^{2}

\mu

Following the previous convention, we will write the iterate as
{\bm{x}}_{t}=({\bm{x}}^{\sf err}_{t},{\bm{y}}_{t},{\bm{z}}_{t},w_{t},u_{t})\in(\mathbb{R}^{T})^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
We define the inexact gradient as:

\displaystyle g({\bm{x}}_{t})=\nabla f({\bm{x}}_{t})+\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{2}}{\bm{e}}_{1+t}+\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{2}}{\bm{e}}_{3T+3}\,,

where
{\bm{e}}_{j}
is the
j
-th coordinate vector.
Then following the same argument as
D.8
, it holds that

\displaystyle w_{T}=\sum_{i=1}^{T}{\bm{y}}_{T}[i]+{\bm{z}}_{T}[i]\quad\text{and}\quad\delta/\sqrt{2}\cdot w_{T}=u_{T}\,.

(D.21)

From the fact that
{\bm{x}}_{T}
achieves
\varepsilon
-suboptimality, it must be that

\displaystyle w_{T}\in\left[-\frac{1}{2\mu}-O(\sqrt{\varepsilon}),-\frac{1}{2\mu}+O(\sqrt{\varepsilon})\right]\,.

Now the rest of the proof follows similarly to that of
Theorem D.7
.
Using the facts
{\bm{y}}_{T}^{\sf exact}=0
,
{\bm{z}}_{T}^{\sf exact}=0
and

, we have the following

\displaystyle\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{T}-{\bm{x}}_{T}^{\sf exact}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\geq u_{T}^{2}+\sum_{i=2}^{T}[({\bm{y}}_{T}[i])^{2}+({\bm{z}}_{T}[i])^{2}]

From the
\varepsilon
-suboptimality of
{\bm{x}}_{T}
, it must be that
u_{T}^{2}+\sum_{i=2}^{T}({\bm{y}}_{T}[i])^{2}+({\bm{z}}_{T}[i])^{2}\leq\frac{2}{\mu}\varepsilon
.
Thus, either
u_{T}^{2}+\sum_{i=2}^{T}({\bm{y}}_{T}[i])^{2}+({\bm{z}}_{T}[i])^{2}=\Omega(\frac{\varepsilon}{\mu})
(in which case the deviation is lower bounded by
\Omega(\frac{\varepsilon}{\mu})
), or we use the fact that
(\sum_{i=2}^{T}{\bm{y}}_{T}[i]+{\bm{z}}_{T}[i])^{2}\gtrsim\frac{1}{\mu^{2}}
to conclude that

\displaystyle u_{T}^{2}+\sum_{i=2}^{T}[({\bm{y}}_{T}[i])^{2}+({\bm{z}}_{T}[i])^{2}]\geq\frac{\delta^{2}}{2}w_{T}^{2}+\frac{1}{2(T-1)}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{T}{\bm{y}}_{T}[i]+{\bm{z}}_{T}[i]\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\gtrsim\delta^{2}w_{T}^{2}+\frac{1}{T}w_{T}^{2}\gtrsim\frac{\delta^{2}}{\mu^{2}}+\frac{1}{T\mu^{2}}\,.

This completes the proof.

∎

D.8
Inexact initialization model (strongly-convex costs)

has its

-deviation lower bounded by
\Omega(\frac{1}{T\mu^{2}}\wedge\frac{\varepsilon}{\mu})

For the initialization error model, we use a simpler construction.
For
{\bm{x}}=({\bm{x}}^{\sf err},{\bm{y}},w)\in(\mathbb{R}^{T})^{2}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
, consider the cost defined as

\displaystyle f({\bm{x}}^{\sf err},{\bm{y}},w)=\max\{0,~{\bm{x}}^{\sf err}[1]+{\bm{y}}[1],~\dots,~{\bm{x}}^{\sf err}[T]+{\bm{y}}[T]\}+w+\frac{\mu}{2}\left\lVert({\bm{x}}^{\sf err},{\bm{y}},w)\right\rVert^{2}\,.

Then, clearly

is clearly

-Lipschitz. We set the reference and inexact intializations as follows:

\displaystyle{\bm{x}}_{0}^{\sf ref}=(0,0,\dots,0)\quad\text{and}\quad{\bm{x}}_{0}=(\underbrace{\delta,\cdots,\delta}_{\text{first $T$}},\underbrace{0,\cdots,0}_{\text{second $T$}},0)\,.

(D.22)

Following the previous proofs, we will write
{\bm{x}}_{t}=({\bm{x}}^{\sf err}_{t},{\bm{y}}_{t},w_{t})\in(\mathbb{R}^{T})^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
and the iterates corresponding to the reference initialization as

{\bm{x}}_{t}^{\sf ref}=(({\bm{x}}^{\sf err}_{t})^{\sf ref},{\bm{y}}_{t}^{\sf ref},w_{t}^{\sf ref})
.
From the fact that the algorithm has to achieve
\varepsilon
-suboptimality, it follows that

\displaystyle w_{T}\in\left[-\frac{1}{2\mu}-O(\sqrt{\varepsilon}),-\frac{1}{2\mu}+O(\sqrt{\varepsilon})\right].

Then following the same argument as
D.8
it holds that
w_{T}=\sum_{i=1}^{T}{\bm{y}}_{T}[i]
.
Using the fact that
{\bm{y}}_{T}^{\sf ref}=\bm{0}
, we have the following deviation lower bound:

\displaystyle\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{T}-{\bm{x}}_{T}^{\sf ref}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\left\lVert{\bm{y}}_{T}-{\bm{y}}_{T}^{\sf ref}\right\rVert^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{T}({\bm{y}}_{T}[i])^{2}

Moreover, from the
\varepsilon
-suboptimality of
{\bm{x}}_{T}
, it must be that
\sum_{i=1}^{T}({\bm{y}}_{T}[i])^{2}\leq\frac{2}{\mu}\varepsilon
.
Thus, either
\sum_{i=1}^{T}({\bm{y}}_{T}[i])^{2}=\Omega(\frac{\varepsilon}{\mu})
(in which case the deviation is lower bounded by
\Omega(\frac{\varepsilon}{\mu})
), or the following deviation lower bound holds:

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{T}({\bm{y}}_{T}[i])^{2}\geq\frac{1}{T}\cdot\left(\sum_{i=1}^{T}{\bm{y}}_{T}[i]\right)^{2}=\frac{1}{T}\cdot\left(w_{T}\right)^{2}\gtrsim\frac{1}{T\mu^{2}}\,.

This completes the proof.
∎

Appendix E
Proof of upper bounds (smooth costs)

E.1
Stochastic inexact gradient model

Theorem E.1
.

(Upper Bound)

Let
f
be an
O(1)
-smooth convex cost function. Let
\varepsilon>0
, and
T
be a given number of iterations.
Under the stochastic inexact gradient model, the
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-deviation of standard SGD with an appropriately chosen step size is
O(\frac{\delta^{2}}{T\varepsilon^{2}})
, provided that
T=\Omega(1/\varepsilon^{2})
.

Proof.

Throughout the proof, let
L
be the smoothness constant of
f
.
We first derive the deviation bound.
Let
\{{\bm{x}}_{t}\}
be the GD iterates with stochastic inexact gradients and
\{{\bm{y}}_{t}\}
be the GD iterates with exact gradients.
Assuming that
\eta_{t}\leq\frac{2}{L}
, the standard convex analysis yields the following one-step deviation inequality (
\E
denotes the conditional expectation over the randomness in
g({\bm{x}}_{t})
)

\displaystyle\E\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t+1}-{\bm{y}}_{t+1}\right\rVert^{2}=\E\left\lVert({\bm{x}}_{t}-\eta_{t}g({\bm{x}}_{t})-({\bm{y}}_{t}-\eta_{t}\nabla f({\bm{y}}_{t}))\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\quad=\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{y}}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}-2\eta_{t}\underbrace{\E\left\langle{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{y}}_{t},g({\bm{x}}_{t})-\nabla f({\bm{y}}_{t})\right\rangle}_{=\left\langle{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{y}}_{t},\nabla f({\bm{x}}_{t})-\nabla f({\bm{y}}_{t})\right\rangle}+\eta_{t}^{2}\E\left\lVert g({\bm{x}}_{t})-\nabla f({\bm{y}}_{t})\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\quad=\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{y}}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}-2\eta_{t}\left\langle{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{y}}_{t},\nabla f({\bm{x}}_{t})-\nabla f({\bm{y}}_{t})\right\rangle+\eta_{t}^{2}\E\left\lVert g({\bm{x}}_{t})-\nabla f({\bm{x}}_{t})\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+\underbrace{2\eta_{t}^{2}\E\left\langle g({\bm{x}}_{t})-\nabla f({\bm{x}}_{t}),\nabla f({\bm{x}}_{t})-\nabla f({\bm{y}}_{t})\right\rangle}_{=0}+\eta_{t}^{2}\left\lVert\nabla f({\bm{x}}_{t})-\nabla f({\bm{y}}_{t})\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\quad=\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{y}}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}\underbrace{-2\eta_{t}\left\langle{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{y}}_{t},\nabla f({\bm{x}}_{t})-\nabla f({\bm{y}}_{t})\right\rangle+\eta_{t}^{2}\left\lVert\nabla f({\bm{x}}_{t})-\nabla f({\bm{y}}_{t})\right\rVert^{2}}_{\leq 0}

\displaystyle\qquad+\eta_{t}^{2}\E\left\lVert\nabla f({\bm{x}}_{t})-g({\bm{x}}_{t})\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{y}}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}+\eta_{t}^{2}\delta^{2}\,.

Here the last inequality is due to the standard fact about smooth and convex function that for any
{\bm{x}},{\bm{y}}
,
\frac{1}{L}\left\lVert\nabla f({\bm{x}})-\nabla f({\bm{y}})\right\rVert^{2}\leq\left\langle\nabla f({\bm{x}})-\nabla f({\bm{y}}),{\bm{x}}-{\bm{y}}\right\rangle
(see, e.g.,
[
Nesterov 2018
, (2.1.11)]
), together with the fact
\eta_{t}\leq\frac{2}{L}
.
Hence, we have proved

\displaystyle\E\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{T}-{\bm{y}}_{T}\right\rVert^{2}\leq\delta^{2}\sum_{t}\eta_{t}^{2}\,.

(E.1)

Now for the upper bound, we consider variants of SGD.
From the standard convergence result (see, e.g.,
[
Bubeck 2014
, Thm. 6.3]
), with step size
\eta_{t}\equiv\frac{1}{L+\nicefrac{{1}}{{\eta}}}
for some
\eta>0
,

\displaystyle\E f\left(\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}{\bm{x}}_{t}\right)-f({\bm{x}}_{*})\leq\frac{L\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{0}-{\bm{x}}_{*}\right\rVert^{2}}{2T}+\frac{\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{0}-{\bm{x}}_{*}\right\rVert^{2}}{2\eta T}+\frac{\eta\delta^{2}}{2}\,.

(E.2)

For simplicity, let
\bar{{\bm{x}}}_{T}:=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t}{\bm{x}}_{t}
, and

\bar{{\bm{y}}}_{T}:=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t}{\bm{y}}_{t}
.
From the convexity of
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\E\left\lVert\bar{{\bm{x}}}_{T}-\bar{{\bm{y}}}_{T}\right\rVert^{2}\leq\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t}\E\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{y}}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}\,.

(E.3)

Now let us combine above results to upper bound
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-deviation.

As a warm-up, let us first consider SGD with
\eta=O(\nicefrac{{1}}{{\sqrt{T}}})
.
From (
E.2
), it follows that the convergence rate reads
\E f(\bar{{\bm{x}}}_{T})-f({\bm{x}}_{*})\leq O(\nicefrac{{1}}{{\sqrt{T}}})
.
With such a choice of
\eta
, the stepsize is

\displaystyle\eta_{t}\equiv\frac{1}{L+\Omega(\sqrt{T})}=O(\frac{1}{\sqrt{T}}).

Hence, for the deviation bound, using (
E.1
) together with (
E.3
), we have

\displaystyle\E\left\lVert\bar{{\bm{x}}}_{T}-\bar{{\bm{y}}}_{T}\right\rVert^{2}\leq\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t}\E\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{y}}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}\lesssim\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left[t\cdot\frac{1}{T}\cdot\delta^{2}\right]\lesssim\delta^{2}.

This shows that with
T=\Omega(\nicefrac{{1}}{{\varepsilon^{2}}})
, the
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-deviation is
O(\delta^{2})
.

In order to recover the bound in the theorem statement, we consider a mini-batch SGD.
In particular, the above calculation shows that using a mini-batch of size
b
at each iteration, it follows that with
O(\frac{b}{\varepsilon^{2}})
gradient queries, the deviation is upper bounded by
O(\frac{\delta^{2}}{b})
.
This precisely corresponds to the
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-deviation bound of
O(\frac{\delta^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}T})
.

An alternative way is to let the learning rate
\eta_{t}=1/(\varepsilon T)
.
It then follows from (
E.2
) that

\displaystyle\E f(\bar{{\bm{x}}}_{T})-f({\bm{x}}_{*})\leq O\left(\frac{1}{T}+\varepsilon+\frac{\delta^{2}}{\varepsilon T}\right)=O(\varepsilon),

since
T=\Omega(1/\varepsilon^{2})
.
Moreover, (
E.1
) and (
E.3
) imply

\displaystyle\E\left\lVert\bar{{\bm{x}}}_{T}-\bar{{\bm{y}}}_{T}\right\rVert^{2}\leq\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t}\E\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{y}}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}\leq\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left[t\cdot\delta^{2}\cdot\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}T^{2}}\right]\leq\frac{\delta^{2}}{T\varepsilon^{2}}\,,

which is the desired upper bound.
∎

E.2
Non-stochastic gradient errors

Theorem E.2
.

(Upper Bound)

For
L=O(1)
and
D=O(1)
, let
f
be an
L
-smooth convex cost function whose optimum lies in a ball of radius
D
.
Let
\varepsilon>0
and
\delta>0
are such that
\delta\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2LD}
. Let
T
be a given number of iterations.
Under the non-stochastic inexact gradient model, there exists a FOI algorithm whose
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-deviation is
O(\frac{\delta^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}})
, provided that
T=\Omega(\frac{1}{\varepsilon})
.

Proof.

Throughout the proof, let
L
be the smoothness constant of
f
. We consider the projected gradient descent with step size
\eta_{t}=\frac{1}{L}
onto the ball of radius
D
that contains the optimum
{\bm{x}}_{*}
.
It is important to note that this algorithm is a FOI because the projection onto the ball of radius
D
is a re-scaling, and hence after the projection, the coefficients
\lambda^{(t)}_{i}
are still positive.

The proximal inequality (e.g.,
[
Bauschke et al. 2011
, Proposition 12.26]
) implies that

\displaystyle\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t+1}-{\bm{x}}_{*}\right\rVert^{2}-\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}_{*}\right\rVert^{2}\leq-\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t+1}-{\bm{x}}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}-2\eta_{t}\left\langle g({\bm{x}}_{t}),{\bm{x}}_{t+1}-{\bm{x}}_{*}\right\rangle

Let
\Delta_{t}
denote the error due to the non-stochastic inexact gradient model at iteration
t
, i.e.,
\Delta_{t}:=g({\bm{x}}_{t})-\nabla f({\bm{x}}_{t})
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t+1}-{\bm{x}}_{*}\right\rVert^{2}-\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}_{*}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq-\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t+1}-{\bm{x}}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}-2\eta_{t}\left\langle\nabla f({\bm{x}}_{t})+\Delta_{t},{\bm{x}}_{t+1}-{\bm{x}}_{*}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq-\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t+1}-{\bm{x}}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}+2\eta_{t}\left\langle\nabla f({\bm{x}}_{t}),{\bm{x}}_{*}-{\bm{x}}_{t+1}\right\rangle+2\delta D

\displaystyle=-\underbrace{\left(\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t+1}-{\bm{x}}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}+2\eta_{t}\left\langle\nabla f({\bm{x}}_{t}),{\bm{x}}_{t+1}-{\bm{x}}_{t}\right\rangle\right)}_{(a)}+\underbrace{2\eta_{t}\left\langle\nabla f({\bm{x}}_{t}),{\bm{x}}_{*}-{\bm{x}}_{t}\right\rangle}_{(b)}+2\delta D

\displaystyle\leq-2\eta_{t}(f({\bm{x}}_{t+1})-f({\bm{x}}_{t}))+2\eta_{t}(f({\bm{x}}_{*})-f({\bm{x}}_{t}))+2\delta D

\displaystyle=2\eta_{t}(f({\bm{x}}_{*})-f({\bm{x}}_{t+1}))+2\delta D,

where
(a)
is upper bounded using the
L
-smoothness together with
\eta_{t}=\frac{1}{L}
as follows:

\displaystyle f({\bm{x}}_{t+1})-f({\bm{x}}_{t})

\displaystyle\leq\left\langle\nabla f({\bm{x}}_{t}),{\bm{x}}_{t+1}-{\bm{x}}_{t}\right\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t+1}-{\bm{x}}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\nabla f({\bm{x}}_{t}),{\bm{x}}_{t+1}-{\bm{x}}_{t}\right\rangle+\frac{1}{2\eta_{t}}\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t+1}-{\bm{x}}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}\,,

and
(b)
is handled using convexity.

Summing this over all
t=0,1,\dots,T-1
gives

\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\frac{2}{L}(f({\bm{x}}_{t+1})-f({\bm{x}}_{*}))\leq\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{0}-{\bm{x}}_{*}\right\rVert^{2}+2\delta TD,

which implies the following average-iterate guarantee:

\displaystyle f(\bar{{\bm{x}}}_{T})-f({\bm{x}}_{*})\leq\frac{LD^{2}}{2T}+\delta LD.

Thus, in order to achieve
\varepsilon
-suboptimality, we need
\Omega(1/\varepsilon)
iterations, since the theorem statement assumed that
\delta\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2LD}
.
Next, let us bound the deviation.

Lemma E.3
.

Suppose that
f
is
L
-smooth.
Let
\Delta_{t}
and
\Delta_{t}^{\prime}
denote noises in the gradients.
If
\eta_{t}\leq\frac{2}{L}
, then the following one-step deviation inequality holds

\displaystyle\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t}-\eta_{t}(\nabla f({\bm{x}}_{t})+\Delta_{t})-({\bm{x}}^{\prime}_{t}-\eta_{t}(\nabla f({\bm{x}}^{\prime}_{t})+\Delta^{\prime}_{t}))\right\rVert\leq\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert+\eta_{t}\left\lVert\Delta_{t}\right\rVert+\eta_{t}\left\lVert\Delta_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert.

Proof.

The proof follows from the following inequality:

\displaystyle\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t+1}-{\bm{x}}_{t+1}^{\prime}\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}_{t}^{\prime}-\eta_{t}(g({\bm{x}}_{t})-g({\bm{x}}_{t}^{\prime}))\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}_{t}^{\prime}-\eta_{t}({\nabla}f({\bm{x}}_{t})-{\nabla}f({\bm{x}}_{t}^{\prime}))\right\rVert+\eta_{t}\left\lVert\Delta_{t}\right\rVert+\eta_{T}\left\lVert\Delta_{t}\right\rVert^{\prime}

\displaystyle\overset{(a)}{\leq}\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert+\eta_{t}\left\lVert\Delta_{t}\right\rVert+\eta_{t}\left\lVert\Delta_{t}\right\rVert^{\prime}\,,

Here (
a
) follows from the following fact:

\displaystyle\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}_{t}^{\prime}-\eta_{t}({\nabla}f({\bm{x}}_{t})-{\nabla}f({\bm{x}}_{t}^{\prime}))\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert^{2}-2\eta_{t}\left\langle{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}_{t}^{\prime},{\nabla}f({\bm{x}}_{t})-{\nabla}f({\bm{x}}_{t}^{\prime})\right\rangle+\eta_{t}^{2}\left\lVert{\nabla}f({\bm{x}}_{t})-{\nabla}f({\bm{x}}_{t}^{\prime})\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\overset{(b)}{\leq}\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert^{2}-\frac{2}{L}\eta_{t}\left\lVert{\nabla}f({\bm{x}}_{t})-{\nabla}f({\bm{x}}_{t}^{\prime})\right\rVert^{2}+\eta_{t}^{2}\left\lVert{\nabla}f({\bm{x}}_{t})-{\nabla}f({\bm{x}}_{t}^{\prime})\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\overset{(c)}{\leq}\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert^{2}\,,

where
(b)
is due to the fact that for a
L
-smooth and convex function
f
, it holds that

\frac{1}{L}\left\lVert\nabla f({\bm{x}})-\nabla f({\bm{y}})\right\rVert^{2}\leq\left\langle\nabla f({\bm{x}})-\nabla f({\bm{y}}),{\bm{x}}-{\bm{y}}\right\rangle\quad\text{for any ${\bm{x}},{\bm{y}}$,}

and
(c)
is because
\eta_{t}^{2}\leq\frac{2}{L}\eta_{t}
.
∎

Now given the convergence rate and the deviation inequality, we are ready to prove the desired upper bound on (
\varepsilon,\delta
)-deviation. From the triangle inequality, we get

\displaystyle\left\lVert\bar{{\bm{x}}}_{T}-\bar{{\bm{x}}}^{\prime}_{T}\right\rVert

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}{\bm{x}}_{t}-\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}{\bm{x}}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert\leq\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}O(t\delta)\leq O(T\delta)\,,

where the second line follows from Lemma
E.3
.
Thus, the
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-deviation is bounded by
O(\frac{\delta^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}})
using the averaged iterate with
T=\Theta(1/\varepsilon)
. ∎

One limitation of
Theorem E.2
is that it requires the optimum to lie in a bounded domain.
Next we show that without this requirement, the gradient descent iterate is still bounded when it first attains
\varepsilon
-accuracy.

Theorem E.4
.

Suppose
f
is
L
-smooth with optimum
x^{*}
.
Let
\varepsilon,\delta>0
be given such that
\varepsilon\leq 2L\|{\bm{x}}_{0}-{\bm{x}}_{*}\|^{2}
and
\delta\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{\|{\bm{x}}_{0}-{\bm{x}}_{*}\|}
.
Consider gradient descent with a constant learning rate
\eta=\frac{1}{L}
.
Under the non-stochastic inexact gradient model, for the first iterate
{\bm{x}}_{T}
with
f({\bm{x}}_{T})-f({\bm{x}}_{*})\leq\varepsilon
, it holds that
\|{\bm{x}}_{T}-{\bm{x}}_{*}\|\leq 2\|{\bm{x}}_{0}-{\bm{x}}_{*}\|
.

Proof.

Let
D_{t}:=\|{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}_{*}\|
.
Following the proof of
Theorem E.2
(which still holds when the domain is unbounded), we can show that

\displaystyle D_{t+1}^{2}-D_{t}^{2}\leq 2\eta(f({\bm{x}}_{*})-f({\bm{x}}_{t+1}))+2\eta\delta D_{t+1}.

(E.4)

Let
T
denote the first step with
f({\bm{x}}_{T})\leq f({\bm{x}}_{*})+\varepsilon
.
We claim that for all
0\leq t\leq T-2
,

\displaystyle D_{t+1}\leq D_{t}\leq D_{0}.

(E.5)

We will prove (
E.5
) by induction.
Given
0\leq t\leq T-2
, suppose
D_{t}\leq D_{0}
, and note that the definition of
T
implies
f({\bm{x}}_{t+1})>f({\bm{x}}_{*})+\varepsilon
.
It then follows from (
E.4
) that

\displaystyle D_{t+1}^{2}-2\eta\delta D_{t+1}+2\eta\varepsilon-D_{t}^{2}<0.

Let
h(z):=z^{2}-2\eta\delta z+2\eta\varepsilon-D_{t}^{2}
.
First,

\displaystyle h(D_{t})=-2\eta\delta D_{t}+2\eta\varepsilon\geq 0,

since
\delta D_{t}\leq\delta D_{0}\leq\varepsilon
by the condition of Theorem
E.4
.
Moreover,
D_{t}
is larger than
\eta\delta
, the minimum of
h
, because if it is not true, then

\displaystyle f({\bm{x}}_{t})-f({\bm{x}}_{*})\leq\frac{L}{2}D_{t}^{2}\leq\frac{L\eta^{2}\delta^{2}}{2}\leq\frac{L\eta^{2}\varepsilon^{2}}{2D_{0}^{2}}=\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2LD_{0}^{2}}\leq\varepsilon,

which contradicts the definition of
T
.
Since
h(D_{t+1})<0
, it then follows that
D_{t+1}\leq D_{t}\leq D_{0}
, and in particular
D_{T-1}\leq D_{0}
.
Finally, note that smoothness implies

\displaystyle\|\nabla f({\bm{x}}_{T-1})\|\leq LD_{T-1}\leq LD_{0},

and thus

\displaystyle D_{T}\leq\|{\bm{x}}_{T}-{\bm{x}}_{T-1}\|+D_{T-1}\leq\eta\|\nabla f({\bm{x}}_{T-1})\|+D_{T-1}\leq D_{0}+D_{T-1}\leq 2D_{0}.

This completes the proof
∎

E.3
Inexact initialization model

Theorem E.5
.

(Upper Bound)

Let
f
be an
O(1)
-Lipschitz convex cost function. Let
\varepsilon>0
be a small constant, and
T
be a given number of iterations.
Then there exists a FOI algorithm whose
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-deviation is
O(\delta^{2})
, provided that
T=\Omega(1/\varepsilon)
.

Proof.

In view of
E.3
, two different runs
{\bm{x}}_{T},{\bm{x}}_{T}^{\prime}
of gradient descent satisfies

\displaystyle\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{T}-{\bm{x}}_{T}^{\prime}\right\rVert\leq\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{T-1}^{\prime}-{\bm{x}}_{T-1}^{\prime}\right\rVert\leq\cdots\leq\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{0}-{\bm{x}}_{0}^{\prime}\right\rVert\leq 2\delta.

Hence, the statement follows.
∎

In this section, we consider smooth and strongly-convex costs.

E.4
Stochastic inexact gradient model (strongly convex costs)

We first show an upper bound for the stochastic inexact gradient oracle.

Theorem E.6
.

(Upper Bound)

Let
f
be an
O(1)
-smooth
\mu
-strongly convex cost function. Let
\varepsilon>0
be a small constant, and
T
be a given number of iterations.
Under the stochastic inexact gradient model, there exists a FOI algorithm whose
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-deviation is
O\left(\frac{\delta^{2}}{T\mu^{2}}\wedge\frac{\varepsilon}{\mu}\right)
, provided that
T=\Omega(\frac{1}{\varepsilon\mu})
.

Proof.

The following proof is based on the proof of
[
Bubeck 2014
, Theorem 6.3]
, but here we further make use of strong convexity.

Let
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
denote the domain of
f
, and assume it is convex and closed.
We simply run stochastic gradient descent: starting from some
{\bm{x}}_{0}\in C
, let

\displaystyle{\bm{x}}_{t+1}:=\Pi_{C}\left[{\bm{x}}_{t}-\eta_{t}g({\bm{x}}_{t})\right]\quad\textup{where}\quad\eta_{t}:=\frac{1}{L+1/\lambda_{t}}.

We will pick a value for each
\lambda_{t}
below.
Note that
C
can just be
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, in which case no projection is needed, but our analysis can also handle a bounded domain.

Let
{\bm{x}}_{*}
denote the optimal solution, and suppose
f
is
L
-smooth.
It follows that

\displaystyle f({\bm{x}}_{t+1})-f({\bm{x}}_{t})

\displaystyle\leq\langle\nabla f({\bm{x}}_{t}),{\bm{x}}_{t+1}-{\bm{x}}_{t}\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\|{\bm{x}}_{t+1}-{\bm{x}}_{t}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\langle g({\bm{x}}_{t}),{\bm{x}}_{t+1}-{\bm{x}}_{t}\rangle+\langle\nabla f({\bm{x}}_{t})-g({\bm{x}}_{t}),{\bm{x}}_{t+1}-{\bm{x}}_{t}\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\|{\bm{x}}_{t+1}-{\bm{x}}_{t}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\langle g({\bm{x}}_{t}),{\bm{x}}_{t+1}-{\bm{x}}_{t}\rangle+\frac{\lambda_{t}}{2}\|\nabla f({\bm{x}}_{t})-g({\bm{x}}_{t})\|^{2}+\frac{L+1/\lambda_{t}}{2}\|{\bm{x}}_{t+1}-{\bm{x}}_{t}\|^{2}.

Moreover, the projection step ensures

\displaystyle\frac{1}{L+1/\lambda_{t}}\langle g({\bm{x}}_{t}),{\bm{x}}_{t+1}-{\bm{x}}_{*}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\langle{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}_{t+1},{\bm{x}}_{t+1}-{\bm{x}}_{*}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\|{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}_{*}\|^{2}-\|{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}_{t+1}\|^{2}-\|{\bm{x}}_{t+1}-{\bm{x}}_{*}\|^{2}\right).

Consequently,

\displaystyle f({\bm{x}}_{t+1})-f({\bm{x}}_{t})

\displaystyle\leq\langle g({\bm{x}}_{t}),{\bm{x}}_{*}-{\bm{x}}_{t}\rangle+\frac{\lambda_{t}}{2}\|\nabla f({\bm{x}}_{t})-g({\bm{x}}_{t})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{L+1/\lambda_{t}}{2}\left(\|{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}_{*}\|^{2}-\|{\bm{x}}_{t+1}-{\bm{x}}_{*}\|^{2}\right).

Taking expectation with respect to
g({\bm{x}}_{t})
, we have

\displaystyle\E[f({\bm{x}}_{t+1})]\leq f({\bm{x}}_{t})+\langle\nabla f({\bm{x}}_{t}),{\bm{x}}_{*}-{\bm{x}}_{t}\rangle+\frac{\lambda_{t}}{2}\delta^{2}+\frac{L+1/\lambda_{t}}{2}\E\left[\|{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}_{*}\|^{2}-\|{\bm{x}}_{t+1}-{\bm{x}}_{*}\|^{2}\right].

Further invoking strong convexity, we have

\displaystyle\E[f({\bm{x}}_{t+1})]\leq f({\bm{x}}_{*})-\frac{\mu}{2}\|{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}_{*}\|^{2}+\frac{\lambda_{t}\delta^{2}}{2}+\frac{L+1/\lambda_{t}}{2}\E\left[\|{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}_{*}\|^{2}-\|{\bm{x}}_{t+1}-{\bm{x}}_{*}\|^{2}\right].

(E.6)

Pick a small enough
k
which also satisfies
k\geq 4L/\mu
, and let

\displaystyle\lambda_{t}=\frac{2}{(t+k)\mu-2L}.

It follows that
\lambda_{t}>0
by construction, and also

\displaystyle L+\frac{1}{\lambda_{t}}=\frac{t+k}{2}\mu,\quad\textup{and}\quad L+\frac{1}{\lambda_{t}}-\mu=\frac{t+k-2}{2}\mu,

and that

\displaystyle(t+k-1)\lambda_{t}=\frac{2}{\mu}\cdot\frac{t+k-1}{t+k-2L/\mu}\leq\frac{2}{\mu}\cdot\frac{t+k-1}{(t+k)/2}\leq\frac{4}{\mu},

since by the definition of
k
, we have
2L/\mu\leq k/2\leq(t+k)/2
.
Therefore if we multiply both sides of (
E.6
) by
(t+k-1)
, we get

\displaystyle(t+k-1)\E\left[f({\bm{x}}_{t+1})-f({\bm{x}}_{*})\right]\leq

\displaystyle\ \frac{(t+k-1)(t+k-2)}{4}\mu\E[\|{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}_{*}\|^{2}]

\displaystyle\ -\frac{(t+k)(t+k-1)}{4}\mu\E[\|{\bm{x}}_{t+1}-{\bm{x}}_{*}\|^{2}]+\frac{2\delta^{2}}{\mu}.

Now taking the sum from
t=0
to
T-1
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}(t+k-1)\E\left[f({\bm{x}}_{t+1})-f({\bm{x}}_{*})\right]\leq\frac{(k-1)(k-2)\mu}{4}\|{\bm{x}}_{0}-{\bm{x}}_{*}\|^{2}+\frac{2\delta^{2}T}{\mu}.

Define

\displaystyle\tilde{{\bm{x}}}_{T}:=\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\frac{t+k-1}{\sum_{j=0}^{T-1}(j+k-1)}{\bm{x}}_{t+1}\,.

Then, we have

\displaystyle\E\left[f(\tilde{{\bm{x}}}_{T})-f({\bm{x}}_{*})\right]\leq O\left(\frac{(k-1)(k-2)\mu}{4T^{2}}\|{\bm{x}}_{0}-{\bm{x}}_{*}\|^{2}+\frac{2\delta^{2}}{T\mu}\right).

Since
k=\Theta(L/\mu)
, it follows that as long as
T=\Omega(\frac{1}{\varepsilon\mu})
, we have

\displaystyle\E\left[f(\tilde{{\bm{x}}}_{T})-f({\bm{x}}_{*})\right]\leq\varepsilon.

Next we analyze the deviation bound.
Similarly to the proof of
Theorem E.1
, let
\{{\bm{x}}_{t}\}
denote GD iterates with stochastic inexact gradients, and let
\{{\bm{y}}_{t}\}
denote GD iterates with exact gradients, we can show

\displaystyle\E\left[\|{\bm{x}}_{t+1}-{\bm{y}}_{t+1}\|^{2}\right]\leq

\displaystyle\ \|{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{y}}_{t}\|^{2}-2\eta_{t}\langle{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{y}}_{t},\nabla f({\bm{x}}_{t})-\nabla f({\bm{y}}_{t})\rangle+\eta_{t}^{2}\|\nabla f({\bm{x}}_{t})-\nabla f({\bm{y}}_{t})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\ +\eta_{t}^{2}\E\left[\|\nabla f({\bm{x}}_{t})-\nabla g({\bm{x}}_{t})\|^{2}\right].

Next we need the following lemma.

Lemma E.7
.

Suppose
f
is
L
-smooth and
\mu
-strongly convex.
For
\eta\leq 1/L
, it holds for any
{\bm{x}},{\bm{y}}
that

\displaystyle\left\lVert{\bm{x}}-{\bm{y}}-\eta({\nabla}f({\bm{x}})-{\nabla}f({\bm{y}}))\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-\eta\mu\right)\|{\bm{x}}-{\bm{y}}\|^{2}.

Proof.

First we have

\displaystyle\ \left\lVert{\bm{x}}-{\bm{y}}-\eta({\nabla}f({\bm{x}})-{\nabla}f({\bm{y}}))\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \left\lVert{\bm{x}}-{\bm{y}}\right\rVert^{2}-2\eta\left\langle{\bm{x}}-{\bm{y}},{\nabla}f({\bm{x}})-{\nabla}f({\bm{y}})\right\rangle+\eta^{2}\left\lVert{\nabla}f({\bm{x}})-{\nabla}f({\bm{y}})\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \|{\bm{x}}-{\bm{y}}\|^{2}-2\eta\langle{\bm{x}}-{\bm{y}},\nabla f({\bm{x}})-\nabla f({\bm{y}})\rangle+\eta\frac{1}{L}\cdot L\langle{\bm{x}}-{\bm{y}},\nabla f({\bm{x}})-\nabla f({\bm{y}})\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \|{\bm{x}}-{\bm{y}}\|^{2}-\eta\langle{\bm{x}}-{\bm{y}},\nabla f({\bm{x}})-\nabla f({\bm{y}})\rangle,

where the inequality is due to smoothness and
\eta\leq 1/L
.
Strong convexity then implies

\displaystyle\left\lVert{\bm{x}}-{\bm{y}}-\eta({\nabla}f({\bm{x}})-{\nabla}f({\bm{y}}))\right\rVert^{2}\leq\|{\bm{x}}-{\bm{y}}\|^{2}-\eta\mu\|{\bm{x}}-{\bm{y}}\|^{2}.

∎

Note that in the current setting,
\eta_{t}=1/(L+1/\lambda_{t})\leq 1/L
, therefore we can invoke
E.7
and obtain

\displaystyle\E\left[\|{\bm{x}}_{t+1}-{\bm{y}}_{t+1}\|^{2}\right]\leq(1-\eta_{t}\mu)\E\left[\|{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{y}}_{t}\|^{2}\right]+\eta_{t}^{2}\delta^{2},

which further implies

\displaystyle\E\left[\|{\bm{x}}_{T}-{\bm{y}}_{T}\|^{2}\right]\leq\delta^{2}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\eta_{t}^{2}\prod_{j=t+1}^{T-1}(1-\eta_{j}\mu),

since
{\bm{x}}_{0}={\bm{y}}_{0}
.
Note that

\displaystyle\eta_{t}=\frac{2}{(t+k)\mu},\quad\textup{and}\quad 1-\eta_{t}\mu=\frac{t+k-2}{t+k},

therefore

\displaystyle\E\left[\|{\bm{x}}_{T}-{\bm{y}}_{T}\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\delta^{2}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\frac{4}{(t+k)^{2}\mu^{2}}\frac{(t-1+k)(t+k)}{(T-2+k)(T-1+k)}

\displaystyle\leq\delta^{2}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\frac{4}{\mu^{2}}\frac{1}{(T-2+k)(T-1+k)}

\displaystyle\leq\delta^{2}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\frac{4}{\mu^{2}T^{2}}\leq\frac{4\delta^{2}}{T\mu^{2}}.

Now define

\displaystyle\tilde{{\bm{y}}}_{T}:=\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\frac{t+k-1}{\sum_{j=0}^{T-1}(j+k-1)}{\bm{y}}_{t+1}.

Since
\tilde{{\bm{x}}}_{T}
and
\tilde{{\bm{y}}}_{T}
are weighted averages of
{\bm{x}}_{t}
and
{\bm{y}}_{t}
respectively, we have

\displaystyle\E\left[\|\tilde{{\bm{x}}}_{T}-\tilde{{\bm{y}}}_{T}\|^{2}\right]\leq\frac{4\delta^{2}}{T\mu^{2}},

Moreover, since
\tilde{{\bm{y}}}_{T}
is deterministic, an
O(\frac{\delta^{2}}{T\mu^{2}})
deviation bound also follows.
∎

E.5
Non-stochastic inexact gradient model (strongly convex costs)

Next we consider the non-stochastic inexact gradient oracle.

Theorem E.8
.

(Upper Bound)

For
L=O(1)
and
D=O(1)
, let
f
be an
L
-smooth
\mu
-strongly convex cost function whose optimum lies in a ball of radius
D
.
Let
\varepsilon>0
and
\delta>0
are such that
\delta\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2LD}
, and let
T
be a given number of iterations.
Under the non-stochastic inexact gradient model, there exists a FOI algorithm whose
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-deviation is
O\left(\frac{\delta^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\wedge\frac{\varepsilon}{\mu}\right)
, provided that
T=\Omega(1/\varepsilon)
.

Proof.

We run projected gradient descent with a constant learning rate
\eta=1/L
.
For the upper bound on excess error, we simply invoke
Theorem E.2
: as long as
T=\Omega(1/\varepsilon)
, it holds that

\displaystyle f(\bar{{\bm{x}}}_{T})\leq\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}f({\bm{x}}_{t})\leq f({\bm{x}}_{*})+\varepsilon.

To bound the deviation, we follow a similar analysis as in the proof of
E.3
.
Consider two gradient descent runs
\{{\bm{x}}_{t}\}
and
\{{\bm{x}}^{\prime}_{t}\}
, and let

g({\bm{x}}_{t})=\nabla f({\bm{x}}_{t})+\Delta_{t}
, and
g({\bm{x}}_{t}^{\prime})=\nabla f({\bm{x}}_{t}^{\prime})+\Delta_{t}^{\prime}
.
First we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t+1}-{\bm{x}}_{t+1}^{\prime}\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}_{t}^{\prime}-\eta_{t}(g({\bm{x}}_{t})-g({\bm{x}}_{t}^{\prime}))\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}_{t}^{\prime}-\eta_{t}({\nabla}f({\bm{x}}_{t})-{\nabla}f({\bm{x}}_{t}^{\prime}))\right\rVert+\eta_{t}\left\lVert\Delta_{t}\right\rVert+\eta_{t}\left\lVert\Delta_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}_{t}^{\prime}-\eta_{t}({\nabla}f({\bm{x}}_{t})-{\nabla}f({\bm{x}}_{t}^{\prime}))\right\rVert+\frac{2\delta}{L}.

Moreover,
E.7
implies

\displaystyle\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}_{t}^{\prime}-\eta_{t}({\nabla}f({\bm{x}}_{t})-{\nabla}f({\bm{x}}_{t}^{\prime}))\right\rVert\leq\sqrt{1-\frac{\mu}{L}}\|{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}^{\prime}_{t}\|.

Therefore

\displaystyle\|{\bm{x}}_{t+1}-{\bm{x}}^{\prime}_{t+1}\|\leq\sqrt{1-\frac{\mu}{L}}\|{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}^{\prime}_{t}\|+\frac{2\delta}{L},

and for all
t\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\|{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}^{\prime}_{t}\|

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-\frac{\mu}{L}\right)^{t/2}\|{\bm{x}}_{0}-{\bm{x}}^{\prime}_{0}\|+\frac{2\delta}{L}\cdot\frac{1}{1-\sqrt{1-\mu/L}}

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{\mu}{L}\right)^{t/2}\|{\bm{x}}_{0}-{\bm{x}}^{\prime}_{0}\|+\frac{2\delta}{L}\cdot\frac{1+\sqrt{1-\mu/L}}{\mu/L}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-\frac{\mu}{L}\right)^{t/2}\|{\bm{x}}_{0}-{\bm{x}}^{\prime}_{0}\|+\frac{4\delta}{\mu}.

Finally,

\displaystyle\|\bar{{\bm{x}}}_{T}-\bar{{\bm{x}}}^{\prime}_{T}\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\|{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}^{\prime}_{t}\|\leq\frac{4\delta}{\mu}+\frac{1}{T}\underbrace{\|{\bm{x}}_{0}-{\bm{x}}^{\prime}_{0}\|}_{=0}\frac{1}{1-\sqrt{1-\mu/L}}=O\left(\frac{\delta}{\mu}\right).

This completes the proof.
∎

E.6
Inexact initialization model (strongly convex costs)

Theorem E.9
.

(Upper Bound)

Let
f
be an
L
-smooth
\mu
-strongly convex cost function. Let
\varepsilon>0
be a small constant, and
T
be a given number of iterations.
Then there exists a FOI algorithm whose
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-deviation is
O(\exp(-\mu T/L)\delta^{2}\wedge\frac{\varepsilon}{\mu})
.

Proof.

Let
{\bm{x}}_{0}
,
{\bm{x}}_{0}^{\prime}
denote two initial iterates.

E.7
implies

\displaystyle\|{\bm{x}}_{t+1}-{\bm{x}}^{\prime}_{t+1}\|^{2}=\|{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}^{\prime}_{t}-\eta_{t}(\nabla f({\bm{x}}_{t})-\nabla f({\bm{x}}^{\prime}_{t}))\|^{2}\leq\left(1-\frac{\mu}{L}\right)\|{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}^{\prime}_{t}\|^{2},

Appendix F
Proof of upper bounds (nonsmooth costs)

F.1
Stochastic inexact gradient model

Theorem F.1
.

(Upper Bound)

Let
f
be an
O(1)
-Lipschitz convex cost function. Let
\varepsilon>0
be a small constant, and
T
be a given number of iterations.
Under the stochastic inexact gradient model, there exists a FOI algorithm whose
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-deviation is
O(\frac{1}{T\varepsilon^{2}})
, provided that
T=\Omega(1/\varepsilon^{2})
.

Proof.

Assume now that
f
is
G
-Lipschitz but otherwise nonsmooth (
G=O(1)
). Let
\{{\bm{x}}_{t}\}
be the GD iterates with stochastic inexact gradients and
\{{\bm{y}}_{t}\}
be the GD iterates with exact gradients.
Then the one-step deviation bound can be derived as follows (
\E
denotes the conditional expectation over the randomness in
g({\bm{x}}_{t})
):

\displaystyle\E\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t+1}-{\bm{y}}_{t+1}\right\rVert^{2}=\E\left\lVert({\bm{x}}_{t}-\eta_{t}g({\bm{x}}_{t})-({\bm{y}}_{t}-\eta_{t}\nabla f({\bm{y}}_{t}))\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\quad=\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{y}}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}-2\eta_{t}\underbrace{\left\langle{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{y}}_{t},\nabla f({\bm{x}}_{t})-\nabla f({\bm{y}}_{t})\right\rangle}_{\leq 0~~(\because~\text{convexity})}

\displaystyle\qquad+\eta_{t}^{2}\E\left\lVert g({\bm{x}}_{t})-\nabla f({\bm{x}}_{t})\right\rVert^{2}+\eta_{t}^{2}\left\lVert\nabla f({\bm{x}}_{t})-\nabla f({\bm{y}}_{t})\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{y}}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}+\eta_{t}^{2}(4G^{2}+\delta^{2})\,.

Since we consider the regime
\delta^{2}\lesssim 1
, the one-step bound leads to the following deviation inequality:

\displaystyle\E\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{T}-{\bm{y}}_{T}\right\rVert^{2}\lesssim\sum_{t}\eta_{t}^{2}.

(F.1)

Note that the above deviation bound is worse than the smooth case deviation bound (
E.1
) which reads
\E\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{T}-{\bm{y}}_{T}\right\rVert^{2}\leq\delta^{2}\sum_{t}\eta_{t}^{2}
.

For the algorithm, we again consider SGD. Invoking the standard convergence guarantee of SGD for nonsmooth costs (see, e.g.,
[
Bubeck 2014
, Thm. 6.1]
), with step size
\eta_{t}\equiv\eta
for some
\eta>0
, we have the following convergence rate:

\displaystyle\E f\left(\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}{\bm{x}}_{t}\right)-f({\bm{x}}_{*})\leq\frac{\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{0}-{\bm{x}}_{*}\right\rVert^{2}}{2\eta T}+\frac{\eta G^{2}}{2}\,.

(F.2)

From (
F.2
), it follows that with
\eta=O(\nicefrac{{1}}{{\sqrt{T}}})
, the convergence rate reads
\E f(\bar{{\bm{x}}}_{T})-f({\bm{x}}_{*})\leq O(\nicefrac{{1}}{{\sqrt{T}}})
. With such a choice of
\eta
, the deviation can be bounded using (
E.1
) together with (
E.3
),

\displaystyle\E\left\lVert\bar{{\bm{x}}}_{T}-\bar{{\bm{y}}}_{T}\right\rVert^{2}\leq\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t}\E\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{y}}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}\lesssim\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left[t\cdot\frac{1}{T}\cdot G^{2}\right]\lesssim G^{2}.

In fact, by choosing
\eta=\frac{1}{\varepsilon T}
(since
T=\Omega(1/\varepsilon^{2})
, it must be that
\eta=O(\varepsilon)
), it follows that the
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-deviation is upper bounded by

\displaystyle O\left(\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left[t\cdot\frac{1}{T^{2}\varepsilon^{2}}\cdot G^{2}\right]\right)\lesssim O(\frac{G^{2}}{T\varepsilon^{2}})\lesssim O(\frac{1}{T\varepsilon^{2}}).

This completes the proof.
∎

F.2
Non-stochastic inexact gradient model

We first prove a deviation bound.

Lemma F.2
.

Suppose that
f
is convex and
G
-Lipschitz. Let
\{{\bm{y}}_{t}\}
be the iterates of (projected) GD with stepsize
\eta_{t}
with exact gradients and
\{x_{t}\}
be the iterates of (projected) GD with the same stepsize with inexact gradients with noise
\{\Delta_{t}\}
. Assuming that
\left\lVert\Delta_{t}\right\rVert\leq\delta
for each
t
, we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{T}-{\bm{y}}_{T}\right\rVert\leq\sqrt{3(2G^{2}+\delta^{2})\cdot\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\eta_{t}^{2}}+2\delta\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\eta_{t}\,.

(F.3)

Proof.

The proof is analogous to
[
Bassily et al. 2020
, Lemma 3.1]
.
First, note that

\displaystyle\left\lVert{\bm{y}}_{t+1}-{\bm{x}}_{t+1}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\overset{(a)}{\leq}\left\lVert{\bm{y}}_{t}-\eta_{t}\nabla f({\bm{y}}_{t})-({\bm{x}}_{t}-\eta_{t}(\nabla f({\bm{x}}_{t})+\Delta_{t}))\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{y}}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}-2\eta_{t}\left\langle{\bm{y}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}_{t},\nabla f({\bm{y}}_{t})-\nabla f({\bm{x}}_{t})-\Delta_{t}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad+\eta_{t}^{2}\left\lVert\nabla f({\bm{y}}_{t})-\nabla f({\bm{x}}_{t})-\Delta_{t}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\overset{(b)}{\leq}\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{y}}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}+2\eta_{t}\left\langle{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{y}}_{t},\Delta_{t}\right\rangle+\eta_{t}^{2}\left\lVert\nabla f({\bm{y}}_{t})-\nabla f({\bm{x}}_{t})-\Delta_{t}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\overset{(c)}{\leq}\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{y}}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}+2\delta\eta_{t}\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{y}}_{t}\right\rVert+3\eta_{t}^{2}(2G^{2}+\delta^{2})\,,

where
(a)
is due to the non-expansiveness of the projection step,
(b)
is due to convexity, and
(c)
is due to the inequality
\left\lVert v_{1}+v_{2}+v_{3}\right\rVert\leq 3\left\lVert v_{1}\right\rVert^{2}+3\left\lVert v_{2}\right\rVert^{2}+3\left\lVert v_{3}\right\rVert^{2}
.
Denoting
d_{t}:=\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{y}}_{t}\right\rVert
, we obtain

\displaystyle d_{T}^{2}\leq 2\delta\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\eta_{t}d_{t}+3(2G^{2}+\delta^{2})\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\eta_{t}^{2}.

(F.4)

We now prove (
F.3
) by induction.
If
d_{T}\leq\max_{t=0,\dots,T-1}d_{t}
, then the conclusion follows from the induction hypothesis.
Hence we may assume that
d_{T}>\max_{t=0,\dots,T-1}d_{t}
. Then the following inequality follows from (
F.4
):

\displaystyle d_{T}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\delta\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\eta_{t}d_{t}+3(2G^{2}+\delta^{2})\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\eta_{t}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\delta d_{T}\cdot\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\eta_{t}+3(2G^{2}+\delta^{2})\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\eta_{t}^{2}\,.

Solving this, we obtain the desired conclusion (
F.3
).
∎

Theorem F.3
.

(Upper Bound)

For
G=O(1)
and
D=O(1)
, let
f
be an
G
-Lipschitz convex cost function whose optimum lies in a ball of radius
D
.
Let
\varepsilon>0
and
\delta>0
are such that
\delta\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2D}
. Let
\varepsilon>0
be a small constant, and
T
be a given number of iterations.
Under the non-stochastic inexact gradient model, there exists a FOI algorithm whose
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-deviation is
O(\frac{1}{T\varepsilon^{2}}+\frac{\delta^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}})
, provided that
T=\Omega(1/\varepsilon^{2})
.

Proof.

We consider the projected gradient descent with constant stepsize
\eta
onto the ball of radius
D
that contains the optimum
{\bm{x}}_{*}
.

Let
{\bm{y}}_{t}
denote the iterate before projection.
Let
\Delta_{t}
denote the error due to the non-stochastic inexact gradient model at iteration
t
, i.e.,
\Delta_{t}:=g({\bm{x}}_{t})-\nabla f({\bm{x}}_{t})
.
Then, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t+1}-{\bm{x}}_{*}\right\rVert^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}_{*}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert{\bm{y}}_{t+1}-{\bm{x}}_{*}\right\rVert^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}_{*}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq-\eta\left\langle\nabla f({\bm{x}}_{t})+\Delta_{t},{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}_{*}\right\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\eta^{2}\left\lVert\nabla f({\bm{x}}_{t})+\Delta_{t}\right\rVert^{2}

Hence,

\displaystyle f({\bm{x}}_{t})-f({\bm{x}}_{*})+\frac{1}{2\eta}\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t+1}-{\bm{x}}_{*}\right\rVert^{2}-\frac{1}{2\eta}\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}_{*}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=f({\bm{x}}_{t})-f({\bm{x}}_{*})-\left\langle\nabla f({\bm{x}}_{t})+\Delta_{t},{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}_{*}\right\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\eta\left\lVert\nabla f({\bm{x}}_{t})+\Delta_{t}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\delta D+\eta(G+\delta)^{2}.

After telescoping the above inequalities from
t=0,\dots,{T-1}
, we obtain the bound

\displaystyle f\left(\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}{\bm{x}}_{t}\right)-f({\bm{x}}_{*})\lesssim\frac{D^{2}}{\eta T}+\eta G^{2}+\delta D.

Thus, for
\varepsilon
-accuracy, we need
\eta\lesssim\varepsilon
and
\eta T\gtrsim 1/\varepsilon
, since the theorem statement assumed that
\delta\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2D}
. Hence, choosing
\eta=\Theta(\frac{1}{\varepsilon T})
,
F.2
gives

\displaystyle\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}_{t}^{\prime}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{t}{\varepsilon^{2}T^{2}}+(t\delta\eta)^{2}=\frac{t}{\varepsilon^{2}T^{2}}+\frac{\delta^{2}t^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}T^{2}}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\bar{{\bm{x}}}_{T}-\bar{{\bm{x}}}^{\prime}_{T}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t}\left\lVert{x}_{t}-{x}^{\prime}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t}\left[\frac{t}{\varepsilon^{2}T^{2}}+\frac{\delta^{2}t^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}T^{2}}\right]\approx\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}T}+\frac{\delta^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\,,

as desired.
∎

F.3
Inexact initialization model

Theorem F.4
.

(Upper Bound)

Let
f
be an
O(1)
-Lipschitz convex cost function. Let
\varepsilon>0
be a small constant, and
T
be a given number of iterations.
Then there exists a FOI algorithm whose
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-deviation is
O(\frac{1}{T\varepsilon^{2}}+\delta^{2})
, provided that
T=\Omega(1/\varepsilon^{2})
.

Proof.

We consider the subgradient descent with constant step size
\eta
.
A standard convergence guarantee for GD reads (see, e.g.,
[
Bubeck 2014
, Theorem 3.2]
)

\displaystyle f(\bar{{\bm{x}}}_{T})-f({\bm{x}}_{*})\lesssim\frac{\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{0}-{\bm{x}}_{*}\right\rVert^{2}}{\eta T}+\eta G^{2}\,.

Hence, in order to have
\varepsilon
-suboptimality, we need to have
\eta T\approx\frac{1}{\varepsilon}
and
\eta\lesssim\varepsilon
.

We now derive a deviation bound.
A similar calculation to
F.2
yields the following:

\displaystyle\left\lVert{\bm{y}}_{t+1}-{\bm{x}}_{t+1}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{y}}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}-2\eta\left\langle{\bm{y}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}_{t},\nabla f({\bm{y}}_{t})-\nabla f({\bm{x}}_{t})\right\rangle+\eta^{2}\left\lVert\nabla f({\bm{y}}_{t})-\nabla f({\bm{x}}_{t})\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{y}}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}+\eta^{2}\left\lVert\nabla f({\bm{y}}_{t})-\nabla f({\bm{x}}_{t})\right\rVert^{2}\leq\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{y}}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}+4G^{2}\eta^{2}\,.

Hence, it holds that

\displaystyle\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{y}}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}\leq\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{0}-{\bm{y}}_{0}\right\rVert^{2}+G^{2}\sum_{t=0}^{t-1}\eta^{2}\leq\delta^{2}+G^{2}t\eta^{2}=\delta^{2}+G^{2}\frac{t}{T^{2}}(\eta T)^{2}\approx\delta^{2}+G^{2}\frac{t}{T^{2}}\cdot\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\,.

Thus, it follows that

\displaystyle\left\lVert\bar{{\bm{x}}}_{t}-\bar{{\bm{y}}}_{T}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{y}}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}\lesssim\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\left(\delta^{2}+G^{2}\frac{t}{T^{2}}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right)\lesssim\delta^{2}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}T},

as desired.
∎

F.5
Non-stochastic inexact gradient model (strongly convex costs)

Theorem F.6
.

(Upper Bound)

For
G=O(1)
and
D=O(1)
, let
f
be an
G
-Lipschitz
\mu
-strongly convex cost function whose optimum lies in a ball of radius
D
.
Let
\varepsilon>0
and
\delta>0
are such that
\delta\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2D}
. Let
\varepsilon>0
be a small constant, and
T
be a given number of iterations.
Under the non-stochastic inexact gradient model, there exists a FOI algorithm whose
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-deviation is
O((\frac{1}{T\mu^{2}}+\frac{\delta^{2}}{\mu^{2}})\wedge\frac{\varepsilon}{\mu})
, provided that
T=\Omega(1/\varepsilon)
.

Proof.

We first prove the convergence rate bound.
We run projected gradient descent with a constant learning rate
\eta_{t}=\frac{1}{\mu(t+1)}
.
Then, it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{\mu(t+1)}{2}\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t+1}-{\bm{x}}^{*}\right\rVert^{2}-\frac{\mu t}{2}\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}^{*}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{\mu}{2}\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}^{*}\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{1}{2\eta_{t}}\left(\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t+1}-{\bm{x}}^{*}\right\rVert^{2}-\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}^{*}\right\rVert^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{\mu}{2}\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}^{*}\right\rVert^{2}+\left\langle g({\bm{x}}_{t}),{\bm{x}}^{*}-{\bm{x}}_{t}\right\rangle+\frac{\eta_{t}}{2}\left\lVert g({\bm{x}}_{t})\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{\mu}{2}\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}^{*}\right\rVert^{2}+\left\langle\nabla f({\bm{x}}_{t}),{\bm{x}}^{*}-{\bm{x}}_{t}\right\rangle+\frac{\eta_{t}}{2}\left\lVert g({\bm{x}}_{t})\right\rVert^{2}+\delta D\,,

where the last line follows since every iterate lies in the ball of radius
D
.
Hence,

\displaystyle f({\bm{x}}_{t})-f({\bm{x}}^{*})+\frac{\mu(t+1)}{2}\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t+1}-{\bm{x}}^{*}\right\rVert^{2}-\frac{\mu t}{2}\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}^{*}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\underbrace{f({\bm{x}}_{t})-f({\bm{x}}^{*})+\frac{\mu}{2}\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}^{*}\right\rVert^{2}+\left\langle\nabla f({\bm{x}}_{t}),{\bm{x}}^{*}-{\bm{x}}_{t}\right\rangle}+\frac{\eta_{t}}{2}\left\lVert g({\bm{x}}_{t})\right\rVert^{2}+\delta D

\displaystyle\quad\overset{(a)}{\leq}\frac{\eta_{t}}{2}\left\lVert g({\bm{x}}_{t})\right\rVert^{2}+\delta D\lesssim\frac{\eta_{t}}{2}G^{2}+\delta D\,,

where (
a
) follows from strong convexity.
Therefore, it holds that

\displaystyle f\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{2(t+1)}{(T+1)(T+2)}{\bm{x}}_{t}\right)-f({\bm{x}}^{*})\leq\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{2(t+1)}{(T+1)(T+2)}(f({\bm{x}}_{t})-f({\bm{x}}^{*}))

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{2(t+1)}{(T+1)(T+2)}(\frac{\eta_{t}}{2}G^{2}+\delta D)\lesssim\frac{G^{2}}{\mu T}+\delta D.

Let
\bar{{\bm{x}}}_{T}:=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{2t}{T(T+1)}{\bm{x}}_{t}
.
We next bound the deviation.
Again, let
\{{\bm{y}}_{t}\}
be the iterates of (projected) GD with stepsize
\eta_{t}
with exact gradients and
\{{\bm{x}}_{t}\}
be the iterates of (projected) GD with the same stepsize with inexact gradients with noise
\{\Delta_{t}\}
.

\displaystyle\left\lVert{\bm{y}}_{t+1}-{\bm{x}}_{t+1}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\overset{(a)}{\leq}\left\lVert{\bm{y}}_{t}-\eta_{t}\nabla f({\bm{y}}_{t})-({\bm{x}}_{t}-\eta_{t}(\nabla f({\bm{x}}_{t})+\Delta_{t}))\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{y}}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}-2\eta_{t}\left\langle{\bm{y}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}_{t},\nabla f({\bm{y}}_{t})-\nabla f({\bm{x}}_{t})-\Delta_{t}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad+\eta_{t}^{2}\left\lVert\nabla f({\bm{y}}_{t})-\nabla f({\bm{x}}_{t})-\Delta_{t}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\overset{(b)}{\leq}(1-2\mu\eta_{t})\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{y}}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}+2\eta_{t}\left\langle{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{y}}_{t},\Delta_{t}\right\rangle+\eta_{t}^{2}\left\lVert\nabla f({\bm{y}}_{t})-\nabla f({\bm{x}}_{t})-\Delta_{t}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\overset{(c)}{\lesssim}(1-\frac{2}{t+1})\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{y}}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}+2\delta\eta_{t}\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{y}}_{t}\right\rVert+\eta_{t}^{2}G^{2}\,,

where
(a)
is due to the non-expansiveness of the projection step,
(b)
is due to convexity, and
(c)
is due to the inequality
\left\lVert v_{1}+v_{2}+v_{3}\right\rVert\leq 3\left\lVert v_{1}\right\rVert^{2}+3\left\lVert v_{2}\right\rVert^{2}+3\left\lVert v_{3}\right\rVert^{2}
.
Denoting
d_{t}:=\left\lVert{\bm{x}}_{t}-{\bm{y}}_{t}\right\rVert
, we obtain

\displaystyle d_{T}^{2}\lesssim\delta\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\frac{t^{2}}{T^{2}}\eta_{t}d_{t}+\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\frac{t^{2}}{T^{2}}\eta_{t}^{2}.

(F.6)

Now similarly to
F.2
, one can deduce from this inequality that

\displaystyle d_{T}^{2}\lesssim\frac{\delta}{\mu}d_{T}+\frac{1}{T\mu^{2}}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad d_{T}^{2}\lesssim\frac{\delta^{2}}{\mu^{2}}+\frac{1}{T\mu^{2}}\,.

Now, after applying the Jensen’s inequality, we obtain the desired deviation bound of

\left\lVert\bar{{\bm{x}}}_{T}-\bar{{\bm{x}}}_{T}^{\prime}\right\rVert^{2}\leq(\frac{\delta^{2}}{\mu^{2}}+\frac{1}{T\mu^{2}})\wedge\frac{\varepsilon}{\mu}
,
where
\frac{\varepsilon}{\mu}
follows from the fact that
\bar{{\bm{x}}}_{T}
achieves
\varepsilon
-accuracy.
∎

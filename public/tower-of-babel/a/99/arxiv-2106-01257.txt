Tight High Probability Bounds for Linear Stochastic Approximation with Fixed Stepsize
Alain Durmus, Eric Moulines, Alexey Naumov, Sergey Samsonov, Kevin Scaman, Hoi-To Wai

Abstract
This paper provides a non-asymptotic analysis of linear stochastic approximation (LSA) algorithms with fixed stepsize. This family of methods arises in many machine learning tasks and is used to obtain approximate solutions of a linear system A ¯ ​ θ = b ¯ \bar{A}\theta=\bar{b} for which A ¯ \bar{A} and b ¯ \bar{b} can only be accessed through random estimates { ( 𝐀 n , 𝐛 n ) : n ∈ ℕ ∗ } \{({\bf A}_{n},{\bf b}_{n}):n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\} . Our analysis is based on new results regarding moments and high probability bounds for products of matrices which are shown to be tight. We derive high probability bounds on the performance of LSA under weaker conditions on the sequence { ( 𝐀 n , 𝐛 n ) : n ∈ ℕ ∗ } \{({\bf A}_{n},{\bf b}_{n}):n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\} than previous works. However, in contrast, we establish polynomial concentration bounds with order depending on the stepsize. We show that our conclusions cannot be improved without additional assumptions on the sequence of random matrices { 𝐀 n : n ∈ ℕ ∗ } \{{\bf A}_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\} , and in particular that no Gaussian or exponential high probability bounds can hold. Finally, we pay a particular attention to establishing bounds with sharp order with respect to the number of iterations and the stepsize and whose leading terms contain the covariance matrices appearing in the central limit theorems.

Introduction

This paper provides a detailed analysis of Linear Stochastic Approximation (LSA) schemes which aim at finding a solution

and

\bar{b}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
which are typically unknown. This class of
algorithms and the corresponding setting have a long history and
important applications in signal processing such as channel
equalization and echo cancellation

[
3
,
22
]
. It has renewed
interests in machine learning and computational statistics especially
for least-square estimation, Reinforcement learning (RL)
and
Q
-learning

[
4
,
7
,
38
,
35
]
.
The recursion (
1

several works which derive asymptotic

[
31
,
22
,
6
,
3
]

and non-asymptotic

[

,

,
2

,

,

,

]

However, in most cases, there is a consistent gap
between these two types of analyses. While asymptotic analysis gives
important insights on the qualitative convergence of
(
1
) based on statistical key quantities of the problem on
hand, they do not provide finite-time convergence, or high probability
bounds, necessary to obtain non-asymptotic confidence sets, see

[
26
,
10
]
and the references therein. On the other
hand, non-asymptotic studies are in general too coarse and lose
significant statistical information in their derivation. Further,
their upper bounds are generally loose when used in predicting the
actual performance of LSA. We aim at filling this gap and provide
conditions on

This problem has been addressed in several contributions but at the expense of strong conditions on the sequence

[

derived concentration inequalities but assuming that the innovations in (

In contrast, we aim at giving simple and mild conditions ensuring high probability bounds.
More precisely, one of our key contributions (

\begin{split}&\mathbb{P}\left(|u^{\top}(\theta_{n}-\theta^{\star})|\leq c\{\sqrt{\alpha u^{\top}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}u}+\alpha\}\sqrt{\log(1/\delta)}+c\{\rho_{\alpha}^{n}+\alpha p_{0}^{2}\}\delta^{-\frac{1}{p_{0}}}\right)\geq 1-\delta\;,\end{split}

2
Linear Stochastic Approximation: Setting and Assumptions

Consider the LSA recursion (
1
) with a deterministic initial point
\theta_{0}
. The main assumption required in this paper is as follows:

A 1
.

\{({\bf A}_{n},{\bf b}_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
is an i.i.d. sequence satisfying the following conditions.

(i)

\mathbb{E}[{\bf b}_{1}]=\bar{b}
and there exists
\operatorname{C}_{{b}}>0
such that, for any
u\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
,
u^{\top}({\bf b}_{1}-\bar{b})\in\operatorname{SG}(\operatorname{C}_{{b}}^{2})
.

(ii)

There exists
\operatorname{C}_{{A}}>0
such that

\|{{\bf A}_{1}}\|\leq\operatorname{C}_{{A}}
almost surely.

(iii)

The matrix
-\bar{A}=-\mathbb{E}[{\bf A}_{1}]
is Hurwitz, i.e. for any eigenvalue
\lambda
of
\bar{A}
,
\mathrm{Re}(\lambda)>0
.

Both conditions
1
-
(i)
,
(ii)
are standard in analysis of LSA, e.g., in
[
11
,
34
,
24
]
.
Meanwhile,
1
-
(iii)
guarantees the existence of a unique solution
\theta^{\star}
to
\bar{A}\theta=\bar{b}
.
It is also a sufficient and necessary condition for the solution of the ordinary differential equation
\dot{\theta}_{t}=-\bar{A}\theta_{t}
to converge exponentially to
\theta^{\star}

[
18
, Lemma 4.1.2]
. The same kind of result holds for the discrete system
\theta_{n+1}^{\sf d}-\theta_{n}^{\sf d}=-\alpha\bar{A}\theta_{n}^{\sf d}
.

Proposition 1
.

Assume that
-\bar{A}
is a Hurwitz matrix. Then there exists a unique positive definite matrix
Q
satisfying the Lyapunov equation

\bar{A}^{\top}Q+Q\bar{A}=\mathrm{I}
. In addition, setting

a=\|{Q}\|^{-1}/2\;,\quad\text{and}\quad\alpha_{\infty}=(1/2)\|{\bar{A}}\|_{{Q}}^{-2}\|{Q}\|^{-1}\;,

(3)

then for any
\alpha\in[0,\alpha_{\infty}]
,
we get
\|{\mathrm{I}-\alpha\bar{A}}\|_{{Q}}^{2}\leq 1-a\alpha
.
If in addition
\alpha\leq\|{Q}\|^{2}
then

1-a\alpha\geq 1/2
.

This result is well known but its proof can be found in
Section

A.1
for completeness. The above proposition implies that the discrete system converges exponentially as
\|\theta_{n+1}^{\sf d}\|\leq\sqrt{\kappa_{Q}}(1-a\alpha)^{n/2}\|\theta_{0}^{\sf d}\|
for
\alpha\in(0,\alpha_{\infty})
.

Recall that the aim of this paper is to derive high probability bounds on
u^{\top}\{\theta_{n}-\theta^{\star}\}
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
u\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
.
Below, we present a counterexample to show that under only
1
, if
\alpha>0
is fixed, then there exists
\bar{p}>0
such that
\lim_{n\to+\infty}\mathbb{E}[\|\theta_{n}-\theta^{\star}\|^{p}]=+\infty

. As a corollary, it is impossible to obtain exponential high probability bounds for
\{\|\theta_{n}-\theta^{\star}\|\!~:\!~n\in\mathbb{N}\}
.

Example 1
.

Consider (
1
) with
d=1
taking
{\bf b}_{n}=0
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
and for

\{{\bf A}_{n}\!~:\!~n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\}
an i.i.d. sequence of biased Rademacher r.v.s with parameter

:

{\bf A}_{n}=\begin{cases}1&\text{ with probability $q_{A}$}\;,\\
-1&\text{ with probability $1-q_{A}$}\;.\end{cases}

(4)

This choice is associated with
\theta^{\star}=0
and corresponds to the recursion:
\theta_{n}=\prod_{k=1}^{n}(1-\alpha{\bf A}_{k})\theta_{0}
, for some
\theta_{0}\neq 0

For any

and
\alpha\in\left(0,1\right)
, we have by definition,

\mathbb{E}\left[|\theta_{n}|^{p}\right]=\{q_{A}(1-\alpha)^{p}+(1-q_{A})(1+\alpha)^{p}\}^{n}|\theta_{0}|^{p}\;.

Using the lower bounds
(1-\alpha)^{p}\geq 1-\alpha p

(1+\alpha)^{p}\geq 1+\alpha p+p(p-1)\alpha^{2}/2

p\geq 1
and
\alpha\in\left(0,1\right)

\mathbb{E}\left[|\theta_{n}|^{p}\right]\geq\{1-p\alpha[(2q_{A}-1)-(p-1)\alpha(1-q_{A})/2]\}^{n}|\theta_{0}|^{p}\;.

If
\alpha\in\left(0,1\right)
is fixed, then for any

,
we have
\lim_{n\to+\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[|\theta_{n}|^{p}\right]=+\infty
.
On the other hand, if
\alpha\in\left(0,2(2q_{A}-1)/(1-q_{A})\right)
, then

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\mathbb{E}[\theta_{n}^{2}]=0
.
Therefore

p
moments for any

.

However, this result is specific to this particular case and
does not hold if only
1

\{\theta_{n}\!~:\!~n\in\mathbb{N}\}

It is, however, possible to obtain any

by strengthening

-
(iii)
to:

.

There exist
\tilde{a}\in\left(0,1\right)

Examples such that

On the other hand, examples where

{\sf X}

{\rm P}:{\sf X}\times{\cal X}\rightarrow[0,1]

{\rm R}:{\sf X}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

(5)

where

is drawn from the stationary distribution of

and
x_{n}^{\prime}\sim{\rm P}(x_{n},\cdot)
. It is easy to verify

\|\phi(x)\|
,

Our next endeavor is to establish moment estimates on the product below:

\textstyle\Gamma^{(\alpha)}_{m:n}=\prod_{i=m}^{n}(\mathrm{I}-\alpha{\bf A}_{i})\;,\quad m,n\in\mathbb{N}^{*},\quad m\leq n\;.

(6)

We also define its expected value as
G^{(\alpha)}_{m:n}=\mathbb{E}[\Gamma^{(\alpha)}_{m:n}]=(\mathrm{I}-\alpha\bar{A})^{n-m+1}
.

The above product naturally appears after re-centering the LSA recursion (
1

n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}

\theta_{n}-\theta^{\star}=\bigl(\mathrm{I}-\alpha{\bf A}_{n}\bigr)\{\theta_{n-1}-\theta^{\star}\}+\alpha\varepsilon_{n}\;,\quad\varepsilon_{n}={\bf b}_{n}-\bar{b}-\{{\bf A}_{n}-\bar{A}\}\theta^{\star}\;.

(7)

An easy induction implies that

\textstyle\theta_{n}-\theta^{\star}=\tilde{\theta}^{\sf(tr)}_{n}+\tilde{\theta}^{\sf(fl)}_{n}\;,\quad\tilde{\theta}^{\sf(tr)}_{n}=\Gamma^{(\alpha)}_{1:n}\{\theta_{0}-\theta^{\star}\}\;,\quad\tilde{\theta}^{\sf(fl)}_{n}=\alpha\sum_{j=1}^{n}\Gamma^{(\alpha)}_{j+1:n}\varepsilon_{j}\;.

(8)

The decomposition (

\theta^{\star}
by
\{\theta_{n}\!~:\!~n\in\mathbb{N}\}
which will be separately tackled:

corresponds to the transient (or bias) term and
\{\tilde{\theta}^{\sf(fl)}_{n}\!~:\!~n\in\mathbb{N}\}
to the fluctuation term. Both errors are controlled by the product of matrices
\Gamma^{(\alpha)}_{m:n}
, thereby motivating the study of the moment bound on
\Gamma^{(\alpha)}_{1:n}
as we present next.

3
Moment and High-probability Bounds for Products of Random Matrices

Recall from
1
that the expected value
G^{(\alpha)}_{1:n}=\mathbb{E}[\Gamma^{(\alpha)}_{1:n}]
decays exponentially with
n
, here we expect a similar phenomenon to hold for the moment bound of
\Gamma^{(\alpha)}_{1:n}
.
Precisely, in this section, we show that if
p
is fixed, then there exists
\alpha_{p}>0
such that for any
\alpha\in\left(0,\alpha_{p}\right]
, the
p
-th moment of
\Gamma^{(\alpha)}_{m:n}
decays exponentially with

To facilitate our discussions, we introduce the following notations.
For
B\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
, we denote by

its singular values. For
p\geq 1
, the Schatten
p
-norm is denoted by
\|B\|_{{p}}=\{\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}\sigma_{\ell}^{p}(B)\}^{1/p}
. For
p,q\geq 1
and random matrix

, we write
\|{\bf X}\|_{{p,q}}=\{\mathbb{E}[\|{\bf X}\|_{{p}}^{q}]\}^{1/q}
.

In the following, we present the main technical result on the product of general random matrices. The proof is based on the framework introduced in
[

]

Proposition 2
.

Let
\{{\bf Y}_{\ell}\!~:\!~\ell\in\mathbb{N}\}
be an independent sequence and
P
be a positive definite matrix. Assume that for each
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
there exist

and

such that
\|\mathbb{E}[{\bf Y}_{\ell}]\|_{{P}}^{2}\leq 1-m_{\ell}
and

almost surely. Define
\mathbf{Z}_{n}=\prod_{\ell=0}^{n}{\bf Y}_{\ell}={\bf Y}_{n}\mathbf{Z}_{n-1}

. Then, for any

and

\|\mathbf{Z}_{n}\|_{{p,q}}^{2}\leq\kappa_{P}\prod_{\ell=1}^{n}(1-m_{\ell}+(p-1)\sigma_{\ell}^{2})\|P^{1/2}\mathbf{Z}_{0}P^{-1/2}\|_{{p,q}}^{2}\;,

(9)

2

Let

Therefore, we obtain for any

.
Since
\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{A}_{n}|\mathbf{B}_{n}]=0

\|f_{P}(\mathbf{Z}_{n})\|_{{p,q}}^{2}\leq\|\mathbf{B}_{n}\|_{{p,q}}^{2}+(p-1)\|\mathbf{A}_{n}\|_{{p,q}}^{2}\;.

(10)

It remains to bound the two terms on the right hand side. To this end, we use
[
16

which implies that for any

(11)

As a result and using that for any

\|B\|_{{P}}=\|{f_{P}(B)}\|

\|{\bf Y}_{n}-\mathbb{E}[{\bf Y}_{n}]\|_{{P}}\leq\sigma_{n}

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{A}_{n}\|_{{p,q}}

\displaystyle=\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\|f_{P}({\bf Y}_{n}-\mathbb{E}[{\bf Y}_{n}])f_{P}(\mathbf{Z}_{n-1})\|_{{p}}^{q}\right]\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\|{{\bf Y}_{n}-\mathbb{E}[{\bf Y}_{n}]}\|_{{P}}^{q}\|f_{P}(\mathbf{Z}_{n-1})\|_{{p}}^{q}\right]\right)^{1/q}\leq\sigma_{n}\|f_{P}(\mathbf{Z}_{n-1})\|_{{p,q}}\;.

(12)

Similarly, applying
\|\mathbb{E}[{\bf Y}_{n}]\|_{{P}}^{2}\leq 1-m_{n}

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{B}_{n}\|_{{p,q}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\|f_{P}(\mathbb{E}[{\bf Y}_{n}])f_{P}(\mathbf{Z}_{n-1})\|_{{p}}^{q}\right]\right)^{2/q}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\|\mathbb{E}[{\bf Y}_{n}]\|_{{P}}^{q}\|f_{P}(\mathbf{Z}_{n-1})\|_{{p}}^{q}\right]\right)^{2/q}\leq(1-m_{n})\|f_{P}(\mathbf{Z}_{n-1})\|_{{p,q}}^{2}\;.

(13)

Combining (

) and (

\|f_{P}(\mathbf{Z}_{n})\|_{{p,q}}^{2}\leq(1-m_{n}+(p-1)\sigma_{n}^{2})\|f_{P}(\mathbf{Z}_{n-1})\|_{{p,q}}^{2}\leq\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-m_{n}+(p-1)\sigma_{n}^{2})\|f_{P}(\mathbf{Z}_{0})\|_{{p,q}}^{2}
.
The proof is then completed upon using (

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we provided a novel non-asymptotic analysis of LSA algorithms with fixed stepsize. For any
\delta\in(0,1)
, we obtain bounds on the sequence
\{\|\theta_{n}-\theta^{\star}\|\!~:\!~n\in\mathbb{N}\}
that holds with probability at least
1-\delta
. The bounds are proven to be tight with respect to the stepsize, and we show that these bounds necessarily have polynomial dependency in
\delta
.
Importantly, our results do not require the matrices
{\bf A}_{n}
to be symmetric but only Hurwitz, which enables one to apply them to various scenarios such as reinforcement learning.
Future work includes extending our high probability bounds to a larger panel of random noise, e.g., with heavy tailed distribution, Markovian dependency, as well as Polyak-Ruppert averaging.

Lemma 3
.

Random variable
X\in\operatorname{SG}(\sigma^{2})
for some
\sigma>0
if and only if for all
t\geq 0
the condition
\mathbb{P}(|X|\geq t)\leq 2\exp\{-t^{2}/(2\sigma^{2})\}
holds. In addition, in such a case, for any

Proof.

The first statement is well-known, see for example
[
8
, Theorem 2.1]
.
We now show the second statement.
By the Fubini theorem,
\mathbb{E}[|X|^{p}]=p\int_{0}^{+\infty}u^{p-1}\mathbb{P}(|X|>u)\,\mathrm{d}u
, we get

\mathbb{E}[|X|^{p}]=2p\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{p-1}\mathrm{e}^{-u^{2}/(2\sigma^{2})}\,\mathrm{d}u=p2^{p/2}\sigma^{p}\Gamma(p/2),

using the change of variable
t=u^{2}/(2\sigma^{2})
. Now, using an upper bound
\Gamma(p/2)\leq(p/2)^{(p-1)/2}\mathrm{e}^{1-p/2}
(see e.g.
[
14
, Theorem 2]
), and
p^{1/2}\leq 2^{p/2}

\mathbb{E}[|X|^{p}]\leq\sqrt{2}\mathrm{e}(2/\mathrm{e})^{p/2}p^{p/2}\sigma^{p}\;.

∎

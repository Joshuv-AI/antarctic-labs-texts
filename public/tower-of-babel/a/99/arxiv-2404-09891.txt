Convolution Identities of Stirling Numbers Thanks: * Corresponding author (N. N. Li): lina20190606@outlook.com
Nadia N. Li and Wenchang Chu

Abstract
. By means of the generating function method, a linear recurrence relation is explicitly resolved. The solution is expressed in terms of the Stirling numbers of both the first and the second kind. Two remarkable pairs of combinatorial identities are established as applications, that contain some well–known convolution formulae on Stirling numbers as special cases.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 11B65, 11B73 Secondary 05A10

1.
Introduction and Motivation

Denote by
\mathbb{N}
the set of natural numbers with
\mathbb{N}_{0}=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
.
For an indeterminate
x
, define the rising and falling factorials by

(x)_{0}=\langle{x}\rangle_{0}\equiv 1
and

\displaystyle(x)_{n}

\displaystyle=x(x+1)\cdots(x+n-1)\quad\text{for}\quad n\in\mathbb{N},

\displaystyle\langle{x}\rangle_{n}

\displaystyle=x(x-1)\cdots(x-n+1)\quad\text{for}\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

Then the unsigned Stirling numbers of the first kind
\bigg[\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{n}{k}\bigg]

are determined as the connection coefficients:

\langle{x}\rangle_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-k}\bigg[\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{n}{k}\bigg]x^{k}.

Analogously, the Stirling numbers of the second kind
\bigg\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{n}{k}\bigg\}

are given by

x^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\bigg\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{n}{k}\bigg\}\langle{x}\rangle_{k}

which admits the explicit expression

\bigg\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{n}{k}\bigg\}=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{k-j}\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{k}{j}\bigg)j^{n}.

These numbers appear frequently in mathematical literatures
and have wide applications in combinatorics and number theory
(see
[
3
, Chapter 5]
,
[
5
, §6.2]
and
[
6
]
,
just for example).

Recently, Stenlund
[
7
]
introduced an interesting bivariate polynomial
sequence
\{P_{n}(x,z)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
through the following recurrence relation:

P_{n+1}(x,z)=x\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{n+z}{n}\bigg)-x\sum_{m=1}^{n}\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{n-m+z}{n-m+1}\bigg)P_{m}(x,z)\quad\text{with\quad}P_{1}(x,z)=x.

(1)

The same author not only found out an explicit double sum expression

P_{n}(x,z)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{(-1)^{j-1}(j-1)!}{(n-1)!}\bigg[\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{n}{k}\bigg]\bigg\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{k}{j}\bigg\}x^{j}z^{k-1},

(2)

but also explored applications to combinatorial identities and probability theory.

Inspired by the above work of Stenlund
[
7
]
,
we shall examine the extended polynomial sequence

\{Q_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
defined by the recurrence relation

Q_{n}=\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{\lam}{n}\bigg)(-1)^{n}-x\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k}\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{y}{k}\bigg)Q_{n-k}\quad\text{with\quad}Q_{0}=1.

(3)

The first three terms are recorded as follows:

\displaystyle Q_{1}

\displaystyle=xy-\lambda,\qquad Q_{2}=\frac{xy}{2}(1-y+2xy)-\frac{\lambda}{2}(1-\lambda+2xy),

\displaystyle Q_{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{xy}{6}(y-1)(y-2)-\frac{\lambda}{6}(\lambda-1)(\lambda-2)-xy(1-y+xy)(\lambda-xy)+\frac{\lambda xy}{2}(\lambda-y).

These
Q_{n}
-polynomials in (
3
) generalize the
P_{n}
-polynomials
in (
1
) because it is not hard to verify that
Q_{n}
becomes

P_{n+1}/x
when
\lambda=-1-z
and
y\to-z
.

The rest of the paper will be organized as follows. In the next section,
we shall derive the generating function and the explicit formulae for
the
P_{n}
-polynomials. As applications, two pais of convolution sums containing
both kinds of Stirling numbers will be evaluated in closed forms in Section 3.

2.
Generating Function and Explicit Expressions

For the sequence
\{Q_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
defined by (
3
),
we shall derive, by making use of generating function approach,
their generating function and explicit formulae. The informed
reader will notice that this treatment is more transparent than
the induction approach adopted by Stenlund
[
7
]
.

Multiplying both sides of (
3
) by
\tau^{n}
and then summing
over
n
for
n\geq 0
, we can manipulate the generating function

\displaystyle Q(\tau):=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}Q_{n}\tau^{n}=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}Q_{n}\tau^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{\lam}{n}\bigg)(-\tau)^{n}-x\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k}\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{y}{k}\bigg)Q_{n-k}\tau^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-\tau)^{\lambda}-x\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{y}{k}\bigg)\tau^{k}\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}Q_{n-k}\tau^{n-k}.

This leads us to the functional equation

Q(\tau)=(1-\tau)^{\lambda}-xQ(\tau)\{(1-\tau)^{y}-1\}

and the rational function expression.

Lemma 1

(Generating function)
.

Q(\tau)=\frac{(1-\tau)^{\lambda}}{1-x+x(1-\tau)^{y}}.

By writing in the geometric series

\displaystyle Q(\tau)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-x}\times\frac{(1-\tau)^{\lambda}}{1-\frac{x}{x-1}(1-\tau)^{y}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-x)^{k}}{(1-x)^{k+1}}(1-\tau)^{\lambda+ky}

and then extracting the coefficient of
\tau^{n}
,
we find the infinite series expression.

Proposition 2

(Single sum formula)
.

Q_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n+k}\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{\lam+ky}{n}\bigg)\frac{x^{k}}{(1-x)^{k+1}}.

Expanding the rightmost fraction into binomial series,
we have further

\displaystyle Q_{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n+k}\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{\lam+ky}{n}\bigg)\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{k+j}{j}\bigg)x^{k+j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}x^{m}\sum_{k=0}^{m}(-1)^{k}\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{m}{k}\bigg)\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{\lam+ky}{n}\bigg),

where the last line is justified by the replacement
k+j=m
.
Observe that the inner sum with respect to
k
results substantially
in the differences of order
m
about a polynomial of degree
n
,
which is equal to zero when
m>n
. Therefore, we have derived
a finite double sum expression.

Proposition 3

(Double sum formula)
.

Q_{n}=\sum_{m=0}^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{m}(-1)^{n+k}\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{m}{k}\bigg)\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{\lam+ky}{n}\bigg)x^{m}.

Expressing the last binomial coefficient in terms
of unsigned Stirling numbers and then making use
of the binomial theorem, we have

\displaystyle\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{\lam+ky}{n}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{\langle{\lambda+ky}\rangle_{n}}{n!}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{n-i}}{n!}\bigg[\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{n}{i}\bigg](\lambda+yk)^{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{n-i}}{n!}\bigg[\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{n}{i}\bigg]\sum_{j=0}^{i}\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{i}{j}\bigg)\lambda^{i-j}(ky)^{j}.

By substitutions,
Q_{n}
can be written as a four–fold sum

\displaystyle Q_{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{m}{k}\bigg)x^{m}\sum_{i=0}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{k-i}}{n!}\bigg[\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{n}{i}\bigg]\sum_{j=0}^{i}\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{i}{j}\bigg)\lambda^{i-j}(ky)^{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{n}x^{m}\sum_{i=0}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{m-i}}{n!}\bigg[\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{n}{i}\bigg]\sum_{j=0}^{i}\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{i}{j}\bigg)\lambda^{i-j}y^{j}\sum_{k=0}^{m}(-1)^{m-k}\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{m}{k}\bigg)k^{j}.

Writing the rightmost sum in terms of Stirling number of the second kind,
we get a triple sum expression

Q_{n}=\sum_{m=0}^{n}x^{m}\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{m-i}\frac{m!}{n!}\bigg[\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{n}{i}\bigg]\sum_{j=0}^{i}\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{i}{j}\bigg)\bigg\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{j}{m}\bigg\}\lambda^{i-j}y^{j}.

(4)

Taking into account that
\bigg\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{j}{m}\bigg\}=0
for
0\leq j<m

and
\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{i}{j}\bigg)=0
for
0\leq i<j
, we can restate
the last formula as follows.

Proposition 4

(Triple sum formula)
.

Q_{n}=\sum_{m=0}^{n}\sum_{i=m}^{n}\sum_{j=m}^{i}(-1)^{m-i}\frac{m!}{n!}\bigg[\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{n}{i}\bigg]\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{i}{j}\bigg)\bigg\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{j}{m}\bigg\}x^{m}\lambda^{i-j}y^{j}.

By comparing the coefficients of
x^{m}
between the two expressions
in Proposition
3
and Proposition
4
, we derive
the following summation formula.

Theorem 5

(Convolution formula)
.

\sum_{i=m}^{n}\sum_{j=m}^{i}(-1)^{n+i}\bigg[\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{n}{i}\bigg]\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{i}{j}\bigg)\bigg\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{j}{m}\bigg\}\lambda^{i-j}y^{j}=\frac{n!}{m!}\sum_{k=0}^{m}(-1)^{m+k}\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{m}{k}\bigg)\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{\lam+ky}{n}\bigg).

3.
Applications to Convolution Formulae

By specifying
y
to particular values, we shall derive
from Theorem
5
two pairs of combinatorial
identities, that extend, with an extra parameter
“
\lambda
”, the related known convolution formulae
on Stirling numbers.

The first pair of identities are given in the following theorem.

Theorem 6

(
1\leq m\leq n
)
.

\displaystyle\emph{(a)}\quad

\displaystyle\sum_{i=m}^{n}\sum_{j=m}^{i}(-1)^{n+i}\bigg[\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{n}{i}\bigg]\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{i}{j}\bigg)\bigg\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{j}{m}\bigg\}\lambda^{i-j}=\frac{n!}{m!}\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{\lam}{n-m}\bigg),

\displaystyle\emph{(b)}\quad

\displaystyle\sum_{i=m}^{n}\sum_{j=m}^{i}(-1)^{i+j}\bigg[\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{n}{i}\bigg]\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{i}{j}\bigg)\bigg\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{j}{m}\bigg\}\lambda^{i-j}=\frac{n!}{m!}\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{\lam-m}{n-m}\bigg)(-1)^{m+n}.

They contain the two well–known results
below as special cases of
\lambda=0
.

Corollary 7

(
1\leq m\leq n
)
.

\displaystyle\emph{(a)}\quad

\displaystyle\sum_{k=m}^{n}(-1)^{n-k}\bigg[\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{n}{k}\bigg]\bigg\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{k}{m}\bigg\}=\begin{cases}1,&m=n;\\
0,&m\not=n;\end{cases}

\displaystyle\emph{(b)}\quad

\displaystyle\sum_{k=m}^{n}\bigg[\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{n}{k}\bigg]\bigg\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{k}{m}\bigg\}=\frac{n!}{m!}\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{n-1}{m-1}\bigg).\qquad\framebox{\emph{Lah number}}

Proof of Theorem
6
.
Letting
y=1
in Theorem
5
, the corresponding
equality becomes

\sum_{i=m}^{n}\sum_{j=m}^{i}(-1)^{n+i}\bigg[\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{n}{i}\bigg]\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{i}{j}\bigg)\bigg\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{j}{m}\bigg\}\lambda^{i-j}=\frac{n!}{m!}\sum_{k=0}^{m}(-1)^{m+k}\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{m}{k}\bigg)\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{\lam+k}{n}\bigg).

Then the first formula “(a)” follows by evaluating
the above sum on the right

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{m}(-1)^{m+k}\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{m}{k}\bigg)\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{\lam+k}{n}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{m}(-1)^{m+k}\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{m}{k}\bigg)\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{\lam}{n-\ell}\bigg)\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{k}{\ell}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{\lam}{n-\ell}\bigg)\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{m}{\ell}\bigg)\sum_{k=\ell}^{m}(-1)^{m+k}\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{m-\ell}{k-\ell}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{\lam}{n-\ell}\bigg)\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{m}{\ell}\bigg)(1-1)^{m-\ell}=\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{\lam}{n-m}\bigg),

where the Chu–Vandemonde convolution formula
and the binomial theorem have been utilized.

Alternatively for
y=-1
, the corresponding equality
in Theorem
5
reads as

\sum_{i=m}^{n}\sum_{j=m}^{i}(-1)^{i+j}\bigg[\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{n}{i}\bigg]\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{i}{j}\bigg)\bigg\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{j}{m}\bigg\}\lambda^{i-j}=(-1)^{m-n}\frac{n!}{m!}\sum_{k=0}^{m}(-1)^{k}\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{m}{k}\bigg)\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{\lam-k}{n}\bigg).

Then we can analogously evaluate the sum on the right as follows:

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{m}(-1)^{k}\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{m}{k}\bigg)\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{\lam-k}{n}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{m}(-1)^{k}\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{m}{k}\bigg)\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{\lam-m}{n-\ell}\bigg)\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{m-k}{\ell}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{\lam-m}{n-\ell}\bigg)\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{m}{\ell}\bigg)\sum_{k=0}^{m-\ell}(-1)^{k}\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{m-\ell}{k}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{\lam-m}{n-\ell}\bigg)\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{m}{\ell}\bigg)(1-1)^{m-\ell}=\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{\lam-m}{n-m}\bigg).

This proves the second formula “(b)” in the theorem.∎

We can further show another pair of identities.

Theorem 8

(
1\leq m\leq n
)
.

\displaystyle\emph{(a)}\quad\sum_{i=m}^{n}

\displaystyle\sum_{j=m}^{i}(-1)^{n+i}\bigg[\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{n}{i}\bigg]\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{i}{j}\bigg)\bigg\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{j}{m}\bigg\}\lambda^{i-j}2^{n-j}

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\frac{n!}{m!}\sum_{\ell=m}^{n}\frac{m}{\ell}\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{\lam}{n-\ell}\bigg)\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{-\ell}{\ell-m}\bigg)2^{m+n-2\ell},

\displaystyle\emph{(b)}\quad\sum_{i=m}^{n}

\displaystyle\sum_{j=m}^{i}(-1)^{m+i}\bigg[\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{n}{i}\bigg]\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{i}{j}\bigg)\bigg\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{j}{m}\bigg\}\lambda^{i-j}2^{j-m}

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\frac{n!}{m!}\sum_{\ell=m}^{n}(-1)^{m+n}\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{\lam}{n-\ell}\bigg)\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{m}{\ell-m}\bigg)2^{m-\ell}.

Proof of Theorem
8
.
For
y=\frac{1}{2}
, the equality in Theorem
5
reduces to

\sum_{i=m}^{n}\sum_{j=m}^{i}(-1)^{n+i}\bigg[\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{n}{i}\bigg]\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{i}{j}\bigg)\bigg\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{j}{m}\bigg\}\lambda^{i-j}2^{n-j}=2^{n}\frac{n!}{m!}\sum_{k=0}^{m}(-1)^{m+k}\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{m}{k}\bigg)\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{\lam+\frac{k}2}{n}\bigg).

In this case, the first formula “(a)” is confirmed
by first reformulating the above sum on the right

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{m}(-1)^{m+k}\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{m}{k}\bigg)\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{\lam+\frac{k}2}{n}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{m}(-1)^{m+k}\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{m}{k}\bigg)\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{\lam}{n-\ell}\bigg)\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{\frac{k}2}{\ell}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{\lam}{n-\ell}\bigg)\sum_{k=0}^{m}(-1)^{m+k}\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{m}{k}\bigg)\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{\frac{k}2}{\ell}\bigg)

and then evaluating the inner sum by the binomial
formula (cf.
[
4
, Equation 3.164]
)

\sum_{k=0}^{m}(-1)^{m-k}\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{m}{k}\bigg)\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{\frac{k}2}{\ell}\bigg)=\frac{m}{\ell}\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{-\ell}{\ell-m}\bigg)2^{m-2\ell}.

When
y=2
, the corresponding equality in Theorem
5
can be stated as

\sum_{i=m}^{n}\sum_{j=m}^{i}(-1)^{m+i}\bigg[\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{n}{i}\bigg]\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{i}{j}\bigg)\bigg\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{j}{m}\bigg\}\lambda^{i-j}2^{j-m}=2^{-m}\frac{n!}{m!}\sum_{k=0}^{m}(-1)^{n+k}\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{m}{k}\bigg)\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{\lam+2k}{n}\bigg).

The above sum on the right can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{m}(-1)^{n+k}\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{m}{k}\bigg)\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{\lam+2k}{n}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{m}(-1)^{n+k}\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{m}{k}\bigg)\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{\lam}{n-\ell}\bigg)\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{2k}{\ell}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}(-1)^{n}\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{\lam}{n-\ell}\bigg)\sum_{k=0}^{m}(-1)^{k}\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{m}{k}\bigg)\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{2k}{\ell}\bigg).

Denote by
[x^{n}]f(x)
the coefficient of
x^{n}

in the formal power series
f(x)
. Observe that
the last inner sum can be expressed and then evaluated as

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{m}(-1)^{k}\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{m}{k}\bigg)\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{2k}{\ell}\bigg)

\displaystyle=[x^{\ell}]\sum_{k=0}^{m}(-1)^{k}\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{m}{k}\bigg)(1+x)^{2k}

\displaystyle=[x^{\ell}]\Big\{1-(1+x)^{2}\Big\}^{m}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{m}[x^{\ell-m}](2+x)^{m}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{m}\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{m}{\ell-m}\bigg)2^{2m-\ell}.

After substitutions, the second formula “(b)”
in the theorem is done.∎

Finally letting
\lambda=0
in Theorem
8
, we recover the
following two elegant identities due to Yang and Qiao
[
8
]
,
who discovered them by employing the Riordan array and expressed
the results in terms of Bessel numbers
[
2
]
.

Corollary 9

(
1\leq m\leq n
)
.

\displaystyle\emph{(a)}\quad

\displaystyle\sum_{k=m}^{n}\bigg[\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{n}{k}\bigg]\bigg\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{k}{m}\bigg\}(-2)^{n-k}=\frac{(n-1)!}{(m-1)!}\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{-n}{n-m}\bigg)2^{m-n},

\displaystyle\emph{(b)}\quad

\displaystyle\sum_{k=m}^{n}\bigg[\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{n}{k}\bigg]\bigg\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{k}{m}\bigg\}(-2)^{j-m}=\frac{n!}{m!}\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{m}{n-m}\bigg)(-2)^{m-n}.

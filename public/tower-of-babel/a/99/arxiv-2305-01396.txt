Gap Theorem on Riemannian manifolds using Ricci flow
Pak-Yeung Chan, Man-Chun Lee

Abstract
. In this work, we use the Ricci flow approach to study the gap phenomenon of Riemannian manifolds with non-negative curvature and sub-critical scaling invariant curvature decay. The first main result is a quantitative Ricci flow existence theory without non-collapsing assumption. We use it to show that complete non-compact manifolds with non-negative complex sectional curvature and sufficiently small average curvature decay are necessarily flat. The second main result concerns three-manifolds with non-negative Ricci curvature of quadratic decay. By combining our newly established curvature estimate and method in Kähler geometry, we show that if the curvature decays slightly faster even in average sense, the manifold must be flat. This strengthens a result of Reiris. In the compact case, we use the Ricci flow regularization to generalize the celebrated Gromov-Ruh Theorem in this direction.

1.
Introduction

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a complete Riemannian manifold. The purpose of this work is to study the geometric quantity

k(x,r)=r^{2}\fint_{B_{g_{0}}(x,r)}|\text{\rm Rm}(g_{0})|\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}

on manifolds with non-negative or almost non-negative curvature. The quantity is natural in the sense that it is scaling invariant and is sometimes regarded as the
L^{1}
version of Morrey bound on curvature. We consider the case where volume is rescaled so that
k(\cdot,r)
measures the flatness in an average sense. In Kähler geometry, it has been studied extensively and is deeply related to the function theory, for example see
[
37
,
36
]
.

The geometric significance of
k(\cdot,r)
is on two-folds. In the non-compact case, when
r
tends to infinity, it measures the complexity of the infinity in an averaging sense and is related to the gap phenomenon of flat metric. When
M
is complete non-compact with non-negative curvature, we are interested in asking how much positive curvature such a manifold could have. By the classical theorem of Bonnet-Meyer, it is clear that the curvature can’t be too positive point-wise otherwise the manifold will be closing up at infinity contradicting the non-compactness. One might ask to what extent a non-flat metric will stay away from the flat metric at infinity. In dimension two, it is not difficult to construct metric with zero curvature outside compact set, non-negative curvature everywhere, and positive curvature somewhere while in higher dimensions, the situation is different. The first result of this type was originated by Mok-Siu-Yau
[
34
]
where it was proved that when the complex dimension
m\geq 2
,
M
is isometrically biholomorphic to the Euclidean space if it has non-negative holomorphic bisectional curvature, Euclidean volume growth and has “faster-than-quadratic” curvature decay. Shortly after, Greene-Wu
[
19
]
considered the more general Riemannian case and proved that manifold with a pole, with “faster-than-quadratic” curvature decay and with non-negative sectional curvature is necessarily flat if the dimension of the manifold is
\geq 3
except when the dimension is 4 or 8. The pole assumption was later removed by Eschenburg-Schroeder-Strake
[
15
]
and Dress
[
14
]
. See also the relatively more recent work of Greene-Petersen-Zhu
[
20
]
.

On the other hand, in the Kähler case, effort has been made to improve the gap in Theorem of Mok-Siu-Yau. In
[
38
]
, Ni found the optimal gap in term of
k(\cdot,r)
which states that a complete non-compact Kähler manifold must be flat if it has non-negative holomorphic bisectional curvature and
k(x_{0},r)=o(1)
as
r\to+\infty
for some
x_{0}\in M
. See also
[
39
,
8
,
30
]
for more related works. The method employed by Ni
[
38
]
is based on finding the Ricci potential via solving the Poincaré-Lelong equation on non-compact Kähler manifolds. With the sub-critical decay rate of
k(o,r)
as
r\to+\infty
for some
o\in M
. One can find
u\in C^{\infty}_{loc}(M)
such that
\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}u=\text{\rm Ric}(g_{0})
and
u=o(\log r)
. Hence, the flatness of
g_{0}
follows from a Liouville Theorem for pluri-subharmonic on non-negatively curved Kähler manifolds. Thus, the Kähler structure of the manifold plays an important role.

In this work we are interested in its Riemannian analogy. One first needs to identify a suitable non-negativity of curvature. Our first result is partially motivated by the parallel theory of Kähler-Ricci flow. We consider the complex sectional curvature in general dimension. To clarify the notation, we say that an algebraic curvature tensor
\mathrm{R}
has non-negative complex sectional curvature
\mathrm{K}^{\mathbb{C}}(\mathrm{R})\geq 0
if to each two-complex-dimensional subspace
\Sigma
, the complexified
\mathrm{R}
satisfies
\mathrm{R}(u,v,\bar{u},\bar{v})\geq 0
for all unitary basis
\{u,v\}
of
\Sigma
. If one instead asks for non-negativity of complex sectional curvature only for PIC1 sections, defined to be those
\Sigma
that contain some non-zero vector
v
whose conjugate
\bar{v}
is orthogonal to
\Sigma
, then we say that
\mathrm{R}\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{PIC1}}
. When
n=3
, non-negative complex sectional curvature is equivalent to non-negative sectional curvature while
\mathrm{R}\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{PIC1}}
is equivalent to
\text{\rm Ric}\geq 0
. When
n\geq 4
, one can equivalently describe
\mathrm{K}^{\mathbb{C}}(\mathrm{R})\geq 0
by requiring

\mathrm{R}_{1331}+\lambda^{2}\mathrm{R}_{1441}+\mu^{2}\mathrm{R}_{2332}+\lambda^{2}\mu^{2}\mathrm{R}_{2442}+2\lambda\mu\mathrm{R}_{1234}\geq 0

for any orthonormal four-frames
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{4}
and
\lambda,\mu\in[0,1]
. Similarly,
\mathrm{R}\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{PIC1}}
if

\mathrm{R}_{1331}+\lambda^{2}\mathrm{R}_{1441}+\mathrm{R}_{2332}+\lambda^{2}\mathrm{R}_{2442}+2\lambda\mathrm{R}_{1234}\geq 0

for any orthonormal four-frames
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{4}
and
\lambda\in[0,1]
. We refer interested readers to the book by Brendle
[
3
]
for an overview on their importance in differentiable sphere Theorem. Motivated by Ni’s optimal gap Theorem in the Kähler case, we have the following gap Theorem in the Riemannian case under an asymptotic condition of
k(\cdot,r)
and
without
volume growth assumption.

Theorem 1.1
.

There exists
\varepsilon_{0}(n)>0
such that the following holds: If
(M^{n},g_{0})
is a complete non-compact manifold such that
n\geq 3
,
\mathrm{K}^{\mathbb{C}}(g_{0})\geq 0
and

(1.1)

\int^{+\infty}_{0}s\left(\fint_{B_{g_{0}}(x,s)}|\text{\rm Rm}(g_{0})|\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}\right)ds<\varepsilon_{0}

for all
x\in M
. Then
(M,g_{0})
is flat.

We would like to stress that the gap here is scaling invariant and can be computed explicitly. Moreover,
g_{0}
is not necessarily of Euclidean volume growth. In particular, the Ricci flow theory and compactness theory are not well-understood in this setting. To the best of our knowledge, this seems to be the first gap Theorem in Riemannian case under condition in
k(\cdot,r)
. In contrast with the Kähler case by Ni
[
38
]
, the asymptotic assumption on
k(\cdot,r)
is stronger. The Kähler structure is however unnecessary and in particular, attacking the question using the function theory is in fact unavailable at the present. Our approach is largely motivated by a gap Theorem of Chen-Zhu
[
9
]
which relies on using the long-time asymptotic of Kähler-Ricci flow. The strategy we use is to deform the given metric by Ricci flow for all time and to analyse its long-time asymptotic behaviour. By showing that the blow-down Ricci flow is asymptotically flat, one can prove that the metric is initially flat using Brendle’s Harnack inequality
[
2
]
in case of
\mathrm{K}^{\mathbb{C}}(g(t))\geq 0
or Colding’s volume convergence
[
7
]
in case of Euclidean volume growth.
To implement the strategy, the fundamental difficulty
is to run the flow for all time. Although the metric is expected to be flat or close to be flat under suitable average curvature decay condition, the manifold can still be a-priori very complicated at infinity. In particular, there is no general existence theory on producing the Ricci flow even for a short-time. Although a theory for Ricci flow starting with general complete manifolds with non-negative complex sectional curvature was developed by Cabezas-Rivas and Wilking
[
5
]
, we need to develop one with quantitative estimates. And more importantly, we need to find a way to extract curvature estimate from the
initial
asymptotic behaviour of
k(\cdot,r)
, both in
r\to 0
and
r\to+\infty
. In this regard, we obtain a heat kernel estimate for the operator
\partial_{t}-\Delta_{g(t)}-\mathcal{R}_{g(t)}
. The heat kernel estimate was previously studied in
[
5
]
for
non-collapsing
Ricci flows with scaling invariant curvature decay. In this work, we combine the ideas in
[
5
]
with the idea of the relative volume comparison Theorem in
[
50
]
to establish a heat kernel estimate which
does not
rely on volume non-collapsing. Using this, we establish a quantitative short-time existence theory which
only
relies on
k(\cdot,r)
and a lower bound of curvature. For notation convenience, throughout this work we will use
a\wedge b
to denote
\min\{a,b\}
for
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

For any
n\geq 3
, there exist
\varepsilon_{0}(n),{\alpha}_{n},S_{n},L_{n}>0
such that the following holds: Suppose
(M,g_{0})
is a complete manifold and for some
r>0
and
\Lambda_{0}\in(0,1)
, the initial metric
g_{0}
satisfies

(a)

\inf_{M}K(g_{0})>-\infty
;

(b)

(i)

\mathrm{Rm}(g_{0})+\Lambda_{0}r^{-2}g_{0}\owedge g_{0}/2\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{PIC1}}
if
n\geq 4
; or

(ii)

K(g_{0})+\Lambda_{0}r^{-2}\geq 0
if
n=3
,

(c)

for all
x\in M
,

(1.2)

\int^{r}_{0}s\left(\fint_{B_{g_{0}}(x,s)}|\text{\rm Rm}(g_{0})|\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}\right)ds<\varepsilon_{0}.

Then there exists a complete short-time solution
g(t)
to the Ricci flow on
M\times[0,S_{n}(r^{2}\wedge\mathrm{diam}(M,g_{0})^{2})]
with
g(0)=g_{0}
and satisfies

(I)

\sup_{M}|\text{\rm Rm}(g(t))|\leq{\alpha}_{n}t^{-1}
;

(II)

\mathrm{Rm}(g(t))+L_{n}\Lambda_{0}r^{-2}g(t)\owedge g(t)/2\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{PIC1}}
if
n\geq 4
;

(III)

K(g(t))+L_{n}\Lambda_{0}r^{-2}\geq 0
if
n=3

on
M\times(0,S_{n}(r^{2}\wedge\mathrm{diam}(M,g_{0})^{2})]
. When
M
is non-compact,
\mathrm{diam}(M,g_{0})
is understood to be
+\infty
.

Here
\owedge
denotes the Kulkarni-Nomizu product and
\frac{1}{2}g\owedge g
refers to the curvature tensor of standard sphere. The quantitative existence theory also provides us a long-time existence of Ricci flow with estimates under the assumption in Theorem
1.1
, see Proposition
5.1
. We require small integral bound on
k(x,r)
uniform in
x\in M
. This is deeply related to the uniform regularization of the Ricci flow throughout
M
. In fact, the asymptotic of
k(\cdot,r)
when
r\to 0
detects how regular the centre is. In particular, if the curvature is bounded,
k(\cdot,r)=O(r^{2})
while the metric cone as a singular model will have
k(o,r)=O(1)
at the tip
o
. In this sense, the integrability of
r^{-1}k(\cdot,r)
at
r=0
is indeed measuring asymptotically how flat the tangent cone is. It is also interesting to compare this with Shi’s long-time existence criteria of the Kähler-Ricci flow
[
46
]
in the Kähler case with bounded non-negative bisectional curvature. In comparison with Theorem
1.2
, Shi showed that in this case, the Kähler-Ricci flow exists for all time with curvature decay in
{\alpha}t^{-1}
for some
{\alpha}>0
if
k(x,r)
is uniformly bounded for all
x\in M
and
r>0
. In view of the application of the Kähler-Ricci flow to Yau’s uniformization conjecture, it will be important to see to what extent the result of Kähler-Ricci flow can be generalized to Ricci flow.

On the other hand, it is interesting to compare Theorem
1.1
with the Bryant expanding Ricci soliton with positive curvature operator which has Euclidean volume growth and
|\text{\rm Rm}|\leq C(d_{g}(x,x_{0})^{2}+1)^{-1}
but is non-flat (see
[
4
,
12
]
). Using the method we develop in the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we shows that in the case of Euclidean volume growth, if we
strengthen the decay rate slightly even in the average sense, then the manifold is necessarily isometric to the Euclidean space. In this sense, the gap Theorem is optimal.

Theorem 1.3
.

Suppose
(M^{n},g_{0})
is a complete non-compact manifold for
n\geq 3
such that
g_{0}
has Euclidean volume growth and

(i)

\mathrm{Rm}(g_{0})\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{PIC1}}
if
n\geq 4
or;

(ii)

\mathrm{K}(g_{0})\geq 0
if
n=3
.

If there exists
x_{0}\in M
such that

k(x_{0},r)=r^{2}\fint_{B_{g_{0}}(x_{0},r)}|\text{\rm Rm}(g_{0})|\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}=o(1)

as
r\to+\infty
, then
(M,g_{0})
is isometric to the flat Euclidean space.

Our second main result concerns the three dimension case which is motivated by the work of Reiris. In
[
41
]
, he studied the relation between the point-wise quadratic curvature decay and volume non-collapsing. Among other things, he showed that if
g_{0}
is a complete metric on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with quadratic curvature decay, then it must be of Euclidean volume growth. It is natural to ask what we can say if we strengthen the curvature decay slightly. At the same time, one might compare the setting with Theorem
1.3
. Even if the
\mathrm{PIC1}
is a natural generalization of
\text{\rm Ric}\geq 0
in dimension three, the Ricci flow does not behave in the same way as in the proof of Theorem
1.3
. We are able to overcome this by using some special feature in dimension three. By using Liu’s splitting Theorem
[
31
]
, method in Kähler geometry
[
38
]
and the heat kernel estimate, we establish the following:

Theorem 1.4
.

Suppose
(M^{3},g_{0})
is complete non-compact manifold such that
\text{\rm Ric}(g_{0})\geq 0
and for some
x_{0}\in M
, we have

(1.3)

\limsup_{x\to+\infty}\left(d_{g_{0}}(x,x_{0})^{2}\cdot\mathcal{R}(g_{0}(x))\right)<+\infty\quad\text{and}\quad k(x_{0},r)=o(1)

as
r\to+\infty
. Then
(M^{3},g_{0})
is flat.

Here
\mathcal{R}
denotes the scalar curvature. By taking the product of
\mathbb{R}
with a metric in dimension two which is conical at infinity, we can easily see that the asymptotic of
k(x_{0},\cdot)
is optimal. This strengthens the result of Reiris
[
41
]
and improves the gap Theorem in
[
19
,
20
]
. The analogous statement in higher dimensions cannot be true unless we strengthen the curvature or topological conditions which can be easily seen from the Eguchi-Hanson metric.

Theorem
1.2
can be regarded as a result of regularization with possibly collapsing initial data. The situation where the volume is collapsing with almost vanishing curvature has been studied extensively in the past. In particular, the Gromov–Ruh Theorem
[
21
,
43
]
states that if one normalizes the diameter of
(M,g)
to be 1, then
M
is diffeomorphic to an infranil manifold if its curvature is sufficiently small depending only on some dimensional constant. It has recently been generalized by Chen-Wei-Ye
[
11
]
to
L^{n/2}
bound of curvature weighed by squared Sobolev constant. Motivated by their method, we generalize the Gromov–Ruh Theorem in direction using
k(\cdot,r)
.

Theorem 1.5
.

For any
n\geq 3
, there exists
\varepsilon_{0}(n)>0
such that the following holds: A compact manifold
M^{n}
is diffeomorphic to an infranil manifold if it admits a metric
g_{0}
such that

(i)

\mathrm{Rm}(g_{0})+\varepsilon_{0}\cdot\mathrm{diam}(g_{0})^{-2}g_{0}\owedge g_{0}\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{PIC1}}
if
n\geq 4
;

(ii)

\mathrm{K}(g_{0})\geq-\varepsilon_{0}\cdot\mathrm{diam}(g_{0})^{-2}
if
n=3
;

(iii)

for all
x\in M
,

(1.4)

\int^{\mathrm{diam}(M,g_{0})}_{0}s\left(\fint_{B_{g_{0}}(x,s)}|\text{\rm Rm}(g_{0})|\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}\right)\,ds<\varepsilon_{0}.

2.
Some preliminaries on Ricci flow

The novel idea of this work is to regularize the initial metric
g_{0}
using Ricci flow. This is a one parameter family of metrics
g(t)
satisfying

(2.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\partial_{t}g(t)=-2\text{\rm Ric}(g(t));\\
g(0)=g_{0}\end{array}\right.

In this section, we will collect some preliminaries of Ricci flow technique which will be used throughout this work.

2.1.
Heat kernel estimates

To obtain curvature estimate, we will make use of the heat kernel coupled with the Ricci flow. Let
g(t)
be a complete Ricci flow on
M\times[0,T]
with
g(0)=g_{0}
. Let
\Omega\Subset M
be an open set with smooth boundary. We let
G(x,t;y,s),t>s
be the dirichlet heat kernel for the backward heat equation coupled with the Ricci flow
g(t)
:

(2.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\left(\partial_{s}+\Delta_{y,g(s)}\right)G(x,t;y,s)=0,\quad\text{on}\quad\Omega\times\Omega\times[0,t);\\
\lim_{s\to t^{-}}G(x,t;y,s)=\delta_{x}(y),\quad\text{for}\;x\in\Omega;\\
G(x,t;y,s)=0,\quad\text{for}\;x\in\Omega\;\text{and}\;y\in\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

Then,

(2.3)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\left(\partial_{t}-\Delta_{x,g(t)}-\mathcal{R}(g(x,t))\right)G(x,t;y,s)=0,\quad\text{on}\quad\Omega\times\Omega\times(s,T];\\
\lim_{t\to s^{+}}G(x,t;y,s)=\delta_{y}(x),\quad\text{for}\;y\in\Omega;\\
G(x,t;y,s)=0,\quad\text{for}\;y\in\Omega\;\text{and}\;x\in\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

Such
G
exists and is positive in the interior of
\Omega
, see
[
22
]
. In this work, all heat kernel will be referring to the heat kernel with respect to
\partial_{t}-\Delta_{g(t)}-\mathcal{R}
as described above. We have the following heat kernel estimates modified from
[
29
]
building on
[
1
]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

For any
n,{\alpha}>0
, there exists
C_{0}(n,{\alpha})>0
such that the following holds: Suppose
(M^{n},g(t))
is a complete solution to the Ricci flow on
M\times[0,T]
with
g(0)=g_{0}
and satisfies

|\text{\rm Rm}(g(t))|\leq{\alpha}t^{-1}\text{ for some }{\alpha}>0.

Then for
0\leq 2s\leq t\leq T
and
x,y\in B_{g_{0}}(x_{0},r)
,

(2.4)

G(x,t;y,s)\leq\frac{C_{0}}{\mathrm{Vol}_{g(t)}\left(B_{g(t)}(x,\sqrt{t})\right)}\exp\left(-\frac{d_{g(s)}^{2}(x,y)}{C_{0}t}\right).

where
G
denotes the dirichlet heat kernel on
\Omega=B_{g_{0}}(x_{0},2r)
. The same estimate also holds for heat kernel on
M
.

.

It is not difficult to see that the completeness condition of

Proof.

Proof of Proposition
2.1
.

The proof is almost identical to
[
1

By parabolic scaling and time shift, W.L.O.G., we may assume that
t=1
,
s=0

It suffices to show the following

(2.9)

G_{\Omega}(x,1;y,0)\leq\frac{C}{\mathrm{Vol}_{g(1)}\left(B_{g(1)}(x,1)\right)}\exp\left(-\frac{d_{g(0)}^{2}(x,y)}{C}\right).

In particular, by taking

By the continuity of

,

(2.13)

\lim_{k\to\infty}a_{k}\geq\displaystyle\sup_{\Omega\setminus B_{g_{0}}(x,4d)}G_{\Omega}(x,1;\cdot,0).

We claim that there are positive constants

and

and positive integer

Let us assume the above claim and prove (

) and the volume comparison,

\begin{split}G_{\Omega}(x,1;y,0)&\leq\frac{C}{\mathrm{Vol}_{g(t_{1})}\left(B_{g(t_{1})}(x,\sqrt{1-t_{1}})\right)}\\

Then (

) holds in this case. Suppose

, we see from (

(2.15)

\begin{split}G_{\Omega}(x,1;y,0)&\leq\frac{C}{\mathrm{Vol}_{g(t_{1})}\left(B_{g(t_{1})}(x,\sqrt{1-t_{1}})\right)}\exp\left(-\frac{d_{g_{0}}^{2}(x,y)}{16C}\right)\\

\end{split}

for some

. It remains to justify the claim. By the semi-group property, for any

We estimate the integral on the right hand side by splitting it into the integrals over

and

\begin{split}&\int_{B_{k}\cap\Omega}G_{\Omega}(x,1;z,t_{k})G_{\Omega}(z,t_{k};y,t_{k+1})\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g(t_{k})}(z)\\
&\leq\int_{\Omega\setminus B_{0}(x,r_{k})}G_{\Omega}(x,1;z,t_{k})G_{\Omega}(z,t_{k};y,t_{k+1})\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g(t_{k})}(z)\leq a_{k}.\end{split}

By (

\begin{split}&\quad\int_{\Omega\setminus B_{k}}G_{\Omega}(x,1;z,t_{k})G_{\Omega}(z,t_{k};y,t_{k+1})\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g(t_{k})}(z)\\

(2.19)

\begin{split}a_{k+1}&\leq a_{k}+\frac{2C^{\prime}}{\mathrm{Vol}_{g(t_{1})}\left(B_{g(t_{1})}(x,\sqrt{1-t_{1}})\right)}\exp\left(-\frac{4^{-k}d^{2}}{2C^{\prime}16^{1-k}}\right)\\
&\leq a_{1}+\frac{C^{\prime\prime}}{\mathrm{Vol}_{g(t_{1})}\left(B_{g(t_{1})}(x,\sqrt{1-t_{1}})\right)}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\exp\left(-\frac{4^{i}d^{2}}{C^{\prime\prime}}\right)\\
&\leq\frac{C^{\prime\prime\prime}}{\mathrm{Vol}_{g(t_{1})}\left(B_{g(t_{1})}(x,\sqrt{1-t_{1}})\right)}\Big[\exp\left(-\frac{d_{g(t_{1})}^{2}(x,y)}{C(1-t_{1})}\right)\\
&\quad+\exp\left(-\frac{d^{2}}{C^{\prime\prime}}\right)\sum_{i=1}^{k}\exp\left(-\frac{(4^{i}-1)d^{2}}{C^{\prime\prime}}\right)\Big],\end{split}

where
y\in\overline{\Omega}\setminus B_{0}(x,2d)
. We also used (
2.11
) to bound

in the last inequality. Again by Hamilton Perelman distance distortion
[
40
]
(c.f.
[

]

a_{k+1}\leq\frac{C}{\mathrm{Vol}_{g(t_{1})}\left(B_{g(t_{1})}(x,\sqrt{1-t_{1}})\right)}\exp\left(-\frac{d^{2}}{C}\right)

as required.
∎

2.2.
Almost monotonicity of local entropy

In view of Proposition
2.1
, it is important to compare the volume of the evolving ball with the initial one. We will make use of the entropy. We recall the concept of local entropy introduced by Wang
[
52
]
. Let
\Omega
be a connected domain with possibly empty boundary in
M
, denote

D_{g}(\Omega):=\left\{u:u\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega),u\geq 0\text{ and }\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=1\right\}

and consider the following quantities

(2.20)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}&\displaystyle W(\Omega,g,u,\tau):=\int_{\Omega}\left[\tau(\mathcal{R}u^{2}+4|\nabla u|^{2})-2u^{2}\log u\right]\;d\mathrm{vol}_{g}-\frac{n}{2}\log(4\pi\tau)-n;\\
\\
&\displaystyle\mu(\Omega,g,\tau):=\inf_{u\in D_{g}(\Omega)}W(\Omega,g,u,\tau);\\
\\
&\displaystyle\nu(\Omega,g,\tau):=\inf_{s\in(0,\tau]}\mu(\Omega,g,s)\end{array}\right.

where
\mathcal{R}
denotes the scalar curvature of
(M,g)
.

The following Lemmas provide us the relation between entropy and the volume ratio under Ricci lower bound.

Lemma 2.2
.

There exists
C_{n}>0
such that the following holds. Suppose

\log\frac{\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{0}}(B_{g_{0}}(x_{0},r))}{r^{n}}\leq C_{n}+\nu\left(B_{g_{0}}(x_{0},r),g_{0},r^{2}\right).

In particular, if
\text{\rm Ric}(g_{0})\geq 0
on

, then (
2.21
) holds for all
r>0
.

Proof.

This follows from
[
50
, Corollary 2.2]
and
[
52
, Theorem 3.6]
.
∎

The next result is proved by Wang stating that the entropy lower bound also gives rise to a lower bound of volume ratio under a bound on scalar curvature.

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose

Proof.

This follows directly from
[

, Theorem 3.3]

∎

When
g(t)
is a complete Ricci flow on

with bounded curvature, it is proved by Wang

Theorem 2.1

3.

In this section, we will consider

Theorem 3.1
.

For any

, there exist
\varepsilon_{0}(n),{\alpha}_{n},S_{n},L_{n}>0
such that the following holds:
Suppose
(M,h_{0})
is a complete manifold with bounded curvature. If for some

h_{0}
satisfies

(a)

for some

\mathrm{Rm}(h_{0})+\Lambda_{0}r^{-2}h_{0}\owedge h_{0}/2\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{PIC1}}

; or

(ii)

K(h_{0})+\Lambda_{0}r^{-2}\geq 0
if
n=3
,

(b)

for all
x\in M

\int^{r}_{0}s\left(\fint_{B_{g_{0}}(x,r)}|\text{\rm Rm}(h_{0})|\,d\mathrm{vol}_{h_{0}}\right)\,ds<\varepsilon_{0}.

Then the complete bounded curvature Ricci flow
h(t)
on
M
starting with
h(0)=h_{0}
exists up to
S_{n}\cdot(r^{2}\wedge\mathrm{diam}(M,h_{0})^{2})
and satisfies

(I)

\sup_{N}|\text{\rm Rm}(h(t))|\leq{\alpha}_{n}t^{-1}

(II)

\mathrm{Rm}(h(t))+L_{n}\Lambda_{0}r^{-2}h(t)\owedge h(t)/2\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{PIC1}}
if
n\geq 4
;

(III)

K(h(t))+L_{n}\Lambda_{0}r^{-2}\geq 0
if
n=3
.

If
M
is complete non-compact,
\mathrm{diam}(M,h_{0})
is understood to be
+\infty
.

For notation convenience, we denote

(3.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}&k_{h_{0}}(x,r)=\displaystyle r^{2}\fint_{B_{h_{0}}(x,r)}|\text{\rm Rm}(h_{0})|\,d\mathrm{vol}_{h_{0}};\\
&f_{h_{0}}(x,r)=\displaystyle\int^{r}_{0}s^{-1}k_{h_{0}}(x,s)ds.\end{array}\right.

The notation is scaling invariant in the sense that
k_{\lambda^{2}h_{0}}(\lambda r)=k_{h_{0}}(r)
and
f_{\lambda^{2}h_{0}}(\lambda r)=f_{h_{0}}(r)
for
\lambda>0
. We will omit the index

if the content is clear.

We first observe that the bound on

will imply a bound on

by a simple comparison argument under assumption on Ricci lower bound.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any
n\geq 3
, there exists
C_{n}>0
such that the following holds: Suppose
\text{\rm Ric}(h_{0})\geq-(n-1)
on
M
, then for all

and

k(x,r)\leq C_{n}(f(x,2r)-f(x,r)).

Proof.

By volume comparison for all
s\in[r,2r]
and
0<r\leq 1
,

(3.2)

\begin{split}k(x_{0},r)&=r^{2}\fint_{B_{h_{0}}(x_{0},r)}|\text{\rm Rm}(h_{0})|\,d\mathrm{vol}_{h_{0}}\\
&\leq\frac{r^{2}}{s^{2}}\frac{{\mathrm{Vol}_{h_{0}}(B_{h_{0}}(x_{0},s))}}{\mathrm{Vol}_{h_{0}}(B_{h_{0}}(x_{0},r))}s^{2}\fint_{B_{h_{0}}(x_{0},s)}|\text{\rm Rm}(h_{0})|\,d\mathrm{vol}_{h_{0}}\\
&\leq C_{n}\cdot k(x_{0},s).\end{split}

Hence,

(3.3)

\begin{split}k(x_{0},r)&\leq C_{n}\int^{2r}_{r}s^{-1}k(x_{0},s)ds\\
&=C_{n}\left(f(x_{0},2r)-f(x_{0},r)\right).\end{split}

∎

The key observation is a curvature inequality, which says that
\mathcal{R}\,g-2\text{\rm Ric}\geq 0
under nonnegative sectional curvature condition (see, for instances,
[
35
, Proposition 5.4]
and
[
27

).
We observe that a similar inequality still holds under
\mathrm{PIC}_{1}
condition, this eventually leads to an improvement on the evolution inequality of scalar curvature under almost
\mathrm{PIC}_{1}
condition.

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose
\mathrm{R}\in\mathrm{C}
, i.e. the cone of curvature type operator, such that

(a)

\mathrm{R}\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{PIC1}}
if
n\geq 4

(b)

\mathrm{K}(\mathrm{R})\geq 0
if

,

then

\text{\rm Ric}(\mathrm{R})\leq\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{scal}(\mathrm{R})\cdot g.

Proof.

Suppose
\lambda_{i}
is the eigenvalues of
\text{\rm Ric}(\mathrm{R})
with respect to
g
so that
\lambda_{n}\geq\lambda_{n-1}\geq...\geq\lambda_{1}
. It suffices to show that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\geq 2\lambda_{n}
.

(3.4)

\begin{split}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}-2\lambda_{n}&=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{n}\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}R_{ijji}-\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}R_{inni}\\
&=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n-1}R_{ijji}.\end{split}

Since
\mathrm{R}\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{PIC1}}
,
R_{ijji}+R_{ikki}\geq 0
for all
i,j,k
distinct. Without loss of generality, assume
i=1

(3.5)

\begin{split}2\sum_{j=2}^{n-1}R_{ijji}&=\left(\sum_{j=2}^{n-2}(R_{1jj1}+R_{1(j+1)(j+1)1})\right)+R_{1(n-1)(n-1)1}+R_{1221}\\
&\geq 0.\end{split}

Replacing
i=1
by

, we see that the sum is non-negative. Alternatively,
\sum_{i,j=1}^{n-1}R_{ijji}
is the scalar curvature of

restricted on the orthogonal sub-space of

which is non-negative using

Lemma 3.3

]
, the function

\phi(x,t)=\displaystyle\inf\left\{s>0:\mathrm{Rm}(h(x,t))+\tfrac{1}{2}s\,h(x,t)\owedge h(x,t)\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{PIC1}}\right\}

satisfies
\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta_{g(t)}\right)\phi\leq\mathcal{R}\phi+c_{n}\phi^{2}
in the sense of barrier and hence in the distributional sense (see
[
33
, Appendix]
). By assumption,
\phi(x,0)\leq\Lambda_{0}L^{-2}
,
\phi(x,t)\leq 1
and
\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta_{g(t)}\right)\phi\leq\mathcal{R}\phi+c_{n}\phi
so that maximum principle implies

(3.14)

\begin{split}e^{-c_{n}t}\phi(x,t)&\leq\int_{M}G(x,t;y,0)\phi(y,0)\,d\mathrm{vol}_{h_{0}}(y).\end{split}

Using Lemma
3.3
, Stokes’ Theorem and volume comparison,

(3.15)

\begin{split}e^{-c_{n}t}\phi(x,t)&\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{Vol}_{h_{0}}\left(B_{h_{0}}(x,r)\right)}{\mathrm{Vol}_{h_{0}}\left(B_{h_{0}}(x,\sqrt{t})\right)}\exp\left(-\frac{r^{2}}{C_{1}t}\right)\frac{2\Lambda_{0}r}{L^{2}t}\,dr\\
&=\left(\int_{0}^{\sqrt{t}}+\int^{\infty}_{\sqrt{t}}\right)\frac{\mathrm{Vol}_{h_{0}}\left(B_{h_{0}}(x,r)\right)}{\mathrm{Vol}_{h_{0}}\left(B_{h_{0}}(x,\sqrt{t})\right)}\exp\left(-\frac{r^{2}}{C_{1}t}\right)\frac{2\Lambda_{0}r}{L^{2}t}\,dr\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{\sqrt{t}}\exp\left(-\frac{r^{2}}{C_{1}t}\right)\frac{2\Lambda_{0}r}{L^{2}t}\,dr+\int_{\sqrt{t}}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{r^{2}}{C_{1}t}+C_{n}\frac{r}{\sqrt{t}}\right)\frac{2\Lambda_{0}r}{L^{2}t}\,dr\\
&\leq\frac{C_{2}\Lambda_{0}}{L^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{r^{2}}{C_{2}}\right)dr=C_{3}\Lambda_{0}L^{-2}.\end{split}

Now we fix
L(n)=2\sqrt{C_{3}}
so that on
M\times[0,t_{1}]
,
\phi(x,t)\leq\frac{1}{4}\Lambda_{0}e^{c_{n}t}
and therefore,

(3.16)

\mathrm{Rm}(h(t))+\frac{1}{8}\exp(c_{n}t)\Lambda_{0}h(t)\owedge h(t)\in\mathrm{int}\left(\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{PIC1}}\right).

Now we proceed to estimate (i). On
[0,t_{1}]
, define the twisted curvature operator
\widetilde{\mathrm{Rm}}=\mathrm{Rm}(h(t))+h(t)\owedge h(t)\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{PIC1}}

and its corresponding scalar
\tilde{\mathcal{R}}
and Ricci curvature
\widetilde{\text{\rm Ric}}
so that
\tilde{\mathcal{R}}\geq n(n-1)
.

By the Ricci flow equation and Lemma
3.2
, it satisfies

(3.17)

\begin{split}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta_{g(t)}\right)\tilde{\mathcal{R}}&=2|\text{\rm Ric}|^{2}\\
&=2|\widetilde{\text{\rm Ric}}-2(n-1)h(t)|^{2}\\
&\leq\tilde{\mathcal{R}}^{2}-8(n-1)\tilde{\mathcal{R}}+8n(n-1)^{2}\\
&\leq\mathcal{R}\cdot\tilde{\mathcal{R}}+2n(n-1)\tilde{\mathcal{R}}.\end{split}

Hence, the function
\varphi=e^{-2n(n-1)t}\tilde{\mathcal{R}}
satisfies

(3.18)

\begin{split}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta_{g(t)}\right)\varphi&\leq\mathcal{R}\varphi.\end{split}

Fix an arbitrary
x_{0}\in M
and define

u(x,t)=\int_{\Omega}G_{\Omega}(x,t;y,0)\varphi(y,0)\,d\mathrm{vol}_{h_{0}}(y)

where
\Omega=B_{h_{0}}(x_{0},\frac{1}{2}r_{0})
and
G_{\Omega}
is the dirichlet heat kernel on
\Omega
if
\Omega
is a proper subset of
M
. If
\Omega=M
, then we take
G
to be the global heat kernel. In this way,
v=\varphi-u
satisfies
\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta_{g(t)}\right)v\leq\mathcal{R}v
and
v=0
in the interior of
\Omega
. Then the average twisted scalar curvature satisfies

(3.19)

\begin{split}0&\leq\tau^{2}\fint_{B_{h_{0}}(x_{0},\tau)}\tilde{\mathcal{R}}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{h_{0}}\\
&=\tau^{2}\fint_{B_{h_{0}}(x_{0},\tau)}\left(\mathcal{R}+2n(n-1)\right)\,d\mathrm{vol}_{h_{0}}\\
&\leq C_{n}k(x_{0},\tau)+2n(n-1)\tau^{2}.\end{split}

We now estimate
u
at
x\in B_{h_{0}}(x_{0},\frac{1}{4}r_{0})
.

Then Lemma
3.3
, Stokes’ Theorem and co-area formula imply that if
t\leq(20{\alpha})^{-2}\wedge(10^{3}(n-1)^{2}{\alpha}^{2})^{-1}\mathrm{diam}(M,h_{0})^{2}
, then

(3.20)

\begin{split}u(x,t)&\leq\int_{B_{h_{0}}(x,r_{0})}\frac{C_{1}}{\mathrm{Vol}_{h_{0}}\left(B_{h_{0}}(x,\sqrt{t})\right)}\exp\left(-\frac{d_{h_{0}}^{2}(x,y)}{C_{1}t}\right)\tilde{\mathcal{R}}(y,0)\,d\mathrm{vol}_{h_{0}}(y)\\
&\leq\frac{C_{1}}{4}\frac{\mathrm{Vol}_{h_{0}}\left(B_{h_{0}}(x,r_{0})\right)}{\mathrm{Vol}_{h_{0}}\left(B_{h_{0}}(x,\sqrt{t})\right)}\exp\left(-\frac{r_{0}^{2}}{C_{1}t}\right)\cdot\left(C_{n}k(x,r_{0})+8n(n-1)\right)\\
&\quad+\frac{C_{n}}{t}\int_{0}^{r_{0}}\frac{k(x,r)}{r}\frac{\mathrm{Vol}_{h_{0}}\left(B_{h_{0}}(x,r)\right)}{\mathrm{Vol}_{h_{0}}\left(B_{h_{0}}(x,\sqrt{t})\right)}\exp\left(-\frac{r^{2}}{C_{1}t}\right)\,dr\\
&\quad+\frac{8n(n-1)}{t}\int^{r_{0}}_{0}\frac{\mathrm{Vol}_{h_{0}}\left(B_{h_{0}}(x,r)\right)}{\mathrm{Vol}_{h_{0}}\left(B_{h_{0}}(x,\sqrt{t})\right)}\exp\left(-\frac{r^{2}}{C_{1}t}\right)\,dr\\
&=\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{II}+\mathbf{III}.\end{split}

If
\sqrt{t}\leq r_{0}
, then volume comparison implies

(3.21)

\begin{split}\mathbf{I}&\leq\frac{C_{4}}{t^{n/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{C_{4}t}\right)\cdot\left(C_{n}k(x,r_{0})+8n(n-1)\right)\\
&\leq C_{4}(C_{n}\varepsilon_{0}+1)\end{split}

and

(3.22)

\begin{split}\mathbf{III}&\leq\frac{C_{5}}{t}\left(\int^{r_{0}}_{\sqrt{t}}\frac{r^{n}}{t^{n/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{r^{2}}{C_{1}t}\right)\,dr+\int^{\sqrt{t}}_{0}\exp\left(-\frac{r^{2}}{C_{1}t}\right)dr\right)\\
&\leq\frac{C_{5}}{t}\left(\sqrt{t}\int^{\infty}_{1}r^{n}\exp\left(-\frac{r^{2}}{C_{1}}\right)\,dr+\sqrt{t}\int^{1}_{0}\exp\left(-\frac{r^{2}}{C_{1}}\right)dr\right)\\
&\leq C_{5}t^{-1/2}.\end{split}

It remains to estimate the second term
\mathbf{II}
. This is the leading term when
t\to 0
. Using volume comparison as above to see that if
\sqrt{t}\leq r_{0}
, then

(3.23)

\begin{split}\mathbf{II}&=\frac{C_{n}}{t}\left(\int_{\sqrt{t}}^{r_{0}}+\int_{0}^{\sqrt{t}}\right)\frac{k(x,r)}{r}\frac{\mathrm{Vol}_{h_{0}}\left(B_{h_{0}}(x,r)\right)}{\mathrm{Vol}_{h_{0}}\left(B_{h_{0}}(x,\sqrt{t})\right)}\exp\left(-\frac{r^{2}}{C_{1}t}\right)\,dr\\
&\leq\frac{C_{6}}{t}\int^{r_{0}}_{0}\frac{k(x,r)}{r}\exp\left(-\frac{r^{2}}{C_{6}t}\right)\,dr\\
&\leq\frac{C_{6}}{t}f(x,r_{0})\leq C_{6}\varepsilon_{0}t^{-1}.\end{split}

We now require
\varepsilon_{0}
to be sufficiently small so that
\mathbf{II}\leq\frac{1}{2}t^{-1}
and thus
u(x,t)\leq t^{-1}
if
t\leq\min\{t_{1},c_{n},r_{0}^{2},(20{\alpha})^{-2}\}
for some small
c_{n}>0
. By
[
29
, Theorem 1.1]
, there exists
S_{1}(n)>0
such that if

t\leq\min\{S_{1}r_{0}^{2},t_{1},c_{n},r_{0}^{2},(20{\alpha})^{-2},c_{n}{\alpha}^{-1}\mathrm{diam}(M,h_{0})^{2}\},

then

(3.24)

\begin{split}e^{-2n(n-1)t}\tilde{\mathcal{R}}(x,t)&=\varphi(x,t)\leq u(x,t)+v\\
&\leq t^{-1}+(\frac{1}{4}r_{0})^{-2}\end{split}

at
x=x_{0}
and
t\in[0,\min\{S_{1}r_{0}^{2},t_{1},c_{n},r_{0}^{2},(20{\alpha})^{-2},c_{n}{\alpha}^{-1}\mathrm{diam}(M,h_{0})^{2}\}]
.
Since
x_{0}
is arbitrary, by the choice of
{\alpha}
and (
3.16
), we see that
t_{1}\geq S_{2}(n)>0
if
M
is complete non-compact. And if
M
is compact, then
t_{1}\geq S_{2}(n)\cdot\mathrm{diam}(M,h_{0})^{2}
. This completes the proof.
∎

4.
Existence of Ricci flow: general case

In this section, we will mainly consider the non-compact case and will construct a short-time solution
g(t)
to the Ricci flow using pseudo-locality. We remark that the initial metric can a-priori be very complicated at infinity. In particular, the curvature is not necessarily bounded uniformly so that Shi’s construction
[
45
]
does not apply directly. To overcome this, we use a trick of Topping
[
51
]
to construct local solution.

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose
(M^{n},g_{0})
is a complete non-compact Riemannian manifold and
x_{0}\in M
such that
K(g_{0})\geq-L
on
M
for some
L>1
. Then there exist
C_{n}>0
and a smooth positive proper function
\rho
on
M
such that
|\rho(x)-d_{g_{0}}(x,x_{0})|\leq 1
,
|\nabla\rho|^{2}\leq 2
on
M
and
\nabla^{2}\rho\leq C_{n}Lg_{0}
outside
B_{g_{0}}(x_{0},\sqrt{L})
.

Proof.

This follows from the standard Hessian comparison and the approximation method of Greene-Wu
[
16
,
17
,
18
]
. The linear dependence on
L
can be seen from scaling argument.
∎

We will use the smoothed distance function
\rho
from Lemma
4.1
to construct a sequence of complete bounded curvature manifolds to approximate the original manifold
(M,g_{0})
in suitable sense. To do this, let
\kappa\in(0,1)
,
f:[0,1)\to[0,\infty)
be the function:

(4.1)

f(s)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&\hbox{$s\in[0,1-\kappa]$;}\\
-\displaystyle{\log\left[1-\left(\frac{s-1+\kappa}{\kappa}\right)^{2}\right]},&\hbox{$s\in(1-\kappa,1)$.}\end{array}\right.

Let
\varphi\geq 0
be a smooth function on
\mathbb{R}
such that
\varphi(s)=0
if
s\leq 1-\kappa+\kappa^{2}
,
\varphi(s)=1
for
s\geq 1-\kappa+2\kappa^{2}

(4.2)

\varphi(s)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&\hbox{$s\in[0,1-\kappa+\kappa^{2}]$;}\\
1,&\hbox{$s\in(1-\kappa+2\kappa^{2},1)$.}\end{array}\right.

such that
\displaystyle{\frac{2}{\kappa^{2}}}\geq\varphi^{\prime}\geq 0
. The function

\mathfrak{F}(s):=\int_{0}^{s}\varphi(\tau)f^{\prime}(\tau)d\tau.

satisfies the important properties.

Lemma 4.2

(Lemma 4.1 in
[
28
]
)
.

Suppose
0<\kappa<\frac{1}{8}
. Then the function
\mathfrak{F}\geq 0
defined above is smooth and satisfies the following:

(i)

\mathfrak{F}(s)=0
for
0\leq s\leq 1-\kappa+\kappa^{2}
.

(ii)

\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}\geq 0
and for any
k\geq 1
,
\exp(-k\mathfrak{F})\mathfrak{F}^{(k)}
is uniformly bounded.

(iii)

For any
1-2\kappa<s<1
, there is
\tau>0
with
0<s-\tau<s+\tau<1
such that

1\leq\exp(\mathfrak{F}(s+\tau)-\mathfrak{F}(s-\tau))\leq(1+c_{2}\kappa);\ \ \tau\exp(\mathfrak{F}(s-\tau))\geq c_{3}\kappa^{2}

for some absolute constants
c_{2}>0,c_{3}>0
.

We now prove the main Theorem, Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

We only consider the case
n\geq 4
since the case of
n=3
can be proved using similar but simpler argument. We will also focus on the non-compact case since the compact case follows directly from Theorem
3.1
.

By scaling, we will assume
r=1
. To construct the approximation, take
\rho
to be the smooth function obtained from Lemma
4.1
. For any
R>0
sufficiently large, we let
U_{R}
be the component of
\{x\in M:\rho(x)<R\}
which contains a fixed point
x_{0}\in M
. In this way,
U_{R}
will exhaust
M
as
R\to+\infty
. Without loss of generality, we might assume
U_{R}
to have smooth boundary. On each
U_{R}
, define

F_{R}(x)=\mathfrak{F}\left(\frac{\rho(x)}{R}\right),\;\;g_{R,0}=e^{2F_{R}}g_{0}.

By
[
28
, Lemma 4.3]
(see also
[
25
]
),
g_{R,0}
is a complete metric on
U_{R}
with bounded curvature (in fact, bounded geometry of infinity order). We fix a small
\kappa
in the construction of
\mathfrak{F}
. We claim that for all sufficiently large
R
, each
g_{R,0}
satisfies the assumptions in Theorem
3.1
regardless of how large
L
is. We will omit the
R
on
F_{R}
for notational convenience.

Claim 4.1
.

There is
R_{0}>0
such that for all
R>R_{0}
,

\mathrm{Rm}(g_{R,0})+g_{R,0}\owedge g_{R,0}\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{PIC1}}.

Proof of claim.

For notational convenience, We use
\tilde{g},\tilde{R}
to denote
g_{R,0}
and the curvature tensor of
g_{R,0}
. Recall the curvature under conformal change:

\widetilde{\mathrm{Rm}}=e^{2F}\mathrm{Rm}-e^{2F}g\owedge\left(\nabla^{2}F-dF\otimes dF+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla F|^{2}g\right).

Since
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{PIC1}}
is convex, it suffices to estimate the lower bound of each terms with respect to
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{PIC1}}
. By assumption,
\mathrm{Rm}+r^{-2}g\owedge g/2\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{PIC1}}
so that

(4.3)

e^{2F}\mathrm{Rm}+e^{-2F}r^{-2}\tilde{g}\owedge\tilde{g}/2\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{PIC1}}.

Let
\{\tilde{e}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{4}
and
\{e_{i}=e^{F}\tilde{e}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{4}
be an orthonormal frame w.r.t
\tilde{g}
and
g
respectively. Then for any
\lambda\in[0,1]
,

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathrm{Rm}}_{\tilde{1}\tilde{3}\tilde{3}\tilde{1}}+(\tilde{g}\owedge\tilde{g})_{\tilde{1}\tilde{3}\tilde{3}\tilde{1}}+\lambda^{2}\widetilde{\mathrm{Rm}}_{\tilde{1}\tilde{4}\tilde{4}\tilde{1}}+\lambda^{2}(\tilde{g}\owedge\tilde{g})_{\tilde{1}\tilde{4}\tilde{4}\tilde{1}}

\displaystyle+\widetilde{\mathrm{Rm}}_{\tilde{2}\tilde{3}\tilde{3}\tilde{2}}+(\tilde{g}\owedge\tilde{g})_{\tilde{2}\tilde{3}\tilde{3}\tilde{2}}+\lambda^{2}\widetilde{\mathrm{Rm}}_{\tilde{2}\tilde{4}\tilde{4}\tilde{2}}+\lambda^{2}(\tilde{g}\owedge\tilde{g})_{\tilde{2}\tilde{4}\tilde{4}\tilde{2}}

\displaystyle+2\lambda\widetilde{\mathrm{Rm}}_{\tilde{1}\tilde{2}\tilde{3}\tilde{4}}+2\lambda(\tilde{g}\owedge\tilde{g})_{\tilde{1}\tilde{2}\tilde{3}\tilde{4}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathrm{Rm}}_{\tilde{1}\tilde{3}\tilde{3}\tilde{1}}+\lambda^{2}\widetilde{\mathrm{Rm}}_{\tilde{1}\tilde{4}\tilde{4}\tilde{1}}+\widetilde{\mathrm{Rm}}_{\tilde{2}\tilde{3}\tilde{3}\tilde{2}}+\lambda^{2}\widetilde{\mathrm{Rm}}_{\tilde{2}\tilde{4}\tilde{4}\tilde{2}}+2\lambda\widetilde{\mathrm{Rm}}_{\tilde{1}\tilde{2}\tilde{3}\tilde{4}}

\displaystyle+4(1+\lambda^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-2F}\left(\mathrm{Rm}_{1331}+\lambda^{2}\mathrm{Rm}_{1441}+\mathrm{Rm}_{2332}+\lambda^{2}\mathrm{Rm}_{2442}+2\lambda\mathrm{Rm}_{1234}\right)

\displaystyle+4(1+\lambda^{2})+E_{0}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(4-2e^{-2F})(1+\lambda^{2})+E_{0}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 2(1+\lambda^{2})+E_{0},

where the error term
E_{0}
is given by

\displaystyle-e^{-2F}\left((1+\lambda^{2})(F_{11}+F_{22}-F_{1}F_{1}-F_{2}F_{2})+2F_{33}-2F_{3}F_{3}+2\lambda^{2}F_{44}-2\lambda^{2}F_{4}F_{4}\right)

\displaystyle-2e^{-2F}|\nabla F|^{2}(1+\lambda^{2}).

We shall use Lemma
4.2
to estimate the derivatives of
F
. By Lemma
4.2
(ii)

e^{-2F}|\nabla F|^{2}=e^{-2F}\left(\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}\right)^{2}\frac{|\nabla\rho|^{2}}{R^{2}}\leq C\frac{|\nabla\rho|^{2}}{R^{2}}\leq\frac{2C}{R^{2}}.

For large
R\gg 1
,
\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}\frac{\rho_{ii}}{R}\leq\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}\frac{C_{n}L}{R}
, hence by Lemmas
4.1
and
4.2
(ii), for
i=1,\dots,4
,

e^{-2F}F_{ii}=e^{-2F}\left(\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}\frac{\rho_{ii}}{R}+\mathfrak{F}^{\prime\prime}\frac{\rho_{i}\rho_{i}}{R^{2}}\right)\leq\frac{C_{n}L}{R}+\frac{C_{n}}{R^{2}},

here we are not using Einstein summation convention, repeated indices are not summed.
From this we see that

E_{0}\geq-\frac{C_{n}L(1+\lambda^{2})}{R}

and for all sufficiently large
R

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathrm{Rm}}_{\tilde{1}\tilde{3}\tilde{3}\tilde{1}}+(\tilde{g}\owedge\tilde{g})_{\tilde{1}\tilde{3}\tilde{3}\tilde{1}}+\lambda^{2}\widetilde{\mathrm{Rm}}_{\tilde{1}\tilde{4}\tilde{4}\tilde{1}}+\lambda^{2}(\tilde{g}\owedge\tilde{g})_{\tilde{1}\tilde{4}\tilde{4}\tilde{1}}

\displaystyle+\widetilde{\mathrm{Rm}}_{\tilde{2}\tilde{3}\tilde{3}\tilde{2}}+(\tilde{g}\owedge\tilde{g})_{\tilde{2}\tilde{3}\tilde{3}\tilde{2}}+\lambda^{2}\widetilde{\mathrm{Rm}}_{\tilde{2}\tilde{4}\tilde{4}\tilde{2}}+\lambda^{2}(\tilde{g}\owedge\tilde{g})_{\tilde{2}\tilde{4}\tilde{4}\tilde{2}}

\displaystyle+2\lambda\widetilde{\mathrm{Rm}}_{\tilde{1}\tilde{2}\tilde{3}\tilde{4}}+\lambda(\tilde{g}\owedge\tilde{g})_{\tilde{1}\tilde{2}\tilde{3}\tilde{4}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(2-C_{n}LR^{-1})(1+\lambda^{2})>0.

This completes the proof of the claim.
∎

Claim 4.2
.

There exists
C_{n},R_{0}>0
such that if
R>R_{0}
, then for all
(x,r)\in U_{R}\times[0,1)
,

(4.4)

f_{g_{R,0}}(x,r)=\displaystyle\int^{r}_{0}s^{-1}k_{g_{R,0}}(x,s)\,ds\leq C_{n}\varepsilon_{0}+C_{n}r.

Proof of Claim.

Let
x\in U_{R}
and
r<1
. If
x
is such that
\rho(x)<(1-2\kappa)R
, then for all
z\in B_{g_{0}}(x,1)
,
\rho(z)\leq(1-\kappa+\kappa^{2})R
provided that
R
is sufficiently large. This particularly implies
g_{R,0}=g_{0}
on
B_{g_{0}}(x,1)
so that the conclusion holds trivially thanks to the assumptions.

If
(1-2\kappa)R\leq\rho(x)<R
, then (iii) in Lemma
4.2
implies that as long as
R
is sufficiently large, we have
B_{g_{R,0}}(x,1)\subset\{z\in U_{R}:s-\tau<R^{-1}\rho(z)<s+\tau\}
where
s=R^{-1}\rho(x)
and
\tau=\tau(s)
is a constant depending only on
s
. In particular, we have

(4.5)

e^{2\mathfrak{F}(s-\tau)}g_{0}\leq g_{R,0}\leq e^{2\mathfrak{F}(s+\tau)}g_{0}

on
B_{g_{R,0}}(x,1)
. By the conformal change formula for scalar curvature, for all
r\in(0,1]
,

(4.6)

\begin{split}&\quad r^{2}\fint_{B_{g_{R,0}}(x,r)}\mathcal{R}_{g_{R,0}}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{R,0}}\\
&=r^{2}\fint_{B_{g_{R,0}}(x,r)}e^{-2F}\mathcal{R}_{g_{0}}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{R,0}}-\frac{2(n-1)}{R}r^{2}\fint_{B_{g_{R,0}}(x,r)}e^{-2F}\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}\Delta\rho\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{R,0}}\\
&\quad+r^{2}\fint_{B_{g_{R,0}}(x,r)}e^{-2F}\left[-\frac{4(n-1)}{n-2}\left(\frac{(n-2)^{2}}{4R^{2}}|\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}|^{2}|\nabla\rho|^{2}+\frac{n-2}{2R^{2}}\mathfrak{F}^{\prime\prime}|\nabla\rho|^{2}\right)\right]\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{R,0}}\\
&=\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{II}+\mathbf{III}.\end{split}

By Lemma
4.1
and Lemma
4.2
, for all
r\in(0,1]
,

(4.7)

\begin{split}\mathbf{III}&\leq\frac{C_{n}}{R^{2}}\cdot r^{2}\fint_{B_{g_{R,0}}(x,r)}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{R,0}}\leq\frac{C_{n}r^{2}}{R^{2}}=o(1)r^{2}.\end{split}

For
\mathbf{II}
, we control it using Stokes’ Theorem, Lemma
4.2
and Lemma
4.1
:

(4.8)

\begin{split}\mathbf{II}&=-\frac{2(n-1)}{R}\frac{r^{2}}{\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{R,0}}\left(B_{g_{R,0}}(x,r)\right)}\int_{B_{g_{R,0}}(x,r)}e^{(n-2)F}\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}\Delta\rho\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}\\
&=\frac{2(n-1)}{R}\frac{r^{2}}{\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{R,0}}\left(B_{g_{R,0}}(x,r)\right)}\int_{B_{g_{R,0}}(x,r)}\nabla\rho\cdot\nabla(e^{(n-2)F}\mathfrak{F}^{\prime})\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}\\
&\quad-\frac{2(n-1)}{R}\frac{r^{2}}{\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{R,0}}\left(B_{g_{R,0}}(x,r)\right)}\int_{\partial B_{g_{R,0}(x,r)}}e^{(n-2)F}\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}\cdot\nabla_{\nu}\rho\,dA_{g_{0}}\\
&\leq\frac{C_{n}r^{2}}{R^{2}}+\frac{C_{n}}{R}\frac{r^{2}}{\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{R,0}}\left(B_{g_{R,0}}(x,r)\right)}|\partial B_{g_{R,0}}(x,r)|_{g_{R,0}}\leq\frac{C_{n}r}{R^{2}}=o(1)r.\end{split}

Here we have used the volume comparison and Claim
4.1
on the last inequality.

It remains to control
\mathbf{I}
. Using (
4.5
), Lemma
4.2
and volume comparison, for
r\in(0,1]
,

(4.9)

\begin{split}&\quad r^{2}\fint_{B_{g_{R,0}}(x,r)}e^{-2F}\mathcal{R}_{g_{0}}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{R,0}}\\
&\leq\frac{C_{n}r^{2}}{\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{R,0}}\left(B_{g_{R,0}}(x,r)\right)}\int_{B_{g_{R,0}}(x,r)}e^{(n-2)F}|\text{\rm Rm}(g_{0})|\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}\\
&\leq\frac{C_{n}r^{2}\cdot e^{(n-2)\mathfrak{F}(s+\tau)-n\mathfrak{F}(s-\tau)}}{\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{0}}\left(B_{g_{0}}(x,e^{-\mathfrak{F}(s+\tau)}r)\right)}\int_{B_{g_{0}}(x,e^{-\mathfrak{F}(s-\tau)}r)}|\text{\rm Rm}(g_{0})|\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}\\
&\leq C_{n}e^{(n-2)(\mathfrak{F}(s+\tau)-\mathfrak{F}(s-\tau))}\frac{\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{0}}\left(B_{g_{0}}(x,e^{-\mathfrak{F}(s-\tau)}r)\right)}{\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{0}}\left(B_{g_{0}}(x,e^{-\mathfrak{F}(s+\tau)}r)\right)}\cdot k(x,e^{-\mathfrak{F}(s-\tau)}r)\\
&\leq C_{n}k(x,e^{-\mathfrak{F}(s-\tau)}r).\end{split}

Therefore for each
(x,r)\in U_{R}\times[0,1)
,

(4.10)

\begin{split}\int^{r}_{0}w^{-1}k(x,e^{-\mathfrak{F}(s-\tau)}w)\,dw&=\int^{re^{-\mathfrak{F}(s-\tau)}}_{0}w^{-1}k(x,w)\,dw\\
&=f(re^{-\mathfrak{F}(s-\tau)})\leq f(1)\leq\varepsilon_{0}.\end{split}

Hence for all
(x,r)\in U_{R}\times[0,1)
and
R
sufficiently large,

(4.11)

\displaystyle\int^{r}_{0}s\left(\fint_{B_{g_{R,0}}(x,s)}\mathcal{R}_{g_{R,0}}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{R,0}}\right)dr\leq C_{n}\varepsilon_{0}+o(1)r^{2}.

The claim now follows from Claim
4.1
and the fact that
|\mathrm{R}|\leq{\beta}_{n}|\mathrm{scal}(\mathrm{R})|
for all
\mathrm{R}\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{PIC1}}
.
∎

By Claim
4.2
, if we require
\varepsilon_{0}
to be small enough, we see that for all
R\to+\infty
,
\varepsilon_{0}^{-2}g_{R,0}
satisfies the assumptions in Theorem
3.1
. By re-scaling back,
U_{R}
admits a short-time solution
g_{R}(t),t\in[0,\varepsilon_{0}^{2}S_{n}]
to the Ricci flow with

and

(a)

|\text{\rm Rm}_{g_{R}}(x,t)|\leq{\alpha}_{n}t^{-1}
;

(b)

\mathrm{Rm}(g_{R}(t))+\frac{1}{2}L_{n}\varepsilon_{0}^{-2}\,g_{R}(t)\owedge g_{R}(t)\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{PIC1}}
.

on
U_{R}\times(0,\varepsilon_{0}^{2}S_{n}]
. Since
g_{R,0}=g_{0}
on any compact subset
\Omega\Subset M
as
R\to+\infty
. By
[
10
, Corollary 3.2]
(see also
[
47
]
) and the modified Shi’s higher order estimates
[

, we infer that for any

and
\Omega\Subset M
, we can find

(4.12)

\displaystyle\sup_{\Omega\times[0,\hat{\varepsilon}_{0}S_{n}]}|\nabla^{m}\mathrm{Rm}(g_{R}(t))|\leq C(n,m,\Omega,g_{0}).

By working on coordinate charts and Ascoli-Arzelà theorem, we may pass to a subsequence to obtain a smooth solution

of the Ricci flow on

5.
application to Gap Theorem

In this section, we will consider complete non-compact manifolds with non-negative complex sectional curvature and small integral average quadratic curvature decay. Before we prove the main Theorem, we first prove Theorem
1.3
, i.e. the gap Theorem under Euclidean volume condition. The relaxation of curvature condition is motivated by the recent work of Lott
[
32
]
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

By
[
24
, Theorem 1.1 & Corollary 4.1]
(see also
[
26
,
49
]
),
M
is diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and admits a long-time solution
g(t)
to the Ricci flow on
M\times[0,+\infty)
with
\mathrm{Rm}(g(t))\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{PIC1}}
and
|\text{\rm Rm}(g(t))|\leq{\alpha}t^{-1}
for some
{\alpha}>0
. It is well-known that the asymptotic volume ratio is preserved under Ricci flow with
\text{\rm Ric}(g(t))\geq 0
and
|\text{\rm Rm}(g(t))|\leq{\alpha}t^{-1}
, for instances see the proof of
[
53
, Theorem 7]
. If
n=3
, it also follows from
[
29
]
that the non-negativity of sectional curvature is also preserved. Therefore, there exists
v_{0}>0
such that for all
x\in M,r>0
and
t>0
, we have

(5.1)

\mathrm{Vol}_{g(t)}\left(B_{g(t)}(x,r)\right)\geq v_{0}r^{n}.

By Lemma
3.2
,
\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta_{g(t)}\right)\mathcal{R}\leq\mathcal{R}^{2}
and hence the maximum principle implies that for all
(x,t)\in M\times(0,+\infty)
,

(5.2)

\mathcal{R}(x,t)\leq\int_{M}G(x,t;y,0)\mathcal{R}(y,0)\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}(y)

where
G(x,t;y,s)
denotes the heat kernel on
M
. The maximum principle can be justified by applying it on
[s,t]
for
s>0
and followed by passing
s\to 0
or applying the localized maximum principle
[
29
, Theorem 1.1]
as in the proof of Theorem
3.1
and followed by exhaustion argument.
By (
5.1
) and Lemma
3.3
,
G(x,t;y,s)
satisfies the Gaussian estimate (see also

[
1
, Proposition 3.1]
):

(5.3)

G(x,t;y,0)\leq\frac{C(n,v_{0},{\alpha})}{t^{n/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{d_{g_{0}}(x,y)^{2}}{C(n,v_{0},{\alpha})t}\right)

for all
x,y\in M
and
t>0
. Fix
x_{0}\in M
and
t
sufficiently large. In what follows, we will use
C_{i}
to denote constants depending only on
n,v_{0},{\alpha}
.

Then, argue as in the proof of Theorem
3.1
. Stokes’ Theorem and co-area formula imply

(5.4)

\begin{split}\mathcal{R}(x_{0},t)&\leq\int_{M}G(x_{0},t;y,0)\,\mathcal{R}(y,0)\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}(y)\\
&\leq\int^{\infty}_{0}\frac{r^{n}}{t^{n/2}}\cdot\exp\left(-\frac{r^{2}}{C_{1}t}\right)\frac{C_{1}}{rt}k(x_{0},r)dr.\end{split}

By assumption, for any
\delta>0
, there exists
r_{0}>0
such that for all
r>r_{0}
,
k(x_{0},r)<\delta
. We split the integral using
r_{0}
.

(5.5)

\begin{split}&\quad\int^{\infty}_{0}\frac{r^{n}}{t^{n/2}}\cdot\exp\left(-\frac{r^{2}}{C_{1}t}\right)\frac{C_{1}}{rt}k(x_{0},r)dr\\
&=\left(\int^{\infty}_{r_{0}}+\int^{r_{0}}_{0}\right)\frac{r^{n}}{t^{n/2}}\cdot\exp\left(-\frac{r^{2}}{C_{1}t}\right)\frac{C_{1}}{rt}k(x_{0},r)dr\\
&=\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{II}.\end{split}

We let
\Lambda(g_{0},r_{0},\delta)>0
be such that
\mathcal{R}_{g_{0}}\leq\Lambda
on
B_{g_{0}}(x_{0},r_{0})
and hence
k(x_{0},r)\leq C_{r_{0}}r^{2}
for all
r\leq r_{0}
. Therefore,

(5.6)

\begin{split}\mathbf{II}&\leq C_{1}\Lambda\int_{0}^{r_{0}}\frac{r^{n}}{t^{n/2}}\cdot\exp\left(-\frac{r^{2}}{C_{1}t}\right)\frac{r}{t}dr\\
&=C_{1}\Lambda\int^{r_{0}t^{-1/2}}_{0}r^{n+1}dr\\
&\leq C^{\prime}(n,r_{0},v_{0},g_{0},\delta)t^{-1-\frac{n}{2}}\end{split}

while

(5.7)

\begin{split}\mathbf{I}&\leq C_{1}\delta\int^{\infty}_{r_{0}}\frac{r^{n}}{t^{n/2}}\cdot\exp\left(-\frac{r^{2}}{C_{1}t}\right)\frac{1}{rt}dr\\
&\leq t^{-1}C_{1}\delta\int^{\infty}_{0}r^{n-1}\exp\left(-\frac{r^{2}}{C_{1}}\right)dr\\
&\leq C_{2}t^{-1}\delta.\end{split}

By letting
t\to+\infty
and followed by
\delta\to 0
and the fact that
\mathrm{Rm}(g(t))\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{PIC1}}
, we conclude

(5.8)

\limsup_{t\to+\infty}\,t|\text{\rm Rm}(x_{0},t)|=0

Now consider the re-scaled Ricci flow
g_{i}(t),t\in[0,+\infty)
where
g_{i}(t)=i^{-2}g(i^{2}t)
. By Hamilton’s compactness
[
23
]
,
(M,g_{i}(t),x_{0})
sub-converges to
(M_{\infty},g_{\infty}(t),x_{\infty})
for
t\in(0,+\infty)
in the pointed
C^{\infty}
Cheeger-Gromov sense as
i\to+\infty
. By the proof of
[
44
, Theorem 1.2]
(which is based on Cheeger and Colding’s volume continuity
[
7
]
),

satisfies
\mathrm{AVR}(g_{\infty}(t))=\mathrm{AVR}(g_{0})
and
\mathrm{Rm}(g_{\infty}(t))\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{PIC1}}
for all
t>0
. Moreover, (
5.8
) implies that
\mathcal{R}(g_{\infty}(x_{\infty},t))=0
for all
t>0
. The strong maximum principle implies
g_{\infty}(t)
is Ricci-flat and hence flat globally on
M_{\infty}
. This forces
\mathrm{AVR}(g_{\infty}(t))=\mathrm{AVR}(g_{0})=1
and hence
(M,g_{0})
is flat Euclidean by the rigidity of volume comparison.
∎

Now we study the case with strong curvature condition but
without
volume growth assumption. We start with the long-time existence under a slightly weaker condition.

Proposition 5.1
.

There exists
\varepsilon_{0}(n)>0
such that the following holds:
Let
(M^{n},g_{0})
be a complete non-compact manifold such that
n\geq 3
, and

(i)

\inf_{M}\mathrm{K}(g_{0})>-\infty
;

(ii)

\mathrm{Rm}(g_{0})\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{PIC1}}
if
n\geq 4
;

(iii)

\mathrm{K}(g_{0})\geq 0
if
n=3
.

Suppose for all
x\in M
,

\int^{+\infty}_{0}s\left(\fint_{B_{g_{0}}(x,s)}|\text{\rm Rm}(g_{0})|\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}\,\right)ds<\varepsilon_{0}.

Then there exist
{\alpha}_{n}>0
and a long-time solution
g(t)
to the Ricci flow on
M\times[0,+\infty)
with
g(0)=g_{0}
such that
|\text{\rm Rm}(g(t))|\leq{\alpha}_{n}t^{-1}
and
\mathrm{Rm}(g(t))\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{PIC1}}
for all

. If in addition,
\mathrm{K}^{\mathbb{C}}(g_{0})\geq 0
, then
\mathrm{K}^{\mathbb{C}}(g(t))\geq 0
for all

Proof.

By Theorem
1.2
, for any
i>0
, there exists a solution
g_{i}(t)
to the Ricci flow on

with

and

. Using the sub-sequential convergence argument in the proof of Theorem

,

sub-converges uniformly locally in

to

on

. This proves the existence part. The assertion of

and

follow from

.
∎

Remark 5.1
.

The lower bound of the sectional curvature is purely for technical reason. It is easy to see that it can be further relaxed to quadratic sectional lower bound. We expect that it can be removed in full generality.

We now finish the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

By assumption and integrability, for any
\delta>0
, there exists
r_{0}>1
such that for all
r>r_{0}
,

\int^{\infty}_{r}s^{-1}k(x_{0},s)\,ds<\delta.

We also let
\Lambda(g_{0},r_{0},\delta)>0
be large constant such that
\mathcal{R}\leq\Lambda
on
B_{g_{0}}(x_{0},r_{0})
. We will also use
C_{i}
to denote any dimensional constants.

We let
g(t)
be the long-time solution obtained from Proposition
5.1
. Since
\mathrm{K}^{\mathbb{C}}(g(t))\geq 0
, Lemma
3.2
implies
\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta_{g(t)}\right)\mathcal{R}\leq\mathcal{R}^{2}
and hence for all
t>0
,

(5.9)

\mathcal{R}(x_{0},t)\leq\int_{M}G(x_{0},t;y,0)\mathcal{R}(y,0)\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}(y)

as in the proof of Theorem
1.3
. By using Lemma
3.3
, Stokes’ Theorem and co-area formula implies that for all
\sqrt{t}>r_{0}
,

(5.10)

\begin{split}\mathcal{R}(x_{0},t)&\leq\int_{M}\frac{C_{1}}{\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{0}}\left(B_{g_{0}}(x_{0},\sqrt{t})\right)}\exp\left(-\frac{d_{g_{0}}(x,y)^{2}}{C_{1}t}\right)\mathcal{R}(y,0)\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}(y)\\
&=\frac{C_{n}}{t}\left(\int^{\infty}_{\sqrt{t}}+\int^{\sqrt{t}}_{r_{0}}+\int^{r_{0}}_{0}\right)\frac{\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{0}}\left(B_{g_{0}}(x_{0},r)\right)}{\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{0}}\left(B_{g_{0}}(x_{0},\sqrt{t})\right)}\exp\left(-\frac{r^{2}}{C_{1}t}\right)\cdot\frac{k(x_{0},r)}{r}\,dr\\
&=\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{II}+\mathbf{III}.\end{split}

When

, we can appeal to volume comparison to deduce that

(5.11)

\begin{split}\mathbf{I}&\leq\frac{C_{2}}{t}\int^{\infty}_{\sqrt{t}}\left(\frac{r}{\sqrt{t}}\right)^{n}\exp\left(-\frac{r^{2}}{C_{1}t}\right)\cdot\frac{k(x_{0},r)}{r}\,dr\leq\frac{C_{3}}{t}\int^{\infty}_{\sqrt{t}}\frac{k(x_{0},r)}{r}\,dr.\end{split}

When

(5.12)

\begin{split}\mathbf{II}&\leq\frac{C_{n}}{t}\int^{\sqrt{t}}_{r_{0}}\exp\left(-\frac{r^{2}}{C_{1}t}\right)\frac{k(x_{0},r)}{r}\,dr\leq\frac{C_{4}}{t}\int^{\sqrt{t}}_{r_{0}}\frac{k(x_{0},r)}{r}\,dr\end{split}

.

When

, we use the estimate

and Yau’s linear volume growth to deduce that

(5.13)

\begin{split}\mathbf{III}&\leq\frac{C_{6}\Lambda}{t}\int^{r_{0}}_{0}\frac{\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{0}}\left(B_{g_{0}}(x_{0},r)\right)}{\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{0}}\left(B_{g_{0}}(x_{0},\sqrt{t})\right)}\exp\left(-\frac{r^{2}}{C_{1}t}\right)r\,dr\\

Therefore, for all

,

(5.14)

t\cdot\mathcal{R}(x_{0},t)\leq C_{5}\delta+\frac{C_{7}r_{0}^{n+2}\Lambda t^{-\frac{1}{2}}}{{\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{0}}\left(B_{g_{0}}(x_{0},1)\right)}}.

By Brendle’s Harnack inequality
[
2
]
and letting

, we deduce that for all

,
\mathcal{R}(x_{0},t)=0
. Since

, strong maximum principle implies that

is flat for all

and hence

is flat. This completes the proof.
∎

6.
Gap Theorem in dimension three

The three dimensional case with only
\text{\rm Ric}\geq 0
is more challenging due to the failure of Lemma
3.2
. In the presence of point-wise curvature decay, we will combine it with the methods in Kähler geometry to prove an optimal gap result.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

By the work of Liu
[
31
]
, either
M
is diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{3}
or the universal cover of
(M^{3},g_{0})
splits isometrically as
\Sigma^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
such that
\Sigma
has non-negative sectional curvature.
In case
M
is not topologically Euclidean.
Since
g_{0}
has quadratic curvature decay at infinity, the universal cover cannot split isometrically as
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
.
We may argue as follows: Let
\varepsilon>0
such that
K_{0}:=\{\mathcal{R}_{M}\geq\varepsilon\}
is a nonempty compact set of
M
and
\pi:\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}\to M
be a Riemannian covering map. Suppose
(a,z_{0})\in\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
and
\pi(a,z_{0})\in K_{0}
. Then for any
w\in\mathbb{R},b\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
,
\pi(a,w)\in K_{0}
and

\displaystyle d_{M}(\pi(b,w),\pi(a,z_{0}))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\text{diam}_{M}(K_{0})+d_{M}(\pi(b,w),\pi(a,w))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\text{diam}_{M}(K_{0})+d_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(a,b)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\text{diam}_{M}(K_{0})+\text{diam}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(\mathbb{S}^{2}).

This implies that
M
has finite diameter. Contradiction.
Therefore,
\Sigma^{2}
must be topologically
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. We now use an argument originated from Kähler geometry. By
[
36
, Theorem 1.1, Theorem 1.2 and Proposition 1.1]
, there exists
u\in C^{\infty}_{loc}(M)
such that
\Delta_{M}u=\mathcal{R}_{M}\geq 0
on
M
with
u=o(\log r)
as
r\to+\infty
. By
[
36
, Theorem 1.3]
,
|\nabla u|
is uniformly bounded on
M
. We lift
u
to
\tilde{M}
so that we might assume
\Delta_{\tilde{M}}u=\mathcal{R}_{\tilde{M}}
on
\tilde{M}
and
u
is still sub-logarithm growth on
\tilde{M}
since the covering map is distance non-increasing.
For
(z,y)\in\Sigma\times\mathbb{R}=\tilde{M}
, since
\tilde{M}
splits isometrically,
\partial_{y}u
is bounded harmonic function on
\tilde{M}
. Hence the Cheng-Yau’s gradient estimate implies
\partial_{y}\partial_{y}u=0
on
\tilde{M}
. In particular,
\Delta_{\Sigma}u(z,y_{0})=\mathcal{R}_{\Sigma}(z)
for any fixed
y_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
.

Since
\Sigma^{2}
is two-dimensional with non-negative curvature, the Laplacian comparison implies that for a fixed
z_{0}\in\Sigma
,
r_{\Sigma}(\cdot)=d_{\Sigma}(\cdot,z_{0})
satisfies
\Delta_{\Sigma}\log r_{\Sigma}\leq 0
in the sense of barrier for
r_{\Sigma}>0
. By applying the maximum principle to
u(z,y_{0})-\varepsilon\log r_{\Sigma}
for
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
, we conclude that
u(z,y_{0})
attains its maximum at interior of
\Sigma
and hence it must be constant by the strong maximum principle. Therefore,
\mathcal{R}_{\Sigma}=\mathcal{R}_{M}=0
. It follows that
M
is flat in this case since its sectional curvature is non-negative.

It remains to consider the case of
M^{3}=\mathbb{R}^{3}
topologically. We first appeal to the work of Reiris
[
41
, Theorem 1.1]
to see that
g_{0}
must be of Euclidean volume growth. In particular, as in the proof of Theorem
1.3
, there exist
v,{\alpha}>0
and a long-time solution
g(t)
on
M\times[0,+\infty)
such that for all
(x,t)\in M\times(0,+\infty)
,

(i)

|\text{\rm Rm}(g(x,t))|\leq{\alpha}t^{-1}
;

(ii)

\mathrm{Vol}_{g(t)}\left(B_{g(t)}(x,r)\right)\geq v_{0}r^{3}
for all
r>0
;

(iii)

\text{\rm Ric}(g(x,t))\geq 0
.

The properties of
g(t)
are scaling invariant.

We consider the blow-down Ricci flow as usual. Let
R_{i}\to+\infty
and
g_{i}(t)=R_{i}^{-2}g(R_{i}^{2}t)
so that
(M_{i}^{3},g_{i}(t),x_{i})=(M,g_{i}(t),x_{0})
sub-converges to
(M_{\infty},g_{\infty}(t),x_{\infty})
in the smooth pointed Cheeger-Gromov sense for
t\in(0,+\infty)
as
i\to+\infty
. Moreover, the limiting Ricci flow
g_{\infty}(t)
satisfies
\mathrm{AVR}(g_{\infty}(t))=\mathrm{AVR}(g_{0})>0
for all
t>0
. Clearly,
M_{\infty}
is still topologically
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Our goal is to show that the asymptotic volume ratio satisfies
\mathrm{AVR}(g_{\infty}(t))=1
. In what follow, we will use
C_{i}
to denote constant depending only on
{\alpha},v_{0}
.

For
r>1
sufficiently large. Let
z_{\infty}\in M_{\infty}
be such that
d_{g_{\infty}(1)}(x_{\infty},z_{\infty})=r
. By the smooth convergence and distance distortion
[
48
, Corollary 3.3]
, there exists
z_{i}\in M
with
d_{g_{0}}(x_{0},z_{i})\in(\frac{1}{2}R_{i}r,2R_{i}r)
such that
\mathcal{R}(g_{\infty}(z_{\infty},1))
is the limit of
\mathcal{R}(g_{i}(z_{i},1))
. Denote
g_{i,0}=R_{i}^{-2}g_{0}
for notation convenience. On
B_{g_{i,0}}(z_{i},\frac{1}{2}r)
, assumption implies
|\text{\rm Rm}(g_{i,0})|\leq 4C_{0}r^{-2}
. Moreover, by volume comparison for
z\in B_{g_{i,0}}(z_{i},\frac{1}{2}r)
and
0<s\leq\frac{1}{2}r
,

(6.1)

\begin{split}k_{g_{i,0}}(z,s)&=(R_{i}s)^{2}\fint_{B_{g_{0}}(z,R_{i}s)}|\text{\rm Rm}(g_{0})|\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}\\
&\leq(R_{i}s)^{2}\cdot\left(\frac{s+4r}{s}\right)^{n}\fint_{B_{g_{0}}(x_{0},R_{i}(s+2r))}|\text{\rm Rm}(g_{0})|\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}\\
&\leq\left(\frac{s+4r}{s}\right)^{n}\cdot k(x_{0},R_{i}(s+2r)).\end{split}

Using the assumption on asymptotic of
k(x_{0},\cdot)
, for any
\sigma>0
, there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
i>N
, we have

(6.2)

\begin{split}k_{g_{i,0}}(z,s)&\leq\min\left\{\left(\frac{4r+s}{s}\right)^{n}\sigma,4C_{0}s^{2}r^{-2}\right\}\end{split}

for all
z\in B_{g_{i,0}}(z_{i},\frac{1}{2}r)
and
0<s\leq\frac{1}{2}r
.

By
[
29
, Corollary 4.1]
and scaling argument, there exist

S_{1},C_{1}>0
such that

\mathrm{K}(g_{i}(t))\geq-C_{1}r^{-2}

on
B_{g_{i,0}}(z_{i},\frac{1}{4}r)\times[0,S_{1}r^{2}]
. We now intend to improve the lower bound of the sectional curvature. Let
\phi(x,t)
be the negative part of the lowest eigenvalue of
\mathrm{Rm}(g_{i}(x,t))
with respect to the cone of non-negative sectional curvature. It is known that
\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta_{g(t)}\right)\phi\leq\mathcal{R}\phi+c_{0}\phi^{2}
for some dimensional constant
c_{0}>0
by
[
1
]
and hence

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta_{g(t)}\right)(e^{-C_{1}c_{0}r^{-2}t}\phi)\leq\mathcal{R}(e^{-C_{1}c_{0}r^{-2}t}\phi)

on
B_{g_{i,0}}(z_{i},\frac{1}{4}r)\times[0,S_{1}r^{2}]
. We now apply
[
29
, Corollary 3.1]
to conclude that, for any
\ell>0
there exists
S_{\ell}>0
such that

(6.3)

e^{-c_{0}C_{1}r^{-2}t}\phi(x,t)-\int_{B_{g_{i,0}}(z_{i},\frac{1}{4}r)}G_{i}(x,t;y,0)\phi(y,0)\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{i,0}}(y)\leq 16^{\ell+1}t^{\ell}r^{-2(\ell+1)}

on
B_{g_{i,0}}(z_{i},\frac{1}{8}r)\times[0,S_{\ell}r^{2}]
,
where
G_{i}
denotes the dirichlet heat kernel of
g_{i}(t)
on
B_{g_{i,0}}(z_{i},\frac{1}{4}r)
.
By the properties of
g_{i}(t)
and Lemma
3.3
,
G_{i}(x,t;y,s)
satisfies a uniform Gaussian estimate and hence for
x\in B_{g_{i,0}}(z_{i},\frac{1}{8}r)
,

(6.4)

\begin{split}&\quad\int_{B_{g_{i,0}}(z_{i},\frac{1}{4}r)}G_{i}(x,t;y,0)\phi(y,0)\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{i,0}}(y)\\
&\leq\sigma^{\varepsilon}\int^{\frac{3}{8}r}_{0}\frac{C_{2}s^{n-1}}{t^{1+\frac{n}{2}}}\exp\left(-\frac{s^{2}}{C_{2}t}\right)\left(\frac{4r+s}{s}\right)^{n\varepsilon}s^{2(1-\varepsilon)}r^{-2(1-\varepsilon)}ds\\
&\quad+\frac{C_{2}r^{n-2}}{t^{\frac{n}{2}}}\exp\left(-\frac{r^{2}}{C_{2}t}\right)k_{g_{i,0}}\left(z_{i},\frac{3}{8}r\right)\\
&\leq C_{3}t^{-\varepsilon(1+\frac{1}{2}n)}r^{-2(1-\varepsilon)+n\varepsilon}\sigma^{\varepsilon}\int^{+\infty}_{0}s^{n-1+2(1-\varepsilon)-n\varepsilon}\exp\left(-\frac{s^{2}}{C_{2}}\right)ds\\
&\quad+C_{3}r^{-2}\sigma\end{split}

where
\varepsilon
is arbitrary in
(0,1)
and
n=3
now. Here we have used the bounds of
k_{g_{i,0}}(x,\cdot)
from (
6.2
), interpolating between both of them. If we choose
\varepsilon
sufficiently small so that
\varepsilon(1+\frac{n}{2})<\frac{1}{2}
and then we see that the sectional curvature of
g_{i}(t)
is bounded from below by

\mathrm{K}(g_{i}(t))\geq-L(t)=-C_{4}\left(16^{\ell+1}t^{\ell}r^{-2(\ell+1)}+\sigma^{\varepsilon}t^{-\varepsilon(1+\frac{1}{2}n)}r^{-2(1-\varepsilon)+n\varepsilon}+r^{-2}\sigma\right)

on
B_{g_{i,0}}(z_{i},\frac{1}{8}r)\times[0,S_{\ell}r^{2}]
.

Now we can proceed in a similar way as in the proof of Theorem
3.1
. Since
\mathrm{Rm}(g_{i}(t))+L\,g_{i}(t)\owedge g_{i}(t)/2
is in the cone of non-negative sectional curvature, the scalar curvature
\mathcal{R}
satisfies

(6.5)

\begin{split}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta_{g(t)}\right)\mathcal{R}&=2|\widetilde{Ric}-2Lg|^{2}\\
&\leq\mathcal{R}^{2}+8L\mathcal{R}+36L^{2}.\end{split}

In particular,
\varphi=\exp(-8\int^{t}_{0}L(s)\,ds)\cdot\mathcal{R}
satisfies

(6.6)

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta_{g(t)}\right)\varphi\leq\mathcal{R}\varphi+36L^{2}

on
B_{g_{i,0}}(z_{i},\frac{1}{8}r)\times[0,S_{\ell}r^{2}]
.

Define

(6.7)

\begin{split}u(x,t)&=\int_{B_{g_{i,0}}(z_{i},\frac{1}{4}r)}G_{i}(x,t;y,0)\mathcal{R}(y,0)\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{i,0}}(y)\\
&\quad+36\int^{t}_{0}\int_{B_{g_{i,0}}(z_{i},\frac{1}{4}r)}G_{i}(x,t;y,s)L^{2}(s)\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{i}(s)}(y)ds\end{split}

so that
\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta_{g(t)}\right)u=\mathcal{R}u+36L^{2}
and hence
[
29
, Corollary 3.1]
shows that by shrinking
S_{\ell}
if necessary, for all
t\in[0,S_{\ell}r^{2}]
,

(6.8)

\varphi(z_{i},t)=\exp\left(-8\int^{t}_{0}L(s)\,ds\right)\cdot\mathcal{R}_{g_{i}(t)}(z_{i})\leq u(z_{i},t)+32^{\ell+1}t^{\ell}r^{-2(\ell+1)}

We can use
[
29
, Proposition 4.1]
or Lemma
3.3
, and argue in the same way as in (
6.4
) to see that

(6.9)

\begin{split}&\int_{B_{g_{i,0}}(z_{i},\frac{1}{4}r)}G_{i}(z_{i},t;y,0)\mathcal{R}(y,0)\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{i,0}}(y)\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{\frac{r}{4}}\frac{C}{t^{\frac{n}{2}}}\exp\left(-\frac{s^{2}}{Ct}\right)\int_{\partial B_{g_{i,0}}(z_{i},\frac{1}{4}s)}\mathcal{R}\,ds\\
&=\frac{Cr^{n-2}}{t^{\frac{n}{2}}}\exp\left(-\frac{r^{2}}{Ct}\right)k_{g_{i,0}}\left(z_{i},\frac{r}{4}\right)\\
&\quad+\int^{\frac{1}{4}r}_{0}\frac{C_{2}s^{n-1}}{t^{1+\frac{n}{2}}}\exp\left(-\frac{s^{2}}{C_{2}t}\right)k_{g_{i,0}}\left(z_{i},s\right)ds\\
&\leq Cr^{-2}\sigma+\sigma^{\varepsilon}\int^{\frac{1}{4}r}_{0}\frac{C_{2}s^{n-1}}{t^{1+\frac{n}{2}}}\exp\left(-\frac{s^{2}}{C_{2}t}\right)\left(\frac{4r+s}{s}\right)^{n\varepsilon}s^{2(1-\varepsilon)}r^{-2(1-\varepsilon)}ds\\
&\leq C_{5}\left(r^{-2}\sigma+\sigma^{\varepsilon}t^{-\varepsilon(1+\frac{1}{2}n)}r^{-2(1-\varepsilon)+n\varepsilon}\right).\end{split}

Similarly, by
[
29
, Proposition 4.1]

(6.10)

\begin{split}\int_{B_{g_{i,0}}(z_{i},\frac{1}{4}r)}G_{i}(z_{i},t;y,s)\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{i}(s)}(y)&\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{C^{\prime}w^{n-1}}{(t-s)^{\frac{n}{2}}}\exp\left(-\frac{w^{2}}{C(t-s)}\right)\,dw\\
&=C^{\prime}\int_{0}^{\infty}\tau^{n-1}\exp\left(-\frac{\tau^{2}}{C}\right)\,d\tau\leq C_{6}.\end{split}

Hence

(6.11)

\begin{split}&\quad 36\int^{t}_{0}\int_{B_{g_{i,0}}(z_{i},\frac{1}{4}r)}G_{i}(x,t;y,s)L^{2}(s)\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{i}(s)}(y)\,ds\\
&\leq C\int_{0}^{t}L^{2}(s)\,ds\\
&\leq C_{7}\left(t^{2l+1}r^{-4(l+1)}+\sigma^{2\varepsilon}t^{1-2\varepsilon(1+\frac{1}{2}n)}r^{-4(1-\varepsilon)+2n\varepsilon}+r^{-4}\sigma^{2}t\right).\end{split}

We fix
t=1
and
\ell=10
. We might assume
r
to be large enough so that
1\in[0,S_{\ell}r^{2}]
and hence the estimates applies at

. Also note that
\varepsilon
is sufficiently small so that we have

\int_{0}^{1}L(s)\,ds\leq C\left(t^{l+1}r^{-2(l+1)}+\sigma^{\varepsilon}t^{1-\varepsilon(1+\frac{1}{2}n)}r^{-2(1-\varepsilon)+n\varepsilon}+r^{-2}\sigma t\right)\Big|_{t=1}\leq C_{8}

and

(6.12)

\begin{split}\varphi(z_{i},1)\leq C_{9}r^{-2}\left(r^{-2\ell}+\sigma+\sigma^{\varepsilon}r^{(n+2)\varepsilon}+r^{-2(2l+1)}+\sigma^{2\varepsilon}r^{-2(1-2\varepsilon)+2n\varepsilon}+r^{-2}\sigma^{2}\right).\end{split}

Since

is any arbitrarily large number and
\varepsilon
is a small positive number, we conclude after passing

and followed by

that
g_{\infty}(1)
has
\text{\rm Ric}\geq 0
and satisfies

(6.13)

|\mathrm{Rm}(g_{\infty}(z_{\infty},1))|\leq C_{10}\left(d_{g_{\infty}(1)}(x_{\infty},z_{\infty})\right)^{-22}

. Now a result of Greene-Petersen-Zhu

can be applied to show that

\mathbb{R}^{3}

and hence

. Now the flatness of

follows from the rigidity case of the volume comparison theorem. Alternatively, one can blow-down the manifold again together with the fast curvature decay to deduce flatness by pseudo-locality. This completes the proof.
∎

7.
application to almost flatness

In this section, we discuss another application of the Ricci flow smoothing. We consider compact manifolds with almost vanishing curvature in the sense of integral average curvature. This is motivated by the recent work of Chen-Wei-Ye
[
11
]
.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

By re-scaling, we assume
\mathrm{diam}(M,g_{0})=1
. We will use the Ricci flow smoothing to find a better metric from
g_{0}
using pseudo-locality Theorem. We only consider the case
n\geq 4
while the case of
n=3
can be done similarly as by strengthening the cone from
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{PIC1}}
to cone of non-negative sectional curvature.

We let
\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}>0
be small dimensional constants to be determined. We will determine
\varepsilon_{i}
so that metric
g_{0}
satisfying

(7.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathrm{Rm}(g_{0})+\varepsilon_{1}g_{0}\owedge g_{0}\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{PIC1}};\\
\displaystyle\int^{1}_{0}s\left(\fint_{B_{g_{0}}(x,s)}|\text{\rm Rm}(g_{0})|\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}\right)\,ds<\varepsilon_{2}\end{array}\right.

for all
x\in M
, can be deformed to one which is almost flat.

We apply Theorem
3.1
on
g_{0}
with
r=\mathrm{diam}(M,g_{0})=1
with
\Lambda_{0}=\varepsilon_{1}
so that there exists a Ricci flow
g(t),t\in[0,S_{n}]
with
g(0)=g_{0}
and
S_{n}<1
. We claim that if
\varepsilon_{1}
and
\varepsilon_{2}
are sufficiently small, then
\tilde{g}=g(S_{n})
will satisfy the almost flatness condition in Gromov–Ruh Theorem
[
21
,
43
]
. This follows by refining the proof of Theorem
3.1
. If
\varepsilon_{1}
is sufficiently small, we know that
\text{\rm Ric}(g(t))\geq-1
along the flow and hence,

(7.2)

d_{g(t)}(x,y)\leq e^{t}d_{g_{0}}(x,y)

for all
t\in[0,S_{n}]
and
x,y\in M
. Hence,
\mathrm{diam}(M,g(t))\leq C_{0}(n)
. Since
\mathrm{Rm}(g(t))+L_{n}\varepsilon_{1}g(t)\owedge g(t)\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{PIC1}}
, it suffices to estimate
\mathcal{R}(g(t))
from above by shrinking
\varepsilon_{1}
. Here we might assume
L_{n}
to be large. As in the proof of Theorem
3.1
, we consider the twisted curvature tensor
\widetilde{\text{\rm Rm}}=\text{\rm Rm}+2L_{n}\varepsilon_{1}g(t)\owedge g(t)
so that
\varphi=e^{-4L\varepsilon_{1}n(n-1)t}\tilde{\mathcal{R}}
satisfies
\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta_{g(t)}\right)\varphi\leq\mathcal{R}\varphi
and thus the same analysis as in the proof of Theorem
3.1
(but simpler) yields

(7.3)

\begin{split}\varphi(x,t)&\leq\int_{M}G(x,t;y,0)\tilde{\mathcal{R}}(y,0)\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}(y)\leq C_{n}(\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{2})t^{-1}.\end{split}

And hence,
\mathrm{diam}(M,\tilde{g})^{2}|\text{\rm Rm}(\tilde{g})|\leq C_{n}(\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{2})S_{n}^{-1}+C_{n}\varepsilon_{1}
for some dimensional constant
C_{n}>0
. By choosing
\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}>0
both small enough so that
C_{n}(\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{2})S_{n}^{-1}
is smaller than the dimensional constant in Gromov–Ruh Theorem
[
21
,
43
]
, we conclude that
M
supports metric
\tilde{g}
which is almost flat and hence
M
is diffeomorphic to an infranil manifold. Result follows by relabelling the constants.
∎

Complex algebraic stacks and morphisms of intersection complexes
Matthew Huynh

Abstract
We generalize a construction of Barthel-Brasselet-Fieseler-Gabber-Kaup in the setting of complex varieties to the setting of finite type, complex algebraic stacks. Given two such stacks 𝒳 , 𝒴 \mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y} with affine stabilizers, and a morphism between them, we construct a morphism from the pullback of the intersection complex of 𝒴 \mathcal{Y} to the intersection complex of 𝒳 \mathcal{X} . As an application, we show that the Borel-Moore fundamental class of a closed substack 𝒵 \mathcal{Z} in a Deligne-Mumford stack 𝒳 \mathcal{X} lifts to a class in the intersection cohomology of 𝒳 \mathcal{X} .

Introduction

The rational cohomology groups of a smooth, complex projective variety enjoy many nice properties. They satisfy Poincaré duality, underlie pure Hodge structures, and form an
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-representation via the Hard Lefschetz Theorem. Goresky and MacPherson defined intersection (co)homology groups in
[
5
,
6
]
for a general class of topological spaces, and the intersection (co)homology groups of a singular projective variety enjoy the properties listed above.

The original cycle-theoretic definition of intersection homology proceeds by specifying geometric cycles whose supports suitably intersect the singular strata of the space; these are called intersection cycles. On a complex variety
X
, it makes sense to ask: if we are given an algebraic cycle
Z
in
X
, is there an intersection cycle
\gamma
that is homologous to
Z
?

To answer this question, Barthel-Brasselet-Fieseler-Gabber-Kaup
[
1
]
used the sheaf-theoretic description of intersection homology and the shifted intersection complex. Their main theorem is that for any morphism
f:X\to Y
between two pure-dimensional complex varieties, there exists a morphism
\mu^{f}:f^{\ast}IC_{Y}\to IC_{X}
, (not necessarily unique), fitting into the commutative diagram

\textstyle{f^{\ast}IC_{Y}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mu^{f}}
\textstyle{IC_{X}}
\textstyle{f^{\ast}\mathbb{Q}_{Y}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\sim}
\scriptstyle{f^{\ast}c_{Y}}
\textstyle{\mathbb{Q}_{X}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces,}
\scriptstyle{c_{X}}

where
c_{X}:\mathbb{Q}_{X}\to IC_{X}
is the natural comparison morphism. The morphisms they construct are called
associated morphisms
.

One global consequence of their main theorem for proper maps
f:X\to Y
is the following. There exists a morphism of intersection homology groups,

\nu_{f}:IH_{\ast}^{BM}(X)\to IH_{\ast}^{BM}(Y),

such that for any intersection homology class
[\gamma]\in IH_{\ast}^{BM}(X)
, the Borel-Moore homology class of any intersection cycle in
\nu_{f}([\gamma])
is the Borel-Moore homology class of
f(\gamma)
. By applying this to the inclusion of an irreducible subvariety in a complex variety, they give a positive answer to the question posed earlier. Namely, if
Z
is an irreducible subvariety of a pure-dimensional complex variety
X
, then there exists an intersection cycle
\gamma
on
X
, whose Borel-Moore homology class coincides with the Borel-Moore fundamental class of
Z
.

The purpose of this paper is to establish the existence of associated morphisms in the setting of complex algebraic stacks. We give a precise formulation of our main result, Theorem
1.2
, after introducing our notation and conventions. As an application, we show in §4 that for any closed, irreducible substack
\mathcal{Z}
of a pure-dimensional, finite type, Deligne-Mumford stack
\mathcal{X}
over
\mathbb{C}
, the Borel-Moore homology class of
\mathcal{Z}
lifts to a class in the intersection cohomology of
\mathcal{X}
.

A corollary of the Decomposition Theorem for stacks

Setup 2.1
.

Throughout this section,
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
denote stacks with affine stabilizers, and
\pi:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
is a proper, surjective morphism that is representable by schemes.

The purpose of this section is to prove an auxiliary result, which is a corollary of Sun’s version of the Decomposition Theorem
[

]
*Theorem 4.2.4. The setting of Setup

satisfies the assumptions of the referenced theorem, and thus we have an isomorphism of objects in
\mathbf{D}^{b}_{c}(\mathcal{B},\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})

\pi_{\ast}IC_{\mathcal{A}}\simeq\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}(\prescript{p}{}{\mathscr{H}}^{i}\pi_{\ast}IC_{\mathcal{A}})[-i].

(2.1)

Furthermore, the perverse sheaves

appearing in the right-hand side of (

Proposition 2.2
.

Assume Setup

is a direct summand of
\pi_{\ast}IC_{\mathcal{A}}

Remark 2.3
.

A proof of this proposition for morphisms of varieties over finite fields, (which can be adapted to the setting of complex varieties) can be found in

*§5.3, and our proof strategy follows theirs. The proposition does not seem to immediately follow from the case of schemes and descent, because the constructed splitting is a morphism of objects in the derived category.

4
Lifts of classes of closed substacks

Setup 4.1
.

Throughout this section,
\mathcal{X}
denotes a Deligne-Mumford stack with affine stabilizers, which has pure-dimension
n
.
\mathcal{Z}
denotes an
m
-dimensional irreducible, closed substack of
\mathcal{X}
, and the inclusion is denoted
i:\mathcal{Z}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{X}
.

Here we present an application of Theorem
1.2
, which is a generalization of
[
1
]
*Théorème 2.4. Recall that the Borel-Moore homology groups of
\mathcal{X}
are defined by

H^{BM}_{q}(\mathcal{X}):=H^{-q}(\mathcal{X},\omega_{\mathcal{X}}),

where
\omega_{\mathcal{X}}
is the dualizing complex constructed in
[
7
]
. These groups are zero when
q\notin[0,2n]
, and the rank of
H^{BM}_{2n}(\mathcal{X})
equals the number of irreducible components of
\mathcal{X}
. In particular,
H^{BM}_{2m}(\mathcal{Z})
is generated by a single element
[\mathcal{Z}]
, called the fundamental class. For more on the basic properties of Borel-Moore homology for Deligne-Mumford stacks, see
[
10
]
*§2.

The goal of this section is to show that the image of
[\mathcal{Z}]
in
H^{BM}_{2m}(\mathcal{X})
lifts to a class in the intersection cohomology of
\mathcal{X}
. By applying Verdier duality to the comparison morphism
c_{\mathcal{Z}}:(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})_{\mathcal{Z}}\to IC_{\mathcal{Z}}
and then shifting by
-2m
, we obtain a morphism
IC_{\mathcal{Z}}\to\omega_{\mathcal{Z}}[-2m]
. This induces an isomorphism on cohomology in degree 0, as we show below.

Lemma 4.2
.

Assume Setup
4.1
. Then the morphism
IC_{\mathcal{Z}}\to\omega_{\mathcal{Z}}[-2m]
, which is obtained by dualizing the comparison morphism and shifting, induces an isomorphism

H^{0}(\mathcal{Z},IC_{\mathcal{Z}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{\longrightarrow}\rule{0.0pt}{1.72218pt}}}H^{-2m}(\mathcal{Z},\omega_{\mathcal{Z}}).

Proof.

Let
j:\mathcal{U}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{Z}
be the inclusion of an open, dense, smooth substack, and let
i:\mathcal{W}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{Z}
be the inclusion of the closed complement. Apply the functorial distinguished triangle
i_{!}i^{!}\to\mathop{\mathrm{id}}\nolimits\to j_{\ast}j^{\ast}
to the composition
(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})_{\mathcal{Z}}\to IC_{\mathcal{Z}}\to\omega_{\mathcal{Z}}[-2m]
and then apply the functor
H^{0}(\mathcal{Z},-)
. This yields a commutative diagram

\textstyle{H^{0}(\mathcal{W},i^{!}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})_{\mathcal{Z}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{H^{0}(\mathcal{Z},(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})_{\mathcal{Z}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\simeq}
\textstyle{H^{0}(\mathcal{Z},j_{\ast}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})_{\mathcal{U}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\simeq}
\textstyle{H^{1}(\mathcal{W},i^{!}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})_{\mathcal{Z}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{H^{0}(\mathcal{W},i^{!}IC_{\mathcal{Z}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{H^{0}(\mathcal{Z},IC_{\mathcal{Z}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

where the rows are exact. Because

H^{0}(\mathcal{Z},IC_{\mathcal{Z}})\to H^{0}(\mathcal{Z},j_{\ast}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})_{\mathcal{U}})

Observe that by
[
2
]
*Corollaire 1.4.24 and
[

]
*Lemma 6.2, we have
i^{!}IC_{\mathcal{Z}}\simeq\prescript{p}{}{\tau}_{\geq m+1}i^{!}IC_{\mathcal{Z}}

\prescript{p}{}{\mathscr{H}}^{s}(i^{!}IC_{\mathcal{Z}})=0
for all

E_{1}^{r,s}=H^{r}(\mathcal{W},\prescript{p}{}{\mathscr{H}}^{s}(i^{!}IC_{\mathcal{Z}}))\implies H^{r+s}(\mathcal{W},i^{!}IC_{\mathcal{Z}}),

shows that
H^{0}(\mathcal{W},i^{!}IC_{\mathcal{Z}})=0
, and the lemma follows.

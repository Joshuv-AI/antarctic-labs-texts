L p L^{p} - L q L^{q} Multipliers on commutative hypergroups
Vishvesh Kumar, Michael Ruzhansky

Abstract
. The main purpose of this paper is to prove Hörmander’s L p L^{p} - L q L^{q} boundedness of Fourier multipliers on commutative hypergroups. We carry out this objective by establishing Paley inequality and Hausdorff-Young-Paley inequality for commutative hypergroups. We show the L p L^{p} - L q L^{q} boundedness of the spectral multipliers for the generalised radial Laplacian by examining our results on Chébli-Trimèche hypergroups. As a consequence, we obtain embedding theorems and time asymptotics for the L p L^{p} - L q L^{q} norms of the heat kernel for generalised radial Laplacian. Finally, we present applications of the obtained results to study the well-posedness of nonlinear partial differential equations.

The Paley-type inequality describes the growth of the Fourier transform of a function in terms of its
L^{p}
-norm. Interpolating the Paley-inequality with the Hausdorff-Young inequality one can obtain the following Hörmander’s version of the Hausdorff-Young-Paley inequality,

\left(\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|(\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}f)(\xi)\phi(\xi)^{\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}|^{r}\,d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}\leq\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},\,\,\,1<p\leq r\leq p^{\prime}<\infty,\,\,1<p<2.

(1)

Also, as a consequence of the Hausdorff-Young-Paley inequality, Hörmander
[
34
, page 106]
proved that the condition

\sup_{t>0}t^{b}|\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:m(\xi)\geq t\}|<\infty,\quad\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{b},

(2)

where
1<p\leq 2\leq q<\infty,
implies the existence of a bounded extension of a Fourier multiplier
T_{m}
with symbol

from
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to

Recently, the second author with R. Akylzhanov studied classical Hörmander results for unimodular locally compact groups and homogeneous spaces
[
4

5

\|T_{m}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\sup_{s>0}s\left(\int_{\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\,m(\xi)\geq s\}}d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}\simeq\|m\|_{L^{r,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\simeq\|T_{m}\|_{L^{r,\infty}(\textnormal{VN}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))},

where
\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q},

\|m\|_{L^{r,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
is the Lorentz norm of
m,
and
\|T_{m}\|_{L^{r,\infty}(\textnormal{VN}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}
is the norm of the operator

in the Lorentz space on the group von Neumann algebra

\mathbb{R}^{n}.

They use the Lorentz spaces and group von Neumann algebra technique for extending it to general locally compact unimodular groups. The unimodularity assumption has its own advantages such as existence of the canonical trace on the group von Neumann algebra and consequently, Plancherel formula and the Hausdorff-Young inequality.

In this paper, we prove Paley-type inequality, Hausdorff-Young-Paley inequality and Hörmander multiplier theorem for commutative hypergroups. We refer to Section

2.
Fourier analysis on commutative hypergroups

In this section, we present essentials of the Fourier analysis on commutative hypergroups.
We begin this section with the definition of a hypergroup. In
[
35
]
, Jewett refers to hypergroups as convos.

Definition 2.1
.

A
hypergroup
is a non empty locally compact Hausdorff space
H
with a weakly continuous, associative convolution
*
on the Banach space
M(H)
of all bounded regular Borel measures on
H
such that
(M(H),*)
becomes a Banach algebra and the following properties hold:

(i)

For any
x,y\in H,
the convolution
\delta_{x}*\delta_{y}
is a probability measure with compact support, where
\delta_{x}
is the point mass measure at
x.
Also, the mapping
(x,y)\mapsto\textnormal{supp}(\delta_{x}*\delta_{y})
is continuous from
K\times K
to the space
\mathcal{C}(H)
of all nonempty compact subsets of
H
equipped with the Michael (Vietoris) topology (see
[
44
]
for details).

(ii)

There exists a unique element
e\in H
such that
\delta_{x}*\delta_{e}=\delta_{e}*\delta_{x}=\delta_{x}
for every
x\in H.

(iii)

There is a homeomophism
x\mapsto\check{x}
on
H
of order two which induces an involution on
M(H)
where
\check{\mu}(E)=\mu(\check{E})
for any Borel set
E,
and
e\in\textnormal{supp}(\delta_{x}*\delta_{y})
if and only if
x=\check{y}.

Note that the weak continuity assures that the convolution of bounded measures on a hypergroup is uniquely determined by the convolution of point measures. A hypergroup is called a commutative hypergroup if the convolution is commutative. A hypergroup
H
is called
hermitian
if the involution on
H
is the identity map, i.e.,
\check{x}=x
for all
x\in H.

Note that a hermitian hypergroup is commutative. Every locally compact abelian group is a trivial example of a commutative hypergroup. Other important examples include Gelfand pairs, Bessel-Kingman hypergroups, Jacobi hypergroups and Chébli-Trimèche hypergroups.

A
left Haar measure

\lambda
on
H
is a non-zero positive Radon measure
\lambda
such that

\int_{H}f(x*y)d\lambda(y)=\int_{H}f(y)\,d\lambda(y)\quad(\forall x\in H,\,f\in C_{c}(H)),

where we used the notation
f(x*y)=(\delta_{x}*\delta_{y})(f)
. It is well known that a Haar measure is unique (up to scalar multiple) if it exists
[
35
]
. Throughout this article, a left Haar measure is simply called a Haar measure.
We would like to make a remark here that it is still not known if a general hypergroup has a Haar measure but several important classes of hypergroups including commutative hypergroups, compact hypergroups, discrete hypergroups, nilpotent hypergroups possess a Haar measure, see
[
35
,
30
,
14
,
54
,
6
,
36
,
37
,
38
]
for more detail on harmonic analysis on hypergroups and its applications.

Denote the space of all bounded continuous complex valued functions on
H
by
C^{b}(H).
The dual space of a commutative hypergroup
H
is defined by

\widehat{H}=\{\chi\in C^{b}(H):\chi\neq 0,\,\chi(\check{x})=\overline{\chi(x)}\mbox{ and }\chi(x*y)=\chi(x)\chi(y)\ \forall\ x,y\in H\}.

The elements of
\widehat{H}
are called
characters
of
H.
We equip
\widehat{H}
also with the compact-open topology so that
H
becomes a locally compact Hausdorff space. Note that
\widehat{H}
need not possess a hypergroup structure. For example, see
[
35
, Example 9.3C]
.

The Fourier transform of
f\in L^{1}(H,\lambda)
is defined by

\displaystyle\widehat{f}(\chi)=\widehat{f}(\chi)=\int_{H}f(x)\overline{\chi(x)}\ d\lambda(x),\,\,\,\forall\ \chi\in\widehat{H}.

(9)

By
[
14
, Theorem 2.2.4]
, the mapping
f\mapsto\widehat{f}
is a norm-decreasing
*
-algebra homomorphism from
L^{1}(H,\lambda)
into
L^{\infty}(\widehat{H},\pi).
Furthermore,
\widehat{f}
vanishes at infinity. Also, as in the case of locally compact abelian groups, there exists a unique positive Borel measure
\pi
on
\widehat{H}
such that

\int_{H}|f(x)|^{2}\ d\lambda(x)=\int_{\widehat{H}}|\widehat{f}(\chi)|^{2}\ d\pi(\chi)\,\,\,\forall\ f\in L^{2}(H,\lambda)\cap L^{1}(H,\lambda).

In fact, the Fourier transform extends to a unitary operator from
L^{2}(H,\lambda)
onto
L^{2}(\widehat{H},\pi).
We would like to remark here that the support of
\pi,
denoted
\mathcal{S},
need not be equal to
\widehat{H}
(see
[
14
, Example 2.2.49]
). When
\mathcal{S}=\widehat{H},

H
is called a
strong hypergroup
.

The following theorem is the Hausdorff-Young inequality for commutative hypergroups
[
28
]
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
p,p^{\prime}
be such that
1\leq p\leq 2
and
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1.
Then for
f\in L^{2}(H,\,d\lambda)\cap L^{1}(H,\,d\lambda)
we have the inequality

\displaystyle\|\widehat{f}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\widehat{H},d\pi)}\leq\|f\|_{L^{p}(H,\,d\lambda)}.

3.
Fourier multipliers for commutative hypergroups

3.1.
Paley inequality for commutative hypergroups

In this subsection, we prove the Paley inequality for commutative hypergroups. The Paley inequality has been proved for many different setting, e.g., compact homogeneoups spaces
[
4
,
2
]
, compact quantum groups
[
3
,
56
]
, compact hypergroups
[
40
]
and locally compact groups
[
5
]
.

Paley inequality is an important inequality in itself but also plays a vital role in obtaining the Hausdorff-Young-Paley inequality for commutative hypergroups. We follow the strategy of the proof of the Paley inequality in
[
5
]
in the non-commutative setting.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
H
be a commutative hypergroup equipped with a Haar measure
\lambda
and let
\widehat{H}
be the dual of
H
equipped with measure
\pi.

Suppose that
\psi
is a positive function on
\widehat{H}
satisfying the condition

M_{\psi}:=\sup_{t>0}t\int_{\underset{\psi(\chi)\geq t}{\chi\in\widehat{H}}}d\pi(\chi)<\infty.

(10)

Then for
f\in L^{p}(H,d\lambda),

1<p\leq 2,
we have

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\widehat{H}}|\widehat{f}(\chi)|^{p}\,\psi(\chi)^{2-p}d\pi(\chi)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\lesssim M_{\psi}^{\frac{2-p}{p}}\,\|f\|_{L^{p}(H,d\lambda)}.

(11)

Proof.

Let us consider a measure
\nu
on
\widehat{H}
given by

\nu(\chi)=\psi(\chi)^{2}d\pi(\chi).

(12)

We define the corresponding
L^{p}(\widehat{H},\nu)
- space,
1\leq p<\infty,
as the space of all complex-valued function
f
defined by
\widehat{H}
such that

\|f\|_{L^{p}(\widehat{H},\nu)}:=\left(\int_{\widehat{H}}|f(\chi)|^{p}\,\psi(\chi)^{2}d\pi(\chi)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty.

We define a sublinear operator
T
for
f\in L^{p}(H,d\lambda)
by

Tf(\chi):=\frac{|\widehat{f}(\chi)|}{\psi(\chi)}\in L^{p}(\widehat{H},\nu).

We will show that
T
is well-defined and bounded from
L^{p}(H,d\lambda)
to
L^{p}(\widehat{H},\nu)
for any
1<p\leq 2.
In other words, we claim the following estimate:

\|Tf\|_{L^{p}(\widehat{H},\nu)}=\left(\int_{\widehat{H}}\frac{|\widehat{f}(\chi)|^{p}}{\psi(\chi)^{p}}\,\psi(\chi)^{2}d\pi(\chi)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\lesssim M_{\psi}^{\frac{2-p}{p}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(H,d\lambda)},

(13)

which will give us the required inequality (
11
) with
M_{\psi}:=\sup_{t>0}\int_{\underset{\psi(\chi)\geq t}{\chi\in\widehat{H}}}d\pi(\lambda).

We will show that
T
is weak-type
(2,2)
and weak-type
(1,1).
More precisely, with the distribution function,

\nu(y;Tf)=\int_{\underset{\frac{|\widehat{f}(\chi)|}{\psi(\chi)}\geq y}{\chi\in\widehat{H}}}\psi(\chi)^{2}d\pi(\chi),

where
\nu
is given by formula (
12
), we show that

\nu(y;Tf)\leq\left(\frac{M_{2}\|f\|_{L^{2}(H,d\lambda)}}{y}\right)^{2}\,\,\,\,\,\,\text{with norm}\,\,M_{2}=1,

(14)

\nu(y;Tf)\leq\frac{M_{1}\|f\|_{L^{1}(H,d\lambda)}}{y}\,\,\,\,\,\,\text{with norm}\,\,M_{1}=M_{\psi}.

(15)

Then the estimate (
13
) follows from the Marcinkiewicz interpolation Theorem. Now, to show (
14
), using Plancherel identity we get

\displaystyle y^{2}\nu(y;Tf)

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{y>0}y^{2}\nu(y;Tf)=:\|Tf\|^{2}_{L^{2,\infty}(\widehat{H},\nu)}\leq\|Tf\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\widehat{H},\nu)}

\displaystyle=\int_{\widehat{H}}\left(\frac{|\widehat{f}(\chi)|}{\psi(\chi)}\right)^{2}\psi(\chi)^{2}d\pi(\chi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\widehat{H}}|\widehat{f}(\chi)|^{2}\,d\pi(\chi)=\|f\|_{2}^{2}.

Thus,
T
is type
(2,2)
with norm
M_{2}\leq 1.
Further, we show that
T
is of weak type
(1,1)
with norm
M_{1}=M_{\psi}
; more precisely, we show that

\displaystyle\nu\left\{\chi\in\widehat{H}:\frac{|\widehat{f}(\chi)|}{\psi(\chi)}>y\right\}\lesssim M_{\psi}\frac{\|f\|_{L^{1}(H,d\lambda)}}{y}.

(16)

The left hand side is an integral
\int\psi(\chi)d\pi(\chi)
taken over all those
\chi\in\widehat{H}
for which
\frac{|\widehat{f}(\chi)|}{\psi(\chi)}>y.

Since
|\widehat{f}(\chi)|\leq\|f\|_{1}
for all
\chi\in\widehat{H}
we have

\left\{\chi\in\widehat{H}:\frac{|\widehat{f}(\chi)|}{\psi(\chi)}>y\right\}\subset\left\{\chi\in\widehat{H}:\frac{\|f\|_{1}}{\psi(\chi)}>y\right\},

for any
y>0
and, therefore,

\nu\left\{\chi\in\widehat{H}:\frac{|\widehat{f}(\chi)|}{\psi(\chi)}>y\right\}\leq\nu\left\{\chi\in\widehat{H}:\frac{\|f\|_{1}}{\psi(\chi)}>y\right\}.

Now by setting
w:=\frac{\|f\|_{1}}{y},
we have

\displaystyle\nu\left\{\chi\in\widehat{H}:\frac{\|f\|_{1}}{\psi(\chi)}>y\right\}\leq\int_{\overset{\chi\in\widehat{H}}{\psi(\chi)\leq w}}\psi(\chi)^{2}\,d\pi(\chi).

(17)

Now we claim that

\displaystyle\int_{\overset{\chi\in\widehat{H}}{\psi(\chi)\leq w}}\psi(\chi)^{2}\,d\pi(\chi)\lesssim M_{\psi}w.

(18)

Indeed, first we notice that

\displaystyle\int_{\overset{\chi\in\widehat{H}}{\psi(\chi)\leq w}}\psi(\chi)^{2}\,d\pi(\chi)=\int_{\overset{\chi\in\widehat{H}}{\psi(\chi)\leq w}}\,d\pi(\chi)\int_{0}^{\psi(\chi)^{2}}d\tau.

By interchanging the order of integration we get

\displaystyle\int_{\overset{\chi\in\widehat{H}}{\psi(\chi)\leq w}}\,d\pi(\chi)\int_{0}^{\psi(\chi)^{2}}d\tau=\int_{0}^{w^{2}}d\tau\int_{\underset{\tau^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\psi(\chi)\leq w}{\chi\in\widehat{H}}}d\pi(\chi).

Further, by making substitution
\tau=t^{2},
it gives

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{w^{2}}d\tau\int_{\underset{\tau^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\psi(\chi)\leq w}{\chi\in\widehat{H}}}d\pi(\chi)

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{w}t\,dt\int_{\underset{t\leq\psi(\chi)\leq w}{\chi\in\widehat{H}}}d\pi(\chi)

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{0}^{w}t\,dt\int_{\underset{t\leq\psi(\chi)}{\chi\in\widehat{H}}}d\pi(\chi).

Since

t\int_{\underset{t\leq\psi(\chi)}{\chi\in\widehat{H}}}d\pi(\chi)\leq\sup_{t>0}t\int_{\underset{t\leq\psi(\chi)}{\chi\in\widehat{H}}}d\pi(\chi)=M_{\psi}

is finite by assumption
M_{\psi}<\infty,
we have

\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{w}t\,dt\int_{\underset{t\leq\psi(\chi)}{\chi\in\widehat{H}}}d\pi(\chi)\lesssim M_{\psi}w.

This establishes our claim (
18
) and eventually proves (
16
). So, we have proved (
14
) and (
15
). Then by using the Marcinkiewicz interpolation theorem with
p_{1}=1
and
p_{2}=2
and
\frac{1}{p}=1-\theta+\frac{\theta}{2}
we now obtain

\left(\int_{\widehat{H}}\left(\frac{|\widehat{f}(\chi)|}{\psi(\chi)}\right)^{p}\psi(\chi)^{2}\,d\pi(\chi)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}=\|Tf\|_{L^{p}(\widehat{H},\,\nu)}\lesssim M_{\psi}^{\frac{2-p}{p}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(H,d\lambda)}.

This completes the proof of the theorem. ∎

3.2.
Hausdorff-Young-Paley inequality for commutative hypergroups

In this subsection, we prove the Hausdorff-Young-Paley inequality for commutative hypergroups. The Hausdorff-Young-Paley inequality is an important inequality in itself but it serves as an essential tool to prove
L^{p}
-
L^{q}
Fourier multiplier for commutative hypergroups.
The following theorem
[
11
]
is useful to obtain our result.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
d\mu_{0}(x)=\omega_{0}(x)d\mu^{\prime}(x),

d\mu_{1}(x)=\omega_{1}(x)d\mu^{\prime}(x),
and write
L^{p}(\omega)=L^{p}(\omega d\mu^{\prime})
for the weight
\omega.
Suppose that
0<p_{0},p_{1}<\infty.
Then

(L^{p_{0}}(\omega_{0}),L^{p_{1}}(\omega_{1}))_{\theta,p}=L^{p}(\omega),

where
0<\theta<1,\,\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1-\theta}{p_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{p_{1}}
and
\omega=\omega_{0}^{\frac{p(1-\theta)}{p_{0}}}\omega_{1}^{\frac{p\theta}{p_{1}}}.

The following corollary is immediate.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
d\mu_{0}(x)=\omega_{0}(x)d\mu^{\prime}(x),

d\mu_{1}(x)=\omega_{1}(x)d\mu^{\prime}(x).
Suppose that
0<p_{0},p_{1}<\infty.
If a continuous linear operator
A
admits bounded extensions,
A:L^{p}(Y,\mu)\rightarrow L^{p_{0}}(\omega_{0})
and
A:L^{p}(Y,\mu)\rightarrow L^{p_{1}}(\omega_{1}).
Then, there exists a bounded extension
A:L^{p}(Y,\mu)\rightarrow L^{b}(\omega)
of
A
, where
0<\theta<1,\,\frac{1}{b}=\frac{1-\theta}{p_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{p_{1}}
and

\omega=\omega_{0}^{\frac{b(1-\theta)}{p_{0}}}\omega_{1}^{\frac{b\theta}{p_{1}}}.

Using the above corollary we now present the Hausdorff-Young-Paley inequality.

Theorem 3.4

(Hausdorff-Young-Paley inequality)
.

Let
H
be a commutative hypergroup equipped with a Haar measure
\lambda
and let
\widehat{H}
be the dual of
H
equipped with measure
\pi.
Let
1<p\leq 2,
and let
1<p\leq b\leq p^{\prime}\leq\infty,
where
p^{\prime}=\frac{p}{p-1}.
If
\psi(\chi)
is a positive function on
\widehat{H}
such that

M_{\psi}:=\sup_{t>0}t\int_{\underset{\psi(\chi)\geq t}{\chi\in\widehat{H}}}d\pi(\chi)

(19)

is finite, then for every
f\in L^{p}(H,d\lambda)

we have

\left(\int_{\widehat{H}}\left(|\widehat{f}(\chi)|\psi(\chi)^{\frac{1}{b}-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\right)^{b}d\pi(\chi)\right)^{\frac{1}{b}}\lesssim M_{\varphi}^{\frac{1}{b}-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(H,d\lambda)}.

(20)

This naturally reduces to the Hausdorff-Young inequality when
b=p^{\prime}
and to the Paley inequality (
11
) when
b=p.

Proof.

From Theorem
3.1
, the operator defined by

Af(\chi)=\widehat{f}(\chi),\,\,\,\,\chi\in\widehat{H}

is bounded from
L^{p}(H,d\lambda)
to
L^{p}(\widehat{H},\omega_{0}d\mu^{\prime}),
where
d\mu^{\prime}(\chi)=d\pi(\chi)
and
\omega_{0}(\chi)=\psi(\chi)^{2-p}.
From Theorem
2.2
, we deduce that
A:L^{p}(H,d\lambda)\rightarrow L^{p^{\prime}}(\widehat{H},\omega_{1}d\mu^{\prime})
with
d\mu^{\prime}(\chi)=d\pi(\chi)
and
\omega_{1}(\chi)=1
admits a bounded extension. By using the real interpolation (Corollary
3.3
above) we will prove that
A:L^{p}(H,d\lambda)\rightarrow L^{b}(\widehat{H},\omega d\mu^{\prime}),

p\leq b\leq p^{\prime},
is bounded,
where the space
L^{p}(\widehat{H},\,\omega d\mu^{\prime})
is defined by the norm

\|\sigma\|_{L^{p}(\widehat{H},\,\omega d\mu^{\prime})}:=\left(\int_{\widehat{H}}|\sigma(\chi)|^{p}w(\chi)\,d\mu^{\prime}(\chi)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}=\left(\int_{\widehat{H}}|\sigma(\chi)|^{p}w(\chi)d\pi(\chi)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

and
\omega(\chi)
is positive function over
\widehat{H}
to be determined. To compute
\omega,
we can use Corollary
3.3
, by fixing
\theta\in(0,1)
such that
\frac{1}{b}=\frac{1-\theta}{p}+\frac{\theta}{p^{\prime}}
. In this case
\theta=\frac{p-b}{b(p-2)},
and

\omega=\omega_{0}^{\frac{p(1-\theta)}{p_{0}}}\omega_{1}^{\frac{p\theta}{p_{1}}}=\psi(\chi)^{1-\frac{b}{p^{\prime}}}.

(21)

Thus we finish the proof.
∎

3.3.
L^{p}
-
L^{q}
boundedness of Fourier multipliers on commutative hypergroups

We begin with the definition of Fourier multipliers on commutative hypergroups. For a function
h\in L^{\infty}(\widehat{H},d\pi),
define the operator
T_{h}
as

\widehat{T_{h}f}(\chi)=h(\chi)\widehat{f}(\chi),\,\,\,\,\chi\in\widehat{H},

for all
f
belonging to a suitable function space on
\widehat{H}.
The operator
T_{h}
is called the Fourier multiplier on
H
with symbol
h.
It is clear that
T_{h}
is a bounded operator on
L^{2}(H,d\lambda)
by the Plancherel theorem. A Fourier multiplier commutes with the translation operators. In fact, the Fourier multipliers can be characterized using translation operators
[
47
,
14
,
39
]
.

In the next result, we show that if the symbol
h
of a Fourier multipliers
A
defined on
C_{c}(H)
satisfies certain Hörmander’s condition then
T_{h}
can be extended as a bounded linear operator from
L^{p}(H,d\lambda)
to
L^{q}(H,d\lambda)
for the range
1<p\leq 2\leq q<\infty.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
1<p\leq 2\leq q<\infty
and let
H
be a commutative hypergroup. Suppose that
T
is a Fourier multiplier with symbol
h.
Then we have

\|T\|_{L^{p}(H,\,d\lambda)\rightarrow L^{q}(H,\,d\lambda)}\lesssim\sup_{s>0}s\left[\int_{\{\chi\in\widehat{H}:|h(\chi)|\geq s\}}d\pi(\chi)\right]^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}.

Proof.

Let us first assume that
p\leq q^{\prime},
where
\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}=1.
Since
q^{\prime}\leq 2,
dualising the Hausdorff-Young inequality gives that

\displaystyle\|Tf\|_{L^{q}(H,\,d\lambda)}\leq\|\widehat{Tf}\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\widehat{H},\,d\pi)}=\|h\widehat{f}\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\widehat{H},d\pi)}.

The case
q^{\prime}\leq(p^{\prime})^{\prime}=p
can be reduced to the case
p\leq q^{\prime}
as follows. Using the duality of
L^{p}
-spaces we have
\|T\|_{L^{p}(H,\,d\lambda)\rightarrow L^{q}(H,\,d\lambda)}=\|T^{*}\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(H,\,d\lambda)\rightarrow L^{p^{\prime}}(H,\,d\lambda)}.
The symbol of adjoint operator
T^{*}
is equal to
\bar{h}
and obviously we have
|\bar{h}|=|h|
(see Theorem 4.2 in
[
2
]
).
Now, we are in a position to apply Theorem
3.4
. Set
\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{r}.
Now, by applying Theorem
3.4
with
\psi=|h|^{r}
with
b=q^{\prime}
we get

\|h\widehat{f}\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(H,d\lambda)}\lesssim\left(\sup_{s>0}s\int_{\underset{|h(\chi)|^{r}>s}{\chi\in\widehat{H}}}d\pi(\chi)\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(H,\,d\lambda)}

for all
f\in L^{p}(H,d\lambda),
in view of
\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{r.}
Thus, for
1<p\leq 2\leq q<\infty,
we obtain

\|Tf\|_{L^{q}(H,d\lambda)}\lesssim\left(\sup_{s>0}s\int_{\underset{|h(\chi)|^{r}>s}{\chi\in\widehat{H}}}d\pi(\chi)\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(H,d\lambda)}.

Further, the proof follows from the following inequality:

\displaystyle\left(\sup_{s>0}s\int_{\underset{|h(\chi)|^{r}>s}{\chi\in\widehat{H}}}d\pi(\chi)\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}

\displaystyle=\left(\sup_{s>0}s\int_{\underset{|h(\chi)|>s^{\frac{1}{r}}}{\chi\in\widehat{H}}}d\pi(\chi)\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}

\displaystyle=\left(\sup_{s>0}s^{\frac{1}{r}}\int_{\underset{|h(\chi)|>s}{\chi\in\widehat{H}}}d\pi(\chi)\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{s>0}s\left(\int_{\underset{|h(\chi)|>s}{\chi\in\widehat{H}}}d\pi(\chi)\right)^{\frac{1}{r}},

proving Theorem
3.5
.
∎

4.
L^{p}
-
L^{q}
multipliers on Chébli-Trimèche hypergroups

4.1.
Interlude on Chébli-Trimèche hypergroups

An important and motivating example of a hypergroup is the algebra of finite radial measures on a noncompact rank one symmetric space under the usual convolution; as radial measures on a noncompact symmetric space can be viewed as measures on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}:=[0,\infty),
this endows
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
with a hypergroup structure with involution being the identity map. The hypergroup structures arising this way on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
is a particular case of general class of hypergroup structure on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
arising from the Strum-Liouville boundary value problems where the solutions coincide with the characters of the hypergroup in question
[
15
, Section 3.5]
.

In this paper, we are interested in a special class of “one dimensional hypergroups”
[
57
]
on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
called
Chébli-Trimèche hypergroups
with the convolution structure related to the following second-order differential operator

L=L_{A,x}:=-\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}-\frac{A^{\prime}(x)}{A(x)}\frac{d}{dx},

(22)

where the function
A,
called a
Chébli-Trimèche function
, is continuous on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+},
twice continuously differentiable on
{\mathbb{R}}^{*}_{+}:=(0,\infty)
and satisfies the following properties:

(i)

A(0)=0
and
A
is positive on
{\mathbb{R}}^{*}_{+}.

(ii)

A
is an increasing function and
A(x)\rightarrow\infty
as
x\rightarrow\infty.

(iii)

\frac{A^{\prime}}{A}
is a decreasing
C^{\infty}
- function on
{\mathbb{R}}^{*}_{+}
and hence
\rho:=\frac{1}{2}\lim\limits_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{A^{\prime}(x)}{A(x)}\geq 0
exists.

(iv)

\frac{A^{\prime}(x)}{A(x)}=\frac{2\alpha}{x}+B(x)
on a neighbourhood of
0
, where
\alpha>-\frac{1}{2}
and
B
is an odd
C^{\infty}
-function on
{\mathbb{R}}.

Now, we define the main object of this section, namely, the Chébli-Trimèche hypergroups. For a detailed study of these hypergroups one can refer to
[
14
,
51
,
50
,
49
,
19
,
20
,
23
]
.

Definition 4.1
.

[
15
,
16
,
57
]

A hypergroup
({\mathbb{R}}_{+},*)
is called a Chébli-Trimèche hypergroup if there exists a Chébli-Trimèche function
A
such that for any real-valued
C^{\infty}
-function
f
on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+},
i.e., the restriction of an even non-negative
C^{\infty}
-function on
{\mathbb{R}},
the generalised translation
u(x,y)=T_{x}f(y):=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(t)\,d(\delta_{x}*\delta_{y})(t),\,\,\,\,y\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
is the solution of the following Cauchy problem:

\displaystyle(L_{A,x}-L_{A,y})u(x,y)=0,

\displaystyle u(x,0)=f(x),\,\,\,u_{y}(x,0)=0,\,\,\,\,x>0.

The Chébli-Trimèche hypergroup associated with the Chébli-Trimèche function
A
will be denoted by
({\mathbb{R}}_{+},*(A)).
The growth of
({\mathbb{R}}_{+},*(A))
is determined by the number
\rho:=\frac{1}{2}\lim\limits_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{A^{\prime}(x)}{A(x)}.
We say that
({\mathbb{R}}_{+},*(A))
is of
exponential growth
if and only if
\rho>0.
Otherwise we say that the hypergroup is of
subexponential growth
which also includes the polynomial growth.

The class of Chébli-Trimèche hypergroups contains many important classes of hypergroups. We will discuss three of them here.

(i)

If
A(x):=x^{2\alpha+1}
with
2\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
and
\alpha>-\frac{1}{2}
then
L_{A,x}
is the radial part of the Laplace operator on the Euclidean space and
({\mathbb{R}}_{+},*(A))
is a Bessel-Kingman hypergroup.

(ii)

If
A(x):=(sinh\,x)^{2\alpha+1}(cosh\,x)^{2\beta+1}
with
2\alpha,2\beta\in\mathbb{N},\,

\alpha\geq\beta\geq-\frac{1}{2}
and
\alpha\neq-\frac{1}{2}
then
L_{A,x}
is the radial part of the Laplace-Beltrami operator on a noncompact Riemannian symmetric space of rank one (also of Damek-Ricci spaces) and
({\mathbb{R}}_{+},*(A))
is a Jacobi hypergroup (
[
9
,
14
]
).

(iii)

If
A
is the density function on the simply connected harmonic manifold
X
of purely exponential volume growth then
L_{A,x}
is the radial part of the Laplace-Beltrami operator on
X
and
({\mathbb{R}}_{+},*(A))
is the “radial hypergroup” of
X
(see
[
13
]
).

The Chébli-Trimèche hypergroup
({\mathbb{R}}_{+},*(A))
is a noncompact commutative hypergroup with the identity element
0
and involution as the identity map. The Haar measure
m
on
({\mathbb{R}}_{+},*(A))
is given by
m(x):=A(x)\,dx,
where
dx
is the usual Lebesgue measure on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}.
For any
x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+},
the probability measure
\delta_{x}*\delta_{y}
is absolutely continuous with respect to
m
and

\textnormal{supp}(\delta_{x}*\delta_{y})\subset[\,|x-y|,x+y].
For
1\leq p\leq\infty,
the Lebesgue space
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},m)
on
({\mathbb{R}}_{+},*(A))
is defined as usual; we denote by
\|f\|_{p,A}
the
L^{p}
-norm of
f\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},Adx).

For the Chébli-Trimèche hypergroup
({\mathbb{R}}_{+},*(A)),
the multiplicative functions on
({\mathbb{R}}_{+},*(A))
are given by the eigenfunctions of the operator
L:=L_{A,x}
defined in (
22
). For any
\lambda\in\mathbb{C},
the equation

Lu=(\lambda^{2}+\rho^{2})u

(23)

has a unique solution
\phi_{\lambda}
on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{*}
which extends continuously to
0
and satisfies
\phi_{\lambda}(0)=1.
We would like to point out here that since the coefficient
A^{\prime}/A
of
L
is singular at
x=0
as
A(0)=0,
the existence of a solution continuous at
0
is not immediate. Since (
23
) reads the same for
\lambda
and
-\lambda,
by uniqueness we have
\phi_{\lambda}=\phi_{-\lambda}.

The multiplicative functions, i.e., semicharacters, on
({\mathbb{R}}_{+},*(A))
are then exactly the functions
\phi_{\lambda},\,\,\lambda\in\mathbb{C}.
But a semicharacter
\phi_{\lambda}
is bounded, and therefore becomes a character, if and only if
|\text{Im}\lambda|\leq\rho.
Since the involution of
({\mathbb{R}}_{+},*(A))
is just the identity map it follows that the characters
\phi_{\lambda}
of the hypergroup
({\mathbb{R}}_{+},*(A))
are real-valued which is the case for every
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}\cup i{\mathbb{R}}.
Therefore, in view of above discussion, the dual space
\widehat{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+}
of
({\mathbb{R}}_{+},*(A))
is described by
\{\phi_{\lambda}:\lambda\in[0,\infty)\cup[0,i\rho]\}
which can be identified with the parameter set
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\cup[0,i\rho].

We define the Fourier transform
\widehat{f}
of
f\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},Adx)
at a point
\lambda\in\widehat{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+}
by

\widehat{f}(\lambda):=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(x)\,\phi_{\lambda}(x)\,A(x)dx.

It is worth noting that the above Fourier transform turns to be Jacobi transform
[
42
]
when the hypergroup arises from convolution of radial measures on a rank one symmetric space, or, equivalently,
A(x):=(sinh\,x)^{2\alpha+1}(cosh\,x)^{2\beta+1}
with
\alpha\geq\beta\geq-\frac{1}{2}
and
\alpha\neq-\frac{1}{2}.

The following theorem is the Levitan-Plancherel theorem for
({\mathbb{R}}_{+},*(A)).

Theorem 4.2
.

[
14
, Theorem 2.1]

There exists a unique non-negative measure
\pi
on
\widehat{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+}
with support
[0,\infty)
such that the Fourier transform induces an isometric isomorphism from
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},Adx)
onto
L^{2}(\widehat{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+},\pi)
and for any
f\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},Adx)\cap L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},Adx)
,

\int_{0}^{\infty}|f(x)|^{2}\,A(x)dx=\int_{\widehat{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+}}\,|\widehat{f}(\lambda)|^{2}\,d\pi(\lambda).

In addition to the above theorem, we also have the following identity known as Parseval’s identity. For
f_{1},f_{2}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)
we have

\int_{0}^{\infty}f_{1}(x)\,f_{1}(x)\,A(x)dx=\int_{\widehat{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+}}\widehat{f}_{1}(\lambda)\,\widehat{f}_{2}(\lambda)\,d\pi(\lambda).

Condition (P):
We say that a function
f
satisfies
Condition (P)
if for some
a>0,

f
can be expressed as

f(x)=\frac{a^{2}-\frac{1}{4}}{x^{2}}+\zeta(x)

for all large
x
, where

\int_{x_{0}}^{\infty}x^{\gamma(a)}|\zeta(x)|\,dx<\infty

for some
x_{0}>0
and
\zeta(x)
is bounded for
x>x_{0};
here
\gamma(a)=a+\frac{1}{2}
if
a\geq\frac{1}{2}
and
\gamma(a)=1
otherwise.

Now consider the function
G
defined by

G(x):=\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{A^{\prime}(x)}{A(x)}\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dx}\left(\frac{A^{\prime}(x)}{A(x)}\right)-\rho^{2}.

(24)

It is possible to determine the Plancherel measure
\pi
explicitly. In fact, Bloom and Xu
[
15
]
determined it by placing an extra growth condition on
A
described as follows:

Theorem 4.3
.

[
15
, Proposition 3.17]

If the function
G
defined above satisfies Condition (P) with any of the following conditions:

(i)

a>\frac{1}{2};

(ii)

a\neq|\alpha|,
where
\alpha
is the constant appearing in the definition of Chébli-Triméche function.

(iii)

a=\alpha\leq\frac{1}{2}
and
\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\frac{1}{2}-\alpha}\zeta(x)\phi_{0}(x)A(x)^{\frac{1}{2}}dx\neq-2\alpha\sqrt{M_{A}}
or

\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\frac{1}{2}+\alpha}\zeta(x)\phi_{0}(x)A(x)^{\frac{1}{2}}dx=0
where
M_{A}=\lim\limits_{x\rightarrow 0^{+}}x^{-2\alpha-1}A(x)
and

\zeta(x)=G(x)+(\frac{1}{4}-a^{2})/x^{2},

then the Plancherel measure
\pi
is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure and has density
|c(\lambda)|^{2},
where the function
c
satisfies the following: there exist positive constants
C_{1},C_{2}
and
K
such that for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
with
\textnormal{Im}(\lambda)\leq 0,

C_{1}|\lambda|^{a+\frac{1}{2}}\leq|c(\lambda)|^{-1}\leq C_{2}|\lambda|^{a+\frac{1}{2}}\,\,\,\,\,\text{for}\quad|\lambda|\leq K,

C_{1}|\lambda|^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}\leq|c(\lambda)|^{-1}\leq C_{2}|\lambda|^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}\,\,\,\,\,\text{for}\quad|\lambda|>K.

In the sequel we always assume that the function
A
(and so
G
) satisfies the Condition (P) of Theorem
4.3
for
\alpha\geq 0
. Therefore,
d\pi=C_{0}|c(\lambda)|^{-2}d\lambda,
where
C_{0}
is a positive constant and
c
is a certain complex function on
\mathbb{C}\backslash\{0\}.
We also assume that if case
\rho=0,

A
also satisfies
A(x)=O(x^{2\alpha+1}),
as
x\rightarrow\infty.

Note that the space of infinitely differentiable and compactly supported functions on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+},
denoted by,
C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
is the space of all even
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
restricted to
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}.

We have the following Fourier inversion formula, for
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}_{+}),

f(x)=C_{0}\int_{0}^{\infty}\widehat{f}(\lambda)\phi_{\lambda}(x)\,|c(\lambda)|^{-2}\,d\lambda.

For any
\lambda\in\mathbb{C},

\phi_{\lambda}
is an even
C^{\infty}
-function and
\lambda\mapsto\phi_{\lambda}(x)
is analytic. Also,
|\phi_{\lambda}(x)|\leq 1
for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+},\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
with
|\textnormal{Im}\,\lambda|\leq\rho.
For each
\lambda\in\mathbb{C},

\phi_{\lambda}
has a Laplace representation

\phi_{\lambda}(x)=\int_{-x}^{x}e^{(i\lambda-\rho)t}d\nu_{x}(t)\,\,\,\,\text{for}\,\,x\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+},

where
\nu_{x}
is a probability measure on
{\mathbb{R}}
supported in
[-x,x].
Further, if
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
then
|\phi_{\lambda}|\leq C(1+x)e^{(Im\,\lambda-\rho)x}.

4.2.
L^{p}
-
L^{q}
boundedness of multipliers on Chébli-Trimèche hypergroups

The following theorem is an analogue of the Paley inequality for Chébli-Trimèche hypergroups on half line.

Theorem 4.4
.

Suppose that
\psi
is a positive function on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
satisfying the condition

M_{\psi}:=\sup_{t>0}t\int_{\underset{\psi(\lambda)\geq t}{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}}|c(\lambda)|^{-2}\,d\lambda<\infty.

Then for
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx),

we have

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}|\widehat{f}(\lambda)|^{p}\,\psi(\lambda)^{2-p}|c(\lambda)|^{-2}\,d\lambda\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\lesssim M_{\psi}^{\frac{2-p}{p}}\,\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}.

The following Hausdorff-Young-Paley inequality holds for the Chébli-Trimèche hypergroups.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
1<p\leq 2,
and let
1<p\leq b\leq p^{\prime}\leq\infty,
where
p^{\prime}=\frac{p}{p-1}.
If
\psi(\lambda)
is a positive function on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that

M_{\psi}:=\sup_{t>0}t\int_{\underset{\psi(\lambda)\geq t}{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}}|c(\lambda)|^{-2}\,d\lambda

(25)

is finite, then for every
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)

we have

\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\left(|\widehat{f}(\lambda)|\psi(\lambda)^{\frac{1}{b}-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\right)^{b}|c(\lambda)|^{-2}\,d\lambda\right)^{\frac{1}{b}}\lesssim M_{\varphi}^{\frac{1}{b}-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}.

(26)

Next, we establish the following
L^{p}
-
L^{q}
boundedness result for Fourier multipliers on Chébli-Trimèche hypergroups.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
1<p\leq 2\leq q<\infty
and suppose that
T
is a Fourier multiplier with symbol
h.
Then we have

\|T\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)\rightarrow L^{q}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}\lesssim\sup_{s>0}s\left[\int_{\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}:|h(\lambda)|\geq s\}}|c(\lambda)|^{-2}\,d\lambda\right]^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}.

Remark 1
.

It is well-known that it is necessary for a Fourier multiplier
T_{h}
to be bounded from
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)
to
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx),
that the symbol
h
must be holomorphic in a strip in the complex plane (see
[
9
,
15
]
). It is evident from our theorem, it is no longer a necessary condition for
T_{h}
to bounded from
L^{p}
to
L^{q},
for
1<p\leq 2\leq q<\infty.

Now, we apply Theorem

\varphi\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},Adx),

The

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
1<p\leq 2\leq q<\infty
and let
\varphi
be a monotonically decreasing continuous function on
[\rho^{2},\infty)
such that
\lim_{u\rightarrow\infty}\varphi(u)=0.
Then we have

\|\varphi(L)\|_{\textnormal{op}}\lesssim\sup_{u>\rho^{2}}\varphi(u)\begin{cases}(u-\rho^{2})^{(a+1)(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q})}&\quad\textnormal{if}\quad(u-\rho^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq K,\\
\left[K^{2a+2}-K^{2\alpha+2}+(u-\rho^{2})^{(\alpha+1)}\right]^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}&\quad\textnormal{if}\quad(u-\rho^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}>K,\end{cases}

(27)

where
K
is a constant appearing in the estimate of the
c
-function and
\|\cdot\|_{\textnormal{op}}
denotes the operator norm from
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)
to
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx).

Proof.

Since
\varphi(L)
is a Fourier multiplier with the symbol
\varphi(\lambda^{2}+\rho^{2}),
as an application of Theorem
3.5
, we get

\displaystyle\|\varphi(L)\|_{\textnormal{op}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{s>0}s\left[\int_{\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}:\,\varphi(\lambda^{2}+\rho^{2})\geq s\}}|c(\lambda)|^{-2}\,d\lambda\right]^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{0<s<\varphi(\rho^{2})}s\left[\int_{\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}:\,\varphi(\lambda^{2}+\rho^{2})\geq s\}}|c(\lambda)|^{-2}\,d\lambda\right]^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}},

since
\varphi\leq\varphi(\rho^{2}).
Now, as
s\in(0,\varphi(\rho^{2})]
we can write
s=\varphi(u)
for some
u\in[\rho^{2},\infty)
and, therefore, we have

\displaystyle\|\varphi(L)\|_{\textnormal{op}}\lesssim\sup_{\varphi(u)<\varphi(\rho^{2})}\varphi(u)\left[\int_{\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}:\,\varphi(\lambda^{2}+\rho^{2})\geq\varphi(u)\}}|c(\lambda)|^{-2}\,d\lambda\right]^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}.

Since
\varphi
is monotonically decreasing we get

\displaystyle\|\varphi(L)\|_{\textnormal{op}}\lesssim\sup_{u>\rho^{2}}\varphi(u)\left[\int_{\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}:\,\lambda\leq(u-\rho^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\}}|c(\lambda)|^{-2}\,d\lambda\right]^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}.

Now, we use the estimate of the
c
-function (see Theorem
4.3
) to get

\displaystyle\|\varphi(L)\|_{\textnormal{op}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{u>\rho^{2}}\varphi(u)\begin{cases}\left[\int_{0}^{(u-\rho^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\lambda^{2a+1}\,d\lambda\right]^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}\quad&\textnormal{if}\quad(u-\rho^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq K,\\
\left[\int_{0}^{K}\lambda^{2a+1}d\lambda+\int_{K}^{(u-\rho^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\lambda^{2\alpha+1}\,d\lambda\right]^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}\quad&\textnormal{if}\quad(u-\rho^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}>K\end{cases}

\displaystyle=\sup_{u>\rho^{2}}\varphi(u)\begin{cases}(u-\rho^{2})^{(a+1)(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q})}&\quad\textnormal{if}\quad(u-\rho^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq K,\\
\left[K^{2a+2}-K^{2\alpha+2}+(u-\rho^{2})^{(\alpha+1)}\right]^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}&\quad\textnormal{if}\quad(u-\rho^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}>K,\end{cases}

proving Theorem
4.7
.
∎

4.3.1.
Heat equation

Let us consider the
L
-heat equation

\partial_{t}u+Lu=0,\quad u(0)=u_{0}.

(28)

One can verify that for each
t>0,

u(t,x)=e^{-tL}u_{0}
is a solution of initial value problem (
28
). To apply Theorem
4.7
we consider the function
\varphi(u)=e^{-tu}
which satisfies the condition of Theorem
4.7
and therefore we get

\|e^{-tL}\|_{\textnormal{op}}\leq\sup_{u>\rho^{2}}e^{-tu}\begin{cases}(u-\rho^{2})^{(a+1)(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q})}&\quad\textnormal{if}\quad(u-\rho^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq K,\\
\left[K^{2a+2}-K^{2\alpha+2}+(u-\rho^{2})^{(\alpha+1)}\right]^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}&\quad\textnormal{if}\quad(u-\rho^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}>K,\end{cases}

(29)

by setting
s=(u-\rho^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}
we get

\displaystyle\|e^{-tL}\|_{\textnormal{op}}\leq e^{-t\rho^{2}}\sup_{s>0}e^{-ts^{2}}\begin{cases}s^{2(a+1)(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q})}&\quad\textnormal{if}\quad s\leq K,\\
\left[K^{2a+2}-K^{2\alpha+2}+s^{2(\alpha+1)}\right]^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}&\quad\textnormal{if}\quad s>K,\end{cases}

(30)

Now, we will first calculate
e^{-t\rho^{2}}\,\,\sup_{s>0}e^{-ts^{2}}s^{\frac{2(a+1)}{r}}
where
\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}.
Consider the function

\phi(s)=e^{-ts^{2}}s^{\frac{2(a+1)}{r}}.

Then

\phi^{\prime}(s)=e^{-ts^{2}}\left(-2st+\frac{2(a+1)}{r}\right)s^{\frac{2(a+1)}{4}-1}.

So,
\phi
will be zero only at
s_{0}=\frac{(a+1)}{rt}
and derivative changes the sign from positive to negative at
s_{0}.
Thus,
s_{0}
is point of maximum of
\phi
if
s_{0}\leq K
, i.e.,
\frac{(a+1)}{rt}\leq K
implies that
t\geq\frac{(a+1)}{rK}.
Thus,

\displaystyle e^{-t\rho^{2}}\,\,\sup_{s>0}e^{-ts^{2}}s^{\frac{2(a+1)}{r}}=e^{-\frac{(a+1)^{2}}{r^{2}t}}\left(\frac{a+1}{rt}\right)^{\frac{2(a+1)}{r}}\leq C_{a,r}\,\,e^{-t\rho^{2}}\,\,e^{-\frac{(a+1)^{2}}{r^{2}t}}t^{\frac{-2(a+1)}{r}}.

(31)

Now, for
s>K,
consider the function

\psi(s)=e^{-ts^{2}}[K^{2a+2}-K^{2\alpha+2}+s^{2(\alpha+1)}]^{\frac{1}{r}}.
Then
\psi
attains its maximum at
s_{0}=\frac{(\alpha+1)}{rt}
if
s_{0}>K,
i.e.,
\frac{(\alpha+1)}{rt}>K
implies that
0<t<\frac{\alpha+1}{rK}.
So,

\displaystyle e^{-t\rho^{2}}\,\,\sup_{s>0}\,e^{-ts^{2}}[K^{2a+2}-K^{2\alpha+2}+s^{2(\alpha+1)}]^{\frac{1}{r}}

\displaystyle=e^{-t\rho^{2}}\,\,e^{-\frac{(\alpha+1)}{r^{2}t}}\left[K^{2a+2}-K^{2\alpha+2}+\left(\frac{(\alpha+1)}{rt}\right)^{2(\alpha+1)}\right]^{\frac{1}{r}}

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\frac{K}{r}}\left[K^{2a+2}-K^{2\alpha+2}+\left(\frac{(\alpha+1)}{rt}\right)^{2(\alpha+1)}\right]^{\frac{1}{r}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\alpha,r,K}\,t^{-\frac{2(\alpha+1)}{r}}.

(32)

Next, by putting the estimates from (
31
) and (
4.3.1
) in (
30
) we obtain

\displaystyle\|e^{-tL}\|_{\textnormal{op}}\lesssim\begin{cases}t^{-2(\alpha+1)(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q})}\quad&\text{if}\quad 0<t<\frac{\alpha+1}{K}\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right)\\
e^{-t\rho^{2}}\,\,e^{-\frac{(a+1)^{2}}{t}\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right)^{2}}t^{-2(a+1)(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q})}\quad&\text{if}\quad t\geq\frac{a+1}{K}\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right).\end{cases}

(33)

Therefore,

\displaystyle\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}

\displaystyle\leq\|e^{-tL}u_{0}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}\leq\|e^{-tL}\|_{\text{op}}\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}\begin{cases}t^{-2(\alpha+1)(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q})}&\text{if}\quad 0<t<\frac{\alpha+1}{K}\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right)\\
e^{-t\rho^{2}}\,\,e^{-\frac{(a+1)^{2}}{t}\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right)^{2}}t^{-2(a+1)(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q})}&\text{if}\quad t\geq\frac{a+1}{K}\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right).\end{cases}

(34)

4.3.2.
Sobolev type embedding

Let
1<p\leq 2\leq q<\infty.
As an application of Theorem
4.7
, we will obtain Sobolev type embedding theorem for operator
L.
Consider the function
\varphi(u)=(1+u)^{-b},

u>\rho^{2},
then
\varphi
satisfies the condition of Theorem

4.7
. Therefore, from Theorem
4.7
we have

\|(1+L)^{-b}\|_{\text{op}}\lesssim\sup_{u>\rho^{2}}(1+u)^{-b}\begin{cases}(u-\rho^{2})^{(a+1)(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q})}&\quad\textnormal{if}\quad(u-\rho^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq K,\\
\left[K^{2a+2}-K^{2\alpha+2}+(u-\rho^{2})^{(\alpha+1)}\right]^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}&\quad\textnormal{if}\quad(u-\rho^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}>K,\end{cases}

5.
Applications to nonlinear PDEs

In this section, we present applications of our main result on
L^{p}
-
L^{q}
boundedness of Fourier multipliers on Chébli-Triméche hypergroups to the well-posedness of abstract Cauchy problems on positive real line. Here, we adopt the techniques developed in
[
21
]
in the case of Fourier analysis associated to the biorthogonal eigenfunction expansion of a model operator having discrete spectrum.

5.1.
Nonlinear heat equation

Let us consider the following Cauchy problem for a nonlinear evolution equation in the space
L^{\infty}(0,T,L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)),

u_{t}-|Bu(t)|^{p}=0,\quad u(0)=u_{0},

(35)

where
B
is a linear operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)
and
1<p<\infty.

We say that the heat equation (
35
) admits a solution
u
if we have

u(t)=u_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}|Bu(\tau)|^{p}\,d\tau

(36)

in the space
L^{\infty}(0,T,L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx))
for every
T<\infty.

We say that
u
is a local solution of (
35
) if it satisfies equation (
36
) in the space
L^{\infty}(0,T^{*},L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx))
for some
T^{*}>0.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
1<p<\infty.
Suppose that
B
is a Fourier multiplier from
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)
into
L^{2p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)
such that its symbol
h
satisfies

\sup_{s>0}s\left[\int_{\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}:|h(\lambda)|\geq s\}}|c(\lambda)|^{-2}\,d\lambda\right]<\infty.

Then the Cauchy problem (
35
) has a local solution in the space
L^{\infty}(0,T^{*},L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx))
for some
T^{*}>0.

Proof.

By integrating equation (
35
) w.r.t.
t
one get

u(t)=u_{0}+\int\limits_{0}^{t}|Bu(\tau)|^{p}d\tau.

By taking the
L^{2}
-norm on both sides, one obtains

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\Bigg(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}^{2}+\left\|\int_{0}^{t}|Bu(t)|^{p}\,d\tau\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}\Bigg)

\displaystyle=C\Bigg(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\left|\int_{0}^{t}|Bu(t)|^{p}\,d\tau\right|^{2}A(x)\,dx\Bigg).

Using the inequality
\int_{0}^{t}|Bu(\tau)|^{p}\,d\tau\leq(\int_{0}^{t}1\,d\tau)^{\frac{1}{2}}(\int_{0}^{t}|Bu(\tau)|^{2p}\,d\tau)^{\frac{1}{2}}=t^{\frac{1}{2}}(\int_{0}^{t}|Bu(\tau)|^{2p}\,d\tau)^{\frac{1}{2}}
we get

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\Bigg(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}^{2}+t\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\int_{0}^{t}|Bu(t)|^{2p}\,d\tau\,A(x)\,dx\Bigg)

\displaystyle\leq C\Bigg(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},A\,dx)}^{2}+t\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}|Bu(t)|^{2p}\,A(x)\,dx\,d\tau\Bigg)

\displaystyle\leq C\Bigg(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}^{2}+t\int_{0}^{t}\|Bu(t)\|^{2p}_{L^{2p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}\,d\tau\Bigg).

Next, using the condition on the symbol it can be seen, as an application of Theorem
4.6
, that the operator
B
is a bounded operator, that is,
\|Bu(t)\|_{L^{2p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}\leq C_{1}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}
and, therefore, the above inequality yields

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},A\,dx)}^{2}\leq C\Bigg(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}^{2}+t\int_{0}^{t}\|u(t)\|^{2p}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}\,d\tau\Bigg),

(37)

for some constant
C
independent from
u_{0}
and
t
.

Finally, by taking
L^{\infty}
-norm in time on both sides of the estimate (
37
), one obtains

\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx))}^{2}\leq C\Big(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}^{2}+T^{2}\|u\|^{2p}_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx))}\Big).

(38)

Let us introduce the following set

S_{c}:=\left\{u\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)):\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx))}\leq c\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}\right\},

(39)

for some constant
c\geq 1
. Then, for
u\in S_{c}
we have

\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}^{2}+T^{2}\|u\|^{2p}_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx))}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}^{2}+T^{2}c^{2p}\|u_{0}\|^{2p}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}.

Finally, for
u
to be from the set
S_{c}
it is enough to have, by invoking (
38
), that

\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}^{2}+T^{2}c^{2p}\|u_{0}\|^{2p}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}\leq c^{2}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}^{2}.

It can be obtained by requiring the following,

T\leq T^{\ast}:=\frac{\sqrt{c^{2}-1}}{c^{p}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}}.

Thus, by applying the fixed point theorem, there exists a unique local solution
u\in L^{\infty}(0,T^{\ast};L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx))
of the Cauchy problem (
35
).
∎

5.2.
Nonlinear wave equation

In this subsection, we will consider that the initial value problem for the nonlinear wave equation

\displaystyle u_{tt}(t)-b(t)|Bu(t)|^{p}=0,

(40)

with the initial condition

u(0)=u_{0},\,\,\,u_{t}(0)=u_{1},

where
b
is a positive bounded function depending only on time,
B
is a linear operator in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)
and
1<p<\infty
. We intend to study the well-posedness of the equation (
40
).

We say that initial valued problem (
40
) admits a global solution
u
if it satisfies

u(t)=u_{0}+tu_{1}+\int\limits_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)b(\tau)|Bu(\tau)|^{p}d\tau

(41)

in the space
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx))
for every
T<\infty
.

We say that (
40
) admits a local solution
u
if it satisfies
the equation (
41
) in the space
L^{\infty}(0,T^{\ast};L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx))
for some
T^{\ast}>0
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
. Suppose that
B
is a Fourier multiplier from
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)
into
L^{2p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)
such that its symbol
h
satisfies

\sup_{s>0}s\left[\int_{\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}:|h(\lambda)|\geq s\}}|c(\lambda)|^{-2}\,d\lambda\right]<\infty.

(i)

If
\|b\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}<\infty
for some
T>0
then the Cauchy problem (
40
) has a local solution in
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx))
.

(ii)

Suppose that
u_{1}
is identically equal to zero. Let
\gamma>3/2
. Moreover, assume that
\|b\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}\leq c\,T^{-\gamma}
for every
T>0
, where
c
does not depend on
T
. Then, for every
T>0
, the Cauchy problem (
40
) has a global solution in the space
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx))
for sufficiently small
u_{0}
in
L^{2}
-norm.

Proof.

(i) By integrating the equation (
40
) two times in
t
one gets

u(t)=u_{0}+tu_{1}+\int\limits_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)b(\tau)|Bu(\tau)|^{p}d\tau.

By taking the
L^{2}
-norm on both sides, for
t<T
one obtains by simple calculation that

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}^{2}\leq C\left\{\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}^{2}+t^{2}\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}^{2}+\left\|\int\limits_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)b(\tau)|Bu(\tau)|^{p}d\tau\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\{\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}^{2}+t^{2}\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\Big|\int\limits_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)b(\tau)|Bu(\tau)|^{p}d\tau\Big|^{2}A(x)dx\right\}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\{\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}^{2}+t^{2}\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\Big(t\int\limits_{0}^{t}\Big|b(\tau)|Bu(\tau)|^{p}\Big|d\tau\Big)^{2}A(x)dx\right\}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\{\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}^{2}+t^{2}\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}t^{2}\int\limits_{0}^{t}\Big|b(\tau)\Big|^{2}d\tau\int\limits_{0}^{t}\Big|Bu(\tau)\Big|^{2p}d\tau A(x)dx\right\}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\{\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}^{2}+t^{2}\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}^{2}+t^{2}\|b\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\int\limits_{0}^{t}\Big|Bu(\tau)\Big|^{2p}d\tau A(x)dx\right\}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\{\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}^{2}+t^{2}\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}^{2}+t^{2}\|b\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}^{2}\int\limits_{0}^{t}\|Bu(\tau)\|^{2p}_{L^{2p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}d\tau\right\}.

Next, using the condition on the symbol it can be seen, as an application of Theorem
4.6
, that the operator
B
is a bounded operator, that is,
\|Bu(t)\|_{L^{2p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}\leq C_{1}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}
and, therefore, the above inequality yields

\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}^{2}\leq C(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}^{2}+t^{2}\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}^{2}+t^{2}\|b\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}^{2}\int\limits_{0}^{t}\|u(\tau)\|^{2p}_{L^{2p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}d\tau),

(42)

for some constant
C
not depending on
u_{0},u_{1}
and
t
. Finally, by taking the
L^{\infty}
-norm in time on both sides of the estimate (
42
), one obtains

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx))}^{2}\leq C(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}^{2}+T^{2}\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}^{2}+T^{3}\|b\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}^{2}\|u\|^{2p}_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx))}).

(43)

Let us introduce the set

Q_{c}:=\Big\{u\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)):\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx))}^{2}\leq c(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}^{2}+T^{2}\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}^{2})\Big\}

(44)

for some constant
c\geq 1
. Then, for
u\in Q_{c}
we have

\displaystyle\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}^{2}+T^{2}\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}^{2}+T^{3}\|b\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}^{2}\|u\|^{2p}_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx))}

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}^{2}+T^{2}\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}^{2}+T^{3}\|b\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}^{2}c^{p}\Big(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}^{2}+T^{2}\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}^{2}\Big)^{p}.

(45)

Observe that, to be
u
from the set
Q_{c}
it is enough to have, by invoking (
43
) and using (
5.2
), that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}^{2}+&T^{2}\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}^{2}+T^{3}\|b\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}^{2}c^{p}\Big(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}^{2}+T^{2}\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}^{2}\Big)^{p}\\
&\leq c(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}^{2}+T^{2}\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}^{2}).\end{split}

It can be obtained by requiring the following

T\leq T^{\ast}:=\min\left[\left(\frac{c-1}{\|b\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}^{2}c^{p}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}^{2p-2}}\right)^{1/3},\,\left(\frac{c-1}{\|b\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}^{2}c^{p}\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}^{2p-2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}\right].

Thus, by applying the fixed point theorem, there exists a unique local solution
u\in L^{\infty}(0,T^{\ast};L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx))
of the Cauchy problem (
40
).

To prove Part (ii), we repeat the arguments of the proof of Part (i) to get (
43
). Now, by taking into account assumptions on
u_{1}
and
b
inequality (
43
) yields

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx))}^{2}\leq C\Big(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}^{2}+T^{3-2\gamma}\|u\|^{2p}_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx))}\Big).

(46)

For a fixed constant
c\geq 1
, let us introduce the set

R_{c}:=\Big\{u\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)):\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx))}^{2}\leq cT^{\gamma_{0}}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}^{2}\Big\},

with
\gamma_{0}>0
is to be defined later. Now, note that

\displaystyle\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}^{2}+T^{3-2\gamma}\|u\|^{2p}_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx))}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}^{2}+T^{3-2\gamma+\gamma_{0}p}c^{p}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}^{2p}.

To guarantee
u\in R_{c}
, by invoking (
46
) we require that

\displaystyle\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}^{2}+T^{3-2\gamma+\gamma_{0}p}c^{p}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}^{2p}\leq cT^{\gamma_{0}}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}^{2}.

Now by choosing
0<\gamma_{0}<\frac{2\gamma-3}{p}
such that

\tilde{\gamma}:=3-2\gamma+\gamma_{0}p<0,
we obtain

c^{p}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}^{2p-2}\leq cT^{-\tilde{\gamma}+\gamma_{0}}.

From the last estimate, we conclude that for any
T>0
there exists sufficiently small
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},Adx)}
such that IVP (
40
) has a solution. It proves Part (ii) of Theorem
5.2
.

1.
Introduction: A bit of history

It was just fifty years ago at the Vancouver ICM that Enrico Bombieri received
the Fields Medal. This sweet anniversary provokes us to refresh the memory of some of Enrico’s early achievements in Analytic Number Theory. We choose to write about the Large Sieve Inequalities for Dirichlet characters, in part because they are fundamental to the theory of
L
-functions and in part because Enrico’s role was so central to their development.

The modern version of the LSI asserts that

(1.1)

\sum_{q\leqslant Q}\frac{q}{\varphi(q)}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\chi(\mathop{\rm{mod}}q)}\bigl|\sum_{M<n\leqslant M+N}a_{n}\chi(n)\bigr|^{2}\leqslant(N+Q^{2})\sum_{M<n\leqslant M+N}|a_{n}|^{2},

the asterisk restricting the summation to primitive characters
\chi(\mathop{\rm{mod}}q)
. This holds for all complex numbers
a_{n}
supported on any interval of length
N\geqslant 1
. In numerous applications to prime numbers the
LSI serves as a substitute for the Riemann Hypothesis for
L(s,\chi)
. In fact, the LSI is found to be quite robust. Using (
1.1
), one can recover territories beyond the threshold of the GRH.

The first ideas of the Large Sieve appear in the short paper of Ju. V. Linnik
[Li] followed by that of A. Rényi [Re]. Great innovations began in the 1960’s with the
works of K. Roth [Ro] and E. Bombieri [B1]. Various conceptual interpretations
of the LSI have been gradually revealed, the common ground of all lying in the
super-orthogonality of primitive characters, the pseudo-characters of Selberg
[S1] included.

Many results, with
N+Q^{2}
in (
1.1
) replaced by
c_{1}N+c_{2}Q^{2}
or by

\max(c_{1}N,c_{2}Q^{2})
for specific constants
c_{1},c_{2}
, have been produced by
different authors, among them Davenport, Halberstam, Gallagher and Elliott. Ultimately,
the inequality (
1.1
), best possible in general, was established independently by Montgomery and Vaughan [MV] and by Selberg [S2] by different arguments.

For our considerations in this work, especially in Section 3, the value of
c_{1}=1
is crucial while that of
c_{2}
is less important. The first LSI of that strength in the literature is
the following.

Theorem 1.1:
(Bombieri-Davenport 1969)
For all complex numbers
a_{n}

supported on an interval of length
N\geqslant 1
we have

(1.2)

\sum_{q\leqslant Q}\frac{q}{\varphi(q)}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\chi(\mathop{\rm{mod}}q)}\bigl|\sum_{n}a_{n}\chi(n)\bigr|^{2}\leqslant({\sqrt{N}}+Q)^{2}\sum_{n}|a_{n}|^{2}.

Because, in these notes,
Q
will be much smaller than
\sqrt{N}
, the BD
inequality (
1.2
) is, for our purposes, as good as the MV-S inequality (
1.1
). They are essentially sharp in general, however for special coefficients
a_{n}
some improvements are possible. We have

Theorem 1.2:
(Bombieri-Davenport 1969)
Let
\mathcal{Q}
be a set of positive integers
\leqslant Q
which have no prime divisors in a set
\mathcal{P}
. Then

(1.3)

\sum_{q\in\mathcal{Q}}\sum_{\chi(\mathop{\rm{mod}}q)}\frac{|\tau(\chi)|^{2}}{\varphi(q)}\bigl|\sum_{n}a_{n}\chi(n)\bigr|^{2}\leqslant({\sqrt{N}}+Q)^{2}\sum_{n}|a_{n}|^{2}

for all complex numbers
a_{n}
supported on an interval of length
N\geqslant 1
with
n
being free of prime divisors in
\mathcal{P}
.

Note that
\chi
in (
1.3
) ranges over all characters in
\mathcal{Q}
not only the
primitive characters as in (
1.2
). However, every
\chi(\mathop{\rm{mod}}q)

with
1\leqslant q\leqslant Q
is induced by a unique primitive character of conductor
q_{1}
with

q=q_{1}r\leqslant Q
. Taking all these characters into account one obtains

(1.4)

\mathop{\sum\!\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}qr\leqslant Q\\
(q,r)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{q\mu^{2}(r)}{\varphi(qr)}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\chi(\mathop{\rm{mod}}q)}|\sum_{n}a_{n}\chi(n)|^{2}\leqslant({\sqrt{N}}+Q)^{2}\sum_{n}|a_{n}|^{2}

for all complex numbers
a_{n}
supported on an interval of length
N\geqslant 1
with

n
being free of prime divisors
\leqslant Q
. Because

(1.5)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\leqslant X\\
(r,q)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu^{2}(r)}{\varphi(r)}\geqslant\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\log X,\quad{\rm if}\quad X\geqslant 1,

we have

(1.6)

\sum_{q\leqslant Q}\bigl(\log\frac{Q}{q}\bigr)\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\chi(\mathop{\rm{mod}}q)}\bigl|\sum_{n}a_{n}\chi(n)\bigr|^{2}\leqslant({\sqrt{N}}+Q)^{2}\sum_{n}|a_{n}|^{2}.

See the arguments on page 21 of [BD] and a slightly stronger inequality in Theorem 8 of [B2], wherein the right hand side is the same as the right hand side of (
1.1
).

Discarding all but the principal character in (
1.6
)
and taking
a_{p}=1
for
M<p\leqslant M+N
and zero elsewhere, Bombieri and Davenport show that (choose

Q=\sqrt{N}/\log N
)

\pi(M+N)-\pi(M)\leqslant\frac{2N}{\log N}\biggl(1+O\bigl(\frac{\log\log N}{\log N}\bigl)\biggl),

if
M>\sqrt{N}
. Recall that the linear sieve (whether Selberg or beta) produces the same interesting constant 2 and similar error terms.

Our task in these notes is to exploit further the Bombieri-Davenport large sieve arguments, first in Section 2 for coefficients which have restricted support. Then, in Section 3, we show how an exceptional real character interacts with all of the others.
Our point of departure for these results is the following specific
inequality which occurs with a nice proof as Theorem 7A in [B2].

Theorem 1.3:

For all complex numbers
a_{n}
supported on
an interval of length
N\geqslant 1
we have

(1.7)

\mathop{\sum\!\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}qr\leqslant Q\\
(q,r)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{q}{\varphi(qr)}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\chi(\mathop{\rm{mod}}q)}|\sum_{n}a_{n}\chi(n)c_{r}(n)|^{2}\leqslant(N+Q^{2})\sum_{n}|a_{n}|^{2},

where
c_{r}(n)
is the Ramanujan sum

(1.8)

c_{r}(n)=\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{u(\mathop{\rm{mod}}r)}e(un/r)=\sum_{d|(n,r)}d\mu(r/d).

2.
The Large Sieve of Restricted Support

Our first observation is that (
1.7
) implies the following.

Proposition 2.1:

Let
\mathcal{R}
be a set of numbers
r\leqslant R
. Put

(2.1)

\mathcal{L}_{q}(\mathcal{R})=\frac{q}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\in\mathcal{R}\\
(r,q)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu^{2}(r)}{\varphi(r)},

(2.2)

\mathcal{L}(Q,R)=\min_{q\leqslant Q}\mathcal{L}_{q}(\mathcal{R}).

Then

(2.3)

\sum_{q\leqslant Q}\,\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\chi(\mathop{\rm{mod}}q)}|\sum_{M<n\leqslant M+N}a_{n}\chi(n)|^{2}\leqslant\frac{Q^{2}R^{2}+N}{\mathcal{L}(Q,R)}\sum_{M<n\leqslant M+N}|a_{n}|^{2}

for sequences
a_{n}
having
(n,r)=1
for every
r\in\mathcal{R}
.

Proof:

This follows from the fact that
c_{r}(n)=\mu(r)
in this case.

Example:

Taking
\mathcal{R}
to be the set of all numbers
r\leqslant R
with
R\geqslant 2
, we get

(2.4)

\mathcal{L}(Q,R)\geqslant\log R.

The number of primitive characters
\chi\mathop{\rm{mod}}q
with
q\leqslant Q
is about
Q^{2}

so we have lost the factor
R^{2}
in the first term of
Q^{2}+N
in order to gain

\log R
in both terms. Although, at first glance this seems wasteful, it
could be only a minor complication in some important applications when the
character sums
\sum a_{n}\chi(n)
are quite a bit longer than
Q^{2}
.

The previous example covers the situation where the
a_{n}
are supported
on the primes in an interval. With more work and only half the success we
consider what might be regarded the next natural example.

Proposition 2.2:

Let
r(n)
denote the number of representations of
n
as the sum of two co-prime squares. We have

(2.5)

\sum_{q\leqslant Q}\,\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\chi(\mathop{\rm{mod}}q)}|\sum_{M<n\leqslant M+N}r(n)a_{n}\chi(n)|^{2}\leqslant\frac{2N}{\sqrt{\log N/Q^{2}}}\sum_{M<n\leqslant M+N}r^{2}(n)|a_{n}|^{2}

if
N\geqslant Q^{2}\exp(\alpha\log\log Q)^{3}
, where
\alpha
is an
absolute constant.

To prove (
2.5
) using Proposition 2.1 we want to show that

\mathcal{L}(Q,R)\gg(\log R)^{1/2}

for the relevant choice of
\mathcal{R}
.
In preparation, we are first going to evaluate asymptotically

(2.6)

S_{q}(x)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leqslant x\\
(n,q)=1\end{subarray}}\nu(n)\tau(n)n^{-1},

where
\nu(n)=1
if
n
is the product of distinct primes
\equiv 3(\mathop{\rm{mod}}4)

and
\nu(n)=0
otherwise.

Lemma 2.1:

For every
x\geqslant 1
we have

(2.7)

S_{q}(x)=c\prod_{p|q_{3}}\bigl(1+\frac{2}{p}\bigr)^{-1}\log x+O\bigl((1+\sum_{p|q_{3}}p^{-1}\log p)\prod_{p|q_{3}}(1+\frac{2}{p})\bigr),

where the implied constant is absolute, where
q_{3}
denotes the product of all distinct prime divisors of
q
which are

\equiv 3(\mathop{\rm{mod}}4)
and

(2.8)

c=\frac{3}{\pi}\prod_{p\equiv 3(\mathop{\rm{mod}}4)}\bigl(1-\frac{2}{p(p+1)}\bigr).

Proof:
We start from the generating Dirichlet series

(2.9)

Z_{q}(s)=\sum_{(n,q)=1}\frac{\nu(n)\tau(n)}{n^{s}}=\prod_{p\nmid q_{3}}\bigl(1+\frac{1-\chi_{4}(p)}{p^{s}}\bigr),

where
\chi_{4}
is the character of conductor 4. For
q=1
this yields

(2.10)

\displaystyle Z(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{\zeta(s)}{L(s,\chi_{4})}\prod_{p}\bigl(1-\frac{1}{p^{s}}\bigr)\bigl(1-\frac{\chi_{4}(p)}{p^{s}}\bigr)^{-1}\bigl(1+\frac{1-\chi_{4}(p)}{p^{s}}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\frac{\zeta(s)}{L(s,\chi_{4})}\bigl(1-\frac{1}{4^{s}}\bigr)\prod_{p\equiv 3(\mathop{\rm{mod}}4)}\bigl(1+\frac{1}{p^{s}}-\frac{2}{p^{2s}}\bigr)\bigl(1+\frac{1}{p^{s}}\bigr)^{-1}.

Here the product over
p\equiv 3(\mathop{\rm{mod}}4)
converges absolutely in
{\rm Re}(s)>\frac{1}{2}
. Using the zero-free region for
L(s,\chi_{4})
(or elementary methods) we obtain

(2.11)

\sum_{n\leqslant x}\nu(n)\tau(n)=cx+O\bigl(x(\log x)^{-2}\bigr).

Note that
c
is the residue of
Z(s)
at
s=1
and
L(1,\chi_{4})=\pi/4

(Leibniz formula). Hence, by partial summation we get

(2.12)

S(x)=\sum_{n\leqslant x}\nu(n)\tau(n)n^{-1}=c\log x+O(1)

which is the case of (
2.7
) for
q=1
. In general we write

(2.13)

Z_{q}(s)=Z(s)F_{q}(s)

where

F_{q}(s)=\prod_{p|q_{3}}\bigl(1+\frac{2}{p^{s}}\bigr)^{-1}=\sum_{a=1}^{\infty}\frac{f(a)}{a^{s}}

and
f
is multiplicative with
f(p^{\alpha})=(-2)^{\alpha}
if
p|q_{3}

and
f(p^{\alpha})=0
if
p\nmid q_{3}
. Therefore, the coefficients of

Z_{q}(s)
are the convolution of the coefficients of
Z(s)
with those of

F_{q}(s)
. Hence our sum (
2.6
) is equal to (see (
2.12
))

\displaystyle S_{q}(x)

\displaystyle=\mathop{\sum\!\sum}_{am\leqslant x}f(a)\nu(m)\tau(m)(am)^{-1}=\sum_{a\leqslant x}\frac{f(a)}{a}\bigl(c\log\frac{x}{a}+O(1)\bigr)

\displaystyle=c\bigl(\sum_{a}\frac{f(a)}{a}\bigr)\log x+O\bigl(\sum_{a}\frac{|f(a)|}{a}\log 2a\bigr).

The first sum above is equal to
F_{q}(1)
which appears in (
2.7
). The
second sum is bounded up to a constant by

\sum_{a}\frac{|f(a)|}{a}\bigl(1+\sum_{p|a}\log p\bigr)\ll\bigl(1+\sum_{p|q_{3}}\frac{\log p}{p}\bigr)\prod_{p|q_{3}}\bigl(1+\frac{2}{p}\bigr).

This completes the proof of Lemma 2.1

Estimating the error terms in Lemma 2.1 as follows:

\sum_{p|q_{3}}p^{-1}\log p\ll\sum_{p<2\log q}p^{-1}\log p\ll\log\log 3q,

\prod_{p|q_{3}}\bigl(1+\frac{2}{p}\bigr)^{2}\ll\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p<2\log q\\
p\equiv 3(\mathop{\rm{mod}}4)\end{subarray}}\bigl(1+\frac{1}{p}\bigr)^{4}\ll(\log\log 3q)^{2},

we may write (
2.7
) in the form

(2.14)

S_{q}(x)=c\prod_{p|q_{3}}\bigl(1+\frac{2}{p}\bigr)^{-1}\bigl(\log x+O((\log\log 3q)^{3})\bigr).

Hence

(2.15)

S_{q}(x)\gg\prod_{p|q_{3}}\bigl(1+\frac{2}{p}\bigr)^{-1}\log x

if

(2.16)

x\geqslant\exp(\alpha\log\log 3q)^{3},

with some absolute constant
\alpha\geqslant 1
. Next, we remove
the divisor function
\tau(n)
in (
2.6
) and estimate

(2.17)

T_{q}(x)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leqslant x\\
(n,q)=1\end{subarray}}\nu(n)n^{-1}.

Clearly
T_{q}(x)^{2}\geqslant S_{q}(x)
, so (
2.15
) yields

(2.18)

T_{q}(x)\gg\prod_{p|q_{3}}\bigl(1+\frac{1}{p}\bigr)^{-1}(\log x)^{1/2}

if
x
satisfies (
2.16
). This implies that (
2.2
)
satisfies

(2.19)

\mathcal{L}(Q,R)\gg(\log R)^{1/2}

with
R=\sqrt{N}Q^{-1}
, provided that
N>Q^{2}\exp(\alpha\log\log Q)^{3}
.
This completes the proof of Proposition 2.2.

Next, we consider coefficients
a_{n}
with
a_{n}\sqrt{n}
small whenever
n
has a relatively small prime divisor. One may think of
n
as being almost a prime.

Theorem 2.1

Suppose that
a_{n}
with
M<n<M+N
satisfy

(2.20)

\sum_{n}|a_{n}|^{2}\leqslant 1,\quad\sum_{n\equiv 0(\mathop{\rm{mod}}p)}|a_{n}|^{2}\leqslant\frac{1}{p}\bigl(\frac{\log p}{\log N}\bigr)^{2}.

We have

(2.21)

\sum_{q\leqslant Q}\,\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\chi(\mathop{\rm{mod}}q)}|\sum_{M<n\leqslant M+N}a_{n}\chi(n)|^{2}\leqslant 24N(\log N/Q^{2})^{-1}

if

8Q^{2}\leqslant N
.

Proof:
Let
2\leqslant R,\,Q^{2}R^{3}<N
. For the character sums
\sum a_{n}\chi(n)
running over
n
having no prime divisors
\leqslant R
we apply (
2.3
)
and (
2.4
), obtaining the bound

S_{1}=\frac{2N}{\log R}\sum_{n}|a_{n}|^{2}\leqslant\frac{2N}{\log R}.

Then we estimate the character sums
\sum a_{n}\chi(n)
over
n
having a prime divisor
\leqslant R
by

\sum_{p\leqslant R}\bigl|\sum_{(n,P(p))=1}a_{np}\chi(n)\bigr|,

where
P(z)
denotes the product of all primes
<z
.
This part of the sum (
2.21
) is bounded by

S_{2}=\sum_{p_{1}}\sum_{p_{2}}S(p_{1},p_{2})
with
p_{1},p_{2}\leqslant R
and

\displaystyle S(p_{1},p_{2})=\sum_{q\leqslant Q}\,\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\chi(\mathop{\rm{mod}}q)}|\sum_{(n,P(p_{1}))=1}a_{np_{1}}\chi(n)||\sum_{(n,P(p_{2}))=1}a_{np_{2}}\chi(n)|

\displaystyle\leqslant S(p_{1},p_{1})^{1/2}S(p_{2},p_{2})^{1/2}.

Applying (
2.3
) we get

S(p,p)\leqslant\frac{Q^{2}p^{2}+N/p}{\log p}\sum_{n}|a_{np}|^{2}\leqslant\frac{2N\log p}{(p\log N)^{2}}.

Hence,

S_{2}\leqslant\frac{2N}{(\log N)^{2}}\bigl(\sum_{p\leqslant R}\frac{1}{p}\sqrt{\log p}\bigr)^{2}\leqslant 18N(\log N)^{-2}\log R\leqslant 2N(\log R)^{-1}.

This completes the proof of Theorem 2.1 on choosing
R=(N/Q^{2})^{1/3}
.

Remark:

We could assume the somewhat stronger bound

(2.22)

a_{n}\ll\frac{\rho(n)}{\sqrt{n}},\quad 1\leqslant n\leqslant N

with

(2.23)

\rho(n)\ =\prod_{p|n}\rho(p),\quad\rho(p)=\frac{\log p}{\log N}.

These coefficients satisfy (
2.20
) up to a constant. Indeed, we have

\sum_{n\leqslant N}\frac{\rho(n)^{2}}{n}\ll\prod_{p\leqslant N}\bigl(1+\frac{\rho(p)^{2}}{p}\bigr)\leqslant\exp\biggl(\sum_{p\leqslant N}\frac{\rho(p)^{2}}{p}\biggr),

\sum_{p\leqslant N}\frac{\rho(p)^{2}}{p}\ll\sum_{p\leqslant N}\frac{1}{p}\biggl(\frac{\log p}{\log N}\biggr)^{2}\ll 1.

The above estimates verify both conditions in (
2.20
).

Example:
The coefficients

a_{n}=n^{-1/2}\Lambda_{k}(n)(\log N)^{-k},\quad{\rm with}\quad 1\leqslant n\leqslant N,

satisfy (
2.20
). Here
\Lambda_{k}
is the generalized von-Mangoldt
function
\Lambda_{k}=\mu*\log^{k}
. Apply induction in
k
using

\Lambda_{k+1}=\Lambda_{k}\cdot\log+\Lambda_{k}*\Lambda
.

3.
The Large Sieve with an Exceptional Character

Recall that taking only the one term of (
1.6
) coming from the principal character over primes in a segment Bombieri and Davenport obtained a strong Brun-Titchmarsh upper bound for the primes in an interval.
One may additionally extract the contribution of a second real character, say
\chi_{D}
of conductor
D
, which is

\bigl(\log\frac{Q}{D}\bigr)\biggl(\sum_{M<p\leqslant M+N}\chi_{D}(p)\biggr)^{2}.

In this section we study some of the effects of doing so.

We put
\lambda(p)=1+\chi_{D}(p)
and write

\bigl(\sum_{p}\chi_{D}(p)\bigr)^{2}=\bigl(-\sum_{p}1+\sum_{p}\lambda(p)\bigr)^{2}\geqslant\bigl(\sum_{p}1\bigr)^{2}-2\bigl(\sum_{p}1\bigr)\bigl(\sum_{p}\lambda(p)\bigr).

Hence, Theorem 8 of [B2] yields

(3.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{1<q\leqslant Q}(\log\frac{Q}{q})

\displaystyle\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\mathop{\rm{mod}}q\\
\chi\neq\chi_{D}\end{subarray}}|\sum_{p}\chi(p)|^{2}\leqslant(Q^{2}+N)(\sum_{p}1)

\displaystyle-(\log\frac{Q^{2}}{D})(\sum_{p}1)^{2}+2(\log\frac{Q}{D})(\sum_{p}1)(\sum_{p}\lambda(p)).

Note that the last part of (
3.1
) is negligible if
\chi_{D}(p)=-1
very frequently. We are going to use the above arrangement for the coefficients
in (
1.6
) given by

(3.2)

a_{n}=\Lambda(n)f(n/N)

where
f(u)
is a nice function supported on
0\leqslant u\leqslant 1
and with
0\leqslant f(u)\leqslant 1
. Let
3\leqslant Q\leqslant\sqrt{N}
. We get

(3.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{q\leqslant Q}(\log\frac{Q}{q})\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\chi(\mathop{\rm{mod}}q)}

\displaystyle|\sum_{n}\chi(n)\Lambda(n)f(n/N)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant(Q^{2}+N)\sum_{n}|a_{n}|^{2}+O\bigl(N^{7/4}\log N\bigr),

where the error term on the right side comes from an estimation of the contribution of prime powers
p^{2},p^{3},\ldots\leqslant N
and primes
p\leqslant Q
.

For
q=1
we have the principal character whose contribution is

(3.4)

(\log Q)(\sum_{n}a_{n})^{2}.

Let
\chi_{D}
be a primitive real character of conductor
D\leqslant Q
. Its contribution is

(3.5)

\bigl(\log\frac{Q}{D}\bigr)\bigl(\sum_{n}\chi_{D}(n)a_{n}\bigr)^{2}.

Put
\lambda=1*\chi_{D}
so
\chi_{D}=\mu*\lambda
. Note that

\lambda(p)=1+\chi_{D}(p)
. We think of
\lambda
as being lacunary. We write

(3.6)

\sum_{n}\chi_{D}(n)a_{n}=-\sum_{n}a_{n}+\sum_{n}\rho(n)a_{n}

where
\rho(n)=1+\chi_{D}(n)
. Hence

(3.7)

(\sum_{n}\chi_{D}(n)a_{n})^{2}\geqslant(\sum_{n}a_{n})^{2}-2(\sum_{n}a_{n})(\sum_{n}\rho(n)a_{n}).

Summing up the contributions of
\chi_{0},\chi_{D}
, we obtain from (
3.3
)

(3.8)

\displaystyle\sum_{1<q\leqslant Q}(\log\frac{Q}{q})

\displaystyle\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi(\mathop{\rm{mod}}q)\\
\chi\neq\chi_{D}\end{subarray}}|\sum_{n}\chi(n)\Lambda(n)f(n/N)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant(Q^{2}+N)\sum_{n}a_{n}^{2}-\bigl(\log\frac{Q^{2}}{D}\bigr)(\sum_{n}a_{n})^{2}

\displaystyle+2\bigl(\log\frac{Q}{D}\bigr)(\sum_{n}a_{n})(\sum_{n}\lambda(n)a_{n})+O\bigl(N^{7/4}\log N\bigr).

Note that we have here replaced
\rho(n)
by
\lambda(n)
by estimating trivially the terms at higher prime powers. Next, we estimate as follows.

\displaystyle\sum_{n}\lambda(n)a_{n}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{n\leqslant N}\lambda(n)\log N

\displaystyle=L(1,\chi_{D})N\log N+O(N^{1/2}D^{1/4}\log N),

where
L(1,\chi_{D})
is the residue of
\zeta(s)L(s,\chi_{D})

at
s=1
.

Moreover, we have
\sum_{n}a_{n}\ll N
, but we need
more precise estimations for the leading terms.
Using the prime number theorem we get

(3.9)

\sum_{n}a_{n}=A_{1}N+O(N/\log N),\quad A_{1}=\int_{0}^{1}f(u)du;

(3.10)

\sum_{n}a_{n}^{2}=A_{2}N\log N+O(N),\quad A_{2}=\int_{0}^{1}f^{2}(u)du.

Introducing these estimates into (
3.8
) and choosing
Q={\sqrt{N}}/\log N
, we obtain the following.

Lemma 3.1:

Let
3\leqslant D\leqslant Q={\sqrt{N}}/\log N
. We have

(3.11)

\displaystyle\sum_{1<q\leqslant Q}(\log\frac{Q}{q})

\displaystyle\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi(\mathop{\rm{mod}}q)\\
\chi\neq\chi_{D}\end{subarray}}|\sum_{n}\chi(n)\Lambda(n)f(n/N)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant(A_{2}\log N-A_{1}^{2}\log N/D)N^{2}

\displaystyle+2A_{1}(\log Q/D)N^{2}L(1,\chi_{D})\log N+O(N^{2}).

Choosing
f(u)=1
we find
A_{1}=A_{2}=1
. Hence,

Proposition 3.1:

Let
3\leqslant D\leqslant Q={\sqrt{N}}/\log N
. We have

(3.12)

\displaystyle\sum_{1<q\leqslant Q}(\log\frac{Q}{q})

\displaystyle\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi(\mathop{\rm{mod}}q)\\
\chi\neq\chi_{D}\end{subarray}}|\sum_{n}\chi(n)\Lambda(n)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant N^{2}\bigl(\log cD+L(1,\chi_{D})(\log N)^{2}\bigr),

where
c\geqslant 1
is an absolute constant.

Proposition 3.2:

Let
D
be larger than a suitable absolute constant. Suppose

(3.13)

L(1,\chi_{D})\log D\leqslant{\varepsilon}^{5}.

Then, for every primitive character
\chi(\mathop{\rm{mod}}q),\chi\neq\chi_{0},\chi_{D}

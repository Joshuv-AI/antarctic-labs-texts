Abstract

1.
introduction

1.1.
Deformation rigidity for projective manifolds

Let
\varphi\colon X\to B
be a proper smooth morphism between complex manifolds, then the fibers
X_{t}=\varphi^{-1}(t)
are compact complex manifolds for all
t\in B
. We call such
\varphi
a
smooth family
of compact complex manifolds. On deformation rigidity of compact complex manifolds, the study of the following problem dates at least from Bott
[
9
]
and Kodaira-Spencer
[
25
]
.

Question 1.1
.

Let
\pi\colon X\to\Delta=\{t\in\mathbb{C}||t|<1\}
be a smooth family of compact complex manifolds. Suppose that all the fibers
X_{t}
with
t\in\Delta^{*}=\Delta\setminus\{0\}
are biholomorphic to a given compact complex manifold
S
, when is the central fiber
X_{0}
also biholomorphic to
S
, that is,
X_{0}\cong S
?

We are interested in the special case that
S
is a projective manifold. From the point of view of algebraic geometry, projective manifolds
S
can be coarsely classified by the Kodaira dimension
\kappa(S)
. For projective manifolds with negative Kodaira dimension, there are many results on the deformation rigidity. First of all, it is well-known that the deformation of Hirzebruch surfaces is not rigid.
For the affirmative results, Siu
[
39
]
, Hwang and Mok
[
18
,
19
,
20
]
made a lot of important contributions. For example, they obtained the rigidty for rational homogeneous spaces
S
with the second Betti number
b_{2}(S)=1
, under the assumption that the morphism
\pi
is a Kähler morphism. In the following context, we always assume that the morphism
\pi
in Question
1.1
is Kähler.

The algebraic version of the deformation rigidity was studied by Matsusaka and Mumford in 1964, although they studied the deformation rigidity for smooth polarized varieties. A
polarized variety
is a pair
(S,{\cal H})
, where
S
is a projective variety and
{\cal H}
is an ample line bundle on
S
. Assume that all fibers of
\pi\colon X\to\Delta
are smooth projective polarized varieties, and all the fibers over
\Delta^{*}
are isomorphic as polarized varieties. Then the first fundamental theorem (Theorem 2) of
[
32
]
shows that all the fibers of
\pi
are isomorphic if the generic fiber of
\pi
is non-ruled.

For projective manifolds with low Kodaira dimension, the first author
[
31
]
provided some results on the deformation rigidity. Precisely, if
S
is an abelian variety or a principal bundle of abelian varieties over a curve
C
with genus
g(C)>1
, the first author proved in
[
31
, Theorem 1.2]
that the above deformation rigidity also holds (i.e.,
X_{0}\cong S
). In this result, one has that
0\leq\kappa(S)\leq 1
.

But, as far as the authors know, there are seldom study on the deformation rigidity Question
1.1
for projective manifolds with high Kodaira dimension, which is the aim of this paper. We consider the deformation rigidity for projective manifolds
S
with semiample canonical line bundles. In this case, we have that
\kappa(S)\geq 0
. Our main result is as follows.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
X
be a complex manifold,
\pi\colon X\to\Delta=\{t\in\mathbb{C}||t|<1\}
be a proper smooth Kähler morphism, and
S
be a projective manifold with semiample canonical line bundle. If all the fibers
X_{t}
with
t\in\Delta^{*}
are biholomorphic to
S
, then the central fiber
X_{0}
is also biholomorphic to
S
.

Firstly, we want to compare this result with some known results.

On the one hand, we consider the above mentioned Matsusaka-Mumford’s theorem on smooth polarized varieties. We note that the positivity assumption in our result is semiampleness while ampleness is needed in
[
32
, Theorem 2]
. Moreover, we do not need the assumption that
X_{0}
is projective polarized and that the isomorphisms between fibers over
\Delta^{*}
are polarized. In particular, when the canonical line bundle of the generic fiber is semiample, our result is stronger than
[
32
, Theorem 2]
.

On the other hand, we remark that Theorem
1.2
generalizes the main result (i.e.,
[
31
, Theorem 1.2]
) of
[
31
]
, where one knows that the canonical line bundle
\omega_{S}
of
S
is semiample.

Now, combining with the abundance conjecture, it is clear that we can obtain more results as applications of Theorem
1.2
.

Recall that a projective manifold
S
is called
minimal
, if
\omega_{S}
is nef. It is conjectured that a projective manifold which is not uniruled has a birational model which is minimal. A minimal projective manifold
S
is called
good
if
\omega_{S}
is semiample. The abundance conjecture implies that every minimal projective manifold should be good.

The abundance conjecture is classically known for curves and surfaces, whereas for threefolds it was a fantastic achievement (see
[
23
]
). In arbitrary dimension, the conjecture also holds for manifolds of general type (see
[
21
]
). By these known results, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
X
be a complex manifold,
\pi\colon X\to\Delta
be a proper smooth Kähler morphism, and
S
be a minimal projective manifold. Suppose that either
\dim S\leq 3
or
S
is of general type. If all the fibers
X_{t}
with
t\in\Delta^{*}
are biholomorphic to
S
, then the central fiber
X_{0}
is also biholomorphic to
S
.

It is natural to have the following conjecture which seems to be easier than the abundance conjecture.

Conjecture 1.4
.

Let
X
be a complex manifold,
\pi\colon X\to\Delta
be a proper smooth Kähler morphism, and
S
be a minimal projective manifold with
\dim S\geq 4
. If all the fibers
X_{t}
with
t\in\Delta^{*}
are biholomorphic to
S
, then the central fiber
X_{0}
is also biholomorphic to
S
.

1.2.
Two types of isotriviality criteria of families over curves

Now we want to give some applications to the triviality of
smooth fibrations

f\colon X\to B
(i.e., smooth projective morphisms with connected fibers) over projective curves. Throughout this paper, we always work over the complex number field
\mathbb{C}
.

A family
f
of varieties is called
isotrivial
if all the fibers of
f
are isomorphic. Remark that isotriviality has different definitions in different papers. The following theorem is directly from Theorem
1.2
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
f\colon X\to B
be a smooth fibration between smooth projective varieties where
B
is a curve. Suppose that the generic fiber of
f
is good minimal. If there is a Zariski open subset
U
of
B
such that all the fibers over
U
are isomorphic, then
f
is isotrivial.

Let
B
be a smooth projective curve and
\Sigma
be a finite subset of
B
. Shafarevich conjectured in 1962 that the set of non-isotrivial families of smooth projective curves of genus
g\geq 2
over
B-\Sigma
is finite. Since then isotriviality criteria for families has fascinated scholars. There are two kinds of isotriviality criteria as follows induced from the Shafarevich conjecture.

1). Hyperbolic isotriviality criterion
. One type of isotriviality criterion is contained in the hyperbolicity part of the Shafarevich conjecture. The hyperbolic criterion says that:
if
2g(B)-2+\#\Sigma\leq 0
, then
f
is isotrivial.
The Shafarevich conjecture, which later played an important role in Faltings’ proof of the Mordell conjecture, was confirmed by Parshin
[
33
]
for
\Sigma=\emptyset
and by Arakelov
[
1
]
in general.

It is a natural and important question whether similar hyperbolic isotriviality criteria hold for families of higher dimensional varieties over higher dimensional bases. Because of the absence of minimal models among smooth varieties in higher dimensions, the isotriviality becomes to be birational isotriviality.

A family
f\colon X\to B
of varieties is called
birationally isotrivial
if there exists a dense open subset
B^{0}\subset B
, a finite cover
B^{\prime}\to B^{0}
and a commutative diagram

\textstyle{X\times_{B}B^{\prime}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Phi}
\scriptstyle{f_{B^{\prime}}}
\textstyle{B^{\prime}\times X_{0}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{pr_{1}}
\textstyle{B^{\prime}}

with
\Phi
is birational. In other words, denoting by
K=\mathbb{C}(B)
the function field of
B
, if the geometric generic fiber
X\times_{B}\overline{K}
is birational, over
\overline{K}
, to
X_{0}\times_{\mathbb{C}}\overline{K}
, then
f
is called birationally isotrivial. Here
\overline{K}
is the algebraic closure.
It is known that a family of varieties is birationally isotrivial if all the fibers are birational to each other
[
3
]
.

Families of higher dimensional varieties over a curve have been studied by several authors in recent years and the birational hyperbolic isotriviality criteria are now fairly well understood–the strongest results known were obtained in
[
44
,
45
]
and
[
29
]
, please see the survey articles
[
43
]
and
[
30
]
. For higher dimensional bases, the birational hyperbolic isotriviality was also studied, we refer the reader to
[
47
]
for an overview and recent progress.

The natural question is when birational isotriviality implies isotriviality. As an application of Theorem
1.5
, we can show that, for smooth families over curves whose generic fiber is good minimal, the birational isotriviality equals to the isotriviality.

Theorem 1.6
.

(=Theorem
7.1
)
Let
f\colon X\to B
be a smooth fibration between smooth projective varieties where
B
is a curve. Suppose that the generic fiber of
f
is good minimal. If
f
is birationally isotrivial, then
f
is isotrivial.

Combining with the above mentioned well understood birational hyperbolic isotriviality criteria and the known results on the abundance conjecture, it is easy to obtain many hyperbolic isotriviality results. We do not want to list them, but just to give an example (see
[
44
, Theorem 0.1]
).

Corollary 1.7
.

Let
f\colon X\to B
be a smooth fibration between smooth projective varieties where
B
is a curve. Suppose that the generic fiber of
f
is good minimal. If
g(B)\leq 1
, then
f
is isotrivial.

Remark that if the relative canonical line bundle
\omega_{X/B}
of the smooth family
f
is not ample but the restriction of
\omega_{X/B}
to every fiber is ample, then the above result has been obtained by Kovács
[
28
, Theorem 2.16]
, which uses the first fundamental theorem (Theorem 2) of
[
32
]
.

2). Parshin-Arakelov isotriviality criterion
. The other type of isotriviality criterion is contained in Parshin-Arakelov’s proof of the Shafarevich conjecture.
This criterion is as follows.

Let
f\colon X\to B
be a family of curves of genus
g\geq 2
where
B
is a smooth projective curve. Suppose that there is no
(-1)
-curve in any fiber. If
\deg(\det f_{*}\omega_{X/B})=0
, then
f
is isotrivial.

It is interesting to note that, in this case,
\deg(\det f_{*}\omega_{X/B})=0
if and only if
\deg(\det f_{*}\omega_{X/B}^{\otimes k})=0
for some
k\geq 1
, which is also equivalent to
\deg(\det f_{*}\omega_{X/B}^{\otimes k})=0
for each
k\geq 1
by the theory of fibered surfaces.

But the generalization of this kind of isotriviality criterion is still missing up to now. In order to obtain a further understanding, we give the following generalized Parshin-Arakelov isotriviality criteria for smooth families over curves, see Corollary
7.2
and Corollary
7.3
.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
f\colon X\to B
be a smooth fibration between smooth projective varieties where
B
is a curve. Assume that the generic fiber
F
of
f
is minimal, and one of the following two conditions is true,

i)
\dim F\leq 3
and
\deg(\det f_{*}\omega_{X/B}^{\otimes k})=0
for each
k\geq 2
;

ii)
F
is of general type and
\deg(\det f_{*}\omega_{X/B}^{\otimes k})=0
for some
k\geq 2
.

Then
f
is isotrivial.

Analogous to the well-studied hyperbolic isotriviality criterion, many cases of the Parshin-Arakelov isotriviality criterion are still open.

1.3.
Ideas of the proof of Theorem
1.2

The main idea of the proof of our main result Theorem
1.2
is to use the separatedness of the moduli space
P_{h}
of polarized manifolds.
Our major task is the following two parts: one is to obtain a
\pi
-ample line bundle on
X
, and the other is to show that the canonical line bundle
\omega_{X_{0}}
of
X_{0}
is semiample.

In the following explanation, we not only sketch our proof, but also interpret the difficulties we encountered. The innovations to overcome these difficulties are also mentioned.

(1) New extension of line bundles

The desired
\pi
-ample line bundle on
X
is obtained by extension. Different from the ordinary extension, we give a new kind of extension. Precisely, we extend a line bundle
{\cal L}_{0}
on the central fiber
X_{0}
to a global one
{\cal L}
over
X
. Moreover, if
{\cal L}_{0}
is ample, then the extended line bundle
{\cal L}
over
X
is
\pi
-ample. Here we use the Hodge theory and the relative Barlet cycle space for the Kähler morphism
\pi
.

It is worth to note that such a new extension is crucial in our proof of the nonexistence of divisorial components of stable base loci.

(2) Semiampleness of
\omega_{X_{0}}

We need to obtain the semiampleness of
\omega_{X_{0}}
from that of
\omega_{X_{t}}
for each
t\neq 0
. But such a result is not true for a general line bundle
{\cal L}
on
X
, even assuming that
{\cal L}_{t}:={\cal L}|_{X_{t}}
is very ample on
X_{t}\cong S
. In fact, the positivity of
\omega_{X_{0}}
becomes the most difficult part of this paper. Fortunately, we only need positivities of the pluricanonical and the adjoint linear systems on
X_{0}
.

Let
{\cal L}
be a
\pi
-ample line bundle over
X
. We can reduce the proof of the semiampleness of
\omega_{X_{0}}
to that of the nefness of
\omega^{\otimes m}_{X_{0}}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{0}
for each
m\in\mathbb{N}
, by Kawamata’s work on the theory of numerical Kodaira dimension. For such nefness, we apply analytic method to check the metric characterization of nefness. Roughly speaking, we use the theory of variation of Hodge structure to construct enough
(1,1)
-forms on
X_{0}
, which have some positivity on
X_{0}
outside a closed subset
Z
with
\mathrm{codim}(Z,X_{0})\geq 2
. Then the extension of nefness is reduced to the known results on extensions of plurisubharmonic functions.

(3) Divisorial components of stable base loci
\mathbf{B}(\omega^{\otimes m}_{X_{0}}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{0})

For convenience of readers, we first sketch the difficulty to obtain the above expected closed subset
Z\subset X_{0}
satisfying that
\mathrm{codim}(Z,X_{0})\geq 2
.

For a general big line bundle
{\cal F}
on
X
, the linear system
|{\cal F}|
induces a rational map

\phi\colon X\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}(H^{0}(X,{\cal F})).

It is natural to assume that
\phi
is well-defined outside a closed subset
W\subset X
with
\mathrm{codim}(W,X)\geq 2
, but we can not hope that the same thing is still true when we restrict
\phi
to
X_{0}
at the same time in general.

Back to our proof, to overcome this difficulty, we need to show that the stable base locus
\mathbf{B}(\omega^{\otimes m}_{X_{0}}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{0})
has no divisorial component, which can be reduced to the vanishing of the restricted volume. Due to the lack of the nefness of
\omega^{\otimes m}_{X_{0}}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{0}
, such vanishing of the restricted volume should be characterized by the “canonical movable intersection product”, which is difficult to calculate in general. Luckily, the duality of positive cones and the new extension of line bundles mentioned above help us to obtain such a calculation.

We believe that this is an instructive geometric application of the movable intersection theory.

The organization of this paper is as follows.

In §2, we give notations of moduli of polarized manifolds and some basics of stable base loci and positivie cones, which will be used in our proofs. In §3, we give our new extension of line bundles and construct
\pi
-ample line bundles on
X
. In §4, we use the theory of movable intersection to prove that the stable base locus
\mathbf{B}(\omega^{\otimes m}_{X_{0}}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{0})
has no divisorial component. Since the semiampleness of
\omega_{X_{0}}
is the most difficult part of this paper, we first prove some positivity on the central fiber in §5 as part of the preparation. The proof of our main result Theorem
1.2
is given in §6. In the last section, §7, we give our application to the isotriviality criteria.

2.1.
Moduli of polarized manifolds

Let
\varphi\colon X\to B
be a proper morphism of complex manifolds. A smooth
d
-closed
(1,1)
-form
\alpha
on
X
is called a
relative Kähler form
for
\varphi
, if there exits an open covering
\{U_{\lambda}\}
of
B
and Kähler forms
\eta_{\lambda}
on
U_{\lambda}
such that
\alpha|_{\varphi^{-1}(U_{\lambda})}+\varphi^{*}\eta_{\lambda}
are Kähler forms on
\varphi^{-1}(U_{\lambda})
. A morphism
\varphi
is said to be
Kähler

[
15
, Definition 4.1]
, if there exists a relative Kähler form for
\varphi
.

Let
S
be a compact complex manifold. In the following context, we always assume that
\dim S=n
and
\dim X=n+1
without mention.

Let
\Omega_{S}
be the cotangent bundle of
S
,
\Omega^{p}_{S}=\wedge^{p}\Omega_{S}
, and
\omega_{S}=\Omega^{n}_{S}
be the canonical line bundle of
S
. The
Kodaira dimension

\kappa(S)
of
S
is defined as

\kappa(S)=\max\{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}|\limsup\limits_{m\to\infty}\frac{h^{0}(S,\omega_{S}^{\otimes m})}{m^{k}}>0\},

and the
numerical Kodaira dimension

\nu(S)
is defined as

\nu(S)=\max\{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}|c^{k}_{1}(\omega_{S})\neq 0\in H^{2k}(S,\mathbb{C})\}.

It is known (see
[
22
]
) that, if
\omega_{S}
is nef, then

(2.1)

\kappa(S)\leq\nu(S),

and the equality holds if and only if
\omega_{S}
is semiample.

Let
{\cal L}
be a line bundle on
X
, and
{\cal L}_{t}={\cal L}|_{X_{t}}
for any
t\in B
. We recall the following classical definitions for the reader’s convenience:

•

{\cal L}
is
\varphi
-free
if the natural morphism
\varphi^{*}\varphi_{*}{\cal L}\to{\cal L}
is surjective. In particular, if
{\cal L}
is
\varphi
-free then it induces a morphism
X\to\mathbb{P}(\varphi_{*}{\cal L})
over
B
;

•

{\cal L}
is
\varphi
-very ample
if
{\cal L}
is
\varphi
-free and the induced morphism
X\to\mathbb{P}(\varphi_{*}{\cal L})
is a closed immersion;

•

{\cal L}
is
\varphi
-semiample
(resp.
\varphi
-ample
) if
{\cal L}^{\otimes k}
is
\varphi
-free (resp.
\varphi
-very ample) for some positive integer
k
.

For a polynomial
h\in\mathbb{Q}[T]
, let
{\cal P}_{h}
be the fibered category over the category of schemes, such that for a scheme
B
the groupoid
{\cal P}_{h}(B)
is as follows

\displaystyle{\cal P}_{h}(B)=

\displaystyle\bigg\{(f\colon X\to B,{\cal H})|f\,\mbox{is a smooth morphism};\,{\cal H}\,\mbox{is a line bundle on $X$;}

\displaystyle(X_{b},{\cal H}_{b})\,\mbox{is a polarized manifold,\, $\omega_{X_{b}}$ is semiample, $\forall b\in B$};

\displaystyle h(m)=\chi({\cal H}^{\otimes m}),\mbox{ $\forall m\in\mathbb{Z}$}\bigg\}.

The arrow of
{\cal P}_{h}(B)
from
(f\colon X\to B,{\cal H})
to
(f^{\prime}\colon X^{\prime}\to B,{\cal H}^{\prime})
is an isomorphism
\tau\colon X\to X^{\prime}
such that
{\cal H}_{b}
and
(\tau^{*}{\cal H}^{\prime})|_{X_{b}}
are numerical equivalent for all
b\in B
. By
[
42
, Theorem 4.6]
, there exists a coarse separated moduli algebraic space
P_{h}
for
\mathcal{P}_{h}
. Note that
P_{h}
is the quotient of the moduli space of polarized manifolds by compact equivalence relations (see
[
42
]
).

If
(\varphi\colon X\to B,{\cal H})
is a smooth family of polarized manifolds with Hilbert polynomial
h(m)=\chi({\cal H}^{\otimes m})
for all
m\in\mathbb{Z}
, then we have an induced morphism

\mu_{\varphi}\colon B\to P_{h}

satisfying that
\mu_{\varphi}(b)=(X_{b},{\cal H}_{b})\in P_{h}
for all
b\in B
.

2.2.
Stable base loci

In this subsection, we recall several concepts related to stable base loci. The readers can find more details in
[
8
,
12
]
. For a line bundle
{\cal L}
on a projective variety
Y
, let
\text{Bs}(|{\cal L}^{\otimes m}|)
be the base locus of the complete linear system
|{\cal L}^{\otimes m}|
. The
stable base locus
of
{\cal L}
is the algebraic set

\mathbf{B}({\cal L}):=\bigcap_{m\geq 1}\text{Bs}(|{\cal L}^{\otimes m}|)_{\scriptsize\mbox{red}}.

It is known that there exists a positive integer
m_{0}
such that

(2.2)

\mathbf{B}({\cal L})=\text{Bs}(|{\cal L}^{\otimes km_{0}}|)

for all
k\geq 1
, and thus
\mathbf{B}({\cal L})=\mathbf{B}({\cal L}^{\otimes p})
for all
p\geq 1
.

The
augmented base locus
of
{\cal L}
is defined as

\mathbf{B}_{+}({\cal L}):=\bigcap_{m\geq 1}\mathbf{B}({\cal L}^{\otimes m}{\otimes}{\cal A}^{\vee}),

where
{\cal A}
is any ample line bundle on
Y
and
{\cal A}^{\vee}
is the dual of
{\cal A}
. By
[
12
, §1]
, the augmented base loci satisfy the following properties.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
{\cal L}
be a line bundle on a projective manifold, then

(1)

\mathbf{B}({\cal L})\subseteq\mathbf{B}_{+}({\cal L})
;

(2)

\mathbf{B}_{+}({\cal L})
is empty if
{\cal L}
is ample;

(3)

for all
p\geq 1
,
\mathbf{B}_{+}({\cal L})=\mathbf{B}_{+}({\cal L}^{\otimes p})
;

(4)

for any line bundles
{\cal L}_{1},{\cal L}_{2}
,
\mathbf{B}_{+}({\cal L}_{1}\otimes{\cal L}_{2})\subseteq\mathbf{B}_{+}({\cal L}_{1})\cup\mathbf{B}_{+}({\cal L}_{2}).

2.3.
Positive cones

For a projective manifold
Y
of dimension
n
, let
H^{k,k}_{\geq 0}(Y)
be the cone of cohomology classes of type
(k,k)
which have non-negative intersection with all closed semi-positive smooth forms of bidegree
(n-k,n-k)
, and

\mathcal{N}:=H^{n-1,n-1}_{\geq 0}(Y).

Let
NE(Y)
be the convex cone generated by the cohomology classes of all effective curves in
H^{n-1,n-1}(Y,\mathbb{R})
. This cone is contained in the following group

NS(Y)_{\mathbb{R}}:=\bigg(H^{n-1,n-1}(Y,\mathbb{R})\cap H^{2n-2}(Y,\mathbb{Z})/\mbox{Tor}\bigg)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}.

Therefore we have

\overline{NE(Y)}\subset\mathcal{N}_{NS}:=\mathcal{N}\cap NS(Y)_{\mathbb{R}}.

Let
\mathcal{K}(Y)
be the
ample cone
of
Y
which is the convex cone of all ample
\mathbb{R}
-divisor classes on
Y
, and let the
nef cone

\overline{\mathcal{K}(Y)}
be the closed convex cone of all nef
\mathbb{R}
-divisor classes. The
pseudoeffective cone

{\cal E}_{NS}
is the closed convex cone of all pseudoeffective
\mathbb{R}
-divisor classes. The interior
{\cal E}_{NS}^{\circ}
of
{\cal E}_{NS}
is called the
big cone
which is the convex cone of all big
\mathbb{R}
-divisor classes on
Y
. There are several relations between these cones.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
6
, Proposition 1.4]
)
.

For a projective manifold
Y
, we have

\overline{NE(Y)}=\mathcal{N}_{NS}.

Moreover, the two cones
\overline{NE(Y)}
and
\overline{\mathcal{K}(Y)}
are dual to each other with respect to the Poincaré dual pairing.

Now we introduce some basics on the theory of the canonical movable intersection product, which are used to calculate the vanishing of the restricted volumes of big divisors in the present paper.

Definition-Proposition 2.3

(
[
6
, Theorem 3.5]
)
.

Let
Y
be a projective manifold of dimension
n
.
For each integer
k=1,2,\ldots,n
, there exists a “canonical movable intersection product”

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{NS}\times\cdots\times\mathcal{E}_{NS}\to\overline{NE(Y)}\subset H^{k,k}_{\geq 0}(Y),

\displaystyle(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{k})\mapsto\langle\alpha_{1}\cdot\alpha_{2}\cdots\alpha_{k-1}\cdot\alpha_{k}\rangle

such that

•

if
\alpha=c_{1}({\cal L})
for some big line bundle
{\cal L}
, then

(2.3)

\langle\alpha^{n}\rangle=\mathrm{Vol}({\cal L}):=\limsup\limits_{k\to\infty}\frac{n!}{k^{n}}\dim H^{0}(Y,{\cal L}^{\otimes k});

•

the product is increasing, homogeneous of degree 1 and superadditive in each argument, i.e.

\langle\alpha_{1}\cdots(\alpha_{j}^{\prime}+\alpha_{j}^{\prime\prime})\cdots\alpha_{k}\rangle\geq\langle\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{j}^{\prime}\cdots\alpha_{k}\rangle+\langle\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{j}^{\prime\prime}\cdots\alpha_{k}\rangle;

•

the product coincides with the ordinary intersection product when the
\alpha_{j}
are nef classes.

There is another equivalent definition of the above intersection product by using the definition of non-pluripolar product, see
[
5
, Definition 1.17]
.

The volume function

in (
2.3
) has many beautiful properties, see the following two theorems for example. These two theorems are proved by Boucksom-Favre-Jonsson in
[
7
]
, and one can see
[
11
]
for more details.

Theorem 2.4

(
[
11
, Theorem 1.4]
)
.

The volume function
\langle\alpha^{n}\rangle
in (
2.3
) is differentiable on
{\cal E}_{NS}^{\circ}
and the partial derivatives are given by

\frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{t=0}\langle(\alpha+t\gamma)^{n}\rangle=n\langle\alpha^{n-1}\rangle\cdot\gamma.

The vanishing of the restricted volume is equivalent to the existence of divisorial components in stable base loci, see the following result for a precise description.

Theorem 2.5

(
[
11
, Theorem 1.5]
)
.

Let
{\cal L}
be a big line bundle on
Y
, then a prime divisor

will lie in the augmented base locus

of

if and only if

\langle c_{1}({\cal L})^{n-1}\rangle\cdot c_{1}(D)=0.

3.
Extension of line bundles

3.1.
Extension from the central fiber

When
\pi\colon X\to\Delta
is smooth, one gets that

H^{2}(X_{t},\mathbb{Z})\cong H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z}),\mbox{ for all $t\in\Delta$}.

Let
[A_{t}]
be a cohomology class in
H^{2}(X_{t},\mathbb{Z})
, and
\widetilde{[A_{t}]}
be its corresponding cohomology class in
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
by (
3.1
).
So, for any

, we regard the cohomology class
[A_{t_{1}}]\in H^{2}(X_{t_{1}},\mathbb{Z})
the same as
[A_{t_{2}}]\in H^{2}(X_{t_{2}},\mathbb{Z})
, whenever
\widetilde{[A_{t_{1}}]}=\widetilde{[A_{t_{2}}]}\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
. We make the same assumption for sheaves
\mathbb{C}
and
{\cal O}_{X}
in the following context.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\pi\colon X\to\Delta
be a proper smooth Kähler morphism from a complex manifold
X
to the unit disc
\Delta
, and
S
be a projective manifold. Suppose that
X_{t}\cong S
for all
t\neq 0
. Let
A
be a divisor on
X_{0}
, and
[A]
be the corresponding cohomology class in
H^{2}(X_{0},\mathbb{C})
. Then there exists a line bundle
{\cal L}
on
X
such that
c_{1}({\cal L}_{t})=[A]
for all
t\in\Delta
.

Proof.

Since
\pi
is a proper smooth Kähler morphism, we can assume that
X
is Kähler after shrinking the disc small enough. So the dimension
\dim H^{q}(X_{t},\Omega_{X_{t}}^{p})
is independent of the choice of
t\in\Delta
. By
[
4
, Theorem 1.0.3]
(see also
[
34
, Theorem 1.2]
or
[
35
, Theorem 1.4]
),

is Moishezon, and thus
X_{0}
is projective for it is also Kähler.

Because the sheaf
R^{2}\pi_{*}\mathbb{C}
(resp.
R^{2}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Z}
) is locally constant, we can assume that
R^{2}\pi_{*}\mathbb{C}
(resp.
R^{2}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Z}
) is a constant sheaf after shrinking the disc small enough.

By the exponential sequence, we have the following exact sequence

(3.1)

H^{1}(X,{\cal O}_{X}^{*})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle c_{1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\iota}}{{\longrightarrow}}H^{2}(X,{\cal O}_{X})\to.

Applying the Leray spectral sequence to
\pi
for the sheaves
\mathbb{Z}
and
{\cal O}_{X}
, we have the following commutative diagram

\begin{CD}H^{1}(X,{\cal O}_{X}^{*})@>{{}^{c_{1}}}>{}>H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})@>{{}^{\iota}}>{}>H^{2}(X,{\cal O}_{X})@>{}>{}>\\
@V{{}^{\cong}}V{}V@V{{}^{\cong}}V{}V\\
H^{0}(\Delta,R^{2}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Z})@>{{}^{\psi}}>{}>H^{0}(\Delta,R^{2}\pi_{*}{\cal O}_{X})@>{}>{}>.\end{CD}

Similarly, for each
t\in\Delta
, we have the following morphism

\iota_{t}:H^{2}(X_{t},\mathbb{Z})\to H^{2}(X_{t},{\cal O}_{X_{t}}).

Moreover, by the Hodge decomposition

H^{2}(X_{t},\mathbb{C})=\bigoplus_{p,q\geq 0,p+q=2}H^{q}(X_{t},\Omega^{p}_{X_{t}}),

we have the following projection

\psi_{t}=\iota_{t}\colon H^{2}(X_{t},\mathbb{Z})\subset H^{2}(X_{t},\mathbb{C})\to H^{2}(X_{t},{\cal O}_{X_{t}}).

Claim A
: The projection
\psi_{t}
is independent of
t\in\Delta
.

Assume Claim A first. Under the following composed morphism

H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})\subset H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})\to H^{0}(\Delta,R^{2}\pi_{*}{\cal O}_{X}),

the image of
{\widetilde{[A]}}\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
in
H^{0}(\Delta,R^{2}\pi_{*}{\cal O}_{X})

is a section which is independent of
t\in\Delta
by Claim A. Thus
\iota(\widetilde{[A]})
is constant over
\Delta
.

If
A
is a divisor on
X_{0}
, then
\iota_{0}([A])=0
by the exact sequence (
3.1
) for
X_{0}
. Furthermore, we have that
\iota(\widetilde{[A]})=0
. Thus
[A]
can be considered as the first Chern class of some line bundle
{\cal L}
on
X
by (
3.1
).

Now we only need to give the proof of Claim A.

Proof of Claim A
:
For each
0\leq p\leq 2
, let

F^{p}H^{2}(X_{t},\mathbb{C})=\bigoplus_{r+q=2,r\geq p}H^{q}(X_{t},\Omega^{r}_{X_{t}}),~~~\mbox{and~}~b^{p,2}=\dim F^{p}H^{2}(X_{t},\mathbb{C}).

By Hodge theory as in
[
46
, Chapter 10]
, we have the following holomorphic morphism

{\cal P}^{p,2}\colon\Delta\to\mbox{Grass}(b^{p,2},H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})),

where
\mbox{Grass}(b^{p,2},H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C}))
is the Grassmannian of subspaces in

with dimension

. Since
X_{t}\cong S
for all
t\neq 0
, we know that
{\cal P}^{p,2}
is constant. Therefore the subspaces

are the same for

and all

. So the projection

\psi_{t}
is independent of
t\in\Delta
.
∎

3.2.
Existence of relative ample line bundles

Let
\varphi\colon X\to B
be a Kähler morphism with
\dim X=n+1
, and
0\leq p\leq n
be an integer. Let
C^{p}(X/B)
be the relative Barlet space
[
2
]
, which parameterizes the

\mu:C(X/B):=\bigcup\limits_{0\leq p\leq n}C^{p}(X/B)\to B.

Let
A
be a connected component of

, then the restriction morphism
\mu|_{A}
is proper by
[
15
, Theorem 4.5]
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\pi\colon X\to\Delta
be a proper smooth Kähler morphism from a complex manifold
X
to the unit disc, and
S
be a projective manifold. Suppose
X_{t}\cong S
for all
t\neq 0
, then
\pi
is a projective morphism.

Proof.

As showed in the first paragraph of the proof of Lemma
3.1
,
X_{0}
is projective. So we can take an ample line bundle
{\cal A}_{0}
on
X_{0}
. By Lemma
3.1
, there exits a line bundle
{\cal A}
on
X
satisfying
c_{1}({\cal A}|_{X_{0}})=c_{1}({\cal A}_{0})
. Let
{\cal A}_{t}={\cal A}|_{X_{t}}
be the restriction of
{\cal A}
on
X_{t}
for
t\neq 0
. We only need to show that the line bundle
{\cal A}
on
X
is
\pi
-ample.

Let
1\leq p\leq n-1
where
\dim S=n
. For an arbitrary irreducible subvariety of

with
\dim Z=p
, let
\widetilde{Z}
be the connected component of
C^{p}(X/\Delta)
where
Z
locates. Then we have the following claim.

Claim B
:
\mu(\widetilde{Z})=\Delta
.

Assume Claim B first. Then
\widetilde{Z}\cap\mu^{-1}(t)\neq\emptyset
for all
t\in\Delta
. For a point
z\in\widetilde{Z}\cap\mu^{-1}(t)
, let

be its corresponding cycle. Then

and the cohomology classes

are the same as

where

. So

\int_{Z_{t}}c_{1}({\cal A}_{t})^{p}=\int_{Z_{0}}c_{1}({\cal A}_{0})^{p}>0,~~~\forall~1\leq p\leq n-1.

Therefore
{\cal A}_{t}
is ample on
X_{t}
for all

, and the line bundle

on

is
\pi
-ample.

In order to finish the proof, we shall prove Claim B.

Proof of Claim B
: Let
\tilde{\pi}\colon\widetilde{X}:=\pi^{-1}(\Delta^{*})\to\Delta^{*}
be the restriction of
\pi
on
X^{*}
. Since the fibers
X_{t}\cong S
for all
t\neq 0
, the smooth family
\tilde{\pi}
is locally trivial by
[
FG
]
. Thus, for each

, there exists a small neighborhood

of

such that

\tilde{\pi}^{-1}(U_{t})\cong U_{t}\times S.

So

is a projective morphism. By the functorial property of the relative Barlet space, we have

C^{p}(\tilde{\pi}^{-1}(U_{t})/U_{t})\cong C^{p}(S)\times U_{t},~~~\forall~1\leq p\leq n-1.

Therefore
U_{t}\times_{\Delta}\widetilde{Z}\to U_{t}
is surjective. Note that

is a proper morphism, so

must be
\Delta

∎

4.
Divisorial components of stable base loci

In this section, we will use the theory of movable intersection to prove that some stable base loci have no divisorial component. First we calculate the needed movable intersection in our proof.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\pi\colon X\to\Delta
be a proper smooth Kähler morphism from a complex manifold
X
to the unit disc
\Delta
,
S
be a given minimal projective manifold, and
{\cal L}
be a
\pi
-very ample line bundle on
X
. If
X_{t}\cong S
for each
t\neq 0
, then for each
m\in\mathbb{N}
,

\langle c_{1}(\omega_{X_{0}}^{\otimes m}\otimes{\cal L}_{0})^{n-1}\rangle=c_{1}(\omega_{X_{0}}^{\otimes m}\otimes{\cal L}_{0})^{n-1}.

Proof.

Let
\gamma\in{\cal K}(X_{0})
be any ample class on
X_{0}
. Then by Lemma
3.1
, there exists a global
\pi
-ample line bundle
{\cal F}
on
X
such that
c_{1}({\cal F}_{t})=\gamma\in{\cal K}(X_{t})
for all
t\in\Delta
.
Let
r
be a small positive rational number, for all
k
such that
kr
is an integer, the twisted plurigenera
h^{0}\big(X_{t},(\omega_{X_{t}}^{\otimes m}\otimes{\cal L}_{t}\otimes{\cal F}_{t}^{\otimes r})^{\otimes k}\big)
is invariant by
[
41
]
or
[
14
, Theorem 4.1]
, that is,

h^{0}\big(X_{s},(\omega_{X_{s}}^{\otimes m}\otimes{\cal L}_{s}\otimes{\cal F}_{s}^{\otimes r})^{\otimes k}\big)=h^{0}\big(X_{t},(\omega_{X_{t}}^{\otimes m}\otimes{\cal L}_{t}\otimes{\cal F}_{t}^{\otimes r})^{\otimes k}\big),~~\forall~s,t\in\Delta.

Therefore, for the big divisor classes
\alpha_{t}:=c_{1}(\omega_{X_{t}}^{\otimes m}\otimes{\cal L}_{t})
with
t\in\Delta
,

\displaystyle\langle(\alpha_{0}+r\gamma)^{n}\rangle

\displaystyle=\limsup\limits_{k\to\infty}\frac{n!}{k^{n}}h^{0}\big(X_{0},(\omega_{X_{0}}^{\otimes m}\otimes{\cal L}_{0}\otimes{\cal F}_{0}^{\otimes r})^{\otimes k}\big)

\displaystyle=\limsup\limits_{k\to\infty}\frac{n!}{k^{n}}h^{0}\big(X_{t},(\omega_{X_{t}}^{\otimes m}\otimes{\cal L}_{t}\otimes{\cal F}_{t}^{\otimes r})^{\otimes k}\big)

\displaystyle=\big(c_{1}(\omega_{X_{t}}^{\otimes m}\otimes{\cal L}_{t}\otimes{\cal F}_{t}^{\otimes r})\big)^{n}

\displaystyle=\big(c_{1}(\omega_{X_{0}}^{\otimes m}\otimes{\cal L}_{0}\otimes{\cal F}_{0}^{\otimes r})\big)^{n}=(\alpha_{0}+r\gamma)^{n}.

Furthermore,

\frac{d}{dr}\bigg|_{r=0}\langle(\alpha_{0}+r\gamma)^{n}\rangle=\frac{d}{dr}\bigg|_{r=0}(\alpha_{0}+r\gamma)^{n}=n\alpha_{0}^{n-1}\cdot\gamma.

On the other hand, by Theorem
2.4
, we know that

\frac{d}{dr}\bigg|_{r=0}\langle(\alpha_{0}+r\gamma)^{n}\rangle=n\langle\alpha_{0}^{n-1}\rangle\cdot\gamma.

So, by the above two equations,

\alpha_{0}^{n-1}\cdot\gamma=\langle\alpha_{0}^{n-1}\rangle\cdot\gamma

for all
\gamma\in{\cal K}(X_{0})
. Because the cones
\overline{{\cal K}(X_{0})}
and
\overline{NE(X_{0})}
are dual to each other by Theorem
2.2
, we obtain the needed result

\langle c_{1}(\omega_{X_{0}}^{\otimes m}\otimes{\cal L}_{0})^{n-1}\rangle=c_{1}(\omega_{X_{0}}^{\otimes m}\otimes{\cal L}_{0})^{n-1}.

∎

Now we can show the vanishing of the restricted volume and obtain that some stable base loci have no divisorial component.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\pi\colon X\to\Delta
be a proper smooth Kähler morphism from a complex manifold
X
to the unit disc
\Delta
,
S
be a given minimal projective manifold, and
{\cal L}
be a
\pi
-very ample line bundle on
X
. If
X_{t}\cong S
for each
t\neq 0
, then, for each
p\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists an integer

such that

\text{Bs}(|(\omega_{X_{0}}^{\otimes p}\otimes{\cal L}_{0})^{\otimes k_{p}}|)
has no divisorial component.

Proof.

Since
{\cal L}_{0}
is ample on
X_{0}
, we know that, by (2)-(4) of Lemma
2.1
,

(4.1)

\mathbf{B}_{+}(\omega_{X_{0}}^{\otimes m}\otimes{\cal L}_{0})\subseteq\mathbf{B}_{+}(\omega_{X_{0}}^{\otimes(m+1)}\otimes{\cal L}_{0}),~~\forall m\in\mathbb{N}.

Suppose that for some
p\in\mathbb{N}
, the set of divisorial components of
\mathbf{B}_{+}(\omega_{X_{0}}^{\otimes p}\otimes{\cal L}_{0})
is non-empty. Let
E
be any irreducible effective divisorial component of
\mathbf{B}_{+}(\omega_{X_{0}}^{\otimes p}\otimes{\cal L}_{0})
, then
E
is a divisorial component of
\mathbf{B}_{+}(\omega_{X_{0}}^{\otimes m}\otimes{\cal L}_{0})
for each

by (
4.1
).
By Theorem
2.5
and Lemma
4.1
, for
m\geq p
,

\displaystyle\big(mc_{1}(\omega_{X_{0}})+c_{1}({\cal L}_{0})\big)^{n-1}\cdot c_{1}(E)=0,

that is,

m^{n-1}c_{1}(\omega_{X_{0}})^{n-1}c_{1}(E)+\cdots+mc_{1}(\omega_{X_{0}})c_{1}({\cal L}_{0})^{n-2}c_{1}(E)+c_{1}({\cal L}_{0})^{n-1}c_{1}(E)=0.

So the above polynomial on

with degree no more than

This is impossible since

is ample and

Note that, by (1) of Lemma

).

∎

X_{0}

7.
Isotriviality for smooth families over projective curves

In this section, we will give some application of our main result to the isotriviality of smooth families. To this end, we will give a detailed description for readers’ convenience.

Let
f\colon X\to B
be a smooth fibration between smooth projective varieties. A
minimal closed field of definition
of

denotes the fraction field.

This condition is equivalent to the following one: there are a fibration
f^{!}\colon X^{!}\to B^{!}
with
\overline{\mathbb{C}(B^{!})}=K
, a generically finite and generically surjective morphism
\pi\colon\bar{B}\to B
and a generically surjective morphism
\rho\colon\bar{B}\to B^{!}
such that the induced fibration
\bar{f}\colon\bar{X}\to\bar{B}
from
f
by
\pi
is birationally equivalent to that from
f^{!}
by
\rho
as in the following commutative diagram

\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f}
\textstyle{\bar{X}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\bar{f}}
\textstyle{X^{!}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f^{!}}
\textstyle{B}
\textstyle{\bar{B}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi}
\scriptstyle{\rho}
\textstyle{B^{!}.}

A minimal closed field of definition of

is a priori not unique. But it is unique in the case where the geometric generic fiber
X_{\eta}
has a good minimal model. The
variation

\mathrm{Var}(f)
of

is defined by

\mathrm{Var}(f)=\mathrm{trans.deg.}_{\mathbb{C}}K

for an arbitrary chosen minimal closed field of definition

of

So, we know that

\begin{CD}X@<{}<{}<\bar{X}@>{}>{}>X^{!}\\

Corollary 7.3
.

Let
f\colon X\to B
be a smooth fibration between smooth projective varieties where
B
is a curve. Suppose that the generic fiber
F
of
f
is minimal of general type and
\deg(\det f_{*}\omega_{X/B}^{\otimes k})=0
for some
k\geq 2
. Then
f
is isotrivial.

Proof.

Suppose
f
is not isotrivial,
\mathrm{Var}(f)\neq 0
by Theorem
7.1
and thus
\mathrm{Var}(f)=\dim B=1
. Then by
[
27
, Theorem on p.363]

\det f_{*}\omega_{X/B}^{\otimes\eta}
is ample for some
\eta>0
. However, this implies that
\det f_{*}\omega_{X/B}^{\otimes m}
is ample for all
m\geq 2
by
[
13
, Theorem 0.1]
, which leads to a contradiction.
∎

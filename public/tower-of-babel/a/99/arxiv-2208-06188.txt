Solvability of a class of mean-field BSDEs with quadratic growth
Tao Hao, Jiaqiang Wen, Jie Xiong

Abstract




Abstract:
In this paper, we study the multi-dimensional mean-field backward stochastic differential equations (BSDEs, for short) with quadratic growth.
Under small terminal value, the existence and uniqueness are proved for the multi-dimensional situation when the generator
f(t,Y,\mathbb{E}[Y],Z,\mathbb{E}[Z])
is of quadratic growth with respect to the last four items, using some new methods. Besides, a kind of comparison theorem is obtained.

Key words:
mean-field backward stochastic differential equation, backward stochastic
differential equation, quadratic growth.

AMS subject classifications.
60H10, 60H30

Introduction

Mean-field stochastic differential equations (SDEs, for short), also called McKean-Vlasov equations, can be traced back to the works of Kac
[
19
]
in the 1950s.
Recently, mean-field backward stochastic differential equations (BSDEs, for short) were introduced by Buckdahn, Djehiche, Li and Peng
[
6
]
and Buckdahn, Li and Peng
[
7
]
, owing to that mathematical mean-field approaches play an important role in many fields, such as economics, physics, and game theory (see Lasry and Lions
[
21
]
). Since then, mean-field BSDEs have received intensive attention.
In order to present the work more clearly, we describe the problem in detail.

Assume that
\{W_{t};0\leqslant t<\infty\}
is a
d
-dimensional standard Brownian motion defined on a complete filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{F},\mathbb{P})
, where
\mathbb{F}=\{\mathscr{F}_{t};0\leqslant t<\infty\}
is the filtration generated by
W
and augmented by all the
\mathbb{P}
-null sets in
\mathscr{F}
.
Consider the following mean-field backward stochastic differential equations over a finite horizon
[0,T]
:

\left\{\negthinspace\negthinspace\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle-dY_{t}=f(t,Y_{t},\mathbb{E}[Y_{t}],Z_{t},\mathbb{E}[Z_{t}])dt-Z_{t}dW_{t},\quad~t\in[0,T];\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle Y_{T}=\xi,\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

where the random variable
\xi
is called the
terminal value
and the mapping
f(\cdot)
is called the
generator
. The pair of unknown processes
(Y,Z)
, called an adapted solution of
(\ref{BSDE})
, are
\mathbb{F}
-adapted with values in
\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
.
For convenience, hereafter, by
quadratic
mean-field BSDEs, or mean-field BSDEs with
quadratic growth
, we mean that in (
1.1
), the generator
f(\cdot)
grows in
Z
quadratically.
Meanwhile, we call
\xi
the
bounded terminal value
if it is bounded. In addition,
we call
\xi
the
small terminal value
, if there exists a small positive constant
\varepsilon
such that
\|\xi\|_{\infty}\leqslant\varepsilon
.

As a natural extension of BSDEs (see below for precise description),
the mean-field BSDEs (
1.1
) were introduced by Buckdahn et al.
[
6
,
7
]
, where they established the existence and uniqueness of adapted solutions under the condition that
f(\cdot)
is uniformly Lipschitz in the last four arguments.
From then on, the theory and applications of mean-field BSDEs have been developed significantly.
For example, Carmona and Delarue provided a detailed probabilistic analysis of the optimal control of nonlinear stochastic dynamical systems of the McKean-Vlasov type in
[
10
]
, and
studied some special class of quadratic forward-backward stochastic differential equations (FBSDEs, for short) of mean-field type in
[
11
]
.
Buckdahn et al.
[
8
]
studied the general mean-field stochastic differential equations and their relation with the associated PDEs.
Zhang, Sun and Xiong
[
30
]
obtained a general stochastic maximum principle for a Markov regime switching jump-diffusion model of mean-field type.
Briand, Elie and Hu
[
3
]
introduced and studied the BSDEs with mean reflection.
Li, Sun and Xiong
[
22
]
established the results for linear quadratic optimal control problems for mean-field backward stochastic differential equations.
Douissi, Wen and Shi
[
13
]
and Shi, Wen and Xiong
[
26
]
studied the optimal control problem of mean-field forward-backward stochastic systems driven by fractional Brownian motion.

Now, we recall the following nonlinear BSDEs:

\left\{\negthinspace\negthinspace\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle-dY_{t}=f(t,Y_{t},Z_{t})dt-Z_{t}dW_{t},\quad~t\in[0,T];\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle Y_{T}=\xi.\end{array}\right.

(1.2)

When the generator
f(\cdot)
is of linear growth with respect to
(Y,Z)
, the existence and uniqueness of (
1.2
) were firstly proved by Pardoux and Peng
[
24
]
. Since then, a lot of researchers have found that BSDEs have important applications in mathematical finance, stochastic optimal control and partial differential equation (see
[
14
,
23
,
29
]
, to mention a few).
Meanwhile, owing to many important applications, a lot of efforts have been made to relax the conditions on the generator
f(\cdot)
of (
1.2
) with respect to
Z
. For example,
Kobylanski
[
20
]
proved the existence and uniqueness of one-dimensional BSDE with bounded terminal condition and with
f(\cdot)
growing quadratically in
Z
.
The well-posedness of one-dimensional quadratic BSDE with unbounded terminal value was obtained by
Briand and Hu
[
4
,
5
]
.
Hibon et al.
[
15
]
studied a class of quadratic BSDEs with mean reflection. Hu, Li and Wen
[
18
]
obtained the existence and uniqueness of anticipated BSDEs with quadratic growth.
Some other recent developments of quadratic BSDEs can be found in Bahlali, Eddahbi and Ouknine
[
1
]
, Barrieu and El Karoui
[
2
]
, Cheridito and Nam
[
9
]
, Hibon, Hu and Tang
[
16
]
, Hu and Tang
[
17
]
, Tevzadze
[
27
]
,
Xing and Zitkovic
[
28
]
, and references cited therein.

Let us introduce a motivation to study (
1.1
) at the particles level. Consider the following particle system with
N
particles:

Y^{i}_{t}=\xi^{i}+\int_{t}^{T}f^{i}\Big(s,Y^{i}_{s},\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}Y^{i}_{s},Z^{ii}_{s},\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}Z^{ii}_{s}\Big)ds-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\int_{t}^{T}Z^{ij}_{s}dW^{j}_{s},\quad~t\in[0,T],

where for each
i,j=1,...,N
,
Z^{ij}
is an
m\times d
matrix;

\{W^{j};1\leqslant j\leqslant N\}
are
N
independent
d
-dimensional Brownian motions;

\xi^{i}
and
f^{i}
are
N
independent copies of
\xi
and
f
, respectively.
Following Lions’s idea and the law of large numbers, we conjecture that when
N
tends to
\infty
, the mean-field limit of solutions of the above particle system corresponds to that of the mean-field BSDE (
1.1
).

On the other hand, in the last two decades, stimulated by the broad applications and the open problem proposed by Peng
[
25
]
, a lot of efforts have been made to relax the conditions on the generator
f
of the mean-field BSDEs.
Hibon, Hu and Tang
[
16
]
considered quadratic mean-field BSDEs in one-dimensional situation when the generator
f
depends on the expectation of
(Y,Z)
, and studied the existence and uniqueness of related equations.
However, the mean-field BSDEs (
1.1
) with quadratic growth in the multi-dimensional situation is still open.
In this paper, along with the work of
[
16
]
,
we study the solvability of mean-field BSDEs (
1.1
) with quadratic growth in the multi-dimensional situation.
First, borrowing some ideas from Tevzadze
[
27
]
, we construct an artful method to prove that, under small terminal value, the mean-field BSDE (
1.1
) admits a unique adapted solution in the multi-dimensional situation. It should be pointed out that, besides the multidimensional situation, our contribution also includes the generator
f(\cdot)
is of quadratic growth with respect to all items of
(Y,\mathbb{E}[Y],Z,\mathbb{E}[Z])
.
Then, a comparison theorem for such equations is obtained for the one-dimensional situation.

This article is organized as follows.
Some preliminaries are presented in Section 2.
The existence and uniqueness of multi-dimensional quadratic mean-field BSDEs (
1.1
) with small terminal value are proved by the fixed point argument in Section 3. A comparison theorem for such BSDEs is given in Section 4.

2
Preliminaries

Recall that
\{W_{t}\;;0\leqslant t<\infty\}
is a
d
-dimensional standard Brownian motion defined on
the complete filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{F},\mathbb{P})
, where
\mathbb{F}=\{\mathscr{F}_{t}\}_{t\geqslant 0}
is the filtration generate generated by
W
and augmented by all the
\mathbb{P}
-null sets in
\mathscr{F}
.
We denote by
\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
the space of the
m\times d
-matrix
C
with Euclidean norm
|C|=\sqrt{tr(CC^{*})}
.
In the following, for Euclidean space
\mathbb{H}
and
t\in[0,T]
, we denote

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle L^{\infty}_{\mathscr{F}_{t}}(\Omega;\mathbb{H})=\Big\{\theta:\Omega\to\mathbb{H}\bigm|\theta\hbox{ is $\mathscr{F}_{t}$-measurable, }\|\theta\|_{\infty}\triangleq\mathop{\rm esssup}_{\omega\in\Omega}|\theta(\omega)|<\infty\Big\},\\
\end{array}

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(t,T;\mathbb{H})\negthinspace=\negthinspace\Big\{\varphi:[t,T]\negthinspace\times\negthinspace\Omega\to\mathbb{H}\bigm|\varphi\hbox{ is
$\mathbb{F}$-progressively measurable, }\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad~\|\varphi\|_{L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(t,T)}\triangleq\negthinspace\Big(\mathbb{E}\int^{T}_{t}\negthinspace|\varphi_{s}|^{2}ds\Big)^{1\over 2}\negthinspace<\negthinspace\negthinspace\infty\Big\},\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle L_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(t,T;\mathbb{H})=\Big\{\varphi:[t,T]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{H}\bigm|\varphi\hbox{ is
$\mathbb{F}$-progressively measurable, }\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad~\|\varphi\|_{L_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(t,T)}\triangleq\mathop{\rm esssup}_{(s,\omega)\in[t,T]\times\Omega}|\varphi_{s}(\omega)|<\infty\Big\},\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle S_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(t,T;\mathbb{H})=\Big\{\varphi:[t,T]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{H}\bigm|\varphi\hbox{ is
$\mathbb{F}$-adapted, continuous, }\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad~\|\varphi\|_{S_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(t,T)}\triangleq\Big\{\mathbb{E}\Big(\sup_{s\in[t,T]}|\varphi_{s}|^{2}\Big)\Big\}^{\frac{1}{2}}<\infty\Big\}.\end{array}

Let
M=(M_{t},\mathscr{F}_{t})
be a uniformly integrable martingale with
M_{0}=0
, and for
p\in[1,\infty)
, we set

\|M\|_{BMO_{p}(\mathbb{P})}\triangleq\sup_{\tau}\bigg\|\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\Big[\big(\langle M\rangle_{\tau}^{\infty}\big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\Big]^{\frac{1}{p}}\bigg\|_{\infty},

where the supremum is taken over all
\mathbb{F}
-stopping times
\tau
, and
\mathbb{E}_{\tau}
is the conditional expectation given
\mathscr{F}_{\tau}
. The class
\big\{M:\|M\|_{BMO_{p}(\mathbb{P})}<\infty\big\}
is denoted by
BMO_{p}(\mathbb{P})
. Observe that
\|\cdot\|_{BMO_{p}}
is a norm on this space and
BMO_{p}(\mathbb{P})
is a Banach space. In the sequel, we denote
BMO(\mathbb{P})
the space of
BMO_{2}(\mathbb{P})
for simplicity. Next, for any
Z\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{H})
, by Burkholder-Davis-Gundy’s inequalities, one has

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle c_{2}\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\Big[\Big(\int_{\tau}^{T}|Z_{s}|^{2}ds\Big)\Big]\leqslant\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\Big[\sup_{t\in[\tau,T]}\Big|\int_{\tau}^{t}Z_{s}dW_{s}\Big|^{2}\Big]\leqslant C_{2}\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\Big[\Big(\int_{\tau}^{T}|Z_{s}|^{2}ds\Big)\Big],\end{array}

for some constants
c_{2},C_{2}>0
. Thus,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle c_{2}\sup_{\tau\in\mathscr{T}[t,T]}\Big\|\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\Big[\Big(\int_{\tau}^{T}|Z_{s}|^{2}ds\Big)\Big]\Big\|_{\infty}\leqslant\sup_{\tau\in\mathscr{T}[t,T]}\Big\|\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\Big[\sup_{t\in[\tau,T]}\Big|\int_{\tau}^{t}Z_{s}dW_{s}\Big|^{2}\Big]\Big\|_{\infty}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\leqslant C_{2}\sup_{\tau\in\mathscr{T}[t,T]}\Big\|\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\Big[\Big(\int_{\tau}^{T}|Z_{s}|^{2}ds\Big)\Big]\Big\|_{\infty},\end{array}

where
\mathscr{T}[t,T]
denotes the set of all
\mathbb{F}
-stopping times
\tau
valued in
[t,T]
. Note that the above quantities could be infinite. Therefore, we introduce the following:

{\cal Z}^{2}[t,T]=\Big\{Z\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(t,T;\mathbb{H})\Bigm|\|Z\|_{{\cal Z}^{2}[t,T]}\equiv\sup_{\tau\in\mathscr{T}[t,T]}\Big\|\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\Big[\int_{\tau}^{T}|Z_{s}|^{2}ds\Big]\Big\|_{\infty}^{1\over 2}<\infty\Big\}.

Recall that for
Z\in{\cal Z}^{2}[0,T]
, the process
s\mapsto\int_{0}^{s}Z_{r}dW_{r}
(denoted by
Z\cdot W
),
s\in[0,T]
, is a
BMO-martingale
. Moreover, note that on
[0,T]
,
Z\cdot W
belongs to
BMO(\mathbb{P})
if and only if
Z\in{\cal Z}^{2}[0,T]
, that is,

\|Z\cdot W\|^{2}_{BMO(\mathbb{P})}\equiv\|Z\|^{2}_{{\cal Z}^{2}[0,T]}.

Definition 2.1
.

A pair of processes
(Y,Z)\in S^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{m})\times L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{m\times d})
is called an
adapted solution
of BSDE (
1.1
), if
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely, it satisfies (
1.1
). Moreover, if
(Y,Z)\in L^{\infty}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{m})\times{\cal Z}^{2}[0,T]
, it is called a bounded adapted solution.

3
Existence and Uniqueness

In this section, we study multi-dimensional mean-field BSDEs with quadratic growth and small terminal value.
By the theory of ordinary differential equations, we see that if the generator
f(\cdot)
is super-linear with respect to
Y
, then the equations may not have global solutions. However, we pointed out that, under small bounded value, the generator could be of quadratic growth with respect to
Y
and
Z
(see Hu, Li and Wen
[
18
]
).
Now let us introduce the following hypothesis.

Assumption 3.1
.

Let
C
be a positive constant. For all

s\in[0,T]
,
y_{i},\bar{y}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{m},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
with
i=1,2
,
f(s,0,0,0,0)
is bounded and

\displaystyle\big|f(s,y_{1},\bar{y}_{1},z_{1},\bar{z}_{1})-f(s,y_{2},\bar{y}_{2},z_{2},\bar{z}_{2})\big|\leqslant

\displaystyle C\Big(|y_{1}|+|\bar{y}_{1}|+|y_{2}|+|\bar{y}_{2}|+|z_{1}|+|\bar{z}_{1}|+|z_{2}|+|\bar{z}_{2}|\Big)

\displaystyle\cdot\Big(|y_{1}-y_{2}|+|\bar{y}_{1}-\bar{y}_{2}|+|z_{1}-z_{2}|+|\bar{z}_{1}-\bar{z}_{2}|\Big).

Example 3.2
.

Assumption
3.1
implies that the generator
f(\cdot)
could be of quadratic growth with respect to the last four arguments. The following generator

f(s,y_{1},y_{2},z_{1},z_{2})=y^{2}+\bar{y}^{2}+z^{2}+\bar{z}^{2},\qquad s\in[0,T],y,\bar{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{m},z,\bar{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}

satisfies such an assumption.

In the following, we state and prove the main result of this section, which establishes the existence and uniqueness of multi-dimensional BSDE (
1.1
) with quadratic growth and small terminal value.

Theorem 3.3
.

Under the Assumption
3.1
, for any bounded
\xi\in L^{\infty}_{\mathscr{F}_{T}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
satisfying

\|\xi\|_{\infty}+\bigg\|\int_{0}^{T}|f(t,0,0,0,0)|dt\bigg\|_{\infty}\leqslant\rho,

(3.1)

where

\rho^{2}=\frac{1}{4097C^{2}(T^{2}+1)},

we have that mean-field BSDE (
1.1
) admits a unique adapted solution
(Y,Z)
in
{\cal B}_{\rho}
, where

{\cal B}_{\rho}\triangleq\Big\{(Y,Z)\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{m})\times{\cal Z}^{2}[0,T]\Bigm|\|Y\|^{2}_{L_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(0,T)}+\|Z\|_{{\cal Z}^{2}[0,T]}^{2}\leqslant\rho^{2}\Big\}.

Proof.

The proof is divided into two steps.

Step 1
.
We firstly consider the existence and uniqueness of the following BSDE

Y_{t}=\xi+\int_{t}^{T}\Big(f(s,Y_{s},\mathbb{E}[Y_{s}],Z_{s},\mathbb{E}[Z_{s}])-f(s,0,0,0,0)\Big)ds-\int_{t}^{T}Z_{s}dW_{s},\quad 0\leqslant t\leqslant T.

(3.2)

In order to solve the above equation, for every
(y,z)\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{m})\times{\cal Z}^{2}[0,T]
,
we define the mapping
(Y,Z)=\Gamma(y,z)
by

Y_{t}=\xi+\int_{t}^{T}\Big(f(s,y_{s},\mathbb{E}[y_{s}],z_{s},\mathbb{E}[z_{s}])-f(s,0,0,0,0)\Big)ds-\int_{t}^{T}Z_{s}dW_{s},\quad 0\leqslant t\leqslant T.

(3.3)

For (
3.3
), using Itô’s formula to
|Y|^{2}
on
[t,T]
, we obtain

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle|Y_{t}|^{2}+\int_{t}^{T}|Z_{r}|^{2}dr=|\xi|^{2}+\int_{t}^{T}2Y_{r}\cdot\Big(f(r,y_{r},\mathbb{E}[y_{r}],z_{r},\mathbb{E}[z_{r}])-f(r,0,0,0,0)\Big)dr\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\ -2\int_{t}^{T}Y_{r}\cdot Z_{r}dW_{r}.\end{array}

Taking the conditional expectation and using the
inequality
2ab\leqslant\frac{1}{2}a^{2}+2b^{2}
, we get

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle|Y_{t}|^{2}+\mathbb{E}_{t}\int_{t}^{T}|Z_{r}|^{2}dr\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\leqslant\|\xi\|_{\infty}^{2}+2\|Y\|_{L_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(0,T)}\Bigg(\mathbb{E}_{t}\int_{t}^{T}|f(r,y_{r},\mathbb{E}[y_{r}],z_{r},\mathbb{E}[z_{r}])-f(r,0,0,0,0)|dr\Bigg)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\leqslant\|\xi\|_{\infty}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|Y\|_{L_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(0,T)}^{2}+2\Bigg(\mathbb{E}_{t}\int_{t}^{T}|f(r,y_{r},\mathbb{E}[y_{r}],z_{r},\mathbb{E}[z_{r}])-f(r,0,0,0,0)|dr\Bigg)^{2}.\end{array}

(3.4)

It follows from Jensen’s inequality that the last term of (
3.4
) naturally reduces to

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{t}\int_{t}^{T}|f(r,y_{r},\mathbb{E}[y_{r}],z_{r},\mathbb{E}[z_{r}])-f(r,0,0,0,0)|dr\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\leqslant C\mathbb{E}_{t}\int_{t}^{T}\Big(|y_{r}|+|\mathbb{E}[y_{r}]|+|z_{r}|+|\mathbb{E}[z_{r}]|\Big)^{2}dr\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\leqslant 4C\mathbb{E}_{t}\int_{t}^{T}\Big(|y_{r}|^{2}+|\mathbb{E}[y_{r}]|^{2}+|z_{r}|^{2}+|\mathbb{E}[z_{r}]|^{2}\Big)dr\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\leqslant 4C\bigg[\mathbb{E}_{t}\int_{t}^{T}\big(|y_{r}|^{2}+|z_{r}|^{2}\big)dr+\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}\big(|y_{r}|^{2}+|z_{r}|^{2}\big)dr\bigg].\end{array}

(3.5)

Hence, combining (
3.4
) and (
3.5
), we can obtain

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|Y\|^{2}_{L_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(0,T)}+\|Z\|_{{\cal Z}^{2}[0,T]}^{2}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\leqslant\|\xi\|_{\infty}^{2}+32C^{2}\mathop{\rm esssup}_{(t,\omega)\in[0,T]\times\Omega}\bigg[\mathbb{E}_{t}\int_{t}^{T}\big(|y_{r}|^{2}+|z_{r}|^{2}\big)dr+\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}\big(|y_{r}|^{2}+|z_{r}|^{2}\big)dr\bigg]^{2}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\leqslant\|\xi\|_{\infty}^{2}+64C^{2}\bigg(T^{2}\|y\|_{L_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(0,T)}^{4}+\|z\|_{{\cal Z}^{2}[0,T]}^{4}\bigg).\end{array}

Then, it follows from the elementary inequality
a^{2}+b^{2}\leqslant(|a|+|b|)^{2}
that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\|Y\|^{2}_{L_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(0,T)}+\|Z\|_{{\cal Z}^{2}[0,T]}^{2}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\leqslant 4\|\xi\|_{\infty}^{2}+\beta^{2}\Big(\|y\|_{L_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(0,T)}^{2}+\|z\|_{{\cal Z}^{2}[0,T]}^{2}\Big)^{2},\end{array}

where
\beta\triangleq 16C\sqrt{T^{2}+1}
. Now, we would like to pick
R
such that

4\|\xi\|_{\infty}^{2}+\beta^{2}R^{4}\leqslant R^{2}.

In fact, the above inequality is solvable if and only if

\|\xi\|_{\infty}\leqslant\frac{1}{4\beta}.

(3.6)

For example, we can take

R=2\sqrt{2}\|\xi\|_{\infty}

in order to satisfy this quadratic inequality. Then the ball

{\cal B}_{R}\triangleq\Big\{(Y,Z)\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{m})\times{\cal Z}^{2}[0,T]\Bigm|\|Y\|^{2}_{L_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(0,T)}+\|Z\|_{{\cal Z}^{2}[0,T]}^{2}\leqslant R^{2}\Big\}

is such that
\Gamma({\cal B}_{R})\subset{\cal B}_{R}
.

Step 2
. We prove that the mapping
\Gamma
is a contraction on
{\cal B}_{R}
.

For every
(y,z)
,
(\bar{y},\bar{z})\in{\cal B}_{R}
, let
(Y,Z)=\Gamma(y,z)
and

(\bar{Y},\bar{Z})=\Gamma(\bar{y},\bar{z})
. For simplicity of presentation, we denote

{\hat{y}}=y-\bar{y},\quad~{\hat{z}}=z-\bar{z},\quad~{\hat{Y}}=Y-\bar{Y},\quad~{\hat{Y}}=Y-\bar{Y}.

Similar to the above discussion, we deduce that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|{\hat{Y}}\|^{2}_{L_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(0,T)}+\|{\hat{Z}}\|_{{\cal Z}^{2}[0,T]}^{2}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\leqslant 2\mathop{\rm esssup}_{(t,\omega)\in[0,T]\times\Omega}\bigg[\mathbb{E}_{t}\int_{t}^{T}\Big|f(r,y_{r},\mathbb{E}[y_{r}],z_{r},\mathbb{E}[z_{r}])-f(r,\bar{y}_{r},\mathbb{E}[\bar{y}_{r}],\bar{z}_{r},\mathbb{E}[\bar{z}_{r}])\Big|dr\bigg]^{2}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\leqslant 2C^{2}\mathop{\rm esssup}_{(t,\omega)\in[0,T]\times\Omega}\bigg[\mathbb{E}_{t}\int_{t}^{T}\Big(|y_{r}|+|\bar{y}_{r}|+\mathbb{E}[|y_{r}|]+\mathbb{E}[|\bar{y}_{r}|]+|z_{r}|+|\bar{z}_{r}|+\mathbb{E}[|z_{r}|]+\mathbb{E}[|\bar{z}_{r}|]\Big)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\ \cdot\Big(|{\hat{y}}_{r}|+|{\hat{z}}_{r}|+\mathbb{E}\big[|{\hat{y}}_{r}|]+\mathbb{E}\big[|{\hat{z}}_{r}|\big]\Big)dr\bigg]^{2}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\leqslant 2C^{2}\mathop{\rm esssup}_{(t,\omega)\in[0,T]\times\Omega}\bigg[\mathbb{E}_{t}\int_{t}^{T}\Big(|y_{r}|+|\bar{y}_{r}|+\mathbb{E}[|y_{r}|]+\mathbb{E}[|\bar{y}_{r}|]+|z_{r}|+|\bar{z}_{r}|+\mathbb{E}[|z_{r}|]+\mathbb{E}[|\bar{z}_{r}|]\Big)^{2}dr\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad~\cdot\mathbb{E}_{t}\int_{t}^{T}\Big(|{\hat{y}}_{r}|+|{\hat{z}}_{r}|+\mathbb{E}\big[|{\hat{y}}_{r}|]+\mathbb{E}\big[|{\hat{z}}_{r}|\big]\Big)^{2}dr\bigg]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\leqslant 64C^{2}\mathop{\rm esssup}_{(t,\omega)\in[0,T]\times\Omega}\bigg[\mathbb{E}_{t}\int_{t}^{T}\Big(|y_{r}|^{2}+|\bar{y}_{r}|^{2}+\mathbb{E}[|y_{r}|^{2}]+\mathbb{E}[|\bar{y}_{r}|^{2}]+|z_{r}|^{2}+|\bar{z}_{r}|^{2}+\mathbb{E}[|z_{r}|^{2}]+\mathbb{E}[|\bar{z}_{r}|^{2}]\Big)dr\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad~\cdot\mathbb{E}_{t}\int_{t}^{T}\Big(|{\hat{y}}_{r}|^{2}+|{\hat{z}}_{r}|^{2}+\mathbb{E}\big[|{\hat{y}}_{r}|^{2}]+\mathbb{E}\big[|{\hat{z}}_{r}|^{2}\big]\Big)dr\bigg]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\leqslant 256C^{2}(T^{2}+1)\Big[\|y\|^{2}_{L_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(0,T)}+\|z\|^{2}_{{\cal Z}^{2}[0,T]}+\|\bar{y}\|^{2}_{L_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(0,T)}+\|\bar{z}\|^{2}_{{\cal Z}^{2}[0,T]}\Big]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\quad\ \times\Big(\|{\hat{y}}\|^{2}_{L_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(0,T)}+\|{\hat{z}}\|^{2}_{{\cal Z}^{2}[0,T]}\Big).\end{array}

Noting that

\|y\|^{2}_{L_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(0,T)}+\|z\|^{2}_{{\cal Z}^{2}[0,T]}\leqslant R^{2},\qquad\|\bar{y}\|^{2}_{L_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(0,T)}+\|\bar{z}\|^{2}_{{\cal Z}^{2}[0,T]}\leqslant R^{2},

we obtain

\|{\hat{Y}}\|^{2}_{L_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(0,T)}+\|{\hat{Z}}\|_{{\cal Z}^{2}[0,T]}^{2}\leqslant MR^{2}\big(\|{\hat{y}}\|^{2}_{L_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(0,T)}+\|{\hat{z}}\|^{2}_{{\cal Z}^{2}[0,T]}\big),

where

{M\triangleq 512C^{2}(T^{2}+1).}

Now, note that

{R=2\sqrt{2}\|\xi\|_{\infty},}

we have that if

{\|\xi\|_{\infty}^{2}<\frac{1}{8M},}

(3.7)

then

{MR^{2}<1,}

which implies that
\Gamma
is a contraction on
{\cal B}_{R}
. By the contraction principle, under the condition (
3.7
), the mapping
\Gamma
admits a unique fixed point, which is the solution of (
3.2
).

Finally, we come back to BSDE (
1.1
), which can be rewritten into the form of (
3.2
) as follows:

Y_{t}=\widetilde{\xi}+\int_{t}^{T}\Big(f(s,Y_{s},\mathbb{E}[Y_{s}],Z_{s},\mathbb{E}[Z_{s}])-f(s,0,0,0,0)\Big)ds-\int_{t}^{T}Z_{s}dW_{s},\quad 0\leqslant t\leqslant T,

(3.8)

where

\widetilde{\xi}\triangleq\xi+\int_{t}^{T}f(s,0,0,0,0)ds.

Note that (
3.7
) is stronger than (
3.6
). So if we define
\rho>0
by letting

\rho^{2}\triangleq\frac{1}{4097C^{2}(T^{2}+1)},

then BSDE (
1.1
) admits a unique adapted solution

(Y,Z)\in{\cal B}_{\rho}
under the following condition

\|\xi\|_{\infty}+\bigg\|\int_{0}^{T}|f(t,0,0,0,0)|dt\bigg\|_{\infty}\leqslant\rho.

(3.9)

This completes the proof.
∎

.

.

\left\{\begin{aligned} \displaystyle dX_{t}&=b(t,X_{t},\mathcal{L}(X_{t}),\hat{\alpha}(t,X_{t},\mathcal{L}(X_{t}),(\sigma(t,X_{t},\mathcal{L}(X_{t}))^{-1})^{\intercal}Z_{t}))dt\\

(3.10)

4
Comparison Theorem

In this section, we study the comparison theorem of mean-field BSDEs with quadratic growth of the following form:

Y_{t}=\xi+\int_{t}^{T}f(s,Y_{s},\mathbb{E}[Y_{s}],Z_{s})ds-\int_{t}^{T}Z_{s}dW_{s},\quad 0\leqslant t\leqslant T.

(4.1)

We consider BSDE (
4.1
) in one-dimensional case only, i.e.,
m=1
. For simplicity of presentation, we let
d=1
too. For this situation, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
C
be a positive constant, and suppose that there are two increasing functions

\lambda:\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
and
\bar{\lambda}:\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
,
bounded on all bounded subsets, and a predictable process
k\in{\cal Z}^{2}[0,T]
such that
for all
s\in[0,T]
,
y,\bar{y},z\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\big|f(s,y,\bar{y},z)\big|\leqslant

\displaystyle k_{t}^{2}\big[\lambda(|y|)+\bar{\lambda}(|\bar{y}|)\big]+Cz^{2}.

(4.2)

Then, for bounded terminal value
\xi\in L^{\infty}_{\mathscr{F}_{T}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
,
the martingale part of any bounded solution of BSDE (
4.1
) belongs to the space BMO
(\mathbb{P})
, i.e.,
Z\in{\cal Z}^{2}[0,T]
.

Proof.

Let
Y
be a solution of BSDE (
4.1
) and there be a positive constant
M
such that

Y_{t}\leqslant M,\quad\hbox{ a.s. for all }t\in[0,T].

So we have that
\|\xi\|_{\infty}\leqslant M
. Applying Itô formula to
\exp\{\beta Y_{s}\}
on
[\tau,T]
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}\int_{\tau}^{T}e^{\beta Y_{s}}Z^{2}_{s}ds-\beta\int_{\tau}^{T}e^{\beta Y_{s}}f(s,Y_{s},\mathbb{E}[Y_{s}],Z_{s})ds+\beta\int_{\tau}^{T}e^{\beta Y_{s}}Z_{s}dW_{s}=e^{\beta\xi}-e^{\beta T_{\tau}}\leqslant e^{\beta M},

or

\displaystyle\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}\int_{\tau}^{T}e^{\beta Y_{s}}Z^{2}_{s}ds+\beta\int_{\tau}^{T}e^{\beta Y_{s}}Z_{s}dW_{s}\leqslant e^{\beta M}+\beta\int_{\tau}^{T}e^{\beta Y_{s}}f(s,Y_{s},\mathbb{E}[Y_{s}],Z_{s})ds,

where
\beta
is a constant which will be determined later. Now, if
Z\cdot W
is square integrable martingale, then taking conditional expectations on the above inequality, we obtain that

\displaystyle\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\int_{\tau}^{T}e^{\beta Y_{s}}Z^{2}_{s}ds\leqslant e^{\beta M}+\beta\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\int_{\tau}^{T}e^{\beta Y_{s}}f(s,Y_{s},\mathbb{E}[Y_{s}],Z_{s})ds.

Using the estimate (
4.2
) we obtain that

\displaystyle\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\int_{\tau}^{T}e^{\beta Y_{s}}Z^{2}_{s}ds\leqslant e^{\beta M}+\beta[\lambda(M)+\bar{\lambda}(M)]\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\int_{\tau}^{T}e^{\beta Y_{s}}k_{s}^{2}ds+\beta C\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\int_{\tau}^{T}e^{\beta Y_{s}}|Z_{s}|^{2}ds.

Thus we have

\displaystyle[\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}-\beta C]\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\int_{\tau}^{T}e^{\beta Y_{s}}Z^{2}_{s}ds\leqslant e^{\beta M}+\beta[\lambda(M)+\bar{\lambda}(M)]\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\int_{\tau}^{T}e^{\beta Y_{s}}k_{s}^{2}ds.

Taking
\beta=4C
, we deduce that

\displaystyle 4C^{2}\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\int_{\tau}^{T}e^{4CY_{s}}Z^{2}_{s}ds\leqslant

\displaystyle e^{4CM}+4C[\lambda(M)+\bar{\lambda}(M)]\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\int_{\tau}^{T}e^{4CY_{s}}k_{s}^{2}ds

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle e^{4CM}\Big(1+4C[\lambda(M)+\bar{\lambda}(M)]\|k\|_{{\cal Z}^{2}[0,T]}\Big).

Note that
-M\leqslant Y\leqslant M
, from the latter inequality we deduce that for any stopping time
\tau
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\int_{\tau}^{T}Z^{2}_{s}ds\leqslant\frac{e^{8CM}\Big(1+4C[\lambda(M)+\bar{\lambda}(M)]\|k\|_{{\cal Z}^{2}[0,T]}\Big)}{4C^{2}}.

Hence
Z\cdot W\in
BMO, i.e.,
Z\in{\cal Z}^{2}[0,T]
. This completes the proof.
∎

For simplicity of presentation, in the following we introduce some more notations. Let
(Y,Z)
and
(\tilde{Y},\tilde{Z})
be two pairs of processes, and the coefficients
(f,\xi)
and
(\tilde{f},\tilde{\xi})
be two pairs of generators. We define

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\delta f=f-\tilde{f},\qquad\delta\xi=\xi-\tilde{\xi},

\displaystyle\delta_{y}f(t)\equiv

\displaystyle\ \delta_{y}f(t,Y_{t},\tilde{Y}_{t},\mathbb{E}[Y_{t}],Z_{t})=\frac{f(t,Y_{t},\mathbb{E}[Y_{t}],Z_{t})-f(t,\tilde{Y}_{t},\mathbb{E}[Y_{t}],Z_{t})}{Y_{t}-\tilde{Y}_{t}},

\displaystyle\delta_{\bar{y}}f(t)\equiv

\displaystyle\ \delta_{\bar{y}}f(t,\tilde{Y}_{t},\mathbb{E}[Y_{t}],\mathbb{E}[\tilde{Y}_{t}],Z_{t})=\frac{f(t,\tilde{Y}_{t},\mathbb{E}[Y_{t}],Z_{t})-f(t,\tilde{Y}_{t},\mathbb{E}[\tilde{Y}_{t}],Z_{t})}{\mathbb{E}[Y_{t}]-\mathbb{E}[\tilde{Y}_{t}]},

\displaystyle\delta_{z}f(t)\equiv

\displaystyle\ \delta_{z}f(t,\tilde{Y}_{t},\mathbb{E}[\tilde{Y}_{t}],Z_{t},\tilde{Z}_{t})=\frac{f(t,\tilde{Y}_{t},\mathbb{E}[\tilde{Y}_{t}],Z_{t})-f(t,\tilde{Y}_{t},\mathbb{E}[\tilde{Y}_{t}],\tilde{Z}_{t})}{Z_{t}-\tilde{Z}_{t}},

and
\delta Y
and
\delta Z
could be defined similarly. Then we have

\displaystyle f(t,Y_{t},\mathbb{E}[Y_{t}],Z_{t})-f(t,\tilde{Y}_{t},\mathbb{E}[\tilde{Y}_{t}],\tilde{Z}_{t})=\delta_{y}f(t)\delta Y_{t}+\delta_{\bar{y}}f(t)\mathbb{E}[\delta Y_{t}]+\delta_{z}f(t)\delta Z_{t}.

(4.3)

For one dimensional BSDE (
4.1
), we have the following comparison theorem.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
(Y,Z)
and
(\tilde{Y},\tilde{Z})
be the bounded solutions of BSDE (
4.1
) with generators
(f,\xi)
and
(\tilde{f},\tilde{\xi})
respectively, which satisfy the condition of Lemma
4.1
. Moreover, if

\xi\leqslant\tilde{\xi}
a.s.,
f(t,y,\bar{y},z)\leqslant\tilde{f}(t,y,\bar{y},z)
a.e., and the following conditions hold:

\displaystyle\hbox{{\rm(A1)} for every }Y,\tilde{Y},Z,\ \ \delta_{y}f(t),\delta_{\bar{y}}f(t)\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R}),

\displaystyle\hbox{{\rm(A2)} for every }Z,\tilde{Z}\in{\cal Z}^{2}[0,T],\hbox{ and any bounded process }Y,\quad\delta_{z}f(t)\in{\cal Z}^{2}[0,T],

\displaystyle\hbox{{\rm(A3)} one of the two coefficients $f$ and $\tilde{f}$ is nondecreasing in }\bar{y},

then we have
Y_{t}\leqslant\tilde{Y}_{t}
a.s. for every
t\in[0,T]
.

Remark 4.3
.

The conditions
(A1)
and
(A2)
hold if there exists a positive constant
C
such that

\displaystyle|f(t,y_{1},\bar{y}_{1},z_{1})-f(t,y_{2},\bar{y}_{2},z_{2})|\leqslant C(|y_{1}-y_{2}|+|\bar{y}_{1}-\bar{y}_{2}|)+C({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}1+}|z_{1}|+|z_{2}|)|z_{1}-z_{2}|

for all
t\in[0,T]
and
y_{1},\bar{y}_{1},y_{2},\bar{y}_{2},z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
. Moreover,
(A1)
and
(A2)
hold too if
f(t,y,\bar{y},z)
satisfies the global Lipschitz condition.

Proof of Theorem
4.2
.

Without lose of generality, we would like to let that
f
is nondecreasing in
\bar{y}
.
Taking the difference of BSDE (
4.1
) with coefficients
(f,\xi)
and
(\tilde{f},\tilde{\xi})
respectively, we obtain that

\displaystyle Y_{t}-\tilde{Y}_{t}=

\displaystyle\ Y_{0}-\tilde{Y}_{0}-\int_{0}^{t}[f(s,Y_{s},\mathbb{E}[Y_{s}],Z_{s})-f(s,\tilde{Y}_{s},\mathbb{E}[\tilde{Y}_{s}],\tilde{Z}_{s})]ds

(4.4)

\displaystyle\ -\int_{0}^{t}[f(s,\tilde{Y}_{s},\mathbb{E}[\tilde{Y}_{s}],\tilde{Z}_{s})-\tilde{f}(s,\tilde{Y}_{s},\mathbb{E}[\tilde{Y}_{s}],\tilde{Z}_{s})]ds+\int_{0}^{t}(Z_{s}-\tilde{Z}_{s})dW_{s}.

(4.5)

We define the measure
\mathbb{Q}
by
d\mathbb{Q}=\mathcal{E}_{T}(\Lambda)d\mathbb{P}
, where

\displaystyle\Lambda_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}\delta_{z}f(t)dW_{s}.

By Lemma
4.1
, we have
Z,\tilde{Z}\in{\cal Z}^{2}[0,T]
. So the conditions (A1) and (A2) imply that
\Lambda\in\hbox{\rm BMO}
and hence
\mathbb{Q}
is a probability measure equivalent to
\mathbb{P}
.

We denote by
\bar{\Lambda}
the martingale part of
\delta Y=Y-\tilde{Y}
, in other words,

\displaystyle\bar{\Lambda}_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}(Z_{s}-\tilde{Z}_{s})dW_{s},\quad~t\in[0,T].

Therefore, on the one hand, from (
4.3
), we have that the process

\displaystyle\delta Y_{t}+\int_{0}^{t}[f(s,Y_{s},\mathbb{E}[Y_{s}],Z_{s})-f(s,\tilde{Y}_{s},\mathbb{E}[\tilde{Y}_{s}],\tilde{Z}_{s})]ds

\displaystyle\quad\ +\int_{0}^{t}[f(s,\tilde{Y}_{s},\mathbb{E}[\tilde{Y}_{s}],\tilde{Z}_{s})-\tilde{f}(s,\tilde{Y}_{s},\mathbb{E}[\tilde{Y}_{s}],\tilde{Z}_{s})]ds

\displaystyle=\delta Y_{t}+\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\delta_{y}f(s)\delta Y_{s}+\delta_{\bar{y}}f(s)\mathbb{E}[\delta Y_{s}]+\delta_{z}f(s)\delta Z_{s}\Big)ds+\int_{0}^{t}\delta f(s,\tilde{Y}_{s},\mathbb{E}[\tilde{Y}_{s}],\tilde{Z}_{s})ds

\displaystyle=\delta Y_{t}+\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\delta_{y}f(s)\delta Y_{s}+\delta_{\bar{y}}f(s)\mathbb{E}[\delta Y_{s}]+\delta f(s,\tilde{Y}_{s},\mathbb{E}[\tilde{Y}_{s}],\tilde{Z}_{s})\Big)ds+\int_{0}^{t}\delta_{z}f(s)\delta Z_{s}ds.

On the other hand, from (
4.4
), the process

\displaystyle\delta Y_{t}+\int_{0}^{t}[f(s,Y_{s},\mathbb{E}[Y_{s}],Z_{s})-f(s,\tilde{Y}_{s},\mathbb{E}[\tilde{Y}_{s}],\tilde{Z}_{s})]ds

\displaystyle\quad\ +\int_{0}^{t}[f(s,\tilde{Y}_{s},\mathbb{E}[\tilde{Y}_{s}],\tilde{Z}_{s})-\tilde{f}(s,\tilde{Y}_{s},\mathbb{E}[\tilde{Y}_{s}],\tilde{Z}_{s})]ds

\displaystyle=\delta Y_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}(Z_{s}-\tilde{Z}_{s})dW_{s}.

So, by Girsanov’s theorem, noting
\delta Y_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, we obtain that the process

\displaystyle\delta Y_{t}+\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\delta_{y}f(s)\delta Y_{s}+\delta_{\bar{y}}f(s)\mathbb{E}[\delta Y_{s}]+\delta f(s,\tilde{Y}_{s},\mathbb{E}[\tilde{Y}_{s}],\tilde{Z}_{s})\Big)ds

\displaystyle=\delta Y_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}(Z_{s}-\tilde{Z}_{s})dW_{s}-\int_{0}^{t}\delta_{z}f(s)\delta Z_{s}ds

\displaystyle=\delta Y_{0}+\bar{\Lambda}_{t}-\langle\Lambda,\bar{\Lambda}\rangle_{t}

is a local martingale under the measure
\mathbb{Q}
. Moreover, Proposition 11 of
[
12
]
implies that

\displaystyle\bar{\Lambda}_{t}-\langle\Lambda,\bar{\Lambda}\rangle_{t}\in\hbox{BMO}(\mathbb{Q}).

Finally, using the martingale property, the duality principle between SDEs and BSDEs, and the boundary conditions
Y_{T}=\xi
,
\tilde{Y}_{t}=\tilde{\xi}
, we have that

\displaystyle Y_{t}-\tilde{Y}_{t}=\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}_{t}\bigg(

\displaystyle\ e^{\int_{t}^{T}\delta_{y}f(s)ds}(\xi-\tilde{\xi})+\int_{t}^{T}e^{\int_{t}^{s}\delta_{y}f(r)dr}\delta_{\bar{y}}f(s)\mathbb{E}[Y_{s}-\tilde{Y}_{s}]ds

\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{T}e^{\int_{t}^{s}\delta_{y}f(r)dr}\big[f(s,\tilde{Y}_{s},\mathbb{E}[\tilde{Y}_{s}],\tilde{Z}_{s})-\tilde{f}(s,\tilde{Y}_{s},\mathbb{E}[\tilde{Y}_{s}],\tilde{Z}_{s})\big]ds\bigg),

which, noting
\xi\leqslant\tilde{\xi}
a.s. and
f(t,y,z)\leqslant\tilde{f}(t,y,z)
a.e., implies that

\displaystyle Y_{t}-\tilde{Y}_{t}\leqslant

\displaystyle\ \mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}_{t}\int_{t}^{T}e^{\int_{t}^{s}\delta_{y}f(r)dr}\delta_{\bar{y}}f(s)\mathbb{E}[Y_{s}-\tilde{Y}_{s}]ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \int_{t}^{T}\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}_{t}\Big(e^{\int_{t}^{s}\delta_{y}f(r)dr}\delta_{\bar{y}}f(s)\Big)\mathbb{E}[Y_{s}-\tilde{Y}_{s}]ds.

Since
\delta_{y}f(t)
and
\delta_{\bar{y}}f(t)
are bounded processes, there exists a positive constant
K
such that

\Big\|e^{\int_{t}^{s}\delta_{y}f(r)dr}\delta_{\bar{y}}f(s)\Big\|_{L_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(0,T)}\leqslant K.

Hence,

\displaystyle Y_{t}-\tilde{Y}_{t}\leqslant

\displaystyle\ K\int_{t}^{T}\mathbb{E}[Y_{s}-\tilde{Y}_{s}]ds.

Notice that
\mathbb{E}[Y_{s}-\tilde{Y}_{s}]\leqslant\mathbb{E}[(Y_{s}-\tilde{Y}_{s})^{+}]
and then

\int_{t}^{T}\mathbb{E}[Y_{s}-\tilde{Y}_{s}]ds\leqslant\int_{t}^{T}\mathbb{E}[(Y_{s}-\tilde{Y}_{s})^{+}]ds,

which implies that

\Big(\int_{t}^{T}\mathbb{E}[Y_{s}-\tilde{Y}_{s}]ds\Big)^{+}\leqslant\Big(\int_{t}^{T}\mathbb{E}[(Y_{s}-\tilde{Y}_{s})^{+}]ds\Big)^{+}=\int_{t}^{T}\mathbb{E}[(Y_{s}-\tilde{Y}_{s})^{+}]ds.

Note the inequality
(ab)^{+}\leqslant a\cdot b^{+}
when
a\geqslant 0
, it follows

\displaystyle(Y_{t}-\tilde{Y}_{t})^{+}\leqslant

\displaystyle\ K\Big(\int_{t}^{T}\mathbb{E}[Y_{s}-\tilde{Y}_{s}]ds\Big)^{+}\leqslant K\int_{t}^{T}\mathbb{E}[(Y_{s}-\tilde{Y}_{s})^{+}]ds,\quad~t\in[0,T],

from which we can conclude with the help of Gronwall’s lemma that
Y_{t}\leqslant\tilde{Y}_{t}
,
t\in[0,T]
,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.
∎

5
Conclusion

The multi-dimensional BSDEs with quadratic growth is a difficult yet important topic in the field of BSDEs.
In this work, using an artful method to construct the contracting mapping principle, we proved the existence and uniqueness of multi-dimensional mean-field BSDEs with quadratic growth under a small terminal value. Moreover, a comparison theorem is obtained. For the general mean-field BSDEs (see Carmona and Delarue
[
10
,
11
]
) with quadratic growth, the relevant results are under our investigation.

Abstract
. This study investigates the structure of Arf rings. From the perspective of ring extensions, a decomposition of integrally closed ideals is given. Using this, we present a kind of their prime ideal decomposition in Arf rings, and determine their structure in the case where both R R and the integral closure R ¯ \overline{R} are local rings.



1.
Introduction

The purpose of this paper is to give a decomposition of integrally closed ideals that extends the property of discrete valuation rings to Arf rings.

In 1971, J. Lipman introduced the notion of Arf rings for Cohen-Macaulay semi-local rings
R
satisfying that every localization
R_{M}
at a maximal ideal
M
is dimension one.
Arf rings were originally studied in the classification of certain singular points of plane curves by C. Arf
[
1
]
, and Lipman generalized them by extracting the essence of the rings.
A typical example of an Arf ring is a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension one with multiplicity at most two
[
7
, Example, page 664]
. It is also known that a semi-normal Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension one is Arf (see
[
3
, Theorem 4.4]
,
[
7
, Lemma 4.1]
,
[
6
]
).
Using this notion, it was proved that if
R
is a one-dimensional complete Noetherian local domain with an algebraically closed residue field of characteristic zero and if
R
is
saturated
in the sense of O. Zariski
[
10
]
, then
R
has minimal multiplicity (i.e., the embedding dimension of
R
equals the multiplicity of
R
). This result depends on the fact that such a ring is an Arf ring.
In
[
7
]
, Arf rings are characterized in terms of the stability of integrally closed open ideals and the behavior of the blow-ups (
[
7
, Theorem 2.2]
), which in turn tells us that every Arf local ring has minimal multiplicity.

Additionally, the Arf property is strongly related to the strict closedness of rings, which was given in the same paper
[
7
]
.
For the sake of simplicity, let
R
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension one.
As is mentioned in
[
7
]
, it was conjectured that
R
is an Arf ring if and only if
R=R^{*}
, where
R^{*}
is an intermediate ring between
R
and the integral closure
\overline{R}
,
the strict closure of
R
, consisting of those elements
\alpha
in
\overline{R}
such that
\alpha\otimes 1=1\otimes\alpha
in
\overline{R}\otimes_{R}\overline{R}
.
This conjecture was solved in
[
7
]
when
R
contains a field (
[
7
, Proposition 4.5, Theorem 4.6]
), and was settled in
[
3
]
for its general case (
[
3
, Theorem 4.4]
).
The core of the theory is that the ring structure is explored in connection with the behavior of ring extensions. The reader may consult with
[
7
,
9
,
2
,
4
,
3
]
about further study of Arf rings and strictly closed rings.

Recently, E. Celikbas, O. Celikbas, C. Ciupercă, N. Endo, S. Goto, this author, and N. Matsuoka
[
3
]
introduced the notion of weakly Arf rings by weakening the defining conditions of Arf rings, and extended the theory over arbitrary commutative rings.
It is known by
[
3
, Corollary 4.6]
that if
R
is a Noetherian ring that satisfies the Serre’s
(S_{2})
condition and
{\rm ht}_{R}M\geq 2
for every
M\in\operatorname{Max}R
, then
R
is a weakly Arf ring if and only if
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
is an Arf ring for every
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}R
with
{\rm ht}_{R}\mathfrak{p}=1
, which is also equivalent to
R=R^{*}
.
Thus, in this assumption, because
R=\overline{R}
if and only if
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a discrete valuation ring (DVR) for every
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}R
with
{\rm ht}_{R}\mathfrak{p}=1
(i.e.
R
is normal), we can develop the theory of weakly Arf rings parallel to that of integrally closed rings by replacing a DVR with an Arf ring, and the integral closure
\overline{R}
with the strict closure
R^{*}
.
Hence, in this study, to build the foundation for this theory, we explore the structure of Arf rings compared with the structure of DVRs.
The main result of this paper is stated as follows:

Theorem 1.1

(Corollary
4.8
)
.

Let
R
be an Arf ring, and let
I
be an integrally closed ideal in
R
that contains a non-zerodivisor on
R
.
Then, we can construct a certain tower
R=R_{(I,0)}\subseteq R_{(I,1)}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq R_{(I,n)}
of Arf rings in
\overline{R}
and an integrally closed ideal
I_{i}
in
R_{(I,i)}
for each
0\leq i\leq n
, such that
I=\sqrt{I_{0}}\sqrt{I_{1}}\cdots\sqrt{I_{n}}
, where
I_{0}=I
.

If
I\neq R
, then we can choose
n\geq 0
such that
I=\sqrt{I_{0}}\sqrt{I_{1}}\cdots\sqrt{I_{n}}
and
\sqrt{I_{i}}=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in V(I_{i})}\mathfrak{p}
for each
0\leq i\leq n
. Therefore, corresponding to the fact that every non-zero ideal in a DVR is the power of the maximal ideal,
I
can be decompose into the products of maximal ideals in rings
R
,
R_{(I,1)},R_{(I,2)},\cdots,R_{(I,n)}
.

We now explain the organization of this paper. In Section 2, based on
[
7
]
, we introduce the definition and basic properties of Arf rings, which we subsequently need. Section 3 provides the key property in this study regarding elements in the set
\mathcal{X}_{R}
of integrally closed ideals in
R
that contain a non-zerodivisor on
R
.
In Section 4, we prove the main result of this paper.
In Sections 5, we study Corollary
4.8
in detail. This section studies the case where
R
and
\overline{R}
are local rings, while introducing some examples.

Throughout this paper, unless otherwise specified, let
R
be an arbitrary commutative ring, let
W(R)
be the set of non-zerodivisors on
R
, and let
\mathcal{F}_{R}
be the set of
open
ideals in
R
, that is, the ideals of
R
that contain a non-zerodivisor on
R
. For an ideal
I
in
R
,
\overline{I}
denotes the integral closure of
I
in
R
, and set
\mathcal{X}_{R}=\{I\in\mathcal{F}_{R}\mid\overline{I}=I\}
. We set
X:Y=\{a\in\mathrm{Q}(R)\mid aY\subseteq X\}
for
R
-submodules
X
and
Y
of the total ring of fractions
\mathrm{Q}(R)
.

When
R
is a Noetherian local ring with the maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
, let
v(R)
(resp.
\mathrm{e}(R)
) denote the embedding dimension of
R
(resp. the multiplicity of
R
with respect to
\mathfrak{m}
).

2.
Arf rings

In this section, we introduce the definition and basic properties of Arf rings, based on
[
7
]
. For an arbitrary commutative ring
R
, let
W(R)
be the set of non-zerodivisors on
R
. We denote by
\mathcal{F}_{R}
the set of ideals in
R
that contain a non-zerodivisor on
R
.

First, we define the algebra
R^{I}
for each
I\in\mathcal{F}_{R}
. Let
I\in\mathcal{F}_{R}
. We consider the tower of
R
-algebras as follows:

R\subseteq I:I\subseteq I^{2}:I^{2}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq I^{n}:I^{n}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\mathrm{Q}(R).

We set
R^{I}=\bigcup_{n\geq 0}[I^{n}:I^{n}]
. Then,
R^{I}
is an intermediate ring between
R
and
\mathrm{Q}(R)
. If
a\in I
is a reduction of
I
, that is,
I^{r+1}=aI^{r}
for some
r\geq 0
, then
R^{I}=I^{n}:I^{n}
for any
n\geq r
and we have

R^{I}=R\left[\tfrac{I}{a}\right]=\frac{I^{r}}{a^{r}},\ \text{where}\ \frac{I}{a}=\{\frac{x}{a}\mid x\in I\}\subseteq\mathrm{Q}(R).

In particular, the following holds true.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
I\in\mathcal{F}_{R}
. Suppose that there exists
a\in I
and
r\geq 0
such that
I^{r+1}=aI^{r}
. Then, the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

R^{I}=I:I.

(2)

I:I=\frac{I}{a}.

(3)

I^{2}=aI.

Therefore, condition
(3)
is independent of the choice of reductions
a\in I
. An ideal
I\in\mathcal{F}_{R}
that satisfies condition
(3)
is called a stable ideal.

Proof.

The implication
(3)\Rightarrow(1)
is true. Let
A=I:I
. The inclusion
A\subseteq\frac{I}{a}
always holds.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
Because
R^{I}=A\subseteq\frac{I}{a}\subseteq R\left[\frac{I}{a}\right]=R^{I}
, we have
A=\frac{I}{a}
.

(2)\Rightarrow(3)
Because
I=IA=\frac{I^{2}}{a}
, we have
I^{2}=aI
.
∎

In what follows, unless otherwise specified, we assume that
R
satisfies the condition
(A)
.

(A)

R
is a Cohen-Macaulay semi-local ring of dimension one with
\dim R_{M}=1
for every
M\in\operatorname{Max}R
.

Under this assumption, the notion of Arf rings is defined as follows. We set
\mathcal{X}_{R}=\{I\in\mathcal{F}_{R}\mid\overline{I}=I\}
.

Definition 2.2

(
[
7
]
)
.

R
is called an
Arf ring
if the following conditions hold:

(1)

For every
I\in\mathcal{X}_{R}
, there exists an element
a\in I
such that
I=\overline{aR}
, that is,
I^{n+1}=aI^{n}
for some
n\geq 0
.

(2)

If
x,y,z\in R
such that
x\in W(R)
and
\frac{y}{x},\frac{z}{x}\in\overline{R}
, then
\frac{yz}{x}\in R
.

Arf rings are characterized in terms of the stability of the integrally closed ideals in
\mathcal{X}_{R}
.

Theorem 2.3

(
[
7
, Theorem 2.2]
)
.

The following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

R
is an Arf ring.

(2)

For every
I\in\mathcal{X}_{R}
, there exists
a\in I
such that
I^{2}=aI
.

For a Noetherian local ring
R
,
v(R)
denotes the embedding dimension of
R
, and
\mathrm{e}(R)
denotes the multiplicity of
R
with respect to the maximal ideal. When
R
is an Arf ring, the equation
v(R_{M})=\mathrm{e}(R_{M})
holds for every
M\in\operatorname{Max}R
(i.e.,
R_{M}
has minimal multiplicity), because
M^{2}=aM
for some
a\in M
. In contrast, even if
R_{M}
has minimal multiplicity for every
M\in\operatorname{Max}R
,
R
is not necessarily an Arf ring.

The structure of
R^{I}
plays an important role in understanding the structure and properties of an Arf ring
R
. Let us begin with the following:

Lemma 2.4

(c.f.
[
7
, Lemma 2.3]
)
.

Let
R
be an arbitrary commutative ring and let
I\in\mathcal{X}_{R}
. Suppose that there exists
a\in I
such that
I=\overline{aR}
and
I^{2}=aI
. We set
A=I:I
. Then, we have

\mathcal{X}_{A}=\{\frac{\mathfrak{a}}{a}\mid\mathfrak{a}\in\mathcal{X}_{R},\ \mathfrak{a}\subseteq I\}.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathfrak{a}\in\mathcal{X}_{R}
and
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq I
. We set
\mathfrak{b}=\frac{\mathfrak{a}}{a}
. First, we show that
\mathfrak{b}\in\mathcal{F}_{A}
. Because

I=\overline{aR}=a\overline{R}\cap R
, we have
A=\frac{I}{a}\subseteq\overline{R}
. Additionally, we have

\mathfrak{a}A=\frac{\mathfrak{a}I}{a}\subseteq\frac{I^{2}}{a}=I\subseteq R.

Then, we get

\mathfrak{a}A\subseteq\mathfrak{a}\overline{R}\cap R\subseteq\overline{\mathfrak{a}\overline{R}}\cap R=\overline{\mathfrak{a}}=\mathfrak{a},

which implies that
\mathfrak{a}A=\mathfrak{a}
. Therefore, we also have
\mathfrak{b}A=\mathfrak{b}
, so that
\mathfrak{b}
is an ideal of
A
.
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathfrak{b}
implies that
\mathfrak{b}\in\mathcal{F}_{A}
.
Second, we show that
\mathfrak{b}\in\mathcal{X}_{A}
. Take
\alpha\in\overline{\mathfrak{b}}\subseteq A
. Then

\alpha^{n}+b_{1}\alpha^{n-1}+\cdots+b_{n-1}\alpha+b_{n}=0

for some
n\geq 1
and
b_{i}\in\mathfrak{b}^{i}

(1\leq i\leq n)
. Because
a\alpha\in a\mathfrak{b}=\mathfrak{a}\subseteq R
, we obtain

(a\alpha)^{n}+ab_{1}(a\alpha)^{n-1}+\cdots+a^{n-1}b_{n-1}(a\alpha)+a^{n}b_{n}=0\ \ \text{and}

a^{i}b_{i}\in a^{i}\mathfrak{b}^{i}=\mathfrak{a}^{i}\ \ (1\leq i\leq n),

which implies that
a\alpha\in\overline{\mathfrak{a}}=\mathfrak{a}
. Therefore,
\alpha\in\frac{\mathfrak{a}}{a}=\mathfrak{b}
, which yields
\overline{\mathfrak{b}}=\mathfrak{b}
.

Conversely, let
\mathfrak{b}\in\mathcal{X}_{A}
and set
\mathfrak{a}=a\mathfrak{b}
. Then,
\mathfrak{a}\in\mathcal{F}_{R}
and
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq I
. For
x\in\overline{\mathfrak{a}}
, we take the equation

x^{n}+c_{1}x^{n-1}+\cdots+c_{n-1}x+c_{n}=0

where
n\geq 1
and
c_{i}\in\mathfrak{a}^{i}

(1\leq i\leq n)
. Then, because
x\in\overline{\mathfrak{a}}\subseteq\overline{I}=I
, we get
\frac{x}{a}\in\frac{I}{a}=A
and

\left(\frac{x}{a}\right)^{n}+\left(\frac{c_{1}}{a}\right)\left(\frac{x}{a}\right)^{n-1}+\cdots+\left(\frac{c_{n-1}}{a^{n-1}}\right)\left(\frac{x}{a}\right)+\left(\frac{c_{n}}{a^{n}}\right)=0,

where
\frac{c_{i}}{a^{i}}\in\mathfrak{b}^{i}

(1\leq i\leq n)
, which implies that
\frac{x}{a}\in\overline{\mathfrak{b}}=\mathfrak{b}
. Therefore, we have
x\in a\mathfrak{b}=\mathfrak{a}
, so that
\mathfrak{a}\in\mathcal{X}_{R}
, as desired.
∎

When
R
satisfies condition
(A)
,
R^{I}
also satisfies the condition for every
I\in\mathcal{X}_{R}
. Therefore, by using Lemma
2.1
, Theorem
2.3
, and Lemma
2.4
, we obtain the following:

Corollary 2.5
.

If
R
is an Arf ring,
R^{I}(=I:I)
is also an Arf ring for every
I\in\mathcal{X}_{R}
.

Corollary 2.6
.

If
R
is an Arf ring,
v([R^{I}]_{M})=\mathrm{e}([R^{I}]_{M})
holds for every
I\in\mathcal{X}_{R}
and
M\in\operatorname{Max}R^{I}
.

Remark 2.7
.

R^{I}
is not necessarily a local ring, even if
R
is a local ring.

We denote by
\operatorname{J}(R)
as the Jacobson radical of
R
. Then,
\operatorname{J}(R)\in\mathcal{X}_{R}
. Here, we set
R_{1}=R^{\operatorname{J}(R)}
and define recursively

R_{n}=

\begin{cases}\ R&\ \ (n=0)\\
\ [R_{n-1}]_{1}&\ \ (n>0)\\
\end{cases}

for each
n\geq 0
. Then, we get the tower

R=R_{0}\subseteq R_{1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq R_{n}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\overline{R}

of rings, and every
R_{n}
satisfies condition
(A)
. Moreover, every
R_{n}
is an Arf ring if so is
R
.

By using this tower of rings, we get another characterization of Arf rings.

Theorem 2.8

(
[
7
, Theorem 2.2]
)
.

The following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

R
is an Arf ring.

(2)

v([R_{n}]_{M})=\mathrm{e}([R_{n}]_{M})
holds for every
n\geq 0
and
M\in\operatorname{Max}R_{n}
.

Example 2.9

(
[
7
, Example, P. 664]
)
.

Let
R
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring with
\mathrm{e}(R)\leq 2
. Then,
v([R_{n}]_{M})=\mathrm{e}([R_{n}]_{M})\leq 2
holds for every
n\geq 0
and
M\in\operatorname{Max}R_{n}
. Therefore,
R
is an Arf ring according to Theorem
2.8
.

3.
Integrally closed ideals

The purpose of this section is to show the key properties of ideals in
\mathcal{X}_{R}
, which we need in this study. Throughout this section, let
R
be a Noetherian ring.
We begin with the following:

Lemma 3.1
.

I:I=(R:I)\cap\overline{R}
for every
I\in\mathcal{X}_{R}
.

Proof.

It is sufficient to show that
I:I\supseteq(R:I)\cap\overline{R}
. Let
\varphi\in\overline{R}
and
\varphi\cdot I\subseteq R
. Then, we have

a\varphi\in a\overline{R}\cap R=\overline{aR}\subseteq\overline{I}=I

for any
a\in I\cap W(R)
. Because
I
is generated by non-zerodivisors on
R
, we obtain
\varphi\cdot I\subseteq I
, which yields
\varphi\in I:I
.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
I,J\in\mathcal{X}_{R}
. If
I\subseteq J
, then
I:I\supseteq J:J
, and
I
is an ideal of the ring
J:J
.

Proof.

The inclusion
I\subseteq J
implies
R:I\supseteq R:J
, so that

I:I=(R:I)\cap\overline{R}\supseteq(R:J)\cap\overline{R}=J:J

from Lemma
3.1
. Because
I
is an ideal of
I:I
and
I:I\supseteq J:J
,
I
is also an ideal of
J:J
.
∎

The following theorem is the key to our decomposition:

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
I,J\in\mathcal{X}_{R}
and suppose that
I\subseteq J
. We set
B=J:J
. Then, the following assertions hold true:

(1)

I:J\in\mathcal{X}_{B}
.

(2)

Suppose that there exists
b\in J
such that
J^{2}=bJ
. Then,
I:J=\frac{I}{b}
. Therefore, we have
I=J\cdot(I:J)
and
I:J\cong I
as
B
-modules.

Proof.

(1)
Note that
I:J\subseteq J:J=B
and

J\cdot[B\cdot(I:J)]=JB\cdot(I:J)=J\cdot(I:J)\subseteq I.

Thus,
I:J
is an ideal of
B
. Because
I\subseteq I:J
, we have
I:J\in\mathcal{F}_{B}
.

Next, we show that
\overline{I:J}=I:J
in
B
.
This follows from the proof of
[
8
, Remark 1.3.2 (2)]
. In fact,
let
\alpha\in\overline{I:J}\subseteq B
. We take the equation

\alpha^{n}+a_{1}\alpha^{n-1}+\cdots+a_{n-1}\alpha+a_{n}=0,

where
n\geq 1
and
a_{i}\in(I:J)^{i}

(1\leq i\leq n)
. For any
x\in J
, we have
x\alpha\in JB=J\subseteq R
,

(x\alpha)^{n}+xa_{1}(x\alpha)^{n-1}+\cdots+x^{n-1}a_{n-1}(x\alpha)+x^{n}a_{n}=0\ \ \text{and}

x^{i}a_{i}\in x^{i}\cdot(I:J)^{i}\subseteq I^{i}\ \ (1\leq i\leq n),

so that
x\alpha\in\overline{I}=I
. This implies that
\alpha\cdot J\subseteq I
, which yields
\alpha\in I:J
and
\overline{I:J}=I:J
, as desired.

(2)

Suppose that
J^{2}=bJ
for some
b\in J
. Then,
J=bB
, and the inclusion
I:J\subseteq\frac{I}{b}
holds. Additionally, because
I
is an ideal of
B
by Proposition
3.2
, we have

J\cdot\frac{I}{b}=bB\cdot\frac{I}{b}=IB=I,

which yields
\frac{I}{b}=I:J
and
I=J\cdot(I:J)
. We also have
I:J=\frac{I}{b}\cong I
as
B
-modules.
∎

For any
I\in\mathcal{F}_{R}
, we have
\sqrt{I}\in\mathcal{X}_{R}
, and the following holds true:

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
I\in\mathcal{X}_{R}
. Suppose that
I\neq R
, and there exist
a,b\in W(R)
such that
I=\overline{aR}
and
\sqrt{I}=\overline{bR}
. We set
{\mathrm{H}}_{1}(R)=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}R\mid{\rm ht}_{R}\mathfrak{p}=1\}
. If
R
satisfies
(S_{2})
and
\operatorname{Ass}_{R}R/I\subseteq{\mathrm{H}}_{1}(R)
, then we have
\sqrt{I}=\underset{\mathfrak{p}\in V(I)\cap{\mathrm{H}}_{1}(R)}{\bigcap}\mathfrak{p}
, and the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

I=aR
.

(2)

\sqrt{I}=bR
.

(3)

R_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a DVR for any
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}R/I
.

Proof.

Because
\operatorname{Ass}_{R}R/I\subseteq{\mathrm{H}}_{1}(R)
, we have
\operatorname{Ass}_{R}R/I=V(I)\cap{\mathrm{H}}_{1}(R)
. Thus,
\sqrt{I}=\underset{\mathfrak{p}\in V(I)\cap{\mathrm{H}}_{1}(R)}{\bigcap}\mathfrak{p}
. By using
[
5
, Theorem 1.1]
, it is sufficient to show that
\operatorname{Ass}_{R}R/aR=\operatorname{Ass}_{R}R/I=\operatorname{Ass}_{R}R/bR
.
In fact, let
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}R/aR
. Because
\operatorname{depth}R_{\mathfrak{p}}=1
and
R
satisfies
(S_{2})
, we have
\mathfrak{p}\in{\mathrm{H}}_{1}(R)
and

\operatorname{Ass}_{R}R/aR=V(aR)\cap{\mathrm{H}}_{1}(R)=V(I)\cap{\mathrm{H}}_{1}(R)=\operatorname{Ass}_{R}R/I.

Similarly, we have

\operatorname{Ass}_{R}R/bR=\operatorname{Ass}_{R}R/\sqrt{I}=V(\sqrt{I})\cap{\mathrm{H}}_{1}(R)=V(I)\cap{\mathrm{H}}_{1}(R),

which yields
\operatorname{Ass}_{R}R/aR=\operatorname{Ass}_{R}R/I=\operatorname{Ass}_{R}R/bR
, as desired.
∎

We will use this proposition in Section 4.

4.
Main result: Equation
I=\sqrt{I_{0}}\sqrt{I_{1}}\cdots\sqrt{I_{n}}

In the following sections, we assume that
R
is an Arf ring. We fix
I\in\mathcal{X}_{R}
and set
B=I:I
. Then, if
I\neq R
, we can write

\sqrt{I}=\underset{\mathfrak{p}\in V(I)}{\bigcap}\mathfrak{p}=\underset{\mathfrak{p}\in V(I)}{\prod}\mathfrak{p}.

Definition 4.1
.

We set

R_{(I,1)}=\sqrt{I}:\sqrt{I}~~\text{and}~~I_{1}=I:\sqrt{I}.

By Corollary
2.5
and Theorem
3.3
,
R_{(I,1)}
is also an Arf ring,
I_{1}\in\mathcal{X}_{R_{(I,1)}}
, and
I=\sqrt{I}\cdot I_{1}
.
For each
n\geq 0
, we define recursively

R_{(I,n)}=\begin{cases}R&(n=0)\\
\sqrt{I_{n-1}}:\sqrt{I_{n-1}}=[R_{(I,n-1)}]_{(I_{n-1},1)}&(n>0),\end{cases}

I_{n}=\begin{cases}I&(n=0)\\
I_{n-1}:\sqrt{I_{n-1}}=[I_{n-1}]_{1}&(n>0).\end{cases}

Every
R_{(I,n)}
is also an Arf ring, and we have the tower

R=R_{(I,0)}\subseteq R_{(I,1)}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq R_{(I,n)}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\overline{R}

of Arf rings. We also have
I_{n}\in\mathcal{X}_{R_{(I,n)}}
and

I_{n}=\sqrt{I_{n}}\cdot I_{n+1}.

Note that
I_{n}
is also an ideal in
R_{(I,n+1)}
,
I_{n}\subseteq I_{n+1}
, and
I_{n}\cong I_{n+1}
as
R_{(I,n+1)}
-modules.

Summing the above discussion, we have the following:

Theorem 4.2
.

I=\left[\sqrt{I_{0}}\sqrt{I_{1}}\cdots\sqrt{I_{n}}\right]\cdot I_{n+1}
for any
n\geq 0
.

To delete
I_{n+1}
in the equation, we require the following works.

Lemma 4.3
.

I_{n}:I_{n}=I_{n+1}:I_{n+1}
and
R_{(I,n)}\subseteq B
for any
n\geq 0
.

Proof.

For any
n\geq 0
, because
I_{n}\cong I_{n+1}
as
R_{(I,n+1)}
-modules, we have
I_{n}:I_{n}=I_{n+1}:I_{n+1}
. When
n=0
, it is clear that
R_{(I,0)}=R\subseteq B
. If
n>0
, because
I_{n-1}\subseteq\sqrt{I_{n-1}}
in
R_{(I,n-1)}
, we obtain

R_{(I,n)}=\sqrt{I_{n-1}}:\sqrt{I_{n-1}}\subseteq I_{n-1}:I_{n-1}=\cdots=I_{0}:I_{0}=B

by Proposition
3.2
.
∎

Proposition 4.4
.

We can choose
n\geq 0
such that
R_{(I,n)}=R_{(I,\ell)}
for every
\ell\geq n
.

Proof.

This follows from Lemma
4.3
, because
B
is a Noetherian
R
-module.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

For each
n\geq 0
, the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

R_{(I,n)}=R_{(I,n+1)}
.

(2)

I_{n}
is a principal ideal of
R_{(I,n)}
.

(3)

\sqrt{I_{n}}
is a principal ideal of
R_{(I,n)}
.

Proof.

For each
n\geq 0
, we can take
a_{n}\in W(R_{(I,n)})
such that
\sqrt{I_{n}}=\overline{a_{n}R_{(I,n)}}
, because
R_{(I,n)}
is an Arf ring. Then, we have

R_{(I,n+1)}=\sqrt{I_{n}}:\sqrt{I_{n}}=\frac{\sqrt{I_{n}}}{a_{n}}.

Therefore,
R_{(I,n)}=R_{(I,n+1)}
if and only if
\sqrt{I_{n}}=a_{n}R_{(I,n)}
, which implies that
(1)\Leftrightarrow(3)
.
(2)\Leftrightarrow(3)
follows from Proposition
3.4
.
∎

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
n\geq 0
. If
R_{(I,n)}=R_{(I,n+1)}
, then
R_{(I,\ell)}=B
for any
\ell\geq n
.

Proof.

Suppose that
R_{(I,n)}=R_{(I,n+1)}
. Then,
I_{n}\cong R_{(I,n)}=R_{(I,n+1)}
, because
I_{n}
is a principal ideal of
R_{(I,n)}
by Lemma
4.5
.
Therefore, we have

R_{(I,n)}=R_{(I,n)}:R_{(I,n)}=I_{n}:I_{n}=B.

In contrast, because
I_{n}\cong I_{n+1}
as
R_{(I,n+1)}
-modules, we also have
I_{n+1}\cong R_{(I,n+1)}
as
R_{(I,n+1)}
-modules, which implies that
R_{(I,n+1)}=R_{(I,n+2)}
by Lemma
4.5
. Thus, we obtain
R_{(I,\ell)}=R_{(I,n)}=B
for all
\ell\geq n
.
∎

We then have the following:

Theorem 4.7
.

We can choose
n\geq 0
such that
I_{\ell}=R_{(I,\ell)}=B
for every
\ell\geq n
.

Proof.

We take
m\geq 0
such that
R_{(I,m)}=R_{(I,m+1)}
. Then,
R_{(I,\ell)}=B
for any
\ell\geq m
by Proposition
4.6
. Additionally, because
I_{\ell}=\sqrt{I_{\ell}}\cdot I_{\ell+1}
, we have

I_{m}\subseteq I_{m+1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq I_{\ell}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq B.

If
I_{\ell}\subsetneq B
for every
\ell\geq m
, we also have
\sqrt{I_{\ell}}\subsetneq B
, so that

I_{\ell}=\sqrt{I_{\ell}}\cdot I_{\ell+1}\subsetneq I_{\ell+1}.

This is impossible, because
B
is a Noetherian ring.
∎

Consequently, we get the following, which is the main result of this paper.

Corollary 4.8
.

We can choose
n\geq 0
such that
I=\sqrt{I_{0}}\sqrt{I_{1}}\cdots\sqrt{I_{n}}
.

Proof.

We choose
n\geq 0
such that
I_{\ell}=R_{(I,\ell)}=B
for every
\ell\geq n
. Then, because
\sqrt{I_{n}}
is an ideal of
B
and
I_{n+1}=B
, we have

I=\left[\sqrt{I_{0}}\sqrt{I_{1}}\cdots\sqrt{I_{n}}\right]\cdot I_{n+1}=\left[\sqrt{I_{0}}\sqrt{I_{1}}\cdots\sqrt{I_{n}}\right]\cdot B=\sqrt{I_{0}}\sqrt{I_{1}}\cdots\sqrt{I_{n}}.

∎

Remark 4.9
.

Suppose
I\neq R
. We choose
n\geq 0
such that
I_{\ell}=R_{(I,\ell)}=B
for every
\ell\geq n
and set

\mathcal{S}=\{i\geq 0\mid I_{i}\subsetneq R_{(I,i)}\}
.
Then,
i<n
holds for any
i\in\mathcal{S}
, and we have

\mathcal{S}=\{0,1,\ldots,q\}

where
q=\max\mathcal{S}
. Hence, we have
\sqrt{I_{i}}\subsetneq R_{(I,i)}
and

\sqrt{I_{i}}=\underset{\mathfrak{p}\in V(I_{i})}{\prod}\mathfrak{p}

for any
0\leq i\leq q
. Therefore, corresponding to the fact that every non-zero ideal in a DVR is the power of the maximal ideal, every ideal in
\mathcal{X}_{R}
can be described as the products of maximal ideals in rings
R
,
R_{(I,1)},R_{(I,2)},\cdots,R_{(I,q)}
.

5.
Case where
R
and
\overline{R}
are local rings

In this section, we study in detail the local case of Corollary
4.8
. We assume that
(R,\mathfrak{m})
is an Arf local ring, and the integral closure
\overline{R}
is also a local ring.
For each
i\geq 0
, we set

A_{i}=\begin{cases}R&\ \ (i=0)\\
A_{i-1}^{{\mathfrak{m}}_{i-1}}&\ \ (i>0)\end{cases}

where
{\mathfrak{m}}_{i}=\operatorname{J}(A_{i})
and
{\mathfrak{m}}_{0}={\mathfrak{m}}
.
Then, every
A_{i}
is also a local ring with the maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}_{i}
.

Let
I\in\mathcal{X}_{R}
and assume that
I\neq R
. For simplicity, we write
R_{(I,i)}=R_{i}
. Then, because
\sqrt{I}=\mathfrak{m}
, we have

R_{1}=\mathfrak{m}:\mathfrak{m}=A_{1}.

We choose
q\geq 0
such that
\mathcal{S}=\{i\geq 0\mid I_{i}\subsetneq R_{i}\}=\{0,1,\ldots,q\}
. Then, we also have the following:

Lemma 5.1
.

R_{i}=A_{i}
for any
i\in\mathcal{S}
.

Proof.

Because
\overline{R}
is a local ring,
R_{i}
is also a local ring for every
i\geq 0
. Therefore, for each
1\leq i\leq q
, we have

V(I_{i})=\operatorname{Max}R_{i}=\{\mathfrak{m}_{i}\},

which yields
R_{i}=\mathfrak{m}_{i-1}:\mathfrak{m}_{i-1}=A_{i}
.
∎

Therefore,
\sqrt{I_{i}}=\mathfrak{m}_{i}\in\operatorname{Max}R_{i}
for each
0\leq i\leq q
and
I_{\ell}=R_{\ell}
for any
\ell\geq q+1
. By combining this with Corollary
4.8
, we immediately obtain the following:

Theorem 5.2
.

I=\mathfrak{m}_{0}\mathfrak{m}_{1}\cdots\mathfrak{m}_{q}
.

Remark 5.3
.

The decomposition of Theorem
5.2
can be considered to be a generalization of the properties of DVRs for integrally closed ideals in Arf rings. Indeed, suppose that
R
is integrally closed. Then, with our assumption,
R
is a DVR (of course, an Arf ring). It is well known that every nonzero ideal
I
can be written as
I=\mathfrak{m}^{q+1}
, where
q=\max\mathcal{S}
. This is the case where
\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{m}_{0}=\mathfrak{m}_{1}=\cdots=\mathfrak{m}_{q}
in Theorem
5.2
.

We assume that
\overline{R}
is a finitely generated
R
-module. Then,
\overline{R}
is a DVR. Because
R
is an Arf ring, we have

\underset{i\geq 0}{\bigcup}A_{i}=\overline{R}

using Theorem
2.8
. Therefore, there exists an integer
N\geq 0
such that

R=A_{0}\subseteq A_{1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq A_{N}=\overline{R}.

Let
I\in\mathcal{X}_{R}
and assume that
I\neq R
. We set
q=\max\mathcal{S}
. If
q\leq N
, then
I=\mathfrak{m}_{0}\mathfrak{m}_{1}\cdots\mathfrak{m}_{q}
, so that there are only finite possibilities for
I
.

Suppose that
q>N
. Then,
R_{i}=A_{i}=A_{N}=\overline{R}
for any
N\leq i\leq q
, so that

B=I:I=R_{q}=\overline{R}.

Therefore, because
I
is also an ideal of
\overline{R}
, we have

I=\mathfrak{m}_{0}\cdots\mathfrak{m}_{N-1}\mathfrak{m}_{N}^{q+1-N}=a\overline{R}

for some
a\in I
.

Consequently, we can completely describe every
I\in\mathcal{X}_{R}
when
\overline{R}
is a finitely generated
R
-module. We obtain the following corollary:

Corollary 5.4
.

Suppose that
\overline{R}
is a finitely generated
R
-module. Then, the set

\{I\in\mathcal{X}_{R}\mid I~~\text{is not an ideal of}~~\overline{R}\}

is finite.

Closing this paper, let us note an example:

Example 5.5
.

Let
V=k[[t]]
be the formal power-series ring over a field
k
, and let
R=k[[t^{3},t^{11},t^{13}]]
. Then,
R
is an Arf ring, and

R=A_{0}\subsetneq A_{1}=k[[t^{3},t^{8},t^{10}]]\subsetneq A_{2}=k[[t^{3},t^{5},t^{7}]]\subsetneq A_{3}=k[[t^{2},t^{3}]]\subsetneq A_{4}=V.

Let
\mathfrak{m}_{i}
be the maximal ideal of
A_{i}
, and let
\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{m}_{0}
. We set
I=\overline{t^{6}R}
. Then,
I=(t^{6},t^{11},t^{13})
, and we have

\sqrt{I}=\mathfrak{m}=(t^{3},t^{11},t^{13}),

I_{1}=I:\mathfrak{m}=\frac{I}{t^{3}}=_{R}\langle t^{3},t^{8},t^{10}\rangle=_{R_{1}}\langle t^{3},t^{8},t^{10}\rangle=\mathfrak{m}_{1}=\sqrt{I_{1}},

R_{1}=\sqrt{I}:\sqrt{I}=\mathfrak{m}:\mathfrak{m}=A_{1},

I_{2}=\mathfrak{m}_{1}:\mathfrak{m}_{1}=\sqrt{I_{1}}:\sqrt{I_{1}}=R_{2}=A_{2},

where
{}_{R}\langle t^{3},t^{8},t^{10}\rangle
denotes an
R
-module generated by
t^{3},t^{8},t^{10}
.
This means
q=1
. Because
I_{2}=R_{2}
, we also have

R_{2}=R_{3}=\cdots=B~(=I:I),

and

I=(t^{6},t^{11},t^{13})=_{R}\langle t^{3},t^{11},t^{13}\rangle\cdot_{R}\langle t^{3},t^{8},t^{10}\rangle=_{R}\langle t^{3},t^{11},t^{13}\rangle\cdot_{R_{1}}\langle t^{3},t^{8},t^{10}\rangle=\mathfrak{m}_{0}\mathfrak{m}_{1}.

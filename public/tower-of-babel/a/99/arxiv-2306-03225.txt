Skein (3+1)-TQFTs from non-semisimple ribbon categories.
Francesco Costantino, Nathan Geer, Benjamin Haïoun, Bertrand Patureau-Mirand

Abstract
. Using skein theory very much in the spirit of the Reshetikhin–Turaev constructions, we define a ( 3 + 1 ) (3+1) -TQFT associated with possibly non-semisimple finite unimodular ribbon tensor categories. State spaces are given by admissible skein modules, and we prescribe the TQFT on handle attachments. We give some explicit algebraic conditions on the category to define this TQFT, namely to be “chromatic non-degenerate”. As a by-product, we obtain an invariant of 4-manifolds equipped with a ribbon graph in their boundary, and in the “twist non-degenerate” case, an invariant of 3-manifolds. Our construction generalizes the Crane–Yetter–Kauffman TQFTs in the semi-simple case, and the Lyubashenko (hence also Hennings and WRT) invariants of 3-manifolds. The whole construction is very elementary, and we can easily characterize invertibility of the TQFTs, study their behavior under connected sum and provide some examples.



Introduction

Since Atiyah’s pioneering paper
[
Ati88
]
, the construction of Topological Quantum Field Theories (TQFTs) of smooth compact oriented manifolds has flourished. Many of the results in this area apply to manifold of dimension
3
and
(2+1)
-dimensional cobordisms.
Some of the milestones in this dimension include Turaev’s
[
Tur94
]
general TQFT construction associated to a semi-simple modular category (which is an extension of the Witten-Reshetikhin-Turaev 3-manifold invariant) and the construction of a TQFT associated to the Kauffman bracket skein algebra
[
BHMV95
]
. Related constructions have been defined for non-semisimple categories, see for example
[
KL01
,
BCGP16
,
DGGPR22
,
CGPV23a
]
. These non-semisimple TQFTs have new properties, often proving more powerful than their semi-simple analogs. For example, non-semisimple TQFTs have shown to distinguish diffeomorphism types of homotopically equivalent lens spaces which were not distinguished by semi-simple TQFTs.

Less research has been done in dimension
4
. A notable exception is due to Crane and Yetter
[
CY93
]

who defined invariants of closed smooth compact oriented
4
-manifolds associated to any semi-simple modular category. These invariants determine (3+1)-TQFTs (which we will denote CY) which for simply connected manifolds, only depend on the Euler characteristic of the manifold and its signature.
This implies that the invariant can not distinguish exotic pairs of 4-manifolds, i.e. pairs of homeomorphic but non diffeomorphic manifolds.

This construction was generalized to certain non-modular semi-simple categories by Crane, Kauffman and Yetter
[
CKY97
]
. In
[
BB18
]
, Bärentz and Barrett generalize the CKY construction to pairs of finite semi-simple categories
\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
(the CKY construction corresponds to the inclusion of
\mathcal{C}
in its Drinfeld center). The underlying 4-manifold invariants of these TQFTs, at least in a broad list of cases, are conjecturally related to the Euler characteristic, the signature and the fundamental group, see
[
BB18
, Conjecture 8.1]
.

A different kind of quantum topology construction in dimension 4 has
recently been defined by Beliakova and De Renzi in

[
BD23
,
BD22
]
.
They consider
4
-manifolds which admit a handle decomposition without

3
and
4
-handles, and considered up to a suitable
2
-equivalence
relations. In some sense, the TQFT of this paper might be seen as
an extension of their work to include higher index handle (but using
different methods and reversing the labeling of the index of handle decomposition).

Let us now explain obstructions for the constructions discussed above to detect exotic pairs of 4-manifolds.

First, extending a previous result of Wall, in
[
Gom84
]
Gompf showed that two compact orientable
4
-manifolds (possibly with boundary) which are homeomorphic, become diffeomorphic after some finite sequence of connected sums with
S^{2}\times S^{2}
. Therefore, a
(3+1)
-TQFT which is multiplicative under connected sum, has a hope to distinguish exotic pairs only if the numerical invariant it associates to
S^{2}\times S^{2}
is not invertible.
A similar restriction is that if one stabilizes a closed simply connected
4
-manifold by a sequence of connected sums with
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
or
\overline{\mathbb{CP}^{2}}
then it can make it diffeomorphic to a connected sum of the form
\#_{m}\mathbb{CP}^{2}\#_{n}\overline{\mathbb{CP}^{2}}
for some
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
. Therefore, again, in order for a
(3+1)
-TQFT to have interesting invariants on closed simply-connected
4
-manifolds, it needs to have non-invertible values on
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
and
\overline{\mathbb{CP}^{2}}
.

Furthermore, given a
(3+1)
-TQFT
Z
then both
Z(S^{3})
and
Z(S^{2}\times S^{1})
can be endowed with the structure of commutative Frobenius algebras (this is done in a similar to the standard approach in dimension
(1+1)
).
In
[
Reu23
]
, Reutter showed that a
4
-dimensional TQFT which is “semisimple” (i.e. for which
Z(S^{3})
and
Z(S^{2}\times S^{1})
are semi-simple algebras) cannot detect exotic pairs.
Finally, we mention that Schommer-Pries in
[
Sch17
, Theorem 7.6]
shows that if
Z
is invertible (i.e.
Z(M)
is
1
-dimensional for each
3
-manifold
M
and
Z(W)
is an invertible linear map for each 4-dimensional cobordism
W
) then its
4
-dimensional invariant only depends on the Euler characteristic and the first Pontryagin number (for instance CY theories are all invertible).

In this paper we construct 3-manifold invariants and (3+1)-TQFT, which
have different qualities depending on the underlying category, see
Figure
1
.
Our main algebraic tool is a chromatic category, which loosely
speaking, is a
{\Bbbk}
-linear pivotal category with a modified trace and
a chromatic map. For example, any finite tensor category (in the
sense of
[
EGNO15
]
) has a structure of a chromatic category.
Spherical chromatic categories were used in
[
CGPV23a
]
to
produce non-compact
(2+1)
-TQFTs.

In Section 4,
we show that a
twist non-degenerate
ribbon chromatic category
\mathcal{C}
gives rise to a 3-manifold invariant
\mathrm{B}_{\mathcal{C}}
.
Moreover, we show that this construction includes and generalizes many well known invariants: a) WRT-invariants
[
RT91
]
(when
\mathcal{C}
is semi-simple modular), b) the (modified) Hennings invariants
[
Hen96
,
DGP18
]
(when
\mathcal{C}
is the category of representations over a finite dimensional, unimodular ribbon Hopf algebra), and c) the (modified) Lyubashenko invariants
[
Lyu95
,
DGGPR22
]
(when
\mathcal{C}
is a finite ribbon tensor category). The construction of
\mathrm{B}_{\mathcal{C}}
is very similar to that of Reshetikhin-Turaev invariants with the chromatic map playing the role of a non-semisimple Kirby color.

Then in Section 5, we build a
(3+1)
-TQFT from
a ribbon chromatic category
\mathcal{C}
with certain properties.
The idea is to use work of Juhász
[
Juh18
]
which allows one to build a TQFT by associating a vector space to each closed
3
-manifold (all manifolds are smooth from now on) and a set of operators associated to
4
-cobordisms formed by a single handle of index
k
for each
0\leq k\leq 4
(see Section
3
for details).
We use the vector space of admissible skeins introduced in
[
CGP22
]
as value for the TQFT on closed
3
-manifolds. In order to define the maps associated to handles of index
k
we need to progressively add some structure or restrictions on the category
\mathcal{C}
, when
k
starts from
4
and ranges down to
0
. Schematically:

(4)

To each
4
-handle we associate a linear map
F^{\prime}
on the skein module of
S^{3}
whose existence is equivalent to that of a
modified trace
on the ideal
of projective objects of
\mathcal{C}
, see
[
CGP22
, Theorem 3.1]
.

(3)

To each
3
-handle we associate the
cutting map
first introduced in
[
CGPT20
]
and already used in
[
CGPV23a
]
, whose existence imposes a non-degeneracy condition on the modified trace.

(2)

To each
2
-handle we associate a surgery over a link via a
chromatic map
. The existence of such a map is proved in
[
CGPV23b
]
under suitable finiteness conditions on
\mathcal{C}
, even for spherical categories, but we reprove it here when
\mathcal{C}
is ribbon and finite (see Subsection
1.7
).

(1)

To each
1
-handle we associate a new kind of morphism called the
gluing map
, whose existence we prove is equivalent to a non-degeneracy condition on the chromatic map and the double braiding. When
\mathcal{C}
satisfies this condition we shall say it is
chromatic non-degenerate
(see Definition
1.7
).

(0)

To each
0
-handle we associate a standard non-zero vector in the skein module of
S^{3}
. We only consider
0
-handles when
\mathcal{C}
is
chromatic compact
(see Definition
1.7
).

As already mentioned, the
(3+1)
-TQFT of this paper has different qualities depending on
the new notions of the category
\mathcal{C}
, see Figure
1
.
The most general notion we
consider is called a
chromatic non-degenerate
category.

Theorem
5.4
says such a category gives rise to a non-compact
(3+1)
-TQFT. Here non-compact means we only
consider cobordisms whose connected components all have non-empty sources and so we do not consider
0
-handles. Therefore this construction associates morphisms to
k
-handles,
for
k=1,2,3,4
as described above. To extend this TQFT to
all cobordisms the category must satisfy a stronger condition, namely it should be

chromatic compact
. We show that if we make further
restrictions on the category we can characterize the corresponding
TQFTs: the
(3+1)
-TQFT is invertible if and only if
\mathcal{C}
is

factorizable
which is a generalization of factorizability of
finite ribbon tensor categories, see Theorem
5.8
.

In Section
6
we consider several examples. First, in Subsection
6.1
we consider the semi-simple case.
Then in Subsection
6.2
we make some computation for the TQFT associated to finite
dimensional versions of quantum
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
at root of unity. In many
cases, the associated category is shown to be chromatic compact but not
factorizable. At

8r
th
root of unity, the category is twist
degenerate and
{\mathscr{S}}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbb{CP}^{2})=0
but even then,

{\mathscr{S}}_{\mathcal{C}}(S^{2}\times S^{2})\neq 0
, so this example cannot detect
exotic pairs. But it is our hope that other examples of categories

\mathcal{C}
might yield interesting invariants through our construction.
Finally, in Subsection
6.3
, we conclude with a toy example
in characteristic
p
showing the existence of chromatic
non-degenerate categories which fail to be chromatic compact.

It is natural to ask if
the construction of this paper is an explicit construction in dimension four of the work of Kevin Walker and David Reutter announced in
[
Wal21
]
. Indeed we expect that the TQFTs we describe can be extended down to the point using skein theory. It has been shown by Brochier, Jordan, Safronov and Snyder in
[
BJSS21
]
that a (possibly non-semisimple) modular category
\mathcal{C}
is fully dualizable as an object of the Morita 4-category of braided tensor categories. We expect that our construction recovers the 3-4-part of the fully extended theory associated with
\mathcal{C}
by the Cobordism Hypothesis. It is also expected there that a finite tensor category with semi-simple Müger center is fully dualizable, and this seems to correspond to our chromatic compact case.

A particularity of skein TQFTs is that vectors in the TQFT space are
represented by
\mathcal{C}
-colored links or ribbon graphs in 3-manifolds.
Hence the abstract TQFT comes with an invariant of pair
(W,T)
where

T
is a
\mathcal{C}
-colored links or ribbon graph in the boundary of
W
. We show in Theorem
5.9
that if the category is twist
non-degenerate, this invariant recovers
\mathrm{B}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{C}}
. This invariant might be much stronger than the TQFT itself. Indeed the invertible CY TQFTs are known to only depend on two complex numbers
[
Sch17
, Theorem 7.6]
, but given the empty skein they recover the WRT invariant of the boundary of a 4-manifold. This extra data corresponds to a boundary condition for the TQFT. It was conjectured in
[
Haï23
]
that such a boundary condition (in a fully extended setting) on the invertible TQFT associated with a non-semisimple modular category will recover the non-semisimple (2+1)-TQFT from
[
DGGPR22
]
. Assuming the expectations above, Theorem
5.9
can be seen as a partial confirmation of this conjecture.

One advantage of the techniques of this paper is that they are based on algebraic data which is easy to formulate with low-level technology using monoidal categories, in particular the notions and properties of non-degenerate modified trace and chromatic morphisms.
These properties are easily represented graphically and most of our proofs reduce to diagrammatic ones.

Hypothesis on the category

Throughout this paper, we fix an algebraically closed field
{\Bbbk}
and
an essentially small strict ribbon
{\Bbbk}
-linear
category
\mathcal{C}
. We do not need
\mathcal{C}
to be finite abelian but we
assume that it has finite dimensional hom-spaces, is additive,
idempotent complete, has a simple unit
\mathds{1}
such that any non-zero
morphism to
\mathds{1}
is an epimorphism, and admits a non-zero projective
generator.

We will also require that
\mathcal{C}
is a chromatic category i.e. it has a
non-degenerate m-trace and that there exists a chromatic morphism for a projective generator (see the
next section for the definitions).

1.
Algebraic setting

1.1.
Non-degenerate m-traces

We use the notation and terminology of
[
CGP22
]
. In particular,

\mathcal{C}
is a braided category with braiding

c_{V,W}:V\otimes W\to W\otimes V
and a compatible pivotal structure
which associates to each
V\in\mathcal{C}
an object
V^{*}\in\mathcal{C}
and the
(co)evaluation morphisms
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\longleftarrow}}{{\operatorname{ev}}}\!\!_{V},\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\longleftarrow}}{{\operatorname{coev}}}_{V},\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\longrightarrow}}{{\operatorname{ev}}}\!\!_{V},\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\longrightarrow}}{{\operatorname{coev}}}_{V}
which are
related by a canonical isomorphism
\phi_{V}:V\to V^{**}
. We consider

\mathcal{C}
-colored ribbon graphs in oriented manifolds. These ribbon
graphs may have coupons which we denote by a box filled with a morphism.

Let
\operatorname{Proj}
be the ideal of projective objects of
\mathcal{C}
. A

non-degenerate m-trace on
\operatorname{Proj}
is family of linear maps

{\operatorname{\mathsf{t}}}=\{{\operatorname{\mathsf{t}}}_{P}:\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(P)\to{\Bbbk}\}_{P\in\operatorname{Proj}}
satisfying the
following properties:

(1)

Cyclicity:
For all
U,V\in\operatorname{Proj}
and morphisms

V\xrightarrow{f}U,\,U\xrightarrow{g}V
, we have
\operatorname{\mathsf{t}}_{V}(gf)=\operatorname{\mathsf{t}}_{U}(fg)
.

(2)

Right partial trace:
If

U\in\operatorname{Proj}
and
W\in\mathcal{C}
, then for any

f\in\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(U\otimes W)
,

(1)

\operatorname{\mathsf{t}}_{U\otimes W}{\left(f\right)}=\operatorname{\mathsf{t}}_{U}\big((\operatorname{Id}_{U}\otimes\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\longrightarrow}}{{\operatorname{ev}}}\!\!_{W})(f\otimes\operatorname{Id}_{W^{*}})(\operatorname{Id}_{U}\otimes\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\longleftarrow}}{{\operatorname{coev}}}_{W})\big).

(3)

non-degeneracy
: For any
P\in\operatorname{Proj}
, the pairing

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathds{1},P)\otimes_{\Bbbk}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(P,\mathds{1})\to{\Bbbk}
given by

(x,y)\mapsto{\operatorname{\mathsf{t}}}_{P}(x\circ y)
is non-degenerate.

Note since
\mathcal{C}
is ribbon the m-trace also satisfies the left
partial trace property similar to Equation
(
1
).

Let
F
be the Reshetikhin-Turaev functor (R-T functor from now on) from the category of
\mathcal{C}
-ribbon graphs
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[0,1]
to
\mathcal{C}
, see
[
Tur94
]
.
Let
\mathcal{L}_{adm}
be the class of all
\mathcal{C}
-colored ribbon graphs in

S^{3}
obtained as the braid closure of a (1,1)-ribbon graph
T_{V}

whose open edge is colored with an object
V\in\operatorname{Proj}
. Then the
assignment
F^{\prime}
given by

F^{\prime}:\mathcal{L}_{adm}\to\Bbbk\text{ given by }F^{\prime}(L)={\operatorname{\mathsf{t}}}_{V}(F(T_{V}))

is an isotopy invariant of
L
in
S^{3}
(see
[
GPT09
,
GP18
]
).

1.2.
Copairing

For any
P\in\operatorname{Proj}
, we set

(2)

\Omega_{P}=\sum_{i}x^{i}\otimes_{\Bbbk}x_{i}\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(P,\mathds{1})\otimes_{\Bbbk}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathds{1},P)\quad\text{and}\quad\Lambda_{P}=\sum_{i}x_{i}\circ x^{i}\in\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(P),

where
\{x^{i}\}_{i}
and
\{x_{i}\}_{i}
are basis of
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(P,\mathds{1})
and

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathds{1},P)
which are dual with respect to the m-trace, that
is, such that
{\operatorname{\mathsf{t}}}_{P}(x_{i}\circ x^{j})=\delta_{i,j}
. Clearly,
\Omega_{P}

and
\Lambda_{P}
are independent of the choice of such dual basis.

The properties of the
m
-trace translate to the copairings
\Omega_{P}

as follows:

Lemma 1.1
.

(1)

Duality: If
\Omega_{P}=\sum_{i}x^{i}\otimes x_{i}
, then

\Omega_{P^{*}}=\sum_{i}x_{i}^{*}\otimes(x^{i})^{*}\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(P^{*},\mathds{1})\otimes_{\Bbbk}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathds{1},P^{*}).

(2)

Naturality: If
f:P\to Q
is a morphism in
\operatorname{Proj}
,
\Omega_{P}=\sum_{i}x^{i}\otimes x_{i}
, and

\Omega_{Q}=\sum_{i}y^{i}\otimes y_{i}
, then

\sum_{i}x^{i}\otimes(f\circ x_{i})=\sum_{i}(y^{i}\circ f)\otimes y_{i}\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(P,\mathds{1})\otimes_{\Bbbk}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathds{1},Q).

(3)

Rotation: If
V\in\mathcal{C}
and
\Omega_{P\otimes V}=\sum_{i}z^{i}\otimes z_{i}

then
\Omega_{V\otimes P}=\sum_{i}\widetilde{z}^{i}\otimes\widetilde{z}_{i}
where

\widetilde{z}_{i}=(\operatorname{Id}\otimes\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\longrightarrow}}{{\operatorname{ev}}}\!\!_{V})\circ(\operatorname{Id}\otimes z_{i}\otimes\operatorname{Id})\circ(\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\longleftarrow}}{{\operatorname{coev}}}_{V})\quad\text{and}\quad\widetilde{z}^{i}=(\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\longleftarrow}}{{\operatorname{ev}}}\!\!_{V})\circ(\operatorname{Id}\otimes z^{i}\otimes\operatorname{Id})\circ(\operatorname{Id}\otimes\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\longrightarrow}}{{\operatorname{coev}}}_{V}).

1.5.
Statement of main results

Definition 1.7
.

We say that

(1)

\mathcal{C}
is
twist non-degenerate
if

\Delta_{+}\Delta_{-}\neq 0
,

(2)

\mathcal{C}
is
chromatic non-degenerate
if

\Delta_{0}^{P_{\mathds{1}}}\neq 0
, i.e. if
\mathcal{C}
admits a gluing
morphism,

(3)

\mathcal{C}
is
chromatic compact
if there exists a scalar

\zeta\in{\Bbbk}^{*}
such that

\Delta_{0}^{P_{\mathds{1}}}=\zeta\Lambda_{P_{\mathds{1}}}
,

(4)

\mathcal{C}
is
factorizable
if there exists a scalar

\zeta\in{\Bbbk}^{*}
such that for any projective
P
,

\Delta_{0}^{P}=\zeta\Lambda_{P}
.

We will see in Section
1.7
that the notion of factorizable
and twist non-degenerate coincide with the usual ones in finite tensor
categories.

Clearly, factorizable

\implies
chromatic compact (with the same
scalar
\zeta
)
\implies
chromatic non-degenerate. We will also see in Lemma
2.6

that factorizable

\implies
twist non-degenerate with

\Delta_{+}\Delta_{-}=\zeta
.

The main constructions of this paper are the following:

(1)

If
\mathcal{C}
is twist non-degenerate, there exists 3-manifold
invariants
\mathrm{B}_{\mathcal{C}}
and
\mathrm{B}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{C}}
(see Theorem

4.1
) that generalize many quantum invariants defined
through link surgery.

(2)

If
\mathcal{C}
is chromatic non-degenerate, there exists a non-compact (3+1)-TQFT
{\mathscr{S}}_{\mathcal{C}}
(see Theorem
5.4
) whose vector
spaces are the admissible skein modules of 3-manifolds.

(3)

If

\mathcal{C}
is chromatic compact, then
{\mathscr{S}}_{\mathcal{C}}
extends to a full (3+1)-TQFT (see Theorem
5.4
).

(4)

If
\mathcal{C}
is factorizable, then
{\mathscr{S}}_{\mathcal{C}}
is an invertible
(3+1)-TQFT (see Theorem
5.8
).

Remark 1.8
.

The name
chromatic compact
refers to the fact that
{\mathscr{S}}_{\mathcal{C}}
extends from a non-compact TQFT (i.e. one defined only cobordisms whose components have non-empty source) to a
full TQFT, i.e. one defined
on all cobordisms.

1.6.
Existence of gluing morphisms

The proof of the existence of a gluing morphism as stated in Proposition

1.6
is a direct consequence of the last statement of
the following lemma:

Lemma 1.9
.

With our hypothesis on
\mathcal{C}
, we have:

(1)

For any non-zero morphism
P\xrightarrow{f}\mathds{1}
where
P
is
projective, there exists
an
epimorphism
\widetilde{f}:P\to P_{\mathds{1}}
with

f=\varepsilon\circ\widetilde{f}
.

(2)

P_{\mathds{1}}\simeq P_{\mathds{1}}^{*}
, i.e.
\mathcal{C}
is unimodular.

(3)

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(P_{\mathds{1}},\mathds{1})={\Bbbk}\varepsilon
and
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathds{1},P_{\mathds{1}})={\Bbbk}\eta
.

(4)

\Lambda_{P_{\mathds{1}}}=\eta\circ\varepsilon
.

(5)

For any
f\in\operatorname{End}(P_{\mathds{1}})
,
f
is nilpotent if and only if

\Lambda_{P_{\mathds{1}}}\circ f=0
.

(6)

For any non-zero endomorphism
f\in\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(P_{\mathds{1}})
, there exists

g\in\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(P_{\mathds{1}})
with
g\circ f=\Lambda_{P_{\mathds{1}}}
.

Proof.

First remark that since
{\Bbbk}
is algebraically closed (so

{\Bbbk}
is the unique finite dimensional division
{\Bbbk}
-algebra) and
since
P_{\mathds{1}}
is indecomposable, then by the Fitting Lemma (see

[
DK94
]
) we have
\operatorname{End}_{\Bbbk}(P_{\mathds{1}})={\Bbbk}\operatorname{Id}\oplus J

where
J
is the Jacobson radical which is a
nilpotent ideal formed by the nilpotent endomorphism of

P_{\mathds{1}}
.

(1)

For any nilpotent
n\in\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(P_{\mathds{1}})
, if

\varepsilon\circ n\neq 0
, then
\varepsilon\circ n
is an epimorphism and since

P_{\mathds{1}}
is projective,
\varepsilon
factors through it:

\varepsilon=\varepsilon\circ n\circ g
for some
g\in\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(P_{\mathds{1}})
. But

n\circ g
belongs to
J
which is nilpotent thus

\varepsilon=\varepsilon\circ(n\circ g)^{\dim_{\Bbbk}(J)}=0

and we have a contradiction. Then the kernel of

\varepsilon\circ\_:\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(P_{\mathds{1}})\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(P_{\mathds{1}},\mathds{1})

contains
J
which is a maximal left
ideal so it is equal to
J
. Now if
\varepsilon^{\prime}:P\to\mathds{1}
is non-zero, then it is an epimorphism and since
P,P_{\mathds{1}}
are
projective, there exist
P\xrightarrow{f}P_{\mathds{1}},P_{\mathds{1}}\xrightarrow{g}P
such that

\varepsilon^{\prime}=\varepsilon f
,
\varepsilon=\varepsilon^{\prime}g=\varepsilon fg
. In particular since
fg
is not
nilpotent, it is an isomorphism and so
f
is an epimorphism which
is split since
P_{\mathds{1}}
is projective.

(2)

Apply the previous to
P_{\mathds{1}}^{*}\xrightarrow{\eta^{*}}\mathds{1}
implies
that
P_{\mathds{1}}
is a direct summand of
P_{\mathds{1}}^{*}
which is
indecomposable so
P_{\mathds{1}}\simeq P_{\mathds{1}}^{*}
.

(3)

If
\varepsilon^{\prime}:P_{\mathds{1}}\to\mathds{1}
is non-zero, then there is a split
isomorphism
P_{\mathds{1}}\xrightarrow{f}P_{\mathds{1}}
such that
\varepsilon^{\prime}=\varepsilon f
. Write

f=\lambda\operatorname{Id}+n
with
n
nilpotent and
\lambda\in{\Bbbk}
then,

\varepsilon^{\prime}=\lambda\varepsilon+\varepsilon n=\lambda\varepsilon
. Hence

\operatorname{Hom}(P_{\mathds{1}},\varepsilon)={\Bbbk}\varepsilon
. Then

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathds{1},P_{\mathds{1}})\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(P_{\mathds{1}}^{*},\mathds{1})\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(P_{\mathds{1}},\mathds{1})
also
has dimension
1
so it is generated by
\eta
.

(4)

This follows since
\{\varepsilon\}
and
\{\eta\}
are dual basis.

(5)

From the proof of (1), we have for any

f\in\operatorname{End}(P_{\mathds{1}})
,
f
is nilpotent if and only if

\varepsilon\circ f=0
. Since
\eta
is a monomorphism,
f
is nilpotent
if and only if
\eta\circ\varepsilon\circ f=0
.

(6)

The symmetric pairing
(f,g)\mapsto{\operatorname{\mathsf{t}}}_{P_{\mathds{1}}}(fg)
is
non-degenerate on
\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(P_{\mathds{1}})
. From (5),

{\Bbbk}\Lambda_{P_{\mathds{1}}}
is the orthogonal of
J
. Recall that
J

is nilpotent and let
k=\max\{n\in\mathbb{N}:J^{n}f\neq 0\}
and let

g\in J^{k}
be such that
gf\neq 0
. Then
Jgf=0
thus
gf
is
orthogonal to
J
and
gf\in{\Bbbk}^{*}\Lambda_{P_{\mathds{1}}}
. Up to
rescaling
g
, we thus have
gf=\Lambda_{P_{\mathds{1}}}
.

∎

1.7.
Existence of m-traces and chromatic morphisms

In this subsection we discuss some sufficient conditions for the existence of chromatic maps as well as some cases when
\mathcal{C}
is chromatic compact.

Tensor categories:
In
[
EGNO15
]
a
tensor category

\mathcal{T}
is defined as a locally
finite
{\Bbbk}
-linear abelian rigid monoidal category with

\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathds{1})\cong{\Bbbk}
where
{\Bbbk}
is an algebraically closed
field. A category is
unimodular
if its unit object has a
self dual projective cover. If
\mathcal{T}
is unimodular and pivotal then
it has a unique (up to scalar) right m-trace on
\operatorname{Proj}
(see
[
GKP22
, Corollary
5.6]
). When
\mathcal{T}
is ribbon this right m-trace is an m-trace.

In
[
CGPV23b
]
, a more general notion of “left” and “right” chromatic maps is defined and proved to exist for general rigid finite tensor categories. We provide a sketch of proof for the special case of interest below:

Theorem 1.10
.

If
\mathcal{T}
is a finite, unimodular, ribbon tensor category then it admits a chromatic morphism.

Sketch of proof.

The finite
tensor category
\mathcal{T}
has a coend

\mathscr{L}=\int^{V\in\mathcal{C}}V^{*}\otimes V
with dinatural transformations

i_{V}:V^{*}\otimes V\to\mathscr{L}
. The coend is a Hopf algebra object in

\mathcal{C}
and every object
V\in\mathcal{T}
has a structure of right

\mathscr{L}
-comodule given by

\tilde{\imath}_{V}=(V\xrightarrow{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\longleftarrow}}{{\operatorname{coev}}}_{V}\otimes\operatorname{Id}}V\otimes V^{*}\otimes V\xrightarrow{\operatorname{Id}\otimes i_{V}}V\otimes\mathscr{L})
compatible with the monoidal
structure: the product
m_{\!\mathscr{L}}:\mathscr{L}\otimes\mathscr{L}\to\mathscr{L}
is
used to define the coaction on a tensor product. Moreover, the coend
is known to have (unique up to a scalar) right integrals

\lambda:\mathds{1}\to\mathscr{L}
and
\Lambda:\mathscr{L}\to\mathds{1}
with

\Lambda\circ\lambda=\operatorname{Id}_{\mathds{1}}
(since
\mathcal{T}
is unimodular,
\Lambda

is a two-sided integral). It is shown in
[
CGPV23b
]
that for a
good choice of
\Lambda
, we have for any projective
P\in\mathcal{T}
:

(6)

(\operatorname{Id}_{P}\otimes\Lambda)\circ\tilde{\imath}_{P}=\Lambda_{P}

where
\Lambda_{P}
is defined in Equation
2
with the
copairing of the m-trace. Let
G
be a projective generator, then the
map
i_{G}
is an epimorphism thus the map

\lambda\circ\varepsilon_{G}:G\to\mathscr{L}
factors through it and there exists a
map
f_{\lambda}:G\to G^{*}\otimes G
such that

(7)

\lambda\circ\varepsilon_{G}=i_{G}\circ f_{\lambda}\;.

Let

\widetilde{f}_{\lambda}=(\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\longrightarrow}}{{\operatorname{ev}}}\!\!_{G}\otimes\operatorname{Id}_{G})\circ(\operatorname{Id}_{G}\otimes f_{\lambda})
and
fix any map
e:G\to G\otimes G
such that

(\varepsilon_{G}\otimes\operatorname{Id}_{G})\circ e=\operatorname{Id}_{G}
. Then the map

(8)

{\mathsf{c}}=(\widetilde{f}_{\lambda}\otimes\operatorname{Id}_{G})\circ(\operatorname{Id}_{G}\otimes e)

is a chromatic map. Indeed, the integral property of
\lambda
is
that

m_{\mathscr{L}}\circ(\lambda\otimes\operatorname{Id}_{\mathscr{L}})=\varepsilon_{\mathscr{L}}\otimes\lambda

where
m_{\mathscr{L}}
and
\varepsilon_{\mathscr{L}}
are the product and counit of

\mathscr{L}
. Then we make a graphical proof:

The first equality comes from the duality property of
\Omega
(see Lemma
1.1
); the second is expressing
\Lambda_{G\otimes G^{*}}
as the coaction of
\Lambda
. This coaction on a tensor product is given by the product of the coactions. The third equality is the defining property of
f_{\lambda}
and the fourth is the integral property of
\lambda
.
∎

The coend in a braided tensor category
\mathcal{T}
also gives an alternative description of the morphisms

\Delta_{0}
and
\Delta_{\pm}
as follows: Let
\theta:\mathscr{L}\to\mathscr{L}
be defined by

\theta\circ i_{V}=i_{V}\circ(\operatorname{Id}_{V^{*}}\otimes\theta_{V})
. Then

\Delta_{\pm}=\varepsilon_{\mathscr{L}}\circ\theta^{\pm 1}\circ\lambda
. Let

\omega:\mathscr{L}\otimes\mathscr{L}\to\mathds{1}

and let

\Delta_{0}=\omega\circ(\lambda\otimes\operatorname{Id}_{\mathscr{L}}):\mathscr{L}\to\mathds{1}
. Then
for any projective object
P
,

\Delta_{0}^{P}=(\operatorname{Id}_{P}\otimes\Delta_{0})\circ\tilde{\imath},\text{ indeed }

Thus,
\mathcal{T}
is factorizable if and only if

\Delta_{0}=\Lambda
which is equivalent (see
[

, Lemma 2.7]
)
to the non-degeneracy of
\omega
. We thus recover the usual notion
of factorizable and twist non-degenerate given for example in

[
DGGPR22
]
.

Hopf algebras.
A particular example is when
\mathcal{T}
is the category of finite dimensional left
modules over a finite dimensional unimodular ribbon Hopf algebra

H
. By Theorem 1 of
[
BBG17
]
it has a non-degenerate left
m-trace on
\operatorname{Proj}
, which is also a right m-trace since
H
is ribbon.
The module
H

Fusion categories.
Recall that the global dimension of a ribbon fusion category

is
the sum of the squares of the dimensions of its simple

. It is
shown in

has characteristic zero.

Proposition 1.11
.

A ribbon fusion (possibly non-modular) category
\mathcal{C}
with non-zero
global dimension
is chromatic compact.

Proof.

Let
\{S_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be a set of representatives of the isomorphism
classes of simple objects of
\mathcal{C}
.
Observe that here
G=\oplus_{i\in I}S_{i}
is
a generator of
\operatorname{Proj}=\mathcal{C}
and
the quantum trace
{\operatorname{\mathsf{t}}}=\operatorname{qTr}_{\mathcal{C}}
is
a non-degenerate m-trace on
\mathcal{C}
. It follows that

\left\{x_{i}=\frac{1}{\operatorname{qdim}(S_{i})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\longleftarrow}}{{\operatorname{coev}}}_{S_{i}}\right\}_{i\in I}\text{ and }\left\{y_{i}=\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\longrightarrow}}{{\operatorname{ev}}}\!\!_{S_{i}}\right\}_{i\in I}

are dual bases of
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathds{1},G\otimes G^{*})
and
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(G\otimes G^{*},\mathds{1})
, respectively. Using the expansion

\Omega_{G\otimes G^{*}}=\sum_{i\in I}x_{i}\otimes_{\Bbbk}y_{i}
, it is straightforward to check that

{\mathsf{c}}_{P}=(\oplus_{i\in I}\operatorname{qdim}(S_{i})\operatorname{Id}_{S_{i}})\otimes\operatorname{Id}_{P}

is a chromatic morphism for
G
based on
P
(it is essentially the
Kirby color tensor the identity of
P
).

Let
d(\mathcal{C})=\sum_{i\in I}\operatorname{qdim}(S_{i})^{2}
be the global dimension of

\mathcal{C}
.
As
P_{\mathds{1}}=\mathds{1}
, the
morphism
\Delta_{0}^{P_{\mathds{1}}}
is multiplication by

, so

\mathcal{C}
is chromatic compact with
\zeta=d(\mathcal{C})
. A gluing morphism
is given by
\frac{1}{d(\mathcal{C})}Id_{\mathds{1}}

∎

Symmetric categories.
It seems that having semi-simple Müger center is a good criterion for being chromatic compact in characteristic 0. On one extreme, we have seen above that if
\mathcal{C}
has trivial Müger center (i.e. is factorizable) then it is chromatic compact. We consider the other extreme, the symmetric case, below:

Proposition 1.12
.

If
\mathcal{C}
is a symmetric monoidal tensor category and
char({\Bbbk})=0
, then the following are equivalent:
\mathcal{C}
is chromatic non-degenerate,
\mathcal{C}
is chromatic compact,
\mathcal{C}
is semi-simple.

Proof.

First, let us observe that
\mathcal{C}
admits a fiber functor. By Deligne’s theorem, see
[
EGNO15
, Theorem 9.11.4]
, it is enough to check that
\mathcal{C}
has sub-exponential growth. Find
C\in\mathbb{N}
such that
G\otimes G\subseteq G^{\oplus C}
and let
L
be the length of
G
. Then the length of
G^{\otimes n}
is at most
(C.L)^{n}
and
\mathcal{C}
has sub-exponential growth.

Using Tannakian reconstruction,
\mathcal{C}\simeq H-mod^{fd}
for some Hopf algebra
H
. (Note that, despite the fact
\mathcal{C}
is symmetric, it is not clear that
H
is cocommutative as it might be a super-Hopf algebra.)
We use the chromatic map from Equation
9
(it does not matter which chromatic map we use by Lemma
2.3
). We can ignore the double braiding in the definition and compute
\Delta_{0}^{H}(1)=\lambda(1)1
. If
\mathcal{C}
is chromatic non-degenerate it has to be non-zero, hence
\lambda(1)\neq 0
. This is equivalent to semisimplicity of
H^{*}
by
[
Rad12
]
which is equivalent to semisimplicity of
H
in characteristic 0 (
[
RL88
]
).

To conclude, we have seen in Proposition
1.11
that semi-simple implies chromatic compact, and it is immediate that chromatic compact implies chromatic non-degenerate.
∎

and that two such presentations of the same manifold are related by the following two moves. A Kirby I move which replaces a link
L
with itself disjoint union a unknot
U_{\pm}
with framing
\pm 1
:

(12)

L\quad\longleftrightarrow\quad L\sqcup U_{\pm}

and
a Kirby II move which replaces a component
L_{i}
of a link with a connected
sum of
L_{i}
with a parallel copy of a different component
L_{j}
:

(13)

\begin{array}[]{c}\hskip-3.69885pt\raisebox{-4.0pt}{\hbox{\psfig{figure,height}}}\end{array}\quad\longleftrightarrow\quad\begin{array}[]{c}\hskip-3.69885pt\raisebox{-4.0pt}{\hbox{\psfig{figure,height}}}\end{array}\;.

The result of surgery along a link
L
is a 3-manifold

S^{3}(L)
uniquely defined up to isotopy. If
L
and
L^{\prime}
are related by a
Kirby move, it induces a canonical diffeomorphism

S^{3}(L)\overset{\sim}{\to}S^{3}(L^{\prime})
up to isotopy.

Now given a pair
(M,T)
where
M
is a closed 3-manifold containing an admissible
\mathcal{C}
-colored ribbon graph
T
.
We say the ribbon graph

is a surgery presentation of

if
L
is a link surgery presentation representing
M
. The components of
L
are called the surgery components of
L\cup T
.
We have the following theorem (see
[
CGP14
]
).

Theorem 3.1
.

For
i=1,2
, let
L_{i}\cup T_{i}
be a surgery presentation in
S^{3}
of a 3-manifold
M_{i}
containing a
\mathcal{C}
-colored ribbon graph
T_{i}
. Let

f:M_{1}\to M_{2}
be an orientation preserving diffeomorphism such that

f(T_{1})=T_{2}
as
\mathcal{C}
-colored ribbon graphs. Then
L_{1}\cup T_{1}
and

L_{2}\cup T_{2}
are related by a sequence of orientation changes of
the surgery components, Kirby I moves away from
T_{i}
, Kirby II
moves on the surgery components and Kirby II moves obtained by
sliding an edge of
T_{i}
on a component of the surgery link such
that the induced diffeomorphism between
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
is isotopic
to
f
.

3.2.
Presentation of the (3+1)-cobordism categories

A generator and relation presentation of the
n
-dimensional oriented cobordism category
is given in
[
Juh18
]
. The (3+1)-TQFT construction given here
uses this work. Here we recall
some of the definitions and results of
[
Juh18
]
, in the case of 3-manifolds, i.e.
n=3
.

Let
\operatorname{\textbf{Man}}
be the category whose objects are closed oriented 3-manifolds and whose morphisms are
orientation preserving diffeomorphisms.
Let
\operatorname{\textbf{Cob}}
be the category whose objects are closed oriented 3-manifolds and morphisms are equivalence classes (up to oriented diffeomorphism that are the identity on the boundary) of oriented cobordisms.
Finally, let
{\operatorname{\textbf{Cob}}^{\mathsf{nc}}}
(resp.
\operatorname{\textbf{Cob}}^{\prime}
) be the largest subcategory of
\operatorname{\textbf{Cob}}
such that each component of every cobordism has a non-empty source (resp. has a non-empty source and target).
Here we add the non-compact cobordism category that
was not consider
by Juhász but that we will use later.

Let
M
be a closed oriented 3-manifold. For

k\in\{0,1,2,3\}
, a
framed k-sphere
in
M
is an orientation reversing embedding
\mathbb{S}=\mathbb{S}^{k}:S^{k}\times D^{3-k}\hookrightarrow M
.
Then we can perform surgery on
M
along
\mathbb{S}
by removing the interior of the image of
\mathbb{S}
and gluing in
D^{k+1}\times S^{2-k}
, getting a well defined topological manifold
M(\mathbb{S})
which, using the framing of the sphere, can be endowed with a canonical smooth structure.
Juhász considers additional types of framed sphere, namely
\mathbb{S}=\mathbb{S}^{-1}=0
and
\mathbb{S}=\emptyset
, where
M(0)=M\sqcup S^{3}
and

M(\emptyset)=M
. Given a framed k-sphere
\mathbb{S}

we denote by
W(\mathbb{S})
an associated smooth cobordism from
M\to M(\mathbb{S})
(unique up to diffeomorphism relative to the boundary see for instance
[
Mil65
, Theorem 3.13]
),
W(0)=(M\times[-1,1])\sqcup D^{4}
and
W(\emptyset)=M\times[-1,1]
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be the directed graph described as follows. The vertices correspond to objects of
\operatorname{\textbf{Man}}
. There are two kinds of edges of
\mathcal{G}
: 1) if
d:M\to M^{\prime}
is a diffeomorphism then there is an edge
e_{d}
going from
M
to
M^{\prime}
, 2) if
\mathbb{S}
is a
k
-framed sphere in
M
then
there is an edge
e_{M,\mathbb{S}}
from
M
to
M(\mathbb{S})
. Let
{\mathcal{G}^{\mathsf{nc}}}
(resp.
\mathcal{G^{\prime}}
) be the subgraph of
\mathcal{G}
obtained by removing the edges
e_{M,\mathbb{S}}
where
\mathbb{S}=0
(resp. where
\mathbb{S}=0
or
\mathbb{S}
is a framed
3
-sphere). Let
\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{G})
(resp.
\mathcal{F}({\mathcal{G}^{\mathsf{nc}}})
, resp.
\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{G}^{\prime})
) be the free category generated by
\mathcal{G}
(resp.
{\mathcal{G}^{\mathsf{nc}}}
, resp.
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
).

In
[
Juh18
, Definition 1.4]
Juhász considers a set of relations
\mathcal{R}
in
\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{G})
which we recall now. If
w
and
w^{\prime}
are words consisting of composing arrows, then we write
w\sim w^{\prime}
if

(w,w^{\prime})\in\mathcal{R}
.

(R1)

For diffeomorphisms
d
and

whose composition is defined we have the relation
e_{d\circ d^{\prime}}\sim e_{d}\circ e_{d^{\prime}}
. We also have the relations
e_{M,\emptyset}\sim e_{\operatorname{Id}_{M}}
and
e_{d}\sim e_{\operatorname{Id}_{M}}
if

is a diffeomorphism isotopic to the identity.

(R2)

If

is an orientation preserving diffeomorphism between 3-manifolds and
\mathbb{S}
a framed sphere in
M
then let
\mathbb{S}^{\prime}=d\circ\mathbb{S}
be the framed sphere in
M^{\prime}
; then
let
d^{\mathbb{S}}:M(\mathbb{S})\to M^{\prime}(\mathbb{S}^{\prime})
be the induced diffeomorphism. Then the commutativity of the following diagram defines a relation:

\textstyle{M\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{d}
\scriptstyle{e_{M,\mathbb{S}}}

\textstyle{M^{\prime}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\textstyle{M^{\prime}(\mathbb{S}^{\prime}).}

(R3)

If
\mathbb{S},\mathbb{S}^{\prime}
are disjoint framed sphere in an oriented 3-manifold
M
then

M(\mathbb{S})(\mathbb{S}^{\prime})=M(\mathbb{S}^{\prime})(\mathbb{S})
and we denote this 3-manifold by

\textstyle{M\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

(R4)

Let
\mathbb{S}
be a framed

and
\mathbb{S}^{\prime}
a framed

-sphere in

intersects the belt sphere

(defined in Definition 2.17 of

If

{\mathcal{R}^{\mathsf{nc}}}
(resp.

(resp.

(Definition 1.5 of

]
)
.

Let

]
) and

and a set of relations

as morphisms.

(Theorem 1.7 of

]
)
.

The functor

Remark 3.4
.

The theorem is stated in a slightly different way by Juhász as an

-handle cancellations can be avoided so that one

and
\operatorname{\textbf{Cob}}^{\prime}
but it has no maps
to any other object.

Corollary 3.5
.

The functor
c:\mathcal{F}({\mathcal{G}^{\mathsf{nc}}})\to{\operatorname{\textbf{Cob}}^{\mathsf{nc}}}

a functor

\mathcal{F}({\mathcal{G}^{\mathsf{nc}}})/{\mathcal{R}^{\mathsf{nc}}}\to{\operatorname{\textbf{Cob}}^{\mathsf{nc}}}

Proof.

This corollary follows from Juhász’s argument in
[
Juh18
]

using parameterized Cerf decomposition. For an argument based on
the statements of Juhász’s theorems one can easily translate in
dimension 4 the one made in dimension 3 of

, Corollary

.

Let

,

is the pivotal element,

is the antipode, and

is the double coproduct of

.
When
\mathcal{D}
is twist non-degenerate, let

be the Hennings invariant defined in
[

]
and let
\operatorname{H}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{D}}
be its renormalized version defined in
[
DGP18
]

of

.

Theorem 4.3
.

If
\mathcal{D}
is twist non-degenerate,
M
is a closed connected 3-manifold and
T
is a admissible closed
\mathcal{D}
-colored bichrome graph inside
M
then

\operatorname{H}_{H}(M)=\mathbb{D}^{-1-b_{1}}\mathrm{B}_{\mathcal{D}}(M,\emptyset)\text{ and }\operatorname{H}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{D}}(M,T)=\mathbb{D}^{-1-b_{1}}\mathrm{B}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{D}}(M,T)

where

is the first Betti number of
M
.

Proof.

We prove the second equality first. Let
L\cup T
be a surgery presentation of
(M,T)
. Let
F_{\lambda}
be the extension to bichrome graphs of the R-T functor
F
and let
F_{\lambda}^{\prime}
be its renormalization with the m-trace, see
[
DGP18
]
. From Theorem 2.9 of
[
DGP18
]
we have

\operatorname{H}_{H}^{\prime}(M,T)=\mathbb{D}^{-1-\ell}\delta^{-\sigma(L)}F^{\prime}_{\lambda}(L\cup T)

where
\sigma(L)=r-s
is the signature linking matrix of a surgery presentation
L
of
M
,
\delta=\frac{\Delta_{+}}{D}=\frac{D}{\Delta_{-}}
and
\ell=b_{1}+r+s
.
The main property of the map
F^{\prime}_{\lambda}
is that it gives a well defined meaning to a red circle that can be made blue using a map

as in Figure 23 of
[
DGP18
]
. Now Lemma 6.3 of
[
CGPT20
]
says that this red to blue operation is exactly the chromatic morphism given in Equation (
9
). Thus, with the notation of Theorem
4.1
it follows that
F^{\prime}_{\lambda}(L\cup T)=F^{\prime}(L^{blue})
.
Finally, it is easy to show that
\mathbb{D}^{-1-\ell}\delta^{-\sigma(L)}=\frac{\mathbb{D}^{-1-b_{1}}}{\Delta_{+}^{r}\Delta_{-}^{s}}
for a surgery presentation
L
. Thus, we have proven the second equality of the theorem.
Now to see the first equality, from Proposition 2.11 of
[
DGP18
]
and Equation (

) we have

\operatorname{H}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{D}}(M\sharp S^{3},\Gamma_{0})=\operatorname{H}_{H}(M)\operatorname{H}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{D}}(S^{3},\Gamma_{0})=\operatorname{H}_{\mathcal{D}}(M)\text{ and }\mathrm{B}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{D}}(M\sharp S^{3},\Gamma_{0})=\mathrm{B}_{\mathcal{D}}(M)\mathrm{B}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{D}}(S^{3},\Gamma_{0})=\mathrm{B}_{\mathcal{D}}(M).

Using these equations the second equality implies the first.
∎

Proposition 5.1
.

The linear map
\chi_{M,\mathbb{S}^{0}}
is well defined and does not depend
on the ordering of
\{x,y\}

Proof.

First we note that the admissible skein module is generated by skeins
in
M
where every component of
M

\varepsilon

\eta
. Indeed, consider a box
containing a part of an edge colored by

\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\longleftarrow}}{{\operatorname{ev}}}\!\!_{P}

1.9

\eta

\epsilon

\eta
-colored coupon in each connected component of

.

{\mathsf{g}}

{\mathsf{g}}^{\prime}

{\mathsf{g}}^{\prime}
instead of
{\mathsf{g}}
and two
different coupons colored with
\varepsilon
and
\eta

if

Theorem 5.4
.

There exists a unique symmetric monoidal functor

{\mathscr{S}}_{\mathcal{C}}:{\operatorname{\textbf{Cob}}^{\mathsf{nc}}}\to\mathrm{Vec}

extending
{\mathscr{S}}_{\mathcal{C}}:\operatorname{\textbf{Man}}\to\mathrm{Vec}

such that
{\mathscr{S}}_{\mathcal{C}}(e_{\Sigma,\mathbb{S}})=\chi_{\Sigma,\mathbb{S}}
.

If
\mathcal{C}
is chromatic compact, then the functor extends to a symmetric monoidal functor on
\operatorname{\textbf{Cob}}
:

{\mathscr{S}}_{\mathcal{C}}:\operatorname{\textbf{Cob}}\to\mathrm{Vec}.

Proof.

We only need to prove that the relation (R1)–(R5) are satisfied by

{\mathscr{S}}_{\mathcal{C}}
.

(R1)

Since
{\mathscr{S}}_{\mathcal{C}}:\operatorname{\textbf{Man}}\to\mathrm{Vec}
is functorial we have

{\mathscr{S}}_{\mathcal{C}}(e_{d\circ d^{\prime}})={\mathscr{S}}_{\mathcal{C}}(e_{d})\circ{\mathscr{S}}_{\mathcal{C}}(e_{d^{\prime}})
. Also, since elements of

{\mathscr{S}}_{\mathcal{C}}(M)
are defined by ribbon graphs up to isotopy
we clearly have
{\mathscr{S}}_{\mathcal{C}}(e_{d})=\operatorname{Id}
if
d
is isotopic to

\operatorname{Id}_{\Sigma}
.

(R2)

Since the construction of the maps
\chi_{M,\mathbb{S}}

are local, they are covariant under diffeomorphisms of the pair

({M,\mathbb{S}})
.

(R3)

Again, since the construction of the maps

\chi_{M,\mathbb{S}}
are local, they commute for disjoint framed
spheres.

(R4)

The 2-3-handle cancellations reduces to the chromatic
identity (
4
) as shown in Figure
7
. Indeed since the attaching framed
2-sphere of the 3-handle intersects the belt circle of the

2
-handle once, the attaching circle for the 2-handle bounds a
disc in the intermediate 3-manifold. This is why we can represent
the green circle in Figure
7
as an unknot.

The
1-2-handle cancellations reduces to the defining property of
the gluing map. Indeed the sphere
S^{2}
created by the 1-handle can’t
be separating since it is intersected once by the attaching

6.
Examples and relations with other works

6.1.
Semisimple case

Using the chain-mail construction of
[
Rob95
]
, we can rewrite our construction in the semi-simple case as a state sum. We then recover the Crane–Yetter–Kauffman 4-manifold invariant associated with a semi-simple fusion category
\mathcal{C}
. The chain mail construction has been carried out for the state spaces in
[
Tha21
]
in characteristic 0 and we will use this description.

The state-sum 4-manifold invariant was defined for all fusion categories in
[
CKY97
]
. It was first only defined in the modular case by Crane and Yetter, and mentioned to extend to a TQFT there. It is well-known that in the modular case the TQFT is invertible, and the associated 4-manifold invariant is classical, namely only depends on the signature and Euler characteristic, see
[
CKY97
, Proposition 6.2]
. Note however that given the extra data of a boundary condition, which corresponds to the empty skein in our description, this TQFT recovers the Reshetikhin–Turaev invariants of 3-manifolds. It was shown in
[
BB18
]
that when the category
\mathcal{C}
is not modular, i.e. has non-trivial Müger center, it is no longer true that the 4-manifold invariants depend only on the signature and the Euler characteristic, but at least also on the fundamental group. It is however still almost trivial on simply connected manifolds, see
[
BB18
]
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a fusion category over an algebraically closed field of characteristic 0. Choose
{\operatorname{\mathsf{t}}}=\operatorname{qTr}

In the semi-simple case, the admissible skein modules are the usual skein modules.

Let us describe the TQFT

in this setting. Let

be a set of representatives of the isomorphism classes of simple objects of

. We described in Proposition
1.11
the chromatic map

and the gluing morphism
{\mathsf{g}}=\frac{1}{d(\mathcal{C})}Id_{\mathds{1}}
, hence
\zeta=d(\mathcal{C})
.

0.

A 0-handle introduces
d(\mathcal{C})\cdot\emptyset
in the created
S^{3}
.

1.

A 1-handle on a skein disjoint from the attaching sphere multiplies by
\frac{1}{d(\mathcal{C})}
without affecting the skein.

2.

A 2-handle introduces a Kirby-colored circle along the attaching sphere.

3.

A 3-handle cuts the strands passing through the canceling 2-handle represented by the green arc by introducing a copairing.

4.

A 4-handle does Reshetikhin–Turaev evaluation on a skein in

.

This is exactly the description of
[

6.3.
Characteristic
p

We give an example of a category which is chromatic non-degenerate but
not chromatic compact, and therefore gives a non-compact TQFT. The
example we give is very simple and unlikely to give interesting
4-manifold invariant, but the TQFT already shows some very interesting
features. Its associated algebra on
S^{2}\times S^{1}
is non-semisimple,
so it does not fall under Reutter’s theorem
[
Reu23
]
showing
that semi-simple TQFTs cannot detect exotic structures.

The proof of Proposition
1.12
hints at this example. In
characteristic
p
, one may find a cocommutative Hopf algebra
H

which is non-semisimple but such that
H^{*}
is semi-simple. This gives
a symmetric monoidal, non-semisimple and chromatic non-degenerate
category, therefore with non-semisimple Müger center.

Definition 6.6
.

Let
{\Bbbk}
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p

\mathcal{C}=H

Proposition 6.7
.

The category
\mathcal{C}
is chromatic non-degenerate, but not chromatic
compact. It gives a non-compact TQFT
{\mathscr{S}}_{\mathcal{C}}
.

Proof.

The cointegral is
\Lambda=\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}\alpha^{i}
, and the right

\lambda=1^{*}
in the basis

(1,\alpha,\dots,\alpha^{p-1})
. We observe indeed that

\varepsilon(\Lambda)=p=0
whereas
\lambda(1)=1\neq 0
, so
H^{*}
is
semi-simple whereas
H
is not. One computes the central element

\Delta_{0}=\lambda(1)1=1\in H
, thus

for any projective. Therefore, the gluing morphism
{\mathsf{g}}
is given by

\Lambda_{P_{\mathds{1}}}

∎

Note that

means that the 1-handle map does not
affect the skein. Similarly,
\mathcal{C}
being symmetric and
\lambda(1)=1

implies that a homotopically-trivial red links can be ignored.

As explained in
[
Reu23
]
, the vector space

has a natural algebra structure induced
by the cobordism
\overset{\dots}{S^{3}}\times S^{1}
where

\overset{\dots}{S^{3}}

Note that this
algebra is non-unital as the TQFT is non-compact.

Proposition 6.8
.

The non-unital algebra
{\mathscr{S}}_{\mathcal{C}}(S^{2}\times S^{1})
is
non-semisimple (i.e. it is non-semisimple if one freely adjoins a

Proof.

For

the skein
\{pt\}\times S^{1}\subseteq S^{2}\times S^{1}

P
with a single coupon

O_{f\circ g}=O_{g\circ f}

and

\overset{\dots}{B^{3}}\times S^{1}

As
H

If one freely adjoins a unit to

∎

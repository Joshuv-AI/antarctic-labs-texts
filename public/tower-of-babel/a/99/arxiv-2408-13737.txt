SPECIAL VALUES OF DERIVATIVES OF CERTAIN
L

Abstract
In this paper we address the question of non-vanishing of L ′ ​ ( 0 , f ) L^{\prime}(0,f) where f f is an algebraic valued periodic function. In 2011, Gun, Murty and Rath studied the nature of special values of the derivatives of even Dirichlet-type functions and proved that it can be either zero or transcendental. Here for some special cases we characterize the set of functions for which L ′ ​ ( 0 , f ) L^{\prime}(0,f) is zero or transcendental. Using a theorem of Ramachandra about multiplicative independence of cyclotomic units we also provide some non-trivial examples of functions where L ′ ​ ( 0 , f ) L^{\prime}(0,f) is zero. Finally, assuming Schanuel’s conjecture we derive the algebraic independence of special values of derivatives of L L -functions.

For any periodic arithmetic function
f,

\displaystyle L(s,f)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{f(n)}{n^{s}}

for
\Re(s)>1

Suppose

\displaystyle L(s,f)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{f(n)}{n^{s}}=q^{-s}\sum_{a=1}^{q}f(a)\zeta\left(s,\frac{a}{q}\right),

\displaystyle\zeta(s,a)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(n+a)^{s}}

\Re{(s)}>1

Dirichlet series to all values of

possibly at

also provided
\sum_{a=1}^{\infty}f(a)=0

The study of the special values of the

-functions has been the focus for a very long time and a
series of interesting results are known in this direction. In fact the motivation for studying certain number
theoretic constants such as digamma function at rational arguments, generalized Stieltjes constants comes

-functions and the derivatives of

For the case when

Let
q>1
be any prime power and
f_{1},\cdots,f_{n}
be even Dirichlet type functions with period
q
. Suppose
f_{1},\cdots,f_{n}
are algebraically independent functions, then weak Schanuel’s conjecture implies
L^{\prime}(0,f_{1}),\cdots,L^{\prime}(0,f_{n})
are algebraically independent.

Proof.

Suppose there exists a polynomial
P(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})
not identically zero such that

\displaystyle P(L^{\prime}(0,f_{1}),\cdots,L^{\prime}(0,f_{n}))=0.

Substituting the value of
L^{\prime}(0,f_{i})
in the above equation, we get an equation of the form

\displaystyle Q(\log 2\sin\pi/q,\cdots,\log 2\sin a\pi/q)=0

for some polynomial
Q(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})
where
2\leq a\leq q/2
with
(a,q)=1.
Also observe that the numbers
\log 2\sin a\pi/q
are linearly independent by using Proposition
2.4
, then by using weak Schanuel’s conjecture we must have
Q(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})\equiv 0
. Thus by using,

\displaystyle L^{\prime}(0,f)=-\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1\\
(a,q)=1\end{subarray}}^{[q/2]}f(a)\left(\log 2\sin\frac{\pi a}{q}\right).

and the fact that
Q(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})\equiv 0
gives us a non-trivial algebraic relation between the functions
f_{1},\cdots,f_{n}
which is a contradiction.
∎

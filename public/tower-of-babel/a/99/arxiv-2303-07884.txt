Distributed least square approach for solving a multiagent linear algebraic equation
Viet Hoang Pham, Hyo-Sung Ahn

Abstract
This paper considers a linear algebraic equation over a multiagent network. The coefficient matrix is partitioned into multiple blocks; each agent only knows a subset of these blocks in different row and column partitions. Based on a proximal ADMM algorithm, we design a distributed method for every agent to find its corresponding parts in one least square solution of the considered linear algebraic equation. Each agent uses only its information and communicates with its neighbors. We show that the designed method achieves an exponentially fast convergence for an arbitrarily initial setup. Numerical simulations in MATLAB are provided to verify the effectiveness of the designed method.

Introduction

The control and optimization problems of multiagent systems have become popular in many applications. The main reason is inherent complexity of large-scale problems and memory dispersion with the growth of big data. Depending on the communication network among agents, a multiagent system can have a hierarchical or distributed scheme. In a hierarchical setup, one master agent plays as a coordinator. This agent communicates with and/or assigns tasks to all others. In a distributed setup, agents work in parallel, and each agent can exchange information with only its neighbors. Because of the scalability and privacy protection requirements, distributed ones have become more desired recently.

In addition, our designed method has no requirements for step-size selection or initial point setup.

II-B

Proximal ADMM Algorithm

Consider the following convex optimization problem

\min\limits_{\textbf{x}\in\mathcal{X},\textbf{y}\in\mathcal{Y}}\textrm{ }\Psi_{x}(\textbf{x})+\Psi_{y}(\textbf{y})\textrm{ s.t. }\textbf{F}_{x}\textbf{x}+\textbf{F}_{y}\textbf{y}=\textbf{f}.

(1)

where
\Psi_{x}(\textbf{x}),\Psi_{y}(\textbf{y})
are convex functions,
\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}
are convex sets,
\textbf{F}_{x},\textbf{F}_{y}
and
f
are known matrices and vector.
The augmented Lagrangian function associated to the problem (
1
) is defined as

\mathcal{L}_{\rho}(\textbf{x},\textbf{y},\bm{\lambda})=\Psi_{x}(\textbf{x})+\Psi_{y}(\textbf{y})+\frac{\rho}{2}\Bigl|\Bigl|\textbf{F}_{x}\textbf{x}+\textbf{F}_{y}\textbf{y}-\textbf{f}-\frac{1}{\rho}\bm{\lambda}\Bigr|\Bigr|^{2}

where
\bm{\lambda}
is the dual variable corresponding to the equality constraint in (
1
) and
\rho>0
is a positive penalty parameter.
The proximal ADMM algorithm proposed in
[
15
]
for solving the convex optimization problem (
1
) is given as follows.

\displaystyle\textbf{x}(s+1)

\displaystyle=\arg\min\limits_{\textbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\Bigl\{\mathcal{L}_{\rho}(\textbf{x},\textbf{y}(s),\bm{\lambda}(s))+\frac{1}{2}\left|\left|\textbf{x}-\textbf{x}(s)\right|\right|_{\textbf{G}}^{2}\Bigr\},

(2a)

\displaystyle\textbf{y}(s+1)

\displaystyle=\arg\min\limits_{\textbf{y}\in\mathcal{Y}}\mathcal{L}_{\rho}(\textbf{x}(s+1),\textbf{y},\bm{\lambda}(s)),

(2b)

\displaystyle\bm{\lambda}(s+1)

\displaystyle=\bm{\lambda}(s)-\rho\Bigl(\textbf{F}_{x}\textbf{x}(s+1)+\textbf{F}_{y}\textbf{y}(s+1)-\textbf{f}\Bigr),

(2c)

where
G
is a symmetric and positive semidefinite matrix, and the initial point
(\textbf{x}(0),\textbf{y}(0),\bm{\lambda}(0))
can be chosen arbitrarily such that
\textbf{x}(0)\in\mathcal{X},\textbf{y}(0)\in\mathcal{Y}
.
The following theorem is Lemma 1 in
[
16
]
, which is based on the convergence result of the proximal ADMM algorithm (
2
) in
[
15
]
.

Theorem 1

Let
\bm{\Theta}=blkdiag\left\{\textbf{G},\rho\textbf{F}_{y}^{T}\textbf{F}_{y},\frac{1}{\rho}\textbf{I}\right\}
and
\textbf{w}(s)=[\textbf{x}(s)^{T},\textbf{y}(s)^{T},\bm{\lambda}(s)^{T}]^{T}
. Then we have

1.

||\textbf{w}(s)-\textbf{w}(s+1)||_{\bm{\Theta}}^{2}=o\left(\frac{1}{s}\right)

2.

||\textbf{F}_{x}\textbf{x}(s)+\textbf{F}_{y}\textbf{y}(s)-\textbf{f}||=o\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}\right)

3.

|\Psi_{x}(\textbf{x}(s))+\Psi_{y}(\textbf{y}(s))-\psi^{opt}|=o\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}\right)
where
\psi^{opt}
is the optimal value of the cost function in (
1
).

III
Problem Formulation

III-A

Distributed linear algebraic equation

Consider a linear algebraic equation

\textbf{H}\textbf{z}=\textbf{h},

(3)

where
\textbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is unknown vector,
\textbf{H}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
and
\textbf{h}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
are coefficients matrix and vector, respectively.
Let
\textbf{z}^{opt}
be one least square solution to (
3
). Then
\textbf{z}^{opt}
is one optimal solution to the following optimization problem.

\min_{\textbf{z}}\frac{1}{2}\bigl|\bigl|\textbf{H}\textbf{z}-\textbf{h}\bigr|\bigr|^{2}.

(4)

In general, the linear algebraic equation (
3
) can have one or more least square solutions.
Define
\Psi^{opt}=\frac{1}{2}||\textbf{H}\textbf{z}^{opt}-\textbf{h}||^{2}
.
We have
\Psi^{opt}=0
if the linear algebraic equation (
3
) has at least one exact solution. If
H
is a full column rank matrix, we have
\textbf{z}^{opt}=(\textbf{H}^{T}\textbf{H})^{-1}\textbf{H}^{T}\textbf{h}
is unique and
\Psi^{opt}=\frac{1}{2}\textbf{h}^{T}\textbf{h}-\frac{1}{2}\textbf{h}^{T}\textbf{H}(\textbf{H}^{T}\textbf{H})^{-1}\textbf{H}^{T}\textbf{h}
.

In this paper, we are interested in determining one least square solution to the linear algebraic equation (
3
) when the coefficient matrix
H
can be subdivided into multiple blocks
\textbf{H}_{kl}
, where
1\leq k\leq K
and
1\leq l\leq L
, as in (
5
) and each nonzero block is known by only one agent in a network of
N
agents.

\textbf{H}=\left[\begin{matrix}\textbf{H}_{11}&\textbf{H}_{12}&\cdots&\textbf{H}_{1L}\\
\textbf{H}_{21}&\textbf{H}_{22}&\cdots&\textbf{H}_{2L}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\textbf{H}_{K1}&\textbf{H}_{K2}&\cdots&\textbf{H}_{KL}\end{matrix}\right].

(5)

The coefficient matrix
H
in (
5
) includes
KL
blocks corresponding to
K
row and
L
column partitions.
As each agent
i
, where
1\leq i\leq N
, knows some nonzero blocks, we have
KL\geq N
in general setting. We define
\mathcal{A}[\textbf{H}_{kl}]
as an operator to express the agent knowing the block
\textbf{H}_{kl}
. Since there may be some zero blocks in
H
, we conventionally define
\mathcal{A}[\textbf{H}_{kl}]=0
if
\textbf{H}_{kl}=\textbf{0}
. Note that
0
is not an agent.
For each agent
i
, we define the relation between the block
\textbf{H}_{kl}
and this agent by
\delta_{i}[\textbf{H}_{kl}]
where
\delta_{i}[\textbf{H}_{kl}]=1
if
\mathcal{A}[\textbf{H}_{kl}]=i
and
\delta_{i}[\textbf{H}_{kl}]=0
, otherwise.
Let
\mathcal{B}_{k}^{(i)}=\{\textbf{H}_{kl}:\delta_{i}[\textbf{H}_{kl}]=1,\forall 1\leq l\leq L\}
be the set of blocks in the row partition
k

i
, where
1\leq k\leq K
and
1\leq i\leq N
.

For each row partition
k
, where
1\leq k\leq K
, of the coefficient matrix
H
, we define the index list of agents that have information of blocks in this row partition as
\mathcal{R}_{k}=\{\mathcal{A}[\textbf{H}_{k1}],\mathcal{A}[\textbf{H}_{k2}],\dots,\mathcal{A}[\textbf{H}_{kL}]\}

\mathcal{C}^{l}
be the index list of agents that have information of blocks in the column partition

\mathcal{C}^{l}=\{\mathcal{A}[\textbf{H}_{1l}],\mathcal{A}[\textbf{H}_{2l}],\dots,\mathcal{A}[\textbf{H}_{Kl}]\}

1\leq l\leq L
.
As
\mathcal{R}_{k}
and
\mathcal{C}^{l}
, where
1\leq k\leq K
and
1\leq l\leq L
, are lists, they can contain duplicated indexes. We define the set
\mathbb{S}(\mathcal{R}_{k})
derived from the list
\mathcal{R}_{k}
by removing the index

and merging duplicated indexes into one element. Similarly, we define
\mathbb{S}(\mathcal{C}^{l})
for the list
\mathcal{C}^{l}
where
1\leq l\leq L
.
We can obtain an equivalent linear algebraic equation to (
3
) if we exchange rows in the matrix
H
and the vector

. So, the rows in

can be interchanged such that the ones dispensed across the same list of agents are grouped in the same row partition.
Without loss of generality, we assume that
\mathcal{R}_{k}\neq\mathcal{R}_{k^{\prime}}
if
k\neq k^{\prime}
for all
1\leq k,k^{\prime}\leq K
.

III-B

Optimization approach

Denote by
m_{k},n_{l}
the dimensions of the block
\textbf{H}_{kl}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{k}\times n_{l}}
, where
1\leq k\leq K
and
1\leq l\leq L
. We have
m=\sum_{k=1}^{K}m_{k}
and
n=\sum_{l=1}^{L}n_{l}
.
According to the partition of the coefficient matrix
H
, the unknown vector
z
and the coefficient vector
h
can be partitioned as
\textbf{z}=\textrm{col}\{\textbf{z}_{1},\textbf{z}_{2},\dots,\textbf{z}_{L}\}
and
\textbf{h}=\textrm{col}\{\textbf{h}_{1},\textbf{h}_{2},\dots,\textbf{h}_{K}\}
where

\displaystyle\textbf{z}_{l}

\displaystyle=\textrm{blkrow}\bigl\{\textbf{0}_{n_{l}\times\sum_{j=0}^{l-1}n_{j}},\textbf{I}_{n_{l}},\textbf{0}_{n_{l}\times\bigl(n-\sum_{j=0}^{l}n_{j}\bigr)}\bigr\}\textbf{z},

\displaystyle\textbf{h}_{k}

\displaystyle=\textrm{blkrow}\bigl\{\textbf{0}_{m_{k}\times\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}m_{j}},\textbf{I}_{m_{k}},\textbf{0}_{m_{k}\times\bigl(m-\sum_{j=0}^{k}m_{j}\bigr)}\bigr\}\textbf{h}.

Here we set
m_{0}=n_{0}=0
conventionally.
Assume that each agent
i\in\mathbb{S}(\mathcal{C}^{l})
has a copy
\textbf{z}_{l}^{(i)}
for the part
\textbf{z}_{l}
of the unknown vector
z
. We have

\sum_{l=1}^{L}\textbf{H}_{kl}\textbf{z}_{l}=\sum_{l=1,\textbf{H}_{kl}\neq\textbf{0}}^{L}\textbf{H}_{kl}\textbf{z}_{l}^{(\mathcal{A}[\textbf{H}_{kl}])},\forall 1\leq k\leq K.

(6)

If
\mathcal{A}[\textbf{H}_{kl}]\neq 0
, the vector
\textbf{H}_{kl}\textbf{z}_{l}^{\mathcal{A}[\textbf{H}_{kl}]}
is private information of the agent
\mathcal{A}[\textbf{H}_{kl}]
.
To rewrite the error of the linear equations corresponding to the row partition
k
as the sum of local private functions, we make the following assumption:

Assumption 1

For every row partition
k
, where
1\leq k\leq K
, each agent
i\in\mathbb{S}(\mathcal{R}_{k})
can access to a part of
\textbf{h}_{k}
(i.e., can have a part of information of
\textbf{h}_{k}
). Let us denote the part of information as
\textbf{h}_{i,k}
. However, the combination of
\textbf{h}_{i,k}
is equivalent to
\textbf{h}_{k}
such that
\sum\limits_{i\in\mathbb{S}(\mathcal{R}_{k})}\textbf{h}_{i,k}=\textbf{h}_{k}
.

Under Assumption
1
, we have

\sum_{l=1}^{L}\textbf{H}_{kl}\textbf{z}_{l}-\textbf{h}_{k}=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{S}(\mathcal{R}_{k})}\Bigl(\sum_{\textbf{H}_{kl}\in\mathcal{B}_{k}^{(i)}}\textbf{H}_{kl}\textbf{z}_{l}^{(i)}-\textbf{h}_{i,k}\Bigr),

(7)

for all
1\leq k\leq K
.
So, we have an equivalent problem of the optimization problem (
4
) as follows.

\displaystyle\min_{\textbf{z}_{l}^{(i)}}\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left|\left|\sum_{i\in\mathbb{S}(\mathcal{R}_{k})}\left(\sum_{\textbf{H}_{kl}\in\mathcal{B}_{k}^{(i)}}\textbf{H}_{kl}\textbf{z}_{l}^{(i)}-\textbf{h}_{i,k}\right)\right|\right|^{2}

(8a)

\displaystyle\textrm{s.t. }\textbf{z}_{l}^{(i)}=\textbf{z}_{l}^{(j)},\forall i,j\in\mathbb{S}(\mathcal{C}^{l}),i\neq j,\forall 1\leq l\leq L.

(8b)

It is clear that the optimal cost value
\Phi^{opt}=\frac{1}{2}||\textbf{H}\textbf{z}^{opt}-\textbf{h}||^{2}
is also the optimal value for the cost function in the problem (
8
). In addition, each optimal solution to the optimization problem (
8
) can be used to determine one least square solution (
4
) of the linear algebraic equation (
3
).

III-C

Communication graph

In this paper, we use an undirected graph
\mathcal{G}=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})
to illustrate the communication network among agents. In which,
\mathcal{V}=\{1,2,\dots\}
is the node set and
\mathcal{E}\subset\mathcal{V}\times\mathcal{V}
is the edge set.
Each node
i\in\mathcal{V}=\{1,2,\dots,N\}
corresponds to an agent while an edge
(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}
represents that the agent
i
can exchange information with the agent
j
.
As
\mathcal{G}
is an undirected graph,
(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}
implies
(j,i)\in\mathcal{E}
for every pair of nodes
i,j\in\mathcal{V}
.
The neighbor set of the agent

is denoted by
\mathcal{N}_{i}=\left\{j\in\mathcal{V}:(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}\right\}
.
Since agents need to cooperate to find the optimal solution of the problem (

), the communication graph
\mathcal{G}
is required to be connected. That means there is at least one path from
i
to

for any pair
i,j\in\mathcal{V}
, i.e., there is a sequence of edges
(i_{1},i_{2}),(i_{2},i_{3}),\dots,(i_{k-1},i_{k})
such that

and
i_{l}\in\mathcal{V},(i_{l-1},i_{l})\in\mathcal{E},\forall 2\leq l\leq k
.

Let
\tilde{\mathcal{V}}
be a node subset, i.e.,
\tilde{\mathcal{V}}\subset\mathcal{V}
. The subgraph induced by
\tilde{\mathcal{V}}
is defined as
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}=(\tilde{\mathcal{V}},\tilde{\mathcal{E}})

\tilde{\mathcal{E}}=\{(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}:i,j\in\tilde{\mathcal{V}}\}

We define
\mathcal{G}_{k}=(\mathbb{S}(\mathcal{R}_{k}),\mathcal{E}_{k})
as the subgraph induced by the node set
\mathbb{S}(\mathcal{R}_{k})
for each row partition
k
, where
1\leq k\leq K
.
Similarly, we define
\mathcal{G}^{l}=(\mathbb{S}(\mathcal{C}^{l}),\mathcal{E}^{l})
for each column partition

, where

.
The following assumption is necessary.

Assumption 2

The communication graph satisfies that

\mathcal{G}^{l}
is a connected graph for all

2.

is a connected graph for all

The first item in Assumption

\textbf{z}_{l}^{(i)}=\textbf{z}_{l}^{(j)},\forall j\in\mathcal{N}_{i}\cap\mathbb{S}(\mathcal{C}^{l}),\forall i\in\mathbb{S}(\mathcal{C}^{l}),

(9)

for all

When

, the agent

takes part in the linear equations corresponding to the row partition

In general, there may be some row partitions

Problem 1

Design a distributed method for each agent to find its corresponding parts in one optimal solution of the constrained optimization problem (

\textbf{P}_{ij}\bar{\textbf{z}}_{i}=\textbf{P}_{ji}\bar{\textbf{z}}_{j},\forall(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}.

\min_{\bar{\textbf{z}}_{i},\forall i\in\mathcal{V}}\Psi(\bar{\textbf{z}}_{1},\bar{\textbf{z}}_{2},\dots,\bar{\textbf{z}}_{N})\textrm{ s.t. }\eqref{eq_coupling_commonvariable}.

\overline{\textbf{w}}(s+1)=\textbf{M}\overline{\textbf{w}}(s).

By choosing
\textbf{w}(0)=\textbf{0}
, we have
\overline{\textbf{w}}(s)=\textbf{M}^{s}\textbf{m}
. So,
\lim_{s\rightarrow\infty}\textbf{M}^{s}\textbf{m}=\textbf{0}
.
Let
\textbf{v}_{(\sigma)}
be the eigenvector of the matrix
M
corresponding to the eigenvalue
\sigma
, i.e.,
\textbf{M}\textbf{v}_{(\sigma)}=\sigma\textbf{v}_{(\sigma)}
. By choosing
\textbf{w}(0)=\textbf{v}_{(\sigma)}
, we have
\overline{\textbf{w}}(s)=(\sigma-1)\sigma^{s}\textbf{v}_{(\sigma)}+\textbf{M}^{s}\textbf{m}
. The fact
\lim_{s\rightarrow\infty}\overline{\textbf{w}}(s)=\lim_{s\rightarrow\infty}(\sigma-1)\sigma^{s}\textbf{v}_{(\sigma)}=\textbf{0}
can be guaranteed if
|\sigma|<1
or
\sigma=1
.
That means, for every eigenvalue
\sigma
of the matrix
M
, if
\sigma\neq 1
then
|\sigma|<1
.
So, if the matrix
M
does not have an eigenvalue at
1
,
\textbf{w}(s+1)
converges to
\textbf{w}^{\infty}
exponentially because
\textbf{w}(s+1)-\textbf{w}^{\infty}=\textbf{M}\left(\textbf{w}(s)-\textbf{w}^{\infty}\right)
.

Assume that the matrix
M
has an eigenvalue at
1
and its algebraic multiplicity is bigger than its geometric multiplicity. That means there exists a vector
\textbf{u}_{(1)}
such that
(\textbf{M}-\textbf{I})\textbf{u}_{(1)}=\textbf{v}_{(1)}
.
Let
\textbf{w}(0)=\textbf{u}_{(1)}
, we have
\overline{\textbf{w}}(s)=\textbf{v}_{(1)}+\textbf{M}^{s}\textbf{m}
. This contradicts the asymptotic convergence to zero vector of
\overline{\textbf{w}}(s)
.
So, if
M
has an eigenvalue at
1
, its algebraic multiplicity is equal to its geometric multiplicity.
This fact implies there exists a nonsingular matrix
T
such that
\textbf{M}=\textbf{T}\left[\begin{matrix}\textbf{I}_{k}&\textbf{0}\\
\textbf{0}&\textbf{J}\end{matrix}\right]\textbf{T}^{-1}
where
k
is the algebraic multiplicity corresponding to the eigenvalue
1
of the matrix
M
and the matrix
J
has all eigenvalues lying within the unit open disk.
Since
\textbf{w}(s+1)-\textbf{w}^{\infty}=\textbf{M}\left(\textbf{w}(s)-\textbf{w}^{\infty}\right)
, we have

\textbf{w}(s+l)-\textbf{w}^{\infty}=\textbf{T}\left[\begin{matrix}\textbf{I}_{k}&\textbf{0}\\
\textbf{0}&\textbf{J}^{l}\end{matrix}\right]\textbf{T}^{-1}\left(\textbf{w}(s)-\textbf{w}^{\infty}\right).

The asymptotic convergence of the estimated solution
\textbf{w}(s)
to the converged point
\textbf{w}^{\infty}
implies

\textbf{0}=\lim_{l\rightarrow\infty}\left(\textbf{w}(s+l)-\textbf{w}^{\infty}\right)=\textbf{T}\left[\begin{matrix}\textbf{I}_{k}&\textbf{0}\\
\textbf{0}&\textbf{0}\end{matrix}\right]\textbf{T}^{-1}\left(\textbf{w}(s)-\textbf{w}^{\infty}\right)

because
\textbf{J}^{l}\rightarrow\textbf{0}
as
l\rightarrow\infty
.
So we have

\displaystyle\textbf{w}(s+1)-\textbf{w}^{\infty}

\displaystyle=\textbf{M}(\textbf{w}(s)-\textbf{w}^{\infty})-\textbf{0}

\displaystyle=\left(\textbf{M}-\textbf{T}\left[\begin{matrix}\textbf{I}_{k}&\textbf{0}\\
\textbf{0}&\textbf{0}\end{matrix}\right]\textbf{T}^{-1}\right)\left(\textbf{w}(s)-\textbf{w}^{\infty}\right)

\displaystyle=\textbf{T}\left[\begin{matrix}\textbf{0}_{k\times k}&\textbf{0}\\
\textbf{0}&\textbf{J}\end{matrix}\right]\textbf{T}^{-1}\left(\textbf{w}(s)-\textbf{w}^{\infty}\right)

\displaystyle=\textbf{T}\left[\begin{matrix}\textbf{0}_{k\times k}&\textbf{0}\\
\textbf{0}&\textbf{J}^{s+1}\end{matrix}\right]\textbf{T}^{-1}\left(\textbf{w}(0)-\textbf{w}^{\infty}\right).

Since all eigenvalues in the matrix
J
are within the unit open disk,
\textbf{w}(s)-\textbf{w}^{\infty}
converges exponentially to the origin.
∎According to Theorem
2
,
\bar{\textbf{z}}_{i}(s)=\textrm{blkrow}\{\textbf{I}_{\bar{n}_{i}},\textbf{0}\}\textbf{x}_{i}(s)
converges exponentially to
\bar{\textbf{z}}_{i}^{\infty}=\textrm{blkrow}\{\textbf{I}_{\bar{n}_{i}},\textbf{0}\}\textbf{x}_{i}^{\infty},\forall i\in\mathcal{V}
.
Lemma
1
guarantees that the stacked vector
\bar{\textbf{z}}_{i}^{\infty},\forall i\in\mathcal{V},
consists of parts corresponding to the agent
i
in one least square solution of the linear algebraic equation (
3
).

VII
Conclusion

This paper considers a linear algebraic equation
\textbf{H}\textbf{z}=\textbf{h}
when rows and columns of the coefficient matrix
H
are dispensed across a network of agents. By introducing some virtual variables, the problem of finding one least square solution to the considered linear algebraic equation is reformulated into a distributed optimization problem. Then, we apply a proximal ADMM algorithm to design a distributed method for agents to solve the formulated optimization problem cooperatively.
The estimated solution of every agent converges exponentially to its corresponding parts in one exact least square solution of the considered linear algebraic equation.

Orthogonality Measure on the Torus for Vector-Valued Jack Polynomials This paper is a contribution to the Special Issue on Orthogonal Polynomials, Special Functions and Applications. The full collection is available at http://www.emis.de/journals/SIGMA/OPSFA2015.html
Charles F. DUNKL

Abstract
For each irreducible module of the symmetric group on N N objects there is a set of parametrized nonsymmetric Jack polynomials in N N variables taking values in the module. These polynomials are simultaneous eigenfunctions of a commutative set of operators, self-adjoint with respect to certain Hermitian forms. These polynomials were studied by the author and J.-G. Luque using a Yang–Baxter graph technique. This paper constructs a matrix-valued measure on the N N -torus for which the polynomials are mutually orthogonal. The construction uses Fourier analysis techniques. Recursion relations for the Fourier–Stieltjes coefficients of the measure are established, and used to identify parameter values for which the construction fails. It is shown that the absolutely continuous part of the measure satisfies a first-order system of differential equations.

Introduction

The Jack polynomials form a parametrized basis of symmetric polynomials. A special case of these consists of the Schur polynomials, important in the
character theory of the symmetric groups. By means of a commutative algebra of
differential-difference operators the theory was extended to nonsymmetric Jack
polynomials, again a parametrized basis but now for all polynomials in
N

variables. These polynomials are orthogonal for several different inner
products, and in each case they are simultaneous eigenfunctions of a commutative set of self-adjoint operators. These inner products are invariant
under permutations of the coordinates, that is, the symmetric group. One of
these inner products is that of
L^{2}\big(\mathbb{T}^{N},K_{\kappa}(x)\mathrm{d}m(x)\big)
, where

\displaystyle\mathbb{T}^{N}:=\big\{x\in\mathbb{C}^{N}\colon|x_{j}|=1,\,1\leq j\leq N\big\},

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}m(x)=(2\pi)^{-N}\mathrm{d}\theta_{1}\cdots\mathrm{d}\theta_{N},\qquad x_{j}=\exp(\mathrm{i}\theta_{j}),\qquad-\pi<\theta_{j}\leq\pi,1\leq j\leq N,

\displaystyle K_{\kappa}(x)=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq N}|x_{i}-x_{j}|^{2\kappa},\qquad\kappa>-\frac{1}{N};

defining the
N
-torus, the Haar measure on the torus, and the weight function
respectively. Beerends and Opdam
[
1
]
discovered this
orthogonality property of symmetric Jack polynomials. Opdam
[
12
]

established orthogonality structures on the torus for trigonometric
polynomials associated with Weyl groups; the nonsymmetric Jack polynomials
form a special case. Details on the derivation of the norm formulae can be
found in the treatise by Xu and the author
[
6
, Section 10.6.3]
. The
weight function
K_{\kappa}
turned out to be the square of the base state for
the Calogero–Sutherland quantum mechanical model of
N
identical particles
located at
x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{N}
on the circle with a
1/r^{2}

potential. This means that the particles repel each other with a force
corresponding to a potential
C|x_{i}-x_{j}|^{-2}
. See
Lapointe and Vinet
[
10
]
for the construction of wavefunctions
in terms of Jack polynomials for this model. More recently Griffeth
[
8
]
constructed vector-valued Jack polynomials for the family

G\left(n,p,N\right)
of complex reflection groups. These are the groups
of permutation matrices (exactly one nonzero entry in each row and each
column) whose nonzero entries are
n^{th}
roots of unity and the product of
these entries is a
(n/p)^{\rm th}
root of unity. The symmetric
groups and the hyperoctahedral groups are the special cases
G(1,1,N)
and
G(2,1,N)
respectively. The term
“vector-valued” means that the polynomials
take values in irreducible modules of the underlying group, and the action of
the group is on the range as well as the domain of the polynomials. The author
[
3
]
together with Luque
[
5
]
investigated the
symmetric group case more intensively. The results from these two papers are
the foundation for the present work.

Since the torus structure is such an important aspect of the theory of Jack
polynomials it seemed like an obvious research topic to find the role of the
torus in the vector-valued Jack case. Is there a matrix-valued weight function
on the torus for which the vector-valued Jack polynomials are mutually
orthogonal? Some explorations in the
N=3
and
N=4
situation showed that the
theory is much more complicated than the ordinary (scalar) case. For
two-dimensional representations the weight function has hypergeometric
function entries (see
[
4
]
); this is quite different from the
rather natural product
\prod\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq N}|x_{i}-x_{j}|^{2\kappa}
, a power of the discriminant.

In this paper we will produce a matrix-valued measure on the torus for which
the vector-valued nonsymmetric polynomials are mutually orthogonal. The result
applies to arbitrary irreducible representations of the symmetric groups. In
each case there is a permitted range of the parameter. We start with a concise
outline of the definitions and construction of the polynomials using the
Yang–Baxter graph technique in Section
2
, based on
[
3
]
and
[
5
]
. Section
3
contains the construction of the abstract
Hermitian form which is designed to act like an integral over the torus; that
is, multiplication by a coordinate function
x_{j}
is an isometry. The method
is algebraic and based on the Yang–Baxter graph. In Section
4
we use
techniques from Fourier analysis to produce the desired measure. The Section
begins by using the formulae from the previous sections to define the
hypothetical Fourier–Stieltjes coefficients, defined on
\mathbb{Z}^{N}
which
is the dual group of the torus, a multiplicative group, and then applying a
matrix version of a theorem of Bochner about positive-definite functions to
get the measure. There is an application of approximate-identity theory using
a Cesàro kernel to construct a sequence of positive matrix-valued Laurent
polynomials which converges to the orthogonality measure.

Section
5
develops a recurrence relation satisfied by the Fourier–Stieltjes
coefficients of the orthogonality measure. The relation allows an inductive
calculation for the coefficients (but actual work, even with symbolic
computation software, may not be feasible unless the dimensions are reasonably
small), and it describes the list of parameter values (certain rational
numbers) for which the construction fails.

The scalar weight function on the torus

\displaystyle K_{\kappa}(x):=\prod\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq N}\big\{(x_{i}-x_{j})\big(x_{i}^{-1}-x_{j}^{-1}\big)\big\}^{\kappa}

satisfies a first-order differential system,

\displaystyle x_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}K_{\kappa}(x)=\kappa K(x)\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{x_{i}+x_{j}}{x_{i}-x_{j}},\qquad 1\leq i\leq N.

In Section
6
we show that there is an analogous matrix differential system
which is solved in a distribution sense by the orthogonality measure. We
outline a result asserting that the orthogonality measure restricted to the
complement of
\bigcup\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq N}\{x\colon x_{i}=x_{j}\}
is equal to an analytic solution of the differential system times the Haar
measure
\mathrm{d}m
. Finally there is Appendix
A
containing some technical
background results.

\alpha^{+}=(4,2,1,1)
. Let
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{N,+}
denote the set of partition multi-indices, that

\big\{\lambda\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{N}\colon\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{N}\big\}

Definition 2.1
.

\displaystyle\alpha\prec\beta~\Longleftrightarrow\sum_{j=1}^{i}\alpha_{j}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{i}\beta_{j},\qquad 1\leq i\leq N,\qquad\alpha\neq\beta,

\displaystyle\alpha\vartriangleleft\beta\Longleftrightarrow(|\alpha|=|\beta|)\wedge\big[(\alpha^{+}\prec\beta^{+})\vee(\alpha^{+}=\beta^{+}\wedge\alpha\prec\beta)\big].

For example
(3,2,1)\vartriangleleft(0,2,4)\vartriangleleft(4,0,2)
, while
(4,1,1)
,

are not
\vartriangleleft
-comparable. The

symmetric group

\mathcal{S}_{N}
, the set of permutations of

\mathbb{C}^{N}
by permutation of
coordinates. The action is extended to polynomials by
wp(x)=p(xw)
where
(xw)_{i}=x_{w(i)}

(consider
x
as a row vector and
w
as a permutation matrix,
[w]_{ij}=\delta_{i,w(j)}
, then

). This is a representation of
\mathcal{S}_{N}
, that is,
w_{1}(w_{2}p)(x)=(w_{2}p)(xw_{1})=p(xw_{1}w_{2})=(w_{1}w_{2})p(x)
for all

.

Furthermore
\mathcal{S}_{N}
is generated by reflections in the mirrors

\{x\colon x_{i}-x_{j}=0\}
for
1\leq i<j\leq N
. These are

transpositions,
denoted by
(i,j)
, interchanging

x_{i}
and
x_{j}
. Define the
\mathcal{S}_{N}
-action on
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{N}
so that
(xw)^{\alpha}=x^{w\alpha}

\displaystyle(xw)^{\alpha}=\prod_{i=1}^{N}x_{w(i)}^{\alpha_{i}}=\prod_{j=1}^{N}x_{j}^{\alpha_{w^{-1}(j)}},

that is
(w\alpha)_{i}=\alpha_{w^{-1}(i)}
(take

\alpha
as column vector, then
w\alpha=[w]\alpha
).

The
simple reflections

s_{i}:=(i,i+1)
,

, generate
\mathcal{S}_{N}
. They are the key devices for applying

\displaystyle s_{i}s_{j}=s_{j}s_{i},\qquad|i-j|\geq 2;

\displaystyle s_{i}s_{i+1}s_{i}=s_{i+1}s_{i}s_{i+1}.

We consider the situation where the group
\mathcal{S}_{N}
acts on the range

\mathcal{S}_{N}
-modules) on which
\mathcal{S}_{N}
has an irreducible
unitary (orthogonal) representation:
\tau\colon\mathcal{S}_{N}\rightarrow O_{m}(\mathbb{R})
(

). See James and Kerber
[
9
]
for representation theory, including a modern discussion of
Young’s methods. We will specify an orthogonal basis and the images

\tau(s_{i})
for each
i
, which suffices for our purposes.
Identify
\tau
with a partition of
N
:
(\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\ldots)\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{N,+}
such that
|\tau|=N
. The length of
\tau
is
\ell(\tau)=\max\{i\colon\tau_{i}>0\}
. There is a Ferrers diagram of shape
\tau
(this
diagram is given the same name), with boxes at points
(i,j)

with

and

. A

of shape

is a filling of the boxes with numbers, and a

(N)
or one-column
(1,1,\ldots,1)

. The

\displaystyle\operatorname{hook}(\tau;i,j):=\tau_{i}-j+\#\big\{k\colon i<k\leq\ell(\tau)\wedge j\leq\tau_{k}\big\}+1.

We will need the key quantity

2.2
Vector-valued nonsymmetric Jack polynomials

The main concern of this paper is
\mathcal{P}_{\tau}=\mathcal{P}\otimes V_{\tau}
, the space of
V_{\tau}
valued polynomials in
x
, which is
equipped with the
\mathcal{S}_{N}
action:

\displaystyle w\big(x^{\alpha}\otimes T\big)=(xw)^{\alpha}\otimes\tau(w)T,\qquad\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{N},\qquad T\in\mathcal{Y}(\tau),

extended by linearity to

\displaystyle wp(x)=\tau(w)p(xw),\qquad p\in\mathcal{P}_{\tau}.

Definition 2.5
.

The
Dunkl
and
Cherednik–Dunkl
operators are (
1\leq i\leq N
,
p\in\mathcal{P}_{\tau}
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{i}p(x):=\partial_{i}p(x)+\kappa\sum_{j\neq i}\tau(i,j)\frac{p(x)-p(x(i,j))}{x_{i}-x_{j}},

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{i}p(x):=\mathcal{D}_{i}(x_{i}p(x))-\kappa\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\tau(i,j)p(x(i,j)).

The commutation relations analogous to the scalar case hold, that is,

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{i}\mathcal{D}_{j}=\mathcal{D}_{j}\mathcal{D}_{i},\qquad\mathcal{U}_{i}\mathcal{U}_{j}=\mathcal{U}_{j}\mathcal{U}_{i},\qquad 1\leq i,j\leq N,

\displaystyle w\mathcal{D}_{i}=\mathcal{D}_{w(i)}w,\qquad\forall\,w\in\mathcal{S}_{N},\qquad s_{j}\mathcal{U}_{i}=\mathcal{U}_{i}s_{j},\qquad j\neq i-1,i,

\displaystyle s_{i}\mathcal{U}_{i}s_{i}=\mathcal{U}_{i+1}+\kappa s_{i},\qquad\mathcal{U}_{i}s_{i}=s_{i}\mathcal{U}_{i+1}+\kappa,\qquad\mathcal{U}_{i+1}s_{i}=s_{i}\mathcal{U}_{i}-\kappa.

The simultaneous eigenfunctions of
\{\mathcal{U}_{i}\}
are
called (vector-valued) nonsymmetric Jack polynomials (NSJP). For generic

\kappa
these eigenfunctions form a basis of
\mathcal{P}_{\tau}
(we will
specify the excluded rational values in the sequel). They have a triangularity
property with respect to the partial order
\vartriangleright
. However the
structure does not merely rely on leading terms of the type
x^{\alpha}\otimes T
. We need the rank function:

Definition 2.6
.

For
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{N}
,
1\leq i\leq N

\displaystyle r_{\alpha}(i):=\#\{j\colon\alpha_{j}>\alpha_{i}\}+\#\{j\colon 1\leq j\leq i,\,\alpha_{j}=\alpha_{i}\},

then

A consequence is that

, the nonincreasing
rearrangement of
\alpha

\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{N}
. For example

\alpha=(1,2,1,4)

(recall

r_{\alpha}=I
if and
only if
\alpha
is a partition (
\alpha_{1}\geq\alpha_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\alpha_{N}
).

For each
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{N}
and
T\in\mathcal{Y}(\tau)
there is a NSJP
\zeta_{\alpha,T}
with leading term

x^{\alpha}\otimes\tau\big(r_{\alpha}^{-1}\big)T
, that is,

\displaystyle\zeta_{\alpha,T}=x^{\alpha}\otimes\tau\big(r_{\alpha}^{-1}\big)T+\sum_{\alpha\vartriangleright\beta}x^{\beta}\otimes t_{\alpha\beta}(\kappa),\qquad t_{\alpha\beta}(\kappa)\in V_{\tau},

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{i}\zeta_{\alpha,T}=\big(\alpha_{i}+1+\kappa c(r_{\alpha}(i),T)\big)\zeta_{\alpha,T},\qquad 1\leq i\leq N.

2.3
The Yang–Baxter graph

The NSJP’s can be constructed by means of a Yang–Baxter graph. The details are
in
[
5
]
; this paper has several figures illustrating some typical graphs.

A node consists of

\displaystyle(\alpha,T,\xi_{\alpha.T},r_{\alpha},\zeta_{\alpha,T}),

where
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{N}
,
\xi_{\alpha,T}
is the spectral vector

\xi_{\alpha,T}(i)=\alpha_{i}+1+\kappa c(r_{\alpha}(i),T)
,

. The root is
\big(\mathbf{0},T_{0},[1+\kappa c(i,T_{0})]_{i=1}^{N},I,1\otimes T_{0}\big)
where
T_{0}
is formed by entering

N,N-1,\ldots,1
column-by-column in the Ferrers diagram, for example

3
Hermitian forms

For a complex vector space
V
a Hermitian form is a mapping
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\colon V\otimes V\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
such that

\langle u,cv\rangle=c\langle u,v\rangle
,

\langle u,v_{1}+v_{2}\rangle=\langle u,v_{1}\rangle+\langle u,v_{2}\rangle
and
\langle u,v\rangle=\overline{\langle v,u\rangle}
for
u,v_{1},v_{2}\in V
,

c\in\mathbb{C}
. The form is positive semidefinite if
\langle u,u\rangle\geq 0
for all
u\in V
. The concern of this paper is with a particular Hermitian form on
\mathcal{P}_{\tau}
which has the properties
(for all
f,g\in\mathcal{P}_{\tau}
):

\displaystyle\big\langle 1\otimes T,1\otimes T^{\prime}\big\rangle=\big\langle T,T^{\prime}\big\rangle_{0},\qquad T,T^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Y}(\tau),

(3.1)

\displaystyle\langle wf,wg\rangle=\langle f,g\rangle,\qquad w\in\mathcal{S}_{N},

\displaystyle\langle x_{i}\mathcal{D}_{i}f,g\rangle=\langle f,x_{i}\mathcal{D}_{i}g\rangle,\qquad 1\leq i\leq N.

The commutation
\mathcal{U}_{i}=\mathcal{D}_{i}x_{i}-\kappa\sum\limits_{j<i}(i,j)=x_{i}\mathcal{D}_{i}+1+\kappa\sum\limits_{j>i}(i,j)

together with
\langle(i,j)f,g\rangle=\langle f,(i,j)g\rangle
show that
\langle\mathcal{U}_{i}f,g\rangle=\langle f,\mathcal{U}_{i}g\rangle
for all
i
. Thus the uniqueness of the spectral vectors discussed above implies that

\langle\zeta_{\alpha,T},\zeta_{\beta,T^{\prime}}\rangle=0

whenever
(\alpha,T)\neq(\beta,T^{\prime})
. In
particular polynomials homogeneous of different degrees are mutually
orthogonal, by the basis property of
\{\zeta_{\alpha,T}\}
. We
can deduce contiguity relations corresponding to the steps described above and
implied by the properties of the form. Consider step type (1) with

\displaystyle s_{i}\zeta_{\alpha,T}=\zeta_{s_{i}\alpha,T}+b^{\prime}\zeta_{\alpha,T},\qquad b^{\prime}=\frac{\kappa}{\xi_{\alpha,T}(i)-\xi_{\alpha,T}(i+1)}.

The conditions
\langle s_{i}\zeta_{\alpha,T},s_{i}\zeta_{\alpha,T}\rangle=\langle\zeta_{\alpha,T},\zeta_{\alpha,T}\rangle
and
\langle\zeta_{\alpha,T},\zeta_{s_{i}\alpha,T}\rangle=0

imply

\displaystyle\langle\zeta_{\alpha,T},\zeta_{\alpha,T}\rangle=\big\langle\zeta_{s_{i}\alpha,T}+b^{\prime}\zeta_{\alpha,T},\zeta_{s_{i}\alpha,T}+b^{\prime}\zeta_{\alpha,T}\big\rangle=\langle\zeta_{s_{i}\alpha,T},\zeta_{s_{i}\alpha,T}\rangle+b^{\prime 2}\langle\zeta_{\alpha,T},\zeta_{\alpha,T}\rangle,

\displaystyle\langle\zeta_{s_{i}\alpha,T},\zeta_{s_{i}\alpha,T}\rangle=\big(1-b^{\prime 2}\big)\langle\zeta_{\alpha,T},\zeta_{\alpha,T}\rangle.

A necessary condition that the form be positive-definite (

implies

) is that

in each of the
possible steps. Since (with

and

\displaystyle 1-b^{\prime 2}=\frac{[\alpha_{i+1}-\alpha_{i}+(c(\ell,T)-c(j,T)+1)\kappa][\alpha_{i+1}-\alpha_{i}+(c(\ell,T)-c(j,T)-1)\kappa]}{[\alpha_{i+1}-\alpha_{i}+(c(\ell,T)-c(j,T))\kappa]^{2}},

4
Fourier–Stieltjes coefficients on the torus

The torus
\mathbb{T}^{N}:=\big\{x\in\mathbb{C}^{N}\colon|x_{i}|=1,\,1\leq i\leq N\big\}
is a multiplicative compact
abelian group with dual group
\mathbb{Z}^{N}
. We will use this property to
find the measure of orthogonality for the NSJP’s on the torus. First we
produce the Fourier–Stieltjes coefficients of the hypothetical measure and
then use a matrix version of a theorem of Bochner to deduce the existence of
the measure.

When
\kappa
is generic the NSJP’s form a basis for
\mathcal{P}_{\tau}
and
it is possible to make the definition

\displaystyle\widetilde{A}\big(\alpha,\beta,T,T^{\prime}\big):=\big(\langle T,T\rangle_{0}\big\langle T^{\prime},T^{\prime}\big\rangle_{0}\big)^{-1/2}\big\langle x^{\alpha}\otimes T,x^{\beta}\otimes T^{\prime}\big\rangle_{\mathbb{T}}

for
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{N}
and
T,T^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Y}(\tau)
. In effect this uses the orthonormal basis of
V_{\tau}
. By
the symmetry of the form
\widetilde{A}(\alpha,\beta,T,T^{\prime})=\widetilde{A}(\beta,\alpha,T^{\prime},T)
. By
Proposition

|\alpha|\neq|\beta|
implies
\widetilde{A}(\alpha,\beta,T,T^{\prime})=0
. Another consequence is
\widetilde{A}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{0},T,T^{\prime})=\delta_{T,T^{\prime}}
.

Definition 4.1
.

For each
\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
with
\sum_{i=1}^{N}\gamma_{i}=0
let

\gamma_{i}^{\pi}=\max(\gamma_{i},0)
and
\gamma_{i}^{\nu}=-\min(\gamma_{i},0)
for

; then
\gamma=\gamma^{\pi}-\gamma^{\nu}
and
\gamma^{\pi},\gamma^{\nu}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{N}
. Furthermore
|\gamma^{\pi}|=|\gamma^{\nu}|
and
\sum_{i}|\gamma_{i}|=|\gamma^{\pi}|+|\gamma^{\nu}|
is even.

Introduce the index set
\boldsymbol{Z}_{N}
and its graded components by

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{Z}_{N}:=\left\{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}\colon\sum_{i=1}^{N}\alpha_{i}=0\right\},

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{Z}_{N,n}:=\left\{\alpha\in\boldsymbol{Z}_{N}\colon\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\alpha_{i}|=2n\right\},\qquad n=0,1,2,\ldots.

Formula (
A.1
) for

is in Appendix
A
.

Definition 4.2
.

For
\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
the matrix
A_{\gamma}
(of size
\#\mathcal{Y}(\tau)\times\#\mathcal{Y}(\tau)
) is given by

\displaystyle(A_{\gamma})_{T,T^{\prime}}=\widetilde{A}\big(\gamma^{\pi},\gamma^{\nu},T,T^{\prime}\big),\qquad T,T^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Y}(\tau),\qquad\gamma\in\boldsymbol{Z}_{N},

\displaystyle A_{\gamma}=0,\qquad\gamma\notin\boldsymbol{Z}_{N}.

Proposition 4.3
.

Suppose
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{N}
and
T,T^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Y}(\tau)
then
\widetilde{A}(\alpha,\beta,T,T^{\prime})=(A_{\alpha-\beta})_{T,T^{\prime}}
.

Proof.

If
|\alpha|\neq|\beta|
then

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}(\alpha_{i}-\beta_{i})\neq 0
,
\widetilde{A}(\alpha,\beta,T,T^{\prime})=0
and
A_{\alpha-\beta}=0
by
definition. If
|\alpha|=|\beta|

let
\zeta_{i}=\min(\alpha_{i},\beta_{i})
for
1\leq i\leq N

then
x^{\zeta}
is a factor of both
x^{\alpha}
and
x^{\beta}
; by
Proposition
3.4

\langle x^{\alpha}\otimes T,x^{\beta}\otimes T^{\prime}\rangle_{\mathbb{T}}=\langle x^{\alpha-\zeta}\otimes T,x^{\beta-\zeta}\otimes T^{\prime}\rangle_{\mathbb{T}}
. By
construction
(\alpha-\beta)^{\pi}=\alpha-\zeta,(\alpha-\beta)^{\nu}=\beta-\zeta
. It follows that

\displaystyle\big\langle x^{\alpha}\otimes T,x^{\beta}\otimes T^{\prime}\big\rangle=T^{\ast}A_{\alpha-\beta}T^{\prime}=\big(\langle T,T\rangle_{0}\big\langle T^{\prime},T^{\prime}\big\rangle_{0}\big)^{1/2}(A_{\alpha-\beta})_{T,T^{\prime}}.

∎

For a formal Laurent series
h(x)=\sum\limits_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}c_{\alpha}x^{\alpha}
let
\operatorname{CT}(h(x))=c_{\mathbf{0}}
, the constant term. Then

\displaystyle\big\langle x^{\alpha}\otimes T,x^{\beta}\otimes T^{\prime}\big\rangle=\big(\langle T,T\rangle_{0}\big\langle T^{\prime},T^{\prime}\big\rangle_{0}\big)^{1/2}\operatorname{CT}\bigg(x^{-\alpha}\sum_{\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}(A_{\gamma})_{T,T^{\prime}}x^{\gamma}x^{\beta}\bigg).

In the next section we investigate analytical properties of the formal series,
but first we consider algebraic properties, that is, those not needing any
convergence results.

Theorem 4.4
.

Suppose
\gamma\in\boldsymbol{Z}_{N}
and
w\in\mathcal{S}_{N}
then

A_{-\gamma}=A_{\gamma}^{\ast}
and
A_{w\gamma}=\tau(w)A_{\gamma}\tau\big(w^{-1}\big)
.

Proof.

The relation
\widetilde{A}(\alpha,\beta,T,T^{\prime})=\widetilde{A}(\beta,\alpha,T^{\prime},T)
shows
(A_{\alpha-\beta})_{T,T^{\prime}}=(A_{\beta-\alpha})_{T^{\prime},T}
. By definition

\displaystyle\big\langle w\big(x^{\alpha}\otimes T\big),w\big(x^{\beta}\otimes T^{\prime}\big)\big\rangle_{\mathbb{T}}=\big\langle x^{w\alpha}\otimes\tau(w)T,x^{w\beta}\otimes\tau(w)T^{\prime}\big\rangle_{\mathbb{T}}

\displaystyle\hphantom{\big\langle w\big( x^{\alpha}\otimes T\big) ,w\big( x^{\beta}\otimes T^{\prime}\big) \big\rangle_{\mathbb{T}}}{}=\big(\langle T,T\rangle_{0}\big\langle T^{\prime},T^{\prime}\big\rangle_{0}\big)^{1/2}T^{\ast}\tau(w)^{\ast}A_{w\alpha-w\beta}\tau(w)T^{\prime}

\displaystyle\hphantom{\big\langle w\big( x^{\alpha}\otimes T\big) ,w\big( x^{\beta}\otimes T^{\prime}\big) \big\rangle_{\mathbb{T}}}{}=\big\langle x^{\alpha}\otimes T,x^{\beta}\otimes T^{\prime}\big\rangle_{\mathbb{T}}=\big(\langle T,T\rangle_{0}\big\langle T^{\prime},T^{\prime}\big\rangle_{0}\big)^{1/2}T^{\ast}A_{\alpha-\beta}T^{\prime}

and thus
A_{\gamma}=\tau(w)^{-1}A_{w\gamma}\tau(w)
(recall
\tau
is real-orthogonal so
\tau(w)^{\ast}=\tau\big(w^{-1}\big)

∎

Summing over the graded components

produces Laurent

Definition 4.5
.

For
n=0,1,2,\ldots
let

\displaystyle H_{n}(x):=\sum_{\alpha\in\boldsymbol{Z}_{N,n}}A_{\alpha}x^{\alpha},

a Laurent polynomial with matrix coefficients.

For complex Laurent polynomials

Proposition 4.6
.

Suppose
n=0,1,2,\ldots
and
w\in\mathcal{S}_{N}
then

H_{n}(xw)=\tau(w)^{-1}H_{n}(x)\tau(w)
and
H_{n}(x)^{\ast}=H_{n}(x)
.

Proof.

Compute

\displaystyle H_{n}(xw)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in\boldsymbol{Z}_{N,n}}A_{\alpha}(wx)^{\alpha}=\sum_{\alpha\in\boldsymbol{Z}_{N,n}}A_{\alpha}x^{w\alpha}=\sum_{\beta\in\boldsymbol{Z}_{N,n}}A_{w^{-1}\beta}x^{\beta}

\displaystyle=\tau\big(w^{-1}\big)\sum_{\beta\in\boldsymbol{Z}_{N,n}}A_{\beta}x^{\beta}\tau(w)=\tau(w)^{-1}H_{n}(x)\tau(w).

Also
H_{n}(x)^{\ast}=\sum\limits_{\alpha\in\boldsymbol{Z}_{N,n}}A_{\alpha}^{\ast}x^{-\alpha}=\sum\limits_{\alpha\in\boldsymbol{Z}_{N,n}}A_{-\alpha}x^{-\alpha}=H_{n}(x)
.
∎

As a consequence we find an important commutation satisfied by a particular
point value of
H_{n}(x)
(recall the
N
-cycle
w_{0}=(1,2,\ldots,N)
).

Corollary 4.7
.

Suppose
n=1,2,3,\ldots
then
\tau(w_{0})^{-1}H_{n}(x_{0})\tau(w_{0})=H_{n}(x_{0})
, where

x_{0}=\big(1,\omega,\ldots,\omega^{N-1}\big)
,
\omega=\exp\frac{2\pi\mathrm{i}}{N}
.

Proof.

By definition
x_{0}w_{0}=\big(\omega,\ldots,\omega^{N-1},1\big)=\omega x_{0}
. Each monomial
x^{\alpha}
for
\alpha\in\boldsymbol{Z}_{N}
is
homogeneous of degree zero (suppose

H_{n}(x_{0}w_{0})=\tau(w_{0})^{-1}H_{n}(x_{0})\tau(w_{0})
.
∎

We turn to the harmonic analysis significance of the matrices
\{A_{\alpha}\}
. For an integrable function
f
on
\mathbb{T}^{N}
the
Fourier transform (coefficient) is

\displaystyle\widehat{f}(\alpha)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}f(x)x^{-\alpha}\mathrm{d}m(x),\qquad\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{N},

where
x:=(\exp(\mathrm{i}\theta_{1}),\ldots,\exp(\mathrm{i}\theta_{N}))
and
\mathrm{d}m(x)=(2\pi)^{-N}\mathrm{d}\theta_{1}\cdots\mathrm{d}\theta_{N}
;
and for a Baire measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{T}^{N}
the Fourier–Stieltjes
transform is

\displaystyle\widehat{\mu}(\alpha)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}x^{-\alpha}\mathrm{d}\mu(x),\qquad\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}.

We will show that there is a matrix-valued measure
\mu
, positive in a
certain sense, such that
\widehat{\mu}(\alpha)=A_{\alpha}
for
all
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
provided that
-1/h_{\tau}<\kappa<1/h_{\tau}
.
There is a version of a theorem of Bochner about positive-definite functions
on a locally compact abelian group which proves this claim. The details of the
proof and some consequences are in Appendix
A
.

Let
n=\dim V_{\tau}
and identify
V_{\tau}
with
\mathbb{C}^{n}
whose
elements are considered as column vectors (in effect we use indices
1\leq i\leq n
instead of
\{T\in\mathcal{Y}(\tau)\}
). The inner product on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
is
\langle u,v\rangle:=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\overline{u_{i}}v_{i}
, and the norm is
|v|=\sqrt{\langle v,v\rangle}
. Note that
\langle u,Av\rangle=u^{\ast}Av
. A positive-definite matrix

satisfies

\langle u,Pu\rangle\geq 0
for all

(this
implies
P^{\ast}=P
).

Definition 4.8
.

A function
F\colon\mathbb{Z}^{N}\rightarrow M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
is
positive-definite if

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}f(\alpha)^{\ast}F(\alpha-\beta)f(\beta)\geq 0

for any finitely supported
\mathbb{C}^{n}
-valued function
f
on

\mathbb{Z}^{N}
.

Theorem 4.9
.

Suppose
F
is positive-definite then there exist Baire measures
\{\mu_{jk}\colon 1\leq j,k\leq n\}
on
\mathbb{T}^{N}
such that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}x^{-\alpha}\mathrm{d}\mu_{jk}(x)=F(\alpha)_{jk},\qquad\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{N},\qquad 1\leq j,k\leq n.

Furthermore each
\mu_{jj}
is positive and

\displaystyle\left\langle f,g\right\rangle_{F}:=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}\overline{f(x)_{i}}g(x)_{j}\mathrm{d}\mu_{ij}(x)

defines a positive-semidefinite Hermitian form on
C\big(\mathbb{T}^{N};\mathbb{C}^{n}\big)

(
continuous
\mathbb{C}^{n}
-valued functions on

\mathbb{T}^{N})
satisfying
|\langle f,g\rangle_{F}|\leq B\|f\|_{\infty}\|g\|_{\infty}
for
f,g\in C\big(\mathbb{T}^{N};\mathbb{C}^{n}\big)
with

.

The proof is in Appendix
A.1
and Theorem
A.3
. (In
general the measures
\mu_{jk}
are not real-valued for
j\neq k
.) For
notational simplicity we introduce

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}f(x)^{\ast}\mathrm{d}\mu(x)g(x):=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}\overline{f(x)_{i}}g(x)_{j}\mathrm{d}\mu_{ij}(x).

(4.1)

To show that
\alpha\mapsto A_{\alpha}
is positive-definite let
f
be a
finitely supported
\mathbb{C}^{n}
-valued function
f
on
\mathbb{Z}^{N}

and let
p(x)=\sum\limits_{\alpha,T}\langle T,T\rangle_{0}^{-1/2}f_{T}(\alpha)x^{\alpha}\otimes T

be the associated Laurent polynomial (now we use the
T
indices on

\mathbb{C}^{n}
). Because this is a finite sum there is a nonnegative integer

m
such that
e_{N}^{m}p(x)
is polynomial (no negative
powers). Then for
-1/h_{\tau}<\kappa<1/h_{\tau}

\displaystyle 0\leq\big\langle e_{N}^{m}p,e_{N}^{m}p\big\rangle_{\mathbb{T}}=\!\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\sum_{T,T^{\prime}}\big(\langle T,T\rangle_{0}\big\langle T^{\prime},T^{\prime}\big\rangle_{0}\big)^{-1/2}\overline{f_{T}(\alpha)}f_{T^{\prime}}(\beta)\big\langle x^{\alpha+m\boldsymbol{1}}\otimes T,x^{\beta+m\boldsymbol{1}}\otimes T^{\prime}\big\rangle

\displaystyle\hphantom{0 \leq\big\langle e_{N}^{m}p,e_{N}^{m}p\big\rangle_{\mathbb{T}}}=\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\sum_{T,T^{\prime}}\overline{f_{T}(\alpha)}f_{T^{\prime}}(\beta)(A_{\alpha-\beta})_{T,T^{\prime}}.

Let
\mu=[\mu_{T,T^{\prime}}]
be the matrix of measures
produced by the theorem, that is,

\displaystyle(A_{\alpha})_{T,T^{\prime}}=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}x^{-\alpha}\mathrm{d}\mu_{T,T^{\prime}}(x),\qquad\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{N},\qquad T,T^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Y}(\tau).

Theorem 4.10
.

For
-1/h_{\tau}<\kappa<1/h_{\tau}
there exists a matrix of Baire measures

\mu=[\mu_{T,T^{\prime}}]
on
\mathbb{T}^{N}
such that

\displaystyle\langle f,g\rangle_{\mathbb{T}}=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}f(x)^{\ast}\mathrm{d}\mu(x)g(x)

for all Laurent polynomials
f
,
g
with coefficients in
V_{\tau}
, in
particular for all NSJP’s
f
,
g
.

Of course we want more detailed information about these measures. The first
step is to apply an approximate identity, a tool from the convolution
structure for measures and functions on the torus. We consider Cesàro
summation of the series
\sum_{\alpha}A_{\alpha}x^{\alpha}
based on summing
first over each
\boldsymbol{Z}_{N,n}
. Set

\displaystyle S_{n}(x):=\sum_{\alpha\in\boldsymbol{Z}_{N,n}}x^{\alpha},

a Laurent polynomial, and the corresponding
(C,\delta)
-kernel
(for
\delta>0
) is defined to be (the Pochhammer symbol is
(t)_{m}=\prod\limits_{i=1}^{m}(t+i-1)
)

\displaystyle\sigma_{n}^{\delta}(x):=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{(-n)_{k}}{(-n-\delta)_{k}}S_{k}(x).

The point is that
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{(-n)_{k}}{(-n-\delta)_{k}}=1
for fixed
k
. In terms of
convolution

\displaystyle\sigma_{n}^{\delta}\ast\mu(x)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}\sigma_{n}^{\delta}\big(xy^{-1}\big)\mathrm{d}\mu(y),

\displaystyle\widehat{(\sigma_{n}^{\delta}\ast\mu)}(\alpha)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}x^{-\alpha}\sigma_{n}^{\delta}\big(xy^{-1}\big)\mathrm{d}\mu(y)\mathrm{d}m(x)

\displaystyle\hphantom{\widehat{( \sigma_{n}^{\delta}\ast\mu) }(\alpha)}{}=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}(xy)^{-\alpha}\sigma_{n}^{\delta}(x)\mathrm{d}\mu(y)\mathrm{d}m(x)=A_{\alpha}\widehat{\sigma_{n}^{\delta}}(\alpha),

and
\widehat{\sigma_{n}^{\delta}}(\alpha)=\frac{(-n)_{k}}{(-n-\delta)_{k}}
for
\alpha\in\boldsymbol{Z}_{N,k}
for
0\leq k\leq n
and
=0
for
|\alpha|>2n
(or
\alpha\notin\boldsymbol{Z}_{N}
). Thus
\sigma_{n}^{\delta}\ast\mu(x)=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}\frac{(-n)_{k}}{(-n-\delta)_{k}}H_{k}(x)
. In
fact
\sigma_{n}^{N-1}(x)\geq 0
for all
x\in\mathbb{T}^{N}

(Corollary
4.12
below) which implies
\sigma_{n}^{N-1}\ast\mu

converges to
\mu
in a useful sense (weak-
\ast
, see Theorem
4.17
(4)) and
\sigma_{n}^{N-1}\ast\mu(x)
is a Laurent polynomial
all of whose point values are positive-semidefinite matrices. Also
\big\|\sigma_{n}^{N-1}\big\|_{1}:=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}\big|\sigma_{n}^{N-1}\big|\mathrm{d}m=1
.

The
complete symmetric polynomial
in
N
variables and degree
n
is
given by

\displaystyle h_{n}(x):=\sum\left\{x^{\alpha}\colon\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{N}\colon\sum_{i=1}^{N}\alpha_{i}=n\right\}.

Recall

\displaystyle\#\left\{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{N}\colon\sum_{i=1}^{N}\alpha_{i}=m\right\}=\frac{(N)_{m}}{m!}\qquad\text{for}\quad m=0,1,2,3,\ldots.

Theorem 4.11
.

For
n\geq 0

\displaystyle h_{n}\left(\frac{1}{x_{1}},\frac{1}{x_{2}},\ldots,\frac{1}{x_{N}}\right)h_{n}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N})=\frac{(N)_{n}}{n!}\sigma_{n}^{N-1}(x).

(4.2)

Proof.

The product is a sum of terms
x^{\alpha-\beta}
with
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{N}
and
|\alpha|=n=|\beta|
. For example the term
x^{0}=1
appears exactly

\frac{(N)_{n}}{n!}
times, because the number of terms in

h_{n}
is
\frac{(N)_{n}}{n!}
. Consider a fixed
\gamma\in\boldsymbol{Z}_{N,m}
for some
m
with
0\leq m\leq n
. The term

x^{\gamma}
appears in the product for each pair
(\alpha,\beta)
with

\displaystyle\alpha=\gamma^{\pi}+\alpha^{\prime},\qquad\beta=\gamma^{\nu}+\alpha^{\prime},\qquad\gamma=\alpha-\beta,

where
\alpha^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{N}
and
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\alpha_{i}^{{}^{\prime}}=n-m
. Recall
\gamma_{i}^{\pi}=\max(\gamma_{i},0)
and
\gamma_{i}^{\nu}=-\min(0,\gamma_{i})=\max(0,-\gamma_{i})
; thus
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\gamma_{i}^{\pi}=m

and
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\alpha_{i}=n
. Therefore the coefficient of
x^{\gamma}
is

\displaystyle\#\left\{\alpha^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{N}\colon\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\alpha_{i}^{{}^{\prime}}=n-m\right\}=\frac{(N)_{n-m}}{(n-m)!}.

Hence

\displaystyle h_{n}\left(\frac{1}{x_{1}},\frac{1}{x_{2}},\ldots,\frac{1}{x_{N}}\right)h_{n}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N})=\sum_{m=0}^{N}\frac{(N)_{n-m}}{(n-m)!}S_{m}(x).

To finish the proof multiply this relation by
\frac{n!}{(N)_{n}}
and compute

\displaystyle\frac{n!}{(N)_{n}}\frac{(N)_{n-m}}{(n-m)!}=(-1)^{m}(-n)_{m}\frac{(N)_{n-m}}{(N)_{n-m}(N+n-m)_{m}}

\displaystyle\hphantom{\frac{n!}{(N) _{n}}\frac{(N) _{n-m}}{(n-m) !}}{}=(-1)^{m}\frac{(-n)_{m}}{(N+n-m)_{m}}=\frac{(-n)_{m}}{(1-N-n)_{m}}.

∎

Corollary 4.12
.

\sigma_{n}^{N-1}(x)\geq 0
for all
x\in\mathbb{T}^{N}
.

Proof.

\overline{h_{n}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N})}=h_{n}\left(\frac{1}{x_{1}},\frac{1}{x_{2}},\ldots,\frac{1}{x_{N}}\right)
for
x\in\mathbb{T}^{N}
.
∎

Observe that this kernel applies to the quotient space
\mathbb{T}^{N}/\mathbb{D}
where

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}:=\{(u,u,\ldots,u)\colon u\in\mathbb{C},\,|u|=1\},

is the diagonal subgroup. That is, each
S_{n}(x)
is
homogeneous of degree zero, constant on sets
\{(ux_{1},ux_{2},\ldots,ux_{N})\colon|u|=1\}
for fixed

x\in\mathbb{T}^{N}
.

Here are approximate identity properties of
\sigma_{n}^{N-1}
; we use

\mathbb{T}^{N}/\mathbb{D}
to refer to functions homogeneous of degree zero.
There is a standard formula:

Lemma 4.13
.

Suppose
g,h\in C\big(\mathbb{T}^{N}\big)
and
\nu
is
a Baire measure on
\mathbb{T}^{N}
then for
h^{{\dagger}}(x):=h\big(x^{-1}\big)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}g(x)(h\ast\nu)(x)\mathrm{d}m(x)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}\big(g\ast h^{{\dagger}}\big)(y)\mathrm{d}\nu(y).

Proof.

The left side equals

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}g(x)h\big(xy^{-1}\big)\mathrm{d}\nu(y)\mathrm{d}m(x)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}g(x)h^{{\dagger}}\big(yx^{-1}\big)\mathrm{d}m(x)\mathrm{d}\nu(y)

(by Fubini’s theorem) which equals the right side.
∎

The following is a standard result on approximate identities.

Proposition 4.14
.

Suppose
f\in C\big(\mathbb{T}^{N}/\mathbb{D}\big)
then

\big\|f-f\ast\sigma_{n}^{N-1}\big\|_{\infty}\rightarrow 0
as

n\rightarrow\infty
.

Proof.

For
\varepsilon>0
there exists a Laurent polynomial
p
on
\mathbb{T}^{N}/\mathbb{D}
such that
\|f-p\|_{\infty}<\varepsilon
.
Then

\displaystyle f-f\ast\sigma_{n}^{N-1}=(f-p)+\big(p-\sigma_{n}^{N-1}\ast p\big)+(p-f)\ast\sigma_{n}^{N-1},

and
\big\|(p-f)\ast\sigma_{n}^{N-1}\big\|_{\infty}\leq\|p-f\|_{\infty}\big\|\sigma_{n}^{N-1}\big\|_{1}<\varepsilon
. Let
p(x)=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{M}\sum\limits_{\alpha\in\boldsymbol{Z}_{N,m}}c_{\alpha}x^{\alpha}
for some
coefficients
c_{\alpha}
(and finite
M
); thus

\displaystyle\big(p-\sigma_{n}^{N-1}\ast p\big)(x)=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{M}\sum\limits_{\alpha\in\boldsymbol{Z}_{N,m}}\left(1-\frac{(-n)_{m}}{(1-N-n)_{m}}\right)c_{\alpha}x^{\alpha},

which tends to zero in norm as
n\rightarrow\infty
.
∎

Corollary 4.15
.

Suppose
\nu
is a Baire measure on
\mathbb{T}^{N}
and
f\in C\big(\mathbb{T}^{N}/\mathbb{D}\big)
then

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}f(x)\big(\sigma_{n}^{N-1}\ast\nu\big)(x)\mathrm{d}m(x)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}f(x)\mathrm{d}\nu(x).

Proof.

By Lemma
4.13

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}f(x)\big(\sigma_{n}^{N-1}\ast\nu\big)(x)\mathrm{d}m(x)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}\big(f\ast\sigma_{n}^{N-1}\big)(x)\mathrm{d}\nu(x),

since
\big(\sigma_{n}^{N-1}\big)^{{\dagger}}=\sigma_{n}^{N-1}
, and

f\ast\sigma_{n}^{N-1}
converges uniformly to

as
n\rightarrow\infty
.
∎

Definition 4.16
.

Define the
\mu

\displaystyle K_{n}(x):=\sigma_{n}^{N-1}\ast\mu(x)=\sum_{m=0}^{n}\frac{(-n)_{m}}{(1-N-n)_{m}}\sum_{\alpha\in\boldsymbol{Z}_{N,n}}A_{\alpha}x^{\alpha}.

Theorem 4.17
.

For
-1/h_{\tau}<\kappa<1/h_{\tau}
and

6
The differential equation

We will show that
\mu
satisfies a differential system in a distributional
sense. Let
\mathbb{T}_{\rm reg}^{N}:=\mathbb{T}^{N}\backslash\bigcup\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq N}\{x\colon x_{i}=x_{j}\}
(this avoids the
singularities of the system) and
\partial_{j}:=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}
for
1\leq j\leq N
. The system is

\displaystyle x_{i}\partial_{i}K(x)=\kappa\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{x_{j}}{x_{i}-x_{j}}\tau(i,j)K(x)+\kappa K(x)\sum_{j\neq i}\tau(i,j)\frac{x_{i}}{x_{i}-x_{j}},\qquad 1\leq i\leq N.

(6.1)

Any solution of this system satisfies
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}x_{i}\partial_{i}K\left(x\right)=0
and thus is homogeneous of degree zero.

The relation
\langle x_{i}\mathcal{D}_{i}f,g\rangle_{\mathbb{T}}=\langle f,x_{i}\mathcal{D}_{i}g\rangle_{\mathbb{T}}
extends to

C^{(1)}\big(\mathbb{T}^{N};V_{\tau}\big)
, the
continuously differentiable
V_{\tau}
-valued functions, because Laurent
polynomials are dense in this space. We have shown

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}\big\{(x_{i}\mathcal{D}_{i}f(x))^{\ast}K_{n}(x)g(x)-f(x)^{\ast}K_{n}(x)x_{i}\mathcal{D}_{i}g(x)\big\}\mathrm{d}m(x)=0.

Suppose
p,q\in C^{(1)}\big(\mathbb{T}^{N}\big)
(scalar

\mathbb{C}
-valued) then by periodicity
\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{j}}(pq)\mathrm{d}m=0
thus

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{j}}p\right)q\mathrm{d}m=-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}p\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{j}}q\right)\mathrm{d}m.

Also from
\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{j}}f(x)=\mathrm{i}e^{\mathrm{i}\theta_{j}}\partial_{j}f(x)=\mathrm{i}x_{j}\partial_{j}f(x)
and
\partial_{j}f^{\ast}(x)=-x_{j}^{-2}(\partial_{j}f)^{\ast}(x)
we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}(x_{j}\partial_{j}f(x))^{\ast}g(x)\mathrm{d}m=-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}x_{j}^{-1}\big({-}x_{j}^{2}\big)\partial_{j}f^{\ast}(x)g(x)\mathrm{d}m=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}f^{\ast}(x)x_{j}\partial_{j}g(x)\mathrm{d}m.

That is,
x_{j}\partial_{j}
is self-adjoint with respect to
\langle f,g\rangle:=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}f^{\ast}(x)g(x)\mathrm{d}m
. The result extends to
C^{(1)}\big(\mathbb{T}^{N};V_{\tau}\big)
.

Specialize to a closed
\mathcal{S}_{N}
-invariant subset
E\subset\mathbb{T}_{\rm reg}^{N}
which is the closure of its interior (for example

E_{\varepsilon}=\big\{x\in\mathbb{T}^{N}\colon\min_{i<j}|x_{i}-x_{j}|\geq\varepsilon\big\}
for
\varepsilon>0
), and let

f,g\in C^{(1)}\big(\mathbb{T}^{N};V_{\tau}\big)
have
supports contained in
E
, that is,
f(x)=0=g(x)
for
x\notin E
. For fixed
f
,
g
,
i
let

\displaystyle I_{n}=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}\big\{(x_{i}\mathcal{D}_{i}f(x))^{\ast}K_{n}(x)g(x)-f(x)^{\ast}K_{n}(x)x_{i}\mathcal{D}_{i}g(x)\big\}\mathrm{d}m(x)

\displaystyle\hphantom{I_{n} }{}=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}\big\{(x_{i}\partial_{i}f(x))^{\ast}K_{n}(x)g(x)-f(x)^{\ast}K_{n}(x)x_{i}\mathcal{\partial}_{i}g(x)\big\}

\displaystyle\hphantom{I_{n}=}{}+\kappa\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}\sum_{j\neq i}\left(\tau(i,j)x_{i}\frac{f(x)-f(x(i,j))}{x_{i}-x_{j}}\right)^{\ast}K_{n}(x)g(x)\mathrm{d}m(x)

\displaystyle\hphantom{I_{n}=}{}-\kappa\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}f(x)^{\ast}K_{n}(x)\sum_{j\neq i}\left(\tau(i,j)x_{i}\frac{g(x)-g(x(i,j))}{x_{i}-x_{j}}\right)\mathrm{d}m(x).

By using
\big(\frac{x_{i}}{x_{i}-x_{j}})^{\ast}=-\frac{x_{j}}{x_{i}-x_{j}}
,
\tau(i,j)^{\ast}=\tau(i,j)
,
and rearranging the sums we obtain

\displaystyle I_{n}=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}\big\{(x_{i}\partial_{i}f(x))^{\ast}K_{n}(x)g(x)-f(x)^{\ast}K_{n}(x)x_{i}\mathcal{\partial}_{i}g(x)\big\}\mathrm{d}m(x)

\displaystyle\hphantom{I_{n} =}{}-\kappa\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}f(x)^{\ast}\sum_{j\neq i}\left\{\frac{x_{j}}{x_{i}-x_{j}}\tau(i,j)K_{n}(x)+K_{n}(x)\tau(i,j)\frac{x_{i}}{x_{i}-x_{j}}\right\}g(x)\mathrm{d}m(x)

\displaystyle\hphantom{I_{n} =}{}+\kappa\sum_{j\neq i}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}\big\{x_{j}f(x(i,j))^{\ast}\tau(i,j)K_{n}(x)g(x)+x_{i}f(x)^{\ast}K_{n}(x)\tau(i,j)g(x(i,j))\big\}\frac{\mathrm{d}m(x)}{x_{i}-x_{j}}.

Each integral in the third line is finite because
f
and

vanish on a
neighborhood of
\bigcup\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq N}\{x\colon

x_{i}=x_{j}\}
. The

are invariant under the change of

\tau(i,j)K_{n}(x(i,j))=K_{n}(x)\tau(i,j)

changes

and the integral vanishes.

Because terms like
\tau(i,j)\frac{x_{i}}{x_{i}-x_{j}}g(x)
are in
C^{(1)}\big(\mathbb{T}^{N};V_{\tau}\big)
(assumption on the support of

) we can take the limit as

in the second line. By the adjoint property of

we find

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}(x_{i}\partial_{i}f(x))^{\ast}K_{n}(x)g(x)\mathrm{d}m(x)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}f(x)^{\ast}x_{i}\mathcal{\partial}_{i}\{K_{n}(x)g(x)\}\mathrm{d}m(x)

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}f(x)^{\ast}\big\{K_{n}(x)x_{i}\mathcal{\partial}_{i}g(x)+(x_{i}\partial_{i}K_{n}(x))g(x)\big\}\mathrm{d}m(x).

Thus the fact that
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}I_{n}=0
implies (recall the
matrix-valued integral notation (

))

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}f(x)^{\ast}(x_{i}\partial_{i}K_{n}(x))g(x)\mathrm{d}m(x)

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\kappa\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}f(x)^{\ast}\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{1}{x_{i}-x_{j}}\big\{x_{j}\tau(i,j)\mathrm{d}\mu(x)+\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\tau(i,j)x_{i}\big\}g(x).

(6.2)

This statement is valid for all
f,g\in C^{(1)}\big(\mathbb{T}^{N};V_{\tau}\big)
that vanish on a neighborhood of

\bigcup\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq N}\{x\colon

x_{i}=x_{j}\}
. The first
line of the equation can be written as

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}\big\{(x_{i}\partial_{i}f(x))^{\ast}K_{n}(x)g(x)-f(x)^{\ast}K_{n}(x)x_{i}\mathcal{\partial}_{i}g(x)\big\}\mathrm{d}m(x)

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}\big\{(x_{i}\partial_{i}f(x))^{\ast}\mathrm{d}\mu(x)g(x)-f(x)^{\ast}\mathrm{d}\mu(x)x_{i}\mathcal{\partial}_{i}g(x)\big\},

which expresses the distributional derivative of
\mathrm{d}\mu
. Thus the
distribution-sense differential system is satisfied by
\mathrm{d}\mu
on
closed subsets of
\mathbb{T}_{\rm reg}^{N}
.

In the scalar case (
\tau=(N)
) the orthogonality weight is
known to be (due to
[
1

for the symmetric Jack polynomials)

A.1
The matrix Bochner theorem

For a matrix
A\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
the operator norm is

\displaystyle\left\|A\right\|:=\sup\{|Av|\colon|v|=1\}=\sup\{|\langle u,Av\rangle|\colon|u|=1=|v|\}.

Proposition A.1
.

Suppose
F
is a positive-definite
M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
-valued
function on
\mathbb{Z}^{N}
then

(1)

F(\mathbf{0})
is positive-definite,

(2)

F(-\alpha)=F(\alpha)^{\ast}
and
\|F(\alpha)\|\leq\|F(\mathbf{0})\|
for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
.

Proof.

Part (1) follows immediately from taking
f(\mathbf{0})=u
,

f(\alpha)=0
for
\alpha\neq 0
. For part (2) fix
\alpha\neq 0

and let
f(\mathbf{0})=u
,
f(\alpha)=v
and

f(\beta)=0
otherwise. By definition

\displaystyle u^{\ast}F(\mathbf{0})u+v^{\ast}F(\mathbf{0})v+v^{\ast}F(\alpha)u+u^{\ast}F(-\alpha)v\geq 0.

Thus
\operatorname{Im}(v^{\ast}F(\alpha)u+u^{\ast}F(-\alpha)v)=0
for all
u
,
v
. For
1\leq j,k\leq n

let
u=\varepsilon_{j}
,
v=c\varepsilon_{k}
,
c\in\mathbb{C}
, then

\displaystyle 0=\operatorname{Im}\big(\overline{c}F(\alpha)_{kj}+cF(-\alpha)_{jk}\big).

Set
c=1
and
c=\mathrm{i}
to show
F(-\alpha)_{jk}=\overline{F(\alpha)_{kj}}
, that is,
F(-\alpha)=F(\alpha)^{\ast}
. Thus
\overline{u^{\ast}F(-\alpha)v}=v^{\ast}F(\alpha)u
and

\displaystyle-2\operatorname{Re}(v^{\ast}F(\alpha)u)\leq u^{\ast}F(\mathbf{0})u+v^{\ast}F(\mathbf{0})v.

Specialize to vectors
u
,
v
such that
|u|=1=|v|
and
v^{\ast}F(\alpha)u=\langle v,F(\alpha)u\rangle=-\|F(\alpha)\|
. Since
F(\mathbf{0})
is positive-definite it follows that

u^{\ast}F(\mathbf{0})u+v^{\ast}F(\mathbf{0})v\leq 2\|F(\mathbf{0})\|
, and so
\|F(\alpha)\|\leq\|F(\mathbf{0})\|
.
∎

Proposition A.2
.

Suppose
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
and
\alpha,\beta\neq\mathbf{0}
then

\displaystyle\|F(\alpha)-F(\beta)\|^{2}\leq 2\|F(\mathbf{0})\|\{v^{\ast}F(\mathbf{0})v-\operatorname{Re}(v^{\ast}F(\alpha-\beta)v)\},

where
|v|=1
and
|(F(\alpha)-F(\beta))^{\ast}v|=\|F(\alpha)-F(\beta)\|
.

Proof.

Assume
\alpha\neq\beta
and let
f(\mathbf{0})=u
,
f(\alpha)=w
,
f(\beta)=-w
, and
f(\gamma)=0
otherwise. By definition

\displaystyle u^{\ast}F(\mathbf{0})u+2w^{\ast}F(\mathbf{0})w+w^{\ast}(F(\alpha)-F(\beta))u+u^{\ast}(F(-\alpha)-F(-\beta))w

\displaystyle\qquad{}-w^{\ast}F(\alpha-\beta)w-w^{\ast}F(\beta-\alpha)w\geq 0.

Let
u,v\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
satisfy
|u|=1=|v|
and
|(F(\alpha)-F(\beta))^{\ast}v|=v^{\ast}(F(\alpha)-F(\beta))u=\|F(\alpha)-F(\beta)\|
. Set
w=tv
with
t\in\mathbb{R}
. The inequality becomes

\displaystyle u^{\ast}F(\mathbf{0})u+2t^{2}v^{\ast}F(\mathbf{0}t)v+2t\|F(\alpha)-F(\beta)\|-2t^{2}\operatorname{Re}\big(v^{\ast}F(\alpha-\beta)v\big)\geq 0.

The discriminant of the quadratic polynomial in
t
must be nonpositive and
this implies the stated inequality, since
u^{\ast}F(\mathbf{0})u\leq\|F(\mathbf{0})\|
.
∎

This is a sort of uniform continuity.

For any fixed
u\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
the scalar function
g_{u}(\alpha)=u^{\ast}F(\alpha)u
is positive-definite in the
classical sense and thus by Bochner’s theorem (see
[
13

) there exists a unique positive Baire measure
\mu_{u}
on
\mathbb{T}^{N}
such that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}x^{-\alpha}\mathrm{d}\mu_{u}(x)=u^{\ast}F(\alpha)u,\qquad\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}.

In particular, for
1\leq j\leq n
let
u=\varepsilon_{j}
then
u^{\ast}F(\alpha)u=F(\alpha)_{jj}
and set
\mu_{jj}=\mu_{\varepsilon_{j}}
. For
1\leq j,k\leq n
(with
j\neq k
) let

u=\varepsilon_{j}+\varepsilon_{k}
,
v=\varepsilon_{j}+\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{k}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}x^{-\alpha}\mathrm{d}\mu_{u}(x)=F(\alpha)_{jj}+F(\alpha)_{jk}+F\left(\alpha\right)_{kj}+F(\alpha)_{kk},

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}x^{-\alpha}\mathrm{d}\mu_{v}(x)=F(\alpha)_{jj}+\mathrm{i}F(\alpha)_{jk}-\mathrm{i}F(\alpha)_{kj}+F(\alpha)_{kk}.

Thus

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}x^{-\alpha}\mathrm{d}(\mu_{u}-\mathrm{i}\mu_{v})=2F(\alpha)_{jk}+(1-\mathrm{i})\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}x^{-\alpha}\mathrm{d}(\mu_{jj}+\mu_{kk}),

and define

\displaystyle\mu_{jk}=\frac{1}{2}(\mu_{u}-\mathrm{i}\mu_{v})-\frac{1-\mathrm{i}}{2}(\mu_{jj}+\mu_{kk}),

with the result

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}x^{-\alpha}\mathrm{d}\mu_{jk}(x)=F(\alpha)_{jk},\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}.

From the above equations we also obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}x^{-\alpha}\mathrm{d}(\mu_{u}+\mathrm{i}\mu_{v})=2F(\alpha)_{kj}+(1+\mathrm{i})\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}x^{-\alpha}\mathrm{d}(\mu_{jj}+\mu_{kk}).

The formula

\displaystyle\mu_{kj}=\frac{1}{2}(\mu_{u}+\mathrm{i}\mu_{v})-\frac{1+\mathrm{i}}{2}(\mu_{jj}+\mu_{kk}),

\widehat{\mu_{u}}(-\alpha)=\overline{\widehat{\mu_{u}}(\alpha)}

a restatement of

From

\displaystyle\|\mu_{jk}\|\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(F(\mathbf{0})_{jj}+F(\mathbf{0})_{kk})+F(\mathbf{0})_{jj}+F(\mathbf{0})_{kk}+|\operatorname{Re}F(\mathbf{0})_{jk}|+|\operatorname{Im}F(\mathbf{0})_{jk}|.

In particular, if

. For the space

\mathbb{T}^{N}

\displaystyle\langle f,g\rangle_{F}:=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}\overline{f(x)_{i}}g(x)_{j}\mathrm{d}\mu_{ij}(x),

then for

A.2
Some results for the index set

For

\displaystyle\#\boldsymbol{Z}_{N,n}=\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\binom{N}{j}\binom{n-1}{j-1}\binom{N-j+n-1}{n};

(A.1)

in each

-subset (there are
\binom{N}{j}
) of
[1,2,\ldots,N]
take

\alpha_{i_{1}},\ldots,\alpha_{i_{j}}
with
\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{j}\alpha_{i_{\ell}}=n
and each

(
\binom{n-1}{j-1}

(N-j)

\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{N-j}\alpha_{i_{\ell}}=-n
and each

\alpha_{i_{\ell}}\leq 0
(
\binom{N-j+n-1}{n}

when

\displaystyle\#\boldsymbol{Z}_{2,n}=2,\quad\#\boldsymbol{Z}_{3,n}=6n,\quad\#\boldsymbol{Z}_{4,n}=10n^{2}+2,\quad\#\boldsymbol{Z}_{5,n}=\frac{5n}{3}\big(7n^{2}+5\big),\quad\ldots.

Proposition A.5
.

Among

\beta

and such

\beta_{j}>0

the minimal multi-index for the order

and

(

Proof.

The claim is that
\big(\gamma_{i}^{(0)}\big)_{i=1}^{k}

is
\prec
-minimal among partitions
\alpha
of length
\leq k
and

. Argue by induction on the length. The

. Suppose it is true for

and let

be a partition of length
\leq k+1
. Let

\displaystyle n_{1}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i},\qquad n_{2}=n_{1}+\alpha_{k+1},\qquad p_{1}=\left\lfloor\frac{n_{1}}{k}\right\rfloor,\qquad p_{2}=\left\lfloor\frac{n_{2}}{k+1}\right\rfloor,

\displaystyle m_{1}=n_{1}-kp_{1},\qquad m_{2}=n_{2}-(k+1)p_{2}.

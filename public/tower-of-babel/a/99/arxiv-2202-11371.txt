Construction of G 2 G^{2} planar Hermite interpolants with prescribed arc lengths
Marjeta Knez, Francesca Pelosi, Maria Lucia Sampoli

Abstract
In this paper we address the problem of constructing G 2 G^{2} planar Pythagorean–hodograph (PH) spline curves, that interpolate points, tangent directions and curvatures, and have prescribed arc-length. The interpolation scheme is completely local. Each spline segment is defined as a PH biarc curve of degree 7 7 , which results in having a closed form solution of the G 2 G^{2} interpolation equations depending on four free parameters. By fixing two of them to zero, it is proven that the length constraint can be satisfied for any data and any chosen ratio between the two boundary tangents. Length interpolation equation reduces to one algebraic equation with four solutions in general. To select the best one, the value of the bending energy is observed. Several numerical examples are provided to illustrate the obtained theoretical results and to numerically confirm that the approximation order is 5 5 .



1
Introduction

One of the fundamental problems of computer-aided design is to pass a curve through a given sequence of points. For example, in the design of cars and ships it is a standard practice to construct surfaces by first constructing networks of curves.
In addition to satisfy functional or aesthetic criteria, designed objects often have to exactly match not only a series of points but also derivatives. The problem can be efficiently addressed by
C^{1}
or
C^{2}
Hermite interpolation.
In industrial design, fair parametric curves and surfaces are the most preferred representation to meet the requirements of design and modeling.
To this aim, it is sometimes preferable to consider a geometric continuity, that is simply the continuity of unit tangents
(
G^{1}
continuity), or even of the curvature function (
G^{2}
continuity), see for instance
[
1
, Chapter 8]
.
This kind of continuity is advantageous for many applications.
For instance, in NC milling applications, using spline curves with
G^{2}
continuity produces motions with continuous accelerations, hence continuous cutting forces. This increases the possible speed of the manufacturing process, and/or the lifetime of the machinery.

On the other hand the possibility to construct curved paths satisfying given boundary conditions and with prescribed arc lengths is a fundamental problem in geometric design. Indeed such problems may arise in robot path planning, computer animation, path planning for unmanned or autonomous vehicles, and related applications. In this context, it is well known that polynomial Pythagorean–hodograph curves possess polynomial cumulative arc length functions, so they are perfectly suited for the construction of exact solutions to such problems. For instance the efficient use of PH curves in real-time control of CNC machines can be found in
[
2
]
, where PH curves are used for contour error computation of general free-form curved paths, while applications to robot motion path planning are presented in
[
3
,
4
]
.

For planar PH curves, one of the first interpolation methods was given in
[
5
]
, where the interpolation of first order Hermite data was analysed. Later the problem was revisited in
[
6
]
. In
[
7
]
a characterization of the set of
G^{1}
Hermite data for which planar PH cubic interpolants exist was given. The method of
[
5
]
was extended in
[
8
]
to accommodate interpolation of
C^{1}
data together with end–point curvatures, using planar PH curves of degree
7
, and it was shown that up to eight distinct interpolants exist. For quintic planar PH curves, several results on first and second order continuous spline interpolation can be found in the literature, see for instance
[
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
]
.
In
[
14
]
a local
G^{2}
interpolation with PH quintics that interpolate end points together with unit tangents and curvature values is considered. Although multiple solutions exist, a method to obtain a “good” interpolant is suggested through an asymptotic analysis. However, the problem is highly non-linear and any general existence results seems to be unachievable.

The construction of curved paths with prescribed arc lengths satisfying given boundary conditions has received relatively little attention in the past, but it has recently been exposed as an important problem. The imposition of arc length constraints in the construction of PH curves was first considered in
[
15
]
, where a numerical scheme was employed to solve the system of non–linear equations that define a spatial
C^{2}
PH quintic spline interpolating a sequence of nodal points with specified internodal arc lengths.
A closed–form solution to the problem of interpolating planar
G^{1}
Hermite data under arc length constraints was developed in
[
16
]
, using planar PH quintics, and in
[
17
]
this approach was generalized to the spatial case. In
[
18
]
rational PH curves are used to provide a closed-form solution to the problem of interpolating spatial
G^{1}
data with prescribed arc lengths.

In this work we extend the interpolation problem analysed in
[
8
]
, by adding the length
interpolation condition, while relaxing the
C^{1}
to
G^{1}
continuity conditions. In more detail, we consider the interpolation of two planar data points, two tangent directions and two curvatures by degree
7
planar PH curves, that in addition have prescribed length. The problem reduces to three non-linear equations with one free shape parameter. However, the analysis of existence of solutions is still too involved, and there exist data for which no interpolants can be found. To make the problem theoretically as well as numerically simpler, we propose to replace a single PH curve with a PH biarc curve. Moreover, in order to reduce the number of free parameters coming from the splitting, we assume that the biarc is
C^{3}
continuous at the joint point. In this way we derive a closed form expression for the
G^{2}
interpolant, which still depends on four free parameters. Then we fix two of them to zero by requiring that the first and the second order derivatives at boundary points are orthogonal, and set the other two proportional to the lengths of boundary tangents. Eventually we are able to prove that the length interpolation condition, that reduces to one simple algebraic equation, has a solution for any data and any value of the remaining shape parameter. Beside of being easy to implement and simple to use in practise, the proposed method can be directly applied to a (local) construction of
G^{2}
continuous interpolating splines. To measure the fairness of the resulting interpolants, we observe the value of the curve bending energy. To visualize the results, we use the porcupine plot of the curvature (see for instance
[
19
]
). The porcupine curvature plot consists of lines emanating from points on the curve in the direction of
{\rm sign}\;\!{(\kappa)}\,{\bf n}
(where
{\bf n}
is a unit normal) with lengths proportional to the magnitude of the curvature
\kappa
. In this way it is possible also to see immediately the curvature continuity and its sign changes.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. In the next section we introduce the notation and give some basic definitions. Then in Section
3
we present the problem and propose a possible approach to solve it. We show in particular that a solution could not exist for some given data with a single PH curve of degree
7
.
Section
4
presents a biarc construction and its theoretical analysis.
In Section
5
some numerical examples are given to illustrate the performance of the proposed method. Finally, Section
6
briefly summarizes the contributions of the present study, and identifies possible directions for further investigation.

2
Preliminaries

The distinctive property of a polynomial planar PH curve
{{\bf r}}(t)=\left(r_{1}(t),r_{2}(t)\right)
is that its hodograph

{{\bf r}}^{\prime}(t)=(r_{1}^{\prime}(t),r_{2}^{\prime}(t))
satisfies the Pythagorean condition

r_{1}^{\prime\,2}(t)\,+\,r_{2}^{\prime\,2}(t)\,=\,\sigma^{2}(t)

(1)

for some polynomial
\sigma(t)
, which specifies the parametric speed of
{\bf r}(t)
, i.e. the derivative of the arc length
s
with respect to the curve parameter
t
. This feature provides planar PH curves with many attractive computational properties: they have rational unit tangents and normals, curvatures, and offset curves. Moreover their arc lengths are exactly computable so that they are ideally suited to real-time precision motion control applications,
[
20
]
. In the complex representation,
[
21
]
, a planar PH curve segment is constructed from a complex polynomial
{{\bf w}}(t)=u(t)+{\bf i}\,v(t)
by integrating the expression

{\bf r}^{\prime}(t)={{\bf w}}^{2}(t).

(2)

We use bold characters to interchangeably denote planar points/vectors and complex
numbers, while real (scalar) quantities are denoted by italic characters. Note also that we identify complex numbers with points/vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Thus, the curve
{\bf r}(t)
is further expressed as
{\bf r}(t)=r_{1}(t)+{\bf i}\,r_{2}(t)
, and similarly, all the interpolation points/vectors will be given as complex numbers.

If
{\bf w}(t)
is of degree
m
, it may be defined by its complex Bernstein coefficients
{\bf w}_{0},\ldots,{\bf w}_{m}
,

{\bf w}(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{m}{\bf w}_{i}B_{i}^{m}(t),\;\;B_{i}^{m}(t)={{m}\choose{i}}(1-t)^{m-i}\ t^{i}.

(3)

The above expression is also called
complex preimage curve
of
{\bf r}
.
Integrating (
2
) yields a planar PH curve of degree
n=2m+1
. Denoting the hodograph (
2
) with
{\bf h}(t)
, it can be written in Bézier form as

{\bf h}(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{2m}{\bf h}_{i}B_{i}^{2m}(t),

(4)

where the coefficients are related to the complex polynomial
{\bf w}(t)
as

{\bf h}_{i}=\sum_{j=\max(0,i-m)}^{\min(m,i)}\frac{{{m}\choose{j}}{{m}\choose{i-j}}}{{{2m}\choose{i}}}{\bf w}_{j}\ {\bf w}_{i-j},\;\;i=0,\ldots,2m.

(5)

The planar PH curve of degree
n
can be also expressed in the Bézier representation

{\bf r}(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}{\bf p}_{i}B_{i}^{n}(t),

(6)

and taking into account that
{\bf h}(t)={\bf r}^{\prime}(t)
we have

{\bf h}(t)=n\ \sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\Delta{\bf p}_{i}B_{i}^{n-1}(t),\quad\Delta{\bf p}_{i}:={\bf p}_{i+1}-{\bf p}_{i},

(7)

and

{\bf p}_{i}={\bf p}_{i-1}+\frac{1}{n}{\bf h}_{i-1},\;i=1,\ldots,n,

(8)

where
{\bf p}_{0}
is a free integration constant. From (
2
) the parametric speed
\sigma(t)
, unit tangent
{\bf t}(t)
and curvature
\kappa(t)
of the curve
{\bf r}(t)
may be formulated in terms of
{\bf w}(t)
as, see
[
21
]
,

\sigma(t)=|{\bf w}(t)|^{2},\;\quad{\bf t}(t)=\frac{{\bf w}(t)^{2}}{\sigma(t)},\;\quad\kappa(t)=2\ \frac{{\rm Im}\;\!(\overline{{\bf w}}(t)\ {\bf w}^{\prime}(t))}{\sigma^{2}(t)},

(9)

where
|\cdot|
denotes the absolute value of a complex number, and
\overline{{\bf w}}
is a conjugate of
{\bf w}
.
Since
\sigma(t)
is a polynomial of degree
2m
, the cumulative arc length function

s(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(\lambda)\ d\lambda

is likewise just a polynomial of degree
2m+1
.

Remark 1

A normal vector at each point on the curve
{\bf r}
is perpendicular to the unit tangent
{\bf t}
. It can be computed by a
90^{\circ}
clockwise or counterclockwise rotation of
{\bf t}
. In what follows we choose the
later one, i.e.
{\bf n}={{\bf t}}^{\perp}
, where

(x,y)^{\perp}:=(-y,x)
.

The quintics, obtained by integrating
{\bf r}^{\prime}(t)={\bf w}^{2}(t)
with a complex quadratic
{\bf w}(t)
, are the lowest–order PH curves with sufficient shape flexibility for free–form design.
However, for our purposes they do not possess enough degrees of freedom to fulfil the
G^{2}
interpolation at the boundary, and the arc-length interpolation. Hence in this paper we shall focus on PH curves of degree 7.

In this case, by choosing a cubic polynomial
{\bf w}(t)
with Bernstein coefficients
{\bf w}_{0},\ {\bf w}_{1},\ {\bf w}_{2},\ {\bf w}_{3}
, the expression (
5
) reduces to

\begin{split}{\bf h}_{0}&={\bf w}_{0}^{2},\quad{\bf h}_{1}={\bf w}_{0}{\bf w}_{1},\quad{\bf h}_{2}=\frac{1}{5}(2{\bf w}_{0}{\bf w}_{2}+3{\bf w}_{1}^{2}),\quad{\bf h}_{3}=\frac{1}{10}({\bf w}_{0}{\bf w}_{3}+9{\bf w}_{1}{\bf w}_{2}),\\
{\bf h}_{4}&=\frac{1}{5}(2{\bf w}_{1}{\bf w}_{3}+3{\bf w}_{2}^{2}),\quad{\bf h}_{5}={\bf w}_{2}{\bf w}_{3},\quad{\bf h}_{6}={\bf w}_{3}^{2}.\end{split}

(10)

The parametric speed is a polynomial of degree
6
, which can be expressed in the Bézier form as

\sigma(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{6}\sigma_{i}B_{i}^{6}(t)

(11)

with coefficients

\begin{split}\sigma_{0}&=\left|{\bf w}_{0}\right|^{2},\quad\sigma_{1}={\rm Re}\;\!\left({\bf w}_{0}\ \overline{{\bf w}}_{1}\right),\quad\sigma_{2}=\frac{1}{5}\left(2{\rm Re}\;\!({\bf w}_{0}\ \overline{{\bf w}}_{2})+3\left|{\bf w}_{1}\right|^{2}\right),\\
\sigma_{3}&=\frac{1}{10}{\rm Re}\;\!\left({\bf w}_{0}\ \overline{{\bf w}}_{3}+9{\bf w}_{1}\ \overline{{\bf w}}_{2}\right),\\
\sigma_{4}&=\frac{1}{5}\left(2{\rm Re}\;\!({\bf w}_{1}\ \overline{{\bf w}}_{3})+3\left|{\bf w}_{2}\right|^{2}\right),\quad\sigma_{5}={\rm Re}\;\!\left({\bf w}_{2}\ \overline{{\bf w}}_{3}\right),\quad\sigma_{6}=\left|{\bf w}_{3}\right|^{2}.\end{split}

(12)

The total arc length is

L_{{\bf r}}=s(1)=\frac{\sigma_{0}+\sigma_{1}+\ldots+\sigma_{6}}{7}.

(13)

3
The interpolation problem

Given two data points
{\bf P}_{0}
and
{\bf P}_{1}
, two associated tangent directions
{\bf t}_{0}
and
{\bf t}_{1}
(with
\left|{\bf t}_{0}\right|=\left|{\bf t}_{1}\right|=1
) and two signed curvatures

\kappa_{0}
,
\kappa_{1}
, the problem we want to address is to find a PH curve
{\bf r}:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
of degree 7 that interpolates the data

{\bf r}(i)={\bf P}_{i},\quad\frac{{\bf r}^{\prime}(i)}{|{\bf r}^{\prime}(i)|}={\bf t}_{i}\,\quad\kappa(i)=\kappa_{i},\quad i=0,1,

(14)

and has the length equal to a given
L
, such that
L>|{\Delta{\bf P}_{0}}|
.
It is easy to verify that these
G^{2}
interpolation conditions are satisfied if the control points are equal to (see
[
22
]
)

\begin{split}{\bf p}_{0}&={\bf P}_{0},\quad{\bf p}_{1}={\bf P}_{0}+\frac{\alpha_{0}^{2}}{7}{\bf t}_{0},\quad{\bf p}_{2}={\bf P}_{0}+\left(\frac{2\alpha_{0}^{2}}{7}+\frac{\beta_{0}}{42}\right){\bf t}_{0}+\kappa_{0}\frac{\alpha_{0}^{4}}{42}{\bf n}_{0},\\
{\bf p}_{7}&={\bf P}_{1},\quad{\bf p}_{6}={\bf P}_{1}-\frac{\alpha_{1}^{2}}{7}{\bf t}_{1},\quad{\bf p}_{5}={\bf P}_{1}+\left(-\frac{2\alpha_{1}^{2}}{7}+\frac{\beta_{1}}{42}\right){\bf t}_{1}+\kappa_{1}\frac{\alpha_{1}^{4}}{42}{\bf n}_{1},\end{split}

(15)

for some
\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1}
and
\beta_{0},\beta_{1}
, where
{\bf n}_{0}={\bf i}\,{\bf t}_{0}
,
{\bf n}_{1}={\bf i}\,{\bf t}_{1}
are the unit normals at the end-points (see Remark
1
).
Of course, these parameters need to be computed so that
{\bf r}
satisfies the PH condition.

From the expressions for
{\bf p}_{1}
and
{\bf p}_{6}
of (
15
) and relations
(
8
) and (
10
) we get the following complex quadratic equations

{\bf w}_{0}^{2}=\alpha_{0}^{2}\ {\bf t}_{0},\;\quad{\bf w}_{3}^{2}=\alpha_{1}^{2}\ {\bf t}_{1}.

(16)

The next lemma follows by the elementary computations.

Lemma 1

Let
{\bf c}:=c_{1}+{\bf i}\,c_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
, with
{\bf c}\neq 0
. Then the equation
{\bf z}^{2}={\bf c}
has two solutions

{\bf z}=\zeta\,{\boldsymbol{\chi}}({\bf c}),\quad{\boldsymbol{\chi}}({\bf c}):=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\left(\sqrt{\left|{\bf c}\right|+c_{1}}+{\bf i}\frac{c_{2}}{\sqrt{\left|{\bf c}\right|+c_{1}}}\right),\quad\zeta\in\{-1,1\}.

Using Lemma
1
we can solve the equations (
16
) in terms of the preimage coefficients:

{\bf w}_{0}=\alpha_{0}\ \zeta_{0}\ {\boldsymbol{\chi}}({\bf t}_{0})\quad\quad{\bf w}_{3}=\alpha_{1}\ \zeta_{1}\ {\boldsymbol{\chi}}({\bf t}_{1}),\quad\zeta_{0},\zeta_{1}\in\{-1,1\}.

(17)

Without loosing generality we set
\zeta_{0}=\zeta_{1}=1
and observe positive as well as negative values for
\alpha_{0}
and
\alpha_{1}
. Now, considering the expressions for
{\bf p}_{2}
and
{\bf p}_{5}
of (
15
), the relations in (
10
) for
{\bf h}_{1}
and
{\bf h}_{5}
, and (
8
) we obtain

{\bf w}_{1}=\frac{1}{{\bf w}_{0}}\left(\left(\alpha_{0}^{2}+\frac{\beta_{0}}{6}\right){\bf t}_{0}+\kappa_{0}\frac{\alpha_{0}^{4}}{6}{\bf n}_{0}\right),\quad{\bf w}_{2}=\frac{1}{{\bf w}_{3}}\left(\left(\alpha_{1}^{2}-\frac{\beta_{1}}{6}\right){\bf t}_{1}-\kappa_{1}\frac{\alpha_{1}^{4}}{6}{\bf n}_{1}\right).

(18)

From (
17
) and (
18
) we see that using the end tangents and end curvature conditions we have been able to express all the coefficients of the preimage function
{\bf w}(t)
in terms of the free parameters
\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},

\beta_{0}
and
\beta_{1}
.
To complete the interpolation conditions (
14
) we have to impose the end point interpolation:

7\ \Delta{\bf P}_{0}=\sum_{i=0}^{6}{\bf h}_{i},\quad\;\Delta{\bf P}_{0}={\bf P}_{1}-{\bf P}_{0}.

From (
10
), this results in the following quadratic equation

\displaystyle 10({\bf w}_{0}^{2}+{\bf w}_{3}^{2})+6({\bf w}_{1}^{2}+{\bf w}_{2}^{2})

\displaystyle+10({\bf w}_{0}\ {\bf w}_{1}+{\bf w}_{2}\ {\bf w}_{3})+{\bf w}_{0}\ {\bf w}_{3}+

(19)

\displaystyle 4({\bf w}_{0}\ {\bf w}_{2}+{\bf w}_{1}\ {\bf w}_{3})+9{\bf w}_{1}\ {\bf w}_{2}=70\Delta{\bf P}_{0}.

At last, from (
12
) and (
13
), requiring the interpolant to have a specified length
L
yields the condition

\displaystyle 10(|{\bf w}_{0}|^{2}+|{\bf w}_{3}|^{2})+6(|{\bf w}_{1}|^{2}+|{\bf w}_{2}|^{2})+

\displaystyle{\rm Re}\;\!\left(10({\bf w}_{0}\ \overline{{\bf w}}_{1}+{\bf w}_{2}\ \overline{{\bf w}}_{3})+{\bf w}_{0}\ \overline{{\bf w}}_{3}\right.

(20)

\displaystyle\left.4({\bf w}_{0}\ \overline{{\bf w}}_{2}+{\bf w}_{1}\ \overline{{\bf w}}_{3})+9{\bf w}_{1}\ \overline{{\bf w}}_{2}\right)=70L.

Equations (
19
) and (
20
) give three (highly) non-linear scalar equations for four unknowns

\alpha_{0}
,
\alpha_{1}
,
\beta_{0}
and
\beta_{1}
, thus the solutions depend on one extra parameter. This one degree of freedom can be fixed by prescribing the ratio between lengths of boundary tangents.

We note that if we omit the length constraint (
20
) and fix the parameters
\alpha_{0}
and
\alpha_{1}
by assigning the lengths of both boundary tangent vectors, we obtain a different interpolation problem, which has been studied by Jüttler in
[
8
]
, resulting with
C^{1}/G^{2}
PH interpolants. More precisely, it is shown in
[
8
]
that
for fixed
\alpha_{0}
and
\alpha_{1}
, there exist up to eight possible solutions of equations (
19
) that come as roots of two bivariate quartic polynomials. Thus, any general result on the existence of solutions seems to be impossible, so a comprehensive asymptotic analysis is provided instead and it is shown that for data taken from a smooth parametric curve, defined on some small interval, there exist solutions having the approximation order
6
.

Relaxing the
C^{1}
boundary conditions to
G^{1}
, as done in (
14
), and adding the equation (
20
) makes the interpolation problem even more non-linear and more difficult to analyse. In addition, it is easy to come up with examples for which no solutions exist or the solutions are not visually pleasing, as demonstrated in the next example. To numerically solve the nonlinear equations in this example we have used the program package
Mathematica
and its function

4
Biarc construction

In order to address the Hermite interpolation constraints as well as to match the prescribed length for any arbitrary set of data, we consider the construction of a PH biarc segment.

A PH biarc of degree 7 can be defined as a piecewise curve

{\bf r}(t)=\begin{cases}{\bf r}_{A}(t),&t\in\left[0,\frac{1}{2}\right)\\
{\bf r}_{B}(t),&t\in\left[\frac{1}{2},1\right]\end{cases},

(22a)

having each segment
expressed in the Bézier form as

{\bf r}_{A}(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{7}{\bf p}_{A,i}B_{i}^{7}(2t),\quad{\bf r}_{B}(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{7}{\bf p}_{B,i}B_{i}^{7}(2t-1).

(22b)

Considering their hodographs

{\bf h}_{A}(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{6}{\bf h}_{A,i}B_{i}^{6}(2t),\quad\;{\bf h}_{B}(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{6}{\bf h}_{B,i}B_{i}^{6}(2t-1),

(23)

it holds that
{\bf h}_{A}(t)={\bf w}_{A}^{2}(t)
and
{\bf h}_{B}(t)={\bf w}_{B}^{2}(t)
, where the preimages are expressed in terms of cubic complex polynomials

{\bf w}_{A}(t)=\displaystyle{\sum_{i=0}^{3}{\bf w}_{A,i}B_{i}^{3}(t)},\;\quad{\bf w}_{B}(t)=\displaystyle{\sum_{i=0}^{3}{\bf w}_{B,i}B_{i}^{3}(t)}.

(24)

Hence, from (
4
) and (
10
), the hodograph of the PH biarc curve of degree 7 is completely defined once the complex coefficients
{\bf w}_{A,i}
,
{\bf w}_{B,i}
,

,

23
), we obtain the control points of each PH segment
(
22b
). Choosing the free integration constants as
{\bf r}_{A,0}={\bf P}_{0}
and
{\bf r}_{B,7}={\bf P}_{1}

{\bf p}_{A,i+1}={\bf P}_{0}+\frac{1}{14}\sum_{j=0}^{i}{\bf h}_{A,j},\;\;i=0,1,\dots,6,\quad{\bf p}_{B,i}={\bf P}_{1}-\frac{1}{14}\sum_{j=i}^{6}{\bf h}_{B,j},\;\;i=6,5,\dots,0.

Thus, the interpolation conditions
{\bf r}(0)={\bf P}_{0}
and
{\bf r}(1)={\bf P}_{1}
are already
achieved.

17

{\bf w}_{A,0}=\alpha_{0}\,\zeta_{0}\,{\boldsymbol{\chi}}({\bf t}_{0}),\quad{\bf w}_{B,3}=\alpha_{1}\,\zeta_{1}\,{\boldsymbol{\chi}}({\bf t}_{1})

for four different sign choices
\left(\zeta_{0},\zeta_{1}\right)\in\left\{(1,1),(1,-1),(-1,1),(-1,-1)\right\}
.
Again, we can fix

\alpha_{0}
and
\alpha_{1}

Analogously to (

\displaystyle{\bf w}_{A,1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{{\bf w}_{A,0}}\left(\left(\alpha_{0}^{2}+\frac{\beta_{0}}{12}\right){\bf t}_{0}+\kappa_{0}\frac{\alpha_{0}^{4}}{12}{\bf n}_{0}\right),

\displaystyle{\bf w}_{B,2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{{\bf w}_{B,3}}\left(\left(\alpha_{1}^{2}-\frac{\beta_{1}}{12}\right){\bf t}_{1}-\kappa_{1}\frac{\alpha_{1}^{4}}{12}{\bf n}_{1}\right),

where we should note that, in comparison to (

What is left is to assure that

continuous at the joint parameter
t=\frac{1}{2}
. As in the case of macro-elements, the usual approach to reduce the number of free parameters, coming from splitting, to the number of free parameters needed to get a simple interpolation construction, is to require additional smoothness at the joint point. In our case, requiring

C^{3}
continuity of
{\bf w}_{A}
and
{\bf w}_{B}
at
t=\frac{1}{2}
would lead to a polynomial curve
{\bf r}
, i.e. the curve examined in the previous section. Thus, to obtain the additional freedom needed while keeping the interpolation scheme as simple as possible, we require that the preimage biarc is
C^{2}
continuous, which implies the biarc (
22
) to be
C^{3}
continuous provided
{\bf r}_{A}(\frac{1}{2})={\bf r}_{B}(\frac{1}{2})
holds true.

Recalling the geometrical construction in terms of control points for achieving
C^{2}
continuity of
{\bf w}_{A}
and
{\bf w}_{B}
at

t=\frac{1}{2}
, we introduce a new control point, expressed with a complex number
{\bf d}\in\mathbb{C}
, and define

{\bf w}_{A,2}=\frac{1}{2}({\bf w}_{A,1}+{\bf d}),\quad{\bf w}_{B,1}=\frac{1}{2}({\bf w}_{B,2}+{\bf d})

(25c)

and

{\bf w}_{A,3}={\bf w}_{B,0}=\frac{1}{2}({\bf w}_{A,2}+{\bf w}_{B,1})=\frac{1}{4}{\bf w}_{A,1}+\frac{1}{2}{\bf d}+\frac{1}{4}{\bf w}_{B,2}.

(25d)

It remains to impose the
C^{0}
continuity at the joint point,
{\bf r}_{A}(\frac{1}{2})={\bf r}_{B}(\frac{1}{2})
. Hence we must choose
{\bf d}
so that

\Delta{\bf P}_{0}=\frac{1}{14}\sum_{i=0}^{6}({\bf h}_{A,i}+{\bf h}_{B,i}).

(26)

Now considering the relations, given in (
10
), between the coefficients of the hodograph and those of the preimage for each subsegment, after some computation, we can rewrite the equation (
26
) in terms of
{\bf d}
,
{\bf w}_{A,0}
,
{\bf w}_{A,1}
,
{\bf w}_{B,2}
and
{\bf w}_{B,3}
as

{\bf d}^{2}+2\ {\bf U}\ {\bf d}+{\bf V}=0

(27)

with

\displaystyle 52\ {\bf U}

\displaystyle=5\ ({\bf w}_{A,0}+{\bf w}_{B,3})+39\ ({\bf w}_{A,1}+{\bf w}_{B,2}),

\displaystyle 52\ {\bf V}

\displaystyle=40\ ({\bf w}_{A,0}^{2}+{\bf w}_{B,3}^{2})+49\ ({\bf w}_{A,0}{\bf w}_{A,1}+{\bf w}_{B,2}{\bf w}_{B,3})+62\ ({\bf w}_{A,1}^{2}+{\bf w}_{B,2}^{2})

(28)

\displaystyle\hskip 99.58464pt+\ {\bf w}_{A,0}{\bf w}_{B,2}+{\bf w}_{A,1}{\bf w}_{B,3}+28{\bf w}_{A,1}{\bf w}_{B,2}-560\ \Delta{\bf P}_{0}.

The quadratic complex equation (
27
) can be rewritten as
\left({\bf d}+{\bf U}\right)^{2}={\bf U}^{2}-{\bf V}
, and so using Lemma
1
,
we obtain an explicit solution for
{\bf d}
,

{\bf d}=\zeta_{{\bf d}}\,{\boldsymbol{\chi}}\left({\bf U}^{2}-{\bf V}\right)-{\bf U},\quad\zeta_{{\bf d}}\in\{-1,1\},

(29)

in terms of
{\bf w}_{A,0}
,
{\bf w}_{A,1}
,
{\bf w}_{B,2}
and
{\bf w}_{B,3}
, which depend on the free parameters
\alpha_{0}
,
\alpha_{1}
,
\beta_{0}
and
\beta_{1}
(see (
25a
), (
25
)).
The results are summarized in the next theorem.

Theorem 1

A planar PH biarc (
22
) of degree
7
that interpolates
G^{2}
data (
14
) is given in a closed form, that follows from the preimage, given by
(
25
) and (
29
), and depends on four free parameters
\alpha_{0}
,
\alpha_{1}
,
\beta_{0}
,
\beta_{1}
and the sign choice
\zeta_{{\bf d}}\in\{-1,1\}
.

The last step is to require that the resulting biarc curve has the prescribed length
L
.
Taking into account (
13
), in the biarc case, this constraint leads to the equation

L=\frac{1}{14}\sum_{i=0}^{6}(\sigma_{A,i}+\sigma_{B,i}),

where
\sigma_{A,i}
and
\sigma_{B,i}
are the Bézier ordinates of
\sigma_{A}(t)={\bf w}_{A}(t)\overline{{\bf w}}_{A}(t)
and

\sigma_{B}(t)={\bf w}_{B}(t)\overline{{\bf w}}_{B}(t)
, respectively.
By using (
12
), (
25
) and (
29
), we obtain, with some computation, the equation

\left|{\bf d}\right|^{2}+2\,{\rm Re}\;\!\left({\bf d}\,\overline{{\bf U}}\right)+v=0,

where

\begin{split}52\ v=&40\left|{\bf w}_{A,0}\right|^{2}+40\left|{\bf w}_{B,3}\right|^{2}+62\left|{\bf w}_{A,1}\right|^{2}+62\left|{\bf w}_{B,2}\right|^{2}\\
&+{\rm Re}\;\!\left(49{\bf w}_{A,0}\overline{{\bf w}}_{A,1}+49{\bf w}_{B,2}\overline{{\bf w}}_{B,3}+28{\bf w}_{A,1}\overline{{\bf w}}_{B,2}+{\bf w}_{A,0}\overline{{\bf w}}_{B,2}+{\bf w}_{A,1}\overline{{\bf w}}_{B,3}\right)-560\ L.\end{split}

(30)

If we denote
{\bf K}=\zeta_{{\bf d}}\,{\boldsymbol{\chi}}\left({\bf U}^{2}-{\bf V}\right)
, then
\left|{\bf K}\right|^{2}=\left|{\bf U}^{2}-{\bf V}\right|

and

\displaystyle\left|{\bf d}\right|^{2}+2\ {\rm Re}\;\!\left({\bf d}\,\overline{{\bf U}}\right)=\left({\bf K}-{\bf U}\right)\left(\overline{{\bf K}}-\overline{{\bf U}}\right)+2\ {\rm Re}\;\!\left({\bf K}\overline{{\bf U}}\right)-2\ \left|{\bf U}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left|{\bf K}\right|^{2}-2\ {\rm Re}\;\!\left({\bf K}\overline{{\bf U}}\right)+\left|{\bf U}\right|^{2}+2\ {\rm Re}\;\!\left({\bf K}\overline{{\bf U}}\right)-2\left|{\bf U}\right|^{2}=\left|{\bf K}\right|^{2}-\left|{\bf U}\right|^{2}=\left|{\bf U}^{2}-{\bf V}\right|-\left|{\bf U}\right|^{2}.

This expression is clearly independent of
\zeta_{{\bf d}}
and the same is true for
v
.
The equation for the length interpolation thus simplifies to

\left|{\bf U}^{2}-{\bf V}\right|-\left|{\bf U}\right|^{2}+v=0.

Observing (
28
) and (
30
) together with (
25
) we see that
{\bf U}
,
{\bf V}
and
v
depend only on the given data and free parameters
\alpha_{0}
,
\alpha_{1}
,
\beta_{0}
and
\beta_{1}
.
To emphasize the dependence of the final equation on these parameters, we write it as

e(\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},\beta_{0},\beta_{1})=0\quad{\rm where}\quad e:=\left|{\bf U}^{2}-{\bf V}\right|-\left|{\bf U}\right|^{2}+v.

(31)

Again, we have one scalar equation for four unknown parameters
\alpha_{0}
,
\alpha_{1}
,
\beta_{0}
and
\beta_{1}
.
To further simplify the solvability analysis, we additionally assume that

\beta_{0}=\beta_{1}=0
. Since

{\bf r}^{\prime}(0)=\alpha_{0}^{2}{\bf t}_{0},\;{\bf r}^{\prime\prime}(0)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\beta_{0}{\bf t}_{0}+\kappa_{0}\alpha_{0}^{4}{\bf n}_{0}\right)\quad{\rm and}\quad{\bf r}^{\prime}(1)=\alpha_{1}^{2}{\bf t}_{1},\;{\bf r}^{\prime\prime}(1)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\beta_{1}{\bf t}_{1}+\kappa_{1}\alpha_{1}^{4}{\bf n}_{1}\right)

this assumption implies that the first and the second order derivative at boundary points are orthogonal; the property that holds for all the points in the case of an arc-length parameterization. Moreover, by introducing a new parameter

\lambda:=\frac{\left|\alpha_{1}\right|}{\left|\alpha_{0}\right|}
, such that
\lambda^{2}
prescribes the ratio between lengths of boundary tangents
{\bf r}^{\prime}(1)
and
{\bf r}^{\prime}(0)
,
we formulate the next theorem.

Theorem 2

Suppose
{\bf r}
is the interpolating planar PH biarc (
22
) of degree
7
, given in Theorem
1
with
\beta_{0}=\beta_{1}=0
. For any chosen
L>\left|\Delta{\bf P}_{0}\right|
and any positive
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, there exist at least one positive
\alpha^{(1)}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, such that
e(\pm\alpha^{(1)}_{0},\pm\lambda\alpha^{(1)}_{0},0,0)=0
and at least one positive
\alpha^{(2)}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, such that
e(\pm\alpha^{(2)}_{0},\mp\lambda\alpha^{(2)}_{0},0,0)=0
.

Proof
:
Since
{\bf t}_{0}
and
{\bf t}_{1}
are normalized, we can express them as

{\bf t}_{\ell}=\cos{\theta_{\ell}}+{\bf i}\sin{\theta_{\ell}}
,
\ell=0,1
, for some angles
\theta_{\ell}\in[0,2\pi)
. Under the assumption
\beta_{0}=\beta_{1}=0
and
\alpha_{1}=\pm\lambda\alpha_{0}
, it is easy to see from (
25
) that

e(\alpha_{0},\pm\lambda\alpha_{0},0,0)
includes only even terms of
\alpha_{0}
, i.e. it is an even function of the unknown
\alpha_{0}
.
Moreover,
expression
e_{1}(\alpha_{0},\pm\lambda\alpha_{0},0,0)
,
e_{1}:=-\left|{\bf U}\right|^{2}+v
,
is an even polynomial of degree
6
in a variable
\alpha_{0}
, and

e_{2}(\alpha_{0},\pm\lambda\alpha_{0},0,0)
,
e_{2}:=|{\bf U}^{2}-{\bf V}|^{2}
,
is an even
polynomial of degree
12
in a variable
\alpha_{0}
. We further compute that the free and the leading coefficient of

e_{1}(\alpha_{0},\pm\lambda\alpha_{0},0,0)

are equal to

c_{0}=-\frac{140}{13}L,\quad c_{1}=\frac{1}{29952}\left(131\kappa_{0}^{2}\pm 61\Theta\lambda^{3}\kappa_{0}\kappa_{1}+131\lambda^{6}\kappa_{1}^{2}\right)

where

\Theta=\sqrt{\cos\left(\theta_{0}\right)+1}\sqrt{\cos\left(\theta_{1}\right)+1}\left(\tan\left(\frac{\theta_{0}}{2}\right)\tan\left(\frac{\theta_{1}}{2}\right)+1\right),

and that
c_{2}=(\frac{140}{13})^{2}\left|\Delta{\bf P}_{0}\right|^{2}
is the free coefficient of

e_{2}(\alpha_{0},\pm\lambda\alpha_{0},0,0)

Thus, as
e=\sqrt{e_{2}}+e_{1}
, we get

e(0,0,0,0)=\frac{140}{13}\left(\left|\Delta{\bf P}_{0}\right|-L\right)<0.

If we prove that
c_{1}
is always positive, then the limit
\displaystyle{\lim_{\alpha_{0}\to\infty}e(\alpha_{0},\pm\lambda\alpha_{0},0,0)}
is also positive. Thus there exist at least one positive zero
\alpha^{(1)}_{0}
of
the function
e(\alpha_{0},\lambda\alpha_{0},0,0)
, and at least one positive zero
\alpha^{(2)}_{0}
of the function

e(\alpha_{0},-\lambda\alpha_{0},0,0)
. Since these two functions are even, the statement of the theorem follows.

It remains to show that
c_{1}>0
independently of the chosen
\lambda
,
\kappa_{\ell}
and
\theta_{\ell}
,
\ell=0,1
.
From trigonometric identities it follows that

\left|\Theta\right|=2\left|\cos{\left(\frac{1}{2}(\theta_{0}-\theta_{1})\right)}\right|\leq 2,

and this implies

\displaystyle 29952c_{1}=

\displaystyle 131\kappa_{0}^{2}\pm 61\Theta\lambda^{3}\kappa_{0}\kappa_{1}+131\lambda^{6}\kappa_{1}^{2}\geq 131\kappa_{0}^{2}-122\lambda^{3}\left|\kappa_{0}\kappa_{1}\right|+131\lambda^{6}\kappa_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle>131\kappa_{0}^{2}-262\lambda^{3}\left|\kappa_{0}\kappa_{1}\right|+131\lambda^{6}\kappa_{1}^{2}=\left(\sqrt{131}\left|\kappa_{0}\right|-\sqrt{131}\lambda^{3}\left|\kappa_{1}\right|\right)^{2}\geq 0,

which concludes the proof.
∎

From Theorem
2
it follows that there exist at least four solutions of the equation (
31
) for
\beta_{0}=\beta_{1}=0
and a fixed parameter
\lambda
. Since this equation is independent of the sign
\zeta_{{\bf d}}
, this would imply at least eight biarc solutions, obtained for two different choices of
\zeta_{{\bf d}}\in\{-1,1\}
. However, simultaneously changing the signs of
\alpha_{0}
,
\alpha_{1}
and
\zeta_{{\bf d}}
does not change the control points of the biarc. Therefore, we fix the sign
\zeta_{{\bf d}}=1
, and in general we have four solution interpolants.

6
Conclusions

Most methods to construct curves rely on the interpolation of discrete data, such as points, tangents or curvatures. If, in addition, prescribed arc length is imposed, in general the use of iterative approximate methods is necessary.
Pythagorean–hodograph (PH) curves are polynomial curves with the distinctive property of possessing arc lengths exactly determined by simple algebraic expressions in their coefficients. Hence the problem of constructing
G^{2}
planar curves, that interpolate points, tangent directions and curvatures, and in addition have prescribed arc-length, can be exactly addressed.
In this paper such problem is investigated considering PH curves of degree
7
and it is shown that it reduces to three non-linear equations with one free shape parameter. However there exist data for which no interpolants can be found. A way to overcome this drawback is to consider
biarcs
keeping the degree to
7
. In this way
the solution of the
G^{2}
continuity equations can be derived in a closed form,
depending on four free parameters. By fixing two of them to zero, it is proven that the length constraint can be satisfied for any data. Beside of being easy to implement and simple to use in practice, the proposed method can be directly applied to a (local) construction of
G^{2}
continuous interpolating splines, as shown in the final examples.

As a matter of further research, we aim to extend the proposed approach to the spatial case. We believe the extension is possible but not so straightforward.
Another interesting issue would be to consider the described
G^{2}
interpolation problem using quintic PH biarcs. Counting the number of degrees of freedom, this could be possible, but the theoretical analysis of the existence of the solution is expected to be much more complicated.

A fully nonlinear locally constrained curvature flow for capillary hypersurface
Xinqun Mei, Liangjun Weng

Abstract
. In this article, we study a locally constrained fully nonlinear curvature flow for convex capillary hypersurfaces in half-space. We prove that the flow preserves the convexity, exists for all time, and converges smoothly to a spherical cap. This can be viewed as the fully nonlinear counterpart of the result in [ 14 ] . As a byproduct, a high-order capillary isoperimetric ratio ( 1.6 ) evolves monotonically along this flow, which yields a class of the Alexandrov-Fenchel inequalities.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we are concerned with constructing the geometric curvature flows to solve isoperimetric type problems for
capillary hypersurfaces
in the half-space. A smooth, compact, embedded hypersurface in
\overline{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1}}
with boundary supported on
\partial{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1}}
is called capillary hypersurface if it intersects with
\partial{{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+}^{n+1}}
at a constant
contact angle

\theta\in(0,\pi)
. The primary objective of this article is that design a more general curvature flow to study the Alexandrov-Fenchel inequalities for capillary hypersurfaces in
[
21
]
, i.e., flow (
1.5
) below, which is a fully nonlinear type flow compared to our previous work together with Wang in
[
14
]
on a mean curvature type flow, a semi-linear flow.

Such kind of fully nonlinear type flow is inspired by the seminal works of Guan-Li
[
7
,
5
,
4
,
6
]
for
closed hypersurfaces and is constructed by using the Minkowski formulas
[
21
, Proposition 2.5]
for capillary hypersurface
\Sigma
in
\overline{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}
, i.e.,

(1.1)

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}H_{k-1}\left(1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle\right)dA=\int_{\Sigma}H_{k}\langle x,\nu\rangle dA,

where
\nu
is the unit normal vector of
\Sigma\subset\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
and
x
is the position vector of
\Sigma
in
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
.

The simplest example of capillary hypersurfaces in half-space is the family of the spherical caps lying entirely in
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
and intersecting
\partial{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}_{+}}
with a constant contact angle
\theta\in(0,\pi)
, and it is given by

(1.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\theta,r}(e):=\left\{x\in\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}\Big|\penalty\ |x-r\cos\theta e|=r\right\},\penalty\ r\penalty\ \in[0,\infty),

which has radius
r
and centered at
r\cos\theta e
. Here
e:=(0,\cdots,0,-1)
is the unit outward normal vector of
\partial{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
in
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
. If without confusion, we just write
\mathcal{C}_{\theta,r}
for
\mathcal{C}_{\theta,r}(e)
in the rest of the paper.

Very recently, Wang-Weng-Xia
[
21
]
introduced a class of new quermassintegrals

for capillary hypersurfaces
\Sigma\subset\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
from the variational viewpoint, where
\widehat{\Sigma}
is the bounded domain in
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
enclosed by
\Sigma
and
\partial{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
. They are, for
1\leq k\leq n
,

(1.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{k+1,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma}):=\frac{1}{n+1}\left(\int_{\Sigma}H_{k}dA-\frac{\cos\theta\sin^{k}\theta}{n}\int_{\partial\Sigma}H_{k-1}^{\partial\Sigma}ds\right),

where
H_{k}
is the normalized
k
-th mean curvature of
\Sigma\subset\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
and
H_{k-1}^{\partial\Sigma}
is the normalized
(k-1)
-th mean curvature of
\partial\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
(see also Section
2.1
or
[
21
, Section 2.2]
). Besides,

(1.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{0,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma}):=|\widehat{\Sigma}|,\penalty\ \penalty\ \mathcal{V}_{1,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma}):=\frac{1}{n+1}(|\Sigma|-\cos\theta|\widehat{\partial\Sigma}|),

where
\widehat{\partial\Sigma}
is the bounded domain enclosed by
\partial\Sigma
in
\partial\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
.
\mathcal{V}_{1,\theta}
is the so-called
capillary area
of
\Sigma
(see e.g.
[
3
]
), up to a multiple constant.

The flow is constructed for a family of capillary hypersurfaces in
\overline{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}_{+}}
given by isometric embeddings
x(\cdot,t):M\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
from a compact
n
-dimensional manifold

with boundary
\partial M
(
n\geq 2

\displaystyle{\rm int}(\Sigma_{t})=x\left({\rm int}(M),t\right)\subset\mathbb{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}_{+},\quad\partial\Sigma_{t}=x(\partial M,t)\subset\partial{\mathbb{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1}},

and

satisfying

(1.5)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{llll}(\partial_{t}x)^{\perp}&=&\vskip 5.69054pt\left(1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle-\langle x,\nu\rangle F\right)\nu,&\hbox{ in }M\times[0,T^{*}),\\
\langle\nu,e\rangle&=&-\cos\theta&\hbox{ on }\partial M\times[0,T^{*}),\end{array}\right.

with
F=H_{k}^{\frac{1}{k}}
and

Other than flows considered in
[
21

[

In particular, when

When
k=1
, flow (
1.5
) reduces to a mean curvature type flow, which is volume-preserving and capillary area-decreasing for functional (
1.4
). However,

(1.6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{k,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma}):=\frac{\mathcal{V}_{k-1,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})}{\mathcal{V}_{0,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})^{\frac{n+2-k}{n+1}}},

Another new ingredient in this paper is that the convexity property is preserved along the general locally constrained curvature flows (

Let

8

As an application of Theorem

For

We remark that (

Proposition 2.6

(

Along flow (

.

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u

\displaystyle=

3.1.

Assume that a capillary hypersurface

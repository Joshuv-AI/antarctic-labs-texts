Abstract
. We define fractional derivatives ∂ t α \partial_{t}^{\alpha} in Sobolev spaces based on L p ​ ( 0 , T ) L^{p}(0,T) by an operator theory, and characterize the domain of ∂ t α \partial_{t}^{\alpha} in subspaces of the Sobolev-Slobodecki spaces W α , p ​ ( 0 , T ) W^{\alpha,p}(0,T) . Moreover we define ∂ t α u \partial_{t}^{\alpha}u for u ∈ L p ​ ( 0 , T ) u\in L^{p}(0,T) in a sense of distribution. Then we discuss initial value problems for linear fractional ordinary differential equations by means of such ∂ t α \partial_{t}^{\alpha} and establish several results on the unique existence of solutions within specified classes according to the regularity of the coefficients and the non-homogeneous terms in the equations. Introduction

Recently fractional differential equations have attracted great attention,
not only by theoretical interests but also for by the necessity for
various applications. For example, time-fractional differential equations
are understood as reasonable model equations in various anomalous diffusion
phenomena which are frequently observed in diffusion in heterogeneous media
around us (e.g., Metzler and Klafter
[
19
]
, Chapter 10 in
Podlubny
[
20
]
).

For
0<\alpha<1
, we can formally define
the pointwise Caputo derivative by

[formula]

d_{t}^{\alpha}v(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{-\alpha}\frac{dv}{ds}(s)ds,

as long as the right-hand side exists.
Here and henceforth, for
\beta>0
by
\Gamma(\beta)
we denote
the gamma function:
\Gamma(\beta):=\int^{\infty}_{0}e^{-t}t^{\beta-1}dt
.
As for classical treatments on fractional derivatives,
we can refer to monographs Gorenflo, Kilbas, Mainardi and Rogosin

[
5
]
, Kilbas, Srivastava and Trujillo
[
13
]
,
Podlubny
[
20
]
for example.

We can interpret
d_{t}^{\alpha}v
as the
\alpha
-th derivaive of
v
, but
in (1.1), the first derivative
\frac{dv}{ds}
appears and so
intuitively
d_{t}^{\alpha}v
requires the existence of
\frac{dv}{dt}

even for defining the lower-order derivative of order
\alpha<1
.
Moreover, a corresponding initial value problem for a time-fractional
ordinary differential equation can be formulated as

[formula]

d_{t}^{\alpha}u(t)=b(t)u(t)+f(t),\quad 0<t<T,

and

[formula]

u(0)=a.

Since only
\alpha
-th derivative appears with
0<\alpha<1
in (1.2),
the initial condition (1.3) is not trivial. This can be understood
in a simple case
where
b\equiv 0
and
f(t)=t^{-\gamma}
with
0<\gamma<1
. Then

u(t)=\frac{\Gamma(1-\gamma)}{\Gamma(\alpha-\gamma+1)}t^{\alpha-\gamma}+a

satisfies (1.2) - (1.3) for
0<\gamma<\alpha<1
, but cannot satisfy
(1.2) - (1.3) if
0<\alpha<\gamma<1
.

This observation suggests us to make adequate formulations for
both fractional derivatives and initial value problems, when we consider
not smooth functions.
There are several studies and here we refer to
In Gorenflo, Luchko and Yamamoto
[
6
]
and Kubica, Ryszewska and
Yamamoto
[
15
]
, Yamamoto
[
23
]
, where one constructs the
fractional derivative operator in Sobolev spaces based on

L^{2}(0,T)
and apply it to fractional differential equations
including partial differential equations in spatial variables.
Also see Kian
[
11
]
, Kian and Yamamoto
[
12
]
,
Luchko and Yamamoto
[
18
]
, Sakamoto and Yamamoto
[
21
]
,
Zacher
[
24
]
.
The method in those works relies on the structure of Hilbert spaces,
that is,
p=2
.

The purpose of this article is to establish the corresponding theory based on

L^{p}(0,T)
with
1\leq p<\infty
.

Throughout this article, we assume

1\leq p<\infty.

Henceforth let

L^{p}(0,T):=\{v;\,\int_{0}^{T}|v(t)|^{p}dt<\infty\}
with

1\leq p<\infty

and
W^{1,p}(0,T):=\{v\in L^{p}(0,T);\,\frac{dv}{dt}\in L^{p}(0,T)\}
.
We define the norm by

\|v\|_{L^{p}(0,T)}:=\left(\int_{0}^{T}|v(t)|^{p}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},\quad\|v\|_{W^{1,p}(0,T)}:=\|v\|_{L^{p}(0,T)}+\left\|\frac{dv}{dt}\right\|_{L^{p}(0,T)}.

For
\alpha>0
, we set

[formula]

J^{\alpha}v(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}v(s)ds,\quad 0<t<T,\,v\in L^{1}(0,T),

which is called the Riemann-Liouville fractional integral operator, and
can be interpreted as an
\alpha
-times integral of
v
.
By the Young inequality on the convolution (e.g., Lemma A.1 in

[
15
]
), we see that

[formula]

J^{\alpha}L^{q}(0,T)\subset L^{q}(0,T)\quad\mbox{with $q\geq 1$}.

Our definition of the fractional derivative relies on
the inverse to
J^{\alpha}
in
L^{p}(0,T)
.
Henceforth by
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}
we denote such a fractional derivative in order to
distinguish from
d_{t}^{\alpha}
. The first step is to first clarify the
space
J^{\alpha}L^{p}(0,T)
and introduce a suitable norm.
After establishing
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}
, we formulate an initial value problem
and discuss the unique existence of solutions with suitable
regularity.

2.
Fractional derivative
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}
in
L^{p}
-Sobolev-Slobodecki spaces
of positive orders: characterization of the domain
\mathcal{D}(\partial_{t}^{\alpha})
and
norm estimates

Henceforth by
\mathcal{D}(K)
, we denote the domain of an operator
K
under
consideration, and
C>0
denotes generic constants depending on

\alpha,p
but not on choices of functions
v
.

We recall that
J^{\alpha}
is defined by (1.4) and we set

\mathcal{D}(J^{\alpha})=L^{p}(0,T)
.
Then by (1.5) we see that

[formula]

\|J^{\alpha}v\|_{L^{p}(0,T)}\leq C\|v\|_{L^{p}(0,T)}\quad\mbox{for all $v\in L^{p}(0,T)$}.

First we prove:

Lemma 2.1.

Let
\alpha,\beta>0
and
\alpha,\beta\not\in\mathbb{N}
.

(i)
J^{\alpha}J^{\beta}v=J^{\alpha+\beta}v
for each
v\in L^{1}(0,T)
.

(ii)
J^{\alpha}:L^{p}(0,T)\,\longrightarrow\,L^{p}(0,T)
is injective for
1\leq p<\infty
..

Proof of Lemma 2.1.

(i) Exchanging the order of the integral, we have

\displaystyle J^{\alpha}(J^{\beta}v)(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}J^{\beta}v(s)ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\left(\int^{s}_{0}(s-\xi)^{\beta-1}v(\xi)d\xi\right)ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(\beta)}\int^{t}_{0}\left(\int^{t}_{\xi}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}(s-\xi)^{\beta-1}ds\right)v(\xi)d\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(\beta)}\int^{t}_{0}\frac{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(\beta)}{\Gamma(\alpha+\beta)}(t-\xi)^{\alpha+\beta-1}v(\xi)d\xi=(J^{\alpha+\beta}v)(t),\quad 0<t<T,

which completes the proof of (i).
\blacksquare

(ii) We assume that
v\in L^{1}(0,T)
satisfies

J^{\alpha}v(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}v(s)ds=0,\quad 0<t<T.

We set
\alpha=(m-1)+\sigma
with
m\in\mathbb{N}
and

0<\sigma<1
. We operate
J^{1-\sigma}
to obtain

\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{-\sigma}\left(\int^{s}_{0}(s-\xi)^{\alpha-1}v(\xi)d\xi\right)ds=0,\quad 0<t<T.

Again exchanging the order of the integral and using
\alpha-\sigma=m-1
, we obtain

\displaystyle 0=\int^{t}_{0}\left(\int^{t}_{\xi}(t-s)^{-\sigma}(s-\xi)^{\alpha-1}ds\right)v(\xi)d\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(1-\sigma)\Gamma(\alpha)}{\Gamma(1-\sigma+\alpha)}\int^{t}_{0}(t-\xi)^{m-1}v(\xi)d\xi,\quad 0<t<T.

Therefore, the
m
-times differentiation yields
v(t)=0
for

0<t<T
. Thus the proof of Lemma 2.1 is complete.

\blacksquare

The proof of (ii) is suggested by the classical formula
(e.g., Gorenflo and Vessella
[
7
]
)

D_{t}^{\alpha}J^{\alpha}v=v,\quad v\in L^{1}(0,T),

where for
\alpha=(m-1)+\sigma
with

m\in\mathbb{N}
and
0<\sigma<1
,
we define the Riemann-Liouville fractional derivative

[formula]

D_{t}^{\alpha}v(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\sigma)}\frac{d^{m}}{dt^{m}}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{-\sigma}v(s)ds,\quad v\in C^{\infty}[0,T].

By Lemma 2.1 (ii), there exists an inverse operator
(J^{\alpha})^{-1}

in
L^{p}(0,T)
to
J^{\alpha}
algebraically.

We define

[formula]

W_{\alpha,p}(0,T):=J^{\alpha}L^{p}(0,T)\quad\mbox{as a set}

with the norm

[formula]

\|u\|_{W_{\alpha,p}(0,T)}:=\|(J^{\alpha})^{-1}u\|_{L^{p}(0,T)}.

We note that

\|J^{\alpha}w\|_{W_{\alpha,p}(0,T)}=\|w\|_{L^{p}(0,T)}.

Therefore,

Lemma 2.2.

Let
\alpha>0
. If

w_{n}\longrightarrow w
in
L^{p}(0,T)
, then
J^{\alpha}w_{n}\longrightarrow J^{\alpha}w
in

W_{\alpha,p}(0,T)
.

Moreover, we prove

Lemma 2.3.

The space
W_{\alpha,p}(0,T)
is complete with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{W_{\alpha,p}(0,T)}
, that is,

W_{\alpha,p}(0,T)
is a Banach space.

Proof.

Let
u_{n}\in W_{\alpha,p}(0,T)
and
\lim_{m,n\to\infty}\|u_{n}-u_{m}\|_{W_{\alpha,p}(0,T)}=0
.
Then for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we can find
w_{n}\in L^{p}(0,T)
such that

u_{n}=J^{\alpha}w_{n}
. Hence,

\|u_{m}-u_{n}\|_{W_{\alpha,p}(0,T)}=\|J^{\alpha}(w_{m}-w_{n})\|_{W_{\alpha,p}(0,T)}=\|w_{m}-w_{n}\|_{L^{p}(0,T)}\,\longrightarrow 0

as
m,n\to\infty
. By the completeness of
L^{p}(0,T)
, there exists

w_{0}\in L^{p}(0,T)
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|w_{n}-w_{0}\|_{L^{p}(0,T)}=0
. Set
u_{0}:=J^{\alpha}w_{0}
.
Lemma 2.2 yields
J^{\alpha}w_{n}\longrightarrow J^{\alpha}w_{0}
in
W_{\alpha,p}(0,T)
, that is,

u_{n}\longrightarrow J^{\alpha}w_{0}
in
W_{\alpha,p}(0,T)
, where
J^{\alpha}w_{0}\in J^{\alpha}L^{p}(0,T)=W_{\alpha,p}(0,T)
. Thus the proof of Lemma 2.3 is complete.

\blacksquare

We define the fractional derivative.

Defintion 2.1.

Let
\alpha>0
and
\alpha\not\in\mathbb{N}
. Then

\partial_{t}^{\alpha}:=(J^{\alpha})^{-1}\quad\mathcal{D}(\partial_{t}^{\alpha})=W_{\alpha,p}(0,T).

At least
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}
is well-defined, but is not useful if we do not specify
the domain
\mathcal{D}(\partial_{t}^{\alpha})
.
In a special case of
p=2
, by the structure of the Hilbert space

L^{2}(0,T)
, we can make the complete characterization in terms of

W^{\alpha,2}(0,T)
(e.g.,
[
6
]
,
[
15
]
,
[
23
]
).
However for
p\neq 2
, to the author’s best knowledge, such characterization
is not known.

Now we will make characterization of
W_{\alpha,p}(0,T)
and for it we introduce
function spaces.
For
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we set
\frac{d^{0}v}{dt^{0}}(t)=v(t)
and

{}_{0}C^{m}[0,T]:=\left\{v\in C^{m}[0,T];\,\frac{d^{k}v}{dt^{k}}(0)=0\quad\mbox{for $k=0,1,...,m-1$}\right\}.

For
\alpha=m-1+\sigma
with
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
0<\sigma<1
and

1\leq p<\infty

\|u\|_{W^{\alpha,p}(0,T)}:=\left(\|u\|^{p}_{W^{m-1,p}(0,T)}+\int^{T}_{0}\int^{T}_{0}\frac{\left|\frac{d^{m-1}u}{dt^{m-1}}(t)-\frac{d^{m-1}u}{dt^{m-1}}(s)\right|^{p}}{|t-s|^{1+\sigma p}}dtds\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

[

By
\overline{X}^{Y}
we denote the closure of
X\subset Y

Y
.
Moreover

{}_{0}W^{\alpha,p}(0,T):=\,\overline{{}_{0}C^{m}[0,T]}^{W^{\alpha,p}(0,T)}.

We set
W^{0,p}(0,T)=\,_{0}W^{\alpha,p}(0,T)=L^{p}(0,T)

We state the main result in this section.

Theorem 2.1.

Let
\alpha=m-1+\sigma
with
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
0<\sigma<1
, and

\varepsilon>0
be arbitrarily given such that
0<\varepsilon<\alpha
.
Then

(i) There exists a constant
C=C(\alpha,\varepsilon)>0
such that

[formula]

\|J^{\alpha}v\|_{W^{\alpha-\varepsilon,p}(0,T)}\leq C\|v\|_{L^{p}(0,T)}

for all
v\in L^{p}(0,T)
and

W_{\alpha,p}(0,T)\subset\,_{0}W^{\alpha-\varepsilon,p}(0,T).

(ii)
We have

{}_{0}W^{\alpha+\varepsilon,p}(0,T)\subset W_{\alpha,p}(0,T)

and

[formula]

J^{\alpha}\left(\frac{d^{m}}{dt^{m}}J^{m-\alpha}u\right)(t)=u(t)\quad\mbox{for each $u\in\,_{0}W^{\alpha+\varepsilon,p}(0,T)$}.

(iii) The embedding
W_{\alpha+\varepsilon,p}(0,T)\longrightarrow W_{\alpha,p}(0,T)
is compact.

In terms of the Riemann-Liouville fractional derivative (2.2), we can
rewrite (2.6) as

J^{\alpha}D_{t}^{\alpha}u=u\quad\mbox{for each $u\in\,_{0}W^{\alpha+\varepsilon,p}(0,T)$}.

We note that for
\alpha=m-1
with
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we can easily prove

J^{\alpha}L^{p}(0,T)=\left\{v\in W^{m,p}(0,T);\,\frac{d^{k}v}{dt^{k}}(0)=0\quad\mbox{for $k=0,1,...,m-1$}\right\}.

In the case of
p=2
and
0<\alpha<1
, it is proved in

[

]
that
W_{\alpha,2}(0,T)=\,\overline{{}_{0}C^{1}[0,T]}^{W^{\alpha,2}(0,T)}
, which means that we can

in (i) and (ii).
However, in the case

, we do not know the equivalent representation of

, but (i) and (ii) imply

is interpolated between
{}_{0}W^{\alpha+\varepsilon,p}(0,T)
and

Proof of Theorem 2.1 (i).

First Step:
0<\alpha<1
.

First we show that for
\delta\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right)
,
there exists a constant
C_{\delta}>0
such that

[formula]

|1-\eta^{\alpha-1}|\leq(1-\alpha)(1-\eta)+C_{\delta}(1-\eta)^{2}\quad\mbox{if $1-\delta\leq\eta\leq 1$}.

Verification of (2.7).

Applying the Taylor theorem to
g(\eta):=\eta^{\alpha-1}
,
we obtain

g(\eta)=g(1)+g^{\prime}(1)(\eta-1)+\frac{g^{\prime\prime}(\zeta)}{2!}(\eta-1)^{2}\quad\mbox{for $1-\delta\leq\eta\leq 1$},

where
\zeta\in(1-\delta,1)
is some number. Therefore

|\eta^{\alpha-1}-1-(\alpha-1)(\eta-1)|\leq C_{\delta}(\eta-1)^{2}
, which implies (2.7).

\blacksquare

We set
\beta:=\alpha-\varepsilon
with
0<\varepsilon<\alpha
.
We recall that

\|v\|^{p}_{W^{\beta,p}(0,T)}=\|v\|^{p}_{L^{p}(0,T)}+\int^{T}_{0}\int^{T}_{0}\frac{|v(t)-v(s)|^{p}}{|t-s|^{1+\beta p}}dtds.

We can see that it suffices to estimate for
0<s<t
.
Indeed, dividing
[0,T]^{2}=\{(t,s)\in[0,T]^{2};\,0\leq s\leq t\leq T\}\cup\{(t,s)\in[0,T]^{2};\,0\leq t\leq s\leq T\}
, we
have

\int^{T}_{0}\int^{T}_{0}\frac{|v(t)-v(s)|^{p}}{|t-s|^{1+\beta p}}dtds=2\int^{T}_{0}\left(\int^{t}_{0}\frac{|v(t)-v(s)|^{p}}{|t-s|^{1+\beta p}}ds\right)dt.

We have

\displaystyle|\Gamma(\alpha)(J^{\alpha}v(t)-J^{\alpha}v(s))|=\left|\int^{t}_{0}(t-\xi)^{\alpha-1}v(\xi)d\xi-\int^{s}_{0}(s-\xi)^{\alpha-1}v(\xi)d\xi\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\int^{t}_{s}(t-\xi)^{\alpha-1}v(\xi)d\xi+\int^{s}_{0}((t-\xi)^{\alpha-1}-(s-\xi)^{\alpha-1})v(\xi)d\xi\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int^{t}_{s}(t-\xi)^{\alpha-1}|v(\xi)|d\xi+\int^{s}_{0}|(t-\xi)^{\alpha-1}-(s-\xi)^{\alpha-1}||v(\xi)|d\xi,

and so

\displaystyle|J^{\alpha}v(t)-J^{\alpha}v(s)|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\int^{t}_{s}(t-\xi)^{\alpha-1}|v(\xi)|d\xi\right)^{p}+C\left(\int^{s}_{0}|(t-\xi)^{\alpha-1}-(s-\xi)^{\alpha-1}||v(\xi)|d\xi\right)^{p}.

Hence

\int^{T}_{0}\int^{t}_{0}\frac{|J^{\alpha}v(t)-J^{\alpha}v(s)|^{p}}{|t-s|^{1+\beta p}}dsdt\leq C\int^{T}_{0}dt\int^{t}_{0}ds(t-s)^{-1-\beta p}\left(\int^{t}_{s}(t-\xi)^{\alpha-1}|v(\xi)|d\xi\right)^{p}

[formula]

+C\int^{T}_{0}dt\int^{t}_{0}ds(t-s)^{-1-\beta p}\left(\int^{s}_{0}|(t-\xi)^{\alpha-1}-(s-\xi)^{\alpha-1}||v(\xi)|d\xi\right)^{p}=:CI_{1}+CI_{2}.

Case 1:
p=1
.

Equation (2.8) implies

\int^{T}_{0}\int^{t}_{0}\frac{|J^{\alpha}v(t)-J^{\alpha}v(s)|}{|t-s|^{1+\beta}}dsdt\leq CI_{1}+CI_{2},

where

I_{1}=\int^{T}_{0}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{-1-\beta}\left(\int^{t}_{s}(t-\xi)^{\alpha-1}|v(\xi)|d\xi\right)dsdt

and

I_{2}=\int^{T}_{0}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{-1-\beta}\left(\int^{s}_{0}|(t-\xi)^{\alpha-1}-(s-\xi)^{\alpha-1}||v(\xi)|d\xi\right)dsdt.

Exchanging the order of the integral, we obtain

\displaystyle I_{1}=\int^{T}_{0}\int^{t}_{0}|v(\xi)|(t-\xi)^{\alpha-1}\left(\int^{\xi}_{0}(t-s)^{-1-\beta}ds\right)d\xi dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta}\int^{T}_{0}\left(\int^{t}_{0}|v(\xi)|(t-\xi)^{\alpha-1}((t-\xi)^{-\beta}-t^{-\beta})d\xi\right)dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int^{T}_{0}\int^{t}_{0}|v(\xi)|(t-\xi)^{\alpha-\beta-1}\left(1-\left(\frac{t-\xi}{t}\right)^{\beta}\right)d\xi dt\leq C\int^{T}_{0}\left|\int^{t}_{0}|v(\xi)|(t-\xi)^{\varepsilon-1}d\xi\right|dt

by
\left|\frac{t-\xi}{t}\right|\leq 1
and

\beta>0
for
0<\xi<t
.
Therefore, the Young inequality for the convolution yields

|I_{1}|\leq C\|s^{\xi-1}\,*\,|v|\|_{L^{1}(0,T)}\leq C\|v\|_{L^{1}(0,T)}.

As for the term
I_{2}
, by the change
s:=\eta(t-\xi)+\xi
of the variables,
exchanging the order of the integral, we deduce

\displaystyle\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{-1-\beta}\left(\int^{s}_{0}|(t-\xi)^{\alpha-1}-(s-\xi)^{\alpha-1}||v(\xi)|d\xi\right)ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int^{t}_{0}\left(\int^{t}_{\xi}(t-s)^{-1-\beta}|(s-\xi)^{\alpha-1}-(t-\xi)^{\alpha-1}|ds\right)|v(\xi)|d\xi

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int^{t}_{0}|v(\xi)|(t-\xi)^{\varepsilon-1}\left(\int^{1}_{0}(1-\eta)^{-1-\beta}(\eta^{\alpha-1}-1)d\eta\right)d\xi.

Here for arbitrarily fixed constant
\delta\in\left(0,\,\frac{1}{2}\right)
,
we apply (2.7) to estimate

\displaystyle\int^{1}_{1-\delta}(1-\eta)^{-1-\beta}|\eta^{\alpha-1}-1|d\eta\leq\int^{1}_{1-\delta}(1-\eta)^{-1-\beta}((1-\alpha)(1-\eta)+C_{\delta}(1-\eta)^{2})d\eta

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{\delta}\int^{1}_{1-\delta}((1-\eta)^{-\beta}+(1-\eta)^{1-\beta})d\eta<\infty

by
0<\beta<\alpha<1
. Furthermore

\int^{1-\delta}_{0}(1-\eta)^{-1-\beta}|\eta^{\alpha-1}-1|d\eta\leq C_{\delta}\int^{1-\delta}_{0}(|\eta^{\alpha-1}|+1)d\eta<\infty.

Consequently, the Young inequality yields

|I_{2}|\leq C_{\delta}\int^{T}_{0}\left|\int^{t}_{0}|v(\xi)|(t-\xi)^{\varepsilon-1}d\xi\right|dt=C_{\delta}\|s^{\varepsilon-1}\,*\,|v|\|_{L^{1}(0,T)}\leq C_{\delta}\|v\|_{L^{1}(0,T)}.

Thus the proof of (i) for
p=1
and
0<\alpha<1
is completed.

\blacksquare

Case 2:
p>1
.

First we note that since
W^{\alpha-\varepsilon,\,p}(0,T)\subset W^{\alpha-\varepsilon^{\prime},\,p}(0,T)
if
\alpha>\varepsilon^{\prime}>\varepsilon>0
, it suffices to
prove (i) for sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
, and in particular, we can assume
that

[formula]

\alpha>\varepsilon p.

Let
q\in(1,\infty)
satisfy
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
.
By
1<p<\infty
, we have
q=\frac{p}{p-1}\in(1,\infty)
.

In
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
, the integrands contain
|v(\xi)|
, not

|v(\xi)|^{p}
, so that we need to change
such factors to
|v(\xi)|^{p}
. To this end, we resort to
the Hölder inequality after factorizing

|t-\xi|^{\alpha-1}|v(\xi)|=(|t-\xi|^{\alpha-1})^{\frac{1}{q}}\{(|t-\xi|^{\alpha-1})^{1-\frac{1}{q}}|v(\xi)|\}.

Then the Hölder inequality implies

\displaystyle\int^{t}_{s}|t-\xi|^{\alpha-1}|v(\xi)|d\xi=\int^{t}_{s}(|t-\xi|^{\alpha-1})^{\frac{1}{q}}\{(|t-\xi|^{\alpha-1})^{1-\frac{1}{q}}|v(\xi)|\}d\xi

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int^{t}_{s}(t-\xi)^{\alpha-1}d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int^{t}_{s}((t-\xi)^{\alpha-1})^{p\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)}|v(\xi)|^{p}d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

and so

\displaystyle\left(\int^{t}_{s}|t-\xi|^{\alpha-1}|v(\xi)|d\xi\right)^{p}\leq\left(\int^{t}_{s}(t-\xi)^{\alpha-1}d\xi\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}\int^{t}_{s}|t-\xi|^{\alpha-1}|v(\xi)|^{p}d\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha^{-\frac{p}{q}}(t-s)^{\frac{p}{q}\alpha}\int^{t}_{s}(t-\xi)^{\alpha-1}|v(\xi)|^{p}d\xi.

Therefore, since
\frac{1}{q}=1-\frac{1}{p}
implies

[formula]

-1-\beta p+\frac{p}{q}\alpha=-1-\beta p+\alpha p-\alpha=-1-\alpha+\varepsilon p,

using
\int^{t}_{0}\left(\int^{t}_{s}\cdots d\xi\right)ds=\int^{t}_{0}\left(\int^{\xi}_{0}\cdots ds\right)d\xi
,
we obtain

\displaystyle I_{1}\leq C\int^{T}_{0}dt\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{-1-\alpha+\varepsilon p}\left(\int^{t}_{s}(t-\xi)^{\alpha-1}|v(\xi)|^{p}d\xi\right)ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\int^{T}_{0}dt\int^{t}_{0}\left(\int^{\xi}_{0}(t-s)^{-1-\alpha+\varepsilon p}ds\right)(t-\xi)^{\alpha-1}|v(\xi)|^{p}d\xi.

Since
0<s<\xi<t
, we have

\int^{\xi}_{0}(t-s)^{-1-\alpha+\varepsilon p}ds=\frac{1}{\varepsilon p-\alpha}((t-\xi)^{-\alpha+\varepsilon p}-t^{-\alpha+\varepsilon p}),

and by (2.9) we deduce

[formula]

|I_{1}|\leq C\int^{T}_{0}\left(\int^{t}_{0}|t^{-\alpha+\varepsilon p}-(t-\xi)^{-\alpha+\varepsilon p}||t-\xi|^{\alpha-1}|v(\xi)|^{p}d\xi\right)dt.

On the other hand,

|t^{-\alpha+\varepsilon p}-(t-\xi)^{-\alpha+\varepsilon p}|=|t-\xi|^{-\alpha+\varepsilon p}\left|1-\left|\frac{t-\xi}{t}\right|^{\alpha-\varepsilon p}\right|.

From (2.9) and
\left|\frac{t-\xi}{t}\right|\leq 1
, it
follows that
\left|\frac{t-\xi}{t}\right|^{\alpha-\varepsilon p}\leq 1

and

|t^{-\alpha+\varepsilon p}-(t-\xi)^{-\alpha+\varepsilon p}|\leq 2|t-\xi|^{-\alpha+\varepsilon p}\quad\mbox{for $0<\xi<t$}.

Hence,

\displaystyle|I_{1}|\leq 2C\int^{T}_{0}\left(\int^{t}_{0}|t-\xi|^{-\alpha+\varepsilon p}|t-\xi|^{\alpha-1}|v(\xi)|^{p}d\xi\right)dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\int^{T}_{0}\left|\int^{t}_{0}|t-\xi|^{\varepsilon p-1}|v(\xi)|^{p}d\xi\right|dt=C\|s^{\varepsilon p-1}\,*\,|v|^{p}\|_{L^{1}(0,T)}.

The Young inequality implies

[formula]

|I_{1}|\leq C\|s^{\varepsilon p-1}\|_{L^{1}(0,T)}\||v|^{p}\|_{L^{1}(0,T)}\leq C\|v\|^{p}_{L^{p}(0,T)}.

We now proceed to the proof of
I_{2}
. Similarly to
I_{1}
, we
factorize

\displaystyle|(t-\xi)^{\alpha-1}-(s-\xi)^{\alpha-1}||v(\xi)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|(t-\xi)^{\alpha-1}-(s-\xi)^{\alpha-1}|^{\frac{1}{q}}\{|(t-\xi)^{\alpha-1}-(s-\xi)^{\alpha-1}|^{1-\frac{1}{q}}|v(\xi)|\}

and apply the Hölder inequality to obtain

\displaystyle\left(\int^{s}_{0}|(t-\xi)^{\alpha-1}-(s-\xi)^{\alpha-1}||v(\xi)|d\xi\right)^{p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int^{s}_{0}|(t-\xi)^{\alpha-1}-(s-\xi)^{\alpha-1}|d\xi\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}\int^{s}_{0}|(t-\xi)^{\alpha-1}-(s-\xi)^{\alpha-1}||v(\xi)|^{p}d\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{(t-s)^{\alpha}-(t^{\alpha}-s^{\alpha})}{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}\int^{s}_{0}|(t-\xi)^{\alpha-1}-(s-\xi)^{\alpha-1}||v(\xi)|^{p}d\xi

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(t-s)^{\frac{p\alpha}{q}}\int^{s}_{0}|(t-\xi)^{\alpha-1}-(s-\xi)^{\alpha-1}||v(\xi)|^{p}d\xi.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{-1-\beta p}\left(\int^{s}_{0}|(t-\xi)^{\alpha-1}-(s-\xi)^{\alpha-1}||v(\xi)|d\xi\right)^{p}ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{-1-\beta p+\frac{p\alpha}{q}}\left(\int^{s}_{0}|(t-\xi)^{\alpha-1}-(s-\xi)^{\alpha-1}||v(\xi)|^{p}d\xi\right)ds.

Exchanging the order of the integral and using (2.10), we have

\displaystyle\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{-1-\beta p}\left(\int^{s}_{0}|(t-\xi)^{\alpha-1}-(s-\xi)^{\alpha-1}||v(\xi)|^{p}d\xi\right)ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int^{t}_{0}\left(\int^{t}_{\xi}(t-s)^{-1-\alpha+p\varepsilon}((s-\xi)^{\alpha-1}-(t-\xi)^{\alpha-1})ds\right)|v(\xi)|^{p}d\xi.

Changing the variables
s\mapsto\eta
by
s:=\eta(t-\xi)+\xi
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{-1-\alpha+\varepsilon p}\left(\int^{s}_{0}|(t-\xi)^{\alpha-1}-(s-\xi)^{\alpha-1}||v(\xi)|^{p}d\xi\right)ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\int^{t}_{0}|v(\xi)|^{p}(t-\xi)^{\varepsilon p-1}\left(\int^{1}_{0}(1-\eta)^{-1-\alpha+\varepsilon p}(\eta^{\alpha-1}-1)d\eta\right)d\xi.

Here

\int^{1}_{0}(1-\eta)^{-1-\alpha+\varepsilon p}(\eta^{\alpha-1}-1)d\eta<\infty.

Indeed, for fixed
\delta\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right)
, we deduce

\int^{\delta}_{0}(1-\eta)^{-1-\alpha+\varepsilon p}(\eta^{\alpha-1}-1)d\eta\leq\int^{\delta}_{0}(\eta^{\alpha-1}-1)d\eta<\infty

and (2.7) yields

\displaystyle\int^{1}_{1-\delta}(1-\eta)^{-1-\alpha+\varepsilon p}|\eta^{\alpha-1}-1|d\eta

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int^{1}_{1-\delta}(1-\eta)^{-1-\alpha+\varepsilon p}((1-\alpha)(1-\eta)+C_{\delta}(1-\eta)^{2})d\eta\leq C_{\delta}\int^{1}_{1-\delta}(1-\eta)^{-\alpha+\varepsilon p}d\eta<\infty,

because
-\alpha+\varepsilon p>-1
by
0<\alpha<1
.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{-1-\alpha+\varepsilon p}\left(\int^{s}_{0}|(t-\xi)^{\alpha-1}-(s-\xi)^{\alpha-1}||v(\xi)|^{p}d\xi\right)ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C|(s^{\varepsilon p-1}\,*\,|v|^{p})(t)|,\quad 0<t<T.

Then the Young inequality yields

[formula]

|I_{2}|\leq C\|s^{\varepsilon p-1}\,*\,|v|^{p}\|_{L^{1}(0,T)}\leq C\|s^{\varepsilon p-1}\|_{L^{1}(0,T)}\||v|^{p}\|_{L^{1}(0,T)}\leq C\|v\|_{L^{p}(0,T)}^{p}.

Hence, for
p>1
and
0<\alpha<1
, by (2.8), (2.12) and (2.13),
we complete the proof of the estimate in Theorem 2.1 (i).

Second Step:
\alpha>1
.

Let
\alpha=\ell+\sigma
where
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
and
0<\sigma<1
.
Then

J^{\alpha}v(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\ell+\sigma)}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\ell+\sigma-1}v(s)ds,\quad 0<t<T,\,v\in L^{p}(0,T).

Therefore

\frac{d^{k}}{dt^{k}}J^{\alpha}v(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\ell-k+\sigma)}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\ell-k+\sigma-1}v(s)ds,\quad 0\leq k\leq\ell,\,0<t<T,\,v\in L^{p}(0,T).

In particular,

\frac{d^{\ell}}{dt^{\ell}}J^{\alpha}v(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\sigma)}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\sigma-1}v(s)ds,\quad 0\leq k\leq\ell,\,0<t<T,\,v\in L^{p}(0,T).

Hence,

[formula]

\left\|\frac{d^{k}}{dt^{k}}J^{\alpha}\right\|_{L^{p}(0,T)}=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\ell-k+\sigma)}\|s^{\ell-k+\sigma-1}\,*\,v\|_{L^{p}(0,T)}\leq C\|v\|_{L^{p}(0,T)},\quad 0\leq k\leq\ell.

Since we have already proved (2.5) with
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
we see

[formula]

\left\|\frac{1}{\Gamma(\sigma)}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\sigma-1}v(s)ds\right\|_{W^{\sigma-\varepsilon,p}(0,T)}\leq C\|v\|_{L^{p}(0,T)}

with small
\varepsilon\in(0,\sigma)
.
Consequently, in terms of (2.14) and (2.15), we deduce

\displaystyle\|J^{\alpha}v\|^{p}_{W^{\alpha-\varepsilon,p}(0,T)}=\sum_{k=0}^{\ell}\left\|\frac{d^{k}}{dt^{k}}J^{\alpha}v\right\|^{p}_{L^{p}(0,T)}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int^{T}_{0}\int^{T}_{0}\frac{\left|\frac{d^{\ell}}{dt^{\ell}}J^{\alpha}v(t)-\frac{d^{\ell}}{dt^{\ell}}J^{\alpha}v(s)\right|^{p}}{|t-s|^{1+(\sigma-\varepsilon)p}}dtds\leq C\|v\|^{p}_{L^{p}(0,T)}

for all
v\in L^{p}(0,T)
.

Finally, for
\alpha=m-1+\sigma
with
m\in\mathbb{N}
and

0<\sigma<1
, we have to prove that

W_{\alpha,p}(0,T)\subset\,_{0}W^{\alpha-\varepsilon,p}(0,T)
.
Let
u\in W_{\alpha,p}(0,T)
be arbitrarily given. By the definition (2.4), there exists

w\in L^{p}(0,T)
such that
u=J^{\alpha}w
. By the density, we can find

w_{n}\in\,_{0}C^{m}[0,T]
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, such that
w_{n}\longrightarrow w
in

L^{p}(0,T)
as
n\to\infty
. Setting
u_{n}:=J^{\alpha}w_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
by
w_{n}\in\,_{0}C^{m}[0,T]
, we can directly prove that

u_{n}\in\,_{0}C^{m}[0,T]
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Indeed, since we can directly see that
J^{\alpha}C[0,T]\subset C[0,T]

with
\alpha>0
by estimating
J^{\alpha}u(t)-J^{\alpha}u(s)
for

u\in C[0,T]
, this follows by applying (2.16) to
v:=w_{n}
and

\gamma:=\alpha
:

[formula]

\frac{d^{k}}{dt^{k}}J^{\gamma}v=J^{\gamma}\frac{d^{k}v}{dt^{k}}\quad\mbox{for $v\in\,_{0}C^{m}[0,T]$ and $\gamma>0$, $k=0,1,...,m$.}

Verification of (2.16).

Since
\frac{d^{k}v}{dt^{k}}(0)=0
for
0\leq k\leq m-1

and
\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\gamma-1}v(s)ds=\int^{t}_{0}s^{\gamma-1}v(t-s)ds
, we have

\frac{d}{dt}J^{\gamma}v(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\gamma)}\left(\int^{t}_{0}s^{\gamma-1}\frac{dv}{dt}(t-s)ds+t^{\gamma-1}v(0)\right)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\gamma)}\int^{t}_{0}s^{\gamma-1}\frac{dv}{dt}(t-s)ds

and

\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}J^{\gamma}v(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\gamma)}\left(\int^{t}_{0}s^{\gamma-1}\frac{d^{2}v}{dt^{2}}(t-s)ds+t^{\gamma-1}\frac{dv}{dt}(0)\right).

We can continue the calculations to finish the proof of (2.16).

\blacksquare

Then, by (2.5), we can deduce that

J^{\alpha}w_{n}\longrightarrow J^{\alpha}w
in
W^{\alpha-\varepsilon,p}(0,T)
, that is,

u_{n}\longrightarrow u
in
W^{\alpha-\varepsilon,p}(0,T)
as
n\to\infty
.
Since
u_{n}\in\,_{0}C^{m}[0,T]
, this means that

u\in\,\overline{{}_{0}C^{m}[0,T]}^{W^{\alpha-\varepsilon,p}(0,T)}=\,_{0}W^{\alpha-\varepsilon,p}(0,T)
.

Thus the proof of Theorem 2.1 (i) is completed.

\blacksquare

Proof of Theorem 2.1 (ii).

First Step.

We show

Lemma 2.4.

Let
\alpha=m-1+\sigma
with
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
0<\varepsilon<1-\sigma
, and

1\leq p<\infty
. Then there exists a constant
C=C(p,\alpha,\varepsilon)>0
such
that

\|J^{m-\alpha}u\|_{W^{m,p}(0,T)}\leq C\|u\|_{W^{\alpha+\varepsilon,p}(0,T)}

for all
u\in\,_{0}W^{\alpha+\varepsilon,p}(0,T)
.

For
p=1
and
m=1
, the proof is found in Theorem 4.2.3 (pp.77-78) in

[
7
]
. Also our proof for the general
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
1<p<\infty
,
relies on direct estimation, but is lengthy.
Thus the proof of Lemma 2.4 is postponed to Section 6.

Second Step.

By (2.16), we can directly prove that formula (2.6) holds for

u\in\,_{0}C^{m}[0,T]
.
Indeed, by
m-\alpha=1-\sigma\in(0,1)
, equality (2.16) yields

\frac{d^{m}}{dt^{m}}J^{m-\alpha}u=J^{m-\alpha}\frac{d^{m}u}{dt^{m}},

and so

J^{\alpha}\left(\frac{d^{m}}{dt^{m}}J^{m-\alpha}u\right)=J^{\alpha}J^{m-\alpha}\frac{d^{m}u}{dt^{m}}=\frac{1}{\Gamma(m)}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{m-1}\frac{d^{m}u}{ds^{m}}(s)ds.

Here we used
J^{\alpha}J^{m-\alpha}=J^{m}
by Lemma 2.1 (i).
Repeating the integration by parts and using

\frac{d^{k}u}{ds^{k}}(0)=0
for
0\leq k\leq m-1
. we see

\frac{1}{\Gamma(m)}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{m-1}\frac{d^{m}u}{ds^{m}}(s)ds=u(t),\quad 0<t<T.

Thus we have verified (2.6) for
u\in\,_{0}C^{m}[0,T]
.

Next let
u\in\,_{0}W^{\alpha+\varepsilon,p}(0,T)
be arbitrarily given.
By the definition, we can choose
u_{n}\in\,_{0}C^{m}[0,T]
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, such that

u_{n}\longrightarrow u
in
W^{\alpha+\varepsilon,p}(0,T)
as
n\to\infty
.
Applying Lemma 2.4, we see that

J^{m-\alpha}u_{n}\longrightarrow J^{m-\alpha}u
in
W^{m,p}(0,T)
, that is,

\frac{d^{m}}{dt^{m}}J^{m-\alpha}u_{n}\,\longrightarrow\,\frac{d^{m}}{dt^{m}}J^{m-\alpha}u

in
L^{p}(0,T)
as
n\to\infty
and
\frac{d^{m}}{dt^{m}}J^{m-\alpha}u\in L^{p}(0,T)
. Therefore, Lemma 2.2 yields

J^{\alpha}\left(\frac{d^{m}}{dt^{m}}J^{m-\alpha}u_{n}\right)\,\longrightarrow\,J^{\alpha}\left(\frac{d^{m}}{dt^{m}}J^{m-\alpha}u\right)\quad\mbox{in $W_{\alpha,p}(0,T)$}

as
n\to\infty
, that is, by (2.6) with
u_{n}\in\,_{0}C^{m}[0,T]
,
we obtain

u_{n}\,\longrightarrow\,J^{\alpha}\left(\frac{d^{m}}{dt^{m}}J^{m-\alpha}u\right)\quad\mbox{in $W_{\alpha,p}(0,T)$}

as
n\to\infty
.
Consequently, since
u_{n}\longrightarrow u
in
W^{\alpha+\varepsilon,p}(0,T)

as
n\to\infty
, we verify (2.6) for all

u\in\,_{0}W^{\alpha+\varepsilon,p}(0,T)
.
Thus the proof of Theorem 2.1 (ii) is complete.

\blacksquare

Proof of Theorem 2.1 (iii).

Since the embedding
W^{\alpha+\varepsilon,p}(0,T)\,\longrightarrow\,L^{p}(0,T)
is compact
(e.g.,
[
1
]
,
[
8
]
), part (i) implies

[formula]

J^{\alpha}:L^{p}(0,T)\,\longrightarrow\,L^{p}(0,T)
is a compact operator.

This compactness is known (e.g.,
[
7
]
) but it follows from (i).

Let
u_{n}\in W_{\alpha+\varepsilon,p}(0,T)
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
satisfy

[formula]

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|u_{n}\|_{W_{\alpha+\varepsilon,p}(0,T)}<\infty.

Then, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}

Proof of Corollary 2.1.

(i) We assume that

[formula]

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|u_{n}\|_{W_{\beta,p}(0,T)}<\infty.

By the definition of
W_{\beta,p}(0,T)
. we can find
w_{n}\in L^{p}(0,T)

such that
u_{n}=J^{\beta}w_{n}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Moreover
\|w_{n}\|_{L^{p}(0,T)}=\|u_{n}\|_{W_{\beta,p}(0,T)}

for
n\in\mathbb{N}
, so that (2.20) implies

[formula]

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|w_{n}\|_{L^{p}(0,T)}<\infty.

Since
J^{\alpha}u_{n}=J^{\alpha}J^{\beta}w_{n}
, by the definition of

\|\cdot\|_{W_{\beta,p}(0,T)}
, we deduce

\|J^{\alpha}u_{n}\|_{W_{\beta,p}(0,T)}=\|J^{\beta}(J^{\alpha}w_{n})\|_{W_{\beta,p}(0,T)}=\|J^{\alpha}w_{n}\|_{L^{p}(0,T)}.

In terms of (2.17) and (2.21), there exists a sequence
\{n(k)\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{N}
such that

J^{\alpha}w_{n(k)}
is convergent in
L^{p}(0,T)
, that is,

J^{\alpha}u_{n(k)}
is convergent in
W_{\beta,p}(0,T)
.
With (2.21), we deduce that
J^{\alpha}:W_{\beta,p}(0,T)\,\longrightarrow\,W_{\beta,p}(0,T)
is a compact operator.

\blacksquare

(ii) If
u\in W_{\beta,p}(0,T)
, then
u=J^{\beta}w
with

w\in L^{p}(0,T)
. By Lemma 2.1 (i), we see that
J^{\alpha}u=J^{\alpha}J^{\beta}w=J^{\alpha+\beta}w\in W_{\alpha+\beta,p}(0,T)
.
Therefore,
J^{\alpha}W_{\beta,p}(0,T)\subset W_{\alpha+\beta,p}(0,T)
.

Conversely, let
u\in W_{\alpha+\beta,p}(0,T)
. Then
there exists
w\in L^{p}(0,T)
such that
u=J^{\alpha+\beta}w=J^{\alpha}(J^{\beta}w)
. By
J^{\beta}w\in W_{\beta,p}(0,T)
, we
see that
u\in J^{\alpha}W_{\beta,p}(0,T)
, that is,

W_{\alpha+\beta,p}(0,T)\subset J^{\alpha}W_{\beta,p}(0,T)
.

Finally we will prove the norm equivalence. Let
u\in W_{\beta,p}(0,T)

be arbitrarily given.
Then
u=J^{\beta}w
with some
w\in L^{p}(0,T)
and

\|u\|_{W_{\beta,p}(0,T)}=\|w\|_{L^{p}(0,T)}
.
Moreover the definition of
\|\cdot\|_{W_{\alpha+\beta,p}(0,T)}

implies

\|J^{\alpha}u\|_{W_{\alpha+\beta,p}(0,T)}=\|J^{\alpha}J^{\beta}w\|_{W_{\alpha+\beta,p}(0,T)}=\|J^{\alpha+\beta}w\|_{W_{\alpha+\beta,p}(0,T)}=\|w\|_{L^{p}(0,T)}.

and so
\|J^{\alpha}u\|_{W_{\alpha+\beta,p}(0,T)}=\|u\|_{W_{\beta,p}(0,T)}
.
Thus the proof of Corollary 2.1 is complete.

\blacksquare

We close this section with the following proposition which is used
in Section 4 and is proved easily.

Proposition 2.1.

(i)
W_{\alpha,p}(0,T)\subset W_{\beta,p}(0,T)
if
\alpha>\beta>0
and
there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\|u\|_{W_{\beta,p}(0,T)}\leq C\|u\|_{W_{\alpha,p}(0,T)}

for each
u\in W_{\alpha,p}(0,T)
.

(ii)
J^{\alpha}\partial_{t}^{\beta}u=J^{\alpha-\beta}u
for
\alpha>\beta>0

and each
u\in W_{\beta,p}(0,T)
.

Proof of Proposition 2.1.

(i) Let
u\in W_{\alpha,p}(0,T)
. Then
u=J^{\alpha}w
with some

w\in L^{p}(0,T)
. Since
J^{\alpha}w=J^{\beta}J^{\alpha-\beta}w

by Lemma 2.1 (i), we have
u=J^{\alpha}w=J^{\beta}(J^{\alpha-\beta}w)\in J^{\beta}L^{p}(0,T)
.
Moreover, by
u=J^{\alpha}w=J^{\beta}(J^{\alpha-\beta}w)
,
the definition of
the norm implies that

\|u\|_{W_{\alpha,p}(0,T)}=\|w\|_{L^{p}(0,T)}
and

\|u\|_{W_{\beta,p}(0,T)}=\|J^{\alpha-\beta}w\|_{L^{p}(0,T)}\leq C\|u\|_{L^{p}(0,T)}.

Therefore,
\|u\|_{W_{\beta,p}(0,T)}\leq C\|u\|_{W_{\alpha,p}(0,T)}
. Thus part (i) is proved.

\blacksquare

(ii) Let
u\in W_{\beta,p}(0,T)
. Then
u=J^{\beta}w
with some

w\in L^{p}(0,T)
and

J^{\alpha}\partial_{t}^{\beta}=J^{\alpha}(J^{\beta})^{-1}J^{\beta}w=J^{\alpha}w.

On the other hand, by
\alpha-\beta,\beta>0
, Lemma 2.1 (i) implies

J^{\alpha-\beta}u=J^{\alpha-\beta}J^{\beta}w=J^{(\alpha-\beta)+\beta}w=J^{\alpha}w.

Hence
J^{\alpha}\partial_{t}^{\beta}=J^{\alpha-\beta}u
for
u\in W_{\beta,p}(0,T)
.
Thus the proof of Proposition 2.1 is complete.

\blacksquare

3.
Fractional derivative
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}
in
L^{p}(0,T)

We consider an initial value problem (1.2) and (1.3):

d_{t}^{\alpha}u(t)=b(t)u(t)+f(t),\quad 0<t<T,\qquad u(0)=a,

where
f
is singular in the sense that
f\not\in L^{r}(0,T)
for any
r\geq 1
.
For example, let
f(t)
be the Dirac delta function which means an
impulsive source at
t=0
. It is desirable to construct a framework
in order to treat such singular terms in fractional differential equations.
For the mathematical treatments, we need the
formulation of time-fractional derivative
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}
in Sobolev spaces of
non-positive orders.
In Yamamoto
[
23
]
, we find such studies in the space
L^{2}(0,T)
and
the treatment with fixed
p=2
is less flexible.

In this section, we define
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}
in
L^{p}(0,T)
-based Sobolev-Slobodecki
spaces of negative orders. Our extension of the domain of
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}

from
W_{\alpha,p}(0,T)
, relies on
the adjoint of fractional differential operator.

We set

[formula]

J_{\alpha}v(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int^{T}_{t}(\xi-t)^{\alpha-1}v(\xi)d\xi,\quad 0<t<T,\,v\in L^{1}(0,T).

Then, similarly to Lemma 2.1, we can prove

Lemma 3.1.

Let
\alpha,\beta>0
.

(i)
J_{\alpha}J_{\beta}v=J_{\alpha+\beta}v
for

v\in L^{1}(0,T)
.

(ii)
J_{\alpha}:L^{p}(0,T)\,\longrightarrow\,L^{p}(0,T)
is an injective compact operator.

We can prove directly but we can transform the corresponding results for

J^{\alpha}
through the following transformation:

[formula]

\tau:\,L^{p}(0,T)\,\longrightarrow\,L^{p}(0,T),\qquad(\tau v)(t)=v(T-t),\quad 0<t<T.

For example, as is readily verified,

[formula]

J_{\alpha}v(t)=(\tau J^{\alpha}(\tau v))(t),\quad 0<t<T,\,v\in L^{p}(0,T).

In view of (3.3), Lemma 3.1 follows directly from Lemma 2.1.

In the same way as in Section 2, for
1\leq p<\infty
and
\alpha>0
, by
Lemma 3.1, we can define

[formula]

\,{}_{\alpha,p}W(0,T):=J_{\alpha}L^{p}(0,T),\quad\|u\|_{\,{}_{\alpha,p}W(0,T)}:=\|(J_{\alpha})^{-1}u\|_{L^{p}(0,T)}\quad\mbox{for $u\in\,_{\alpha,p}W(0,T)$}.

Moreover, we can similarly prove

Lemma 3.2.

Let
\alpha>0
. If

w_{n}\longrightarrow w
in
L^{p}(0,T)
, then
J_{\alpha}w_{n}\longrightarrow J_{\alpha}w
in

\,{}_{\alpha,p}W(0,T)
.

Lemma 3.3.

The space
\,{}_{\alpha,p}W(0,T)
is a Banach space with the norm

\|\cdot\|_{\,{}_{\alpha,p}W(0,T)}
.

For
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we set

{}^{0}C^{m}[0,T]:=\left\{v\in C^{m}[0,T];\,\frac{d^{k}v}{dt^{k}}(T)=0\quad\mbox{for $k=0,1,...,m-1$}\right\}

and

\,{}^{0}W^{\alpha,p}(0,T):=\overline{\,{}^{0}C^{m}[0,T]}^{W^{\alpha,p}(0,T)}.

We remark that
\,{}^{0}C^{m}[0,T]=\tau\,_{0}C^{m}[0,T]
and

\tau:\,W_{\alpha,p}(0,T)\,\longrightarrow\,\,_{\alpha,p}W(0,T)
is an isomorphism.

Then, by means of Theorem 2.1 and the symmetric transform
\tau

in
t
, we can readily prove

Theorem 3.1.

Let
\alpha>0
,
\not\in\mathbb{N}
and
0<\varepsilon<\alpha
.

(i)
\,{}^{0}W^{\alpha+\varepsilon,p}(0,T)\,\subset\,\,_{\alpha,p}W(0,T)\,\subset\,^{0}W^{\alpha-\varepsilon,p}(0,T)
.
Moreover there exists a constant
C=C(\alpha,\varepsilon)>0
such that

\|J_{\alpha}v\|_{W^{\alpha-\varepsilon,p}(0,T)}\leq C\|v\|_{L^{p}(0,T)}\quad\mbox{for all $v\in L^{p}(0,T)$.}

(ii) The embedding
\,{}_{\alpha+\varepsilon,p}W(0,T)\,\longrightarrow\,\,_{\alpha,p}W(0,T)
is
compact.

(iii) For
\alpha>0
and
\beta\geq 0
, the operator

J_{\alpha}:\,_{\beta,p}W(0,T)\,\longrightarrow\,_{\beta,p}W(0,T)
is
compact.

We will use
J_{\alpha}
in order to define
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}
in Sobolev spaces of
negative orders through he adjoint. As for notations and terminologies,
we follow Brezis
[
2
]
.

Let
\,{}_{\alpha,p}W(0,T)^{*}
denote the dual space of
\,{}_{\alpha,p}W(0,T)
, that is, the space of all the
bounded linear real-valued functionals defined over
\,{}_{\alpha,p}W(0,T)
.

In place of
u(\psi)
, by
\,{}_{\,{}_{\alpha,p}W(0,T)^{*}}<u,\,\psi>_{\,{}_{\alpha,p}W(0,T)}
, we denote
the value of
u
at

\psi\in\,_{\alpha,p}W(0,T)
. As the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\,{}_{\alpha,p}W(0,T)^{*}}
we follow a usual
definition:

\|u\|_{\,{}_{\alpha,p}W(0,T)^{*}}\,:=\,\sup_{\|\psi\|_{\,{}_{\alpha,p}W(0,T)}\,=1}|\,_{\,{}_{\alpha,p}W(0,T)^{*}}<u,\,\psi>_{\,{}_{\alpha,p}W(0,T)}|.

Henceforth, throughout this article, for
1\leq p<\infty
,
we define
1<q\leq\infty
by

q=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\frac{p}{p-1}&\mbox{if $p>1$},\\
\infty,&\mbox{if $p=1$},\end{array}\right.

and we set

{}_{L^{q}(0,T)}(v,\,u)_{L^{p}(0,T)}:=\int^{T}_{0}u(t)v(t)dt,\quad v\in L^{q}(0,T),\,u\in L^{p}(0,T)\quad\mbox{with $1\leq p<\infty$}.

We note that
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
and

L^{p}(0,T)^{*}=L^{q}(0,T)\quad\mbox{if $1\leq p<\infty$}

(e.g.,
[
2
]
).

Example of
\,{}_{\alpha,p}W(0,T)^{*}
.

Let
\alpha p>1
. Choosing
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small such
that
(\alpha-\varepsilon)p>1
, by the Sobolev embedding
(e.g.,
[
1
]
,
[
8
]
) by Theorem 3.1 (i) we verify that

\,{}_{\alpha,p}W(0,T)\subset C[0,T]
.
Therefore, for
t_{0}\in[0,T]
, we define
\delta_{t_{0}}\in\,_{\alpha,p}W(0,T)^{*}
by

\,{}_{\,{}_{\alpha,p}W(0,T)^{*}}<\delta_{t_{0}},\,\psi>_{\,{}_{\alpha,p}W(0,T)}\,:=\psi(t_{0})

and we can see
\delta_{t_{0}}\in\,_{\alpha,p}W(0,T)^{*}
if
\alpha p>1
.

We here sum up useful results.

Lemma 3.4.

(i)
C^{-1}\|J_{\alpha}u\|_{L^{p}(0,T)}\leq\|u\|_{L^{p}(0,T)}\leq C\|J_{\alpha}u\|_{\,{}_{\alpha,p}W(0,T)}
for each
u\in L^{p}(0,T)
.

(ii) For arbitrarily chosen
v\in L^{q}(0,T)
, we define a mapping:

F_{v}:\,u\mapsto\,_{L^{q}(0,T)}(v,\,u)_{L^{p}(0,T)}\quad\mbox{for each
$u\in\,_{\alpha,p}W(0,T)$}.

Then,
F_{v}
is a bounded linear functional on
\,{}_{\alpha,p}W(0,T)
.
Identifying
F_{v}\in\,_{\alpha,p}W(0,T)^{*}
with
v
, in place of

we write

{}_{\,{}_{\alpha,p}W(0,T)^{*}}<v,\,u>_{\,{}_{\alpha,p}W(0,T)}\,\,=\,_{L^{q}(0,T)}(v,\,u)_{L^{p}(0,T)},

so that

\|F_{v}\|_{\,{}_{\alpha,p}W(0,T)^{*}}=\|v\|_{\,{}_{\alpha,p}W(0,T)^{*}}\leq C\|v\|_{L^{q}(0,T)}.

(iii)

J_{\alpha}^{*}:\,_{\alpha+\beta,p}W(0,T)^{*}\,\longrightarrow\,_{\beta,p}W(0,T)^{*}

is an isomorphism. In particular,

\|J_{\alpha}^{*}u\|_{{}_{\beta,p}W(0,T)^{*}}=\|u\|_{{}_{\alpha+\beta}W(0,T)^{*}}\quad\mbox{for all $u\in\,_{\alpha+\beta}W(0,T)^{*}$.}

Proof of Lemma 3.4.

(i) By the defintion of the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\,{}_{\alpha,p}W(0,T)}
,
we have
\|J_{\alpha}u\|_{\,{}_{\alpha,p}W(0,T)}=\|u\|_{L^{p}(0,T)}

for any
u\in L^{p}(0,T)
.
The Young inequality implies
C\|J_{\alpha}u\|_{L^{p}(0,T)}\leq\|u\|_{L^{p}(0,T)}
. Thus the proof of the lemma (i) is
complete.

\blacksquare

(ii) Immediately we see that
F_{v}
is well-defined for

u\in\,_{\alpha,p}W(0,T)
and is a lineat mapping.
Setting
u:=J_{\alpha}w
with
w\in L^{p}(0,T)
, we have

\|u\|_{\,{}_{\alpha,p}W(0,T)}=\|J_{\alpha}w\|_{\,{}_{\alpha,p}W(0,T)}=\|w\|_{L^{p}(0,T)}
, by Lemma 3.4 (i) we obtain

\displaystyle\|v\|_{\,{}_{\alpha,p}W(0,T)^{*}}=\,\sup_{\|u\|_{\,{}_{\alpha,p}W(0,T)}\,=1}|\,_{L^{q}(0,T)}(v,\,u)_{L^{p}(0,T)}|=\,\sup_{\|w\|_{L^{p}(0,T)}\,=1}|\,_{L^{q}(0,T)}(v,\,J_{\alpha}w)_{L^{p}(0,T)}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|v\|_{L^{q}(0,T)}\sup_{\|w\|_{L^{p}(0,T)}\,=1}\|J_{\alpha}w\|_{L^{p}(0,T)}\leq C\|v\|_{L^{q}(0,T)}.

Hence,
\|v\|_{\,{}_{\alpha,p}W(0,T)^{*}}\leq C\|v\|_{L^{q}(0,T)}
.
Thus the proof of (ii) is complete.

\blacksquare

(iii) Thanks to the operator
\tau
defined by (3.2),
Corollary 2.1 (ii) yields that

J_{\alpha}:\,_{\beta,p}W(0,T)\,\longrightarrow\,_{\alpha+\beta,p}W(0,T)

is an isomorphism for

and
\beta\geq 0
.
Consequently, part (iii) follows directly by the closed range theorem
(e.g., Section 7 in Chapter 2 of
[
2
]
).
Thus the proof of Lemma 3.4 is complete.

\blacksquare

For arbitrarily chosen
v\in L^{q}(0,T)
and
u\in L^{p}(0,T)
, we apply
Lemma 3.4 (ii) by setting
u:=J_{\alpha}u
, we see that

[formula]

{}_{\,{}_{\alpha,p}W(0,T)^{*}}<v,\,J_{\alpha}u>_{\,{}_{\alpha,p}W(0,T)}\,:=\,_{L^{q}(0,T)}(v,\,J_{\alpha}u)_{L^{p}(0,T)}

for all
u\in L^{p}(0,T)
and
v\in L^{q}(0,T)
.

On the other hand, exchanging the order of the integral

\int^{t}_{0}\left(\int^{s}_{0}\cdots d\xi\right)ds=\int^{t}_{0}\left(\int^{t}_{\xi}\cdots ds\right)d\xi
, we can readily verify

\,{}_{L^{q}(0,T)}(J^{\alpha}v,\,u)_{L^{p}(0,T)}\,=\,_{L^{q}(0,T)}(v,\,J_{\alpha}u)_{L^{p}(0,T)}\quad\mbox{for all
$u\in L^{p}(0,T)$ and $v\in L^{q}(0,T)$}.

In terms of (3.5), we can reach

[formula]

{}_{\,{}_{\alpha,p}W(0,T)^{*}}<v,\,J_{\alpha}u>_{\,{}_{\alpha,p}W(0,T)}\,\,=\,_{L^{q}(0,T)}(J^{\alpha}v,\,u)_{L^{p}(0,T)}

for all
u\in L^{p}(0,T)
and
v\in L^{q}(0,T)
.
We note that since
J_{\alpha}\,:L^{p}(0,T)\,\longrightarrow\,\,_{\alpha,p}W(0,T)
is
bounded, the adjoint operator

exists and

J_{\alpha}^{*}
is the operator with the maximal domain among

satisfying

[formula]

{}_{\,{}_{\alpha,p}W(0,T)^{*}}<v,\,J_{\alpha}u>_{\,{}_{\alpha,p}W(0,T)}\,=\,_{L^{q}(0,T)}(Jv,\,u)_{L^{p}(0,T)}

for all

and
u\in L^{p}(0,T)
.
By taking

with

, from (3.6) it follows that
(3.7) holds. Therefore, the maximality of

yields

[formula]

J^{\alpha}\subset J_{\alpha}^{*},\quad\mathcal{D}(J_{\alpha}^{*})=\,\,_{\alpha,p}W(0,T)\,\supset L^{q}(0,T).

Now we define

Definition 3.1.

We define

\partial_{t}^{\alpha}=(J_{\alpha}^{*})^{-1},\quad\mathcal{D}(\partial_{t}^{\alpha})=\,\,_{\alpha,p}W(0,T)^{*}.

In view of (3.8), we extend
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}
defined on
W_{\alpha,p}(0,T)
to the domain

\,\,{}_{\alpha,p}W(0,T)^{*}
, which means that

in Definitions 2.1 and 3.1
coincide in

:

\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u=(J^{\alpha})^{-1}u=(J_{\alpha}^{*})^{-1}u\quad\mbox{if $u\in W_{\alpha,p}(0,T)$}.

We can generalize the domain of

to
\,{}_{\beta,p}W(0,T)^{*}
with
arbitrary

and refer to Yamamoto
[

]
for the case
of

. Furthermore we can prove several fundamental formulae
in the fractional calculus such as

and

. However, we here omit the details and proceed

4.
Initial value problems for fractional ordinary differential equations

We have constructed
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}
in
L^{p}(0,T)
-based Sobolev spaces in Sections 2
and 3. Although we should widely pursue the fractional calculus
of
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}
, our main purpose is to study initial value problems for
time-fractional ordinary differential equations and initial boundary value
problems for time-fractional partial differential equations within

L^{p}
-spaces. See
[
23
]
as for some fractional calculus of
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}
for

p=2
.

In this article, we are restricted to initial value problems for
simple fractional ordinary differential equations and illustrate the
well-posedness of solution within the framework of
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}
in

W_{\alpha,p}(0,T)
or
\,{}_{\alpha,p}W(0,T)^{*}
.

We can make comprehensive studies for general classes, but we consider only
simple types of single linear fractional ordinary differential equations in
the case of
0<\alpha<1
. See Yamamoto
[
22
]
as for
similar treatments
in the case of
p=2
. One can refer to Diethelm
[
4
]
,
Jin
[
10
]
, which treat various topics concerning fractional derivatives
and differential equations from different viewpoints.

Throughout this section, we assume that

1\leq p<\infty,\quad 0<\alpha<1,\quad a\in\mathbb{R}.

Section 4.1. Single linear fractional differential equation with bounded
coefficient

We consider

[formula]

\partial_{t}^{\alpha}(u(t)-a)=b(t)u+f(t),\quad 0<t<T,

and

[formula]

u-a\in W_{\alpha,p}(0,T).

By the definition of
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}
in
W_{\alpha,p}(0,T)
, we see that the initial value problem
(4.1) - (4.2) is equivalent to

[formula]

u-a=J^{\alpha}(b(t)u)+J^{\alpha}f(t),\quad 0<t<T.

Similarly to
[
15
]
and
[
23
]
, we interpret the initial condition
by (4.2). If
\alpha p>1
, then the Sobolev embedding (e.g.,

[
8
]
) and Theorem 2.1 (i) yield that
W_{\alpha,p}(0,T)\subset\,_{0}W^{\alpha-\varepsilon,p}(0,T)\subset C[0,T]
, so that we conclude that

u\in C[0,T]
and
\lim_{t\to 0}u(t)=a
, that is, the initial condition
(4.2) can be understood in a usual sense. If
\alpha p\leq 1
, then such
an intepretation is impossible, but we can prove the unique existence
of solution to (4.1)-(4.2) for all
0<\alpha<1
and
1\leq p<\infty
.

Theorem 4.1.

Let
b\in L^{\infty}(0,T)
and
f\in L^{p}(0,T)
. Then there exists a unique
solution
u=u(t)
to (4.1) - (4.2) and we can find a constant
C>
such that

[formula]

\|u-a\|_{W_{\alpha,p}(0,T)}\leq C(\|f\|_{L^{p}(0,T)}+|a|)

for all
f\in L^{p}(0,T)
and
a\in\mathbb{R}
.

For
p\neq 2
, we can not completely characterize
W_{\alpha,p}(0,T)
,
but Theorem 2 (i) and (ii) provide the properties of the domain
W_{\alpha,p}(0,T)
of

\partial_{t}^{\alpha}
intermediated between
\,{}_{0}W^{\alpha+\varepsilon,p}(0,T)
and

\,{}_{0}W^{\alpha-\varepsilon,p}(0,T)
with any small gap
\varepsilon>0
, and
we can apply
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}
for the regularity of solutions
to fractional equations in a flexible way.

If
\alpha p<1
, then

a=\frac{a}{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}s^{-\alpha}ds\quad\mbox{for $0<t<T$ and $s^{-\alpha}\in L^{p}(0,T)$},

so that
a\in W_{\alpha,p}(0,T)
. Therefore for
\alpha p<1
,
the estimate (4.4) is rewritten as

\|u\|_{W_{\alpha,p}(0,T)}\leq C(\|f\|_{L^{p}(0,T)}+|a|).

However, if
\alpha p\geq 1
, then we can not obtain the above
estimate.

In the case
b\in L^{\infty}(0,T)
, Theorem 4.1 can be found in
the existing works (e.g., Theorem 3.2 (p.124) in
[
20
]
).
We here present Theorem 4.1 in order to illustrate our arguments
which can work for more general cases,
as are discussed in Sections 4.3 and 4.4 later.

Proof of Theorem 4.1.

It suffices to consider (4.3). Setting
v:=u-a
and defining an operator

K:L^{p}(0,T)\,\longrightarrow\,L^{p}(0,T)
by

Kv(t):=J^{\alpha}(b(t)v)
, we see that (4.3) is
equivalent to

[formula]

v(t)=Kv(t)+J^{\alpha}(b(t)a+f(t)),\quad 0<t<T.

The theorem will be proved if we can show that
the operator
K
possesses a unique fixed point.

By
b\in L^{\infty}(0,T)
, we deduce that
v\in L^{p}(0,T)\longrightarrow bv\in L^{p}(0,T)
is
a bounded operator. By (2.17), it follows that
v\in L^{p}(0,T)\,\longrightarrow\,J^{\alpha}(bv)\in L^{p}(0,T)
is a compact operator.
Therefore,
K:L^{p}(0,T)\,\longrightarrow\,L^{p}(0,T)
is compact.

Assume that
a=0
and
f=0
in (4.5), that is,
v\in L^{p}(0,T)
satisfies

v=Kv
in
(0,T)
. Then

v(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}v(s)ds,\quad 0<t<T.

Hence,

|v(t)|\leq C\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}|v(s)|ds,\quad 0<t<T.

By the generalized Gronwall inequality (e.g., Lemma 7.1.1 (p.188) in
Henry
[
9
]
or Lemma A.2 in
[
15
]
), we obtain that
v=0
in

(0,T)
. Consequently the Fredholm alternative implies that there exists
a unique fixed point to (4.5) and

\|v\|_{L^{p}(0,T)}\leq C(\|J^{\alpha}(ba+f)\|_{L^{p}(0,T)}\leq C(|a|+\|f\|_{L^{p}(0,T)})

by
b\in L^{\infty}(0,T)
. Hence, since

v=J^{\alpha}(bv+ba+f)
by (4.5), using the norm of
\|\cdot\|_{W_{\alpha,p}(0,T)}
and
b\in L^{\infty}(0,T)
, we obtain

\|v\|_{W_{\alpha,p}(0,T)}=\|bv+ba+f\|_{L^{p}(0,T)}\leq C(|a|+\|f\|_{L^{p}(0,T)}).

Thus the proof of Theorem 4.1 is complete.

\blacksquare

Section 4.2. Single linear multi-term fractional differential equation
with bounded coefficient

Let

0<\alpha_{1}<\cdots<\alpha_{N}<\alpha<1.

We consider an initial value problem

[formula]

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&\partial_{t}^{\alpha}(u-a)+\sum_{k=1}^{N}b_{k}(t)\partial_{t}^{\alpha_{k}}(u-a)=b(t)u(t)+f(t),\quad 0<t<T,\\
&u-a\in W_{\alpha,p}(0,T).\end{array}\right.

By Proposition 2.1 (i), we note that
W_{\alpha,p}(0,T)\subset W_{\alpha_{k},p}(0,T)
, and so

\partial_{t}^{\alpha_{k}}(u-a)\in L^{p}(0,T)
with
k=1,...,N
, are well-defined if

u-a\in W_{\alpha,p}(0,T)
. Now we can prove

Theorem 4.2.

Let
b_{1},...,b_{N},b\in L^{\infty}(0,T)
and
f\in L^{p}(0,T)
. Then there exists a unique solution
u=u(t)
to (4.6) and we can find a constant
C>
such that the estimate (4.4) holds for all
f\in L^{p}(0,T)
and
a\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof of Theorem 4.2.

Setting
w:=\partial_{t}^{\alpha}(u-a)=(J^{\alpha})^{-1}(u-a)
, by Lemma 2.1 (i) we have

u-a=J^{\alpha}w
and

\partial_{t}^{\alpha_{k}}(u-a)=\partial_{t}^{\alpha_{k}}J^{\alpha}w=\partial_{t}^{\alpha_{k}}(J^{\alpha_{k}}J^{\alpha-\alpha_{k}})w=J^{\alpha-\alpha_{k}}w.

Then (4.6) is equivalent to

w(t)=-\sum_{k=1}^{N}b_{k}(t)J^{\alpha-\alpha_{k}}w+b(t)J^{\alpha}w+b(t)a+f(t),\quad 0<t<T.

By (2.17) and
b,b_{1},....,b_{N}\in L^{\infty}(0,T)
, we can verify that
the operator

Kw(t):=-\sum_{k=1}^{N}b_{k}(t)J^{\alpha-\alpha_{k}}w+b(t)J^{\alpha}w

is compact from
L^{p}(0,T)
to itself. Similarly to Theorem 4.1, we can verify
the unique existence of the fixed point to
w=Kw+(ba+f)
.
Thus we can complete the proof of Theorem 4.2.

\blacksquare

Section 4.3. Single linear fractional differential equation with
unbounded coefficient

We return to a simple equation:

[formula]

\partial_{t}^{\alpha}(u-a)=b(t)u,\quad 0<t<T,\qquad u-a\in W_{\alpha,p}(0,T).

Here we study the unique existence and the regularity of the solution to
(4.7) for
b\in L^{p}(0,T)
with
1\leq p<\infty
within our framework.

As is discussed in Section 4.1, the regularity
b\in L^{\infty}(0,T)

makes the total arguments very simple, and for
b\not\in L^{\infty}(0,T)
,
we need more careful discussions.

We remark that the case
\alpha=1
does not require us any
special consideration because a formula of solution

u(t)=a\exp\left(\int^{t}_{0}b(s)ds\right),\quad 0<t<T

implies the unqiue existence of the solution in

W^{1,\infty}(0,T)
for arbitrary
b\in L^{1}(0,T)
.
However, for
0<\alpha<1
, we do not have such an explicit formula of
solution to (4.7) and we need proper arguments even though the equation
in (4.7) is extremely simple.
In order to concentrate on the regularity issue of the coefficient

b(t)
, we consider (4.7) without the right-hand side of the
equation.

In terms of
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}
with
\mathcal{D}(\partial_{t}^{\alpha})=W_{\alpha,p}(0,T)
, we prove

Theorem 4.3.

Let
0<\alpha<1
. We assume that

[formula]

b\in L^{q}(0,T)\quad\mbox{with $1<q<\infty$}.

If

[formula]

1<p<\infty,\quad\frac{1}{\alpha}<q<\infty,\quad p-1>\frac{1}{q-1},

then there exists a unique solution to (4.7) such that

[formula]

u-a\in W_{\alpha,\,\frac{pq}{p+q}}(0,T),\quad u\in L^{p}(0,T).

Moreover there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\|u-a\|_{W_{\alpha,\,\frac{pq}{p+q}}(0,T)}+\|u\|_{L^{p}(0,T)}\leq C|a|

for all
a\in\mathbb{R}
.

As is seen by the proof below, if
1<q\leq\frac{1}{\alpha}
, then our
proof does not work, and we do not know the unique existence of
u
.

Proof of Theorem 4.3.

Setting
v:=u-a
, we see that (4.7) is equivalent to

[formula]

v=Kv(t)+aJ^{\alpha}b(t),\quad 0<t<T,

where we set
Kv(t):=J^{\alpha}(b(t)v(t))
,
0<t<T
for

v\in L^{p}(0,T)
.
Theorem 2.1 yields

[formula]

J^{\alpha}b\in W_{\alpha,q}(0,T)\subset W^{\alpha-\varepsilon,q}(0,T).

Here
\varepsilon>0
is a sufficiently small constant.

We note that
p-1>\frac{1}{q-1}
in (4.9) implies
p>1
.
We set

r=\frac{pq}{p+q}.

Then by the same condition in (4.9), we deduce

[formula]

1<r\leq p,q,\quad\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}.

Therefore, for
b\in L^{q}(0,T)
, the Hölder inequality implies

bv\in L^{r}(0,T)
for
v\in L^{p}(0,T)
, Theorem 2.1 (i) implies

[formula]

J^{\alpha}(bv)\in W^{\alpha-\varepsilon,r}(0,T)\quad\mbox{if $v\in L^{p}(0,T)$}.

We choose a small constant
\varepsilon>0
such that
0<\varepsilon<\frac{\alpha}{2}
.
Since
q\geq r
in (4.13) yields
W^{\alpha-\varepsilon,q}(0,T)\subset W^{\alpha-\varepsilon,r}(0,T)
, so that by (4.12) we have

J^{\alpha}b\in W^{\alpha-\varepsilon,r}(0,T)
.

Therefore, by (4.14), we see that if

[formula]

W^{\alpha-2\varepsilon,r}(0,T)\subset L^{p}(0,T),

then

Kv+aJ^{\alpha}b\in L^{p}(0,T)\quad\mbox{for each $v\in L^{p}(0,T)$}.

Moreover, Theorem 2.1 (i) and (4.14) yield that

v\in L^{p}(0,T)\,\longrightarrow\,J^{\alpha}(bv)\in W^{\alpha-\varepsilon,r}(0,T)
is
a bounded operator. Since the embedding
W^{\alpha-\varepsilon,r}(0,T)\,\longrightarrow\,W^{\alpha-2\varepsilon,r}(0,T)
is compact, it follows that
under (4.15), the operator
v\in L^{p}(0,T)\,\longrightarrow\,Kv\in L^{p}(0,T)
is compact.

Thus in view of the Fredholm alternative, similarly to the proof of
Theorem 4.1, it is sufficient to verify that (4.9) implies (4.15).
We consider the following three cases separately.

Case 1:
\alpha r<1
:

Under condition
q>\frac{1}{\alpha}
, we see that
\alpha r<1
is
equivalent to

[formula]

p<\frac{q}{\alpha q-1}.

By
(\alpha-2\varepsilon)r<1
, the Sobolev embedding implies

W^{\alpha-2\varepsilon,r}(0,T)\,\subset L^{\frac{r}{1-(\alpha-2\varepsilon)r}}(0,T).

We can directly verify that
q>\frac{1}{\alpha}
yields

\frac{r}{1-\alpha r}>p
. Hence, with sufficiently small

\varepsilon>0
, we have

\frac{r}{1-(\alpha-2\varepsilon)r}>p
, that is,

L^{p}(0,T)\,\supset\,L^{\frac{r}{1-(\alpha-2\varepsilon)r}}(0,T).

Thus (4.15) holds if (4.9) and (4.16) are satisfied.

Case 2:
\alpha r=1
.

We note that
\alpha r=1
is equivalent to

p=\frac{q}{\alpha q-1}
.
For sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
, we have
(\alpha-2\varepsilon)r<1
and

W^{\alpha-2\varepsilon,r}(0,T)\,\supset\,L^{\frac{r}{1-(\alpha-2\varepsilon)r}}(0,T)

by the Sobolev embedding. Therefore, in the same was as Case 1, we can
verify (4.15) under (4.9) and

[formula]

p=\frac{q}{\alpha q-1}.

Case 3:
\alpha r>1
.

We note that
\alpha r>1
is equivalent to

p>\frac{q}{\alpha q-1}
.
Choosing
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small, we obtain
(\alpha-2\varepsilon)r>1
.
Therefore, the Sobolev embedding yields that
W^{\alpha-2\varepsilon,r}(0,T)\subset L^{\infty}(0,T)
. Hence, (4.15) holds if (4.9) and

[formula]

p>\frac{q}{\alpha q-1}

are satisfied.

Thus, taking the union of the sets of
(p,q)
satisfying (4.16) - (4.18),
we verify that (4.15) holds if (4.9) is satisfied.
Thus we complete the proof of Theorem 4.3.

\blacksquare

Section 4.4. Single linear fractional differential equation with
singular non-homogeneous term

Let
0<\alpha<1
,
\beta\geq 0
and
1<p\leq\infty
. Henceforth we define

1\leq q<\infty
by

p=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&\frac{q}{q-1}\quad\mbox{if $1<q<\infty$},\\
&\infty\quad\mbox{if $q=1$}.\end{array}\right.

We consider

[formula]

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&\partial_{t}^{\alpha}(u-a)=b(t)u(t)+f(t),\quad 0<t<T,\\
&u-a\in L^{p}(0,T),\end{array}\right.

where
b\in L^{\infty}(0,T)
and

f\in\,_{\alpha,q}W(0,T)^{*}
.

In (4.19), we understand as

\partial_{t}^{\alpha}=(J_{\alpha}^{*})^{-1}:\,L^{q}(0,T)^{*}\,\longrightarrow\,\,_{\alpha,q}W(0,T)^{*},

that is,

[formula]

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&\partial_{t}^{\alpha}:\,L^{p}(0,T)\,\longrightarrow\,\,_{\alpha,q}W(0,T)^{*}\quad\mbox{if $1<p<\infty$},\\
&\partial_{t}^{\alpha}:\,L^{\infty}(0,T)\,\longrightarrow\,\,_{\alpha,1}W(0,T)^{*}\quad\mbox{if $p=\infty$}.\end{array}\right.

Moreover, by Lemma 3.4 (iii), we see that

J_{\alpha}^{*}:\,_{\alpha+\beta,q}W(0,T)^{*}\,\longrightarrow\,_{\beta,q}W(0,T)^{*}

is an isomorphism.

In terms of (4.20), by setting
v:=u-a
, the initial value problem
(4.19) is equivalent to

[formula]

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&v=J_{\alpha}^{*}(bv)+J_{\alpha}^{*}(ba+f),\\
&v\in L^{p}(0,T).\end{array}\right.

For simplicity, we assume that
1<p<\infty
.
Now we can prove

Theorem 4.4.

Let
b\in L^{\infty}(0,T)
be arbitrary. Then there exists a unique
solution
u
to (4.19) and we can find a constant
C>0
such that

\|u-a\|_{L^{p}(0,T)}\leq C(|a|+\|f\|_{{}_{\alpha,q}W(0,T)^{*}})

for all
a\in\mathbb{R}
and
f\in\,_{\alpha,q}W(0,T)^{*}
.

Example.

Given constant
t_{0}\in[0,T]
, we consider a Dirac delta function

f(t):=\delta_{t_{0}}
, that is,

\,{}_{C[0,T]^{*}}<\delta_{t_{0}},\,\psi>_{C[0,T]}:=\psi(t_{0})
for all
\psi\in C[0,T]
.
Let
\alpha q>1
with
q=\frac{p}{p-1}
. Then the Sobolev
embedding yields that
\,{}_{\alpha,q}W(0,T)\subset C[0,T]
, and so
it turns that

\delta_{t_{0}}\in\,W_{\alpha,q}(0,T)^{*}
.
Therefore Theorem 4.4 asserts that there exists a unique solution

u\in L^{p}(0,T)
to
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}(u-a)=b(t)u(t)+\delta_{t_{0}}(t)

in
\,{}_{\alpha,q}W(0,T)^{*}
.

Proof of Theorem 4.4.

By Lemma 3.4 (iii), noting that

\,{}_{0,q}W(0,T)^{*}=L^{q}(0,T)^{*}=L^{p}(0,T)
, we obtain

\|J_{\alpha}^{*}f\|_{L^{p}(0,T)}\leq C\|f\|_{{}_{\alpha,q}W(0,T)^{*}}.

By
b\in L^{\infty}(0,T)
, we apply (3.8) to have

J_{\alpha}^{*}b=J^{\alpha}b\in L^{\infty}(0,T)
by the Young
inequality. Therefore,

[formula]

J_{\alpha}^{*}(ba+f)\in L^{p}(0,T),\quad\|J_{\alpha}^{*}(ba+f)\|_{L^{p}(0,T)}\leq C(|a|+\|f\|_{{}_{\alpha,q}W(0,T)^{*}}).

Since
b\in L^{\infty}(0,T)
implies
bv\in L^{p}(0,T)
. Again by
(3.8), we deduce that
J_{\alpha}^{*}(bv)=J^{\alpha}(bv)
for all

v\in L^{p}(0,T)
. Thus (4.21) is rewritten as

v=J^{\alpha}(bv)+G,\quad v\in L^{p}(0,T),

where
G:=J^{\alpha}(ba+f)\in L^{p}(0,T)
.
Consequently our argument can be executed within
L^{p}(0,T)
, and we can
repeat the proof of Theorem 4.1.
Thus the proof of Theorem 4.4 is complete.

\blacksquare

Concluding remarks

1.
In Section 2 of this article, we have established a time-fractional
derivative
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}
in

-based Sobolev-Slobodecki spaces for

1\leq p<\infty
. Theorem 2.2 provides characterization of
the domain
\mathcal{D}(\partial_{t}^{\alpha})
in terms of the Sobolev-Slobodecki spaces,
which still admits a gap with any small
\varepsilon>0
in Sobolev orders.

We remark that the domain
\mathcal{D}(\partial_{t}^{\alpha})
in a special case

, is
completely characterized by the Sobolev-Slobodecki spaces
(see
[
6
]
,
[
15
]
,
[
23
]

2.
By the duality, in Section 3, for
1\leq p<\infty
,
we extend the domain
\mathcal{D}(\partial_{t}^{\alpha})
to
L^{p}(0,T)
with the range in the dual space

\,{}_{\alpha,p}W(0,T)^{*}
.

3.
In Section 4, we established the unique existence of solutions to
initial value problems for fractional ordinary differential equations on the
basis of
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}
. We can develop more comprehensive treatments for wider
classes of equations, but we postpone them and are restricted to single
fractional equations. Even for such simple equations, there are no works
for example in the case where a coefficient
b(t)
is not bounded or
non-homogeneous term
f(t)
does not belong to
L^{p}(0,T)
,
and we established the unique
existences of solution for
b\in L^{p}(0,T)
with
p\neq\infty
or

f\in\,_{\alpha,q}W(0,T)^{*}
with
\alpha>0
.

Furthermore we here considered
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}
only in the case of
0<\alpha<1

although in Sections 2 and 3 the fractional derivative
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}
is defined for
all
\alpha>0,\not\in\mathbb{N}
, and more general treatments will be provided in

We recall that Theorems 2.1 and 2.2 give characterization of the domain
of
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}
which is not the best possible because of regularity loss
\varepsilon>0
,
even though
\varepsilon>0
can be choosen arbitrarily small.
However, for studying fractional
differential equations, such characterization of the domain by

as is illustrated in Section 4, we emphasize that the
\varepsilon
-gap is
not a serious disadvantage.

4.
We do not discuss time-fractional partial differential equations
at all. The related topics should be studied also in future works
for

. We remark that the case

is relatively satisfactorily
argued already in e.g.,
[
6
]
,
[
15
]
.

6.
Appendix: Proof of Lemma 2.4.

For
m=1
and
p=1
, Lemma 2.4 is proved as
Theorem 4.2.3 (p.77) in
[
7
]
, and our
proof is based on the expression (6.1) (which is used also on p.77
of
[
7
]
).

First Step:
0<\alpha<1
.

We note that
{{}_{0}{C^{1}[0,T]}}\subset W^{\alpha+\varepsilon,p}(0,T)
if

0<\alpha+\varepsilon<1
.
Let
u\in{{}_{0}{C^{1}[0,T]}}
. Then

\displaystyle J^{1-\alpha}u(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{-\alpha}u(s)ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{-\alpha}(u(s)-u(t))ds+\frac{u(t)}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{-\alpha}ds,

that is,

J^{1-\alpha}u(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{-\alpha}(u(s)-u(t))ds+\frac{u(t)t^{1-\alpha}}{(1-\alpha)\Gamma(1-\alpha)},\quad 0<t<T.

Since
u\in{{}_{0}{C^{1}[0,T]}}
, we see

|(t-s)^{-\alpha}(u(s)-u(t))|\leq C|t-s|^{1-\alpha}

and

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial t}((t-s)^{-\alpha}(u(s)-u(t)))\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|-\alpha(t-s)^{-\alpha-1}(u(s)-u(t))-(t-s)^{-\alpha}\frac{du}{dt}(t)\right|\leq C|t-s|^{-\alpha}

so that
(t-s)^{-\alpha}(u(s)-u(t))
is in
W^{1,1}(0,t)
as a function
in
s\in(0,t)
for arbitrarily fixed
t\in(0,T)
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{-\alpha}(u(s)-u(t))ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\alpha\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{-\alpha-1}(u(s)-u(t))ds+\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{-\alpha}\left(-\frac{du}{dt}(t)\right)ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\alpha\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{-\alpha-1}(u(s)-u(t))ds-\frac{t^{1-\alpha}}{1-\alpha}\frac{du}{dt}(t).

Hence,

\frac{d}{dt}J^{1-\alpha}u(t)=-\alpha\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{-\alpha-1}(u(s)-u(t))ds+\frac{t^{-\alpha}}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}u(t)

[formula]

=:S_{1}(t)+S_{2}(t),\quad 0<t<T\quad\mbox{for $u\in{{}_{0}{C^{1}[0,T]}}$.}

Second Step: Estimation of
S_{1}(t)
for
0<\alpha<1
and

u\in{{}_{0}{C^{1}[0,T]}}
.

Case 1:
p=1
.

We have

\|S_{1}\|_{L^{1}(0,T)}\leq\int^{T}_{0}\left|\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{-\alpha-1}(u(s)-u(t))ds\right|dt

[formula]

\leq\int^{T}_{0}\int^{T}_{0}\frac{|u(t)-u(s)|}{|t-s|^{\alpha+1}}dsdt\leq\|u\|_{W^{\alpha,1}(0,T)}.

Case 2:
1<p<\infty
.

We have

\left|\frac{|u(t)-u(s)|}{|t-s|^{\alpha+1}}\right|=\frac{|u(t)-u(s)|}{|t-s|^{\frac{1}{p}+\alpha+\varepsilon}}\frac{1}{|t-s|^{1-\frac{1}{p}-\varepsilon}}.

Set
\frac{1}{q}:=1-\frac{1}{p}
, that is,

q=\frac{p}{p-1}>1
. Then

\int^{T}_{0}|t-s|^{q\left(\frac{1}{p}+\varepsilon-1\right)}ds=\int^{T}_{0}|t-s|^{-1+\frac{p\varepsilon}{p-1}}ds\leq\left(T^{\frac{p\varepsilon}{p-1}}+(T-t)^{\frac{p\varepsilon}{p-1}}\right)\frac{p-1}{p\varepsilon}<\infty.

Consequently, the Hölder inequality yields

\displaystyle\int^{T}_{0}\frac{|u(t)-u(s)|}{|t-s|^{\alpha+1}}ds=\int^{T}_{0}\frac{|u(t)-u(s)|}{|t-s|^{\frac{1}{p}+\alpha+\varepsilon}}\frac{1}{|t-s|^{1-\frac{1}{p}-\varepsilon}}ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int^{T}_{0}\frac{|u(t)-u(s)|^{p}}{|t-s|^{1+p(\alpha+\varepsilon)}}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\int^{T}_{0}|t-s|^{q\left(\frac{1}{p}+\varepsilon-1\right)}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq C\left(\int^{T}_{0}\frac{|u(t)-u(s)|^{p}}{|t-s|^{1+p(\alpha+\varepsilon)}}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Therefore,

\int^{T}_{0}|S_{1}(t)|^{p}dt\leq C\int^{T}_{0}\left|\int^{t}_{0}\frac{|u(t)-u(s)|}{|t-s|^{1+\alpha}}ds\right|^{p}dt

[formula]

\leq C\int^{T}_{0}\int^{T}_{0}\frac{|u(t)-u(s)|^{p}}{|t-s|^{1+p(\alpha+\varepsilon)}}dsdt\leq C\|u\|_{W^{\alpha+\varepsilon,p}(0,T)}.

By (6.2) and (6.3), for
p\geq 1
, we obtain

[formula]

\|S_{1}\|_{L^{p}(0,T)}\leq C\|u\|_{W^{\alpha+\varepsilon,p}(0,T)}.

Third Step: Estimation of
S_{2}(t)
for
0<\alpha<1
and

u\in{{}_{0}{C^{1}[0,T]}}
.

We have

\|S_{2}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(0,T)}\leq C\int^{T}_{0}t^{-p\alpha}|u(t)|^{p}dt.

Case 1:
p(\alpha+\varepsilon)<1
.

We can have the following Sobolev embedding:

[formula]

W^{\alpha+\varepsilon,p}(0,T)\subset L^{\frac{p(1-\delta_{0})}{1-p(\alpha+\varepsilon)}}(0,T),

where we fix a sufficiently small constant
\delta_{0}>0
.
The inclusion (6.5) is proved in Adams
[
1
]
or Grisvard
[
8
]

for
p>1
and as Lemma 4.2.1 (pp.72-73) in Gorenflo and Vessella
[
7
]

for
p=1
.

We set
q:=\frac{1-\delta_{0}}{1-p(\alpha+\varepsilon)}
and

r:=\frac{1-\delta_{0}}{p(\alpha+\varepsilon)-\delta_{0}}
.
By
p(\alpha+\varepsilon)<1
, choosing sufficiently
small
\delta_{0}>0
, see that
q,r>1

and
\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{r}=1
.
Hence the Hölder inequality yields

\displaystyle\int^{T}_{0}t^{-\alpha p}|u(t)|^{p}dt\leq\left(\int^{T}_{0}|u(t)|^{pq}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int^{T}_{0}t^{-\alpha pr}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int^{T}_{0}|u(t)|^{\frac{p(1-\delta_{0})}{1-p(\alpha+\varepsilon)}}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int^{T}_{0}t^{-\alpha p\frac{1-\delta_{0}}{p(\alpha+\varepsilon)-\delta_{0}}}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}.

In view of
\alpha p<p(\alpha+\varepsilon)<1
, choosing
\delta_{0}>0
smaller,
we can assume that

\delta_{0}<\frac{p\varepsilon}{1-\alpha p}
. Then direct calculations imply

\alpha p\frac{1-\delta_{0}}{p(\alpha+\varepsilon)-\delta_{0}}<1,

and so

\left(\int^{T}_{0}t^{-\alpha p\frac{1-\delta_{0}}{p(\alpha+\varepsilon)-\delta_{0}}}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}<\infty.

With (6.5), we obtain

[formula]

\|S_{2}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(0,T)}\leq C\left(\|u\|_{W^{\alpha+\varepsilon,p}(0,T)}^{\frac{p(1-\delta_{0})}{1-p(\alpha+\varepsilon)}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}=C\|u\|_{W^{\alpha+\varepsilon,p}(0,T)}^{p}.

Case 2:
p(\alpha+\varepsilon)=1
.

By
\alpha+\varepsilon<1
, we have
p>1
. Then the Sobolev embedding
(e.g.,
[
1
]
,
[
8
]
) implies

[formula]

W^{\alpha+\varepsilon,p}(0,T)\subset L^{pq}(0,T)

for any
q\geq 1
. We set
r:=\frac{q}{q-1}
with
q>1
.
Choosing
q>1
sufficiently large, since
\lim_{q\to\infty}r=1
and

\alpha p<\alpha(p+\varepsilon)=1
, we can obtain
\alpha pr<1
.
Therefore, (6.7) and the Hölder inequality imply

\displaystyle\int^{T}_{0}t^{-\alpha p}|u(t)|^{p}dt\leq\left(\int^{T}_{0}|u(t)|^{pq}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int^{T}_{0}t^{-\alpha pr}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|u\|^{p}_{L^{pq}(0,T)}\leq C\|u\|^{p}_{W^{\alpha+\varepsilon,p}(0,T)},

that is, we reach (6.6) in Case 2.

Case 3:
p(\alpha+\varepsilon)>1
.

Then we note that
p>1
. The Sobolev embedding yields

[formula]

W^{\alpha+\varepsilon,p}(0,T)\subset C^{\theta}[0,T],

where

0<\theta<\alpha+\varepsilon-\frac{1}{p}

(e.g.,
[
8
]
).
Here we set

\displaystyle C^{\theta}[0,T]:=\{v\in C[0,T];\,\mbox{there exists a constant $C=C_{v}>0$ such that}

\displaystyle|v(t)-v(s)|\leq C_{v}|t-s|^{\theta}\}

and

\|v\|_{C^{\theta}[0,T]}:=\|v\|_{C[0,T]}+\sup_{t,s\in[0,T],t\neq s}\frac{|u(t)-u(s)|}{|t-s|^{\theta}}.

By
u\in{{}_{0}{C^{1}[0,T]}}
, we have
u(0)=0
and

|u(t)|=|u(t)-u(0)|\leq\|u\|_{C^{\theta}[0,T]}t^{\theta}.

Therefore,

\|S_{2}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(0,T)}\leq C\int^{T}_{0}\frac{|u(t)|^{p}}{t^{\alpha p}}dt\leq C\int^{T}_{0}t^{p(\theta-\alpha)}\|u\|_{C^{\theta}[0,T]}^{p}dt.

Since
0<\theta<\alpha+\varepsilon-\frac{1}{p}
, we choose
0<\delta_{1}<\varepsilon
and
set
\theta:=\alpha+\varepsilon-\frac{1}{p}-\delta_{1}
.
Then
p(\theta-\alpha)=-1+p(\varepsilon-\delta_{1})>-1
, and

\int^{T}_{0}t^{p(\theta-\alpha)}dt<\infty
.
Consequently, by (6.8) we reach

\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(0,T)}\leq C\|u\|_{W^{\alpha+\varepsilon,p}(0,T)}^{p},

and (6.6) is proved for Case 3.

Thus, in view of (6.1), (6.4) and (6.6), we finished the proof of

[formula]

\|J^{1-\alpha}u\|_{W^{1,p}(0,T)}\leq C\|u\|_{W^{\alpha+\varepsilon,p}(0,T)}\quad\mbox{for
$u\in{{}_{0}{C^{1}[0,T]}}$}

with
0<\alpha<1
,
where
p\geq 1
and the constant
C>0
depends only on
T
,
p
,
\alpha
,
and
\varepsilon
.

Fourth Step: Completion of the proof for
0<\alpha<1
.

Let
u\in\,_{0}W^{\alpha+\varepsilon,p}(0,T)
be arbitrary. By the definition,
we can choose
u_{n}\in{{}_{0}{C^{1}[0,T]}}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
u_{n}\longrightarrow u
in

W^{\alpha+\varepsilon,p}(0,T)
as
n\to\infty
.
In view of (6.9), we deduce that

\|J^{1-\alpha}u_{n}-J^{1-\alpha}u_{m}\|_{W^{1,p}(0,T)}\leq C\|u_{n}-u_{m}\|_{W^{\alpha+\varepsilon,p}(0,T)}\,\longrightarrow\,0

as
n,m\to\infty
. Therefore, again by (6.9) and

J^{1-\alpha}u_{n}\in{{}_{0}{C^{1}[0,T]}}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists

\widetilde{w}\in\,_{0}W^{\alpha+\varepsilon,p}(0,T)
such that

[formula]

J^{1-\alpha}u_{n}\,\longrightarrow\,\widetilde{w}\quad\mbox{in
$W^{1,p}(0,T)$ as $n\to\infty$},

and

[formula]

\|\widetilde{w}\|_{W^{1,p}(0,T)}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\|J^{1-\alpha}u_{n}\|_{W^{1,p}(0,T)}\leq C\lim_{n\to\infty}\|u_{n}\|_{W^{\alpha+\varepsilon,p}(0,T)}.

Here we applied the part of the lemma already proved for
0<\alpha+\varepsilon<1
and

u_{n}\in{{}_{0}{C^{1}[0,T]}}
.

Since
u_{n}\longrightarrow u
in
W^{\alpha+\varepsilon,p}(0,T)
, implies
that
u_{n}\,\longrightarrow\,u
in
L^{p}(0,T)
, by the Young inequality, we obtain that

J^{1-\alpha}u_{n}\,\longrightarrow\,J^{1-\alpha}u
in
L^{p}(0,T)
as

n\to\infty
.

Combining with (6.10), we see that
J^{1-\alpha}u=\widetilde{w}
.
Therefore, in terms of (6.11), we reach the conclusion of
Lemma 2.4 for
0<\alpha<1
and
u\in\,_{0}W^{\alpha+\varepsilon,p}(0,T)
.

\blacksquare

Fifth Step: Completion of the proof of Lemma 2.4.

Let
\alpha=m-1+\sigma
with
m=2,3,4,...
and
0<\sigma<1
.
By the density argument, similarly to Fourth Step, it suffices to prove
for
u\in\,_{0}C^{m}[0,T]
.

Let
u\in\,_{0}C^{m}[0,T]
be arbitrarily given. Then

\frac{d^{m-1}u}{dt^{m-1}}\in\,{{}_{0}{C^{1}[0,T]}}

by the definition of
\,{}_{0}W^{\alpha,p}(0,T)
.

Since the conclusion of Lemma 2.4 with
0<\alpha<1
and

u\in\,{{}_{0}{C^{1}[0,T]}}
was already proved, we replace

m:=1
and

u:=\frac{d^{m-1}u}{dt^{m-1}}\in\,{{}_{0}{C^{1}[0,T]}}
to apply, so that
we obtain

[formula]

\left\|J^{1-\sigma}\frac{d^{m-1}u}{dt^{m-1}}\right\|_{W^{1,p}(0,T)}\leq C\left\|\frac{d^{m-1}u}{dt^{m-1}}\right\|_{W^{\sigma+\varepsilon,p}(0,T)}.

Similarly to (2.16), we can see

\frac{d^{m-1}}{dt^{m-1}}J^{1-\sigma}u=J^{1-\sigma}\frac{d^{m-1}u}{dt^{m-1}}\quad\mbox{for $u\in\,_{0}C^{m}[0,T]$}.

Consequently, (6.12) yields

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{d^{m}}{dt^{m}}J^{1-\sigma}u\right\|_{L^{p}(0,T)}\leq\left\|\frac{d^{m-1}}{dt^{m-1}}J^{1-\sigma}u\right\|_{W^{1,p}(0,T)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|J^{1-\sigma}\left(\frac{d^{m-1}u}{dt^{m-1}}\right)\right\|_{W^{1,p}(0,T)}\leq C\left\|\frac{d^{m-1}u}{dt^{m-1}}\right\|_{W^{\sigma+\varepsilon,p}(0,T)}.

Hence, the definition of the norm
\|\cdot\|_{W^{\alpha+\varepsilon,p}(0,T)}

yields

[formula]

\left\|\frac{d^{m}}{dt^{m}}J^{1-\sigma}u\right\|_{L^{p}(0,T)}\leq C\|u\|_{W^{m-1+\sigma+\varepsilon,p}(0,T)}.

Since
\frac{d^{k}}{dt^{k}}(J^{1-\sigma}u)(0)=0

by
\frac{d^{k}u}{dt^{k}}(0)=0
for
0\leq k\leq m-1
, we have

\frac{d^{m-k}}{dt^{m-k}}(J^{1-\sigma}u)(t)=\int^{t}_{0}\frac{(t-s)^{k-1}}{(k-1)!}\frac{d^{m}}{dt^{m}}(J^{1-\sigma}u)(s)ds,\quad k=1,...,m

and so

\|J^{1-\sigma}u\|_{W^{m-1,p}(0,T)}\leq C\left\|\frac{d^{m}}{dt^{m}}J^{1-\sigma}u\right\|_{L^{p}(0,T)}.

In view of (6.13) and
m-\alpha=1-\sigma
, we reach

\|J^{m-\alpha}u\|_{W^{m,p}(0,T)}=\|J^{1-\sigma}u\|_{W^{m,p}(0,T)}\leq C\|u\|_{W^{\alpha+\varepsilon,p}(0,T)}

for
u\in\,_{0}C^{m}[0,T]
.
Thus the proof of Lemma 2.4 is complete.

\blacksquare

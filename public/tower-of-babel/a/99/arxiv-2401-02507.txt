Boundedness of the Bergman projection on some weighted mixed norm Lebesgue spaces of the upper-half space
Jean − - Marcel Tanoh Dje, Felix Ofori and Benoît F. Sehba

Abstract
. In this paper, we prove the boundedness of the Bergman projection on weighted mixed norm spaces of the upper-half space for some weights that are constructed using the logarithm function and growth functions. Our necessary and sufficient condition is the same as in the unweighted case, that is it involves only the parameters and not the weight. We then provide some applications in terms of dual and derivative characterization, and an atomic decomposition of the corresponding Bergman spaces.

1.
Introduction

We put ourselves in the upper-half space
{\mathbb{C}}_{+}:=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}:\Im mz>0\}
. We denote by
\mathcal{H}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
the set of holomorphic functions in
{\mathbb{C}}_{+}
. Let
\alpha>-1
, and let
1\leq p,q<\infty
. For
\Omega
a weight (positive function) on
(0,\infty)
, we denote by
L_{\Omega,\alpha}^{p,q}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
the set of all measurable functions
f
on
\mathbb{C}_{+}
such that

\|f\|_{\Omega,\alpha,p,q}^{q}:=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(x+iy)|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}\Omega(y)y^{\alpha}dy<\infty.

In particular, when
\Omega(y)=1
and
\alpha>-1
, we use the notations
L_{\alpha}^{p,q}(\mathbb{C}_{+})=L^{p,q}(\mathbb{C}_{+},(\Im mz)^{\alpha}dV(z))
for the corresponding space and
\|\cdot\|_{\alpha,p,q}
for the corresponding norm.

For
1\leq p,q<\infty
and
\alpha>-1
,
the mixed norm Bergman space
A_{\Omega,\alpha}^{p,q}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
is the intersection
L_{\Omega,\alpha}^{p,q}(\mathbb{C}_{+})\cap\mathcal{H}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
. We note that depending on the weight, this space can be trivial.

When
p=q
, the above space is just denoted
L_{\Omega,\alpha}^{p}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
, and its norm
\|\cdot\|_{\Omega,\alpha,p}
.

For simplicity, we will be using the notation

dV_{\alpha}(z)=\left(\Im m(z)\right)^{\alpha}dV(z)=y^{\alpha}dxdy\quad\textrm{for}\quad z=x+iy.

For
\alpha>-1
, the Bergman projection
P_{\alpha}
is the orthogonal projection from
L_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
into its closed subspace
A_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
. This operator is given by

P_{\alpha}f(z)=\int_{\mathbb{C}_{+}}K_{\alpha}(z,w)f(w)dV_{\alpha}(w)

where

K_{\alpha}(z,w)=\frac{c_{\alpha}}{\left(z-\bar{w}\right)^{2+\alpha}}

is the Bergman kernel, and
c_{\alpha}
is a constant that depends only on
\alpha
.

We denote by
P_{\alpha}^{+}
, the operator with kernel
\left|K_{\alpha}(\cdot,\cdot)\right|
.

It is a well known and easy to verify fact that the Bergman projection
P_{\alpha}
(and equivalently
P_{\alpha}^{+}
) extends to a bounded projection on
L_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
for any
1<p<\infty
. The problem of characterizing the weights
\omega
such that
P_{\alpha}
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{C}_{+},\omega(z)dV_{\alpha}(z))
was considered and solved by D. Békollé and A. Bonami in 1978 and 1982 (see
[
2
,
3
]
). They proved that a necessary and sufficient condition for this to hold, is that the weight
\omega
should satisfy the following condition also known as the
B_{p}
-condition:

(1.1)

[\omega]_{p}:=\sup_{I}\left(\frac{1}{|Q_{I}|_{\alpha}}\int_{Q_{I}}\omega(z)dV_{\alpha}(z)\right)\left(\frac{1}{|Q_{I}|_{\alpha}}\int_{Q_{I}}\left(\omega(z)\right)^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}dV_{\alpha}(z)\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{\prime}}}<\infty.

Here the supremum is taken over all subintervals
I
of
\mathbb{R}
,
Q_{I}
is the square above
I
, and
pp^{\prime}=p^{\prime}+p
.

Obviously, this condition is difficult to verify for general weights. Moreover, this result doesn’t say anything about the case of mixed norm spaces defined above.

Our aim in this paper is to give an answer to the problem of boundedness of the Bergman projection on weighted mixed norm spaces for an appropriate family of weights on
(0,\infty)
. The particularity here is that for the weights considered here, the condition obtained is the same as in the unweighted case (see
[
1
]
).

Let us defined our weights. We recall that a growth function is any function

\Phi:\mathbb{R}_{+}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
, not identically null, that is continuous, nondecreasing and onto.

We observe that if
\Phi
is a growth function, then
\Phi(0)=0
and
\lim_{t\to\infty}\Phi(t)=\infty
.

Let
p>0
be a real and
\Phi
a growth function. We say that
\Phi
is of upper-type (resp. lower-type)
p>0
if there exists a constant

C_{p}>0
such that for all
t\geq 1
(resp.
0<t\leq 1
),

(1.2)

\Phi(st)\leq C_{p}t^{p}\Phi(s),~~\forall~s>0.

We denote by
\mathscr{U}^{p}
(resp.
\mathscr{L}_{p}
) the set of all growth functions of upper-type
p\geq 1
(resp. lower-type
0<p\leq 1
) such that the function

t\mapsto\frac{\Phi(t)}{t}
is non decreasing (resp. non-increasing) on
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}=\mathbb{R}_{+}\backslash\{0\}
. We put

\mathscr{U}:=\bigcup_{p\geq 1}\mathscr{U}^{p}
(resp.
\mathscr{L}:=\bigcup_{0<p\leq 1}\mathscr{L}_{p}
).

The weights we consider here are powers of the following function

(1.3)

\mathcal{T}_{\Phi}^{\vec{\varepsilon}}(t)=1+\varepsilon_{1}\ln_{+}\left(\Phi\left(\frac{1}{t}\right)\right)+\varepsilon_{2}\ln_{+}\left(\Phi(t)\right),t>0.

Here
\vec{\varepsilon}=(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})\in\{0,1\}^{2}
, and

\Phi
is a growth function.

In fact in the upper half plane, these weights are taken as powers of

\mathcal{T}_{\Phi}^{\vec{\varepsilon}}(z)=1+\varepsilon_{1}\ln_{+}\left(\Phi\left(\frac{1}{\Im mz}\right)\right)+\varepsilon_{2}\ln_{+}\left(\Phi(|z|)\right),(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})\in\{0,1\}^{2}.

When
\Phi(t)=t
, and
(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})=(1,1)
, the corresponding weight
\omega(z)
is exactly the one that appears in the study of Bloch functions of the upper half plane (see
[
6
]
).

Recall that a function
f
is a Bloch function if

(1.4)

\Im m(z)|f^{\prime}(z)|\leq C.

Let us put

\|f\|_{*}:=|f(i)|+\sup_{z\in{\mathbb{C}}_{+}}\Im m(z)|f^{\prime}(z)|.

Then one has (see
[
6
]
) that there is a constant
C>0
such that for any Bloch function
f
,

|f(z)|\leq C\|f\|_{*}\left(\ln\left((e+\frac{1}{\Im mz}\right)+\ln\left(e+|z|\right)\right),z\in{\mathbb{C}}_{+}.

Our main result is as follows.

Theorem 1
.

Let
1\leq p,q<\infty
,
\alpha,\beta>-1
. Assume that
\Phi\in\mathscr{L}\cup\mathscr{U}
. Put
\omega=\mathcal{T}_{\Phi}^{\vec{\varepsilon}}
, and let
k\in\mathbb{R}
. Then the following assertions are equivalent.

(a)

The operator
P_{\beta}
is bounded on
L^{p,q}({\mathbb{C}}_{+},\omega^{k}(\Im mz)dV_{\alpha}(z))
.

(b)

The operator
P_{\beta}^{+}
is bounded on
L^{p,q}({\mathbb{C}}_{+},\omega^{k}(\Im mz)dV_{\alpha}(z))
.

(c)

The parameters satisfy

(1.5)

\alpha+1<q(\beta+1).

.

It is known that the boundedness of
P_{\beta}^{+}
on
L_{\alpha}^{q}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
is equivalent to the boundedness of a corresponding Hilbert-type operator of the positive line (see for example
[
1
,
10
]
). The same observation can be made here. The Hilbert-type operator
H_{\beta}
is defined by

H_{\beta}f(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{f(y)y^{\beta}dy}{\left(x+y\right)^{1+\beta}}.

Let
\Omega
be a weight on
(0,\infty)
, and
1\leq p<\infty
. We denote by
L_{\Omega}^{p}((0,\infty))
the set of all measurable functions
f
such that

\|f\|_{\Omega,p}^{p}:=\int_{0}^{\infty}|f(x)|^{p}\Omega(x)dx<\infty.

In particular, when
\Omega(x)=x^{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, we use the notations
L_{\alpha}^{p}((0,\infty))=L^{p}((0,\infty),x^{\alpha}dx)
for the corresponding space and
\|\cdot\|_{\alpha,p}
for the corresponding norm.

It is a well known that the operator
H_{\beta}
is bounded on
L_{\alpha}^{p}((0,\infty))
for any
1<p<\infty
if and only if the relation
1+\alpha<p(1+\beta)
holds (see for example
[
1
]
). We extend this result to the weighted space
L^{p}((0,\infty),\omega(x)x^{\alpha}dx)
for the above family of weights.
Our main results on the weighted boundedness of
H_{\beta}
are given in the following.

Theorem 2
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
,
\alpha,\beta>-1
. Assume that
\Phi\in\mathscr{L}\cup\mathscr{U}
. Put
\omega=\mathcal{T}_{\Phi}^{\vec{\varepsilon}}
, and let
k\in\mathbb{R}
. Then the following assertions are equivalent.

(a)

The operator
H_{\beta}
is bounded on
L^{p}((0,\infty),\omega^{k}(y)y^{\alpha}dy)
.

(b)

The parameters satisfy

(1.6)

\alpha+1<p(\beta+1).

To prove the above result, we will prove a kind of weighted Forelli-Rudin type estimate adapted to our setting and then combine it with a Schur’s lemma in the case
p>1
.

Remark 1
.

In general, to study the boundededness of operators with kernel on weighted Lebesgue spaces, one needs to develop a weighted theory as the ones of Muckenhoupt weights for Calderón-Zygmund operators (see for example
[
7
]
) or of the Békollé-Bonami weights for the Bergman operator (see
[
2
,
3
]
).Our family of weights allows us to avoid this approach here and to reduce the question to a relation between the parameters
\alpha,\beta
and the exponent
p
. The weighted theory for the operator
H_{\beta}
(for general weights) will be considered elsewhere.

The choice of the weights in this paper was inspired by
[
6
]
which also serves as motivation for this paper. Among our applications, we have an atomic decomposition of the above Bergman spaces which generalizes the work of J. Gonessa on the same topic in
[
8
]
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we prove the result on the Hilbert-type operators. The boundedness of the Bergman projection on mixed norm spaces is proved in Section 3. In the last section, we provide some applications of our results.

As usual, we use the the notation

A\lesssim B
(respectively
A\gtrsim B
) whenever there exists a uniform constant
C>0
such that
A\leq CB
(respectively
A\geq CB
). The notation
A\simeq B
means that
A\lesssim B
and
A\gtrsim B
.

2.
Boundedness of the Hilbert-type operator
H_{\beta}

In this section, we consider the boundedness of the operator
H_{\beta}
on
L^{p}((0,\infty),\omega^{k}(y)y^{\alpha}dy)
.

2.1.
Weighted estimates on the positive line

In the following, we give estimates of the measure of any interval
(0,t)
with respect to
\omega^{k}(y)y^{\beta}dy
.

Lemma 1
.

Let
\beta>-1
. Suppose that
\Phi\in\mathscr{L}\cup\mathscr{U}
. Put
\omega=\mathcal{T}_{\Phi}^{\vec{\varepsilon}}
, and let
k>0
. Then the following holds.

(2.1)

\int_{0}^{t}\omega^{-k}(y)y^{\beta}dy\simeq\omega^{-k}(t)\,t^{1+\beta}.

Proof.

We restrict ourselves to the case
\vec{\varepsilon}=(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})=(1,1)
as the other cases follow the same way. Recall that in this case,

\omega(y)=1+\ln_{+}\left(\Phi\left(\frac{1}{y}\right)\right)+\ln_{+}\left(\Phi(y)\right),

and this is comparable to

\ln\left(e+\Phi\left(\frac{1}{y}\right)\right)+\ln\left(e+\Phi(y)\right).

We start by proving the upper estimate. If
0<t\leq 1
, then the estimate above in (
2.1
) follows directly from the fact that
\omega
is nonincreasing in this interval.

More generally, let
t>0
. Then using that
\Phi
satisfies (
) or (
), and the notation

(2.2)

p_{\Phi}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcr}q&\mbox{if}&\Phi\in\mathscr{U}^{q}\\
1&\mbox{if}&\Phi\in\mathscr{L}_{p}.\end{array},\right.

we obtain

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\omega^{-k}(y)y^{\beta}dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\int_{2^{-n-1}t}^{2^{-n}t}\frac{y^{\beta}dy}{\left[\ln\left(e+\Phi\left(\frac{1}{y}\right)\right)+\ln\left(e+\Phi(y)\right)\right]^{k}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{(2^{1+\beta}-1)}{1+\beta}t^{1+\beta}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{2^{-(n+1)(1+\beta)}}{\left[\ln\left(e+\Phi\left(\frac{2^{n}}{t}\right)\right)+\ln\left(e+\Phi\left(\frac{t}{2^{n+1}}\right)\right)\right]^{k}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle t^{1+\beta}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{2^{-(n+1)(1+\beta)}\ln^{k}\left(2^{(n+1)p_{\Phi}}\right)}{\left[\ln\left(e+\Phi\left(\frac{1}{t}\right)\right)+\left[\ln\left(2^{(n+1)p_{\Phi}}\right)+\ln\left(e+\Phi\left(\frac{t}{2^{n+1}}\right)\right)\right]\right]^{k}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{t^{1+\beta}\left(p_{\Phi}\ln 2\right)^{k}}{\omega^{k}(t)}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}2^{-(n+1)(1+\beta)}(n+1)^{k}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{t^{1+\beta}}{\omega^{k}(t)}.

The lower inequality follows by considering the integral over
(\frac{t}{2},t)
. The proof is complete.
∎

We also have the following.

Lemma 2
.

Let
\beta>-1
. Suppose that
\Phi\in\mathscr{L}\cup\mathscr{U}
. Put
\omega=\mathcal{T}_{\Phi}^{\vec{\varepsilon}}
, and let
k>0
. Then the following

(2.3)

\int_{0}^{t}\omega^{k}(y)y^{\beta}dy\simeq\omega^{k}(t)\,t^{1+\beta}.

Proof.

The lower estimate is obtained as above. For the upper estimate, we again restrict to the weight

\omega(y)=\ln\left(e+\Phi\left(\frac{1}{y}\right)\right)+\ln\left(e+\Phi(y)\right).

If
t\geq 1
, then the upper estimate in (
2.3
) follows directly from the fact that
\omega
is nonincreasing in this range.

More generally, let
t>0
. We obtain

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\omega^{k}(y)y^{\beta}dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\int_{2^{-n-1}t}^{2^{-n}t}\left[\ln\left(e+\Phi\left(\frac{1}{y}\right)\right)+\ln\left(e+\Phi(y)\right)\right]^{k}y^{\beta}dy

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle(2^{1+\beta}-1)t^{1+\beta}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}2^{-(n+1)(1+\beta)}\left[\ln\left(e+\Phi\left(\frac{2^{n+1}}{t}\right)\right)+\ln\left(e+\Phi(2^{-n}t)\right)\right]^{k}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle t^{1+\beta}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}2^{-(n+1)(1+\beta)}\left[(n+1)p_{\Phi}\ln 2+\ln\left(e+\Phi\left(\frac{1}{t}\right)\right)+\ln\left(e+\Phi(t)\right)\right]^{k}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle t^{1+\beta}\left(p_{\Phi}\right)^{k}\omega^{k}(t)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}2^{-(n+1)(1+\beta)}(n+1)^{k}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle t^{1+\beta}\omega^{k}(t).

The proof is complete.
∎

We have the following estimate.

Lemma 3
.

Let
\alpha>0
, and
\beta>-1
. Suppose that
\Phi\in\mathscr{L}\cup\mathscr{U}
. Put
\omega=\mathcal{T}_{\Phi}^{\vec{\varepsilon}}
, and let
k>0
. Then the following holds

(2.4)

\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\omega^{-k}(y)y^{\beta}dy}{\left(x+y\right)^{1+\alpha+\beta}}\simeq\omega^{-k}(x)\,x^{-\alpha},x>0.

Proof.

Again we only present the proof for
\vec{\varepsilon}=(1,1)
. For
x>0
fixed, let
I_{0}=(0,x]
. For
j\in\mathbb{N}
, let
I_{j}=(0,2^{j}x]
. Then define
J_{0}=I_{0}
, and for
j\geq 1
,
J_{j}=I_{j}\setminus I_{j-1}
.

We obtain using Lemma
1
that

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\omega^{-k}(y)y^{\beta}dy}{\left(x+y\right)^{1+\alpha+\beta}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\int_{J_{j}}\frac{\omega^{-k}(y)y^{\beta}dy}{\left(x+y\right)^{1+\alpha+\beta}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle x^{-(1+\alpha+\beta)}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}2^{-(j-1)(1+\alpha+\beta)}\int_{I_{j}}\omega^{-k}(y)y^{\beta}dy

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle x^{-(1+\alpha+\beta)}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}2^{-(j-1)(1+\alpha+\beta)}\frac{\left(2^{j}x\right)^{1+\beta}}{\omega^{k}(2^{j}x)}.

If
x\geq 1
, then we directly have that
\omega(2^{j}x)>\omega(x)
. If
0<x<1
, then for any
j>0,
we obtain

\displaystyle\omega(2^{j}x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ln\left(e+\Phi\left(2^{j}x\right)\right)+\ln\left(e+\Phi\left(\frac{1}{2^{j}x}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\gtrsim

\displaystyle\frac{\ln\left(e+\Phi(x)\right)+\ln\left(2^{jp_{\Phi}}\right)+\ln\left(e+\Phi\left(\frac{1}{2^{j}x}\right)\right)}{\ln\left(2^{jp_{\Phi}}\right)}

\displaystyle\gtrsim

\displaystyle\frac{\ln\left(e+\Phi(x)\right)+\ln\left(e+\Phi\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)\right)}{\ln\left(2^{jp_{\Phi}}\right)}.

Hence we finally obtain

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\omega^{-k}(x)\,x^{-\alpha}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}2^{-(j-1)\alpha}j^{k}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\omega^{-k}(x)\,x^{-\alpha}.

The lower estimate is obtained by taking the integral from
\frac{x}{2}
to
x
. The proof is complete.
∎

We have the following.

Lemma 4
.

Let
\alpha>0
, and
\beta>-1
. Suppose that
\Phi\in\mathscr{L}\cup\mathscr{U}
. Put
\omega=\mathcal{T}_{\Phi}^{\vec{\varepsilon}}
, and let
k>0
. Then the following holds

(2.5)

\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\omega^{k}(y)y^{\beta}dy}{\left(x+y\right)^{1+\alpha+\beta}}\simeq\omega^{k}(x)\,x^{-\alpha},x>0.

The proof is essentially the same as above. One only needs the upper estimate of
\omega(2^{j}x)
,
j>0
. If
x>0
, then

\displaystyle\omega(2^{j}x)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\ln\left(e+\Phi\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)\right)+\ln\left(2^{jp_{\Phi}}\right)+\ln\left(e+\Phi(x)\right)

2.2.
Proofs of Theorem
2

We start by considering the case
p=1
. We prove the following.

Theorem 3
.

Let
\alpha,\beta>-1
. Assume that
\Phi\in\mathscr{L}\cup\mathscr{U}
. Put
\omega=\mathcal{T}_{\Phi}^{\vec{\varepsilon}}
, and let
k\in\mathbb{R}
. Then the following assertions are equivalent.

(a)

The operator
H_{\beta}
is bounded on
L^{1}((0,\infty),\omega^{k}(y)y^{\alpha}dy)
.

(b)

The parameters satisfy

\beta>\alpha.

Proof.

That (b) implies (a) follows from Fubini’s theorem and Lemma
3
and Lemma
4
.

Conversely, suppose that
H_{\beta}
is bounded on
L^{1}((0,\infty),\omega^{-k}(y)y^{\alpha}dy)
,
k>0
. Then its adjoint
H_{\beta}^{*}
defined by

H_{\beta}^{*}f(x)=\omega^{k}(x)x^{\beta-\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{f(y)\omega^{-k}(y)y^{\alpha}dy}{\left(x+y\right)^{1+\beta}}

is bounded on
L^{\infty}((0,\infty))
. Take
f(y)=\chi_{[1,2]}(y)
. Then

H_{\beta}^{*}f(x)\simeq\frac{\omega^{k}(x)x^{\beta-\alpha}}{\left(x+1\right)^{1+\beta}}.

Clearly, for
0<x<1
, one has that
H_{\beta}^{*}f(x)\gtrsim x^{\beta-\alpha}.

Hence the boundedness of
H_{\beta}^{*}f(x)
implies that
x^{\beta-\alpha}
is bounded on
(0,1)
. Thus, necessarily,
\beta-\alpha>0
.

Now, suppose that
H_{\beta}
is bounded on
L^{1}((0,\infty),\omega^{k}(y)y^{\alpha}dy)
,
k>0
i.e there exists a constant
C>0
such that for any
f\in L^{1}((0,\infty),\omega^{k}(y)y^{\alpha}dy)
,

(2.6)

\int_{0}^{\infty}|H_{\beta}f(x)|\omega^{k}(x)x^{\alpha}dx\leq C\int_{0}^{\infty}|f(x)|\omega^{k}(x)x^{\alpha}dx

Take
f(y)=\chi_{[1,2]}(y)
. Then

H_{\beta}f(x)\simeq\frac{1}{(x+1)^{1+\beta}}

and

\int_{0}^{\infty}|f(x)|\omega^{k}(x)x^{\alpha}dx\simeq 1.

Taking this into (
2.6
), we obtain

\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{\alpha}}{(x+1)^{1+\beta}}dx\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\omega^{k}(x)x^{\alpha}}{(x+1)^{1+\beta}}dx<\infty.

This necessarily implies that
1+\beta>\alpha+1
.
The proof is complete.
∎

We next consider the case
p>1
.

Theorem 4
.

Let
1<p<\infty
, and
\alpha,\beta>-1
. Assume that
\Phi\in\mathscr{L}\cup\mathscr{U}
. Put
\omega=\mathcal{T}_{\Phi}^{\vec{\varepsilon}}
, and let
k\in\mathbb{R}
. Then the following assertions are equivalent.

(a)

The operator
H_{\beta}
is bounded on
L^{p}((0,\infty),\omega^{k}(y)y^{\alpha}dy)
.

(b)

The parameters satisfy
(
1.6
).

Proof.

We restrict to the case of
\omega^{-k}
,
k>0
as the other case follows from the same ideas. Recall that

H_{\beta}f(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{f(y)y^{\beta}dy}{\left(x+y\right)^{1+\beta}}.

We note that the kernel of
H_{\beta}
with respect to the measure
\omega^{-k}(y)y^{\alpha}dy
is

K(x,y)=\frac{\omega^{k}(y)y^{\beta-\alpha}}{\left(x+y\right)^{1+\beta}}.

Assume that (
1.6
) holds. By the Schur’s lemma (see
[
9
]
), to prove that
H_{\beta}
is bounded on

L^{p}((0,\infty),\omega^{-k}(y)y^{\alpha}dy)
, it suffices to find a positive function
\phi
such that

(2.7)

\int_{0}^{\infty}K(x,y)\phi^{p^{\prime}}(y)\omega^{-k}(y)y^{\alpha}dy\leq C\phi^{p^{\prime}}(x)

and

(2.8)

\int_{0}^{\infty}K(x,y)\phi^{p}(x)\omega^{-k}(x)x^{\alpha}dx\leq C\phi^{p}(y).

Let us take
\phi(t)=t^{-\frac{\alpha+1}{pp^{\prime}}}
. Then using Lemma
3
, we obtain

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}K(x,y)\phi^{p^{\prime}}(y)\omega^{-k}(y)y^{\alpha}dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{y^{\beta-\frac{\alpha+1}{p}}dy}{\left(x+y\right)^{1+\beta}}

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle x^{-\frac{\alpha+1}{p}}=\phi^{p^{\prime}}(x).

In the same way, we have

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}K(x,y)\phi^{p}(x)\omega^{-k}(x)x^{\alpha}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega^{k}(y)y^{\beta-\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\omega^{-k}(x)x^{-\frac{\alpha+1}{p^{\prime}}+\alpha}dx}{\left(x+y\right)^{1+\beta}}

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle x^{-\frac{\alpha+1}{p^{\prime}}}=\phi^{p}(x).

This completes the proof of the sufficient part.

Now suppose that
H_{\beta}
is bounded on

defined by

. Take

. Then

Thus that

implies that

Thus, necessarily,

4.2.
Derivative characterization of weighted mixed norm Bergman spaces

In this section, we prove that
f\in A^{p,q}_{\omega^{k},\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
if and only if the function
x+iy\mapsto yf^{\prime}(x+iy)
belongs to
L^{p,q}_{\omega^{k},\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
.

Theorem 5
.

Let
1<p,q<\infty
, and
\alpha>-1
. Assume that
\Phi\in\mathscr{L}\cup\mathscr{U}
. Put
\omega=\mathcal{T}_{\Phi}^{\vec{\varepsilon}}
, and let
k\in\mathbb{R}
. Let
f
be a holomorphic function on
\mathbb{C}_{+}
. Then the following assertions are equivalent.

(a)

f
belongs to
A^{p,q}_{\omega^{k},\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
.

(b)

The function
x+iy\mapsto yf^{\prime}(x+iy)
belongs to
L^{p,q}_{\omega^{k},\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
.

Proof.

(a)
\Rightarrow
(b): Recall the following inequality for the derivative
f^{\prime}
of the holomorphic function
f
on the upper half-plane (see
[
5
, Problem 2]
)

y|f^{\prime}(x+iy)|\leq\frac{C}{y^{2}}\int_{\frac{y}{2}<v<2y}\int_{|x-u|<y}|f(u+iv)|dudv.

Using Minkowski’s inequality, we obtain

y\|f^{\prime}(\cdot+iy)\|_{p}\leq\left(\frac{C}{y}\int_{\frac{y}{2}<v<2y}\|f(\cdot+iv)\|_{p}^{p}dv\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Since the function
v\mapsto\|f(\cdot+iv)\|_{p}
is non-increasing, we deduce that

y\|f^{\prime}(\cdot+iy)\|_{p}\leq C\|f(\cdot+iy/2)\|_{p}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\|f^{\prime}(\cdot+iy)\|_{p}^{q}y^{q+\alpha}\omega^{k}(y)dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{\infty}\|f(\cdot+iy/2)\|_{p}^{q}y^{\alpha}\omega^{k}(y)dy

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\|f(\cdot+iy)\|_{p}^{q}y^{\alpha}\omega^{k}(y)dy.

(b)
\Rightarrow
(a): Using assertion (iii) of Proposition
3
, one obtains that

|f(x+iy)|=\left|\int_{y}^{\infty}-f^{\prime}(x+iv)dv\right|\leq\int_{y}^{\infty}|f^{\prime}(x+iv)|dv.

Using the above and Minkowski’s inequality, we derive that

\displaystyle\|f(\cdot+iy)\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\int_{y}^{\infty}|f^{\prime}(x+iv)|dv\right)^{p}dx\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{y}^{\infty}\|f^{\prime}(\cdot+iv)\|_{p}dv

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\|f^{\prime}(\cdot+iv)\|_{p}v^{1+\alpha}dv}{(y+v)^{1+\alpha}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H_{\alpha}\left(v\|f^{\prime}(\cdot+iv)\|_{p}\right)(y).

The conclusion then follows from Theorem
2
.
∎

The Bergman distance in the upper-half plane is given by

with radius

Let

and

\frac{\log\left(\frac{1+\frac{\delta^{2}}{20}}{1-\frac{\delta^{2}}{20}}\right)}{4\log 2}<\gamma<\frac{\log\left(\frac{1+\frac{\delta^{2}}{4}}{1-\frac{\delta^{2}}{4}}\right)}{4\log 2}.

\displaystyle:=\left\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}:\left|2^{-\gamma j}(x-x_{l,j})\right|<\frac{\delta^{2}}{4}\right\}

\displaystyle:=\left\{y\in(0,\infty):\left|2^{-\gamma j}(y-y_{j})\right|<\frac{\delta^{2}}{20}\right\}.

We have also the following properties.

Lemma 5
.

Let

]0;\infty[=\cup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}J_{j}

Lemma 6

. For
F\in A^{\Phi}_{\omega^{k},\alpha}(\mathbb{C_{+}})

(4.11)

\mathcal{I}(F)\leq C\|F\|_{A^{p,q}_{\omega^{k},\alpha}}^{q}.

Proof.

From the property (iii) in Lemma
5
, we have that

\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\chi_{\{x\in I_{l,j}:|(x+iy)-(u+iv)|<\frac{y}{2\sqrt{2}}\}}\leq 4.

Hence

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\chi_{\{x\in I_{l,j}:|(x+iy)-(u+iv)|<\frac{y}{2\sqrt{2}}\}}(x)dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 4\int_{\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}:\,|(x+iy)-(u+iv)|<\frac{y}{2\sqrt{2}}\}}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 4\chi_{\{|y-v|<\frac{y}{2\sqrt{2}}\}}(v)\int_{\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}:\,|x-u|<\frac{y}{2\sqrt{2}}\}}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Cv\chi_{\{|y-v|<\frac{y}{2\sqrt{2}}\}}(v)

where we have used in the last inequality the fact that if
x+iy\in{\mathbb{C}}_{+}
and
u+iv\in{\mathbb{C}}_{+}
are such that

|(x+iy)-(u+iv)|<\frac{y}{2\sqrt{2}}
, then
y\simeq v
.
It follows that,

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(F)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\chi_{J_{j}}(y)\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}|F(u+iv)|^{p}\left(v\chi_{\{y/C<v<Cy\}}(v)\right)\frac{dudv}{v^{2}}\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}d\omega_{\alpha,k}(y)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\chi_{J_{j}}(y)\left(\int_{y/C}^{Cy}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}|F(u+iv)|^{p}v\frac{dudv}{v^{2}}\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}d\omega_{\alpha,k}(y)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\chi_{J_{j}}(y)\left(\int_{y/C}^{Cy}\|F(.+iv)\|_{L^{p}}^{p}\frac{dv}{v}\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}d\omega_{\alpha,k}(y).

Given that
y\mapsto\|F(.+iy)\|_{L^{p}}
is non-increasing on
(0,\infty)
, we have that

\int_{y/C}^{Cy}\|F(.+iv)\|_{L^{p}}^{p}\frac{dv}{v}\lesssim\left\|F\left(.+i\frac{y}{C}\right)\right\|_{L^{p}}^{p}.

Therefore, using property (v) of Lemma
5
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(F)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\chi_{J_{j}}(y)\left(\int_{y/C}^{Cy}\|F(.+iv)\|_{L^{p}}^{p}\frac{dv}{v}\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}d\omega_{\alpha,k}(y)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{j\in L}\chi_{J_{j}}(y)\left\|F\left(.+i\frac{y}{C}\right)\right\|_{L^{p}}^{q}d\omega_{\alpha,k}(y)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}\left\|F\left(.+iy\right)\right\|_{L^{p}}^{q}d\omega_{\alpha,k}(y)\lesssim\|F\|_{A^{p,q}_{\omega^{k},\alpha}}^{q}.

∎

Lemma 8
.

Suppose that

or H’́older’s inequality when

\displaystyle\|F(\cdot+iy)\|_{p}^{q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|F_{y}(\cdot+i)\|_{p}^{q}

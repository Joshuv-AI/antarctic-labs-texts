Note on the group of vertical diffeomorphisms of a principal bundle, & its relation to the Frölicher-Nijenhuis bracket
J. François

Abstract
The group of vertical diffeomorphisms of a principal bundle forms the generalised action Lie groupoid associated to the bundle. The former is generated by the group of maps with value in the structure group, which is also the group of bisections of the groupoid. The corresponding Lie algebra of general vertical vector fields is generated by maps with value in the Lie algebra of the structure group. The bracket on these maps, induces by the bracket of vertical vector fields, is an “extended” bracket on gauge parameters: it has been introduced heuristically in physics, notably in the study asymptotic symmetries of gravity. Seeing the set of Lie algebra-valued maps as sections of the action Lie algebroid associated to the bundle, the extended bracket is understood to be a Lie algebroid bracket on those sections. Here, we highlight that this bracket can also be seen to arise from the Frölicher-Nijenhuis bracket of vector-valued differential forms. The benefit of this viewpoint is to insert this extended bracket within the general framework of derivations of forms on the bundle. Identities relating it to usual operations – inner product, exterior and (Nijenhuis-) Lie derivative – are immediately read as special cases of general results. We also look at the generalised gauge transformations induced by vertical diffeomorphisms, and discuss their peculiar features. In particular, locally, and contrary to standard gauge transformations arising from vertical bundle automorphisms, they are distinguishable from local gluings when iterated. Yet, the gauge principle still holds.

1
Introduction

In the some of the literature on gauge field theory and gravity, field-dependent gauge parameters or diffeomorphisms are often considered. These are needed, is it often argued, because of gauge-fixing or when boundary conditions are imposed that needs to be preserved by gauge symmetries.
Consequently, a bracket extending the Lie algebra bracket of gauge parameters (the bracket of vector fields in the case of diffeomorphisms) is introduced heuristically, so as to take into account their field-dependence.
Such a bracket features often e.g. in the covariant phase space literature, notably in investigations of spacetime asymptotic symmetries (BMS and extensions). See e.g.
[
1
;
2
;
3
;
4
;
5
]

and references therein. Indeed this literature often traces back the introduction of this bracket to a paper by Barnich and Troessaert
[
6
]
.
While these authors certainly deserve credit for independently coming up with it, the extended bracket was introduced earlier (maybe first) by Bergmann and Komar in
[
7
]
, and then further used by Salisbury and Sundermeyer in
[
8
]
.
To the best of our knowledge, the first clarification of its mathematical origin is to be found in
[
9
]
: it is there understood as a Lie algebroid bracket. Indeed it can be shown that it corresponds essentially to the bracket of the action Lie algebroid of vertical vector fields on the space of fields
\Phi
of a gauge theory, seen as a principal bundle whose structure group is the gauge group of the theory.

In this note, we highlight that it can also be understood as the simplest instance , in form-degree 0,
of the the Frölicher-Nijenhuis (FN) bracket of vector-valued differential forms
[
10
]
on
\Phi
. The field space Lie derivative along field-dependent vector fields, generating field-dependent gauge transformations, is then simply the Nijenhuis-Lie derivative.

The observation holds in the simpler case of a finite dimensional principal bundle
P
with structure group
H
.
As it is rarely articulated in basic treatments of the differential geometry of principal bundles, to advertise the fact to a broader audience, in section
2.1
-
2.2
we lay the case in some details from simple bundle theoretic considerations.
We first describe the group
\Diff_{v}(P)
of vertical diffeomorphisms of
P
, and characterise the corresponding group of generating
H
-valued maps on
P
. Then, we consider their respective Lie algebras. We only briefly remind how these objects are naturally understood in Lie groupoid and Lie algebroid terms.

Then, in section
2.3
we proceed to show that the bracket of general vertical vector fields is but an instance of the FN bracket, which indeed corresponds to the natural
extended bracket
on the associated Lie algebra of Lie
H
-valued maps on
P
.
A benefit of viewing the extended bracket as a FN bracket, is that the many identities relating it to the exterior, Lie, and inner derivatives – which are virtually always derived heuristically in the physics literature – are then understood as special cases of the general theory of derivations of the algebra
\Omega^{\bullet}(P)
, of which we remind the basics. This should at least streamlines technical aspects for the physical applications (mainly to the covariant phase space literature).

In section
3
, we consider the issue of
general vertical transformations
of forms on
P
, resulting from the action of
\Diff_{v}(P)
, and we show that the infinitesimal version of those is given by the Nijenhuis-Lie derivative along general vertical vector fields.
A noticeable feature is that natural spaces of forms on
P
– tensorial forms and connections – are not preserved by the action of
\Diff_{v}(P)
.
In
4
, we consider the local picture, on the base manifold, and what it means for gauge theories living there: A peculiarity is that, contrary to local gauge transformations, local generalised gauge transformations do distinguish themselves from local gluings of the bundle when iterated.

As mentioned, these considerations apply with very little adjustments (mainly notational) to the infinite dimensional case of the field space
\Phi
of gauge field theories, whose structure group can either be the internal gauge group
\mathcal{H}
of the theory (i.e. of
P
) or
\Diff(M)
. We give a detailed account of the later case in a separate work
[
11
]
, for which this paper can be considered a preparatory technical note.

2
Vertical diffeomorphisms of a principal bundle and the Frölicher-Nijenhuis bracket

2.1
Groups of vertical and gauge transformations

Classical gauge gauge field theory is founded on the geometry of connections on fiber bundles, whose central objects are principal bundles.
Consider a
H
-principal bundle
P
over a base manifold
M
(“spacetime” in gauge field theory),
H
its structure (Lie) group.
As a manifold,
P
has a group of diffeomorphisms
\Diff(P)
, but as a bundle its maximal group of transformations is the group of bundle automorphisms
\Aut(P)\vcentcolon=\left\{\psi\in\Diff(P)\,|\,\psi\circ R_{h}=R_{h}\circ\psi\right\}
. The latter, commuting with the right action of
H
, preserve fibers, and thus project as diffeomorphisms of
M
.

The subgroup of
vertical
diffeomorphisms of the
P
is
\Diff_{v}(P)\vcentcolon=\left\{\psi\in\Diff(P)\,|\,\pi\circ\psi=\pi\right\}
. These are diffeos
\psi
moving along fibers, therefore there is a unique smooth map

\gamma:P\rightarrow H
s.t.
\psi(p)=R_{\gamma(p)}p=p\gamma(p)
.
In other words, to
\psi\in\Diff_{v}(P)
corresponds a unique
\gamma\in C^{\infty}(P,H)
.

This generalises the subgroup of vertical automorphisms
\Aut_{v}(P)\vcentcolon=\left\{\psi\in\Aut(P)\,|\,\pi\circ\psi=\pi\right\}
, isomorphic to the gauge group
\mathcal{H}\vcentcolon=\big\{\gamma:P\rightarrow H\,|\,R^{*}\gamma=h^{-1}\gamma h\big\}
via
\psi(p)=R_{\gamma(p)}\,p
: The constraint of
H
-equivariance of
\psi
defining the group of bundle automorphisms
\Aut(P)
is responsible for the specific equivariance required on elements
\gamma
of the gauge group.
Obviously then,
\Diff_{v}(P)\cap\Aut(P)=\Aut_{v}(P)
.

We may also notice that bundle automorphisms belong to the normaliser of vertical diffeomorphisms: Indeed, for
\varphi\in\Aut(P)
and
\psi\in\Diff_{v}(P)\sim\gamma\in C^{\infty}(P,H)
we have,

\displaystyle\big(\varphi^{-1}\circ\psi\circ\varphi\big)(p)=\varphi^{-1}\circ\psi\big(\varphi(p)\big)=\varphi^{-1}\big(R_{\gamma\left(\varphi(p)\right)}\,\varphi(p)\big)=R_{\gamma\left(\varphi(p)\right)}\,\varphi^{-1}\circ\varphi(p)=R_{\gamma\left(\varphi(p)\right)}\,p.

Therefore,
\pi\circ\big(\varphi^{-1}\circ\psi\circ\varphi\big)=\pi
, i.e.
\varphi^{-1}\circ\psi\circ\varphi\in\Diff_{v}(P)
. Since naturally a group is a subgroup of its normaliser, we have that,

\displaystyle N_{\Diff(P)}\big(\!\Diff_{v}(P)\big)=\Diff_{v}(P)\cup\Aut(P).

(1)

As a special case, we have
N_{\Diff(P)}\big(\!\Aut_{v}(P)\big)=\Aut(P)
, i.e. the well-known fact that
\Aut_{v}(P)\triangleleft\Aut(P)
, which in turn gives us the short exact sequence (SES) of groups characteristic of a principal bundle:

\displaystyle\text{id}\rightarrow\Aut_{v}(P)\simeq\mathcal{H}\xrightarrow{\iota}\Aut(P)\xrightarrow{\pi}\Diff(M)\rightarrow\text{id}

(2)

The linearised version is the SES of Lie algebras, a.k.a. the Atiyah Lie algebroid associated to a principal bundle
P
.

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow\mathfrak{aut}_{v}(P)=\Gamma_{H}(VP)\simeq\text{Lie}\mathcal{H}\xrightarrow{\iota}\mathfrak{aut}(P)=\Gamma_{H}(TP)\xrightarrow{\pi_{*}}\mathfrak{diff}(M)\simeq\Gamma(TM)\rightarrow 0.

(3)

Here
\Gamma_{H}(TP)\vcentcolon=\big\{\boldsymbol{X}\in\Gamma(TP)\,|\,R_{h*}\boldsymbol{X}_{{}_{p}}=\boldsymbol{X}_{|ph}\big\}
are the right-invariant vector fields of
P\,
:
Indeed, for
\boldsymbol{X}\vcentcolon=\tfrac{d}{d\tau}\,\psi_{\tau}\,\big|_{\tau=0}
with flow
\psi_{\tau}\in\Aut(P)
, we have

\displaystyle R_{h*}\boldsymbol{X}_{|p}\vcentcolon=\tfrac{d}{d\tau}\,R_{h}\big(\psi_{\tau}(p)\big)\,\big|_{\tau=0}=\tfrac{d}{d\tau}\,\psi_{\tau}\big(R_{h}\,p\big)\,\big|_{\tau=0}=\vcentcolon\boldsymbol{X}_{ph}.

(4)

Naturally,
\Gamma_{H}(VP)\vcentcolon=\big\{\boldsymbol{X}\in\Gamma_{H}(TP)\,|\,\pi_{*}\boldsymbol{X}\equiv 0\big\}
, while Lie
\mathcal{H}\vcentcolon=\big\{X:P\rightarrow\text{Lie}H\,|\,R^{*}X=\text{Ad}_{h^{-1}}X\big\}
. A
H
-invariant vertical vector field is written as
X^{v}\vcentcolon=\tfrac{d}{d\tau}\,\psi_{\tau}\,\big|_{\tau=0}
with flow
\psi_{\tau}(p)=R_{\gamma_{\tau}(p)}\,p\in\Aut_{v}(P)
and
X\vcentcolon=\tfrac{d}{d\tau}\,\gamma_{\tau}\,\big|_{\tau=0}
. In other words, elements of Lie
\mathcal{H}
generate
H
-invariant fundamental vector fields.

The composition law in
\Diff_{v}(P)
induces a peculiar composition law for the corresponding elements in
C^{\infty}(P,H)
. Indeed, for
\psi,\varphi\in\Diff_{v}(P)
to which are respectively associated
\gamma,\eta\in C^{\infty}(P,H)
, we have
\varphi\circ\psi\in\Diff_{v}(P)
(since
\pi\circ\varphi\circ\psi=\pi\circ\psi=\pi
) so one expects it has a corresponding generating element in
C^{\infty}(P,H)
.
The question is how the latter is expressed in terms of
\eta
and
\gamma
. We have,

\displaystyle(\varphi\circ\psi)(p)

\displaystyle=\varphi\left(\psi(p)\right)=R_{\eta\left(\psi(p)\right)}\psi(p)=R_{\eta\left(R_{\gamma(p)}p\right)}R_{\gamma(p)}p,

(5)

\displaystyle=R_{\gamma(p)\,\eta\left(R_{\gamma(p)}p\right)}\,p.

So,

\displaystyle\varphi\circ\psi\in\Diff_{v}(P)\quad\text{ is associated to }\quad\gamma\,(\eta\circ R_{\gamma})\in C^{\infty}(P,H).

(6)

Observe that for vertical automorphisms
\psi,\varphi\in\Aut_{v}(P)
to which correspond the gauge group elements
\gamma,\eta\in\mathcal{H}
, we have
\eta\circ R_{\gamma}=R^{*}_{\gamma}\eta=\gamma^{-1}\eta\gamma
.
So
\gamma(\eta\circ R_{\gamma})=\eta\gamma
, and (
6
) reduces to the standard result
(\varphi\circ\psi)(p)=\big(R_{\eta\gamma}\big)p
.

For the inverse diffeomorphism
\psi^{-1}
,
\psi^{-1}\circ\psi=\text{id}_{P}=\psi\circ\psi^{-1}
, we must have
\tilde{\gamma}\in C^{\infty}(P,H)
s.t.
\psi^{-1}(p)=R_{\tilde{\gamma}(p)}p
. According to (
6
) we must then have that on the one hand,

\displaystyle\psi^{-1}\circ\psi=\text{id}_{P}\quad\text{ is associated to }\quad\gamma(\tilde{\gamma}\circ R_{\gamma})=\text{id}_{H}.

(7)

And on the other hand,

\displaystyle\psi\circ\psi^{-1}=\text{id}_{P}\quad\text{ is associated to }\quad\tilde{\gamma}(\gamma\circ R_{\tilde{\gamma}})=\text{id}_{H}.

(8)

From (
7
) in particular, we get

\displaystyle\tilde{\gamma}\circ R_{\gamma}=\gamma^{-1}_{R}.

(9)

Observe that for
\gamma\in\mathcal{H}
, (
8
) is
\gamma\tilde{\gamma}=\text{id}_{H}
, i.e.
\tilde{\gamma}=\gamma^{-1}_{R}
for all
p\in P
. In particular for
ph
:
\tilde{\gamma}(ph)=\gamma_{R}(ph)^{-1}=h^{-1}\gamma_{R}(p)^{-1}h=h^{-1}\tilde{\gamma}(p)h
, i.e.
R^{*}_{h}\tilde{\gamma}=h^{-1}\tilde{\gamma}h
. So, (
7
) is
\tilde{\gamma}\gamma=\text{id}_{H}
and
\tilde{\gamma}=\gamma^{-1}_{L}
. Thus,
\tilde{\gamma}=\gamma^{-1}
, as one would expect.

Following on (
6
), one shows that for
\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\psi_{3}\in\Diff_{v}(P)
associated respectively to
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\gamma_{3}\in C^{\infty}(P,H)
:

\displaystyle\psi_{3}\circ\psi_{2}\circ\psi_{1}\quad\text{ is associated to }\quad\gamma_{1}(\gamma_{2}\circ R_{\gamma_{1}})\big(\gamma_{3}\circ R_{\gamma_{1}(\gamma_{2}\circ R_{\gamma_{1}})}\big).

(10)

The pattern is clear, for
k
iterated compositions we have,

\displaystyle\circ_{i=1}^{k}\psi_{i}\quad\text{ is associated to }\quad\Pi_{i=1}^{k}t_{i},\ \ \text{ with }t_{i}\vcentcolon=\gamma_{i}\circ R_{t_{i-1}}.

(11)

We therefore see that the composition law of
\Diff_{v}(P)
doesn’t give rise to a simple pointwise multiplicative group structure on the corresponding generating elements in
C^{\infty}(P,H)
, but rather to a recursive nested one involving the (yet unspecified) equivariance.

This is but an example of the composition law of the infinite dimensional group of bisections of a Lie groupoid
[
12
;
13
]
– see also
[
14
]
chap. 6 for a nice introduction. Indeed, the above can be reframed in the groupoid framework in the following way:
One defines the generalised action groupoid
\Gamma\rightrightarrows P
with
\Gamma=P\rtimes\big(C^{\infty}(P,H)\simeq\Diff_{v}(P)\big)
, and with source and target maps

s:\Gamma\rightarrow P
,
(p,\gamma\simeq\psi)\mapsto p
, and

t:\Gamma\rightarrow P
,
(p,\gamma\simeq\psi)\mapsto\psi(p)=R_{\gamma(p)}\,p
.
There is an associative composition law:
g\circ f\in\Gamma
for
f,g\in\Gamma
defined whenever
t(f)=s(g)
.
It naturally generalises the action groupoid
\bar{\Gamma}=P\rtimes H\rightrightarrows P
associated with the right action of
H
on
P
. The group of bisections
\mathcal{B}(\Gamma)
of
\Gamma
is the set of sections of
s
– i.e. maps
\sigma:P\rightarrow\Gamma
,
p\mapsto\sigma(p)=(p,\gamma\sim\psi)
s.t.
s\circ\sigma=\text{id}_{P}
– such that
t\circ\sigma:P\rightarrow P
is invertible: thus
t\circ\sigma\in\Diff(P)
. As a matter of fact, we here have indeed, for
\sigma\in\mathcal{B}(\Gamma)
, that
t\circ\sigma=\psi=R_{\gamma}\in\Diff_{v}(P)\simeq C^{\infty}(P,H)
.
The group law of bisections is defined as

\big(\sigma_{2}\star\sigma_{1}\big)(p)\vcentcolon=\sigma_{2}\big((t\circ\sigma_{1}(p)\big)\circ\sigma_{1}(p)
.
Composing this with the target map
t
on the left reproduces precisely the peculiar group law (
6
).
Thus, by a slight abuse of terminology, we may refer to
C^{\infty}(P,H)
as the group of bisections of the generalised action groupoid
\Gamma
associated to
P
. More simply, we may accurately just refer to it as the group of generating maps of
\Diff_{v}(P)
.

2.2
Linearisation and Lie algebraic structures

We now turn to the question of linearisation, i.e. of describing the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{diff}_{v}(P)
: Consider a 1-parameter element
\psi_{\tau}\in\Diff_{v}(P)
s.t.
\psi_{\tau}(p)=R_{\gamma_{\tau}(p)}p
with
\gamma_{\tau}\in C^{\infty}(P,H)
.
We have by definition,

\displaystyle\tfrac{d}{d\tau}\gamma_{\tau}(p)\,\big|_{\tau=0}=\vcentcolon X(p)\in\text{Lie}H,

(12)

and
X\in C^{\infty}(P,\text{Lie}H)=\Omega^{0}(P,\text{Lie}H)
.
By definition, an element of
\mathfrak{diff}_{v}(P)
is a
general
vertical vector field:

\displaystyle\tfrac{d}{d\tau}\psi_{\tau}(p)\,\big|_{\tau=0}

\displaystyle=\vcentcolon X(p)^{v}_{|p}\in V_{p}P,

(13)

\displaystyle=pX(p),

and
X^{v}\in\mathfrak{diff}_{v}(P)\simeq\Omega^{0}(P,VP)
.
Now,

\displaystyle\tfrac{d}{d\tau}\,\psi_{\tau}^{-1}\circ\psi_{\tau}(p)\,\big|_{\tau=0}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{d}{d\tau}\,\psi_{\tau}(p)\,\tilde{\gamma}_{\tau}\big(\psi_{\tau}(p)\big)\,\big|_{\tau=0},

\displaystyle 0\ \

\displaystyle=\tfrac{d}{d\tau}\,\psi_{\tau}(p)\,\big|_{\tau=0}\,\tilde{\gamma}_{0}\big(\psi_{0}(p)\big)+\psi_{0}(p)\,\tfrac{d}{d\tau}\,\tilde{\gamma}_{\tau}\big(\psi_{\tau}(p)\big)\,\big|_{\tau=0},

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\quad\tfrac{d}{d\tau}\,\tilde{\gamma}_{\tau}\big(\psi_{\tau}(p)\big)\,\big|_{\tau=0}

\displaystyle=-X(p)^{v}_{|p}.

(14)

The bracket of vertical vector fields (
13
) must be a vertical vector field itself. We want the expression of its generating element in
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(P,\text{Lie}H)
.
Consider
X^{v}
generated by
\psi_{\tau}\in\Diff_{v}(P)
with
X(p)=\tfrac{d}{d\tau}\gamma_{\tau}(p)\,\big|_{\tau=0}\in C^{\infty}(P,\text{Lie}H)
,
and
Y^{v}
generated by
\varphi_{\tau}\in\Diff_{v}(P)
with
Y(p)=\tfrac{d}{d\tau}\eta_{\tau}(p)\,\big|_{\tau=0}\in C^{\infty}(P,\text{Lie}H)
. Their bracket is:

\displaystyle\big[X(p)^{v},Y(p)^{v}\big]_{|p}\vcentcolon=

\displaystyle\,\tfrac{d}{d\tau}\tfrac{d}{ds}\,\psi_{\tau}^{-1}\circ\varphi_{s}\circ\psi_{\tau}(p)\,\big|_{s=0}\big|_{\tau=0},

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\tfrac{d}{d\tau}\tfrac{d}{ds}\,\psi_{\tau}(p)\cdot\eta_{s}\big(\psi_{\tau}(p)\big)\cdot\tilde{\gamma}_{\tau}\left(\varphi_{s}\big(\psi_{\tau}(p)\big)\right)\big|_{s=0}\big|_{\tau=0},\quad\text{by \eqref{comp-3},}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\tfrac{d}{d\tau}\bigg\{\psi_{\tau}(p)\,\underbrace{\tfrac{d}{ds}\,\eta_{s}\big(\psi_{\tau}(p)\big)\,\big|_{s=0}}_{Y\big(\psi_{\tau}(p)\big)}\cdot\underbrace{\tilde{\gamma}_{\tau}\bigg(\underbrace{\varphi_{0}\big(\psi_{\tau}(p)\big)}_{\psi_{\tau}(p)}\bigg)}_{\gamma_{\tau}(p)^{-1}\text{ by \eqref{inverse}}}\ +\ \psi_{\tau}(p)\cdot\underbrace{\eta_{0}\big(\psi_{\tau}(p)\big)}_{\text{id}_{H}}\,\underbrace{\tfrac{d}{ds}\,\tilde{\gamma}_{\tau}\left(\varphi_{s}\big(\psi_{\tau}(p)\big)\right)\big|_{s=0}}_{\big[Y^{v}(\tilde{\gamma}_{\tau})\big]\big(\psi_{\tau}(p)\big)}\bigg\}\big|_{\tau=0},

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\tfrac{d}{d\tau}\bigg\{p\,\text{Ad}\big(\gamma_{\tau}(p)\big)Y\big(\psi_{\tau}(p)\big)+\psi_{\tau}(p)\cdot\big[Y^{v}(\tilde{\gamma}_{\tau})\big]\big(\psi_{\tau}(p)\big)\bigg\}\big|_{\tau=0},

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,p\bigg\{\tfrac{d}{d\tau}\,\text{Ad}\big(\gamma_{\tau}(p)\big)Y(p)\,\big|_{\tau=0}+\text{Ad}\big(\underbrace{\gamma_{0}(p)}_{\text{id}_{H}}\big)\tfrac{d}{d\tau}\,Y\big(\psi_{\tau}(p)\big)\,\big|_{\tau=0}\,+\,\tfrac{d}{d\tau}\,\big[Y^{v}(\tilde{\gamma}_{\tau})\big]\big(\psi_{\tau}(p)\big)\,\big|_{\tau=0}\bigg\},

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,p\bigg\{\text{ad}\big(X(p)\big)Y(p)+\big[X^{v}(Y)\big](p)+\big[Y^{v}(-X)\big](p)\bigg\},

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!:[X(p),Y(p)]^{v}_{|p}+\big\{[X^{v}(Y)](p)\big\}^{v}_{|p}-\big\{[Y^{v}(X)](p)\big\}^{v}_{|p}.

Thus we obtain, for
X^{v},Y^{v}\in\mathfrak{diff}_{v}(P)
generated by
X,Y\in C^{\infty}(P,\text{Lie}H)
:

\displaystyle\big[X^{v},Y^{v}\big]=

\displaystyle\{X,Y\}^{v},

(15)

\displaystyle\text{with}\quad\{X,Y\}\vcentcolon=

\displaystyle[X,Y]_{\text{\tiny{Lie$H$}}}+X^{v}(Y)-Y^{v}(X).

The sought after generating element of the bracket of general vertical vector fields is then
\{X,Y\}\in C^{\infty}(P,\text{Lie}H)
.
This
extended
bracket
\{\ ,\,\}
, reflecting the recursive nested structure (

)-(

) on the elements of

is a Lie algebra: We have thus the Lie algebra isomorphism
\mathfrak{diff}_{v}(P)\simeq C^{\infty}(P,\text{Lie}H)
(respective brackets tacitly understood).
It follows in particular that the Lie derivative and inner product along

\displaystyle[L_{{X}^{v}},L_{{Y}^{v}}]

\displaystyle=L_{[{X}^{v},{Y}^{v}]}=L_{\{{X},{Y}\}^{v}},

\displaystyle[L_{{X}^{v}},\iota_{{Y}^{v}}]

Notice that for

\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle\Omega(X^{v},Y^{v})

\displaystyle=d\omega(X^{v},Y^{v})+[\omega(X^{v}),\omega(Y^{v})]_{\text{\tiny{Lie$H$}}},

\displaystyle=X^{v}\cdot\omega(Y^{v})-Y^{v}\cdot\omega(X^{v})-\omega([X^{v},Y^{v}])\ +\ [X,Y]_{\text{\tiny{Lie$H$}}},

\displaystyle=X^{v}(Y)-Y^{v}(X)-\omega(\{X,Y\}^{v})\ +\ [X,Y]_{\text{\tiny{Lie$H$}}}\equiv 0.

It indeed reproduces (

that

is indeed a Lie algebra morphism.

Quite intuitively, the action Lie algebroid

is the limit of the action Lie groupoid
\Gamma

is the Lie algebra of the group (of bisections)

In the next section, we show that the extended bracket (
15

P
, while the Lie derivative along
\mathfrak{diff}_{v}(P)
(

As we are about to see, the benefit of this viewpoint is that it nests this bracket within a general theory of derivations of the algebra of differential forms
\Omega^{\bullet}(P)
: As such, its links to the usual operations, such as the exterior derivative and inner product, are readily understood as simple instances of general results.

In the physics literature (e.g. on covariant phase space methods), some computational efforts are often spent on re-deriving various identities relating the extended bracket to the familiar Cartan calculus of forms.
On account of the viewpoint highlighted here, these efforts can be spared. One needs only to point to the established mathematical literature.

2.3
The Nijenhuis-Lie derivative and the Frölicher-Nijenhuis bracket on a principal bundle

As a manifold,

The

\displaystyle L:=[\iota,d]:\Omega^{\bullet}(P,TP)

\displaystyle\rightarrow\Der_{\bullet}\big(\Omega^{\bullet}(P)\big)

\displaystyle\mapsto L_{K}:=\iota_{K}d-(-)^{k-1}d\iota_{K}

(22)

Finite vertical and gauge transformations

\ \ 0\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \text{ for }\psi(p)=f(p)\vcentcolon=pu(p)\text{ with }\gamma=u:P\rightarrow H\text{ s.t. }R^{*}_{h}u=h^{-1}u\ \text{ (dressing field)}.\end{matrix}\right.

The second case involves the

,

, associated to the
dressing field

The map

\displaystyle\beta^{u}(X^{v},\ldots)\vcentcolon=

\displaystyle\,f^{*}\beta\,(X^{v}\ldots)=\beta(f_{*}X^{v},\ldots)=\beta(0,\ldots)=0,

\displaystyle R^{*}_{h}\,\beta^{u}\vcentcolon=

\displaystyle\,R^{*}_{h}\,f^{*}\beta=(f\circ R_{h})^{*}\beta=f^{*}\beta=\beta^{u}.

(40)

That is
\beta^{u}\in\Omega^{\bullet}_{\text{basic}}(P)

For example, a dressed connection is

\displaystyle(\beta^{u})^{\gamma}\vcentcolon=\psi^{*}(\beta^{u})=\psi^{*}(f^{*}\beta)=(f\circ\psi)^{*}\beta=f^{*}\beta\vcentcolon=\beta^{u},

(41)

as can also be found via (

Thus, dressed objects are in particular gauge invariant, i.e. invariant under

Returning to the generic case, (

), for
\omega\in\mathcal{C}

\displaystyle\omega^{\gamma}(X^{v})

\displaystyle=\psi^{*}\omega\,(X^{v})=\omega(\psi_{*}X^{v})=\text{Ad}_{\gamma^{-1}}X+\gamma^{-1}X^{v}(\gamma),\qquad\text{and}

(42)

\displaystyle R^{*}_{h}\omega^{\gamma}

\displaystyle=R^{*}_{h}\psi^{*}\omega=(\psi\circ R_{h})^{*}\omega.

(43)

In general then,

That is,

preserving the space of connections
\mathcal{C}
or
P

This is to be expected as

From above, it is obvious that the second special case in (

In the same way, for

\displaystyle\alpha^{\gamma}(X^{v})

\displaystyle=\psi^{*}\alpha\,(X^{v})=\alpha(\psi_{*}X^{v})=0,\qquad\text{and}

(44)

\displaystyle R^{*}_{h}\alpha^{\gamma}

\displaystyle=R^{*}_{h}\psi^{*}\alpha=(\psi\circ R_{h})^{*}\alpha.

So, if horizontality is preserved by the action of

This shows the importance of being mindful of the equivariance of

We stress that, a dressing map

Connections

.
But it also means that
\alpha
and

have the same generalised vertical transformations under
\Diff_{v}(P)\simeq C^{\infty}(P,H)
:

\displaystyle D\alpha\in\Omega^{\bullet+1}_{\text{tens}}(P,\rho)\quad\Rightarrow\quad(D\alpha)^{\gamma}=\rho(\gamma^{-1})D\alpha.

(46)

Yet, it is also the case that:

\displaystyle(D\alpha)^{\gamma}=\psi^{*}D\alpha=\psi^{*}\big(d\alpha+\rho_{*}(\omega)\alpha\big)

\displaystyle=d\psi^{*}\alpha+\rho_{*}(\psi^{*}\omega)\psi^{*}\alpha,

(47)

\displaystyle=\vcentcolon d\alpha^{\gamma}+\rho_{*}(\omega^{\gamma})\alpha^{\gamma}.

This goes to show that the covariant derivative
D
is compatible with the action of
\Diff_{v}(P)\simeq C^{\infty}(P,H)
, even if under its action it may be that
\omega^{\gamma}\notin\mathcal{C}
and
\alpha^{\gamma}\notin\Omega^{\bullet}_{tens}(P,\rho)
: In this case, if

, it is in the algebraic sense – dear to physicists – of preserving the form of the
\Diff_{v}(P)\simeq C^{\infty}(P,H)
transformation of

– by (

). If this transformation is instead a dressing operation, we have in particular that:

(D\alpha)^{u}=D^{u}\alpha^{u}=d\alpha^{u}+\rho_{*}(\omega^{u})\alpha^{u}
.

3.3
Infinitesimal vertical transformations

Naturally, the infinitesimal version of the general vertical transformation
\beta^{\gamma}=\psi^{*}\beta
for
\psi\in\Diff_{v}(P)\sim\gamma\in C^{\infty}(P,H)
is given by the Lie derivative along the associated general vertical vector field
X^{v}\in\mathfrak{diff}_{v}(P)\sim X\in C^{\infty}(P,\text{Lie}H)
, (
12
)-(
13
):

\displaystyle L_{X^{v}}\,\beta\vcentcolon=\tfrac{d}{d\tau}\,\beta^{\gamma_{\tau}}\,\big|_{|\tau=0}=\tfrac{d}{d\tau}\,\psi_{\tau}^{*}\,\beta\,\big|_{|\tau=0}.

(48)

The Lie derivative belongs to the Lie algebra of derivations of
\Omega^{\bullet}(P)
, it is the case that it is defined by
L_{X^{v}}=[\iota_{X^{v}},d]
(Cartan’s formula).
Since,
X^{v}
can be seen as an element of
\Omega^{0}(P,VP)
,
L_{X^{v}}
is to be understood as a special case of the Nijenhuis-Lie derivative along the vertical vector-valued
0
-form
X^{v}
.
Naturally then, by (
30
), the commutator of Nijenhuis-Lie derivatives involves the Frölicher-Nijehnhuis brachet on
\Omega^{0}(P,VP)
, i.e. the extended bracket
\{\,,\,\}
on
C^{\infty}(P,\text{Lie}H)
(
15
)-(
22
):

\displaystyle[L_{X^{v}},L_{Y^{v}}]\,\beta=L_{[X^{v},Y^{v}]_{\text{\tiny{FN}}}}\,\beta=L_{\{X,Y\}^{v}}\,\beta.

(49)

To illustrate, let us have the
\mathfrak{diff}_{v}(P)\simeq\Omega^{0}(P,VP)\sim\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(P,\text{Lie}H)
transformations of
\omega\in\mathcal{C}
and
\alpha\in\Omega^{\bullet}_{tens}(P,\rho)
.
For a connection, using (
35
), we get:

\displaystyle L_{X^{v}}\omega\vcentcolon=\tfrac{d}{d\tau}\,\psi_{\tau}^{*}\,\omega\,\big|_{|\tau=0}=\tfrac{d}{d\tau}\,\text{Ad}_{\gamma^{-1}_{\tau}}\omega+\gamma^{-1}_{\tau}d\gamma_{\tau}\,\big|_{|\tau=0}=-\text{ad}_{X}\omega+dX=DX.

(50)

This is cross-checked via Cartan’s formula, using Cartan’s structure equation for the curvature, and the latter’s tensoriality: For
\boldsymbol{X}\in\Gamma(TP)
, we have in general

\displaystyle L_{\boldsymbol{X}}\omega=\iota_{\boldsymbol{X}}d\omega+d(\iota_{\boldsymbol{X}}\omega)=\iota_{\boldsymbol{X}}\big(\Omega-\tfrac{1}{2}[\omega,\omega]\big)+d\big(\omega(\boldsymbol{X})\big)=\iota_{\boldsymbol{X}}\Omega+d\big(\omega(\boldsymbol{X})\big)+[\omega,\omega(\boldsymbol{X})].

(51)

Thus, for
\boldsymbol{X}=X^{v}
we get
L_{X^{v}}\omega=dX+[\omega,X]=DX
.
We must remain mindful that if
X\notin\Omega^{0}_{\text{tens}}(P,\text{Ad})=
Lie
\mathcal{H}
then
DX=dX+[\omega,X]\notin\Omega^{1}_{\text{tens}}(P,\text{Ad})
: only for
X^{v}\in\mathfrak{aut}_{v}(P)\sim X\in
Lie
\mathcal{H}
does

answers the geometric definition of the covariant derivative on tensorial forms.
This echoes the observation made earlier that
\omega^{\gamma}=\psi^{*}\omega\notin\mathcal{C}
for a generic
\psi\in\Diff_{v}(P)\sim\gamma\in C^{\infty}(P,H)
.

As
\mathcal{C}
is an affine space modeled on
\Omega^{1}_{\text{tens}}(P,\text{Ad})
, for
\omega^{\prime},\omega\in C
it must be that
\omega^{\prime}-\omega\in\Omega^{1}_{\text{tens}}(P,\text{Ad})
. So, in particular the linear action of a transformation group of
\mathcal{C}
would result in an element of
\Omega^{1}_{\text{tens}}(P,\text{Ad})
. As a matter of fact,
\Aut(P)
is the maximal such group: Indeed, for
\psi\in\Aut(P)
it is the case that,

\displaystyle\psi_{*}X(p)^{v}_{|p}\vcentcolon=\tfrac{d}{d\tau}\,\psi\big(\phi_{\tau}(p)\big)\,\big|_{\tau=0}=\tfrac{d}{d\tau}\,\psi\big(R_{\gamma_{\tau}(p)}\,p\big)\,\big|_{\tau=0}=\tfrac{d}{d\tau}\,R_{\gamma_{\tau}(p)}\psi(p)\,\big|_{\tau=0}=\vcentcolon X(p)^{v}_{|\psi(p)}.

(52)

So we have first
\psi^{*}\omega_{|p}(X^{v}_{|p})=\omega_{|\psi(p)}(X^{v}_{|\psi(p)})=X
, and second

R^{*}_{h}\psi^{*}\omega=\psi^{*}R^{*}_{h}\omega=\psi^{*}\text{Ad}_{h^{-1}}\omega=\text{Ad}_{h^{-1}}\psi^{*}\omega
: i.e.
\psi^{*}\omega\in\mathcal{C}
.
It follows indeed that
L_{\boldsymbol{X}}\omega\in\Omega^{1}_{\text{tens}}(P,\text{Ad})
for
\boldsymbol{X}\in\mathfrak{aut}(P)
, thus also for the special case
\boldsymbol{X}=X^{v}\in\mathfrak{aut}_{v}(P)
with explicit result
L_{X^{v}}\omega=DX\in\Omega^{1}_{\text{tens}}(P,\text{Ad})
.

Applying a second Nijenhuis-Lie derivative we get, using the fact that
[L_{\boldsymbol{X}},d]=0
:

\displaystyle L_{Y^{v}}L_{X^{v}}\omega=d(L_{Y^{v}}X)+[L_{Y^{v}}\omega,X]+[\omega,L_{Y^{v}}X]

\displaystyle=d\big(Y^{v}(X)\big)+[DY,X]+[\omega,Y^{v}(X)],

\displaystyle=D\big(Y^{v}(X)\big)+[dY,X]+\big[[\omega,Y],X\big]

From this follows that,

\displaystyle[L_{X^{v}},L_{Y^{v}}]\omega

\displaystyle=D\big(X^{v}(Y)\big)+[dX,Y]+\big[[\omega,X],Y\big]-D\big(Y^{v}(X)\big)-[dY,X]-\big[[\omega,Y],X\big],

\displaystyle=D\big(X^{v}(Y)\big)-D\big(Y^{v}(X)\big)+d\big([X,Y]\big)+\big[[\omega,X],Y\big]-\big[[\omega,Y],X\big],

\displaystyle=D\big(X^{v}(Y)\big)-D\big(Y^{v}(X)\big)+d\big([X,Y]\big)+\big[\omega,[X,Y]\big],

\displaystyle=D\big(\{X,Y\}\big)=L_{\{X,Y\}^{v}}\omega=L_{[X^{v},Y^{v}]_{\text{ \tiny{FN} }}}\omega,

(53)

by Jacobi identity in Lie
H
from the second to third line, and by definition of the extended/FN bracket (
15
)-(
2.3
) from the third to the last. This result is as expected from (
49
).

Similarly for a
\alpha\in\Omega^{\bullet}_{tens}(P,\rho)
we have,

\displaystyle L_{X^{v}}\alpha\vcentcolon=\tfrac{d}{d\tau}\,\psi_{\tau}^{*}\,\alpha\,\big|_{|\tau=0}=\tfrac{d}{d\tau}\,\rho(\gamma^{-1}_{\tau})\alpha\,\big|_{|\tau=0}=-\rho_{*}(X)\alpha.

(54)

This can be cross-checked via Cartan’s formula: For
\boldsymbol{X}\in\Gamma(TP)
, we have in general

\displaystyle L_{\boldsymbol{X}}\alpha

\displaystyle=\iota_{\boldsymbol{X}}d\alpha+d(\iota_{\boldsymbol{X}}\alpha)=\iota_{\boldsymbol{X}}\big(D\alpha-\rho_{*}(\omega)\alpha+d(\iota_{\boldsymbol{X}}\alpha)=\iota_{\boldsymbol{X}}D\alpha-\rho_{*}(\iota_{\boldsymbol{X}}\omega)\alpha+\rho_{*}(\omega)\iota_{\boldsymbol{X}}\alpha+d(\iota_{\boldsymbol{X}}\alpha)

\displaystyle=\iota_{\boldsymbol{X}}D\alpha+D(\iota_{\boldsymbol{X}}\alpha)-\rho_{*}(\iota_{\boldsymbol{X}}\omega)\alpha.

(55)

Thus, for
\boldsymbol{X}=X^{v}
we get
L_{X^{v}}\alpha=-\rho_{*}(X)\alpha
.
Here again, we notice that since generically

, generically
L_{X^{v}}\alpha\notin\Omega^{\bullet}_{tens}(P,\rho)
. Which echoes the earlier observation that
\alpha^{\gamma}=\psi^{*}\alpha\notin\Omega^{\bullet}_{tens}(P,\rho)
for a generic
\psi\in\Diff_{v}(P)\sim\gamma\in C^{\infty}(P,H)
. Upon applying the Nijenhuis-Lie derivative again we get,

\displaystyle L_{Y^{v}}L_{X^{v}}\alpha=-\rho_{*}(L_{Y^{v}}X)\alpha-\rho_{*}(X)L_{Y^{v}}\alpha=-\rho_{*}\big(Y^{v}(X)\big)\alpha+\rho_{*}(X)\rho_{*}(Y)\alpha.

Then it follows that,

\displaystyle[L_{X^{v}},L_{Y^{v}}]\alpha

\displaystyle=-\rho_{*}\big(X^{v}(Y)\big)\alpha+\rho_{*}(Y)\rho_{*}(X)\alpha+\rho_{*}\big(Y^{v}(X)\big)\alpha-\rho_{*}(X)\rho_{*}(Y)\alpha,

\displaystyle=-\rho_{*}\big([X,Y]+X^{v}(Y)-Y^{v}(X)\big)\alpha,

\displaystyle=-\rho_{*}\big(\{X,Y\}\big)\alpha=L_{\{X,Y\}^{v}}\alpha=L_{[X^{v},Y^{v}]_{\text{ \tiny{FN} }}}\alpha.

(56)

Which is as expected from (
49
).

P
exposed up to now descend as local structures on the base space

. These may be noteworthy for gauge field theory, as vertical transformations generalise the usual (active or passive) gauge transformations. A gauge argument still applies to them.

4
Local structure

We first summarize the standard local structure, and extend it to general vertical transformations in the next section.

4.1
Gluings and local active gauge transformations

Given a open subset
U\subset M
and a local section
\sigma:U\rightarrow P_{|U}
, a local representative of
\beta\in\Omega^{\bullet}(P)
is
b\vcentcolon=\sigma^{*}\beta\in\Omega^{\bullet}(U)
.
Any other local section is
s^{\prime}=sg
with
g:U\rightarrow H
a transition function of the bundle (encoding its topology, from a local viewpoint), so another local representative is
b^{\prime}\vcentcolon={\sigma^{\prime}}^{*}\beta=b^{g}
. The notation
b^{\prime}=b^{g}
is meant to indicate that
b^{\prime}
can be seen as obtained from
b
by a right action transformation by a transition function
g
.
For yet another local section
s^{\prime\prime}=s^{\prime}g^{\prime}=sgg^{\prime}
, we have that
b^{\prime\prime}=(b^{\prime})^{g^{\prime}}=b^{gg^{\prime}}
. These constitute the
gluing relations
of local representatives
5
5

5

The terminology stems from considering
s
,
s^{\prime}
and
s^{\prime\prime}
as local sections over overlapping opens
U
,
U^{\prime}
and
U^{\prime\prime}
respectively, related by the transition functions
g
and
g^{\prime}
. Then the gluing relations reflects the fact that the local representatives on
M
, the
b
’s, comes from the same global object
\beta
on
P
– which can be reconstructed from its local representatives.
and are known in gauge field theory as
passive gauge transformations
.

To illustrate, for
\beta=\omega\in\mathcal{C}
, we define
A\vcentcolon=\sigma^{*}\omega\,\in\mathcal{A}
(the gauge potential) and we have the well known result
A^{\prime}=\text{Ad}_{g^{-1}}A+g^{-1}dg=\vcentcolon A^{g}
, and
A^{\prime\prime}=\text{Ad}_{{g^{\prime}}^{-1}}A^{\prime}+{g^{\prime}}^{-1}dg^{\prime}=A^{gg^{\prime}}
.
In the case of
\beta=\alpha\in\Omega^{\bullet}_{\text{tens}}(P,\rho)
, one has
a\vcentcolon=\sigma^{*}\alpha\,\in\Omega^{\bullet}_{\text{tens}}(U,\rho)
and it is well-known that
a^{\prime}=\rho(g^{-1})\,a=\vcentcolon a^{g}
, and
a^{\prime\prime}=\rho({g^{\prime}}^{-1})\,a^{\prime}=\rho\big((gg^{\prime})^{-1}\big)\,a=a^{gg^{\prime}}
.
The local representative of the curvature
\Omega\in\Omega^{2}_{\text{tens}}(P,\text{Ad})
of
\omega
is
F\vcentcolon=\sigma^{*}\Omega
. As a special case of the above, we have then
F^{\prime}=\text{Ad}_{g^{-1}}F=\vcentcolon F^{g}
and
F^{\prime\prime}=\text{Ad}_{{g^{\prime}}^{-1}}F^{\prime}=F^{gg^{\prime}}
.
A matter field would be some
\alpha=\phi\in\Omega^{0}_{\text{tens}}(P,\rho)
with local representative
a=\upphi
. Its covariant derivative
D^{\omega}\phi=d\phi+\rho_{*}(\omega)\phi
has local representative
D^{A}\upphi=d\upphi+\rho_{*}(A)\upphi
, which represents the minimal coupling to the gauge potential.
It is well known that
(D^{A}\upphi)^{g}=D^{A^{g}}\upphi^{g}=\rho(g^{-1})D^{A}\upphi
, which expresses the requirement of the (passive)
gauge principle
.

Of course, the group
\Aut_{v}(P)
cannot act on local representatives, but there is naturally such a thing as the local representative of the gauge transformed
\beta^{\gamma}\vcentcolon=\psi^{*}\beta
, for
\gamma\in\mathcal{H}\simeq\psi\in\Aut_{v}(P)
.

Given the defining equivariance of elements of
\mathcal{H}
, making them tensorial 0-forms for the conjugate action of
H
, the
\Aut_{v}(P)\simeq\mathcal{H}
-transformation of
\eta\in\mathcal{H}
is – as a special case of the one discussed below (
37
) –
\eta^{\gamma}\vcentcolon=\psi^{*}\eta=\gamma^{-1}\eta\gamma
. This relation defines the action of gauge group
\mathcal{H}
on itself.
Defining
\upgamma\vcentcolon=\sigma^{*}\gamma
and
\upeta\vcentcolon=\sigma^{*}\eta
, we have
\upeta^{\upgamma}\vcentcolon=\sigma^{*}(\eta^{\gamma})=\upgamma^{-1}\upeta\upgamma
. Therefore, the local representative of
\mathcal{H}
, the
local gauge group
, is defined as:

\mathcal{H}_{\text{\tiny{loc}}}\vcentcolon=\{\upeta,\upgamma:U\rightarrow H\,|\,\upeta^{\upgamma}=\upgamma^{-1}\upgamma\upgamma\}
.

We have therefore the local representative of a gauge transformed form:
b^{\upgamma}\vcentcolon=\sigma^{*}(\beta^{\gamma})
.
Given that
(\beta^{\eta})^{\gamma}=\beta^{\eta\gamma}
, it is the case that
(b^{\upeta})^{\upgamma}=b^{\upeta\upgamma}
.
This secures the consistency of a heurisitic “field theoretic rule”: Considering
\upgamma
as acting on
b^{\upeta}
seen as a concatenation of the fields
b
and
\upeta
, one may write
(b^{\upeta})^{\upgamma}=(b^{\upgamma})^{\upeta^{\upgamma}}
, using then their respective
\mathcal{H}_{\text{\tiny{loc}}}
-transformations, one writes the concatenation as
(b^{\upgamma})^{\upeta^{\upgamma}}=(b^{\upgamma})^{\upgamma^{-1}\upeta\upgamma}=b^{\upgamma\,\upgamma^{-1}\upeta\upgamma}=b^{\upeta\upgamma}
.

The relation
(b^{\upeta})^{\upgamma}=b^{\upeta\upgamma}
expresses the fact that the action of the local gauge group
\mathcal{H}_{\text{\tiny{loc}}}
on local representatives – the fields of physics – is a right action.
Hence, the field space
\Phi=\{b\}
can be seen (under proper restrictions) as an infinite dimensional bundle with structure group

\mathcal{H}_{\text{\tiny{loc}}}
. A fact rarely stressed, or fully exploited, in the covariant phase space literature – see e.g.
[
1
;
21
;
20
;
19
;
11
]
.

To illustrate as above, we have that
A^{\upgamma}\vcentcolon=\sigma^{*}(\omega^{\gamma})=\text{Ad}_{\upgamma^{-1}}A+\upgamma^{-1}d\upgamma
. And
(A^{\upeta})^{\upgamma}=A^{\upeta\upgamma}
, as can be checked from using the previous definition and
(A^{\upeta})^{\upgamma}=(A^{\upgamma})^{\upeta^{\upgamma}}
.
Similarly for a tensorial form we have
a^{\upgamma}\vcentcolon=\sigma^{*}(\alpha^{\gamma})=\rho(\upgamma^{-1})\,a
, from which one checks that
(a^{\upeta})^{\upgamma}=a^{\upeta\upgamma}
via
(a^{\upeta})^{\upgamma}=(a^{\upgamma})^{\upeta^{\upgamma}}
.
As a special cases,
F^{\upgamma}\vcentcolon=\sigma^{*}(\Omega^{\gamma})=\text{Ad}_{\upgamma^{-1}}F
, and
\upphi^{\upgamma}\vcentcolon=\sigma^{*}(\phi^{\gamma})=\rho(\upgamma^{-1})\,\upphi
while its covariant derivative is

(D^{A}\upphi)^{\upgamma}\vcentcolon=\sigma^{*}\big((D^{\omega}\phi)^{\gamma}\big)=\rho(\upgamma^{-1})\,D^{A}\upphi
. Here, the local covariant derivative operator can be
defined
as the expression
D\vcentcolon=d\ +\rho_{*}(A)
.
It is then easily checked that the previous result is also recovered from the field theoretic rule:
(D^{A}\upphi)^{\upgamma}=D^{A^{\upgamma}}\upphi^{\upgamma}=\rho(\upgamma^{-1})\,D^{A}\upphi
, which is yet another expression of the gauge principle.

Notice that, from the standpoint of gauge field theory, there is no way to distinguish between local gluings
b^{g}
and the action
b^{\upgamma}
of

\mathcal{H}_{\text{\tiny{loc}}}
, i.e. between passive gauge transformations and local active gauge transformations. The “gauge principle” therefore actually encapsulates two gauge principles: a passive and an active one.
Yet, the conceptual meaning of each is quite different, as different as coordinate changes (a.k.a. passive diffeomorphisms) and (active) diffeomorphisms
\Diff(M)
are in GR.

A theory being given by a Lagrangian
L
, invariance under gluings,
L(b)=L(b^{g})
, mean that a gauge field theory is only sensitive to the intrinsic geometry of the bundle
P
, and to global object
\beta
living in it. Invariance under
\mathcal{H}_{\text{\tiny{loc}}}
,

L(b)=L(b^{\upgamma})
, means that a gauge field theory is sensitive only to the geometry of the
\Aut_{v}(P)
-class of
P
, i.e. to the
\mathcal{H}
-orbits of global objects.
In the same way that the combination of the hole argument and the point-coincidence argument
[
22
]
clarified that
\Diff(M)
-invariance in GR encodes the
relational
nature of spacetime, and of general relativistic physics more generally, arguably a combination of an “internal hole argument” and “internal point-coincidence argument” suggests that
\mathcal{H}_{\text{\tiny{loc}}}
-invariance encodes the
relational
character of the enriched spacetime, represented by a (class of) principal bundle, and of gauge field physics more generally. We will elaborate on this in a forthcoming work.

4.2
Linear versions

All of the above naturally have linear versions. Consider a transition function
g_{\tau}:U\rightarrow H
, so that
\sigma^{\prime}_{\tau}=\sigma g_{\tau}
, s.t.

The object
\lambda=\tfrac{d}{d\tau}g_{\tau}\,\big|_{\tau=0}:U\rightarrow

4.3
Generalised local active gauge transformations

Like

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we have detailed the global and local geometry arising from the group of vertical diffeomorphisms
\Diff_{v}(P)\simeq C^{\infty}(P,H)
of a principal bundle
P
. Notably, we have shown that its Lie algebra is realised by the Nijenhuis-Lie derivative: This shows that the extended bracket often encountered in the gauge field theory literature – mainly on gravity
[
7
;
8
]
and its asymptotic symmetries (BMS and extensions)
[
6
;
1
;
2
;
3
;
5
;
4
]

– not only can be seen as the bracket on the sections of the action Lie algebroid associated to
P
,
but also as an instance of the Frölicher-Nijenhuis bracket of vector-valued forms on
P
.

The action of
\Diff_{v}(P)\simeq C^{\infty}(P,H)
on forms of
P
defines generalised gauge transformations. They are distinct from standard gauge transformations induced by
\Aut_{v}(P)\simeq\mathcal{H}
, as can be seen when both are iterated.
This implies in particular that, on the base
M
, and contrary to local active gauge transformations (induced by
\mathcal{H}_{\text{\tiny{loc}}}
), local generalised active gauge transformations (induced by
C^{\infty}(U,H)

C^{\infty}(U,H)
or of
\mathcal{H}_{\text{\tiny{loc}}}
.
) are a priori distinct from local gluings.
Since the usual BRST framework
[
23

indistinguishably encodes both infinitesimal gluings and
Lie
\mathcal{H}_{\text{\tiny{loc}}}
,
one may inquire as to the possibility that it must be adjusted to accommodate generalised gauge transformations. It is known that BRST cohomology is just the Chevaley-Eilenberg (CE) cohomology of Lie
\mathcal{H}\simeq\mathfrak{aut}_{v}(\mathcal{P})
(or Lie
\mathcal{H}_{\text{\tiny{loc}}}

An extended BRST framework would probably just be the Lie algebroid cohomology of
C^{\infty}(P,\text{Lie}H)\simeq\mathfrak{diff}_{v}(P)
with coefficients in
\Omega^{\bullet}(P)
. We may address this question elsewhere.

As we observed, most basic treatments of bundle geometry do not mention
\Diff_{v}(P)
, or elaborate much on it.
A good reason for this could be that, since its elements generically do no commute with the right action of the structure group
H
, these are generically not bundle automorphisms: i.e. they are not morphisms in the category of principal bundles. They are not “relevant structures” from a categorical perspective.
Indeed, the action of
\Diff_{v}(P)
on objects well defined for that category, such as the spaces of connections, equivariant and tensorial forms, is “problematic”: it doesn’t preserve those spaces.

Still,
\Diff_{v}(P)
maps a bundle to itself by preserving the fibration – hence the well-defined action Lie groupoid and Lie algebroid viewpoint – so the local picture is less troublesome.
Indeed there is no issue for local gauge field theory, which can accommodate this extended notion of gauge transformations: A gauge argument still carries through, and the covariant derivative still does its job of preserving covariance under the action of
C^{\infty}(U,H)\supset\mathcal{H}_{\text{\tiny{loc}}}
.
In a companion paper
[
11

, we show how the the finite dimensional geometry exposed here may apply to the infinite dimensional bundle geometry of the field space
\Phi=\{b\}
of a gauge theory: further clarifications of the literature mentioned above ensue.

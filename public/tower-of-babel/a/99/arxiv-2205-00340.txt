Reliability and limitations of inferring birth radii in the Milky Way disk 2022 Reliability and limitations of inferring birth radii in the Milky Way disk – References
Yuxi(Lucy) Lu, Tobias Buck, Ivan Minchev, Melissa K. Ness

Abstract
Recovering the birth radii of observed stars in the Milky Way is one of the ultimate goals of Galactic Archaeology. One method to infer the birth radius and the evolution of the ISM metallicity assumes a linear relation between the ISM metallicity with radius at any given look-back time. Here we test the reliability of this assumption by using 4 zoom-in cosmological hydrodynamic simulations from the NIHAO-UHD project. We find that one can infer precise birth radii only when the stellar disk starts to form, which for our modeled galaxies happens ∼ \sim 10 Gyr ago, in agreement with recent estimates for the Milky Way. At later times the linear correlation between the ISM metallicity and radius increases, as stellar motions become more ordered and the azimuthal variations of the ISM metallicity start to drop. The formation of a central bar and perturbations from mergers can increase this uncertainty in the inner and outer disk, respectively.



1
Introduction

It is widely accepted that stars move away from their birth locations over time in the process commonly referred to as radial migration.
Radial migration was first suggested by
Grenon 1972
;
Grenon 1989
, in which they found old metal-rich stars in the solar neighborhood that exhibited kinematic and abundance properties consistent with those of the inner disk.
Stars can move away from their birth radii either because they have high eccentricities or due to a permanent change in their angular momentum.
The latter process is also referred to as radial migration or mixing.
Stars oscillating around their guiding radii can blur the intrinsic relations, however, this process can be accounted for by estimating the stellar guiding radius.
On the other hand, migrating stars are largely indistinguishable in their kinematics from stars born locally
(
Sellwood &
Binney 2002
;
Roškar et al. 2008
;
Minchev &
Famaey 2010
, e.g.)
.
Moreover, they can flatten the intrinsic abundance gradient in the disk significantly over time
(
Minchev
et al. 2013
, e.g. see Figure 5 from)
.

As a result, to truly understand the enrichment history of the Milky Way (MW), we need to connect the present-day observables to the birth properties of stars.
Without such knowledge, reconstructing MW’s formation history remains unreachable, and one can easily infer an incorrect enrichment history of the Galaxy. One example, pointed out in
Minchev
et al. 2018
, is if we attempt to infer the temporal evolution of the ISM metallicity gradient with stars in mono-age bins, assuming age is equivalent to look-back time, we would end up with a flatter ISM metallicity gradient evolution compared to the truth.

The most ambitious approach for recovering common birth environments of stars is perhaps that of chemical tagging
(
Freeman &
Bland-Hawthorn 2002
)
.
This method assumes stars that were born in the same cluster are chemically homogeneous, and that clusters can differentiated from one another given sufficiently precise and numerous individual abundance measurements from different nucloesynthetic channels.
Then, by grouping stars chemically, one can trace the stars back to their original birth aggregates in the Galaxy.
However, this method is a challenge for the current data

(
Bland-Hawthorn et al. 2010
;
Ness
et al. 2018
;
DeSilva
et al. 2007
;
Manea
et al. 2022
;
Nelson et al. 2021
;
Ting &
Weinberg 2022
;
Ratcliffe et al. 2020
;
Ness et al. 2022
, e.g.)
.
Nevertheless, even with the currently available abundance measurements, we can still hope to obtain a subset of birth properties of stars with sensible assumptions, one of which is the birth radius of a star.

Minchev
et al. 2018
presented a largely model-independent approach for estimating both stellar birth radii, R
birth
, and the Galactic ISM metallicity evolution with time and radius, [Fe/H](t, R), using metallicity and age estimates from the
local HARPS sample
(
Adibekyan et al. 2012
)
. The method relied on the following assumptions: (1) the gas is well mixed azimuthally, (2) stars are born from a narrow metallicity range at a certain radius at any given time, (3) the ISM [Fe/H](t, R) evolved smoothly with both radius and time, and (4) the MW disk formed inside-out.
Feltzing
et al. 2020
concluded the method shows promising results by inferring R
birth
with APOGEE DR14 data and comparing the resulted R
birth
distributions to those in a hydrodynamic simulation from
Agertz
et al. 2021
.
Other derivations of the radial migration strength can be found in the works by, e.g.,
Frankel et al. 2019
;
Frankel et al. 2020
;
Lian
et al. 2022
.

Although the method described in
Minchev
et al. 2018
has shown to be successful, the information about birth radius in the MW is lost, and therefore, the inferred R
birth
from data cannot be truly validated.
Moreover, inferring a star’s birth radius with its metallicity and age requires a correlation between the ISM [Fe/H] and R at the time when the star was born (look-back time = age of the star).
In this letter, we will show that precise R
birth
can only be inferred when a rotationally supported stellar disk is starting to form.
We use MW-like cosmological zoom-in simulations from NIHAO-UHD
(
Buck 2020
;
Buck et al. 2020
)
, in which we have access to the enrichment history and R
birth
of stars.
We will do this by linking the evolution of ISM metallicity, the orderliness of the gas-particle orbits, mergers, and the existence of a stellar bar with the intrinsic uncertainty on inferring R
birth
.

In section
3.1
, we investigate at what time does the correlation between the ISM metallicity and R is strong enough to infer reliable R
birth
.
Once the correlation is established, the dispersion around the metallicity – R linear relation can still lead to intrinsic uncertainty in inferring R
birth
.
This scatter can be created from the azimuthal variation of ISM metallicity caused by satellite infall, the spiral arms, the bar, or possibly other perturbations
(
Bellardini
et al. 2021
;
Spitoni
et al. 2019
;
Sánchez-Menguiano et al. 2020
, e.g.)
.
We examine the connection between the ISM metallicity gradient, azimuthal ISM metallicity variation, the orderliness of the gas-particle orbits, and the uncertainty of inferring R
birth
, assuming a linear relation between the ISM metallicity and R at all times in section
3.2
.
Finally, we discuss the implication for inferring R
birth
in the MW in section
4
.

2
Data & Methods

2.1
Simulated Data

The simulated galaxies used in this work were first presented by
(
Buck et al. 2018
)
.
They are taken from a suite of high-resolution cosmological hydrodynamical simulations of MW-mass galaxies from the NIHAO-UHD project
(
Buck et al. 2020
)
.
A modified version of the smoothed particle hydrodynamics (SPH) solver GASOLINE2
(
Wadsley
et al. 2017
)
was used to calculate the simulations from cosmological initial conditions. Star formation and feedback (both energetic and chemical elements) are modeled following the prescriptions in
(
Stinson et al. 2006
;
Stinson
et al. 2013
)
.
The simulations used in this work trace oxygen and iron separately
(see
Buck
et al. 2021
, for a recent udate to the chemical enrichment)
and adopted a metal diffusion algorithm between particles as described in
Wadsley et al. 2008
.
The parameters for simulations used in this letter are taken from
Buck et al. 2020
.
The numbers in the naming represent the total Dark Matter (DM) halo mass in unites of solar mass.
The time separation between every two consecutive output snapshots is
\sim
200 Myr, and all of the galaxies have been integrated until redshift 0.
For more information on the simulation details and galaxy properties, we refer the readers to
Buck et al. 2020
.

3
Results

To infer the birth radius for a star from its [Fe/H] and stellar age alone, a correlation between the ISM metallicity and R at a look-back time equal to the stellar age of the star is required.
However, as shown in simulations
(
Gurvich
et al. 2022
;
Bird et al. 2013
;
Bellardini et al. 2022
, e.g.)
, observations of high-redshift galaxies
(
Ciucă et al. 2021
;
Wisnioski
et al. 2015
;
Übler
et al. 2019
;
Elmegreen et al. 2007
, e.g.)
and observations of the MW
(
Belokurov &
Kravtsov 2022
;
Conroy
et al. 2022
, e.g.)
, hot, turbulent gas is likely to be common in early galaxy formation.
This means a correlation between the ISM metallicity and R at early times may not exist.
Similarly, as pointed out in
Minchev
et al. 2018
, if the MW started with a flat gradient, their method would fail for stars
\gtrsim
10 Gyr.

However, once the frequent mergers/accretions have subsided and a well ordered stellar disk begins to form, the |PCC| stays above 0.7 for all galaxies

3.2
ISM metallicity gradient and azimuthal variation, circularisation of the orbits, and the uncertainty on R
birth

How well does a straight line fit the metallicity gradient at all times, and what uncertainty will that bring in inferring R
birth
?
The answer lies within Figure
2
.
The top plot shows the metallicity gradient evolution over time, and the solid lines display the smoothed version of the points, using a 1D Gaussian filter after the disk has started to form for each galaxy.
The slopes are calculated by fitting a line between the gas particles’ metallicity and R within 25 kpc of the galaxy.
The red dashed line shows a natural log function to guide the eye.

Minchev
et al. 2018
derived a natural-log functional form for the metallicity gradient evolution, and it fits well with most of the galaxies soon after their disks have begun to form.
As pointed out earlier, the g6.96e11 galaxy has frequent mergers/infalls at low redshift, causing a more chaotic ISM evolution.
The metallicity dispersion ([Fe/H]
disp
) around the linear relation between the ISM metallicity and R at each snapshot is shown in the middle plot.
Similar to the conclusion from section
3.1
, the ISM is more turbulent at earlier times and thus, the |PCC| between ISM [Fe/H] and R is low, and [Fe/H]
disp
is high.
However, as the stellar disk started to form, [Fe/H]
disp
settled to
<
0.1 dex.

We can also estimate the intrinsic uncertainty in inferring R
birth
,
\sigma_{Rb}
, assuming 1) we know the linear fits of the ISM metallicity at a given look-back time, and that 2) there is no error on the observables ([Fe/H] and age; see the bottom plot of Figure
2
).
The intrinsic uncertainty is estimated using
\sigma_{Rb}
= [Fe/H]
disp
/(|
d
[Fe/H]
ISM
/
d
R|).
It is clear that even with perfectly measured observables, the intrinsic scattering around the ISM metallicity evolution, assuming a linear relation between the ISM [Fe/H] and R at all times, yields an uncertainty on the R
birth
measurement to be
\gtrsim
2 kpc for all 4 simulated galaxies.
For the MW, based on the estimation of the current metallicity gradient of
\sim
-0.05 dex/kpc with a dispersion of 0.1 dex derived from Cepheids
(
Luck 2018
)
, the intrinsic uncertainty would be
\sigma_{\rm Rbirth}
\sim
2 kpc, which is comparable with the simulated galaxies.
However, as pointed out by
(
Luck 2018
)
, the intrinsic dispersion of 0.1 dex is smaller than the measurement uncertainty of 0.14 dex, implying the actual dispersion can be smaller.
We also note that the metallicity gradient in the Galaxy is still not well constrained
(

et al. 2022

Eilers
et al. 2022

The metallicity variation increases towards the outer disk, and can also be affected by the bar, agreeing with findings from other simulations and chemical evolution models

suggest when the stellar disk begins to form, the orbits transition rapidly into a more ordered configuration and the ISM metallicity azimuthal variation also started to decrease.
The rapid increase in
<v_{\phi}>/\sigma_{v\phi}
and the decrease of ISM metallicity azimuthal variation shows a fast transition from the turbulent gas phase to the quiesent disk phase
(
Gurvich
et al. 2022

.
This rapid phase transition also leads to a well-established metallicity gradient with a high correlation (|PCC|
>

This means, after the rapid increase in the orderliness of the gas-particle orbits, a star formed from the ISM has a well-constraint metallicity at a certain radius, meaning we can infer birth radii with smaller
\sigma_{\rm Rbirth}
(Figure

However, late mergers can induce turbulence in gas, and therefore, increase
\sigma_{\rm[Fe/H]}
and
\sigma_{\rm Rbirth}
in mostly the outer galaxy, c.f. dispersion in
<v_{\phi}>/\sigma_{v\phi}

The bar can also induce variation in metallicity in the inner galaxy.
As a result, the uncertainty in inferred R
birth
for the inner Galaxy after the formation of the bar, and the outer Galaxy during a time of merger could be higher.

4
Conclusions and Discussion of the implications for the MW

By using MW-like simulations from the NIHAO-UHD project, we are able to connect the phases of galaxy formation with the possibility of inferring birth radii of stars. In particular, this letter shows:

•

Precise birth radii can be inferred from stellar ages, guiding center radii, and metallicities only once the stellar disk starts to form -
\sim
10 Gyr ago for the MW
(
Belokurov &
Kravtsov 2022
)
.

•

At later times the linear correlation between the ISM metallicity and R increases, as stellar motions become more ordered and the azimuthal variations of the ISM metallicity decrease by an order of magnitude (see Figure
1
and Figure
3
).

•

A central bar and perturbations from mergers can increase the intrinsic uncertainty of inferred R
birth
in the inner and outer disk by up to 50%, respectively (see Figure
3
).

•

Considering the observed present-day ISM metallicity gradient, derived from Cepheids, of -0.05 dex/kpc with a scatter of 0.1 dex
(
Luck 2018
)
, the minimum intrinsic uncertainty on inferring R
birth
in the MW is
\sim
2 kpc.
However, as pointed out in
Luck 2018
, this scatter is smaller than the measurement uncertainty (0.14 dex), and therefor, the true scatter and the intrinsic uncertainty is likely to be smaller than 2 kpc.

However, in this study, we only investigated a linear relation between the ISM metallicity and R.
Future work should investigate the possibility that other functional forms exist, that better describe this correlation.
We also only used the ISM gradient derived from Cepheids and other studies have found slightly different gradients ranging from around -0.05 to -0.07 dex/kpc
(
Anders
et al. 2017
;
Maciel &
Andrievsky 2019
, e.g.)
.
Studies derive local metallicity gradient using neutral gas and HII regions have found a slightly shallower gradient of
\sim
-0.04 dex/kpc
(
De Cia
et al. 2021
;
Esteban et al. 2022
, e.g.)
.
Most of the studies either do not report the metallicity intrinsic dispersion around the relations or have a scatter that is on the order of the measurement uncertainty.
As a result, the intrinsic uncertainty on R
birth
for the MW is not constrained.
We also want to point out that the evolution of the simulated galaxies may not match exactly with that of the MW.
One discrepancy is that the disk seems to form earlier for the MW compared to simulations
(
Belokurov &
Kravtsov 2022
, see also)
, which could indicate missing/incorrect physics in the simulations.
Future work is needed to further investigate this discrepancy.

Chemical sensing with graphene: A quantum field theory perspective
Horacio Falomir, Marcelo Loewe, Enrique Muñoz

Abstract
We studied theoretically the effect of a low concentration of adsorbed polar molecules on the optical conductivity of graphene, within the Kubo linear response approximation. Our analysis is based on a continuum model approximation that includes up to next to nearest neighbors in the pristine graphene effective Hamiltonian, thus extending the field-theoretical analysis developed in Refs. Falomir et al. 2018 ; Falomir et al. 2020 . Our results show that the conductivity can be expressed in terms of renormalized quasiparticle parameters v ~ F \tilde{v}_{F} , M ~ \tilde{M} and μ ~ \tilde{\mu} that include the effect of the molecular surface concentration n d ​ i ​ p n_{dip} and dipolar moment 𝓟 \bm{\mathcal{P}} , thus providing an analytical model for a graphene-based chemical sensor.

Scattering by randomly adsorbed molecules

We assume for simplicity that the electric properties of a single molecule adsorbed at a distance
a>0
above the surface of graphene, can be modeled through a dipole potential, with two point charges
+Q
and
-Q

(\mathbf{d}/2,a)
and
(-\mathbf{d}/2,a)

\mathbf{d}
a two-dimensional vector. The correspondig potential at a position
\mathbf{r}=(x,y,0)
on the
surface of graphene (
z=0

\displaystyle V_{\mathbf{d}}(\mathbf{r})

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{P}}{4\pi\epsilon d}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{d}/2)^{2}+a^{2}}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{(\mathbf{r}+\mathbf{d}/2)^{2}+a^{2}}}\right)

(2)

\bm{\mathcal{P}}=Q\mathbf{d}

d\rightarrow 0

\bm{\mathcal{P}}

A

\displaystyle\hat{V}_{\bm{\mathcal{P}}}(\mathbf{q})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{d\rightarrow 0}\hat{V}_{\mathbf{d}}(\mathbf{q})=\frac{i\bm{\mathcal{P}}\cdot\mathbf{q}}{2\epsilon q}e^{-qa}

(3)

Let us now consider the effect of scattering by adsorbed polar molecules, randomly distributed with a surface concentration
n_{dip}
, on the
optical conductivity of graphene. For this purpose, we first analyze their effect on the
effective propagators arising from the Lagrangian Eq. (
1

\displaystyle V(\mathbf{r})=\sum_{j=1}^{N}V_{\bm{d}_{j}}(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{R}_{j}).

(4)

The basic assumptions are that the positions
\mathbf{R}_{j}
,
as well as the orientations of the dipolar moments
\bm{\mathcal{P}}_{j}
are independent and identically distributed random variables, i.e.

\displaystyle\langle\mathbf{R}_{i}\cdot\mathbf{R}_{j}\rangle=\delta_{ij}\langle\mathbf{R}^{2}\rangle,\,\,\langle\bm{\mathcal{P}}_{i}\cdot\bm{\mathcal{P}}_{j}\rangle=\delta_{ij}\frac{\mathcal{P}^{2}}{2},

(5)

Using standard diagrammatic methods and average over disorder

B

\displaystyle\hat{\Sigma}(i\omega_{n},\mathbf{p})=n_{dip}\int\frac{d^{2}q}{\left(2\pi\right)^{2}}|\hat{V}_{avg}(\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{q})|^{2}\hat{\Delta}_{E}(i\omega_{n},\mathbf{q}).

while the Fourier transform of the dipole potential, averaged

\displaystyle|\hat{V}_{avg}(\mathbf{p})|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{e^{2}\mathcal{P}^{2}}{4\epsilon^{2}}e^{-2pa}.

(8)

\displaystyle\hat{\Delta}_{E0}^{-1}(i\omega_{n},\mathbf{p})=\left(i\omega_{n}+\mu-\frac{\mathbf{p}^{2}}{2M}\right)\mathbf{1}-v_{F}\bm{\sigma}\cdot\mathbf{p},

Conductivity

From the Kubo linear response theory, the optical conductivity
tensor is given by the expression
Falomir et al. 2018
;
Falomir et al. 2020

\displaystyle\sigma_{kl}(\omega)=4\times\left.\frac{\Pi_{kl}^{R}(p)}{i\omega}\right|_{p=(\omega,0)}

(16)

The retarded component of the polarization tensor is obtained via
analytic continuation from the Euclidean, following the standard prescription
\Pi_{kl}^{R}(\omega,\mathbf{p})=\Pi_{kl}^{E}(i\omega_{n}\rightarrow\omega+i\epsilon,\mathbf{p})
.

The Euclidean polarization tensor, within the quasiparticle approximation, is given by
Falomir et al. 2018
;
Falomir et al. 2020

\displaystyle\Pi_{kl}^{E}(i\omega_{n},\mathbf{p})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{2}}{4\tilde{M}^{2}}\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{q_{4}=\omega_{n},n\in\mathbb{Z}}\int\frac{d^{2}q}{(2\pi)^{2}}\Gamma_{ab}^{k}(p+2q)

(17)

\displaystyle\times\tilde{\Delta}_{bc}^{E}(p+q)\Gamma_{cd}^{l}(p+2q)\tilde{\Delta}_{da}^{E}(q),

where the integral involves the quasiparticle propagators defined by Eq.(
15
), and we defined the matrices
Falomir et al. 2018

\displaystyle\Gamma_{ab}^{k}(p)=\left[\delta_{ab}p^{k}+2\tilde{M}\tilde{v}_{F}\left[\sigma^{k}\right]_{ab}\right].

(18)

Following the analysis in our previous work
Falomir et al. 2018
;
Falomir et al. 2020
, we obtain
that the tensor is symmetric and diagonal,
\sigma_{11}=\sigma_{22}
,
\sigma_{12}=\sigma_{21}=0
. In particular,
for the real part of the optical conductivity that can be measured in electronic transport experiments, we obtain the
analytical expression
Falomir et al. 2020

\displaystyle\text{Re}\,\sigma_{11}(\omega,T)=\frac{e^{2}}{8\hbar}{\rm sign}(\omega)\left(\tanh\left[\frac{1}{2k_{B}T}\left(\frac{\hbar^{2}\omega^{2}}{8\tilde{M}\tilde{v}_{F}^{2}}+\frac{\hbar\omega}{2}-\tilde{\mu}\right)\right]-\tanh\left[\frac{1}{2k_{B}T}\left(\frac{\hbar^{2}\omega^{2}}{8\tilde{M}\tilde{v}_{F}^{2}}-\frac{\hbar\omega}{2}-\tilde{\mu}\right)\right]\right)

(19)

We notice that the zero temperature limit of the former expression reduces to the universal value
e^{2}/(4\hbar)
, as follows
Falomir et al. 2020

\displaystyle\text{Re}\,\sigma_{11}(\omega,T\rightarrow 0)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{e^{2}}{4\hbar},&\frac{\hbar|\omega|}{2|\tilde{M}|\tilde{v}_{F}^{2}}>1-\sqrt{1-\frac{2\tilde{\mu}}{|\tilde{M}|\tilde{v}_{F}}},\\
0,&{\text{otherwise}}\end{array}\right.

Conclusion

Based on a quantum field theory description, we obtained a model for the optical conductivity of graphene
at different concentrations of adsorbed polar molecules. Our analytical results show that from electric transport measurements at finite
frequency, it is possible to read the shift
in the conductivity curve, and hence to infer the numerical
values of the corresponding renormalized parameters
\tilde{\mu}
,
\tilde{M}
and
\tilde{v}_{F}
. By comparing these
values with the bare graphene parameters
M
and
v_{F}
, as well as with the actual chemical potential
\mu
,
using the analytical Eq. (
13
) and Eq. (
14
)
the concentration of adsorbed molecules
n_{dip}
can be estimated.

Appendix A

In this Appendix, we present the basic considerations
involved in the disorder averaging that leads to the NCA approximation applied in this work. This analysis closely follows
Rammer and Smith 1986
.
We start by considering the potential experienced at site
\mathbf{r}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, due to the presence of a
collection of
N
dipolar molecules adsorbed on the graphene
surface. The position of the center of mass of each molecule is at
(\mathbf{R}_{j},a)
, with
a>0
the height with respect to the graphene surface (at
z=0
) and
\mathbf{R}_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
the position over the plane,
for
1\leq j\leq N
. The corresponding potential is

\displaystyle V\left(\mathbf{r}\right)=\sum_{j=1}^{N}V_{\bm{d}_{j}}\left(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{R}_{j}\right),

(22)

where, as displayed in the main text, the potential for each dipole is

\displaystyle V_{\mathbf{d}_{j}}(\mathbf{r})=\frac{\mathcal{P}}{4\pi\epsilon d}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{(\mathbf{r}-\frac{\mathbf{d}_{j}}{2})^{2}+a^{2}}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{(\mathbf{r}+\frac{\mathbf{d}_{j}}{2})^{2}+a^{2}}}\right)

The 2D Fourier transform of this potential is given by the expression

\displaystyle\hat{V}_{\mathbf{d}_{j}}(\mathbf{q})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}d^{2}re^{i\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{r}}V_{\mathbf{d}_{j}}(\mathbf{r})

(24)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{i\mathcal{P}\sin\left(\frac{\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{d}_{j}}{2}\right)}{2\pi\epsilon d}\int_{0}^{\infty}dr\frac{r}{\sqrt{r^{2}+a^{2}}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\phi e^{iqr\cos(\phi)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{i\mathcal{P}\sin\left(\frac{\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{d}_{j}}{2}\right)}{\epsilon d}\int_{0}^{\infty}dr\frac{rJ_{0}(qr)}{\sqrt{r^{2}+a^{2}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{i\mathcal{P}\sin\left(\frac{\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{d}_{j}}{2}\right)}{\epsilon qd}e^{-qa}

In the strict dipole approximation, i.e.
d\rightarrow 0
but
\mathcal{P}_{j}
finite,
the Fourier transform reduces to the simpler expression

\displaystyle\lim_{d\rightarrow 0}\hat{V}_{\mathbf{d}_{j}}(\mathbf{q})\equiv\hat{V}_{\bm{\mathcal{P}}_{j}}(\mathbf{q})=\frac{i\mathbf{q}\cdot\bm{\mathcal{P}}_{j}}{2\epsilon q}e^{-qa}

(25)

With this result, the 2D Fourier transform of the potential is given by

\displaystyle\hat{V}(\mathbf{q})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}d^{2}re^{i\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{r}}V(\mathbf{r})

(26)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}d^{2}re^{i\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{r}}V_{\bm{d}_{j}}(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{R}_{j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}e^{i\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{R}_{j}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}d^{2}re^{i\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{r}}V_{\bm{d}_{j}}(\mathbf{r})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}\hat{V}_{\bm{d}_{j}}(\mathbf{q})e^{i\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{R}_{j}}\xrightarrow{d\rightarrow 0}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\hat{V}_{\bm{\mathcal{P}}_{j}}(\mathbf{q})e^{i\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{R}_{j}}

We shall assume that the positions of the adsorbed molecules
over the graphene surface, i.e. the set
\{\mathbf{R}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
, as well as the orientation of the dipole
moments
\{\bm{\mathcal{P}}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
are independent and identically distributed random variables. Therefore,
writing
\bm{\mathcal{P}}_{j}=\mathcal{P}\left(\cos(\varphi_{j}),\sin(\varphi_{j})\right)
,
with
\varphi_{j}\in[0,2\pi]
a uniformly distributed random variable

\displaystyle\langle\bm{\mathcal{P}}_{j}\rangle=\mathcal{P}\left(\langle\cos(\varphi_{j})\rangle,\langle\sin(\varphi_{j})\rangle\right)=0

(27)

\displaystyle\langle\hat{V}_{\bm{\mathcal{P}}_{j}}(\mathbf{q})\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{ie^{-qa}}{2\epsilon q}\langle\mathbf{q}\cdot\bm{\mathcal{P}}_{j}\rangle

(28)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{ie^{-qa}}{2\epsilon q}\mathbf{q}\cdot\langle\bm{\mathcal{P}}_{j}\rangle=0.

Similarly, for
\mathbf{q}_{1}=q_{1}\left(\cos(\phi_{1}),\sin(\phi_{1})\right)

and
\mathbf{q}_{2}=q_{2}\left(\cos(\phi_{2}),\sin(\phi_{2})\right)

\displaystyle\langle\hat{V}_{\bm{\mathcal{P}}_{j}}(\mathbf{q}_{1})\hat{V}_{\bm{\mathcal{P}}_{j^{\prime}}}(\mathbf{q}_{2})\rangle=\frac{i^{2}e^{-(q_{1}+q_{2})a}}{4\epsilon^{2}q_{1}q_{2}}

(29)

\displaystyle\times\langle\mathbf{q}_{1}\cdot\bm{\mathcal{P}}_{j}\mathbf{q}_{2}\cdot\bm{\mathcal{P}}_{j^{\prime}}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{i^{2}e^{-(q_{1}+q_{2})a}\mathcal{P}^{2}}{4\epsilon^{2}}\left(\cos\phi_{1}\cos\phi_{2}\langle\cos\varphi_{j}\cos\varphi_{j^{\prime}}\rangle\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\sin\phi_{1}\sin\phi_{2}\langle\sin\varphi_{j}\sin\varphi_{j^{\prime}}\rangle+\cos\phi_{1}\sin\phi_{2}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\times\langle\cos\varphi_{j}\sin\varphi_{j^{\prime}}\rangle+\sin\phi_{1}\cos\phi_{2}\langle\cos\varphi_{j}\sin\varphi_{j^{\prime}}\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{i^{2}e^{-(q_{1}+q_{2})a}\mathcal{P}^{2}}{8\epsilon^{2}}\delta_{j,j^{\prime}}\left(\cos\phi_{1}\cos\phi_{2}+\sin\phi_{1}\sin\phi_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\delta_{j,j^{\prime}}\frac{i^{2}e^{-(q_{1}+q_{2})a}\mathcal{P}^{2}}{8\epsilon^{2}}\frac{\mathbf{q}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{q}_{2}}{q_{1}q_{2}}

Generalizing this procedure, it is straightforward to
show that the average of the product of an odd number
of potential terms is identically zero, since

\displaystyle\langle\left(\cos(\varphi_{j})\right)^{2n+1}\rangle=\langle\left(\sin(\varphi_{j})\right)^{2n+1}\rangle=0

(30)

Similarly, for the average over the molecular positions
\mathbf{R}_{j}
, we have

\displaystyle\langle e^{i\left(\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{q}^{\prime}\right)\cdot\mathbf{R}_{j}}\rangle=\frac{1}{A}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}d^{2}R_{j}e^{i\left(\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{q}^{\prime}\right)\cdot\mathbf{R}_{j}}=\frac{(2\pi)^{2}}{A}\delta\left(\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{q}^{\prime}\right)

Let us now consider the Lippmann-Schwinger series, in momentum space, for the scattering process across a given realization of the ensemble of adsorbed molecules,

\displaystyle\hat{G}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{p^{\prime}};\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{2}\delta(\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}^{\prime})\hat{\Delta}_{0}^{R}(\mathbf{p},\omega)

(32)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\hat{\Delta}_{0}^{R}(\mathbf{p},\omega)e\hat{V}\left(\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}^{\prime}\right)\hat{\Delta}_{0}^{R}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\omega)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{2}p^{\prime\prime}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\hat{\Delta}_{0}^{R}(\mathbf{p},\omega)e\hat{V}\left(\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}^{\prime\prime}\right)\hat{\Delta}_{0}^{R}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime\prime},\omega)

\displaystyle\times e\hat{V}\left(\mathbf{p}^{\prime\prime}-\mathbf{p}^{\prime}\right)\hat{\Delta}_{0}^{R}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\omega)+\ldots

We can directly verify that, after performing a statistical
average over the disordered distribution of positions and
dipole orientations, the translational invariance of the
Green’s function is recovered. For instance, for the first-order term, we have

\displaystyle\langle\hat{G}^{(1)}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{p^{\prime}};\omega)\rangle=\hat{\Delta}_{0}^{R}(\mathbf{p},\omega)\langle\hat{V}\left(\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}^{\prime}\right)\rangle\hat{\Delta}_{0}^{R}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\omega)

(33)

Notice that the disorder average of the potential, under
the assumptions of statistical independence of the
parameters, is

\displaystyle\langle\hat{V}\left(\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}^{\prime}\right)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}\langle e^{i(\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}^{\prime})\cdot\mathbf{R}_{j}}\rangle\langle\hat{V}_{\bm{\mathcal{P}}_{j}}(\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}^{\prime})\rangle

(34)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{N}{A}(2\pi)^{2}\delta(\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}^{\prime})\langle\hat{V}_{\bm{\mathcal{P}}_{j}}(0)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0

Therefore, clearly this first correction vanishes after averaging over dipole orientations. For the second order contribution, we have

\displaystyle\langle\hat{G}^{(2)}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{p}^{\prime};\omega)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{2}p^{\prime\prime}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\hat{\Delta}_{0}^{R}(\mathbf{p},\omega)\hat{\Delta}_{0}^{R}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime\prime},\omega)\hat{\Delta}_{0}^{R}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\omega)

(35)

\displaystyle\times e^{2}\langle\hat{V}(\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}^{\prime\prime})\hat{V}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime\prime}-\mathbf{p}^{\prime})\rangle

Here, the disorder average of the product of potential
factors is

\displaystyle\langle e^{2}\hat{V}(\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}^{\prime\prime})\hat{V}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime\prime}-\mathbf{p}^{\prime})\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{j^{\prime}=1}^{N}\langle e^{i(\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}^{\prime\prime})\cdot\mathbf{R}_{j}}e^{i(\mathbf{p}^{\prime\prime}-\mathbf{p}^{\prime})\cdot\mathbf{R}_{j^{\prime}}}\langle e^{2}\hat{V}_{\bm{\mathcal{P}}_{j}}(\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}^{\prime\prime})\hat{V}_{\bm{\mathcal{P}}_{j^{\prime}}}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime\prime}-\mathbf{p}^{\prime})\rangle

(36)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}\langle e^{i(\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}^{\prime})\cdot\mathbf{R}_{j}}\rangle\langle e^{2}\hat{V}_{\bm{\mathcal{P}}_{j}}(\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}^{\prime\prime})\hat{V}_{\bm{\mathcal{P}}_{j}}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime\prime}-\mathbf{p}^{\prime})\rangle=\frac{N}{A}(2\pi)^{2}\delta(\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}^{\prime})\frac{e^{2}\mathcal{P}^{2}}{8\epsilon^{2}}e^{-2a|\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}^{\prime\prime}|}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{2}\delta(\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}^{\prime})n_{dip}|\hat{V}_{avg}(\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}^{\prime\prime})|^{2}

Therefore, from this result we obtain that the second order contribution is

\displaystyle\langle\hat{G}^{(2)}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{p^{\prime}};\omega)\rangle=(2\pi)^{2}\delta(\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}^{\prime})\hat{\Delta}^{R}(\mathbf{p},\omega),

(37)

with

\displaystyle\hat{\Delta}^{R}(\mathbf{p},\omega)=\hat{\Delta}^{R}_{0}(\mathbf{p},\omega)+\hat{\Delta}^{R}_{0}(\mathbf{p},\omega)

(38)

\displaystyle\times n_{dip}\int\frac{d^{2}p^{\prime\prime}}{(2\pi)^{2}}|\hat{V}_{avg}(\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}^{\prime\prime})|^{2}\hat{\Delta}^{R}_{0}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime\prime},\omega)\hat{\Delta}^{R}_{0}(\mathbf{p},\omega)+\ldots,

where we defined the disordered-averaged potential energy

\displaystyle|V_{avg}(\mathbf{p})|^{2}=\frac{e^{2}\mathcal{P}^{2}}{8\epsilon^{2}}e^{-2a|\mathbf{p}|}

Hair and entropy for slowly rotating quantum black holes
Wenbin Feng, Roldao da Rocha, Roberto Casadio

Abstract
We study the quantum hair associated with coherent states describing slowly rotating black holes and show how it can be naturally related with the Bekenstein-Hawking entropy and with 1-loop quantum corrections of the metric for the (effectively) non-rotating case. We also estimate corrections induced by such quantum hair to the temperature of the Hawking radiation through the tunnelling method.

2
Coherent quantum states for slowly rotating geometry

Like in Ref.
[
10
]
, the quantum vacuum is here assumed
to correspond to a spacetime devoid of matter and gravitational excitations.
Any classical metric should then emerge from a suitable (highly excited) quantum state.
A standard approach for recovering classical behaviours employs coherent states,
which is generically motivated by their property of minimising the quantum uncertainty,
and is further supported by studies of electrodynamics
[
9
,
22
]
,
linearised gravity
[
23
]
, and the de Sitter
spacetime
[
24
,
25
]
.

In particular, we want to reproduce the slowly rotating stationary geometry (
1.1
)
as the full general relativistic extension of the Newtonian potential.
The latter can be derived from the longitudinal mode of gravitons in the linearised theory and,
like for the static case of Ref.
[
10
]
, we assume that this feature is preserved
in the stationary limit of full general relativity.
We therefore try and obtain the complete metric function (
1.2
) as the expectation value of
an effective free massless scalar field
\sqrt{G_{\rm N}}\,\Phi=V
satisfying the Klein-Gordon equation

\displaystyle\Box\Phi=0\ .

(2.1)

It is convenient to employ spherical coordinates in which a complete (normalised)
set of positive frequency solutions is given by

\displaystyle u_{\omega\ell m}=\frac{e^{-i\,\omega\,t}}{\sqrt{2\,\omega}}\,j_{\ell}(\omega\,r)\,Y_{\ell m}(\theta,\varphi)\ ,

(2.2)

where
j_{\ell}
are spherical Bessel functions of the first kind, and

\displaystyle Y_{\ell}^{m}=(-1)^{m}\sqrt{\frac{(2\,{\ell}+1)({\ell}-m)!}{4\,\pi\,({\ell}+m)!}}\,P_{\ell}^{m}(\cos\theta)\,e^{i\,m\,\varphi}\ ,

(2.3)

are spherical harmonics of degree
{\ell}
and order
m
,

P_{\ell}^{m}
being associated Legendre polynomials.
We recall that these solutions are orthonormal,
3

3

See Appendix
A

\displaystyle\left(u_{\omega\ell m}|u_{\omega^{\prime}\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}\right)=\frac{\pi}{2\,\omega^{2}}\,\delta(\omega-\omega^{\prime})\,\delta_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}\,\delta_{mm^{\prime}}\ ,\quad\left(u_{\omega\ell m}|u_{\omega^{\prime}\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}^{\ast}\right)=0\ ,

(2.4)

in the Klein-Gordon scalar product

\displaystyle\left(f_{1}|f_{2}\right)=i\int\mbox{${\rm d}$}^{3}x\left(f_{1}^{\ast}\,\partial_{t}f_{2}-f_{2}\,\partial_{t}f_{1}^{\ast}\right)\ .

(2.5)

The quantum theory is built by mapping the field
\Phi
into an operator
expanded in terms of the normal modes (

\displaystyle\hat{\Phi}=\sum_{\ell}\sum_{m=-{\ell}}^{{\ell}}\frac{2}{\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\omega^{2}\,{\mbox{${\rm d}$}}\omega\,\sqrt{\hbar}\left[u_{\omega\ell m}\,\hat{a}_{\ell m}(\omega)+u_{\omega\ell m}^{\ast}\,\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\ell m}(\omega)\right]\ .

(2.6)

Likewise, its conjugate momentum reads

\displaystyle\hat{\Pi}=i\,\sum_{\ell}\sum_{m=-{\ell}}^{\ell}\frac{2}{\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\omega^{3}\,{\mbox{${\rm d}$}}\omega\,\sqrt{\hbar}\left[u_{\omega\ell m}\,\hat{a}_{\ell m}(\omega)-u_{\omega\ell m}^{\ast}\,\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\ell m}(\omega)\right]\ .

(2.7)

These operators satisfy the equal-time commutation relations,

\displaystyle\left[\hat{\Phi}(t,r,\theta,\varphi),\hat{\Pi}(t,r^{\prime},\theta^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime})\right]=i\,\hbar\,\frac{\delta(r-r^{\prime})}{r^{2}}\,\frac{\delta(\theta-\theta^{\prime})}{\sin\theta}\,\delta(\varphi-\varphi^{\prime})\ ,

(2.8)

provided the creation and annihilation operators obey the commutation rules

\displaystyle\left[\hat{a}_{\ell m}(\omega),\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime})\right]=\frac{\pi}{2\,\omega^{2}}\,\delta(\omega-\omega^{\prime})\,\delta_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}\,\delta_{mm^{\prime}}\ .

(2.9)

The vacuum state is first defined by
\hat{a}_{\ell m}(\omega)\ket{0}=0
for
all allowed values of
\omega
,
\ell
and
m
, and a basis for the Fock space is
constructed by the usual action of creation operators.

2.1
Semiclassical metric function

We seek a quantum state of
\Phi
which effectively reproduces (as closely as possible)
the expected slow-rotation limit of the Kerr geometry (
1.1
), that is

\displaystyle\sqrt{G_{\rm N}}\bra{V}\hat{\Phi}(t,r,\theta,\varphi)\ket{V}\simeq V(r)\ .

(2.10)

We can build
\ket{V}
as a superposition of coherent states satisfying

\displaystyle\hat{a}_{\ell m}(\omega)\ket{g_{\ell m}(\omega)}=g_{\ell m}(\omega)\,e^{i\,\gamma_{\ell m}(\omega)}\ket{g_{\ell m}(\omega)}\ ,

(2.11)

where
g_{\ell m}=g_{\ell m}^{*}
and
\gamma_{\ell m}=\gamma_{\ell m}^{*}
, so that

\displaystyle\sqrt{G_{\rm N}}\bra{V}\hat{\Phi}\ket{V}

\displaystyle\!\!=\!\!

\displaystyle\ell_{\rm p}\,\sum_{\ell}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}\frac{2}{\pi}\,\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\omega^{2}\,\mbox{${\rm d}$}\omega\,j_{\ell}(\omega\,r)\,\frac{(-1)^{m}}{\sqrt{2\,\omega}}\,\sqrt{\frac{(2\,{\ell}+1)({\ell}-m)!}{4\,\pi\,({\ell}+m)!}}

(2.12)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\,2\,\cos(\omega\,t-\gamma_{\ell m}+m\,\varphi)\,P_{\ell}^{m}(\cos\theta)\,g_{\ell m}(\omega)\ .

Since the Kerr metric is stationary and axially symmetric, we impose that the phases

\gamma_{\ell m}\simeq\omega\,t+m\,\varphi
.
Indeed, one could argue that recovering exact spacetime symmetries with such a limiting procedure
reflects the fact that no perfect isometries exist in nature
[
10
]
.

The coefficients
g_{\ell m}
can be determined by expanding the metric field
V

on the spatial part of the normal modes (
2.2
),

\displaystyle V(r,\theta)=\sum_{\ell}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}\frac{2}{\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\omega^{2}\,\mbox{${\rm d}$}\omega\,j_{\ell}(\omega r)\,(-1)^{m}\,\sqrt{\frac{(2\,\ell+1)\,(\ell-m)!}{4\,\pi\,(\ell+m)!}}\,P_{\ell}^{m}(\cos\theta)\,\tilde{V}_{\ell m}(\omega)\ .

(2.13)

By comparing the expansions (
2.12
) and (
2.13
), we obtain

\displaystyle g_{\ell m}=\sqrt{\frac{\omega}{2}}\,\frac{\tilde{V}_{\ell m}(\omega)}{\ell_{\rm p}}\ .

(2.14)

The coherent state finally reads

\displaystyle\ket{V}=\prod_{\ell}\prod_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}e^{-N_{\ell m}/2}\exp\left\{\frac{2}{\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\omega^{2}\,\mbox{${\rm d}$}\omega\,g_{\ell m}(\omega)\,\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\ell m}(\omega)\right\}\ket{0}\ ,

(2.15)

where

\displaystyle N_{\ell m}=\frac{2}{\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\omega^{2}\,\mbox{${\rm d}$}\omega\left|g_{\ell m}(\omega)\right|^{2}\ ,

(2.16)

is the occupation number for the state
\ket{g_{\ell m}(\omega)}
.
We note in particular that
N_{V}=\sum_{\ell m}N_{\ell m}
measures the “distance” of
\ket{V}

from the vacuum
\ket{0}
in the Fock space and should be finite
[
10
]
.

2.2
Schwarzschild geometry

For zero angular momentum, hence
a=W_{a}=0
, the metric function (
1.3
) is obtained from

\displaystyle\tilde{V}_{00}=-\frac{2\,\sqrt{\pi}}{\omega^{2}}\,G_{\rm N}\,M\ ,

(2.17)

so that the only contributions to the coherent state
\ket{V_{M}}
are given by
the eigenvalues
[
10
]

\displaystyle g_{00}=-\sqrt{\frac{2\,\pi}{\omega^{3}}}\,\frac{M}{m_{\rm p}}\ ,

(2.18)

yielding the total occupation number

\displaystyle N_{M}=N_{00}=4\,\frac{M^{2}}{m_{\rm p}^{2}}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mbox{${\rm d}$}\omega}{\omega}\ .

(2.19)

The number
N_{M}
diverges logarithmically both in the infrared (IR) and in the ultraviolet (UV).
In particular, the UV divergence arises from demanding a Schwarzschild geometry for all
values of
r>0
and can be formally regularised by introducing a cut-off

\omega_{\rm UV}\sim 1/R_{\rm s}
, where
R_{\rm s}
can be interpreted as the finite radius
of a regular matter source
[
26
]
.

Note in fact that the static geometry we are reconstructing from the coherent state should
be completely determined by the energy-momentum of the matter source in general relativity,
like the Newtonian potential is fully determined by the energy density in the linearised theory.
The UV cut-off is therefore just a mathematically simple way of accounting for the fact that the very
existence of a proper quantum state
\ket{V_{M}}
requires the coefficients
g_{00}=g_{00}(\omega)

to depart from their purely classical expression (
2.18
) for
\omega\to\infty
.
This departure from the classical expression would in turn be related with the actual state of matter in
the interior of the black hole.
Since we aim at a general analysis of the geometry, we just demand that
R_{\rm s}\lesssim R_{\rm H}=2\,G_{\rm N}\,M

for a (quantum) black hole
[
10
]
and do not investigate the connection between the geometry
and possible matter sources any further here (see Refs.
[
27
,
28
,
29
]
).
Likewise, we introduce a IR cut-off
\omega_{\rm IR}=1/R_{\infty}
to account for the
necessarily finite lifetime
\tau\sim R_{\infty}
of the system, and finally write

\displaystyle N_{M}=4\,\frac{M^{2}}{m_{\rm p}^{2}}\ln\!\left(\frac{R_{\infty}}{R_{\rm s}}\right)\ .

(2.20)

The coherent state
\ket{V_{M}}
so defined corresponds to a quantum-corrected metric function

\displaystyle V_{{\rm q}M}

\displaystyle\!\!\simeq\!\!

\displaystyle\sqrt{G_{\rm N}}\bra{V_{M}}\hat{\Phi}\ket{V_{M}}=\frac{1}{\pi^{3/2}}\int\limits_{\omega_{\rm IR}}^{\omega_{\rm UV}}\omega^{2}\,\mbox{${\rm d}$}\omega\,j_{0}(\omega r)\,\tilde{V}_{00}(\omega)

(2.21)

\displaystyle\!\!\simeq\!\!

\displaystyle-\frac{2\,G_{\rm N}\,M}{\pi\,r}\int_{R_{\infty}^{-1}}^{R_{\rm s}^{-1}}\mbox{${\rm d}$}\omega\,\frac{\sin(\omega\,r)}{\omega}

\displaystyle\!\!\simeq\!\!

\displaystyle-\frac{G_{\rm N}\,M}{r}\left\{1-\left[1-\frac{2}{\pi}\,{\rm Si}\!\left(\frac{r}{R_{\rm s}}\right)\right]\right\}\ ,

where we let
\omega_{\text{IR}}=1/R_{\infty}\to 0
and
{\rm Si}
denotes the sine
integral function.
The emerging quantum-corrected geometry is correspondingly given by
4
4

4

For
R_{\rm s}\to 0
,
the term in square brackets in Eq. (
2.21
) vanishes at any
r>0
and the Schwarzschild
metric is formally recovered.

\displaystyle\mbox{${\rm d}$}s^{2}\simeq-\left(1+2\,V_{{\rm q}M}\right)\mbox{${\rm d}$}t^{2}+\frac{\mbox{${\rm d}$}r^{2}}{1+2\,V_{{\rm q}M}}+r^{2}\,\mbox{${\rm d}$}\Omega^{2}\ ,

(2.22)

which was already analysed in Ref.
[
10
]
, where further details can be found.

2.3
Slowly rotating black hole

The classical metric (
1.1
) is characterised by an angular momentum of modulus

\hbar\ll J=|a|\,M\ll G_{\rm N}\,M^{2}
oriented along the axis of symmetry, so that
J^{z}=J
for
a>0
, and
by the metric function
W_{a}
in Eq. (
1.4
).
We can now show that a quantum state that reproduces such a metric,
as closely as possible, like we discussed in the previous Section
2.2
,
can be obtained by linearly combining the coherent state
\ket{V_{M}}
of the
Schwarzschild geometry with a suitable coherent state
\ket{W_{a}}
.

The normal modes (
2.2
) are eigenfunctions of the angular momentum
operators
\hat{L}^{2}
and
\hat{L}_{z}
(in Minkowski spacetime) with eigenvalues
\hbar^{2}\,\ell\,(\ell+1)

and
\hbar\,m
, respectively.
The expectation values of the angular momentum operators on the coherent state

\ket{g_{\ell m}(\omega)}
are therefore given by (see Appendix
B
)

\displaystyle J_{\ell m}=\bra{g_{\ell m}(\omega)}\sqrt{\hat{L}^{2}}\ket{g_{\ell m}(\omega)}=\hbar\,\sqrt{\ell\left(\ell+1\right)}\left|g_{\ell m}(\omega)\right|^{2}\ ,

(2.23)

and

\displaystyle J_{\ell m}^{z}=\bra{g_{\ell m}(\omega)}\hat{L}_{z}\ket{g_{\ell m}(\omega)}=\hbar\,m\left|g_{\ell m}(\omega)\right|^{2}\ .

(2.24)

The total angular momentum for a superposition
\ket{W}

of states
\ket{g_{\ell m}(\omega)}
can be obtained as

\displaystyle J\equiv\bra{W}\sqrt{\hat{L}^{2}}\ket{W}=\sum_{\ell>0}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}\frac{2}{\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\omega^{2}\,\mbox{${\rm d}$}\omega\,J_{\ell m}(\omega)=\sum_{\ell>0}\hbar\,\sqrt{\ell\left(\ell+1\right)}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}N_{\ell m}\ .

(2.25)

Likewise,

\displaystyle J^{z}\equiv\bra{W}\hat{L}_{z}\ket{W}=\sum_{\ell>0}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}\frac{2}{\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\omega^{2}\,\mbox{${\rm d}$}\omega\,J_{\ell m}^{z}(\omega)=\sum_{\ell>0}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}\hbar\,m\,N_{\ell m}\ .

(2.26)

Let us next consider coherent states defined by the eigenvalues

\displaystyle g_{\ell m}=C_{\ell m}\,\frac{\sqrt{2\,\pi}\,\ell_{\rm p}^{\alpha}\,M}{\omega^{3/2-\alpha}\,m_{\rm p}}\ ,

(2.27)

where
C_{\ell m}
are numerical coefficients that do not depend on
\omega
and
\ell\geq 1
.
The corresponding occupation numbers (
2.16
) are given by

\displaystyle N_{\ell m}\simeq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}C_{\ell m}^{2}\,N_{M}&{\rm for}\ \alpha=0\\
\displaystyle 4\,C_{\ell m}^{2}\,\frac{M^{2}}{m_{\rm p}^{2}}\left[\left(\frac{\ell_{\rm p}}{R_{\rm s}}\right)^{2\,\alpha}-\left(\frac{\ell_{\rm p}}{R_{\infty}}\right)^{2\,\alpha}\right]&{\rm for}\ \alpha\neq 0\ ,\end{array}\right.

where
N_{M}\sim M^{2}/m_{\rm p}^{2}
is given in Eq. (
2.20
).
Note that the IR limit
R_{\infty}\to\infty
is regular only for
\alpha>0
,
for which
N_{\ell m}\ll N_{M}
if
R_{\rm s}\gg\ell_{\rm p}
.
In this case, we can further approximate

\displaystyle N_{\ell m}\simeq 4\,C_{\ell m}^{2}\,\frac{M^{2}}{m_{\rm p}^{2}}\left(\frac{\ell_{\rm p}}{R_{\rm s}}\right)^{2\,\alpha}\sim C_{\ell m}^{2}\ ,

(2.31)

where we considered
R_{\rm s}\sim R_{\rm H}
for a black hole.
5
5

5

All numerical factors can
be included in

.

Moreover, the modification (

) to the metric function is given by

\displaystyle W_{\ell m}

\displaystyle\!\!\simeq\!\!

\displaystyle\ell_{\rm p}\,\frac{2}{\pi}\,\int\limits_{\omega_{\rm IR}}^{\omega_{\rm UV}}\omega^{2}\,\mbox{${\rm d}$}\omega\,j_{\ell}(\omega\,r)\,\frac{(-1)^{m}}{\sqrt{2\,\omega}}\sqrt{\frac{(2\,\ell+1)\,(\ell-m)!}{\pi\,(\ell+m)!}}\,P_{\ell}^{m}(\cos\theta)\,g_{\ell m}(\omega)

\displaystyle\!\!\simeq\!\!

\displaystyle\frac{G_{\rm N}\,M}{r}\left(\frac{\ell_{\rm p}}{r}\right)^{\alpha}\left[C_{\ell m}\,(-1)^{m}\,\sqrt{\frac{(2\,\ell+1)\,(\ell-m)!}{(\ell+m)!}}\,P_{\ell}^{m}(\cos\theta)\,\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{r/R_{\rm s}}{z^{\alpha}}\,\mbox{${\rm d}$}z\,j_{\ell}(z)\right]\ ,\qquad

We then see that the leading terms in the correction (

in Eq. (

) if

.

Finally, the contribution to the angular momentum satisfies the classicality conditions

\displaystyle\hbar\ll J_{\ell m}\simeq\hbar\,\sqrt{\ell\,(\ell+1)}\,N_{\ell m}\simeq\hbar\,m\,N_{\ell m}\simeq J^{z}_{\ell m}\ ,

(2.33)

provided

and
\ell\,N_{\ell m}\sim\ell\,C_{\ell m}^{2}\gg 1

We can build a coherent state
\ket{W_{a}}
that reproduces the geometry (

)
by including different coherent states (

) with

and

The rotation coefficient will then be given by

\displaystyle\frac{m_{\rm p}}{M}\ll\frac{a}{G_{\rm N}\,M}\sim\frac{m_{\rm p}^{2}}{M^{2}}\,\sum_{\ell=1}^{\ell_{\rm c}}\sqrt{\ell\left(\ell+1\right)}\,N_{\ell\ell}\sim\frac{1}{N_{M}}\,\sum_{\ell=1}^{\ell_{\rm c}}\sqrt{\ell\left(\ell+1\right)}\,N_{\ell\ell}\lesssim\delta_{J}\ll 1\ ,

where we introduced a parameter

, denoted by

.

The inclusion of states
\ket{W}
like the above will give rise to quantum-corrected geometries

\displaystyle\mbox{${\rm d}$}s^{2}\simeq-\left(1+2\,V_{{\rm q}M}+2\,W_{{\rm q}a}\right)\mbox{${\rm d}$}t^{2}+\frac{\mbox{${\rm d}$}r^{2}}{1+2\,V_{{\rm q}M}+2\,W_{{\rm q}a}}-\frac{4\,G_{\rm N}\,J}{r}\,\sin^{2}\theta\,\mbox{${\rm d}$}t\,\mbox{${\rm d}$}\phi+r^{2}\,\mbox{${\rm d}$}\Omega^{2}\ ,

(2.35)

where

is given in Eq. (

),

in Eq. (

in Eq. (

).
These geometries do not entail a weak-field approximation but are perturbative in
the angular momentum contributions, that is in

and
W_{{\rm q}a}
.

3
Quantum hair

Black hole solutions in general relativity are determined only by
the total mass, angular momentum, and electric charge (if present).
These uniqueness theorems
[
30
]
strongly limit the information about the internal state
of a black hole that can be obtained by outside observers.
However, the situation changes when we consider the quantum description
of black holes given by coherent states already for the spherically
symmetric case of Section
2.2
.
In fact, the coherent states from which the geometry emerges as a mean field effect
cannot accommodate for perfect Schwarzschild spacetimes
[
10
]
,
but they instead depend on the internal structure of the matter sources (classically)
hidden inside the horizon, as we recalled in Section
2.2
.

The classical case of slow rotation was considered in Section
2.3
,
where we assumed that the quantum states of the geometry only include specific coherent
states (
2.27
) with
\alpha=1
satisfying the relations in Eq. (
2.33
) for the angular momentum.
However, the possibility that other states contribute can only be limited
from the condition of recovering the classical metric (
1.1
) within the experimental
bounds.
Their presence, on the other hand, will constitute a further example of
quantum hair
[
19
]
, with departures from the classical geometry.

Instead of attempting a general analysis, we shall only consider states that
violate one of the classicality conditions defined in Section
2.3
at a time.
In particular, we will study a) quantum contributions with
J^{z}\simeq J
but
\alpha>1

inducing departures from
V_{M}
smaller than
W_{a}
at large
r
in Section
3.1

and b) modes with
\alpha=1
and
a>0
given by Eq. (
2.34
) but such that
|J^{z}|\ll J

in Section
3.2
.

3.1
Quantum metric corrections

An explicit example of a coherent state which satisfies the classical conditions

J^{z}\simeq J
for the angular momentum but leads to a geometry with terms
that fall off at
r\gg R_{\rm H}=2\,G_{\rm N}\,M
faster than
W_{a}
in Eq. (
1.4
) is built from

\displaystyle g_{\bar{\ell}\bar{\ell}}=C_{\bar{\ell}}\,\frac{\sqrt{2\,\pi}\,\ell_{\rm p}^{\alpha}\,M}{\omega^{3/2-\alpha}\,m_{\rm p}}\ ,

(3.1)

where
\alpha>1
and
\bar{\ell}
is a fixed integer value.
The hairy geometry can now be obtained from

\displaystyle W_{\bar{\ell}\bar{\ell}}\simeq\frac{\ell_{\rm p}}{\pi^{2}}\,\int\limits_{\omega_{\rm IR}}^{\omega_{\rm UV}}\omega^{3/2}\,\mbox{${\rm d}$}\omega\,j_{\bar{\ell}}(\omega\,r)\,\frac{2\,{\bar{\ell}}+1}{2^{\bar{\ell}-1/2}\,\bar{\ell}!}\,(\sin\theta)^{\bar{\ell}}\,g_{\bar{\ell}\bar{\ell}}(\omega)\ ,

(3.2)

where we used Eq. (
A.8
).
We thus find

\displaystyle W_{\bar{\ell}\bar{\ell}}

\displaystyle\!\!\simeq\!\!

\displaystyle C_{\bar{\ell}}\,\frac{G_{\rm N}\,M}{\pi^{3/2}\,r}\left(\frac{\ell_{\rm p}}{r}\right)^{\alpha}\frac{2\,{\bar{\ell}}+1}{2^{\bar{\ell}-1}\,\bar{\ell}!}\,(\sin\theta)^{\bar{\ell}}\int_{0}^{r/R_{\rm s}}{z^{\alpha}}\,\mbox{${\rm d}$}z\,j_{\bar{\ell}}(z)

We can in particular estimate the correction on the (unperturbed) Schwarzschild horizon at

\displaystyle W_{\bar{\ell}\bar{\ell}}(R_{\rm H})\sim\left(\frac{m_{\rm p}}{M}\right)^{\alpha}(\sin\theta)^{\bar{\ell}}\ .

Such corrections with different

with vanishingly small amplitude for macroscopically large black holes of mass

3.2
Quantum angular momentum

States that lead to metric functions of the classical form

in Eq. (

As an example, we consider

The metric correction is now given by

\displaystyle W_{\bar{\ell}\bar{\ell}}\simeq\frac{\ell_{\rm p}}{\pi^{2}}\,\int\limits_{\omega_{\rm IR}}^{\omega_{\rm UV}}\omega^{2}\,\mbox{${\rm d}$}\omega\,j_{\bar{\ell}}(\omega\,r)\,\frac{(-1)^{\bar{\ell}}+1}{\sqrt{2\,\omega}}\,\frac{2\,{\bar{\ell}}+1}{2^{\bar{\ell}-1}\,\bar{\ell}!}\,(\sin\theta)^{\bar{\ell}}\,g_{\bar{\ell}\bar{\ell}}(\omega)\ ,

From the above few examples, it should be clear that one can engineer many different axially
symmetric configurations, all of which differ from the (slowly-rotating)
Kerr geometry only by terms of order

for

Of course, this ambiguity would be removed by computing the coherent state generated

4
Entropy and evaporation

In the previous Sections, for simplicity, we have modelled the dependence of the
geometry from the internal structure of the black hole
by introducing cut-offs
\omega_{\rm IR}\sim 1/R_{\infty}

and
\omega_{\rm UV}\sim 1/R_{\rm s}
in momentum space and allowing
for contributions of angular momentum that have no classical counterpart.
Were we able to test the gravitational field with sufficient accuracy, for instance from the motion of
test particles and light in the outer region to the horizon, we could remove these uncertainties
and gather information about the interior of the black hole.

4.1
Bekenstein-Hawking entropy

A common way to measure our ignorance about the actual state of a system is provided
by the thermodynamic entropy, which is obtained by counting the possible microstates
corresponding to a given macroscopic configuration.
For a Schwarzschild black hole, the Bekenstein-Hawking entropy
[
5
]

\displaystyle S_{\rm BH}=\frac{\pi\,R_{\rm H}^{2}}{\ell_{\rm p}^{2}}=\frac{4\,\pi\,M^{2}}{m_{\rm p}^{2}}

(4.1)

can be obtained
[
11
]
by supplementing a pure coherent state of the
Schwarzschild geometry (
2.18
) with the Planckian distribution of Hawking quanta
at the temperature
[
20
]

\displaystyle T_{\rm H}=\frac{m_{\rm p}^{2}}{8\,\pi\,M}\ .

(4.2)

Given a black hole of mass
M
, instead of one pure coherent state, we could consider all possible
states giving rise to (practically) indistinguishable semiclassical geometries with the mass
M
.
We can employ the total occupation number (
2.20
)
6
6

6

We just mention that the
same quantisation law is obtained for the ground state of a dust ball
[
28
]
.

of the corresponding coherent state to estimate the total number of microstates available
to build such configurations
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{M}\sim\sum_{n=0}^{N_{M}}\left(\!\!\begin{array}[]{c}N_{M}\\
n\end{array}\!\!\right)=\sum_{n=0}^{N_{M}}\frac{N_{M}!}{(N_{M}-n)!\,n!}=2^{N_{M}}\ .

The thermodynamic entropy is thus

\displaystyle S_{M}\propto\ln(\mathcal{N}_{M})\sim\left(\frac{M}{m_{\rm p}}\right)^{2}\ ,

(4.6)

which is clearly proportional to the Bekenstein-Hawking entropy (
4.1
).
One can therefore envisage that the coherent states giving rise to Schwarzschild black
hole geometries contain the precursors (or proxies) of the Hawking particles,
like in the original corpuscular picture
[
2
]
.

4.2
Entropy and angular momentum

We can also estimate the number of quantum states with angular momentum
corresponding to geometric configurations that cannot be observationally distinguished
from a non-rotating Schwarzschild black hole.
For this purpose, we can consider again a maximum angular momentum parameter

\delta_{J}\ll 1
such that configurations with

\displaystyle\frac{J}{M\,R_{\rm H}}\simeq\frac{a}{G_{\rm N}\,M}\lesssim\delta_{J}\ ,

(4.7)

cannot be distinguished from the coherent state reproducing the quantum-corrected
Schwarzschild geometry (
2.21
).
Furthermore, we shall include in this count only those contributions of the form
in Eq. (
2.27
),

\displaystyle g_{\ell m}\sim C_{\ell m}\,\frac{\omega^{\alpha}\,\ell_{\rm p}^{\alpha}\,M}{\omega^{3/2}\,m_{\rm p}}\ ,

(4.8)

that violate both of the classicality conditions considered in Sections
3.1
and
3.2
,
that is
\alpha\gtrsim 1
and
0\leq|m|\ll\ell

In particular, the contribution of modes with
m\simeq 0
to the angular momentum (
2.25

is approximately given by

\displaystyle\frac{a_{\ell}}{G_{\rm N}\,M}\simeq\ell\,\frac{m_{\rm p}^{2}}{M^{2}}\,N_{\ell 0}\sim\ell\left(\frac{\ell_{\rm p}}{R_{\rm s}}\right)^{2\,\alpha}\ ,

(4.9)

in which we assumed
N_{\ell 0}\sim C^{2}_{\ell 0}\sim 1
and
\ell\gg 1

Imposing the constraint (
4.7
) on the total angular momentum,

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{\ell_{\rm c}}\frac{a_{\ell}}{G_{\rm N}\,M}\sim\ell_{\rm c}^{2}\left(\frac{\ell_{\rm p}}{R_{\rm s}}\right)^{2\,\alpha}\lesssim\delta_{J}\ ,

(4.10)

yields

\displaystyle\ell_{\rm c}\lesssim\left(\frac{R_{\rm s}}{\ell_{\rm p}}\right)^{\alpha}\sqrt{\delta_{J}}\sim\left(\frac{M}{m_{\rm p}}\right)^{\alpha}\sqrt{\delta_{J}}\ ,

(4.11)

where we again set
R_{\rm s}\sim R_{\rm H}
for a black hole.
Upon allowing for the inclusion of modes
\ket{g_{\ell 0}}
with
1\leq\ell\leq\ell_{\rm c}
,
we can estimate the degeneracy of the quantum black hole
given by the total number of possible combinations in angular momentum, that is

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\rm c}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\ell_{\rm c}}\left(\!\!\begin{array}[]{c}\ell_{\rm c}\\
\ell\end{array}\!\!\right)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\ell_{\rm c}}\frac{\ell_{\rm c}!}{(\ell_{\rm c}-\ell)!\,\ell!}=2^{\ell_{\rm c}}\ .

The corresponding thermodynamic entropy,

\displaystyle S\propto\ln(\mathcal{N}_{\rm c})\sim\left(\frac{M}{m_{\rm p}}\right)^{\alpha}\sqrt{\delta_{J}}\ ,

(4.15)

is also proportional to the Bekenstein-Hawking entropy (
4.1

\alpha=2
.

It is then interesting to notice that the metric corrections for
\alpha=2
are
of the same order in
G_{\rm N}
,
\ell_{\rm p}
and
1/r
as those obtained from 1-loop corrections
to the Schwarzschild metric
[
21

\displaystyle W_{{\rm q}a}

\displaystyle\!\!\simeq\!\!

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{\ell_{\rm c}}\,W_{\ell 0}\simeq\frac{G_{\rm N}\,M}{r}\left(\frac{\ell_{\rm p}}{r}\right)^{2}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\ell_{\rm c}}\left[C_{\ell 0}\,\sqrt{2\,\ell+1}\,P_{\ell}(\cos\theta)\,\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{r/R_{\rm s}}{z^{2}}\,\mbox{${\rm d}$}z\,j_{\ell}(z)\right]

\displaystyle\frac{G_{\rm N}\,M}{r}\left(\frac{\ell_{\rm p}}{r}\right)^{2}\ ,

where
P_{\ell}=P_{\ell}^{0}
are Legendre polynomials.
The corresponding quantum corrected Schwarzschild geometry is now given by

\displaystyle\mbox{${\rm d}$}s^{2}\simeq-\left(1+2\,V_{{\rm q}M}+2\,W_{{\rm q}a}\right)\mbox{${\rm d}$}t^{2}+\frac{\mbox{${\rm d}$}r^{2}}{1+2\,V_{{\rm q}M}+2\,W_{{\rm q}a}}+r^{2}\,\mbox{${\rm d}$}\Omega^{2}\ ,

(4.17)

where
V_{{\rm q}M}
is the metric function in Eq. (
2.21

It is important to remark that
W_{{\rm q}a}
represents a perturbation over the full
quantum-corrected geometry (
2.22

employed to perform 1-loop corrections.

Finally, we can check that the condition (
4.11

significantly from the unperturbed Schwarzschild radius.
In fact, for
\alpha=2
and neglecting the effect of

, we can write the metric
function

\displaystyle V\simeq V_{M}+\epsilon\,\frac{\ell_{\rm p}^{2}\,G_{\rm N}\,M}{r^{3}}\ ,

(4.18)

where
\epsilon\sim\sqrt{\delta_{J}}
now contains all the parameters (and angular dependence)
shown in the first line of Eq. (

The largest solution to

\displaystyle r_{\rm H}\simeq 2\,G_{\rm N}\,M-\epsilon\,\ell_{\rm p}\ ,

(4.19)

which represents a negligible correction to
R_{\rm H}=2\,G_{\rm N}\,M

Given the fast fall-off of the metric correction in Eq. (

as being “confined” about the horizon

, like in the membrane approach
[
31
]
and
in derivation of the entropy (
4.1

[
32

4.3
Hawking radiation

The Hawking evaporation has been studied with several methods since its discovery
[
20
]
.
In particular, semiclassical approaches describe this effect
as particles that tunnel out from within the event horizon on classically forbidden
paths
[
33
,
34
,
35
,
36
]
.
We will employ the WKB approach to compute corrections to the Hawking temperature
for slowly rotating black holes described by the quantum-corrected Schwarzschild
metric (
2.21
) with the metric modification in Eq. (
4.16
) that we showed
can contribute to the Bekenstein-Hawking entropy.

We start by noting that, replacing the WKB ansatz

\displaystyle\Phi\simeq\exp\left[\frac{i}{\hbar}\,S(t,r,\theta,\phi)\right]

(4.20)

in the Klein-Gordon Eq. (
2.1
) at leading order in
\hbar
,
yields the Hamilton-Jacobi equation

\displaystyle g^{\mu\nu}\,\partial_{\mu}S\,\partial_{\nu}S\simeq 0\ .

(4.21)

Solutions can be written in the form

\displaystyle S=-E\,t+\mathcal{W}(r)+\mathcal{J}(\theta,\phi)+K\ ,

(4.22)

where
E
represents the energy of the emitted boson and
K
is a complex constant that
will be fixed later.
The ratio
E/M
regulates the magnitude of the backreaction of the emission on the black hole,
which can alter the thermal nature of the Hawking radiation
[
33
]
.
We only consider large black holes with mass
M\gg m_{\rm p}
, hence this effect can be
neglected for
E\ll M
.

Given the inverse of the metric (
1.1
), Eq. (
4.21
) can be written as

\displaystyle{(1+2\,V)}\left[\left(\frac{\partial\mathcal{W}}{\partial r}\right)^{2}+\frac{r^{2}}{w\,\sin\theta}\left(\frac{\partial\mathcal{J}}{\partial\phi}\right)^{2}\right]+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\left(\frac{\partial\mathcal{J}}{\partial\theta}\right)^{2}+\frac{4\,a\,G_{\rm N}\,M\,r}{w}E\,\frac{\partial\mathcal{J}}{\partial\phi}\simeq\frac{r^{4}}{w}E^{2}\ ,

(4.23)

where we used the form (
4.22
) for
S
and defined

\displaystyle w\equiv r^{4}\,(1+2\,V)+4\,a^{2}\,G_{\rm N}^{2}\,M^{2}\,\sin^{2}\theta\ .

(4.24)

For Hawking particles in a quantum corrected Schwarzschild geometry,
we can just consider purely radial trajectories
[
35
]
,
along which
\mathcal{J}
is constant, and further approximate
w\simeq r^{4}\left(1+2\,V\right)
.
In this case, Eq. (
4.23
) is solved by

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{\pm}\simeq\pm E\int^{r}\frac{\mbox{${\rm d}$}\bar{r}}{1+2\,V}\ ,

(4.25)

with
+
(
-
) for outgoing (ingoing) particles.

Imaginary terms in the action
S
correspond to the Boltzmann factor for emission
and absorption across the event horizon.
Such terms can only arise due to the pole at
r=r_{\rm H}\simeq R_{\rm H}
, where
1+2\,V=0
,
and from the imaginary part of
K
in Eq. (
4.22
), resulting in the probabilities

\displaystyle P_{\pm}\propto\exp\left[-\frac{2}{\hbar}\left(\Im\mathcal{W}_{\pm}+\Im K\right)\right]\ ,

(4.26)

where
\Im
denotes the imaginary part.
Assuming that ingoing particles necessarily cross the event horizon, that is
P_{-}\simeq 1
,
one must set
\Im K=-\Im\mathcal{W}_{-}
.
Since
\mathcal{W}_{+}=-\mathcal{W}_{-}
, the probability of a particle tunnelling out
then reads

\displaystyle P_{+}\simeq\exp\left(-\frac{4}{\hbar}\,\Im\mathcal{W}_{+}\right)\ .

(4.27)

The integral (
4.25
) around the pole at
r\simeq R_{\rm H}
with the Feynman prescription
for the propagator
[
35
]
yields

\displaystyle\Im\mathcal{W}_{+}\simeq\lim_{r\to R_{\rm H}}\frac{\pi\,E}{2\,\hbar\,V^{\prime}(r)}\ ,

(4.28)

where
V^{\prime}=\partial_{r}V
.
Finally,

\displaystyle P_{+}\simeq\exp\left[-\frac{2\,\pi\,E}{V^{\prime}(R_{\rm H})}\right]\

(4.29)

which implies that the temperature must be given by

\displaystyle T_{M}\simeq\frac{\hbar}{2\,\pi}\,V^{\prime}(R_{\rm H})=\frac{\hbar}{2\,\pi}\left[V_{{\rm q}M}^{\prime}(R_{\rm H})+W_{{\rm q}a}^{\prime}(R_{\rm H})\right]\ .

(4.30)

This expression with the metric function (
2.21
) and the contribution (
4.16
) with
\alpha=2
for the case of
Section
4.1
gives

\displaystyle T_{M}\simeq T_{\rm H}\,\frac{2}{\pi}\left[{\rm Si}\left(\frac{R_{\rm H}}{R_{s}}\right)-\sin\!\left(\frac{R_{\rm H}}{R_{s}}\right)-\frac{3\,m_{\rm p}^{2}}{4\,\pi\,M^{2}}\,\sum_{\ell=1}^{\ell_{c}}C_{\ell 0}\,\sqrt{2\,\ell+1}\,P_{\ell}(\cos\theta)\!\!\!\int\limits_{0}^{R_{\rm H}/R_{s}}\!\!\!z^{2}\,\mbox{${\rm d}$}z\,j_{\ell}(z)\right]\ ,

(4.31)

where
T_{\rm H}
is the standard Hawking temperature (
4.2
),
which is therefore recovered asymptotically for
M\gg m_{\rm p}
and
R_{\rm s}\ll R_{\rm H}
.

Using the metric function in Eq. (
4.18
), one analogously finds

\displaystyle T_{M}\simeq T_{\rm H}\left(1-\frac{3\,\epsilon\,m_{\rm p}^{2}}{4\,M^{2}}\right)\ .

(4.32)

On equating the two corrections of order
m_{\rm p}^{2}/M^{2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\epsilon\simeq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi^{3}}}\,\sum_{\ell=1}^{\ell_{c}}C_{\ell 0}\,\frac{\sqrt{2\,\ell+1}\,\Gamma(\ell/2+3/2)\,P_{\ell}(\cos\theta)}{{2^{\ell}}\,\Gamma\left(\ell+3/2\right)\,\Gamma(\ell/2+5/2)}\,{}_{1}F_{2}\left(\frac{\ell+3}{2},\ell+\frac{3}{2},\frac{\ell+5}{2},-\frac{1}{4}\right)\ ,

(4.33)

where we used Eq. (
A.11
) with
\alpha=2
.

5
Conclusions and outlook

In this work, the semiclassical metric function reproducing a Kerr geometry in the slow-rotation
regime was shown to arise from suitable highly-excited coherent states, thus generalising previous results
obtained for spherically symmetric geometries
[
10
,
14
,
25
]
.
Quantum hair naturally emerges in this context, since the existence of the quantum coherent state
does not allow for any possible IR and UV divergences in general.

An additional source of quantum hair was then identified in angular momentum modes that do not satisfy
the conditions for giving rise to a classical rotating geometry described in Section
2.3
.
Such modes were further associated with the Bekenstein-Hawking entropy of Schwarzschild black holes
and are therefore expected to play the role of precursors of the Hawking radiation, at least
for very massive black holes.
The Hawking evaporation was then studied with the Hamilton-Jacobi method, from which
modes representing quantum hair in the geometry were related to metric corrections of the
form that one expects from 1-loop quantum corrections in the weak-field
approximation
[
21
]
.

There are different directions along which the present results could be improved and
developed.
First of all, results regarding the Hawking evaporation can be straightforwardly generalised
to massive bosons and fermions
[
36
]
.
One could furthermore study other black hole solutions that can emerge from coherent
quantum states and eventually attempt at a quantum description of black holes with arbitrary
angular momentum
[
15
]
.

(A.4)

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}Y_{\ell}^{m}(\theta,\varphi)\left[Y_{\ell}^{m}(\theta^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime})\right]^{*}=\frac{\delta(\theta-\theta^{\prime})}{\sin\theta}\,\delta(\varphi-\varphi^{\prime})\ .

\displaystyle\left[Y_{\ell}^{m}\right]^{*}=(-1)^{m}\,Y_{\ell}^{-m}

\displaystyle P_{\ell}^{-m}=(-1)^{m}\,\frac{(\ell-m)!}{(\ell+m)!}\,P_{\ell}^{m}\ .

The Dirac delta is defined by

\displaystyle\int\mbox{${\rm d}$}z\,\delta(z-z_{0})\,f(z)=f(z_{0})\ ,

(A.12)

Appendix B
Angular momentum

The normal modes (
2.2
) are eigenfunctions of the angular momentum,
that is

\displaystyle\hat{L}^{2}\,u_{\omega\ell m}=\hbar^{2}\,\ell\left(\ell+1\right)u_{\omega\ell m}\qquad{\rm and}\qquad\hat{L}_{z}\,u_{\omega\ell m}=\hbar\,m\,u_{\omega\ell m}\ .

(B.1)

It then follows that

\displaystyle\hat{L}^{2}\ket{1_{\ell m}(\omega)}=\hbar^{2}\,\ell\left(\ell+1\right)\ket{1_{\ell m}(\omega)}\qquad{\rm and}\qquad\hat{L}_{z}\ket{1_{\ell m}(\omega)}=\hbar\,m\ket{1_{\ell m}(\omega)}\ ,

(B.2)

where
\ket{1_{\ell m}(\omega)}=\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\ell m}(\omega)\ket{0}
.
We can also write the first relation as defining the operator

\displaystyle\sqrt{\hat{L}^{2}}\ket{1_{\ell m}(\omega)}=\hbar\,\sqrt{\ell\left(\ell+1\right)}\ket{1_{\ell m}(\omega)}\ .

(B.3)

Likewise, we have

\displaystyle\sqrt{\hat{L}^{2}}\ket{n_{\ell m}(\omega)}=\hbar\,\sqrt{\ell\left(\ell+1\right)}\,n_{\ell m}\ket{n_{\ell m}(\omega)}\quad{\rm and}\quad\hat{L}_{z}\ket{n_{\ell m}(\omega)}=\hbar\,m\,n_{\ell m}\ket{n_{\ell m}(\omega)}\ ,

(B.4)

where
\ket{n_{\ell m}(\omega)}=(n!)^{-1/2}\left[\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\ell m}(\omega)\right]^{n}\ket{0}

(with
n=n_{\ell m}
for brevity).

Let us consider a coherent state of fixed
\omega
(which we omit for simplicity),
\ell
and
m

\displaystyle\ket{g_{\ell m}}

\displaystyle\!\!=\!\!

\displaystyle e^{-N_{\ell m}/2}\,\exp\left\{g_{\ell m}\,\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\ell m}\right\}\ket{0}

(B.5)

\displaystyle\!\!=\!\!

\displaystyle e^{-N_{\ell m}/2}\,\sum_{n}\frac{\left(g_{\ell m}\,\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\ell m}\right)^{n}}{n!}\ket{0}

\displaystyle\!\!=\!\!

\displaystyle e^{-N_{\ell m}/2}\,\sum_{n}\frac{g_{\ell m}^{n}}{\sqrt{n!}}\ket{n_{\ell m}}\ .

From
\mbox{$\langle\,n_{\ell m}\mid n^{\prime}_{\ell m}\,\rangle$}=\delta_{nn^{\prime}}
, the normalisation

\displaystyle\mbox{$\langle\,g_{\ell m}\mid g_{\ell m}\,\rangle$}=e^{-N_{\ell m}}\,\sum_{n}\frac{g^{2\,n}_{\ell m}}{n!}=1

(B.6)

implies

.
From Eq. (
B.4
), we then find

\displaystyle\bra{g_{\ell m}}\sqrt{\hat{L}^{2}}\ket{g_{\ell m}}

\displaystyle\!\!=\!\!

\displaystyle e^{-g^{2}_{\ell m}}\sum_{n,s}\frac{g^{s}_{\ell m}}{\sqrt{s!}}\,\frac{g^{n}_{\ell m}}{\sqrt{n!}}\bra{s_{\ell m}}\sqrt{\hat{L}^{2}}\ket{n_{\ell m}}

(B.7)

\displaystyle\!\!=\!\!

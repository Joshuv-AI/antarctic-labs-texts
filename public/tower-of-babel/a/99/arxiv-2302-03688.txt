A 10d construction of Euclidean axion
wormholes in flat and AdS space

Abstract




KEK-TH-2495

Euclidean wormhole geometries sourced by axions and dilatons are puzzling objects in quantum gravity. From one side of the wormhole to the other, the scalar fields traverse a few Planck lengths in field space and so corrections from the UV might potentially affect the consistency of the solution, even when the wormholes are large. Motivated by this, we carry out the first explicit 10d lifts of regular Euclidean axion wormholes. We start off with the lift of Giddings-Strominger wormholes in
\mathcal{N}=8
Euclidean supergravity over a 6-torus to 10d type IIA supergravity and find the solution can be everywhere tuned into the parametrically controlled supergravity regime. Secondly, we construct explicit wormholes in AdS spaces and find them again to be under parametric control. We find the first wormhole solutions in massive type IIA on
S^{3}\times S^{3}
and in type IIB on
T^{1,1}
. The latter has an explicit holographic dual, and similar to the earlier constructions in
\mathrm{AdS}_{5}\times S^{5}/\mathbb{Z}_{k}
, the wormholes violate operator positivity since
\Tr(F\pm{\star F})^{2}<0
. This puzzle might arise from subtleties related to computing holographic
n
-point functions in the presence of multiple boundaries.

2
Regular Giddings-Strominger wormholes

In this section we recall the generalised Giddings-Strominger wormholes and their associated regularity condition pioneered in
[
29
]
. Consider the following Euclidean action in
d\geq 3
dimensions:

S=\frac{1}{2\kappa_{d}^{2}}\int\left({\star(\mathcal{R}-2\Lambda)}-\frac{1}{2}G_{ij}(\varphi)\mathrm{d}{\varphi^{i}}\wedge{\star\mathrm{d}{\varphi^{j}}}\right)\,.

(2.1)

A simple set of solutions with
\mathrm{O}(d)
symmetry take the form

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}{s^{2}}

\displaystyle=f(r)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}{r^{2}}+a(r)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}{\Omega_{d-1}^{2}}\,,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{a^{\prime}}{f}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=1+\frac{a^{2}}{\ell^{2}}+\frac{\mathsf{c}}{2(d-1)(d-2)a^{2d-4}}\,,

\displaystyle\mathsf{c}

\displaystyle=G_{ij}(\varphi)\frac{\mathrm{d}{\varphi^{i}}}{\mathrm{d}{h}}\frac{\mathrm{d}{\varphi^{j}}}{\mathrm{d}{h}}\,,

where
h(r)
is a harmonic function, normalized to
h^{\prime}=f/a^{d-1}
so that
{\star\mathrm{d}{h}}=\mathrm{vol}_{d-1}
, and
\Lambda=-(d-1)(d-2)\ell^{-2}
. The scalars trace out a geodesic curve on the target space: the constant
\mathsf{c}
is the geodesic velocity and the harmonic function
h
plays the role of the affine parameter along the geodesic.

Wormhole geometries have
\mathsf{c}<0
and correspond to time-like geodesics on the target space. In Euclidean spacetime signature the metric on the target space has indefinite signature with the time-like directions being axion directions. The above wormhole metrics are always regular, but the expressions for the geodesic curves (i.e. the axion and dilaton profiles) need not be. For example, a consistent truncation of Euclidean type IIB supergravity compactified over
S^{5}
gives an action of the above form with the following kinetic term for the axio-dilaton:

G_{ij}(\varphi)\partial{\varphi^{i}}\partial{\varphi^{j}}=(\partial\phi)^{2}-e^{2\phi}(\partial\chi)^{2}\,.

(2.3)

The corresponding wormholes have singular axion and dilaton profiles
[
33
]
. The reason for this was understood in
[
29
]
in a general fashion and requires the notion of the geodesic length
D
for the time-like geodesics, which can be computed using

\displaystyle D_{d}\big(\tfrac{a_{0}}{\ell}\big)

\displaystyle=\int^{\text{right}}_{\text{left}}\!\!\!\!\sqrt{-\mathsf{c}}\,\mathrm{d}{h}=2\times\int_{a_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{\sqrt{-\mathsf{c}}\,\mathrm{d}{a}}{a^{d-1}\sqrt{1+\frac{a^{2}}{\ell^{2}}+\frac{\mathsf{c}}{2(d-1)(d-2)a^{2d-4}}}}

(2.4)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{8(d-1)(d-2)}\sqrt{1+\tfrac{a_{0}^{2}}{\ell^{2}}}\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}{\xi}}{\xi^{d-1}\sqrt{1+\frac{a_{0}^{2}}{\ell^{2}}\xi^{2}-\big(1+\frac{a_{0}^{2}}{\ell^{2}}\big)\frac{1}{\xi^{2d-4}}}}\,.

The geodesic speed
\mathsf{c}
has been traded in favor of the wormhole size
a_{0}
, which is the radius of the cross-sectional
S^{d-1}
at the wormhole neck and is the largest real root of the equation
a^{\prime}=0
in (
2.2
). This geodesic length is largest in the flat space limit,
\ell\to\infty
, and becomes smaller when the wormhole size is large in AdS units,
a_{0}\gg\ell
:

\pi\sqrt{\frac{2(d-1)}{d-2}}=D_{d}(0)\geq D_{d}\big(\tfrac{a_{0}}{\ell}\big)\geq D_{d}(\infty)=\pi\sqrt{\frac{2(d-2)}{d-1}}\,.

(2.5)

It is now easy to state the simple criterion which characterizes exactly when regular GS wormholes exist: there must exist a time-like geodesic for the target space
\mathrm{d}{s^{2}}=G_{ij}(\varphi)\,\mathrm{d}{\varphi^{i}}\mathrm{d}{\varphi^{j}}
which is
longer
than
D_{d}(\frac{a_{0}}{\ell})
. For an axio-dilaton pair with coupling
\beta
,

G_{ij}(\varphi)\,\mathrm{d}{\varphi^{i}}\mathrm{d}{\varphi^{j}}=\mathrm{d}{\phi^{2}}-e^{\beta\phi}\,\mathrm{d}{\chi^{2}}\,,

(2.6)

the longest time-like geodesic is of length
\frac{2\pi}{|\beta|}
and the flat space condition reads

\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}>\frac{d-1}{2(d-2)}\qquad(\ell\to\infty)\,.

(2.7)

With several decoupled axio-dilaton pairs the geodesic length can be made larger by traversing a “diagonal” direction and the regularity condition is weakened to

\sum_{i}\frac{1}{\beta_{i}^{2}}>\frac{d-1}{2(d-2)}\qquad(\ell\to\infty)\,.

(2.8)

In section
4
we will encounter AdS compactifications for which there is a scalar potential, in which case the solutions are no longer described by geodesics in the target space. Nevertheless, these GS solutions and their simple regularity criterion can provide some intuition for determining whether wormhole solutions exist in a more general setting.

Lifting flat space wormholes

3.1
The reduction

Consider IIA supergravity compactified on a 6-torus. We will only require the following subsector of the 10d IIA action (in 10d Einstein frame) involving metric, dilaton and RR 4-form flux:

S=\frac{1}{2\kappa_{4}^{2}}\int\mathrm{d}^{4}{x}\,\sqrt{|g|}\left(\mathcal{R}-\frac{1}{2}(\partial\phi)^{2}-\frac{1}{2\cdot 4!}e^{\phi/2}F_{4}^{2}\right)\,.

In 4d language this intersection is described by a supersymmetric instanton solution sourced by four axio-dilaton pairs. The reduction ansatz to 4d Einstein frame is

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}{s^{2}_{10}}

\displaystyle=e^{2a\varphi}\mathrm{d}{s^{2}_{4}}+e^{2b\varphi}\mathcal{M}_{mn}\mathrm{d}{\theta^{m}}\mathrm{d}{\theta^{n}}\,,

(3.3)

\displaystyle C_{3}

\displaystyle=\chi_{1}\,\mathrm{d}{\theta^{1}}\wedge\mathrm{d}{\theta^{2}}\wedge\mathrm{d}{\theta^{3}}+\chi_{2}\,\mathrm{d}{\theta^{1}}\wedge\mathrm{d}{\theta^{4}}\wedge\mathrm{d}{\theta^{5}}+\chi_{3}\,\mathrm{d}{\theta^{2}}\wedge\mathrm{d}{\theta^{5}}\wedge\mathrm{d}{\theta^{6}}+\chi_{4}\,\mathrm{d}{\theta^{3}}\wedge\mathrm{d}{\theta^{4}}\wedge\mathrm{d}{\theta^{6}}\,.

The

(3.4)

The

\sum_{i}\vec{\beta}_{i}=0

In total we count 11 scalars in 4d in our truncation: the dilaton

a^{2}=\frac{3}{16}\,,\qquad a=-3b\,.

(3.5)

The kinetic term for the

\frac{1}{4}\text{Tr}\!\left(\partial\mathcal{M}\partial\mathcal{M}^{-1}\right)\,,

(3.6)

\vec{\beta}_{i}\cdot\vec{\beta}_{j}=2\delta_{ij}-\frac{1}{3}

\displaystyle S=\frac{1}{2\kappa_{4}^{2}}\int\mathrm{d}^{4}{x}\,\sqrt{|g|}\left(\mathcal{R}-\frac{1}{2}(\partial\phi)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}(\partial\varphi)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{a=1}^{5}(\partial\Phi_{a})^{2}+\mathcal{L}_{\text{axions}}\right)\,,

(3.8)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{axions}}=-\frac{1}{2}e^{\phi/2-6b\varphi}\Big[

\displaystyle e^{-(\vec{\beta}_{1}+\vec{\beta}_{2}+\vec{\beta}_{3})\cdot\vec{\Phi}}(\partial\chi_{1})^{2}+e^{-(\vec{\beta}_{1}+\vec{\beta}_{4}+\vec{\beta}_{5})\cdot\vec{\Phi}}(\partial\chi_{2})^{2}

\displaystyle+e^{-(\vec{\beta}_{2}+\vec{\beta}_{5}+\vec{\beta}_{6})\cdot\vec{\Phi}}(\partial\chi_{3})^{2}+e^{-(\vec{\beta}_{3}+\vec{\beta}_{4}+\vec{\beta}_{6})\cdot\vec{\Phi}}(\partial\chi_{4})^{2}\Big]\,.

(3.9)

If one groups the seven scalars
\phi,\varphi,\Phi_{1},\ldots,\Phi_{5}

\vec{t}

\mathcal{L}_{\text{axions}}=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{4}e^{\vec{\alpha}_{i}\cdot\vec{t}}(\partial\chi_{i})^{2}\,,

(3.10)

where the
\vec{\alpha}_{i}

\vec{\alpha}_{i}\cdot\vec{\alpha}_{j}=4\delta_{ij}
.
Hence we can construct a new base of canonically normalised scalars as

s_{i}=\frac{1}{2}\vec{\alpha}_{i}\cdot\vec{t}

(3.11)

\displaystyle S=\frac{1}{2\kappa_{4}^{2}}\int\mathrm{d}^{4}{x}\,\sqrt{|g|}\left(\mathcal{R}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{4}\left[(\partial s_{i})^{2}+e^{2s_{i}}(\partial\chi_{i})^{2}\right]-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=5}^{7}(\partial s_{k})^{2}\right)\,.

(3.12)

Upon going to Euclidean signature the axion kinetic terms effectively flip sign, or alternatively, we can describe them by tensors obtained from
F_{6}
reduction.
We will further truncate

\sum_{i=1}^{4}\frac{1}{\beta_{i}^{2}}=1>\frac{3}{4}=\left(\frac{D_{4}(0)}{2\pi}\right)^{2}\,.

(3.13)

Therefore we conclude that there exist regular GS wormholes. It is critical that there were (at least) four decoupled axio-dilaton pairs so as to exceed the

We can make a further truncation and work with the single effective axio-dilaton pair that has

3.4
Wick rotation

Since a stack of parallel Euclidean D2-branes can be Wick-rotated to a stack of Lorentzian D2-branes, one can wonder whether we have found a contradiction with the non-existence of over-extremal D-branes, since the Wick rotation of our wormhole would be an intersection of Lorentzian D2-branes with total tension smaller than charge, which cannot exist (i.e. be without naked singularities, let alone regular). This is, however, not the case, and the reason is that our solutions involve
intersecting
branes. For example, consider Wick rotating the
\theta_{1}
coordinate. This would make the D2-branes, wrapping directions 123 and 145 Lorentzian, but the others remain Euclidean, with “wrong sign”
F_{4}
energies. Hence we find no contradiction; this heavily relies upon the separate axio-dilaton pairs having too-large couplings, such that we were forced to invoke multiple axions and hence multiple intersecting Euclidean branes.

4
Lifting AdS wormholes

In this section we construct regular AdS wormholes in type IIB. The new solutions necessarily involve models in which the dilaton partners of the axions are not massless so that the geodesic motion is distorted. In such a case there is no easy regularity criterion as for geodesics. A recent simple bottom-up analysis
[
35
]
, related to older work
[
13
]
, studies the possible wormhole solutions for an axion with a massive dilaton of the following kind

(4.1)

where

(4.2)

In
[

it was found (for

) that if

, then there is at least a critical mass above which the wormhole exists. After all, in the large-

m^{2}
is of the order of
\Lambda
it is unclear what happens, especially when there is more than a single axio-dilaton pair present.

We start first by recalling the AdS embeddings of Euclidean wormholes pioneered in
[
27

and comment on their relation with the distance conjecture.

4.1
Type IIB on
S^{5}/\mathbb{Z}_{k}

It was noticed in
[
27
]
that the problem of fitting regular wormholes into the well-known
\mathrm{AdS}_{5}\times S^{5}
background could be alleviated by orbifolding to
\mathrm{AdS}_{5}\times S^{5}/\mathbb{Z}_{k}
. The orbifolding generates extra moduli and the effective geodesic distance can be enlarged in a similar way to what was envisaged in
[
29

. The dual description is the so-called
\mathcal{N}=2

[
36

and an interpretation in terms of the dual CFT was carried out in
[

[
33
]
: the wormholes violate the dual BPS bound since

\langle\Tr(F_{i}-\star F_{i})^{2}\rangle<0\,.

(4.3)

Here

and
F_{i}
is the Yang-Mills field strength tensor of the

node. Although the operator is manifestly positive, the holographic computation of the dual one-point function (vev) fails to respect it. This is simply a manifestation that in gravity we can violate BPS bounds, but not in field theories and wormholes are therefore problematic since they correspond to over-extremal instantons. One would hope that the gravity computation somehow signals the sickness, yet the solution is completely smooth inside the
\mathrm{AdS}_{5}
factor. What happens inside the orbifolded 5-sphere is less clear since there are already the orbifold singularities and the flow of the scalars happens in the twisted sector. Hence the problem of lifting the wormholes is not so easy and we refrain from attempting this. Instead in the next subsection we discuss the closely related solution of type IIB on
T^{1,1}
which is related by an RG flow to the
S^{5}/\mathbb{Z}_{2}
theory and has the benefit of being completely explicit as there is no orbifolding. But before we do so we want to comment on the distance conjecture.

For this purpose we need some details about the wormhole solutions. The sigma model of the orbifolded setup is given by
\SL(k+1,\mathbb{R})/\GL(k,\mathbb{R})
in the Euclidean version of the supergravity theory. The corresponding metric on this coset space (i.e. kinetic term) reads

\mathrm{d}{s^{2}}=4\,\mathrm{d}{U^{2}}-e^{4U}\mathcal{N}^{2}+e^{-2U}\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\big(\mathrm{d}{\zeta_{i}^{2}}-\mathrm{d}{\tilde{\zeta}_{i}^{2}}\big)

(4.4)

(4.5)

So we have
2k

\tilde{\zeta}_{i}
are the “Wick-rotated axions.” To cut a long story short
[
27

36
]
, one can show that the regular geodesics can be brought, by means of an isometry transformation, to a simple geodesic of an
\SL(2,\mathbb{R})/\SO(1,1)
subspace formed by a single axio-dilaton pair with coupling
\beta=1

2(k-1)
fields are constant. The surprising fact is that the geodesic length, in string units, remains fixed with respect to

M_{\text{Pl}}^{3}=M_{\text{s}}^{3}g_{\text{s}}^{-2}\,\text{Vol}_{5}\,,

(4.6)

where

is the string scale and Vol

is the volume of the internal space in string units. Note that this volume goes down with a factor
k

k^{1/3}
in Planck units and can therefore be made arbitrary large, indicating an apparent violation of the distance conjecture. However, note that our system is such that the number of massless degrees of freedom scales with

so that in terms of the species scale the distance remains constant. This is therefore consistent with the most naïve formulation of the distance conjecture for Euclidean theories.

We want to emphasize there is yet no clear definition of the distance conjecture in Euclidean theories because the target space metric is not positive definite.

A similar subtlety arises in formulating the AdS distance conjecture

with metric spaces whose metrics are not positive definite. This subtlety motivated a recent study

We will be agnostic about this and have a pragmatic view in which we simply compute the absolute value of the geodesic distance, but it could very well be that only the distance in the dilaton space is to be considered for the distance conjecture. The reason is that axion momenta are really more like quantised fluxes and, when dualised to higher forms, would not be considered in the standard definition of the target space distance.

5
Conclusion

In this paper we have described, for the first time, Euclidean wormholes of Giddings-Strominger type from a 10d viewpoint. This has furthermore lead to new Euclidean axion wormhole solutions in AdS compactifications from considering type IIB supergravity on
T^{1,1}
. Let us summarize our findings.

•

Both the Euclidean wormhole solutions in flat space and AdS can be tuned parametrically into the supergravity regime such that they can be trusted as
bona fide
string backgrounds. This is despite the scalars flowing through distances of a few Planck lengths.

•

Since the extremal limits of the wormholes give BPS Euclidean branes there is a naïve paradox how wormholes can be regarded in this 10d picture since branes do not have regular over-extremal limits. We noticed that supergravity evades this paradox by only allowing regular wormholes when enough branes intersect in such a way that a Lorentzian interpretation in terms of over-extremal branes is impossible.

•

We have found entirely new and explicit, yet numerically constructed, wormhole solutions in AdS compactifications. In the main text we discussed the solutions in type IIB on
\mathrm{AdS}_{5}\times T^{1,1}
which implies a concrete holographic dual in the form of the Klebanov-Witten gauge theory. In an appendix we present a somewhat simpler solution in massive type IIA, but since the vacuum is non-SUSY a holographic dual is not known. An important ingredient in both constructions is the inclusion of massive scalars.

•

Since the wormholes are sourced by scalars whose dual operators are known, we could verify that the wormholes violate operator positivity in the dual CFT. This was also found earlier in
[
28
]
but for wormholes inside
\mathrm{AdS}_{5}\times S^{5}/\mathbb{Z}_{k}
where the orbifolding obscures a simple 10d understanding.

•

We have then speculated how changing these wormhole solutions into wormholes connected to the same boundary would escape violating the positivity bound. This suggests, once again, that AdS/CFT with multiple boundaries is not well understood.

These findings have also raised further puzzles. Our wormholes traversed super-Planckian distances in field space, but not parametrically large, so there is no sharp contradiction with the distance conjecture. Yet we have made clear that, strictly speaking, there is no clear definition of the distance conjecture in Euclidean spacetime signature since the scalar manifold then becomes of indefinite signature. Should only the distance in dilaton space be measured? A more striking puzzle is the understanding of the two-boundary wormholes we constructed. The violation of operator positivity shows that the holographic dictionary in the presence of two boundaries is not fully understood. It is our hope that our explicit wormholes can help further studies in this regard.

Finally, we note that the explicit AdS axion wormholes that we have constructed have little bearing on discussions of baby universes,
\alpha
-parameters and factorization, other than reinforcing that the issues they raise are alive and well in string theory. It has been argued in
[
4
]
that the baby universe Hilbert space should be one-dimensional, meaning that there is a great redundancy amongst states of different topologies. Indeed, in
[
44
]
it was argued that a dramatic reduction in
\dim\mathcal{H}_{\text{BU}}
compared to naïve expectations can occur due to a large number of null states. There, this effect was made explicit in toy models where the sum over topologies could be done exactly. Importantly, it was shown that this effect is completely invisible in perturbation theory, e.g. by truncating a topological expansion or working only at low-energies, as we have done here. Also, it is perhaps suggestive that for our solutions the limit of small wormholes in AdS units (but still very large in string or Planck units) where one would hope to integrate out their effects
à la
Coleman is
exactly
where the scalars
u,v,\phi
controlling the 10d curvatures and couplings become large, invalidating the low-energy effective description.

\displaystyle=i\sqrt{10}\,\ell^{-1}g_{\text{s}}^{-1/4}\,\left(\ell^{4}\mathrm{vol}_{4}\right)\,.

This solution was first found in
[
45
]
(see also
[
46
]
).
The ansatz we use for wormholes inside this background is the following,

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}{s_{10}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\ell^{2}e^{-3u}\left[f(r)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}{r^{2}}+q(r)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}{\Omega_{3}^{2}}\right]+\ell^{2}e^{u}\big(\mathrm{d}{\Omega_{3,1}^{2}}+\mathrm{d}{\Omega_{3,2}^{2}}\big)\,,

(B.2)

\displaystyle e^{\Phi}

\displaystyle=g_{\text{s}}e^{\phi}\,,

\displaystyle F_{0}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2}\,\ell^{-1}g_{\text{s}}^{-5/4}\,,

\displaystyle F_{4}

\displaystyle=i\sqrt{10}\,\ell^{-1}g_{\text{s}}^{-1/4}e^{-9u-\phi/2}\,\left(\ell^{4}\mathrm{vol}_{4}\right)+\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}\,\ell^{3}g_{\text{s}}^{-1/4}\,\mathrm{d}{\chi}\wedge\big(\mathrm{vol}_{3,1}+\mathrm{vol}_{3,2}\big)\,,

with
u,\phi\to 0
for
r\to\infty
. In this ansatz we already restrict to an isotropic axion for simplicity, analogous to what was done at the end of section
3.1
. It is straightforward to extend the analysis that follows to two axions, one for each
S^{3}
, and two radions.

The 4d equations of motion for
g_{\mu\nu}
,
u
,
\phi
and
\chi
which result from the above ansatz can be derived from the 4d action

S_{4}=\frac{1}{2\kappa_{4}^{2}}\int\Big({\star\mathcal{R}}-6\,\mathrm{d}{u}\wedge{\star\mathrm{d}{u}}-\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{d}{\phi}\wedge{\star\mathrm{d}{\phi}}+\frac{1}{2}e^{-3u+\phi/2}\mathrm{d}{\chi}\wedge{\star\mathrm{d}{\chi}}-{\star\mathcal{V}}\Big)

(B.3)

with scalar potential

\mathcal{V}=-12e^{-4u}+5e^{-9u-\phi/2}+e^{-3u+5\phi/2}\,.

(B.4)

Note that we scaled out
\ell
and
g_{\text{s}}
; by design, the scalar potential takes on its minimum value of
\mathcal{V}=-6
at
u=\phi=0
. Upon finding solutions in terms of these rescaled fields, one can always arrange for weak coupling and small curvature everywhere by choosing
\ell,g_{\text{s}}
in (
B.2
) appropriately.

Because of the scalar potential in the action of equation (
B.3
), there are no GS wormhole solutions. We can, however, gain some insight into whether regular wormholes exist by artificially making
u
and
\phi
massless via the
ad hoc
substitution
\mathcal{V}\to-6
just like what was done in section
4.2.4
. Having done this, the 4d action can be rewritten as

\frac{1}{2\kappa_{4}^{2}}\int\Big({\star(\mathcal{R}+6)}-\frac{1}{2}\delta_{ij}\mathrm{d}{\varphi^{i}}\wedge{\star\mathrm{d}{\varphi^{j}}}+\frac{1}{2}e^{\varphi^{1}}\mathrm{d}{\chi}\wedge{\star\mathrm{d}{\chi}}\Big)\,,

(B.5)

where

\varphi^{1}=-3u+\frac{\phi}{2}\,,\qquad\varphi^{2}=\sqrt{3}\left(u+\frac{\phi}{2}\right)\,.

(B.6)

Thus in this “approximation” there is a GS wormhole solution with
\varphi^{2}
decoupled and
\varphi^{1},\chi
comprising an axio-dilaton pair with
\beta=1
, which is regular everywhere for any choice of wormhole size
q_{0}
(equivalently, any
\mathsf{c}<0
):

\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}>\frac{3}{4}=\left(\frac{D_{4}(0)}{2\pi}\right)^{2}\geq\left(\frac{D_{4}(q_{0})}{2\pi}\right)^{2}\,,\qquad\mathsf{c}=-12q_{0}^{4}(1+q_{0}^{2})\,.

(B.7)

In restoring the correct functional form for
\mathcal{V}
it is clear that regular wormhole solutions will continue to exist, since the potential only helps to further stabilize
u
and
\phi
near their asymptotic values of zero.

As in section
4.2
we again choose

q(r)=\sqrt{q_{0}^{2}+r^{2}}

(B.8)

and solve the axion equation of motion in terms of a charge
\mathfrak{q}
as

{\star(e^{-3u+\phi/2}\,\mathrm{d}{\chi})}\equiv\mathfrak{q}\,\mathrm{vol}_{3}\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad\mathrm{d}{\chi}=e^{3u-\phi/2}\mathfrak{q}\,\mathrm{d}{h}\,,

(B.9)

where here the harmonic function
h(r)
solves
h^{\prime}=f/q^{3}
, i.e.
\star\mathrm{d}{h}=\mathrm{vol}_{3}
. This leaves only
f
,
u
and
\phi
to be found numerically. Here the string dilaton has mass of order the AdS scale, ultimately originating from the non-zero background fluxes. Expanding the potential around
u=\phi=0
, one identifies the two mass eigenstates:

\displaystyle 6u-5\phi

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle\qquad m_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle=6\,,

\displaystyle\qquad\Delta_{1}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{3}{2}+\sqrt{\tfrac{9}{4}+6}=4.3722\ldots\,,

(B.10)

\displaystyle 10u+\phi

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle m_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=20\,,

\displaystyle\Delta_{2}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{3}{2}+\sqrt{\tfrac{9}{4}+20}=6.2169\ldots\,.

Both
u
and
\phi
are sourced by the axion with a term that goes as
\mathfrak{q}^{2}(\partial\chi)^{2}\sim\mathfrak{q}^{2}f^{-2}(h^{\prime})^{2}\sim\mathfrak{q}^{2}/r^{6}
for large
r
. Therefore we should find that for
r\to\infty
the scalars go to zero as

6u-5\phi=\frac{A_{1}}{r^{\Delta_{1}}}+\mathfrak{q}^{2}\,\frac{A_{2}}{r^{6}}+\cdots\,,\qquad 10u+\phi=\mathfrak{q}^{2}\,\frac{A_{3}}{r^{6}}+\frac{A_{4}}{r^{\Delta_{2}}}+\cdots\,,

(B.11)

with
A_{i}
some numerical coefficients.

The shooting method used is structured as follows. For fixed
q_{0}>0
and
r_{\text{max}}\gg\max(1,q_{0})
the equations of motion are solved for
r\leq r_{\text{max}}
with initial conditions

f(0)=f_{0}\,,\qquad u(0)=u_{0}\,,\qquad\phi(0)=\phi_{0}\,,\qquad f^{\prime}(0)=u^{\prime}(0)=\phi^{\prime}(0)=0\,.

(B.12)

Constructing a perturbative solution around
r=0
shows that
f_{0}
and
\mathfrak{q}
are determined in terms of
u_{0},\phi_{0}
:

\displaystyle f_{0}^{-2}

\displaystyle=2-\frac{q_{0}^{2}}{2}\mathcal{V}(u_{0},\phi_{0})\,,

(B.13)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{q}^{2}

\displaystyle=4e^{-3u_{0}+\phi_{0}/2}q_{0}^{4}\left(1+f_{0}^{-2}\right)\,.

Generic choices for
u_{0},\phi_{0}
lead to singular geometries where
f(r)
diverges: the integration is halted at
r=r_{\text{halt}}
if
f(r_{\text{halt}})>\frac{3}{q}
. The value of
r_{\text{halt}}\leq r_{\text{max}}
in the
(u_{0},\phi_{0})
parameter space, along with curves where the final values of the fields,
{u_{\text{halt}}=u(r_{\text{halt}})}
and
{\phi_{\text{halt}}=\phi(r_{\text{halt}})}
, vanish are shown in figure
7
for
q_{0}=1
.

One may engineer an objective function
\mathcal{U}
which depends implicitly on
u_{0},\phi_{0}
through
r_{\text{halt}},u_{\text{halt}},\phi_{\text{halt}}
so that minimizing
\mathcal{U}
ensures the solution is smooth and may be glued onto the AdS boundary conditions. We choose

\mathcal{U}(u_{0},\phi_{0})=\begin{cases}A_{1}+|f_{0}^{-2}|&f_{0}^{-2}\leq 0\\
1+A_{2}\big(\frac{r_{\text{max}}}{r_{\text{halt}}}-1\big)+u_{\text{halt}}^{2}+\phi_{\text{halt}}^{2}&r_{\text{halt}}<r_{\text{max}}\\
1-\frac{1}{1+[(\phi-1.2u)_{\infty}^{\text{est}}]^{2}+[(u+0.1\phi)_{\infty}^{\text{est}}]^{2}}&r_{\text{halt}}=r_{\text{max}}\end{cases}

(B.14)

with
A_{1},A_{2}
two large, fixed constants. The values
(\phi-1.2u)_{\infty}^{\text{est}}
and
(u+0.1\phi)_{\infty}^{\text{est}}
are estimates for the boundary values of the mass eigenstates, leveraging the known power-law behavior in equation (
B.11
):

\displaystyle(\phi-1.2u)_{\infty}^{\text{est}}

\displaystyle=(\phi-1.2u)_{\text{halt}}+\frac{r_{\text{halt}}}{\Delta_{1}}(\phi^{\prime}-1.2u^{\prime})_{\text{halt}}\,,

(B.15)

\displaystyle(u+0.1\phi)_{\infty}^{\text{est}}

\displaystyle=(u+0.1\phi)_{\text{halt}}+\frac{r_{\text{halt}}}{6}(u^{\prime}+0.1\phi^{\prime})_{\text{halt}}\,.

The objective function has a minimal value of zero where
r_{\text{halt}}=r_{\text{max}}
and both extrapolated values
(\phi-1.2u)_{\infty}^{\text{est}}
and
(u+0.1\phi)_{\infty}^{\text{est}}
are zero. Standard optimization techniques, such as gradient descent or the Nelder–Mead method, applied to
\mathcal{U}
yield the optimal initial conditions. By successively increasing
r_{\text{max}}
and extrapolating to
r_{\text{max}}=\infty
, one may quickly determine
u_{0},\phi_{0}
very accurately; for example, the initial conditions found for
q_{0}=1
are

\displaystyle u_{0}

\displaystyle=\mathtt{-0.07891\;87232\ldots}\;,

(B.16)

\displaystyle\phi_{0}

\displaystyle=\mathtt{+0.29432\;16217\ldots}\;,

corresponding to the red ‘
+
’ and red profiles in figure
7
.

We have thus found a one-parameter family of solutions, controlled by
q_{0}
, for which the scalar field ranges and axion charge are easily determined in terms of the optimized initial conditions. Figure
8
shows how these characteristics of the wormhole depend on the wormhole size; for all

the field ranges are bounded by that of the GS “approximation” corresponding to (

), as expected. In particular, even for wormholes much smaller than the AdS scale the scalars always lie in the bounded regions

and

. This is in stark contrast to the solutions for

of section

B.2
Relationship with flat space solutions

Because the scalars
u
and
\phi
are well-behaved in the limit
q_{0}\to 0
where the wormhole is made small in AdS units, we can make the flat space limit of these solutions precise. Returning to equation (
B.2
) and introducing the rescaled variables
\rho
and
a
through
r=\rho/\ell
and
q=a/\ell
and using stereographic projection to write the 3-sphere metrics as
{\ell^{2}\,\mathrm{d}{\Omega_{3,i}^{2}}=\big(1+\frac{|{\bf x}|^{2}}{4\ell^{2}}\big)^{-2}(\mathrm{d}{\bf x}\cdot\mathrm{d}{\bf x})}
,
we can then take
\ell\to\infty
to find

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}{s_{10}^{2}}

\displaystyle=e^{-3u}\left(f^{2}\,\mathrm{d}{\rho^{2}}+a^{2}\,\mathrm{d}{\Omega_{3}^{2}}\right)+e^{u}\,\mathrm{d}{s_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}^{2}}\,,

(B.17)

\displaystyle e^{\Phi}

\displaystyle=g_{\text{s}}e^{\phi}\,,

\displaystyle F_{0}

\displaystyle=0\,,

\displaystyle F_{4}

\displaystyle=0+\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}\,g_{\text{s}}^{-1/4}\,\mathrm{d}{\chi}\wedge\big(\mathrm{d}{x^{1}}\wedge\mathrm{d}{x^{2}}\wedge\mathrm{d}{x^{3}}+\mathrm{d}{x^{4}}\wedge\mathrm{d}{x^{5}}\wedge\mathrm{d}{x^{6}}\big)\,,

In particular the background
F_{0},F_{4}
fluxes vanish and one has “zoomed in” on the north poles of the
S^{3}
s to obtain an
\mathbb{R}^{6}
factor. After quotienting to obtain a 6-torus, we arrive at
non-supersymmetric
solutions of GS type, similar to those in section
3
.
We can explicitly see this flat space limit in the numerical solutions by looking at a family of solutions with fixed
a_{0}=\ell q_{0}
and increasing
\ell
. In figure
9
we see several rescaled profiles for
a_{0}=1
which exhibit the point-wise convergence to a flat space GS solution with
\beta=1
. An intermediate region,
a_{0}\lesssim\rho\lesssim\ell
, emerges in which
f\approx 1
and
u,\phi\sim\rho^{-2}
, before matching on to the AdS solution for
\rho\gtrsim\ell
. In the strict
\ell\to\infty
limit this intermediate region becomes the asymptotically-flat region at infinity.

Weighted Norm Inequalities for the Opdam–Cherednik Transform Thanks: Research supported by ERC Starting Grant No. 713927.
Shyam Swarup Mondal, Anirudha Poria

Abstract
. In this paper, we study several weighted norm inequalities for the Opdam–Cherednik transform. We establish different versions of the Heisenberg–Pauli–Weyl inequality for this transform. In particular, we give an extension of this inequality using weights with different exponents and present a variation of the inequality that incorporates L p L^{p} -norms for the Opdam–Cherednik transform. Also, we prove a version of the Hardy–Littlewood inequality for this transform. Finally, we give other variations of the Heisenberg–Pauli–Weyl inequality such as the Nash-type and Clarkson-type inequalities for the Opdam–Cherednik transform.

1.
Introduction

Uncertainty principles have long been a mainstay of mathematical physics and classical Fourier analysis and have been studied from different points of view by several authors
[
8
,
12
]
. The classical uncertainty principle is an essential restriction in harmonic analysis and it states that a non-zero function and its Fourier transform cannot be simultaneously sharply localized, i.e., it is impossible for a non-zero function and its Fourier transform to be simultaneously small. Depending on various ways of measuring the localization of a function, one can obtain different interpretations of the uncertainty principle in different contexts. Some classical uncertainty principles for the Fourier transform are Hardy
[
11
]
, Cowling and Price
[
5
]
, Morgan
[
20
]
, and Beurling
[
14
]
theorems. Uncertainty inequalities are some special class of uncertainty principles that give us information about how a function and its Fourier transform relate and have got considerable importance in signal analysis, physics, optics, and many other well-known areas
[
28
,
10
,
7
,
3
]
. A well-known example of uncertainty inequality is the Heisenberg–Pauli–Weyl (HPW) inequality. The remarkable Heisenberg uncertainty principle
[
13
]
was generalized by Weyl in
[
35
]
and attributed the result to Pauli. The HPW inequality states that for every
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have

\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}x_{j}^{2}|f(x)|^{2}\;dx\right)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\lambda_{j}^{2}|\widehat{f}(\lambda)|^{2}\;d\lambda\right)\geq\frac{1}{4}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x)|^{2}\;dx\right)^{2},\quad j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}.

Another variation of the HPW inequality states that for every
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\alpha>0
, there exists a positive constant
C_{\alpha}
such that

\||\cdot|^{\alpha}f\|_{2}\;\||\cdot|^{\alpha}\widehat{f}\|_{2}\geq C_{\alpha}\|f\|_{2}^{2}.

A strong additive version of this inequality for the classical Fourier transform on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
was proved by Cowling and Price in
[
5
]
and it states that for all
p,q\in[1,\infty]
and for all tempered functions
f
for which
\widehat{f}
is a function, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\||\cdot|^{a}f\|_{p}+\||\cdot|^{b}\widehat{f}\|_{q}\geq C\|f\|_{2}^{2},\quad a,b>0

if and only if

a>\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}
and
b>\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}.

Considerable attention has been devoted to discovering generalizations to new contexts for the HPW inequality and its weighted variations for various generalized transforms by several researchers. For instance, these uncertainty inequalities were obtained in
[
27
]
for the classical Fourier transform, in
[
23
]
for the Fourier transform associated with the Riemann–Liouville operator, in
[
21
]
for the Fourier transform associated with the spherical mean operator, in
[
16
]
for the Heckman–Opdam transform, in
[
30
,
32
]
for the Dunkl transform, and in
[
29
]
for the generalized Hankel transform. Further, Wiener in
[
36
]
established the HPW inequality using the Hermite polynomials for the classical Fourier transform.
Building on the ideas of Ciatti–Ricci–Sundari
[
4
]
, Soltani in
[
33
]
proved a more general form of the HPW inequality for the Dunkl transform using weights with different exponents. Moreover, Johansen in
[
15
]
proved several weighted inequalities and uncertainty principles for the
(k,a)
-generalized Fourier transform.
In this paper, we study the HPW inequality and its several weighted variations for the Opdam–Cherednik transform.

Recently, various uncertainty principles were investigated for the Opdam–Cherednik transform. Daher et al. in
[
6
]
studied some qualitative uncertainty principles for the Cherednik transform as a generalization of the Euclidean uncertainty principles for the Fourier transform. Mejjaoli in
[
18
]
obtained some qualitative uncertainty principles for the Opdam–Cherednik transform. These results were further extended to modulation spaces by the second author in
[
26
]
. Moreover, the Benedicks-type uncertainty principle for the Opdam–Cherednik transform was investigated by Achak and Daher in
[
1
]
. In this paper, we establish several weighted norm inequalities for the Opdam–Cherednik transform. We prove different versions of the HPW inequality for this transform. In particular, we give an extension of this inequality using weights with different exponents and present a variation of the inequality that incorporates
L^{p}
-norms for the Opdam–Cherednik transform. Also, we study a version of the Hardy–Littlewood inequality for this transform. Finally, we give other variations of the HPW inequality such as the Nash-type and Clarkson-type inequalities for the Opdam–Cherednik transform. Mainly, we establish the following uncertainty inequalities for the Opdam–Cherednik transform. First, we prove that for every
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
, there exists a constant
C(\alpha,\beta)>0
such that

\||\cdot|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}\;\||\cdot|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}\geq C(\alpha,\beta)\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}^{2}.

Then, we give another variation of the HPW inequality using weights with different exponents as follows:
for every
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
and
a,b\geq 1
, there exists a constant
C(\alpha,\beta)>0
such that

\||\cdot|^{a}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}^{\frac{b}{a+b}}\;\||\cdot|^{b}{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}^{\frac{a}{a+b}}\geq C(\alpha,\beta)^{\frac{ab}{a+b}}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}.

Moreover, using the heat kernel decay estimates, we obtain a version of the HPW inequality which incorporates
L^{p}
-norms and it states as follows: let
p\in(1,2],b>0,0<a<\frac{1}{q}
, where
q
is the conjugate exponent of
p
. Then for every
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
and any
t>1
, there exists a constant
C(a,b)>0
such that for all
b\leq 2
, we have

\left\|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}\leq C(a,b)\;t_{0}^{\frac{1}{2q}+1}\;e^{\frac{2\rho t_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{q}}\||\cdot|^{a}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}^{\frac{b}{a+b}}\;\||\cdot|^{b}{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}^{\frac{a}{a+b}},

and for all
b>2
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}

\displaystyle\leq C(a,1)^{\frac{b(a+1)}{a+b}}\left[b(b-1)^{\frac{1}{b}-1}\right]^{\frac{ab}{a+b}}\bigg(t_{1}^{\frac{1}{2q}+1}e^{\frac{2\rho t_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{q}}\bigg)^{\frac{b(a+1)}{a+b}}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\||\cdot|^{a}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}^{\frac{b}{a+b}}\;\||\cdot|^{b}{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}^{\frac{a}{a+b}},

where
t_{0}=t_{0}(a,b)=\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^{\frac{2}{a+b}}\big(N\frac{\left\||\cdot|^{a}f\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},A{\alpha,\beta})}}{\||\cdot|^{b}{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}}\big)^{\frac{2}{a+b}},N\in\mathbb{N}
and
t_{1}=t_{0}(a,1)
.
Further, we give some other variations of the HPW inequality that involves a mixed of
L^{1}
and
L^{2}
norms estimate. In particular, we prove the Nash-type and Clarkson-type inequalities for the Opdam–Cherednik transform. The Nash-type inequality states as follows: for every
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
and
s>0
, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\left\|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}^{2}\leq C\left\|f\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}^{\frac{4s}{2\alpha+3+2s}}\left\||\cdot|^{s}{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}^{\frac{2(2\alpha+3)}{2\alpha+3+2s}}.

Finally, we prove the Clarkson-type inequality as follows: for every
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
and
s>0
, there exists a constant
C>0
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\left\|f\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}\leq C\exp\left\{{\rho\left(N\frac{\left\||\cdot|^{2s}f\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}}{\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}}\right)^{\frac{2}{1+4s}}}\right\}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}^{\frac{4s}{1+4s}}\left\||\cdot|^{2s}f\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}^{\frac{1}{1+4s}}.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we present some preliminaries related to the Opdam–Cherednik transform. Also, we recall some basic definitions and properties of the weak type
L^{p}
-space for
\sigma
-finite measure spaces. In Section
3
, we prove several weighted norm inequalities for the Opdam–Cherednik transform. First, we establish a version of the Hardy–Littlewood inequality for this transform. Then, we prove a version of the HPW inequality for the Opdam–Cherednik transform and give an extension of this inequality using weights with different exponents. Also, we present another variation of the HPW inequality for this transform, which incorporates
L^{p}
-norms. Finally, we study other variations of the HPW inequality. In particular, we give the Nash-type and Clarkson-type inequalities for the Opdam–Cherednik transform.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Harmonic analysis and the Opdam–Cherednik transform

In this subsection, we collect the necessary definitions and results from the harmonic analysis related to the Opdam–Cherednik transform. The main references for this subsection are
[
2
,
17
,
24
,
25
,
31
]
. However, we will use the same notation as in
[
26
]
.

Let
T_{\alpha,\beta}
denote the Jacobi–Cherednik differential–difference operator (also called the Dunkl–Cherednik operator)

T_{\alpha,\beta}f(x)=\frac{d}{dx}f(x)+\Big[(2\alpha+1)\coth x+(2\beta+1)\tanh x\Big]\frac{f(x)-f(-x)}{2}-\rho f(-x),

where
\alpha,\beta
are two parameters satisfying
\alpha\geq\beta\geq-\frac{1}{2}
and
\alpha>-\frac{1}{2}
, and
\rho=\alpha+\beta+1
. Let
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}
. The Opdam hypergeometric functions
G^{\alpha,\beta}_{\lambda}
on
\mathbb{R}
are eigenfunctions
T_{\alpha,\beta}G^{\alpha,\beta}_{\lambda}(x)=i\lambda G^{\alpha,\beta}_{\lambda}(x)
of
T_{\alpha,\beta}
that are normalized such that
G^{\alpha,\beta}_{\lambda}(0)=1
. The eigenfunction
G^{\alpha,\beta}_{\lambda}
is given by

G^{\alpha,\beta}_{\lambda}(x)=\varphi^{\alpha,\beta}_{\lambda}(x)-\frac{1}{\rho-i\lambda}\frac{d}{dx}\varphi^{\alpha,\beta}_{\lambda}(x)=\varphi^{\alpha,\beta}_{\lambda}(x)+\frac{\rho+i\lambda}{4(\alpha+1)}\sinh 2x\;\varphi^{\alpha+1,\beta+1}_{\lambda}(x),

where
\varphi^{\alpha,\beta}_{\lambda}(x)={}_{2}F_{1}\left(\frac{\rho+i\lambda}{2},\frac{\rho-i\lambda}{2};\alpha+1;-\sinh^{2}x\right)
is the classical Jacobi function.

For every
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
, the eigenfunction

G^{\alpha,\beta}_{\lambda}
satisfy

|G^{\alpha,\beta}_{\lambda}(x)|\leq C\;e^{-\rho|x|}e^{|\text{Im}(\lambda)||x|},

where
C
is a positive constant. Since
\rho>0
, we have

|G^{\alpha,\beta}_{\lambda}(x)|\leq C\;e^{|\text{Im}(\lambda)||x|}.

(1)

Let us denote by
C_{c}(\mathbb{R})
the space of continuous functions on
\mathbb{R}
with compact support. The Opdam–Cherednik transform is the Fourier transform in the trigonometric Dunkl setting, and it is defined as follows.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\alpha\geq\beta\geq-\frac{1}{2}
with
\alpha>-\frac{1}{2}
. The Opdam–Cherednik transform
\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\beta}(f)
of a function
f\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R})
is defined by

{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}(f)(\lambda)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)\;G^{\alpha,\beta}_{\lambda}(-x)\;A_{\alpha,\beta}(x)dx\quad\text{for all }\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}},

where

\displaystyle A_{\alpha,\beta}(x)=(\sinh|x|)^{2\alpha+1}(\cosh|x|)^{2\beta+1}.

(2)

The inverse Opdam–Cherednik transform for a suitable function
g
on
\mathbb{R}
is given by

{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}^{-1}(g)(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(\lambda)\;G^{\alpha,\beta}_{\lambda}(x)\;d\sigma_{\alpha,\beta}(\lambda)\quad\text{for all }x\in\mathbb{R},

where

d\sigma_{\alpha,\beta}(\lambda)=\left(1-\dfrac{\rho}{i\lambda}\right)\dfrac{d\lambda}{8\pi|C_{\alpha,\beta}(\lambda)|^{2}}

and

C_{\alpha,\beta}(\lambda)=\dfrac{2^{\rho-i\lambda}\Gamma(\alpha+1)\Gamma(i\lambda)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{\rho+i\lambda}{2}\right)\;\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha-\beta+1+i\lambda}{2}\right)},\quad\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus i\mathbb{N}.

We have the following estimates for
C_{\alpha,\beta}
(see
[
18
]
). There exists
N>0
such that for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
with
|\lambda|\geq N
and for constants
k_{1},k_{2}>0
, we have

\displaystyle k_{1}|\lambda|^{2\alpha+2}\leq|C_{\alpha,\beta}(\lambda)|^{-2}\leq k_{2}|\lambda|^{2\alpha+2}.

(3)

The Plancherel formula is given by

\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(x)|^{2}A_{\alpha,\beta}(x)dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}(f)(\lambda)\overline{{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}(\check{f})(-\lambda)}\;d\sigma_{\alpha,\beta}(\lambda),

(4)

where
\check{f}(x):=f(-x)
.

Let
L^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
(resp.
L^{p}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})
),
p\in[1,\infty]
, denote the
L^{p}
-spaces corresponding to the measure
A_{\alpha,\beta}(x)dx
(resp.
d|\sigma_{\alpha,\beta}|(x)
). Let
p\in[1,2)
and
p^{\prime}
is the conjugate exponent of
p
. Then there exists a constant
C_{p}>0
such that

\displaystyle\left\|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\right\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}\leq C_{p}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})},

(5)

for all
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
(see
[
16
]
). In particular, if
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
, then

\displaystyle\left\|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}\leq\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}.

(6)

Let
t>0
. The heat kernel
E^{\alpha,\beta}_{t}
associated with the Jacobi–Cherednik operator is defined by

E^{\alpha,\beta}_{t}(x)={\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}^{-1}(e^{-t\lambda^{2}})(x)\quad\text{for all }x\in\mathbb{R}.

(7)

For all
t>0
,
E^{\alpha,\beta}_{t}
is an
C^{\infty}
-function on
\mathbb{R}
. Moreover, for all
t>0
and all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}(E^{\alpha,\beta}_{t})(\lambda)=e^{-t\lambda^{2}}.

(8)

Let
r>1
and
B_{r}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}:~|x|\leq r\}
. In the following proposition, we obtain estimates for the measure of
B_{r}
with respect to
A_{\alpha,\beta}
and
\sigma_{\alpha,\beta}.

Proposition 2.2
.

(1)

A_{\alpha,\beta}\left(B_{r}\right)\leq 2re^{2\rho r},
where
\rho>0
.

(2)

\sigma_{\alpha,\beta}\left(B_{r}\right)\leq C~r^{2\alpha+3}
, where
C
is a positive constant.

Proof.

(1) Since
A_{\alpha,\beta}(x)=(\sinh|x|)^{2\alpha+1}(\cosh|x|)^{2\beta+1}\leq e^{2(\alpha+\beta+1)|x|}=e^{2\rho|x|}
, where
\rho=\alpha+\beta+1
, we have

\displaystyle A_{\alpha,\beta}\left(B_{r}\right)=\int_{|x|\leq r}A_{\alpha,\beta}(x)~dx\leq\int_{|x|\leq r}e^{2\rho|x|}~dx\leq 2re^{2\rho r}.

(2) Using the estimates in (
3
), we have

\displaystyle\sigma_{\alpha,\beta}\left(B_{r}\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{|\lambda|\leq r}d|\sigma_{\alpha,\beta}|(\lambda)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{k_{2}}{4\pi}\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{\lambda^{2}+\rho^{2}}\;{\lambda}^{2\alpha+1}\;d\lambda+\frac{k_{2}}{4\pi}\int_{1}^{r}\sqrt{1+\frac{\rho^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}}\;{\lambda}^{2\alpha+2}\;d\lambda

\displaystyle\leq\frac{k_{2}}{4\pi}\sqrt{1+\rho^{2}}+\frac{k_{2}}{4\pi}\sqrt{1+\rho^{2}}\int_{0}^{r}\lambda^{2\alpha+2}\;d\lambda

\displaystyle\leq\frac{k_{2}}{4\pi}\sqrt{1+\rho^{2}}\left(1+\frac{1}{2\alpha+3}\right)\;{r}^{2\alpha+3}=C{r}^{2\alpha+3},

where
C=\frac{k_{2}}{4\pi}\sqrt{1+\rho^{2}}(1+\frac{1}{2\alpha+3}).

∎

Let
\gamma_{t}(\lambda)={\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}(E^{\alpha,\beta}_{t})(\lambda)=e^{-t|\lambda|^{2}}
for all
t>0
. Then, we obtain the following estimate for
\gamma_{t}.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
q\geq 1.
Then
\|\gamma_{t}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}\leq\left(C+D~t^{-(\alpha+\frac{3}{2})}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}
, where
C
and
D
are positive constants.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\|\gamma_{t}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}^{q}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-qt|\lambda|^{2}}d|\sigma_{\alpha,\beta}|(\lambda)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{k_{2}}{4\pi}\int_{0}^{1}e^{-qt\lambda^{2}}\sqrt{\lambda^{2}+\rho^{2}}\;{\lambda}^{2\alpha+1}\;d\lambda+\frac{k_{2}}{4\pi}\int_{1}^{\infty}e^{-qt\lambda^{2}}\sqrt{1+\frac{\rho^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}}\;{\lambda}^{2\alpha+2}\;d\lambda

\displaystyle\leq\frac{k_{2}}{4\pi}\sqrt{1+\rho^{2}}+\frac{k_{2}}{4\pi}\sqrt{1+\rho^{2}}\int_{1}^{\infty}e^{-qt\lambda^{2}}\;{\lambda}^{2\alpha+2}\;d\lambda

\displaystyle\leq\frac{k_{2}}{4\pi}\sqrt{1+\rho^{2}}\left(1+\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-qt\lambda^{2}}\;{\lambda}^{2\alpha+2}\;d\lambda\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{k_{2}}{4\pi}\sqrt{1+\rho^{2}}\left(1+\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+\frac{3}{2})}{2q^{\alpha+\frac{3}{2}}}t^{-(\alpha+\frac{3}{2})}\right)

\displaystyle=C+D~t^{-(\alpha+\frac{3}{2})},

where
C=\frac{k_{2}}{4\pi}\sqrt{1+\rho^{2}}
and
D=\frac{k_{2}}{4\pi}\sqrt{1+\rho^{2}}\;\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+\frac{3}{2})}{2q^{\alpha+\frac{3}{2}}}
.
∎

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
q\geq 2
and
0<a<\frac{1}{q}
. Then
\left\||\cdot|^{-a}\chi_{B_{r}}\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}\leq C~r^{\frac{1}{q}}e^{{\frac{2\rho r}{q}}},
where
C
is a positive constant.

Proof.

Using the relation (1) of Proposition
2.2
, we get

\displaystyle\left\||\cdot|^{-a}\chi_{B_{r}}\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}^{q}

\displaystyle=\int_{|x|\leq r}|x|^{-aq}\;A_{\alpha,\beta}(x)~dx

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{1}x^{-aq}\;A_{\alpha,\beta}(x)~dx+2\int_{1}^{r}x^{-aq}\;A_{\alpha,\beta}(x)~dx

\displaystyle\leq 2\;A_{\alpha,\beta}(1)~\int_{0}^{1}x^{-aq}~dx+2\int_{1}^{r}A_{\alpha,\beta}(x)~dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\;A_{\alpha,\beta}(1)}{1-aq}+2\int_{0}^{r}A_{\alpha,\beta}(x)~dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\;A_{\alpha,\beta}(1)}{1-aq}+2re^{2\rho r}\leq\left(\frac{2\;A_{\alpha,\beta}(1)}{1-aq}+2\right)re^{2\rho r}.

Therefore,
\left\||\cdot|^{-a}\chi_{B_{r}}\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}\leq C~r^{\frac{1}{q}}e^{{\frac{2\rho r}{q}}},

where
C=\left(\frac{2\;A_{\alpha,\beta}(1)}{1-aq}+2\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

∎

2.2.
Weak type
L^{p}
-spaces

In this subsection, we recall some basic definitions and properties of the weak type
L^{p}
-space on
\sigma
-finite measure spaces. The main references for this subsection are
[
9
,
15
,
34
]
.

Let
(X,\mu)
be a
\sigma
-finite measure space,
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
and let
f
be a measurable function on
X
. We define the norm

\|f\|_{L^{p,q}(X,\mu)}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\left(\frac{q}{p}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{q/p-1}f^{*}(t)^{q}dt\right)^{1/q}&\text{if}~q<\infty,\\
\sup_{t>0}t(\lambda_{f}(t))^{1/p}&\text{ if }q=\infty,\end{array}\right.

where
\lambda_{f}
is the distribution function of
f
and
f^{*}
denotes the non-increasing rearrangement of
f
, i.e.,

\lambda_{f}(s)=\mu(\{x\in X:|f(x)|>s\})\quad\text{ and }\quad f^{*}(t)=\inf\left\{s:\lambda_{f}(s)\leq t\right\}.

The Lorentz space
L^{p,q}(X,\mu)
consists of measurable functions
f
on
X
for which
\|f\|_{L^{p,q}(X,\mu)}<\infty.
When
p=q
, then
L^{p,p}(X,\mu)=L^{p}(X,\mu)
.

Let
(X,\mu)
and
(Y,\nu)
be
\sigma
-finite measure spaces,
1\leq p,q<\infty
, and let
T
be a linear operator
from
L^{p}(X,\mu)
to
L^{q}(Y,\nu)
. Then
T
is said to be a weak type
(p,q)
or
(p,q)
-weak operator, if there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\nu(\{y\in Y:|Tf(y)|>t\})\leq\left(C\frac{\|f\|_{L^{p}(X,\mu)}}{t}\right)^{q},

(9)

for all
t>0
and all
f\in L^{p}(X,\mu)
, i.e.,
T
maps
L^{p}(X,\mu)
boundedly into
L^{q,\infty}(Y,\nu)
. Moreover, we say that
T
is of strong type
(p,q)
if it is bounded from
L^{p}(X,\mu)
to
L^{q}(Y,\nu).

The inclusion relation between Lorentz spaces is given in the following proposition.

Proposition 2.5
.

[
9
]

(1)

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
1\leq q<r\leq\infty
. Then
L^{p,q}(X,\mu)\subset L^{p,r}(X,\mu)
and consequently, there exists a constant
C_{p,q,r}>0
such that

\|f\|_{L^{p,r}(X,\mu)}\leq C_{p,q,r}\|f\|_{L^{p,q}(X,\mu)}.

(2)

For any
1\leq p<\infty
and any
f\in L^{p}(X,\mu)
, we have
\|f\|_{L^{p,\infty}(X,\mu)}\leq\|f\|_{L^{p}(X,\mu)}
.

The following result on the pointwise product of two functions in Lorentz spaces is due to O’Neil
[
22
]
.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
q\in(2,\infty)
and set
r=\frac{q}{q-2}
. If
g\in L^{q}(X,\mu)
and
h\in L^{r,\infty}(X,\mu)
, then
gh\in L^{q^{\prime},q}(X,\mu)
with

\|gh\|_{L^{q^{\prime},q}(X,\mu)}\leq\|g\|_{L^{q}(X,\mu)}\|h\|_{L^{r,\infty}(X,\mu)}.

The interpolation result between Lorentz spaces is given in the following theorem and can be found in
[
34
]
.

Theorem 2.7
.

Suppose
T
is a subadditive operator of (restricted) weak types
\left(r_{j},p_{j}\right),j=0,1
, with
r_{0}<r_{1}
and
p_{0}\neq p_{1}
, then there exists a constant
B=B_{\theta}
such that
\|Tf\|_{L^{p,q}(X,\mu)}\leq B\|f\|_{L^{r,q}(X,\mu)}
for all
f
belonging to the domain of
T
and to
L^{r,q}(X,\mu)
, where
1\leq q\leq\infty
,

\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1-\theta}{p_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{p_{1}},\quad\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1-\theta}{r_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{r_{1}}\quad\text{ and }\quad 0<\theta<1.

3.
Weighted Norm Inequalities for the Opdam–Cherednik transform

In this section, we prove several weighted norm inequalities for the Opdam–Cherednik transform. First, we establish a version of the Hardy–Littlewood inequality for the Opdam–Cherednik transform. To obtain the result, we specialize the definition of Orlicz-type space, given in
[
15
]
, to the case of the measure
A_{\alpha,\beta}
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
A_{\alpha,\beta}
be a positive measure on
\mathbb{R}
given in (
2
). A Young function is a measurable function
\phi:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
with
A_{\alpha,\beta}\left(\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}:|\phi(x)|\leq t\right\}\right)\leq Ct

for all
t>0
and for some constant
C>0
. Given such a function
\phi
, let
L_{\phi}^{(p)}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta}),2<p<\infty
, denote the Orlicz-type space of measurable functions
f
on
\mathbb{R}
for which

\|f\|_{L_{\phi}^{(p)}\left(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta}\right)}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(x)|^{p}|\phi(x)|^{p-2}\;A_{\alpha,\beta}(x)dx\right)^{1/p}<\infty,

i.e.,
f\in L_{\phi}^{(p)}\left(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta}\right)
if and only if
f\phi^{1-\frac{2}{p}}\in L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta}\right)
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
q>2
and
f\in L_{\phi}^{(q)}\left(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta}\right)
, where
\phi
is a Young function relative to
A_{\alpha,\beta}.
There exists a constant
C_{q}>0
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f(\lambda)\right|^{q}d|\sigma_{\alpha,\beta}|(\lambda)\leq C_{q}^{q}\|f\|_{L_{\phi}^{(q)}\left(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta}\right)}^{q}.

Proof.

Assume that
f
is a simple function on
\mathbb{R}.
Let
Tf(\lambda)={\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f(\lambda),\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
. Then, using the relation (
6
), we get

\|Tf\|_{L^{\infty,\infty}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}=\|Tf\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}\leq\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}=\|f\|_{L^{1,1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}.

Also, using the Plancherel formula (
4
) and Proposition
2.5
, we get

\|Tf\|_{L^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}\leq\|Tf\|_{L^{2,2}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}=\|Tf\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}=\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}\leq\|f\|_{L^{2,1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}.

Therefore, from Theorem
2.7
, we obtain
\|Tf\|_{L^{q,q}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}\leq\|f\|_{L^{q^{\prime},q}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}
.
Now, we consider a function
g(x)=f(x)\phi(x)^{1-\frac{2}{q}}.
Then by hypothesis,
g\in L^{q}\left(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta}\right)
.
Since
\phi
is a Young function, we have

A_{\alpha,\beta}\left(\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}:|\phi(x)|^{\frac{2}{q}-1}>t\right\}\right)=A_{\alpha,\beta}\left(\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}:|\phi(x)|^{1-\frac{2}{q}}<\frac{1}{t}\right\}\right)\leq Ct^{-\frac{q}{q-2}},

and consequently
\phi^{\frac{2}{q}-1}\in L^{r,\infty}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
, where
r=\frac{q}{q-2}.
Now, using O’Neil’s theorem
2.6
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}(f)(\lambda)\right|^{q}d\sigma_{\alpha,\beta}(\lambda)\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{L^{q^{\prime},q}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}\leq\|g\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}\big\|\phi^{\frac{2}{q}-1}\big\|_{L^{r,\infty}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{q}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(x)|^{q}|\phi(x)|^{q-2}\;A_{\alpha,\beta}(x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}=C_{q}\|f\|_{L_{\phi}^{(q)}\left(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta}\right)}.

Thus, the desired conclusion holds for simple functions. Now, using a standard density argument we extend the result for general functions in
L_{\phi}^{(q)}\left(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta}\right)
. This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Next, we obtain a version of the HPW inequality for the Opdam–Cherednik transform. To prove the result, we use the fact that the differential-difference operator
\Delta_{\alpha,\beta}-|x|^{2}
is an essentially self-adjoint operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
. The operator
\Delta_{\alpha,\beta}-|x|^{2}
has a discrete spectrum. Also, for every
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and every
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta}),

we have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}(\Delta_{\alpha,\beta}f)(\lambda)=-|\lambda|^{2}{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f(\lambda).

(10)

For a more detailed study on the operator
\Delta_{\alpha,\beta}
, we refer to
[
31
,
19
]
. We begin with the following additive inequality.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
, then

\||\cdot|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}^{2}+\||\cdot|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}^{2}\geq\lambda_{\min}{(|x|^{2}-\Delta_{\alpha,\beta})}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}^{2},

where
\lambda_{\min}{(|x|^{2}-\Delta_{\alpha,\beta})}
is the minimum eigenvalue of the operator
|x|^{2}-\Delta_{\alpha,\beta}
.

Proof.

Using the Plancherel formula (
4
) and the relation (
10
), we get

\displaystyle\||\cdot|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\langle|\cdot|^{2}{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f,{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}

\displaystyle=-\langle{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}(\Delta_{\alpha,\beta}f),{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}

\displaystyle=-\langle\Delta_{\alpha,\beta}f,f\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}.

Therefore

\displaystyle\||\cdot|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}^{2}+\||\cdot|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\langle(|\cdot|^{2}-\Delta_{\alpha,\beta})f,f\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}.

Since the self-adjoint operator
|x|^{2}-\Delta_{\alpha,\beta}
has only discrete spectra, we obtain

\displaystyle\||\cdot|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}^{2}+\||\cdot|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}^{2}\geq\lambda_{\min}{(|x|^{2}-\Delta_{\alpha,\beta})}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}^{2},

where
\lambda_{\min}{(|x|^{2}-\Delta_{\alpha,\beta})}
is the minimum eigenvalue of the operator
|x|^{2}-\Delta_{\alpha,\beta}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
. Then there exists a constant
C(\alpha,\beta)>0
such that

\||\cdot|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}\;\||\cdot|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}\geq C(\alpha,\beta)\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}^{2}.

Proof.

For
c>1
, we define
f_{c}(x):=f(cx).
Now, replacing
f_{c}
for
f
in Lemma
3.3
, we get

\displaystyle\||\cdot|f_{c}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}^{2}+\||\cdot|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f_{c}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}^{2}\geq\lambda_{\min}{(|x|^{2}-\Delta_{\alpha,\beta})}\|f_{c}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}^{2}.

(11)

Using the Plancherel formula (
4
) and the
relation (
3
), we obtain

\displaystyle\||\cdot|f_{c}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}(|\cdot|f_{c})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}(|\lambda|f(c\lambda))\right|^{2}d|\sigma_{\alpha,\beta}|(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}\left(\frac{|\lambda|}{c}f(\lambda)\right)\right|^{2}\;\left|1-\frac{c\rho}{i\lambda}\right|\frac{d\lambda}{8\pi|C_{\alpha,\beta}(\frac{\lambda}{c})|^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{k_{2}}{8\pi c^{2\alpha+4}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}(|\lambda|f(\lambda))\right|^{2}\;\sqrt{\frac{1}{c^{2}}+\frac{\rho^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}}\;|{\lambda}|^{2\alpha+2}\;d\lambda

\displaystyle\leq\frac{k_{2}}{8\pi c^{2\alpha+4}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}(|\lambda|f(\lambda))\right|^{2}\;\sqrt{1+\frac{\rho^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}}|{\lambda}|^{2\alpha+2}\;d\lambda

\displaystyle\leq\frac{k_{2}}{k_{1}c^{2\alpha+4}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}(|\lambda|f(\lambda))\right|^{2}\;\left|1-\frac{\rho}{i\lambda}\right|\frac{d\lambda}{8\pi|C_{\alpha,\beta}(\lambda)|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{k_{2}}{k_{1}c^{2\alpha+4}}\|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}(|\cdot|f)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}^{2}=\frac{k_{2}}{k_{1}c^{2\alpha+4}}\||\cdot|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}^{2}.

Moreover, using the relation (
3
), we get

\displaystyle\||\cdot|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f_{c}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\lambda|^{2}\left|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f(c\lambda)\right|^{2}d|\sigma_{\alpha,\beta}|(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\lambda|^{2}\left|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f(\lambda)\right|^{2}\;\left|1-\frac{c\rho}{i\lambda}\right|\frac{d\lambda}{8\pi|C_{\alpha,\beta}(\frac{\lambda}{c})|^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{k_{2}}{8\pi c^{2\alpha+4}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\lambda|^{2}\left|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f(\lambda)\right|^{2}\;\sqrt{\frac{1}{c^{2}}+\frac{\rho^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}}\;|{\lambda}|^{2\alpha+2}\;d\lambda

\displaystyle\leq\frac{k_{2}}{8\pi c^{2\alpha+4}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\lambda|^{2}\left|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f(\lambda)\right|^{2}\;\sqrt{1+\frac{\rho^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}}\;|{\lambda}|^{2\alpha+2}\;d\lambda

\displaystyle\leq\frac{k_{2}}{k_{1}c^{2\alpha+4}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\lambda|^{2}\left|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f(\lambda)\right|^{2}\;\left|1-\frac{\rho}{i\lambda}\right|\frac{d\lambda}{8\pi|C_{\alpha,\beta}(\lambda)|^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{k_{2}}{k_{1}c^{2\alpha+4}}\||\cdot|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}^{2}.

Therefore

\displaystyle\||\cdot|f_{c}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}^{2}+\||\cdot|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f_{c}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{k_{2}}{k_{1}c^{2\alpha+3}}[c^{-1}\||\cdot|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}^{2}+c\||\cdot|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}^{2}].

(12)

Further, using the Plancherel formula (
4
) and the relation (
3
), we get

\displaystyle\|f_{c}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f_{c}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f(c\lambda)\right|^{2}d|\sigma_{\alpha,\beta}|(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f(\lambda)\right|^{2}\;\left|1-\frac{c\rho}{i\lambda}\right|\frac{d\lambda}{8\pi|C_{\alpha,\beta}(\frac{\lambda}{c})|^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{k_{1}}{8\pi c^{2\alpha+3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f(\lambda)\right|^{2}\;\sqrt{1+\frac{c^{2}\rho^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}}\;|{\lambda}|^{2\alpha+2}\;d\lambda

\displaystyle\geq\frac{k_{1}}{8\pi c^{2\alpha+3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f(\lambda)\right|^{2}\;\sqrt{1+\frac{\rho^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}}|{\lambda}|^{2\alpha+2}\;d\lambda

\displaystyle\geq\frac{k_{1}}{k_{2}c^{2\alpha+3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f(\lambda)\right|^{2}\;\left|1-\frac{\rho}{i\lambda}\right|\frac{d\lambda}{8\pi|C_{\alpha,\beta}(\lambda)|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{k_{1}}{k_{2}c^{2\alpha+3}}\|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}^{2}=\frac{k_{1}}{k_{2}c^{2\alpha+3}}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}^{2}.

(13)

Therefore, from (
11
), (
3
) and (
3
), we obtain

\displaystyle[c^{-1}\||\cdot|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}^{2}+c\||\cdot|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}^{2}]\geq\frac{k_{1}^{2}}{k_{2}^{2}}\lambda_{\min}{(|x|^{2}-\Delta_{\alpha,\beta})}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}^{2}.

(14)

Since for any
f\neq 0
,
\frac{\||\cdot|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}}{\||\cdot|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}}>0,
by the Archimedean property of real numbers, there exists a natural number
N
such that
N\frac{\||\cdot|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}}{\||\cdot|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}}>1
. Choosing
c=N\frac{\||\cdot|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}}{\||\cdot|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}}
, from (
14
), we get

\||\cdot|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}\;\||\cdot|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}\geq C(\alpha,\beta)\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}^{2},

where
C(\alpha,\beta)=\frac{k_{1}^{2}N}{k_{2}^{2}(N^{2}+1)}\lambda_{\min}{(|x|^{2}-\Delta_{\alpha,\beta})}
.
∎

In the following, we obtain an extension of the HPW inequality for the Opdam–Cherednik transform using weights with different exponents.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
and
a,b\geq 1
. Then there exists a constant
C(\alpha,\beta)>0
such that

\||\cdot|^{a}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}^{\frac{b}{a+b}}\;\||\cdot|^{b}{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}^{\frac{a}{a+b}}\geq C(\alpha,\beta)^{\frac{ab}{a+b}}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}.

Proof.

Let
a>1
and
a^{\prime}
such that
\frac{1}{a}+\frac{1}{a^{\prime}}=1
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\||\cdot|^{a}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}^{\frac{1}{a}}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}^{\frac{1}{a^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|x|^{2a}|f(x)|^{2}A_{\alpha,\beta}(x)\;dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2a}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(x)|^{2}A_{\alpha,\beta}(x)\;dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2a^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=\||\cdot|^{2}|f|^{\frac{2}{a}}\|_{L^{a}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}^{\frac{1}{2}}\||f|^{\frac{2}{a^{\prime}}}\|_{L^{a^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Further, using Hölder’s inequality, we get

\displaystyle\||\cdot|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(|x|^{2}|f(x)|^{\frac{2}{a}}\right)^{a}A_{\alpha,\beta}(x)\;dx\right)^{\frac{1}{a}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(|f(x)|^{\frac{2}{a^{\prime}}}\right)^{a^{\prime}}A_{\alpha,\beta}(x)\;dx\right)^{\frac{1}{a^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|x|^{2a}|f(x)|^{2}A_{\alpha,\beta}(x)\;dx\right)^{\frac{1}{a}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(x)|^{2}A_{\alpha,\beta}(x)\;dx\right)^{\frac{1}{a^{\prime}}},

and consequently

\displaystyle\||\cdot|^{a}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}^{\frac{1}{a}}\geq\frac{\||\cdot|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}}{\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}^{1-\frac{1}{a}}}.

(15)

Also, by applying the same argument on
{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f
, we obtain

\displaystyle\||\cdot|^{b}{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}^{\frac{1}{b}}\geq\frac{\||\cdot|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}}{\|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}^{1-\frac{1}{b}}}.

(16)

From (
15
), (
16
) and Theorem
3.4
, we get

\displaystyle\||\cdot|^{a}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}^{\frac{b}{a+b}}\;\||\cdot|^{b}{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}^{\frac{a}{a+b}}

\displaystyle=\left[\||\cdot|^{a}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}^{\frac{1}{a}}\;\||\cdot|^{b}{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}^{\frac{1}{b}}\right]^{\frac{ab}{a+b}}

\displaystyle\geq\left[\frac{\||\cdot|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}\;\||\cdot|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}}{\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}^{1-\frac{1}{a}}\;\|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}^{1-\frac{1}{b}}}\right]^{\frac{ab}{a+b}}

\displaystyle\geq C(\alpha,\beta)^{\frac{ab}{a+b}}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}^{\left(2-2+\frac{1}{a}+\frac{1}{b}\right)\frac{ab}{a+b}}

\displaystyle=C(\alpha,\beta)^{\frac{ab}{a+b}}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})},

where the constant
C(\alpha,\beta)
as in Theorem
3.4
. This completes the proof.
∎

Next, we give another variation on the HPW inequality for the Opdam–Cherednik transform, which incorporates
L^{p}
-norms. To prove the result, we use the heat kernel decay estimates. Recall that
\gamma_{t}(\lambda)={\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}(E^{\alpha,\beta}_{t})(\lambda)=e^{-t|\lambda|^{2}}
for all
t>0
, where
E^{\alpha,\beta}_{t}
is the heat kernel given in (
7
). In the following lemma, we obtain the decay estimate for the Opdam–Cherednik transform.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
p\in(1,2]
and
0<a<\frac{1}{q}
, where
q
is the conjugate exponent of
p
. Then for every
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
and any
t>1
, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\left\|\gamma_{t}{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}\leq Ct^{\frac{1}{2q}+1}\;e^{\frac{2\rho t^{\frac{1}{2}}}{q}}t^{-\frac{a}{2}}\left\||\cdot|^{a}f\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume that
\||\cdot|^{a}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},A{\alpha,\beta})}<\infty
. Let
r>1
and
B_{r}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}:~|x|\leq r\}
. Then using the relation (
5
), we get

\displaystyle\left\|\gamma_{t}{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}(f\chi_{B_{r}^{c}})\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\gamma_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}\left\|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}(f\chi_{B_{r}^{c}})\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\left\|f\chi_{B_{r}^{c}}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},A{\alpha,\beta})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}r^{-a}\left\||\cdot|^{a}f\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}.

(17)

Also, using Hölder’s inequality and the relation (
6
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\gamma_{t}{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}(f\chi_{B_{r}})\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\gamma_{t}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}\left\|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}(f\chi_{B_{r}})\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\gamma_{t}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}\left\|f\chi_{B_{r}}\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\gamma_{t}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}\left\||\cdot|^{-a}\chi_{B_{r}}\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}\left\||\cdot|^{a}f\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}.

Using estimates of
\|\gamma_{t}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}
and
\left\||\cdot|^{-a}\chi_{B_{r}}\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}
from Propositions
2.3
and
2.4
, respectively, we get

\displaystyle\left\|\gamma_{t}{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}(f\chi_{B_{r}})\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}

\displaystyle\leq C\big(1+t^{-(\alpha+\frac{3}{2})}\big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(re^{2\rho r}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left\||\cdot|^{a}f\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}.

(18)

Thus, from (
3
) and (
18
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\gamma_{t}{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\gamma_{t}{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}(f\chi_{B_{r}^{c}})\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}+\left\|\gamma_{t}{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}(f\chi_{B_{r}})\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}

\displaystyle\leq\left[C_{p}+Cr^{a}\big(1+t^{-(\alpha+\frac{3}{2})}\big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(re^{2\rho r}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\right]r^{-a}\left\||\cdot|^{a}f\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}.

Choosing
r=t^{\frac{1}{2}}
, we get

\displaystyle\left\|\gamma_{t}{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}

\displaystyle=\left[C_{p}+Ct^{\frac{a}{2}}\big(1+t^{-(\alpha+\frac{3}{2})}\big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\big(t^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{2\rho t^{\frac{1}{2}}}\big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\right]t^{-\frac{a}{2}}\left\||\cdot|^{a}f\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}

\displaystyle\leq\left[C_{p}+Ct^{\frac{1}{2q}+1}e^{\frac{2\rho t^{\frac{1}{2}}}{q}}\right]t^{-\frac{a}{2}}\left\||\cdot|^{a}f\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}

\displaystyle\leq C\;t^{\frac{1}{2q}+1}\;e^{\frac{2\rho t^{\frac{1}{2}}}{q}}t^{-\frac{a}{2}}\left\||\cdot|^{a}f\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}.

∎

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
a,b>0
. Then under the same assumptions as in Lemma
3.6
, there exists a constant
C(a,b)>0
such that for all
b\leq 2
, we have

\left\|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}\leq C(a,b)\;t_{0}^{\frac{1}{2q}+1}\;e^{\frac{2\rho t_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{q}}\||\cdot|^{a}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}^{\frac{b}{a+b}}\;\||\cdot|^{b}{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}^{\frac{a}{a+b}},

and for all
b>2
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}

\displaystyle\leq C(a,1)^{\frac{b(a+1)}{a+b}}\left[b(b-1)^{\frac{1}{b}-1}\right]^{\frac{ab}{a+b}}\bigg(t_{1}^{\frac{1}{2q}+1}e^{\frac{2\rho t_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{q}}\bigg)^{\frac{b(a+1)}{a+b}}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\||\cdot|^{a}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}^{\frac{b}{a+b}}\;\||\cdot|^{b}{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}^{\frac{a}{a+b}},

where
t_{0}=t_{0}(a,b)=\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^{\frac{2}{a+b}}\big(N\frac{\left\||\cdot|^{a}f\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},A{\alpha,\beta})}}{\||\cdot|^{b}{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}}\big)^{\frac{2}{a+b}},N\in\mathbb{N}
and
t_{1}=t_{0}(a,1)
.

Proof.

For a fixed
p\in(1,2]
, we assume that
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
satisfy
\left\||\cdot|^{a}f\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},A{\alpha,\beta})}+\||\cdot|^{b}{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}<\infty.
For all
t>1
, by Lemma
3.6
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\gamma_{t}{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}+\left\|(1-\gamma_{t}){\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}

\displaystyle\leq Ct^{\frac{1}{2q}+1}\;e^{\frac{2\rho t^{\frac{1}{2}}}{q}}t^{-\frac{a}{2}}\left\||\cdot|^{a}f\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}+\left\|(1-\gamma_{t}){\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}.

(19)

Moreover,

\displaystyle\left\|(1-\gamma_{t}){\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}

\displaystyle=t^{\frac{b}{2}}\big\|\left(t|\cdot|^{2}\right)^{-\frac{b}{2}}(1-\gamma_{t})|\cdot|^{b}{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\big\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}

\displaystyle\leq t^{\frac{b}{2}}\big\|\left(t|\cdot|^{2}\right)^{-\frac{b}{2}}(1-\gamma_{t})\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}\||\cdot|^{b}{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\;t^{\frac{b}{2}}\;\||\cdot|^{b}{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})},

(20)

whenever
b\leq 2.
Thus, from (
3
) and (
3
), we have

\displaystyle\left\|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}

\displaystyle\leq C\;t^{\frac{1}{2q}+1}\;e^{\frac{2\rho t^{\frac{1}{2}}}{q}}t^{-\frac{a}{2}}\left\||\cdot|^{a}f\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}+C_{1}\;t^{\frac{b}{2}}\||\cdot|^{b}{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}\;t^{\frac{1}{2q}+1}\;e^{\frac{2\rho t^{\frac{1}{2}}}{q}}\left[t^{-\frac{a}{2}}\||\cdot|^{a}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},A{\alpha,\beta})}+t^{\frac{b}{2}}\||\cdot|^{b}{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}\right].

(21)

Since for any
f\neq 0
,
\frac{a\left\||\cdot|^{a}f\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},A{\alpha,\beta})}}{b\||\cdot|^{b}{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}}>0
, there exists a natural number
N
such that
N\frac{a\left\||\cdot|^{a}f\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},A{\alpha,\beta})}}{b\||\cdot|^{b}{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}}>1
.
We consider the function
g
defined on
[1,\infty)
by

g(t)=t^{-\frac{a}{2}}\||\cdot|^{a}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}+t^{\frac{b}{2}}\||\cdot|^{b}{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}.

Then the minimum of the function
g
is attained at the point

\displaystyle t_{0}=\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^{\frac{2}{a+b}}\bigg(N\frac{\left\||\cdot|^{a}f\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},A{\alpha,\beta})}}{\||\cdot|^{b}{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}}\bigg)^{\frac{2}{a+b}}

(22)

and

g(t_{0})=\left[\left(\frac{b}{aN}\right)^{\frac{a}{a+b}}+\left(\frac{aN}{b}\right)^{\frac{b}{a+b}}\right]\||\cdot|^{a}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}^{\frac{b}{a+b}}\;\||\cdot|^{b}{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}^{\frac{a}{a+b}}.

Thus, from (
3
), we get

\displaystyle\left\|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}

\displaystyle\leq C(a,b)\;t_{0}^{\frac{1}{2q}+1}\;e^{\frac{2\rho t_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{q}}\||\cdot|^{a}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}^{\frac{b}{a+b}}\;\||\cdot|^{b}{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}^{\frac{a}{a+b}},

(23)

where
C(a,b)=C_{2}\left[\left(\frac{b}{aN}\right)^{\frac{a}{a+b}}+\left(\frac{aN}{b}\right)^{\frac{b}{a+b}}\right]
.

Now, we consider the case for
b>2
. Since
u\leq 1+u^{b}
for all
u>0
, in particular for
u
of the form
u=\frac{|\lambda|}{\varepsilon}
for all
\varepsilon>0
, the inequality becomes
\frac{|\lambda|}{\varepsilon}\leq 1+\left(\frac{|\lambda|}{\varepsilon}\right)^{b}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\||\cdot|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}\leq\varepsilon\|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}+\varepsilon^{1-b}\||\cdot|^{b}{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}.

(24)

Let

\displaystyle g(\varepsilon)=\varepsilon\|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}+\varepsilon^{1-b}\||\cdot|^{b}{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}.

(25)

Then, the minimum of the function
g
is attain at the point

\varepsilon_{0}=(b-1)^{\frac{1}{b}}\left(\frac{\||\cdot|^{b}{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}}{\|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}}\right)^{\frac{1}{b}}.

Optimizing in
\varepsilon_{0}
, from (
24
) and (
25
), we get

\displaystyle\||\cdot|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}\leq b(b-1)^{\frac{1}{b}-1}\|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}^{1-\frac{1}{b}}~\||\cdot|^{b}{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}^{\frac{1}{b}}.

(26)

Again, from the relation (
23
) with
b=1
, we get

\displaystyle\left\|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}\leq C(a,1)\;t_{1}^{\frac{1}{2q}+1}\;e^{\frac{2\rho t_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{q}}\||\cdot|^{a}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}^{\frac{1}{a+1}}\;\||\cdot|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}^{\frac{a}{a+1}},

(27)

where
t_{1}=t_{0}(a,1).
Therefore, using the relation (
27
), from (
26
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}

\displaystyle\leq C(a,1)\left[b(b-1)^{\frac{1}{b}-1}\right]^{\frac{a}{a+1}}\;t_{1}^{\frac{1}{2q}+1}\;e^{\frac{2\rho t_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{q}}

\displaystyle\times\||\cdot|^{a}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}^{\frac{1}{a+1}}\|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}^{\frac{a(b-1)}{b(a+1)}}~\||\cdot|^{b}{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}^{\frac{a}{b(a+1)}}.

Thus

\displaystyle\left\|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}^{\frac{a+b}{b(a+1)}}

\displaystyle\leq C(a,1)\left[b(b-1)^{\frac{1}{b}-1}\right]^{\frac{a}{a+1}}\;t_{1}^{\frac{1}{2q}+1}e^{\frac{2\rho t_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{q}}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\||\cdot|^{a}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}^{\frac{1}{a+1}}\||\cdot|^{b}{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}^{\frac{a}{b(a+1)}},

and consequently

\displaystyle\left\|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}

\displaystyle\leq C(a,1)^{\frac{b(a+1)}{a+b}}\left[b(b-1)^{\frac{1}{b}-1}\right]^{\frac{ab}{a+b}}\bigg(t_{1}^{\frac{1}{2q}+1}e^{\frac{2\rho t_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{q}}\bigg)^{\frac{b(a+1)}{a+b}}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\||\cdot|^{a}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}^{\frac{b}{a+b}}\;\||\cdot|^{b}{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}^{\frac{a}{a+b}}.

This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Next, we study other variations of the HPW inequality. In particular, we give the Nash-type and Clarkson-type inequalities for the Opdam–Cherednik transform. These inequalities involve a mixed
L^{1}
and
L^{2}
norms estimate.

Theorem 3.8

(Nash-type inequality)
.

Let
s>0
. Then for every
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\left\|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}^{2}\leq C\left\|f\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}^{\frac{4s}{2\alpha+3+2s}}\left\||\cdot|^{s}{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}^{\frac{2(2\alpha+3)}{2\alpha+3+2s}}.

Proof.

Let
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
,
r>1
and
B_{r}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}:~|x|\leq r\}
. Then, using the Plancherel formula (
4
), we have

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}^{2}=\left\|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}^{2}=\left\|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}(f\chi_{B_{r}})\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}^{2}+\left\|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}(f\chi_{B_{r}^{c}})\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}^{2}.

(28)

Now, from the relation (2) of Proposition
2.2
, we get

\displaystyle\left\|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}(f\chi_{B_{r}})\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}^{2}=\int_{B_{r}}\left|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f(\lambda)\right|^{2}d|\sigma_{\alpha,\beta}|(\lambda)\leq C_{1}~\left\|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}^{2}r^{2\alpha+3}

and

\displaystyle\left\|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}(f\chi_{B_{r}^{c}})\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{r}^{c}}\left|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f(\lambda)\right|^{2}d|\sigma_{\alpha,\beta}|(\lambda)

\displaystyle\leq r^{-2s}\int_{B_{r}^{c}}|\lambda|^{2s}\left|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f(\lambda)\right|^{2}d|\sigma_{\alpha,\beta}|(\lambda)

\displaystyle\leq r^{-2s}\left\||\cdot|^{s}{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}^{2}.

Hence, using the relation (
6
), from (
28
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}~r^{2\alpha+3}\left\|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}^{2}+r^{-2s}\left\||\cdot|^{s}{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}~r^{2\alpha+3}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}^{2}+r^{-2s}\left\||\cdot|^{s}{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}^{2}.

(29)

Since for any
f\neq 0
,
\frac{\left\||\cdot|^{s}{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}^{2}}{\left\|f\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}^{2}}>0,
by the Archimedean property of real numbers, there exists a natural number
N
such that
N\frac{\left\||\cdot|^{s}{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}^{2}}{\left\|f\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}^{2}}>1
. Then, the right hand side of (
3
) is minimized for
r^{2\alpha+3+2s}=N\frac{\left\||\cdot|^{s}{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}^{2}}{\left\|f\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}^{2}}
. Therefore

\displaystyle\left\|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}^{2}\leq C\left\|f\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}^{\frac{4s}{2\alpha+3+2s}}\left\||\cdot|^{s}{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}^{\frac{2(2\alpha+3)}{2\alpha+3+2s}},

where
C=(C_{1}N+1)N^{-\frac{2s}{2\alpha+3+2s}}.

∎

Theorem 3.9

(Clarkson-type inequality)
.

Let
s>0
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
. Then for every
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\left\|f\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}\leq C\exp\left\{{\rho\left(N\frac{\left\||\cdot|^{2s}f\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}}{\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}}\right)^{\frac{2}{1+4s}}}\right\}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}^{\frac{4s}{1+4s}}\left\||\cdot|^{2s}f\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}^{\frac{1}{1+4s}}.

Proof.

Let
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
,
r>1
and
B_{r}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}:~|x|\leq r\}
. Using Hölder’s inequality and the relation (1) of Proposition
2.2
, we get

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{r}}|f(x)|A_{\alpha,\beta}(x)\;dx+\int_{B_{r}^{c}}|f(x)|A_{\alpha,\beta}(x)\;dx

\displaystyle\leq A_{\alpha,\beta}(B_{r})^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}+r^{-2s}\int_{B_{r}^{c}}|x|^{2s}|f(x)|A_{\alpha,\beta}(x)\;dx

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\frac{1}{2}}r^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{\rho r}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}+r^{-2s}\||\cdot|^{2s}f\|_{L^{1}{(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}}

\displaystyle\leq e^{\rho r}(2^{\frac{1}{2}}r^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}+r^{-2s}\||\cdot|^{2s}f\|_{L^{1}{(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}}).

(30)

Since for any
f\neq 0
,
\frac{\left\||\cdot|^{2s}f\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}}{\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}}>0,
by the Archimedean property of real numbers, there exists a natural number
N
such that
N\frac{\left\||\cdot|^{2s}f\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}}{\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}}>1
. Then, the right hand side of (
3
) is minimized for
r^{\frac{1}{2}+2s}=N\frac{\left\||\cdot|^{2s}f\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}}{\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}}
. Therefore

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}

\displaystyle\leq C\exp\left\{{\rho\left(N\frac{\left\||\cdot|^{2s}f\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}}{\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}}\right)^{\frac{2}{1+4s}}}\right\}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}^{\frac{4s}{1+4s}}\left\||\cdot|^{2s}f\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}^{\frac{1}{1+4s}},

where
C=(2^{\frac{1}{2}}N+1)N^{-\frac{4s}{1+4s}}.

∎

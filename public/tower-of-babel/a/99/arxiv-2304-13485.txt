An upper bound for the solving degree in terms of the degree of regularity
Flavio Salizzoni

Abstract
The solving degree is an important parameter for estimating the complexity of solving a system of polynomial equations. In this paper, we provide an upper bound for the solving degree in terms of the degree of regularity. We also show that this bound is optimal. As a direct consequence, we prove an upper bound for the last fall degree and a Macaulay bound.



1
Introduction

Many problems can be modeled through a system of polynomial equations. Solutions to such a system can be found in polynomial time once we know a reduced Gröbner basis of the system. Nowadays, there are many algorithms for the computation of Gröbner bases, among which the most efficient ones belong to the family of linear-algebra based algorithms. This family includes among others F4, F5, and XL algorithms
[
6
,
7
,
8
]
. Estimating their computational complexity has become increasingly important because of their applications in post-quantum cryptography.

Given a linear-algebra based algorithm
H
for solving a system of polynomial equations
\mathcal{F}
, the solving degree for
\mathcal{F}
with respect to
H
is defined as the largest degree of a polynomial that appears while we solve
\mathcal{F}
using
H
. The interest in this parameter stems from the fact that an upper bound on it, results in an upper bound on the complexity of these algorithms. However, computing explicitly the solving degree is in general extremely difficult. For this reason, it is useful to provide upper bounds for the solving degree in terms of other parameters, such as the degree of regularity, the last fall degree, and the Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity. For instance, in
[
11
]
Huang, Kosters, Yang, and Yeo described an algorithm for solving polynomial systems whose complexity is bounded from above by the last fall degree. In
[
4
]
, Caminata and Gorla proved that the solving degree for a family
\mathcal{F}=\{f_{1},\dots,f_{k}\}
is upper bounded by the Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity of the family
\mathcal{F}^{h}=\{f_{1}^{h},\dots,f_{k}^{h}\}
, where
f_{i}^{h}
is the homogenization of
f_{i}
.

The degree of regularity
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{reg}}(\mathcal{F})
was introduced by Bardet, Faugère, and Salvy in
[
1
,
9
]
, where they also provide an upper bound for this degree for cryptographic semi-regular sequences. Even if many authors use the degree of regularity as a heuristic upper bound for the solving degree, it is not completely clear what is the exact relation between these two parameters. For instance, see
[
4
, Examples 4.7]
and
[
2
, Examples 2.2, 2.3 and 2.4]
. In
[
15
,
16
]
, Semaev and Tenti proved that under certain conditions the solving degree is bounded from above by
2\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{reg}}(\mathcal{F})-2
(see
[
16
, Corollary 3.67]
and the discussion below
[
15
, Theorem 2.1]
).

In this paper we consider the solving degree
\mathrm{sd}_{\sigma}
for a family of algorithms that contains among others MutantXL
[
3
]
and MXL2
[
14
]
. The main result is the following bound for the solving degree in terms of the degree of regularity.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}=\{f_{1},\dots,f_{n}\}
be a family of polynomials such that
\max\deg(f_{i})\leq\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{reg}}(\mathcal{F})
and let
\sigma
be a degree-compatible term order. Then,
\mathrm{sd}_{\sigma}(\mathcal{F})\leq\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{reg}}(\mathcal{F})+1
.

We prove that this bound is optimal showing that for every
1<d\in\mathbb{N}
there exists a family
\mathcal{F}
such that
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{reg}}(\mathcal{F})=d
and
\mathrm{sd}_{\sigma}(\mathcal{F})=d+1
. As a consequence of Theorem
1.1
, we obtain that the last fall degree is always bounded from above by the degree of regularity.
Finally, we improve the Macaulay bound for a family of polynomials with finite degree of regularity.

2
Preliminaries

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a field (not necessarily finite). For an ideal
\mathcal{I}
in
\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
, we denote by
\mathcal{I}_{d}
the set of all homogeneous polynomial of degree
d
in
\mathcal{I}
. Let
\mathcal{F}=\{f_{1},\dots,f_{k}\}
be a finite family of
\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
and let
(\mathcal{F})
be the ideal generated by
\mathcal{F}
.

Definition 2.1
.

Fix a term order
\sigma
. We denote by
\mathrm{Gbd}_{\sigma}(\mathcal{F})
the maximum degree of an element of a reduced Gröbner basis of
(\mathcal{F})
with respect to
\sigma
.

We now recall the definition of degree of regularity, as was originally given in
[
1
,
9
]
by Bardet, Faugère, and Salvy. For a polynomial
p\in\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
, let
p^{\mathrm{top}}
be the homogeneous part of largest degree of
p
.

Definition 2.2
.

The degree of regularity
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{reg}}(\mathcal{F})
of
\mathcal{F}
is the smallest integer
d
for which

\left(\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{top}}\right)_{d}=(\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}])_{d},

where
\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{top}}=\left\{f_{1}^{\mathrm{top}},\dots,f_{k}^{\mathrm{top}}\right\}
. If such a
d
does not exist, we set
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{reg}}(\mathcal{F})=+\infty
.

Remark 2.3
.

If the term order
\sigma
is degree-compatible, then
\mathrm{Gbd}_{\sigma}(\mathcal{F})\leq\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{reg}}(\mathcal{F})
, as it has been already observed in
[
4
, Remark 4.6]
by Caminata and Gorla.

Another important parameter for a family
\mathcal{F}
is the last fall degree, which was defined for the first time in
[
12
]
by Huang, Kosters, and Yeo.

Definition 2.4
.

For an integer
d
, we denote by
V_{\mathcal{F},d}
the smallest
\mathbb{K}
-linear space with respect to inclusion such that

•

\{f\in\mathcal{F}:\deg(f)\leq d\}\subseteq V_{\mathcal{F},d}
,

•

for every
m
monomial in
\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
and every
f\in V_{\mathcal{F},d}
such that
\deg(mf)\leq d
, then
mf\in V_{\mathcal{F},d}
.

We set
V_{\mathcal{F},+\infty}=(\mathcal{F})
. The last fall degree
\mathrm{Lfd}(\mathcal{F})
is the minimal
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\cup\{+\infty\}
such that
f\in V_{\mathcal{F},\max\{d,\deg(f)\}}
for all
f\in(\mathcal{F})
.

The solving degree is usually defined starting from the Macaulay matrix
M(\mathcal{F},d)
to which we add a certain number of rows, see for instance
[
10
, Section 2.2]
for an explicit discussion. However, for the purpose of this paper, it is more convenient for us to define the solving degree directly in terms of the spaces
V_{\mathcal{F},d}
. Notice that the following definition is equivalent to the standard one, as shown in
[
10
, Theorem 1]
.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
\sigma
be a degree-compatible term order. The solving degree
\mathrm{sd}_{\sigma}(\mathcal{F})
of
\mathcal{F}
is the smallest
d
for which
V_{\mathcal{F},d}
contains a Gröbner basis of
\mathcal{F}
with respect to
\sigma
.

In
[
5
]
, Caminata and Gorla proved the following result that highlights a relation between the solving degree and the last fall degree.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a polynomial system and let
\sigma
be a degree-compatible term order. Then

\mathrm{sd}_{\sigma}(\mathcal{F})=\max\{\mathrm{Lfd}(\mathcal{F}),\mathrm{Gbd}_{\sigma}(\mathcal{F})\}.

For a more detailed discussion about the solving degree, the degree of regularity, and the last fall degree we refer the interested reader to
[
4
,
5
]
.

3
Proof of Theorem
1.1

From now on, let
\mathcal{F}=\{f_{1},\dots,f_{k}\}
be a family of polynomials in
\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
such that
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{reg}}(\mathcal{F})=d<+\infty
and
\deg(f_{i})\leq d
for all
1\leq i\leq k
.

Lemma 3.1
.

For each monomial
m
of degree
d
there exists
p\in V_{\mathcal{F},d}
such that
p^{\mathrm{top}}=m
.

Proof.

Since
d
is the degree of regularity of
\mathcal{F}
, there exist
m_{1},\dots,m_{k}
monomials such that
\sum_{i=1}^{k}m_{i}f_{i}^{\mathrm{top}}=m
,
m_{i}\neq 0
, and
\deg(m_{i}f_{i})\leq d
for all
1\leq i\leq k
. Let
p=\sum_{i=1}^{k}m_{i}f_{i}
. It is clear that
\deg(p)=\deg(m)=d
,
p\in V_{\mathcal{F},d}
, and
p^{\mathrm{top}}=m
.
∎

Let
D=\binom{d+n-1}{d}
and let
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{F}}=\{p_{1},\dots,p_{D}\}\subseteq V_{\mathcal{F},d}
be a set such that for each monic monomial
m
of degree
d
there exists
1\leq i\leq D
such that
p_{i}^{\mathrm{top}}=m
. The existence of
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{F}}
follows from Lemma
3.1
. Notice that
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{F}}\subseteq V_{\mathcal{F},d+1}
, since
V_{\mathcal{F},d}\subseteq V_{\mathcal{F},d+1}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

For every
f\in V_{\mathcal{F},d+1}
of degree
d
, there exist
a_{1},\dots,a_{D}\in\mathbb{K}
and
\bar{f}\in V_{\mathcal{F},d+1}
with
\deg(\bar{f})<d
, such that
f=\bar{f}+\sum_{i=1}^{D}a_{i}p_{i}
, where
p_{i}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{F}}
.

Proof.

Since
\langle p_{1}^{\mathrm{top}},\dots,p_{D}^{\mathrm{top}}\rangle_{\mathbb{K}}=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{d}
, there exist
a_{1},\dots,a_{d}\in\mathbb{K}
such that
f-\sum_{i=1}^{D}a_{i}p_{i}=\bar{f}
has degree strictly smaller than
d
. As
f,p_{1},\dots,p_{D}\in V_{\mathcal{F},d+1}
, we conclude that
\bar{f}\in V_{\mathcal{F},d+1}
too.
∎

We denote by
\mathscr{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]^{3})
the set of all finite subsets of
\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]^{3}
. We now define a subset
A_{d}
of
\mathscr{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]^{3})
giving a concrete description of its elements. A set
\{(m_{1},a_{1},g_{1}),\dots,(m_{\ell},a_{\ell},g_{\ell})\}\in A_{d}
if and only if the followings are satisfied:

•

there is exactly one element such that
m_{i}=1
. For simplicity, we assume
m_{1}=1
. Then,
a_{1}=1
and
g_{1}\in V_{\mathcal{F},d+1}
,

•

m_{2},\dots,m_{\ell}
are monic monomials of degree at least
2
,

•

a_{2},\dots,a_{\ell}\in\mathbb{K}\setminus 0
and
g_{2},\dots,g_{\ell}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{F}}
,

•

if
m_{i}=m_{j}
then
g_{i}\neq g_{j}
.

For
X,Y\in A_{d}
, we define
X\oplus Y\in A_{d}
as follows. If
(1,1,f_{x})\in X
and
(1,1,f_{y})\in Y
, then
(1,1,f_{x}+f_{y})\in X\oplus Y
. Moreover, for
m
of degree at least
2
, we have that
(m,a,p)\in X\oplus Y
if and only if and only if one of the following occurs:

•

(m,a,p)\in X
, and for all
b\in\mathbb{K}\setminus 0
we have that
(m,b,p)\notin Y
,

•

(m,a,p)\in Y
, and for all
b\in\mathbb{K}\setminus 0
we have that
(m,b,p)\notin X
,

•

there exist
(m,b,p)\in X
and
(m,c,p)\in Y
such that
a=b+c\neq 0
.

Lemma 3.3
.

We have that
(A_{d},\oplus)
is an abelian group whose identity element is
\{(1,1,0)\}
.

Proof.

The fact that
\oplus
is associative and commutative comes from the fact that
+
is associative and commutative over
\mathbb{K}
. Moreover, it is clear from the definition that
\{(1,1,0)\}\oplus X=X
for all
X\in A_{d}
. Finally, let
X=\{(1,1,f_{x}),(m_{2},a_{2},g_{2}),\dots,(m_{\ell},a_{\ell},g_{\ell})\}\in A_{d}
, then the set
\{(1,1,-f_{x}),(m_{2},-a_{2},g_{2}),\dots,(m_{\ell},-a_{\ell},g_{\ell})\}
is also an element of
A_{d}
. We denote this set by
-X
. It is easy to verify that
X\oplus-X=\{(1,1,0)\}
.
∎

For
X=\{(1,1,f_{x}),(m_{2},a_{2},g_{2}),\dots(m_{\ell},a_{\ell},g_{\ell})\}\in A_{d}
and
a\in\mathbb{K}
, we define
a\star X
as

a\star X=\begin{cases}\{(1,1,af_{x}),(m_{2},aa_{2},g_{2}),\dots(m_{\ell},aa_{\ell},g_{\ell})\}&a\neq 0\\
\{(1,1,0)\}&a=0\end{cases}

Clearly, we have that
a\star X\in A_{d}
. One can also verify that
(A_{d},\oplus,\star)
is a vector space over
\mathbb{K}
.

For
X\in A_{d}
we define the degree of
X
as
\deg(X)=\max\deg(m_{i}g_{i})
. Directly from the definition of
\oplus
and of
\star
, we obtain the following lemma.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
X,Y\in A_{d}
and
0\neq a\in\mathbb{K}
, then
\deg(X\oplus Y)\leq\max\{\deg(X),\deg(Y)\}
and
\deg(a\star X)=\deg(X)
.

For
f\in\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
, let
A_{d,f}
be the subset of
A_{d}
given by

A_{d,f}=\left\{\{(m_{1},a_{1},g_{1}),\dots,(m_{\ell},a_{\ell},g_{\ell})\}\in A_{d}:\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}m_{i}a_{i}g_{i}=f\right\}.

Notice that the sets
A_{d,f}
with
f\in\mathcal{F}
form a partition of
A_{d}=\bigsqcup_{f}A_{d,f}
. The following lemma shows how the operations
\oplus
and
\star
act on this partition. We omit the proof since it is straightforward.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
X\in A_{d,f_{x}}
,
Y\in A_{d,f_{y}}
, and
0\neq a\in\mathbb{K}
. Then
X\oplus Y\in A_{d,f_{x}+f_{y}}
and
a\star X\in A_{d,af_{x}}
.

Our goal now is to prove that for every
f\in(\mathcal{F})
, the corresponding
A_{d,f}
is not empty. To achieve this result, we need one last lemma.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
m
be a monic monomial. If
f\in V_{\mathcal{F},d+1}
and
\deg(f)\leq d
, then there exists
X\in A_{d,mf}
such that
\deg(X)=\deg(mf)
.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
\deg(mf)
. If
\deg(mf)\leq d+1
, since
f\in V_{\mathcal{F},d+1}
we have
mf\in V_{\mathcal{F},d+1}
and therefore
X=\{(1,1,mf)\}\in A_{d,mf}
. So, suppose
\deg(mf)>d+1
. If
\deg(f)<d
, there exist
m_{1},m_{2}
monic monomials such that
m=m_{1}m_{2}
and
\deg(m_{1}f)=d
. Since
m_{1}f\in V_{\mathcal{F},d+1}
, without loss of generality, we can assume
\deg(f)=d
.

By Lemma
3.2
, we write
f=\bar{f}+\sum_{i=1}^{D}a_{i}p_{i}
with
\deg(\bar{f})<d
. By inductive hypothesis we have that there exists
\bar{X}\in A_{d,m\bar{f}}
such that
\deg(\bar{X})\leq\deg(m\bar{f})
. Let
f^{\prime}=\sum_{i=1}^{D}a_{i}p_{i}
. It is clear that
X^{\prime}=\{(1,1,0)\}\cup\{(m,a_{i},p_{i}):a_{i}\neq 0\}\in A_{d,mf^{\prime}}
and that
\deg(X^{\prime})=\deg(mf^{\prime})
. Since
mf=m\bar{f}+mf^{\prime}
, by Lemma
3.5
we conclude that
\bar{X}\oplus X^{\prime}\in A_{d,mf}
and that
\deg(X\oplus X^{\prime})=\max\{\deg(m\bar{f}),\deg(mf^{\prime})\}=\deg(mf)
.
∎

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
f\in(\mathcal{F})
, then
A_{d,f}\neq\emptyset
.

Proof.

Since
f\in(\mathcal{F})
, there are
q_{1},\dots,q_{k}\in\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
such that
f=\sum q_{i}f_{i}
. Each
q_{i}f_{i}
can be written as
\sum m_{i,j}a_{i,j}f_{i}
with
m_{i,j}
monic monomial. Since for each
i,j
,
\deg(a_{i,j}f_{i})\leq d
, by Lemma
3.6
there exists
X_{i,j}\in A_{d,a_{i,j}m_{i,j}f_{i}}
. By Lemma
3.5
we conclude that
\sum_{i,j}X_{i,j}\in A_{d,f}
.
∎

The next theorem is central in the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 3.8
.

If
f\in(\mathcal{F})
and
\deg(f)\leq d+1
, then
f\in V_{\mathcal{F},d+1}
.

Proof.

Let
\delta=\min_{X\in A_{d,f}}\deg X
and
A_{\delta}=\{X\in A_{d,f}:\deg(X)=\delta\}
. By Proposition
3.7
we have that
A_{d,f}\neq\emptyset
and so
A_{\delta}\neq\emptyset
. For
X,Y\in A_{\delta}
we say that
X\leq Y
if the greatest monic monomial appearing in
X
is smaller than the greatest monic monomial appearing in
Y
(in degree lexicographic order with:
x_{1}>x_{2}>\dots>x_{n}
). Let
X=\{(m_{1},a_{1},g_{1}),\dots,(m_{\ell},a_{\ell},g_{\ell})\}
be a minimal element of
A_{\delta}
according to this order.
If
\delta\leq d+1
, then
X=\{(1,1,f)\}
and this implies
f\in V_{\mathcal{F},d+1}
.

If
\delta\geq d+2
, suppose for instance that
m_{2}=x_{1}^{b_{1}}\dots x_{n}^{b_{n}}
is maximal among the monomials of
X
and
g_{2}=x_{1}^{c_{1}}\dots x_{n}^{c_{n}}+\bar{g}_{2}
where
\deg(\bar{g}_{2})<d
. Since
\deg(f)\leq d+1
, there exists an index
i
such that
(m_{i}g_{i})^{\mathrm{top}}=(m_{2}g_{2})^{\mathrm{top}}
and
m_{i}<m_{2}
. This implies that

\bar{i}=\min\{i:b_{i}\neq 0\}<\max\{j:c_{j}\neq 0\}=\bar{j}.

Let
p\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{F}}
such that
p^{\mathrm{top}}=x_{1}^{c_{1}}\dots x_{\bar{i}}^{c_{\bar{i}}+1}\dots x_{\bar{j}}^{c_{\bar{j}}-1}
and
m=x_{\bar{i}}^{b_{\bar{i}}-1}\dots x_{\bar{j}}^{b_{\bar{j}}+1}\dots x_{n}^{b_{n}}
. Then, we can write
f
as

f=\sum a_{i}m_{i}g_{i}+a_{2}mp-a_{2}mp=x_{\bar{i}}^{b_{\bar{i}}-1}\dots x_{\bar{j}}^{b_{\bar{j}}}\dots x_{n}^{b_{n}}a_{2}(x_{\bar{i}}g_{2}-x_{\bar{j}}p)+\sum_{i\neq 2}a_{i}m_{i}g_{i}+a_{2}mp.

Notice that
\deg(x_{\bar{i}}g_{2}-x_{\bar{j}}p)\leq d
, and
g=x_{\bar{i}}g_{2}-x_{\bar{j}}p\in V_{\mathcal{F},d+1}
since
g_{2},p\in V_{\mathcal{F},d}
. So, by Lemma
3.6
, there exists
X_{1}\in A_{d,a_{2}x_{\bar{i}}^{b_{\bar{i}}-1}\dots x_{\bar{j}}^{b_{\bar{j}}}\dots x_{n}^{b_{n}}g}
such that
\deg(X_{1})\leq\delta-1
. Let
h=\sum_{i\neq 2}a_{i}m_{i}g_{i}
, then

X_{2}=\{(1,1,0),(m_{3},a_{3},g_{3}),\dots,(m_{\ell},a_{\ell},g_{\ell})\}\in A_{d,h}
with
\deg(X_{2})\leq\delta
. Obviously
X_{3}=\{(1,1,0),(m,a_{2},p)\}\in A_{d,a_{2}mp}
. By Lemma
3.5
,
X_{1}\oplus X_{2}\oplus X_{3}\in A_{d,f}
. Since
m
is smaller than
m_{2}
, one of the following is true:

•

the maximal monomial in
X_{1}\oplus X_{2}\oplus X_{3}
is equal to the maximal monomial in
X
, but it appears a strictly smaller number of times,

•

the maximal monomial in
X_{1}\oplus X_{2}\oplus X_{3}
is smaller than the maximal monomial in
X
.

Since at each step the number of monomials equal to
m_{2}
strictly decreases, repeating this procedure a finite number of times, we find an
\tilde{X}
in
A_{d,f}
that has a smaller maximal monomial than
X
. This contradicts the minimality of
X
. Therefore, we conclude that
\delta\leq d+1
and so
f\in V_{\mathcal{F},d+1}
.
∎

At this point the proof of Theorem
1.1
is straightforward.

Proof of theorem
1.1
.

If
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{reg}}(\mathcal{F})=+\infty
, the statement is trivially true. Otherwise, by Remark
2.3
, we know that
\mathrm{Gbd}_{\sigma}(\mathcal{F})\leq\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{reg}}(\mathcal{F})
. Then, by Theorem
3.8
, we obtain that every element that belongs to a reduced Gröbner basis is in
V_{\mathcal{F},d+1}
.
∎

The next example shows that the bound in Theorem
1.1
is optimal.

Example 3.9
.

Let
1<k\in\mathbb{N}
and let
\mathcal{F}_{k}=\{x^{k}+y,y^{k}+x,xy\}
a family of polynomial in
\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
. We have that
\mathcal{F}_{k}^{\mathrm{top}}=\{x^{k},y^{k},xy\}
and therefore
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{reg}}(\mathcal{F}_{k})=k
. We notice that

x=-y^{k-1}(x^{k}+y)+(y^{k}+x)+x^{k-1}y^{k-2}(xy),

and so
x\in(\mathcal{F}_{k})
. By symmetry, we obtain that also
y\in(\mathcal{F}_{k})
. In particular, we have that
\{x,y\}
is a reduced Gröbner basis for
\mathcal{F}_{k}
for every term order. On the one hand, we observe that
x,y\notin V_{\mathcal{F}_{k},k}
. On the other hand, we have that

y^{2}=y(x^{k}+y)-x^{k-1}(xy)\in V_{\mathcal{F}_{k},k+1}\implies x=(y^{k}+x)-y^{k-2}(y^{2})\in V_{\mathcal{F}_{k},k+1}\implies y\in V_{\mathcal{F}_{k},k+1}.

So,
\{x,y\}\subseteq V_{\mathcal{F}_{k},k+1}
. This implies that
\mathrm{sd}_{\sigma}(\mathcal{F}_{k})=k+1=\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{reg}}(\mathcal{F}_{k})+1
, independently of the choice of the degree-compatible term order
\sigma
.

The next proposition generalizes Theorem
1.1
to an arbitrary family of polynomials.

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
\mathcal{F}=\{f_{1},\dots,f_{k}\}
be a family of polynomials and let
\sigma
be a degree-compatible term order. Then,

\mathrm{sd}_{\sigma}\leq\max\{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{reg}}(\mathcal{F})+1,\deg(f_{1}),\dots,\deg(f_{k})\}.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{F}=\{f_{1},\dots,f_{k}\}
be a family of polynomial,
t=\max\deg(f_{i})
and suppose that
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{reg}}(\mathcal{F})<t
. We denote by
\mathrm{LT}(f)
the leading term of
f
. If there exist indices
i,j
and a monomial
m
such that
\mathrm{LT}(f_{i})=m\mathrm{LT}(f_{j})
, we substitute
f_{i}
with
f_{i}-mf_{j}
. By proceeding in this way, in a finite number of steps we find a family
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}=\{f_{1}^{\prime},\dots,f_{s}^{\prime}\}\subseteq V_{\mathcal{F},t}
such that
(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})=(\mathcal{F})
and for every pair of indices
i,j
we have that
\mathrm{LT}(f_{i})\nmid\mathrm{LT}(f_{j})
. This implies that
\max\deg(f_{i}^{\prime})\leq\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{reg}}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})=d^{\prime}\leq\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{reg}}(\mathcal{F})
. By Theorem
1.1
, we have that
V_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime},d^{\prime}+1}
contains a Gröbner basis of
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
. Since
(\mathcal{F})=(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
,
d^{\prime}<t
, and
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\subseteq V_{\mathcal{F},t}
, we conclude that
V_{\mathcal{F},t}
contains a Gröbner basis of
\mathcal{F}
.
∎

As direct consequence of the previous proposition, we obtain the following upper bound on the last fall degree.

Corollary 3.11
.

Let
\mathcal{F}=\{f_{1},\dots,f_{n}\}
be a family of polynomials. Then,

\mathrm{Lfd}(\mathcal{F})\leq\max\{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{reg}}(\mathcal{F})+1,\deg(f_{1}),\dots,\deg(f_{k})\}.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.6
and Proposition
3.10
, we obtain that

\mathrm{Lfd}(\mathcal{F})\leq\max\{\mathrm{Lfd}(\mathcal{F}),\mathrm{Gbd}_{\sigma}(\mathcal{F})\}=\mathrm{sd}_{\sigma}(\mathcal{F})\leq\max\{\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{reg}}(\mathcal{F})+1,\deg(f_{1}),\dots,\deg(f_{k})\}.\qed

In the next corollary, we provide an upper bound on the solving degree that is linear in both the number of variables and the degree of the elements of the system
\mathcal{F}
.

Corollary 3.12

(Macaulay bound)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}=\{f_{1},\dots,f_{k}\}\subseteq\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be a family of polynomial equations of degree at least
1
such that
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{reg}}(\mathcal{F})<\infty
(and so
k\geq n
). Let
d_{i}=\deg(f_{i})
and suppose that
d_{1}\geq d_{2}\geq\dots\geq d_{k}
. Then, for every degree-compatible term order
\sigma

\mathrm{sd}_{\sigma}(\mathcal{F})\leq d_{1}+\dots+d_{n}-n+2.

Proof.

Since
\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{reg}}(\mathcal{F})<\infty
, there exists
J=\{j_{1},\dots,j_{n}\}\subseteq[k]
such that

\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{reg}}(\mathcal{F})\leq d_{j_{1}}+\dots d_{j_{n}}-n+1
.
Since we assumed that
d_{1}\geq d_{2}\geq\dots\geq d_{k}
, we have that

\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{reg}}(\mathcal{F})\leq d_{1}+\dots d_{n}-n+1
. Moreover for every
f\in\mathcal{F}
we have that

\deg(f)\leq d_{1}\leq d_{1}+(d_{2}-1)+\dots+(d_{n}-1)=d_{1}+\dots d_{n}-n+1.

We conclude applying Proposition
3.10
.
∎

The Macaulay bound was originally proved by Lazard in
[
13
, Theorem 3]
for the maximum degree of an element of a reduced Gröbner basis of
\mathcal{F}
. Notice that our bound (when it can be applied) is stronger. In fact, it depends only on the degree of
n
polynomials, while the bound of Lazard depends on the degree of
n+1
polynomials. A similar but weaker bound was given in
[
4
, Corollary 3.25]
.

We conclude with an explicit upper bound on the complexity of finding a reduced Gröbner basis (with respect to a degree-compatible term order) of a system of polynomial equations in terms of the degree of regularity. For instance, this bound applies when we use algorithms such as MutantXL, MXL2, and similar variants.

Proposition 3.13
.

Let
\mathcal{F}=\{f_{1},\dots,f_{k}\}\subseteq\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be a family such that
\deg(f_{i})\leq\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{reg}}(\mathcal{F})=d
. Then, a Gröbner basis of
\mathcal{F}
can be find in
\mathcal{O}\left((n+1)^{4(d+1)}\right)
operations in
\mathbb{K}
.

Proof.

Let
N=\binom{d+1+n}{d+1}
be the number of monomials of degree at most
d=\mathrm{d}_{\mathrm{reg}}(\mathcal{F})
. By Theorem
1.1
, to construct a Gröbner basis, it is sufficient to find a basis of
V_{\mathcal{F},d+1}
. In order to do that we proceed as follows. With linear operations we can ensure that
\mathrm{LT}(f_{i})\neq\mathrm{LT}(f_{j})
for all
f_{i},f_{j}
. This is equivalent to computing the reduced row echelon form of a matrix with
N
columns and
N
rows, which can be done in
\mathcal{O}(N^{3})
operations in
\mathbb{K}
. We set
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{M}=\{mf_{i}:f_{i}\in\mathcal{F},\text{ m monomial of degree at least 1, and }\deg(mf)\leq d+1\}
. At each step, we pick an element
f
of
\mathcal{M}
, and with linear operations involving only multiple of elements of
\mathcal{B}
we reduce
f
until we obtain an element
\bar{f}
such that either
\mathrm{LT}(\bar{f})=0
or
\mathrm{LT}(\bar{f})\notin\{\mathrm{LT}(b):b\in\mathcal{B}\}
. We remove
f
from
\mathcal{M}
. Then, if
\mathrm{LT}(\bar{f})\notin\{\mathrm{LT}(b):b\in\mathcal{B}\}
, we add
\bar{f}
to
\mathcal{B}
and we add to
\mathcal{M}
all
m\bar{f}
such that
m
is a monomial of degree at least
1
, and
\deg(m\bar{f})\leq d+1
. This operation costs
\mathcal{O}(N^{2})
operations. We repeat this operation until either
\lvert\mathcal{B}\rvert=N
or
\lvert\mathcal{M}\rvert=0
. Every time that we add a new element to
\mathcal{B}
, we add at most
N
elements to
\mathcal{M}
. Since in
\mathcal{B}
we can add at most
N
elements, this algorithm terminates in at most
N^{2}
steps. The cost of each step is
\mathcal{O}(N^{2})
, therefore we have that the a Gröbner basis of
\mathcal{F}
can be find in
\mathcal{O}(N^{4})
operations. Since
N\leq(n+1)^{d+1}
, we conclude.
∎

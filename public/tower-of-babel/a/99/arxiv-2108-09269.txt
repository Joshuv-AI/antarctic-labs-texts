Separation of 𝐏 \mathbf{P} and 𝐍𝐏 \mathbf{NP}
Reiner Czerwinski

Abstract
There have been many attempts to solve the 𝐏 {\mathbf{P}} versus 𝐍𝐏 {\mathbf{NP}} problem. However, with a new proof method, 𝐏 ≠ 𝐍𝐏 {\mathbf{P}}\not={\mathbf{NP}} can be proved. A time limit is set for an arbitrary Turing machine and an input word is rejected on a timeout. The time limit goes toward ∞ \infty . Due to the halting problem, whether a word is accepted can only be determined at run time. It can be shown by Rice’s theorem, if a finite set of words are to be checked, they all have to be tested by brute force.

In

{\mathbf{NP}}

3
Time Complexity of
L(M,t)

In this section, the running time is considered to compute
L(M,t)
.
It is concluded that to check whether a TM
M
in
t

accepts a word,
t

Unfortunately, it takes
0(t)

M

x

x\in L(M,t)
due to the linear speedup theorem. See
[
8

Definition 1

Let
M
be a TM and
x
be an input word. Then,

t_{M}(x)
is the running time of
M
on input
x
if
M
terminates with it.

t_{M}
is a partial function.
t_{M}
is total iff
M
terminates on every
x
.
And

T_{M}(n):=\max\bigl(\{t_{M}(x)\mid|x|\leq n\text{ and }x\in\dom(t_{M})\}\cup\{0\}\bigr)

see
[
12
, Chapter 2.4]
.

Lemma 1

For an arbitrary multi-taped TM
M
with input
x
, it takes
\Omega(t/\log^{2}(t))
computation time in the worst case to find out whether
x
is accepted by
M
within
t
steps, i.e., whether
x\in L(M,t)
.

Proof

Let
T
be a fully time-constructible function. According to the
time hierarchy theorem
[
11
]
, there exists a TM
M
with
L(M)\in{\mathbf{DTIME}}(T)
, but
L(M)\not\in{\mathbf{DTIME}}(T^{\prime})
if
T^{\prime}*\log(T^{\prime})\in o(T)
[
6
, p. 112]
. Let
T^{\prime}=T/\log^{2}(T)
. Then

T^{\prime}*\log(T^{\prime})=T/\log^{2}(T)*\log(T/\log^{2}(T))\leq T/\log(T)\in o(T)
.

Let
x_{n}
with
n\in\bbbn
be a sequence with
|x_{n}|\leq n
and
T_{M}(n)=t_{M}(x_{n})
. To check, if
x_{n}\in L(M,T_{M}(n))
one need
\Omega(T_{M}/\log^{2}(T_{M}))
computation time.

Remark 1

Lemma
1
can also be applied to single taped TMs as a special case of multi-taped TMs.

4
Proof of
{\mathbf{P}}\neq{\mathbf{NP}}

Lemma 2

Let
W=\{w_{1},...,w_{m}\}
be a finite set of words and
M
be a TM.
To find out if every
w\in W
is not accepted by
M
within
t
steps,
i.e.
W\cap L(M,t)=\emptyset
, one need a running time of
\Omega(m*t/\log^{2}(t))
in worst case.

Proof

Proof via induction over
|W|
.
For
W=\{w_{1}\}
it has been proven in Lemma
1
.
In the inductive step
let
M
be an arbitrary deterministic TM and
W=\{w_{1},\ldots,w_{m}\}
be a finite set
of input words.

According to Rice’s theorem, it is undecidable whether
W\cap L(M)=\emptyset
or
W\cap L(M)=\{w_{m}\}
or
W\cap L(M)
is something else.

W
is finite, so

\exists T_{W}\forall t\geq T_{W}\forall w\in W:w\in L(M)\Leftrightarrow w\in L(M,t)

Assume that
t\geq T_{W}
. If one wants to check if
W\cap L(M,t)=\emptyset
, one has to check whether
\{w_{1},\dots,w_{m-1}\}\cap L(M,t)=\emptyset
and
w_{m}\not\in L(M,t)
. This requires by inductions hypothesis
\Omega((m-1)*t/\log^{2}(t))+\Omega(t/\log^{2}(t))

computation time in the worst case.

If
t<T_{W}
should be, then this property is undecidable.
So it is valid too that one needs
\Omega(m*t/\log^{2}(t))
computation time.

Remark 2

That each
x\in W
must be tested independently of the other words from
W
,
can be seen especially if
M
is a UTM and each
x\in W
is the code of a TM.

Consider the following set from Section
1.1
:

S=\{(M,1^{n},1^{t})\mid\text{ DTM }M\text{ and }\exists y\in\{0,1\}^{n}\text{ with }y\in L(M,t)\}

The problem whether
(M,1^{n},1^{t})
is in
S
is
{\mathbf{NP}}
-complete for an arbitrary
M
.
Moreover, it holds:

(M,1^{n},1^{t})\in S\Longleftrightarrow\exists y\in\{0,1\}^{n}:y\in{\mathbf{L}}(M,t)\Longleftrightarrow\{0,1\}^{n}\cap L(M,t)\not=\emptyset

The set
\{0,1\}^{n}
has the cardinality
2^{n}
.
Due to Lemma
2
, one needs a computation time of
\Omega(2^{n}*t/\log^{2}(t))
to check whether a tuple is in
S
in the worst case.

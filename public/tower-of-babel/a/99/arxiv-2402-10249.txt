Abstract
Linear superposition of gravitational fields is shown to be possible for a large class of spacetimes, in some specific coordinates. Explicit examples are presented.

Superposition of gravitational fields.

1
Introduction.

It is well-known that gravitational fields in the framework of General Relativity (GR), do not obey the superposition law; that is, the addition of two free gravitational fields characterized locally by two metrics,
g^{(1)}_{\mu\nu}(x)
and
g^{(2)}_{\mu\nu}(x)
, say
g^{(12)}_{\mu\nu}(x)=g^{(1)}_{\mu\nu}(x)+g^{(2)}_{\mu\nu}(x)
is not itself a free gravitational field. In the presence of an energy-momentum acting as a source the same thing happens; the gravitational field induced by the addition of two different energy-momentum tensors
T^{(1)}_{\mu\nu}(x)
and
T^{(2)}_{\mu\nu}(x)
, namely
T^{(12)}_{\mu\nu}(x)\equiv T^{(1)}_{\mu\nu}(x)+T^{(2)}_{\mu\nu}(x)
is not the sum of the two fields (spacetime metrics) induced by each energy-momentum individually.

g_{\mu\nu}\left[T^{(1)}_{\mu\nu}(x)+T^{(2)}_{\mu\nu}(x)\right]\neq g_{\mu\nu}\left[T^{(1)}_{\mu\nu}(x)\right]+g_{\mu\nu}\left[T^{(2)}_{\mu\nu}(x)\right]

(1)

All this of course stems from the fact that GR is a non-linear theory, and so are Einstein’s equations of motion (EM), which reduce to Ricci flatness in the free case. The addition of two Ricci flat metrics is not itself Ricci flat in general.

One line of research
[
12
]
[
13
]
attempted to define the combined metric
g_{\mu\nu}^{(12)}

through some operation more complex that just a sum. It would be nice to be able to define a
star
operation
g^{(1)}_{\mu\nu}(x)\star g^{(2)}_{\mu\nu}(x)
such that

R_{\mu\nu}\left[g^{(1)}_{\alpha\beta}(x)\star g^{(2)}_{\alpha\beta}(x)\right]=R_{\mu\nu}\left[g^{(1)}_{\alpha\beta}(x)\right]+R_{\mu\nu}\left[g^{(2)}_{\alpha\beta}(x)\right]

(2)

This has however been met with only limited success.

There are exceptions however, and this
in certain coordinates
only. We shall see in detail that in some important cases, superposition holds. Namely there is a class of Ricci flat spacetimes introduced by Kerr-Schild (KS), for which Gürses and Gürsey have shown that the equations of motion linearize. This class is rather large; in it one can find some type D spacetimes, such as Kerr-Newman (and of course some important particular cases, such as Kerr, Schwarzschild and Reissner-Nordstrom) as well as some type N spacetimes, such as gravitational plane waves. It follows that for these spacetimes and in the right coordinates, superposition holds.

It may come at first sight as a surprise that this superposition property only happens in certain reference frames. If one thinks about it, it is only natural. After all, by the equivalence principle, an accelerated frame is locally equivalent to a gravitational field, so that there is always a (free falling) frame in which the gravitational field vanishes at a given point. Of course this free falling frame depends on the particular gravitational field under consideration and would be in general different for each field to be superposed.
The existence of
linear frames
in our case follows the same philosophy valid in this case for all KS spacetimes.

On the other hand, the idea that not all frames are equivalent from the physical point of view is quite old, and can be traced back at least to Fock’s work
[
8
]
, who used to claim that physics laws hold simpler in harmonic coordinates. That said, KS coordinates are not harmonic
[
3
]
ones in general.

It should be stressed that all our results are valid in GR; no extra symmetry (e.g. supersymmetry) needs to be invoked.

2
When is GR linear?

There is an important class of metrics, namely the Kerr-Schild class
[
11
]
[
1
]
[
5
]
characterized by the fact that

g_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}-2V(x)\lambda_{\mu}\lambda_{\nu}

(3)

where
\eta_{\mu\nu}
Minkowski’s metric and the vector
\lambda
is null

g_{\mu\nu}\lambda^{\mu}\lambda^{\nu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}\lambda^{\mu}\lambda^{\nu}=0

(4)

This class contains many physically important type D metrics, like Kerr (and of course Schwarzschild) as well as type N plane gravitational waves. The electromagnetic generalizations like Kerr-Newman are also in this class.

In cartesian coordinates the metric is also unimodular

|g|=1

(5)

and in fact

g^{\mu\nu}=\eta^{\mu\nu}+2V\lambda^{\mu}\lambda^{\nu}

(6)

If the vector is also geodesic, that is

\lambda^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\lambda_{\alpha}=\lambda^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}\lambda_{\alpha}=0

(7)

then the spacetime is algebraically special.

There are two arguments to show that gravity linearizes in the KS frame, which are to some stent independent.

•

A straightforward calculation shows that Ricci’s tensor in the KS frame reduces to

\displaystyle R_{\mu\nu}=-\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\alpha}(l^{\alpha}l_{\nu})-\partial_{\nu}\partial_{\alpha}(l^{\alpha}l_{\mu})+\Box(l_{\mu}l_{\nu})+

\displaystyle+2\left[\partial_{\beta}l^{\alpha}\partial^{\beta}l_{\alpha}-L^{2}+A^{2}-\partial_{\alpha}(K\lambda^{\alpha})\right]l_{\mu}l_{\nu}

(8)

where

\displaystyle K\equiv-\partial_{\alpha}(V\lambda^{\alpha})

\displaystyle l_{\mu}\equiv\sqrt{V}\lambda_{\mu}

\displaystyle A\equiv-\lambda^{\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}\left(\sqrt{V}\right)

\displaystyle L\equiv\partial_{\alpha}l^{\alpha}

(9)

It easily follows that

\displaystyle G^{\mu}_{\penalty\ \nu}=\partial^{\mu}(K\lambda_{\nu})+\partial_{\nu}(K\lambda^{\mu})+\Box(l^{\mu}l_{\nu})-\delta^{\mu}_{\nu}\partial_{\alpha}(K\lambda^{\alpha})

(10)

Bianchi identity implies

\nabla_{\mu}G^{\mu}_{\penalty\ \beta}=\partial_{\mu}G^{\mu}_{\penalty\ \beta}+\Gamma^{\mu}_{\mu\lambda}G^{\lambda}_{\penalty\ \beta}-\Gamma^{\lambda}_{\mu\beta}G^{\mu}_{\penalty\ \lambda}=0

(11)

but given that
\Gamma^{\mu}_{\mu\lambda}=0
because
|g|=1
and

\Gamma^{\lambda}_{\mu\beta}G^{\mu}_{\penalty\ \lambda}=0

(12)

Einstein’s tensor is also conserved in the ordinary sense

\nabla_{\mu}G^{\mu}_{\penalty\ \beta}=\partial_{\mu}G^{\mu}_{\penalty\ \beta}=0

(13)

Now,

2\eta^{\mu\rho}G^{\nu}_{\penalty\ \rho}=\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}\left[\eta^{\mu\nu}g^{\alpha\beta}+\eta^{\alpha\beta}g^{\mu\nu}-\eta^{\mu\alpha}g^{\nu\beta}-\eta^{\nu\alpha}g^{\mu\beta}\right]=2\kappa^{2}T^{\mu\nu}

(14)

Einstein’s equations in the KS frame are linear.

•

Let us now present another argument.
Remember
[
6
]
the definition of
Einstein’s energy-momentum pseudotensor
. Starting with Einstein’s lagrangian

L=\sqrt{|g|}g^{\mu\nu}\left(\Gamma^{\sigma}_{\mu\nu}\Gamma^{\rho}_{\sigma\rho}-\Gamma^{\rho}_{\mu\sigma}\Gamma^{\sigma}_{\nu\rho}\right)

(15)

which only involves the metric components and their first derivatives, we define

\sqrt{|g|}t_{\mu}^{\nu}\equiv-{\partial L\over\partial\left(\partial^{\mu}g_{\alpha\beta}\right)}\partial^{\nu}g_{\alpha\beta}+\delta_{\mu}^{\nu}L

(16)

The result reads

2\kappa^{2}\sqrt{|g|}t_{\mu}^{\nu}=-\left(\Gamma^{\nu}_{\alpha\beta}-\delta_{\beta}^{\nu}\Gamma^{\sigma}_{\alpha\sigma}\right)\partial_{\mu}\left(\sqrt{|g|}g^{\alpha\beta}\right)+L\delta_{\mu}^{\nu}

(17)

It can be proved
[
6
]
that

\partial_{\nu}\left(\sqrt{|g|}t_{\mu}^{\nu}\right)=-{1\over 2\kappa^{2}}\sqrt{|g|}G^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\mu}g_{\alpha\beta}=-{1\over 2}\sqrt{|g|}T^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\mu}g_{\alpha\beta}

(18)

that is

\partial_{\nu}\left(\sqrt{|g|}\left(T_{\mu}\,^{\nu}+t_{\mu}\,^{\nu}\right)\right)=0

(19)

where we have used the fact that

0=\sqrt{|g|}\nabla_{\nu}T_{\mu}^{\nu}=\partial_{\nu}\left(\sqrt{|g|}T_{\mu}^{\nu}\right)-{1\over 2}\sqrt{|g|}T^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\mu}g_{\alpha\beta}

(20)

On the other hand
[
4
]
[
9
]

\sqrt{|g|}\left(T^{\mu}_{\penalty\ \nu}+t^{\mu}_{\penalty\ \nu}\right)=-\frac{1}{2\kappa^{2}}\partial_{\rho}\cal{U}^{\rho\mu}_{\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \nu}

(21)

where we have defined
Freud’s superpotential

[
4
]

\cal{U}^{\mu\rho}_{\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \nu}=-\cal{U}^{\rho\mu}_{\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \nu}=\mathfrak{g}_{\nu\sigma}\partial_{\lambda}\left(\mathfrak{g}^{\sigma\mu}\mathfrak{g}^{\rho\lambda}-\mathfrak{g}^{\sigma\rho}\mathfrak{g}^{\mu\lambda}\right)

(22)

with

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}^{\mu\nu}\equiv\sqrt{-g}g^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}_{\mu\nu}\equiv\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}g_{\mu\nu}

(23)

In the particular case of the KS ansatz the superpotential reads

{\cal{U}}^{\mu\rho}_{\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \nu}=2\partial_{\beta}\left[\delta^{\mu}_{\nu}l^{\rho}l^{\beta}+\eta^{\beta\rho}l_{\nu}l^{\mu}-\eta^{\beta\mu}l_{\nu}l^{\rho}-\delta^{\rho}_{\nu}l^{\mu}l^{\beta}\right]

(24)

Einstein equations tell us that

G^{\mu}_{\penalty\ \nu}=\kappa^{2}T^{\mu}_{\penalty\ \nu}=-{1\over 2\sqrt{|g|}}\partial_{\rho}{\cal U}^{\rho\mu}\,_{\nu}

(25)

It is an important fact
[
10
]
that for all KS spacetimes the pseudo tensor
t^{\mu}_{\penalty\ \nu}=0
.

This second argument is a little bit roundabout, but physically, all nonlinearities are hidden in this pseudotensor, so that its vanishing is one technical explanation as to why the equations of motion linearize.

We conclude that Einstein’s field equation take the linear form

\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}\left[\eta^{\mu\nu}g^{\alpha\beta}+\eta^{\alpha\beta}g^{\mu\nu}-\eta^{\mu\alpha}g^{\nu\beta}-\eta^{\nu\alpha}g^{\mu\beta}\right]=2\kappa^{2}\eta^{\mu\rho}T^{\nu}_{\penalty\ \rho}

(26)

The Fierz-Pauli
[
7
]
equation (which are equivalent to the linearized Einstein’s) reads

\Box\left(h_{\mu\nu}-h\eta_{\mu\nu}\right)-\partial_{\mu}\partial^{\lambda}h_{\lambda\nu}-\partial_{\nu}\partial^{\lambda}h_{\lambda\mu}+\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}h+\eta_{\mu\nu}\partial^{\lambda}\partial^{\delta}h_{\lambda\sigma}=0

(27)

(where
h\equiv\eta^{\alpha\beta}h_{\alpha\beta}
. The KS equation of motion is then equivalent to Fierz-Pauli’s with
h=0
(which stems from unimodularity).

In conclusion, all this means that in all KS spacetimes, there is superposition of gravitational fields,
provided we are using coordinates in which the basic equation (

) holds true

3
Kerr-Schild coordinates.

The general Kerr-Newman metric reads
[
1
]

g_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}-2V\lambda_{\mu}\lambda_{\nu}

(28)

with

2V={2Gmr^{3}-Q^{2}r^{2}\over r^{4}+a^{2}z^{2}}

(29)

and

\lambda_{\mu}dx^{\mu}=dt+{z\over r}dz+{r\over r^{2}+a^{2}}(xdx+ydy)-{a\over r^{2}+a^{2}}(xdt-ydx)

(30)

where
r
is defined through

{x^{2}+y^{2}\over r^{2}+a^{2}}+{z^{2}\over r^{2}}=1

(31)

Here the parameter
m
defines the mass of the spacetime,
a
is related to the angular momentum, and
Q
stands for the electromagnetic charge.

3.1
Superposition of Schwarzschild metrics in different frames.

•

In order to stress the importance of the reference system, let us consider the simplest example of Schwarzschild space time in the KS frame where

V={r_{s}\over 2r}

(32)

and

\lambda_{\mu}dx^{\mu}=dt+{xdx+ydy+zdz\over r}=dt+dr

(33)

We use
r
only as a abbreviation, not as a coordinate. To be specific, the form of the metric in KS coordinates reads

ds^{2}=\left(1-{r_{s}\over r}\right)dt^{2}-\left(\delta_{ij}+{r_{s}\over r^{3}}\sum_{ij}x^{i}x^{j}\right)dx^{i}dx^{j}-2r_{s}\sum_{i}{x^{i}\over r^{2}}dx^{i}dt

(34)

It can be easily verified that this metric is Ricci flat and unimodular.

Let us now consider a superposition of two such metrics

\text{d}s^{2}_{(12)}=\text{d}s^{2}_{(1)}+\text{d}s^{2}_{(2)}=2\left[\text{d}t^{2}-\text{d}x^{2}-\text{d}y^{2}-\text{d}z^{2}\right]-\frac{r_{1}+r_{2}}{r}\left[\text{d}t+\frac{1}{r}\left(x\text{d}x+\text{d}y+z\text{d}z\right)\right]^{2}

(35)

It is possible to check that this superposition is Ricci flat as well.

•

In the usual Schwarzschild coordinates

\text{d}s^{2}_{(1)}=\left(1-\frac{r_{1}}{r}\right)\text{d}t-\frac{1}{\left(1-\frac{r_{1}}{r}\right)}\text{d}r-\text{d}\Omega^{2}

(36)

and

\text{d}s^{2}_{(2)}=\left(1-\frac{r_{2}}{r}\right)\text{d}t-\frac{1}{\left(1-\frac{r_{2}}{r}\right)}\text{d}r-\text{d}\Omega^{2}

(37)

for simplicity we call
r_{1}
and
r_{2}
the Schwarzschild radii.

In spite of the fact that both metrics are Ricci flat, their sum

\text{d}s^{2}_{(12)}=\left(2-\frac{r_{1}+r_{2}}{r}\right)\text{d}t-\left[\frac{1}{\left(1-\frac{r_{1}}{r}\right)}+\frac{1}{\left(1-\frac{r_{2}}{r}\right)}\right]\text{d}r-2\text{d}\Omega^{2}

(38)

is not. To be specific,

\displaystyle R_{tt}=\frac{(r_{1}-r_{2})^{2}(r_{1}+r_{2})}{4r^{3}\left(r_{1}+r_{2}-2r\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle R_{rr}=\frac{(r_{1}-r_{2})^{2}\left[3(r_{1}+r_{2})-8r\right]}{4r(r-r_{1})(r-r_{2})\left(r_{1}+r_{2}-2r\right)^{2}}

(39)

This means that there is no superposition in this frame.

•

In fact one can pass from the usual reference system to the Kerr-Schild one as follows. Start from

\text{d}s^{2}=\left(1-\frac{r_{s}}{r}\right)\text{d}t^{2}-\left(1+\frac{r_{s}}{r}\right)\text{d}r^{2}-\frac{2r_{s}}{r}\text{d}r\text{d}t-r^{2}\text{d}\Omega^{2}

(40)

Define a new temporal coordinate

\text{d}\bar{t}=f\left[A\text{d}t-B\text{d}r\right]

(41)

Then

A\text{d}t^{2}-2B\text{d}t\text{d}r=\frac{\text{d}\bar{t}^{2}}{f^{2}A}-\frac{B^{2}\text{d}r^{2}}{A}

(42)

where in our case

\displaystyle A=1-\frac{r_{s}}{r}

\displaystyle B=\frac{r_{s}}{r}

(43)

and the metric reduces to

\text{d}s^{2}=\frac{\text{d}\bar{t}^{2}}{f^{2}\left(1-\frac{r_{s}}{r}\right)}-\frac{1}{\left(1-\frac{r_{s}}{r}\right)}\text{d}r^{2}-r^{2}\text{d}\Omega^{2}

(44)

Taking
f^{2}\equiv\frac{1}{\left(1-\frac{r_{s}}{r}\right)^{2}}
(this makes the second member of (
41
) a total differential) yields the traditional metric in Schwarzschild coordinates

\text{d}s^{2}=\left(1-\frac{r_{s}}{r}\right)\text{d}\bar{t}^{2}-\frac{1}{\left(1-\frac{r_{s}}{r}\right)}\text{d}r^{2}-r^{2}\text{d}\Omega^{2}

(45)

•

In fact, it is even simpler. In Eddington-Filkenstein outgoing coordinates, where

u=t-r^{*}

(46)

and

r^{*}\equiv r+r_{s}\log\,(r-r_{s})

(47)

is the usual tortoise coordinate, Schwarzschild’s metric reads

ds^{2}=du^{2}+2dudr-r^{2}d\Omega^{2}-{r_{s}\over r}du^{2}

(48)

which is already in the KS frame with

-2V\lambda_{\mu}\lambda_{\nu}dx^{\mu}dx^{\nu}=-{r_{s}\over r}du^{2}

(49)

It is however plain that this form is not unimodular. This can be fixed by defining new coordinates
\mu
and
x
such that

\displaystyle x={r^{3}\over 3}

\displaystyle\mu=\cos\,\theta

(50)

The unimodular metric reads

ds^{2}=\left(1-{r_{s}\over(3x)^{1/3}}\right)du^{2}-2(3x)^{-2/3}dudx-(3x)^{2/3}\left({d\mu^{2}\over 1-\mu^{2}}+(1-\mu^{2})d\phi^{2}\right)

(51)

It is remarkable that superposition works with both metrics (
48
) and (
51
).

3.2
Superposition of plane waves

Consider now gravitational plane waves

\text{d}s^{2}_{(1)}=\text{d}u\text{d}v-H^{(1)}_{ab}[u]x^{a}x^{b}\text{d}u^{2}-\delta_{ab}\text{d}x^{a}\text{d}x^{b}

(52)

and

\text{d}s^{2}_{(2)}=\text{d}u\text{d}v-H^{(2)}_{ab}[u]x^{a}x^{b}\text{d}u^{2}-\delta_{ab}\text{d}x^{a}\text{d}x^{b}

(53)

which are Ricci flat if
H_{ab}
is traceless (cf.
[
2
]
for a recent reference)

H_{pq}[u]x^{p}x^{q}\equiv a_{+}[u](x^{2}-y^{2})+2a_{\times}[u]xy

(54)

It is plain that the sum of two such Ricci flat waves is also another Ricci flat wave of the same type.

4
Conclusions

In spite of the fact that GR is a non-linear theory, and so are Einstein’s equations of motion (EM), which reduce to Ricci flatness in the free case, in this paper we have pointed out that in some cases one can superpose gravitational fields, but this
in certain coordinates
only. This happens in a class of spacetimes introduced by Kerr-Schild, for which Einstein’s equations linearize. This class is rather large; in it one can find some type D spacetimes, such as Kerr-Newman (and of course some important particular cases, such as Kerr, Schwarzschild and Reissner-Nordstrom) as well as some type N spacetimes, such as gravitational plane waves. It follows that for these spacetimes and in the right coordinates, superposition holds.

We have also discussed why it is not physically surprising that this superposition property only takes place in certain reference frames. In fact, it is only natural. Not all frames are equivalent from the physical point of view. After all, by the equivalence principle, an accelerated frame is locally equivalent to a gravitational field, so that there is always a (free falling) frame in which the gravitational field vanishes at a given point. Of course this free falling frame depends on the particular gravitational field under consideration and would be in general different for each field to be superposed.

The existence of
linear frames
in our case follows the same philosophy valid in this case for all Kerr-Schild spacetimes; this is the main surprising result. In some sense, the KS frame is the generalization of the free falling frame valid for all spacetimes in this family. In this frame and for those spacetimes, gravity is linear.

We learn that superposition in this framework is not a diffeomorphism invariant property.

We are still a long way from being able to elucidate the physical implications of our results. One of the most important questions is what is the meaning of the KS frame in the general case and how it can it be related to observables.
Some quantum effects in these spacetimes have been studied in
[
2
]
. It would be very interesting to see whether some generalization of wave packets is possible for gravitational plane waves.

It has been also pointed out that all our results are valid in GR; no extra symmetry (e.g. supersymmetry) needs to be invoked.

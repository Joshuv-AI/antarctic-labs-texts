Location estimation for symmetric log-concave densities
Nilanjana Laha ??

Abstract
We revisit the problem of estimating the center of symmetry θ \theta of an unknown symmetric density f f . Although Stone 1975 , Van Eeden 1970 , and Sacks 1975 constructed adaptive estimators of θ \theta in this model, their estimators depend on tuning parameters. In an effort to circumvent the dependence on tuning parameters, we impose an additional assumption of log-concavity on f f . We show that in this shape-restricted model, the maximum likelihood estimator (MLE) of θ \theta exists. We also study some truncated one-step estimators and show that they are n − \sqrt{n}- consistent, and nearly achieve the asymptotic efficiency bound. We also show that the rate of convergence for the MLE is O p ( n − 2 / 5 ) O_{p}(n^{-2/5}) . Furthermore, we show that our estimators are robust with respect to the violation of the log-concavity assumption. In fact, we show that the one step estimators are still n \sqrt{n} -consistent under some mild conditions. These analytical conclusions are supported by simulation studies.

1
Introduction

In this paper, we revisit the symmetric location model with an additional shape-restriction of log-concavity.
We let
\mathcal{P}
denote the class of all densities on the real line
\mathbb{R}
.
For any
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
, denote by
\mathcal{S}_{\theta}
the class of all densities that are symmetric about
\theta
.
We take

\mathcal{C}:=\bigg\{\phi:\mathbb{R}\mapsto[-\infty,\infty)\ \bigg|\ \phi\text{ is concave, closed, and proper}\bigg\},

(1.1)

and define
\mathcal{SC}_{\theta}=\mathcal{S}_{\theta}\cap\mathcal{C}
. Here a proper and closed concave function is as defined in
Rockafellar 1970
, page 24 and 50.
Letting
\mathcal{LC}
denote the class of log-concave densities

\mathcal{LC}:=\bigg\{f\in\mathcal{P}\ \bigg|\ \phi=\log f\in\mathcal{C}\bigg\},

we set
\mathcal{SLC}_{\theta}=\mathcal{LC}\cap\mathcal{S}_{\theta}
.
We aim to estimate
\theta
in the log-concave symmetric location model

\mathcal{P}_{0}=\bigg\{f\in\mathcal{P}\ \bigg|\ f(x;\theta)=g(x-\theta),\ \theta\in\mathbb{R},\ g\in\mathcal{SLC}_{0},\ \mathcal{I}_{f}<\infty\bigg\},

(1.2)

where
\mathcal{I}_{f}
is the Fisher information for location.
It is well-established that
(
Huber 1964
, Theorem 3)

\mathcal{I}_{f}
is finite if and only if
f
is an absolutely continuous density satisfying

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\bigg(\dfrac{f^{\prime}(x)}{f(x)}\bigg)^{2}f(x)dx<\infty,

where
f^{\prime}
is an
L_{1}
-derivative of
f
. Also, in this case,
\mathcal{I}_{f}
takes the form

\mathcal{I}_{f}=\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\bigg(\dfrac{f^{\prime}(x)}{f(x)}\bigg)^{2}f(x)dx.

Estimation of
\theta
in the full symmetric location model

\mathcal{P}_{s}=\bigg\{f\in\mathcal{P}\ \bigg|\ f(x;\theta)=g(x-\theta),\ \theta\in\mathbb{R},\ g\in\mathcal{S}_{0},\ \mathcal{I}_{f}<\infty\bigg\}

(1.3)

is an old semi-parametric problem, dating back to
Stein 1956
. From then on, this problem has been considered by many early authors including, but not limited to,
Stone 1975
,
Beran 1974
,
Sacks 1975
, and
Van Eeden 1970
.
There are two main reasons behind the assumption of symmetry in this model. First, as
Stone 1975
has noted, if
f
is totally unrestricted,
\theta
is not identifiable. Second,
the definition of location becomes less clear in the absence of symmetry
(
Takeuchi 1975
)
.

The appeal of the above model lies in the fact that
adaptive estimation of
\theta
is possible in this model
(
Stone 1975
)
.
In other words, there exist consistent estimators of
\theta
in
\mathcal{P}_{s}
, whose variances attain the parametric lower bound, which is
\mathcal{I}_{f}^{-1}
in this case.
See Sections
3.2
,
3.3
, and
6.3
of
Bickel et al. 1998
for more discussion on adaptive estimation in
\mathcal{P}_{s}
.

Now we will briefly discuss some existing methods of estimating
\theta
in
\mathcal{P}_{s}
.

Stone 1975
considers a one-step estimator. One-step estimators generally depend on the estimation of the score
-f^{\prime}/f
. This paper estimates the scores using symmetrized Gaussian kernels based on truncated data, thereby incorporating two tuning parameters, one for the truncation, and another for the scaling of the Gaussian Kernel. Although
Stone 1975
requires the tuning parameters to satisfy some conditions
(
Stone 1975
, Theorem
5.3
)
, he does not provide any prescription for choosing them.
Sacks 1975
considers a subclass of
\mathcal{P}_{s}
with some restrictions on the score function that are weaker than the assumption of log-concavity, and includes heavy-tailed distributions like Cauchy. His estimator is motivated by a linear function of the order statistics, whose coefficients depend on the score functions. The estimation procedure involves three tuning parameters.
Sacks 1975
gives some examples of these tuning parameters, but does not provide any data-dependent method for choosing them.
Beran 1974
uses linearized rank estimate for adaptive estimation of
\theta
in
\mathcal{P}_{s}
. But he makes the additional assumption that the score is twice continuously differentiable, which excludes densities like Laplace. His method is based on the Fourier series expansion of the score function and requires a tuning parameter for choosing the degree of the associated complex trigonometric polynomial.
Beran 1974
does not provide any data-dependent procedure to choose this tuning parameter.

Now we provide some justification for imposing the shape restriction of log-concavity. The class of log-concave densities,
\mathcal{LC}
, belongs to the larger class of unimodal densities. Unimodality is a reasonable assumption in our context, because, as
Takeuchi 1975
points out, in practice, multimodal densities generally result from unimodal mixtures. Separate procedures are available for the latter class. The problem with the class of unimodal densities, however, is that it does not admit an MLE
(
Birgé 1997
)
. Many common symmetric unimodal continuous densities, notwithstanding, are in
\mathcal{LC}
, which, being a smaller and richer class, admit MLE
(
Pal et al. 2007
;
Dümbgen and Rufibach 2009
;
Doss and Wellner 2019
;
Xu and Samworth 2019
, cf.)
. Since the MLE does not depend on tuning parameters, it is possible to estimate
f
without relying on any tuning parameter, which can lead to tuning parameter free estimation of
\theta
. This is the main reason to opt for the shape-restriction of log-concavity.

Although shape-constrained estimation has a rich history, so far there has been very little to no use of shape-constraints in connection with semiparametric
models.
In fact, to the best of our knowledge,
Van Eeden 1970
is the only one to incorporate shape-constraints in treating the one-sample symmetric location problem considered here. Actually
Van Eeden 1970
uses log-concavity and considers estimation in
\mathcal{P}_{0}
although her paper does not mention log-concavity. She restricts
\mathcal{P}_{s}
by assuming that
f^{\prime}(F^{-1}(u))/f(F^{-1}(u))
is non-increasing in
u
, which is equivalent to
f
being log-concave.
Van Eeden 1970
considers data-partitioning to estimate scores from a small fraction of the data and constructs the Hodges-Lehmann rank estimate of location
(
Hodges and Lehmann 1963
)
from the remaining data.
The partitioning of the data and the construction of the score function involve some tuning parameters.

Van Eeden 1970
does not explicitly describe how to choose these tuning parameters.
We also want to remark about
Bhattacharyya and Bickel 2013
, who consider both location and scale estimation in an elliptical symmetry model, which albeit bearing some resemblance, is different from
\mathcal{P}_{0}
. Also,
Bhattacharyya and Bickel 2013
’s estimation procedure is completely different from ours.

To summarize, our goal is to bridge the gap between the symmetric location model and log-concavity. In doing so, we also try to exploit the structure in the class
\mathcal{LC}
to get rid of the dependence on external tuning parameters. Here is a brief account of our main contributions.

The MLE

We show that the MLE of
\theta
and
g
exist in
\mathcal{P}_{0}
. It also follows that
\widehat{\theta}_{n}
, i.e. the MLE of
\theta
, has the rate of convergence
O_{p}(n^{-2/5})
. In addition, we show that the Hellinger distance between
f\in\mathcal{P}_{0}
and its MLE
\widehat{f}_{n}
is of order
O_{p}(n^{-2/5})
, where the Hellinger distance
H(f,g)
between two densities
f
and
g
is defined by

H^{2}(f,g)=2^{-1}\int(\sqrt{f(x)}-\sqrt{g(x)})^{2}dx.

The above-mentioned rate is analogous to the rate obtained by
Doss and Wellner 2019
for the MLE of
f
in
\mathcal{SLC}_{0}
, or by
Doss and Wellner 2016
for the MLE of
f
in
\mathcal{LC}
.

The One step estimator

We also propose four one step estimators for
\theta
, which generally use on the mean, median or a trimmed mean as the preliminary estimator. We estimate the score using either the MLE of
f
in
\mathcal{LC}
, or in
\mathcal{SLC}_{\bar{\theta}_{n}}
, which can be efficiently computed using the R packages ”logcondens” and ”logcondens.mode”, respectively. We establish that a truncated version of the one-step estimators is
\sqrt{n}-
consistent, where its variance also nearly achieves the asymptotic efficiency bound
\mathcal{I}_{f}^{-1}
. The truncated estimator
\tilde{\theta}_{n}
, however, use a tuning parameter.

Model misspecification

We show that our estimators are robust to the violation of the log-concavity assumption.
In particular, we show that, even if
f\notin\mathcal{P}_{0}
, as long as
f
is symmetric about
\theta
, the MLE and the truncated one step estimators are still strongly consistent. Additionally, for the specific case of the truncated one-step estimators,
\sqrt{n}
-consistency is also preserved. The MLE, on the other hand, may still possess a limit even when
f
is not symmetric, or if the data generating distribution does not even possess a density. For the analysis in the misspecified case, we rely on the log-concave projection theory developed by
Dümbgen et al. 2011
,
Cule and Samworth 2010
, and
Xu and Samworth 2019
.
The early authors,
Stone 1975
,
Beran 1974
,
Sacks 1975
, and
Van Eeden 1970
did not discuss the behavior of their estimators under model miss-specification although Stone (1975) did discuss the behavior of his estimator when
\mathcal{I}_{f_{0}}=\infty
.

The article is organized as follows. In Section
2.1
, we introduce the one step estimators, and in
2.2
, we discuss the existence of the MLE and its characterizations. We study the asymptotic properties of our estimators in Section
3
. We provide a simulation study in Section
4
. The subtleties of our estimators are deferred to the Discussion Section. We collect the proofs in Section
6
.

1.1
Preliminaries/ Notation and terminology

We assume that
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
are independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) random variables with density
f_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{0}
. Therefore, (
1.2
) indicates that
f_{0}=g_{0}(\mathord{\cdot}-\theta_{0})
, where
g_{0}\in\mathcal{SLC}_{0}
, the class of all log-concave densities symmetric about
0
. We set
\phi_{0}=\log f_{0}
, and
\psi_{0}=\log g_{0}
. We let
F_{0}
and
G_{0}
denote the respective distribution functions of
f_{0}
and
g_{0}
, and denote by
P_{0}
the measure corresponding to
F_{0}
. We denote the empirical distribution function of the
X_{i}
’s by
\mathbb{F}_{n}
, and write
\mathbb{P}_{n}
for the corresponding empirical measure. The (classical) empirical process of the
X_{i}
’s will be denoted by
\mathbb{Z}_{n}=\sqrt{n}(\mathbb{F}_{n}-F_{0})
.
For any integrable function
h:\mathbb{R}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
, we write

\mathbb{P}_{n}h:=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}{h(X_{i})}\quad{\text{and}}\quad P_{0}h=\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}h(x)f_{0}(x)dx.

As usual, we denote the order statistics of a sample
(Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{n})
by

Y_{()}=(Y_{(1)},\ldots,Y_{(n)}).

In context of a concave function
\psi:\mathbb{R}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
, the domain
\mathrm{dom}(\psi)
will be defined as in
Rockafellar 1970
(page
40
), that is,

\mathrm{dom}(\psi)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}|\psi(x)>-\infty\}
.
For any concave function
\psi
, we say
x\in\mathbb{R}
is a knot of
\psi
, if either
\psi^{\prime}(x+)\neq\psi^{\prime}(x-)
, or
x
is at the boundary of
\mathrm{dom}(\psi)
. We denote by
\mathcal{K}(\psi)
the set of the knots of
\psi
. Unless otherwise mentioned, for a real valued function
h
, provided they exist,
h^{\prime}
and
h^{\prime}(\mathord{\cdot}-)
will refer to the right and left derivatives of
h
respectively. For a distribution function
F
, we let
J(F)
denote the set
\{0<F<1\}
. For any non-negative real-valued function
h
, we denote the support by
\text{supp}(h)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}|h(x)>0\}
.
As usual, we denote the set of natural numbers by
\mathbb{N}
.

Next, we develop some notation for maximum likelihood estimation.
For
\psi\in\mathcal{SC}_{0}
and
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
, following
Dümbgen et al. 2011
and
Xu and Samworth 2019
, we define the criterion function for maximum likelihood estimation by

\Psi(\theta,\psi,F)=\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\psi(x-\theta)dF(x)-\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{\psi(x-\theta)}dx.

(1.4)

Following
Silverman 1982
, we included a Lagrange term to get rid of the normalizing constant involved in density estimation. This is a common device in log-concave density estimation literature
(
Dümbgen and Rufibach 2009
;
Doss and Wellner 2019
, cf.)
.
We use the notation
\Psi_{n}(\theta,\psi)
to denote the sample version
\Psi(\theta,\psi,\mathbb{F}_{n})
of
\Psi(\theta,\psi,F)
. Thus,

\Psi_{n}(\theta,\psi)=\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\psi(x-\theta)d\mathbb{F}_{n}(x)-\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{\psi(x-\theta)}dx.

(1.5)

We denote the maximized criterion function by

L(F)=\sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{R},\psi\in\mathcal{SC}_{0}}\Psi(\theta,\psi,F).

(1.6)

2
Estimators

2.1
One-step estimators

This section focuses on constructing the one-step estimators of
\theta
. To this end, our first task is to choose suitable preliminary estimator
\bar{\theta}_{n}
of
\theta
. We will impose two requirements on
\bar{\theta}_{n}
:
\sqrt{n}
-consistency and strong consistency. The sample mean and the sample median also satisfy our requirements on
\bar{\theta}_{n}
when
f_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{0}
. For a general
f_{0}
, the
Z
-estimator of the shift in the logistic location shift model satisfies these requirements under minimal regularity conditions
(
Van der Vaart 1998
, cf. Example 5.40 and Theorem
5.23
,)
.

Suppose
\tilde{g}_{n}
is an estimator of the centered density
g_{0}=f_{0}(\cdot+\theta_{0})
. We let
\tilde{\psi}_{n}
denote the respective log-density
\log\tilde{g}_{n}
,
and write
\tilde{G}_{n}
for the distribution function corresponding to
\tilde{g}_{n}
.
Ideally, to construct a one-step estimator in this setting, one would first estimate the Fisher information by

\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{n}=\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\tilde{\psi}^{\prime}_{n}(x-\bar{\theta}_{n})^{2}d\mathbb{F}_{n}(x),

(2.1)

and set

\check{\theta}_{n}=\bar{\theta}_{n}-\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\dfrac{\tilde{\psi}^{\prime}_{n}(x-\bar{\theta}_{n})}{\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{n}}d\mathbb{F}_{n}(x).

(2.2)

It is not hard to see that the choice of
\tilde{g}_{n}
will heavily influence the performance of
\check{\theta}_{n}
. We want
\tilde{g}_{n}
to be symmetric about
0
, and if possible, log-concave.

A simple way of estimating
f_{0}=g_{0}(\mathord{\cdot}-\theta_{0})
is to compute its MLE in
\mathcal{LC}
, the class of all log-concave densities. However, this log-concave MLE, which we will denote by
\widehat{h}_{n}
from now on, may violate the requirement of symmetry. In what follows, we show that, this naive estimator
\widehat{h}_{n}
can be improved to yield better estimators of
g_{0}
.

Symmetrized estimator
\tilde{g}_{n}^{sym}

The simplest way of obtaining a symmetrized estimator of
g_{0}
from
\widehat{h}_{n}
is by symmetrizing the latter about
\bar{\theta}_{n}
, which yields

\tilde{g}_{n}^{sym}(z)=\dfrac{1}{2}\bigg(\widehat{h}_{n}(\bar{\theta}_{n}+z)+\widehat{h}_{n}(\bar{\theta}_{n}-z)\bigg),\quad z\in\mathbb{R}.

(2.3)

Although
\tilde{g}_{n}^{sym}
is symmetric, it is not, in general, log-concave. Additionally, the performance of
\tilde{g}_{n}^{sym}
can suffer from the lack of smoothness of
\widehat{h}_{n}
. Our simulations show that the one-step estimator based on
\tilde{g}_{n}^{sym}
has considerably less efficiency, especially for smaller sample sizes. Therefore, we seek to improve the performance of
\tilde{g}_{n}^{sym}
by incorporating smoothing, which leads to our next estimator of
g_{0}.

Smoothed symmetrized estimator
(\tilde{g}_{n}^{sym})^{sm}

To perform smoothing, our first task is to choose a data-dependent smoothing parameter
\widehat{b}_{n}
. Towards this end, following
Chen and Samworth 2013
, we take

\widehat{b}_{n}^{2}:=\widehat{\sigma}^{2}_{n}-\tilde{\sigma}_{n}^{2},

(2.4)

where
\widehat{\sigma}^{2}_{n}
is the sample variance and
\tilde{\sigma}_{n}^{2}
is the variance corresponding to the density
\widehat{h}_{n}
, i.e.

\widehat{\sigma}^{2}_{n}=\dfrac{1}{n-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(X_{i}-\bar{X})^{2},

and

\tilde{\sigma}_{n}^{2}=\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}z^{2}\widehat{h}_{n}(z)dz-\bigg(\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}z\widehat{h}_{n}(z)dz\bigg)^{2}.

that the right hand side of (
2.4
) is positive follows from (2.1) of
Chen and Samworth 2013
.
We define the smoothed symmetrized estimator by

(\tilde{g}_{n}^{sym})^{sm}(z):=\dfrac{1}{\widehat{b}_{n}}\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\tilde{g}_{n}^{sym}(z-t)\varphi(t/\widehat{b}_{n})dt,\quad z\in\mathbb{R},

(2.5)

where
\varphi
is the standard normal density.
The estimator
{(\tilde{g}_{n}^{sym})^{sm}}
can also be represented in terms of the
smoothed version of
\widehat{h}_{n}
, which we define by

\widehat{h}_{n}^{sm}(z)=\dfrac{1}{\widehat{b}_{n}}\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\widehat{h}_{n}(z-t)\varphi(t/\widehat{b}_{n})dt,\quad z\in\mathbb{R}.

(2.6)

To see this, note that

\displaystyle{(\tilde{g}_{n}^{sym})^{sm}}(z)=\dfrac{{\widehat{h}_{n}^{sm}}(\bar{\theta}_{n}+z)+{\widehat{h}_{n}^{sm}}(\bar{\theta}_{n}-z)}{2}.

(2.7)

Though
(\tilde{g}_{n}^{sym})^{sm}
leads to a more efficient one-step estimator of
\theta_{0}
in small samples, it is not necessarily log-concave, which leaves room for further improvement.

Partial MLE estimator
\widehat{g}_{\bar{\theta}_{n}}

The following estimator of
g_{0}
is obtained by maximizing the criterion function
\Psi_{n}(\bar{\theta}_{n},\psi)
in (
1.5
) over
\psi\in\mathcal{SC}_{0}
. From Theorem 2.1(C) of
Doss and Wellner 2019
, it follows that the maximizer
\widehat{\psi}_{\bar{\theta}_{n}}
satisfies

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{\widehat{\psi}_{\bar{\theta}_{n}}(x)}dx=1,

which leads to the density estimator

\widehat{g}_{\bar{\theta}_{n}}=e^{\widehat{\psi}_{\bar{\theta}_{n}}}.

(2.8)

We call this estimator a
Partial MLE estimator
to distinguish it from the traditional MLE of
g_{0}
, which we will discuss in Section
2.2
.
Notice that
\widehat{g}_{\bar{\theta}_{n}}
satisfies both requirements of symmetry and log-concavity.

From
Dümbgen and Rufibach 2009
and
Doss and Wellner 2019
, it follows that the three symmetric density estimators
\tilde{g}_{n}
we have discussed so far share the same support
[-d,d]
, where

d=\max(X_{(n)}-\bar{\theta}_{n},\bar{\theta}_{n}-X_{(1)}).

Geometric mean type symmetrized estimator
\tilde{g}_{n}^{geo,sym}

Similar to
\tilde{g}_{n}^{sym}
, this estimator is also a symmetrized version of
\widehat{h}_{n}
. However, this time the symmetrization is based on the geometric mean of
\widehat{h}_{n}(\bar{\theta}_{n}+\mathord{\cdot})
and
\widehat{h}_{n}(\bar{\theta}_{n}-\mathord{\cdot})
. For
z\in\mathbb{R}
, we define this estimator by

\tilde{g}_{n}^{geo,sym}(z):=C_{n}^{geo}\bigg(\widehat{h}_{n}(\bar{\theta}_{n}+z)\widehat{h}_{n}(\bar{\theta}_{n}-z)\bigg)^{1/2},

(2.9)

where
C_{n}^{geo}
is a random normalizing constant. Since
the sum of two concave functions is again concave, it is not hard to see that
\tilde{g}_{n}^{geo,sym}
is log-concave. However, its support

\text{supp}(\tilde{g}_{n}^{geo,sym})=[-\min(X_{(n)}-\bar{\theta}_{n},\bar{\theta}_{n}-X_{(1)}),\min(X_{(n)}-\bar{\theta}_{n},\bar{\theta}_{n}-X_{(1)})]

is smaller than that of the previous estimators of
g_{0}
.

Constructing the truncated one-step estimator

Ideally, one’s first choice for a one step estimator will be the estimator
\check{\theta}_{n}
suggested by (
2.2
), which we will refer to as the untruncated one-step estimator from now on. However, when
\phi^{\prime}_{0}
is unbounded near the boundary of its domain,
\tilde{\psi}_{n}^{\prime}
is also unbounded on its domain (see Corollary
3
in Section 3.1). In this case,
\tilde{\psi}_{n}
’s behavior can be hard to control in the tails. To avoid these difficulties, we consider a truncated one-step estimator.
Groeneboom and Hendrickx 2018

used a similar idea in a current status linear regression model where a truncated log-likelihood was considered to avoid problems in the tails of the unknown error distribution.

We take the truncation parameter
\eta
to be a small fixed positive number in the interval
(0,1/2)
, and denote by
\xi_{n}
the
(1-\eta)
-th quantile of
\tilde{G}_{n}
, the distribution function of
\tilde{g}_{n}
.
The symmetry of
\tilde{g}_{n}
about
0
implies that
\tilde{G}_{n}^{-1}(\eta)=-\xi_{n}
.

Two potential estimators of the Fisher information are

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{n}^{*}(\eta)=\displaystyle\int_{\bar{\theta}_{n}-\xi_{n}}^{\bar{\theta}_{n}+\xi_{n}}\tilde{\psi}^{\prime}_{n}(x-\bar{\theta}_{n})^{2}d\tilde{G}_{n}(x-\bar{\theta}_{n}),

and

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{n}(\eta)=\displaystyle\int_{\bar{\theta}_{n}-\xi_{n}}^{\bar{\theta}_{n}+\xi_{n}}\tilde{\psi}^{\prime}_{n}(x-\bar{\theta}_{n})^{2}d\mathbb{F}_{n}(x).

(2.10)

Though it can be shown that the above estimators differ by a
o_{p}(1)
term, our simulations indicate that the estimator
\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{n}(\eta)
yields a more efficient one-step estimator. Therefore, we take
\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{n}(\eta)
to be our estimator of the Fisher information.

In the same spirit as the untruncated one-step estimator
\check{\theta}_{n}
in (
2.2
), we now construct a truncated one-step estimator of
\theta_{0}
as follows:

\tilde{\theta}_{n}=\bar{\theta}_{n}-\displaystyle\int_{\bar{\theta}_{n}-\xi_{n}}^{\bar{\theta}_{n}+\xi_{n}}\dfrac{\tilde{\psi}_{n}^{\prime}(x-\bar{\theta}_{n})}{\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{n}(\eta)}d\mathbb{F}_{n}(x).

(2.11)

Note that we use all the data to estimate
\bar{\theta}_{n}
,
\tilde{\psi}^{\prime}_{n}
, and
\tilde{g}_{n}
. The truncation comes only in the step where we set up the one step estimator. Next, observe that
\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{n}(\eta)
is always smaller than
\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{n}
, the estimator of the untruncated Fisher information (see (
2.1
)). In Section
3.1
, we show that the asymptotic variance of
\tilde{\theta}_{n}
has an inverse relation with the Fisher information, indicating that the untruncated estimator in (
2.2
) will be asymptotically more efficient. However, our simulations in Section
4
show that for sufficiently small
\eta
, this loss in efficiency is negligible for most densities. It is also evident that
\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{n}(\eta)
increases in
\eta
, so smaller values of
\eta
will lead to higher asymptotic efficiency. We will discuss about the relation between
\eta
and this asymptotic efficiency in more detail in Section
3.1
.

2.2
Maximum likelihood estimator (MLE)

This section aims to establish the existence and the basic properties of the MLE of
(\theta_{0},g_{0})
. We already mentioned in Section
1
that the MLE of
f_{0}
in
\mathcal{SLC}_{\theta}
exists for a fixed
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
. As we will see, this fact plays a major role in the derivation of the MLE
(\widehat{\theta}_{n},\widehat{g}_{n})
of
(\theta_{0},g_{0})
.

Let
\widehat{\psi}_{n}=\log\widehat{g}_{n}
. Recalling the definition of
\Psi_{n}(\theta,\psi)
from (
1.5
), we observe that
(\widehat{\theta}_{n},\widehat{\psi}_{n})
satisfies

(\widehat{\theta}_{n},\widehat{\psi}_{n})=\argmax_{\theta\in\mathbb{R},\psi\in\mathcal{SC}_{0}}\Psi_{n}(\theta,\psi).

Let us define

\widehat{\psi}_{\theta}:=\argmax_{\psi\in\mathcal{SC}_{0}}\Psi_{n}(\theta,\psi),

which exists for a fixed
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
by Theorem 2.1(c) of
Doss and Wellner 2019
. It is not hard to see that

\widehat{\theta}_{n}=\argmax_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}}\Psi_{n}(\theta,\widehat{\psi}_{\theta})\quad\text{and}\quad\widehat{\psi}_{n}=\widehat{\psi}_{\widehat{\theta}_{n}}.

Note that
\widehat{g}_{n}=e^{\widehat{\psi}_{n}}
is the MLE of
g_{0}
, and
\widehat{f}_{n}=\widehat{g}_{n}(\mathord{\cdot}-\widehat{\theta}_{n})
is the MLE of
f_{0}
. We let
\widehat{G}_{n}
and
\widehat{F}_{n}
denote the distribution functions corresponding to
\widehat{g}_{n}
and
\widehat{f}_{n}
, respectively. Also, we let
\widehat{\phi}_{n}
denote the log-density
\log\widehat{f}_{n}
.

Before getting into the analysis of the existence of
(\widehat{\theta}_{n},\widehat{\psi}_{n})
for a general case, let us consider a special case first. Suppose
\mathbb{F}_{n}
is degenerate, i.e.
\mathbb{F}_{n}\{x_{0}\}=1
for some
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
.
Intuition leads us to make the guess that in this case,
\widehat{\theta}_{n}=x_{0}
. Now for
k\geq 1
, we denote
A_{k}
to be the set
[-1/(2k),1/(2k)]
, and consider
the sequence of functions
\{\psi_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}\in\mathcal{SC}_{0}
defined by

\psi_{k}(x)=\begin{cases}\begin{matrix}\log k,&\quad x\in A_{k}\\
-\infty,&\quad\text{o.w.}\end{matrix}\end{cases}

Observe that

\Psi_{n}(x_{0},\psi_{k})=\log k-1\to\infty,\quad\text{ as }k\to\infty.

Therefore,
x_{0}
indeed is a candidate for the MLE of
\theta_{0}
. However, the MLE of
\psi_{0}
, i.e.
\widehat{\psi}_{n}
, does not exist in this case. To verify, observe that if
\widehat{\psi}_{n}
does exist for some
\widehat{\theta}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
, we also have

\widehat{\psi}_{n}(x_{0}-\widehat{\theta}_{n})-\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{\widehat{\psi}_{n}(x)}dx=\Psi_{n}(x_{0},\widehat{\psi}_{n})\geq\lim_{k\to\infty}\Psi_{n}(x_{0},\psi_{k})=\infty,

leading to

\widehat{\psi}_{n}(x_{0}-\widehat{\theta}_{n})=\infty,

which contradicts the fact that
\widehat{\psi}_{n}
is a proper concave function.
Hence, we conclude that the MLE of
(\theta_{0},\psi_{0})
does not exist when
\mathbb{F}_{n}
is degenerate. So we only need to consider the case when
\mathbb{F}_{n}
is non-degenerate.
In this case, we will show that, the map
\theta\mapsto\Psi_{n}(\theta,\widehat{\psi}_{\theta})
is continuous, and the MLE
(\widehat{\theta}_{n},\widehat{\psi}_{n})
exists.

Lemma 1
.

If
\mathbb{F}_{n}
is non-degenerate,

\theta\mapsto\Psi_{n}(\theta,\widehat{\psi}_{\theta})
is a continuous map.

\Psi_{n}(\theta,\widehat{\psi}_{\theta})

.

Our next theorem establishes the existence of a maximizer of
\Psi_{n}(\theta,\widehat{\psi}_{\theta})
for non-degenerate
\mathbb{F}_{n}
.

Theorem 1
.

When
\mathbb{F}_{n}
is non-degenerate, the MLE
(\widehat{\theta}_{n},\widehat{\psi}_{n})
of
(\theta_{0},\psi_{0})
exists. Moreover,
\widehat{\theta}_{n}\in[X_{(1)},X_{(n)}]
.

Observe that Theorem
1
does not ensure the uniquenesss of
\widehat{\theta}_{n}
. Since
\Psi_{n}(\theta,\psi)
may not be jointly concave in
\theta
and
\psi
, existence of a maximizer does not lead to its uniqueness. For a particular choice of
\widehat{\theta}_{n}
though, the estimator
\widehat{\psi}_{n}=\widehat{\psi}_{\widehat{\theta}_{n}}
is unique by Theorem
2.1(c)
of
Doss and Wellner 2019
. Therefore, if
(\theta,\psi_{1})
and
(\theta,\psi_{2})
both are MLEs of
(\theta_{0},\psi_{0})
, we must have
\psi_{1}=\psi_{2}
.

In all our simulations,
\widehat{\theta}_{n}
turned out to be unique, even when the underlying density
f_{0}
was skewed or non-log-concave. Considering this fact, in what follows, we refer to
\widehat{\theta}_{n}
as “the MLE” instead of “an MLE”. We must remark that even if
\widehat{\theta}_{n}
is not unique, all our theorems still hold for each version of
\widehat{\theta}_{n}
.

Now we discuss some finite-sample properties of
\widehat{\psi}_{n}
. The following theorem sheds some light on the structure of
\widehat{\psi}_{n}
. We find that
\widehat{\psi}_{n}
is piecewise linear, and
\mathcal{K}(\widehat{\psi}_{n})
, i.e. the set of the knots of
\widehat{\psi}_{n}
, is a subset of the dataset. This theorem is a direct consequence of Theorem
2.1(c)
of
Doss and Wellner 2019
.

Theorem 2
.

For
\mathbb{F}_{n}
non-degenerate, the MLE
\widehat{\psi}_{n}
of
\psi_{0}
is piecewise linear with knots belonging to a subset of the set
\{0,\pm|X_{1}-\widehat{\theta}_{n}|,\ldots,\pm|X_{n}-\widehat{\theta}_{n}|\}
. Also, for
x\notin[-|X-\widehat{\theta}_{n}|_{(n)},|X-\widehat{\theta}_{n}|_{(n)}]
, we have
\widehat{\psi}_{n}(x)=-\infty
.
Moreover if
0\notin\{\pm|X_{1}-\widehat{\theta}_{n}|,\ldots,\pm|X_{n}-\widehat{\theta}_{n}|\}
, then
\widehat{\psi}_{n}^{\prime}(0\pm)=0.

There is no closed form for
\widehat{\psi}_{n}
, though
\widehat{\psi}_{n}
can be characterized by a family of inequalities. The following two theorems provide characterizations of
\widehat{\psi}_{n}
.

Theorem 3
.

Suppose
\widehat{\theta}_{n}
is the MLE of
\theta_{0}
. Consider
g=e^{\psi}\in\mathcal{SLC}_{0}
. Then
(\widehat{\theta}_{n},g)
is the MLE of
(\theta_{0},g_{0})
if and only if

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\Delta(x-\widehat{\theta}_{n})d\mathbb{F}_{n}(x)\leq\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\Delta(x)g(x)dx

for all
\Delta:\mathbb{R}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
such that
\psi+t\Delta\in\mathcal{SC}_{0}
for some
t>0.

Note that the MLE in
\mathcal{LC}

(
Dümbgen and Rufibach 2009
)
or
\mathcal{SLC}_{0}

(
Doss and Wellner 2019
)
also satisfy similar characterizations. The proof of Theorem
3
follows from Theorem 2.2(c) of
Doss and Wellner 2019
.

Our next theorem exhibits another characterization of
\widehat{\psi}_{n}
. This theorem also follows as a direct consequence of Theorem 2.4(C) of
Doss and Wellner 2019
.

Theorem 4
.

Suppose
\widehat{\theta}_{n}
is the MLE of
\theta_{0}
. For
g\in\mathcal{SLC}_{0}
, define

{\widehat{G}^{+}}_{n}(x)=2\int_{x}^{|X-\widehat{\theta}_{n}|_{(n)}}g(y)dy,

and

\mathbb{F}_{n}^{+}(x)=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}1\{|X-\widehat{\theta}_{n}|_{(i)}\geq x\}.

Then
(\widehat{\theta}_{n},g)
is the MLE of
(\theta_{0},g_{0})
if and only if

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{t}^{|X-\widehat{\theta}_{n}|_{(n)}}{\widehat{G}^{+}}_{n}(x)dx\begin{cases}\begin{matrix}\leq\displaystyle\int_{t}^{|X-\widehat{\theta}_{n}|_{(n)}}\mathbb{F}_{n}^{+}(x)dx,&\text{ if }t\in[0,|X-\widehat{\theta}_{n}|_{(n)}],\\
=\displaystyle\int_{t}^{|X-\widehat{\theta}_{n}|_{(n)}}\mathbb{F}_{n}^{+}(x)dx,&\text{ if }t\in\mathcal{K}(\psi)\cap[0,|X-\widehat{\theta}_{n}|_{(n)}],\end{matrix}\end{cases}

(2.12)

where
\psi=\log g
.

The implication of Theorem
4
is that the function

h_{g}(t)=\int_{t}^{|X-\widehat{\theta}_{n}|_{(n)}}[\mathbb{F}_{n}^{+}(x)-{\widehat{G}^{+}}_{n}(x)]dx,\quad t\geq 0

(2.13)

is non-negative for
g=\widehat{g}_{n}
.

displays the plot of

constructed from a standard Gaussian sample of size
50
.

The characterization theorems stated above lead to some interesting facts about the distribution function
\widehat{G}_{n}
of
\widehat{g}_{n}
. In fact, we show that
\widehat{G}_{n}
has a close connection with the empirical distribution function of the
|X_{i}-\widehat{\theta}_{n}|
’s.

Corollary 1
.

Suppose
(\widehat{\theta}_{n},\widehat{\psi}_{n})
is the MLE of
(\theta_{0},\psi_{0}).
Let us denote the empirical distribution function of the
|X_{i}-\widehat{\theta}_{n}|
’s by
\mathbb{F}_{n,|X-\widehat{\theta}_{n}|}
. Then the following holds almost surely on
\mathcal{K}(\widehat{\psi}_{n})\cap(0,\infty):

\mathbb{F}_{n,|X-\widehat{\theta}_{n}|}-n^{-1}\leq 2\widehat{G}_{n}-1\leq\mathbb{F}_{n,|X-\widehat{\theta}_{n}|}.

The function
2\widehat{G}_{n}-1
can be interpreted as the distribution function of the random variable
|Y|
, where
Y\sim\widehat{G}_{n}
.
Corollary
1
implies that at the knots,
\mathbb{F}_{n,|X-\widehat{\theta}_{n}|}
and
2\widehat{G}_{n}-1
differ at most by
1/n
. The proof of Corollary
1
follows from The proof of Corollary 2.7(C) of
Doss and Wellner 2019
.

Our next corollary provides an upper bound for the second central moment of
\widehat{G}_{n}
. This corollary is analogous to Corollary 2.8(C) of
Doss and Wellner 2019
.

Corollary 2
.

Suppose
(\widehat{\theta}_{n},\widehat{\psi}_{n})
is the MLE of
(\theta_{0},\psi_{0}).
Then

\text{Var}(\widehat{G}_{n})=\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x^{2}d\widehat{G}_{n}(x)\leq\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}(x-\widehat{\theta}_{n})^{2}d\mathbb{F}_{n}(x),

where
\widehat{G}_{n}
is the distribution function corresponding to the density
\widehat{g}_{n}=e^{\widehat{\psi}_{n}}
.

Since
\int xd\widehat{G}_{n}(x)=0
, the proof follows by taking
\Delta=-x^{2}
in Theorem
3
.

3
Asymptotic properties

We now move on to a discussion of the asymptotic properties of the estimators proposed in sections
2.1
and
2.2
.
Before proceeding any further, we introduce some new notations.
For any real valued function
h:\mathbb{R}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
, we let
||h||_{p}
denote its
L_{p}
norm, i.e.

||h||_{p}=\bigg(\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|h(x)|^{p}dx\bigg)^{1/p},\quad p\geq 1.

We define the
Wasserstein distance between two measures
\mu
and
\nu
on
\mathbb{R}
by

d_{W}(\mu,\nu)=\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|F(x)-G(x)|dx,

(3.1)

where
F
and
G
are the distribution functions corresponding to
\mu
and
\nu
respectively.
This representation of
d_{W}(\mu,\nu)
follows from
Villani 2003
, page
75
. By an abuse of notation, sometime we will denote the above distance by
d_{W}(F,G)
as well. For a sequence of distribution functions
\{F_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
, we say
F_{n}
converges weakly to
F
, and write
F_{n}\to_{d}F
, if for all bounded continuous functions
h:\mathbb{R}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
, we have
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\int hdF_{n}=\int hdF
.

3.1
Asymptotic properties of the one step estimators

In this section, we will explore the asymptotic properties of the truncated one-step estimator
\tilde{\theta}_{n}
. First, we will establish
\sqrt{n}
-consistency of this estimator when
f_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{0}
. Then we will study the case when
f_{0}
is not log-concave. In this section, we keep following the notations developed in Section
2.1
. Thus,
\tilde{g}_{n}
denotes any of the density estimators of
g_{0}
developed in Section
2.1
.

3.1.1
Correctly specified model

We have already mentioned in Section
2.1
that, since
\tilde{\theta}_{n}
depends on the nuisance score, its performance relies on how well
\tilde{g}_{n}
estimates
g_{0}
.
Therefore, before going into the analysis of the asymptotic efficiency of
\tilde{\theta}_{n}
, first we have to show that
\tilde{g}_{n}
is a reasonably good estimator of
g_{0}
.
Towards that end, first we establish that
\tilde{g}_{n}
is
L_{1}
consistent for
g_{0}
.

Lemma 2
.

Suppose
f_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{0}
. Let
\tilde{g}_{n}
be one of the estimators of
g_{0}
defined in Section
2.1
. Suppose the preliminary estimator
\bar{\theta}_{n}
is a consistent estimator of
\theta_{0}
. Then it follows that

||\tilde{g}_{n}-g_{0}||_{1}\to_{p}0.

(3.2)

If
\bar{\theta}_{n}
is a strongly consistent estimator of
\theta_{0}
, then
``\to_{p}"
can be replaced by
``\to_{a.s.}"
in the above display.

Proving Lemma
2
is a key step for us because it leads to further consistency results involving
\tilde{\psi}_{n}
,
\tilde{\psi}_{n}^{\prime}
, etc.
In particular, we find that
\tilde{\psi}_{n}
converges uniformly to
\psi_{0}
over all compact subsets of
\text{int}(\mathrm{dom}(\psi_{0}))
with probability one.
We can derive a very similar result concerning the convergence of
\tilde{\psi}_{n}^{\prime}(x)
to
\psi_{0}^{\prime}(x)
except that at the derivative level, the convergence can only be proved at the continuity points of
\psi_{0}^{\prime}.

The above discussion can be formulated in the following consistency theorem.

Theorem 5
.

Assume
f_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{0}
. Suppose
\tilde{g}_{n}
is one of the estimators of
g_{0}
defined in Section
2.1
, and
\bar{\theta}_{n}
is a consistent estimator of
\theta_{0}
. Let
\{y_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
be any sequence of random variables converging to
0
in probability. Then on any compact set
K\subset\text{int}(\mathrm{dom}(\psi_{0}))
we have,

(A)

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|\tilde{g}_{n}(x+y_{n})-g_{0}(x)|\to_{p}0.

(B)

\sup_{x\in K}|\tilde{\psi}_{n}(x+y_{n})-\psi_{0}(x)|\to_{p}0.

(C)

Suppose
x\in\text{int}(\mathrm{dom}(\psi_{0}))
is a continuity point of
\psi_{0}^{\prime}.
Then

\tilde{\psi}_{n}^{\prime}(x+y_{n})\to_{p}\psi_{0}^{\prime}(x).

If
\bar{\theta}_{n}
is a strongly consistent estimator of
\theta_{0}
, then
``\to_{p}"
can be replaced by
``\to_{a.s.}"
in the above displays.

Now we present a corollary to Theorem
5
. We show that if
\psi_{0}
is unbounded,
\tilde{\psi}_{n}^{\prime}
imitates
\psi_{0}^{\prime}.
However, we do not know whether the sequence of concave functions
\tilde{\psi}_{n}^{\prime}
stays uniformly bounded inside the domain when
\psi_{0}^{\prime}
is bounded.

Corollary 3
.

Suppose
f_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{0}
. Then under the assumptions of Theorem
5
, if
\psi_{0}
satisfies

\sup_{x\in\text{int}(\mathrm{dom}(\psi_{0}))}|\psi_{0}^{\prime}(x)|=\infty,

we have

\limsup_{n}\sup_{x\in\text{int}(\mathrm{dom}(\tilde{\psi}_{n}))}|\tilde{\psi}_{n}^{\prime}(x)|=\infty\quad a.s.

Our next step is analyzing the asymptotic limit of the truncated Fisher information estimator
\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{n}(\eta)
.
It turns out that
\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{n}(\eta)
consistently estimates

\mathcal{I}_{f_{0}}(\eta)=\displaystyle\int_{F_{0}^{-1}(\eta)}^{F_{0}^{-1}(1-\eta)}\phi_{0}^{\prime}(x)^{2}dF_{0}(x),

(3.3)

which can be seen as a truncated version of the original Fisher information
\mathcal{I}_{f_{0}}
.
The convergence of
\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{n}(\eta)
, combined with the consistency results in Lemma
2
and Theorem
5
, leads to the desired consistency of
\tilde{\theta}_{n}
.

Lemma 3
.

Under the conditions of Theorem
5
, we have
\widehat{\theta}_{n}\to_{p}\theta_{0}
and
\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{n}(\eta)\to_{p}\mathcal{I}_{f_{0}}(\eta)
, where
\mathcal{I}_{f_{0}}(\eta)
was defined in (
3.3
).
If
\bar{\theta}_{n}
is a strongly consistent estimator of
\theta_{0}
, then
\widehat{\theta}_{n}\to_{a.s.}\theta_{0}
and
\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{n}(\eta)\to_{a.s.}\mathcal{I}_{f_{0}}(\eta)
.

Now we focus on the rate of convergence of
\tilde{\theta}_{n}-\theta_{0}.
In this case, we make the assumption that
\bar{\theta}_{n}
is
\sqrt{n}
-consistent. A similar assumption was made by
Stone 1975
.

Theorem 6
.

Suppose
f_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{0}
and
\bar{\theta}_{n}
is a
\sqrt{n}
-consistent estimator of
\theta_{0}
. Then the following assertion holds for the estimator
\tilde{\theta}_{n}
of
\theta_{0}
defined in (
2.11
):

\sqrt{n}(\tilde{\theta}_{n}-\theta_{0})\to_{d}N(0,\mathcal{I}_{f_{0}}(\eta)^{-1}),

(3.4)

where
\mathcal{I}_{f_{0}}(\eta)
is as defined in (
3.3
).

Remark 1
.

Although we do not know the rate of convergence of
\check{\theta}_{n}
for a general
f_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{0}
,
in the special case when
f_{0}
is bounded away from
0
on its support, it can be shown that
\sqrt{n}(\check{\theta}_{n}-\theta_{0})
is asymptotically normal with variance
\mathcal{I}_{f_{0}}^{-1}
.
We conjecture that the same holds for any
f_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{0}
. Our simulation study in Section
4
does not refute this conjecture.

Theorem
7
underscores the importance of

\mathcal{I}_{f_{0}}(\eta)
for us in that it is inversely proportional to the asymptotic efficiency of
\tilde{\theta}_{n}.
Now we describe in greater detail
how
\eta
affects the asymptotic efficiency of
\tilde{\theta}_{n}.

It is natural to expect
\mathcal{I}_{f_{0}}(\eta)
to be close to
\mathcal{I}_{f_{0}}
if
\eta\in(0,1/2)
is small. However, the magnitude of this closeness is likely to be controlled by the underlying density
f_{0}
. Keeping that in mind, we seek to investigate how the ratio
\mathcal{I}_{f_{0}}(\eta)/\mathcal{I}_{f_{0}}
behaves as a function of
\eta
for different choices of
f_{0}.
Our choices of
f_{0}
include the standard normal, Laplace and logistic densities. Note that all these densities are members of
\mathcal{P}_{0}
with the center of symmetry
\theta_{0}=0.
We have plotted them in Figure
3
(a) for convenience.

Another choice for
f_{0}
is the standard symmetrized beta density with parameter
r>2
(see Figure
3
(b)), which we define by

f_{0}(x)\equiv f_{0,r}(x)=\dfrac{\Gamma\bigg((3+r)/2\bigg)}{\sqrt{\pi r}\Gamma(1+r/2)}\bigg(1-\dfrac{x^{2}}{r}\bigg)^{r/2}1_{[-\sqrt{r},\sqrt{r}]}(x),\quad r>0,

(3.5)

where
\Gamma
is the usual Gamma function.
It is straightforward to verify that in this case the score equals

\phi_{0}^{\prime}(x)=\dfrac{-x}{1-x^{2}/r}1_{[-\sqrt{r},\sqrt{r}]}(x).

Some computation shows that
r\leq 2
leads to
\mathcal{I}_{f_{0,r}}=\infty.
However for
r>2
,

\mathcal{I}_{f_{0,r}}=\dfrac{r\Gamma\bigg(\dfrac{r}{2}-1\bigg)\Gamma\bigg(\dfrac{3+r}{2}\bigg)}{2\Gamma\bigg(\dfrac{r}{2}+1\bigg)\Gamma\bigg(\dfrac{1+r}{2}\bigg)}<\infty,

and
f_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{0}
.
Figure
3
(c) displays the plot of

for the symmetrized beta density, which depicts that
\mathcal{I}_{f_{0,r}}
decreases steeply for

. This finding is consistent with
\mathcal{I}_{f_{0}}
being
\infty
when
f_{0}
is the uniform density on
[-1,1].

Now we examine the ratio
\mathcal{I}_{f_{0}}(\eta)/\mathcal{I}_{f_{0}}
, which gives the asymptotic efficiency of the truncated one step estimators in the correctly specified model.

It may not be observable from the plot but we verified that for logistic, normal, and the Laplace distribution, the asymptotic efficiency is greater than

However, Figure

Therefore,

3.1.2
Misspecified model: when
f_{0}
may not be log-concave

The consistency properties of
\tilde{\theta}_{n}
we have discussed so far rely on the log-concavity of
f_{0}
. However, even if
f_{0}
is not log-concave, the parameter
\theta_{0}
is still well-defined as the location parameter as long as
f_{0}\in\mathcal{S}_{\theta_{0}}
. It is natural to ask then, in such a case, can
\tilde{\theta}_{n}
be still consistent? It turns out that the answer is affirmative under some additional conditions. In what follows, we address this question in detail.

Let us assume that
f_{0}\in\mathcal{S}_{\theta_{0}}
. First we will state the additional conditions which are required for the consistency of
\tilde{\theta}_{n}
when
f_{0}\notin\mathcal{P}_{0}
.
First of all, we impose a restriction on
F_{0}
. We require
F_{0}
to be non-degenerate and to possess a finite first moment.

Condition A
.

F_{0}
is a non-degenerate distribution function satisfying

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|x|dF_{0}(x)<\infty.

We will see that this restriction, which also features in
Dümbgen et al. 2011
,
Chen and Samworth 2013
, and
Xu and Samworth 2019
, is needed for the existence of an
L_{1}
-limit of
\tilde{g}_{n}
. The latter is an important step in proving the consistency of
\tilde{\theta}_{n}
. Note that, in the special case when
f_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{0}
, condition A is automatically satisfied
(
Cule and Samworth 2010
, Lemma 1)
. We also require
f_{0}
to be bounded above, which is needed to ensure the pointwise convergence of
\tilde{g}_{n}
to a limit. Note that any member of
\mathcal{P}_{0}
is bounded by Lemma 1 of
Cule and Samworth 2010
.
Thus, we restrict our attention to the model

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{1}=\bigg\{f\in\mathcal{P}\ \bigg|\ f\in\mathcal{S}_{\theta}\text{ for some }\theta\in\mathbb{R},\ \sup_{x}f(x)<\infty,\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|x|f(x)dx<\infty\bigg\}.

(3.6)

Note that
\mathcal{P}_{0}\subsetneq\mathcal{P}_{1}
but
\mathcal{P}_{1}\nsubseteq\mathcal{P}_{s}
, where the latter was considered by
Stone 1975
and
Beran 1974
(see (
1.3
)) because
\mathcal{P}_{1}
allows densities with infinite Fisher information.

The log-concave projection theorem developed in
Dümbgen et al. 2011
,
Xu and Samworth 2019
, and
Chen and Samworth 2013
plays a key role in our analysis.
Therefore, we will provide some background on it. For the time being, we assume that
F
is any distribution function.
we denote by
\tilde{\phi}
the maximizer of the criterion function

\omega(\phi,F)=\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\phi(x)dF(x)-\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{\phi(x)}dx

(3.7)

subject to
\phi\in\mathcal{C}
, the class of concave functions defined in (
1.1
). Theorem
2.7
of
Dümbgen et al. 2011
implies that when
F
satisfies Condition A, a unique maximizer
\tilde{\phi}
exists, and
\tilde{f}=e^{\tilde{\phi}}
integrates to one. Also, in the case when
F
has a density
f
,
\tilde{f}
can be interpreted as the minimizer of the Kullback-Leiber divergence

d_{KL}(f,h)=\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\log\dfrac{f(x)}{h(x)}f(x)dx

over all
h\in\mathcal{LC}
. The distribution function
\tilde{F}
of
\tilde{f}
is regarded as the log-concave projection of
F
onto the space of all distributions with densities in
\mathcal{LC}
.

Now for
f_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{1}
and
\omega
as defined in (
3.7
), we denote

\tilde{\phi}_{0}=\argmax_{\phi\in\mathcal{C}}\omega(\phi,F_{0}),\quad\text{ and }\quad\tilde{\psi}_{0}=\tilde{\phi}_{0}(\mathord{\cdot}+\theta_{0}).

(3.8)

By our earlier arguments,
e^{\tilde{\phi}_{0}}
yields a density; which we denote by
\tilde{f}_{0}
, and write
\tilde{F}_{0}
for the corresponding distribution function. We also set
\tilde{g}_{0}=e^{\tilde{\psi}_{0}}
, and
\tilde{G}_{0}=\tilde{F}_{0}(\mathord{\cdot}+\theta_{0})
.

We are interested in
\tilde{f}_{0}
because under Condition A, the unconstrained log-concave MLE
\widehat{h}_{n}
converges almost surely to
\tilde{f}_{0}
in
L_{1}
. See Theorem
2.15
of
Dümbgen et al. 2011
for a proof. Therefore, from (
2.3
) and (
2.9
), it is not hard to guess that the limit of
\tilde{g}_{n}^{sym}
and
\tilde{g}_{n}^{geo,sym}
will be dependent on
\tilde{f}_{0}.
We will show that for
f_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{1}
, both these density estimators are strongly
L_{1}
consistent for
\tilde{g}_{0}
when
f_{0}
satisfies Condition A.

Next we consider the maximization of the criterion function
\omega(\phi,F)
in
\phi
over the constrained class
\mathcal{SC}_{\theta}
for some fixed
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
.
We denote

\overline{\phi}_{\theta}=\argmax_{\phi\in\mathcal{SC}_{\theta}}\omega(\phi,F),\quad\text{ and }\quad\overline{\psi}_{\theta}=\overline{\phi}_{\theta}(\mathord{\cdot}+\theta).

Observe that (
1.4
) provides an alternative representation of
\overline{\psi}_{\theta}
:

\overline{\psi}_{\theta}=\argmax_{\psi\in\mathcal{SC}_{0}}\Psi(\theta,\psi,F).

Proposition 2(iii) of
Xu and Samworth 2019
entails that under Condition A,
\overline{\phi}_{\theta}
exists, it is unique, and its exponential yields a density. Notice that when
F=\mathbb{F}_{n}
, the density

e^{\overline{\psi}_{\bar{\theta}_{n}}}
equals our partial MLE estimator
\widehat{g}_{\bar{\theta}_{n}}
, which is why we study the constrained projection of
F
as well.

For a general
F
satisfying condition A,
\overline{\phi}_{\theta}
need not equal
\tilde{\phi}
since
\overline{\phi}_{\theta}
is symmetric while
\tilde{\phi}
is not necessarily symmetric. However, there is some connection between these two. Our next lemma underscores this connection.

Lemma 4
.

Suppose
F
satisfies condition A.
Define

F^{sym}_{\theta}(x)=2^{-1}\bigg(F(x)+1-F(2\theta-x)\bigg).

(3.9)

Then it follows that

\argmax_{\phi\in\mathcal{SC}_{\theta}}\omega(\phi,F)=\argmax_{\phi\in\mathcal{C}}\omega(\phi,F^{sym}_{\theta}),

where
\omega
is the criterion function defined in (
3.7
).

Lemma
4
indicates that if
F
has a density in
\mathcal{S}_{\theta}
, we do have
\overline{\phi}_{\theta}=\tilde{\phi}
. From the above, it follows that the projection of
f_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{1}
onto
\mathcal{SLC}_{\theta_{0}}
yields
\tilde{f}_{0}
.

Now let us consider the case of the partial MLE estimator
\widehat{g}_{\bar{\theta}_{n}}
.
It turns out that if additionally
\bar{\theta}_{n}
is consistent,
\widehat{g}_{\bar{\theta}_{n}}
is an
L_{1}
consistent estimator of
\tilde{g}_{0}
, where
\tilde{g}_{0}
denotes the centered density
\tilde{f}_{0}(\mathord{\cdot}+\theta_{0})
.

Indeed for
f_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{1}
and
\bar{\theta}_{n}\to_{p}\theta_{0}
, all the estimators
\tilde{g}_{n}
developed in Section
3.1
are
L_{1}
consistent estimators of
\tilde{g}_{0}
, except the smoothed symmetrized estimator
{(\tilde{g}_{n}^{sym})^{sm}}
. Before describing the limit for this case, we will elaborate a little bit on why this difference occurs.

The different behavior of
{(\tilde{g}_{n}^{sym})^{sm}}
stems from the fact that the smoothing parameter
\widehat{b}_{n}
defined in (
2.4
) does not necessarily decrease to
0
as
n
increases unless
f_{0}
is log-concave. Also, Condition A is not sufficient for
\lim_{n\to\infty}\widehat{b}_{n}
to exist, for which, the finiteness of the second central moment of
f_{0}
is also required
(
Chen and Samworth 2013
)
. Provided

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}z^{2}f_{0}(z)dz<\infty,

(3.10)

It can be shown that

\widehat{b}_{n}^{2}\to_{a.s.}\tilde{b}^{2}=\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}z^{2}(f_{0}(z)-\tilde{f}_{0}(z))dz,

(3.11)

which is non-negative for
f_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{1}

(
Dümbgen et al. 2011
, cf. Remark 2.3 of)
, and
0
for
f_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{0}
. Denote by
\tilde{g}^{sm}_{0}
the convolution of
\tilde{g}_{0}
and a centered Gaussian density with variance
\tilde{b}^{2}
. It turns out that
{\widehat{h}_{n}^{sm}}
and
{(\tilde{g}_{n}^{sym})^{sm}}
are
L_{1}
consistent for
\tilde{g}^{sm}_{0}=\tilde{f}^{sm}_{0}(\mathord{\cdot}-\theta_{0})
.

Lemma
5
, which is a generalization of Lemma
2
, summarizes the above discussion.

Lemma 5
.

Suppose
f_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{1}
, which was defined in (
3.6
). Let
\tilde{g}_{n}
be one of the estimators of
g_{0}
defined in Section
2.1
, and suppose that the preliminary estimator
\bar{\theta}_{n}
is a consistent estimator of
\theta_{0}
. Let
\tilde{g}_{0}=e^{\tilde{\psi}_{0}}
, where
\tilde{\psi}_{0}
was defined in (
3.8
). Then for
\tilde{g}_{n}=\tilde{g}_{n}^{sym}
,
\widehat{g}_{\bar{\theta}_{n}}
, and
\widehat{h}_{n}^{geo,sym}
, it follows that

||\tilde{g}_{n}-\tilde{g}_{0}||_{1}\to_{p}0.

(3.12)

Suppose
f_{0}
additionally satisfies (
3.10
). Then we also have

||{(\tilde{g}_{n}^{sym})^{sm}}-\tilde{g}^{sm}_{0}||_{1}\to_{p}0,

where
\tilde{g}^{sm}_{0}
is the convolution of
\tilde{g}_{0}
and a centered Gaussian density with variance
\tilde{b}^{2}
defined in (
2.4
).
If
\bar{\theta}_{n}
is a strongly consistent estimator of
\theta_{0}
, then “
\to_{p}
” can be replaced by “
\to_{a.s.}
” in the above displays.

We also have an analog of Theorem
5
for
f_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{1}
. (See Theorem
12
in Section
6.1
.)

Now we are in a position to address the convergence of
\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{n}(\eta)
defined in (
2.10
). It turns out that, unlike the case for
f_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{0}
,
\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{n}(\eta)
is no longer a consistent estimator of
\mathcal{I}_{f_{0}}(\eta)
because the asymptotic limit of the former now depends on
\tilde{\phi}_{0}^{\prime}
. Let us denote
\xi_{0}=\tilde{G}_{0}^{-1}(\eta).
We find that, for
\tilde{g}_{n}\neq{(\tilde{g}_{n}^{sym})^{sm}}
,
\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{n}(\eta)
consistently estimates
\tilde{\mathcal{I}}_{f_{0}}(\eta)
, where

\tilde{\mathcal{I}}_{f_{0}}(\eta)=\displaystyle\int_{\tilde{F}_{0}^{-1}(\eta)}^{\tilde{F}_{0}^{-1}(1-\eta)}\tilde{\phi}_{0}^{\prime}(x)^{2}f_{0}(x)dx=\displaystyle\int_{-\xi_{0}}^{\xi_{0}}\tilde{\psi}_{0}^{\prime}(x)^{2}g_{0}(z)dz.

(3.13)

\tilde{\mathcal{I}}_{f_{0}}(\eta)
can be thought of as a truncated Fisher information under model miss-specification. When
\tilde{\psi}_{0}=\psi_{0}
,
\tilde{\mathcal{I}}_{f_{0}}(\eta)
equals
\mathcal{I}_{f_{0}}(\eta)
.

Now suppose
\tilde{g}_{n}={(\tilde{g}_{n}^{sym})^{sm}}
. In this case, Lemma
5
says that
\tilde{g}_{n}
consistently estimates
\tilde{g}^{sm}_{0}
in
L_{1}
metric. Letting
\tilde{f}^{sm}_{0}=\tilde{g}^{sm}_{0}(\cdot-\bar{\theta}_{n})
, we denote the distribution function corresponding to
\tilde{g}^{sm}_{0}
and
\tilde{f}^{sm}_{0}
by
\tilde{G}_{0}^{sm}
and
\tilde{F}_{0}^{sm}
, respectively. We also denote the corresponding log-densities
\log\tilde{g}^{sm}_{0}
and
\log\tilde{f}^{sm}_{0}
by
\tilde{\psi}^{sm}_{0}
and
\tilde{\phi}_{0}^{sm}
. It follows that
\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{n}(\eta)
converges in probability to

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{I}}_{f_{0}}^{sm}(\eta)\ =

\displaystyle\ \displaystyle\int_{(\tilde{F}_{0}^{sm})^{-1}(\eta)}^{(\tilde{F}_{0}^{sm})^{-1}(1-\eta)}(\tilde{\phi}_{0}^{sm})^{\prime}(x)^{2}f_{0}(x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \displaystyle\int_{-\xi^{sm}_{0}}^{\xi^{sm}_{0}}(\tilde{\psi}^{sm}_{0})^{\prime}(z)^{2}g_{0}(z)dz,

(3.14)

where
\xi^{sm}_{0}
is the
(1-\eta)
-th quantile of
\tilde{G}_{0}^{sm}.

Even though the limits of
\tilde{g}_{n}
and
\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{n}(\eta)
deviate from
g_{0}
and
\mathcal{I}_{f_{0}}(\eta)
in the misspecified case, it turns out that
\tilde{\theta}_{n}
is still consistent for
\theta_{0}
, provided
f_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{1}
. This establishes that
\tilde{\theta}_{n}
is robust to the violation of log-concavity of
f_{0}
as long as
f_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{1}
. Our next lemma formalizes the above discussion.

Lemma 6
.

Under the conditions of Lemma
5
, for
\tilde{g}_{n}=\tilde{g}_{n}^{sym}
,
\widehat{g}_{\bar{\theta}_{n}}
, and
\tilde{g}_{n}^{geo,sym}
, we have
\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{n}(\eta)\to_{p}\tilde{\mathcal{I}}_{f_{0}}(\eta)
, which was defined in (
3.13
). Also,
\widehat{\theta}_{n}\to_{p}\theta_{0}
in these cases.

When
\tilde{g}_{n}={(\tilde{g}_{n}^{sym})^{sm}}
, if additionally (
3.10
) holds, we have
\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{n}(\eta)\to_{p}\tilde{\mathcal{I}}_{f_{0}}^{sm}(\eta)

(
defined in (
3.1.2
)
)
, and
\widehat{\theta}_{n}\to_{p}\theta_{0}
. When
\bar{\theta}_{n}
is a strongly consistent estimator of
\theta_{0}
, all the above convergences hold almost surely.

Theorem
6
established the
\sqrt{n}
-consistency of
\tilde{\theta}_{n}
under the
\sqrt{n}
-consistency of
\bar{\theta}_{n}
when
f_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{0}
. Likewise, Theorem
7
provides an asymptotic representation of
\sqrt{n}(\tilde{\theta}_{n}-\theta_{0})
for
f_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{1}
.

Theorem 7
.

Suppose
f_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{1}
is absolutely continuous and

\bar{\theta}_{n}
is a
\sqrt{n}
-consistent estimator of
\theta_{0}
. Let
\tilde{\theta}_{n}
be the truncated one-step estimator defined in (
2.11
) with truncation parameter
\eta\in(0,1/2)
. Suppose
\tilde{g}_{n}=\tilde{g}_{n}^{sym}
,
\widehat{g}_{\bar{\theta}_{n}}
or
\tilde{g}_{n}^{geo,sym}
.
Then under the conditions of Theorem
12
, the following assertion holds:

\displaystyle\sqrt{n}(\tilde{\theta}_{n}-\theta_{0})=\int_{\theta_{0}-\xi_{0}}^{\theta_{0}+\xi_{0}}\dfrac{\tilde{\psi}_{0}^{\prime}(x-\theta_{0})}{\tilde{\mathcal{I}}_{f_{0}}(\eta)}d\mathbb{Z}_{n}(x)+\sqrt{n}(1-\gamma_{\eta})(\bar{\theta}_{n}-\theta_{0})+o_{p}(1),

where
\mathbb{Z}_{n}=\sqrt{n}(\mathbb{F}_{n}-F_{0})
,

\gamma_{\eta}=1-\dfrac{\displaystyle\int_{-\xi_{0}}^{\xi_{0}}\tilde{\psi}_{0}^{\prime}(z)\bigg(\tilde{\psi}_{0}^{\prime}(z)-\psi_{0}^{\prime}(z)\bigg)g_{0}(z)dz}{\tilde{\mathcal{I}}_{f_{0}}(\eta)},

(3.16)

\xi_{0}=\tilde{G}_{0}^{-1}(1-\eta)
, and
\tilde{\mathcal{I}}_{f_{0}}(\eta)
is as defined in (
3.13
).
When
\tilde{g}_{n}={(\tilde{g}_{n}^{sym})^{sm}}
, we have

\displaystyle\sqrt{n}(\tilde{\theta}_{n}-\theta_{0})=\int_{\theta_{0}-\xi_{0}}^{\theta_{0}+\xi_{0}}\dfrac{(\tilde{\psi}^{sm}_{0})^{\prime}(x-\theta_{0})}{\tilde{\mathcal{I}}_{f_{0}}^{sm}(\eta)}d\mathbb{Z}_{n}(x)+\sqrt{n}(1-\gamma^{sm}_{\eta})(\bar{\theta}_{n}-\theta_{0})+o_{p}(1),

where

\gamma^{sm}_{\eta}=1-\dfrac{\displaystyle\int_{-\xi^{sm}_{0}}^{\xi^{sm}_{0}}(\tilde{\psi}^{sm}_{0})^{\prime}(z)\bigg((\tilde{\psi}^{sm}_{0})^{\prime}(z)-\psi_{0}^{\prime}(z)\bigg)g_{0}(z)dz}{\tilde{\mathcal{I}}_{f_{0}}^{sm}(\eta)},

(3.18)

\xi_{0}^{sm}
is the
(1-\eta)
-th quantile of
\tilde{G}_{0}^{sm}
, and
\tilde{\mathcal{I}}_{f_{0}}^{sm}(\eta)
is as defined in (
3.1.2
).

Theorem
7
shows that if
\bar{\theta}_{n}
is an asymptotically linear estimator of
\theta_{0}
, then so is
\tilde{\theta}_{n}
. Moreover, the central limit theorem, (
3.13
), and (
3.1.2
) entail that the first terms in the expansion of
\sqrt{n}(\tilde{\theta}_{n}-\theta_{0})
in (
7
) and (
7
) converge weakly to mean zero Gaussian random variables with variances
\tilde{\mathcal{I}}_{f_{0}}^{-1}
and
(\tilde{\mathcal{I}}_{f_{0}}^{sm})^{-1}
, respectively.
From (
7
) and (
7
), it is also clear that for
f_{0}\notin\mathcal{P}_{0}
, the asymptotic distribution of
\sqrt{n}(\tilde{\theta}_{n}-\theta_{0})
may depend on
\bar{\theta}_{n}
. The coefficient
\gamma_{\eta}
or
\gamma_{\eta}^{sm}
quantifies this dependence.

For
\tilde{g}_{n}\neq{(\tilde{g}_{n}^{sym})^{sm}}
, using Cauchy-Schwartz inequality, we obtain that

\displaystyle|1-\gamma_{\eta}|=

\displaystyle\ \dfrac{\displaystyle\int_{-\xi_{0}}^{\xi_{0}}\tilde{\psi}_{0}^{\prime}(z)(\tilde{\psi}_{0}^{\prime}(z)-\psi_{0}^{\prime}(z))g_{0}(z)dz}{\tilde{\mathcal{I}}_{f_{0}}(\eta)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \dfrac{\bigg(\displaystyle\int_{-\xi_{0}}^{\xi_{0}}\tilde{\psi}_{0}^{\prime}(z)^{2}g_{0}(z)dz\bigg)^{1/2}}{\tilde{\mathcal{I}}_{f_{0}}(\eta)}\bigg(\displaystyle\int_{-\xi_{0}}^{\xi_{0}}(\tilde{\psi}_{0}^{\prime}(z)-\psi_{0}^{\prime}(z))^{2}g_{0}(z)dz\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \bigg({\tilde{\mathcal{I}}_{f_{0}}(\eta)}^{-1}\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}(\tilde{\phi}_{0}^{\prime}(z)-\phi_{0}^{\prime}(z))^{2}p_{0}(z)dz\bigg)^{1/2},

where the last inequality follows from (
3.13
).
The above bound on
|1-\gamma_{\eta}|
hints that the asymptotic dependence of
\sqrt{n}(\tilde{\theta}_{n}-\theta_{0})
on
\bar{\theta}_{n}
may be controlled by the
L_{2}(P_{0})
distance between
\tilde{\phi}_{0}^{\prime}
and
\phi^{\prime}_{0}
. We can draw a similar conclusion for
\gamma_{\eta}^{sm}
replacing
\tilde{\psi}_{0}
by
\tilde{\psi}^{sm}_{0}
.

3.2
Asymptotic properties of the MLE

In this section, we explore the asymptotic properties of the MLE
\widehat{\theta}_{n}
and
\widehat{g}_{n}
. First, we will consider the case when
f_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{0}
, and then we will move on to the discussion of the misspecified case.

3.2.1
Correctly specified model

For
f_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{0}
, Lemma
3
showed that the one-step estimators are consistent, whereas Lemma
2
established the
L_{1}
-consistency of the density estimators
\tilde{g}_{n}
’s established in Section
2.1
. Theorem
8
below shows that the MLEs
\widehat{\theta}_{n}
and
\widehat{g}_{n}
satisfy similar consistency
properties.

Theorem 8
.

Suppose
f_{0}=g_{0}(\mathord{\cdot}-\theta_{0})\in\mathcal{P}_{0}
for some
\theta_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
.
Then as
n\to\infty
,
L(\mathbb{F}_{n})\to_{a.s.}L(F_{0})
, and the following assertions hold:

(a)

\widehat{\theta}_{n}\to_{a.s.}\theta_{0}
.

(b)

\widehat{\psi}_{n}(x)\to_{a.s.}\psi_{0}(x)
, for all
x\in\text{int}(\mathrm{dom}(\psi_{0}))
.

(c)

\limsup_{n}\widehat{\psi}_{n}(x)\leq\psi_{0}(x)
, a.s. for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
.

(d)

\widehat{g}_{n}(x)\to_{a.s.}g_{0}(x)
, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
.

(e)

There exists
\alpha>0
such that for all
\alpha_{1}<\alpha
,

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{\alpha_{1}|x|}|\widehat{g}_{n}(x)-g_{0}(x)|dx\to_{a.s.}0.

Moreover,

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}e^{\alpha_{1}|x|}|\widehat{g}_{n}(x)-g_{0}(x)|\to_{a.s.}0.

Since
g_{0}
is a log-concave density, there exist
\alpha
and
\beta
such that
\psi_{0}(x)\leq-\alpha|x|+\beta
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}

(
Cule and Samworth 2010
, Lemma 1)
. This is the
\alpha
that appears in Theorem
8
.

Our next lemma is an analog of Theorem
12
for one step estimators, and addresses the uniform convergence of
\widehat{\phi}_{n}
over compact subsets of
\text{int}(\mathrm{dom}(\psi_{0}))
and the pointwise convergence of
\widehat{\phi}_{n}^{\prime}
.

Lemma 7
.

Suppose
f_{0}=g_{0}(\mathord{\cdot}-\theta_{0})\in\mathcal{P}_{0}
for some
\theta_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
. Denoting
\phi_{0}=\log f_{0}
, on any compact set
K\subset\mathrm{dom}(\phi_{0})
, we have,

\sup_{x\in K}|\widehat{\phi}_{n}(x)-\phi_{0}(x)|\to_{a.s.}0.

Suppose
\phi_{0}
is differentiable at
x\in K.
Then it also follows that

\widehat{\phi}_{n}^{\prime}(x)\to_{a.s.}\phi_{0}^{\prime}(x),

and

\widehat{f}_{n}^{\prime}(x)\to_{a.s.}f_{0}^{\prime}(x).

Similarly for
\psi_{0}=\log g_{0}
, on any compact set
K\subset\mathrm{dom}(\psi_{0})
, we have,

\sup_{x\in K}|\widehat{\psi}_{n}(x)-\psi_{0}(x)|\to_{a.s.}0.

If
\psi_{0}
is differentiable at
x\in K
, then we also have

\widehat{\psi}_{n}^{\prime}(x)\to_{a.s.}\psi_{0}^{\prime}(x),

and

\widehat{g}_{n}^{\prime}(x)\to_{a.s.}g_{0}^{\prime}(x).

Now we turn our attention to the global convergence of
\widehat{g}_{n}
. Our Theorem
9
establishes that
H(\widehat{f}_{n},f_{0})^{2}
and
H(\widehat{g}_{n},g_{0})^{2}
are
O_{p}(n^{-4/5})
. This rate is standard for a density estimator in log-concave class
\mathcal{LC}
. For instance, the unrestricted log-concave MLE
\widehat{h}_{n}
satisfies
H(\widehat{h}_{n},f_{0})^{2}=O_{p}(n^{-4/5})

(
Doss and Wellner 2016
, Theorem 3.2)
. Moreover, Theorem 1 of
Kim and Samworth 2016
establishes that
n^{-4/5}
is the minimax rate of convergence for density estimation in
\mathcal{LC}
.
Theorem 4.1(c) of
Doss and Wellner 2019
obtains the same rate in connection to the MLE in the subclass
\mathcal{SLC}_{0}
of
\mathcal{LC}
.
Doss and Wellner 2019
conjectured in their Remark 4.2 that
n^{-4/5}
is the minimax rate of convergence for estimation in
\mathcal{SLC}_{0}
as well.
In light of the above, we conjecture that the minimax rate for estimating
f_{0}
in
\mathcal{P}_{0}
is also
n^{-4/5}
.
As a corollary to Theorem
9
, it follows that
||\widehat{g}_{n}-g_{0}||_{1}=O_{p}(n^{-2/5})
. Also, for the MLE of
\theta_{0}
, we can show that
|\widehat{\theta}_{n}-\theta_{0}|
is
O_{p}(n^{-2/5})
.

Theorem 9
.

Suppose
\theta_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
and
f_{0}=g_{0}(\mathord{\cdot}-\theta_{0})
for some
g_{0}\in\mathcal{SLC}_{0}
.
Then the following assertions hold:

(A)

As
n\to\infty
,

H(\widehat{f}_{n},f_{0})\to_{a.s.}0\quad\text{and}\quad H(\widehat{g}_{n},g_{0})\to_{a.s.}0.

Moreover,

H^{2}(\widehat{f}_{n},f)=O_{p}(n^{-4/5}).

(B)

We also have
|\widehat{\theta}_{n}-\theta_{0}|=O_{p}(n^{-2/5})
and

H^{2}(\widehat{g}_{n},g_{0})=O_{p}(n^{-4/5}).

In the special case when
f_{0}
is bounded away from
0
on its support, it can be shown that
\widehat{\theta}_{n}
is
\sqrt{n}
-consistent.
We conjecture that for any
f_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{0}
, the MLE
\widehat{\theta}_{n}
is
\sqrt{n}
-consistent and asymptotically efficient.

3.2.2
Misspecified model: when
f_{0}
may not be log-concave

In Subsection
3.2.1
, we saw that even if
f_{0}\notin\mathcal{P}_{0}
, the one step estimators are still consistent as long as
f_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{1}
.
It turns out that, for
f_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{1}
, not only the one step estimators, but also the MLEs
\widehat{\theta}_{n}
and
\widehat{g}_{n}
are consistent. However, in case of the MLE, it is possible to say more. In particular, if
f_{0}\notin\mathcal{P}_{1}
, it turns out that
\widehat{\theta}_{n}
converges almost surely to some limit under some minimal conditions. Much as in Section
3.1
, we again appeal to the log-concave projection theory to analyze the asymptotic properties. However, in this case we need to consider projection onto the class of distributions with densities in
\mathcal{P}_{0}
, which is not covered by the existing literature. Therefore, we construct a new framework for projection onto the above-mentioned class. The consistency results for
f_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{0}
follow as a special case of the general theory developed in this subsection.

In order to study the log-concave projection in context of the class
\mathcal{P}_{0}
, first we need to introduce some new notation. For any distribution function
F
, let us denote

(\theta^{*}(F),\psi^{*}(F))=\argmax_{\theta\in\mathbb{R},\psi\in\mathcal{SC}_{0}}\Psi(\theta,\psi,F),

(3.19)

where
\Psi
was defined in (
1.4
). This maximization problem is different from those considered in Section
3.1
because in those cases, the maximization was only with respect to
\psi
. We are interested in
\theta^{*}(F)
and
\psi^{*}(F)
because, as we will see, under some mild conditions,
\widehat{\theta}_{n}\to_{a.s.}\theta^{*}(F)
and
||\widehat{g}_{n}-e^{\psi^{*}(F)}||_{1}\to_{a.s.}0
as
n\to\infty
provided that
\theta^{*}(F)
is unique.

Proposition
1
below entails that Condition A is sufficient for the existence of the maximizers.
Following the same argument as in Theorem 2.1(c) of
Doss and Wellner 2019
, one can show that when
\psi^{*}(F)
exists,
g^{*}(F)=e^{\psi^{*}(F)}
is a density. We denote the corresponding distribution function by
G^{*}(F)
. We also denote
\phi^{*}(F)=\psi^{*}(F)(\mathord{\cdot}-\theta^{*}(F))
, and write
f^{*}(F)
for
e^{\phi^{*}(F)}
. The corresponding distribution function will be denoted by
F^{*}(F)
, which can be interpreted as the projection of
F
onto the space of all distribution functions with densities in
\mathcal{SLC}_{\theta^{*}(F)}
.

Proposition 1
.

Suppose
F
satisfies Condition A. Then
L(F)<\infty
, and
\theta^{*}(F)
and
\psi^{*}(F)
exist for
F
, where
\theta^{*}(F)
and
\psi^{*}(F)
were defined in (
3.19
).

Our next lemma provides a representation of
\theta^{*}(F)
and
\psi^{*}(F)
in terms of the unconstrained log-concave projection in (
3.8
). Lemma
8
is a direct consequence of Lemma
4
.

Lemma 8
.

Suppose a distribution function
F
satisfies condition A. Then

\theta^{*}(F)=\argmax_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}}\bigg(\max_{\phi\in\mathcal{C}}\omega(\phi,F^{sym}_{\theta})\bigg),

and

\psi^{*}(F)=\argmax_{\phi\in\mathcal{C}}\omega\bigg(\phi,F^{sym}_{\theta^{*}(F)}\bigg),

where
F^{sym}_{\theta}
is defined in (
3.9
) for any
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
, and
\omega
is the criterion function defined in (
3.7
).

The benefit of the representation of
\theta^{*}(F)
and
\psi^{*}(F)
in terms of the unrestricted log-concave projection lies in the fact that characterizations and tools for computing the latter are available
(cf.
Dümbgen et al. 2011
, Remark 2.3, Theorem 2.7, Remark 2.10, Remark 2.11)
.

Note that neither Proposition
1
nor Lemma
8
provides any information about the uniqueness of
\theta^{*}(F)
and
\psi^{*}(F)
. However, the uniqueness of
\theta^{*}(F)
is critical for the consistency of
\widehat{\theta}_{n}
. Therefore, we only consider those distribution functions which satisfy the following condition.

Condition B
.

The maximizer
\theta^{*}(F)
defined in (
3.19
) is unique for distribution function
F
.

Though Condition B does not say anything about the uniqueness of
\psi^{*}(F)
, from Theorem 2.1(c) of
Doss and Wellner 2019
or Proposition
2
of
Xu and Samworth 2019
, it follows that if
\theta^{*}(F)
is unique, so is
\psi^{*}(F)
. We have not yet discovered whether there exist some characteristics of
F
which enforce the uniqueness condition B.
Interestingly, we have not yet found an example of
F
satisfying condition A but violating Condition B. However, it is easy to find distributions satisfying both Conditions A and B, a trivial example being distribution functions satisfying

F(x)=1-F(2\theta-x)

(3.20)

for some
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
. If
F
has a density in
\mathcal{S}_{\theta}
, (
3.20
) holds.

Lemma 9
.

Suppose a distribution function
F
satisfies (
3.20
) for some
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
, and set
G=F(\mathord{\cdot}+\theta)
. Then condition B is satisfied with

\theta^{*}(F)=\theta,

and

\psi^{*}(F)=\argmax_{\psi\in\mathcal{SC}_{0}}\Psi(0,\psi,G)=\argmax_{\phi\in\mathcal{C}}\omega(\phi,G),

where
\omega
is the criterion function defined in (
3.7
).

As a corollary to Lemma
9
, it follows that the density class
\mathcal{P}_{1}
satisfies Condition B. The following example, which is taken from Example
2.9
of
Dümbgen et al. 2011
, helps in understanding the implications of Lemma
9
.

Example 1
.

Consider a rescaled version of Student’s
t_{2}
distribution, whose density takes the form
f(x)=g(x-\theta)
,
with
g
satisfying

g(x)=2^{-1}(1+x^{2})^{-3/2},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}.

Lemma
9
implies that
\theta^{*}(F)=\theta
. Lemma
9
also indicates that
\psi^{*}(F)
can be found using the unrestricted log-concave projection theorem developed in
Dümbgen et al. 2011
. In fact, using Example
2.9
of
Dümbgen et al. 2011
, we derive that
\psi^{*}(F)(x)=-|x|-\log 2
, which corresponds to the Laplace density.

Example 2
.

This example focuses on some bimodal densities. First, we consider a Gaussian mixture density

f(x)=2^{-1}(\varphi(x-2)+\varphi(x+2)).

(3.21)

Here
\varphi
denotes the standard Gaussian density. Since
f\in\mathcal{S}_{0}
, Lemma
9
implies that
\theta^{*}(F)=0
. As in Example
1
,
\psi^{*}(F)
can be obtained using the log-concave projection illustrated in
Dümbgen et al. 2011
. Remark 2.11 of
Dümbgen et al. 2011
implies that there exists
z>2
such that the projection
f^{*}(F)
equals
f
on
\mathbb{R}\setminus[-z,z]
, and is constant on
[-z,z]
(see Figure
5
). In addition, since
f^{*}(F)\in\mathcal{SLC}_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f^{*}(F)(x)dx=2zf(z)+2\bigg(1-F(z)\bigg),

(3.22)

which implies

1=z\bigg(\varphi(z-2)+\varphi(z+2)\bigg)+2-\Phi(z-2)-\Phi(z+2),

where
\Phi
is the standard Gaussian distribution function. Solving the above equation, we obtain that
z\approx 2.83
.

Similarly, consider the following mixture of Laplace densities:

f(x)=4^{-1}\bigg(e^{-|x-2|}+e^{-|x+2|}\bigg).

(3.23)

Its projection
f^{*}(F)
can also be found by Remark 2.11 of
Dümbgen et al. 2011
which implies that
f^{*}(F)
equals
f
on
\mathbb{R}\setminus[-z,z]
, and is constant on
[-z,z]
, where
z>2
. Using (
3.22
), which holds in this case as well, we find that
z=2.61
. Figure
5
displays the above bimodal densities and their symmetric log-concave projections.

Our next lemma gives some insight into the domain of
\psi^{*}(F).

Lemma 10
.

Suppose Conditions A and B hold for a distribution function
F
. Then
\theta^{*}(F)\in\overline{J(F)}
, where
J(F)=\{0<F<1\}
. Moreover, letting
a
and
b
denote the left and right end-points of the set
J(F)
respectively, we have,

\text{int}(\mathrm{dom}(\psi^{*}(F)))=(-d,d),

where
d=(b-\theta^{*}(F))\wedge(\theta^{*}(F)-a).

Lemma
10
implies that for a general
F_{0}
, the domain of
\psi_{0}
is contained the domain of
\psi^{*}(F_{0})
. In the special case when
\psi_{0}\in\mathcal{S}_{0}
, which is the case for
f_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{1}
, we have
b-\theta^{*}(F)=\theta^{*}(F)-a
, leading to

\text{int}(\mathrm{dom}(\psi^{*}(F_{0})))=\text{int}(\mathrm{dom}(\psi_{0})).

On the other hand, substituting
F=\mathbb{F}_{n}
in Lemma
10
, we obtain that
\widehat{\theta}_{n}\in[X_{(1)},X_{(n)}]
, which was already stated in Theorem
1
.

Now we move on to the consistency properties of the MLE, which is connected with the continuity of the maximized criterion function
F\mapsto L(F)
defined in (
1.6
).
As mentioned earlier in Section
3.1
,
Dümbgen et al. 2011
considered projection of
F
onto the class of distributions with density in
\mathcal{LC}
.

Dümbgen et al. 2011
showed that under some regularity conditions, the maximized criterion function corresponding to their projection operator
is continuous with respect to the Wasserstein distance
d_{W}
.
Xu and Samworth 2019
obtained similar continuity result in context of
\mathcal{SLC}_{0}
, which is a subclass of
\mathcal{LC}
. We are working with a further subclass
\mathcal{P}_{0}
. In light of the above, it is not unnatural to expect the same continuity result to hold in our set-up. Indeed, our Theorem
10
establishes the continuity of the map
F\mapsto L(F)
at an
F
satisfying Conditions A and B. Moreover, the map
F\mapsto\theta^{*}(F)
is also continuous in this case.

Theorem 10
.

Let
\{F_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
be a sequence of distribution functions such that
d_{W}(F_{n},F)\to 0
for some distribution function
F
. Further suppose,
F
and
F_{n}
satisfy Conditions A and B for each
n\geq 1
. Then as
n\to\infty
,

L(F_{n})\to L(F),

(3.24)

and the following assertions hold:

(a)

\lim\limits_{n}\theta^{*}(F_{n})=\theta^{*}(F).

(b)

\lim\limits_{n}\psi^{*}(F_{n})(x)=\psi^{*}(F)(x)
for all
x\in\text{int}(\mathrm{dom}(\psi^{*}(F))).

(c)

\limsup_{n}\psi^{*}(F_{n})(x)\leq\psi^{*}(F)(x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}.

(d)

\lim\limits_{n}g^{*}(F_{n})(x)=g^{*}(F)(x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}.

(e)

There exists
\alpha>0
such that for all
\alpha_{1}<\alpha
,

\lim_{n}\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{\alpha_{1}|x|}|g^{*}(F_{n})(x)-g^{*}(F)(x)|dx=0.

Moreover, if
g^{*}(F)
is continuous on
\mathbb{R}
, then as
n\to\infty
,

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}e^{\alpha_{1}|x|}|g^{*}(F_{n})(x)-g^{*}(F)(x)|\to 0.

Now we are in a situation to address the convergence of
\widehat{\theta}_{n}
and
\widehat{g}_{n}
. Suppose
F_{0}
satisfies Conditions A and B. Then Theorem
11
entails that
\widehat{\theta}_{n}\to_{a.s.}\theta^{*}(F_{0})
and
\widehat{g}_{n}
converges to
e^{\psi^{*}(F_{0})}
almost surely, both pointwise and in
L_{1}
. Theorem
11
follows as a corollary of Theorem
10
. This can be verified easily observing that under Condition A,
d_{W}(\mathbb{F}_{n},F_{0})\to_{a.s.}0

(
Villani 2009
, Theorem
6.9
)
, and
\mathbb{F}_{n}
is non-degenerate with probability
1
for large
n
.

Theorem 11
.

Suppose
F_{0}
satisfies Conditions A and B.
Then as
n\to\infty
,

L(\mathbb{F}_{n})\to_{a.s.}L(F_{0})
, and the following assertions hold:

(a)

\widehat{\theta}_{n}\to_{a.s.}\theta^{*}(F_{0})
.

(b)

\widehat{\psi}_{n}(x)\to_{a.s.}\psi^{*}(F_{0})(x)
, for all
x\in\text{int}(\mathrm{dom}(\psi^{*}(F_{0})))
.

(c)

\limsup_{n}\widehat{\psi}_{n}(x)\leq\psi^{*}(F_{0})(x)
, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
a.s.

(d)

\widehat{g}_{n}(x)\to_{a.s.}g^{*}(F_{0})(x)
, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}.

(e)

There exists
\alpha>0
such that for all
\alpha_{1}<\alpha
,

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{\alpha_{1}|x|}|\widehat{g}_{n}(x)-g^{*}(F_{0})(x)|dx\to_{a.s.}0.

Moreover, if
g^{*}(F_{0})
is continuous on
\mathbb{R}
,

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}e^{\alpha_{1}|x|}|\widehat{g}_{n}(x)-g^{*}(F_{0})(x)|\to_{a.s.}0.

Theorem
11
has some important consequences for
F_{0}
satisfying (
3.20
) with
\theta_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
. Note that
f_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{1}
corresponds to such a distribution.
Lemma
9
implies that Condition B holds for
F_{0}
satisfying (
3.20
) with
\theta_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, which leads to the following corollary.

Corollary 4
.

If
F_{0}
has density
f_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{1}
, all conclusions of Theorem
11
hold with
\theta^{*}(F_{0})=\theta_{0}
and
f^{*}(F_{0})=f_{0}
.

For
f_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{1}
, not only
\widehat{\theta}_{n}
is a consistent estimator of
\theta_{0}
, but also
\widehat{g}_{n}
converges to
g_{0}=f_{0}(\mathord{\cdot}+\theta_{0})
almost surely, both pointwise and in
L_{1}
. Also, note that the results for the correctly specified model

in Theorem

follow directly from Theorem

and Corollary

.

In this section, we study the performance of the one step estimators
\tilde{\theta}_{n}
and the MLE
\widehat{\theta}_{n}
for several log-concave densities symmetric about
0

The general set-up of the simulations is as follows. We generate

5
Discussion

In this article, we revisit the problem of estimating the location parameter
\theta_{0}
in the symmetric location model. We show that the additional assumption of log-concavity can ameliorate the dependence on tuning parameters. In particular, our one-step estimators use only one additional parameter, and the MLE does not rely on any tuning parameter.

We show that the rate of convergence of the MLE
\widehat{\theta}_{n}
is
O_{p}(n^{-2/5})
but we conjecture that it is actually
\sqrt{n}
-consistent, and asymptotically efficient.
In our case, the parameter of interest is bundled with the infinite dimensional nuisance parameter. Obtaining the precise rate of convergence for the MLE in such semiparametric models is typically difficult. For example, in context of the monotone current status regression model, this was noted by
Murphy et al. 1999
, who obtained
n^{-1/3}
rate for the parametric part in this model. Whether the MLE is
\sqrt{n}
-consistent in this model is still unknown
(
Groeneboom and Hendrickx 2018
)
. Bundled parameters also appear in single index models
(
Kuchibhotla et al. 2017
)
, where not much is known about the MLE.

Chen and Samworth 2016
proved the consistency of the joint MLE in shape-constrained additive index model, which is more general than the single index model, but nothing is known about MLE’s rate of convergence.

Although our truncated one step estimator is
\sqrt{n}
-consistent, it is not fully asymptotically efficient. The loss of efficiency can be attributed to the use of a fixed truncation parameter. A similarity can be drawn with
Groeneboom and Hendrickx 2018
, who study a current status regression model. They also use a fixed truncation parameter to construct a
\sqrt{n}
-consistent
z
-estimator, which, similar to our case, is not fully asymptotic efficient. However, our simulations do not exhibit a big difference between the truncated and untruncated one step estimator, where the latter is conjectured to be
\sqrt{n}
-consistent.

Despite having the above limitations, our estimators are easily computable because fast algorithms are available for computing log-concave density estimators (using the R packages “logcondens” and “logcondens.mode”). Moreover, we show that even if the log-concavity assumption fails, all our estimators of
\theta_{0}
are still consistent under very mild conditions. In fact, the one step estimators are still
\sqrt{n}
-consistent. This is a consequence of the impressive stability of log-concave density estimators
(
Dümbgen et al. 2011
;
Xu and Samworth 2019
)
. This demonstrates the usefulness of log-concavity assumption in semiparametric models in facilitating a simplified and robust estimation procedure.

Proof of Corollary

Suppose that

Proof of Lemma
4

Note that each
\phi\in\mathcal{SC}_{\theta}
can be written as
\psi(\mathord{\cdot}-\theta)
for some
\psi\in\mathcal{SC}_{0}
, and by (
3.7
) and (
1.4
), we also have

\omega(\phi,F)=\Psi(\theta,\psi,F).

(6.2)

For
\psi\in\mathcal{SC}_{0}
we calculate,

\displaystyle\Psi(0,\psi,F)=

\displaystyle\ \displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{0}\psi(x)dF(x)+\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi(x)dF(x)-\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{\psi(x)}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ -\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi(-x)dF(-x)+\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi(x)dF(x)-\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{\psi(x)}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi(x)d(F(x)-F(-x))-\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{\psi(x)}dx.

It also follows that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi(x)d(F(x)-F(-x))=\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{0}\psi(x)d(F(x)-F(-x)).

Therefore,

\Psi(0,\psi,F)=2^{-1}\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\psi(x)d(F(x)-F(-x))-\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{\psi(x)}dx,

implying

\displaystyle\Psi(\theta,\psi,F)=

\displaystyle\ 2^{-1}\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\psi(x)d(F(\theta+x)-F(\theta-x))-\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{\psi(x)}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ 2^{-1}\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\psi(z-\theta)d(F(z)-F(2\theta-z))-\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{\psi(z)}dz

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\psi(z-\theta)dF^{sym}_{\theta}(z)-\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{\psi(z)}dz

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \Psi(\theta,\psi,F^{sym}_{\theta}).

Thus (
6.2
) shows that for any
\phi\in\mathcal{SC}_{\theta}
,

\omega(\phi,F)=\omega(\phi,F^{sym}_{\theta})

Therefore,

\displaystyle\argmax_{\phi\in\mathcal{SC}_{\theta}}\omega(\phi,F)=\argmax_{\phi\in\mathcal{SC}_{\theta}}\omega(\phi,F^{sym}_{\theta}),

but the distribution function

F^{sym}_{\theta}(x)=2^{-1}\bigg(F(x)+1-F(2\theta-x)\bigg)

satisfies Condition A and (
3.20
). Hence, applying Lemma
14
, we obtain that

\argmax_{\phi\in\mathcal{SC}_{\theta}}\omega(\phi,F^{sym}_{\theta})=\argmax_{\phi\in\mathcal{C}}\omega(\phi,F^{sym}_{\theta}),

which exists by Condition A, thus completing the proof.

\Box

Proof of Lemma
5

First note that it suffices to prove the current lemma when
\bar{\theta}_{n}\to_{a.s.}\theta_{0}
. Suppose the strong consistency does not hold but
\bar{\theta}_{n}\to_{p}\theta_{0}
. Then given any subsequence of
\{\bar{\theta}_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
, there exists a further subsequence
\{\bar{\theta}_{n_{k}}\}_{k\geq 1}
such that
\bar{\theta}_{n_{k}}\to_{a.s.}\theta_{0}
as
k\to\infty
. Therefore, along this subsequence
\{n_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
, the
L_{1}
distance between
\tilde{g}_{n}
and
\tilde{f}_{0}
or
\tilde{f}_{0}^{sym}
(in case of
\tilde{g}_{n}={(\tilde{g}_{n}^{sym})^{sm}}
) approaches
0
almost surely. Hence, if
||\tilde{g}_{n}-\tilde{g}_{0}||_{1}
or
||\tilde{g}_{n}-\tilde{g}_{0}^{sm}||_{1}
has any convergent subsequence at all, the subsequence converges to
0
almost surely. In that case
Shorack 2000
(Theorem 5.7, page 57) implies that the whole sequence converges in probability to
0
. Therefore, in what follows, we assume that
\bar{\theta}_{n}\to_{a.s.}\theta_{0}
.

For our first estimator
\tilde{g}_{n}^{sym}
, the proof is motivated by Theorem
4
of
Cule and Samworth 2010
, which states that if
\int\log f_{0}(x)dF_{0}(x)<\infty
, and Condition A holds,

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\widehat{h}_{n}(x)-\tilde{f}_{0}(x)|dx\to_{a.s.}0.

(6.3)

Note that
f_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{1}
is bounded, so
\int\log_{+}f_{0}(x)dF_{0}(x)<\infty
trivially holds, where
\log_{+}(y)=\max\{\log y,0\}
.
To prove the
L_{1}
consistency of
\tilde{g}_{n}^{sym}
, we now exploit the symmetry of
\tilde{f}_{0}
and
\widehat{h}_{n}
about
\theta_{0}
and
\bar{\theta}_{n}
respectively. Recalling the definition of
\tilde{g}_{n}^{sym}
from (
2.3
), we calculate that

\displaystyle 2\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\tilde{g}_{n}^{sym}(x)-\tilde{g}_{0}(x)|dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ 2\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\tilde{g}_{n}^{sym}(x)-\tilde{f}_{0}(\theta_{0}+x)|dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\widehat{h}_{n}(\bar{\theta}_{n}+x)-\tilde{f}_{0}(\bar{\theta}_{n}+x)|dx+\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\widehat{h}_{n}(\bar{\theta}_{n}-x)-\tilde{f}_{0}(\bar{\theta}_{n}-x)|dx

\displaystyle\ +\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\tilde{f}_{0}(\bar{\theta}_{n}+x)-\tilde{f}_{0}(\theta_{0}+x)|dx+\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\tilde{f}_{0}(\bar{\theta}_{n}-x)-\tilde{f}_{0}(\theta_{0}-x)|dx,

whose first two terms approach
0
almost surely by (
6.3
). Therefore, we only need to take care of the last two terms of (
6.1
). Since
\tilde{f}_{0}
is log-concave, it is continuous almost everywhere on
\mathbb{R}
with respect to the Lebesgue measure. Therefore,
|\tilde{f}_{0}(\bar{\theta}_{n}+x)-\tilde{f}_{0}(\theta_{0}+x)|\to_{a.s.}0
almost everywhere with respect to the Lebesgue measure. Application of Glick’s Theorem
(
Devroye 1987
, Theorem 2.6)
then ensures that the last two terms of (
6.1
) converge to
0
almost surely as well.

To establish the convergence of
{(\tilde{g}_{n}^{sym})^{sm}}
to
\tilde{g}^{sm}_{0}
in
L_{1}
, we will exploit the representation of
{(\tilde{g}_{n}^{sym})^{sm}}
in terms of
(\widehat{h}_{n})^{sm}
established in (
2.7
).
Since
\tilde{g}^{sm}_{0}
and
{(\tilde{g}_{n}^{sym})^{sm}}
are symmetric about
\theta_{0}
and
\bar{\theta}_{n}
respectively, similar algebra as in the case of
\tilde{g}_{n}=\tilde{g}_{n}^{sym}
leads to

\displaystyle 2\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|{(\tilde{g}_{n}^{sym})^{sm}}(x)-\tilde{g}^{sm}_{0}(x)|dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|{\widehat{h}_{n}^{sm}}(\bar{\theta}_{n}+x)-\tilde{f}^{sm}_{0}(\bar{\theta}_{n}+x)|dx+\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|{\widehat{h}_{n}^{sm}}(\bar{\theta}_{n}-x)-\tilde{f}^{sm}_{0}(\bar{\theta}_{n}-x)|dx

\displaystyle\ +\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\tilde{f}^{sm}_{0}(\bar{\theta}_{n}+x)-\tilde{f}^{sm}_{0}(\theta_{0}+x)|dx

\displaystyle\ +\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\tilde{f}^{sm}_{0}(\bar{\theta}_{n}-x)-\tilde{f}^{sm}_{0}(\theta_{0}-x)|dx.

The first two terms can be handled by using Theorem 1 of
Chen and Samworth 2013
, which implies that when
f_{0}
satisfies (
3.10
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|{\widehat{h}_{n}^{sm}}(x)-\tilde{f}^{sm}_{0}(x)|dx\to_{a.s.}0.

(6.6)

Since
\tilde{f}^{sm}_{0}
is the convolution of a log-concave density and a Gaussian density, it is continuous almost everywhere on
\mathbb{R}
with respect to the Lebesgue measure. Therefore, imitating the proof for the case of
\tilde{g}_{n}=\tilde{g}_{n}^{sym}
, we can show that the last two terms of (
6.1
) are negligible.

To establish the
L_{1}
consistency of the partial MLE estimator
\widehat{g}_{\bar{\theta}_{n}}
, we appeal to the projection theory developed in
Xu and Samworth 2019
. In this case
\widehat{g}_{\bar{\theta}_{n}}
is the density of the projection of
{\mathbb{F}}_{n,X-\bar{\theta}_{n}}
, the empirical distribution function of the
X_{i}-\bar{\theta}_{n}
’s, onto the space of the distribution functions with density in
\mathcal{SLC}_{0}
. By Proposition
6
of
Xu and Samworth 2019
, if

d_{W}({\mathbb{F}}_{n,X-\bar{\theta}_{n}},G_{0})\to_{a.s.}0,

(6.7)

we have
||\widehat{g}_{\bar{\theta}_{n}}-\tilde{g}_{0}||_{1}\to_{a.s.}0

as long as Condition A holds. Hence, we only need to show that
d_{W}({\mathbb{F}}_{n,X-\bar{\theta}_{n}},G_{0})\to_{a.s.}0
, for which, by Theorem
6.9
of
Villani 2009
,
it suffices to show the following. First, we need to show that

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|x|d{\mathbb{F}}_{n,X-\bar{\theta}_{n}}(x)\to_{a.s.}\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|x|dG_{0}(x),

(6.8)

and that
{\mathbb{F}}_{n,X-\bar{\theta}_{n}}
converges to
G_{0}
weakly with probability one.
Since
\bar{\theta}_{n}
is strongly consistent for
\theta_{0}
, and

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|x|d{\mathbb{F}}_{n,X-\bar{\theta}_{n}}(x)=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|x-\bar{\theta}_{n}|d\mathbb{F}_{n}(x),

(6.9)

for any
d>0
, an application of Glivenko-Cantelli Theorem (for example, see Theorem
2.4.1
of
Van der Vaart and Wellner 1996
) yields

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{\bar{\theta}_{n}\in[\theta_{0}-d,\theta+d]}\bigg|\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|x-\bar{\theta}_{n}|d(\mathbb{F}_{n}-F_{0})(x)\bigg|\to_{a.s.}0.

On the other hand, strong consistency of
\bar{\theta}_{n}
implies
|x-\bar{\theta}_{n}|\leq|x-\theta_{0}|+1
with probability one for all sufficiently large
n
, where the latter is integrable. Therefore, the dominated convergence theorem leads to

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|x-\bar{\theta}_{n}|dF_{0}(x)\to_{a.s.}\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|x-\theta_{0}|dF_{0}(x)=\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|x|dG_{0}(x),

which proves (
6.8
).

Our next step is to prove the weak convergence of
{\mathbb{F}}_{n,X-\bar{\theta}_{n}}
to
G_{0}
. To this end, we note that

{\mathbb{F}}_{n,X-\bar{\theta}_{n}}(x)=\mathbb{F}_{n}(x+\bar{\theta}_{n})=\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}1_{(-\infty,x+\bar{\theta}_{n}]}(z)d\mathbb{F}_{n}(z),

(6.10)

which converges almost surely to

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}1_{(-\infty,x+\theta_{0}]}(z)dF_{0}(z)=G_{0}(x)

by an application of basic Glivenko-Cantelli Theorem (see Theorem
2.4.1
of
Van der Vaart and Wellner 1996
), and the fact that
F_{0}(x+\bar{\theta}_{n})\to_{a.s.}F_{0}(x+\theta_{0})
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
. This completes the proof of strong
L_{1}
consistency of
\widehat{g}_{\bar{\theta}_{n}}
for
\tilde{g}_{0}
.

Finally, we consider the geometric mean estimator
\widehat{f}_{n}^{geo,sym}
.
Recall that in the course of showing the consistency of
\tilde{g}_{n}^{sym}
, we have shown that the right hand side of (
6.1
) converges to
0
almost surely, from which one can derive that

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\widehat{h}_{n}(\bar{\theta}_{n}\pm x)-\tilde{g}_{0}(x)|dx\to_{a.s.}0.

(6.11)

Proposition
2
(b) of
Cule and Samworth 2010
shows that (
6.11
) leads to almost sure convergence of

\widehat{h}_{n}(\bar{\theta}_{n}\pm x)
to
\tilde{g}_{0}(x)
almost everywhere on
\mathbb{R}
with respect to the Lebesgue measure. As a consequence, it follows that

\widehat{f}_{n}^{geo,sym}(x)/C^{geo}_{n}\to_{a.s.}\bigg(\tilde{g}_{0}(x)\tilde{g}_{0}(-x)\bigg)^{1/2}=\tilde{g}_{0}(x)\quad a.e.\ \ x.

Recall from (
2.9
) that

C^{geo}_{n}=\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\sqrt{\widehat{h}_{n}(\bar{\theta}_{n}+x)\widehat{h}_{n}(\bar{\theta}_{n}-x)}dx.

From Scheffé’s Lemma it follows that
C^{geo}_{n}\to_{a.s.}\int d\tilde{G}_{0}=1
.
We have thus established that
\widehat{f}_{n}^{geo,sym}
converges pointwise to
\tilde{g}_{0}(x)
almost surely. The desired
L_{1}
consistency then follows from Proposition
2
(c) of
Cule and Samworth 2010
.
\Box

Theorem
12

Theorem 12
.

Assume
f_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{1}
. Suppose
\tilde{g}_{n}
is one of the estimators of
g_{0}
defined in Section
2.1
, where
\bar{\theta}_{n}
is a consistent estimator of
\theta_{0}
. Let
\{y_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
be any sequence of random variables converging to
0
in probability. Then for
\tilde{g}_{n}=\tilde{g}_{n}^{sym}
,
\widehat{g}_{\bar{\theta}_{n}}
,
\tilde{g}_{n}^{geo,sym}
, or
\widehat{h}_{n}(\bar{\theta}_{n}\pm\mathord{\cdot})
, on any compact set
K\subset\text{int}(\text{supp}(g_{0}))
we have,

(A)

\sup_{x\in K}|\tilde{g}_{n}(x+y_{n})-\tilde{g}_{0}(x)|\to_{p}0.

(B)

\sup_{x\in K}|\tilde{\psi}_{n}(x+y_{n})-\tilde{\psi}_{0}(x)|\to_{p}0.

(C)

Suppose
x\in\text{int}(\mathrm{dom}(\tilde{\psi}_{0}))
is a continuity point of
\tilde{\psi}_{0}^{\prime}.
Then

\tilde{\psi}_{n}^{\prime}(x+y_{n})\to_{p}\tilde{\psi}_{0}^{\prime}(x).

Suppose
f_{0}
also satisfies (
3.10
).
Then for
\tilde{g}_{n}={(\tilde{g}_{n}^{sym})^{sm}}
or
{\widehat{h}_{n}^{sm}}(\bar{\theta}_{n}\pm\mathord{\cdot})
, (A)-(C) hold for any compact set
K\subset\mathbb{R}
, with
\tilde{g}_{0}
and
\tilde{\psi}_{0}
replaced by
\tilde{g}^{sm}_{0}
and
\tilde{\psi}^{sm}_{0}
respectively. If
\bar{\theta}_{n}
is a strongly consistent estimator of
\theta_{0}
, and
y_{n}\to_{a.s.}0
, then the
``\to_{p}"
in the above displays can be replaced by
``\to_{a.s.}"
.

Proof.

As in the proof of Lemma
5
, one can show that it suffices to prove Theorem
12
when
\bar{\theta}_{n}\to_{a.s.}\theta_{0}
, and
y_{n}\to_{a.s.}0
. Hence, in what follows, we assume that
\bar{\theta}_{n}\to_{a.s.}\theta_{0}
, and
y_{n}\to_{a.s.}0
.

First note that by theorem
2.2
of
Dümbgen et al. 2011
,

\text{int}(\mathrm{dom}(\tilde{\psi}_{0}))=\text{int}(\text{supp}(g_{0})).

Also, since
\tilde{g}^{sm}_{0}=e^{\tilde{\psi}^{sm}_{0}}>0
on
\mathbb{R}
, we find that
\text{int}(\mathrm{dom}(\tilde{\psi}^{sm}_{0}))=\mathbb{R}
.
Hence,

K\subset\text{int}(\mathrm{dom}(\tilde{\psi}_{0}))\subset\text{int}(\mathrm{dom}(\tilde{\psi}^{sm}_{0}))=\mathbb{R}.

When
\tilde{g}_{n}
equals
\widehat{g}_{\bar{\theta}_{n}}
,
\tilde{g}_{n}^{geo,sym}
,
\widehat{h}_{n}(\bar{\theta}_{n}\pm\mathord{\cdot})
, or
{\widehat{h}_{n}^{sm}}(\bar{\theta}_{n}\pm\mathord{\cdot})
,
\tilde{g}_{n}
is log-concave, which simplifies the proof of part A and B. We first consider the cases when
\tilde{g}_{n}=\widehat{g}_{\bar{\theta}_{n}}
,
\tilde{g}_{n}^{geo,sym}
or
\widehat{h}_{n}(\bar{\theta}_{n}\pm\mathord{\cdot})
.
Now to prove part A, note that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\tilde{g}_{n}(x+y_{n})-\tilde{g}_{0}(x)|dx\leq

\displaystyle\ \displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\tilde{g}_{n}(x+y_{n})-\tilde{g}_{0}(x+y_{n})|dx

\displaystyle\ +\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\tilde{g}_{0}(x+y_{n})-\tilde{g}_{0}(x)|dx,

whose first term

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\tilde{g}_{n}(x+y_{n})-\tilde{g}_{0}(x+y_{n})|dx=\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\tilde{g}_{n}(x)-\tilde{g}_{0}(x)|dx\to_{a.s.}0,

which follows from either Lemma
5
or (
6.11
).
Also, noting

\tilde{g}_{0}(x+y_{n})\to_{a.s.}\tilde{g}_{0}(x)\quad a.e.\ x.\ \text{Lebesgue},

and applying Glick’s Theorem
(
Devroye 1987
, Theorem 2.6)
, we derive that

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\tilde{g}_{0}(x+y_{n})-\tilde{g}_{0}(x)|dx\to_{a.s.}0.

Thus we obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\tilde{g}_{n}(x+y_{n})-\tilde{g}_{0}(x)|dx\to_{a.s.}0,

which combined with Proposition 2(c) of
Cule and Samworth 2010
yields that

\tilde{g}_{n}(x+y_{n})\to_{a.s.}\tilde{g}_{0}(x)\quad a.e.\ x

with respect to Lebesgue measure. As a consequence,

\tilde{\psi}_{n}(x+y_{n})=\log(\hn(x+y_n))\to_{a.s.}\tilde{\psi}_{0}(x)\quad a.e.\ x.\text{ Lebesgue}.

Since
\tilde{\psi}_{n}
is concave for our choices of
\tilde{g}_{n}
, Theorem 10.8 of
Rockafellar 1970
indicates that
pointwise convergence on
\mathbb{R}
translates to uniform convergence on all compact sets inside
\text{int}(\mathrm{dom}(\tilde{\psi}_{0}))
, leading to

\sup_{x\in K}|\tilde{\psi}_{n}(x+y_{n})-\tilde{\psi}_{0}(x)|\to_{a.s.}0.

As a corollary,

\sup_{x\in K}|\tilde{g}_{n}(x+y_{n})-\tilde{g}_{0}(x)|\to_{a.s.}0.

Thus we have established part A and part B of Theorem
12
for
\tilde{g}_{n}=\widehat{g}_{\bar{\theta}_{n}}
,
\tilde{g}_{n}^{geo,sym}
and
\widehat{h}_{n}(\bar{\theta}_{n}\pm\mathord{\cdot})
.
Now consider the case when
\tilde{g}_{n}={\widehat{h}_{n}^{sm}}(\bar{\theta}_{n}\pm x)
. Note that (
6.6
) and continuity of
\tilde{g}^{sm}_{0}
imply that

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|{\widehat{h}_{n}^{sm}}(\bar{\theta}_{n}\pm x)-\tilde{g}^{sm}_{0}(x)|dx\to_{a.s.}0.

Therefore, following the same arguments as in the case of
\tilde{g}_{n}=\widehat{g}_{\bar{\theta}_{n}}
,
\tilde{g}_{n}^{geo,sym}
and
\widehat{h}_{n}(\bar{\theta}_{n}\pm\mathord{\cdot})
, we can prove part A and part B for
\tilde{g}_{n}={\widehat{h}_{n}^{sm}}(\bar{\theta}_{n}\pm\mathord{\cdot})
.

Now we focus on proving part A when
\tilde{g}_{n}
is the mixture density
\tilde{g}_{n}^{sym}
. From the definition of
\tilde{g}_{n}^{sym}
in (
2.5
) we obtain that

\displaystyle 2\sup_{x\in K}|\tilde{g}_{n}^{sym}(x+y_{n})-\tilde{g}_{0}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \sup_{x\in K}|\widehat{h}_{n}(\bar{\theta}_{n}+x+y_{n})-\tilde{g}_{0}(x)|+\sup_{x\in K}|\widehat{h}_{n}(\bar{\theta}_{n}-x+y_{n})-\tilde{g}_{0}(x)|.

Since we have already proved part A for
\widehat{h}_{n}(\bar{\theta}_{n}\pm\mathord{\cdot})
, it is not hard to see that both terms on the right hand side of the above display converge to
0
almost surely, which proves part A for
\tilde{g}_{n}^{sym}
. Part A follows for
{(\tilde{g}_{n}^{sym})^{sm}}
in a similar way noting that (
2.7
) implies

\displaystyle 2\sup_{x\in K}|{(\tilde{g}_{n}^{sym})^{sm}}(x+y_{n})-\tilde{g}^{sm}_{0}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \sup_{x\in K}|\widehat{h}_{n}^{sm}(\bar{\theta}_{n}+x+y_{n})-\tilde{g}^{sm}_{0}(x)|+\sup_{x\in K}|\widehat{h}_{n}^{sm}(\bar{\theta}_{n}-x+y_{n})-\tilde{g}^{sm}_{0}(x)|.

Now we prove part B of Theorem
12
when
\tilde{g}_{n}=\tilde{g}_{n}^{sym}
.
To this end, note that we can write

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{x\in K}(\tilde{\psi}_{n}^{sym}(x+y_{n})-\tilde{\psi}_{0}(x))=

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{x\in K}\log\bigg(\dfrac{\tilde{g}_{n}^{sym}(x+y_{n})-\tilde{g}_{0}(x)}{\tilde{g}_{0}(x)}+1\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \sup\limits_{x\in K}\dfrac{\tilde{g}_{n}^{sym}(x+y_{n})-\tilde{g}_{0}(x)}{\tilde{g}_{0}(x)},

because
\log(1+x)\leq x
for any
x>-1
.
Similarly we can show that

\sup\limits_{x\in K}(\tilde{\psi}_{0}(x)-\tilde{\psi}_{n}^{sym}(x+y_{n}))\leq\sup\limits_{x\in K}\dfrac{\tilde{g}_{0}(x)-\tilde{g}_{n}^{sym}(x+y_{n})}{\tilde{g}_{n}^{sym}(x+y_{n})},

leading to

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in K}\bigg|\tilde{\psi}_{n}^{sym}(x+y_{n})-\tilde{\psi}_{0}(x)\bigg|\leq\dfrac{\sup_{x\in K}|\tilde{g}_{n}^{sym}(x+y_{n})-\tilde{g}_{0}(x)|}{\min\bigg(\inf\limits_{x\in K}\tilde{g}_{n}^{sym}(x+y_{n}),\inf\limits_{x\in K}\tilde{g}_{0}(x)\bigg)},

whose numerator converges to
0
almost surely by part A of Theorem
12
. Thus, to prove part B for
\tilde{\psi}_{n}^{sym}
, we only need to show that the denominator of the term on the right hand side of last display is bounded away from
0
. To this end, notice that since
\tilde{g}_{0}
is unimodal, and
\tilde{g}_{n}^{sym}
is a convex combination of two unimodal densities, they attain their infimum over
K
at either of the endpoints. By part A, it also follows that as

, the respective minimum values approach
\inf\limits_{K}\tilde{g}_{0}
, which is bounded away from
0
as
K
is a closed subset of
\text{int}(\mathrm{dom}(\tilde{\psi}_{0}))
. Thus part B is proved for
\tilde{\psi}_{n}^{sym}
.

One can prove part B for
{(\tilde{g}_{n}^{sym})^{sm}}
in a similar fashion because
\tilde{g}^{sm}_{0}
is unimodal, and
{(\tilde{g}_{n}^{sym})^{sm}}
is a convex combination of two unimodal densities.

Now we can proceed to prove part C of the Theorem
12
. First, we consider the log-concave density estimators
\widehat{g}_{\bar{\theta}_{n}}
,
\tilde{g}_{n}^{geo,sym}
, and
\widehat{h}_{n}(\bar{\theta}_{n}\pm\mathord{\cdot})
, all of which are strongly
L_{1}
consistent for
\tilde{g}_{0}
. Note that if
\tilde{\psi}_{0}^{\prime}
is continuous at some
x\in\text{int}(\mathrm{dom}(\tilde{\psi}_{0}))
, then there exists a neighborhood
[x-\xi,x+\xi]
where
\tilde{\psi}_{0}
is continuous. It follows that for sufficiently large
n
,
x+y_{n}\in[x-\xi,x+\xi]
with probability
1
. Since
\tilde{\psi}_{n}
is concave, and converges to
\tilde{\psi}_{0}
uniformly over
K
by part B, Lemma
3.10
of
Seijo and Sen 2011
entails that

\sup\limits_{t\in[x-\xi,x+\xi]}|\tilde{\psi}_{n}^{\prime}(t\pm)-\tilde{\psi}_{0}^{\prime}(t)|\to_{a.s.}0,

(6.12)

from which we obtain that

|\tilde{\psi}_{n}^{\prime}(x+y_{n})-\tilde{\psi}_{0}^{\prime}(x+y_{n})|\to_{a.s.}0,

which results in

|\tilde{\psi}_{n}^{\prime}(x+y_{n})-\tilde{\psi}_{0}^{\prime}(x)|\to_{a.s.}0

(6.13)

because
\tilde{\psi}_{0}^{\prime}
is continuous at
x
, thereby proving part C for
\tilde{g}_{n}=\widehat{g}_{\bar{\theta}_{n}}
,
\tilde{g}_{n}^{geo,sym}
, and
\widehat{h}_{n}(\bar{\theta}_{n}\pm\mathord{\cdot})
.
For
\tilde{g}_{n}={\widehat{h}_{n}^{sm}}(\bar{\theta}_{n}\pm x)
, part C follows similarly. Therefore, we skip the proof here.
To prove part C for the non-logconcave estimators,
\tilde{g}_{n}^{sym}
and
{(\tilde{g}_{n}^{sym})^{sm}}
, we have to exploit their connection to
\widehat{h}_{n}
and
\widehat{h}_{n}^{sm}
, respectively.
Beginning with
\tilde{g}_{n}^{sym}
, we observe that

(\tilde{\psi}_{n}^{sym})^{\prime}(x)=\varrho_{n}(x)\bigg((\log\widehat{h}_{n})^{\prime}(\bar{\theta}_{n}+x)\bigg)-(1-\varrho_{n}(x))\bigg((\log\widehat{h}_{n})^{\prime}(\bar{\theta}_{n}-x)\bigg)

(6.14)

where

.
Thus

\displaystyle|(\tilde{\psi}_{n}^{sym})^{\prime}(x+y_{n})-\tilde{\psi}_{0}^{\prime}(x)|\leq\varrho_{n}(x+y_{n})|(\log\widehat{h}_{n})^{\prime}(\bar{\theta}_{n}+x+y_{n})-\tilde{\psi}_{0}^{\prime}(x)|

\displaystyle\ +\ (1-\varrho_{n}(x+y_{n}))|(\log\widehat{h}_{n})^{\prime}(\bar{\theta}_{n}-x-y_{n})-\tilde{\psi}_{0}^{\prime}(-x)|.

Since
\varrho_{n}
is uniformly bounded above by
1
, (
6.13
) (applied on
\log\widehat{h}_{n}(\bar{\theta}_{n}\pm\mathord{\cdot})
) completes the proof of part C for
\tilde{\psi}_{n}^{sym}
. Analogously using (
6.14
), for
\tilde{g}_{n}={(\tilde{g}_{n}^{sym})^{sm}}

((\tilde{\psi}_{n}^{sym})^{sm})^{\prime}(x)=\varrho^{sm}_{n}(x)\bigg((\log\widehat{h}_{n}^{sm})^{\prime}(\bar{\theta}_{n}+x)\bigg)-(1-\varrho^{sm}_{n}(x))\bigg((\log\widehat{h}_{n}^{sm})^{\prime}(\bar{\theta}_{n}-x)\bigg)

(6.15)

for some
\varrho^{sm}_{n}(x)<1
. Therefore, following the same steps as in the case of
\tilde{g}_{n}^{sym}
, we can establish part C for
{(\tilde{g}_{n}^{sym})^{sm}}
.

From Lemma

because the central limit theorem and (

.

To deal with the second term, observe that the indicator function

belongss to

, the class of all indicator functions of the form

), (
6.19
), (

entail that for all sufficiently large

Since Condition B holds, note that if we can show

Proof of Theorem
9

In their proof of Theorem
3.1
,
Pal et al. 2007
show that if a sequence of log-concave functions
\{f_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
(which can be stochastic as well) satisfies

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log f_{n}(X_{i})\geq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log f_{0}(X_{i})\quad a.s.,

(6.53)

we have
H(f_{n},f_{0})\to_{a.s.}0
, provided

P\bigg(\sup_{x}\log f_{n}(x)=o\bigg(\dfrac{\sqrt{n}}{\log n}\bigg)\bigg)=1.

If we take
f_{n}=\widehat{f}_{n}
, we have

\sup_{x}\log\widehat{f}_{n}(x)=\widehat{\phi}_{n}(\widehat{\theta}_{n})=\widehat{\psi}_{n}(0).

Theorem
11
(c) and Corollary
4
entail that

P\bigg(\limsup_{n}\widehat{\psi}_{n}(0)<\infty\bigg)=1.

Also, note that being the MLE of
f_{0}
,
\widehat{f}_{n}
automatically satisfies (
6.53
),
which implies

H(\widehat{f}_{n},f_{0})\to_{a.s.}0.

(6.54)

Since
f_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{0}
is log-concave and continuous, Proposition 2(c) of
Cule and Samworth 2010
and (
6.54
) entail that

\sup\limits_{z\in\mathbb{R}}|\widehat{f}_{n}(z)-f_{0}(z)|\to_{a.s.}0.

(6.55)

Note that

\displaystyle 2H^{2}(\widehat{g}_{n},g_{0})=

\displaystyle\ \displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\bigg(\sqrt{\widehat{g}_{n}(z-\widehat{\theta}_{n})}-\sqrt{g_{0}(z-\widehat{\theta}_{n})}\bigg)^{2}dz

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ 2\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\bigg(\sqrt{\widehat{g}_{n}(z-\widehat{\theta}_{n})}-\sqrt{g_{0}(z-\theta_{0}\vphantom{\widehat{\theta}_{n}})}\bigg)^{2}dz

\displaystyle\ +2\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\bigg(\sqrt{g_{0}(z-\widehat{\theta}_{n})}-\sqrt{g_{0}(z-\theta_{0}\vphantom{\widehat{\theta}_{n}})}\bigg)^{2}dz,

where the first term on the right hand side of the last display approaches
0
almost surely by (
6.54
). The second term is also bounded above by a constant multiple of
g_{0}(z-\widehat{\theta}_{n})+g_{0}(z-\theta_{0})
, which is integrable. Therefore, using Lemma
16
, Theorem
11
(a), and Corollary
4
, we deduce that this term also converge to
0
almost surely. Hence
H^{2}(\widehat{g}_{n},g_{0})\to_{a.s.}0
follows.

Our next step is to find the rate of convergence of
H(\widehat{f}_{n},f_{0})
. To do so, we first introduce the class of functions

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{M,0}=\bigg\{f\in\mathcal{LC}\ \bigg|\

\displaystyle\ \sup\limits_{x\in\mathbb{R}}f(x)<M,\ f(x)>1/M\text{ for all }|x|<1,

\displaystyle\ \text{Supp}(f)\subset\text{Supp}(f_{0})\bigg\}.

We will show that without loss of generality, one can assume that
f_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{M,0}
for some
M>0.
To this end, we translate and rescale the data letting
\tilde{X}_{i}=\alpha X_{i}+\beta
, where
\alpha>0
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. Observe that the rescaled data has density
\tilde{f}_{0}(x)=\alpha^{-1}f_{0}((x-\beta)/\alpha)
. Denote by
\tilde{f}_{0,n}
the MLE of
f_{0}
based on the rescaled data. Note that the MLE is affine-equivalent, which entails that
\tilde{f}_{0,n}(x)=\alpha^{-1}\widehat{f}_{n}((x-\beta)/\alpha).
Noting Hellinger distance is invariant under affine transformations, we observe that
H(\widehat{f}_{n},f_{0})=H(\tilde{f}_{0,n},\tilde{f}_{0}).

Therefore, it suffices to show that
H(\tilde{f}_{0,n},\tilde{f}_{0})\to_{a.s.}0
. Note that since
f_{0}
is log-concave,
\text{int}(\mathrm{dom}(f_{0}))
contains an interval. We can choose
\alpha
and
\beta
in a way such that
(x-\beta)/\alpha
lie inside that interval for
x=\pm 1
. Then it is possible to find
M>0
large enough such that

f_{0}((x-\beta)/\alpha)>\alpha/M,\quad x=\pm 1,

or

\min(\tilde{f}_{0}(-1),\tilde{f}_{0}(1))>1/M,

leading to

\inf_{x\in[-1,1]}\tilde{f}_{0}(x)>1/M,

since
f_{0},
or equivalently
\tilde{f}_{0}
is unimodal. Hence, without loss of generality, we can assume that there exists
M>0
such that
f_{0}(x)>1/M
for
x\in[-1,1].

We can choose
M
large enough such that additionally,
\sup\limits_{x\in\mathbb{R}}f_{0}(x)<M
. On the other hand, (
6.55
) implies that the following inequalities hold with probability one:

\limsup_{n}\sup\limits_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\widehat{f}_{n}(x)<M,

and

\lim_{n}\widehat{f}_{n}(\pm 1)>1/M.

Therefore,
f_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{M,0}
, and with probability one,
\widehat{f}_{n}\in\mathcal{P}_{M,0}
as well for all sufficiently large
n
.
Doss and Wellner 2016
obtained the bracketing entropy of the class
\mathcal{P}_{M,0}
. They showed that for any
\epsilon>0
,

\log N_{[\ ]}(\epsilon,\mathcal{P}_{M,0},H)\lesssim\epsilon^{-1/2}.

The rest of the proof now follows from an application of Theorem
3.4.1
and
3.4.4
of
Van der Vaart and Wellner 1996
.
To this end, fixing
\epsilon>0,
for
f\in\mathcal{P}_{M,0}
we define the function

m_{f}(x)=\log\bigg(\dfrac{f(x)+f_{0}(x)}{2f_{0}(x)}\bigg),

and we let
\mathcal{M}_{\epsilon}
denote the class

\mathcal{M}_{\epsilon}=\{m_{f}-m_{f_{0}}\ :\ H(f,f_{0})\leq\epsilon\}.

Also, we denote

||\mathbb{Z}_{n}||_{\mathcal{M}_{\epsilon}}=\sup_{h\in\mathcal{M}_{\epsilon}}\int hd\mathbb{Z}_{n}.

Then Theorem
3.4.4
of
Van der Vaart and Wellner 1996
yields

P(m_{f}-m_{f_{0}})\lesssim-H^{2}(f,{f_{0}}),

and

E_{P}||\mathbb{Z}_{n}||_{\mathcal{M}_{\epsilon}}\lesssim\tilde{J}_{[\ ]}(\epsilon,\mathcal{P}_{M,0},H)\bigg(1+\dfrac{\tilde{J}_{[\ ]}(\epsilon,\mathcal{P}_{M,0},H)}{\epsilon^{2}\sqrt{n}}\bigg),

where

\displaystyle\tilde{J}_{[\ ]}(\epsilon,\mathcal{P}_{M,0},H)=

\displaystyle\ \displaystyle\int_{0}^{\epsilon}\sqrt{\log N_{[\ ]}(t,\mathcal{P}_{M,0},H)}dt\lesssim\epsilon^{3/4}.

Therefore, it is easy to see that

E_{P}||\mathbb{Z}_{n}||_{\mathcal{M}_{\epsilon}}\lesssim\epsilon^{3/4}+\epsilon^{-1/2}n^{-1/2}=g(\epsilon),

where

g(t)=t^{3/4}+t^{-1/2}n^{-1/2},\quad t>0.

Notice that
g(t)/t^{3/4}
is decreasing in
t.
Also, for any constant
c>0,
we have

n^{4/5}g(cn^{-2/5})=(c^{3/4}+c^{-1/2})n^{1/2}.

Therefore, an application of Theorem
3.4.1
of
Van der Vaart and Wellner 1996
yields

H(\widehat{f}_{n},{f_{0}})=O_{p}(n^{-2/5}),

which completes the proof of part A of Theorem
9
.

Now we turn to the proof of part B. If
x-\theta_{0}
is a continuity point of
g_{0}^{\prime}
, noting
\widehat{\theta}_{n}\to_{a.s.}\theta_{0}

and Corollary

\dfrac{\sqrt{g_{0}(x-\widehat{\theta}_{n})}-\sqrt{\vphantom{\widehat{\theta}_{n}}g_{0}(x-\theta_{0})}}{(\widehat{\theta}_{n}-\theta_{0})}\to_{a.s.}\dfrac{g_{0}^{\prime}(x-\theta_{0})}{2\sqrt{g_{0}(x-\theta_{0})}}.

Noting
g_{0}^{\prime}
is continuous almost everywhere with respect to Lebesgue measure, and using Fatou’s lemma and part A of the current theorem, we obtain that

\displaystyle\liminf_{n}\dfrac{\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\bigg(\sqrt{g_{0}(x-\widehat{\theta}_{n})}-\sqrt{\vphantom{\widehat{\theta}_{n}}g_{0}(x-\theta_{0})}\bigg)^{2}dx}{(\widehat{\theta}_{n}-\theta_{0})^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\ \displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\bigg(\dfrac{g_{0}^{\prime}(x-\theta_{0})}{2\sqrt{g_{0}(x-\theta_{0})}}\bigg)^{2}dx=\dfrac{\mathcal{I}_{f_{0}}}{4}\ a.s.

(6.56)

Now observe that

\displaystyle 2H(\widehat{f}_{n},f_{0})^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\bigg(\sqrt{\widehat{g}_{n}(x-\widehat{\theta}_{n})}-\sqrt{\vphantom{\widehat{g}_{n}}g_{0}(x-\theta_{0})}\bigg)^{2}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\bigg(\sqrt{\widehat{g}_{n}(x-\widehat{\theta}_{n})}-\sqrt{\vphantom{\widehat{g}_{n}}g_{0}(x-\widehat{\theta}_{n})}\bigg)^{2}dx

\displaystyle\ +\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\bigg(\sqrt{g_{0}(x-\widehat{\theta}_{n})}-\sqrt{\vphantom{\widehat{\theta}_{n}}g_{0}(x-\theta_{0})}\bigg)^{2}dx+T_{c},

where

T_{c}=2\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\bigg(\sqrt{\widehat{g}_{n}(x-\widehat{\theta}_{n})}-\sqrt{g_{0}(x-\widehat{\theta}_{n})}\bigg)\bigg(\sqrt{g_{0}(x-\widehat{\theta}_{n})}-\sqrt{\vphantom{\widehat{g}_{n}}g_{0}(x-\theta_{0})}\bigg)dx.

The inequality in (
6.2
) entails that for all sufficiently large
n
,

2H(\widehat{f}_{n},f_{0})^{2}\geq 2H(\widehat{g}_{n},g_{0})^{2}+\dfrac{(\widehat{\theta}_{n}-\theta_{0})^{2}\mathcal{I}_{f_{0}}}{4}-|T_{c}|\quad a.s.

We aim to show that the cross-term
|T_{c}|
is small. In fact, we show that

\dfrac{|T_{c}|}{|\widehat{\theta}_{n}-\theta_{0}|^{2}+H(\widehat{g}_{n},g_{0})^{2}}=o_{p}(1).

(6.57)

Suppose (
6.57
) holds. Then it follows that

\displaystyle 2H(\widehat{f}_{n},f_{0})^{2}\geq

\displaystyle\ 2H(\widehat{g}_{n},g_{0})^{2}

\displaystyle\ +\dfrac{(\widehat{\theta}_{n}-\theta_{0})^{2}\mathcal{I}_{f_{0}}}{4}-o_{p}(1)H(\widehat{g}_{n},g_{0})^{2}-o_{p}(1)(\widehat{\theta}_{n}-\theta_{0})^{2},

which completes the proof because
\mathcal{I}_{f_{0}}>0
.

Hence, it remains to prove (
6.57
). To this end, notice that
T_{c}
can be written as

\displaystyle T_{c}=2\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\bigg(\sqrt{\widehat{g}_{n}(x)}-\sqrt{\vphantom{\widehat{g}_{n}}g_{0}(x)}\bigg)\bigg(\sqrt{\vphantom{\widehat{g}_{n}}g_{0}(x)}-\sqrt{\vphantom{\widehat{g}_{n}}g_{0}(x+\widehat{\theta}_{n}-\theta_{0})}\bigg)dx.

For the sake of simplicity, we will denote
\theta_{0}-\widehat{\theta}_{n}
by
\delta_{n}
from now on.
Since
g_{0}
is absolutely continuous, we can write

\displaystyle|T_{c}|=

\displaystyle\bigg|2\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\bigg(\sqrt{\widehat{g}_{n}(x)}-\sqrt{\vphantom{\widehat{g}_{n}}g_{0}(x)}\bigg)\bigg(\displaystyle\int_{-\delta_{n}}^{0}\dfrac{g_{0}^{\prime}(x+t)}{2\sqrt{g_{0}(x+t)}}dt\bigg)dx\bigg|.

Since
g_{0}\in\mathcal{S}_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle|T_{c}|=

\displaystyle\ 2\bigg|\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\bigg(\sqrt{\widehat{g}_{n}(x)}-\sqrt{\vphantom{\widehat{g}_{n}}g_{0}(x)}\bigg)\bigg(\displaystyle\int_{-\delta_{n}}^{0}\dfrac{g_{0}^{\prime}(x+t)}{2\sqrt{g_{0}(x+t)}}dt\bigg)dx

\displaystyle\ -\ \displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{0}\bigg(\sqrt{\widehat{g}_{n}(-x)}-\sqrt{\vphantom{\widehat{g}_{n}}g_{0}(-x)}\bigg)\bigg(\displaystyle\int_{-\delta_{n}}^{0}\dfrac{g_{0}^{\prime}(-x-t)}{2\sqrt{g_{0}(-x-t)}}dt\bigg)dx\bigg|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ 2\bigg|\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\bigg(\sqrt{\widehat{g}_{n}(x)}-\sqrt{\vphantom{\widehat{g}_{n}}g_{0}(x)}\bigg)\bigg(\displaystyle\int_{-\delta_{n}}^{0}\dfrac{g_{0}^{\prime}(x+t)}{2\sqrt{g_{0}(x+t)}}dt\bigg)dx

\displaystyle\ -\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\bigg(\sqrt{\widehat{g}_{n}(x)}-\sqrt{\vphantom{\widehat{g}_{n}}g_{0}(x)}\bigg)\bigg(\displaystyle\int_{-\delta_{n}}^{0}\dfrac{g_{0}^{\prime}(x-t)}{2\sqrt{g_{0}(x-t)}}dt\bigg)dx\bigg|,

yielding

|T_{c}|=2\bigg|\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\bigg(\sqrt{\widehat{g}_{n}(x)}-\sqrt{\vphantom{\widehat{g}_{n}}g_{0}(x)}\bigg)\bigg(\displaystyle\int_{-\delta_{n}}^{0}\bigg(\dfrac{g_{0}^{\prime}(x+t)}{2\sqrt{g_{0}(x+t)}}-\dfrac{g_{0}^{\prime}(x-t)}{2\sqrt{g_{0}(x-t)}}\bigg)dt\bigg)dx\bigg|.

(6.58)

Using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we obtain that

\displaystyle\dfrac{|T_{c}|}{2|\delta_{n}|}\leq

\displaystyle\ \bigg(\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\bigg(\sqrt{\widehat{g}_{n}(x)}-\sqrt{\vphantom{\widehat{g}_{n}}g_{0}(x)}\bigg)^{2}dx\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\ \bigg(\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\bigg(\displaystyle\int_{-\delta_{n}}^{0}\dfrac{1}{|\delta_{n}|}\bigg(\dfrac{g_{0}^{\prime}(x+t)}{2\sqrt{g_{0}(x+t)}}-\dfrac{g_{0}^{\prime}(x-t)}{2\sqrt{g_{0}(x-t)}}\bigg)dt\bigg)^{2}dx\bigg)^{1/2}.

Since
\sqrt{\widehat{g}_{n}(x)}-\sqrt{\vphantom{\widehat{g}_{n}}g_{0}(x)}
is an even function, the first term on the right hand side of the last inequality is
\sqrt{2}H(\widehat{g}_{n},g_{0}).
Hence,

\displaystyle\dfrac{T_{c}^{2}}{8H(\widehat{g}_{n},g_{0})^{2}\delta_{n}^{2}}\leq

\displaystyle\ \displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\bigg(\displaystyle\int_{-\delta_{n}}^{0}\dfrac{1}{|\delta_{n}|}\bigg(\dfrac{g_{0}^{\prime}(x+t)}{2\sqrt{g_{0}(x+t)}}-\dfrac{g_{0}^{\prime}(x-t)}{2\sqrt{g_{0}(x-t)}}\bigg)dt\bigg)^{2}dx,

which, noting that

t\mapsto\dfrac{g_{0}^{\prime}(x+t)}{2\sqrt{g_{0}(x+t)}}-\dfrac{g_{0}^{\prime}(x-t)}{2\sqrt{g_{0}(x-t)}}

is an even function for each
x>0
, can be bounded above by

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|\delta_{n}|\bigg(\displaystyle\int_{0}^{|\delta_{n}|}\dfrac{1}{(\delta_{n})^{2}}\bigg(\dfrac{g_{0}^{\prime}(x+t)}{2\sqrt{g_{0}(x+t)}}-\dfrac{g_{0}^{\prime}(x-t)}{2\sqrt{g_{0}(x-t)}}\bigg)^{2}dt\bigg)dx

using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality. Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\dfrac{T_{c}^{2}}{2H(\widehat{g}_{n},g_{0})^{2}\delta_{n}^{2}}\leq

\displaystyle\ \dfrac{1}{|\delta_{n}|}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{|\delta_{n}|}\bigg[\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\bigg(\dfrac{g_{0}^{\prime}(x+t)}{\sqrt{g_{0}(x+t)}}\bigg)^{2}dx+\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\bigg(\dfrac{g_{0}^{\prime}(x-t)}{\sqrt{g_{0}(x-t)}}\bigg)^{2}dx

\displaystyle\ -2\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\dfrac{g_{0}^{\prime}(x-t)}{\sqrt{g_{0}(x-t)}}\dfrac{g_{0}^{\prime}(x+t)}{\sqrt{g_{0}(x+t)}}dx\bigg]dt.

(6.59)

For
t\geq 0,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\bigg(\dfrac{g_{0}^{\prime}(x+t)}{\sqrt{g_{0}(x+t)}}\bigg)^{2}dx=\displaystyle\int_{t}^{\infty}\bigg(\dfrac{g_{0}^{\prime}(x)}{\sqrt{g_{0}(x)}}\bigg)^{2}dx\leq\dfrac{\mathcal{I}_{f_{0}}}{2}.

Now observe that for
z\in(-|\delta_{n}|,0),

|g_{0}^{\prime}(z)/\sqrt{g_{0}(z)}|=|\psi_{0}^{\prime}(z)|\sqrt{g_{0}(z)}\leq|\psi_{0}^{\prime}(\delta_{n})|\sqrt{g_{0}(0)}=O_{p}(1),

(6.60)

since
\psi_{0}\in\mathcal{SC}_{0}
, and
\delta_{n}\to_{a.s.}0
.
Hence, for
t\in(0,|\delta_{n}|)
,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\bigg(\dfrac{g_{0}^{\prime}(x-t)}{\sqrt{g_{0}(x-t)}}\bigg)^{2}dx\ =

\displaystyle\ \displaystyle\int_{-t}^{\infty}\bigg(\dfrac{g_{0}^{\prime}(z)}{\sqrt{g_{0}(z)}}\bigg)^{2}dz

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \displaystyle\int_{-t}^{0}\bigg(\dfrac{g_{0}^{\prime}(x)}{\sqrt{g_{0}(z)}}\bigg)^{2}dz+\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\bigg(\dfrac{g_{0}^{\prime}(z)}{\sqrt{g_{0}(z)}}\bigg)^{2}dz

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ |\delta_{n}|\psi_{0}^{\prime}(\delta_{n})^{2}\sqrt{g_{0}(0)}+\mathcal{I}_{f_{0}}/2

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ |\delta_{n}|O_{p}(1)+\mathcal{I}_{f_{0}}/2,

where the last step follows from (
6.60
).
Hence, for any
t\in(0,|\delta_{n}|),

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\bigg(\dfrac{g_{0}^{\prime}(x+t)}{\sqrt{g_{0}(x+t)}}\bigg)^{2}dx+\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\bigg(\dfrac{g_{0}^{\prime}(x-t)}{\sqrt{g_{0}(x-t)}}\bigg)^{2}dx=|\delta_{n}|O_{p}(1)+\mathcal{I}_{f_{0}}.

(6.61)

Our objective is to apply Fatou’s lemma on the third term on the right hand side of (
6.2
). Therefore, we want to ensure that the integrand is non-negative.
Note that when
x\geq|\delta_{n}|
and
t\in(0,|\delta_{n}|)
, we have
x>t
, which leads to

g_{0}^{\prime}(x-t)g_{0}^{\prime}(x+t)\geq 0.

(6.62)

Keeping that in mind, we partition the term

\displaystyle-\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\dfrac{g_{0}^{\prime}(x-t)}{\sqrt{g_{0}(x-t)}}\dfrac{g_{0}^{\prime}(x+t)}{\sqrt{g_{0}(x+t)}}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ -\displaystyle\int_{|\delta_{n}|}^{\infty}\dfrac{g_{0}^{\prime}(x-t)}{\sqrt{g_{0}(x-t)}}\dfrac{g_{0}^{\prime}(x+t)}{\sqrt{g_{0}(x+t)}}dx-\displaystyle\int_{0}^{|\delta_{n}|}\dfrac{g_{0}^{\prime}(x-t)}{\sqrt{g_{0}(x-t)}}\dfrac{g_{0}^{\prime}(x+t)}{\sqrt{g_{0}(x+t)}}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ -\displaystyle\int_{|\delta_{n}|}^{\infty}\dfrac{g_{0}^{\prime}(x-t)}{\sqrt{g_{0}(x-t)}}\dfrac{g_{0}^{\prime}(x+t)}{\sqrt{g_{0}(x+t)}}dx+|\delta_{n}|O_{p}(1),

where the last step follows from (
6.60
).
The above combined with (
6.2
) and (
6.61
) leads to

\displaystyle\limsup_{n}\dfrac{T_{c}^{2}}{2H(\widehat{g}_{n},g_{0})^{2}\delta_{n}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \limsup_{n}\dfrac{1}{|\delta_{n}|}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{|\delta_{n}|}\bigg[|\delta_{n}|O_{p}(1)+\mathcal{I}_{f_{0}}-2\displaystyle\int_{|\delta_{n}|}^{\infty}\dfrac{g_{0}^{\prime}(x-t)}{\sqrt{g_{0}(x-t)}}\dfrac{g_{0}^{\prime}(x+t)}{\sqrt{g_{0}(x+t)}}dx\bigg]dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ O_{p}(1)\limsup_{n}|\delta_{n}|+\mathcal{I}_{f_{0}}

\displaystyle\ -2\liminf_{n}\dfrac{1}{|\delta_{n}|}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{|\delta_{n}|}\displaystyle\int_{|\delta_{n}|}^{\infty}\dfrac{g_{0}^{\prime}(x-t)}{\sqrt{g_{0}(x-t)}}\dfrac{g_{0}^{\prime}(x+t)}{\sqrt{g_{0}(x+t)}}dxdt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ 0+\mathcal{I}_{f_{0}}-2\liminf_{n}\displaystyle\int_{|\delta_{n}|}^{\infty}\dfrac{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{|\delta_{n}|}\dfrac{g_{0}^{\prime}(x+t)}{\sqrt{g_{0}(x+t)}}\dfrac{g_{0}^{\prime}(x-t)}{\sqrt{g_{0}(x-t)}}dt}{|\delta_{n}|}dx.

(6.63)

Therefore, an application of Fatou’s Lemma and (
6.62
) yield

\liminf_{n}\displaystyle\int_{|\delta_{n}|}^{\infty}\dfrac{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{|\delta_{n}|}\dfrac{g_{0}^{\prime}(x+t)}{\sqrt{g_{0}(x+t)}}\dfrac{g_{0}^{\prime}(x-t)}{\sqrt{g_{0}(x-t)}}dt}{|\delta_{n}|}dx\geq\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\dfrac{g_{0}^{\prime}(x)^{2}}{g_{0}(x)}dx=\dfrac{\mathcal{I}_{f_{0}}}{2}.

Thus (
6.2
) leads to

\dfrac{2T_{c}^{2}}{4H(\widehat{g}_{n},g_{0})^{2}\delta_{n}^{2}}=o_{p}(1).

from which it is obvious that

\dfrac{\sqrt{2}|T_{c}|}{|\delta_{n}|^{2}+H(\widehat{g}_{n},g_{0})^{2}}\leq\dfrac{\sqrt{2}|T_{c}|}{2H(\widehat{g}_{n},g_{0})|\delta_{n}|}=o_{p}(1),

which proves (
6.57
) and thus completes the proof of part B of Theorem
9
.
\Box

Lemma 13
.

Suppose
F
is a distribution function satisfying Condition A. Denote

\text{int}(J(F))=(a,b),

for some
a,b\in[-\infty,\infty]
.
Suppose
\psi_{\theta}
is the maximizer of
\Psi(\theta,\psi,F)
over
\psi\in\mathcal{SC}_{0}
for some
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
. Then

\text{int}(\mathrm{dom}(\psi_{\theta}))=(-d,d),

where
d=(b-\theta)\vee(\theta-a).

Note that
a
or
b
can be
\infty
, in which case
d=\infty
, and
\text{int}(\mathrm{dom}(\psi_{\theta}))=\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

Note that
\text{int}(\mathrm{dom}(\psi_{\theta}))=(-c,c)
for some
c>0
. We first show that
c\geq d
. Consider the set
A=\overline{(-d,d)}\setminus\mathrm{dom}(\psi_{\theta})
.
Note that unless
d=\infty
,
\overline{(-d,d)}=[-d,d]
. Letting
\Delta
denote the indicator function
1_{A}
, we observe that
\psi_{\theta}+\Delta=\psi_{\theta}
. Also, note that

\psi_{\theta}=\argmax_{\psi\in\mathcal{SC}_{0}}\Psi(0,\psi,F(\mathord{\cdot}+\theta))=\argmax_{\psi\in\mathcal{SC}_{0}}\omega(\psi,F(\mathord{\cdot}+\theta)),

which follows from (
1.4
) and (
3.7
).
Hence, Proposition
5
(ii) of

leads to

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\Delta(x)dF(x+\theta)\leq\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\Delta(x)e^{\psi_{\theta}(x)}dx.

Since
\Delta=0
on
\mathrm{dom}(\psi_{\theta})
, it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{A}dF(x+\theta)=0,

implying
(a,b)\cap(A+\theta)=\emptyset
.

It is easy to see that if either
a
or
b
equals
\infty
,
d=\infty
, and

. Therefore,

(a,b)\cap(A+\theta)=(a,b)\setminus\bigg(\mathrm{dom}(\psi_{\theta})+\theta\bigg)=\emptyset,

which indicates that

(6.67)

implying

. Hence
(-c,c)=(-d,d)
, which completes the proof for this case. Therefore, it only remains to prove Lemma
13
for the case when both

and

When

, there can be two sub-cases. In the first case,

, which indicates

from which (

) follows.
Therefore,

, or

. In the second case

,

and

. Proceeding similarly as in the first case, we can again show that

. Hence, we have established that

in general.

Now suppose

, which implies that
a,b<\infty
. Observe that in this case, there exists a
\psi\in\mathcal{SC}_{0}
, which equals
\psi_{\theta}
on
(-d,d)
and
-\infty
everywhere else. It is easy to verify that when

,

\displaystyle\Psi(\theta,\psi_{\theta},F)-\Psi(\theta,\psi,F)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \displaystyle\int_{-d}^{d}e^{\psi_{\theta}(x)}dx-\displaystyle\int_{-c}^{c}e^{\psi_{\theta}(x)}dx<0.

However,
\psi_{\theta}
satisfies

An Eulerian finite element method for tangential Navier-Stokes equations on evolving surfaces
Maxim A. Olshanskii, Arnold Reusken, Paul Schwering

Abstract
The paper introduces a geometrically unfitted finite element method for the numerical solution of the tangential Navier–Stokes equations posed on a passively evolving smooth closed surface embedded in ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} . The discrete formulation employs finite difference and finite elements methods to handle evolution in time and variation in space, respectively. A complete numerical analysis of the method is presented, including stability, optimal order convergence, and quantification of the geometric errors. Results of numerical experiments are also provided.



keywords

surface Navier–Stokes system, surface PDEs, evolving surfaces, TraceFEM

Introduction

Lipid membranes and liquid crystal shells are examples of deformable thin structures exhibiting lateral fluidity
[
8
,
6
,
21
]
.
Continuum based modeling of such materials leads to systems of PDEs posed on evolving surfaces embedded in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
For example, the motion of an inextensible viscous fluid layer represented by a material surface is governed by the evolving surface Navier–Stokes equations derived in e.g.,
[
50
,
22
,
17
,
28
,
44
]
.
While the evolving surface Navier–Stokes system was discussed in the literature, to the best of our knowledge there is no existing well-posedness or numerical analysis of this problem.
The recent paper
[
37
]
addressed well-posedness of a simplified problem consisting of
tangential
surface Navier–Stokes equations (TSNSE) posed on a
passively
evolving surface embedded in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
A weak variational formulation of TSNSE was shown to be well-posed for any finite final time and without smallness conditions on the data. For that variational formulation we introduce a discretization method and prove its stability and optimal order convergence. This is the first study addressing numerical analysis of a fluid PDE system posed on an evolving surface.

For discretization of the TSNSE we consider a geometrically unfitted finite element method known as TraceFEM
[
32
,
35
]
. The TraceFEM applies to a fully Eulerian formulation of the problem and does not require a surface triangulation, which makes it convenient for deforming surfaces. In TraceFEM one uses standard (bulk) finite element spaces to approximate unknown quantities on the surface
\Gamma(t)
which propagates through a given triangulation of an ambient volume
\Omega
, i.e.
\Gamma(t)\subset\Omega
for all times
t
. The discrete formulation does not need a surface parametrization and uses tangential calculus in the embedding space
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. For scalar PDEs on evolving surfaces, a space–time and a hybrid (finite difference in time – finite elements in space) variant of TraceFEM are known in the literature
[
36
,
26
]
. For the TSNSE we choose the hybrid approach since it is more flexible in terms of implementation and the choice of elements.

A variant of the hybrid FEM that we consider in this paper has recently been applied to time-dependent Stokes equations in a moving volume domain
\Omega(t)\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
in
[
49
,
4
]
. In
[
49
]
Taylor-Hood finite elements are used, whereas in
[
4
]
equal order finite element spaces combined with a continuous interior penalty pressure stabilization are applied for space discretization. Both papers present a complete discretization error analysis. The resulting error bounds in
[
49
]
are suboptimal both for velocity and pressure and in
[
4
]
the bound for the pressure error is suboptimal. In both papers it is mentioned that the suboptimality of the bounds is essentially due to the lack of a uniform discrete pressure stability bound, cf
[
49
, Remark 5.11]
,
[
4
, Section 4.1]
. Related to this we mention a further
new contribution of this paper.
We introduce, in the surface case, a new argument which leads to a discrete pressure stability bound that is uniform in the parameter range
h^{2}\lesssim\Delta t
, cf. Remark
4.2
. Using this we derive error estimates for velocity and pressure that are optimal in the parameter range
h^{2}\lesssim\Delta t\lesssim h
. These parameter range restrictions are reasonable if one considers BDF1 or BDF2 time discretization methods and low order finite element pairs, e.g. Taylor-Hood
P_{2}
-
P_{1}
.

The authors of

The rest of the paper is organized in four sections. In section

we introduce necessary notations from tangential calculus and formulate the TSNSE as a well-posed variational problem. Section

Section

Results of a numerical experiment presented in section

2
Problem formulation

In this section we explain the tangential surface Navier-Stokes equations that we treat in this paper.
Consider, for
t\in[0,T]
, a
material
surface
\Gamma(t)
embedded in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
as defined in
[
15
,
30
]
, with a density distribution
\rho(t,\mathbf{x})
. By
\mathbf{u}(t,\mathbf{x})
,
\mathbf{x}\in\Gamma(t)
, we denote the velocity field of the density flow on
\Gamma(t)
, i.e.
\mathbf{u}(t,\mathbf{x})
is the velocity of a material point
\mathbf{x}\in\Gamma(t)
.
We further assume that the geometric evolution of
\Gamma(t)
is determined by a given smooth velocity field
\mathbf{w}=\mathbf{w}(t,\mathbf{x})
, which passively advects the initial surface
\Gamma_{0}:=\Gamma(0)
:

\Gamma(t)=\{\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}~|~\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{x}(t,\mathbf{z}),~\mathbf{z}\in\Gamma_{0}\},

(1)

with the trajectories
\mathbf{x}(t,\mathbf{z})
being the unique solutions of the Cauchy problem

\frac{d}{dt}\mathbf{x}(t,\mathbf{z})=\mathbf{w}(t,\mathbf{x}(t,\mathbf{z})),\quad\mathbf{x}(0,\mathbf{z})=\mathbf{z}

(2)

for all
\mathbf{z}\in\Gamma_{0}
.
This induces the smooth space-time manifold

\mathcal{G}=\bigcup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\{t\}\times\Gamma(t)\subset\mathbb{R}^{4}.

We need a few notations of geometric quantities and tangential differential operators. For a given
t\in[0,T]
we write
\Gamma=\Gamma(t)
.
The outward pointing normal vector on
\Gamma
is denoted by
\mathbf{n}
. The normal projector on the tangential space at
\mathbf{x}\in\Gamma
is given by
\mathbf{P}=\mathbf{P}(\mathbf{x})=\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{n}\mathbf{n}^{T}
. For a scalar function
p:\,\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}
or a vector field
\mathbf{u}:\,\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
their smooth extensions to a neighborhood
\mathcal{O}(\Gamma)
of
\Gamma
are denoted by
p^{e}
and
\mathbf{u}^{e}
, respectively. The surface gradient, covariant derivative and surface divergence on
\Gamma
can be defined through derivatives in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
as
\nabla_{\Gamma}p=\mathbf{P}\nabla p^{e}
,
\nabla_{\Gamma}\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{P}\nabla\mathbf{u}^{e}\mathbf{P}
, and
\operatorname{\rm div}_{\Gamma}\mathbf{u}=\mbox{tr}(\nabla_{\Gamma}\mathbf{u})
. These definitions are independent of a particular smooth extension of
p
and
\mathbf{u}
off
\Gamma
.
The surface rate-of-strain tensor
[
15
]
is given by

E_{s}(\mathbf{u})=\frac{1}{2}(\nabla_{\Gamma}\mathbf{u}+\nabla_{\Gamma}\mathbf{u}^{T})
.
By
\mathbf{H}=\nabla_{\Gamma}\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}
we denote the Weingarten mapping and by
\kappa:=\mbox{tr}(\mathbf{H})
twice the mean curvature.
For velocity fields on
\Gamma(t)
we use a splitting into tangential and normal components

\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{u}_{T}+\mathbf{u}_{N}=\mathbf{u}_{T}+u_{N}\mathbf{n},\quad\text{with}~u_{N}=\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{n}.

In our setting, the normal component of
\mathbf{u}
is completely determined by the ambient flow
\mathbf{w}
, i.e.

\mathbf{u}_{N}=\mathbf{w}_{N}
on
\Gamma
. For the normal component of the given smooth velocity field we have
\mathbf{w}_{N}=w_{N}\mathbf{n}
, wit
w_{N}=\mathbf{w}\cdot\mathbf{n}
the given smooth normal velocity of the surface
\Gamma
. Besides derivatives on
\Gamma
we also need the material derivative along
\mathcal{G}
, denoted by
\accentset{\mbox{\large.}}{g}
, which is the derivative of a surface quantity
g
along the trajectories
\mathbf{x}(t,\mathbf{z})
of material points. It can be written as

\accentset{\mbox{\large.}}{g}=\frac{\partial g^{e}}{\partial t}+(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)g^{e}
on
\mathcal{G}
. We also introduce the so-called normal time derivative of
g
, denoted by
\partial^{\circ}g
, which describes the variation of
g
along normal trajectories of points on
\Gamma(t)
:

\partial^{\circ}g:=\accentset{\mbox{\large.}}{g}-\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}g=\frac{\partial g^{e}}{\partial t}+(\mathbf{w}_{N}\cdot\nabla)g^{e}\quad\text{on}~\mathcal{G}.

(3)

For a vector valued quantity we use this definition componentwise.
Conservation of momentum and an inextensibility condition lead to the following system governing the free lateral motion of the material viscous surface
[
17
,
37
]
:
For a given density distribution
\rho>0
, viscosity coefficient
\mu>0
, find the tangential velocity field
\mathbf{u}_{T}
and surface pressure
p
satisfying the initial condition
\mathbf{u}_{T}(0)=\mathbf{u}_{0}
and the system of equations, which we call the tangential surface Navier-Stokes equations (TSNSE):

\left\{\begin{aligned} \rho\big(\,\mathbf{P}\partial^{\circ}\mathbf{u}_{T}+w_{N}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{u}_{T}+(\nabla_{\Gamma}\mathbf{u}_{T})\mathbf{u}_{T}\big)-2\mu\mathbf{P}{\mathop{\,\rm div}}_{\Gamma}E_{s}(\mathbf{u}_{T})+\nabla_{\Gamma}p&=\mathbf{f}\\
{\mathop{\,\rm div}}_{\Gamma}\mathbf{u}_{T}&=f\\
\end{aligned}\right.\quad\text{on}~\Gamma(t),

(4)

with right-hand sides known in terms of geometric quantities,
w_{N}
and the tangential component of the external area force
\mathbf{b}
:

f=-w_{N}\kappa,\qquad\mathbf{f}=\mathbf{b}_{T}+2\mu\mathbf{P}{\mathop{\,\rm div}}_{\Gamma}(w_{N}\mathbf{H})+\tfrac{\rho}{2}\nabla_{\Gamma}w_{N}^{2}.

(5)

The system can be seen as an idealized model for the motion of a thin fluid layer embedded in bulk fluid, where one neglects
friction forces between the surface and bulk fluids as well as any effect of the layer on the bulk flow (more precisely, one may assume that a lateral component of the normal bulk stress is given by
\mathbf{b}_{T}
).
System (
4
)–(
5
) also appears as an auxiliary problem if one applies directional splitting to the full system of equations governing the evolution of a material inextensible fluidic surface; see
[
17
]
. We further set
\rho=1
.

In this paper, we represent
\Gamma(t)
as the zero level set of a smooth level-set function
\phi(t,\mathbf{x})
,

\Gamma(t)=\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\,:\,\phi(t,\mathbf{x})=0\},

such that
|\nabla\phi|\geq c>0
in
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{G})
, a neighborhood of
\mathcal{G}
.
To simplify the presentation and analysis, we make the assumption that the
level set function has the signed distance property
. This assumption, however, is not essential. In Remark
3.2
we comment on the generalization of the method to the case where
\phi
is not necessarily a signed distance function.

3
Discretization method

In this section we present a fully Eulerian finite element method for the TSNSE (
4
). The method is based on the same ideas as used in
[
26
]
, namely a combination of an implicit time stepping scheme with a TraceFEM in space.
We start with the discretization of the system’s evolution in time.

3.1
Time-stepping scheme

Consider uniformly distributed time nodes
t_{n}=n\Delta t
,
n=0,\dots,N
, with the time step
\Delta t=T/N
and
I_{n}:=[t_{n-1},t_{n}]
,
1\leq n\leq N
. We assume that the time step
\Delta t
is sufficiently small such that

\Gamma(t_{n})\subset\mathcal{O}(\Gamma(t_{n-1})),\quad~n=1,\dots,N,~

(6)

with
\mathcal{O}(\Gamma(t))
a neighborhood of
\Gamma(t)
where a smooth extension of surface quantities from
\Gamma(t)
is well defined. Among smooth extensions of a (scalar or vector valued) function
g
we now choose the constant extension in normal direction
g^{e}
, i.e.,
\nabla d\cdot\nabla g^{e}=0
in
O(\Gamma(t))
, with
d=d(t,\cdot)
the signed distance function to
\Gamma(t)
. For the normal extension the last term in (
3
) vanishes and thus we have

\partial^{\circ}g=\frac{\partial g^{e}}{\partial t}\quad\text{on}~\mathcal{G}.

(7)

Based on this we introduce on
\Gamma(t_{n})
the normal time derivative approximation

\mathbf{P}\partial^{\circ}\mathbf{u}_{T}=\mathbf{P}\frac{\partial\mathbf{u}_{T}^{e}}{\partial t}\approx\frac{\mathbf{u}_{T}(t_{n})-\mathbf{P}(t_{n})\mathbf{u}_{T}(t_{n-1})^{e}}{\Delta t}.

(8)

Due to (
6
)
\mathbf{u}_{T}(t_{n-1})^{e}
is defined on
\Gamma(t_{n})
.
For the normal
\mathbf{n}
on
\Gamma(t_{j})
its constant extension in
\mathcal{O}(\Gamma(t_{j}))
is also denoted by
\mathbf{n}
, i.e.,

\mathbf{n}=\nabla d
.
We further use the notation
\mathbf{u}_{T}^{j}
and
p^{j}
for an approximation of
\mathbf{u}_{T}(t_{j})^{e}
and
p(t_{j})
, respectively. Based on (
8
) and (
6
) we consider the following time discretization method for (
4
). Given
\mathbf{u}_{T}^{0}=\mathbf{u}_{T}(0)^{e}
in
\mathcal{O}(\Gamma_{0})
, for
n=1,\ldots N
, find
\mathbf{u}^{n}_{T}
, defined in
\mathcal{O}(\Gamma(t_{n}))
and tangential to
\Gamma(t_{n})
, i.e.
(\mathbf{u}^{n}_{T}\cdot\mathbf{n})|_{\Gamma(t_{n})}=0
, and
p^{n}
defined on
\Gamma(t_{n})
such that

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \frac{\mathbf{u}^{n}_{T}-\mathbf{P}\mathbf{u}^{n-1}_{T}}{\Delta t}+w_{N}^{n}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{u}_{T}^{n}+(\nabla_{\Gamma}\mathbf{u}_{T}^{n})\mathbf{u}_{T}^{n-1}-2\mu\mathbf{P}{\mathop{\,\rm div}}_{\Gamma}E_{s}(\mathbf{u}_{T}^{n})+\nabla_{\Gamma}p^{n}&=\mathbf{f}^{n}\\
{\mathop{\,\rm div}}_{\Gamma}\mathbf{u}_{T}^{n}&=f^{n}\\
\end{aligned}\right.\quad

\displaystyle\text{on}~~\Gamma(t_{n}),

(9)

\displaystyle\mathbf{n}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{u}^{n}_{T}=0~\quad

\displaystyle\text{in}~~\mathcal{O}(\Gamma(t_{n})),

(10)

with
w_{N}^{n}:=w_{N}(t_{n})
,
\mathbf{f}^{n}:=\mathbf{f}(t_{n})
,
f^{n}:=f(t_{n})
. Note that in the inertia term we also use
\mathbf{P}\mathbf{u}_{T}^{n-1}
, since
(\nabla_{\Gamma}\mathbf{u}_{T}^{n})\mathbf{u}_{T}^{n-1}=(\nabla_{\Gamma}\mathbf{u}_{T}^{n})\mathbf{P}\mathbf{u}_{T}^{n-1}
holds.
Geometric information in (
9
) is taken for
\Gamma(t_{n})
, i.e.
\mathbf{n}=\mathbf{n}(t_{n})
,
\mathbf{P}=\mathbf{P}(t_{n})
,
\mathbf{H}=\mathbf{H}(t_{n})
. In (
9
) we use a BDF1 (i.e., Euler implicit) type time discretization, which has local truncation error
\mathcal{O}(\Delta t^{2})
. Related to that we use a simple, first order in
\Delta t
accurate, linearization of the quadratic nonlinearity, i.e.,
(\nabla_{\Gamma}\mathbf{u}_{T}^{n})\mathbf{u}_{T}^{n}
is replaced by
(\nabla_{\Gamma}\mathbf{u}_{T}^{n})\mathbf{u}_{T}^{n-1}
. This approach has a straightforward extension to higher order in
\Delta t
schemes, cf. the discussion in Remark
3.2
below. The space discretization of (
9
)–(
10
) is presented in the next section.

3.2
Space discretization method

Consider a fixed polygonal domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
that strictly contains
\Gamma(t)
for all
t\in[0,T]
.
Let
\{\mathcal{T}_{h}\}_{h>0}
be a family of shape-regular consistent triangulations of
\Omega
, with
\max\limits_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\mbox{diam}(K)\leq h
. Corresponding to the bulk triangulation we define a standard finite element space of piecewise polynomial continuous functions of a fixed degree
k\geq 1
:

V_{h,k}=\{v_{h}\in C(\Omega)\,:\,v_{h}\in P_{k}(K),~~\forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\}.

(11)

The bulk velocity and pressure finite element spaces are standard Taylor–Hood spaces:

\mathbf{U}_{h}=(V_{h,m+1})^{3},\quad Q_{h}=V_{h,m},\quad\text{with}~m\geq 1.

(12)

We want to avoid the assumption that all
\Gamma(t_{n})
are given in an explicit parametric form or that the exact level set functions
\phi(t_{n},\cdot)
are known. If, for example, (
4
) is part of a system, where tangential surface motions are coupled to the normal ones, then finding
\Gamma(t_{n})
is part of the problem, and knowledge of only a (finite element) approximation to
\phi(t_{n},\cdot)
would be a more realistic assumption. In turn, this results in approximation of all geometric quantities involved in (
4
). This issue of geometry approximation is explained in section
3.2.1
below.

If not specified otherwise, all constants
C
,
c
,
c_{0}
,
c_{1}
, etc. appearing later in the text are generic positive constants which are
independent
of
h
,
\Delta t
, other discretization parameters, time instance
t_{n}
, and the position of
\Gamma
in the background mesh, but
may depend
on
w_{N}
,
\mathcal{G}
,
\mathbf{u}
, and the shape regularity of
\mathcal{T}_{h}
.
In order to reduce the repeated use of such constants, we often write
x\lesssim y
to state that the inequality
x\leq cy
holds for quantities
x,y
with such generic constant
c
. Similarly for
x\gtrsim y
, and
x\simeq y
will mean that both
x\lesssim y
and
x\gtrsim y
hold.

3.2.1
Geometry approximation

For any fixed
t\in[0,T]
,
\phi_{h}(\cdot)=\phi_{h}(t,\cdot)
is a given continuous piecewise polynomial approximation (with respect to
\mathcal{T}_{h}
) of
\phi(\cdot)=\phi(t,\cdot)
, which satisfies

\|\phi-\phi_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+h\|\nabla(\phi-\phi_{h})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\lesssim\,h^{q+1},

(13)

with some
q\geq 1
. For this estimate to hold, we assume that the level set function has the smoothness property
\phi(t,\cdot)\in C^{q+1}(\Omega)
.
Moreover, we assume that
|\nabla\phi_{h}|\geq C>0
in
\mathcal{O}(\Gamma(t))
,
t\in[0,T]
, and that
\phi_{h}
is sufficiently regular in time such that with
\phi_{h}^{n}(\mathbf{x})=\phi_{h}(t_{n},\mathbf{x}),~n=0,\dots,N
, there holds

\displaystyle\|\phi_{h}^{n-1}-\phi_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\,\Delta t\|{w_{N}}\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{n}\times\Omega)},

(14a)

\displaystyle\|\nabla\phi_{h}^{n-1}-\nabla\phi_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\,\Delta t\left(\|{w_{N}}\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{n}\times\Omega)}+\|\nabla{w_{N}}\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{n}\times\Omega)}\right),\text{ for }n=1,\dots,N.

(14b)

We define the discrete surfaces
\Gamma_{h}\approx\Gamma
as the zero level of
\phi_{h}
:

\Gamma_{h}(t):=\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\,:\,\phi_{h}(t,\mathbf{x})=0\}.

(15)

From the property (
13
) it follows that the Lipschitz surface
\Gamma_{h}
is an approximation to
\Gamma
with

\operatorname{dist}(\Gamma^{n}_{h},\Gamma)=\max_{x\in\Gamma_{h}}|\phi(\mathbf{x})|=\max_{x\in\Gamma_{h}}|\phi(\mathbf{x})-\phi_{h}(\mathbf{x})|\leq\|\phi-\phi_{h}\|_{\infty,\Omega}\lesssim h^{q+1}.

(16)

Furthermore, it also follows that the vector field
\mathbf{n}_{h}=\nabla\phi_{h}/|\nabla\phi_{h}|
(
\mathbf{n}_{h}
is the
normal to
\Gamma_{h}
), and the extended normal vector to
\Gamma
satisfy

|\mathbf{n}_{h}(\mathbf{x})-\mathbf{n}(\mathbf{x})|\leq c|\nabla\phi_{h}(\mathbf{x})-\nabla\phi(\mathbf{x})|\lesssim h^{q},\quad\text{in}~\mathcal{O}(\Gamma(t)).

(17)

On
\Gamma_{h}
we have the tangential projection operator
\mathbf{P}_{h}=\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{n}_{h}\mathbf{n}_{h}^{T}
.
Besides the geometric quantity
\mathbf{n}_{h}
we also need a discrete approximation of the Weingarten mapping
\mathbf{H}_{h}\approx\mathbf{H}
.
We assume that this approximation is of the form

\mathbf{H}_{h}=\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}\overline{\mathbf{n}}_{h},\quad\text{with}~\overline{\mathbf{n}}_{h}\in H^{1}(\Gamma_{h})^{3}\quad\text{that satisfies}~|\overline{\mathbf{n}}_{h}(\mathbf{x})-\mathbf{n}(\mathbf{x})|\lesssim h^{q},\quad\mathbf{x}\in\Gamma_{h}.

(18)

Here
\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}
is defined (a.e. on
\Gamma_{h}
) analogous to
\nabla_{\Gamma}
.
The normal approximation
\overline{\mathbf{n}}_{h}
may be chosen as a suitable interpolation operator in a finite element space applied to
\mathbf{n}_{h}
, e.g.,
\overline{\mathbf{n}}_{h}=I_{h}\mathbf{n}_{h}
, with
I_{h}
the (componentwise) Oswald averaging operator that maps into the finite element space
(V_{h,q-1})^{3}
.

We emphasize that all bounds in (
13
)–(
18
) are uniform in
t
(as well as in
h
,
\Delta t
,
n
and position of
\Gamma
or
\Gamma_{h}
in the mesh).

Remark 3.1
.

In the method that we present below integrals over
\Gamma_{h}
occur. We
assume that these integrals can be computed accurately
. In practice, this is straightforward for piecewise linear
\phi_{h}(\cdot,t)
. The higher order case
q>1
is more involved and requires special approaches for the construction of quadrature rules or the use of an parametric FEM technique
[
12
,
11
,
24
,
29
,
38
,
45
,
47
]
.

3.2.2
Fully discrete method

For computational efficiency reasons, we use an extension not in the given (
h
and
\Delta t
-independent) neighborhood
\mathcal{O}(\Gamma(t_{n}))
of
\Gamma(t_{n})
but in a smaller (
\Delta t
-dependent)
narrow band
around
\Gamma^{n}_{h}=\Gamma_{h}(t_{n})
. This narrow band consists of all tetrahedra within a
\delta_{n}
distance from the surface, with
\delta_{n}\simeq\Delta t
.
More precisely, we define the mesh-dependent narrow bands

\displaystyle\begin{split}U_{\delta}(\Gamma^{n}_{h})&:=\left\{\mathbf{x}\in\Omega\,:\mbox{dist}(\mathbf{x},\Gamma^{n}_{h})\leq\delta_{n}\right\},\\
\mathcal{O}_{\delta}(\Gamma^{n}_{h})&:={\bigcup}\left\{\overline{K}\,:\,K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}~\text{and}~\mbox{dist}(\mathbf{x},\Gamma^{n}_{h})\leq\delta_{n}\text{ for some }\mathbf{x}\in K\right\}\supset U_{\delta}(\Gamma^{n}_{h}).\end{split}

(19)

We also need a subdomain of
\mathcal{O}_{\delta}(\Gamma^{n}_{h})

In the derivation of a finite element formulation based on the discrete-in-time system (

, extension of the velocity along normal directions as in (

First, we relax the condition for the solution to be tangential to

to allow for

as trial and test velocity space. The tangentiality condition is weakly enforced using a penalty approach, which is often used in finite element methods for vector-valued surface PDEs

.
The constraint in (

For this we use a penalty (or stabilization) approach that is standard in trace finite element method and based on adding a volume normal derivative term to the discrete bilinear form. The treatment of inertia also follows an established approach. For this we rewrite the corresponding trilinear form, where we use integrals over the exact surface
\Gamma=\Gamma(t_{n})
and
\mathbf{u}_{T}\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}\mathbf{v}_{T}=(\nabla_{\Gamma}\mathbf{v}_{T})\mathbf{u}_{T}
:

\int_{\Gamma}(\mathbf{u}_{T}\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}\mathbf{u}_{T})\cdot\mathbf{v}_{T}\,ds=\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{\Gamma}(\mathbf{u}_{T}\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}\mathbf{u}_{T})\cdot\mathbf{v}_{T}-(\mathbf{u}_{T}\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}\mathbf{v}_{T})\cdot\mathbf{u}_{T}\,ds-\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{\Gamma}{\mathop{\,\rm div}}_{\Gamma}\mathbf{u}_{T}(\mathbf{u}_{T}\cdot\mathbf{v}_{T})\,ds.

Using

on
\Gamma
, the second term on the right-hand side becomes linear. The first term is linearized using the

approximation from the previous time step leading to an antisymmetric bilinear form, which is convenient for the stability analysis of the discretization.

Putting these components together leads to the following

(one time step), where we use (sufficiently accurate) extensions

,

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma^{n}_{h}}\left(\frac{\mathbf{u}^{n}_{h}-\mathbf{u}^{n-1}_{h}}{\Delta t}+w_{N}^{e,n}\mathbf{H}_{h}\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n}\right)\cdot\mathbf{P}_{h}\mathbf{v}_{h}\,ds_{h}

\displaystyle+\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{\Gamma^{n}_{h}}(\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n-1}\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}\mathbf{P}_{h}\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n})\cdot\mathbf{v}_{h}-(\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n-1}\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}\mathbf{P}_{h}\mathbf{v}_{h})\cdot\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n}\,ds_{h}-\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{\Gamma^{n}_{h}}f^{e,n}\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n}\cdot\mathbf{P}_{h}\mathbf{v}_{h}\,ds_{h}

3.3
Discussion of the method

In addition to the mesh size parameter
h
and time step
\Delta t
, the finite element method involves several other discretization parameters summarized below.

•

\rho_{u}
: a normal stabilization and extension parameter for velocity;

•

\rho_{p}
: a normal stabilization parameter for pressure;

•

\tau
: a penalty parameter for
\mathbf{n}_{h}\cdot\mathbf{u}_{h}=0
constraint;

•

\delta_{n}
: a narrow band parameter at time
t_{n}
.

We assume that these parameters satisfy the conditions

\displaystyle h

\displaystyle\leq c_{1},~~\Delta t\leq c_{2},

(24a)

\displaystyle c_{\rho}h^{-1}

\displaystyle\leq\rho_{u}\lesssim h^{-1},

(24b)

\displaystyle\rho_{p}

\displaystyle\simeq h,

(24c)

\displaystyle\tau

\displaystyle\simeq h^{-2},

(24d)

\displaystyle\Delta t

\displaystyle\geq 2\,\tau^{-1},

(24e)

\displaystyle c_{\delta}\Delta t

\displaystyle\leq\delta_{n}\lesssim h,

(24f)

where
c_{1}
,
c_{2}
are sufficiently small
O(1)
and
c_{\delta}
,
c_{\rho}
are sufficiently large
O(1)
constants independent of the parameters, time, and position of
\Gamma_{h}
in the mesh. The parameter conditions (
24b
) and (
24c
) are known from the literature on trace finite element methods. The condition “
c_{\rho}
sufficiently large” is needed to obtain sufficient control of a velocity extension in the narrow band (it is used to prove a key estimate (
36c
)). Parameter
\tau
as in (
24d
) guarantees accurate enough fulfillment of the tangentiality condition for the discrete velocity. Taking
\tau
even larger may lead to a ’locking’ phenomenon. Eq. (
24e
) is a technical condition that we need to prove the stability estimate in Theorem
7
. The upper bound in (
24f
) on the narrow band parameter
\delta_{n}
keeps the complexity of the method optimal, i.e. the number of active degrees of freedom is
O(h^{-2})
on each time step. Concerning the lower bound on
\delta_{n}
we note the following.
In a time step from
t_{n-1}
to
t_{n}
, the surface
\Gamma(t)
can move at most
\Delta t\sup_{t\in I_{n}}\|w_{N}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma(t))}
distance in normal direction. Thanks to assumption (
14a
) the maximum distance from
\Gamma^{n}_{h}
to
\Gamma^{n-1}_{h}
is also proportional to
\Delta t\sup_{t\in I_{n}}\|w_{N}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma(t))}
.
Therefore,
c_{\delta}
in (
24f
) can be taken such that

\omega^{n}_{\Gamma}\subset\mathcal{O}_{\delta}(\Gamma^{n-1}_{h}).

(25)

This condition is the discrete analog of (
6
) and is essential for the well-posedness of the finite element problem. The assumptions (
24d
)–(
24f
) can be satisfied if we take
\Delta t
and
h
such that the scaling conditions

h^{2}\lesssim\Delta t\lesssim h

(26)

hold. These scalings are reasonable. Consider Taylor–Hood elements with
m=1
. For optimal
O(h^{2})
-convergence in the energy norm of (
21
) one needs
\Delta t\simeq h^{2}
. For the more time-accurate BDF2 scheme (this is our practical choice, cf. Remark
3.3
)
\Delta t\simeq h
leads to
O(h^{2})
-convergence in the energy norm, and
\Delta t\simeq h^{3/2}
is the choice consistent with the best possible
O(h^{3})
-order in the velocity
L^{2}
-norm.
In the remarks below we briefly address a few further aspects of the method.

Remark 3.2
.

In practice we typically do not use a level set function that has the signed distance property. Then the extension does not satisfy (
10
), but is approximately constant along the normals to the level lines and instead of
\mathbf{P}\partial^{\circ}\mathbf{u}_{T}=\mathbf{P}\frac{\partial\mathbf{u}_{T}}{\partial t}
, cf. (
7
), one uses the general relation

\mathbf{P}\partial^{\circ}\mathbf{u}_{T}=\mathbf{P}\left(\frac{\partial\mathbf{u}_{T}^{e}}{\partial t}+(\nabla\mathbf{u}_{T}^{e})\mathbf{w}_{N}\right),

as basis for the discretization. Hence, for the case of a general level set function we include in (
21
) the term
\int_{\Gamma_{h}^{n}}(\mathbf{w}_{N}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{P}_{h}\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n})\cdot\mathbf{P}_{h}\mathbf{v}_{h}\,ds_{h}
. Apart from this, the discretization method stays the same.

Remark 3.3
.

The BDF2 variant is very similar to the method introduced above. The time difference
\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n}-\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n-1}
in the first line in (
21
) is then replaced by
\tfrac{3}{2}\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n}-2\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n-1}+\tfrac{1}{2}\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n-2}
and in the linearization of the inertia term we use
2\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n-1}-\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n-2}
instead of
\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n-1}
. All other terms in (
21
)–(
22
) remain the same. For BDF2, in addition to (
25
) one has to guarantee
\omega^{n}_{\Gamma}\subset\mathcal{O}_{\delta}(\Gamma^{n-2}_{h})
, which means that the narrow bands
\mathcal{O}_{\delta}(\Gamma^{n}_{h})
have to be taken thicker than in the BDF1 method.

Remark 3.4
.

In the surface gradient operators
\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}
in the inertia term and the surface rate-of-strain tensor
E_{s,h}(\cdot)
we use projected vector fields
\mathbf{P}_{h}\mathbf{v}
. Differentiating projector
\mathbf{P}_{h}
(which in general is discontinuous across element faces) we loose
H^{1}
conformity, cf.
[
20
]
. Based on the formula
\nabla_{\Gamma}\mathbf{v}_{T}=\nabla_{\Gamma}\mathbf{v}-v_{N}\mathbf{H}
, in order to avoid differentiation of
\mathbf{P}_{h}
, in the implementation of the method we replace
\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}\mathbf{P}_{h}\mathbf{v}_{h}
by
\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}\mathbf{v}_{h}-(\mathbf{n}_{h}\cdot\mathbf{v}_{h})\mathbf{H}_{h}
.

Remark 3.5
.

For convenience, in the error analysis below we assume that the data extensions
w^{e}_{N}
,
f^{e}
,
\mathbf{f}^{e}
are constant extensions along the normals
\mathbf{n}
. In practice one typically uses other (sufficiently accurate) extensions.

Remark 4.1
.

We outline the structure of the error analysis. We first collect some preliminary results and prove a few helpful estimates, with a particular emphasis on having them uniform in discretization parameters, time and the surface position in the bulk mesh. Using these results it is easy to derive a suitable coercivity and continuity estimates for the trilinear form
a_{n}(\cdot;\cdot,\cdot)
used in (
28
).
Based on rather straightforward arguments we derive in Theorem
7
a stability result for the discrete velocity solution
\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n}
of (
28
) in a natural energy norm. The analysis continues with addressing the more delicate question of the finite element pressure stability. Here we introduce new arguments, namely the use of a specific (non-standard) pressure norm and of a special discrete velocity function that gives control of the pressure via the discrete inf-sup stability condition, cf. Remark
4.2
. Thus we obtain a uniform discrete stability estimate (Theorem
8
) in the parameter range (
26
).
In Section
4.4
we present a consistency analysis that quantifies geometric errors resulting from the approximation of surface and geometric quantities (normal vectors, tangential projectors, curvatures) in the finite element formulation. In that analysis we use results already available in the literature on surface vector-Laplace and Stokes problems. Finally, in Section
4.5
we apply a standard technique for proving discretization error bounds. The discretization error is split into an interpolation error and an error component that lies in the finite element space. Bounds for the latter can be derived using the discrete stability, consistency error bounds, and bounds for the linearization error. With respect to the interpolation error a key point is that in the Euclidean setting of this trace method we can use a (nodal) interpolation operator on a fixed bulk mesh. Due to this the time differentiation and interpolation operator commute, which then leads to optimal bounds for the difference of interpolation errors at
t=t_{n}
and
t=t_{n-1}
, cf. Lemma

.

\|v_{h}\|_{\Gamma^{n}_{h}}^{2}\lesssim\|v_{h}\|_{\Gamma^{n-1}_{h}}^{2}+\delta_{n-1}\|\mathbf{n}_{h}^{n-1}\cdot\nabla v_{h}\|_{\mathcal{O}_{\delta}(\Gamma^{n-1}_{h})}^{2}.

(37)

A proof is given in Appendix
B
. It uses arguments similar to those found in
[
26
]
.

In the analysis of two-dimensional Navier-Stokes equations one typically needs bounds for
\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{4}}
to control the quadratic nonlinearity. We will derive such a bound in Lemma
4
. As a preparation, in the next lemma we give an analog of the result (
37
), with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}}
replaced by
\|\cdot\|_{L^{4}}
.

Lemma 3
.

The following uniform estimate holds

\|v_{h}\|_{L^{4}(\Gamma_{h}^{n})}^{2}\lesssim\|v_{h}\|_{L^{4}(\Gamma^{n-1}_{h})}^{2}+\|\mathbf{n}_{h}\cdot\nabla v_{h}\|_{\mathcal{O}_{\delta}(\Gamma^{n-1}_{h})}^{2},\quad\text{for all}~v_{h}\in V^{n-1}_{h,m}.

(38)

Proof.

We use the notation
\mathcal{O}_{\delta}=\mathcal{O}_{\delta}(\Gamma^{n-1}_{h})
. First we consider an inverse estimate for
\|\mathbf{n}_{h}\cdot\nabla v_{h}\|_{L^{4}(\mathcal{O}_{\delta})}
. Using
\mathbf{n}_{h}=|\nabla\phi_{h}|^{-1}\nabla\phi_{h}
, where
\phi_{h}
is a finite element function with
|\nabla\phi_{h}|\simeq 1
in
\mathcal{O}_{\delta}
, and finite element inverse estimates we get

\begin{split}\|\mathbf{n}_{h}\cdot\nabla v_{h}\|_{L^{4}(\mathcal{O}_{\delta})}&\leq\||\nabla\phi_{h}|^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{O}_{\delta})}\|\nabla\phi_{h}\cdot\nabla v_{h}\|_{L^{4}(\mathcal{O}_{\delta})}\lesssim\|\nabla\phi_{h}\cdot\nabla v_{h}\|_{L^{4}(\mathcal{O}_{\delta})}\\
&\lesssim h^{-\frac{3}{4}}\|\nabla\phi_{h}\cdot\nabla v_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\delta})}\lesssim h^{-\frac{3}{4}}\||\nabla\phi_{h}|\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{O}_{\delta})}\|\mathbf{n}_{h}\cdot\nabla v_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\delta})}\\
&\lesssim h^{-\frac{3}{4}}\|\mathbf{n}_{h}\cdot\nabla v_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\delta})}.\end{split}

(39)

Using this and (
36b
) applied to
v_{h}^{2}
we get

\displaystyle\|v_{h}\|_{L^{4}(\mathcal{O}_{\delta})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|v_{h}^{2}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\delta})}\lesssim(\delta_{n-1}+h)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|v_{h}^{2}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma^{n-1}_{h})}+(\delta_{n-1}+h)\|\mathbf{n}_{h}\cdot\nabla v_{h}^{2}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\delta})}

\displaystyle\lesssim(\delta_{n-1}+h)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|v_{h}\|_{L^{4}(\Gamma^{n-1}_{h})}^{2}+(\delta_{n-1}+h)\|v_{h}\|_{L^{4}(\mathcal{O}_{\delta})}\|\mathbf{n}_{h}\cdot\nabla v_{h}\|_{L^{4}(\mathcal{O}_{\delta})}

\displaystyle\leq c_{0}(\delta_{n-1}+h)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|v_{h}\|_{L^{4}(\Gamma^{n-1}_{h})}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|v_{h}\|_{L^{4}(\mathcal{O}_{\delta})}^{2}+c_{1}h^{-\frac{3}{2}}(\delta_{n-1}+h)^{2}\|\mathbf{n}_{h}\cdot\nabla v_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\delta})}^{2}.

Shifting the term
\|v_{h}\|_{L^{4}(\mathcal{O}_{\delta})}^{2}
to the left-hand side we obtain

\|v_{h}\|_{L^{4}(\mathcal{O}_{\delta})}^{2}\lesssim(\delta_{n-1}+h)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|v_{h}\|_{L^{4}(\Gamma^{n-1}_{h})}^{2}+h^{-\frac{3}{2}}(\delta_{n-1}+h)^{2}\|\mathbf{n}_{h}\cdot\nabla v_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\delta})}^{2}.

(40)

We use (
37
) with
v_{h}^{2}
, which is a finite element function of degree
2m
and so (
37
) applies:

\|v_{h}\|_{L^{4}(\Gamma_{h}^{n})}^{2}=\|v_{h}^{2}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h}^{n})}\lesssim\|v_{h}^{2}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h}^{n-1})}+\delta_{n-1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\mathbf{n}_{h}\cdot\nabla v_{h}^{2}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\delta})}.

(41)

From the chain rule and the result (
39
) above we obtain

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{n}_{h}\cdot\nabla v_{h}^{2}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\delta})}

\displaystyle\leq\|v_{h}\|_{L^{4}(\mathcal{O}_{\delta})}\|\mathbf{n}_{h}\cdot\nabla v_{h}\|_{L^{4}(\mathcal{O}_{\delta})}\lesssim h^{-\frac{3}{4}}\|v_{h}\|_{L^{4}(\mathcal{O}_{\delta})}\|\mathbf{n}_{h}\cdot\nabla v_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\delta})}

Substituting this in (
41
) and using (
40
) we get

\displaystyle\|v_{h}\|_{L^{4}(\Gamma_{h}^{n})}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|v_{h}\|_{L^{4}(\Gamma_{h}^{n-1})}^{2}+\delta_{n-1}^{\frac{1}{2}}h^{-\frac{3}{4}}\|v_{h}\|_{L^{4}(\mathcal{O}_{\delta})}\|\mathbf{n}_{h}\cdot\nabla v_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\delta})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|v_{h}\|_{L^{4}(\Gamma_{h}^{n-1})}^{2}+\delta_{n-1}h^{-\frac{3}{2}}\|v_{h}\|_{L^{4}(\mathcal{O}_{\delta})}^{2}+\|\mathbf{n}_{h}\cdot\nabla v_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\delta})}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\big(1+\delta_{n-1}h^{-\frac{3}{2}}(\delta_{n-1}+h)^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)\|v_{h}\|_{L^{4}(\Gamma_{h}^{n-1})}^{2}+\big(\delta_{n-1}h^{-3}(\delta_{n-1}+h)^{2}+1\big)\|\mathbf{n}_{h}\cdot\nabla v_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\delta})}^{2}.

Due to (
24f
) (i.e.
\delta_{n-1}\lesssim h
) we get
\delta_{n-1}h^{-\frac{3}{2}}(\delta_{n-1}+h)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim 1
and
\delta_{n-1}h^{-3}(\delta_{n-1}+h)^{2}\lesssim 1
, which completes the proof.
∎

Lemma
3
is used in the proof of the next lemma.

Lemma 4
.

The following uniform estimate holds

\|\mathbf{v}_{h}\|_{L^{4}(\Gamma_{h}^{n})}^{2}\lesssim\|\mathbf{v}_{h}\|_{\Gamma_{h}^{n-1}}|\!|\!|\mathbf{v}_{h}|\!|\!|_{U^{n-1}}+h|\!|\!|\mathbf{v}_{h}|\!|\!|_{U^{n-1}}^{2}\quad\text{for all}~\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{U}_{h}^{n-1}.

(42)

Proof.

Take
\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{U}_{h}^{n-1}
. Component-wise application of (
38
) yields

\|\mathbf{v}_{h}\|_{L^{4}(\Gamma_{h}^{n})}\lesssim\|\mathbf{v}_{h}\|_{L^{4}(\Gamma_{h}^{n-1})}+\|\mathbf{n}_{h}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{v}_{h}\|_{\mathcal{O}_{\delta}(\Gamma_{h}^{n-1})}\lesssim\|\mathbf{v}_{h}\|_{L^{4}(\Gamma_{h}^{n-1})}+h^{\frac{1}{2}}|\!|\!|\mathbf{v}_{h}|\!|\!|_{U^{n-1}}.

(43)

Using (
34
) and (
35
) we get

\|\mathbf{v}_{h}\|_{L^{4}(\Gamma_{h}^{n-1})}\lesssim\|\mathbf{v}_{h}\|_{\Gamma_{h}^{n-1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}|\!|\!|\mathbf{v}_{h}|\!|\!|_{U^{n-1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Substituting the latter estimate in (

4.2
Coercivity and continuity estimates

We derive coercivity and continuity estimates for the trilinear form
a_{n}(\cdot\,;\cdot,\cdot)
.

Lemma 5
.

For arbitrary
\mathbf{z}\in L^{2}(\Gamma_{h}^{n})
and
\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{U}_{h}^{n}
it holds

a_{n}(\mathbf{z};\mathbf{v}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})\geq|\!|\!|\mathbf{v}_{h}|\!|\!|_{U^{n}}^{2}-\xi_{h}\|\mathbf{P}_{h}\mathbf{v}_{h}\|^{2}_{\Gamma^{n}_{h}}\quad\text{with}~\xi_{h}:=1+\max_{n=0,..,N}\|w_{N}^{e,n}\mathbf{H}_{h}(t_{n})\|_{L^{\infty}}.

(44)

Proof.

From (
17
) and (
23
) it follows for sufficiently small
h
that

|\mathbf{v}|^{2}=|\mathbf{n}_{h}\cdot\mathbf{v}|^{2}+|\mathbf{P}_{h}\mathbf{v}|^{2}\leq(1+ch^{q})\big(|\widetilde{\mathbf{n}}_{h}\cdot\mathbf{v}|^{2}+|\mathbf{P}_{h}\mathbf{v}|^{2}\big)\quad\forall\,\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

Using this,
c_{n}(\mathbf{z};\mathbf{v}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})=0
,
\mathbf{H}_{h}=\mathbf{P}_{h}\mathbf{H}_{h}\mathbf{P}_{h}
, which follows from the definition of
\mathbf{H}_{h}
, cf. (
18
), and
\tau\geq 1+ch^{q}
(for
h,\tau
satisfying (
24a
) and (
24d
)), we obtain the following lower estimate for
a_{n}(\mathbf{z};\cdot,\cdot)
:

\begin{split}a_{n}(\mathbf{z};\mathbf{v}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})&=\widehat{a}_{n}(\mathbf{v}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})=(w_{N}^{e,n}\mathbf{H}_{h}\mathbf{v}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})_{\Gamma^{n}_{h}}+|\!|\!|\mathbf{v}_{h}|\!|\!|_{U^{n}}^{2}+\tfrac{\tau}{2}\|\widetilde{\mathbf{n}}_{h}\cdot\mathbf{v}\|^{2}_{\Gamma^{n}_{h}}-\tfrac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{v}_{h}\|^{2}_{\Gamma_{h}^{n}}\\
&\geq|\!|\!|\mathbf{v}_{h}|\!|\!|_{U^{n}}^{2}-\big(\|w_{N}^{e,n}\mathbf{H}_{h}(t_{n})\|_{L^{\infty}}+\tfrac{1}{2}(1+ch^{q})\big)\|\mathbf{P}_{h}\mathbf{v}_{h}\|^{2}_{\Gamma^{n}_{h}}\quad\forall~\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{U}_{h}^{n},\end{split}

from which the result follows.
∎

Lemma 6
.

The following uniform estimate holds:

a_{n}(\mathbf{z};\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})\lesssim\big(\|\mathbf{P}_{h}\mathbf{z}\|_{L^{4}(\Gamma_{h}^{n})}+1\big)|\!|\!|\mathbf{u}|\!|\!|_{U^{n}}|\!|\!|\mathbf{v}|\!|\!|_{U^{n}},\quad\mathbf{z}\in L^{4}(\Gamma_{h}^{n}),~\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\in\mathbf{U}_{h}^{n}.

(45)

Proof.

For the terms in the trilinear form
a(\cdot\,;\cdot,\cdot)
that do not depend on the first argument, cf. (
27
), we apply Cauchy-Schwarz inequality:

\left|\widehat{a}_{n}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})\right|\lesssim|\!|\!|\mathbf{u}|\!|\!|_{U^{n}}|\!|\!|\mathbf{v}|\!|\!|_{U^{n}}.

(46)

We now consider the term
\int_{\Gamma^{n}_{h}}(\mathbf{z}\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}\mathbf{P}_{h}\mathbf{u})\cdot\mathbf{v}\,ds_{h}
. The term
\int_{\Gamma^{n}_{h}}(\mathbf{z}\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}\mathbf{P}_{h}\mathbf{v})\cdot\mathbf{u}\,ds_{h}
can be treated similarly. Using (
34
) and (
35
) we obtain

\begin{split}\Big|\int_{\Gamma^{n}_{h}}(\mathbf{z}\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}\mathbf{P}_{h}\mathbf{u})\cdot\mathbf{v}&\,ds_{h}\Big|\lesssim\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}\mathbf{P}_{h}\mathbf{u}\|_{\Gamma_{h}^{n}}\|\mathbf{P}_{h}\mathbf{z}\|_{L^{4}(\Gamma_{h}^{n})}\|\mathbf{P}_{h}\mathbf{v}\|_{L^{4}(\Gamma_{h}^{n})}\\
&\lesssim\|\mathbf{P}_{h}\mathbf{z}\|_{L^{4}(\Gamma_{h}^{n})}\|\mathbf{P}_{h}\mathbf{u}\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{h}^{n})}\|\mathbf{P}_{h}\mathbf{v}\|_{\Gamma_{h}^{n}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\mathbf{P}_{h}\mathbf{v}\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{h}^{n})}^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\lesssim\|\mathbf{P}_{h}\mathbf{z}\|_{L^{4}(\Gamma_{h}^{n})}|\!|\!|\mathbf{u}|\!|\!|_{U^{n}}|\!|\!|\mathbf{v}|\!|\!|_{U^{n}}.\end{split}

(47)

Combining this with the same estimate for the other trilinear term and with (
46
) we obtain the result (
45
).
∎

4.3
Stability estimate

First we derive a stability result for the discrete velocity. Using this stability result and a suitable discrete inf-sup property, we then derive a stability estimate for the discrete pressure in Theorem
8
.

Theorem 7
.

A solution of (
9
) satisfies the following estimate:

\|\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n}\|^{2}_{\Gamma^{n}_{h}}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|\mathbf{P}_{h}\mathbf{u}_{h}^{k}-\mathbf{u}_{h}^{k-1}\|^{2}_{\Gamma^{k}_{h}}+\Delta t\sum_{k=1}^{n}\big(|\!|\!|\mathbf{u}_{h}^{k}|\!|\!|^{2}_{U^{k}}+2\rho_{p}\|\mathbf{n}_{h}\cdot\nabla p_{h}^{k}\|^{2}_{\omega_{\Gamma}^{k}}\big)\\
\lesssim\exp(c\,t_{n})\left(\!\|\mathbf{u}_{h}^{0}\|^{2}_{\Gamma^{0}_{h}}\!+\Delta t|\!|\!|\mathbf{u}_{h}^{0}|\!|\!|_{U^{0}}^{2}+\Delta t\sum_{k=0}^{n}\|\mathbf{f}^{e,k}\|^{2}_{\Gamma^{k}_{h}}\right),

(48)

with
c
independent of
h
,
\Delta t
and
n
.

Proof.

We test (
28
) with
\mathbf{v}_{h}=\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n}
,
q_{h}=-\Delta tp^{n}_{h}
. Adding the two identities leads to

\tfrac{1}{2}(\|\mathbf{P}_{h}\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n}\|^{2}_{\Gamma^{n}_{h}}+\|\mathbf{P}_{h}\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n}-\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n-1}\|^{2}_{\Gamma^{n}_{h}})+\Delta t\,a_{n}(\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n-1};\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n},\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n})+\Delta t\rho_{p}\|\mathbf{n}_{h}\cdot\nabla p_{h}^{n}\|^{2}_{\omega_{\Gamma}^{n}}\\
=\tfrac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n-1}\|^{2}_{\Gamma^{n}_{h}}+\Delta t(\mathbf{f}^{e,n},\mathbf{P}_{h}\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n})_{\Gamma^{n}_{h}}.

(49)

We use the lower bound (
44
) and apply (
36c
):

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{P}_{h}\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n}\|^{2}_{\Gamma^{n}_{h}}

\displaystyle+\|\mathbf{P}_{h}\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n}-\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n-1}\|^{2}_{\Gamma^{n}_{h}}+{2\Delta t}\big(|\!|\!|\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n}|\!|\!|_{U^{n}}^{2}+\rho_{p}\|\mathbf{n}_{h}\cdot\nabla p_{h}^{n}\|^{2}_{\omega_{\Gamma}^{n}}\big)

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n-1}\|^{2}_{\Gamma^{n}_{h}}+2\xi_{h}\Delta t\|\mathbf{P}_{h}\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n}\|^{2}_{\Gamma^{n}_{h}}+\Delta t\|\mathbf{P}_{h}\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n}\|^{2}_{\Gamma^{n}_{h}}+\Delta t\|\mathbf{f}^{e,n}\|^{2}_{\Gamma^{n}_{h}}

(50)

\displaystyle\leq(1+c\,\Delta t)\|\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n-1}\|^{2}_{\Gamma^{n-1}_{h}}+\tfrac{1}{2}\Delta t\rho_{u}\|\mathbf{n}_{h}^{n-1}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n-1}\|_{\mathcal{O}_{\delta}(\Gamma^{n-1}_{h})}^{2}+C\Delta t\|\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n}\|^{2}_{\Gamma^{n}_{h}}+\Delta t\|\mathbf{f}^{e,n}\|^{2}_{\Gamma^{n}_{h}}.

Due to (
24d
) we have

\begin{split}\tfrac{1}{2}\Delta t|\!|\!|\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n}|\!|\!|_{U^{n}}^{2}&\geq\tfrac{1}{2}\Delta t\,\tau\|\widetilde{\mathbf{n}}_{h}\cdot\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n}\|^{2}_{\Gamma^{n}_{h}}\geq\tfrac{1}{2}\Delta t\,\tau\|\mathbf{n}_{h}\cdot\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n}\|^{2}_{\Gamma^{n}_{h}}-c\Delta t\,\tau\,h^{2q}\|\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n}\|^{2}_{\Gamma^{n}_{h}}\\
&\geq\|\mathbf{n}_{h}\cdot\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n}\|^{2}_{\Gamma^{n}_{h}}-c\Delta th^{2q-2}\|\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n}\|^{2}_{\Gamma^{n}_{h}}.\end{split}

(51)

Using this and
\|\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n}\|^{2}_{\Gamma^{n}_{h}}=\|\mathbf{P}_{h}\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n}\|^{2}_{\Gamma^{n}_{h}}+\|\mathbf{n}_{h}\cdot\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n}\|^{2}_{\Gamma^{n}_{h}}
in (
50
) we obtain

\|\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n}\|^{2}_{\Gamma^{n}_{h}}+\|\mathbf{P}_{h}\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n}-\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n-1}\|^{2}_{\Gamma^{n}_{h}}+\tfrac{3}{2}\Delta t|\!|\!|\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n}|\!|\!|_{U^{n}}^{2}+2\Delta t\rho_{p}\|\mathbf{n}_{h}\cdot\nabla p_{h}^{n}\|^{2}_{\omega_{\Gamma}^{n}}\\
\leq(1+c\,\Delta t)\|\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n-1}\|^{2}_{\Gamma^{n-1}_{h}}+\tfrac{1}{2}\Delta t|\!|\!|\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n-1}|\!|\!|_{U^{n-1}}^{2}+C\Delta t\|\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n}\|^{2}_{\Gamma^{n}_{h}}+\Delta t\|\mathbf{f}^{e,n}\|^{2}_{\Gamma^{n}_{h}}.

(52)

We sum up the inequalities for
n=1,\dots,k
, and with
c^{\ast}=c+C
we get

\|\mathbf{u}_{h}^{k}\|^{2}_{\Gamma^{k}_{h}}+\sum_{n=1}^{k}\|\mathbf{P}_{h}\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n}-\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n-1}\|^{2}_{\Gamma^{n}_{h}}+\Delta t\sum_{n=1}^{k}\big(|\!|\!|\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n}|\!|\!|_{U^{n}}^{2}+2\rho_{p}\|\mathbf{n}_{h}\cdot\nabla p_{h}^{n}\|^{2}_{\omega_{\Gamma}^{n}}\big)\\
\leq(1+c\Delta t)\|\mathbf{u}_{h}^{0}\|^{2}_{\Gamma^{0}_{h}}+\tfrac{1}{2}\Delta t|\!|\!|\mathbf{u}_{h}^{0}|\!|\!|_{U^{0}}^{2}+\Delta t\sum_{n=0}^{k}c^{\ast}\|\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n}\|^{2}_{\Gamma^{n}_{h}}+\Delta t\sum_{n=0}^{k}\|\mathbf{f}^{e,n}\|^{2}_{\Gamma^{n}_{h}}.

Finally, we shift the term
\Delta t\,c^{\ast}\|\mathbf{u}_{h}^{k}\|^{2}_{\Gamma^{k}_{h}}
from the right-hand side to the left hand side and apply, for
\Delta t\leq(2c^{\ast})^{-1}
, a discrete Gronwall inequality.
∎

We now derive a stability bound for the discrete pressure. The analysis is based on results derived in

We use the scaled

,

\begin{split}\|p_{h}^{n}\|_{1,\omega_{\Gamma}^{n},h}&\lesssim\frac{h}{\Delta t}\|\mathbf{P}_{h}\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n}-\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n-1}\|_{\Gamma_{h}^{n}}+h\|\mathbf{f}^{e,n}\|_{\Gamma_{h}^{n}}+\rho_{p}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\mathbf{n}_{h}\cdot\nabla p_{h}^{n}\|_{\omega_{\Gamma}^{n}}\\
&\quad+|\!|\!|\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n-1}|\!|\!|_{U^{n-1}}^{2}+|\!|\!|\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n}|\!|\!|_{U^{n}}^{2}+|\!|\!|\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n}|\!|\!|_{U^{n}}.\end{split}

(60)

Summing over

, yields

\displaystyle\Delta t\sum_{n=1}^{k}\|p_{h}^{k}\|_{1,\omega_{\Gamma}^{k},h}

\displaystyle\lesssim{h}\sum_{n=1}^{k}\|\mathbf{P}_{h}\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n}-\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n-1}\|_{\Gamma_{h}^{n}}+h\Delta t\sum_{n=1}^{k}\|\mathbf{f}^{e,n}\|_{\Gamma_{h}^{n}}

(61)

\displaystyle+\Delta t\sum_{n=1}^{k}\rho_{p}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\mathbf{n}_{h}\cdot\nabla p_{h}^{n}\|_{\omega_{\Gamma}^{n}}\quad+\Delta t\sum_{n=0}^{k}|\!|\!|\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n}|\!|\!|_{U^{n}}^{2}+\Delta t\sum_{n=0}^{k}|\!|\!|\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n}|\!|\!|_{U^{n}}.

We use the estimate (
48
), with upper bound denoted by
\mathbf{F}_{n}
, and
k\lesssim 1/\Delta t
. The different terms on the right-hand side can be bounded as follows, using Cauchy-Schwarz,

\displaystyle h\sum_{n=1}^{k}\|\mathbf{P}_{h}\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n}-\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n-1}\|_{\Gamma_{h}^{n}}\lesssim\frac{h}{\sqrt{\Delta t}}\big(\sum_{n=1}^{k}\|\mathbf{P}_{h}\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n}-\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n-1}\|_{\Gamma_{h}^{n}}^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\frac{h}{\sqrt{\Delta t}}\mathbf{F}_{k}^{\frac{1}{2}},

(62)

\displaystyle h\Delta t\sum_{n=1}^{k}\|\mathbf{f}^{e,n}\|_{\Gamma_{h}^{n}}+\Delta t\sum_{n=1}^{k}\rho_{p}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\mathbf{n}_{h}\cdot\nabla p_{h}^{n}\|_{\omega_{\Gamma}^{n}}

\displaystyle\lesssim h\big(\Delta t\sum_{n=1}^{k}\|\mathbf{f}^{e,n}\|_{\Gamma_{h}^{n}}^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\big(\Delta t\sum_{n=1}^{k}\rho_{p}\|\mathbf{n}_{h}\cdot\nabla p_{h}^{n}\|_{\omega_{\Gamma}^{n}}^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\mathbf{F}_{k}^{\frac{1}{2}},

\displaystyle\Delta t\sum_{n=0}^{k}|\!|\!|\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n}|\!|\!|_{U^{n}}^{2}+\Delta t\sum_{n=0}^{k}|\!|\!|\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n}|\!|\!|_{U^{n}}\lesssim\mathbf{F}_{k}+\mathbf{F}_{k}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Combining these estimates completes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

We briefly explain why our analysis yields
a uniform bound if
h^{2}\lesssim\Delta t
.
First note that (
55
)–(
56
) implies the discrete inf-sup property. In the stability analysis of the discrete pressure we do not use this discrete inf-sup property. We rather use the specific choice of the function
\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{h}
in (

56

\|\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\|_{\Gamma_{h}^{n}}\lesssim h\|p_{h}^{n}\|_{1,\omega_{\Gamma}^{n},h}

\|\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\|_{\Gamma_{h}^{n}}\lesssim|\!|\!|\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{h}|\!|\!|_{U^{n}}\lesssim\|p_{h}^{n}\|_{1,\omega_{\Gamma}^{n},h}
. Due to this we get the factor

in front of the time difference term

in (

),
which leads to
\frac{h}{\sqrt{\Delta t}}
, instead of
\frac{1}{\sqrt{\Delta t}}
in (

.
It looks plausible that this approach can be used to improve the suboptimal pressure stability estimate in

as well.

Remark 4.3
.

The treatment of non-homogeneous divergence condition in (
4
) involves handling the
(f^{e,n},p_{h})_{\Gamma_{h}^{n}}
term on the right-hand side of eq. (
49
). Standard arguments would result in an additional term
on the right hand side of the stability bound, which involves the quantity

\|f\|_{\ast}=\max\limits_{n}\sup_{q_{h}\in Q_{h}^{n}}\frac{(f^{e,n},q_{h})_{\Gamma_{h}^{n}}}{\|q_{h}\|_{1,\omega_{\Gamma}^{n},h}}

.

The nonlinear term requires a more careful handling, which is carried out below.

Lemma 10
.

It holds

|I_{3}|\lesssim(\Delta t+h^{q})\|\mathbf{v}_{h}\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma^{n}_{h})}.

Proof.

Recall that the spatial-normal extension of
\mathbf{u}
to
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{G})
is denoted by
\mathbf{u}
, too. We start with estimating the differences between the quadratic nonlinear terms, i.e. the third and fourth terms in

With very similar arguments we obtain for the third term in

For the approximate surface

\begin{split}&\left|\int_{\Gamma^{n}_{h}}(\mathbf{u}^{n-1}\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}\mathbf{P}_{h}\mathbf{v}_{h})\cdot\mathbf{u}^{n}\,ds_{h}-\int_{\Gamma^{n}}(\mathbf{u}^{n-1}\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}\mathbf{P}\mathbf{v}_{h}^{\ell})\cdot\mathbf{u}^{n}\,ds\right|\\

in (

\int_{\Gamma^{n}}(\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{v}_{h}^{\ell})\mathbf{u}^{n-1}\cdot\mathbf{H}\mathbf{u}^{n}\,ds=-\int_{\Gamma^{n}}(\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{v}_{h}^{\ell})\mathbf{n}\cdot\nabla(\mathbf{P}\mathbf{u}^{n-1})\mathbf{u}^{n}\,ds.

4.5
Error estimates

In this section we derive a bound for the velocity error
\mathbb{E}^{n}:=\mathbf{u}^{n}-\mathbf{u}^{n}_{h}\in H^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{\delta}(\Gamma^{n}_{h}))
and for the pressure error
r^{n}:=p^{n}-p^{n}_{h}
,
r^{n}\in H^{1}(\omega_{\Gamma}^{n})
. From (
28
) and (
65
) we get the error equation, for arbitrary
\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{U}^{n}_{h}
,

\tfrac{1}{\Delta t}(\mathbb{E}^{n}-\mathbb{E}^{n-1},\mathbf{P}_{h}\mathbf{v}_{h})_{\Gamma_{h}^{n}}+c_{n}(\mathbb{E}^{n-1};\mathbf{u}^{n},\mathbf{v}_{h})+a_{n}(\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n-1};\mathbb{E}^{n},\mathbf{v}_{h})+b_{n}(r^{n},\mathbf{v}_{h})=\mathcal{E}_{C}^{n}(\mathbf{v}_{h})+\delta_{f}^{n}(\mathbf{v}_{h}),

(74)

with the data error term
\delta_{f}^{n}(\mathbf{v}_{h}):=\int_{\Gamma^{n}}\mathbf{f}^{n}\cdot\mathbf{v}_{h}^{\ell}\,ds-\int_{\Gamma_{h}^{n}}\mathbf{f}^{e,n}\mathbf{P}_{h}\cdot\mathbf{v}_{h}^{\ell}\,ds_{h}
. Let
\mathbf{u}_{I}^{n}\in\mathbf{U}_{h}^{n}
and
p_{I}^{n}\in Q_{h}^{n}
be the
nodal interpolants for
\mathbf{u}^{n}
in
\mathcal{O}_{\delta}(\Gamma^{n}_{h})
and
p^{n}
in
\omega_{\Gamma}^{n}
, respectively.

We proceed using standard techniques, based on splitting the error
\mathbb{E}^{n}
into approximation and finite element parts,

\mathbb{E}^{n}=\underset{\mbox{$\mathbf{e}_{I}^{n}$}}{\underbrace{(\mathbf{u}^{n}-\mathbf{u}^{n}_{I})}}+\underset{\mbox{$\mathbf{e}^{n}_{h}$}}{\underbrace{(\mathbf{u}^{n}_{I}-\mathbf{u}^{n}_{h})}}.

Using

a_{n}(\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n-1};\mathbb{E}^{n},\mathbf{v}_{h})=\widehat{a}_{n}(\mathbf{e}_{I}^{n},\mathbf{v}_{h})+\widehat{a}_{n}(\mathbf{e}_{h}^{n},\mathbf{v}_{h})+c_{n}(\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n-1};\mathbb{E}^{n},\mathbf{v}_{h})

equation (
74
) can be reformulated as

\tfrac{1}{\Delta t}(\mathbf{e}^{n}_{h}-\mathbf{e}_{h}^{n-1},\mathbf{P}_{h}\mathbf{v}_{h})_{\Gamma_{h}^{n}}+\widehat{a}_{n}(\mathbf{e}^{n}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})+b_{n}(p^{n}_{I}-p^{n}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})=\mathcal{E}_{C}^{n}(\mathbf{v}_{h})+\mathcal{E}_{I}^{n}(\mathbf{v}_{h})+\mathcal{C}^{n}(\mathbf{v}_{h})+\delta_{f}^{n}(\mathbf{v}_{h}),

(75)

with the interpolation and nonlinear terms

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}_{I}^{n}(\mathbf{v}_{h})&:=-\tfrac{1}{\Delta t}(\mathbf{e}_{I}^{n}-\mathbf{e}_{I}^{n-1},\mathbf{P}_{h}\mathbf{v}_{h})_{\Gamma_{h}^{n}}-\widehat{a}_{n}(\mathbf{e}_{I}^{n},\mathbf{v}_{h})-b_{n}(p^{n}-p^{n}_{I},\mathbf{v}_{h}),\\
\mathcal{C}^{n}(\mathbf{v}_{h})&:=-c_{n}(\mathbb{E}^{n-1};\mathbf{u}^{n},\mathbf{v}_{h})-c_{n}(\mathbf{u}^{n-1}_{h};\mathbb{E}^{n},\mathbf{v}_{h}).\end{split}

An estimate for the interpolation terms is given in the following lemma.

Lemma 12
.

It holds

|\mathcal{E}_{I}^{n}(\mathbf{v}_{h})|\lesssim(h^{m+1}+h^{q})\,|\!|\!|\mathbf{v}_{h}|\!|\!|_{U^{n}},\quad\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{U}_{h}^{n}.

(76)

Proof.

For this to hold it is important to use the nodal interpolation. This operator has the key property that it interpolates velocity in the fixed (i.e., time independent) finite element space
\mathbf{U}_{h}
, which allows to shift time differentiation from outside the interpolation operator to inside. Using this approach, which is standard in the analysis of parabolic problems on Euclidean domains, one avoids a factor
\tfrac{1}{\Delta t}
while handling the first term in
\mathcal{E}_{I}^{n}(\mathbf{v}_{h})
, cf.
[
26
, Lemma 12]
.
Estimates of the remaining two terms in

n=1,\dots,k
, apply the discrete Gronwall inequality and use the discrete stability estimate
\Delta t\sum_{n=1}^{k}|\!|\!|\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n-1}|\!|\!|_{U^{n-1}}\lesssim\big(\Delta t\sum_{n=1}^{k}|\!|\!|\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n-1}|\!|\!|_{U^{n-1}}^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C
to get

Q_{e,k}:=\|\mathbf{e}_{h}^{k}\|^{2}_{\Gamma^{k}_{h}}+\tfrac{1}{8}\Delta t\sum_{n=1}^{k}\big(|\!|\!|\mathbf{e}_{h}^{n}|\!|\!|_{U^{n}}^{2}+\rho_{p}\|\mathbf{n}_{h}\cdot\nabla(p_{h}^{n}-p^{n}_{I})\|^{2}_{\omega_{\Gamma}^{n}}\big)\lesssim\exp(c\,t_{k})(\Delta t^{2}+h^{2m+2}+h^{2q}).

(93)

The triangle inequality and standard FE interpolation properties give

\begin{split}\|\mathbb{E}^{k}\|^{2}_{\Gamma^{k}_{h}}&+\tfrac{1}{8}\Delta t\sum_{n=1}^{k}\big(|\!|\!|\mathbb{E}^{n}|\!|\!|_{U^{n}}^{2}+\rho_{p}\|\mathbf{n}_{h}\cdot\nabla r^{n}\|^{2}_{\omega_{\Gamma}^{n}}\big)\\
&\leq 2Q_{e,k}+2\|\mathbf{e}^{k}\|^{2}_{\Gamma^{k}_{h}}+\tfrac{1}{4}\Delta t\sum_{n=1}^{k}\big(|\!|\!|\mathbf{e}^{n}|\!|\!|_{U^{n}}^{2}+\rho_{p}\|\mathbf{n}_{h}\cdot\nabla(p^{n}-p^{n}_{I})\|^{2}_{\omega_{\Gamma}^{n}}\big)~\lesssim Q_{e,k}+Ch^{2m+2}.\end{split}

This completes the proof of (
79
).

We now derive a pressure error bound. The approach is similar to the one used in the proof of Theorem
8
. First note that if we do not drop the term
\tfrac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{P}_{h}\mathbf{e}_{h}^{n}-\mathbf{e}_{h}^{n-1}\|_{\Gamma_{h}^{n}}^{2}
when going from (
92
) to (
93
) we get

\tilde{Q}_{e,k}:=Q_{e,k}+\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{k}\|\mathbf{P}_{h}\mathbf{e}_{h}^{n}-\mathbf{e}_{h}^{n-1}\|_{\Gamma_{h}^{n}}^{2}\lesssim\exp(c\,t_{k})(\Delta t^{2}+h^{2m+2}+h^{2q})=:F_{k}.

(94)

From (
91
) we obtain, with the same arguments as in the proof of Theorem
8
:

\Delta t\sum_{n=1}^{k}\|p_{I}^{n}-p_{h}^{n}\|_{1,\omega_{\Gamma}^{n},h}\lesssim\left(\tfrac{h}{\sqrt{\Delta t}}+1\right)\tilde{Q}_{e,k}^{\frac{1}{2}}+Q_{e,k}+\Delta t+h^{m+1}+h^{q}+\Delta t\sum_{n=1}^{k}|\!|\!|\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n-1}|\!|\!|_{U^{n-1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}(h^{m+1}+|\!|\!|\mathbf{e}_{h}^{n}|\!|\!|_{U^{n}})

Similar to above, using discrete stability for the last term we get

\Delta t\sum_{n=1}^{k}|\!|\!|\mathbf{u}_{h}^{n-1}|\!|\!|_{U^{n-1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}(h^{m+1}+|\!|\!|\mathbf{e}_{h}^{n}|\!|\!|_{U^{n}})\lesssim h^{m+1}+Q_{e,k}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Recall that
h^{2}\lesssim\Delta t
. Hence, we obtain

\Delta t\sum_{n=1}^{k}\|p_{I}^{n}-p_{h}^{n}\|_{1,\omega_{\Gamma}^{n},h}\lesssim\tilde{Q}_{e,k}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\tilde{Q}_{e,k}+\Delta t+h^{m+1}+h^{q}\lesssim F_{k}^{\frac{1}{2}}+F_{k}\lesssim F_{k}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Finally we combine this result with the interpolation error estimate

\|p_{I}^{n}-p^{n}\|_{1,\omega_{\Gamma}^{n},h}=h^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla(p_{I}^{n}-p^{n})\|_{\omega_{\Gamma}^{n}}\lesssim h^{m+\frac{1}{2}}\|p\|_{H^{m}(\omega_{\Gamma}^{n})}\lesssim h^{m+1}\|p\|_{H^{m}(\Gamma)}

which then proves the result (
80
).
∎

Both bounds in (
79
) and in (
80
) are optimal in terms of convergence order in
\Delta t
and
h
.

\nabla(\mathbf{w}\cdot\mathbf{n})^{T}=\mathbf{w}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{n}+\mathbf{n}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{w}
we get

\begin{split}&\int_{\Gamma^{n}}w_{N}^{n}\mathbf{v}_{h}^{\ell}\cdot\mathbf{H}\mathbf{u}^{n}\,ds=\int_{\Gamma^{n}}w_{N}^{n}\nabla(\mathbf{P}\mathbf{v}_{h}^{\ell}\cdot\mathbf{n})\cdot\mathbf{u}^{n}\,ds-\int_{\Gamma^{n}}w_{N}^{n}\mathbf{n}\cdot\nabla(\mathbf{P}\mathbf{v}_{h}^{\ell})\mathbf{u}^{n}\,ds\\
&=\int_{\Gamma^{n}}w_{N}^{n}\nabla_{\Gamma}(\mathbf{v}_{h}^{\ell}\cdot\mathbf{P}\mathbf{n})\cdot\mathbf{u}^{n}\,ds-\int_{\Gamma^{n}}w_{N}^{n}\mathbf{n}\cdot\nabla(\mathbf{P}\mathbf{v}_{h}^{\ell})\mathbf{u}^{n}\,ds=-\int_{\Gamma^{n}}w_{N}^{n}\mathbf{n}\cdot\nabla(\mathbf{P}\mathbf{v}_{h}^{\ell})\mathbf{u}^{n}\,ds.\end{split}

(99)

Using the definition
\mathbf{H}_{h}=\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}\overline{\mathbf{n}}_{h}=\mathbf{P}_{h}\nabla\overline{\mathbf{n}}_{h}^{\ell}\mathbf{P}_{h}
, the transformation formula (
31
) applied to
\overline{\mathbf{n}}_{h}
,
\|d\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{h}^{n})}\lesssim h^{q+1}
,
\|\mathbf{P}-\mathbf{P}_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{h}^{n})}\lesssim h^{q}
, the bound for the surface measure change in (
95
) and the smoothness of
\mathbf{u}
we obtain

\left|\int_{\Gamma^{n}_{h}}w_{N}^{e,n}\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot\mathbf{H}_{h}\mathbf{u}^{n}\,ds_{h}-\int_{\Gamma^{n}}w_{N}^{n}\mathbf{v}_{h}^{\ell}\cdot\mathbf{P}\nabla\overline{\mathbf{n}}_{h}^{\ell}\mathbf{P}\mathbf{u}^{n}\,ds\right|\lesssim h^{q}\|\mathbf{v}_{h}\|_{\Gamma_{h}^{n}}.

(100)

We apply partial integration as in (
99
), which yields

\int_{\Gamma^{n}}w_{N}^{n}\mathbf{v}_{h}^{\ell}\cdot\mathbf{P}\nabla\overline{\mathbf{n}}_{h}^{\ell}\mathbf{P}\mathbf{u}^{n}\,ds=\int_{\Gamma^{n}}w_{N}^{n}\nabla_{\Gamma}(\mathbf{v}_{h}^{\ell}\cdot\mathbf{P}\overline{\mathbf{n}}_{h}^{\ell})\cdot\mathbf{u}^{n}\,ds-\int_{\Gamma^{n}}w_{N}^{n}\overline{\mathbf{n}}_{h}^{\ell}\cdot\nabla(\mathbf{P}\mathbf{v}_{h}^{\ell})\mathbf{u}^{n}\,ds.

(101)

For the first term on the right-hand side we apply partial integration and using
\|\mathbf{P}\overline{\mathbf{n}}_{h}^{\ell}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma^{n})}=\|\mathbf{P}(\overline{\mathbf{n}}_{h}^{\ell}-\mathbf{n})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma^{n})}\lesssim h^{q}
, cf. (
18
), we get

\left|\int_{\Gamma^{n}}w_{N}^{n}\nabla_{\Gamma}(\mathbf{v}_{h}^{\ell}\cdot\mathbf{P}\overline{\mathbf{n}}_{h}^{\ell})\cdot\mathbf{u}^{n}\,ds\right|=\left|\int_{\Gamma^{n}}(\mathbf{v}_{h}^{\ell}\cdot\mathbf{P}\overline{\mathbf{n}}_{h}^{\ell}){\,\operatorname{div_{\Gamma}}}(w_{N}^{n}\mathbf{u}^{n})\,ds\right|\lesssim h^{q}\|\mathbf{v}_{h}\|_{\Gamma_{h}^{n}}.

(102)

With the results (
99
)-(
102
) we get, using again (
18
),

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int_{\Gamma^{n}}w_{N}^{n}\mathbf{n}\cdot\nabla(\mathbf{P}\mathbf{v}_{h}^{\ell})\mathbf{u}^{n}\,ds-\int_{\Gamma^{n}}w_{N}^{n}\overline{\mathbf{n}}_{h}^{\ell}\cdot\nabla(\mathbf{P}\mathbf{v}_{h}^{\ell})\mathbf{u}^{n}\,ds\right|+h^{q}\|\mathbf{v}_{h}\|_{\Gamma_{h}^{n}}

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\Gamma^{n}}w_{N}^{n}(\mathbf{n}-\overline{\mathbf{n}}_{h}^{\ell})\cdot\nabla(\mathbf{P}\mathbf{v}_{h}^{\ell})\mathbf{u}^{n}\,ds\right|+h^{q}\|\mathbf{v}_{h}\|_{\Gamma_{h}^{n}}\lesssim h^{q}\|\mathbf{v}_{h}\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{h}^{n})},

which completes the proof.

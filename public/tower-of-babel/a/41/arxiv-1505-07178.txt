Abstract




Abstract
We improve a known result on the strong consistency of M-estimates of the regression parameters in a linear model for independent and identically distributed random errors under some mild conditions.

Keywords
linear model, strong consistency, M-estimate

MR(2010) Subject Classification
62F12

We consider the linear model

\displaystyle Y_{i}=x_{i}^{\prime}\beta_{0}+e_{i},i=1,2,\cdots,n,

(1)

where
x_{i}
are
p\times 1
known design vectors,
\beta_{0}
is a
p\times 1
unknown vector of regression coefficients and
\{e_{i}\}
are error variables. The M-estimate
\hat{\beta}_{n}
of
\beta_{0}
is defined by minimizing

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\rho(Y_{i}-x_{i}^{\prime}\beta),

(2)

where
\rho
is a convex function. Important examples include Huber’s estimate with
\rho(x)=(x^{2}I(|x|\leq c))/2+(c|x|-c^{2}/2)I(|x|>c)
,
c>0
, where
I(A)
is the indicator function of the set
A
, the
\mathscr{L}^{q}
regression estimate with
\rho(x)=|x|^{q}
,
1\leq q\leq 2
, and regression quantiles with
\rho(x)=\rho_{\alpha}(x)=\alpha x^{+}+(1-\alpha)(-x)^{+}
,
0<\alpha<1
, where
x^{+}=\max(x,0)
. In particular, if
q=1
or
\alpha=1/2
, then the minimizer of (
2
) is called the least absolute deviation estimate. There is a substantial amount of work concerning asymptotic properties of M-estimates.

We assume that
\rho
is a non-monotonic convex function on
\mathbb{R}
with right and left derivatives
\psi_{+}
and
\psi_{-}
. Choose
\psi
such that
\psi_{-}(u)\leq\psi(u)\leq\psi_{+}(u)
for all
u\in\mathbb{R}
. Write
S_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}x_{i}^{\prime}
,
d_{n}=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}x_{i}^{\prime}S_{n}^{-1}x_{i}
, and assume that
S_{n_{0}}>0
for some integer
n_{0}
and that
n\geq n_{0}
.

Zhao (2002) established the following result.

Theorem A.
Assume that
\{e_{i},i\geq 1\}
is a sequence of independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) random variables. Suppose there exist positive constants
\Delta
,
C_{0}
,
C_{1}
and
\delta\in(0,1]
such that the following conditions are satisfied:

\displaystyle\psi(u+h)-\psi(u)\leq C_{0}~~\textrm{for}~~h\in(0,\Delta)~~\textrm{and}~~u\in\mathbb{R},

(3)

\displaystyle\textrm{E}\psi(e_{1})=0,~~~|\textrm{E}\psi(e_{1}+u)|\geq C_{1}|u|~~\textrm{ for}~~|u|<\Delta,

(4)

d_{n}=O(n^{-\delta}).

Assume further that
\textrm{E}|\psi(e_{1})|^{1/\delta}<\infty
if
0<\delta<1
and
\textrm{E}|\psi(e_{1})|^{q}<\infty
for some
q>1
if
\delta=1
. Then
\hat{\beta}_{n}\rightarrow\beta_{0}
a.s as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

We will further discuss the strong consistency of
\hat{\beta}_{n}
and obtain the following result.

Theorem 1.
In model (
1
), assume that
\{e_{i},i\geq 1\}
is a sequence of i.i.d. random variables. Suppose that conditions (
3
) and (
4
) are satisfied and

\displaystyle d_{n}=O(n^{-1}).

Then
\hat{\beta}_{n}\rightarrow\beta_{0}
a.s as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

Proof
For the technical proof, see the Appendix.

Remark.
In Theorem A,
\textrm{E}|\psi(e_{1})|^{q}<\infty
for some
q>1
is needed for the case
\delta=1
. Theorem 1 is obtained without the condition
\textrm{E}|\psi(e_{1})|^{q}<\infty
for some
q>1
in the case
\delta=1
. Theorem 1 improves the result of Theorem A.

Appendix

To prove the main results of the paper, we need the following lemmas.

Lemma 1
(c.f. Bennett, 1962). Assume that
\{X_{n},n\geq 1\}
is a sequence of independent random variables such that
\textrm{E}X_{n}=0
and
|X_{n}|\leq b
for all
n\geq 1
and some
b>0
. Denote
B_{n}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\textrm{E}X_{i}^{2}
. Then for all
\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle P\Big(\Big|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\Big|>\varepsilon\Big)\leq 2\exp\Big\{-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2b\varepsilon+2B_{n}^{2}}\Big\}.

Lemma 2
(c.f. Choi and Sung, 1987). Let
\{X_{n},n\geq 1\}
be a sequence of i.i.d. random variables with
\textrm{E}X_{1}=0
. Assume that
\{a_{ni},1\leq i\leq n,n\geq 1\}
is an array of constants. If
\max_{1\leq i\leq n}|a_{ni}|=O(n^{-1})
, then

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}a_{ni}X_{i}\rightarrow 0~~\textrm{as}~~n\rightarrow\infty.

Proof of Theorem 1.
Let
x_{ni}=S_{n}^{-1/2}x_{i}
,
\beta_{n0}=S_{n}^{1/2}\beta_{0}
and
\hat{\beta}_{n}^{*}=S_{n}^{1/2}\hat{\beta}_{n}
. Then

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}x_{ni}x_{ni}^{\prime}=I_{p},~~\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}||x_{ni}||^{2}=p,~~d_{n}=\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq n}||x_{ni}||^{2},

(A.1)

where
I_{p}
is the
p\times p
identity matrix, and
||\cdot||
is the Euclidean norm on
\mathbb{R}^{p}
.

Model (
1
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle Y_{i}=x_{ni}^{\prime}\beta_{n0}+e_{i},~~i=1,2,\cdots,n

(A.2)

and

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\rho(Y_{i}-x_{ni}^{\prime}\hat{\beta}_{n}^{*})=\min\limits_{\beta}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\rho(Y_{i}-x_{ni}^{\prime}\beta).

(A.3)

Without loss of generality, we assume that the true parameter
\beta_{0}=0
in model (
1
), i.e.,
\beta_{n0}=0
in (
A.2
). Denote the unit sphere
U=\{\beta:\beta\in\mathbb{R}^{p},||\beta||=1\}
. Let
\varepsilon>0
be any given constant. Without loss of generality, it can be assumed that
2C_{2}\varepsilon<\Delta
. Define

\displaystyle D_{n}(\beta)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\{\rho(e_{i}-x_{ni}^{\prime}\beta)-\rho(e_{i})\},~~\beta\in\mathbb{R}^{p}

and

\displaystyle D_{n}(\varepsilon n^{1/2}\gamma)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\{\rho(e_{i}-\varepsilon n^{1/2}x_{ni}^{\prime}\gamma)-\rho(e_{i})\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{w_{ni}^{\prime}\gamma}\{\psi(e_{i}+t)-\psi(e_{i})\}dt+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}w_{ni}^{\prime}\gamma\psi(e_{i}),~~\gamma\in U,

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle I_{1n}(\gamma)+I_{2n}(\gamma),

where
w_{ni}=-\varepsilon n^{1/2}x_{ni}
. Hence

\displaystyle\inf\limits_{\gamma\in U}D_{n}(\varepsilon n^{1/2}\gamma)\geq\inf\limits_{\gamma\in U}I_{1n}(\gamma)+\inf\limits_{\gamma\in U}I_{2n}(\gamma)\geq\inf\limits_{\gamma\in U}I_{1n}(\gamma)-\sup\limits_{\gamma\in U}|I_{2n}(\gamma)|.

(A.4)

We can divide
U
into
N
parts,
U_{1}
,
U_{2}
,
\cdots
,
U_{N}
, such that the diameter of each part is less than
n^{-2}
and
N\leq(2n^{2}+1)^{p}
. Let
T_{j}
be the smallest close convex set covering
U_{j}
. For a fixed
T_{j}
, there are three cases as follows.

i)
w_{ni}^{\prime}\gamma\geq 0
for each
\gamma\in T_{j}
, then there exists a
\gamma_{ij}\in T_{j}
such that
w_{ni}^{\prime}\gamma_{ij}=\inf\{w_{ni}^{\prime}\gamma:\gamma\in T_{j}\}
.

ii)
w_{ni}^{\prime}\gamma\leq 0
for each
\gamma\in T_{j}
, then there exists a
\gamma_{ij}\in T_{j}
such that
w_{ni}^{\prime}\gamma_{ij}=\sup\{w_{ni}^{\prime}\gamma:\gamma\in T_{j}\}
.

iii)
w_{ni}^{\prime}\gamma>0
for some
\gamma\in T_{j}
, and
w_{ni}^{\prime}\gamma<0
for some
\gamma\in T_{j}
, then there exists a
\gamma_{ij}\in T_{j}
such that
w_{ni}^{\prime}\gamma_{ij}=0
.

Write

\displaystyle G(t)=\textrm{E}\psi(e_{i}+t),~~\Psi_{i}(t)=\psi(e_{i}+t)-\psi(e_{i})-G(t),~~t\in\mathbb{R}.

By the monotonicity of
\psi
,

\displaystyle\inf\limits_{\gamma\in U}I_{1n}(\gamma)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\inf\limits_{1\leq j\leq N}\inf\limits_{\gamma\in T_{j}}I_{1n}(\gamma)\geq\inf\limits_{1\leq j\leq N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{w_{ni}^{\prime}\gamma_{ij}}\{\psi(e_{i}+t)-\psi(e_{i})\}dt

(A.5)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\inf\limits_{1\leq j\leq N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{w_{ni}^{\prime}\gamma_{ij}}G(t)dt\Big\{1-\frac{|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{w_{ni}^{\prime}\gamma_{ij}}\Psi_{i}(t)dt|}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{w_{ni}^{\prime}\gamma_{ij}}G(t)dt}\Big\}.

Let
\gamma\in U_{j}
and
\gamma_{ij}\in T_{j}
. By (
A.1
) and the definition of
U_{j}
and
T_{j}
, for large enough
n
,

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}(x_{ni}^{\prime}\gamma_{ij})^{2}-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}(x_{ni}^{\prime}\gamma)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}(x_{ni}^{\prime}\gamma_{ij})^{2}-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}(x_{ni}^{\prime}\gamma)^{2}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}(\gamma_{ij}-\gamma)^{\prime}x_{ni}x_{ni}^{\prime}(\gamma_{ij}+\gamma)\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\parallel\gamma_{ij}-\gamma\parallel\parallel x_{ni}\parallel^{2}(\parallel\gamma_{ij}-\gamma\parallel+2\parallel\gamma\parallel)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}n^{-2}(n^{-2}+2)\parallel x_{ni}\parallel^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 3pn^{-2}<1/2,

combining (
A.1
), we obtain that for
1\leq j\leq N
,

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}(x_{ni}^{\prime}\gamma)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}(x_{ni}^{\prime}\gamma)^{2}-1/2=\gamma^{\prime}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}x_{ni}x_{ni}^{\prime}\gamma-1/2

(A.6)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\parallel\gamma\parallel^{2}-1/2=1/2.

By (
A note on the strong consistency of M-estimates in linear models
) and the selection of
\varepsilon
, for large enough
n
and for
i=1,2,\cdots,n
and
j=1,2,\cdots,N
,

\displaystyle|w_{ni}^{\prime}\gamma_{ij}|=|\varepsilon n^{1/2}x_{ni}^{\prime}\gamma_{ij}|\leq C_{2}\varepsilon\parallel\gamma_{ij}\parallel\leq C_{2}\varepsilon(1+n^{-2})<2C_{2}\varepsilon<\Delta.

It follows that by (
4
) and (
A.6
),

\displaystyle\inf\limits_{1\leq j\leq N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{w_{ni}^{\prime}\gamma ij}G(t)dt

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\inf\limits_{1\leq j\leq N}C_{1}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{w_{ni}^{\prime}\gamma_{ij}}tdt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf\limits_{1\leq j\leq N}\frac{C_{1}}{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}(w_{ni}^{\prime}\gamma_{ij})^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{C_{1}\varepsilon^{2}n}{4}.

For
1\leq j\leq N
,, denote
Y_{ni}^{(j)}=\int_{0}^{w_{ni}^{\prime}\gamma_{ij}}\Psi_{i}(t)dt
. By (
3
) and (
A.6
), one has that

\displaystyle|Y_{ni}^{(j)}|\leq 2C_{0}|w_{ni}^{\prime}\gamma_{ij}|<4C_{0}C_{2}\varepsilon=:C_{3}.

For
1\leq j\leq N
, we also obtain that by

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\textrm{Var}(Y_{ni}^{(j)})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\textrm{E}\{\int_{0}^{w_{ni}^{\prime}\gamma_{ij}}(\psi(e_{i}+t)-\psi(e_{i}))dt\}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{0}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}|w_{ni}^{\prime}\gamma_{ij}|\int_{0}^{w_{ni}^{\prime}\gamma_{ij}}G(t)dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C_{3}}{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{w_{ni}^{\prime}\gamma_{ij}}G(t)dt.

Define event
A_{n}
by

\displaystyle A_{n}:=\Big\{\sup\limits_{1\leq j\leq N}\frac{|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{w_{ni}^{\prime}\gamma_{ij}}\Psi_{i}(t)dt|}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{w_{ni}^{\prime}\gamma_{ij}}G(t)dt}\geq\frac{1}{2}\Big\}.

Noting the fact that
N\leq(2n^{2}+1)^{p}
and using Lemma 1, we have that

\displaystyle P(A_{n})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}P\Big(\Big|\sum_{i=1}^{n}Y_{ni}^{(j)}\Big|\geq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{w_{ni}^{\prime}\gamma_{ij}}G(t)dt\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\exp\Big\{-\frac{\frac{1}{4}(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{w_{ni}^{\prime}\gamma_{ij}}G(t)dt)^{2}}{2C_{3}\times\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{w_{ni}^{\prime}\gamma_{ij}}G(t)dt+2C_{3}\times\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{w_{ni}^{\prime}\gamma_{ij}}G(t)dt}\Big\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}2\exp\Big\{-\frac{1}{8C_{3}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{w_{ni}^{\prime}\gamma_{ij}}G(t)dt\Big\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2(2n^{2}+1)^{p}\exp(-C_{4}n),

thus
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}P(A_{n})<\infty
. By Borel-Cantelli lemma, it follows that
P(A_{n},i.o.)=0
. Hence with probability one for large enough
n
,

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{1\leq j\leq N}\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{w_{ni}^{\prime}\gamma_{ij}}\Psi_{i}(t)dt}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{w_{ni}^{\prime}\gamma_{ij}}G(t)dt}<\frac{1}{2},

which implies by (
A.5
) that with probability one for large enough
n
,

\displaystyle\inf\limits_{\gamma\in U}I_{1n}(\gamma)\geq\frac{C_{1}\varepsilon^{2}n}{8}.

(A.7)

Denote

\displaystyle x_{ni}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}x_{ni1}\\
x_{ni2}\\
\vdots\\
x_{nip}\end{array}\right),\gamma=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\gamma_{1}\\
\gamma_{2}\\
\vdots\\
\gamma_{p}\end{array}\right),~~a_{ni}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{x_{nik}}{n^{1/2}},1\leq i\leq n\\
0,i\geq n\end{array}\right.\textrm{for fixed}~~k=1,2,\cdots,p.

By (
A.1
) and
d_{n}=O(n^{-1})
, for fixed
k=1,2,\cdots,N
,
|x_{nik}|\leq\parallel x_{ni}\parallel\leq d_{n}^{1/2}\leq\sqrt{C_{2}}n^{-1/2}
and
\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{nik}^{2}=1
, thus

\displaystyle|a_{ni}|\leq\sqrt{C_{2}}n^{-1}~~\textrm{for}~~n\geq 1.

Using Lemma 2, it follows that

\displaystyle n^{-1/2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}x_{nik}\psi(e_{i})=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}a_{ni}\psi(e_{i})\rightarrow 0~~a.s.

Hence,

\displaystyle n^{-1}\sup\limits_{\gamma\in U}|I_{2n}(\gamma)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n^{-1}\sup\limits_{\gamma\in U}|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}w_{ni}^{\prime}\gamma\psi(e_{i})|=n^{-1}\sup\limits_{\gamma\in U}|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\varepsilon n^{1/2}x_{ni}^{\prime}\gamma\psi(e_{i})|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon n^{-1/2}\sup\limits_{\gamma\in U}\Big|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{p}x_{nik}\gamma_{k}\psi(e_{i})\Big|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon n^{-1/2}\sup\limits_{\gamma\in U}\Big|\sum\limits_{k=1}^{p}\Big(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}x_{nik}\psi(e_{i})\Big)\gamma_{k}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon n^{-1/2}\sup\limits_{\gamma\in U}\sqrt{\sum\limits_{k=1}^{p}\Big(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}x_{nik}\psi(e_{i})\Big)^{2}}\sqrt{\sum\limits_{k=1}^{p}\gamma_{k}^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon\sqrt{\sum\limits_{k=1}^{p}\Big(n^{-1/2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}x_{nik}\psi(e_{i})\Big)^{2}}\rightarrow 0~~a.s.,

which implies that with probability one for large enough
n
,

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{\gamma\in U}|I_{2n}(\gamma)|\leq\frac{C_{2}\varepsilon^{2}n}{16}.

(A.9)

Combining (
A.4
) with (
A.7
) and (
A.9
), we have that with probability one for large enough
n
,

\displaystyle\inf\limits_{\gamma\in U}D_{n}(\varepsilon n^{1/2}\gamma)\geq\frac{C_{2}\varepsilon^{2}n}{16}.

By the convexity of
D_{n}(\cdot)
,
D_{n}(0)=0
and the definition (
A.3
) of
\hat{\beta}_{n}^{*}
, it follow that

\displaystyle\Big\{\inf\limits_{\gamma\in U}D_{n}(\varepsilon n^{1/2}\gamma)>0\Big\}\subset\{\parallel\hat{\beta}_{n}^{*}\parallel\leq\varepsilon n^{1/2}\}.

Thus for any given
\varepsilon>0
, with probability one for large enough
n
,

\displaystyle\parallel\hat{\beta}_{n}^{*}\parallel\leq\varepsilon n^{1/2},

which implies that

\displaystyle n^{-1/2}\parallel\hat{\beta}_{n}^{*}\parallel\rightarrow 0~~a.s.~~\textrm{as}~~n\rightarrow\infty.

Denote
\zeta(A)
for the smallest eigenvalue of a positive definite matrices
A
. Based on the result that if
A
and
B
are two positive definite matrices of order
p
, then

\displaystyle\textrm{tr}(AB)\geq\mu(A)\zeta(B),

where
\mu(A)
is the largest eigenvalue of
A
(see Chen & Zhao, 1995). Now we take
M=n_{0}
and
n>n_{0}
so that
S_{n}\geq S_{M}>0
. By (
A note on the strong consistency of M-estimates in linear models
), one has that

\displaystyle\zeta(S_{M})(\zeta(S_{M}))^{-1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\zeta(S_{M})\textrm{tr}(S_{n}^{-1})\leq\textrm{tr}(S_{M}^{1/2}S_{n}^{-1}S_{M}^{1/2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\textrm{tr}(S_{n}^{-1}S_{M})=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{M}x_{i}^{\prime}S_{n}^{-1}x_{i}\leq Md_{n}\leq MC_{2}n^{-1},

and there exists a positive constant
C_{5}
such that
1\leq C_{5}n^{-1/2}\zeta(S_{n}^{1/2})
. It follows that

\displaystyle\parallel\hat{\beta}_{n}\parallel\leq C_{5}n^{-1/2}\parallel S_{n}^{1/2}\hat{\beta}_{n}\parallel=C_{5}n^{-1/2}\parallel\hat{\beta}_{n}^{*}\parallel\rightarrow 0~~a.s.~~\textrm{as}~~n\rightarrow\infty.

The proof of the theorem is completed.

Idempotent completion of certain n \bm{n} -exangulated categories † † footnotetext: Jian He was supported by the National Natural Science Foundation of China (Grant No. 12171230). Panyue Zhou was supported by the National Natural Science Foundation of China (Grant No. 11901190).
Jian He, Jing He, Panyue Zhou

Abstract
It was shown recently that an n n -extension closed subcategory 𝒜 \mathscr{A} of a Krull-Schmidt ( n + 2 ) (n+2) -angulated category has a natural structure of an n n -exangulated category. In this article, we prove that its idempotent completion 𝒜 ~ \widetilde{\mathscr{A}} admits an n n -exangulated structure. It is not only a generalization of the main result of Lin, but also gives an n n -exangulated category which is neither n n -exact nor ( n + 2 ) (n+2) -angulated in general. Keywords: idempotent completion; ( n + 2 ) (n+2) -angulated categories; n n -extension closed subcategories; n n -exangulated categories 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 18G80; 18E10



1
Introduction

Triangulated categories and exact categories are two fundamental structures in algebra, geometry and topology.
They are also important tools in many mathematical branches.
Nakaoka and Palu
[
NP
]
introduced the notion
of extriangulated categories, whose extriangulated structures are given by
\mathbb{E}
-triangles with some axioms. Triangulated categories and exact categories are extriangulated categories. There are a lot of examples of extriangulated categories which are neither triangulated categories nor exact categories, see
[
NP
,
ZZ
,
HZZ1
,
ZhZ
,
NP1
]
.

In
[
GKO
]
, Geiss, Keller and Oppermann introduced
(n+2)
-angulated categories for any positive integer
n
. These
are a “higher dimensional” analogue of triangulated categories,
in the sense that triangles are replaced by
(n+2)
-angles, that is, morphism sequences of length
(n+2)
. Thus a
3
-angulated category is precisely a triangulated category.
An important source of examples of
(n+2)
-angulated categories are certain cluster tilting subcategories of triangulated categories.
Jasso
[
Ja
]
introduced
n
-exact categories as
higher analogs of exact categories. Moreover, he also proved that any
n
-cluster-tilting subcategory of an
exact category is an
n
-exact category. Recently, Herschend, Liu and Nakaoka
[
HLN
]
introduced the
notion of
n
-exangulated categories for any positive integer
n
. It is not only a higher
dimensional analogue of extriangulated categories defined by Nakaoka and Palu
[
NP
]
,
but also gives a common generalization of
n
-exact categories in the sense of
Jasso
[
Ja
]
and
(n+2)
-angulated categories in the sense of Geiss-Keller-Oppermann
[
GKO
]
.

Balmer and Schlichting
[
BM
]
proved that the idempotent completion of a triangulated category admits a natural triangulated structure. Bühler
[
B
]
showed that the idempotent completion of an exact category is still an exact category. Wang-Wei-Zhang-Zhao
[
WWZZ
]
and Msapato
[
M
]
independently unified their results of
Balmer and Schlichting
[
BM
]
and Bühler
[
B
]
in the framework of extriangulated
categories. Specifically speaking, they proved that the idempotent completion of an extriangulated category is extriangulated.
In order to construct more examples of
(n+2)
-angulated categories, Lin
[
L2
]
extended Balmer and Schlichting’s result from 3 to
(n+2)
. More precisely, Lin proved that the idempotent completion of an
(n+2)
-angulated category admits a natural
(n+2)
-angulated structure.
Let
(\mathscr{C},\Sigma,\Theta)
be a Krull-Schmidt
(n+2)
-angulated category and
\mathscr{A}
be an
n
-extension closed subcategory of
\mathscr{C}
. Zhou
[
Z
]
proved that
\mathscr{A}
admits the structure of an
n
-exangulated category.

Based on their results of Lin
[
L2
]
and Zhou
[
Z
]
, we prove the following conclusion which gives
n
-exangulated categories which are neither
n
-exact nor
(n+2)
-angulated in general.

Theorem 1.1
.

(see Theorem
3.12
for details) Let
(\mathscr{C},\Sigma,\Theta)
be a Krull-Schmidt
(n+2)
-angulated category and

\mathscr{A}
be an
n
-extension closed subcategory of
\mathscr{C}
. Then the idempotent completion
\widetilde{\mathscr{A}}
of
\mathscr{A}
admits an
n
-exangulated structure.

Note that any
(n+2)
-angulated category
\mathscr{C}
can be regarded as an
n
-extension closed subcategory of itself, hence Theorem
1.1
generalizes the main result of Lin
[
L2
]
. Our proof method is to avoid verifying that (EA1) holds, in fact, the verification of this axiom is complicated and difficult.

This article is organized as follows. In Section 2, we review some elementary definitions on
(n+2)
-angulated categories and
n
-exangulated categories. In Section 3, we prove our main result in this article.

Lemma 2.4

Let

-angulated category.
Then any monomorphism in

Dually any epimorphism

Assume that

Remark 3.2
.

(1)

The assignment
A\mapsto(A,1)
defines a fully faithful additive functor
\iota:\mathscr{C}\rightarrow\widetilde{\mathscr{C}}
. Namely, we can view
\mathscr{C}
as a full subcategory of
\widetilde{\mathscr{C}}
.

(2)

For each object
X\in\widetilde{\mathscr{C}}
, there exists an object
X^{\prime}\in\widetilde{\mathscr{C}}
such that
X\oplus X^{\prime}\in\mathscr{C}
. In fact, if
X=(A,e)
, then we can take
X^{\prime}=(A,1-e)
and
X\oplus X^{\prime}\cong A\in\mathscr{C}
.

(3)

From the definition, it is obvious that if
\mathscr{C}
is Krull-Schmidt,
then
\widetilde{\mathscr{C}}
is also Krull-Schmidt.

.

and

A_{\bullet}=(A_{0}\xrightarrow{f_{0}}A_{1}\xrightarrow{f_{1}}\cdots\xrightarrow{f_{n-2}}A_{n-1}\xrightarrow{f_{n-1}}A_{n}\xrightarrow{\binom{f_{n}}{g_{n}}}A_{n+1}\oplus B_{n+1}\xrightarrow{(f_{n+1},\hskip 2.27621ptg_{n+1})}\Sigma A_{0})\in\Theta.

, then

, where

Let
(\mathscr{C},\Sigma,\Theta)
be an
(n+2)
-angulated category. Since
\Sigma\colon\mathscr{C}\xrightarrow{~\simeq~}\mathscr{C}
is an automorphism, then

\Sigma
gives an additive bifunctor

\mathbb{E}_{\Sigma}=\mathscr{C}(-,\Sigma-)\colon\mathscr{C}^{\rm op}\times\mathscr{C}\to{\rm Ab},

defined by the following.

(i)

For any
A,C\in\mathscr{C}
,
\mathbb{E}_{\Sigma}(C,A)=\mathscr{C}(C,\Sigma A)
;

(ii)

For any
a\in\mathscr{C}(A,A^{\prime})
and
c\in\mathscr{C}(C^{\prime},C)
, the map
\mathbb{E}_{\Sigma}(c,a)\colon\mathscr{C}(C,\Sigma A)\to\mathscr{C}(C^{\prime},\Sigma A^{\prime})

sends
\delta\in\mathscr{C}(C,\Sigma A)
to
c^{\ast}a_{\ast}\delta=(\Sigma a)\circ\delta\circ c
.

For each
\delta\in\mathbb{E}_{\Sigma}(C,A)
, we complete
it into an
(n+2)
-angle

A\xrightarrow{f_{0}}X_{1}\xrightarrow{f_{1}}X_{2}\xrightarrow{f_{2}}\cdots\xrightarrow{f_{n-1}}X_{n}\xrightarrow{f_{n}}C\xrightarrow{\delta}\Sigma A_{0}

Define
\mathfrak{s}_{\Theta}(\delta)=[X^{\hbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}}]
by using
X^{\hbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}}\in{\mathbf{C}^{n+2}_{(A,\hskip 2.27621ptC)}}
given by

A\xrightarrow{f_{0}}X_{1}\xrightarrow{f_{1}}X_{2}\xrightarrow{f_{2}}\cdots\xrightarrow{f_{n-1}}X_{n}\xrightarrow{f_{n}}C

The following result shows that any
(n+2)
-angulated category can be viewed as
an
n
-exangulated category.

Theorem 3.7
.

[
HLN
, Proposition 4.5]

With the above definition,
(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E}_{\Sigma},\mathfrak{s}_{\Theta})
is an
n
-exangulated category.

Let
(\mathscr{C},\Sigma,\Theta)
be an
(n+2)
-angulated category and

\mathscr{A}
be an
n
-extension closed subcategory of
\mathscr{C}
.
Define
\mathbb{E}_{\mathscr{A}}
to be the restriction of

\mathbb{E}_{\Sigma}
onto
\mathscr{A}^{\rm op}\times\mathscr{A}
, and define
\mathfrak{s}_{\mathscr{A}}
by restricting
\mathfrak{s}_{\Theta}
.
Recall that an additive category
\mathscr{C}
is
Krull-Schmidt
if any object
A
has a decomposition
A=A_{1}\oplus A_{2}\oplus\cdots\oplus A_{n}
such that each
A_{i}
is indecomposable with local endomorphism ring.

Theorem 3.8
.

[
Z
, Theorem 3.4]

Let
(\mathscr{C},\Sigma,\Theta)
be a Krull-Schmidt
(n+2)
-angulated category and

\mathscr{A}
be an
n
-extension closed subcategory of
\mathscr{C}
. Then
(\mathscr{A},\mathbb{E}_{\mathscr{A}},\mathfrak{s}_{\mathscr{A}})

is an
n
-exangulated category.

Remark 3.9
.

In Theorem
3.8
,
(\mathscr{A},\mathbb{E}_{\mathscr{A}},\mathfrak{s}_{\mathscr{A}})

is an
n
-exangulated category which is neither
n
-exact nor
(n+2)
-angulated
in general
.

(a)

If
(\mathscr{A},\mathbb{E}_{\mathscr{A}},\mathfrak{s}_{\mathscr{A}})
is
n
-exact, by
[
HLN1
, Proposition 4.37]
, we know that
any inflation is monomorphism. By Lemma
2.4
,
any monomorphism is section. This shows that all distinguished
n
-exangles are split.
This is not possible in general.

(b)

Since
\mathscr{A}
is not necessarily closed under
\Sigma
, by
[
HLN1
, Proposition 4.8]
,
we know that
(\mathscr{A},\mathbb{E}_{\mathscr{A}},\mathfrak{s}_{\mathscr{A}})
is not
(n+2)
-angulated in general.

Theorem 3.10
.

[
L2
, Theorem 3.1]

Let
(\mathscr{C},\Sigma,\Theta)
be an
(n+2)
-angulated category and

\widetilde{\mathscr{C}}
the idempotent completion of
\mathscr{C}
. Denote by
\widetilde{\Sigma}
the isomorphism of
\widetilde{\mathscr{C}}
induced by the isomorphism
\Sigma
of
\mathscr{C}
and by
\widetilde{\Theta}
the class of
(n+2)
-
\widetilde{\Sigma}
-sequences in
\widetilde{\mathscr{C}}
that are direct summands of
(n+2)
-
{\Sigma}
-sequences in
\Theta
. Then
(\widetilde{\mathscr{C}},\widetilde{\Sigma},\widetilde{\Theta})

is an
(n+2)
-angulated category.

Remark 3.11
.

In Theorem
3.10
,
\widetilde{\Sigma}
is induced by the isomorphism
\Sigma
of
\mathscr{C}
, more precisely, for any object
\widetilde{C}=(C,e_{c})
in

, Define

by
\widetilde{\Sigma}(\widetilde{C})=\widetilde{\Sigma}(C,e_{c})=(\Sigma C,\Sigma(e_{c}))

Our main result is the following.

Theorem 3.12
.

Let
(\mathscr{C},\Sigma,\Theta)
be a Krull-Schmidt
(n+2)
-angulated category and

\mathscr{A}
be an
n
-extension closed subcategory of
\mathscr{C}
. Then the idempotent completion
\widetilde{\mathscr{A}}
of
\mathscr{A}
admits an
n
-exangulated structure.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.10
, we know that the idempotent completion
(\widetilde{\mathscr{C}},\widetilde{\Sigma},\widetilde{\Theta})
of
(\mathscr{C},\Sigma,\Theta)
is an
(n+2)
-angulated category. We claim that
\widetilde{\mathscr{A}}
is an
n
-extension closed subcategory of
\widetilde{\mathscr{C}}
. Indeed, for each morphism
f_{n+1}:{A_{n+1}}\rightarrow\widetilde{\Sigma}{A_{0}}
with
{A_{n+1}},{A_{0}}\in\widetilde{\mathscr{A}}
, we can choose two objects

{A^{\prime}_{n+1}},{A^{\prime}_{0}}\in\widetilde{\mathscr{A}}
such that
{A_{n+1}}\oplus{A^{\prime}_{n+1}},{A_{0}}\oplus{A^{\prime}_{0}}\in{\mathscr{A}}
by Remark
3.2
. Note that
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}f_{n+1}&0\\
0&0\\
\end{smallmatrix}\right):{A_{n+1}}\oplus{A^{\prime}_{n+1}}\rightarrow{\Sigma}{A_{0}}\oplus{\Sigma}{A^{\prime}_{0}}
is a morphism in
\mathscr{C}
. Since
\mathscr{A}
is an
n
-extension closed subcategory of
\mathscr{C}
, there exists an
(n+2)
-angle

\widetilde{X}_{\bullet}=({A_{0}}\oplus{A^{\prime}_{0}}\xrightarrow{f_{0}}{A_{1}}\xrightarrow{f_{1}}{A_{2}}\xrightarrow{f_{3}}\cdots\xrightarrow{f_{n-1}}A_{n}\xrightarrow{{f_{n}}}{{A_{n+1}}\oplus{A^{\prime}_{n+1}}}\xrightarrow{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}f_{n+1}&0\\
0&0\\
\end{smallmatrix}\right)}{\Sigma}{A_{0}}\oplus{\Sigma}{A^{\prime}_{0}})

in
\Theta
, where
A_{1},A_{2},\cdots,A_{n}\in\mathscr{A}
. Since
\widetilde{\mathscr{C}}
is idempotent complete and
\widetilde{X}_{\bullet}\in\widetilde{\Theta}
, It follows that
\widetilde{X}_{\bullet}\cong X_{\bullet}\oplus X^{\prime}_{\bullet}
by Lemma
3.6
, where

X_{\bullet}=(A_{0}\xrightarrow{f^{\prime}_{0}}A^{\prime}_{1}\xrightarrow{f_{1}^{\prime}}A_{2}\xrightarrow{f_{3}}\cdots\xrightarrow{f_{n-2}}A_{n-1}\xrightarrow{f_{n-1}^{\prime}}A_{n}^{\prime}\xrightarrow{f_{n}^{\prime}}A_{n+1}\xrightarrow{f_{n+1}}\widetilde{\Sigma}A_{0}),

X^{\prime}_{\bullet}=(A_{0}^{\prime}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{id}}A_{0}^{\prime}\xrightarrow{0}0\rightarrow 0\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow 0\xrightarrow{0}A^{\prime}_{n+1}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{id}}A^{\prime}_{n+1}\xrightarrow{0}\widetilde{\Sigma}A_{0}^{\prime}).

So

\widetilde{\Theta}

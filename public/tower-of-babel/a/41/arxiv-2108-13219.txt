Vortex lattices in binary Bose-Einstein condensates: Collective modes, quantum fluctuations, and intercomponent entanglement
Takumi Yoshino, Shunsuke Furukawa, Masahito Ueda

Abstract
We study binary Bose-Einstein condensates subject to synthetic magnetic fields in mutually parallel or antiparallel directions. Within the mean-field theory, the two types of fields have been shown to give the same vortex-lattice phase diagram. We develop an improved effective field theory to study properties of collective modes and ground-state intercomponent entanglement. Here, we point out the importance of introducing renormalized coupling constants for coarse-grained densities. We show that the low-energy excitation spectra for the two kinds of fields are related to each other by suitable rescaling using the renormalized constants. By calculating the entanglement entropy, we find that for an intercomponent repulsion (attraction), the two components are more strongly entangled in the case of parallel (antiparallel) fields, in qualitative agreement with recent studies for a quantum (spin) Hall regime. We also find that the entanglement spectrum exhibits an anomalous square-root dispersion relation, which leads to a subleading logarithmic term in the entanglement entropy. All of these are confirmed by numerical calculations based on the Bogoliubov theory with the lowest-Landau-level approximation. Finally, we investigate the effects of quantum fluctuations on the phase diagrams by calculating the correction to the ground-state energy due to zero-point fluctuations in the Bogoliubov theory. We find that the boundaries between rhombic-, square-, and rectangular-lattice phases shift appreciably with a decrease in the filling factor.



Keywords
: multicomponent Bose-Einstein condensates, synthetic gauge fields, vortex lattices, quantum entanglement

In this paper, we present a detailed comparative study of vortex lattices of binary BECs in parallel and antiparallel fields
concerning ground-state and excitation properties.
We first formulate an improved effective field theory for such vortex lattices, and derive some properties of collective modes and ground-state intercomponent entanglement.
Here, a major improvement is the introduction of renormalized coupling constants for coarse-grained densities.

In the formulation of Ref.
[
46
]
, the necessity of renormalization was not transparent.
This was because we integrated out the density variables, before coarse graining,
to obtain an effective Lagrangian for phase variables.
In this paper, we keep both the phase and density variables in the Lagrangian, and perform coarse graining of both the variables at the same time.
The renormalization of density-density interactions can naturally be understood in this formulation.
This formulation also allows us to move easily to the operator formalism, in which ground-state and excitation properties can be studied in an algebraic manner.

We show that the low-energy excitation spectra for the two types of fields are related to each other by suitable rescaling using the renormalized constants.
Namely, the rescaling relations proposed in our previous work
[
46
]
must be modified using the renormalized constants.
By calculating the entanglement entropy (EE), we find that for an intercomponent repulsion (attraction),
the two components are more strongly entangled in the case of parallel (antiparallel) fields,
in qualitative agreement with recent exact diagonalization results for a quantum (spin) Hall regime
[
42
,
52
]
.
As a by-product, we also find that the entanglement spectrum (ES) exhibits an anomalous square-root dispersion relation,
and that the EE exhibits a volume-law scaling with a subleading logarithmic correction.
This anomalous feature of the ES is associated with the emergence of a long-range interaction in terms of the density in the entanglement Hamiltonian.
All these predictions are confirmed by numerical calculations based on the Bogoliubov theory with the LLL approximation.
Finally, we investigate the effects of quantum fluctuations on the phase diagrams
by calculating the Lee-Huang-Yang correction, which is a correction to the ground-state energy due to zero-point fluctuations
in the Bogoliubov theory
[
60
]
.
We find that the boundaries between rhombic-, square-, and rectangular-lattice phases shift appreciably with a decrease in
\nu
.
Here, the shift occurs more significantly for parallel fields.

Let us comment on the relation to another recent work of our own
[
61
]

concerning intercomponent ES and EE in binary BECs in
d
spatial dimensions
in the absence of synthetic gauge fields.
Here we employ effective field theory to show that
the ES exhibits a gapless square-root dispersion relation in the presence of an intercomponent tunneling (a Rabi coupling)
and a gapped dispersion relation in its absence (see also Refs.
[
62
,
63
]
for related results in two coupled Tomonaga-Luttinger liquids).
In the present work, in contrast, the ES exhibits a square-root dispersion relation
in the absence of an intercomponent tunneling in both the parallel- and antiparallel-field cases.
This qualitative distinction is related to the fact that binary BECs in parallel and antiparallel fields
have a higher density of low-energy excitations and thus experience larger quantum fluctuations than those without synthetic gauge fields.
References
[
61
,
63
]
and the present paper demonstrate that
a variety of long-range interactions can be emulated in a subsystem of multicomponent BECs that have only short-range interactions.
We also note that the field-theoretical methods for investigating intercomponent entanglement in the present paper
are closely analogous to those in Refs.
[
61
,
62
,
63
]
.

2
Effective field theory

Effective field theory for a vortex lattice in a scalar BEC has been developed in Refs.
[
18
,
23
,
64
,
65
]
.
In our previous work
[
46
]
, we applied the formulation by Watanabe and Murayama
[
23
]

to binary BECs in parallel and antiparallel fields.
For parallel fields, this approach was essentially equivalent to the hydrodynamic theory by Keçeli and Oktel
[
45
]
.
Here, we formulate an improved effective field theory for binary BECs in parallel and antiparallel fields
by introducing renormalized coupling constants
\bar{g}_{\alpha\beta}
for coarse-grained densities.
In doing so, we keep both the phase and density variables in the Lagrangian, rather than integrating out the density variables as in Ref.
[
46
]
.
We then move to the operator formalism, and study excitation spectra, intercomponent entanglement, and correlation functions in an algebraic manner
(see Refs.
[
61
,
62
,
63
]
for analogous calculations in different systems).

2.1
Systems

We consider a system of 2D binary (pseudospin-
\frac{1}{2}
) BECs having two hyperfine spin states (labeled by
\alpha=\uparrow,\downarrow
)
and subject to synthetic magnetic fields
B_{\uparrow}
and
B_{\downarrow}
in mutually parallel or antiparallel directions.
The Lagrangian density of the system is given by

\mathcal{L}=\sum_{\alpha=\uparrow,\downarrow}\left[\frac{i\hbar}{2}(\psi_{\alpha}^{*}\dot{\psi}_{\alpha}-\dot{\psi}_{\alpha}^{*}\psi_{\alpha})-\frac{1}{2M}|(-i\hbar\nabla-q\mathbf{A}_{\alpha})\psi_{\alpha}|^{2}\right]-\sum_{\alpha,\beta=\uparrow,\downarrow}\frac{g_{\alpha\beta}}{2}|\psi_{\alpha}|^{2}|\psi_{\beta}|^{2},

(1)

where
\psi_{\alpha}({\mathbf{r}},t)
is the bosonic field for the spin-
\alpha
component (with
{\mathbf{r}}=(x,y)
being the 2D coordinate)
and
M
and
q
are the mass and the fictitious charge of an atom.
The gauge field
\mathbf{A}_{\alpha}({\mathbf{r}})
for spin-
\alpha
bosons
is given by

\mathbf{A}_{\alpha}=\frac{B_{\alpha}}{2}{\mathbf{e}}_{z}\times{\mathbf{r}}=\epsilon_{\alpha}\frac{B}{2}(-y,x),

(2)

where we assume
qB>0
and
\epsilon_{\uparrow}=\epsilon_{\downarrow}=1

(\epsilon_{\uparrow}=-\epsilon_{\downarrow}=1)
for parallel (antiparallel) fields.
The number of magnetic flux quanta piercing each component
is given by
N_{\mathrm{v}}=qBA/(2\pi\hbar)=A/(2\pi\ell^{2})
,
where
A
is the area of the system and
\ell:=\sqrt{\hbar/qB}
is the magnetic length.
In the Lagrangian (
1
), the numbers of spin-
\uparrow
and
\downarrow
atoms,
N_{\uparrow}
and
N_{\downarrow}
, are separately conserved.
We introduce the total filling factor
\nu:=N/N_{\mathrm{v}}
, where
N:=N_{\uparrow}+N_{\downarrow}
is the total number of atoms.

We assume a contact interaction between atoms.
For simplicity, we set
g_{\uparrow\uparrow}=g_{\downarrow\downarrow}\equiv g>|g_{\uparrow\downarrow}|
and
N_{\uparrow}=N_{\downarrow}
in the following.
With these conditions, the system in parallel fields is invariant under the interchange of the two components,
while the system in antiparallel fields is invariant under time reversal.
To apply the LLL approximation, we further assume that
the scale of the interaction energy per atom,
|g_{\alpha\beta}|n
, is much smaller than the Landau-level spacing
\hbar\omega_{\mathrm{c}}
.
Here,
n:=N_{\uparrow}/A=N_{\downarrow}/A
is the average density of
\uparrow
or
\downarrow
atoms, and
\omega_{\mathrm{c}}:=qB/M
is the cyclotron frequency.

2.2
Effective field theory

To obtain a low-energy effective description, it is useful to rewrite the field as

\psi_{\alpha}={\mathrm{e}}^{-\mathrm{i}\theta_{\alpha}}\sqrt{n_{\alpha}}
,
where
n_{\alpha}({\mathbf{r}},t)
and
\theta_{\alpha}({\mathbf{r}},t)
are the density and phase variables, respectively.
The Lagrangian density (
1
) is rewritten in terms of these variables as

\mathcal{L}=\sum_{\alpha}\left[\hbar n_{\alpha}\dot{\theta}_{\alpha}-\frac{n_{\alpha}}{2M}(\hbar\nabla\theta_{\alpha}+q\mathbf{A}_{\alpha})^{2}-\frac{\hbar^{2}(\nabla n_{\alpha})^{2}}{8n_{\alpha}M}\right]-\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\frac{g_{\alpha\beta}}{2}n_{\alpha}n_{\beta}.

(3)

In the presence of vortices,
the phase variables
\{\theta_{\alpha}({\mathbf{r}},t)\}
involve singularities.
This motivates us
to decompose
\theta_{\alpha}
into regular and singular contributions as

\theta_{\alpha}=\theta_{\mathrm{reg},\alpha}+\theta_{\mathrm{sing},\alpha}
.
We also introduce the displacement
\mathbf{u}_{\alpha}({\mathbf{r}},t)
of a vortex from the equilibrium position.
The derivatives of the singular part
\theta_{\mathrm{sing},\alpha}
of the phase
can be related to the displacement field
{\mathbf{u}}_{\alpha}
as
[
23
,
64
]

\hbar\dot{\theta}_{\mathrm{sing},\alpha}=-\frac{qB_{\alpha}}{2}({\mathbf{u}}_{\alpha}\times\dot{{\mathbf{u}}}_{\alpha})_{z},~~\hbar\nabla\theta_{\mathrm{sing},\alpha}+q{\mathbf{A}}_{\alpha}=qB_{\alpha}{\mathbf{e}}_{z}\times{\mathbf{u}}_{\alpha}-\frac{qB_{\alpha}}{2}\epsilon_{ij}u^{i}_{\alpha}\nabla u^{j}_{\alpha},

(4)

where
\epsilon_{ij}
is an antisymmetric tensor with
\epsilon_{xy}=-\epsilon_{yx}=+1
.
The displacement
{\mathbf{u}}_{\alpha}({\mathbf{r}},t)
also results in
a change
in the elastic energy
\int\mathrm{d}^{2}{\mathbf{r}}~\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{el}}({\mathbf{u}}_{\alpha},\partial_{i}{\mathbf{u}}_{\alpha})
,
whose explicit form will be given in Sec.
2.3
.
The Lagrangian density can then be expressed in terms of
\{\theta_{\mathrm{reg},\alpha},{\mathbf{u}}_{\alpha},n_{\alpha}\}
as

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}=&\sum_{\alpha}\Biggl[\hbar n_{\alpha}\dot{\theta}_{\mathrm{reg},\alpha}-\frac{qB_{\alpha}n_{\alpha}}{2}({\mathbf{u}}_{\alpha}\times\dot{{\mathbf{u}}}_{\alpha})_{z}-\frac{n_{\alpha}}{2M}\left(\hbar\nabla\theta_{\mathrm{reg},\alpha}+qB_{\alpha}\mathbf{e}_{z}\times{\mathbf{u}}_{\alpha}-\frac{qB_{\alpha}}{2}\epsilon_{ij}u_{\alpha}^{i}\nabla u_{\alpha}^{j}\right)^{2}\\
&~~~~~-\frac{\hbar^{2}(\nabla n_{\alpha})^{2}}{8n_{\alpha}M}\Biggr]-\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\frac{\bar{g}_{\alpha\beta}}{2}n_{\alpha}n_{\beta}-\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{el}}.\end{split}

(5)

Henceforth, we ignore the term
-\frac{qB_{\alpha}}{2}\epsilon_{ij}u_{\alpha}^{i}\nabla u_{\alpha}^{j}
in the round brackets
as it only gives more than quadratic contributions to
\mathcal{L}
in terms of
\nabla\theta_{\mathrm{reg},\alpha}
and
{\mathbf{u}}_{\alpha}
.
We also omit the subscript “reg” in
\theta_{\mathrm{reg},\alpha}
.

In the effective Lagrangian density (
5
), we have introduced renormalized coupling constants
\bar{g}_{\alpha\beta}

with
\bar{g}_{\uparrow\uparrow}=\bar{g}_{\downarrow\downarrow}\equiv\bar{g}
and
\bar{g}_{\uparrow\downarrow}=\bar{g}_{\downarrow\uparrow}
.
The necessity of this renormalization has been overlooked in previous studies
[
45
,
46
]
and can be explained as follows.
The introduction of the vortex displacement fields
\{u_{\alpha}({\mathbf{r}},t)\}
necessarily involves the coarse graining of the theory.
Namely, we smooth out details within the scale of the lattice constants and instead focus on the physics at larger scales.
Therefore, the density variable
n_{\alpha}({\mathbf{r}},t)
should now be understood as the density averaged over the unit cell containing
{\mathbf{r}}
.
The renormalized coupling constants
\bar{g}_{\alpha\beta}
can then be introduced through the relation

\int_{\mathrm{u.c.}}\mathrm{d}^{2}{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime}~g_{\alpha\beta}|\psi_{\alpha}({\mathbf{r}}^{\prime},t)|^{2}|\psi_{\beta}({\mathbf{r}}^{\prime},t)|^{2}=2\pi\ell^{2}\bar{g}_{\alpha\beta}n_{\alpha}({\mathbf{r}},t)n_{\beta}({\mathbf{r}},t),

(6)

where
|\psi_{\alpha}|^{2}
and
n_{\alpha}
are the original and coarse-grained densities of the spin-
\alpha
atoms, respectively,
and the integration on the left-hand side is taken over the unit cell (with the area
2\pi\ell^{2}
) that contains
{\mathbf{r}}
.
As explained later in this section and in Sec.
4.1
, the renormalized constants
\bar{g}_{\alpha\beta}
for describing the low-energy physics
can be determined by calculating the contribution of each interaction term to the mean-field ground-state energy.
As seen in Eq. (
6
), a positive (negative) correlation between the density fluctuations
|\psi_{\alpha}|^{2}-n_{\alpha}
and
|\psi_{\beta}|^{2}-n_{\beta}

leads to an enhanced (reduced) renormalized coupling
|\bar{g}_{\alpha\beta}|>|g_{\alpha\beta}|
(
|\bar{g}_{\alpha\beta}|<|g_{\alpha\beta}|
).
In particular, the intracomponent coupling
g
is always enhanced by the renormalization
while the intercomponent repulsion
g_{\uparrow\downarrow}>0
(attraction
g_{\uparrow\downarrow}<0
) is reduced (enhanced) by the renomalization
owing to the displacement (overlap) of vortices between the components; this can be confirmed in Fig.
2
below.
For a rotating scalar BEC with a repulsive coupling
g>0
, a similar renormalization with
\bar{g}/g=1.1596
has been discussed in Refs.
[
7
,
18
,
66
]
.
At high filling factors
\nu\gg 1
and for not too large a number of vortices
N_{\mathrm{v}}
, we can assume that the condensates are only weakly depleted;
we therefore have
|n_{\alpha}({\mathbf{r}},t)-n|\ll n
for the coarse-grained density
n_{\alpha}
.

Because the displacement fields
\{{\mathbf{u}}_{\alpha}\}
involve the mass term
-{\mathbf{u}}_{\alpha}^{2}
in Eq. (
5
),
one may safely integrate them out
in the discussion of low-energy dynamics.
Instead of performing the integration directly,
it is useful to derive the Euler-Lagrange equations for
\{{\mathbf{u}}_{\alpha}\}
:

{\mathbf{u}}_{\alpha}-\epsilon_{\alpha}\ell^{2}{\mathbf{e}}_{z}\times\nabla\theta_{\alpha}-\frac{\epsilon_{\alpha}}{\omega_{\mathrm{c}}}{\mathbf{e}}_{z}\times\dot{{\mathbf{u}}}_{\alpha}+\frac{\ell^{2}}{n\hbar\omega_{\mathrm{c}}}\left[\frac{\partial\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{el}}}{\partial{\mathbf{u}}_{\alpha}}-\partial_{j}\left(\frac{\partial\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{el}}}{\partial\left(\partial_{j}{\mathbf{u}}_{\alpha}\right)}\right)\right]=0,

(7)

where we have made the approximation
n_{\alpha}\approx n
.
We can ignore
the third and fourth terms on the left-hand side in the LLL approximation
with
\hbar\omega,\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{el}}/n\ll\hbar\omega_{\mathrm{c}}
,
where
\omega
is the frequency of our concern.
Introducing
{\mathbf{u}}_{\pm}:={\mathbf{u}}_{\uparrow}\pm{\mathbf{u}}_{\downarrow}
and
\theta_{\pm}:=\theta_{\uparrow}\pm\theta_{\downarrow}
,
we can rewrite Eq. (
7
) as

{\mathbf{u}}_{\pm}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\ell^{2}\mathbf{e}_{z}\times\nabla\theta_{\pm}\qquad\text{(parallel fields)};\\
&\ell^{2}\mathbf{e}_{z}\times\nabla\theta_{\mp}\qquad\text{(antiparallel fields)}.\end{aligned}\right.\qquad\text{}

(8)

These relations indicate that the (anti)symmetric movement of vortices is coupled to the (anti)symmetric component of the phase variables in parallel fields
while they are coupled in a crossed manner in antiparallel fields.
By substituting Eq. (
8
) into Eq. (
5
), we obtain the effective Lagrangian
in terms of
\theta_{\pm}
and their conjugate momenta
\hbar n_{\pm}=\hbar(n_{\uparrow}\pm n_{\downarrow})/2
.
The Hamiltonian density is then obtained as

\mathcal{H}=\sum_{\nu=\pm}\left[\bar{g}_{\nu}n_{\nu}^{2}+\frac{\hbar^{2}(\nabla n_{\nu})^{2}}{4nM}\right]+\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{el}},~~~\bar{g}_{\pm}:=\bar{g}\pm\bar{g}_{\uparrow\downarrow}.

(9)

where
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{el}}
is expressed in terms of the phase variables
\theta_{\pm}
by using Eq. (
8
).
The theory can be quantized by requiring the canonical commutation relations

[\theta_{\nu}({\mathbf{r}}),n_{\nu^{\prime}}({\mathbf{r}}^{\prime})]=\mathrm{i}\delta_{\nu\nu^{\prime}}\delta({\mathbf{r}}-{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime})~~~(\nu,\nu^{\prime}=\pm).

(10)

In the present coarse-grained description, the mean-field ground state corresponds to the uniform state with

n_{+}({\mathbf{r}})=n
,
n_{-}({\mathbf{r}})=0
, and
\nabla\theta_{\pm}({\mathbf{r}})=0
.
Therefore, using Eq. (
9
),
we obtain the mean-field ground-state energy density as

\frac{E_{\mathrm{GS}}^{\mathrm{MF}}}{A}=\bar{g}_{+}n^{2}=(\bar{g}+\bar{g}_{\uparrow\downarrow})n^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\bar{g}_{\alpha\beta}n^{2}.

(11)

In contrast, the same energy density obtained by the microscopic calculation [the first term of Eq. (
67
) shown later]
has the form
E_{\mathrm{GS}}^{\mathrm{MF}}/A=(\beta g+\beta_{\uparrow\downarrow}g_{\uparrow\downarrow})n^{2}
,
where
\beta
and
\beta_{\uparrow\downarrow}
are dimensionless constants that depend on the lattice structure.
The renormalized coupling constants are thus determined as
\bar{g}=\beta g
and
\bar{g}_{\uparrow\downarrow}=\beta_{\uparrow\downarrow}g_{\uparrow\downarrow}
.

2.3
Elastic energy

The expression of the elastic energy density
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{el}}
has been determined in Refs.
[
45
,
46
]
, and we summarize it in the following.
We first note that the elastic energy must be
invariant under a constant change in
{\mathbf{u}}_{\alpha}({\mathbf{r}},t)
, i.e., translation of the lattices.
Therefore, to the leading order in the derivative expansion,

\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{el}}
should be a function of
\partial_{i}{\mathbf{u}}_{+}~(i=x,y)
and
{\mathbf{u}}_{-}
,
resulting in the decomposition

\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{el}}=\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{el}}^{(+)}(\partial_{i}{\mathbf{u}}_{+})+\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{el}}^{(-)}({\mathbf{u}}_{-})+\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{el}}^{(+-)}(\partial_{i}{\mathbf{u}}_{+},{\mathbf{u}}_{-}).

(12)

To express
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{el}}^{(+)}
, it is useful to introduce

w_{1}:=\partial_{x}u_{+}^{x}-\partial_{y}u_{+}^{y},~~w_{2}:=\partial_{y}u_{+}^{x}+\partial_{x}u_{+}^{y}.

(13)

On the basis of
a symmetry consideration, each term in Eq. (
12
) can be expressed as

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{el}}^{(+)}(\partial_{i}{\mathbf{u}}_{+})=\frac{gn^{2}}{2}\left(C_{1}w_{1}{}^{2}+C_{2}w_{2}{}^{2}+C_{3}w_{1}w_{2}\right),

(14a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{el}}^{(-)}({\mathbf{u}}_{-})=\frac{gn^{2}}{2\ell^{2}}\left[D_{1}\left(u_{-}^{x}\right)^{2}+D_{2}\left(u_{-}^{y}\right)^{2}+D_{3}u_{-}^{x}u_{-}^{y}\right],

(14b)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{el}}^{(+-)}(\partial_{i}{\mathbf{u}}_{+},{\mathbf{u}}_{-})=\frac{gn^{2}}{2\ell}F_{1}\left(w_{1}u_{-}^{y}+w_{2}u_{-}^{x}\right).

(14c)

For each of the vortex-lattice structures in Fig.
1
(a)-(e), the dimensionless elastic constants
\{C_{1},C_{2},C_{3},D_{1},D_{2},D_{3},F_{1}\}
satisfy

\begin{split}&\text{(a)}~C_{1}=C_{2}\equiv C>0,~D_{1}=D_{2}\equiv D>0,~C_{3}=D_{3}=F_{1}=0;\\
&\text{(b)}~C_{1}=C_{2}\equiv C>0,D_{1}=D_{2}\equiv D>0,~C_{3}=D_{3}=0,~F_{1}\neq 0;\\
&\text{(c)}~C_{1},C_{2},D_{1},D_{2}>0,~C_{3},D_{3}\neq 0,~F_{1}=0;\\
&\text{(d)}~C_{1},C_{2}>0,~D_{1}=D_{2}\equiv D>0,~C_{3}=D_{3}=F_{1}=0;\\
&\text{(e)}~C_{1},C_{2}>0,~D_{1},D_{2}>0,~C_{3}=D_{3}=F_{1}=0.\end{split}

(15)

Keçeli and Oktel
[
45
]
have determined the constants
\{C_{1},C_{2},C_{3},D_{1},D_{2},D_{3}\}

by calculating a change in the mean-field energy under deformation of vortex lattices.
In our previous work
[
46
]
, we have pointed out the presence of the
F_{1}
term for interlaced triangular vortex lattices (b),
which was missed in Ref.
[
45
]
.
For
\nu\gg 1
, the elastic constants should be the same for the two types of fields
because of the exact correspondence of the GP energy functionals
[
42
]
. In Sec.
4.2
, we will determine all the elastic constants above
as a function of
g_{\uparrow\downarrow}/g
by using the data of the excitation spectra.

2.4
Diagonalization of the effective Hamiltonian

We are now in a position to calculate the energy spectrum of the Hamiltonian
H=\int d^{2}{\mathbf{r}}~{\cal H}

where the Hamiltonian density
{\cal H}
is given by Eq. (

We perform Fourier expansions

{\mathbf{k}}={\mathbf{0}}
component

\theta_{{\mathbf{k}},-}\end{pmatrix}+\frac{1}{2n}\sum_{{\mathbf{k}}}\sum_{\nu=\pm}e_{{\mathbf{k}},\nu}n_{-{\mathbf{k}},\nu}n_{{\mathbf{k}},\nu},\end{split}

It is useful to decompose the Hamiltonian (

2.5
Intercomponent entanglement

We now calculate the reduced density matrix (RDM)
\rho_{\uparrow}
for the spin-
\uparrow

which is defined by starting from the ground state

We then discuss the properties of the intercomponent entanglement.
Because of the decoupling of the zero and oscillator modes,
the RDM takes the form of
\rho_{\uparrow}=\rho_{\uparrow}^{\mathrm{zero}}\otimes\rho_{\uparrow}^{\mathrm{osc}}

As the zero-mode ground state

\rho_{\uparrow}^{\mathrm{zero}}=|N_{\uparrow}=N/2\rangle\langle N_{\uparrow}=N/2|

Below we consider the oscillator-mode part
\rho_{\uparrow}^{\mathrm{osc}}

n_{\uparrow}({\mathbf{r}})
in real space, it can be expressed as

\begin{split}H_{\mathrm{e}}=\int\mathrm{d}^{2}{\mathbf{r}}\int\mathrm{d}^{2}{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime}\Biggl[\frac{n\ell^{2}}{2}U_{F}({\mathbf{r}}-{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime})\nabla\theta_{\uparrow}({\mathbf{r}})\cdot\nabla\theta_{\uparrow}({\mathbf{r}}^{\prime})+\frac{1}{2n}U_{G}({\mathbf{r}}-{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime})n_{\uparrow}({\mathbf{r}})n_{\uparrow}({\mathbf{r}}^{\prime})\Biggr],\end{split}

(47)

where we introduce the interaction potentials

\begin{split}U_{F}({\mathbf{r}}-{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime})=\frac{1}{A}\sum_{{\mathbf{k}}}F(\varphi){\mathrm{e}}^{\mathrm{i}{\mathbf{k}}\cdot({\mathbf{r}}-{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime})},~~U_{G}({\mathbf{r}}-{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime})=\lim_{\alpha\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{A}\sum_{{\mathbf{k}}\neq{\mathbf{0}}}\frac{G(\varphi)}{k\ell}{\mathrm{e}}^{-\alpha k+\mathrm{i}{\mathbf{k}}\cdot({\mathbf{r}}-{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime})}.\end{split}

(48)

Here, we use the convergence factor
{\mathrm{e}}^{-\alpha k}
to regularize the infinite sum for
U_{G}({\mathbf{r}}-{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime})
.
For simplicity, we consider the case of overlapping triangular lattices, in which
C(\varphi)
and
D(\varphi)
[and thus
F(\varphi)
and
G(\varphi)
as well] are constant; see (a) in Eq. (
15
).
In this case, the potentials in Eq. (
48
) are calculated as

U_{F}({\mathbf{r}}-{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime})=F\delta({\mathbf{r}}-{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime}),~~U_{G}({\mathbf{r}}-{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime})=\frac{G}{2\pi\ell|{\mathbf{r}}-{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime}|}.

(49)

For the calculation of
U_{G}({\mathbf{r}}-{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime})
, we refer the reader to Appendix A of Ref.
[
61
]
.
We note that
\theta_{\uparrow}({\mathbf{r}})
is the regular part of the superfluid phase of the spin-
\uparrow
component,
and that its gradient is related to the regular part of the superfluid velocity,
{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathrm{s},\uparrow}({\mathbf{r}})=-\frac{\hbar}{M}\nabla\theta_{\uparrow}({\mathbf{r}})
.
Therefore, the entanglement Hamiltonian (
47
) has a short-range interaction in terms of the superfluid velocity
{\mathbf{v}}_{s,\uparrow}({\mathbf{r}})

and a long-range one in terms of the density
n_{\uparrow}({\mathbf{r}})
.
If the density interaction were short-ranged, the ES would show a phonon mode with a linear dispersion relation.
Therefore, the anomalous square-root dispersion relation in Eq. (
43
) is closely related with the presence of a long-range interaction in
H_{\mathrm{e}}
.

Using the single-particle ES
\xi_{\mathbf{k}}
in Eq. (
43
), we can calculate the intercomponent EE
S_{\mathrm{e}}
.
For simplicity, we assume that
\xi_{\mathbf{k}}
is isotropic, i.e.,
c(\varphi)
is constant, as in the case of overlapping triangular lattices;
however, we expect that the result holds qualitatively for all the lattice structures.
With this simplification, the EE is calculated as (see Appendix B of Ref.
[
61
]
for the derivation)

S_{\mathrm{e}}=\frac{\sigma A}{c^{4}\ell^{2}}-\frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{\sqrt{A}}{2\pi c^{2}\ell}+O(1)=\frac{2\pi\sigma N_{\mathrm{v}}}{c^{4}}-\frac{1}{4}\ln\frac{N_{\mathrm{v}}}{2\pi c^{4}}+O(1).

(50)

Here, the leading contribution is given by the first term, which is proportional to the area
A
with a non-universal coefficient
\sigma
that depends, e.g., on the choice of the high-momentum cutoff.
Besides, there is a subleading logarithmic term with the universal coefficient (equal to
-1/2
when written as a function of the linear system size
\sqrt{A}
),
which is identified through a careful examination of small-
k
contributions and therefore originates from the Nambu-Goldstone modes.
The intercomponent EE per flux in the thermodynamic limit is calculated as

\lim_{N_{\mathrm{v}}\to\infty}\frac{S_{\mathrm{e}}}{N_{\mathrm{v}}}=\frac{2\pi\sigma}{c^{4}}=\frac{\pi\sigma\bar{g}_{\pm}D}{128\bar{g}_{\mp}C}.

(51)

Because of the factor
\bar{g}_{\pm}/\bar{g}_{\mp}
in Eq. (
51
), when the intercomponent interaction
g_{\uparrow\downarrow}
is repulsive (attractive),
the intercomponent EE is expected to be larger for the case of parallel (antiparallel) fields.
We note that the above calculation of the intercomponent EE
S_{\mathrm{e}}
is simple yet approximate
as it is based on the single-particle ES
\xi_{\mathbf{k}}
for long wavelengths.
In Sec.
4.3
, we will present numerical results on
S_{\mathrm{e}}
based on the Bogoliubov theory,
in which
\xi_{\mathbf{k}}
over the full Brillouin zone is taken into account,
and confirm the consistency with the field-theoretical predictions.

2.6
Intracomponent correlation functions

Here we calculate some intracomponent correlation functions, and discuss their connections with the (long-wavelength) entanglement Hamiltonian
H_{\mathrm{e}}
obtained in the preceding section.
Let
\langle{\cal O}\rangle
denote the expectation value of an operator
{\cal O}
with respect to the ground state
|0^{\mathrm{zero}}\rangle\otimes|0^{\mathrm{osc}}\rangle
of the total system.
If
{\cal O}
acts only on the spin-
\uparrow
component,
\langle{\cal O}\rangle
should be equal to
\Tr\left({\cal O}{\mathrm{e}}^{-H_{\mathrm{e}}}\right)/\Tr{\mathrm{e}}^{-H_{\mathrm{e}}}

as far as long-distance properties are concerned.
Our purpose here is to investigate how the unusual long-range interactions in
H_{\mathrm{e}}
manifest themselves in the correlation properties of the system.

Owing to the gapless ES
\xi_{\mathbf{k}}
, we can approximate the Bose distribution function (
41
) as

f_{\mathrm{B}}(\xi_{\mathbf{k}})\approx\xi_{\mathbf{k}}^{-1}=(F_{\mathbf{k}}G_{\mathbf{k}})^{-1/2}
for sufficiently small
k
.
Then, in the long-wavelength limit, Eq. (
42
) gives

\displaystyle\langle\theta_{-{\mathbf{k}},\uparrow}\theta_{{\mathbf{k}},\uparrow}\rangle\approx\frac{1}{nF_{\mathbf{k}}}\approx\frac{1}{nF(\varphi)k^{2}\ell^{2}},~~~\langle n_{-{\mathbf{k}},\uparrow}n_{{\mathbf{k}},\uparrow}\rangle\approx\frac{n}{G_{\mathbf{k}}}\approx\frac{nk\ell}{G(\varphi)}~~({\mathbf{k}}\neq{\mathbf{0}}),

(52)

where we use Eq. (
43
).
Therefore, the phase and density fluctuations are directly related to the coefficients
F_{\mathbf{k}}
and
G_{\mathbf{k}}
, respectively,
in the entanglement Hamiltonian (
38
).
Equation (
52
) indicates that in the long-wavelength limit
k\to 0
,
the phase fluctuation diverges and the density fluctuation is suppressed.
From the viewpoint of the entanglement Hamiltonian, suppression of the density fluctuation is a consequence of the long-range interaction in terms of the density.
The enhanced phase fluctuation in the long-wavelength limit leads to a quasi-long-range order in the one-particle density matrix, as we explain in the following.

The one-particle density matrix plays a key role in the characterization of the Bose-Einstein condensation
[
70
,
71
,
72
]
.
To analyze its behavior in our course-grained description, we introduce the modified bosonic field
\tilde{\psi}_{\uparrow}=e^{-i\theta_{\uparrow}}\sqrt{n_{\uparrow}}
.
As
\theta_{\uparrow}({\mathbf{r}})
is the regular part of the superfluid phase and
n_{\uparrow}({\mathbf{r}})
is the course-grained density,
\tilde{\psi}({\mathbf{r}})
is expected to vary slowly over space.
We consider the modified one-particle density matrix

\langle\tilde{\psi}_{\uparrow}({\mathbf{r}})^{\dagger}\tilde{\psi}_{\uparrow}({\mathbf{0}})\rangle=\left\langle\sqrt{n_{\uparrow}({\mathbf{r}})}{\mathrm{e}}^{i(\theta_{\uparrow}({\mathbf{r}})-\theta_{\uparrow}({\mathbf{0}}))}\sqrt{n_{\uparrow}({\mathbf{0}})}\right\rangle,

(53)

which describes the slowly varying component of the ordinary one-particle density matrix.
Its long-distance behavior is determined dominantly by the phase fluctuation
as seen in the small-
k
behavior of Eq. (
52
).
Here we again consider the case of overlapping triangular lattices where
F(\varphi)
is constant.
By using Eq. (
52
), the phase correlation function in real space is obtained as
(see
B
for the derivation)

\left\langle\left[\theta_{\uparrow}({\mathbf{r}})-\theta_{\uparrow}({\mathbf{0}})\right]^{2}\right\rangle=\frac{2}{A}\sum_{{\mathbf{k}}\neq{\mathbf{0}}}{\mathrm{e}}^{-\alpha k}\left[1-\cos\left({{\mathbf{k}}\cdot{\mathbf{r}}}\right)\right]\langle\theta_{-{\mathbf{k}},\uparrow}\theta_{{\mathbf{k}},\uparrow}\rangle\approx\frac{1}{\pi nF\ell^{2}}\ln\frac{r}{2\alpha}~~(r\gg\alpha),

(54)

where we again introduce the convergence factor
{\mathrm{e}}^{-\alpha k}
to regularize the infinite sum.
The modified one-particle density matrix is then obtained as

\langle\tilde{\psi}_{\uparrow}({\mathbf{r}})^{\dagger}\tilde{\psi}_{\uparrow}({\mathbf{0}})\rangle\approx n\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\left[\theta_{\uparrow}({\mathbf{r}})-\theta_{\uparrow}({\mathbf{0}})\right]^{2}\right\rangle\right\}\approx n\left(\frac{r}{2\alpha}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2\pi nF\ell^{2}}}.

(55)

We thus have a quasi-long-range order in the one-particle density matrix.

For a finite system of area
A
, the particle density
n_{0}
of the condensate can be evaluated
from the one-particle density matrix (
55
) at the large separation
r=\sqrt{A}/2
.
The density
n^{\prime}
of the depletion is then given by
n^{\prime}=n-n_{0}
.
Assuming
n^{\prime}\ll n
as required for the Bogoliubov theory and the present effective theory,
the fraction of depletion is estimated as

\frac{n^{\prime}}{n}\approx\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\left[\theta_{\uparrow}({\mathbf{r}})-\theta_{\uparrow}({\mathbf{0}})\right]^{2}\right\rangle\bigg|_{r=\sqrt{A}/2}\approx\frac{1}{2\pi nF\ell^{2}}\ln\frac{\sqrt{A}}{4\alpha}=\frac{1}{2\nu}\left(\frac{\bar{g}_{\pm}}{2gC}\right)^{1/2}\ln\frac{\sqrt{A}}{4\alpha}.

(56)

where we use Eq. (
44
) and
\nu=2nA/N_{\mathrm{v}}=4\pi n\ell^{2}
.
For fixed
\nu
, the fraction of quantum depletion of the condensate increases logarithmically as a function of the vortex number
N_{\mathrm{v}}=A/(2\pi\ell^{2})
.
Furthermore, for an intercomponent repulsion (attraction), it is larger for the case of parallel (antiparallel) fields, indicating larger quantum fluctuations.
This behavior is in accord with the larger intercomponent EE given in Eq. (
51
).
We note that a quasi-long-range order in the one-particle density matrix
and a logarithmic increase of the fraction of depletion as in Eqs. (
55
) and (
56
)
have also been discussed for a vortex lattice in a scalar BEC
[
18
,
20
,
21
,
22
]
.

[

,

]
.
In Ref.
[

]

3.1
and

, we summarize the formulation of Ref.

3.1
LLL magnetic Bloch states and Hamiltonian

We employ the LLL magnetic Bloch states
\{\Psi_{{\mathbf{k}}\alpha}({\mathbf{r}})\}

[
22
,
73
,
74
,
75
]

as a convenient single-particle basis for describing vortex lattices.
The expressions of these states are shown in Ref.
[
46
]
, and we summarize their main features in the following.
Let
\mathbf{a}_{1}
and
\mathbf{a}_{2}
be the primitive vectors of a vortex lattice,
and let
{\mathbf{K}}_{\alpha}
be the pseudomomentum operator for a spin-
\alpha
atom in a synthetic magnetic field
B_{\alpha}~(\alpha=\uparrow,\downarrow)
.
As expected for a “Bloch state”,
\Psi_{{\mathbf{k}}\alpha}({\mathbf{r}})
is an eigenstate of
the magnetic translation
{\mathrm{e}}^{-\mathrm{i}{\mathbf{K}}_{\alpha}\cdot{\mathbf{a}}_{j}/\hbar}
with an eigenvalue
{\mathrm{e}}^{-i{\mathbf{k}}\cdot{\mathbf{a}}_{j}}~(j=1,2)
.
By taking
N_{\mathrm{v}}
discrete wave vectors
{\mathbf{k}}
consistent with the boundary conditions of the system,

\{\Psi_{{\mathbf{k}}\alpha}({\mathbf{r}})\}
form a complete orthogonal basis of
the LLL manifold.
5
5

5

In numerical calculations presented later, we set
{\mathbf{k}}=\frac{n_{1}}{N_{\mathrm{v1}}}{\mathbf{b}}_{1}+\frac{n_{2}}{N_{\mathrm{v2}}}{\mathbf{b}}_{2}

with
n_{j}\in\{0,1,\dots,N_{\mathrm{v}j}-1\}
and
N_{\mathrm{v1}}N_{\mathrm{v2}}=N_{\mathrm{v}}
.
Here,
{\mathbf{b}}_{1}
and
{\mathbf{b}}_{2}
are the reciprocal primitive vectors as shown in Fig.
1
.

Notably,
\Psi_{{\mathbf{k}}\alpha}({\mathbf{r}})
has a periodic pattern of zeros at
[
74
]

{\mathbf{r}}=n_{1}\mathbf{a}_{1}+n_{2}\mathbf{a}_{2}+\frac{1}{2}({\mathbf{a}}_{1}+{\mathbf{a}}_{2})-\epsilon_{\alpha}\ell^{2}{\mathbf{e}}_{z}\times{\mathbf{k}},~~n_{1},n_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}.

(57)

Therefore,
\Psi_{{\mathbf{k}}\alpha}({\mathbf{r}})
represents a vortex lattice with primitive vectors
{\mathbf{a}}_{1}
and
{\mathbf{a}}_{2}
for any
{\mathbf{k}}
,
and the locations of vortices (zeros) can be shifted by varying
{\mathbf{k}}
.
Vortex lattices of binary BECs in Fig.
1
are obtained
when spin-
\alpha
bosons condense into
\Psi_{{\mathbf{q}}_{\alpha},\alpha}({\mathbf{r}})
,
where the wave vectors
{\mathbf{q}}_{\uparrow}
and
{\mathbf{q}}_{\downarrow}
are chosen in a way consistent
with the displacement
u_{1}{\mathbf{a}}_{1}+u_{2}{\mathbf{a}}_{2}
between the components.

In the LLL approximation, the kinetic energy of each particle stays constant,
and therefore we can focus on the interaction Hamiltonian
H_{\mathrm{int}}
.
Using the LLL magnetic Bloch states
\{\Psi_{{\mathbf{k}}\alpha}({\mathbf{r}})\}
as the basis, it is represented as

H_{\mathrm{int}}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\sum_{\mathbf{k}_{1},\mathbf{k}_{2},\mathbf{k}_{3},\mathbf{k}_{4}}V_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf{k}_{1},\mathbf{k}_{2},\mathbf{k}_{3},\mathbf{k}_{4})b^{{\dagger}}_{\mathbf{k}_{1}\alpha}b^{{\dagger}}_{\mathbf{k}_{2}\beta}b_{\mathbf{k}_{3}\beta}b_{\mathbf{k}_{4}\alpha},

(58)

where
b_{{\mathbf{k}}\alpha}
is a bosonic annihilation operator for the state
\Psi_{{\mathbf{k}}\alpha}({\mathbf{r}})
and

V_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf{k}_{1},\mathbf{k}_{2},\mathbf{k}_{3},\mathbf{k}_{4})=g_{\alpha\beta}\int\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathbf{r}~\Psi^{\ast}_{\mathbf{k}_{1}\alpha}(\mathbf{r})\Psi^{\ast}_{\mathbf{k}_{2}\beta}(\mathbf{r})\Psi_{\mathbf{k}_{3}\beta}(\mathbf{r})\Psi_{\mathbf{k}_{4}\alpha}(\mathbf{r}).

(59)

The expression of the interaction matrix element
V_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf{k}_{1},\mathbf{k}_{2},\mathbf{k}_{3},\mathbf{k}_{4})

that is convenient for numerical calculations is given in Ref.
[
46
]
.

3.2
Bogoliubov approximation

We now apply the Bogoliubov approximation
[
18
,
21
,
22
,
70
]
,
assuming that the condensation occurs at the wave vector
{\mathbf{q}}_{\alpha}
in the spin-
\alpha
component.
To this end, it is useful to introduce

\tilde{b}_{{\mathbf{k}}\alpha}:=b_{{\mathbf{q}}_{\alpha}+{\mathbf{k}},\alpha},~~\tilde{V}_{\alpha\beta}({\mathbf{k}}_{1},{\mathbf{k}}_{2},{\mathbf{k}}_{3},{\mathbf{k}}_{4}):=V_{\alpha\beta}({\mathbf{q}}_{\alpha}+{\mathbf{k}}_{1},{\mathbf{q}}_{\beta}+{\mathbf{k}}_{2},{\mathbf{q}}_{\beta}+{\mathbf{k}}_{3},{\mathbf{q}}_{\alpha}+{\mathbf{k}}_{4}).

(60)

By substituting

\tilde{b}_{{\mathbf{0}}\alpha}\simeq\tilde{b}_{{\mathbf{0}}\alpha}^{\dagger}\simeq\sqrt{N_{\alpha}-\sum_{{\mathbf{k}}\neq{\mathbf{0}}}\tilde{b}_{{\mathbf{k}}\alpha}^{\dagger}\tilde{b}_{{\mathbf{k}}\alpha}}

(61)

in Eq. (
58
) and retaining terms up to the second order
in
\tilde{b}_{{\mathbf{k}}\alpha}
and
\tilde{b}_{{\mathbf{k}}\alpha}^{\dagger}
(
{\mathbf{k}}\neq 0
), we obtain the Bogoliubov Hamiltonian

\begin{split}H_{\mathrm{int}}=&\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha,\beta}N_{\alpha}N_{\beta}\tilde{V}_{\alpha\beta}({\mathbf{0}},{\mathbf{0}},{\mathbf{0}},{\mathbf{0}})-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{{\mathbf{k}}\neq{\mathbf{0}}}J({\mathbf{k}})\\
&+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{{\mathbf{k}}\neq{\mathbf{0}}}\left(\tilde{b}_{{\mathbf{k}}\uparrow}^{\dagger},\tilde{b}_{{\mathbf{k}}\downarrow}^{\dagger},\tilde{b}_{-{\mathbf{k}},\uparrow},\tilde{b}_{-{\mathbf{k}},\downarrow}\right)\mathcal{M}({\mathbf{k}})\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{b}_{{\mathbf{k}}\uparrow}\\
\tilde{b}_{{\mathbf{k}}\downarrow}\\
\tilde{b}_{-{\mathbf{k}},\uparrow}^{\dagger}\\
\tilde{b}_{-{\mathbf{k}},\downarrow}^{\dagger}\end{pmatrix},\end{split}

(62)

where

\begin{split}J({\mathbf{k}}):=\sum_{\alpha,\beta}N_{\beta}\left[\tilde{V}_{\alpha\beta}({\mathbf{k}},{\mathbf{0}},{\mathbf{0}},{\mathbf{k}})-\tilde{V}_{\alpha\beta}({\mathbf{0}},{\mathbf{0}},{\mathbf{0}},{\mathbf{0}})\right]+\sum_{\alpha}N_{\alpha}\tilde{V}_{\alpha\alpha}({\mathbf{k}},{\mathbf{0}},{\mathbf{k}},{\mathbf{0}}),\end{split}

(63)

and the expression of the
4\times 4
matrix
\mathcal{M}({\mathbf{k}})
is shown in Ref.
[
46
]
.

To diagonalize Eq. (
62
), we perform the Bogoliubov transformation

\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{b}_{{\mathbf{k}}\uparrow}\\
\tilde{b}_{{\mathbf{k}}\downarrow}\\
\tilde{b}_{-{\mathbf{k}},\uparrow}^{\dagger}\\
\tilde{b}_{-{\mathbf{k}},\downarrow}^{\dagger}\end{pmatrix}=W({\mathbf{k}})\begin{pmatrix}\gamma_{{\mathbf{k}},1}\\
\gamma_{{\mathbf{k}},2}\\
\gamma_{-{\mathbf{k}},1}^{\dagger}\\
\gamma_{-{\mathbf{k}},2}^{\dagger}\end{pmatrix},~W({\mathbf{k}})=\begin{pmatrix}{\cal U}({\mathbf{k}})&{\cal V}^{*}(-{\mathbf{k}})\\
{\cal V}({\mathbf{k}})&{\cal U}^{*}(-{\mathbf{k}})\end{pmatrix}.

(64)

Here, the paraunitary matrix
W({\mathbf{k}})
is chosen to satisfy

\tau_{3}\mathcal{M}({\mathbf{k}})W({\mathbf{k}})=W({\mathbf{k}})\mathrm{diag}(E_{1}({\mathbf{k}}),E_{2}({\mathbf{k}}),-E_{1}(-{\mathbf{k}}),-E_{2}(-{\mathbf{k}})),

(65)

where
\tau_{3}:=\mathrm{diag}(1,1,-1,-1)
.
Namely,
W({\mathbf{k}})
and
E_{j}({\mathbf{k}})~(j=1,2)
are obtained by solving the right eigenvalue problem of
\tau_{3}\mathcal{M}({\mathbf{k}})
.
Equation (
62
) is then diagonalized as

\begin{split}H_{\mathrm{int}}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha,\beta}N_{\alpha}N_{\beta}\tilde{V}_{\alpha\beta}({\mathbf{0}},{\mathbf{0}},{\mathbf{0}},{\mathbf{0}})-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{{\mathbf{k}}\neq{\mathbf{0}}}J({\mathbf{k}})+\sum_{{\mathbf{k}}\neq{\mathbf{0}}}\sum_{j=1,2}E_{j}({\mathbf{k}})\left(\gamma_{{\mathbf{k}}j}^{\dagger}\gamma_{{\mathbf{k}}j}+\frac{1}{2}\right).\end{split}

(66)

We thus find that the Bogoliubov excitations (bogolons) are created by
\gamma_{{\mathbf{k}}j}^{\dagger}~(j=1,2)

and have the dispersion relations
\{E_{j}({\mathbf{k}})\}
.

The ground state of Eq. (
66
) is given by the bogolon vacuum
|0\rangle
,
which is specified by the condition that
\gamma_{{\mathbf{k}}j}|0\rangle=0
for all
{\mathbf{k}}\neq{\mathbf{0}}
and
j=1,2
.
The ground-state energy
E_{\mathrm{GS}}
(scaled by the interaction energy scale
gn^{2}A
) is therefore given by

\begin{split}\frac{E_{\mathrm{GS}}}{gn^{2}A}=&\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\frac{A}{g}\tilde{V}_{\alpha\beta}({\mathbf{0}},{\mathbf{0}},{\mathbf{0}},{\mathbf{0}})+\frac{1}{gn\nu N_{\mathrm{v}}}\sum_{{\mathbf{k}}\neq{\mathbf{0}}}\left[\sum_{j}E_{j}({\mathbf{k}})-J({\mathbf{k}})\right]\\
=&\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\frac{A}{g}\tilde{V}_{\alpha\beta}({\mathbf{0}},{\mathbf{0}},{\mathbf{0}},{\mathbf{0}})+\frac{1}{2\pi gn\nu}\int_{\mathrm{BZ}}d^{2}{\mathbf{k}}\ell^{2}\left[\sum_{j}E_{j}({\mathbf{k}})-J({\mathbf{k}})\right].\end{split}

(67)

Here, in the final expression, we take the thermodynamic limit
N_{\mathrm{v}}\to\infty
so that the sum is replaced by the integral over the Brillouin zone as

\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{v}}}\sum_{{\mathbf{k}}\neq{\mathbf{0}}}\to\frac{1}{|{\mathbf{b}}_{1}\times{\mathbf{b}}_{2}|}\int\mathrm{d}^{2}{\mathbf{k}}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathrm{BZ}}\mathrm{d}^{2}{\mathbf{k}}\ell^{2}
;
this integral is convergent as the integrand is finite over the entire Brillouin zone.
The first term of Eq. (
67
) corresponds to the mean-field ground-state energy,
which has been analyzed by Mueller and Ho
[
36
]
.
The other term gives a quantum correction and is inversely proportional to the filling factor
\nu
.
In Sec.
4.5
, we numerically calculate Eq. (
67
),
and discuss how the quantum correction affects the ground-state phase diagrams.

Using Eq. (
64
), we can calculate the following correlators in the ground state:

\displaystyle\langle 0|\tilde{b}_{{\mathbf{k}},\alpha}^{\dagger}\tilde{b}_{{\mathbf{k}},\alpha}|0\rangle=\sum_{j}|{\cal V}_{\alpha,j}(-{\mathbf{k}})|^{2},~~\langle 0|\tilde{b}_{-{\mathbf{k}},\alpha}\tilde{b}_{-{\mathbf{k}},\alpha}^{\dagger}|0\rangle=\sum_{j}|{\cal U}_{\alpha,j}(-{\mathbf{k}})|^{2},

(68a)

\displaystyle\langle 0|\tilde{b}_{-{\mathbf{k}},\alpha}\tilde{b}_{{\mathbf{k}},\alpha}|0\rangle=\sum_{j}{\cal U}_{\alpha,j}(-{\mathbf{k}}){\cal V}_{\alpha,j}^{\ast}(-{\mathbf{k}}),~~\langle 0|\tilde{b}_{{\mathbf{k}},\alpha}^{\dagger}\tilde{b}_{-{\mathbf{k}},\alpha}^{\dagger}|0\rangle=\sum_{j}{\cal U}_{\alpha,j}^{\ast}(-{\mathbf{k}}){\cal V}_{\alpha,j}(-{\mathbf{k}}).

(68b)

Here, we have nonzero “anomalous” correlators in Eq. (
68b
) as the particle numbers
N_{\uparrow}
and
N_{\downarrow}
are not conserved in the Bogoliubov Hamiltonian (
62
).
Using Eq. (
68a
), we can further calculate the fraction of depletion
n^{\prime}/n
, which is equal for the two components, as

\frac{n^{\prime}}{n}=\frac{1}{N_{\alpha}}\sum_{{\mathbf{k}}\neq{\mathbf{0}}}\langle 0|\tilde{b}_{{\mathbf{k}},\alpha}^{\dagger}\tilde{b}_{{\mathbf{k}},\alpha}|0\rangle=\frac{2}{\nu N_{\mathrm{v}}}\sum_{{\mathbf{k}}\neq{\mathbf{0}}}\sum_{j=1,2}|{\cal V}_{\alpha,j}(-{\mathbf{k}})|^{2}~~(\alpha=\uparrow,\downarrow).

(69)

As discussed in Sec.
2.6
[see Eq. (
56
)], this quantity is expected to diverge logarithmically as a function of
N_{\mathrm{v}}
.
We will confirm this behavior numerically in Sec.
4.4
.
This diverging behavior comes from the divergence of
|{\cal V}_{\alpha,2}(-{\mathbf{k}})|^{2}
for
{\mathbf{k}}\to{\mathbf{0}}
in Eq. (
69
).
We note that the Bogoliubov theory should be applied in the condition of weak depletion
n^{\prime}/n\ll 1
;
this condition is satisfied in typical experiments of ultracold atomic gases,
where
N_{\mathrm{v}}
is at most of the order of 100
[
12
]
.

3.3
Intercomponent entanglement

For the RDM
\rho_{\uparrow}
for the spin-
\uparrow

we introduce the following Gaussian ansatz
[

,

,

,

\rho_{\uparrow}=\frac{1}{Z_{\mathrm{e}}}{\mathrm{e}}^{-H_{\mathrm{e}}},~~H_{\mathrm{e}}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{{\mathbf{k}}\neq{\mathbf{0}}}\left(\tilde{b}_{{\mathbf{k}},\uparrow}^{\dagger},\tilde{b}_{-{\mathbf{k}},\uparrow}\right)M_{\mathrm{e}}({\mathbf{k}})\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{b}_{{\mathbf{k}},\uparrow}\\

By performing a Bogoliubov transformation

\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{b}_{{\mathbf{k}}\uparrow}\\

{\mathrm{e}}^{\mathrm{i}\phi_{\mathbf{k}}}\sinh\theta_{\mathbf{k}}&\cosh\theta_{\mathbf{k}}\end{pmatrix}

(72)

with

\cosh 2\theta_{\mathbf{k}}=\frac{h_{\mathbf{k}}+h_{-{\mathbf{k}}}}{2\tilde{h}_{\mathbf{k}}},~~{\mathrm{e}}^{-i\phi_{\mathbf{k}}}\sinh 2\theta_{\mathbf{k}}=\frac{\lambda_{\mathbf{k}}}{\tilde{h}_{\mathbf{k}}},~~\tilde{h}_{\mathbf{k}}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{4}\left(h_{\mathbf{k}}+h_{-{\mathbf{k}}}\right)^{2}-|\lambda_{\mathbf{k}}|^{2}},

(73)

the entanglement Hamiltonian in
H_{\mathrm{e}}
in Eq. (
70
) is diagonalized as

H_{\mathrm{e}}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{{\mathbf{k}}\neq{\mathbf{0}}}\left(\xi_{\mathbf{k}}\eta_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\eta_{\mathbf{k}}+\xi_{-{\mathbf{k}}}\eta_{-{\mathbf{k}}}\eta_{-{\mathbf{k}}}^{\dagger}\right)=\sum_{{\mathbf{k}}\neq{\mathbf{0}}}\xi_{\mathbf{k}}\left(\eta_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\eta_{\mathbf{k}}+\frac{1}{2}\right),

(74)

where

\xi_{\mathbf{k}}:=\tilde{h}_{\mathbf{k}}+\frac{h_{\mathbf{k}}-h_{-{\mathbf{k}}}}{2}

(75)

is the single-particle ES.

Using the relation (
72
) and the Bose distribution functions

\Tr\left(\eta_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}\eta_{\mathbf{k}}\rho_{\uparrow}\right)=\frac{1}{{\mathrm{e}}^{\xi_{\mathbf{k}}}-1}=f_{\mathrm{B}}(\xi_{\mathbf{k}}),~~\Tr\left(\eta_{-{\mathbf{k}}}\eta_{-{\mathbf{k}}}^{\dagger}\rho_{\uparrow}\right)=1+f_{\mathrm{B}}(\xi_{-{\mathbf{k}}})=-f_{\mathrm{B}}(-\xi_{-{\mathbf{k}}}),

(76)

we obtain

\displaystyle\Tr\left(\tilde{b}_{{\mathbf{k}},\uparrow}^{\dagger}\tilde{b}_{{\mathbf{k}},\uparrow}\rho_{\uparrow}\right)

\displaystyle=f_{\mathrm{B}}(\xi_{\mathbf{k}})\cosh^{2}\theta_{\mathbf{k}}-f_{\mathrm{B}}(-\xi_{-{\mathbf{k}}})\sinh^{2}\theta_{\mathbf{k}},

(77a)

\displaystyle\Tr\left(\tilde{b}_{-{\mathbf{k}},\uparrow}\tilde{b}_{-{\mathbf{k}},\uparrow}^{\dagger}\rho_{\uparrow}\right)

\displaystyle=-f_{\mathrm{B}}(-\xi_{-{\mathbf{k}}})\cosh^{2}\theta_{\mathbf{k}}+f_{\mathrm{B}}(\xi_{\mathbf{k}})\sinh^{2}\theta_{\mathbf{k}},

(77b)

\displaystyle 2\Tr\left(\tilde{b}_{-{\mathbf{k}},\uparrow}\tilde{b}_{{\mathbf{k}},\uparrow}\rho_{\uparrow}\right)

\displaystyle=\left[f_{\mathrm{B}}(\xi_{\mathbf{k}})-f_{\mathrm{B}}(-\xi_{-{\mathbf{k}}})\right]{\mathrm{e}}^{-i\phi_{\mathbf{k}}}\sinh\left(2\theta_{\mathbf{k}}\right).

(77c)

We require these to be equal to the correlators (
68
) with respect to the Bogoliubov ground state.
We can then express
f_{\mathrm{B}}(\xi_{\mathbf{k}})
in terms of the correlators (
68
) in the following way.
First, by taking the sum and the difference of Eqs. (
77a
) and (
77b
), we have

\displaystyle\langle\tilde{b}_{{\mathbf{k}}\uparrow}^{\dagger}\tilde{b}_{{\mathbf{k}}\uparrow}\rangle+\langle\tilde{b}_{-{\mathbf{k}},\uparrow}\tilde{b}_{-{\mathbf{k}},\uparrow}^{\dagger}\rangle

\displaystyle=\left[f_{\mathrm{B}}(\xi_{\mathbf{k}})-f_{\mathrm{B}}(-\xi_{-{\mathbf{k}}})\right]\cosh\left(2\theta_{\mathbf{k}}\right),

(78a)

\displaystyle\langle\tilde{b}_{{\mathbf{k}}\uparrow}^{\dagger}\tilde{b}_{{\mathbf{k}}\uparrow}\rangle-\langle\tilde{b}_{-{\mathbf{k}},\uparrow}\tilde{b}_{-{\mathbf{k}},\uparrow}^{\dagger}\rangle

\displaystyle=f_{\mathrm{B}}(\xi_{\mathbf{k}})+f_{\mathrm{B}}(-\xi_{-{\mathbf{k}}}),

(78b)

where we take the shorthand notation
\langle\cdot\rangle:=\langle 0|\cdot|0\rangle
.
Next, using Eqs. (
77c
) and (
78a
), we have

f_{\mathrm{B}}(\xi_{\mathbf{k}})-f_{\mathrm{B}}(-\xi_{-{\mathbf{k}}})=\sqrt{\left(\langle\tilde{b}_{{\mathbf{k}}\uparrow}^{\dagger}\tilde{b}_{{\mathbf{k}}\uparrow}\rangle+\langle\tilde{b}_{-{\mathbf{k}},\uparrow}\tilde{b}_{-{\mathbf{k}},\uparrow}^{\dagger}\rangle\right)^{2}-4\big|\langle\tilde{b}_{-{\mathbf{k}},\uparrow}\tilde{b}_{{\mathbf{k}}\uparrow}\rangle\big|^{2}}.

(79)

Lastly, using Eqs. (
78b
) and (
79
), we obtain

f_{\mathrm{B}}(\xi_{\mathbf{k}})=\frac{1}{2}\left(\langle\tilde{b}_{{\mathbf{k}}\uparrow}^{\dagger}\tilde{b}_{{\mathbf{k}}\uparrow}\rangle-\langle\tilde{b}_{-{\mathbf{k}},\uparrow}\tilde{b}_{-{\mathbf{k}},\uparrow}^{\dagger}\rangle\right)+\sqrt{\frac{1}{4}\left(\langle\tilde{b}_{{\mathbf{k}}\uparrow}^{\dagger}\tilde{b}_{{\mathbf{k}}\uparrow}\rangle+\langle\tilde{b}_{-{\mathbf{k}},\uparrow}\tilde{b}_{-{\mathbf{k}},\uparrow}^{\dagger}\rangle\right)^{2}-\big|\langle\tilde{b}_{-{\mathbf{k}},\uparrow}\tilde{b}_{{\mathbf{k}}\uparrow}\rangle\big|^{2}},

(80)

from which we can calculate the single-particle ES
\xi_{\mathbf{k}}=\ln\left[1+f_{B}(\xi_{\mathbf{k}})^{-1}\right]
.

We can further use Eq. (
80
) to calculate the intercomponent EE as

S_{\mathrm{e}}=\sum_{{\mathbf{k}}\neq{\mathbf{0}}}\left\{-f_{\mathrm{B}}(\xi_{\mathbf{k}})\ln f_{\mathrm{B}}(\xi_{\mathbf{k}})+\left[1+f_{\mathrm{B}}(\xi_{\mathbf{k}})\right]\ln\left[1+f_{\mathrm{B}}(\xi_{\mathbf{k}})\right]\right\}.

(81)

As discussed in Sec.
2.5
[see Eq. (
50
)],
S_{\mathrm{e}}
is expected to show a volume-law behavior with a subleading logarithimic correction.
The EE per flux quantum in the thermodynamic limit is then expressed in the integral form

\lim_{N_{\mathrm{v}}\to\infty}\frac{S_{\mathrm{e}}}{N_{\mathrm{v}}}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathrm{BZ}}\mathrm{d}^{2}{\mathbf{k}}\ell^{2}\left\{-f_{\mathrm{B}}(\xi_{\mathbf{k}})\ln f_{\mathrm{B}}(\xi_{\mathbf{k}})+\left[1+f_{\mathrm{B}}(\xi_{\mathbf{k}})\right]\ln\left[1+f_{\mathrm{B}}(\xi_{\mathbf{k}})\right]\right\}.

(82)

We note that the correlators (
68
) are independent of
\nu
once the lattice structure is fixed.
Therefore, the EE per flux quantum in Eq. (
82
) is also independent of
\nu
in a similar manner.
In Sec.
4.3
, we will calculate the EE per flux quantum assuming the structure in the mean-field ground state.

In this section, we present numerical results that are obtained using the Bogoliubov theory formulation of Sec.

As demonstrated by Mueller and Ho

As we lower the filling factor

5
Summary and outlook

In this paper, we have presented a detailed comparative study of vortex lattices of binary BECs in parallel and antiparallel synthetic magnetic fields.
Within the GP mean-field theory valid for high filling factors
\nu\gg 1
, the two types of fields are known to lead to the same phase diagram
that consists of a variety of vortex lattices
[
36
,
37
,
38
,
39
,
42
]
.
We have formulated an improved effective field theory for such vortex lattices
by introducing renormalized coupling constants for coarse-grained densities,
and studied properties of collective modes and ground-state intercomponent entanglement.
We have also performed numerical calculations based on the Bogoliubov theory with the LLL approximation to confirm the field-theoretical predictions.
We have shown that the low-energy excitation spectra for the two types of fields are related to each other by suitable rescaling using the renormalized coupling constants
[Eqs. (
36
) and (
37
) and Figs.
4
and
5
].
By calculating the intercomponent EE in the ground state, we have found that for an intercomponent repulsion (attraction),
the two components are more strongly entangled in the case of parallel (antiparallel) fields [Eq. (
51
) and Fig.
8
(a)],
in qualitative agreement with recent numerical results for a quantum (spin) Hall regime
[
42
,
52
]
.
As a by-product, we have also found that the ES exhibits an anomalous square-root dispersion relation [Eq. (
43
) and Fig.
7
],
and that the EE exhibits a volume-law scaling followed by a subleading logarithmic term [Eq. (
50
) and Fig.
8
(b)].
Finally, we have investigated the effects of quantum fluctuations on the phase diagrams
by calculating the correction to the ground-state energy due to zero-point fluctuations in the Bogoliubov theory [Eq. (
67
) and Fig.
10
].
We have found that the boundaries between rhombic-, square-, and rectangular-lattice phases shift appreciably with a decrease in
\nu
.

We have seen a similarity between the regimes of high (
\nu\gg 1
) and low (
\nu=O(1)
) filling factors
in the behavior of intercomponent entanglement.
It will be interesting to investigate how the two regimes are connected
by applying sophisticated numerical methods such as a variational wave function
[
22
]

and the infinite density matrix renormalization group
[
51
]
.
Furthermore, the similarity between the two regimes suggests that
the behavior of intercomponent entanglement does not depend on the details of the systems
and can be universal for a wide range of Hamiltonians.
In fact, it has been found in lattice models that two coupled bosonic Laughlin states with opposite chiralities
(i.e., fractional quantum spin Hall states
[
44
]
)
are more robust against an intercomponent repulsion than the ones with the same chirality
[
79
]
.
The stability of fractional quantum spin Hall states against an intercomponent repulsion
has also been discussed in fermionic models
[
80
,
81
,
82
,
83
]
.
Comparative investigation of multicomponent systems in gauge fields as conducted in the present work
will be a useful approach for exploring universal features of interacting topological states of matter.

Appendix B
Phase correlation function

Here we describe the derivation of the phase correlation function (
54
).
Using Eq. (
52
), this correlation function is expressed as

\langle\left[\theta_{\uparrow}({\mathbf{r}})-\theta_{\uparrow}({\mathbf{0}})\right]^{2}\rangle\approx\frac{2}{nF\ell^{2}}\left[G({\mathbf{0}};\alpha)-G({\mathbf{r}};\alpha)\right].

(92)

Here,
G({\mathbf{r}};\alpha)
is Green’s function for the 2D Poisson equation

G({\mathbf{r}};\alpha)=\frac{1}{A}\sum_{{\mathbf{k}}\neq{\mathbf{0}}}\frac{1}{k^{2}}{\mathrm{e}}^{-\alpha k+\mathrm{i}{\mathbf{k}}\cdot{\mathbf{r}}}=\frac{1}{A}\sum_{{\mathbf{k}}\neq{\mathbf{0}}}\frac{1}{k^{2}}{\mathrm{e}}^{-\alpha k+\mathrm{i}kr\cos\theta},

(93)

where we express the wave vector
{\mathbf{k}}
using the polar coordinate
(k,\theta)
with
\theta
being the angle relative to
{\mathbf{r}}
.
Although the logarithmic behavior of Green’s function for the 2D Poisson equation is known,
we derive it within the present regularization scheme using the convergence factor
{\mathrm{e}}^{-\alpha k}
.

By differentiating Eq. (
93
) with respect to
r
, we have

\begin{split}-\frac{\partial}{\partial r}G({\mathbf{r}};\alpha)&=\frac{1}{A}\sum_{{\mathbf{k}}\neq{\mathbf{0}}}\frac{-\mathrm{i}\cos\theta}{k}{\mathrm{e}}^{-\alpha k+\mathrm{i}kr\cos\theta}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\!\!\!\!\mathrm{d}\theta\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\!\!\mathrm{d}k~(-\mathrm{i}\cos\theta){\mathrm{e}}^{-k(\alpha-\mathrm{i}r\cos\theta)}\\
&=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathrm{d}\theta\frac{-\mathrm{i}\cos\theta}{\alpha-\mathrm{i}r\cos\theta}.\end{split}

(94)

With a change of the integration variable as
z={\mathrm{e}}^{\mathrm{i}\theta}
, the last integral can be written as a contour integral along the unit circle:

-\frac{\partial}{\partial r}G({\mathbf{r}};\alpha)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\oint\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{\mathrm{i}z}\frac{z^{2}+1}{r(z^{2}+1)+2\mathrm{i}\alpha z}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}\mathrm{i}r}\oint\mathrm{d}z\frac{z^{2}+1}{z(z-z_{+})(z-z_{-})},

(95)

where
z_{\pm}=\mathrm{i}(-\alpha\pm\sqrt{r^{2}+\alpha^{2}})/r
are the locations of poles.
Since
|z_{+}|<1<|z_{-}|
, the integral picks up the residues at
z=0
and
z_{+}
, leading to

-\frac{\partial}{\partial r}G({\mathbf{r}};\alpha)=\frac{1}{2\pi r}\left[\frac{1}{z_{+}z_{-}}+\frac{z_{+}^{2}+1}{z_{+}(z_{+}-z_{-})}\right]=\frac{1}{2\pi r}\left(1-\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{r^{2}+\alpha^{2}}}\right).

(96)

Therefore,
G({\mathbf{0}};\alpha)-G({\mathbf{r}};\alpha)
in Eq. (
92
) can be calculated as

\begin{split}G({\mathbf{0}};\alpha)-G({\mathbf{r}};\alpha)&=\frac{1}{2\pi}\lim_{a_{0}\to 0}\int_{a_{0}}^{r}\mathrm{d}r^{\prime}\left(\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}-\frac{\alpha}{r^{\prime}\sqrt{{r^{\prime}}^{2}+\alpha^{2}}}\right)\\
&=\frac{1}{2\pi}\lim_{a_{0}\to 0}\left(\ln\frac{r}{a_{0}}+\mathrm{arsinh}~\frac{\alpha}{r}-\mathrm{arsinh}~\frac{\alpha}{a_{0}}\right)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\left(\ln\frac{r}{2\alpha}+\mathrm{arsinh}~\frac{\alpha}{r}\right).\end{split}

(97)

By substituting this into Eq. (
92
) and taking the limit
r\gg\alpha
,
we obtain Eq. (
54
).

Degenerate r r -Whitney numbers and degenerate r r -Dowling polynomials via boson operators
Taekyun Kim, DAE SAN KIM

Abstract
. Dowling showed that the Whitney numbers of the first kind and of the second kind satisfy Stirling number-like relations. Recently, Kim-Kim introduced the degenerate r r -Whitney numbers of the first kind and of the second kind, as degenerate versions and further generalizations of the Whitney numbers of both kinds. The normal ordering of an integral power of the number operator in terms of boson operators is expressed with the help of the Stirling numbers of the second kind. In this paper, it is noted that the normal ordering of a certain quantity involving the number operator is expressed in terms of the degenerate r r -Whitney numbers of the second kind. We derive some properties, recurrence relations, orthogonality relations and several identities on those numbers from such normal ordering. In addition, we consider the degenerate r r -Dowling polynomials as a natural extension of the degenerate r r -Whitney numbers of the second kind and investigate their properties.

For any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, the degenerate exponential functions are defined by

(3)

e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(x)_{k,\lambda}}{k!}t^{k},\quad e_{\lambda}(t)=e_{\lambda}^{1}(t),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [11]).

Here we recall that the generalized falling factorial sequence is defined by

(4)

(x)_{0,\lambda}=1,\quad(x)_{n,\lambda}=x(x-\lambda)(x-(n-1)\lambda),\quad(n\geq 1),

and that the generalized rising factorial sequence is given by

(5)

\langle x\rangle_{0,\lambda}=1,\quad\langle x\rangle_{n,\lambda}=x(x+\lambda)(x+2\lambda)\cdots(x+(n+1)\lambda),\quad(n\geq 1).

The degenerate Stirling numbers of the first kind are defined by

(6)

(x)_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{1,\lambda}(n,k)(x)_{k,\lambda},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [1-16]),

where
(x)_{0}=1,\ (x)_{n}=x(x-1)\cdots(x-n+1),\ (n\geq 1)
.

Note that
\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}S_{1,\lambda}(n,k)=S_{1}(n,k)\displaystyle
, where

are the ordinary Stirling number of the first kind given by

(x)_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{1}(n,k)x^{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [8,10,12,13]).

As the inversion formula of (
6
), the degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind are defined by

(7)

(x)_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)(x)_{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [8]).

Note that
\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)=S_{2}(n,k)\displaystyle
, where
S_{2}(n,k)
are the ordinary Stirling numbers of the second kind defined by

x^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2}(n,k)(x)_{k},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [10,13]).

For

, the unsigned degenerate
r
-Stirling numbers of the first kind are defined by

(8)

\langle x+r\rangle_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n+r\brack k+r}_{r,\lambda}\langle x\rangle_{k,\lambda},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [13,14]),

\langle x\rangle_{0}=1,\ \langle x\rangle_{n}=x(x+1)\cdots(x+n-1),\ (n\geq 1)

In view of (
7
), the degenerate
r
-Stirling numbers of the second kind are defined by

(9)

(x+r)_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n+r\brace k+r}_{r,\lambda}(x)_{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [13,14]).

The Whitney numbers
W_{m}(n,k)
and
V_{m}(n,k)

(10)

(mx+1)^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}W_{m}(n,k)m^{k}(x)_{k},\quad(n\geq 0),

(11)

m^{n}(x)_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}V_{m}(n,k)(mx+1)^{k},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [2,3,4,5,10]).

For
n\geq 0
, Dowling polynomials are defined by

(12)

D_{m}(n,x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}W_{m}(n,k)x^{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [2,3,4,5,10]).

We remark that, as generalizations of Whitney numbers of both kinds, the
r
-Whitney numbers of the second

(mx+r)^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}W_{m}^{(r)}(n,k)m^{k}(x)_{k},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [10]),

\displaystyle(mx+1)_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}W_{m,\lambda}(n,k)m^{k}(x)_{k},\quad(n\geq 0),

\displaystyle m^{n}(x)_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}V_{m,\lambda}(n,k)(mx+1)_{k,\lambda},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [10]).

As further generalizations of the degenerate Whitney numbers of both kinds, the degenerate

Note that

When

The boson operators

and

The number states

(22)

a|m\rangle=\sqrt{m}|m-1\rangle,\quad a^{+}|m\rangle=\sqrt{m+1}|m+1\rangle.

The coherent states

2.
Degenerate
r
-Whitney numbers and
r
-Dowling polynomials via boson operators

First, we recall that the standard bosonic commutation relations
aa^{+}-a^{+}a=1
can be considered formally, in a suitable space of functions
f
, by letting
a=\frac{d}{dx}
and
a^{+}=x
(the operator of multiplication by
x
). From the definition of coherent state
a|z\rangle=z|z\rangle
, we note that
\langle z|a=\langle z|\bar{z}
, where
z\in\mathbb{C}
.

Let
f
be a polynomial. Then we easily get

(23)

\displaystyle\bigg(x\frac{d}{dx}\bigg)_{n,\lambda}f(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)x^{k}\bigg(\frac{d}{dx}\bigg)^{k}f(x)

\displaystyle x^{n}\bigg(\frac{d}{dx}\bigg)^{n}f(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{1,\lambda}(n,k)\bigg(x\frac{d}{dx}\bigg)_{k,\lambda}f(x),\quad(n\geq 0).

In view of (
23
), the normal ordering of the degenerate
n
-th power of the number operator
a^{+}a
in terms of boson operators
a,a^{+}
can be written in the form

(24)

\displaystyle(a^{+}a)_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)(a^{+})^{k}a^{k},

(25)

\displaystyle(a^{+})^{n}a^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{1,\lambda}(n,k)(a^{+}a)_{k,\lambda},\quad(n\geq 0).

Now, (
17
) and (
18
) can be written as

(26)

m^{n}(a^{+})^{n}a^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}V_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}(n,k)(ma^{+}a+r)_{k,\lambda},

and

(27)

(ma^{+}a+r)_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}W_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}(n,k)m^{k}(a^{+})^{k}a^{k},\quad(n\geq 0).

In [10], it is shown that

V_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}(n,k)=W_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}(n,k)=0\quad\textrm{if $n<k$ or $k<0$},

and
V_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}(0,0)=W_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}(0,0)=1
.

For
k\in\mathbb{N}
, by (
22
), we get

(28)

(a^{+})^{k}a^{k}|m\rangle=(m)_{k}|m\rangle,

where
m
is nonnegative integer.

From (
26
) and (
27
), we note that

(29)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}V_{m,\lambda}^{(0)}(n,k)(ma^{+}a)_{k,\lambda}=m^{n}(a^{+})^{n}a^{n}

\displaystyle=m^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{1,\frac{\lambda}{m}}(n,k)(a^{+}a)_{k,\frac{\lambda}{m}}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}m^{n-k}S_{1,\frac{\lambda}{m}}(n,k)(ma^{+}a)_{k,\lambda},

and

(30)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}W_{m,\lambda}^{(0)}(n,k)m^{k}(a^{+})^{k}a^{k}

\displaystyle=(ma^{+}a)_{m,\lambda}=m^{n}(a^{+}a)_{n,\frac{\lambda}{m}}

\displaystyle=m^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2,\frac{\lambda}{m}}(n,k)(a^{+})^{k}a^{k}.

Therefore, by (
29
) and (
30
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 1
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

V_{m,\lambda}^{(0)}(n,k)=m^{n-k}S_{1,\frac{\lambda}{m}}(n,k),\quad W_{m,\lambda}^{(0)}(n,k)=m^{n-k}S_{2,\frac{\lambda}{m}}(n,k).

From (
8
), we note that

(31)

\displaystyle(x-r)_{n}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}\langle-x+r\rangle_{n}=(-1)^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n+r\brack k+r}_{r,\lambda}\langle-x\rangle_{k,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-k}{n+r\brack k+r}_{r,\lambda}(x)_{k,\lambda},

and hence that

(32)

\displaystyle(x)_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-k}{n+r\brack k+r}_{r,\lambda}(x+r)_{k,\lambda}.

Let
f
be a polynomial and let
D=\frac{d}{dx}
. Then, by (
32

(33)

\displaystyle x^{n}D^{n}f(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-k}{n+r\brack k+r}_{r,\lambda}(xD+r)_{k,\lambda}f(x),

and, by inversion, we have

(34)

\displaystyle(xD+r)_{n,\lambda}f(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n+r\brace k+r}_{r,\lambda}x^{k}D^{k}f(x).

From (
33
) and (
34
), we note that

(35)

\displaystyle(a^{+})a^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-k}{n+r\brack k+r}_{r,\lambda}(a^{+}a+r)_{k,\lambda},

and

(36)

\displaystyle(a^{+}a+r)_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n+r\brace k+r}_{r,\lambda}(a^{+})^{k}a^{k}.

By (
26
), (
27
), (
35
) and (

\displaystyle(a^{+})^{n}a^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}V_{1,\lambda}^{(r)}(n,k)(a^{+}a+r)_{k,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-k}{n+r\brack k+r}_{r,\lambda}(a^{+}a+r)_{k,\lambda},

and

(38)

\displaystyle(a^{+}a+r)_{n,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}W_{1,\lambda}^{(r)}(n,k)(a^{+})^{k}a^{k}

Therefore, by (
37
) and (
38

Theorem 2
.

For
n,k\geq 0
with
n\geq k

\displaystyle V_{1,\lambda}^{(r)}(n,k)=(-1)^{n-k}{n+r\brack k+r}_{r,\lambda},

\displaystyle W_{1,\lambda}^{(r)}(n,k)={n+r\brace k+r}_{r,\lambda}.

From (
24
) and (
26

(39)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}V_{m,m\lambda}^{(r)}(n,k)(ma^{+}a+r)_{k,m\lambda}=m^{n}(a^{+})^{n}a^{n}

\displaystyle=m^{n}\sum_{i=0}^{n}S_{1,\lambda}(n,i)(a^{+}a)_{i,\lambda}=m^{n}\sum_{i=0}^{n}S_{1,\lambda}(n,i)\bigg(\frac{ma^{+}a+r-r}{m}\bigg)_{i,\lambda}

Therefore, by comparing the coefficients on both sides of (

Theorem 3
.

V_{m,m\lambda}^{(r)}(n,k)=\sum_{i=k}^{n}\binom{i}{k}m^{n-i}S_{1,\lambda}(n,i)(-r)_{i-k,m\lambda}.

By (
25
) and (

), we get

(40)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}W_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}(n,k)m^{k}(a^{+})^{k}a^{k}=(ma^{+}a+r)_{n,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}(r)_{n-i,\lambda}(ma^{+}a)_{i,\lambda}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}(r)_{n-i,\lambda}m^{i}(a^{+}a)_{i,\frac{\lambda}{m}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}(r)_{n-i,\lambda}m^{i}\sum_{k=0}^{i}S_{2,\frac{\lambda}{m}}(i,k)(a^{+})^{k}a^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\bigg\{\sum_{i=k}^{n}\binom{n}{i}(r)_{n-i,\lambda}m^{i}S_{2,\frac{\lambda}{m}}(i,k)\bigg\}(a^{+})^{k}a^{k}.

Therefore, by comparing the coefficients on both sides of (

), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 4
.

For
n,k\geq 0
with
n\geq k
, we have

W_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}(n,k)=\sum_{i=k}^{n}\binom{n}{i}(r)_{n-i,\lambda}m^{i-k}S_{2,\frac{\lambda}{m}}(i,k).

The degenerate unsigned Stirling numbers of the first kind are defined by

(41)

\langle x\rangle_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brack k}_{\lambda}\langle x\rangle_{k,\lambda},\quad(n\geq 0).

Thus, by (

(42)

(-1)^{n-i}S_{1,\lambda}(n,i)={n\brack i}_{\lambda},\quad(n\geq i\geq 0).

Let us take

. Then, by Theorems 2, 3 and 4, we get

\sum_{i=k}^{n}\binom{i}{k}S_{1,\lambda}(n,i)(-r)_{i-k,\lambda}=V_{1,\lambda}^{(r)}(n,k)=(-1)^{n-k}{n+r\brack k+r}_{r,\lambda},

\sum_{i=k}^{n}\binom{n}{i}(r)_{n-i,\lambda}S_{2,\lambda}(i,k)=W_{1,\lambda}^{(r)}(n,k)={n+r\brace k+r}_{r,\lambda}.

Therefore, by (
42
), (
43
) and (

), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 5
.

For
n,k\geq 0
with

, we have

and

{n+r\brace k+r}_{r,\lambda}=\sum_{i=k}^{n}\binom{n}{i}(r)_{n-i,\lambda}S_{2,\lambda}(i,k).

We claim that

(45)

[a^{n},a^{+}]=a^{n}a^{+}-a^{+}a^{n}=na^{n-1},\quad(n\in\mathbb{N}).

Note that

Then we have

\displaystyle[a^{n+1},a^{+}]

\displaystyle=a^{n+1}a^{+}-a^{+}a^{n+1}=a(a^{n}a^{+})-a^{+}a^{n+1}

\displaystyle=a(a^{+}a^{n}+na^{n-1})-a^{+}a^{n+1}

\displaystyle=na^{n}+(aa^{+}-a^{+}a)a^{n}=(n+1)a^{n}.

In a similar manner to (

[a,(a^{+})^{n}]=a(a^{+})^{n}-(a^{+})^{n}a=n(a^{+})^{n-1},\quad(n\in\mathbb{N}).

By (

(47)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}V_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}(n+1,k)(ma^{+}a+r)_{k,\lambda}=m^{n+1}(a^{+})^{n+1}a^{n+1}

\displaystyle=m^{n+1}(a^{+})^{n}(a^{+}a^{n})a=m^{n+1}(a^{+})^{n}(a^{n}a^{+}-na^{n-1})a

\displaystyle=m^{n}(a^{+})^{n}a^{n}(ma^{+}a)-(mn)m^{n}(a^{+})^{n}a^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}V_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}(n,k)(ma^{+}a+r)_{k,\lambda}(ma^{+}a+r-k\lambda+k\lambda-r)

\displaystyle\quad-mn\sum_{k=0}^{n}V_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}(n,k)(ma^{+}a+r)_{k,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}V_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}(n,k)(ma^{+}a+r)_{k+1,\lambda}+\sum_{k=0}^{n}(k\lambda-r-nm)V_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}(n,k)(ma^{+}a+r)_{k,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}V_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}(n,k-1)(ma^{+}a+r)_{k,\lambda}+\sum_{k=0}^{n}(k\lambda-r-nm)V_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}(n,k)(ma^{+}a+r)_{k,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}\Big\{V_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}(n,k-1)+(k\lambda-r-mn)V_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}(n,k)\big\}(ma^{+}a+r)_{k,\lambda}.

Therefore, by comparing the coefficients on both sides of (

Theorem 6
.

For
n,k\in\mathbb{N}
with

From (

Theorem 7

From Theorem 6, we note that

\displaystyle V_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}(n+1,0)

\displaystyle=-(r+mn)V_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}(n,0)=(-1)^{2}(r+mn)(r+m(n-1))V_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}(n-1,0)

\displaystyle V_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}(n,0)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}(mk+r),\quad(n\in\mathbb{N}).

By Theorem 7, we get

From Theorems 2 and 6, we have

(53)

\displaystyle(-1)^{n+1-k}{n+1+r\brack k+r}_{r,\lambda}

(54)

Also, from Theorems 2 and 7, we get

(55)

\displaystyle{n+r+1\brace k+r}_{r,\lambda}

\displaystyle=W_{1,\lambda}^{(r)}(n+1,k)=W_{1,\lambda}^{(r)}(n,k-1)+(k+r-n\lambda)W_{1,\lambda}^{(r)}(n,k)

Therefore, by (
54

Theorem 8
.

For
n,k\geq 0
with

\displaystyle{n+r+1\brack k+r}_{r,\lambda}={n+r\brack k+r-1}_{r,\lambda}+(r+n-k\lambda){n+r\brack k+r}_{r,\lambda},

\displaystyle{n+r+1\brace k+r}_{r,\lambda}={n+r\brace k+r-1}_{r,\lambda}+(r+k-n\lambda){n+r\brace k+r}_{r,\lambda}.

On the one hand, from (

(56)

On the other hand, from (

), we get

(57)

Therefore, by (
56
) and (

), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 9
.

For
n,l\in\mathbb{Z}
with
n\geq l\geq 0
, we have

From (
27

(58)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}m^{k}W_{m,\lambda}^{(r+1)}(n,k)(a^{+})^{k}a^{k}=(ma^{+}a+r+1)_{n,\lambda}

Therefore, by comparing the coefficients on both sides of (
58
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 10
.

For
n,k\in\mathbb{Z}
with
n\geq k\geq 0
, we have

W_{m,\lambda}^{(r+1)}(n,k)=\sum_{l=k}^{n}\binom{n}{l}(1)_{n-l,\lambda}W_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}(l,k).

From Theorems 2 and 9, we note that

\displaystyle(-1)^{n-l}{n+r+1\brack l+r+1}_{r+1,\lambda}

\displaystyle=V_{1,\lambda}^{(r+1)}(n,l)=\sum_{k=l}^{n}\binom{k}{l}(-1)^{k-l}\langle 1\rangle_{k-l,\lambda}V_{1,\lambda}^{(r)}(n,k)

(59)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=l}^{n}\binom{k}{l}(-1)^{k-l}\langle 1\rangle_{k-l,\lambda}(-1)^{n-k}{n+r\brack k+r}_{r,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=l}^{n}\binom{k}{l}(-1)^{n-l}\langle 1\rangle_{k-l,\lambda}{n+r\brack k+r}_{r,\lambda},

and from Theorems 2 and 10, we observe that

(60)

\displaystyle{n+r+1\brace k+r+1}_{r+1,\lambda}=W_{1,\lambda}^{(r+1)}(n,k)

Therefore, by (

Theorem 11
.

For

with

On the one hand, from (

\displaystyle f_{n}=\sum_{l=0}^{n}V_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}(n,l)g_{l}\displaystyle

In view of (
19

We recall that the coherent state

|\gamma\rangle=e^{-\frac{|d|^{2}}{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\gamma^{k}}{\sqrt{k!}}|k\rangle,

\displaystyle\langle\gamma|(ma^{+}a+\gamma)_{n,\lambda}|\gamma\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}m^{k}W_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}(n,k)|\gamma|^{2k}.

Theorem 14

Taking

Theorem 15

For any

3.
Conclusion

Explorations for degenerate versions of some special numbers and polynomials, which began by Carlitz’s work on the degenerate Bernoulli and degenerate Euler polynomials, have recently regained interests of many mathematicians.
In this paper, we derived some properties, recurrence relations, orthogonality relations and several identities on the degenerate
r
-Whitney numbers of both kinds from the normal ordering of
(ma^{+}a+r)_{n,\lambda}
in (
26
) and its inversion in (
27
). The normal ordering is expressed with the help of the degenerate
r
-Whitney numbers of the second kind, while its inversion is represented by means of the degenerate
r
-Whitney numbers of the first kind. In addition, the degenerate
r
-Dowling polynomials are considered as a natural extension of the degenerate
r
-Whitney numbers of the second kind and their properties are investigated.

It is one of our future projects to continue to explore various degenerate versions of many special polynomials and numbers by using such tools as mathematical physics, combinatorial methods, generating functions, umbral calculus techniques,
p
-adic analysis, differential equations, special functions, probability theory and analytic number theory.

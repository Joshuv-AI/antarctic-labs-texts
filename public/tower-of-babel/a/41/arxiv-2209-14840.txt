Necessary and sufficient conditions for a subclass of P P -tensor
R. Deb, A. K. Das

Abstract
In this article, we introduce the class B B -Nekrasov tensor in the context of tensor complementarity problem. We study some tensor theoretic properties. We show that the class of B-Nekrasov tensor contains the class of Nekrasov Z Z -tensor with positive diagonal entries. We present a necessary and sufficient condition for a B B -Nekrasov tensor. We show that the class of P P -tensor contains the class of B B -Nekrasov tensor. Keywords: Tensor complementarity problem, P P -tensors, Nekrasov tensors, Nekrasov Z Z -tensor, B B -Nekrasov tensors, Nonsingular H H -tensor, Diagonally dominant tensor. \mathscr{A}=\mathscr{B}^{+}+\mathscr{C}
where
\mathscr{B}^{+}
and
\mathscr{C}
are given by (
3.1
). We prove that the tensor
\mathscr{B^{+}}
is a Nekrasov tensor. Now

\displaystyle(0\leq)\;\Lambda_{1}(\mathscr{B}^{+})

\displaystyle=\sum_{(i_{2},...,i_{m})\neq(i,...,i)}|b_{1i_{2}...i_{m}}^{+}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{(i_{2},...,i_{m})\neq(i,...,i)}|a_{1i_{2}...i_{m}}-r_{1}^{+}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{(i_{2},...,i_{m})\neq(i,...,i)}(r_{1}^{+}-a_{1i_{2}...i_{m}})

\displaystyle=(n^{m-1}-1)r_{1}^{+}-\sum_{(i_{2},...,i_{m})\neq(i,...,i)}a_{1i_{2}...i_{m}}

\displaystyle=\left(n^{m-1}r_{1}^{+}-\sum_{i_{2},...,i_{m}\in[n]}a_{1i_{2}...i_{m}}\right)+(a_{11\cdots 1}-r_{1}^{+}).

(3.9)

Now from (a)
\sum_{i_{2}...i_{m}\in[n]^{m-1}}a_{1i_{2}...i_{m}}>0
and
\frac{1}{n^{m-1}}\left(\sum_{i_{2}...i_{m}\in[n]^{m-1}}a_{1i_{2}...i_{m}}\right)>a_{1j_{2}\cdots j_{m}},
for
j_{2},\cdots,j_{m}\in[n].
Which implies

r_{1}^{+}=\max\{0,a_{1i_{2}...i_{m}}\}<\frac{1}{n^{m-1}}\left(\sum_{i_{2}...i_{m}\in[n]^{m-1}}a_{1i_{2}...i_{m}}\right)

(3.10)

Therefore,

\left(n^{m-1}r_{1}^{+}-\sum_{i_{2},...,i_{m}\in[n]}a_{1i_{2}...i_{m}}\right)<0.

(3.11)

Now from (
3.9
) and (
3.11
) we obtain,

\Lambda_{1}(\mathscr{B}^{+})<a_{11\cdots 1}-r_{1}^{+}=|a_{11...1}-r_{1}^{+}|=|b_{1\cdots 1}^{+}|.

(3.12)

Thus for
i=1,
the tensor
\mathscr{B}^{+}
satisfies the condition of Nekrasov tensor.

Now for
i=2,3,...,n
, we have

\displaystyle 0\leq\Lambda_{i}(\mathscr{B}^{+})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i_{2}...i_{m}\in[i-1]^{m-1}}|b^{+}_{ii_{2}...i_{m}}|\left(\frac{\Lambda_{i_{2}}(\mathscr{B}^{+})}{|b^{+}_{i_{2}i_{2}...i_{2}}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}\cdots\left(\frac{\Lambda_{i_{m}}(\mathscr{B}^{+})}{|b^{+}_{i_{m}i_{m}...i_{m}}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{i_{2}...i_{m}\notin[i-1]^{m-1},(i_{2}...i_{m})\neq(i...i)}|b^{+}_{ii_{2}...i_{m}}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{i_{2}...i_{m}\in[i-1]^{m-1}}|a_{ii_{2}\cdots i_{m}}-r_{i}^{+}|\left(\frac{\Lambda_{i_{2}}(\mathscr{B}^{+})}{|b^{+}_{i_{2}i_{2}...i_{2}}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}\cdots\left(\frac{\Lambda_{i_{m}}(\mathscr{B}^{+})}{|b^{+}_{i_{m}i_{m}...i_{m}}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{i_{2}...i_{m}\notin[i-1]^{m-1},(i_{2}...i_{m})\neq(i...i)}|a_{ii_{2}\cdots i_{m}}-r_{i}^{+}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{i_{2}...i_{m}\in[i-1]^{m-1}}(r_{i}^{+}-a_{ii_{2}\cdots i_{m}})\left(\frac{\Lambda_{i_{2}}(\mathscr{B}^{+})}{|b^{+}_{i_{2}i_{2}...i_{2}}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}\cdots\left(\frac{\Lambda_{i_{m}}(\mathscr{B}^{+})}{|b^{+}_{i_{m}i_{m}...i_{m}}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{i_{2}...i_{m}\notin[i-1]^{m-1},(i_{2}...i_{m})\neq(i...i)}(r_{i}^{+}-a_{ii_{2}\cdots i_{m}})

\displaystyle=r_{i}^{+}\sum_{i_{2}...i_{m}\in[i-1]^{m-1}}\alpha_{i_{2}}\cdots\alpha_{i_{m}}-\sum_{i_{2}...i_{m}\in[i-1]^{m-1}}a_{ii_{2}\cdots i_{m}}\alpha_{i_{2}}\cdots\alpha_{i_{m}}

\displaystyle\qquad+(n^{m-1}-(i-1)^{m-1}-1)r_{i}^{+}-\sum_{i_{2}...i_{m}\notin[i-1]^{m-1},(i_{2}...i_{m})\neq(i...i)}a_{ii_{2}\cdots i_{m}}

\displaystyle=\left[r_{i}^{+}\left(\sum_{i_{2}...i_{m}\in[i-1]^{m-1}}\alpha_{i_{2}}\cdots\alpha_{i_{m}}+n^{m-1}-(i-1)^{m-1}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\quad\left.-\sum_{i_{2}...i_{m}\in[i-1]^{m-1}}a_{ii_{2}\cdots i_{m}}\alpha_{i_{2}}\cdots\alpha_{i_{m}}-\sum_{i_{2}...i_{m}\notin[i-1]^{m-1}}a_{ii_{2}\cdots i_{m}}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-r_{i}^{+}+a_{i\cdots i}

(3.13)

Now by (b) we have
r_{i}^{+}<\min\left\{\frac{\sum_{i_{2}...i_{m}\in[n]}a_{ii_{2}...i_{m}}\alpha_{i_{2}}\cdots\alpha_{i_{m}}+\sum_{i_{2}...i_{m}\notin[i-1]^{m-1}}a_{ii_{2}\cdots i_{m}}}{\sum_{i_{2}...i_{m}\in[i-1]^{m-1}}\alpha_{i_{2}}\cdots\alpha_{i_{m}}+n^{m-1}-(i-1)^{m-1}},a_{i...i}\right\}
, where
i=2,3,...,n.
This implies

\displaystyle r_{i}^{+}\left(\sum_{i_{2}...i_{m}\in[i-1]^{m-1}}\alpha_{i_{2}}\cdots\alpha_{i_{m}}+n^{m-1}-(i-1)^{m-1}\right)

\displaystyle<\sum_{i_{2}...i_{m}\in[i-1]^{m-1}}a_{ii_{2}\cdots i_{m}}\alpha_{i_{2}}\cdots\alpha_{i_{m}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{i_{2}...i_{m}\notin[i-1]^{m-1}}a_{ii_{2}\cdots i_{m}}

(3.14)

Now by (
3.13
) and (
3.14
) we conclude that

\Lambda_{i}(\mathscr{B}^{+})<a_{i\cdots i}-r_{i}^{+}=|a_{i\cdots i}-r_{i}^{+}|=|b_{i\cdots i}^{+}|,

(3.15)

Therefore
\mathscr{B}^{+}
is a Nekrasov tensor. Note that by construction
\mathscr{B}^{+}
is a
Z
-tensor and by the conditions (a) and (b), we have
b_{i...i}^{+}=a_{i...i}-r_{i}^{+}>0\;\forall\;i\in[n].
Therefore
\mathscr{B}^{+}
is a Nekrasov
Z
-tensor with positive diagonal entries. Hence
\mathscr{A}
is a
B
-Nekrasov tensor.

Conversely, let us assume that
\mathscr{A}
is a
B
-Nekrasov tensor. We prove that (a) and (b) hold. Since

is a Nekrasov tensor with positive diagonal entries, we have

\displaystyle=|a_{11...1}-r_{1}^{+}|

\displaystyle>\sum_{(i_{2},...,i_{m})\neq(i,...,i)}|a_{1i_{2}...i_{m}}-r_{1}^{+}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{(i_{2},...,i_{m})\neq(i,...,i)}(r_{1}^{+}-a_{1i_{2}...i_{m}})

\displaystyle=(n^{m-1}-1)r_{1}^{+}-\sum_{(i_{2},...,i_{m})\neq(i,...,i)}a_{1i_{2}...i_{m}},

and so,

r_{1}^{+}<\frac{1}{n^{m-1}}\left(\sum_{i_{2}...i_{m}\in[n]^{m-1}}a_{1i_{2}...i_{m}}\right).

(3.16)

This implies
\sum_{i_{2}...i_{m}\in[n]^{m-1}}a_{1i_{2}...i_{m}}>0
and
\frac{1}{n^{m-1}}\left(\sum_{i_{2}...i_{m}\in[n]^{m-1}}a_{1i_{2}...i_{m}}\right)>a_{1j_{2}\cdots j_{m}},
for

and

Hence condition (a) holds.

Again since
\mathscr{B}^{+}
is a Nekrasov tensor with positive diagonal entries,
|a_{ii...i}-r_{i}^{+}|

=a_{ii...i}-r_{i}^{+}
and we also have,

\displaystyle a_{ii...i}-r_{i}^{+}

\displaystyle=|a_{ii...i}-r_{i}^{+}|

\displaystyle>\Lambda_{i}(\mathscr{B}^{+})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i_{2}...i_{m}\in[i-1]^{m-1}}|a_{ii_{2}\cdots i_{m}}-r_{i}^{+}|\alpha_{i_{2}}\cdots\alpha_{i_{m}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{i_{2}...i_{m}\notin[i-1]^{m-1},(i_{2}...i_{m})\neq(i...i)}|a_{ii_{2}\cdots i_{m}}-r_{i}^{+}|

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{i_{2}...i_{m}\notin[i-1]^{m-1},(i_{2}...i_{m})\neq(i...i)}(r_{i}^{+}-a_{ii_{2}\cdots i_{m}})

\displaystyle=r_{i}^{+}\sum_{i_{2}...i_{m}\in[i-1]^{m-1}}\alpha_{i_{2}}\cdots\alpha_{i_{m}}-\sum_{i_{2}...i_{m}\in[i-1]^{m-1}}a_{ii_{2}\cdots i_{m}}\alpha_{i_{2}}\cdots\alpha_{i_{m}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\left(n^{m-1}-(i-1)^{m-1}-1\right)r_{i}^{+}-\sum_{i_{2}...i_{m}\notin[i-1]^{m-1},(i_{2}...i_{m})\neq(i...i)}a_{ii_{2}\cdots i_{m}}.

which scales the Nekrasov tensor and transforms it to a strictly diagonally dominated tensor.

theorem 3.2
:

Let for even
m,

\mathscr{B}=(b_{i_{1}i_{2}...i_{m}})\in\mathbb{R}^{[m,n]}
be a Nekrasov tensor such that, for each
i=1,2,...,n-1,\;b_{ii_{2}...i_{m}}\neq 0
for some
i_{2},...,i_{m}>i.
Then the matrix
W=diag(w_{1},...,w_{n}),
with
w_{i}=\left(\frac{\Lambda_{i}(\mathscr{B})}{|b_{i...i}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}
for
i\in[n-1]
and
w_{n}=\left(\frac{\Lambda_{n}(\mathscr{B})}{|b_{n...n}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}+\epsilon,

\epsilon\in\left(0,1-\left(\frac{\Lambda_{n}(\mathscr{B})}{|b_{n...n}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}\right),
has positive diagonal entries and the tensor
\mathscr{A}=\mathscr{B}W
is strictly diagonally dominated tensor.

Proof.

Note that, by the hypothesis, for each
i\in[n-1],

b_{ii_{2}...i_{m}}\neq 0
for some
i_{2},...,i_{m}>i.
Then
\Lambda_{i}(\mathscr{B})>0
for
i\in[n-1]
and so the diagonal entries of the matrix
W
are all positive. Let
W=diag(w_{1},...,w_{n}).
Now for
i\in[n-1]
we have,

\displaystyle|b_{i...i}|w_{i}^{m-1}

\displaystyle=\Lambda_{i}(\mathscr{B})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i_{2}...i_{m}\in[i-1]^{m-1}}|b_{ii_{2}...i_{m}}|\left(\frac{\Lambda_{i_{2}}(\mathscr{B})}{|b_{i_{2}i_{2}...i_{2}}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}\cdots\left(\frac{\Lambda_{i_{m}}(\mathscr{B})}{|b_{i_{m}i_{m}...i_{m}}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{(i_{2}...i_{m})\in[n-1]^{m-1}\backslash\ [i-1]^{m-1},(i_{2}...i_{m})\neq(i...i)}|b_{ii_{2}...i_{m}}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{i_{2}...i_{m}\in[i-1]^{m-1}}|b_{ii_{2}...i_{m}}|\left(\frac{\Lambda_{i_{2}}(\mathscr{B})}{|b_{i_{2}i_{2}...i_{2}}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}\cdots\left(\frac{\Lambda_{i_{m}}(\mathscr{B})}{|b_{i_{m}i_{m}...i_{m}}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{(i_{2}...i_{m})\in[n-1]^{m-1}\backslash\ [i-1]^{m-1},(i_{2}...i_{m})\neq(i...i)}|b_{ii_{2}...i_{m}}|

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\sum_{(i_{2}...i_{m})\in[n]^{m-1}\backslash\ [n-1]^{m-1}}|b_{ii_{2}...i_{m}}|

(3.17)

Since
\mathscr{B}
is a Nekrasov tensor,
\Lambda_{i}(\mathscr{B})<|b_{ii...i}|
and so
w_{i}<1
for all
i\in[n-1].
Therefore

|b_{ii_{2}...i_{m}}\geq|b_{ii_{2}...i_{m}}|w_{i_{2}}\cdots w_{i_{m}},\;(i_{2}...i_{m})\in([n-1]^{m-1}\backslash\ [i-1]^{m-1}),(i_{2}...i_{m})\neq(i...i).

(3.18)

Also by the assumption,
\epsilon<1-\left(\frac{\Lambda_{n}(\mathscr{B})}{|b_{n...n}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}
an so
w_{n}<1.
Therefore

|b_{ii_{2}...i_{m}}|\geq|b_{ii_{2}...i_{m}}|w_{i_{2}}\cdots w_{i_{m}},\text{ for }(i_{2}...i_{m})\in[n]^{m-1}\backslash\ [n-1]^{m-1}.

(3.19)

From (
3.17
), (
3.18
) and (
3.19
) we deduce

|b_{ii...i}|w_{i}\cdots w_{i}\geq\sum_{(i_{2}...i_{m})\neq(ii...i)}|b_{ii_{2}...i_{m}}|w_{i_{2}}\cdots w_{i_{m}}.

(3.20)

Now, if either (
3.18
) or (
3.19
) are strict inequalities, then (
3.20
) is also strict.

If
b_{ii_{2}...i_{m}}\neq 0,
for
(i_{2}...i_{m})\in[n]^{m-1}\backslash\ [n-1]^{m-1}
, since
w_{n}<1,
then
|b_{ii_{2}...i_{m}}|w_{i_{2}}\cdots w_{i_{m}}<|b_{ii_{2}...i_{m}}|
and so (
3.19
) and (
3.20
) are strict.

If
b_{ii_{2}...i_{m}}=0,
the by the assumption
b_{ii_{2}...i_{m}}\neq 0
for some
(i_{2}...i_{m})\in([n-1]^{m-1}\backslash\ [i-1]^{m-1}),(i_{2}...i_{m})\neq(i...i)
Since
w_{i}<1,
then
|b_{ii_{2}...i_{m}}|w_{i_{2}}\cdots w_{i_{m}}<|b_{ii_{2}...i_{m}}|
and so
(\ref{2nd main equation for scalling})
and (
3.20
) are strict. Therefore the condition of strictly diagonally dominance for the tensor
\mathscr{A}W
holds for
i\in[n-1].

Finally, since
\epsilon|b_{n...n}|>0,
we have

\displaystyle|b_{n...n}w_{n}w_{n}\cdots w_{n}|

\displaystyle=\Lambda_{n}(\mathscr{B})+\epsilon|b_{n...n}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{i_{2}...i_{m}\in[n-1]^{m-1}}|b_{ni_{2}...i_{m}}|\left(\frac{\Lambda_{i_{2}}(\mathscr{B})}{|b_{i_{2}i_{2}...i_{2}}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}\cdots\left(\frac{\Lambda_{i_{m}}(\mathscr{B})}{|b_{i_{m}i_{m}...i_{m}}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{(i_{2}...i_{m})\in[n]^{m-1}\backslash\ [n-1]^{m-1},(i_{2}...i_{m})\neq(n...n)}|b_{ni_{2}...i_{m}}|+\epsilon|b_{n...n}|

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{(i_{2}...i_{m})\neq(n...n)}|b_{ni_{2}...i_{m}}|w_{i_{2}}\cdots w_{i_{m}}+\epsilon|b_{n...n}|\text{ using (\ref{3rd main equation for scalling}) }

\displaystyle>\epsilon|b_{n...n}|

and so the last condition of strictly diagonally dominance for the tensor
\mathscr{A}W
holds for
i=n.

∎

theorem 3.3
:

Let for even

\mathscr{A}=(a_{i_{1}i_{2}...i_{m}})\in\mathbb{R}^{[m,n]},

lemma 3.1
:

Let
\mathscr{B},\;\mathscr{C}\in\mathbb{R}^{[m,n]},

m
be even. If
\mathscr{B}
is a
P
-tensor and
\mathscr{C}
is a nonnegative tensor with constant row subtensors, then
\mathscr{B}+\mathscr{C}
is a
P
-tensor.

Proof.

Let
\mathscr{B},\;\mathscr{C}\in\mathscr{R}^{[m,n]}
with the condition that
\mathscr{B}
is a
P
-tensor and
\mathscr{C}
is a nonnegative tensor with constant row subtensors. Then
\forall\;0\neq v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\;\exists\;i\in[n]
such that

v_{i}(\mathscr{B}v^{m-1})_{i}>0.

(3.21)

Let
\mathscr{C}=(c_{i_{1}i_{2}...i_{m}})
be a nonnegative tensor with costant row subtensors. Let the row subtensors of
\mathscr{C}
are
\mathscr{R}_{i}(\mathscr{C})\;\forall\;i\in[n].
Then for each
i\in[n],

(\mathscr{R}_{i}(\mathscr{C}))_{i_{2}...i_{m}}=c_{i},\;\forall\;i_{2},...,i_{m}\in[n],
for some
c_{i}\geq 0.

If possible let
\mathscr{A}=\mathscr{B}+\mathscr{C}
is not a
P
-tensor. Then
\exists\;0\neq v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
v_{i}(\mathscr{C}v^{m-1})_{i}\leq 0,\;\forall\;i\in[n].
This implies for all
i\in[n],

\displaystyle v_{i}(\mathscr{B}v^{m-1})_{i}+v_{i}(\mathscr{C}v^{m-1})_{i}\leq 0

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle v_{i}(\mathscr{B}v^{m-1})_{i}+v_{i}c_{i}(\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i})^{m-1}\leq 0

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\frac{v_{i}^{m-2}}{(\tau^{m-1})^{2}}\left[v_{i}(\mathscr{B}v^{m-1})_{i}+v_{i}c_{i}\tau^{m-1}\right]\leq 0\text{ where, }\tau=(\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i})\neq 0,

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\left(\frac{v_{i}}{\tau}\right)^{m-1}\left(\mathscr{B}\left(\frac{v}{\tau}\right)^{m-1}\right)_{i}+\left(\frac{v_{i}}{\tau}\right)^{m-1}c_{i}\leq 0

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle y_{i}(\mathscr{A}y^{m-1})_{i}+y_{i}c_{i}\leq 0,\text{ where }y=\frac{1}{\tau}v.

(3.22)

Now we prove that for all
i\in[n],

y_{i}(\mathscr{A}y^{m-1})_{i}+y_{i}c_{i}\leq 0
for arbitrary
c_{i}\geq 0
implies
y_{i}(\mathscr{A}y^{m-1})_{i}\leq 0.

If not, then
\exists\;k\in[n]
such that
y_{k}(\mathscr{A}y^{m-1})_{k}>0.
Now if
y_{k}\geq 0
then
y_{k}(\mathscr{A}y^{m-1})_{k}+y_{k}r_{k}>0.
Again, if
y_{k}<0
then for
\epsilon>0
such that
y_{k}(\mathscr{A}y^{m-1})_{k}-\epsilon>0
we can choose
r_{k}=\frac{y_{k}(\mathscr{A}y^{m-1})_{k}-\epsilon}{|y_{k}|}>0.
Then
y_{k}(\mathscr{A}y^{m-1})_{k}+y_{k}r_{k}=\epsilon>0.
This contradicts the assumption. Thus (
3.22
) implies
y_{i}(\mathscr{A}y^{m-1})_{i}\leq 0,\;\forall\;i\in[n].
Therefore
\exists\;0\neq y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
y_{i}(\mathscr{A}y^{m-1})_{i}\leq 0,\;\forall\;i\in[n].
This implies
\mathscr{A}
is not a
P
-tensor.
∎

we conclude that

remark 3.1
:

The
P
-tensors play an important role in tensor complementarity theory. We know that the SOL
(\mathscr{A},q)

\mathscr{A}
is a

-Nekrasov tensor of even order

Conclusion

In this article, we introduce
B

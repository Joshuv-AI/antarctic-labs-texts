A general sufficient criterion for energy conservation in the Navier-Stokes system
Yanqing Wang and Yulin Ye

Abstract
In this paper, we derive an energy conservation criterion based on a combination of velocity and its gradient for the weak solutions of both the homogeneous incompressible Navier-Stokes equations and the general compressible Navier-Stokes equations. For the incompressible case, this class implies most known corresponding results on periodic domain via either the velocity or its gradient including the famous Lions’ energy conservation criterion obtained in [ 20 ] . For the compressible case, this helps us to extend the previously known criteria for the energy conservation of weak solutions from the incompressible fluid to compressible flow and improve the recent results due to Nguyen-Nguyen-Tang in [ 21 , Nonlinearity 32 (2019)] and Liang in [ 17 , Proc. Roy. Soc. Edinburgh Sect. A (2020)] .

35Q30, 35Q35, 76N06, 76N10

Navier-Stokes equations; Compressible Navier-Stokes equations; Energy conservation

Introduction

The homogeneous Navier-Stokes equations describing the motion of incompressible fluid in
three-dimensional space
read

\left\{\begin{aligned} &v_{t}-\Delta v+\mathrm{div}\,(v\otimes v)+\nabla\pi=0,\quad(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,+\infty),\\
&\mathrm{div}\,v=0,\\
&v_{0}=v(x,0).\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

Here,
v
stands for the velocity field of the flow and

\pi
represents the pressure of the fluid, respectively. The initial datum satisfies
\mathrm{div}\,v_{0}=0
.
Usually, one considers the Navier-Stokes equations on the periodic domain
(\Omega=\mathbb{T}^{3})
, on smooth bounded domain
\Omega
with Dirichlet boundary condition or on the whole space

(\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{3})
.

It is well known that Leray-Hopf weak solutions of the Navier-Stokes equations (
1.1
) obeys the energy inequality

\|v(T)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+2\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}ds\leq\|v_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2},

rather than energy equality.
The first attempts to determine sufficient conditions implying energy conservation of Leray-Hopf weak solutions in the homogeneous incompressible Navier-Stokes equations were given by Lions
[
20
]
and Prodi
[
25
]
provided that the criterion

v\in L^{4}(0,T;L^{4}(\Omega))

(1.2)

is satisfied. Then Serrin
[
26
]
showed the energy conservation by giving a criterion in a scaling invarant space, that is

v\in L^{p}(0,T;L^{q}(\Omega)),\ \text{with}\ \frac{2}{p}+\frac{d}{q}\leq 1\ \text{and}\ q\geq d,

where
d
is the spatial dimension. However, the weak solution which satisfy the given criterion will immediately become a classical one.
Later, Shinbrot extended Lions’ condition in
[
20
]

for energy conservation to

v\in L^{p}(0,T;L^{q}(\Omega))~\text{ with}~\frac{2}{p}+\frac{2}{q}=1,q\geq 4.

(1.3)

It is worth remarking that condition (
1.3
) is weaker than (
1.2
) when the dimension
d\geq 4
and more importantly, it is true regardless of the dimension of the underlying space. On the other hand, the energy conservation condition (
1.3
) can be replaced by

v\in L^{p}(0,T;L^{q}(\Omega))\text{ with}\ \frac{1}{p}+\frac{3}{q}=1,3<q<4;

(1.4)

which was recently obtained by Beirao da Veiga-Yang in
[
4
]
. Using the Fourier methods, Cheskidov-Friedlander-Shvydkoy
[
10
]

gave the following sufficient condition for energy conservation (here
A
denotes the Stokes operator associated to the Dirichlet boundary conditions) that

A^{5/12}v\in L^{3}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)),

in fact, this criterion is equivalent in terms of scaling to
v\in L^{3}(0,T;L^{\frac{9}{2}}(\Omega)).

Very recently, Berselli-Chiodaroli
[
5
]
and Zhang
[
35
]
obtained energy equality via the following condition,

\nabla v\in L^{p}\left(0,T;L^{q}\left(\Omega\right)\right),\frac{1}{p}+\frac{3}{q}=2,\frac{3}{2}<q<\frac{9}{5}~~\text{ or}~~\frac{1}{p}+\frac{6}{5q}=1,\frac{9}{5}\leq q.

(1.5)

It is worth remarking that the domain
\Omega
in most the aforementioned conditional results is the smooth bounded. The first objective of this paper is to show the following sufficient criterion for the weak solutions keeping the energy of the Navier-Stokes equations
on the periodic domain
\mathbb{T}^{3},

v\in L^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}(0,T;L^{\frac{2q}{q-1}}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))~~\text{and}~~\nabla v\in L^{p}(0,T;L^{q}(\mathbb{T}^{3})).

(1.6)

Surprisingly, this result covers the corresponding results of (
1.3
), (
1.4
) and (
1.5
) on periodic domain and further discussion will be found in Remark
1.2
and
1.3
. Indeed, we will also prove this class for the following compressible Navier-Stokes equations with
degenerate viscosities and general pressure law (GNS)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\rho_{t}+\mathrm{div}\,(\rho v)=0,\\
&(\rho v)_{t}+\mathrm{div}\,(\rho v\otimes v)+\nabla p(\rho)-\mathrm{div}\,(\nu(\rho)\mathbb{D}v)-\nabla(\mu(\rho)\mathrm{div}\,v)=0,\\
\end{aligned}\right.

(1.7)

with the initial data

\displaystyle\rho(0,x)=\rho_{0}(x)\ \text{and}\ (\rho v)(0,x)=\rho_{0}(x)v_{0}(x),\ \ x\in\Omega,

(1.8)

where the unknown functions
\rho
and
v
denote the density of the fluid and velocity of the fluid, respectively;
\mathbb{D}v=\frac{1}{2}(\nabla v\otimes\nabla v^{T})
stands for the stain tensor; The general pressure
0\leq p(\rho)\in C^{1}(0,\infty)
with
p^{{}^{\prime}}(\cdot)>0
and the viscosity coefficients
\nu(\rho),\mu(\rho):(0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
are continuous functions of density. We will
consider the case of bounded domain with periodic boundary conditions, namely
\Omega=\mathbb{T}^{d}
, where
d\geq 2
is the dimension of the domain.
It should be noted that when
\nu(\rho)\equiv\nu,\mu(\rho)\equiv\mu
and
p(\rho)=\rho^{\gamma}
with
\gamma>1
, then (GNS) will reduce to the classical isentropic compressible Navier-Stokes equations (ICNS):

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\rho_{t}+\mathrm{div}\,(\rho v)=0,\\
&(\rho v)_{t}+\mathrm{div}\,(\rho v\otimes v)+\nabla\rho^{\gamma}-\nu\Delta v-\mu\nabla\text{div\,}v=0,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.9)

and when
\nu(\rho)=\rho,\mu(\rho)\equiv 0
and
p(\rho)=\rho^{\gamma}
with
\gamma>1
, then (GNS) reduces to the compressible Navier-Stokes equations with degenerate viscosity and
\gamma
-pressure law (CNSD) as follows:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\rho_{t}+\mathrm{div}\,(\rho v)=0,\\
&(\rho v)_{t}+\mathrm{div}\,(\rho v\otimes v)+\nabla\rho^{\gamma}-\mathrm{div}\,(\rho\mathbb{D}v)=0.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.10)

The global existence of weak solutions which satisfy the energy inequality has already been known, see P. L. Lions
[
19
]
and Feireisl-Novotný-Petzeltová
[
14
]
for (ICNS) with constant viscosity coefficients case, Vassure-Yu
[
30
]
for (CNSD) and Li-Xin
[
16
]
for (GNS) with degenerate viscosity coefficients case, but the regularity and even the uniqueness of weak solutions are still open problems. Since the weak solutions satisfy the energy inequality rather than equality due to the basic a priori estimates and the lack of regularity, this anomalous dissipation of the energy opens a possibility for an energy sink other than the natural viscous
dissipation, however, such a property of real fluids is not expected to exist
physically, so it is therefore a famous problem to consider the sufficient criterion for the energy conservation of weak solutions. Roughly speaking, this addresses the question how much regularities are needed for a weak solution to conserve energy, which is also involving the uniqueness of the weak solutions. On the other hand, energy conservation is also one aspect of the Onsager’s conjecture in the context of homogeneous incompressible Euler equations in
[
24
]
, in which Onsager conjectured that the kinetic energy is globally conserved for Hölder continuous solutions with the exponent greater than
\frac{1}{3}
, while
an energy dissipation phenomenon occurs for Hölder continuous solutions with the exponent less than
\frac{1}{3}
. For the positive part, the milestone work is due to Constantin-E-Titi

[
12
]
, in which it was proved that the energy of 3D incompressible
Euler equations is conserved for every weak solution in
L^{3}(0,T;B_{3,\infty}^{\alpha})
with
\alpha>1/3
. On the other hand, Isett resolved
the “negative”part of Onsager’s conjecture
for 3D incompressible Euler equations in
[
15
]
, where he proved
that for any
\alpha<\frac{1}{3}
there is a nonzero weak solution to the
incompressible Euler equations in the class
v\in C^{\alpha}_{t,x}
and
p\in C^{2\alpha}_{t,x}
such that
v
is identically
0
outside a finite time
interval. In particular, the solution
v
fails to conserve the energy. We
refer the reader to
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
7
,
8
,

,
13
,

,

,

]
for
recent
progress in
this direction.

Compared with the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations, due to the stronger nonlinearity, the energy conservation of the weak solutions is more challenging and hence the results are few. When the density is strictly away from vacuum, for the weak solutions to the general compressible models (
1.7
),
Nguye-Nguye-Tang
[

established the Shinbrot-type criterion and showed that if the weak solutions satisfied

\displaystyle

\displaystyle 0<c_{1}\leq\rho\leq c_{2}<\infty,v\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})),\nabla v\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})),

(1.11)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in(0,T)}\sup_{|h|<\varepsilon}|h|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\rho(\cdot+h,t)-\rho(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}<\infty,

\displaystyle v\in L^{p}(0,T;L^{q}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))\text{ with}~~\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{2}{p}+\frac{2}{q}=1,q\geq 4,\\
&\frac{1}{p}+\frac{3}{q}=1,3<q<4,\end{aligned}\right.

then the energy of weak solutions is globally conserved, which means the energy equality holds for any
t\in[0,T]
.
It is worth noting that though the part

Later, Liang

\displaystyle 0<c_{1}\leq\rho\leq c_{2}<\infty,v\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})),\nabla v\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})),

(1.12)

\displaystyle\nabla v\in L^{p}\left(0,T;L^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{3})\right)~\text{with}~\left\{\begin{aligned} \frac{1}{p}+\frac{3}{s}<2,\ \ \ \ \ \ &\frac{3}{2}<s<\frac{9}{5},\\

\frac{1}{p}+\frac{2}{s+2}<1,\ \ &3<s<\infty,\end{aligned}\right.

then the energy of weak solutions is locally conserved, which means the energy equality holds in the sense of distribution in
(0,T)
.

When the density may contain vacuum, in the spirit of well-known Shinbrot’s criterion in

\displaystyle v\in L^{p}(0,T;L^{q}(\Omega))~~\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}\leq\frac{5}{12}\ \text{ and}\ q\geq 6,

Before stating the main results, we introduce the definition of the weak solutions.

Definition 1.1
.

A pair (
\rho,v
) is called a weak solution to (
1.7
) with initial data (
\rho_{0},v_{0}
) if (
\rho,v
) satisfy

(i)

equations (
1.7
) hold in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(0,T;\mathbb{T}^{d})
and

P(\rho),\rho|v|^{2}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})),\ \ \ \nabla v\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})),

(1.15)

(ii)

\rho(\cdot,t)\rightharpoonup\rho_{0}
in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega)
as
t\rightarrow 0
, i.e.

\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\rho(x,t)\varphi(x)dx=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\rho_{0}(x)\varphi(x)dx,

(1.16)

for every test function
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d}).

(iii)

(\rho v)(\cdot,t)\rightharpoonup\rho_{0}v_{0}
in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
as
t\rightarrow 0
i.e.

\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(\rho v)(x,t)\psi(x)dx=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(\rho_{0}v_{0})(x)\psi(x)dx,

(1.17)

for every test vector field
\psi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})^{d}
.

(iv)

the energy inequality holds

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(t)+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}

\displaystyle\left[\nu(\rho)|\mathbb{D}v|^{2}+\mu(\rho)|\mathrm{div}\,v|^{2}\right]dxdt\leq\mathcal{E}(0),

(1.18)

where
\mathcal{E}(t)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left[\frac{1}{2}\rho|v|^{2}+P(\rho)\right]dx
and
P(\rho)=\rho\int_{1}^{\rho}\frac{p(z)}{z^{2}}dz.

We formulate our first result as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

For any dimension
d\geq 2
, let
(\rho,v)
be a weak solution to the general compressible Navier-Stokes equations (
1.7
). Assume that
1<p,q<\infty
and

\left\{\begin{aligned} &0<c_{1}\leq\rho\leq c_{2}<\infty,v\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})),\nabla v\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})),\\
&v\in L^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}(0,T;L^{\frac{2q}{q-1}}(\mathbb{T}^{d})),\ \nabla v\in L^{p}(0,T;L^{q}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))\ and\ \sqrt{\rho_{0}}v_{0}\in L^{2+\delta}\ for\ any\ \delta>0,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.19)

then the energy of weak solutions is globally conserved, that is, for any
t\in[0,T]

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(t)+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}

\displaystyle\left[\nu(\rho)|\mathbb{D}v|^{2}+\mu(\rho)|\mathrm{div}\,v|^{2}\right]dxdt=\mathcal{E}(0),

(1.20)

where
\mathcal{E}(t)=\int_{\Omega}\left[\frac{1}{2}\rho|v|^{2}+P(\rho)\right]dx
and
P(\rho)=\rho\int_{1}^{\rho}\frac{p(z)}{z^{2}}dz.

p\in[1,\,\infty]
, the notation
L^{p}(0,\,T;X)
stands for the set of measurable functions on the interval
(0,\,T)
with values in
X
and
\|f(\cdot,t)\|_{X}
belonging to
L^{p}(0,\,T)
. The classical Sobolev space
W^{k,p}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
is equipped with the norm
\|f\|_{W^{k,p}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}=\sum\limits_{\alpha=0}^{k}\|D^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}
. The space
C^{\infty}_{b}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
is the bounded smooth functions on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
.
c_{1},c_{2}
and
C
are positive constants. For simplicity, we denote by

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}f(x,t)dxdt=\int_{0}^{T}\int f\ ~~\text{and}~~\|f\|_{L^{p}(0,T;X)}=\|f\|_{L^{p}(X)}.

Let
\eta_{\varepsilon}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a standard mollifier.i.e.
\eta(x)=C_{0}e^{-\frac{1}{1-|x|^{2}}}
for
|x|<1
and
\eta(x)=0
for
|x|\geq 1
, where
C_{0}
is a constant such that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\eta(x)dx=1
. For
\varepsilon>0
, we define the rescaled mollifier
\eta_{\varepsilon}(x)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d}}\eta(\frac{x}{\varepsilon})
. For any function
f\in L^{1}_{loc}(\Omega)
, its mollified version is defined as

f^{\varepsilon}(x)=(f*\eta_{\varepsilon})(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x-y)\eta_{\varepsilon}(y)dy,\ \ x\in\Omega_{\varepsilon},

where
\Omega_{\varepsilon}=\{x\in\Omega:d(x,\partial\Omega)>\varepsilon\}.

We first recall the results involving the mollifier established in

[
21
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

(
[
21
]
)
Suppose that
f\in L^{p}(0,T;L^{q}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
. Then for any
\varepsilon>0
, there holds

\|\nabla f^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{q}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))}\leq C\varepsilon^{-1}\|f\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{q}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))},

(2.1)

and, if
p,q<\infty

\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\varepsilon\|\nabla f^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{q}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))}=0.

Moreover, if
0<c_{1}\leq g\leq c_{2}<\infty
, then there holds, for any
\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\Big\|\nabla\frac{f^{\varepsilon}}{g^{\varepsilon}}\Big\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{q}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))}\leq C\varepsilon^{-1}\|f\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{q}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))},

(2.2)

and if
p,q<\infty

\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\varepsilon\Big\|\nabla\frac{f^{\varepsilon}}{g^{\varepsilon}}\Big\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{q}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))}=0.

(2.3)

The next lemma with
p=q,p_{1}=q_{1},p_{2}=q_{2}
was proved in
[
21
]
.
We generalize it by extending the integral norms with different exponents in space and time.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
1\leq p,q,p_{1},p_{2},q_{1},q_{2}\leq\infty
with
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}
and
\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{q_{1}}+\frac{1}{q_{2}}
. Assume
f\in L^{p_{1}}(0,T;W^{1,q_{1}}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
and
g\in L^{p_{2}}(0,T;L^{q_{2}}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
. Then for any
\varepsilon>0
, there holds

\displaystyle\|(fg)^{\varepsilon}-f^{\varepsilon}g^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{q}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))}\leq C\varepsilon\|f\|_{L^{p_{1}}(0,T;W^{1,q_{1}}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))}\|g\|_{L^{p_{2}}(0,T;L^{q_{2}}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))}.

(2.4)

Moreover, if
p_{2},q_{2}<\infty
then

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon^{-1}\|(fg)^{\varepsilon}-f^{\varepsilon}g^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{q}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))}=0.

(2.5)

Proof.

Thanks to the fact observed in
[
12
]
and the ideas in
[
18
]
, we know that

\displaystyle(fg)^{\varepsilon}-f^{\varepsilon}g^{\varepsilon}=R^{\varepsilon}-(f^{\varepsilon}-f)(g^{\varepsilon}-g),

(2.6)

where

\displaystyle R^{\varepsilon}(x,t):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big(f\left(y,t\right)-f(x,t)\big)\big(g(y,t)-g(x,t)\big)\eta_{\varepsilon}(x-y)dy.

Using the triangle’s inequality, it yields that

\displaystyle\|(fg)^{\varepsilon}-f^{\varepsilon}g^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\leq C\left(\|R^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}+\|(f-f^{\varepsilon})(g-g^{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\right).

(2.7)

Let
B(x,\varepsilon)=\{y\in\mathbb{T}^{d};|x-y|<\varepsilon\}
, then by means of Hölder’s inequality and direct computation, we see that

\displaystyle|R^{\varepsilon}|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B(x,\varepsilon)}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d}}|f(y)-f(x)||g(y)-g(x)|dy

(2.8)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d}}\int_{B(x,\varepsilon)}|f(y)-f(x)|^{s_{1}}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{s_{1}}}\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d}}\int_{B(x,\varepsilon)}|g(y)-g(x)|^{s_{2}}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{s_{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d}}\int_{B(x,\varepsilon)}\int_{0}^{1}|\nabla f(x+(y-x)s)|^{s_{1}}dsdy\right)^{\frac{1}{s_{1}}}\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d}}\int_{B(x,\varepsilon)}|g(y)|^{s_{2}}dy+|g(x)|^{s_{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{s_{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\left(\int_{B(0,1)}\int_{0}^{1}|\nabla f(x+\omega\varepsilon s)|^{s_{1}}dsd\omega\right)^{\frac{1}{s_{1}}}\left(\int_{B(0,1)}|g(x+\omega\varepsilon)|^{s_{2}}d\omega+|g(x)|^{s_{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{s_{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla f(x-z)|^{s_{1}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\bf{1}_{B(0,\varepsilon s)}(z)}{(\varepsilon s)^{d}}dsdz\right)^{\frac{1}{s_{1}}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|g(x-z)|^{s_{2}}\frac{\bf{1}_{B(0,\varepsilon)(z)}}{\varepsilon^{d}}dz+|g(x)|^{s_{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{s_{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(|\nabla f|^{s_{1}}*J_{\varepsilon}\right)^{\frac{1}{s_{1}}}\left(|g|^{s_{2}}*J_{1\varepsilon}+|g(x)|^{s_{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{s_{2}}},

where
s_{1}\leq q_{1},s_{2}\leq q_{2}
with
\frac{1}{s_{1}}+\frac{1}{s_{2}}=1
,
J_{\varepsilon}=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\bf{1}_{B(0,\varepsilon s)}}{(\varepsilon s)^{d}}ds\geq 0
,
J_{1\varepsilon}=\frac{\bf{1}_{B(0,\varepsilon)}}{\varepsilon^{d}}\geq 0
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\bf{1}_{B(0,\varepsilon s)}}{(\varepsilon s)^{d}}dsdz=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\bf{1}_{B(0,\varepsilon)}}{\varepsilon^{d}}dz=measure(B(0,1)).

Then in view of the Minkowski inequality, we conclude that

\displaystyle\|R^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{q}}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\|\left(|\nabla f|^{s_{1}}*J_{\varepsilon}\right)^{\frac{1}{s_{1}}}\left(|g|^{s_{2}}*J_{1\varepsilon}+|g(x)|^{s_{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{s_{2}}}\|_{L^{q}}

(2.9)

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\left[\|(|\nabla f|^{s_{1}}*J_{\varepsilon})^{\frac{1}{s_{1}}}\|_{L^{q_{1}}}\left(\|(|g|^{s_{2}}*J_{1\varepsilon})^{\frac{1}{s_{2}}}\|_{L^{q_{2}}}+\|g\|_{L^{q_{2}}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\|\nabla f\|_{L^{q_{1}}}\|g\|_{L^{q_{2}}}.

Furthermore, one has

\displaystyle|(f^{\varepsilon}-f)(g^{\varepsilon}-g)|

(2.10)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int|(f(y)-f(x))|\eta_{\varepsilon}(x-y)dy\int|(g(y)-g(x))|\eta_{\varepsilon}(x-y)dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\varepsilon\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d}}\int_{B(x,\varepsilon)}\int_{0}^{1}|\nabla f(x+(y-x)s)|dsdy\right)\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d}}\int_{B(x,\varepsilon)}|g(y)-g(x)|dy\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\varepsilon\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d}}\int_{B(x,\varepsilon)}\int_{0}^{1}|\nabla f(x+(y-x)s)|^{s_{1}}dsdy\right)^{\frac{1}{s_{1}}}\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d}}\int_{B(x,\varepsilon)}|g(y)-g(x)|^{s_{2}}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{s_{2}}}.

Along the same lines of derivation of (
2.8
) and (
2.9
), we arrive at

\|(f^{\varepsilon}-f)(g^{\varepsilon}-g)\|_{L^{q}}\leq C\varepsilon\|\nabla f\|_{L^{q_{1}}}\|g\|_{L^{q_{2}}}.

(2.11)

In combination with (
2.6
), (
2.9
) and (
2.11
) and using the Hölder’s inequality with respect to time, we can deduce the result (
2.4
).

Furthermore, if
q_{1},q_{2}<\infty
, let

with

strongly in

. Thus, by density arguments, we find that

\displaystyle\|(fg)^{\varepsilon}-f^{\varepsilon}g^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{q}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|(\left(f(g-g_{n})\right)^{\varepsilon}+(fg_{n})^{\varepsilon}-f^{\varepsilon}(g-g_{n})^{\varepsilon}-f^{\varepsilon}g_{n}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{q}}\right)

(2.12)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|\left(f(g-g_{n})\right)^{\varepsilon}-f^{\varepsilon}(g-g_{n})^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{q}}+\|(fg_{n})^{\varepsilon}-f^{\varepsilon}g_{n}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{q}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\varepsilon\|\nabla f\|_{L^{q_{1}}}\|g-g_{n}\|_{L^{q_{2}}}+\varepsilon^{2}\|\nabla f\|_{L^{q_{1}}}\|\nabla g_{n}\|_{L^{q_{2}}}\right),

which means

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{-1}\|(fg)^{\varepsilon}-f^{\varepsilon}g^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{q}}\leq C\left(\|\nabla f\|_{L^{q_{1}}}\|g-g_{n}\|_{L^{q_{2}}}+\varepsilon\|\nabla f\|_{L^{q_{1}}}\|\nabla g_{n}\|_{L^{q_{2}}}\right),

(2.13)

hence, as

and

, we can obtain that

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{-1}\|(fg)^{\varepsilon}-f^{\varepsilon}g^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(L^{q})}

(2.14)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\int_{0}^{T}\left(\|\nabla f\|_{L^{q_{1}}}\|g-g_{n}\|_{L^{q_{2}}}+\varepsilon\|\nabla f\|_{L^{q_{1}}}\|\nabla g_{n}\|_{L^{q_{2}}}\right)^{p}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\int_{0}^{T}(\|\nabla f\|_{L^{q_{1}}}\|g-g_{n}\|_{L^{q_{2}}})^{p}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}+C\varepsilon\left(\int_{0}^{T}(\|\nabla f\|_{L^{q_{1}}}\|\nabla g_{n}\|_{L^{q_{2}}})^{p}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p_{1}}(L^{q_{1}})}\|g-g_{n}\|_{L^{p_{2}}(L^{q_{2}})}+\varepsilon\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p_{1}}(L^{q_{1}})}\|\nabla g_{n}\|_{L^{p_{2}}(L^{q_{2}})}\rightarrow 0.

Then, we have completed the proof of Lemma
2.2
.
∎

The next lemma is the key to remove

Lemma 2.3
.

Assume that
0<\underline{\rho}\leq\rho(x,t)\leq\overline{\rho}<\infty
and
v\in W^{1,p}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
with
1\leq p\leq\infty
. Then

\Big\|\partial\left(\frac{(\rho v)^{\varepsilon}}{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\right)\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\leq C\|\nabla v\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}.

(2.15)

Proof.

By direct computation, one has

\partial\left(\frac{(\rho v)^{\varepsilon}}{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\right)=\frac{\partial(\rho v)^{\varepsilon}-v\partial\rho^{\varepsilon}}{\rho^{\varepsilon}}-\frac{\left((\rho v)^{\varepsilon}-\rho^{\varepsilon}v\right)\partial\rho^{\varepsilon}}{(\rho^{\varepsilon})^{2}}:=I_{1}+I_{2}.

(2.16)

Let
B(x,\varepsilon)=\{y\in\mathbb{T}^{d};|x-y|<\varepsilon\}
, then Using the Hölder’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle|I_{1}|

\displaystyle\leq C|\int\rho(y)\left(v(y)-v(x)\right)\nabla_{x}\eta_{\varepsilon}(x-y)dy|

(2.17)

\displaystyle\leq C\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}}|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|v(y)-v(x)|\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d}}\nabla\eta(\frac{x-y}{{}^{\varepsilon}})\frac{1}{{}^{\varepsilon}}dy|

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d}}\int_{B(x,\varepsilon)}\frac{|v(y)-v(x)|^{p}}{\varepsilon^{p}}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Then using the mean value theorem, one can obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d}}\int_{B(x,\varepsilon)}\frac{|v(y)-v(x)|^{p}}{\varepsilon^{p}}dy

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d}}\int_{B(x,\varepsilon)}\int_{0}^{1}|\nabla v(x+(y-x)s)|^{p}\frac{|y-x|^{p}}{\varepsilon^{p}}dsdy

(2.18)

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{1}\int_{B(0,1)}|\nabla v(x+s\varepsilon\omega)|^{p}d\omega ds

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla v(x-z)|^{p}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\bf{1}_{(B\textsterling\textasciidieresis(0,\varepsilon s)}(z)}{(\varepsilon s)^{d}}dsdz

\displaystyle=(|\nabla v|^{p}*J_{\varepsilon})(x),

where
J_{\varepsilon}(z)=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\bf{1}_{(B\textsterling\textasciidieresis(0,\varepsilon s)}(z)}{(\varepsilon s)^{d}}ds\geq 0
and it’s easy to check that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}J_{\varepsilon}dz={\text{\ }measure\ of\ }(B(0,1))
.
Next, to estimate
I_{2}
, due to the Hölder’s inequality, one deduces

\displaystyle|I_{2}|

\displaystyle=|\int\rho(y)\left(v(y)-v(x)\right)\eta_{\varepsilon}(x-y)dy\frac{\int\rho(y)\nabla_{x}\eta_{\varepsilon}(x-y)dy}{\left(\int\rho(y)\eta_{\varepsilon}(x-y)dy\right)^{2}}|

(2.19)

\displaystyle\leq C\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\int_{B(x,\varepsilon)}|v(y)-v(x)|\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d}}dy\int_{B(x,\varepsilon)}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d}}|\nabla\eta(\frac{x-y}{\varepsilon})|\frac{1}{\varepsilon}dy

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d}}\int_{B(x,\varepsilon)}\frac{|v(y)-v(x)|^{p}}{\varepsilon^{p}}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Therefore, by the same arguments as in (
2.18
), in combination with (
2.16
)-(
2.19
), we have

|I_{1}|+|I_{2}|\leq C\left(\nabla v|^{p}*J_{\varepsilon}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

(2.20)

Then from the Minkowski’s inequality, we arrive at

\displaystyle\Big\|\partial\left(\frac{(\rho v)^{\varepsilon}}{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\right)\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\left(|\nabla v|^{p}*J_{\varepsilon}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\|_{L^{p}}

(2.21)

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla v\|_{L^{p}}\|J_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq C\|\nabla v\|_{L^{p}}.

Then we have completed the proof of lemma (
2.3
).
∎

\displaystyle\|(\rho v)^{\varepsilon}-\rho^{\varepsilon}v^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(L^{2})}\leq C\varepsilon\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}(L^{\infty})}\|v\|_{L^{2}(W^{1,2})}.

Moreover, due to the Hölder’s inequality, we can obtain that

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{0}^{T}\int\phi(t)\mathrm{div}\,(\nu(\rho)\mathbb{D}v)^{\varepsilon}\frac{(\rho v)^{\varepsilon}-\rho^{\varepsilon}v^{\varepsilon}}{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\Big|

(3.12)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\mathrm{div}\,(\nu(\rho)\mathbb{D}v)^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(L^{2})}\|\frac{(\rho v)^{\varepsilon}-\rho^{\varepsilon}v^{\varepsilon}}{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{2}(L^{2})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\varepsilon\|\mathrm{div}\,(\nu(\rho)\mathbb{D}v)^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(L^{2})}\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}\|v\|_{L^{2}(W^{1,2})}.

As a consequence, in combination with (
3.11
) and (
3.12
), we have

\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\Big|\int_{0}^{T}\int\phi(t)\mathrm{div}\,(\nu(\rho)\mathbb{D}v)^{\varepsilon}\frac{(\rho v)^{\varepsilon}-\rho^{\varepsilon}v^{\varepsilon}}{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\Big|=0.

Likewise, there also holds

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\Big|\int_{0}^{T}\int\phi(t)\nabla(\mu(\rho)\mathrm{div}\,v)^{\varepsilon}\frac{(\rho v)^{\varepsilon}-\rho^{\varepsilon}v^{\varepsilon}}{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\Big|=0.

(3.13)

Next, by means of the triangle inequality, the Hölder inequality and Lemma
2.3
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int\phi(t)\mathrm{div}\,\Big[\frac{(\rho v)^{\varepsilon}}{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\Big][(p(\rho))^{\varepsilon}-p(\rho^{\varepsilon})]

(3.14)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int\phi(t)\Big|\mathrm{div}\,\Big[\frac{(\rho v)^{\varepsilon}}{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\Big]\Big||(p(\rho))^{\varepsilon}-p(\rho)|+\int_{0}^{T}\int\phi(t)\Big|\mathrm{div}\,\Big[\frac{(\rho v)^{\varepsilon}}{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\Big]\Big||p(\rho)-p(\rho^{\varepsilon})|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\mathrm{div}\,\Big[\frac{(\rho v)^{\varepsilon}}{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\Big]\|_{L^{2}(L^{2})}\Big(\|(p(\rho))^{\varepsilon}-p(\rho)\|_{L^{2}(L^{2})}+\|p(\rho)-p(\rho^{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2}(L^{2})}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(L^{2})}\Big(\|(p(\rho))^{\varepsilon}-p(\rho)\|_{L^{2}(L^{2})}+\|p^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(L^{\infty})}\|\rho-\rho^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(L^{2})}\Big),

which implies that

\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\int_{0}^{T}\int\phi(t)\mathrm{div}\,\left(\frac{(\rho v)^{\varepsilon}}{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\right)\big((p(\rho))^{\varepsilon}-p(\rho^{\varepsilon})\big)=0.

At this stage, it is enough to show

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\int_{0}^{T}\int

\displaystyle\phi(t)\nabla\Big(\frac{(\rho v)^{\varepsilon}}{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\Big)[(\rho v\otimes v)^{\varepsilon}-(\rho v)^{\varepsilon}\otimes v^{\varepsilon}]

(3.15)

\displaystyle+\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\int_{0}^{T}\int\phi(t)\Big[\rho^{\varepsilon}v^{\varepsilon}-(\rho v)^{\varepsilon}\Big]\frac{(\rho v)^{\varepsilon}}{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\nabla\frac{(\rho v)^{\varepsilon}}{\rho^{\varepsilon}}=0,

under the hypothesis

v\in L^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}(L^{\frac{2q}{q-1}})~~\text{ and }~~\nabla v\in L^{p}(L^{q}).

(3.16)

To do this, applying Lemma
2.2
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\|(\rho v\otimes v)^{\varepsilon}-(\rho v)^{\varepsilon}\otimes v^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}(L^{\frac{2q}{q+1}})}\leq C\varepsilon\|v\|_{L^{p}(W^{1,q})}\|\rho v\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}(L^{\frac{2q}{q-1}})},

(3.17)

\displaystyle\Big\|\nabla\Big(\frac{(\rho v)^{\varepsilon}}{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\Big)\Big\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}(L^{\frac{2q}{q-1}})}\leq C\varepsilon^{-1}\|\rho v\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}(L^{\frac{2q}{q-1}})},

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\varepsilon\Big\|\nabla\Big(\frac{(\rho v)^{\varepsilon}}{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\Big)\Big\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}(L^{\frac{2q}{q-1}})}=0.

Using the Hölder’s inequality and Lemma
2.1
, we find

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{0}^{T}\int\phi(t)\nabla\Big(\frac{(\rho v)^{\varepsilon}}{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\Big)[(\rho v\otimes v)^{\varepsilon}-(\rho v)^{\varepsilon}\otimes v^{\varepsilon}]\Big|

(3.18)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\Big\|\nabla\Big(\frac{(\rho v)^{\varepsilon}}{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\Big)\Big\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}(L^{\frac{2q}{q-1}})}\|(\rho v\otimes v)^{\varepsilon}-(\rho v)^{\varepsilon}\otimes v^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}(L^{\frac{2q}{q+1}})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\varepsilon\Big\|\nabla\Big(\frac{(\rho v)^{\varepsilon}}{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\Big)\Big\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}(L^{\frac{2q}{q-1}})}\|v\|_{L^{p}(W^{1,q})}\|\rho v\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}(L^{\frac{2q}{q-1}})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\varepsilon\Big\|\nabla\Big(\frac{(\rho v)^{\varepsilon}}{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\Big)\Big\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}(L^{\frac{2q}{q-1}})}\|v\|_{L^{p}(W^{1,q})}\|v\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}(L^{\frac{2q}{q-1}})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\varepsilon\Big\|\nabla\Big(\frac{(\rho v)^{\varepsilon}}{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\Big)\Big\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}(L^{\frac{2q}{q-1}})},

which in turn gives

\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\Big|\int_{0}^{T}\int\phi(t)\nabla\Big(\frac{(\rho v)^{\varepsilon}}{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\Big)[(\rho v\otimes v)^{\varepsilon}-(\rho v)^{\varepsilon}\otimes v^{\varepsilon}]\Big|=0.

Now, we turn our attentions to the term
\int_{0}^{T}\int\phi(t)\Big[\rho^{\varepsilon}v^{\varepsilon}-(\rho v)^{\varepsilon}\Big]\frac{(\rho v)^{\varepsilon}}{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\nabla\frac{(\rho v)^{\varepsilon}}{\rho^{\varepsilon}}
. Since

2.1

that

\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\varepsilon\Big\|\nabla\left(\frac{(\rho v)^{\varepsilon}}{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\right)\Big\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}(L^{\frac{2q}{q-1}})}=0.

(3.19)

In addition, we conclude from Lemma
2.2
that

\|\rho^{\varepsilon}v^{\varepsilon}-(\rho v)^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(L^{q})}\leq C\varepsilon\|v\|_{L^{p}(W^{1,q})}\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}(L^{\infty})}.

(3.20)

Then, together with the Hölder’s inequality and (

), we have,

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{0}^{T}\int\phi(t)\Big[\rho^{\varepsilon}v^{\varepsilon}-(\rho v)^{\varepsilon}\Big]\frac{(\rho v)^{\varepsilon}}{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\nabla\left(\frac{(\rho v)^{\varepsilon}}{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\right)\Big|

(3.21)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\rho^{\varepsilon}v^{\varepsilon}-(\rho v)^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(L^{q})}\Big\|\frac{(\rho v)^{\varepsilon}}{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\Big\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}(L^{\frac{2q}{q-1}})}\Big\|\nabla\left(\frac{(\rho v)^{\varepsilon}}{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\right)\Big\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}(L^{\frac{2q}{q-1}})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|v\|_{L^{p}(W^{1,q})}\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}(L^{\infty})}\|v\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}(L^{\frac{2q}{q-1}})}\varepsilon\Big\|\nabla\left(\frac{(\rho v)^{\varepsilon}}{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\right)\Big\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}(L^{\frac{2q}{q-1}})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\varepsilon\Big\|\nabla\left(\frac{(\rho v)^{\varepsilon}}{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\right)\Big\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}(L^{\frac{2q}{q-1}})},

which together with (
3.19

Collecting all the above estimates, using the integration by parts with respect to

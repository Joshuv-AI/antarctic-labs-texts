Generalized Preservation Principle in Finite Theta Correspondence Thanks: This work is partially supported by Taiwan MOST-grant 106-2115-M-007-015.
Shu-Yen Pan

Abstract
. It is known that irreducible cuspidal characters satisfy the preservation principle in the Howe correspondences of finite reductive dual pairs. In this article, we generalize the preservation principle to any irreducible characters of classical groups.



1991 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary: 20C33; Secondary: 22E50

1.
Introduction

1.1.

Let
(\text{\bf G},\text{\bf G}^{\prime})
be a reductive dual pair over a finite field
{\mathbf{F}}_{q}
of odd characteristic.
By restricting the Weil character (
cf
.
[
Gér77
]
) to the dual pair
(\text{\bf G},\text{\bf G}^{\prime})
with respect to
a nontrivial additive character
\psi
of
{\mathbf{F}}_{q}
,
we have the
Weil character

\omega^{\psi}_{\text{\bf G},\text{\bf G}^{\prime}}
of
G\times G^{\prime}
,
which has non-negative integral decomposition

\omega^{\psi}_{\text{\bf G},\text{\bf G}^{\prime}}=\sum_{\rho\in{\mathcal{E}}(G),\ \rho^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{E}}(G^{\prime})}m_{\rho,\rho^{\prime}}\rho\otimes\rho^{\prime}

where
{\mathcal{E}}(G)
denotes the set of irreducible characters of group of rational points
G
of
G
.
We define the
Howe correspondence

\Theta^{\psi}_{\text{\bf G},\text{\bf G}^{\prime}}=\{\,(\rho,\rho^{\prime})\in{\mathcal{E}}(G)\times{\mathcal{E}}(G^{\prime})\mid m_{\rho,\rho^{\prime}}\neq 0\,\}

which is a relation between
{\mathcal{E}}(G)
and
{\mathcal{E}}(G^{\prime})
.
We say that an irreducible character
\rho\in{\mathcal{E}}(G)

occurs
in the correspondence
for the dual pair
(\text{\bf G},\text{\bf G}^{\prime})
if there exists
\rho^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{E}}(G^{\prime})
such that

(\rho,\rho^{\prime})\in\Theta^{\psi}_{\text{\bf G},\text{\bf G}^{\prime}}
.

Now we consider the following types of
related Witt series
:

(I)

{\mathbf{v}}
is a hermitian space (over a quadratic extension of
{\mathbf{F}}_{q}
),
and
\{{\mathbf{v}}^{\prime+}\}
(resp.
\{{\mathbf{v}}^{\prime-}\}
) is the Witt series of even-dimensional
(resp. odd-dimensional) hermitian spaces;

(II)

{\mathbf{v}}
is a quadratic space,
and
\{{\mathbf{v}}^{\prime}\}
is the Witt series of symplectic spaces;

(III)

{\mathbf{v}}
is a symplectic space,
and
\{{\mathbf{v}}^{\prime+}\}
(resp.
\{{\mathbf{v}}^{\prime-}\}
) is the Witt series of even-dimensional quadratic spaces
with trivial (resp. two-dimensional) anisotropic kernel;

(IV)

{\mathbf{v}}
is a symplectic space,
and
\{{\mathbf{v}}^{\prime}\}
is the Witt series of odd-dimensional quadratic spaces.

For each case the pair
(G({\mathbf{v}}),G({\mathbf{v}}^{\prime}))
(or
(G({\mathbf{v}}),G({\mathbf{v}}^{\prime\epsilon}))
where
\epsilon=\pm
)
forms a reductive dual pair over
{\mathbf{F}}_{q}
where
G({\mathbf{v}})
denotes the group of isometries of
{\mathbf{v}}

with the given form.

Let
\rho\in{\mathcal{E}}(G({\mathbf{v}}))
.
For (I) and (III),
let
n_{0}^{\prime\epsilon}(\rho)
denote the minimal dimension of
{\mathbf{v}}^{\prime\epsilon}
such that

\rho
occurs in the Howe correspondence for the dual pair
(G({\mathbf{v}}),G({\mathbf{v}}^{\prime\epsilon}))
.
For (II) and (IV),
let
n_{0}^{\prime}(\rho)
denote the minimal dimension of
{\mathbf{v}}^{\prime}
such that

\rho
occurs in the Howe correspondence for the dual pair
(G({\mathbf{v}}),G({\mathbf{v}}^{\prime}))
.
The following
preservation principle
for cuspidal characters is from
[
Pan02
]
theorem 12.3:

Proposition 1.1
.

Let
\rho
be an irreducible cuspidal character of
G({\mathbf{v}})
.

(i)

If
G({\mathbf{v}})={\rm U}({\mathbf{v}})
, then

n^{\prime+}_{0}(\rho)+n^{\prime-}_{0}(\rho)=2\,\dim({\mathbf{v}})+1
.

(ii)

If
G({\mathbf{v}})={\rm O}({\mathbf{v}})
, then

n_{0}^{\prime}(\rho)+n_{0}^{\prime}(\rho\cdot{\rm sgn})=2\,\dim({\mathbf{v}})
.

(iii)

If
G({\mathbf{v}})={\rm Sp}({\mathbf{v}})
and
{\mathbf{v}}^{\prime\pm}
even-dimensional, then

n^{\prime+}_{0}(\rho)+n^{\prime-}_{0}(\rho)=2\,\dim({\mathbf{v}})+2
.

(iv)

If
G({\mathbf{v}})={\rm Sp}({\mathbf{v}})
and
{\mathbf{v}}^{\prime}
odd-dimensional, then

n^{\prime}_{0}(\rho)+n^{\prime}_{0}(\rho^{c})=2\,\dim({\mathbf{v}})+2
.

Note that here “
{\rm sgn}
” denotes the sign character of
{\rm O}({\mathbf{v}})
,
and “
\rho^{c}
” denotes the character obtained from
\rho
by conjugating
certain element in the symplectic similitude group (
cf
.
[
Wal04
]
§4.11).

1.2.

The purpose of this article is to generalize the above preservation principle
to all irreducible characters.
To achieves this we need to recall Lusztig’s parametrzation of irreducible
characters of finite classical groups.

Suppose that
\text{\bf G}={\rm U}_{n}
,
{\rm Sp}_{2n}
,
{\rm O}^{\epsilon}_{2n}
, or
{\rm SO}_{2n+1}
.
Let
{\mathcal{E}}(G)_{s}
denote the
Lusztig series
associated to the conjugacy class
(s)

of a semisimple element
s
in the connected component
(G^{*})^{0}
of the dual group
G^{*}
of
G
.
In particular,
{\mathcal{E}}(G)_{1}
is the set of irreducible unipotent characters of
G
.
It is known that these Lusztig series partition the set of irreducible characters:

{\mathcal{E}}(G)=\bigcup_{(s)\subset(G^{*})^{0}}{\mathcal{E}}(G)_{s}.

In
[
Lus77
]
Lusztig shows that there exists a bijection

{\mathfrak{L}}_{s}\colon{\mathcal{E}}(G)_{s}\rightarrow{\mathcal{E}}(C_{G^{*}}(s))_{1}

where
C_{G^{*}}(s)
denotes the centralizer of
s
.
Recall that we can define groups
G^{(j)}
(where
j=0,1
of
G
is a unitary group,
and
j=0,1,2
if
G
is an orthogonal group or a symplectic group), and then
we have a bijection

\Xi_{s}\colon{\mathcal{E}}(G)_{s}\rightarrow\begin{cases}{\mathcal{E}}(G^{(0)}\times G^{(1)})_{1},&\text{if $G$ is a unitary group};\\
{\mathcal{E}}(G^{(0)}\times G^{(1)}\times G^{(2)})_{1},&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

modified from
{\mathfrak{L}}_{s}
above.

A
\beta
-set

A=\{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m}\}
is a finite subset of non-negative integers with
elements written in decreasing order, i.e.,
a_{1}>a_{2}>\cdots>a_{m}
.
A
symbol
(by Lusztig)
\Lambda=\binom{A}{B}
is an ordered pair of two
\beta
-sets.
Lusztig gives a parametrization of the set
{\mathcal{E}}(G)_{1}
of irreducible unipotent characters of
a classical group
G
in terms of certain set of symbols denoted by
{\mathcal{S}}_{\text{\bf G}}

(
cf
. Subsection
2.2
, Subsection
2.4
).
The unipotent character associated to
\Lambda
is denoted by
\rho_{\Lambda}
.

For a
\beta
-set
A
and a symbol
\Lambda=\binom{A}{B}
, we define

(1.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\delta(A)&=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if $A=\emptyset$};\\
\max(A)-|A|+1,&\text{if $A\neq\emptyset$}\end{cases}\\
\delta(\Lambda)&=\delta(A)+\delta(B)\end{split}

where
\max(A)
denotes the maximal element of
A
,
and
|A|
denotes the number of elements in
A
.
For
\Lambda\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\text{\bf G}}
, we define
\delta(\rho_{\Lambda})=\rho(\Lambda)
.

Now we consider types (I), (II), (III), (IV) of related Witt series given in Subsection
1.1
.
Let
\rho\in{\mathcal{E}}(G({\mathbf{v}}))
.
If
{\mathbf{v}}
is not an odd-dimensional quadratic space,
we suppose that
\rho\in{\mathcal{E}}(G({\mathbf{v}}))_{s}
for some
s
.
If
{\mathbf{v}}
is an odd-dimensional quadratic space,
we let
G^{0}({\mathbf{v}})
denote the connected component of
G({\mathbf{v}})
and suppose that

\rho|_{G^{0}({\mathbf{v}})}\in{\mathcal{E}}(G^{0}({\mathbf{v}}))_{s}
for some
s
.
Now we write

\begin{cases}\Xi_{s}(\rho)=\rho^{(0)}\otimes\rho^{(1)},&\text{if $G({\mathbf{v}})$ is a unitary group};\\
\Xi_{s}(\rho|_{G^{0}({\mathbf{v}})})=\rho^{(0)}\otimes\rho^{(1)}\otimes\rho^{(2)},&\text{if $G({\mathbf{v}})$ is an odd-orthogonal group};\\
\Xi_{s}(\rho)=\rho^{(0)}\otimes\rho^{(1)}\otimes\rho^{(2)},&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

(
cf
. (
2.15
), (
2.24
)).
Then we define

(1.3)

\delta(\rho)=\begin{cases}\delta(\rho^{(1)}),&\text{for types (I), (IV)};\\
\delta(\rho^{(2)}),&\text{for types (II), (III)}.\end{cases}

It is known that
\delta(\rho)=\delta(\rho^{c})
if
G
is a symplectic group;
and
\delta(\rho)=\delta(\rho\cdot{\rm sgn})
if
G
is an orthogonal group.

Now we state the generalization of the preservation principle
(
cf
. Theorems
4.1
,
4.2
,
4.6
, and
4.9
) for any irreducible characters:

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\rho
be an irreducible character of
G({\mathbf{v}})
.

(i)

If
G({\mathbf{v}})={\rm U}({\mathbf{v}})
, then

n^{\prime+}_{0}(\rho)+n^{\prime-}_{0}(\rho)=2\,\dim({\mathbf{v}})-2\delta(\rho)+1
.

(ii)

If
G({\mathbf{v}})={\rm O}({\mathbf{v}})
, then

n_{0}^{\prime}(\rho)+n_{0}^{\prime}(\rho\cdot{\rm sgn})=2\,\dim({\mathbf{v}})-2\delta(\rho)
.

(iii)

If
G({\mathbf{v}})={\rm Sp}({\mathbf{v}})
and
\{{\mathbf{v}}^{\prime\pm}\}
even-dimensional, then

n^{\prime+}_{0}(\rho)+n^{\prime-}_{0}(\rho)=2\,\dim({\mathbf{v}})-2\delta(\rho)+2
.

(iv)

If
G({\mathbf{v}})={\rm Sp}({\mathbf{v}})
and
\{{\mathbf{v}}^{\prime}\}
odd-dimensional, then

n^{\prime}_{0}(\rho)+n^{\prime}_{0}(\rho^{c})=2\,\dim({\mathbf{v}})-2\delta(\rho)+2
.

It is known that
\delta(\rho)=0
if
\rho
is cuspidal.
Hence, the above theorem is the generalization of the preservation principles
for cuspidal characters in Proposition
1.1
.
Now we list two applications of our main theorem:

(1)

For an irreducible character
\rho
of a classical group
G
.
A non-negative integer called
tensor rank
is attached to
\rho
by Gurevich-Howe in

[
GH20
]
.
The tensor rank is called
\Theta
-rank
and denoted by
\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\rho)
in

]
.
Then Theorem

.
More precisely, for

, we have

\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\rho)\leq\begin{cases}2n,&\text{if $\text{\bf G}={\rm Sp}_{2n},{\rm O}^{\epsilon}_{2n},{\rm O}_{2n+1}$};\\
n,&\text{if $\text{\bf G}={\rm U}_{n}$}.\end{cases}

More details can be found in
[

]
.

(2)

In
[
How
]

1.3.

Let
\text{\bf G}_{n}
denote
{\rm Sp}_{2n}
,
{\rm O}_{2n+1}
,
{\rm O}^{+}_{2n}
,
{\rm O}^{-}_{2n+2}
,
{\rm U}_{2n}
or
{\rm U}_{2n+1}
.
For
(\rho,\rho^{\prime})\in\Theta^{\psi}_{\text{\bf G}_{n},\text{\bf G}^{\prime}_{n^{\prime}}}
, we denote

\displaystyle\Theta_{\text{\bf G}^{\prime}_{n^{\prime}}}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\{\,\rho^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{E}}(\text{\bf G}^{\prime}_{n^{\prime}})\mid(\rho,\rho^{\prime})\in\Theta^{\psi}_{\text{\bf G}_{n},\text{\bf G}^{\prime}_{n^{\prime}}}\,\},

\displaystyle\Theta_{\text{\bf G}_{n}}(\rho^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\{\,\rho\in{\mathcal{E}}(\text{\bf G}_{n})\mid(\rho,\rho^{\prime})\in\Theta^{\psi}_{\text{\bf G}_{n},\text{\bf G}^{\prime}_{n^{\prime}}}\,\}.

It is known that
\Theta_{\text{\bf G}^{\prime}_{n^{\prime}}}(\rho)\neq\emptyset
implies that

\Theta_{\text{\bf G}^{\prime}_{n^{\prime\prime}}}(\rho)\neq\emptyset
for any
n^{\prime\prime}\geq n^{\prime}
.
Therefore, we say that
(\rho,\rho^{\prime})\in\Theta^{\psi}_{\text{\bf G}_{n},\text{\bf G}^{\prime}_{n^{\prime}}}
is a
first occurrence

if
\Theta_{\text{\bf G}^{\prime}_{n^{\prime}-1}}(\rho)=\emptyset
and
\Theta_{\text{\bf G}_{n-1}}(\rho^{\prime})=\emptyset
.
It is well known that if
(\rho,\rho^{\prime})
occurs in the correspondence and both
\rho,\rho^{\prime}
are cuspidal,
then
(\rho,\rho^{\prime})
is a first occurrence.
Of course, the converse implication is in general not true.

Let
\rho\in{\mathcal{E}}(G({\mathbf{v}}))
.
It is shown in
[
Pan
]
that
\delta(\rho)=0
if and only if there exists

\rho^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{E}}(G({\mathbf{v}}^{\prime}))
for some
{\mathbf{v}}^{\prime}
in its Witt series such that
(\rho,\rho^{\prime})
is a first occurrence.

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
\rho
be an irreducible character of a classical
G({\mathbf{v}})
.
Then
\rho
satisfies the preservation principle in Proposition
1.1

if and only if there exists
\rho^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{E}}(G({\mathbf{v}}^{\prime}))
for some
{\mathbf{v}}^{\prime}
in its Witt series
such that
(\rho,\rho^{\prime})
is a first occurrence in the Howe correspondence.

1.4.

It is known that the unipotent characters are not preserved by the correspondence
for synmplectic/odd-orthogonal dual pairs.
To understand the correspondence for this kind of dual pairs, we need to introduce some new notations.
An irreducible character
\rho
of
G
is called
pseudo-unipotent
if

\langle\rho,R_{\text{\bf T}_{w},\theta_{w}}\rangle\neq 0
for some
\text{\bf T}_{w}
where
\theta_{w}
is an order
2
character
of
\text{\bf T}_{w}
(
cf
.
[
Pan16
]
§3.3).

When
G
is a unitary group or an orthogonal group,
an irreducible character
\rho
of
G
is pseudo-unipotent if and only if

\rho\chi_{\text{\bf G}}
is unipotent
where
\chi_{\text{\bf G}}
is a fixed character of
G
of order
2
(
cf
. Lemma
5.1
).
However, the situation for symplectic groups is more interesting.

Proposition 1.6
.

A symplectic group
{\rm Sp}_{2n}(q)
has a pseudo-unipotent cuspidal character if and only if

n=m^{2}
for some integer
m
.
Moreover
{\rm Sp}_{2m^{2}}(q)
,
m>0
, has exactly two irreducible pseudo-unipotent cuspidal characters.

The description of the first occurrences of unipotent or pseudo-unipotent cuspidal charatcers
for the dual pairs
({\rm Sp}_{2n},{\rm O}^{\epsilon}_{2n^{\prime}})
or
({\rm Sp}_{2n},{\rm O}_{2n^{\prime}+1})
are given
in Proposition
5.4
, Proposition
5.5
, and Proposition
5.6
.

1.5.

The contents of this article are as follows.
In Section 2 we first give the definitions and notations which are used in this paper.
Then we recall some basic properties of Deligne-Lusztig virtual character and the characterization
of unipotent characters by Lusztig.
In Section 3 we discuss the “
generalized preservation principle
” for the Howe correspondence
of irreducible unipotent characters.
In Section 4 we deduce the generalized preservation principle for general irreducible characters from
the preservation principle of irreducible unipotent characters discussed in Section 3.
In the final section, we define “pseudo-unipotent characters” for symplectic groups
and give a complete description of the Howe correspondence on unipotent and pseudo-unipotent
cuspidal characters.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Lusztig’s symbols

Recall that a symbol
\Lambda=\binom{A}{B}
is an ordered pair of two
\beta
-sets
(
cf
. Subsection
1.2
).
The set
A
(resp.
B
) is called the
first row
(resp. the
second row
) of
\Lambda
and is also
denoted by
\Lambda^{*}
(resp.
\Lambda_{*}
).
One the set of symbols, we define an equivalence relation generated by

\binom{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m_{1}}}{b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{m_{2}}}\sim\binom{a_{1}+1,a_{2}+1,\ldots,a_{m_{1}}+1,0}{b_{1}+1,b_{2}+1,\ldots,b_{m_{2}}+1,0}.

The set of equivalence classes of symbols is denoted by
{\mathcal{S}}
.

For a symbol
\Lambda=\binom{a_{1},\ldots,a_{m_{1}}}{b_{1},\ldots,b_{m_{2}}}
,
we define its
rank
and
defect
by

(2.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\rm rk}(\Lambda)&=\sum_{i=1}^{m_{1}}a_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{m_{2}}b_{j}-\biggl\lfloor\biggr(\frac{m_{1}+m_{2}-1}{2}\biggr)^{2}\biggr\rfloor,\\
{\rm def}(\Lambda)&=m_{1}-m_{2}.\end{split}

It is easy to check that

{\rm rk}(\Lambda)\geq\biggl\lfloor\biggl(\frac{{\rm def}(\Lambda)}{2}\biggr)^{2}\biggr\rfloor

for any
\Lambda
.
A symbol
\Lambda
is called
cuspidal
if the above inequality is in fact an equality.
It is known that
\Lambda
is cuspidal if and only if the unipotent character
\rho_{\Lambda}
is cuspidal.

Recall that a
bi-partition

\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{\mu}{\nu}
of
n
is an ordered pair of two
partitions
\mu,\nu
such that
\bigl|\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{\mu}{\nu}\bigr|:=|\mu|+|\nu|=n
.
We define a mapping from symbols to bi-partitions by

(2.2)

\Upsilon\colon\binom{a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{m_{2}}}{b_{1},b_{2},\cdots,b_{m_{2}}}\mapsto\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{a_1-(m_1-1),a_2-(m_2-1),\ldots,a_{m_1}}{b_1-(m_2-1),b_2-(m_2-2),\ldots,b_{m_2}}.

It is easy to see that
\Lambda_{1}\sim\Lambda_{2}
if and only if
\Upsilon(\Lambda_{1})=\Upsilon(\Lambda_{2})
.
Moreover, it is not difficult to check that

(2.3)

{\rm rk}(\Lambda)=|\Upsilon(\Lambda)|+\biggl\lfloor\biggl(\frac{{\rm def}(\Lambda)}{2}\biggr)^{2}\biggr\rfloor=|\Upsilon(\Lambda)|+\begin{cases}\bigl(\frac{{\rm def}(\Lambda)}{2}\bigr)^{2},&\text{if ${\rm def}(\Lambda)$ even};\\
\bigl(\frac{{\rm def}(\Lambda)-1}{2}\bigr)\bigl(\frac{{\rm def}(\Lambda)+1}{2}\bigr),&\text{if ${\rm def}(\Lambda)$ odd}.\end{cases}

Therefore
\Lambda
is cuspidal if and only if
\Upsilon(\Lambda)
is the
empty partition
\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{-}{-}
.

Recall that for a
\beta
-set
A
, a number
\delta(A)
is given in Subsection
1.2
.
It is clear that
\delta(A)\geq 0
,
and
\delta(A)=0
if and only if
A=\emptyset
or
A=\{m-1,m-2,\ldots,0\}
for some
m\geq 1
.
For a symbol
\Lambda=\binom{A}{B}
,
it is clear that
\delta(\Lambda)=\delta(\Lambda^{\rm t})
where
\binom{A}{B}^{\rm t}=\binom{B}{A}
,
and
\delta(\Lambda)=\delta(\Lambda^{\prime})
if
\Lambda\sim\Lambda^{\prime}
.

Lemma 2.4
.

A symbol
\Lambda
is cuspidal if and only if
\delta(\Lambda)=0
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\delta(\Lambda)=0
and write
\Lambda=\binom{A}{B}
.
Now
\delta(\Lambda)=0
implies that
\delta(A)=\delta(B)=0
.
Hence
A
is either empty or
A=\{m_{1},m_{1}-1,\ldots,1,0\}
for some
m_{1}\geq 0
.
Similarly,
B
is either empty or
B=\{m_{2},m_{2}-1,\ldots,1,0\}
for some
m_{2}\geq 0
.
If
A
or
B
is empty, it is clear that
\Lambda
is cuspidal.
If
A
and
B
are both non-empty,
without loss of generality, may assume that
m_{1}\geq m_{2}
,
then

\Lambda=\binom{m_{1},m_{1}-1,\ldots,1,0}{m_{2},m_{2}-1,\ldots,1,0}\sim\binom{m_{1}-m_{2}-1,m_{1}-m_{2}-2,\ldots,1,0}{-}.

So
\Lambda
is again cuspidal.
On the other hand,
it is also clear by definition that
\delta(\Lambda)=0
if
\Lambda
is cuspidal.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Suppose that
{\rm rk}(\Lambda)=n
.
Then we have
\delta(\Lambda)\leq n
.
Moreover,
\delta(\Lambda)=n
if and only if

\Lambda\sim\begin{cases}\binom{k}{n-k+1,0},&\text{if\/ ${\rm def}(\Lambda)=-1$};\\
\binom{n-k}{k},&\text{if\/ ${\rm def}(\Lambda)=0$};\\
\binom{n-k+1,0}{k},&\text{if\/ ${\rm def}(\Lambda)=1$}\end{cases}

for some
0\leq k\leq n
.

Proof.

Suppose that
{\rm rk}(\Lambda)=n
and write
\Lambda=\binom{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m_{1}}}{b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{m_{2}}}
.
Because
\delta(\Lambda)
is invariant on the similar class of
\Lambda
,
we may assume that
m_{1},m_{2}
are both nonzero.
Now by definition we have

\delta(\Lambda)=(a_{1}-m_{1}+1)+(b_{1}-m_{2}+1)\leq|\Upsilon(\Lambda)|={\rm rk}(\Lambda)-\biggl\lfloor\biggl(\frac{{\rm def}(\Lambda)}{2}\biggr)^{2}\biggr\rfloor\leq n.

It is easy to check that if
\Lambda
is similar to one of the symbols listed in the lemma,
then
\delta(\Lambda)=n
.
On the other hand, suppose that
\delta(\Lambda)=n
.
Then we must have
\bigl\lfloor\bigl(\frac{{\rm def}(\Lambda)}{2}\bigr)^{2}\bigr\rfloor=0
and

\Upsilon(\Lambda)=\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{a_1-m_1+1}{b_1-m_2+1}=\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{n-k}{k}
for some
0\leq k\leq n
.
This implies that
{\rm def}(\Lambda)=0,\pm 1
and
\Lambda
is similar to one the symbols listed in the lemma.
∎

Lemma 2.6
.

Suppose that
{\rm rk}(\Lambda)=n
and
{\rm def}(\Lambda)=\pm 2
.
Then we have
\delta(\Lambda)\leq n-1
.
Moreover,
\delta(\Lambda)=n-1
if and only if

\Lambda\sim\textstyle\binom{k}{n-k+1,1,0},\binom{n-k+1,1,0}{k}

for some
0\leq k\leq n-1
.

Proof.

Write
\Lambda=\binom{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m_{1}}}{b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{m_{2}}}
.
From the proof of the previous lemma, we see that

\delta(\Lambda)\leq|\Upsilon(\Lambda)|={\rm rk}(\Lambda)-\biggl\lfloor\biggl(\frac{{\rm def}(\Lambda)}{2}\biggr)^{2}\biggr\rfloor=n-1.

Moreover, if
\delta(\Lambda)=n-1
, we need

∎

3.
Generalized Preservation Principle of Unipotent Characters

3.1.
Preservation principle for unipotent cuspidal characters

Irreducible unipotent cuspidal characters of classical groups (other than the general linear groups)
are characterized by Lusztig as follows:

(1)

{\rm U}_{n}(q)
has an irreducible unipotent character if
and only if
n=\frac{1}{2}m(m+1)
for some
m\geq 0
.
In this case, there is a unique irreducible unipotent cuspidal character.

(2)

{\rm O}_{2n+1}(q)
has an irreducible unipotent character if
and only if
n=m(m+1)
for some
m\geq 0
.
In this case, there are two irreducible unipotent cuspidal characters different by the sign character.

(3)

{\rm Sp}_{2n}(q)
has an irreducible unipotent character if
and only if
n=m(m+1)
for some
m\geq 0
.
In this case, there is a unique irreducible unipotent cuspidal character.

(4)

{\rm O}^{\epsilon}_{2n}(q)
has an irreducible unipotent character if
and only if
n=m^{2}
and
\epsilon={\rm sgn}((-1)^{m})
for some
m\geq 0
.
In this case, there are two irreducible unipotent cuspidal characters different by the sign character.

When
(\text{\bf G},\text{\bf G}^{\prime})=({\rm U}_{n},{\rm U}_{n^{\prime}})
or
({\rm Sp}_{2n},{\rm O}^{\epsilon}_{2n^{\prime}})
,
the unipotent characters are preserved by the Howe correspondence,
and the first occurrence of irreducible unipotent cuspidal characters are completely
determined in
[
AM93
]
theorems 4.1 and 5.2.
And we have the following preservation principle of unipotent cuspidal characters:

(I)

For the unipotent cuspidal character
\zeta^{\rm U}_{m}
of
{\rm U}_{\frac{m(m+1)}{2}}(q)
,
we have

n_{0}^{\prime+}(\zeta_{m}^{\rm U})+n_{0}^{\prime-}(\zeta_{m}^{\rm U})=\tfrac{1}{2}m(m-1)+\tfrac{1}{2}(m+1)(m+2)=m(m+1)+1.

(II)

For the unipotent cuspidal characters
\zeta^{\rm I}_{m},\zeta^{\rm II}_{m}
of
{\rm O}^{\epsilon}_{2m^{2}}(q)
,
we have

n_{0}^{\prime}(\zeta_{m}^{\rm I})+n_{0}^{\prime}(\zeta_{m}^{\rm II})=2m(m-1)+2m(m+1)=4m^{2}.

(III)

For the unipotent cuspidal character
\zeta_{m}^{\rm Sp}
of
{\rm Sp}_{2m(m+1)}(q)
and all the
orthogonal spaces
{\mathbf{v}}^{\prime\pm}
are even-dimensional,
we have

n_{0}^{\prime+}(\zeta_{m}^{\rm Sp})+n_{0}^{\prime-}(\zeta_{m}^{\rm Sp})=2m^{2}+2(m+1)^{2}=4m(m+1)+2.

Note that unipotent characters are not preserved by the Howe correspondence for the dual pair

({\rm Sp}_{2n},{\rm O}_{2n^{\prime}+1})
.

3.2.
Generalized preservation principle for symplectic groups

For a symbol
\Lambda=\binom{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m_{1}}}{b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{m_{2}}}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm Sp}
and
\epsilon=\pm
,
we define
\theta^{\epsilon}_{0}(\Lambda)
by

\displaystyle\theta_{0}^{+}(\Lambda)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\binom{b_{1},b_{2},b_{3},\ldots,b_{m_{2}}}{a_{2},a_{3},\ldots,a_{m_{1}}}=\Lambda^{\rm t}\smallsetminus\binom{-}{a_{1}},&\text{if $m_{1}>0$};\\
\binom{b_{1}+1,b_{2}+1,\ldots,b_{m_{2}}+1,0}{-},&\text{if $m_{1}=0$}.\end{cases}

\displaystyle\theta_{0}^{-}(\Lambda)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\binom{b_{2},b_{3},\ldots,b_{m_{2}}}{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m_{1}}}=\Lambda^{\rm t}\smallsetminus\binom{b_{1}}{-},&\text{if $m_{2}>0$};\\
\binom{-}{a_{1}+1,a_{2}+1,\ldots,a_{m_{1}}+1,0},&\text{if $m_{2}=0$}.\end{cases}

It is known that
{\rm def}(\Lambda)\equiv 1\pmod{4}
,
so it is easy to see that

{\rm def}(\theta_{0}^{+}(\Lambda))\equiv 0\pmod{4}
and
{\rm def}(\theta_{0}^{-}(\Lambda))\equiv 2\pmod{4}
.
Then we have
\theta_{0}^{\epsilon}(\Lambda)\in{\mathcal{S}}_{{\rm O}^{\epsilon}_{\rm even}}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

For
\Lambda\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm Sp}
and
\epsilon=\pm
,

\theta^{\epsilon}_{0}(\Lambda)
is a symbol of minimal rank in

\Theta^{\epsilon}(\Lambda)=\{\,\Lambda^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{{\rm O}^{\epsilon}_{\rm even}}\mid(\Lambda,\Lambda^{\prime})\in{\mathcal{B}}^{\epsilon}\,\},

i.e.,
\theta^{\epsilon}_{0}(\Lambda)\in\Theta^{\epsilon}(\Lambda)
and

{\rm rk}(\theta^{\epsilon}_{0}(\Lambda))\leq{\rm rk}(\Lambda^{\prime})
for any

\Lambda^{\prime}\in\Theta^{\epsilon}(\Lambda)
.

Proof.

From
[
Pan21b
]
lemma 2.6,
it is not difficult to see that
(\Lambda,\theta^{\epsilon}_{0}(\Lambda))\in{\mathcal{B}}^{\epsilon}

and
\theta^{\epsilon}_{0}(\Lambda)
is of minimal rank in
\Theta^{\epsilon}(\Lambda)
.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

For
\Lambda\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm Sp}
,
we have

{\rm rk}(\theta_{0}^{+}(\Lambda))+{\rm rk}(\theta_{0}^{-}(\Lambda))=2\,{\rm rk}(\Lambda)-\delta(\Lambda)+1.

Proof.

Write
\Lambda=\binom{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m_{1}}}{b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{m_{2}}}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm Sp}
.
Note that
m_{1}-m_{2}\equiv 1\pmod{4}
.
Now
m_{1}+m_{2}
is odd and hence

\bigl\lfloor\bigl(\tfrac{m_{1}+m_{2}}{2}\bigr)^{2}\bigr\rfloor-\bigl\lfloor\bigl(\tfrac{m_{1}+m_{2}-1}{2}\bigr)^{2}\bigr\rfloor=\bigl\lfloor\bigl(\tfrac{m_{1}+m_{2}-1}{2}\bigr)^{2}\bigr\rfloor-\bigl\lfloor\bigl(\tfrac{m_{1}+m_{2}-2}{2}\bigr)^{2}\bigr\rfloor=\tfrac{1}{2}(m_{1}+m_{2}-1).

If
m_{1}=0
,
then
m_{2}\neq 0
and

\displaystyle{\rm rk}(\theta_{0}^{+}(\Lambda))

\displaystyle={\rm rk}(\Lambda)+m_{2}-\tfrac{1}{2}(m_{1}+m_{2}-1);

\displaystyle{\rm rk}(\theta_{0}^{-}(\Lambda))

\displaystyle={\rm rk}(\Lambda)-b_{1}+\tfrac{1}{2}(m_{1}+m_{2}-1)

by (
2.1
).
If
m_{2}=0
,
then
m_{1}\neq 0
and

\displaystyle{\rm rk}(\theta_{0}^{+}(\Lambda))

\displaystyle={\rm rk}(\Lambda)+m_{1}-\tfrac{1}{2}(m_{1}+m_{2}-1);

\displaystyle{\rm rk}(\theta_{0}^{-}(\Lambda))

\displaystyle={\rm rk}(\Lambda)-a_{1}+\tfrac{1}{2}(m_{1}+m_{2}-1).

If both
m_{1},m_{2}
are nonzero,
then

\displaystyle{\rm rk}(\theta_{0}^{+}(\Lambda))

\displaystyle={\rm rk}(\Lambda)-a_{1}+\tfrac{1}{2}(m_{1}+m_{2}-1);

\displaystyle{\rm rk}(\theta_{0}^{-}(\Lambda))

\displaystyle={\rm rk}(\Lambda)-b_{1}+\tfrac{1}{2}(m_{1}+m_{2}-1).

For all three cases, we have

{\rm rk}(\theta_{0}^{+}(\Lambda))+{\rm rk}(\theta_{0}^{-}(\Lambda))=2\,{\rm rk}(\Lambda)-\delta(\Lambda)+1.

∎

Corollary 3.3
.

For an irreducible unipotent character
\rho_{\Lambda}
of
{\rm Sp}_{2n}(q)
,
we have

n^{+}_{0}(\rho_{\Lambda})+n_{0}^{-}(\rho_{\Lambda})=4n-2\delta(\Lambda)+2.

Proof.

We know that
{\rm rk}(\Lambda)=n
and

n_{0}^{\epsilon}(\rho_{\Lambda})=2\,{\rm rk}(\theta^{\epsilon}_{0}(\Lambda))
by Lemma
3.1
.
So the corollary follows from Lemma
3.2
immediately.
∎

3.3.
Generalized preservation principle for even orthogonal groups

For a symbol
\Lambda=\binom{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m_{1}}}{b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{m_{2}}}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{{\rm O}^{\epsilon}_{\rm even}}
,
we define
\theta_{0}(\Lambda)
by

\theta_{0}(\Lambda)=\begin{cases}\binom{b_{1},b_{2},b_{3},\ldots,b_{m_{2}}}{a_{2},a_{3},\ldots,a_{m_{1}}}=\Lambda^{\rm t}\smallsetminus\binom{-}{a_{1}},&\text{if $\epsilon=+$ and $m_{1}>0$};\\
\binom{b_{1}+1,b_{2}+1,\ldots,b_{m_{2}}+1,0}{-},&\text{if $\epsilon=+$ and $m_{1}=0$};\\
\binom{b_{2},b_{3},\ldots,b_{m_{2}}}{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m_{1}}}=\Lambda^{\rm t}\smallsetminus\binom{b_{1}}{-},&\text{if $\epsilon=-$ and $m_{2}>0$};\\
\binom{-}{a_{1}+1,a_{2}+1,\ldots,a_{m_{1}}+1,0},&\text{if $\epsilon=-$ and $m_{2}=0$}.\end{cases}

If
\epsilon=+
, then
{\rm def}(\Lambda)\equiv 0\pmod{4}
;
if
\epsilon=-
, then
{\rm def}(\Lambda)\equiv 2\pmod{4}
.
Then we have
{\rm def}(\theta_{0}(\Lambda))\equiv 1\pmod{4}
for both cases,
and hence
\theta_{0}(\Lambda)\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm Sp}
.

Lemma 3.4
.

For
\Lambda\in{\mathcal{S}}_{{\rm O}^{\epsilon}_{\rm even}}
,

\theta_{0}(\Lambda)
is a symbol of minimal rank in

\Theta(\Lambda)=\{\,\Lambda^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm Sp}\mid(\Lambda^{\prime},\Lambda)\in{\mathcal{B}}^{\epsilon}\,\}.

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of Lemma
3.1
.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

For
\Lambda\in{\mathcal{S}}_{{\rm O}^{\epsilon}_{\rm even}}
,
we have

{\rm rk}(\theta_{0}(\Lambda))+{\rm rk}(\theta_{0}(\Lambda^{\rm t}))=2\,{\rm rk}(\Lambda)-\delta(\Lambda).

Proof.

Write
\Lambda=\binom{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m_{1}}}{b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{m_{2}}}
.
Note that now
m_{1}+m_{2}
is even.
Suppose first that
m_{1}=m_{2}=0
.
Then
\Lambda=\Lambda^{\rm t}=\binom{-}{-}
and
\epsilon=+
.
It is clearly that
\theta_{0}(\Lambda)=\theta_{0}(\Lambda^{\rm t})=\binom{0}{-}
,
and the assertion is obvious.

Now we suppose that
m_{1}+m_{2}\geq 2
and we have

\displaystyle\bigl\lfloor\bigl(\tfrac{m_{1}+m_{2}}{2}\bigr)^{2}\bigr\rfloor-\bigl\lfloor\bigl(\tfrac{m_{1}+m_{2}-1}{2}\bigr)^{2}\bigr\rfloor

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}(m_{1}+m_{2});

\displaystyle\bigl\lfloor\bigl(\tfrac{m_{1}+m_{2}-1}{2}\bigr)^{2}\bigr\rfloor-\bigl\lfloor\bigl(\tfrac{m_{1}+m_{2}-2}{2}\bigr)^{2}\bigr\rfloor

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}(m_{1}+m_{2}-2).

If
m_{1}=0
,
then
m_{2}\neq 0
and

{\rm rk}(\theta_{0}(\Lambda))+{\rm rk}(\theta_{0}(\Lambda^{\rm t}))={\rm rk}(\Lambda)+m_{2}-\tfrac{1}{2}(m_{1}+m_{2})+{\rm rk}(\Lambda)-b_{1}+\tfrac{1}{2}(m_{1}+m_{2}-2).

If
m_{2}=0
,
then
m_{1}\neq 0
and

{\rm rk}(\theta_{0}(\Lambda))+{\rm rk}(\theta_{0}(\Lambda^{\rm t}))={\rm rk}(\Lambda)+m_{1}-\tfrac{1}{2}(m_{1}+m_{2})+{\rm rk}(\Lambda)-a_{1}+\tfrac{1}{2}(m_{1}+m_{2}-2).

If both
m_{1},m_{2}
are nonzero,
then

{\rm rk}(\theta_{0}(\Lambda))+{\rm rk}(\theta_{0}(\Lambda^{\rm t}))=2\,{\rm rk}(\Lambda)-(a_{1}+b_{1})+(m_{1}+m_{2}-2).

For all cases we have

{\rm rk}(\theta_{0}(\Lambda))+{\rm rk}(\theta_{0}(\Lambda^{\rm t}))=2\,{\rm rk}(\Lambda)-\delta(\Lambda).

∎

Corollary 3.6
.

For an irreducible unipotent character
\rho_{\Lambda}
of
{\rm O}^{\epsilon}_{2n}(q)
,
we have

n^{\prime}_{0}(\rho_{\Lambda})+n^{\prime}_{0}(\rho_{\Lambda}\cdot{\rm sgn})=4n-2\delta(\Lambda).

Proof.

Now
{\rm rk}(\Lambda)={\rm rk}(\Lambda^{\rm t})=n
,

\rho_{\Lambda^{\rm t}}=\rho_{\Lambda}\cdot{\rm sgn}
, and
n^{\prime}_{0}(\rho_{\Lambda})=2\,{\rm rk}(\theta_{0}(\Lambda))

by Lemma
3.4
.
So the corollary follows from Lemma
3.5
immediately.
∎

3.4.

Proposition 3.8
.

For an irreducible unipotent character
\rho_{\lambda}
of

\displaystyle n_{0}^{+}(\rho_{\lambda})+n_{0}^{-}(\rho_{\lambda})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}d(d-1)+\frac{1}{2}(d+1)(d+2)+2\,{\rm rk}(\theta_{0}^{+}(\Lambda))+2\,{\rm rk}(\theta_{0}^{-}(\Lambda))

\displaystyle\qquad-2\biggl[\frac{-{\rm def}(\Lambda_{\lambda})-1}{2}\biggr]^{2}-2\biggl[\frac{-{\rm def}(\Lambda_{\lambda})+1}{2}\biggr]^{2}

\displaystyle=d(d+1)+1+4\,{\rm rk}(\Lambda_{\lambda})-4\biggl[\frac{{\rm def}(\Lambda_{\lambda})-1}{2}\biggr]\biggl[\frac{{\rm def}(\Lambda_{\lambda})+1}{2}\biggr]-2\delta(\Lambda_{\lambda})

\displaystyle n_{0}^{+}(\rho_{\lambda})+n_{0}^{-}(\rho_{\lambda})

\displaystyle\quad-2\biggl[\frac{-{\rm def}(\Lambda_{\lambda})-2}{2}\biggr]\biggl[\frac{-{\rm def}(\Lambda_{\lambda})}{2}\biggr]-2\biggl[\frac{-{\rm def}(\Lambda_{\lambda})}{2}\biggr]\biggl[\frac{-{\rm def}(\Lambda_{\lambda})+2}{2}\biggr]

\displaystyle=d(d+1)+1+4\,{\rm rk}(\Lambda_{\lambda})-4\biggl[\frac{{\rm def}(\Lambda_{\lambda})}{2}\biggr]^{2}-2\delta(\Lambda_{\lambda})

\displaystyle=2\,|\lambda|-2\delta(\Lambda_{\lambda})+1.

4.1.
For unitary groups

In this subsection, we consider type (I), i.e.,

Theorem 4.1
.

Let

be an irreducible character of

Let

and suppose that
\rho\in{\mathcal{E}}(G)_{s}
for some
s

\Xi_{s}(\rho)=\rho^{(0)}\otimes\rho^{(1)}
by (

Suppose that
\rho
first occurs in the correspondences for both dual pairs

(G({\mathbf{v}}),G({\mathbf{v}}^{\prime+}))

(G({\mathbf{v}}),G({\mathbf{v}}^{\prime-}))

Recall that

we identify

Then we have decompositions

{\mathbf{v}}=\Biggl[\bigoplus_{\langle\xi\rangle,\ \xi\neq 1}{\mathbf{v}}_{[\xi]}\Biggr]\oplus{\mathbf{v}}_{[1]},\qquad{\mathbf{v}}^{\prime\pm}=\Biggl[\bigoplus_{\langle\xi\rangle,\ \xi\neq 1}{\mathbf{v}}_{[\xi]}^{\prime\pm}\Biggr]\oplus{\mathbf{v}}_{[1]}^{\prime\pm}.

\dim({\mathbf{v}}_{[\xi]})=\dim({\mathbf{v}}_{[\xi]}^{\prime\pm})
for

Moreover the unipotent character
\rho^{(1)}
first occurs in the correspondences

(G({\mathbf{v}}_{[1]}),G({\mathbf{v}}_{[1]}^{\prime+}))

Note that

by Proposition
3.8
.
Therefore

\displaystyle\dim({\mathbf{v}}^{\prime+})+\dim({\mathbf{v}}^{\prime-})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\langle\xi\rangle,\ \xi\neq 1}\dim({\mathbf{v}}_{[\xi]}^{\prime+})+\dim({\mathbf{v}}_{[1]}^{\prime+})+\sum_{\langle\xi\rangle,\ \xi\neq 1}\dim({\mathbf{v}}_{[\xi]}^{\prime-})+\dim({\mathbf{v}}_{[1]}^{\prime-})

).
∎

5.
Pseudo-unipotent Cuspidal Characters

5.1.
Pseudo-unipotent cuspidal characters

Pseudo-unipotent characters are defined in Subsection
1.4
.
By convention,
we regard the trivial character of the trivial group
{\rm Sp}_{0}(q)={\rm O}_{0}^{+}(q)={\rm SO}_{1}(q)
to be both
unipotent and pseudo-unipotent.

Recall that for a general linear group, a unitary group, a special orthogonal group or an orthogonal group

G
, a character
\chi_{\text{\bf G}}
of order
2
is given in
[
Pan16
]
§3.3.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
G
be a unitary group, a special orthogonal group or an orthogonal group.
Then
\rho\in{\mathcal{E}}(G)
is pseudo-unipotent if and only if
\rho\chi_{\text{\bf G}}
is unipotent, i.e.,

\rho
is pseudo-unipotent if and only if it belongs to the Lusztig series
{\mathcal{E}}(G)_{-1}
.

Proof.

For such a classical group
G
,

-1
is in the center of
G^{*}
,
and hence is in every rational maximal torus
\text{\bf T}^{*}
of
\text{\bf G}^{*}
.
It is known that
R_{\text{\bf T}_{w},\theta_{w}}=\chi_{\text{\bf G}}R_{\text{\bf T}_{w},1}
for any
w\in\text{\bf W}_{\text{\bf G}}

(
cf
.
[
DM91
]
proposition 13.30),
and the mapping

{\mathcal{E}}(G)_{-1}\rightarrow{\mathcal{E}}(G)_{1}\quad\text{given by }\rho\mapsto\rho\chi_{\text{\bf G}}

is a bijection.
Therefore,
\langle\rho,R_{\text{\bf T}_{w},\theta_{w}}\rangle_{G}\neq 0
if and only if

\langle\rho\chi_{\text{\bf G}},R_{\text{\bf T}_{w},1}\rangle_{G}\neq 0
.
∎

From the previous lemma we see that a pseudo-unipotent cuspidal character of a unitary group or
an orthogonal group
G
is exactly of the form
\zeta\chi_{G}
where

\zeta
is a unipotent cuspidal character of
G
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\text{\bf G}={\rm Sp}_{2n}
.
Then

is pseudo-unipotent if and only if it is

\rho

Let

be the pair corresponding to
(\text{\bf T}_{w},\theta_{w})

\text{\bf T}^{*}
is a rational maximal torus of
{\rm SO}_{2n+1}
and

.
From the definition of

we know that each

Moreover,

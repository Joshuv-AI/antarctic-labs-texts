Extremal number of graphs from geometric shapes
Jun Gao, Oliver Janzer, Hong Liu, Zixiang Xu

Abstract
We study the Turán problem for highly symmetric bipartite graphs arising from geometric shapes and periodic tilings commonly found in nature. 1. The prism C 2 ​ ℓ □ := C 2 ​ ℓ □ K 2 C_{2\ell}^{\square}:=C_{2\ell}\square K_{2} is the graph consisting of two vertex disjoint 2 ​ ℓ 2\ell -cycles and a matching pairing the corresponding vertices of these two cycles. We show that for every ℓ ≥ 4 \ell\geq 4 , ex ( n , C 2 ​ ℓ □ ) = Θ ⁡ ( n 3 / 2 ) (n,C_{2\ell}^{\square})=\Theta(n^{3/2}) . This resolves a conjecture of He, Li and Feng. 2. The hexagonal tiling in honeycomb is one of the most natural structures in the real world. We show that the extremal number of honeycomb graphs has the same order of magnitude as their basic building unit 6-cycles. 3. We also consider bipartite graphs from quadrangulations of the cylinder and the torus. We prove near optimal bounds for both configurations. In particular, our method gives a very short proof of a tight upper bound for the extremal number of the 2-dimensional grid, improving a recent result of Bradač, Janzer, Sudakov and Tomon. Our proofs mix several ideas, including shifting embedding schemes, weighted homomorphism and subgraph counts and asymmetric dependent random choice.



1
Introduction

Understanding symmetric structures is a prominent objective across various branches of mathematics due to their exceptional regularity and aesthetic appeal. Studies on such structures not only serve as a pursuit of beauty but also form the foundation for several fields of mathematics. In this paper, we investigate the Turán problem for highly symmetric bipartite graphs corresponding to various geometric structures.

The Turán problem, one of the most central topics in extremal combinatorics, is concerned with determining the maximum possible density a graph can have without containing a given graph as a subgraph. Its origin dates back to the result of Mantel
[
33
]
in
1907
, stating that every
n
-vertex triangle-free graph has at most
\frac{n^{2}}{4}
edges, and the work of Erdős
[
10
]
that connects the study of Sidon sets with the Turán problem for 4-cycles. Turán
[
40
]
in 1941 extended Mantel’s theorem to graphs without a copy of the clique
K_{r}
. In the same paper, Turán proposed the study of the five graphs corresponding to platonic solids, and his result covers the tetrahedron graph
K_{4}
. The problem of octahedron, dodecahedron and icosahedron graphs were later resolved by Erdős and Simonovits
[
16
]
and by Simonovits
[
37
,
38
]
respectively, while the innocent looking cube graph remains elusive. Apart from the aforementioned study of Sidon sets in combinatorial number theory, the Turán problem has connections to other fields and during its course of advancement, many tools and methods have been developed, see e.g. the use of (random) algebraic and finite geometric structures
[
3
,
6
,
7
,
9
,
13
,
30
,
32
,
41
]
for lower bound constructions, and applications of Turán type results in discrete geometry
[
1
,
11
]
and information theory
[
29
,
36
,
39
]
.

Formally, for a graph
F
, the
extremal number of
F
, denoted by
\mathrm{ex}(n,F)
, is the maximum number of edges in an
n
-vertex graph not containing
F
as a subgraph. The Erdős-Stone-Simonovits Theorem
[
14
,
17
]
, considered to be a fundamental theorem in extremal graph theory, states that

\mathrm{ex}(n,F)=\bigg(1-\frac{1}{\chi(F)-1}+o(1)\bigg)\binom{n}{2},

where
\chi(F)
is the chromatic number of
F
. This theorem asymptotically solves the problem when
\chi(F)\geq 3
. However, for bipartite graphs, not even the order of magnitude is known in general. It also explains why the cube graph is the most difficult among the five platonic solids as it is the only one which is bipartite.

Two fundamental classes of bipartite graphs with high symmetry are even cycles and complete bipartite graphs. For the even cycle
C_{2\ell}
, Bondy and Simonovits
[
4
]
gave an upper bound
\mathrm{ex}(n,C_{2\ell})=O_{\ell}(n^{1+1/\ell})
, see
[
23
]
for the current best bound. Matching lower bound constructions are only known for the
4
-,
6
-, and
10
-cycle, utilizing generalized polygons from finite geometry, see
[
6
,
13
,
41
]
. The order of magnitude of
\mathrm{ex}(n,C_{2\ell})
is still unknown for any
\ell\notin\{2,3,5\}
. For the complete bipartite graphs
K_{s,t}
, a well-known result of Kővári, Sós and Turán
[
31
]
in 1954 showed that
\textup{ex}(n,K_{s,t})=O(n^{2-1/s})
for any integers
t\geqslant s
. Erdős, Rényi and Sós
[
13
]
and Brown
[
6
]
respectively proved matching lower bounds for the cases
s=2
and
s=3
. For larger
s
, Kollár, Rónyai and Szabó
[
30
]
first showed that
\text{ex}(n,K_{s,t})=\Omega(n^{2-1/s})
when
t\geqslant s!+1
, and then the bound on
t
was improved to
t\geqslant(s-1)!+1
by Alon, Rónyai and Szabó
[
3
]
. The current best dependence
t\geq 9^{s}\cdot s^{4s^{2/3}}
was shown by Bukh
[
8
]
. For more on the bipartite Turán problem, we refer the readers to the comprehensive survey of Füredi and Simonovits
[
21
]
.

In this paper, we continue this line of study and determine the order of magnitude of the extremal number for highly symmetric bipartite graphs stemming from certain geometric shapes and periodic tilings.

1.1
The prisms

The
2\ell
-prism

C_{2\ell}^{\square}:=C_{2\ell}\square K_{2}
is the Cartesian product of the
2\ell
-cycle and an edge. In other words,
C_{2\ell}^{\square}
consists of two vertex disjoint
2\ell
-cycles and a matching joining the corresponding vertices on these two cycles. As
C_{2\ell}^{\square}
contains many
4
-cycles, we have a lower bound
\mathrm{ex}(n,C_{2\ell}^{\square})\geq\mathrm{ex}(n,C_{4})=\Omega(n^{3/2})
. Note that
C_{4}^{\square}
is the notorious cube graph, and the best known upper bound is
\mathrm{ex}(n,C_{4}^{\square})=O(n^{8/5})

[
15
,
35
]
. Studying
C_{2\ell}^{\square}
could shed some light on the cube problem. An upper bound
\mathrm{ex}(n,C_{2\ell}^{\square})=O(n^{5/3})
can be easily obtained via the celebrated dependent random choice method
[
2
,
20
]
.

Very recently, He, Li and Feng
[
22
]
studied the odd prisms, determined
\mathrm{ex}(n,C_{2k+1}^{\square})
for any
k\geq 1
for large
n
and characterized
the extremal graphs. They proposed the following conjecture to break the
5/3
barrier for
2\ell
-prism.

Conjecture 1

(
[
22
]
)
.

For every
\ell\geq 2
, there exists
c=c(\ell)>0
such that
\mathrm{ex}(n,C_{2\ell}^{\square})=O(n^{5/3-c})
.

Our first result provides an optimal upper bound for
C_{2\ell}^{\square}
for every
\ell\geq 4
.

Theorem 1.1
.

For any integer
\ell\geqslant 4
, we have

\mathrm{ex}(n,C_{2\ell}^{\square})=\Theta_{\ell}(n^{3/2}).

We remark that larger prisms are easier to handle. In Subsection
5.1
, we provide a shorter and different proof of
\mathrm{ex}(n,C_{2\ell}^{\square})=O_{\ell}(n^{3/2})
for
\ell\geq 7
, which also yields
\mathrm{ex}(n,C_{6}^{\square})=O(n^{21/13}(\log{n})^{24/13})
. This, together with the known bound for the cube and Theorem
1.1
, confirms Conjecture
1
.

It is worth mentioning that Erdős offered $250 for a proof and $500 for a counterexample of the following conjecture. A graph is
r
-degenerate
if each of its subgraphs has minimum degree at most
r
.

Conjecture 2

(
[
12
]
)
.

Let
H
be a bipartite graph. Then
\mathrm{ex}(n,H)=O(n^{3/2})
if and only if
H
is
2
-degenerate.

This conjecture was recently disproved by Janzer
[
24
]
, who constructed, for each
\varepsilon>0
, a
3
-regular bipartite graph

with girth 6 such that
\mathrm{ex}(n,H)=O(n^{4/3+\varepsilon})
. Theorem
1.1
provides a new family of 3-regular
girth-4
counterexamples to Conjecture
2
.

1.3
The grid

We will also give an improved bound for the extremal number of the grid. For a positive integer
t
,
F_{t,t}
is the graph with vertex set
[t]\times[t]
in which two vertices are joined by an edge if they differ in exactly one coordinate and in that coordinate they differ by one. Bradač, Janzer, Sudakov and Tomon
[
5
]
determined the extremal number of
F_{t,t}
up to a multiplicative constant which depends on
t
, showing that for any
t\geq 2
,

\Omega(t^{1/2}n^{3/2})\leq\mathrm{ex}(n,F_{t,t})\leq e^{O(t^{5})}n^{3/2}.

They have asked to determine the correct dependence on
t
. We make substantial progress on this question by giving a very short proof of the following bound, which shows that the dependence on
t
is polynomial.

Theorem 1.3
.

For any positive integer
t
, if
n
is sufficiently large in terms of
t
, then

\mathrm{ex}(n,F_{t,t})\leq 5t^{3/2}n^{3/2}.

It would be interesting to determine the correct power of
t
in
\mathrm{ex}(n,F_{t,t})
.

For the quadrangulated torus, we provide a general upper bound as follows.

Theorem 1.5
.

For an even integer
k\geq 4
and an integer

, we have

\mathrm{ex}(n,T_{k,\ell})=O_{k,\ell}(n^{\frac{3}{2}+\frac{\ell}{k}}(\log n)^{2}).

Thus, when

is sufficiently large compared to

. On the other hand, the exponent is always strictly greater than

as the probabilistic deletion method (see, e.g, Theorem 2.26 in
[
21
]
) yields the lower bound
\mathrm{ex}(n,T_{k,\ell})=\Omega_{k,\ell}(n^{\frac{3}{2}+\frac{3}{4k\ell-2}})
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
t\in\mathbb{N}
and
C=20t
. Suppose that

Proof.

It suffices to show that
H
has a non-empty subgraph
H^{\prime}
such that for every edge
xy\in E(H^{\prime})
,
y
has at least
2t

z\in V(H^{\prime})
with
d_{H^{\prime}}(x,z)\geq 2t
. Indeed, if such a subgraph exists, then one can embed
P_{t}^{\square}
by greedily attaching
4
-cycles along an edge one by one.

Define a sequence
H=H_{0}\supseteq H_{1}\supseteq H_{2}\supseteq\cdots
of graphs as follows. Having defined
H_{i}
, if there is a vertex
y\in Y
with
1\leq d_{H_{i}}(y)\leq\frac{e(H)}{4|Y|}
, then let
H_{i+1}=H_{i}-y
, and call this deletion type 1. If no such vertex exists but there is an edge

for which
y\in Y
has less than
\frac{e(H)}{8|Y|}
neighbors
z
in
H_{i}
satisfying
d_{H_{i}}(x,z)\geq 2t
, then let
H_{i+1}=H_{i}-e
, and call this deletion type 2. If no such vertex or edge exists, then set
H^{\prime}=H_{i}
and terminate the process.

Now for any
xy\in E(H^{\prime})
with
y\in Y
, it follows immediately from the definition that
y
has at least
\frac{e(H)}{8|Y|}\geq\frac{C}{8}\geq 2t

in
H^{\prime}
such that

. It remains to check that

is non-empty.

We shall prove that in total at most
\frac{e(H)}{4}
edges are deleted by type 1 deletions and at most
\frac{e(H)}{4}
edges are deleted by type 2 deletions. Indeed, there are at most
|Y|
type 1 deletion steps and each of them removes at most
\frac{e(H)}{4|Y|}
edges, so it is clear that at most
\frac{e(H)}{4}
edges are removed during type 1 deletions.

Since in each type 2 deletion step, we remove precisely one edge, it suffices to prove that there are at most
\frac{e(H)}{4}
such deletion steps throughout the process. Assume that the edge
xy
gets deleted from

because
y
has less than
\frac{e(H)}{8|Y|}

in
H_{i}
such that

. Since no deletion of type 1 was applied to

, we have

. It follows that
y
has more than
\frac{e(H)}{8|Y|}
neighbors
z
in
H_{i}
such that
d_{H_{i}}(x,z)<2t
. For each such

is a common neighbor of

. However, the condition

of vertices in

can be involved in at most
2t
such type 2 deletions. Let

be the number of edges deleted in type 2 deletions. Via double counting the number of such triples

Thus, there are at most
\frac{16t|X|^{2}|Y|}{e(H)}

∎

Proposition 3.5

(Finding a quadrangulated torus from a rich collection of cycles)
.

Let

Lemma 3.6
.

Let

Define a sequence of collections of paths of length

in

\mathcal{P}_{0}\supseteq\mathcal{P}_{1}\supseteq\cdots

Claim 3.7
.

There are at most

There are less than

\displaystyle\hom(P_{\ell+1},G^{\prime})\hom(P_{2t-3-\ell},G^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq m\left(\frac{\hom(P_{2t-1},G^{\prime})}{m}\right)^{\ell/(2t-2)}m\left(\frac{\hom(P_{2t-1},G^{\prime})}{m}\right)^{(2t-4-\ell)/(2t-2)}

-good collection of paths

For any

and positive real numbers

and

Now for

, if there exist some

x^{\prime}_{\ell-3},y^{\prime}_{\ell-3},x^{\prime}_{\ell-1},y^{\prime}_{\ell-1}
, the total weight of the possible copies of
P_{3}^{\square}
formed by
x^{\prime}_{\ell-3},y^{\prime}_{\ell-3},x^{\prime}_{\ell-2},y^{\prime}_{\ell-2},x^{\prime}_{\ell-1},y^{\prime}_{\ell-1}
is at most
8\ell
. Overall, we obtain an upper bound
O_{K}(nd^{\ell+1}\cdot(C_{0}d^{1/2})^{2}\cdot(Kd)^{\ell-4}\cdot 8\ell)
, which is indeed of the form
O_{K,\ell}(nd^{2\ell-2})
.
∎

It remains to bound the total weight of those nice homomorphic copies of
C_{2\ell}^{\square}
which have a single degeneracy, at
x^{\prime}_{1}
,
y^{\prime}_{1}
,
x^{\prime}_{\ell-1}
or
y^{\prime}_{\ell-1}
.

Lemma 5.18
.

Let
\ell\geq 4
and let
G
be a
C_{2\ell}^{\square}
-free
n
-vertex graph with average degree
d
and maximum degree at most
Kd
. Then the total weight of nice homomorphic copies of
C_{2\ell}^{\square}
in which for all
1\leq i\leq\ell-1
, we have
x^{\prime}_{i}\neq x_{i}
and
y^{\prime}_{i}\neq y_{i}
except we have precisely one of

(a)

x^{\prime}_{1}=x_{1}
, or

(b)

y^{\prime}_{1}=y_{1}
, or

(c)

x^{\prime}_{\ell-1}=x_{\ell-1}
, or

(d)

y^{\prime}_{\ell-1}=y_{\ell-1}

is
O_{K,\ell}(nd^{2\ell-2})
.

Proof.

We only prove that the total weight of nice homomorphic copies of
C_{2\ell}^{\square}
with
x^{\prime}_{1}=x_{1}
but
x^{\prime}_{i}\neq x_{i}
for all
2\leq i\leq\ell-1
and
y^{\prime}_{i}\neq y_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq\ell-1
is at most
O_{K,\ell}(nd^{2\ell-2})
. (The upper bounds for the cases (b), (c) and (d) can be proven almost identically.)

Consistently with the convention in this section, the weight of a subgraph of a nice homomorphic copy of
C_{2\ell}^{\square}
is the product of the weights of the individual 4-cycles that it contains. With this definition and using Lemma
5.15
(i), the total weight of the possible copies of
P_{\ell}^{\square}
with vertices
x_{0},y_{0},x_{1},y_{1},\dots,x_{\ell-1},y_{\ell-1}
is at most
O_{K}(nd^{\ell})
. By the definition of nice copies of
C_{2\ell}^{\square}
and Lemma
5.15
(iii), given
x^{\prime}_{0}
,
y^{\prime}_{0}
and the vertex
x^{\prime}_{1}=x_{1}
, the total weight of the possible
4
-cycles with vertices
x^{\prime}_{0},y^{\prime}_{0},x^{\prime}_{1},y^{\prime}_{1}
is at most
C_{0}d^{1/2}
. Hence, the total weight of the possible subgraphs induced by the vertices
x_{0},y_{0},\dots,x_{\ell-1},y_{\ell-1},x^{\prime}_{1},y^{\prime}_{1}
is
O_{K,\ell}(nd^{\ell+1/2})
. It is therefore sufficient to prove that the total weight of the possible subgraphs spanned by
x_{\ell-1},y_{\ell-1},x_{\ell},y_{\ell},x^{\prime}_{1},y^{\prime}_{1},x^{\prime}_{2},y^{\prime}_{2},\dots,x^{\prime}_{\ell-1},y^{\prime}_{\ell-1}
is
O_{K,\ell}(d^{\ell-5/2})
for any fixed choices of
x_{\ell-1},y_{\ell-1},x^{\prime}_{1},y^{\prime}_{1}
. Observe that these subgraphs are isomorphic to
P_{\ell+1}^{\square}
(with a non-standard labelling), so by the
C_{2\ell}^{\square}
-freeness assumption, any two such subgraphs which correspond to the same
x_{\ell-1},y_{\ell-1},x^{\prime}_{1},y^{\prime}_{1}
must have another vertex in common (in addition to
x_{\ell-1},y_{\ell-1},x^{\prime}_{1},y^{\prime}_{1}
). This implies that for a given
x_{\ell-1},y_{\ell-1},x^{\prime}_{1},y^{\prime}_{1}
, all possible sets
\{x_{\ell},y_{\ell},x^{\prime}_{2},y^{\prime}_{2},\dots,x^{\prime}_{\ell-1},y^{\prime}_{\ell-1}\}
intersect a fixed set of size at most
2\ell
. However, as
\ell\geq 4
, it is easy to show by
\ell-3
applications of Lemma
5.15
(i) and (ii), one application of Lemma
5.15
(iii), (iv), (v) or (vi) and one application of Lemma
5.14
that for given
x_{\ell-1},y_{\ell-1},x^{\prime}_{1},y^{\prime}_{1}
and given one more vertex from the set
\{x_{\ell},y_{\ell},x^{\prime}_{2},y^{\prime}_{2},\dots,x^{\prime}_{\ell-1},y^{\prime}_{\ell-1}\}
, the total weight of all possible subgraphs spanned by
x_{\ell-1},y_{\ell-1},x_{\ell},y_{\ell},x^{\prime}_{1},y^{\prime}_{1},x^{\prime}_{2},y^{\prime}_{2},\dots,x^{\prime}_{\ell-1},y^{\prime}_{\ell-1}
is
O_{K,\ell}((Kd)^{\ell-3}\cdot C_{0}d^{1/2}\cdot 8\ell)
, which is indeed of the form
O_{K,\ell}(d^{\ell-5/2})
.
∎

Combining Lemmas
5.16
,
5.17
and
5.18
, it follows that for any
\ell\geq 4
, if
G
is a
C_{2\ell}^{\square}
-free
n
-vertex graph with average degree
d
and maximum degree at most

, then the total weight of nice homomorphic copies of
C_{2\ell}^{\square}
is
O_{K,\ell}(nd^{2\ell-2})
. This, combined with Lemma
5.13
, implies the following result.

Lemma 5.19
.

Let
\ell\geq 4
. If
C
is sufficiently large compared to
K
and
\ell
, then any clean, bipartite,

Using Lemmas

6
Concluding remarks

In this paper, we gave optimal bounds for the order of magnitude of the extremal number for several classes of bipartite graphs arising from geometric shapes, including prisms, grids, hexagonal tilings and certain quadrangulations of the cylinder and the torus. Our method can be used to give tight bounds for several other families of graphs as well, some of which we shall briefly discuss here.

Let
R_{s,t}
be the
s\times t
hexagonal grid, the graph obtained from replacing each copy of unit
C_{4}
in the
s\times t
normal grid with a copy of
C_{6}
in the way depicted in Figure
6.1
. Lemma
4.3
implies the following tight upper bound for
\textup{ex}(n,R_{s,t})
.

Theorem 6.1
.

For any integers
s,t\geq 2
, we have

\textup{ex}(n,R_{s,t})=\Theta(n^{4/3}).

As a corollary, we obtain a tight upper bound for the Turán number of the rooted
2
-blowup of a certain balanced rooted tree. Let
T_{s,2s-1}
be the tree obtained from an
s
-vertex path
x_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{s}
by adding
s
new vertices
y_{1},y_{2}\ldots y_{s}
and
s
new edges
x_{1}y_{1},x_{2}y_{2}\ldots,x_{s}y_{s}
. The rooted
\ell
-blowup of
T_{s,2s-1}
, denoted
T_{s,2s-1}^{\ell}
, is obtained by taking
\ell
vertex-disjoint copies of
T_{s,2s-1}
and identifying the
\ell
copies of
y_{i}
for each
1\leq i\leq s
. Balanced rooted trees played a key role in the resolution of the rational exponents conjecture for families
[
9
]
. Later Jiang, Ma and Yepremyan
[
25
]
, and Kang, Kim and Liu
[
27
]
showed that
\textup{ex}(n,T_{3,5}^{\ell})=O(n^{7/5})
and
\textup{ex}(n,T_{4,7}^{\ell})=O(n^{10/7})
, respectively, both of which are tight via the random algebraic method
[
9
]
when
\ell
is very large. As
T_{s,2s-1}^{2}=R_{s,2}
, we have the following tight result for
T_{s,2s-1}^{2}
.

Corollary 6.2
.

For any integer
s\geq 2
, we have

\textup{ex}(n,T_{s,2s-1}^{2})=\Theta(n^{4/3}).

Since
\mathrm{ex}(n,T_{s,2s-1}^{\ell})=\Omega(n^{1+\frac{s-1}{2s-1}})

[
9
]

exhibits a phase transition phenomenon in relation to the number of blown-up copies.

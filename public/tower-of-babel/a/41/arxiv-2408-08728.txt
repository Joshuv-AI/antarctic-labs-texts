Abstract
. In this work we discuss stability and nondegeneracy properties of some special families of minimal hypersurfaces embedded in ℝ m × ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n} with m , n ≥ 2 m,n\geq 2 . These hypersurfaces are asymptotic at infinity to a fixed Lawson cone C m , n C_{m,n} . In the case m + n ≥ 8 m+n\geq 8 , we show that such hypersurfaces are strictly stable and we provide a full classification of their bounded Jacobi fields, which in turn allows us to prove the non-degeneracy of such surfaces. In the case m + n ≤ 7 m+n\leq 7 , we prove that such hypersurfaces have infinite Morse index.



Keywords.
Lawson cone, Morse index, stability, nondegeneracy, jacobi fields.

1.
Introduction

In this work we are interested in stability and nondegeneracy properties of certain minimal hypersurfaces
\Sigma
embedded in
\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
with
N\geq 3
. To make our aims precise let us start with some preliminaries.

In what follows we assume that
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
and we will denote its singular set by
{\rm sing}(\Sigma)
. Assume that
{\rm sing}(\Sigma)
has zero
N-
dimensional Hausdorff measure
and that
\Sigma\setminus{\rm sing}(\Sigma)
is an orientable hypersurface. Let
\nu_{\Sigma}:\Sigma\setminus{\rm sing}(\Sigma)\to S^{N}
denote a choice of the continuous unit normal vector to
\Sigma
. Here
S^{N}
denotes the standard unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
.

These assumptions allow us in particular to consider the Fermi coordinates

(y,z)\mapsto y+z\nu_{\Sigma}(y)

as a smooth diffeomorphism from
\Sigma\setminus{\rm sing}(\Sigma)\times(-\delta,\delta)
onto the open set set

\mathcal{N}:=\big\{y+z\nu_{\Sigma}(y)\,:\,y\in\Sigma\setminus{\rm sing}(\Sigma)\quad\hbox{and}\quad|z|<\delta\big\}.

It is known that the area of
\Sigma
is given by the formula

{\rm Area}(\Sigma):=\int_{\Sigma}d\sigma,

where
d\sigma
is the
N-
dimensional Haussdorff measure. We note that
{\rm Area}(\Sigma)\in[0,\infty]
and that whenever
\Sigma
is smooth,
{\rm Area}(\Sigma)
coincides with the classic definition of area of a manifold with respect to the metric induced by the inner product in
\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
.

Minimality, stability and nondegeneracy properties of
\Sigma
can be understood through appropriate derivatives of
{\rm Area}(\Sigma)
. In order to make these concepts precise we next introduce the notion of normal perturbation of
\Sigma
.

Let
\delta>0
be small and fixed. Consider a function
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Sigma\setminus{\rm sing}(\Sigma))
, a function
f:\Sigma\times(-\delta,\delta)\to\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
such that
f
is smooth in
\big(\Sigma\setminus{\rm sing}(\Sigma)\big)\times(-\delta,\delta)
,
f=f(y,\varepsilon)
and

f(y,0)=0\quad\mbox{and}\quad\partial_{\varepsilon}f(y,0)=\phi(y)\nu_{\Sigma}(y)\quad\mbox{for}\quad y\in\Sigma\setminus{\rm sing}(\Sigma).

For
\varepsilon\in(-\delta,\delta)
the
\varepsilon-
normal variation of
\Sigma
with respect to the function
f
is given by the hypersurface

(1.1)

\Sigma_{\varepsilon}:=\{y+f(y,\varepsilon):\,y\in\Sigma\}\quad\hbox{for}.

Let
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
be an open ball such that
{\rm supp}(\phi)\subset B
and consider the function

(-\delta,\delta)\ni\varepsilon\mapsto{\rm Area}(\Sigma_{\varepsilon}\cap B).

Notice that
{\rm Area}(\Sigma_{\varepsilon}\cap B)\in[0,\infty)
and it is a smooth function. Even more,

{\rm Area}(\Sigma_{\varepsilon}\cap B)=\int_{\Sigma_{\varepsilon}\cap B}d\sigma.

It is well known that the local change of area of
\Sigma
with respect to the formal perturbation
f
is given by

(1.2)

\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}{\rm Area}(\Sigma_{\varepsilon}\cap B)\bigg|_{\varepsilon=0}=-\int_{\Sigma}H_{\Sigma}\phi d\sigma,

where
H_{\Sigma}:=\kappa_{1}+\cdots+\kappa_{N}
is the mean curvature of
\Sigma
and
\kappa_{1},\ldots,\kappa_{N}:\Sigma\setminus{\rm sing}(\Sigma)\to\mathbb{R}
correspond to the principal curvatures of
\Sigma
. We remark that the left-hand side in (
1.2
) does not depend on
B
but only on
\phi
.

The hypersurface
\Sigma
is said to be
minimal
if
H_{\Sigma}\equiv 0
in
\Sigma\backslash{\rm sing}(\Sigma)
or equivalently, since the function
\phi
is arbitrary, if
\Sigma
is a critical point of the Area functional with respect to compactly supported normal variations. Here it is crucial that
\phi
is supported outside the singular set of
\Sigma
and
\Sigma
is smooth outside
{\rm sing}(\Sigma)
.

Assume that
\Sigma
is minimal. In the above notations for the normal variation of
\Sigma
, we introduce the second variation of
{\rm Area}(\Sigma)
, which is given by

(1.3)

\mathcal{Q}_{\Sigma}(\phi):=\frac{d^{2}}{d\varepsilon^{2}}{\rm Area}(\Sigma_{\varepsilon}\cap B)\bigg|_{\varepsilon=0}=\int_{\Sigma}\big(|\nabla_{\Sigma}\phi|^{2}-|A_{\Sigma}|^{2}\phi^{2})\,d\sigma.

where
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
is any open ball such that
{\rm supp}(\phi)\subset B
. We refer the reader to
[
16
]
for the detailed calculations.

Now, observe that
\Sigma
being minimal does not necessarily imply that
\Sigma
minimises the area functional. In order to describe the character of the minimality of
\Sigma
, the second variation of
{\rm Area}(\Sigma)
with respect to the normal variation
\Sigma_{\varepsilon}
plays a crucial role. Hence we introduce the following definition.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
be a minimal hypersurface of codimension one with
{\rm sing}(\Sigma)
having measure zero and such that
\Sigma\setminus{\rm sing}(\Sigma)
is an orientable hypersurface. Assume that
\Sigma
is minimal. We say that

(1)

\Sigma
is stable if for any
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Sigma\setminus{\rm sing}(\Sigma))
,
\mathcal{Q}_{\Sigma}(\phi)\geq 0
;

(2)

\Sigma
is strictly stable if for any
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Sigma\setminus{\rm sing}(\Sigma))\setminus\{0\}
,
\mathcal{Q}_{\Sigma}(\phi)>0
.

It follows from the definitions and Taylor expansions that any minimising hypersurface is stable.

Integrating by parts it is possible to see that

\mathcal{Q}_{\Sigma}(\phi)=\int_{\Sigma}-J_{\Sigma}\phi\,\phi\,d\sigma\qquad\forall\,\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Sigma\setminus{\rm sing}(\Sigma)),

where
J_{\Sigma}:=\Delta_{\Sigma}+|A_{\Sigma}|^{2}
is known as the
Jacobi operator
of
\Sigma
,
\Delta_{\Sigma}
is the Laplace-Beltrami operator of
\Sigma
and
|A_{\Sigma}|^{2}:=\kappa_{1}^{2}+\dots+\kappa_{N}^{2}
is the squared norm of the second fundamental form, or equivalently the sum of the squared principal curvatures of
\Sigma
. A distributional solution
\phi
to the Jacobi equation

(1.4)

J_{\Sigma}\phi=0

is known as a
Jacobi field
of
\Sigma
. We are also interested in Jacobi fields which are not necessarily smooth, for instance unbounded Jacobi fields, even on smooth minimal hypersurfaces (see Section
5
).

As mentioned above, a minimal hypersurface is not necessarily a minimiser of the Area functional. For this reason, it is worth introducing the concept of
Area minimising hypersurfaces
.

We define the perimeter of a measurable subset
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
in an open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
as

(1.5)

{\rm Per}(E,\Omega):=\sup\left\{\int_{E}\text{div}X\,d\xi:\,X\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N+1}),\,|X|\leq 1\right\}.

If
E
has smooth boundary
\partial E
, it follows from the Divergence Theorem that
{\rm Per}(E,\Omega)
coincides with the
N
-dimensional
Hausdorff measure
of
\partial E\cap\Omega
. However, the definition in (
1.5
) allows us to treat the case of sets
E
with non-smooth boundary
\partial E
.

Assuming, without loss of generality, that
0\in\partial E
, we define area minimising hypersurfaces as follows.

Definition 1.2
.

We say that
\Sigma:=\partial E
is an area-minimising (or minimising) hypersurface if for any
\rho>0
and for any smooth set
F\subset\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
such that
F\backslash B_{\rho}(0)=E\backslash B_{\rho}(0)
,

{\rm Per}(E,B_{2\rho}(0))\leq{\rm Per}(F,B_{2\rho}(0)).

In view of Definition
1.2
, Area-minimising hypersurfaces are true minimisers of the Area, so that, if they are regular enough, they are minimal hypersurfaces too, while the converse is not necessarily true.

A hypersurface
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
is said to be a
cone
if there exists
y_{0}\in C
such that

y\in C\text{ if and only if }r(y-y_{0})\in C,\,\forall\,r\geq 0.

Assuming, up to a translation, that
y_{0}=0
, any cone can be written as

C=\{r\theta:\,\theta\in\Gamma,\,r>0\},

where
\Gamma=C\cap S^{N}
is a suitable
(N-1)
-dimensional hypersurface in the
N
-dimensional unit sphere
S^{N}
.

It is known that there are no singular minimising cones of dimension
N\leq 6
(see
[
3
,
10
,
22
]
), while such cones do exist in higher dimension. For instance the Lawson cone

C_{m,n}:=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}:\,(n-1)|x|^{2}=(m-1)|y|^{2}\},\qquad m,n\geq 2

is area minimising provided
m+n\geq 9
or
m+n\geq 8
and
m,n\geq 3
, while
C_{2,6}
and
C_{6,2}
have zero mean curvature everywhere, except at the origin, but they are not area minimising. This result was proved in
[
9
]
using some previous results by
[
7
,
14
,
19
]
.

Let

C:=\{r\theta:\,\theta\in\Gamma,\,r>0\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N+1}

be a cone with zero mean curvature at any non-singular point and let

\lambda_{0}<\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\dots

be the eigenvalues of
-J_{\Gamma}
in
H^{1}(\Gamma)
. It is known that a minimal cone
C
is stable if and only if

(1.6)

\left(\frac{N-2}{2}\right)^{2}+\lambda_{0}\geq 0.

If (
1.6
) is satisfied, we set
\Lambda_{0}:=\sqrt{\left(\frac{N-2}{2}\right)^{2}+\lambda_{0}}
. In particular,
C
is strictly stable if and only if
\Lambda_{0}>0
. (See
[
13
]
and references there in). We will see in Section
2
that
\lambda_{0}<0
.

In this paper we consider minimal hypersurfaces which are asymptotic to some fixed cone
C
.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
13
]
)
.

Let
N\geq 7
. Assume that
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
is a minimising cone. Then there exist exactly two oriented, embedded smooth area minimising hypersurfaces
\Sigma^{\pm}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
such that

(1)

\Sigma^{\pm}
are asymptotic to
C
at infinity and do not intersect
C
.

(2)

dist(\Sigma^{\pm},\{0\})=1
.

Moreover, the Jacobi field
y\cdotp\nu_{\Sigma^{\pm}}(y)
never vanishes.

Assumption (
1.6
) is fulfilled, for instance, by the Lawson cone
C_{m,n}
for
m,n\geq 2,\,N+1=m+n\geq 8
, since
\lambda_{0}=-(N-1)
, hence

\Lambda_{0}=\sqrt{\left(\frac{N-2}{2}\right)^{2}-(N-1)}>0.

Such cones enjoy some specific symmetry
properties. Let us make this statement precise. Given a subgroup
S
of
O(N+1)
, we say that a hypersurfaces
\Sigma
is
S
-invariant if for any
y\in\Sigma
and for any
\rho\in S
, we have
\rho(y)\in\Sigma
. The Lawsons cone are
O(m)\times O(n)
-invariant. Setting

E^{\pm}_{m,n}:=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}:\,\pm\left((m-1)|y|^{2}-(n-1)|x|^{2}\right)>0\},

we have the following result.

Theorem 1.2
.

[
4

Let
m,n\geq 2
,
m+n\geq 8
. Then there exist exactly two smooth minimal hypersurfaces

satisfying that

(1)

\Sigma^{\pm}_{m,n}
are asymptotic to

at infinity.

(2)

{\rm dist}(\Sigma^{\pm}_{m,n},\{0\})=1
;

(3)

\Sigma^{\pm}_{m,n}

.

Each of the hypersurfaces mentioned in Theorems

Next, we discuss a maximum principle and an injectivity result for the Jacobi operator of smooth minimal hypersurfaces which are asymptotic at infinity to a stable cone. These two results hold in particular for the Jacobi operators of the hypersurfaces constructed in Theorems

and

Theorem 1.3
.

The hypersurfaces constructed in Theorem

Then we will consider hypersurfaces which are asymptotic to a fixed Lawson cone in lower dimension, so that condition (

Theorem 1.4

(

Let

(1)

For such hypersurfaces it is relevant to compute the Morse index, defined as

(1.7)

Morse(\Sigma):=\sup\{\dim(X):\,X\text{ subspace of }C^{\infty}_{c}(\Sigma):\,\mathcal{Q}_{\Sigma}(\phi\backslash sing(\Sigma))<0,\,\forall\,\phi\in X\backslash\{0\}\}.

It follows from the definition that
Morse(\Sigma)=0
if and only if
\Sigma
is stable. For our hypersurfaces we have the following result.

Theorem 1.5
.

The hypersurfaces constructed in Theorem
1.4
have infinite Morse index.

Theorem
1.5
shows that these hypersurfaces are far from being stable. Roughly speaking, the stability properties of the cone are preserved when passing to the asymptotic smooth minimal hypersurface.

Moreover, we are interested in classifying the bounded Jacobi fields of our hypersurfaces, that is bounded solutions to the Jacobi equation (
1.4
). Since the zero mean curvature equation is invariant under translations, rotations and dilations, if we consider a normal variation such that, for any
\varepsilon\in(-\delta,\delta)
,
\Sigma_{\varepsilon}
is a translated, rotated or dilated copy of
\Sigma
, we have
H_{\Sigma_{\varepsilon}}=0
for any
\varepsilon\in(-\delta,\delta)
. Keeping the above notations and differentiating in
\varepsilon
we have

0=\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\bigg|_{\varepsilon=0}H_{\Sigma_{\varepsilon}}=J_{\Sigma}\phi

(see
[
16
]
), thus there are at most
2N+3
linearly independent Jacobi fields coming from such geometric transformations. These Jacobi fields and their linear combinations are called
geometric Jacobi fields
and the vector space of such Jacobi fields will be denoted by
G(\Sigma)
. We note that the dimension of
G(\Sigma)
can be strictly less than
2N+3
in case
\Sigma
enjoys some symmetry properties. The Jacobi fields coming from rotations constitute a subspace of dimension at most
N+1
and are unbounded, so that the space of bounded geometric Jacobi fields has dimension at most
N+2
. In fact, it is generated by the Jacobi fields
\{\nu_{\Sigma}(y)\cdotp e_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq N+1}
associated to translations and by the Jacobi field
y\cdotp\nu_{\Sigma}(y)
associated to dilation provided it is bounded.

Definition 1.3
.

A minimal hypersurface
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
is said to be nondegenerate if all its bounded Jacobi fields are geometric.

This definition was given, for instance, in
[
11
]
for the Costa-Hofmann-Meeks surfaces.

Since we are often interested in hypersurfaces enjoying some symmetry properties, we introduce the concept of
S
-nondegenerate hypersurface.

Definition 1.4
.

Let
S
be a subgroup of
O(N+1)
and let
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
be an
S
-invariant minimal hypersurface.

•

We say that a function
\phi:\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}
is
S
-invariant if
\phi\circ\rho=\phi
, for any
\rho\in S
.

•

We say that
\Sigma
is
S
-nondegenerate if all its bounded
S
-invariant Jacobi fields are geometric.

We note that nondegeneracy implies
S
-nondegeneracy, while the converse is not necessarily true.

We have nondegeneracy results for our hypersurfaces.

Theorem 1.6
.

(1)

The hypersurfaces constructed in Theorem
1.2
are nondegenerate.

(2)

The hypersurface
\Sigma_{m,n}
constructed in Theorem

is

-nondegenerate,
for any
m,n\geq 2
,
m+n\leq 7
.

We will actually prove more. We will see that the bounded Kernel of the Jacobi operator of

exactly has dimension

if

is one of the hypersurfaces constructed in Theorem

Preliminaries and notations

In this paper we say that a hypersurfaces
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
is asymptotic to a cone

C:=\{r\theta:\,\theta\in\Gamma,\,r>0\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N+1}

if, outside a compact set,
\Sigma
is the normal graph of a smooth decaying function over
C
. More precisely, we introduce the following Definition.

Definition 2.1
.

We say that
\Sigma
is asymptotic to
C
if there exists
R>0
such that

\Sigma=K_{R}\cup\Sigma_{R},

where

(2.1)

\Sigma_{R}:=\{r\theta+w(r,\theta)\nu_{C}(\theta):\,\theta\in\Gamma,\,r>R\},

\nu_{C}(\theta)=\nu_{C}(r\theta)
is a choice of normal vector to
C
, which is actually independent of
r
due to homogeneity,
w:(R,\infty)\times\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}
is a smooth function decaying as
r\to\infty
, uniformly in
\theta\in\Gamma
, and
K_{R}:=\Sigma\backslash\Sigma_{R}
is compact.

In
[
2
,
20
]
the authors give a more abstract definition of hypersurface asymptotic to a cone. However, they actually show that the two definitions are equivalent. See
[
13
]
page 105 and the citations there.

In case
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
fulfils (
1.6
), or equivalently
C
is stable, the decay rate of
w
is discussed in Theorem
4.2
of
[
18
]
. More precisely, we have the following result.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
18
]
)
.

Let
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
be a stable cone. Let
\Sigma
be a minimal hypersurface which, outside a compact set, is the normal graph over
C
of a smooth function
w:(R,\infty)\times\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}
which never vanishes and
w(r,\theta)\to 0
as
r\to\infty
, uniformly in
\theta\in\Gamma
. Then, in the above notations, we have

(2.2)

w(r,\theta)=(b\log r+a)r^{-\frac{N-2}{2}+\Lambda_{0}}(1+O(r^{-\delta}))

or

(2.3)

w(r,\theta)=ar^{-\frac{N-2}{2}-\Lambda_{0}}(1+O(r^{-\delta}))

as
r\to\infty
uniformly in
\theta\in\Gamma
, for some
\delta>0
, where
a\neq 0
and
b=0
unless
\Lambda_{0}=0
.These relations can be differentiated.

Theorem
2.1
applies to the hypersurfaces constructed in Theorem
1.1
if (
1.6
) holds and to the hypersurfaces constructed in Theorem

, since the Lawson cone
C_{m,n}
fulfils (

,
m+n\geq 8
.

Remark 2.1
.

If
\Sigma
is one of the hypersurfaces constructed in Theorem (
1.2
), then Theorem (
2.1
) holds with
b=0
, because

\left(\frac{N-2}{2}\right)^{2}+\lambda_{0}=\left(\frac{N-2}{2}\right)^{2}-(N-1)>0

since
N\geq 7
.

The next Lemma shows that the result of Theorem
2.1
is consistent with the assumptions, that is the function
w
is actually decaying at infinity.

Lemma 2.1
.

If (
1.6
) holds, then in the above notations we have
-\frac{N-2}{2}+\Lambda_{0}<0
.

Proof.

It is enough to prove that
\lambda_{0}<0
. In fact, using the variational characterisation

\lambda_{0}=\inf\left\{\int_{\Gamma}-J_{\Gamma}\phi\,\phi\,d\tilde{\sigma}:\,\phi\in H^{1}(\Gamma),\,\int_{\Gamma}\phi^{2}\,d\tilde{\sigma}=1\right\},

and taking the constant function
\phi=|\Gamma|^{-1/2}\in H^{1}(\Gamma)
, where
|\Gamma|
is the
(N-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure of
\Gamma
, so that
\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}=1
, we can see that

\lambda_{0}\leq-\int_{\Gamma}\frac{|A_{\Gamma}|^{2}}{|\Gamma|}d\sigma\leq 0.

More precisely, we have
\lambda_{0}<0
, since
|A_{\Gamma}|^{2}\neq 0
. In fact, if we assume by contradiction that
|A_{\Gamma}|^{2}\equiv 0
, all the principal curvatures
\kappa_{i}
of
\Gamma
are identically zero. This yields that the differential of the Gauss map vanishes identically, so that the normal vector field is constant. This implies that
\Gamma
is contained in the intersection between
S^{N}
and a hyperplane of dimension
N-1
, that is a sphere of dimension
N-2
, which is impossible since
\Gamma
has dimension
N-1
.
∎

Proof.

Multiplying by

\displaystyle|y|^{\frac{N+2}{2}}J_{\Sigma}(|y|^{-\frac{N-2}{2}}u)

\displaystyle=|y|^{2}(\Delta_{\Sigma}u+|A_{\Sigma}|^{2}u)+2|y|^{\frac{N+2}{2}}\nabla_{\Sigma}(|y|^{-\frac{N-2}{2}})\cdotp\nabla_{\Sigma}u+|y|^{\frac{N+2}{2}}\Delta_{\Sigma}(|y|^{-\frac{N-2}{2}})u=

\displaystyle=|y|^{2}(\Delta_{\Sigma}u+|A_{\Sigma}|^{2}u)+(2-N)|y|\nabla_{\Sigma}|y|\cdotp\nabla_{\Sigma}u+|y|^{\frac{N+2}{2}}\Delta_{\Sigma}(|y|^{-\frac{N-2}{2}})u,

On the other hand, it is possible to see that

4.
Stability properties

In this section we will deal with
O(m)\times O(n)
-invariant hypersurfaces. More precisely, we take
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
m,n\geq 2
and such that
N+1=m+n\geq 8
and we assume that
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
is one of the hypersurfaces constructed in Theorem
1.2
.

We consider the space

(4.1)

X:=\left\{\phi\in H^{1}_{loc}(\Sigma):\,|\nabla_{\Sigma}\phi|\in L^{2}(\Sigma),\,|y|^{-1}\phi\in L^{2}(\Sigma)\right\}.

Note that the function
y\in\Sigma\mapsto|y|^{-1}\in(0,\infty)
is smooth and bounded, since
dist(\Sigma,\{0\})=1
, and
|A_{\Sigma}|\phi\in L^{2}(\Sigma)
, for any
\phi\in X
, because
|A_{\Sigma}|^{2}\leq c|y|^{-2}
.

In this section, we prove the following statement.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\Sigma
be one of the minimal hypersurfaces constructed in Theorem
1.2
. Then there exists
\lambda>0
such that

\int_{\Sigma}\big(|\nabla_{\Sigma}\phi|^{2}-|A_{\Sigma}|^{2}\phi^{2}\big)d\sigma\geq\lambda\int_{\Sigma}|A_{\Sigma}|^{2}\phi^{2}d\sigma\qquad\forall\,\phi\in X.

In particular, Proposition
4.1
proves Theorem
1.3
.

Remark
3.3
point
(3)
of
[
13
]
asserts that if
\Sigma
is strictly stable and the asymptotic cone
C
is minimizing, then
C
is strictly stable too. Here we prove that the opposite implication is also true, at least in the case of the Lawson cones, without any minimality assumption about the cone.

Proof.

Considering the function
u:\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}
defined by
\phi(y)=|y|^{-\frac{N-2}{2}}u(y)
and setting
H:=\mathcal{W}^{1,2}_{0}(\Sigma,|y|^{-N})
, the statement is equivalent to prove that

\int_{\Sigma}(|\nabla_{\Sigma}u|^{2}|y|^{2}-Vu^{2})|y|^{-N}d\sigma\geq\mu\int_{\Sigma}u^{2}|y|^{-N}d\sigma\qquad\forall\,u\in H,

for some
\mu>0
, where
V:=|y|^{2}|A_{\Sigma}|^{2}+|y|^{\frac{N+2}{2}}\Delta_{\Sigma}(|y|^{-\frac{N-2}{2}})
.

In other words, we need to prove that

\mu:=\inf\left\{\int_{\Sigma}(|\nabla_{\Sigma}u|^{2}|y|^{2}-Vu^{2})|y|^{-N}d\sigma:\,u\in H,\,\int_{\Sigma}u^{2}|y|^{-N}d\sigma=1\right\}>0.

We already know that
\mu\geq 0
, due to the stability of
\Sigma
. It remains to exclude the case
\mu=0
. If we assume by contradiction that
\mu=0
, we can find a sequence
u_{k}\in H
such that
\|u_{k}|y|^{-\frac{N}{2}}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}=1
and

\int_{\Sigma}(|\nabla_{\Sigma}u_{k}|^{2}|y|^{2}-Vu_{k}^{2})|y|^{-N}d\sigma\to 0.

As a consequence
u_{k}
is bounded in
H
, so that it admits a subsequence converging weakly in
H
and strongly in
L^{2}_{loc}(\Sigma)
to some function
u\in H
, due to Rellich-Kondrakov’s Theorem. If we assume that there exist
R,\,\delta,\,k_{0}>0
such that, for any
k\geq k_{0}
, we have

(4.2)

\int_{K_{R}}u_{k}^{2}|y|^{-N}d\sigma>\delta>0,

we get that
u\neq 0
. Moreover, since
\Sigma
is
O(m)\times O(n)
-invariant,
|A_{\Gamma}|^{2}=N-1
is explicit, so that there exists
\bar{R}\geq R
such that

(4.3)

V<-\frac{1}{2}\left(\left(\frac{N-2}{2}\right)^{2}-(N-1)\right)<0\qquad\text{on $\Sigma_{\bar{R}}$,}

so that

\int_{\Sigma_{\bar{R}}}-Vu^{2}|y|^{-N}d\sigma\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\int_{\Sigma_{\bar{R}}}-Vu_{k}^{2}|y|^{-N}d\sigma

by the Fatou Lemma. As a consequence, using the strong convergence in

and the weak lower semicontinuity of the seminorm

v\in H\mapsto\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla_{\Sigma}v|^{2}|y|^{2-N}d\sigma,

and the strong convergence in

, we get

0\leq\int_{\Sigma}(|\nabla_{\Sigma}u|^{2}|y|^{2}-Vu^{2})|y|^{-N}d\sigma\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\int_{\Sigma}(|\nabla_{\Sigma}u_{k}|^{2}|y|^{2}-Vu_{k}^{2})|y|^{-N}d\sigma=\mu=0,

which yields that

0=\int_{\Sigma}-\mathcal{L}u\,u|y|^{-N}d\sigma=\inf\left\{\int_{\Sigma}-\mathcal{L}v\,v|y|^{-N}d\sigma:\,v\in H\right\},

since
\Sigma
is stable, which yields that
\mathcal{L}u=0
. Moreover, since
u\in L^{2}_{0}(\Sigma,|y|^{-N})
, Proposition
3.1
(
\Lambda_{0}>0
and
\delta=0
) yields that
u=0
, a contradiction since

\int_{K_{R}}u^{2}|y|^{-N}d\sigma\geq\delta>0.

Therefore (
4.2
) is false, so that there exists a sequence

fulfilling

\int_{\Sigma_{j}}u_{k_{j}}^{2}|y|^{-N}d\sigma\geq 1-\frac{1}{j}.

As a consequence, choosing

, with

as in (

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}(|\nabla_{\Sigma}u_{k}|^{2}|y|^{2}-Vu_{k}^{2})|y|^{-N}d\sigma\geq\int_{\Sigma}-Vu_{k}^{2}|y|^{-N}d\sigma

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma_{j}}-Vu_{k}^{2}|y|^{-N}d\sigma+\int_{K_{j}}-Vu_{k}^{2}|y|^{-N}d\sigma\geq c+o(1),

for some constant

∎

In the proof of Proposition

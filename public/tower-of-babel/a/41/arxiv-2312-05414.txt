Fisher Zeros and its Critical Behavior for the Ising Model on Sierpinski Gasket
Shaosong Liu

Abstract
In this paper, we provide a proof of the explicit formula for the partition function of the Ising model on the Sierpinski gasket. Additionally, we demonstrate the dynamic behavior of the zero distribution of the partition function when a sequence of appropriate regions is selected. Through this analysis, it is shown that the density of zeros around temperature 0 0 exponentially diminishes at a rate of 1 3 n \frac{1}{3^{n}} , and the pressure function approaches the logarithmic function.



1
Introduction

1.1
Background

The Ising model emerges as a powerful tool in characterizing magnetic materials, unraveling intricate physical phenomena such as spontaneous magnetization and phase transitions. In their seminal work, Lee and Yang [1] first proposed their fundamental approach to phase transitions, which involves studying the zeros of the partition function of a statistical system as a function of a complex parameter. For finite graphs, the partition function has positive coefficients so there are no real zeros. However, in the thermodynamic limit, the zeros have the potential to pinch the real axis. The pinching points represent phase transition locations on the parameter axis, and the distribution of zeros around them can be linked to the critical properties of the system.

1.2
Ising Model

Assume we have a graph
\mathcal{G}
representing a magnetic matter in a certain scale. The Ising model is to associate a spin variable
\sigma(v)=\pm 1
to every vertex
v
of the graph. Let
\mathcal{V}
be the set of all vertices in
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{E}
be the set of all edges. In this context, for
v,w\in\mathcal{V}
, we use
<v,w>\in\mathcal{E}
to denote the oriented edge. The map
\sigma:\mathcal{V}\to\left\{\pm 1\right\}
represents a spin configuration on
\mathcal{G}
.

This paper focuses on the ferromagnetic model, where the total energy or Hamiltonian of
\sigma
is given by

H(\sigma):=-J\sum_{<v,w>\in\mathcal{E}}\sigma(v)\sigma(w)

where
J
is a positive constant.

The Gibbs weight of a spin configuration
\sigma
is defined as

W(\sigma):=e^{-H(\sigma)/T}

where
T>0
denotes the temperature.

To define the partition function, we introduce the configuration space
\Conf(\mathcal{G})
. It is the space of all spin configurations on
\mathcal{G}
. The partition function is the total Gibbs weight of the space:

Z_{\mathcal{G}}:=\sum_{\sigma\in\Conf(\mathcal{G})}W(\sigma).

1.3
Sierpinski Gasket

Now we introduce our the Sierpinski gasket. It is a fractal graph built recursively. Initially we have a triangle
\mathcal{G}_{0}
with three vertices and three edges. Then for
n
th stage
\mathcal{G}_{n}
, it is built by joining three copies of
\mathcal{G}_{n-1}
at their external corners(figure 1). As
n\to\infty
, we obtain our hierachical gasket.

Figure 1:
Gasket’s construction

In the following, we write
W_{n}
and
Z_{n}
representing the Gibbs weight and partition function of
\mathcal{G}_{n}
respectively in terms of the variable

\displaystyle y:=e^{\frac{J}{T}}.

The pressure function is the limit of the logarithm of partition functions

\displaystyle p:=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{4\cdot 3^{n}}\log|Z_{n}|.

(1.1)

When
p
blows up near some tempature
T
, that is where the phase transition occurs.

1.4
Main results

Our goal is to illustrate the pressure function for Sierpinski gasket with Ising model. In [2], they show such partition functions satisfy a recurrence relation in terms of a certain multivalued map. Here we introduce a sequence of integer polynomials
\left\{T_{n}\right\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
defined recursively in terms of a quadratic polynomial, from which we can factorize partition functions. In particular, we avoid multivalued maps entirely in our approach.

\displaystyle=(g^{\circ(n-1)}(g(z))+1)(g^{0}(z)+2)^{3^{n-1}}\prod_{j=0}^{n-2}(g^{\circ j}(g(z))+2)^{3^{n-j-2}}

\displaystyle=(g^{\circ n}(z)+1)\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}(g^{\circ j}(z)+2)^{3^{n-j-1}}.

∎

At the end, we show properties for coefficients of

Proof of Corollary

In step 2 we distribute

The polynomial

2

Recall from (

\displaystyle\zeta_{n}=\frac{1}{4\cdot 3^{n}}\Delta\log|Z_{n}|.

In this section we prove Theorem

For later use, here we also introduce the normalized Laplacian associated to

\displaystyle\mu_{n}:=\frac{1}{3^{n}}\Delta\log|M_{n}|.

(4.1)

and its property:

Corollary 4.1

\left\{\mu_{n}\right\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
converges in the norm of total variation and its limit

\displaystyle\mu_{\infty}:=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{3^{j+1}}\left(\sum_{\omega\in f^{-j}(-1)}\delta(\omega)\right)-\delta(\infty).

For the beginning, we need to clarify the multiplicities for all distinct roots of

, which is helpful in the proof of

Lemma 4.2
.

1.

The polynomial
g^{\circ n}(z)+1
, as well as polynomials
g^{\circ j}(z)+2
for
m\in\left\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\right\}
only have simple roots.

2.

Every pair of distinct polynomials from item 1 have no common roots.

Proof.

For
g^{\circ j}(z)+2=0
with
j\in\left\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\right\}
, if it had a multiple root
z_{1}\in\mathbb{C}
, its derivative would vanish at
z_{1}
. By the chain rule, we compute the derivative as
\left(g^{\circ j}(z)+2\right)^{\prime}=\prod_{k=0}^{j-1}g^{\prime}(g^{k}(z))
. Thus, at least one factor must be zero at
z_{1}
. We know
g^{\prime}(z)=2z+1
has a single root at
-\frac{1}{2}
. In other words,
g^{k}(z_{1})=-\frac{1}{2}
for at least one
k\in\left\{0,1,\ldots,j-1\right\}
. Notice the inequality
z<z(z+1)=g(z)<0
for
-1<z<0
; by induction, we can prove
\left\{g^{j}(-\frac{1}{2})\right\}_{j=0}^{j-k}
is an increasing sequence with negative terms starting from
-\frac{1}{2}
. This implies we can’t have
g^{\circ j}(z_{1})=g^{j-k}(-\frac{1}{2})=-2
. In other words,
g^{\circ j}(z)+2=0
has only simple roots for
j\in\left\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\right\}
. Since
-1<-\frac{1}{2}
, the same argument holds if
2
is replaced by
1
. Therefore,
g^{\circ n}(z)+1
has no repeated roots either.

Now, consider Item 2. Assume
g^{\circ j_{1}}(z_{2})=g^{\circ j_{2}}(z_{2})=-2
for some
j_{1}<j_{2}<n\in\mathbb{N}
and
z_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
. We compute
g^{\circ(j_{1}+1)}(z_{2})=g(-2)=2
and all the future iterations give positive results, excluding
-2
. This means
g^{\circ j_{2}}(z_{2})
can’t be
-2
; the assumption is wrong, so there are no common roots between
g^{\circ j_{1}}(z)+2=0
and
g^{\circ j_{2}}(z)+2=0
. Notice that we can’t get
-1
either from
g^{\circ j}(z_{2})=-2
. Therefore,
g^{\circ n}(z)+1=0
has no common roots with
g^{\circ j}(z)+2=0
for any integer
j
between
1
and
n-1
.
∎

Now we can show the convergence of
\zeta_{n}
.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

At first, we define:

\displaystyle\tau_{n}:=\frac{1}{3^{n}}\Delta\log|T_{n}|.

Then we can also express

In here

counting multiplicities.

To understand the convergence of

, we need to focus on the distribution of zeros for

.From the factorization of

and Lemma
4.2
, we divide its zeros into different groups and rewrite

\tau_{n}=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\frac{1}{3^{j+1}}\left(\sum_{\omega\in g^{-j}(-2)}\delta(\omega)\right)+\frac{1}{3^{n}}\sum_{\omega\in g^{-n}(-1)}\delta(\omega)-\delta(\infty).

Then we use
\phi\circ q
and the connection between

and

to pull-back

to

. Through (

) and (

\displaystyle\zeta_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\cdot 3^{n}}\Delta\log|Z_{n}|

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4}\left(\delta(0)-\delta(\infty)\right)-\frac{1}{4}(\phi\circ q)^{*}(\delta(0)-\delta(\infty))+\frac{1}{4}(\phi\circ q)^{*}\tau_{n}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4}\left(\delta(0)-\delta(\infty)\right)-\frac{1}{4}(\phi\circ q)^{*}(\delta(0)-\delta(\infty))

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ +\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\frac{1}{4\cdot 3^{j+1}}\left(\sum_{\omega\in q^{-1}\circ f^{-j}(-1)}\delta(\omega)\right)+\frac{1}{4\cdot 3^{n}}\sum_{\omega\in q^{-1}\circ f^{-n}(-3)}\delta(\omega)-\frac{1}{4}(\phi\circ q)^{*}\delta(\infty)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4}\delta(0)-\frac{3}{4}\delta(\infty)+\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\frac{1}{4\cdot 3^{j+1}}\left(\sum_{\omega\in q^{-1}\circ f^{-j}(-1)}\delta(\omega)\right)+\frac{1}{4\cdot 3^{n}}\sum_{\omega\in q^{-1}\circ f^{-n}(-3)}\delta(\omega).

(4.2)

In the sense of total variation, for any

\displaystyle|\zeta_{n}-\zeta_{\infty}|

\displaystyle=\left|-\frac{1}{12}\sum_{j=n}^{\infty}\frac{1}{3^{j}}\left(\sum_{\omega\in q^{-1}\circ f^{-j}(-1)}\delta(\omega)\right)+\frac{1}{4\cdot 3^{n}}\sum_{\omega\in q^{-1}\circ f^{-n}(-3)}\delta(\omega)\right|

It goes to zero when

∎

With (

7
The Pressure Function

For the last section, we compute the pressure function
p
.

First, we introduce

\displaystyle m:=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{3^{n}}\log|M_{n}|,

the pressure-like function corresponding to
M_{n}
. With Corollary
4.1
, we have

\displaystyle m=\int_{\mathbb{C}}\log|y-t|d\mu_{\infty}(t).

Using the partition of
\supp(\mu_{\infty})
in (
6.9
), we compute

\displaystyle m(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathfrak{R}_{n}}\log|x-t|d\mu_{\infty}(t).

Then

\displaystyle|m(x)-\log x|=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathfrak{R}_{n}}\log\left|1-\frac{t}{x}\right|d\mu_{\infty}(t).

Before further computation, we state some properties of
\mathfrak{R}_{n}
. Since
-1\in\mathfrak{R}_{0}
with weight
\frac{1}{3}
, we have

\displaystyle\mu_{\infty}(\mathfrak{R}_{0})>\frac{1}{3}.

Then

\displaystyle\mu_{\infty}(\cup_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathfrak{R}_{n})<\frac{2}{3}

In the mean time, from the fact that
h(\mathfrak{R}_{n})=\mathfrak{R}_{n+1}
, we get

\displaystyle\mu_{\infty}(\mathfrak{R}_{n})=3\mu_{\infty}(\mathfrak{R}_{n+1}).

So the estimate

\displaystyle\mu_{\infty}(\mathfrak{R}_{n})<\frac{4}{3^{n+2}}

holds for every
n\in\mathbb{N^{+}}
.

For any small
0<\epsilon<1
, we pick
N^{\prime}>0
so that

\displaystyle\frac{3N^{\prime}}{\epsilon}>10\text{ and }\frac{4}{3^{\frac{3N^{\prime}}{5\epsilon}+2}}<\frac{\epsilon}{6\log|Q|}

hold. Then for any
x>\frac{3N^{\prime}}{\epsilon^{2}}
, we seperate
\supp(\mu_{\infty})
into four parts:

\displaystyle\supp(\mu_{\infty})_{1}

\displaystyle:=\supp(\mu_{\infty})\cap\left\{t\in\mathbb{C}|\Re(t)<x\cdot\epsilon\right\}

\displaystyle\supp(\mu_{\infty})_{2}

\displaystyle:=\supp(\mu_{\infty})\cap\left\{t\in\mathbb{C}|x\cdot\epsilon\leq\Re(t)<x-1\right\}

\displaystyle\supp(\mu_{\infty})_{3}

\displaystyle:=\supp(\mu_{\infty})\cap\left\{t\in\mathbb{C}|x-1\leq\Re(t)<x+1\right\}

\displaystyle\supp(\mu_{\infty})_{4}

\displaystyle:=\supp(\mu_{\infty})\cap\left\{t\in\mathbb{C}|x+1\leq\Re(t)\cdot\epsilon\right\}

For the first set, we estimate the integral

\displaystyle\int_{\supp(\mu_{\infty})_{1}}\log\left|1-\frac{t}{x}\right|d\mu_{\infty}(t)<\log\left|1-\frac{\epsilon}{3}\right|<\frac{\epsilon}{3}.

For
\mathfrak{R}_{n}
contained in
\supp(\mu_{\infty})_{2}
and
\supp(\mu_{\infty})_{4}
, we know

\displaystyle\frac{\log|x-t-4|-\log|x|}{\log|x-t|-\log|x|}<\frac{t+4}{t}<\frac{3}{2}

So for
\mathfrak{R}_{n}
contained in
\supp(\mu_{\infty})_{2}
and
\supp(\mu_{\infty})_{4}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathfrak{R}_{n}}\log\left|1-\frac{t}{x}\right|d\mu_{\infty}(t)<\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathfrak{R}_{n+1}}\log\left|1-\frac{t}{x}\right|d\mu_{\infty}(t).

For such
\mathfrak{R}_{n}
, the index
n
must be bigger than
\frac{3N^{\prime}}{5\epsilon}
so for all such
\mathfrak{R}_{n}
, their weight is controled by
\frac{4}{3^{\frac{3N^{\prime}}{5\epsilon}+2}}
. The integral on those two sets can be controled by

\displaystyle\int_{\supp(\mu_{\infty})_{2}}\log\left|1-\frac{t}{x}\right|d\mu_{\infty}(t)+\int_{\supp(\mu_{\infty})_{4}}\log\left|1-\frac{t}{x}\right|d\mu_{\infty}(t)<2\log|Q|\frac{4}{3^{\frac{3N^{\prime}}{5\epsilon}+2}}<\frac{\epsilon}{3}.

For
\mathfrak{R}_{n}
contained in
\supp(\mu_{\infty})_{3}
, the number of such
\mathfrak{R}_{n}
is no greater than
1
. The weight must be smaller than
\frac{4}{3^{\frac{3N^{\prime}}{5\epsilon}+2}}
. Then the integral on the third set can be estimated like

\displaystyle\int_{\supp(\mu_{\infty})_{3}}\log\left|1-\frac{t}{x}\right|d\mu_{\infty}(t)<\frac{4}{3^{\frac{3N^{\prime}}{5\epsilon}+2}}\log|Q|<\frac{\epsilon}{3}.

Combining all estimates we get

\displaystyle|m(x)-\log x|<\epsilon.

Then we are prepared to control the pressure function
p
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

By definition of
p
and (
), for
y\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
we get

\displaystyle p(y)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{4\cdot 3^{n}}\log|Z_{n}(y)|

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{4\cdot 3^{n}}\log\left|\frac{2M_{n}(y^{4})}{y^{3^{n}}}\right|

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{4\cdot 3^{n}}\log|2M_{n}(y^{4})|-\frac{1}{4}\log|y|

\displaystyle=m(y)-\frac{1}{4}\log|y|.

From the previous discussion we know
m(y)
approaches to
\log y
when
y
goes to
\infty
, so

\displaystyle\lim_{y\to\infty}\left|p(y)-\frac{3}{4}\log y\right|=0

∎

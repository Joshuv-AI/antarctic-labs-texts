Quantum dynamics of gravitational massive shell
Andrzej Góźdź, Marcin Kisielowski, Włodzimierz Piechocki

Abstract
The quantum dynamics of a self-gravitating thin matter shell in vacuum has been considered. Quantum Hamiltonian of the system is positive definite. Within chosen set of parameters, the quantum shell bounces above the horizon. Considered quantum system does not collapse to the gravitational singularity of the corresponding classical system.



I
Introduction

The dynamics of a self-gravitating thin matter shell is one of the simplest models describing gravitational
collapse of an isolated gravitational system. In the case of a spherically symmetric shell in vacuum,
a satisfactory Hamiltonian description of that dynamics has been found; see the paper
KMM
and
references therein. By shell in vacuum one means a thin matter shell with a region of flat Minkowski
space in the interior and the Schwarzschild geometry in the exterior of the shell.
The global Hamiltonian of that system is explicitly time independent and is a function
of two canonically conjugated phase space variables. That Hamiltonian is equal to the Arnowit-Deser-Misner (ADM)
mass at spacial infinity. Having well defined Hamiltonian description of matter shell, we have decided to quantize
that system to get insight into corresponding quantum dynamics. Present paper is devoted to the examination of such
an issue.

The shell system is simple enough to be treated satisfactory at classical level, and rich enough for the examination
of various aspects of corresponding quantum system. Recently, it was used for addressing the issue of the importance
of the choice of time parameter at quantum level
Vaz
. It was shown that quantum theories of the shell for
different choices of time are not unitarily equivalent.

We have found that our quantum Hamiltonian is positive definite which supports its classical property.
Within chosen set of parameters describing our system, the quantum shell bounces above the horizon that is in contrast
to the classical case. It means that due to quantum effects our quantum system does not collapse to gravitational
singularity.

The paper is organized as follows:
In Sec. II we present the solution to Hamilton’s dynamics restricting considerations to the subspace
of phase space for which the Hamiltonian is positive definite. In Sec. III we recall the coherent states
quantization method applied in this paper. Sec. IV concerns the calculations of the matrix elements of quantum
observables in specific basis of considered Hilbert space. That includes the operator of Hamiltonian and
operators of canonical variables. The quantum evolution of the system is presented in Sec. V. We conclude
in Sec. VI.

In the following we choose
\;G=c=1=\hbar\;
except where otherwise noted.

III
Quantum level

The affine coherent states quantization applied in this article is based on the
formalism presented in our papers
Gozdz:2018aai
and
AWT
.

The physical phase space of our gravitational system

\Pi:=\{(p,q)\;|\;p\in\mathbb{R},q\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\}\,,

(11)

can be identified with the affine group
\mathrm{G}\equiv\mathrm{Aff}(\mathbb{R})
, by defining the multiplication law as follows

(p^{\prime},q^{\prime})\cdot(p,q):=(q^{\prime}p+p^{\prime},q^{\prime}q)\,,

(12)

with the unity
(0,1)
and the inverse

(p^{\prime},q^{\prime})^{-1}=(-\frac{p^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}},\frac{1}{q^{\prime}})\,.

(13)

III.1
Affine coherent states

The affine group has two, nontrivial, inequivalent irreducible unitary
representations
Gel
;
AK1
;
AK2
. Both are realized in the Hilbert space

\mathcal{H}=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},d\nu(x))
, where
d\nu(x):=dx/x
is an invariant
measure on the multiplicative group
(\mathbb{R}_{+},\cdot)
.
In what follows we choose the one defined by the following action

U(p,q)\psi(x)=e^{ipx}\psi(qx)\,,

(14)

where
1
1

1

We use Dirac’s notation whenever we wish to deal with abstract
vector, instead of functional representation of the vector.

\psi(x)=\langle x|\psi\rangle
and
|\psi\rangle\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},d\nu(x))
.

We define the integrals over the affine group

\mathrm{G}=\mathrm{Aff}(\mathbb{R})
as follows

\int_{\mathrm{G}}d\mu(p,q)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dp\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dq}{q^{2}}\,.

(15)

Fixing the normalized vector
|\Phi\rangle\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},d\nu(x))
, called the
fiducial vector, we can define a continuous family of affine
coherent states
|p,q\rangle\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},d\nu(x))
as follows

|p,q\rangle=U(p,q)|\Phi\rangle.

(16)

The irreducibility of the representation, used to define the coherent
states (
16
), enables making use of Schur’s lemma
BR
, which leads
to the resolution of the unity in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},d\nu(x))

\frac{1}{A_{\Phi}}\int_{\mathrm{G}}d\mu(p,q)|p,q\rangle\langle p,q|=\mathbb{I}\;,

(17)

where the constant
A_{\Phi}
can be determined by using any arbitrary, normalized
vector
|f\rangle\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},d\nu(x))
as follows

A_{\Phi}=\int_{\mathrm{G}}d\mu(p,q)\,\langle f|p,q\rangle\langle p,q|f\rangle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dq}{q^{2}}|\Phi(q)|^{2}\,.

(18)

III.2
Quantum observables

Using the resolution of the identity (
17
), we define the
quantization of a classical observable
f
as follows

\mathcal{F}\ni f\longrightarrow\hat{f}:=\frac{1}{A_{\Phi}}\int_{\mathrm{G}}d\mu(p,q)|p,q\rangle f(p,q)\langle p,q|\in\mathcal{A}\,,

(19)

where
\mathcal{F}
is a vector space of real continuous functions on a phase
space, and
\mathcal{A}
is a vector space of operators (quantum observables)
acting in the Hilbert space
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},d\nu(x))

19
)
defines a linear mapping and the observable
\hat{f}
is a symmetric
operator. Self-adjointness of
\hat{f}
is an open problem as symmetricity
does not assure self-adjointness so that further examination is required

(20)

For calculation of the matrix elements of the operator (

In this case one gets

\displaystyle\mathrm{epq}(\alpha,p,q;z):=\sum_{n}\langle e^{(\alpha)}_{n}(x)|pq\rangle\left[\sqrt{\frac{n!}{(n+\alpha)!\alpha!}}\right]^{-\frac{1}{2}}z^{n}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\sqrt{q}}{1-z}\right)^{1+\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}dx\,x^{\alpha}\exp\left(-\left[\frac{q+1}{2}+\frac{z}{1-z}-ip\right]x\right)\,.

The required overlaps turn out to be defined by the derivatives of these functions calculated at

\langle e^{(\alpha)}_{n}(x)|pq\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha!n!(n+\alpha)!}}\left[\frac{d^{n}}{dz^{n}}\mathrm{epq}(\alpha,p,q;z)\right]_{|z=0}\,.

Using exactly the same method, the matrix elements of the operator (

\displaystyle\cdot\left[\frac{\partial^{n}}{\partial z_{1}^{n}}\frac{\partial^{n^{\prime}}}{\partial z_{2}^{n^{\prime}}}\int_{\mathrm{Aff}(\mathbb{R})}d\mu_{L}(p,q)\mathrm{epq}(\alpha,p,q;z_{1})f(p,q)\mathrm{epq}(\alpha,p,q;z_{2})^{\star}\right]_{|z_{1}=z_{2}=0}\,.

(25)

The simplest basis, satisfying required conditions, is obtained by taking

(26)

III.3
Quantum dynamics

The mapping (
19
) applied to the classical Hamiltonian (
4
) reads

\hat{H}_{\rm unbounded}=\frac{1}{A_{\Phi}}\int_{G}d\mu(p,q)|p,q\rangle H(p,q)\langle p,q|\,.

(27)

However, our classical analysis applies only to the region of phase space for which
H(p,q)>0
.

An important problem is introducing constraints into the integral
quantization approach.
This quantization is based on deformation of quantum measure represented by a set of positive
self-adjoint operators determining the operator valued measure (POV). They are considered as
generalization of more standard self-adjoint quantum observables
Busch1996
.

In our case the set of operators

\hat{M}(Q):=\frac{1}{A_{\phi}}\int_{G}d\mu(p,q)|p,q\rangle\chi_{Q}(g)\langle p,q|\,,

(28)

where
\chi_{Q}(g)=1
if
g\in Q
and
0
otherwise, and where
Q\subset\mathrm{G}=\mathrm{Aff}(\mathbb{R})
,
describe the localization of the system in
the subspace
Q
of the phase space
\mathrm{G}
.

The normalization condition

\hat{M}(G)=\frac{1}{A_{\phi}}\int_{G}d\mu(p,q)|p,q\rangle\langle p,q|=\hat{1\kern-4.75pt1}\,

(29)

is required to get the so called minimal
probabilistic interpretation of quantum mechanics
Busch1996
:

\mathrm{Prob}(Q,\Psi)=\langle\Psi|\hat{M}(\mathrm{Q})|\kern 1.29167pt\Psi\kern 1.29167pt\rangle\,,

(30)

which describes the probability of finding our system in
Q
, under condition
that this system is in the state
\Psi
.

On the other hand, to construct the condition (
29
) one
needs to integrate over the whole group manifold and the representation
(
16
) has to be irreducible.
This excludes the possibility of using a smaller region
\Omega\subset\mathrm{G}

of the phase space to fulfill the classical constraint, where

\Omega=\{(p,q):H(p,q)>0\}
.

To have a consistent quantization method, the only possibility of quantizing any
observable restricted to a smaller region of the phase space is to quantize it
over the whole phase space, represented in this approach by the group

\mathrm{G}
.

Following these requirements, to keep physical interpretation of the classical
Hamiltonian, we quantize it as a function restricted to the required region of the
phase space by considering:

\hat{H}=\frac{1}{A_{\Phi}}\int_{G}d\mu(p,q)|p,q\rangle\theta(H(p,q))H(p,q)\langle p,q|\,,

(31)

where
\theta
is the Heaviside theta function.

An important feature of the operator
\hat{H}
is that it acts in a
nontrivial way on the whole phase space. Let
(p^{\prime},q^{\prime})\not\in\Omega
, then

\hat{H}|p^{\prime},q^{\prime}\rangle=\frac{1}{A_{\Phi}}\int_{\Omega}d\mu(p,q)|p,q\rangle\theta(H(p,q))H(p,q)\langle p,q|p^{\prime},q^{\prime}\rangle

(32)

is usually a non-zero vector.
This behavior is due to non-orthogonality of the states
|p,q\rangle
, i.e., the
quantum phase space regions
\Omega
and
\mathrm{G}\setminus\Omega
are not
independent.

Another very important problem related is the quantization
of the elementary observables
(p,q)
. These observables
represent position of the system in the quantum full phase space

\mathrm{G}
. The corresponding quantum operators
(\hat{p},\hat{q})
should satisfy
the following consistency conditions
AOW
:

\langle p,q|\hat{p}|p,q\rangle=p\text{\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ and\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ }\langle p,q|\hat{q}|p,q\rangle=q\,,

(33)

where

\hat{p}=\frac{1}{A_{\Phi}}\int_{G}d\mu(p,q)|p,q\rangle p\langle p,q|\text{\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ and\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ }\hat{q}=\frac{1}{A_{\Phi}}\int_{G}d\mu(p,q)|p,q\rangle q\langle p,q|\,.

(34)

Satisfying (
33
) is possible only when the operators are defined
on the entitre phase space
\mathrm{G}
.
The consistency conditions support the physical interpretation of the POV
measure (
28
).

The above analysis allow us to be consistent and to quantize the restricted
form of the Hamiltonian
H
and unrestricted form of the elementary
observables
(p,q)
.

We will restrict to this region of a phase space by considering:

\hat{H}=\frac{1}{A_{\Phi}}\int_{G}d\mu(p,q)|p,q\rangle\theta(H(p,q))H(p,q)\langle p,q|\,,

(35)

where
\theta
is the Heaviside theta function. Let us notice that the operator
\hat{H}
is positive definite. Indeed, for any state
|\kern 1.29167pt\Psi\kern 1.29167pt\rangle
we have:

\langle\kern 1.29167pt\Psi\kern 1.29167pt|\kern 1.29167pt\hat{H}\Psi\kern 1.29167pt\rangle=\frac{1}{A_{\Phi}}\int_{G}d\mu(p,q)|\Psi(p,q)|^{2}\theta(H(p,q))H(p,q)\,>0.

(36)

The quantum evolution of our gravitational system is defined by the
Schrödinger equation:

i\frac{\partial}{\partial s}|\Psi(s)\rangle=\hat{H}|\Psi(s)\rangle\;,

(37)

where
|\Psi\rangle\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},d\nu(x))
, and where
s
is an evolution
parameter of the quantum level.

In general, the parameters
t
of the classical level and
s
are different. To get the consistency
between the classical and quantum levels we postulate that
t=s
, which defines the time
variable at both levels. This way we support the interpretation that Hamiltonian is the generator
of classical and corresponding quantum dynamics.

where

\displaystyle L_{n,k}=(-1)^{n}\binom{n-1}{k-1}\frac{n!}{k!},\quad n\geq k\geq 1,

(47)

\displaystyle L_{0,0}=1,\quad L_{n,0}=0,\,n\geq 1.

(48)

We extend the sum in (
45
) to
k=0
and obtain a formula valid for
n=0
:

\frac{d^{n}}{dz^{n}}F(G(0))=\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}|L_{n,k}|\,x^{k}e^{-x}.

(49)

In order to calculate
\langle e^{(1)}_{n}|pq\rangle
, we need to evaluate the integral

\int_{0}^{\infty}dx\ x^{k}\exp\left(-\left(\frac{q+1}{2}-\texttt{i}p\right)x\right)=\frac{k!}{\left(\frac{q+1}{2}-\texttt{i}p\right)^{k+1}}.

(50)

This integral directly follows from the Euler’s integral formula (see for example equation 6.1.1 from
AbramowitzStegun
:

\Gamma(n)=z^{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}dx\,x^{n-1}e^{-zx},\penalty\ \quad{\rm for\ }\penalty\ \Re(n)>0,\penalty\ \Re{(z)}>0.

(51)

In the formula above
\Gamma(z)
is the Gamma function.
Inserting this evaluation into (
38
) gives:

\langle e^{(1)}_{n}|pq\rangle=\sqrt{n+1}\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}\frac{(-1)^{k+1}k!}{(n+1)!}|L_{n+1,k}|\frac{q}{\left(\frac{q+1}{2}-\texttt{i}p\right)^{k+1}}\,.

(52)

We will define coefficients
E_{n,k}
:

E_{n,k}=\frac{(-1)^{k+1}k!}{n!}|L_{n,k}|.

(53)

Due to cancellation of terms, the coefficients have the following explicit form:

\displaystyle E_{n,k}=(-1)^{k-1}\binom{n-1}{k-1},\quad n\geq k\geq 1,

(54)

\displaystyle E_{0,0}=\frac{(-1)^{k-1}k!}{n!},\quad E_{n,0}=0,\,n\geq 1.

(55)

Let us notice, that in our formulas we need only the coefficients with
n\geq 1
. Since,
E_{n,0}=0
for
n\geq 1
,
we can assume that
k\geq 1
. The formula (
52
) takes now a more compact form:

\langle e^{(1)}_{n}|pq\rangle=\sqrt{n+1}\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}(-1)^{k-1}\binom{n}{k-1}\frac{q}{\left(\frac{q+1}{2}-\texttt{i}p\right)^{k+1}}\,.

(56)

We will change the summation variable into
k^{\prime}=k-1
and obtain:

\langle e^{(1)}_{n}|pq\rangle=\frac{q\,\sqrt{n+1}}{\left(\frac{q+1}{2}-\texttt{i}p\right)^{2}}\,\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{\left(\frac{q+1}{2}-\texttt{i}p\right)^{k}}.

(57)

The last sum is the binomial expansion. Therefore

\langle e^{(1)}_{n}|pq\rangle=\frac{q\,\sqrt{n+1}}{\left(\frac{q+1}{2}-\texttt{i}p\right)^{2}}\,\left(1-\frac{1}{\frac{q+1}{2}-\texttt{i}p}\right)^{n}=q\,\sqrt{n+1}\,\frac{\left(\frac{q-1}{2}-\texttt{i}p\right)^{n}}{\left(\frac{q+1}{2}-\texttt{i}p\right)^{n+2}}.

(58)

IV.2
The matrix elements of the Hamiltonian operator

The new expression for the basis elements that we found in the previous section allows us to calculate the matrix
elements of the Hamiltonian:

H_{nm}:=\langle e^{(1)}_{n}|\hat{H}e^{(1)}_{m}\rangle=\frac{1}{A_{\Phi}}\int_{G}d\mu(p,q)\langle e^{(1)}_{n}|pq\rangle\theta(H(q,p))H(p,q)\langle e^{(1)}_{m}|pq\rangle^{*}.

(59)

After applying the formula (
58
) to the equation above, we obtain:

H_{nm}:=\frac{\sqrt{(n+1)(m+1)}}{2\pi}\,\int_{0}^{\infty}dq\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dp\ \theta(H(q,p))H(q,p)\frac{\left(\frac{q-1}{2}-\texttt{i}p\right)^{n}\left(\frac{q-1}{2}+\texttt{i}p\right)^{m}}{\left(\frac{q+1}{2}-\texttt{i}p\right)^{n+2}\left(\frac{q+1}{2}+\texttt{i}p\right)^{m+2}}.

(60)

Since
\hat{H}
is hermitian, it is enough to calculate the lower triangular part of the matrix,
i.e.
H_{nm}
for
n\geq m
. In this case:

H_{nm}=\frac{\sqrt{(n+1)(m+1)}}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}dq\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dp\ \theta(H(q,p))H(q,p)\frac{\left(\frac{q-1}{2}-\texttt{i}p\right)^{n}\left(\frac{q-1}{2}+\texttt{i}p\right)^{m}\left(\frac{q+1}{2}+\texttt{i}p\right)^{n-m}}{\left(\left(\frac{q+1}{2}\right)^{2}+p^{2}\right)^{n+2}}.

(61)

In order to calculate the real and the imaginary part of the matrix, we write the Hamiltonian in the form

H_{nm}=\frac{\sqrt{(n+1)(m+1)}}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}dq\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dp\ \theta(H(q,p))H(q,p)\frac{\left(\left(\frac{q-1}{2}\right)^{2}+p^{2}\right)^{m}\left(\frac{q^{2}-1}{4}+p^{2}-\texttt{i}p\right)^{n-m}}{\left(\left(\frac{q+1}{2}\right)^{2}+p^{2}\right)^{n+2}}.

(62)

After expanding the expression
\left(\frac{q^{2}-1}{4}+p^{2}-\texttt{i}p\right)^{n-m}
in powers of
i
,
we notice that the real part of the integrant is an even function in
p
and the imaginary part is
an odd function in
p
. As a result, the imaginary part of
H_{nm}
vanishes:

\Im H_{nm}=0.

(63)

We conclude that
H_{nm}
is a real symmetric matrix. We will calculate the matrix elements by evaluating
the integrals over
q
and
p
numerically.

The diagonal elements
H_{nn}
are positive since the integrand of (
62
) is a positive function
of the variables
q
and
p
.

IV.3
The matrix elements of the momentum operator

The matrix elements of the momentum operator:

p_{nm}:=\frac{\sqrt{(n+1)(m+1)}}{2\pi}\,\int_{0}^{\infty}dq\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dp\ \frac{p\left(\frac{q-1}{2}-\texttt{i}p\right)^{n}\left(\frac{q-1}{2}+\texttt{i}p\right)^{m}}{\left(\frac{q+1}{2}-\texttt{i}p\right)^{n+2}\left(\frac{q+1}{2}+\texttt{i}p\right)^{m+2}}.

(64)

The integral over
p
can be done using a contour method. We take the sunset contour in the lower half plane and obtain

p_{nm}:=-\texttt{i}\sqrt{(n+1)(m+1)}\,\int_{0}^{\infty}dq\ {\rm Res}|_{p=-\texttt{i}\frac{q+1}{2}}\left(\frac{p\left(\frac{q-1}{2}-\texttt{i}p\right)^{n}\left(\frac{q-1}{2}+\texttt{i}p\right)^{m}}{\left(\frac{q+1}{2}-\texttt{i}p\right)^{n+2}\left(\frac{q+1}{2}+\texttt{i}p\right)^{m+2}}\right).

(65)

We will calculate now the residuum:

{\rm Res}|_{p=-\texttt{i}\frac{q+1}{2}}\left(\frac{p\left(\frac{q-1}{2}-\texttt{i}p\right)^{n}\left(\frac{q-1}{2}+\texttt{i}p\right)^{m}}{\left(\frac{q+1}{2}-\texttt{i}p\right)^{n+2}\left(\frac{q+1}{2}+\texttt{i}p\right)^{m+2}}\right)=\\
=\texttt{i}^{n+2}\frac{1}{(n+1)!}\frac{d^{n+1}}{dp^{n+1}}\left({\frac{p\left(\frac{q-1}{2}-\texttt{i}p\right)^{n}\left(\frac{q-1}{2}+\texttt{i}p\right)^{m}}{\left(\frac{q+1}{2}+\texttt{i}p\right)^{m+2}}}\right)=\\
=\frac{\texttt{i}^{n+2}}{(n+1)!}p\frac{d^{n+1}}{dp^{n+1}}\left({\frac{\left(\frac{q-1}{2}-\texttt{i}p\right)^{n}\left(\frac{q-1}{2}+\texttt{i}p\right)^{m}}{\left(\frac{q+1}{2}+\texttt{i}p\right)^{m+2}}}\right)|_{p=-\texttt{i}\frac{q+1}{2}}+\\
+\frac{\texttt{i}^{n+2}}{n!}\frac{d^{n}}{dp^{n}}\left({\frac{\left(\frac{q-1}{2}-\texttt{i}p\right)^{n}\left(\frac{q-1}{2}+\texttt{i}p\right)^{m}}{\left(\frac{q+1}{2}+\texttt{i}p\right)^{m+2}}}\right)|_{p=-\texttt{i}\frac{q+1}{2}}.

(66)

Let us notice that the two terms in the last line are proportional to expressions of the form:

I(l,n,m,q)=\frac{1}{l!}\frac{d^{l}}{dp^{l}}\left({\frac{\left(\frac{q-1}{2}-\texttt{i}p\right)^{n}\left(\frac{q-1}{2}+\texttt{i}p\right)^{m}}{\left(\frac{q+1}{2}+\texttt{i}p\right)^{m+2}}}\right)|_{p=-\texttt{i}\frac{q+1}{2}}

(67)

with
l=n
or
l=n+1
. We will expand the derivative using a Leibnitz rule generalized to three factors:

I(l,n,m,q)=\frac{1}{l!}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}\\
k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3}=l\end{subarray}}\binom{l}{k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}}\frac{d^{k_{1}}}{dp^{k_{1}}}\left(\frac{q-1}{2}-\texttt{i}p\right)^{n}\cdot\\
\cdot\frac{d^{k_{2}}}{dp^{k_{2}}}\left(\frac{q-1}{2}+\texttt{i}p\right)^{m}\frac{d^{k_{3}}}{dp^{k_{3}}}\left(\frac{q+1}{2}+\texttt{i}p\right)^{-m-2}|_{p=-\texttt{i}\frac{q+1}{2}},

(68)

where
\binom{l}{k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}}=\frac{l!}{k_{1}!k_{2}!k_{3}!}
. The derivatives of power functions can be calculated and give (
j,k\in\mathbb{Z},j\geq 0,\ k\geq 0
):

\displaystyle\frac{d^{k}}{dx^{k}}x^{j}=\begin{cases}\frac{j!}{(j-k)!}\cdot x^{j-k},&{\rm for}\ j\geq k,\\
0,&{\rm for}\ j<k,\end{cases}

(69)

\displaystyle\frac{d^{k}}{dx^{k}}x^{-j}=(-1)^{k}\frac{(j+k-1)!}{(j-1)!}\cdot x^{-j-k}.

(70)

Inserting this result into (
68
) gives:

I(l,n,m,q)=\frac{1}{l!}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}\\
k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3}=l\\
k_{1}\leq n,k_{2}\leq m\end{subarray}}\binom{l}{k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}}\frac{n!}{(n-k_{1})!}(-\texttt{i})^{k_{1}}\left(\frac{q-1}{2}-\texttt{i}p\right)^{n-k_{1}}\cdot\\
\cdot(\texttt{i})^{k_{2}}\frac{m!}{(m-k_{2})!}\left(\frac{q-1}{2}+\texttt{i}p\right)^{m-k_{2}}(-\texttt{i})^{k_{3}}\frac{(m+1+k_{3})!}{(m+1)!}\left(\frac{q+1}{2}+\texttt{i}p\right)^{-m-2-k_{3}}|_{p=-\texttt{i}\frac{q+1}{2}}.

(71)

After evaluating at
p=-\texttt{i}\frac{q+1}{2}
we get:

I(l,n,m,q)=(-1)^{n}(\texttt{i})^{l}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}\\
k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3}=l\\
k_{1}\leq n,k_{2}\leq m\end{subarray}}(-1)^{k_{3}}\binom{n}{k_{1}}\binom{m}{k_{2}}\binom{m+1+k_{3}}{k_{3}}\frac{q^{m-k_{2}}}{\left(q+1\right)^{m+k_{3}+2}}.

(72)

In order to find the expression for the matrix elements of the momentum operator, we need to evaluate 2 integrals:

I_{1}(n,m)=\int_{0}^{\infty}dq\ (q+1)I(n+1,n,m,q),\quad I_{2}(n,m)=\int_{0}^{\infty}dq\ I(n,n,m,q).

(73)

In order to perform the integrals, we notice that they can be expressed in terms of the beta function

B(x,y)=\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\frac{t^{x-1}}{(1+t)^{x+y}},\quad{\rm for\ }\penalty\ \Re{(x)}>0,\penalty\ \Re{y}>0.

(74)

The first integral is:

I_{1}(n,m)=(-1)^{n}(\texttt{i})^{n+1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}\\
k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3}=n+1\\
k_{1}\leq n,k_{2}\leq m\end{subarray}}(-1)^{k_{3}}\binom{n}{k_{1}}\binom{m}{k_{2}}\binom{m+1+k_{3}}{k_{3}}B(m-k_{2}+1,k_{2}+k_{3}).

(75)

If
x,y\in\mathbb{N}-\{0\}
, the beta function takes the form:

B(x,y)=\frac{(x-1)!(y-1)!}{(x+y-1)!}.

(76)

Inserting this property into equation (
75
) we get:

I_{1}(n,m)=(-1)^{n}\texttt{i}^{n+1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}\\
k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3}=n+1\\
k_{1}\leq n,k_{2}\leq m\end{subarray}}(-1)^{k_{3}}\binom{n}{k_{1}}\binom{m}{k_{2}}\binom{m+1+k_{3}}{k_{3}}\frac{(m-k_{2})!(k_{2}+k_{3}-1)!}{(m+k_{3})!}=\\
=\frac{(-1)^{n}\texttt{i}^{n+1}}{(n+1)(m+1)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}\\
k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3}=n+1\\
k_{1}\leq n,k_{2}\leq m\end{subarray}}(-1)^{k_{3}}\frac{(n+1)!}{k_{1}!k_{2}!k_{3}!}\left(m+1+k_{3}\right)=\\
=\frac{(-1)^{n}\texttt{i}^{n+1}}{(n+1)(m+1)}\left(-(m+1)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}\\
k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3}=n+1\\
k_{1}\leq n+1,k_{2}\leq m\end{subarray}}(-1)^{k_{3}}\frac{(n+1)!}{k_{1}!k_{2}!k_{3}!}\left(m+1+k_{3}\right)\right)\,.

(77)

The second integral is:

I_{2}(n,m)=(-1)^{n}(\texttt{i})^{n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}\\
k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3}=n\\
k_{1}\leq n,k_{2}\leq m\end{subarray}}(-1)^{k_{3}}\binom{n}{k_{1}}\binom{m}{k_{2}}\binom{m+1+k_{3}}{k_{3}}\frac{(m-k_{2})!(k_{2}+k_{3})!}{(m+k_{3}+1)!}=\\
=\frac{(-1)^{n}(\texttt{i})^{n}}{m+1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}\\
k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3}=n\\
k_{1}\leq n,k_{2}\leq m\end{subarray}}(-1)^{k_{3}}\frac{n!}{k_{1}!k_{2}!k_{3}!}\,.

(78)

The matrix elements of the momentum operator can be expressed in terms of the functions
I_{1}(n,m)
and
I_{2}(n,m)
:

p_{nm}:=\sqrt{(n+1)(m+1)}\texttt{i}^{n+2}\left(-\frac{1}{2}I_{1}(n,m)-\texttt{i}I_{2}(n,m)\right)

(79)

IV.3.1
Entries below and on the diagonal

We will look for a compact formula for the matrix elements. To this end we will find expressions for

S_{n,m}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}\\
k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3}=n\\
k_{1}\leq n,k_{2}\leq m\end{subarray}}(-1)^{k_{3}}\frac{n!}{k_{1}!k_{2}!k_{3}!},\quad Z_{n,m}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}\\
k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3}=n\\
k_{1}\leq n,k_{2}\leq m\end{subarray}}(-1)^{k_{3}}k_{3}\frac{n!}{k_{1}!k_{2}!k_{3}!}.

(80)

We will find
S_{n,m}
and
Z_{n,m}
recursively. In this derivation we will rely on the following identity holding for any polynomial
P
of degree smaller than
n
:

\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}\binom{n}{k}P(k)=0.

(81)

Let us consider first
S_{n,m}
. From the definition, it is clear that

S_{n,m+1}=S_{n,m}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}\\
k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3}=n\\
k_{1}\leq n,k_{2}=m+1\end{subarray}}(-1)^{k_{3}}\frac{n!}{k_{1}!k_{2}!k_{3}!}=\\
=S_{n,m}+\frac{n!}{(n-m-1)!(m+1)!}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},k_{3}\\
k_{1}+k_{3}=n-m-1\end{subarray}}(-1)^{k_{3}}\frac{(n-m-1)!}{k_{1}!k_{3}!}=\\
=\begin{cases}S_{n,m}+1,&\textrm{ if }m=n-1,\\
S_{n,m},&\textrm{ if }m<n-1.\\
\end{cases}

(82)

In order to find the desired identity, we notice that

S_{n,0}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}\\
k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3}=n\\
k_{1}\leq n,k_{2}=0\end{subarray}}(-1)^{k_{3}}\frac{n!}{k_{1}!k_{2}!k_{3}!}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},k_{3}\\
k_{1}+k_{3}=n\end{subarray}}(-1)^{k_{3}}\frac{n!}{k_{1}!k_{3}!}=\delta_{n,0}.

(83)

Combining the results we get that :

S_{n,m}=\delta_{n,m}\textrm{ for }m\leq n.

(84)

The second sum will be calculated in completely similar manner. We have the following recurrence:

Z_{n,m+1}=Z_{n,m}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}\\
k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3}=n\\
k_{1}\leq n,k_{2}=m+1\end{subarray}}(-1)^{k_{3}}k_{3}\,\frac{n!}{k_{1}!k_{2}!k_{3}!}=\\
=Z_{n,m}+\binom{n}{m+1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},k_{3}\\
k_{1}+k_{3}=n-m-1\end{subarray}}(-1)^{k_{3}}k_{3}\binom{n-m-1}{k_{3}}=\\
=\begin{cases}Z_{n,m},&\textrm{ if }m=n-1,\\
Z_{n,m}-n,&\textrm{ if }m=n-2.\\
Z_{n,m},&\textrm{ if }m\leq n-3.\end{cases}

(85)

We can express the formula above using Kronecker delta:

Z_{n,m+1}=Z_{n,m}-n\delta_{n,m+2}.

(86)

The recurrence starts at
Z_{n,0}
:

Z_{n,0}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}\\
k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3}=n\\
k_{1}\leq n,k_{2}=0\end{subarray}}(-1)^{k_{3}}k_{3}\,\frac{n!}{k_{1}!k_{2}!k_{3}!}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},k_{3}\\
k_{1}+k_{3}=n\end{subarray}}(-1)^{k_{3}}k_{3}\,\binom{n}{k_{3}}=\begin{cases}1,&\textrm{ if }n=0,\\
-1,&\textrm{ if }n=1.\\
0,&\textrm{ if }n\geq 2.\end{cases}

(87)

In our study, we will only need to consider the following two cases.

1.

n=1
. In this case
Z_{1,0}=-1
and
Z_{1,1}=-1
.

2.

n\geq 2
. In this case
Z_{n,0}=0
and

Z_{n,m+1}=Z_{n,m}-n\delta_{n,m+2}.

(88)

As a result,

Z_{n,m}=-n\delta_{n,m+1}-n\delta_{n,m}.

(89)

Let us notice that the two cases can be written with one formula. For
n\geq 1
:

Z_{n,m}=-n\delta_{n,m+1}-n\delta_{n,m}.

(90)

Let us now go back to the expression for
I_{1}(n,m)
and
I_{2}(n,m)
.

I_{1}(n,m)=\frac{(-1)^{n}\texttt{i}^{n+1}}{(n+1)(m+1)}\left((m+1)(S_{n+1,m}-1)+Z_{n+1,m}\right)=\\
=\frac{(-1)^{n}\texttt{i}^{n+1}}{(n+1)(m+1)}\left((m+1)(\delta_{n+1,m}-1)-(n+1)\delta_{n+1,m+1}-(n+1\delta_{n+1,m}\right).

(91)

The second object is:

I_{2}(n,m)=\frac{(-1)^{n}(\texttt{i})^{n}}{m+1}S_{n,m}=\frac{(-1)^{n}(\texttt{i})^{n}}{m+1}\delta_{n,m}.

(92)

We will combine the results to obtain the expression for the matrix elements of the momentum operator:

p_{nm}=\frac{\texttt{i}}{\sqrt{(n+1)(m+1)}}\left(\frac{m+1}{2}(\delta_{n+1,m}-1)-\frac{n+1}{2}\delta_{n+1,m+1}-\frac{n+1}{2}\delta_{n+1,m}+(n+1)\delta_{n,m}\right).

(93)

p_{nm}=\frac{\texttt{i}}{\sqrt{(n+1)(m+1)}}\left(-\frac{m+1}{2}+\frac{n+1}{2}\delta_{n,m}+\frac{m-n}{2}\delta_{n+1,m}\right).

(94)

Let us notice that we make calculation for
m\leq n
only. Therefore

p_{nm}=\frac{\texttt{i}}{\sqrt{(n+1)(m+1)}}\left(-\frac{m+1}{2}+\frac{n+1}{2}\delta_{n,m}\right).

(95)

This means that:

p_{nn}=0,\quad p_{nm}=-\frac{\texttt{i}}{2}\sqrt{\frac{m+1}{n+1}}\textrm{ if }m<n.

(96)

IV.3.2
The entries above the diagonal

For a cross-check, we will calculate the entries above the diagonal as well
m>n
. In this case

S_{n,m}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}\\
k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3}=n\\
k_{1}\leq n,k_{2}\leq m\end{subarray}}(-1)^{k_{3}}\frac{n!}{k_{1}!k_{2}!k_{3}!}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}\\
k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3}=n\\
k_{1}\leq n,k_{2}\leq n\end{subarray}}(-1)^{k_{3}}\frac{n!}{k_{1}!k_{2}!k_{3}!}=(1+1-1)^{n}=1

(97)

and

Z_{n,m}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}\\
k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3}=n\\
k_{1}\leq n,k_{2}\leq m\end{subarray}}(-1)^{k_{3}}k_{3}\frac{n!}{k_{1}!k_{2}!k_{3}!}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}\\
k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3}=n\\
k_{1}\leq n,k_{2}\leq n\end{subarray}}(-1)^{k_{3}}k_{3}\frac{n!}{k_{1}!k_{2}!k_{3}!}=\\
=x_{3}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{3}}(x_{1}+x_{2}-x_{3})^{n}|_{x_{1}=x_{2}=x_{3}=1}=-n(1+1-1)^{n}=-n.

(98)

Inserting the formulas into
I_{1}(n,m),I_{2}(n,m)
and into
p_{nm}
afterwards, we get:

p_{nm}=\frac{\texttt{i}}{2}\sqrt{\frac{n+1}{m+1}}\textrm{ if }m>n.

(99)

This is consistent with the fact that the matrix
p
is hermitian.

IV.3.3
Summary

The calculations above show that

p_{nn}=0,\quad p_{nm}=-\frac{\texttt{i}}{2}\sqrt{\frac{m+1}{n+1}}\textrm{ if }m<n,\quad p_{nm}=\frac{\texttt{i}}{2}\sqrt{\frac{n+1}{m+1}}\textrm{ if }m>n.

(100)

IV.4
The matrix elements of the coordinate operator

The matrix elements of the coordinate operator:

q_{nm}:=\frac{\sqrt{(n+1)(m+1)}}{2\pi}\,\int_{0}^{\infty}dq\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dp\ \frac{q\left(\frac{q-1}{2}-\texttt{i}p\right)^{n}\left(\frac{q-1}{2}+\texttt{i}p\right)^{m}}{\left(\frac{q+1}{2}-\texttt{i}p\right)^{n+2}\left(\frac{q+1}{2}+\texttt{i}p\right)^{m+2}}.

(101)

The integral over
p
can be done using a contour method. We take the sunset contour in the lower half plane and obtain

q_{nm}:=-\texttt{i}\sqrt{(n+1)(m+1)}\,\int_{0}^{\infty}dq\ q\,{\rm Res}|_{p=-\texttt{i}\frac{q+1}{2}}\left(\frac{\left(\frac{q-1}{2}-\texttt{i}p\right)^{n}\left(\frac{q-1}{2}+\texttt{i}p\right)^{m}}{\left(\frac{q+1}{2}-\texttt{i}p\right)^{n+2}\left(\frac{q+1}{2}+\texttt{i}p\right)^{m+2}}\right).

(102)

We will calculate now the residuum:

{\rm Res}|_{p=-\texttt{i}\frac{q+1}{2}}\left(\frac{\left(\frac{q-1}{2}-\texttt{i}p\right)^{n}\left(\frac{q-1}{2}+\texttt{i}p\right)^{m}}{\left(\frac{q+1}{2}-\texttt{i}p\right)^{n+2}\left(\frac{q+1}{2}+\texttt{i}p\right)^{m+2}}\right)=\\
=\texttt{i}^{n+2}\frac{1}{(n+1)!}\frac{d^{n+1}}{dp^{n+1}}\left({\frac{\left(\frac{q-1}{2}-\texttt{i}p\right)^{n}\left(\frac{q-1}{2}+\texttt{i}p\right)^{m}}{\left(\frac{q+1}{2}+\texttt{i}p\right)^{m+2}}}\right).

(103)

The integral that we will need to calculate this time is

I_{3}(n,m)=\int_{0}^{\infty}dq\,q\,I(n+1,n,m,q).

(104)

With this integral, we can express the matrix elements of the coordinate operator in the following form:

q_{nm}=\texttt{i}^{n+1}\sqrt{(n+1)(m+1)}I_{3}(n,m).

(105)

As previously, we can express the integral
I_{3}(n,m)
as a sum.

I_{3}(n,m)=(-1)^{n}\texttt{i}^{n+1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}\\
k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3}=n+1\\
k_{1}\leq n,k_{2}\leq m\end{subarray}}(-1)^{k_{3}}\binom{n}{k_{1}}\binom{m}{k_{2}}\binom{m+1+k_{3}}{k_{3}}\int_{0}^{\infty}dq\,\frac{q^{m-k_{2}+1}}{(q+1)^{m+k_{3}+2}}=\\
=(-1)^{n}\texttt{i}^{n+1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}\\
k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3}=n+1\\
k_{1}\leq n,k_{2}\leq m\end{subarray}}(-1)^{k_{3}}\binom{n}{k_{1}}\binom{m}{k_{2}}\binom{m+1+k_{3}}{k_{3}}\frac{(m-k_{2}+1)!(k_{2}+k_{3}-1)!}{(m+k_{3}+1)!}=\\
=-\frac{(-\texttt{i})^{n+1}}{(n+1)(m+1)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}\\
k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3}=n+1\\
k_{1}\leq n,k_{2}\leq m\end{subarray}}(-1)^{k_{3}}\frac{(n+1)!}{k_{1}!k_{2}!k_{3}!}(m+1-k_{2})=\\
=\frac{(-\texttt{i})^{n+1}}{(n+1)(m+1)}\left((m+1)-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}\\
k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3}=n+1\\
k_{1}\leq n+1,k_{2}\leq m\end{subarray}}(-1)^{k_{3}}\frac{(n+1)!}{k_{1}!k_{2}!k_{3}!}(m+1-k_{2})\right).

(106)

This time we will consider separately the terms on the diagonal and above the diagonal (and we will not calculate the terms below diagonal.

IV.4.1
Diagonal terms

q_{nn}=\frac{1}{n+1}\left((n+1)-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}\\
k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3}=n+1\\
k_{1}\leq n+1,k_{2}\leq n\end{subarray}}(-1)^{k_{3}}\frac{(n+1)!}{k_{1}!k_{2}!k_{3}!}(n+1-k_{2})\right)=\\
=\frac{1}{n+1}\left((n+1)-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}\\
k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3}=n+1\\
k_{1}\leq n+1,k_{2}\leq n+1\end{subarray}}(-1)^{k_{3}}\frac{(n+1)!}{k_{1}!k_{2}!k_{3}!}(n+1-k_{2})\right)=\\
=\frac{1}{n+1}\left((n+1)-(n+1)(1+1-1)^{n+1}+x_{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}}\left(x_{1}+x_{2}-x_{3}\right)^{n+1}|_{x_{1}=x_{2}=x_{3}=1}\right)=1.

(107)

IV.4.2
The terms above the diagonal

We will consider matrix elements
q_{nm}
for
m>n
. In this case:

q_{nm}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{(n+1)(m+1)}}\left((m+1)-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}\\

V
Evolution of quantum observables

In what follows, we consider the quantum evolution corresponding to the
classical case with
M<\frac{m}{2}
, where
m
is the total rest mass of the
shell. Classically, the shell reaches the horizon
q_{H}=2M^{2}
after an
infinite time (of an observer at spatial infinity), as it is illustrated in Fig. ​
1
.

Remarkably, our quantum model experiences a different behaviour. In a
finite time, the quantum shell reaches a minimum size and after a bounce,
which is above the horizon, it expands until it reaches a maximum size. More
precisely, in our quantum model we choose a state peaked at energy
M
.
One needs to remember that the energy of the system is conserved during the time evolution
given by the Schrödinger equation.
We diagonalize the quantum Hamiltonian numerically. Due to technical limitations of our numerical
procedure we can study the model for small values of
M
only. We calculate the quantum
evolution of the expectation values of the operators
\hat{q}
and

\hat{p}
. We notice that the expectation values
\langle q\rangle_{t}
and

\langle p\rangle_{t}
are periodic functions of time
t
.

V.1
Gaussian state

We choose a state peaked at energy
M
with standard deviation
\sigma
:

|\kern 1.29167pt\Psi\kern 1.29167pt\rangle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i}e^{-\frac{(E_{i}-M)^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}}|\kern 1.29167ptE_{i}\kern 1.29167pt\rangle,

(110)

where
|\kern 1.29167ptE_{i}\kern 1.29167pt\rangle
is the eigenstate of the Hamiltonian operator with the
eigenvalue
E_{i}
and
N
is the normalization constant:

N^{2}=\sum_{i}e^{-\frac{(E_{i}-M)^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}}.

(111)

In practical calculations we limit the sum to the region

[M-4\sigma\sqrt{2\ln(10)},M+4\sigma\sqrt{2\ln(10)}]
. With this choice, at
the end of the interval the exponential factor is

\,\,-\frac{(E_{i}-M)^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}=e^{-16\ln(10)}=10^{-16}
, which is
approximately equal to the machine precision for double accuracy used in our
calculations. We introduce a cut-off in the matrix size. We choose the parameter

\sigma
such that all eigenvalues in the region are good approximations of the
full eigenvalues. We will choose eigenvalues which converge when the cut-off is
increased. The precise definitions will be given in the next section.

V.4
Oscillatory character of the evolution

The oscillatory character of the evolution is related to our choice of the Gaussian state. This can be shown by the following calculation:

<\hat{q}>_{t}=\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{i,j}e^{-\frac{(E_{i}-M)^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}-\frac{(E_{j}-M)^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}}e^{\texttt{i}(E_{i}-E_{j})t}\langle\kern 1.29167ptE_{i}\kern 1.29167pt|\hat{q}|\kern 1.29167ptE_{j}\kern 1.29167pt\rangle=\\
=\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{i,j}e^{-\frac{(E_{i}-M)^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}-\frac{(E_{j}-M)^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}}\cos((E_{i}-E_{j})t)\langle\kern 1.29167ptE_{i}\kern 1.29167pt|\hat{q}|\kern 1.29167ptE_{j}\kern 1.29167pt\rangle.

(115)

In the formula above, we used the fact that the eigenvectors
|\kern 1.29167ptE_{i}\kern 1.29167pt\rangle
have real components in the
e^{(1)}_{n}
basis and the fact

q_{nm}=q_{mn},\penalty\ q_{nm}\in\mathbb{R}
. Let us recall that in our numerical calculations we limited the sum to finite number of terms.
Therefore the resulting function is oscillatory as a finite sum of cosine functions.

Similar analysis applies to
<\hat{p}>_{t}
giving trigonometric series.

We expect an oscillatory character if the spectrum is purely discrete. If the spectrum turns out to be continuous,
we expect that non-oscillatory functions could be achieved. In that case the sum in (
115
) is replaced by an integral
an the resulting function may be non-oscillatory. A typical example of such behaviour is a Fourier transform, which
decomposes non-oscillatory function into
oscillatory modes. However, full spectral analysis is beyond the scope of this paper.

VI
Conclusions

We used the coherent state quantization technique to investigate a quantum model
of a massive shell. We developed the new mathematical tools: we found a closed
formula for the basis elements
e^{(1)}_{n}
, and for the matrix elements of the
operators
\hat{q}
and
\hat{p}
in this basis. We studied the spectrum of the
quantum Hamiltonian operator numerically in the case
m=1.0
. We truncated the
operator spectrum and observed that some of the eigenvalues stabilize as we increase the
truncation.

Using calculated eigenstates, we built a Gaussian state peaked at energy

M=10^{-6}
.
We investigated
the evolution of the expectation values of
\hat{q}
and
\hat{p}
in the
Gaussian state and observed that the quantum shell bounces well above the
horizon. This leads to an oscillatory behaviour of the system which is in
contrast with the classical solution where the shell collapses and reaches the
Schwarzschild horizon
q_{H}=2M^{2}
in infinite time (as measured by an observer at infinity).
The oscillatory character of the evolution may be traced back to our construction of the
Gaussian state. More detailed analysis of this phenomenon needs a careful study of the
spectral properties of the Hamiltonian operator.

Our analysis of the quantum Hamiltonian is based on a series of
numerical experiments. The analytical analysis seems to be out of
range. According to these experiments it seems that this Hamiltonian, in the
investigated region, up to accuracy of our calculations (double accuracy)
has a continuous spectrum.

We realize that the research presented in this paper is not a complete
analysis. We developed the necessary tools and showed that this research
direction may lead to interesting results. In particular, the results concerning
oscillatory behaviour of the quantum system and the quantum origin of a bounce
above the horizon require independent cross-check. The integrals involved in the
expression of matrix elements of the Hamiltonian operator were calculationally
demanding. This resulted in rather limited size of the matrix and small value of
eigenvalues which can be reliably calculated. More efficient numerical
integration methods are needed in order to study the model in more details.
An advantage of our approach is that the quantum Hamiltonian
\hat{H}
we consider is
positive definite, which is generally true and independent on the chosen set of parameters.

The issue of the positivity of both classical and quantum Hamiltonians
are of special interest. At the classical level, it can be realized by
restricting the phase space. The quantum level is more demanding. In our
integral quantization method there is a one-to-one correspondence between the
phase space points and the space of coherent states. However, we cannot follow
the classical procedure of choosing the subspace of all coherent states to
obtain positivity of quantum Hamiltonian. The reason is that such procedure
would violate the mathematical consistency of the integral quantization and
its physical interpretation as deformation of POV measure which results in
problems with the probabilistic interpretation of this approach. Therefore, in the integral
defining the mapping of a classical observable into quantum observable one
cannot restrict the region of integration, but one can restrict the integrant by
taking
\penalty\ \theta(H)H\penalty\
instead of
H
. This way one obtains the quantum
Hamiltonian
\hat{H}
which still acts on the full Hilbert space but it is
positively defined because our quantization procedure maps positive functions
on the phase space into positive operators. Similar logic has been used, for
instance, in the paper
Tomasz
within loop quantum gravity,
where the dynamics is defined by a square root of positive part of quantum
gravitational scalar constraint operator.

To use this procedure consequently, the position and momentum quantum observables have
to be defined on the whole Hilbert space. They determine physical meaning of
the coherent states as quantum counterparts of the configuration space
points. Our procedure is consistent with treating the operators
\hat{M}(Q)
, see
Eq. ​(
28
), as the POV measure.

The basis of the Hilbert space

GM

where

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{(\alpha)}_{n}(x)e^{(\alpha)}_{m}(x)d\nu(x)=\delta_{nm}

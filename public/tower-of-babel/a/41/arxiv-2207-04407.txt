Banach property (T) for SL n ​ ( ℤ ) \rm SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z}) and its applications Thanks: The author was partially supported by ISF grant no. 293/18
Izhar Oppenheim

Abstract
. We prove that a large family of higher rank simple Lie groups (including SL n ​ ( ℝ ) \rm SL_{n}(\mathbb{R}) for n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 ) and their lattices have Banach property (T) with respect to all super-reflexive Banach spaces. Two consequences of this result are: First, we deduce Banach fixed point properties with respect to all super-reflexive Banach spaces for a large family of higher rank simple Lie groups. For example, we show that for every n ≥ 4 n\geq 4 , the group SL n ​ ( ℝ ) \rm SL_{n}(\mathbb{R}) and all its lattices have the Banach fixed point property with respect to all super-reflexive Banach spaces. Second, we settle a long standing open problem and show that the Margulis expanders (Cayley graphs of SL n ​ ( ℤ / m ​ ℤ ) \rm SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}) for a fixed n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 and m m tending to infinity) are super-expanders. All of our results stem from proving Banach property (T) for SL 3 ​ ( ℤ ) \rm SL_{3}(\mathbb{Z}) . Our method of proof for SL 3 ​ ( ℤ ) \rm SL_{3}(\mathbb{Z}) relies on a novel proof for relative Banach property (T) for the uni-triangular subgroup of SL 3 ⁡ ( ℤ ) \operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z}) . This proof of relative property (T) is new even in the classical Hilbert setting and is interesting in its own right.

1.
Introduction

Property (T) was introduced by Kazhdan in
[
Kaž67
]
as a tool to prove finite generation. Since then it was found useful for a wide range of applications in various different areas of mathematics (see
[
BdlHV08
]
and the introduction of
[
BFGM07
]
, and reference therein). We mention two such applications that are relevant in the context of this paper: First, property (T) for a group
G
is equivalent (under some mild assumptions on
G
) to property (FH) which states that every continuous isometric affine action of
G
on a real Hilbert space admits a fixed point. Second, Margulis gave the first explicit construction of expanders using property (T).

More recently, Bader, Furman, Gelander and Monod
[
BFGM07
]
defined a Banach version of property (T) (and its connection to Banach fixed point properties). They conjectured that higher rank algebraic group should have this form of Banach property (T) with respect to the class of all super-reflexive Banach spaces. Roughly simultaneously to the work of
[
BFGM07
]
, V. Lafforgue
[
Laf09
]
proved that groups of the form
\operatorname{SL}_{3}(F)
where
F
is a non-Archimedian local field have a strong form of Banach property (T) for large classes of Banach spaces and that this strong form of Banach property (T) implies the fix point property. In particular, his work corroborates the conjecture of
[
BFGM07
]
: Namely, a consequence of Lafforgue’s work it that the groups
\operatorname{SL}_{3}(F)
where
F
is a non-Archimedian local field have Banach property (T) and the fixed point property with respect to all super-reflexive Banach spaces. Later, Liao
[
Lia14
]
extended the work of Lafforgue and proved the strong version of Banach property (T) holds for every higher rank connected almost
F
-simple algebraic group, where
F
is a non-Archimedean local field. In his work
[
Laf09
]
, Lafforgue also showed how to use his result to construct super-expanders, i.e., families of graphs that are expanders with respect to every super-reflexive Banach space (see exact definition below).

Unlike in the non-Archimedian case, much less was known regarding Banach property (T) for algebraic groups over
\mathbb{R}
(and their lattices). In the paper of Bader, Furman, Gelander and Monod
[
BFGM07
]
they showed that higher rank algebraic groups have Banach property (T) (and fixed point properties) for
L^{p}
spaces where
1<p<\infty
(the case
p=1
was later resolved in
[
BGM12
]
). For general super-reflexive spaces (that are not
L^{p}
spaces), partial results were proven by de Laat, Mimura and de la Salle in various collaborations
[
dLdlS15
,
Sal16
,
dLMdlS16
,
dLdlS18
,
dlS19
]
. However, non of these works cover all super-reflexive spaces even for a single group of the form
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
with some
n\geq 3
.

In this paper, we make a major breakthrough regarding Banach property (T): We show that large family of connected simple (higher rank) Lie groups have Banach property (T) with respect to all super-reflexive Banach spaces. In particular, we show that for every
n\geq 3
, the groups
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
and
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
have Banach property (T) with respect to all super-reflexive Banach spaces. This has several striking consequences: First, it allows us to prove that a family of connected simple (higher rank) Lie groups and their lattices have the fixed point property with respect to all super-reflexive Banach spaces. In particular, we show that for every
n\geq 4
,
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
and all its lattices have the fixed point property with respect to all super-reflexive Banach spaces. Second, we settle a long standing open problem and show that the Margulis expanders (i.e., Cayley graphs of
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}/i\mathbb{Z})
where
n\geq 3
is a fixed integer) are super-expanders. Last, we show that for every
n\geq 5
, the groups
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
and
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
have a strengthening of the fixed point property (property
(FF)
defined below) with respect to all super-reflexive Banach spaces

Our method for proving Banach property (T) is also novel. The prior works of de Laat, Mimura and de la Salle mentioned above were based on generalizing the work of Lafforgue on strong (Hilbert) property (T) of
\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{R})
to the Banach setting. Our approach is very different: We first prove a relative version of Banach property (T) for the uni-triangular matrices in
\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
with respect to super-reflexive Banach spaces. We note that this proof is new even in the Hilbert setting. After that, we use a bounded generation argument à la Shalom to deduce Banach property (T) for
\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
for all super-reflexive spaces. Then using Howe-Moore we deduce Banach property
(T)
for simple Lie groups with whose Lie algebra contain
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}(\mathbb{R})
(see exact formulation below). We note that most of this proof scheme was known to experts, but prior to this work, it was not known how to prove the relative Banach property
(T)
result.

1.1.
Uniformly convex and super-reflexive Banach spaces

A Banach space
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
is called
uniformly convex
if there is a function
\delta:(0,2]\rightarrow(0,1]
called the
modulus of convexity
such that for every
0<\varepsilon\leq 2
and every
\xi,\eta\in\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
with
\|\xi\|=\|\eta\|=1
, if
\|\xi-\eta\|\geq\varepsilon
, then
\|\frac{\xi+\eta}{2}\|\leq(1-\delta(\varepsilon))
.

We will not recall the definition of super-reflexive Banach spaces, but only note that by
[
BL00
, Theorem A.6]
a Banach space
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
is super-reflexive if and only if there is a equivalent uniformly convex norm on
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
(a reader who is not familiar with super-reflexive Banach spaces can take this as a definition).

1.2.
Banach property (T) for
\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
and Simple Lie groups

Given a topological group
G
and a Banach space
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
, a
linear isometric representation of
G
on
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
is a continuous homomorphism
\pi:G\rightarrow O(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}})
, where
O(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}})
denotes the group of all invertible linear isometries of
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
with the strong operator topology. A linear isometric representation
\pi
is said to have
almost invariant vectors
if for every compact set
K\subseteq G
and every
\varepsilon>0
, there is a unit vector
\xi\in\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
such that

\sup_{g\in K}\|\pi(g)\xi-\xi\|<\varepsilon.

In
[
BFGM07
]
, Bader, Furman, Gelander and Monod defined Banach property (T) of a group
G
as follows:

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
be Banach space and
G
be a topological group. The group
G
has property
(T_{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}})
if for every continuous linear isometric representation
\pi:G\rightarrow O(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}})
, the quotient representation
\pi^{\prime}:G\rightarrow O(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}/\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{\pi(G)})
does not have almost invariant vectors.

The main result of this paper is proving Banach property
(T_{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}})
with respect to every super-reflexive Banach space
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
for
\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
be a super-reflexive Banach space. The group
\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
has property
(T_{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}})
.

Using Howe-Moore Theorem, this allows us to deduce the following theorem:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
G
be a connected simple real Lie group with a finite center and
\mathfrak{g}
the Lie algebra of
G
. If
\mathfrak{g}
contains
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}(\mathbb{R})
as a Lie sub-algebra, then
G
and all its lattices have property
(T_{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}})
for every super-reflexive Banach space
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
.

This corroborates a conjecture stated in
[
BFGM07
]
in which it was conjectured that all higher rank almost simple algebraic groups have property
(T_{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}})
for every super-reflexive Banach space
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
(see
[
BFGM07
, Remark 2.28]
). An immediate consequence is:

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
n\geq 3
and
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
be a super-reflexive Banach space. The group
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
and all its lattices have property
(T_{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}})
. In particular, for every
n\geq 3
and every super-reflexive Banach space
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
, the group
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
has property
(T_{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}})
.

1.3.
Applications

Banach fixed point property for simple Lie groups

Given a Banach space
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
, a topological group
G
is said to have property
(F_{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}})
is every affine (continuous) isometric action of
G
on
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
admits a fixed point.

As an application of Banach property
(T)
for
\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{R})
we prove:

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
G
be a connected simple real Lie group with a finite center and
\mathfrak{g}
be the Lie algebra of
G
. If
\mathfrak{g}
contains
\mathfrak{sl}_{4}(\mathbb{R})
as a Lie sub-algebra, then
G
and any lattice
\Gamma<G
have property
(F_{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}})
for every super-reflexive Banach space
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
.

Again, this corroborates a conjecture stated in
[
BFGM07
]
in which it was conjectured that all higher rank algebraic groups have property
(F_{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}})
for every super-reflexive Banach space
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
(see
[
BFGM07
, Conjecture 1.6]
).

The method of proof of Theorem
1.5
is as follows: First, using ideas of
[
BFGM07
]
, we show that Banach property
(T_{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}})
for
\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{R})
implies property
(F_{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}})
for
\operatorname{SL}_{4}(\mathbb{R})
. Second, via a (now standard) use of Maunter phenomenon, we deduce Theorem
1.5
.

Super-expanders

A family of finite graphs with uniformly bounded degree is called a
super-expander family
(or a
super-expander
) if it has a Poincaré inequality with respect to every super-reflexive Banach space (see exact definition in
section

9.2
). The first examples of super-expanders were constructed by Lafforgue in
[
Laf09
]
as a consequence of his work on strong Banach property (T) for
\operatorname{SL}_{3}(F)
, where
F
is a non-Archimedean local field. Since Lafforgue’s work there have been several constructions of super-expanders using two main techniques: Namely, the work of Mendel and Naor on non-linear spectral calculus
[
MN14
]
which gave a zig-zag construction for super-expanders. In a different direction, several works
[
Vig19
,
dLV19
,
FNvL19
,
Saw20
]
gave constructions using warped cones of groups actions arising from groups with Banach property (T).

It was an open problem to determine whether the Margulis expanders is a super-expander family, i.e., if for a fixed
n\geq 3
the Cayley graphs of
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}/i\mathbb{Z})
form a super-expander family. This open question appeared in the literature several times, including in Assaf Naor’s Minerva lecture
[
Nao13
]
where it was attributed to Margulis and in de la Salle’s 2022 ICM lecture
[
dlS22
, Conjecture 4.4]
(see also
[
Obe18
, Problem 5]
,
[
dLdlS18
]
,
[
Mim13
, Remark 5.3]
). Partial results were achieved by de Laat and de la Salle in
[
dLdlS18
]
, but up until our work, the problem remained open. As a consequence of our Theorem
1.4
, we settle this problem to the affirmative and prove the following:

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
n\geq 3
and
S
be a finite generating set of
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
(e.g.,
S=\{e_{i,j}(\pm 1):1\leq i,j\leq n,i\neq j\}
). Also, let
\phi_{i}:\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}/i\mathbb{Z})
be the natural surjective homomorphism for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
. Then the family of Cayley graphs of
\{(\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}/i\mathbb{Z}),\phi_{i}(S))\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is a super-expander family.

As noted above, one can construct super-expanders using warped cones arising from an action of a Banach property (T) group on a compact Riemannian manifold (see
[
Vig19
,
dLV19
,
FNvL19
,
Saw20
]
). Combining this machinery with our Theorem
1.4
also leads to a construction of super-expanders (see
section

9.2
for more details):

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
n\geq 3
and let
M
be a compact Riemannian manifold such that
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
acts on
M
by Lipschitz homeomorphisms.
For every increasing sequence
\{t_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{>0}
tending to infinity, the family
\{(M,d^{t_{i}}_{\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})})\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is quasi-isometric to a super-expander.

Banach property
(FF_{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}})

In
[
Mim11
]
, Mimura defined the notion of property
(FF_{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}})
(see exact definition in
section

9.3
below) that is a Banach version of property (TT) defined by Monod in
[
Mon01
]
. A result of de Laat, Mimura and de la Salle
[
dLMdlS16
]
allows one to deduce property
(FF_{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}})
for the groups
\operatorname{SL}_{n+2}(\mathbb{Z}),\operatorname{SL}_{n+2}(\mathbb{R})
from property
(T_{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}})
for the groups
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}),\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
. Thus, we can deduce the following:

Corollary 1.8
.

For every
n\geq 5
and every super-reflexive Banach space
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
, the groups
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}),\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
have property
(FF_{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}})
.

1.4.
Relative Banach property (T) for uni-triangular in
\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z})

Here we will outline the proof of Theorem
1.2
from which all our other results follow. The proof relies on a relative Banach property (T) argument that is novel even in the Hilbert setting.

Generalizing the definition of relative property (T) given in
[
Jol05
, Theorem 1.2 (b2)]
to the Banach setting, we will define relative Banach property (T) as follows:

Definition 1.9
.

Let
G
be a topological group and
H<G
be a subgroup. For a Banach space
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
, we will say that the pair
(G,H)
has relative Banach property
(T_{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}})
if for every continuous linear isometric representation
\pi:G\rightarrow O(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}})
and every constant
\gamma>0
, there is a compact set
K\subseteq G
and a constant
\varepsilon>0
such that for every unit vector
\xi\in\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
, if
\sup_{g\in K}\|\pi(g)\xi-\xi\|<\varepsilon
, then there exits
\eta\in\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{\pi(H)}
such that
\|\xi-\eta\|<\gamma
.

Remark 1.10
.

This definition is a-priori weaker than definition of weak relative Banach property (T) given in
[
dLMdlS16
]
and is strictly weaker than the definition of strong relative Banach property (T) given in
[
BFGM07
]
.

Let
\operatorname{UT}_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
and
\operatorname{LT}_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
denote the subgroups of uni-upper-triangular and uni-lower-triangular matrices in
\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
, i.e.,

\operatorname{UT}_{3}(\mathbb{Z})=\left\{\left(\begin{matrix}1&a&c\\
0&1&b\\
0&0&1\end{matrix}\right):a,b,c\in\mathbb{Z}\right\},

and

\operatorname{LT}_{3}(\mathbb{Z})=\left\{\left(\begin{matrix}1&0&0\\
a&1&0\\
c&b&1\end{matrix}\right):a,b,c\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}.

We prove the following (with respect to our definition of relative Banach property
(T)
stated above):

Theorem 1.11
.

For any super-reflexive Banach space
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
the pairs
(\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z}),\operatorname{UT}_{3}(\mathbb{Z}))
and
(\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z}),\operatorname{LT}_{3}(\mathbb{Z}))
both have relative property
(T_{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}})
.

The proof of this theorem is via defining a sequence of finitely supported probability measures
f_{n}
on
\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
and showing that for every super-reflexive Banach space
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
and every continuous isometric representation
\pi:\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow O(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}})
it holds that
\pi(f_{n})
converges in the norm topology and that the image of the limit operator is in
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{\pi(\operatorname{UT}_{3}(\mathbb{Z}))}
. We note that this method is new even in the classical Hilbert setting. Moreover, the proof is completely elementary in contrast with the more classical proofs of relative property (T), e.g., the proof that
(\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\ltimes\mathbb{Z}^{2},\mathbb{Z}^{2})
has relative property (T) in
[
BdlHV08
, Theorem 4.2.2]
requires Fourier analysis and projections valued measures while our proof requires neither.

Combining Theorem
1.11
with a bounded generation argument à la Shalom implies Theorem
1.2
.

Preliminaries

2.1.
Uniformly convexity

Below, we will state some needed facts regarding uniformly convex spaces.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
be a uniformly convex Banach space with a modulus of convexity
\delta:(0,2]\rightarrow(0,1]
and denote
O(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}})
to be the group of invertible linear isometries of
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
. Then for every
0<\varepsilon\leq 2
, every
S,T\in O(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}})
such that
TS=ST
and every
\xi\in\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
, if
\|(I-S)\xi\|\geq\varepsilon\|\xi\|
, then

\frac{1}{2}\left\|\frac{I+T}{2}\xi\right\|+\frac{1}{2}\left\|\frac{I+TS}{2}\xi\right\|\leq\max\left\{1-\frac{1}{2}\delta(\delta(\varepsilon)),1-\frac{1}{4}\delta(\varepsilon)\right\}\|\xi\|.

This proposition is probably well-known and we give the proof for completeness:

Proof.

Fix
\xi\in\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
and
0<\varepsilon\leq 2
, and assume that
\|(I-S)\xi\|\geq\varepsilon\|\xi\|
. If
\|(I-T)\xi\|\geq\delta(\varepsilon)
, then

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\|\frac{I+T}{2}\xi\right\|+\frac{1}{2}\left\|\frac{I+TS}{2}\xi\right\|\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\delta(\delta(\varepsilon))\right)\|\xi\|+\frac{1}{2}\|\xi\|=\left(1-\frac{1}{2}\delta(\delta(\varepsilon))\right)\|\xi\|,

as needed.

Otherwise,
\|\frac{I-T}{2}\xi\|\leq\frac{\delta(\varepsilon)}{2}
and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\|\frac{I+T}{2}\xi\right\|+\frac{1}{2}\left\|\frac{I+TS}{2}\xi\right\|\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\xi\|+\frac{1}{2}\left\|\frac{I+S}{2}\xi\right\|+\frac{1}{2}\left\|\frac{S(T-I)}{2}\xi\right\|\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|\xi\|+\frac{1}{2}(1-\delta(\varepsilon))\|\xi\|+\frac{1}{4}\delta(\varepsilon)\|\xi\|=\left(1-\frac{1}{4}\delta(\varepsilon)\right)\|\xi\|.

∎

We will be interested in classes of uniformly convex Banach spaces defined as follows: Let
\delta_{0}:(0,2]\rightarrow(0,1]
be a monotone increasing function. Denote
\mathcal{E}_{us}(\delta_{0})
to be the class of all uniformly convex Banach spaces
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
with such that the modulus of convexity of
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
is bounded by
\delta_{0}
, i.e., for a uniformly convex Banach space
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
with a modulus of convexity
\delta:(0,2]\rightarrow(0,1]
it holds that
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\in\mathcal{E}_{uc}(\delta_{0})
if and only if for every
0<\varepsilon\leq 2
it holds that
\delta(\varepsilon)\geq\delta_{0}(\varepsilon)
. For these classes of Banach space, we state the following immediate corollary of Proposition
2.1
:

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
\delta_{0}:(0,2]\rightarrow(0,1]
be a function and
0<\varepsilon\leq 2
be a constant. There is
r_{0}=r_{0}(\delta_{0},\varepsilon)
,
0\leq r_{0}<1
such that for every
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\in\mathcal{E}_{uc}(\delta_{0})
and every two commuting operators
S,T\in O(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}})
it holds for every
\xi\in\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
that if
\|(I-S)\xi\|\geq\varepsilon\|\xi\|
, then

\frac{1}{2}\left\|\frac{I+T}{2}\xi\right\|+\frac{1}{2}\left\|\frac{I+TS}{2}\xi\right\|\leq r_{0}\|\xi\|.

We will need the following theorems:

Theorem 2.3
.

[
Day41
, Theorem 3]

Let
\delta_{0}:(0,2]\rightarrow(0,1]
be a function and
\{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{uc}(\delta_{0})
a sequence. Then the
\ell^{2}
-sum
\bigoplus_{n}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}_{n}
is a uniformly convex Banach space.

Theorem 2.4
.

[
LT79
, Theorem 1.e.9]

Let
\delta_{0}:(0,2]\rightarrow(0,1]
be a function. There is a function
\delta_{0}^{\prime}:(0,2]\rightarrow(0,1]

that for every finite measure space
(X,\mu)
and every
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\in\mathcal{E}_{uc}(\delta_{0})
it holds that
L^{2}(X,\mu;\operatorname{\mathbb{E}})\in\mathcal{E}_{uc}(\delta_{0}^{\prime})
.

Remark 2.5
.

In the above theorem, we implicitly use the fact that
L^{2}(X,\mu;\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{*})
is isometrically isomorphic to
(L^{2}(X,\mu;\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}))^{*}
. This follows from
[
DU77
, Section IV.1, Theorem 1]
combined with the fact that reflexive Banach spaces (and in particular uniformly convex Banach spaces) have the Radon-Nikodým property (see
[
DU77
, Section III.2, Corollary 13]
).

2.2.
Linear Representation of groups on Banach spaces

Given a topological group
G
and a Banach space
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
, a
linear representation of
G
on
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
is a continuous homomorphism
\pi:G\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}})
, where
\operatorname{GL}(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}})
denotes the group of all invertible linear transformations of
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
with the strong operator topology. Throughout this paper,
\pi
will denote a continuous representation.

For a given linear representation of
G
on
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
, the contragredient representation is the map
\pi^{*}:G\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{*})
defined as

\forall g\in G,\forall\xi\in\operatorname{\mathbb{E}},\eta\in\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{*},\langle\pi(g)\xi,\eta\rangle=\langle\xi,\pi^{*}(g^{-1})\eta\rangle.

We note that if
\pi
is an isometric representation, then
\pi^{*}
is also isometric, but in general
\pi^{*}
need not be continuous. However, for every reflexive (and hence super-reflexive) Banach space
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
, if
\pi
is continuous, then so is
\pi^{*}
.

Below, we will need the following result from
[
BFGM07
]
:

Proposition 2.6
.

[
BFGM07
, Proposition 2.6]

Let
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
be a super-reflexive Banach space,
G
be a topological group and
\pi:G\rightarrow O(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}})
be a continuous linear isometric representation. Denote

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{\prime}(\pi)
to be the annihilator of
(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{*})^{\pi^{*}(G)}
in
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
, i.e.,

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{\prime}(\pi)=\{\xi\in\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}:\forall\eta\in(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{*})^{\pi^{*}(G)},\langle\xi,\eta\rangle=0\}.

Then
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{\pi(G)}\oplus\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{\prime}(\pi)
.

2.3.
Steinberg relations in
\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z})

For
1\leq i,j\leq 3,i\neq j
and
m\in\mathbb{Z}
, denote
e_{i,j}(m)
to be the elementary matrix with
1
’s along the main diagonal,
m
in the
(i,j)
-entry and
0
in all other entries. Using the convention
[a,b]=a^{-1}b^{-1}ab
, the group
\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
has the following relations that are called the
Steinberg relations
:

(1)

For every
1\leq i,j\leq 3,i\neq j
and every
m_{1},m_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
,

e_{i,j}(m_{1})e_{i,j}(m_{2})=e_{i,j}(m_{1}+m_{2}).

(2)

For every
1\leq i,j,k\leq 3,\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}
and every
m_{1},m_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
,

[e_{i,j}(m_{1}),e_{j,k}(m_{2})]=e_{i,k}(m_{1}m_{2}).

(3)

For every
1\leq i,j,k\leq 3,\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}
and every
m_{1},m_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
,

[e_{i,j}(m_{1}),e_{i,k}(m_{2})]=[e_{j,i}(m_{1}),e_{k,i}(m_{2})]=I.

The group
\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
has other relations that do not stem from the Steinberg relations. Forgetting the other relations of
\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
yields the Steinberg group
\operatorname{St}_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
. Explicitly, the Steinberg group
\operatorname{St}_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
is the group generated by the set
S=\{x_{i,j}:1\leq i,j\leq 3,i\neq j\}
with the following relations: For every
m\in\mathbb{Z}
, denote
x_{i,j}(m)=x_{i,j}^{m}
. With this notation, the relations defining
\operatorname{St}_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
are:

(1)

For every
1\leq i,j\leq 3,i\neq j
and every
m_{1},m_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
,

x_{i,j}(m_{1})x_{i,j}(m_{2})=x_{i,j}(m_{1}+m_{2}).

(2)

For every
1\leq i,j,k\leq 3,\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}
and every
m_{1},m_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
,

[x_{i,j}(m_{1}),x_{j,k}(m_{2})]=x_{i,k}(m_{1}m_{2}).

(3)

For every
1\leq i,j,k\leq 3,\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}
and every
m_{1},m_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
,

[x_{i,j}(m_{1}),x_{i,k}(m_{2})]=[x_{j,i}(m_{1}),x_{k,i}(m_{2})]=I.

2.4.
The Heisenberg group
\rm H_{3}(\mathbb{Z})

The Heisenberg group
\rm H_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
is the group

\rm H_{3}(\mathbb{Z})=\langle\left.x,y,z\right|[x,y]=z,[x,z]=e,[y,z]=e\rangle.

Below, we will use the following relations for the Heisenberg group that are not hard verify: for every
k,m\in\mathbb{Z}
it holds that

y^{-k}x^{m}y^{k}=x^{m}z^{km}
and
x^{-k}y^{m}x^{k}=y^{m}z^{-km}
.

In the sequel, we will use the fact that
\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
(and
\operatorname{St}_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
) contain several copies of
\rm H_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
. Explicitly, for every
\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}
, if denote
\tilde{x}=e_{i,j}(1),\tilde{y}=e_{j,k}(1),\tilde{z}=e_{i,k}(1)\in\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
,
then
\langle\tilde{x},\tilde{y},\tilde{z}\rangle<\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
is isomorphic to
\rm H_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
(by the Steinberg relations) via the isomorphism
x\mapsto\tilde{x},y\mapsto\tilde{y},z\mapsto\tilde{z}
.

3.
Banach property (T)

3.1.
Banach property
(T)
for super-reflexive Banach spaces

Bader, Furman, Gelander and Monod
[
BFGM07
]
gave an equivalent version to Banach property (T) for super-reflexive spaces that is more convenient to work with than their general definition. In
[
BFGM07
]
, it is shown that if
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
is a super-reflexive Banach space and
\pi:G\rightarrow O(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}})
is a linear isometric representation, then one can pass to a compatible norm on
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
in which
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
is uniformly convex and
\pi
remains a linear isometric representation with respect to this new norm. It follows that for a given topological group
G
the following are equivalent:

•

The group
G
has property
(T_{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}})
for every uniformly convex Banach space
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
.

•

The group
G
has property
(T_{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}})
for every super-reflexive Banach space
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
.

Thus, below we will focus on property
(T_{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}})
for uniformly convex Banach spaces
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
and the general result for super-reflexive Banach spaces will follow.

For uniformly convex Banach spaces,
[
BFGM07
]
gave the following equivalent definition for property
(T_{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}})
:

Definition 3.1
.

[
BFGM07
, Remark 2.11]

Let
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
be uniformly convex space and
G
be a topological group. Denote

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{\prime}(\pi)
to be the annihilator of
(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{*})^{\pi^{*}(G)}
in
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
, i.e.,

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{\prime}(\pi)=\{\xi\in\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}:\forall\eta\in(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{*})^{\pi^{*}(G)},\langle\xi,\eta\rangle=0\}.

The group
G
has property
(T_{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}})
if for every continuous linear isometric representation
\pi:G\rightarrow O(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}})
, the restricted representation
\pi^{\prime}:G\rightarrow O(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{\prime}(\pi))
does not have almost invariant vectors, i.e., there is a Kazhdan pair
(K,\varepsilon)
(that depends on
\pi
) where
K\subseteq G
is compact and
\varepsilon>0
such that for every vector
\xi\in\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{\prime}(\pi)
it holds that

\sup_{g\in K}\|\pi^{\prime}(g)\xi-\xi\|\geq\varepsilon\|\xi\|.

Observation 3.2
.

Let
G
be a topological group. By the above definition the following are equivalent:

(1)

The group
G
has property
(T_{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}})
for every uniformly convex Banach space
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
.

(2)

For every uniformly convex Banach space
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
and every isometric representation
\pi:G\rightarrow O(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}})
with
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{\pi(G)}=\{0\}
, there is a compact set
K\subseteq G
and a constant
\varepsilon>0
such that for every unit vector
\xi\in\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
,

\sup_{g\in K}\|\pi(g)\xi-\xi\|\geq\varepsilon\|\xi\|.

3.2.
Relative Banach property (T)

Here we introduce a variation of relative Banach property (T) called relative Banach property
(T^{\operatorname{proj}})
that can be seen as a generalization of the definition of Banach property
(T^{\operatorname{proj}})
given in
[
dLdlS18
]
. We will show that relative Banach property
(T^{\operatorname{proj}})
is a-priori stronger than the definition of relative Banach property (T) given in the introduction (we do not know if the two definitions do in fact coincide - see Remark
3.5
below). In order to define relative Banach property
(T^{\operatorname{proj}})
, we will need to first introduce some notation and terminology.

Let
G
be a locally compact group with Haar measure
\mu
. We denote
C_{c}(G)
to be the compactly supported continuous functions
f:G\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
with the convolution product. We further denote
\operatorname{Prob}_{c}(G)\subseteq C_{c}(G)
to be functions
f:G\rightarrow[0,\infty)
such that

\int_{G}f(g)d\mu(g)=1
. Given a continuous representation
\pi:G\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}})
where
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
is a Banach space, we define for every
f\in C_{c}(G)
an operator
\pi(f)
via the Bochner integral

\pi(f)\xi=\int_{G}f(g)\pi(g)\xi d\mu(g),\forall\xi\in\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}.

For a class of Banach spaces
\mathcal{E}
, we denote
\mathcal{U}(G,\mathcal{E})
to be the class of all continuous isometric linear representations
(\pi,\operatorname{\mathbb{E}})
where
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\in\mathcal{E}
. When
G
is obvious from the context, we will denote
\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E})=\mathcal{U}(G,\mathcal{E})
. We define a norm
\|.\|_{\mathcal{U}(G,\mathcal{E})}
on
C_{c}(G)
by

\|f\|_{\mathcal{U}(G,\mathcal{E})}=\sup_{\pi\in\mathcal{U}(G,\mathcal{E})}\|\pi(f)\|,

and denote
C_{\mathcal{U}(G,\mathcal{E})}(G)
to be the completion of
C_{c}(G)
with respect to this norm. We note that for every
f\in C_{\mathcal{U}(G,\mathcal{E})}(G)
and every
\pi\in\mathcal{U}(G,\mathcal{E})
, the operator
\pi(f)\in B(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}})
is well-defined as a limit of operators
\pi(f_{n})
with
f_{n}\in C_{c}(G)
.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
G
be a locally compact group with a subgroup
H<G
. We will say that
(G,H)
has relative property
(T_{\mathcal{E}}^{\operatorname{proj}})
if there is a sequence
h_{n}\in\operatorname{Prob}_{c}(G)
that converges to
f\in C_{\mathcal{U}(G,\mathcal{E})}(G)
(with respect to the norm
\|.\|_{\mathcal{U}(G,\mathcal{E})}
) such that for every
(\pi,\operatorname{\mathbb{E}})\in\mathcal{U}(G,\mathcal{E})
,
\operatorname{Im}(\pi(f))\subseteq\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{\pi(H)}
.

We show that relative property
(T^{\operatorname{proj}}_{\mathcal{E}})
imply relative property
(T_{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}})
for every
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\in\mathcal{E}
as defined above (see Definition
1.9
):

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
G
be a locally compact group,
H<G
a subgroup and
\mathcal{E}
a class of Banach spaces. Assume that
(G,H)
has relative property
(T^{\operatorname{proj}}_{\mathcal{E}})
. Then for every
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\in\mathcal{E}
, the pair
(G,H)
has relative property
(T_{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}})
.

Proof.

We need to show that for every
\gamma>0
, there are
K\subseteq G
compact and
\varepsilon>0
, such that for every
(\pi,\operatorname{\mathbb{E}})\in\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E})
and every unit vector
\xi\in\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
, if
\sup_{g\in K}\|\pi(g)\xi-\xi\|<\varepsilon
, then there is
\eta\in\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{\pi(H)}
such that
\|\xi-\eta\|<\gamma
.

Let
\gamma>0
arbitrary. By the assumption that
(G,H)
has relative property
(T^{\operatorname{proj}}_{\mathcal{E}})
it follows that there is a sequence
h_{n}\in\operatorname{Prob}_{c}(G)
that converges to
f\in C_{\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E})}(G)
such that for every
(\pi,\operatorname{\mathbb{E}})\in\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E})
,
\operatorname{Im}(\pi(f))\subseteq\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{\pi(H)}
.

For
f\in C_{\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E})}(G)
as above, there is
h\in\operatorname{Prob}_{c}(G)
such that
\|f-h\|_{\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E})}<\frac{\gamma}{2}
. We take
K
to be a compact set such that
\operatorname{supp}(h)\subseteq K
and
\varepsilon=\frac{\gamma}{2}
and show that this choice of
K,\varepsilon
fulfils the needed condition.

Indeed, for every
(\pi,\operatorname{\mathbb{E}})\in\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E})
and every unit vector
\xi\in\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
, if
\sup_{g\in K}\|\pi(g)\xi-\xi\|<\varepsilon=\frac{\gamma}{2}
, then for
\eta=\pi(f)\xi\in\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{\pi(H)}
it holds that

\|\xi-\eta\|\leq\|\xi-\pi(h)\xi\|+\|\pi(h)\xi-\eta\|<\left\|\int_{G}h(g)(\xi-\pi(g)\xi)d\mu(g)\right\|+\frac{\gamma}{2}\leq\\
{\int_{G}h(g)\left\|\xi-\pi(g)\xi\right\|d\mu(g)}+\frac{\gamma}{2}\leq\max_{g\in\operatorname{supp}(h)}\left\|\xi-\pi(g)\xi\right\|+\frac{\gamma}{2}\leq\gamma

as needed.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

We do not know if the opposite direction of the above proposition is also true, i.e., if relative property
(T_{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}})
for every
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\in\mathcal{E}
implies property
(T^{\operatorname{proj}}_{\mathcal{E}})
. The problem is that even in the classical setting of Hilbert spaces there is not natural candidate for the sequence
h_{n}\in\operatorname{Prob}_{c}(G)
. To illustrate this, we consider what should be a simple situation: Let
G
be a finitely generating group with a finite generating set
S
and
N\triangleleft G
a normal subgroup and
\mathcal{H}
be the class of all Hilbert spaces.

We recall that from that fact that
N
is a normal subgroup it follows for every unitary
(\pi,\mathbb{H})
of
G
on a Hilbert space
\mathbb{H}
the subspaces
\mathbb{H}^{\pi(N)},(\mathbb{H}^{\pi(N)})^{\perp}
are
G
-invariant subspaces with respect to the
G
action. In this setting, relative property
(T)
for
(G,N)
can be described by the following formulation in
[
Jol05
, Theorem 1.2 (b2)]
: There is
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that for every
\alpha>0
, every unitary representation
(\pi,\mathbb{H})
of
G
on a Hilbert space
\mathbb{H}
and every unit vector
\xi\in\mathbb{H}
, if

\max_{s\in S}\|\pi(s)\xi-\xi\|\leq\alpha\varepsilon_{0}

then
\|\xi-P_{\mathbb{H}^{\pi(N)}}\xi\|\leq\alpha
where
P_{\mathbb{H}^{\pi(N)}}
is the orthogonal projection on
\mathbb{H}^{\pi(N)}
.

Our naive guess for
h_{n}\in\operatorname{Prob}_{c}(G)
is the sequence

h_{n}=\left(\frac{1}{2}I+\frac{1}{2|S|}\sum_{s\in S}s\right)^{n},

(which is the sequence that converges to a Kazhdan projection when
N=G
). For every unitary representation
(\pi,\mathbb{H})
,
\left.\pi\right|_{(\mathbb{H}^{\pi(N)})^{\perp}}(h_{n})
indeed converges to
0
and the rate of convergence can be bounded independently of
\pi
. However, we see no reason that
\left.\pi\right|_{\mathbb{H}^{\pi(N)}}(h_{n})
will converge when
N\neq G
and thus (as far as we can tell) this naive attempt fails.

Proposition 3.6
.

4.
Bounded generation and Banach property (T)

In this section, we adapt a bounded generation argument of Shalom
[
Sha99
]
to our setting and show that, in our setting, relative Banach property (T) and bounded generation imply Banach property (T).

Definition 4.1
.

Let
G
be a group with subgroups
H_{1},...,H_{k}
. We say that
H_{1},...,H_{k}
boundedly generate
G
if there is a number
\nu=\nu(H_{1},...,H_{k})\in\mathbb{N}
such that every element
g\in G
can be written by at most
\nu
elements of
H_{1}\cup...\cup H_{k}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
G
be a group with subgroups
H_{1},...,H_{k}
that boundedly generate
G
and denote
\nu=\nu(H_{1},...,H_{k})
as above. Also, let
\pi:G\rightarrow O(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}})
be a continuous linear isometric representation. Assume that there are
\eta_{1},...,\eta_{k}\in\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
such that for every
1\leq i\leq k
,
\eta_{i}\in\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{\pi(H_{i})}
. Then for every
\xi\in\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
and every
g\in G
,

\|\pi(g)\xi-\xi\|\leq 2\nu\max_{1\leq i\leq k}\|\xi-\eta_{i}\|.

Proof.

Let
g\in G
such that
g=g_{1}...g_{j}
with
g_{1},...,g_{j}\in\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}H_{i}
. We will prove by induction that for every
\xi\in\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
,

(1)

\|\pi(g)\xi-\xi\|\leq 2j\max_{1\leq i\leq k}\|\xi-\eta_{i}\|.

For
j=1
, there is
1\leq i_{0}\leq k
such that
g\in H_{i_{0}}
. Then

\displaystyle\|\pi(g)\xi-\xi\|=\|\pi(g)\xi-\pi(g)\eta_{i_{0}}+\eta_{i_{0}}-\xi\|\leq\|\pi(g)(\xi-\eta_{i_{0}})\|+\|\eta_{i_{0}}-\xi\|=

\displaystyle 2\|\xi-\eta_{i_{0}}\|\leq 2\max_{1\leq i\leq k}\|\xi-\eta_{i}\|.

Assume (
1
) holds for
j
and let
g=g_{1}...g_{j+1}
with
g_{1},...,g_{j+1}\in\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}H_{i}
. Then for every
\xi\in\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
,

\displaystyle\|\pi(g)\xi-\xi\|=\|\pi(g_{1}...g_{j}g_{j+1})\xi-\pi(g_{1}...g_{j})\xi+\pi(g_{1}...g_{j})\xi-\xi\|\leq

\displaystyle\|\pi(g_{1}...g_{j})(\pi(g_{j+1})\xi-\xi)\|+\|\pi(g_{1}...g_{j})\xi-\xi\|=

\displaystyle\|(\pi(g_{j+1})\xi-\xi)\|+\|\pi(g_{1}...g_{j})\xi-\xi\|\leq^{\text{The induction assumption}}

\displaystyle 2\max_{1\leq i\leq k}\|\xi-\eta_{i}\|+2j\max_{1\leq i\leq k}\|\xi-\eta_{i}\|=2(j+1)\max_{1\leq i\leq k}\|\xi-\eta_{i}\|.

By the assumption of bounded generation, every
g\in G
can be written as
g=g_{1}...g_{\nu}
with
g_{1},...,g_{\nu}\in\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}H_{i}
and thus it follows that for every
g\in G
and every
\xi\in\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
,

\|\pi(g)\xi-\xi\|\leq 2\nu\max_{1\leq i\leq k}\|\xi-\eta_{i}\|,

as needed.
∎

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
G
be a locally compact group and
H_{1},...,H_{k}<G
subgroups that boundedly generate
G
. If the pairs
(G,H_{1}),...,(G,H_{k})
has relative property
(T_{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}})
for every uniformly convex Banach space
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
, then
G
has property
(T_{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}})
for every uniformly convex Banach space
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
.

Proof.

By Observation
3.2
, we need to show that for every uniformly convex Banach space
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
and every
\pi:G\rightarrow O(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}})
with
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{\pi(G)}=\{0\}
there is a compact set
K\subseteq G
and
\varepsilon>0
such that for every unit vector
\xi\in\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
,

\sup_{g\in K}\|\pi(g)\xi-\xi\|\geq\varepsilon.

Denote
\nu=\nu(H_{1},...,H_{k})\in\mathbb{N}
as in the definition above. By assumption, there are compact sets
K_{1},...,K_{k}
and constants
\varepsilon_{1},...,\varepsilon_{k}>0
such that for every
i=1,...,k
and every unit vector
\xi\in\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
if

\sup_{g\in K_{i}}\|\pi(g)\xi-\xi\|<\varepsilon_{i},

then there is
\eta_{i}\in\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{\pi(H_{i})}
such that

\|\xi-\eta_{i}\|\leq\frac{1}{4(\nu+1)}.

Denote
K=\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}K_{i}
and
\varepsilon=\min\{\varepsilon_{1},...,\varepsilon_{k}\}
. We will show that for this choice of
K,\varepsilon
it holds for every unit vector
\xi\in\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
that

\sup_{g\in K}\|\pi(g)\xi-\xi\|\geq\varepsilon.

Assume towards contradiction that there is a unit vector
\xi\in\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
such that

\sup_{g\in K}\|\pi(g)\xi-\xi\|<\varepsilon.

Thus, for every
i=1,...,k
there is
\eta_{i}\in\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{\pi(H_{i})}
such that
\|\xi-\eta_{i}\|<\frac{1}{4(\nu+1)}
. Applying Lemma
4.2
, it follows that for every
g\in G
,

\|\pi(g)\xi-\xi\|\leq\frac{\nu}{2(\nu+1)}\leq\frac{1}{2}.

Thus the orbit of
\xi
in
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
under the action of
G
is contained in a closed ball of radius
\frac{1}{2}
around
\xi
. Denote
C
to be the closure of the convex hull of the orbit of
\xi
. Recall that
\xi
is a unit vector and thus
0\notin C
. By uniform convexity there is a unique vector with a minimal norm in
C
and thus this vector is fixed by the
\pi
action of
G
. It follows that
C\cap\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{\pi(G)}\neq\emptyset
which contradicts the assumption that
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{\pi(G)}\neq\{0\}
.
∎

5.
Averaging operations on
\rm H_{3}(\mathbb{Z})

In this section, we will prove norm bounds on averaging operations on the Heisenberg group that are needed in our proof of relative Banach property (T) stated in the introduction.

For every
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, we define
X_{k},Y_{k},Z_{k}\in\operatorname{Prob}_{c}(\rm H_{3}(\mathbb{Z}))
by

X_{k}=\frac{e+x^{2^{k}}}{2},Y_{k}=\frac{e+y^{2^{k}}}{2},Z_{k}=\frac{e+z^{2^{k}}}{2}.

Also, for
d\in\mathbb{N}
, we define

X^{d}=\frac{1}{2^{d}}\sum_{a=0}^{2^{d}-1}x^{a},Y^{d}=\frac{1}{2^{d}}\sum_{b=0}^{2^{d}-1}y^{b},Z^{d}=\frac{1}{2^{d}}\sum_{c=0}^{2^{d}-1}z^{c}.

Observation 5.1
.

For every
d\in\mathbb{N}
it holds that

X^{d}=\prod_{a=0}^{d-1}X_{a},Y^{d}=\prod_{b=0}^{d-1}Y_{b},Z^{d}=\prod_{c=0}^{d-1}Z_{c}.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
be a constant and
A,B\subseteq\{0,...,d-1\}
be sets such that
(\max A)(\max B)\leq d-2
, then for every Banach space
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
and every isometric linear representation
\pi:H_{3}(\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow O(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}})
it holds that

\left\|\pi\left(\left(\left(\prod_{a\in A}X_{a}\right)\left(\prod_{b\in B}Y_{b}\right)-\left(\prod_{b\in B}Y_{b}\right)\left(\prod_{a\in A}X_{a}\right)\right)Z^{d}\right)\right\|\leq 8\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{d-\max A-\max B}.

In particular, for
d_{1},d_{2},d_{3}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, if
d_{1}+d_{2}\leq d_{3}-2
, then for any class of Banach spaces
\mathcal{E}
it holds that

\left\|\left(X^{d_{1}}Y^{d_{2}}-Y^{d_{2}}X^{d_{1}}\right)Z^{d}\right\|_{\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E})}\leq 8\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{d_{3}-(d_{1}+d_{2})}.

Proof.

We note that

\displaystyle\left(\prod_{a\in A}X_{a}\right)\left(\prod_{b\in B}Y_{b}\right)-\left(\prod_{b\in B}Y_{b}\right)\left(\prod_{a\in A}X_{a}\right)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{|A|+|B|}}\sum_{f\in\{0,1\}^{A},h\in\{0,1\}^{B}}x^{\sum_{k\in A}f(k)2^{k}}y^{\sum_{l\in B}h(l)2^{l}}-y^{\sum_{l\in B}h(l)2^{l}}x^{\sum_{k\in A}f(k)2^{k}}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{|A|+|B|}}\sum_{f\in\{0,1\}^{A},h\in\{0,1\}^{B}}x^{\sum_{k\in A}f(k)2^{k}}y^{\sum_{l\in B}h(l)2^{l}}(e-z^{(\sum_{k\in A}f(k)2^{k})(\sum_{l\in B}h(l)2^{l})}).

Note that for every
f\in\{0,1\}^{A},h\in\{0,1\}^{B}
it holds that

(\sum_{k\in A}f(k)2^{k})(\sum_{l\in B}h(l)2^{l})\leq 2^{\max A+\max B+2}.

Thus, it is enough to show that for every
1\leq m\leq 2^{\max A+\max B+2}
it holds that

\|\pi((e-z^{m})Z^{d})\|\leq\frac{2m}{2^{d}}\leq\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{d-\max A-\max B-3},

but this follows immediately from the fact that

(e-z^{m})Z^{d}=\frac{1}{2^{d}}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}z^{k}-\sum_{k=2^{d}}^{2^{d}+m-1}z^{k}\right).

∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\delta_{0}:(0,2]\rightarrow(0,1]
be a function. There is a constant
0\leq r_{0}<1
such that for every
(\pi,\operatorname{\mathbb{E}})\in\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E}_{uc}(\delta_{0}))
, every
k,m\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
and every
\zeta\in\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
, if
\|\pi(e-z^{m})\zeta\|\geq\frac{1}{2}\|\zeta\|
, then

\left\|\pi\left(\frac{e+xz^{k}}{2}\frac{e+y^{m}}{2}\right)\zeta\right\|\leq r_{0}\|\zeta\|.

Proof.

We will show that the needed inequality holds for
r_{0}=r_{0}(\delta_{0},\frac{1}{2})
where this is the constant of Corollary
2.2
.

Fix
(\pi,\operatorname{\mathbb{E}})\in\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E}_{uc}(\delta_{0}))
,
k,m\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
and
\zeta\in\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
such that
\|\pi(e-z^{m})\zeta\|\geq\frac{1}{2}\|\zeta\|
.

We note that

\displaystyle\frac{e+xz^{k}}{2}\frac{e+y^{m}}{2}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{e+xz^{k}}{2}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{y^{m}+xy^{m}z^{k}}{2}\right)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{e+xz^{k}}{2}\right)+\frac{y^{m}}{2}\left(\frac{e+y^{-m}xy^{m}z^{k}}{2}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{e+xz^{k}}{2}\right)+\frac{y^{m}}{2}\left(\frac{e+xz^{k}z^{m}}{2}\right).

Thus

\displaystyle\left\|\pi\left(\frac{e+xz^{k}}{2}\frac{e+y^{m}}{2}\right)\zeta\right\|\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\|\pi\left(\frac{e+xz^{k}}{2}\right)\zeta\right\|+\frac{1}{2}\left\|\pi(y^{m})\pi\left(\frac{e+xz^{k}z^{m}}{2}\right)\zeta\right\|\leq^{\|\pi(y^{m})\|=1}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\|\frac{I+\pi(xz^{k})}{2}\zeta\right\|+\frac{1}{2}\left\|\frac{I+\pi(xz^{k})\pi(z^{m})}{2}\zeta\right\|.

Denote
T=\pi(xz^{k})
and
S=\pi(z^{m})
. Note that
T,S\in O(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}})
are commuting operators and that
\|(I-S)\zeta\|\geq\frac{1}{2}\|\zeta\|
. Thus the conditions of Corollary
2.2
and it follows that

\frac{1}{2}\left\|\frac{I+\pi(xz^{k})}{2}\zeta\right\|+\frac{1}{2}\left\|\frac{I+\pi(xz^{k})\pi(z^{m})}{2}\zeta\right\|=\frac{1}{2}\left\|\frac{I+T}{2}\zeta\right\|+\frac{1}{2}\left\|\frac{I+TS}{2}\zeta\right\|\leq r_{0}\|\zeta\|,

as needed.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
\delta_{0}:(0,2]\rightarrow(0,1]
be a function. There is a constant
0\leq r_{1}<1
such that for every
(\pi,\operatorname{\mathbb{E}})\in\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E}_{uc}(\delta_{0}))
, every
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
1\leq m\leq 2^{n-1}
and every
\zeta\in\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
, if
\|\pi(e-z^{m})\zeta\|\geq\frac{1}{2}\|\zeta\|
, then

\left\|\pi\left(X_{0}Y^{n}\right)\zeta\right\|\leq r_{1}\|\zeta\|.

Proof.

Fix
(\pi,\operatorname{\mathbb{E}})\in\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E}_{uc}(\delta_{0}))
and
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
as above. Let
0\leq r_{0}<1
be the constant of Lemma
5.3
. We will show that for
r_{1}=\frac{r_{0}+1}{2}
the needed inequality holds.

Fix
\zeta\in\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
such that
\|\pi(e-z^{m})\zeta\|\geq\frac{1}{2}\|\zeta\|
.

Denote
t=\lfloor\frac{2^{n}}{2m}\rfloor
and
k=2^{n}-2mt
and note that
t\geq 1,0\leq k\leq\min\{2^{n-1},2m-1\}
and
2^{n}=2mt+k
. It follows that

\displaystyle Y^{n}=\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{b=0}^{2^{n}-1}y^{b}=\frac{2mt}{2^{n}}\left(\frac{1}{2mt}\sum_{b=0}^{2mt-1}y^{b}\right)+\frac{k}{2^{n}}\left(\frac{1}{k}\sum_{b=2mt}^{2^{n}-1}y^{b}\right).

We claim it is sufficient to prove that

(2)

\left\|\pi\left(X_{0}\left(\frac{1}{2mt}\sum_{b=0}^{2mt-1}y^{b}\right)\right)\zeta\right\|\leq r_{0}\|\zeta\|.

Indeed, note that

\left\|\pi\left(X_{0}\left(\frac{1}{k}\sum_{b=2mt}^{2^{n}-1}y^{b}\right)\right)\right\|\leq 1

and thus if (
2
) holds, then

\displaystyle\left\|\pi\left(X_{0}Y^{n}\right)\zeta\right\|\leq\frac{2mt}{2^{n}}\left\|\pi\left(X_{0}\left(\frac{1}{2mt}\sum_{b=0}^{2mt-1}y^{b}\right)\right)\zeta\right\|+\frac{k}{2^{n}}\left\|\pi\left(X_{0}\left(\frac{1}{k}\sum_{b=2mt}^{2^{n}-1}y^{b}\right)\right)\zeta\right\|\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{2mt}{2^{n}}r_{0}+\frac{k}{2^{n}}\right)\|\zeta\|\leq^{k\leq 2^{n-1}}\frac{r_{0}+1}{2}\|\zeta\|,

as needed.

We will finish the proof by proving (
2
). We note that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2mt}\sum_{b=0}^{2mt-1}y^{b}=\left(\frac{1}{m}\sum_{b_{1}=0}^{m-1}y^{b_{1}}\right)\left(\frac{1}{t}\sum_{b_{2}=0}^{t-1}y^{2mb_{2}}\right)\left(\frac{e+y^{m}}{2}\right).

Thus,

\displaystyle X_{0}\left(\frac{1}{2mt}\sum_{b=0}^{2mt-1}y^{b}\right)=\frac{e+x}{2}\left(\frac{1}{m}\sum_{b_{1}=0}^{m-1}y^{b_{1}}\right)\left(\frac{1}{t}\sum_{b_{2}=0}^{t-1}y^{2mb_{2}}\right)\left(\frac{e+y^{m}}{2}\right)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{mt}\sum_{b_{1}=0}^{m-1}\sum_{b_{2}=0}^{t-1}y^{b_{1}+2mb_{2}}\frac{e+y^{-(b_{1}+2mb_{2})}xy^{b_{1}+2mb_{2}}}{2}\left(\frac{e+y^{m}}{2}\right)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{mt}\sum_{b_{1}=0}^{m-1}\sum_{b_{2}=0}^{t-1}y^{b_{1}+2mb_{2}}\frac{e+xz^{b_{1}+2mb_{2}}}{2}\left(\frac{e+y^{m}}{2}\right).

Using the fact that
\|\pi(y^{b_{1}+2mb_{2}})\|=1
for every
b_{1},b_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
, it follows that

\displaystyle\left\|\pi\left(X_{0}\left(\frac{1}{2mt}\sum_{b=0}^{2mt-1}y^{b}\right)\right)\zeta\right\|\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{mt}\sum_{b_{1}=0}^{m-1}\sum_{b_{2}=0}^{t-1}\left\|\pi\left(\frac{e+xz^{b_{1}+2mb_{2}}}{2}\right)\pi\left(\frac{e+y^{m}}{2}\right)\zeta\right\|\leq^{\text{Lemma }\ref{product inequality lemma}}\frac{1}{mt}\sum_{b_{1}=0}^{m-1}\sum_{b_{2}=0}^{t-1}r_{0}\|\zeta\|=r_{0}\|\zeta\|,

and the proof of (
2
) is concluded.
∎

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
\delta_{0}:(0,2]\rightarrow(0,1]
be a function. Let
0\leq r_{1}<1
be the constant given in Lemma
5.4
above. For every
(\pi,\operatorname{\mathbb{E}})\in\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E}_{uc}(\delta_{0}))
, every
n\in\mathbb{N},n\geq 2
and every
\xi\in\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
, it holds that

\left\|\pi\left(X_{0}Y^{n}(e-Z_{0})\right)\xi\right\|\leq\max\left\{r_{1}\|\pi(e-Z_{0})\xi\|,\frac{1}{2^{n-2}}\|\xi\|\right\}.

Proof.

Fix
(\pi,\operatorname{\mathbb{E}})\in\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E}_{uc}(\delta_{0}))
,
n\in\mathbb{N},n\geq 2
and
\xi\in\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
. If

\|\pi(e-Z_{0})\xi\|\leq\frac{1}{2^{n-2}}\|\xi\|
, then

\left\|\pi\left(X_{0}Y^{n}(e-Z_{0})\right)\xi\right\|\leq\|\pi(e-Z_{0})\xi\|\leq\frac{1}{2^{n-2}}\|\xi\|,

and we are done.

Assume that
\|\pi(e-Z_{0})\xi\|>\frac{1}{2^{n-2}}\|\xi\|
. Then

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{n-1}}\sum_{m=0}^{2^{n-1}-1}\|\pi((e-z^{m})(e-Z_{0}))\xi\|\geq\left\|\pi\left(\left(e-\frac{1}{2^{n-1}}\sum_{m=0}^{2^{n-1}-1}z^{m}\right)(e-Z_{0})\right)\xi\right\|\geq

\displaystyle\|\pi(e-Z_{0})\xi\|-\left\|\pi\left(\left(\frac{1}{2^{n-1}}\sum_{m=0}^{2^{n-1}-1}z^{m}\right)(e-Z_{0})\right)\xi\right\|\geq^{e-Z_{0}=\frac{e-z}{2}}

\displaystyle\left\|\pi(e-Z_{0})\xi\right\|-\frac{1}{2^{n-1}}\left\|\pi\left(\frac{e-z^{2^{n-1}}}{2}\right)\xi\right\|\geq

\displaystyle\left\|\pi(e-Z_{0})\xi\right\|-\frac{1}{2^{n-1}}\|\xi\|>^{\|\pi(e-Z_{0})\xi\|>\frac{1}{2^{n-2}}\|\xi\|}

\displaystyle\left\|\pi(e-Z_{0})\xi\right\|-\frac{1}{2}\left\|\pi(e-Z_{0})\xi\right\|=\frac{1}{2}\left\|\pi(e-Z_{0})\xi\right\|.

It follows that for
\zeta=\pi(e-Z_{0})\xi
, there is
1\leq m\leq 2^{n-1}-1
such that
\|\pi(e-z^{m})\zeta\|\geq\frac{1}{2}\|\zeta\|
. Thus, by Lemma
5.4
,

\|\pi\left(X_{0}Y^{n}\right)\zeta\|\leq r_{1}\|\zeta\|,

i.e.,

\left\|\pi\left(X_{0}Y^{n}(e-Z_{0})\right)\xi\right\|\leq r_{1}\|\pi(e-Z_{0})\xi\|,

as needed.
∎

Corollary 5.6
.

Let
\delta_{0}:(0,2]\rightarrow(0,1]
be a function. Let
0\leq r_{1}<1
be the constant given in Lemma
5.4
above. For every
(\pi,\operatorname{\mathbb{E}})\in\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E}_{uc}(\delta_{0}))
, every
n\in\mathbb{N},n\geq 2
, every
a_{0},b_{0}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
and every
\xi\in\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
, it holds that

\left\|\pi\left(\left(X_{a_{0}}\left(\prod_{b=0}^{n-1}Y_{b_{0}+b}\right)\right)(e-Z_{a_{0}+b_{0}})\right)\xi\right\|\leq\max\left\{r_{1}\|\pi(e-Z_{a_{0}+b_{0}})\xi\|,\frac{1}{2^{n-2}}\|\xi\|\right\}.

Proof.

Fix
(\pi,\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}),n,a_{0},b_{0}
as above.

Let
H<H_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
be the subgroup
H=\langle x^{2^{a_{0}}},y^{2^{b_{0}}}\rangle
.
We note that
H
is isomorphic to
H_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
via the isomorphism
\Phi:H_{3}(\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow H
induced by
\Phi(x)=x^{2^{a_{0}}},\Phi(y)=y^{2^{b_{0}}}
. Note that (by extending
\Phi
linearly)

\Phi(X_{0})=X_{a_{0}},\Phi(Y^{n})=\prod_{b=0}^{n-1}Y_{b_{0}+b}.

Also note that

\Phi(z)=\Phi(x^{-1}y^{-1}xy)=x^{-2^{a_{0}}}y^{-2^{b_{0}}}x^{2^{a_{0}}}y^{2^{b_{0}}}=z^{2^{a_{0}+b_{0}}},

and thus
\Phi(Z_{0})=Z_{a_{0}+b_{0}}
.

Define a new representation
(\pi_{0},\operatorname{\mathbb{E}})
of
H_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
by
\pi_{0}=\pi\circ\Phi
. Let
\xi\in\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
, then

\displaystyle\left\|\pi\left(\left(X_{a_{0}}\left(\prod_{b=0}^{n-1}Y_{b_{0}+b}\right)\right)(e-Z_{a_{0}+b_{0}})\right)\xi\right\|=\left\|\pi_{0}\left(\left(X_{0}Y^{n}\right)(e-Z_{0})\right)\xi\right\|\leq

\displaystyle\max\left\{r_{1}\|\pi_{0}(e-Z_{0})\xi\|,\frac{1}{2^{n-2}}\|\xi\|\right\}=\max\left\{r_{1}\|\pi(e-Z_{a_{0}+b_{0}})\xi\|,\frac{1}{2^{n-2}}\|\xi\|\right\},

as needed.
∎

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
\delta_{0}:(0,2]\rightarrow(0,1]
be a function. There are constants
0\leq r_{2}<1,C>0
such that for every
d_{1},d_{2},d_{3}\in\mathbb{N}
with
d_{1},d_{2}\leq d_{3},d_{1}+d_{2}\geq d_{3}
, it holds that

\left\|X^{d_{1}}Y^{d_{2}}\left(Z^{d_{3}}-Z^{d_{3}+1}\right)\right\|_{\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E}_{uc}(\delta_{0}))}\leq Cr_{2}^{\sqrt{d_{1}+d_{2}-d_{3}}}.

Proof.

Let
r_{1}=r_{1}(\delta_{0})
be the constant of Corollary
5.6
. We will prove that the inequality stated above holds for
r_{2}=\max\{r_{1},\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\}
.

Fix
d_{1},d_{2},d_{3}\in\mathbb{N}
as above and denote
t=2\lfloor\frac{\sqrt{d_{1}+d_{2}-d_{3}}}{2}\rfloor
. We note that
t\leq\sqrt{d_{1}+d_{2}-d_{3}}\leq t+2
and thus it is enough to prove that there is a constant
C^{\prime}
such that

\left\|X^{d_{1}}Y^{d_{2}}\left(Z^{d_{3}}-Z^{d_{3}+1}\right)\right\|_{\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E}_{uc}(\delta_{0}))}\leq C^{\prime}r_{2}^{t}.

Without loss of generality, we can assume that
t\geq 4
(for
t<4
, the constant
C^{\prime}
can be chosen to be large enough such that
C^{\prime}r_{2}^{4}\geq 2
and the needed inequality holds trivially).

Denote

A_{0}=\{d_{1}-1-kt:0\leq k\leq t-1\}.

Note that for every
0\leq k\leq t-1

\displaystyle d_{1}-1\geq d_{1}-1-kt>d_{1}-1-t^{2}+t\geq^{t\leq\sqrt{d_{1}+d_{2}-d_{3}}}

\displaystyle d_{1}-1-(d_{1}+d_{2}-d_{3})+t=d_{3}-d_{2}+t-1\geq^{d_{3}\geq d_{2},t\geq 4}0,

and thus
A_{0}\subseteq\{0,...,d_{1}-1\}
.

Also, denote

B_{0}=\bigcup_{k=0}^{t-1}\left\{d_{3}-d_{1}+1+kt+j:0\leq j\leq\frac{t}{2}-1\right\},

(note that
t
is always even and thus
\frac{t}{2}\in\mathbb{N}
). Note that for every
0\leq k\leq t-1
and every
0\leq j\leq\frac{t}{2}-1
it holds that

d_{3}-d_{1}+1+kt+j\geq d_{3}-d_{1}+1\geq^{d_{3}\geq d_{1}}0

and

\displaystyle d_{3}-d_{1}+1+kt+j\leq d_{3}-d_{1}+1+t^{2}-\frac{t}{2}\leq^{t\leq\sqrt{d_{1}+d_{2}-d_{3}}}d_{3}-d_{1}+1+(d_{1}+d_{2}-d_{3})-\frac{t}{2}=

\displaystyle d_{2}+1-\frac{t}{2}\leq^{t\geq 4}d_{2}-1.

It follows that
B_{0}\subseteq\{0,...,d_{2}-1\}
.

Thus,

\displaystyle\left\|X^{d_{1}}Y^{d_{2}}\left(Z^{d_{3}}-Z^{d_{3}+1}\right)\right\|_{\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E}_{uc}(\delta_{0}))}\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\prod_{a\in\{0,...,d_{1}-1\}\setminus A_{0}}X_{a}\right)\right\|_{\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E}_{uc}(\delta_{0}))}\left\|\left(\prod_{a\in A_{0}}X_{a}\right)Y^{d_{2}}\left(Z^{d_{3}}-Z^{d_{3}+1}\right)\right\|_{\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E}_{uc}(\delta_{0}))}\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\prod_{a\in A_{0}}X_{a}\right)\left(\prod_{b\in B_{0}}Y_{b}\right)\left(Z^{d_{3}}-Z^{d_{3}+1}\right)\right\|_{\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E}_{uc}(\delta_{0}))}\left\|\left(\prod_{b\in\{0,...,d_{2}-1\}\setminus B_{0}}Y_{b}\right)\right\|_{\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E}_{uc}(\delta_{0}))}\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\prod_{a\in A_{0}}X_{a}\right)\left(\prod_{b\in B_{0}}Y_{b}\right)\left(Z^{d_{3}}-Z^{d_{3}+1}\right)\right\|_{\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E}_{uc}(\delta_{0}))}.

It follows that it is enough to prove that there is a constant
C^{\prime}
such that

\left\|\left(\prod_{a\in A_{0}}X_{a}\right)\left(\prod_{b\in B_{0}}Y_{b}\right)\left(Z^{d_{3}}-Z^{d_{3}+1}\right)\right\|_{\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E}_{uc}(\delta_{0}))}\leq C^{\prime}r_{2}^{t},

i.e., it is enough to prove that for every
(\pi,\operatorname{\mathbb{E}})\in\mathcal{E}_{uc}(\delta_{0})
and every
\xi\in\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
, it holds that

(3)

\left\|\pi\left(\left(\prod_{a\in A_{0}}X_{a}\right)\left(\prod_{b\in B_{0}}Y_{b}\right)\left(Z^{d_{3}}-Z^{d_{3}+1}\right)\right)\xi\right\|\leq C^{\prime}r_{2}^{t}\|\xi\|.

Fix
(\pi,\operatorname{\mathbb{E}})\in\mathcal{E}_{uc}(\delta_{0})
and
\xi\in\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
. For
1\leq i\leq t-1
, define the sets

A_{i}=\{d_{1}-1-kt:i\leq k\leq t-1\},

B_{i}=\bigcup_{k=i}^{t-1}\left\{d_{3}-d_{1}+1+kt+j:0\leq j\leq\frac{t}{2}-1\right\}.

Also define
A_{t}=B_{t}=\emptyset
. For
0\leq i\leq t
denote

I_{i}=\left\|\pi\left(\left(\prod_{a\in A_{i}}X_{a}\right)\left(\prod_{b\in B_{i}}Y_{b}\right)\left(Z^{d_{3}}-Z^{d_{3}+1}\right)\right)\xi\right\|.

We claim that in order to prove (
3
), it is sufficient to show that for every
0\leq i\leq t-1
it holds that

(4)

I_{i}\leq 36\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)^{t}\|\xi\|+r_{1}I_{i+1},

where
r_{1}
is the constant of Corollary
5.6
. Indeed, if (
4
) holds, then

\displaystyle I_{0}\leq 36\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)^{t}\|\xi\|(1+r_{1}+...+r_{1}^{t-1})+r_{1}^{t}I_{t}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{36}{1-r_{1}}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)^{t}\|\xi\|+r_{1}^{t}\|\pi(Z^{d_{3}}-Z^{d_{3}+1})\xi\|\leq

\displaystyle\frac{36}{1-r_{1}}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)^{t}\|\xi\|+2r_{1}^{t}\|\xi\|\leq\left(\frac{36}{1-r_{1}}+2\right)r_{2}^{t},

as needed.

We are left to prove (
4
). Fix
0\leq i\leq t-1
. For
0\leq k\leq t-1
, denote

B_{k}^{\prime}=\left\{d_{3}-d_{1}+1+kt+j:0\leq j\leq\frac{t}{2}-1\right\},

thus
B_{i}=\bigcup_{k=i}^{t-1}B_{k}^{\prime}
. We note that

d_{3}-\max A_{i+1}-\max B_{i}^{\prime}=d_{3}-(d_{1}-1-(i+1)t)-(d_{3}-d_{1}+it+\frac{t}{2})=\frac{t}{2}+1.

Therefore, by Theorem
5.2
,

\displaystyle\left\|\pi\left(\left(\left(\prod_{a\in A_{i+1}}X_{a}\right)\left(\prod_{b\in B_{i}^{\prime}}Y_{b}\right)-\left(\prod_{b\in B_{i}^{\prime}}Y_{b}\right)\left(\prod_{a\in A_{i+1}}X_{a}\right)\right)Z^{d_{3}}\right)\right\|\leq

\displaystyle 8\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{d_{3}-\max A_{i+1}-\max B_{i}^{\prime}}=16\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)^{t}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\left\|\pi\left(\left(\prod_{a\in A_{i}}X_{a}\right)\left(\prod_{b\in B_{i}}Y_{b}\right)Z^{d_{3}}-\left(X_{d_{1}-1-it}\left(\prod_{b\in B_{i}^{\prime}}Y_{b}\right)\right)\left(\prod_{a\in A_{i+1}}X_{a}\right)\left(\prod_{b\in B_{i+1}}Y_{b}\right)Z^{d_{3}}\right)\right\|\leq

\displaystyle 16\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)^{t}.

By Observation
5.1
,
Z^{d_{3}+1}=Z^{d_{3}}Z_{d_{3}}
and thus

Z^{d_{3}}-Z^{d_{3}+1}=Z^{d_{3}}(e-Z_{d_{3}})
. Using this and the inequality stated above, we can deduce

I_{i}=\left\|\pi\left(\left(\prod_{a\in A_{i}}X_{a}\right)\left(\prod_{b\in B_{i}}Y_{b}\right)Z^{d_{3}}\left(e-Z_{d_{3}}\right)\right)\xi\right\|\leq\left\|\pi\left(\left(X_{d_{1}-1-it}\left(\prod_{b\in B_{i}^{\prime}}Y_{b}\right)\right)\left(\prod_{a\in A_{i+1}}X_{a}\right)\left(\prod_{b\in B_{i+1}}Y_{b}\right)Z^{d_{3}}(e-Z_{d_{3}})\right)\xi\right\|+{16\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)^{t}\|\pi\left(e-Z_{d_{3}}\right)\xi\|}\leq\left\|\pi\left(\left(X_{d_{1}-1-it}\left(\prod_{b\in B_{i}^{\prime}}Y_{b}\right)(e-Z_{d_{3}})\right)\left(\prod_{a\in A_{i+1}}X_{a}\right)\left(\prod_{b\in B_{i+1}}Y_{b}\right)Z^{d_{3}}\right)\xi\right\|+32\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)^{t}\|\xi\|=\\
\left\|\pi\left(X_{d_{1}-1-it}\left(\prod_{b\in B_{i}^{\prime}}Y_{b}\right)(e-Z_{d_{3}})\right)\pi\left(\left(\prod_{a\in A_{i+1}}X_{a}\right)\left(\prod_{b\in B_{i+1}}Y_{b}\right)Z^{d_{3}}\right)\xi\right\|+32\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)^{t}\|\xi\|.

Denote
\xi^{\prime}=\pi\left(\left(\prod_{a\in A_{i+1}}X_{a}\right)\left(\prod_{b\in B_{i+1}}Y_{b}\right)Z^{d_{3}}\right)\xi
. With this notation, we showed that

I_{i}\leq\left\|\pi\left(X_{d_{1}-1-it}\left(\prod_{b\in B_{i}^{\prime}}Y_{b}\right)(e-Z_{d_{3}})\right)\xi^{\prime}\right\|+32\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)^{t}\|\xi\|.

We note that

\left\|\pi(e-Z_{d_{3}})\xi^{\prime}\right\|=\left\|\pi\left(\left(\prod_{a\in A_{i+1}}X_{a}\right)\left(\prod_{b\in B_{i+1}}Y_{b}\right)\left(Z^{d_{3}}-Z^{d_{3}+1}\right)\right)\xi\right\|=I_{i+1}.

Thus, in order to prove (
4
), we are left to prove that

\left\|\pi\left(X_{d_{1}-1-it}\left(\prod_{b\in B_{i}^{\prime}}Y_{b}\right)(e-Z_{d_{3}})\right)\xi^{\prime}\right\|\leq 4\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)^{t}\|\xi\|+r_{1}\|\pi(e-Z_{d_{3}})\xi^{\prime}\|.

We recall that

\displaystyle\left\|\pi\left(X_{d_{1}-1-it}\left(\prod_{b\in B_{i}^{\prime}}Y_{b}\right)(e-Z_{d_{3}})\right)\xi^{\prime}\right\|=\left\|\pi\left(X_{d_{1}-1-it}\left(\prod_{b=0}^{\frac{t}{2}-1}Y_{(d_{3}-d_{1}+1+it)+b}\right)(e-Z_{d_{3}})\right)\xi^{\prime}\right\|.

Denoting
a_{0}=d_{1}-1-it,b_{0}=d_{3}-d_{1}+1+it,n=\frac{t}{2}
and applying Corollary
5.6
(noting that
a_{0}+b_{0}=d_{3}
and that we assumed that
n=\frac{t}{2}\geq 2
) yields that

\displaystyle\left\|\pi\left(X_{d_{1}-1-it}\left(\prod_{b=0}^{\frac{t}{2}-1}Y_{(d_{3}-d_{1}+1+it)+b}\right)(e-Z_{d_{3}})\right)\xi^{\prime}\right\|\leq\max\left\{r_{1}\|\pi(e-Z_{d_{3}})\xi^{\prime}\|,\frac{1}{2^{\frac{t}{2}-2}}\|\xi^{\prime}\|\right\}\leq

\displaystyle 4\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)^{t}\|\xi^{\prime}\|+r_{1}\|\pi(e-Z_{d_{3}})\xi^{\prime}\|\leq 4\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)^{t}\|\xi\|+r_{1}\|\pi(e-Z_{d_{3}})\xi^{\prime}\|,

as needed.
∎

Corollary 5.8
.

Let
\delta_{0}:(0,2]\rightarrow(0,1]
be a function. There are constants
0\leq r<1,C>0
such that for every

d_{1},d_{2},d_{3},d_{4}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
d_{1},d_{2}\geq\frac{1}{4}\min\{d_{3},d_{4}\}
and
d_{1}+d_{2}-\max\{d_{3},d_{4}\}\geq\frac{1}{4}\min\{d_{3},d_{4}\}
, it holds that

\left\|X^{d_{1}}Y^{d_{2}}\left(Z^{d_{3}}-Z^{d_{4}}\right)\right\|_{\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E}_{uc}(\delta_{0}))}\leq|d_{4}-d_{3}|Cr^{\sqrt{\min\{d_{3},d_{4}\}}},

and

\left\|Y^{d_{1}}X^{d_{2}}\left(Z^{d_{3}}-Z^{d_{4}}\right)\right\|_{\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E}_{uc}(\delta_{0}))}\leq|d_{4}-d_{3}|Cr^{\sqrt{\min\{d_{3},d_{4}\}}}.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we will assume that
d_{4}>d_{3}
.

We will start by proving the first inequality.

Let
r_{2},C
be the constants of Theorem
5.7
and take
r=\sqrt{r_{2}}
. We note that it is enough to prove that for any
0\leq j\leq d_{4}-d_{3}-1
it holds that

\left\|X^{d_{1}}Y^{d_{2}}\left(Z^{d_{3}+j}-Z^{d_{3}+j+1}\right)\right\|_{\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E}_{uc}(\delta_{0}))}\leq Cr^{\sqrt{d_{3}}}.

If
d_{1},d_{2}\leq d_{3}+j
this inequality follow immediately from Theorem
5.7
. Otherwise, either
d_{2}>d_{3}+j
or
d_{1}>d_{3}+j
(or both) and thus

\min\{d_{1},d_{3}+j\}+\min\{d_{2},d_{3}+j\}-d_{3}-j\geq\frac{1}{4}d_{3}.

In this case, we apply Theorem
5.7
replacing
d_{i}
with
\min\{d_{i},d_{3}+j\}
for
i=1,2
:

\displaystyle\left\|X^{d_{1}}Y^{d_{2}}\left(Z^{d_{3}+j}-Z^{d_{3}+j+1}\right)\right\|_{\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E}_{uc}(\delta_{0}))}\leq

\displaystyle\left\|X^{\min\{d_{1},d_{3}+j\}}Y^{\min\{d_{2},d_{3}+j\}}\left(Z^{d_{3}+j}-Z^{d_{3}+j+1}\right)\right\|_{\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E}_{uc}(\delta_{0}))}\leq Cr^{\sqrt{d_{3}}},

as needed.

In order to prove the second inequality, we need to prove that for every
(\pi,\operatorname{\mathbb{E}})\in\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E}_{uc}(\delta_{0}))
it holds that

\left\|\pi\left(Y^{d_{2}}X^{d_{1}}\left(Z^{d_{3}}-Z^{d_{4}}\right)\right)\right\|\leq(d_{4}-d_{3})Cr^{\sqrt{d_{3}}}.

Fix
(\pi,\operatorname{\mathbb{E}})\in\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E}_{uc}(\delta_{0}))
. Let
\Phi:\rm H_{3}(\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow\rm H_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
be the isomorphism induced by

\Phi(x^{-1})=y
and
\Phi(y)=x
. We note that

\Phi(z)=\Phi(x^{-1}y^{-1}xy)=yx^{-1}y^{-1}x=[x,y^{-1}]^{-1}=z.

Extending
\Phi
linearly yields that

\Phi(Y^{d_{2}})=X^{d_{2}},\Phi(Z^{d_{3}})=Z^{d_{3}},\Phi(Z^{d_{4}})=Z^{d_{4}},

and

\Phi(X^{d_{1}})=\frac{1}{2^{d_{1}}}\sum_{b=-2^{d_{1}}+1}^{0}y^{b}=y^{-2^{d_{1}}+1}\left(\frac{1}{2^{d_{1}}}\sum_{b=0}^{2^{d_{1}}-1}y^{b}\right)=y^{-2^{d_{1}}+1}Y^{d_{1}}.

Define
\pi_{0}=\pi\circ\Phi
, then
(\pi_{0},\operatorname{\mathbb{E}})\in\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E}_{uc}(\delta_{0}))
and by the first inequality proven above it follows that

\displaystyle Cr^{\sqrt{d_{3}}}\geq\left\|\pi_{0}\left(X^{d_{1}}Y^{d_{2}}\left(Z^{d_{3}}-Z^{d_{4}}\right)\right)\right\|=

\displaystyle\left\|\pi\left(\Phi(X^{d_{1}})\Phi(Y^{d_{2}})\left(\Phi(Z^{d_{3}})-\Phi(Z^{d_{4}})\right)\right)\right\|=

\displaystyle\left\|\pi(y^{-2^{d_{1}}+1})\pi\left(Y^{d_{1}}X^{d_{2}}\left(Z^{d_{3}}-Z^{d_{4}}\right)\right)\right\|=

\displaystyle\left\|\pi\left(Y^{d_{1}}X^{d_{2}}\left(Z^{d_{3}}-Z^{d_{4}}\right)\right)\right\|,

as needed.
∎

6.
Relative Banach property (T) for
(\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z}),\operatorname{UT}_{3}(\mathbb{Z}))
and
(\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z}),\operatorname{LT}_{3}(\mathbb{Z}))

In this section we will prove our main relative Banach property (T) result stated in the introduction.

For any
\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}
, we denote

H_{i,k}=\langle e_{i,j}(1),e_{j,k}(1),e_{i,k}(1)\rangle<\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z}),

\widetilde{H}_{i,k}=\langle x_{i,j}(1),x_{j,k}(1),x_{i,k}(1)\rangle<\operatorname{St}_{3}(\mathbb{Z}),

For example,
H_{1,3}
is the group of uni-upper-triangular matrices
\operatorname{UT}_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
that appeared in the introduction.

We will prove the following theorem:

Theorem 6.1
.

For any function
\delta_{0}:(0,2]\rightarrow(0,1]
and any
1\leq i,k\leq 3,i\neq k
, the pairs
(\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z}),H_{i,k})
and
(\operatorname{St}_{3}(\mathbb{Z}),\widetilde{H}_{i,k})
have relative property
(T^{\operatorname{proj}}_{\mathcal{E}_{uc}(\delta_{0})})
.

In particular, for any function
\delta_{0}:(0,2]\rightarrow(0,1]
, the pairs
(\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z}),\operatorname{UT}_{3}(\mathbb{Z}))
and
(\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z}),\operatorname{LT}_{3}(\mathbb{Z}))
have relative property
(T^{\operatorname{proj}}_{\mathcal{E}_{uc}(\delta_{0})})
.

Below, we will prove this theorem only for the pair
(\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z}),UT_{3}(\mathbb{Z}))
. The proof will only use the Steinberg relations of
\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
and thus it applies verbatim to the pair
(\operatorname{St}_{3}(\mathbb{Z}),\widetilde{H}_{1,3}(\mathbb{Z}))
(replacing each
e_{i,j}
with
x_{i,j}
). The proof for any other
H_{i,k}
(or
\widetilde{H}_{i,k}
in the case of the Steinberg group) follows from the case
UT_{3}(\mathbb{Z})=H_{1,3}
after permuting the indices.

In order to prove this theorem, we define the following: Let
1\leq i,k\leq 3,i\neq k
and
d\in\mathbb{N}
. Define
X_{i,k}^{d}\in\operatorname{Prob}_{c}(\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z}))
by

X_{i,k}^{d}=\frac{1}{2^{d}}\sum_{a=0}^{2^{d}-1}e_{i,k}(a).

Informally, the idea of the proof is to look a product of all the
X_{i,k}^{d}
’s and to preform “moves” on this product with a small “norm cost” (such that this “cost” decrease as
d
increases). We will consider the following “moves” for
\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}
:

(1)

Switch moves on
H_{i,k}
: Replacing
X_{i,j}^{d_{1}}X_{i,k}^{d_{3}}X_{j,k}^{d_{2}}
with
X_{j,k}^{d_{2}}X_{i,k}^{d_{3}}X_{i,j}^{d_{1}}
and vice-versa.

(2)

Up/down moves on
H_{i,k}
: Replacing
X_{i,j}^{d_{1}}X_{i,k}^{d_{3}}X_{j,k}^{d_{2}}
with
X_{j,k}^{d_{2}}X_{i,k}^{d_{4}}X_{i,j}^{d_{1}}
where
d_{4}\in\mathbb{N}
(when
d_{4}>d_{3}
this will be called an up move and when
d_{4}<d_{3}
this will be called a down move).

The following lemma bounds the “norm cost” of these moves:

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
\delta_{0}:(0,2]\rightarrow(0,1]
and
\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}
.
For any
P_{1},P_{1},T\in\operatorname{Prob}_{c}(\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z}))
the following holds:

(1)

Switch moves on
H_{i,k}
have small “norm cost”: For any constants
d_{1},d_{2},d_{3}\in\mathbb{N}
with
d_{1}+d_{2}\leq d_{3}-2
it holds that

\displaystyle\left\|P_{1}\left(X_{i,j}^{d_{1}}X_{i,k}^{d_{3}}X_{j,k}^{d_{2}}\right)P_{2}-T\right\|_{\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E}_{uc}(\delta_{0}))}\leq

\displaystyle\left\|P_{1}\left(X_{j,k}^{d_{2}}X_{i,k}^{d_{3}}X_{i,j}^{d_{1}}\right)P_{2}-T\right\|_{\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E}_{uc}(\delta_{0}))}+8\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{d_{3}-(d_{1}+d_{2})},

\displaystyle\left\|P_{1}\left(X_{j,k}^{d_{2}}X_{i,k}^{d_{3}}X_{i,j}^{d_{1}}\right)P_{2}-T\right\|_{\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E}_{uc}(\delta_{0}))}\leq

\displaystyle\left\|P_{1}\left(X_{i,j}^{d_{1}}X_{i,k}^{d_{3}}X_{j,k}^{d_{2}}\right)P_{2}-T\right\|_{\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E}_{uc}(\delta_{0}))}+8\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{d_{3}-(d_{1}+d_{2})}.

(2)

Up/down moves on
H_{i,k}
have small “norm cost”: Let
0\leq r<1,C>0
be the constants of Corollary
5.8
. For every
d_{1},d_{2},d_{3},d_{4}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
d_{1},d_{2}\geq\frac{1}{4}\min\{d_{3},d_{4}\}
and
d_{1}+d_{2}-\max\{d_{3},d_{4}\}\geq\frac{1}{4}\min\{d_{3},d_{4}\}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\left\|P_{1}\left(X_{i,j}^{d_{1}}X_{i,k}^{d_{3}}X_{j,k}^{d_{2}}\right)P_{2}-T\right\|_{\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E}_{uc}(\delta_{0}))}\leq

\displaystyle\left\|P_{1}\left(X_{i,j}^{d_{1}}X_{i,k}^{d_{4}}X_{j,k}^{d_{2}}\right)P_{2}-T\right\|_{\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E}_{uc}(\delta_{0}))}+|d_{4}-d_{3}|Cr^{\sqrt{\min\{d_{3},d_{4}\}}},

\displaystyle\left\|P_{1}\left(X_{j,k}^{d_{2}}X_{i,k}^{d_{3}}X_{i,j}^{d_{1}}\right)P_{2}-T\right\|_{\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E}_{uc}(\delta_{0}))}\leq

\displaystyle\left\|P_{1}\left(X_{j,k}^{d_{2}}X_{i,k}^{d_{4}}X_{i,j}^{d_{1}}\right)P_{2}-T\right\|_{\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E}_{uc}(\delta_{0}))}+|d_{4}-d_{3}|Cr^{\sqrt{\min\{d_{3},d_{4}\}}}.

Proof.

The bounds of the “norm cost” of switch moves follow directly from Theorem
5.2
. We will prove the bound of the first switch move (the proof of second bound is similar). Let
d_{1},d_{2},d_{3}\in\mathbb{N}
with
d_{1}+d_{2}\leq d_{3}-2
, then by Theorem
5.2

\displaystyle\left\|P_{1}\left(X_{i,j}^{d_{1}}X_{i,k}^{d_{3}}X_{j,k}^{d_{2}}\right)P_{2}-T\right\|_{\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E}_{uc}(\delta_{0}))}\leq

\displaystyle\left\|P_{1}\left(X_{j,k}^{d_{2}}X_{i,k}^{d_{3}}X_{i,j}^{d_{1}}\right)P_{2}-T\right\|_{\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E}_{uc}(\delta_{0}))}+\left\|P_{1}\left(X_{i,j}^{d_{1}}X_{i,k}^{d_{3}}X_{j,k}^{d_{2}}-X_{j,k}^{d_{2}}X_{i,k}^{d_{3}}X_{i,j}^{d_{1}}\right)P_{2}\right\|_{\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E}_{uc}(\delta_{0}))}\leq

\displaystyle\left\|P_{1}\left(X_{j,k}^{d_{2}}X_{i,k}^{d_{3}}X_{i,j}^{d_{1}}\right)P_{2}-T\right\|_{\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E}_{uc}(\delta_{0}))}+\left\|X_{i,j}^{d_{1}}X_{i,k}^{d_{3}}X_{j,k}^{d_{2}}-X_{j,k}^{d_{2}}X_{i,k}^{d_{3}}X_{i,j}^{d_{1}}\right\|_{\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E}_{uc}(\delta_{0}))}\leq

\displaystyle\left\|P_{1}\left(X_{j,k}^{d_{2}}X_{i,k}^{d_{3}}X_{i,j}^{d_{1}}\right)P_{2}-T\right\|_{\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E}_{uc}(\delta_{0}))}+8\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{d_{3}-(d_{1}+d_{2})}.

The bounds of the “norm cost” of up/down moves follow directly from Corollary
5.8
. We will prove the bound of the first up/down move (the proof of second bound is similar). Let
d_{1},d_{2},d_{3},d_{4}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
d_{1},d_{2}\geq\frac{1}{4}\min\{d_{3},d_{4}\}
and
d_{1}+d_{2}-\max\{d_{3},d_{4}\}\geq\frac{1}{4}\min\{d_{3},d_{4}\}
. By Corollary
5.8
,

\displaystyle\left\|P_{1}\left(X_{i,j}^{d_{1}}X_{i,k}^{d_{3}}X_{j,k}^{d_{2}}\right)P_{2}-T\right\|_{\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E}_{uc}(\delta_{0}))}\leq

\displaystyle\left\|P_{1}\left(X_{i,j}^{d_{1}}X_{i,k}^{d_{4}}X_{j,k}^{d_{2}}\right)P_{2}-T\right\|_{\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E}_{uc}(\delta_{0}))}+\left\|P_{1}\left(X_{i,j}^{d_{1}}X_{i,k}^{d_{3}}X_{j,k}^{d_{2}}-X_{i,j}^{d_{1}}X_{i,k}^{d_{4}}X_{j,k}^{d_{2}}\right)P_{2}\right\|_{\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E}_{uc}(\delta_{0}))}\leq

\displaystyle\left\|P_{1}\left(X_{i,j}^{d_{1}}X_{i,k}^{d_{4}}X_{j,k}^{d_{2}}\right)P_{2}-T\right\|_{\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E}_{uc}(\delta_{0}))}+\left\|X_{i,j}^{d_{1}}X_{i,k}^{d_{3}}X_{j,k}^{d_{2}}-X_{i,j}^{d_{1}}X_{i,k}^{d_{4}}X_{j,k}^{d_{2}}\right\|_{\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E}_{uc}(\delta_{0}))}\leq

\displaystyle\left\|P_{1}\left(X_{i,j}^{d_{1}}X_{i,k}^{d_{4}}X_{j,k}^{d_{2}}\right)P_{2}-T\right\|_{\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E}_{uc}(\delta_{0}))}+|d_{4}-d_{3}|Cr^{\sqrt{\min\{d_{3},d_{4}\}}}.

∎

For
d\in\mathbb{N}
, we define
T_{d},S_{d}\in\operatorname{Prob}_{c}(\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z}))
as follows:

T_{d}=X_{1,2}^{4d}X_{1,3}^{10d}X_{2,3}^{9d}X_{2,1}^{9d}X_{3,1}^{10d}X_{3,2}^{4d},

S_{d}=X_{2,3}^{4d}X_{1,3}^{10d}X_{1,2}^{9d}X_{3,2}^{9d}X_{3,1}^{10d}X_{2,1}^{4d}.

Using the lemma above, we will show that
\|T_{d}-S_{d\pm 1}\|
is small:

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
\delta_{0}:(0,2]\rightarrow(0,1]
be some function. Then there are constants
0\leq r<1
and
L>0
such that for every
d\in\mathbb{N}
it holds that

\left\|T_{d}-S_{d\pm 1}\right\|_{\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E}_{uc}(\delta_{0}))}\leq Ldr^{\sqrt{d}}.

Proof.

We will prove the bound only for
\|T_{d}-S_{d+1}\|
, the proof for
\|T_{d}-S_{d-1}\|
is similar. Let
r,C
be the constants of Corollary
5.8
. Without loss of generality, we can assume that
d>10
(otherwise, we can choose
L
to be large enough such that
Lr^{\sqrt{10}}\geq 2
and the inequality holds trivially).

The idea of the proof is to use switch moves and up/down moves to change
T_{d}
into
S_{d+1}
while book-keeping the “norm cost” using Lemma
6.2
. We remark that when preforming up/down moves on
H_{i,k}
, the order of the product of
X_{i,j}^{d_{1}},X_{j,k}^{d_{2}},X_{i,k}^{d_{3}}
(where
\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}
) does not matter, since
X_{i,k}^{d_{3}}
commutes with
X_{i,j}^{d_{1}}
and
X_{j,k}^{d_{2}}
.

Step 1 (Up moves on
H_{1,3},H_{3,1}
)
: We note that we assumed that
d>10
and thus for
d_{1}=4d,d_{2}=9d,d_{3}=10d,d_{4}=10(d+1)
the conditions of Lemma
6.2
(2) hold. Preforming up moves on
H_{1,3}
and
H_{3,1}
, we get by Lemma
6.2
that

\displaystyle\left\|T_{d}-S_{d+1}\right\|_{\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E}_{uc}(\delta_{0}))}=

\displaystyle\left\|\left(X_{1,2}^{4d}X_{1,3}^{10d}X_{2,3}^{9d}\right)\left(X_{2,1}^{9d}X_{3,1}^{10d}X_{3,2}^{4d}\right)-S_{d+1}\right\|_{\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E}_{uc}(\delta_{0}))}\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\left(X_{1,2}^{4d}X_{1,3}^{10(d+1)}X_{2,3}^{9d}\right)\left(X_{2,1}^{9d}X_{3,1}^{10(d+1)}X_{3,2}^{4d}\right)-S_{d+1}\right\|_{\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E}_{uc}(\delta_{0}))}+20Cr^{\sqrt{10d}}.

Bottom line: The “norm cost” of this step is
20Cr^{\sqrt{10d}}\leq 20Cdr^{\sqrt{d}}
.

Step 2 (Down moves on
H_{2,3},H_{2,1}
):
For
d_{1}=10(d+1),d_{2}=9d,d_{3}=9d,d_{4}=4(d+1)
, the conditions of Lemma
6.2
(2) are fulfilled and thus we can preform the following down move on
H_{2,3}
:

\displaystyle\left\|X_{1,2}^{4d}\left(X_{1,3}^{10(d+1)}X_{2,3}^{9d}X_{2,1}^{9d}\right)X_{3,1}^{10(d+1)}X_{3,2}^{4d}-S_{d+1}\right\|_{\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E}_{uc}(\delta_{0}))}\leq

\displaystyle\left\|X_{1,2}^{4d}\left(X_{1,3}^{10(d+1)}X_{2,3}^{4(d+1)}X_{2,1}^{9d}\right)X_{3,1}^{10(d+1)}X_{3,2}^{4d}-S_{d+1}\right\|_{\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E}_{uc}(\delta_{0}))}+(9d-4(d+1))Cr^{\sqrt{4(d+1)}}.

After that, we preform a down move on
H_{2,1}
with
d_{1}=4(d+1),d_{2}=10(d+1),d_{3}=9d,d_{4}=4(d+1)
(using Lemma
6.2
(2) again):

\displaystyle\left\|X_{1,2}^{4d}X_{1,3}^{10(d+1)}\left(X_{2,3}^{4(d+1)}X_{2,1}^{9d}X_{3,1}^{10(d+1)}\right)X_{3,2}^{4d}-S_{d+1}\right\|_{\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E}_{uc}(\delta_{0}))}\leq

\displaystyle\left\|X_{1,2}^{4d}X_{1,3}^{10(d+1)}\left(X_{2,3}^{4(d+1)}X_{2,1}^{4(d+1)}X_{3,1}^{10(d+1)}\right)X_{3,2}^{4d}-S_{d+1}\right\|_{\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E}_{uc}(\delta_{0}))}+(9d-4(d+1))Cr^{\sqrt{4(d+1)}}.

Bottom line: The “norm cost” of this step is
2(9d-4(d+1))Cr^{\sqrt{4(d+1)}}\leq 10dCr^{\sqrt{d}}
.

Step 3 (Switch moves on
H_{1,3},H_{3,1}
):
Preforming switch moves on
H_{1,3}
and
H_{3,1}
with
d_{1}=4d,d_{2}=4(d+1),d_{3}=10(d+1)
, we get by Lemma
6.2
(1) that

\displaystyle\left\|\left(X_{1,2}^{4d}X_{1,3}^{10(d+1)}X_{2,3}^{4(d+1)}\right)\left(X_{2,1}^{4(d+1)}X_{3,1}^{10(d+1)}X_{3,2}^{4d}\right)-S_{d+1}\right\|_{\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E}_{uc}(\delta_{0}))}\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\left(X_{2,3}^{4(d+1)}X_{1,3}^{10(d+1)}X_{1,2}^{4d}\right)\left(X_{3,2}^{4d}X_{3,1}^{10(d+1)}X_{2,1}^{4(d+1)}\right)-S_{d+1}\right\|_{\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E}_{uc}(\delta_{0}))}+16\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2(d+1)}.

Bottom line: The “norm cost” of this step is

(by the choice of

Step 4 (Up moves on

For

\displaystyle\left\|X_{2,3}^{4(d+1)}\left(X_{1,3}^{10(d+1)}X_{1,2}^{9(d+1)}X_{3,2}^{4d}\right)X_{3,1}^{10(d+1)}X_{2,1}^{4(d+1)}-S_{d+1}\right\|_{\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E}_{uc}(\delta_{0}))}+(9(d+1)-4d)Cr^{\sqrt{4d}}.

After that, we preform an up move on

with

(using Lemma

\displaystyle\left\|X_{2,3}^{4(d+1)}X_{1,3}^{10(d+1)}\left(X_{1,2}^{9(d+1)}X_{3,2}^{4d}X_{3,1}^{10(d+1)}\right)X_{2,1}^{4(d+1)}-S_{d+1}\right\|_{\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E}_{uc}(\delta_{0}))}\leq

8.

The aim of the section is to prove Theorem

that appeared in the introduction. We start by stating Howe-Moore’s Theorem for reflexive Banach spaces:

Theorem 8.1
.

[Howe-Moore’s Theorem for reflexive Banach spaces
[
Vee79
]
]
Let
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
be a reflexive Banach space and
G
be a connected simple real Lie group with a finite center. Then for every continuous linear isometric representation
\pi:G\rightarrow O(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}})
such that
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{\pi(G)}=\{0\}
it holds for every
\xi\in\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
and
\eta\in\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{*}
that

\lim_{g\rightarrow\infty}\langle\pi(g)\xi,\eta\rangle=0.

Corollary 8.2
.

Let
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
be a uniformly convex Banach space and
G
be a connected simple real Lie group with a finite center. For every unbounded subgroup
H<G
and every continuous linear isometric representation
\pi:G\rightarrow O(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}})
it holds that
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{\pi(G))}=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{\pi(H)}
and that
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{\prime}(\pi)=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{\prime}(\left.\pi\right|_{H})
.

Proof.

Let
\xi\in\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{\pi(H)}\cap\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{\prime}(\pi)
and denote
\pi^{\prime}
the restriction of
\pi
to
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{\prime}(\pi)
. Fix

tending to infinity. By Howe-Moore it follows for every
\eta\in\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{*}
that

\langle\xi,\eta\rangle=\lim_{n}\langle\pi(h_{n})\xi,\eta\rangle=0,

thus
\xi=0
. This shows that
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{\pi(G)}=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{\pi(H)}
. Similarly,
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{\pi^{*}(G)}=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{\pi^{*}(H)}
. Recall that
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{\prime}
and
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{\prime}(\left.\pi\right|_{H})
are the annihilators of
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{\pi^{*}(G)}
and
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{\pi^{*}(H)}
, and thus
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{\prime}(\pi)=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{\prime}(\left.\pi\right|_{H})
.
∎

Corollary 8.3
.

Let
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
be a uniformly convex Banach space and
G
be a connected simple real Lie group with a finite center. For every unbounded subgroup
H<G
, if
H
has property
(T_{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}})
, then so does
G
.

Proof.

By Observation
3.2
, it is enough to show that every continuous isometric representation

with

\pi:G\rightarrow O(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}})
with
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{\pi(G)}=\{0\}
.

By Corollary

, it holds that
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{\pi(H)}=\{0\}
. Thus it follows that
\left.\pi\right|_{H}
does not have (
H
-)almost invariant vectors and as a result
\pi
does not have (
G
-)almost invariant vectors.
∎

Corollary 8.4

The group

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
.

Proof.

For every uniformly convex Banach space
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}

9.1.
Banach fixed point property for simple Lie groups

Let
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
be a Banach space and
G
be a topological group. An affine isometric action of
G
on
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
is a continuous homomorphism
\rho:G\rightarrow\operatorname{Isom}_{aff}(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}})
, where
\operatorname{Isom}_{aff}(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}})
denotes the group of affine isometric automorphisms of
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
. We recall such that
\rho
is of the form

\rho(g)\xi=\pi(g)\xi+c(g),\forall\xi\in\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}

where
\pi:G\rightarrow O(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}})
is a continuous isometric linear representation that is called the linear part of
\rho
and
c:G\rightarrow\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
is a continuous
1
-cocycle into
\pi
, i.e., it is a continuous map such that for every
g,h\in G
,

c(gh)=c(g)+\pi(g)c(h).

The group
G
is said to have
property
(F_{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}})
if every affine isometric action of
G
on
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
admits a fixed point.

Below, we will prove Theorem
1.5
that states that a simple Lie group whose Lie algebra contains
\mathfrak{sl}_{4}(\mathbb{R})
has property
(F_{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}})
for every super-reflexive Banach space. For this we will state the following results:

Theorem 9.1
.

Metric Mautner phenomenon,
[
BG17
, Theorem 1.3]

Let

be a connected simple real Lie group with a finite center. Assume that

acts isometrically and continuously on a metric space

G
on
\mathbb{X}
), then the action of
G
on
\mathbb{X}
admits a fixed point.

Lemma 9.2
.

[
BFGM07
, Lemma 5.6]

Let

be a topological group that acts by a continuous affine isometric action

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}

\rho
restricted to

does not have almost invariant vectors, then the action of
H_{2}
on
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
admits a fixed point.

Last, using
[

]
we can show that for higher rank Lie groups property

9.2.
Super-expanders

We start by recalling the definition of Mendel and Naor
[
MN14
]
for super-expanders.

Let
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
be a Banach space and
\{(V_{i},E_{i})\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of finite graphs with uniformly bounded degree, such that
\lim_{i}|V_{i}|=\infty
. We say that
\{(V_{i},E_{i})\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
has a
Poincaré inequality with respect to
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
if there are constants
p,\gamma\in(0,\infty)
such that for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
and every
\phi:V_{i}\rightarrow\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
we have

\frac{1}{|V_{i}|^{2}}\sum_{(u,v)\in V_{i}\times V_{i}}\|\phi(u)-\phi(v)\|^{p}\leq\frac{\gamma}{|V_{i}|}\sum_{(x,y)\in E_{i}}\|\phi(x)-\phi(y)\|^{p}.

The sequence
\{(V_{i},E_{i})\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is called a
super-expander family
if it has a Poincaré inequality with respect to every super-reflexive Banach space (or equivalently for every uniformly convex Banach space).

For Cayley graphs, the following proposition of Lafforgue gives a relation between Poincaré inequality of Cayley graphs and Banach property
(T)
:

Proposition 9.6
.

[
Laf08
, Proposition 5.2]

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated discrete group and let
\{N_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of finite index normal subgroups of
\Gamma
such that
\bigcap_{i}N_{i}=\{1\}
. Also let
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
be a Banach space. If
\Gamma
has Banach property
(T_{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{\prime}})
for
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{\prime}=\ell^{2}(\bigcup_{i}G/N_{i};\operatorname{\mathbb{E}})
, then for every fixed finite symmetric generating set
S
, the family of Cayley graphs of
\{(G/N_{i},S/N_{i})\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
has a Poincaré inequality with respect to
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
.

A consequence of this proposition and Theorem
1.4
implies the following theorem that appeared in the introduction (Theorem
1.6
):

Theorem 9.7
.

Let
n\geq 3
and let
S
be a finite generating set of
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
(e.g.,

Proof.

Let
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
be some uniformly convex Banach space. By Theorem
2.3
,
\ell^{2}(\bigcup_{i}G/N_{i};\operatorname{\mathbb{E}})
is a uniformly convex Banach space. Thus by Theorem

, the family of Cayley graphs of
\{(\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}/i\mathbb{Z}),\Phi_{i}(S))\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}

-expander family.
∎

It was shown in
[
Vig19
,
dLV19
,
FNvL19
,
Saw20
]
that one can construct super-expanders using warped cones arising from an action of a Banach property (T) group on a compact manifold. Combining this machinery with our Theorem
1.4
also leads to a construction of super-expanders as we will briefly now explain. Let
(M,d_{M})
be a compact Riemannian manifold and
\Gamma
be a finitely generated group with finite symmetric generating set
S
. Assume that
\Gamma
acts on
M
by Lipschitz homeomorphisms. For
t>0
, define the
t
-level warped cone denoted
(M,d_{\Gamma}^{t})
to be the metric space such that
d_{\Gamma}^{t}
is the largest metric satisfying:

•

d_{\Gamma}^{t}(x,y)\leq td_{M}(x,y)
for every
x,y\in M
.

•

d_{\Gamma}^{t}(x,s.x)\leq 1
for every
x\in M
and
s\in S
.

Remark 9.8
.

The metric
d_{\Gamma}^{t}
is dependent on the choice of the generating set, but this dependence will be irrelevant with respect to our application below, because of the following fact (see
[

]
): For metrics

and
(d_{\Gamma}^{t})^{\prime}
that correspond to generating sets
S
and

, it holds that
d_{\Gamma}^{t}
and

are Lipschitz equivalent.

The following theorem is a straight-forward implication of Sawicki’s main result in
[

]

Theorem 9.9
.

[
Saw20
, Theorem 1.1]

Let
(M,d_{M})
be a compact Riemannian manifold and
\Gamma
be a finitely generated group acting on

by Lipschitz homeomorphisms. If
\Gamma
has property

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}

Combining this theorem with Theorem

.

and let

9.3.
Property
(FF_{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}})
for
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{R}),\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})

When
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
is reflexive it follows for the Ryll-Nardzewski fixed-point Theorem that
G
has property
(F_{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}})
if and only if for every isometric linear representation
\pi:G\rightarrow O(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}})
it holds that every continuous
1
-cocycle into
\pi
is bounded. This lead to the stronger notion of property
(FF_{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}})
defined by Mimura
[
Mim11
]
as a Banach version of Monod’s
[
Mon01
]
property (TT): Given a continuous isometric linear representation
\pi:G\rightarrow O(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}})
, a continuous
quasi-
1
-cocycle
into
\pi
is a continuous map
c:G\rightarrow\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
such that

\sup_{g,h\in G}\|c(gh)-(c(g)+\pi(g)c(h))\|<\infty.

A group
G
is said to have property
property
(FF_{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}})
if for every continuous isometric linear representation
\pi:G\rightarrow O(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}})
it holds that every continuous quasi-
1
-cocycle into
\pi
is bounded.

The following result of de Laat, Mimura and de la Salle allows one to deduce property
(FF_{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}})
from property
(T_{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}})
:

.

[

, then

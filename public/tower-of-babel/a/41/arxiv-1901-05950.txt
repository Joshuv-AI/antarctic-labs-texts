Approximative K K -Atomic Decompositions and frames in Banach Spaces
Shah Jahan

Abstract
. [L. Gavruta, Frames for Operators, Appl. comput. Harmon. Anal. 32(2012), 139-144] introduced a special kind of frames, named K K -frames, where K K is an operator, in Hilbert spaces, is significant in frame theory and has many applications. In this paper, first of all, we have introduced the notion of approximative K K -atomic decomposition in Banach spaces. We gave two characterizations regarding the existence of approximative K K -atomic decompositions in Banach spaces. Also some results on the existence of approximative K K -atomic decompositions are obtained. We discuss several methods to construct approximative K K -atomic decomposition for Banach Spaces. Further, approximative 𝒳 d \mathcal{X}_{d} -frame and approximative 𝒳 d \mathcal{X}_{d} -Bessel sequence are introduced and studied. Two necessary conditions are given under which an approximative 𝒳 d \mathcal{X}_{d} -Bessel sequence and approximative 𝒳 d \mathcal{X}_{d} -frame give rise to a bounded operator with respect to which there is an approximative K K -atomic decomposition. Examples and counter examples are provided to support our concept. Finally, a possible application is given.

Introduction and Preliminaries

Fourier transform has been a major tool in analysis for over a century. It has a serious lacking for signal analysis in which it hides in its phase information concerning the moment of emission and duration of a signal. What actually needed was a localized time frequency representation which has this information encoded in it. In 1946, Dennis Gabor
[
13
]
filled this gap and formulated a fundamental approach to signal decomposition in terms of elementary signals. On the basis of this development, in 1952, Duffin and Schaeffer
[
9
]
introduced frames for Hilbert spaces to study some deep problems in non-harmonic Fourier series. In fact, they abstracted the fundamental notion of Gabor for studying signal processing.
Let
\mathcal{H}
be a real (or complex) separable Hilbert space with inner product
\langle.,.\rangle.
A countable sequence
\{f_{n}\}\subset\mathcal{H}
is called a frame for the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H},
if there exist positive constants
A,~B>0
such that

\displaystyle A\|f\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\langle f,f_{n}\rangle|^{2}\leq B\|f\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}},~~~\mbox{for all}~~\in\mathcal{H}

(1.1)

The positive numbers
A
and
B
are called the lower and upper frame bounds of the frame, respectively. These bounds are not unique. The inequality in (
1.1
) is called the frame inequality of the frame. If
\{f_{n}\}
is a frame for
\mathcal{H}
then the following operators are associated with it.

(a)

Pre-frame operator
T:l^{2}(\mathbb{N})\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}
is defined as
T\{c_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}c_{n}f_{n},~~\{c_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\in l^{2}(\mathbb{N}).

(b)

Analysis operator
T^{*}:\mathcal{H}\longrightarrow l^{2}(\mathbb{N}),T^{*}=\{\langle f,f_{k}\rangle\}_{k=1}^{\infty}~~~f\in\mathcal{H}.

(c)

Frame operator
S=TT^{*}=:\mathcal{H}\longrightarrow\mathcal{H},~~S_{f}=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\langle f,f_{k}\rangle f_{k},~~f\in\mathcal{H}.
The frame
S
is bounded, linear and invertible on
\mathcal{H}
. Thus, a frame for
\mathcal{H}
allows each vector in
\mathcal{H}
to be written as a linear combination of the elements in the frame, but the linear independence between the elements is not required; i.e for each vector
f\in\mathcal{H}
we have,

\displaystyle f=SS^{-1}f=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\langle f,f_{k}\rangle f_{k}.

2.
Main Results

Poumai and Jahan
[
23
]
defined and studied
K
-atomic decomposition as a generalization of
K
-frames in Banach spaces. Here we shall extend this study further and introduced the concept of approximative K-atomic decomposition in Banach spaces and obtained new and interesting results. We start this section with the following definition of approximative K-Atomic decomposition:

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a Banach Space,
\{x_{n}\}\subset\mathcal{X},\{h_{n,i}\}\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{{}_{i=1,2,3,...,m_{n}}}\subset\mathcal{X}^{*},
where
\{m_{n}\}
is an increasing sequence of positive integer and
K\in L(\mathcal{X})
. A pair
(\{x_{n}\},\{h_{n,i}\}\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{{}_{i=1,2,3,...,m_{n}}})
is called an
approximative K-atomic decomposition
for
\mathcal{X}
with respect to
\mathcal{X}_{d},
if the following statements holds:

(a)

\{h_{n,i}(x)\}\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{{}_{i=1,2,3,...,m_{n}}}\in\mathcal{X}_{d}
, for all
x\in\mathcal{X}.

(b)

There exist constants
A
and
B
with
0<A\leq B<\infty
such that

\displaystyle A\parallel K(x)\parallel_{\mathcal{X}}~\leq~\parallel\{h_{n,i}(x)\}\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{{}_{i=1,2,3,...,m_{n}}}\|_{\mathcal{X}_{d}}\leq B\parallel x\parallel_{\mathcal{X}},~\mbox{for all}\ x\in\mathcal{X}.

(c)

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m_{n}}h_{n,i}(x)x_{i}
converges for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
and

K(x)=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m_{n}}h_{n,i}(x)x_{i}
.

The constants
A
and
B
are called lower and upper bounds of the approximative
K
-atomic decomposition
(\{x_{n}\},\{h_{n,i}\}_{i=1,2,3,...,m_{n}}).

Observations
.

If
(\{x_{n}\},\{f_{n}\})
is a
K
-atomic decomposition for
\mathcal{X}
with respect to
\mathcal{X}_{d},
then for
h_{n,i}=f_{i},i=1,2,...,n,n\in\mathbb{N},

(\{x_{n}\},\{h_{n,i}\})
is an approximative
K
-atomic decomposition for
\mathcal{X}
with respect to some associated Banach space
\mathcal{X}_{d}.

Remark 2.2
.

Let
(x_{n},h_{n,i})
be an approximative
K
-atomic decomposition for
\mathcal{X}
with respect to
\mathcal{X}_{d}
with bounds
A
and
B
.

(I)

If
K=I_{\mathcal{X}}
, then
(x_{n},h_{n,i})
is an approximative atomic decomposition for
\mathcal{X}
with respect to
\mathcal{X}_{d}
with bounds
A
and
B
.

(II)

If
K
is invertible, then
(K^{-1}(x_{n}),h_{n,i})
is an approximative atomic decomposition for
\mathcal{X}
with respect to
\mathcal{X}_{d}
.
In the following example, we show the existence of approximative
K
-atomic decomposition for a Banach space
\mathcal{X}
with respect to an associated BK space
\mathcal{X}_{d}
.

Example 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a Banach Space. Let
\{x_{n}\}\subseteq\mathcal{X}
,
\{h_{n,i}\}\subseteq\mathcal{X}^{*}
such that
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m_{n}}h_{n,i}(x)x_{n}
converges for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
and
x_{n}\neq 0,
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Also, let
\mathcal{X}_{d}=\{\{h_{n,i}\}|\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m_{n}}h_{n,i}x_{i}~\mbox{converges}\}.
Then
\mathcal{X}_{d}
is a BK-space with norm
\|\{h_{n,i}(x)\}\|_{\mathcal{X}_{d}}=\sup\limits_{1\leq n<\infty}\parallel\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}h_{n,i}x_{i}\parallel
. Define an operator as
T:\mathcal{X}_{d}\longrightarrow\mathcal{X}
as
T\{h_{n,i}\}=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m_{n}}h_{n,i}x_{i}
and define
S:\mathcal{X}\longrightarrow\mathcal{X}_{d}
as
S(x)=\{h_{n,i}(x)\},~x\in\mathcal{X}
. Take
K=TS.
Then
K:\mathcal{X}\longrightarrow\mathcal{X}
is such that
K(x)=TS(x)=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m_{n}}h_{n,i}(x)x_{i},
for all
x\in\mathcal{X},~i=1,2,...,n,~n\in\mathbb{N}.
Clearly,
\{h_{n,i}(x)\}\in\mathcal{X}_{d}
and

\displaystyle\|K(x)\|_{\mathcal{X}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\bigg\|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m_{n}}h_{n,i}(x)x_{i}\bigg\|\leq\sup\limits_{1\leq n<\infty}\bigg\|\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}h_{k}(x)x_{k}\bigg\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\{h_{n,i}(x)\}\|_{\mathcal{X}_{d}}\leq C\parallel x\parallel_{\mathcal{X}},~\mbox{for all}~x\in\mathcal{X},

where
C=\sup\limits_{1\leq n<\infty}\parallel S_{n}\parallel
and
S_{n}(x)=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m_{n}}h_{n,i}(x)x_{i}.

Hence,
(x_{n},h_{n,i})
is an approximative
K
-atomic decomposition for
\mathcal{X}
with respect to
\mathcal{X}_{d}.

In the following result, we give the characterization regarding the existence of approximative
K
-atomic decompositions in Banach spaces.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
K\in L(\mathcal{X})
with
K\neq 0.
Then a Banach space
\mathcal{X}
has an approximative
K
-atomic decomposition if and only if there exists a sequence
\{v_{i}\}\subset B(\mathcal{X})
of finite rank endomorphism such that
K(x)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}(x),~~x\in\mathcal{X}.

Proof.

Let
\{x_{n}\}\subset\mathcal{X}
and
\{h_{n,i}\}\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{{}_{i=1,2,3,...,m_{n}}}\subset\mathcal{X}^{*},
where
\{m_{n}\}
is an increasing sequence of positive integer such that
(\{x_{n}\},\{h_{n,i}\}\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{{}_{i=1,2,3,...,m_{n}}})
is an
approximative K-atomic decomposition
for
\mathcal{X}
with respect to
\mathcal{X}_{d}.
Define

\displaystyle S_{n}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{m_{n}}h_{n,i}(x)x_{i},~~\mbox{for all}~x\in\mathcal{X},n\in\mathbb{N}.

Then for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
x\in\mathcal{X}
,
S_{n}(x)
is a well defined continuous linear mapping on
\mathcal{X}
such that
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}S_{n}(x)=x,~~x\in\mathcal{X}.
Also by uniform boundedness principle we have
\sup\limits_{1\leq n\leq\infty}\|S_{n}(x)\|<\infty.

Assume that
v_{1}=S_{1}
,
v_{2n}=v_{2n+1}=\frac{1}{2}(S_{n+1}-S_{n})
,
n\in\mathbb{N}.
Now, we compute

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}(x)

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}(S_{1}(x)+\frac{1}{2}(S_{2}(x)-S_{1}(x))+\frac{1}{2}(S_{2}(x)-S_{1}(x))

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}(S_{3}(x)-S_{2}(x))+\frac{1}{2}(S_{3}(x)-S_{2}(x))+...)

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}S_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=K(x),~~\mbox{for all}~~x\in\mathcal{X},K\in L(X).

Therefore,
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}(x)=K(\mathcal{X}).

Conversely assume that there exists a sequence of finite rank endomorphism
\{S_{n}\}\subset L(\mathcal{X})
such that
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}S_{n}(x)=K(x),~~x\in\mathcal{X}.
Then, each
S_{n}(x)
is of finite rank, there exist a sequence
\{y_{n,i}\}_{i=m_{n-1}+1}^{m_{n}}\subset\mathcal{X}
and a total sequence of row finite matrix of functionals
\{g_{n,i}\}_{i=m_{n-1}+1}^{m_{n}}\subset\mathcal{X^{*}}
such that

\displaystyle S_{n}(x)=\sum_{i=m_{n-1}+1}^{m_{n}}g_{n,i}(x)y_{n,i},\mbox{for all}~~x\in\mathcal{X},~~n\in\mathbb{N}.

Define sequences
\{x_{n}\}\subset\mathcal{X}
and
\{h_{n,i}\}\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{{}_{i=1,2,3,...,m_{n}}}\subset\mathcal{X}^{*},
where
\{m_{n}\}
is an increasing sequence of positive integers, by

\displaystyle x_{i}=y_{n,i},~i=m_{n-1}+1,...m_{n};n=1,2,3...

and

\displaystyle h_{n,i}=\begin{cases}0,~~~\mbox{for}~~~i=1,2,...,m_{n-1}\\
g_{n,i},~~~\mbox{for}~~~i=m_{n-1}+1,...,m_{n}.\end{cases}

Then
x_{n}\neq 0,
so for each
x\in\mathcal{X}
and
n\in\mathbb{N},
we get

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{m_{n}}h_{n,i}(x)x_{i}=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}S_{n}(x)=K(x).

(2.4)

Let
x\in\mathcal{X}
be such that
h_{n,i}(x)=0,\mbox{for all}~i=1,2,...m_{n},~~n\in\mathbb{N}.
Then by equation (
2.4
)
K(x)=0.
Thus by Lemma
1.4
there exist an associated Banach space
\mathcal{X}_{d}=\{\{h_{n,i}\}\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{{}_{i=1,2,3,...,m_{n}}},x\in\mathcal{X}\}
with norm given by
\|\{h_{n,i}\}\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{{}_{i=1,2,3,...,m_{n}}}\|_{\mathcal{X}_{d}}=\|x\|_{\mathcal{X}},~~\mbox{for all}~x\in\mathcal{X}.
Hence
(\{h_{n,i}\},\{x_{n}\})
is an approximative
K
-atomic decomposition for
\mathcal{X}
with respect to
\mathcal{X}_{d}.

∎

3.
Approximative
\mathcal{X}_{d}
-frame

Casazza et al.
[
5
]
defined and studied
\mathcal{X}_{d}
-Bessel sequences and
\mathcal{X}_{d}
-frames in Banach spaces. Later on Stoeva
[
27
]
studied perturbation of
\mathcal{X}_{d}
-Bessel sequences,
\mathcal{X}_{d}
-frames, atomic decomposition and
\mathcal{X}_{d}
-Riesz bases in separable Banach spaces. We have generalized this concept and defined approximative
\mathcal{X}_{d}
-Bessel sequences and approximative
\mathcal{X}_{d}
-frames in Banach spaces. We begin this section with the following definitions:

Definition 3.1
.

A sequence
\{h_{n,i}\}\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{{}_{i=1,2,3,...,m_{n}}}\subseteq\mathcal{X}^{*},
where
\{m_{n}\}
is an increasing sequence of positive integers, is called an approximative
\mathcal{X}_{d}
-frame
for
\mathcal{X}
if

(a)

\{h_{n,i}(x)\}\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{{}_{i=1,2,3,...,m_{n}}}\in\mathcal{X}_{d}
, for all
x\in\mathcal{X}.

(b)

There exist constants
A
and
B
with
0<A\leq B<\infty
such that

\displaystyle A\|x\|_{\mathcal{X}}~\leq\|\{h_{n,i}(x)\}\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{{}_{i=1,2,3,...,m_{n}}}\|_{\mathcal{X}_{d}}~\leq B\|x\|_{\mathcal{X}},~\text{for all}\ x\in\mathcal{X}.

(3.5)

The constants
A
and
B
are called approximative
\mathcal{X}_{d}
-frame
bounds.
If atleast (a) and the upper bound condition in (
3.5
) are satisfied, then
\{h_{n,i}\}
is called an approximative
\mathcal{X}_{d}
-Bessel sequence
for
\mathcal{X}.

One may note that if
\{f_{n}\}
is an
\mathcal{X}_{d}
-frame for
\mathcal{X},
then for
\{h_{n,i}\}=f_{i},~i=1,2,3,...,n;~n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\{h_{n,i}\}
is an approximative
\mathcal{X}_{d}
-frame for
\mathcal{X}.
Also, note that if
\{f_{n}\}
is an
\mathcal{X}_{d}
-Bessel sequence for
\mathcal{X},
then for
\{h_{n,i}\}=f_{i},~i=1,2,3,...,n;~n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\{h_{n,i}\}
is an approximative
\mathcal{X}_{d}
-Bessel sequence for
\mathcal{X}.

In the next two results, we give necessary conditions under which an approximative
\mathcal{X}_{d}
-frame gives rise to a bounded operator
K
with respect to which there is an approximative
K
-atomic decomposition for
\mathcal{X}
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\{h_{n,i}\}\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{{}_{i=1,2,3,...,m_{n}}}\subseteq\mathcal{X}^{*}
be an approximative
\mathcal{X}_{d}
-frame for
\mathcal{X}
with bounds
A
and
B
. Let
\{x_{n}\}\subseteq\mathcal{X}
with
\sup\limits_{1\leq n<\infty}\|x_{n}\|<\infty
and let
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m_{n}}|h_{n,i}(x)|<\infty,
for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
. Then there exists an operator
K\in L(\mathcal{X})
such that
(\{x_{n}\},\{h_{n,i}\})
is an approximative
K
-atomic decomposition for
\mathcal{X}
with respect to
\mathcal{X}_{d}
.

Proof.

Since
\{h_{n,i}\}\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{{}_{i=1,2,3,...,m_{n}}}\subseteq\mathcal{X}^{*}
is an approximative
\mathcal{X}_{d}
-frame for
\mathcal{X}
with
\sup\limits_{1\leq n<\infty}\|x_{n}\|<\infty
and
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m_{n}}|h_{n,i}(x)|<\infty.
Then, by Theorem
2.4
, we have
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m_{n}}h_{n,i}(x)x_{i}
exist for all
x\in\mathcal{X},~n\in\mathbb{N}.

Define
K:\mathcal{X}\longrightarrow\mathcal{X}
by
K(x)=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m_{n}}h_{n,i}(x)x_{i}
,
x\in\mathcal{X}
. Then
K
is a bounded linear operator such that

\|K(x)\|_{\mathcal{X}}\leq\sup\limits_{1\leq n<\infty}\|\sum_{i=1}^{m_{n}}h_{n,i}(x)x_{i}\|_{\mathcal{X}}\leq C\|x\|_{\mathcal{X}},

where
C=\sup\limits_{1\leq n<\infty}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m_{n}}h_{n,i}(x)x_{i}
. Thus

\dfrac{A}{C}\|K(x)\|_{\mathcal{X}}\leq\parallel\{h_{n,i}(x)\}\|_{\mathcal{X}_{d}}\leq B\parallel x\parallel_{\mathcal{X}},~\mbox{for all}~x\in\mathcal{X}.

Hence,
(\{x_{n}\},\{h_{n,i}\})
is an approximative
K
-atomic decomposition for
\mathcal{X}
with respect to
\mathcal{X}_{d}
with bounds
\dfrac{A}{C}
and
B
.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\{h_{n,i}\}\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{{}_{i=1,2,3,...,m_{n}}}\subseteq\mathcal{X}^{*}
be an approximative
\mathcal{X}_{d}
-frame with bounds
A
,
B
and let
\{x_{n}\}\subseteq\mathcal{X}
. Let
T:\mathcal{X}_{d}\longrightarrow\mathcal{X}
given by
T(\{h_{n,i}\})=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m_{n}}h_{n,i}x_{i}
be a well defined operator. Then, there exists a linear operator
K\in L(\mathcal{X})
such that
(\{x_{n}\},\{h_{n,i}\})
is an approximative
K
-atomic decomposition for
\mathcal{X}
with respect to
\mathcal{X}_{d}.

Proof.

Define
U:\mathcal{X}\longrightarrow\mathcal{X}_{d}
by
U(x)=\{h_{n,i}(x)\}
,

. Then
U
is well defined and
\|U\|\leq B
. Take

. Then
K(x)=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m_{n}}h_{n,i}(x)x_{i},~x\in\mathcal{X}

\|K(x)\|_{\mathcal{X}}\leq\sup\limits_{1\leq n<\infty}\|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m_{n}}h_{n,i}(x)x_{i}\|_{\mathcal{X}}\leq C\|x\|_{\mathcal{X}},~x\in\mathcal{X},

where
C=\sup\limits_{1\leq n<\infty}\|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m_{n}}h_{n,i}(x)x_{i}\|_{\mathcal{X}}

\dfrac{A}{C}\|K(x)\|~\leq~\parallel\{h_{n,i}(x)\}\|\leq B\parallel x\parallel,~\mbox{for all}\ x\in\mathcal{X}.

Hence
(\{x_{n}\},\{h_{n,i}\})
is an approximative
K
-atomic decomposition for
\mathcal{X}
with respect to
\mathcal{X}_{d}
with bounds
\dfrac{A}{C}
and
B
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a reflexive Banach space and
\mathcal{X}_{d}
be a BK-space which has a sequence of canonical unit vectors
\{e_{n}\}
as a basis. Let
\{h_{n,i}\}\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{{}_{i=1,2,3,...,m_{n}}}\subseteq\mathcal{X}^{*}
be an approximative
\mathcal{X}_{d}
-Bessel sequence with bound
B
and let
\{x_{n}\}\subseteq\mathcal{X}

for all

K\in L(\mathcal{X})
such that
(\{x_{n}\},~\{h_{n,i}\}\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{{}_{i=1,2,3,...,m_{n}}})
is an approximative
K
-atomic decomposition for
\mathcal{X}
with respect to
\mathcal{X}_{d}.

Proof.

Clearly
U:\mathcal{X}\longrightarrow\mathcal{X}_{d}
given by
U(x)=\{h_{n,i}(x)\},~x\in\mathcal{X}
is well defined. Define a map
R:\mathcal{X}^{*}\longrightarrow(\mathcal{X}_{d})^{*}
by
R(h)=\{h(x_{n})\},~x\in\mathcal{X}
. Then, its adjoint
R^{*}:(\mathcal{X}_{d})^{**}\longrightarrow\mathcal{X}^{**}
is given by
R^{*}(e_{j})(h)=e_{j}(R(h))=h(x_{j})
. Let
T=(R^{*})|_{\mathcal{X}_{d}}
and
\{h_{n,i}\}\in\mathcal{X}_{d}
. Then

\displaystyle T(\{h_{n,i}\})=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m_{n}}h_{n,i}T(e_{i})=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m_{n}}h_{n,i}x_{i}.

But
\{h_{n,i}(x)\}\in\mathcal{X}_{d}
. So
T(\{h_{n,i}(x)\})=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m_{n}}h_{n,i}(x)x_{i}
.
Take
K=TU
.
Then
K\in L(\mathcal{X})\ \text{and}\ K(x)=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m_{n}}h_{n,i}(x)x_{i}.

Moreover,
T
is a bounded linear operator such that
\|K(x)\|\leq\|T\|\|\{h_{n,i}(x)\}\|
.
Hence

\dfrac{1}{\|T\|}\|K(x)\|\leq\|\{h_{n,i}(x)\}\|\leq B\|x\|,~x\in\mathcal{X}

where each elements

NM

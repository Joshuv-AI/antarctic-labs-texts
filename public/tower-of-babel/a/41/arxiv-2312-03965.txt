On the formal Peterson subalgebra and its dual
Rui Xiong, Kirill Zainoulline, Changlong Zhong

Abstract
. In the present notes, we study a generalization of the Peterson subalgebra to an oriented (generalized) cohomology theory which we call the formal Peterson subalgebra. Observe that by recent results of Zhong the dual of the formal Peterson algebra provides an algebraic model for the oriented cohomology of the affine Grassmannian. Our first result shows that the centre of the formal affine Demazure algebra generates the formal Peterson subalgebra. Our second observation is motivated by the Peterson conjecture. We show that a certain localization of the formal Peterson subalgebra for the extended Dynkin diagram of type A ^ 1 \hat{A}_{1} provides an algebraic model for ‘quantum’ oriented cohomology of the projective line. Our last result can be viewed as an extension of the previous results on Hopf algebroids of structure algebras of moment graphs to the case of affine root systems. We prove that the dual of the formal Peterson subalgebra (an oriented cohomology of the affine Grassmannian) is the 0 0 th Hochshild homology of the formal affine Demazure algebra.

Introduction

Equivariant cohomology of an affine Grassmannian has been a topic of intensive investigations for decades. For the small torus action it can be identified with a certain commutative subalgebra of the associated nil-Hecke algebra of a Kac-Moody root system called the Peterson subalgebra
[
P97
]
. One of its remarkable properties says that after taking localization it becomes isomorphic to the (small) quantum cohomology of the respective finite part (flag variety)
[
P97
,
LS10
]
. A parallel isomorphism for the
K
-theory was conjectured and discussed in
[
LSS10
,
LLMS18
]
and is known as the Peterson Conjecture. This conjecture was recently proven by Kato in
[
K18
]
using a language of semi-infinite flag varieties.

In the present notes, we study a generalization of the Peterson subalgebra to an oriented (generalized) cohomology theory
h(\text{-})
, e.g. algebraic cobordism
\Omega(\text{-})
of Levine-Morel. Such a cohomology theory was first introduced and studied in
[
LM07
]
, and extended to the torus-equivariant setup in
[
Kr12
,
HM13
]
for arbitrary smooth varieties. As for flag varieties associated to root systems, it can be described using the Kostant-Kumar localization approach (for finite root systems see
[
CZZ16
,
CZZ19
,
CZZ15
]
, and for Kac-Moody see
[
CZZ20
]
). The respective generalization of the nil Hecke algebra is called the formal affine Demazure algebra (FADA). The generalization of the Peterson algebra introduced recently in
[
Z23
]
which we call a formal Peterson subalgebra is then the centralizer of the equivariant coefficient ring in the small torus FADA.

To state our first result, let
R=h(pt)
denote the coefficient ring of the oriented theory
h
, let
S=h_{T}(pt)
denote the respective small torus
T
equivariant coefficient ring, let
{\mathbf{D}}_{W_{\operatorname{a}}}
denote the small torus FADA, and let
{\mathbf{D}}_{Q^{\vee}}
denote the formal Peterson subalgebra as constructed in
[
Z23
]
. We then obtain the following important property of the centre of FADA:

Theorem A

(cf. Theorem
3.4
)
.

If
\mathbb{Q}\subseteq R
, then the centre
Z({\mathbf{D}}_{W_{\operatorname{a}}})
of the small torus FADA generates the formal Peterson subalgebra
{\mathbf{D}}_{Q^{\vee}}
as an
S
-module. Moreover, the centre
Z({\mathbf{D}}_{W_{\operatorname{a}}})
generates
{\mathbf{D}}_{W_{\operatorname{a}}}
as a
{\mathbf{D}}_{W}
-module, where
{\mathbf{D}}_{W}
stands for the FADA associated to the finite part of the Kac-Moody root system.

Our next result can be viewed as an extension of the Peterson conjecture.
We first introduce certain localization
{\mathbf{D}}_{Q^{\vee},\loc}
of the formal Peterson subalgebra
{\mathbf{D}}_{Q^{\vee}}
with respect to an affine root system of type
\hat{A}_{1}
. We then show

Theorem B

(cf. Theorem
4.6
)
.

{\mathbf{D}}_{Q^{\vee},\loc}\cong S[\mathfrak{t},{\mathfrak{t}}^{-1}][\mathfrak{s}]/(\mathfrak{s}^{2}=x_{-1}\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{t}+\mu\mathfrak{t})
,
where
x_{-1}
is a certain characteristic class in
h
and

\mu
is an element depending on
x_{-1}
.

Observe that for cohomology and
K
-theory, this localization computes quantum cohomology and quantum
K
-theory of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
respectively. Hence,
{\mathbf{D}}_{Q^{\vee},\loc}
can be viewed as the ‘quantum’ oriented cohomology of the projective line
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
.

As for our last result, observe that the
S
-linear dual
{\mathbf{D}}_{Q^{\vee}}^{*}
of the formal Peterson subalgebra is a natural model for the (small torus) equivariant oriented cohomology of the affine Grassmannian
[
Z23
]
. We obtain the following ‘Kac-Moody’ version of results of
[
LXZ23
]
:

Theorem C

(cf. Theorem
5.3
)
.

The
S
-linear dual
{\mathbf{D}}_{Q^{\vee}}^{*}
of the formal Peterson subalgebra is isomorphic to the
0
-th Hochschild homology of the dual
{\mathbf{D}}_{W_{\operatorname{a}}}^{*}
of the small torus FADA.

Here the dual
{\mathbf{D}}_{W_{\operatorname{a}}}^{*}
can be interpreted as a model for the
T
-equivariant oriented cohomology of the respective affine flag variety. Therefore, it has two commuting actions by the equivariant coefficient ring
S
. Following the ideas of
[
LXZ23
]
one defines its 0-th Hochschild homology as the quotient obtained by merging these two
S
-module structures.

To prove this result we introduce a special filtration on the dual
{\mathbf{D}}_{Q^{\vee}}^{*}
(to reduce it to finite cases). This approach seems to be new even for cohomology and the
K
-theory.

The paper is organized as follows: Section
1
revisits the definition of the formal Peterson subalgebra
{\mathbf{D}}_{Q^{\vee}}
from
[
Z23
]
. In Section 2 we establish some basic properties of
{\mathbf{D}}_{Q^{\vee}}
and study the action of
{\mathbf{D}}_{W_{\operatorname{a}}}
on it. Section 3 is dedicated to the study of
{\mathbf{D}}_{Q^{\vee}}
and
{\mathbf{D}}_{W_{\operatorname{a}}}
, culminating in the proof of Theorem
3.4
. Section 4 focuses on the example of type
\hat{A}^{1}
and on the proof of Theorem
4.6
. Finally, in Section 5 we investigate the dual of the formal Peterson subalgebra, and we prove Theorem
5.3
.

1.
The formal Peterson subalgebra

In this section we recall the definition of a small torus formal affine Demazure algebra (FADA) and the formal Peterson algebra
following
[
CZZ20
,
Z23
]
.

Given an oriented algebraic cohomology theory
h(\text{-})
in the sense of Levine-Morel (see
[
LM07
]
) there is an associated formal group law
F
over a commutative ring
R
with characteristic 0. Here
R=h(pt)
is the coefficient ring, and
F
is defined from the Quillen formula for the characteristic class of a tensor product of line bundles. For example, for connective
K
-theory (see e.g.
[
RZ23
]
) we have
F_{\beta}(x,y)=x+y-\beta xy
over the polynomial ring
R={\mathbb{Z}}[\beta]
. Specializing to
\beta=1
(resp.
\beta=0
) one obtains the usual
K
-theory (resp. cohomology). In these notes, by usual cohomology we always mean its algebraic part: the Chow ring (modulo rational equivalence) with rational coefficients.

Given a lattice
\Lambda
(free abelian group of finite rank) and a formal group law
F
, consider the associated formal group algebra
S
of
[
CPZ13
]
that is the quotient of the power series ring

S=R[[\Lambda]]_{F}=R[[x_{\lambda}\mid\lambda\in\Lambda]]/{\mathcal{J}}_{F},

where
{\mathcal{J}}_{F}
is the closure of the ideal of relations (
x_{0}
,
x_{\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}}-F(x_{\lambda_{1}},x_{\lambda_{2}})
) for all
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in\Lambda
. In the case
F=F_{\beta}
we will take
S
to be the quotient
R[\Lambda]_{F}=R[x_{\lambda}\mid\lambda\in\Lambda]/\langle x_{0},x_{\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}}-F_{\beta}(x_{\lambda_{1}},x_{\lambda_{2}})\rangle
of the polynomial ring.

Consider a finite irreducible root system. Let
\Phi
denote the set of roots, and
I=\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\}
denote the set of simple roots. Let
Q
be the root lattice, and
Q^{\vee}
be the coroot lattice. Let
W
denote the Weyl group, generated by simple reflections
s_{\alpha_{i}}
,
\alpha_{i}\in I
. Let
\theta\in\Phi
be the highest root. Consider an affine root system corresponding to the extended Dynkin diagram for
\Phi
. Let
\alpha_{0}=-\theta+\delta\in Q\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}\delta
denote the extra simple root (here
\delta
is the so called null root) so that

s_{\alpha_{0}}=t_{\theta^{\vee}}s_{\theta}
is an extra generator of the respective affine Weyl group
W_{\operatorname{a}}=Q^{\vee}\rtimes W
.
Recall that the latter is generated by reflections
s_{\alpha+k\delta}=t_{-k\alpha^{\vee}}s_{\alpha}
, where
s_{\alpha}\in W
is a reflection and
t_{\lambda}
is a translation. The affine Weyl group
W_{\operatorname{a}}
acts on
Q
via
W
that is
t_{\lambda}w(\mu)=w(\mu)
,
\mu\in Q
,
w\in W
,
\lambda\in Q^{\vee}
.
Therefore, it also acts on the formal group algebra
S=R[[Q]]_{F}
.

Suppose
x_{\alpha}
is regular for each
\alpha
(see
[
CZZ16
, Lemma 2.2 and Definition 4.4]
). In particular, this holds if
2
is not a zero-divisor in
R
.
Consider the localization
\mathcal{Q}=S[\tfrac{1}{x_{\alpha}}\mid\alpha\in\Phi]
of
S
. Define the twisted group algebra
\mathcal{Q}_{W_{\operatorname{a}}}=\mathcal{Q}\otimes_{R}R[W_{\operatorname{a}}]
. It is a free left
\mathcal{Q}
-module with basis
\eta_{u}
,
u\in W_{\operatorname{a}}
. It is also an
R
-algebra with the product given by

c\eta_{u}\cdot c^{\prime}\eta_{u^{\prime}}=cu(c^{\prime})\eta_{uu^{\prime}},~c,c^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Q},~u,u^{\prime}\in W_{\operatorname{a}}.

For each
\alpha\in\Phi
, define
\kappa_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{x_{\alpha}}+\frac{1}{x_{-\alpha}}
(which is an element of
S
),
X_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{x_{\alpha}}(1-\eta_{s_{\alpha}})
(the Demazure element) and
Y_{\alpha}=\kappa_{\alpha}-X_{\alpha}
(the push-pull element). Also define
X_{\alpha_{0}}=\frac{1}{x_{-\theta}}(1-\eta_{s_{0}})
and
Y_{\alpha_{0}}=\kappa_{\theta}-X_{\alpha_{0}}
. All these elements satisfy the quadratic relations (e.g.
X_{\alpha}^{2}=\kappa_{\alpha}X_{\alpha}
) and the twisted braid relations (see e.g.
[
CZZ20
]
). We denote by
X_{I_{u}}
,
Y_{I_{u}}
products corresponding to a reduced sequence
I_{u}
of
u\in W_{\operatorname{a}}
. For simplicity of notations, we will omit
\alpha
or
s
in the indices, i.e., we will write

x_{i}=x_{\alpha_{i}}
,
s_{i}=s_{\alpha_{i}}
,
\eta_{i}=\eta_{s_{i}}
,
X_{i}=X_{\alpha_{i}}
, and
Y_{i}=Y_{\alpha_{i}}
.

Similarly, define the twisted group algebra
\mathcal{Q}_{Q^{\vee}}=\mathcal{Q}\otimes_{R}R[Q^{\vee}]
. It is a free
\mathcal{Q}
-module with basis
\eta_{t_{\lambda}}
,
\lambda\in Q^{\vee}
. Observe that
\mathcal{Q}_{Q^{\vee}}
is commutative since
t_{\lambda}(c)=c
,
c\in\mathcal{Q}
. Consider two homomorphisms of left
\mathcal{Q}
-modules

\displaystyle\pr\colon

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{W_{\operatorname{a}}}\to\mathcal{Q}_{Q^{\vee}},~c\eta_{t_{\lambda}w}\mapsto c\eta_{t_{\lambda}},~c\in\mathcal{Q},w\in W,

\displaystyle\imath\colon

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{Q^{\vee}}\to\mathcal{Q}_{W_{\operatorname{a}}},~c\eta_{t_{\lambda}}\mapsto c\eta_{t_{\lambda}}.

By definition,
\imath
is a section of
\pr
, and it is a ring homomorphism. Set
\psi=\imath\circ\pr
, so
\psi|_{\imath(\mathcal{Q}_{Q^{\vee}})}=\id
.
Define elements
Z_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{x_{-\alpha}}(1-\eta_{t_{\alpha^{\vee}}})
,
\alpha\in\Phi
.

Lemma 1.1
.

We have
\psi(zX_{i})=0
for any
z\in\mathcal{Q}_{W_{\operatorname{a}}}
,
\alpha_{i}\in I
, and
\psi(X_{0})=Z_{\theta}
.

Proof.

For
z=c\eta_{u}
,
c\in\mathcal{Q}
,
u\in W_{\operatorname{a}}
, and
\alpha_{i}\in I
we have

\pr(c\eta_{u}X_{i})=\pr\big(c\eta_{u}\tfrac{1}{x_{\alpha_{i}}}(1-\eta_{s_{i}})\big)=\pr\big(\tfrac{c}{u(x_{\alpha_{i}})}(\eta_{u}-\eta_{us_{i}})\big)=0.

As for the
X_{0}
, we have

\psi(X_{0})=\imath\circ\pr(\tfrac{1}{x_{-\theta}}(1-\eta_{s_{0}}))=\tfrac{1}{x_{-\theta}}\imath\circ\pr((1-\eta_{t_{\theta^{\vee}}s_{\theta}}))=\tfrac{1}{x_{-\theta}}(1-\eta_{t_{\theta^{\vee}}})=Z_{\theta}.\qed

Denote
I_{\operatorname{a}}=\{\alpha_{0},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\}
. Following
[
CZZ20
]
define the small torus FADA
{\mathbf{D}}_{W_{\operatorname{a}}}
to be the subring of
\mathcal{Q}_{W_{\operatorname{a}}}
generated by
S
and the elements
X_{i}
,
\alpha_{i}\in I_{\operatorname{a}}
. Set
{\mathbf{D}}_{W_{\operatorname{a}}/W}=\pr({\mathbf{D}}_{W_{\operatorname{a}}})
to be the image in
\mathcal{Q}_{Q^{\vee}}
. We called it the relative FADA. Assuming
\mathbb{Q}\subset R
and using the small torus GKM descripition it is proven in
[
Z23
, Lemma 5.1]
that the map
\imath
induces a map
{\mathbf{D}}_{W_{\operatorname{a}}/W}\to{\mathbf{D}}_{W_{\operatorname{a}}}
. We then introduce a formal Peterson subalgebra to be the image
{\mathbf{D}}_{Q^{\vee}}=\imath({\mathbf{D}}_{W_{\operatorname{a}}/W})
of the relative FADA.
One of the results in
[
Z23
, Theorem 5.7]
says that

Proposition 1.2
.

Suppose
R
has characteristic 0, then the formal Peterson subalgebra
{\mathbf{D}}_{Q^{\vee}}
is a Hopf subalgebra in
\mathcal{Q}_{Q^{\vee}}
. Moreover,
{\mathbf{D}}_{Q^{\vee}}
coincides with the centralizer
C_{{\mathbf{D}}_{W_{\operatorname{a}}}}(S)
of the formal group algebra
S
in the FADA
{\mathbf{D}}_{W_{\operatorname{a}}}
.

2.
Properties of the FADA and the Peterson subalgebra

In the present section we establish several properties of the FADA and the Peterson subalgebra.
For
K
-theory some of these properties were proven in
[
K18
]
with different arguments.
We start with the following version of the projection formula:

Lemma 2.1
.

For any
z
,
z^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Q}_{W_{\operatorname{a}}}
and
\xi\in\mathcal{Q}_{Q^{\vee}}
we have in
\mathcal{Q}_{Q^{\vee}}
:

(i)

\pr(\imath(\xi)z)=\xi\pr(z)
, and

(ii)

\pr(z\sigma z^{\prime})=\pr(z)\pr(\sigma z^{\prime})
, where
\sigma=\sum_{w\in W}\eta_{w}
.

Observe that in the
K
-theory case the second property played a key role in
[
K18
, Theorem 1.7]
.

Proof.

(i) Let
\xi=c_{1}\eta_{t_{\lambda_{1}}}
and
z=c_{2}\eta_{t_{\lambda_{2}}w}
, where
w\in W
,
c_{i}\in\mathcal{Q}
,
\lambda_{i}\in Q^{\vee}
. Then we obtain

\pr(\imath(\xi)z)=\pr(c_{1}\eta_{t_{\lambda_{1}}}c_{2}\eta_{t_{\lambda_{2}}w})=\pr(c_{1}t_{\lambda_{1}}(c_{2})\eta_{t_{\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}}w})=c_{1}t_{\lambda_{1}}(c_{2})\eta_{t_{\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}}}=c_{1}\eta_{t_{\lambda_{1}}}c_{2}\eta_{t_{\lambda_{2}}}=\xi\pr(z).

(ii) Let
z=c\eta_{t_{\lambda}v}
and
z^{\prime}=c^{\prime}\eta_{t_{\lambda^{\prime}}v^{\prime}}
,
v,v^{\prime}\in W
. Then we get

\pr(z\sigma z^{\prime})=\pr\big(c\eta_{t_{\lambda}v}\sum_{w\in W}\eta_{w}c^{\prime}\eta_{t_{\lambda^{\prime}}v^{\prime}}\big)=\pr\big(\sum_{w\in W}ct_{\lambda}vw(c^{\prime})\eta_{t_{\lambda}vwt_{\lambda^{\prime}}v^{\prime}}\big).

Since
t_{\lambda}vwt_{\lambda^{\prime}}v^{\prime}=t_{\lambda}(vwt_{\lambda^{\prime}}(vw)^{-1})vwv^{\prime}=t_{\lambda}t_{vw(\lambda^{\prime})}vwv^{\prime}
, reindexing the sum by
w^{\prime}=vw
we obtain

\pr(z\sigma z^{\prime})=\pr(\sum_{w^{\prime}\in W}ct_{\lambda}w^{\prime}(c^{\prime})\eta_{t_{\lambda}t_{w^{\prime}(\lambda^{\prime})}w^{\prime}v^{\prime}})=\sum_{w^{\prime}\in W}ct_{\lambda}w^{\prime}(c^{\prime})\eta_{t_{\lambda}t_{w^{\prime}(\lambda^{\prime})}}.

On the other side
\pr(z)=c\eta_{t_{\lambda}}
and

\pr(\sigma z^{\prime})=\pr\big(\sum_{w}\eta_{w}c^{\prime}\eta_{t_{\lambda^{\prime}}v^{\prime}}\big)=\pr\big(\sum_{w}w(c^{\prime})\eta_{wt_{\lambda^{\prime}}v^{\prime}}\big)=\pr\big(\sum_{w}w(c^{\prime})\eta_{t_{w(\lambda^{\prime})}wv^{\prime}}\big)=\sum_{w}w(c^{\prime})\eta_{t_{w(\lambda^{\prime})}}.

The result then follows.
∎

We now extend the Hecke action on the Peterson algebra for the
K
-theory introduced in
[
K18
, §2]
to the action on the formal Peterson algebra:

We define an action of
\mathcal{Q}_{W_{\operatorname{a}}}
on
\mathcal{Q}_{Q^{\vee}}
by

z\diamond\xi=\pr(z\imath(\xi)),~z\in\mathcal{Q}_{W_{\operatorname{a}}},\xi\in\mathcal{Q}_{Q^{\vee}}.

More explicitly, we have

(1)

c\eta_{t_{\lambda}w}\diamond c^{\prime}\eta_{t_{\lambda^{\prime}}}=cw(c^{\prime})\eta_{t_{\lambda+w(\lambda^{\prime})}},~c,c^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Q},w\in W.

Direct computation shows that
\diamond
is an action.

For
w\in W
,
\xi\in\mathcal{Q}_{Q^{\vee}}
and
\alpha\in\Phi
define

w(\xi)=\eta_{w}\diamond\xi,\;\text{and}\;\Delta_{\alpha}(\xi)=X_{\alpha}\diamond\xi=\tfrac{1}{x_{\alpha}}(\xi-s_{\alpha}(\xi)).

Lemma 2.2
.

For any
z,z^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Q}_{W_{\operatorname{a}}}
and
\xi\in\mathcal{Q}_{Q^{\vee}}
we have

(i)

\pr(zz^{\prime})=z\diamond\pr(z^{\prime})
, in particular,
\pr(X_{i}z)=X_{i}\diamond\pr(z)=\Delta_{i}(\pr(z))
,
\alpha_{i}\in I
,

(ii)

X_{0}\diamond\xi=\Delta_{-\theta}(\xi)+Z_{\theta}s_{\theta}(\xi)
.

Proof.

(i) Let
z=c\eta_{t_{\lambda}w}
and
z^{\prime}=c^{\prime}\eta_{t_{\lambda^{\prime}}w^{\prime}}
. Then we obtain

\pr(zz^{\prime})=\pr(c\eta_{t_{\lambda}w}c^{\prime}\eta_{t_{\lambda^{\prime}}w^{\prime}})=\pr(ct_{\lambda}w(c^{\prime})\eta_{t_{\lambda+w(\lambda^{\prime})}ww^{\prime}})=ct_{\lambda}w(c^{\prime})\eta_{t_{\lambda+w(\lambda^{\prime})}}=c\eta_{t_{\lambda}w}\diamond c^{\prime}\eta_{t_{\lambda^{\prime}}}.

(ii) For
\xi=c\eta_{t_{\lambda}}
we get

\displaystyle X_{0}\diamond(c\eta_{t_{\lambda}})

\displaystyle=\tfrac{c}{x_{-\theta}}\eta_{t_{\lambda}}-\tfrac{s_{0}(c)}{x_{-\theta}}\eta_{t_{s_{\theta}(\lambda)}t_{\theta^{\vee}}}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{c}{x_{-\theta}}\eta_{t_{\lambda}}-\tfrac{s_{\theta}(c)}{x_{-\theta}}\eta_{t_{s_{\theta}(\lambda)}t_{\theta^{\vee}}}-\tfrac{s_{\theta}(c)}{x_{-\theta}}\eta_{t_{s_{\theta}(\lambda)}}+\tfrac{s_{\theta}(c)}{x_{-\theta}}\eta_{t_{s_{\theta}(\lambda)}}

\displaystyle=\Delta_{-\theta}(c\eta_{t_{\lambda}})+Z_{\theta}s_{\theta}(c\eta_{t_{\lambda}}).\qed

Lemma 2.3
.

The
\diamond
-action of
\mathcal{Q}_{W_{\operatorname{a}}}
on
\mathcal{Q}_{Q^{\vee}}
induces an action of
{\mathbf{D}}_{W_{\operatorname{a}}}
on
{\mathbf{D}}_{W_{\operatorname{a}}/W}
.

Proof.

Let
z,z^{\prime}\in{\mathbf{D}}_{W_{\operatorname{a}}}
, and let
\xi=\pr(z^{\prime})
. Then we have

z\diamond\xi=z\diamond\pr(z^{\prime})=\pr(zz^{\prime})\in\pr({\mathbf{D}}_{W_{\operatorname{a}}})={\mathbf{D}}_{W_{\operatorname{a}}/W}.\qed

Identifying the formal Peterson algebra
{\mathbf{D}}_{Q^{\vee}}
(resp.
\imath(\mathcal{Q}_{Q^{\vee}})
) with
{\mathbf{D}}_{W_{\operatorname{a}}/W}
(resp.
\mathcal{Q}_{Q^{\vee}}
) via the ring homomorphism
\imath
we obtain an action of
{\mathbf{D}}_{W_{\operatorname{a}}}
on
{\mathbf{D}}_{Q^{\vee}}
and an action of
\mathcal{Q}_{W_{\operatorname{a}}}
on
\imath(\mathcal{Q}_{Q^{\vee}})
. From this point on, we write
\xi
as both an element in
{\mathbf{D}}_{W_{\operatorname{a}}/W}
(resp.
\mathcal{Q}_{Q^{\vee}}
) and in
{\mathbf{D}}_{Q^{\vee}}=\imath({\mathbf{D}}_{W_{\operatorname{a}}/W})
(resp.
\imath(\mathcal{Q}_{Q^{\vee}})
). If we consider a product
\xi_{1}\xi_{2}
with
\xi_{i}\in\mathcal{Q}_{Q^{\vee}}
, we may assume it is in
\mathcal{Q}_{W_{\operatorname{a}}}
. However, for the product
z\xi
with
z\in\mathcal{Q}_{W_{\operatorname{a}}}
and
\xi\in\mathcal{Q}_{Q^{\vee}}
, we view
\xi
as an element in
\mathcal{Q}_{W_{\operatorname{a}}}
via the map
\imath
. Following these identifications we obtain

(2)

z\diamond\xi=\psi(z\xi),~\text{where}\;\xi\in{\mathbf{D}}_{Q^{\vee}}\subset\imath(\mathcal{Q}_{Q^{\vee}}),\,z\in{\mathbf{D}}_{W_{\operatorname{a}}}\subset\mathcal{Q}_{W_{\operatorname{a}}},

and Lemma
2.1
gives

(3)

\psi(\xi z)=\xi\psi(z).

Example 2.4
.

Consider the affine root system of extended Dynkin type
\hat{A}_{2}
. It has three simple roots
\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
and the highest root
\theta=\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}
. Denote
X_{ij}=X_{i}X_{j}
for simplicity. Direct computations then give:

\displaystyle\psi(X_{10})

\displaystyle=X_{1}\diamond X_{0}=\tfrac{1}{x_{1}}Z_{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}-\tfrac{1}{x_{1}}Z_{\alpha_{2}},

\displaystyle\psi(X_{20})

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{x_{2}}Z_{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}-\tfrac{1}{x_{2}}Z_{\alpha_{1}},

\displaystyle\psi(X_{210})

\displaystyle=X_{2}\diamond\psi(X_{10}).

Finally, we describe the centre of FADA.

Lemma 2.5
.

(i)
For any
\xi\in\mathcal{Q}_{Q^{\vee}}
and
\alpha_{i}\in I
, we have

\eta_{i}\diamond\xi=\xi~\Longleftrightarrow~\eta_{i}\xi=\xi\eta_{i}.

Moreover, if this condition is satisfied, we have

c\eta_{i}\diamond(\xi\xi^{\prime})=\xi(c\eta_{i}\diamond\xi^{\prime}).

(ii)
The centres of
\mathcal{Q}_{W_{\operatorname{a}}}
and
{\mathbf{D}}_{W_{\operatorname{a}}}
can be described as follows:

\displaystyle Z(\mathcal{Q}_{W_{\operatorname{a}}})

\displaystyle=\{\xi\in\mathcal{Q}_{Q^{\vee}}\mid\eta_{w}\xi=\xi\eta_{w},\forall w\in W\}=(\mathcal{Q}_{Q^{\vee}})^{W},

\displaystyle Z({\mathbf{D}}_{W_{\operatorname{a}}})

\displaystyle=\{\xi\in{\mathbf{D}}_{Q^{\vee}}\mid\eta_{w}\xi=\xi\eta_{w},\forall w\in W\}=({\mathbf{D}}_{Q^{\vee}})^{W}.

(iii)
There are ring homomorphisms

\mathcal{Q}_{W_{\operatorname{a}}}\to\End_{(\mathcal{Q}_{Q^{\vee}})^{W}}(\mathcal{Q}_{Q^{\vee}}),~{\mathbf{D}}_{W_{\operatorname{a}}}\to\End_{({\mathbf{D}}_{Q^{\vee}})^{W}}({\mathbf{D}}_{Q^{\vee}}),~z\mapsto z\diamond\text{-}.

Proof.

(i) For a given
\xi=\sum_{\lambda}c_{\lambda}\eta_{t_{\lambda}}
,
c_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{Q}
we get

\eta_{i}\xi=\sum_{\lambda}s_{i}(c_{\lambda})\eta_{i}\eta_{t_{\lambda}}=\sum_{\lambda}s_{i}(c_{\lambda})\eta_{t_{s_{i}(\lambda)}}\eta_{i}=\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}}s_{i}(c_{s_{i}(\lambda^{\prime})})\eta_{t_{\lambda^{\prime}}}\eta_{i},\text{ where }\lambda^{\prime}=s_{i}(\lambda).

On the other side, we have

\eta_{i}\diamond\xi=\psi(\eta_{i}\xi)=\psi(\eta_{i}\sum_{\lambda}c_{\lambda}\eta_{t_{\lambda}})=\sum_{\lambda}s_{i}(c_{s_{i}(\lambda)})\eta_{t_{\lambda}}.

Therefore,
\eta_{i}\diamond\xi=\xi
if and only if
s_{i}(c_{s_{i}(\lambda)})=c_{\lambda}

It is also easy to see that the surjection
\imath^{*}\colon\mathcal{Q}_{W_{\operatorname{a}}}^{*}\to\mathcal{Q}_{Q^{\vee}}^{*}
induces a surjective map

\imath^{*\prime}\colon\mathop{\rm HH_{0}}({\mathbf{D}}_{W_{\operatorname{a}}}^{*})\to{\mathbf{D}}_{Q^{\vee}}^{*}.

Our goal is to show that the isomorphism and the diagram of Lemma
5.2
can be restricted to the formal Peterson subalgebra
{\mathbf{D}}_{Q^{\vee}}
. Namely, we want to prove the following

Theorem 5.3
.

The map
\imath^{*\prime}
gives an isomorphism of Hopf algebras
\mathop{\rm HH_{0}}({\mathbf{D}}_{W_{\operatorname{a}}}^{*})\simeq{\mathbf{D}}_{Q^{\vee}}^{*}
.

Since the product structure on both the domain and the codomain is induced by the coproduct structure

\mathcal{Q}_{W_{\operatorname{a}}}\to\mathcal{Q}_{W_{\operatorname{a}}}\otimes\mathcal{Q}_{W_{\operatorname{a}}},\quad a\eta_{w}\mapsto a\eta_{w}\otimes\eta_{w},

where the codomain is the tensor product of left
\mathcal{Q}
-modules, the map
\imath^{*\prime}
is a ring homomorphism. Moreover, since the coproduct structure on both the domain and the codomain is induced by the product structure in
\mathcal{Q}_{W_{\operatorname{a}}}
and
\mathcal{Q}_{Q^{\vee}}
, the map
\imath^{*\prime}
is a coalgebra homomorphism. Therefore, it only suffices to prove the injectivity of
\imath^{*\prime}
.

To prove the latter we introduce the following filtration on the dual
{\mathbf{D}}_{W_{\operatorname{a}}}^{*}
of the FADA:

Definition 5.4
.

Let
w_{\lambda}
be as defined in the appendix. Set
F_{i}=\bigcup_{\ell(w_{\lambda})\geq i}t_{\lambda}W
.
For any
f\in\mathcal{Q}_{W_{\operatorname{a}}}^{*}=\operatorname{Hom}(W_{\operatorname{a}},\mathcal{Q})
set
\supp f=\{w\in W_{\operatorname{a}}\mid f(w)\neq 0\}
.
Define the
i
th stratum of the filtration to be

\mathcal{Z}_{i}=\{f\in{\mathbf{D}}_{W_{\operatorname{a}}}^{*}\mid\supp f\subseteq F_{i}\}.

We have

\mathcal{Z}_{i}=\prod_{w\in F_{i}}S\cdot Y_{I_{w}}^{*}
.
Since
x\bullet af_{u}=u(x)af_{u}
and
x\odot af_{u}=xaf_{u}
where
x,a\in S
,
u\in W_{\operatorname{a}}
, so each

\mathcal{Z}_{i}
is a
S
-bimodule.

Consider the following two conditions:

(10)

\displaystyle f(\eta_{t_{\lambda}u})\in x_{\alpha}^{\ell_{\alpha}(w_{\lambda})}S\quad\text{ for any $u\in W$},

(11)

\displaystyle f(\eta_{t_{\lambda}u}-\eta_{t_{\lambda}s_{\alpha}u})\in x_{\alpha}^{\ell_{\alpha}(w_{\lambda})+1}S\quad\text{ for any $u\in W$ and root $\alpha\in\Phi$}.

Lemma 5.5
.

Elements of
{\mathcal{Z}}_{i}

satisfy the conditions
(

).

Proof.

Let

and
\ell(w_{\lambda})=i

Assume

For

below,

\displaystyle(Z_{\alpha}^{\ell_{\alpha}(w_{\lambda})}\eta_{t_{\lambda}}\odot f)(\eta_{u})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{x_{-\alpha}^{\ell_{\alpha}(w_{\lambda})}}f\big((1-\eta_{t_{\alpha}^{\vee}})^{\ell_{\alpha}(w_{\lambda})}\eta_{t_{\lambda}u}\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{x_{-\alpha}^{\ell_{\alpha}(w_{\lambda})}}f(\eta_{t_{\lambda}u})\in S,\;\text{ and}

\displaystyle(Z_{\alpha}^{\ell_{\alpha}(w_{\lambda})}\eta_{t_{\lambda}}X_{\alpha}\odot f)(\eta_{u})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{x_{-\alpha}^{\ell_{\alpha}(w_{\lambda})}x_{\alpha}}f\big((1-\eta_{t_{\alpha}^{\vee}})^{\ell_{\alpha}(w_{\lambda})}(\eta_{t_{\lambda}u}-\eta_{t_{\lambda}s_{\alpha}u})\big)

\displaystyle(Z_{-\alpha}^{\ell_{\alpha}(w_{\lambda})}\odot f)(\eta_{u})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{x_{\alpha}^{\ell_{\alpha}(w_{\lambda})}}f(\eta_{t_{\lambda}w})\in S,\;\text{ and}

Appendix A

In this section, we prove several combinatorial properties of the affine Weyl group that are used in the proof of Theorem
5.3
.

For
w\in W_{a}
, denote

\ell_{\alpha}(w)=|\{\beta=\pm\alpha+k\delta>0|w^{-1}(\beta)<0\}|.

It is clear that
\ell(w)=\sum_{\alpha>0}\ell_{\alpha}(w)
.
Also denote by
w_{\lambda}\in W_{a}^{-}
the minimal representative of
t_{\lambda}W
. Then

w_{\lambda}\leq w_{\mu}
if and only if there exists
w\in t_{\lambda}W
and
y\in t_{\mu}W
such that
w\leq y
. Note that
w\leq y
implies
\ell_{\alpha}(w)\leq\ell_{\alpha}(y)
for all
\alpha\in\Phi_{+}
, so after fixing
\alpha

\ell_{\alpha}(w_{\lambda})
becomes minimal for elements
w
from
t_{\lambda}W
.

Lemma A.1
.

We have the following property:

\ell_{\alpha}(w_{\lambda})=\begin{cases}-\left<\lambda,\alpha\right>,&\text{ if }\left<\lambda,\alpha\right>\leq 0,\\
\left<\lambda,\alpha\right>-1,&\text{ if }\left<\lambda,\alpha\right>>0.\end{cases}

Proof.

For
w=t_{\lambda}u\in t_{\lambda}W
,
\beta=\pm\alpha+k\delta>0
(so
k\geq 0
), we have

(13)

w^{-1}(\beta)=w^{-1}(\pm\alpha+k\delta)=\pm u^{-1}\alpha+(k\pm\left<\lambda,\alpha\right>)\delta.

If
\langle\lambda,\alpha\rangle\leq 0
, then
w^{-1}(\beta)<0
implies
\beta=\alpha+k\delta
, and moreover,
k\in[0,\ell_{\alpha}(w)-1]
(since
\ell_{\alpha}(w)=|Inv_{\alpha}(w)|
). So we have

(14)

\ell_{\alpha}(w)=-\left<\lambda,\alpha\right>+\begin{cases}1,&u^{-1}(\alpha)<0,\\
0,&u^{-1}(\alpha)>0,\end{cases}

and the minimal value is
-\langle\lambda,\alpha\rangle
.

If
\left<\lambda,\alpha\right>>0
, then
w^{-1}(\beta)<0
if and only if
\beta=-\alpha+k\delta
and
k\in[1,\ell_{\alpha}(w)]
, in which case we have

(15)

\ell_{\alpha}(w)=\left<\lambda,\alpha\right>-\begin{cases}1,&u^{-1}(\alpha)<0,\\
0,&u^{-1}(\alpha)>0.\end{cases}

The minimal value is
\langle\lambda,\alpha\rangle-1
.
∎

Lemma A.2
.

Let
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}
,
\lambda\in Q^{\vee}
, and
k\in[0,\ell_{\alpha}(w_{\lambda})]
.

If
\langle\lambda,\alpha\rangle\leq 0
, then

w_{\lambda}>w_{\lambda+k\alpha^{\vee}}
. If
\langle\lambda,\alpha\rangle>0
, then

w_{\lambda}>w_{\lambda-k\alpha^{\vee}}
.

Proof.

If
\left<\lambda,\alpha\right>\leq 0
, consider
w\in t_{\lambda}W
such that
w=t_{\lambda}u
with
u^{-1}(\alpha)<0
.
We have

w^{-1}(\alpha)=u^{-1}(\alpha)+\langle\lambda,\alpha\rangle\delta<0,

so
w>s_{\alpha}w
. From (
14
), we get
\ell_{\alpha}(w)=\ell_{\alpha}(w_{\lambda})+1=-\left<\lambda,\alpha\right>+1
.
Since
1\leq k\leq\ell_{\alpha}(w_{\lambda})=-\left<\lambda,\alpha\right>
, we get

(s_{\alpha}w)^{-1}(-\alpha+k\delta)=u^{-1}\alpha+(k+\left<\lambda,\alpha\right>)\delta<0,

which implies

s_{\alpha}w>s_{-\alpha+k\delta}s_{\alpha}w=t_{k\alpha^{\vee}}w.

Therefore,
w>t_{k\alpha^{\vee}}w
. Since
w\in t_{\lambda}W
and
t_{k\alpha^{\vee}}w\in t_{\lambda+k\alpha^{\vee}}W
, we get

w_{\lambda}>w_{\lambda+k\alpha^{\vee}}
.

If
\left<\lambda,\alpha\right>>0
, consider
w=t_{\lambda}u
with
u^{-1}(\alpha)>0
. We have

w^{-1}(-\alpha+\delta)=u^{-1}(\alpha)+(1-\left<\lambda,\alpha\right>)\delta<0,

so
w>s_{-\alpha+\delta}w
. From (
15
), we have
\ell_{\alpha}(w)=\langle\lambda,\alpha\rangle=\ell_{\alpha}(w_{\lambda})+1.

Since
1\leq k\leq\ell_{\alpha}(w)-1=\ell_{\alpha}(w_{\lambda})=\left<\lambda,\alpha\right>-1
, we get

s_{-\alpha+\delta}w>s_{\alpha+(k-1)\delta}s_{-\alpha+\delta}x=t_{-k\alpha^{\vee}}w.

So
w>t_{-k\alpha^{\vee}}w
. Since
w\in t_{\lambda}W
and
t_{k\alpha^{\vee}}w\in t_{\lambda+k\alpha^{\vee}}W
, we have

w_{\lambda}>w_{\lambda+k\alpha^{\vee}}
.
∎

The following lemma is not used in this paper, but it is interesting on its own.

Lemma A.3
.

If
\langle\lambda,\alpha\rangle=0
, then we have the following sequence

w_{\lambda}<w_{\lambda+\alpha^{\vee}}<w_{\lambda-\alpha^{\vee}}<w_{\lambda+2\alpha^{\vee}}<w_{\lambda-2\alpha^{\vee}}<\cdots.

If
\langle\lambda,\alpha\rangle=1
, then we have the following sequence:

w_{\lambda}<w_{\lambda-\alpha^{\vee}}<w_{\lambda+\alpha^{\vee}}<w_{\lambda-2\alpha^{\vee}}<w_{\lambda+2\alpha^{\vee}}<\cdots.

The lengths
\ell_{\alpha}
are given by
(0,1,2,3,4,\ldots)
.

Proof.

We only prove the case when
\langle\lambda,\alpha\rangle=0
. Consider
\lambda+k\alpha^{\vee}
and
\lambda-k\alpha^{\vee}
with
k>0
, then
\langle\lambda-k\alpha^{\vee},\alpha\rangle=-2k<0
, and
2k=\ell_{\alpha}(w_{\lambda-k\alpha^{\vee}})
, so by Lemma

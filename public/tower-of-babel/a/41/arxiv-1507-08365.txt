A Family of Finite-Dimensional Representations of Generalized Double Affine Hecke Algebras of Higher Rank
Yuchen FU and Seth SHELLEY-ABRAHAMSON

Abstract
We give explicit constructions of some finite-dimensional representations of generalized double affine Hecke algebras (GDAHA) of higher rank using R R -matrices for U q ​ ( 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 N ) U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{N}) . Our construction is motivated by an analogous construction of Silvia Montarani in the rational case. Using the Drinfeld–Kohno theorem for Knizhnik–Zamolodchikov differential equations, we prove that the explicit representations we produce correspond to Montarani’s representations under a monodromy functor introduced by Etingof, Gan, and Oblomkov.

Introduction

Generalized double affine Hecke algebras of higher rank (GDAHA) are a family of algebras that generalize the well-known Cherednik algebras in representation theory and were first introduced by Etingof, Oblomkov, and Rains in
[
5
]
(the rank 1 case) and
[
4
]
(the rank
n
case). They have been related to del Pezzo surfaces in algebraic geometry and Calogero–Moser integrable systems. A degenerate version of GDAHA, known as the rational GDAHA, was also introduced in
[
4
]
. In
[
14
]
, Montarani introduces two constructions of finite-dimensional representations of rational GDAHA, one using
D
-modules and the other using explicit Lie theoretic methods. The latter of these methods, involving an isotypic subspace of a tensor product with a tensor power of the vector representation of
\mathfrak{sl}_{N}
, is similar in spirit to the Arakawa–Suzuki functor from the BGG category
\mathcal{O}
of
\mathfrak{sl}_{N}
-modules to the category of finite-dimensional representations of degenerate affine Hecke algebras of type
A
constructed in
[
1
]
and to the construction of representations of affine braid groups given by Orellana and Ram in
[
15
]
. There has been much interest in and effort devoted to developing similar techniques for the representation theory of related algebraic objects, for instance by Calaque, Enriquez, and Etingof
[
2
]
for degenerate double affine Hecke algebras and by Jordan
[
9
]
in the nondegenerate case, among others.

In this paper, we generalize the Montarani’s Lie theoretic construction to the non-degenerate GDAHA using
R
-matrices for the quantum groups
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{N})
. Furthermore, we show that the explicit representations we produce are equivalent to the image of Montarani’s representations under a monodromy functor constructed in
[
4
]
which introduces an action of a nondegenerate GDAHA on a finite-dimensional representation of a rational GDAHA. In the same sense that Jordan’s work
[
9
]
is a generalization of the work of Calaque, Enriquez, and Etingof
[
2
]
to the nondegenerate case, this paper generalizes Montarani’s construction to the nondegenerate case.

The structure of this paper is as follows. In Section
2
we fix the notation we use for quantum groups and review their basic properties and also introduce the definition of GDAHA. Following that, our explicit construction of representations is given in Section
3
, along with its proof. The calculation that relates this representation with the monodromy representation given in
[
4
]
is done in Section
4
.

2
Background

2.1
Generalized double affine Hecke algebras of higher rank (GDAHA)

We recall the definition of GDAHA in
[
4
]
. Let
D
be a star-shaped finite graph that is not finite Dynkin with
m
legs and leg lengths
d_{1},\dots,d_{m}
(number of vertices on each leg, including the center).

Definition 2.1

(GDAHA)
.

H_{n}(D)
, the generalized double affine Hecke algebra of rank
n
associated with graph
D
, is the associative algebra generated over
\mathbb{C}[u_{1,1}^{\pm 1},\dots,u_{1d_{1}}^{\pm 1},u_{2,1}^{\pm 1},\dots
,
u_{m,d_{m}}^{\pm 1},t^{\pm 1}]

by invertible generators
U_{1},\dots,U_{m},T_{1},\dots,T_{n-1}
and the following relations:

1.

U_{1}U_{2}\cdots U_{m}T_{1}T_{2}\cdots T_{n-2}T_{n-1}T_{n-1}T_{n-2}\cdots T_{2}T_{1}=1
;

2.

T_{i}T_{i+1}T_{i}=T_{i+1}T_{i}T_{i+1}
for
1\leq i<n-1
;

3.

[T_{i},T_{j}]=0
for
|i-j|>1
;

4.

[U_{i},T_{j}]=0
for
1<i\leq n-1,1<j\leq m
;

5.

[U_{i},T_{1}U_{i}T_{1}]=0
for
1\leq i\leq m
;

6.

[U_{i},T_{1}^{-1}U_{j}T_{1}]=0
for
1\leq i<j\leq m
;

7.

\prod\limits_{j=1}^{d_{k}}(U_{k}-u_{k,j})=0
for
1\leq k\leq m
;

8.

T_{i}-T_{i}^{-1}=t-t^{-1}
for
1\leq i\leq n-1
.

The rank-
n
GDAHA
H_{n,m}
can be seen as a quotient of the group algebra of the fundamental group of the configuration space of
n
unordered points on the
m
-punctured sphere, where the quotient is by the eigenvalue relations (
7
) and (
8
). From this perspective,
U_{i}
is represented by a path in the configuration space in which one of the points loops around a missing point
\alpha_{i}
, and
T_{i}
is represented by a typical braid group generator exchanging the positions of two points in the configuration. Note that the generators
T_{i}
satisfy the relations of the finite-type Hecke algebra of type
A_{n-1}
.

2.2
The quantum group
\boldsymbol{U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{N})}

Due to the large volume of literature and varying conventions regarding quantum groups, it is necessary to fix the notations that we work with. We use conventions compatible with, for example,
[
6
,
8
,
12
]
. Throughout this paper, it is assumed that
q
is a nonzero complex number that is not a root of unity.

For every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, let
[n]_{q}
denote the associated symmetrized
q
-number

\displaystyle[n]_{q}:=\dfrac{q^{n}-q^{-n}}{q-q^{-1}}.

In particular, we have

\displaystyle[n]_{q}=q^{n-1}+q^{n-3}+\cdots+q^{-n+3}+q^{-n+1}

for
n>0
and
[n]_{q}=-[-n]_{q}
for all
n
. Define
q
-binomial coefficients by

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}a\\
n\end{bmatrix}_{q}:=\dfrac{[a]_{q}[a-1]_{q}\cdots[a-n+1]_{q}}{[1]_{q}[2]_{q}\cdots[n]_{q}}

for all
a\in\mathbb{Z}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, so in particular
\begin{bmatrix}a\\
0\end{bmatrix}_{q}=1
by the usual convention on empty products. Define
q
-factorials
[a]_{q}^{!}
for
a\in\mathbb{N}
by

\displaystyle[a]_{q}^{!}=[1]_{q}[2]_{q}\cdots[a]_{q}.

Note that
[0]_{q}^{!}=1
.

Definition 2.2

(quantum group
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{N})
)
.

Let
A=(a_{ij})
be the Cartan matrix for the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sl}_{N}
. The quantum group
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{N})
is the associative
\mathbb{C}
-algebra with generators
E_{i}
,
F_{i}
,
K_{i}
,
K_{i}^{-1}
(
1\leq i<N
) and relations:

\displaystyle[K_{i},K_{j}]=0,

\displaystyle K_{i}K_{i}^{-1}=1=K_{i}^{-1}K_{i},

\displaystyle K_{i}E_{j}=q^{a_{ij}}E_{j}K_{i},

\displaystyle K_{i}F_{j}=q^{-a_{ij}}F_{j}K_{i},

\displaystyle[E_{i},F_{j}]=\delta_{ij}\frac{K_{i}-K_{i}^{-1}}{q_{i}-q_{i}^{-1}},

\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{1-a_{ij}}(-1)^{r}\begin{bmatrix}1-a_{ij}\\
r\end{bmatrix}_{q}E_{i}^{1-a_{ij}-r}E_{j}E_{i}^{r}=0,\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{1-a_{ij}}(-1)^{r}\begin{bmatrix}1-a_{ij}\\
r\end{bmatrix}_{q}F_{i}^{1-a_{ij}-r}F_{j}F_{i}^{r}=0\quad\text{if}\ \ i\neq j,

where
\delta_{ij}
is the Kronecker delta and
[a,b]=ab-ba
.

We will use the Hopf algebra structure on
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{N})
specified in the following standard proposition:

Proposition 2.3

(Hopf algebra structure on
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{N})
)
.

Denote
U=U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{N})
. The assignments below extend to unique algebra homomorphisms
(\Delta\colon U\to U\otimes U,

\varepsilon\colon U\to\mathbb{C},

\eta\colon\mathbb{C}\to U,

S\colon U\to U^{\rm op})
that give
U
a Hopf algebra structure:

\displaystyle\Delta(K_{i})=K_{i}\otimes K_{i},\qquad\Delta(E_{i})=E_{i}\otimes K_{i}+1\otimes E_{i},\qquad\Delta(F_{i})=F_{i}\otimes 1+K_{i}^{-1}\otimes F_{i},

\displaystyle\varepsilon(E_{i})=\varepsilon(F_{i})=0,\qquad\varepsilon(K_{i})=1,\qquad S(K_{i})=K_{i}^{-1},\qquad S(E_{i})=-E_{i}K_{i}^{-1},

\displaystyle S(F_{i})=-K_{i}F_{i}.

\boldsymbol{R}
-matrices

In this section we will fix our conventions for

]

Definition 2.4
.

For a given pair

Proposition 2.5
.

There exists an element

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}\in 1+U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})_{>0}\,\hat{\otimes}\,U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl_{n}})_{<0}

in an appropriate completion
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{N})\hat{\otimes}U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{N})
such that the operators

\displaystyle\mathfrak{R}:=f\circ\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}},\qquad R:=P\circ\mathfrak{R}=P\circ f\circ\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}

on tensor products of finite-dimensional
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{N})
-modules satisfy, for any finite-dimensional modules

,
V_{2}
,
V_{3}
:

•

is an isomorphism of

-modules,

•

\mathfrak{R}_{12,3}=\mathfrak{R}_{13}\mathfrak{R}_{23}
,

•

\mathfrak{R}_{1,23}=\mathfrak{R}_{13}\mathfrak{R}_{12}
,

where
P
denotes the operator exchanging tensor factors and the subscripts indicate the tensor factor positions on which
\mathfrak{R}
acts.

For an explicit description of the element
\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}
, see
[
12
, Section 8.3.3]
.

The operator
\mathfrak{R}
satisfies the quantum Yang–Baxter equation:

Proposition 2.6

(QYBE)
.

\mathfrak{R}_{12}\mathfrak{R}_{13}\mathfrak{R}_{23}=\mathfrak{R}_{23}\mathfrak{R}_{13}\mathfrak{R}_{12}
.

Corollary 2.7
.

For
1\leq i\neq j<n
, let
R_{i}=P_{i,i+1}\circ\mathfrak{R}_{i,i+1}
, then we have
R_{i}R_{i+1}R_{i}=R_{i+1}R_{i}R_{i+1}
and
R_{i}R_{j}=R_{j}R_{i}
for

, and in particular the
R_{i}
operators yield a representation of the braid group
B_{n}
on
V^{\otimes n}
for any finite-dimensional
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{N})
-representation

, and this representation is functorial in

.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
\mathbb{C}^{N}
be the
N
-dimensional vector representation of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{N})
. Then
R_{i}

(1\leq i<n)
act on the space
\big(\mathbb{C}^{N}\big)^{\otimes n}
. The operators

on
\big(\mathbb{C}^{N}\big)^{\otimes n}
act with eigenvalues

,
-q^{-1}
and in particular define a representation of the Hecke algebra of type

.

]

2.4
Ribbon category structure on
\boldsymbol{U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{N})\text{-}\mathrm{mod}_{\rm f.d.}}

Recall that a type-1 representation of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{N})
is a representation
V
such that
V
has a weight decomposition
V=\oplus_{\lambda}V_{\lambda}
where the direct sum is over the weight lattice for
\mathfrak{sl}_{N}
and
V_{\lambda}
is the subspace

\displaystyle V_{\lambda}:=\big\{v\in C\colon K_{\mu}(v)=q^{(\lambda,\mu)}v~\text{for all roots}~\mu\big\}.

Let
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{N})\text{-}\mathrm{mod}_{\rm f.d.}
denote the category of finite-dimensional type-1 representations of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{N})
. We will only consider representations in this category.

The operator
R
, along with the Hopf algebra structure on
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{N})
, gives the category
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{N})\text{-}\mathrm{mod}_{\rm f.d.}
of finite-dimensional type-1 representations of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{N})
the structure of a braided rigid tensor category. In fact,
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{N})\text{-}\mathrm{mod}_{\rm f.d.}
has even richer structure – it is a
ribbon category
. In particular, there exists an automorphism
\theta
of the identity functor on
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{N})\text{-}\mathrm{mod}_{\rm f.d.}
which is given by multiplication by
q^{(\lambda,\lambda+2\rho)}
(where, as usual,
\rho
is the half-sum of the positive roots) on any irreducible representation with highest weight
\lambda
, and
\theta
satisfies the compatibility

\theta_{V(\lambda)\otimes V(\mu)}=R^{2}\circ\big(\theta_{V(\lambda)}\otimes\theta_{V(\mu)}\big),

(2.1)

where
V(\lambda)
and
V(\mu)
are the irreducible highest weight representations with highest weights
\lambda
and
\mu
, respectively, and
R
is the
R
-matrix introduced above. An
h
-adic version of these statements can be found in Proposition 21 in Section 8.4.3 of
[
12
]
, and it is routine to translate the result into the setting of numeric
q
.

\boldsymbol{R}
-matrices

3.1
The construction

Again, we assume
q
is a nonzero complex number which is not a root of unity, and all representations of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{N})
we consider will be type-1 and finite-dimensional.

Let
n\geq 1
be an integer, let
V_{1},\dots,V_{m}
be irreducible finite-dimensional highest weight modules for
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{N})
, let
V_{m+1}=V_{m+2}=\dots=V_{m+n}=\mathbb{C}^{N}
be copies of its vector representation, and let

\displaystyle V=V_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes V_{m}\otimes V_{m+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes V_{m+n}.

Let
E
be the
0
-isotypic component of
V
. For
1\leq i<m+n
let
R_{i}
denote
\operatorname{id}^{\otimes i-1}\otimes R\otimes\operatorname{id}^{\otimes m+n-i-1}
.

Theorem 3.1

(main theorem)
.

Let
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{m}\in\mathbb{C}
be any complex numbers and let

. Then for
t=q
, some specific values of
u_{kj}
, and some graph

, the following formulas for
1\leq i<n
and
1\leq k\leq m
define a representation of the GDAHA of rank
n
attached to
D
on
E

•

T_{i}=q^{1/N}R_{m+i}
, and

•

U_{k}=q^{2[(N-c)/m+\lambda_{k}/N]}R_{m}R_{m-1}\cdots R_{k+1}R_{k}^{2}R_{k+1}^{-1}\cdots R_{m-1}^{-1}R_{m}^{-1}
.

Note that the
T_{i}
are endomorphisms of
E
because
V_{m+1},\dots,V_{m+n}
are all copies of
\mathbb{C}^{N}
and
R_{m+i}
acts as a
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{N})
-module isomorphism. The
U_{k}
act on
E
for similar reasons. The dependence of the graph
D
and the parameters
u_{kj}
on the representations
V_{1},\dots,V_{m}
and the scalars
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{m}\in\mathbb{C}
is given later in equation (
3.1
).

3.2
Validity of the defining relations

We need to verify that the relations (

3.2.1
Eigenvalues of

Recall from Section

Lemma 3.2

3.2.2
The first relation

If we expand relation (

[

. In this case the power of

4
Equivalence with the monodromy representation

4.1
The monodromy functor

In
[
4
]
, the authors introduced a certain connection of Knizhnik–Zamolodchikov type whose monodromy defines a functor from the category of finite-dimensional representations of a rational GDAHA (rGDAHA)
B_{n}
to the category of finite-dimensional representations of a corresponding non-degenerate GDAHA
H_{n}
attached to the same diagram

with m legs of lengths
d_{1},\dots,d_{m}
. Recall that the rGDAHA
B_{n}
is the algebra generated over
\mathbb{C}[\gamma_{1,1},\dots,\gamma_{m,d_{m}},\nu]
by elements

(
1\leq i\leq n,\,1\leq k\leq m
) and the symmetric group
S_{n}
, such that the following hold for every

with
i\neq j
and every
k,l\in[1,m]
:

1.

s_{ij}Y_{i,k}=Y_{j,k}s_{ij}
;

2.

s_{ij}Y_{h,k}=Y_{h,k}s_{ij}
if
h\neq i,j
;

3.

\prod\limits_{j=1}^{d_{k}}(Y_{i,k}-\gamma_{k,j})=0
;

4.

\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}Y_{i,j}=\nu\sum\limits_{j\neq i}s_{ij}
;

5.

[Y_{i,k},Y_{j,k}]=\nu(Y_{i,k}-Y_{j,k})s_{ij}
;

6.

[Y_{i,k},Y_{j,l}]=0
if
k\neq l
.

Let us recall the monodromy functor from
[
4
]
. Fix a finite-dimensional
B_{n}
module
M
. For convenience, let
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{m}\in\mathbb{C}
be distinct points defined by
\alpha_{i}=-m-1+i
and choose the basepoint
\textbf{z}_{0}=(1,\dots,n)
in the ordered configuration space
\text{Conf}_{n}(\mathbb{C}\backslash\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{m}\})
of
n
-points in the
m

[
4
]
the Knizhnik–Zamolodchikov-style connection

\displaystyle\nabla_{\rm EGO}:=d-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{Y_{i,k}}{z_{i}-\alpha_{k}}-\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{\nu s_{ij}}{z_{i}-z_{j}}\right)dz_{i}

(4.1)

is introduced on the trivial vector bundle
E_{M}
with fiber
M
over
\text{Conf}_{n}(\mathbb{C}\backslash\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{m}\})
. It follows readily from the defining relations of
B_{n}
and a calculation that the connection
\nabla
is flat and has trivial residue at
\infty
. This connection is visibly
S_{n}
-equivariant and so descends to a connection on the unordered configuration space
\text{UConf}_{n}(\mathbb{C}\backslash\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{m}\})
. The residue condition implies that the monodromy of this connection at
\textbf{z}_{0}
defines a representation

\displaystyle\rho_{M}\colon\ \pi_{1}\big(\text{UConf}_{n}\big(\mathbb{CP}^{1}\backslash\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{m}\}\big),\textbf{z}_{0}\big)\to\operatorname{Aut}(M).

The algebra
H_{n}
may be interpreted as the quotient of the group algebra

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}\pi_{1}\big(\text{UConf}_{n}\big(\mathbb{CP}^{1}\backslash\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{m}\}\big),\textbf{z}_{0}\big)

by the eigenvalue relations (
7
) and (
8
) of Definition
2.1
, where
U_{i}
and
T_{i}
are the generators represented by the following loops:

In
[
4
, Section 4.2]
it is shown that the monodromy operators
\rho_{M}(T_{i})
and
\rho_{M}(U_{k})
satisfy the eigenvalue conditions (
7
) and (
8
) for
t=e^{-\pi i\nu}
,
u_{k,j}=e^{2\pi i\gamma_{k,j}}
and therefore define a finite-dimensional representation of

with these parameter values. This construction is clearly functorial in
M
and defines a functor

\displaystyle F\colon\ B_{n}\text{-}\mathrm{mod}_{\rm f.d.}\rightarrow H_{n}\text{-}\mathrm{mod}_{\rm f.d.},

where as before
\text{-}\mathrm{mod}_{\rm f.d.}
denotes the category of finite-dimensional representations. Note that this functor is the identity at the level of vector spaces.

4.2
Montarani’s rGDAHA representation

We now recall the construction of a family of finite-dimensional representations of the rGDAHA
B_{n}
given in
[
14
, Section 5]
. Let
V_{1},\dots,V_{m}
be irreducible finite-dimensional representations of
\mathfrak{gl}_{N}
, and let
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{m}\in\mathbb{C}
be the scalars by which the identity matrix
I\in\mathfrak{gl}_{N}
acts, respectively. Let
\chi
be a character of
\mathfrak{gl}_{N}
, and denote the
\chi
-isotypic subspace of
V_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes V_{m}\otimes\big(\mathbb{C}^{N}\big)^{\otimes n}
by

\displaystyle E_{n,\chi}:=\big\{v\in V_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes V_{m}\otimes\big(\mathbb{C}^{N}\big)^{\otimes n}\,|\,xv=\chi^{\prime}(x)v~\forall\,x\in\mathfrak{gl}_{N}\big\}.

Let
c\in\mathbb{C}
be the scalar such that
\chi=c\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}
. Note that if
E_{n,\chi}\neq 0
then we have the relation
n+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}\lambda_{j}=cN
defining
c
in Theorem
3.1
.

Let
\Omega^{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}\in\mathfrak{gl}_{N}\otimes\mathfrak{gl}_{N}
be the Casimir tensor of
\mathfrak{gl}_{N}
, and let
\Omega^{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}_{ij}
represent the tensor that acts as
\Omega^{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}
on the
i
th and
j
th tensor factors and as identity on other factors.

Theorem 4.1

(
[
14
, Theorem 5.1]
)
.

For any choice of
\nu\in\mathbb{C}
, the assignments
s_{ij}=\Omega^{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}_{m+i,m+j}
, and
Y_{i,k}=-\nu\big(\Omega^{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}_{k,m+i}+\frac{N-c}{m}\big)
define a representation of the rGDAHA
B_{n}
on
E_{n,\chi^{\prime}}
for an appropriate parameter value
\gamma
.

4.3
Equivalence of the representations

We may apply the monodromy functor of
[
4
]
to the representation of the rGDAHA
B_{n}
in the previous theorem to produce a representation
F(E_{n,\chi})
of a corresponding GDAHA
H_{n}
. On the other hand, given the representations
V_{1},\dots,V_{m+n}
of
\mathfrak{gl}_{N}
, we can extract the constants
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}
as above. When
\nu\notin\mathbb{Q}
, the associated parameter
t=e^{-\pi i\nu}
is a nonzero complex number which is not a root of unity. Let
q=t
. If
V_{i}^{q}
denotes the irreducible representation of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{N})
with highest weight corresponding to the highest weight of
V_{i}
, then from the
V_{i}^{q}
and the
\lambda_{i}
we may produce, using Theorem
3.1
, a finite-dimensional representation
E
of an associated non-degenerate GDAHA
H_{n}^{\prime}
.

Theorem 4.2
.

For
\nu\notin\mathbb{Q}
, the parameters of the GDAHAs
H_{n}
and
H_{n}^{\prime}
agree, and the representations
F(E_{n,\chi})
and
E
are equivalent.

Proof.

Let us first check that the eigenvalue parameters agree. The parameters
t
for
H_{n}
and
H_{n}^{\prime}
agree and equal
e^{-\pi i\nu}
by definition. Let
\mu_{k}
denote the highest weight of
V_{k}
, let
d_{k}
be the number of non-isomorphic irreducible subrepresentations in
V_{k}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{N}=\bigoplus_{j}W_{j}
, and let their highest weights be
\eta_{k,1},\dots,\eta_{k,d_{k}}
. Then, by
[
14
, Lemma 5.2]
, the graph
D
attached to the rGAHA
B_{n}
in Theorem
4.1
has leg lengths
d_{1},\dots,d_{m}
and parameter values
\gamma_{k,j}=-\nu(w_{j}+(N-c)/m)
where
w_{j}
is the eigenvalue of
\Omega^{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}
on
W_{j}
. Therefore, with
q=e^{-i\pi\nu}
, the
u_{kj}
parameter for the algebra
H_{n}
is given by

\displaystyle u_{kj}:=e^{2\pi i\gamma_{k,j}}=q^{2(N-c)/m+(\eta_{k,j},\eta_{k,j}+2\rho)-(\mu_{k},\mu_{k}+2\rho)-(N-1/N)+2\lambda_{j}/N},

where the term
2\lambda_{j}/N
comes from the discrepancy
\Omega^{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}=\Omega^{\mathfrak{sl}_{N}}+\frac{1}{N}I\otimes I
between the Casimir tensors for
\mathfrak{gl}_{N}
and
\mathfrak{sl}_{N}
. This agrees with the parameter values for
H_{n}^{\prime}
obtained in Section
3.2.1
.

The strategy for proving the equivalence statement in Theorem
4.2
is to relate the connection
\nabla_{\rm EGO}
to the classical KZ connection and to use the Drinfeld–Kohno theorem to relate the monodromy of the latter connection to
R
-matrices for
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{N})
. Let
V_{1},\dots,V_{m+n}
be finite-dimensional irreducible
\mathfrak{gl}_{N}
-representations as in Montarani’s construction, with
V_{m+i}
a copy of the vector representation
\mathbb{C}^{N}
for
1\leq i\leq n
. We have the associated KZ-connection

\displaystyle\nabla_{\rm KZ}:=d+\nu\sum_{1\leq j\leq m+n,\ j\neq i}\frac{\Omega_{ij}^{\mathfrak{sl}_{N}}}{z_{i}-z_{j}}dz_{i}

on the trivial vector bundle
E_{Y_{m+n}}
with fiber
E_{n,\chi}
over the unordered configuration space

\displaystyle Y_{m+n}:=\big\{(z_{1},\dots,z_{m+n})\in\mathbb{C}^{m+n}\colon z_{i}\neq z_{j}\text{ for all }i\neq j\big\}

of
m+n
points in
\mathbb{C}
. Here
\Omega^{\mathfrak{sl}_{N}}_{ij}
denotes the Casimir tensor for
\mathfrak{sl}_{N}
acting on the
i^{\rm th}
and
j^{\rm th}
tensor factors and we view the
\mathfrak{gl}_{N}
representations as
\mathfrak{sl}_{N}
representations. It is well-known, and easy to check, that this connection is flat. Observe that
\nabla_{\rm KZ}
is
S_{n}
-equivariant, where
S_{n}
acts on
Y_{m+n}
by permuting the last
n
coordinates and on the fiber by permuting the last
n
tensor factors.

Let
Y:=\text{Conf}_{n}(\mathbb{C}\backslash\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{m}\})
be the space on which
\nabla_{\rm EGO}
is defined. There is a natural
S_{n}
-equivariant map

\displaystyle r\colon\ Y\rightarrow Y_{m+n}

given by
(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})\mapsto(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{m},z_{1},\dots,z_{n})
. Pulling back the connection
\nabla_{\rm KZ}
along

we obtain the
S_{n}
-equivariant flat connection

\displaystyle r^{*}\nabla_{\rm KZ}=d+\nu\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{\Omega_{k,m+i}^{\mathfrak{sl}_{N}}}{z_{i}-\alpha_{k}}+\sum_{1\leq j\neq i\leq n}\frac{\Omega^{\mathfrak{sl}_{N}}_{m+i,m+j}}{z_{i}-z_{j}}\right)dz_{i}

on the trivial vector bundle
E_{Y}
with fiber
E_{n,\chi}
over
Y
. On the other hand, inserting the operators
Y_{i,k}
and
s_{i,j}
on
E_{n,\chi}
defined in Theorem
4.1
into the connection
\nabla_{\rm EGO}
defined in equation (
4.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle\nabla_{\rm EGO}=d+\nu\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{\Omega_{k,m+i}^{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}+\frac{N-c}{m}}{z_{i}-\alpha_{k}}+\sum_{1\leq j\neq i\leq n}\frac{\Omega^{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}_{m+i,m+j}}{z_{i}-z_{j}}\right)dz_{i}.

Both connections above are flat
S_{n}
-equivariant connections on the trivial vector bundle
E_{Y}
over
Y
. To relate their monodromy, first recall that
\Omega^{\mathfrak{sl}_{N}}
and
\Omega^{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}
are related by the equation
\Omega^{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}=\Omega^{\mathfrak{sl}_{N}}+\frac{1}{N}I\otimes I
where

is the identity matrix. As

acts on

by
\lambda_{i}
for

and as
1
for

, we have

\displaystyle\nabla_{\rm EGO}=r^{*}\nabla_{\rm KZ}+\nu\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{\lambda_{k}/N+(N-c)/m}{z_{i}-\alpha_{k}}+\sum_{1\leq j\neq i\leq n}\frac{1/N}{z_{i}-z_{j}}\right)dz_{i}.

(4.2)

Note that the connection

\displaystyle\nabla_{\rm dif\/f}:=d+\nu\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{\lambda_{k}/N+(N-c)/m}{z_{i}-\alpha_{k}}+\sum_{1\leq j\neq i\leq n}\frac{1/N}{z_{i}-z_{j}}\right)dz_{i}

(4.3)

on
E_{Y}
is itself flat and scalar-valued, so it follows from equation (
4.2
) that the parallel transport operator associated to a path
\gamma
in
Y
for the connection
(E_{Y},\nabla_{\rm EGO})
is obtained by multiplying the parallel transport operator associated to
\gamma
for the connection
(E_{Y},r^{*}\nabla_{\rm KZ})
by the scalar-valued parallel transport operator associated to
\gamma
for the connection
(E_{Y},\nabla_{\rm dif\/f})
. By inspection of the residues in

in equation (

), it follows that for the loops

and

about

in

, the monodromies

and

for the connections

and

are related by

\displaystyle\mu_{\nabla_{\rm EGO}}(U_{k})=q^{2(\lambda_{k}/m+(N-c)/m)}\mu_{\nabla_{r^{*}{\rm KZ}}}(U_{k})

(4.4)

and

\displaystyle\mu_{\nabla_{\rm EGO}}(T_{i})=q^{1/N}\mu_{\nabla_{r^{*}{\rm KZ}}}(T_{i}),

(4.5)

where as before

is defined by

.

All that remains is to relate the monodromy operators

and

to the

-matrix expressions appearing in Theorem

using the Drinfeld–Kohno theorem. The original formulation of the Drinfeld–Kohno theorem as stated in

was for the

, but here we need a version of this theorem for

of

on

at

is equivalent to the representation given by

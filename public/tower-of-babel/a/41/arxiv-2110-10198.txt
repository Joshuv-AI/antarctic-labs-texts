The Rayleigh–Bénard problem for compressible fluid flows
Eduard Feireisl, Agnieszka Świerczewska-Gwiazda

Abstract
We consider the physically relevant fully compressible setting of the Rayleigh–Bénard problem of a fluid confined between two parallel plates, heated from the bottom, and subjected to the gravitational force. Under suitable restrictions imposed on the constitutive relations we show that this open system is dissipative in the sense of Levinson, meaning there exists a bounded absorbing set for any global–in–time weak solution. In addition, global–in–time trajectories are asymptotically compact in suitable topologies and the system possesses a global compact trajectory attractor 𝒜 \mathcal{A} . The standard technique of Krylov and Bogolyubov then yields the existence of an invariant measure – a stationary statistical solution sitting on 𝒜 \mathcal{A} . In addition, the Birkhoff–Khinchin ergodic theorem provides convergence of ergodic averages of solutions belonging to 𝒜 \mathcal{A} a.s. with respect to the invariant measure.

Introduction

The Rayleigh–Bénard problem concerns the motion of a fluid confined between two parallel planes, where the temperature of the

\Theta_{\rm B}

\Theta_{\rm U}

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\varrho+{\rm div}_{x}(\varrho{\bf u})

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\varrho{\bf u})+{\rm div}_{x}(\varrho{\bf u}\otimes{\bf u})+\nabla_{x}p(\varrho,\vartheta)

\displaystyle={\rm div}_{x}\mathbb{S}+\varrho\nabla_{x}G,

(1.2)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\varrho e(\varrho,\vartheta))+{\rm div}_{x}(\varrho e(\varrho,\vartheta){\bf u})+\nabla_{x}{\bf q}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{S}:\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}-p(\varrho,\vartheta){\rm div}_{x}{\bf u},

]

, the energy of

The paper is organized as follows. In Section

2.2
Constitutive relations

Following
[
16

we consider the equation of state

•

•

P(0)=0,\ P^{\prime}(Z)>0\ \mbox{for}\ Z\geq 0,\ 0<\frac{\frac{5}{3}P(Z)-P^{\prime}(Z)Z}{Z}\leq c\ \mbox{for}\ Z>0.

(2.11)

In particular, the function

is decreasing, and we suppose

\lim_{Z\to\infty}\frac{P(Z)}{Z^{\frac{5}{3}}}=p_{\infty}>0.

(2.12)

•

In accordance with Gibbs’ relation (

In addition, the

]

Note that (

\displaystyle 0<\underline{\mu}(1+\vartheta)

\displaystyle 0\leq\eta(\vartheta)

\displaystyle 0<\underline{\kappa}(1+\vartheta^{\beta})

3
Main results

Our main result states that the Navier–Stokes–Fourier system in the Rayleigh–Bénard regime (
1.5
), (
1.6
) admits a bounded absorbing set.

Theorem 3.1

(
Bounded absorbing set).

Let
\Theta_{\rm B}
,
\Theta_{\rm U}
be two strictly positive constants. Let the pressure
p
, the internal energy
e
, and the entropy
s

satisfy the hypotheses (
2.10
)–(
2.15
). Let the transport coefficients
\mu
,
\eta
, and
\kappa
satisfy (
2.17
), (
2.18
).

Then for any global–in–time weak solution
(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u})
defined on a time interval
(T,\infty)
, there exists a constant
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}
that depends only on
\Theta_{\rm B}
,
\Theta_{\rm U}
, the total mass of the fluid

M=\int_{\Omega}\varrho\ \,{\rm d}{x},

and the structural properties of

p=p(\varrho,\vartheta)
,
e=a(\varrho,\vartheta)
,
s=s(\varrho,\vartheta)
such that

{\rm ess}\limsup_{t\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}E(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u})(t,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\leq\mathcal{E}_{\infty}.

(3.1)

If, moreover,

{\rm ess}\limsup_{t\to T+}\int_{\Omega}E(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u})(t,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\leq\mathcal{E}_{0}<\infty,

then the convergence is uniform in
\mathcal{E}_{0}
. Specifically, for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a time
T(\varepsilon,\mathcal{E}_{0})
such that

{\rm ess}\sup_{t>T(\varepsilon,\mathcal{E}_{0})}\int_{\Omega}E(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u})(t,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\leq\mathcal{E}_{\infty}+\varepsilon.

Remark 3.2
.

The same result can be shown for a general bounded domain with an arbitrary (nonconstant) profile of the boundary temperature and a general potential volume force
{\bf g}=\nabla_{x}G
,
G=G(x)
. In particular, the problem posed in the inclined layer studied e.g. by
Daniels et al.
[
12
]

can be included.

The existence of a bounded absorbing set for the isentropic (
p=a\varrho^{\gamma}
) Navier–Stokes system with the no–slip
boundary conditions was established in
[
19
]
under the condition
\gamma>\frac{5}{3}
, see also Wang and Wang
[
37
]
. For similar results related to the conservative boundary conditions see
[
18
]
and the monograph
[
21
]
. The existence of a bounded absorbing set for the Navier–Stokes–Fourier system with general Dirichlet boundary conditions was shown in
[
15
]
under rather restrictive assumption postulating a hard sphere equation of state for the pressure. Note that this considerably simplifies the analysis
as
uniform
bounds on the fluid density are

available. It is exactly this missing piece of information that makes the analysis of the present paper much more delicate.

The second result concerns the asymptotic compactness of bounded trajectories.

\displaystyle\varrho_{n}

\displaystyle\to\varrho\ \mbox{in}\ C_{\rm weak}([-M,M];L^{\frac{5}{3}}(\Omega))\cap C([-M,M];L^{1}(\Omega)),

\displaystyle\vartheta_{n}

\displaystyle\to\vartheta\ \mbox{in}\ L^{q}((-M,M);L^{4}(\Omega))\ \mbox{for any}\ 1\leq q<\infty,

\displaystyle{\bf u}_{n}

\displaystyle\to{\bf u}\ \mbox{weakly in}\ L^{2}((-M,M);W^{1,2}(\Omega;R^{3}))

for any

where the limit
(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u})
is an entire weak solution of the Navier–Stokes–Fourier system defined for

4
Dissipativity

Our goal is to prove Theorem
3.1
.
Suppose that we are given a global–in–time solution
(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u})
defined on a time interval
(T,\infty)
. The proof
of asymptotic boundedness leans on several estimates that follow from the basic physical conservation laws.
Here and hereafter, we fix
\tilde{\vartheta}
to be the unique solution of the Dirichlet problem

\Delta_{x}\tilde{\vartheta}=0\ \mbox{in}\ \Omega,\ \tilde{\vartheta}|_{x_{3}=0}=\Theta_{\rm B},\ \tilde{\vartheta}|_{x_{3}=1}=\Theta_{\rm U}.

(4.1)

As
\Theta_{\rm B}
,
\Theta_{\rm U}
are constant, we easily compute

\tilde{\vartheta}=\tilde{\vartheta}(x_{3})=\Theta_{\rm B}+x_{3}\left({\Theta_{\rm U}}-\Theta_{\rm B}\right).

Obviously, the same ansatz can be used in the case of general
x-
dependent boundary data.

4.1
Mass conservation

It follows from the equation of continuity (
2.4
) that the total mass of the fluid is a constant of motion,

M=\int_{\Omega}\varrho(t,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \mbox{for any}\ t>T.

(4.2)

In addition, as the volume force is potential, we can write

\int_{\Omega}\varrho{\bf u}\cdot\nabla_{x}G\ \,{\rm d}{x}=\frac{{\rm d}}{\,{\rm d}t}\int_{\Omega}\varrho G\ \,{\rm d}{x},\ G=-x_{3}.

Consequently, the ballistic energy balance (
2.8
) takes the form

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{\,{\rm d}t}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left[\frac{1}{2}\varrho|{\bf u}|^{2}+\varrho e-\tilde{\vartheta}\varrho s-\varrho G\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}+\int_{\Omega}\frac{\tilde{\vartheta}}{\vartheta}\left[\mathbb{S}:\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}-\frac{{\bf q}\cdot\nabla_{x}\vartheta}{\vartheta}\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle\leq-\int_{\Omega}\left[\varrho s{\bf u}\cdot\nabla_{x}\tilde{\vartheta}+\frac{{\bf q}}{\vartheta}\cdot\nabla_{x}\tilde{\vartheta}\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\ \mbox{in}\ \mathcal{D}^{\prime}(T,\infty).

(4.3)

It is worth–noting that the same argument applies for a general Lipschitz potential
G=G(x)
.

4.2
Coercivity of the dissipative term

It follows from the hypotheses (
2.17
) and Korn–Poincaré inequality that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{\tilde{\vartheta}}{\vartheta}\left(\mathbb{S}(\vartheta,\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}):\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}-\frac{{\bf q}(\vartheta,\nabla_{x}\vartheta)\cdot\nabla_{x}\vartheta}{\vartheta}\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle\quad\geq c\inf\{\Theta_{\rm U},\Theta_{\rm B}\}\left(\|{\bf u}\|^{2}_{W^{1,2}(\Omega;R^{3})}+\|\nabla_{x}\vartheta^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega;R^{3})}+\|\nabla_{x}\log(\vartheta)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega;R^{3})}\right).

Consequently, adding the boundary integrals to the left–hand side and using Poincaré inequality, we get

\displaystyle\left(\|{\bf u}\|^{2}_{W^{1,2}(\Omega;R^{3})}+\|\vartheta^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\|^{2}_{W^{1,2}(\Omega)}+\|\log(\vartheta)\|^{2}_{W^{1,2}(\Omega)}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\leq c(\Theta_{\rm U},\Theta_{\rm B})\left[1+\int_{\Omega}\frac{\tilde{\vartheta}}{\vartheta}\left(\mathbb{S}(\vartheta,\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}):\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}-\frac{{\bf q}(\vartheta,\nabla_{x}\vartheta)\cdot\nabla_{x}\vartheta}{\vartheta}\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right]

(4.4)

4.3
Energy estimates

To simplify the ballistic energy inequality (
4.3
), we first realize, by virtue of (
4.1
),

\int_{\Omega}\frac{{\bf q}(\vartheta,\nabla_{x}\vartheta)}{\vartheta}\cdot\nabla_{x}\tilde{\vartheta}\ \,{\rm d}{x}=-\int_{\Omega}\frac{\kappa(\vartheta)}{\vartheta}\nabla_{x}\vartheta\cdot\nabla_{x}\tilde{\vartheta}\ \,{\rm d}{x}=\int_{\partial\Omega}\mathcal{K}(\tilde{\vartheta})\nabla_{x}\tilde{\vartheta}\ {\rm d}\sigma_{x},

where

\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(\vartheta)=\kappa(\vartheta).

Consequently, the ballistic energy inequality (
4.3
) reduces to

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{\,{\rm d}t}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho|{\bf u}|^{2}+\varrho e-\tilde{\vartheta}\varrho s-\varrho G\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}+\int_{\Omega}\frac{\tilde{\vartheta}}{\vartheta}\left(\mathbb{S}(\vartheta,\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}):\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}-\frac{{\bf q}(\vartheta,\nabla_{x}\vartheta)\cdot\nabla_{x}\vartheta}{\vartheta}\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle\leq-\int_{\Omega}\left[\varrho s{\bf u}\cdot\nabla_{x}\tilde{\vartheta}\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}+c(\Theta_{\rm U},\Theta_{\rm B}).

(4.5)

4.4
Entropy estimates

In accordance with hypothesis (
2.13
),

\int_{\Omega}\varrho s{\bf u}\cdot\nabla\tilde{\vartheta}\ \,{\rm d}{x}=\int_{\Omega}\varrho\mathcal{S}\left(\frac{\varrho}{\vartheta^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right){\bf u}\cdot\nabla_{x}\tilde{\vartheta}\ \,{\rm d}{x}+\frac{4a}{3}\int_{\Omega}\vartheta^{3}{\bf u}\cdot\nabla\tilde{\vartheta}\ \,{\rm d}{x},

where, by virtue of (
2.14
), (
2.15
),

\varrho\mathcal{S}\left(\frac{\varrho}{\vartheta^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)\leq\varrho\mathcal{S}(r)\ \mbox{provided}\ \frac{\varrho}{\vartheta^{\frac{3}{2}}}\geq r,\ \mbox{where}\ \mathcal{S}(r)\to 0\ \mbox{as}\ r\to\infty.

(4.6)

If

\frac{\varrho}{\vartheta^{\frac{3}{2}}}<r\ \mbox{or}\ \varrho<r\vartheta^{\frac{3}{2}},

we get, by virtue of (
2.16
),

0\leq\varrho\mathcal{S}\left(\frac{\varrho}{\vartheta^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)\leq c\left(1+r\vartheta^{\frac{3}{2}}\left[\log^{+}(r\vartheta^{\frac{3}{2}})+\log^{+}(\vartheta)\right]\right).

(4.7)

As
\beta>6
in hypothesis (
2.17
), we may combine (
4.4
) with (
4.5
)–(
4.7
) to obtain

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{\,{\rm d}t}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho|{\bf u}|^{2}+\varrho e-\tilde{\vartheta}\varrho s-\varrho G\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle+c_{1}(\Theta_{\rm U},\Theta_{\rm B})\left(\|{\bf u}\|^{2}_{W^{1,2}(\Omega;R^{3})}+\|\vartheta^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\|^{2}_{W^{1,2}(\Omega)}+\|\log(\vartheta)\|^{2}_{W^{1,2}(\Omega)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq c_{2}(\Theta_{\rm U},\Theta_{\rm B})\mathcal{S}(r)\int_{\Omega}\varrho|{\bf u}|\ \,{\rm d}{x}+\Lambda(\Theta_{\rm U},\Theta_{\rm B},r),

\displaystyle\quad\quad\mbox{where}\ c_{1}>0,\ c_{2}>0,\ \mbox{and}\ \Lambda(r)\to\infty\ \mbox{if}\ r\to\infty.

(4.8)

The main problem to conclude is the fact that forcing term
\int_{\Omega}\varrho|{\bf u}|\ \,{\rm d}{x}
on the right–hand side is not directly controlled by the dissipation on the left–hand side. To this end, we need the so–called pressure estimates which we recall in the next section.

4.5
Pressure estimates

To continue, we recall the inverse of the divergence known as Bogovskii operator:

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}:L^{q}_{0}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\equiv\left\{f\in L^{q}(\Omega)\ \Big|\ \int_{\Omega}f\ \,{\rm d}{x}=0\right\}\to W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega,R^{d}),\ 1<q<\infty,

\displaystyle{\rm div}_{x}\mathcal{B}[f]

\displaystyle=f,

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{B}[{\rm div}_{x}{\bf g}]\|_{L^{r}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq c\|{\bf g}\|_{L^{r}(\Omega)},\ 1<r<\infty\ \mbox{whenever}\ {\bf g}\cdot{\bf n}|_{\partial\Omega}=0.

(4.9)

see e.g. Galdi
[
25
, Chapter 3]
or Geißert, Heck, and Hieber
[
26
]
.

Now, the test function

\varphi(t,x)=\mathcal{B}\left[b(\varrho)-\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}b(\varrho)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right]

in the momentum equation yields

\displaystyle\int_{\tau}^{\tau+1}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}p(\varrho,\vartheta)b(\varrho)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t=\int_{\tau}^{\tau+1}\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\left(\int_{\Omega}b(\varrho)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right)\left(\int_{\Omega}p(\varrho,\vartheta)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right)\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle-\int_{\tau}^{\tau+1}\int_{\Omega}\varrho({\bf u}\otimes{\bf u}):\nabla_{x}\mathcal{B}\left[b(\varrho)-\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}b(\varrho)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{\tau}^{\tau+1}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{S}(\vartheta,\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}):\nabla_{x}\mathcal{B}\left[b(\varrho)-\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}b(\varrho)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle-\int_{\tau}^{\tau+1}\int_{\Omega}\varrho\nabla_{x}G\cdot\mathcal{B}\left[b(\varrho)-\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}b(\varrho)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle+\left[\int_{\Omega}\varrho{\bf u}\cdot\mathcal{B}\left[b(\varrho)-\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}b(\varrho)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right]_{t=\tau}^{t=\tau+1}

\displaystyle-\int_{\tau}^{\tau+1}\int_{\Omega}\varrho{\bf u}\cdot\partial_{t}\mathcal{B}\left[b(\varrho)-\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}b(\varrho)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t.

(4.10)

In addition, as
\varrho
satisfies the renormalized equation of continuity, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\tau}^{\tau+1}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\varrho{\bf u}\cdot\partial_{t}\mathcal{B}\left[b(\varrho)-\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}b(\varrho)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=-\int_{\tau}^{\tau+1}\int_{\Omega}\varrho{\bf u}\cdot\mathcal{B}[{\rm div}_{x}(b(\varrho){\bf u}]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{\tau}^{\tau+1}\int_{\Omega}\varrho{\bf u}\cdot\mathcal{B}\left[(b(\varrho)-b^{\prime}(\varrho)\varrho){\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}-\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}(b(\varrho)-b^{\prime}(\varrho)\varrho){\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t.

(4.11)

The unit length of the time interval has been chosen just for simplicity.

4.6
Uniform bounds

In view of the structural restrictions imposed through hypotheses (
2.11
), (
2.13
) and (
2.16
), for any
\lambda>1
there exist
two constants
c_{1}(\lambda)

such that

c_{1}(\lambda,{\rm data})+\frac{1}{\lambda}E(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u})\leq E_{\tilde{\vartheta}}(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u})-\varrho G\leq\lambda E(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u})+c_{2}(\lambda,{\rm data}).

(4.12)

The following result is crucial for showing the existence of a bounded absorbing set.

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose that

\int_{\Omega}\left[E_{\tilde{\vartheta}}(\tau,\cdot)-\varrho(\tau,\cdot)G\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}-\int_{\Omega}\left[E_{\tilde{\vartheta}}(\tau+1,\cdot)-\varrho(\tau+1,\cdot)G\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\leq K.

(4.13)

Then there exists
L=L(K,M,{\rm data})
such that

{\rm ess}\sup_{\tau\leq t\leq\tau+1}\int_{\Omega}E(t,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\leq L.

(4.14)

Remark 4.2
.

Strictly speaking, the pointwise values of the ballistic energy appearing in (
4.13
) are defined only for a.a.
\tau\in(T;\infty)
. However, thanks to the inequality (
4.8
), we may identify
E_{\tilde{\vartheta}}
with its, say, càglàd representative defined for any
\tau>T
.

The rest of this subsection is devoted to the proof of Lemma
4.1
.

\displaystyle\int_{\tau}^{\tau+1}

\displaystyle\leq c({\rm data},K)\left(1+\mathcal{S}(r)\int_{\tau}^{\tau+1}\int_{\Omega}\varrho|{\bf u}|\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\right)+\Lambda({\rm data},K,r).

\displaystyle-\int_{\tau}^{\tau+1}\int_{\Omega}\varrho({\bf u}\otimes{\bf u}):\nabla_{x}\mathcal{B}\left[\varrho^{\alpha}-\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}\varrho^{\alpha}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

) we get

\displaystyle\int_{\tau}^{\tau+1}\int_{\Omega}\varrho^{\frac{5}{3}+\alpha}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\leq\Lambda(K,M,{\rm data},r)\left[\left(1+\sup_{t\in(\tau,\tau+1)}\|\varrho\|_{L^{\frac{5}{3}}(\Omega)}\right)\,{\rm d}t\right.

\displaystyle+c(K,M,{\rm data})\mathcal{S}(r)\left(1+\sup_{t\in(\tau,\tau+1)}\|\varrho\|_{L^{\frac{5}{3}}(\Omega)}\right)\int_{\tau}^{\tau+1}\|\varrho\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\Omega)}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle+\left.\sup_{t\in(\tau,\tau+1)}\int_{\Omega}\varrho|{\bf u}|\ \,{\rm d}{x}+1\right]

\displaystyle\leq\Lambda(K,M,{\rm data},r)\left[\left(1+\sup_{t\in(\tau,\tau+1)}\|\varrho\|_{L^{\frac{5}{3}}(\Omega)}\right)\,{\rm d}t\right.

\displaystyle+c(K,M,{\rm data})\mathcal{S}(r)\left(1+\sup_{t\in(\tau,\tau+1)}\|\varrho\|_{L^{\frac{5}{3}}(\Omega)}\right)\int_{\tau}^{\tau+1}\|\varrho\|_{L^{\frac{5}{3}}(\Omega)}^{\frac{5}{6}}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle+\left.\sup_{t\in(\tau,\tau+1)}\int_{\Omega}\varrho|{\bf u}|\ \,{\rm d}{x}+1\right],\ \alpha=\frac{1}{15}.

(4.29)

Now, interpolating
L^{1}
and
L^{\frac{5}{3}+\alpha}
, we get

\int_{\tau}^{\tau+1}\int_{\Omega}\varrho^{\frac{5}{3}}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\leq c(M)\left(\int_{\tau}^{\tau+1}\int_{\Omega}\varrho^{\frac{5}{3}+\alpha}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\right)^{\frac{10}{11}}\ \mbox{provided}\ \alpha=\frac{1}{15}.

Gathering the available bounds we conclude

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in(\tau,\tau+1)}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}E(t,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\leq c({\rm data})\left(1+\int_{\tau}^{\tau+1}E(s,\cdot){\rm d}s\right)

\displaystyle\leq c({\rm data})

\displaystyle\left(1+\int_{\tau}^{\tau+1}\left(\|{\bf u}\|^{2}_{W^{1,2}(\Omega;R^{3})}+\|\vartheta^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega)}+\|\log(\vartheta)\|^{2}_{W^{1,2}(\Omega)}\right)\,{\rm d}t\right)

\displaystyle+c({\rm data})

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\tau}^{\tau+1}\int_{\Omega}\varrho|{\bf u}|^{2}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{\tau}^{\tau+1}\int_{\Omega}\varrho^{\frac{5}{3}}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\right)

\displaystyle\leq\Lambda({\rm data},K,r)

\displaystyle\left[1+\left(\sup_{t\in(\tau,\tau+1)}\int_{\Omega}E\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\right)^{\lambda}\right]+c({\rm data},K,M)\mathcal{S}(r)\sup_{t\in(\tau,\tau+1)}\int_{\Omega}E\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

(4.30)

for certain
0<\lambda<1
.
Consequently, choosing
r=r({\rm data},K,M)
large enough, the desired conclusion follows since
\mathcal{S}(r)\to 0

as
r\to\infty
.

We have proved Lemma
4.1
.

5
Applications, long–time behavior

We finish the paper by discussing the impact of Theorems
3.1
,
3.3
on the long time behavior of solutions to the
Rayleigh–Bénard problem in the framework of compressible viscous and heat conducting fluids.

5.1
Trajectory space

We start by introducing a suitable
trajectory space

\mathcal{T}
. In view of the framework of Theorems
3.1
,
3.3
, the “natural” trajectory space should be based on the standard phase variables
(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u})
. Unfortunately, neither
\vartheta
nor
{\bf u}
admit well defined
instantaneous values
at any time
t\in R
. It is therefore more convenient to consider the
conservative entropy
variables
(\varrho,S,{\bf m})
, with

\mbox{momentum}\ {\bf m}=\varrho{\bf u},\ \mbox{and total entropy}\ S=\varrho s(\varrho,\vartheta).

On the one hand, the state variables
(\varrho,S,{\bf m})
are uniquely determined by
(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u})
. On the other hand, knowing

(\varrho,S,{\bf m})
we first obtain
\vartheta
as
\vartheta\mapsto s(\varrho,\vartheta)
is a strictly increasing function. The velocity
{\bf u}

is
a priori
not well defined on the hypothetical vacuum zone, however, it can be recovered in terms of
(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf m})

from the momentum equation (
2.6
).

The phase variables
(\varrho,S,{\bf m})
admit well defined instantaneous values understood in the weak sense. Specifically,
it follows from the weak formulation (
2.4
), (
2.6
), (
2.7
) that the one sided limits

\displaystyle\left<\varrho(\tau-,\cdot);\phi\right>

\displaystyle\equiv\lim_{\delta\to 0+}\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{\tau-\delta}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\varrho(t,\cdot)\phi\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t,\ \left<\varrho(\tau+,\cdot);\phi\right>\equiv\lim_{\delta\to 0+}\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{\tau}^{\tau+\delta}\int_{\Omega}\varrho(t,\cdot)\phi\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\left<{\bf m}(\tau-,\cdot);\boldsymbol{\varphi}\right>

\displaystyle\equiv\lim_{\delta\to 0+}\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{\tau-\delta}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}{\bf m}(t,\cdot)\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t,\ \left<{\bf m}(\tau+,\cdot);\boldsymbol{\varphi}\right>\equiv\lim_{\delta\to 0+}\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{\tau}^{\tau+\delta}\int_{\Omega}{\bf m}(t,\cdot)\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t,

\displaystyle\left<S(\tau-,\cdot);\phi\right>

\displaystyle\equiv\lim_{\delta\to 0+}\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{\tau-\delta}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\varrho s(t,\cdot)\phi\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t,\ \left<S(\tau+,\cdot);\phi\right>\equiv\lim_{\delta\to 0+}\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{\tau}^{\tau+\delta}\int_{\Omega}\varrho s(t,\cdot)\phi\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

exist for
any

\tau\in R
and any
\phi\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega)
,
\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega;R^{3})
. In addition,

\displaystyle\left<\varrho(\tau-,\cdot);\phi\right>

\displaystyle=\left<\varrho(\tau+,\cdot);\phi\right>,\ \mbox{and}\ \tau\mapsto\left<\varrho(\tau,\cdot);\phi\right>\in BC(R),

\displaystyle\left<{\bf m}(\tau-,\cdot);\boldsymbol{\varphi}\right>

\displaystyle=\left<{\bf m}(\tau+,\cdot);\boldsymbol{\varphi}\right>,\ \mbox{and}\ \tau\mapsto\left<{\bf m}(\tau,\cdot);\boldsymbol{\varphi}\right>\in BC(R),

and

\displaystyle\left<S(\tau-,\cdot);\phi\right>

\displaystyle\leq\left<S(\tau+,\cdot);\phi\right>\ \mbox{whenever}\ \phi\geq 0,

\displaystyle\mbox{and}\ \tau

\displaystyle\mapsto\left<S(\tau-,\cdot);\phi\right>=h_{\phi}(\tau)+g_{\phi}(\tau),\ g_{\phi}\in C_{\rm loc}(R),\ h_{\phi}\ \mbox{non--decreasing c\`{a}gl\`{a}d.}

(5.1)

The trajectory space can be therefore identified with “weakly càglàd” and bounded functions defined on
R
. To this end, consider the Hilbert space

W^{k,2}_{0}(\Omega),\ k>\frac{3}{2}\ \mbox{so that}\ W^{k,2}_{0}\hookrightarrow C(\overline{\Omega})

with an orthonormal basis of smooth functions
\{\phi_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
. Similarly, we consider the same space of vector valued
functions
W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega;R^{3})
with a basis
\{\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
.
Finally, we define a metrics

\displaystyle d_{\mathcal{T}}

\displaystyle\left[(\varrho^{1},S^{1},{\bf m}^{1});(\varrho^{2},S^{2},{\bf m}^{2})\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{n}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\exp\left(-t^{2}\right)G\left(\|\left<\varrho^{1}-\varrho^{2};\phi_{n}\right>\|_{C[-t,t]}\right)\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{n}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\exp\left(-t^{2}\right)G\left(\|\left<{\bf m}^{1}-{\bf m}^{2};\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{n}\right>\|_{C([-t,t];R^{3})}\right)\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{n}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\exp\left(-t^{2}\right)G\left(\left[\left<S^{1};\phi_{n}\right>;\left<S^{2};\phi_{n}\right>\right]_{D[-t,t]}\right)\,{\rm d}t,

(5.2)

where

G(Z)=\frac{Z}{1+Z}

and
D[-t,t]
denotes the Skorokhod space of of càglàd functions defined on
[-t,t]
with the associated
complete metrics
[\cdot;\cdot]_{D[-t,t]}
, see e.g. Whitt
[
38
]
.

The trajectory space is defined as

\mathcal{T}=\cup_{L=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{T}_{L},

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{L}=\Big\{(\varrho,S,{\bf m})\ \Big|\

\displaystyle\varrho\in L^{\infty}(R;W^{-k,2}(\Omega)),\ \left<\varrho;\phi_{n}\right>\in C(R),\ n=1,2,\dots,

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in R}\|\varrho(t,\cdot)\|_{W^{-k,2}(\Omega)}\leq L,

\displaystyle{\bf m}\in L^{\infty}(R;W^{-k,2}(\Omega;R^{3})),\ \left<{\bf m};\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{n}\right>\in C(R),\ n=1,2,\dots,

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in R}\|{\bf m}(t,\cdot)\|_{W^{-k,2}(\Omega;R^{3})}\leq L,

\displaystyle S\in L^{\infty}(R;W^{-k,2}(\Omega)),\ \left<S;\phi_{n}\right>\ \mbox{c\`{a}gl\`{a}d in}\ R,\ n=1,2,\dots,

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in R}\|S(t,\cdot)\|_{W^{-k,2}(\Omega)}\leq L\Big\}.

Note that the trajectory space is larger then the set of entire solutions to the Navier–Stokes–Fourier system
and consists of time dependent functionals ranging in the space of distributions on
\Omega
.

Each set
\mathcal{T}_{L}
endowed with the metrics
d_{\mathcal{T}}
is a Polish space. We define inductive topology on
\mathcal{T}
:

\displaystyle(\varrho_{n},S_{n},{\bf m}_{n})\to(\varrho,S,{\bf m})\ \ \mbox{in}\ \mathcal{T}\ \Leftrightarrow\

\displaystyle\mbox{{\bf(i)} there exists}\ L\ \mbox{such that}\ (\varrho_{n},S_{n},{\bf m}_{n})\in\mathcal{T}_{L}\ \mbox{for all}\ n=1,2,\dots

\displaystyle\mbox{{\bf(ii)}}\ d_{\mathcal{T}}\left[(\varrho_{n},S_{n},{\bf m}_{n});(\varrho,S,{\bf m})\right]\to 0.

Our choice of the topology of the trajectory space may seem a bit awkward at the first glance but accommodates
the instantaneous convergence of the state variables. Alternatively, a weaker
L^{p}
topology can be used being equivalent on the attractor
\mathcal{A}
to
d_{\mathcal{T}}
.

5.2
Attractor

As the weak solutions are not (known to be) uniquely determined by the initial/boundary data, we adopt the approach of Sell
[
35
]
and Málek and Nečas
[
27
]

replacing the standard phase space by the trajectory space
\mathcal{T}
. Here and hereafter, we always assume that the principal hypotheses of Theorem
3.1
concerning the constitutive
relations are satisfied. Moreover, we fix the total mass of the fluid,

\int_{\Omega}\varrho(t,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}=M>0.

(5.3)

Accordingly, the set
\mathcal{A}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}=\Big\{

\displaystyle(\varrho,S,{\bf m})\ \Big|\ (\varrho,S,{\bf m})\ \mbox{a weak solution of the Navier--Stokes--Fourier system}

\displaystyle\mbox{in the sense of Definition \ref{Dw1}
on the time interval}\ t\in R\Big\},

(5.4)

is a natural candidate to be
global attractor
in the trajectory space
\mathcal{T}
. Indeed, as shown in Theorem
3.1
,

\sup_{t\in R}\int_{\Omega}E\left(\varrho,S,{\bf m}\right)(t,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\leq\mathcal{E}_{\infty}<\infty.

(5.5)

In particular,
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{T}_{L}\subset\mathcal{T}
for a sufficiently large
L
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Under the hypotheses of Theorem
3.1
, the set
\mathcal{A}
is

•

non–empty;

•

time–shift invariant,

(\varrho,S,{\bf m})\in\mathcal{A}\ \Rightarrow\ (\varrho,S,{\bf m})(\cdot+T)\in\mathcal{A}\ \mbox{for any}\ T\in R;

•

compact in the metric topology
(\mathcal{T}_{L},d_{\mathcal{T}})
for a sufficiently large
L
.

Proof.

As shown in
[
9
, Theorem 4.2]
, the Navier–Stokes–Fourier system with the boundary conditions (
1.5
), (
1.6
) admits a global–in–time weak solution
(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u})
on the time interval

[0,\infty)
for any initial data with finite energy. It follows from Theorem
3.3
that there exists a sequence of times

T_{n}\to\infty
such that
((\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u})(\cdot+T_{n}))_{n=1}^{\infty}
converge to a weak solution
(\tilde{\varrho},\tilde{\vartheta},{\tilde{\bf u}})
of the same problem defined for all
t\in R
with globally bounded energy. Obviously,

(\varrho,S,{\bf m})=(\tilde{\varrho},\tilde{\varrho}s(\tilde{\varrho},\tilde{\vartheta}),\tilde{\varrho}{\tilde{\bf u}})\in\mathcal{A}.

Moreover, as the underlying system is autonomous, the set
\mathcal{A}
is time–shift invariant.

Compactness of the set
\mathcal{A}
follows again from Theorem
3.3
, where we consider
T_{n}=-\infty
. As pointed out,
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{T}_{L}
, where the latter is a metric space; whence compactness is equivalent to sequential compactness. At the level of the density, convergence in the metric
d_{\mathcal{T}}
follows from (
3.2
). Moreover, since the
momenta
\varrho{\bf u}
satisfy equation (
2.6
), they are precompact in the topology of the space

C_{\rm weak,loc}(R;L^{\frac{5}{4}}(\Omega;R^{3})),

which implies compactness in the momentum component of
\mathcal{T}_{L}
with the
d_{\mathcal{T}}
metrics.

Thus it remains to show compactness at the level of the total entropy
S
. First observe that

\tau\mapsto\int_{\Omega}S(\tau,\cdot)\phi\ \,{\rm d}{x}-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\varrho s(\varrho,\vartheta){\bf u}\cdot\nabla_{x}\phi\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\kappa(\vartheta)}{\vartheta}\cdot\nabla_{x}\phi\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

is a non–decreasing function of
\tau
for any test function
\phi\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega)
,
\phi\geq 0
. In view of boundedness of the total energy, the family

\tau\mapsto\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\varrho s(\varrho,\vartheta){\bf u}\cdot\nabla_{x}\phi\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\kappa(\vartheta)}{\vartheta}\cdot\nabla_{x}\phi\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

is precompact in
C_{\rm loc}(R)
; whence precompactness of
S
in
d_{\mathcal{T}}
reduces to precompactness of a
non–decreasing (in time)
sequence of functions

\tau\mapsto\left<\widetilde{S}_{n};\phi\right>\equiv\int_{\Omega}S_{n}(\tau,\cdot)\phi\ \,{\rm d}{x}-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{n}s(\varrho_{n},\vartheta_{n}){\bf u}_{n}\cdot\nabla_{x}\phi\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\kappa(\vartheta_{n})}{\vartheta_{n}}\cdot\nabla_{x}\phi\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

with respect to the metrics
d
of the Skorokhod space
D[-M,M]
of càglàd functions defined on compact time intervals
[-M,M]
. To this end, we recall the criterion due to Whitt
[
38
, Chapter 12, Corollary 12.5.1]
:

Let

h_{n}:[-M,M]\to R

be a sequence of monotone functions.

Then

d[h_{n};h]\to 0\ \mbox{as}\ n\to\infty\ \mbox{for some}\ h\in D[-M,M]

if and only if

h_{n}(t)\to h(t)\ \mbox{for all}\ t\ \mbox{belonging to a dense set in}\ [-M,M]\ \mbox{including the end points}\ -M\ \mbox{and}\ M.

Now, as
S_{n}
are the total entropies generated by a family of uniformly bounded weak solutions of the Navier–Stokes–Fourier system, we have

\displaystyle\left<\widetilde{S}_{n}(\tau,\cdot);\phi\right>

\displaystyle\to\left<\widetilde{S}(\tau,\cdot);\phi\right>

\displaystyle\equiv\int_{\Omega}S(\tau,\cdot)\phi\ \,{\rm d}{x}-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\varrho s(\varrho,\vartheta){\bf u}\cdot\nabla_{x}\phi\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\kappa(\vartheta)}{\vartheta}\cdot\nabla_{x}\phi\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

for a.a.
\tau\in R
at least for a suitable subsequence, where
(\varrho,S=\varrho s(\varrho,\vartheta),{\bf m}=\varrho{\bf u})\in\mathcal{A}
, is another entire solution of the same problem.
In particular,

\left<\widetilde{S}_{n}(\tau,\cdot);\phi\right>\to\left<\widetilde{S}(\tau,\cdot);\phi\right>\ \mbox{for a dense set of times for any compact interval}\ [-M,M];

whence, in accordance with the above convergence criterion,

\left<\widetilde{S}_{n}(\tau,\cdot);\phi\right>\to\left<\widetilde{S}(\tau,\cdot);\phi\right>\ \mbox{in}\ D[-M,M]\ \mbox{for a.a.}\ M>0

yielding the desired conclusion

\left<S_{n}(\tau,\cdot);\phi\right>\to\left<S(\tau,\cdot);\phi\right>\ \mbox{in}\ D[-M,M]\ \mbox{for a.a.}\ M>0.

∎

In accordance with Theorems
3.1
,
3.3
, and Lemma
5.1
, we may state our main result concerning the existence of a trajectory attractor for the Rayleigh–Bénard problem:

Theorem 5.2

(
Trajectory attractor).

Let
M>0
,
\mathcal{E}_{0}
be given. Let
\mathcal{F}[M,\mathcal{E}_{0}]
be a family of weak solutions to the Rayleigh–Bénard problem for the Navier–Stokes–Fourier system
on the time interval
(0,\infty)

satisfying

\int_{\Omega}\varrho\ \,{\rm d}{x}=M,\ {\rm ess}\limsup_{\tau\to 0+}\int_{\Omega}E(\varrho,S,{\bf m})(\tau,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\leq\mathcal{E}_{0}.

We identify the set
\mathcal{F}[M,\mathcal{E}_{0}]
with a subset of the trajectory space
\mathcal{T}
extending

\varrho(\tau,\cdot)=\lim_{t\to 0+}\varrho(t,\cdot),\ {\bf m}(\tau,\cdot)=\lim_{t\to 0+}{\bf m}(t,\cdot),\ 0\leq S(\tau,\cdot)\leq\lim_{t\to 0+}S(t,\cdot)\ \mbox{for}\ \tau<0,

where the limits are understood in the weak (distributional) sense.

Then for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a time
T(\varepsilon)
such that

d_{\mathcal{T}}[(\varrho,S,{\bf m})(\cdot+T);\mathcal{A}]<\varepsilon\ \mbox{for any}\ (\varrho,S,{\bf m})\in\mathcal{F}[M,\mathcal{E}_{0}]\ \mbox{and any}\ T>T(\varepsilon).

5.3
Stationary statistical solutions

Following the ideas of the preceding section we are ready to identify
statistical solutions
with shift invariant probability measures on
{\mathcal{T}}
, which are supported by solutions to the Navier-Stokes-Fourier system. In accordance with Theorem
5.2
, these shift invariant probability measures supported by the global trajectory attractor
\mathcal{A}
.

The construction of a bounded invariant measure is the same as in
[
14
]
, note that a similar approach in the incompressible setting was used by Foias, Rosa and Temam
[
23
]
,
[
24
]
.
Given a trajectory
(\varrho,S,{\bf m})\in\mathcal{T}
we consider a probability measure

\mathcal{V}_{T}\equiv\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\delta_{(\varrho,S,{\bf m})(\cdot+t)}\,{\rm d}t,

where
\delta
denotes the Dirac mass. Obviously,
\mathcal{V}_{T}
is a probability measure. If, in addition,

(\varrho,S,{\bf m})\in\mathcal{A},

then
\mathcal{V}_{T}\in\mathfrak{P}(\mathcal{A})
, where
\mathfrak{P}(\mathcal{A})
denotes the set of all probablity measures on a compact Polish space
\mathcal{A}
. In particular, the family

\{\mathcal{V}_{T}\}_{T\geq 0}\ \mbox{is tight.}

By Prokhorov theorem, there is a sequence
T_{n}\to\infty
such that

\mathcal{V}_{T_{n}}\to\mathcal{V}\ \mbox{narowly in}\ \mathfrak{P}(\mathcal{A}).

Finally, exactly as in
[
14
, Section 5.1]
, we may show that the measure
\mathcal{V}
is time–shift invariant, meaning

\mathcal{V}[\mathfrak{B}(\cdot+T)]=\mathcal{V}[\mathfrak{B}]\ \mbox{for any Borel set}\ \mathfrak{B}\subset\mathcal{T}.

(5.6)

A Borel probability measure
\mathcal{V}\in\mathfrak{P}(\mathcal{A})
enjoying the property (
5.6
) is called
statistical stationary solution
of the
Rayleigh–Bénard problem for the Navier–Stokes–Fourier system.

Finally, observe that the above construction may be restricted to any shift–invariant subset
\mathcal{U}\subset\mathcal{A}
.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
{\mathcal{U}}\subset\mathcal{A}
be a non-empty time–shift invariant set, meaning

(\varrho,S,{\bf m})\in\mathcal{U}\ \Rightarrow\ (\varrho,S,{\bf m})(\cdot+T)\in\mathcal{U}\ \mbox{for any}\ T\in R.

Then there exists a stationary statistical solution
{\mathcal{V}}
supported by
\overline{\mathcal{U}}
:

•

{\mathcal{V}}
is a Borel probability measure,
\mathcal{V}\in\mathfrak{P}(\overline{\mathcal{U}})
;

•

\supp{\mathcal{V}}\subset\overline{\mathcal{U}}
, where the closure of a
{\mathcal{U}}
is a compact invariant set;

•

{\mathcal{V}}
is shift invariant, i.e.,
{\mathcal{V}}[\mathfrak{B}]={\mathcal{V}}[{\mathfrak{B}(\cdot+T)}]

for any Borel set
\mathfrak{B}\subset{\mathcal{T}}
and any
T\in R
.

5.4
Convergence of ergodic means

We conclude this section by a direct application of Birkhoff–Khinchin ergodic theorem. Similarly to
[
14
, Section 5]
, we may consider the state space

H=W^{-k,2}(\Omega)\times W^{-k,2}(\Omega)\times W^{-k,2}(\Omega;R^{3}).

Let
\mathcal{V}\in\mathfrak{P}(\mathcal{T})
be a statistical stationary solution, and thus a Borel probability measure. Consider a probability basis
(\mathcal{T},\mathfrak{B}[\mathcal{T}],\mathcal{V})
where
\mathcal{T}
is the trajectory space and
\mathfrak{B}[\mathcal{T}]
is the family of Borel sets.
Given a trajectory
(\varrho,S,{\bf m})\in\mathcal{T}
and

\tau\in R
we may consider the associated canonical process

(\varrho,S,{\bf m})\times\tau\mapsto(\varrho,S,{\bf m})(\tau,\cdot)\in H.

As
\mathcal{V}
is shift invariant, the above process is a stationary process with respect to the probability basis

(\mathcal{T},\mathfrak{B}[\mathcal{T}],\mathcal{V})
defined for
\tau\in R
, with càglàd paths ranging in
H
.

Exactly as in
[
14
, Theorem 6.4, Section 6]
we can establish the following result.

Theorem 5.4

(
Convergence of ergodic averages).

Let
\mathcal{V}
be a stationary statistical solution and
(\varrho,S,{\bf m})
the associated stationary process. Let
F:H\to R
be a Borel measurable function such that

\int_{\mathcal{T}}|F(\varrho(0,\cdot),S(0,\cdot),{\bf m}(0,\cdot)|\ {\rm d}\mathcal{V}<\infty.

Then there exists a measurable function
\overline{F}
,

\overline{F}:(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{V})\to R

such that

\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}F(\varrho(t,\cdot),S(t,\cdot),{\bf m}(t,\cdot))\,{\rm d}t\to\overline{F}\ \mbox{as}\ T\to\infty

\mathcal{V}-
a.s. and in
L^{1}(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{V})
.

Abstract

We explicitly describe bases for the ordinary and restricted 1- and 2-cohomology spaces with trivial coefficients, and give formulas for the bracket and [ p ] [p] -operations in the corresponding restricted one-dimensional central extensions.

Introduction

\mathbb{N}
-graded Lie algebras of maximal class have been intensively studied in the last decade. A Lie algebra of
maximal class
is
a graded Lie algebra

\mathfrak{g}=\oplus_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathfrak{g}_{i}

over a field
\mathbb{F}
, where
\dim(\mathfrak{g}_{1})=\dim(\mathfrak{g}_{2})=1
, dim
(\mathfrak{g}_{i})\leq 1

for
i\geq 3
and
[\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{g}_{i}]=\mathfrak{g}_{i+1}
for
i\geq 1
.

A Lie algebra of dimension
n
is called
filiform
if

\dim\big(\mathfrak{g}^{k}\big)=n-k,\qquad 2\leq k\leq n,\qquad{\rm where}\quad\mathfrak{g}^{k}=\big[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}^{k-1}\big].

Lie algebras of maximal class with two generators over fields of characteristic zero have been classified, and exactly three of these algebras are of filiform type
[
9
]
. We list them with the nontrivial bracket structures:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}_{0}\colon\quad

\displaystyle[e_{1},e_{i}]=e_{i+1},\qquad

\displaystyle i\geq 2,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}_{2}\colon\quad

\displaystyle[e_{1},e_{i}]=e_{i+1},\qquad

\displaystyle i\geq 2,

\displaystyle[e_{2},e_{j}]=e_{j+2},\qquad

\displaystyle j\geq 3,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{V}\colon\quad

\displaystyle[e_{i},e_{j}]=(j-i)e_{i+j},\qquad

\displaystyle i,j\geq 1.

Filiform
\mathbb{N}
-graded Lie algebras
\mathfrak{g}
of dimension
n
over a field of characteristic zero that satisfy
[\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{g}_{i}]=\mathfrak{g}_{i+1}
and
\dim(\mathfrak{g}_{i})=1
for
i<n
(which is equivalent to having 2 generators) are classified in
[
14
]
. They include the natural “truncations” of
\mathfrak{m}_{0}(n)
and
\mathfrak{m}_{2}(n)
obtained by taking the quotient by the ideal generated by
e_{n+1}
. The algebra
\mathfrak{V}
(the Witt algebra) is isomorphic to the algebra of derivations of the polynomial algebra
\mathbb{F}[x]
. If
\mathbb{F}
has characteristic
p>0
, then the truncation
\mathfrak{V}(p)
of
\mathfrak{V}
is the derivation algebra of the quotient of
\mathbb{F}[x]
by the ideal generated by
x^{p}-1
. The algebra
\mathfrak{V}(p)
is called the (modular) Witt algebra.

The above picture is more complicated in the modular case (that is,
over fields of positive characteristic), see
[
1
,
2
,
13
]
, but

\mathfrak{m}_{0}
,
\mathfrak{m}_{2}
,
\mathfrak{V}
and their truncations always show up. We refer the
reader to the book
[
15
]
for a general treatment of modular Lie
algebras. In this paper, we show that if the field
\mathbb{F}
has
characteristic
p\geq 5
, then the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{m}_{2}(p)
admits a
family of restricted Lie algebra structures
\mathfrak{m}_{2}^{\lambda}(p)

parameterized by elements
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}^{p}
. We describe the
isomorphism classes of these algebras, calculate the ordinary and restricted cohomology spaces with trivial coefficients
H^{q}\big(\mathfrak{m}_{2}^{\lambda}(p)\big)
and
H_{*}^{q}\big(\mathfrak{m}_{2}^{\lambda}(p)\big)
for
q=1,2
and give explicit bases for those spaces. We also give the bracket
structures and
[p]
-operations for the corresponding restricted
one-dimensional central extensions of these restricted Lie algebras.

With this, we complete the description of all three types of truncated filiform restricted Lie algebras (
\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{\lambda}(p)
,
\mathfrak{m}_{2}^{\lambda}(p)
, and
\mathfrak{V}(p)
), their low dimensional cohomology spaces with trivial coefficients and their restricted one-dimensional central extensions. The algebras
\mathfrak{m}_{0}^{\lambda}(p)
were studied in
[
5
]
, and the algebra
\mathfrak{V}(p)
was studied in
[
6
]
(where it is denoted by
W
).

Remark 1.1
.

For
p=2
and
p=3
,
\mathfrak{m}_{0}(p)=\mathfrak{m}_{2}(p)
so these algebras were
treated in
[
5
]
.

In this paper, all cochain and cohomology spaces are with coefficients in the trivial
\mathbb{F}
-module. The organization is as follows. In Section
2
we construct the restricted Lie algebra family
\mathfrak{m}_{2}^{\lambda}(p)
, determine the isomorphism classes of these restricted Lie algebras, and describe both the ordinary and restricted 1- and 2-cochains, including formulas for all differentials. In Section
3
we calculate both the ordinary and restricted 1-cohomology by giving explicit cocycles. Section
4
contains the calculation of the ordinary and restricted 2-cohomology spaces, again by giving explicit cocycles. In Section
5
we describe all restricted one-dimensional central extensions and give their brackets and
[p]
-operations.

Preliminaries

2.1
The Lie algebra
\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{m}_{2}(p)}

Let
p\geq 5
be a prime, and let
\mathbb{F}
be a field of characteristic

p
. Define the
\mathbb{F}
-vector space

\mathfrak{m}_{2}(p)=\mathop{\rm span}\nolimits_{\mathbb{F}}(\{e_{1},\dots,e_{p}\}),

and define a bracket on
\mathfrak{m}_{2}(p)
by

\displaystyle[e_{1},e_{i}]=e_{i+1},\qquad 1<i<p,

\displaystyle[e_{2},e_{i}]=e_{i+2},\qquad 2<i<p-1,

with all other brackets
[e_{i},e_{j}]
(for
i<j
) being
0
. Note that

\mathfrak{m}_{2}(p)
is a graded Lie algebra with
k
-th graded component

(\mathfrak{m}_{2}(p))_{k}=\mathbb{F}e_{k}
for
1\leq k\leq p
. If
\alpha_{i},\beta_{i}\in\mathbb{F}

and
g=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\alpha_{i}e_{i}
,
h=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\beta_{i}e_{i}
, then

\displaystyle[g,h]=(\alpha_{1}\beta_{2}-\alpha_{2}\beta_{1})e_{3}+(\alpha_{1}\beta_{3}-\alpha_{3}\beta_{1})e_{4}

\displaystyle\hphantom{[g,h] =}{}+\sum_{j=5}^{p}((\alpha_{1}\beta_{j-1}-\alpha_{j-1}\beta_{1})+(\alpha_{2}\beta_{j-2}-\alpha_{j-2}\beta_{2}))e_{j}.

(2.1)

2.2
The restricted Lie algebras
\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{m}_{2}^{\lambda}(p)}

We refer the reader to
[
12
, Chapter V, Section 7]
and
[
15
, Section 2.2]
for the definition of a restricted Lie algebra,
and for the construction of the
[p]
-mapping on a given Lie algebra
(
\mathfrak{m}_{2}(p)
in the current paper) used below. For any
j\geq 2
and

g_{1},\dots,g_{j}\in\mathfrak{m}_{2}(p)
, we denote the
j
-fold bracket

[g_{1},g_{2},g_{3},\dots,g_{j}]=[[\dots[[g_{1},g_{2}],g_{3}],\dots],g_{j}].

Since

p\geq 5
, (
2.1
) implies that the center of the algebra is

Z(\mathfrak{m}_{2}(p))=\mathbb{F}e_{p},
and
p
-fold brackets are zero. Therefore for
each
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{p})\in\mathbb{F}^{p}
, setting

e_{k}^{[p]}=\lambda_{k}e_{p}
for each

defines a restricted Lie
algebra that we denote by
\mathfrak{m}_{2}^{\lambda}(p)
. Because
p
-fold
brackets in
\mathfrak{m}_{2}^{\lambda}(p)
are zero, for all

g,h\in\mathfrak{m}_{2}^{\lambda}(p)
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}
,

(g+h)^{[p]}=g^{[p]}+h^{[p]}\qquad{\rm and}\qquad(\alpha g)^{[p]}=\alpha^{p}g^{[p]},

and therefore the
[p]
-mapping on

\mathfrak{m}_{2}^{\lambda}(p)
is
p
-semilinear (see also
[
15
, Chapter 2, Lemma 1.2]
). From this we get that if

g=\sum\alpha_{k}e_{k}\in\mathfrak{m}_{2}^{\lambda}(p)
, then

\displaystyle g^{[p]}=\left(\sum_{k=1}^{p}\alpha_{k}^{p}\lambda_{k}\right)e_{p}.

(2.2)

Everywhere below, we write
\mathfrak{m}_{2}^{\lambda}(p)
to denote both the graded
Lie algebra
\mathfrak{m}_{2}(p)
and the graded restricted Lie algebra

\mathfrak{m}_{2}^{\lambda}(p)
for a given
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}^{p}
. The Lie brackets and
restricted
[p]
-operators for these algebras are explicitly given by (
2.1
) and (
2.2
), respectively.

Remark 2.1
.

For
p=2
there are several other possible
[2]
-mappings, namely any 2-semilinear transformation on
\mathfrak{m}_{2}(2)
.

2.3
Isomorphism classes

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
p\geq 5
. If
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}^{p}
, the graded restricted Lie algebras
\mathfrak{m}_{2}^{\lambda}(p)
and
\mathfrak{m}_{2}^{\lambda^{\prime}}(p)
are isomorphic if and only if there exists a non-zero
\mu\in\mathbb{F}
such that
\lambda_{k}=\mu^{(k-1)p}\lambda^{\prime}_{k}
for
k=1,\dots,p
.

Proof.

We only consider isomorphisms that preserve the grading as we are
interested in these algebras as graded restricted Lie
algebras. Assume that there exists a graded restricted Lie algebra
isomorphism
\varphi\colon\mathfrak{m}_{2}^{\lambda}(p)\to\mathfrak{m}_{2}^{\lambda^{\prime}}(p)
, and
let
\varphi(e_{1})=\mu e_{1},\varphi(e_{2})=\nu e_{2}
for some non-zero

\mu,\nu\in\mathbb{F}
. Since
\varphi
preserves the Lie bracket, we must
have
\varphi(e_{3})=\mu\nu e_{3}
,
\varphi(e_{4})=\mu^{2}\nu e_{4}
,

\varphi(e_{5})=\mu^{3}\nu e_{5}
. On the other hand, as

[e_{1},e_{4}]=[e_{2},e_{3}]
, we also must have

\varphi(e_{5})=\mu\nu^{2}e_{5}
. From this it follows that
\nu=\mu^{2}
and

\varphi(e_{k})=\mu^{k}e_{k}
for
k=1,\dots,p
.

Moreover,
\varphi
preserves the restricted
[p]
-structure so that

\varphi(e_{k}^{[p]})=\varphi(e_{k})^{[p]^{\prime}}

for
k=1,\dots,p
(here
[p]^{\prime}
denotes the restricted
[p]
-structure on

\mathfrak{m}_{2}^{\lambda^{\prime}}(p)
). Now,

\varphi(e_{k}^{[p]})=\varphi(\lambda_{k}e_{p})=\lambda_{k}\mu^{p}e_{p}\qquad{\rm and}\qquad\varphi(e_{k})^{[p]^{\prime}}=(\mu^{k}e_{k})^{[p]^{\prime}}=\mu^{kp}\lambda_{k}^{\prime}e_{p}

so
\lambda_{k}\mu^{p}=\mu^{kp}\lambda^{\prime}_{k},
and hence

\lambda_{k}=\mu^{(k-1)p}\lambda^{\prime}_{k}.

It remains to show that the above condition on
\lambda_{k}
gives rise to a graded restricted Lie algebra isomorphism between
\mathfrak{m}_{2}^{\lambda}(p)
and
\mathfrak{m}_{2}^{\lambda^{\prime}}(p)
. If, for
0\neq\mu\in\mathbb{F}
, we define
\varphi(e_{1})=\mu e_{1}
,
\varphi(e_{k})=\mu^{k}e_{k}

(2\leq k\leq p)
, then it is easy to check that the argument above is reversible, and we obtain a graded isomorphism between the restricted Lie algebras.
∎

3
The cohomology
\boldsymbol{H^{1}\big(\mathfrak{m}_{2}^{\lambda}(p)\big)}
and
\boldsymbol{H^{1}_{*}\big(\mathfrak{m}_{2}^{\lambda}(p)\big)}

In this short section we compute, for
p\geq 5
, both the ordinary and restricted 1-cohomology spaces
H^{1}(\mathfrak{m}_{2}^{\lambda}(p))
and
H^{1}_{*}(\mathfrak{m}_{2}^{\lambda}(p))
, and in particular we show that these spaces are equal.

Theorem 3.1
.

If
p\geq 5
and
{\bf\lambda}\in\mathbb{F}^{p}
, then

H^{1}\big(\mathfrak{m}_{2}^{\lambda}(p)\big)=H^{1}_{*}\big(\mathfrak{m}_{2}^{\lambda}(p)\big)

and the classes of
\big\{e^{1},e^{2}\big\}
form a basis.

Proof.

Easily, the differential (
2.3
) has a kernel spanned by
\big\{e^{1},e^{2}\big\}
, and
d^{0}=0
, so that

H^{1}\big(\mathfrak{m}_{2}^{\lambda}(p)\big)\cong\mathbb{F}e^{1}\oplus\mathbb{F}e^{2}.

As for any restricted Lie algebra,
H^{1}_{*}\big(\mathfrak{m}_{2}^{\lambda}(p)\big)
consists of those ordinary cohomology classes
[\psi]\in H^{1}\big(\mathfrak{m}_{2}^{\lambda}(p)\big)
for which
\operatorname{ind}^{1}(\psi)=0
(see
[
11
, Theorem 2.1]
or
[

Ringel Duality for Extended ZigZag Schur Algebra
Alexander Kleshchev, Ilan Weinschelbaum

Abstract
. Extended zigzag Schur algebras are quasi-hereditary algebras which are conjecturally Morita equivalent to RoCK blocks of classical Schur algebras. We prove that extended zigzag Schur algebras are Ringel self-dual.



1.
Introduction

Let
S(m,r)
be a classical Schur algebra over the ground field
\mathbb{F}
of characteristic
p>0
, see
[
10
]
. A fundamental fact going back to
[
11
,
3
]
is that the algebra
S(m,r)
is (based) quasi-hereditary. The blocks of
S(m,r)
are classified in
[
6
, (2.12)]
,
[
7
]
.

From now on let us assume for simplicity that
r\leq m
. Then the blocks of
S(m,r)
and of the symmetric group algebra
\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{r}
are parametrized by pairs
(\rho,d)
where
\rho
is a
p
-core and
d
is a non-negative integer
such that
|\rho|+pd=r
. Let
B_{\rho,d}
denote the corresponding block of
S(m,r)
and
\bar{B}_{\rho,d}
denote the corresponding block of
\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{r}
.

A special role in representation theory of
\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{r}
is played by the so-called
RoCK blocks
going back to
[
18
,
1
]
. These are the blocks
\bar{B}_{\rho,d}
with
\rho
satisfying certain combinatorial genericity condition with respect to
d
. The corresponding block
B_{\rho,d}
of the Schur algebra
S(m,r)
is then also called
RoCK
. The RoCK blocks of symmetric groups are important because they admit a nice ‘local description.’ Namely, by
[
9
]
, we have that
\bar{B}_{\rho,d}
is Morita equivalent to the zigzag Schur algebra
T^{{\bar{\mathsf{Z}}}}(d,d)
defined by Turner
[
20
]
, see also
[
8
]
. In view of
[
2
]
, this yields a ‘local description’ of all blocks of symmetric groups up to derived equivalence.

On the other hand, an arbitrary RoCK block
B_{\rho,d}
of
S(m,r)
is
conjecturally
Morita equivalent to the
extended zigzag Schur algebra

T^{\mathsf{Z}}(d,d)
, see
[
14
, Conjecture 7.60]
. As a first evidence for this conjecture, it is proved in
[
14
, Theorem 1]
that
T^{\mathsf{Z}}(d,d)
is quasi-hereditary. In this paper we obtain further evidence for this conjecture in terms of Ringel duality.

In fact, Donkin
[
4
, (3.7),(3.11)]
,
[
7
, §5(2)]
,
[
5
, §4.1]
proves that
S(m,r)
is Ringel self-dual.
It follows from the results of Donkin that the Ringel dual of the block
B_{\rho,d}
is the block
B_{\rho^{\prime},d}
. On the other hand, if

B_{\rho,d}
is RoCK then so is
B_{\rho^{\prime},d}
, so we expect that the extended zigzag Schur algebra
T^{\mathsf{Z}}(d,d)
must be Ringel self-dual. This is what we prove in this paper:

Main Theorem.

Let
d\leq n
. Then the extended zigzag Schur algebra
T^{\mathsf{Z}}(n,d)
is Ringel self-dual.

Here
{\mathsf{Z}}
stands for the extended zigzag algebra corresponding to the quiver with
p
vertices, see §
5.1
.
In fact,
T^{\mathsf{Z}}(n,d)
is a special case of the generalized Schur algebras
T^{A}_{\mathfrak{a}}(n,d)
introduced in
[
13
]
, with
A={\mathsf{Z}}
and
\mathfrak{a}
being the degree zero component of the graded algebra
{\mathsf{Z}}
. It is well known that
{\mathsf{Z}}
is quasi-hereditary and Ringel self-dual. So our Main Theorem is a special case of the following conjecture.

Conjecture.

Let
A
be a based quasi-hereditary algebra and
d\leq n
. If
A^{\prime}
is a Ringel dual of
A
, then a Ringel dual of
T^{A}_{\mathfrak{a}}(n,d)
is of the form
T^{A^{\prime}}_{\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}}(n,d)
for some canonical choice of
\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}
.

The paper is organized as follows. Section
2
is preliminaries.
In particular, §
2.3
details necessary facts on based quasi-hereditary algebras and §
2.4
is on the combinatorics of partitions and tableaux.
Section
4
describes the construction of
T^{A}(n,d)
and important results about its (co)standard modules.
In Section
3
, we define the modified divided power which we will use extensively to construct a full tilting module for
T^{{\mathsf{Z}}}(n,d)
.
Lastly, in Section
5
prove the Main Theorem. In particular, in §
5.2
we describe a full tilting module for
T^{\mathsf{Z}}(n,d)
; and in §
5.3
we compute its endomorphism algebra.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
General notation

For
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, we denote
[n]:=\{1,2,\dots,n\}
.
Throughout the paper,
I
denotes a non-empty finite partially ordered set. We always identify
I
with the set
\{0,1,\dots,\ell\}
for
\ell=|I|-1
, so that the standard total order on integers refines the partial order on
I
. For a set
S
, we often write elements of
S^{d}
as words
\text{\boldmath$s$}=s_{1}\cdots s_{d}
with
s_{1},\dots,s_{d}\in S
.

The symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{d}
acts on the right on
S^{d}
by place permutations:

(s_{1}\cdots s_{d})\sigma=s_{\sigma 1}\cdots s_{\sigma d}.

For
\text{\boldmath$s$}=s_{1}\cdots s_{d}\in S^{d}
we have the stabilizer

\mathfrak{S}_{\text{\boldmath$s$}}:=\{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{d}\mid\text{\boldmath$s$}\sigma=\text{\boldmath$s$}\}.
For
\text{\boldmath$s$},\text{\boldmath$t$}\in S^{d}
, we write
\text{\boldmath$s$}\sim\text{\boldmath$t$}
if
\text{\boldmath$s$}\sigma=\text{\boldmath$t$}
for some
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{d}
. If
S_{1},\dots,S_{m}
are sets, then
\mathfrak{S}_{d}
acts on
S_{1}^{d}\times\dots\times S_{m}^{d}
diagonally. We write

(\text{\boldmath$s$}_{1},\dots,\text{\boldmath$s$}_{m})\sim(\text{\boldmath$t$}_{1},\dots,\text{\boldmath$t$}_{m})

if

(\text{\boldmath$s$}_{1},\dots,\text{\boldmath$s$}_{m})\sigma=(\text{\boldmath$t$}_{1},\dots,\text{\boldmath$t$}_{m})
for some
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{d}
.
If
U\subseteq S_{1}^{d}\times\dots\times S_{m}^{d}
is a
\mathfrak{S}_{d}
-invariant subset, we denote by
U/\mathfrak{S}_{d}
a complete set of the
\mathfrak{S}_{d}
-orbit representatives in
U
and we identify
U/\mathfrak{S}_{d}
with the set of
\mathfrak{S}_{d}
-orbits on
U
.

An (arbitrary) ground field is denoted by
\mathbb{F}
.
Often we will also need to work over a characteristic
0
principal ideal domain
R
such that
\mathbb{F}
is a
R
-module, so that we can change scalars from
R
to
\mathbb{F}
(in all examples of interest to us, one can use
R=\mathbb{Z}
). When considering
R
-supermodules (or
\mathbb{F}
-superspaces) below, we always consider
R
(and
\mathbb{F}
) as concentrated in degree
{\bar{0}}
.

We use
\Bbbk
to denote
\mathbb{F}
or
R
and use it whenever the nature of the ground ring is not important. On the other hand, when it is important to emphasize whether we are working over
R
or
\mathbb{F}
, we will use lower indices; for example for an
R
-algebra
A_{R}
and an
A_{R}
-module
V_{R}
, after extending scalars we have
V_{\mathbb{F}}:=\mathbb{F}\otimes_{R}V_{R}
is a module over
A_{\mathbb{F}}:=\mathbb{F}\otimes_{R}A_{R}
.

2.2.
Superalgebras and supermodules

Let
V=\bigoplus_{{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{Z}/2}V_{\varepsilon}
be a
\Bbbk
-supermodule.
If
v\in V_{\varepsilon}\setminus\{0\}
for
{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{Z}/2
, we say
v
is
homogeneous
, we write
|v|={\varepsilon}
, and we refer to
{\varepsilon}
as the
parity
of
v
.
If
S\subseteq V
, we denote
S_{\bar{0}}:=S\cap V_{\bar{0}}
and
S_{\bar{1}}:=S\cap V_{\bar{1}}
. If
W
is another
\Bbbk
-supermodule, the set of all
\Bbbk
-linear homomorphisms
\operatorname{Hom}_{\Bbbk}(V,W)
is a
\Bbbk
-supermodule such that for
{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{Z}/2
, we have

\operatorname{Hom}_{\Bbbk}(V,W)_{\varepsilon}=\{f\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\Bbbk}(V,W)\mid|f(v)|=|v|+{\varepsilon}\ \text{for all homogeneous $v$}\}.

The group
\mathfrak{S}_{d}
acts on
V^{\otimes d}
on the right by automorphisms, such that for all homogeneous
v_{1},\dots,v_{d}\in V
and
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{d}
, we have

(2.1)

(v_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes v_{d})^{\sigma}=(-1)^{\langle\sigma;\text{\boldmath$v$}\rangle}v_{\sigma 1}\otimes\dots\otimes v_{\sigma d},

where, setting
\text{\boldmath$v$}:=v_{1}\cdots v_{d}\in V^{d}
, we have put:

(2.2)

\langle\sigma;\text{\boldmath$v$}\rangle:=\sharp\{(k,l)\in[d]^{2}\mid k<l,\ \sigma^{-1}k>\sigma^{-1}l,\ |v_{k}|=|v_{l}|={\bar{1}}\}.

We consider the
d
th divided power

\Gamma^{d}V
, which by definition is the subspace of invariants

(2.3)

\Gamma^{d}V:=\{w\in V^{\otimes d}\mid w^{\sigma}=w\ \text{for all $\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{d}$}\}.

Let
0\leq c\leq d
. Given
w_{1}\in V^{\otimes c}
and
w_{2}\in V^{\otimes(d-c)}
, we define

(2.4)

w_{1}*w_{2}:=\sum_{\sigma}(w_{1}\otimes w_{2})^{\sigma}\in V^{\otimes d},

where the sum is over all shortest coset representatives
\sigma
for
(\mathfrak{S}_{c}\times\mathfrak{S}_{d-c})\backslash\mathfrak{S}_{d}
.

Let
V
and
W
be
\Bbbk
-supermodules,
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, and let
\text{\boldmath$v$}=v_{1}\cdots v_{d}\in V^{d}
,
\text{\boldmath$w$}=w_{1}\cdots w_{d}\in W^{d}
be
d
-tuples of homogeneous elements. We denote

(2.5)

\langle\text{\boldmath$v$},\text{\boldmath$w$}\rangle:=\sharp\{(k,l)\in[d]^{2}\mid k>l,\ |v_{k}|=|w_{l}|={\bar{1}}\}.

Let
A
be a (unital)
\Bbbk
-superalgebra and
V,W
be
A
-supermodules.
A
homogeneous
A
-supermodule homomorphism

f:V\to W
is a homogeneous
\Bbbk
-linear map
f:V\to W
satisfying
f(av)=(-1)^{|f||a|}af(v)
for all (homogeneous)
a,v
. For
{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{Z}/2
, let
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(V,W)_{\varepsilon}
be the set of all homogeneous
A
-supermodule homomorphisms of parity
{\varepsilon}
, and let

\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(V,W):=\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(V,W)_{\bar{0}}\oplus\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(V,W)_{\bar{1}}.

We refer to the elements of
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(V,W)
as the
A
-supermodule homomorphisms from
V
to
W
.
We denote by
A\!\operatorname{-mod}
the category of all finitely generated (left)
A
-supermodules and all
A
-supermodule homomorphisms. We denote by ‘
\cong
’ an isomorphism in this category and by ‘
\simeq
’ an
even isomorphism
in this category.

We have the parity change functor
\Pi
on
A\!\operatorname{-mod}
:
for
V\in A\!\operatorname{-mod}
we have
\Pi V\in A\!\operatorname{-mod}
with
(\Pi V)_{\varepsilon}=V_{{\varepsilon}+{\bar{1}}}
for all
{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{Z}/2
and the new action

a\cdot v=(-1)^{|a|}av
for
a\in A,v\in V
. We have
V\cong\Pi V
via the identity map.

Suppose there is an even superalgebra anti-involution
\tau:A\to A
. In particular,

\tau(ab)=(-1)^{|a||b|}\tau(b)\tau(a)

for all
a,b\in A
. Then
\tau
is an isomorphism
A\to A^{{\mathrm{sop}}}
, where the multiplication in
A^{{\mathrm{sop}}}
is defined as
a\cdot b:=(-1)^{|a||b|}ba
.
If
V\in A\!\operatorname{-mod}
then the
\tau
-dual
V^{\tau}\in A\!\operatorname{-mod}
is the dual
V^{*}
as a
\Bbbk
-supermodule considered as a left
A
-supermodule via
(af)(v):=(-1)^{|a||f|}f(\tau(a)v)
for
a\in A,f\in V^{*},v\in V
.

As usual, the tensor product
A^{\otimes d}
is a superalgebra with respect to

(a_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes a_{d})(b_{1}\otimes\dots b_{d})=(-1)^{\langle\text{\boldmath$a$},\text{\boldmath$b$}\rangle}a_{1}b_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes a_{d}b_{d},

where we have put
\text{\boldmath$a$}:=a_{1}\cdots a_{d}
,
\text{\boldmath$b$}:=b_{1}\cdots b_{d}
(here and below, in expressions like this, we assume that all elements are homogeneous). If
V
is an
A
-supermodule then
V^{\otimes d}
is a supermodule over
A^{\otimes d}
with respect to

(a_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes a_{d})(v_{1}\otimes\dots v_{d})=(-1)^{\langle\text{\boldmath$a$},\text{\boldmath$v$}\rangle}a_{1}v_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes a_{d}v_{d},

where we have again put
\text{\boldmath$a$}:=a_{1}\cdots a_{d}
,
\text{\boldmath$v$}:=v_{1}\cdots v_{d}
.

The divided power
\Gamma^{d}A
is a subsuperalgebra of
A^{\otimes d}
. If
V
is an
A
-supermodule then

\big((a_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes a_{d})(v_{1}\otimes\dots v_{d})\big)^{\sigma}=(a_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes a_{d})^{\sigma}(v_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes v_{d})^{\sigma}

for all
a_{1},\dots,a_{d}\in A,\,v_{1},\dots,v_{d}\in V,\,\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{d}
. So
\Gamma^{d}V
is a subsupermodule of the restriction of
V^{\otimes d}
to
\Gamma^{d}A
. Thus we will always consider
\Gamma^{d}V
as a
\Gamma^{d}A
-supermodule.

2.3.
Based quasi-hereditary algebras

The main reference here is
[
12
]
.
Let
A
be a
\Bbbk
-superalgebra.

Definition 2.6
.

[
12
]
Let
I
be a finite partially ordered set and let
X=\bigsqcup_{i\in I}X(i)
and
Y=\bigsqcup_{i\in I}Y(i)
be finite sets of homogeneous elements of
A
with distinguished elements
e_{i}\in X(i)\cap Y(i)
for each
i\in I
. For each
i\in I
, we set

A^{>i}:=\operatorname{span}\{xy\mid j>i,\,x\in X(j),\,y\in Y(j)\}.

We say that

is
heredity data
if the following axioms hold:

(a)

B:=\{xy\mid i\in I,\,x\in X(i),\,y\in Y(i)\}
is a basis of
A
;

(b)

For all
i\in I
,
x\in X(i)
,
y\in Y(i)
and
a\in A
, we have

ax\equiv\sum_{x^{\prime}\in X(i)}l^{x}_{x^{\prime}}(a)x^{\prime}\text{ }(\text{\rm mod }A^{>i})\,\ \ \text{and}\ \ ya\equiv\sum_{y^{\prime}\in Y(i)}r^{y}_{y^{\prime}}(a)y^{\prime}\text{ }(\text{\rm mod }A^{>i})\,

for some
l^{x}_{x^{\prime}}(a),r^{y}_{y^{\prime}}(a)\in\Bbbk
;

(c)

For all
i,j\in I
and
x\in X(i),\ y\in Y(i)
we have

\displaystyle xe_{i}=x,\ e_{i}x=\delta_{x,e_{i}}x,\ e_{i}y=y,\ ye_{i}=\delta_{y,e_{i}}y,

\displaystyle e_{j}x=x\ \text{or}\ 0,\ ye_{j}=y\ \text{or}\ 0.

If
A
is endowed with heredity data
I,X,Y
, we call
A

based quasi-hereditary
, and refer to
B
as a
heredity basis
of
A
. By (c),
e_{i}^{2}=e_{i}
for all
i\in I
, so from now on we call

the
standard idempotents
of the heredity data. We set

(2.7)

\displaystyle B_{\mathfrak{a}}

\displaystyle:=\{xy\mid i\in I,x\in X(i)_{\bar{0}},y\in Y(i)_{\bar{0}}\},\

(2.8)

\displaystyle B_{\mathfrak{c}}

\displaystyle:=\{xy\mid i\in I,x\in X(i)_{\bar{1}},y\in Y(i)_{\bar{1}}\},

so that

(2.9)

B_{\bar{0}}=B_{\mathfrak{a}}\sqcup B_{\mathfrak{c}}.

The heredity data

of

is called

spans a unital subalgebra of

.

Let
A
be a based quasi-hereditary superalgebra with heredity data

(not necessarily conforming). By
[
12
, Lemma 3.3]
,

is quasi-hereditary in the sense of Cline, Parshall and Scott and

is a highest weight category (see
[
3
, Theorem 3.6]
).
The corresponding standard and costandard modules are defined as follows. Let
i\in I
. Note that
A^{>i}
the ideal of
A
generated by
\{e_{j}\mid j>i\}
, and denote

\tilde{A}:=A/A^{>i}
,
\tilde{a}:=a+A^{>i}\in\tilde{A}
for

.
The
standard
A
-module of highest weight

is defined by

, which is a free
\Bbbk
-module with basis

\{v_{x}:=\tilde{x}\mid x\in X(i)\}
, see
[

, §2.3]
.
We also have the

, and by symmetry every result we have about

.
The

Let

. A

.

. If

is called a

of

. The algebra

is defined uniquely up to Morita superequivalence and is quasi-hereditary, see
[

, Proposition 4.26]
.

2.4.
Multipartitions and tableaux

For a partition
\lambda
, we have the conjugate partition
\lambda^{\prime}
, see
[
16
, p.2]
.
For partitions
\lambda,\mu,\nu
, we denote by
c^{\,\lambda}_{\mu,\nu}
the corresponding Littlewood-Richardson coefficient, see
[
16
, § I.9]
. The Young diagram of
\lambda
is
[\lambda]:=\{(r,s)\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\times\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\mid s\leq\lambda_{r}\}
. We refer to
(r,s)\in[\lambda]
as the
nodes
of
\lambda
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. We denote
\Lambda(n):=\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
and interpret it as the set of
compositions

\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})
with
n
non-negative parts.
For such
\lambda
, we set
|\lambda|:=\lambda_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{n}
. The partitions with at most
n
parts are identified with

\Lambda_{+}(n):=\{\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})\in\Lambda(n)\mid\lambda_{1}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{n}\}.

For
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, we let

\Lambda(n,d):=\{\lambda\in\Lambda(n)\mid|\lambda|=d\}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Lambda_{+}(n,d):=\{\lambda\in\Lambda_{+}(n)\mid|\lambda|=d\}.

For
\lambda,\mu\in\Lambda(n)
, we define
\lambda+\mu:=(\lambda_{1}+\mu_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}+\mu_{n})
.

Recall that
I
denotes a finite poset.
We will consider the set of
I
-
multicompositions

(2.11)

\Lambda^{I}(n):=\Lambda(n)^{I}=\{\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}=(\lambda^{(i)})_{i\in I}\mid\lambda^{(i)}\in\Lambda(n)\ \text{for all $i\in I$}\}.

For
\text{\boldmath$\lambda$},\text{\boldmath$\mu$}\in\Lambda^{I}(n)
we define
\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}+\text{\boldmath$\mu$}
to be
\text{\boldmath$\nu$}\in\Lambda^{I}(n)
with
\nu^{(i)}=\lambda^{(i)}+\mu^{(i)}
for all
i\in I
. For
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, we have the sets of
I
-multicompositions and
I
-multipartitions of

:

\displaystyle\Lambda^{I}(n,d)

\displaystyle:=\{\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}\in\Lambda^{I}(n)\mid\sum_{i\in I}|\lambda^{(i)}|=d\},

\displaystyle\Lambda_{+}^{I}(n,d)

\displaystyle:=\{\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}\in\Lambda^{I}(n,d)\mid\lambda^{(i)}\in\Lambda_{+}(n)\ \text{for all $i\in I$}\}.

For
\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}\in\Lambda^{I}_{+}(n,d)

Lemma 3.16

[

5.1.
The extended zigzag algebra

Fix
\ell\geq 1
and set

I:=\{0,1,\dots,\ell\},\quad J:=\{0,1,\dots,\ell-1\}.

Let
\Gamma
be the quiver with vertex set
I
and arrows
\{{\mathsf{a}}_{j,j+1},{\mathsf{a}}_{j+1,j}\mid j\in J\}
as in the picture:

0
1
2
\cdots
\ell-1
\ell
{\mathsf{a}}_{1,0}
{\mathsf{a}}_{2,1}
{\mathsf{a}}_{3,2}
{\mathsf{a}}_{\ell-2,\ell-1}
{\mathsf{a}}_{\ell,\ell-1}
{\mathsf{a}}_{0,1}
{\mathsf{a}}_{1,2}
{\mathsf{a}}_{2,3}
{\mathsf{a}}_{\ell-2,\ell-1}
{\mathsf{a}}_{\ell-1,\ell}

The
extended zigzag algebra
{\mathsf{Z}}
is the path algebra
\Bbbk\Gamma
modulo the following relations:

(i)

All paths of length three or greater are zero.

(ii)

All paths of length two that are not cycles are zero.

(iii)

All length-two cycles based at the same vertex are equivalent.

(iv)

{\mathsf{a}}_{\ell,\ell-1}{\mathsf{a}}_{\ell-1,\ell}=0
.

Length zero paths yield the standard idempotents
\{{\mathsf{e}}_{i}\mid i\in I\}
with
{\mathsf{e}}_{i}{\mathsf{a}}_{i,j}{\mathsf{e}}_{j}={\mathsf{a}}_{i,j}
for all admissible
i,j
. The algebra
{\mathsf{Z}}
is graded by the path length:

{\mathsf{Z}}={\mathsf{Z}}^{0}\oplus{\mathsf{Z}}^{1}\oplus{\mathsf{Z}}^{2}.

We consider
{\mathsf{Z}}
as a superalgebra with

{\mathsf{Z}}_{\bar{0}}={\mathsf{Z}}^{0}\oplus{\mathsf{Z}}^{2}\quad\text{and}\quad{\mathsf{Z}}_{\bar{1}}={\mathsf{Z}}^{1}.

Define

{\mathsf{c}}_{j}:={\mathsf{a}}_{j,j+1}{\mathsf{a}}_{j+1,j}
for all
j\in J
.

The superalgebra
{\mathsf{Z}}
has an anti-involution
\tau
with

\tau({\mathsf{e}}_{i})={\mathsf{e}}_{i},\ \tau({\mathsf{a}}_{ij})={\mathsf{a}}_{ji},\ \tau({\mathsf{c}}_{j})=-{\mathsf{c}}_{j}.

We consider the total order on
I

given by
0<1<\dots<\ell
. For
i\in I
, we set

(5.1)

{\mathsf{X}}(i):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\{{\mathsf{e}}_{i},{\mathsf{a}}_{i-1,i}\}&\hbox{if $i>0$,}\\
\{{\mathsf{e}}_{0}\}&\hbox{if $i=0$,}\end{array}\right.\quad{\mathsf{Y}}(i):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\{{\mathsf{e}}_{i},{\mathsf{a}}_{i,i-1}\}&\hbox{if $i>0$,}\\
\{{\mathsf{e}}_{0}\}&\hbox{if $i=0$.}\end{array}\right.

With respect to this data we have:

Lemma 5.2
.

[
12
, Lemma 4.14]

The graded superalgebra
{\mathsf{Z}}
is based quasi-hereditary with conforming heredity data
I,{\mathsf{X}},{\mathsf{Y}}
.
For the corresponding heredity basis
{\mathsf{B}}
we have

{\mathsf{B}}_{\mathfrak{a}}=\{{\mathsf{e}}_{i}\mid i\in I\},\ {\mathsf{B}}_{\mathfrak{c}}=\{{\mathsf{c}}_{j}\mid j\in J\},\ {\mathsf{B}}_{\bar{1}}=\{{\mathsf{a}}_{j,j+1},{\mathsf{a}}_{j+1,j}\mid j\in J\}.

For
i\in I
, let
{\mathsf{L}}(i)=\Bbbk\cdot{\mathsf{v}}_{i}
with
|{\mathsf{v}}_{i}|={\bar{0}}
and the action
{\mathsf{e}}_{i}{\mathsf{v}}_{i}={\mathsf{v}}_{i}
,
{\mathsf{b}}{\mathsf{v}}_{i}=0
for all
{\mathsf{b}}\in{\mathsf{B}}\setminus\{{\mathsf{e}}_{i}\}
. This makes
{\mathsf{L}}(i)
a
{\mathsf{Z}}
-supermodule, and, up to isomorphism,
\{{\mathsf{L}}(i)\mid i\in I\}
is a complete set of irreducible
{\mathsf{Z}}
-supermodules. Note that
L(i)^{\tau}\simeq L(i)
.

The standard modules
\Delta(i)
have similarly explicit description:
\Delta(0)\simeq{\mathsf{L}}(0)
, and for
i>0
,
\Delta(i)
has basis
\{{\mathsf{v}}_{i},{\mathsf{w}}_{i}\}
with
|{\mathsf{v}}_{i}|={\bar{0}}
,
|{\mathsf{w}}_{i}|={\bar{1}}
and the only non-trivial action is:
{\mathsf{e}}_{i}{\mathsf{v}}_{i}={\mathsf{v}}_{i}
,
{\mathsf{e}}_{i-1}{\mathsf{w}}_{i}={\mathsf{w}}_{i}
,
{\mathsf{a}}_{i-1,i}{\mathsf{v}}_{i}={\mathsf{w}}_{i}
. For the costandard modules we have

\nabla(0)\simeq\Delta(0)^{\tau}\simeq{\mathsf{L}}(0)
, and for
i>0
,
\nabla(i)\simeq\Delta(i)^{\tau}
has basis
\{{\mathsf{v}}_{i}^{*},{\mathsf{w}}_{i}^{*}\}
with
|{\mathsf{v}}_{i}^{*}|={\bar{0}}
,
|{\mathsf{w}}_{i}^{*}|={\bar{1}}
and the only non-trivial action is:
{\mathsf{e}}_{i}{\mathsf{v}}_{i}^{*}={\mathsf{v}}_{i}^{*}
,
{\mathsf{e}}_{i-1}{\mathsf{w}}_{i}^{*}={\mathsf{w}}_{i}^{*}
,
{\mathsf{a}}_{i,i-1}{\mathsf{w}}_{i}^{*}=-{\mathsf{v}}_{i}^{*}
.

The indecomposable tilting supermodules over
{\mathsf{Z}}
are as follows:
\mathsf{T}(0)\simeq{\mathsf{L}}(0)
and
\mathsf{T}(i)=\Pi{\mathsf{Z}}{\mathsf{e}}_{i-1}
for
i>0
. It will actually be more convenient for us to work with the tilting modules
\Pi\mathsf{T}(i)
. Thus
\Pi\mathsf{T}(0)=\Pi{\mathsf{L}}(0)
is
1
-dimensional with basis
\{{\mathsf{v}}_{0}\}
where
{\mathsf{v}}_{0}
is odd,
\Pi\mathsf{T}(1)={\mathsf{Z}}{\mathsf{e}}_{0}

\{{\mathsf{e}}_{0},{\mathsf{a}}_{1,0},{\mathsf{c}}_{0}\}
, and, for

\{{\mathsf{e}}_{i-1},{\mathsf{a}}_{i-2,i-1},{\mathsf{a}}_{i,i-1},{\mathsf{c}}_{i-1}\}

\mathsf{T}:=\bigoplus_{i\in I}\Pi\mathsf{T}(i)=\Pi L(0)\oplus\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\ell-1}{\mathsf{Z}}{\mathsf{e}}_{i}.

We have the right multiplication maps

\rho_{{\mathsf{c}}_{i}}:{\mathsf{Z}}{\mathsf{e}}_{i}\to{\mathsf{Z}}{\mathsf{e}}_{i},{\mathsf{v}}\mapsto{\mathsf{v}}{\mathsf{c}}_{i}

Lemma 5.3
.

Mapping

. In particular,
{\mathsf{Z}}
is Ringel self-dual.

Proof.

Using the fact that

. Hence the given map is a linear isomorphism.

To check that the map is an algebra homomorphism, one proceeds by cases. For example, for
i\in J
, in

\displaystyle{\mathsf{a}}_{i,i+1}^{\prime}\cdot{\mathsf{a}}_{i+1,i}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=-{\mathsf{a}}_{i+1,i}^{\prime}\circ{\mathsf{a}}_{i,i+1}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=-\iota_{\ell-i}\circ\rho_{{\mathsf{a}}_{\ell-i-2,\ell-i-1}}\circ\pi_{\ell-i-1}\circ\iota_{\ell-i-1}\circ\rho_{{\mathsf{a}}_{\ell-i-1,\ell-i-2}}\circ\pi_{\ell-i}

\displaystyle=-\iota_{\ell-i}\circ\rho_{{\mathsf{a}}_{\ell-i-2,\ell-i-1}}\circ\rho_{{\mathsf{a}}_{\ell-i-1,\ell-i-2}}\circ\pi_{\ell-i}

\displaystyle=\iota_{\ell-i}\circ\rho_{c_{\ell-i-1}}\circ\pi_{\ell-i}

\displaystyle={\mathsf{c}}_{i}^{\prime}.

The other cases are checked similarly.

We use the isomorphism

\operatorname{End}_{T^{{\mathsf{Z}}}(n,d)}({\mathscr{T}})^{\mathrm{sop}}\cong T^{{\mathsf{Z}}^{\prime}}(n,d)
. In particular,
T^{{\mathsf{Z}}}(n,d)
is Ringel self-dual.

Proof.

In view of Lemma
5.3
, we have that
T^{\mathsf{Z}}(n,d)\cong T^{{\mathsf{Z}}^{\prime}}(n,d)
. In particular the second statement of the theorem follows from the first one.

By Corollary
5.13
,
T^{{\mathsf{Z}}^{\prime}}(n,d)
embeds into
\operatorname{End}_{T^{{\mathsf{Z}}}(n,d)}({\mathscr{T}})^{\mathrm{sop}}
. So it suffices to show that

\dim\operatorname{End}_{T^{{\mathsf{Z}}}(n,d)}({\mathscr{T}})=\dim T^{{\mathsf{Z}}}(n,d)
.

In view of (
5.5
), we have that each
{\mathscr{T}}^{\text{\boldmath$\mu$}}
is
\tau_{n,d}
-self-dual and
({\mathscr{T}}^{\text{\boldmath$\mu$}}:\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}))=({\mathscr{T}}^{\text{\boldmath$\mu$}}:\nabla(\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}))
for all
\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}\in\Lambda^{I}_{+}(n,d)
. We now have:

\displaystyle\dim\operatorname{End}_{T^{{\mathsf{Z}}}(n,d)}({\mathscr{T}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\text{\boldmath$\mu$},\text{\boldmath$\nu$}\in\Lambda^{I}(n,d)}\dim\operatorname{Hom}_{T^{\mathsf{Z}}(n,d)}({\mathscr{T}}^{\text{\boldmath$\mu$}},{\mathscr{T}}^{\text{\boldmath$\nu$}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}\in\Lambda^{I}_{+}(n,d)}\sum_{\text{\boldmath$\mu$},\text{\boldmath$\nu$}\in\Lambda^{I}(n,d)}({\mathscr{T}}^{\text{\boldmath$\mu$}}:\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}))({\mathscr{T}}^{\text{\boldmath$\nu$}}:\nabla(\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}\in\Lambda^{I}_{+}(n,d)}\sum_{\text{\boldmath$\mu$},\text{\boldmath$\nu$}\in\Lambda^{I}(n,d)}({\mathscr{T}}^{\text{\boldmath$\mu$}}:\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}))({\mathscr{T}}^{\text{\boldmath$\nu$}}:\Delta(\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}\in\Lambda^{I}_{+}(n,d)}\sum_{\text{\boldmath$\mu$},\text{\boldmath$\nu$}\in\Lambda^{I}(n,d)}k_{\overleftarrow{\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}}^{\prime},\overleftarrow{\text{\boldmath$\mu$}}}k_{\overleftarrow{\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}}^{\prime},\overleftarrow{\text{\boldmath$\nu$}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}\in\Lambda^{I}_{+}(n,d)}\sum_{\text{\boldmath$\mu$},\text{\boldmath$\nu$}\in\Lambda^{I}(n,d)}k_{\text{\boldmath$\lambda$},\text{\boldmath$\mu$}}k_{\text{\boldmath$\lambda$},\text{\boldmath$\nu$}}

\displaystyle=\dim T^{{\mathsf{Z}}}(n,d),

where we have used (
5.10
) for the first equality,
[
5
, Proposition A2.2]
for the second equality, Proposition
5.17
for the fourth equality and

[
14
, Theorem 5.17]
for the last equality.
∎

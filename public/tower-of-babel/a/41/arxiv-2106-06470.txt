Eigendamage: an Eigendeformation model for the variational approximation of cohesive fracture – a one-dimensional case study


Abstract
We study an approximation scheme for a variational theory of cohesive fracture in a one-dimensional setting. Here, the energy functional is approximated by a family of functionals depending on a small parameter 0 < ε ≪ 1 0<\varepsilon\ll 1 and on two fields: the elastic part of the displacement field and an eigendeformation field that describes the inelastic response of the material beyond the elastic regime. We measure the inelastic contributions of the latter in terms of a non-local energy functional. Our main result shows that, as ε → 0 \varepsilon\to 0 , the approximate functionals Γ \Gamma -converge to a cohesive zone model.

Introduction

A tension test on a bar will typically show that small deformations are completely reversible (elastic regime) while large deformations lead to complete failure (fracture regime). Only for very brittle materials one observes a sharp transition between these two regimes (brittle fracture). By way of contrast, many materials exhibit an intermediate
cohesive zone
(
damage regime
) in which plastic flow occurs and a body shows gradually increasing damage before eventual rupture (ductile fracture).

Variational models have been extremely successfully applied to problems in fracture mechanics, cf., e.g.,
[
8
,
2
,
7
,
18
]
and the references therein including, in particular, the seminal contribution of Francfort and Marigo
[
23
]
. Here energy functionals are considered that act on deformations in the class of functions of bounded variation (or deformation). The derivatives of these functions are merely measures, and the singular part of such a measure is directly related to the inelastic behavior of the bar. The resulting variational problems are of
free discontinuity type
allowing for solutions with jump discontinuity (macroscopic cracks). Moreover, within a damage regime the strain can contain a diffuse singular part describing continuous deformations beyond the elastic regime that can be related to the occurrence of microcracks, see e.g.
[
22
,
24
,
21
,
25
,
19
,
12
,
35
,
1
]
. In fact, when considering variational problems with stored energy functions of linear growth at infinity and surface energy contributions that scale linearly for small crack openings, all these contributions to the total strain interact, cp.
[
6
]
, which renders the problem challenging, both from a theoretical and a computational point of view.

As free discontinuity problems are of great interest not only in fracture mechanics but also in image processing, several approximation schemes have been proposed with the aim to devise efficient numerical approaches to simulations. Most notably, the Ambrosio–Tortorelli approximation
[
4
,
3
]
has triggered a still continuing interest in
phase field models
in which a second field (the ‘phase field’) is introduced that can be interpreted as a damage indicator and whose value influences the elastic response of the material.

With a particular focus on cohesive zone damage models we refer to, e.g.,
[
16
,
26
,
13
,
17
,
15
]
. A small parameter
\varepsilon
is introduced in such models that corresponds to an intrinsic length scale over which sharp interfaces of the phase field variable are smeared out. A different approach has been initiated by Braides and Dal Maso for the Mumford–Shah functional, and then extended to various generalized settings in, e.g.,
[
11
,
8
,
29
,
14
,
30
,
32
,
27
,
28
]
which involves a non-local approximation of the original field
u
in terms of convolution kernels with intrinsic length scale
\varepsilon\ll 1
.

Our main motivation comes from the Eigenfracture approach to brittle materials that has been developed in
[
37
]
and further considered in
[
34
,
33
,
36
]
. Our main aim is to extend this model to a ductile fracture regime with a significant damage zone. The variables of the model are the deformation field
u_{\varepsilon}
and an
eigendeformation
field
g_{\varepsilon}
, which induces a decomposition of the strain
u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}=(u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}-g_{\varepsilon})+g_{\varepsilon}
into an elastic and an inelastic part, the latter describing deformation modes that cost no local elastic energy. (We refer to
[
31
]
for more details on the concept of eigendeformations to describe inelastic deformations and, in particular, plastic deformations.) The energy associated to the formation and increase of damage is accordingly modeled in terms of a non-local functional acting on
g_{\varepsilon}
, which replaces the non-local contribution defined in terms of a simple
\varepsilon
-neighborhood of the crack set in the original Eigenfracture model by a more general (and softer) convolution approximation.

We would like to point out that our set-up thus introduces a novel modeling aspect to damage functionals. Instead of an explicit dependence of the stored energy function on the damage as being encoded in a phase field, in our model the constitutive laws, i.e., the linear elastic energy
|\cdot|^{2}
and fracture contribution
f
(see below) remain unchanged. An increase of damage is rather related to a transition from the elastic deformation field to the eigendeformation field. In particular, plastic deformations at the onset of the inelastic regime need not immediately lead to softening of the material. With respect to non-local convolution approximation schemes of the deformation field
u
we remark that in our model such non-local contributions need to be evaluated only near the support of
g_{\varepsilon}
but not on purely elastic regions.

In the present contribution we focus on the one-dimensional case. In this setting our analysis will benefit from the corresponding studys
[
29
]
of Lussardi and Vitali for pure convolution functional. Indeed, we will follow along the same path in order to adapt and extend their methods to our two field set-up. There are, however, a number of notable differences in our analysis which lead us, also in view of later extensions to higher dimensions
[
5
]
, to provide a self-contained account of our results. A main difficulty stems from the fact that there is no pre-assigned functional relation between the eigendeformation fields
g_{\varepsilon}
and the strain fields
u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}
. Rather these quantities are merely ‘coupled by regularity’ in the sense that
u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}-g_{\varepsilon}\in L^{2}
. As the limit of
g_{\varepsilon}
needs to be studied in a rather weak space, this leads to technical difficulties when transferring asymptotic properties from
u_{\varepsilon}
to
g_{\varepsilon}
.

Our results also constitute the first step towards higher-dimensional models. In particular, the case of antiplane shear will be addressed in a forthcoming contribution
[
5
]
. Here the lack of a direct relation between
g_{\varepsilon}
and
\nabla u_{\varepsilon}
and hence the absence of an underlying gradient structure will pose severe additional challenges.

Remark 2.2
.

Our result can be seen as a two-field extension of the setting considered in
[
29
]
. Indeed, introducing the constraints
g=u^{\prime}
, respectively
\gamma=Du
, one is lead to functionals
F_{\varepsilon}(u)=E_{\varepsilon}(u,u^{\prime}\mathcal{L}^{1})
and
F(u)=E(u,Du)
depending on

only. In this case the
\Gamma
-convergence of the sequence
\{F_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
to

has been obtained in
[
29
]
.

The unrestricted problem is in fact strongly related. Indeed, even for
K=\infty
an energy bound implies
L^{\infty}
bounds away from an asymptotically small exceptional set. The complement of the exceptional set can be chosen as a union of a bounded number of intervals, concentrating on the points of a finite partition

in the limit

such that

and

converge with respect to the

(a,b)\setminus\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{m-1}\}
. On the exceptional set, however,
u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}
and

can assume extremely large values, spoiling their convergence even in a weak distributional sense. As a result, large jumps can develop in the limit and parts of

may elapse to

. In order to account for such a possibility we consider limiting functions taking values in

\mathcal{P}=\big\{(x_{0},\ldots,x_{m})\colon a=x_{0}<x_{1}<\ldots<x_{m}=b,\ m\in\mathbb{N}\big\}

as consisting of functions

of the form

(4)

with

,

. We denote the part where

Remark 2.5
.

Our methods can easily be adapted to obtain an alternative asymptotic description by considering renormalized functionals: From the above partition

Remark 2.7

\liminf
inequality follow exactly as before with
W^{**}
instead of
|\cdot|^{2}
by taking account of the obvious adaptions such as replacing
L^{2}
by
L^{p}
and
SBV^{2}
by
SBV^{p}
. The
\Gamma
-
\limsup
inequality requires an extra relaxation step, which is detailed in Remark
6.2
, and is otherwise straightforward as well.

For completeness we also give the corresponding approximation results for the minimal energies with respect to the second variable
\gamma
, which are defined as

\tilde{E}_{\varepsilon}(u)\coloneqq\inf_{\gamma\in\mathcal{M}(a,b)}E_{\varepsilon}(u,\gamma)=\inf_{g\in L^{1}(a,b)}E_{\varepsilon}(u,g\mathcal{L}^{1})

(5)

and

\tilde{E}(u)\coloneqq\inf_{g\in L^{1}(a,b)}E(u,D^{s}u+g\mathcal{L}^{1}),

(6)

for
u\in L^{1}(a,b)
and
u\in L^{0}((a,b),\overline{{\mathbb{R}}})
, respectively. As a direct consequence of the previous
\Gamma
-convergence result we obtain:

Corollary 2.8
.

The family
\{\tilde{E}_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}

\Gamma
-converges to
\tilde{E}
, on
L^{1}(a,b)
equipped with the strong topology if
K<\infty
and with respect to convergence a.e. on
L^{0}((a,b),\overline{{\mathbb{R}}})
if
K=\infty
.

3
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall some basics on
BV
-functions, for simplicity on a one-dimensional interval
(a,b)\subset{\mathbb{R}}
, and convergence of measures.

Functions of bounded variation.

A function
u\in L^{1}(a,b)
is said to belong to the space
BV(a,b)
of
functions of bounded variation
if its distributional derivative
is a finite Radon measure, i.e, if the integration-by-parts formula

\int_{a}^{b}u\varphi^{\prime}\,\textup{d}x=-\int_{a}^{b}\varphi\,\textup{d}Du\quad\text{ for every }\varphi\in C_{c}^{1}(a,b)

is valid for a (unique) measure
Du\in\mathcal{M}(a,b)
. The space
BV(a,b)
is a Banach space endowed with the norm

\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{BV(a,b)}\coloneqq\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{1}(a,b)}+|Du|(a,b),

where
|Du|(a,b)
is the total variation of
Du
. We here collect some basic facts from
[
2
]
for functions of bounded variation, which are relevant for our paper.

We recall the notions of weak-
*
and strict convergence for sequences
\{u_{n}\}_{n}
in
BV(a,b)
, which are useful for compactness properties and approximation arguments, respectively. We say that
\{u_{n}\}_{n}

converges weakly-
*
to
u\in BV(a,b)
, denoted by
u_{n}\overset{*}{\rightharpoonup}u
, if
u_{n}\to u
in
L^{1}(a,b)
and
Du_{n}\overset{*}{\rightharpoonup}Du
in
\mathcal{M}(a,b)
. We notice that every weakly-
*
converging sequence in
BV(a,b)
is norm-bounded by Banach–Steinhaus, while every norm-bounded sequence in
BV(a,b)
contains a weakly-
*
converging subsequence (see
[
2
, Theorem 3.23]
). We further say that
\{u_{n}\}_{n}

converges strictly
to
u\in BV(a,b)
if
u_{n}\to u
in
L^{1}(a,b)
and
|Du_{n}|(a,b)\to|Du|(a,b)
. As a matter of fact, the space
C^{\infty}(a,b)
is dense in
BV(a,b)
with respect to the strict topology (see
[
2
, Theorem 3.9]
).

We next discuss approximate continuity and discontinuity properties of a function
u\in L^{1}_{\textnormal{loc}}(a,b)
. We say that
u
has an
approximate limit
at
x\in(a,b)
if there exists a (unique)
\widetilde{u}(x)\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that

\lim_{\rho\to 0^{+}}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{(x-\rho,x+\rho)\cap(a,b)}|u(y)-\widetilde{u}(x)|\,\textup{d}y=0.

We denote by
S_{u}
the set, where this condition fails, and call it the
approximate discontinuity set
of
u
. It is
\mathcal{L}^{1}
-negligible, and
\widetilde{u}
coincides
\mathcal{L}^{1}
-a.e. in
(a,b)\setminus S_{u}
with
u
. Furthermore, we say that
u
has an
approximate jump point
at
x\in S_{u}
if there exist (unique)
u(x+),u(x-)\in{\mathbb{R}}
with
u(x+)\neq u(x-)
such that

\lim_{\rho\to 0^{+}}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{(x,x+\rho)}|u(y)-u(x+)|\,\textup{d}y=0\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{\rho\to 0^{+}}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{(x-\rho,x)}|u(y)-u(x-)|\,\textup{d}y=0.

We denote by
J_{u}
the set of approximate jump points and call it the
jump set
of
u
. Notice that
u(x+)
and
u(x-)
can be considered as one-sided limits from the right and from the left, respectively.

For
u\in BV(a,b)
the set
J_{u}
coincides with
S_{u}
and is at most countable. It is also convenient to work with the precise representative

u^{\ast}(x)\coloneqq\begin{cases}\widetilde{u}(x)&\quad\text{if }x\in(a,b)\setminus J_{u},\\
[u](x)/2&\quad\text{if }x\in J_{u}.\end{cases}

According to the Radon–Nikodým theorem the measure derivative
Du=D^{a}u\mathcal{L}^{1}+D^{s}u
can be decomposed into the absolutely continuous and the singular part with respect to the Lebesgue measure
\mathcal{L}^{1}
. We then define the jump and the Cantor part of
Du
as

D^{j}u\coloneqq D^{s}u\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}J_{u}\quad\text{and}\quad D^{c}u\coloneqq D^{s}u\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}((a,b)\setminus J_{u}).

From the identifications
D^{a}u=u^{\prime}\mathcal{L}^{1}
with the approximate gradient
u^{\prime}
for the absolutely continuous part and
D^{j}u=[u]\mathcal{H}^{0}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}J_{u}
with
[u]\coloneqq u(\cdot\,+)-u(\cdot\,-)
for the jump part (see
[
2
, Theorem 3.83 and formula (3.90)]
) we arrive at the decomposition

Du=u^{\prime}\mathcal{L}^{1}+[u]\mathcal{H}^{0}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}J_{u}+D^{c}u.

We can actually decompose the function
u
as

u=u_{a}+u_{j}+u_{c}

(7)

for an absolutely continuous function
u_{a}\in W^{1,1}(a,b)
with
Du_{a}=D^{a}u
, a jump function
u_{j}
with
Du_{j}=D^{j}u
, and a (continuous) Cantor function
u_{c}
with
Du_{c}=D^{c}u
(notice that these functions are determined uniquely up to additive constants). Thus, the decomposition of
Du
is recovered from a corresponding decomposition of the function itself (which for
BV
-functions defined on open subsets of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with
d>1
in general is not possible).

We finally mention the subspace
SBV(a,b)
of
special functions of bounded variation
, which contains all functions
u\in BV(a,b)
with
D^{c}u=0
. In addition, we define

SBV^{2}(a,b)\coloneqq\big\{u\in SBV(a,b)\colon u^{\prime}\in L^{2}(a,b)\text{ and }\mathcal{H}^{0}(J_{u})<\infty\big\}.

Convergence in negative Sobolev spaces and in the flat topology.

The negative Sobolev spaces
W^{-1,q}(a,b)
with
1<q\leq\infty
are defined as usual as the dual spaces of
W^{1,q^{\prime}}_{0}(a,b)
(with
q^{\prime}\in[1,\infty)
denoting the conjugate exponent to
q
with
\tfrac{1}{q}+\tfrac{1}{q^{\prime}}=1
), and correspondingly the norm is defined via the duality pairing as

\|T\|_{W^{-1,q}(a,b)}\coloneqq\sup\Big\{T(\varphi)\colon\varphi\in W^{1,q^{\prime}}_{0}(a,b)\text{ with }\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{W^{1,q^{\prime}}(a,b)}\leq 1\Big\},

for every
T\in W^{-1,q}(a,b)
. Consequently, the spaces
W^{-1,q}(a,b)
with
1<q<\infty
are reflexive and separable. For later purposes, we mention two specific situations. Let
v\in L^{q}(a,b)
and
w\in L^{r}(a,b)
with
1\leq r\leq\infty
such that the embedding
W^{1,q^{\prime}}_{0}(a,b)\subset L^{r^{\prime}}(a,b)
is continuous. If we set

T_{Dv}(\varphi)\coloneqq-\int_{a}^{b}v\varphi^{\prime}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\quad\text{and}\quad T_{w}(\varphi)\coloneqq\int_{a}^{b}w\varphi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\quad\text{for all }\varphi\in W^{1,q^{\prime}}_{0}(a,b),

then, by the Hölder inequality and the continuous embedding (with constant
C^{\prime}
), we obtain
T_{Dv},T_{w}\in W^{-1,q}(a,b)
with

\|T_{Dv}\|_{W^{-1,q}(a,b)}\leq\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{L^{q}(a,b)}\quad\text{and}\quad\|T_{w}\|_{W^{-1,q}(a,b)}\leq C^{\prime}\left\lVert w\right\rVert_{L^{r}(a,b)}.

(8)

Because of the continuous and dense embedding
W^{1,1}_{0}(a,b)\subset C_{0}(a,b)
, the negative Sobolev norms can actually be considered on the space
\mathcal{M}(a,b)
of all finite Radon measures on
(a,b)
, for which the duality pairing reads as

\|\mu\|_{W^{-1,q}(a,b)}=\sup\bigg\{\int_{a}^{b}\varphi\,\textup{d}\mu\colon\varphi\in W^{1,q^{\prime}}_{0}(a,b)\text{ with }\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{W^{1,q^{\prime}}_{0}(a,b)}\leq 1\bigg\}

for
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(a,b)
. If we allow
q^{\prime}=\infty
in this expression, we obtain the flat norm

\left\lVert\mu\right\rVert_{\text{flat}}\coloneqq\sup\bigg\{\int_{a}^{b}\varphi\,\textup{d}\mu\colon\varphi\in W^{1,\infty}_{0}(a,b)\text{ with }\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{W^{1,\infty}_{0}(a,b)}\leq 1\bigg\},

for
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(a,b)
. Here we have the inequalities

\left\lVert Dv\right\rVert_{\text{flat}}\leq\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{L^{1}(a,b)}\quad\text{and}\quad\|w\mathcal{L}^{1}\|_{\text{flat}}\leq\left\lVert w\right\rVert_{L^{1}(a,b)}

(9)

for all functions
v\in BV(a,b)
and
w\in L^{1}(a,b)
. Let us still notice that due to Schauder’s theorem and the compact embedding
W^{1,\infty}_{0}(a,b)\Subset C_{0}(a,b)
, the flat topology metrizes weak-
\ast
convergence of (uniformly bounded) measures. Therefore, we have the following relations for the convergence of measure with respect to convergence in
W^{-1,q}(a,b)
, the flat norm and in the weak-
\ast
sense.

Lemma 3.1

(on convergence of measures)
.

For a measure
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(a,b)
and a sequence
\{\mu_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
of measures in
\mathcal{M}(a,b)
, we have:

(i)

If
\mu_{n}\to\mu
in
W^{-1,q}(a,b)
for some
1<q\leq\infty
, then
\mu_{n}\to\mu
in the flat norm.

(ii)

If
\mu_{n}\overset{*}{\rightharpoonup}\mu
in
\mathcal{M}(a,b)
, then
\mu_{n}\to\mu
in the flat norm.

(iii)

If
\mu_{n}\to\mu
in the flat norm and
\sup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}|\mu_{n}|(a,b)<\infty
, then
\mu_{n}\overset{*}{\rightharpoonup}\mu
in
\mathcal{M}(a,b)
.

4
Compactness

In this section we establish a compactness result for sequences in
L^{1}(a,b)\times\mathcal{M}(a,b)
with bounded energy
E_{\varepsilon}
. This result together with a
{\Gamma\text{-convergence}}
result implies the convergence of minimizers and the corresponding minimum values. In order to bound suitable norms of the two fields in terms of the energy, we first prove the following technical lemma:

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
g\in L^{1}(a,b)
. For
\varepsilon>0
there exists
x_{\varepsilon}\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that the grid

\mathscr{G}_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq\big\{x_{\alpha}\coloneqq x_{\varepsilon}+2\varepsilon\alpha\colon\alpha\in{\mathbb{Z}}\text{ and }x_{\alpha}\in(a+\varepsilon,b-\varepsilon)\big\}

contains a subset
\mathscr{G}^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}\subset\mathscr{G}_{\varepsilon}
with

\int_{\bigcup\{I_{\varepsilon}(x_{\alpha})\colon x_{\alpha}\in\mathscr{G}^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}\}}|g|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+2\#(\mathscr{G}_{\varepsilon}\setminus\mathscr{G}^{\prime}_{\varepsilon})\leq\frac{1}{c_{0}\varepsilon}\int_{a}^{b}f\bigg(\varepsilon\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.98003pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.26338pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.6363pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.45924pt}}\!\int_{I_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap(a,b)}{|g|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}\bigg)\,\textup{d}x.

Proof.

We proceed analogously to
[
29
, proof of Lemma 4.2]
. Let
\phi_{\varepsilon}\in C^{\infty}_{0}(a,b)
be a cut-off function with
0\leq\phi_{\varepsilon}\leq 1
in
(a,b)
and
\phi_{\varepsilon}\equiv 1
in
(a+\varepsilon,b-\varepsilon)
. We then consider
\psi_{\varepsilon}\in C_{0}({\mathbb{R}})
defined via

\psi_{\varepsilon}(x)\coloneqq\phi_{\varepsilon}(x)\,f\bigg(\varepsilon\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{I_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap(a,b)}{|g|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}\bigg)

for
x\in(a,b)
and
\psi_{\varepsilon}(x)\coloneqq 0
for
x\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus(a,b)
. The application of
[
10
, Lemma 4.2]
(with
\eta=2\varepsilon
), which is a consequence of the mean value theorem for integrals, shows that

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\psi_{\varepsilon}\,\textup{d}x=2\varepsilon\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\psi_{\varepsilon}(x_{\varepsilon}+2\varepsilon\alpha)

holds for a suitable
x_{\varepsilon}\in{\mathbb{R}}
. By non-negativity of
f
, the choice of the cut-off function
\phi_{\varepsilon}
and the definition of
\mathscr{G}_{\varepsilon}
, we hence have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{a}^{b}f\bigg(\varepsilon\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{I_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap(a,b)}{|g|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}\bigg)\,\textup{d}x

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi_{\varepsilon}(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\geq 2\sum_{x_{\alpha}\in\mathscr{G}_{\varepsilon}}\psi_{\varepsilon}(x_{\alpha})=2\sum_{x_{\alpha}\in\mathscr{G}_{\varepsilon}}f\bigg(\varepsilon\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{I_{\varepsilon}(x_{\alpha})}{|g|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}\bigg).

(10)

If we now set

\mathscr{G}^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq\bigg\{x_{\alpha}\in\mathscr{G}_{\varepsilon}\colon\varepsilon\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{I_{\varepsilon}(x_{\alpha})}|g|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t<1\bigg\},

then the claim follows directly from (
10
), after rewriting the right-hand side via the definition of
f
as

\displaystyle 2\sum_{x_{\alpha}\in\mathscr{G}_{\varepsilon}}f\bigg(\varepsilon\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{I_{\varepsilon}(x_{\alpha})}{|g|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}\bigg)

\displaystyle=2c_{0}\sum_{x_{\alpha}\in\mathscr{G}^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}}\varepsilon\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{I_{\varepsilon}(x_{\alpha})}{|g|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}+2\sum_{x_{\alpha}\in\mathscr{G}_{\varepsilon}\setminus\mathscr{G}^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}}c_{0}

\displaystyle=c_{0}\sum_{x_{\alpha}\in\mathscr{G}^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}}\int_{I_{\varepsilon}(x_{\alpha})}|g|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+2c_{0}\#(\mathscr{G}_{\varepsilon}\setminus\mathscr{G}^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}).\qed

We can now address the aforementioned compactness results.

Theorem 4.2

(Compactness for
K<\infty
)
.

Assume
K<\infty
. Let
\{(u_{\varepsilon},\gamma_{\varepsilon})\}_{\varepsilon}
be a sequence in
{L^{1}}(a,b)\times\mathcal{M}(a,b)
with

E_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon},\gamma_{\varepsilon})\leq C_{0}

for a positive constant
C_{0}
and all
\varepsilon>0
. There exist a function
u\in BV(a,b)
with
\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{{L^{\infty}}(a,b)}\leq K
and a measure
\gamma\in\mathcal{M}(a,b)
such that, up to subsequences,
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
converges to
u
in
L^{1}(a,b)
and
\{\gamma_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
converges to
\gamma=\gamma^{s}+g\mathcal{L}^{1}
in
{W^{-1,q}}(a,b)
for all
1<q<\infty
and in particular in the flat norm. Moreover, there holds
\gamma^{s}=D^{s}u
and
u^{\prime}-g\in L^{2}(a,b)
.

Proof.

We first observe from the finiteness of
E_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon},\gamma_{\varepsilon})
that we necessarily have
u_{\varepsilon}\in{W^{1,1}}(a,b)
with
\left\lVert u_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(a,b)}\leq K
and
\gamma_{\varepsilon}=g_{\varepsilon}\mathcal{L}^{1}
for some functions
g_{\varepsilon}\in L^{1}(a,b)
for all
\varepsilon>0
. By the uniform boundedness of
\left\lVert u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}-g_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(a,b)}
and
\left\lVert u_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(a,b)}
we deduce from (
8
)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\gamma_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{W^{-1,\infty}(a,b)}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\mathcal{L}^{1}\right\rVert_{W^{-1,\infty}(a,b)}+\left\lVert\gamma_{\varepsilon}-u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\mathcal{L}^{1}\right\rVert_{W^{-1,\infty}(a,b)}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert u_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(a,b)}+C^{\prime}\left\lVert g_{\varepsilon}-u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(a,b)}\leq C(a,b,K),

independently of
\varepsilon
. Therefore,
\{\gamma_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
is bounded in
W^{-1,\infty}(a,b)
and consequently contains a subsequence, which converges weakly-
\ast
in
W^{-1,\infty}(a,b)
to some
\gamma\in W^{-1,\infty}(a,b)
. We next study the convergence of the sequence
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
. To this end, we consider the function
v_{\varepsilon}
defined by

v_{\varepsilon}(x)\coloneqq\begin{cases}u_{\varepsilon}(x)&\quad x\in\bigcup\{I_{\varepsilon}(x_{\alpha})\colon x_{\alpha}\in\mathscr{G}^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}\},\\
0&\quad\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Since
u_{\varepsilon}\in W^{1,1}(a,b)
with
\left\lVert u_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(a,b)}\leq K
is assumed, we clearly have
{v_{\varepsilon}\in SBV(a,b)}
with
\left\lVert v_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(a,b)}\leq K
, for every
\varepsilon
. Moreover, jump discontinuities of
v_{\varepsilon}
can only occur at points
x_{\alpha}\pm\varepsilon
for
x_{\alpha}\in\mathscr{G}_{\varepsilon}\setminus\mathscr{G}^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}
and close to the boundary at
\min\mathscr{G}^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}
or at
\max\mathscr{G}^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}
. As a consequence of Lemma
4.1
and the definition of the energy
E_{\varepsilon}
, we deduce

\#J_{v_{\varepsilon}}\leq 2\#(\mathscr{G}_{\varepsilon}\setminus\mathscr{G}^{\prime}_{\varepsilon})+2\leq\frac{1}{c_{0}\varepsilon}\int_{a}^{b}f\bigg(\varepsilon\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{I_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap(a,b)}{|g|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}\bigg)\,\textup{d}x+2\leq\frac{C_{0}}{c_{0}}+2,

i.e., that
\#J_{v_{\varepsilon}}
is bounded independently of
\varepsilon
. By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and again by Lemma
4.1
, we additionally have

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}{|v^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(x)|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\bigcup\{I_{\varepsilon}(x_{\alpha})\colon x_{\alpha}\in\mathscr{G}^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}\}}|u^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(x)-g_{\varepsilon}(x)|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\bigcup\{I_{\varepsilon}(x_{\alpha})\colon x_{\alpha}\in\mathscr{G}^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}\}}|g_{\varepsilon}(x)|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq|b-a|^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{a}^{b}|u^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(x)-g_{\varepsilon}(x)|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{1}{c_{0}\varepsilon}\int_{a}^{b}f\bigg(\varepsilon\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{I_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap(a,b)}{|g_{\varepsilon}|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}\bigg)\,\textup{d}x

\displaystyle\leq|b-a|^{\frac{1}{2}}C_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{C_{0}}{c_{0}},

independently of
\varepsilon
. Hence,
\{v_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
is bounded in
BV(a,b)
. By the Rellich–Kondrachov theorem, there exists a subsequence that converges in
L^{q}(a,b)
to some
u\in BV(a,b)
with
\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{{L^{\infty}}(a,b)}\leq K
, for any
1\leq q<\infty
. To identify
u
as the limit in
L^{q}(a,b)
of (the same subsequence of)
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
we notice from the definition of
v_{\varepsilon}

\left\lVert v_{\varepsilon}-u_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert^{q}_{L^{q}(a,b)}\leq\int_{(a,b)\setminus\bigcup\{I_{\varepsilon}(x_{\alpha})\colon x_{\alpha}\in\mathscr{G}^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}\}}K^{q}\,\textup{d}x\leq 2\varepsilon(\#(\mathscr{G}_{\varepsilon}\setminus\mathscr{G}^{\prime}_{\varepsilon})+2)K^{q}.

Since
\#(\mathscr{G}_{\varepsilon}\setminus\mathscr{G}^{\prime}_{\varepsilon})
is bounded uniformly in
\varepsilon
, this allows to conclude the convergence of
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
to
u
in
L^{q}(a,b)
, for any
1\leq q<\infty
.

It remains to show convergence of (the subsequence of)
\{\gamma_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
in

5
Estimate from below of the
\Gamma
-lower limit

Next, we start with the proof of the
\Gamma
-
\liminf

and

and every open subset

of

\displaystyle E_{\varepsilon}(u,\gamma,A)\coloneqq\begin{cases}\int_{A}{|u^{\prime}-g|^{2}\,\textup{d}x}&\quad\text{if }u\in{W^{1,1}}(a,b),\,\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{{L^{\infty}}(a,b)}\leq K,\\

We denote the
\Gamma
-lower and
\Gamma
-upper limit of

by

\displaystyle E^{\prime}(u,\gamma)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\inf\big\{\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}E_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon},\gamma_{\varepsilon})\colon u_{\varepsilon}\to u\text{ in }L^{1}(a,b),\,\gamma_{\varepsilon}\to\gamma\text{ in the flat norm}\big\},

\displaystyle E^{\prime\prime}(u,\gamma)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\inf\big\{\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}E_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon},\gamma_{\varepsilon})\colon u_{\varepsilon}\to u\text{ in }L^{1}(a,b),\,\gamma_{\varepsilon}\to\gamma\text{ in the flat norm}\big\},

for every open subset

Remark 5.1

(Properties of the localized
\Gamma
-lower limit)
.

Let
(u,\gamma)\in{L^{1}}(a,b)\times\mathcal{M}(a,b)
.

(i)

The properties that the set functions
{A\mapsto E_{\varepsilon}(u,\gamma,A)}
are increasing and superadditive (on disjoint sets) for each
\varepsilon
carry immediately over to
{A\mapsto E^{\prime}(u,\gamma,A)}
.

(ii)

A direct consequence of (i) is that lower bounds for
A\mapsto E^{\prime}(u,\gamma,A)
transfer from intervals in
(a,b)
to arbitrary open subsets of
(a,b)
, i.e., if for a positive Borel measure
\lambda
an estimate of the form

E^{\prime}(u,\gamma,A)\geq\lambda(A)

holds for all intervals
A\subset(a,b)
, then the estimate actually holds for any open subset
A\subset(a,b)
, cp.
[
29
, Remark 4.6]
for a similar statement.

In this section we prove the
\Gamma
-
\liminf
inequality, where in view of Remark
4.5
it is sufficient to consider
(u,\gamma)\in BV(a,b)\times\mathcal{M}(a,b)
with
\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(a,b)}\leq K
,

and

. The basic idea is to derive three separate estimates for the jump part, the volume term and the Cantor term, respectively, and to infer the desired estimate then from a combination of these estimates by means of measure theory.

5.1
Estimate from below of the jump term

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
A
be an open subset of
(a,b)
. For every
(u,\gamma)\in BV(a,b)\times\mathcal{M}(a,b)
with
\gamma=D^{s}u+g\mathcal{L}^{1}
and

we have

E^{\prime}(u,\gamma,A)\geq 2\,\sum_{x\in J_{u}\cap A}f\bigg(\frac{1}{2}|[u](x)|\bigg).

Proof.

Step 1: For every
{\bar{x}}\in J_{u}\cap A
we have

E^{\prime}(u,\gamma,A)\geq 2f\bigg(\frac{1}{2}|[u](\bar{x})|\bigg).

(13)

Since only finite energy approximations are of interest, we consider a sequence
\{(u_{\varepsilon},\gamma_{\varepsilon})\}_{\varepsilon}
in
{W^{1,1}}(a,b)\times\mathcal{M}(a,b)
with
\gamma_{\varepsilon}=g_{\varepsilon}\mathcal{L}^{1}
for some
g_{\varepsilon}\in L^{1}(a,b)
and
E_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon},\gamma_{\varepsilon})\leq C_{0}
for some uniform constant
C_{0}
, for all
\varepsilon>0
, such that
u_{\varepsilon}\to u
in
L^{1}(a,b)
and
\gamma_{\varepsilon}\to\gamma
in the flat norm. We fix an arbitrary
\delta>0
such that
(\bar{x}-2\delta,\bar{x}+2\delta)\subset A
(recall that
A\subset(a,b)
is open). By definition of
(u(\bar{x}+),u(\bar{x}-))
and since
u_{\varepsilon}\to u
in measure, we readily find points
\bar{x}_{\varepsilon}^{-}\in(\bar{x}-\delta,\bar{x})
and
\bar{x}_{\varepsilon}^{+}\in(\bar{x},\bar{x}+\delta)
such that, for sufficiently small
\varepsilon
,

|u_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x}^{+}_{\varepsilon})-u(\bar{x}+)|<\delta\quad\text{and}\quad|u_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x}^{-}_{\varepsilon})-u(\bar{x}-)|<\delta

(14)

(also cp.
[
29
, Lemma 5.1]
). Using the monotonicity of
A\mapsto E_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon},\gamma_{\varepsilon},A)
and applying the estimate (
10
) with
(a,b)
replaced by
(\bar{x}-2\delta,\bar{x}+2\delta)
on an associated grid of points
\bar{\mathscr{G}}_{\varepsilon}
, we obtain from the subadditivity and non-negativity of
f

\displaystyle E_{\varepsilon}({u_{\varepsilon}},{\gamma_{\varepsilon}},A)

\displaystyle\geq 2\sum_{x_{\alpha}\in\bar{\mathscr{G}}_{\varepsilon}}f\bigg(\varepsilon\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{I_{\varepsilon}(x_{\alpha})}{|g_{\varepsilon}|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}\bigg)

\displaystyle\geq 2f\bigg(\sum_{x_{\alpha}\in\bar{\mathscr{G}}_{\varepsilon}}\frac{1}{2}\int_{I_{\varepsilon}(x_{\alpha})}{|{g_{\varepsilon}}|\,\textup{d}x}\bigg)\geq 2f\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\int_{(\bar{x}_{\varepsilon}^{-},\bar{x}_{\varepsilon}^{+})}{|g_{\varepsilon}|\,\textup{d}x}\bigg),

(15)

for all
\varepsilon<\delta
(notice the inclusion
(\bar{x}_{\varepsilon}^{-},\bar{x}_{\varepsilon}^{+})\subset\bigcup\{I_{\varepsilon}(x_{\alpha})\colon x_{\alpha}\in\bar{\mathscr{G}}_{\varepsilon}\}
). For the argument on the right-hand side, we observe from the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, the inequalities in (
14
) and from the energy bound

\displaystyle\int_{(\bar{x}_{\varepsilon}^{-},\bar{x}_{\varepsilon}^{+})}{|g_{\varepsilon}|\,\textup{d}x}

\displaystyle\geq\Bigg[\int_{(\bar{x}_{\varepsilon}^{-},\bar{x}_{\varepsilon}^{+})}{|{u}^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}|\,\textup{d}x}-\int_{(\bar{x}_{\varepsilon}^{-},\bar{x}_{\varepsilon}^{+})}{|{g_{\varepsilon}}-{u}^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}|\,\textup{d}x}\Bigg]_{+}

\displaystyle\geq\Big[|{u}_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x}_{\varepsilon}^{+})-u_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x}_{\varepsilon}^{-})|-|\bar{x}_{\varepsilon}^{+}-\bar{x}_{\varepsilon}^{-}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\lVert g_{\varepsilon}-u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(a,b)}\Big]_{+}

\displaystyle\geq\Big[|u(\bar{x}+)-u(\bar{x}-)|-2\delta-(2\delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}C_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big]_{+}.

Using once again the fact that
f
is increasing, we can continue to estimate (
15
) from below via

E_{\varepsilon}({u_{\varepsilon}},{\gamma_{\varepsilon}},A)\geq 2f\left(\frac{1}{2}\Big[|[u](\bar{x})|-2\delta-C_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}\,(2\delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big]_{+}\right).

Now, passing to the
\liminf
as
\varepsilon\to 0
and then letting
\delta\to 0^{+}
, we obtain (
13
).

Step 2.
For an arbitrary
M\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
M\leq\#(J_{u}\cap A)
we select a set
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{M}\}
containing
M
points of

and pairwise disjoint open intervals

in

such that

for all

. First, we apply the monotonicity and superadditivity of

(see Remark

Since

∎

5.2
Estimate from below of the volume and Cantor terms

We basically follow the idea of Lussardi and Vitali from
[

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
(u,\gamma)\in BV(a,b)\times\mathcal{M}(a,b)
with
\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(a,b)}\leq K
,
\gamma=D^{s}u+g\mathcal{L}^{1}
and
u^{\prime}-g\in L^{2}(a,b)
. Furthermore, let
\{(u_{\varepsilon},\gamma_{\varepsilon})\}_{\varepsilon}
be a sequence in
W^{1,1}(a,b)\times\mathcal{M}(a,b)
with
\left\lVert u_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(a,b)}\leq K
,
\gamma_{\varepsilon}=g_{\varepsilon}\mathcal{L}^{1}
and
u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}-g_{\varepsilon}\in L^{2}(a,b)
for all
\varepsilon>0
such that
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
converges to
u
a.e. in
(a,b)
and in
L^{1}(a,b)
and such that
\{\gamma_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
converges to
\gamma
in the flat norm. There exists a sequence
\{(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon},\bar{\gamma}_{\varepsilon})\}_{\varepsilon}
in
W^{1,1}(a,b)\times\mathcal{M}(a,b)
with
\left\lVert\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(a,b)}\leq K
,
\bar{\gamma}_{\varepsilon}=\bar{g}_{\varepsilon}\mathcal{L}^{1}
for some
\bar{g}_{\varepsilon}\in L^{1}(a,b)
and
E_{\varepsilon}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon},\bar{\gamma}_{\varepsilon})\leq E_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon},\gamma_{\varepsilon})
for all
\varepsilon
such that
\{\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
converges to
u
a.e. in
(a,b)
and in
L^{q}(a,b)
for all

and

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\left\lVert\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}-u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(a,b)}\leq\sup\big\{|[u](x)|\colon x\in J_{u}\big\}.

If, in addition, the energies
\{E_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon},\gamma_{\varepsilon})\}_{\varepsilon}
are bounded, then
\{\bar{\gamma}_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
converges to
\gamma
in the flat norm.

Proof.

We follow the outline of the proof for
[
29
, Lemma 4.3]
, which, however, needs some modifications due to the additional variable
\gamma
. In what follows, we may assume that the precise representatives of
u
and
u_{\varepsilon}
for each
\varepsilon
are considered. The function
u
can be decomposed as
u_{a}+u_{j}+u_{c}
, see (
7
), where
u_{j}
is a jump function with jump discontinuities at any point of
J_{u}
and where
u_{a}+u_{c}
is uniformly continuous in
(a,b)
. We set

\sigma\coloneqq\sup\big\{|[u_{j}](x)|\colon x\in J_{u}\big\}

and first claim that for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}

\delta_{n}\in(0,\tfrac{1}{n}]
such that

\displaystyle x,y\in(a,b)\text{ with }|x-y|<\delta_{n}\quad\Rightarrow\quad

\displaystyle|u_{j}(x)-u_{j}(y)|<\sigma+\frac{1}{n},

(16)

\displaystyle|u_{a}(x)+u_{c}(x)-u_{a}(y)-u_{c}(y)|<\frac{1}{n}.

(17)

In fact, there are only finitely many points
\bar{x}_{1},\dots,\bar{x}_{m(n)}
in
J_{u}
that have to be excluded to deduce that

\displaystyle\sum_{z\in J_{u}\setminus\{\bar{x}_{1},\dots,\bar{x}_{m(n)}\}}|[u_{j}](z)|<\frac{1}{n}.

(18)

By choosing
\delta_{n}>0
sufficiently small we can guarantee (
16
) by (
18
) and the definition of
\sigma
provided that each interval of length
\delta_{n}
contains at most one
\bar{x}_{j}
, and we can further ensure (
17
), by the uniform continuity of
u_{a}+u_{c}
. We then consider a partition
P_{n}
of
(a,b),
i.e.,

a=x_{0}<x_{1}<\ldots<x_{k}<x_{k+1}=b

(where the dependence of the points on

is not written explicitly) such that the mesh size is less than
\delta_{n}
, i.e.,
x_{i+1}-x_{i}<\delta_{n}
for all

,
x_{i}\notin J_{u}
and
u_{\varepsilon}(x_{i})\to u(x_{i})
as
\varepsilon\to 0
for every
i\in\{1,\dots,k\}
, which is possible by the pointwise convergence
u_{\varepsilon}\to u
a.e. in
(a,b)
. Since by construction of
\delta_{n}
at most one of the points
\bar{x}_{1},\dots,\bar{x}_{m(n)}\in J_{u}
, where a large jump of

occurs, may belong to the interval
[x_{i},x_{i+1}]
, we necessarily have
|u_{j}(x)-u_{j}(y)|<\tfrac{1}{n}
for
y=x_{i}

For
(u,\gamma)\in BV(a,b)\times\mathcal{M}(a,b)
with
\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(a,b)}\leq K

1.

2.

for every open subset

-lower limit which shows the first part of Theorem

Theorem 5.6
.

For every
(u,\gamma)\in BV(a,b)\times\mathcal{M}(a,b)
with
\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(a,b)}\leq K
,
\gamma=D^{s}u+g\mathcal{L}^{1}
and
u^{\prime}-g\in L^{2}(a,b)
we have

E^{\prime}(u,\gamma)\geq E(u,\gamma).

Proof.

We consider the Radon measure
\kappa
defined by

\kappa(B)\coloneqq\mathcal{L}^{1}(B)+\#(J_{u}\cap B)+|D^{c}u|(B)

for every Borel subset
B
of
(a,b)
. Let
C
be a Borel subset of
(a,b)\setminus J_{u}
with
|C|=0
such that
|D^{c}u|((a,b)\setminus C)=0
. Then, we obtain

E^{\prime}(u,\gamma,A)\geq\int_{A}\psi_{i}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\kappa

for
i\in\{1,2,3\}
and for every open set
A\subset(a,b)
, where

\displaystyle\psi_{1}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\big(|u^{\prime}-g|^{2}+c_{0}|g|\big)\mathbbm{1}_{(a,b)\setminus(J_{u}\cup C)},

\displaystyle\psi_{2}

\displaystyle\coloneqq 2f\left(\tfrac{1}{2}|[u]|\right)\mathbbm{1}_{J_{u}},

\displaystyle\psi_{3}

\displaystyle\coloneqq c_{0}\mathbbm{1}_{C}.

Next, we define

\displaystyle\psi(x)\coloneqq\sup_{i}\psi_{i}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l l}|u^{\prime}(x)-g(x)|^{2}+c_{0}|g(x)|&\quad\text{if }x\in(a,b)\setminus(J_{u}\cup C),\\
2\,f\left(\frac{1}{2}|[u](x)|\right)&\quad\text{if }x\in J_{u},\\
c_{0}&\quad\text{if }x\in C.\end{array}\right.

By a measure theoretic result (see e.g.
[
9
, Lemma 15.2]
applied with the set function
\mu(\cdot)\coloneqq E^{\prime}(u,\gamma,\cdot)
) we conclude that

E^{\prime}(u,\gamma,A)\geq\int_{A}\sup_{i}\psi_{i}\,\textup{d}\kappa=\int_{A}\psi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\kappa=E(u,\gamma,A)

for every open subset
A
of
(a,b)
. With
A=(a,b)
, this proves the theorem.
∎

Corollary 5.7
.

Let

Theorem 6.3

(u,\gamma)\in BV(a,b)\times\mathcal{M}(a,b)

\hat{u}_{h}\to u

\liminf_{h\to 0}E(\hat{u}_{h},\hat{\gamma}_{h})\leq E(u,\gamma).

E^{\prime\prime}(u,\gamma)\leq\liminf_{h\to 0}E^{\prime\prime}(\hat{u}_{h},\hat{\gamma}_{h})\leq\liminf_{h\to 0}E(\hat{u}_{h},\hat{\gamma}_{h})\leq E(u,\gamma).

We first recall that in dimension one every function

as

, and for all

(37)

For the Cantor function

L^{1}(a,b)
and

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}\int_{a}^{b}|u^{\prime}_{c,h}|\,\textup{d}x=|Du_{c}|(a,b).

(38)

The absolutely continuous part
u_{a}
is first extended to a
W^{1,1}({\mathbb{R}})
function with compact support and we then set
u_{a,h}\coloneqq u_{a}*\psi_{h}
for all
h>0
, where
\psi_{h}
is a standard
h
-mollifier given by
\psi_{h}(x)\coloneqq h^{-1}\psi(h^{-1}x)
for
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
, for a fixed non-negative, symmetric function
\psi\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
with compact support and normalized to
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi\,\textup{d}x=1
. We then have
u_{a,h}\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
for all
h>0
,
u_{a,h}\to u_{a}
in
L^{1}(a,b)
and
u_{a,h}^{\prime}=u_{a}^{\prime}*\psi_{h}
, see e.g.
[
20
, Theorem 4.2.1]
. Then we set

u_{h}\coloneqq u_{a,h}+u_{j,h}+u_{c,h}\quad\text{for all }h>0.

We clearly have
\{u_{h}\}_{h}
in
SBV^{2}(a,b)
for all
h>0
and
u_{h}\to u
in
L^{1}(a,b)
, which implies
Du_{h}\to Du
in the flat norm.

We next address the modification of
\gamma
. We extend the absolutely continuous part
g
outside of
(a,b)
by
0
and set
g_{a,h}\coloneqq g*\psi_{h}
for all
h>0
.
Then we have
g_{a,h}\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
for all
h>0
and

\displaystyle g_{a,h}\to g\text{ in }L^{1}(a,b).

(39)

Because of
u_{a}^{\prime}-g\in L^{2}(a,b)
and
u_{a,h}^{\prime}-g_{a,h}=(u_{a}^{\prime}-g)*\psi_{h}
we further notice

\displaystyle u_{a,h}^{\prime}-g_{a,h}\to u_{a}^{\prime}-g\text{ in }L^{2}(a,b).

(40)

Now, we set

\gamma_{h}:=Du_{j,h}+g_{h}\mathcal{L}^{1}\quad\text{with }g_{h}\coloneqq g_{a,h}+u_{c,h}^{\prime}\quad\text{for all }h>0.

With the convergences
Du_{h}\to Du
and
g_{a,h}-u_{a,h}^{\prime}\to g-u_{a}^{\prime}
in the flat norm (via (
40
)), we infer
\gamma_{h}\to\gamma
in the flat norm.
Since
u_{c,h}^{\prime}
is canceled in the first term, we deduce

\int_{a}^{b}|u_{h}^{\prime}-g_{h}|^{2}\,\textup{d}x+c_{0}\int_{a}^{b}|g_{h}|\,\textup{d}x+2\sum_{x\in J_{u_{h}}}f\left(\frac{1}{2}|[u_{h}](x)|\right)\\
\leq\int_{a}^{b}|u_{a,h}^{\prime}-g_{a,h}|^{2}\,\textup{d}x+c_{0}\int_{a}^{b}\big(|g_{a,h}|+|u_{c,h}^{\prime}|\big)\,\textup{d}x+2\sum_{x\in J_{u_{j,h}}}f\left(\frac{1}{2}|u_{j,h}](x)|\right),

which, via (
37
), (
38
), (
39
) and (
40
), implies

\limsup_{h\to\infty}\bigg[\int_{a}^{b}|u_{h}^{\prime}-g_{h}|^{2}\,\textup{d}x+c_{0}\int_{a}^{b}|g_{h}|\,\textup{d}x+2\sum_{x\in J_{u_{h}}}f\left(\frac{1}{2}|[u_{h}](x)|\right)\bigg]\leq E(u,\gamma).

(41)

This does not yet show (
36
), since
\left\lVert u_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(a,b)}\leq K
might not be satisfied for all
h>0
. We resolve this problem in two steps. With
\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(a,b)}\leq K
and
u_{h}\to u
in
L^{1}(a,b)
, we can fix a sequence
\{\eta_{h}\}_{h}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
with
\eta_{h}\to 0^{+}
as
h\to 0
and

|\{x\in(a,b)\colon|u_{h}(x)|\geq K+\eta_{h}\}|\to 0\quad\text{as }\,h\to 0.

(42)

We next define the truncated versions

\tilde{u}_{h}(x)\coloneqq\min\{\max\{u_{h}(x),-K-\eta_{h}\},K+\eta_{h}\}\quad\text{for all }h>0.

We then have
\tilde{u}_{h}\to u
in
L^{1}(a,b)
,
D\tilde{u}_{h}\to Du
in the flat norm and, in addition, also
\left\lVert\tilde{u}_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(a,b)}\leq K+\eta_{h}
for all
h>0
. Correspondingly, we set

\tilde{\gamma}_{h}\coloneqq D^{j}\tilde{u}_{h}+g_{h}\mathbbm{1}_{\{\tilde{u}_{h}=u_{h}\}}\mathcal{L}^{1}\quad\text{for all }h>0.

By using (
9
) and by applying subsequently the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we get

\displaystyle\left\lVert\tilde{\gamma}_{h}-\gamma_{h}\right\rVert_{\textnormal{flat}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert(\tilde{\gamma}_{h}-D\tilde{u}_{h})-(\gamma_{h}-Du_{h})\right\rVert_{\textnormal{flat}}+\left\lVert D\tilde{u}_{h}-Du_{h}\right\rVert_{\textnormal{flat}}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert(g_{h}-u_{h}^{\prime})\mathbbm{1}_{\{\tilde{u}_{h}\neq u_{h}\}}\mathcal{L}^{1}\right\rVert_{\textnormal{flat}}+\left\lVert D\tilde{u}_{h}-Du_{h}\right\rVert_{\textnormal{flat}}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert(g_{a,h}-u_{a,h}^{\prime})\mathbbm{1}_{\{\tilde{u}_{h}\neq u_{h}\}}\mathcal{L}^{1}\right\rVert_{\textnormal{flat}}+\left\lVert D\tilde{u}_{h}-Du_{h}\right\rVert_{\textnormal{flat}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert(g_{a,h}-u_{a,h}^{\prime})\mathbbm{1}_{\{\tilde{u}_{h}\neq u_{h}\}}\right\rVert_{L^{1}(a,b)}+\left\lVert\tilde{u}_{h}-u_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{1}(a,b)}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert g_{a,h}-u_{a,h}^{\prime}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(a,b)}|\{x\in(a,b)\colon\tilde{u}_{h}\neq u_{h}\}|^{\frac{1}{2}}+\left\lVert\tilde{u}_{h}-u_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{1}(a,b)}.

If we pass to the limit
h\to 0
on the right-hand side, the first term vanishes because of the uniform boundedness of
u_{h}^{\prime}-g_{h}=u_{a,h}^{\prime}-g_{a,h}
in
L^{2}(a,b)
due to (
40
) combined with the convergence

\displaystyle\{x\in(a,b)\colon\tilde{u}_{h}(x)\neq u_{h}\}=\{x\in(a,b)\colon|u_{h}(x)|>K+\eta_{h}\}\to 0\quad\text{as }h\to 0

as a consequence from (
42
). Since with
u_{h}\to u
and
\tilde{u}_{h}\to u
in
L^{1}(a,b)
also the second term vanishes, we conclude that
\tilde{\gamma}_{h}-\gamma_{h}\to 0
in the flat norm. Consequently, we have established
\tilde{\gamma}_{h}\to\gamma
in the flat norm. For
h>0
we finally define

\displaystyle\hat{u}_{h}(x)\coloneqq\frac{K}{K+\eta_{h}}\tilde{u}_{h}\quad\text{and}\quad\hat{\gamma}_{h}(x)\coloneqq\frac{K}{K+\eta_{h}}\tilde{\gamma}_{h}(x)\quad\text{for }x\in(a,b).

We clearly have
\left\lVert\hat{u}_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(a,b)}\leq K
,
J_{u_{h}}\subset J_{\hat{u}_{h}}
and
|[\hat{u}_{h}]|\leq|[u_{h}]|
for all
h>0
. In view of
K/(K+\eta_{h})\to 1
, we also have
\hat{u}_{h}\to u
in
L^{1}(a,b)
and
\hat{\gamma}_{h}\to\gamma
in the flat norm. Moreover, if we denote the density of
\hat{\gamma}_{h}
with respect to
\mathcal{L}^{1}
by
\hat{g}_{h}
, we observe
|\hat{u}_{h}^{\prime}-\hat{g}_{h}|\leq|u_{h}^{\prime}-g_{h}|
and
|\hat{g}_{h}|\leq|g_{h}|
on
(a,b)
for all
h>0
. This shows, that the energy
E(\hat{u}_{h},\hat{\gamma}_{h})
is finite for all
h>0
, with

\displaystyle E(\hat{u}_{h},\hat{\gamma}_{h})

\displaystyle=\int_{a}^{b}|\hat{u}_{h}^{\prime}-\hat{g}_{h}|^{2}\,\textup{d}x+c_{0}\int_{a}^{b}|\hat{g}_{h}|\,\textup{d}x+2\sum_{x\in J_{\hat{u}_{h}}}f\left(\frac{1}{2}|[\hat{u}_{h}](x)|\right)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{a}^{b}|u_{h}^{\prime}-g_{h}|^{2}\,\textup{d}x+c_{0}\int_{a}^{b}|g_{h}|\,\textup{d}x+2\sum_{x\in J_{u_{h}}}f\left(\frac{1}{2}|[u_{h}](x)|\right).

By taking into account (
41
), we then obtain the claim (
36
) (even for the
\limsup
), which ends the proof.
∎

Remark 6.4
.

The function
\hat{u}_{h}
can even be chosen such that
\hat{u}_{h}(x)=u(a+)
on
(a,\delta_{h})
and
\hat{u}_{h}(x)=u(b-)
on
(b-\delta_{h},b)
for a sequence
\delta_{h}\searrow 0
. To see this, note that from
\hat{u}_{h}\to u
a.e. and
\lim_{x\searrow a}u(x)=u(a+)
,
\lim_{x\nearrow b}u(x)=u(b-)
, one finds
\delta_{h}\searrow 0
such that
a+\delta_{h}
and
b-\delta_{h}
are not contained in
J_{u_{h}}
and
\lim_{h\to\infty}u_{h}(a+\delta_{h})=u(a+)
,
\lim_{h\to\infty}u_{h}(b-\delta_{h})=u(b-)
. Now consider
\hat{\hat{u}}_{h}\in SBV^{2}(a,b)
defined by

\hat{\hat{u}}_{h}(x)=\begin{cases}u(a+)&\text{for }x\in(a,a+\delta_{h}],\\
\hat{u}_{h}(x)&\text{for }x\in(a+\delta_{h},b-\delta_{h}),\\
u(b-)&\text{for }x\in[b-\delta_{h},b)\\
\end{cases}

and

\hat{\hat{\gamma}}_{h}=\hat{\gamma}_{h}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}(a+\delta_{h},b-\delta_{h})+\big(u_{h}(a+\delta_{h})-u(a+)\big)\delta_{a+\delta_{h}}+\big(u(b-)-u_{h}(b-\delta_{h})\big)\delta_{b-\delta_{h}}

The claim follows from observing that

Again, the case

Corollary 6.5
.

For every
(u,\gamma)\in L^{0}((a,b);\overline{{\mathbb{R}}})\times\mathcal{M}(a,b)
there is a sequence
\{(u_{\varepsilon},\gamma_{\varepsilon})\}_{\varepsilon}
converging to
(u,\gamma)
in
L^{0}((a,b);\overline{{\mathbb{R}}})\times\mathcal{M}(a,b)
such that

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}E_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon},\gamma_{\varepsilon})\leq E(u,\gamma).

Proof.

Without loss of generality we assume
u\in BV_{\infty,\mathcal{P}}(a,b)
. Let
a=x_{0}<\ldots<x_{m}=b
such that
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}\}=\{x\in(a,b):|[u](x)|=\infty\}
. Note
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{F}(u))}<\infty
. With the help of Theorem
6.3
we choose
\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon},\tilde{g}_{\varepsilon}:(a,b)\to{\mathbb{R}}
such that
\big(\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|_{(x_{i-1},x_{i})},\tilde{g}_{\varepsilon}\mathcal{L}^{1}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}(x_{i-1},x_{i})\big)
is a recovery sequence for
\big(u|_{(x_{i-1},x_{i})},\gamma\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}(x_{i-1},x_{i})\big)
whenever
(x_{i-1},x_{i})\subset\mathcal{F}(u)
and
\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}\equiv\pm\varepsilon^{-1}
,
\tilde{g}_{\varepsilon}\equiv 0
on
(x_{i-1},x_{i})
in case
u\equiv\pm\infty
on
(x_{i-1},x_{i})
.

As in the proof of Proposition
6.1
now define
u_{\varepsilon}\in W^{1,1}(a,b)
by linear interpolation near the
x_{i}
as

\begin{array}[]{lll}&u_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}&\text{on }(a,b)\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{m-1}(x_{i}-\varepsilon^{2}-2\varepsilon,x_{i}+\varepsilon^{2}+2\varepsilon),\\
&u^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq\frac{\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}(x_{i}+\varepsilon^{2}+2\varepsilon)-\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}(x_{i}-\varepsilon^{2}-2\varepsilon)}{2\varepsilon^{2}}&\text{on }(x_{i}-\varepsilon^{2},x_{i}+\varepsilon^{2})\text{ for }i\in\{1,\ldots,m-1\},\\[2.84544pt]
&u^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq 0&\text{otherwise}.\end{array}

Clearly,
u_{\varepsilon}\to u
a.e. on
(a,b)
. Accordingly we define
\gamma_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{M}(a,b)
as
\gamma_{\varepsilon}=g_{\varepsilon}\mathcal{L}^{1}
with

g_{\varepsilon}:=\begin{cases}\tilde{g}_{\varepsilon}&\text{on }(a,b)\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{m-1}(x_{i}-\varepsilon^{2}-2\varepsilon,x_{i}+\varepsilon^{2}+2\varepsilon),\\
u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}&\text{on }(x_{i}-\varepsilon^{2},x_{i}+\varepsilon^{2})\text{ for }i\in\{1,\ldots,m-1\},\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

So still
\gamma_{\varepsilon}\to\gamma
locally in the flat norm on each
(x_{i-1},x_{i})\subset\mathcal{F}(u)
. Estimating the energy as

\displaystyle E_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon},\gamma_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathcal{F}(u)}|\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}-\tilde{g}_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,\textup{d}x+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\mathcal{F}(u)}f\bigg(\varepsilon\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{I_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap(a,b)}{|\tilde{g}_{\varepsilon}|\,\textup{d}t}\bigg)\,\textup{d}x

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\bigcup_{i=1}^{m-1}(x_{i}-\varepsilon^{2}-\varepsilon,x_{i}+\varepsilon^{2}+\varepsilon)}f\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\int_{(x_{i}-\varepsilon^{2},x_{i}+\varepsilon^{2})}|u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|\,\textup{d}t\bigg)\,\textup{d}x,

where the last term on the right-hand side is bounded by

2(1+\varepsilon)(m-1)c_{0}=2(1+\varepsilon)c_{0}\#J_{u}\setminus\mathcal{F}(u)
, we find that indeed

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to\infty}E_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon},\gamma_{\varepsilon})\leq E(u,\gamma).\qed

.

in

\limsup

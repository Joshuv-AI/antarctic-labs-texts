Hausdorff Dimension of Singular Vectors in Function Fields
Noy Soffer Aranov, Taehyeong Kim

Abstract
. We compute the Hausdorff dimension of the set of singular vectors in function fields and bound the Hausdorff dimension of the set of ε \varepsilon -Dirichlet improvable vectors in this setting. This is a function field analogue of the results of Cheung and Chevallier [Duke Math. J. 165 (2016), 2273–2329].

1.
Introduction

A fundamental theorem in Diophantine approximation is Dirichlet’s Theorem which says that for all
\boldsymbol{\theta}=(\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
T>1
there exists
(\mathbf{a},b)=(a_{1},\dots,a_{d},b)\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\mathbb{Z}
such that

\max_{1\leq i\leq d}|b\theta_{i}-a_{i}|<\frac{1}{T^{\frac{1}{d}}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad 0<|b|<T.

It is natural to ask whether Dirichlet’s Theorem can be improved, and this question leads to the following definition. Given
\varepsilon>0
, a vector
\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is said to be
\varepsilon
-Dirichlet improvable
if there is
T_{0}>1
such that for all
T>T_{0}
there exists
(\mathbf{a},b)\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\mathbb{Z}
such that

\max_{1\leq i\leq d}|b\theta_{i}-a_{i}|<\frac{\varepsilon}{T^{\frac{1}{d}}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad 0<|b|<T.

We say that a vector
\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is
singular
if it is
\varepsilon
-Dirichlet improvable for every
\varepsilon>0
. Denote by
\mathbf{DI}_{d}(\varepsilon)
and
\mathbf{Sing}_{d}
the set of
\varepsilon
-Dirichlet improvable vectors and singular vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, respectively.

Khintchine
[
Khi26
]
originally introduced singular vectors and showed that
\mathbf{Sing}_{d}
has Lebesgue measure zero. Davenport and Schmidt
[
DS70
]
proved that
\mathbf{DI}_{d}(\varepsilon)
has Lebesgue measure zero for any
0<\varepsilon<1
. It is worth noting that
\mathbf{Sing}_{1}=\mathbb{Q}
, but if
d\geq 2
, the singular set is nontrivial. The computation of the Hausdorff dimension of
\mathbf{Sing}_{d}
with
d\geq 2
has been a challenge until the first breakthrough by Cheung
[
Che11
]
, who proved that the Hausdorff dimension of
\mathbf{Sing}_{2}
is
4/3
and obtained a certain bound for the Hausdorff dimension of
\mathbf{DI}_{2}(\varepsilon)
. This was extended by Cheung and Chevallier
[
CC16
]
to higher dimensions as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

[
CC16
]

Let
d\geq 2
. Then

(1)

the Hausdorff dimension of
\mathbf{Sing}_{d}
is
\frac{d^{2}}{d+1}
;

(2)

for any
t>d
, there is a constant
C>0
such that for all small enough
\varepsilon>0
,

\frac{d^{2}}{d+1}+\varepsilon^{t}\leq\mathrm{dim}_{H}\mathbf{DI}_{d}(\varepsilon)\leq\frac{d^{2}}{d+1}+C\varepsilon^{\frac{d}{2}}.

Here and hereafter,
\mathrm{dim}_{H}
stands for the Hausdorff dimension.
We remark that this result improves the bounds in
[
Bak77
,
Bak92
,
Ryn90
]
and refer to
[
KKLM17
,
DFSU24
]
for the Hausdorff dimension of singular matrices.

In this paper, we shall prove a function field analogue of Theorem
1.1
. We first introduce the function field setting. Let
p
be a prime number,
q
be a power of
p
, and let
\mathcal{R}=\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
be the ring of polynomials over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Let
\mathcal{K}=\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)
be the field of rational functions over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. We define an absolute value on
\mathcal{R}
by
|f|=q^{\mathrm{deg}(f)}
and extend it to an absolute value on
\mathcal{K}
by
\left|\frac{f}{g}\right|=q^{\mathrm{deg}(f)-\mathrm{deg}(g)}
. Then the topological completion of
\mathcal{K}
with respect to the metric
d(f,g)=|f-g|
is the field of Laurent series
\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}
defined by

\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}\>=\mathbb{F}_{q}\left(\left(x^{-1}\right)\right)=\Bigg\{\sum_{n=-N}^{\infty}a_{n}x^{-n}:a_{n}\in\mathbb{F}_{q},N\in\mathbb{Z}\Bigg\}.

We equip
\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}^{d}
with the supremum norm

\|\mathbf{v}\|=\max_{i=1,\dots,d}|v_{i}|
for
\mathbf{v}=(v_{1},\dots,v_{d})\in\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}
and consider the Hausdorff dimension of subsets of
\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}^{d}
with respect to this supremum norm.

We remark that the ring of polynomials
\mathcal{R}
is discrete in
\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}
and plays a similar role to the ring of integers
\mathbb{Z}
in
\mathbb{R}
.
In this point of view, there have been numerous developments
on Diophantine approximation in the function field setting, see for instance
[
dM70
,
Las00
]
.
In particular, Dirichlet’s Theorem also holds (see e.g.
[
dM70
, Chapter IV]
).
Hence, it makes sense to consider the following definition as in the real case:

Given
\varepsilon>0
, we say that
\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}^{d}
is
\varepsilon
-Dirichlet improvable
if there is
T_{0}>1
such that for all
T>T_{0}
there exists
(\mathbf{a},b)\in\mathcal{R}^{d}\times\mathcal{R}
such that

\|b\boldsymbol{\theta}-\mathbf{a}\|<\frac{\varepsilon}{T^{\frac{1}{d}}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad 0<|b|<T.

We say that a vector
\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}^{d}
is
singular
if it is
\varepsilon
-Dirichlet improvable for every
\varepsilon>0
. Denote by
\mathbf{DI}_{d}(\varepsilon)
and
\mathbf{Sing}_{d}
the set of
\varepsilon
-Dirichlet improvable vectors and singular vectors in
\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}^{d}
, respectively.

Compared to intensive studies in the real setting as mentioned above, there has been less attention to singular sets over function fields. In particular, it was proved in
[
GG17
, Theorem 3.6]
that
\mathbf{DI}_{d}(\varepsilon)
has zero Haar measure on
\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}^{d}
for every
\varepsilon
small enough. Hence,
\mathbf{Sing}_{d}
has also zero Haar measure.
Recently, it has been proved by Bang, the second named author, and Lim
[
BKL24
]
that the Hausdorff dimension of
\mathbf{Sing}_{d}
is at most
\frac{d^{2}}{d+1}
. We prove that their upper bound is sharp, and moreover we bound the Hausdorff dimension of
\mathbf{DI}_{d}(\varepsilon)
.

Theorem 1.2

(Corollaries
5.6
+
6.13
+
6.14
)
.

Let
d\geq 2
. Then

(1)

the Hausdorff dimension of
\mathbf{Sing}_{d}
is
\frac{d^{2}}{d+1}
;

(2)

for any
\varepsilon>0
, we have

\begin{split}&\frac{d^{2}}{d+1}+\frac{d}{d+1}\log_{q}\left(1+\frac{(q-1)^{2}}{q^{2d+\frac{d^{2}}{d-1}}}\left(\frac{q-1}{q}-\varepsilon^{d-1}\right)\varepsilon^{d}\right)\\
&\leq\mathrm{dim}_{H}\mathbf{DI}_{d}(\varepsilon)\leq\frac{d^{2}}{d+1}+\frac{d}{d+1}\log_{q}\left(1+\sqrt{(q-1)^{2}(q+1)^{d}q^{2d}\varepsilon^{d}}\right).\end{split}

Remark 1.3
.

We remark several things about the second argument in Theorem
1.2
.

(1)

Since
\log_{q}(1+y)=y/\log q+o(y)
as
y\to 0
, Theorem
1.2
has the same order of magnitude as in Theorem
1.1
for all small enough
\varepsilon
. (In fact, the lower bound of Theorem
1.2
is slightly better than
\epsilon^{t}
in Theorem
1.1
.)

(2)

Note that the lower bound is increasing with
0<\varepsilon\leq\left(\frac{d(q-1)}{(2d-1)q}\right)^{\frac{1}{d-1}}
and the upper bound is nontrivial for
0<\varepsilon\leq(q+1)^{-1}q^{-2}
, otherwise the upper bound is greater than
d
. Therefore, the nontrivial regions of
\varepsilon
are
\left(0,\left(\frac{d(q-1)}{(2d-1)q}\right)^{\frac{1}{d-1}}\right]
and
(0,(q+1)^{-1}q^{-2}]
for the lower bound and the upper bound, respectively.

(3)

While Theorem
1.1
only works for
\varepsilon
small enough, our result works for relatively large
\varepsilon
. We are able to obtain this stronger result by the ultrametric nature which will be explained at the end of the introduction. In both real and function field cases, it is totally open to determine/bound reasonably the Hausdorff dimension of
\mathbf{DI}_{d}(\varepsilon)
for any
\varepsilon>0
. See
[
KM23
,
KSY22
]
for
\varepsilon<1
sufficiently near
1
.

(4)

We note that in
[
CC16
, Section 7]
, Cheung and Chevallier proved that when
d=2
, the exponent
t
in Theorem
1.1
can be taken to be greater than
1
, instead of greater than
2
. In order to do so, they defined a more refined fractal structure, and used a claim about chord length in balls in
\mathbb{R}^{2}

[
CC16
, Lemma 7.7]
to show that the structure they defined is indeed nested. Since the geometry in the function field setting is non-Euclidean, similar results cannot be achieved immediately and new ideas would be needed.

One may think of the case that the base field is infinite. In this case, there have been several developments in Diophantine approximation as follows: Roth theorem
[
Uch60
]
and Schmidt subspace theorem
[
Rat78
]
over base fields of characteristic zero; a disproof of Littlewood conjecture
[
DL63
]
when the base field is infinite (see also
[
AB07
]
).
However, the polynomial ring over an infinite base field behaves differently from integers. In particular, the number of polynomials within a certain radius is infinite. Since our proof relies heavily on counting polynomials, we cannot handle this case.

We will now discuss a dynamical interpretation of our main theorem. Let
\operatorname{SL}_{d+1}(\widetilde{\mathcal{K}})
be the group of invertible
(d+1)\times(d+1)
matrices
g
over
\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}
with
|\det(g)|=1
and let
\operatorname{SL}_{d+1}(\mathcal{R})
be the subgroup of
\operatorname{SL}_{d+1}(\widetilde{\mathcal{K}})
with entries in
\mathcal{R}
. Then, the homogeneous space
\mathcal{L}_{d+1}=\operatorname{SL}_{d+1}(\widetilde{\mathcal{K}})/\operatorname{SL}_{d+1}(\mathcal{R})
is identified with the space of unimodular lattices in
\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}^{d+1}
under the identification

g\operatorname{SL}_{d+1}(\mathcal{R})\mapsto g\mathcal{R}^{d+1}.

There is a well-known dynamical interpretation of singular vectors. For
\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}^{d}
, consider the lattice
\Delta_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{I}_{d}&\boldsymbol{\theta}\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\mathcal{R}^{d+1}
and the diagonal flow
g_{t}=\operatorname{diag}\{x^{t},\dots,x^{t},x^{-dt}\}
. Then,
\boldsymbol{\theta}
is singular if and only if the orbit
\{g_{t}\Delta_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}:t\in\mathbb{N}\}
is divergent, that is eventually leaves every compact set in
\mathcal{L}_{d+1}
. See for instance
[
BKL24
, Lemma 2.1]
.

This relationship was originally studied by Dani
[
Dan85
]
in the real setting. Divergent trajectories for diagonal flows have been well studied in the real setting (see for example
[
KW05
,
Wei04
,
Wei06
,
Tam19
,
ST23
,
DFSU24
,
KKLM17
]
).

Theorem
1.2
implies the following dynamical result as in
[
CC16
, Corollary 1.2]
.

Corollary 1.4
.

For
d\geq 2
, the Hausdorff dimension of the set

\{\Delta\in\mathcal{L}_{d+1}:\text{ the orbit }\{g_{t}\Delta:t\in\mathbb{N}\}\text{ is divergent}\}

is
\mathrm{dim}_{H}\operatorname{SL}_{d+1}(\widetilde{\mathcal{K}})-\frac{d}{d+1}
.

Proof.

The same proof verbatim of
[
CC16
, Corollary 1.2]
works in our setting. See also the proof of
[
BKL24
, Proposition 6.5]
.
∎

Finally, we discuss how we are able to obtain stronger results than Theorem
1.1
. We basically follow the proof of
[
CC16
]
, but the ultrametric nature enables us to obtain better results. By the ultrametric nature, we mean the following lemma.

Lemma 1.5

(Ultrametric inequality)
.

For every
\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}^{d}
, we have

(1.1)

\|\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{w}\|\leq\max\{\|\mathbf{v}\|,\|\mathbf{w}\|\}.

Moreover, the inequality in (
1.1
) is an equality if
\|\mathbf{v}\|\neq\|\mathbf{w}\|
.

There are two main reasons for the difference between Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
. The first reason is about the characterization of
\varepsilon
-Dirichlet improvability in terms of the length of the shortest vector in the
Farey lattice
given in Definition
4.9
with respect to best approximations. This corresponds to
[
CC16
, Corollary 4.4]
. In the real case, the characterization works up to multiplication by
1/2
and by
2
, but the ultrametric nature allows us to obtain the exact equivalence as in Corollary
4.7
. The second reason comes from Minkowski’s second theorem (Theorem
2.4
). While Minkowski’s second theorem on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
works with
2^{d}/d!
and
2^{d}
multiplications (see
[
CC16
, Equation (M)]
and
[
Cas59
]
), we have the equality over
\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}^{d}
as in Theorem
2.4
. These two reasons ultimately ensure that the fractal structures we build work well for all
0<\varepsilon<1
, unlike
[
CC16
]
.

The article is organized as follows. In
section

2
, we recall some basic estimates in number theory and geometry of numbers which will be used later. In
section

3
, we recall the definition of self-similar structures and some related dimension results given in
[
CC16
]
.
In
section

4
, we characterize
\varepsilon
-Dirichlet improvability in terms of best approximations, define the Farey lattice, and connect it with best approximations. In
section

5
and
section

6
, we define the fractal structures using the Farey lattices and compute the upper and lower bounds of the Hausdorff dimensions of
\mathbf{DI}_{d}(\varepsilon)
and
\mathbf{Sing}_{d}
, respectively.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Basic number theory

In this subsection, we gather some notation and facts about basic number theory in function fields following
[
Ros13
]
.

Recall that
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is a finite field with
q
elements for a prime power
q
and
\mathcal{R}=\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
is the ring of polynomials over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. A non-zero polynomial
f\in\mathcal{R}
is called a
monic polynomial
if the leading coefficient is equal to 1. Monic polynomials play the role of positive integers. For
f,g\in\mathcal{R}
, the
greatest common divisor
of
f
and
g
, denoted
\mathrm{gcd}(f,g)
, is the monic polynomial of largest degree which divides both
f
and
g
. Define the Euler totient function
\phi(f)
of
f\in\mathcal{R}
to be the number of non-zero polynomials of degree less than
\mathrm{deg}(f)
and relatively prime to
f
, that is,

\phi(f)=\#\{g\in\mathcal{R}\setminus\{0\}:\mathrm{deg}(g)<\mathrm{deg}(f)\ \text{ and }\mathrm{gcd}(f,g)=1\}.

Consider the divisor function
D_{1}(f)
of
f\in\mathcal{R}
defined by
D_{1}(f)=\sum_{g|f}|g|
, where
\sum_{g|f}
stands for the summation over all monic divisors of
f
.
We will need the following estimates:

Lemma 2.1
.

[
Ros13
, Proposition 2.7]

For every integer
\ell\geq 1
, we have

\sum_{\mathrm{deg}(f)=\ell}\phi(f)=\frac{(q-1)^{2}}{q}q^{2\ell}.

Proof.

It follows from
[
Ros13
, Proposition 2.7]
that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathrm{deg}(f)=\ell\\
f\ \text{monic}\end{subarray}}\phi(f)=\frac{q-1}{q}q^{2\ell}.

Given monic polynomial
f\in\mathcal{R}
, if
g\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}f
, then
\phi(g)=\phi(f)
. Hence the lemma follows.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

For every integer
\ell\geq 1
, we have

\sum_{\mathrm{deg}(f)=\ell}\phi(f)D_{1}(f)\leq(q-1)q^{3\ell}.

Proof.

Given a non-zero polynomial
f\in\mathcal{R}
, let
f=\alpha P_{1}^{e_{1}}P_{2}^{e_{2}}\cdots P_{t}^{e_{t}}
be its decomposition to irreducible components, where
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
,
P_{i}
are distinct monic irreducible polynomials, and
e_{i}
are non-negative integers.
It follows from
[
Ros13
, Propositions 1.7 and 2.4]
that

\phi(f)=\prod_{i=1}^{t}\left(|P_{i}|^{e_{i}}-|P_{i}|^{e_{i}-1}\right)\quad\text{and}\quad D_{1}(f)=\prod_{i=1}^{t}\frac{|P_{i}|^{e_{i}+1}-1}{|P_{i}|-1}.

Hence, we have

\phi(f)D_{1}(f)=|f|\prod_{i=1}^{t}\frac{|P_{i}|^{e_{i}+1}-1}{|P_{i}|}=|f|^{2}\prod_{i=1}^{t}\left(1-\frac{1}{|P_{i}|^{e_{i}+1}}\right)\leq|f|^{2}.

Therefore,

\sum_{\mathrm{deg}(f)=\ell}\phi(f)D_{1}(f)\leq\sum_{\mathrm{deg}(f)=\ell}q^{2\ell}=(q-1)q^{3\ell}.

∎

2.2.
Geometry of numbers

An important concept in the geometry of numbers is successive minima. Minkowski’s second theorem connects between the successive minima and the determinant/covolume of a lattice. The function field version of this theorem was proved by Mahler
[
Mah41
]
. In order to state it, we define the determinant of a lattice.

Definition 2.3
.

For a lattice
\Lambda=g\mathcal{R}^{d}
in
\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}^{d}
with
g\in\mathrm{GL}_{d}(\widetilde{\mathcal{K}})
, we define its determinant by

\det(\Lambda)=|\det(g)|.

Theorem 2.4

(Minkowski’s second theorem)
.

For any lattice
\Lambda\subseteq\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}^{d}
and
i=1,...d
, let

\lambda_{i}(\Lambda)=\min\{r>0:\text{ there exist }i\text{ linearly independent vectors in }\Lambda\text{ of norm }\leq r\}.

Then,

\prod_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{i}(\Lambda)=\det(\Lambda).

Throughout this paper,
B(\mathbf{x},r)
stands for the closed ball of radius
r>0
centered at
\mathbf{x}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}^{d}
, that is,
B(\mathbf{x},r)=\{\mathbf{y}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}^{d}:\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\|\leq r\}
.
The following lattice point counting result will be used for the upper bound of
\mathrm{dim}_{H}\mathbf{DI}_{d}(\varepsilon)
.

Proposition 2.5
.

[
BK23
, Lemma 6.2]

Let
\Lambda\subseteq\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}^{d}
be a lattice. Then

\#(\Lambda\cap B(0,r))=\prod_{i=1}^{d}\left\lceil\frac{qr}{\lambda_{i}(\Lambda)}\right\rceil.

We shall prove positive characteristic analogues of the results of
[
CC16
, Section 2]
. Firstly, given
X\subseteq\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}^{d}
, we denote the radius of the largest ball disjoint from
X
by

e(X)=\sup\{r>0:\exists\alpha\in\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}^{d}:B(\alpha,r)\cap X=\emptyset\}

Let
\mathcal{O}
be the maximal compact subgroup of
\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}
, that is

\mathcal{O}=\mathbb{F}_{q}\left[\left[x^{-1}\right]\right]=\{f\in\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}:|f|\leq 1\}.

Then, the group of isometries for the
\operatorname{SL}_{d}(\widetilde{\mathcal{K}})
action on
\mathcal{L}_{d}
is
\operatorname{SL}_{d}(\mathcal{O})
.

Lemma 2.6
.

The function
e
is
\operatorname{SL}_{d}(\mathcal{O})
invariant.

Proof.

Denote
r:=e(X)
and let
u\in\operatorname{SL}_{d}(\mathcal{O})
. Assume towards a contradiction that for every
\alpha\in\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}^{d}
,
B(\alpha,r)\cap uX\neq\emptyset
. Let
\mathbf{v}\in B(\alpha,r)\cap uX
. Then,
u^{-1}\mathbf{v}\in X
and

\|u^{-1}\alpha-u^{-1}\mathbf{v}\|=\|\alpha-\mathbf{v}\|\leq r.

Hence, for every
\alpha\in\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}^{d}
,
B(u^{-1}\alpha,r)\cap X\neq\emptyset
, which is a contradiction to the definition of
r
. Thus,
e(uX)\geq e(X)
, and by replacing
u
with
u^{-1}
and
X
with
uX
, we obtain that
e(X)=e(uX)
.
∎

Lemma 2.7
.

For any lattice
\Lambda\subseteq\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}^{d}
,

q^{2}e(\Lambda)=\lambda_{d}(\Lambda)

Proof.

Since
e
is
\operatorname{SL}_{d}(\mathcal{O})
invariant, by
[
Ara24
, Theorem 1.24]
, it suffices to assume that
\Lambda=\mathcal{R}x^{\log_{q}\lambda_{1}(\Lambda)}\mathbf{e}_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathcal{R}x^{\log_{q}\lambda_{d}(\Lambda)}\mathbf{e}_{d}
, where
\mathbf{e}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{e}_{d}
is the canonical basis for
\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}^{d}
. Define
\mathbf{v}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}x^{\log_{q}\lambda_{i}(\Lambda)-1}\mathbf{e}_{i}
. We claim that

B\left(\mathbf{v},\frac{1}{q^{2}}\lambda_{d}(\Lambda)\right)\cap\Lambda=\emptyset.

Assume towards a contradiction that
\mathbf{u}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}a_{i}x^{\log_{q}\lambda_{i}(\Lambda)}\mathbf{e}_{i}\in B\left(\mathbf{v},\frac{1}{q^{2}}\lambda_{d}(\Lambda)\right)\cap\Lambda
, where
a_{1}\dots a_{d}\in\mathcal{R}
. Then,

(2.1)

\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v}\|=\max_{i=1,\dots,d}\lambda_{i}(\Lambda)\left|a_{i}-\frac{1}{x}\right|\leq q^{-2}\lambda_{d}(\Lambda).

In particular, (
2.1
) holds for
i=d
, so that
\left|a_{i}-\frac{1}{x}\right|\leq q^{-2}
, but this cannot hold for
a_{i}\in\mathcal{R}
. Hence,
B\left(\mathbf{v},\frac{1}{q^{2}}\lambda_{d}(\Lambda)\right)\cap\Lambda\neq\emptyset
, so that
e(\Lambda)\geq q^{-2}\lambda_{d}(\Lambda)
.

On the other hand, by
[
Ara24
, Theorem 1.25]
,

\Lambda+B\left(0,\frac{1}{q}\lambda_{d}(\Lambda)\right)=\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}^{d}.

Thus, every ball of radius
\frac{1}{q}\lambda_{d}(\Lambda)
intersects
\Lambda
. Hence,
e(\Lambda)\leq q^{-2}\lambda_{d}(\Lambda)
.
∎

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
L\subseteq\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}^{d}
, let
L^{\prime}\subset L
be a sublattice of dimension
d-1
with minimal volume and let
H^{\prime}=\operatorname{span}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}}(L^{\prime})
. Then,
q^{2}e(L+H^{\prime})=\lambda_{d}(L)
.

Proof.

Since
e
is
\operatorname{SL}_{d}(\mathcal{O})
invariant, then, by
[
Ara24
, Theorem 1.24]
, it suffices to assume that
L=\mathcal{R}x^{\log_{q}\lambda_{1}(L)}\mathbf{e}_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathcal{R}x^{\log_{q}\lambda_{d}(L)}\mathbf{e}_{d}
.
In this case, Theorem
2.4
implies that

H^{\prime}=\operatorname{span}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}}\big\{x^{\log_{q}\lambda_{1}(L)}\mathbf{e}_{1},\dots,x^{\log_{q}\lambda_{d-1}(L)}\mathbf{e}_{d-1}\big\}.

Thus,

L+H^{\prime}=x^{\log_{q}\lambda_{d}(L)}\mathbf{e}_{d}+H^{\prime}.

If
\mathbf{v}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}a_{i}x^{\log_{q}\lambda_{d}(L)}\mathbf{e}_{i}\in B\left(x^{\log_{q}\lambda_{d}(L)-1},\frac{1}{q^{2}}\lambda_{d}(L)\right)\cap(L+H^{\prime})
, where
a_{d}\in\mathcal{R}
, then,

\lambda_{d}(L)\left|a_{d}-x^{-1}\right|=\left|a_{d}x^{\log_{q}\lambda_{d}(L)}-x^{\log_{q}\lambda_{d}(L)-1}\right|\leq\frac{1}{q^{2}}\lambda_{d}(L).

Thus,
\left|a_{d}-x^{-1}\right|\leq q^{-2}
, but this cannot hold for
a_{d}\in\mathcal{R}
. Hence,

B\left(x^{\log_{q}\lambda_{d}(L)}\mathbf{e}_{d},\frac{1}{q^{2}}\lambda_{d}(L)\right)\cap(L+H^{\prime})=\emptyset,

so that
e(L+H^{\prime})\geq\frac{1}{q^{2}}\lambda_{d}(L)
. On the other hand, since
L\subseteq L+H^{\prime}
, then,
e(L+H^{\prime})\leq e(L)=\frac{1}{q^{2}}\lambda_{d}(L)
. Hence,
e(L+H^{\prime})=\frac{1}{q^{2}}\lambda_{d}(L)
.
∎

3.
Self-similar coverings

In this section, we recall the definition of self-similar structures and some related dimension results following
[
Che07
,
CC16
]
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
X
be a metric space with a metric
d_{X}
. A
self-similar structure
on
X
is a triple
(J,\sigma,B)
, where
J
is countable,
\sigma\subseteq J\times J
, and
B
is a map from
J
into the set of bounded subsets of
X
.
For
\alpha\in J
, we denote

\sigma(\alpha)=\{\beta\in J:(\alpha,\beta)\in\sigma\}.

We say that a sequence
\{\alpha_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq J
is
\sigma
-admissible
if for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
\alpha_{k+1}\in\sigma(\alpha_{k})
. For a bounded subset
E
of
X
, we denote
\operatorname{diam}(E)=\sup\{d_{X}(y,z):y,z\in E\}
.

For a subset
S
of
X
, a
self-similar covering
of
S
is a self-similar structure
(J,\sigma,B)
such that for every
\theta\in S
, there exists a
\sigma
-admissible sequence
\{\alpha_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
in
J
satisfying

•

\lim_{k\to\infty}\operatorname{diam}B(\alpha_{k})=0
;

•

\bigcap_{k\in\mathbb{N}}B(\alpha_{k})=\{\theta\}
.

In this case, we say that the self-similar structure
(J,\sigma,B)
covers the subset
S
.

We call a triple
(J,\sigma,B)
a
strictly nested self-similar structure
on
X
if it is a self-similar structure on
X
satisfying

•

for all
\alpha\in J
,
\sigma(\alpha)
is finite,
B(\alpha)
is a nonempty compact subset of
X
, and for all
\beta\in\sigma(\alpha)
we have
B(\beta)\subset B(\alpha)
;

•

for all
\sigma
-admissible sequences
(\alpha_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
,
\lim_{k\to\infty}\operatorname{diam}B(\alpha_{k})=0
;

•

for all
\alpha\in J
and each
\beta\in\sigma(\alpha)
,
\operatorname{diam}B(\beta)<\operatorname{diam}B(\alpha)
.

Theorem 3.2
.

[
Che07
, Theorem 5.3]

Let
X
be a metric space,
S
be a subset of
X
,
(J,\sigma,B)
be a self-similar covering of
S
, and
s>0
. If for every
\alpha\in J
,

\sum_{\beta\in\sigma(\alpha)}\operatorname{diam}(B(\beta))^{s}\leq\operatorname{diam}(B(\alpha))^{s},

then
\mathrm{dim}_{H}(S)\leq s
.

Theorem 3.3
.

[
CC16
, Theorem 3.4]

Let
X
be a metric space and
S
be a subset of
X
. Suppose that there is a strictly nested self-similar structure
(J,\sigma,B)
that covers a subset of
S
and constants
c,s\geq 0
and
\rho\in(0,1)
such that

(1)

for all
\alpha\in J
and all
\beta\in\sigma(\alpha)
, there are at most
c
points
\gamma
in
\sigma(\alpha)\setminus\{\beta\}
such that

d_{X}\left(B(\beta),B(\gamma)\right)\leq\rho\operatorname{diam}B(\alpha);

(2)

for all
\alpha\in J,

\sum_{\beta\in\sigma(\alpha)}\operatorname{diam}B(\beta)^{s}\geq(c+1)\operatorname{diam}B(\alpha)^{s}.

Then
S
contains a subset of positive
s
-dimensional Hausdorff measure.

Theorem 3.4
.

[
CC16
, Theorem 3.6]

Let
X
be a metric space and
S
be a subset of
X
. Suppose that there is a strictly nested self-similar structure
(J,\sigma,B)
that covers a subset of
S
, a subset
J_{0}\subset J
that contains a tail of any
\sigma
-admissible sequence, a function
\rho:J\to(0,1)
, and two constants
c,s\geq 0
such that

(1)

for all
\alpha\in J_{0}
and all
\beta\in\sigma(\alpha)
, there are at most
c
points
\gamma
in
\sigma(\alpha)\setminus\{\beta\}
such that

d_{X}(B(\beta),B(\gamma))\leq\rho(\alpha)\operatorname{diam}B(\alpha);

(2)

for all
\alpha\in J_{0}
,

\sum_{\beta\in\sigma(\alpha)}\left(\rho(\beta)\operatorname{diam}B(\beta)\right)^{s}\geq(c+1)\left(\rho(\alpha)\operatorname{diam}B(\alpha)\right)^{s};

(3)

for all
\alpha\in J_{0}
and all
\beta\in\sigma(\alpha)
,
\rho(\beta)\operatorname{diam}B(\beta)<\rho(\alpha)\operatorname{diam}B(\alpha)
.

Then
S
contains a subset of positive
s
-dimensional Hausdorff measure.

4.
Best approximation vectors, orthogonality, and Farey lattices

In this section, we recall the definition of best approximation vectors and prove several properties of best approximations. Then, we introduce orthogonality and Farey lattices over function fields and prove some related properties. In particular, we are able to obtain stronger properties than those in the real case due to Lemma
1.5
.

4.1.
Best approximation vectors

We denote

Q=\{(a_{1},\dots,a_{d},b)\in\mathcal{R}^{d+1}:\mathrm{gcd}(a_{1},\dots,a_{d},b)=1,b\neq 0\}.

For
\mathbf{u}=(a,b)=(a_{1},\dots,a_{d},b)\in Q
, we denote
\hat{\mathbf{u}}=\frac{a}{b}
and
|\mathbf{u}|=|b|
. Given
\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}^{d}
and
\mathbf{u}\in Q
, we denote
A(\boldsymbol{\theta},\mathbf{u})=\|b\boldsymbol{\theta}-a\|
.

Definition 4.1
.

We say that
\mathbf{u}=(a,b)\in Q
is a
best approximation of
\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}^{d}
if the following conditions hold.

(1)

For every
\mathbf{v}\in Q
with
|\mathbf{v}|<|\mathbf{u}|
,
A(\boldsymbol{\theta},\mathbf{u})<A(\boldsymbol{\theta},\mathbf{v})
;

(2)

For every
\mathbf{v}\in Q
with
|\mathbf{v}|\leq|\mathbf{u}|
,
A(\boldsymbol{\theta},\mathbf{u})\leq A(\boldsymbol{\theta},\mathbf{v})
.

For each
\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}^{d}\setminus\mathcal{K}^{d}
, we associate
a sequence
\{\mathbf{u}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of best approximation vectors for
\boldsymbol{\theta}
with the following properties:

(1)

|\mathbf{u}_{1}|=1
;

(2)

|\mathbf{u}_{n}|<|\mathbf{u}_{n+1}|
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
;

(3)

there is no best approximation
\mathbf{u}\in Q
of
\boldsymbol{\theta}
with
|\mathbf{u}_{n}|<|\mathbf{u}|<|\mathbf{u}_{n+1}|
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Remark 4.2
.

See
[
BZ19
,
KLP23
]
for the existence of a sequence of best approximation vectors for
\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}^{d}\setminus\mathcal{K}^{d}
.
We note that multiplying by
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
does not effect the norm of a vector. Thus, if
\mathbf{u}=(a,b)\in Q
is a best approximation of
\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}^{d}
, then every element of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}\mathbf{u}
is also a best approximation. Furthermore,
b
is unique up to
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
-multiples in the following sense. If
\mathbf{v}=(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})\in Q
satisfies
|b|=|b^{\prime}|
and
b^{\prime}\notin\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}b
, then
\mathbf{v}
is not a best approximation of
\boldsymbol{\theta}
. To see this, assume that
\mathbf{v}
is a best approximation of
\boldsymbol{\theta}
. Note that there exists some element
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
such that
0<|b-\alpha b^{\prime}|<|b|
. Since
\mathbf{u}
and
\alpha\mathbf{v}
are best approximations of
\boldsymbol{\theta}
with
|\mathbf{u}|=|\alpha\mathbf{v}|
, we have
A(\boldsymbol{\theta},\mathbf{u})=A(\boldsymbol{\theta},\alpha\mathbf{v})
, hence,

\|(b-\alpha b^{\prime})\boldsymbol{\theta}-(a-\alpha a^{\prime})\|\leq\max\{A(\boldsymbol{\theta},\mathbf{u}),A(\boldsymbol{\theta},\alpha\mathbf{v})\}=A(\boldsymbol{\theta},\mathbf{u}).

This is a contradiction to the definition of a best approximation.

We note that by Definition
4.1
(
1
), if
\{\mathbf{u}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of best approximation vectors, then, the sequence
\{A(\boldsymbol{\theta},\mathbf{u}_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is strictly decreasing.

Lemma 4.3
.

Assume that
\mathbf{u}\in Q
is a best approximation of
\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}^{d}
. Then, for every
\mathbf{v}\in Q
such that
|\mathbf{v}|\leq|\mathbf{u}|
and
\mathbf{v}\notin\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}\mathbf{u}
, we have

A(\boldsymbol{\theta},\mathbf{v})=A(\hat{\mathbf{u}},\mathbf{v}).

Proof.

Write
\mathbf{u}=(a,b)
and
\mathbf{v}=(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})
.
Since
|b^{\prime}|\leq|b|
, by Lemma
1.5
it follows that

(4.1)

\begin{split}A(\boldsymbol{\theta},\mathbf{v})&=\|b^{\prime}\boldsymbol{\theta}-a^{\prime}\|=|b^{\prime}|\Bigg\|\boldsymbol{\theta}-\frac{a^{\prime}}{b^{\prime}}\Bigg\|\leq|b^{\prime}|\cdot\max\Bigg\{\bigg\|\boldsymbol{\theta}-\frac{a}{b}\bigg\|,\bigg\|\frac{a^{\prime}}{b^{\prime}}-\frac{a}{b}\bigg\|\Bigg\}\\
&\leq\max\Bigg\{\|b\boldsymbol{\theta}-a\|,\bigg\|b^{\prime}\frac{a}{b}-a^{\prime}\bigg\|\Bigg\}=\max\{A(\boldsymbol{\theta},\mathbf{u}),A(\hat{\mathbf{u}},\mathbf{v})\}.\end{split}

By Remark
4.2
, since
b
is unique up to
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
-multiples, if
b^{\prime}\notin\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}b
, then we have

A(\boldsymbol{\theta},\mathbf{u})<A(\boldsymbol{\theta},\mathbf{v})
. We note that
b
is unique as soon as the value
A(\boldsymbol{\theta},\mathbf{u})
is fixed. Thus, the right hand side of (
4.1
) is equal to
A(\hat{\mathbf{u}},\mathbf{v})
, that is,
A(\boldsymbol{\theta},\mathbf{v})\leq A(\hat{\mathbf{u}},\mathbf{v})
. Suppose that
b^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}b
and we may assume that
b^{\prime}=b
and
A(\boldsymbol{\theta},\mathbf{u})=A(\boldsymbol{\theta},\mathbf{v})
.
Since
\mathbf{v}\notin\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}\mathbf{u}
and
A(\boldsymbol{\theta},\mathbf{u})\leq 1
, it follows that
A(\hat{\mathbf{u}},\mathbf{v})=\|a-a^{\prime}\|\geq 1\geq A(\boldsymbol{\theta},\mathbf{v})
.

On the other hand, by Lemma
1.5
,

\begin{split}A(\hat{\mathbf{u}},\mathbf{v})&=\Bigg\|b^{\prime}\frac{a}{b}-a^{\prime}\Bigg\|\leq\max\Bigg\{A(\boldsymbol{\theta},\mathbf{v}),\left|\frac{b^{\prime}}{b}\right|\|b\boldsymbol{\theta}-a\|\Bigg\}\\
&\leq\max\{A(\boldsymbol{\theta},\mathbf{v}),A(\boldsymbol{\theta},\mathbf{u})\}=A(\boldsymbol{\theta},\mathbf{v}).\end{split}

Therefore, we have
A(\hat{\mathbf{u}},\mathbf{v})=A(\boldsymbol{\theta},\mathbf{v})
.
∎

Definition 4.4
.

For
\mathbf{u}\in Q
and
r>0
, denote

\mathcal{B}(\mathbf{u},r)=\{\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}^{d}:A(\boldsymbol{\theta},\mathbf{u})<r\}\quad\text{and}\quad\bar{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbf{u},r)=\{\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}^{d}:A(\boldsymbol{\theta},\mathbf{u})\leq r\}.

For
\mathbf{v}\in Q
, define

\Delta_{\mathbf{v}}(\mathbf{u})=\{\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}^{d}:A(\boldsymbol{\theta},\mathbf{u})<A(\boldsymbol{\theta},\mathbf{v})\}\quad\text{and}\quad\bar{\Delta}_{\mathbf{v}}(\mathbf{u})=\{\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}^{d}:A(\boldsymbol{\theta},\mathbf{u})\leq A(\boldsymbol{\theta},\mathbf{v})\}.

Then,
\mathbf{u}
is a best approximation vector for
\boldsymbol{\theta}
if and only if
\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\Delta(\mathbf{u})
, where

\Delta(\mathbf{u})=\left(\bigcap_{|\mathbf{v}|<|\mathbf{u}|}\Delta_{\mathbf{v}}(\mathbf{u})\right)\cap\left(\bigcap_{|\mathbf{v}|=|\mathbf{u}|}\bar{\Delta}_{\mathbf{v}}(\mathbf{u})\right).

In a view of Lemma
4.3
, we define

r(\mathbf{u})=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{v}\in Q:|\mathbf{v}|\leq|\mathbf{u}|,\\
\mathbf{v}\notin\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}\mathbf{u}\end{subarray}}A(\hat{\mathbf{u}},\mathbf{v}).

Lemma 4.5
.

For
\mathbf{u}\in Q
,
we have

\mathcal{B}(\mathbf{u},r(\mathbf{u}))\subseteq\Delta(\mathbf{u})\subseteq\bar{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbf{u},r(\mathbf{u})).

Proof.

If
\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\Delta(\mathbf{u})
, that is,
\mathbf{u}
is a best approximation vector for
\boldsymbol{\theta}
,
then it follows from Lemma
4.3
that for every
\mathbf{v}\in Q
with
|\mathbf{v}|\leq|\mathbf{u}|
and
\mathbf{v}\notin\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}\mathbf{u}
,
we have

A(\boldsymbol{\theta},\mathbf{u})\leq A(\boldsymbol{\theta},\mathbf{v})=A(\hat{\mathbf{u}},\mathbf{v}).

Hence,
\Delta(\mathbf{u})\subseteq\bar{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbf{u},r(\mathbf{u}))
.

If
\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbf{u},r(\mathbf{u}))
,
then we have
A(\boldsymbol{\theta},\mathbf{u})<A(\hat{\mathbf{u}},\mathbf{v})

for every
\mathbf{v}\in Q
with
|\mathbf{v}|\leq|\mathbf{u}|
and
\mathbf{v}\notin\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}\mathbf{u}
.
It follows that for every such
\mathbf{v}
,

A(\boldsymbol{\theta},\mathbf{u})<A(\hat{\mathbf{u}},\mathbf{v})=|\mathbf{v}|\|\hat{\mathbf{u}}-\hat{\mathbf{v}}\|\leq|\mathbf{v}|\max\left\{\|\boldsymbol{\theta}-\hat{\mathbf{u}}\|,\|\boldsymbol{\theta}-\hat{\mathbf{v}}\|\right\}\leq\max\{A(\boldsymbol{\theta},\mathbf{u}),A(\boldsymbol{\theta},\mathbf{v})\}.

Hence,
A(\boldsymbol{\theta},\mathbf{u})<A(\boldsymbol{\theta},\mathbf{v})
, that is,
\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\Delta_{\mathbf{v}}(\mathbf{u})
for every such
\mathbf{v}
.
Since
A(\boldsymbol{\theta},\mathbf{u})=A(\boldsymbol{\theta},\mathbf{v})
for every
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}\mathbf{u}
, we finally have

\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\Delta(\mathbf{u})
.
∎

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}^{d}\setminus\mathcal{K}^{d}
and let
\{\mathbf{u}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of best approximation vectors for
\boldsymbol{\theta}
. Then, for every
i,j\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

A(\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{i+j},\mathbf{u}_{i})=A(\boldsymbol{\theta},\mathbf{u}_{i})=r(\mathbf{u}_{i+1}).

Proof.

For the first equality, observe that by Lemma
4.3
,
A(\boldsymbol{\theta},\mathbf{v})=A(\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{i+j},\mathbf{v})
for every
\mathbf{v}\in Q
with
|\mathbf{v}|\leq|\mathbf{u}_{i+j}|
and
\mathbf{v}\notin\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}\mathbf{u}_{i+j}
. In particular, this holds for
\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{u}_{i}
, which gives rise to the first equality.

For the second part, by Lemma
4.3
, we have

r(\mathbf{u}_{i+1})=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{v}\in Q:|\mathbf{v}|\leq|\mathbf{u}_{i+1}|,\\
\mathbf{v}\notin\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}\mathbf{u}_{i+1}\end{subarray}}A(\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{i+1},\mathbf{v})=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{v}\in Q:|\mathbf{v}|\leq|\mathbf{u}_{i+1}|,\\
\mathbf{v}\notin\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}\mathbf{u}_{i+1}\\
\end{subarray}}A(\boldsymbol{\theta},\mathbf{v}).

Then it is clear that
r(\mathbf{u}_{i+1})\leq A(\boldsymbol{\theta},\mathbf{u}_{i})
.
Denoting
\mathbf{u}_{i+1}=(a,b)
and using Remark
4.2
,
it follows from the definition of best approximations that
A(\boldsymbol{\theta},\mathbf{v})\geq A(\boldsymbol{\theta},\mathbf{u}_{i})
for every
\mathbf{v}=(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})\in Q
with
|\mathbf{v}|\leq|\mathbf{u}_{i+1}|
and
b^{\prime}\notin\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}b
.
For every
\mathbf{v}=(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})\in Q
with
b^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}b
but
\mathbf{v}\notin\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}\mathbf{u}_{i+1}
, we have
A(\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{i+1},\mathbf{v})\geq 1
.
Hence, we have the second equality of the lemma.
∎

Corollary 4.7
.

Let
\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}^{d}\setminus\mathcal{K}^{d}
and let
\{\mathbf{u}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be the sequence of best approximation vectors for
\boldsymbol{\theta}
. Then

\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\mathbf{DI}_{d}(\varepsilon)
if and only if
|\mathbf{u}_{n}|^{1/d}r(\mathbf{u}_{n})<\varepsilon
for all large enough
n\geq 1
.
In particular,
\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\mathbf{Sing}_{d}
if and only if
|\mathbf{u}_{n}|^{1/d}r(\mathbf{u}_{n})\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.

Proof.

Write
\mathbf{u}_{n}=(a_{n},b_{n})
. It is clear that

\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\mathbf{DI}_{d}(\varepsilon)
if and only if
|b_{n}|^{1/d}\|b_{n-1}\boldsymbol{\theta}-a_{n-1}\|<\varepsilon
for all large enough
n\geq 2
. Hence, the corollary follows from Lemma
4.6
.
∎

4.2.
Orthogonality and Farey lattice

One of the main differences from the real case is orthogonality. We use the following definition of orthogonality from
[
KST16
, Lemma 3.3]
.

Definition 4.8
.

A subset
\{\mathbf{x}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{k}\}
of
\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}^{d}
is said to be
orthogonal
if

\|r_{1}\mathbf{x}_{1}+\cdots+r_{k}\mathbf{x}_{k}\|=\max\{|r_{1}|\|\mathbf{x}_{1}\|,\dots,|r_{k}|\|\mathbf{x}_{k}\|\}

for any
r_{1},\dots,r_{k}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}
.

By the Hadamard inequality
[
RW17
, Lemma 2.4]
and Theorem
2.4
, any lattice
\Lambda
in
\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}^{d}
admits an orthogonal basis
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{d}
of the lattice
\Lambda
with
\|\xi_{i}\|=\lambda_{i}(\Lambda)
for each
i=1,\dots,d
. This corresponds to a “Minkowski reduced basis” in the real case.

Definition 4.9
.

For
\mathbf{u}\in Q
, we define the
Farey lattice

\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}}
in
\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}^{d}
by

\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}}=\operatorname{span}_{\mathcal{R}}\{\mathbf{e}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{e}_{d},\hat{\mathbf{u}}\}=\pi_{\mathbf{u}}(\mathcal{R}^{d+1}),

where
\pi_{\mathbf{u}}(a,b)=b\hat{\mathbf{u}}-a
, where
(a,b)\in\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}^{d}\times\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}
.
Note that by Lemma
4.10
,
\det(\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}})=|\mathbf{u}|^{-1}
. We define

\hat{\lambda}_{i}(\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}})=|\mathbf{u}|^{\frac{1}{d}}\lambda_{i}(\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}})\quad\text{for every }i=1,\dots,d,

and we say that
\hat{\lambda}_{i}
are the
normalized successive minima
of
\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}}
.

For
\mathbf{u}\in Q
, let
\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}}^{\prime}
be the
d-1
dimensional sublattice of
\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}}
of minimal covolume, and denote
H_{\mathbf{u}}^{\prime}=\operatorname{span}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}}(\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}}^{\prime})
and
H_{\mathbf{u}}=\pi_{\mathbf{u}}^{-1}(H_{\mathbf{u}}^{\prime})
. It can be easily checked that
\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}}^{\prime}=\mathrm{span}_{\mathcal{R}}\{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{d-1}\}
and
H_{\mathbf{u}}^{\prime}=\mathrm{span}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}}\{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{d-1}\}
.

One of the reasons we consider the Farey lattice is because
\lambda_{1}(\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}})
is indeed equal to
r(\mathbf{u})
in Definition
4.4
.

Lemma 4.10
.

For
\mathbf{u}\in Q
, we have
\det(\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}})=|\mathbf{u}|^{-1}
and
\lambda_{1}(\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}})=r(\mathbf{u}).

Proof.

In order to prove the first equality, let
\mathbf{u}=(a,b)
and consider the group homomorphism
\varphi:\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}}\to\mathcal{R}/b\mathcal{R}
given by

\varphi\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}c_{i}\mathbf{e}_{i}+c_{d+1}\hat{\mathbf{u}}\right)=c_{d+1}+b\mathcal{R}.

Since
\mathrm{gcd}(a,b)=1
, the map
\varphi
is well-defined. Since
\varphi
is surjective and
\mathrm{Ker}(\varphi)=\mathcal{R}^{d}
, we have
[\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}}:\mathcal{R}^{d}]=\#\mathcal{R}/b\mathcal{R}=|b|
, where
[\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}}:\mathcal{R}^{d}]
denotes the index of the subgroup
\mathcal{R}^{d}
in
\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}}
. Hence, it follows from
\det(\mathcal{R}^{d})=1
that
\det(\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}})=|b|^{-1}=|\mathbf{u}|^{-1}
.

Now, let us prove the second equality. First, observe that

\lambda_{1}(\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}})=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}(a,b)\in\mathcal{R}^{d}\times\mathcal{R}\\
b\hat{\mathbf{u}}-a\neq 0\end{subarray}}\|b\hat{\mathbf{u}}-a\|=\min_{\mathbf{v}\in Q,\mathbf{v}\notin\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}\mathbf{u}}A(\hat{\mathbf{u}},\mathbf{v})\leq r(\mathbf{u}).

If
\lambda_{1}(\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}})=A(\hat{\mathbf{u}},\mathbf{v})
for some
\mathbf{v}\in Q
with
|\mathbf{v}|>|\mathbf{u}|
, then writing
\mathbf{u}=(a,b),\mathbf{v}=(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})
and using Euclidean division of polynomials, we have
b^{\prime}=kb+r
for some
k,r\in\mathcal{R}
with
0<|r|<|b|
. Since
A(\hat{\mathbf{u}},\mathbf{v})=A(\hat{\mathbf{u}},\mathbf{v}-k\mathbf{u})
and
0<|\mathbf{v}-k\mathbf{u}|<|\mathbf{u}|
, we have
\lambda_{1}(\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}})\geq r(\mathbf{u})
.
∎

The following two lemmas are a function field analogue of the results of
[
CC16
, Section 5.2]
for the geometry associated with the subspace
H_{\mathbf{u}}
.

Lemma 4.11
.

Let
\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\in Q
. Then,
\mathbf{v}\in H_{\mathbf{u}}
if and only if
\hat{\mathbf{v}}\in\hat{\mathbf{u}}+H_{\mathbf{u}}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Notice that
\mathbf{v}\in H_{\mathbf{u}}
if and only if
\pi_{\mathbf{u}}(\mathbf{v})\in H_{\mathbf{u}}^{\prime}
. Hence, if we write
\mathbf{v}=(a,b)
, then it is equivalent to
b\hat{\mathbf{u}}-a\in H_{\mathbf{u}}^{\prime}
. Hence,
b^{-1}\pi_{\mathbf{u}}(\mathbf{v})=\hat{\mathbf{u}}-\hat{\mathbf{v}}\in H_{\mathbf{u}}^{\prime}
. Thus, it is equivalent to
\hat{\mathbf{v}}\in\hat{\mathbf{u}}+H_{\mathbf{u}}^{\prime}
.
∎

Lemma 4.12
.

Let
\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\in Q
be such that
\mathbf{v}\in H_{\mathbf{u}}
and
\|\hat{\mathbf{u}}-\hat{\mathbf{v}}\|\leq\frac{\lambda_{1}(\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}})}{|\mathbf{u}|}
, and let
\alpha\in\Lambda_{\mathbf{v}}\setminus H_{\mathbf{u}}^{\prime}
. Then we have
\|\alpha\|\geq\lambda_{d}(\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}})\geq q^{-2}\lambda_{d}(\Lambda_{\mathbf{v}})
.

Proof.

Since
\mathbf{v}\in H_{\mathbf{u}}
, by Lemma
4.11
, we have
\hat{\mathbf{v}}-\hat{\mathbf{u}}\in H_{\mathbf{u}}^{\prime}
. Hence,
\Lambda_{\mathbf{v}}\subseteq\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}}+\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}(\hat{\mathbf{v}}-\hat{\mathbf{u}})\subseteq\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}}+H_{\mathbf{u}}^{\prime}
.
Let
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{d}
be an orthogonal basis of
\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}}
with
\|\xi_{i}\|=\lambda_{i}(\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}})
for each
i=1,\dots,d
.
Then we have
\alpha\in(\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}}+H_{\mathbf{u}}^{\prime})\setminus H_{\mathbf{u}}^{\prime}=(\mathcal{R}\setminus\{0\})\xi_{d}+H_{\mathbf{u}}^{\prime}
. Since
H_{\mathbf{u}}^{\prime}=\mathrm{span}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}}\{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{d-1}\}
, it follows from the orthogonality of
\{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{d}\}
that
\|\alpha\|\geq\lambda_{d}(\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}})
.

We now claim that
e(\Lambda_{\mathbf{v}})\leq q^{2}e(\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}})
. Then, by Lemma
2.7
, we have

\|\alpha\|\geq\lambda_{d}(\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}})=q^{2}e(\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}})\geq e(\Lambda_{\mathbf{v}})=q^{-2}\lambda_{d}(\Lambda_{\mathbf{v}}),

which completes the proof.

Observe that by definition of
e(\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}})
, for any
\beta\in\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}^{d}
,
B(\beta,q^{2}e(\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}}))\cap\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}}\neq\emptyset
. Thus, there exist
(a,b)\in\mathcal{R}^{d}\times\mathcal{R}
with
|b|\leq|\mathbf{u}|
, such that
\|\beta-(b\hat{\mathbf{u}}+a)\|\leq q^{2}e(\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}})
.
Since
\|\hat{\mathbf{u}}-\hat{\mathbf{v}}\|\leq\frac{\lambda_{1}(\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}})}{|\mathbf{u}|}
and
q^{2}e(\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}})=\lambda_{d}(\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}})\geq\lambda_{1}(\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}})
, we have

\|\beta-(b\hat{\mathbf{v}}+a)\|\leq\max\{\|\beta-(b\hat{\mathbf{u}}+a)\|,\|b(\hat{\mathbf{u}}-\hat{\mathbf{v}})\|\}\leq\max\{q^{2}e(\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}}),\lambda_{1}(\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}})\}=q^{2}e(\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}}).

Hence,
e(\Lambda_{\mathbf{v}})\leq q^{2}e(\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}})
, which completes the proof.
∎

We will often consider inverse images of the map
\pi_{\mathbf{u}}
of lattice vectors in
\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}}
. Therefore, we will need the following lemma.

Lemma 4.13
.

Let
\mathbf{u}\in Q
,
\alpha\in\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}}
, and
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. Then we have

\#\{\mathbf{v}\in\pi^{-1}_{\mathbf{u}}(\alpha)\cap Q:|\mathbf{v}|=q^{k}|\mathbf{u}|\}\leq(q-1)q^{k}.

Moreover, if
\alpha\in\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}}
is primitive, then

\#\{\mathbf{v}\in\pi^{-1}_{\mathbf{u}}(\alpha)\cap Q:|\mathbf{v}|=q^{k}|\mathbf{u}|\}=(q-1)q^{k}.

Proof.

Denote
\mathbf{u}=(a_{0},b_{0})
. Using Euclidean division of polynomials, we can choose
(a_{1},b_{1})\in\pi_{\mathbf{u}}^{-1}(\alpha)\cap\mathcal{R}^{d+1}
with
|b_{1}|<|b_{0}|
.
We first claim that

\{\mathbf{v}\in\pi_{\mathbf{u}}^{-1}(\alpha)\cap\mathcal{R}^{d+1}:|\mathbf{v}|=q^{k}|\mathbf{u}|\}=\big\{(a_{1}+a_{0}s,b_{1}+b_{0}s):s\in\mathcal{R}\text{ with }|s|=q^{k}\big\}.

It is clear that the right hand side is contained in the left hand side. For
\mathbf{v}=(a,b)\in\mathcal{R}^{d+1}
with
\pi_{\mathbf{u}}(\mathbf{v})=\alpha
and
|\mathbf{v}|=q^{k}|\mathbf{u}|
, we have
\alpha=b\frac{a_{0}}{b_{0}}-a=b_{1}\frac{a_{0}}{b_{0}}-a_{1}
.
Thus,
(b-b_{1})\frac{a_{0}}{b_{0}}=a-a_{1}
.
Since
\mathrm{gcd}(a_{0},b_{0})=1
, it follows that
b_{0}
divides
b-b_{1}
. Hence, there exists some
s\in\mathcal{R}
such that
b=b_{1}+b_{0}s
. Thus,
a=a_{1}+a_{0}s
. Since
|b_{1}|<|b_{0}|
, we have

q^{k}|\mathbf{u}|=|\mathbf{v}|=|b|=|b-b_{1}|=|b_{0}s|=|s||\mathbf{u}|,

which completes the proof of the claim.

Since
\#\{s\in\mathcal{R}:|s|=q^{k}\}=(q-1)q^{k}
, the first argument of the lemma follows from this claim. For the second argument, it is enough to show that if
\alpha\in\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}}
is primitive, then
(a_{1}+a_{0}s,b_{1}+b_{0}s)\in Q
for any
s\in\mathcal{R}
with
|s|=q^{k}
. Indeed, if not,
(a_{1}+a_{0}s,b_{1}+b_{0}s)=s_{0}\mathbf{w}
for some
s_{0}\in\mathcal{R}
with
|s_{0}|>1
and
\mathbf{w}\in Q
. Since
\pi_{\mathbf{u}}
is linear, we have

\alpha=\pi_{\mathbf{u}}(a_{1}+a_{0}s,b_{1}+b_{0}s)=s_{0}\pi_{\mathbf{u}}(\mathbf{w}).

This is a contradiction to the primitivity of
\alpha
. Hence, the second argument follows.

∎

5.
Upper Bound

In order to estimate the upper bound of the Hausdorff dimension of
\mathbf{DI}_{d}(\varepsilon)
, we will define the following self-similar structure for
\mathbf{DI}_{d}(\varepsilon)
.

Definition 5.1
.

For
\mathbf{u}\in Q
, we define

D(\mathbf{u})=\bigg\{\mathbf{v}\in Q:\ |\mathbf{u}|\leq|\mathbf{v}|,\ \mathbf{v}\in H_{\mathbf{u}},\ \text{and }\|\hat{\mathbf{u}}-\hat{\mathbf{v}}\|\leq\frac{\lambda_{1}(\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}})}{|\mathbf{u}|}\bigg\}.

For
\mathbf{u}\in Q
and
\mathbf{v}\in D(\mathbf{u})
, we define

E(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v},\varepsilon)=\Bigg\{\mathbf{w}\in Q:\ |\mathbf{v}|<|\mathbf{w}|,\ \mathbf{w}\notin H_{\mathbf{u}},\ \text{and }\|\hat{\mathbf{v}}-\hat{\mathbf{w}}\|<\frac{\varepsilon}{|\mathbf{v}||\mathbf{w}|^{1/d}}\Bigg\},

and

\sigma_{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{u})=\bigcup_{\mathbf{v}\in D(\mathbf{u})}E(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v},\varepsilon),\quad Q_{\varepsilon}=\{\mathbf{u}\in Q:\hat{\lambda}_{1}(\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}})<\varepsilon\},\quad B(\mathbf{u})=B\left(\hat{\mathbf{u}},\frac{1}{|\mathbf{u}|^{1+1/d}}\right).

Remark 5.2
.

We will consider the triple
(Q_{\varepsilon},\sigma_{\varepsilon},B)
as a self-similar structure on
\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}^{d}
. Recall Definition
3.1
and note that
(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{w})\in\sigma_{\varepsilon}
if and only if
\mathbf{w}\in E(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v},\varepsilon)
for some
\mathbf{v}\in D(\mathbf{u})
. Observe that
\sigma_{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{u})\subseteq Q_{\varepsilon}
for every
\mathbf{u}\in Q
since for any
\mathbf{w}\in\sigma_{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{u})
we have

\hat{\lambda}_{1}(\mathbf{w})\leq|\mathbf{w}|^{\frac{1}{d}}\|\pi_{\mathbf{w}}(\mathbf{v})\|=|\mathbf{w}|^{\frac{1}{d}}|\mathbf{v}|\cdot\|\hat{\mathbf{v}}-\hat{\mathbf{w}}\|<\varepsilon.

Thus, the triple
(Q_{\varepsilon},\sigma_{\varepsilon},B)
is indeed a self-similar structure.

For given
\varepsilon>0
, it follows from Corollary
4.7
and Lemma
4.10
that

\mathbf{DI}_{d}(\varepsilon)\setminus\mathcal{K}^{d}=\{\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}^{d}\setminus\mathcal{K}^{d}:\mathbf{u}_{n}\in Q_{\varepsilon}\text{ for all large enough }n\geq 1\},

where
\{\mathbf{u}_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
is the sequence of best approximation vectors for
\boldsymbol{\theta}
. We denote by
\widetilde{\mathbf{DI}}_{d}(\varepsilon)
the above set in the equation. Since the Hausdorff dimension of the rationals
\mathcal{K}^{d}
is zero, we can work with
\widetilde{\mathbf{DI}}_{d}(\varepsilon)
instead of
\mathbf{DI}_{d}(\varepsilon)
. We also define

\widetilde{\mathbf{DI}}_{d}^{*}(\varepsilon)=\{\boldsymbol{\theta}=(\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{d})\in\widetilde{\mathbf{DI}}_{d}(\varepsilon):1,\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{d}\text{ are linearly independent over }\mathcal{K}\}.

Proposition 5.3
.

The triple
(Q_{\varepsilon},\sigma_{\varepsilon},B)
is a self-similar covering of
\widetilde{\mathbf{DI}}_{d}^{*}(\varepsilon)
.

Proof.

Fix
\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\widetilde{\mathbf{DI}}_{d}^{*}(\varepsilon)
. We now claim that the sequence of best approximations
\{\mathbf{u}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of
\boldsymbol{\theta}
contains a
\sigma_{\varepsilon}
-admissible sequence. By definition, there exists some
n_{0}
such that
\mathbf{u}_{n}\in Q_{\varepsilon}
for every
n>n_{0}
. Choose
n_{1}=n_{0}+1
and assume that we have already constructed a
\sigma_{\varepsilon}
-admissible sequence
\mathbf{u}_{n_{1}},\dots,\mathbf{u}_{n_{i}}
. We let
n_{i+1}
be the smallest
m>n_{i}
such that
\mathbf{u}_{m}\notin H_{\mathbf{u}_{n_{i}}}
. Since
1,\theta_{1}\dots\theta_{d}
are linearly independent over
\mathcal{K}
, such
n_{i+1}
indeed exists. Now we let
\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{u}_{n_{i}}
,
\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{u}_{n_{i+1}-1}
, and
\mathbf{w}=\mathbf{u}_{n_{i+1}}
. Then,
|\mathbf{u}|\leq|\mathbf{v}|<|\mathbf{w}|
,
\mathbf{v}\in H_{\mathbf{u}}
, and
\mathbf{w}\notin H_{\mathbf{u}}
. Moreover, it follows from Lemmas
4.6
and
4.10
that

\|\hat{\mathbf{u}}-\hat{\mathbf{v}}\|=\frac{A(\hat{\mathbf{v}},\mathbf{u})}{|\mathbf{u}|}<\frac{\lambda_{1}(\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}})}{|\mathbf{u}|}

and

\|\hat{\mathbf{v}}-\hat{\mathbf{w}}\|=\frac{A(\hat{\mathbf{w}},\mathbf{v})}{|\mathbf{v}|}=\frac{\lambda_{1}(\Lambda_{\mathbf{w}})}{|\mathbf{v}|}=\frac{\hat{\lambda}_{1}(\Lambda_{\mathbf{w}})}{|\mathbf{v}|\cdot|\mathbf{w}|^{1/d}}<\frac{\varepsilon}{|\mathbf{v}|\cdot|\mathbf{w}|^{1/d}}.

Thus,
\mathbf{u}_{n_{i+1}}\in\sigma_{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{u}_{n_{i}})
so that
\{\mathbf{u}_{n_{i}}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is
\sigma_{\varepsilon}
-admissible.

It is obvious that
\mathrm{diam}\ B(\mathbf{u}_{n_{i}})\to 0
as
i\to\infty
. We shall now prove that
\bigcap_{i\in\mathbb{N}}B(\mathbf{u}_{n_{i}})=\{\boldsymbol{\theta}\}
.
Note that Theorem
2.4
implies
\lambda_{1}(\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}})\leq|\mathbf{u}|^{-1/d}
for any
\mathbf{u}\in Q
.
By Lemmas
4.5
and
4.10
, for any best approximation vector
\mathbf{u}
of
\boldsymbol{\theta}
, we have

\Delta(\mathbf{u})\subseteq\bar{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbf{u},r(\mathbf{u}))=B\left(\hat{\mathbf{u}},\frac{\lambda_{1}(\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}})}{|\mathbf{u}|}\right)\subseteq B\left(\hat{\mathbf{u}},|\mathbf{u}|^{-(1+1/d)}\right)=B(\mathbf{u}).

This implies that
\boldsymbol{\theta}\in B(\mathbf{u})
for every best approximation vector
\mathbf{u}
of
\boldsymbol{\theta}
. Therefore, we have
\bigcap_{i\in\mathbb{N}}B(\mathbf{u}_{n_{i}})=\{\boldsymbol{\theta}\}
.
∎

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
t>d
and
\mathbf{u}\in Q
. Then,

\sum_{\mathbf{v}\in D(\mathbf{u})}\left(\frac{|\mathbf{u}|}{|\mathbf{v}|}\right)^{t}\leq\frac{(q-1)q^{t-1}}{q^{t-d}-1}.

Proof.

For
k\geq 0
, denote

D_{k}(\mathbf{u})=\{\mathbf{v}\in D(\mathbf{u}):|\mathbf{v}|=q^{k}|\mathbf{u}|\}.

Then,

(5.1)

\sum_{\mathbf{v}\in D(\mathbf{u})}\left(\frac{|\mathbf{u}|}{|\mathbf{v}|}\right)^{t}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\mathbf{v}\in D_{k}(\mathbf{u})}\frac{1}{q^{kt}}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\#D_{k}(\mathbf{u})}{q^{kt}}.

To estimate
\#D_{k}(\mathbf{u})
,
consider the map
\pi_{\mathbf{u}}
restricted to
D_{k}(\mathbf{u})
. By definition, we have

\pi_{\mathbf{u}}(D_{k}(\mathbf{u}))\subseteq\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}}^{\prime}=\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}}\cap\pi_{\mathbf{u}}(H_{\mathbf{u}})\quad\text{and}\quad\|\pi_{\mathbf{u}}(\mathbf{v})\|=|\mathbf{v}|\|\hat{\mathbf{u}}-\hat{\mathbf{v}}\|\leq q^{k}\lambda_{1}(\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}})

for any
\mathbf{v}\in D_{k}(\mathbf{u})
.
Hence, it follows that
\pi_{\mathbf{u}}(D_{k}(\mathbf{u}))\subseteq B(0,q^{k}\lambda_{1}(\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}}))\cap\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}}^{\prime}
.

Let
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{d}
be an orthogonal basis of the lattice
\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}}
with
\|\xi_{i}\|=\lambda_{i}(\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}})
for each
i=1,\dots,d
as in the paragraph after Definition
4.8
. Since
\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}}^{\prime}=\mathrm{span}_{\mathcal{R}}\{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{d-1}\}
, it follows that

\begin{split}\#\left(B(0,q^{k}\lambda_{1}(\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}}))\cap\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}}^{\prime}\right)&=\#\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}a_{i}\xi_{i}:a_{i}\in\mathcal{R},|a_{i}|\leq q^{k}\frac{\lambda_{1}(\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}})}{\lambda_{i}(\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}})}\right\}\\
&\leq\prod_{i=1}^{d-1}\#\{a_{i}\in\mathcal{R}:|a_{i}|\leq q^{k}\}=q^{(k+1)(d-1)}.\end{split}

Thus, it follows from Lemma
4.13
that

(5.2)

\#D_{k}(\mathbf{u})\leq(q-1)q^{k}\#\left(B(0,q^{k}\lambda_{1}(\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}})\cap\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}}^{\prime})\right)\leq(q-1)q^{d-1}q^{kd}.

Hence, by plugging in (
5.2
) into (
5.1
), we obtain that

\sum_{\mathbf{v}\in D(\mathbf{u})}\left(\frac{|\mathbf{u}|}{|\mathbf{v}|}\right)^{t}\leq(q-1)q^{d-1}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}q^{-k(t-d)}=\frac{(q-1)q^{d-1}}{1-q^{-(t-d)}}=\frac{(q-1)q^{t-1}}{q^{t-d}-1}.

∎

Proposition 5.5
.

For every
t>d
, we have

\sum_{\mathbf{w}\in E(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v},\varepsilon)}\left(\frac{|\mathbf{v}|}{|\mathbf{w}|}\right)^{t}\leq\frac{(q-1)(q+q^{2})^{d}\varepsilon^{d}}{q^{t-d}-1}.

Proof.

For
k\geq 1
, denote

E_{k}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v},\varepsilon)=\{\mathbf{w}\in E(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v},\varepsilon):|\mathbf{w}|=q^{k}|\mathbf{v}|\}.

Then,

(5.3)

\sum_{\mathbf{w}\in E(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v},\varepsilon)}\left(\frac{|\mathbf{v}|}{|\mathbf{w}|}\right)^{t}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\mathbf{w}\in E_{k}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v},\varepsilon)}\frac{1}{q^{kt}}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\#E_{k}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v},\varepsilon)}{q^{kt}}.

Observe that for any
\mathbf{w}\in E_{k}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v},\varepsilon)
,

\|\pi_{\mathbf{v}}(\mathbf{w})\|=|\mathbf{w}|\|\hat{\mathbf{v}}-\hat{\mathbf{w}}\|<|\mathbf{w}|\frac{\varepsilon}{|\mathbf{v}||\mathbf{w}|^{1/d}}=\varepsilon q^{k\frac{d-1}{d}}|\mathbf{v}|^{-1/d}.

Since
\mathbf{v}\in H_{\mathbf{u}}
and
\mathbf{w}\notin H_{\mathbf{u}}
, it follows from Lemma
4.11
that
\pi_{\mathbf{v}}(\mathbf{w})\notin H_{\mathbf{u}}^{\prime}
.
Hence, we have

\pi_{\mathbf{v}}\left(E_{k}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v},\varepsilon)\right)\subseteq B\left(0,\varepsilon q^{k\frac{d-1}{d}}|\mathbf{v}|^{-1/d}\right)\cap\Lambda_{\mathbf{v}}\setminus H_{\mathbf{u}}^{\prime}.

If the set
B\left(0,\varepsilon q^{k\frac{d-1}{d}}|\mathbf{v}|^{-1/d}\right)\cap\Lambda_{\mathbf{v}}\setminus H_{\mathbf{u}}^{\prime}

is non-empty, then it follows from Lemma
4.12
that

(5.4)

\lambda_{d}(\Lambda_{\mathbf{v}})\leq q^{2}\varepsilon q^{k\frac{d-1}{d}}|\mathbf{v}|^{-1/d}.

It follows from Theorem
2.4
, Proposition
2.5
, Lemma
4.13
, and (
5.4
) that

(5.5)

\begin{split}\#E_{k}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v},\varepsilon)&\leq(q-1)q^{k}\#\left(B(0,\varepsilon q^{k\frac{d-1}{d}}|\mathbf{v}|^{-\frac{1}{d}})\cap\Lambda_{\mathbf{v}}\setminus H_{\mathbf{u}}^{\prime}\right)\\
&\leq(q-1)q^{k}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\Bigg\lceil\frac{q\varepsilon q^{k\frac{d-1}{d}}|\mathbf{v}|^{-1/d}}{\lambda_{i}(\Lambda_{\mathbf{v}})}\Bigg\rceil\\
&\leq(q-1)q^{k}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\left(\frac{q\varepsilon q^{k\frac{d-1}{d}}|\mathbf{v}|^{-1/d}+\lambda_{d}(\Lambda_{\mathbf{v}})}{\lambda_{i}(\Lambda_{\mathbf{v}})}\right)\\
&\leq(q-1)q^{k}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\frac{(q+q^{2})\varepsilon q^{k\frac{d-1}{d}}|\mathbf{v}|^{-1/d}}{\lambda_{i}(\Lambda_{\mathbf{v}})}\\
&=(q-1)q^{k}(q+q^{2})^{d}\varepsilon^{d}q^{k(d-1)}.\end{split}

Hence, by plugging in (
5.5
) into (
5.3
), we obtain that

\begin{split}\sum_{\mathbf{w}\in E(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v},\varepsilon)}\left(\frac{|\mathbf{v}|}{|\mathbf{w}|}\right)^{t}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(q-1)(q+q^{2})^{d}\varepsilon^{d}q^{kd}}{q^{kt}}&=(q-1)(q+q^{2})^{d}\varepsilon^{d}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}q^{-k(t-d)}\\
&=\frac{(q-1)(q+q^{2})^{d}\varepsilon^{d}}{q^{t-d}-1}.\end{split}

∎

Corollary 5.6
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
, we have

\mathrm{dim}_{H}\mathbf{DI}_{d}(\varepsilon)\leq\frac{d^{2}}{d+1}+\frac{d}{d+1}\log_{q}\left(1+\sqrt{(q-1)^{2}(q+1)^{d}q^{2d}\varepsilon^{d}}\right).

In particular, we have
\mathrm{dim}_{H}\mathbf{Sing}_{d}\leq\frac{d^{2}}{d+1}
.

Proof.

For any
\frac{d^{2}}{d+1}<s\leq d
, it follows from Propositions
5.4
and
5.5
with
t=\frac{d+1}{d}s
that

(5.6)

\begin{split}\sum_{\mathbf{w}\in\sigma_{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{u})}\frac{\operatorname{diam}(B(\mathbf{w}))^{s}}{\operatorname{diam}(B(\mathbf{u}))^{s}}&=\sum_{\mathbf{w}\in\sigma_{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{u})}\left(\frac{|\mathbf{u}|}{|\mathbf{w}|}\right)^{(1+1/d)s}\\
&\leq\sum_{\mathbf{v}\in D(\mathbf{u})}\left(\frac{|\mathbf{u}|}{|\mathbf{v}|}\right)^{(1+1/d)s}\sum_{\mathbf{w}\in E(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v},\varepsilon)}\left(\frac{|\mathbf{v}|}{|\mathbf{w}|}\right)^{(1+1/d)s}\\
&\leq\frac{(q-1)^{2}(q+q^{2})^{d}\varepsilon^{d}q^{\frac{d+1}{d}s-1}}{\left(q^{\frac{d+1}{d}s-d}-1\right)^{2}}\leq\frac{(q-1)^{2}(q+1)^{d}q^{2d}\varepsilon^{d}}{\left(q^{\frac{d+1}{d}s-d}-1\right)^{2}}.\end{split}

If
s\geq\frac{d^{2}}{d+1}+\frac{d}{d+1}\log_{q}\left(1+\sqrt{(q-1)^{2}(q+1)^{d}q^{2d}\varepsilon^{d}}\right)
,
then the right hand side of (
5.6
) is
\leq 1
. Thus, by Theorem
3.2
and Proposition
5.3
,

(5.7)

\mathrm{dim}_{H}\widetilde{\mathbf{DI}}_{d}^{*}(\varepsilon)\leq\frac{d^{2}}{d+1}+\frac{d}{d+1}\log_{q}\left(1+\sqrt{(q-1)^{2}(q+1)^{d}q^{2d}\varepsilon^{d}}\right).

Since the set of
\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}^{d}
such that
1,\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{d}
are linearly dependent over
\mathcal{K}
has Hausdorff dimension
d-1
, the corollary follows from (
5.7
).
∎

6.
Lower bound

In this section, we will estimate the lower bound of the Hausdorff dimension of
\mathbf{DI}_{d}(\varepsilon)
.
For simplicity, we will use the following notation throughout this section: for
\mathbf{u}\in Q
and
i=1,\dots,d
,

\lambda_{i}(\mathbf{u})=\lambda_{i}(\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}})\qquad\text{and}\qquad\hat{\lambda}_{i}(\mathbf{u})=\hat{\lambda}_{i}(\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}}).

6.1.
Self-similar structure

While a certain orthogonal projection was used in
[
CC16
]
, we will utilize the orthogonal basis given in the paragraph after Definition
4.8
. The basic idea is to directly use the lattice given in
[
CC16
, Lemma 8.7]
. For this, we need the following definition.

Given
\mathbf{u}\in Q
, we fix an orthogonal basis
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{d}
of the lattice
\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}}
with
\|\xi_{i}\|=\lambda_{i}(\mathbf{u})
for each
i=1,\dots,d
as in the paragraph after Definition
4.8
.
Note that by Theorem
2.4
,
\det(\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}})=\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{u})\cdots\lambda_{d}(\mathbf{u})=1/|\mathbf{u}|
.

Definition 6.1
.

Consider
\alpha\in\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}}
of the form
\alpha=\sum_{i<d}m_{i}\xi_{i}+n\xi_{d}
with
n\neq 0
.

(1)

Define the lattice

\Lambda_{\alpha}=\mathcal{R}\xi_{1}+\cdots+\mathcal{R}\xi_{d-1}+\mathcal{R}\sum_{i<d}\frac{m_{i}}{n}\xi_{i}.

Recall that
H_{\mathbf{u}}^{\prime}=\mathrm{span}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}}\{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{d-1}\}
.
Define the linear map
\pi_{\alpha}:\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}^{d}\to H_{\mathbf{u}}^{\prime}
by

\pi_{\alpha}(\xi)=c_{1}\xi_{1}+\cdots+c_{d-1}\xi_{d-1}-c_{d}\sum_{i<d}\frac{m_{i}}{n}\xi_{i},

where
\xi=\sum_{i\leq d}c_{i}\xi_{i}
. One can easily check that
\pi_{\alpha}(\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}})=\Lambda_{\alpha}
and
\mathrm{Ker}(\pi_{\alpha})=\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}\alpha
.

(2)

Let
\mathbf{v}\in Q
be such that
\pi_{\mathbf{u}}(\mathbf{v})=\alpha
, and assume that
\alpha
is primitive and
\|\alpha\|=|n|\lambda_{d}(\mathbf{u})
. By Lemma
6.2
, we have

\det(\Lambda_{\alpha})=\frac{1}{|\mathbf{u}|\|\alpha\|}=\frac{1}{|\mathbf{u}\wedge\mathbf{v}|},

where
|\mathbf{u}\wedge\mathbf{v}|=|\mathbf{u}||\mathbf{v}|\|\hat{\mathbf{u}}-\hat{\mathbf{v}}\|
. It is worth noting that the quantity
|\mathbf{u}\wedge\mathbf{v}|
is the supremum norm of the natural projection of
\mathbf{u}\wedge\mathbf{v}\in\bigwedge^{2}\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}^{d+1}
onto the subspace spanned by
\mathbf{e}_{1}\wedge\mathbf{e}_{d+1},\dots,\mathbf{e}_{d}\wedge\mathbf{e}_{d+1}
. For each
i=1\dots,d-1
, denote
\lambda_{i}(\alpha)=\lambda_{i}(\Lambda_{\alpha})
and
define
\hat{\lambda}_{i}(\alpha)=\hat{\lambda}_{i}(\Lambda_{\alpha})=|\mathbf{u}\wedge\mathbf{v}|^{\frac{1}{d-1}}\lambda_{i}(\Lambda_{\alpha})
.

We first calculate the covolume of the lattice
\Lambda_{\alpha}
when
\alpha
is primitive.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
\mathbf{u}\in Q
and let
\alpha=\sum_{i<d}m_{i}\xi_{i}+n\xi_{d}\in\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}}
be primitive with
n\neq 0
. Then

\det(\Lambda_{\alpha})=\frac{\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{u})\cdots\lambda_{d-1}(\mathbf{u})}{|n|}=\frac{1}{|\mathbf{u}||n|\lambda_{d}(\mathbf{u})}.

Proof.

Note that
\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}}^{\prime}=\mathrm{span}_{\mathcal{R}}\{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{d-1}\}
and by the orthogonality of
\{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{d-1}\}
we have
\det(\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}}^{\prime})=\|\xi_{1}\|\cdots\|\xi_{d-1}\|=\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{u})\cdots\lambda_{d-1}(\mathbf{u})
.
Since
\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}}^{\prime}
is a sublattice of
\Lambda_{\alpha}
of full rank,

\det(\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}}^{\prime})=[\Lambda_{\alpha}:\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}}^{\prime}]\det(\Lambda_{\alpha}),

where
[\Lambda_{\alpha}:\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}}^{\prime}]
denotes the index of the subgroup
\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}}^{\prime}
of
\Lambda_{\alpha}
.
Consider the group homomorphsim
\varphi:\Lambda_{\alpha}\to\mathcal{R}/n\mathcal{R}
given by

\varphi\left(\sum_{i<d}c_{i}\xi_{i}+c_{d}\sum_{i<d}\frac{m_{i}}{n}\xi_{i}\right)=c_{d}+n\mathcal{R}.

Since
\alpha
is primitive, we have
\mathrm{gcd}(m_{1},\dots,m_{d-1},n)=1
. Hence,
\varphi
is well-defined.
Since
\varphi
is surjective and
\mathrm{Ker}(\varphi)=\Lambda_{\mathrm{u}}^{\prime}
, we have
[\Lambda_{\alpha}:\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}}^{\prime}]=\#\mathcal{R}/n\mathcal{R}=|n|
.
∎

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
\mathbf{u}\in Q
,
\alpha=\sum_{i<d}m_{i}\xi_{i}+n\xi_{d}\in\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}}
be primitive with
n\neq 0
, and
\mathbf{v}\in Q
be such that
\pi_{\mathbf{u}}(\mathbf{v})=\alpha
. Assume that
\|\alpha\|=|n|\lambda_{d}(\mathbf{u})
. Then
\frac{|\mathbf{u}\wedge\mathbf{v}|}{|\mathbf{v}|}\leq\lambda_{1}(\alpha)
implies
\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{v})=\frac{|\mathbf{u}\wedge\mathbf{v}|}{|\mathbf{v}|}
.

Proof.

We first claim that for any
\xi\in\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}^{d}
,
\|\xi\|\geq\|\pi_{\alpha}(\xi)\|
. Indeed, writing
\xi=\sum_{i\leq d}c_{i}\xi_{i}
, we have

\|\pi_{\alpha}(\xi)\|\leq\max_{i=1,\dots,d-1}\left|c_{i}-c_{d}\frac{m_{i}}{n}\right|\|\xi_{i}\|\leq\max_{i=1,\dots,d-1}\max\Big\{|c_{i}|\lVert\xi_{i}\|,\left|c_{d}\frac{m_{i}}{n}\right|\|\xi_{i}\|\Big\}.

Since
\|\alpha\|=|n|\lambda_{d}(\mathbf{u})
, we have
|\frac{m_{i}}{n}|\|\xi_{i}\|\leq\|\xi_{d}\|
. Hence by the orthogonality of
\{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{d}\}
,

\|\pi_{\alpha}(\xi)\|\leq\max_{i=1,\dots,d}|c_{i}|\|\xi_{i}\|=\|\xi\|
.

Observe that since
\mathrm{Ker}(\pi_{\alpha})=\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}\alpha=\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}(\widehat{\mathbf{u}}-\widehat{\mathbf{v}})
, it follows that
\pi_{\alpha}(b\widehat{\mathbf{u}}-a)=\pi_{\alpha}(b\widehat{\mathbf{v}}-a)
for any
(a,b)\in\mathcal{R}^{d}\times\mathcal{R}
. Hence
\pi_{\alpha}(\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}})=\pi_{\alpha}(\Lambda_{\mathbf{v}})=\Lambda_{\alpha}
.

Now, let
\beta=\pi_{\mathbf{v}}(\mathbf{u})
. For any
\gamma\in\Lambda_{\mathbf{v}}\setminus\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}\beta
, since
\mathrm{Ker}(\pi_{\alpha})=\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}\beta
, it follows from the claim and the observation that
\|\gamma\|\geq\|\pi_{\alpha}(\gamma)\|\geq\lambda_{1}(\alpha)\geq\frac{|\mathbf{u}\wedge\mathbf{v}|}{|\mathbf{v}|}
.
Finally, we claim that
\beta
is primitive. Assuming this claim, if
\gamma\in\Lambda_{\mathbf{v}}\cap\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}\beta\setminus\{0\}
, then
\|\gamma\|\geq\|\beta\|=\frac{|\mathbf{u}\wedge\mathbf{v}|}{|\mathbf{v}|}
.
Therefore,
\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{v})=\frac{|\mathbf{u}\wedge\mathbf{v}|}{|\mathbf{v}|}
.

In order to prove the last claim, suppose that
\beta
is not primitive and write
\beta=f\gamma
with
\gamma\in\Lambda_{\mathbf{v}}
and
f\in\mathcal{R}
with
\mathrm{deg}f\geq 1
. Choose
\mathbf{w}\in Q
such that
\gamma=\pi_{\mathbf{v}}(\mathbf{w})
. By linearity of
\pi_{\mathbf{v}}
,
\pi_{\mathbf{v}}(f\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{u})=0
, hence
f\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{u}=f^{\prime}\mathbf{v}
for some
f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{R}
. This implies that
f^{\prime}\alpha=f\pi_{\mathbf{u}}(\mathbf{w})
. Since
\alpha
is primitive, we have
f|f^{\prime}
. This contradicts the fact that
f\mathbf{w}-f^{\prime}\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{u}\in Q
.
∎

We shall now define the fractal structure for the lower bound of the Hausdorff dimension of
\mathbf{DI}_{d}(\varepsilon)
.

Definition 6.4
.

Given
\mathbf{u}\in Q
and
\varepsilon>0
, define

\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}}(\varepsilon)=\{\alpha\in\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}}:\alpha\text{ is primitive and }\hat{\lambda}_{1}(\alpha)>\varepsilon\}.

Given
n\in\mathcal{R}
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
, define

C_{n}^{\prime}({\mathbf{u}})=\Bigg\{\mathbf{v}=\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}\alpha_{i}\xi_{i}+n\xi_{d}:\alpha_{i}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{K}},|\alpha_{i}|<\frac{|n|\lambda_{d}(\mathbf{u})}{\lambda_{i}(\mathbf{u})}\Bigg\};\quad C_{N}(\mathbf{u})=\bigcup_{0<|n|\leq q^{N}}C_{n}^{\prime}(\mathbf{u}),

and define

F_{N}(\mathbf{u},\varepsilon)=\bigcup_{\alpha\in\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}}(\varepsilon)\cap C_{N}(\mathbf{u})}\zeta(\mathbf{u},\alpha,\varepsilon);\qquad F(\mathbf{u},\varepsilon)=\bigcup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}F_{N}(\mathbf{u},\varepsilon),

where

\zeta(\mathbf{u},\alpha,\varepsilon)=\Bigg\{\mathbf{v}\in Q:\pi_{\mathbf{u}}(\mathbf{v})=\alpha,\left(\frac{|\mathbf{u}\wedge\mathbf{v}|}{\varepsilon}\right)^{\frac{d}{d-1}}\leq|\mathbf{v}|\leq\left(\frac{q|\mathbf{u}\wedge\mathbf{v}|}{\varepsilon}\right)^{\frac{d}{d-1}}\Bigg\}.

Finally, we define

\sigma_{\varepsilon,N}(\mathbf{u})=F_{N}(\mathbf{u},\varepsilon),\quad Q_{\varepsilon,N}=\bigcup_{\mathbf{u}\in Q}\sigma_{\varepsilon,N}(\mathbf{u}),\quad B(\mathbf{u})=B\left(\hat{\mathbf{u}},\frac{\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{u})}{q|\mathbf{u}|}\right).

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
0<\varepsilon<1
and
\mathbf{v}\in F(\mathbf{u},\varepsilon)
. Then
\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{v})=\frac{|\mathbf{u}\wedge\mathbf{v}|}{|\mathbf{v}|}
,
\frac{1}{q}\varepsilon\leq\hat{\lambda}_{1}(\mathbf{v})\leq\varepsilon
, and
|\mathbf{v}|>|\mathbf{u}|
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha=\pi_{\mathbf{u}}(\mathbf{v})
. Since

\frac{\varepsilon}{q|\mathbf{u}\wedge\mathbf{v}|}\leq\frac{1}{|\mathbf{v}|^{\frac{d-1}{d}}}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{|\mathbf{u}\wedge\mathbf{v}|}

and
\hat{\lambda}_{1}(\alpha)=|\mathbf{u}\wedge\mathbf{v}|^{\frac{1}{d-1}}\lambda_{1}(\alpha)>\varepsilon
, it follows from
\varepsilon<1
that

\frac{|\mathbf{u}\wedge\mathbf{v}|}{|\mathbf{v}|}\leq\frac{\varepsilon^{\frac{d}{d-1}}}{|\mathbf{u}\wedge\mathbf{v}|^{\frac{1}{d-1}}}<\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{d-1}}\lambda_{1}(\alpha)<\lambda_{1}(\alpha).

Since
\alpha
is primitive, by Lemma
6.3
, we have
\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{v})=\frac{|\mathbf{u}\wedge\mathbf{v}|}{|\mathbf{v}|}
. Thus,

\frac{\varepsilon}{q}\leq\hat{\lambda}_{1}(\mathbf{v})=\frac{|\mathbf{u}\wedge\mathbf{v}|}{|\mathbf{v}|^{\frac{d-1}{d}}}\leq\varepsilon.

Since
\alpha\in\Lambda_{\mathbf{u}}\setminus H_{\mathbf{u}}^{\prime}
, by Lemma
4.12
, we have
\|\alpha\|\geq\lambda_{d}(\mathbf{u})
. Note that by Theorem
2.4
,
\hat{\lambda}_{1}(\mathbf{u})\cdots\hat{\lambda}_{d}(\mathbf{u})=1
. Hence,
\hat{\lambda}_{d}(\mathbf{u})\geq 1
. Therefore,

(6.1)

1\leq\hat{\lambda}_{d}(\mathbf{u})=|\mathbf{u}|^{1/d}\lambda_{d}(\mathbf{u})\leq|\mathbf{u}|^{1/d}\|\alpha\|.

It follows from (
6.1
) that

\frac{|\mathbf{u}|}{|\mathbf{v}|}\leq\frac{\varepsilon^{\frac{d}{d-1}}|\mathbf{u}|}{|\mathbf{u}\wedge\mathbf{v}|^{\frac{d}{d-1}}}=\frac{\varepsilon^{\frac{d}{d-1}}|\mathbf{u}|}{\|\alpha\|^{\frac{d}{d-1}}|\mathbf{u}|^{\frac{d}{d-1}}}=\frac{\varepsilon^{\frac{d}{d-1}}}{(|\mathbf{u}|^{\frac{1}{d}}\|\alpha\|)^{\frac{d}{d-1}}}\leq\varepsilon^{\frac{d}{d-1}}.

Since
\varepsilon<1
, we have
|\mathbf{v}|>|\mathbf{u}|
.
∎

Lemma 6.6
.

Let
0<\varepsilon<1
and
\mathbf{v}\in F(\mathbf{u},\varepsilon)
. Then,
B\left(\hat{\mathbf{v}},\frac{\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{v})}{|\mathbf{v}|}\right)\subset B\left(\hat{\mathbf{u}},\frac{\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{u})}{q|\mathbf{u}|}\right)
.

Proof.

We first bound
\|\hat{\mathbf{u}}-\hat{\mathbf{v}}\|
and
\frac{\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{v})}{|\mathbf{v}|}
. Let
\alpha=\pi_{\mathbf{u}}(\mathbf{v})
. Then it follows from
\xi_{1}\in\Lambda_{\alpha}
that
\lambda_{1}(\alpha)\leq\|\xi_{1}\|=\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{u})
. Since
\varepsilon<1
, we have

(6.2)

\|\hat{\mathbf{u}}-\hat{\mathbf{v}}\|=\frac{|\mathbf{u}\wedge\mathbf{v}|}{|\mathbf{u}||\mathbf{v}|}\leq\frac{\varepsilon^{\frac{d}{d-1}}}{|\mathbf{u}\wedge\mathbf{v}|^{\frac{1}{d-1}}|\mathbf{u}|}<\frac{\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{d-1}}\hat{\lambda}_{1}(\alpha)}{|\mathbf{u}\wedge\mathbf{v}|^{\frac{1}{d-1}}|\mathbf{u}|}\leq\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{d-1}}\frac{\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{u})}{|\mathbf{u}|}<\frac{\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{u})}{|\mathbf{u}|}.

By Lemma
6.5
and (
6.2
), we have

(6.3)

\frac{\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{v})}{|\mathbf{v}|}=\frac{|\mathbf{u}\wedge\mathbf{v}|}{|\mathbf{v}|^{2}}=\frac{|\mathbf{u}|}{|\mathbf{v}|}\|\hat{\mathbf{u}}-\hat{\mathbf{v}}\|<\frac{\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{u})}{|\mathbf{v}|}<\frac{\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{u})}{|\mathbf{u}|}.

Now let
\boldsymbol{\theta}\in B\left(\hat{\mathbf{v}},\frac{\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{v})}{|\mathbf{v}|}\right)
. By Lemma
1.5
, (
6.2
), and (
6.3
),

\|\boldsymbol{\theta}-\hat{\mathbf{u}}\|\leq\max\{\|\hat{\mathbf{u}}-\hat{\mathbf{v}}\|,\|\boldsymbol{\theta}-\hat{\mathbf{v}}\|\}<\frac{\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{u})}{|\mathbf{u}|}.

Since the image of the norm
\|\cdot\|
is in
q^{\mathbb{Z}}\cup\{0\}
,then,
\frac{\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{u})}{|\mathbf{u}|}\in q^{\mathbb{Z}}\cup\{0\}
. Hence,
\|\boldsymbol{\theta}-\hat{\mathbf{u}}\|\leq\frac{\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{u})}{q|\mathbf{u}|}
, that is,

\boldsymbol{\theta}\in B\left(\hat{\mathbf{u}},\frac{\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{u})}{q|\mathbf{u}|}\right)
.
∎

Proposition 6.7
.

For
0<\varepsilon<1
, the triple
(Q_{\varepsilon,N},\sigma_{\varepsilon,N},B)
is a strictly nested self-similar structure covering a subset of
\mathbf{DI}_{d}(\varepsilon)
.

Proof.

Note that by the definition of
Q_{\varepsilon,N}
, if
\mathbf{u}\in Q_{\varepsilon,N}
, then,
\sigma_{\varepsilon,N}(\mathbf{u})\subseteq Q_{\varepsilon,N}
. We need to verify that for every
\sigma_{\varepsilon,N}
admissible sequence
\{\mathbf{u}_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
,

(1)

for all
k
,
B(\mathbf{u}_{k+1})\subset B(\mathbf{u}_{k})
and
\operatorname{diam}(B(\mathbf{u}_{k+1}))<\operatorname{diam}(B(\mathbf{u}_{k}))
;

(2)

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\operatorname{diam}(B(\mathbf{u}_{k}))=0
and
\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty}B(\mathbf{u}_{k})
is a point contained in
\mathbf{DI}_{d}(\varepsilon)
.

By Lemma
6.6
, it is clear that (
1
) holds. Moreover, due to the definition of
\sigma_{\varepsilon,N}
and Lemma
6.5
,
|\mathbf{u}_{k}|\rightarrow\infty
. Therefore,
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\operatorname{diam}(B(\mathbf{u}_{k}))=0
. Hence,
\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty}B(\mathbf{u}_{k})
is a single point
\boldsymbol{\theta}
.

In order to prove that
\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\mathbf{DI}_{d}(\varepsilon)
, we observe that
\boldsymbol{\theta}\in B(\mathbf{u}_{k})
for every
k\geq 1
. Thus, for every
k\geq 1
, we have
\|\boldsymbol{\theta}-\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}\|\leq\frac{\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{u}_{k})}{q|\mathbf{u}_{k}|}
. Thus, it follows that

A(\boldsymbol{\theta},\mathbf{u}_{k})=\|\boldsymbol{\theta}-\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}\||\mathbf{u}_{k}|<\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{u}_{k}).

By Lemma
4.5
,
\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\Delta(\mathbf{u}_{k})
, so that for every
k\geq 1
,
\mathbf{u}_{k}
is a best approximation of
\boldsymbol{\theta}
.

Fix any large enough
T\geq 1
so that there is
k\geq 1
such that
|\mathbf{u}_{k}|\leq T<|\mathbf{u}_{k+1}|
.
Since
\mathbf{u}_{k}
’s are best approximations of
\boldsymbol{\theta}
, it follows from Lemma
4.6
and
|\mathbf{u}_{k+1}|\geq\left(\frac{|\mathbf{u}_{k}\wedge\mathbf{u}_{k+1}|}{\varepsilon}\right)^{\frac{d}{d-1}}
that

A(\boldsymbol{\theta},\mathbf{u}_{k})=A(\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k+1},\mathbf{u}_{k})=\frac{|\mathbf{u}_{k}\wedge\mathbf{u}_{k+1}|}{|\mathbf{u}_{k+1}|}\leq\varepsilon|\mathbf{u}_{k+1}|^{-\frac{1}{d}}<\varepsilon T^{-\frac{1}{d}}.

This proves that
\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\mathbf{DI}_{d}(\varepsilon)
.
∎

6.2.
Well-separatedness

We will need the following claim regarding the distance between two balls in
\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}^{d}
.

Lemma 6.8
.

Let
\mathbf{u},\mathbf{u}^{\prime}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}^{d}
and let
r,r^{\prime}>0
. Then,

d\left(B(\mathbf{u},r),B(\mathbf{u}^{\prime},r^{\prime})\right)=\begin{cases}0&\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}^{\prime}\|\leq\max\{r,r^{\prime}\}\\
\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}^{\prime}\|&\text{else}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Notice that an element in
B(\mathbf{u},r)
will be of the form
\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{v}
where
\|\mathbf{v}\|\leq r
. Hence, if
\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}^{\prime}\|\leq\max\{r,r^{\prime}\}
, then,
-(\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}^{\prime})\in B(0,\max\{r,r^{\prime}\})
. Thus,

d\left(B(\mathbf{u},r),B(\mathbf{u}^{\prime},r^{\prime})\right)=\inf_{\|\mathbf{v}\|\leq r,\|\mathbf{v}^{\prime}\|\leq r^{\prime}}\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}^{\prime}+\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{v}^{\prime}\|\leq\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}^{\prime}-(\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}^{\prime})\|=0.

On the other hand, assume that
\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}^{\prime}\|>\max\{r,r^{\prime}\}
. Then, for every
\|\mathbf{v}\|\leq r
and
\|\mathbf{v}^{\prime}\|\leq r^{\prime}
, we have that

\|\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{v}^{\prime}\|\leq\max\{\|\mathbf{v}\|,\|\mathbf{v}^{\prime}\|\}\leq\max\{r,r^{\prime}\}<\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}^{\prime}\|.

Thus, by Lemma
1.5
,

d\left(B(\mathbf{u},r),B(\mathbf{u}^{\prime},r^{\prime})\right)=\inf_{\|\mathbf{v}\|\leq r,\|\mathbf{v}^{\prime}\|\leq r^{\prime}}\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}^{\prime}+\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{v}^{\prime}\|=\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}^{\prime}\|.

∎

Lemma 6.9
.

Let
\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}\in F(\mathbf{u},\varepsilon)
be distinct. Then,
d(B(\mathbf{v}),B(\mathbf{w}))=\|\hat{\mathbf{v}}-\hat{\mathbf{w}}\|
.

Proof.

By Lemma
6.8
, it suffices to prove that
\|\hat{\mathbf{v}}-\hat{\mathbf{w}}\|>\max\Bigg\{\frac{\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{w})}{q|\mathbf{w}|},\frac{\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{v})}{q|\mathbf{v}|}\Bigg\}
. Assume towards a contradiction that
\|\hat{\mathbf{w}}-\hat{\mathbf{v}}\|\leq\max\Bigg\{\frac{\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{w})}{q|\mathbf{w}|},\frac{\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{v})}{q|\mathbf{v}|}\Bigg\}
, and assume without loss of generality that
\frac{\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{v})}{|\mathbf{v}|}\leq\frac{\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{w})}{|\mathbf{w}|}
. It follows that

\frac{\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{v})}{|\mathbf{w}|}\leq\frac{\|\pi_{\mathbf{v}}(\mathbf{w})\|}{|\mathbf{w}|}=\|\hat{\mathbf{w}}-\hat{\mathbf{v}}\|\leq\frac{\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{w})}{q|\mathbf{w}|}.

Thus,
\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{v})\leq\frac{\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{w})}{q}<\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{w})
.
It follows from Lemma
6.5
that
\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{v})=\frac{|\mathbf{u}\wedge\mathbf{v}|}{|\mathbf{v}|}=|\mathbf{u}|\|\hat{\mathbf{u}}-\hat{\mathbf{v}}\|
,
\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{w})=\frac{|\mathbf{u}\wedge\mathbf{w}|}{|\mathbf{w}|}=|\mathbf{u}|\|\hat{\mathbf{u}}-\hat{\mathbf{w}}\|
, and

\|\hat{\mathbf{u}}-\hat{\mathbf{w}}\|=\frac{\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{w})}{|\mathbf{u}|}>\frac{\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{w})}{|\mathbf{w}|}>\|\hat{\mathbf{w}}-\hat{\mathbf{v}}\|.

By Lemma
1.5
we have

\|\hat{\mathbf{w}}-\hat{\mathbf{v}}\|<\|\hat{\mathbf{u}}-\hat{\mathbf{w}}\|\leq\max\{\|\hat{\mathbf{w}}-\hat{\mathbf{v}}\|,\|\hat{\mathbf{u}}-\hat{\mathbf{v}}\|\}.

It follows that
\|\hat{\mathbf{w}}-\hat{\mathbf{v}}\|<\|\hat{\mathbf{u}}-\hat{\mathbf{v}}\|
, so that
by Lemma
1.5
,
\|\hat{\mathbf{u}}-\hat{\mathbf{w}}\|=\|\hat{\mathbf{u}}-\hat{\mathbf{v}}\|
. Thus, we have

\frac{\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{w})}{|\mathbf{u}|}=\|\hat{\mathbf{u}}-\hat{\mathbf{w}}\|=\|\hat{\mathbf{u}}-\hat{\mathbf{v}}\|=\frac{\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{v})}{|\mathbf{u}|}.

This implies that
\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{w})=\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{v})
, which is a contradiction. Hence
d\left(B(\mathbf{w}),B(\mathbf{v})\right)=\|\hat{\mathbf{w}}-\hat{\mathbf{v}}\|
.
∎

Lemma 6.10
.

Let
\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}\in F_{N}(\mathbf{u},\varepsilon)
be distinct. Then,

d\left(B(\mathbf{v}),B(\mathbf{w})\right)\geq\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{q^{N+1}\hat{\lambda}_{d}(\mathbf{u})}\right)^{\frac{2d}{d-1}}\frac{\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{u})}{|\mathbf{u}|}.

Proof.

By Lemma
6.5
, it follows that

(6.4)

\|\hat{\mathbf{v}}-\hat{\mathbf{w}}\|=\frac{\|\pi_{\mathbf{v}}(\mathbf{w})\|}{|\mathbf{w}|}\geq\frac{\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{v})}{|\mathbf{w}|}=\frac{|\mathbf{u}\wedge\mathbf{v}|}{|\mathbf{v}||\mathbf{w}|}=\frac{\|\pi_{\mathbf{u}}(\mathbf{v})\||\mathbf{u}|}{|\mathbf{v}||\mathbf{w}|}\geq\frac{\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{u})}{|\mathbf{u}|}\left(\frac{|\mathbf{u}|}{|\mathbf{v}|}\right)\left(\frac{|\mathbf{u}|}{|\mathbf{w}|}\right).

By definition of
F_{N}(\mathbf{u},\varepsilon)
, we have

|\mathbf{v}|\leq\left(\frac{q|\mathbf{u}\wedge\mathbf{v}|}{\varepsilon}\right)^{\frac{d}{d-1}}
and
\|\pi_{\mathbf{u}}(\mathbf{v})\|\leq q^{N}\lambda_{d}(\mathbf{u})
. Thus,

(6.5)

\frac{|\mathbf{u}|}{|\mathbf{v}|}\geq\frac{|\mathbf{u}|\varepsilon^{\frac{d}{d-1}}}{(q|\mathbf{u}\wedge\mathbf{v}|)^{\frac{d}{d-1}}}=\frac{\varepsilon^{\frac{d}{d-1}}}{(q|\mathbf{u}|^{\frac{1}{d}}\|\pi_{\mathbf{u}}(\mathbf{v})\|)^{\frac{d}{d-1}}}\geq\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{q^{N+1}\hat{\lambda}_{d}(\mathbf{u})}\right)^{\frac{d}{d-1}}.

By the same argument with
\mathbf{w}
instead of
\mathbf{v}
, we have (
6.5
) for
\mathbf{w}
.
Combining with Lemma
6.9
, (
6.4
), and (
6.5
) for
\mathbf{v}
and
\mathbf{w}
, it follows that

d\left(B(\mathbf{v}),B(\mathbf{w})\right)=\|\hat{\mathbf{v}}-\hat{\mathbf{w}}\|\geq\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{q^{N+1}\hat{\lambda}_{d}(\mathbf{u})}\right)^{\frac{2d}{d-1}}\frac{\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{u})}{|\mathbf{u}|}.

∎

6.3.

Proposition 6.11

Let

,

Remark 6.12

.

by Remark

\begin{split}\sum_{\mathbf{v}\in\sigma_{\varepsilon,N}(\mathbf{u})}&\frac{\operatorname{diam}(B(\mathbf{v}))^{s}}{\operatorname{diam}(B(\mathbf{u}))^{s}}=\sum_{\mathbf{v}\in F_{N}(\mathbf{u},\varepsilon)}\left(\frac{\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{v})}{|\mathbf{v}|}\right)^{s}\left(\frac{|\mathbf{u}|}{\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{u})}\right)^{s}\\

, then
I_{k}\cap\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
is non-empty. Hence, we have

\sum_{\ell\in I_{k}\cap\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}q^{\ell d}\geq q^{(d-1)k-d}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{\hat{\lambda}_{d}(\mathbf{u})}\right)^{d}=q^{(d-1)k}\frac{\varepsilon^{d}}{q^{d}}\frac{\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{u})\cdots\lambda_{d-1}(\mathbf{u})}{\lambda_{d}^{d-1}(\mathbf{u})}.

This completes the proof.

∎

Abstract

Efficient computation of sensitivities is a promising approach for efficiently designing and optimizing
hvdc
cable joints. This paper presents the
am
for coupled nonlinear transient electrothermal problems as an efficient approach to compute sensitivities with respect to a large number of design parameters.
The method is used to compute material sensitivities of a 320 kV
hvdc
cable joint specimen. The results are validated against sensitivities obtained via the
dsm
.

1
Introduction

Cable joints are known to be the most vulnerable components of
hvdc
(
hvdc
) cable systems as they suffer from high internal electric field stresses
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
]
. One solution to mitigate these stresses is the integration of a layer of nonlinear
fgm
(
fgm
)
[
5
,
6
]
. This material becomes highly conductive in areas of high stress, effectively reducing electric field stress and redistributing voltage drop to lower-stress areas.

Recent developments in material science allow for the customization of an
fgm
’s nonlinear conductivity, enabling more precise designs to fit specific applications
[
7
,
6
]
. In addition to practical know-how,
fe
(
fe
) simulations play an increasingly vital role for the development of
fgm
. However, only few studies systematically investigated the various design parameters of
fgm

[
6
,
5
,
8
,
9
]
.

One way to study the influence of individual design parameters without extensive parameter sweeps is the use of sensitivities, i.e. gradients. Sensitivities provide insights on the impact of small changes in a design parameter on a
qoi
(
qoi
), and allow an efficient optimization of
fgm

[
10
]
.

Different methods for sensitivity computation exist. Commonly used methods like, e.g., finite differences and the
dsm
,
have the drawback that their computational costs scale linearly with the number of parameters
[
11
,
12
]
. The
am
, on the other hand, features computational costs that are nearly unaffected by the number of parameters
[
11
,
13
,
12
]
. In
hv
engineering, the
am
has recently been formulated for linear
eqs
(
eqs
) problems in frequency domain
[
14
]
, nonlinear
eqs
problems in the time domain
[
15
]
, and stationary nonlinear coupled electrothermal problems
[
16
]
.
However, for an investigation of the electrothermal behavior of a cable joint during transient overvoltages, a fully coupled transient
eqst
(
eqst
) analysis is required
[
17
,
18
,
19
]
. This study formulates and numerically solves the
am
for transient coupled
eqst
problems with nonlinear material properties. The method is implemented within the Python-based FE framework
Pyrit

[
20
]
and applied to the model of a 320 kV
hvdc
cable joint under switching impulse operation. The method is validated using results obtained via the
dsm
as a reference. Moreover, the benefits of a multi-rate time-integration approach are demonstrated. The outcome of the paper is an
fe
-based adjoint sensitivity analysis tool incorporating electrothermal multiphysics, nonlinear material properties and transient overvoltages, which are the three challenges to be tackled in cable joint design.

3
Sensitivities of Cable Joint Materials

As shown in Sec.
2.1
, the nonlinear conductivity curve of an
fgm
is represented as an analytical function shaped by various design parameters, denoted as
\bm{p}=[p_{1},...,p_{N_{\text{P}}}]
. When designing an
fgm
, the quality of the nonlinear conductivity curve is evaluated based on a number of
qoi
,
G_{k}
,
k=1,...,N_{\text{QoI}}
, such as the Joule losses or the electric field at critical positions
[
6
,
5
]
. The design process, i.e. the optimization of
\bm{p}
, can be accelerated by using sensitivity information
[
10
]
. Sensitivities quantify the influence of small changes in a design parameter,
p_{j}
, on a given QoI,
G_{k}
, expressed as
\frac{\text{d}G_{k}}{\text{d}p_{j}}(\bm{p}_{0})
, where
\bm{p}_{0}
represents the current parameter configuration. In this section, it is discussed how sensitivities of
hvdc
cable joint materials can be computed most effectively.

3.1
Direct Sensitivity Method

One of the most frequently used methods for sensitivity computation is the
dsm
(
dsm
). The
dsm
appeals with its simple derivation and uncomplicated implementation. It is based on the derivatives of (
2
) and (
3
) to
p_{j}
, from which the derivatives of the electric potential and the temperature to
p_{j}
can be computed, i.e.
\frac{\text{d}\phi}{\text{d}p_{j}}(\bm{p}_{0})
, and
\frac{\text{d}\theta}{\text{d}p_{j}}(\bm{p}_{0})
. The sensitivity can then be calculated directly using the chain rule:

\frac{\text{d}G_{k}}{\text{d}p_{j}}(\bm{p}_{0})=\frac{\partial G_{k}}{\partial p_{j}}(\bm{p}_{0})+\frac{\partial G_{k}}{\partial\phi}\frac{\text{d}\phi}{\text{d}p_{j}}(\bm{p}_{0})+\frac{\partial G_{k}}{\partial\theta}\frac{\text{d}\theta}{\text{d}p_{j}}(\bm{p}_{0})\,{.}

(7)

Since this process has to be repeated for each parameter,
p_{1},...,p_{N_{\text{P}}}
, the
dsm
requires the solution of
N_{\text{P}}
individually coupled systems of linear
pde
in addition to the nominal solution. Consequently, for large numbers of parameters, the
dsm
leads to prohibitively long simulation times.

3.2
Adjoint Variable Method

An alternative approach for sensitivity computation is the
am
(
am
). The
am
is very efficient when the number of parameters,
N_{\text{P}}
, is greater than the number of quantities of interest (
qoi
),
N_{\text{QoI}}
. It has originally been applied for the analysis of electric networks and only recently gained interest in the
hv
(
hv
) engineering community
[
14
,
16
,
15
]
. In this paper, the method is adapted for the
transient coupled electrothermal
analysis of HVDC cable joints.

The
am
avoids the computation of
\frac{\text{d}\phi}{\text{d}p_{j}}(\bm{p}_{0})
and
\frac{\text{d}\theta}{\text{d}p_{j}}(\bm{p}_{0})
for each parameter by a clever representation of the
qoi
: Each
qoi
,
G_{k}
, is formulated as an integral over the computational domain in space and time,
\Omega\times[t_{\text{s}},t_{\text{f}}]
, by means of a functional,
g_{k}
. Furthermore, the
eqs
equation (
2
) and the transient heat conduction equation (
3
) are added, multiplied by test functions
w_{\text{el},k}
and
w_{\text{th},k}
, respectively as additional terms:

\displaystyle G_{k}

\displaystyle(\phi,\theta,\bm{p})=\int_{\Omega}\int_{t_{\text{s}}}^{t_{\text{f}}}g_{k}(\phi,\theta,\bm{r},t,\bm{p})\text{d}t\,\text{d}\Omega

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\int_{t_{\text{s}}}^{t_{\text{f}}}w_{\text{el},k}(\bm{r},t)\underbrace{\left(\,\text{div}\left(\partial_{t}\bm{D}+\bm{J}\right)\right)}_{{}_{\overset{\text{Eq.}\eqref{eq:eqs:1}}{=}0}}\text{d}t\,\text{d}\Omega

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\int_{t_{\text{s}}}^{t_{\text{f}}}w_{\text{th},k}(\bm{r},t)\underbrace{\left(\partial_{t}\left(c_{\text{V}}\theta\right)+\,\text{div}\left(\bm{\dot{q}}\right)-\dot{q}_{\text{Joule}}\right)}_{{}_{\overset{\text{Eq.}\eqref{eq:thc:1}}{=}0}}\text{d}t\,\text{d}\Omega\,{.}

(8)

5
Conclusion

The
am
is an efficient approach for computing sensitivities of quantities of interest with respect to a large number of the design parameters. In this work, the
am
is formulated for coupled transient electrothermal problems with nonlinear media and implemented in the
fe
framework
Pyrit
. The method is applied to the numerically challenging example of a 320 kV cable joint specimen under switching operation. The computed sensitivities are compared to results obtained by the
dsm
and the method is successfully validated. The convergence of the
am
is discussed and the benefits of a multi-rate time-integration approach are demonstrated. This is an important step towards efficient and systematic design and optimization of
hvdc
cable joints.

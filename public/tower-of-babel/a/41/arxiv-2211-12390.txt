On the profinite rigidity of free and surface groups
Ismael Morales

Abstract
. Let S S be either a free group or the fundamental group of a closed hyperbolic surface. We show that if G G is a finitely generated residually- p p group with the same pro- p p completion as S S , then two-generated subgroups of G G are free. This generalises (and gives a new proof of) the analogous result of Baumslag for parafree groups. Our argument relies on the following new ingredient: if G G is a residually-(torsion-free nilpotent) group and H ⩽ G H\leqslant G is a virtually polycyclic subgroup, then H H is nilpotent and the pro- p p topology of G G induces on H H its full pro- p p topology. Then we study applications to profinite rigidity. Remeslennikov conjectured that a finitely generated residually finite G G with profinite completion G ^ ≅ S ^ \widehat{G}\cong\widehat{S} is necessarily G ≅ S G\cong S . We confirm this when G G belongs to a class of groups ℋ 𝐚𝐛 {\bf\mathcal{H}_{\bf ab}} that has a finite abelian hierarchy starting with finitely generated residually free groups. This strengthens a previous result of Wilton that relies on the hyperbolicity assumption. Lastly, we prove that the group S × ℤ n S\times\mathbb{Z}^{n} is profinitely rigid within finitely generated residually free groups.



1.
Introduction

A fundamental idea in the study of infinite groups consists of understanding how much algebraic structure of a group
G
can be detected in its collection of finite quotients. We address the following question: Suppose that
S
is either a free group or the fundamental group of a closed hyperbolic surface. Given a finitely generated residually finite group
G
with the same profinite completion
\widehat{G}\cong\widehat{S}
, what can be said about
G?
Our main result is
A
and provides a partial answer which concerns the subgroup structure of
G
. Before stating it, we remark that a recent breakthrough of Jaikin-Zapirain
[
35
, Theorem 1.1]
implies that such
G
is residually-
p
, so a group
G
as above will lie under the assumptions of the following theorem.

Theorem A
.

Let
p
be a prime and let
S
be either a free or hyperbolic surface group. Suppose that
G
is a finitely generated residually-
p
group with the same pro-
p
completion as
S
. Then two-generated subgroups of
G
are free.

This is proven in
4.1
.
A
generalises (and gives a new proof of) the analogous result of Baumslag
[
8
, Theorem 4.2]
for parafree groups
G
. Recall that a finitely generated group
G
is termed
parafree
(resp.
parasurface
) if it is residually nilpotent and its quotients by the terms of its lower central series are the same as those of some free group (resp. surface group). Parafree groups were introduced by Baumslag
[
7
]
and these seem to resemble free groups in many of their structural properties. The proof of Baumslag relies on the fact that a parafree group can be embedded into the unit group of a ring of power series
\mathbb{Z}\llbracket X\rrbracket
, an approach which does not seem to carry over to parasurfaces. Our proof of
A
relies on different cohomological and separability arguments, including our next theorem.

Theorem B
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated residually-(torsion-free nilpotent) group and let
H\leqslant G
be a virtually polycyclic subgroup. Then
H
is nilpotent and, for all primes
p
, the pro-
p
topology of
G
induces on
H
its full pro-
p
topology.

This is proven in
3.12
.
B
provides a fairly general setting in which nilpotent subgroups are witnessed by the pro-
p
completion. This is also crucial ingredient of
[
21
, Theorem E]
, where Fruchter and the author show that direct products of free and surface groups are profinitely rigid amongst finitely presented residually free groups. We survey during
Section

3.4
more applications that motivate the study of pro-
p
topologies of infinite groups. The proof of
B
relies on separability properties of nilpotent groups that are particular to this class (at least amongst the class of virtually polycyclic groups), as our next result exhibits.

Theorem C
.

Let
G
be a virtually polycyclic group. Then the following two statements are equivalent:

•

The group
G
is nilpotent.

•

For all subgroups
H\leqslant G
and all primes
p
, the pro-
p
topology of
G
induces on
H
its full pro-
p
topology.

One implication is proven in
3.2
and the other in
3.7
. Virtually polycyclic groups have been known to be subgroup separable for long
(after the work of Hirsch and Malcev
[
62
, Chapter 1]
). However, the situation about their pro-
p
topology does not seem to be as clear and, to the best of the author’s knowledge, this result has not appeared explicitly in the literature before.

We now put
A
in more context regarding previous research on relating subgroup structure and profinite completions, which so far has been done specially for three-manifolds.
For example, a remarkable result is the profinite recognition of the topology of geometric three-manifolds by Wilton–Zalesskii
[
77
]
, where they show that a compact orientable aspherical three-manifold
M
is hyperbolic if and only if
\widehat{\pi_{1}M}
contains no
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{2}
. This was generalised by Zalesskii
[
87
, Theorem 1.1]
, showing that a relatively hyperbolic virtually compact special group
G
is hyperbolic if and only if its profinite completion
\widehat{G}
contains no
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{2}
. In this sense, one of the difficulties of
A
is relating subgroup obstructions of an abstract group to subgroup obstructions of its pro-
p
completion in the flavour of the previous results but in the generality of finitely generated residually-
p
groups, where there is a priori no hierarchy at our disposal.

Note that
A
is not immediate even for residually free groups. An elementary property of residually free groups is that non-abelian two-generated subgroups are free (see
[
5
, Section 4]
). However, these can clearly contain the group
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
and, even in this context, one needs an additional argument to rule it out. One may expect the analogous result of
A
to hold for limit groups. As observed in
Remark

4.4
, our methods establish
A
for the
p
-genus of more hyperbolic limit groups, other than free and surface groups. However, we cannot solve the general case.

Question 1.1
.

Let
p
be a prime and let
G
be a group in the
p
-genus of a hyperbolic limit group
L
. Are two-generated subgroups of
G
free?

An important property of our argument is that, when
S
is a free or surface group,
S_{\widehat{p}}
is residually-(torsion-free nilpotent), proven in Propositions
2.15
and
2.16
. For a general limit group
L
, this feature about
L_{\widehat{p}}
is not known. Kochloukova–Zalesskii study in
[
39
, Theorem 4.2]
the residual properties of a class of pro-
p
analogues of limit groups (which includes, for example, pro-
p
completions of ICE groups). However, it is an open problem to determine whether their class contains the pro-
p
completions of all limit groups (we refer to
[
39
, Section 9]
for more open questions).

1.1.
Applications to profinite rigidity

It is a well-known conjecture of Remeslennikov that free groups are profinitely rigid (see, for example,
[
38
]
*Question 5.48 and
[
57
]
*Question 12). This can be naturally restated to include surface groups as follows.

Conjecture 1.2
.

Let
S
be either a free or a surface group and let
G
be a finitely generated residually finite group with
\widehat{G}\cong\widehat{S}
. Then
G\cong S
.

Wilton
[
82
,
83
]
solved
1.2
for limit groups
G
.
There is a second proof of
1.2
when
S
is a surface group and
G
is a finitely generated residually free group by Fruchter and the author
[
21
, Corollary D]
. In
D
, we establish
1.2
when
G
belongs to the following abelian hierarchy, which naturally includes limit groups (since these admit an abelian hierarchy terminating in free groups
[
63
]
*Theorem 4.1).

Definition 1.3
.

Let
{\bf\mathcal{H}_{\bf ab}}
denote the smallest class of groups such that

(1)

{\bf\mathcal{H}_{\bf ab}}
contains all finitely generated residually free groups.

(2)

If
A,B
are in
{\bf\mathcal{H}_{\bf ab}}
and
C
is a virtually abelian subgroup of
A
and
B
, then the amalgamated product
A*_{C}B
belongs to
{\bf\mathcal{H}_{\bf ab}}
.

(3)

If
A
is in
{\bf\mathcal{H}_{\bf ab}}
and
\theta\colon C\longrightarrow C^{\prime}
is an isomorphism between virtually abelian subgroups of
A
, then the HNN extension
A\,*_{C,\theta}
belongs to
{\bf\mathcal{H}_{\bf ab}}
.

(4)

If
A
is in
{\bf\mathcal{H}_{\bf ab}}
and
A
embeds as a finite-index subgroup in
B
, then
B
belongs to
{\bf\mathcal{H}_{\bf ab}}
.

Once we know that a group
G
as in
1.2
does not have Baumslag–Solitar subgroups by
A
and
[
35
, Theorem 1.1]
,
G
will be hyperbolic by the Bestvina–Feighn combination theorem
[
9
]
. From this, we follow the outline of the proof of Wilton (while also incorporating Wise–Haglund’s separability and structural properties of hyperbolic groups with a malnormal
quasi-convex hierarchy
[
25
,
86
]
) to prove the following generalisation.

Corollary D
.

1.2
holds for groups
G
belonging to the class
{\bf\mathcal{H}_{\bf ab}}
.

This is proven in Theorems
11.1
and
11.2
. There are several reasons why it is natural to study
{\bf\mathcal{H}_{\bf ab}}
, other than being a family that naturally incorporates other groups for which
1.2
was solved.
The consideration of hierarchies with abelian edges has provided a fruitful setting in which combination theorems for relevant classes of groups have been established: for coherent groups
[
85
, Corollary 5.4]
, for hyperbolic groups
[
9
]
, for free groups
[
66
,
71
,
68
]
and for parafree groups
[
31
]
. We pose a question that already seems to contain most of the difficulties that
1.2
presents for more complicated group hierarchies.

Question 1.4
.

Let
F
be a finitely generated free group and let
H
be a finitely generated malnormal subgroup. Consider the double
\Gamma=F*_{H=\overline{H}}\overline{F}
of the group
F
along its subgroup
H
. In particular,
\Gamma
is hyperbolic and virtually compact special by
[
22
, Corollary 5.3]
and
[
29
, Corollary B]
. Is
1.2
true for
\Gamma
?

Another class of groups where the conclusion of
A
ensures hyperbolicity is one-relator groups (by the advances of Louder–Wilton
[
47
,
48
]
and Linton
[
45
]
).
D
covers some one-relator groups such as amalgamated products or HNN extensions of free groups along cyclic subgroups, which have the form
\langle F_{1},F_{2}\,|\,u_{1}=u_{2}\rangle
and
\langle F,t\,|\,tut^{-1}=v\rangle
. Nevertheless, we do not know whether residually finite one-relator groups
G
with the same profinite completion as a free or surface group
S
necessarily satisfy
G\cong S
. Opportunely,
E
at least ensures that such
\Gamma
are hyperbolic and virtually special.

Corollary E
.

Let
p
be a prime and let
G
be a one-relator group in
p
-genus of a free or hyperbolic surface group. Then
G
is hyperbolic and virtually special.

Again we stress that
E
extends to other genera by
[
35
, Theorem 1.1]
. Our last result is
F
, which provides infinitely many examples of groups that are determined within the class of residually free groups by their finite quotients. We extend this class of groups using similar methods in
[
54
]
by looking at examples coming from three-manifold groups.

Theorem F
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated residually free group and let
S
be a free or surface group. Suppose that
\widehat{G}\cong\widehat{S}\times\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
. Then
G\cong S\times\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.

We prove this theorem in
Section

7
. The first step consists on showing that
G
and
S\times\mathbb{Z}^{n}
have isomorphic centre, for which we use the theory of
L^{2}
-Betti numbers. This does not occur in general because Lubotzky proved in
[
49
, Proposition 1.5]
that having a non-trivial centre is not a profinite property. As we mentioned above, our methods give an extension of
F
to direct products of free and surface groups, under the extra assumption of
G
being finitely presented, in a joint work with Fruchter
[
21
, Theorem E]
.

2.
Pro-
\mathcal{C}
groups, topologies and completions

A non-empty class of finite groups
\mathcal{C}
is a
formation
if it is closed under taking homomorphic images and subdirect products.
Given a finitely generated group
\Lambda
, we say that it is
residually-
\mathcal{C}
if, for all
1\neq\lambda\in\Lambda
, there exists
N\unlhd\Lambda
such that
\lambda\notin N
and
\Lambda/N\in\mathcal{C}
. In this case, following the terminology of
[
24
]
, we define the
\mathcal{C}
-genus
of
\Lambda
as the collection of finitely generated residually-
\mathcal{C}
groups
\Gamma
with isomorphic pro-
\mathcal{C}
completion
\Gamma_{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}\cong\Lambda_{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}
. Equivalently, the latter happens if
\Gamma
and
\Lambda
have the same collection of isomorphism types of finite quotients belonging to
\mathcal{C}
(
2.4
). We will mostly think of
\mathcal{C}
as being the formations of all finite groups, all finite soluble groups, all finite nilpotent groups or all finite
p
-groups (for some prime
p
). In these cases, we refer to the
\mathcal{C}
-genus as the finite, soluble, nilpotent and
p
-genus, respectively. Lastly, we say that a finitely generated residually finite
\Lambda
is
profinitely rigid
if the only
\Gamma
in its finite genus is
\Gamma\cong\Lambda
.

Notation 2.1
.

Given an abstract group
\Gamma
, we denote its profinite completion (resp. pro-
p
completion) by
\widehat{\Gamma}
(resp.
\Gamma_{\widehat{p}}
). Bold letters
{\bf G},{\bf H},{\bf K},{\bf U}
will denote profinite groups. In addition, we write
{\bf H}\leqslant_{c}{\bf G}
or
{\bf H}\leqslant_{o}{\bf G}
(resp.
{\bf H}\unlhd_{c}{\bf G}
or
{\bf H}\unlhd_{o}{\bf G}
) to indicate that the subgroup
{\bf H}\leqslant{\bf G}
(resp. normal subgroup
{\bf H}\unlhd{\bf G}
) is closed or open.

It will be important to keep in mind during the rest of the exposition the following theorem, which has already been discussed during the introduction.

Theorem 2.2

(Theorem 1.1,
[
35
]
)
.

Let
S
be a finitely generated free or surface group. Suppose that
G
belongs to the finite, soluble or nilpotent genus of
S
. Then
G
belongs to the
p
-genus of
S
for all primes
p
.

These inclusions between genera are not true in general, since Lubotzky
[
49
]
showed that the properties of being residually soluble or residually-
p
are not profinite invariants.

2.1.
Pro-
\mathcal{C}
completions

We now fix a formation
\mathcal{C}
of finite groups. A pro-
\mathcal{C}
group can be defined as a profinite group
{\bf G}
such that for all its open normal subgroups
{\bf U}\unlhd_{o}{\bf G}
, one has that
{\bf G}/{\bf U}\in\mathcal{C}
. Alternatively, a pro-
\mathcal{C}
group is just an inverse limit of groups belonging to
\mathcal{C}
that is endowed with the profinite topology.

In order to relate residual properties of abstract groups and profinite invariants, we recall the pro-
\mathcal{C}
completion functor. Given an abstract group
G
, its collection of quotients belonging to
\mathcal{C}
is encoded in its pro-
\mathcal{C}
completion
G_{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}
. As an abstract group, it is defined to be the inverse limit of finite groups

G_{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}=\varprojlim_{U}G/U

where
U
ranges over finite-index normal subgroups of
G
such that
G/U\in\mathcal{C}
. We always consider
G_{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}
endowed with the profinite topology, which turns it into a pro-
\mathcal{C}
group. There is a natural and canonical group homomorphism
\iota_{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}\colon G\longrightarrow G_{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}
and, in these terms, a group
G
is residually-
\mathcal{C}
if and only if
\iota_{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}
is injective. This map
\iota_{\mathcal{C}}
enjoys the following universal property (which, in fact, provides a more functorial definition of
G_{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}
).

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\Gamma
be an abstract group. Let
\iota_{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}\colon\Gamma\longrightarrow\Gamma_{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}
be the canonical map. The data
(\Gamma_{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}},\iota_{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}})
is characterised by the following properties.

(1)

\Gamma_{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}
is a pro-
\mathcal{C}
group.

(2)

The map
\iota_{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}
has dense image.

(3)

For any pro-
\mathcal{C}
group
{\bf K}
and every group homomorphism
f\colon\Gamma\longrightarrow{\bf K}
with dense image, there exists a continuous homomorphism
f_{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}\colon\Gamma_{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}\longrightarrow{\bf K}
such that the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Gamma}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Gamma_{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\bf K}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota_{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}}

is commutative.

When we say that the pair
(\Gamma_{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}},\iota_{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}})
is unique, we mean the following: if
{\bf H}
is a pro-
\mathcal{C}
group and
\iota\colon\Gamma\longrightarrow{\bf H}
is a group homomorphism such that the pair
({\bf H},\iota)
verifies the three properties above, then there exists an isomorphism of pro-
\mathcal{C}
groups
\alpha\colon\Gamma_{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}\longrightarrow{\bf H}
such that
\alpha\circ\iota_{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}=\iota
.

We stress the fact that the previous discussion does not rely on whether
\iota_{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}
is injective (equivalently
G
is residually-
\mathcal{C}
) or not. A priori the pro-
\mathcal{C}
completion only captures information about finite quotients of
G
in
\mathcal{C}
and not about how we can distinguish elements of
G
in these quotients. There are, for example, finitely generated residually finite groups with free pro-
p
completion for all primes
p
that are not residually-
p
for any prime
p
, such as the trefoil knot group
\langle a,b\,|a^{2}b^{3}\rangle
. The philosophy that finite quotients of a group are encoded in their profinite completion is precised in the following folkloric theorem.

Theorem 2.4

(Dixon–Formanek–Poland–Ribes
[
17
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a formation of finite groups. Two abstract groups
\Gamma
and
\Lambda
have the same class of isomorphism types of quotients belonging to
\mathcal{C}
if and only if
\Gamma_{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}\cong\Lambda_{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}
.

So we can completely reformulate in terms of the pro-
\mathcal{C}
completions what it means for a group to be in the
\mathcal{C}
-genus of another one. This is important because now we have turned a bare collection of finite groups into a group and now we can do, say, group cohomology with this information. Crucially, this functor “pro-
\mathcal{C}
completion” is right-exact.

Proposition 2.5

(
[
59
]
, Proposition 3.2.5)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a formation that is closed under taking normal subgroups. Given a exact sequence of abstract groups of the form
N\longrightarrow G\longrightarrow Q\longrightarrow 1
, the induced sequence of maps

N_{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}\longrightarrow G_{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}\longrightarrow Q_{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}\longrightarrow 1
is exact.

Given an injective map
N\longrightarrow G
, the question of whether the induced map
N_{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}\longrightarrow G_{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}
is also injective will be a crucial topic of discussion later in this section and during
Section

3
, especially with the aim of proving
A
. On the other hand,
2.5
also tells us that pro-
\mathcal{C}
completions are better understood when studying quotients than when studying subgroups, as the following direct consequence exhibits.

Proposition 2.6

(
Pro-
\mathcal{C}
completion of a quotient)
.

Let
G
be an abstract group and let
N\unlhd G
be a normal subgroup. Suppose that
\mathcal{C}
is a formation as in
2.5
. Denote by
\iota\colon N\longrightarrow G
the inclusion. Then the canonical and natural map

\left(G/N\right)_{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}\longrightarrow G_{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}\Big/\overline{\iota_{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}(N)}

is an isomorphism.

2.2.
The notion of index in profinite groups

Given a profinite group
{\bf G}
and a subgroup
{\bf H}
, we will not need for our purposes to define the formal meaning of
|{\bf G}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}{\bf H}|
as a
supernatural number
(as in, for example,
[
59
, Section 2.3]
).

However, recall we say that
p^{\infty}
divides
the index
|{\bf G}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}{\bf H}|
if and only if for all
n
there exists a normal open
{\bf U}\unlhd_{o}{\bf G}
such that
p^{n}
divides the index of
{\bf H}{\bf U}/{\bf U}
in the finite group
{\bf G}/{\bf U}
. Observe that, if
{\bf G}
is a pro-
p
group, then
{\bf H}\leqslant_{c}{\bf G}
has infinite index if and only if
p^{\infty}
divides the index
|{\bf G}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}{\bf H}|
. However, for general profinite groups, the latter is much stronger, as the following example shows.

Example 2.7
.

The profinite group
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
is canonically isomorphic to
\prod_{p}\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, where
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
denotes the additive part of the ring of
p
-adic integers (isomorphic to the pro-
p
completion of
\mathbb{Z}
). So
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
surjects in a natural way onto the profinite group
\prod_{p}\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
, where the kernel
{\bf K}
is isomorphic to
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
and will have an index in
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
equal to the supernatural number
\prod_{p}p
. In particular, no prime
p
will satisfy that
p^{2}
divides the index
|\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}{\bf K}|
.

2.3.
Nilpotent groups and lower central series

The terms of the
lower central series
of a group
G
are defined recursively as
\gamma_{1}G=G
and
\gamma_{n+1}G=[\gamma_{n}G,G]
if
n\geqslant 1
. Similarly, the terms of the
p
-
lower central series
of
G
are defined as
\gamma_{1,p}G=G
and
\gamma_{n+1,p}G=[\gamma_{n,p}G,G]\gamma_{n,p}G^{p}
if
n\geqslant 1
. The expression
\gamma_{n}\Gamma_{\widehat{p}}
may be ambiguous. We clarify that it means
\gamma_{n}G
with
G=\Gamma_{\widehat{p}}
, defined as a subgroup of
\Gamma_{\widehat{p}}
(so we do not mean to denote the pro-
p
completion of
\gamma_{n}\Gamma
).

A group
G
is
nilpotent
if there exists an integer
c\geqslant 0
such that
\gamma_{c+1}G=1
.
For example, abelian groups are nilpotent groups of nilpotency class 1. We will study the profinite and pro-
p
topology of nilpotent groups in Sections
2.4
and
3
.

Proposition 2.8

(
[
16
]
, Proposition 1.19 and Exercise 17)
.

Let
{\bf G}
be a finitely generated pro-
p
group. Then
\gamma_{k}{\bf G}
is a closed subgroup of
{\bf G}
.

In fact, the previous is true for finitely generated profinite groups, although this is a much deeper result due to Nikolov and Segal
[
56
, Theorem 1.4]
.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated group and let
k\geqslant 0
be an integer. Then the canonical map
\Gamma/\gamma_{k}\Gamma\longrightarrow\Gamma_{\widehat{p}}/\gamma_{k}\Gamma_{\widehat{p}}
is a pro-

Proof.

A useful result that relates the open subgroups of a group

Let

Let

Proposition 2.15

Let

Proof.

Since

is residually-

\bigcap_{k}\gamma_{k,p}G_{\widehat{p}}=\{1\}

\bigcap_{k}\gamma_{k}G_{\widehat{p}}=1

We know that

is a finitely generated torsion-free nilpotent group. We are going to prove that this is enough to conclude that its pro-

\Gamma_{\widehat{p}}
is torsion-free. The proof of the proposition would then be complete because the canonical map
\Gamma_{\widehat{p}}\longrightarrow G_{\widehat{p}}/\gamma_{k}G_{\widehat{p}}

Now let

be a finitely generated torsion-free nilpotent group. Then

is also torsion-free (see

). Then, iterating the previous observation, we deduce that the lower central series

of

Notice that the canonical maps
(\gamma_{k}\Gamma)_{\widehat{p}}\longrightarrow\gamma_{k}\Gamma_{\widehat{p}}
are isomorphisms (for example, by

(\gamma_{k+1}\Gamma/\gamma_{k}\Gamma)_{\widehat{p}}\longrightarrow\gamma_{k+1}\Gamma_{\widehat{p}}/\gamma_{k}\Gamma_{\widehat{p}}

So there is a lower central series of

by closed subgroups

for which each quotient

is torsion-free, as we claimed before.
∎

It is not hard to see that the class of groups satisfying the assumptions of

is closed under taking free products. However, the following proposition ensures many one-ended examples as well.

Proposition 2.16
.

Let

be isomorphic to a RAAG or to a one-relator group

such that if

Proof.

When

[

. When

, this proposition is a direct consequence of
[

. The reason is the following. The main theorem of

]
states that, under our assumptions on the relator

is a free

-Lie algebra. In particular, the subsequent quotients

are free abelian (the author also computes their rank). Since surface groups are defined by a single relator that satisfies the assumptions of

∎

We observe that the class of one-relator groups of

includes many limit groups. For example, those arising as doubles

of a free group

along a relator

that satisfies the assumptions of

in

3.

We return to analysing the structure and pro-

It was proven by Hirsch

that polycyclic groups are residually finite, and Malcev later in 1948 proved that they are, in fact, LERF

a pro-

Given a set of primes

if all the primes dividing

. We say that a group

-

, where

. Notice that, by Cauchy’s theorem, the set of primes

appearing as the orders of elements of a group

is exactly the smallest set of primes

such that every element with finite order in

has a

Theorem 3.1

(
[

]
, Theorem 2.1)
.

Let

be a finitely generated nilpotent group.

(a)

If

is torsion-free, then

is residually-

.

(b)

is residually-

.

3.1.
Induced pro-

Continuing the discussion at the beginning of the section, one may also try to extend the result of Malcev to the pro-

-LERF groups. A tentative definition is to say that a group

Theorem 3.2
.

Let

be a finitely generated nilpotent group and let

induces on

its full pro-

By

, the previous condition on each

Definition 3.3

Let

Let

Let

is finitely generated. So

is generated by a finite subset of

and that

∎

Proof of
3.2
.

We divide the proof in two steps. First, we prove in

that the conclusion follows if we additionally suppose that

is normal, and then we prove the general case when

is any subgroup.

Claim 3.5
.

The conclusion follows if

is normal in

.

Proof of
3.5
.

We follow the strategy depicted in

. Let

be a characteristic subgroup of

is finite and a

is normal in

. Let

be the collection of primes different from

and it contains

and that the finite group

We now finish the proof of the theorem. Consider any subgroup

3.2.

As we anticipated, although the profinite version of

Theorem 3.7
.

Let

be a virtually polycyclic group. Suppose that for every prime

, the pro-

induces on

is nilpotent.

3.7
in conjunction with

constitute

from the introduction. Before the proof of

, we start with a chain of preliminary lemmas.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let

be a finite

-group and let

be a finite

-group, with

and

being different primes. Let

The map

is trivial.

(2)

is isomorphic to

.

(3)

(4)

The subgroup

is pro-

embeddable into

.

The equivalence between (1) and (2) is well-known and follows from the definition of semi-direct product (since

, a finite group is nilpotent if and only it is a direct product of finite

, as well as

. So

and

are conjugated to each other by the second Sylow’s theorem, which shows that

is also normal and (1) follows.

.

Let

be a prime. Suppose that

If the induced maps

, a commutative diagram of the form

.

Let

, where each

(2)

Lemma 3.11
.

Let

be a finite group such that for every subgroup

and all primes

We can prove this by induction on the order of

We have gathered all the ingredients required to show

.

.

By

, any finite quotient

∎

3.3.
Pro-

We are ready to show that in residually-(torsion-free nilpotent) groups, polycyclic groups are pro-
p
embeddable (see
3.12
below). Before doing so, we remind the reader of several classical properties of the class of virtually polycyclic groups (we refer the reader to the book of D. Segal
[

]
for a more thorough account). This class is closed under taking subgroups, quotients and extensions; they are noetherian (i.e. all their subgroups are finitely generated); and, crucially, that they are endowed with the useful notion of Hirsch length, which can be defined as follows.

Given a virtually polycyclic group
G
and a subnormal series
1=G_{0}\unlhd G_{1}\unlhd\cdots\unlhd G_{n}=G
such that the successive quotients
G_{i+1}/G_{i}
are cyclic, the number of subscripts

such that
G_{i+1}/G_{i}
is infinite is an invariant of such a series (by the Jordan–Hölder theorem). We call this number the
Hirsch length
of

, denoted by

. Recall that
h(G)=0
if and only if
G
is finite and that
h(G)=h(N)+h(G/N)
for all normal subgroups
N\unlhd G
. In particular, a surjective map between torsion-free virtually polycyclic groups
G_{1}\longrightarrow G_{2}
with
h(G_{1})=h(G_{2})
must be an isomorphism. This will be important during the proof of the following.

Theorem 3.12

(
B
)
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated residually-(torsion-free nilpotent) group and let
H\leqslant G
be a virtually polycyclic subgroup. Then

is nilpotent and, for all primes

, the induced map
H_{\widehat{p}}\longrightarrow G_{\widehat{p}}
is injective.

Proof.

Let
\iota\colon H\longrightarrow G
denote the injection and let
\pi^{n}
, for any positive integer

, denote the canonical projection
\pi^{n}\colon G\longrightarrow G/\gamma_{n}G
.

Claim 3.13
.

There exists a positive integer
n
such that the composition
f^{n}=\pi^{n}\circ\iota\colon H\longrightarrow G/\gamma_{n}G
is injective.

We will show by induction on the Hirsch length of
H
the stronger statement that there exists a torsion-free nilpotent quotient
G/N
such that the projection
\pi_{N}\colon G\longrightarrow G/N
is injective on
H
. This is trivial if
h(H)=0
. If
h(H)>0
, there exists a subgroup
H_{1}\leqslant H
with
h(H_{1})=h(H)-1
. By the induction hyphotesis, there exists a normal subgroup
N\unlhd G
such that the surjection
\pi_{N}\colon G\longrightarrow G/N
is injective on
H_{1}
. In particular,
h(\pi_{N}(H))\geqslant h(H_{1})
. If
\pi_{N}
is already injective on

then we are done. Otherwise, there exists
1\neq x\in H\cap\ker\pi_{N}
. Since

is residually-(torsion-free nilpotent), there exists a normal subgroup
K\unlhd G
such that
\pi_{K}\colon G\longrightarrow G/K
is injective on the subgroup
\langle x\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}
. Now look at the induced surjection of torsion-free nilpotent groups
\pi_{N\cap K}(H)\longrightarrow\pi_{N}(H)
. It is not injective because
\pi_{N\cap K}(x)\neq 1
belongs to the kernel, so

\pi_{N\cap K}\colon H\longrightarrow\pi_{N\cap K}(H)
of torsion-free virtually polycyclic groups is injective, completing the proof of
3.13
. In particular,

is nilpotent.

Now we are ready to show that the induced map
H_{\widehat{p}}\longrightarrow G_{\widehat{p}}
is injective. Let
n\geqslant 1
be an integer and consider the following natural commutative diagram.

(3)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H_{\widehat{p}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G_{\widehat{p}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G/\gamma_{n}G}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G_{\widehat{p}}/\gamma_{n}G_{\widehat{p}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{n}}

Remark 3.15

4.
The

In this section we prove

Theorem 4.1
.

Let

be a prime and let

be a free or hyperbolic surface group. Suppose that

is a finitely generated residually-

. Assume that

If

(ii)

If

is two-generated, then it is free.

(iii)

If

is finitely presented and non-cyclic, then it is large.

The property (i) of this theorem will follow from (ii) by the following lemma.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated residually-(torsion-free polycyclic) group and let

be locally-cyclic. Then
H
is either trivial or infinite cyclic.

Proof.

If
H
is finitely generated, then the conclusion follows immediately. We proceed by contradiction. Suppose, otherwise, that
H
is not finitely generated. Take

. Since

is locally cyclic but not cyclic, there exists
a_{2}\notin\langle h_{1}\rangle
and also
h_{2}\in H
such that
\langle h_{2}\rangle=\langle h_{1},a_{2}\rangle
. So there exists

with
h_{2}^{n_{1}}=h_{1}
and, due to the fact that
a_{2}\notin\langle h_{1}\rangle
, we derive that
|n_{1}|>1
. Iterating this process, we show that then there must exist a sequence
(h_{i})_{i\geqslant 1}
in

of non-trivial elements and a sequence of integers
(n_{i})_{i\geqslant 1}
with
|n_{i}|>1
such that

for all

. Let
\phi\colon G\longrightarrow Q
be a surjective map to a torsion-free polycyclic group

such that
\phi(h_{1})\neq 1
(and hence
\phi(h_{i})\neq 1
for all

). Now consider the ascending chain of subgroups

\langle\phi(h_{1})\rangle\subseteq\langle\phi(h_{2})\rangle\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\langle\phi(h_{n})\rangle\subseteq\cdots

of

. Since

is noetherian, this sequence must stabilise. So there exists

such that
\phi(h_{k+1})\in\langle\phi(h_{k})\rangle=\langle\phi(h_{k+1})^{n_{k}}\rangle.
Since

, this implies that

has finite order in

, and so

, which is a contradiction.
∎

5.
The first

-Betti number as a pro-
\mathcal{C}
invariant

We will describe some scenarios in which the first
L^{2}
-Betti number of a group
G
, denoted by
b_{1}^{(2)}(G)
, is a profinite invariant. This plays an important role in the proof of
F
. An standard reference about
L^{2}
-invariants is the book of Lück
[
52
]
and, in alignment with the approach taken in this section, the survey paper of Jaikin-Zapirain
[
34
]
.

For torsion-free groups
G
satisfying the Strong Atiyah conjecture, Linnell
[
44
]
shows that one can define
b_{1}^{(2)}(G)
as follows. Suppose that the group ring
\mathbb{Q}G
of
G
with rational coefficients has a universal division ring of fractions
\mathbb{Q}G\xhookrightarrow{}\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{Q}G}
. This provides a notion of dimension for all
\mathbb{Q}G
-modules as follows: given a left
\mathbb{Q}G
-module
M
, we extend scalars
\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{Q}G}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}G}M
and take its linear-algebraic dimension as a left
\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{Q}G}
-module. We can define the first
L^{2}
-Betti number for these groups
G
as

b_{1}^{(2)}(G)=\dim_{\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{Q}G}}H_{1}(G;\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{Q}G}).

Since locally indicable groups satisfy the Strong Atiyah conjecture by a result of Jaikin-Zapirain and López-Álvarez
[
30
]
, the previous is a definition of
b_{1}^{(2)}
for these groups. A general philosophy that explains why we work with first
L^{2}
-Betti numbers instead of usual Betti numbers is that the former behave in a better way while containing similar information. For example, they are multiplicative in the following sense.

Proposition 5.1

(Theorem 1.35(9),
[
52
]
)
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated group and let
H
be a finite-index subgroup. Then
b_{1}^{(2)}(H)=b_{1}^{(2)}(G)\,|G\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}H|.

5.1.
Estimations using Lück approximation

When it comes to relate the first
L^{2}
-Betti number
b_{1}^{(2)}(G)
of a group
G
to its profinite completion
\widehat{G}
, it is particularly helpful the characterisation of
b_{1}^{(2)}(G)
as a limit of (normalised) usual Betti numbers over a filtration of
G
by normal finite-index subgroups.
A pioneering result in this direction is due to Lück
[
51
]
.

Theorem 5.2

(Lück’s approximation theorem)
.

Let
G
be a group of type
\mathtt{FP}_{2}
and let
G=N_{1}>N_{2}>\dots>N_{m}>\dots
be a sequence of finite-index normal subgroups with
\bigcap_{m}N_{m}=1
. Then

\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\frac{b_{1}(N_{m})}{|G\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}N_{m}|}=b_{1}^{(2)}(G).

Importantly, for residually-finite groups that are not
\mathtt{FP}_{2}
, we still have a one-sided estimate of the same type.

Theorem 5.3

(
[
50
]
)
.

Let
G
be finitely generated and let
G=N_{1}>N_{2}>\dots>N_{m}>\dots
be a sequence of finite-index normal subgroups with
\bigcap_{m}N_{m}=1
. Then

\limsup_{m\rightarrow\infty}\frac{b_{1}(N_{m})}{|G\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}N_{m}|}\leqslant b_{1}^{(2)}(G).

We move on to the observation that groups
G
satisfying the following inequality also have a Lück approximation theorem:

(4)

b_{1}(G_{0})\geqslant b_{1}^{(2)}(G_{0})+1\,\mbox{for every finite-index $G_{0}\leqslant G$.}

Examples of groups
G
with this property are limits of left-orderable amenable
groups in the space of marked group presentations (by a result of Osin
[
58
, Theorem 1.4]
).
Interestingly, the property of (
4
) can be proven in greater generality.

Proposition 5.4

(Jaikin-Zapirain
[
36
]
)
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated residually-(locally indicable and amenable) group. Then
b_{1}^{(2)}(G)\leqslant b_{1}(G)-1
.

For our purposes, the interest behind
5.4
is that it allows to extend the Lück approximation
5.2
of
b_{1}^{(2)}
to other classes of groups.

Corollary 5.5
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated residually-(amenable and locally indicable) group. Let
G=N_{1}>N_{2}>\dots>N_{m}>\dots
be a sequence of finite-index normal subgroups with
\bigcap_{m}N_{m}=1
. Then

(5)

\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\frac{b_{1}(N_{m})}{|G\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}N_{m}|}=b_{1}^{(2)}(G).

Proof.

We know from
5.3
that

\limsup_{m\rightarrow\infty}\frac{b_{1}(N_{m})}{|G\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}N_{m}|}\leqslant b_{1}^{(2)}(G).

On the other hand,
b_{1}(N_{m})\geqslant b_{1}^{(2)}(N_{m})+1=b_{1}^{(2)}(G)|G\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}N_{m}|+1
for all
m
by
5.4
and the multiplicativity of
b_{1}^{(2)}
(

). So

\liminf_{m\rightarrow\infty}\frac{b_{1}(N_{m})}{|G\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}N_{m}|}\geqslant b_{1}^{(2)}(G).

Hence the limit of (
5
) exists and equals
b_{1}^{(2)}(G)
.
∎

Definition 5.6
.

We say that a finitely generated residually finite group
G
is
\mathrm{L\ddot{u}cky}
if for every descending sequence
G=N_{1}>N_{2}>\dots>N_{m}>\dots
of finite-index normal subgroups
N_{m}
with
\bigcap_{m}N_{m}=1
, it satisfies that

\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\frac{b_{1}(N_{m})}{|G\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}N_{m}|}=b_{1}^{(2)}(G).

We summarise this subsection by saying that both
\mathtt{FP}_{2}
groups and finitely generated residually-(amenable and locally indicable) groups are
\mathrm{L\ddot{u}cky}
by

and

.

Theorem 6.6

Let

8.
Cohomological dimension and Poincaré duality

Here we recall the finiteness properties of groups that we shall use.
We denote by

a unital and associative ring (not necessarily commutative).

Definition 8.1
.

We say that an

is of type

if there exists a projective resolution

\longrightarrow P_{n+1}\longrightarrow P_{n}\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow P_{1}\longrightarrow P_{0}\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow 0

with

finitely generated for
0\leqslant i\leqslant n
. Moreover, we say that an
R
-module
M
is of type
\mathtt{FP}_{\infty}(R)
if it is of type
\mathtt{FP}_{n}(R)
for all
n
. Analogously, we say that a group
G
is of type
\mathtt{FP}_{n}(R)
(resp. of type
\mathtt{FP}_{\infty}(R)
) if the trivial
RG
-module

R
is of type
\mathtt{FP}_{n}(R)
(resp. of type
\mathtt{FP}_{n}(R)
for all

).

8.1.
Profinite groups

We refer the reader to
[
55
, Chapter 2, Section 7]
for details about the cohomology theory of profinite groups with profinite coefficient modules. Computations on profinite group cohomology can be reduced to analogous computations on abstract group cohomology via Serre’s fundamental notion of goodness
[
65
, Section I. 2. 6]
. A group is said to be
cohomologically good
(or simply
good
) if for every finite discrete
\widehat{G}
-module
A
, the natural homomorphism

H^{k}(\widehat{G};A)\longrightarrow H^{k}(G;A),

induced by
G\longrightarrow\widehat{G}
, is an isomorphism.
A group
G
is said to be
subgroup separable
or
LERF
(standing for
locally extended residually finite
) if every finitely generated subgroup
H\leqslant G
is closed in the profinite topology. This implies that the topology induced on
H
from the profinite topology of
G
is the full profinite topology of
H
. Hence, by
Lemma

2.13
, if
G
is LERF, then the natural map
\widehat{H}\longrightarrow\overline{H}\subseteq\widehat{G}
is an isomorphism of profinite groups for all finitely generated
H\leqslant G
.
Given a profinite group
{\bf G}
, the
completed group algebra of
{\bf G}
over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is a profinite ring defined as the inverse limit of the usual group rings
\left(\mathbb{Z}/p^{i}\mathbb{Z}\right)[{\bf G}/{\bf U}]
,
where
i\geqslant 0
and
{\bf U}
ranges over open normal subgroup of
{\bf G}
. We say that
{\bf G}
is of type
p
-
\mathtt{FP}_{n}
if the
\llbracket\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}{\bf G}\rrbracket
-module
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
(resp. the
\llbracket\mathbb{Z}_{p}{\bf G}\rrbracket
-module
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
) is of type
\mathtt{FP}_{n}
.

Proposition 8.2

(
[
33
]
, Proposition 3.1)
.

If
\Gamma
is an abstract
\mathtt{FP}_{\infty}
and good group then its profinite completion
\widehat{\Gamma}
is of type
p
-
\mathtt{FP}_{\infty}
for every prime
p
.

Recall we say a module
M
is
simple
if and only if it has exactly two submodules.

Definition 8.3
.

We say that a profinite group
{\bf G}
has
p
-cohomological dimension
n=\mathrm{cd}_{p}({\bf G})
if
n
is the largest non-negative integer
m
such that
H^{m+1}({\bf G},A)=0
for all simple discrete
\llbracket\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}{\bf G}\rrbracket
-modules that are annihilated by
p
.

Recall from
[
65
, Corollary 1 of Chapter 3, and Proposition 21]
that if
{\bf G}_{p}
is the
p
-Sylow of a profinite group
{\bf G}
, then
\mathrm{cd}_{p}({\bf G})=\mathrm{cd}_{p}({\bf G}_{p})
. Good groups
G
enjoy the convenient property that

\mathrm{cd}_{p}(\widehat{G})=\mathrm{cd}_{p}(G)\,\,\,\mbox{for all primes $p$.}

In particular, a free profinite group has
\mathrm{cd}_{p}=1
and profinite surfaces have
\mathrm{cd}_{p}=2
. We should remark that focusing on
p
-primary modules in the
8.3
of the “cohomological dimension function”
\mathrm{cd}_{p}
allows to avoid the pathology of
H^{2}(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}};\mathbb{Z})
being non-zero (as this is isomorphic to
H^{1}(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}},\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}

[
65
, Chapter 1, Section 3.2]
).
The dimension function
\mathrm{cd}_{p}
enjoys nice “geometric” properties. For example, by Shapiro’s lemma given a closed subgroup
{\bf H}
of a profinite group
{\bf G}
, we have
\mathrm{cd}_{p}({\bf H})\leqslant\mathrm{cd}_{p}({\bf G})
(see
[
59
, Theorem 7.3.1]
). Furthermore,
\mathrm{cd}_{p}
is sometimes additive with respect to short exact sequences (as reflected by
[
74
, Theorem 1.1]
).

A classical characteristic feature of groups of finite cohomological dimension is the following well-known lemma.

Lemma 8.4
.

Let
{\bf G}
be a profinite group with
\mathrm{cd}_{p}({\bf G})<\infty
for all primes
p
. Then
{\bf G}
is torsion-free.

This is esentially the only tool we have to recognise torsion-freeness of an abstract group from the profinite completion. Interestingly, Lubotzky shows that being torsion-free is not a profinite property (
[
49
, Proposition 1.5]
). The following is the analogous theorem of Stallings’
[
67
]
and Swan’s
[
70
]
for the category of pro-
p
groups.

Theorem 8.5

(Theorem 7.7.4,
[
59
]
)
.

Let
{\bf G}
be a pro-
p
group with
\mathrm{cd}_{p}({\bf G})\leqslant 1
. Then
{\bf G}
is a free pro-
p
group.

8.2.
Poincaré Duality and the recognition of surfaces

Eckman and Müller
[
18
]
proved that surface groups are the only Poincaré duality groups of dimension two. Before discussing the analogous principles for profinite groups, we recall the following definition.

Definition 8.6
.

Let
{\bf G}
be a profinite group of type
p
-
\mathtt{FP}_{\infty}
. We say that
{\bf G}
is a
Poincaré duality group at
p
of dimension
n
(written as
\mathtt{PD}^{n}
at
p
) if
\mathrm{cd}_{p}({\bf G})=n
and

\displaystyle H^{i}({\bf G},\llbracket\mathbb{Z}_{p}{\bf G}\rrbracket)=0\,\,\,\,\mbox{for $i\neq n$},

\displaystyle H^{i}({\bf G},\llbracket\mathbb{Z}_{p}{\bf G}\rrbracket)\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}\,\,\,\,\mbox{(as abelian groups).}

These denote the
continuous cochain cohomology
groups in the sense of
[
55
, Definition 2.7.1]
.

A pro-
p
group
{\bf G}
is
\mathtt{PD}_{1}
if and only if
{\bf G}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}
(see
[
72
, Example 4.4.4]
) and Poincaré duality pro-
p
groups of dimension 2 are known to coincide with the class of
Demushkin
groups (see
[
65
, Section I.4.5, Example 2]
), which is a class of one-relator pro-
p
groups that was classified by Demushkin, Serre and Labute
[
14
,
15
,
64
,
41
]
. However, this question is poorly understood for general profinite groups. Similarly, it is not known whether a finitely generated residually finite group whose profinite completion is
\mathtt{PD}_{2}
at a prime
p
is itself
\mathtt{PD}_{2}
. Wilton
[
83
]
showed that if such group is additionally residually free then it is a surface group, which we restate in
8.10
. This has been recently generalised in
[
32
]
with a different argument. In order to restate Wilton’s criterion, we require the following profinite variant of Strebel’s theorem
[
69
]
on infinite-index subgroups of
\mathtt{PD}_{n}
abstract groups (which can be found in
[
65
, Page 44, exercise 5(b)]
and
[
55
, Chapter 3, Section 7, Exercise 3]
).

Lemma 8.7

(Serre)
.

Let
{\bf G}
be a profinite
\mathtt{PD}_{n}
group at a prime
p
and let
{\bf H}
be a closed subgroup such that
p^{\infty}
divides the index
|{\bf G}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}{\bf H}|
. Then
\mathrm{cd}_{p}({\bf H})<n
.

The previous lemma was of fundamental importance in the work of Wilton–Zalesskii
[
78
]
on the profinite detection of prime and JSJ decompositions of three-manifolds.

Remark 8.8
.

When
{\bf G}
is a pro-
p
group,
8.7
only requires
{\bf H}
to have infinite index. Nevertheless, for general profinite groups
{\bf G}
, the conclusion of the theorem would not be true by only requiring infinite index. In fact, from
2.7
, we know that
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
, which is
\mathtt{PD}_{1}
at
p
, contains closed subgroups isomorphic to
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
of index
\prod_{p}p
.

The most natural source of examples of profinite Poincaré duality groups consists of profinite completions of abstract Poincaré duality groups, as the following result from
[
40
, Theorem 4.1]
ensures.

Theorem 8.9
.

Let
\Gamma
be an abstract cohomologically good
\mathtt{PD}_{n}
group. Then
\widehat{\Gamma}
is a profinite
\mathtt{PD}_{n}
group at every prime and
\Gamma_{\widehat{p}}
is
\mathtt{PD}_{n}
at
p
.

Finally, we are ready to give a more refined criterion to detect surfaces, used by Wilton
[
83
]
to establish
when
G
is a limit group.

Proposition 8.10
.

Let
p
be a prime and let
G
be a torsion-free residually finite group with separable cyclic subgroups such that its profinite completion
\widehat{G}
is
\mathtt{PD}_{2}
at
p
. Suppose that
G
contains a surface subgroup as a virtual retract. Then
G
is a surface group.

Proof.

By assumption, there exists a finite-index subgroup
L
of
G
containing a surface group
\pi_{1}\Sigma
as a retract. Let
K
be the kernel of the retraction
L\longrightarrow\pi_{1}\Sigma
. Our aim is to prove that
K=1
. This way, we would have that
L\cong\pi_{1}\Sigma
and that the torsion-free group
G
would be virtually a surface group. So the conclusion would follow from Kerckhoff’s solution to the Nielsen realisation problem for surfaces.

Hence, it remains to show that
K=1
. We proceed by contradiction. Suppose that
K\neq 1
. The retraction
\pi_{1}\Sigma\xhookrightarrow{}L\longrightarrow\pi_{1}\Sigma
induces a retraction
\widehat{\pi_{1}\Sigma}\xhookrightarrow{}\widehat{L}\longrightarrow\widehat{\pi_{1}\Sigma}
at the level of the profinite completions. Take
1\neq k\in K
. Since
G
is torsion-free and cyclic subgroups are separable, then
\langle k\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}
and the supernatural number
p^{\infty}
divides the order of
\overline{\langle k\rangle}\cong\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
. Since
k
is contained in the kernel
{\bf K}
of the retraction
\widehat{L}\longrightarrow\widehat{\pi_{1}\Sigma}
, then
|{\bf K}|=|\overline{\langle k\rangle}||{\bf K}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\overline{\langle k\rangle}|
is also divisible by
p^{\infty}
. We now claim that
p^{\infty}
also divides the index
|\widehat{L}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\widehat{\pi_{1}\Sigma}|
. This way, it would also divide the index
|\widehat{G}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\widehat{\pi_{1}\Sigma}|
and, by
8.7
, the
p
-cohomological dimension of
\widehat{\pi_{1}\Sigma}
should be at most
\mathrm{cd}_{p}(\widehat{G})-1=1
, which is a contradiction.

Thus, it simply remains to prove that
p^{\infty}
divides
|\widehat{L}\!\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\!\widehat{\pi_{1}\Sigma}|
. From the comments above, we know that, for every
n\geqslant 1
, there exists a characteristic open subgroup
{\bf U}\unlhd_{o}{\bf K}
such that
p^{n}
divides
|{\bf K}\!\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\!{\bf U}|
. We observe that
{\bf H}={\bf U}\cdot\widehat{\pi_{1}\Sigma}
is an open subgroup of
\widehat{L}
. After taking a normal open subgroup
{\bf H}_{0}\unlhd_{o}\widehat{L}
contained in
{\bf H}
(for example, its core), we are going to check that
p^{n}
divides
|\widehat{L}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}{\bf H}_{0}\cdot\widehat{\pi_{1}\Sigma}|
. For this, is suffices to notice that the previous index is divisible by

|\widehat{L}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}{\bf H}\cdot\widehat{\pi_{1}\Sigma}|=|{\bf K}\cdot\widehat{\pi_{1}\Sigma}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}{\bf U}\cdot\widehat{\pi_{1}\Sigma}|=|{\bf K}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}{\bf U}|,

since
{\bf U}\unlhd_{o}{\bf K}
and
{\bf K}\cap\widehat{\pi_{1}\Sigma}=\{1\}
. We chose
{\bf U}
so
p^{n}
divides
|{\bf K}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}{\bf U}|
, so this proves that
p^{\infty}
divides
|\widehat{L}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\widehat{\pi_{1}\Sigma}|
, as we wanted.
∎

For the purpose of proving

using
8.10
, we remark that the profinite completion of a surface
\widehat{\pi_{1}\Sigma}
is
\mathtt{PD}_{2}
at
p
for all primes
p

]
for more examples and discussion. A group is said to be
special
(resp.
compact special
) if it is the fundamental group of a non-positively curved (resp. compact) cube complex that satisfies Haglund–Wise’s special condition on its hyperplanes. We will not delve into this definition because we are only interested in the retraction properties of these groups (as reflected by
9.3
) and their consequences in the proof of
D
. Nevertheless, in order to offer a more precise idea of what these groups look like, we recall the following alternative description.

Theorem 9.1

(
[
25
]
, Theorem 4.2)
.

A group
G
is special (resp. compact special) if and only if it is a subgroup (resp. a quasiconvex subgroup) of a right-angled Artin group.

As anticipated in the introduction, many groups from
{\bf\mathcal{H}_{\bf ab}}
will be hyperbolic and virtually compact special.

Proposition 9.2
.

Let
G
be a group belonging to
{\bf\mathcal{H}_{\bf ab}}
with the following properties:

(1)

It is torsion-free.

(2)

It contains no Baumslag–Solitar subgroups.

(3)

Its abelian subgroups are cyclic.

Then
G
is hyperbolic and virtually compact special.

Proof.

The group
G
is hyperbolic by
6.3
. Now the conclusion that
G
is virtually compact special follows directly from Wise’s classification of hyperbolic groups with a malnormal
quasi-convex hierarchy
[
86
, Theorem 11.2]
.
∎

From the proof of
[
25
, Theorem 7.3]
, we know that quasi-convex subgroups of hyperbolic compact special groups are virtual retracts (recall
6.5
).

Theorem 9.3
.

Let
G
be a hyperbolic virtually compact special group and let
H\leqslant G
be a quasi-convex subgroup. Then there exists a finite-index subgroup
H^{\prime}
of
H
such that
H^{\prime}
is a virtual retract of
G
.

We can use Wilton’s partial solution to Gromov’s surface conjecture (
6.2
) to find surface subgroups in certain groups of
{\bf\mathcal{H}_{\bf ab}}
that will be relevant for the proof of
D
.

Proposition 9.4
.

Let
G
be as in
9.2
. Suppose that
G
is not free. Then
G
contains a surface subgroup as a virtual retract.

Proof.

By
9.2
,
G
is hyperbolic and virtually compact special. So, by
9.3
, in order to conclude that
G
contains a hyperbolic surface subgroup as a virtual retract, it is enough to ensure that
G
contains a quasi-convex surface subgroup. Furthermore, we know that for finitely generated subgroups of a hyperbolic group, being quasi-convex and quasi-isometrically embedded is equivalent. Hence, it suffices to ensure that there exists a quasi-isometrically embedded surface subgroup in
G
to prove
9.4
, which we prove by induction on the level of the hierarchy.

Let us start with the level 0, that is, when
G
is a finitely generated residually free group. Since
G
contains no
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, then
G
is limit by
6.4
and the statement follows directly from
6.2
. Now we establish the inductive step by distinguishing two cases.

•

Suppose that
G=A*_{C}B
(resp.
G=A_{C,\theta}
for some injection
\theta\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}C\longrightarrow A
), where
A
and
B
(resp.
A
) are groups in
{\bf\mathcal{H}_{\bf ab}}
for which the statement holds and
C
is cyclic. Since both
A
and
B
are quasi-isometrically embedded in
G
(for example, by
[
37
, Theorem 1.2]
), then
G
will contain a quasi-isometrically embedded surface if at least one of
A
or
B
does. So it remains to ensure the conclusion in the case when
A
and
B
(resp.
A
) are free. Viewing
G
as the fundamental group of a graph of two free groups (resp. one free group) with infinite cyclic edge groups, we apply a variant of Shenitzer’s theorem
[
81
, Theorem 18]
to deduce that, if
G
is not one-ended, then some vertex will split freely relative to its incident edge groups. In particular, applying this result a finite number of times (and taking out an infinite cyclic free factor of
G
each time), we deduce that
G
is isomorphic to the free product
F*G^{\prime}
for a free group
F
and a one-ended group
G^{\prime}=A^{\prime}*_{C}B^{\prime}
(resp.
G=A^{\prime}_{C,\theta}
for the injection
\theta\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}C\longrightarrow A^{\prime}
), where
A^{\prime}
and
B^{\prime}
are free factors of
A
and
B
(resp.
A^{\prime}
is a free factor of
A
). Since
G
was hyperbolic, and
G^{\prime}
is a quasi-convex subgroup, then
G^{\prime}
is also hyperbolic. So
G^{\prime}
is a hyperbolic one-ended fundamental group of a graph of free groups with cyclic edges. By
6.2
, the group
G^{\prime}
(and hence also
G
) contains a quasi-convex surface subgroup.

•

Suppose that
G
has a finite-index subgroup
H\in{\bf\mathcal{H}_{\bf ab}}
for which the statement is already true. Since
G
is torsion-free and not free, then
H
is not free by
[
67
, Theorem 2]
. So
H
(and hence also
G
) must contain a surface subgroup that is a virtual retract. ∎

10.
Pro-
p

We recall that by

, groups in the finite, soluble or nilpotent genus of a free or surface group
S

p

for all primes

. So all the results here about any

-genus of

10.1.
Lücky groups

It is generally not well understood if being Lücky (
5.6
) is a profinite invariant (see
10.2
below). However, Jaikin-Zapirain
[
35
]
can answer affirmatively to this question for the genera of free and surface groups as a consequence of the following.

Proposition 10.1
.

Let
S
be a free or surface group and let
G
be an abstract subgroup of
S_{\widehat{p}}
. Then
G
is
\mathrm{L\ddot{u}cky}
.

Proof.

By
2.15
and
2.16
,
G
is residually-(torsion-free nilpotent). By
5.5
,
G
is
\mathrm{L\ddot{u}cky}
.
∎

It is not difficult to see that the previous
G
have to be RFRS (there is, in fact, a stronger statement in
[
35
, Proposition 4.1]
).

Question 10.2
.

Let
\Gamma
and
\Lambda
be finitely generated residually finite groups with
\widehat{\Gamma}\cong\widehat{\Lambda}
. If
\Gamma
is
\mathrm{L\ddot{u}cky}
, does it follow that
\Lambda
is
\mathrm{L\ddot{u}cky}
?

This relates the question of whether being RFRS is a profinite property.

Question 10.3
.

Let
\Gamma
and
\Lambda
be finitely generated residually finite groups with
\widehat{\Gamma}\cong\widehat{\Lambda}
. If
\Gamma
is RFRS, does it follow that
\Lambda
is RFRS?

10.2.
Hyperbolic and special groups

Here we see how
4.1
allows us to study when groups from
{\bf\mathcal{H}_{\bf ab}}
(
1.3
) or one-relator groups can be proven to be hyperbolic and virtually special by looking at their pro-
p
completion.

Corollary 10.4
.

Let
p
be a prime and let
G
be a group in the class
{\bf\mathcal{H}_{\bf ab}}
that belongs to the
p
-genus of a free or hyperbolic surface group. Then
G
is hyperbolic and virtually compact special.

Proof.

By
4.1
,
G
is torsion-free, contains no Baumslag–Solitar groups and contains no non-cyclic abelian subgroups. Hence, by
9.2
,
G
is hyperbolic virtually compact special.
∎

Recently, Linton
[
45
, Theorem 8.2]
established that all one-relator groups with no negative immersions are hyperbolic and virtually special. By
[
47
, Theorem 1.3]
, a one-relator group that is two-free has no negative immersions. Hence the following is a consequence of these results and
4.1
.

Corollary 10.5

(
E
)
.

Let
G
be a one-relator group that belongs to the
p
-genus of a free group or a hyperbolic surface group. Then
G
is hyperbolic and virtually special.

11.
On the profinite rigidity of free and surface groups

We are now ready to establish the profinite rigidity of free and surface groups within the family
{\bf\mathcal{H}_{\bf ab}}
. The proof of
D
is divided in
11.1
and
11.2
.

Theorem 11.1
.

Let
G
be a group belonging to the class
{\bf\mathcal{H}_{\bf ab}}
and let
F
be a finitely generated free group. If
G
is residually finite and
\widehat{G}\cong\widehat{F}
, then
G\cong F
.

Proof.

Since free groups are distinguished from each other by their abelianisation, it suffices to show that
G
is free. Let
G
be a group in
{\bf\mathcal{H}_{\bf ab}}
that belongs to the finite genus of a free group
F
. By
[
35
, Theorem 1.1]
,
G
also belongs to the
p
-genus of
F
. Consequently, by
10.4
,
G
is hyperbolic virtually compact special. To prove that
G
is free, we proceed by contradiction. Suppose that is
G
is not free. Then, by
9.4
, there exists a surface subgroup
\pi_{1}S
that it is a virtual retract of
G
. In particular,
\widehat{\pi_{1}S}\xhookrightarrow{}\widehat{G}\cong\widehat{F}
. This implies by the goodness of the surface
\pi_{1}S
and the monotonicity of
\mathrm{cd}_{p}
the following contradiction:

2=\mathrm{cd}_{p}(\pi_{1}S)=\mathrm{cd}_{p}(\widehat{\pi_{1}S})\leqslant\mathrm{cd}_{p}(\widehat{F})=1.\qed

Theorem 11.2
.

Let
G
be a group belonging to the class
{\bf\mathcal{H}_{\bf ab}}
and let
\pi_{1}\Sigma
be the fundamental group of a closed orientable surface. If
G
is residually finite and
\widehat{G}\cong\widehat{\pi_{1}\Sigma}
, then
G\cong\pi_{1}\Sigma
.

Proof.

Since surface groups are distinguished from each other by their abelianisation, it suffices to show that
G
is a surface group. Let
G
be a group in
{\bf\mathcal{H}_{\bf ab}}
that belongs to the finite genus of a free group
\pi_{1}\Sigma
. By
[
35
, Theorem 1.1]
,
G
also belongs to the
p
-genus of
\pi_{1}\Sigma
. Consequently, by
10.4
,
G
is hyperbolic virtually compact special. Since
G
cannot be free, it follows from
9.4
that there exists a surface subgroup
\pi_{1}S
that it is a virtual retract of
G
. Lastly, by
8.10
,
G
is a surface group.
∎

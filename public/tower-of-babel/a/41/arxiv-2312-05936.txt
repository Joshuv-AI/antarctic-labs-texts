1.
Introduction

In
[
11
]
, Srinivasa Ramanujan gave Fourier series like expansions for certain arithmetical functions. These pointwise expansions were of the form
f(n)=\sum\limits_{q=1}^{\infty}a_{q}c_{q}(n)
where
a_{q}
are complex numbers and
c_{q}(n)
denotes the
Ramanujan sum
defined as

\displaystyle c_{q}(n):=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}{m=1}\\
(m,q)=1\end{subarray}}^{q}e^{\frac{2\pi imn}{q}}.

Later, A. Wintner
[
12
]
proved that if

(1)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{|f^{\prime}(k)|}{k}<\infty,

where

(2)

\displaystyle f^{\prime}(k)=\sum\limits_{d|k}\mu(d)f\left(\frac{k}{d}\right)

and
\mu
is the Möbius function,
then with
a_{q}=\sum\limits_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{f(mq)}{mq}
,
\sum\limits_{q=1}^{\infty}a_{q}c_{q}(n)
converges absolutely to
f(n)
.

H. Delange improved the inequality (
1
) in
[
4
]
. He proved that the absolute convergence of
\sum\limits_{q=1}^{\infty}a_{q}c_{q}(n)
holds with the weaker condition
\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}2^{\omega(k)}\frac{|f^{\prime}(k)|}{k}<\infty
where
\omega(k)
is the number of distinct prime divisors of
k
. The most important part of his proof to establish this was the inequality
\sum\limits_{q|k}|c_{q}(n)|\leq n\,2^{\omega(k)}
. Later, A. Grytczuk gave an exact equality for the sum
\sum\limits_{q|k}|c_{q}(n)|
in
[
7
]
. He used this equality to prove that the absolute convergence implication given by H. Delange has an easy converse (
[
5
, Theorem 1]
).

Ramanujan sum was generalized by many later. E. Cohen gave a generalization of the Ramanujan sum in
[
2
]
. He defined the sum

(3)

\displaystyle c_{q}^{s}(n):=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}{h=1}\\
(h,q^{s})_{s}=1\end{subarray}}^{q^{s}}e^{\frac{2\pi inh}{q^{s}}},

where
(a,b)_{s}
is the generalized gcd of
a
and
b
. A. Chandran and K. V. Namboothiri derived some conditions for the existence of Ramanujan series like expansions for certain arithmetical functions in terms of the generalized Ramanujan sum (
3
) (henceforth called as the
Cohen-Ramanujan sum
) in
[
1
]
. The method of arguments they used were those employed by L. Lucht in
[
9
]
and
[
10
]
. We will call the expansions in terms of the Cohen-Ramanujan sums by the name
Cohen-Ramanujan expansions
.

The aim of this paper is to show that the bounds and other results mentioned above proved by H. Delange and A. Grytczuk have natural generalizations involving Cohen-Ramanujan sums. Hence we will give a bound and an exact value for the sum
\sum\limits_{q|k}|c_{q}^{(s)}(n)|
and use them to give a sufficient condition for the Cohen-Ramanujan expansions to exist.

2.
Notations and basic results

By
(a,b)_{s}
, we mean the
generalized GCD
of
a
and
b
defined to be the largest
d^{s}\in\mathbb{N}
(
d\in\mathbb{N}
) such that
d^{s}|a
and
d^{s}|b
. Hence
(a,b)_{1}
is the usual GCD
(a,b)
. By
J_{s}
we mean the
Jordan totient function
defined as
J_{s}(n):=n^{s}\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}{p\mid n}\end{subarray}}(1-\frac{1}{p^{s}})
. Note that
J_{1}(n)=\varphi(n)
where
\varphi
is the Euler totient function.

The Cohen-Ramanujan sum
(or
generalized Ramanujan sum
) given by E. Cohen in
[
2
]
is the sum given in equation (
3
).
By (
[
2
, Theorem 1]
),
c_{q}^{(s)}(n)
is multiplicative in
q
. When
q
is a prime power, the values of the Cohen-Ramanujan sum are as folows (
[
2
, Theorem 3]
).

(4)

\displaystyle c_{p^{j}}^{(s)}(n)=\begin{cases}p^{sj}-p^{s(j-1)}&\quad\text{if }p^{sj}|n\\
-p^{s(j-1)}&\quad\text{if }p^{s(j-1)}||n\\
0&\quad\text{if }p^{s(j-1)}\nmid n.\end{cases}

Cohen-Ramanujan sum satisfies the following relation with the Möbius function
\mu
(
[
2
, Theorem 3]
):

(5)

\displaystyle c_{r}^{s}(n)=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d|r\\
d^{s}|n\end{subarray}}\mu(r/d)d^{s}.

Also, with the Jordan totient function, it holds the relation (
[
3
, Theorem 2]
)

(6)

\displaystyle c_{r}^{(s)}(n)=\frac{J_{s}(n)\mu(m)}{J_{s}(m)}\quad\text{ where }m=\frac{n}{(r,n)}.

Sometimes we club the conditions
d|r
and
d^{s}|n
into the single condition
d^{s}|(n,r^{s})_{s}
.

By the notation
d^{m}||n
we mean that
d^{m}|n
and
d^{m+1}\nmid n
.
For a prime
p
, the exponent of
p
in the prime factorization of
k
will be denoted by
e_{p}(k)
. Hence
p^{e_{p}(k)}||k
. Analogously, we write
a:=e_{p}^{(s)}(n)
if
p^{as}|n
and
p^{(a+1)s}\nmid n
. In this case, we also use the notation
p^{as}||_{s}n
.
We use the Möbius inversion formula for the Dirichlet product of two arithmetical functions which states that

(7)

\displaystyle g(k)=(\mu*f)(k):=\sum\limits_{d|k}\mu(d)f\left(\frac{k}{d}\right)\Longleftrightarrow f(k)=(g*u)(k)=\sum\limits_{d|k}g(k)

where
u\equiv 1
is the unit function defined by
u(n)=1
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

3.
Main Results and proofs

We start this section giving a generalization of the bound
\sum\limits_{q|k}|c_{q}(n)|\leq n\,2^{\omega(k)}
given by H. Delange in
[
4
]
.

Theorem 3.1
.

The bound
\sum\limits_{q|k}|c_{q}^{(s)}(n)|\leq n.2^{\omega(k)}
holds for all positive integers
k,n
, and
s
.

Proof.

Since
c_{q}^{(s)}(n)
is multiplicative in
q
,
|c_{q}^{(s)}(n)|
is also multiplicative in
q
. Then
h_{n}(k)=(c_{\bullet}^{(s)}(n)*u)(k)=\sum\limits_{q|k}|c_{q}^{(s)}(n)|u(k/q)=\sum\limits_{q|k}|c_{q}^{(s)}(n)|
is multiplicative in
k
it being the Dirichlet product two multiplicative functions. Hence we may write
h_{n}(k)=h_{n}(\prod\limits_{p|k}p^{e_{p}(k)})=\prod\limits_{p|k}h_{n}(p^{e_{p}(k)})
. Now for any positive integer
r
, we have
h_{n}(p^{r})=\sum\limits_{q|p^{r}}|c_{q}^{(s)}(n)|=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{r}|c_{p^{j}}^{(s)}(n)|
.
If
p^{s}\nmid n
, then by property
4
,
c_{p}^{(s)}(n)=-1
and
c_{p^{j}}^{(s)}(n)=0
for any
j>1
. By identity
5
,
c_{1}^{(s)}(n)=1
. Therefore, if
p^{s}\nmid n
, then
h_{n}(p^{r})=|c_{1}^{(s)}(n)|+|c_{p}^{(s)}(n)|=2
.

Now suppose that
p^{s}|n
. If
p^{rs}|n
, that is,
r\leq e_{p}^{(s)}(n)
, then

\displaystyle h_{n}(p^{r})=

\displaystyle|c_{1}^{(s)}(n)|+|c_{p}^{(s)}(n)|+\ldots+|c_{p^{r}}^{(s)}(n)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+|p^{s}-1|+|p^{2s}-p^{s}|+\ldots+|p^{rs}-p^{(r-1)s)}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p^{rs}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2p^{e_{p}^{(s)}(n)s}.

On the other hand, if
e_{p}^{(s)}(n)=r-m
for some positive integer
m
, then

\displaystyle h_{n}(p^{r})=

\displaystyle|c_{1}^{(s)}(n)|+|c_{p}^{(s)}(n)|+\ldots+|c_{p^{r-m}}^{(s)}(n)|+|c_{p^{r-m+1}}^{(s)}(n)|+\ldots+|c_{p^{r}}^{(s)}(n)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+|p^{s}-1|+\ldots+|p^{(r-m)s}-p^{(r-m+1)s}|+|-p^{(r-m)s}|+0+0+\ldots

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2p^{(r-m)s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2p^{e_{p}^{(s)}(n)s}.

Therefore
h_{n}(p^{r})=2
if
p^{s}\nmid n
and
h_{n}(p^{r})\leq 2p^{e_{p}^{(s)}(n)s}
if
p^{s}|n
.
Hence for distinct primes
p
, we get

\displaystyle h_{n}(k)

\displaystyle=\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}p|k\\
p^{s}|n\end{subarray}}h_{n}(p^{e_{p}(k)})\times\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}p|k\\
p^{s}\nmid n\end{subarray}}h_{n}(p^{e_{p}(k)})

\displaystyle\leq\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}p|k\\
p^{s}|n\end{subarray}}2p^{e_{p}^{(s)}(n)s}\times\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}p|k\\
p^{s}\nmid n\end{subarray}}2

\displaystyle\leq\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{s}|n\end{subarray}}p^{e_{p}^{(s)}(n)s}\times\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}p|k\end{subarray}}2

\displaystyle\leq n\,2^{\omega(k)}

∎

Remark 3.2
.

For equality to hold in the above, it should be true that
p|k\Rightarrow p^{s}|n
and
p^{r}||k\Rightarrow r>e_{p}^{(s)}(n)
. In short, the bound is an equality when
n=m^{s}
and
k
is a multiple of
m\prod\limits_{p|m}p
. So the bound in the theorem is the best possible.

Let
f^{\prime}
be defined by equation
2
. We now derive a sufficient condition for the Cohen-Ramanujan series expansions to exist for
f
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
a_{q}=\sum\limits_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{f^{\prime}(mq)}{(mq)^{s}}
. If
\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}2^{\omega(k)}\frac{|f^{\prime}(k)|}{k^{s}}<\infty
, then
\sum\limits_{q=1}^{\infty}a_{q}c_{q}^{(s)}(n^{s})
converges absolutely to
f(n)
.

Proof.

We will first prove the absolute convergence of
\sum\limits_{q=1}^{\infty}a_{q}c_{q}^{(s)}(n^{s})
assuming the bound given. We have

\displaystyle|\sum\limits_{q=1}^{\infty}a_{q}c_{q}^{(s)}(n^{s})|

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{m,q\geq 1}^{\infty}\frac{|f^{\prime}(mq)|}{(mq)^{s}}|c_{q}^{(s)}(n^{s})|

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{mq=k}\frac{|f^{\prime}(k)|}{k^{s}}|c_{q}^{(s)}(n^{s})|

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{|f^{\prime}(k)|}{k^{s}}\sum\limits_{q|k}|c_{q}^{(s)}(n^{s})|

\displaystyle\leq n^{s}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{|f^{\prime}(k)|}{k^{s}}2^{\omega(k)}

which is bounded by the assumption.
Now we will show that the series converges to
f(n)
.

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{q=1}^{\infty}a_{q}c_{q}^{(s)}(n^{s})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{m,q\geq 1}^{\infty}\frac{f^{\prime}(mq)}{(mq)^{s}}c_{q}^{(s)}(n^{s})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{f^{\prime}(k)}{k^{s}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}mq=k\end{subarray}}c_{q}^{(s)}(n^{s}).

Fix
k
. With thee notation
e(x)=exp(2\pi ix)
, we have

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}mq=k\\
m=1\end{subarray}}^{k}c_{q}^{(s)}(n^{s})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{m|k}\,\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(h,k^{s}/m^{s})_{s}=1\\
h=1\end{subarray}}^{k^{s}/m^{s}}e(\frac{hn^{s}}{k^{s}/m^{s}})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{m|k}\,\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(h,k^{s})_{s}=m^{s}\\
h=1\end{subarray}}^{k^{s}}e(\frac{hn^{s}}{k^{s}})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{h=1}^{k^{s}}e(\frac{hn^{s}}{k^{s}})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}k^{s}\quad\text{if }k^{s}|n^{s}\text{ or equivalently, if }k|n\\
0\quad\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Therefore

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{q=1}^{\infty}a_{q}c_{q}^{(s)}(n^{s})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\
k|n\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{f^{\prime}(k)}{k^{s}}k^{s}=f(n)\text{ by the Mobius inversion formula}.

∎

For the sum discussed in Theorem
3.1
, A. Grytczuk (
[
7
]
) gave the equality

\sum\limits_{q|k}|c_{q}(n)|=2^{\omega(\frac{k}{(k,n)})}(k,n).

We will derive an analogous equality in the next theorem and use it to give a converse for the above theorem, as Grytczuk did in
[
5
, Section 2]
.

Theorem 3.4
.

For positive integers
k,s
, and
n
, we have

(8)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{d|k}|c_{d}^{(s)}(n)|=2^{\omega\left(\frac{k^{s}}{(k^{s},n)_{s}}\right)}(k^{s},n)_{s}.

Proof.

Note that
(a,b)_{s}
is multiplicative in both
a
and
b
as a single variable function. Now if
(k,l)=1
, then

\displaystyle\frac{k^{s}l^{s}}{(k^{s}l^{s},n)_{s}}=\frac{k^{s}}{(k^{s},n)_{s}}\frac{l^{s}}{(l^{s},n)_{s}}

so that

\displaystyle 2^{\omega\left(\frac{k^{s}l^{s}}{(k^{s}l^{s},n)_{s}}\right)}=2^{\omega\left(\frac{k^{s}}{(k^{s},n)_{s}}\frac{l^{s}}{(l^{s},n)_{s}}\right)}.

Since
(k,l)=1
, so is
\frac{k^{s}}{(k^{s},n)_{s}}
and
\frac{l^{s}}{(l^{s},n)_{s}}
and so the above is equal to

\displaystyle 2^{\omega\left(\frac{k^{s}}{(k^{s},n)_{s}}\right)+\omega\left(\frac{l^{s}}{(l^{s},n)_{s}}\right)}=2^{\omega\left(\frac{k^{s}}{(k^{s},n)_{s}}\right)}\,2^{\omega\left(\frac{l^{s}}{(l^{s},n)_{s}}\right)}

so that the right hand side of equation (
8
) is multiplicative. Now the left hand side of this equation is
h_{n}(k)
in the notation used in the proof of Theorem
3.1
and it multiplicative. Hence it enough to prove our claim when
k
is a prime power.

Let
k=p^{r}
for some positive integer
r
. We have to prove that
h_{n}(p^{r})=2^{\omega\left(\frac{p^{rs}}{(p^{rs},n)_{s}}\right)}(p^{rs},n)_{s}
for this
k
. We have already seen that if
p^{s}\nmid n
, then
h_{n}(p^{r})=2
. In this case,
(p^{rs},n)_{s}=1
and so
2^{\omega\left(\frac{p^{rs}}{(p^{rs},n)_{s}}\right)}(p^{rs},n)_{s}=2=h_{n}(p^{r})
. Now let
p^{s}|n
. If
r\leq e_{p}^{(s)}(n)
, then

\displaystyle h_{n}(p^{r})

\displaystyle=p^{rs}

and

\displaystyle 2^{\omega\left(\frac{p^{rs}}{(p^{rs},n)_{s}}\right)}(p^{rs},n)_{s}=2^{\omega\left(\frac{p^{rs}}{p^{rs}}\right)}p^{rs}=p^{rs}

so that the claimed equality holds. On the other hand, if
r>e_{p}^{(s)}(n)
, then we may take
r-m=e_{p}^{(s)}(n)
for some positive integer
m
. So
p^{(r-m)s}||_{s}n
. Then, as we have seen previously,

\displaystyle h_{n}(p^{r})

\displaystyle=2p^{(r-m)s}.

Now
(k^{s},n)_{s}=p^{(r-m)s}
and
\frac{p^{rs}}{(p^{rs},n)_{s}}=p^{ms}
so that
2^{\omega\left(\frac{p^{rs}}{(p^{rs},n)_{s}}\right)}(p^{rs},n)_{s}=2p^{(r-m)s}
. This completes our proof.
∎

It was observed previously in Remark
3.2
that equality in Theorem
3.1
holds when
n=m^{s}
and
k
is a multiple of
m\prod\limits_{p|m}p
. So the next statement follows easily.

Corollary 3.5
.

When
n=m^{s}
, where
m
is a positive integer, and
k
is a multiple of
m\prod\limits_{p|m}p
, we have

n.2^{\omega(k)}=2^{\omega(\frac{k^{s}}{(k^{s},n)_{s}})}(k^{s},n)_{s}
.

In
[
6
]
, A. Grytczuk used the sum
\sum\limits_{q|k}|c_{q}(n)|
to evaluate
S(k,n):=\sum\limits_{d|k}2^{\omega\left(\frac{k}{(k,n)}\right)}(k,n)
. We will now show how to evaluate an analogous sum.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
S(k,n)=\sum\limits_{d|k}2^{\omega\left(\frac{k^{s}}{(k^{s},n)_{s}}\right)}(k^{s},n)_{s}\,\mu(k/d)
, then we have

(9)

\displaystyle S(k,n)=\begin{cases}\frac{J_{s}(k)}{J_{s}\left(\frac{k}{(k,n)}\right)}\quad&\text{if }\frac{k}{(k,n)}\text{ is square-free}\\
0\quad&\text{ otherwise}\end{cases}

Proof.

From (
8
), we have
\sum\limits_{d|k}|c_{d}^{(s)}(n)|=2^{\omega\left(\frac{k^{s}}{(k^{s},n)_{s}}\right)}(k^{s},n)_{s}
. Then by the Möbius inversion formula,
|c_{k}^{(s)}(n)|=\sum\limits_{d|k}2^{\omega\left(\frac{d^{s}}{(d^{s},n)_{s}}\right)}(d^{s},n)_{s}\,\mu(k/d)
. Using equation
6
, we get

\displaystyle\left|\frac{J_{s}(n)\mu(\frac{k}{(k,n)})}{J_{s}(\frac{k}{(k,n)})}\right|=\sum\limits_{d|k}2^{\omega\left(\frac{d^{s}}{(d^{s},n)_{s}}\right)}(d^{s},n)_{s}\mu(k/d).

Since
\mu
is 0 on positive integers that are not square-free and
\pm 1
on other positive integers, the statement in the theorem follows.

∎

Now we give the converse of the absolute convergence in Theorem
3.3
to conclude this paper.

A. Grytczuk
[
5
, Section 2]
proved that the absolute convergence of the second series in Theorem
3.3
(with
s=1)
is sufficient for the absolute convergence of the first series. This is true for any
s
.

Theorem 3.7
.

If
\sum\limits_{q=1}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{|f^{\prime}(mq)|}{(mq)^{s}}|c_{q}^{(s)}(n^{s})|<\infty
then
\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}2^{\omega(k)}\frac{|f^{\prime}(k)|}{k^{s}}<\infty
.

Proof.

Suppose
\sum\limits_{q=1}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{|f^{\prime}(mq)|}{(mq)^{s}}|c_{q}^{(s)}(n^{s})|<\infty
.

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{q=1}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{|f^{\prime}(mq)|}{(mq)^{s}}|c_{q}^{(s)}(n^{s})|

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{mq=k}\frac{|f^{\prime}(mq)|}{(mq)^{s}}|c_{q}^{(s)}(n^{s})|

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{|f^{\prime}(k)|}{k^{s}}\sum\limits_{q|k}|c_{q}^{(s)}(n^{s})|

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{|f^{\prime}(k)|}{k^{s}}2^{\omega(\frac{k^{s}}{(k^{s},n)_{s}})}(k^{s},n)_{s}.

To arrive at the last step above, we used identity (
8
).
Therefore
\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{|f^{\prime}(k)|}{(k)^{s}}2^{\omega\left(\frac{k^{s}}{(k^{s},n)_{s}}\right)}(k^{s},n)_{s}<\infty
. Since
\omega(m/n)\geq\omega(m)-\omega(n)
with equality holding if and only if
(m,n)=1
, we have

\displaystyle 2^{\omega\left(\frac{k^{s}}{(k^{s},n)_{s}}\right)}\geq\frac{2^{\omega(k^{s})}}{2^{\omega\left((k^{s},n)_{s}\right)}}\geq\frac{2^{\omega(k^{s})}}{(k^{s},n)_{s}}.

Hence

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{|f^{\prime}(k)|}{k^{s}}2^{\omega\left(\frac{k^{s}}{(k^{s},n)_{s}}\right)}(k^{s},n)_{s}\geq\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{|f^{\prime}(k)|}{k^{s}}2^{\omega(k^{s})}.

Since
\omega(k^{s})=\omega(k)
, the theorem follows.
∎

Remark 3.8
.

We would like to note here that K. R. Johnson evaluated the sum
\sum\limits_{q|k}|c^{(s)}_{q}(n)|
(with
s=1
) in the other variable of the Ramanujan sum in
[
8
]
. Precisely, he evaluated
\sum\limits_{q|n}|c_{q}(n)|
. It is possible to evaluate such a sum in our case also.

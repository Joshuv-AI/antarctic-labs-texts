Abstract
. Toda conformal field theories are natural generalizations of Liouville conformal field theory that enjoy an enhanced level of symmetry. In Toda conformal field theories this higher-spin symmetry can be made explicit, thanks to a path integral formulation of the model based on a Lie algebra structure. The purpose of the present document is to explain how this higher level of symmetry can manifest itself within the rigorous probabilistic framework introduced by R. Rhodes, V. Vargas and the first author in [ 8 ] . One of its features is the existence of holomorphic currents that are introduced via a rigorous derivation of the Miura transformation. More precisely, we prove that the spin-three Ward identities, that encode higher-spin symmetry, hold in the 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 3 \mathfrak{sl}_{3} Toda conformal field theory; as an original input we provide explicit expressions for the descendent fields which were left unidentified in the physics literature. This representation of the descendent fields provides a new systematic method to find the degenerate fields of the 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 3 \mathfrak{sl}_{3} Toda (and Liouville) conformal field theory, which in turn implies that certain four-point correlation functions are solutions of a hypergeometric differential equation of the third order.

1.1.
Toda theories and higher-spin symmetry

Providing a definition to the notion of random surface in the context of two-dimensional quantum gravity has been a seminal topic since the pioneering work of Polyakov
[
33
]

(1.1)

S_{L}(\varphi,g)\coloneqq\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\Sigma}\Big(\left\lvert\partial_{g}\varphi(x)\right\rvert^{2}+QR_{g}\varphi(x)+4\pi\mu e^{\gamma\varphi(x)}\Big)\,{\rm v}_{g}(dx).

The metric

This question was addressed in a subsequent work

Besides it is not clear at this stage to what extent our probabilistic framework would allow to find formulas for correlation functions which are not predicted in the physics literature. For the time being we shed light on the fact that our method for proving Theorem

2.
Background on the probabilistic construction of the
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
Toda theory

We recall here the basic materials from
[
8

\mathfrak{sl}_{3}

W_{3}

2.1.
Some reminders on conformal geometry and Lie algebras

2.1.1.
Conformal geometry on the Riemann sphere

It is usually more convenient (for the probabilistic formulation) to map conformally the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{2}
to the Riemann sphere (or the extended complex plane)
\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}
or
\mathbb{R}^{2}\cup\{\infty\}
. To do so, we use the standard stereographic projection, so that the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{2}
with its standard metric is identified (using conformal invariance of the model) with
{\mathbb{C}}\cup\{\infty\}
endowed with the spherical metric

(2.1)

\hat{g}=\frac{4}{(1+|z|^{2})^{2}}|dz|^{2}.

This Riemannian metric
\hat{g}
has constant scalar curvature equal to
R_{\hat{g}}=2
and gives total volume to the plane
{\rm v}_{\hat{g}}(\mathbb{C})=4\pi
. Here and after
{\rm v}_{\hat{g}}
denotes the volume form in the
\hat{g}
metric.

To this metric on the Riemann sphere we can associate a Green kernel

(2.2)

G_{\hat{g}}(x,y)\coloneqq\ln\frac{1}{\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert}-\frac{1}{4}\left(\ln\hat{g}(x)+\ln\hat{g}(y)\right)+\ln 2-\frac{1}{2}.

It is such that the
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\hat{g})
solution
[
8
, Equation (2.3)]
of the problem with
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},{\rm v}_{\hat{g}})

\triangle_{\hat{g}}u=-2\pi\left(f-m_{\hat{g}}(f)\right)\quad\text{on }\mathbb{R}^{2},\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}u(x)\,{\rm v}_{\hat{g}}(dx)=0,

can be written as

(2.3)

u=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}G_{\hat{g}}(\cdot,x)f(x){\rm v}_{\hat{g}}(dx).

The notations
\triangle_{\hat{g}}
and
m_{\hat{g}}
respectively stand for the Laplacian and the mean-value in the metric
\hat{g}
with

m_{\hat{g}}(f)\coloneqq\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f(x){\rm v}_{\hat{g}}(dx).

2.1.2.
Lie algebras and the Toda field action

Toda theories rely on a Lie algebra structure; basic materials on the notion of Lie algebra can be found for instance in the introductory textbook
[
24

. We consider in this manuscript the case of the
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
Lie algebra, which we identify with
3\times 3
real matrices with zero trace. Its Cartan sub-algebra then corresponds to the subset of
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
made of diagonal matrices, that is
\mathfrak{h}_{3}\coloneqq\{x=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}~|~\sum_{i=1}^{3}x_{i}=0\}

e_{i}\coloneqq v_{i}-v_{i+1}

\mathbb{R}^{3}
and are viewed as elements of the dual space
\mathfrak{h}_{3}^{*}
of
\mathfrak{h}_{3}

We denote by

Here

Explicitly we have

There are special elements of the Cartan sub-algebra

They prove to be useful in particular thanks to the following decomposition relation, which holds for any two vectors

We now turn to the Toda field action. For

The

2.2.
The probabilistic framework

We present here the main probabilistic objects for the path integral definition of the
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
Toda theory.

2.2.1.
Gaussian Free Fields

The geometric form of the Toda field action naturally leads to the introduction of GFFs with values in
\mathfrak{h}_{3}^{*}
. More precisely, set

(2.13)

\bm{\mathrm{X}}\coloneqq X_{1}\omega_{1}+X_{2}\omega_{2}

where
X_{1},X_{2}
are two real-valued GFFs (see
[
11
,
39
]
for instance) with covariance structure given by

(2.14)

\mathds{E}[X_{i}(x)X_{j}(y)]=A_{i,j}G_{\hat{g}}(x,y),\quad x\neq y\in\mathbb{C}\text{ and }i,j\in\{1,2\}.

The field
\bm{\mathrm{X}}
has almost surely zero-mean in the
\hat{g}
-metric. We will add the zero-mode
\bm{c}\in\mathfrak{h}_{3}^{*}\simeq\mathbb{R}^{2}
, which corresponds to the mean-value of the Toda field, in the next paragraph.

2.2.2.
Gaussian Multiplicative Chaos

It is a classical fact that the field
\bm{\mathrm{X}}
is highly non-regular and only exists in the distributional sense. We will see that the Toda field inherits the same regularity from
X
; as a consequence the exponential potential
e^{\gamma\left\langle e_{i},\varphi(x)\right\rangle}
that appears in the Toda field action is a priori not well-defined. In order to make sense of this term we introduce a limiting procedure that allows to define the random measure
e^{\gamma\left\langle e_{i},\varphi(x)\right\rangle}v_{\hat{g}}(dx)
, known as a
Gaussian Multiplicative Chaos
[
25
,
35
]
.

To start with, we define a regularization
\bm{\mathrm{X}}_{\varepsilon}
of the GFF by setting

(2.15)

\bm{\mathrm{X}}_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq\bm{\mathrm{X}}*\eta_{\varepsilon}=\int_{\mathbb{C}}\bm{\mathrm{X}}(\cdot-y)\eta_{\varepsilon}(y)dy.

where
\eta_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\eta(\frac{\cdot}{\varepsilon})
is a smooth, compactly supported mollifier. Without loss of generality we assume that
\eta=0
outside of some ball of radius
R
centered at the origin. The field
\bm{\mathrm{X}}_{\varepsilon}
obtained via this regularization procedure is now smooth, so the exponential of
\bm{\mathrm{X}}_{\varepsilon}
is therefore well-defined.
Under the bound
\gamma<\sqrt{2}
, the theory of GMC allows us to claim that, for each
e_{i}
with
i=1,2
, within the space of Radon measures equipped with the weak topology, the regularized exponential term converges in probability to some non-trivial random measure
[
3
,
38
]
:

(2.16)

M^{\gamma e_{i}}(dx)\coloneqq\underset{\varepsilon\to 0}{\lim}M_{\varepsilon}^{\gamma e_{i}}(dx),

where we have set

(2.17)

M_{\varepsilon}^{\gamma e_{i}}(dx):=\>e^{\langle\gamma e_{i},\bm{\mathrm{X}}_{\varepsilon}(x)\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\langle\gamma e_{i},\bm{\mathrm{X}}_{\varepsilon}(x)\rangle^{2}\right]}{\rm v}_{\hat{g}}(dx).

This limiting random measure is called a
Gaussian Multiplicative Chaos measure
. Following the ideas presented in
[
8
]
, this formalism allows to translate the Toda path integral with respect to the Toda action (
1.8
) as a probabilistic object. This is done by setting for
F
positive and continuous on
\mathrm{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{C}\to\mathfrak{h}_{3}^{*},\hat{g})
(see
[
8
, Equation (2.32)]
):

\int F(\varphi)e^{-S_{T}(\varphi,\hat{g})}D\varphi\coloneqq\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\mathfrak{h}_{3}^{*}}e^{-2\left\langle Q,\bm{c}\right\rangle}\mathds{E}\left[F\left(\bm{\mathrm{X}}_{\varepsilon}+\frac{Q}{2}\ln\hat{g}_{\varepsilon}+\bm{c}\right)e^{-\sum_{i=1}^{2}\mu_{i}e^{\gamma\langle e_{i},\bm{c}\rangle}M^{\gamma e_{i}}_{\varepsilon}(\mathbb{C})}\right]d\bm{c}

with
\hat{g}_{\varepsilon}(z)\coloneqq\hat{g}*\eta_{\varepsilon}(z)
, and where the integral over
\mathfrak{h}_{3}^{*}
is defined by

(2.18)

\int_{\mathfrak{h}_{3}^{*}}F(\bm{c})d\bm{c}\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}F(c_{1}\omega_{1}+c_{2}\omega_{2})dc_{1}dc_{2}.

In the sequel, we denote with brackets the above quantity (not to be confused with the brackets for the scalar product on
\mathfrak{h}_{3}^{*}
):

(2.19)

\left\langle F\right\rangle\coloneqq\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\int_{\mathfrak{h}_{3}^{*}}e^{-2\left\langle Q,\bm{c}\right\rangle}\mathds{E}\left[F\left(\varphi_{\varepsilon}\right)e^{-\sum_{i=1}^{2}\mu_{i}e^{\gamma\langle e_{i},\bm{c}\rangle}M^{\gamma e_{i}}_{\varepsilon}(\mathbb{C})}\right]d\bm{c},

where we introduced the shorthand

(2.20)

\varphi_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq\left(\bm{\mathrm{X}}+\frac{Q}{2}\ln\hat{g}+\bm{c}\right)*\eta_{\varepsilon}.

The field
\varphi_{\varepsilon}
will be referred to as the regularized Toda field.

2.3.1.

For

\left\langle\prod_{k=1}^{N}V_{\alpha_{k},\varepsilon}(z_{k})\right\rangle\coloneqq\int_{\mathfrak{h}_{3}^{*}}e^{-2\left\langle Q,\bm{c}\right\rangle}\mathds{E}\left[\prod_{k=1}^{N}V_{\varepsilon,\alpha_{k}}(z_{k})e^{-\sum_{i=1}^{2}\mu_{i}e^{\gamma\langle\bm{c},e_{i}\rangle}M^{\gamma e_{i},g}_{\varepsilon}(\mathbb{C})}\right]d\bm{c}.

For future convenience we introduce

(2.23)

s_{i}\coloneqq\frac{\left\langle\sum_{k=1}^{N}\alpha_{k}-2Q,\omega_{i}\right\rangle}{\gamma}\cdot

An important result is the existence of constraints on

The correlation functions

\begin{split}&\bullet\quad\text{For all }i=1,2,\quad s_{i}>0.\\

\begin{split}&\langle V_{\alpha_{1}}(z_{1})\cdots V_{\alpha_{N}}(z_{N})\rangle=\\

(2.27)

\text{For }i=1,2,\quad-s_{i}<\frac{2}{\gamma^{2}}\wedge\min_{k=1,\dots,N}\frac{1}{\gamma}\langle Q-\alpha_{k},e_{i}\rangle.

2.3.2.
Miura transformation and holomorphic currents

The W-symmetry manifests itself via the holomorphicity of some currents, which implies existence of the so-called
Ward identities
recalled in the introduction.

In order to define these currents, let us write down the general form of the holomorphic currents associated to the symmetries of the model via the Miura transformation

(2.28)

\left(\frac{q}{2}\partial+\left\langle h_{3},\partial\varphi\right\rangle\right)\left(\frac{q}{2}\partial+\left\langle h_{2},\partial\varphi\right\rangle\right)\left(\frac{q}{2}\partial+\left\langle h_{1},\partial\varphi\right\rangle\right)\coloneqq\sum_{i=0}^{3}\bm{\mathrm{W}^{(3-i)}}\left(\frac{q}{2}\partial\right)^{i}.

The expression (
2.28
) should be understood in the sense of operators, by which we mean that the factors
\left\langle h_{i},\partial\varphi\right\rangle
act multiplicatively while
\partial
acts as differential operator.
For instance we have
\bm{\mathrm{W}^{(0)}}=1
,
\bm{\mathrm{W}^{(1)}}=0
(since
h_{1}+h_{2}+h_{3}=0
) and

\bm{\mathrm{W}^{(2)}}=\frac{q}{2}\left\langle 2h_{1}+h_{2},\partial^{2}\varphi\right\rangle+\left\langle h_{1},\partial\varphi\right\rangle\left\langle h_{2},\partial\varphi\right\rangle+\left\langle h_{2},\partial\varphi\right\rangle\left\langle h_{3},\partial\varphi\right\rangle+\left\langle h_{3},\partial\varphi\right\rangle\left\langle h_{1},\partial\varphi\right\rangle.

The latter actually corresponds (up to a multiplicative factor) to the
stress-energy tensor
of the
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
Toda theory, which we denote as
\bm{\mathrm{T}}\coloneqq 2\bm{\mathrm{W}^{(2)}}
. Indeed since
2h_{1}+h_{2}=\rho
and for any
u,v\in\mathfrak{h}_{3}^{*}
,

(2.29)

\left\langle h_{1},u\right\rangle\left\langle h_{2},v\right\rangle+\left\langle h_{2},u\right\rangle\left\langle h_{3},v\right\rangle+\left\langle h_{3},u\right\rangle\left\langle h_{1},v\right\rangle=-\frac{1}{2}\left\langle u,v\right\rangle,

this tensor can be rewritten in a more standard way as

\bm{\mathrm{T}}(z)=\left\langle Q,\partial^{2}\varphi(z)\right\rangle-\left\langle\partial\varphi(z),\partial\varphi(z)\right\rangle.

Since we are working with a quantum theory, the products that appear above are understood as Wick products and make sense when
\varepsilon
-regularized. Therefore we introduce

(2.30)

\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{\varepsilon}(z)\coloneqq\left\langle Q,\partial^{2}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(z)\right\rangle-:\left\langle\partial\varphi_{\varepsilon}(z),\partial\varphi_{\varepsilon}(z)\right\rangle:

where the notation
:XY:
is shorthand for
XY-\mathbb{E}\left[XY\right]
and
\varphi_{\varepsilon}
is the regularized Toda field
\varphi_{\varepsilon}=\bm{\mathrm{X}}_{\varepsilon}+\frac{Q}{2}\ln\hat{g}_{\varepsilon}+\bm{c}
as before.

The regularized higher-spin current
\bm{\mathrm{W}^{(3)}}_{\varepsilon}
is defined with a similar procedure applied to the expression

\frac{q^{2}}{4}\left\langle h_{1},\partial^{3}\varphi\right\rangle+\frac{q}{2}\left(\left\langle h_{1},\partial^{2}\varphi\right\rangle\left\langle h_{2}+h_{3},\partial\varphi\right\rangle+\left\langle h_{2},\partial^{2}\varphi\right\rangle\left\langle h_{1},\partial\varphi\right\rangle\right)+\left\langle h_{1},\partial\varphi\right\rangle\left\langle h_{2},\partial\varphi\right\rangle\left\langle h_{3},\partial\varphi\right\rangle.

As suggested in the physics literature
[
5
,
18
,
16
]
, in order to recover the standard commutation relations of the
W_{3}
-algebra and obtain an elegant expression for the quantum number associated to the spin-three tensor
w(\alpha)
, we may choose to shift this current by an additional factor
-\frac{q}{8}\partial\bm{\mathrm{T}}
. Explicitly, we redefine the spin-three current by setting

(2.31)

\begin{split}\bm{\mathrm{W}^{(3)}}_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq&-\frac{q^{2}}{8}\left\langle h_{2},\partial^{3}\varphi_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle+\frac{q}{4}\left(:\left\langle h_{2}-h_{1},\partial^{2}\varphi_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle\left\langle h_{1},\partial\varphi_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle:+:\left\langle h_{3}-h_{2},\partial^{2}\varphi_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle\left\langle h_{3},\partial\varphi_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle:\right)\\
&+:\left\langle h_{1},\partial\varphi_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle\left\langle h_{2},\partial\varphi_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle\left\langle h_{3},\partial\varphi_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle:\end{split}

with
:XYZ\coloneqq XYZ-X\mathbb{E}\left[YZ\right]-Y\mathbb{E}\left[XZ\right]-Z\mathbb{E}\left[XY\right]
.

In the sequel we will study the
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
limit of the quantity

\left\langle\bm{\mathrm{W}^{(3)}}_{\varepsilon}\prod_{k=1}^{N}V_{\alpha_{k},\varepsilon}(z_{k})\right\rangle.

In order not to overload notations, we will write
\bm{\mathrm{W}}
for
\bm{\mathrm{W}^{(3)}}
.

3.
Regularity of the Toda correlation functions

The purpose of this section is to provide some elementary results needed in order to prove the local Ward identities (
1.21
) and (
1.22
). Namely we explain how to make sense of the quantities presented in the previous Section
2
thanks to
Gaussian integration by parts
and give some regularity results for the Toda correlation functions.

3.1.
Probabilistic tools

3.1.1.
The regularized Toda correlation functions

According to the expression of the holomorphic currents of the
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
Toda theory, we will consider expressions that involve derivatives of the Toda field. As a consequence, we will work with a regularized versions of the objects introduced before. However in the computation of the Ward identities, there will be one extra point (that we denoted
z_{0}
) that does not appear within the Vertex Operators (that are evaluated at
z_{1},\cdots,z_{N}
) but rather in the holomorphic current
\bm{\mathrm{T}}
or
\bm{\mathrm{W}}
. Therefore we will use two different kinds of regularization corresponding either to the variables
(z_{1},\cdots,z_{N})
appearing in the Vertex Operators or
z_{0}
appearing in the currents.
As we will see having these two regularizations prove to be useful in order to simplify the computations. We follow the approach developed in
[
44
]
.

Let
\theta:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to[0,1]
be a smooth function which is
0
in
[0,\frac{1}{2}]
and
1
in
[1,+\infty)
. For positive
\delta
and complex
z
we set
\theta_{\delta}(z)\coloneqq\theta\left(\frac{\left\lvert z\right\rvert}{\delta}\right)
, such that
\theta_{\delta}(z)\rightarrow 1
when
\delta\rightarrow 0
for any
z\neq 0
. In the sequel,
\bm{z}
will denote the vector of complex numbers
(z_{1},\dots,z_{N})
and
\bm{\alpha}
the vector of parameters
(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{N})
in
\left(\mathfrak{h}_{3}^{*}\right)^{N}
.

Let us assume that
(z_{0},\bm{z})
are distinct complex points with
z_{0}\neq 0
and that we are given
\bm{\alpha}\in\left(\mathfrak{h}_{3}^{*}\right)^{N}
satisfying the Seiberg bounds (
2.25
).
Then for positive
\varepsilon
and
\delta
, the
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-regularized correlation functions are defined by

(3.1)

\left\langle\prod_{k=0}^{N}V_{\alpha_{k},\varepsilon}(z_{k})\right\rangle_{\delta}\coloneqq\int_{\mathfrak{h}_{3}^{*}}e^{-2\left\langle Q,\bm{c}\right\rangle}\mathds{E}\left[\left(\prod_{k=1}^{N}V_{\varepsilon,\alpha_{k}}(z_{k})\right)e^{-\sum_{i=1}^{2}\mu e^{\gamma\langle\bm{c},e_{i}\rangle}M^{\gamma e_{i}}_{\varepsilon,\delta}(\mathbb{C})}\right]d\bm{c}.

The only difference with the expression (
2.22
) is that we also regularize the total mass of the GMC inside the expectation term above around the point
z_{0}

M^{\gamma e_{i}}_{\varepsilon,\delta}(\mathbb{C})\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{C}}\theta_{\delta}(x-z_{0})M^{\gamma e_{i}}_{\varepsilon}(dx).

The reason for this extra regularization will be made clear later; informally we remove the singularities coming from the holomorphic currents. For future convenience, we introduce the shorthand

\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq\prod_{k=1}^{N}V_{\alpha_{k},\varepsilon}(z_{k}).

Recall that
\hat{g}_{\varepsilon}(z)\coloneqq\hat{g}*\eta_{\varepsilon}(z)
with
\eta_{\varepsilon}
as in (
2.15
) and set

\left\lvert x\right\rvert_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq\exp\left(\int_{\mathbb{C}}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\ln\left\lvert x-z+z^{\prime}\right\rvert\eta_{\varepsilon}(z)\eta_{\varepsilon}(z^{\prime})d^{2}zd^{2}z^{\prime}\right).

We also use a regularization of the complex-valued map
x\mapsto\frac{1}{x^{p}}
for a positive integer
p
by setting

\frac{1}{(x)^{p}_{\varepsilon}}\coloneqq\frac{(-1)^{p}}{(p-1)!}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{1}{(x-z+z^{\prime})}\partial_{z}^{p-1}\eta_{\varepsilon}(z)\eta_{\varepsilon}(z^{\prime})d^{2}zd^{2}z^{\prime}.

The connection with the map
x\mapsto\frac{1}{x^{p}}
is made clear by the following statement.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and
p
be a positive integer, and recall
R
from the regularization (
2.15
). Then for any
\left\lvert x\right\rvert>4R\varepsilon
it holds that

\frac{x^{p}}{(x)_{\varepsilon}^{p}}-1=\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{x}\right)^{2}\mathrm{F}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{x}\right)

where
\mathrm{F}
is continuous over
\mathbb{C}
and depends only on the mollifier
\eta
of the
\varepsilon
-regularization. In particular, the family of functions
\left(\frac{1}{(x)_{\varepsilon}^{p}}\right)_{\varepsilon>0}
converges uniformly to
\frac{1}{x^{p}}
on every compact set of
\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
.
Similarly, the quantity

\sup\limits_{0<\left\lvert x\right\rvert\;\leqslant\;4R\varepsilon}\left\lvert\frac{x^{p}}{(x)_{\varepsilon}^{p}}\right\rvert

remains bounded uniformly on
\varepsilon
.

We postpone the proof of this claim and of the next one to Section
6
since they are not informative.
Along the proof of Theorem
1.1
we will need to integrate certain correlation functions over the complex plane. The integration variables will show up in these correlation functions in the form of Vertex Operators with weights
V_{\gamma e_{i}}
,
i=1,2
. The following statement addresses this issue by ensuring that the quantities involved do make sense:

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume that the
\bm{z}\coloneqq(z_{1},\cdots,z_{N})
are distinct and
\bm{\alpha}\coloneqq(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{N})\in\left(\mathfrak{h}_{3}^{*}\right)^{N}
are such that the Seiberg bounds (
2.25
) are satisfied. Consider complex vectors
\bm{x}_{1}=(x_{1}^{(1)},\cdots,x_{1}^{(r_{1})})
and
\bm{x}_{2}=(x_{2}^{(1)},\cdots,x_{2}^{(r_{2})})
.

(1)

For
\varepsilon,\delta>0
, there exists a positive constant
C_{\varepsilon}
, independent of
\delta
, such that

\left\langle\prod\limits_{i=1}^{r_{1}}V_{\gamma e_{1},\varepsilon}\left(x_{1}^{(i)}\right)\prod\limits_{j=1}^{r_{2}}V_{\gamma e_{2},\varepsilon}\left(x_{2}^{(j)}\right)\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}\;\leqslant\;C_{\varepsilon}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{r_{1}}\left(1+\left\lvert x_{1}^{(i)}\right\rvert\right)^{-4}\prod_{j=1}^{r_{2}}\left(1+\left\lvert x_{2}^{(j)}\right\rvert\right)^{-4}.

(2)

For
\varepsilon=\delta=0
and
\rho>0
, if
\bm{x}_{1},\bm{x}_{2}
and
\bm{z}
are in the domain
U_{\rho}\coloneqq\left\{{\bm{w},\rho<\min\limits_{i\neq j}\left\lvert w_{i}-w_{j}\right\rvert}\right\}
, the above bound remains true uniformly over
(\bm{x}_{1},\bm{x_{2}})
, with a constant
C
that depends only on
\rho
.

(3)

Assume that all pairs of points in
\bm{z}
are separated by some distance
\rho>0
except for one pair
(z_{1},z_{2})
, and that all the
z
’s stay in a compact subset of
\mathbb{C}
. Further assume that
\left\langle\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}-Q,e_{1}\right\rangle<0
. Then, for any positive
\eta
, there exists a positive constant
K=K(\rho)
such that, uniformly on
\varepsilon
and
\delta
,

(3.2)

\left\langle\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}\;\leqslant\;K(\rho)\left\lvert z_{1}-z_{2}\right\rvert^{-\left\langle\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\right\rangle-\eta+\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}-Q,e_{2}\right\rangle^{2}\mathds{1}_{\left\langle\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}-Q,e_{2}\right\rangle>0}}.

If
\left\langle\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}-Q,e_{2}\right\rangle<0
, we have the same estimate by switching
e_{1}\leftrightarrow e_{2}
.

(4)

In the previous estimate, if
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in\{\gamma e_{1},\gamma e_{2}\}
, then there exists some
\zeta>0
such that

\left\langle\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}\;\leqslant\;K\left\lvert z_{1}-z_{2}\right\rvert^{-2+\zeta}.

(5)

For some
p>1
that depends on
\gamma
and
\left\langle\alpha_{0},e_{1}\right\rangle
, the map

x_{1}^{(1)}\mapsto\sup\limits_{\varepsilon}\left\langle\prod\limits_{i=1}^{r_{1}}V_{\gamma e_{1},\varepsilon}(x_{1}^{(i)})\prod\limits_{j=1}^{r_{2}}V_{\gamma e_{2},\varepsilon}(x_{2}^{(j)})\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}

is in
L^{p}(\mathbb{C})
.

As a first application of these technical results we provide a rather useful identity. This identity is derived from the
\mu
-dependence of the correlation functions, usually referred to as a “KPZ-relation”
[
26
]
.

Lemma 3.3

(KPZ identity)
.

Assume that the
\bm{z}
are distinct and that the
\bm{\alpha}
are such that the Seiberg bounds (
2.25
) hold. Then for non-negative
\varepsilon
and
\delta
, the following equality holds in
\mathfrak{h}_{3}^{*}
:

(3.3)

\left(\sum_{k=1}^{N}\alpha_{k}-2Q\right)\left\langle\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}=\gamma\sum_{i=1}^{2}\mu_{i}e_{i}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x)\left\langle V_{\gamma e_{i},\varepsilon}(x)\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}d^{2}x.

Proof.

It suffices to check that for all
i=1,2
one has that

\left\langle\sum_{k=1}^{N}\alpha_{k}-2Q,\omega_{i}\right\rangle\left\langle\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}=\mu_{i}\gamma\int_{\mathbb{C}}\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x)\left\langle V_{\gamma e_{i},\varepsilon}(x)\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}d^{2}x.

To do so, we differentiate with respect to
\mu_{i}
the expression

\left\langle\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\delta}=\int_{\mathfrak{h}_{3}^{*}}e^{-\left\langle 2Q,\bm{c}\right\rangle}\mathds{E}\left[\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}e^{-\sum_{i=1}^{2}\mu_{i}e^{\gamma\langle\bm{c},e_{i}\rangle}M^{\gamma e_{i}}_{\varepsilon,\delta}(\mathbb{C})}\right]d\bm{c}

to get for
i=1,2
the following:

-\int_{\mathfrak{h}_{3}^{*}}e^{-\left\langle 2Q,\bm{c}\right\rangle}\mathds{E}\left[\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}e^{\gamma\langle\bm{c},e_{i}\rangle}M^{\gamma e_{i}}_{\varepsilon,\delta}(\mathbb{C})e^{-\sum_{i=1}^{2}\mu_{i}e^{\gamma\langle\bm{c},e_{i}\rangle}M^{\gamma e_{i}}_{\varepsilon,\delta}(\mathbb{C})}\right]d\bm{c}.

By positivity of the above quantities and Fubini-Tonelli, the latter expression is equal to

-\int_{\mathbb{C}}\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x)\int_{\mathfrak{h}_{3}^{*}}e^{-\left\langle 2Q,\bm{c}\right\rangle}\mathds{E}\left[\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}V_{\gamma e_{i},\varepsilon}(x)e^{-\sum_{i=1}^{2}\mu_{i}e^{\gamma\langle\bm{c},e_{i}\rangle}M^{\gamma e_{i}}_{\varepsilon,\delta}(\mathbb{C})}\right]d\bm{c}d^{2}x

where we have used the fact that
M_{\varepsilon,\delta}^{\gamma e_{i}}(dx)=\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x)V_{\gamma e_{i},\varepsilon}(x)d^{2}x
. This yields

\frac{\partial}{\partial{\mu_{i}}}\left\langle\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\delta}=-\int_{\mathbb{C}}\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x)\left\langle V_{\gamma e_{i},\varepsilon}(x)\prod_{k=1}^{N}V_{\alpha_{k},\varepsilon}(z_{k})\right\rangle_{\delta}d^{2}x.

However, by making a change of variables in the zero-mode
\bm{c}\to\bm{c}-\frac{\rho}{\gamma}\ln\mu_{i}
, the
\mu_{i}
-dependence of the correlation functions is explicit and in agreement with Equation (
2.26
):

\left\langle\prod_{k=1}^{N}V_{\alpha_{k},\varepsilon}(z_{k})\right\rangle_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\delta}=\mu_{1}^{-s_{1}}\mu_{2}^{-s_{2}}\left\langle\prod_{k=1}^{N}V_{\alpha_{k},\varepsilon}(z_{k})\right\rangle_{1,1,\delta}.

When we differentiate it with respect to
\mu_{i}
, we end up with (recall Equation (
2.23
))

-\frac{\left\langle\sum_{k=1}^{N}\alpha_{k}-2Q,\omega_{i}\right\rangle}{\mu_{i}\gamma}\left\langle\prod_{k=1}^{N}V_{\alpha_{k},\varepsilon}(z_{k})\right\rangle_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\delta}.

We recover the desired result for positive
\varepsilon
and
\delta
by identification.

For the
\varepsilon\to 0
limit, we use the last item of Lemma
3.2
. Finally, all arguments go in the same way without the
\delta
-regularization.
∎

3.1.2.
Gaussian integration by parts

When computing the Ward identity (
1.22
), we insert the spin-three current
\bm{\mathrm{W}}(z_{0})
within the correlation functions. Put differently we will need to make sense of terms that take the form

\Big\langle\left\langle\alpha,\partial^{p}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(z_{0})\right\rangle\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\Big\rangle_{\delta}.

It is important to highlight that the extra
\delta
-regularization introduced is done with respect to a special point
z_{0}
, which does not appear within the Vertex Operators
\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}
.

The following proposition explains how to apply Gaussian integration by parts in our context in order to treat the insertion of this current.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
p
be a positive integer and
\alpha
in
\mathfrak{h}_{3}^{*}
. Then for any positive
\varepsilon
and
\delta
:

\begin{split}&\Big\langle\left\langle\alpha,\partial^{p}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(z_{0})\right\rangle\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\Big\rangle_{\delta}\\
=&\frac{(-1)^{p}(p-1)!}{2}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\left\langle\alpha,\alpha_{k}\right\rangle}{(z_{0}-z_{k})_{\varepsilon}^{p}}\left\langle\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}-\sum_{i=1}^{2}\mu_{i}\left\langle\alpha,\gamma e_{i}\right\rangle\int_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x)}{(z_{0}-x)^{p}_{\varepsilon}}\left\langle V_{\gamma e_{i},\varepsilon}(x)\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}d^{2}x\right).\end{split}

Proof.

We recall the Gaussian integration by parts formula for a centered Gaussian vector
(X,Y_{1},\dots,Y_{N})
and
f
a smooth function on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with bounded derivatives:

(3.4)

\mathds{E}\left[Xf(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{N})\right]=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\mathds{E}\left[XY_{k}\right]\mathds{E}\left[\partial_{Y_{k}}f(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{N})\right],

as well as the defining expression which follows from Equation (
2.19
):

\begin{split}&\left\langle\langle\alpha,\bm{\mathrm{X}}_{\varepsilon}+\frac{Q}{2}\ln\hat{g}_{\varepsilon}\rangle(z_{0})\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}=\int_{\mathfrak{h}_{3}^{*}}e^{-\left\langle 2Q,\bm{c}\right\rangle}\mathds{E}\left[\langle\alpha,\bm{\mathrm{X}}_{\varepsilon}+\frac{Q}{2}\ln\hat{g}_{\varepsilon}\rangle(z_{0})\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}e^{-\sum_{i=1}^{2}\mu_{i}e^{\gamma\langle\bm{c},e_{i}\rangle}M^{\gamma e_{i},\hat{g}}_{\varepsilon,\delta}(\mathbb{C})}\right]d\bm{c}.\end{split}

In order to apply Gaussian integration by parts (
3.4
) to the expectation term

\mathds{E}\left[\langle\alpha,\bm{\mathrm{X}}_{\varepsilon}+\frac{Q}{2}\ln\hat{g}_{\varepsilon}\rangle(z_{0})\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}e^{-\sum_{i=1}^{2}\mu_{i}e^{\gamma\langle\bm{c},e_{i}\rangle}M^{\gamma e_{i},\hat{g}}_{\varepsilon,\delta}(\mathbb{C})}\right],

we may write the integral over the complex plane that appears in the exponential term,
M^{\gamma e_{i},\hat{g}}_{\varepsilon,\delta}(\mathbb{C})
as a Riemann sum. Since we can apply Equation (
3.4
) to the partial sum and then take the limit, this means that we may directly apply Gaussian integration by parts to the regularized correlation functions to get that

\begin{split}\left\langle\langle\alpha,\bm{\mathrm{X}}_{\varepsilon}+\frac{Q}{2}\ln\hat{g}_{\varepsilon}\rangle(z_{0})\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}&=\left(\sum_{k=1}^{N}\langle\alpha,\alpha_{k}\rangle G_{\hat{g},\varepsilon}(z_{0},z_{k})+\langle\alpha,\frac{Q}{2}\ln\hat{g}_{\varepsilon}\rangle(z_{0})\right)\left\langle\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}\\
&-\sum_{i=1}^{2}\frac{\mu_{i}\gamma}{2}\left\langle\alpha,e_{i}\right\rangle\int_{\mathbb{C}}\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x)G_{\hat{g},\varepsilon}(z_{0},x)\left\langle V_{\gamma e_{i},\varepsilon}(x)\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}d^{2}x,\end{split}

where
G_{\hat{g},\varepsilon}
is the covariance kernel of the regularized field
\bm{\mathrm{X}}_{\varepsilon}
. One key point is that when taking derivatives of the Toda field we no longer take into account the zero mode
\bm{c}
in the field
\varphi_{\varepsilon}
. As a consequence we see that for positive
p
,

\Big\langle\left\langle\alpha,\partial^{p}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(z_{0})\right\rangle\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\Big\rangle_{\delta}=\Big\langle\langle\alpha,\partial^{p}\left(\bm{\mathrm{X}}_{\varepsilon}+\frac{Q}{2}\ln\hat{g}_{\varepsilon}\right)\rangle(z_{0})\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\Big\rangle_{\delta}\cdot

Therefore we end up with:

\begin{split}\left\langle\langle\alpha,\partial^{p}\left(\bm{\mathrm{X}}_{\varepsilon}+\frac{Q}{2}\ln\hat{g}_{\varepsilon}\right)(z_{0})\rangle\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}=&\partial^{p}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left\langle\alpha,\alpha_{k}\right\rangle G_{\hat{g},\varepsilon}(z_{0},z_{k})+\langle\alpha,\frac{Q}{2}\ln\hat{g}_{\varepsilon}\rangle(z_{0})\right)\left\langle\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}\\
-&\sum_{i=1}^{2}\frac{\mu_{i}\gamma}{2}\left\langle\alpha,e_{i}\right\rangle\int_{\mathbb{C}}\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x)\partial^{p}G_{\hat{g},\varepsilon}(z_{0},x)\left\langle V_{\gamma e_{i},\varepsilon}(x)\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}d^{2}x.\end{split}

Note that here we do
not
take derivatives of
\theta_{\delta}
in the integral: one should think of the equation above as taking the derivative of the field
\varphi_{\varepsilon}(z)
then evaluate at
z=z_{0}
.

From the explicit expression of the Green function
G_{\hat{g}}
in the round metric (
2.2
) we get

\begin{split}&\Big\langle\left\langle\alpha,\partial^{p}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(z_{0})\right\rangle\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\Big\rangle_{\delta}\\
={}&\frac{(-1)^{p}(p-1)!}{2}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\left\langle\alpha,\alpha_{k}\right\rangle}{(z_{0}-z_{k})^{p}_{\varepsilon}}-\sum_{i=1}^{2}\mu_{i}\left\langle\alpha,\gamma e_{i}\right\rangle\int_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x)}{(z_{0}-x)^{p}_{\varepsilon}}\left\langle V_{\gamma e_{i},\varepsilon}(x)\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}d^{2}x\right)\\
&+\frac{1}{4}\partial^{p}\ln\hat{g}_{\varepsilon}(z_{0})\left\langle\alpha,\left(\sum_{k=1}^{N}\alpha_{k}-2Q\right)\left\langle V_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}-\sum_{i=1}^{2}\mu_{i}\gamma e_{i}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x)\left\langle V_{\gamma e_{i},\varepsilon}(x)\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}d^{2}x\right\rangle.\end{split}

The above integrals are indeed well-defined thanks to item (5) of Lemma
3.2
. Now we can use the KPZ identity (
3.3
) to see that the metric-dependent term —the last line— equals zero.
∎

As can be seen from the expression of
\bm{W}
, we will need to extend the previous statement to products of derivatives of the field. The proper way to treat such terms is to apply recursively the following formula, that follows from Proposition
3.4

(3.5)

\begin{split}\frac{2}{(-1)^{p_{r}}(p_{r}-1)!}&\left\langle:\prod_{l=1}^{r}\left\langle\beta_{l},\partial^{p_{l}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(z_{0})\right\rangle:\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}\\
&=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\left\langle\beta_{r},\alpha_{k}\right\rangle}{(z_{0}-z_{k})_{\varepsilon}^{p_{r}}}\left\langle:\prod_{l=1}^{r-1}\left\langle\beta_{l},\partial^{p_{l}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(z_{0})\right\rangle:\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}\\
&-\sum_{i=1}^{2}\mu_{i}\left\langle\beta_{r},\gamma e_{i}\right\rangle\int_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x)}{(z_{0}-x)^{p}_{\varepsilon}}\left\langle:\prod_{l=1}^{r-1}\left\langle\beta_{l},\partial^{p_{l}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(z_{0})\right\rangle:V_{\gamma e_{i},\varepsilon}(x)\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}d^{2}x,\end{split}

valid for
p_{1},\cdots,p_{r}
positive integers and
\beta_{1},\cdots,\beta_{r}
any elements of
\mathfrak{h}_{3}^{*}
. This can be derived using the same reasoning and the definition of the Wick product as before.

Remark 3.5
.

We assume in the sequel that
\mu_{1}=\mu_{2}=\mu
for better readability. This is not restrictive thanks to the explicit dependence in
\mu_{1}
,
\mu_{2}
of the correlation functions.

3.2.
Derivatives of the correlation functions

We turn to the study of the regularity of Toda correlation functions. In this subsection, we keep the
\delta
-regularization around the point
z_{0}
, but this point is now a localization where a Vertex Operator is evaluated (instead of a point where the currents were elavuated as it was before). Therefore the extra
\delta
-regularization is now made around a singular point which appears in the product of Vertex Operators.

We do so to make the proof more natural and describe a general algorithmic procedure to show that the Toda correlation functions are smooth. In the present document we will not prove the smoothness, and only require the weaker result:

Proposition 3.6
.

Assume that the weights
\bm{\alpha}=(\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{N})
are such that the Seiberg bounds (
2.25
) hold.
Then
z_{0}\mapsto\left\langle\prod_{k=0}^{N}V_{\alpha_{k}}(z_{k})\right\rangle
is
C^{2}
on the set
\mathbb{C}\setminus\{z_{1},\cdots,z_{N}\}
.

The same results holds true for the other points
z_{k}\mapsto\left\langle\prod_{k=0}^{N}V_{\alpha_{k}}(z_{k})\right\rangle
,
k=1,\dots,N
, over which no
\delta
-regularization is made.

Proof.

Let us consider the
(\varepsilon,\delta)

\displaystyle\left.+\int_{A(z_{0},\delta/2,r)}\left(\frac{\gamma\left\langle\alpha_{0},e_{i}\right\rangle}{2}\frac{\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x)}{(z_{0}-x)_{\varepsilon}}+\partial_{x}\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x)\right)\left\langle V_{\gamma e_{i},\varepsilon}(x)V_{\alpha_{0},\varepsilon}(z_{0})\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}d^{2}x\right),

4.

With all these tools at hand, we are now in position to prove the main result of the present document, that is that the spin-two and spin-three Ward identities (

) do hold:

\displaystyle\left\langle\bm{\mathrm{T}}(z_{0})\bm{\mathrm{V}}\right\rangle=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(\frac{\Delta_{\alpha_{k}}}{(z_{0}-z_{k})^{2}}+\frac{\partial_{z_{k}}}{z_{0}-z_{k}}\right)\left\langle\bm{\mathrm{V}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\left\langle\bm{\mathrm{W}}(z_{0})\bm{\mathrm{V}}\right\rangle=-\frac{1}{8}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(\frac{w(\alpha_{k})}{(z_{0}-z_{k})^{3}}+\frac{\bm{\mathcal{W}_{-1}^{(k)}}}{(z_{0}-z_{k})^{2}}+\frac{\bm{\mathcal{W}_{-2}^{(k)}}}{z_{0}-z_{k}}\right)\left\langle\bm{\mathrm{V}}\right\rangle\cdot

The derivatives in the first line are holomorphic derivatives (and will be in the sequel).

4.1.
Computation of the Ward identities: strategy of the proof

In order to prove these identities, we work with the regularized version of the correlation functions. We can apply Proposition
3.4
to rewrite explicitly the left-hand side
\left\langle\bm{\mathrm{W}}(z_{0})\bm{\mathrm{V}}\right\rangle
in terms of multiple integrals in the
x
variable containing singularities of the form
x\mapsto\frac{1}{(x-z_{0})^{p}}
. We proceed in the same way with the right-hand side in the Ward identity, which will also yield multiple integrals containing singularities of the form
x\mapsto\frac{1}{(x-z_{k})^{p}}
. Our strategy to treat the terms we get is as follows:

•

We transform the right-hand side so that the only singularities appearing within integrals are of the form
\frac{1}{(x-z_{0})^{p}}
. Using identities such as
{\frac{1}{(z_{0}-z_{k})(x-z_{k})}=\frac{1}{z_{0}-x}\left(\frac{1}{x-z_{k}}-\frac{1}{z_{0}-z_{k}}\right)}
(that we call “symmetrization” identities) we rewrite terms containing expressions of the form
\frac{1}{x-z_{k}}
as derivatives of the correlation functions. We then use integration by parts to transform them into the desired form.

•

We take the
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
limit of the expression obtained. Since singularities are only present around
z_{0}
this limit makes sense, so we only have to take care of the residual terms coming from the symmetrization. This is done by using Lemma
3.1
which shows that these become negligible in the
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
limit.

•

Finally we let
\delta
go to zero: this step requires a careful study of the remaining terms and is based on a reasoning similar to the one developed in the proof of Proposition
3.6
on the differentiability of the correlation functions. The fact that the correlation functions are
C^{1}
will be used during this last step.

In the limit we recover the desired result; the rest of this section provides details on this procedure.

4.2.
Computation of the spin-two Ward identity

As a warm-up, let us discuss in detail the proof of the Ward identity for the stress-energy tensor
\bm{\mathrm{T}}
. In the regularized expression for
\bm{\mathrm{T}}
given by Equation (
2.30

\left\langle\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{\varepsilon}(z_{0})\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}=\left\langle\left\langle Q,\partial^{2}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(z_{0})\right\rangle\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}+\sum_{p=1}^{3}\left\langle:\left\langle h_{p},\partial\varphi_{\varepsilon}(z_{0})\right\rangle\left\langle h_{p+1},\partial\varphi_{\varepsilon}(z_{0})\right\rangle:\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}

by using Equation (
2.29
) (with
h_{4}\coloneqq h_{1}
). We can apply Proposition
3.4
on the first term and Equation (
3.5
) for the second one to rewrite the right-hand side under the form

\begin{split}&\quad\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\left\langle Q,\alpha_{k}\right\rangle}{(z_{0}-z_{k})^{2}_{\varepsilon}}\left\langle\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}-\frac{\mu\gamma q}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\left\langle 2h_{1}+h_{2},e_{i}\right\rangle}{(z_{0}-x)^{2}_{\varepsilon}}\left\langle V_{\gamma e_{i},\varepsilon}(x)\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}d^{2}x\\

&-\frac{\mu\gamma}{2}\sum_{p=1}^{3}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\left\langle h_{p},\alpha_{k}\right\rangle\left\langle h_{p+1},e_{i}\right\rangle}{(z_{0}-z_{k})_{\varepsilon}(z_{0}-x)_{\varepsilon}}+\gamma\frac{\left\langle h_{p},e_{i}\right\rangle\left\langle h_{p+1},e_{i}\right\rangle}{(z_{0}-x)_{\varepsilon}(z_{0}-x)_{\varepsilon}}\right)\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x)\left\langle V_{\gamma e_{i},\varepsilon}(x)\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}d^{2}x\\

We now turn to the other side of the expression. Along the same lines as in the proof of Proposition
3.6

\begin{split}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(\frac{\Delta_{\alpha_{k}}}{(z_{0}-z_{k})^{2}}+\frac{\partial_{z_{k}}}{(z_{0}-z_{k})}\right)\left\langle\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}&=\left(\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\Delta_{\alpha_{k}}}{(z_{0}-z_{k})^{2}}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k\neq l}\frac{\left\langle\alpha_{k},\alpha_{l}\right\rangle}{(z_{0}-z_{k})(z_{k}-z_{l})_{\varepsilon}}\right)\left\langle\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}\\
&+\frac{\mu\gamma}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\left\langle\alpha_{k},e_{i}\right\rangle\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x)}{(z_{0}-z_{k})(z_{k}-x)_{\varepsilon}}\left\langle V_{\gamma e_{i},\varepsilon}(x)\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}d^{2}x.\end{split}

Here the symmetrization step is rather straightforward. For the terms that do not involve integrals:

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k\neq l}\frac{\left\langle\alpha_{k},\alpha_{l}\right\rangle}{(z_{0}-z_{k})(z_{k}-z_{l})_{\varepsilon}}&=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{k\neq l}\frac{\left\langle\alpha_{k},\alpha_{l}\right\rangle}{(z_{k}-z_{l})_{\varepsilon}}\left(\frac{1}{z_{0}-z_{k}}-\frac{1}{z_{0}-z_{l}}\right)\\

We then transform the terms that contain one integral as

\displaystyle\frac{\mu\gamma}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\left\langle\alpha_{k},e_{i}\right\rangle\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x)}{(z_{0}-z_{k})(z_{k}-x)_{\varepsilon}}\left\langle V_{\gamma e_{i},\varepsilon}(x)\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}d^{2}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mu\gamma}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(\frac{\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x)\left\langle\alpha_{k},e_{i}\right\rangle}{(z_{0}-x)(z_{k}-x)_{\varepsilon}}+\frac{\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x)\left\langle\alpha_{k},e_{i}\right\rangle}{(z_{0}-x)(z_{0}-z_{k})}\right)\left\langle V_{\gamma e_{i},\varepsilon}(x)\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}d^{2}x

\displaystyle+\frac{\mu\gamma}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x)\left\langle\alpha_{k},e_{i}\right\rangle}{(z_{0}-x)(z_{0}-z_{k})}\left(\frac{z_{k}-x}{(z_{k}-x)_{\varepsilon}}-1\right)\left\langle V_{\gamma e_{i},\varepsilon}(x)\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}d^{2}x.

Combining our expressions yields

\displaystyle\left\langle\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{\varepsilon}(z_{0})\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}-\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(\frac{\Delta_{\alpha_{k}}}{(z_{0}-z_{k})^{2}}+\frac{\partial_{z_{k}}}{(z_{0}-z_{k})}\right)\left\langle\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{1}{(z_{0}-z_{k})^{2}}\left[\left\langle Q,\alpha_{k}\right\rangle\left(\frac{(z_{0}-z_{k})^{2}}{(z_{0}-z_{k})^{2}_{\varepsilon}}-1\right)-\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\alpha_{k},\alpha_{k}\right\rangle\left(\frac{(z_{0}-z_{k})^{2}}{(z_{0}-z_{k})_{\varepsilon}(z_{0}-z_{k})_{\varepsilon}}-1\right)\right]\left\langle\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}\sum_{k\neq l}\frac{\left\langle\alpha_{k},\alpha_{l}\right\rangle}{(z_{0}-z_{k})(z_{0}-z_{l})}\left(\frac{(z_{0}-z_{k})(z_{0}-z_{l})}{(z_{0}-z_{k})_{\varepsilon}(z_{0}-z_{l})_{\varepsilon}}-1+\frac{z_{k}-z_{l}}{(z_{k}-z_{l})_{\varepsilon}}-1\right)\left\langle\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}

\displaystyle-\frac{\mu\gamma}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x)}{(z_{0}-x)^{2}}\left(\gamma-\frac{\gamma(z_{0}-x)^{2}}{(z_{0}-x)_{\varepsilon}(z_{0}-x)_{\varepsilon}}\right)\left\langle V_{\gamma e_{i},\varepsilon}(x)\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}d^{2}x

\displaystyle-\frac{\mu\gamma}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x)}{(z_{0}-x)^{2}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{(z_{0}-x)\left\langle\alpha_{k},e_{i}\right\rangle}{(z_{0}-z_{k})}\left(\frac{z_{k}-x}{(z_{k}-x)_{\varepsilon}}-1\right)\right)\left\langle V_{\gamma e_{i},\varepsilon}(x)\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}d^{2}x

\displaystyle-\mu\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x)}{z_{0}-x}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\left\langle\alpha_{k},\gamma e_{i}\right\rangle}{2(z_{k}-x)_{\varepsilon}}-\frac{\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x)}{(z_{0}-x)^{2}_{\varepsilon}}\left\langle V_{\gamma e_{i},\varepsilon}(x)\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}d^{2}x

\displaystyle-\frac{(\mu\gamma)^{2}}{4}\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}\left\langle e_{i},e_{j}\right\rangle\int_{\mathbb{C}^{2}}\frac{\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x_{1})\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x_{2})}{(z_{0}-x_{1})_{\varepsilon}(z_{0}-x_{2})_{\varepsilon}}\left\langle V_{\gamma e_{i},\varepsilon}(x_{1})V_{\gamma e_{j},\varepsilon}(x_{2})\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}d^{2}x_{1}d^{2}x_{2}.

where we have used the fact that

From Lemma

\varepsilon\to 0

\displaystyle\partial_{x}\left\langle V_{\gamma e_{i},\varepsilon}(x)\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\left\langle\alpha_{k},\gamma e_{i}\right\rangle}{(x-z_{k})_{\varepsilon}}\left\langle V_{\gamma e_{i},\varepsilon}(x)\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}+\frac{\mu\gamma^{2}}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\left\langle e_{i},e_{j}\right\rangle}{(x-x_{2})_{\varepsilon}}\left\langle V_{\gamma e_{i},\varepsilon}(x)V_{\gamma e_{j},\varepsilon}(x_{2})\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}d^{2}x_{2}.

Using integration by parts for the term

\begin{split}&-\mu\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\left(\frac{\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x)}{z_{0}-x}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\left\langle\alpha_{k},\gamma e_{i}\right\rangle}{2(z_{k}-x)_{\varepsilon}}-\frac{\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x)}{(z_{0}-x)^{2}_{\varepsilon}}\right)\left\langle V_{\gamma e_{i},\varepsilon}(x)\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}d^{2}x\\

where

In order to prove the spin-two Ward identity (

When

Since

\lim\limits_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\left(\left\langle\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{\varepsilon}(z_{0})\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}-\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(\frac{\Delta_{\alpha_{k}}}{(z_{0}-z_{k})^{2}}+\frac{\partial_{z_{k}}}{(z_{0}-z_{k})}\right)\left\langle\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}\right)=0.

Since the correlation functions are differentiable in
z_{0}
and all

, according to Proposition

, we already know that the
\varepsilon,\delta\rightarrow 0
limit of

exists. This entails the existence of
\lim\limits_{\delta\to 0}\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\left\langle\bm{\mathrm{T}}_{\varepsilon}(z_{0})\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}
, concluding the proof of the spin-two Ward identity.

4.3.
Computation of the spin-three Ward identity

We proceed in a similar way for the spin-three Ward identity. We start by considering the regularized version of the probabilistic objects

\left\langle\bm{\mathrm{W}}_{\varepsilon}(z_{0})\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}=-\frac{q^{2}}{8}\left\langle\left\langle h_{2},\partial^{3}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(z_{0})\right\rangle\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}+\frac{q}{4}\left\langle:\left\langle h_{2}-h_{1},\partial^{2}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(z_{0})\right\rangle\left\langle h_{1},\partial\varphi_{\varepsilon}(z_{0})\right\rangle:\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}\\
+\frac{q}{4}\left\langle:\left\langle h_{3}-h_{2},\partial^{2}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(z_{0})\right\rangle\left\langle h_{3},\partial\varphi_{\varepsilon}(z_{0})\right\rangle:\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}+\left\langle:\left\langle h_{1},\partial\varphi_{\varepsilon}(z_{0}\right\rangle\left\langle h_{2},\partial\varphi_{\varepsilon}(z_{0})\right\rangle\left\langle h_{3},\partial\varphi_{\varepsilon}(z_{0})\right\rangle:\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}.

Applying Proposition
3.4
and more generally Equation (

\left\langle\bm{\mathrm{W}}_{\varepsilon}(z_{0})\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}=-\frac{1}{8}\left(q^{2}\mathrm{I}_{1}+\frac{q}{2}\mathrm{I}_{2}+\mathrm{I}_{3}\right),

where the

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt-\mu\gamma\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{B(\alpha_{k},e_{i})}{(z_{0}-z_{k})_{\varepsilon}^{2}(z_{0}-x)_{\varepsilon}}+\frac{B(e_{i},\alpha_{k})}{(z_{0}-x)^{2}_{\varepsilon}(z_{0}-z_{k})_{\varepsilon}}+\frac{\gamma B(e_{i},e_{i})}{(z_{0}-x)^{2}_{\varepsilon}(z_{0}-x)_{\varepsilon}}\right)\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x)\left\langle V_{\gamma e_{i},\varepsilon}(x)\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}d^{2}x

\displaystyle\hskip-42.67912pt-\mu\gamma\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\left(\sum_{k,l=1}^{N}\frac{C(\alpha_{k},\alpha_{l},e_{i})}{(z_{0}-z_{k})_{\varepsilon}(z_{0}-z_{l})_{\varepsilon}(z_{0}-x)_{\varepsilon}}+\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\gamma C(\alpha_{k},e_{i},e_{i})}{(z_{0}-z_{k})_{\varepsilon}(z_{0}-x)_{\varepsilon}(z_{0}-x)_{\varepsilon}}\right)\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x)\left\langle V_{\gamma e_{i},\varepsilon}(x)\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}d^{2}x

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\mu\gamma}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\left(\sum_{l\neq k}\frac{2C^{\sigma}(\alpha_{k},\alpha_{l},e_{i})}{(z_{k}-z_{l})_{\varepsilon}(z_{k}-x)_{\varepsilon}}\right)\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x)\left\langle V_{\gamma e_{i},\varepsilon}(z)\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}d^{2}x

4.3.1.
First step: symmetrizing

We now turn to the first step of the proof which consists in using symmetrization identities so that in the expression of the terms
\mathrm{J}_{-1,2}^{(k)}
the only singularities that will occur will do so around the distinguished point

. To illustrate this, let us consider the first term

we can write that

\displaystyle-\frac{\mu}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{qB(\alpha_{k},\gamma e_{i})+2C(\alpha_{k},\alpha_{k},\gamma e_{i})}{(z_{0}-x)^{2}(z_{k}-x)_{\epsilon}}\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x)\left\langle V_{\gamma e_{i},\varepsilon}(z)\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}d^{2}x+\mathfrak{R}_{-1}(\varepsilon,\delta)

with

According to Lemma
3.1
, we readily see that

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\mathfrak{R}_{-1}(\varepsilon,\delta)=0.

In the new expression provided for

\displaystyle\mu^{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{2}}\frac{C(\alpha_{k},e_{i},e_{j})}{(z_{0}-z_{k})}\left(\frac{1}{(z_{k}-x_{1})_{\varepsilon}(z_{k}-x_{2})_{\varepsilon}}-\frac{1}{(x_{1}-x_{2})_{\varepsilon}}\left(\frac{1}{(z_{k}-x_{1})_{\varepsilon}}-\frac{1}{(z_{k}-x_{2})_{\varepsilon}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x_{1})\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x_{2})\left\langle V_{\gamma e_{i},\varepsilon}(x_{1})V_{\gamma e_{j},\varepsilon}(x_{2})\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}d^{2}x_{1}d^{2}x_{2}.

However making the change of variables
x\leftrightarrow 4R\varepsilon x
(where recall
R
from the regularization scheme (
2.15
)) and using the same reasoning as in the proof of Lemma
3.1
we see that actually such quantities will vanish in the
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
limit. Therefore
\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\mathfrak{R}(\varepsilon,\delta)=0
so we may no longer consider it in subsequent computations.

4.3.2.
Second step: integrating by parts

We are now in position to address the second step of our proof, that is to use integration by parts in order to remove the singular terms that occur away from
z_{0}
. Indeed we can notice that

\partial_{x}\left(\frac{1}{(z_{x}-x)_{\varepsilon}}\left\langle V_{\gamma e_{i},\varepsilon}(x)\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}\right)=\left(\frac{1+\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\alpha_{k},\gamma e_{i}\right\rangle}{(z_{k}-x)^{2}_{\varepsilon}}+\sum_{l\neq k}\frac{\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\alpha_{l},\gamma e_{i}\right\rangle}{(z_{k}-x)_{\varepsilon}(z_{l}-x)_{\varepsilon}}\right)\left\langle V_{\gamma e_{i},\varepsilon}(x)\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}\\
+\frac{\mu\gamma^{2}}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\left\langle e_{i},e_{j}\right\rangle\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x_{2})}{(z_{k}-x)_{\varepsilon}(x-x_{2})_{\varepsilon}}\left\langle V_{\gamma e_{i},\varepsilon}(x)V_{\gamma e_{j},\varepsilon}(x_{2})\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}d^{2}x_{2}.

This implies, using integration by parts (thanks to Lemma
3.2
this is indeed possible), that

\displaystyle 2\mu\sum_{i=1}^{2}\left\langle h_{2},e_{i}\right\rangle\int_{\mathbb{C}}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x)\omega_{3-i}(\alpha_{k})}{(z_{0}-x)(z_{k}-x)_{\varepsilon}}\left(\frac{1+\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\alpha_{k},\gamma e_{i}\right\rangle}{(z_{k}-x)_{\varepsilon}}+\sum_{l\neq k}\frac{\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\alpha_{l},\gamma e_{i}\right\rangle}{(z_{l}-x)_{\varepsilon}}\right)\left\langle V_{\gamma e_{i},\varepsilon}(x)\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}d^{2}x

\displaystyle=-2\mu\sum_{i=1}^{2}\left\langle h_{2},e_{i}\right\rangle\int_{\mathbb{C}}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\partial_{x}\left(\frac{\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x)}{(z_{0}-x)}\right)\frac{\omega_{3-i}(\alpha_{k})}{(z_{k}-x)_{\varepsilon}}\left\langle V_{\gamma e_{i},\varepsilon}(x)\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}d^{2}x

\displaystyle-(\mu\gamma)^{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}\left\langle h_{2},e_{i}\right\rangle\left\langle e_{i},e_{j}\right\rangle\int_{\mathbb{C}^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x_{1})\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x_{2})\omega_{3-i}(\alpha_{k})}{(z_{0}-x_{1})(z_{k}-x_{1})_{\varepsilon}(x_{1}-x_{2})_{\varepsilon}}\left\langle V_{\gamma e_{i},\varepsilon}(x_{1})V_{\gamma e_{j},\varepsilon}(x_{2})\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}d^{2}x_{1}d^{2}x_{2}.

Since
B(e_{i},\alpha_{k})+2C(\alpha_{k},e_{i},e_{i})=2\left\langle h_{2},e_{i}\right\rangle\omega_{i}(\alpha_{k})+\left\langle h_{2},e_{i}\right\rangle\omega_{3-i}(\alpha_{k})=\left\langle h_{2},e_{i}\right\rangle\left\langle e_{i},\alpha_{k}\right\rangle
, we can use again integration by parts to get that

\displaystyle\frac{\mu\gamma}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{qB(e_{i},\alpha_{k})+2C(\alpha_{k},\gamma e_{i},e_{i})}{(z_{0}-x)^{2}(z_{k}-x)_{\varepsilon}}\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x)\left\langle V_{\gamma e_{i},\varepsilon}(x)\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}d^{2}x

\displaystyle+2\mu\sum_{i=1}^{2}\left\langle h_{2},e_{i}\right\rangle\int_{\mathbb{C}}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x)\omega_{3-i}(\alpha_{k})}{(z_{0}-x)(z_{k}-x)_{\varepsilon}}\left(\frac{1+\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\alpha_{k},\gamma e_{i}\right\rangle}{(z_{k}-x)_{\varepsilon}}+\sum_{l\neq k}\frac{\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\alpha_{l},\gamma e_{i}\right\rangle}{(z_{l}-x)_{\varepsilon}}\right)\left\langle V_{\gamma e_{i},\varepsilon}(x)\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}d^{2}x

is actually equal to

\displaystyle-\mu q\sum_{i=1}^{2}\left\langle h_{2},e_{i}\right\rangle\int_{\mathbb{C}}\partial_{x}\left(\frac{\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x)}{(z_{0}-x)^{2}}\right)\left\langle V_{\gamma e_{i},\varepsilon}(x)\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}d^{2}x

\displaystyle-2\mu\sum_{i=1}^{2}\left\langle h_{2},e_{i}\right\rangle\int_{\mathbb{C}}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\partial_{x}\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x)}{(z_{0}-x)}\frac{\left\langle\alpha_{k},\omega_{3-i}\right\rangle}{(z_{k}-x)_{\varepsilon}}\left\langle V_{\gamma e_{i},\varepsilon}(x)\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}d^{2}x

\displaystyle-\frac{(\mu\gamma)^{2}q}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}\left\langle h_{2},e_{i}\right\rangle\left\langle e_{i},e_{j}\right\rangle\int_{\mathbb{C}^{2}}\frac{\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x_{1})\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x_{2})}{(z_{0}-x)^{2}(x_{1}-x_{2})_{\varepsilon}}\left\langle V_{\gamma e_{i},\varepsilon}(x)\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}d^{2}x

\displaystyle-(\mu\gamma)^{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}\left\langle h_{2},e_{i}\right\rangle\left\langle e_{i},e_{j}\right\rangle\int_{\mathbb{C}^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x_{1})\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x_{2})\omega_{3-i}(\alpha_{k})}{(z_{0}-x_{1})(z_{k}-x_{1})_{\varepsilon}(x_{1}-x_{2})_{\varepsilon}}\left\langle V_{\gamma e_{i},\varepsilon}(x_{1})V_{\gamma e_{j},\varepsilon}(x_{2})\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}d^{2}x_{1}d^{2}x_{2}.

As a consequence we see that in the expression of

q^{2}\mathrm{I}_{1}+\frac{q}{2}\mathrm{I}_{2}+\mathrm{I}_{3}-\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(\frac{w^{(3)}(\alpha_{k})}{(z_{0}-z_{k})^{3}}+\frac{\mathrm{J}_{-1}^{(k)}}{(z_{0}-z_{k})^{2}}+\frac{\mathrm{J}_{-2}^{(k)}}{z_{0}-z_{k}}\right)

the only remaining terms are the
1
-fold integral given by

\mathfrak{R}_{1}(\varepsilon,\delta)\coloneqq-\mu\sum_{i=1}^{2}\left\langle h_{2},e_{i}\right\rangle\int_{\mathbb{C}}\left(\frac{q\partial_{x}\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x)}{(z_{0}-x)^{2}}+2\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\partial_{x}\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x)}{(z_{0}-x)}\frac{\left\langle\alpha_{k},\omega_{3-i}\right\rangle}{(z_{k}-x)_{\varepsilon}}\right)\left\langle V_{\gamma e_{i},\varepsilon}(z)\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}d^{2}x

as well as, using the definitions of
B
and
C
, the
2
and
3
-fold integrals

\displaystyle+2\mu^{2}\gamma\sum_{i\neq j}\left\langle h_{2},e_{i}\right\rangle\int_{\mathbb{C}^{2}}\frac{\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x_{1})}{(z_{0}-x_{1})^{2}}\frac{\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x_{2})}{(x_{1}-x_{2})_{\varepsilon}}\left\langle V_{\gamma e_{i},\varepsilon}(x_{1})V_{\gamma e_{j},\varepsilon}(x_{2})\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}d^{2}x_{1}d^{2}x_{2}

\displaystyle-2\mu^{2}\gamma\sum_{i\neq j}\left\langle h_{2},e_{i}\right\rangle\int_{\mathbb{C}^{2}}\frac{\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x_{1})}{(z_{0}-x_{1})}\frac{\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x_{2})}{(x_{1}-x_{2})_{\varepsilon}^{2}}\left\langle V_{\gamma e_{i},\varepsilon}(x_{1})V_{\gamma e_{j},\varepsilon}(x_{2})\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}d^{2}x_{1}d^{2}x_{2}

\displaystyle+(\mu\gamma)^{2}\sum_{i\neq j}\left\langle h_{2},e_{i}\right\rangle\int_{\mathbb{C}^{2}}\frac{\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x_{1})\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x_{2})}{(z_{0}-x_{1})(x_{1}-x_{2})_{\varepsilon}}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\left\langle\alpha_{k},e_{i}\right\rangle}{(z_{k}-x_{1})_{\varepsilon}}\left\langle V_{\gamma e_{i},\varepsilon}(x_{1})V_{\gamma e_{j},\varepsilon}(x_{2})\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}d^{2}x_{1}d^{2}x_{2}

\displaystyle+(\mu\gamma)^{3}\sum_{i\neq j}\left\langle h_{2},e_{i}\right\rangle\int_{\mathbb{C}^{3}}\frac{\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x_{1})\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x_{2})\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x_{3})}{(z_{0}-x_{1})(x_{1}-x_{2})_{\varepsilon}(x_{1}-x_{3})_{\varepsilon}}\left\langle V_{\gamma e_{i},\varepsilon}(x_{1})V_{\gamma e_{i},\varepsilon}(x_{2})V_{\gamma e_{j},\varepsilon}(x_{3})\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}d^{2}x_{1}d^{2}x_{2}d^{2}x_{3}.

We can use integration by parts in the same way as we have proceeded for the
1
-fold integrals. For this we notice that the above quantity is nothing but

-2\mu^{2}\gamma\sum_{i\neq j}\left\langle h_{2},e_{i}\right\rangle\int_{\mathbb{C}^{2}}\partial_{x_{1}}\left(\frac{\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x_{1})}{(z_{0}-x_{1})}\frac{\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x_{2})}{(x_{1}-x_{2})_{\varepsilon}}\left\langle V_{\gamma e_{i},\varepsilon}(x_{1})V_{\gamma e_{j},\varepsilon}(x_{2})\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}\right)d^{2}x_{1}d^{2}x_{2}+\mathfrak{R}_{2}(\varepsilon,\delta)

where we have introduced another remainder term

\mathfrak{R}_{2}(\varepsilon,\delta)\coloneqq-2\mu^{2}\gamma\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}\left\langle h_{2},e_{i}\right\rangle\int_{\mathbb{C}^{2}}\frac{\partial_{x_{1}}\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x_{1})\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x_{2})}{(z_{0}-x_{1})(x_{1}-x_{2})_{\varepsilon}}\left\langle V_{\gamma e_{i},\varepsilon}(x_{1})V_{\gamma e_{j},\varepsilon}(x_{2})\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}d^{2}x_{1}d^{2}x_{2}.

Combining all the terms we get

q^{2}\mathrm{I}_{1}+\frac{q}{2}\mathrm{I}_{2}+\mathrm{I}_{3}-\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(\frac{w^{(3)}(\alpha_{k})}{(z_{0}-z_{k})^{3}}+\frac{\mathrm{J}_{-1}^{(k)}}{(z_{0}-z_{k})^{2}}+\frac{\mathrm{J}_{-2}^{(k)}}{z_{0}-z_{k}}\right)=\mathfrak{R}(\varepsilon,\delta)+\mathfrak{R}_{1}(\varepsilon,\delta)+\mathfrak{R}_{2}(\varepsilon,\delta).

4.3.3.
Last step: taking the limit

Therefore in order to prove the Ward identity (
1.22
) it suffices to show that the remaining terms
\mathfrak{R}_{1,2}
actually vanish as
\varepsilon,\delta\rightarrow 0
, since we have already seen that the term
\mathfrak{R}(\varepsilon,\delta)
coming from the symmetrization was disappearing in the limit. As we will see this is ensured by the regularity of the correlation functions, as stated in Proposition
3.6
.

Indeed, let us start by considering
\mathfrak{R}_{1}(\varepsilon,\delta)
. We need to ensure the convergence as
\varepsilon,\delta\rightarrow 0
of the one-fold integral involved. To start with, we can use integration by parts following the lines of the proof of Proposition
3.6
to see that the limit of this term is actually given by the
\delta\rightarrow 0
limit of expressions of the type

\int_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\partial_{x}\theta_{\delta}(x)}{x^{2}}F_{\delta}(x)d^{2}x,

where, thanks to Proposition
3.6
,
(F_{\delta})_{\delta>0}
is uniformly bounded in
C^{1}
in a neighbourhood of the origin. Noticing that
\partial_{x}\theta_{\delta}(x)
is supported in the annulus
A(1/2,1)
of radii
1/2,1
centered at the origin and making the change of variable
x\leftrightarrow\delta x
the latter may be put under the form

\int_{A(1/2,1)}\frac{\partial_{x}\theta_{1}(x)}{x^{2}}\frac{F_{\delta}(\delta x)}{\delta}d^{2}x.

Since
F_{\delta}
is
C^{1}
we can write its Taylor expansion near the origin; also since
\theta(x)
is rotation invariant we know that

\int_{A(1/2,1)}\frac{\partial\theta_{1}(x)}{x^{2}}x^{k}d^{2}x=\int_{A(1/2,1)}\frac{\partial\theta_{1}(x)}{x^{2}}\bar{x}^{-k}d^{2}x=0

for any integer
k\neq 3
. As a consequence integrating the Taylor expansion
F_{\delta}(\delta x)=F_{\delta}(0)+\delta\left(x\partial_{x}F_{\delta}(0)+\bar{x}\partial_{\bar{x}}F_{\delta}(0)\right)+o(\delta)
yields
0
plus a term which does vanish in the limit.
The expression
\mathfrak{R}_{2}(\varepsilon,\delta)
vanishes using the same reasoning; one subtlety here being that we have to consider what happens when
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
get close one to the other. However the same reasoning as the one we have described above combined with the one used in the proof of Proposition
3.6
allows to conclude in the same way. This shows that the following is true:

\lim\limits_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\left(\left\langle\bm{\mathrm{W}}_{\varepsilon}(z_{0})\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}+\frac{1}{8}\sum_{l=1}^{N}\left(\frac{w(\alpha_{l})}{(z_{0}-z_{l})^{3}}+\frac{\bm{\mathcal{W}_{-1,\varepsilon}}(z_{l},\alpha_{l})}{(z_{0}-z_{l})^{2}}+\frac{\bm{\mathcal{W}_{-2,\varepsilon}}(z_{l},\alpha_{l})}{z_{0}-z_{l}}\right)\left\langle\prod_{k=1}^{N}V_{\alpha_{k},\varepsilon}(z_{k})\right\rangle_{\delta}\right)=0.

To finish up with the proof of our main result, it remains to show that the limiting quantities

\bm{\mathcal{W}}_{-i}(z_{l},\alpha_{l})\left\langle\bm{\mathrm{V}}\right\rangle=\lim\limits_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\bm{\mathcal{W}}_{-i,\varepsilon}(z_{l},\alpha_{l})\left\langle\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}

do exist for
i=1,2
. Convergence of
\bm{\mathcal{W}}_{-1}(z_{l},\alpha_{l})\left\langle\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}
follows from the very same argument that allows to prove the differentiability of the correlation functions. Namely quantities of the form

\int_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{1}{(z_{l}-x)_{\varepsilon}}\left\langle V_{\gamma e_{i},\varepsilon}(x)\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle d^{2}x

admit a limit when
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
. As for
\bm{\mathcal{W}}_{-2,\varepsilon}(z_{l},\alpha_{l})\left\langle\bm{\mathrm{V}_{\varepsilon}}\right\rangle_{\delta}
, we note that

q\left(B(e_{i},\alpha)-B(\alpha,e_{i})\right)+2C(\alpha,\gamma e_{i}-\alpha,e_{i})=\frac{4}{\gamma}\left\langle h_{2},e_{i}\right\rangle\omega_{3-i}(\alpha)\left(1+\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\alpha,\gamma e_{i}\right\rangle\right).

As a consequence symmetrization identities allow to rewrite the integrals in
\bm{\mathcal{W}}_{-2,\varepsilon}(z_{k},\alpha_{k})\left\langle\bm{\mathrm{V}_{\varepsilon}}\right\rangle
as

\displaystyle-\mu\gamma\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\sum_{l\neq k}\left(\frac{C^{\sigma}(\alpha_{k},\alpha_{l},e_{i})}{(z_{k}-z_{l})_{\varepsilon}(z_{k}-x)_{\varepsilon}}-\frac{\left\langle h_{2},e_{i}\right\rangle\omega_{3-i}(\alpha_{k})\left\langle\alpha_{l},e_{i}\right\rangle}{(z_{l}-x)_{\varepsilon}(z_{k}-x)_{\varepsilon}}\right)\left\langle V_{\gamma e_{1},\varepsilon}(x)\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}d^{2}x

\displaystyle+(\mu\gamma)^{2}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{2}}\frac{\left\langle e_{1},\alpha_{k}\right\rangle}{(z_{k}-x_{1})_{\varepsilon}(x_{1}-x_{2})_{\varepsilon}}+\frac{\left\langle e_{2},\alpha_{k}\right\rangle}{(z_{k}-x_{2})_{\varepsilon}(x_{1}-x_{2})_{\varepsilon}}\left\langle V_{\gamma e_{1},\varepsilon}(x_{1})V_{\gamma e_{2},\varepsilon}(x_{2})\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}d^{2}x_{1}d^{2}x_{2}.

We can proceed in the same way as in the proof that the correlation functions are
C^{2}
(see Subsection
6.3
) to show that the limit does exist. This wraps up the proof of our main result.

4.4.
Global Ward identities

The Ward identity (
1.22
) that we have just proved shows that the probabilistic model thus defined is indeed consistent with the expectations of the physics literature and may be understood as a manifestation of the higher-spin symmetry enjoyed by the
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
Toda theory. In addition to this identity there is a second building block which is fundamental in the study of Toda theories: the existence of global Ward identities (
1.27
) which are manifestations of the (global) higher-spin symmetry of the theory. In order to prove that the equations (
1.27
) hold we will prove that the higher-spin current is “holomorphic at infinity”, a fact which is axiomatic in the physics literature and by which is meant that

\bm{\mathrm{W}}(z_{0})\sim\frac{1}{z_{0}^{6}}

as
z_{0}\rightarrow\infty
. To do so we rely on the fact that
\bm{\mathrm{W}}
behaves like a covariant tensor of order three in the following sense.

Proposition 4.1
.

Under the Seiberg bounds (
2.25
) and for any Möbius transform
\psi
of the plane

(4.1)

\left\langle\bm{\mathrm{W}}(z_{0})\prod_{k=1}^{N}V_{\alpha_{k}}(z_{k})\right\rangle=\psi^{\prime}(z_{0})^{3}\prod_{l=1}^{N}\left\lvert\psi^{\prime}(z_{l})\right\rvert^{2\Delta_{\alpha_{l}}}\left\langle\bm{\mathrm{W}}(\psi(z_{0}))\prod_{k=1}^{N}V_{\alpha_{k}}(\psi(z_{k}))\right\rangle.

In particular when
\psi:z\mapsto\frac{1}{z}
the asymptotic behaviour of
\bm{\mathrm{W}}(z_{0})
as
z_{0}\rightarrow\infty
is given by

(4.2)

\left\langle\bm{\mathrm{W}}(z_{0})\prod_{k=1}^{N}V_{\alpha_{k}}(z_{k})\right\rangle\sim-\frac{1}{z_{0}^{6}}\prod_{l=1}^{N}\left\lvert z_{l}\right\rvert^{-4\Delta_{\alpha_{l}}}\left\langle\bm{\mathrm{W}}(0)\prod_{k=1}^{N}V_{\alpha_{k}}(\frac{1}{z_{k}})\right\rangle.

Assume for now that this statement holds. Then we see that, when
z_{0}\rightarrow\infty
the left-hand side in the Ward identity (
1.22
) is therefore asymptotic to
\frac{1}{z_{0}^{6}}
. On the other hand the leading term in the right-hand side of Equation (
1.22
) is given (up to the prefactor
-\frac{1}{8}
) by

\frac{1}{z_{0}}\sum_{l=1}^{N}\bm{\mathcal{W}_{-2}}(z_{l},\alpha_{l})\left\langle\prod_{k=1}^{N}V_{\alpha_{k}}(z_{k})\right\rangle.

Therefore in order for these two asymptotics to be consistent we need to assume that that the
n=0
global Ward identity (
1.27
) holds:

\sum_{l=1}^{N}\bm{\mathcal{W}_{-2}}(z_{l},\alpha_{l})\left\langle\prod_{k=1}^{N}V_{\alpha_{k}}(z_{k})\right\rangle=0.

We may proceed in the same way for the other terms that appear in the asymptotic of the right-hand side of Equation (
1.27
). To do so let us wite the right-hand side of Equation (
1.22
) as

-\frac{1}{8}\sum_{l=1}^{N}\left(\frac{1}{z_{0}^{3}}\frac{w(\alpha_{l})}{(1-\frac{z_{l}}{z_{0}})^{3}}+\frac{1}{z_{0}^{2}}\frac{\bm{\mathcal{W}_{-1}^{(l)}}}{(1-\frac{z_{l}}{z_{0}})^{2}}+\frac{1}{z_{0}}\frac{\bm{\mathcal{W}_{-2}^{(l)}}}{1-\frac{z_{l}}{z_{0}}}\right)\left\langle\prod_{k=1}^{N}V_{\alpha_{k}}(z_{k})\right\rangle

and expand in powers of
\frac{1}{z_{0}}
the terms of the form
\frac{1}{(1-\frac{z_{l}}{z_{0}})^{p}}
,
p=1,2,3
. Then the asymptotic as
z_{0}\rightarrow\infty
can be expanded as negative powers of
z_{0}
, and corresponding coefficients in this expansion are given by

\frac{1}{z_{0}^{p}}\sum_{l=1}^{N}\left(z_{l}^{p-1}\bm{\mathcal{W}_{-2}}(z_{l},\alpha_{l})+(p-1)z_{l}^{p-2}\bm{\mathcal{W}_{-1}}(z_{l},\alpha_{l})+\frac{(p-1)(p-2)}{2}z_{l}^{n-2}w(\alpha_{l})\right)\left\langle\prod_{k=1}^{N}V_{\alpha_{k}}(z_{k})\right\rangle.

Therefore to be consistent with the asymptotic prescribed by Equation (
4.2
) we need to have, as soon as
0\;\leqslant\;n\;\leqslant\;4
,

\sum_{l=1}^{N}\left(z_{l}^{n}\bm{\mathcal{W}_{-2}}(z_{l},\alpha_{l})+nz_{l}^{n-1}\bm{\mathcal{W}_{-1}}(z_{l},\alpha_{l})+\frac{n(n-1)}{2}z_{l}^{n-3}w(\alpha_{l})\right)\left\langle\prod_{k=1}^{N}V_{\alpha_{k}}(z_{k})\right\rangle=0.

In other words the global Ward identities (
1.27
) hold.

Remark 4.2
.

With a little bit of extra work it is possible to show that the right-hand side in the asymptotic of Equation (
4.2
) is given by

-\frac{1}{8}\sum_{l=1}^{N}\left(z_{l}^{5}\bm{\mathcal{W}_{-2}}(z_{l},\alpha_{l})+5z_{l}^{4}\bm{\mathcal{W}_{-1}}(z_{l},\alpha_{l})+10z_{l}^{3}w(\alpha_{l})\right)\left\langle\prod_{k=1}^{N}V_{\alpha_{k}}(z_{k})\right\rangle

so we may not learn anything new from the exact value of the leading term in the asymptotic of the higher-spin current.

Proof of Proposition
4.1
.

Let us come back to the regularized correlation functions
\left\langle\bm{\mathrm{W}}_{\varepsilon}(z_{0})\prod_{k=1}^{N}V_{\alpha_{k},\varepsilon}(z_{k})\right\rangle
defined thanks to the mollified field

\varphi_{\varepsilon}=\bm{\mathrm{X}}_{\varepsilon}+\frac{Q}{2}\ln\hat{g}_{\varepsilon}+\bm{c}
. Since the latter is smooth, its derivatives and the ones of
\varphi_{\varepsilon}\circ\psi
are well-defined. They are given by

\displaystyle\partial\left(\varphi_{\varepsilon}\circ\psi\right)

\displaystyle=\psi^{\prime}\partial\varphi_{\varepsilon}\circ\psi

\displaystyle\partial^{2}\left(\varphi_{\varepsilon}\circ\psi\right)

\displaystyle=\psi^{\prime\prime}\partial\varphi_{\varepsilon}\circ\psi+(\psi^{\prime})^{2}\partial^{2}\varphi_{\varepsilon}\circ\psi

\displaystyle\partial^{3}\left(\varphi_{\varepsilon}\circ\psi\right)

\displaystyle=\psi^{\prime\prime\prime}\partial\varphi_{\varepsilon}\circ\psi+3\psi^{\prime\prime}\psi^{\prime}\partial^{2}\varphi_{\varepsilon}\circ\psi+(\psi^{\prime})^{3}\partial^{3}\varphi_{\varepsilon}\circ\psi.

We can proceed in the same way for the map
\ln\left\lvert\psi^{\prime}\right\rvert
and yields

\displaystyle\partial\left(\ln\left\lvert\psi^{\prime}\right\rvert\right)=\frac{\psi^{\prime\prime}}{2\psi^{\prime}}\quad\text{and}\quad\partial^{2}\left(\ln\left\lvert\psi^{\prime}\right\rvert\right)=\frac{\psi^{\prime\prime\prime}\psi^{\prime}-(\psi^{\prime\prime})^{2}}{2(\psi^{\prime})^{2}}\cdot

We can now apply the tensor
\bm{\mathrm{W}}
to the field
\varphi_{\varepsilon}\circ\psi+Q\ln\left\lvert\psi^{\prime}\right\rvert
instead of
\varphi_{\varepsilon}
, yielding

\displaystyle\bm{\mathrm{W}}(\varphi_{\varepsilon}\circ\psi+Q\ln\left\lvert\psi^{\prime}\right\rvert)=-\frac{q^{2}}{8}\left\langle h_{2},(\psi^{\prime})^{3}\partial^{3}\varphi_{\varepsilon}+3\psi^{\prime\prime}\psi^{\prime}\partial^{2}\varphi_{\varepsilon}+\psi^{\prime\prime\prime}\partial\varphi_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle\circ\psi

\displaystyle+\frac{q}{4}\Big(\psi^{\prime}\left\langle h_{2}-h_{1},\psi^{\prime\prime}\partial\varphi_{\varepsilon}+(\psi^{\prime})^{2}\partial^{2}\varphi_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle\left\langle h_{1},\partial\varphi_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle+\psi^{\prime}\left\langle h_{3}-h_{2},\psi^{\prime\prime}\partial\varphi_{\varepsilon}+(\psi^{\prime})^{2}\partial^{2}\varphi_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle\left\langle h_{3},\partial\varphi_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle

\displaystyle+q\frac{\psi^{\prime\prime}}{2\psi^{\prime}}\left\langle 3h_{2},\psi^{\prime\prime}\partial\varphi_{\varepsilon}+(\psi^{\prime})^{2}\partial^{2}\varphi_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle+q\frac{\psi^{\prime\prime\prime}\psi^{\prime}-(\psi^{\prime\prime})^{2}}{2\psi^{\prime}}\left\langle h_{2},\partial\varphi_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle\Big)\circ\psi

\displaystyle+(\psi^{\prime})^{3}\left\langle h_{1},\partial\varphi_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle\left\langle h_{2},\partial\varphi_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle\left\langle h_{3},\partial\varphi_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle\circ\psi+\frac{q}{2}\psi^{\prime}\psi^{\prime\prime}\left\langle h_{2},\partial\varphi_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle\left\langle h_{3}-h_{1},\partial\varphi_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle\circ\psi-q^{2}\frac{(\psi^{\prime\prime})^{2}}{4\psi^{\prime}}\left\langle h_{2},\partial\varphi_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle\circ\psi.

Therefore we end up with the equality

\bm{\mathrm{W}}(\varphi_{\varepsilon}\circ\psi+Q\ln\left\lvert\psi^{\prime}\right\rvert)=(\psi^{\prime})^{3}\bm{\mathrm{W}}(\varphi_{\varepsilon})\circ\psi.

On the other hand we know from
[
8
, Equation (3.8)]
that, provided that the limit exists,

(4.3)

\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\left\langle F(\varphi_{\varepsilon})\prod_{k=1}^{N}V_{\alpha_{k},\varepsilon}(z_{k})\right\rangle=\prod_{k=1}^{N}\left\lvert\psi^{\prime}(z_{k})\right\rvert^{2\Delta_{\alpha_{k}}}\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\left\langle F(\varphi_{\varepsilon}\circ\psi+Q\ln\left\lvert\psi^{\prime}\right\rvert)\prod_{k=1}^{N}V_{\alpha_{k},\varepsilon}(\psi(z_{k}))\right\rangle

for any continuous map
F
on
\mathrm{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{C}\to\mathfrak{h}_{3}^{*},\hat{g})
.
As a consequence with
F=\bm{\mathrm{W}}
, in the
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
limit we will be left with

\left\langle\bm{\mathrm{W}}(z_{0})\prod_{k=1}^{N}V_{\alpha_{k}}(z_{k})\right\rangle=\psi^{\prime}(z_{0})^{3}\prod_{k=1}^{N}\left\lvert\psi^{\prime}(z_{k})\right\rvert^{2\Delta_{\alpha_{k}}}\left\langle\bm{\mathrm{W}}(\psi(z_{0}))\prod_{k=1}^{N}V_{\alpha_{k}}(\psi(z_{k}))\right\rangle.

This is the result we were looking for.
∎

5.
Degenerate fields and a BPZ-type equation

In this section we shed light on another manifestation, beyond the Ward identities (
1.22
) and (
1.27
), of the higher-spin symmetry enjoyed by the
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
Toda theory. This is achieved by showing existence of degenerate fields —that correspond to singular vectors in
W
-algebras— which in turn implies a BPZ-type differential equation on certain correlation functions. To do so we rely on the explicit form of the
W
-descendent fields shown in the present document. To the best of our knowledge this approach is entirely new and differs from the standard method based on the study of singular vectors of
W
-algebras. For more details on this method see
e.g.
Parts 2 and 3 in
[
17
]
. Throughout this section we will denote by
\bm{\mathrm{V}}=\prod_{k=1}^{N}V_{\alpha_{k}}(z_{k})
a product of Vertex Operators and by
\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}
its regularized counterpart.

5.1.
A first application to degenerate fields at level one

To start with let us investigate the existence of (partially) degenerate fields at the level one. These correspond to Vertex Operators
V_{\alpha}
for which the weight
\alpha
takes a special value, which allows to write down the quantity
\bm{\mathcal{W}_{-1}}
in terms of derivatives of the correlation functions.

As explained throughout this document, the first
W
-descendent can be expressed in terms of the Toda field via

\bm{\mathcal{W}_{-1}^{(1)}}\left\langle\bm{\mathrm{V}}\right\rangle=\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\left\langle\bm{\mathrm{W}_{-1,\varepsilon}}(z_{1},\alpha_{1})\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle

where we have set, as before,

\bm{\mathrm{W}_{-1,\varepsilon}}(z,\alpha)=-qB(\alpha,\partial\varphi_{\varepsilon}(z))-2C(\alpha,\alpha,\partial\varphi_{\varepsilon}(z)).

Our goal here is to express
\bm{\mathcal{W}_{-1}}
in terms of derivatives of the field, and to do so we may introduce the first Virasoro descendent of
V_{\alpha_{1}}
by setting

\bm{\mathcal{L}_{-1}^{(1)}}\left\langle\bm{\mathrm{V}}\right\rangle\coloneqq\partial_{z_{1}}\left\langle\bm{\mathrm{V}}\right\rangle.

Then we use Equation (
2.22
) to rewrite the derivative of the correlation functions (we omit the
\delta
-dependence of the correlation functions since
z_{1}
does not appear in the correlation functions we consider) as

\partial_{z_{1}}\left\langle\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle=\Big\langle\left\langle\alpha_{1},\partial\varphi_{\varepsilon}(z_{1})\right\rangle\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\Big\rangle.

Hence the Virasoro descendent
\bm{\mathcal{L}_{-1}^{(1)}}\left\langle\bm{\mathrm{V}}\right\rangle
can be represented as the limit

\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\Big\langle\left\langle\alpha_{1},\partial\varphi_{\varepsilon}(z_{1})\right\rangle\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\Big\rangle.

As a consequence we can express the first
W
-descendent in terms of the first Virasoro descendent when
\bm{\mathrm{W}_{-1,\varepsilon}}(z,\alpha)
and
\left\langle\alpha,\partial\varphi_{\varepsilon}(z)\right\rangle
are proportional. Therefore we will say that the primary field is degenerate at the level one when there exists a real
\kappa
for which

\bm{\mathrm{W}_{-1,\varepsilon}}(z,\alpha)=\kappa\left\langle\alpha,\partial\varphi_{\varepsilon}(z)\right\rangle.

Put differently we need to find
\alpha
such that for all
u\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
,

-qB(\alpha,u)-2C(\alpha,\alpha,u)=\kappa\left\langle\alpha,u\right\rangle.

Taking respectively
u=e_{1}
and
u=e_{2}
and setting
\alpha\coloneqq\alpha^{1}\omega_{1}+\alpha^{2}\omega_{2}
we see that we must have

\displaystyle\kappa\alpha^{1}=q\alpha^{1}-2\alpha^{1}\frac{2\alpha^{2}+\alpha^{1}}{3}\quad\text{and}\quad\kappa\alpha^{2}=-q\alpha^{2}+2\alpha^{2}\frac{2\alpha^{1}+\alpha^{2}}{3}

which implies that either
\alpha^{1}\alpha^{2}=0
or
q=\alpha^{1}+\alpha^{2}
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Degenerate fields at level one are given by the
V_{\alpha}
with
\alpha
of the form
\chi\omega_{1}
or
\chi\omega_{2}
for
\chi<q
, or
\lambda\omega_{1}+(q-\lambda)\omega_{2}
for some
0<\lambda<q
. In that case we have the relation

(5.1)

\bm{\mathcal{W}_{-1}^{(1)}}\left\langle V_{\alpha}(z_{1})\prod_{k=2}^{N}V_{\alpha_{k}}(z_{k})\right\rangle=\frac{3w(\alpha)}{2\Delta_{\alpha}}\partial_{z_{1}}\left\langle V_{\alpha}(z_{1})\prod_{k=2}^{N}V_{\alpha_{k}}(z_{k})\right\rangle,

valid as soon as the
\bm{z}
are distinct and the
\bm{\alpha}
satisfies the Seiberg bounds (
2.25
).

5.2.
Degenerate fields at the levels two and three

We may proceed in the same way for other descendents at the levels two and three. Vertex Operators that satisfy similar requirements as above but for descendents at the second or third level will be called
degenerate fields at the second or third level
. These are also sometimes referred to as
fully degenerate fields
by contrast with degenerate fields at the first level only. In order to keep notations simple we will omit the Wick product convention in this subsection: for instance quantities such as
\left\langle\partial\varphi(z),\partial\varphi(z)\right\rangle
are to be understood as
:\left\langle\partial\varphi(z),\partial\varphi(z)\right\rangle:
. Similarly we no longer use the
\theta
-regularization of the correlation functions in order not to overload notations.

We start by considering the Virasoro descendents of order two. We define them by the expressions

(5.2)

\displaystyle\bm{\mathrm{L}_{-(1,1)}}(z,\alpha)\coloneqq\left\langle\alpha,\partial^{2}\varphi(z)\right\rangle+\left\langle\alpha,\partial\varphi(z)\right\rangle^{2},

(5.3)

\displaystyle\bm{\mathrm{L}_{-2}}(z,\alpha)\coloneqq\left\langle Q+\alpha,\partial^{2}\varphi(z)\right\rangle-\left\langle\partial\varphi(z),\partial\varphi(z)\right\rangle.

By doing so it is immediate to see that

\displaystyle\left\langle\bm{\mathrm{L}_{-(1,1)}}(z_{1},\alpha_{1})\bm{\mathrm{V}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\partial_{z_{1}}^{2}\left\langle\bm{\mathrm{V}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left(\bm{\mathcal{L}_{-1}^{(1)}}\right)^{2}\left\langle\bm{\mathrm{V}}\right\rangle.

The effect of the
L_{-2}
descendent on the correlation functions is less obvious; however conformal invariance allows to claim the following statement.

Lemma 5.2
.

Set
\bm{\mathcal{L}_{-2}^{(1)}}\left\langle\bm{\mathrm{V}}\right\rangle\coloneqq\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\left\langle\bm{\mathrm{L}_{-2,\varepsilon}}(z,\alpha)V_{\alpha,\varepsilon}(z)\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle
. Then

(5.4)

\bm{\mathcal{L}_{-2}^{(1)}}\left\langle V_{\alpha}(z)\bm{\mathrm{V}}\right\rangle=\sum_{k=2}^{N}\left(\frac{\partial_{z_{k}}}{z-z_{k}}+\frac{\Delta_{\alpha_{k}}}{(z-z_{k})^{2}}\right)\left\langle V_{\alpha}(z)\bm{\mathrm{V}}\right\rangle.

Proof.

At the regularized level, the right-hand side divided by the non-zero quantity
\left\langle V_{\alpha}(z)\bm{\mathrm{V}}\right\rangle
is given by

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\neq l}\frac{\left\langle\alpha_{k},\alpha_{l}\right\rangle}{2(z-z_{k})(z_{l}-z_{k})_{\varepsilon}}+\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\left\langle\alpha_{k},\alpha\right\rangle}{2(z-z_{k})(z-z_{k})_{\varepsilon}}+\frac{\Delta_{\alpha_{k}}}{(z-z_{k})^{2}}\quad+\rm I

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\neq l}\frac{\left\langle\alpha_{k},\alpha_{l}\right\rangle}{4(z_{l}-z_{k})}\left(\frac{1}{z-z_{k}}-\frac{1}{z-z_{l}}\right)+\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\left\langle\alpha_{k},Q+\alpha\right\rangle}{2(z-z_{k})^{2}}-\frac{\left\langle\alpha_{k},\alpha_{k}\right\rangle}{4(z-z_{k})^{2}}\quad+o_{\varepsilon}(1)\quad+\rm I

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k\neq l}\frac{\left\langle\alpha_{k},\alpha_{l}\right\rangle}{4(z-z_{k})(z-z_{l})}+\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\left\langle\alpha_{k},Q+\alpha\right\rangle}{2(z-z_{k})^{2}}-\frac{\left\langle\alpha_{k},\alpha_{k}\right\rangle}{4(z-z_{k})^{2}}\quad+o_{\varepsilon}(1)\quad+\rm I

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k,l=1}^{N}\frac{\left\langle\alpha_{k},\alpha_{l}\right\rangle}{4(z-z_{k})(z-z_{l})}+\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\left\langle\alpha_{k},Q+\alpha\right\rangle}{2(z-z_{k})^{2}}\quad+o_{\varepsilon}(1)\quad+\rm I,

where
\left\langle V_{\alpha}(z)\bm{\mathrm{V}}\right\rangle\rm I
is equal to:

\frac{\mu\gamma}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\left\langle\alpha_{k},e_{i}\right\rangle}{2(z-z_{k})(z_{k}-x)_{\varepsilon}}\left\langle V_{\gamma e_{i},\varepsilon}(x)V_{\alpha,\varepsilon}(z)\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}d^{2}x.

Using the same reasoning as in Section
4
involving symmetrisation and Stokes’ formula, the latter is equal (up to terms that vanish in the
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
limit) to

\displaystyle\frac{\mu\gamma}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\left\langle\alpha_{k},e_{i}\right\rangle}{2(z-z_{k})(z-x)_{\varepsilon}}-\frac{\left\langle\alpha,e_{i}\right\rangle+\frac{2}{\gamma}}{(z-x)^{2}_{\varepsilon}}\right)\left\langle V_{\gamma e_{i},\varepsilon}(x)V_{\alpha,\varepsilon}(z)\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}d^{2}x

\displaystyle-\frac{(\mu\gamma)^{2}}{4}\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{2}}\frac{\left\langle e_{i},e_{j}\right\rangle}{(z-x_{1})_{\varepsilon}(z-x_{2})_{\varepsilon}}\left\langle V_{\gamma e_{i},\varepsilon}(x_{1})V_{\gamma e_{j},\varepsilon}(x_{2})V_{\alpha,\varepsilon}(z)\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}d^{2}x_{1}d^{2}x_{2}.

This expression coincides with the left-hand side in the
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
limit by applying Gaussian integration by parts in the same spirit as in Section
4
, concluding the proof:

\displaystyle\left\langle\bm{\mathrm{L}_{-2,\varepsilon}}(z,\alpha)V_{\alpha,\varepsilon}(z)\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\left\langle Q+\alpha,\alpha_{k}\right\rangle}{2(z-z_{k})^{2}_{\varepsilon}}\left\langle V_{\alpha,\varepsilon}(z)\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle-\frac{\mu\gamma}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\left\langle Q+\alpha,e_{i}\right\rangle}{(z-x)^{2}_{\varepsilon}}\left\langle V_{\gamma e_{i},\varepsilon}(x)V_{\alpha,\varepsilon}(z)\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle d^{2}x

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\sum_{k,l=1}^{N}\frac{\left\langle\alpha_{k},\alpha_{l}\right\rangle}{4(z-z_{k})_{\varepsilon}(z-z_{l})_{\varepsilon}}\left\langle V_{\alpha,\varepsilon}(z)\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle

\displaystyle+\frac{\mu\gamma}{4}

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\left\langle\alpha_{k},e_{i}\right\rangle}{(z-z_{k})_{\varepsilon}(z-x)_{\varepsilon}}+\frac{\gamma\left\langle e_{i},e_{i}\right\rangle}{(z-x)_{\varepsilon}(z-x)_{\varepsilon}}\right)\left\langle V_{\gamma e_{i},\varepsilon}(x)V_{\alpha,\varepsilon}(z)\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle d^{2}x

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{(\mu\gamma)^{2}}{4}\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{2}}\frac{\left\langle e_{i},e_{j}\right\rangle}{(z-x_{1})_{\varepsilon}(z-x_{2})_{\varepsilon}}\left\langle V_{\gamma e_{i},\varepsilon}(x_{1})V_{\gamma e_{j},\varepsilon}(x_{2})V_{\alpha,\varepsilon}(z)\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle d^{2}x_{1}d^{2}x_{2}.

∎

Thanks to the expression of the Virasoro descendents at the level two, we can readily search for degenerate fields at the level two. As before, we think of them as Vertex Operators for which the
W
-descendent at the level two,
\bm{\mathrm{W}_{-2}}(z,\alpha)
, can be expressed as a linear combination of Virasoro descendents at the level two. Put differently, these are
\alpha
such that there exist
\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2}
real numbers with

\kappa_{1}\bm{\mathrm{L}_{-(1,1)}}+\kappa_{2}\bm{\mathrm{L}_{-2}}=\bm{\mathrm{W}_{-2}}.

The latter implies that
\alpha=\alpha_{1}\omega_{1}+\alpha_{2}\omega_{2}
is a solution of the following set of equations

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\kappa_{1}\alpha_{1}^{2}-2\kappa_{2}&=2\frac{2\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{1}}{3}\\
\kappa_{1}\alpha_{2}^{2}-2\kappa_{2}&=-2\frac{2\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}{3}\\
\kappa_{1}\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}+\kappa_{2}&=2\frac{\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}}{3}\\
\kappa_{1}\alpha_{1}+\kappa_{2}(q+\alpha_{1})&=-(q+\alpha_{1})\frac{2\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{1}}{3}\\
\kappa_{1}\alpha_{2}+\kappa_{2}(q+\alpha_{2})&=(q-\alpha_{2})\frac{2\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}{3}.\end{array}\right.

Explicit computations show the following.

Proposition 5.3
.

Degenerate fields at the level two are given by the Vertex Operators
V_{\alpha}
with
\alpha
of the form
-\chi\omega_{i}
where
\chi
is either
\frac{2}{\gamma}
or
\gamma
and
i\in\{1,2\}
. In that case

(5.5)

\begin{split}\bm{\mathrm{W}_{-2}}(\cdot,\alpha)&=-\frac{4}{\chi}\bm{\mathrm{L}_{-(1,1)}}(\cdot,\alpha)-\frac{4\chi}{3}\bm{\mathrm{L}_{-2}}(\cdot,\alpha),\text{ hence}\\
\bm{\mathcal{W}_{-2}^{(1)}}\left\langle V_{\alpha}(z_{1})\prod_{k=2}^{N}V_{\alpha_{k}}(z_{k})\right\rangle&=-\left(\frac{4}{\chi}\left(\bm{\mathcal{L}_{-1}^{(1)}}\right)^{2}+\frac{4\chi}{3}\bm{\mathcal{L}_{-2}^{(1)}}\right)\left\langle V_{\alpha}(z_{1})\prod_{k=2}^{N}V_{\alpha_{k}}(z_{k})\right\rangle.\end{split}

A similar reasoning remains true when we turn to descendents at the level three; nevertheless calculations are slightly more involved. Like before, we shall first introduce the three Virasoro descendents at the third level by setting

(5.6)

\begin{split}\bm{\mathrm{L}_{-(1,1,1)}}(z,\alpha)\coloneqq&\left\langle\alpha,\partial^{3}\varphi(z)\right\rangle+3\left\langle\alpha,\partial\varphi(z)\right\rangle\left\langle\alpha,\partial^{2}\varphi(z)\right\rangle+\left\langle\alpha,\partial\varphi(z)\right\rangle^{3}\\
\bm{\mathrm{L}_{-(1,2)}}(z,\alpha)\coloneqq&\left\langle Q+\alpha,\partial^{3}\varphi(z)\right\rangle+\left\langle Q+\alpha,\partial^{2}\varphi(z)\right\rangle\left\langle\alpha,\partial\varphi(z)\right\rangle\\
&-2\left\langle\partial^{2}\varphi(z),\partial\varphi(z)\right\rangle-\left\langle\partial\varphi(z),\partial\varphi(z)\right\rangle\left\langle\alpha,\partial\varphi(z)\right\rangle\cdot\end{split}

These are such that (provided that the objects exist)

(5.7)

\begin{split}\left\langle\bm{\mathrm{L}_{-(1,1,1)}}(z_{1},\alpha_{1})\bm{\mathrm{V}}\right\rangle&=\left(\bm{\mathcal{L}_{-1}^{(1)}}\right)^{3}\left\langle\bm{\mathrm{V}}\right\rangle\\
\left\langle\bm{\mathrm{L}_{-(1,2)}}(z_{1},\alpha_{1})\bm{\mathrm{V}}\right\rangle&=\bm{\mathcal{L}_{-1}^{(1)}}\bm{\mathcal{L}_{-2}^{(1)}}\left\langle\bm{\mathrm{V}}\right\rangle\cdot\end{split}

We also introduce

(5.8)

\bm{\mathrm{L}_{-3}}(z,\alpha)\coloneqq\left\langle Q+\frac{\alpha}{2},\partial^{3}\varphi(z)\right\rangle-2\left\langle\partial^{2}\varphi(z),\partial\varphi(z)\right\rangle

which is has been defined in order to satisfy the below equation:

(5.9)

\left\langle\bm{\mathrm{L}_{-3}}(z_{1},\alpha_{1})\bm{\mathrm{V}}\right\rangle=\sum_{k=2}^{N}\left(\frac{\partial_{z_{k}}}{(z_{1}-z_{k})^{2}}+\frac{2\Delta_{\alpha_{k}}}{(z_{1}-z_{k})^{3}}\right)\left\langle\bm{\mathrm{V}}\right\rangle.

To see that we may proceed like in the proof of Lemma
5.2
and the very same arguments still apply. Nonetheless to motivate this claim simply note that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(\frac{\partial_{z_{k}}}{(z-z_{k})^{2}}+\frac{2\Delta_{\alpha_{k}}}{(z-z_{k})^{3}}\right)\left\langle V_{\alpha}(z)\bm{\mathrm{V}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\neq l}\frac{\left\langle\alpha_{k},\alpha_{l}\right\rangle}{2(z-z_{k})^{2}(z_{l}-z_{k})}+\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\left\langle\alpha_{k},\alpha\right\rangle}{2(z-z_{k})^{3}}+\frac{2\Delta_{\alpha_{k}}}{(z-z_{k})^{3}}\quad+\quad\text{integral terms}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\neq l}\frac{\left\langle\alpha_{k},\alpha_{l}\right\rangle}{4(z_{l}-z_{k})}\left(\frac{1}{(z-z_{k})^{2}}-\frac{1}{(z-z_{l})^{2}}\right)+\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\left\langle\alpha_{k},Q+\frac{\alpha}{2}\right\rangle}{(z-z_{k})^{3}}-\frac{\left\langle\alpha_{k},\alpha_{k}\right\rangle}{2(z-z_{k})^{3}}\quad+\quad\text{integral terms}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k\neq l}\frac{\left\langle\alpha_{k},\alpha_{l}\right\rangle}{2(z-z_{k})^{2}(z-z_{l})}+\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\left\langle\alpha_{k},Q+\frac{\alpha}{2}\right\rangle}{(z-z_{k})^{3}}-\frac{\left\langle\alpha_{k},\alpha_{k}\right\rangle}{2(z-z_{k})^{3}}\quad+\quad\text{integral terms}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k,l=1}^{N}\frac{\left\langle\alpha_{k},\alpha_{l}\right\rangle}{2(z-z_{k})^{2}(z-z_{l})}+\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\left\langle\alpha_{k},Q+\frac{\alpha}{2}\right\rangle}{(z-z_{k})^{3}}\quad+\quad\text{integral terms}

\displaystyle=\Big\langle-2\left\langle\partial^{2}\varphi(z),\partial\varphi(z)\right\rangle V_{\alpha}(z)\bm{\mathrm{V}}\Big\rangle+\Big\langle\langle Q+\frac{\alpha}{2},\partial^{3}\varphi(z)\rangle V_{\alpha}(z)\bm{\mathrm{V}}\Big\rangle.

We now turn to the
W
-descendents at the third level. With a reasoning similar to the one of Lemma
5.2
we end up with:

Lemma 5.4
.

Let us set

(5.10)

\begin{split}\bm{\mathrm{W}_{-3}}(z,\alpha)&\coloneqq q^{2}\left\langle h_{2},\partial^{3}\varphi(z)\right\rangle+\frac{q}{2}\left(2B(\partial^{3}\varphi(z),\alpha)-B(\alpha,\partial^{3}\varphi(z))\right)-C(\partial^{3}\varphi(z),\alpha,\alpha)\\
&-2qB(\partial^{2}\varphi(z),\partial\varphi(z))+4C(\partial^{2}\varphi(z),\partial\varphi(z),\alpha)+4C(\partial\varphi(z),\partial^{2}\varphi(z),\alpha)\\
&-8\left\langle h_{1},\partial\varphi(z)\right\rangle\left\langle h_{2},\partial\varphi(z)\right\rangle\left\langle h_{3},\partial\varphi(z)\right\rangle.\end{split}

Then

(5.11)

\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\left\langle\bm{\mathrm{W}_{-3,\varepsilon}}(z_{1},\alpha_{1})\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle=\sum_{k=2}^{N}\left(\frac{\bm{\mathcal{W}_{-2}^{(k)}}}{z_{1}-z_{k}}+\frac{\bm{\mathcal{W}_{-1}^{(k)}}}{(z_{1}-z_{k})^{2}}+\frac{w(\alpha_{k})}{(z_{1}-z_{k})^{3}}\right)\left\langle\bm{\mathrm{V}}\right\rangle.

Proof.

Computations parallel the ones made in Section
4
. The right-hand side in (
5.11
), when divided by
\left\langle V_{\alpha}(z)\bm{\mathrm{V}}\right\rangle
, can be expanded as

\displaystyle\sum_{k,l,p\text{ distinct}}\frac{C(\alpha_{k},\alpha_{l},\alpha_{p})}{(z-z_{k})(z_{k}-z_{l})_{\varepsilon}(z_{k}-z_{p})_{\varepsilon}}+\sum_{k\neq l}\frac{C^{\sigma}(\alpha,\alpha_{k},\alpha_{l})}{(z-z_{k})(z_{k}-z)_{\varepsilon}(z_{k}-z_{l})_{\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{k\neq l}\frac{q\left(B(\alpha_{l},\alpha_{k})-B(\alpha_{k},\alpha_{l})\right)+2C(\alpha_{k},\alpha_{l}-\alpha_{k},\alpha_{l})}{2(z-z_{k})(z_{k}-z_{l})_{\varepsilon}^{2}}+\frac{qB(\alpha_{k},\alpha_{l})+2C(\alpha_{k},\alpha_{k},\alpha_{l})}{2(z-z_{k})^{2}(z_{k}-z_{l})_{\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{q\left(B(\alpha,\alpha_{k})-B(\alpha_{k},\alpha)\right)+2C(\alpha_{k},\alpha-\alpha_{k},\alpha)}{2(z-z_{k})(z-z_{k})_{\varepsilon}^{2}}+\frac{qB(\alpha_{k},\alpha)+2C(\alpha_{k},\alpha_{k},\alpha)}{2(z-z_{k})^{2}(z_{k}-z)_{\varepsilon}}+\frac{w(\alpha_{k})}{(z-z_{k})^{3}}

\displaystyle+\text{integral terms}.

The first quantity can be easily dealt with by recursive application of symmetrisation identities:

\displaystyle\sum_{k,l,p\text{ distinct}}\frac{C(\alpha_{k},\alpha_{l},\alpha_{p})}{(z-z_{k})(z_{k}-z_{l})(z_{k}-z_{p})}=\frac{1}{3}\sum_{k,l,p\text{ distinct}}\frac{C(\alpha_{k},\alpha_{l},\alpha_{p})}{(z-z_{k})(z-z_{l})(z-z_{p})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,l,p}\frac{\left\langle\alpha_{k},h_{1}\right\rangle\left\langle\alpha_{l},h_{2}\right\rangle\left\langle\alpha_{p},h_{3}\right\rangle}{(z-z_{k})(z-z_{l})(z-z_{p})}-\sum_{k,l}\frac{C(\alpha_{k},\alpha_{k},\alpha_{l})}{(z-z_{k})^{2}(z-z_{l})}-\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\left\langle\alpha_{k},h_{1}\right\rangle\left\langle\alpha_{k},h_{2}\right\rangle\left\langle\alpha_{k},h_{3}\right\rangle}{(z-z_{k})^{3}}.

Similarly we can write that

\displaystyle\sum_{k\neq l}\frac{qB(\alpha_{k},\alpha_{l})+2C(\alpha_{k},\alpha_{k},\alpha_{l})}{2(z-z_{k})^{2}(z_{k}-z_{l})}+\frac{q\left(B(\alpha_{l},\alpha_{k})-B(\alpha_{k},\alpha_{l})\right)+2C(\alpha_{k},\alpha_{l}-\alpha_{k},\alpha_{l})}{2(z-z_{k})(z_{k}-z_{l})^{2}}

5.3.
Implications on a four-point correlation function

The expression of the BPZ-type equation (

5.4.
Explicit expression for the descendent fields: some perspectives

One of the contributions of the present document is to provide an explicit expression for the descendent fields. In the above subsections we have shed light on some applications of this property to the study of degenerate fields at the first, second and third level, and its implications on a BPZ-type differential equation. Let us now stress some future directions of work allowed by this new framework.

5.4.1.
Liouville theory

Using the same reasoning as above it is possible to write down an explicit expression of the descendent fields in Liouville theory. Namely if
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{r})
is a finite, non-increasing sequence of positive integers (a
Young diagram
) we can write

(5.23)

\bm{\mathrm{L}_{-\lambda_{1}}}\cdots\bm{\mathrm{L}_{-\lambda_{r}}}V_{\alpha}(z)=\bm{\mathrm{L}_{-\lambda}}(z,\alpha)V_{\alpha}(z),

where
\bm{\mathrm{L}_{-\lambda}}(z,\alpha)
admits an expansion of the form

(5.24)

\bm{\mathrm{L}_{-\lambda}}(z,\alpha)=\sum_{\left\lvert\mu\right\rvert=\left\lvert\lambda\right\rvert}a_{\mu|\lambda}(\alpha)\partial^{\mu}\varphi.

Here the sum ranges over Young diagrams with
\left\lvert\mu\right\rvert\coloneqq\sum_{i\;\geqslant\;1}\mu_{i}=\left\lvert\lambda\right\rvert
and we have denoted

\partial^{\mu}\varphi\coloneqq\partial^{\mu_{1}}\varphi\times\cdots\times\partial^{\mu_{r}}\varphi.

Coefficients
a_{\mu|\lambda}(\alpha)
can be determined via a simple recursive procedure. Determining these coefficients could provide an algorithmic way of deriving BPZ-type equations for general singular vectors
V_{\left\langle r,s\right\rangle}\coloneqq V_{-(r-1)\frac{\gamma}{2}-(s-1)\frac{2}{\gamma}}
. We stress that this method, new to the best of our knowledge, can be implemented within the probabilistic framework of Liouville theory, in contrast with the usual one based on irreducible representations of Verma modules
[
19
]
.

5.4.2.
Toda theories

The reasoning presented in the context of Liouville theory extends to the study of Toda theories. Namely it is possible to write down
W
-descendents in a form similar to that of Equations (
5.23
) and (
5.24
). This could be the starting point to the derivation of BPZ-type equations for Toda theories. Again one of the key advantages of the formulation presented is that it could be implemented within our probabilistic framework and provide a general procedure for rigorously proving that BPZ-type equations hold true for Toda theories. In particular this representation should allow us to recover some of the results from
[
7
]
,
[
2
]
and
[
36
]
.

6.1.
Proof of Lemma
3.1

The first one corresponds to the fact that the definition we have provided for regularizing the map
x\mapsto\frac{1}{x^{p}}
gives the expected result in the
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
limit.

Proof of Lemma
3.1
.

First assume that we are given
0<\varepsilon<\frac{\left\lvert x\right\rvert}{4R}
. If
\left\lvert x+\varepsilon(z_{1}-z_{2})\right\rvert\;\leqslant\;\frac{\left\lvert x\right\rvert}{2}
, then necessarily either
\left\lvert z_{1}\right\rvert\;\geqslant\;R
or
\left\lvert z_{2}\right\rvert\;\geqslant\;R
, and this implies that
\eta(z_{1})\eta(z_{2})
vanishes since
\eta
is compactly supported in the domain
B(0,R)
. Therefore we can apply integration by parts and the change of variables
z_{i}\leftrightarrow\varepsilon z_{i}
to get

\frac{1}{(x)_{\varepsilon}^{p}}=\int_{\left\lvert x+\varepsilon(z_{1}-z_{2})\right\rvert>\frac{\left\lvert x\right\rvert}{2}}\frac{1}{(x+\varepsilon(z_{1}-z_{2}))^{p}}\eta(z_{1})\eta(z_{2})d^{2}z_{1}d^{2}z_{2}.

As a consequence

\frac{x^{p}}{(x)_{\varepsilon}^{p}}-1=\int_{\left\lvert x+\varepsilon(z_{1}-z_{2})\right\rvert>\frac{\left\lvert x\right\rvert}{2}}\frac{x^{p}-(x+\varepsilon(z_{1}-z_{2}))^{p}}{(x+\varepsilon(z_{1}-z_{2}))^{p}}\eta(z_{1})\eta(z_{2})d^{2}z_{1}d^{2}z_{2}.

We also know that, since we have assumed that
0<\varepsilon<\frac{\left\lvert x\right\rvert}{4R}
, we have the bound
\left\lvert\frac{\varepsilon(z_{1}-z_{2})}{x}\right\rvert<1
on the domain where
\eta(z_{1})\eta(z_{2})
does not vanish. As a consequence we can expand the integrand as a power series in the variable
\frac{\varepsilon(z_{1}-z_{2})}{x}
. Since
\eta
is compactly supported, the integral of this power series is absolutely convergent. Therefore, we conclude by noticing that the first two terms in the expansion vanish in the limit (the first one is identically zero; for the second order term we use the
z_{1}\leftrightarrow z_{2}
symmetry), so that we can factorize by
\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{x}\right)^{2}
.

Now if we assume that
\left\lvert x\right\rvert\;\leqslant\;4R\varepsilon
then

\frac{x^{p}}{(x)_{\varepsilon}^{p}}=\int_{\left\lvert x+\varepsilon(z_{1}-z_{2})\right\rvert>\frac{\left\lvert x\right\rvert}{2}}\frac{(\frac{x}{\varepsilon})^{p}}{(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}+(z_{1}-z_{2}))^{p}}\eta(z_{1})\eta(z_{2})d^{2}z_{1}d^{2}z_{2}.

Since
\left\lvert x\right\rvert\;\leqslant\;4R\varepsilon
, the integrand can be bounded by the quantity
3^{-p}
, uniformly on
\varepsilon>0
. This allows to conclude our proof of Lemma
3.1
.
∎

\gamma e_{1}
or
\gamma e_{2}
are inserted:

\left\langle\prod_{i=1}^{2}V_{\gamma e_{i},\varepsilon}(\bm{x}_{i})\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}\coloneqq\left\langle\prod_{k=1}^{r_{1}}V_{\gamma e_{1},\varepsilon}\left(x_{1}^{(k)}\right)\prod_{j=1}^{r_{2}}V_{\gamma e_{2},\varepsilon}\left(x_{2}^{(j)}\right)\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}.

As before, this correlation is the product of a constant, a GFF prefactor and an expectation
H_{\varepsilon,\delta}(\bm{z},\bm{\alpha})
. The exact expression for
H_{\varepsilon,\delta}(\bm{z},\bm{\alpha})
is lengthy to write down: one should take Equation (
6.1
) and appropriately modify indices.

6.2.1.
Bounds on the Toda correlation functions: proof of Lemma
3.2

Let us start by observing that with the
\varepsilon
-regularization, no singularities come from the prefactor. Moreover, viewed as a function of
x_{i}^{(l)}
, the quantity
\left\lvert x_{i}^{(l)}-z_{k}\right\rvert_{\varepsilon}^{-\gamma\left\langle e_{i},\alpha_{k}\right\rangle}
can be bounded by some constant times
\left\lvert 1+x_{i}^{(l)}\right\rvert_{\varepsilon}^{-\gamma\left\langle e_{i},\alpha_{k}\right\rangle}
. As a consequence this prefactor together with the constant part can be bounded by

(6.2)

C_{\varepsilon}\prod_{l=1}^{r_{1}}\left\lvert 1+|x_{1}^{(l)}|\right\rvert_{\varepsilon}^{-\gamma\left\langle\sum\limits_{k}\alpha_{k},e_{1}\right\rangle}\prod_{m=1}^{r_{2}}\left\lvert 1+|x_{2}^{(m)}|\right\rvert_{\varepsilon}^{-\gamma\left\langle\sum\limits_{k}\alpha_{k},e_{2}\right\rangle}\prod_{(i,l)\neq(j,m)}\left\lvert x_{i}^{(l)}-x_{j}^{(m)}\right\rvert_{\varepsilon}^{-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}A_{ij}}.

For item (1), we need to study the expectation part
H^{(\bm{x}_{1},\bm{x}_{2})}_{\varepsilon,\delta}
for large
\bm{x}_{1},\bm{x}_{2}
. Inside the integrals in the expression
H^{(\bm{x}_{1},\bm{x}_{2})}_{\varepsilon,\delta}
, we identify singularities of the form with
i=1,2
:

\frac{1}{\prod_{k=1}^{N}\left\lvert z_{k}-y_{i}\right\rvert_{\varepsilon}^{\gamma\left\langle\alpha_{k},e_{i}\right\rangle}}\frac{1}{\prod_{l=1}^{r_{1}}\left\lvert x_{1}^{(l)}-y_{i}\right\rvert_{\varepsilon}^{\gamma\left\langle e_{1},e_{i}\right\rangle}}\frac{1}{\prod_{m=1}^{r_{2}}\left\lvert x_{2}^{(m)}-y_{i}\right\rvert_{\varepsilon}^{\gamma\left\langle e_{2},e_{i}\right\rangle}}\cdot

We can factorize out these deterministic factors on
\bm{x}_{1},\bm{x}_{2}
“at infinity” from the expectation. Then what is left in the expectation is approximately the part corresponding to
H_{\varepsilon,\delta}
. More precisely, write

\frac{1}{\prod_{l=1}^{r_{1}}\left\lvert x_{1}^{(l)}-y_{i}\right\rvert_{\varepsilon}^{\gamma\left\langle e_{1},e_{i}\right\rangle}}=\frac{\prod_{l=1}^{r_{1}}\left\lvert 1+|x_{1}^{(l)}|\right\rvert_{\varepsilon}^{\gamma\left\langle e_{1},e_{i}\right\rangle}}{\prod_{l=1}^{r_{1}}\left\lvert x_{1}^{(l)}-y_{i}\right\rvert_{\varepsilon}^{\gamma\left\langle e_{1},e_{i}\right\rangle}}\cdot\prod_{l=1}^{r_{1}}\left\lvert 1+|x_{1}^{(l)}|\right\rvert_{\varepsilon}^{-\gamma\left\langle e_{1},e_{i}\right\rangle}

and similarly for
\bm{x}_{2}
. Remark that the first factor above goes to
1
as
x^{(l)}\to\infty
. It follows that, for some positive constant
C
,

\begin{split}H^{(\bm{x}_{1},\bm{x}_{2})}_{\varepsilon,\delta}&\;\leqslant\;C\prod_{i=1}^{2}\left(\prod_{l=1}^{r_{1}}\left\lvert 1+|x_{1}^{(l)}|\right\rvert_{\varepsilon}^{-\gamma\left\langle e_{1},e_{i}\right\rangle}\prod_{m=1}^{r_{2}}\left\lvert 1+|x_{2}^{(m)}|\right\rvert_{\varepsilon}^{-\gamma\left\langle e_{2},e_{i}\right\rangle}\right)^{-s_{i}}\\
&\;\leqslant\;C\prod_{l=1}^{r_{1}}\left\lvert 1+|x_{1}^{(l)}|\right\rvert_{\varepsilon}^{\gamma\left\langle\sum\limits_{k}\alpha_{k}-2Q+\gamma r_{1}e_{1}+\gamma r_{2}e_{2},e_{1}\right\rangle}\prod_{m=1}^{r_{2}}\left\lvert 1+|x_{2}^{(m)}|\right\rvert_{\varepsilon}^{\gamma\left\langle\sum\limits_{k}\alpha_{k}-2Q+\gamma r_{1}e_{1}+\gamma r_{2}e_{2},e_{2}\right\rangle}.\end{split}

Combining with the bound (
6.2
) for the prefactor yields:

\begin{split}&\left\langle\prod_{i=1}^{r_{1}}V_{\gamma e_{1},\varepsilon}\left(x_{1}^{(i)}\right)\prod_{j=1}^{r_{2}}V_{\gamma e_{2},\varepsilon}\left(x_{2}^{(j)}\right)\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}\\
&\;\leqslant\;C_{\varepsilon}\prod_{l=1}^{r_{1}}\left\lvert 1+|x_{1}^{(l)}|\right\rvert_{\varepsilon}^{\gamma\left\langle-2Q+\gamma r_{1}e_{1}+\gamma r_{2}e_{2},e_{1}\right\rangle}\prod_{m=1}^{r_{2}}\left\lvert 1+|x_{2}^{(m)}|\right\rvert_{\varepsilon}^{\gamma\left\langle-2Q+\gamma r_{1}e_{1}+\gamma r_{2}e_{2},e_{2}\right\rangle}\prod_{(i,l)\neq(j,m)}\left\lvert x_{i}^{(l)}-x_{j}^{(m)}\right\rvert_{\varepsilon}^{-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}A_{ij}}\\
&=C_{\varepsilon}\prod_{l=1}^{r_{1}}\left\lvert 1+|x_{1}^{(l)}|\right\rvert_{\varepsilon}^{-4}\prod_{m=1}^{r_{2}}\left\lvert 1+|x_{2}^{(m)}|\right\rvert_{\varepsilon}^{-4}\prod_{(i,l)\neq(j,m)}\left(\frac{\left\lvert 1+x_{i}^{(l)}\right\rvert\left\lvert 1+x_{j}^{(m)}\right\rvert}{\left\lvert x_{i}^{(l)}-x_{j}^{(m)}\right\rvert_{\varepsilon}}\right)^{\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}A_{ij}}.\end{split}

The last term on the right-hand side being bounded this finishes the proof of item (1).

For item (2), the same reasoning remains valid and therefore the same bounds are still true when looking at the behaviour near infinity of the correlation functions when we take the
\varepsilon,\delta\to 0
limit of the correlation functions.

For item (3), we investigate the behaviour when
\varepsilon
goes to zero of the fusion of two insertions, that is when
\left\lvert z_{1}-z_{2}\right\rvert\rightarrow 0
but with all other insertions staying at fixed positive distance at least
\rho
. Suppose that for both
k=1,2
,
\left\langle\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}-Q,e_{k}\right\rangle<0
. In this case,
H_{\varepsilon,\delta}
remains bounded when
z_{1}
and
z_{2}
merge (since in that case the expectation remains well-defined according to
[
8
, Lemma 4.1]
) and therefore the behaviour of the correlation functions is governed by the prefactor, of which the singularity is of order
\left\lvert z_{1}-z_{2}\right\rvert^{-\left\langle\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\right\rangle}
.

The analysis is a bit more subtle when
\left\langle\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2},e_{2}\right\rangle\;\geqslant\;\gamma+\frac{2}{\gamma}
but
\left\langle\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2},e_{1}\right\rangle<\gamma+\frac{2}{\gamma}
. However we can adapt the result in the Liouville case by a simple argument. Indeed, by Hölder’s inequality we have that for positive
p_{1},p_{2}
with
\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}=1
,

H_{\varepsilon,\delta}\;\leqslant\;\prod_{i=1}^{2}\mathds{E}\left[\left(\int_{\mathbb{C}}\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-y_{i})\frac{\hat{g}_{\varepsilon}(y_{i})^{-\frac{\gamma}{4}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left\langle\alpha_{k},e_{i}\right\rangle}}{\prod_{k=1}^{N}\left\lvert z_{k}-y_{i}\right\rvert_{\varepsilon}^{\gamma\left\langle\alpha_{k},e_{i}\right\rangle}}M^{\gamma e_{i},\hat{g}}_{\varepsilon}(d^{2}y_{i})\right)^{-p_{i}s_{i}}\right]^{\frac{1}{p_{i}}}.

We can further ignore the
\theta_{\delta}
term since
z_{0}
is away from the singularities in the integrand. Since
\left\langle\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2},e_{1}\right\rangle<\gamma+\frac{2}{\gamma}
and
s_{1}>0
, the expectation corresponding to the index
i=1
remains bounded for any
p_{1}>1
(see again
[
8
, Lemma 4.1]
). The behaviour of the expectation with index
i=2
when
z_{1}
and
z_{2}
merge is reduced to the Liouville case (the so-called freezing estimate, see
[
27
, Lemma 6.5]
): for any
\eta>0
,

\mathds{E}\left[\left(\int_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\hat{g}_{\varepsilon}(y_{2})^{-\frac{\gamma}{4}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left\langle\alpha_{k},e_{2}\right\rangle}}{\prod_{k=1}^{N}\left\lvert z_{k}-y_{2}\right\rvert_{\varepsilon}^{\gamma\left\langle\alpha_{k},e_{2}\right\rangle}}M^{\gamma e_{2},\hat{g}}_{\varepsilon}(d^{2}y_{2})\right)^{-p_{2}s_{2}}\right]\;\leqslant\;C_{\varepsilon}\left\lvert z_{1}-z_{2}\right\rvert^{\frac{\left\langle\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}-Q,e_{2}\right\rangle^{2}}{2}-\eta}.

Choosing
p_{2}
close enough to
1
, we see that the two-point fusion estimate is governed by

(6.3)

\left\lvert z_{1}-z_{2}\right\rvert^{-\left\langle\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\right\rangle}\left\lvert z_{1}-z_{2}\right\rvert^{-\eta^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}-Q,e_{2}\right\rangle^{2}}.

Item (4) is a direct consequence of the previous bound.

For item (5): the bound of item (3) ensures local integrability near
z_{1}
of
\left\langle V_{\gamma e_{1}}(x)\bm{\mathrm{V}}\right\rangle
in
L^{p}(\mathbb{C})
for
1\;\leqslant\;p<\frac{4}{4-\gamma^{2}}
if we have
\frac{2}{\gamma}-\gamma\;\leqslant\;\left\langle\alpha_{0},e_{1}\right\rangle<\frac{2}{\gamma}+\gamma
, for
1\;\leqslant\;p<\frac{2}{\gamma\left\langle\alpha_{0},e_{1}\right\rangle}
if we assume that
0<\left\langle\alpha_{0},e_{1}\right\rangle<\frac{2}{\gamma}-\gamma
, and in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{C})
if
\left\langle\alpha_{0},e_{1}\right\rangle\;\leqslant\;0
.
∎

6.2.2.
A generalized fusion estimate

We now provide a generalized fusion estimate for which we consider the situation where finitely many points merge within a correlation functions. In our applications, we only need the special case where the weights of the merging Vertex Operators are of the form
\gamma e_{i}
, for
i=1,2
, and where points merge pairwise. This will be the setup that we investigate in this section.

We assume that
\bm{z}
are distinct and that
\bm{\alpha}
are such that the Seiberg bounds (
2.25
) hold. Without loss of generality we assume that
\rho\coloneqq\min\limits_{1\;\leqslant\;k\;\leqslant\;N}\left\lvert z_{k}\right\rvert
is positive. We are interested in estimating the following correlation functions with
2p
extra points:

\left\langle\prod_{l=1}^{p}V_{\gamma e_{\sigma(l)}}(x_{l})V_{\gamma e_{\tau(l)}}(y_{l})\bm{\mathrm{V}}\right\rangle

where
\sigma(l),\tau(l)\in\{1,2\}
so the extra weights are either
\gamma e_{1}
or
\gamma e_{2}
.

To get a reasonable estimate, we need to first reorder the points
(\bm{x},\bm{y})
in such a way that the collision can only happen pairwise, i.e. between each pair
x_{i}
and
y_{i}
. In short, we restrict the locations on the points: we assume that
x_{l}
,
y_{l}
belong to sets
A_{l}
,
B_{l}
in such a way that any two of these sets are disjoint, except for
A_{l}
and
B_{l}
with the same indices.

More precisely, we introduce domains
\bm{A}\coloneqq A_{1}\times\cdots\times A_{p}
and
\bm{B}\coloneqq B_{1}\times\cdots\times B_{p}
such that:

•

For any
1\;\leqslant\;l\;\leqslant\;p
,
A_{l}
and
B_{l}
are either annuli or balls, and are contained in
B(0,\frac{\rho}{2})
. This is to avoid collisions between
(\bm{x},\bm{y})
and the given
\bm{z}
;

•

There exists some positive distance
d>0
, for which the
(A_{l})_{1\;\leqslant\;l\;\leqslant\;p}
are at distance at least
d
one to the other (and similarly for the
(B_{l})_{1\;\leqslant\;l\;\leqslant\;p}
);

•

For any
1\;\leqslant\;l,m\;\leqslant\;p
, the distance between
A_{l}
and
B_{m}
is zero if and only if
l=m
.

Lemma 6.1
.

There exist two positive constants
C
and
\zeta
such that, for any
(\bm{x},\bm{y})\in\bm{A}\times\bm{B}
:

(6.4)

\left\langle\prod_{l=1}^{p}V_{\gamma e_{\sigma(l)},\varepsilon}(x_{l})V_{\gamma e_{\tau(l)},\varepsilon}(y_{l})\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}\;\leqslant\;C\prod_{l=1}^{p}\left\lvert x_{l}-y_{l}\right\rvert^{-2+\zeta}

uniformly in
\varepsilon
and
\delta
. In particular the integral

(6.5)

\int_{\bm{A}}\int_{\bm{B}}\frac{1}{\prod_{l=1}^{p}(x_{l}-y_{l})}\left\langle\prod_{i=1}^{2}V_{\gamma e_{i}}(\bm{x}_{i})V_{\gamma e_{i}}(\bm{y}_{i})\bm{\mathrm{V}}\right\rangle d^{2}\bm{x}d^{2}\bm{y}

is absolutely convergent.

Proof of Lemma
6.1
.

The proof of this claim parallels the one in the Liouville case
[
32
, Lemma 3.1]
. The study is slightly more involved in the present case because of the presence of an additional GFF in the construction of the correlation functions, but the basic idea remains the same.

We separate as before between the prefactor and the expectation term, see Equation (
6.1
). Since the domains are disjoint except for
A_{l}
and
B_{m}
with
l=m
, the prefactor part is bounded by

C\prod_{l=1}^{p}\left\lvert x_{l}-y_{l}\right\rvert^{-\gamma^{2}\left\langle e_{\sigma(l)},e_{\tau(l)}\right\rangle}

where
C
is some positive constant.

For the expectation term the analysis is slightly more subtle, but one can simplify the problem by noticing that when
\sigma(l)\neq\tau(l)
, there is no singularity in the integral (since the sign in the power is the opposite one). As a consequence, and without loss of generality, we may assume
\sigma(l)=\tau(l)=1
for
l\in E_{1}\coloneqq\{1,\dots,r\}
and
\sigma(l)=\tau(l)=2
for
l\in E_{2}\coloneqq\{r+1,\dots,p\}
. Therefore the integrand can be bounded by some constant times

\mathds{E}\left[\prod_{i=1,2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{F_{i}(w_{i})}{\prod_{l\in E_{i}}\left\lvert x_{l}-w_{i}\right\rvert_{\varepsilon}^{2\gamma^{2}}\left\lvert y_{l}-w_{i}\right\rvert_{\varepsilon}^{2\gamma^{2}}}M^{\gamma e_{i},\hat{g}}_{\varepsilon}(d^{2}w_{i})\right)^{-s_{i}}\right]

where
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
are smooth in a neighbourhood of the
x_{l},y_{l}
. We are now to distinguish between two cases:

•

If
\gamma<\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}
, then when
x_{l}
and
y_{l}
merge the singularity remains integrable since in that case
4\gamma^{2}<\gamma\left\langle Q,e_{i}\right\rangle
so the expectation still makes sense according to
[
8
, Lemma 4.1]
. Therefore if
\gamma<\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}
, the fusion estimate is governed by the prefactor, which scales as
|x_{l}-y_{l}|^{-2\gamma^{2}}
with
-2\gamma^{2}>-\frac{4}{3}>-2
.

•

If
\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}\;\leqslant\;\gamma<\sqrt{2}
, we claim that, for any positive
\zeta
, it is bounded by

\prod_{l=1}^{p}\left\lvert x_{l}-y_{l}\right\rvert^{\frac{(3\gamma-\frac{2}{\gamma})^{2}}{2}-\zeta}.

For
\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}\;\leqslant\;\gamma<\sqrt{2}
, one checks that
-2\gamma^{2}+\frac{(3\gamma-\frac{2}{\gamma})^{2}}{2}>-2
.

We now prove the last claim following
[
32
, Lemma 3.1]
. Using that the exponents
s_{i}
are positive, we see that we can bound the expectation by (some constant times)

\mathds{E}\left[\left(\sum_{l=1}^{r}\int_{B(x_{l},\frac{d}{4})}\frac{M^{\gamma e_{1},\hat{g}}_{\varepsilon}(d^{2}w_{1})}{\left\lvert x_{l}-w_{1}\right\rvert_{\varepsilon}^{2\gamma^{2}}\left\lvert y_{l}-w_{1}\right\rvert_{\varepsilon}^{2\gamma^{2}}}\right)^{-s_{1}}\left(\sum_{l=r+1}^{p}\int_{B(x_{l},\frac{d}{4})}\frac{M^{\gamma e_{2},\hat{g}}_{\varepsilon}(d^{2}w_{2})}{\left\lvert x_{l}-w_{2}\right\rvert_{\varepsilon}^{2\gamma^{2}}\left\lvert y_{l}-w_{2}\right\rvert_{\varepsilon}^{2\gamma^{2}}}\right)^{-s_{2}}\right].

We can suppose that the GFFs in different balls
B(x_{l},\frac{d}{4})
are independent: this is a classical manipulation of Kahane’s convexity inequality, since the covariance of different GFFs in different balls is uniformly bounded from below and above by a global constant (see the proof of
[
8
, Lemma 4.1]
). Furthermore, we use the elementary inequality, for positive
a
’s and
s>0
,

\left(\sum\limits_{l=1}^{r}a_{l}\right)^{-s}\;\leqslant\;\prod\limits_{l=1}^{r}a_{l}^{-s/r}

to reduce the problem to the following estimate: for
s>0
and any
\zeta>0
there exists a positive constant
C
such that

\mathds{E}\left[\left(\int_{B(x,\frac{d}{4})}\frac{M^{\gamma e_{1},\hat{g}}_{\varepsilon}(d^{2}w)}{\left\lvert x-w\right\rvert_{\varepsilon}^{2\gamma^{2}}\left\lvert y-w\right\rvert_{\varepsilon}^{2\gamma^{2}}}\right)^{-s}\right]\;\leqslant\;C\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{\frac{(3\gamma-\frac{2}{\gamma})^{2}}{2}-\zeta}.

Since for
\gamma^{2}\;\geqslant\;\frac{2}{3}
,
4\gamma\;\geqslant\;q=\gamma+\frac{2}{\gamma}
, the classical freezing estimate
[
27
, Lemma 6.5]
yields the bound
|x-y|^{\frac{(4\gamma-q)^{2}}{2}-\zeta}
for any
\zeta>0
, which is the above.

For the second item of Lemma
6.1
we use the first estimate to see that all one needs to prove is that for positive
\zeta
the quantity

\int_{\bm{A}}\int_{\bm{B}}\frac{1}{\prod_{l=1}^{p}\left\lvert x_{l}-y_{l}\right\rvert^{3-\zeta}}d^{2}\bm{x}d^{2}\bm{y}

is finite. Since the domains are disjoint we see that it is enough to check that the two-fold integral

\int_{B(0,\frac{\rho}{6})}\int_{A(0,\frac{\rho}{6},\frac{\rho}{3})}\frac{1}{\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{3-\zeta}}d^{2}xd^{2}y

converges. This can be easily seen by using polar coordinates.
∎

6.3.

)

Recall the expression for the first derivative of the regularized correlation functions (we keep the same notations and performed an integration by parts with Stokes’ formula):

&-\mu^{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}\int_{A}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\left\langle\alpha_{k},\gamma e_{i}\right\rangle\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x_{1})}{2(z_{k}-x_{1})_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\left\langle\alpha_{0},\gamma e_{j}\right\rangle\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x_{2})}{2(z_{0}-x_{2})_{\varepsilon}}\left\langle V_{\gamma e_{i},\varepsilon}(x_{1})V_{\gamma e_{j},\varepsilon}(x_{2})V_{\alpha_{0},\varepsilon}(z_{0})\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}d^{2}x_{1}d^{2}x_{2}\\

For the terms involving derivatives of

\displaystyle\hskip-56.9055pt\mu\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{A}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\left\langle\alpha_{k},\gamma e_{i}\right\rangle}{2(z_{k}-x)_{\varepsilon}}\left(\sum_{l=1}^{N}\frac{\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x)\left\langle\alpha_{0},\alpha_{l}\right\rangle}{2(z_{0}-z_{k})_{\varepsilon}}+\frac{\left\langle\alpha_{l},\gamma e_{i}\right\rangle\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x)}{2(x-z_{l})_{\varepsilon}}+\frac{\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x)}{2(x-z_{k})_{\varepsilon}}\right)\left\langle V_{\gamma e_{i},\varepsilon}(x)V_{\alpha_{0},\varepsilon}(z_{0})\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}

\displaystyle d^{2}x

\displaystyle-\mu^{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}\int_{A}\int_{B(z_{0},r)^{c}}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\left\langle\alpha_{k},\gamma e_{i}\right\rangle\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x_{1})}{2(z_{k}-x_{1})_{\varepsilon}}\times\hskip 256.0748pt

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\left\langle\alpha_{0},\gamma e_{j}\right\rangle\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x_{2})}{2(z_{0}-x_{2})_{\varepsilon}}+\frac{\left\langle\gamma e_{i},\gamma e_{j}\right\rangle\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x_{2})}{2(x_{1}-x_{2})_{\varepsilon}}-\partial_{z_{0}}\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x_{2})\right)\left\langle V_{\gamma e_{i},\varepsilon}(x_{1})V_{\gamma e_{j},\varepsilon}(x_{2})V_{\alpha_{0},\varepsilon}(z_{0})\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}d^{2}x_{1}

\displaystyle d^{2}x_{2}

\displaystyle-\mu^{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}\int_{A^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\left\langle\alpha_{k},\gamma e_{i}\right\rangle\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x_{1})}{2(z_{k}-x_{1})_{\varepsilon}}\times\hskip 284.52756pt

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\left\langle\alpha_{0},\gamma e_{j}\right\rangle\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x_{2})}{2(z_{0}-x_{2})_{\varepsilon}}+\frac{\left\langle\gamma e_{i},\gamma e_{j}\right\rangle\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x_{2})}{2(x_{1}-x_{2})_{\varepsilon}}-\partial_{z_{0}}\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x_{2})\right)\left\langle V_{\gamma e_{i},\varepsilon}(x_{1})V_{\gamma e_{j},\varepsilon}(x_{2})V_{\alpha_{0},\varepsilon}(z_{0})\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}d^{2}x_{1}

\displaystyle d^{2}x_{2}.

All but the two last lines remain bounded as
\varepsilon,\delta\rightarrow 0
thanks to Lemmas
3.2
and
6.1
.

Using again integration by parts for the last line we get regular terms plus:

\displaystyle-\mu^{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}\int_{A^{2}}\sum_{k,l=1}^{N}\frac{\left\langle\alpha_{k},\gamma e_{i}\right\rangle\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x_{1})}{2(z_{k}-x_{1})_{\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle\frac{\left\langle\alpha_{k},\gamma e_{j}\right\rangle\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x_{2})}{2(z_{k}-x_{2})_{\varepsilon}}\left\langle V_{\gamma e_{i},\varepsilon}(x_{1})V_{\gamma e_{j},\varepsilon}(x_{2})V_{\alpha_{0},\varepsilon}(z_{0})\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}d^{2}x_{1}d^{2}x_{2}

\displaystyle+\mu^{3}\sum_{i,j,l=1}^{2}\int_{A^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\left\langle\alpha_{k},\gamma e_{i}\right\rangle\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x_{1})}{2(z_{k}-x_{1})_{\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle\frac{\left\langle\gamma e_{j},\gamma e_{l}\right\rangle\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x_{2})}{2(x_{3}-x_{2})_{\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle\left\langle V_{\gamma e_{i},\varepsilon}(x_{1})V_{\gamma e_{j},\varepsilon}(x_{2})V_{\gamma e_{l},\varepsilon}(x_{3})V_{\alpha_{0},\varepsilon}(z_{0})\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}d^{2}x_{1}d^{2}x_{2}d^{2}x_{3}.

Using the symmetries in the
x_{2},x_{3}
variables, the last integral vanishes on
A^{3}
. This means that this term remains bounded when
\varepsilon,\delta\rightarrow 0
by using Lemma
6.1
.

To finish up with the proof that the correlation functions are
C^{2}
it remains to take care of the derivative of the term

-\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}\frac{(\mu\gamma)^{2}\left\langle e_{i},e_{j}\right\rangle}{2}\int_{A}\int_{B(z_{0},r)^{c}}\frac{\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x_{1})\theta_{\delta}(z_{0}-x_{2})}{(x_{1}-x_{2})_{\varepsilon}}\left\langle V_{\gamma e_{i},\varepsilon}(x_{1})V_{\gamma e_{j},\varepsilon}(x_{2})V_{\alpha_{0},\varepsilon}(z_{0})\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}d^{2}x_{1}d^{2}x_{2}.

We proceed in the same way by using integration by parts to get rid of the terms involving derivatives of
\theta_{\delta}
. We may follow the same lines as in the previous computation, apart from the fact that the new integration domains we will consider either contain or don’t the previous integration variables. We won’t write down the details here since they are quite lengthy and not informative, nonetheless we see that in the end it is enough to show that the integrals (here
\rho
is some positive number)

\int_{A^{2}}\int_{B(z_{0},r)^{c}}\frac{\mathds{1}_{\left\lvert x_{1}-x_{2}\right\rvert>\rho}}{(x_{1}-x_{3})(x_{2}-x_{3})}\left\langle\prod\limits_{k=1}^{3}V_{\gamma e_{\star},\varepsilon}(x_{k})V_{\alpha_{0},\varepsilon}(z_{0})\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}d^{2}x_{1}d^{2}x_{2}d^{2}x_{3}

and

\int_{A^{2}}\int_{(B(z_{0},r)^{c})^{2}}\frac{\mathds{1}_{\left\lvert x_{1}-x_{2}\right\rvert>\rho}\mathds{1}_{\left\lvert x_{3}-x_{4}\right\rvert>\rho}}{(x_{1}-x_{3})(x_{2}-x_{4})}\left\langle\prod\limits_{k=1}^{4}V_{\gamma e_{\star},\varepsilon}(x_{k})V_{\alpha_{0},\varepsilon}(z_{0})\bm{\mathrm{V}}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{\delta}d^{2}x_{1}d^{2}x_{2}d^{2}x_{3}d^{2}x_{4}

are absolutely convergent, which follows from Lemma
6.1
. Therefore the
\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z_{0}^{2}}
derivative of the correlation functions are well-defined. Treating the mixed derivatives
\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z_{0}\partial\bar{z}_{0}}
leads to the same conclusion. Hence existence of both the
\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z_{0}^{2}}
and
\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z_{0}\partial\bar{z}_{0}}
derivatives: this implies that the correlation functions are
C^{2}
.

Remark 6.2
.

The same reasoning shows that the correlation functions are smooth, in conjunction with Lemma
6.1
. This is in agreement with the Liouville case as shown in
[
32
]
.

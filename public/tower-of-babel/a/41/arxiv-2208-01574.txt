Cohomogeneity-One Lagrangian Mean Curvature Flow
Jesse Madnick, Albert Wood

Abstract
We study mean curvature flow of Lagrangians in ℂ n \mathbb{C}^{n} that are cohomogeneity-one with respect to a compact Lie group G ≤ SU ⁡ ( n ) G\leq\mathrm{SU}(n) acting linearly on ℂ n \mathbb{C}^{n} . Each such Lagrangian necessarily lies in a level set μ − 1 ​ ( ξ ) \mu^{-1}(\xi) of the standard moment map μ : ℂ n → 𝔤 ∗ \mu\colon\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathfrak{g}^{*} , and mean curvature flow preserves this containment. We classify all cohomogeneity-one self-similarly shrinking, expanding and translating solutions to the flow, as well as cohomogeneity-one smooth special Lagrangians lying in μ − 1 ​ ( 0 ) \mu^{-1}(0) . Restricting to the case of almost-calibrated flows in the zero level set μ − 1 ​ ( 0 ) \mu^{-1}(0) , we classify finite-time singularities, explicitly describing the Type I and Type II blowup models. Finally, given any cohomogeneity-one special Lagrangian in μ − 1 ​ ( 0 ) \mu^{-1}(0) , we show it occurs as the Type II blowup model of a Lagrangian MCF singularity. Throughout, we give explicit examples of suitable group actions, including a complete list in the case of G G simple. This yields infinitely many new examples of shrinking and expanding solitons for Lagrangian MCF, as well as infinitely many new singularity models.

Introduction

The discovery that Lagrangian submanifolds of Calabi-Yau manifolds are preserved by mean curvature flow
[
42
]
, a phenomenon referred to as
Lagrangian mean curvature flow
, has inspired ambitious conjectures in geometry and theoretical physics. Most notably, the Thomas-Yau conjecture (proposed by Thomas and Yau
[
48
]
and refined by Joyce
[
25
]
) states that, under a stability assumption, an almost-calibrated Lagrangian submanifold deformed by mean curvature flow should converge to the unique special Lagrangian in its Hamiltonian isotopy class. Recently, Lagrangian MCF has been utilised to find special Lagrangian fibrations in log Calabi-Yau manifolds
[
12
]
, confirming a particular case of the SYZ conjecture in Mirror Symmetry. Lagrangian MCF is also of interest in geometric analysis as a particular case of high-codimension mean curvature flow, a challenging subject which is presently far less well understood than the hypersurface case.

Lagrangian mean curvature flow typically forms finite-time singularities. Therefore, to resolve the Thomas-Yau conjecture, a surgery procedure for continuing the flow past a singularity must be developed. Surgery for mean curvature flow has been defined in several special cases, e.g., for two-convex hypersurfaces by Huisken-Sinestrari
[
20
]
and for quadratically pinched manifolds in high-codimension by Nguyen
[
37
]
. In those works, defining the surgery procedure hinges on a complete understanding of the nature of finite-time singularities. To that end, the precise geometry of singularities may be analysed using Type I and Type II blowup procedures, which are limits of rescaled flows at the singular space-time point (see §
2.3
for definitions). Type I blowups are self-similarly shrinking solutions, and in all known cases, Type II blowups are static or translating soliton solutions. To complete the surgery procedure, one must glue in suitable model manifolds. Self-similarly expanding soliton solutions are ideal candidates for this gluing, see for example
[
7
]
. Therefore, to define a suitable surgery procedure for Lagrangian mean curvature flow, we must classify the possible Type I and II blowups of finite-time singularities, and classify the soliton solutions.

In this work, we study mean curvature flow of Lagrangian submanifolds
L\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
that are invariant under the Hamiltonian action of a compact subgroup
G\leq\mathrm{SU}(n)
.
The crucial advantage of working with this sub-class is that a
G
-invariant Lagrangian
L
must lie in a single level set of the
moment map
of the action
\mu:\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathfrak{g}^{*}
at a central value
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, i.e.
L\subset\mu^{-1}(\xi)
(see §
3
for details). Moreover, this containment is preserved under mean curvature flow. This containment effectively reduces the codimension of
L
, mitigating the key difficulty of working with high-codimension submanifolds.

To maximise this advantage, we study
cohomogeneity-one
Lagrangians. By this, we mean that there exists a conjugacy class
(H)
of subgroups of
G
such that for all
z\in L
, the orbit
\mathcal{O}_{z}\cong G/{G_{z}}
has dimension
n-1
, and the isotropy subgroup
G_{z}
belongs to
(H)
. It turns out that a cohomogeneity-one Lagrangian is a
hypersurface
within the
(n+1)
-dimensional coisotropic smooth manifold
M_{\xi}:=\mu^{-1}(\xi)\cap\mathbb{C}^{n}_{(H)}
, where
\mathbb{C}^{n}_{(H)}
is the subset of points with isotropy subgroup conjugate to
H
. For example,
\mathrm{SO}(n)
-invariant Lagrangians
L\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus 0
are necessarily cohomogeneity-one, with isotropy type
\mathrm{SO}(n-1)
. Taking the quotient of
M_{\xi}
by the
G
-action, we obtain a bijection between
G
-invariant Lagrangians in
M_{\xi}
and curves in the 2-dimensional
Kähler quotient

Q:=M_{\xi}/G
(Proposition
3.16
). In short, cohomogeneity-one Lagrangian mean curvature flow in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
corresponds to a modified curve shortening flow in
Q
.

We will focus primarily on the case
\xi=0
, i.e. we assume our Lagrangian submanifolds satisfy
L\subset\mu^{-1}(0)
. This condition occurs naturally in several important settings:

•

An almost-calibrated, cohomogeneity-one Lagrangian is exact if and only if it lies in
\mu^{-1}(0)
(Proposition
4.10
).

•

All cohomogeneity-one self-similarly shrinking solitons, expanding solitons, and special Lagrangian cones lie in
\mu^{-1}(0)
.

•

If
G
is compact and semisimple (e.g.
G=\mathrm{SO}(n)
), then
0\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
is the only central value, so
all

G
-invariant Lagrangian submanifolds lie in
\mu^{-1}(0)
.

A key feature of
\mu^{-1}(0)
is its invariance under the multiplicative action of
\mathbb{C}^{*}
, i.e. if
z\in\mu^{-1}(0)
, then the complex line
P_{z}:=\mathbb{C}\cdot z
is contained in
\mu^{-1}(0)
. Given a cohomogeneity-one Lagrangian
L\subset\mu^{-1}(0)
, the intersection
l:=L\cap P_{z}
is a smooth curve, which we refer to as the
profile curve of
L
. This yields an alternative bijection, one between cohomogeneity-one Lagrangians in
\mu^{-1}(0)
and smooth curves in
P_{z}
(Proposition
4.12
).

A surprising observation is that the mean curvature of a cohomogeneity-one Lagrangian
L\subset\mu^{-1}(0)
may be expressed solely in terms of the profile curve
l\subset P_{z}
, independently of the group
G
and isotropy type
(H)
(Proposition
4.13
). This provides a correspondence between cohomogeneity-one Lagrangian mean curvature flows, and solutions to the following flow of immersed curves in
P_{z}
:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f_{t}}{\partial t}^{\perp}

\displaystyle=\vec{k}-(n-1)\frac{f_{t}^{\perp}}{|f_{t}|^{2}}.

(1)

1
) has been well-studied in the context of the
\mathrm{SO}(n)
-action on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. There are two distinct static solutions, corresponding to the
\mathrm{SO}(n)
-invariant
Lawlor neck
(first documented by Harvey and Lawson

) and the flat special Lagrangian plane. Furthermore, Anciaux, Castro, and Romon
[

classify all connected shrinking and expanding solutions to (

l^{(p,q)}
and

respectively, where
(p,q)

\frac{p}{q}\in(\frac{1}{2n},\frac{1}{\sqrt{2n}})

represents the angle between the asymptotes. This yields a classification of
\mathrm{SO}(n)
-invariant shrinking/expanding solitons of Lagrangian mean curvature flow. Prior work on
\mathrm{SO}(n)
-equivariant Lagrangian MCF also includes singularity analysis by Neves
[

]
, Savas-Halilaj and Smoczyk
[

, Viana

, the second author

, as well as long-time existence and convergence results by Evans, Lambert and the second author
[

]
and Su
[

; for a survey, see
[

. Self-similar solutions to Lagrangian mean curvature flow have also been studied by Lee-Wang
[

,
32
]
, Joyce-Lee-Tsui
[

]
, Castro-Lerma
[

and Su
[

.

Symmetry methods have been utilised in the study of Lagrangian mean curvature flow and special Lagrangians in other ways. For example, Lagrangian MCF of orbits (i.e. the cohomogeneity-zero case) was investigated by Pacini
[

, and a construction of

Overview of Results

In Section
5
, we classify self-similarly shrinking, expanding, and translating solutions to Lagrangian mean curvature flow (henceforth LMCF), as well as special Lagrangians, in the setting of cohomogeneity-one Lagrangians in
\mu^{-1}(0)
. In particular, this generalises the work of Anciaux, Castro and Romon.

Theorem 1.1

(Solitons for Cohomogeneity-one LMCF)
.

Let
G\leq\mathrm{SU}(n)
be a compact connected Lie group. Define the immersed curves:

\displaystyle\widetilde{c}_{k,\overline{\theta}}:(0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{C},\quad\widetilde{c}_{k,\overline{\theta}}(r)

\displaystyle:=re^{i\left(\tfrac{\overline{\theta}}{n}+\tfrac{k\pi}{n}\right)},

\displaystyle\widetilde{l}_{B,k,\overline{\theta}}:\left[-\tfrac{\pi}{2n},\tfrac{\pi}{2n}\right]\rightarrow\mathbb{C},\quad\widetilde{l}_{B,k,\overline{\theta}}(\alpha)

\displaystyle:=\frac{B}{\sqrt[n]{\cos\left(n\alpha\right)}}e^{i\left(\alpha+\tfrac{\overline{\theta}}{n}-\tfrac{\pi}{2n}+\tfrac{k\pi}{n}\right)},

and let
l^{(p,q)}
,
l^{\alpha}
be the self similarly shrinking/expanding solutions to (
1
) of Anciaux
[
3
]
. Let
L\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a connected immersed cohomogeneity-one
G
-invariant Lagrangian submanifold,
z\in L
, and
P_{z}:=\mathbb{C}\cdot z\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Then:

•

L
is a special Lagrangian cone if and only if it is the
G
-orbit of
\widetilde{c}_{k,\overline{\theta}}\subset P_{z}
for unique
k\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\overline{\theta}\in\mathbb{R}
.

•

L
is a complete special Lagrangian contained in
\mu^{-1}(0)
if and only if it is the
G
-orbit of
\widetilde{l}_{B,k,\overline{\theta}}\subset P_{z}
for some
B>0
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\overline{\theta}\in\mathbb{R}
.

•

L
is a complete shrinking soliton for LMCF if and only if it is the
G
-orbit of
e^{i\phi}\cdot l^{(p,q)}\subset P_{z}
for some coprime
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}
satisfying
\frac{p}{q}\in\left(\frac{1}{2n},\frac{1}{\sqrt{2n}}\right)
and
e^{i\phi}\in S^{1}
.

•

L
is a complete expanding soliton for LMCF if and only if it is the
G
-orbit of
e^{i\phi}\cdot l^{\alpha}\subset P_{z}
for some
\alpha\in(0,\frac{\pi}{n})
,
e^{i\phi}\in S^{1}
.

•

L
is a complete translating soliton for LMCF if and only if
n=1
and
L\subset\mathbb{C}
is the grim reaper curve - the unique non-static translating solution to curve shortening flow in
\mathbb{C}
.

We remark that Theorem
1.1
implicitly identifies
P_{z}
and
\mathbb{C}
. When this identification is made carefully, the value of
\overline{\theta}
in the first two results is the Lagrangian angle of the special Lagrangian submanifolds. See Theorems
5.1
,
5.2
,
5.9
and
5.10
for the precise statements.

Theorem
1.1
completely classifies cohomogeneity-one shrinking, expanding and translating solitons, and cohomogeneity-one special Lagrangian cones. However, there exist cohomogeneity-one special Lagrangians in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
that do not lie in the zero level set of the corresponding moment map, and are therefore not included in Theorem
1.1
. For example, there exists a foliation of
\mathbb{C}^{3}
by
T^{2}
-invariant special Lagrangians, discovered by Harvey and Lawson
[
17
]
.

In Section
6
, we turn our attention to singularity analysis, and with a view to applications to the Thomas-Yau conjecture, we focus on flows of almost-calibrated Lagrangians. In
[
50
]
, the author works with
\mathrm{SO}(n)
-invariant Lagrangians, demonstrating that every singularity must occur at the origin, and explicitly describing the Type I and Type II blowup models. We establish analogous results in the general cohomogeneity-one case.

1
) forming a finite-time singularity at the origin, in the case
n=3
. The uppermost curve is the initial condition.

Theorem 1.2

(Singularities of Cohomogeneity-one LMCF)
.

Let
G\leq\mathrm{SU}(n)
be a compact connected Lie group. Let
L_{t}\subset\mu^{-1}(0)
be a connected cohomogeneity-one
G
-invariant almost-calibrated LMCF for
t\in(0,T)
, with a finite-time singularity at a space-time point
(X,T)
.

Then
X
must be the origin. Moreover, there exist
\overline{\theta}\in\mathbb{R}
,
B>0
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}
and a complex line
P_{z}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that:

•

Every Type I blowup at time
T
is the special Lagrangian cone
L^{\infty}
given by the
G
-orbit of the curve
\widetilde{c}_{k-1,\overline{\theta}}\cup\widetilde{c}_{k,\overline{\theta}}\subset P_{z}
.

•

Every Type II blowup at time
T
is the special Lagrangian
\widetilde{L}^{\infty}
given by the
G
-orbit of the profile curve
\widetilde{l}_{B,k,\overline{\theta}}\subset P_{z}
. The asymptotic cone of
\widetilde{L}^{\infty}
is the Type I blowup
L^{\infty}
.

The reader may wonder whether there exist almost-calibrated Lagrangian mean curvature flows with a finite-time singularity, to which Theorem
1.2
may be applied. We answer this in the affirmative, and therefore prove the following existence statement for singularities modelled on cohomogeneity-one special Lagrangians.

Theorem 1.3

(Existence of Singularities with Prescribed Models)
.

Let
L^{\infty}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a complete connected cohomogeneity-one special Lagrangian such that
L^{\infty}\subset\mu^{-1}(0)
.

Then
L^{\infty}
is asymptotically conical, and there exists an almost-calibrated Lagrangian mean curvature flow
L_{t}
forming a Type II singularity at the origin, such that:

•

Any Type I blowup is the asymptotic cone of
L^{\infty}
,

•

Any Type II blowup is
L^{\infty}
.

These are proven as Theorems
6.2
,
6.7
,
6.9
and
6.12
.

Explicit examples of Type II blowups are rare in the literature, especially in the Lagrangian case. Theorem
1.3
provides infinitely many previously unobserved singularity models – one for each group action admitting cohomogeneity-one Lagrangians. In particular, taking
G=T^{n-1}\leq\mathrm{SU}(n)
as in the work of Harvey-Lawson, we find a singularity with Type I blowup equal to a
pair
of
T^{n-1}
-invariant cones. This contrasts with the result of Lambert-Lotay-Schulze
[
29
, Thm. 1.2]
that there is no singularity of almost-calibrated Lagrangian mean curvature flow in a Calabi-Yau 3-fold such that the blowdown of the Type II blowup is given by a
single
Harvey-Lawson
T^{2}
-cone.

It should be noted that Type I and Type II blowups are typically non-unique, and so Theorem
1.2
includes a uniqueness statement for blowups of cohomogeneity-one flows. It also provides further evidence for the conjecture that the blowdown of a Type II blowup of a singular mean curvature flow should be equal to a Type I blowup.

The assumptions of Theorem
1.2
are necessary. Examples of Neves
[
36
]
exhibit several distinct singular behaviours for
\mathrm{SO}(2)
-equivariant LMCF in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
, showing that the almost-calibrated condition is required for uniqueness of blowup models. Indeed, our work requires the almost-calibrated condition to rule out double-density planes in the Type I blowup (see Lemma
6.6
). If one considers cohomogeneity-one LMCF in a level set
\mu^{-1}(\xi)
for
\xi\neq 0
, then the quotient
l:=L/G\subset Q
must be used in place of the profile curve. We expect that as in the
\xi=0
case, singularities will occur only as a result of the orbits collapsing, i.e. the singular space-time point
(X,T)
will have a different isotropy type than the flow. Finally, in order to consider flows with non-constant isotropy type, one would need to work within the full level set
\mu^{-1}(\xi)
in place of
M_{\xi}
; these level sets are not in general smooth manifolds.

Finally in Section
7
, we consider the question of which compact connected Lie groups
G\leq\mathrm{SU}(n)
admit
(n-1)
-dimensional isotropic orbits in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, and so yield
G
-invariant cohomogeneity-one Lagrangian submanifolds. Restricting to the case of
G
simple, a result of Bedulli-Gori
[
6
]
quickly implies a classification of admissible group actions:

Theorem 1.4

(Classification for
G
Simple)
.

Let
G\leq\mathrm{SU}(n)
be a compact simple Lie group. Then there exists a cohomogeneity-one
G
-invariant Lagrangian submanifold in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
if and only if
G
appears in Bedulli-Gori’s table, given in Figure
3
.

In particular, for each group action in this table, by Theorem
1.1
there exists a cohomogeneity-one
G
-invariant special Lagrangian, and by Theorem
1.3
there exists a Lagrangian mean curvature flow with a finite-time singularity modelled on this special Lagrangian.

Conventions:
We set the following conventions.

1.

A connected immersed submanifold
L
in a manifold
M
is a subset that is the image of an immersion with connected domain.

2.

We say
L
is cohomogeneity-one if
L
is
G
-invariant of isotropy type
(H)
for a Lie group
G
and
H\leq G
satisfying
\text{dim}(G/H)=n-1
. This definition is slightly more restrictive than usual, in that we disallow
L
from containing exceptional or singular orbits.

3.

We say that a mean curvature flow
L_{t}
has a singularity at time
T
if there is some singular spacetime point
(X,T)
(see Section
2.3
for the definition). In particular, we do not consider singularities at infinity.

Preliminaries

2.1
Hermitian Linear Algebra

Let
(V,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle,J,\omega)
be a Hermitian vector space of real dimension
2n
, where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is a positive-definite inner product,
J\in\mathrm{End}(V)
is a complex structure, and
\omega\in\Lambda^{2}(V^{*})
is the non-degenerate
2
-form given by
\omega(X,Y)=\langle JX,Y\rangle
. A subspace
W\subset V
is called
isotropic
if
\omega|_{W}=0
. Note that

W\text{ is isotropic }\iff JW\text{ is isotropic }\iff JW\subset W^{\perp}.

If
W\subset V
is isotropic, then
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}(W)\leq n
.
In the other direction, a subspace
F\subset V
is called
coisotropic
if
JF\supset F^{\perp}
. Note that
F
is coisotropic if and only if
(JF)^{\perp}
is isotropic. In particular, if
F\subset V
is coisotropic, then
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}(F)\geq n
.

A subspace
L\subset V
is
Lagrangian
if
L
is both isotropic and coisotropic. Thus,
L
is Lagrangian if and only if
\omega|_{L}=0
and
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}(L)=n
, or equivalently, if
JL=L^{\perp}
. For future use, we record the following easy linear algebra fact.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(V,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle,J,\omega)
be a Hermitian vector space, and let
W\subset V
be an isotropic subspace. Then there exists a complex subspace
E\subset V
and an orthogonal decomposition

V=E\oplus W\oplus JW.

We now let
(V,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle,J,\omega,\Omega)
be a special Hermitian vector space of real dimension
2n
, meaning that
(\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle,J,\omega)
is a Hermitian structure as above, and
\Omega\in\Lambda^{n,0}(V^{*})
is a
complex volume form
— i.e. , an
(n,0)
-form satisfying

\textstyle\frac{1}{n!}\omega^{n}=(-1)^{n(n-1)/2}\left(\frac{i}{2}\right)^{n}\,\Omega\wedge\overline{\Omega}.

(2)

In particular, since
\frac{1}{n!}\omega^{n}=\text{vol}_{V}
is the volume form of
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
, the
(n,0)
-form
\Omega
is non-zero. Finally, note that for each Lagrangian subspace
L\subset V
, there exists
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
, unique up to adding an integer multiple of
\pi
, for which

\Omega|_{L}=e^{i\theta}\,\text{vol}_{L}

(3)

where
\text{vol}_{L}
is a volume form for
L
. The angle
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
is called a
Lagrangian angle
(or
phase
) of
L
.

2.2
Lagrangian Submanifolds of Kähler and Calabi-Yau Manifolds

Let
(\overline{M}^{2n},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle,J,\overline{\omega})
be a Kähler manifold, with Levi-Civita connection
\overline{\nabla}
.
An immersed submanifold
F:L\to\overline{M}
is
Lagrangian
(resp.,
isotropic
,
coisotropic
) if each of the subspaces
F_{*}(T_{x}L)\leq T_{F(x)}\overline{M}
is Lagrangian (resp., isotropic, coisotropic).

If
F:L\to\overline{M}
is Lagrangian, then
J:TL\rightarrow T^{\perp}L
is a bundle isometry by the compatibility of
J
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
. Thus, the second fundamental form
A\in\Gamma(\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(T^{*}L)\otimes T^{\perp}L)
of
L
may be realised as a fully symmetric
(0,3)
-tensor on
TL
:

h\in\Gamma(\mathrm{Sym}^{3}(T^{*}L)),\quad h(X,Y,Z):=\langle\overline{\nabla}_{X}Y,JZ\rangle.

(4)

The mean curvature
\vec{H}\in\Gamma(TL)
may be similarly represented by a 1-form,
\alpha\in\Omega^{1}(L)
, which is obtained by taking a trace of
h
. In local coordinates, denoting the components of
h
by
h_{ijk}
and the components of the inverse of the metric by
g^{ij},

\alpha\in\Gamma(T^{*}L),\quad\alpha_{i}=g^{ij}h_{ijk}.

(5)

Note that since
J
is an isometry, the norms of these new tensors are the same as originally:

\big|H\big|=|\alpha|,\quad|A|=|h|.

Now let
(\overline{M}^{2n},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle,J,\omega,\Omega)
be a Calabi-Yau manifold, so that
(\overline{M}^{2n},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle,J,\omega)
is a Kähler manifold and
\Omega
is a
holomorphic volume form
, i.e. a complex volume form satisfying
\nabla\Omega=0
. If
F\colon L\to\overline{M}
is an immersed oriented Lagrangian, and
\text{vol}_{L}
a volume form for
L
, then by (
3
) there exists
\phi\colon L\rightarrow S^{1}
for which
F^{*}\Omega=\phi\cdot\text{vol}_{L}
. If there exists a function
\theta:L\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that
\phi=e^{i\theta}
, then the Lagrangian is said to be
zero-Maslov
. The function
\theta
is known as a
Lagrangian angle
for
L
, and the pair
(L,\theta)
is known as a
graded Lagrangian
.

The importance of
\theta
is that it is a primitive for the mean curvature:

d\theta=\alpha,\quad\quad J\nabla\theta=\vec{H}.

(6)

In particular, if
\theta=\overline{\theta}
is constant, then
L
is an immersed minimal submanifold. In fact,
L
is calibrated by the form
\text{Re}(e^{-i\overline{\theta}}\Omega)
, and is therefore volume minimising by the theory of calibrations
[
17
]
. A graded Lagrangian with constant angle
\overline{\theta}
is known as a
special Lagrangian of angle
\overline{\theta}
. It is also natural to consider graded Lagrangians satisfying the weaker condition
\theta\in(\overline{\theta}-\frac{\pi}{2}+\varepsilon,\,\overline{\theta}+\frac{\pi}{2}-\varepsilon)
for some
\varepsilon>0
and
\overline{\theta}\in\mathbb{R}
; these are known as
almost-calibrated Lagrangians
.

In the case of
\overline{M}^{2n}=\mathbb{C}^{n}
, there is a natural primitive for
\omega
known as the
Liouville form
:

\lambda:=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}dy_{j}-y_{j}dx_{j}.

(7)

We say
L
is
exact
if the closed
1
-form
F^{*}\lambda\in\Omega^{1}(L)
is exact. We say
L
is
rational
if there is
a\in\mathbb{R}
with

\lambda\left(H_{1}(L,\mathbb{Z})\right)=\{2\pi ka\,|\,k\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

(8)

Note that
L
is exact if and only if
L
is rational with
a=0
.

2.3
Mean Curvature Flow

Consider a Riemannian manifold
(M^{m},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
and a smooth manifold
L^{n}
. A smooth family of immersions
F_{t}:L\rightarrow M
for
t\in[t_{0},t_{1}]
is a
mean curvature flow
if

\frac{dF}{dt}^{\perp}=\vec{H}.

(9)

We denote the image of the immersion by
L_{t}:=F_{t}(L)
, and will often refer to a mean curvature flow
L_{t}
, suppressing mention of the immersion.

A submanifold
F:L\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
with
\vec{H}=0
is known as a
minimal submanifold
; such submanifolds provide static solutions to the mean curvature flow equation (
9
). Other simple solutions are given by
soliton solutions
— flows which move by ambient isometries or scaling.
Firstly, if a submanifold
F:L\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
satisfies

\vec{H}+\lambda\frac{F^{\perp}}{2}=0

(10)

then it follows that
F_{t}:=\sqrt{-\lambda t}F
is a solution to mean curvature flow. If
\lambda<0
, then
F
is known as a
shrinking soliton
, and if
\lambda>0
, then
F
is known as an
expanding soliton
.
Secondly, if
F
satisfies

\displaystyle\vec{H}-V^{\perp}=0,

(11)

for a constant vector
V\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, then it follows that
F_{t}:=F+tV
is a solution to mean curvature flow, which translates in the direction
V
. Such submanifolds are known as
translating solitons
.

For compact submanifolds, the following theorem (proven by Huisken in the hypersurface case
[
19
]
) describes the behaviour of the flow at the maximal time of existence:

Theorem 2.2

(
[
44
]
)
.

Let
L
be a closed manifold,
(M,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
a complete Riemannian manifold, and
F:L\times[0,T)\to(M,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
a smooth solution to the mean curvature flow. Suppose
T
is the maximal time of existence. Then

\lim_{t\rightarrow T}\sup_{L_{t}}|A|^{2}=\infty.

Furthermore, if
(M,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)=(\mathbb{R}^{n},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\text{Eucl}})
, then there exists a constant
c>0
such that

\max_{L_{t}}|A|^{2}\geq\frac{c}{T-t}\quad\forall t\in[0,T).

This theorem motivates the following definition. If
F_{t}:L\to M
is a mean curvature flow for
t\in[0,T)
, then
X=(x,T)\in M\times[0,T]
is a
singular space-time point
if there exists a sequence of space-time points
(p_{i},t_{i})\in L\times[0,T]
such that

(F_{t_{i}}(p_{i}),t_{i})\rightarrow X,\quad\limsup_{i\rightarrow\infty}|A(p_{i},t_{i})|=\infty,

and we say that
F_{t}
has a
singularity at time
T
. We say the singularity is
Type I
if there exists
C>0
such that

\max_{L_{t}}|A|^{2}\leq\frac{C}{T-t}\quad\forall t\in[0,T),

and otherwise we call it
Type II
.

There are two common procedures for analysing the structure of a singularity at a singular space-time point
X
; we describe only the case where
M=\mathbb{R}^{m}
since it is most relevant to our setting, though these procedures are possible also for general manifolds
M
. Consider a mean curvature flow
F_{t}:L\rightarrow M
, with singular space-time point
(x,T)
and area bounds
\mathcal{H}^{n}(L_{0}\cap B_{R}(0))\leq C_{0}R^{n}
, and consider a sequence
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
with
\lambda_{i}\to\infty
, and the corresponding sequence of
Type I rescalings
,

F_{s}^{\lambda_{i}}:=\lambda_{i}(F_{\lambda_{i}^{-2}s+T}-x),

which may be seen to be solutions to mean curvature flow. It may be proven that the flows subsequentially converge in the sense of Radon measures to a limiting flow
F^{\infty}_{s}
, which is known as a
Type I blowup
of
F_{t}
at
T
. In general, this is a weak solution to mean curvature flow given by a family of rectifiable varifolds, known as a Brakke flow. However, if the singularity is Type I, then this convergence may be shown to be smooth by the bound on
|A|
, and the limiting smooth flow of complete submanifolds is a shrinking soliton mean curvature flow. In the Type II case, by choosing a
sequence
of points and scaling around these points so as to normalise the curvature, it is also possible to extract a smooth limit from a blowup sequence. To achieve this, for each integer
k\in\mathbb{N}
, choose
(p_{k},t_{k})
such that

|A(p_{k},t_{k})|^{2}\left(T-\frac{1}{k}-t_{k}\right)=\max_{t<T-\frac{1}{k},\,p\in M}|A(p,t)|\left(T-\frac{1}{k}-t\right),

and define
A_{k}:=|A(p_{k},t_{k})|,\,\,\alpha_{k}=-A_{k}^{2}t_{k}
, and
\omega_{k}:=A_{k}^{2}(T-\tfrac{1}{k}-t_{k})
. Then the family of
Type II rescalings

F_{s}^{(p_{k},t_{k})}:=A_{k}\left(F_{A_{k}^{-2}s+t_{k}}-F_{t_{k}}(p_{k})\right),\quad s\in[\alpha_{k},\omega_{k})

are mean curvature flows, and subsequentially smoothly converge to an eternal mean curvature flow
\widetilde{F}^{\infty}_{s}
with
\sup_{L_{0}^{\infty}}|A|=1
, known as a
Type II blowup
. Such blowups are typically static solutions or translating solutions to mean curvature flow, although this has not been proven in the general case. Note that neither Type I nor Type II blowups are unique in general, in the sense that they depend on the choice of blowup sequence.

Of key importance to this work is the fact that in a Calabi-Yau manifold
(\overline{M},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle,J,\overline{\omega},\Omega)
, the class of Lagrangian submanifolds is preserved by mean curvature flow
[
42
]
. A flow of Lagrangian submanifolds will be referred to as a
Lagrangian mean curvature flow
, or LMCF for short. We will require the following key facts about LMCF:

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
F_{t}:L^{n}\to(\overline{M}^{2n},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle,J,\overline{\omega},\Omega)
be a graded Lagrangian mean curvature flow in a Calabi-Yau manifold, with
\theta_{t}
the Lagrangian angle of
L_{t}
. Then:

(a) The Lagrangian angle satisfies
\frac{d\theta_{t}}{dt}=\Delta\theta_{t}
, and therefore the graded and almost-calibrated conditions are preserved by the flow.

(b)
[
36
]
Any singularity of
F_{t}
is Type II.

Restricting to the case of flows in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, note that by
[
25
, Lem. 3.26]
a graded and embedded Lagrangian MCF must be noncompact; we therefore will often be dealing with noncompact flows. In this situation it is natural to make the assumption of
bounded area ratios
, i.e. there exist
R,C>0
such that
\forall r>R
,
\mathcal{H}^{n}(L_{t}\cap B_{r}(0))<C.

Under this assumption, it was shown by Neves that Type I blowups of singularities of graded Lagrangian mean curvature flows are unions of special Lagrangian cones:

Theorem 2.4

(
[
36
]
)
.

Let
F_{t}:L\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a graded Lagrangian mean curvature flow with bounded area ratios and
\theta
bounded,
(x,T)
be a singular spacetime point, and
F^{i}_{s}
be a sequence of Type I rescalings.

(A) There exist angles
\{\overline{\theta}_{1},\ldots\overline{\theta}_{N}\}
and integral special Lagrangian cones
L_{1},\ldots,L_{N}
such that after passing to a subsequence, for all
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
,
f\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, and
s<0
,

\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\int_{L^{i}_{s}}f(\theta_{i,s})\phi\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}\,=\,\sum_{k=1}^{N}m_{j}f(\overline{\theta}_{j})\mu_{j}(\phi),

where
m_{j}
and
\mu_{j}
denote the multiplicity and underlying Radon measure of
L_{j}
respectively. Furthermore, the set of angles is independent of rescaling sequence.

(B) If furthermore
L_{t}
is almost-calibrated and rational, then for all
R>0
and almost all
s<0
and for any convergent subsequence of connected components
\Sigma^{i}
of
B_{4R}(0)\cap L^{i}_{s}
intersecting
B_{R}(0)
, there exists a special Lagrangian cone
L
with angle
\overline{\theta}
such that for every
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B_{2R})
,
f\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R})
,

\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Sigma^{i}}f(\theta_{i,s})\phi\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}\,=\,m\,f(\overline{\theta})\mu(\phi).

2.4
Group Actions on Manifolds

Let
M
be a smooth manifold equipped with a smooth left
G
-action, where
G
is a Lie group. The left action of
g\in G
on
p\in M
will be denoted by
L_{g}(p)=g\cdot p\in M
. For each point
p\in M
, we let
\mathcal{O}_{p}\subset M
denote its
G
-orbit, and let
H_{p}:=\text{Stab}(p)\leq G
denote its stabiliser subgroup. If
G
is a compact Lie group, then
H_{p}\leq G
is a closed Lie subgroup, and
\mathcal{O}_{p}\subset M
is a compact embedded submanifold diffeomorphic to
G/H_{p}
.

For each point
p\in M
, consider the orbit map
L^{(p)}\colon G\to M
via
L^{(p)}(g)=g\cdot p
. The derivative of
L^{(p)}
at the identity
e\in G
yields the
infinitesimal action

\displaystyle\rho_{p}

\displaystyle=-dL^{(p)}_{e}\colon\mathfrak{g}\to T_{p}M

\displaystyle\rho_{p}(X)

\displaystyle=dL^{(p)}_{e}(-X)=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\exp(-tX)\cdot p.

The image of
\rho_{p}
is the tangent space to the orbit of
p
, and the kernel of
\rho_{p}
is the Lie algebra of the stabiliser:

\displaystyle\text{Im}(\rho_{p})

\displaystyle=T_{p}\mathcal{O}_{p},

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ker}(\rho_{p})

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{h}_{p}=\text{Lie}(H_{p}).

Varying the point
p\in M
yields a map

\rho\colon\mathfrak{g}\to\Gamma(TM)
, which is both
G
-equivariant and (by our sign convention) a Lie algebra homomorphism:

\displaystyle\rho(\text{Ad}_{g}(X))

\displaystyle=(L_{g})_{*}\rho(X),

(12)

\displaystyle\rho([X,Y])

\displaystyle=[\rho(X),\rho(Y)].

(13)

Two stabiliser groups
H_{p}
and
H_{q}
are said to have the same
isotropy type
if they are conjugate in
G
. Isotropy type is an equivalence relation on the collection of stabiliser groups
\{\text{Stab}(p)\colon p\in M\}
; the equivalence class of
H
, denoted
(H)
, is simply the conjugacy class of
H
in
G
. We write
(H)\leq(K)
if
H
is conjugate to a subgroup of
K
. Similarly, two
G
-orbits
\mathcal{O}_{p}
and
\mathcal{O}_{q}
are said to have the same
orbit type
if their stabiliser groups
H_{p}
and
H_{q}
have the same isotropy type.

Theorem 2.5

(Principal Orbit Theorem
[
2
]
)
.

Let
G
be a compact Lie group acting isometrically on a connected Riemannian manifold
M
. Then there exists a unique maximal orbit type (called the
principal orbit type
). Let
M_{0}\subset M
denote the union of principal orbits. Then:

1.

The subset
M_{0}
is open and dense in
M
.

2.

The quotient
M_{0}/G
is a connected smooth manifold, and is open and dense in
M/G
.

3.

The projection
M_{0}\to M_{0}/G
is a fiber bundle with fiber
G/H
, where
H
is a principal stabiliser group.

More generally, for any isotropy type
(H)
, we let
M_{(H)}\subset M
denote the union of
G
-orbits in
M
with isotropy type
(H)
. The projection
M_{(H)}\to M_{(H)}/G
is again a fiber bundle with fiber
G/H
. For more information, we refer the reader to
[
8
, pg. 43-47]
,
[
2
,
\lx@sectionsign
3.4-3.5]
.

Throughout this work, we will be concerned with
G
-invariant submanifolds. We say that a
G
-invariant subset
L\subset M
is a
G
-invariant immersed submanifold
if there exists a smooth manifold
\hat{L}
with a smooth left
G
-action and a
G
-equivariant immersion
F:\hat{L}\rightarrow M
(i.e. such that
F(g\cdot p)=g\cdot F(p)
for
p\in\hat{L}
,
g\in G
) with image equal to
L
. The following proposition provides a sufficient condition for the quotient of such an immersion to be smooth.

Proposition 2.6

(Quotients of Equivariant Immersions)
.

Let
\hat{L},M
be smooth manifolds with a smooth left
G
-action, and
F:\hat{L}\rightarrow M
be a smooth
G
-equivariant immersion. Assume that the
G
-action on
M
has constant isotropy type, i.e.
M=M_{(H)}
.

Then
\hat{l}:=\hat{L}/G
,
Q:=M/G
are smooth manifolds, and there exists a smooth immersion
f:\hat{l}\rightarrow Q
, such that
f\circ\pi=\pi\circ F
, where
\pi\colon M\to M/G
is the quotient map.

Proof.

Choose
y\in\hat{l}:=\hat{L}/G
, and
x\in\hat{L}
such that
\pi(x)=y
. Since
F
is an immersion, there exists
U\subset\hat{L}
,
U\ni x
such that
F|_{U}\colon U\rightarrow M
is a diffeomorphism onto its image. The
G
-invariant set
G\cdot U\subset\hat{L}
is an open subset, and
\pi(G\cdot U)=\pi(U)
is an open neighbourhood of
y\in\hat{l}
.

Since
F
is a
G
-invariant immersion with image in
M
, it follows that
F(G\cdot U)\subset M
is a
G
-invariant submanifold of constant isotropy type. From Theorem
2.5
, the quotient
Q:=M/G
is a smooth manifold, and the quotient
\pi\colon F(G\cdot U)\rightarrow F(G\cdot U)/G
is a smooth submersion to a smooth manifold
l_{U}\subset Q
. Since
F|_{U}
is a
G
-invariant diffeomorphism to its image, it follows that there are diffeomorphisms
\pi(U)\approx\pi(F(U))\approx\pi(F(G\cdot U))\approx l_{U}
. We have therefore found a diffeomorphism from a neighbourhood of an arbitrary
y\in\hat{l}
to a smooth manifold, and so
\hat{l}
is itself a smooth manifold.

Finally, choosing a suitable open cover
U_{\alpha}
such that
\bigcup U_{\alpha}=\hat{L}
, the smooth maps
\pi(U_{\alpha})\xrightarrow{\approx}l_{U_{\alpha}}\subset Q
produced by the above construction glue together to produce the required immersion
f\colon\hat{l}\rightarrow Q
.
∎

Remark 2.7
.

We note that the proof of Proposition
2.6
shows that a
G
-invariant immersed submanifold
L\subset M
is the union of
G
-invariant embedded submanifolds,
L=\bigcup F(G\cdot U_{\alpha})
.

Definition 2.8
.

A
G
-invariant submanifold
L\subset M
is
type
(H)
if

. In analogy with Proposition
2.6
, this will ensure that our quotients are smooth manifolds in the sequel.

3
Hamiltonian Kähler Actions and
G
-Invariant Lagrangians

In this section, we deal with the theory of Hamiltonian actions on Kähler and Calabi-Yau manifolds, and of
G
-invariant Lagrangian submanifolds. Section
3.1
provides an introduction to moment maps for Hamiltonian Kähler actions and Kähler reduction in the case of general group actions. Section
3.2
is concerned with
G
-invariant Lagrangians in Kähler manifolds, particularly the crucial fact that such submanifolds lie in level sets of the moment map (Proposition
3.11
). This allows one to leverage Kähler reduction to reduce their dimension and codimension. Finally, in
\lx@sectionsign
3.3
we consider the Calabi-Yau case, and in particular show that if the
G
-action preserves the Calabi-Yau structure, then
G
-invariant Lagrangians remain in the same level set of the moment map under LMCF (Proposition
3.20
).

We maintain the following setup throughout this section. Let
(\overline{M}^{2n},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle,J,\omega)
be a connected Kähler manifold equipped with a smooth
G
-action, where
G
is a compact connected Lie group. The
G
-action on
\overline{M}
is always assumed to be
Kähler
, meaning that it preserves the Kähler structure:

\displaystyle L_{g}^{*}\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle

\displaystyle L_{g}^{*}J

\displaystyle=J

\displaystyle L_{g}^{*}\omega

\displaystyle=\omega

for all
g\in G
. Let
\mathfrak{g}
be the Lie algebra of
G
, and recall that
\rho\colon\mathfrak{g}\to\Gamma(T\overline{M})
denotes the infinitesimal action. Since the
G
-action is Kähler, the vector fields
\rho(X)
are Killing, real-holomorphic, and symplectic:

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{\rho(X)}\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{\rho(X)}J

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{\rho(X)}\omega

\displaystyle=0.

(14)

We let
\mathrm{Ad}\colon G\to\mathrm{GL}(\mathfrak{g})
be the adjoint action, and
\mathrm{Ad}^{*}\colon G\to\mathrm{GL}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
denote the coadjoint action, where
\mathfrak{g}^{*}=\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{g};\mathbb{R})
. These
G
-actions are related via

\langle\mathrm{Ad}_{g}^{*}(\xi),X\rangle=\langle\xi,\mathrm{Ad}_{g^{-1}}(X)\rangle

for
\xi\in\mathfrak{g^{*}}
,
X\in\mathfrak{g}
, and
g\in G
, where here
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\colon\mathfrak{g}^{*}\times\mathfrak{g}\to\mathbb{R}
is the dual pairing.

3.1
Hamiltonian Kähler Actions on Kähler Manifolds

Definition 3.1
.

A
G
-action on
\overline{M}
is
Hamiltonian
if there exists a smooth map
\mu\colon\overline{M}\to\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, called a
moment map
, such that:

1. For each
X\in\mathfrak{g}
, the function
\mu^{X}\colon\overline{M}\to\mathbb{R}
given by
\mu^{X}(p)=\langle\mu(p),X\rangle
is a Hamiltonian for the vector field
\rho(X)
, meaning that:

d\mu^{X}=-\iota_{\rho(X)}\omega.

2. The map
\mu
is
G
-equivariant:

\mu(g\cdot p)=\mathrm{Ad}_{g}^{*}(\mu(p)).

If there exists a
G
-invariant primitive of the symplectic form, then the action is Hamiltonian with a canonical choice of moment map:

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\lambda\in\Omega^{1}(\overline{M})
be a
1
-form. If
\lambda
is a primitive of
\omega
and a symplectic
G
-action on
(\overline{M},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle,J,\omega)
infinitesimally preserves
\lambda
, meaning that

\displaystyle d\lambda

\displaystyle=\omega,

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{\rho(X)}\lambda

\displaystyle=0,\ \ \ \forall X\in\mathfrak{g},

then
\mu^{*}:=\lambda\circ\rho\colon\mathfrak{g}\to C^{\infty}(\overline{M})
defines a moment map for the
G
-action via
\mu^{X}=\mu^{*}(X)
.

Henceforth, we consider a Hamiltonian Kähler
G
-action on
(\overline{M},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle,J,\omega)
and fix a moment map
\mu\colon\overline{M}\to\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. The
G
-action then gives rise to two distinguished classes of subsets: namely, the
G
-orbits
\mathcal{O}_{z}\subset\overline{M}
and the
\mu
-level sets
\mu^{-1}(\xi)\subset\overline{M}
. The following series of lemmas explores the relationships between them.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
p\in\overline{M}
, let
\mathcal{O}\subset\overline{M}
be the
G
-orbit of
p
, let
G_{p}=\mathrm{Stab}(p)
be the stabiliser of
p
, and let
\mathfrak{g}_{p}
be the Lie algebra of

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ker}(d\mu_{p})

\displaystyle=[J(T_{p}\mathcal{O})]^{\perp}.

\displaystyle\mathrm{Im}(d\mu_{p})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Ann}(\mathfrak{g}_{p})=\{\xi\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}\colon\xi(X)=0,\ \forall X\in\mathfrak{g}_{p}\}.

Proof.

For any
V\in T_{p}\overline{M}
and

, we have

\langle d\mu(V),X\rangle=d\mu^{X}(V)=\omega(V,\rho(X))=-\langle V,J\rho(X)\rangle.

Thus,
V\in\text{Ker}(d\mu_{p})
if and only if

for all

for all

\text{Im}(d\mu_{p})\subset\text{Ann}(\mathfrak{g}_{p})

\dim(\text{Im}(d\mu_{p}))=\dim(T_{p}\overline{M})-\dim(J(T_{p}\mathcal{O})^{\perp})=\dim(T_{p}\mathcal{O})=\dim(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{g}_{p})=\dim(\text{Ann}(\mathfrak{g}_{p})).

∎

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}}

.

Let

\displaystyle G_{\xi}=G\,\iff\,\text{Ad}_{g}^{*}(\xi)=\xi,\,\forall g\in G\,\iff\,\text{Ad}_{g}\xi^{\sharp}=\xi^{\sharp},\,\forall g\in G

\lx@sectionsign
4.2]

Remark 3.5

3. There exists

Proof.

\displaystyle\mu\text{ constant on }\mathcal{O}

\displaystyle\iff\mathscr{L}_{\rho(X)}\mu^{Y}=0\text{ on }\mathcal{O}

\displaystyle\text{ for all }X,Y\in\mathfrak{g}

\displaystyle\iff\omega(\rho(X),\rho(Y))=0\text{ on }\mathcal{O}

\displaystyle\text{ for all }X,Y\in\mathfrak{g}

\displaystyle\iff\mathcal{O}\text{ isotropic}.

For the equivalence of 2, 3, and 4:

\displaystyle\mu(p)\text{ central}\iff\text{Ad}_{g}^{*}(\mu(p))=\mu(p),\ \forall g\in G

\displaystyle\iff\mu(g\cdot p)=\mu(p),\ \forall g\in G

\displaystyle\pi^{*}f^{*}\omega_{Q}

so that
f^{*}\omega_{Q}=0
, and hence
f
is a Lagrangian immersion.

In the other direction, consider a Lagrangian immersion
f\colon\hat{l}\to Q_{\xi}
with image
l
. By the Principal Orbit Theorem (Theorem
2.5
),
\pi\colon M\to Q
is a
G/H
-bundle, and we may define
\hat{L}:=f^{*}M
to be the pullback of this bundle by
f
. Then
\hat{L}
has a fibrewise
G
-action, and there is a natural
G
-equivariant map
F\colon\hat{L}\rightarrow M
which is the inclusion when restricted to each fiber. It is easy to check that
F
is a Lagrangian immersion, and the image of
F
is
L:=\pi^{-1}(l)
.

Finally, it is clear that the maps
\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2}
are inverses of each other, and therefore are bijections.
∎

3.3
G
-Invariant Lagrangians of Calabi-Yau Manifolds

We now suppose that our connected Kähler manifold
(\overline{M}^{2n},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle,J,\omega)
is a Calabi-Yau manifold, equipped with a holomorphic volume form
\Omega
. The following lemma shows how the
G
-action interacts with the Calabi-Yau structure
\Omega
:

Lemma 3.17

(
[
28
]
)
.

There exists a unique element
a\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
such that

(L_{\exp(X)})^{*}\Omega=e^{ia(X)}\Omega

for all
X\in\mathfrak{g}
.

Remark 3.18
.

The above lemma does not require the Kähler
G
-action to be Hamiltonian.

Definition 3.19
.

A Kähler
G
-action on
\overline{M}
is called
Calabi-Yau
if it preserves
\Omega
(equivalently, if and only if
a=0
in Lemma
3.17
).

If
L\subset\overline{M}
is a connected
G
-invariant Lagrangian submanifold, then Proposition
3.11
tells us that there exists a central value
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
such that
L\subset\mu^{-1}(\xi)
. Using the extra structure of the Lagrangian angle, we may explicitly describe how a mean curvature flow of
G
-invariant Lagrangians moves through the level sets of
\mu
. Most importantly, in the case of a Calabi-Yau action, the flow remains in a single level set.

Proposition 3.20
.

Let
\overline{M}
be a Calabi-Yau manifold equipped with a Hamiltonian Kähler
G
-action, and let
a\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
be the Lie coalgebra value of Lemma
3.17
. Let
L_{0}\subset\overline{M}
be a
G
-invariant immersed graded Lagrangian submanifold with Lagrangian angle
\theta
and mean curvature vector
\vec{H}
, so that
L_{0}\subset\mu^{-1}(\xi)
for some central
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. Then

d\mu(\vec{H})=-a.

Therefore, if
F_{t}:L\rightarrow\overline{M}
is a mean curvature flow starting at
L_{0}
, i.e.

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial t}\right)^{\perp}

\displaystyle=\vec{H}

\displaystyle\text{for }t\in[0,T),

\displaystyle F_{0}(L)

\displaystyle=L_{0}

\displaystyle\text{at }t=0,

then
L_{t}
is also a
G
-invariant Lagrangian, and
L_{t}\subset\mu^{-1}(\xi-at)
.
In particular, if the
G
-action on
\overline{M}
is Hamiltonian and Calabi-Yau, then
d\mu(\vec{H})=0
and
L_{t}\subset\mu^{-1}(\xi)
.

Proof.

For
z\in L
and
V\in\mathfrak{g}
, using Lemma
3.17
,

\displaystyle e^{i\theta(z)}\text{vol}_{L}=\Omega\big|_{T_{z}L}=e^{-ia(tV)}\left(L^{*}_{\exp{tV}}\Omega\big|_{T_{\exp{tV}\cdot z}L}\right)=e^{-ia(tV)}e^{i\theta(\exp{tV}\cdot z)}\text{vol}_{L},

and hence

e^{i\theta(z)}=e^{i(-a(tV)+\theta(\exp{tV}\cdot z))}.

Differentiating with respect to
t
and setting
t=0
,

\displaystyle e^{i\theta(z)}\left(d\theta_{z}(\rho_{z}(V))-a(V)\right)

\displaystyle=0

(19)

\displaystyle d\theta_{z}(\rho_{z}(V))

\displaystyle=a(V).

(20)

Since
\vec{H}=J\nabla\theta
, it follows that

\displaystyle\langle d\mu(\vec{H}),V\rangle

\displaystyle=\omega(J\nabla\theta,\rho(V))\,=\,-\langle\nabla\theta,\rho(V)\rangle\,=\,-d\theta(\rho(V))\,=\,-a(V).

Finally, for an MCF
F_{t}:L\rightarrow M
, it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{d\mu}{dt}

\displaystyle=d\mu(\vec{H})=-a

so
L_{t}\subset\mu^{-1}(\xi-at).

∎

4.1
Hamiltonian Kähler Actions on
\mathbb{C}^{n}

Our first observation is that the Kähler
G
-action on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
is Hamiltonian, and its moment map admits a well-known explicit formula.

To derive it, we equip
\mathfrak{u}(n)
with the standard
\mathrm{Ad}
-invariant inner product given by
\langle X,Y\rangle_{\mathfrak{u}(n)}:=-\text{tr}(XY)
, orthogonally decompose
\mathfrak{u}(n)=\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{\perp}
with respect to
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathfrak{u}(n)}
, and let
\pi_{\mathfrak{g}}\colon\mathfrak{u}(n)\to\mathfrak{g}
denote the orthogonal projection. We also note that our
G
-action on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
has infinitesimal action
\rho_{z}\colon\mathfrak{g}\to T_{z}\mathbb{C}^{n}
given by
\rho_{z}(X)=-Xz
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
G\leq\mathrm{U}(n)
. Then the induced
G
-action on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
is a Hamiltonian Kähler action, with moment map

\displaystyle\mu^{*}

\displaystyle:=\lambda\circ\rho\colon\mathfrak{g}\to C^{\infty}(\mathbb{C}^{n};\mathbb{R})

\displaystyle\mu(z)^{\sharp}

\displaystyle:=-\frac{1}{2}\pi_{\mathfrak{g}}(iz\overline{z}^{T}),

where
\lambda
is the standard Liouville form given by (
7
). Moreover, if
G\leq\mathrm{SU}(n)
, then the
G
-action is Calabi-Yau.

Proof.

Note that the Liouville form
\lambda
satisfies
d\lambda=\omega
and
\mathscr{L}_{X}\lambda=0
for
X\in\mathfrak{g}
. Therefore, by Lemma
3.2
a moment map for the action is given by
\langle\mu(z),X\rangle:=\lambda\circ\rho(X)
. The explicit form above is then given by a calculation (see for example
[
13
]
).
∎

The complex cone structure of
\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
yields a natural
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action given by scalar multiplication: that is, the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-orbit of a point
z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
is a complex line with the origin removed. We note that the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action and
\mathrm{U}(n)
-action commute with one another. Two notable sub-actions are the
Hopf
\mathrm{U}(1)
-action
, which is also a sub-action of
\mathrm{U}(n)
:

e^{i\alpha}\cdot(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})=(e^{i\alpha}z_{1},\ldots,e^{i\alpha}z_{n}),

(22)

and the
\mathbb{R}^{+}
-action:

r\cdot(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})=(rz_{1},\ldots,rz_{n}).

For emphasis, we will occasionally write
\mathrm{U}(1)_{\Delta}\leq\mathrm{U}(n)
to denote the particular
\mathrm{U}(1)
-subgroup defined by the Hopf action (
22
). These two actions give rise to two distinguished vector fields on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\alpha}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}-y_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}+x_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{j}}

\displaystyle r\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial r}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}+y_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{j}},

satisfying
J\!\left(r\cdot\tfrac{\partial}{\partial r}\right)=\tfrac{\partial}{\partial\alpha}
, where here
(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})=(x_{1}+iy_{1},\ldots,x_{n}+iy_{n})
.

We now observe that the level sets of
\mu\colon\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathfrak{g}^{*}
inherit the
\mathrm{U}(1)
symmetry:

Proposition 4.2

(
\mathrm{U}(1)
-invariance of
\mu^{-1}(\xi)
)
.

Let
G\leq\mathrm{U}(n)
, let
(H)
be an isotropy type of the
G
-action on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, and fix a central value
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
.

(a) The subsets
\mu^{-1}(\xi)
,
\mathbb{C}^{n}_{(H)}
and
M_{\xi}=\mu^{-1}(\xi)\cap\mathbb{C}^{n}_{(H)}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
are all
\mathrm{U}(1)
-invariant. Moreover, the
\mathrm{U}(1)
-action on
M_{\xi}
preserves horizontal vectors.

(b) For a connected component
M\subset M_{\xi}
, the
\mathrm{U}(1)
-action on
M
descends to a
\mathrm{U}(1)
-action on
Q:=M/G
. That is, there is a
\mathrm{U}(1)
-action on
Q
such that
L_{e^{i\alpha}}\circ\pi=\pi\circ L_{e^{i\alpha}}
.

Proof.

(a) By Proposition
4.1
, a moment map for the standard
\mathrm{U}(n)
-action on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
is given by
\mu^{\sharp}_{\mathfrak{u}(n)}(z):=-\frac{1}{2}iz\overline{z}^{T}
, and we note that
\mu^{\sharp}=\pi_{\mathfrak{g}}\circ\mu^{\sharp}_{\mathfrak{u}(n)}
. Therefore, for any
\alpha\in(0,2\pi]
and
z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, the equivariance of the moment map gives:

\displaystyle\mu^{\sharp}(e^{i\alpha}\cdot z)=\pi_{\mathfrak{g}}[\mu^{\sharp}_{\mathfrak{u}(n)}(e^{i\alpha}\cdot z)]=\pi_{\mathfrak{g}}[\text{Ad}_{e^{i\alpha}}\mu^{\sharp}_{\mathfrak{u}(n)}(z)]=(\pi_{\mathfrak{g}}\circ\mu^{\sharp}_{\mathfrak{u}(n)})(z)=\mu^{\sharp}(z).

This shows that
\mu^{-1}(\xi)
is
\mathrm{U}(1)_{\Delta}
-invariant. Since the
\mathrm{U}(1)_{\Delta}
-action commutes with the
G
-action on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, the remaining claims in part (a) follow directly.

(b) This follows from the commuting of the
\mathrm{U}(1)_{\Delta}
- and
G
-actions.
∎

4.2
The Zero Level Set; Profile Planes

The level set
\mu^{-1}(0)\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
is particularly special as it is invariant under the full
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action. This additional symmetry allows us to work with
profile planes
instead of the Kähler quotient, which will greatly simplify the analysis.

Definition 4.3
.

The
profile plane
at
z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
is the complex line
P_{z}=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{C}}(z)
containing both
z
and the origin. The reason for this terminology will become clear in Proposition
4.12
.

Proposition 4.4

(
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-invariance of
\mu^{-1}(0)
)
.

Let
G\leq\mathrm{U}(n)
, and let
(H)
be an isotropy type of the
G
-action on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Let
M_{0}:=\mu^{-1}(0)\cap\mathbb{C}_{(H)}^{n}
.

(a) The subsets
\mu^{-1}(0)
,
\mathbb{C}^{n}_{(H)}
and
M_{0}
are
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-invariant.

(b) The set
\mu^{-1}(0)
is a complex cone. That is, if
z\in\mu^{-1}(0)\setminus\{0\}
, then
P_{z}\subset\mu^{-1}(0)
.

(c) For a connected component
M\subset M_{0}
, the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action on
M
descends to a
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action on
Q:=M/G
. That is, there is a
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action on
Q
such that

L_{re^{i\alpha}}\circ\pi=\pi\circ L_{re^{i\alpha}}.

(d) The complex line
P_{z}
is orthogonal to
T_{z}\mathcal{O}_{z}\oplus J\,T_{z}\mathcal{O}_{z}
. Therefore, by (
21
),
P_{z}\leq\mathcal{H}_{z}
.

Proof.

(a) From Proposition
4.2
, each of these subsets is
\mathrm{U}(1)
-invariant. Noting that for
r\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, we have
\mu(rz)=-\frac{1}{2}\pi_{\mathfrak{g}}(irz\overline{rz}^{T})=r^{2}\mu(z)
,
it follows that
\mu(rz)=0
if and only if
\mu(z)=0
, and so
\mu^{-1}(0)
is also
\mathbb{R}^{+}
-invariant, hence
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-invariant. Since the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action preserves the stabiliser under the
G
-action, the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-invariance of
\mathbb{C}^{n}_{(H)}
and
M_{0}
follows.

(b) This follows immediately from (a) and the fact that
0\in\mu^{-1}(0)
.

(c) This follows from the commuting of the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
- and
G
-actions.

(d) Since
M_{0}
is
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-invariant, the infinitesimal
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action vector fields lie in

. In particular, the complex line
P=\text{span}_{\mathbb{R}}(\frac{\partial}{\partial r},J(\frac{\partial}{\partial r}))\subset\mathcal{H}\oplus T\mathcal{O}
. Since
P
is a complex line contained in
\mathcal{H}\oplus T\mathcal{O}
, where
\mathcal{H}
is complex and

is isotropic, it follows that
P\subset\mathcal{H}=(T\mathcal{O}\oplus J(T\mathcal{O}))^{\perp}
.

∎

Remark 4.5
.

The preceding proposition is trivial when
\mathrm{U}(1)_{\Delta}\leq G
, since then
\mu^{-1}(0)=\{0\}
. It is therefore primarily of interest in the case
G\leq\mathrm{SU}(n)
.

Since
G\leq\mathrm{U}(n)\leq\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
, the
G
-action on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
induces a natural
G
-action on
\mathbb{CP}^{n-1}=\{P_{z}\colon z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus 0\}
via
g\cdot P_{z}:=P_{gz}
. It is easy to check that this action is well-defined, independent of representative
z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus 0
. As we now explain, this
G
-action in turn yields a natural action of a finite cyclic group on each profile plane, which will be of vital importance for our study of
G
-invariant Lagrangian submanifolds.

Proposition 4.6

(Symmetry of the profile planes)
.

Let
G\leq\mathrm{SU}(n)
,
z\in\mu^{-1}(0)\setminus\{0\}
, and
P_{z}
be the complex line through
z
. Let
\widetilde{H}:=\{g\in G:\,g\cdot P_{z}=P_{z}\}
and
H:=G_{z}
denote the stabilisers of
P_{z}\in\mathbb{CP}^{n-1}
and
z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, respectively. Then:

(a)
H
is a normal subgroup of
\widetilde{H}
, and

\widetilde{H}/H\cong C_{m}=\{1,e^{\tfrac{2\pi i}{m}},\ldots,e^{\tfrac{(m-1)2\pi i}{m}}\}\leq\mathrm{U}(1)_{\Delta}

for some positive
m\in\mathbb{Z}
.

(b) There is a natural action of
\widetilde{H}/H\cong C_{m}
on
P_{z}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
given both by
[\,\widetilde{h}\,]\cdot w:=\widetilde{h}\cdot w
and by the standard inclusion
C_{m}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{U}(1)_{\Delta}
.

Proof.

Let
h\in H
and
\widetilde{h}\in\widetilde{H}
. By definition, this means
h\cdot z=z
and
\widetilde{h}\cdot z=\lambda z
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
. Therefore,

\widetilde{h}^{-1}h\widetilde{h}\cdot z\,=\,\widetilde{h}^{-1}h\cdot\lambda z\,=\,\widetilde{h}^{-1}\cdot\lambda z\,=\,z\ \implies\ \widetilde{h}^{-1}h\widetilde{h}\in H,

and so
H\leq\widetilde{H}
is a normal subgroup.

Now, the natural
\widetilde{H}
-action on
P_{z}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
is unitary, so gives rise to a Lie group homomorphism
\widetilde{H}\to\mathrm{U}(1)_{\Delta}
. Since
\widetilde{H}\leq G
is compact, its image
K\leq\mathrm{U}(1)_{\Delta}
is compact. On the other hand, the
\widetilde{H}
-action on
P_{z}
descends to a unitary
(\widetilde{H}/H)
-action on
P_{z}
, which is faithful since

\widetilde{h_{1}}\cdot z=\widetilde{h}_{2}\cdot z\,\iff\,\,\widetilde{h_{1}}^{-1}\widetilde{h_{2}}\in H\,\iff\,\left[\,\widetilde{h_{1}}\,\right]=\left[\,\widetilde{h_{2}}\,\right].

Hence, there is an injective group homomorphism
\widetilde{H}/H\hookrightarrow\mathrm{U}(P_{z})\cong\mathrm{U}(1)_{\Delta}
whose image is
K
.
Consequently,
\widetilde{H}/H\cong K\leq\mathrm{U}(1)_{\Delta}
is a compact subgroup of
\mathrm{U}(1)_{\Delta}
.

Let
\widetilde{\mathfrak{h}}
,
\mathfrak{h}
be the Lie algebras of
\widetilde{H}
,
H
, respectively. Choose a complement
\mathfrak{k}
of
\mathfrak{h}\subset\widetilde{\mathfrak{h}}
, so that
\widetilde{\mathfrak{h}}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{k}
. Let
X\in\mathfrak{k}
, and consider the vector
\rho(X)|_{z}\in T_{z}\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Observe that
\rho(X)|_{z}\in P_{z}
and
\rho(X)|_{z}\in T_{z}\mathcal{O}_{z}
, so Proposition
4.4
(d) gives
\rho(X)|_{z}=0
. Since the
G
-action is faithful, we have
X=0
, and hence
\mathfrak{k}=0
. We conclude that the compact subgroup
K\leq\mathrm{U}(1)_{\Delta}
is
0
-dimensional, and hence
K\cong C_{m}
is a finite cyclic group.
∎

We now restrict our attention to

\mathbb{C}^{n}

G
-invariant, immersed Lagrangian submanifolds

of a fixed isotropy type
(H)
for which the orbits

are
(n-1)
-dimensional. By Proposition
3.11
, there exists a central value
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
and a connected component

such that

. Our first result gives a formula for the Lagrangian angle in terms of the quotient curve

.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
U\subset Q
be an open subset equipped with a global unit vector field
e_{1}
. Then there exists a smooth function
f:U\rightarrow S^{1}
depending on
e_{1}
such that the following is true.

Let
L\subset M
be a connected, immersed, cohomogeneity-one graded Lagrangian submanifold with Lagrangian angle
\theta
, and let

be the quotient curve in

\theta(z)\equiv\arg\left(f\left(\pi(z)\right)\right)+\arg\left(l^{\prime}(\pi(z))\right)\quad(\emph{mod}\pi),

where the argument of
l^{\prime}
is defined relative to
e_{1}
.

Proof.

Let
\pm\nu_{z}
denote the pair of unit elements of
\Lambda^{n-1}(T_{z}\mathcal{O})
, for
z\in M
. Then we may define the 2-valued complex-linear unit 1-form
\widehat{\beta}\colon T\mathbb{C}^{n}|_{M}\to\mathbb{C}
,

\widehat{\beta}:=\Omega(\pm\nu,\cdot).

Using
d\Omega=0
, it follows from a calculation that for
X\in T\mathcal{O}
,
\iota_{X}\widehat{\beta}=\iota_{X}d\widehat{\beta}=0
. Therefore,
\widehat{\beta}
descends to a smooth
2
-valued complex-linear unit
1
-form
\beta\in\Omega^{1}(Q;\mathbb{C})
, in the sense that
\widehat{\beta}(X)=\beta(\pi_{*}X)
for
X\in TQ
. Note that each
\beta_{p}(X)
is a pair of complex numbers of opposite sign.

Now, define the function
f\colon U\to S^{1}
by
f(p)=\beta_{p}(e_{1})
. Denoting by
T
a global unit tangent vector of
l
and by
\widehat{T}
the horizontal lift of

\displaystyle\pm e^{i\theta(z)}\,

\displaystyle=\,\pm\Omega(\nu_{z},\widehat{T})\,=\,\beta_{\pi(z)}(T)\,=\,\pm f(\pi(z))e^{i\arg(l^{\prime}(\pi(z)))},

which implies the result.
∎

We next show that if such a Lagrangian submanifold is almost-calibrated, then it must be embedded and diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}\times G/H
.

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
L\subset M
be a connected, immersed, cohomogeneity-one
G
-invariant Lagrangian submanifold of type
(H)
. Then:

(a)

If
L
is zero-Maslov and embedded, then

is diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}\times G/H
. Moreover, if
L
is complete, then the ends of

are unbounded.

(b)

If

is almost-calibrated, then

is embedded.

Proof.

(a) If
L
is a Lagrangian satisfying the conditions of (a), then
[
25
, Lem. 3.26]
implies that
L
is non-compact. Now, note that
L
is a
G/H
-bundle over the
1
-manifold

, and that
G/H
is compact. Therefore, since
L
is non-compact and connected, it follows that

is a non-compact connected

-manifold, and hence is diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}
. Thus,

is a

-bundle over
\mathbb{R}
, and any embedding

lifts to a
G
-equivariant embedding

.

Finally, suppose

is complete, so that

is a closed subset. For all

, the sequences

and
F(-k,p)\in L
are unbounded as

. Indeed, if
\{F(k,p)\}
(say) were bounded, then it would admit a convergent subsequence in

, and hence (since

is an embedding) the sequence

would admit a convergent subsequence, which is absurd.

(b) If

satisfies the conditions of (b), then by Proposition
2.6
the quotient

is a connected immersed 1-manifold in

, and so may be parametrised with one real parameter. Assume for a contradiction that

is not embedded, from which it follows that

is not embedded. We choose an immersion

whose image is

.

Since

is a continuous map that is not a homeomorphism onto its image, it follows that either

is not injective or that there exists an open set

for which

is not a closed subset of

. In either case, there exists an open set

, a sequence

, and a real number

, for which

. Without loss of generality, we may assume that

.

Now, the distance between

and

can be made arbitrarily small. Therefore, for any

, using Lemma
4.8
and choosing

sufficiently large, we may complete the curve segment

to a smooth embedded loop
\widetilde{\gamma}=F((s,s_{k}))\cup\gamma
in

in such a way that the change in Lagrangian angle across

is at most

. By choosing
\epsilon>0
sufficiently small and using the fact that

is almost-calibrated, we can make the loop
\widetilde{\gamma}
in

correspond to a cohomogeneity-one Lagrangian in

that is almost-calibrated, embedded, and compact, which contradicts (a).
∎

Finally, we prove that a cohomogeneity-one Lagrangian

is exact if and only if

.

Proposition 4.10
.

Let
L\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a connected, almost-calibrated, cohomogeneity-one
G
-invariant Lagrangian submanifold of type
(H)
, so that
L\subset\mu^{-1}(\xi)
for some central value
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. Then
L
is exact if and only if
\xi=0
.

Proof.

First, assume that
\xi=0
. Let
\gamma:S^{1}\rightarrow L
be a loop, and choose
z\in L
. By Proposition
4.9
,
L
is homeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}\times G/H
, and so
\gamma
is homotopic to a loop
\overline{\gamma}:[0,2\pi)\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{z}
within
L
. Then there exists a smooth path
X_{s}\in\mathfrak{g}
such that

\frac{d\overline{\gamma}}{ds}=\rho(X_{s}).

Therefore, by Proposition
4.1
, and using the fact that
d\lambda|_{L}=\omega|_{L}=0
:

\displaystyle[\lambda][\gamma]

\displaystyle=\int_{\gamma}\lambda=\int_{\overline{\gamma}}\lambda=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\lambda(\rho(X_{s}))\,ds=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mu^{*}(X_{s})=0,

and so
L
is exact.

Now assume
L
is exact, and choose
z\in L
. We wish to show that
\mu(z)=0
, for which it is sufficient to show that
\mu^{X}(z)=0
for all
X\in\mathfrak{g}
such that
|\rho_{z}(X)|=1
. To do this, we would like to use a test loop obtained by exponentiating
X
. However, we cannot be sure that
\{\exp(tX)\cdot z:t\in\mathbb{R}\}
contains a closed loop.

Instead, note that since the orbit
\mathcal{O}_{z}
is compact, for any
\varepsilon>0
there must exist
t_{0}
,
t_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
with
t_{1}-t_{0}>1
and such that

\displaystyle|\exp(t_{0}X)\cdot z-\exp(t_{1}X)\cdot z|<\varepsilon

\displaystyle\implies|z-\exp((t_{1}-t_{0})X)\cdot z|<\varepsilon.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
and
t_{0},t_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
satisfying the above. Choosing
\varepsilon<\widetilde{\varepsilon}<2\varepsilon
, we may complete the curve
\gamma(s):=\exp(sX)\cdot z
to a smooth closed curve with constant speed

,

\displaystyle\overline{\gamma}:[0,t_{1}-t_{0}+\widetilde{\varepsilon}]\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{z},

\displaystyle\overline{\gamma}(0)=\overline{\gamma}(t_{1}-t_{0}+\widetilde{\varepsilon}),

\displaystyle\overline{\gamma}(s)=\gamma(s)\quad\text{ for }s\in[0,t_{1}-t_{0}].

It then follows that

\displaystyle 0=\left|\int_{\overline{\gamma}}\lambda\right|\,=\,\left|-\int_{0}^{t_{1}-t_{0}}\lambda(\rho(X))\,ds\,+\,\int_{t_{1}-t_{0}}^{t_{1}-t_{0}+\widetilde{\varepsilon}}\lambda\left(\frac{d\overline{\gamma}}{ds}\right)\,ds\right|\,\geq\,|\mu^{X}(z)|-\max_{z\in\mathcal{O}_{z}}|z|\cdot 2\varepsilon.

Since
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary, this implies that

, as required.
∎

We now study cohomogeneity-one

of the

In fact, we can say more:

Proposition 4.11
.

Let

be a connected component of

and

(a)

(b) For

(a) Note that

is a closed submanifold without boundary of the same dimension as

. Since

is connected, it follows that
G\cdot P_{z}=M
.

(b) By part (a), we have
w=g\cdot\lambda z
for some

and

.

(c) As in the proof of Lemma
4.8
, let
\pm\nu_{z}
denote the pair of unit elements of

Now fix an element

We may then calculate the value

\displaystyle(L_{g})^{*}\widehat{\beta}_{w}(X)\,

\displaystyle=\,\Omega_{g\cdot w}(\pm\nu_{g\cdot w},(L_{g})_{*}X)\,=\,(L_{e^{i\overline{\alpha}}})^{*}\Omega_{w}(\pm\nu_{w},X)\,=\,e^{in\overline{\alpha}}\widehat{\beta}_{w}(X).

\displaystyle(L_{g})^{*}\widehat{\beta}_{w}(X)\,

∎

When working in the level set

, cohomogeneity-one

-invariant Lagrangians are characterised by their intersection with a complex line

. This gives the following bijection:

Proposition 4.12

(Bijection between
G
-invariant Lagrangians and profile curves)
.

Let
M
be a connected component of
M_{0}:=\mu^{-1}(0)\cap\mathbb{C}^{n}_{(H)}
and let

. Define
P_{z}
and

as in Proposition

. Then there are bijections:

\displaystyle\{G\text{-invariant immersed Lagrangians in }M\}

\displaystyle\longleftrightarrow\{C_{m}\text{-invariant immersed curves in }P_{z}\}

\displaystyle\{G\text{-invariant embedded Lagrangians of }M\}

\displaystyle\longleftrightarrow\{C_{m}\text{-invariant embedded curves in }P_{z}\}

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle\longmapsto L\cap P_{z}

\displaystyle G\cdot l

\displaystyle\longmapsfrom l

Proof.

Consider first a
G
-equivariant immersion
F:\widehat{L}\to M
. We will demonstrate that the map
F|_{F^{-1}(P_{z})}:F^{-1}(P_{z})\rightarrow P_{z}
is a
C_{m}
-equivariant immersion.

Take
p\in F^{-1}(P_{z})
, and choose an open neighbourhood
U\ni p
in
\widehat{L}
such that
F|_{U}
is an embedding; the image
F(U)
is therefore an embedded Lagrangian. By the decomposition (
23
), we have
T_{p}F(U)+T_{p}P_{z}=T_{p}M
, so the intersection of
F(U)
and
P_{z}
is transverse. It follows that
F(U)\cap P_{z}
is a smooth 1-manifold. Therefore,
U\cap F^{-1}(P_{z})
is a 1-manifold and
F|_{U\cap F^{-1}(P_{z})}
is an embedding, which shows that
F|_{F^{-1}(P_{z})}
is an immersion. The
C_{m}
-equivariance simply follows from the
G
-equivariance of
F
, and if
F
is an embedding then clearly
F|_{F^{-1}(P_{z})}
also is.

In the other direction, consider a
C_{m}
-equivariant immersed curve
f:\widehat{l}\rightarrow P_{z}
, and denote the stabiliser group
H:=G_{z}
. We note that
G/H\times\widehat{l}
is a smooth compact
n
-manifold, with a natural
\widetilde{H}/H\cong C_{m}
-action:

[\alpha]\cdot([g],p):=([g\alpha^{-1}],\alpha\cdot p),

and a natural
G
-action:

\overline{g}\cdot([g],p):=([\overline{g}g],p),

which commute with each other. Therefore, the
G
-action descends to the smooth
n
-dimensional compact quotient
\widehat{L}:=(G/H\times\widehat{l}\,\,)/C_{m}
. Now, define the
G
-equivariant map

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle:\widehat{L}\rightarrow M,\quad F([[g],p]):=g\cdot f(p).

This map is well-defined, in the sense that it is independent of choice of representatives, and may be seen to be a proper immersion. Moreover, if
f
is injective, then
F
is also injective, since:

\displaystyle F([[g],p])=F([\widetilde{g}],p])

\displaystyle\implies g\cdot f(p)=\widetilde{g}\cdot f(\widetilde{p})

\displaystyle\implies\widetilde{g}^{-1}g\cdot p=\widetilde{p}

\displaystyle\implies[\widetilde{g}^{-1}g]\cdot([g],p)=([gg^{-1}\widetilde{g}],\widetilde{g}gp)=([\widetilde{g}],\widetilde{p})

\displaystyle\implies[[g],p]=[[\widetilde{g}],p].

It follows that if
f
is an embedding, then
F
is also an embedding.

To see that
F
is a Lagrangian immersion, note that for

and

,
T_{w}\mathcal{O}_{w}\subset F_{*}(T_{p}\widehat{L})\subset T_{w}M=P_{w}\oplus T_{w}\mathcal{O}_{w}
, recalling the decomposition (
23
). Since
T_{w}\mathcal{O}_{w}
is an
(n-1)
-dimensional isotropic subspace,
\widehat{L}
is
n
-dimensional, and
P_{w}
is a complex line, it follows that
F_{*}(T_{p}L)
is isotropic, and hence is Lagrangian for dimension reasons.

The above two maps correspond to
L\mapsto L\cap P_{z}
and

on the level of subsets, for a
G
-invariant immersed Lagrangian
L\subset M
and a
C_{m}
-invariant immersed curve

respectively. These maps may be seen to be inverses of each other, and therefore the bijection is complete.

∎

It is a remarkable fact that the mean curvature of a cohomogeneity-one Lagrangian submanifold of

that lies in

Proposition 4.13

(Curvature of a

-invariant Lagrangian)

(b) Recalling the definitions of

\displaystyle p(w)

\displaystyle r(w)

\displaystyle a_{ij}

Corollary 4.14

(Equivariant LMCF Equation)
.

Let
M
be a connected component of
M_{0}:=\mu^{-1}(0)\cap\mathbb{C}^{n}_{(H)}
and
z\in M
. Let
F_{t}:\widehat{L}\rightarrow M
be a family of connected cohomogeneity-one
G
-invariant immersed Lagrangian submanifolds, and define the profile curves
f_{t}:\widehat{l}\rightarrow P_{z}
as in Proposition
4.12
.

Then
F_{t}
is a mean curvature flow if and only if
f_{t}
is a solution to the flow of curves in
P_{\overline{z}}
given by

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f_{t}}{\partial t}^{\perp}

\displaystyle=\vec{k}-(n-1)\frac{f_{t}^{\perp}}{|f_{t}|^{2}}.

(26)

Finally, we prove the following important formula for the Lagrangian angle of a
G
-invariant Lagrangian submanifold.

Lemma 4.15

(Lagrangian Angle of a
G
-Invariant Lagrangian in
\mu^{-1}(0)
)
.

Let
M
be a connected component of
M_{0}:=\mu^{-1}(0)\cap\mathbb{C}^{n}_{(H)}
, and let
z\in M
. There exists a unitary isomorphism
\Phi_{z}\colon P_{z}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
such that the following is true.

Let
L\subset M
be a connected, immersed, cohomogeneity-one graded Lagrangian submanifold with Lagrangian angle
\theta
, and let
\gamma\colon\mathbb{R}\rightarrow l:=L\cap P_{z}
be a unit-speed parametrised component of the profile curve. Then

\theta\equiv\emph{arg}\!\left((\Phi_{z}\circ\gamma)^{\prime}\right)+(n-1)\emph{arg}(\Phi_{z}\circ\gamma)\,\quad(\emph{mod}\pi).

Proof.

Take
\pm\nu
,
\widehat{\beta}
as in the proofs of Lemma
4.8
and Proposition
4.11
(c). Since
L_{e^{i\phi}}^{*}\Omega=e^{in\phi}\Omega
, it is possible to choose a unit vector
V\in P_{z}
such that
\Omega_{V}(\pm\nu,V)=\pm 1.
Define
\Phi_{z}\colon P_{z}\to\mathbb{C}
to be the unique unitary isomorphism with
\Phi_{z}(V)=1
. Then, taking arguments relative to
V
and writing
\gamma(s)=r(s)e^{i\alpha(s)}V
,

\displaystyle\pm e^{i\theta(s)}\,

\displaystyle=\,\Omega_{\gamma(s)}(\pm\nu,\gamma^{\prime}(s))\,=\,\beta_{\gamma(s)}(V)\cdot e^{i\,\text{arg}(\gamma^{\prime}(s))}

\displaystyle=\Omega_{r(s)e^{i\alpha(s)}\cdot V}\left(\pm\nu_{r(s)e^{i\alpha(s)}\cdot V},V\right)\cdot e^{i\,\text{arg}(\gamma^{\prime}(s))}

\displaystyle=(L_{e^{i\alpha(s)}})^{*}\Omega_{r(s)\cdot V}\left(\pm\nu_{r(s)\cdot V},e^{-i\alpha(s)}V\right)\cdot e^{i\,\text{arg}(\gamma^{\prime}(s))}

\displaystyle=\pm e^{(n-1)\alpha(s)}\cdot e^{i\,\text{arg}(\gamma^{\prime}(s))},

so

\displaystyle\theta(s)

\displaystyle\equiv\text{arg}(\gamma^{\prime}(s))+(n-1)\text{arg}(\gamma(s))\quad(\text{mod }\pi)

\displaystyle\equiv\text{arg}\!\left(\Phi_{z}(\gamma(s))^{\prime}\right)+(n-1)\text{arg}\left(\Phi_{z}(\gamma(s))\right)\,\quad(\text{mod }\pi).

∎

In the almost-calibrated case, this formula for the Lagrangian angle implies that each connected component of the profile curve
l:=L\cap P_{z}
lies in a wedge.

Lemma 4.16

(Wedge Lemma)
.

Let
M
be a connected component of
M_{0}:=\mu^{-1}(0)\cap\mathbb{C}^{n}_{(H)}
. Let
L\subset M
be a connected, almost-calibrated, cohomogeneity-one
G
-invariant Lagrangian submanifold of type
(H)
. Let
z\in L
, and let
l:=L\cap P_{z}
be the profile curve.

Then
l
consists of
m
connected components
\{l_{1},\ldots,l_{m}\}
, such that
l=C_{m}\cdot l_{i}
for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
. Moreover, each connected component
l_{i}
is contained in a wedge
W\subset P
bounded by two half-lines spanning an angle
\alpha<\frac{2\pi}{n}
.

Proof.

The formula for the Lagrangian angle given by Lemma
4.15
is independent of
G,H
and
M
. Therefore, the proof of
[
50
, Lem. 4.6]
is valid for the general cohomogeneity-one case, and it follows as in that Lemma that each connected component
l_{i}
of
l
is contained in a wedge spanning an angle
\alpha<\frac{2\pi}{n}
.

To show that there are
m
connected components, first note that the composition of the maps
\iota:P_{z}\to M
,
\pi:M\rightarrow Q:=M/G
is a smooth
m
-fold covering map. The profile curve may be expressed as
l=(\pi\circ\iota)^{-1}(L/G)
. Taking a point
q\in L/G\subset Q
, there are
m
lifts
\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{m}\}\subset P_{z}
of
q
, related by
e^{\frac{2\pi i}{m}}\cdot p_{1}=p_{k}
. Denoting by
l_{1}
the unique lift of
L/G
to
P_{z}
, there exists a wedge
W_{1}
spanning an angle
\alpha<\frac{2\pi}{n}
such that
l_{1}\subset W_{1}
. Then, defining
W_{k}:=e^{{2\pi ik}{n}}\cdot W_{1}
,
l_{k}:=e^{{2\pi ik}{n}}\cdot l_{1}
, it follows that
l_{k}
is the unique lift of
L/G
to
P_{z}
containing
p_{k}
, and
l=\cup_{k=1}^{n}l_{k}
. Finally, since
\alpha<\frac{2\pi}{n}
, the wedges
W_{k}
are pairwise disjoint, and so
l_{k}
are distinct connected components of
l
.
∎

5.1
Special Lagrangians

The local existence of

Theorem 5.1

(Uniqueness of cohomogeneity-one special Lagrangian cones)
.

For
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, define the parametrised curve

\widetilde{c}_{k,\overline{\theta}}:(0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{C},\quad\widetilde{c}_{k,\overline{\theta}}(r)=re^{i\left(\tfrac{\overline{\theta}}{n}+\tfrac{k\pi}{n}\right)}.

Then for each
\overline{\theta}\in\mathbb{R}
, the correspondence of Proposition
4.12
provides a bijection between:

•

G
-invariant connected special Lagrangian cones
\widetilde{C}\subset M
with Lagrangian angle
\overline{\theta}
and connected link; and

•

C_{m}
-orbits of curves
\widetilde{c}_{k,\overline{\theta}}
for
k\in\{1,\ldots,\tfrac{2n}{m}\}
.

Proof.

Let
L\subset M
be a connected special Lagrangian cone of angle
\overline{\theta}
. By Proposition
4.12
,
L
corresponds to a
C_{m}
-invariant curve

. Since

is connected and
\mathbb{R}^{+}
-invariant, it must be the
C_{m}
-orbit of a single ray,
\widetilde{c}(r):=re^{i\overline{\alpha}}
for some
\overline{\alpha}\in(\frac{\overline{\theta}}{n},\frac{\overline{\theta}}{n}+\frac{2\pi}{m}]
. By Lemma

\displaystyle\overline{\theta}\,\equiv\,\arg(\widetilde{c}^{\prime})+(n-1)\arg(\widetilde{c})\,\equiv\,n\overline{\alpha}\,\,(\text{mod }\pi),

\overline{\alpha}=\frac{\overline{\theta}+k\pi}{n}
for some unique
k\in\{1,\ldots,\frac{2n}{m}\}
.
∎

Theorem 5.2

(Uniqueness of cohomogeneity-one special Lagrangians)
.

For
k\in\mathbb{Z}
,
B>0
, define the parametrised curve

\displaystyle\widetilde{l}_{B,k,\overline{\theta}}

\displaystyle:\left[-\tfrac{\pi}{2n},\tfrac{\pi}{2n}\right]\rightarrow\mathbb{C},\quad\widetilde{l}_{B,k,\overline{\theta}}(\alpha):=\frac{B}{\sqrt[n]{\cos\left(n\alpha\right)}}e^{i\left(\alpha+\tfrac{\overline{\theta}}{n}-\tfrac{\pi}{2n}+\tfrac{k\pi}{n}\right)}.

Then for each
\overline{\theta}\in\mathbb{R}
, the correspondence of Proposition
4.12
provides a bijection between:

•

G
-invariant complete connected special Lagrangians
\widetilde{L}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
contained in
M
, with Lagrangian angle
\overline{\theta}
; and

•

C_{m}
-orbits of curves
\widetilde{l}_{B,k,\overline{\theta}}
for
k\in\{1,\ldots,\tfrac{2n}{m}\}
and
B>0
.

Proof.

Let
L
be a complete connected smooth special Lagrangian of angle
\overline{\theta}
contained in
M
, with profile curve
l
. By the
C_{m}
-invariance of
l
, we may choose a connected component
\gamma\subset l
and a point
w=Be^{i\overline{\alpha}}\in\gamma
minimising the distance to the origin such that
\overline{\alpha}\in(\frac{\overline{\theta}}{n}-\frac{\pi}{2n},\frac{\overline{\theta}}{n}-\frac{\pi}{2n}+\frac{2\pi}{m}]
. By Lemma
4.15
, at the point

,

\displaystyle\overline{\theta}\,\equiv\,\arg(\gamma^{\prime})+(n-1)\arg(\gamma)\,\,(\text{mod }\pi),

and therefore
\overline{\alpha}\,=\,\frac{\overline{\theta}}{n}-\frac{\pi}{2n}+\frac{k\pi}{n}
for some unique

.

Locally near

as
\gamma(\alpha)=r(\alpha)e^{i(\alpha+\overline{\alpha})}

\displaystyle\gamma^{\prime}(\alpha)=(r^{\prime}(\alpha)+ir(\alpha))e^{i(\alpha+\overline{\alpha})}

\displaystyle\implies\overline{\theta}\equiv\tan^{-1}\left(\tfrac{r(\alpha)}{r^{\prime}(\alpha)}\right)+n(\alpha+\overline{\alpha})\,\,(\text{mod }\pi)

\displaystyle\implies\frac{r^{\prime}(\alpha)}{r(\alpha)}\,=\,\tan(n\alpha).

Integrating this equation gives the unique complete solution given in the statement.
∎

5.2
Shrinkers and Expanders

We now classify the cohomogeneity-one shrinkers and expanders of Lagrangian mean curvature flow — i.e. , Lagrangian immersions
F\colon L\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
satisfying the elliptic equation (
10
), where the case of
\lambda>0
corresponds to a self-similarly shrinking solution, and the case of
\lambda<0
corresponds to a self-similarly expanding solution. Observing that the corresponding mean curvature flows sweep out an
\mathbb{R}^{+}
-invariant set, and that the only level set of
\mu
containing an
\mathbb{R}^{+}
-invariant set is
\mu^{-1}(0)
, it follows that any solution to (
10
) must lie in
\mu^{-1}(0)
, and we may therefore restrict to this case.

Using Proposition
4.13
and the decomposition (
23
), we see that for a
G
-invariant Lagrangian
L\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
satisfying (
10
), its profile curve
l:=L\cap P_{z}
satisfies the equation

\vec{k}+\left(\lambda-\frac{n-1}{|l|^{2}}\right)l^{\perp}=0.

(27)

Conversely, any curve in
\mathbb{C}
satisfying (
27
) corresponds by the bijection of Proposition
4.12
to a solution of (
10
).

In the particular case of
G=\mathrm{SO}(n)
acting diagonally on
\mathbb{C}^{n}=\mathbb{R}^{n}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{n}
(recall Example
3.7
), solutions of (
10
,
27
) were classified in a collection of papers by Anciaux, Castro and Romon
[
3
,
4
,
5
]
; these solutions are now referred to as the Anciaux shrinkers and expanders. The following theorem summarises the results:

Theorem 5.7

(Anciaux Shrinkers and Expanders in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
)
.

Consider the diagonal
\mathrm{SO}(n)
-action on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, let
M:=\mu^{-1}(0)\setminus\{0\}
and fix
w\in M
.

(a) For
\lambda>0
, there is a countable family of
\mathrm{SO}(n)
-invariant shrinking solutions of (
10
):

\left\{L^{(p,q)}\,:\,p,q\text{ relatively prime, }\tfrac{p}{q}\in\left(\tfrac{1}{2n},\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2n}}\right)\right\},

where
p
is the winding number of the profile curve
l^{(p,q)}:=L^{(p,q)}\cap P_{w}
, and
q
is the number of maxima of its curvature. In the case of
n=1
, these curves are the Abresch-Langer solutions of self-similarly shrinking curve shortening flow
[
1
]
.

(b) For
\lambda<0
, there is a one-parameter family of expanding solutions of (
10
):

\left\{L^{\alpha}\,:\,\alpha\in(0,\tfrac{\pi}{n})\right\},

with the ends of the profile curve
l^{\alpha}:=L^{\alpha}\cap P_{w}
asymptotic to two lines spanning an angle
\alpha
.

(c) Up to the
\mathrm{U}(1)_{\Delta}
-action on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, these are the only complete
\mathrm{SO}(n)
-invariant solutions to (
10
) in
M\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

In fact, these examples may be used to describe all connected cohomogeneity-one Lagrangian shrinkers and expanders. Indeed, since equation (
27
) is independent of the subgroup
G\leq\mathrm{SU}(n)
, we may use the bijection of Proposition
4.12
to identify
G
-invariant solutions in

with
\mathrm{SO}(n)
-invariant solutions in
\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
, and thereby upgrade Theorem
5.7
to the general cohomogeneity-one case. More precisely:

Proposition 5.8
.

There is a bijection between the following three classes:

1.

Connected immersed curves
\gamma\colon l\to\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
satisfying
\vec{k}+\left(\lambda-\frac{n-1}{|\gamma|^{2}}\right)\gamma^{\perp}=0
, up to
\mathrm{U}(1)
-rotation;

2.

\mathrm{SO}(n)
-invariant immersed Lagrangians
F\colon L\to\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
satisfying
\vec{H}+\lambda F^{\perp}=0
, up to
\mathrm{U}(1)_{\Delta}
-rotation;

3.

G
-invariant immersed Lagrangians
F\colon L\to M
satisfying
\vec{H}+\lambda F^{\perp}=0
, up to
\mathrm{U}(1)_{\Delta}
-rotation.

Proof.

The bijection between classes
1
and
3
is given by Proposition
4.12
, albeit choosing a single connected component of the profile curve. Note that two connected immersed curves are related by a
\mathrm{U}(1)
-rotation if and only if the corresponding
G
-invariant Lagrangian is related by a
\mathrm{U}(1)_{\Delta}
-rotation.

The bijection between classes
2
and
3
is given in the same way, choosing
G=\mathrm{SO}(n)
and

to be the unique connected component of

, and noting that any
\mathrm{SO}(n)
-invariant Lagrangian

.
∎

Theorem 5.9

(Classification of cohomogeneity-one expanding and shrinking solitons)
.

Let
G\leq\mathrm{SU}(n)
be compact and connected, and
H\leq G
a subgroup such that
\dim(G/H)=n-1
. Any complete connected
G
-invariant type
(H)
immersed Lagrangian expanding or shrinking soliton in

corresponds to one of the examples of Theorem
5.7
via the bijection of Proposition
5.8
.

Note that although the profile curves of these solutions are the same as those of Theorem
5.7
, the corresponding Lagrangian submanifolds satisfying (

, and so are in general distinct from the Anciaux examples.

5.3
Translators

Finally, we consider translating solitons of Lagrangian mean curvature flow — i.e. solutions of (
11
). In the case of curve-shortening flow (
n=1
), the grim reaper translating soliton
\gamma(x):=\left(x,\log(\cos(x))\right)
is the unique translating soliton up to scaling and rigid motions, and may be considered a trivial example of a cohomogeneity-one Lagrangian MCF with
G=\{e\}
. For
n>1
, however, there are no cohomogeneity-one examples:

Theorem 5.10
.

Suppose
n>1
. For any compact connected
G\leq\mathrm{SU}(n)
, there are no cohomogeneity-one
G
-invariant immersed translating solitons in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

Proof.

Assume for a contradiction that
L_{t}
is a solution to (
11
) with translation vector
V
. Since the
G
-action preserves
L_{t}
for all
t
, it follows that
G
stabilises
V
, and hence
G
stabilises the complex line
E:=\text{span}_{\mathbb{R}}(V,JV)
. Therefore,

G\leq\mathrm{SU}(E^{\perp})\cong\mathrm{SU}(n-1).

It follows that for any
z\in L_{t}
, the
G
-orbit
\mathcal{O}_{z}
lies in the affine complex hyperplane
\pi_{E}(z)+E^{\perp}\cong\mathbb{C}^{n-1}
. By these identifications, the
(n-1)
-dimensional submanifold
\mathcal{O}_{z}
may be viewed as a homogeneous Lagrangian submanifold of
\mathbb{C}^{n-1}
. Since
G\leq\mathrm{SU}(n-1)
, the Lagrangian angle of
\mathcal{O}_{z}
is preserved by the
G
-action, so
\mathcal{O}_{z}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n-1}
is a special Lagrangian. However,
\mathcal{O}_{z}
is compact, and there are no compact special Lagrangian submanifolds in
\mathbb{C}^{n-1}
for
n>1
.
∎

Although Theorem
5.10
rules out cohomogeneity-one translators for compact
G\leq\mathrm{SU}(n)
, there exist other examples if we allow
G\leq\mathrm{SU}(n)\ltimes\mathbb{C}^{n}
to be noncompact. For example, the product of a grim reaper curve with a real line yields a cohomogeneity-one translator in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
.

6
Analysis of Singularities

In this section, we study singularity formation for cohomogeneity-one LMCF in the zero level set. In particular, we prove that any singular point of the flow must be the origin (Theorem

), classify and prove uniqueness of Type I and Type II blowups (Theorems

and

occurs as the blowup of a mean curvature flow (Theorem

\mathrm{SO}(n)\circlearrowright\mathbb{C}^{n}
of Example

in
[

]
.

We maintain the setup of the previous section. That is, we fix a compact connected Lie group
G\leq\mathrm{SU}(n)
, which acts linearly on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, and an isotropy type
(H)
for which the
G
-orbits are
(n-1)
-dimensional. We also fix a connected component
M
of the
(n+1)
-dimensional coisotropic
M_{0}=\mu^{-1}(0)\cap\mathbb{C}^{n}_{(H)}
, and a point
z_{0}\in M
, letting
P:=P_{z_{0}}
denote the complex line through
z_{0}
. We often identify
P
with
\mathbb{C}
via the unitary isomorphism
\Phi\colon P\to\mathbb{C}
of Lemma
4.15
. For any
G
-invariant Lagrangian
L\subset M
, we denote its profile curve by

. Note that

implies that

is of type
(H)
. By a slight abuse of notation, we will view

as a curve in

by the above identification, so that quantities like

make sense.

6.1
Curvature Estimates

In order to simplify the proofs of this section, we first derive curvature bounds for the equivariant flow. The following argument is due to Lambert, and was conveyed to the authors by private correspondence.

Theorem 6.1

(Curvature Estimates)
.

Let
F:L\times[0,T)\rightarrow M
be a connected
G
-invariant Lagrangian MCF that is almost-calibrated — i.e. , assume that the Lagrangian angle
\theta
satisfies
\theta\in(\overline{\theta}-\frac{\pi}{2}+\varepsilon_{\theta},\overline{\theta}+\frac{\pi}{2}-\varepsilon_{\theta})
for some
\varepsilon_{\theta}>0
,
\overline{\theta}>0
. Assume that the mean curvature
\vec{H}
is bounded on
L_{0}
. Denote by
r:\mathbb{C}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
the distance from the origin.

Then there exists
C=C(n,G,M,\varepsilon_{\theta},L_{0})
(independent of
t
) such that

\big|\vec{H}\big|^{2},|A|^{2}\,\leq\,C\left(1+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\right).

Proof.

Throughout, we allow the constant
C
to change from line to line.
We aim to estimate the function
\Gamma\colon L\times[0,T)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
,
\Gamma:=\big|\vec{H}\big|^{2}f(\theta)\psi
, where
f\colon(-\frac{\pi}{2}+\varepsilon_{\theta},\frac{\pi}{2}-\varepsilon_{\theta})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
is bounded, and
\psi
will be a cutoff function defined on the ambient space. Using this estimate, we will prove that there exists
C(n,G,M,\varepsilon_{\theta},L_{0})
such that

\big|\vec{H}\big|^{2}\leq C\left(1+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\right).

(28)

Then, defining
\vec{p}:=\frac{l^{\perp}}{r^{2}}
,
p=|\vec{p}|
,
k=|\vec{k}|
and using Proposition
4.13
:

\displaystyle p

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{r},\quad k^{2}\leq\left(\big|\vec{H}\big|+\frac{(n-1)}{r}\right)^{2},

so

\displaystyle|A|^{2}\,

\displaystyle\leq\,k^{2}+C(n,G,M)r^{-2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\big|\vec{H}\big|+\frac{C(n)}{r}\right)^{2}+\frac{C(n,G,M)}{r^{2}}

6.2

We now prove two key propositions regarding convergence of blowup sequences. Proposition

-invariant Lagrangian

Proposition 6.3

(Convergence of
G
-invariant Lagrangians)
.

Let
L^{i}\subset M
be a sequence of connected,
G
-invariant, almost-calibrated Lagrangian submanifolds with Lagrangian angles
\theta_{i}
, let
L^{\infty}_{j}\subset M\cup\{0\}
be connected smooth Lagrangians or Lagrangian cones with connected link which are pairwise disjoint for
1\leq j\leq N
, with Lagrangian angles
\overline{\theta}_{j}
, and let
m_{j}
be integer multiplicities. Assume that for any
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
,
f\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R})
,

\int_{L^{i}}f(\theta_{i})\phi\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}\longrightarrow\sum_{j=1}^{N}m_{j}\int_{L^{\infty}_{j}}f(\overline{\theta}_{j})\phi\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}\,\text{ as }i\rightarrow\infty.

Then:

(a)
L^{\infty}_{j}\cap M
are connected,
G
-invariant Lagrangians,

(b) For any
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{C})
,
f\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R})
,

\int_{l^{i}}f(\theta_{i})\psi\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}\longrightarrow\sum_{j=1}^{N}m_{j}\int_{l^{\infty}_{j}}f(\overline{\theta}_{j})\psi\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}\,\text{ as }i\rightarrow\infty.

Proof.

We work with the underlying Radon measures. Denoting the indicator function of a subset

by
\chi_{A}
, we make the definitions

\displaystyle\Theta_{i}:\mathbb{C}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R},\,\,\Theta_{i}

\displaystyle:=f(\theta_{i})\chi_{L^{i}},\quad\quad\quad\Theta_{\infty}:\mathbb{C}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R},\,\,\Theta_{i}:=\sum_{j=1}^{N}m_{j}\,f(\overline{\theta}_{j})\chi_{L^{\infty}_{j}},

\displaystyle\mu_{i}

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{H}^{n}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\Theta_{i},\quad\quad\quad\mu_{\infty}:=\mathcal{H}^{n}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\Theta_{\infty}.

Then, for
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
, and denoting by

the action of
g\in G
on

\mu_{i}(\phi)\rightarrow\,\mu_{\infty}(\phi)\implies\mu_{\infty}(\phi\circ L_{g})=\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\mu_{i}(\phi\circ L_{g})=\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\mu_{i}(\phi)=\mu_{\infty}(\phi).

Note that since

L^{\infty}_{j}
. This proves (a). Defining in the same way the profile curve measures,

\displaystyle\overline{\Theta}_{i}:\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{R},\,\,\overline{\Theta}_{i}

\displaystyle:=f(\theta_{i})\chi_{l^{i}},\quad\quad\quad\overline{\Theta}_{\infty}:\mathbb{C}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R},\,\,\overline{\Theta}_{i}:=\sum_{j=1}^{N}m_{j}\,f(\overline{\theta}_{j})\chi_{l^{\infty}_{j}},

\displaystyle\overline{\mu}_{i}

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{H}^{1}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\overline{\Theta}_{i},\quad\quad\quad\overline{\mu}_{\infty}:=\mathcal{H}^{1}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\overline{\Theta}_{\infty},

our aim is to prove that for all
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{C})
, we have
\overline{\mu}_{i}(\psi)\rightarrow\overline{\mu}_{\infty}(\psi)
.

Consider
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{C})
supported in
B_{\delta}(x)
for some

and
\delta<|x|
. For each

. Picking such an

Now, by

\displaystyle\mu_{\infty}(\phi)\,=\,\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\int_{L^{i}}f(\theta_{i})\phi\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}\,

\displaystyle=\,\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\int_{l^{i}}\left(\int_{\mathcal{O}_{l^{i}(s)}}f(\theta_{i})\phi\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\right)\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}

\displaystyle=\,\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\mathcal{O}_{1})\cdot m\cdot\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\int_{l^{i}}|l^{i}(s)|^{n-1}f(\theta_{i})\psi\,\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}

\displaystyle\geq\left||x|-\delta\right|^{n-1}\cdot\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\mathcal{O}_{1})\cdot m\cdot\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\overline{\mu}_{i}(\psi),

\displaystyle\mu_{\infty}(\phi)=\sum_{j=1}^{N}m_{j}\,\int_{L^{\infty}_{j}}f(\overline{\theta}_{j})\,\phi\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}\,

\displaystyle\mu_{\infty}(\phi)\,\,

\displaystyle\frac{||x|-\delta|^{n-1}}{||x|+\delta|^{n-1}}\overline{\mu}_{\infty}(\psi)\geq\overline{\mu}(\psi)\geq\frac{||x|+\delta|^{n-1}}{||x|-\delta|^{n-1}}\overline{\mu}_{\infty}(\psi),

Proposition 6.4

(Convergence of Translated
G
-invariant Lagrangians)
.

Let
L^{i}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a sequence of connected, almost-calibrated Lagrangian submanifolds with
O\in L^{i}
, and let
L^{\infty}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a connected Lagrangian submanifold,
\nu\in S^{2n-1}
and
y_{i}\in M
such that

•

L^{i}+y_{i}
is a
G
-invariant Lagrangian in
M
,

•

y_{i}\rightarrow\infty
and
\frac{y_{i}}{|y_{i}|}\rightarrow\nu
,

•

L^{i}
converges to
L^{\infty}
in
C^{\infty}_{\emph{loc}}
.

Then
\nu\in M
, and
T_{\nu}\mathcal{O}_{\nu}\subset L^{\infty}
. Since
\mathcal{O}_{\nu}
is isotropic, it follows that
L^{\infty}
contains an isotropic
(n-1)
-plane.

Proof.

Recall
z_{0}\in M
, so that
\frac{z_{0}}{|z_{0}|}\in M\cap S^{2n-1}
. By Proposition
4.11
,
M\cap S^{2n-1}=(\mathrm{U}(1)\times G)\cdot\frac{z_{0}}{|z_{0}|}
, and so
M\cap S^{2n-1}
is closed. Therefore, since
y_{i}\in M
, we have
\hat{y}_{i}:=\frac{y_{i}}{|y_{i}|}\in M\cap S^{2n-1}
, and hence
\nu\in M
.

Now by Proposition
4.11
, there exist
g_{i}\in G
such that
g_{i}\hat{y}_{i}\in P_{\nu}
. Since
G
is compact, by passing to a subsequence we may assume that
g_{i}\rightarrow\overline{g}
for
\overline{g}\in G
such that
\overline{g}\nu\in P_{\nu}
, from which it follows that
\overline{g}^{-1}g_{i}\hat{y}_{i}\in P_{\nu}
,
\overline{g}^{-1}g_{i}\rightarrow\text{Id}
, and
\overline{g}^{-1}g_{i}\hat{y}_{i}\rightarrow\nu
. Furthermore, defining
\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
such that
\overline{g}^{-1}g_{i}\hat{y}_{i}=\alpha_{i}\cdot\nu
, it follows that
\alpha_{i}\to 1
. Then, since

is

-invariant,

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{y_{i}}\subset L^{i}+y_{i}

\displaystyle\implies|y_{i}|\left(\mathcal{O}_{\hat{y}_{i}}-\hat{y}_{i}\right)\subset L^{i}

\displaystyle\implies|y_{i}|g_{i}^{-1}\overline{g}\left(\mathcal{O}_{\overline{g}^{-1}g_{i}\hat{y}_{i}}-\overline{g}^{-1}g_{i}\hat{y}_{i}\right)\subset L^{i}

\displaystyle\implies g_{i}^{-1}\overline{g}\alpha_{i}\cdot|y_{i}|\left(\mathcal{O}_{\nu}-\nu\right)\subset L^{i}.

(36)

Since

is a smooth
(n-1)
-dimensional submanifold and

, it follows that

converges locally smoothly to

. Since

, it follows that the left hand side of the inclusion of (

, which along with the smooth convergence of

to

implies that

, as required.
∎

.

In fact, more is true: given the assumptions of Proposition

6.3
The Type I Blowup

In this subsection, we prove Theorem
6.7
characterising Type I blowups of cohomogeneity-one almost-calibrated Lagrangian mean curvature flow, which we show to be unit-density pairs of Theorem
5.1
’s special Lagrangian cones. A sketch of the proof is as follows. Applying Theorem A of
[
36
]
and Proposition
6.3
, a Type I blowup must be a union of
G
-invariant special Lagrangian cones, and the Lagrangian angle converges in an integral sense. In Lemma

, we use this convergence along with the curvature bounds of Theorem

to rule out higher-multiplicity cones in the blowup, demonstrating unit-density. By Lemma
4.16
and Theorem

, for each Lagrangian angle

there are only two possible cones that may occur in the blowup, and we may argue that the blowup is their union.

Lemma 6.6

(Multiplicity-One Lemma)
.

Let
l^{i}\subset\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
be a sequence of complete connected embedded smooth curves. Let
l^{\infty}\subset\mathbb{C}
be a half-line from the origin,
\overline{x}\in l^{\infty}
,
0<d<|\overline{x}|
, and define
\theta^{i}=\emph{arg}\!\left((l^{i})^{\prime}\right)+(n-1)\emph{arg}\!\left(l^{i}\right)
. Assume further that:

(a)

There exist
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
\overline{\theta}\equiv\emph{arg}(l^{\infty})\,\,(\emph{mod}\pi)
such that for all
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B_{d}(\overline{x}))
and

\int_{l^{i}}f(\theta^{i})\psi\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}\longrightarrow m\cdot\int_{l^{\infty}}f(\overline{\theta})\psi\,d\mathcal{H}^{1},

(b)

There exists

Then

Proof.

Without loss of generality (for example, by applying a rotation and a scaling) we may assume that

is the positive

\overline{x}=1
, and
\overline{\theta}\equiv 0\,\,(\text{mod }\pi)
. We will make the assumption that
\overline{\theta}\equiv 0\,\,(\text{mod }2\pi)
, since the other case is identical. Throughout the proof, all balls are centred at
\overline{x}

Claim 1.

(Uniform convergence of angle)

and
\varepsilon>0

such that for all

.

\overline{\delta}

.

Claim 3.

(Convergence of Length)

,

From these claims, we may deduce the result as follows. Choose

-invariant special Lagrangian cones whose profile curve is
C_{n}\cdot(\widetilde{c}_{k-1,\overline{\theta}}\cup\widetilde{c}_{k,\overline{\theta}})
.

•

There is a unique
B>0
such that any Type II blowup at time
T
is a translation of the connected
T^{n-1}
-invariant special Lagrangian with profile curve
C_{n}\cdot\widetilde{l}_{B,k,\overline{\theta}}
.

Moreover, by Theorem
6.12
, there exists a flow exhibiting such a finite-time singularity. This contrasts with the result of Lambert-Lotay-Schulze
[
29
, Thm. 1.2]
that there is no singularity of almost-calibrated Lagrangian mean curvature flow in a Calabi-Yau 3-fold such that the blowdown of the Type II blowup is given by a
single
Harvey-Lawson
T^{2}
-cone.

7
Examples

Above, we studied
G
-invariant Lagrangians and
G
-equivariant LMCF, where
G\leq\mathrm{SU}(n)
is a compact connected Lie subgroup acting linearly on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Our analysis focused on the cohomogeneity-one case, in which there exists an orbit type with
(n-1)
-dimensional isotropic orbits. In this section, we provide examples of compact connected Lie subgroups
G\leq\mathrm{SU}(n)
admitting such an orbit type.

In
\lx@sectionsign
7.1, we explain how, if
G
is semisimple,
(n-1)
-dimensional isotropic orbits in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
may be recast as Lagrangian orbits in
\mathbb{CP}^{n-1}
. Then, in
\lx@sectionsign
7.2, we quote Bedulli and Gori’s classification
[
6
]
of the unitary representations of compact
simple
Lie groups admitting a Lagrangian orbit in
\mathbb{CP}^{n-1}
. In this way, their list — consisting of 7 infinite families and 14 sporadic exceptions — also classifies the compact simple Lie subgroups
G\leq\mathrm{SU}(n)
that admit
(n-1)
-dimensional isotropic orbits in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Finally, in
\lx@sectionsign
7.3, we mention a further example in which the compact Lie group
G
is not semisimple.

7.1
Preliminaries on the Semisimple Case

Let
G\leq\mathrm{U}(n)
be a compact semisimple Lie group, so that
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})=0
. For
z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus 0
, let
\mathcal{O}_{z}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be the
G
-orbit of
z
, and let
H
be the stabiliser of
z
. By Lemma
3.6
, the orbit
\mathcal{O}_{z}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
is isotropic if and only if
z\in\mu^{-1}(0)
.

As discussed in
\lx@sectionsign
4.2, the
G
-action on
\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus 0
induces a
G
-action on
\mathbb{CP}^{n-1}
via
g\cdot[z]:=[gz]
, where we write
[z]=P_{z}\in\mathbb{CP}^{n-1}
for the complex line through
z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus 0
. We let

\mathcal{O}_{[z]}=\{[w]\in\mathbb{CP}^{n-1}\colon w\in\mathcal{O}_{z}\}\subset\mathbb{CP}^{n-1}

denote the
G
-orbit of
[z]
, and let
\widetilde{H}
be the stabiliser of
[z]
. Note that the
G
-equivariant map
q_{z}\colon\mathcal{O}_{z}\to\mathcal{O}_{[z]}
via
w\mapsto[w]
is a principal
(\widetilde{H}/H)
-bundle. In particular,
\dim(\mathcal{O}_{z})=\dim(\mathcal{O}_{[z]})+\dim(\widetilde{H}/H)
.

Let
\tau\colon\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus 0\to\mathbb{CP}^{n-1}
be the usual
\mathrm{U}(n)
-equivariant surjective submersion
\tau(z)=[z]
, and equip
\mathbb{CP}^{n-1}
with its standard (Fubini-Study) Kähler structure (
g_{\text{FS}},J_{\text{FS}},\omega_{\text{FS}})
. With respect to the Fubini-Study structure, the
G
-action on
\mathbb{CP}^{n-1}
is Kähler and Hamiltonian. To see that the action is Hamiltonian, we recall a well-known formula for the moment map (see, e.g.,
[
27
, pg. 12-14]
). Let
\widetilde{\mu}\colon\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus 0\to\mathfrak{g}^{*}
be

\widetilde{\mu}(z)=\frac{1}{|z|^{2}}\mu(z).

Then
\widetilde{\mu}
is
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-invariant, so descends to a function
\nu\colon\mathbb{CP}^{n-1}\to\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, meaning
\widetilde{\mu}=\tau^{*}\nu
. In the sequel, we use the same symbol
\rho
to denote the infinitesimal
G
-action on both
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
\mathbb{CP}^{n-1}
.

Lemma 7.1
.

The map
\nu\colon\mathbb{CP}^{n-1}\to\mathfrak{g}^{*}
is a moment map for the
G
-action on
\mathbb{CP}^{n-1}
, i.e. :

\displaystyle d\nu^{X}

\displaystyle=-\iota_{\rho(X)}\omega_{\mathrm{FS}},\ \ \text{ for all }X\in\mathfrak{g},

\displaystyle\nu(g\cdot[z])

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Ad}_{g}^{*}\!\left(\nu([z])\right),\ \ \text{ for all }g\in G,\,[z]\in\mathbb{CP}^{n-1}.

Proof.

Before beginning, we set
\gamma:=\partial(|z|^{2})=\sum\overline{z}_{j}dz_{j}
for convenience. Noting that
\text{Re}(\gamma)=\frac{1}{2}(\gamma+\overline{\gamma})=\frac{1}{2}d(|z|^{2})
, we see that every
X\in\mathfrak{g}
satisfies
\text{Re}(\gamma)(\rho(X))=0
, so that
\gamma(\rho(X))=-\overline{\gamma}(\rho(X))
. Recalling the Liouville
1
-form

\lambda=\frac{1}{2}\sum x_{j}dy_{j}-y_{j}dx_{j}=-\frac{i}{4}\sum\overline{z}_{j}dz_{j}-z_{j}d\overline{z}_{j}=-\frac{i}{4}(\gamma-\overline{\gamma}),

we use Lemma
4.1
to observe

\mu^{X}(z)=\lambda(\rho(X))=-\frac{i}{4}\left[\gamma(\rho(X))-\overline{\gamma}(\rho(X))\right]=-\frac{i}{2}\gamma(\rho(X)).

Now, for the first claim of the lemma, we note that
\omega=\frac{i}{2}\partial\overline{\partial}(|z|^{2})=\frac{i}{2}\partial\overline{\gamma}
to compute

\displaystyle\tau^{*}\omega_{\text{FS}}=\frac{i}{2}\partial\overline{\partial}(\log|z|^{2})=\frac{i}{2}\partial\!\left(|z|^{-2}\overline{\gamma}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{i}{2}|z|^{-4}\gamma\wedge\overline{\gamma}+|z|^{-2}\omega,

and hence

\displaystyle\tau^{*}(-\iota_{\rho(X)}\omega_{\text{FS}})=-\iota_{\rho(X)}(\tau^{*}\omega_{\text{FS}})

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{2}|z|^{-4}\iota_{\rho(X)}(\gamma\wedge\overline{\gamma})-|z|^{-2}\omega(\rho(X),\cdot)

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{2}|z|^{-4}\gamma(\rho(X))\left(\gamma+\overline{\gamma}\right)-|z|^{-2}d\mu^{X}

\displaystyle=-|z|^{-4}\mu^{X}(z)\left(\gamma+\overline{\gamma}\right)-|z|^{-2}d\mu^{X}.

On the other hand, we may also compute

\displaystyle\tau^{*}(d\nu^{X})=d(\tau^{*}\nu^{X})=d\widetilde{\mu}^{X}=d(|z|^{-2}\mu^{X})

\displaystyle=-|z|^{-4}\mu^{X}(z)d(|z|^{2})-|z|^{-2}d\mu^{X}

from which we obtain

\tau^{*}(d\nu^{X})=\tau^{*}(-\iota_{\rho(X)}\omega_{\text{FS}}).

Noting that
\tau^{*}
is injective gives the first claim. For the
G
-equivariance, we simply note that

\nu([gz])=\widetilde{\mu}(gz)=\frac{1}{|gz|^{2}}\mu(gz)=\text{Ad}_{g}^{*}\!\left[\frac{1}{|z|^{2}}\mu(z)\right]=\text{Ad}_{g}^{*}(\nu([z])).

∎

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
G\leq\mathrm{U}(n)
be a compact semisimple Lie group, and let
z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus 0
.

(a) The
G
-orbit
\mathcal{O}_{z}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
is isotropic if and only if the
G
-orbit
\mathcal{O}_{[z]}\subset\mathbb{CP}^{n-1}
is isotropic.

(b) If
G\leq\mathrm{SU}(n)
and
\mathcal{O}_{z}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
is isotropic, then
\dim(\mathcal{O}_{z})=\dim(\mathcal{O}_{[z]})
.

Proof.

(a) By definition of
\nu
, we have
\mu(z)=|z|^{2}\widetilde{\mu}(z)=|z|^{2}\nu([z])
. Consequently,

\mathcal{O}_{z}\text{ is isotropic }\iff z\in\mu^{-1}(0)\iff[z]\in\nu^{-1}(0)\iff\mathcal{O}_{[z]}\text{ is isotropic.}

(b) Suppose that
G\leq\mathrm{SU}(n)
. If
\mathcal{O}_{z}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
is isotropic, then
z\in\mu^{-1}(0)
. Now, Proposition
4.6
implies that
\widetilde{H}/H\cong C_{m}
is finite, so
q_{z}
is a local diffeomorphism and
\dim(\mathcal{O}_{[z]})=\dim(\mathcal{O}_{z})
. ∎

Corollary 7.3
.

Let
G\leq\mathrm{SU}(n)
be a compact semisimple Lie group, and let
z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus 0
. The
G
-orbit
\mathcal{O}_{[z]}\subset\mathbb{CP}^{n-1}
is Lagrangian if and only if the
G
-orbit
\mathcal{O}_{z}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
is an isotropic submanifold of dimension
(n-1)
.

Remark 7.4
.

In fact, if
G\leq\mathrm{SU}(n)
is a compact semisimple Lie group, each Lagrangian
G
-orbit in
\mathbb{CP}^{n-1}
is a minimal submanifold; see
[
6
, Prop. 3.1]
. Consequently, the corresponding isotropic
G
-orbit in the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{2n-1}(1)\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
is a special Legendrian submanifold, and hence is the link of a special Lagrangian cone in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, which accords with our discussion in
\lx@sectionsign
5.1.

.

Let

Remark 7.6
.

If
G\leq\mathrm{U}(n)
is a compact simple Lie group, then
G\leq\mathrm{SU}(n)
. To see this, let
K
be the kernel of the determinant map
\det\colon G\to\mathrm{U}(1)
, and let
K^{0}
be its identity component. Since
K^{0}
is a connected normal subgroup of
G
, we have

or

by the simplicity of

. In the former case,

\dim(G)=\dim(G/K)=\dim(\det(G))\leq 1
, contradicting the simplicity of
G
. Thus,
K^{0}=G
, so

, so

.

The upshot is that, by Corollary
7.3

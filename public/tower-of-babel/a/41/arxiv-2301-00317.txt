A Note On Acyclic Token Sliding Reconfiguration Graphs of Independent Sets
David Avis, Duc A. Hoang

Abstract
We continue the study of token sliding reconfiguration graphs of independent sets initiated by the authors in an earlier paper (arXiv:2203.16861). Two of the topics in that paper were to study which graphs G G are token sliding graphs and which properties of a graph are inherited by a token sliding graph. In this paper we continue this study specializing on the case of when G G and/or its token sliding graph 𝖳𝖲 k ​ ( G ) \mathsf{TS}_{k}(G) is a tree or forest, where k k is the size of the independent sets considered. We consider two problems. The first is to find necessary and sufficient conditions on G G for 𝖳𝖲 k ​ ( G ) \mathsf{TS}_{k}(G) to be a forest. The second is to find necessary and sufficient conditions for a tree or forest to be a token sliding graph. For the first problem we give a forbidden subgraph characterization for the cases of k = 2 , 3 k=2,3 . For the second problem we show that for every k k -ary tree T T there is a graph G G for which 𝖳𝖲 k + 1 ​ ( G ) \mathsf{TS}_{k+1}(G) is isomorphic to T T . A number of other results are given along with a join operation that aids in the construction of 𝖳𝖲 k ​ ( G ) \mathsf{TS}_{k}(G) -graphs.

Another way of looking at these reconfiguration problems is via the so-called

The mentioned question now becomes deciding whether there is a path between two given nodes in the reconfiguration graph.

Proposition 12
.

Let

and let

and

be two

-consistent graphs.

\mathsf{TS}_{k}(H(G_{1},G_{2}))

,

\mathsf{TS}_{k}(G_{2})

-element independent sets

in

and
S_{2}

in

satisfying

(1)

the edge between
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
.

Proof.

As remarked, the
k
-element independent sets of

are the same as the union of those of
G_{1}

and
G_{2}
. Therefore,
V(\mathsf{TS}_{k}(H(G_{1},G_{2})))=V(\mathsf{TS}_{k}(G_{1}))\cup V(\mathsf{TS}_{k}(G_{2}))
.
Next, consider an edge in

(respectively,
E(\mathsf{TS}_{k}(G_{2}))
).
It is a token-slide between two independent

S_{1}
and
S_{2}
in
G_{1}
(respectively,
G_{2}
). This remains as a token-slide in

.
Therefore,

.
Now, consider an edge in
E(\mathsf{TS}_{k}(H(G_{1},G_{2})))
between two independent
sets
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
. If both of these are independent sets are in

(respectively,
G_{2}
)

(respectively,

)).

has as

in
G_{1}
(but not
G_{2}
) and an independent set

in
G_{2}

(but not
G_{1}
).

and since

) is satisfied. We have shown that each edge in

\mathsf{TS}_{k}(G_{1})
,

\mathsf{TS}_{k}(G_{2})

), proving the proposition.
∎

(2)

Observe that the graphs

are well-defined because

-consistent.
Moreover, we have

In short, we finally obtain the graph

∎

Proposition 30
.

D_{r,2,s}
(

-graph if and only if

Proof.

(
\Leftarrow
)

(
\Rightarrow
)

Suppose that

Suppose to the contrary that there exists a graph

such that

if and only if

, where

is a size-

Since

has

such that

and

, where

.
Observe that

must be a size-

, where

.

–

Neither

nor

is
p_{1}
.

In this case, we must have

, otherwise it must be adjacent to
p_{2}
, but then

and

must be adjacent in

, a contradiction.
It follows that

nor

cannot be defined.

–

u
is
p_{1}
.

.

5
Conclusions

In this paper, we
considered two token sliding problems for trees and forests.
The two questions studied seem remarkably complicated, even for
this simple class of graphs.
For the first question, finding necessary and sufficient conditions on
G
for
\mathsf{TS}_{k}(G)
to be a
forest, we could only get a complete solution for
k=2,3
.
For the second question, finding necessary and sufficient conditions for a tree or forest to
be a token sliding graph, we could get more general results. Nevertheless, as noted in
Section
4

several interesting important questions remain.
We expect the join and decomposition operations introduced there will be of use for
similar questions for more general graphs.

Total positivity in twisted product of flag varieties
Huanchen Bao, Xuhua He

Abstract
. We show that the totally nonnegative part of the twisted product of flag varieties of a Kac-Moody group admits a cellular decomposition, and the closure of each cell is a topological manifold with boundary. We also establish explicit parameterizations of each totally positive cell. In the special cases of double flag varieties and braid varieties, we show that the totally nonnegative parts are regular CW complexes homeomorphic to closed balls. Moreover, we prove that the link of any totally nonnegative double Bruhat cell in a reductive group is a regular CW complex homeomorphic to a closed ball, solving an open problem of Fomin and Zelevinsky.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Flag varieties

We first give a quick review of the totally nonnegative flag varieties.

Let
G
be a connected Kac-Moody group, split over
\mathbb{R}
. Let
B^{+}=TU^{+}
be a Borel subgroup of
G
and let
B^{-}=TU^{-}
be the opposite Borel subgroup. Let
W
be the Weyl group of
G
. In
[
22
]
and
[
23
]
, Lusztig introduced the totally nonnegative monoid

G_{\geqslant 0}=U^{+}_{\geqslant 0}T_{>0}U^{-}_{\geqslant 0}=U^{-}_{\geqslant 0}T_{>0}U^{+}_{\geqslant 0}.

Let
\mathcal{B}=G/B^{+}
be the flag variety. It admits the decomposition into open Richardson varieties
\mathcal{B}=\sqcup\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v,w}
, where
v
and
w
are elements in the Weyl group
W
, and the open Richardson variety
\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v,w}
, by definition, is the intersection of the Schubert cell
B^{+}\dot{w}B^{+}/B^{+}
and the opposite Schubert cell
B^{-}\dot{v}B^{+}/B^{+}
. The totally nonnegative flag variety
\mathcal{B}_{\geqslant 0}
is defined as the closure of
U^{-}_{\geqslant 0}B^{+}/B^{+}
in
\mathcal{B}
with respect to Hausdorff topology.

The totally nonnegative flag is a “remarkable polyhedral subspace” (cf.
[
22
]
). In particular, we have

(1)

(decomposition into cells)
\mathcal{B}_{\geqslant 0}=\sqcup\mathcal{B}_{v,w,>0}
, where each totally positive stratum
\mathcal{B}_{v,w,>0}=\mathcal{B}_{\geqslant 0}\cap\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v,w}
is a cell and admits an explicit parametrization (see
[
17
]
and
[
3
]
);

(2)

the closure of each totally positive stratum is a union of totally positive strata (see
[
27
]
and
[
4
]
);

(3)

the closure of each totally positive stratum is homeomorphic to a closed ball (see
[
11
]
and
[
4
]
);

(4)

If
G
is a connected reductive group, then the flag variety
\mathcal{B}
admits a natural duality which stabilizes the totally nonnegative part
\mathcal{B}_{\geqslant 0}
and sends each totally positive stratum to another totally positive stratum (see
[
22
]
and
[
24
]
).

1.2.
Main result

The main object in this paper is the twisted product of flag varieties

\mathcal{Z}=G\times^{B^{+}}G\times^{B^{+}}\cdots\times^{B^{+}}G/B^{+}\quad(n\text{ factors}).

For any sequence
\underline{w}=(w_{1},\cdots,w_{n})
of elements in
W
, we define

\mathring{\mathcal{Z}}_{\underline{w}}=B^{+}\dot{w}_{1}B^{+}\times^{B^{+}}B^{+}\dot{w}_{2}B^{+}\times^{B^{+}}\cdots\times^{B^{+}}B^{+}\dot{w}_{n}B^{+}/B^{+}.

This is analogous to Schubert cell in the flag
\mathcal{B}
. For any
v\in W
, we define
\mathring{\mathcal{Z}}^{v}
as
m^{-1}(B^{-}\dot{v}B^{+}/B^{+})
, where
m:\mathcal{Z}\mathcal{B}
is the convolution product. This is analogous to the opposite Schubert cell in the flag
\mathcal{B}
. The open Richardson variety of
\mathcal{Z}
is defined by
\mathring{\mathcal{Z}}_{v,\underline{w}}=\mathring{\mathcal{Z}}_{\underline{w}}\cap\mathring{\mathcal{Z}}^{v}
. These open Richardson varieties include as special cases the Bott-Samelson varieties and the braid varieties.

We define the totally nonnnegative part
\mathcal{Z}_{\geqslant 0}
of
\mathcal{Z}
to be the Hausdorff clsoure of
(U^{-}_{\geqslant 0},U^{-}_{\geqslant 0},\ldots,U^{-}_{\geqslant 0}B^{+}/B^{+})
in
\mathcal{Z}
. We set
\mathcal{Z}_{v,\underline{w},>0}=\mathcal{Z}_{\geqslant 0}\cap\mathring{\mathcal{Z}}_{v,\underline{w}}
and call it a
totally positive stratum
of
\mathcal{Z}
. We have the decomposition
\mathcal{Z}_{\geqslant 0}=\sqcup\mathcal{Z}_{v,\underline{w},>0}
.

The goal of this paper is to show that the totally nonnegative twisted product of flag is still a “remarkable polyhedral subspace”.

Theorem 1
.

[Theorem
3.4
, Proposition
3.5

\&
Proposition
3.7
]
(1) Each stratum
\mathcal{Z}_{v,\underline{w},>0}
is a topological cell and we have an explicit parameterization of the cell;

(2) The closure of each totally positive stratum in
\mathcal{Z}
is a union of some totally positive strata in
\mathcal{Z}
;

(3) The closure of each totally positive stratum in
\mathcal{Z}
is a topological manifold with boundary, where the boundary consists of all lower dimensional strata.

(4) If
G
is a connected reductive group, we have a natural duality on
\mathcal{Z}_{\geqslant 0}
generalizing Lusztig’s duality on totally nonnegative flag varieties.

1.3.
Special case: braid varieties

A braid
\underline{s}
is a sequence
(s_{i_{1}},\dots,s_{i_{n}})
of simple reflections in
W
. Let
w=s_{i_{1}}\ast\cdots\ast s_{i_{n}}
be the Demazure product. The closed (resp. open) braid variety is defined as
\mathcal{Z}_{w,\underline{s}}
(resp.
\mathring{\mathcal{Z}}_{w,\underline{s}}
). When
G
is a reductive group, the open braid varieties contain open Richardson varieties as special cases.

As a special case of Theorem
1
, we see that the totally nonnegative braid variety is a “remarkable polyhedral subspace”. We further establish the regularity theorem in this case.

Theorem 2

(Theorem
5.1
)
.

The totally nonnegative braid variety
\mathcal{Z}_{w,\underline{s},\geqslant 0}
is regular CW complex homeomorphic to a closed ball.

The cluster algebra structure on the coordinate ring of the open braid variety
\mathring{\mathcal{Z}}_{w,\underline{s}}
for reductive groups has recently been established by Casals, Gorsky, Gorsky, Le, Shen, and Simental in
[
6
]
and by Galashin, Lam, Sherman-Bennett, and Speyer in
[
12
,
13
]
. It is interesting to compare the total positivity structure with the cluster algebra structure.

The totally nonnegative braid variety
\mathcal{Z}_{w,\underline{s},\geqslant 0}
also provides a geometric realization of the subword complex considered by Knutson and Miller in
[
21
, Corollary 3.8]
.

1.4.
Special case: double flag varieties

In this subsection, we consider the twisted product of flag varieties with
2
factors. In this case,
\mathcal{Z}
is isomorphic to the double flag
\mathcal{B}\times\mathcal{B}
. Under this identification, the open Richardson varieties in
\mathcal{Z}
are the intersections of the
B^{+}\times B^{-}
-orbits and the diagonal
G
-orbits in
\mathcal{B}\times\mathcal{B}
. Below, we give an explicit example of totally nonnegative double flag varieties.

Example 1.1
.

Let
G=SL_{2}
. In this case
\mathcal{B}\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}
and
\mathcal{B}_{\geqslant 0}=[0,\infty]
. The totally nonnegative double flag variety
\mathcal{Z}_{\geqslant 0}=\{(a,b);a,b\in[0,\infty],b-a\geqslant 0\}
. So
\mathcal{Z}_{\geqslant 0}
is a proper subspace of
\mathcal{B}_{\geqslant 0}\times\mathcal{B}_{\geqslant 0}
defined by some nontrivial inequalities.

The notion of totally nonnegative double flag varieties was introduced by Webster and Yakimov in
[
29
]
. The totally nonnegative double flag variety of
SL_{2}
above was the only case understood for many years. The essential difficulty is to handle the extra nontrivial inequalities arising from the double flag. There is also a representation-theoretical construction of
\mathcal{Z}_{\geqslant 0}
relying on the canonical bases on tensor products of
G
-modules. The difficulty in studying
\mathcal{Z}_{\geqslant 0}
versus
\mathcal{B}_{\geqslant 0}\times\mathcal{B}_{\geqslant 0}
can also be seen from the difficulty in constructing (geometrically or categorically) canonical bases on tensor products of
G
-modules.

As a special case of Theorem
1
, we see that the totally nonnegative double flag variety is a “remarkable polyhedral subspace”. This in particular verifies a conjecture of Webster and Yakimov in
[
29
]
. We further establish the regularity theorem for totally nonnegative double flag varieties.

Theorem 3

(Theorem
5.3
)
.

The totally nonnegative double flag variety is a regular CW complex homeomorphic to a closed ball.

1.5.
Special case: double Bruhat cells

The double Bruhat cells of a reductive group are the intersection of the
B^{+}\times B^{+}
-orbits and the
B^{-}\times B^{-}
-orbits on
G
. They play an important role in the theory of cluster algebras (see
[
10
]
).

It is easy to see that the maximal torus
T
of
G
acts freely on each double Bruhat cell
G^{s,t}
of
G
. We denote by
L^{s,t}=G^{s,t}/T
the reduced double Bruhat cell.

We may identify the quotient space
G/T
with a subvariety of
\mathcal{X}
sending a reduced double Bruhat cell
L^{s,t}
to a stratum
\mathring{\mathcal{X}}^{u}_{v,w}
of the double flag variety. Moreover, under this identification, the image of the totally positive part
G^{s,t}_{>0}:=G^{s,t}\cap G_{\geqslant 0}
of the double Bruhat cells
G^{s,t}
coincides with the totally positive part
{\mathcal{X}}^{u}_{v,w,>0}
of the stratum
\mathring{\mathcal{X}}^{u}_{v,w}
in the double flag variety
\mathcal{X}
. We obtain

Theorem 4

(Theorem
5.8
)
.

The link of each totally positive double Bruhat cell of a connected reductive group is a regular CW complex homeomorphic to a closed ball.

This solves an open problem of Fomin and Zelevinsky (see
[
25
, Page 9]
and
[
11
, Page 571]
). It also generalizes the work of Hersh
[
16
]
on the cells in
U^{-}_{\geqslant 0}
. The special case for
GL_{n}
was first proved by Galashin, Karp and Lam in
[
11
]
using the embedding of the double Bruhat cells in
GL_{n}
into the Grassmannian
Gr(n,2n)
due to Postnikov and the regularity of postroid cells in
Gr(n,2n)
. Our approach for
GL_{n}
is different from
[
11
]
.

Our strategy

,

(see Figure

2.
Flag varieties and their totally nonnegative parts

2.1.
Kac-Moody groups

A
Kac-Moody root datum
is a quintuple

\mathcal{D}=(I,A,X,Y,(\alpha_{i})_{i\in I},(\alpha^{\vee}_{i})_{i\in I}),

where
I
is a finite set,
A=(a_{ij})_{i,j\in I}
is a symmetrizable generalized Cartan matrix in the sense of
[
18
, §1.1]
,
X
is a free
\mathbb{Z}
-module of finite rank with
\mathbb{Z}
-dual
Y
, and the elements
\alpha_{i}
of
X
and
\alpha^{\vee}_{i}
of
Y
such that
\langle\alpha^{\vee}_{j},\alpha_{i}\rangle=a_{ij}
for
i,j\in I
.

Let
\Delta^{re}=\{w(\pm\alpha_{i})\in X\mid i\in I,w\in W\}\subset X
be the set of real roots. Then
\Delta^{re}=\Delta^{re}_{+}\sqcup\Delta^{re}_{-}
is the union of positive real roots and negative real roots. Let
\mathbf{k}
be an algebraically closed field. The
minimal Kac-Moody group

G
associated with the Kac-Moody root datum
\mathcal{D}
is the group generated by the torus
T=Y\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{k}^{\times}
and the root subgroup
U_{\alpha}\cong\mathbf{k}
for each real root
\alpha
, subject to the Tits relations
[
28
]
. Let
U^{+}\subset G
(resp.
U^{-}\subset G
) be the subgroup generated by
U_{\alpha}
for
\alpha\in\Delta^{re}_{+}
(resp.
\alpha\in\Delta^{re}_{-}
). Let
B^{\pm}\subset G
be the Borel subgroup generated by
T
and
U^{\pm}
.
For each
i\in I
, we fix isomorphisms
x_{i}:\mathbb{C}U_{\alpha_{i}}
and
y_{i}:\mathbb{C}U_{-\alpha_{i}}
such that the map

\begin{pmatrix}1&a\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}x_{i}(a),\begin{pmatrix}b&0\\
0&b^{-1}\end{pmatrix}\alpha^{\vee}_{i}(a),\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
c&1\end{pmatrix}y_{i}(c)

defines a homomorphism
SL_{2}G
. The data
(T,B^{+},B^{-},x_{i},y_{i};i\in I)
is called a
pinning
for
G
.

Let
W
be the Weyl group of
G
. For
i\in I
, we denote by
s_{i}\in W
the corresponding simple reflection and write
\dot{s}_{i}=x_{i}(1)y_{i}(-1)x_{i}(1)\in G
. For any
w\in W
with reduced expression
w=s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{n}}
, we define
\dot{w}=\dot{s}_{i_{1}}\cdots\dot{s}_{i_{n}}\in G
. It is known that
\dot{w}
is independent of the choice of the reduced expressions.

Let
J\subset I
(not necessarily of finite type). We denote by
P^{+}_{J}
the standard parabolic subgroup of
G
corresponding to
J
and
P^{-}_{J}
the opposite parabolic subgroup of
G
. Let
L_{J}=P^{+}_{J}\cap P^{-}_{J}
be the standard Levi subgroup. Let
W_{J}
be the subgroup of
W
generated by
\{s_{j}\}_{j\in J}
. Then
W_{J}
is the Wey group of
L_{J}
. For any parabolic subgroup
P
, we denote by
U_{P}
its unipotent radical. We have the Levi decomposition
P^{\pm}_{J}=L_{J}\ltimes U_{P^{\pm}_{J}}
.

2.2.
Flag varieties

We denote by
\mathcal{B}=G/B^{+}
the (thin) full flag variety
[
19
]
, equipped with the ind-variety structure. Let
v,w\in W
. Let
\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{w}=B^{+}\dot{w}B^{+}/B^{+}
be the
Schubert cell
corresponding to
w
,
\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{v}=B^{-}\dot{v}B^{+}/B^{+}
be the
opposite Schubert cell
corresponding to
v
and
\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v,w}=\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{w}\cap\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{v}
be the
open Richardson variety
corresponding to the pair
(v,w)
. It follows from
[
20
]
that

(a)
\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v,w}\neq\emptyset
if and only if
v\leqslant w
. In this case,
\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v,w}
is irreducible of dimension
\ell(w)-\ell(v)
.

We have the decompositions
\mathcal{B}=\sqcup_{w\in W}\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{w}=\sqcup_{v\in W}\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{v}=\sqcup_{v\leqslant w}\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v,w}
. Let
\mathcal{B}_{w},\mathcal{B}^{v},\mathcal{B}_{v,w}
be the (Zariski) closure of
\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{w},\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{v},\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v,w}
respectively. By
[
19
, Proposition 7.1.15 & 7.1.21]
, we have

\mathcal{B}_{w}=\bigsqcup_{w^{\prime}\leqslant w}\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{w^{\prime}},\qquad\mathcal{B}^{v}=\bigsqcup_{v^{\prime}\geqslant v}\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{v^{\prime}},\qquad\mathcal{B}_{v,w}=\bigsqcup_{v\leqslant v^{\prime}\leqslant w^{\prime}\leqslant w}\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v^{\prime},w^{\prime}}.

2.3.
Expressions and subexpressions

An
expression
is a sequence

\mathbf{w}=(t_{1},t_{2},\ldots,t_{n})

in
I\sqcup\{1\}
. In this case, we write
w=t_{1}t_{2}\cdots t_{n}\in W
, and we say that
\mathbf{w}
is an expression of
w
. The expression
\mathbf{w}
is called
reduced
if
n=\ell(w)
. In this case,
t_{i}\in I
for all
i
.

A subexpression of
\mathbf{w}
is a sequence
\mathbf{w}^{\prime}=(t^{\prime}_{1},t^{\prime}_{2},\ldots,t^{\prime}_{n})
, where
t^{\prime}_{i}\in\{t_{i},1\}
for all
i
. If
\mathbf{w}
is an reduced expression, then the subexpression
\mathbf{w}^{\prime}
is called
positive
if
t^{\prime}_{1}\cdots t^{\prime}_{i-1}<t^{\prime}_{1}\cdots t^{\prime}_{i-1}t_{i}
for all
i
.

By
[
17
, Lemma 3.5]
, for any
w,w^{\prime}\in W
with
w^{\prime}\leqslant w
and for any reduced expression
\mathbf{w}
of
w
, there exists a unique positive subexpression for
w^{\prime}
in
\mathbf{w}
. We denote it by
\mathbf{w}^{\prime}_{+}
.

2.4.
Totally nonnegative flag varieties

We follow
[
22
]
and
[
24
]
. Let
U^{+}_{\geqslant 0}
be the submonoid of
G
generated by
x_{i}(a)
for
i\in I
and
a\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and
U^{-}_{\geqslant 0}
be the submonoid of
G
generated by
y_{i}(a)
for
i\in I
and
a\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
. Let
T_{>0}
be the identity component of
T(\mathbb{R})
. The totally nonnegative monoid
G_{\geqslant 0}
is defined to be the submonoid of
G
generated by
U^{\pm}_{\geqslant 0}
and
T_{>0}
. By
[
24
, §2.5]
,
G_{\geqslant 0}=U^{+}_{\geqslant 0}T_{>0}U^{-}_{\geqslant 0}=U^{-}_{\geqslant 0}T_{>0}U^{+}_{\geqslant 0}
.

Let
\mathcal{B}_{\geqslant 0}=\overline{U^{-}_{\geqslant 0}B^{+}/B^{+}}
be the closure of
U^{-}_{\geqslant 0}B^{+}/B^{+}
in
\mathcal{B}
with respect to the Hausdorff topology. For any
v\leqslant w
, let
\mathcal{B}_{v,w,>0}=\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v,w}\bigcap\mathcal{B}_{\geqslant 0}
. Then
\mathcal{B}_{\geqslant 0}=\bigsqcup_{v\leqslant w}\mathcal{B}_{v,w,>0}
.

Following
[
17
, Definition 5.1]
, we set

G_{\bf v_{+},\bf w,>0}=\{g_{1}g_{2}\cdots g_{n}|g_{j}=\dot{s}_{i_{j}},\text{ if }t_{i_{j}}=s_{i_{j}};\text{ and }g_{j}\in y_{i_{j}}(\mathbb{R}_{>0}),\text{ if }t_{i_{j}}=1\}.

Note that the obvious map
\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\ell(w)-\ell(v)}\rightarrow G_{\bf v_{+},\bf w,>0}

is a homeomorphism.

By
[
17
, Theorem 11.3]
for the reductive groups and
[
3
, Theorem 4.10]
for the Kac-Moody groups, we have the following result.

(a)
Let
v\leqslant w
. For any reduced expression
{\bf w}
of
w
, the map
gg\cdot B^{+}
gives a homeomorphism
G_{\bf v_{+},\bf w,>0}\cong\mathcal{B}_{v,w,>0}
.
In particular,
\mathcal{B}_{v,w,>0}\cong\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\ell(w)-\ell(v)}
is a topological cell.

Proposition 2.1
.

[
4
, Proposition 5.2]

Let
v\leqslant w
. Then

(1)
\mathcal{B}_{v,w,>0}
is a connected component of
\mathcal{B}_{v,w}(\mathbb{R})
.

(2) The Hausdorff closure of
\mathcal{B}_{v,w,>0}
equals
\sqcup_{v\leqslant v^{\prime}\leqslant w^{\prime}\leqslant w}\mathcal{B}_{v^{\prime},w^{\prime},>0}
.

For reductive groups, the above results were established by Rietsch in
[
26
,
27
]
.

2.5.
Twisted product of flag varieties

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Recall the twisted product
\mathcal{Z}_{n}
of the flag varieties:

\mathcal{Z}_{n}=G\times^{B^{+}}G\times^{B^{+}}\cdots\times^{B^{+}}G/B^{+}\quad(n\text{ factors}).

We often write
\mathcal{Z}=\mathcal{Z}_{n}
whenever the number of factors is clear. The isomorphism
G^{n}\rightarrow G^{n}
,
(g_{1},g_{2},\dots,g_{n})(g_{1},g_{1}g_{2},\dots,g_{1}g_{2}\cdots g_{n})
induces an isomorphism

\alpha:\mathcal{Z}_{n}\mathcal{B}^{n},\qquad(g_{1},g_{2},\ldots,g_{n})(g_{1}B^{+},g_{1}g_{2}B^{+},\ldots,g_{1}g_{2}\cdots g_{n}B^{+}).

In particular, we have the convolution product

m:\mathcal{Z}_{n}\mathcal{Z}_{1}=G/B^{+},\qquad(g_{1},\ldots,g_{n})g_{1}g_{2}\cdots g_{n}B^{+}.

The stratification on
\mathcal{Z}_{n}
we are interested in is different from the stratification on
\mathcal{B}^{n}
given by the product of
\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{-,-}
. Let
\underline{w}=(w_{1},\cdots,w_{n})
be a sequence of elements in
W
. Recall that the Schubert cell in
\mathcal{Z}_{n}
associated with
\underline{w}
is defined to be

\mathring{\mathcal{Z}}_{n,\underline{w}}=B^{+}\dot{w}_{1}B^{+}\times^{B^{+}}B^{+}\dot{w}_{2}B^{+}\times^{B^{+}}\cdots\times^{B^{+}}B^{+}\dot{w}_{n}B^{+}/B^{+}.

Also, recall that the opposite Schubert cell in
\mathcal{Z}_{n}
associated with
v\in W
is defined to be

\mathring{\mathcal{Z}}^{v}_{n}=\{x\in\mathcal{Z}_{n};m(x)\in\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{v}\}.

The open Richardson variety of
\mathcal{Z}_{n}
is defined by
\mathring{\mathcal{Z}}_{n,v,\underline{w}}=\mathring{\mathcal{Z}}_{n,\underline{w}}\cap\mathring{\mathcal{Z}}^{v}_{n}
.

2.6.
Monoid structure on
W

We follow
[
14
, §1]
. For any
x,y\in W
, the subset
\{xy^{\prime};y^{\prime}\leqslant y\}
(resp.
\{x^{\prime}y;x^{\prime}\leqslant x\}
,
\{x^{\prime}y^{\prime};x^{\prime}\leqslant x,y^{\prime}\leqslant y\}
) of
W
contains a unique maximal element (with respect to the Bruhat order
\leqslant
). Moreover, we have

\max\{xy^{\prime};y^{\prime}\leqslant y\}=\max\{x^{\prime}y;x^{\prime}\leqslant x\}=\max\{x^{\prime}y^{\prime};x^{\prime}\leqslant x,y^{\prime}\leqslant y\}.

We denote this element by
x\ast y
and call it the
Demazure product
of
x
and
y
. Moreover,
(W,\ast)
is a monoid and the Demazure product can be determined by the following two rules

•

4.1.
Some properties on posets

Let
(P,\leqslant)
be a poset. For any
x,y\in P
with
x\leqslant y
, the interval between
x
and
y
is defined as
[x,y]=\{z\in P\mid x\leqslant z\leqslant y\}
. A subset
C\subset P
is called

if for any
x,y\in C
, we have
[x,y]\subset C
.

The covering relation is denoted by
\gtrdot
. For any
x,y\in P
with
x\leqslant y
, a maximal chain from
x
to
y
is a finite sequence of elements
y=w_{0}\gtrdot w_{1}\gtrdot\cdots\gtrdot w_{n}=x
. In general, the maximal chain may not exist.

The
P
is called

if for any
x,y\in P
with
x\leqslant y
, the maximal chains from
x
to
y
always exist and have the same length. A pure poset
P
is called

if every interval of length
2
has exactly
4
elements, i.e., it has exactly two elements between
x
and
y
.

The order complex
\Delta_{ord}(P)
of a finite poset

is a simplicial complex whose vertices are the elements of
P
and whose simplices are the chains
x_{0}<x_{1}<\cdots<x_{k}
in
P
. A finite pure poset is called

if its order complex

is shellable, that is, its maximal faces can be ordered as

so that

is a nonempty union of the maximal proper faces of

for

.

4.2.
Regular CW complexes

Let
X
be a Hausdorff space. We call a finite disjoint union

a

if it satisfies the following two properties.

(1)

For each
\alpha\in P
, there exists a homeomorphism from a closed ball to
\overline{X}_{\alpha}
mapping the interior of the ball to
X_{\alpha}
.

(2)

For each
\alpha
, there exists

, such that
\overline{X}_{\alpha}=\bigsqcup_{\beta\in P^{\prime}}X_{\beta}
.

We then have a natural poset
({P},\leqslant)
, where

if and only if

. The face poset of

is defined to be the augmented poset
\hat{P}=P\sqcup\{\hat{0}\}
, where
\hat{0}
is the added unique minimal element of
\hat{P}
.

One of the motivations for the combinatorial properties in §
4.1
comes from the following result in
[
2
, Proposition 2.2]
.

(a)
Let
\hat{0}\neq x\in\hat{P}
. If the closed interval
[\hat{0},x]
is pure, thin, and shellable, then it is the face poset of some regular CW complex which is homeomorphic to a closed ball.

We also recall the following result from
[
2
]
that will be used in Proposition

.

(b)
Suppose that

is a regular CW complex with (finite) face poset

. If

(adjoining a maximum

.

5.1.
Braid varieties

Let
\underline{s}=(s_{i_{1}},\dots,s_{i_{n}})\in W^{n}
be such that all
s_{i_{j}}
are simple reflections. Let
w=m_{\ast}(\underline{s})
. We define the open braid variety associated to
\underline{s}
as

\{(g_{1},g_{2},\dots,g_{n})\in B^{+}\dot{s}_{i_{1}}B^{+}\times^{B^{+}}\cdots\times^{B^{+}}B^{+}\dot{s}_{i_{n}}B^{+}/B^{+}|g_{1}g_{2}\cdots g_{n}/B^{+}=\dot{w}B^{+}/B^{+}\}.

Note that
B^{+}\dot{s}_{i_{1}}B^{+}\cdots B^{+}\dot{s}_{i_{n}}B^{+}/B^{+}\subset\overline{B^{+}\dot{w}B^{+}/B^{+}}
and
\overline{B^{+}\dot{w}B^{+}/B^{+}}\cap B^{-}\dot{w}B^{+}/B^{+}=\dot{w}B^{+}/B^{+}
. So the open braid variety is exactly
\mathring{\mathcal{Z}}_{n,w,\underline{s}}
. Hence the closure is
{\mathcal{Z}}_{n,w,\underline{s}}
.

We have the following regularity result for the braid varieties.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\underline{s}=(s_{i_{1}},\dots,s_{i_{n}})
. The totally nonnegative braid variety
\mathcal{Z}_{n,m_{\ast}(\underline{s}),\underline{s},\geqslant 0}
is a regular CW complex homeomorphic to a closed ball.

Remark 5.2
.

This theorem provides a geometric realization of the subword complex
[
21
, Corollary 3.8]
.

On the other hand, the cluster algebra structure on the coordinate ring of the open braid variety
\mathring{\mathcal{Z}}_{w,\underline{s}}
has recently been established in
[
6
,
12
]
for a reductive group
G
. It is interesting to compare the total positivity structure with the cluster algebra structure.

Proof.

Let
x=(m_{\ast}(\underline{s}),\underline{s})\in Q_{n}
. The poset
[\hat{0},x]
is the dual face poset of the subword complex considered in
[
21
, §2]
. Since a poset is shellable if and only if the dual poset is shellable,
[\hat{0},x]
is shellable by
[
21
, Theorem 2.5]
. Now the theorem follows from Proposition
4.3
.
∎

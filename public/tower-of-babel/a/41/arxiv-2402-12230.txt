Half Space Property in 𝖱𝖢𝖣 ⁡ ( 0 , N ) \mathsf{RCD}(0,N) spaces
Alessandro Cucinotta, Andrea Mondino

Abstract
The goal of this note is to prove the Half Space Property for 𝖱𝖢𝖣 ⁡ ( 0 , N ) \mathsf{RCD}(0,N) spaces, namely that if ( 𝖷 , 𝖽 , 𝔪 ) (\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m}) is a parabolic 𝖱𝖢𝖣 ⁡ ( 0 , N ) \mathsf{RCD}(0,N) space and C ⊂ 𝖷 × ℝ C\subset\mathsf{X}\times\mathbb{R} is locally the boundary of a locally perimeter minimizing set and it is contained in a half space, then C C is a locally finite union of horizontal slices. If the assumption of local perimeter minimizing is strengthened into global perimeter minimizing, then the conclusion can be strengthened into uniqueness of the horizontal slice. As a consequence, we obtain oscillation estimates and a Half Space Theorem for minimal hypersurfaces in products 𝖬 × ℝ \mathsf{M}\times\mathbb{R} , where 𝖬 \mathsf{M} is a parabolic smooth manifold (possibly weighted and with boundary) with non-negative Ricci curvature. On the way of proving the Half Space Property we also study some properties of Green’s functions on 𝖱𝖢𝖣 ⁡ ( K , N ) \mathsf{RCD}(K,N) spaces that are of independent interest.



1
Introduction

In
[
46
]
Hoffman and Meeks proved the Half Space Theorem, stating that a connected, proper, non-planar minimal surface in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
cannot be contained in a half space. Analogous results in the Riemannian setting were then obtained by different authors (
[
44
]
,
[
25
]
,
[
24
]
,
[
28
]
, etc.) and in
[
65
]
Rosenberg, Schulze and Spruck gave the following definition.

Definition
.

A Riemannian manifold
\mathsf{M}
has the Half Space Property if every proper
minimal hypersurface
S\subset\mathsf{M}\times\mathbb{R}
not intersecting
\mathsf{M}\times\{0\}
equals a horizontal slice
\mathsf{M}\times\{c\}
.

The study of this property, which generalizes the Half Space Theorem, is linked to the understanding of the following more general problem: what are the conditions such that two minimal submanifolds
S_{1}
,
S_{2}
of a Riemannian manifold
\mathsf{M}
must intersect? And if they do not intersect, do they influence each other’s geometry?

Classical results in this direction, aside from the Half Space Theorem, are the fact that the only positive solutions of the minimal surface equation in Euclidean space are constant functions (
[
14
]
) and Frankel’s Theorem, stating that two compact minimal hypersurfaces in a closed manifold with positive Ricci curvature must intersect (
[
30
]
).

More recently, in the aforementioned work
[
65
]
, it was proved that the Half Space Property holds for parabolic manifolds with bounded sectional curvature and the result was then extended to parabolic manifolds with a uniform lower bound on the Ricci curvature in
[
23
]
. It was then proved in the very recent work
[
26
]
that even the lower bound on the Ricci curvature can be removed, so that the Half Space Property holds for general parabolic manifolds.
We recall that a manifold is parabolic if it does not admit a positive Green’s function for the Laplacian and we note that without the parabolicity assumption the Half Space Property fails in general, the catenoid in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
being a counterexample.

The validity of the Half Space Property for parabolic smooth Riemannian manifolds with lower Ricci curvature bounds and the recent generalization of Frankel’s Theorem to the setting of
\mathsf{RCD}
spaces (
[
61
]
), i.e. non-smooth spaces with a synthetic lower bound on the Ricci curvature, suggest that also the Half Space Property might hold in the non-smooth setting.

We recall that an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space is a metric measure space where
K\in\mathbb{R}
plays the role of a lower bound on the Ricci curvature, while
N\in[1,+\infty)
plays the role of an upper bound on the dimension; this class includes measured Gromov-Hausdorff limits of smooth manifolds with uniform Ricci curvature lower bounds and finite dimensional Alexandrov spaces with sectional curvature bounded from below.
Moreover, since a hypersurface in a smooth manifold is minimal if and only if it is locally the boundary of a locally perimeter minimizing set, it is natural, in the non-smooth setting, to replace minimal hypersurfaces with local boundaries of local perimeter minimizers.
Recall that a set
C\subset\mathsf{X}
is locally the boundary of a locally perimeter minimizing set if, for every
x\in\mathsf{X}
there exists a (small) metric ball
B_{r}(x)
and a set of finite perimeter
E\subset\mathsf{X}
minimizing the perimeter in
B_{r}(x)
such that
C\cap B_{r}(x)=\partial E\cap B_{r}(x)
.

We next state the main result of this note.

Theorem 1
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be a parabolic
\mathsf{RCD}(0,N)
space and let
C\subset\mathsf{X}\times\mathbb{R}
.
Then the following two assertions are equivalent:

1.

C
is locally the boundary of a locally perimeter minimizing set, and
C
is contained in a half space, i.e. there exists
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
C\subset\mathsf{X}\times[t_{0},\infty)
;

2.

C
is a locally finite union of horizontal slices, i.e. there exists a discrete set
D\subset[t_{0},\infty)
such that

C=\bigcup_{d\in D}\mathsf{X}\times\{d\}.

The previous theorem, in particular, is part of a wider class of recent results that aim at generalizing to the non-smooth setting properties of perimeter minimizing sets (
[
11
]
,
[
29
]
,
[
16
]
,
[
61
]
, etc.). It is interesting to note that, while in the smooth category the Half Space Property holds for parabolic manifolds even without the lower bound on the Ricci curvature, the same is not true for metric measure spaces (see Example
4.30
).
In particular, in the non-smooth setting, once we remove the
\mathsf{RCD}
assumption, it is not clear which conditions should be imposed (in addition to parabolicity) to guarantee the validity of the Half Space Property.

If the assumption of
local
perimeter minimizing in Theorem
1
is strengthened into
global
perimeter minimizing, then the conclusion can be strengthened into
uniqueness
of the horizontal slice:

Corollary

(Cor.
4.29
)
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be a parabolic
\mathsf{RCD}(0,N)
space and let
E\subset\mathsf{X}\times\mathbb{R}
be a perimeter minimizing set contained in
\mathsf{X}\times[0,+\infty)
. Then, there exists
a\in\mathbb{R}
such that
E=\mathsf{X}\times[a,+\infty)
.

Specializing Theorem
1
to the smooth category, we obtain that the Half Space Property holds for certain free boundary minimal surfaces on weighted manifolds with boundary. This is the content of Theorem
2
.
Given a manifold
(\mathsf{M},g)
we denote by
\mathfrak{m}_{g}
its volume measure and by
\mathsf{d}_{g}
its distance. If
V:\mathsf{M}\to\mathbb{R}
is a smooth function, we say that the metric measure space
(\mathsf{M}^{n},\mathsf{d}_{g},e^{-V}\mathfrak{m}_{g})
is a weighted manifold. A hypersurface
S
in the weighted manifold
\mathsf{M}
is minimal if it is a critical point of the weighted area functional. We say that the boundary of
\mathsf{M}
is convex if its second fundamental form w.r.t. the inward pointing unit normal is positive.

Theorem 2
.

Let
(\mathsf{M}^{n},\mathsf{d}_{g},e^{-V}\mathfrak{m}_{g})
be a parabolic weighted manifold with convex boundary such that there exists
N>n
satisfying

\mathsf{Ric}_{\mathsf{M}}+\mathsf{Hess}_{V}-\frac{\nabla V\otimes\nabla V}{N-n}\geq 0\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ on }\mathsf{M}\setminus\partial\mathsf{M}.

If
S\subset\mathsf{M}\times(0,+\infty)
is a properly embedded connected minimal hypersurface intersecting
\partial\mathsf{M}\times\mathbb{R}
orthogonally, then
S
is a horizontal slice.

The previous result is new already in the boundaryless weighted setting and in the framework of unweighted manifolds with boundary.

A second consequence of Theorem
1
is that the oscillation of area minimizing hypersurfaces in an appropriate class of pointed manifolds grows with a uniform rate as one moves away from the base point in each manifold.
This is stated precisely in Theorem
3
below.

We say that a function
P:[0,+\infty)\to(0,+\infty)
is a modulus of parabolicity if
\int_{1}^{+\infty}tP(t)^{-1}dt=+\infty
and we say that a pointed metric measure space
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m},\mathsf{x})
has modulus of parabolicity
P
if
\mathfrak{m}(B_{r}(\mathsf{x}))\leq\mathfrak{m}(B_{1}(\mathsf{x}))P(r)
for every
r\geq 0
. We say that a hypersurface
S\subset\mathsf{X}
is an area minimizing boundary in an open set
A\subset\mathsf{X}
if the exists a set
E\subset A
minimizing the perimeter in
A
such that
\partial E\cap A=S
.
If
S\subset\mathsf{X}\times\mathbb{R}
and
(\mathsf{x},0)\in S
we define the oscillation of
S
as

\mathsf{Osc}_{\mathsf{x},r}(S):=\sup\{|t|:(y,t)\in S\cap B_{r}(\mathsf{x})\times(-r,r)\}.

Theorem 3
.

Let
P
be a modulus of parabolicity and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. For every
t,r,T>0
there exists
R>0
such that for every pointed manifold
(\mathsf{M}^{n},g,\mathsf{x})
with modulus of parabolicity
P
and non-negative Ricci curvature,
and for every area minimizing boundary
S
in
B_{R}(\mathsf{x})\times(-R,R)
containing
(\mathsf{x},0)
, if
\mathsf{Osc}_{\mathsf{x},r}(S)\geq t
then
\mathsf{Osc}_{\mathsf{x},R}(S)\geq T
.

Remark
.

In Section
5
we actually prove a more general result dealing with weighted manifolds with boundary and with a stronger notion of oscillation (see Corollary
5.9
), but we preferred to state Theorem
3
in this form for the sake of simplicity.

Now we comment on the proof of Theorem
1
.

The proof of the implication
2\Rightarrow 1
is a standard calibration argument, we will thus only discuss the implication
1\Rightarrow 2
.

The proof that we give is totally different from the ones covering the analogous results in the smooth setting.
In the works
[
65
]
and
[
23
]
the general principle is that if
S\subset\mathsf{M}\times\mathbb{R}
is minimal and contained in a half space then, given a compact set
D\neq\emptyset
, it will be possible to construct a solution to the minimal surface equation
u:\mathsf{M}\setminus D\to\mathbb{R}
which is bounded from below and is not constant if
S
is not a slice. The next step is then to prove a suitable generalization of De Giorgi’s a priori gradient estimate for solutions of the minimal surface equation and to use the lower bound on
u
to obtain a uniform bound on its gradient.
Finally one combines the parabolicity of
\mathsf{M}
with the bound on the gradient of
u
to deduce that also the graph of
u
is parabolic, and since
u
is harmonic on its graph, by general properties of parabolic manifolds, it has to be constant and
S
has to be a slice.

There are several points of this strategy that it is not clear how to adapt to the non-smooth framework.
A key issue is that the a priori gradient estimate on
u
under a lower Ricci curvature bound is a consequence of the second variation formula for minimal surfaces, and such formula relies crucially on the smoothness of the ambient space.
Moreover, even if such estimate were available, the graph of a regular function on an
\mathsf{RCD}
space is not known, in general, to be an
\mathsf{RCD}
space and so, losing even this mild regularity, it would be difficult to adapt the previous technique, as it is based on the parabolicity of the graph.

Our proof is instead closer to the one used in
[
61
]
to generalize Frankel’s Theorem. The key idea taken from such proof is that if
M
is a manifold with non-negative Ricci curvature and
S
is a minimal hypersurface contained in
\mathsf{M}\times(0,+\infty)
, then the function
\bar{\mathsf{d}}:=\mathsf{d}_{S}-\mathsf{d}_{\mathsf{M}\times\{0\}}
, defined on
\mathsf{M}\times(-\infty,0)
, has negative Laplacian, is positive and if it is constant then
S
is a slice.
Hence the goal is to use the maximum principle for super-harmonic functions to show that
\bar{\mathsf{d}}
is constant. This is immediate if
\mathsf{M}
is compact, as in this case
\bar{\mathsf{d}}
achieves its minimum on all the points of the geodesic realizing the distance between
\mathsf{M}\times\{-1\}
and
S
.

If
\mathsf{M}
is not compact, the argument is by contradiction. Exploiting the geometric properties of distance functions and the super-harmonicity of
\bar{\mathsf{d}}
, one shows that if
\bar{\mathsf{d}}
is not constant, then there exists an open bounded set
A\subset\mathsf{M}\times(-\infty,0)
and
\tau>0
such that

\Delta e^{-\bar{\mathsf{d}}}\geq 0\text{ on }\mathsf{M}\times(-\infty,0),\hskip 10.00002pt\Delta e^{-\bar{\mathsf{d}}}\geq\tau\text{ on }A.

If the product
\mathsf{M}\times\mathbb{R}
is parabolic, then there exists a continuous function
\phi:\mathsf{M}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\Delta\phi\geq 0\text{ on }\mathsf{M}\times(-\infty,0),\hskip 10.00002pt\Delta\phi\geq-\tau\text{ on }A

which tends to
-\infty
at infinity (as it happens on
\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{R}^{2}
because of their parabolicity). As a consequence, the function
e^{-\bar{\mathsf{d}}}+\phi
is sub-harmonic and has a maximum in
\mathsf{M}\times(-\infty,0]
. Moreover, since
e^{-\bar{\mathsf{d}}}
increases as we move down a vertical line, by constructing
\phi
in an appropriate way we can assume that the maximum of
e^{-\bar{\mathsf{d}}}+\phi
actually lies in
\mathsf{M}\times(-\infty,0)
. The maximum principle then implies that
e^{-\bar{\mathsf{d}}}+\phi
is constant, contradicting the condition at infinity on
\phi
.

If
\mathsf{M}\times\mathbb{R}
is not parabolic the previous argument has to be modified, and working with metric measure spaces instead of Riemannian manifolds becomes crucial. Consider on
\mathsf{M}\times\mathbb{R}
the distance
\mathsf{d}_{\times}
induced by the product Riemannian metric and the measure
\mathfrak{m}_{\times}
which is the product volume measure.
Using the parabolicity assumption on
\mathsf{M}
and the particular form of the function
e^{-\bar{\mathsf{d}}}
, it is possible to replace the measure
\mathfrak{m}_{\times}
with a measure
\tilde{\mathfrak{m}}_{\times}
such that the product space
(\mathsf{M}\times\mathbb{R},\mathsf{d}_{\times},\tilde{\mathfrak{m}}_{\times})
is an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space (possibly for
K<0
), it is parabolic and, calling
\tilde{\Delta}
the Laplacian in this modified space, we have that there exists
\tau^{\prime}>0
such that

\tilde{\Delta}e^{-\bar{\mathsf{d}}}\geq 0\text{ on }\mathsf{M}\times(-\infty,0),\hskip 10.00002pt\tilde{\Delta}e^{-\bar{\mathsf{d}}}\geq\tau^{\prime}\text{ on }A.

To conclude, we can now repeat the previous argument involving the construction of an auxiliary function
\phi
and the maximum principle that, in this case, is used on the modified space
(\mathsf{M}\times\mathbb{R},\mathsf{d}_{\times},\tilde{\mathfrak{m}}_{\times})
.

This strategy works for
\mathsf{RCD}(0,N)
spaces, but not on
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
spaces when
K<0
, as in this case it is not true in general that the function
\bar{\mathsf{d}}
is super-harmonic. In particular the problem remains open for general
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
spaces.

Finally, to implement the previously outlined strategy, we need to generalize to
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
spaces some classical properties of parabolic manifolds. This is obtained by extending the work done in
[
18
]
,
[
61
]
and
[
17
]
on Green’s functions in the non smooth setting. Theorems
4
and
5
are the main results in this regard. For other related results about Green’s functions in
\mathsf{RCD}
spaces, see the recent works by Honda-Peng
[
48
]
and Honda
[
47
]
.
Given an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
we denote by
p_{t}
the corresponding heat kernel and we refer to Section
3
for the precise definitions of Green’s functions and regular sets.

Theorem 4
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m},\mathsf{x})
be a pointed
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space with infinite diameter. The following are equivalent:

1.

There is no positive Green’s function on
\mathsf{X}
with pole
\mathsf{x}
.

2.

For every
(x,y)\in X\times X
we have
\int_{1}^{+\infty}p_{t}(x,y)\,dt=+\infty
.

3.

There exists
(x,y)\in X\times X
such that
\int_{1}^{+\infty}p_{t}(x,y)\,dt=+\infty
.

4.

Let
\{B_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be an exhaustion of regular sets containing
\mathsf{x}
. Let
G_{\mathsf{x}}^{i}
be the Green’s function on
B_{i}
with pole
\mathsf{x}
. Then for every
y\neq\mathsf{x}
we have
G_{\mathsf{x}}^{i}(y)\to+\infty
as
i\to+\infty
.

Theorem 5
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space. If
there exists
x\in X
such that

\int_{1}^{+\infty}\frac{t}{\mathfrak{m}(B_{t}(x))}\,dt=+\infty,

then
\mathsf{X}
is parabolic. This condition is also necessary if
K=0
.

The note is organized as follows.
The first section contains preliminaries.
The second section concerns the theory of Green’s functions on
\mathsf{RCD}
spaces and contains the proofs of Theorems
4
and
5
.
In Section
4
we rigorously prove, following the previously outlined strategy, the Half Space Property for
\mathsf{RCD}(0,N)
spaces, while in the final section we deal with applications in the smooth setting, proving Theorems
2
and
3
.

2
Preliminaries

Throughout the note we will work on metric measure spaces
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
, where
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d})
is a separable complete metric space where balls are precompact and
\mathfrak{m}
is a non-negative Borel measure on
\mathsf{X}
which is finite on bounded sets and whose support is the whole
\mathsf{X}
.
Given a smooth manifold
(\mathsf{M},g)
we denote by
\mathsf{d}_{g}
and
\mathfrak{m}_{g}
respectively the distance induced by
g
and the volume measure.
Given an open set
\Omega\subset\mathsf{X}
we denote by
\mathsf{Lip}(\Omega)
,
\mathsf{Lip}_{loc}(\Omega)
and
\mathsf{Lip}_{c}(\Omega)
respectively Lipschitz functions, locally Lipschitz and Lipschitz functions with compact support in
\Omega
. If
f\in\mathsf{Lip}_{loc}(\Omega)
and
x\in\Omega
we define

\mathsf{lip}(f)(x):=\limsup_{y\to x}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{\mathsf{d}(x,y)}.

Given a closed interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
, we say that a curve
\gamma:I\to\mathsf{X}
is a geodesic if its length coincides with the distance between its endpoints. Unless otherwise specified we assume that geodesics have constant unit speed.

We will consider pointed measured Gromov Hausdorff convergence of metric measure spaces, and we refer to
[
34
]
for the relevant background.
We recall that in the case of a sequence of uniformly locally doubling metric measure spaces
(\mathsf{X}_{i},\mathsf{d}_{i},\mathfrak{m}_{i},\mathsf{x}_{i})

(as in the case of
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
spaces), pointed measured Gromov-Hausdorff convergence to
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m},\mathsf{x})
can be equivalently characterized by asking for the existence of a proper
metric space
(\mathsf{Z},\mathsf{d}_{z})
such that all the metric spaces
(\mathsf{X}_{i},\mathsf{d}_{i})
are isometrically embedded
into
(\mathsf{Z},\mathsf{d}_{z})
,
\mathsf{x}_{i}\to\mathsf{x}
and
\mathfrak{m}_{i}\to\mathfrak{m}
weakly in duality with continuous boundedly supported functions in
\mathsf{Z}
. In this case we say that the convergence is realized in the space
\mathsf{Z}
(see
[
34
]
).
Given a metric measure space
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
and
x\in\mathsf{X}
, we denote by
\mathsf{Tan}_{x}(\mathsf{X})
the (possibly empty) collection of (isometry classes of) metric measure spaces that are pointed measured Gromov Hausdorff limits as
r\downarrow 0
of the family
(\mathsf{X},r^{-1}\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m}(B_{r}(x))^{-1}\mathfrak{m},x)
.

2.1
Sobolev spaces and the
\mathsf{RCD}
condition

We now recall the definition of Sobolev spaces in the setting of metric measure spaces, the main references being
[
21
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
7
]
and
[
33
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be a metric measure space,
\Omega\subset\mathsf{X}
an open set and let
p>1
. A function
f\in\mathsf{L}^{p}(\Omega)
is said to be in the Sobolev space
\mathsf{W}^{1,p}(\Omega)
if there exists a sequence of locally Lipschitz functions
\{f_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathsf{Lip}_{loc}(\Omega)
converging to
f
in
\mathsf{L}^{p}(\Omega)
such that

\limsup_{i\to+\infty}\int_{\Omega}\mathsf{lip}(f_{i})^{p}\,d\mathfrak{m}<+\infty.

A function
f\in\mathsf{L}^{p}_{loc}(\Omega)
is said to be in the Sobolev space
\mathsf{W}^{1,p}_{loc}(\Omega)
if for every
\eta\in\mathsf{Lip}_{c}(\Omega)
we have
f\eta\in\mathsf{W}^{1,p}(\Omega)
.

For any
f\in\mathsf{W}^{1,p}(\Omega)
one can define an object
|\nabla f|
(a priori depending on
p
, but independent of the exponent in the spaces that we will work on) such that for every open set
A\subset\Omega
we have

\int_{A}|\nabla f|^{p}\,d\mathfrak{m}=\inf\Big\{\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\int_{A}\mathsf{lip}(f_{n})^{p}\,d\mathfrak{m}\Big|(f_{n})_{n}\subset\mathsf{L}^{p}(A)\cap\mathsf{Lip}_{loc}(A),\|f_{n}-f\|_{\mathsf{L}^{p}(A)}\to 0\Big\}.

The quantity in the previous expression will be called
p
-Cheeger energy and denoted by
\mathsf{Ch}_{p}(f)
while
|\nabla f|
will be called relaxed gradient. We define
\|f\|_{\mathsf{W}^{1,p}(\Omega)}:=\|f\|_{\mathsf{L}^{p}(\Omega)}+\mathsf{Ch}_{p}(f)
. One can check that with this norm the space
\mathsf{W}^{1,p}(\Omega)
is Banach. Later we will often write
\mathsf{Ch}
in place of
\mathsf{Ch}_{2}
for simplicity of notation.
A deep result of Cheeger
[
21
]
implies that if
f\in\mathsf{Lip}_{loc}(X)
then
|\nabla f|=\mathsf{lip}(f),\,\mathfrak{m}
-a.e., provided
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
is a PI-space, so in particular this holds for
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
-spaces,
N\in[1,\infty)
.
We now introduce functions of bounded variation following
[
59
]
(see also
[
3
]
).

Definition 2.2
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be a metric measure space and let
\Omega\subset\mathsf{X}
be an open set. A function
f\in\mathsf{L}^{1}(\Omega)
is said to be of bounded variation if there exists a sequence of locally Lipschitz functions
\{f_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathsf{Lip}_{loc}(\Omega)
converging to
f
in
\mathsf{L}^{1}(\Omega)
such that

\limsup_{i\to+\infty}\int_{\Omega}\mathsf{lip}(f_{i})\,d\mathfrak{m}<+\infty.

The space of such functions is denoted by
\mathsf{BV}(\Omega)
. A function
f\in\mathsf{L}^{1}_{loc}(\Omega)
is said to be in
\mathsf{BV}_{loc}(\Omega)
if for every
\eta\in\mathsf{Lip}_{c}(\Omega)
we have
f\eta\in\mathsf{BV}(\Omega)
.

For any
f\in\mathsf{BV}(\Omega)
and any open set
A\subset\Omega
we define

|Df|(A)=\inf\Big\{\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\int_{A}\mathsf{lip}(f_{n})\,d\mathfrak{m}\Big|(f_{n})_{n}\subset\mathsf{L}^{1}(A)\cap\mathsf{Lip}_{loc}(A),\|f_{n}-f\|_{\mathsf{L}^{1}(A)}\to 0\Big\}.

One can check that the quantity in the previous expression is the restriction to the open subsets of
\Omega
of a finite measure. We define
\|f\|_{\mathsf{BV}(\Omega)}:=\|f\|_{\mathsf{L}^{1}(\Omega)}+|Df|(\Omega)
. One can check that with this norm the space
\mathsf{BV}(\mathsf{X})
is Banach. A function
f
belongs to
\mathsf{W}^{1,1}(\Omega)
if
f\in\mathsf{BV}(\Omega)
and
|Df|\ll\mathfrak{m}
. In this case we denote by
|\nabla f|
the density of
|Df|
with respect to
\mathfrak{m}
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be a metric measure space and let
\Omega\subset\mathsf{X}
be an open set. For every
p\geq 1
we denote by
\mathsf{W}^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
the closure in
\mathsf{W}^{1,p}(\Omega)
of
\mathsf{Lip}_{c}(\Omega)
.

The previous definition is particularly suitable for proper spaces, while other definitions are to be preferred if one works without this assumption. Since we will work in the setting of
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
spaces with
N\in[1,\infty)
(and these are proper) we do not consider more general definitions.

Definition 2.4
.

We say that a metric measure space
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
is infinitesimally Hilbertian if the space
\mathsf{W}^{1,2}(\mathsf{X})
is a Hilbert space.

If
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
is infinitesimally Hilbertian and
\Omega\subset\mathsf{X}
an open set, for every
f,g\in\mathsf{W}^{1,1}_{loc}(\Omega)
we define the measurable function
\nabla f\cdot\nabla g:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
by

\nabla f\cdot\nabla g:=\frac{|\nabla(f+g)|^{2}-|\nabla(f-g)|^{2}}{4}.

As a consequence of the infinitesimal Hilbertianity assumption, the previously defined product of gradients is bilinear in both entries.
We then recall the definition of Laplacian in the metric setting.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be infinitesimally Hilbertian and let
\Omega\subset\mathsf{X}
be an open set.
Let

. We say that

if there exists a function

such that

\int_{\Omega}gh\,d\mathfrak{m}=-\int_{\Omega}\nabla g\cdot\nabla f\,d\mathfrak{m}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for any }g\in\mathsf{W}^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega).

In this case we say that
\Delta f=h
in
\Omega
.

We also have the following more general definition.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be infinitesimally Hilbertian and let
\Omega\subset\mathsf{X}
be an open set.
Let
f\in\mathsf{W}^{1,1}_{loc}(\Omega)
and let
\mu
be a Radon measure on
\Omega

\Delta f=\mu
in
\Omega
in distributional sense if

\int_{\Omega}h\,d\mu=-\int_{\Omega}\nabla g\cdot\nabla f\,d\mathfrak{m}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for any }g\in\mathsf{Lip}_{c}(\Omega).

We now recall a Bishop-Gromov type inequality for

We define

r&K=0\\
\sqrt{\frac{N-1}{-K}}\sinh\Big(t\sqrt{\frac{-K}{N-1}}\Big)&K<0.\end{cases}

Proposition 2.7

We now recall some properties of the heat flow in the

Given an

is the
\mathsf{L}^{2}(\mathsf{X})

It turns out that one can obtain a stochastically complete heat kernel

can be then extended to

The heat flow has good approximation properties, in particular if

in

; while if

for every

.

Proposition 2.13

.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a non empty perimeter minimizing set contained in a half space. Then
E
is itself a half space.

3
Green’s functions in parabolic metric measure spaces

The goal of this section is to prove Theorems
4
and
5
. The techniques that we use generally follow the lines of the arguments in
[
18
,
61
,
17
]
, although we work in higher generality since we do not assume any non collapsing condition on the space
\mathsf{X}
. Throughout the section we assume that we are working on a fixed
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space
(X,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
.

Definition 3.1
.

The space
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
is said to be parabolic if
\int_{1}^{+\infty}p_{t}(x,y)\,dt=+\infty
for every
(x,y)\in\mathsf{X}\times\mathsf{X}
.

In the smooth setting there are many possible equivalent definitions of parabolicity. In Theorem
4
we show that also in the
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
setting the previous definition is equivalent to the more classical one involving non existence of Green’s functions. In the
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
setting it is convenient to start from Definition
3.1
because it allows to exploit the good properties of the heat flow.

Proposition 3.2
.

The space
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
is parabolic if and only if there exists
(x,y)\in\mathsf{X}\times\mathsf{X}
such that
\int_{1}^{+\infty}p_{t}(x,y)\,dt=+\infty
.

Proof.

It follows immediately by Proposition
2.13
.
∎

We now define Green’s functions on
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
spaces.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
A\subset\subset\mathsf{X}
be a regular set and let
x\in A
. We say that

G_{x}\in\mathsf{W}^{1,1}_{0}(A)\cap C(\bar{A}\setminus\{x\})

is a Green’s function on
A
with pole
x
if

\Delta G_{x}=-\delta_{x}

in distributional sense in
A
and
G_{x}=0
on
\partial A
.
Similarly we say that

G_{x}\in\mathsf{W}^{1,1}_{loc}(\mathsf{X})\cap C(\mathsf{X}\setminus\{x\})

is a Green’s function on
\mathsf{X}
with pole
x
if

\Delta G_{x}=-\delta_{x}

in distributional sense.

Note that our definition of Green’s functions on proper subsets of
\mathsf{X}
requires a pointwise boundary condition as this will be important later on in the proof of Theorem
1
. This is also the reason why we only define Green’s functions on regular sets and not on arbitrary bounded precompact sets. We will indeed show that with our definition if a bounded precompact set admits a Green’s function then it is regular and viceversa.

The next proposition shows that a regular set admits at most one Green’s function.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
A\subset\mathsf{X}
be a regular set and let
x\in A
. Then there exists at most one Green’s function with pole
x
on
A
.

Proof.

Let
f_{1},f_{2}
be two such functions. Then
f_{1}-f_{2}
is harmonic in distributional sense, so that by Proposition
2.24
it belongs to
\mathsf{W}^{1,2}_{loc}(A)
.
This implies that for every
\Omega\subset\subset A
we have
f_{1}-f_{2}\in D(\Delta,\Omega)
and
\Delta(f_{1}-f_{2})=0
. In particular, since we have that
f_{1}-f_{2}\in C(\bar{A}\setminus\{x\})
, we obtain that
f_{1}-f_{2}\in C(\bar{A})
.

Suppose now that
f_{1}-f_{2}\neq 0
, so that there exists a point where such difference is positive (or negative). Restricting to an appropriate precompact set of
A
and using the maximum principle we obtain a contradiction.
∎

Now we give a series of lemmas that are needed to prove existence of Green’s functions on regular domains, which is the content of Proposition
3.10
. The proof of the next lemma is immediate.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
x\in\mathsf{X}
and let
r>0
. If
f:\mathsf{X}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies
\mathsf{lip}(f)(y)\leq c
for every
y\in B_{r}(x)
, then
f
is Lipschitz in
B_{r/4}(x)
.

Let
T>0
and define
G^{T}_{x}:\mathsf{X}\to\mathbb{R}
by
G^{T}_{x}(y):=\int_{0}^{T}p_{t}(x,y)\,dt
.
The function
G^{T}_{x}
is summable as

\int_{\mathsf{X}}G^{T}_{x}(y)\,d\mathfrak{m}(y)=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathsf{X}}p_{t}(x,y)\,d\mathfrak{m}(y)\,dt=T.

As a consequence,
G^{T}_{x}
is real valued
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e. on
\mathsf{X}
and the definition is well posed.

Lemma 3.6
.

The function
G^{T}_{x}
is locally Lipschitz in
\mathsf{X}\setminus\{x\}
.

Proof.

Let
y\in\mathsf{X}\setminus\{x\}
. Thanks to Proposition
2.12
there exist constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that for every
t\in(0,T)
the function
p_{t}(x,\cdot)
is Lipschitz in
B_{\mathsf{d}(x,y)/8}(y)
with local Lipschitz constant bounded from above by

\frac{c_{1}}{\sqrt{t}\mathfrak{m}(B_{\sqrt{t}}(x))}\exp{\Big\{\frac{-\mathsf{d}^{2}(x,y)}{c_{2}t}\Big\}}.

As a consequence, for every
z\in B_{\mathsf{d}(x,y)/8}(y)
, using Fatou Lemma we get

\mathsf{lip}(G^{T}_{x})(z)=\limsup_{z_{1}\to z}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{|p_{t}(x,z_{1})-p_{t}(x,z)|}{\mathsf{d}(z_{1},z)}\,dt\leq\int_{0}^{T}\frac{c_{1}}{\sqrt{t}\mathfrak{m}(B_{\sqrt{t}}(x))}\exp{\Big\{\frac{-\mathsf{d}^{2}(x,y)}{c_{2}t}\Big\}}\,dt.

(1)

Moreover from Remark
2.8
we have that there exists a constant
C>0
such that
\mathfrak{m}(B_{r}(x))\geq Cr^{N}
for every
r\leq\sqrt{T}
, so that the r.h.s. of (
1
) is bounded. Hence
G^{T}_{x}
is locally Lipschitz in a neighbourhood of
y\in\mathsf{X}\setminus\{x\}
because of Lemma
3.5
. Since
y
was arbitrary
G^{T}_{x}
is locally Lipschitz in
\mathsf{X}\setminus\{x\}
.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

G^{T}_{x}\in\mathsf{W}^{1,1}_{loc}(\mathsf{X})
.

Proof.

Consider for every
\epsilon>0
the function
G^{T}_{\epsilon,x}:\mathsf{X}\to\mathbb{R}
given by

G^{T}_{\epsilon,x}(y):=\int_{\epsilon}^{T}p_{t}(x,y)\,dt.

It is easy to see that these converge in
\mathsf{L}^{1}(\mathsf{X})
to
G^{T}_{x}
and, using Proposition
2.12
and Fatou Lemma, that for every
y\in\mathsf{X}

\mathsf{lip}(G^{T}_{\epsilon,x})(y)\leq\int_{\epsilon}^{T}\frac{c_{1}}{\sqrt{t}\mathfrak{m}(B_{\sqrt{t}}(x))}\exp{\Big\{\frac{-\mathsf{d}^{2}(x,y)}{c_{2}t}\Big\}}\,dt\leq\int_{\epsilon}^{T}\frac{c_{3}}{\sqrt{t}\mathfrak{m}(B_{\sqrt{t}}(x))}\,dt<+\infty.

In particular
G^{T}_{\epsilon,x}\in\mathsf{Lip}(\mathsf{X})\subset W^{1,1}_{loc}(\mathsf{X})
.
So if we prove that for every ball
B_{r}(x)\subset\mathsf{X}
with
r\geq T+1
, the functions
G^{T}_{\epsilon}
restricted to
B_{r}(x)
are a Cauchy sequence in
\mathsf{W}^{1,1}(B_{r}(x))
, we obtain that the limit
G^{T}_{x}
is in
\mathsf{W}^{1,1}_{loc}(\mathsf{X})
as well. So we compute

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{B_{r}(x)}|\nabla(G^{T}_{\epsilon_{1},x}-G^{T}_{\epsilon_{2},x})|(y)d\mathfrak{m}(y)&=\int_{B_{r}(x)}\mathsf{lip}\Big(\int_{\epsilon_{1}}^{\epsilon_{2}}p_{t}(x,\cdot)dt\Big)(y)d\mathfrak{m}(y)\\
&\leq\int_{B_{r}(x)}\int_{\epsilon_{1}}^{\epsilon_{2}}\frac{c_{4}}{\sqrt{t}\mathfrak{m}(B_{\sqrt{t}}(x))}\exp{\Big\{\frac{-d^{2}(x,y)}{c_{5}t}\Big\}}\,dt\,d\mathfrak{m}(y),\end{split}

(2)

where for the last inequality we passed the Lipschitz constant inside the integral using Fatou Lemma. Switching the integrals and applying a version of Fubini’s Theorem (i.e. Cavalieri principle, see
[
4
]
) we then obtain that the expression in (
2
) can be bounded as follows:

\displaystyle\int_{\epsilon_{1}}^{\epsilon_{2}}\int_{B_{r}(x)}

\displaystyle\frac{c_{4}}{\sqrt{t}\mathfrak{m}(B_{\sqrt{t}}(x))}\exp{\Big\{\frac{-d^{2}(x,y)}{c_{5}t}\Big\}}\,d\mathfrak{m}(y)\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{\epsilon_{1}}^{\epsilon_{2}}\frac{c_{4}}{\sqrt{t}\mathfrak{m}(B_{\sqrt{t}}(x))}\int_{0}^{1}\mathfrak{m}\Big(\Big\{\exp{\Big\{\frac{-\mathsf{d}^{2}(x,y)}{c_{5}t}\Big\}}>s\Big\}\cap B_{r}(x)\Big)\,ds\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\epsilon_{1}}^{\epsilon_{2}}\frac{c_{6}}{\sqrt{t}}\sup_{z\in[0,T]}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-c_{7}s}\frac{\mathfrak{m}(B_{\sqrt{sz}}(x)\cap B_{r}(x))}{\mathfrak{m}(B_{\sqrt{z}}(x))}\,ds\,dt.

We now prove that the supremum in the previous expression is finite, keeping in mind that this is sufficient to prove that our sequence is Cauchy. We compute:

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in[0,T]}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-c_{7}s}\frac{\mathfrak{m}(B_{\sqrt{sz}(x)}\cap B_{r}(x))}{\mathfrak{m}(B_{\sqrt{z}(x)})}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}e^{-c_{7}s}\,ds+\sup_{z\in[0,T]}\Big(\int_{1}^{r^{2}/z}e^{-c_{7}s}\frac{\mathfrak{m}(B_{\sqrt{sz}(x)})}{\mathfrak{m}(B_{\sqrt{z}(x)})}\,ds+\int_{r^{2}/z}^{+\infty}e^{-c_{7}s}\frac{\mathfrak{m}(B_{r}(x))}{\mathfrak{m}(B_{\sqrt{z}(x)})}\,ds\Big)

\displaystyle\leq c_{8}+\sup_{z\in[0,T]}\Big(\int_{1}^{r^{2}/z}e^{-c_{7}s}\frac{V_{K,N}(B_{\sqrt{sz}(x)})}{V_{K,N}(B_{\sqrt{z}(x)})}\,ds+c_{9}e^{-c_{7}r^{2}/z}z^{-N/2}\Big)

where the last inequality is a consequence of Remark
2.8
. It is clear that the finiteness of the last expression follows if we can prove the finiteness of

\sup_{z\in[0,T]}\int_{1}^{r^{2}/z}e^{-c_{7}s}\frac{V_{K,N}(B_{\sqrt{sz}(x)})}{V_{K,N}(B_{\sqrt{z}(x)})}\,ds.

Since when
s\in(1,r^{2}/z)
we have
\sqrt{zs}\in(0,r)
, there exists a constant
c_{10}
depending on
r
such that
V_{K,N}(B_{\sqrt{zs}(x)})\leq c_{10}(sz)^{N/2}
whenever
s\in(1,r^{2}/z)
. Hence combining with Remark
2.8
once again we get

\sup_{z\in[0,T]}\int_{1}^{r^{2}/z}e^{-c_{7}s}\frac{V_{K,N}(B_{\sqrt{sz}(x)})}{V_{K,N}(B_{\sqrt{z}(x)})}\,ds\leq\sup_{z\in[0,T]}\int_{1}^{r^{2}/z}c_{11}e^{-c_{7}s}s^{N/2}\,ds\leq\int_{1}^{+\infty}c_{11}e^{-c_{7}s}s^{N/2}\,ds<+\infty.

Concluding the proof that
G^{T}_{x}\in\mathsf{W}^{1,1}(B_{r}(x))
.
∎

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathsf{X}
be an open set and let
x\in\Omega
and
g\in\mathsf{W}^{1,1}_{0}(\Omega)
. If for every
\phi\in\mathsf{Test}_{c}(\Omega,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
we have

\int_{\Omega}g(y)\Delta\phi(y)\,d\mathfrak{m}(y)=-\phi(x),

then
\Delta g=-\delta_{x}
in distributional sense in
\Omega
.

Proof.

Let
\phi\in\mathsf{Lip}_{c}(\Omega)
and consider its extension to
0
in
\mathsf{X}
, which we still denote by
\phi
. Consider the heat flow
P_{t}\phi\in\mathsf{Test}(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
.
Let now
\eta\in\mathsf{Test}_{c}(\Omega)
be a positive function which is equal to
1
on an open set containing the support of
\phi
. Since the test functions are an algebra, we get
\eta P_{t}\phi\in\mathsf{Test}_{c}(\Omega,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
. Moreover
\eta P_{t}\phi\to\phi
in
\mathsf{W}^{1,2}(\mathsf{X})
and uniformly, while the functions
\eta P_{t}\phi
are uniformly Lipschitz because
\phi
is bounded. Hence using the Dominated Convergence Theorem we get

\int_{\Omega}\nabla g(y)\cdot\nabla\phi(y)\,d\mathfrak{m}(y)=\lim_{t\to 0}\int_{\Omega}\nabla g(y)\cdot\nabla(\eta P_{t}\phi)\,d\mathfrak{m}(y)=-\lim_{t\to 0}\int_{\Omega}g(y)\Delta(\eta P_{t}\phi)\,d\mathfrak{m}(y)=\lim_{t\to 0}\eta P_{t}\phi(x)=\phi(x),

concluding the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
B\subset\mathsf{X}
be a regular set,
x\in B
,
f\in C(\mathsf{X})
, and
g\in\mathsf{Lip}_{loc}(\bar{B}\setminus\{x\})
. There exists
h\in\mathsf{W}^{1,2}(B)\cap C(\bar{B})
such that
\Delta h=f
on
B
,
h+g
is identically zero on
\partial B
and for every positive
\eta\in\mathsf{Lip}_{c}(B)
equal to
1
in a neighbourhood of
x
and taking value in
[0,1]
we have
(1-\eta)(h+g)\in\mathsf{W}^{1,2}_{0}(B)
.

Moreover, if in addition we have that
g\in\mathsf{W}^{1,2}(B)
, then we can ask that
h+g\in\mathsf{W}^{1,2}_{0}(B)
.

Proof.

Let
U\supset\supset\bar{B}
be an open precompact subset of
\mathsf{X}
and consider the function
h_{1}\in\mathsf{W}^{1,2}_{0}(U)
such that
\Delta h_{1}=f
on
U
.
By Proposition
2.16
this function is Lipschitz on
\bar{B}
.

Let now
\eta\in\mathsf{Lip}_{c}(B)
be as in the statement, then
(1-\eta)g\in\mathsf{W}^{1,2}(B)\cap C(\bar{B})
so that,
B
being regular, there exists

h_{2}\in\mathsf{W}^{1,2}(B)\cap C(\bar{B})
, harmonic on
B
, such that
h_{2}+h_{1}+g
is identically zero on
\partial B
and
h_{1}+h_{2}+(1-\eta)g\in\mathsf{W}^{1,2}_{0}(B)
. We then set
h:=h_{2}+h_{1}
and we note that this function has all the required properties.

The case when
g\in\mathsf{W}^{1,2}(B)
is analogous.
∎

We are finally in position to prove the existence of Green’s functions.

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
B\subset\mathsf{X}
be a regular set and let
x\in B
. For every
T>0
there exists
g_{x}^{T}\in\mathsf{W}^{1,2}(B)\cap C(\bar{B})
such that
G_{x}:=G^{T}_{x}+g^{T}_{x}
is a Green’s function on
B
with pole
x
.

Moreover for every
\eta\in\mathsf{Lip}_{c}(B)
equal to
1
in a neighbourhood of
x
and taking value in
[0,1]
we have
(1-\eta)G_{x}\in\mathsf{W}^{1,2}_{0}(B)
.

Proof.

Let
g^{T}_{x}\in\mathsf{W}^{1,2}(B)\cap C(\bar{B})
be the function given by Lemma
3.9
such that

\Delta g_{x}^{T}=-p_{T}(x,\cdot),\hskip 10.00002ptg_{x}^{T}+G_{x}^{T}=0\hskip 10.00002pt\text{on }\partial B

and such that for every
\eta
as in the statement

(1-\eta)(g_{x}^{T}+G_{x}^{T})\in\mathsf{W}^{1,2}_{0}(B).

Now we define
G_{x}:B\to\mathbb{R}
by
G_{x}(y):=G^{T}_{x}(y)+g^{T}_{x}(y)
and we note that by construction
G_{x}\in\mathsf{W}^{1,1}_{0}(B)\cap C(\bar{B}\setminus\{x\})
.

We now check that
G_{x}
is a Green’s functions: let
\phi\in\mathsf{Test}_{c}(B,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
and note that

\displaystyle\int_{B}

\displaystyle G^{T}_{x}(y)\Delta\phi(y)\,d\mathfrak{m}(y)=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathsf{X}}p_{t}(x,y)\Delta\phi(y)\,d\mathfrak{m}(y)\,dt=\int_{0}^{T}P_{t}\Delta\phi(x)\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\Delta P_{t}\phi(x)\,dt=\int_{0}^{T}\frac{d}{dt}P_{t}\phi(x)\,dt=\int_{B}p_{T}(x,y)\phi(y)\,d\mathfrak{m}(y)-\phi(x),

where the last inequality comes from the fact that the trajectories of the heat flow at a fixed point are locally absolutely continuous. From the previous computation and the definition of
G_{x}
we deduce that for every
\phi\in\mathsf{Test}_{c}(B,\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
we have

\int_{B}G_{x}(y)\Delta\phi(y)\,d\mathfrak{m}(y)=-\phi(x),

which by Lemma
3.8
implies that

G_{x}
is a Green’s function for the Laplacian.
∎

Corollary 3.11
.

The function
G_{x}
constructed in Proposition
3.10
is positive.

Proof.

We would like to use the maximum principle on
G_{x}
, but this function is only
\mathsf{W}^{1,1}(B)\cap C(\bar{B}\setminus\{x\})
, while we would need a function in
\mathsf{W}^{1,2}(B)\cap C(\bar{B})
to apply such theorem.

For every
\epsilon>0
consider the function
G^{\epsilon}_{x}\in\mathsf{W}^{1,2}(B)\cap C(\bar{B})
given by

G_{x}^{\epsilon}(y):=\int_{\epsilon}^{T}p_{t}(x,y)\,dt+g_{x}^{T}(y).

We have that
\Delta G_{x}^{\epsilon}=-p_{\epsilon}(x,\cdot)
and
G_{x}^{\epsilon}=-\int_{0}^{\epsilon}p_{t}(x,\cdot)\,dt
on
\partial B
.

By the maximum principle these functions attain their minimum on the boundary, so that on
B
we have

G_{x}^{\epsilon}\geq\min_{y\in\partial B}-\int_{0}^{\epsilon}p_{t}(x,y)\,dt

(3)

At the same time it is easy to check that as
\epsilon
decreases to zero also the quantity in the right hand side of (
3
) goes to zero. Moreover
G_{x}^{\epsilon}\to G_{x}
pointwise in
B\setminus\{x\}
as
\epsilon\to 0
, so that passing to the limit in (
3
) we conclude.
∎

As a consequence of this corollary we get the following proposition.

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
B_{1}\subset\subset B_{2}
be regular sets, let
x\in B_{1}
and let
G^{1}_{x}
and
G^{2}_{x}
be the corresponding Green’s functions. Then
G^{2}_{x}\geq G^{1}_{x}
.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.24
the restriction to
\bar{B}_{1}
of the difference
G^{2}_{x}-G^{1}_{x}
lies in
W^{1,2}_{loc}(B_{1})\cap C(\bar{B}_{1})
.

At the same time
G^{2}_{x}-G^{1}_{x}\geq 0
on
\partial B_{1}
by Corollary
3.11
, so that using the maximum principle we conclude.
∎

The next proposition shows that we can use the Green’s function to construct functions with prescribed Laplacian.

Proposition 3.13
.

Let
B\subset\mathsf{X}
be regular, and let
\phi\in\mathsf{Lip}_{c}(B)
. Then

f(x):=\int_{B}G_{x}(y)\phi(y)\,d\mathfrak{m}(y)

is the unique function such that
f\in D(\Delta,B)\cap\mathsf{W}^{1,2}_{0}(B)\cap C(\bar{B})
with
\Delta f=-\phi
and
f=0
on
\partial B
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.9
we have that there exists a function

f\in D(\Delta,B)\cap W^{1,2}_{0}(B)\cap C(\bar{B})

with
\Delta f=-\phi
and
f=0
on
\partial B
. By the maximum principle such function is unique.
Since
f\in\mathsf{W}^{1,2}_{0}(B)
it is easy to check that there exists a sequence
\{f_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathsf{Lip}_{c}(B)
of functions such that
\|f_{i}-f\|_{\mathsf{W}^{1,2}(B)}\to 0
and such that for every
K\subset\subset B
we have
f_{i}=f
on
K
for
i
sufficiently large.
In particular, since
f_{i}\in\mathsf{Lip}_{c}(B)
and
G_{x}
is a Green’s function with pole
x
, we have

f_{i}(x)=\int_{B}\nabla G_{x}(y)\cdot\nabla f_{i}(y)\,d\mathfrak{m}(y),

so that to conclude it is sufficient to prove that as
i\to+\infty
we have

\int_{B}\nabla G_{x}(y)\cdot\nabla f_{i}(y)\,d\mathfrak{m}(y)\to-\int_{B}G_{x}(y)\Delta f(y)\,d\mathfrak{m}(y).

(4)

To this aim fix
\epsilon>0
and let
\eta\in\mathsf{Lip}(B)
be a function taking value in
[0,1]
which is equal to zero in a neighbourhood of
x
, equal to
1
out of a precompact open subset
U
of
B
and such that

\Big|\int_{B}G_{x}(y)\Delta f(y)\,d\mathfrak{m}(y)-\int_{B}(\eta G_{x})(y)\Delta f(y)\,d\mathfrak{m}(y)\Big|<\epsilon,

and for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
sufficiently large

\Big|\int_{B}\nabla G_{x}(y)\cdot\nabla f_{i}(y)\,d\mathfrak{m}(y)-\int_{B}\nabla(\eta G_{x})(y)\cdot\nabla f_{i}(y)\,d\mathfrak{m}(y)\Big|<\epsilon.

Note that such
\eta
can be constructed because
\Delta f\in\mathsf{L}^{\infty}(B)
and, for
i
sufficiently large, we have that

|\nabla f_{i}|=|\nabla f|\text{ in }U.

In particular, thanks to the arbitrariness of
\epsilon
, to obtain (
4
) it is now sufficient to show that

\int_{B}\nabla(\eta G_{x})(y)\cdot\nabla f_{i}(y)\,d\mathfrak{m}(y)\to-\int_{B}(\eta G_{x})(y)\Delta f(y)\,d\mathfrak{m}(y)\hskip 10.00002pt\text{as }i\to+\infty.

Since
\eta G_{x}\in\mathsf{W}^{1,2}_{0}(B)
by Proposition
3.10
, we can rewrite the difference of the previous quantities as

\displaystyle\Big|-

\displaystyle\int_{B}\nabla(\eta G_{x})(y)\cdot\nabla f_{i}(y)\,d\mathfrak{m}(y)-\int_{B}(\eta G_{x})(y)\Delta f(y)\,d\mathfrak{m}(y)\Big|

\displaystyle=\Big|-\int_{B}\nabla(\eta G_{x})\cdot\nabla f_{i}(y)\,d\mathfrak{m}(y)+\int_{B}\nabla(\eta G_{x})\cdot\nabla f(y)\,d\mathfrak{m}(y)\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\|\eta G_{x}\|_{\mathsf{W}^{1,2}(B)}\|f_{i}-f\|_{\mathsf{W}^{1,2}(B)}\to 0,

concluding the proof.
∎

We now turn our attention to constructing a global Green’s function for
\mathsf{X}
.

Lemma 3.14
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be non parabolic. Let
S\subset\subset\mathsf{X}
and
x\in\mathsf{X}
. There exists a sequence
t_{i}\to+\infty
such that
p_{t_{i}}(x,\cdot)
and
\int_{1}^{t_{i}+1}p_{t}(x,\cdot)\,dt
are uniformly bounded in
S
.

Proof.

The non parabolicity of
\mathsf{X}
implies that there exists a sequence
t_{i}\to+\infty
such that
p_{t_{i}}(x,x)\leq 1
. By Proposition
2.13
there exists a constant
C>0
such that
p_{t}(x,y)\leq Cp_{t+1}(x,x)
for every
y\in S
and
t>1
. Putting these facts together it is easy to check that the sequence
\{t_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
has the desired properties.
∎

Proposition 3.15
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be non parabolic and let
x\in\mathsf{X}
. Then the function
G_{x}(y):=\int_{0}^{+\infty}p_{t}(x,y)\,dt
is a Green’s function for
\mathsf{X}
with pole
x
.

Proof.

First of all we prove that
G_{x}\in\mathsf{L}^{1}_{loc}(\mathsf{X})
. Let
S\subset\Omega
be a compact set. By Proposition
2.13
we deduce that there exists
C>0
depending on
K,N,S
such that
Cp_{t}(x,x)\geq p_{t-1}(x,y)
for every
t>1
and every
y\in S
. As a consequence for every
y\in S
we have

\int_{1}^{+\infty}p_{t}(x,y)\,dt\leq C\int_{2}^{+\infty}p_{t}(x,x)\,dt<+\infty,

(5)

so that by Dominated Convergence Theorem and the Hölder continuity of the heat kernel the function
\int_{1}^{+\infty}p_{t}(x,\cdot)\,dt
is continuous in
S
.
Using such continuity and the stochastic completeness of the heat kernel we then get

\int_{S}\int_{0}^{+\infty}p_{t}(x,y)\,dt\,d\mathfrak{m}(y)\leq\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathsf{X}}p_{t}(x,y)\,d\mathfrak{m}(y)\,dt+\mathfrak{m}(S)\max_{y\in S}\int_{1}^{+\infty}p_{t}(x,y)\,dt\leq 1+\mathfrak{m}(S)\max_{y\in S}\int_{1}^{+\infty}p_{t}(x,y)\,dt,

showing that
G_{x}\in\mathsf{L}^{1}_{loc}(\mathsf{X})
, as desired.

We now claim that for every
\phi\in\mathsf{Test}(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})\cap\mathsf{Lip}_{c}(\mathsf{X})
we have

\int_{\mathsf{X}}G_{x}(y)\Delta\phi(y)\,d\mathfrak{m}(y)=-\phi(x).

(6)

To this aim fix such a
\phi
and observe that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathsf{X}}G_{x}(y)\Delta\phi(y)\,d\mathfrak{m}(y)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{\mathsf{X}}p_{t}(x,y)\Delta\phi(y)\,d\mathfrak{m}(y)\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{+\infty}P_{t}\Delta\phi(x)\,dt=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\Delta P_{t}\phi(x)\,dt=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{d}{dt}P_{t}\phi(x)\,dt

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\to+\infty}\int_{\mathsf{X}}p_{t}(x,y)\phi(y)\,d\mathfrak{m}(y)-\phi(x).

We now claim that the limit appearing in the last expression is zero (we know that the limit exists since the integral appearing at the beginning of the computation is well defined). To prove the claim we note that since
\phi
is supported in a compact set
S
we have

\int_{1}^{+\infty}\Big|\int_{\mathsf{X}}p_{t}(x,y)\phi(y)\,d\mathfrak{m}(y)\Big|\,dt\leq c_{1}\mathfrak{m}(S)\sup_{y\in S}\int_{1}^{+\infty}p_{t}(x,y)\,dt\leq c_{2}\mathfrak{m}(S)\int_{1}^{+\infty}p_{t}(x,x)\,dt<+\infty,

(7)

where in the last inequality we used Proposition
2.13
. As a consequence there exists a sequence
\{t_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
increasing to
+\infty
such that
\int_{\mathsf{X}}p_{t_{i}}(x,y)\phi(y)\,d\mathfrak{m}(y)\to 0
, as claimed.

To conclude the proof we only need to prove that

G_{x}\in\mathsf{W}^{1,1}_{loc}(\mathsf{X})\cap C(\mathsf{X}\setminus\{x\}).

(8)

Indeed if this holds, we can combine equation (
6
) and Lemma
3.8
(which can be used in our setting via a cut off argument) to obtain that
G_{x}
is a Green’s function for
\mathsf{X}
with pole
x
.

To prove (
8
) it is sufficient to prove that
\int_{1}^{+\infty}p_{t}(x,\cdot)\,dt\in\mathsf{W}^{1,1}_{loc}(\mathsf{X})\cap C(\mathsf{X}\setminus\{x\})
since
\int_{0}^{1}p_{t}(x,\cdot)\,dt
lies in such space by Lemmas
3.6
and
3.7
.
Consider the family of functions indexed by
T
given by
\int_{1}^{T}p_{t}(x,\cdot)\,dt
and observe that these are in
\mathsf{L}^{1}(\mathsf{X})\cap\mathsf{W}^{1,1}_{loc}(\mathsf{X})
and converge to
\int_{1}^{+\infty}p_{t}(x,\cdot)\,dt
in
\mathsf{L}^{1}_{loc}(\mathsf{X})
by repeating the argument of (
7
), so that if we prove that their Lipschitz constants are locally uniformly bounded we are done.

To this aim we recall that for every
T>0

we have the distributional equation

\Delta\int_{1}^{T}p_{t}(x,\cdot)\,dt=p_{T}(x,\cdot)-p_{1}(x,\cdot).

Fix then a compact set
S
, by Lemma
3.14
there exists a sequence
\{t_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
increasing to
+\infty
such that

\Delta\int_{1}^{t_{i}+1}p_{t}(x,\cdot)\,dt=p_{t_{i}+1}(x,\cdot)-p_{1}(x,\cdot)\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\int_{1}^{t_{i}+1}p_{t}(x,\cdot)\,dt

are uniformly bounded in the compact
S
.
As a consequence, by the a priori estimates on the gradient of solutions of the Poisson problem given in Proposition
2.17
, we deduce that the functions
\int_{1}^{t_{i}+1}p_{t}(x,\cdot)\,dt
have locally uniformly bounded Lipschitz constants as desired.
∎

Remark 3.16
.

The function
G_{x}
that we constructed is not only continuous but also locally Lipschitz in
\mathsf{X}\setminus\{x\}
.

We are now in position to prove Theorem
4
, that we recall below.

Theorem 4
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m},\mathsf{x})
be an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
metric measure space with infinite diameter. The following are equivalent:

1.

There is no positive Green’s function on
\mathsf{X}
with pole
\mathsf{x}
.

2.

For every
(x,y)\in\mathsf{X}\times\mathsf{X}
we have
\int_{1}^{+\infty}p_{t}(x,y)\,dt=+\infty
.

3.

There exists
(x,y)\in\mathsf{X}\times\mathsf{X}
such that
\int_{1}^{+\infty}p_{t}(x,y)\,dt=+\infty
.

4.

Let
\{B_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be an exhaustion of regular sets containing
\mathsf{x}
. Let
G_{\mathsf{x}}^{i}
be the Green’s function on
B_{i}
with pole
\mathsf{x}
. Then for every
y\neq\mathsf{x}
we have
G_{\mathsf{x}}^{i}(y)\to+\infty
as
i\to+\infty
.

Proof.

The fact that
2
and
3
are equivalent has been shown in Proposition
3.2
, while
1
implies
2
by Proposition
3.15
. So we only need to show that
2\to 4\to 1
.

(2\to 4)

By Proposition
3.10
, for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
the Green’s function
G^{i}_{\mathsf{x}}
can be written as
G^{i}_{\mathsf{x}}(y)=\int_{0}^{T_{i}}p_{t}(\mathsf{x},y)dt+g^{T_{i}}_{\mathsf{x}}(y)
where
g^{T_{i}}_{\mathsf{x}}\in\mathsf{W}^{1,2}(B_{i})\cap C(\bar{B_{i}})
satisfies

\Delta g^{T_{i}}_{\mathsf{x}}=-p_{T_{i}}(\mathsf{x},\cdot)\hskip 10.00002pt\text{on }B_{i}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002ptg^{T_{i}}_{\mathsf{x}}=-\int_{0}^{T_{i}}p_{t}(\mathsf{x},\cdot)\hskip 10.00002pt\text{on }\partial B_{i}.

Taking into account the parabolicity assumption, it is sufficient to prove that we can choose a sequence
T_{i}
increasing to
+\infty
in such a way that
g^{T_{i}}_{\mathsf{x}}(y_{0})
is bounded from below uniformly in
i
.

Since
\Delta g^{T_{i}}_{\mathsf{x}}\leq 0
, by the maximum principle it is sufficient to consider the restriction of
g^{T_{i}}_{\mathsf{x}}
to the boundary of
B_{i}
, where the minimum is attained. So the problem reduces to choosing
T_{i}
increasing to
+\infty
in such a way that
\int_{0}^{T_{i}}p_{t}(\mathsf{x},\cdot)\,dt
is uniformly bounded when
y\in\partial B_{i}
.

This is clearly possible if we can show that for
T
fixed, the quantity

\sup_{y\in\partial B_{i}}\int_{0}^{T}p_{t}(\mathsf{x},y)\,dt

converges to zero as
i\to+\infty
, and this follows from Proposition
2.12
and the hypothesis on the diameter.

(4\to 1)
Suppose by contradiction that there exists a positive Green’s function
G_{\mathsf{x}}\in\mathsf{W}^{1,1}_{loc}(\mathsf{X})\cap C(\mathsf{X}\setminus\{\mathsf{x}\})
with pole
\mathsf{x}
in
\mathsf{X}
. We claim that
G_{\mathsf{x}}\geq G^{i}_{\mathsf{x}}
on
B_{i}
and we note that, if the claim is true, then we contradict our hypothesis that
G_{\mathsf{x}}^{i}(y)\to+\infty
as
i\to+\infty
.

To prove the claim observe that the difference
G_{\mathsf{x}}-G^{i}_{\mathsf{x}}
is harmonic and belongs to
\mathsf{W}^{1,2}_{loc}(B_{i})\cap C(\bar{B_{i}}\setminus\{\mathsf{x}\})
by Proposition
2.24
.
Moreover the difference
G_{\mathsf{x}}-G^{i}_{\mathsf{x}}
is positive on
\partial B_{i}
, so that by the maximum principle it is positive in
B_{i}
, as claimed.
∎

Now we turn our attention to proving Theorem
5
. To this aim we need the following technical lemma.

Lemma 3.17
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be a non parabolic
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space with infinite diameter and essential dimension
k\geq 2
. Let
x\in\mathsf{X}
be a regular point such that

\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\frac{\mathfrak{m}(B_{r}(x))}{r^{k}}\in(0,+\infty).

Consider an exhaustion
\{\Omega_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of regular sets of
\mathsf{X}
containing
x
, and for each of these sets consider the corresponding Green’s function
G^{i}_{x}
.

These functions admit continuous representatives with values in
[0,+\infty]
such that
G_{x}^{i}(x)=+\infty
. Using these representatives, there exist
T>1

and a sequence
\{c_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\in(1,T)
such that

x\in\{y\in\Omega_{i}:G^{i}_{x}(y)>c_{i}\}\cap B_{1}(x)\subset\subset B_{1}(x)\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ for every }i\in\mathbb{N}

and each of the sets
\{y\in\Omega_{i}:G^{i}_{x}(y)>c_{i}\}
is open, has finite perimeter in
\mathsf{X}
and satisfies

\partial\{y\in\Omega_{i}:G^{i}_{x}(y)>c_{i}\}=\partial\{y\in\Omega_{i}:G^{i}_{x}(y)<c_{i}\}=\{y\in\Omega_{i}:G^{i}_{x}(y)=c_{i}\}.

Proof.

We will write
\{G^{i}_{x}>c_{i}\}
instead of
\{y\in\Omega_{i}:G^{i}_{x}(y)>c_{i}\}
.
Consider the Green’s function
G_{x}
given by Proposition
3.15
. Since
x
is a regular point and
\mathsf{X}
has essential dimension
k\geq 2
, using the bounds of Proposition
2.12
and the explicit expressions for
G^{i}_{x}
and
G_{x}
, we get

\lim_{y\to x}G_{x}(y)=\lim_{y\to x}G^{i}_{x}(y)=+\infty\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for every }i\in\mathbb{N}.

(9)

Hence, we have the desired continuous representatives.
Moreover, since
G_{x}
is bounded on compacts of
\mathsf{X}\setminus\{x\}
we can pick
t>0
such that
\{G_{x}>t\}\cap B_{1}(x)
contains
x
and

\{G_{x}>t\}\cap B_{1}(x)\subset\subset B_{1}(x).

At the same time, using again (
9
) and the fact that
G_{x}\geq G_{x}^{i}
, we get that for every
t^{\prime}\in[t,t+1]
we have

\{G^{i}_{x}>t^{\prime}\}\cap B_{1}(x)\subset\{G_{x}>t\}\cap B_{1}(x)\subset\subset B_{1}(x),

and that
\{G^{i}_{x}>t^{\prime}\}
is open and contains
x
.
Moreover, since
G^{i}_{x}
is locally Lipschitz on compact subsets of
\mathsf{X}\setminus\{x\}
(and tends to infinity in
x
, so that the level sets stay away from
x
), for
\lambda^{1}
-a.e.
t^{\prime}\in[t,t+1]
we have that
\{G^{i}_{x}>t^{\prime}\}
has finite perimeter and
\mathfrak{m}(\{G^{i}_{x}=t^{\prime}\})=0
. We claim that this implies

\partial\{G^{i}_{x}>t^{\prime}\}=\partial\{G^{i}_{x}<t^{\prime}\}=\{G^{i}_{x}=t^{\prime}\}.

(10)

For the first equality we only show that
\partial\{G^{i}_{x}>t^{\prime}\}\subset\partial\{G^{i}_{x}<t^{\prime}\}
, the other case being analogous. Let
y\in\partial\{G^{i}_{x}>t^{\prime}\}
and consider a small ball
B_{\epsilon}(y)
which is precompact in
X\setminus\{x\}
.
Observe that
G_{x}^{i}(y)=t^{\prime}
and that the ball cannot be contained in
\{G^{i}_{x}=t^{\prime}\}
, because otherwise this set would have positive measure.
At the same time it cannot be contained in
\{G^{i}_{x}\geq t^{\prime}\}
, otherwise the maximum principle would imply that
G_{x}^{i}
is constant in the ball. As a consequence such ball intersects
\{G^{i}_{x}<t^{\prime}\}
, and since
\epsilon
was arbitrarily small, we have that
y\in\partial\{G^{i}_{x}<t^{\prime}\}
, as claimed.

The second equality in (
10
) follows because
\{G^{i}_{x}=t^{\prime}\}
cannot contain any balls since it has zero measure, and so it is contained in
\partial\{G^{i}_{x}\neq t^{\prime}\}
, but since
\partial\{G^{i}_{x}>t^{\prime}\}=\partial\{G^{i}_{x}<t^{\prime}\}
we get

\partial\{G^{i}_{x}\neq t^{\prime}\}=\partial\{G^{i}_{x}>t^{\prime}\}=\partial\{G^{i}_{x}<t^{\prime}\}.

The fact that
\partial\{G^{i}_{x}<t^{\prime}\}\subset\{G^{i}_{x}=t^{\prime}\}
is trivial.
Then we define
T:=t+1
and for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
we pick
c_{i}\in[t,t+1)
to be one of the
t^{\prime}
with the previously proved properties.
∎

The next proposition is the key step to prove Theorem
5
. The idea is adapted from the proof of the analogous fact in the smooth setting given in
[
55
]
, but some crucial adjustments have been made to avoid integrating by parts on sets that might not be regular enough. This is achieved by replacing balls in the original proof with level sets of Green’s functions.

Proposition 3.18
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space with essential dimension
k
. If
there exists
x\in\mathsf{X}
such that

\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\frac{\mathfrak{m}(B_{r}(x))}{r^{k}}\in(0,+\infty)\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\int_{1}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{P(B_{t}(x),\mathsf{X})}\,dt=+\infty,

then
\mathsf{X}
is parabolic.

Proof.

Consider first the case when
\mathsf{X}
has finite diameter and assume by contradiction that it is non parabolic. Note that the finiteness of the diameter implies that
\mathsf{X}
is compact.
Consider then the functions
g_{\epsilon}(y):=\int_{\epsilon}^{+\infty}p_{t}(x,y)\,dt
, which converge in
\mathsf{L}^{1}(\mathsf{X})
to the Green’s function
G_{x}
given by Proposition
3.15
. Using ideas appearing in previous proofs one can show that these function are Lipschitz (hence in
\mathsf{W}^{1,2}(\mathsf{X})
), satisfy
\Delta g_{\epsilon}=-p_{\epsilon}(x,\cdot)\leq 0
and have a minimum since
\mathsf{X}
is compact. Hence by the maximum principle they are all constant, so that also
G_{x}
is constant, a contradiction.

So we can suppose that
\mathsf{X}
has infinite diameter.
Suppose by contradiction that
\mathsf{X}
is non parabolic and assume first that the essential dimension of
\mathsf{X}
is not
1
.
Pick an exhaustion
\{\Omega_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of regular sets of
\mathsf{X}
containing
x
, and for each of these sets consider the corresponding Green’s function
G^{i}_{x}
.
By Lemma
3.17
there exist
T>1

and a sequence
\{c_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\in(1,T)
such that

x\in\{G^{i}_{x}>c_{i}\}\cap B_{1}(x)\subset\subset B_{1}(x)\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ for every }i\in\mathbb{N}

and each of the sets
\{G^{i}_{x}>c_{i}\}
is open, has finite perimeter and satisfies

\partial\{G^{i}_{x}>c_{i}\}=\partial\{G^{i}_{x}<c_{i}\}=\{G^{i}_{x}=c_{i}\}.

We can suppose without loss of generality that
\bar{B}_{1}(x)\subset\Omega_{i}
for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
.

We then define

l_{i}:=\sup_{z\in B_{1}(x)}\{\mathsf{d}(x,z):G^{i}_{x}(z)>c_{i}\}

and claim that we have

T\geq\int_{l_{i}}^{\mathsf{d}(\partial\Omega_{i},x)}\frac{1}{P(B_{r}(x),\mathsf{X})}\,dr.

To prove the claim let
0<\epsilon<<1
such that

\partial\{G^{i}_{x}>\epsilon\}=\partial\{G^{i}_{x}<\epsilon\}=\{G^{i}_{x}=\epsilon\}

and such that, setting

s_{i}^{\epsilon}:=\inf_{z\in\Omega_{i}}\{\mathsf{d}(x,z):G^{i}_{x}(z)<\epsilon\},

it holds
s_{i}^{\epsilon}>l_{i}
. The first condition can be achieved by the same argument of Lemma
3.17
, while the second can be obtained using that
G_{x}^{i}>0
on
\Omega_{i}
and
G_{x}^{i}=0
on
\partial\Omega_{i}
.

Consider the function
g_{i}:\mathsf{X}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
g(y)
is equal to
c_{i}
in
\bar{B}_{l_{i}}(x)
,
is equal to
\epsilon
in
{{}^{c}}B_{s_{i}^{\epsilon}}(x)
,
and in
B_{s_{i}^{\epsilon}}(x)\setminus B_{l_{i}}(x)
is defined by

g_{i}(y):=(c_{i}-\epsilon)\frac{\int_{\mathsf{d}(x,y)}^{s_{i}^{\epsilon}}{P(B_{r}(x),\mathsf{X})}^{-1}\,dr}{\int_{l_{i}}^{s_{i}^{\epsilon}}{P(B_{r}(x),\mathsf{X})}^{-1}\,dr}+\epsilon.

Let

A_{i}^{\epsilon}:=\{y\in B_{1}(x):G_{x}^{i}(y)<c_{i}\}\cup\{y\in\Omega_{i}\cap{{}^{c}}B_{1}(0):G_{x}^{i}(y)>\epsilon\}.

Thanks to our conditions on
\{G_{x}^{i}>c_{i}\}
and
\{G_{x}^{i}>\epsilon\}
we have that the set
A_{i}^{\epsilon}
is open, contains
B_{s_{i}^{\epsilon}}(x)\setminus\bar{B}_{l_{i}}(x)
and its boundary is made of disjoint connected sets where
G_{x}^{i}
takes either the value
c_{i}
or
\epsilon
.
As a consequence, modulo translations and inversions, we can use
G_{x}^{i}
as a barrier in Proposition
2.20
to show that
A_{i}^{\epsilon}
is a regular set.

Hence there exists a
unique function
h\in D(\Delta,A_{i}^{\epsilon})\cap C(\bar{A}_{i}^{\epsilon})
such that
h-g_{i}\in\mathsf{W}^{1,2}_{0}(A_{i}^{\epsilon})
,
\Delta h=0
and
h-g_{i}=0
on
\partial A_{i}^{\epsilon}
. From this it follows that
h=G_{x}^{i}
, which in particular gives
G_{x}^{i}-g_{i}\in\mathsf{W}^{1,2}_{0}(A_{i}^{\epsilon})
.
Hence, using the fact that harmonic functions minimize the Dirichlet energy, we get

\int_{A_{i}^{\epsilon}}|\nabla G_{x}^{i}|^{2}\,d\mathfrak{m}\leq\int_{A_{i}^{\epsilon}}|\nabla g_{i}|^{2}\,d\mathfrak{m}.

At the same time, using the coarea formula, one can check that

\int_{A_{i}^{\epsilon}}|\nabla g_{i}|^{2}\,d\mathfrak{m}=(c_{i}-\epsilon)^{2}\Big(\int_{l_{i}}^{s_{i}^{\epsilon}}{P(B_{r}(x),\mathsf{X})}^{-1}\,dr\Big)^{-1},

so that

\int_{A_{i}^{\epsilon}}|\nabla G_{x}^{i}|^{2}\,d\mathfrak{m}\leq(c_{i}-\epsilon)^{2}\Big(\int_{l_{i}}^{s_{i}^{\epsilon}}{P(B_{r}(x),\mathsf{X})}^{-1}\,dr\Big)^{-1}.

(11)

Moreover by construction we have that the vector field
\nabla G_{x}^{i}
is defined in a neighbourhood of
\bar{A}_{i}^{\epsilon}
, where it is bounded and admits bounded divergence, so that we can apply Proposition
2.30
to get

\int_{A_{i}^{\epsilon}}|\nabla G_{x}^{i}|^{2}\,d\mathfrak{m}=-c_{i}\int_{\partial\{G_{i}^{\epsilon}<c_{i}\}\cap B_{1}(x)}(\nabla G_{x}^{\epsilon}\cdot\nu_{A_{i}^{\epsilon}})_{ext}\,d\mathsf{Per}-\epsilon\int_{\partial\{G_{i}^{\epsilon}>\epsilon\}}(\nabla G_{x}^{\epsilon}\cdot\nu_{A_{i}^{\epsilon}})_{ext}\,d\mathsf{Per}.

Using the definitions it is then easy to check that

\int_{\partial\{G_{i}^{\epsilon}<c_{i}\}\cap B_{1}(x)}(\nabla G_{x}^{\epsilon}\cdot\nu_{A_{i}^{\epsilon}})_{ext}\,d\mathsf{Per}=-\int_{\partial\{G_{i}^{\epsilon}>c_{i}\}\cap B_{1}(x)}(\nabla G_{x}^{\epsilon}\cdot\nu_{({{}^{c}}A_{i}^{\epsilon})})_{int}\,d\mathsf{Per},

so that, taking into account that
\Delta G_{i}=-\delta_{x}
and using again Proposition
2.30
, we obtain

\int_{A_{i}^{\epsilon}}|\nabla G_{x}^{i}|^{2}\,d\mathfrak{m}=c_{i}\int_{\partial\{G_{i}^{\epsilon}>c_{i}\}\cap B_{1}(x)}(\nabla G_{x}^{\epsilon}\cdot\nu_{({{}^{c}}A_{i}^{\epsilon})})_{int}\,d\mathsf{Per}-\epsilon\int_{\partial\{G_{i}^{\epsilon}>\epsilon\}}(\nabla G_{x}^{\epsilon}\cdot\nu_{A_{i}^{\epsilon}})_{ext}\,d\mathsf{Per}=c_{i}-\epsilon.

Putting together this with (
11
) we obtain

c_{i}-\epsilon\geq\int_{l_{i}}^{s_{i}^{\epsilon}}{P(B_{r}(x),\mathsf{X})}^{-1}\,dr,

and letting
\epsilon\to 0
we deduce that

T\geq\int_{l_{i}}^{\mathsf{d}(\partial\Omega_{i},x)}\frac{1}{P(B_{r}(x),\mathsf{X})}\,dr

for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
, as claimed.

Taking into account that
l_{i}\leq 1

and letting
i\to+\infty
we obtain that

T\geq\int_{1}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{P(B_{r}(x),\mathsf{X})}\,dr,

a contradiction.

We now need to consider the case when
\mathsf{X}
has essential dimension
1
. A space with infinite diameter and essential dimension
1
must be a weighted version of either
\mathbb{R}
or of a closed half line by Proposition
2.11
.
In this case we can repeat an analogous proof, replacing the sets
\{G_{x}^{i}>c_{i}\}
with a fixed interval containing
x
. This is sufficient because on each endpoint of this interval each
G_{x}^{i}
will trivially have a singleton as image, allowing to construct the analogue of the function
g_{i}
in the previous case. Similarly the computation to estimate
\int_{A_{i}^{\epsilon}}|\nabla G_{x}^{i}|^{2}\,d\mathfrak{m}
can be carried out in the same way, replacing
A_{i}^{\epsilon}
with an appropriate union of two intervals and exploiting their regularity.
∎

Corollary 3.19
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space. If
there exists
x\in\mathsf{X}
such that

\int_{1}^{+\infty}\frac{t}{\mathfrak{m}(B_{t}(x))}\,dt=+\infty,

then
\mathsf{X}
is parabolic.

Proof.

It is clear that if the condition of the statement holds for
x\in\mathsf{X}
, then, thanks to Proposition
2.10
, it holds also for a regular point
x^{\prime}\in\mathsf{X}
satisfying

\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\frac{\mathfrak{m}(B_{r}(x^{\prime}))}{r^{k}}\in(0,+\infty),

where
k
is the essential dimension of
\mathsf{X}
.
The statement then follows from the previous proposition repeating the strategy of
[
41
, Corollary
2
]
.
∎

Putting this together with the heat kernel estimates of Proposition
2.12
we obtain Theorem
5
.

Theorem 5
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space. If
there exists
x\in\mathsf{X}
such that

\int_{1}^{+\infty}\frac{t}{\mathfrak{m}(B_{t}(x))}\,dt=+\infty,

then
\mathsf{X}
is parabolic. This condition is also necessary if
K=0
.

Proof.

Thanks to Corollary
3.19
we only need to prove that if
K=0
the condition of the statement is also necessary.
This follows immediately since by Proposition
2.12
we have that

\int_{1}^{+\infty}p_{t}(x,x)\,dt\leq\int_{1}^{+\infty}\frac{c_{1}}{\mathfrak{m}(B_{\sqrt{t}}(x))}\,dt,

concluding the proof
∎

We conclude the section
with a proposition taken from
[
42
, Example
3.19
]
concerning spaces with finite measure.

Proposition 3.20
.

Let
(\mathsf{M},\mathsf{d}_{g},e^{-V}\mathfrak{m}_{g})
be a geodesically complete weighted manifold with finite measure. Then for every
(x,y)\in\mathsf{M}\times\mathsf{M}
, setting
\mathfrak{m}:=e^{-V}\mathfrak{m}_{g}
, we have

p_{t}(x,y)\to\frac{1}{\mathfrak{m}(\mathsf{M})}\text{ as }t\to+\infty.

Corollary 3.21
.

If
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
is parabolic and
(\mathsf{M},\mathsf{d}_{g},e^{-V}\mathfrak{m}_{g})
is a geodesically complete weighted Riemannian manifold with finite mass then the product space is parabolic.

Proof.

It follows immediately from the previous proposition, the definition of parabolicity, and the tensorization properties of heat kernels.
∎

Proof of the Half Space Property

In this section
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
will be a fixed parabolic
\mathsf{RCD}(0,N)
space and
C\subset\mathsf{X}\times\mathbb{R}
will be locally the boundary of a locally perimeter minimizing set. We assume moreover that
C\subset\mathsf{X}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
. We will denote by
\mathsf{d}_{C}:{{}^{c}}C\to\mathbb{R}
the distance from
C
, by
\mathsf{d}_{0}
the signed distance from
\mathsf{X}\times\{0\}
with positive sign in the negative half space and negative sign otherwise, and by
\bar{\mathsf{d}}:\mathsf{X}\times(-\infty,0)\to\mathbb{R}
the difference
\mathsf{d}_{C}-\mathsf{d}_{0}
. Observe that
\bar{\mathsf{d}}\geq 0
since
C
is contained in the upper half space.

Generic points in
\mathsf{X}\times\mathbb{R}
will be usually denoted by
\bar{x}=(x,t_{x})
, the canonical distance and the canonical measure in the poduct space will be
\mathsf{d}_{\times}
and
\mathfrak{m}_{\times}
respectively.

We now develop the simple machinery needed to prove Propositions
4.3
and
4.8
, which are crucial steps in the previously outlined argument to prove Theorem
1
.

Definition 4.1
.

Given
\bar{x}\in\mathsf{X}\times\mathbb{R}
we say that a geodesic
\gamma:[0,\mathsf{d}_{C}(\bar{x})]\to\mathsf{X}\times\mathbb{R}
is a
connector
if it realizes the distance between
\bar{x}
and
C
. We say that such connector is
vertical
if its image is contained in a set of the form
\{x\}\times\mathbb{R}\subset\mathsf{X}\times\mathbb{R}
.

Connectors will be useful because their behavior is related to the gradient of the function
\bar{\mathsf{d}}
.
The next lemma is taken from
[
37
, Lemma
2.6
]
.

Lemma 4.2
.

For
\mathfrak{m}_{\times}
-a.e.
\bar{x}\in\mathsf{X}\times(-\infty,0)
there exists a unique connector.

Proposition 4.3
.

\mathsf{d}_{C}:{{}^{c}}C\to\mathbb{R}
admits negative distributional Laplacian.

Proof.

From
[
20
, Lemma
4.16
]
we get that
\mathsf{d}_{C}
admits distributional Laplacian in its domain and that the singular part of such distributional Laplacian is a negative Radon measure.
Hence we only need to show that the regular part of this measure is negative as well.
To this aim let
\bar{x}\notin C
be a point with a single
connector, and let
\bar{y}\in C
be such that
\mathsf{d}_{C}(\bar{x})=\mathsf{d}(\bar{x},\bar{y})
. Let
U_{\bar{y}}\ni\bar{y}
and
E\subset U_{\bar{y}}
be the sets as in Definition
2.33
.
We claim that there exists a neighbourhood
V_{\bar{x}}
of
\bar{x}
such that

V_{\bar{x}}\subset\{\bar{z}\in{{}^{c}}C:\exists\bar{z}_{1}\in U_{\bar{y}}:\mathsf{d}_{C}(\bar{z})=\mathsf{d}(\bar{z},\bar{z}_{1})\}.

If this were not the case we could find a sequence
\bar{z}_{n}\to\bar{x}
whose connectors’ endpoints lie outside of
U_{\bar{y}}
. Modulo passing to a subsequence we then have that the endpoints of these connectors converge to a point
\bar{z}\in{{}^{c}}U_{\bar{y}}
, and the geodesic connecting
\bar{x}
and
\bar{z}
will be a second connector for
\bar{x}
, a contradiction.

Hence we can apply Proposition
2.34
to obtain that the distributional Laplacian of
\mathsf{d}_{C}
is negative in
V_{\bar{x}}
. Repeating the same argument for every point with a single connector and taking the union of the resulting open sets we get an open set of full measure where the distributional Laplacian of
\mathsf{d}_{C}
is negative. This implies that the regular part of the distributional Laplacian of
\mathsf{d}_{C}
is negative on its domain, concluding the proof.
∎

The next Lemma shows the first link between connectors and the quantity
|\nabla\bar{\mathsf{d}}|
.

Lemma 4.4
.

For
\mathfrak{m}_{\times}
-a.e.
\bar{x}\in\mathsf{X}\times(-\infty,0)
with a vertical connector we have
|\nabla\bar{\mathsf{d}}|(\bar{x})=0
.

Proof.

For
\mathfrak{m}_{\times}
-a.e. point in
\bar{x}\in\mathsf{X}\times(-\infty,0)
we have

|\nabla\bar{\mathsf{d}}|(\bar{x})^{2}=2-2\nabla\mathsf{d}_{C}\cdot\nabla\mathsf{d}_{0}(\bar{x})

so that we only need to prove that for
\mathfrak{m}_{\times}
-a.e. point
\bar{x}
with a unique vertical connector we have
\nabla\mathsf{d}_{C}\cdot\nabla\mathsf{d}_{0}(\bar{x})=1
. Since distance functions have local Lipschitz constant
1
everywhere, for
\mathfrak{m}_{\times}
-a.e. point
\bar{x}
we get

\nabla\mathsf{d}_{C}\cdot\nabla\mathsf{d}_{0}(\bar{x})\leq|\nabla\mathsf{d}_{C}|(\bar{x})|\nabla\mathsf{d}_{0}|(\bar{x})=1.

Moreover one can easily check that for
\mathfrak{m}_{\times}
-a.e.
\bar{x}
we have

\nabla\mathsf{d}_{C}\cdot\nabla\mathsf{d}_{0}(\bar{x})=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{\mathsf{lip}(\mathsf{d}_{C}+\epsilon\mathsf{d}_{0})^{2}(\bar{x})-\mathsf{lip}(\mathsf{d}_{C})^{2}(\bar{x})}{2\epsilon}.

Then, moving along the vertical connector, we deduce that
\mathsf{lip}(\mathsf{d}_{C}+\epsilon\mathsf{d}_{0})\geq 1+\epsilon
, so that for
\mathfrak{m}_{\times}
-a.e.
\bar{x}

\nabla\mathsf{d}_{C}\cdot\nabla\mathsf{d}_{0}(\bar{x})\geq\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{(1+\epsilon)^{2}-1}{2\epsilon}=1,

concluding the proof.
∎

Definition 4.5
.

Let
\bar{x}=(x,t_{x})\in\mathsf{X}\times(-\infty,0)
, let
\gamma:[0,\mathsf{d}_{C}(\bar{x})]\to\mathsf{X}\times\mathbb{R}
be a connector and let
\bar{y}=(y,t_{y}):=\gamma(\mathsf{d}_{C}(\bar{x}))
. We define the slope
\mathsf{s}(\bar{x},\gamma)>0
of
\gamma
by

\mathsf{s}(\bar{x},\gamma):=1-\frac{|t_{x}-t_{y}|}{\mathsf{d}_{C}(\bar{x})}.

The role played by the slope of a connector is clarified in the next lemma.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\bar{x}\in\mathsf{X}\times(-\infty,0)
and
\gamma
be a connector for
\bar{x}
. Then

\mathsf{lip}(\bar{\mathsf{d}})(\bar{x})\geq\mathsf{s}(\bar{x},\gamma).

Proof.

The projection of
\gamma
to
\mathbb{R}
is a geodesic of constant speed
1-\mathsf{s}(\bar{x},\gamma)
so that

\mathsf{d}_{0}(\gamma(s))=\mathsf{d}_{0}(\bar{x})-(1-\mathsf{s}(\bar{x},\gamma))s,

while, since
\gamma
is a connector, we have

\mathsf{d}_{C}(\gamma(s))=\mathsf{d}_{C}(\bar{x})-s.

Using these facts we obtain

\bar{\mathsf{d}}(\gamma(s))-\bar{\mathsf{d}}(\bar{x})=\mathsf{d}_{C}(\gamma(s))-\mathsf{d}_{0}(\gamma(s))-\mathsf{d}_{C}(\bar{x})+\mathsf{d}_{0}(\bar{x})=-s+(1-\mathsf{s}(\bar{x},\gamma))s=-s\mathsf{s}(\bar{x},\gamma).

Dividing both sides of the previous equation by
s
and taking the limit as
s
goes to zero we immediately obtain that
\mathsf{lip}(\bar{\mathsf{d}})(\bar{x})\geq\mathsf{s}(\bar{x},\gamma)
.
∎

The next lemma shows that the slope is continuous in points with a single connector. It is easy to check that the statement may fail for general points.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
\bar{x}\in\mathsf{X}\times(-\infty,0)
be a point admitting a unique connector
\gamma
.
Let
\{\bar{x}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathsf{X}\times(-\infty,0)
be a sequence such that
\bar{x}_{n}\to\bar{x}
, and
\{\gamma_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
a corresponding sequence of connectors. Then

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\mathsf{s}(\bar{x}_{n},\gamma_{n})=\mathsf{s}(\bar{x},\gamma).

Proof.

For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
we define

\bar{y}_{n}:=\gamma_{n}(\mathsf{d}_{C}(\bar{x}_{n}))\in C

and we note that these points have uniformly bounded distance from
\bar{x}
because of the triangular inequality.
We claim that the sequence
\bar{y}_{n}
converges to the endpoint of the connector
\gamma
.
If we are able to prove the claim, the statement of the lemma immediately follows by noting that the slope of a connector depends continuously on the positions of the starting point and the endpoint.

To prove the claim we observe that, modulo passing to a (non relabeled) subsequence, we have that
\bar{y}_{n}\to\bar{y}\in C
.
Moreover, since
\mathsf{d}_{C}(\bar{x}_{n})=\mathsf{d}_{\times}(\bar{x}_{n},\bar{y}_{n})
, passing to the limit and using the continuity of distance functions we obtain that

\mathsf{d}_{C}(\bar{x})=\mathsf{d}_{\times}(\bar{x},\bar{y}).

This implies, by the uniqueness of the connector in
\bar{x}
, that
\bar{y}
is the endpoint of
\gamma
. In particular the limit point does not depend on the subsequence that we chose, so that the full sequence
\{\bar{y}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to
\bar{y}
, as claimed.
∎

Proposition 4.8
.

If
\bar{\mathsf{d}}:\mathsf{X}\times(-\infty,0)\to\mathbb{R}
is not constant, then there exists a ball
B\subset\mathsf{X}\times(-\infty,0)
and a real number
\tau>0
such that
|\nabla\bar{\mathsf{d}}(\bar{x})|\geq\tau
for
\mathfrak{m}_{\times}
-a.e.
\bar{x}\in B
.

Proof.

Since
\bar{\mathsf{d}}
is not constant there exists a set of positive measure in
\mathsf{X}\times(-\infty,0)
where
|\nabla\bar{\mathsf{d}}|>0
.
By Lemmas
4.2
and
4.4
, this implies that there exists a point
\bar{x}\in\mathsf{X}\times(-\infty,0)
with a single non vertical connector
\gamma
such that

\mathsf{s}(\bar{x},\gamma)>0
. By lemma
4.7
and a standard argument by contradiction, this implies that there exists
\tau>0
such that for every
\bar{y}
in a sufficiently small ball centered in
\bar{x}
, and every connector
\gamma_{\bar{y}}
of
\bar{y}
, we have

p(\bar{y},\gamma_{\bar{y}})>\tau.

The conclusion immediately follows by Lemma
4.6
.
∎

Remark 4.9
.

A direct computation shows that

\Delta e^{-\bar{\mathsf{d}}}\geq e^{-\bar{\mathsf{d}}}|\nabla\bar{\mathsf{d}}|^{2}.

Combining this with the previous proposition we deduce that there exists a ball
B\subset\mathsf{X}\times(-\infty,0)
and
c>0
such that

\Delta e^{-\bar{\mathsf{d}}}\geq c1_{B}.

The next proposition follows from a standard computation and gives the explicit dependence of the Laplacian on the weight in a weighted
\mathsf{RCD}
space.

Proposition 4.10
.

Let
(\mathsf{Y},\mathsf{d}_{y},\mathfrak{m}_{y})
be an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space and let
V\in\mathsf{W}^{1,2}_{loc}(\mathsf{X})
. Suppose that the space
(\mathsf{Y},\mathsf{d}_{y},\tilde{\mathfrak{m}}_{y})
with
\tilde{\mathfrak{m}}=e^{-V}\mathfrak{m}_{y}
is
\mathsf{RCD}(K^{\prime},N^{\prime})
for a suitable couple
(K^{\prime},N^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}\times[1,+\infty)
.
Then for every
u\in\mathsf{Lip}_{loc}(\mathsf{Y})
, denoting by
\tilde{\Delta}
the Laplacian in
(\mathsf{Y},\mathsf{d}_{y},\tilde{\mathfrak{m}}_{y})
, we have that
\tilde{\Delta}u=\Delta u-\nabla u\cdot\nabla V
in distributional sense.

The next step to prove Theorem
1
is to replace the product measure
\mathfrak{m}_{\times}
on
\mathsf{X}\times\mathbb{R}
with a measure
\tilde{\mathfrak{m}}_{\times}
, such that the resulting space is parabolic and the Laplacian bounds given by Remark
4.9
are preserved (modulo reducing
c
) w.r.t. the new measure.
To this aim consider the modified metric measure space
(\mathbb{R},\mathsf{d}_{e},e^{-V}\lambda^{1})
where
\mathsf{d}_{e}
is the Euclidean distance and the function
V:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is defined as follows.

Let
\bar{x}=(p_{x},t_{x})\in\mathsf{X}\times(-\infty,0)
and
\tau,\epsilon>0
be such that
|\nabla\bar{d}|\geq\tau
on
B_{\epsilon}(\bar{x})\subset\mathsf{X}\times(-\infty,0)
.
Note that this is possible thanks to Proposition
4.8
. Let
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be an odd increasing function such that
f(0)=0
,
f(t)=1
for every
t\geq 1
and
f(t)=-1
for every
t\leq-1
. Define

V(s):=\int_{t_{x}}^{s}f(t-t_{x})\,dt.

Note that
V
is symmetric w.r.t.
t_{x}
and that
V^{\prime}(s)=1
if
s\in[t_{x}+1,+\infty)
,
V^{\prime}(s)\in(-1,1)
if
s\in(t_{x}-1,t_{x}+1)
and
V^{\prime}(s)=-1
if
s\in(-\infty,t_{x}-1]
. In particular
\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-V(s)}\,ds<+\infty
.

As anticipated, we use such function to modify the measure in the product space
\mathsf{X}\times\mathbb{R}
.

Proposition 4.11
.

There exists a couple
(K,N)
such that

(\mathsf{X}\times\mathbb{R},\mathsf{d}_{\times},e^{-V(-\mathsf{d}_{0})}\mathfrak{m}_{\times}),

is an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space.

Proof.

Note that
(\mathbb{R},\mathsf{d}_{e},e^{-V(-\mathsf{d}_{0})}\lambda^{1})
is an
\mathsf{RCD}(K_{0},N_{0})
space for some
K_{0}\in(-\infty,0)
and
N_{0}\in(1,+\infty)
. Since

(\mathsf{X}\times\mathbb{R},\mathsf{d}_{\times},e^{-V(-\mathsf{d}_{0})}\mathfrak{m}_{\times})=(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})\otimes(\mathbb{R},\mathsf{d}_{e},e^{-V}\lambda^{1}),

we deduce that also
(\mathsf{X}\times\mathbb{R},\mathsf{d}_{\times},e^{-V(-d_{0})}\mathfrak{m}_{\times})
is an

\mathsf{RCD}(K_{0},N+N_{0})
space, as desired.
∎

Proposition 4.12
.

The space

(\mathsf{X}\times\mathbb{R},\mathsf{d}_{\times},e^{-V(-\mathsf{d}_{0})}\mathfrak{m}_{\times})

is parabolic.

Proof.

As we noted earlier the factor
(\mathbb{R},\mathsf{d}_{e},e^{-V}\lambda^{1})
has finite mass. The statement follows applying Corollary
3.21
.
∎

In what follows we denote by
\tilde{\mathfrak{m}}_{\times}
the measure
e^{-V(-\mathsf{d}_{0})}\mathfrak{m}_{\times}
and by
\tilde{\Delta}
the laplace operator in
(\mathsf{X}\times\mathbb{R},\mathsf{d}_{\times},\tilde{\mathfrak{m}}_{\times})
.
We recall that both in
(\mathsf{X}\times\mathbb{R},\mathsf{d}_{\times},\tilde{\mathfrak{m}}_{\times})
and
(\mathsf{X}\times\mathbb{R},\mathsf{d}_{\times},\mathfrak{m}_{\times})

the relaxed gradient of a Lipschitz function coincides with the local Lipschitz constant, so that in particular it does not change depending on the space that we consider. The next theorem contains the crucial computation showing that the Laplacian bounds of Proposition
4.8
are preserved in the modified product space.

Theorem 4.13
.

The function
e^{-\bar{\mathsf{d}}}
satisfies
\tilde{\Delta}e^{-\bar{\mathsf{d}}}\geq 0
in
\mathsf{X}\times(-\infty,0)
. Moreover there exist
\tau>0
and
B_{\epsilon}(\bar{x})\subset\mathsf{X}\times(-\infty,0)
such that
\tilde{\Delta}e^{-\bar{\mathsf{d}}}\geq\tau>0
on
B_{\epsilon}(\bar{x})
.

Proof.

Using the chain rule for Laplacians and taking into account Proposition
4.10
we have that

\tilde{\Delta}e^{-\bar{\mathsf{d}}}=e^{-\bar{\mathsf{d}}}(-\tilde{\Delta}\bar{\mathsf{d}}+|\nabla\bar{\mathsf{d}}|^{2})=e^{-\bar{\mathsf{d}}}(-\Delta\bar{\mathsf{d}}+\nabla\bar{\mathsf{d}}\cdot\nabla V(-\mathsf{d}_{0})+|\nabla\bar{\mathsf{d}}|^{2})=e^{-\bar{\mathsf{d}}}(-\Delta\bar{\mathsf{d}}-V^{\prime}(-\mathsf{d}_{0})\nabla\bar{\mathsf{d}}\cdot\nabla\mathsf{d}_{0}+|\nabla\bar{\mathsf{d}}|^{2}).

(12)

By the fact that
\Delta\mathsf{d}_{0}=0
and Proposition
4.3
we have that

-\Delta\bar{\mathsf{d}}=-\Delta\mathsf{d}_{C}\geq 0.

Moreover

|\nabla\bar{\mathsf{d}}|^{2}=2-2\nabla\mathsf{d}_{C}\cdot\nabla\mathsf{d}_{0}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\nabla\bar{\mathsf{d}}\cdot\nabla\mathsf{d}_{0}=\nabla\mathsf{d}_{C}\cdot\nabla\mathsf{d}_{0}-1,

so that (
12
) is bounded below by

\displaystyle e^{-\bar{\mathsf{d}}}(V^{\prime}(-\mathsf{d}_{0})-V^{\prime}(-\mathsf{d}_{0})\nabla\mathsf{d}_{C}\cdot\nabla\mathsf{d}_{0}+2-2\nabla\mathsf{d}_{C}\cdot\nabla\mathsf{d}_{0})

\displaystyle=e^{-\bar{\mathsf{d}}}(2+V^{\prime}(-\mathsf{d}_{0}))(1-\nabla\mathsf{d}_{C}\cdot\nabla\mathsf{d}_{0})=e^{-\bar{\mathsf{d}}}(2+V^{\prime}(-\mathsf{d}_{0}))\frac{|\nabla\bar{\mathsf{d}}|^{2}}{2}.

Summing up we obtain

\tilde{\Delta}e^{-\bar{\mathsf{d}}}\geq e^{-\bar{\mathsf{d}}}(2+V^{\prime}(-\mathsf{d}_{0}))\frac{|\nabla\bar{\mathsf{d}}|^{2}}{2}.

(13)

Since by construction
|V^{\prime}(-\mathsf{d}_{0})|\leq 1
the first part of the statement follows. For the second part it is sufficient to apply Proposition
4.8
to equation (
13
).
∎

The next proposition concerns regularity of sets in the modified product space and is an immediate consequence of Corollary
2.22
.

Proposition 4.14
.

Let
B\subset\mathsf{X}
be a regular set in
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
and let
I_{t}:=(t_{x}-t,t_{x}+t)\subset(\mathbb{R},\mathsf{d}_{e},e^{-V}\lambda^{1})
. Then
B\times I_{t}
is regular in
(\mathsf{X}\times\mathbb{R},\mathsf{d}_{\times},\tilde{\mathfrak{m}}_{\times})
.

The next two lemmas concern some simple geometric properties of
\bar{\mathsf{d}}
.

Lemma 4.15
.

Let
y\in\mathsf{X}
and
t\in(-\infty,0)
. Then
\bar{\mathsf{d}}(y,0)\geq\bar{\mathsf{d}}(y,t)
.

Proof.

By triangular inequality we have

\mathsf{d}_{C}(y,t)\leq\mathsf{d}_{C}(y,0)+t,

so that

\bar{\mathsf{d}}(y,t)=\mathsf{d}_{C}(y,t)-t\leq\mathsf{d}_{C}(y,0)=\bar{\mathsf{d}}(y,0),

as claimed.
∎

The next lemma follows easily by the definition of product distance.

Lemma 4.16
.

Suppose that
C
and
\mathsf{X}\times\{0\}
have positive distance and that there exists a geodesic
\gamma:[0,\mathsf{d}_{\times}(C,\mathsf{X}\times\{0\})]\to\mathsf{X}\times\mathbb{R}
realizing such distance. Then
\gamma
is vertical, i.e. its image is contained in a set of the form
\{x\}\times\mathbb{R}
.

The next theorem is the key step to prove the Half Space Property. In the proof we follow the argument outlined in the Introduction.

Theorem 4.17
.

The function
\bar{\mathsf{d}}:\mathsf{X}\times(-\infty,0)\to\mathbb{R}
is constant.

Proof.

Suppose that the statement does not hold.

Assume first that
\mathsf{X}
has finite diameter. In this case
\mathsf{X}
is compact, so that the restriction
\mathsf{d}_{C}:\mathsf{X}\times\{-1\}\to\mathbb{R}
has a minimum on a point
x\in\mathsf{X}\times\{-1\}
.
Let
\gamma
be the connector of
x
to
C
and note that it has to be vertical by Lemma
4.16
.
In particular
\bar{\mathsf{d}}
attains its minimum on all the points in its domain that are in the image of
\gamma
. By Proposition
4.3
and the maximum principle this implies that such function is constant.

Assume now that
\mathsf{X}
has infinite diameter.
Then by Theorem
4.13
there exist
\bar{x}=(x,t_{x})
and
\epsilon,\tau>0
such that
\tilde{\Delta}e^{-\bar{\mathsf{d}}}\geq\tau>0
on
B_{\epsilon}(\bar{x})
. Modulo replacing
x
with a sufficiently close point we may also suppose that

x
is regular for
\mathsf{X}
and that

\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\frac{\mathfrak{m}(B_{r}(x))}{r^{k}}\in(0,+\infty),

where
k\in\mathbb{N}
is the essential dimension of
\mathsf{X}
.

Let
\phi\in\mathsf{Lip}_{c}(B_{\epsilon}(\bar{x}))
be a positive function, which is equal to
\tau
on
B_{\epsilon/2}(\bar{x})
, takes values in
[0,\tau]
, and is symmetric with respect to
\mathsf{X}\times\{t_{x}\}
. It is clear that such a function exists. Let also
\{B_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be an exhaustion of
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
with regular sets containing
x
.

Consider now for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
and
\bar{z}\in B_{i}\times[t_{x}-i,t_{x}+i]
the Green’s function
G_{\bar{z}}^{i}
with pole
\bar{z}
relative to the set

B_{i}\times[t_{x}-i,t_{x}+i]\subset(\mathsf{X}\times\mathbb{R},\mathsf{d}_{\times},\tilde{\mathfrak{m}}_{\times}),

and note that such function exists thanks to Propositions
4.14
and
3.10
.

In addition, since the space

(\mathsf{X}\times\mathbb{R},\mathsf{d}_{\times},\tilde{\mathfrak{m}}_{\times})
is parabolic by Proposition
4.12
, then for every
\bar{y}\neq\bar{x}
(and
i
sufficiently large) we get by Theorem
4
that

G_{\bar{x}}^{i}(\bar{y})\to\infty\hskip 10.00002pt\text{as }i\uparrow+\infty.

We claim that this implies that
for every
S\subset\subset\mathsf{X}\times\mathbb{R}
we have

\inf_{\bar{y}\in S}G_{\bar{x}}^{i}(\bar{y})\to\infty\hskip 10.00002pt\text{as }i\uparrow+\infty.

(14)

This follows because for every
\bar{y}\neq\bar{x}
the sequence
\{G_{\bar{x}}^{i}(\bar{y})\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
increases as
i
increases by Proposition
3.12
while, considering the
[0,+\infty]
-valued continuous representative given by Lemma
3.17
, we have that

G_{\bar{x}}^{i}(\bar{x})=+\infty
(here we are using our particular choice of the point
x\in\mathsf{X}
and the fact that the essential dimension of the product space
\mathsf{X}\times\mathbb{R}
is greater than or equal to
2
).

Consider now for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
the function
g_{i}:B_{i}\times[t_{x}-i,t_{x}+i]\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

g_{i}(\bar{z}):=\int_{\mathsf{X}}G_{\bar{z}}^{i}(\bar{y})\phi(\bar{y})\,d\tilde{\mathfrak{m}}_{\times}(\bar{y}).

By Proposition
3.13
each of these functions, in its domain of definition, satisfies
\tilde{\Delta}g_{i}=-\phi
, is locally Lipschitz and continuous up to the boundary, and takes value zero on the boundary itself.

Moreover using (
14
) and the fact that
\phi=\tau
on
B_{\epsilon/2}(\bar{x})
it is easy to check that

g_{i}(\bar{x})\to+\infty\hskip 10.00002pt\text{as }i\to+\infty.

In particular we can pick
i_{0}
large enough so that
g_{i_{0}}(\bar{x})>2
and
i_{0}>|t_{x}|
. Consider now the function

e^{-\bar{\mathsf{d}}}+g_{i_{0}}.

This function must have a maximum in

B_{i_{0}}\times[t_{x}-i_{0},t_{x}+i_{0}]\cap\mathsf{X}\times(-\infty,0]

since since it is continuous and
(e^{-\bar{\mathsf{d}}}+g_{i_{0}})(\bar{x})\geq 2
while
(e^{-\bar{\mathsf{d}}}+g_{i_{0}})(\bar{y})\leq 1
for every

\bar{y}\in\partial(B_{i_{0}}\times[t_{x}-i_{0},t_{x}+i_{0}]\cap\mathsf{X}\times(-\infty,0])\setminus\mathsf{X}\times\{0\}.

(15)

Moreover by Theorem
4.13
we have

\tilde{\Delta}(e^{-\bar{\mathsf{d}}}+g_{i_{0}})\geq 0\hskip 10.00002pt\text{in }B_{i_{0}}\times[t_{x}-i_{0},t_{x}+i_{0}]\cap\mathsf{X}\times(-\infty,0),

so that if we can prove that the maximum lies in

B_{i_{0}}\times[t_{x}-i_{0},t_{x}+i_{0}]\cap\mathsf{X}\times(-\infty,0),

then by the maximum principle we would obtain that
e^{-\bar{\mathsf{d}}}+g_{i_{0}}
is constant, which contradicts the fact that
(e^{-\bar{\mathsf{d}}}+g_{i_{0}})(\bar{x})\geq 2
while
(e^{-\bar{\mathsf{d}}}+g_{i_{0}})(\bar{y})\leq 1

Corollary 4.18
.

There exists

is constant. Let

To show that

Then there exists a ball

∎

The remaining part of the section is dedicated to showing that

is actually a union of horizontal slices. The idea is that, using the previous corollary, we can prove that

To follow such a plan we first need a few intuitive results whose proofs are quite technical. These are presented in the next propositions. We will repeatedly use the fact that

Proposition 4.21

To prove (

Corollary 4.29
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
be a parabolic
\mathsf{RCD}(0,N)

be a perimeter minimizing set contained in

.

By the previous theorem the boundary of

is made of horizontal slices and we assume by contradiction that
\partial E
has more than
1

Suppose first that
\mathsf{X}
is compact. In this case subtracting from

its lowest connected component we obtain a competitor for

with strictly less perimeter, a contradiction. So we can suppose that
\mathsf{X}
has infinite diameter.

Let

be the distance between the lowest and the second lowest connected component of
\partial E
. We claim that there exists

such that

CP(B_{s}(\mathsf{x}),\mathsf{X})<2\mathfrak{m}(B_{s}(\mathsf{x})).

Recall that, by coarea formula, the function

is absolutely continuous and satisfies

for a.e.

grows exponenentially in

\mathsf{RCD}(0,N)

).

So let

be a value satisfying (

), let
\mathsf{X}\times(a,a+C)\subset E
be the lowest connected component of

(up to replacing

with its complement, we may assume that the lowest connected component has this form), let

be small enough so that

and consider the set

Observe that

, while at the same time
we have that

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle(A,B_{s_{0}+1}(\mathsf{x})\times(a-\epsilon,a+C+\epsilon))

\displaystyle=CP(B_{s_{0}}(\mathsf{x}),\mathsf{X})+P(\mathsf{X}\times(a,a+C),(B_{s_{0}+1}(\mathsf{x})\setminus\bar{B}_{s_{0}}(\mathsf{x}))\times(a-\epsilon,a+C+\epsilon))

\displaystyle<2\mathfrak{m}(B_{s_{0}}(\mathsf{x}))+P(\mathsf{X}\times(a,a+C),(B_{s_{0}+1}(\mathsf{x})\setminus\bar{B}_{s_{0}}(\mathsf{x}))\times(a-\epsilon,a+C+\epsilon))

\displaystyle=P(E,B_{s_{0}+1}(\mathsf{x})\times(a-\epsilon,a+C+\epsilon)),

contradicting the fact that

is a perimeter minimizer and proving that

has only

∎

([0,+\infty),\mathsf{d}_{e},(x\wedge 1)\lambda^{1})

c_{\mathbb{R}}\in(0,1)
, if a function
u\in\mathsf{W}^{1,1}((-c_{\mathbb{R}},c_{\mathbb{R}})\cup(0_{\mathsf{Y}},c_{\mathsf{Y}}))
is harmonic in its domain, then the restriction of
u
to
(-c_{\mathbb{R}},c_{\mathbb{R}})
is affine.

To this aim note that the restriction of
u
to
(-c_{\mathbb{R}},c_{\mathbb{R}})
belongs to
\mathsf{W}^{1,1}((-c_{\mathbb{R}},c\textbf{}))
and in particular it is continuous.

Moreover by restricting
u
to open sets of its domain that do not contain the gluing point we obtain, by harmonicity, that
u
is affine in
(-c_{\mathbb{R}},0_{\mathbb{R}})
and
(0_{\mathbb{R}},c_{\mathbb{R}})
(possibly with different slopes) and that
u
is smooth in
(0_{\mathsf{Y}},c_{\mathsf{Y}})
. Hence, if we manage to prove that the restriction of
u
to
(-c_{\mathbb{R}},c_{\mathbb{R}})
is
C^{1}
we conclude the proof of Step
1
.

To this aim let
\phi\in\mathsf{Lip}_{c}((-c_{\mathbb{R}},c_{\mathbb{R}})\cup(0_{\mathsf{Y}},c_{\mathsf{Y}}))
and consider the harmonicity condition (we are using that relaxed gradients coincide with local Lipschitz constants in our space and we are denoting by
u^{\prime}
and
\phi^{\prime}
standard derivatives):

0=\int_{(-c_{\mathbb{R}},c_{\mathbb{R}})\cup(0_{\mathsf{Y}},c_{\mathsf{Y}})}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\phi\,d\mathfrak{m}=\int_{-c_{\mathbb{R}}}^{0}u^{\prime}(x)\phi^{\prime}(x)\,d\lambda^{1}+\int_{0}^{c_{\mathbb{R}}}u^{\prime}(x)\phi^{\prime}(x)\,d\lambda^{1}+\int_{0_{\mathsf{Y}}}^{c_{\mathsf{Y}}}u^{\prime}(x)\phi^{\prime}(x)x\,d\lambda^{1}(x).

Integrating by parts and using the previously found regularity we obtain

0=\phi(0)\Big(\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in(-c_{\mathbb{R}},0)\\
x\to 0\end{subarray}}u^{\prime}(x)-\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in(0,c_{\mathbb{R}})\\
x\to 0\end{subarray}}u^{\prime}(x)\Big),

which shows that the restriction of
u
to
(-c_{\mathbb{R}},c_{\mathbb{R}})
is
C^{1}
and concludes the proof of Step
1
.

Step 2
: We now prove that
\mathsf{X}
is not parabolic.

By step
1
we deduce that if
x\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
and
G_{x}
is the corresponding Green’s function on
\mathsf{X}
, then the restriction of
G_{x}
to
\mathbb{R}
is a Green’s function on the standard real line. This in particular implies that
G_{x}
cannot be positive, proving that
\mathsf{X}
is parabolic.

We finally show that the Half Space Property fails on
\mathsf{X}
. This is done again in two steps.

Step 1
.
Consider the function
1_{\mathbb{R}}:\mathsf{X}\to\mathbb{R}
defined as
1_{\mathbb{R}}\equiv 1
on
\mathbb{R}
and
1_{\mathbb{R}}\equiv 0
on
\mathsf{X}\setminus\mathbb{R}
.
We claim that its epigraph
\mathsf{Epi}(1_{\mathbb{R}})\subset\mathsf{X}\times\mathbb{R}
satisfy
P(\mathsf{Epi}(1_{\mathbb{R}}),\{0\}\times\mathbb{R})=0
.

Since
P(\mathsf{Epi}(1_{\mathbb{R}}),\{0\}\times\{0,1\})=0
, to prove the above claim
it is sufficient to show that

P(\mathsf{Epi}(1_{\mathbb{R}}),\{0\}\times[\epsilon,1-\epsilon])=0
for every
\epsilon>0
small enough. This would follow if we can show that for
\delta>0
fixed we have

P(\mathsf{Epi}(1_{\mathbb{R}}),B^{\mathsf{X}}_{\delta}(0)\times[\epsilon,1-\epsilon])=0.

So let
\delta>0
be fixed and consider the sequence
f_{n}
given by

f_{n}((x,t)):=1_{(0_{\mathsf{Y}},1_{\mathsf{Y}})}(x)((1-n\mathsf{d}(0,x))\vee 0)+1_{\mathbb{R}}(x).

Using this as approximating sequence in the definition of perimeter we get

P(\mathsf{Epi}(1_{\mathbb{R}}),B^{\mathsf{X}}_{\delta}(0)\times[\epsilon,1-\epsilon])\leq\liminf_{n\to+\infty}(1-2\epsilon)n\int_{0}^{1/n}x\,dx=0,

concluding the proof of Step
1
.

Step 2
: We now show that
\mathsf{Epi}(1_{\mathbb{R}})
is a local perimeter minimizer.

By Step
1
we obtain that in any set of the form
B^{\mathsf{X}}_{R}(0)\times(-1,2)\subset\mathsf{X}\times\mathbb{R}
we have

P(\mathsf{Epi}(1_{\mathbb{R}}),B^{\mathsf{X}}_{R}(0)\times(-1,2))=P(\mathsf{X}\times[0,+\infty),B^{\mathsf{X}}_{R}(0)\times(-1,2)),

while one can check that if
E\Delta\mathsf{Epi}(1_{\mathbb{R}})\subset\subset B^{\mathsf{X}}_{R}(0)\times(-1,2)
, then

P(E,B^{\mathsf{X}}_{R}(0)\times(-1,2))\geq P(\mathsf{X}\times[0,+\infty),B^{\mathsf{X}}_{R}(0)\times(-1,2)).

Putting these facts together we deduce that
E
is a local (actually global) perimeter minimizer, as claimed.

5
Applications

In this section we assume the validity of the Half Space Property for
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
spaces and we study its applications in the smooth setting. Theorems
5.6
and
5.8
are the main results in this sense, while Theorems
2
and
3
follow combining these with Theorem
1
.

In this section whenever we consider a manifold
\mathsf{M}
and a hypersurface
S\subset\mathsf{M}
we implicitly mean that the hypersurface is properly embedded.
Given a manifold
(\mathsf{M},g)
with boundary we say that
\partial\mathsf{M}
is convex if its second fundamental form
\Pi
w.r.t. the inward pointing normal is positive. Moreover, given a hypersurface
S\subset\mathsf{M}
, we denote by
\mathbf{H}
its mean curvature vector.
The next proposition can be obtained repeating an argument in
[
43
, Theorem
2.4
]
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
(\mathsf{M}^{n},\mathsf{d}_{g},e^{-V}d\mathfrak{m}_{g})
be a weighted manifold with convex boundary such that for a number
N>n

\mathsf{Ric}_{\mathsf{M}}+\mathsf{Hess}_{V}-\frac{\nabla V\otimes\nabla V}{N-n}\geq K\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ on }\mathsf{M}\setminus\partial\mathsf{M},

then
(\mathsf{M}^{n},\mathsf{d}_{g},e^{-V}d\mathfrak{m}_{g})
is an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space.

Definition 5.2
.

Let
(\mathsf{M}^{n},\mathsf{d}_{g},e^{-V}d\mathfrak{m}_{g})
be a weighted manifold (possibly with boundary). We say that a hypersurface
S\subset\mathsf{M}
is minimal if its mean curvature vector satisfies
\mathbf{H}=\nabla V^{\perp}
on
S\setminus\partial\mathsf{M}
, where
\nabla V^{\perp}
is the projection of
\nabla V
on the normal bundle of
S
.

As in the classical case, one can characterize minimality in terms of variations of the weighted area functional. In
[
22
]
it is shown that in a weighted manifold
(\mathsf{M}^{n},\mathsf{d}_{g},e^{-V}d\mathfrak{m}_{g})
without boundary a hypersurface
S
is minimal if and only if the first variation of the weighted area functional vanishes at
S
. Moreover the same paper shows that
S
is minimal in a weighted manifold without boundary
(\mathsf{M}^{n},\mathsf{d}_{g},e^{-V}d\mathfrak{m}_{g})
if and only if it is minimal in the manifold
(\mathsf{M},g^{\prime})
, where
g^{\prime}:=e^{-\frac{2}{n-1}V}g
. It is convenient to notice that the weighted area in
(\mathsf{M}^{n},\mathsf{d}_{g},e^{-V}d\mathfrak{m}_{g})
and the area with respect to the conformal metric
g^{\prime}
coincide, and that orthogonality of vectors is preserved under conformal changes of the metric.

Now we state two results that are needed to obtain Corollary
5.5
, which shows that minimal hypersurfaces are locally boundaries of locally perimeter minimizing sets.
The next lemma is a consequence of the general properties of tubular neighbourhoods and Fermi coordinates in smooth manifolds (see for instance
[
39
, Lemma
2.7
]
).

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
(\mathsf{M},g)
be a manifold with boundary and let
\gamma:[0,1]\to\mathsf{M}
be a geodesic intersecting
\partial\mathsf{M}
orthogonally in
\gamma(1)
. Then there exists an interval
I=[1-\epsilon,1)
such that for every
t\in I
the curve
\gamma
realizes the distance between
\partial\mathsf{M}
and
\gamma(t)
. In particular, in an appropriate neighbourhood of
\gamma(1)
, any other curve branching from
\gamma
and ending on
\partial\mathsf{M}
has length greater than or equal to the one of
\gamma
.

The proof of the next lemma can be performed adapting the one in
[
54
, Theorem
2.1
]
and is just sketched, while the subsequent corollary follows from standard approximation arguments.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
(\mathsf{M}^{n},\mathsf{d}_{g},e^{-V}d\mathfrak{m}_{g})
be a weighted manifold (possibly with boundary). Let
S\subset\mathsf{M}
be a minimal hypersurface intersecting
\partial\mathsf{M}
orthogonally. Then
S
locally minimizes the weighted area among smooth competitors.

Proof.

Modulo considering the manifold
(\mathsf{M},g^{\prime})
, where
g^{\prime}:=e^{-\frac{2}{n}V}g
, we can suppose that the weight
V
is identically zero.
We then repeat the argument of
[
54
, Theorem
2.1
]
.

We first consider
x\in S\setminus\partial\mathsf{M}
and we claim that
S
minimizes the area w.r.t. smooth competitors in a neighbourhood of
x
. To prove the claim let
C\subset S
be an
n-2
dimensional submanifold of
S
containing
x
, let
P_{0}:S\to C
be the nearest point projection from
S
to
C
, let
P_{1}:\mathsf{M}\to S
be nearest point projection from
\mathsf{M}
to
S
and let
P:=P_{0}\circ P_{1}
. Let
U
be a sufficiently small neighbourhood of
x
in
\mathsf{M}
and using the coarea formula we can rewrite the area of
S\cap U
as

\mathsf{H}^{n-1}(S\cap U)=\int_{C}\int_{U\cap S_{c}}1/J_{n-2}(P_{|S})\,d\mathsf{H}^{1}\,d\mathsf{H}^{n-2}(c)

where
S_{c}:=P^{-1}(\{c\})\cap S
and
J_{n-2}(P_{|S})
is the appropriate coarea factor. Since
P^{-1}(\{c\})
intersects
S
orthogonally, we have that
J_{n-2}(P_{|S})=J_{n-2}(P)
on
S_{c}
(while in general we would have

J_{n-2}(P_{|S})\leq J_{n-2}(P)
), so that we can rewrite

\mathsf{H}^{n-1}(S\cap U)=\int_{C}\int_{U\cap S_{c}}1/J_{n-2}(P)\,d\mathsf{H}^{1}\,d\mathsf{H}^{n-2}(c)=\int_{C}\text{length}(S_{c}\cap U)\,d\mathsf{H}^{n-2}(c),

where the length of each
S_{c}\cap U
is computed w.r.t. the metric obtained multiplying the standard metric on
\{P=c\}
by
1/J_{n-2}(P)
. The minimality of
S
implies, through the previous equation, that each curve
S_{c}
is a geodesic in
\{P=c\}
with the modified metric. In particular, modulo restricting
U
, we have that each
S_{c}\cap U
is also length minimizing in
\{P=c\}
with the modified metric.

With this in mind we have that if
S^{\prime}
is a competitor of
S
, denoting
S^{\prime}_{c}:=S^{\prime}\cap\{P=c\}
, then

\mathsf{H}^{n-1}(S^{\prime}\cap U)=\int_{C}\text{length}(S^{\prime}_{c}\cap U)\,d\mathsf{H}^{n-2}(c)\geq\int_{C}\text{length}(S_{c}\cap U)\,d\mathsf{H}^{n-2}(c)=\mathsf{H}^{n-1}(S\cap U).

(24)

This proves the statement for points lying outside of
\partial\mathsf{M}
.

For boundary points, one can repeat the previous argument, replacing the fact that geodesics locally minimize the length with Lemma
5.3
to justify the inequality in (
24
).
∎

Corollary 5.5
.

Let
(\mathsf{M}^{n},\mathsf{d}_{g},e^{-V}d\mathfrak{m}_{g})
be a weighted manifold (possibly with boundary). Let
S\subset\mathsf{M}
be a minimal hypersurface intersecting
\partial\mathsf{M}
orthogonally, then
S
is locally a boundary of a locally perimeter minimizing set.

In Theorem
1
we showed that the Half Space Property holds for parabolic
\mathsf{RCD}(0,N)
spaces and we expect it to hold for parabolic
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
spaces, for a general Ricci lower bound
K\in\mathbb{R}
. The next conditional results (Theorems
5.6
and
5.8
) thus hold for
K=0
and we expect them to hold for any
K\in\mathbb{R}
.

Theorem 5.6
.

Fix
K\in\mathbb{R}
,
N\in(1,\infty)
and assume that the Half Space Property holds for
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
spaces. Let
(\mathsf{M}^{n},\mathsf{d}_{g},e^{-V}d\mathfrak{m}_{g})
be a parabolic weighted manifold with convex boundary such that
N>n
and

\mathsf{Ric}_{\mathsf{M}}+\mathsf{Hess}_{V}-\frac{\nabla V\otimes\nabla V}{N-n}\geq K\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ on }\mathsf{M}\setminus\partial\mathsf{M}.

Let
S\subset\mathsf{M}\times(0,+\infty)
be a connected minimal hypersurface intersecting
\partial\mathsf{M}\times\mathbb{R}
orthogonally, then
S
is a horizontal slice.

Proof.

The manifold
(\mathsf{M}^{n},\mathsf{d}_{g},e^{-V}d\mathfrak{m}_{g})
is an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space by Proposition
5.1
.
The manifold
S
is locally a boundary of a locally perimeter minimizing set and is a slice by the Half Space Property.
∎

Definition 5.7
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m},\mathsf{x})
be an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space. We say that a function

P:[0,+\infty)\to(0,+\infty)
is a modulus of parabolicity if

\int_{1}^{+\infty}\frac{t}{P(t)}\,dt=+\infty.

We say that
\mathsf{X}
has modulus of parabolicity
P
if
\mathfrak{m}(B_{r}(\mathsf{x}))\leq\mathfrak{m}(B_{1}(\mathsf{x}))P(r)
for every
r\geq 0
.

By Theorem
5
, if an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m},\mathsf{x})
has a modulus of parabolicity, then it is parabolic, and this condition is also necessary if
K=0
. In general, there are manifolds that are parabolic but do not have a modulus of parabolicity, as shown, for example, in
[
40
, Example
7.2
]
.

We say that a set
S\subset\mathsf{X}
is an area minimizing boundary in an open set
A\subset\mathsf{X}
if the exists a set
E\subset A
minimizing the perimeter in
A
such that
\partial E\cap A=S
. Let
S\subset\mathsf{X}\times\mathbb{R}
be such that
(x,0)\in S
. We define

\mathsf{Osc}_{x,r}(S):=\sup\{|t|:(y,t)\in S\cap B_{r}(x)\times(-r,r)\};

\mathsf{Osc}^{+}_{x,r}(S):=\sup\{|t|:(y,t)\in S\cap B_{r}(x)\times(0,r)\};

and

\mathsf{Osc}^{-}_{x,r}(S):=\sup\{|t|:(y,t)\in S\cap B_{r}(x)\times(-r,0)\}.

Theorem 5.8
.

Fix
K\in\mathbb{R}
,
N\in(1,+\infty)
and assume that the Half Space Property holds for
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
spaces.
Let
P
be a modulus of parabolicity.
For every
t,r,T>0
there exists
R(K,N,P,t,r,T)>0
such that if
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m},\mathsf{x})
is an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space with modulus of parabolicity
P
and
E\subset B_{R}(\mathsf{x})\times(-R,R)
is a perimeter minimizer in
B_{R}(\mathsf{x})\times(-R,R)
such that
(\mathsf{x},0)\in\partial E
and
\mathsf{Osc}_{\mathsf{x},r}(\partial E)\geq t
, then

\mathsf{Osc}^{+}_{\mathsf{x},R}(\partial E)\geq T\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\mathsf{Osc}^{-}_{\mathsf{x},R}(\partial E)\geq T.

Proof.

Suppose by contradiction that the statement is false.
Then, there exist
t,r,T>0
, a sequence
\{R_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(0,+\infty)
increasing to
+\infty
, a sequence of
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
spaces
\{(\mathsf{X}_{i},\mathsf{d}_{i},\mathfrak{m}_{i},\mathsf{x}_{i})\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
with modulus of parabolicity
P
,
and sets
E_{i}\subset\mathsf{X}_{i}\times(-R_{i},R_{i})
such that
(\mathsf{x}_{i},0)\in\partial E_{i}
,
\mathsf{Osc}_{\mathsf{x}_{i},r}(\partial E_{i})\geq t
and

\mathsf{Osc}^{+}_{\mathsf{x}_{i},R_{i}}(\partial E_{i})<T\hskip 10.00002pt\text{or}\hskip 10.00002pt\mathsf{Osc}^{-}_{\mathsf{x}_{i},R_{i}}(\partial E_{i})<T.

Moreover, if we replace each measure
\mathfrak{m}_{i}
with its normalized version
\mathfrak{m}_{i}(B_{1}(\mathsf{x}_{i}))^{-1}\mathfrak{m}_{i}
, we have that each set
E_{i}
is still perimeter minimizing in the normalized product space and that the spaces
(\mathsf{X}_{i},\mathsf{d}_{i},\mathfrak{m}_{i}(B_{1}(\mathsf{x}_{i}))^{-1}\mathfrak{m}_{i},\mathsf{x}_{i})
still have modulus of parabolicity
P
.
Hence it is not restrictive to assume that the measures
\mathfrak{m}_{i}
satisfy
\mathfrak{m}_{i}(B_{1}(\mathsf{x}_{i}))=1
.

Without loss of generality and passing to a (non relabeled) subsequence we may then suppose that for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\mathsf{Osc}^{-}_{\mathsf{x}_{i},R_{i}}(\partial E_{i})<T,

so that modulo replacing
E_{i}
with its complement we may also suppose that

E_{i}\cap B_{R_{i}}(\mathsf{x}_{i})\times[-R_{i},R_{i}]\subset B_{R_{i}}(\mathsf{x}_{i})\times[-T,+\infty).

(25)

Up to passing to another subsequence we have that the spaces
(\mathsf{X}_{i},\mathsf{d}_{i},\mathfrak{m}_{i},\mathsf{x}_{i})
converge in pmGH sense to an
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)
space
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m},\mathsf{x})
. By the continuity of the mass under pmGH convergence,
\mathsf{X}
has modulus of parabolicity
P
and, in particular, it is parabolic.

Passing to yet another subsequence we have that the sets
E_{i}
converge in
\mathsf{L}^{1}_{loc}
sense to a perimeter minimizing set
E\subset\mathsf{X}\times\mathbb{R}
. Observe moreover that if
(\mathsf{Z},\mathsf{d}_{z})
is the metric space realizing the convergence of the sets
\mathsf{X}_{i}
to
\mathsf{X}
, then
(\mathsf{Z}\times\mathbb{R},\mathsf{d}_{z}\times\mathsf{d}_{e})
realizes the convergence of the corresponding product spaces.

Combining this with the Kuratowski convergence in
\mathsf{Z}\times\mathbb{R}
of the boundaries of the sets
E_{i}
to the boundary of the set
E
given by Proposition
2.35
, we deduce that

\mathsf{Osc}_{\mathsf{x},r}(\partial E)\geq t

and

E\subset\mathsf{X}\times[-T,+\infty).

By the Half Space Property
E
must then be a perimeter minimizing set whose boundary is made of horizontal slices.

We claim that this implies that the boundary of
E
is made of a single horizontal slice, contradicting the oscillation condition. To this aim assume by contradiction that
\partial E
has more than
1
connected component.

Suppose first that
\mathsf{X}
is compact. In this case subtracting from
E
its lowest connected component we obtain a competitor for
E
with strictly less perimeter, a contradiction. So we can suppose that
\mathsf{X}
has infinite diameter.

Let
C>0
be the distance between the lowest and the second lowest connected component of
\partial E
. We claim that there exists
s>0
such that

CP(B_{s}(\mathsf{x}),\mathsf{X})<2\mathfrak{m}(B_{s}(\mathsf{x})).

(26)

Recall that, by coarea formula, the function
s\mapsto\mathfrak{m}(B_{s}(\mathsf{x}))
is absolutely continuous and satisfies
\frac{d}{ds}\mathfrak{m}(B_{s}(\mathsf{x}))=P(B_{s}(\mathsf{x}))
for a.e.
s>0
.
Hence, if (
23
) fails for every
s>0
, it would follow that
\frac{d}{ds}\mathfrak{m}(B_{s}(\mathsf{x}))\geq 2C^{-1}\mathfrak{m}(B_{s}(\mathsf{x}))
and thus
\mathfrak{m}(B_{s}(\mathsf{x}))
grows exponenentially in
s
.
This contradicts the fact that
\mathsf{X}
has a modulus of parabolicity.

So let
s_{0}>0
be a value satisfying (
26
), let
\mathsf{X}\times(a,a+C)\subset E
be the lowest connected component of
E
(up to replacing
E
with its complement, we may assume that the lowest connected component has this form), let
\epsilon>0
be small enough so that
\mathsf{X}\times(a,a+C+\epsilon)\cap E=\mathsf{X}\times(a,a+C)
and consider the set

A:=(\mathsf{X}\setminus B_{s_{0}}(\mathsf{x}))\times(a,a+C).

Observe that
A\Delta E\subset\subset B_{s_{0}+1}(\mathsf{x})\times(a-\epsilon,a+C+\epsilon)
, while at the same time
we have that

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle(A,B_{s_{0}+1}(\mathsf{x})\times(a-\epsilon,a+C+\epsilon))

\displaystyle=CP(B_{s_{0}}(\mathsf{x}),\mathsf{X})+P(\mathsf{X}\times(a,a+C),(B_{s_{0}+1}(\mathsf{x})\setminus\bar{B}_{s_{0}}(\mathsf{x}))\times(a-\epsilon,a+C+\epsilon))

\displaystyle<2\mathfrak{m}(B_{s_{0}}(\mathsf{x}))+P(\mathsf{X}\times(a,a+C),(B_{s_{0}+1}(\mathsf{x})\setminus\bar{B}_{s_{0}}(\mathsf{x}))\times(a-\epsilon,a+C+\epsilon))

\displaystyle=P(E,B_{s_{0}+1}(\mathsf{x})\times(a-\epsilon,a+C+\epsilon)),

contradicting the fact that
E
is a perimeter minimizer.
∎

The next corollary implies Theorem
3
when
K=0
.

Corollary 5.9
.

Fix
K\in\mathbb{R}
,
N\in(1,+\infty)
and assume that the Half Space Property holds for
\mathsf{RCD}(K,N)

P
be a modulus of parabolicity and let

. For every
t,r,T>0
there exists
R(N,K,P,T,t,r)>0
such that for every weighted manifold
(\mathsf{M}^{n},\mathsf{d}_{g},e^{-V}\mathfrak{m}_{g},\mathsf{x})
with

, convex boundary, modulus of parabolicity
P
and such that

\mathsf{Ric}_{\mathsf{M}}+\mathsf{Hess}_{V}-\frac{\nabla V\otimes\nabla V}{N-n}\geq K\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ on }\mathsf{M}\setminus\partial\mathsf{M},

and for every area minimizing boundary
S

(\mathsf{x},0)

Proof.

It follows immediately by the previous theorem and Proposition

.

From the proof of Theorem
5.8
one sees that Theorem
3
probably holds without the parabolic volume constraint. This would be true if one could prove an analogue of Theorem
2.36
replacing the Euclidean space with
\mathsf{X}\times\mathbb{R}
where
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
is an
\mathsf{RCD}(0,N)
space. Such result seems plausible but, to the best of our knowledge, at the moment it is not available even when
\mathsf{X}
is a Riemannian manifold with non-negative Ricci curvature.

In Theorem
5.8
asking for the parabolicity of the spaces involved without requiring the existence of a common modulus of parabolicity is not enough, as the next example shows.

Example 5.10
.

Consider a bounded non constant solution
u
of the minimal surface equation in the hyperbolic space
(\mathbb{H}^{2},g,x)
. Such solution exists thanks to
[
62
]
. We claim that for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
there exists a metric
g_{i}
on
B^{g}_{2i}(x)
which coincides with
g
on
B^{g}_{i}(x)
, and such that
(B^{g}_{2i}(x),g_{i})
is complete, parabolic and satisfies
\mathsf{Ric}_{(B^{g}_{2i}(x),g_{i})}\geq-1
.

If we are able to do so, then
\mathsf{Graph}(u)\cap B^{g}_{i}(x)\times\mathbb{R}\subset(B^{g}_{i}(x),g_{i})
will be area minimizing in
B_{i}^{g_{i}}(x)
for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
, but these minimal submanifolds will have uniformly bounded oscillation, showing that Theorem
5.8
fails in this setting.

To construct the metric
g_{i}
we first consider a smooth surjective function
f_{i}:[0,+2i)\to[0,+\infty)

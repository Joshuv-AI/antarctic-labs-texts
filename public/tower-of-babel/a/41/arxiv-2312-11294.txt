1.
Introduction

Let
\{P_{n}(z;t,N)\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
be a family of monic polynomials satisfying the orthogonality relations

(1.1)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}z^{k}P_{n}(z;t,N){\mathrm{e}}^{-NV(z;t)}{\mathrm{d}}z=0\quad\text{for}\quad k=0,1,...,n-1,

where
N\geq 0

V(z;t)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\dfrac{1}{4}z^{4}+\dfrac{t}{2}z^{2}.

Polynomials

In this work, we obtain large-degree asymptotics of

depends on the choice of the complex parameter

[
53
]
*Theorem 2.3 and
[

*Theorem 3 that the normalized counting measures of zeros of

\mu_{t}
supported on a finite union of at most three analytic arcs. The regions labeled

in Figure

where the measure

is supported on one, two, or three analytic arcs, respectively. Remarkably, Figure

1.1.
Connection to Painlevé-IV

Our interest in these polynomials stems from their intimate connections with
special function solutions
of Painlevé-IV. While generic solutions of Painlevé equations are highly transcendental, all but Painlevé-I possess special solutions written in terms of elementary and/or classical special functions. We will focus our attention on the fourth Painlevé equation, given by

(
\textnormal{P}_{IV}
)

\mathinner{\dfrac{\dif{{}^{2}}u}{\dif{x^{2}}}}=\dfrac{1}{2u}\left(\mathinner{\dfrac{\dif{}u}{\dif{x}}}\right)^{2}+\dfrac{3}{2}u^{3}+4xu^{2}+2(x^{2}+1-2\Theta_{\infty})u-\dfrac{8\Theta_{0}^{2}}{u},\quad\Theta_{0},\Theta_{\infty}\in{\mathbb{C}}.

It is well-known that, for particular choices of parameters, Painlevé-IV possesses both rational solutions and solutions written in terms of parabolic cylinder functions, see e.g.
[
6
,
51
,
50
,
54
,
62
,
69
]
. The latter occur whenever any of following conditions hold:

\Theta_{0}\pm\Theta_{\infty}=0\quad\text{or}\quad\Theta_{0}\pm\Theta_{\infty}=1.

Starting with one such solution as a “seed” function and iterating Bäcklund transformations produces families of special function solutions of Painlevé-IV. As an application of our results, we show that a particular family of special solutions of Painlevé-IV have asymptotically pole-free regions in the complex plane. This is done by realizing poles of solutions of Painlevé-IV as zeros of the Hankel determinants associated with the polynomials
P_{n}(z;t,N)
:

(1.5)

H_{n-1}(t,N)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\det\left[\mu_{i+j}(t,N)\right]_{i,j=0}^{n-1},\quad\mu_{k}(t,N)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}s^{k}{\mathrm{e}}^{-NV(s;t)}{\mathrm{d}}s.

The strategy employed in this work for proving the existence of asymptotically pole-free regions of special function solutions of Painlevé equations is not new. Some of the early implementations of this strategy were to study rational solutions of Painlevé-II in
[
8
]
and the Painlevé-II hierarchy in
[
3
]
. Later, this was applied to Airy solutions of Painlevé-II in
[
5
,
4
]
. A different approach which has been extremely successful in studying the asymptotics and pole distribution of special function solutions of Painlevé equations is the recasting of the equations as conditions of isomonodromic deformation for a related
2\times 2
system of differential equations. Indeed, this method was applied to study rational solutions of Painlevé-II in
[
22
,
23
]
, rational solutions of Painlevé-III in
[
19
,
20
]
, and rational solutions of Painlevé-IV in
[
24
,
25
]
. Many of the studies above were motivated by the intricate behavior of the poles of special solutions of Painlevé equations which had been observed for, say, Painlevé-II and Painlevé-IV in e.g.
[
32
,
31
,
27
,
46
,
33
]
.

The relationship between
P_{n}(z;t,N)
,
\gamma_{n}^{2}(t,N)
, and solutions to Painlevé equations is well-documented in the literature. In
[
64
]
, Shohat derived differential equations for polynomials orthogonal with respect to semi-classical weights which include the weight in (
1.1
). Furthermore, he showed that
\gamma_{n}^{2}(t,N)
satisfy discrete version of Painlevé-I:

(1.6)

\gamma_{n}^{2}(t,N)\left(t+\gamma_{n-1}^{2}(t,N)+\gamma_{n}^{2}(t,N)+\gamma_{n+1}^{2}(t,N)\right)=\dfrac{n}{N}.

Equation (
1.6
) reappeared in
[
44
]
, where it was observed that the recurrence coefficients can be written in terms of parabolic cylinder functions. Magnus revisited the semi-classical weights and re-derived (
1.6
) in
[
55
,
56
]
. Polynomials

satisfying (
1.4

[

43
,
29

, where it was shown that the corresponding recurrence coefficients are given in terms of Wronskians of parabolic cylinder functions and are thus related to special function solutions of Painlevé-IV. One can immediately see that a change-of-variables symmetrizes the weight of orthogonality in (
1.4

to a family of orthogonal polynomials

satisfying

(1.7)

\int_{\mathbb{R}}x^{k}S_{n}(x;t,\lambda)|x|^{2\lambda+1}{\mathrm{e}}^{-(x^{4}+tx^{2})}{\mathrm{d}}x=0\quad\text{for}\quad k=0,1,...,n-1,\quad\lambda>-1,

and contains
P_{n}(z;t,N)
as a special case. Indeed, a re-scaling of variables in (

(1.8)

P_{n}(x;t,N)=\left(2^{-\frac{1}{2}}N^{\frac{1}{4}}\right)^{-\text{deg}S_{n}}S_{n}\left(2^{-\frac{1}{2}}N^{\frac{1}{4}}x;-N^{\frac{1}{2}}t,-\frac{1}{2}\right).

The connection between
S_{n}(x;t,\lambda)
and Painlevé-IV was worked out and various properties of the polynomials
S_{n}(x;t,\lambda)
were proved in
[
28
,
30
]
. We note that the weight of orthogonality in (
1.1
) can also be interpreted as a Freud weight (when

) for which there is a massive literature; see
[
48
,
57
]
,
[
58
]
*Section 18.32, and references therein.

1.2.
Quartic matrix model

Another important application of the polynomials
P_{n}(x;t,N)
is to the quartic one-matrix model, which is defined by the distribution

(1.9)

\frac{1}{{\mathcal{Z}}_{n}(u,N)}{\mathrm{e}}^{-N\mathrm{Tr}\left(\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{M}^{2}+\frac{u}{4}\mathbf{M}^{4}\right)}{\mathrm{d}}\mathbf{M},

where
\mathbf{M}
is an
n\times n
Hermitian matrix,
u>0
, and
\mathcal{Z}_{n}(u,N)
is the associated partition function. The quartic model was first investigated in
[
21
,
10
]
, where it was observed that when
n=N
, the associated free energy admits an asymptotic expansion in inverse powers of
N^{-2}
and that the coefficient of
N^{-2g}
, as functions of
u
, serve as generating functions for the number of four-valent graphs on a Riemann surface of genus
g
. This expansion is known as the
topological expansion
, and its existence and interpretation of its coefficients was proven in
[
41
]
for one-cut, even polynomial potentials satisfying a “small-coefficients” assumption, see
[
41
]
*Equation (1.3) and Theorem 1.1. One-cut, even polynomial potentials were revisited in
[
17
]
and the topological expansion was proven using different methods for potentials which remain in the one-cut regime after certain deformations. In fact, an asymptotics expansion of the free energy in the boundary case
t=-2
was also obtained in
[
17
]
. The asymptotics of the partition function in the two-cut region were obtained in
[
26
]
.

Remark 1.1
.

An analogous model, known as the cubic model, was also investigated in
[
21
]
and is connected with the enumeration of 3-valent graphs on Riemann surfaces. This corresponds to replacing the exponent in (
1.9
) by
\mathcal{V}(\mathbf{M})=\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{M}^{2}+\frac{u}{3}\mathbf{M}^{3}
, and results in the replacement of the potential
V(z)
by the polynomial
\tilde{V}(z)=z^{3}/3+tz^{2}/2
. The reader might immediately object since the integrals in (
1.1
) and the total mass of (
1.9
) are not convergent. To make sense of the model, one needs to deform the contour of integration to the complex plane, at which point the model loses its interpretation as an ensemble of random Hermitian matrices. However, the enumeration property still holds as was shown in
[
16
]
. Later, the connection between the partition function and solutions of Painlevé-II was established in
[
5
]
and further expanded on in
[
4
]
. In many ways, this manuscript can be viewed as the analog of
[
5
]
for the quartic model.
∎

In this contribution, we content ourselves with the asymptotic analysis of
P_{n}(z;t,N)

\tau-
and
\sigma

A.3

2.2.
Potential theory and S-curves

It is well understood that the zeros of polynomials satisfying (
1.1
) asymptotically distribute as a certain

on an
S-contour
corresponding to the weight function (
1.2

Definition 2.2
.

Let
V
be an entire function. The logarithmic energy in the external field
\Re V
of a measure
\nu

is defined as

E_{V}(\nu)=\iint\log\frac{1}{|s-t|}\mathrm{d}\nu(s)\mathrm{d}\nu(t)+\int\Re V(s)\mathrm{d}\nu(s).

The equilibrium energy of a contour
\Gamma

\Re V

(2.6)

\mathcal{E}_{V}(\Gamma)=\inf_{\nu\in\mathcal{M}(\Gamma)}E_{V}(\nu),

where
\mathcal{M}(\Gamma)
denotes the space of Borel probability measures on
\Gamma

When
\Re V(s)-\log|s|\to+\infty

\Gamma\ni s\to\infty

of
\Gamma

\Re V

\mu_{\Gamma}

[
63

\mu_{\Gamma}

\Gamma

(2.7)

U^{\mu_{\Gamma}}(z)+\dfrac{1}{2}\Re V(z)\;\left\{\begin{aligned} &=\ell_{\Gamma},\qquad z\in J_{\Gamma},\\
&\geq\ell_{\Gamma},\qquad z\in\Gamma\setminus J_{\Gamma},\end{aligned}\right.

where
\ell_{\Gamma}
is a constant, the Lagrange multiplier, and
U^{\mu}(z)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=-\int\log|z-s|{\mathrm{d}}\mu(s)
is the logarithmic potential of
\mu

[
63

7.
Conclusion

In this work, we studied orthogonal polynomials with a generalized Freud weight depending on a parameter
t
and whose recurrence coefficients are parabolic cylinder solutions of Painlevé-IV. We obtain leading term asymptotics of the orthogonal polynomials and their recurrence coefficients in the so-called two-cut region and, as a consequence, established asymptotically pole-free regions for the corresponding special function solutions of Painlevé-IV.

Feynman checkers: number-theoretic properties
F. Kuyanov, A. Slizkov

Abstract
We study Feynman checkers, an elementary model of electron motion introduced by R. Feynman. In this model, a checker moves on a checkerboard, and we count the turns. Feynman checkers are also known as a one-dimensional quantum walk. We prove some new number-theoretic results in this model, for example, sign alternation of the real and imaginary parts of the electron wave function in a specific area. All our results can be stated in terms of Young diagrams, namely, we compare the number of Young diagrams with an odd and an even number of steps.



Keywords:
Young diagram, Feynman checkers, quantum walk, dip

Informally, a Young diagram is a set of checkered stripes aligned by their left sides, so that their lengths increase from top to bottom (see Figure

The number of steps

This question turns out to be highly nontrivial (see Figure

Formally, a

is the number of distinct integers among

Theorem 1

If

Theorem 2

For any integer

the number of Young diagrams of size

with an odd number of steps exceeds the one with an even number of steps, if and only if

is
1
,
2
,
3
, or

modulo
8
.

Remark 1

In Theorem

As a next step, it is natural to study the

Background

Let us give the definition of Feynman checkers. See
[

,

Definition 1
.

(see
[
7
, Definition 2]
)
Fix
\varepsilon>0
and
m\geq 0
called
lattice step
and
particle mass
respectively. Consider the lattice
\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}^{2}=\{(x,t):x/\varepsilon,t/\varepsilon\in\mathbb{Z}\}
. A
checker path

s
is a finite sequence of lattice points such that the vector from each point (except the last one) to the next one equals either
(\varepsilon,\varepsilon)
or
(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)
. Denote by
\mathrm{turns}(s)
the number of points in
s
(not the first or the last one) such that the vectors from the point to the next and the previous ones are orthogonal. For each
(x,t)\in\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, where
t>0
, the
wave function
is

\displaystyle a(x,t,m,\varepsilon)

\displaystyle:=(1+m^{2}\varepsilon^{2})^{(1-t/\varepsilon)/2}\,i\,\sum_{s}(-im\varepsilon)^{\mathrm{turns}(s)},

(1)

where sum is over all checker paths
s
from
(0,0)
to
(x,t)
containing
(\varepsilon,\varepsilon)
. Denote

\displaystyle P(x,t,m,\varepsilon)

\displaystyle:=|a(x,t,m,\varepsilon)|^{2},

\displaystyle\tilde{a}_{1}(x,t,m,\varepsilon)

\displaystyle:=\operatorname{Re}a(x,t+\varepsilon,m,\varepsilon),

\displaystyle\tilde{a}_{2}(x,t,m,\varepsilon)

\displaystyle:=\operatorname{Im}a(x+\varepsilon,t+\varepsilon,m,\varepsilon).

Hereafter we omit the argument
\varepsilon
, if
\varepsilon=1
; we omit both arguments
m
and
\varepsilon
, if
m=\varepsilon=1
.

One interprets
P(x,t,m,\varepsilon)
as the probability to find an electron of mass
m
in the square
\varepsilon\times\varepsilon
with the center
(x,t)
, if the electron has been emitted from the origin. Notice that the value
m\varepsilon
, hence

, is dimensionless in the natural units, where
\hbar=c=1
.

Let us make the connection between Feynman checkers for

and Young diagrams. As in Figure

(0,0)

with an odd number of turns assign the Young diagram obtained by drawing the lines from
(0,0)

(x,t)

\mathrm{turns}(s)
modulo 4 affects both the parity of the number of steps in the resulting Young diagram and the sign of the corresponding term in (
1

is 1 modulo 4, the number of steps is odd, and the sign of the corresponding term is “+”. Thus the value

can be interpreted as the difference between the number of Young diagrams of size
\frac{t-x}{2}\times\frac{t+x}{2}
with an odd and an even number of steps respectively.

Proposition 1

[

\displaystyle\tilde{a}_{1}(2,4n+3)

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2^{(4n+3)/2}}\left(\binom{2n}{n}-\binom{2n}{n+1}\right).

Theorem 3

\displaystyle b_{k}(x)

\displaystyle:=(-1)^{\frac{t-x+k\varepsilon}{2\varepsilon}-1}\,\tilde{a}_{k}(x,t,m,\varepsilon).

\displaystyle b_{k}(x-2\varepsilon)

\displaystyle>b_{k}(x)\geq 0\quad\text{for}\quad\frac{t}{\sqrt{1+m^{2}\varepsilon^{2}}}+\varepsilon<x\leq t+\varepsilon.

For

\displaystyle b_{1}(x-2\varepsilon)=\frac{2x((1+2m^{2}\varepsilon^{2})(x^{2}-\varepsilon^{2})-t^{2})\,b_{1}(x)-(x-\varepsilon)(t^{2}-(x+\varepsilon)^{2})\,b_{1}(x+2\varepsilon)}{(x+\varepsilon)(t^{2}-(x-\varepsilon)^{2})}\geq

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2x((1+2m^{2}\varepsilon^{2})(x^{2}-\varepsilon^{2})-t^{2})-(x-\varepsilon)(t^{2}-(x+\varepsilon)^{2})}{(x+\varepsilon)(t^{2}-(x-\varepsilon)^{2})}\,b_{1}(x)>b_{1}(x).

Here the first equality follows from Proposition

. Now let us check the last inequality. Since

\displaystyle 2x((1+2m^{2}\varepsilon^{2})(x^{2}-\varepsilon^{2})-t^{2})-(x-\varepsilon)(t^{2}-(x+\varepsilon)^{2})>(x+\varepsilon)(t^{2}-(x-\varepsilon)^{2}).

After expansion and simplification using that

\displaystyle t^{2}<(1+m^{2}\varepsilon^{2})(x^{2}-\varepsilon^{2}).

The resulting inequality is equivalent to
x^{2}>\frac{t^{2}}{1+m^{2}\varepsilon^{2}}+\varepsilon^{2}
, which can be obtained from the assumption
x\geq\frac{t}{\sqrt{1+m^{2}\varepsilon^{2}}}+\varepsilon
by squaring.

Let us prove

similarly. The induction base (
x=t
) follows from the first and third equations in Example
1
by applying the following estimate:

\displaystyle\frac{t}{\varepsilon}>\frac{\sqrt{1+m^{2}\varepsilon^{2}}}{\sqrt{1+m^{2}\varepsilon^{2}}-1}=1+\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+m^{2}\varepsilon^{2}}-1}>1+\frac{1}{1+m^{2}\varepsilon^{2}-1}=1+\frac{1}{m^{2}\varepsilon^{2}},

\displaystyle b_{2}(x-2\varepsilon)=\frac{2x((1+2m^{2}\varepsilon^{2})(x^{2}-\varepsilon^{2})-t^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})\,b_{2}(x)-(x-\varepsilon)((t-\varepsilon)^{2}-(x+\varepsilon)^{2})\,b_{2}(x+2\varepsilon)}{(x+\varepsilon)((t+\varepsilon)^{2}-(x-\varepsilon)^{2})}\geq

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2x((1+2m^{2}\varepsilon^{2})(x^{2}-\varepsilon^{2})-t^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})-(x-\varepsilon)((t-\varepsilon)^{2}-(x+\varepsilon)^{2})}{(x+\varepsilon)((t+\varepsilon)^{2}-(x-\varepsilon)^{2})}\,b_{2}(x)>b_{2}(x).

Here the first equality follows from Proposition
3
. The next inequality holds by the inductive hypothesis and the non-negativity of
(t-\varepsilon)^{2}-(x+\varepsilon)^{2}
. Now let us check the last inequality. Since
(t+\varepsilon)^{2}-(x-\varepsilon)^{2}>0
, it is equivalent to

\displaystyle 2x((1+2m^{2}\varepsilon^{2})(x^{2}-\varepsilon^{2})-t^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})-(x-\varepsilon)((t-\varepsilon)^{2}-(x+\varepsilon)^{2})>(x+\varepsilon)((t+\varepsilon)^{2}-(x-\varepsilon)^{2}).

After expansion and simplification using that

\displaystyle t^{2}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}t}{x}<(1+m^{2}\varepsilon^{2})(x^{2}-\varepsilon^{2}).

The resulting inequality is equivalent to

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon^{2}t/x}{1+m^{2}\varepsilon^{2}}<\frac{\varepsilon^{2}\sqrt{1+m^{2}\varepsilon^{2}}}{1+m^{2}\varepsilon^{2}}=\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{\sqrt{1+m^{2}\varepsilon^{2}}}\leq\frac{2\varepsilon t}{\sqrt{1+m^{2}\varepsilon^{2}}}.

Dips positions

Let us give a precise definition of dips, which describes the positions of the minimal oscillation of the function
\tilde{a}_{1}(x,t,m,\varepsilon)
for fixed

Definition 2
.

Fix real
w
and
d
called
dip width
and
depth exponents
respectively. For a positive integer
T
, a point
v\in\left(-\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+m^{2}}};\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+m^{2}}}\right)
is called a
dip of order
T
of the function
\tilde{a}_{1}(x,t,m)

x_{t}
satisfying

\displaystyle x_{t}=vt+o(t^{w})\qquad\text{as $t\to\infty$},

(4)

Theorem 5
.

Assume

and

\displaystyle v=\frac{\sin(\pi k/T)}{\sqrt{m^{2}\varepsilon^{2}+\sin^{2}(\pi k/T)}}\quad\text{for $k\in\mathbb{Z}\cap\left(-\frac{T}{2};\frac{T}{2}\right)$}.

Remark 3
.

Formula (
6
) has a curious physical interpretation. Consider
\omega_{p}:=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\arccos{\frac{\cos{p\varepsilon}}{\sqrt{1+m^{2}\varepsilon^{2}}}}
, which is equal to the energy of an electron with momentum
p
in Feynman checkers (see
[
7
, after Proposition 12]
). The right side of (
6
) is obviously
\frac{\partial\omega_{p}}{\partial p}\rvert_{p=\frac{\pi k}{T\varepsilon}}
, which corresponds to the velocity of an electron with momentum
p
due to the Hamilton–Jacobi equation
\frac{\partial\omega_{p}}{\partial p}=\frac{dx}{dt}
. Since
\hbar=1
in our units, the de Broglie wavelength is
2\pi/p
. Thus the dips occur for those electron velocities which correspond to de Broglie wavelengths being rational multiplies of
\varepsilon
. In other words, the dips are explained by electron diffraction on the lattice
\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.

In addition to the physical meaning, this result allows us to prove the sharpness of the lower bound in Theorem
3
.

\displaystyle:=x_{t}-2T,

\displaystyle\delta_{t}^{-}=\delta_{t}^{-}(x_{t})

\displaystyle\gamma_{t}=\gamma_{t}(x_{t})

Lemma 2

Assume

Lemma 3
.

Assume (
4

and

converges. Moreover, the limit is 0 in the metric

fulfills (

Proof.

We can rewrite

\displaystyle\theta(x,t,m)=tL(x/t)+\frac{\pi}{4}\quad\text{for some}\quad L(v)\in C^{2}\left(-\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+m^{2}}};\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+m^{2}}}\right).

Write the Taylor expansion of

\displaystyle\theta(x,t,m)=\theta(vt,t,m)-t\left(\frac{x}{t}-v\right)\arcsin\frac{mv}{\sqrt{1-v^{2}}}+O_{\delta_{v},m}\left(t\left(\frac{x}{t}-v\right)^{2}\right).

Lemma 4
.

For every integer
T\geq 1
, real

,

Assume the converse: there is

for every integer sequence

fulfilling (

\displaystyle\delta_{t}^{+}=2\theta(vt,t,m)+(2vt-x_{t}-x^{\prime}_{t})\arcsin\frac{mv}{\sqrt{1-v^{2}}}+o(1).

(8)

Take integer sequences

Proof of Theorem

Set

Proof of Proposition
5
.

Without loss of generality set
\varepsilon=1
. By Theorem
5
and formula (
6
) it follows that the set of dips of an odd order is dense on the interval
\left(-\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+m^{2}}};\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+m^{2}}}\right)
. Therefore, there is a dip

of order
T\mathrel{\not\vdots}2
.

By Lemma
4

there is a sequence

. By Lemma

we have

\displaystyle\tilde{a}_{1}(x_{t},t,m)\neq o(t^{-1/2}).

Since

\displaystyle\tilde{a}_{1}(x_{t},t,m)-\tilde{a}_{1}(x_{t}-2T,t,m)=o(t^{-1/2}).

By the latter two estimates it follows that there is an arbitrarily large

\displaystyle\mathrm{sgn}(\tilde{a}_{1}(x_{t},t,m))=\mathrm{sgn}(\tilde{a}_{1}(x_{t}-2T,t,m)).

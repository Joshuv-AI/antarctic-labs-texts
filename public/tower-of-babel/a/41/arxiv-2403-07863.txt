Spectral invariants for non-compactly supported Hamiltonians on the disc, and an application to the mean action spectrum
Barney Bramham, Abror Pirnapasov

Abstract
. For a symplectic isotopy on the two-dimensional disc we show that the classical spectral invariants of Viterbo [ 20 ] can be extended in a meaningful way to non-compactly supported Hamiltonians. We establish some basic properties of these extended invariants and as an application we show that Hutchings’ inequality in [ 8 ] between the Calabi invariant and the mean action spectrum holds without any assumptions on the isotopy; in [ 8 ] it is assumed that the Calabi invariant is less than the rotation number (or action) on the boundary.



1.
Introduction

We begin with the application. In
[
8
]
Hutchings used embedded contact homology to prove a striking inequality relating the mean action spectrum with the Calabi invariant of
a Hamiltonian isotopy of the disc. Recall that if
\tilde{\varphi}=\{\varphi_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
is a smooth symplectic isotopy on a closed two-dimensional symplectic disc
(\mathbb{D},\omega)
,
starting at the identity
\varphi_{0}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{D}}
, then the Calabi invariant
\mathrm{CAL\,}(\tilde{\varphi})\in\mathbb{R}
can be defined as an average action of
\tilde{\varphi}
, or equivalently as the integral

\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{D}}H\,\omega\,dt
of the unique generating Hamiltonian
H:\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{R}
that has been
normalized to vanish pointwise on the boundary of the disc. Unless stated otherwise, all objects are smooth.

Theorem 1

(Hutchings’ inequality
[
8
]
)
.

Consider a symplectic isotopy
\tilde{\varphi}=\{\varphi_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
on the closed symplectic disc
(\mathbb{D},\omega_{0})
, normalized to have area
1

1
1

1

Some such normalization is necessary, as

\mathrm{CAL\,}(\tilde{\varphi})
has different dimensions to the action
\sigma_{\tilde{\varphi},\lambda}
. Alternatively, in the statement and throughout the paper, one could replace the Calabi invariant by the spatial average
\frac{1}{\int_{\mathbb{D}}\omega_{0}}\mathrm{CAL\,}(\tilde{\varphi})
and replace
a
by the average
\frac{1}{\int_{\partial\mathbb{D}}\lambda_{0}}\int_{\partial\mathbb{D}}\sigma_{\tilde{\varphi},\lambda_{0}}\lambda_{0}
.
. Let
a:=\overline{\sigma}_{\tilde{\varphi}}|_{\partial\mathbb{D}}\in\mathbb{R}
denote the mean action on the boundary. If

(1)

\mathrm{CAL\,}(\tilde{\varphi})<a

then

(2)

\inf\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{mean}}(\tilde{\varphi})\ \leq\ \mathrm{CAL\,}(\tilde{\varphi})

where
\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{mean}}(\tilde{\varphi})
is the set of all mean actions at interior periodic points of
\varphi
, also called the mean spectral values. See
2.1
for precise definitions.

Note that
a
is also the rotation number of
\tilde{\varphi}
restricted to
\partial\mathbb{D}
, which is how the statement is formulated in
[
8
]
. This agreement is due to the normalization of the action function chosen in (
10
), see also Remark
6
. In
[
8
]
it is assumed that the time-
1
map
\varphi
is a rigid rotation on a neighborhood of the boundary of the disc. This boundary
condition was later removed by the second author, see
[
16
]
.

Our main application in this article is that assumption (
1
) can be removed:

Theorem 2
.

For any symplectic isotopy
\tilde{\varphi}=\{\varphi_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
on the disc
(\mathbb{D},\omega_{0})
, normalized to have unit area, there holds

\inf\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{mean}}(\tilde{\varphi})\ \leq\ \mathrm{CAL\,}(\tilde{\varphi}).

This result can also be applied to the inverse isotopy
\tilde{\varphi}^{-1}=\{\varphi^{-1}_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
, something which could not be done from the original statement, because condition (
1
)
can never hold simultaneously for
\tilde{\varphi}
and
\tilde{\varphi}^{-1}
. Therefore:

Corollary 1
.

For any symplectic isotopy
\tilde{\varphi}=\{\varphi_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
on the disc
(\mathbb{D},\omega_{0})
, normalized to have unit area, the associated Calabi invariant and the mean spectral values
satisfy

(3)

\inf\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{mean}}(\tilde{\varphi})\ \leq\ \mathrm{CAL\,}(\tilde{\varphi})\ \leq\ \sup\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{mean}}(\tilde{\varphi}).

In
[
11
]
Le Calvez proved inequality (
3
) for
C^{1}
pseudo-rotations that are a rotation on the boundary using his method of transverse foliations. In this case the inequalities are equalities.

Let us point out that we do not give a new proof of Theorem
1
:
The proof in
[
8
]
ultimately relies on deep connections
between the ECH spectrum and contact volume in three dimensions, see
[
4
]
, and as far as we are aware no alternative proof is known; either using tools from dynamical systems or more classical Floer-theoretic methods. An approach using periodic Floer homology (PFH) is also not known,
despite connections between the PFH spectrum and the Calabi invariant
[
3
]
. One difficulty is that the twisted PFH action is different to the Hamiltonian action. However there are some related results: In
[
17
]
the second author and Prasad used PFH to prove a weaker version of (
3
) for
C^{\infty}
-generic Hamiltonian
diffeomorphisms of compact surfaces, possibly with boundary; in
[
21
,
13
]
Weiler and Nelson-Weiler use ECH to obtain results similar to Theorem
1
for symplectic diffeomorphisms of the annulus and for higher-genus surfaces with one boundary component.

1.1.
Overview of extending the spectral invariants

Recall that the classical spectral invariants
[
20
]
are a pair of continuous sections of the action spectrum
\tilde{\varphi}\mapsto c_{-}(\tilde{\varphi})
and
\tilde{\varphi}\mapsto c_{+}(\tilde{\varphi})
,
that are defined on any smooth Hamiltonian isotopy
\tilde{\varphi}=\{\varphi_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
on the standard symplectic vector space
(\mathbb{R}^{2n},\omega_{0})
, provided the isotopy can be generated by a
compactly supported, time-dependent Hamiltonian
H\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R}^{2n},\mathbb{R})
. This is recalled more precisely in
2.2
.
The
action spectrum
of
\tilde{\varphi}
refers to the set of actions of the
1
-periodic orbits:

(4)

\mathcal{S}(\tilde{\varphi}):=\big\{\mathcal{A}_{H}(p)\in\mathbb{R}\ \big|\ p\in\mathrm{Fix}(\varphi_{1})\big\}

where the action is defined by (
11
). The spectral invariants
c_{-}(\tilde{\varphi})
and
c_{+}(\tilde{\varphi})
enjoy many nice properties recalled in Theorem
6
.
In particular
c_{-}(\tilde{\varphi})
and
c_{+}(\tilde{\varphi})
depend only on the time-one map
\varphi_{1}
and they depend continuously with respect to the Hofer metric.

To extend
c_{+}
and
c_{-}
to a symplectic isotopy
\tilde{\varphi}
on the disc, not necessarily compactly supported in the interior, we use a limiting procedure along the
following lines:
\tilde{\varphi}
is uniquely generated by a time-dependent Hamiltonian function on
\mathbb{D}
that vanishes pointwise on the boundary
\partial\mathbb{D}
for all times, even though in general it
is not supported in the interior
\dot{\mathbb{D}}=\mathbb{D}\backslash\partial\mathbb{D}
. Thus we can extend the Hamiltonian function continuously to
H:\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
by setting
H\equiv 0
on the complement

\mathbb{R}^{2}\backslash\mathbb{D}
. Thus
H
is continuous everywhere, and smooth away from the circle
\partial\mathbb{D}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
Then we take a sequence of smooth approximations
H_{n}:\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
with compact support, which converge to
H
(only) in a
C^{0}
-sense. The spectral invariants for each
H_{n}

are well defined and one can define

c_{\pm}(\tilde{\varphi}):=\lim_{n\to\infty}c_{\pm}(H_{n}).

Indeed, it follows easily from the continuity properties with respect to Hofer’s norm, that the above limits exist and are independent of the choice of approximating sequence
H_{n}
. The limits also trivially inherit
a number of fundamental properties listed in Proposition
12
, including the non-triviality property that at least one of
c_{+}(\tilde{\varphi})
and
c_{-}(\tilde{\varphi})
is non-zero if
\varphi\neq\mathrm{Id}
.
However, two highly desirable properties are not obvious, because the convergence of the Hamiltonians is not in
C^{1}
:

(1)

First, that
c_{+}(\tilde{\varphi})
and
c_{-}(\tilde{\varphi})
depend only on the time-one map - or rather on the
homotopy class of
\tilde{\varphi}
with fixed ends.

(2)

Secondly, that the values
c_{+}(\tilde{\varphi})
,
c_{-}(\tilde{\varphi})

are indeed spectral values, i.e. that they represent the actions of fixed points of the time-one map.

Note that for the classical spectral invariants, i.e. for compactly supported Hamiltonians on
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
, one usually shows the first property after establishing the second together with the
continuity properties. But this strategy doesn’t work here as the second of the desired properties above will no longer be true in general. What remains true, is that
c_{+}(\tilde{\varphi})
,
c_{-}(\tilde{\varphi})
lie
in a discrete set, which is enough. We will show:

Theorem 3
.

Let
\tilde{\varphi}=\{\varphi_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
be a symplectic isotopy on the disc
(\mathbb{D},\omega)
with area
A:=\mathrm{area}_{\omega}(\mathbb{D})
(not necessarily supported in
\mathbb{D}\backslash\partial\mathbb{D}
).
Then the extended definition of spectral invariants
c_{+}(\tilde{\varphi}),c_{-}(\tilde{\varphi})
depend only on the homotopy class of
\tilde{\varphi}
with fixed ends, and

(5)

c_{+}(\tilde{\varphi})\in\mathcal{S}(\tilde{\varphi})\cup\{0\}\cup\{A\},

and

(6)

c_{-}(\tilde{\varphi})\in\mathcal{S}(\tilde{\varphi})\cup\{0\}\cup\{-A\}

where
\mathcal{S}(\tilde{\varphi})\subset\mathbb{R}
is the set of actions at the
1
-periodic orbits in
\mathbb{D}
.

Example 1
.

There are simple examples on
(\mathbb{D},\omega)
where
c_{\pm}(\tilde{\varphi})\notin\mathcal{S}(\tilde{\varphi})
, i.e. where the spectral invariants are not spectral values.
Indeed, consider the case that
\tilde{\varphi}
is the path of rotations on
\mathbb{D}
about the origin through a total angle
\rho\in\mathbb{R}
.
Then the single fixed point (at the origin) has action
A\rho
. On the other hand, from standard properties we will see that

c_{+}(\tilde{\varphi})\in[0,A]
. Thus
c_{+}(\tilde{\varphi})
cannot be a spectral value if
\rho>1
or if
\rho<0
. Similarly
c_{-}(\tilde{\varphi})
cannot be a spectral
value in this example if either
\rho>0
or
\rho<-A
.

In contrast to Example
1
, if the isotopy does not make more than a full rotation on the boundary of the disc, then
at least one of the spectral invariants is a spectral value, i.e. represents the action of a fixed point, which is important for existence results. More precisely:

Theorem 4
.

Let
\tilde{\varphi}=\{\varphi_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
be a symplectic isotopy on the disc
(\mathbb{D},\omega)
, as in Theorem
3
,
and denote by
\rho:=\rho({\tilde{\varphi}}|_{\partial\mathbb{D}})
the rotation number on the boundary. Then:

i)

If
-1<\rho\leq 1
then
c_{+}(\tilde{\varphi})\in\mathcal{S}(\tilde{\varphi})\cup\{0\}
.

ii)

If
0\leq\rho\leq 1
then
c_{+}(\tilde{\varphi})\in\mathcal{S}(\tilde{\varphi})
.

iii)

If
-1\leq\rho<1
then
c_{-}(\tilde{\varphi})\in\mathcal{S}(\tilde{\varphi})\cup\{0\}
.

iv)

If
-1\leq\rho\leq 0
then
c_{-}(\tilde{\varphi})\in\mathcal{S}(\tilde{\varphi})
.

By Example
1
this statement is in some sense optimal.

In proving Theorem
4
we also use the following fact, which may be of independent interest:

Proposition 5
.

For a symplectic isotopy on the disc
\tilde{\varphi}=\{\varphi_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
with rotation number
\rho:=\rho({\tilde{\varphi}}|_{\partial\mathbb{D}})
on the boundary, we have:

\rho>0
implies
c_{+}(\tilde{\varphi})>0
, while
\rho<0
implies
c_{-}(\tilde{\varphi})<0
.

Remark 1
.

We do not carry out any analogous program of extending the spectral invariants in higher dimensions. This is a much more challenging problem
and is not needed for our application.

Remark 2
.

Note that the extension of the spectral invariants in this article is of a different nature to the
C^{0}
-extensions of the spectral invariants in

[
15
,
19
,
2
,
9
]
.
Indeed, in these works
the goal is to extend the spectral invariants to Hamiltonian homeomorphisms, i.e. the
C^{0}
-topology referred to is on the time-one maps, while in this article the

C^{0}
-convergence is on the level of the Hamiltonians.
In particular, in general the time-one maps on the plane undergo a discontinuity in the limit, namely on the boundary of the disc.

Remark 3
.

In
[
8
]
Hutchings’ inequality was stated for the mean action spectrum, rather than the
interior
mean action spectrum
\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{mean}}(\tilde{\varphi})
. This distinction is trivial in the statement of
Theorem
1
because of the assumption (
1
). But it is relevant to Theorem
2
and to Corollary
1
. It also
has bearing on Remark 1.4 from
[
8
]
, where it is noted that the approach in
[
8
]
does not seem to prove the reverse inequality (i.e. the second inequality in (
3
))
and that this reverse inequality may be less interesting since, when the rotation number on the boundary is rational, the periodic points on the boundary
will trivially satisfy the reverse inequality. Hence we emphasize that Theorem
2
and Corollary
1
refer to
interior
periodic points, which make the statements somewhat more interesting.

Remark 4
.

The spectral invariants of Viterbo were extended by Schwarz and Oh
[
18
,
14
]
to Hamiltonian diffeomorphisms on
closed symplectic manifolds using Hamiltonian Floer theory. They were further extended by Frauenfelder-Schlenk
[
5
]
and Lanzat
[
10
]
to compact
symplectic manifolds
(M,\omega)
with contact type boundary (also called convex boundary). In these latter cases the Hamiltonians are compactly supported in
M\backslash\partial M
, in contrast, we allow non-compactly supported Hamiltonians, albeit restricted to this
2
-dimensional setting.

1.2.
Proof of Theorem
2

Here we show how to remove the assumption on the Calabi invariant (
1
), by combining Theorem
1
of Hutchings’
with the properties of the extended spectral invariants described above. We begin with the following Corollary to Theorem
4
:

Corollary 2
.

Consider a symplectic isotopy
\tilde{\varphi}=\{\varphi_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
as in Theorem
3
. Then the mean action on the boundary
a:=\overline{\sigma}_{\tilde{\varphi}}|_{\partial\mathbb{D}}\in\mathbb{R}
lies in the closure of the interior mean action spectrum:

(7)

a\in\overline{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathrm{mean}}(\tilde{\varphi}).

Proof.

It suffices to show that
\varphi
has an interior fixed point
x
satisfying

(8)

|\sigma_{\tilde{\varphi}}(x)-\pi k|\leq\pi

where
k\in\mathbb{Z}
is unique so that
a\in[k\pi,(k+1)\pi)
. Indeed, then applying (
8
) to each iterate
\tilde{\varphi}^{j}
yields a sequence of
j
-periodic points
x_{j}\in\mathbb{D}\backslash\partial\mathbb{D}
with action
|\sigma_{\tilde{\varphi}^{j}}(x)-\pi k_{j}|\leq\pi
, where
k_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}
is such that
aj\in[k_{j}\pi,(k_{j}+1)\pi)
, which after dividing through by
j
implies

\lim_{j\to\infty}\overline{\sigma}_{\tilde{\varphi}}(x_{j})=\lim_{j\to\infty}\pi k_{j}/j=a
as required.

To prove (
8
), first suppose
a\in[0,\pi)
, i.e.
k=0
.
If
\tilde{\varphi}
is isotopic, with fixed ends, to the identity isotopy, then
a=0
is also the action of each interior fixed point of
\tilde{\varphi}
, so that (
7
) is trivially true.
If
\tilde{\varphi}
is not isotopic, with fixed ends, to the identity, then at least one of
c_{+}(\tilde{\varphi})
and
c_{-}(\tilde{\varphi})
is non-zero and so, due to our assumption on
a
, it follows from items (i) or (iii) in Theorem
4
that this non-zero spectral invariant
is represented by the action at a fixed point
x
. This fixed point cannot be on the boundary; if it were, it would have action zero, whereas
x
has non-zero action. Therefore
|\sigma_{\tilde{\varphi}}(x)|=|\mathcal{A}_{H}(x)|
equals
|c_{+}(\tilde{\varphi})|\leq\pi
or
|c_{-}(\tilde{\varphi})|\leq\pi
. Thus (
8
) holds, as
k=0
in this case.

Now suppose
a\notin[0,\pi)
, i.e.
k\neq 0
. Let
\tilde{R}_{2\pi k}
denote the isotopy from the identity to the identity that rotates a total of
k
times around the origin:

\tilde{R}_{2\pi k}=\{R_{2\pi kt}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
. Then the mean action on the boundary of
\tilde{R}_{-2\pi k}\circ\tilde{\varphi}
is
a-\pi k\in[0,\pi)
, so that we can apply
the argument above to the isotopy
\tilde{R}_{-2\pi k}\circ\tilde{\varphi}
and conclude that
\varphi
has an interior fixed point
x
satisfying
|\sigma_{\tilde{R}_{-2\pi k}\circ\tilde{\varphi}}(x)|\leq\pi
,
in other words,
|\sigma_{\tilde{\varphi}}(x)-\pi k|\leq\pi
. Thus (
8
) holds in general.
∎

.

Note that inequality (
8
) can be interpreted as a quantitative version of Brouwer’s fixed point theorem. Curiously, applying
it to the inverse isotopy can yield a slightly different inequality, depending on the boundary behavior.

.

Let

Preliminaries

2.1.
The action and Calabi invariant for symplectic isotopies on the disc

In the following
\omega
is a smooth symplectic form on the closed disc
\mathbb{D}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
, so without loss of generality
\omega=cdx\wedge dy
for some constant
c>0
.
We will denote by
\omega_{0}
the normalization for which
\int_{\mathbb{D}}\omega_{0}=1
. By a
symplectic isotopy
on
(\mathbb{D},\omega)
we mean a smooth path
\tilde{\varphi}=\{\varphi_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
of diffeomorphisms
\varphi_{t}:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
, starting at the identity map
\varphi_{0}=\mathrm{Id}
,
with
\varphi_{t}^{*}\omega=\omega
for each
t\in[0,1]
. Note that we are not assuming these maps are the identity on or near to the boundary of the disc.
Given a smooth primitive
\lambda
of
\omega
, the
action function
of
\tilde{\varphi}

\sigma=\sigma_{\tilde{\varphi},\lambda}:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{R}

is defined to be the unique solution to

(9)

\displaystyle d\sigma=\varphi^{*}\lambda-\lambda\qquad\ \ \ \ \mbox{on }\mathbb{D}

(10)

\displaystyle\sigma(z)=\int_{t\mapsto\varphi_{t}(z)}\lambda\qquad\mbox{for all }z\in\partial\mathbb{D}.

It is not hard to show that
\sigma_{\tilde{\varphi},\lambda}
exists, is unique, and depends only on the isotopy class of
\tilde{\varphi}
with fixed ends,
[
1
,
12
]
.
At fixed points
\sigma_{\tilde{\varphi},\lambda}
is independent of
\lambda
, so we may write
\sigma_{\tilde{\varphi}}(z)
.
The
mean action
at a
k
-periodic point
z\in\mathbb{D}
of
\varphi
is defined by
\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\sigma_{\tilde{\varphi},\lambda}(\varphi^{j}(z))

and is independent of
\lambda
, since it coincides with
(1/k)\sigma_{\tilde{\varphi}^{k},\lambda}(z)
, being the value at a fixed point of
\varphi^{k}
.
More generally, by the ergodic theorem, the

asymptotic mean action function

\overline{\sigma}_{\tilde{\varphi}}:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{R},\qquad\overline{\sigma}_{\tilde{\varphi}}(z):=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\sigma_{\tilde{\varphi},\lambda}(\varphi^{j}(z))

is well defined almost everywhere on
\mathbb{D}
and at the periodic points. It is also not hard to show that
\overline{\sigma}_{\tilde{\varphi}}
is independent of
\lambda
,
see Lemma 2.2 in
[
1
]
, and that it is defined at each point on the boundary
\partial\mathbb{D}
, where it is constant and coincides with
\mathrm{area}(\mathbb{D},\omega)\rho
, where
\rho\in\mathbb{R}
is the rotation number
of
\tilde{\varphi}
restricted to the boundary of the disc.

Finally, we define the
interior mean action spectrum
of
\tilde{\varphi}
to be the set of mean actions at interior periodic points:

\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{mean}}(\tilde{\varphi}):=\Big\{\ \overline{\sigma}_{\tilde{\varphi}}(z)\in\mathbb{R}\ \Big|\ z\in\mathbb{D}\backslash\partial\mathbb{D},\ \ \varphi^{k}(z)=z\mbox{ some }k\in\mathbb{N}\ \Big\}.

Another important quantity is the space average of the action:

\mathrm{CAL\,}_{\omega}(\tilde{\varphi}):=\int_{\mathbb{D}}\sigma_{\tilde{\varphi},\lambda}\,\omega,

called the Calabi invariant, which is infact independent of the primitive
\lambda
.

Remark 6
.

The validity of Hutchings’ inequality is independent of whether we add a constant to the action function
\sigma_{\tilde{\varphi},\lambda}
. In other words, the
choice of the boundary condition (
10
) is irrelevant.

2.2.
The classical spectral invariants on
(\mathbb{R}^{2n},\omega_{0})

Let
\omega_{0}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}dx_{i}\wedge dy_{i}
be the standard symplectic structure on
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
and
\lambda_{0}
the primitive
\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big(x_{i}dy_{i}-y_{i}dx_{i}\big)
.
Consider a smooth and time-dependent Hamiltonian function
H:\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R}^{2n}\to\mathbb{R}
with compact support.

For any smooth loop
x\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{R}^{2n})
the associated action is defined by

(11)

\mathcal{A}_{H}(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}x^{*}\lambda_{0}\ +\ \int_{0}^{1}H(t,x(t))\,dt.

Formally the critical points of
\mathcal{A}_{H}
correspond to the
1
-periodic orbits of the time-dependent Hamiltonian vector field defined by
i_{X_{H^{t}}}\omega=dH^{t}
,
where
H^{t}(\cdot)=H(t,\cdot)
. These sign conventions are convenient for two reasons; first, signs of rotation numbers of trajectories are aligned with the sign of the
Hamiltonian - see for example the statement of Proposition
5
; secondly, so that the action function
\mathcal{A}_{H}
on the loop space agrees with the action function
from the previous section at periodic points in the following sense: Suppose
n=1
and
H^{t}=0
on
\partial\mathbb{D}
for each
t\in[0,1]
. Then
X_{H^{t}}
is tangent to
\partial\mathbb{D}
,
and so it generates a symplectic isotopy
\tilde{\varphi}=\{\varphi_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
on
\mathbb{D}
. With these sign conventions, at each fixed point
p\in\mathbb{D}
of the time-one map
\varphi
,
we have

\sigma_{\tilde{\varphi},\lambda_{0}}(p)=\mathcal{A}_{H}(x)

where
x
is the
1
-periodic orbit starting at
p
, i.e.
x(t)=\varphi_{t}(p)
. For a proof see
[
12
]
or
[
1
]
.
More generally, the set

\mathcal{S}(H):=\big\{\mathcal{A}_{H}(x)\ \big|\ x\in\mathcal{P}_{1}(H)\big\}.

where
\mathcal{P}_{1}(H)
is the collection of
1
-periodic trajectories of
H
in
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
, are called the
spectral values
of
H
.
We also write
\mathcal{S}(\tilde{\varphi})
instead of
\mathcal{S}(H)
, see (
4
), where
\tilde{\varphi}
is the path of Hamiltonian diffeomorphisms generated by

.

Spectral invariants were first introduced by Viterbo
[
20
]
. See also the so called spectral selector in Hofer-Zehnder
[
6
]
.
The fundamental properties are contained in the following statement:

Theorem 6

(
[
20
]
)
.

For any compactly supported Hamiltonian diffeomorphism
\varphi=\varphi_{H}
generated as the time-
1
map of
H\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R}^{2n})
the
spectral invariants

and
c_{-}(\varphi)
have the following fundamental properties:

(1)

c_{+}(\varphi^{-1})=-c_{-}(\varphi)
.

(2)

c_{-}(\varphi)\leq 0\leq c_{+}(\varphi)
.

(3)

Definition 7

Another important feature of the spectral invariants is the following well-known monotonicity property, which is typically used to prove Theorem

Theorem 8

(
[

]
)

If

In this section we explain how to construct the spectral invariants for a non-compactly supported Hamiltonian on the disc and show that many of the usual properties

Throughout this section
H\in C^{\infty}\big(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{D}\big)

Now set:

For any approximating sequence

This definition agrees with the original one if

If

The proof is trivial:

Proof.

For each

\displaystyle|c_{+}(H_{j})-c_{+}(H_{k})|\

For the proofs of Theorems

Lemma 13
.

Suppose
H\in C^{\infty}\big(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{D}\big)
vanishes identically on
\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\times\partial\mathbb{D}
. Then there exists an approximating sequence
H_{n}
for

which satisfies

(1)

\mathrm{supp\,}H_{n}\subset\mathbb{D}_{1+1/n}
,

(2)

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|\nabla H_{n}\|_{C^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\backslash\mathbb{D})}<\infty
.

Note that if the supports converge to
\mathbb{D}
then this is the best we can expect; in general the second order derivatives of the Hamiltonians
H_{n}
will blow up.

Proof.

Fix a smooth function
\rho:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying
\rho(r)=r
on
r\leq 0
,
\rho(r)\geq 0
on
r\geq 0
, and
\rho(r)=0
on
r\geq 1
. Then for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
the
function
\rho_{n}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
given by

\rho_{n}(r):=\frac{\rho(nr)}{n}

satisfies:
\rho_{n}(r)=r
on
r\leq 0
,
\rho_{n}(r)=0
on
r\geq 1/n
, while on
r\geq 0
we have
\rho_{n}\geq 0
,
\|{\rho_{n}}^{\prime}\|_{C^{0}}\leq\|{\rho}^{\prime}\|_{C^{0}}
and
\|{\rho_{n}}\|_{C^{0}}\leq\|{\rho}\|_{C^{0}}/n
.
Fix any smooth extension of
H
to the whole plane, and denote this also by
H
. In polar coordinates this is a map

H:\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\times[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R},(t,r,\theta)\mapsto H(t,r,\theta)
. Now set

(15)

H_{n}(t,1+r,\theta):=H(t,1+\rho_{n}(r),\theta)\qquad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

Then
H_{n}(t,\cdot)
coincides with
H(t,\cdot)
on the unit disc in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
because
\rho_{n}(r)=r
on

, and
H_{n}
is everywhere smooth. Moreover,

H_{n}
has compact support, indeed,

vanishes outside of
\mathbb{D}_{1+1/n}
because
\rho_{n}(r)
vanishes on

and

vanishes
\partial\mathbb{D}
. The
\theta
-derivative is
independent of

so it suffices to bound the
r
-derivative: From the chain rule

\|\partial_{r}H_{n}\|_{C^{0}}\leq\|\nabla H\|_{C^{0}}\|{\rho_{n}}^{\prime}\|_{C^{0}}\leq\|\nabla H\|\|{\rho}^{\prime}\|_{C^{0}}

is uniformly bounded in

. Finally
\|H_{n}(t,\cdot)\|_{C^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\backslash\dot{\mathbb{D}})}\to 0
for

\displaystyle\sup\big\{|H_{n}(t,1+r,\theta)|\ \big|\ 0\leq r\big\}\

\displaystyle=\ \sup\big\{|H(t,1+\rho_{n}(r),\theta)|\ \big|\ 0\leq r\big\}

\displaystyle=\ \sup\big\{|H(t,1+r,\theta)|\ \big|\ 0\leq r\leq\|{\rho_{n}}\|_{C^{0}}\big\}

which decays to zero as

, because
\|{\rho_{n}}\|_{C^{0}}\leq\|{\rho}\|_{C^{0}}/n\to 0
and

is continuous and vanishes on
[0,1]\times\partial\mathbb{D}
.
∎

.

extends to non-compactly supported Hamiltonians:

Lemma 14
.

Suppose

on

cannot hold for smooth extensions to the plane. So there is something small to check.

Assume

and let

and

be approximating sequences with supports in

as in Lemma
13
.
Let

on the disc

of radius

. For
\epsilon>0
sufficiently small

is
sufficiently

-small that

.

For

sufficiently large we have

and

.

Explicit description of Christoffel deformations and Palm measures of the Plancherel measure, the z z -measures and the Gamma process
Pierre Lazag

Abstract
. Christoffel deformation of a measure on the real line consists of multipying this measure by a squared polynomial having its roots in ℝ \mathbb{R} . We introduce Christoffel deformations of discrete orthogonal polynomial ensembles by considering the Christoffel deformations of the underlying measure, and prove that this construction extends to more general point processes describing distribution on partitions: the poissonized Plancherel measure and the z z -measures. These deformations contain the theory of Palm measures, and for example, explicit formulas for the Palm measures of the poissonized Plancherel measure provide a description of the TASEP with initial wedge condition with frozen particles. We also obtain new formulas for Palm measures of the z z -measures. The extension to the Plancherel measure is obtained via a limit transition from the Charlier ensemble, while the extension to the z z -measures follows from an analytic continuation argument. A limit procedure starting from the non-degenerate z z -measures leads to a deformation of the Gamma process introduced by Borodin and Olshanski.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Random partitions as point processes

The aim of this article is to study deformations of discrete determinantal point processes describing distributions or limit distributions on partitions, namely the poissonized Plancherel measure, the
z
-measures and its limiting Gamma process (see e.g.
[
6
]
,
[
24
]
,
[
29
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
9
]
and
[
11
]
). Recall that a partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq...)
is a finite non-increasing sequence of non-negative integers, and that it can be identified with a Young diagram (
[
28
]
). The set of all partitions will be denoted by
\mathbb{Y}
. As it is usual, for a partition
\lambda\in\mathbb{Y}
, we define its length
l(\lambda)
as being the index of its last non-zero entry

\displaystyle l(\lambda):=\max\{i,\hskip 2.84544pt\lambda_{i}\neq 0,\hskip 2.84544pti=1,2,...\},

and denote its size by
|\lambda|

\displaystyle|\lambda|:=\sum_{i\geq 1}\lambda_{i}.

A Young diagram
\lambda\in\mathbb{Y}
can be seen as a subset of
\mathbb{Z}
via the map
\lambda\mapsto\{\lambda_{i}-i,i=1,2,...\}
or some shifted version of it. More precisely, when we consider partitions
\lambda\in\mathbb{Y}
with fixed length
N
, it is convenient to consider the finite set of positive integers

\displaystyle\{\lambda_{i}-i+N,\hskip 2.84544pti=1,...,N\},

while when the lengths may vary, it is convenient to consider the infinite sets

\displaystyle\{\lambda_{i}-i,\hskip 2.84544pti=1,2,...\}\text{ or }\{\lambda_{i}-i+1/2,\hskip 2.84544pti=1,2,...\},

the latter providing a more symmetric picture. In any case, the pushforward of a probability measure on
\mathbb{Y}
by one of these maps is thus a point process on a discrete subset of
\mathbb{R}
. In the cases we deal with, namely the poissonized Plancherel measure, the
z
-measures and its limiting process with the Gamma kernel, these point processes are determinantal point processes, see definition
2.3
below.

1.2.
Determinantal point processes and Christoffel deformations

A specific and important class of determinantal point processes are the so called
orthogonal polynomial ensembles
, see e.g. the survey
[
26
]
and references therein. They are
N
-points random configurations for some fixed deterministic
N
governed by the kernel of the orthogonal projection onto the first
N
polynomials in the Hilbert space
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mu)
for some measure
\mu
with finite moments of all orders. This kernel is namely the
N
-th Christoffel-Darboux kernel of the polynomials orthogonal with respect to
\mu
. For example, the classical G.U.E. from random matrix theory is the orthogonal polynomial ensemble with the Gaussian weight on
\mathbb{R}
, and is thus described by the Hermite polynomials, see e.g.
[
26
]
and references therein.

Multiplying the weight
\mu
by a squared polynomial having its roots in
\mathbb{R}
leads to the so-called Christoffel deformation of the system and an explicit description of the new family of orthogonal polynomials as well as the corresponding deformed point process.

1.3.
Background and motivations: characteristic polynomials and Palm measures

We prove in this note that the multiplicative structure of Christoffel deformations of orthogonal polynomial ensembles carries on to more general determinantal point processes. Our method is essentially by approximation of the models in consideration by orthogonal polynomial ensembles. However, since from
[
17
]
we know that any determinantal point process on the real line is approximated by orthogonal polynomial ensemble in a particular sense, we hope to establish the existence and the explicit descritption of Christoffel deformations of determinantal point processes in full generality in a future work; this method has already been fruitful in order to prove the Giambelli compatibility of determinantal point processes in
[
18
]
.

Besides being a natural deformation of a point process, there are two directions in which Christoffel deformations have applications: characteristic polynomials and Palm measures. Let us also mention that Christoffel deformations have been considered in
[
16
]
in order to describe the ergodic decomposition of inifinite Hua-Pickrell measures.

The study of charcteristic polynomials of random matrices has connections with number theory, see for example
[
25
]
or the more recent paper
[
20
]
. In
[
2
]
, explicit formulas for averages of products and ratios of characteristic polynomials of random matrices are obtained by the formalism of Christoffel deformations.

There are lots of studies of averages or more generally moments of characteristic polynomials in the continuous setting. For the discrete one, in
[
12
]
, the authors develop the notion of
Giambelli compatible point processes
which provides a suitable formalism in the study of characteristic polynomials of general point processes. Among others, the authors show that the
z
-measures are Giambelli compatible, which means that characteristic polynomials of these processes are well defined and involve determinantal formulas.

Rather than directly studying characteristic polynomials, from which determinantal formulas for the correlations can be obtained (see
[
22
]
,
[
33
]
,
[
13
]
, and
[
12
]
), we go the reverse way and study Christoffel deformations of determinantal processes. Direct information on the characteristic polynomials of our processes of interest can be obtained from formulas from
[
2
]
,
[
22
]
or
[
33
]
. For instance, our results concerning the Charlier ensemble were used in
[
3
]
in order to compute the characteristic polynomial of the discrete Bessel point process (which describes the poissonized Plancherel measure, see section
3
below) and in connection with the Gromov-Witten theory of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
(
[
3
]
, Proposition 1.4).

On the other hand, the formalism of Christoffel deformations of orthogonal polynomial ensembles englobes the theory of Palm measures of the latter, see Proposition
2.7
below. Roughly speaking, the Palm measure of a given point process is the same point process conditioned to have particles at specific points. Our results thus provide new explicit and simple formulas for the Palm measures of the examples we are intersted in.

An eloquent example is the one of the Totally Asymmetric Exclusion Process (TASEP) with initial wedge condition, which we now informally describe. We put independent exponential clock at each site
x\in\mathbb{Z}
, and at time
t=0
, we put particles on the whole semi-axis
\mathbb{Z}_{<0}
. When a clock ring, the correponding particle moves to the site directly to the right of it, if this site is not occupied, and do not move else. A picture of this process at any time
t\geq 0
is precisely the image of the poissonized Plancherel measure with parameter
t
by the map

\displaystyle\lambda\mapsto\{\lambda_{i}-i,\hskip 2.84544pti=1,2,...\},

see for example
[
5
]
and references therein. Theorem
3.3
thus gives a description of the TASEP with ”frozen” sites, i.e. sites which must always be occupied, see Theorem
3.3
and remark
3.4
below.

In Theorem
4.4
, we give new and compact formulas for the Palm measures of the
z
-measures.

Our results also connect with those of
[
14
]
and
[
19
]
(see also
[
15
]
). In these papers, the author shows that conditioning at different points leads to equivalent point processes and that the Radon-Nikodym derivatives are explicitly given by regularized multiplicative functionals, analogous to the one given by Christoffel deformations.

The
z
-measures on partitions were introduced in
[
7
]
in order to describe the harmonic analysis of the infinite symmetric group. One of their interset indeed lies in the fact that they constitute a coherent system of measures on the Young graph. However, we were not able to prove whether the Christoffel deformations of the
z
-measures we introduce constitute a coherent system or not, but we believe that the study of their characteristic polynomial and conditional measures is of interest.

1.4.
Main results

As mentioned above, it is easy to define Christoffel deformations of orthogonal polynomial ensembles, but neither the Plancherel nor the
z
-measures are orthogonal polynomial ensembles. As for the characteristic polynomials, an obstacle is that, unlike for orthogonal polynomial ensembles, the number of points might be random or infinite. We still manage to give a meaning to Christoffel deformations of these processes, and also for the process with the Gamma kernel, and we now briefly explain our arguments and state our main results in an informal way.

On the one hand, we know from Johansson’s paper
[
24
]
that the poissonized Plancherel measure is the limit of the Charlier ensemble, i.e. the orthogonal polynomial ensemble with the Poisson weight. The poissonized Plancherel measure is a determinantal point process described by Bessel functions, the discrete Bessel kernel (
[
4
]
). Refining asymptotic results of
[
24
]
, we prove the convergence of the Charlier polynomials towards the Bessel functions, see Lemma
3.2
, and obtain the following Theorem, see Theorems
3.1
and
3.3
for precise statements.

Theorem
.

The Christoffel deformation of the Charlier ensemble converges to a determinantal point process described by discrete Wronskians of Bessel functions, the latter being a deformation of the poissonized Plancherel measure. The Palm measures of the poissonized Plancherel measure are described by similar kernels.

On the other hand, the
z
-measures are determinantal point processes described by specific Gauss hypergeometric functions. The analytic continuation argument presented in
[
11
]
also allows us to describe the deformation of all
z
-measures, a particular case of them being the Meixner ensemble, and we obtain the following Theorem, see Theorems
4.2
and
4.4
for precise statements.

Theorem
.

The Christoffel deformations of the
z
-measures are determinantal point proccesses governed by a kernel involving discrete Wronskians of Gauss hypergeometric functions. Palm measures of the
z
-measures are governed by similar kernels.

Moreover, a somehow tricky but elementary asymptotic analysis, similar to the one performed in
[
9
]
, leads to a deformation of the process with the Gamma kernel, see Theorem
4.5
.

Theorem
.

The one-point deformations of the non-degenerate
z
-measures converge to a determinantal point process described by the Gamma function and its first derivative, which might be considered as a deformation of the process with the Gamma kernel.

We strongly believe that the approximation procedure we use in the last two Theorems could also be applied to the
zw
-measures, since the same argument of analytic continuation from an orthogonal polynomial ensemble holds, see
[
8
]
.

1.5.
Organization of the paper

The paper is organized as follows. In section
2
, we collect definitions and basic properties of Christoffel deformations, orthogonal polnomial ensembles and determinantal point processes, and formally state that such deformations contain the description of their Palm measure.

In section
3
, we introduce the Charlier ensemble and the Plancherel measure. We describe the Christoffel deformation of the Charlier ensemble and pass to the limit, in the same regime as in
[
24
]
. This leads to a deformation of the poissonized Plancherel measure, i.e. the process with the discrete Bessel kernel, see Theorem
3.1
. We finally give the correlation kernel of the Palm measures of the discrete bessel point process.

In section
4
, we introduce the
z
-measures and define their Christoffel deformations. We describe the Christoffel deformations in the case where the parameters belong to the degenerate series (which is nothing but the Meixner ensemble) and prove that a similar determinantal formula also holds for the other series, see Theorem
4.2
. We further give a description of the Palm measures of the
z
-measures in Theorem
4.4
. We then pass to the limit towards the deformed Gamma kernel in the case of the one point deformation, see Theorem
4.5
.

2.
Christoffel deformations of orthogonal polynomial ensembles

2.1.
Notation

The set of non-negative integers is denoted by
\mathbb{N}
. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
a\in\mathbb{C}
,
(a)_{n}
is the Pochhammer symbol:

\displaystyle(a)_{0}=1\quad;\quad(a)_{n}:=a(a+1)...(a+n-1)=\frac{\Gamma(a+n)}{\Gamma(a)},\quad n=1,2,...

where
\Gamma
is the Euler Gamma function.

For
a_{1},a_{2},...,a_{p}\in\mathbb{C}
,
b_{1},...,b_{q}\in\mathbb{C}\setminus(-\mathbb{N})
the hypergeometric function
\left.{}_{p}F_{q}\right.(a_{1},...,a_{p};b_{1},...,b_{q};.)
is defined by

(1)

\displaystyle\left.{}_{p}F_{q}\right.(a_{1},...,a_{p};b_{1},...,b_{q};z)=\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\frac{(a_{1})_{n}...(a_{p})_{n}}{(b_{1})_{n}...(b_{q})_{n}}\frac{z^{n}}{n!},\quad z\in\mathbb{C},

provided the series converges. The function
\left.{}_{2}F_{1}\right.
is the Gauss hypergeometric function.

If
\lambda=(\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq...)\in\mathbb{Y}
is a partition and
a\in\mathbb{C}
is a complex number, we define the generelized Pochhammer symbol
(a)_{\lambda}
by:

\displaystyle(a)_{\lambda}:=\prod_{i=1}^{l(\lambda)}(a-i+1)_{\lambda_{i}}=\prod_{(i,j)\in\lambda}(a-i+j)

where we write

\displaystyle(i,j)\in\lambda\Leftrightarrow 1\leq j\leq\lambda_{i}.

2.2.
Christoffel deformations of discrete orthogonal polynomials

Let
\omega
be a discrete measure on
\mathbb{R}
with infinite support and finite moments of all orders:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}|x|^{n}d\omega(x)<+\infty\quad\text{for all $n\in\mathbb{N}$}.

We denote by
\{p_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
a family of orthogonal polynomials with respect to
\omega
. The number
c_{n}
stands for the leading coefficient of
p_{n}
, and its squared norm will be denoted by
h_{n}
:

\displaystyle p_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=c_{n}x^{n}+\text{terms of degree less than $n$},

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{n}(x)p_{m}(x)d\omega(x)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}0\quad\text{if $n\neq m$},\\
h_{n}\quad\text{if $n=m$}\end{cases}.

Let
k
be a non-negative integer and let
u_{1}
,…,
u_{k}
be pairwise distinct real numbers.

Definition 2.1
.

The Christoffel deformation
\omega^{k}
of
\omega
of order
k
at points
u_{1}
,…,
u_{k}
is the discrete measure on
\mathbb{R}
defined by:

\displaystyle d\omega^{k}(x)=\prod_{j=1}^{k}(x-u_{j})^{2}d\omega(x)

Let
\{p_{n}^{k}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be the family of monic orthogonal polynomials with respect to
\omega^{k}
. We have the following

Proposition 2.1
.

The following explicit formula holds:

\displaystyle p_{n}^{k}(x)=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{k}(x-u_{i})^{2}.\delta_{n}^{k}.c_{n+2k}\right)^{-1}D_{n}^{k}(x)

where

\displaystyle D_{n}^{k}(x)

\displaystyle=\begin{vmatrix}p_{n}(u_{1})&p_{n+1}(u_{1})&\dots&p_{n+2k}(u_{1})\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
p_{n}(u_{k})&\dots&\dots&p_{n+2k}(u_{k})\\
p^{\prime}_{n}(u_{1})&p^{\prime}_{n+1}(u_{1})&\dots&p^{\prime}_{n+2k}(u_{1})\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
p^{\prime}_{n}(u_{k})&\dots&\dots&p^{\prime}_{n+2k}(u_{k})\\
p_{n}(x)&p_{n+1}(x)&\dots&p_{n+2k}(x)\end{vmatrix};

\displaystyle\delta_{n}^{k}

\displaystyle=\begin{vmatrix}p_{n}(u_{1})&p_{n+1}(u_{1})&\dots&p_{n+2k-1}(u_{1})\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
p_{n}(u_{k})&\dots&\dots&p_{n+2k-1}(u_{k})\\
p^{\prime}_{n}(u_{1})&p^{\prime}_{n+1}(u_{1})&\dots&p^{\prime}_{n+2k-1}(u_{1})\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
p^{\prime}_{n}(u_{k})&p^{\prime}_{n+1}(u_{k})&\dots&p^{\prime}_{n+2k-1}(u_{k})\end{vmatrix}.

Its squared
l^{2}(\omega^{k})
-norm is given by

\displaystyle h_{n}^{k}=\frac{h_{n}\delta_{n+1}^{k}}{\delta_{n}^{k}.c_{n+2k}.c_{n}}

Proof.

We slightly adapt the proof from
[
23
]
, where it is assumed that the
u_{i}
are outside the support of the measure
\omega
, to the case when the measure
\omega
has infinite support, see also
[
34
]
and
[
2
]
. We first prove that
\delta_{n}^{k}\neq 0
. Let
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{2k}\in\mathbb{R}
be
2k
real numbers such that the polynomial

P(x):=\sum_{i=1}^{2k}\alpha_{i}p_{n+i-1}(x)

and its derivative vanish for
x\in\{u_{1},\dots,u_{k}\}
. Then there exists a polynomial
Q
of degree at most or equal to
n-1
such that

P(x)=Q(x)\prod_{i=1}^{k}(x-u_{i})^{2}.

Since the polynomials
p_{n+i-1}
,
i=1,\dots,2k
are orthogonal to any polynomial of degree less than or equal to
n-1
with respect to
\omega
, we have

(2)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}Q(x)P(x)d\omega(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}Q^{2}(x)d\omega^{k}(x)=0.

Since the support of the measure
\omega
is infinite, so is the support of the measure
\omega^{k}
, and thus Equation (
2
) implies
Q=0
and so
P=0
i.e.
\alpha_{1}=\dots=\alpha_{2k}=0
. This implies that
\delta_{n}^{k}\neq 0
.

The fact that
\delta_{n}^{k}\neq 0
tells us that
D_{n}^{k}(x)
is a polynomial of degree
n+2k
. Since
D_{n}^{k}(x)
and its derivative vanish for
x\in\{u_{1},\dots,u_{k}\}
, the rational function
p_{n}^{k}(x)
is in facts a monic polynomial of degree
n
.

Let
q(x)
be a polynomial of degree strictly less than
n
. From the orthogonality of the polynomials
p_{n}
with respect to
\omega
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{n}^{k}(x)q(x)d\omega^{k}(x)=\frac{1}{c_{n+2k}\delta_{n}^{k}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}D_{n}^{k}(x)q(x)d\omega(x)=0,

and the polynomials
p_{n}^{k}
are thus orthogonal with respect to
\omega^{k}
. The squared norm
h_{n}^{k}
is computed in
[
16
, Prop. 4.2.]
.
∎

2.3.
Discrete orthogonal polynomial ensembles and determinantal point processes

Let
E
be an infinite countable discrete subset of
\mathbb{R}
. We first recall the definition of an orthogonal polynomial ensemble in our setting. Let
\omega
be a discrete weight as in the previous subsection. We assume that its support lies inside
E
, and in all the sequel, we may identify a measure on
E
with its density with respect to the counting measure on
E
. Let
N
be a positive integer.

Definition 2.2
.

An
N
-orthogonal polynomial ensemble is a probability measure
\mathbb{P}_{N}
on
E^{N}
given by the following formula:

(3)

d\mathbb{P}_{N}(x_{1},...x_{N})=C_{N}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq N}(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2}\prod_{i=1}^{N}d\omega(x_{i})

where
C_{N}
is the normalization constant:

\displaystyle C_{N}^{-1}=\sum_{x_{1},...,x_{N}\in E}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq N}(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\omega(x_{i})

We now recall the definition of a determinantal point process on
E
. We denote by
\text{Conf}(E)
the space of configuration in
E
which is in this context simply the set of all subsets of
E
,

\displaystyle\text{Conf}(E):=2^{E}.

Definition 2.3
.

A
point process
is a probability measure
\mathbb{P}
on
\text{Conf}(E)
. It is said to be
determinantal
if there exists a function

\displaystyle K:E\times E\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

such that for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and any
\{a_{1},...,a_{n}\}\subset E
, one has

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(X\in\text{Conf}(E),\hskip 2.84544pt\{a_{1},...,a_{n}\}\subset X)=\det\left(K(a_{i},a_{j})\right)_{i,j=1}^{n}.

The function
K
is called the
correlation kernel
of the determinantal point process.

Remark 2.2
.

The correlation kernel of a given determinantal point process is not unique. Indeed, if one has a determinantal point process with kernel
K
, for any non-vanishing function
f:E\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
the function

\displaystyle\tilde{K}(x,y)=\frac{f(x)}{f(y)}K(x,y)

can also serve as a kernel for the same determinantal point process.

As a matter of fact, any orthogonal polynomial ensemble gives rise to a determinantal point process, what we resume in the following Proposition (see e.g.
[
26
]
and references therein). Note that since the measure (
3
) is symmetric and does not charge
N
-tuples which contain two equal coordinates, it can and it will be identified with a measure on the space of configurations.

Proposition 2.3
.

An orthogonal polynomial ensemble given by (
3
) is a determinantal point process on
E
. Its correlation kernel is given by the formula

\displaystyle K_{N}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\omega(x)\omega(y)}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\frac{p_{n}(x)p_{n}(y)}{h_{n}}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\sqrt{\omega(x)\omega(y)}\frac{c_{N-1}}{h_{N-1}c_{N}}\frac{p_{N}(x)p_{N-1}(y)-p_{N}(y)p_{N-1}(x)}{x-y},\quad\text{for $x\neq y$},\\
\omega(x)\frac{c_{N-1}}{h_{N-1}c_{N}}\left(p_{N}^{\prime}(x)p_{N-1}(x)-p_{N}(x)p_{N-1}^{\prime}(x)\right),\quad\text{for $x=y$}.\end{cases}

The second equality is just the Christoffel-Darboux formula, while the last one follows from L’Hospital rule.

Remark 2.4
.

Observe that
K_{N}
is the kernel of the orthogonal projection from
l^{2}(E)
(where
E
is equipped with the counting measure) onto

\displaystyle\sqrt{\omega}\mathbb{R}_{N-1}[x]=\{x\mapsto\sqrt{\omega(x)}p(x),\hskip 2.84544ptp\text{ is a polynomial of degree at most $N-1$}\}.

The main object of our study is given by the following definition.

Definition 2.4

(Christoffel deformations of orthogonal polynomial ensembles)
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
be a non-negative integer. Given an orthogonal polynomial ensemble
\mathbb{P}_{N}
with weight
\omega
, its
Christoffel deformation
of order
k
, denoted by
\mathbb{P}_{N}^{k}
, is the orthogonal polynomial ensemble with weight
\omega^{k}
.

The next Proposition gives a compact formula for the kernel of the Christoffel deformation which will be suitable for asymptotic analysis.

Proposition 2.5
.

The Christoffel deformation of order
k
,
\mathbb{P}_{N}^{k}
, is a determinantal point process with kernel
K_{N}^{k}
given by:

\displaystyle K^{k}_{N}(x,y)=\frac{\sqrt{\omega(x)\omega(y)}}{\prod_{i=1}^{k}|(x-u_{i})(y-u_{i})|}\frac{c_{N-1}}{h_{N-1}c_{N+2k}}\frac{D_{N}^{k}(x)D_{N-1}^{k}(y)-D_{N}^{k}(y)D_{N-1}^{k}(x)}{(\delta_{N}^{k})^{2}(x-y)}.

Proof.

This is a straightforward computation, using Propositions
2.3
and
2.1
. Indeed, Proposition
2.3
states that
\mathbb{P}_{N}^{k}
is a determinantal point process with kernel

\displaystyle K_{N}^{k}(x,y)=\sqrt{\omega^{k}(x)\omega^{k}(y)}\frac{p_{N}^{k}(x)p_{N-1}^{k}(y)-p_{N}^{k}(y)p_{N-1}^{k}(x)}{h_{N-1}^{k}(x-y)}.

By the definition of the measure
\omega^{k}
and using Proposition
2.1
and the explicit expression of the polynomials
p_{n}^{k}
, this kernel can be written as

K_{N}^{k}(x,y)\\
=\frac{\sqrt{\omega(x)\omega(y)}}{\prod_{i=1}^{k}|(x-u_{i})(y-u_{i})|}\frac{1}{\delta_{N}^{k}\delta_{N-1}^{k}c_{N+2k}c_{N+2k-1}h_{N-1}^{k}}\frac{D_{N}^{k}(x)D_{N-1}^{k}(y)-D_{N}^{k}(y)D_{N-1}^{k}(x)}{x-y}.

The expression for the squared norm
h_{N-1}^{k}
given in Proposition
2.1
leads to the desired result.
∎

2.4.
Palm measures of determinantal point processes

We here give a probabilistic interpretation of Christoffel deformations of orthogonal polynomial ensembles in the case when the points
u_{1},...,u_{k}
belong to the support of the measure
\omega
. We first recall the basics on Palm distributions of a general point process
\mathbb{P}
on a discrete space
E
, see for instance
[
21
]
,
[
32
]
or
[
15
]
and references therein for a more general treatment.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
\mathbb{P}
be a point process on the discrete set
E
. For pairwise distinct points
u_{1},...,u_{k}\in E
such that
\mathbb{P}(u_{1},...,u_{k}\in X)>0
, the
Palm measure

\hat{\mathbb{P}}^{u_{1},...,u_{k}}
of
\mathbb{P}
at
u_{1},...,u_{k}
is the point process
\mathbb{P}
on
E
conditionned to have particles at
u_{1},...,u_{k}
,

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbb{P}}^{u_{1},...,u_{k}}(\mathcal{B})=\frac{\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{B}\cap\{u_{1},...,u_{k}\in X\})}{\mathbb{P}(u_{1},...,u_{k}\in X)},

for any borel set
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathrm{Conf}(E)
. The
reduced Palm measure

\mathbb{P}^{u_{1},...,u_{k}}
is then defined as the pushforward of the Palm measure
\hat{\mathbb{P}}^{u_{1},...,u_{k}}
under the map

\displaystyle\mathrm{Conf}(E)

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathrm{Conf}(E)

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle\mapsto X\setminus\{u_{1},...,u_{k}\}.

In particular, if the point process
\mathbb{P}
has
N+k
particles, i.e.
\mathbb{P}(|X|=N+k)=1
, then its reduced Palm measure
\mathbb{P}^{u_{1},\dots,u_{k}}
has
N
particles.

We also recall the following Proposition from
[
32
]
.

Proposition 2.6
.

If
\mathbb{P}
is a determinantal point process with kernel
K
such that
\mathbb{P}(u_{1},...,u_{k}\in X)>0
, then, its reduced Palm measure at
u_{1},...,u_{k}
is a determinantal point process with kernel

\displaystyle K^{u_{1},...,u_{k}}(x,y)=\left(\det(K(u_{i},u_{j})\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq k}^{-1}\det\begin{pmatrix}K(x,y)&K(x,u_{1})&\dots&K(x,u_{k})\\
K(u_{1},y)&K(u_{1},u_{1})&\dots&K(u_{1},u_{k})\\
\vdots&&\ddots&\vdots\\
K(u_{k},y)&\dots&\dots&K(u_{k},u_{k})\end{pmatrix}.

acting on
l^{2}(E)
.

We now consider a measure
\omega
as before, with an infinite support lying in a discrete countable subset
E
of
\mathbb{R}
. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we denote by
\mathbb{P}_{n}
the corresponding orthogonal polynomial ensemble. Let
u_{1},...,u_{k}\in\text{supp}(\omega)
be pairwise distinct, and, as before, denote by
\mathbb{P}_{n}^{k}
the Christoffel deformation of
\mathbb{P}_{n}
at
u_{1},...,u_{k}
. The next Proposition formally establishes the fact that the study of Christoffel deformations of orthogonal polynomial ensembles contains the study of their Palm measures.

Proposition 2.7
.

For any natural
N\in\mathbb{N}
, we have the equality between point processes

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{N+k}^{u_{1},...,u_{k}}=\mathbb{P}_{N}^{k}.

Proof.

We know from
[
32
]
(see also
[
15
]
) that, for a determinantal point process with a kernel that is a kernel of an orthogonal projection onto a closed subspace
L\subset l^{2}(E)
, its reduced Palm measure at points
u_{1},...,u_{k}
is a determinantal point process with the kernel of orthogonal projection onto

\displaystyle L^{k}:=\{f\in L,\hskip 2.84544ptf(u_{1})=...=f(u_{k})=0\}.

Specializing this fact to the subspace

\displaystyle L:=\{\sqrt{\omega(x)}p(x),\hskip 2.84544ptp\in\mathbb{R}_{N+k-1}[x]\},

one obtains the statement of the Proposition. ∎

3.
The Charlier ensemble and a deformation of the Plancherel measure

3.1.
On the Plancherel measure on partitions

The Plancherel measure is a probability measure
\text{Pl}_{n}
on the set of Young diagrams of fixed size
n
,
\mathbb{Y}_{n}=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{Y},\hskip 2.84544pt|\lambda|=n\}
. It is given by

\displaystyle\text{Pl}_{n}(\lambda)=\frac{\dim(\lambda)^{2}}{n!},\quad\lambda\in\mathbb{Y}_{n},

where
\dim(\lambda)
is the dimension of the irreducible representation of the
n
-th symmetric group parametrized by
\lambda
, or equivalentely, the number of stantard Young tableaux of shape
\lambda
(see
[
28
]
or
[
31
]
). It is well known that the poissonization of the Plancherel measure is a determinantal point process. More precisely, let
\alpha>0
be a positive parameter and form the poissonized Plancherel measure
\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}
on
\mathbb{Y}
by imposing
n
to have the Poisson distribution with parameter
\alpha
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}(\lambda)=e^{-\alpha}\alpha^{|\lambda|}\left(\frac{\dim(\lambda)}{|\lambda|!}\right)^{2},\quad\lambda\in\mathbb{Y}.

For
\lambda\in\mathbb{Y}
, we set

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}^{0}(\lambda):=\{\lambda_{i}-i,\hskip 2.84544pti=1,2,...\}\in\text{Conf}(\mathbb{Z}).

Then the pushforward of
\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}
under the map
\mathfrak{S}^{0}
, which we call the
discrete Bessel point process
, is a determinantal point process with the discrete Bessel kernel

(4)

K_{\alpha}(x,y)=\sqrt{\alpha}\frac{J_{x}(2\sqrt{\alpha})J_{y+1}(2\sqrt{\alpha})-J_{y}(2\sqrt{\alpha})J_{x+1}(2\sqrt{\alpha})}{x-y},

where the Bessel functions
J_{x}(2\sqrt{\alpha}),\hskip 2.84544ptx\in\mathbb{Z}
are defined via their generating Laurent series (
[
1
]
):

(5)

e^{\sqrt{\alpha}(z-z^{-1})}=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}}J_{x}(2\sqrt{\alpha})z^{x}.

This definition leads to the expression

\displaystyle J_{x}(2\sqrt{\alpha})=\sum_{m=0}^{+\infty}\frac{(-1)^{m}}{m!\Gamma(m+x+1)}\alpha^{m+x/2}

that can be analytically continuated for all values of the indices
x\in\mathbb{C}
. We can thus introduce the derivatives of the Bessel functions with respect to their index

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dx}J_{x}(2\sqrt{\alpha})=:L_{x}(2\sqrt{\alpha}).

3.2.
Christoffel deformations of the Charlier ensemble and main result

We now define the Christoffel deformation of the Charlier ensemble. We know from
[
24
]
that this ensemble approximates the poissonized Plancherel measure, and the aim of this section is to generalize this fact by proving that Christoffel deformations of this ensemble admit a limit that is expressed through Bessel functions. Our discrete measure is

\displaystyle\omega(x)=\omega_{a}(x)=e^{-a}\frac{a^{x}}{x!},\quad x\in\mathbb{N},

where
a>0
is a parameter, and the
N
-th orthogonal polynomial ensemble that this measure defines is denoted by
\mathbb{P}_{N,a}
. The corresponding orthonormal polynomials are the Charlier polynomials (
[
23
]
,
[
1
]
):

\displaystyle C_{n}(x)=C_{n}(x;a)=\frac{a^{\frac{n}{2}}}{\sqrt{n!}}\left.{}_{2}F_{0}\right.(-n,-x;;-1/a)\hskip 2.84544pt,\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

As in the previous section, we define the Christoffel deformations of the Charlier ensemble. Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
be a non-negative integer, and
\tilde{u}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
be distinct numbers. Let
\alpha>0
be a positive real number, and for each
N\in\mathbb{N}
, consider the Christoffel deformation of the
N
-th Charlier ensemble at points
u_{1}=\tilde{u}_{1}+N,...,u_{k}=\tilde{u}_{k}+N
, with parameter
a=\alpha/N
, according to definition
2.4
. This determinantal point process is denoted by
\mathbb{P}_{N,\alpha/N}^{k}
, and its correlation kernel is denoted by
K_{N}^{\alpha/N,k}
. For an integer
p\in\mathbb{Z}
, we form the determinants:

\displaystyle A_{k,p}(x)

\displaystyle=\begin{vmatrix}J_{\tilde{u}_{1}+p}(2\sqrt{\alpha})&\dots&J_{\tilde{u}_{1}-2k+p}(2\sqrt{\alpha})\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
J_{\tilde{u}_{k}+p}(2\sqrt{\alpha})&\dots&J_{\tilde{u}_{k}-2k+p}(2\sqrt{\alpha})\\
L_{\tilde{u}_{1}+p}(2\sqrt{\alpha})&\dots&L_{\tilde{u}_{1}-2k+p}(2\sqrt{\alpha})\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
L_{\tilde{u}_{k}+p}(2\sqrt{\alpha})&\dots&L_{\tilde{u}_{k}-2k+p}(2\sqrt{\alpha})\\
J_{x+p}(2\sqrt{\alpha})&\dots&J_{x-2k+p}(2\sqrt{\alpha})\end{vmatrix},\quad x\in\mathbb{Z},

\displaystyle C_{k,p}

\displaystyle=\begin{vmatrix}J_{\tilde{u}_{1}+p}(2\sqrt{\alpha})&\dots&J_{\tilde{u}_{1}-2k+1+p}(2\sqrt{\alpha})\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
J_{\tilde{u}_{k}+p}(2\sqrt{\alpha})&\dots&J_{\tilde{u}_{k}-2k+1+p}(2\sqrt{\alpha})\\
L_{\tilde{u}_{1}+p}(2\sqrt{\alpha})&\dots&L_{\tilde{u}_{1}-2k+1+p}(2\sqrt{\alpha})\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
L_{\tilde{u}_{k}+p}(2\sqrt{\alpha})&\dots&L_{\tilde{u}_{k}-2k+1+p}(2\sqrt{\alpha})\end{vmatrix}.

The main result of this section is the following Theorem.

Theorem 3.1
.

In the regime described above, we have that, for all
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}
,
x\neq y
,

\displaystyle\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}K_{N}^{\alpha/N,k}(x+N,y+N)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha^{\frac{2k+1}{2}}}{C_{k,0}^{2}\prod_{i=1}^{k}|(x-\tilde{u}_{i})(y-\tilde{u}_{i})|}\frac{A_{k,0}(x)A_{k,1}(y)-A_{k,0}(y)A_{k,1}(x)}{x-y}.

When
x=y
, L’Hospital rule entails:

\displaystyle\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}K_{N}^{\alpha/N,k}(x+N,x+N)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha^{\frac{2k+1}{2}}}{C_{k,0}^{2}\prod_{i=1}^{k}(x-\tilde{u}_{i})^{2}}\left(A_{k,0}^{\prime}(x)A_{k,1}(x)-A_{k,0}(x)A_{k,1}^{\prime}(x)\right).

This implies that the Christoffel deformation of the Charlier ensemble
\mathbb{P}_{N,\alpha/N}^{k}
, shifted by
-N
, weakly converges, as a probability measure on
\text{Conf}(\mathbb{Z})
, to a determinantal point process governed by the limit correlation kernel of the Theorem. In particular, if we take
k=0
, one recovers Johansson’s result (
[
24
]
, Theorem 1.2).

3.3.
Proof of Theorem
3.1

We will use the following Lemma, which we prove at the end of this section. This Lemma gives simple asymptotic results on the Charlier polynomials involving Bessel functions and their derivatives. As far as we know, the asymptotics we give can not be found in the litterature in this simple form. However, one may consult
[
30
]
for a complete study of the asymptotics of Charlier polynomials.

Lemma 3.2
.

For any
l\in\mathbb{Z}
and any
u\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\sqrt{\omega_{\alpha/N}(u+N)}C_{N+l}(u+N;\alpha/N)=J_{u-l}(2\sqrt{\alpha}),

and

\displaystyle\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\sqrt{\omega_{\alpha/N}(u+N)}\left(C^{\prime}_{N+l}(u+n;\alpha/N)-\log\left(N/\sqrt{\alpha}\right)C_{N+l}(u+N;\alpha/N)\right)=L_{u-l}(2\sqrt{\alpha}).

The proof of Theorem
3.1
goes as follows. We use the formula from Proposition
2.5
for the kernel
K_{N}^{\alpha/N,k}
and proceed to the analysis of each factor. First, a straightforward computation shows that:

(6)

\frac{c_{N-1}}{c_{N+2k}}\rightarrow\alpha^{\frac{2k+1}{2}}

when
N\rightarrow+\infty
and where
c_{n}
is the leading coefficient of the Charlier polynomial
C_{n}
. We now analyse the ratio

\displaystyle\sqrt{\omega_{\alpha/N}(x+N)\omega_{\alpha/N}(y+N)}\frac{D_{N}^{k}(x+N)D_{N-1}^{k}(y+N)-D_{N}^{k}(y+N)D_{N-1}^{k}(x+N)}{\left(\delta_{N}^{k}\right)^{2}(x-y)}.

To this end, we multiply both sides of this ratio by

\displaystyle m:=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\omega_{\alpha/N}(\tilde{u}_{i}+N)^{2},

and in each determinant
D_{N}^{k}(.)
,
D_{N-1}^{k}(.)
and
\delta_{N}^{k}
, we perform the following operations on rows
R_{l}
:

\displaystyle R_{k+i}\leftarrow R_{k+i}-\log\left(N/\alpha\right)R_{i}

for
i=1,...,k
. Let
M
be the diagonal
2k\times 2k
matrix:

\displaystyle M=

\displaystyle\text{diag}\left(\sqrt{\omega_{\alpha/N}(u_{1})},...,\sqrt{\omega_{\alpha/N}(u_{k})},\sqrt{\omega_{\alpha/N}(u_{1})},...,\sqrt{\omega_{\alpha/N}(u_{k})}\right).

Then

\displaystyle m=\det(M^{2})=\det(M)^{2}.

Let us denote by
\hat{\delta_{N}^{k}}
the matrix such that
\det\left(\hat{\delta_{N}^{k}}\right)=\delta_{N}^{k}
with changed rows as described above and use the same notation for the other determinants. We have:

\displaystyle m\left(\delta_{N}^{k}\right)^{2}=\det\left(M\hat{\delta_{N}^{k}}\right)^{2}

and each term of this matrix is precisely of the type we can analyse by Lemma
3.2
. In the same fashion, if we denote by
M_{z}
the
(2k+1)\times(2k+1)
matrix

\displaystyle M_{z}=\text{diag}\left(M,\sqrt{\omega_{\alpha/N}(z+N)}\right),

we have that

\sqrt{\omega_{\alpha/N}(x+N)\omega_{\alpha/N}(y+N)}\cdot m\cdot\left(D_{N}^{k}(x+N)D_{N-1}^{k}(y+N)-D_{N}^{k}(x+N)D_{N-1}^{k}(y+N)\right)\\
=\det(M_{x})\det(M_{y})\cdot\left(D_{N}^{k}(x+N)D_{N-1}^{k}(y+N)-D_{N}^{k}(x+N)D_{N-1}^{k}(y+N)\right)\\
=\det\left(M_{x}\hat{D}_{N}^{k}(x+N)\right)\det\left(M_{y}\hat{D}_{N-1}^{k}(y+N)\right)\\
-\det\left(M_{y}\hat{D}_{N}^{k}(y+N)\right)\det\left(M_{x}\hat{D}_{N-1}^{k}(x+N)\right),

and again, we know the asymptotics for the components of each matrix thanks to Lemma
3.2
. Using the continuity of the determinant, we have established that, for all
x\neq y

\displaystyle\lim_{N\rightarrow+\infty}\sqrt{\omega_{\alpha/N}(x+N)\omega_{\alpha/N}(y+N)}

\displaystyle\frac{D_{N}^{k}(x+N)D_{N-1}^{k}(y+N)-D_{N}^{k}(y+N)D_{N-1}^{k}(x+N)}{\left(\delta_{N}^{k}\right)^{k}(x-y)}

\displaystyle=\frac{A_{k,0}(x)A_{k,1}(y)-A_{k,0}(y)A_{k,1}(x)}{C_{k,0}(x-y)}.

The case
x=y
goes as follows. For a
(2k+1)\times(2k+1)
determinant
A
, and for
i=0,...,2k
we denote by
[A]^{i}
its
\left((i+1),(2k+1)\right)
cofactor, i.e. the same determinant with the
(i+1)
-th column and the last line removed, multiplied by
(-1)^{i+1+2k+1}=(-1)^{i}
. We have

(7)

D_{N}^{k}(x+N)^{\prime}D_{N-1}^{k}(x+N)-D_{N}^{k}(x+N)D_{N-1}^{k}(x+N)^{\prime}\\
=\left(\sum_{i=0}^{2k}\left[D_{N}^{k}(x+N)\right]^{i}C_{N+i}^{\prime}(x+N)\right)\left(\sum_{i=0}^{2k}\left[D_{N-1}^{k}(x+N)\right]^{i}C_{N-1+i}(x+N)\right)\\
-\left(\sum_{i=0}^{2k}\left[D_{N}^{k}(x+N)\right]^{i}C_{N+i}(x+N)\right)\left(\sum_{i=0}^{2k}\left[D_{N-1}^{k}(x+N)\right]^{i}C_{N-1+i}^{\prime}(x+N)\right)\\
=\sum_{i,j=0}^{2k}\left[D_{N}^{k}(x+N)\right]^{i}\left[D_{N-1}^{k}(x+N)\right]^{j}\left(C_{N+i}^{\prime}(x+N)C_{N-1+j}(x+N)-C_{N+i}(x+N)C_{N-1+j}^{\prime}(x+N)\right).

Remark that the analysis of the cofactors has been done, and observe now that we have nice symmetries in this expression. Indeed, for any
i,j=0,...,2k
, second part of Lemma
3.2
entails:

\displaystyle C_{N+i}

{}^{\prime}(x+N)C_{N-1+j}(x+N)-C_{N+i}(x+N)C_{N-1+j}^{\prime}(x+N)

\displaystyle=\omega_{\alpha/N}(x+N)^{-1/2}

\displaystyle\times\left\{\left(L_{x-i}(2\sqrt{\alpha})+\log\left(N/\sqrt{\alpha}\right)C_{N+i}(x+N)+o(1)\right)C_{N-1+j}(x+N)\right.

\displaystyle\left.-C_{N+i}(x+N)\left(L_{x-1+j}(2\sqrt{\alpha})+\log\left(N/\sqrt{\alpha}\right)C_{N-1+j}(x+N)+o(1)\right)\right\}.

We see that the term involving the factor
\log(N/\sqrt{\alpha})
vanishes. Multiplying (
7
) by
\omega_{\alpha/N}(x+N)
and applying the first part of Lemma
3.2
, we are left with:

\displaystyle\omega_{\alpha/N}(x+N)

\displaystyle\left(D_{N}^{k}(x+N)^{\prime}D_{N-1}^{k}(x+N)-D_{N}^{k}(x+N)D_{N-1}^{k}(x+N)^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=0}^{2k}\left[D_{N}^{k}(x+N)\right]^{i}\left[D_{N-1}^{k}(x+N)\right]^{j}

\displaystyle\times\left(L_{-x-i}(2\sqrt{\alpha})J_{-x+1-j}(2\sqrt{\alpha})-J_{-x-i}(2\sqrt{\alpha})L_{-x+1-j}(2\sqrt{\alpha})+o(1)\right).

This sum can be factorized, and we obtain that:

\displaystyle\omega_{\alpha/N}(x+N)

\displaystyle\cdot m\cdot\left(D_{N}^{k}(x+N)^{\prime}D_{N-1}^{k}(x+N)-D_{N}^{k}(x+N)D_{N-1}^{k}(x+N)^{\prime}\right)

tends to

\displaystyle A_{k,0}^{\prime}(x)A_{k,1}(x)-A_{k,0}(x)A_{k,1}^{\prime}(x)

as
N
tends to
+\infty
, which is the desired result. Recalling (
6
), the Theorem is proved.
\square

Proof of Lemma
3.2
.

Following
[
24
]
, we will use the integral representation for Charlier polynomials. Since the generating series for the normalized Charlier polynomials is:

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\alpha}{N}\right)^{m/2}\frac{1}{\sqrt{m!}}C_{m}(x;\alpha/N)w^{m}=e^{-\alpha w/N}\left(1+w\right)^{x},

we have by Cauchy’s formula, for
r>1
:

(8)

C_{m}(x;\alpha/N)=\sqrt{m!}\left(\frac{N}{\alpha}\right)^{m/2}\left(\frac{1}{2i\pi}\int_{|w|=r}e^{-\alpha w/N}(1+w)^{x}w^{-m}\frac{dw}{w}\right.\\
\left.-(-1)^{m}\frac{\sin\pi x}{\pi}\int_{1}^{r}e^{\alpha u/N}(u-1)^{x}u^{-m}\frac{du}{u}\right).

The second integral in (
8
) arises as we take the principal branch of logarithm in order to define
(1+w)^{x}
for
w\in\mathbb{C}\setminus(-\infty,-1]
,
x\in\mathbb{R}
. Performing the change of variables
w=Nz/\sqrt{\alpha}
,
u=Ns/\sqrt{\alpha}
, one obtains, for
r>\sqrt{\alpha}/N
:

\displaystyle C_{m}(x;\alpha/N)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{m!}{N^{m}}}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}e^{-\sqrt{\alpha}re^{i\theta}}\left(1+\frac{Nre^{i\theta}}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\right)^{x}\frac{d\theta}{(re^{i\theta})^{m}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.-(-1)^{m}\frac{\sin\pi x}{\pi}\int_{\sqrt{\alpha}/N}^{r}e^{\sqrt{\alpha}s}\left(\frac{Ns}{\sqrt{\alpha}}-1\right)^{x}s^{-m}\frac{ds}{s}\right).

This expression can be suitably factorized:

(9)

\begin{split}C_{m}(x;\alpha/N)&=e^{-\sqrt{\alpha}}\sqrt{\frac{m!}{N^{m}}}\left(1+\frac{N}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\right)^{x}\\
&\times\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}e^{\sqrt{\alpha}(1-re^{i\theta})}\left(\frac{\sqrt{\alpha}+Nre^{i\theta}}{\sqrt{\alpha}+N}\right)^{x}\frac{d\theta}{(re^{i\theta})^{m}}\right.\\
&\left.-(-1)^{m}\frac{\sin\pi x}{\pi}\int_{\sqrt{\alpha}/N}^{r}e^{\sqrt{\alpha}(s+1)}\left(\frac{Ns-\sqrt{\alpha}}{\sqrt{\alpha}+N}\right)^{x}s^{-m}\frac{ds}{s}\right).\end{split}

Take
m=N+l
and denote by
I_{N}^{l}(x)
the sum of the integrals

(10)

\begin{split}I_{N}^{l}(x)&:=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}e^{\sqrt{\alpha}(1-re^{i\theta})}\left(\frac{\sqrt{\alpha}+Nre^{i\theta}}{\sqrt{\alpha}+N}\right)^{x}\frac{d\theta}{(re^{i\theta})^{N+l}}\\
&-(-1)^{N+l}\frac{\sin\pi x}{\pi}\int_{\sqrt{\alpha}/N}^{r}e^{\sqrt{\alpha}(s+1)}\left(\frac{Ns-\sqrt{\alpha}}{\sqrt{\alpha}+N}\right)^{x}s^{-N-l}\frac{ds}{s}\end{split},

for any
l\in\mathbb{Z}
, and define

\displaystyle A_{\alpha}^{N}(x)=e^{-2\sqrt{\alpha}}\frac{N!}{N^{N}}\left(1+\frac{N}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\right)^{2x}\omega_{\alpha/N}(x).

We have:

(11)

\sqrt{\omega_{\alpha/N}(x)}C_{N+l}(x;\alpha/N)=\sqrt{\frac{(N+1)...(N+l)}{N^{l}}A_{\alpha}^{N}(x)}I_{N}^{l}(x).

Recall the integral representation for the Bessel function, which follows from (
5
):

(12)

J_{x}(2\sqrt{\alpha})=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}e^{\sqrt{\alpha}(\frac{1}{r}e^{-i\theta}-re^{i\theta})}(re^{i\theta})^{x}d\theta-\frac{\sin\pi x}{\pi}\int_{0}^{r}e^{\sqrt{\alpha}(-1/s+s)}s^{x}\frac{ds}{s}.

We first show that the integrals (
10
) converges to the integrals (
12
), with a speed of order at least
1/N
, showing that the integrands converge. For the first integrand, we have by definition:

\displaystyle e^{\sqrt{\alpha}(1-z)}

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\sqrt{\alpha}+Nz}{\sqrt{\alpha}+N}\right)^{x+N}z^{-N-l}

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\sqrt{\alpha}(1-z)-(N+l)\log z+(x+N)(\log(\sqrt{\alpha}+Nz)-\log(\sqrt{\alpha}+N))\right).

Factorizing inside the logarithms leads to:

\displaystyle e^{\sqrt{\alpha}(1-z)}

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\sqrt{\alpha}+Nz}{\sqrt{\alpha}+N}\right)^{x+N}z^{-N-l}

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\sqrt{\alpha}(1-z)-(l-x)\log z+(x+N)\left(\log\left(1+\frac{\sqrt{\alpha}}{Nz}\right)-\log\left(1+\frac{\sqrt{\alpha}}{N}\right)\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\sqrt{\alpha}(1/z-z)+O(1/N)\right)z^{x-l},

where the last line follows from straightforward simplifications and first order expansions of the logarithms near
1
. In the same way, we have for the second integrand:

\displaystyle e^{\sqrt{\alpha}(s+1)}\left(\frac{Ns-\sqrt{\alpha}}{\sqrt{\alpha}+N}\right)^{x+N}s^{-N-l}=\exp\left(\sqrt{\alpha}(-1/s+s)+O(1/N)\right)s^{x-l}.

We have shown that there exists a constant
K=K(\alpha,x,r,l)
such that:

\displaystyle\left|e^{\sqrt{\alpha}(1-z)}\left(\frac{\sqrt{\alpha}+Nz}{\sqrt{\alpha}+N}\right)^{x+N}z^{-N-l}-\exp\left(\sqrt{\alpha}(1/z-z)\right)z^{x-l}\right|\leq\frac{K}{N},

and

\displaystyle\left|e^{\sqrt{\alpha}(s+1)}\left(\frac{Ns-\sqrt{\alpha}}{\sqrt{\alpha}+N}\right)^{x+N}s^{-N-l}-e^{\sqrt{\alpha}(-1/s+s)}s^{x-l}\right|\leq\frac{K}{N},

for all involved
z
and
s
. This implies:

\displaystyle|I_{N}^{l}(x+N)-J_{x-l}(2\sqrt{\alpha})|\leq\frac{K}{N},

as announced. Since as
N\rightarrow+\infty
, we have that:

\displaystyle A_{\alpha}^{N}(x+N)\rightarrow 1,

and recalling (
11
), we have established the first part of the Lemma.

We now move to the proof of the second statement of the Lemma. Since the techniques are very similar, we will give less details in our computations. Differentating expression (
9
), we obtain:

\displaystyle C_{m}^{\prime}(x;\alpha/N)

\displaystyle=\log\left(1+\frac{N}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\right)C_{m}(x;\alpha/N)

\displaystyle+e^{-\sqrt{\alpha}}\sqrt{\frac{m!}{N^{m}}}\left(1+\frac{N}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\right)^{x}\frac{d}{dx}\left(I^{m-N}_{N}(x)\right).

We thus only need to proof that the derivative of the integrals converges to the derivative of the Bessel function. Derivations under the sign of the integrals give

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dx}\left(I^{l}_{N}(x)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\log\left(\frac{\sqrt{\alpha}+Nre^{i\theta}}{\sqrt{\alpha}+N}\right)e^{\sqrt{\alpha}(1-re^{i\theta})}\left(\frac{\sqrt{\alpha}+Nre^{i\theta}}{\sqrt{\alpha}+N}\right)^{x}\frac{d\theta}{(re^{i\theta})^{N+l}}

\displaystyle-(-1)^{N+l}\frac{\sin\pi x}{\pi}\int_{\sqrt{\alpha}/N}^{r}\log\left(\frac{Ns-\sqrt{\alpha}}{\sqrt{\alpha}+N}\right)e^{\sqrt{\alpha}(s+1)}\left(\frac{Ns-\sqrt{\alpha}}{\sqrt{\alpha}+N}\right)^{x}s^{-N-l}\frac{ds}{s}

\displaystyle-(-1)^{N+l}\frac{\cos\pi x}{\pi}\int_{\sqrt{\alpha}/N}^{r}e^{\sqrt{\alpha}(s+1)}\left(\frac{Ns-\sqrt{\alpha}}{\sqrt{\alpha}+N}\right)^{x}s^{-N-l}\frac{ds}{s},

and

\displaystyle L_{x}(2\sqrt{\alpha})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\log(re^{i\theta})e^{\sqrt{\alpha}(\frac{1}{r}e^{-i\theta}-re^{i\theta})}(re^{i\theta})^{x}d\theta

\displaystyle-\frac{\sin\pi x}{\pi}\int_{0}^{r}\log(s)e^{\sqrt{\alpha}(-1/s+s)}s^{x}\frac{ds}{s}

\displaystyle-\frac{\cos\pi x}{\pi}\int_{0}^{r}e^{\sqrt{\alpha}(-1/s+s)}s^{x}\frac{ds}{s}.

Analogous computations lead to:

\displaystyle\lim_{N\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{d}{dx}\left(I^{l}_{N}(x+N)\right)=L_{x-l}(2\sqrt{\alpha}).

This completes the proof of the Lemma.
∎

3.4.
Palm measures of the discrete Bessel point process

In regards of subsection
2.4
and Proposition
2.7
, we can now give a description of the Palm measures of the discrete Bessel point process
\mathfrak{S}^{0}_{*}(\mathbb{P}_{\alpha})
, the determinantal point process with kernel

given by (

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\tilde{u}_{1},...,\tilde{u}_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}

, let

\displaystyle K_{\alpha}^{\tilde{u}_{1},...,\tilde{u}_{k}}(x,y):=\frac{\alpha^{\frac{2k+1}{2}}}{C_{k,k}^{2}\prod_{i=1}^{k}|(x-\tilde{u}_{i})(y-\tilde{u}_{i})|}\frac{A_{k,k}(x)A_{k,1+k}(y)-A_{k,k}(y)A_{k,1+k}(x)}{x-y}.

.

From
[

]
, Theorem 4.1, the kernel

serves as the correlation kernel of the TASEP with initial step condition at time

under the condition that the position

3.3
.

By Propositions

and

, the reduced Palm measure at

4.
The
z
-measures and a deformation of the gamma process

4.1.
Christoffel deformations of the
z
-measures

The
z
-measures on partitions were introduced and extensively studied by Borodin and Olshanski in a series of papers, see e.g.
[
7
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
10
]
and
[
11
]
and references therein. We first briefly explain their construcion. The
z
-measures are defined on the set
\mathbb{Y}
of all partitions by:

\displaystyle M_{z,z^{\prime},\xi}(\lambda)=(1-\xi)^{zz^{\prime}}\xi^{|\lambda|}(z)_{\lambda}(z^{\prime})_{\lambda}\left(\frac{\dim(\lambda)}{|\lambda|!}\right)^{2}.

They depend on three complex parameters,
z
,
z^{\prime}
and
\xi
and they are in general complex measures. The following choices ensure that it is a probability measure (see e.g.
[
10
]
Proposition 1.2 for a proof). First of all, the parameter
\xi
is real and lies in the open unit interval
(0,1)
. Next, we distinguish three cases:

\bullet

[Principal series] The parameters
z
and
z^{\prime}
are conjugate to each other.

\bullet

[Complementary series] The parameters
z
and
z^{\prime}
are both real and belong to the same interval
(m,m+1)
where
m\in\mathbb{Z}
.

\bullet

[Degenerate series] One of the parameter, say
z
, is a non-zero integer, while the other has the same sign and satisfy
|z^{\prime}|>|z|-1
.

If
z
and
z^{\prime}
satisfy one of these three conditions, we say that they are
admissible
parameters.
The Christoffel defomations of the
z
-measure are then defined as follows:

Definition 4.1
.

Let
z
,
z^{\prime}
be admissible parameters and
M_{z,z^{\prime},\xi}
the corresponding
z
-measure. Let
u_{1}
,…,
u_{k}
,
v_{1}
,…,
v_{k}\in\mathbb{C}
be
2k
complex numbers which satisfy:

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{k}(x-u_{i}+z)(x-v_{i}+z^{\prime})>0

for any
x\in\mathbb{Z}
. We define the Christoffel deformation of
M_{z,z^{\prime},\xi}
by:

(13)

\displaystyle M_{z,z^{\prime},\xi}^{k}(\lambda)=C\prod_{j=1}^{l(\lambda)}\prod_{i=1}^{k}(\lambda_{j}-j-u_{i}+z)(\lambda_{j}-j-v_{i}+z^{\prime})M_{z,z^{\prime}\xi}(\lambda),

where
C
is the normalisation constant chosen such that
M_{z,z^{\prime},\xi}^{k}
is a probability measure.

We first have to focus on the degenerate case. For simplicity, we assume that
z=N
is a positive integer and
z^{\prime}>z-1
. The
z
-measure
M_{z,z^{\prime}\xi}
is then supported on the subset of Young diagrams of length less than or equal to
N
, and we map
\mathbb{Y}
on
\text{Conf}(\mathbb{N})\subset\text{Conf}(\mathbb{Z})
via

\displaystyle\lambda\mapsto\mathfrak{S}_{N}(\lambda):=\{\lambda_{i}-i+N,\hskip 2.84544pti=1,...,N\}.

The pushforward of the
z
-measure
M_{z,z^{\prime}\xi}
under
\mathfrak{S}_{N}
is then the
N
-th Meixner ensemble (see e.g.
[
10
]
, Proposition 1.4), i.e. the
N
-th orthogonal ensemble with weight

\displaystyle\omega_{\beta,\xi}(x)=\frac{(\beta)_{x}\xi^{x}}{x!},

where
\beta=z^{\prime}-z+1>0
. The corresponding orthogonal polynomials are the Meixner polynomials defined by:

\displaystyle M_{n}(x;\beta,\xi)=\left.{}_{2}F_{1}\right.(-n,-x;\beta;1-1/\xi),\hskip 2.84544ptn\in\mathbb{N}.

Their leading coefficient
c_{n}
and their squared norm
h_{n}
are given by

\displaystyle c_{n}=\frac{\left(1-\frac{1}{\xi}\right)^{n}}{n!(\beta)_{n}}\hskip 2.84544pt,\quad h_{n}=\frac{n!}{\xi^{n}(1-\xi)^{\beta}(\beta)_{n}}.

We now choose
k
real numbers
u_{1},...,u_{k}\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{N}
which are not integers, and set
v_{i}=u_{i}+\beta-1
. We thus have:

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{l(\lambda)}\prod_{i=1}^{k}(\lambda_{j}-j-u_{i}+z)(\lambda_{j}-j-v_{i}+z^{\prime})=\prod_{x\in\mathfrak{S}_{N}(\lambda)}\prod_{j=1}^{k}(x-u_{i})^{2}

from which we deduce that the pushforward of
M_{z,z^{\prime},\xi}^{k}
under
\mathfrak{S}_{N}
is the Christoffel deformation of the
N
-th Meixner ensemble at points
u_{1},...,u_{k}
as defined in definition
2.4
. As a consequence, it defines a determinantal point process with an explicit kernel given by Proposition
2.5
. We now prepare a similar statement for other choices of admissible parameters
z
and
z^{\prime}
. For this aim, we will reproduce the analytic continuation argument developed in
[
10
]
and
[
11
]
, although the simplifications we need are less obvious. We will note:
\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}=\mathbb{Z}+1/2
the set of proper half integers. For
a\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}
,
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}
and
z
and
z^{\prime}
admissible parameters
that are not
in the degenerate case, we define:

(14)

\begin{split}\psi_{a}(x;z,&z^{\prime},\xi):=\left(\frac{\Gamma(x+z+1/2)\Gamma(x+z^{\prime}+1/2)}{\Gamma(z-a+1/2)\Gamma(z^{\prime}-a+1/2)}\right)^{1/2}\xi^{1/2(x+a)}(1-\xi)^{1/2(z+z^{\prime})-a}\\
&\times\frac{\left.{}_{2}F_{1}\right.\left(-z+a+1/2,-z^{\prime}+a+1/2;x+a+1;\frac{\xi}{\xi-1}\right)}{\Gamma(x+a+1)}.\end{split}

Observe that these functions are symmetric in
z,z^{\prime}
. We now recall the following integral representation, which is due to Borodin-Olshanski (see
[
10
]
, Proposition 2.3):

(15)

\begin{split}\psi_{a}(x;z,z^{\prime},\xi)&=\left(\frac{\Gamma(x+z+1/2)\Gamma(x+z^{\prime}+1/2)}{\Gamma(z-a+1/2)\Gamma(z^{\prime}-a+1/2)}\right)^{1/2}\frac{\Gamma(z^{\prime}-a+1/2)}{\Gamma(z^{\prime}+x+1/2)}(1-\xi)^{\frac{z^{\prime}-z+1}{2}}\\
&\times\frac{1}{2i\pi}\oint(1-\sqrt{\xi}\omega)^{-z^{\prime}+a-1/2}\left(1-\frac{\sqrt{\xi}}{\omega}\right)^{z-a-1/2}\omega^{-x-a}\frac{d\omega}{\omega}.\end{split}

Observe that it is now not obvious that such an expression is symmetric in
z,z^{\prime}
, i.e. none of its factor is symmetric in
z,z^{\prime}
, but since expression (
14
) is, it has to be symmetric in
z,z^{\prime}
. We will also need the following Proposition, relating the functions
\psi_{a}
to Meixner polynomials, which comes from Borodin and Olshanski (
[
10
]
, Proposition 2.8).

Proposition 4.1
.

Suppose
z=N\in\mathbb{N}
and
z^{\prime}=N+\beta-1
. Suppose that:

\displaystyle\tilde{x}:=x+N-1/2\in\mathbb{N},\quad\quad n:=N-a-1/2\in\mathbb{N},

are non-negative integers. Then,
\psi_{a}(x;z,z^{\prime},\xi)
is well defined and we have:

\displaystyle\psi_{a}(x;z,z^{\prime},\xi)=\sqrt{\frac{\omega_{\beta,\xi}(\tilde{x})}{h_{n}}}M_{n}(\tilde{x};\beta,\xi).

In what follows, we will sometimes omit the dependance on the parameters
z
,
z^{\prime}
and
\xi
when the context is clear. The first Theorem of this section states that, for any pair of admissible parameters
(z,z^{\prime})

\psi_{a}
and their derivatives.

Theorem 4.2
.

For any admissible parameters
z
and
z^{\prime}
which are not in the degenerate case, and any
u_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
,
v_{i}=u_{i}+z^{\prime}-z-1/2

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}:\lambda\mapsto\{\lambda_{i}-i+1/2\}\in\text{Conf}(\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}),

(16)

K_{z,z^{\prime},\xi}^{k}(x,y):=\frac{C_{z,z^{\prime},k}}{\prod_{i=1}^{k}|(x-u_{i})(y-u_{i})|}\frac{B_{k,0}(x;z,z^{\prime},\xi)B_{k,1}(y;z,z^{\prime},\xi)-B_{k,0}(y;z,z^{\prime},\xi)B_{k,1}(x;z,z^{\prime},\xi)}{D_{k}(z,z^{\prime},\xi)^{2}(x-y)},

with

\displaystyle B_{k,p}(x)

\displaystyle=\begin{vmatrix}\psi_{-1/2+p}(u_{1})&\psi_{-3/2+p}(u_{1})&\dots&\psi_{-1/2-2k+p}(u_{1})\\
\vdots&&\ddots&\vdots\\
\psi_{-1/2+p}(u_{k})&\dots&\dots&\psi_{-1/2-2k+p}(u_{k})\\

\psi^{\prime}_{-1/2}(u_{k})&\dots&\dots&\psi^{\prime}_{1/2-2k}(u_{k})\\
\end{vmatrix},

and

\displaystyle C_{z,z^{\prime},k}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\xi}{\xi-1}\right)^{1+2k}\frac{\Gamma(z+2k+1)}{\Gamma(z)}\frac{\Gamma(z^{\prime}+2k)}{\Gamma(z^{\prime}-1)}.

Proof.

The proof requires some steps. We first establish in step 1 that the statement of the Theorem holds for the degenerate series case. This fact is precisely formulated in Lemma

Step 1.

Assume that

and

\displaystyle\tilde{x}:=x+N-1/2,\quad\tilde{y}:=y+N-1/2\quad\quad\tilde{u}_{i}\in\mathbb{R},\quad\tilde{v}_{i}=\tilde{u}_{i}+\beta-1,\quad i=1,...,k.

Indeed, the factor

For any values of the parameters

Step 3

. We first assume that

For the products

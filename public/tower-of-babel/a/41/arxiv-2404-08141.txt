Algebraic formulas and Geometric derivation of Source Identities
Kohei Motegi and Ryo Ohkawa

Abstract
Source identities are fundamental identities between multivariable special functions. We give a geometric derivation of rational and trigonometric source identities. We also give a systematic derivation and extension of various determinant representations for source functions which appeared in previous literature as well as introducing the elliptic version of the determinants, and obtain identities between determinants. We also show several symmetrization formulas for the rational version.

Introduction

Identities between special functions play
important roles not only in special function theory itself but also various fields in mathematics and mathematical physics
such as geometric representation theory, supersymmetric gauge theory and integrable systems.
Typicial ones are transformation formulas for hypergeometric functions
which appear as wall-crossing phenomena in supersymmetric gauge theory.
For example, Kajihara’s transformation formulas
[
1
]
for hypergeometric functions
appeared in the context of supersymmetric gauge theory
[
2
,
3
]
,
and a geometric derivation of the rational version was given by one of the authors and Yoshida
[
4
]
.
The setting of the algebraic variety used for the derivation was the handsaw quiver variety.

The derivation of transformation formulas for hypergeometric functions by Kajihara
[
1

and
an elliptic version by Kajihara-Noumi
[
5

(see also Rosengren
[
6

is based on specializations of more fundamental identities called as source identities.
The basic idea is that taking multiple principal specializations of the original variables
for the source identities lead to transformation formulas for hypergeometric functions.

This paper is organized as follows.
We discuss the elliptic, trigonometric and rational
version of source functions and source identities
in section 2, 3 and 4 respectively.
We give a systematic derivation of various determinant forms

For the trigonometric and rational case, we discuss a degeneration from the elliptic and trigonometric version

Trigonometric version

In this section, we discuss trigonometric source identity as a degeneration from

We also give a geometric derivation and a complex analytic proof

3.3
A complex analytic proof of trigonometric source identity

See also

We prove the following polynomial version

\displaystyle\sum_{K\subset[1,\dots,m]}(-z)^{|K|}q^{|K|(|K|-1)/2}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in K\\

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\frac{(z;q)_{\infty}}{(q^{m-n}z;q)_{\infty}}}

\displaystyle(-\ell+m-n)_{t}\sum_{\ell\geq h_{1}>\cdots>h_{k-1}\geq 1}\prod_{i=1}^{k-1}(\ell-2h_{i}-i+2+m-n)_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-\ell+m-n)_{t}\frac{(m-n)_{t}!(\ell)_{t}!}{(k-1)_{t}!(m-n-k+1)_{t}!(\ell-k+1)_{t}!}.

(3.78)

The second sum can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\geq h_{1}>h_{2}>\cdots>h_{k}\geq 1}\prod_{i=1}^{k}(\ell-2h_{i}-i+3+m-n)_{t}=\frac{(m-n+1)_{t}!(\ell)_{t}!}{(k)_{t}!(m-n+1-k)_{t}!(\ell-k)_{t}!}.

(3.79)

Combining (
3.78
) and
(
3.79
) and using

\displaystyle(x-u)_{t}(y-v)_{t}-(x-v)_{t}(y-u)_{t}=(x-y)_{t}(u-v)_{t},

(3.80)

for
x=m-n,y=k-1,u=\ell,v=-1
,
we get

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell+1\geq h_{1}>\cdots>h_{k}\geq 1}\prod_{i=1}^{k}(\ell-2h_{i}-i+3+m-n)_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-\ell+m-n)_{t}\frac{(m-n)_{t}!(\ell)_{t}!}{(k-1)_{t}!(m-n-k+1)_{t}!(\ell-k+1)_{t}!}+\frac{(m-n+1)_{t}!(\ell)_{t}!}{(k)_{t}!(m-n+1-k)_{t}!(\ell-k)_{t}!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(m-n)_{t}!(\ell)_{t}!}{(k)_{t}!(m-n-k+1)_{t}!(\ell-k+1)_{t}!}((-\ell+m-n)_{t}(k)_{t}+(m-n+1)_{t}(\ell-k+1)_{t})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(m-n)_{t}!(\ell+1)_{t}!}{(k)_{t}!(m-n-k)_{t}!(\ell-k+1)_{t}!},

(3.81)

which completes the proof.
∎

4.5
Geometric derivation of rational source identity

The rational version (
1.8
) of the
source identity is also obtained from the cohomological
version of the wall-crossing formula.
We follow the similar arguments as in §
3.4
taking the usual Euler class

\mathop{\mathrm{Eu}}\nolimits^{\theta}(M^{\zeta^{\pm}}_{Q}(\ell))=\mathop{\mathrm{Eu}}\nolimits(TM^{\zeta^{\pm}}_{Q}(\ell)\otimes\mathbb{C}_{e^{\theta}})

instead of
\mathop{\mathrm{Eu}}\nolimits^{K}(M^{\zeta^{\pm}}_{Q}(\ell))
.

We see that
\int_{M^{\zeta^{+}}_{Q}(\ell)}\mathop{\mathrm{Eu}}\nolimits^{\theta}(M^{\zeta^{+}}_{Q}(\ell))
is equal to

\displaystyle\sum_{K\subset[n+1,\dots,n+m]\atop\lvert K\rvert=\ell}\prod_{i\in K\atop j\in[n+1,\dots,n+m]\setminus K}{a_{j}-a_{i}+\theta\over a_{j}-a_{i}}\prod_{i\in K\atop 1\leq k\leq n}{a_{i}-a_{k}+\theta\over a_{i}-a_{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{K\subset[1,\dots,m]\atop\lvert K\rvert=\ell}\prod_{i\in K\atop j\not\in K}{v_{i}-v_{j}-c\over v_{i}-v_{j}}\prod_{i\in K\atop 1\leq k\leq n}{v_{i}-u_{k}\over v_{i}-u_{k}-c},

and
\int_{M^{\zeta^{-}}_{Q}(\ell)}\mathop{\mathrm{Eu}}\nolimits^{\theta}(M^{\zeta^{-}}_{Q}(\ell))
is equal to

\displaystyle\sum_{K\subset[1,\dots,n]\atop\lvert K\rvert=\ell}\prod_{i\in K\atop j\in[1,\ldots,n]\setminus K}{a_{i}-a_{j}+\theta\over a_{i}-a_{j}}\prod_{i\in K\atop n+1\leq k\leq n+m}{a_{k}-a_{i}+\theta\over a_{k}-a_{i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{K\subset[1,\dots,n]\atop\lvert K\rvert=\ell}\prod_{i\in K\atop j\not\in K}{u_{i}-u_{j}+c\over u_{i}-u_{j}}\prod_{i\in K\atop 1\leq k\leq m}{u_{i}-v_{k}\over u_{i}-v_{k}+c},

where we put
\theta=-c
, and
a_{j}=\begin{cases}-u_{j}-c&j=1,\ldots,n\\
-v_{j}&j=n+1,\ldots,n+m\end{cases}
.
These are coefficients of

in generating series
appearing in both sides of
(
1.8

Wall-crossing formula
[

and we have

\displaystyle\gamma_{d}(\theta)=\int_{M_{Q^{\sharp}}(d)}\mathop{\mathrm{Eu}}\nolimits^{\theta}(TM^{\zeta^{-}}_{Q^{\sharp}}(d))=\begin{cases}1&d=1\\
0&d\neq 1.\end{cases}

(4.77)

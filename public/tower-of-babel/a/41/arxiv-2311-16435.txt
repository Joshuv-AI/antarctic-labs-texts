Unique determination by a single far-field measurement for an inverse elastic problem
Huaian Diao, Ruixiang Tang, Hongyu Liu, Jiexin Tang

Abstract
. This paper is concerned with the unique identification of the shape of a scatterer through a single far-field pattern in an inverse elastic medium scattering problem with a generalized transmission boundary condition. The uniqueness issue by a single far-field measurement is a challenging problem in inverse scattering theory, which has a long and colorful history. In this paper, we demonstrate the well-posedness of the direct problem by the variational approach. We establish the uniqueness results by a single far-field measurement under a generic scenario when dealing with underlying elastic scatterers exhibiting polygonal-nest or polygonal-cell structures. Furthermore, for a polygonal-nest or polygonal-cell structure scatterer associated with density and boundary impedance parameters as piecewise constants, we show that these physical quantities can be uniquely determined simultaneously by a single far-field measurement. The corresponding proof relies heavily on examining the singular behaviour of a coupled PDE system near a corner in a microlocal manner. Keywords: inverse elastic scattering, generalized transmission boundary condition, single far-field measurement, polygonal-nest and polygonal-cell structure, corner singularity, unique identifiability 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 35R30; 74J20; 86A22

Introduction

1.1.
Mathematical setup

Initially, we will focus on the mathematical model that will be used in our subsequent study. Consider a time-harmonic elastic plane wave, denoted by
{\mathbf{u}}^{i}
, that has a time variation of the form
e^{-{\rm i}\omega t}
, where
\omega\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
is a fixed angular frequency. The homogeneous and isotropic background
\mathbb{R}^{2}
is characterized by Lamé constants,
\lambda
and
\mu
, where the mass density of the background is normalized to 1. Here, Lamé’s first parameter is denoted as
\lambda
and the shear modulus as
\mu
, which satisfy the following strong convexity condition

(1.1)

\mu>0\ \mbox{and}\ \lambda+\mu>0.

Throughout this paper, the compressional and shear wave numbers of the homogeneous and isotropic background are defined as follows:

(1.2)

k_{p}=\frac{\omega}{\sqrt{2\mu+\lambda}},\quad k_{s}=\frac{\omega}{\sqrt{\mu}}.

In this paper we assume that the time-harmonic elastic plane wave
{\mathbf{u}}^{i}
is a superpositions of incident compressional and shear waves given by

(1.3)

\displaystyle{\mathbf{u}}^{i}=c_{1}\mathbf{d}e^{{\rm i}k_{p}\mathbf{d}\cdot\mathbf{x}}+c_{2}\mathbf{d}^{\perp}e^{{\rm i}k_{s}\mathbf{d}\cdot\mathbf{x}},

where
c_{1}
,
c_{2}
are two complex numbers satisfying
(c_{1},c_{2})\neq(0,0)
,
\mathbf{d},\mathbf{d}^{\perp}\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
with
\mathbf{d}\cdot\mathbf{d}^{\perp}=0
. In particular, our focus lies on a fixed incident elastic plane wave
{\mathbf{u}}^{i}
, which means that
c_{1}
,
c_{2}
,
\mathbf{d}
, and
\mathbf{d}^{\perp}
are all fixed as specified in (
1.3
). It can be verified that
{\mathbf{u}}^{i}
is an entire solution to the Navier equation described by

(1.4)

\mathcal{L}{\mathbf{u}}^{i}+\omega^{2}{\mathbf{u}}^{i}={\mathbf{0}}\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{2},

where
\mathcal{L}:=\mu\Delta+(\lambda+\mu)\nabla(\nabla\cdot)
. The incident elastic plane wave
{\mathbf{u}}^{i}
impinges upon an elastic medium scatterer
\Omega\Subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
embedded in an infinite isotropic and homogeneous elastic medium in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. The elastic scatterer
\Omega
is a bounded Lipschitz domain with corresponding medium configuration
q\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
\eta\in L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)
, both of which are real-valued functions. Indeed, the mass density of the scatterer
\Omega
is characterized by
q
, and a generalized transmission condition of the elastic wave across
\partial\Omega
is characterized by the boundary parameter
\eta
.

The aforementioned elastic scattering problem is governed by the following system:

(1.5)

\begin{cases}\mathcal{L}{\mathbf{u}}+\omega^{2}q{\mathbf{u}}={\mathbf{0}},&\mbox{in}\quad\Omega,\\
\mathcal{L}{\mathbf{u}}+\omega^{2}{\mathbf{u}}={\mathbf{0}},&\mbox{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{2}\backslash\overline{\Omega},\\
{\mathbf{u^{+}}}={\mathbf{u^{-}}},\ T_{\nu}{\mathbf{u^{+}}}+\eta{\mathbf{u^{+}}}=T_{\nu}{\mathbf{u^{-}}},\quad&\mbox{on}\quad\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

where
{\mathbf{u}}
is the total elastic wave field. Let
{\mathbf{u}}^{s}:={\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{u}}^{i}
denote the scattered elastic wave field. It is noticed that the notation
{\mathbf{u}}^{\pm}
in the third equation of (
1.5
) indicates that we take the limit of
{\mathbf{u}}
on
\partial\Omega
from inside and outside of
\Omega
, respectively. The following defines the traction operator
T_{\nu}{\mathbf{u}}
on
\partial\Omega
in the third equation of (
1.5

Specifically, we have

(1.6)

T_{\nu}{\mathbf{u}}=\lambda(\nabla\cdot{\mathbf{u}})\nu+2\mu(\nabla^{s}{\mathbf{u}})\nu,\quad\nabla^{s}{\mathbf{u}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\nabla{\mathbf{u}}+\nabla{{\mathbf{u}}^{\top}}\right),

where
\nu
represents the exterior normal direction to

\partial\Omega
.
Let us define
\bf curl
and curl operators in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
as follows:

(1.7)

{\rm curl}~\bm{v}=\partial_{1}v_{2}-\partial_{2}v_{1},\quad{\bf curl}~{w}=(\partial_{1}w,-\partial_{2}w)^{\top}.

Here,
\bm{v}=(v_{1},v_{2})
and

are vector-valued and scalar functions, correspondingly. Introduce the compressional wave
{\mathbf{u}}_{p}^{s}
and shear wave
{\mathbf{u}}_{s}^{s}

(1.8)

{\mathbf{u}}_{p}^{s}:=-\frac{1}{k_{p}^{2}}\nabla(\nabla\cdot{\mathbf{u}}^{s}),\quad{\mathbf{u}}_{s}^{s}:=\frac{1}{k_{s}^{2}}{\bf curl}\ \mathrm{curl}{\mathbf{u}}^{s}.

Then, we define the Helmholtz decomposition of the scattered wave
{\mathbf{u}}^{s}
in (
1.5

{\mathbf{u}}^{s}={\mathbf{u}}_{p}^{s}+{\mathbf{u}}_{s}^{s}.

It is required that the scattered elastic wave
{\mathbf{u}}^{s}
satisfies the Kupradze radiation condition given by

(1.9)

\lim_{r\to\infty}r^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{\partial{\mathbf{u}}_{\beta}^{s}}{\partial r}-{\rm i}k_{\beta}{\mathbf{u}}_{\beta}^{s}\right)=0,\ r:=|{\mathbf{x}}|,\ \beta=p,s,

It should be noted that the generalized transmission condition
T_{\nu}{\mathbf{u^{+}}}+\eta{\mathbf{u^{+}}}=T_{\nu}{\mathbf{u^{-}}}

[

, the scattering model (

is referred to as the boundary impedance parameter.

(1.10)

\begin{split}{\mathbf{u}}^{{s}}({\mathbf{x}})=&\frac{{e}^{\mathrm{i}k_{p}r}}{{r}}u_{p}^{\infty}(\hat{{\mathbf{x}}})\hat{{\mathbf{x}}}+\frac{{e}^{\mathrm{i}k_{s}r}}{{r}}U_{s}^{\infty}(\hat{{\mathbf{x}}})+O\left(\frac{1}{r^{3/2}}\right),\quad U_{s}^{\infty}(\hat{{\mathbf{x}}})\cdot\hat{{\mathbf{x}}}=0,\quad\forall{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathbb{S}^{1}\end{split}

as

and the vector-valued analytic function

defined on the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{1}:=\{\hat{{\mathbf{x}}}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\big||\hat{{\mathbf{x}}}|=1\}

and

of

It is obvious that we have the following relationship
{u}_{p}^{\infty}(\hat{\mathbf{x}})={\mathbf{u}}_{t}^{\infty}(\hat{{\mathbf{x}}})\cdot\hat{{\mathbf{x}}}

This paper primarily focuses on solving the following inverse problem:

(1.11)

\mathcal{F}(\Omega;\lambda,\mu,q,\eta)={\mathbf{u}}_{t}^{\infty}(\hat{\mathbf{x}};\mathbf{u}^{i}),

where
\mathcal{F}
is implicitly defined by the scattering system (

). If the a far-field measurement

1.2.
Connections to previous results and main findings

Determining the geometrical information of an underlying scatterer by measurement is a fundamental problem in inverse scattering. It is referred to as the unique identifiability of the scatterer shape. Since the cardinality of
{\mathbf{u}}_{t}^{\infty}\left(\hat{\mathbf{x}};{\mathbf{u}}^{i}\right)
associated with a single fixed incident wave
{\mathbf{u}}^{i}
is equal to the cardinality of
\partial\Omega
, it is clear that the geometrical inverse problem (
1.11
) is formally determined by a single far-field measurement. On the contrary, the inverse problem of determining the physical parameters
q
and
\eta
associated with
\Omega
through a single far-field measurement is underdetermined, given that
q
and
\eta
are variable functions. The uniqueness of identifying the position and shape of the scatterer from a single far-field measurement was referred to as Schiffer’s problem in the literature
[
7
]
. Up to now, the corresponding developments to Schiffer’s problem in inverse acoustic and electromagnetic scattering problems have been made by imposing a-priori information on the scatterer
\Omega
, such as the size or geometry of
\Omega

[
8
,
7
,
11
]
and the references cited therein for further details.

When an elastic scatter is impenetrable, the corresponding scattering problem is referred to as elastic obstacle scattering. The first uniqueness result for determining a rigid obstacle by multifrequency and a fixed incident wave or a fixed frequency but for all incident directions and a certain set of polarizations was demonstrated in
[
19
]
. Later, it was shown that one can uniquely determine a smooth elastic obstacle from the shear part of the far-field pattern corresponding to all incident plane compressional (or shear) waves (cf.
[
15
]
). A polyhedral elastic obstacle with the third or fourth kind boundary conditions can be uniquely identified by finitely many far field measurements (cf.
[
14
]
), where the reflection principle for the Navier system is utilized to obtain the desired result. An extraction formula for an unknown linear crack or the convex hull of an unknown polygonal cavity in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
was established using the enclosure method and a single set of boundary data
[
20
,
21
]
. A formula was developed to reconstruct cracks in an anisotropic and inhomogeneous elastic body by measuring its elastic displacement and traction at the boundary
[
26
]
. Furthermore, the inclusion or cavity embedded in a plane elastic body with inhomogeneous anisotropic medium can be uniquely reconstructed by infinitely
many localized boundary measurements (cf.
[
25
]
).

For the elastic medium scattering problem, specifically when
\eta\equiv 0
in (
1.5
), we note that
[
18
,
16
]
provided the identifiable result to the inverse problem (
1.11
) when multiple far-field measurements are employed. Under general circumstances, a single far-field measurement is capable of uniquely determining a polygonal elastic scatterer with the generalized transmission boundary condition, as illustrated in the work of
[
12
]
, where the parameter
\eta
for the corresponding generalized transmission condition can also be simultaneously identified. When a scatterer has the polygonal-nest or polygonal-cell structure, in the acoustic scattering, it was shown that the uniqueness result for determining the shape of the underlying scatterer by a single far-field measurement hold under generic physical conditions (cf.
[
4
]
). If a scatterer has a thin layer with high conductivity, a single far-field measurement under generic conditions (cf.
[
5
]
) can uniquely determine the shape and location of the scatterer with the polygon-nest or polygon-cell structure.

In this study, we aim to demonstrate the uniqueness results for the identification of the shape and location of an elastic polygonal nest or polygonal cell structure scatterer associated with (
1.5
) by a single far-field measurement. In addition, a single far-field measurement can also determine the physical parameters
q
and
\eta
in (
1.5
) if they are piecewise constants. To achieve the desired result, we will study the coupled transmission PDE system around the underlying corner and apply the complex geometric optics (CGO) solution to investigate the singularity caused by a corner in a microlocal way.

Finally, the remainder of the paper is structured as follows. In Section
2
, we examine the existence and uniqueness of the solution to the direct scatter problem. In Section
3
, we provide a number of necessary geometrical and mathematical setups. In Section
4
, we derive auxiliary lemmas to facilitate subsequent analysis. In Section
5
, we define admissible scatterers and prove the unique identifiability results.

\displaystyle\int_{R\leq|\mathbf{x}|\leq R+1}\left((\mathcal{C}:\nabla{\mathbf{w}}):\nabla\overline{\bm{\varphi}}-\omega^{2}{\mathbf{w}}\cdot\overline{\bm{\varphi}}\right)\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}+\int_{B_{R}\backslash\overline{\Omega}}\left((\mathcal{C}:\nabla{\mathbf{w}}):\nabla\overline{{\mathbf{w}}}-\omega^{2}|{\mathbf{w}}|^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}

(q_{2}-q_{1}){\mathbf{u}}_{2}^{-}({\mathbf{0}})(e^{-2{{\mathrm{i}}}\theta_{M}}-e^{-2{{\mathrm{i}}}\theta_{m}})\cdot\left(\begin{array}[]{*{20}{c}}1\\
{\mathrm{i}}\end{array}\right)=0.

Part 3
. In this part, we prove (

{\mathbf{u}}_{1}^{-}={\mathbf{u}}_{1}^{+}={\mathbf{u}}_{2}^{+}={\mathbf{u}}_{2}^{-},\ T_{\nu}{\mathbf{u}}_{1}^{+}=T_{\nu}{\mathbf{u}}_{2}^{+},\ T_{\nu}{\mathbf{u}}_{1}^{-}=T_{\nu}{\mathbf{u}}_{2}^{-},\ \mbox{on}\ \Gamma_{h}^{\pm},

Moreover, by using

\begin{cases}\mathcal{L}{\mathbf{v}}+{\omega}^{2}q_{1}{\mathbf{v}}={\mathbf{0}},&\mbox{in}\quad{S_{h}},\\

Remark 4.1
.

To obtain uniquely identifiable outcomes for an elastic scatterer with piecewise polygonal-nest or polygonal-cell structures associated with the scattering problem (
1.5
) through a single far-field measurement, by contradiction argument, we need to study the singular behaviour of the underlying total wave field around a corner of the underlying elastic scatterer, which can be recast as a PDE system (
4.22
). Therefore, the crucial role of Lemma
4.5
in proving Theorem
5.2
and Theorem
5.3
cannot be overlooked.

5.
Main uniqueness results

In this section, we will use a single far-field measurement to demonstrate the local and global uniqueness result for determining the shape of an elastic scatterer with a polygonal-nest or polygonal-cell configuration under a generic physical scenario. Additionally, a single measurement enables the unique identification of both the piecewise constant density and boundary impedance parameter. Before establishing the uniqueness results of the inverse problems (
1.11
), we shall introduce the admissibility conditions for an elastic scatterer with polygonal-nest or polygonal-cell structures, which will be used in the rest of this paper.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
(\Omega;q,\eta)
be a polygonal-nest or polygonal-cell elastic scatterer
(\Omega;q,\eta)
as described by Definitions
3.3
and Definition
3.4
, respectively. The scatterer is said to be admissible if the following conditions is met:

1.

Considering a polygonal-nest elastic scatterer
(\Omega;\lambda,\mu,q,\eta)
associated with the polygonal-nest partition
\{\Pi_{\zeta}\}_{\zeta=1}^{N}
,
\forall{\mathbf{x_{c}}}\in\partial{\Pi_{\zeta}}
,
{\mathbf{u}}({\mathbf{x_{c}}})\neq{\mathbf{0}}
, where
{\mathbf{u}}
is the solution to (
1.5
) corresponding to
(\Omega;\lambda,\mu,q,\eta)
.

2.

Considering a polygonal-cell elastic scatterer
(\Omega;\lambda,\mu,q,\eta)
,
{\mathbf{u}}({\mathbf{x_{c}}})\neq{\mathbf{0}}
,
\forall{\mathbf{x_{c}}}\in\partial\Omega
, where where
{\mathbf{u}}
is the solution to (
1.5
) corresponding to
(\Omega;q,\eta)
.

The non-void admissible condition described in Definition
5.1
was applied in
[
12
]
and can be fulfilled in various generic situations. For instance, when the angular frequency
\omega
is considerably smaller than the diameter of the scatterer
\Omega
, i.e.,

(5.1)

\displaystyle\omega\cdot{\rm diam}(\Omega)\ll 1.

Therefore, as the scatterer
\Omega
is significantly smaller than the operating wavelength, it can be assumed that the total wave field is primarily due to the incident wave
{\mathbf{u}}^{i}
, while the influence of the corresponding scattering wave
{\mathbf{u}}^{sc}
is negligible from a physical standpoint. For the incident wave
{\mathbf{u}}^{i}
, we can choose either a compressional plane wave
\mathbf{d}\exp(\mathrm{i}k_{p}\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{d})
or a shear plane wave
\mathbf{d}^{\perp}\exp(\mathrm{i}k_{s}\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{d})
, where
{\mathbf{d}}
and
{\mathbf{d}}^{\perp}
are both elements of
\mathbb{S}^{1}
and
{\mathbf{d}}\cdot{\mathbf{d}}^{\perp}=0
, it is evident that
{\mathbf{u}}^{i}
is non-vanishing throughout. Consequently, when (
5.1
) is fulfilled,
{\mathbf{u}}={\mathbf{u}}^{i}+{\mathbf{u}}^{sc}
cannot vanish everywhere. Indeed, we can rigorously demonstrate from Remark
2.1
that if (
5.1
) is satisfied and the generalized transmission boundary parameter
\eta
is sufficiently small, the energy of the scattered wave
{\mathbf{u}}^{sc}
of (
1.5
) is negligible. This ensures that the total wave field will not vanish throughout. On other hand, from the physical intuition, when elastic scattering occurs, if the total field of the elastic wave is zero at a certain point, it means that the scattered wave and the incident wave cancel each other at that point. In usual elastic scattering, such a cancelation phenomenon as described above is rare. We will not go into this matter in depth in this paper.

In Theorem
5.1
, we consider an elastic scatterer
\Omega
that is in the form of a polygonal-nest or polygonal-cell. We aim to present a local uniqueness result for the shape determination of
\Omega
. If two scatterers,
\Omega_{1}
and
\Omega_{2}
, have identical far field measurements under a fixed incident wave, we will prove that the difference between these two scatterers cannot contain a corner. The proof will rely on the contraction, Rellich theorem and Lemma
4.3
. By observing the convexity of a scatterer with a polygonal-nest structure, we can further prove a global determination of the boundary of
\mathbb{R}^{2}\backslash\Omega
by a single measurement.

Theorem 5.1
.

Consider the elastic scattering problem (
1.5
) associated with two admissible polygonal-nest or polygonal-cell elastic scatterers
(\Omega_{j};\lambda,\mu,q_{j},\eta_{j})
(
j=1,2
) in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Recall that
{\mathbf{u}}_{t}^{j,\infty}(\hat{{\mathbf{x}}};{{\mathbf{u}}^{i}})
is the far-field pattern associated with the incident field
{{\mathbf{u}}^{i}}
in the form of (
1.3
) and the scatterers
(\Omega_{j};\lambda,\mu,q_{j},\eta_{j})
. Assume that

(5.2)

{\mathbf{u}}_{t}^{1,\infty}(\hat{{\mathbf{x}}};{{\mathbf{u}}^{i}})={\mathbf{u}}_{t}^{2,\infty}(\hat{{\mathbf{x}}};{{\mathbf{u}}^{i}}),\quad\forall\hat{{\mathbf{x}}}\in\mathbb{S}^{1},

where
{\mathbf{u}}^{i}
is a fixed incident wave. Then

(5.3)

\Omega_{1}\Delta\Omega_{2}:=(\Omega_{1}\backslash\Omega_{2})\cup(\Omega_{2}\backslash\Omega_{1}).

cannot contain a sectorial corner.
Moreover, suppose that
\Omega_{1}
and
\Omega_{2}
are two admissible polygonal-nest elastic scatterers, we can conclude that

(5.4)

\partial\Omega_{1}=\partial\Omega_{2}.

Proof.

The first conclusion of this theorem is proved by contradiction. In the following, we suppose that there is a corner contained within
\Omega_{1}\Delta\Omega_{2}
. For a polygonal-nest or a polygonal-cell elastic scatterer, the generalized transmission conditions are introduced in (
3.3
) and (
3.7
), respectively. It is evident that these two generalized transmission conditions for polygonal-cell and polygonal-nest elastic scatterer are coherent. Therefore, it is only necessary to prove the case for the polygonal-nest structure, and the polygonal-cell case can be demonstrated in a similar way.

Following the notation introduced in (
3.1
), let two polygonal-nest elastic scatterers be written as

(5.5)

(\Omega_{j};\lambda,\mu,q_{j},\eta_{j})=\bigcup\limits_{\zeta=1}^{N_{j}}(\mathcal{H}_{\zeta,j};q_{\zeta,j},\eta_{\zeta,j}),

where

(5.6)

\Omega_{j}=\bigcup_{\zeta=1}^{N_{j}}\mathcal{H}_{\zeta,j},\ q=\sum_{\zeta=1}^{N_{j}}q_{\zeta,j}\chi_{\mathcal{H}_{\zeta,j}},\ \eta=\sum_{\zeta=1}^{N_{j}}{\eta_{\zeta,j}}\chi_{\partial\Pi_{\zeta,j}}.

Assume that
\Omega_{1}\Delta\Omega_{2}
possesses a corner. Since
\mathcal{L}
is invariant under rigid motion, without loss of generality, we may suppose that the vertex
O
of the corner
\Omega_{2}\cap W
satisfies that
O\in\partial\Omega_{2}
and
O\notin\partial\overline{\Omega}_{1}
, where
O
is the origin. Moreover we assume that the underlying corner satisfies that
S_{h}\subseteq\Pi_{1,2}
, where
\Pi_{1,2}
is defined in (
5.6
).

Due to
{\mathbf{u}}_{t}^{1,\infty}(\hat{{\mathbf{x}}};{{\mathbf{u}}^{i}})={\mathbf{u}}_{t}^{2,\infty}(\hat{{\mathbf{x}}};{{\mathbf{u}}^{i}}),\ \forall\hat{{\mathbf{x}}}\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
, it is easy to deduce that
{{\mathbf{u}}}_{1}^{sc}={{\mathbf{u}}}_{2}^{sc}
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}\backslash(\overline{\Omega}_{1}\cup\overline{\Omega}_{2})
by making use of Rellich Theorem
[
17
]
. Furthermore, it yields that

(5.7)

{\mathbf{u}}_{1}({\mathbf{x}})={\mathbf{u}}_{2}({\mathbf{x}}),\ \forall{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\backslash(\overline{\Omega}_{1}\cup\overline{\Omega}_{2}).

With the help of the generalized transmission condition and the notations in (
4.2
), one has that

(5.8)

{\mathbf{u}}_{2}^{-}={\mathbf{u}}_{2}^{+}={\mathbf{u}}_{1}^{+},\quad T_{\nu}{\mathbf{u}}_{2}^{-}=T_{\nu}{\mathbf{u}}_{2}^{+}+\eta_{1,2}{\mathbf{u}}_{2}^{+}=T_{\nu}{\mathbf{u}}_{1}^{+}+\eta_{1,2}{\mathbf{u}}_{1}^{+},\quad\mbox{on}\ \Gamma_{h}^{\pm},

where the superscripts
(\cdot)^{-},(\cdot)^{+}
stand for the limits taken from
\Pi_{1,2}\Subset\Omega_{2}
and
\mathbb{R}^{2}\backslash\overline{\Omega}_{2}
respectively. Furthermore, we may assume that the neighbourhood
B_{h}(O)
is sufficiently small such that

\begin{cases}\mathcal{L}{\mathbf{u}}_{1}^{+}+{\omega}^{2}{\mathbf{u}}_{1}^{+}={\mathbf{0}},\ &\mbox{in}\quad B_{h},\\
\mathcal{L}{\mathbf{u}}_{2}^{-}+{\omega}^{2}q_{1,2}{\mathbf{u}}_{2}^{-}={\mathbf{0}},\ &\mbox{in}\quad S_{h}\Subset\Pi_{2,1},\end{cases}

where
q_{1,2}
is defined in (
5.6

{\mathbf{u}}_{1}^{+}\in H^{2}(S_{h})^{2}
and
{\mathbf{u}}_{2}^{-}\in H^{1}(S_{h})^{2}
. From Lemma
4.4
, one has
{\mathbf{u}}_{2}^{-}\in C^{\alpha}(\overline{S_{h}})^{2}
. Applying Lemma
4.3
and utilizing the continuity of
{\mathbf{u_{1}}}
at the vertex

, one has
{\mathbf{u}}_{1}({\mathbf{0}})={\mathbf{0}}
, which contradicts to the admissibility condition in Definition

. Hence we obtain that there is no corner contained within

.

Finally, we prove (
5.4
) using contradiction. We assume that
\partial\Omega_{1}\neq\partial\Omega_{2}
. From Definition
3.1
, it is evident that
\Omega_{1}
and
\Omega_{2}
are convex. Therefore, we claim that the boundary of
\mathbb{R}^{2}\backslash(\overline{\Omega}_{1}\cup\overline{\Omega}_{2})
has a corner, which contradicts the first conclusion of this theorem.

The proof is complete.
∎

For the polygonal-cell elastic scatterer
\Omega

In the following theorem, we consider the global uniqueness result for the identification of the shape of a polygonal-nest elastic scatterer

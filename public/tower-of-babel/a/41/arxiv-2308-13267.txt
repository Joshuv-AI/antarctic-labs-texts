Supersensitive phase estimation by thermal light in a Kerr-nonlinear interferometric setup
Nilakantha Meher, Eilon Poem, Tomáš Opatrný, Ofer Firstenberg, Gershon Kurizki

Abstract
Estimation of the phase delay between interferometer arms is the core of transmission phase microscopy. Such phase estimation may exhibit an error below the standard quantum (shot-noise) limit, if the input is an entangled two-mode state, e.g., a N00N state. We show, by contrast, that such supersensitive phase estimation (SSPE) is achievable by incoherent , e.g., thermal , light that is injected into a Mach-Zehnder interferometer via a Kerr-nonlinear two-mode coupler. Phase error is shown to be reduced below 1 / n ¯ 1/\bar{n} , n ¯ \bar{n} being the mean photon number, by thermal input in such interferometric setups, even for small nonlinear phase-shifts per photon pair or for significant photon loss. Remarkably, the phase accuracy achievable in such setups by thermal input surpasses that of coherent light with the same n ¯ \bar{n} . Available mode couplers with giant Kerr nonlinearity that stems either from dipole-dipole interactions of Rydberg polaritons in a cold atomic gas, or from cavity-enhanced dispersive atom-field interactions, may exploit such effects to substantially advance interferometric phase microscopy using incoherent, faint light sources.

II
Scenario and analysis principles

Consider a two-mode interferometer that incorporates a phase-shifter with an unknown phase
\phi
that we wish to estimate. The two-mode merger/coupler is endowed with self-Kerr (SK) or cross-Kerr (CK) nonlinearity [Fig.
1
(a)]: A nonlinear phase shift
2\chi
is caused by SK for two photons in the same mode, the CK counterpart being the phase shift
\chi
for two photons in different modes. The overall unitary transformation for such a Mach-Zehnder interferometer (MZI) with SK or CK nonlinearity
Chekhova and Ou 2016
;
Kitagawa and Yamamoto 1986
is, respectively,

\displaystyle U_{SK}

\displaystyle=U_{BS}U_{PS}(\phi)U_{BS}e^{i\chi a^{\dagger 2}a^{2}}U_{PS}(\pi/2)U_{BS},

(1a)

\displaystyle U_{CK}

\displaystyle=U_{BS}U_{PS}(\phi)U_{BS}e^{i\chi a^{\dagger}ab^{\dagger}b}U_{PS}(\pi/2)U_{BS}.

(1b)

Here,
U_{PS}(\phi)=e^{i\phi a^{\dagger}a}
is the operator that shifts the phase by
\phi
in mode
a
, and
U_{BS}
is the operator of a 50:50 beam splitter
Gerry and Knight 2004
. Both Hamiltonians
H_{SK}=\chi a^{\dagger 2}a^{2}
and
H_{CK}=\chi a^{\dagger}ab^{\dagger}b
commute with the photon-number operators
a^{\dagger}a
and
b^{\dagger}b
and thus, do not change the mean output photon numbers
Imoto et al. 1985
. We consider the simplest (both experimentally and calculationally) case of one empty input mode and the other mode to be in either number, coherent or thermal states
Gerry and Knight 2004
;
Ronald Waynant 1997
. Analytical results will be given for the strongest nonlinear phase-shift per photon pair,
\chi=\pi/2
.

An insightful (but not optimal) phase estimation method is based on measuring the mean value of the parity operator of one of the output modes:
\Pi_{out}=e^{i\pi b^{\dagger}_{out}b_{out}}

Gerry et al. 2002
;
Birrittella et al. 2021
. Its direct measurement by counting the corresponding photon number is challenging. Instead, Kerr nonlinearity enables a simpler parity measurement of an MZI output by sending it through a second cross-Kerr interferometer along with a vacuum input and detecting which of the two output detectors has clicked [see Fig.
S1
]
Gerry et al. 2002
. The phase error (sensitivity) obtained from the parity-operator mean value
\langle\Pi_{out}\rangle
is compactly expressed as
Gerry et al. 2002
;
Birrittella et al. 2021

\displaystyle\Delta\phi=\frac{\sqrt{1-\langle\Pi_{out}\rangle^{2}}}{\left|\frac{\partial\langle\Pi_{out}\rangle}{\partial\phi}\right|}.

(2)

In an MZI with self-Kerr (SK) and
\chi=\pi/2
, for number state input
\ket{n,0}
, the state right after the phase shifter [Fig.
1
(a)], is

\displaystyle\ket{\psi(\phi)}_{number}=\frac{i^{n}}{\sqrt{2}}e^{i\pi/4}(i^{n-1}e^{in\phi}\ket{n,0}+\ket{0,n}),

(3)

which yields at the output of the microscope
\langle\Pi_{out}\rangle=\sin n\phi
[see Appendix
A
] and
\Delta\phi=1/n
(Heisenberg scaling). One owes this supersensitive phase resolution to the fact that a number state is transformed after the second beam splitter/merger in Fig.
1
(a) into a state akin to a N00N state. Accordingly, an input thermal state is transformed by the nonlinear filter into a mixture of N00N states spanned by all
n

which yields

\displaystyle\langle\Pi_{out}\rangle=\frac{1}{1+\bar{n}}+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\bar{n}^{n}}{(1+\bar{n})^{(n+1)}}\sin n\phi.

(4)

The resulting minimum phase error [see Fig.
1
(b) and Appendix
A
]

\displaystyle\Delta\phi\approx\frac{\sqrt{1-\frac{1}{(\bar{n}+1)^{2}}}}{\bar{n}},

(5)

falls below the Heisenberg limit (HL)

1/\bar{n}

Ou 1997
;
Monras 2006
;
Hofmann 2009
;
Pinel et al. 2013
;
Boixo et al. 2007
. Importantly, similar arguments hold for a general input state
\rho_{in}=\sum_{n,m}\rho_{nm}\ket{n}\bra{m}\otimes\ket{0}\bra{0}
[see Appendix
A
]. We note that there is only moderate difference in phase sensitivity between cross-Kerr (CK) and SK, all parameters being equal [Fig.
1
(b)].

Independently of the measurement setting, the minimal phase error attainable by a given input state is set by the quantum Cramér-Rao bound
Cramér 1999
, which is related to the quantum Fisher information (QFI)
F_{Q}
through
Birrittella et al. 2021

\displaystyle\Delta\phi_{min}\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{F_{Q}}}.

(6)

To evaluate
F_{Q}
, we calculate the state right after the phase-shift operation [Fig.
1
(a)] through

\displaystyle\tilde{\rho}(\phi)=U\rho_{in}U^{\dagger},

(7a)

where

\displaystyle U

\displaystyle=U_{PS}(\phi)U_{BS}e^{i\chi a^{\dagger 2}a^{2}}U_{PS}(\pi/2)U_{BS},~~~\text{for SK}

(7b)

\displaystyle U

\displaystyle=U_{PS}(\phi)U_{BS}e^{i\chi a^{\dagger}ab^{\dagger}b}U_{PS}(\pi/2)U_{BS},~~~\text{for CK}.

(7c)

Using Eqs. (
3
) and (
7
), the transformed states after the phase-shifter in SK interferometer with
\chi=\pi/2
for coherent and thermal states input correspondingly are calculated to be [see Appendix
A
]

\displaystyle\ket{\psi({\phi})}_{coherent}=e^{\frac{-|\alpha|^{2}}{2}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(i\alpha)^{n}}{\sqrt{2}\sqrt{n!}}(i^{n-1}e^{in\phi}\ket{n,0}+\ket{0,n}),

(8a)

\displaystyle\tilde{\rho}_{thermal}(\phi)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\bar{n}^{n}}{(1+\bar{n})^{(n+1)}}\left[\ket{n,0}\bra{n,0}+\ket{0,n}\bra{0,n}\right.

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~+\left.i^{n-1}e^{in\phi}\ket{n,0}\bra{0,n}+(-i)^{n-1}e^{-in\phi}\ket{0,n}\bra{n,0}\right].

(8b)

Similarly, one can obtain the transformed states for CK interferometer.

For
\tilde{\rho}(\phi)
, the QFI is given by the formula

;
Tóth and Apellaniz 2014

\displaystyle F_{Q}(\tilde{\rho})=2\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k,l\\
\lambda_{k}+\lambda_{l}>0\end{subarray}}\frac{(\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{l})^{2}}{(\lambda_{k}+\lambda_{l})}|\langle k|a^{\dagger}a|l\rangle|^{2},

where

;
Lang and Caves 2014

\displaystyle F_{Q}=4(\langle\psi(\phi)|(a^{\dagger}a)^{2}|\psi(\phi)\rangle-|\langle\psi(\phi)|a^{\dagger}a|\psi(\phi)\rangle|^{2})=4\langle\Delta a^{\dagger}a\rangle^{2},

where
|\psi(\phi)\rangle=U\ket{\psi_{in}}

Using Eqs. (

\displaystyle(F_{Q})_{thermal}=2\bar{n}^{2}+\bar{n}>(F_{Q})_{coherent}=\bar{n}^{2}+2\bar{n}>(F_{Q})_{number}=\bar{n}^{2},~~~\text{for SK}

(11a)

III
Conclusion and discussion

Imagine a phase microscope illuminated by faint thermal light, say, under water or in space, without a bright coherent light source to serve as a local oscillator or signal. It is impossible at present to achieve sub-shot noise sensitivity with such a device. Yet, we have shown that Kerr-phase elements in interferometry can yield supersensitive phase estimation (SSPE), down to the Heisenberg limit (HL) and even below it, using incoherent, particularly thermal, input. In the absence of loss, the Kerr-nonlinear Mach-Zehnder interferometer (MZI) conserves the total number of photons, and acts on each Fock layer separately. Thus, fluctuations of the phases between Fock layers do not have any effect on the output intensities, only phases within a Fock layer affect the output photodetection.
Hence, thermal input states are well suited for SSPE with Kerr-nonlinear MZI, as long as the input phase and amplitude fluctuate slower than the time difference between the interferometer arms. Consequently, the Kerr-nonlinear MZI described here are resilient to low-frequency input noise, allowing the use of unlocked lasers, or other spectrally filtered thermal light sources.

Several important and unexpected results have emerged from our analysis:
(1) For any degree of nonlinearity and low photon numbers [Eqs. (
4
),(
5
), Fig.
1
(c)], the
1/\bar{n}
limit (so called HL) is attained by any input distribution wherein the photon number variance grows as the mean photon number increases, be it pure or mixed. Remarkably, the achievable phase sensitivity of thermal input surpasses that of pure states in terms of the quantum Fisher information [Eq. (
11
)] for highly efficient photodetection. For thermal input, SSPE is achievable for detection efficiency down to 0.9 and up to photon loss levels of 25%.
(2) Even for weak nonlinearity, the Kerr-nonlinear interferometer creates states that can strongly improve phase sensitivity [Fig.
1
(c)]. (3) While SSPE is achievable for both cross-Kerr (CK) and self-Kerr (SK) nonlinearities, CK provides better sensitivity when the nonlinear strength per photon pair is much smaller than
\pi/2
[Fig.
1
(c)].

Comparison with previous SSPE schemes based on Gaussian squeezed states plagued by noise
Monras 2006
;
Pinel et al. 2013
;
Nielsen et al. 2023
;
Aspachs et al. 2009
shows the advantages of the present Kerr-nonlinear interferometric scheme that is (i) impervious to noise as opposed to squeezed state schemes [Appendix
G
]; (ii) does not require the satisfaction of standard entanglement [Appendix
E
] or nonclassicality criteria [Appendix
F
] and allows information collection by photon-counting or parity measurements without the resources employed by existing schemes
Takeoka et al. 2017
;
Jarzyna and Demkowicz-Dobrzański 2012
[Appendix
H
].

Suitable Kerr-nonlinear MZI may be implemented based on the giant dipole-dipole interaction between counter-propagating few-photon beams in a cloud of cold alkali atoms excited to a Rydberg state
Friedler et al. 2005
;
Shahmoon et al. 2011
.
The scheme invokes electromagnetically induced transparency (EIT) in the slightly-off resonant case.
The detuning can be chosen such that, while both with and without interaction the transmission is the same, the group velocity, and hence, the induced phase, are different depending on the photon numbers in the beam. This scheme has been implemented to yield a Kerr nonlinear phase of over
\pi
for a single photon pair
Firstenberg et al. 2013
;
Drori et al. 2023
. Similar two-photon phase shifts have also been demonstrated in the dispersive regime of atom-photon interactions in ultra high-
Q
cavities
Vrajitoarea et al. 2020
;
Stolz et al. 2022
;
Bechler et al. 2018
and tapered fibers
Lodahl et al. 2017
.

A Kerr-nonlinear phase element can be used not only as mode coupler within the MZI, but also as a parity filter of the measured output mode [Appendix
B
]. Such filters may bypass the need for photon-number resolved detectors. Another applicaton of Kerr-nonlinear interferometers proposed by us
Opatrný et al. 2023
has been their use as nonlinear coherent heat machines.

The present findings constitute guidelines for nonlinear interferometer implementation that can open new avenues in phase sensing: the ability to achieve SSPE with classical, incoherent, extremely faint light may bring about a paradigm shift in this field.

Appendix A
Parity operator mean values in Kerr-nonlinear Mach-Zehnder interferometer (MZI)

A.1
Self-Kerr (SK) MZI

For an input two-mode number state
\ket{n,0}
, the state right before the final beam splitter [Fig.
1
(a) of text], is

\displaystyle\ket{\psi(\phi)}=\frac{i^{n}}{\sqrt{2}}e^{i\pi/4}(i^{n-1}e^{in\phi}\ket{n,0}+\ket{0,n}).

(12)

To find the expectation value of the corresponding parity operator at the output, we apply to the parity operator the beam splitter (BS) transformation

\displaystyle\Pi_{out}=U_{BS}^{\dagger}\Pi U_{BS}=\sum_{M}(-i)^{M}\sum_{k=0}^{M}(-1)^{k}\ket{k,M-k}\bra{M-k,k}.

(13)

We then have for its expectation value

\displaystyle\langle\Pi_{out}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}((-i)^{n-1}e^{-in\phi}\bra{n,0}+\bra{0,n})\left[\sum_{M}(-i)^{M}\sum_{k=0}^{M}(-1)^{k}\ket{k,M-k}\bra{M-k,k}\right](i^{n-1}e^{in\phi}\ket{n,0}+\ket{0,n})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}((-i)^{n-1}e^{-in\phi}\bra{n,0}+\bra{0,n})\left[(-i)^{n}i^{n-1}e^{in\phi}\ket{0,n}+(-i)^{n}(-1)^{n}\ket{n,0}\right]

\displaystyle=\sin n\phi.

(14)

The phase error is then given by

\displaystyle\Delta\phi=\frac{\sqrt{1-\langle\Pi_{out}\rangle^{2}}}{\left|\frac{\partial\langle\Pi_{out}\rangle}{\partial\phi}\right|}=\frac{1}{n}.

(15)

For coherent state
\ket{\alpha,0}
input, we get

\displaystyle\langle\Pi_{out}\rangle

\displaystyle=e^{-|\alpha|^{2}}\left[1+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{|\alpha|^{2n}}{n!}\sin n\phi\right]

\displaystyle=e^{-|\alpha|^{2}}\left[1+e^{|\alpha|^{2}\cos\phi}\sin(|\alpha|^{2}\sin\phi)\right],

(16)

As
\phi\rightarrow 0
, this expression becomes

\displaystyle\langle\Pi_{out}\rangle\approx e^{-|\alpha|^{2}}+\sin(|\alpha|^{2}\phi),

(17)

so that for large
|\alpha|^{2}

\displaystyle\Delta\phi\approx\frac{\sqrt{1-e^{-|\alpha|^{2}}+\sin(|\alpha|^{2}\phi)}}{\left||\alpha|^{2}\cos(|\alpha|^{2}\phi)\right|}\approx\frac{1}{|\alpha|^{2}}=\frac{1}{\bar{n}}.

(18)

For thermal state input, by averaging Eq. (
A.1
) over the thermal number-state distribution with average
\bar{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\langle\Pi_{out}\rangle=\frac{1}{1+\bar{n}}+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\bar{n}^{n}}{(1+\bar{n})^{(n+1)}}\sin n\phi,

(19)

As
\phi\rightarrow 0
, this expression becomes

\displaystyle\langle\Pi_{out}\rangle\approx\frac{1}{1+\bar{n}},

(20)

and

\displaystyle\langle\frac{\partial\Pi_{out}}{\partial\phi}\rangle\approx\bar{n},

(21)

which yields, for large
\bar{n}
, the phase error

\displaystyle\Delta\phi\approx\frac{\sqrt{1-\frac{1}{(\bar{n}+1)^{2}}}}{\bar{n}}\approx\frac{1}{\bar{n}}.

(22)

For a general input state

\displaystyle\rho_{in}=\sum_{n}\rho_{nm}\ket{n}\bra{m}\otimes\ket{0}\bra{0},

(23)

the output expectation value of the parity operator becomes

\displaystyle\langle\Pi_{out}\rangle=|\rho_{00}|^{2}+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|\rho_{nn}|^{2}\sin n\phi.

(24)

As
\phi\rightarrow 0
,

\displaystyle\langle\Pi_{out}\rangle\approx|\rho_{00}|^{2},

(25)

and

\displaystyle\langle\frac{\partial\Pi_{out}}{\partial\phi}\rangle\approx\bar{n},

(26)

which yields for large
\bar{n}

\displaystyle\Delta\phi\approx\frac{\sqrt{1-|\rho_{00}|^{2}}}{\bar{n}}\approx\frac{1}{\bar{n}}.

(27)

A.2
Cross-Kerr (CK) MZI

For input number state
\ket{n,0}
, we have the output state right before the final BS

\displaystyle\ket{\psi(\phi)}\propto\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(e^{in\phi}\ket{n,0}+\ket{0,n})~~~\text{for odd n}.

(28)

leading to

\displaystyle\langle\Pi\rangle=-i^{n+1}\sin n\phi~~~\text{for odd n}.

(29)

For even
n
, the state
\ket{\psi(\phi)}
is proportional to a beam splitter transformation, leading to

\displaystyle\langle\Pi\rangle=\sin^{n}\phi~~~~\text{for even n}.

(30)

Therefore, number state with odd
n
yields the Heisenberg scaling (HL),
\Delta\phi\sim 1/n
, while even
n
leads to the standard quantum limit (SQL),
\Delta\phi\sim 1/\sqrt{n}
.

For coherent state input, we get

\displaystyle\langle\Pi_{out}\rangle=e^{-|\alpha|^{2}}\left[1-\sum_{\text{odd}~n}\frac{|\alpha|^{2n}}{n!}i^{n+1}\sin n\phi+\sum_{\text{even}~n}\frac{|\alpha|^{2n}}{n!}\sin^{n}\phi\right].

(31)

For
\phi\approx\pi/2
with large
\bar{n}
, we get

\displaystyle\Delta\phi\approx\frac{1}{|\alpha|^{2}}.

(32)

Similarly, for thermal input, we get

\displaystyle\langle\Pi_{out}\rangle=\left[\frac{1}{1+\bar{n}}-\sum_{\text{odd}~n}\frac{\bar{n}^{n}}{(1+\bar{n})^{(n+1)}}i^{n+1}\sin n\phi+\sum_{\text{even}~n}\frac{\bar{n}^{n}}{(1+\bar{n})^{(n+1)}}\sin^{n}\phi\right],

(33)

and the sensitivity for
\phi\approx\pi/2
, for large
\bar{n}
, is then

\displaystyle\Delta\phi\approx\frac{1}{\bar{n}}.

(34)

The parity of the output mode
b_{out}

Gerry et al. 2002

\ket{n_{b},0_{c}}

\displaystyle\tilde{U}_{CK}\ket{n_{b},0_{c}}=i^{n}\ket{n_{1},0_{2}},~~\text{for even n}

\displaystyle\tilde{U}_{CK}\ket{n_{b},0_{c}}=i^{n}\ket{0_{1},n_{2}}.~~\text{for odd n}

and

Appendix C
Quantum Fisher Information (QFI) for CK and SK MZI outputs

According to the quantum Cramér-Rao bound
Cramér 1999
, the optimal phase sensitivity is bounded by

\displaystyle\Delta\phi_{min}\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{kF_{Q}}},

(36)

where
k
is the number of measurements that can be taken to be 1 and
F_{Q}
is the quantum Fisher information that depends neither on the measurement nor on
the estimator and is solely a function of the probing state.

The input state
\rho_{in}
is transformed after the phase-shift operation [Fig.
1
(a) in the text] to

\displaystyle\tilde{\rho}(\phi)=U\rho_{in}U^{\dagger},

(37)

where
U=U_{PS}(\phi)U_{BS}e^{i\chi a^{\dagger 2}a^{2}}U_{PS}(\pi/2)U_{BS}
for SK and
U=U_{PS}(\phi)U_{BS}e^{i\chi a^{\dagger}ab^{\dagger}b}U_{PS}(\pi/2)U_{BS}
for CK.

The corresponding QFI is given by the formula
Birrittella et al. 2021
;
Tóth and Apellaniz 2014

\displaystyle F_{Q}(\tilde{\rho})=2\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k,l\\
\lambda_{k}+\lambda_{l}>0\end{subarray}}\frac{(\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{l})^{2}}{(\lambda_{k}+\lambda_{l})}|\langle k|a^{\dagger}a|l\rangle|^{2},

(38)

where
\{\lambda_{k},\ket{k}\}
are the eigenvalues and their corresponding eigenvectors of
\tilde{\rho}(\phi)
.

For a pure state input
Lang and Caves 2013
;
Lang and Caves 2014
, the above expression reduces to

\displaystyle F_{Q}=4(\langle\dot{\psi}(\phi)|\dot{\psi}(\phi)\rangle-|\langle\dot{\psi}(\phi)|\psi(\phi)\rangle|^{2}),

(39)

where
|\dot{\psi}(\phi)\rangle=\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi}\ket{\psi(\phi)}=ia^{\dagger}a\ket{\psi(\phi)}
. Thus,

\displaystyle F_{Q}=4(\langle\psi(\phi)|(a^{\dagger}a)^{2}|\psi(\phi)\rangle-|\langle\psi(\phi)|a^{\dagger}a|\psi(\phi)\rangle|^{2})=4\langle\Delta a^{\dagger}a\rangle^{2},

(40)

whereas for a mixed state,

\displaystyle F_{Q}\leq 4\langle\Delta a^{\dagger}a\rangle^{2}.

(41)

C.1
SK interferometer output QFI

For number-state input
\ket{n,0}
, we get
\ket{\psi(\phi)}
as given in Eq. (
12
), and the expectation values are

\displaystyle\langle\psi(\phi)|(a^{\dagger}a)^{2}|\psi(\phi)\rangle=\frac{1}{2}n^{2},\langle\psi(\phi)|(a^{\dagger}a)|\psi(\phi)\rangle=\frac{1}{2}n.

(42)

Using these expectation values, we get

\displaystyle(F_{Q})_{number}=n^{2},~~~~\Delta\phi_{min}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{F_{Q}}}=\frac{1}{n}.

(43)

Similarly, for coherent-state input

\displaystyle\langle\psi(\phi)|(a^{\dagger}a)^{2}|\psi(\phi)\rangle=\frac{1}{2}|\alpha|^{4}+\frac{1}{2}|\alpha|^{2},\langle\psi(\phi)|(a^{\dagger}a)|\psi(\phi)\rangle=\frac{1}{2}|\alpha|^{2},

(44)

and

\displaystyle(F_{Q})_{coherent}=|\alpha|^{4}+2|\alpha|^{2}=\bar{n}^{2}+2\bar{n},~~~~\Delta\phi_{min}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{F_{Q}}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\bar{n}^{2}+2\bar{n}}}.

(45)

For thermal-state input, using Eq. (
38
), we get

\displaystyle(F_{Q})_{thermal}=2\bar{n}^{2}+\bar{n},~~~~\Delta\phi_{min}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{F_{Q}}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\bar{n}^{2}+\bar{n}}}.

(46)

Our results do not depend on the choice of the phase generator Hamiltonian
Jarzyna and Demkowicz-Dobrzański 2012
. This can be shown by comparing the QFI obtained for the two-mode phase-generator Hamiltonian
H_{2mode}=\phi N_{d}/2
, where
N_{d}=a^{\dagger}a-b^{\dagger}b
is the two-mode number difference operator, with that obtained for the single-mode (single arm) phase generator Hamiltonian,
H_{1mode}=\phi a^{\dagger}a
in our Kerr-nonlinear MZI scheme.

It is straightforward to show that
H_{2mode}
yields the same form of
F_{Q}
for pure- or mixed-state input as Eqs. (
38
-
41
) upon replacing
a^{\dagger}a
by
N_{d}/2
.

In the self-Kerr (SK) interferometer with
\chi=\pi/2
and
H_{2mode}
, we then obtain the following:

For number-state input,

\displaystyle\langle N_{d}^{2}\rangle=n^{2},\langle N_{d}\rangle=0;(F^{\prime}_{Q})_{number}=n^{2};

(47a)

for coherent-state input,

\displaystyle\langle N_{d}^{2}\rangle=\bar{n}^{2}+\bar{n},\langle N_{d}\rangle=0;(F^{\prime}_{Q})_{coherent}=\bar{n}^{2}+\bar{n};

(47b)

and for thermal-state input,

\displaystyle(F^{\prime}_{Q})_{thermal}=2\bar{n}^{2}+\bar{n}.

(47c)

Comparing these results with Eq. (
11
a) of the main text, we find that the above QFI values are the same as those obtained with the single-mode phase generator
H_{1mode}
for number-state and thermal-state inputs. The QFI for the coherent-state input is different for the two phase generators, but for both the phase sensitivity still surpasses the HL.
Importantly, thermal state input exhibits QFI higher than that of the coherent state input, be it an interferometer with single-mode or two-mode phase generator. Similar conclusions hold for the cross-Kerr (CK) interferometer.

Thus, the QFI in SK/CK MZI for thermal- and number-state input is independent of the choice of the phase generators. Therefore, a local oscillator is not needed for phase reference in the CK/SK MZI scheme.

Appendix G
Comparison of state preparation schemes and their QFI

Quantum states with undefined number of photons are known to allow phase estimation with a sensitivity that surpasses the Heisenberg limit (HL). The few quantum states known to allow such supersensitivity are all Gaussian, and among them, squeezed vacuum states are the optimal
Monras 2006
. However, experimental generation of squeezed vacuum states with large photon numbers, without any noise, is an inherently insurmountable challenge
Vahlbruch et al. 2008
;
Nielsen et al. 2023
because the generated state has inevitably extra noise in its anti-squeezing quadrature
Yu et al. 2020
.

By contrast, in our case, the probe state generated from thermal input in an interferometer with either cross-Kerr (CK) or self-Kerr (SK) nonlinearity is very noisy but this does not preclude its ability to surpass the HL.

Experimentally, the challenge has been for years to achieve large Kerr nonlinearity per photon pair, but this challenge has been overcome by our team in a Rydberg polariton setup
Firstenberg et al. 2013
;
Peyronel et al. 2012
;
Drori et al. 2023
and by others in circuit QED setups
Vrajitoarea et al. 2020
. A large mean photon number does not present an experimental challenge for Kerr nonlinear MZI, and our results show that, if needed, bright noisy sources with
\bar{n}\gg 1
can be employed with the same scaling of the quantum Fisher information (QFI) as per Eqs. (
11
a) and (
11
b) in the text.

Appendix H
Comparison of measurement schemes

The best possible measurement schemes currently employed to surpass the HL using squeezed vacuum states are either positive-operator valued measurement (POVM) or homodyne measurements
Monras 2006
;
Nielsen et al. 2023

In the case of POVM, finding the optimal positive operators for attaining such a precision is a highly nontrivial undertaking. Even if they happen to be known, the optimal POVM depends on the true value of the phase to be estimated
Monras 2006
. Therefore, the measurement at a given step relies on the data from previous steps.

In a homodyne measurement scheme, measuring a quadrature operator
\hat{X}
for squeezed vacuum input does not yield any information about the phase, because
\langle\hat{X}\rangle=0
. One may choose
\hat{X}^{2}
as an estimator. However, the optimal phase to be estimated then becomes dependent on the input photon number
Nielsen et al. 2023
. As a result, for a given input photon number, one needs to set apriori the phase to be estimated. Alternatively, Bayesian estimation process requires to estimate the phase from the data obtained by measuring many samples
Nielsen et al. 2023
. Yet, due to the intrinsic nature of squeezed vacuum, homodyne measurements can attain the HL and surpass it only for a small range of phases.

By contrast, the measurement techniques that we invoke to attain the quantum Cramér-Rao bound are photon-count and parity measurements. The Fisher information for these measurements becomes almost independent of the phase to be estimated for all photon numbers.

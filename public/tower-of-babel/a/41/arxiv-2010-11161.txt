Factoring periodic maps into Dehn twists
Neeraj K. Dhanwani, Ajay K. Nair, Kashyap Rajeevsarathy

Abstract
. Let Mod ⁡ ( S g ) \mathrm{Mod}(S_{g}) be the mapping class group of the closed orientable surface S g S_{g} of genus g ≥ 1 g\geq 1 . In this paper, we develop various methods for factoring periodic mapping classes into Dehn twists, up to conjugacy. As applications, we develop methods for factoring certain roots of Dehn twists as words in Dehn twists. We will also show the existence of conjugates of periodic maps of order 4 ​ g 4g and 4 ​ g + 2 4g+2 , for g ≥ 2 g\geq 2 , whose product is pseudo-Anosov.

2.1.
Periodic mapping classes

For
g\geq 1
, let
F\in\mathrm{Mod}(S_{g})
be of order
n
. By the Nielsen-Kerckhoff theorem
[
14
,
23
]
,
F
is represented by a
standard representative

\mathcal{F}\in\mathrm{Homeo}^{+}(S_{g})
of order
n
. Let
\mathcal{O}_{F}:=S_{g}/\langle\mathcal{F}\rangle
be the
corresponding orbifold
of
F
of genus
g_{0}
(say). Each cone point
x_{i}\in\mathcal{O}_{F}
lifts under the branched cover
S_{g}\to S_{g}/\langle\mathcal{F}\rangle
to an orbit of size
n/n_{i}
on
S_{g}
, where the local rotation induced by
\mathcal{F}
is given by
2\pi c_{i}^{-1}/n_{i}
,
c_{i}c_{i}^{-1}\equiv 1\pmod{n_{i}}
. The tuple
\Gamma(\mathcal{O}_{F}):=(g_{0};n_{1},\ldots,n_{\ell})
, is called the
signature of

\mathcal{O}_{F}
. By the theory of group actions on surfaces (see
[
10
]
and the references therein), we obtain an exact sequence:

1\rightarrow\pi_{1}(S_{g})\rightarrow\pi_{1}^{orb}(\mathcal{O}_{F})\xrightarrow{\Phi_{F}}\langle\mathcal{F}\rangle\rightarrow 1,

where the orbifold fundamental group
\pi_{1}^{orb}(\mathcal{O}_{F})
of
\mathcal{O}_{F}
has the presentation

\left\langle\alpha_{1},\beta_{1},\dots,\alpha_{g_{0}},\beta_{g_{0}},\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{\ell}\,|\,\xi_{1}^{n_{1}},\dots,\xi_{\ell}^{n_{\ell}},\,\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\xi_{j}\prod_{i=1}^{g_{0}}[\alpha_{i},\beta_{i}]\right\rangle

and
\Phi_{F}(\xi_{i})=\mathcal{F}^{(n/n_{i})c_{i}}
, for
1\leq i\leq\ell
. We will now define a tuple of integers that will encode the conjugacy class of a periodic mapping class
F\in\mathrm{Mod}(S_{g})
of order
n
in
\mathrm{Mod}(S_{g})
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
data set of degree
n
is a tuple

D=(n,g_{0},r;(c_{1},n_{1}),\ldots,(c_{\ell},n_{\ell})),

where
n\geq 2
,
g_{0}\geq 0
, and
0\leq r\leq n-1
are integers, and each
c_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{n_{i}}^{\times}
such that:

(i)

r>0
if and only if
\ell=0
and
\gcd(r,n)=1
, whenever
r>0
,

(ii)

each
n_{i}\mid n
,

(iii)

\mathrm{lcm}(n_{1},\ldots\widehat{n_{i}},\ldots,n_{\ell})=N
, for
1\leq i\leq\ell
, where
N=n
, if
g_{0}=0
, and

(iv)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\frac{n}{n_{j}}c_{j}\equiv 0\pmod{n}
.

The number
g
determined by the Riemann-Hurwitz equation

\frac{2-2g}{n}=2-2g_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\left(\frac{1}{n_{j}}-1\right)

is called the genus of the data set, denoted by
g(D)
.

The following proposition, which allows us to use data sets to represent the conjugacy classes of cyclic actions on
S_{g}
, is well known in literature (see
[
1
,
11
]
, and
[
28
, Theorem 3.89]
). However, it follows mainly from a result of Nielsen
[
22
]
.

Proposition 2.2
.

For
g\geq 1
and
n\geq 2
, data sets of degree
n
and genus
g
correspond to conjugacy classes of
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
-actions on
S_{g}
.

We will denote the data set encoding the conjugacy class of a periodic mapping class
F
by
D_{F}
. The parameter
r
(in Definition
2.1
) will come into play in a data set
D_{F}
only when
\mathcal{F}
is a free rotation of
S_{g}
by
2\pi r/n
, in which case,
D_{F}
will take the form
(n,g_{0},r;)
. We will avoid including
r
in the notation of a data set
D_{F}
, whenever
\mathcal{F}
is non-free. Furthermore, for compactness of notation, we also write a data set
D
(as in Definition
2.1
) as

D=(n,g_{0},r;((d_{1},m_{1}),\alpha_{1}),\ldots,((d_{\ell^{\prime}},m_{\ell^{\prime}}),\alpha_{\ell^{\prime}})),

where
(d_{i},m_{i})
are the distinct pairs in the multiset
S=\{(c_{1},n_{1}),\ldots,(c_{\ell},n_{\ell})\}
, and the
\alpha_{i}
denote the multiplicity of the pair
(d_{i},m_{i})
in the multiset
S=\{(c_{1},n_{1}),\ldots,(c_{\ell},n_{\ell})\}
. For simplicity of notation, we shall avoid writing the parameters
\alpha_{i}
when they equal
1
.

Let
F\in\mathrm{Mod}(S_{g})
be of order
n
. Then
F
is said to be
rotational
if
\mathcal{F}
is a rotation of the
S_{g}
through an axis by
2\pi r/n
, where
\gcd(r,n)=1
. It is apparent that
\mathcal{F}
is either has no fixed points, or
2k
fixed points which are induced at the points of intersection of the axis of rotation with
S_{g}
. Moreover, these fixed points will form
k
pairs of points
(x_{i},x_{i}^{\prime})
, for
1\leq i\leq k
, such that the sum of the angles of rotation induced by
\mathcal{F}
around
x_{i}
and
x_{i}^{\prime}
add up to
0
modulo
2\pi
. Consequently, we have the following:

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
F\in\mathrm{Mod}(S_{g})
be a rotational mapping class of order
n
.

(i)

When
\mathcal{F}
is a non-free rotation, then
D_{F}
has the form

(n,g_{0};\underbrace{(s,n),(n-s,n),\ldots,(s,n),(n-s,n)}_{k\,pairs}),

for integers
k\geq 1
and
0<s\leq n-1
with
\gcd(s,n)=1
, and
k=1
if
n>2
.

(ii)

When
\mathcal{F}
is a free rotation, then
D_{F}
has the form

(n,\frac{g-1}{n}+1,r;).

We say
F
is of
Type 1
if
\Gamma(\mathcal{O}_{F})
has the form
(g_{0};n_{1},n_{2},n)
, and
F
is said to be of
Type 2
if
F
is neither rotational nor of Type 1. Gilman
[
9
]
showed that a periodic mapping class
F\in\mathrm{Mod}(S_{g})
is irreducible if and only if
\mathcal{O}_{F}
is a sphere with three cone points. Thus,
F
is an irreducible Type 1 mapping class if and only if
\Gamma(\mathcal{O}_{F})
has the form
(0;n_{1},n_{2},n)
.

2.2.
Decomposing periodic maps into irreducibles

In
[
3
,
24
]
, a method was described to construct an arbitrary non-rotational periodic element
F\in\mathrm{Mod}(S_{g})

g\geq 2

Theorem 2.4

(
[
24
, Theorem 2.7]
)
.

For
g\geq 2
, consider an irreducible Type 1 action
F\in{\mathrm{Mod}}(S_{g})
with

D_{F}=(n,0;(c_{1},n_{1}),\linebreak(c_{2},n_{2}),(c_{3},n)).

Then
F
can be realized explicitly as the rotation
\theta_{F}=2\pi c_{3}^{-1}/n
of a hyperbolic polygon
\mathcal{P}_{F}
with a suitable side-pairing
W(\mathcal{P}_{F})
, where
\mathcal{P}_{F}
is a hyperbolic
k(F)
-gon with

k(F):=\begin{cases}2n,&\text{ if }n_{1},n_{2}\neq 2,\text{ and }\\
n,&\text{otherwise, }\end{cases}

and for
0\leq m\leq n-1
,

W(\mathcal{P}_{F})=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}a_{2i-1}a_{2i}\text{ with }a_{2m+1}^{-1}\sim a_{2z},&\text{if }k(F)=2n,\text{ and }\\
\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\text{ with }a_{m+1}^{-1}\sim a_{z},&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

where
\displaystyle z\equiv m+qj\pmod{n}
with
q=(n/n_{2})c_{3}^{-1}
and
j=n_{2}-c_{2}
.

Consequently, the process yielded a concrete construction of a hyperbolic metric invariant under the cyclic action. Further, it was shown that the process of decomposition can be reversed by piecing together the irreducible Type 1 components (described in Theorem
2.4

Remark 2.9

First we note that the

4.
Chain method

In this section, we provide a method by which one can write certain periodic mapping classes as words in Dehn twists by repeated application of the chain relation. Let
F\in\mathrm{Mod}(S_{g})
be an irreducible Type 1 mapping class. Let
F\in\mathrm{Mod}(S_{g})
be of order
n
, and let
(1,n)
be a pair in
D_{F}
representing a fixed point of the
\langle\mathcal{F}\rangle
-action on
S_{g}
. Now consider the mapping class
F^{m}
, for some integer
1\leq m\leq|F|
. Then in
D_{F^{m}}
, there exists a pair
(c^{\prime},n^{\prime})
(representing a fixed point of the
\langle\mathcal{F}^{m}\rangle
-action on
S_{g}
) that originated from the pair
(1,n)
such that
n^{\prime}=|F^{m}|=n/\gcd(m,n)
and
(c^{\prime})^{-1}\equiv m/\gcd(m,n)\pmod{n^{\prime}}
. We will denote this pair
(c^{\prime},n^{\prime})
in
D_{F^{m}}
by
(1,n)_{m,F}
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let

be realizable as a linear
s
-tuple
(F_{1},\ldots,F_{s})
of degree

and genus

admits a realization as a linear
s
-tuple

of genus

(i)

For

, a filling chain
\mathcal{C}(\tilde{F}_{i})
in
S_{g_{i}}
, and an

such that

is conjugate to

. Then:

(a)

For each

,

has one of the following forms on

(2{g_{i}}+1,0;(2{g_{i}}-1,2{g_{i}}+1),(1,2{g_{i}}+1),(1,2{g_{i}}+1))

(2{g_{i}}+2,0;({g_{i}},{g_{i}}+1),(1,2{g_{i}}+2),(1,2{g_{i}}+2))

(4{g_{i}},0;(1,2),(1,4{g_{i}}),(2{g_{i}}-1,4{g_{i}}))

Definition 4.2
.

A periodic mapping class

-tuple

Given

.

Let

\mathcal{W}(F)=\prod_{i=1}^{s}\mathcal{W}(F_{i})

Proof.

By Proposition

, for each

where

6.2.
Discarding redundant words

To begin with, we apply the basic property that Dehn twists about isotopically disjoint curves in
S_{g}
commute, we would like to discard redundant variants of words that are equivalent up to commutativity of the twists in
\mathcal{L}_{g}

by applying the Penner’s construction
[

Let

7.2.
Roots of Dehn twists

For
g\geq 2
, let
c
be a simple closed curve in
S_{g}
. A
root of
T_{c}
of degree n
is an
F\in\mathrm{Mod}(S_{g})
such that
F^{n}=T_{c}
.

7.2.1.
Dehn twists about nonseparating curves

When
c
is nonseparating, Margalit-Schleimer
[
17
]
gave the first example of such a root of degree
2g-1
in
\mathrm{Mod}(S_{g})
. A complete classification of such roots was obtained
[
21
]
, where it was also shown that the Margalit-Scleimer root (of degree
2g-1
) had the largest possible degree in
\mathrm{Mod}(S_{g})
. A periodic mapping class
\bar{F}\in\mathrm{Mod}(S_{g-1})
is said to be
degree n root-realizing
if the
\langle\bar{\mathcal{F}}\rangle
-action on
S_{g-1}
has two distinguished fixed points where the induced local rotation angles add up to
2\pi/n\pmod{2\pi}
. Given a root-realizing
\bar{F}\in\mathrm{Mod}(S_{g-1})
of order
n
with distinguished fixed points
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
, one can remove
\langle\bar{\mathcal{F}}\rangle
-invariant neighborhoods around the
P_{i}
and then attach an annulus
A
with an
(1/n)^{th}
-twist connecting the resulting boundary components to realize a root
F\in\mathrm{Mod}(S_{g})
of a Dehn twist
T_{c}
about the non-separating curve
c
in
A
. Conversely, for
g\geq 2
, given a root
F\in\mathrm{Mod}(S_{g})
of
T_{c}
of degree
n
, one can reverse this process to recover a root-realizing periodic mapping class
\bar{F}\in\mathrm{Mod}(S_{g-1})
. Thus, the conjugacy class of a typical root realizing
\bar{F}\in\mathrm{Mod}(S_{g-1})
that corresponds to the conjugacy class of a root
F\in\mathrm{Mod}(S_{g})
of degree
n
has the form

D_{\bar{F}}=(n,g_{0};(a,n),(b,n),(c_{1},n_{1}),\ldots,(c_{\ell},n_{\ell})),

where
a+b\equiv ab\pmod{n}
. Here the pairs
(a,n)
and
(b,n)
represent the fixed points (of the
\langle\bar{\mathcal{F}}\rangle
-action) involved in the construction of the root
F
. We will now describe a family of roots that can be represented as words in Dehn twists by using a minor modification of the method described in Algorithm
5.8
.

Definition 7.1
.

A root
F\in\mathrm{Mod}(S_{g})
of
T_{c}
of degree
n
is said to be
star-realizable
if the following conditions hold.

(i)

The root-realizing periodic mapping class
\bar{F}\in\mathrm{Mod}(S_{g-1})
with

D_{\bar{F}}=(n,g_{0};(a,n),(b,n),(c_{1},n_{1}),\ldots,(c_{\ell},n_{\ell})),

a+b\equiv ab\pmod{n}
, is star-realizable.

(ii)

Suppose that
\bar{F}=F_{\mathcal{T}}^{m}
, for some star-realizable
F_{\mathcal{T}}
as in Definition
5.6
so that the pairs
(a,n)
(resp.
(b,n)
) belong to
D_{F_{i}}
(resp.
D_{F_{j}}
). Then

\displaystyle(a,n)\in\{(1,|\tilde{F}_{i}|)_{m_{i},\tilde{F}_{i}},(|\tilde{F}_{i}|/2-1,|\tilde{F}_{i}|)_{m_{i},\tilde{F}_{i}},(|\tilde{F}_{i}|-2,|\tilde{F}_{i}|)_{m_{i},\tilde{F}_{i}}\}

\displaystyle(b,n)\in\{(1,|\tilde{F}_{j}|)_{{m_{j},\tilde{F}_{j}}},(|\tilde{F}_{j}|/2-1,|\tilde{F}_{j}|)_{m_{j},\tilde{F}_{j}},(|\tilde{F}_{j}|-2,|\tilde{F}_{j}|)_{m_{j},\tilde{F}_{j}}\}

Denoting

Consider a star-realizable root

of

Step

7.2.2.

For

, a

T_{c}
of degree
(m,n)
is an
F\in\mathrm{Mod}(S_{g})
such that
F^{n}=T_{c}^{m}
. It is known
[

]
that such a root of
T_{c}
may either preserve or reverse the two sides of

, and a side-preserving
F
of degree
(m,n)
satisfies
n\leq 4g-4
. Further, a side-preserving fractional root of degree
(2g-2,4g-4)
always exists in
\mathrm{Mod}(S_{g})
. As in the case of roots of Dehn twists, a side-preserving fractional root
F\in\mathrm{Mod}(S_{g})
of degree
(m,n)
corresponds to an
\bar{F}\in\mathrm{Mod}(S_{g-1})
of order
n
such that the
\langle\bar{\mathcal{F}}\rangle
-action has two distinguished fixed points where the induced rotation angles add up to
2\pi m/n\pmod{2\pi}
. In the following result, we will assume the notation of Theorem
5.1
.

Proposition 7.4
.

There exists a conjugacy class of a side-preserving fractional root of
T_{a_{1}}
of degree
(2g-2,4g-4)
represented by

T_{a_{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{g-1}(T_{c_{i}}T_{b_{i+1}}).

Proof.

Any root
F
of
T_{a_{1}}
of degree
(2g-2,4g-4)
is realized from an
\bar{F}\in\mathrm{Mod}(S_{g-1})
by attaching an annulus (with a
2\pi m/n
-twist) connecting the two boundary components (
d_{1}
and
d_{2}
) of the subsurface
S_{g-1}^{2}
. In particular, we consider the case when
D_{\bar{F}}=(4g-4,0;(1,2),(1,4g-4),(2g-3,4g-4))
. By Theorem
5.1
(for
k=2
) and Lemma
5.3
, we have

(T_{a_{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{g-1}(T_{c_{i}}T_{b_{i+1}}))^{4g-4}=T_{a_{1}}T_{a_{1}}^{{(2g-3)^{+}}}.

But, as
(2g-3)^{+}\equiv 2g-3\pmod{4g-4}
, this further simplifies to

(T_{a_{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{g-1}(T_{c_{i}}T_{b_{i+1}}))^{4g-4}=T_{a_{1}}^{2g-2},

from which our assertion follows.
∎

7.2.3.
Dehn twists about separating curves

Let
c
be a separating curve in
S_{g}
so that
S_{g}=S_{g_{1}}\#_{c}S_{g_{2}}
. A pair
(F_{1},F_{2})
of periodic mapping classes, where

have distinguished fixed points

where the induced local rotation angles add up to

2\pi
. It was shown in
[
26
]
that root

of
T_{c}
of degree
n
corresponds to a degree
n
root-realizing pair
(F_{1},F_{2})
of periodic mapping classes. Following the theory in
[
26
]
, we consider the degree
4g_{2}+2
root realizing pair
(F_{1},F_{2})
, where
F_{1}\in\mathrm{Mod}(S_{g_{1}})
is the hyperelliptic involution with

and

Proposition 7.5
.

For
g\geq 2
, let
c
be separating curve in
S_{g}
so that

7.3.
A construction of pseudo-Anosov mapping classes

In this subsection, we show that for
g\geq 2
, there exists conjugates of the periodic mapping classes
W_{g,4g}
and
W_{g,4g+2}
(from Definition
5.2
) whose product is pseudo-Anosov. In this connection, we will use the following symplectic (sufficient) criterion
[
18
, Proposition 2]
for a given mapping class to be pseudo-Anosov (originally due to Casson-Bleiler
[
6
]
).

Theorem 7.8
.

Let
F\in\mathrm{Mod}(S_{g})
and let
P_{F}(x)
be the characteristic polynomial of
\Psi(F)
. Suppose that each of the following conditions hold.

(i)

P_{F}(x)
is symplectically irreducible over
\mathbb{Z}
.

(ii)

P_{F}(x)
is not a cyclotomic polynomial.

(iii)

P_{F}(x)
is not a polynomial in
x^{k}
for any
k>1
.

Then
F
is pseudo-Anosov.

We now consider the conjugates

W_{4g+2}^{\prime}=(T_{a_{1}}\prod_{i=1}^{g-1}(T_{b_{i}}T_{c_{i}})T_{b_{g}})\text{ and }W_{4g}^{\prime}=(T_{b_{1}}^{2}\prod_{i=1}^{g-1}(T_{c_{i}}T_{b_{i+1}})T_{a_{g}})

of
W_{g,4g+2}
and
W_{g,4g}
, respectively. (It is not hard to check that these are indeed conjugates). Let
W=W_{4g}^{\prime}W_{4g+2}^{\prime}
. A direct computation shows that

P_{W}(x)=x^{2g}+2x^{2g-1}+3x^{2g-2}+\cdots+gx^{g+1}+(g+3)x^{g}+gx^{g-1}+\cdots+2x+1.

We will require the following technical lemma.

Lemma 7.9
.

No complex root of unity can be a root of
P_{W}(x)
.

Proof.

It is apparent that
x=\pm 1
is not a root of
P_{W}(x).
Suppose that an
n^{\text{th}}
root of unity
e^{\iota\theta}
for some
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
and
n\geq 3
, is a root of
P_{W}(x)
. Then we have
P_{W}(e^{\iota\theta})=0
, which yields the following two equations:

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{2g}\cos(j\theta)+\sum_{j=1}^{2g-1}\cos(j\theta)+\cdots+\sum_{j=g-1}^{g+1}\cos(j\theta)+3\cos(g\theta)=0

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{2g}\sin(j\theta)+\sum_{j=1}^{2g-1}\sin(j\theta)+\cdots+\sum_{j=g-1}^{g+1}\sin(j\theta)+3\sin(g\theta)=0

Applying the formulas

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\cos(\alpha+j\beta)=\frac{\cos(\alpha+(n-1)\beta/2)\sin(n\beta/2)}{\sin(\beta/2)}\text{ and}

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\sin(\alpha+j\beta)=\frac{\sin(\alpha+(n-1)\beta/2)\sin(n\beta/2)}{\sin(\beta/2)}

in the pair of equations above, we obtain the pair of equations

\displaystyle\cos(g\theta)(\sin^{2}((g+1)\theta/2)+2\sin^{2}(\theta/2))=0

\displaystyle\sin(g\theta)(\sin^{2}((g+1)\theta/2)+2\sin^{2}(\theta/2))=0

These equations yield a contradiction, as there does not exist any
\theta
such that
\cos(g\theta)=0=\sin(g\theta)
, from which our assertion follows.
∎

This leads us to the following proposition.

Proposition 7.10
.

For
g\geq 2
, there exists conjugates
W_{4g}^{\prime}
and
W_{4g+2}^{\prime}
of
W_{g,4g}
and
W_{g,4g+2}
, respectively, such that
W=W_{4g}^{\prime}W_{4g+2}^{\prime}
is pseudo-Anosov in
\mathrm{Mod}(S_{g})
.

Proof.

It is apparent that
P_{W}(x)
satisfies condition
(iii)
of Theorem
7.8
. Further, condition (ii) of Theorem
7.8
holds true in view of Lemma
7.9
. Finally, to show condition (i) it suffices to show that
W
does not preserve any subsurface of
S_{g}
with genus greater than
0
. To show this, we consider the chain of simple closed curves
C=\{b_{1},c_{1},b_{2},c_{2},\cdots,c_{g-1},b_{g},a_{g}\}
in
S_{g}
(as indicated in Figure
15
). For simplicity, we relabel the curves (appearing in sequence) in
C
by
\{\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{2g}\}
. By the properties of chain maps, we have

W(\alpha_{i})=\alpha_{i+2},\text{ for }1\leq i<2g-1

and further it is easily seen that there exists a
k
such that each component of
\overline{S_{g}\setminus\cup_{i=0}^{k}W^{i}(\alpha_{j})}
has genus
0
. Consequently, it follows that
W
cannot preserve a subsurface of
S_{g}
of genus greater than
0
, from which our assertion follows.
∎

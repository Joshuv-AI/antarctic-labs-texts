Abstract
. An automorphism of a graph is called quasi-semiregular if it fixes a unique vertex of the graph and its remaining cycles have the same length. This kind of symmetry of graphs was first investigated by Kutnar, Malnič, Martínez and Marušič in 2013, as a generalization of the well-known semiregular automorphism of a graph. Symmetric graphs of valency three or four, admitting a quasi-semiregular automorphism, have been classified in recent two papers. Let p ≥ 5 p\geq 5 be a prime and Γ {\it\Gamma} a connected symmetric graph of valency p p admitting a quasi-semiregular automorphism. In this paper, we first prove that either Γ {\it\Gamma} is a connected Cayley graph Cay ⁡ ( M , S ) \mathrm{Cay}(M,S) such that M M is a 2 2 -group admitting a fixed-point-free automorphism of order p p with S S as an orbit of involutions, or Γ {\it\Gamma} is a normal N N -cover of a T T -arc-transitive graph of valency p p admitting a quasi-semiregular automorphism, where T T is a non-abelian simple group and N N is a nilpotent group. Then in case p = 5 p=5 , we give a complete classification of such graphs Γ {\it\Gamma} such that either Aut ⁡ ( Γ ) \mathrm{Aut}({\it\Gamma}) has a solvable arc-transitive subgroup or Γ {\it\Gamma} is T T -arc-transitive with T T a non-abelian simple group. We also construct the first infinite family of symmetric graphs that have a quasi-semiregular automorphism and an insolvable full automorphism group.



1.
Introduction

Graphs studied in this paper are finite, connected, undirected and simple, and groups are finite.
Let
{\it\Gamma}
be a graph with vertex set
V({\it\Gamma})
. An ordered pair of adjacent vertices of
{\it\Gamma}
is called an

.
Denote by

the full automorphism group of
{\it\Gamma}

(or the arcs of
{\it\Gamma}
); we omit the prefix

when

. In particular, an arc-transitive graph is also called a

graph.

Let

be a permutation group on a set

, the subgroup generated by

\Omega
. Semiregular permutations play an important role in the study of vertex-transitive graphs. For example, Biggs
[
2
]
used semiregular automorphisms to give concise descriptions of some interesting graphs. In 1981, Marušič asked whether every vertex-transitive digraph has a non-identity semiregular automorphism
[
29
, Problem 2.4]
. Over the last forty years, numerous papers have been published on this question, and we refer the readers to
[
5
,
8
,
13
,
27
,
30
,
31
,
32
,
34
,
35
]
for vertex-transitive graphs with certain valencies and orders, to
[
11
,

,

,

,
41
,
42
]
for edge-transitive graphs and to
[

and the references therein for a broader survey on these kind of results. Despite consistent effort made, the above Marušič’s question is still wide open.

Our focus in this paper is on the so-called qusi-semiregular automorphism of a graph, which is a natural generalization of semiregular automorphism and defined below. A permutation group
G
on a set
\Omega
is said to be
quasi-semiregular
if
G
fixes a point, say
\alpha
, of

\Omega\setminus\{\alpha\}
. A non-identity permutation

on a set

is said to be

\Omega

As a matter of convenience, we exclude the identity permutation as a quasi-semiregular permutation, which follows from
[
11
,
43
]
but not from
[

,
25
]

Quasi-semiregular permutation groups were first introduced in 2013 by Kutnar, Malnič, Martínez, Marušič
[
25
]
, where they initiated the study of graphs with a quasi-semiregular automorphism group, and later, Hujdurovi
\acute{\rm c}

[
20
]
classified circulants which have a quasi-semiregular automorphism group with at most
5
orbits. There are a large class of vertex-transitive graphs admitting quasi-semiregular automorphisms which are called GFRs. A
graphical Frobenius representation
(GFR) of a Frobenius group
G
(a non-regular transitive permutation group with every non-identity permutation fixing at most one point) is a graph whose automorphism group is isomorphic to
G
. Clearly, every GFR has a quasi-semiregular automorphism. In 2018, Doyle, Tucker, Watkins
[
9
]
started the study of Frobenius groups admitting a GFR, which has been an active topic of research; see, for example,
[
24
,
37
,
38
]
. Inspired by the facts listed above, we are naturally led to consider the following problem:

Problem A:
Characterize or classify vertex-transitive graphs with a quasi-semiregular automorphism.

The investigation of Problem A was began by the second author of this paper together with Hujdurovi
\acute{\rm c}
, Kov
\acute{\rm a}
cs, Kutnar and Maru
\check{\rm s}
i
\check{\rm c}
in

[
11

gave a classification of cubic symmetric graphs with a quasi-semiregular automorphism, and surprisingly, there are only three such graphs: the complete graph

, the Petersen graph and the Coxeter graph. As for the tetravalent symmetric graph, in a very recent paper
[

to prove that a tetravalent symmetric graph has a quasi-semiregular automorphism if and only if it is a Cayley graph on an abelian group of odd order. In this paper, we shall give a partial solution of Problem A by characterizing prime valent symmetric graphs admitting a quasi-semiregular automorphism. Before stating our main results, we introduce some terminologies.

We now state our first main theorem.

Theorem 1.1

Let

be a prime and let

(1)

If

(2)

If every arc-transitive group of automorphisms of

By Theorem

[

, and the nilpotency class of the group is bounded by

[
19

Theorem

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
p
be prime and let
{\it\Gamma}
be a connected symmetric graph of valency
p
admitting a quasi-semiregular automorphism. Then the quasi-semiregular automorphism has order
p
,
|V({\it\Gamma})|\equiv 1\ ({\rm mod~}p)
,
|\mathrm{Aut}({\it\Gamma})|_{p}=p
, and every automorphism of order
p
of
{\it\Gamma}
is quasi-semiregular.

Proof.

Let
A=\mathrm{Aut}({\it\Gamma})
and let
\alpha
be a quasi-semiregular automorphism of
{\it\Gamma}
fixing
v\in V({\it\Gamma})
. Since
{\it\Gamma}
has prime valency
p
,
\langle\alpha\rangle
is transitive on the neighbours of
v
in
{\it\Gamma}
because all orbits of
\langle\alpha\rangle
on the neighbourhood of
v
have same length, and hence
\alpha
has order
p
. It follows that
|V({\it\Gamma})|\equiv 1\ ({\rm mod~}p)
. Note that
{\it\Gamma}
has valency
p
. Since
{\it\Gamma}
is arc-transitive, we have
p\,\big|\,|A_{v}|
, and the connectedness of
{\it\Gamma}
implies that
p^{2}\nmid|A_{v}|
. Thus,
|\mathrm{Aut}({\it\Gamma})|_{p}=p
, and all Sylow
p
-subgroups of
A
have order
p
. By the Sylow Theorem, every Sylow
p
-subgroup of
A
is conjugate to
\langle\alpha\rangle
, and hence every element of order
p
of
A
is quasi-semiregular, as the element of order
p
generates a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
A
.
∎

Next we consider the case when a symmetric graph of prime valency
p
admitting a quasi-semiregular automorphism contains a solvable arc-transitive group of automorphisms.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
{\it\Gamma}
be a connected symmetric graph of prime valency

. Then
\mathrm{Aut}({\it\Gamma})
has a solvable arc-transitive subgroup admitting a quasi-semiregular automorphism if and only if
{\it\Gamma}=\mathrm{Cay}(M,S)

is a
2
-group admitting a fixed-point-free automorphism of order

with

as an orbit and

To prove the sufficiency, let
M
be a
2
-group admitting a fixed-point-free automorphism of order

, say
\beta
, such that
S
is an orbit of
\langle\beta\rangle

is a generating set of involutions of

and
\mathrm{Cay}(M,S)

is a connected graph of valency
p
. Furthermore,

\beta
is an quasi-semiregular automorphism of
\mathrm{Cay}(M,S)
, and

is an solvable arc-transitive group of automorphisms of
\mathrm{Cay}(M,S)
.

To prove the necessity, assume that
{\it\Gamma}
be a connected symmetric graph of prime valency

and that
\mathrm{Aut}({\it\Gamma})
contains a solvable arc-transitive subgroup, say

, admitting a quasi-semiregular automorphism, say

, of order

. Let

be a maximal intransitive normal subgroup of

, and let

be a minimal normal subgroup of
G/N
. Since

is solvable,

is solvable and so

is elementary abelian.

By Proposition
2.3
(1),
{\it\Gamma}
is not bipartite, and hence

has at least three orbits. Then Proposition
2.2
implies that

is semiregular and
{\it\Gamma}_{N}
is
G/N
-arc-transitive, where

. Note that

implies that

. By the maximality of

,

is transitive

, so

is an abelian transitive subgroup of

. By Proposition

,

is regular on

with

. Since a conjugacy of

is quasi-semiregular, we may assume

is a connected graph of valency

Since

, by Proposition

we have

is quasi-semiregular, every orbit of

is a fixed-point-free automorphism of order

Now we consider the case when a symmetric graph of prime valency

admitting a quasi-semiregular automorphism contains no solvable arc-transitive group of automorphisms.

.

Let

be a connected symmetric graph of prime valency

Let

. Let

is a normal

-cover of

is nilpotent and

. By Lemma

is almost simple and

Let

be a minimal normal subgroup of

. Then

and

. Since

is a maximal intransitive normal subgroup of

,

is transitive on

is not regular, and since

is arc-transitive. The minimality of

Suppose

Suppose that

has another minimal normal subgroup

such that

is arc-transitive and non-abelian simple. Note that

commutes with

1.
Introduction

For a Borel subset
E
of the complex plane
\mathbb{C},
let
M_{0}(E)
denote the set of finite complex-valued Borel measures that are compactly supported in
E
and let
M_{0}^{+}(E)
be the set of positive measures in
M_{0}(E).
The support of
\nu\in M_{0}(\mathbb{C}),

\text{spt}(\nu),
is the smallest closed set that has full
|\nu|
measure.
For a compact subset
K\subset\mathbb{C},

\mu\in M_{0}^{+}(K),
and
1\leq t\leq\infty
, the analytic polynomials and functions in

\ \mbox{Rat}(K):=\{q:\mbox{$q$ is a rational function
with poles off $K$}\}

are members of
L^{t}(\mu)
. If
1\leq t<\infty,
we let
P^{t}(\mu)
denote the closure of the (analytic)
polynomials in
L^{t}(\mu)
and let
R^{t}(K,\mu)
denote the closure of
\mbox{Rat}(K)
in
L^{t}(\mu)
. Define

\ R^{t}(K,\mu)^{\perp}=\left\{g\in L^{s}(\mu):~\int f(z)g(z)d\mu=0,\text{ for }f\in R^{t}(K,\mu)\right\},

where
\frac{1}{t}+\frac{1}{s}=1.

Let
R^{\infty}(K,\mu)
be the weak-star closure of
\mbox{Rat}(K)
in
L^{\infty}(\mu).
Denote

\ R^{t,\infty}(K,\mu)=R^{t}(K,\mu)\cap L^{\infty}(\mu).

We let

\partial_{e}K
(the exterior boundary of
K
) denote the union of
the boundaries of all the
components of
\mathbb{C}\setminus K.
The inner boundary of
K
is denoted
\partial_{i}K:=\partial K\setminus\partial_{e}K.

Let
\mathbb{D}
denote the unit
disk
\{z:|z|<1\}
, let
\mathbb{T}
denote the unit circle
\{z:|z|=1\}
, and let
\mathbb{R}
denote the real line
\{z:\text{Im}(z)=0\}.
For
z_{0}\in\mathbb{C}
and
r>0,
let
\mathbb{D}(z_{0},r):=\{z:~|z-z_{0}|<r\}.

Let
l_{B}
be the arc-length measure on a rectifiable curve
\Gamma
restricted to a subset
B\subset\Gamma.
We use
C_{1},C_{2},...
and
c_{1},c_{2},...
for absolute constants that may change from one step to the next.

A point
z_{0}
in
\mathbb{C}
is called a
bounded point evaluation
for
P^{t}(\mu)
(resp.,
R^{t}(K,\mu)
)
if
f\mapsto f(z_{0})
defines a bounded linear functional, denoted
\phi_{z_{0}},
for the analytic polynomials (resp., functions in
\mbox{Rat}(K)
)
with respect to the
L^{t}(\mu)
norm. The collection of all such points is denoted
\mbox{bpe}(P^{t}(\mu))

(resp.,
\mbox{bpe}(R^{t}(K,\mu)
)). If
z_{0}
is in the interior of
\mbox{bpe}(P^{t}(\mu))
(resp.,
\mbox{bpe}(R^{t}(K,\mu)
))
and there exist positive constants
r
and
M
such that
|f(z)|\leq M\|f\|_{L^{t}(\mu)}
, whenever
z\in\mathbb{D}(z_{0},r)

and
f
is an analytic polynomial (resp.,
f\in\mbox{Rat}(K)
), then we say that
z_{0}
is an

analytic bounded point evaluation
for
P^{t}(\mu)
(resp.,
R^{t}(K,\mu)
). The collection of all such
points is denoted
\mbox{abpe}(P^{t}(\mu))
(resp.,
\mbox{abpe}(R^{t}(K,\mu))
). Actually, it follows from Thomson’s Theorem

[
11
]
(or see Theorem
1.1
, below) that
\mbox{abpe}(P^{t}(\mu))
is the interior of
\mbox{bpe}(P^{t}(\mu))
.
This also holds in the context of
R^{t}(K,\mu)
as was shown by J. Conway and N. Elias in
[
7
]
. Now,

\mbox{abpe}(P^{t}(\mu))
is the largest open subset of
\mathbb{C}
to which every function
f\in P^{t}(\mu)
has an analytic
continuation

\ \rho(f)(z):=\phi_{z}(f),~z\in\mbox{abpe}(P^{t}(\mu)),

that is,

\displaystyle\ f(z)=\rho(f)(z)~\mu|_{\mbox{abpe}(P^{t}(\mu))}-a.a.,

(1.1)

and similarly in the context of
R^{t}(K,\mu)
. The map
\rho
is called the evaluation map.

Let
S_{\mu}
denote the multiplication by
z
on
P^{t}(\mu)
(resp.,
R^{t}(K,\mu)
).
The operator
S_{\mu}
is pure if
P^{t}(\mu)
(resp.,
R^{t}(K,\mu)
) does not have a direct
L^{t}
summand,
and is irreducible if
P^{t}(\mu)
(resp.,
R^{t}(K,\mu)
) contains no non-trivial characteristic functions.
For an open subset
U\subset\mathbb{C},
we let
H^{\infty}(U)
denote the bounded analytic functions on
U
.
This is a Banach algebra in the maximum modulus norm, but it is important to endow it also with a weak-star topology.

We shall use
\overline{E}
to denote the closure of the set
E\subset\mathbb{C}
and
\text{int}(E)
to denote the interior of
E.
Our story begins with celebrated results of J. Thomson, in
[
11
]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

(Thomson 1991)

Let
\mu\in M_{0}^{+}(\mathbb{C})
and suppose that
1\leq t<\infty
.
Then there is a Borel partition
\{\Delta_{i}\}_{i=0}^{\infty}
of
\mbox{spt}(\mu)
such that

\ P^{t}(\mu)=L^{t}(\mu|_{\Delta_{0}})\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\infty}P^{t}(\mu|_{\Delta_{i}})

and the following statements are true:

(a) If
i\geq 1
, then
S_{\mu|_{\Delta_{i}}}
on
P^{t}(\mu|_{\Delta_{i}})
is irreducible.

(b) If
i\geq 1
and
U_{i}:=abpe(P^{t}(\mu|_{\Delta_{i}}))
, then
U_{i}
is a simply connected region and
\Delta_{i}\subset\overline{U_{i}}
.

(c) If
S_{\mu}
is pure (that is, if
\Delta_{0}=\emptyset
) and
U=\text{abpe}(P^{t}(\mu))
, then the evaluation map
\rho
is an isometric isomorphism and a weak-star homeomorphism from
P^{t}(\mu)\cap L^{\infty}(\mu)
onto
H^{\infty}(U)
.

The next result is due to Brennan
[
3
]
, which extends Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 1.2
.

(Brennan 2008)

Let
\mu\in M_{0}^{+}(K)
for a compact set
K
.
Suppose that
1\leq t<\infty
and the diameters of the components of
\mathbb{C}\setminus K
are bounded below.
Then there is a Borel partition
\{\Delta_{i}\}_{i=0}^{\infty}
of
\mbox{spt}(\mu)
and compact subsets
\{K_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
such that
\Delta_{i}\subset K_{i}
for
i\geq 1
,

\ R^{t}(K,\mu)=L^{t}(\mu|_{\Delta_{0}})\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\infty}R^{t}(K_{i},\mu|_{\Delta_{i}}),

and the following statements are true:

(a) If
i\geq 1
, then
S_{\mu|_{\Delta_{i}}}
on
R^{t}(K_{i},\mu|_{\Delta_{i}})
is irreducible.

(b) If
i\geq 1
and
U_{i}:=\mbox{abpe}(R^{t}(K_{i},\mu|_{\Delta_{i}}))
, then
K_{i}=\overline{U_{i}}
.

(c) If
i\geq 1
, then the evaluation map
\rho_{i}
is an isometric isomorphism and a weak-star homeomorphism from
R^{t,\infty}(K_{i},\mu|_{\Delta_{i}})
onto
H^{\infty}(U_{i})
.

In this paper, we show that
when the diameters of the components of
\mathbb{C}\setminus K
are allowed to tend to
0
, then
Theorem
1.2
(c) is no long valid, even though
\text{int}(K)=\text{abpe}(R^{t}(K,\mu))
and
K=\overline{\text{int}(K)}.
In fact, the evaluation map is an isometric isomorphism and a weak-star homeomorphism from
R^{t,\infty}(K,\mu)
onto a proper sub-algebra of
H^{\infty}(\text{int}(K)),
where functions in the sub-algebra have equal non-tangential limits on certain portions of
\partial_{i}K.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}

be a subset of
\mathbb{R}

such that
\mathbb{D}(\lambda_{n},r_{n})
is a subset of
\mathbb{D}
and
\overline{\mathbb{D}(\lambda_{i},r_{i})}\cap\overline{\mathbb{D}(\lambda_{j},r_{j})}=\emptyset
for
i\neq j
. Define

\ K\ :=\ \overline{\mathbb{D}}\setminus\left(\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{D}(\lambda_{n},r_{n})\right).

Assume
K\cap\mathbb{R}
has no interior
as a subset of
\mathbb{R}
,
and that the length of
K\cap\mathbb{R}
is greater than zero (i.e.

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}r_{n}<1
).
A set of this form is called a string of beads set.
Set
\Omega=int(K)
. Define

\Omega_{U}=\{z\in\Omega:~\text{Im}(z)>0\},~\Omega_{L}=\Omega\setminus\Omega_{U}
,
K_{U}=\overline{\Omega_{U}}
, and
K_{L}=\overline{\Omega_{L}}
.

Clearly,
\partial_{i}K=K\cap\mathbb{R},~l_{\mathbb{R}}-a.a..
For
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
0<\alpha\leq 1
, define the upper cone

\displaystyle\ U_{n}(\lambda,\alpha)=\left\{z:~|Re(z-\lambda)|<\alpha(Im(z-\lambda))\right\}\cap\mathbb{D}\left(\lambda,\frac{1}{n}\right)

(1.2)

and the lower cone

\displaystyle\ L_{n}(\lambda,\alpha)=\left\{z:~|Re(z-\lambda)|<-\alpha(Im(z-\lambda))\right\}\cap\mathbb{D}\left(\lambda,\frac{1}{n}\right).

(1.3)

It is easy to verify that for
\lambda\in\partial_{i}K,~l_{\partial_{i}K}-a.a.,
there exists
n_{\lambda}
such that the non-tangential limit region

\displaystyle\ U_{n_{\lambda}}(\lambda,\alpha)\cup L_{n_{\lambda}}(\lambda,\alpha)\subset\Omega.

(1.4)

Fatou’s theorem says that bounded analytic functions in the unit disk have non-tangential limits almost everywhere. Precomposing with a Riemann map from
\mathbb{D}
onto
\Omega_{U}
we get that, for
f\in H^{\infty}(\Omega),
the limits

\displaystyle\ f_{+}(\lambda):=\lim_{z\in U_{n}(\lambda,\alpha)\rightarrow\lambda}f(z)

(1.5)

exist
l_{\partial_{i}K}-a.a.
since the Riemann map is conformal from
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
onto
\overline{\Omega_{U}}
(see
[
9
, pp 208-218]
and
[
10
, pp 80-82]
), and likewise the limits

\displaystyle\ f_{-}(\lambda):=\lim_{z\in L_{n}(\lambda,\alpha)\rightarrow\lambda}f(z)

(1.6)

exist
l_{\partial_{i}K}-a.a.

For a subset
E_{0}\subset\partial_{i}K,

H^{\infty}(\Omega,E_{0})
is the sub-algebra of
f\in H^{\infty}(\Omega)
for which
f_{+}(z)=f_{-}(z),~l_{E_{0}}-a.a..
The set
E_{0}
is called the removable boundary for
H^{\infty}(\Omega,E_{0}).

The aim of this paper is to prove our main theorem below. By (
1.1
), we will also use
f|_{\text{abpe}(R^{t}(K,\mu))}
for
\rho(f).

Main Theorem
.

Let
1\leq t<\infty
and let
K
be a string of beads set defined as in Definition
1.3
. Suppose that
\mu\in M_{0}^{+}(K),

S_{\mu}
on
R^{t}(K,\mu)
is pure, and
\text{abpe}(R^{t}(K,\mu))=\Omega.
Then there exists a Borel subset
E_{0}\subset\partial_{i}K
such that the following statements are true:

(1) If
f\in R^{t}(K,\mu)
, then the non-tangential limit from the top on
E_{0}
,

f_{+}(z)
, and the non-tangential limit from
the bottom on
E_{0}
,
f_{-}(z)
, exist
l_{E_{0}}-a.a.
, and

\ f_{+}(z)=f_{-}(z),~l_{E_{0}}-a.a..

Consequently, if
f\in R^{t,\infty}(K,\mu),
then
\rho(f)\in H^{\infty}(\Omega,E_{0}).

(2) If
F\in H^{\infty}(\Omega,E_{0}),
then there exists
f\in R^{t,\infty}(K,\mu)
such that
F=\rho(f).

(3) The evaluation map
\rho
is an isometric isomorphism and a weak-star homeomorphism from
R^{t,\infty}(K,\mu)
onto
H^{\infty}(\Omega,E_{0}).

Our methods can be applied to a compact subset
K\subset\mathbb{C}
and
\mu\in M_{0}^{+}(K)
if
S_{\mu}
on
R^{t}(K,\mu)
is pure,
\text{abpe}(R^{t}(K,\mu))=\text{int}(K),

\partial_{i}K
is a subset of a Lipschitz graph, and
\text{int}(K)
contains non-tangential limit regions (similar to (
1.4
)) whose vertices are on
\partial_{i}K
almost everywhere with respect to the arc-length measure restricted to
\partial_{i}K.

In section 2, we review some recent results of analytic capacity and Cauchy transform that are needed in our analysis. In the context of
R^{\infty}(K,\mu),
Proposition
3.1
is a simple version of Main Theorem (1) where
E_{0}
is always equal to
\partial_{i}K.
However, for
R^{t}(K,\mu)
when
1\leq t<\infty,

E_{0}
can be an arbitrary subset of
\partial_{i}K
as shown in Proposition
3.4
. section 4 constructs the removable boundary
E_{0}
and proves Main Theorem (1). Main Theorem (2) and (3) are proved in section 5 and section 6.

\displaystyle\ \mathcal{C}_{\epsilon}(\nu)(z)=\int_{|w-z|>\epsilon}\dfrac{1}{w-z}d\nu(w).\

capacity. Throughout this paper, the Cauchy transform of a measure always means the principal value of the transform.

The maximal Cauchy transform is defined by

\ \mathcal{C}_{*}(\nu)(z)=\sup_{\epsilon>0}|\mathcal{C}_{\epsilon}(\nu)(z)|.

\ \gamma(B)=\sup|f^{\prime}(\infty)|,

\ \gamma(E)=\sup\{\gamma(B):B\subset E\text{ compact}\}.

3.
Examples

For a compact subset
K\subset\mathbb{C}
and
\mu\in M_{0}^{+}(K),
the envelope
E=E(K,\mu)
is the subset of
z\in K
that satisfies the property: there exists
\mu_{z}\in M_{0}(K)
, with
\mu_{z}
absolutely continuous with respect to
\mu
, and such that
\mu_{z}(\{z\})=0
and
\int fd\mu_{z}=f(z)
for each

Chaumat’s Theorem says that if
R^{\infty}(K,\mu)
does not contain a
L^{\infty}
summand, then the map
\rho(f)=f
for
f\in\text{Rat}(K)
extends an isometric isomorphism and a weak-star homeomorphism from
R^{\infty}(K,\mu)
onto
R^{\infty}(K,{\mathfrak{m}}|_{E})

(see
[
4
]
or
[
6
, Chapter VI.3]
). The map
\rho
is called Chaumat’s map.
We start with a simple version of Main Theorem (1) for
R^{\infty}(K,\mu)
as the following proposition.

Proposition 3.1
.

For a string of beads set
K
and
\mu\in M_{0}^{+}(K),
assume that
R^{\infty}(K,\mu)
has no
L^{\infty}
summand and the envelope
E
contains
\Omega.

Then
\rho(f)\in H^{\infty}(\Omega,\partial_{i}K)
for
f\in R^{\infty}(K,\mu).

Proof.

Let
a=\frac{1}{2}i
and
b=-\frac{1}{2}i
, where
i=\sqrt{-1}
. Let
\omega_{a}
and
\omega_{b}
be the harmonic measures of
\Omega_{U}
and
\Omega_{L}
evaluated at
a
and
b
,
respectively. Let
\omega=\omega_{a}+\omega_{b}.

It is clear that
\int r(z)(z-a)(z-b)d\omega(z)=0
for
r\in Rat(K)
. So
R^{\infty}(K,\omega)
has no
L^{\infty}
summand and the envelope
E(K,\omega)\supset\Omega.
From Chaumat’s Theorem, the Chaumat map
\rho

is an isometric isomorphism and a weak-star homeomorphism from
R^{\infty}(K,\omega)
onto
R^{\infty}(K,{\mathfrak{m}}_{K}).

Let
\{r_{n}\}\subset Rat(K)
be
such that
r_{n}\rightarrow f
in
L^{\infty}(\omega)
weak-star topology. Hence,
f\in H^{\infty}(\Omega_{U})
and
f\in H^{\infty}(\Omega_{L})
.
Thus,
f_{+}(z)
exists,
f_{+}(z)=f(z),~\omega_{a}|_{\partial_{i}K}-a.a.
; and
f_{-}(z)
exists,

f_{-}(z)=f(z),~\omega_{b}|_{\partial_{i}K}-a.a.
. Since
\partial\Omega_{U}
and
\partial\Omega_{L}

are rectifiable Jordan curves, the measures
\omega_{a}|_{\partial_{i}K}
,
\omega_{b}|_{\partial_{i}K}
, and
l_{\partial_{i}K}
are mutually absolutely continuous. Therefore,

\ f_{+}(z)=f_{-}(z)=f(z),~l_{\partial_{i}K}-a.a.

Hence,
R^{\infty}(K,{\mathfrak{m}}_{K})\subset H^{\infty}(\Omega,\partial_{i}K).
Now the proof follows from Chaumat’s Theorem.
∎

\displaystyle\ R^{2}(K,{\mathfrak{m}}_{W})=R^{2}(K_{U},{\mathfrak{m}}_{W}|_{\Omega_{U}})\oplus R^{2}(K_{L},{\mathfrak{m}}_{W}|_{\Omega_{L}}).

\ &\rightarrow 0.\ \end{aligned}

\mathcal{C}(g{\mathfrak{m}}_{W})(\lambda)=0
for

. Let
\nu
be

restricted to upper circle of

\chi_{\Omega_{U}}(z)\in R^{2}(K,{\mathfrak{m}}_{W})

\|f_{3}\|\leq C_{10},

c_{1}(f_{3})=\frac{\delta}{16},
and
c_{2}(f_{3},c_{S})=0,
Set

\ f_{S}=\dfrac{16c_{1}(T_{\varphi}f)}{\delta}f_{3}+\dfrac{256c_{2}(T_{\varphi}f,c_{S})}{\delta^{2}}f_{2}^{2}.

The proof now follows from Lemma
5.2
.
∎

Let
\delta>0
. We say that

\{\varphi_{ij},S_{ij},\delta\}
is a smooth partition of unity subordinated to
\{2S_{ij}\},

if the following assumptions hold:

(i)
S_{ij}
is a square with vertices
(i\delta,j\delta),~((i+1)\delta,j\delta),~(i\delta,(j+1)\delta),
and
((i+1)\delta,(j+1)\delta);

(ii)
\varphi_{ij}
is a
C^{\infty}
smooth function supported in
2S_{ij},

and with values in
[0,1]
;

(iii)

\ \|\bar{\partial}\varphi_{ij}\|\leq\frac{C_{1}}{\delta},~\sum\varphi_{ij}=1.

Given a square grid
\{S_{ij}\}
, we shall let
c_{ij}
denote the center of
S_{ij}
(See
[
8
, VIII.7]
for details).

Proof.

(Main Theorem (2))
Let
\{\varphi_{ij},S_{ij},\delta\}
be a smooth partition of unity.
Let
f\in H^{\infty}(\Omega,E_{0}),

\|f\|\leq 1,
and set
f(z)=0
for
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\Omega.
Then

\displaystyle\ f=\sum_{2S_{ij}\cap\partial\Omega\neq\emptyset}(f_{ij}:=T_{\varphi_{ij}}f).

(6.2)

Let
J
be the set of
(i,j)
satisfying
i\in\{-2,-1,0,1\}
and
2S_{ij}\subset\mathbb{D}.

We apply Lemma
6.1
when
(i,j)\in J
and Lemma
6.2
when
(i,j)\notin J
for
S=2S_{ij}
and
\varphi=\varphi_{ij}
to get
f_{S_{ij}}.

We rewrite (
6.2
) as the following

\ f=\sum_{(i,j)\in J,~2S_{ij}\cap\partial\Omega\neq\emptyset}(f_{ij}-f_{S_{ij}})+\sum_{(i,j)\notin J,~2S_{ij}\cap\partial\Omega\neq\emptyset}(f_{ij}-f_{S_{ij}})+f_{\delta}

where

\displaystyle\ f_{\delta}=\sum_{2S_{ij}\cap\partial\Omega\neq\emptyset}f_{S_{ij}}\in R^{t,\infty}(K,\mu).

(6.3)

Clearly,
\rho(f_{\delta})=f_{\delta}|_{\Omega}\in H^{\infty}(\Omega,E_{0}).
Using Vitushkin approximation scheme (see
[
8
, VIII.7]
), we get

\displaystyle|f(z)-f_{\delta}(z)|

\displaystyle\ \leq

\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j)\in J,~2S_{ij}\cap\partial\Omega\neq\emptyset}|f_{ij}(z)-f_{S_{ij}}(z)|+\sum_{(i,j)\notin J,~2S_{ij}\cap\partial\Omega\neq\emptyset}|f_{ij}(z)-f_{S_{ij}}(z)|

\displaystyle\ \leq

\displaystyle C_{11}\sum_{\underset{-2\leq i\leq 1}{2S_{ij}\cap\partial\Omega\neq\emptyset}}\min\left(1,\dfrac{\delta^{2}}{dist(z,3S_{ij})^{2}}\right)+C_{9}\sum_{2S_{ij}\cap\partial\Omega\neq\emptyset}\min\left(1,\dfrac{\delta^{3}}{dist(z,3S_{ij})^{3}}\right)

\displaystyle\ \leq

\displaystyle C_{12}\min\left(1,\dfrac{\delta}{dist(z,\partial\Omega)}\right).

Therefore,
f_{\delta}(z)\rightarrow f(z)
uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{C}\setminus\partial\Omega
and
\|f_{\delta}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mu)}\leq C_{13}.
There exists a subsequence
\{f_{\delta_{n}}\}
such that
f_{\delta_{n}}(z)\rightarrow\tilde{f}(z)
in
L^{\infty}(\mu)
weak-star topology. Hence,
\tilde{f}\in R^{t,\infty}(K,\mu)
and
\mathcal{C}(f_{\delta_{n}}g\mu)(z)\rightarrow\mathcal{C}(\tilde{f}g\mu)(z),~{\mathfrak{m}}-a.a..
Thus,
f(z)\mathcal{C}(g\mu)(z)=\mathcal{C}(\tilde{f}g\mu)(z),~{\mathfrak{m}}-a.a.
for
g\perp R^{t}(K,\mu),
which implies
\rho(\tilde{f})=f.

∎

We are now ready to prove Main Theorem (3).

Proof.

(Main Theorem (3))
From Main Theorem (1), we see that
\rho(f)\in H^{\infty}(\Omega,E_{0})
for
f\in R^{t,\infty}(K,\mu).
Clearly,

\ \rho(f_{1}f_{2})=\rho(f_{1})\rho(f_{2}),~f_{1},f_{2}\in R^{t,\infty}(K,\mu).

Therefore, for
f\in R^{t,\infty}(K,\mu),
since
\lambda\in\Omega
is an analytic bounded point evaluation, we see that there exists a constant
C_{\lambda}>0
such that

\ |\rho(f)(\lambda)|\leq C_{\lambda}^{\frac{1}{n}}\|f^{n}\|_{L^{t}(\mu)}^{\frac{1}{n}}.

Taking
n\rightarrow\infty,
we get
\|\rho(f)\|_{\Omega}\leq\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mu)}.

From the last paragraph of the proof of Main Theorem (2), we get
\|f^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mu)}^{\frac{1}{n}}\leq C_{13}^{\frac{1}{n}}\|\rho(f^{n})\|_{\Omega}^{\frac{1}{n}}.

Again letting

n\rightarrow\infty,
we get
\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mu)}\leq\|\rho(f)\|_{\Omega}.
Thus,

\ \|\rho(f)\|_{\Omega}=\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mu)},~f\in\in R^{t,\infty}(K,\mu).

On the other hand,
\rho
is surjective by Main Theorem (2). Therefore,
\rho
is a bijective isomorphism between two Banach algebras
R^{t,\infty}(K,\mu)
and
H^{\infty}(\Omega,E_{0})
. Clearly
\rho
is also weak-star sequentially continuous, so an application of the Krein-Smulian Theorem shows that
\rho
is a weak-star homeomorphism.
∎

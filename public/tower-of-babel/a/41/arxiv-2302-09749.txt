Observation of Electronic Structure Modification in the Hidden Order Phase of CeCoSi
Shin-ichi Kimura 1,2,3, Hiroshi Watanabe 1,2, Shingo Tatsukawa 2, and Hiroshi Tanida 4

Abstract
CeCoSi with no local inversion symmetric crystal structure ( P ​ 4 / n ​ m ​ m P4/nmm ) exhibits a phase transition of unknown origin (Hidden Order: HO) at about 12 K ( T 0 T_{0} ) above the antiferromagnetic transition temperature ( T N T_{\rm N} = 9.4 =9.4 K). The electronic structure change across T 0 T_{0} was investigated with high-precision optical reflection spectroscopy. The optical spectrum changed from a typical metallic behavior above T 0 T_{0} to a gap-like structure at around 15 meV below T 0 T_{0} . The gap-like structure was unchanged across T N T_{\rm N} except for the narrowing of the Drude component of carriers due to the suppression of magnetic fluctuations. This result suggests a slight change from the typical metallic electronic structure above T 0 T_{0} to that with an energy gap near the Fermi level in the HO phase. The change in electronic structure in the HO phase was concluded to be due to electron/valence instability.

The electric field generated by the non-inversion symmetry produces

Such novel characteristic physical properties and electronic structures can be realized in materials with non-inversion symmetric crystal structures.

In rare-earth compounds with non-inversion symmetric / non-centrosymmetric crystal structures, fascinating physical properties have been observed mainly in superconducting properties:
A Cooper pair with a helical spin structure produces a superconducting state with a high upper critical field in global non-centrosymmetric materials CePt
3
Si, CeCoGe

Locally non-centrosymmetric Kondo semiconductors, Ce

CeCoSi, the title compound, is one of the locally non-centrosymmetric rare-earth compounds.
It has a CeFeSi-type crystal structure (

Since the electronic specific-heat coefficient

The Néel temperature (

\sim 10

Recently, HO, in which the order parameter has not been revealed yet,

The origin is still unclear

Experimentally,

With these results, some anomalies in the HO phase have been observed:

These results suggest that the electronic structure changes at

Optical measurements such as optical conductivity (

In URu

In addition, in the HO phase of Ce

These examples show that infrared spectroscopy is useful for observing slight changes in electronic structures.

In this Letter, we performed high-precision infrared spectroscopy to investigate whether a change in the electronic structure of CeCoSi occurs at
T_{0}
.
We observed the change in the reflectivity (
R(\omega)
) spectrum and the
\sigma_{1}(\omega)
spectrum at
T_{0}
and also at
T_{\rm N}
.
Below the photon energy (
\hbar\omega

\hbar\omega

\sim 20
and 40 meV,
a monotonic increase toward lower energies due to a Drude absorption of carriers and interband transitions of low-energy excitations appeared.
High-precision temperature dependence measurements revealed that
the electronic structure near the Fermi level (
E_{\rm F}
) slightly changes to a gap-like form below
T_{0}
.
At
T_{\rm N}
, the relaxation time of the Drude parameter was discontinuously changed, implying the suppression of magnetic fluctuations below the antiferromagnetic transition.

Single-crystalline CeCoSi samples were synthesized by a Ce/Co eutectic flux method
[
13
]
.
The

Near-normal-incident

To get the highly accurate

we adopted a feed-back positioning system combined with an

.
For extrapolation of the low and high energy sides in KKA, we used the conventional methods of the Hagen-Rubens function for metallic

spectra below the lowest accessible energy and measuring
R(\omega)
spectra up to 30 eV only at 300 K
[

connected with a free-electron approximation

[
26

.
Additionally, in order to clarify small changes in the
R(\omega)
spectrum at metallic reflectivity higher than 90 % for the electronic structure modification at

, we measured the relative

change by temperature from 6 K (below
T_{\rm N}
) to 20 K (above

With this method, we were able to capture changes in the

An energy-gap-like structure with a gap size (

The amount of the

\sim 10

The change is much smaller than that of other HO materials;

The reason is considered to be due to the remaining large background of the Drude component and low-energy interband transition that does not change with temperature.
This energy gap-like structure formation suggests that some bands close to
E_{\rm F}
, including conduction bands, are modulated at

The size of
\Delta E
is similar to a broad peak observed by INS

and the

[

The optical gap observed here below

may be related to both the INS peak and the crystal field,
but we must consider that optical measurements detect the excitation at the momentum transfer of

\sim 0
, in contrast to a finite

The origin of the optical gap and INS peak may be the

-

Then, the valence of Ce-ions is slightly shifted from trivalent (

On the other hand, below

, the Ce valence becomes trivalent because the

Therefore, at temperatures slightly above

, an electronic/valence instability can appear.
By applying pressure, the anomaly in the electrical resistivity at

becomes more visible and moves to the high-temperature side,
suggesting that the electronic/valence instability is grown with increasing pressure because the

The pressure dependence typically appears in Ce-based heavy fermions

The INS peak also shifts to the high-energy side with increasing pressure, which is the same as the behavior of the anomaly at

Another possibility is that the electronic/valence instability may originate from the characteristic degeneracy specific to the nonsymmorphic crystal structure discussed in LaMnSi

Optical measurements under pressure may provide conclusive information for the origin of HO and its relation to the INS peak.

The observed electronic/valence instability at

is similar to that of Ce

The crystal structure of Ce

Since all of these phase transitions are considered to originate from electronic/valence instability,
optical measurements, including ARPES, with high accuracy and resolution, will be essential in investigating the origin of HO.

To summarize, we investigated the origin of the HO phase of CeCoSi using temperature-dependent high-precision relative infrared
R(\omega)
spectroscopy.
The
R(\omega)
spectra and the
\sigma_{1}(\omega)
spectra after the Kramer-Kronig analysis clearly show non-monotonic temperature dependence at
T_{0}
and
T_{\rm N}
.
The anomaly at
T_{\rm N}
originates from the elongation of the carrier relaxation time due to the suppression of magnetic fluctuations in the antiferromagnetic phase.
On the other hand, the change at
T_{0}
is derived from the deviation of the low-energy interband transition component, with the emergence of an energy-gap-like structure with the size of
\sim 15
meV.
This result indicates that the electronic structure modification due to electronic/valence instability appears in the HO phase of CeCoSi.
